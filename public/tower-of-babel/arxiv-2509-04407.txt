Magnetic Field Induced by Straight Currents on the Hyperboloid
Tomotoshi Nishino

Abstract
As an introductory exercise of elementary electrodynamics, we consider the static magnetic field induced by the electric currents flow along the straight wires, which are equidistantly put on the hyperboloid. The distribution of the magnetic field and the force acting on the wires are calculated under several typical setups, including the continuum limit.

Introduction

Walking along the seaside of Kobe city in Japan, one encounters the famous red tower,
which is called
The Kobe Port Tower
, shown in Fig. 1, constructed in 1963.
The tower consists of 32 straight jointed pipes, which typically forms the hyperbolic geometry,
and is 108 m high. In the following, we consider an elementary study of the usage of
such a hyperbolic structure as an electromagnet.

Figure 1:
The Kobe Port Tower
in the sea side of Kobe city.

When the uniform electric current of the density
\sigma
flow on the surface of
the infinitely long cylinder of the radius
R
in parallel with the central axis,
the surrounding magnetic field of the strength

H(r)=\sigma\frac{R}{r}=\frac{J}{2\pi r}

(1)

exists outside the cylinder
r>R
, where
r
is the distance from the central axis,
and
J=2\pi R\sigma
represents the total current.
To check the consistency of the above relation with the Maxwell’s equation
[
1
]

is one of the fundamental exercise in the college study of electrodynamics.

Figure 2:
Straight current flow on the hyperboloid, which is specified by Eq. (2).

In this short article, we consider the one parameter deformation to the uniform current on
the cylinder. Suppose that the current flows in the straight manner as shown in Fig. 2
on the hyperboloid, which is specified by the equation

x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}-\left(z\cot\phi\right)^{2}_{~}=R^{2}_{~}\,,

(2)

where
x
,
y
, and
z
represent the Cartesian coordinates. When the angle
\phi
,
which is the deformation parameter, is equal to
\pi/2
, the hyperboloid reduces to the
cylinder of the radius
R
. Throughout this article we assume that the system
extends to infinity (
-\infty<z<\infty
). To
calculate the corresponding magnetic field
inside the hyperboloid, where
x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}-\left(z\cot\phi\right)^{2}_{~}<R^{2}_{~}

is satisfied, and outside,
x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}-\left(z\cot\phi\right)^{2}_{~}>R^{2}_{~}
,
would be a good exercise. We encounter the strong cancellation of electromagnetic
force act on the current when
\phi=\pi/4
.

In the next section we approximately treat the quasi uniform current on the
hyperboloid by the assemble of discrete currents, which flow through the straight wires.
The corresponding magnetic field is obtained, in particular on the central axis of the
hyperboloid. A preliminal observation is done for the continuum limit.
In the last section, we discuss a possible application of the considered geometrical setup
as a high-field magnet, including the helical coil wired on a torus.

2
Straight Currents on a Hyperboloid

We start from the discrete analogue of the quasi uniform current on the hyperboloid
specified by Eq. (2). Suppose that
N
numbers of straight wires are arranged
equidistantly on the hyperboloid, as shown in Fig. 2. The case
N=16
is typically
drawn. Let us label the wires by the index
\ell=0,\,1,\,2,\,\cdots,\,N-1
.
Figure 3 shows the location of the
\ell
-th wire, where the angle

\theta_{\ell}^{~}=2\pi\ell/N
specifies the crossing point of the wire
with respect to the
xy
-plane with the contact angle
\phi
.

Figure 3:
Location of the
\ell
-th wire.

Any point on the
\ell
-th wire can be represented by the position vector

{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}[\alpha]=\left(\begin{matrix}-\alpha\cos\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}+R\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~\alpha\cos\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}+R\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~\alpha\sin\phi~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~\,\end{matrix}\right)\,,

(3)

where
\alpha
corresponds to the one-dimensional coordinate on the wire.
For the latter convenience, we introduce the set of orthogonal unit vectors

{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left(\begin{matrix}-\cos\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~~\cos\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
\sin\phi~~~~~\end{matrix}\right)\,,~~~{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left(\begin{matrix}\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
0\end{matrix}\right)\,,~~~{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}={\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}\times{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left(\begin{matrix}-\sin\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~\,\,\sin\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
-\cos\phi~~~~~~~~\end{matrix}\right)\,,

(4)

which enables us to express
{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}[\alpha]
in Eq. (3) in the linear form

{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}[\alpha]=\alpha{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}+R{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}\,.

(5)

We assume that the current of the magnitude
j
flows through each wire to the direction of

{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}
. The corresponding current vector is thus represented by

{\bm{j}}_{\ell}^{~}=j{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}\,.

(6)

Let us calculate the magnetic field induced by the current at an arbitrary position
{\bm{r}}
,
whose components are
x
,
y
, and
z
. We assume that there is no other source of the
field. In order to obtain the distance
d_{\ell}^{~}
from
{\bm{r}}
to the
\ell
-th wire,
we first find the root of the perpendicular line from
{\bm{r}}
to the wire.
The position of the root,
{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}\left[{\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}\right]
satisfies the
orthogonal relation

\left({\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}[{\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}]\right)\cdot{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left({\bm{r}}-{\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}-R{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}\right)\cdot{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}=0\,,

(7)

which determines the parameter

{\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}={\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}=-x\cos\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}+y\cos\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}+z\sin\phi\,.

(8)

For convenience, we also define the parameters

\displaystyle{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}

\displaystyle={\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}=x\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}+y\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\,,

\displaystyle{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}

\displaystyle={\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}=-x\sin\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}+y\sin\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}-z\cos\phi\,,

(9)

and represent
{\bm{r}}
in the form

{\bm{r}}={\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}+{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}
.
We then have the relation

{\bm{r}}-{\bm{r}}_{\ell}^{~}[{\alpha}_{\ell}^{~}]=\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right){\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}=d_{\ell}^{~}{\bf e}_{\ell}^{~}\,,

(10)

where the distance
d_{\ell}^{~}
is expressed as

d_{\ell}^{~}=\sqrt{\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right)^{2}_{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,,

(11)

and the unit vector
{\bm{e}}_{\ell}^{~}
is given by

{\bm{e}}_{\ell}^{~}=\frac{{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R}{d_{\ell}^{~}}\,{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}+\frac{{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}}{d_{\ell}^{~}}\,{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}\,.

(12)

As a consequence, the magnetic field created by
{\bm{j}}_{\ell}^{~}
at the position
{\bm{r}}
is
obtained as

{\bm{h}}_{\ell}^{~}({\bm{r}})=\frac{j}{2\pi d_{\ell}^{~}}\,{\bm{u}}_{\ell}^{~}\times{\bm{e}}_{\ell}^{~}=j\frac{{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R}{2\pi d_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}-j\frac{{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}}{2\pi d_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}\,.

(13)

Summing up the contributions from all the wires, we obtain the magnetic field

{\bm{H}}({\bm{r}})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}{\bm{h}}_{\ell}^{~}({\bm{r}})=\frac{j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\frac{{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R}{\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right)^{2}_{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}-\frac{j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\frac{{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}}{\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right)^{2}_{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}

(14)

at the position
{\bm{r}}
.

On the central axis
x=y=0
, where
{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}=0
and

{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}=-z\cos\phi
is satisfied, we obtain the simpler form

{\bm{H}}(z{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}{\bm{h}}_{\ell}^{~}(z{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~})=\frac{j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\frac{-R}{R^{2}_{~}+\left(z\cos\phi\right)^{2}_{~}}\,{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}-\frac{j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}\frac{-z\cos\phi}{R^{2}_{~}+\left(z\cos\phi\right)^{2}_{~}}\,{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}\,,

(15)

where
{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}
is the unit vector that points the
z
axis.
Remembering that
\theta_{\ell}^{~}=2\pi\ell/N
, the relation

\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}={\bm{0}}\,,~~~~~~\sum_{\ell=0}^{N-1}{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}=-N\cos\phi\,{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}

(16)

holds in Eq. (15), and thus we further obtain

{\bm{H}}(z{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~})=\frac{Nj}{2\pi}\,\frac{R\cos\phi}{R^{2}_{~}+\left(z\cos\phi\right)^{2}_{~}}\,{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}=\frac{J}{2\pi R}\,\frac{\cos\phi}{1+\left[\,(z/R)\cos\phi\,\right]^{2}_{~}}\,{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}\,,

(17)

where
J=Nj
is the total current that passes through the
xy
-plane.
The magnitude of the field on the
z
-axis is strongest at the origin

\bigl|{\bm{H}}({\bm{0}})\bigr|=J\cos\phi/2\pi R
, and decays proportionally
with
|z|^{-2}_{~}
in the region
|z|\gg R
.

Let us define the magnetic field created by the wires other than the
0
-th one

{\overline{\bm{H}}}({\bf r})=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}{\bm{h}}_{\ell}^{~}({\bm{r}})\,.

(18)

The electro magnetic force per length acting on the
0
-th wire
at the position
{\bm{r}}_{0}^{~}[\alpha]
is then expressed as

\displaystyle\mu{\bm{j}}_{0}^{~}

\displaystyle\times{\overline{\bm{H}}}\left({\bm{r}}_{0}^{~}[\alpha]\right)=\mu j\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}{\bm{u}}_{0}^{~}\times{\bm{h}}_{\ell}^{~}\left({\bm{r}}_{0}^{~}[\alpha]\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\mu j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\frac{{\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R}{\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right)^{2}_{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{u}}_{0}^{~}\times{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}-\frac{\mu j}{2\pi}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\frac{{\gamma}_{\ell}^{~}}{\left({\beta}_{\ell}^{~}-R\right)^{2}_{~}+{\gamma}_{\ell}^{\,2}}\,{\bm{u}}_{0}^{~}\times{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}\,,

(19)

where
\mu
represents the permeability.
Far from the
xy
-plane where
|\alpha|\gg R
is saticefied,
each term in the summation is nearly proportional to
|\alpha|^{-1}_{~}
.
A simple case is
\alpha=0
, where the components of
{\bm{r}}_{0}^{~}[\alpha]

are
x=R
, and
y=z=0
, and the corresponding parameters are

\beta_{\ell}^{~}=R\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}
and

\gamma_{\ell}^{~}=-R\sin\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}
.
Looking at the components of the vectors

\displaystyle{\bm{u}}_{0}^{~}\times{\bm{w}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left(\begin{matrix}-(\cos\phi)^{2}_{~}-(\sin\phi)^{2}_{~}\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~\,-(\sin\phi)^{2}_{~}\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~~~~~~~~~~~~\sin\phi\cos\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\end{matrix}\right)\,,~~~{\bm{u}}_{0}^{~}\times{\bm{v}}_{\ell}^{~}=\left(\begin{matrix}-\sin\phi\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
~~\,\sin\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\\
-\cos\phi\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}\end{matrix}\right)\,,

(20)

and considering the parity with respect to
\theta_{\ell}^{~}=2\pi\ell/N
,
one finds that
y
- and
z
-components of the force vanish.
In particular, when
\phi=\pi/4
, where
\sin\phi=\cos\phi=1/\sqrt{2}
holds,
the
x
-component of the force is represented as

\frac{\mu j}{2\pi R}\sum_{\ell=1}^{N-1}\frac{-(1/2)\left(\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}-1\right)\left(\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}+1\right)-(1/2)\left(\sin\theta_{\ell}^{~}\right)^{2}_{~}}{\left(\cos\theta_{\ell}^{~}-1\right)^{2}_{~}+1/2}=0\,,

(21)

and it also vanishes. Thus no force act to the wires at least on the
xy
-plane.
We leave the calculation of the force when
|\alpha|>0
as an additional exercise.

In the last part of this section, we consider the continuum limit
N\rightarrow\infty
,
keeping the total current
J
constant. It should be noted that
{\bm{H}}(z{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~})

in Eq. (17) is not dependent on
N
under the condition of constant
J
, and therefore
this form can be regarded as that of the continuum limit.

The rotational symmetry with respect to the
z
-axis, which is recovered in the large

N
limit, simplifies the analysis. On the
xy
-plane, where
{\bm{r}}\cdot{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}=0

is satisfied, the magnetic field outside the hyperboloid
x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}>R^{2}_{~}
is
given by

{\bm{H}}({\bf r})=\frac{J\sin\phi}{2\pi r}\,{\bf e}_{z}^{~}\times{\bf e}_{r}^{~}\,,

(22)

where
r=|{\bm{r}}|=\sqrt{x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}}
and
{\bm{e}}_{r}^{~}={\bm{r}}/r
.
Note that the field strength just outside the hyperboloid is
J\sin\phi/2\pi R
.
How about the inside, where
x^{2}_{~}+y^{2}_{~}<R^{2}_{~}
is satisfied?
From the geometrical symmetry, it is apparent that the field points the
z
direction.
Since there is no current inside the hyperboloid, the field strength is always
equal to
J\cos\phi/2\pi R
on the
xy
-plane when
r<R
.
Thus when
\phi=\pi/4
, the field strength inside and that outside is the same
at
r=R
. This means that the Maxwell stress is balanced on the surface
of the hyperboloid along the circle of radius
R
.
The author conjecture that these boundary conditions are sufficient to determine the field
from the partial differential equation

\nabla\cdot{\bm{H}}({\bm{r}})=\frac{1}{r}\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\bigl(rH_{r}^{~}\bigr)+\frac{\partial}{\partial z}H_{z}^{~}=0\,,

(23)

where
{\bm{H}}({\bm{r}})=H_{z}^{~}(r,z)\,{\bm{e}}_{z}^{~}+H_{r}^{~}(r,z)\,{\bm{e}}_{r}^{~}
,
but has not obtained the complete solution yet.
[
2
]

We leave the problem as a future exercise.

3
Discussions

We have calculated the magnetic field created by the line currents on the hyperboloid.
When the contact angle of the current
\phi
with respect to the
xy
-plane is
\pi/4
,
the electromagnetic force acting on each wire is strongly canceled.
The fact corresponds to the balance of the Maxwell stress in the continuum limit.

The force free nature of the straight currents arrangement on the hyperboloid
could be of use for the construction of a high-field magnet.
One of the realistic experimental structure, which approximates the hyperboloid,
is the helical coil winded on a torus, as shown in Fig. 4.
Around the doughnut hole the wire is nearly straight and tilted with
\phi=\pi/4
.
Because of the force cancellation around the hole,
the electromagnetic force imposed on the outer part increases for a certain amount,
but mechanical support can be performed from wider area.

Figure 4:
The mock-up model of the helical coil winded on the “fuzzy torus”,
which is a
Tawashi
brush for the kitchen cleaning use.

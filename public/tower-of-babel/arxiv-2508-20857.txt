Uniform error analysis of a rectangular Morley finite element method on a Shishkin mesh for a 4th-order singularly perturbed boundary value problem
Xiangyun Meng, Martin Stynes

Abstract
The singularly perturbed reaction-diffusion problem ε 2 Δ 2 u − div ( c ∇ u ) = f \varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\mathrm{div}\left(c\nabla u\right)=f is considered on the unit square Ω \Omega in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} with homogenous Dirichlet boundary conditions. Its solution typically contains boundary layers on all sides of Ω \Omega . It is discretised by a finite element method that uses rectangular Morley elements on a Shishkin mesh. In an associated energy-type norm that is natural for this problem, we prove an O ( ε 1 / 2 N − 1 + ε N − 1 ln N + N − 3 / 2 ) O(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-3/2}) rate of convergence for the error in the computed solution, where N N is the number of mesh intervals in each coordinate direction. Thus in the most troublesome regime when ε ≈ N − 1 \varepsilon\approx N^{-1} , our method is proved to attain an O ( N − 3 / 2 ) O(N^{-3/2}) rate of convergence, which is shown to be sharp by our numerical experiments and is superior to the O ( N − 1 / 2 ) O(N^{-1/2}) rate that is proved in Meng & Stynes, Adv. Comput. Math. 2019 when Adini finite elements are used to solve the same problem on the same mesh.

Introduction

Set
\Omega:=(0,1)^{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with closure
\bar{\Omega}
and boundary
\partial\Omega
.
The bending of a simply supported plate is modelled
[
10
,
15
,
26
]

by the following 4th-order singularly perturbed boundary value problem
in which
u
represents the deflection of the plate under the transverse loading
f
:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u(x,y)-\mathrm{div}\left(c(x,y)\nabla u(x,y)\right)

\displaystyle=f(x,y)\quad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,

(1.1a)

\displaystyle u(x,y)=\frac{\partial u(x,y)}{\partial n}

\displaystyle=0\quad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega,

(1.1b)

where the parameter
\varepsilon
(which is the ratio of bending rigidity to tensile stiffness of the plate) satisfies
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
, the function
c
lies in
C(\bar{\Omega})
with
c\geq c_{0}>0
for some constant
c_{0}
and
f
lies in
C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})
.
The vector
n=(n_{x},n_{y})^{T}
is the outward-pointing unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial\Omega
of
\Omega
,
so
\partial u(x,y)/\partial n
denotes the normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
.

During the past two decades, various finite element methods (FEMs) have ben proposed
for the numerical solution of 4th-order singularly perturbed problems.
Error analyses on quasi-uniform meshes using nonconforming FEMs are discussed in

[
5
,
15
,
19
,
20
,
24
,
31
,
33
]
,
and a
C^{0}
interior penalty method in
[
2
]
.
Because it is troublesome to impose global
C^{1}
continuity on the computed solution in the 2D domain
\Omega
for 4th-order problems,
the
C^{0}
interior penalty method relaxes the global
C^{1}
continuity by adding penalty terms
[
2
, eq.(2.3)]
to a globally
C^{0}
finite element space.
Similarly, the discontinuous Galerkin method adds stabilisation terms
[
9
, (3.4) and (3.13)]

to the finite element space without imposing global
C^{0}
continuity.
Compared with these two approaches, nonconforming FEMs discard the global
C^{1}
continuity condition but do not usually add any stabilisation terms.

The well-known nonconforming triangular Morley element for 4th-order problems
was constructed in
[
23
]
and appears in many textbooks
[
3
,
6
,
13
,
29
]
.
This element does not however yield convergence for 2nd-order boundary value problems
and is therefore unsuitable for our singularly perturbed problem (
1.1
);
this issue is discussed in
[
24
, Section 1]
.
Thus to solve (
1.1
), various modifications of the triangular Morley element
have been proposed
[
24
,
33
]
.

An alternative departure from the triangular Morley element is
the
rectangular
Morley element which was introduced
in
[
32
]
and
[
29
, Section 2.7.1]
. It uses the same degrees of freedom
as the triangular Morley element, viz., the point value at each node
and the integral mean of the outer normal derivative on each edge.
But unlike its triangular counterpart, the rectangular Morley element is convergent for both 4th-order and 2nd-order problems, so it is reasonable to consider it for solving (
1.1
),
as has already been pointed out in
[
31
]
.

Note also that the rectangular Morley element has only eight degrees of freedom in each element,
which compares well with the twelve degrees of freedom of the nonconforming Adini element
and the sixteen d.o.f. of the conforming Bogner-Fox-Schmit element (these two finite elements are also suitable for solving the problem (
1.1
)).
We shall use the rectangular Morley element method to solve (
1.1
).

To solve (
1.1
) effectively, one must also use an appropriate mesh.
Since the exact solution of (
1.1
) exhibits boundary layers (see Assumption
1.1
below),
the mesh should be refined near
\partial\Omega
.
Suitable meshes were used with an Adini nonconforming FEM in
[
21
]
,
a
C^{0}
interior penalty method in
[
12
]
,
a mixed FEM in
[
11
]
and a mixed
hp
-FEM in
[
7
]
.
One sees easily that the meshes most suitable for (
1.1
) are severely anisotropic,
so one cannot use results from the literature that assume quasi-uniformity of the mesh,
but mesh quasi-uniformity is assumed in many of the earlier papers that we mentioned earlier.
Consequently, for example, while Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
below
were proved previously assuming mesh quasi-uniformity, we have to prove them
on meshes without this assumption.

We shall use a tensor-product Shishkin mesh when solving (
1.1
).
In an energy-type norm that is natural for this problem,
we prove
O(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-3/2})
convergence,
where
N
is the number of mesh elements in each coordinate direction
and this convergence is uniform in the small parameter
\varepsilon
.
This result is much better than the
O(N^{-1/2})
rate that is attained
on quasi-uniform meshes
[
31
, Corollary 4.1]
.

Furthermore, comparing our Morley result with the nonconforming
Adini element studied in
[
21
]
,
in the most troublesome regime when
\varepsilon\approx N^{-1}
,
our method achieves optimal
O(N^{-3/2})
convergence,
which is better than the
O(N^{-1/2})
attained in
[
21
]
; see Remark
4.16
.

1.1
A decomposition of the solution
u

If
c\in C(\bar{\Omega})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
and
f\in C^{1}(\bar{\Omega})\subset L^{2}(\Omega)
,
then (cf.
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
) one can use the Lax-Milgram lemma to show that (
1.1
) has a unique solution in
H^{2}(\Omega)
,
but for our analysis we need more precise information about the behaviour of
u
,
especially near the boundary
\partial\Omega
.

Number the sides of
\bar{\Omega}
in clockwise order as
1,2,3,4
, where
1
is associated with the interval
[0,1]
on the
x
-axis. Then denote the corner where the sides
i
and
j
meet by
(i;j)
with
i<j
.
As in the papers
[
11
,
21
]
, we make the following assumption.

Assumption 1.1
.

The solution
u
of the boundary value problem (
1.1
) can be decomposed as

\displaystyle u(x,y)

\displaystyle=S(x,y)+\sum_{i=1}^{4}E_{i}(x,y)+E_{12}(x,y)+E_{23}(x,y)+E_{34}(x,y)+E_{14}(x,y)

\displaystyle:=S(x,y)+u^{E}(x,y)

for
(x,y)\in\bar{\Omega}
,
where
S(x,y)
is a smooth function,
each
E_{i}(x,y)
is a boundary layer component associated with the side
i
of
\bar{\Omega}
,
and each
E_{ij}(x,y)
is a corner layer component associated with the corner
(i;j)
, i.e.,
there exists a constant
C
such that for all
(x,y)\in\overline{\Omega}
and
0\leq i+j\leq 4
one has

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{i+j}S(x,y)}{\partial x^{i}\partial y^{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C,

(1.2a)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{i+j}E_{1}(x,y)}{\partial x^{i}\partial y^{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1-j}e^{-y/\varepsilon},

(1.2b)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\partial^{i+j}E_{12}(x,y)}{\partial x^{i}\partial y^{j}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{1-i-j}e^{-x/\varepsilon}e^{-y/\varepsilon},

(1.2c)

with analogous bounds for the remaining components of the decomposition.

2
The rectangular Morley element on anisotropic meshes

For the moment we use an arbitrary tensor product mesh on
\Omega
.
In Section
4
we shall specialise our analysis to a suitable Shishkin mesh.

Let
N
be a positive integer.
Let
0=x_{0}<x_{1}\dots<x_{N}=1
and
0=y_{0}<y_{1}\dots<y_{N}=1
be arbitrary meshes
in the interval
[0,1]
on the
x
and
y
axes respectively.
Draw vertical and horizontal lines through these points to partition
\bar{\Omega}
into mesh rectangular elements with
vertices
\{(x_{i},y_{j}):0\leq i,j\leq N\}
. We write
\mathcal{T}_{N}
for the union of these mesh rectangles, so
\mathcal{T}_{N}
is a partition of
\bar{\Omega}
.

Let
K
be a typical mesh rectangle as shown in Figure
1
.
Its vertices are
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3},a_{4}
and its edges are
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}
with unit normal vectors
n_{e_{j}}
for
j=1,2,3,4
.

Figure 1:
degrees of freedom on element
K

Let
(x_{c},y_{c})
be the center of
K
and suppose
K
has dimensions
2h_{x}\times 2h_{y}
.
Then the coordinates of the vertices
a_{j}
are
a_{1}=(x_{c}-h_{x},y_{c}-h_{y})
,
a_{2}=(x_{c}-h_{x},y_{c}+h_{y})
,

a_{3}=(x_{c}+h_{x},y_{c}+h_{y})
,
a_{4}=(x_{c}+h_{x},y_{c}-h_{y})
.
For
j=1,2,3,4
, let
|e_{j}|
denote the length of edge
e_{j}
and write
n_{e_{j}}
for the outward-pointing unit normal to
e_{j}
.

We now describe the rectangular Morley element of
[
28
,
29
,
32
]
.
For each mesh rectangle
K
, let
P(K)
denote the span of
\{1,x,y,x^{2},xy,y^{2},x^{3},y^{3}\}
.

Define a set of 8 functions in
P(K)
as follows:

\displaystyle p_{1}(x,y)=\frac{1}{8}\left[2\left(1-\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)\left(1-\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)+\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)^{3}-\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}+\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)^{3}-\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right],

\displaystyle p_{2}(x,y)=\frac{1}{8}\left[2\left(1-\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)\left(1+\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)+\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)^{3}-\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}-\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)^{3}+\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right],

\displaystyle p_{3}(x,y)=\frac{1}{8}\left[2\left(1+\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)\left(1+\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)-\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)^{3}+\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}-\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)^{3}+\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right],

\displaystyle p_{4}(x,y)=\frac{1}{8}\left[2\left(1+\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)\left(1-\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)-\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}\right)^{3}+\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}+\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right)^{3}-\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}\right],

\displaystyle q_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{h_{y}}{4}\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}+1\right)\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}-1\right)^{2},\ \ q_{2}(x,y)=-\frac{h_{x}}{4}\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}+1\right)\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}-1\right)^{2},

\displaystyle q_{3}(x,y)=\frac{h_{y}}{4}\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}+1\right)^{2}\left(\frac{y-y_{c}}{h_{y}}-1\right),\ \ q_{4}(x,y)=\frac{h_{x}}{4}\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}+1\right)^{2}\left(\frac{x-x_{c}}{h_{x}}-1\right).

One can verify (see
[
22
, eq.(2.5)]
) that

\begin{cases}p_{i}(a_{j})=\delta_{ij}\ \text{ and }\ \frac{1}{|e_{j}|}\int_{e_{j}}\frac{\partial p_{i}}{\partial{n}_{e_{j}}}\mathrm{d}s=0&\text{ for }i,j=1,2,3,4,\\
q_{i}(a_{j})=0\ \text{ and }\ \frac{1}{|e_{j}|}\int_{e_{j}}\frac{\partial q_{i}}{\partial{n}_{e_{j}}}\mathrm{d}s=\delta_{ij}&\text{for }i,j=1,2,3,4,\end{cases}

(2.1)

where
\delta_{ij}
is the Kronecker symbol.
Hence the
p_{i}
and
q_{j}
are linearly independent and form a basis for
P(K)
.

The
rectangular Morley element space
V_{N}
is now defined to be the set of functions
v\in L^{2}(\Omega)

such that
v|_{K}\in P(K)
for each mesh rectangle
K
,

v
is continuous at all mesh vertices in
\Omega
as one moves from one mesh rectangle to another,

v
vanishes at all vertices in
\partial\Omega
,
and for all
K
one has
\int_{e}\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}\right]\,\mathrm{d}s=0
for all edges
e
of
K

where
n_{e}
is the unit normal to
e
that points out of
K
;
here
\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}\right]
denotes the jump in
\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}
as one moves
from one side of
e
to the other side (i.e., from one mesh rectangle to another) if
e\not\subset\partial\Omega
,
while
\left[\frac{\partial v}{\partial n_{e}}\right]=\frac{\partial v}{\partial n}
if
e\subset\partial\Omega
.

3
The discrete problem and its error analysis

In this section we discretise (
1.1
) using a finite element method based on the rectangular Morley element
and begin the error analysis of this method.

3.1
Discretisation of (
1.1
)

Any function
v_{N}\in V_{N}
may be only piecewise continuous on
\Omega
so one cannot apply
differential operators (such as
\nabla
and
\Delta
) to
v_{N}
globally. Thus, we apply these operators
only piecewise on the interiors of the elements
K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
and indicate this modification by writing

\nabla_{N}
and
\Delta_{N}
instead of
\nabla
and
\Delta
, e.g.,
(\nabla_{N}v_{N})|_{K}=\nabla(v_{N}|_{K})
.
For
v_{N},w_{N}\in V_{N}
, define the bilinear forms

b_{N}(v_{N},w_{N}):=(c\nabla_{N}v_{N},\nabla_{N}w_{N})
and
a_{N}(v_{N},w_{N}):=(\Delta_{N}v_{N},\Delta_{N}w_{N})
.
We shall also use expressions like
b_{N}(u,w_{N})
and
a_{N}(u,w_{N})
, which are defined in the same way.

One can write (
1.1
) in the equivalent weak form

\varepsilon^{2}(\Delta u,\Delta v)+\left(c\nabla u,\nabla v\right)=(f,v)\ \text{ for all }v\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega),

where as usual
H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)=\big\{g\in H^{2}(\Omega):g|_{\partial\Omega}=\frac{\partial g}{\partial n}|_{\partial\Omega}=0\big\}
.

Then our discretisation of (
1.1
) is: Find
u_{N}\in V_{N}
such that

\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(u_{N},v_{N})+b_{N}(u_{N},v_{N})=(f,v_{N})_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\;\quad\forall v_{N}\in V_{N}.

(3.1)

Existence and uniqueness of the solution
u_{N}
of (
3.1
) follows readily from the Lax-Milgram lemma.

3.2
Preliminaries

For each
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{N}
,
let
P_{0}(K)
be the space of constant functions defined on
K
and
let
P_{K}^{0}:L^{2}(K)\rightarrow P_{0}(K)
be the
L^{2}
projector, viz.,

(v,w_{N})_{K}=(P_{K}^{0}v,w_{N})_{K}\quad\forall v\in L^{2}(K),\,\forall w_{N}\in P_{0}(K).

From
[
14
, Lemma 7.5]
, for all
v\in H^{1}(K)
one has the anisotropic error bound

\displaystyle\left\|v-P_{K}^{0}v\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq C\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right),

(3.2)

with
C
independent of
v
and
K
.

Let
Q_{1}(K)
denote the usual space of polynomials of the form
c_{0}+c_{1}x+c_{2}y+c_{3}xy
defined on
K
.
Set
Q_{N}=\big\{v_{N}\in H^{1}(\Omega):v_{N}|_{K}\in Q_{1}(K)\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}\big\}
.
Let
I_{N}:C(K)\rightarrow Q_{N}
denote the standard piecewise bilinear nodal interpolation operator,
viz., given

, for each vertex

I_{N}v(P)=v(P)
.
Then for each

v\in H^{1}(K)\cap C(K)

[

one has

\displaystyle\left\|v-I_{N}v\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq C\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial v}{\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)

(3.3)

and

\displaystyle\left\|I_{N}v\right\|_{H^{1}(K)}\leq C\left\|v\right\|_{H^{1}(K)}.

(3.4)

By applying a standard scaling argument to the

and

variables separately,
one can modify the proof of
[
3
, Lemma 4.5.3]

to obtain the following anisotropic inverse inequalities:
for each
K\in{\mathcal{T}}_{N}
and any
\xi_{N}\in P(K)
, one has

\displaystyle h_{x}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{y}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)},

(3.5a)

\displaystyle h_{y}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{x}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)},

.

3.3
The error equation

Let
v_{N}\in V_{N}
be arbitrary. Set
\xi_{N}=u_{N}-v_{N}
and
\eta_{N}=u-v_{N}
.
Then (
3.1
) and (

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(\xi_{N},\xi_{N})

\displaystyle+b_{N}(\xi_{N},\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})+\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(u_{N}-u,\xi_{N})+b_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})+b_{N}(u_{N}-u,\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})+b_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})+(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u-\mathrm{div}(c\nabla u),I_{N}\xi_{N})

\displaystyle\qquad+(f,\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})-\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(u,\xi_{N})-b_{N}(u,\xi_{N}).

Integrating by parts and using
I_{N}\xi_{N}=0
on
\partial\Omega
,

[

(\Delta^{2}u,I_{N}\xi_{N})=-(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla(I_{N}\xi_{N}))\text{ and }-(\mathrm{div}(c\nabla u),I_{N}\xi_{N})-b_{N}(u,\xi_{N})=b_{N}(u,I_{N}\xi_{N}-\xi_{N}).

Integration by parts on each

\displaystyle a_{N}(u,\xi_{N})=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}(\Delta u,\Delta\xi_{N})_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\quad=-\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}(\nabla\Delta u,\nabla\xi_{N})_{L^{2}(K)}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s,

where
\partial/\partial s
and
\partial/\partial n

\partial K

From these calculcations we get the
error equation

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(\xi_{N},\xi_{N})

\displaystyle+b_{N}(\xi_{N},\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{2}a_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})+b_{N}(\eta_{N},\xi_{N})-b_{N}(u,\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})+(f,\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})

\displaystyle\qquad-\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}(\nabla\Delta u)\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\;\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y.

(3.6)

]
the mesh is shape-regular and the bounds established in
Theorems 4.1 and 4.2 blow up if one takes the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
,
while the bound of
[
31
, Theorem 4.3]
(which remains valid as
\varepsilon\to 0
)
is only
O(N^{-1/2})
, which is inferior to the
O(N^{-3/2})
bound
that we shall prove in Theorem
4.14
.

3.4
Bounds on error equation integrals along
\partial K

In this subsection we bound the terms

\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s

and

\varepsilon^{2}\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s

from the error equation (
3.6
).

For all
v_{N}\in V_{N}
, define
|v_{N}|_{1,N}^{2}:=(\nabla_{N}v_{N},\nabla_{N}v_{N}),\,|v_{N}|_{2,N}^{2}:=(\Delta_{N}v_{N},\Delta_{N}v_{N})

and the norm
\|v_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}^{2}:=\varepsilon^{2}|v_{N}|_{2,N}^{2}+|v_{N}|_{1,N}^{2}
.
One can define
|u|_{1,N},|u|_{2,N}
and
\|u\|_{\varepsilon,N}
in the same way.

Let the edges of each
K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
be labelled
e_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,4
as in Figure
1
.
For each
w\in L^{2}(e_{i})
define

\Pi^{0}_{e_{i}}w=\frac{1}{|e_{i}|}\int_{e_{i}}w\,\mathrm{d}s\ \text{ and }\ \mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{i}}w=w-\Pi^{0}_{e_{i}}w.

The argument used in the next lemma imitates in part the proof of
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
,
where only a uniform mesh was considered.

Lemma 3.2
.

Assume that
w\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{2,N}

(3.7)

and

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}.

(3.8)

Proof.

For any internal edge
e_{i}
, the edge-jump condition in the definition of
V_{N}

implies that
\Pi^{0}_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}
has the same magnitude but different signs
when calculated on the two adjacent mesh rectangles
K
that share
e_{i}
.
Consequently
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\int_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\left(\Pi^{0}_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\right)\;\mathrm{d}s=0
,
as in these integrals
\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}
depends on
e_{i}
on each internal edge but not on the associated
K
,
while if
e_{i}\subset\partial\Omega
then
\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}=0
.
Hence

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\int_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\int_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\right)\;\mathrm{d}s=:\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i},\ \text{say.}

(3.9)

We now imitate the proof of
[
27
, Theorem 4.1]
by considering
|J_{2}+J_{4}|

and also borrow some techniques from
[
18
, Lemma 3.2]
.
For any
K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
, from Figure
1
one sees that

\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}=-\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}
on
e_{2}
,

\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}=\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}
on
e_{4}

and
\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}=\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}
on
e_{2}
and
e_{4}
.
Thus

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s+\int_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\;\mathrm{d}y+\int_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\;\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y.

(3.10)

As
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}=\text{span}\{1,x,y,x^{2},xy,y^{2},x^{3},y^{3}\}
,
it is easy to see that

\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)=\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)\ \text{ for }y_{c}-h_{y}\leq y\leq y_{c}+h_{y},

and the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}
implies that

\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y=0.

Hence

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

A Cauchy-Schwarz inequality now yields

\displaystyle(J_{2}+J_{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)^{2}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(3.11)

In the second integral here, one has

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}(x_{c}+h_{x},t)\right]\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{t=y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{s=t}^{y}\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial s}(x_{c}+h_{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t,

so another Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\left[\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4h_{y}^{2}}\left\{\int_{t=y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{s=t}^{y}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial s}(x_{c}+h_{x},s)\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}\left\{\int_{t=y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{s=t}^{y}1\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{h_{y}}{h_{x}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2},

where we used the property that
\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial s}
is independent of
x
,
which follows quickly from the definition of the Morley shape function space
P(K)
in Section
2
.
Hence

\displaystyle\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right)^{2}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{2h_{y}^{2}}{h_{x}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

(3.12)

For the first integrand in (
3.11
), one has

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)

\displaystyle-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}+h_{x},t)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)+\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}(x_{c}-h_{x},t)\;\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{t}^{y}\frac{\partial^{3}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial s}(x_{c}+h_{x},s)\;\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{t}^{y}\frac{\partial^{3}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial s}(x_{c}-h_{x},s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2h_{y}}\int_{x_{c}-h_{x}}^{x_{c}+h_{x}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{t}^{y}\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial s}(x,s)\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x.

Again appealing to Cauchy-Schwarz, we get

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4h_{y}^{2}}\left[\int_{x_{c}-h_{x}}^{x_{c}+h_{x}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{t}^{y}\left(\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial s}(x,s)\right)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\right]\left[\int_{x_{c}-h_{x}}^{x_{c}+h_{x}}\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\int_{t}^{y}1\,\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}x\right]

\displaystyle\leq 4h_{x}h_{y}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2};

hence

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial x^{2}}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 8h_{x}h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

(3.13)

Substituting (
3.12
) and (
3.13
) into (
3.11
) then taking a square root yields

\left|J_{2}+J_{4}\right|\leq 4h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

(3.14)

One can show similarly that

\left|J_{1}+J_{3}\right|\leq 4h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

(3.15)

Combining (
3.9
), (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{1}+J_{3}|+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{2}+J_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq 4\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla^{2}\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{2,N}.

Thus, we have proved (
3.7
).

Applying the anisotropic inverse inequalities
(
3.5a
) to (
3.14
) and (
3.5b
) to (
3.15
) gives

\displaystyle\left|J_{2}+J_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{y}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)},

\displaystyle\left|J_{1}+J_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{x}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

Then, like the above derivation of (
3.7
), one obtains (
3.8
):

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{1}+J_{3}|+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{2}+J_{4}|

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt\leq C\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

At first sight (
3.7
), which bounds
\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\;\mathrm{d}s
with factors
h_{x}^{2}
and
h_{y}^{2}
,
looks more attractive than (
3.8
), which yields factors
h_{x}
and
h_{y}
.
But to continue the analysis with (
3.7
) means working with
|\xi_{N}|_{2,N}

and using the inequality
|\xi_{N}|_{2,N}\leq\varepsilon^{-1}\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}
, which introduces
an undesirable factor
\varepsilon^{-1}
into our error bounds.
Consequently we shall use (
3.8
) in our subsequent analysis, for then we have available

|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}\leq\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}
, which causes no difficulties.

The next result was proved in
[
18
, Lemma 3.3]
while assuming that
the mesh was quasi-uniform, but here we remove this restriction.

Lemma 3.4
.

Assume that
w\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{2,N}

(3.16)

and

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|

\displaystyle\quad\leq C\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}.

(3.17)

Proof.

The definition of
V_{N}
implies that on each edge
e_{i}
that is shared by two mesh rectangles

K_{1}
and
K_{2}
(say),
\xi_{N}|_{e_{i}}
is the same in both
K_{1}
and
K_{2}
;
but on
e_{i}
the derivative
\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}

will have opposite signs in these two rectangles, so

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\int_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}\int_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{i}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s=:\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{4}J_{i}.

(3.18)

Similarly to the derivation of (
3.10
), in the notation of Figure
1
we have

J_{2}+J_{4}=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\left(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\;\mathrm{d}y.

As
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}=\text{span}\{1,x,y,x^{2},xy,y^{2},x^{3},y^{3}\}
,
it is easy to see that

\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)=\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)\ \text{ for }y_{c}-h_{y}\leq y\leq y_{c}+h_{y},

and the definition of
\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}
implies that

\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\;\mathrm{d}y=0
.
Hence

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

By a Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we get

\displaystyle(J_{2}+J_{4})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\{\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)^{2}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

(3.19)

Replacing
\partial^{2}w/\partial x^{2}
by
\partial^{2}w/\partial y\partial x

in the derivation of (
3.13
) yields

\displaystyle\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{4}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}+h_{x},y)-\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial y\partial x}\right)(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\right]^{2}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq 8h_{x}h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2},

while similarly to the derivation of (
3.12
), one has

\int_{y_{c}-h_{y}}^{y_{c}+h_{y}}\left(\mathcal{R}^{0}_{e_{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right)^{2}(x_{c}-h_{x},y)\,\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{2h_{y}^{2}}{h_{x}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}.

Hence (
3.19
) implies that

\left|J_{2}+J_{4}\right|\leq 4h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

(3.20)

One can show similarly that

\left|J_{1}+J_{3}\right|\leq 4h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}\xi_{N}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

(3.21)

From (
3.18
), (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) and some Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities, we can derive

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}w}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{1}+J_{3}|+\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}|J_{2}+J_{4}|

\displaystyle\leq 4\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla^{2}\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{2,N},

which proves (
3.16
).

Invoking the anisotropic inverse inequalities (
3.5a
)–(
3.5b
),
from (
3.20
) and (
3.21
) one has

\displaystyle\left|J_{2}+J_{4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{y}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)},

\displaystyle\left|J_{1}+J_{3}\right|

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{x}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}w}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial x}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

Then one obtains (
3.17
) by imitating the above derivation of (
3.16
).
∎

Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
are anisotropic generalisations
on nonuniform meshes of the consistency error bounds
proved in
[
17
,
18
]
for discretisations of
a classical biharmonic problem
(
\varepsilon=1
and
c\equiv 0
in (
1.1
)) on uniform meshes.

4
Error analysis on a Shishkin mesh

All our analysis so far is valid on the general tensor-product mesh defined at the start of Section
2
, but to obtain good accuracy in our numerical solution of (
1.1
)
one should use a mesh that is designed to handle boundary layers in singularly perturbed problems.
We now restrict our attention to a well-known tensor-product mesh of this type: the Shishkin mesh.

4.1
The Shishkin mesh

Recall that
N
is the number of mesh intervals in each coordinate direction.
Assume from now on that
N
is divisible by
4
. Define the transition parameter

\lambda=\min\left\{\varepsilon\ln N,\,\frac{1}{4}\right\}.

We assume that
\lambda=\varepsilon\ln N
as otherwise
N
would be exponentially large compared with
1/\varepsilon
,
and then the problem can be solved accurately using a uniform mesh.
In practice
\varepsilon
is small, so it is extremely unlikely that
\varepsilon\ln N>1/4
.

Partition the interval
[0,1]
by a piecewise uniform mesh that is obtained by
dividing
[0,\lambda]
into
N/4
equal subintervals,

[\lambda,1-\lambda]
into
N/2
equal subintervals and
[1-\lambda,1]
into
N/4
equal subintervals.
The tensor product of two such 1D meshes is our 2D Shishkin mesh for the problem (
1.1
);
it is shown in Figure
2
.
(See
[
25
,
30
]
for further discussion of Shishkin meshes.)
We continue to write
\mathcal{T}_{N}
for the 2D mesh.
For any mesh sub-domain
\tilde{\Omega}
of
\Omega
,
denote the mesh on
\tilde{\Omega}
by
\mathcal{T}_{N}(\tilde{\Omega})
.

Figure 2:
A rectangular Shishkin mesh with
N=8

In our error analysis, we use the Shishkin mesh subdomains
\Omega_{1}
,…,
\Omega_{9}

that are displayed in Figure
3
.
For convenience, define
\widehat{\Omega}_{1}:=\Omega_{1}\cup\Omega_{4}\cup\Omega_{7}
,

\widehat{\Omega}_{2}:=\Omega_{2}\cup\Omega_{5}\cup\Omega_{8}
and

\widehat{\Omega}_{3}:=\Omega_{3}\cup\Omega_{6}\cup\Omega_{9}
.

Figure 3:
Mesh subdomains in
\Omega

We shall now derive an error bound for the numerical solution
u_{N}
of (
3.1
)
by specialising our earlier results to the Shishkin mesh.
To do this we analyse the consistency error of the discretisation of each term in the PDE (
1.1a
) in several lemmas,
then combine these bounds in the proof of Theorem
4.14
to give the final convergence result.

4.2
Consistency error of
a_{N}(\cdot,\cdot)

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\varepsilon^{2}\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right)\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

Proof.

We apply the bound (
3.8
) of Lemma
3.2
to each of the components of
u

that are provided by Assumption
1.1
.

For the smooth component
S
, the bound (
1.2a
) and
h_{x}\leq 2N^{-1}
yield

\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}S}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{2}N^{-1}.

(4.1)

For the layer component
E_{1}
, using (
1.2b
) on
\widehat{\Omega}_{2}
we get

\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}(\widehat{\Omega}_{2})}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{1}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\leq CN^{-2}\varepsilon^{-4}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\varepsilon\ln N}^{1-\varepsilon\ln N}e^{-2y/\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\leq CN^{-2}\varepsilon^{-4}\varepsilon\leq C\varepsilon^{-3}N^{-2},

and on
\widehat{\Omega}_{1}\cup\widehat{\Omega}_{3}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}(\widehat{\Omega}_{1}\cup\widehat{\Omega}_{3})}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{1}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{2}\varepsilon^{-4}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\ln N}+\int_{1-\varepsilon\ln N}^{1}\right)e^{-2y/\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{2}\varepsilon^{-4}\varepsilon(\varepsilon\ln N)=CN^{-2}(\ln N)^{3}.

Combining this pair of estimates and recalling that
\varepsilon\ln N\leq 1/4
, we obtain

\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{1}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}.

(4.2)

The estimates for
E_{2}
,
E_{3}
and
E_{4}
are similar.

For the layer component
E_{12}
, using (
1.2c
) on
\widehat{\Omega}_{2}
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}(\widehat{\Omega}_{2})}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{12}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2}\varepsilon^{-6}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\varepsilon\ln N}^{1-\varepsilon\ln N}e^{-2x/\varepsilon}e^{-2y/\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-4}N^{-4},

and on
\widehat{\Omega}_{1}\cup\widehat{\Omega}_{3}

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}(\widehat{\Omega}_{1}\cup\widehat{\Omega}_{3})}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{12}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N)^{2}\varepsilon^{-6}\int_{0}^{1}\left(\int_{0}^{\varepsilon\ln N}+\int_{1-\varepsilon\ln N}^{1}\right)e^{-2x/\varepsilon}e^{-2y/\varepsilon}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{2}.

Thus,

\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}E_{12}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}).

(4.3)

The estimates for
E_{23}
,
E_{34}
and
E_{41}
are similar.

Adding (
4.1
), (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we get

\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}).

(4.4)

Similarly,

\varepsilon^{2}\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right).

Now the bound (
3.7
) of Lemma
3.2
and
|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}\leq\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}
yield

\varepsilon^{2}\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial n^{2}}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial n}\,\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right)\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{\partial K}\frac{\partial^{2}u}{\partial s\partial n}\frac{\partial\xi_{N}}{\partial s}\;\mathrm{d}s\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right)\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

Proof.

Imitate the proof of Lemma
4.1
but invoke Lemma
3.4
instead of Lemma
3.2
.
To be specific, Lemma
3.4
gives bounds on the smooth and corner layer components
that are the same as (
4.1
) and (
4.3
),
while for the boundary layer components, the bound
C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}
of (
4.2
)
is replaced by the smaller bound
C\varepsilon^{3/2}N^{-1}
.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

[Comparison with Adini element]
For the highest-order term
\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}u(x,y)
of (
1.1a
),
the consistency error of the rectangular Morley element on the Shishkin mesh
is better than the corresponding bound for the rectangular Adini element, which was discussed in
[
21
]
.
For in
[
21
, eq.(4.27)]
, the consistency error of the Adini element is bounded by

\displaystyle\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{4}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{2,N},

or

\displaystyle\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]|\xi_{N}|_{1,N}

after using an inverse inequality.
In the above bounds, terms such as
h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

or
h_{x}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}
are troublesome because of the mismatch
where derivatives in one variable are multiplied by mesh widths in the other variable—this is problematic for example
on mesh rectangles
K\subset\Omega_{2}
where
|\partial^{4}u/\partial y^{4}|=O(\varepsilon^{-3})
by (
1.2b
)
but
h_{x}=O(N^{-1})
, leading to bounds involving negative powers of
\varepsilon
.
Remarkably, the rectangular Morley element does not have this drawback;
the estimates of Lemmas
3.2
and
3.4
do not have similar mismatches
and consequently we are able to prove the error bounds of Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2

in which no negative powers of
\varepsilon
appear.

Recall the piecewise bilinear interpolation operator
I_{N}
of Section
3.1

and the piecewise constant projector
P^{0}_{K}
of Section
3.2
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Suppose that
u\in H_{0}^{2}(\Omega)\cap H^{4}(\Omega)
satisfies Assumption
1.1
.
Then there exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}\nabla\Delta u\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right)\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

Proof.

Let
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
and
K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}
.
Similarly to the proof of
[
17
, Lemma 3.1]
, one sees that

\int_{K}\frac{\partial(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})}{\partial x}\;\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=\int_{K}\frac{\partial(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})}{\partial y}\;\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y=0

by verifying this property for each of the functions
1,x,y,x^{2},xy,y^{2},x^{3},y^{3}
.
Hence

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}\nabla\Delta u\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}\left(\nabla\Delta u-P_{K}^{0}\nabla\Delta u\right)\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\hskip-71.13188pt\leq C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|(\nabla\Delta u-P_{K}^{0}\nabla\Delta u)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where we used (
3.4
) and Cauchy-Schwarz inequalities.
But the anisotropic projection error bound (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\hskip-8.53581pt\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|(\nabla\Delta u-P_{K}^{0}\nabla\Delta u)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\bigg(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{2}\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial y^{4}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigg).

Here the worst-behaved terms are
h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}
and
h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u}{\partial x^{3}\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}
.
These terms can be bounded like (
4.4
), obtaining

\varepsilon^{2}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|(\nabla\Delta u-P_{K}^{0}\nabla\Delta u)\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right).

The lemma now follows since
\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}
.
∎

4.3
Consistency error of
b_{N}(\cdot,\cdot)

In this subsection we consider the consistency error of the lower-order term in the PDE (
1.1a
).
The components
u^{E}
and
S
of
u
that were defined in Assumption
1.1

are handled separately.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists a constant
C
such that for all
\xi_{N}\in V_{N}
, one has

\left|b_{N}\left(u^{E},\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N}\right)\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right)\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

Proof.

Recall that
u^{E}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{4}E_{i}(x,y)+E_{12}(x,y)+E_{23}(x,y)+E_{34}(x,y)+E_{14}(x,y)
. Similarly to the proof of Lemma
4.4
, one has

\displaystyle\left|b_{N}(u^{E},\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}c\nabla u^{E}\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}P_{K}^{0}c\left(\nabla u^{E}-P_{K}^{0}\nabla u^{E}\right)\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\qquad+\left|\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}\left(c-P_{K}^{0}c\right)\nabla u^{E}\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y\right|

\displaystyle\hskip-79.66771pt\leq C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|\left(\nabla u^{E}-P_{K}^{0}\nabla u^{E}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip-56.9055pt+C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|\left(\nabla c-P_{K}^{0}\nabla c\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip-79.66771pt\leq C\left[\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|\left(\nabla u^{E}-P_{K}^{0}\nabla u^{E}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}+\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|\left(\nabla c-P_{K}^{0}\nabla c\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\right]\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N},

as
\left|\nabla u^{E}\right|\leq C
on
\Omega
by Assumption
1.1

and
\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla\xi_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leq\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}
.
These two terms have a similar structure, but

c
is well behaved so we give a detailed analysis only for the term containing
u^{E}
.

The anisotropic projection error estimate (
3.2
) yields

\displaystyle\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left\|\left(\nabla u^{E}-P_{K}^{0}\nabla u^{E}\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\left(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right).

The boundary layer and corner layer components of
u^{E}

are handled as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
.
The worst-behaved terms are

h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}

and
h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}
,
whereas in Lemma
4.1
one has

h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{4}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y^{3}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}
; that is,
here there are two fewer orders of derivative but this is balanced by the additional factor

\varepsilon^{2}
that is present in Lemma
4.1
.
Thus, like (
4.2
), we get finally

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\bigg(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{1}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{1}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{1}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{1}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigg)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1},

and like (
4.3
) we get

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\bigg(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{12}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{12}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{12}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}E_{12}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigg)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C(\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}).

These estimates yield

\displaystyle\left[\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\bigg(h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{x}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial x\partial y}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}+h_{y}^{2}\left\|\frac{\partial^{2}u^{E}}{\partial y^{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\bigg)\right]^{1/2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right).

(4.5)

Combining these bounds, we are done.
∎

It now remains only to bound the smooth component term
\left|b_{N}(S,\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\right|
,
but obtaining a sharp estimate for this term turns out to be troublesome.

For the rectangular Shishkin mesh
\mathcal{T}_{N}

Lemma 4.6

Proof.

Recall that

is the center of

To simplify the presentation, set

Lemma 4.8

\displaystyle b_{N}(S,\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}c\nabla S\,\nabla(\xi_{N}-I_{N}\xi_{N})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\int_{K}c\nabla S\,\nabla(\xi_{N}^{\mathcal{X}}-I_{N}\xi_{N}^{\mathcal{X}})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\sum_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{N}}\|\nabla_{N}\eta_{N}\|_{L^{2}(K)}^{2}\right)^{1/2}\|\xi_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}.

∎

It is well known that in the analysis of singularly perturbed problems on layer-adapted meshes
such as the Shishkin mesh, the most troublesome regime is when
\varepsilon\approx N^{-1}
.
For this regime, Theorem
4.14
gives the following result.

Corollary 4.15
.

Let
u
be the solution of (
1.1
) and
u_{N}
the solution of (
3.1
).
Let Assumption
1.1
be satisfied.
If
\varepsilon\approx N^{-1}
, then there exists a constant
C
(which is independent of
\varepsilon
and
N
) such that

\left\|u-u_{N}\right\|_{\varepsilon,N}\leq CN^{-3/2}.

Remark 4.16

(Adini versus Morley)
.

The Adini element is a well-known example of a nonconforming finite element
that is suitable for our singularly perturbed problem (
1.1
).
For the Adini element on an appropriate Shishkin mesh,
one has the error bound
[
21
, Corollary 4.1]

\|u-u_{N}\|_{\varepsilon,N}\leq\begin{cases}C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}+N^{-3}\right)&\text{if }\varepsilon\leq N^{-1},\\
C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}(N^{-1}\ln N)^{2}+\varepsilon^{-3/2}N^{-2}\right)&\text{if }\varepsilon>N^{-1}.\end{cases}

Thus in the most challenging regime when
\varepsilon\approx N^{-1}
,
the Adini element obtains
O(N^{-1/2})
convergence,
but Corollary
4.15
shows that the rectangular Morley element attains
O(N^{-3/2})
convergence.

Remark 4.17
.

An inspection of the entire analysis leading to the result of Theorem
4.14

shows that we are close to proving the sharper error bound

\left\|u-u_{N}\right\|_{\varepsilon,N}\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{1/2}N^{-1}+\varepsilon N^{-1}\ln N+N^{-2}\right),

(4.30)

which would agree with our numerical results in Section
5
.
The weaker term
N^{-3/2}
in Theorem
4.14
appears only because of the
two inequalities (
4.14
) and (
4.18
)—all other steps
of the analysis yield terms in (
4.30
). Unfortunately we are unable at present to improve
(
4.14
) and (
4.18
).

∗ \ast - η \eta -Ricci solitons on weak Kenmotsu f f -manifolds
Vladimir Rovenski

Abstract
Recent interest among geometers in f f -structures of K. Yano is due to the study of topology and dynamics of contact foliations and generalized A. Weinstein conjectures. Weak metric f f -structures, introduced by the author and R. Wolak as a generalization of Hermitian structure, as well as f f -structure allow for a fresh perspective on the classical theory. An important case of such manifolds, which is locally a twisted product, is a weak β ​ f \beta f -Kenmotsu manifold defined as a generalization of K. Kenmotsu’s concept. In this paper, the concept of the ∗ \ast -Ricci tensor of S. Tashibana is adapted to weak metric f f -manifolds, the interaction of ∗ \ast - η \eta -Ricci soliton with the weak β ​ f \beta f -Kenmotsu structure is studied and new characteristics of η \eta -Einstein metrics are obtained. \displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}QX+2\,sn\beta^{2}QX+2\,n\{\bar{\eta}(X)\bar{\xi}-s\sum\nolimits_{j}\eta^{j}(X)\xi_{j}\}=-(1/2)\{g(X,V)-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(X)\eta^{j}(V)\}\bar{\xi}.

The
{\cal D}
-component of the previous equation is

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}QX+2\,sn\beta^{2}QX=0.

(64)

Since
{\cal D}
is invariant for the non-singular operator
Q
, from (
64
) we get
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}X=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}X
for all
X\in{\cal D}
.
From this and (
20
)
we get a contradiction:
r=-2\,s\,n(2\,n+1)\beta^{2}
on
{\cal V}
.
∎

Definition 6
.

A vector field
X
on a weak metric
f
-manifold
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
is called a
contact vector field

if there exists a function
\sigma\in C^{\infty}(M)
such that

\displaystyle\pounds_{X}\,\eta^{i}=\sigma\,\eta^{i}\quad(1\leq i\leq s).

(65)

and if
\rho=0
, i.e., the flow of
X
preserves
\eta^{i}
, then
X
is called a
strictly contact vector field
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
be a weak
\beta f
-KM.
Suppose that
(g,V)
represents a
\ast
-
\eta
-RS (
32
), whose potential vector field

is strictly contact
and
(M,g)
is an
\eta
-Einstein manifold (
11
) with
a=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}
and

of constant scalar curvature

.

Proof.

Taking the Lie derivative of
\eta^{i}(X)=g(X,\xi_{i})
along
V

and using (
5
) and (
65
), we obtain

\pounds_{V}\,\xi_{i}=(\sigma-2(\lambda+\mu))\,\xi_{i}
.
From this and (
50
) we get
\sigma=\lambda+\mu
, and by Proposition
5
,
V
is strict.
Moreover,
\pounds_{V}\,\xi_{i}=0
. Also, (
65
) yields
\pounds_{V}\,\eta^{i}=(\lambda+\mu)\,\eta^{i}=0
.
Recall the formula, e.g.,
[
34
, Eq. (5.13)]
):

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,Y)=\pounds_{V}(\nabla_{X}Y)-\nabla_{X}\,(\pounds_{V}Y)-\nabla_{\pounds_{V}X}Y.

(66)

Setting
Y=\xi_{i}
in (
66
) and using (
14
), we find

\displaystyle(\pounds_{V}\nabla)(X,\xi_{i})=-\nabla_{X}(\pounds_{V}\,\xi_{i})-\beta\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\{(\pounds_{V}\,\eta^{j})(X)\xi_{j}+\eta^{j}(X)\pounds_{V}\,\xi_{j}\}=0.

(67)

Using (
67
) in (
42
) yields

\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}QX=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}QX\ (X\in{\cal D})
.
Since
{\cal D}
is invariant for the non-singular operator
Q
, we get
\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}X=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}X

.
Hence
(M,g)
is an
\eta
-Einstein manifold with
a=-2s\,n\beta^{2}
,

, see (
11
) and (

),
of scalar curvature

{r}=-2\,s\,n(2\,n+1)\beta^{2}
.
∎

Theorem 7
.

Let
(g,V)
represents a
\ast
-
\eta
-RS (
32
) on a weak
\beta f
-KM
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
.
If

V
is orthogonal to
{\cal D}
, i.e.,
V=\sum_{\,i}\delta^{\,i}\xi_{i}\neq 0
for some smooth functions
\delta^{\,i}
on
M
,
then
X(\delta^{\,i})=0
for all
i
and
X\in{\cal D}
; moreover,
(M,g)
is an
\eta
-Einstein manifold (
11
)
with
a=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}
and
b=2(s-1)n\beta^{2}
of constant scalar curvature
r=-2\,s\,n(2\,n+1)\beta^{2}
.

Proof.

From (
5
), using (
9
) and Proposition
5
, we obtain

\displaystyle g(\nabla_{X}V,Y)+g(X,\nabla_{Y}V)=-2\operatorname{Ric}(X,QY)+2\,\lambda\,g(X,Y)-2\,s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}g(QX,Y)

\displaystyle+2\,(s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda)\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\eta^{k}(Y)-4\,n\beta^{2}\bar{\eta}(X)\bar{\eta}(Y).

(68)

Using the condition
V=\sum_{\,i}\delta^{\,i}\xi_{i}
and (
18
) in (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum\nolimits_{\,i}\{(d\delta^{\,i})(X)\,\eta^{i}(Y)+(d\delta^{\,i})(Y)\,\eta^{i}(X)\}+2\,\bar{\delta}s\,\beta\{g(X,Y)-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\eta^{k}(Y)\}

\displaystyle=-2\operatorname{Ric}(X,QY)+2\,\lambda\,g(X,Y)-2\,s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}g(QX,Y)

\displaystyle+2\,(s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda)\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\eta^{k}(Y)-4\,n\beta^{2}\bar{\eta}(X)\,\bar{\eta}(Y),

(69)

where
\bar{\delta}:=\sum_{\,i}\delta^{\,i}
.
Substituting
Y=\xi_{j}
in (
5
) and using (
14
) and (
20
), gives

\displaystyle(d\delta^{\,j})(X)+\sum\nolimits_{\,i}(d\delta^{\,i})(\xi_{j})\,\eta^{i}(X)=\big\{2\,\lambda-2\,s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}+2\,(s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda)\big\}\eta^{j}(X)=0.

(70)

By (
70
), the functions
\delta^{\,j}\ (1\leq j\leq s)
are invariant along

{\cal D}
, that is
(d\delta^{\,j})(X)=0
for all
X\in{\cal D}
.
Hence, (
5
) along
{\cal D}
reads as

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}(X,QY)=(\lambda-\bar{\delta}s\,\beta)g(X,Y)-s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}g(QX,Y)\quad(X,Y\in{\cal D}).

(71)

Taking the covariant derivative of (
71
) in
Z
and using (
16
) yields

\displaystyle(\nabla_{Z}\operatorname{Ric})(X,QY)

\displaystyle=\beta\,\bar{\eta}(Y)\,\operatorname{Ric}(X,\widetilde{Q}Z)-s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{3}\bar{\eta}(Y)\,g(\widetilde{Q}Z,X)

\displaystyle+s\,(4\,n-1)\beta^{3}\bar{\eta}(X)\,g(\widetilde{Q}Z,Y)\quad(X,Y\in{\cal D}).

(72)

Using
Z=\xi_{i}
in (
5
), we get
(\nabla_{\xi_{i}}\operatorname{Ric})(X,QY)=0\ (X,Y\in{\cal D})
.
Applying to this (
21
),
gives

g(\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}X+2\,sn\,\beta^{2}X,QY)=0\ (X,Y\in{\cal D})
.
From the above, in view of non-singularity of
Q
, we find

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}X+2\,sn\,\beta^{2}X=\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\alpha^{k}(X)\xi_{k}

(73)

for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}_{M}
and some functions
\alpha^{k}(X)
on
M
.
Taking the inner product of (
73
) with
\xi_{i}
and using (
20
) gives
\alpha^{j}(X)=2\,s\,n\beta^{2}\eta^{j}(X)-2\,n\beta^{2}\bar{\eta}(X)
.
Hence

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp}X=-2\,s\,n\,\beta^{2}\big\{X-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(X)\,\xi_{j}\big\}-2\,n\,\beta^{2}\,\bar{\eta}(X)\,\bar{\xi}.

(74)

By (
74
) we conclude that

g
is an
\eta
-Einstein metric (
11
) with
a=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}
and
b=2(s-1)n\beta^{2}
.
∎

Definition 7
.

If

\ast
-

For a gradient almost

-

\displaystyle{\rm Hess}_{\,v}(X,Y)+g(\operatorname{Ric}QX,Y)=\lambda\,g(X,Y)-s\,(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}g(QX,Y)

Theorem 8
.

Let a weak
\beta f
-KM
M^{2n+s}({f},Q,\xi_{i},\eta^{i},g)
be a gradient almost
\ast
-
\eta
-RS (

Then

\nabla v=\sum_{\,i}\delta^{\,i}\xi_{i}\neq 0
for some smooth functions
\delta^{\,i}=const
along
{\cal D}
for all
i
on
M
,
and
(M,g)
is an
\eta
-Einstein manifold (
11
)
with
a=-2\,s\,n\beta^{2}
and
b=2(s-1)n\beta^{2}

Proof.

Differentiating
(
5
) along

Y
and using (
14
), (
15
), (
16
) and Proposition
5
, yields

\displaystyle\nabla_{Y}\nabla_{X}\nabla v+(\nabla_{Y}Q\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp})X=Y(\lambda)\big\{X-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\xi_{k}\big\}-s(2\,n-1)\beta^{2}(\nabla_{Y}Q)X

\displaystyle+((2\,n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda)\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\{(\nabla_{Y}\eta^{j})(X)\xi_{j}+\eta^{j}(X)\nabla_{Y}\xi_{j}\}-2\,n\beta^{2}\{(\nabla_{Y}\bar{\eta})(X)\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta}(X)\nabla_{Y}\bar{\xi}\}

\displaystyle=Y(\lambda)\big\{X-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\xi_{k}\big\}+(2\,n-1)\beta^{3}\big\{\bar{\eta}(X)\,\widetilde{Q}Y-g(\widetilde{Q}Y,X)\,\bar{\xi}\big\}

\displaystyle+\beta\big[(2\,(1-s)n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda\big]\big\{\{g(X,Y)-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\,\eta^{k}(Y)\}\bar{\xi}+\bar{\eta}(X)\{Y-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(Y)\,\xi_{k}\}\big\}.

Applying (
5
) and (
5
) in the expression of Riemannian curvature tensor, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{X,Y}\nabla v=(\nabla_{Y}Q\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp})X-(\nabla_{X}Q\operatorname{Ric}^{\sharp})Y

\displaystyle+X(\lambda)\big\{Y-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(Y)\xi_{k}\big\}-Y(\lambda)\big\{X-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\xi_{k}\big\}+s(2\,n-1)\beta^{3}\big\{\bar{\eta}(Y)\,\widetilde{Q}X-\bar{\eta}(X)\,\widetilde{Q}Y\big\}

\displaystyle+\beta\big[(2\,(1-s)n-1)\beta^{2}-\lambda\big]\big\{\bar{\eta}(Y)\{X-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(X)\,\xi_{k}\}-\bar{\eta}(X)\{Y-\sum\nolimits_{\,k}\eta^{k}(Y)\,\xi_{k}\}\big\}.

(79)

An inner product of (
5
) with
\xi_{i}
and use of (

\displaystyle g(R_{X,Y}\nabla v,\xi_{i})=0.

On the other hand, using (

\displaystyle g(R_{X,Y}\nabla v,\xi_{i})=-g(R_{X,Y}\xi_{i},\nabla v)

\displaystyle=\beta^{2}\bar{\eta}(Y)\{X(v)-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(X)\xi_{j}(v)\}-\beta^{2}\bar{\eta}(X)\{Y(v)-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(Y)\xi_{j}(v)\}.

Comparing (

\displaystyle\bar{\eta}(Y)\{X(v)-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(X)\xi_{j}(v)\}=\bar{\eta}(X)\{Y(v)-\sum\nolimits_{\,j}\eta^{j}(Y)\xi_{j}(v)\}.

Taking

6
Conclusion

The concept of the
\ast
-Ricci tensor of S. Tashibana was adapted to weak metric
f
-structure introduced by the author and R. Wolak.
The
\ast
-
\eta
-RS were defined and their interaction with weak
\beta f
-KM – a distinguished class of weak metric
f
-manifolds which
for
s>1
are not Einstein manifolds – was studied.
Conditions under which a weak
\beta f
-KM equipped with a
\ast
-
\eta
-RS structure carries
\eta
-Einstein metric were found.
A future task may be to study in detail the interaction of almost
\ast
-
\eta
-RS (i.e.,
\lambda,\mu
in (
32
) are smooth functions)
with weak
\beta f
-KM as well as other weak metric
f
-manifolds.

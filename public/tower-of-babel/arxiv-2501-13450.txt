Can we hear beats with pulsar timing arrays?
Shun Yamamoto, Hideki Asada

Abstract
An isolated supermassive black hole binary (SMBHB) produces an identical cross-correlation pattern of pulsar timings as an isotropic stochastic background gravitational waves (GWs) generated possibly by inflation. Can there remain the identical cross-correlation pattern in the presence of a secondary SMBHB? To address this issue, the present paper considers GWs with slightly different frequencies f 1 f_{1} and f 2 f_{2} coming from two different directions. Beats between the two GWs can modify angular correlation patterns. The beat-induced correlation patterns are not stationary but modulated with a beat frequency f b ​ e ​ a ​ t ≡ | f 1 − f 2 | f_{beat}\equiv|f_{1}-f_{2}| . We obtain an analytic formula to present a proof-of-principle that f b ​ e ​ a ​ t f_{beat} can be measured from the modulated angular correlations.

III
Can we hear beats?

III.1
Extracting a beat factor

A key is that
the cross term
\langle z_{a1}z_{b2}\rangle+\langle z_{a2}z_{b1}\rangle

is modulated via beat factors,
while the diagonal term

\langle z_{a1}z_{b1}\rangle

\langle z_{a2}z_{b2}\rangle

In order to extract the modulation,

into four equal time intervals as

D_{2}=\{t\in[T_{obs}/4,T_{obs}/2)\}

D_{3}=\{t\in[T_{obs}/2,3T_{obs}/4)\}

and

D_{4}=\{t\in[3T_{obs}/4,T_{obs})\}

From the above discussion,

\langle z_{a1}z_{b1}\rangle+\langle z_{a2}z_{b2}\rangle

remains the same for any
D_{K}

(K=1,2,3
and
4)

while

depends upon
D_{K}
.
The dependence of

plays a crucial role in the following calculations.
In order to manifest this,

denotes
B
on

Namely,

is denoted as

III.2
Inverse problem and its solution

The prefactor
2/\omega_{B}(t_{f}-t_{i})
in Eq. (
9
) cancels out
in a fractional form as Eq. (
12

Eq. (

\displaystyle C_{123}\cos\Phi+S_{123}\sin\Phi=0,

\displaystyle C_{234}\cos\Phi+S_{234}\sin\Phi=0,

where

\eta\equiv\omega_{B}T_{obs}/4
(a phase of the beat during each time interval), and

\displaystyle C_{123}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{123}\sin 3\eta-(1+2\delta_{123})\sin 2\eta+(2+\delta_{123})\sin\eta,

\displaystyle S_{123}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{123}\cos 3\eta-(1+2\delta_{123})\cos 2\eta+(2+\delta_{123})\cos\eta,

\displaystyle C_{234}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{234}\sin 4\eta-(1+2\delta_{234})\sin 3\eta+(2+\delta_{234})\sin 2\eta,

\displaystyle S_{234}

\displaystyle\equiv\delta_{234}\cos 4\eta-(1+2\delta_{234})\cos 3\eta+(2+\delta_{234})\cos 2\eta.

(14)

Eq. (
13

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}C_{123}&S_{123}\\
C_{234}&S_{234}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\cos\Phi\\
\sin\Phi\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}0\\
0\end{pmatrix}.

(15)

By using
(\cos\Phi,\sin\Phi)\neq(0,0)
in Eq. (

we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{vmatrix}C_{123}&S_{123}\\
C_{234}&S_{234}\end{vmatrix}=0.

(16)

This is an equation for
\eta
.
By direct calculations,
Eq. (
16
) is factorized as

\displaystyle\sin\eta(\cos\eta-1)^{2}[2\delta_{234}\cos\eta-(1+\delta_{123}\delta_{234})]=0,

where Eq. (
14

First, we consider a noninterference case (

)
(e.g.

.
Namely,

Secondly, we study an interference case (

\displaystyle\cos\eta=\frac{1+\delta_{123}\delta_{234}}{2\delta_{234}}.

\Phi

Thirdly, we mention that interferences (

This particular case occurs when

Clearly, this case is very rare in the sense that

of the inverse of the beat frequency.
This third case migrates to the second case
when a different observational duration (
\neq T_{obs}
) is used.

Before closing this section,
let us briefly mention a possible range of parameters.
The magnitude of the cross terms are proportional to
h_{1}\times h_{2}
,
while the leading term of the correlations is

\langle z_{a1}z_{b1}\rangle
proportional to
(h_{1})^{2}
.
The ratio of the cross terms to the leading term is
roughly
O(h_{2}/h_{1})
.
When correlations are measured with e.g. 20 percent accuracy,
the beat modulation will be marginally detectable if
h_{2}/h_{1}>0.2
.

Next, we mention a possible beat frequency.
The current results hold when
f_{beat}T_{obs}\sim O(1)
.
When
f_{beat}T_{obs}\gg 1
, there are many beat oscillations in one
D_{K}
,
and thus the information about the beats is smeared out.
It is thus difficult to hear beats in this case.
When
f_{beat}T_{obs}\ll 1
, on the other hand,
only a small portion of the beat oscillation is included in
D_{K}

and thus it is difficult to determine
f_{beat}
.
In order to clarify the parameter ranges,
a detailed and numerical study is needed.
It is left for future.

IV
Conclusion

Beat effects between
GWs with slightly different frequencies coming from two sky directions
have been discussed.
Angular correlations can be modulated
by the beat effects.
We have found an analytic formula to infer the beat frequency
f_{beat}

from modulated angular correlations,
which leads to a proof-of-principle that
we can hear beats with PTAs.

Along the direction of this paper,
it would be interesting to perform detailed calculations
taking account of observational noises in order to make an estimation of
a possible accuracy in measured
f_{beat}
.
The present paper focuses on the minimum number of equal time intervals,
namely four time domains.
What is the optimal number of
D_{K}
in a realistic case?
It would be interesting to address this issue,
because the SKA (Square Kilometre Array) is expected to drastically
increase the number of pulsars suitable for PTAs

SKA
.
These issues are left for future.

The extremal process of two-speed branching random walk
Lianghui Luo

Abstract
We consider a two-speed branching random walk, which consists of two macroscopic stages with different reproduction laws. We prove that the centered maximum converges in law to a Gumbel variable with a random shift and the extremal process converges in law to a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process, which can be viewed as a discrete analog for the corresponding results for the two-speed branching Brownian motion, previously established by Bovier and Hartung [ 12 ] .



Keywords:
Branching random walk; extremal process; time-inhomogeneous environment.

MSC2020 subject classification:
60F05,60G70,60J80.

1
Introduction

A branching random walk on real line
\mathbb{R}
can be described as follows. At time 0, one particle is located at 0. At time 1, the particle dies and produces its children according to an independent copy of some point process
\mathcal{L}
. Each of children repeats the behavior of its parent independently. For each particle
u
, we write
|u|
and
V(u)
for its generation and position respectively. We call
(V(u):|u|<\infty)
a branching random walk with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}
.

Under some mild integrability conditions, the asymptotic behavior of the maximal displacement
M_{n}:=\max_{|u|=n}V(u)
has been well studied. Let
\kappa(\theta):=\log\mathbb{E}(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}e^{\theta\ell})
be the log-Laplace transform of point process
\mathcal{L}
. As
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
for some
\theta>0
and
\kappa(0)>0
, Hammersley
[
22
]
, Kingman
[
26
]
and Biggins
[
8
]
proved that on the non-extinction set, almost surely,

\frac{M_{n}}{n}\to\inf_{\theta>0}\frac{\kappa(\theta)}{\theta}=:x^{*},\quad n\to\infty.

Moreover, if there exists some
\theta^{*}>0
such that
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})=x^{*}
, i.e.
\theta^{*}\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})=\kappa(\theta^{*})
, the logarithmic correction was studied by Hu and Shi
[
23
]
, Addario-Berry and Reed
[
1
]
, and Bramson and Zeitouni
[
15
]
. They showed that

M_{n}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})n+\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n

is tight conditionally on the non-extinction set.
Finally, Aïdékon
[
2
]
obtained that
M_{n}-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})n+\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n
converges in law to a random shift of a Gumbel variable.

The extremal process is a point process formed by
(V(u)-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})n+\frac{3}{2\theta^{*}}\log n:|u|=n)
. In the context of branching Brownian motion, Arguin et al.
[
6
]
and Aïdékon et al.
[
3
]
proved independently the extremal process converges in law to a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process, and
[
3
]
gives a probabilistic representation of the decoration. For a branching random walk, Madaule
[
30
]
showed the weak convergence of the extremal process. In Section
2.4
, we will introduce these results in detail.

In recent years, time-inhomogeneous branching random walk has received a lot of attention, where time-inhomogeneous means that the reproduction law of individuals depend on time. More precisely, fix
M\in\mathbb{N}
, and consider the parameters

0=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{M}=1

and a collection of point processes
(\mathcal{L}_{i}:1\leq i\leq M)
. Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
, at time
0
, here is one individual located at
0
. For
0\leq i\leq M-1
, between time
\lfloor t_{i}n\rfloor
+1 and
\lfloor t_{i+1}n\rfloor
, individuals reproduce their children, which are scattered according to independent copies of the reproduction law
\mathcal{L}_{i}
, with respect to their parent. We write
V^{(n)}(u)
for the position of particle
u
such that
|u|\leq n
and
M_{n}^{(n)}:=\max_{|u|=n}V^{(n)}(u)
for the maximal position of particles at time
n
. When the law of displacement is Gaussian, the first two orders were obtained by Fang and Zeitouni
[
20
]
in the case
M=2
. Under some restriction, Ouimet
[
33
]
showed the logarithm corrections for
M\geq 2
by generalizing the approach in
[
20
]
. Under close to optimal conditions, Mallein
[
31
]
showed the tightness of the centered maximum using spinal decomposition of a time-inhomogeneous branching random walk. In particular,
[
20
]
and
[
31
]
showed that the log-correction has a phase transition when the law of the second environment is modified.

In this paper, we focus on the case
M=2
and
t=t_{1}
, and call
(V^{(n)}(u):|u|\leq n)_{n\geq 0}
a two-speed branching random walk, namely a time-homogeneous branching random walk. Let
\kappa_{i}(\theta):=\log\mathbb{E}(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}_{i}}e^{\theta\ell})
be the log-Laplace transform of point process
\mathcal{L}_{i}
,
i=1,2,
respectively. Note that
\kappa_{i}
is convex and smooth on the interior of
\{\theta\in\mathbb{R}:|\kappa_{i}(\theta)|<\infty\}.
Under some integrability conditions, when there exist
\theta_{1}^{*},\theta_{2}^{*}>0
such that

\theta_{i}^{*}\kappa_{i}^{\prime}(\theta_{i}^{*})-\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i}^{*})=0,\quad i=1,2,

and
\theta>0
such that

t[\theta\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{1}(\theta)]+(1-t)[\theta\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{2}(\theta)]=0,

Mallein
[
31
]
defined three distinct regimes for this model, and proved the tightness of the maximum in each of regimes. The regimes can informally be identified by the sign of
\theta_{1}^{*}-\theta_{2}^{*}
, being the slow regime if this quantity is negative, mean regime if null or fast regime if positive. However, their characterization does not necessarily relies on the existence of these parameters. More precisely, he proved that
M_{n}^{(n)}-m_{n}
is tight, where

m_{n}:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta_{1}^{*})\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta_{2}^{*})(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-(\frac{3}{2\theta_{1}^{*}}+\frac{3}{2\theta_{2}^{*}})\log n,&\hbox{\text{in the slow regime};}\\
\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{3}{2\theta}\log n,&\hbox{\text{in the mean regime};}\\
\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n,&\hbox{\text{in the fast regime}.}\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

In this paper, we consider a two-speed branching random walk
(V^{(n)}(u):|u|\leq n)_{n\geq 0}
in slow regime or fast regime, and aim to prove the weak convergence of the centered maximum
M^{(n)}_{n}-m_{n}
and extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V^{(n)}(u)-m_{n}}
. In the context of two-speed branching Brownian motions, which is a family of branching Brownian motions with inhomogeneous variance, Bovier and Hartung
[
12
]
proved the weak convergence of the maximum position and the extremal process. Moreover, for a variable speed branching Brownian motion, Bovier and Hartung
[
13
]
showed that the limiting extremal process were those that emerged in the two-speed case. For more discussions, see
[
21
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
32
]
,
[
14
]
and
[
4
]
.

We assume throughout the rest of the paper the two-speed branching random walk survives almost surely for all times and is supercritical, i.e.

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R})=0)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{L}_{i}(\mathbb{R})=1)<1,\quad i=1,2.

1.1
Fast regime

We first introduce some basic assumptions in the fast regime. For
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
, we denote by

\kappa^{\prime}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\ell e^{\theta\ell-\kappa(\theta)}\right)

if the expectation in the right hand side is well-defined. Note that if
\kappa
is derivative at point
\theta
, then its derivative is actually
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
, justifying the notation.
We give four assumptions on
\mathcal{L}
and
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
. We assume that

\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta):=\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(\ell-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))^{2}e^{\theta\ell-\kappa(\theta)}\right)\in(0,\infty),

(1.2)

where if the second derivative of
\kappa
at point
\theta
exists and is finite, then its second derivative is
\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)
, justifying the notation.
And we also assume that

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}e^{\theta\ell}\log_{+}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}e^{\theta\ell}\right)\right)<\infty,

(1.3)

where
\log_{+}(x):=\log(\max\{x,1\})
, for
x\geq 0
.
Finally, we assume that for any
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
,

\mathbb{P}(\ell\in a+b\mathbb{Z},\forall\ell\in\mathcal{L})<1,

(1.4)

i.e.
\mathcal{L}
is non-lattice, where
a+b\mathbb{Z}:=\{a+bz:z\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

We first present our main result in the fast regime.

Theorem 1.1

(Fast regime)
.

Fix
t\in(0,1)
. Assume that there exists
\theta>0
such that point processes
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
satisfy assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and

\kappa_{1}(\theta)>\theta\kappa^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)\quad\text{and}\quad t[\theta\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{1}(\theta)]+(1-t)[\theta\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{2}(\theta)]=0.

(1.5)

Set

m_{n}:=\frac{\kappa_{1}(\theta)}{\theta}\lfloor tn\rfloor+\frac{\kappa_{2}(\theta)}{\theta}(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then

(i)

there exists some constant
C_{\mathrm{f}}>0
defined in
(\ref{fast})
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}^{(n)}-m_{n}\leq y)=\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-C_{\mathrm{f}}W^{(1)}(\theta)e^{-\theta y}}\right],

(1.6)

where
W^{(1)}(\theta)
is a positive random variable whose law only depends on
\theta
and
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

(ii)

The point process

\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V^{(n)}(u)-m_{n}}

(1.7)

converges in law to a well defined point process
\mathcal{E}
in the sense of the topology of vague convergence, where

\mathcal{E}:=\sum_{k,i\geq 1}\delta_{p_{k}+d_{i}^{(k)}},

(1.8)

with
p_{k}
being the
k
-th atom of a Poisson point process with intensity measure

C_{\mathrm{f}}W^{(1)}(\theta)\theta e^{-\theta y}dy,

and
(d_{i}^{(k)}:i\geq 1)
being the atoms of independent and identically distributed point processes
\mathcal{D}^{(2,k)}
(called decorations), whose law only depends on
\mathcal{L}_{2}
and
\theta
.

Remark 1.1
.

With the assumption
t[\theta\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{1}(\theta)]+(1-t)[\theta\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa_{2}(\theta)]=0
, it is not hard to see that

m_{n}-\left[\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n\right]=\frac{tn-\lfloor tn\rfloor}{1-t}\left(\kappa^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)-\frac{\kappa_{1}(\theta)}{\theta}\right),

(1.9)

where the right hand side is a bounded sequence.

Theorem
1.1
is an analog of Theorem 1.2 in
[
12
]
for branching Brownian motion. To be more precise, in the fast regime, (
1.6
) tells us that the centered maximum converges in law a Gumbel variable with a random shift, where
W^{(1)}(\theta)
is the almost surely limit of the additive martingale (defined in forthcoming (
2.1
)) of a branching random walk
(V_{1}(u):|u|<\infty)
with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. Because
\mathcal{L}_{1}
verifies
\theta\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)<\kappa_{1}(\theta)
and assumption
(\ref{as3})
, according to
[
9
]
, we have
\mathbb{P}(W^{(1)}(\theta)=0)=0
, which implies that the limit distribution of the centered maximum is non-degenerate. Moreover, we show that the extremal process converges in law to a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process, where the decoration is the limit in law of

\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V_{2}(u)-\max_{|u|=n}V_{2}(u)}

(1.10)

conditionally on
\{\max_{|u|=n}V_{2}(u)\geq\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)n\}
, where
(V_{2}(u):|u|<\infty)
is a branching random walk with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}_{2}
. The existence and probabilistic representation of the decoration have been shown in
[
27
]
. We will introduce in more details these quantities in Section
3
.

The main idea of the proof is to compute the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)})

for all continuous bounded function
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
whose support has a finite left bound,
where
\mu(f):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\mu(\mathrm{d}x)
.
By Markov property at time
\lfloor tn\rfloor
, we can write
\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}_{n}(f)})=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}(\phi_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}[f])}),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}^{(1)}:=\sum_{|u|=\lfloor tn\rfloor}\delta_{V_{1}(u)-\kappa^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)\lfloor tn\rfloor}
and
\phi_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}[f](x)=-\log\mathbb{E}[e^{-\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}(f(\cdot+x))}]
, where

\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}:=\sum_{|u|=n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}\delta_{V_{2}(u)-\kappa^{\prime}_{2}(\theta)(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)+\frac{1}{2\theta}\log n-\frac{tn-\lfloor tn\rfloor}{1-t}\left(\kappa^{\prime}_{1}(\theta)-\frac{\kappa_{1}(\theta)}{\theta}\right)}.

The estimations of
\phi_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}[f]
can be obtained by the results in
[
27
]
, then we are able to compute the asymptotic behavior of
\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_{\lfloor tn\rfloor}(\phi_{n-\lfloor tn\rfloor}[f])})
.

1.2
Slow regime

We first introduce some basic assumptions for the weak convergence of centered maximum of a branching random walk with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}
.
We assume that there exists
\theta^{*}>0
such that

\kappa(\theta^{*})<\infty,\quad\theta^{*}\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*})=\kappa(\theta^{*}),

(1.11)

and

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}(\ell-\kappa^{\prime}(\theta^{*}))^{2}e^{\theta^{*}\ell-\kappa(\theta^{*})}\right)\in(0,\infty).

(1.12)

And we also assume that

\mathbb{E}(X(\log_{+}X)^{2})<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}(\tilde{X}\log_{+}\tilde{X})<\infty,

(1.13)

where

X:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}e^{\theta^{*}\ell}\quad\text{and}\quad\tilde{X}:=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}\max\{-\ell,0\}e^{\theta^{*}\ell}.

Theorem 1.2

(Slow regime)
.

Fix
t\in(0,1)
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}_{1},\mathcal{L}_{2}
are non-lattice and that there exist
\theta_{1}^{*},\theta_{2}^{*}>0
satisfying
\theta_{1}^{*}<\theta_{2}^{*}
and such that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
satisfy assumptions (
1.11
), (
1.12
) and (
1.13
). Let
m_{n}:=\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta_{1}^{*})\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta_{2}^{*})(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{3}{2\theta_{1}^{*}}\log(\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{3}{2\theta_{2}^{*}}\log(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

(i)

there exists some constant
C_{\mathrm{s}}>0
defined in (
4.10
) such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}^{(n)}-m_{n}\leq y)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-C_{\mathrm{s}}Z^{(1)}e^{-\theta_{1}^{*}y}})

(1.14)

where
Z^{(1)}
is a positive random variable whose law only depends on
\mathcal{L}_{1}
.

(ii)

The extremal process

\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V^{(n)}(u)-m_{n}}

converges in law to a well-defined point process
\mathcal{E}
.

Theorem
1.2
extends the results in Theorem 1.3 in
[
12
]
to two-speed branching random walk. It is a direct application of
[
2
]
and
[
30
]
. In fact,
\bar{Z}
is the limit of the derivative martingale
Z_{n}^{(1)}
(defined in Section
2.4
) of a branching random walk
(V_{1}(u):|u|<\infty)
with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}_{1}
. In Section
4
, we will show that the limit point process
\mathcal{E}
can be given by

\mathcal{E}:=\sum_{i,j\geq 1}\delta_{e_{i}+e_{j}^{(i)}}

(1.15)

where
(e_{i}:i\geq 1)
and
(e_{j}^{(i)}:j\geq 1)
are the atoms of the point process
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{E}^{(2,i)}
, where
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
is the limit in law of the extremal process
\mathcal{E}_{n}^{(1)}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V_{1}(u)-\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta_{1}^{*})n+\frac{3}{2\theta_{1}^{*}}\log n}
, and
(\mathcal{E}^{(2,i)})_{i\geq 1}
are independent of
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
and are independent copies of the limit in law of the extremal process
\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V_{2}(u)-\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta_{2}^{*})n+\frac{3}{2\theta_{2}^{*}}\log n}
. Moreover, by
[
30
]
, we know that
\mathcal{E}^{(1)}
is a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process, which, combined with (
1.15
), yields that
\mathcal{E}
is actually a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process.

1.3
Mean regime

In this section, we give a conjecture about the limit law of centered maximum. This case has not been studied in branching Brownian motion setting.

Conjecture 1.3

(Mean regime)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
are non-lattice. Assume that there exists
\theta_{1}^{*},\theta_{2}^{*}>0
such that
\mathcal{L}_{1}
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}
satisfy assumptions (
1.11
), (
1.12
), (
1.13
) and
\theta_{1}^{*}=\theta_{2}^{*}
. Let
\theta:=\theta_{1}^{*}
and
m_{n}:=\kappa_{1}^{\prime}(\theta)\lfloor tn\rfloor+\kappa_{2}^{\prime}(\theta)(n-\lfloor tn\rfloor)-\frac{3}{2\theta}\log n
. Then

(i)

there exists some constant
C_{\mathrm{m}}>0
such that for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(M_{n}^{(n)}-m_{n}\leq y)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-C_{\mathrm{m}}Z^{(1)}e^{-\theta y}}),

where
Z^{(1)}
is the same random variable as in Theorem
1.2
.

(ii)

the extremal process

\mathcal{E}_{n}:=\sum_{|u|=n}\delta_{V^{(n)}(u)-m_{n}}

converges in law to a randomly shifted decorated Poisson point process
\mathcal{E}
with the random intensity measure
C_{\mathrm{m}}Z^{(1)}\theta e^{-\theta y}dy
and the decoration
\mathcal{D}^{(2)}
, where
\mathcal{D}^{(2)}

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we give a construction of two-speed branching random walk. In the rest of Section
2

In the proof of this paper, we denote by

positive constant that may change from line to line.

2
Preliminaries

For convenience, we write
t_{n}
for
\lfloor tn\rfloor
. In this section, we explain how to construct a two-speed branching random walk. Then we go back to the setting of a time-homogeneous branching random walk and introduce the additive martingale and spinal decomposition theorem, which help us to prove a limit theorem associated with additive martingales. Next, we introduce the derivative martingale and some classical results about the limit of centered maximum and extremal process, and prove some properties about the limit of the extremal process.

2.1
Construction of the two-speed branching random walk

Define the set of finite sequence of positive integers

\mathbb{T}:=\bigcup_{n\geq 0}\mathbb{N}^{n},

with the usual convention
\mathbb{N}^{0}:=\{\varnothing\}
.

Let
(\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(u)}:u\in\mathbb{T})
be independent copies of point process
\mathcal{L}_{i}
,
i=1,2
respectively. We assume that
(\mathcal{L}_{1}^{(u)}:u\in\mathbb{T})
and
(\mathcal{L}_{2}^{(u)}:u\in\mathbb{T})
are independent. As there exists some
\theta_{i}>0
such that
\kappa_{i}(\theta_{i})<\infty
, then for any
y\in\mathbb{R}
,
\mathcal{L}_{i}([y,\infty))<\infty
almost surely, therefore we can rank the atoms of
L_{i}
in a non-increasing fashion. For
i\in\{1,2\}
,
u\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(u)}
as
(\ell_{i,j}^{(u)}:j\geq 1)
, where
(\ell_{i,j}^{(u)}:j\geq 1)
is a non-increasing sequence generated by all atoms in
\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(u)}
with the conventions that
\ell_{i,j}^{(u)}:=-\infty
if
j>\mathcal{L}_{i}^{(u)}(\mathbb{R})
and that
\lim_{j\to\infty}\ell_{i,j}^{(u)}=-\infty
.
For
u\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
|u|
for its generation, i.e.
u\in\mathbb{N}^{|u|}
. For
0\leq j\leq|u|
, we denote by
u_{j}
the
j
-th ancestor of particle
u
, i.e.
u_{j}
is the projection of
u
on
\mathbb{N}^{j}
. With the help of notation above, we can construct a two-speed branching random walk
(V^{(n)}(u):|u|\leq n)_{n\geq 1}
as follows. Fix
t\in[0,1]
, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
V^{(n)}(\varnothing):=0
and for
|u|\leq n
and
u\neq\varnothing
,

V^{(n)}(u):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sum_{j=1}^{|u|}\ell_{1,j}^{(u_{j-1})},&\hbox{$1\leq|u|\leq t_{n}$;}\\
\sum_{j=1}^{t_{n}}\ell_{1,j}^{(u_{j-1})}+\sum_{j=t_{n}+1}^{|u|}\ell_{2,j}^{(u_{j-1})},&\hbox{$t_{n}+1\leq|u|\leq n$,}\end{array}\right.

where we say that particle
u
is in death if
V^{(n)}(u)=-\infty
. We denote by
\{|u|=n\}
the set of all particles alive at time
n
. Remark that if
t\in\{0,1\}
, a two-speed branching random walk can be seen as a time-homogeneous branching random walk. We thus assume that
t\in(0,1)
.

2.2
Spinal decomposition: a change of measure

In this section, we only consider a time-homogeneous branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}
. Lyons
[
28
]
introduced the spinal decomposition theorem for the branching random walk as an alternative description of the law of the branching random biased by its additive martingale. This method is an extension of the spinal decomposition obtained by Lyons et al.
[
29
]
for Galton-Watson processes and Chauvin and Rouault
[
17
]
for branching Brownian motion.

Let
(\mathscr{F}_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be the natural filtration of the branching random walk, i.e.

\mathscr{F}_{n}:=\sigma(V(u):|u|\leq n),

with
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}:=\sigma(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
.
Fix
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta):=\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)})<\infty
, we denote by

W_{n}(\theta):=\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}

(2.1)

the additive martingale with parameter
\theta
.
By the branching property,
(W_{n}(\theta))_{n\geq 1}
is a non-negative martingale with respect to
(\mathscr{F}_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, which implies that there exists a non-negative random variable
W(\theta):=\lim_{n\to\infty}W_{n}(\theta)
a.s.

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\mathbb{P}_{x}
the law of branching random walk starting from 0 and
\mathbb{E}_{x}
the corresponding expectation. We write
\mathbb{P}
and
\mathbb{E}
instead of
\mathbb{P}_{0}
and
\mathbb{E}_{0}
for brevity. On the one hand, using Kolmogorov’s extension theorem, there exists a probability
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
on
\mathscr{F}_{\infty}
satisfying for any
n\geq 0
,
A\in\mathscr{F}_{n}
,

\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(A)=e^{-\theta x}\mathbb{E}_{x}(W_{n}(\theta)1_{A}).

We write
\tilde{\mathbb{E}}_{x}
for the expectation with respect to
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
.

On the other hand, we define a point process
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
is such that the law of
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
is equal to the law of
\mathcal{L}
biased by
\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{L}}e^{\theta\ell-\kappa(\theta)}
. A branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
with a spine
(\xi_{n}:n\geq 0)
can be described as follows. At time 0, one particle
\xi_{0}:=\varnothing
locates at position
V(\xi_{0})=x
. At each time
n\geq 1
, all particles die, while giving birth independently to sets of new particles. The displacements (with respect to their parent) of children of normal particle
u
are distributed as
(V(i):i\geq 1)
. The displacements (with respect to their parent) of children of spine particle
\xi_{n-1}
are distributed as
\hat{\mathcal{L}}
; the particle
\xi_{n}
is chosen among the children
v
of
\xi_{n-1}
with probability proportional to
e^{\theta V(v)}
. Let us denote by
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
the law of the branching random walk with a spine and
\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}
the corresponding expectation. The spinal decomposition theorem corresponds in the identification of the law of the size-biased branching random walk and the law of the branching random walk with a spine.

Theorem 2.1

(Lyons
[
28
]
)
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\geq 0
, the law of
(V(u):|u|\leq n)
is identical under
\tilde{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
and
\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{x}
. Moreover, for any
|u|=n
,

\bar{\mathbb{P}}_{x}(u=\xi_{n}|\mathscr{F}_{n})=\frac{e^{\theta V(u)-n\psi(\theta)}}{W_{n}(\theta)}\quad\text{a.s.}

(2.2)

Finally, we introduce the many-to-one formula, which can be seen as an direct corollary of the spinal decomposition theorem.

Corollary 2.2

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
35
]
)
.

Assume that there exists
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
.
Then for any
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
and any measurable function
g
:
\mathbb{R}^{n}\to[0,\infty)
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{x}(\sum_{|u|=n}g(V(u_{1}),\ldots,V(u_{n})))=e^{\theta x}\mathbb{E}_{x}(e^{-\theta S_{n}+n\kappa(\theta)}g(S_{1},\ldots,S_{n})),

where under
\mathbb{P}_{x}
,
(S_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is a random walk starting from
x
and is such that for any measurable function
f:\mathbb{R}\to[0,\infty)
,

e^{-\theta x}\mathbb{E}_{x}(\sum_{|u|=1}f(V(u))e^{\theta V(u)-\kappa(\theta)})=\mathbb{E}_{x}(f(S_{1})).

Moreover, as
\theta>0
such that
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta),\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)<\infty
, we know that
\mathbb{E}(S_{1})=\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
and
\mathbb{E}[(S_{1}-\mathbb{E}(S_{1}))^{2}]=\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta)
.

2.3
Central limit theorem associated with additive martingales

We denote by
C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
the set of all continuous and bounded function
f:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}.
Given a time-homogeneous branching random walk
(V(u):u\in\mathbb{T})
with reproduction law
\mathcal{L}
, we aim to prove the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\theta>0
such that assumptions (
1.2
), (
1.3
) hold and such that
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<\kappa(\theta)
. Then for any
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}f\left(\frac{V(u)-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)=W(\theta)\mathbb{E}(f(N))\quad\text{in }L^{1},

(2.3)

where
N\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\kappa^{\prime\prime}(\theta))
.

Remark 2.1
.

For our purpose, we only need to prove that the convergence in Theorem
2.3

holds in probability. Under the additional assumption that
\mathbb{E}(W_{1}(\theta)^{1+\gamma})<\infty
for some
\gamma>0
, this result has been proved in
[
10
]
,
[
34
]
and
[
24
]
. We remove this integrability condition by a truncation argument.

We first prove a series of lemmas, which are helpful for us to prove Theorem
2.3
in the end of this section. Fix
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
,
we define

\bar{W}_{n}(\theta):=\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}f\left(\frac{V(u)-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right).

Recall that
\mathscr{F}_{k}=\sigma(V(u):|u|\leq k)
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.3
, for any
f\in C_{b}(\mathbb{R})
, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-W(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)\right|\right)=0.

Proof.

By triangular inequality, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-W(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)\right|\right)\\
\leq&\mathbb{E}\left(\left|W_{k}(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})\right|\right)+\mathbb{E}(|W_{k}(\theta)-W(\theta)|)\mathbb{E}f(N).\end{split}

(2.4)

Using the many-to-one formula,

\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})=\sum_{|u|=k}e^{\theta V(u)-k\kappa(\theta)}\mathbb{E}_{V(u)}\left[f\left(\frac{S_{n-k}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right],

which implies that for fixed
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}|W_{k}(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)-\mathbb{E}&(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})|\\
&\leq\sum_{|u|=k}e^{\theta V(u)-k\kappa(\theta)}\left|\mathbb{E}f(N)-\mathbb{E}_{V(u)}\left[f\left(\frac{S_{n-k}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]\right|\end{split}

(2.5)

Because
W_{n}(\theta)
is a martingale, we know that
\mathbb{E}(W_{n}(\theta))=\mathbb{E}(W_{0}(\theta))=1
, which yields that
W_{k}(\theta)<\infty
almost surely. For
|u|=k
, by central limit theorem, we know that almost surely

\lim_{n\to\infty}\left|\mathbb{E}f(N)-\mathbb{E}_{V(u)}\left[f\left(\frac{S_{n-k}-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\right]\right|=0.

Thus, by dominated convergence, we conclude that the right hand side of (
2.5
) decays to 0 almost surely as
n\to\infty
, then

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|W_{k}(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})|=0.

(2.6)

Combined with (
2.4
), we observe that for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\left|\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-W(\theta)\mathbb{E}f(N)\right|\right)\leq\mathbb{E}(|W_{k}(\theta)-W(\theta)|)\mathbb{E}f(N).

By Theorem A in
[
9
]
,
W_{k}(\theta)
converges to
W(\theta)
in
L^{1}
. Combined with the fact that
f
is bounded, we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(|W_{k}(\theta)-W(\theta)|)\mathbb{E}f(N)=0,

(2.7)

which completes the proof.
∎

As
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<\kappa(\theta)
, we can fix
a,L>0
such that
a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)<0
. For
u=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(|u|)})
,
v=(v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{(|v|)})\in\mathbb{T}
, let
uv:=(u^{(1)},\ldots,u^{(|u|)},v^{(1)},\ldots,v^{|v|})
be the concatenation operate with the convention
u\varnothing=\varnothing u=u
. We introduce some truncated set as follows. For any
A>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we define

E_{1}(n,A):=\left\{u\in\mathbb{T}:\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta(V(u_{k}i)-V(u_{k}))}<Ae^{a(k+1)},\forall 0\leq k\leq n-1\right\},

where
E_{1}(n,A)
controls the number and positions of siblings of particles with respect to their parents.
And we denote by

E_{2}(n,A):=\{u\in\mathbb{T}:V(u_{k})-k\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)\leq Lk+A,\forall 1\leq k\leq n\}

a collection of particle
u
whose trajectory
\{V(u_{k}):1\leq k\leq n\}
stays below the line
\{(L+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k+A:1\leq k\leq n\}
.
We construct an auxiliary process

\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta):=\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}f\left(\frac{V(u)-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)1_{\{u\in E_{1}(n,A)\cap E_{2}(n,A)\}}.

The following lemma tells us that
\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)
is a good approximation of
\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
\theta>0
such that assumption (
1.2
) holds and
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<\kappa(\theta)
. Then

\lim_{A\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}(|\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})|)=0

(2.8)

and

\lim_{A\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}(|\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)-\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|)=0.

(2.9)

Proof.

By Jensen inequality, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}(|\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})|)\leq\mathbb{E}(|\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)-\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|).

Thus, (
2.9
) implies (
2.8
). We denote by
\|f\|_{\infty}
the essential supremum of
|f|
.
Note that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(|\bar{W}_{n}(\theta)-\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|)\\
\leq&\|f\|_{\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{1}(n,A)\}\cup\{u\notin E_{2}(n,A)\}}\right)\\
\leq&\|f\|_{\infty}\times\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{1}(n,A)\}}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{2}(n,A)\}}\right)\right].\end{split}

(2.10)

On the one hand, by many-to-one formula, we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{2}(n,A)\}}\right)=&\mathbb{P}(\exists 1\leq k\leq n,S_{k}-k\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)>Lk+A)\\
\leq&\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}>(L+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k+A).\end{split}

(2.11)

Recall that
\mathbb{E}(S_{1})=\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)
, because
\mathbb{E}(S_{1}^{2})<\infty
and
L>0
, by Hsu-Robbins theorem (see
[
19
]
), we obtain

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}(S_{k}>(L+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k)<\infty.

Combined with (
2.11
), by monotone convergence theorem, we conclude that

\lim_{A\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{2}(n,A)\}}\right)=0.

(2.12)

On the other hand, according to spinal decomposition theorem,

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{1}(n,A)\}}\right)\\
=&\bar{\mathbb{E}}(1_{\{\xi_{n}=u\}}1_{\{u\notin E_{1}(n,A)\}})\\
=&\bar{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists 0\leq k\leq n-1,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{k}i)-V(\xi_{k}))}\geq Ae^{a(k+1)}\right).\end{split}

(2.13)

Recall that
(V(\xi_{k}i)-V(\xi_{k}):i\geq 1)_{k\geq 0}
are i.i.d under
\bar{\mathbb{P}}
, we have

\begin{split}&\bar{\mathbb{P}}\left(\exists 0\leq k\leq n-1,\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{k}i)-V(\xi_{k}))}\geq Ae^{a(k+1)}\right)\\
\leq&\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\mathbb{P}}\left(\log\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}>ak+\log A\right).\end{split}

(2.14)

From assumption
\mathbb{E}(\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}\log\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)})<\infty
and spinal decomposition theorem, we know that
\bar{\mathbb{E}}(\log\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)})<\infty
, combined with
a>0
, which implies that

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\bar{\mathbb{P}}\left(\log\sum_{|u|=1}e^{\theta V(u)}>ak\right)<\infty.

Combined with (
2.13
) and (
2.14
), by monotone convergence theorem, we have

\lim_{A\to\infty}\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=n}e^{\theta V(u)-n\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\notin E_{1}(n,A)\}}\right)=0,

which, combined with (
2.10
) and (
2.12
), implies (
2.9
).
∎

Lemma 2.6
.

Fix
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
and
\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<\infty
. For any
a,A,L>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leq k\leq n-1
, we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{v\in E_{1}(n-k,Ae^{ak})\cap E_{2}(n-k,A+Lk+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)k)\}}\right)^{2}\right]\\
\leq&e^{\theta x}(A+1)e^{\theta A+a}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k}\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta))i}.\end{split}

Proof.

Let
E(n,k,A):=E_{1}(n-k,Ae^{ak})\cap E_{2}(n-k,A+Lk+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)k)
. For fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
0\leq k\leq n-1
, by spinal decomposition theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{v\in E(n,k,A)\}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{\theta x}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(1_{\{\xi_{n-k}\in E(n,k,A)\}}\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{v\in E(n,k,A)\}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle e^{\theta x}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(1_{\{\xi_{n-k}\in E(n,k,A)\}}\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}\right).

(2.15)

For
u,v\in\mathbb{T}
, we write
u\prec v
or
v\succ u
, if
u
is the ancestor of
v
. By decomposing
(V(u):|u|\leq n-k)
along the spine, it is not hard to see

\begin{split}&\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}\\
=&e^{\theta V(\xi_{n-k})-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}\\
&\quad+\sum_{i=0}^{n-k-1}\sum_{j\geq 1\atop j\neq\xi^{(i+1)}_{n-k}}\sum_{|v|=n-k\atop v\succ\xi_{i}j}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{i})+V(\xi_{i}j)-V(\xi_{i})+V(v)-V(\xi_{i}j))-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)},\end{split}

which implies that

\begin{split}&\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(1_{\{\xi_{n-k}\in E(n,k,A)\}}\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}\right)\\
\leq&\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}(e^{\theta V(\xi_{n-k})-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{\xi_{n-k}\in B(n-k,k)\}})\\
&+\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n-k-1}\sum_{j\geq 1\atop j\neq\xi^{(i+1)}_{n-k}}\sum_{|v|=n-k\atop v\succ\xi_{i}j}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{i})+V(\xi_{i}j)-V(\xi_{i})+V(v)-V(\xi_{i}j))-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{\xi_{i}\in B(i,k)\}}\right),\end{split}

(2.16)

where
B(i,k):=\{|u|=i:V(u)-(k+i)\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)\leq L(k+i)+A,\sum_{j\geq 1}e^{\theta(V(uj)-V(u))}\leq Ae^{a(k+i+1)}\}.

By independence, for
0\leq i\leq n-k-1
,

\begin{split}&\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(\sum_{j\geq 1\atop j\neq\xi^{(i+1)}_{n-k}}\sum_{|v|=n-k\atop v\succ\xi_{i}j}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{i})+V(\xi_{i}j)-V(\xi_{i})+V(v)-V(\xi_{i}j))-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{\xi_{i}\in B(i,k)\}}\right)\\
=&\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(e^{\theta V(\xi_{i})-i\kappa(\theta)}\sum_{j\geq 1\atop j\neq\xi^{(i+1)}_{n-k}}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{i}j)-V(\xi_{i}))}1_{\{\xi_{i}\in B(i,k)\}}\right),\end{split}

which, combined with (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), implies that

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{v\in E(n,k,A)\}}\right)^{2}\\
\leq&e^{\theta x}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}(e^{\theta V(\xi_{n-k})-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{\xi_{n-k}\in B(n-k,k)\}})\\
&\quad+e^{\theta x}\sum_{i=0}^{n-k-1}\bar{\mathbb{E}}_{x}\left(e^{\theta V(\xi_{i})-i\kappa(\theta)}\sum_{j\geq 1}e^{\theta(V(\xi_{i}j)-V(\xi_{i}))}1_{\{\xi_{i}\in B(i,k)\}}\right)\\
\leq&e^{\theta x}(A+1)e^{\theta A+a}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k}\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta))i},\end{split}

which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Fix
\theta>0
such that
\kappa(\theta)<\infty
and
\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)<\kappa(\theta)
. Then for any
A>0
,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}((\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k}))^{2})=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
and
f\in C_{b}
, we know that

\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}^{2}(\theta))<\infty,

which yields that

\mathbb{E}((\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k}))^{2})=\mathbb{E}(\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}^{2}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})-(\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k}))^{2}).

(2.17)

Therefore, we shall compute
\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}^{2}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})
and
\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})^{2}
respectively. Recall that
E(n,k,A)=E_{1}(n-k,Ae^{ak})\cap E_{2}(n-k,A+Lk+\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)k)
.
By Markov property, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle(\bar{W}_{n,A}^{2}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{|u|=|v|=k,u\neq v}e^{\theta V(u)+\theta V(v)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u,v\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}H_{n,k,A}(V(u))H_{n,k,A}(V(v))

\displaystyle+\sum_{|u|=k}e^{2\theta V(u)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}G_{n,k,A}(V(u))

(2.18)

where for
x\in\mathbb{R}

H_{n,k,A}(x):=e^{-\theta x}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left(\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}f\left(\frac{V(v)-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)1_{\{v\in E(n,k,A)\}}\right)

and

G_{n,k,A}(x):=e^{-2\theta x}\mathbb{E}_{x}\left[\left(\sum_{|v|=n-k}e^{\theta V(v)-(n-k)\kappa(\theta)}f\left(\frac{V(v)-n\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)}{\sqrt{n}}\right)1_{\{v\in E(n,k,A)\}}\right)^{2}\right].

On the other hand, by Markov property again, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{|u|=k}e^{\theta V(u)-k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}H_{n,k,A}(V(u))\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{|u|=|v|=k,u\neq v}e^{\theta V(u)+\theta V(v)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u,v\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}H_{n,k,A}(V(u))H_{n,k,A}(V(v))

\displaystyle+\sum_{|u|=k}e^{2\theta V(u)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}(H_{n,k,A}(V(u)))^{2}.

(2.19)

Combining (
2.18
) with (
2.19
), we have

\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)^{2}|\mathscr{F}_{k})-\mathbb{E}(\bar{W}_{n,A}(\theta)|\mathscr{F}_{k})^{2}\\
\leq&\sum_{|u|=k}e^{2\theta V(u)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}G_{n,k,A}(V(u)).\end{split}

(2.20)

Recall that
A>0
,
a>0
,
L>0
such that
a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)<0
, by Lemma
2.6
, we conclude that

\begin{split}G_{n,k,A}(x)\leq&\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}(A+1)e^{\theta A-\theta x+a}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k}\sum_{i=0}^{n-k}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta))i}\\
\leq&\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}(A+1)e^{\theta A-\theta x+a}}{1-e^{a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)}}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k}=C(A,f)e^{-\theta x}e^{(a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta))k}\end{split}

where
C(A,f):=\frac{\|f\|_{\infty}^{2}Ae^{\theta A+a}}{1-e^{a+\theta L+\theta\kappa^{\prime}(\theta)-\kappa(\theta)}}
.
Thus, we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum_{|u|=k}e^{2\theta V(u)-2k\kappa(\theta)}1_{\{u\in E_{1}(k,A)\cap E_{2}(k,A)\}}G_{n,k,A}(V(u))\right)\\

) and (

\lim_{y\to\infty}\mathbb{P}(\max\mathcal{E}\leq y)=1,

which completes the proof.
∎

.

According to (

), for

\mathbb{P}(\max\mathcal{E}\leq y)=\mathbb{E}(e^{-\mathcal{E}^{(1)}(w_{y})}),

(4.9)

where

where the second equality follows from a change of variables

(4.10)

where

4.3
. By direct computation, for any

, which implies that for

,

, we have

, which yields that

\lim_{z\to 0+}\mathbb{E}(e^{-z\mathcal{E}(\phi)})=1,

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}((y,\infty))<\infty)=1
. According to Lemma 4.4 in

]

Exact non-Lagrangian Schur index in closed form
Yiwen Pan 1, Peihe Yang 2

Abstract
The Schur index is a powerful tool to probe the spectrum and dualities of 4d 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 superconformal field theories (SCFTs), deeply related to 2d vertex operator algebras (VOAs). In this paper, we compute the Schur index in closed form for two series of non-Lagrangian theories. We explore and classify the Argyres-Douglas (AD) theories D p b ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 N , [ Y ] ) D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y]) realized as the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) gauging of two AD matter theories, where we identify several infinite families with interesting central charge relations analogous to the a 4d = c 4d a_{\text{4d}}=c_{\text{4d}} of 𝒩 = 4 \mathcal{N}=4 theories. We focus on D N − 4 ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) , [ N − 4 , 4 ] ) D_{N-4}(\mathfrak{sl}(N),[N-4,4]) and D N − 2 ​ ( 𝔰 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( N ) , [ N − 3 , 3 ] ) D_{N-2}(\mathfrak{sl}(N),[N-3,3]) , and compute their flavored and unflavored Schur and Wilson line indices in compact form. We also explore their large- N N behavior, and show that they arise as special limits of the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) SU(2) SQCD flavored index, also analogous to the relation among the a 4d = c 4d a_{\text{4d}}=c_{\text{4d}} theories. We also generalize the elliptic function integration formula in the presence of higher order poles to compute in closed form the partially flavored indices of the Minahan-Nemeschansky E 6 E_{6} and E 7 E_{7} theories. Our results point to a universal structure underlying the residues of elliptic integrands, Wilson loop indices, and non-vacuum modules of the corresponding VOAs.

Introduction

The superconformal index is a fundamental observable to analyze and characterize 4d
\mathcal{N}=2
superconformal field theories (SCFTs). It encodes the representation-theoretic structure of the protected local operator spectrum. The index admits various simplifying limits, among which is the Schur limit
Gadde:2011uv

\mathcal{N}=2

There are several ways of computing the Schur index. For Lagrangian theories, the index is written as a contour integral of a multivariate elliptic function, where the integrand represents the Schur index in the zero-coupling limit, and the contour integral implements the gauging by projecting onto the gauge-invariant states. The integrand can be expanded in
q
-series, and then perform the contour integral term wise, resulting in a
q

Unphysical theories from the Schur index

There is a physical consistency requirement arising from the structure of the Schur index.
Recall that the Schur index involves the plethystic exponential of

\frac{\sum_{j}q^{1+j}\chi_{R_{j}}(z)-q^{h^{\vee}+k}\sum_{j}q^{-j}\chi_{R_{j}}(z)}{(1-q)(1-q^{h^{\vee}+k})}\ .

(39)

The sum over spin-
j
can be split into two parts,

\sum_{j}\frac{q^{1+j}-q^{p-j}}{1-q}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}(z)=\sum_{j\geq p}\frac{q^{1+j}-q^{p-j}}{1-q}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}(z)+\sum_{j<p}\frac{q^{1+j}-q^{p-j}}{1-q}\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}(z)\ .

(40)

The latter sum can be expanded as

=\sum_{j<p}(q^{1+j}+q^{2+j}+\cdots+q^{p-2j-2})\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}(z)\ ,

(41)

while the former

=-\sum_{j\geq p}q^{p-j}(q^{0}+q^{1}+\cdots+q^{2j-p})\chi_{\mathcal{R}_{j}}(z)\ .

(42)

Each integer spin
j\geq p
contributes one
\frac{-1}{1-q^{p}}
in the plethystic exponential, leading to a factor

\operatorname{PE}\Big[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{N}\\
j\geq p\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{-1}{1-q^{p}}\Big]=(1;q^{p})^{\#\text{integer spin $j\geq p$}}=0\ ,

(43)

which is unphysical.

Now consider a general regular puncture specified by

r\geq h\geq\cdots.
The fundamental representation of

\chi_{\square}^{SU(N)}=\chi_{\frac{r-1}{2}}^{SU(2)}\chi_{\square}^{SU(m)}+\cdots.

(44)

Consequently, the adjoint representation necessarily contains a factor with maximal spin-
j

\chi_{\frac{r-1}{2}}^{SU(2)}\chi_{\frac{r-1}{2}}^{SU(2)}=\chi_{r-1}^{SU(2)}+\cdots.

(45)

Therefore, the consistency requirement becomes

p>r-1.

3.6
Exact Formula for the
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])
Theory

In this subsection we briefly discuss the Schur index of the more general
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])
theory, where
[Y]
is a generic regular puncture. Recall from (
10
) that a generic
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])
theory is dual to a type I Argyres–Douglas theory together with a set of free hypermultiplets. For later convenience, we record here the duality relation explicitly:

\displaystyle D_{p}^{b}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y]\right)\cong

\displaystyle\ D_{p}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{(N-b)p+b},[(p-1)^{N-b},Y]\right)

\displaystyle\ \qquad\otimes(N-b)\sum_{i=p}^{N}(i-p+1)\ell_{i}\text{ free hypermultiplets }.

(131)

The right-hand side includes free hypermultiplets originating from the breaking of flavor symmetry after choosing the general regular puncture
[Y]
. In the special case of the full puncture
[Y]=[1^{N}]
, the duality simplifies to

D_{p}^{b}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{N}\right)\cong D_{p}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{(N-b)p+b},[(p-1)^{N-b},1^{N}]\right).

(132)

Here the right-hand side is a type I Argyres–Douglas theory
D_{p^{\prime}}(\mathfrak{sl}(N^{\prime}),[r^{m},1^{N^{\prime}-rm}])
, where the relevant data are given by

b^{\prime}=N^{\prime}=(N-b)p+b,\quad k^{\prime}=p-b,\quad r=(p-1),\quad m=N-b.

(133)

We would like to compute the Schur index of
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])
. A convenient approach exploits the TQFT nature of the Schur index
Gadde:2011ik
;
Gadde:2011uv
;
Gadde:2009kb
;
Gaiotto:2012xa
. From the TQFT perspective, the Schur index is expressed as a 2d
q
-deformed Yang-Mills correlator

\mathcal{I}_{D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])}(q,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}^{p,b,N}(q)\psi_{\lambda}^{Y}(\boldsymbol{z})\ ,

(134)

where
\psi^{p,b,N}_{\lambda}(q)
denotes the wavefunction of the irregular puncture, and the wavefunction associated with a generic regular puncture is given by

\psi_{\lambda}^{Y}(\boldsymbol{a})=\mathrm{PE}\left[\sum_{j}\frac{q^{j+1}}{1-q}\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}(\boldsymbol{a})\right]\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}}).

(135)

Specializing to
[Y]=[1^{N}]
, using (
132
), the left hand side of (
134
) is known by analyzing the type I Schur index on the right, and one can solve the wavefunction
\psi^{p,b,N}_{\lambda}(q)
of the irregular puncture. Then we can combine
\psi^{p,b,N}_{\lambda}(q)
with the known wavefunction of the chosen regular puncture to deduce the Schur index of
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])
.

For simplicity, we focus on the case with
\gcd(p,b)=1
, although the idea certainly extends beyond this case. In this setting, the theory
D_{p}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{(N-b)p+b},[(p-1)^{N-b},1^{N}]\right)
contains no mass parameters in the irregular puncture and is completely determined by the formula
(
3.1
). The general Schur index of
D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})
takes the form

\mathcal{I}_{D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N})}(q,\boldsymbol{z})=\sum_{\lambda}\psi_{\lambda}^{\mathrm{irregular}}{(q)}\psi_{\lambda}^{\text{full}}(\boldsymbol{z})\ ,

(136)

where

\psi_{\lambda}^{\text{full}}(\boldsymbol{z})=\mathrm{PE}\left[\frac{q}{1-q}\chi_{\mathrm{adj}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right]\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}).

(137)

Denote the Schur index of
D_{p}\left(\mathfrak{sl}_{(N-b)p+b},[(p-1)^{N-b},1^{N}]\right)
by
\mathcal{I}(q,\boldsymbol{z})
. Then the irregular puncture contributes

\psi_{\lambda}^{p,b,N}{(q)}=\oint[d\boldsymbol{z}]\mathrm{PE}\left[-\frac{q}{1-q}\chi_{\mathrm{adj}}(\boldsymbol{z})\right]\mathcal{I}(q,\boldsymbol{z})\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z})\ .

(138)

Substituting
\psi^{p,b,N}_{\lambda}(q)
into (
136
), and using the identity

\sum_{\lambda}\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z})\,\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime})=\frac{1}{\Delta_{J}(\boldsymbol{z})}\,\delta(\boldsymbol{z}-\boldsymbol{z}^{\prime}),

(139)

with respect to the Haar measure
\Delta_{J}(\boldsymbol{z})
, we obtain the wave function associated with a generic regular puncture specified by the Young diagram
Y

\psi^{Y}_{\lambda}(\mathbf{a})=\mathrm{PE}\left[\sum_{j}\frac{q^{j+1}}{1-q}\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}(\boldsymbol{a})\right]\chi_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}})\ .

(140)

We find the
\mathcal{I}_{D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])}
to be simply

\mathcal{I}_{D_{p}^{b}(\mathfrak{sl}_{N},[Y])}=\mathrm{PE}\left[\sum_{j}\frac{q^{j+1}}{1-q}\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}(\boldsymbol{a})\right]\mathrm{PE}\left[-\frac{q}{1-q}\chi_{\mathrm{adj}}(\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}})\right]\mathcal{I}(q,\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}}).

(141)

Recalling
\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}}

(142)

\displaystyle\mathrm{PE}\left[-\sum_{j}\frac{q}{1-q}\mathrm{Tr}_{R_{j}}(\boldsymbol{a})\sum_{m=-j}^{j-1}q^{m}\right]\mathcal{I}(q,\boldsymbol{a}q^{\Lambda_{Y}}).

4.1

The Schur index of the
D_{\frac{N}{2}-2}(\mathfrak{sl}(\frac{N}{2}-4),[\frac{N}{2}-6,1,1])
and

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{I}_{D_{\frac{N}{2}-2}(\mathfrak{sl}(\frac{N}{2}-4),[\frac{N}{2}-6,1,1])}

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{PE}\bigg[\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2})}\Big((q-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2})\,\chi_{\text{adj}}^{SU(2)}(a)+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{N}{2}-6}(q^{i}-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2-i+1})\Big)\bigg]

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{I}_{D_{\frac{N}{2}-2}(\mathfrak{sl}(\frac{N}{2}),[\frac{N}{2}-2,1,1])}

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{PE}\bigg[\frac{1}{(1-q)(1-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2})}\Big((q-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2})\,\chi_{\text{adj}}^{SU(2)}(a)+\sum_{i=1}^{\frac{N}{2}-2}(q^{i}-q^{\frac{N}{2}-2-i+1})\Big)\bigg]

6.1
Integration formula

In this section, we will compute the partially flavored Schur index for the Minahan-Nemeschansky (MN) theories with
E_{6},E_{7}
flavor symmetry, exploiting the generalized partition formula proposed in
Deb:2025ypl
. The formula inevitably involves contour integrals of elliptic functions with higher order poles, which is beyond the scope of the integration formula in
Pan:2021mrw
. We will first generalize the integration formula to higher order poles. In this subsection we first generalized the formula to accommodate higher order poles, and then apply it to compute the Schur index of the MN theories in the next subsection.

Consider an elliptic function of
\mathfrak{a}
with double periodicity,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}+n\tau)=\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}+1)=\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})\ .

(248)

Here for generality we assume a periodicity
\tilde{\tau}\coloneqq n\tau
in the variable
\mathfrak{a}
with some positive integer
n
instead of
\tau
; we also denote
\tilde{q}\coloneqq q^{n}
. Moreover, the integrand may have
n_{j}
-th order poles
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
inside the fundamental parallelogram bounded by the vertices
0,\tilde{\tau},1,\tilde{\tau}+1
. Following the notation in
Pan:2021mrw
, we call
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
a real pole when
\mathfrak{a}_{j}\in[0,1)
, and imaginary if otherwise.

The integrand
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})
can be expanded using Weierstrass
\zeta
functions,

\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})=C(\tilde{\tau})+\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{+\infty}R_{j,m}\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})\ ,

(249)

where
C(\tilde{\tau})
is a function independent of the variable
\mathfrak{a}
, and we define the generalized residues
R_{j,m}
,

R_{j,m}\coloneqq\operatorname{Res}_{\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{j}}\bigg[2\pi i\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})^{m-1}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})\bigg]\ .

(250)

Note that
R_{j,m}=0
when
m
is larger than the order
n_{j}
of the pole
\mathfrak{a}_{j}
, hence the above is a finite sum over
\zeta
functions. Also note that

\sum_{j}R_{j,m}=0\qquad\text{when $m$ is odd}\ .

(251)

The Weierstrass functions
\zeta(\mathfrak{a}|\tilde{\tau})
can be expanded in Fourier series,

\displaystyle\zeta(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})=

\displaystyle\ -4\pi^{2}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})E_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\mp\pi i+\pi\sum_{n}^{\prime}\frac{1}{\sin n\pi\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{\mp\frac{n}{2}}e^{2\pi in(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})}

(252)

\displaystyle\zeta^{(1)}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})=

\displaystyle\ -4\pi^{2}E_{2}(\tilde{\tau})+\pi\sum_{n}^{\prime}\frac{2\pi in}{\sin n\pi\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{\mp\frac{n}{2}}e^{2\pi in(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})}

(253)

\displaystyle\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})=

\displaystyle\ +\pi\sum_{n}^{\prime}\frac{(2\pi in)^{m-1}}{\sin n\pi\tilde{\tau}}\tilde{q}^{\mp\frac{n}{2}}e^{2\pi in(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})},\qquad m\geq 3\ ,

(254)

where the
\mp
corresponds to real/imaginary poles. The Fourier series can then be used to derive the integration formula, giving

\displaystyle\oint\frac{da}{2\pi ia}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})=C(\tilde{\tau})+\sum_{\text{real/img}~\mathfrak{a}_{j}}R_{j,1}\Big(\mp\frac{1}{2}-2\pi i\mathfrak{a}_{j}E_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\Big)+2\pi iE_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\sum_{\text{real/img}~\mathfrak{a}_{j}}R_{j,2}\ .

We can compute
C(\tilde{\tau})
from any reference point
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
,

C(\tilde{\tau})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}\sum_{m\geq 1}R_{j,m}\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})\bigg|_{\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{0}}\ .

(255)

It is often convenient to choose
\mathfrak{a}_{0}
such that
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})=0
to get rid of the first term.

Analogous to
Pan:2021mrw
, we may rewrite the results in terms of Eisenstein series. The Weierstrass
\zeta
function is related to the Eisenstein series by

\displaystyle\zeta(\mathfrak{z}|\tilde{\tau})\pm\pi i+4\pi^{2}\mathfrak{z}E_{2}(\tilde{\tau})=2\pi iE_{1}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
zq^{\mp 1/2}\end{bmatrix}(\tilde{\tau})\ ,

(256)

which hepls simplify the final result,

\displaystyle\oint\frac{da}{2\pi ia}

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})+\sum_{j}R_{j,1}E_{1}\begin{bmatrix}-1\\
a_{j}/a_{0}q^{\pm\frac{1}{2}}\end{bmatrix}(\tilde{\tau})

(257)

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}R_{j,2}\bigg(\zeta^{\prime}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})+4\pi^{2}E_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\bigg)-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}\sum_{m>2}R_{j,m}\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})\ .

The
\zeta^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z}|\tau)
in the second line can be rewritten in Eisenstein series,

\zeta^{\prime}(\mathfrak{z}|\tau)=4\pi^{2}\bigg(E_{1}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
z\end{bmatrix}(\tau)^{2}+2E_{2}\begin{bmatrix}1\\
z\end{bmatrix}(\tau)\bigg)\ .

(258)

The subsequent derivatives
\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{z}|\tau)
can also be reorganized in terms of Eisenstein series, but we shall not spell out here.

Alternatively, one can stick to the Weierstrass
\zeta
function expression, and write

\displaystyle\oint\frac{da}{2\pi ia}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})-\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}\sum_{m=1}^{n_{j}}R_{j,m}\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})

\displaystyle\ +\frac{1}{2\pi i}\sum_{j}R_{j,1}(\pm\pi i)-2\pi iE_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\sum_{j}R_{j,1}\mathfrak{a}_{j}+2\pi iE_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\sum_{j}R_{j,2}\ .

(259)

Using the definition of standard residue, the generalized residue can be written as

R_{j,m}=2\pi i\frac{(-1)^{m-1}}{(m-1)!}\frac{1}{(n_{j}-m)!}\frac{d^{n_{j}-m}}{da^{n_{j}-m}}\bigg|_{\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{j}}\Big[(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})^{n_{j}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})\Big]\ .

(260)

Also note that

\zeta^{(m-1)}(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}_{j}|\tilde{\tau})=(-1)^{m-1}\frac{d^{m-1}}{d\mathfrak{a}^{m-1}}\zeta(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}|\tilde{\tau})

(261)

Putting these ingredients together, the integral can be written simply as combinations of derivatives of the integrand
\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})
,

\displaystyle\oint\frac{da}{2\pi ia}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})-\sum_{j}\frac{1}{(n_{j}-1)!}\frac{d^{n_{j}-1}}{d\mathfrak{a}^{n_{j}-1}}\bigg|_{\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}_{j}}\Big[(\mathfrak{a}-\mathfrak{a}_{j})^{n_{j}}\mathcal{Z}(\mathfrak{a})\zeta(\mathfrak{a}_{0}-\mathfrak{a}|\tilde{\tau})\Big]

\displaystyle\ -\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j}(\pm)R_{j,1}-2\pi iE_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\sum_{j}R_{j,1}\mathfrak{a}_{j}+2\pi iE_{2}(\tilde{\tau})\sum_{j}R_{j,2}\ .

(262)

Here
\pm\pi i=+\pi i
for a real pole, and
-\pi i
for an imaginary pole.

Finally, in the presence of a monomial, the integration of Fourier series is fairly simple, giving

\oint\frac{da}{2\pi ia}a^{k}f(\mathfrak{a})=\sum_{j}\sum_{m\geq 1}R_{j,m}\frac{1}{\tilde{q}^{k/2}-\tilde{q}^{-k/2}}(2\pi ik)^{m-1}\tilde{q}^{\pm\frac{k}{2}}a_{j}^{k}\ .

(263)

This formula can be used to compute the Wilson line index.

Low-energy α-harmonic maps into the round sphere
Authors: Ben Sharp
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe classify low-energy α-harmonic maps from a closed non-spherical Riemannian surface Σ of constant curvature to the round sphere via their bubble scales and centres. In particular we show that as 1<α↓1 and assuming Eα is close to |Σ|+4π then degree-one α-harmonic maps blow a bubble based at a critical point ac of a an explicit function J and at scale |J(ac)|-1(α-1). Up to a constant, J is the sum of the squares of any L2-orthonormal basis of holomorphic one-forms on the domain.

Introduction

One of the founding pillars of geometric analysis concerns the existence and regularity of harmonic maps. Given two Riemannian manifolds M, N, a harmonic map u∈W1,2(M,N) is a critical point (in some suitable sense) of the Dirichlet energy

When M=Σ is two-dimensional this functional is invariant under conformal deformations of the domain which leads to a wonderfully rich theory from both an analytic and geometric viewpoint. In broad strokes this functional does not enjoy straightforward regularity properties, nor does it satisfy a Palais–Smale condition. These difficulties lead to both interesting and challenging mathematical problems. Remarkably critical points are always smooth in two dimensions thanks to Hélein’s moving frame approach [7, 8]; the resulting estimates hold only up to a potentially small regularity scale r(u,N,Σ)>0 whereupon the Dirichlet energy on any ball of radius r is below some threshold. Along a sequence of smooth maps uk (for instance) it is possible for rk to shrink to zero (i.e. for the Dirichlet energy to concentrate) and all the derivatives to blow up. Suitably re-scaling the domain to reverse the energy concentration leads to a sequence of harmonic maps defined on larger and larger regions of R2 which converge to a harmonic sphere ω:R2∪{∞}=S2→N.

Starting with the famous work of Sacks–Uhlenbeck [24] α-harmonic maps have been used to study existence theory for harmonic maps in two-dimensions via a regularised functional Eα and α>1. For α>1 fixed, Eα crucially satisfies the Palais–Smale condition, whilst all finite-energy critical points (α-harmonic maps) are uniformly regular, up to a regularity scale r=r(α,Eα(u),N,Σ) with smooth estimates which potentially degenerate (r→0), as α↓1, on regions where the Dirichlet energy concentrates. Nevertheless, it is well-known that harmonic maps appear as weak W1,2-limits of α-harmonic maps, as α↓1, modulo the appearance of singularities, again taking the form of harmonic spheres (bubbles) and geodesics of length ℓ∈[0,∞] (necks) which connect the weak limit with the bubbles. An ideal outcome would be that all the Dirichlet energy is captured by the limiting harmonic map and bubbles (an energy identity) or further that all necks have zero length (a no-neck result). In general both of these ideals are known to fail during the limiting process for α-harmonic maps: examples of (infinite-length) necks appearing and energy-loss can be found in [16]. Still, one can use analytical methods to find α-harmonic maps (direct minimisation, or min-max procedures) and in turn hope to recover relevant information on harmonic maps, as α↓1 see e.g. the seminal works of Sacks–Uhlenbeck [24] and Micallef–Moore [18].

Natural and important questions present themselves: how precisely does this singular convergence happen and can one recover all harmonic maps from surfaces as a limit of α-harmonic maps?

When the target has positive Ricci curvature and the sequence of α-harmonic maps has finite Morse index, it is known that only finite-length geodesic necks appear and all energy is accounted for in the limiting process [15]. For arbitrary closed targets and certain sequences of α-harmonic maps obtained by min-max methods, similarly, all energy is accounted for in the limit by the work of Lamm [11], although it is not known whether or not necks might appear. If the target is a round sphere Sn, thanks to Jiayu Li and Xiangrong Zhu we know more: all energy is accounted for and the convergence happens in L∞ (no necks appear at all) [14]. See also [2] where this is proven for arbitrary homogeneous targets.

When considering maps between round two-spheres u:S2→S2 it is well-known that, up to reversing orientations, the only harmonic maps are holomorphic and therefore rational maps from the extended complex plane to itself (see e.g. the Theorem of Wood–Lemaire [5, 11.3] for a more general result). Thus if we restrict to degree one harmonic maps, we are left only with the Möbius maps and their complex conjugates. Remarkably the question “are all harmonic maps attained as limits of α-harmonic maps” has only recently received attention. Lamm, Malchiodi and Micallef show that all degree ±1 α-harmonic maps u:S2→S2 with α-energy lower than 8α2π (Dirichlet energy lower than 12π), and α close to 1, must be rotations [12, 13]. Thus in particular the majority of degree-one harmonic maps between spheres are not reached by smooth limits of α-harmonic maps. Indeed they essentially classify all low-energy α-harmonic maps u:S2→S2 of degree 0 or ±1, see [13].

In this article we study α-harmonic maps u:Σ→S2, from non-spherical closed surfaces Σ, which have α-energy bounded above by 4π+|Σ|+δ for δ>0 small (in particular Dirichlet energy bounded above by 4π+δ) and α close to 1. The energy bound immediately tells us that the topological degrees of these maps are either 0 or ±1.

Examples of such degree ±1 low-energy α-harmonic maps are guaranteed to exist by direct methods and we show that any such map must be quantitatively C1-close, with bounds purely in terms of α, to a finite-dimensional space of singularity models Z where each z∈Z approximates a single degree ±1 bubble and constant-body map configuration. In particular we obtain estimates on both the bubble scale and bubble proximity purely in terms of (α-1) and geometric data J:Σ→R.

In this article we restrict N=S2 and study the behaviour of Eα in a neighbourhood of Z - the restriction of the target to a sphere is necessary in order to have the favourable bubble-convergence results of Li and Zhu [14], which are unavailable (and untrue) for arbitrary targets. This then provides the technical benefit that the elements of Z are totally explicit. The flavour of the main results are rather different in the sense that α-harmonic maps are guaranteed to exist, so our focus is very much on gleaning their properties as opposed to a quantitative non-existence result.

Outline of the paper The proofs of the main Theorems (see Sect. 3) rely heavily upon a careful analysis of the behaviour of Eα when restricted to the manifold Z (and in small neighbourhoods thereof). In Sect. 2 we recall the space of singularity models Z and expand the α-energy along Z for λ large. We recall also the crucial background results concerning α-harmonic maps into round spheres [14] which we combine with suitable elliptic estimates to prove first that the α-harmonic maps satisfying our hypotheses are either analytically well-controlled or lie close to Z (Lemmata 2.10–2.12). We also recall the desired non-degeneracy of energy in normal directions which essentially follows from a similar estimate in [22] (cf [17]): this comparison relies heavily on the known theory for α-harmonic maps into spheres, see Lemma 2.13. In Sect. 4 we derive an expansion in λ and (α-1) for ∂λEα(z) and ∇A(Eα(z)) along the submanifold Z. The key point of these expansions are that properties of J determine the behaviour of the Eα-energy profile when restricted to Z. In Sect. 5 we obtain control on dEα and d2Eα when we are on and nearby Z—these estimates are crucially of “lower-order” than those appearing in Sect. 4 which allows us to pass information on the behaviour of Eα on Z to the behaviour of Eα in a neighbourhood of Z. We provide the proofs of our main Theorems in Sect. 3 assuming the technical expansions/estimates from Sects. 4 and 5.

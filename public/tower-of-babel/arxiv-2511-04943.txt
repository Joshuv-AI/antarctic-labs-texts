Positive solutions of elliptic systems with superlinear nonlinearities on the boundary Thanks: Corresponding author: M. Chhetri (m_chhetr@uncg.edu)
Shalmali Bandyopadhyay, Maya Chhetri, Briceyda B. Delgado, Nsoki Mavinga, Rosa Pardo

Abstract
. We consider elliptic systems with superlinear and subcritical boundary conditions and a bifurcation parameter as a multiplicative factor. By combining the rescaling method with degree theory and elliptic regularity theory, we prove the existence of a connected branch of positive weak solutions that bifurcates from infinity as the parameter approaches zero. Furthermore, under additional conditions on the nonlinearities near zero, we obtain a global connected branch of positive solutions bifurcating from zero, which possesses a unique bifurcation point from infinity when the parameter is zero. Finally, we analyze the behavior of this branch and discuss the number of positive weak solutions with respect to the parameter using bifurcation theory, degree theory, and sub- and super-solution methods.



Keywords:
Elliptic systems; superlinear and subcritical; nonlinear boundary condition; bifurcation theory; degree theory; monotonicity methods.

35J57, 35J65, 35J61, 35B32

1.
Introduction

Consider the following linear system with nonlinear boundary conditions of the form

\left.\begin{array}[]{rclll}-\Delta u_{1}+u_{1}&=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,&&\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\eta}&=\lambda f_{1}(u_{2})\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,;\\
-\Delta u_{2}+u_{2}&=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,&&\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\eta}&=\lambda f_{2}(u_{1})\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,;\end{array}\right\}

(1.1)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
(
N>2
) is a bounded domain with a
C^{2,\gamma}
boundary
\partial\Omega
(with
0<\gamma<1
),
\partial/\partial\eta:=\eta(x)\cdot\nabla
denotes the outer normal derivative on
\partial\Omega
, and
\lambda>0
is a bifurcation parameter. The nonlinear reaction terms
f_{i}\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are locally Hölder continuous functions, that is, given any positive constant
M_{0}
, there exists
L
such that
|f_{i}(s)-f_{i}(t)|\leq L|s-t|^{\gamma}
for all
s,t\in[-M_{0},M_{0}]
and
0<\gamma\leq 1
, for
i=1,2
.

The goal of this work is to investigate the number of positive solutions of (
1.1
) with respect to the bifurcation parameter
\lambda
. It is well known that the asymptotic behaviors of the nonlinearities near the origin and/or at infinity influence the number of solutions with respect to
\lambda
. Here, the focus will be on the case when
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
are superlinear and subcritical at infinity, that is, there exist constants
b_{i}>0
(
i=1,2
) such that

\begin{cases}\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{f_{1}(s)}{s^{p_{2}}}=b_{2},\quad\lim_{s\rightarrow\infty}\dfrac{f_{2}(s)}{s^{p_{1}}}=b_{1},\\
1<p_{1}\,,p_{2}\leq\frac{N}{N-2}\quad\mbox{but not both equal to}\quad\frac{N}{N-2}\,.\end{cases}

(
{\rm H}_{\infty}
)

We are concerned with the existence, multiplicity and bifurcation results for positive weak solutions of (
1.1
) in
H^{1}(\Omega)\times H^{1}(\Omega)
with respect to the parameter
\lambda
. However, we show that if
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
satisfy the hypothesis (

H
∞

) and
(u_{1},u_{2})
is a weak solution, then
u_{1},\,u_{2}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, see Theorem
2.2
.
Thus, we can define our solution set by

\Sigma:=\{\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)\in(0,\infty)\times\big(C(\overline{\Omega})\big)^{2}\colon(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2}))\mbox{ is a weak solution to }\eqref{pde}\}\,,

(1.2)

and look for solutions
\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)\in\Sigma.

Throughout the paper, the norm on the underlying function space
(C(\overline{\Omega}))^{2}
is given by

\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})^{2}}:=\|u_{1}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}+\|u_{2}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\,.

(1.3)

Furthermore,
\mu_{1}>0
will denote the first eigenvalue of the Steklov eigenvalue problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta\psi+\psi&=&0&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial\eta}&=&\mu\psi&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,,\end{array}\right.

(1.4)

with corresponding eigenfunction
\varphi_{1}>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
and
\|\varphi_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}=1
, see
[
20
, Prop. 2.3 and Rem. 2.4]
.

Our first result shows that by assuming only the superlinear subcritical behaviors at infinity by the nonlinearities
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
, positive solutions are guaranteed for
\lambda
small.

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose that (

H
∞

) holds. Then, there exists
\widetilde{\lambda}>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\widetilde{\lambda}]
, (
1.1
) has a positive weak solution
\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)
such that

\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})^{2}}\rightarrow\infty\qquad\mbox{as}\quad\lambda\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Moreover, there exists a connected component of positive weak solutions of (
1.1
), namely
\mathscr{C}^{+}\subset\Sigma
, bifurcating from infinity at
\lambda=0
, such that
the projection of
\mathscr{C}^{+}
on the parameter space is
(0,\widetilde{\lambda}].

For results concerning the corresponding scalar elliptic problems with nonlinear boundary conditions, see for instance
[
5
,
17
]
and references therein.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
is motivated by the re-scaling argument of
[
1
,
3
]
for the single equation case and extension of their approach to a coupled system of equations in
[
8
]
, with Dirichlet boundary conditions.
This rescaling method with a multiplicative parameter,
transforms the original system into a limiting problem with pure-power nonlinearities as the multiplicative parameter approaches zero. We incorporate this re-scaling method combined with Leray-Schauder degree theory to prove the result.

For our second result, in addition to (

H
∞

), we impose the following conditions on
f_{i}
with
i=1,2
at zero to further guarantee bifurcation from the line of trivial solutions:

\begin{cases}f_{i}\in C^{1}\\
f_{i}(0)=0,f_{i}^{\prime}(0)>0,\\
\mbox{ and there exists a constant }\nu_{i}>1\mbox{ such that}\\
\ f_{i}(s)=f_{i}^{\prime}(0)s+\mathcal{R}_{i}(s)\mbox{ for }s\geq 0\mbox{ with }\mathcal{R}_{i}(s)=O(s^{\nu_{i}})\mbox{ as }s\rightarrow 0.\end{cases}

(
{\rm H}_{0}
)

We obtain the following global bifurcation result.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
f_{i}
satisfy (

H
0

) and (

H
∞

), and there exists
K>0
such that

f_{i}(s)\geq Ks,\quad\text{ for all }\quad s\geq 0,\quad i=1,2.

(1.5)

Then there exists a connected component
\mathscr{C}^{+}\subset\Sigma
of positive weak solution to (
1.1
), emanating from the trivial solution at the bifurcation point
\big(\mu_{0},(0,0)\big)\in\Sigma
, where

\mu_{0}:=\dfrac{\mu_{1}}{\sqrt{f_{1}^{\prime}(0)f_{2}^{\prime}(0)}},

(1.6)

and possessing a unique bifurcation point from infinity at

. More precisely, if
\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)\in\mathscr{C}^{+}
, then the following holds:

\begin{cases}\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|\to 0\ \ &\hskip-227.62204pt\mbox{ as }\lambda\to\mu_{0},\\
\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|\to\infty&\hskip-227.62204pt\mbox{ as }\lambda\to 0^{+}\,,\text{and}\\

Moreover, for

To prove Theorem

, we establish a version of Crandall-Rabinowitz’s local bifurcation result
[

]
to obtain

, and of Rabinowitz’s global bifurcation result
[

]

Bifurcation from infinity
result by Rabinowitz
[
23
]

applies to problems involving nonlinearities that are asymptotically linear at infinity, that is, when the nonlinear part is a small perturbation of the linear part. This result do not apply to our problem, and hence we obtain bifurcation from infinity by following the approach via re-scaling technique in
[
3
,
1
]
.

We refer the reader to, for instance
[
7
]
for bifurcation from zero results, and
[
4
]
for bifurcation from infinity results.

Finally, concerning the multiplicity of solutions, we define the following quantities which are essential for determining the direction of bifurcation of weak solutions near the bifurcation point. Let
\nu:=\min\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2}\}>1
and define

\underline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}:=\liminf_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{R}_{i}(s)}{s^{\nu}}\quad\text{ and }\quad\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}:=\limsup_{s\rightarrow 0^{+}}\dfrac{\mathcal{R}_{i}(s)}{s^{\nu}},\quad i=1,2.

(1.8)

Using the hypothesis (

H
0

), one can verify that
-\infty<\underline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}\leq\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}<+\infty
.
Now, define

\begin{array}[]{rlcc}\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{\zeta}{1+\zeta}\right)^{\nu-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\dfrac{1}{1+\zeta}\right)^{\nu-1}\,\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{2}\,,\end{array}

(1.9)

where
\zeta:=\sqrt{\frac{f_{2}^{\prime}(0)}{f_{1}^{\prime}(0)}}
. Similarly, we can define
\underline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}
by substituting
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}
by
\underline{\mathcal{R}}_{i}
for
i=1,2
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that assumptions of Theorem
1.2
hold, and that
f_{i}
’s are monotonically non-decreasing. If
\overline{\mathcal{R}}_{0}<0
, then (
1.1
) has at least two positive weak solutions in
\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right),
where

\overline{\lambda}:=\sup\Big\{\lambda\colon\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)\in\mathscr{C}^{+}\Big\}\leq\frac{\mu_{1}}{K}\,.

(1.10)

Moreover, (
1.1
) has at least one weak positive solution for
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}
and also for
\lambda=\mu_{0}
.

To prove this multiplicity result, we use degree theory and the sub- and supersolution method. We apply the sub- and supersolution method for the cooperative system established in
[
6
]
, hence we require the monotonicity assumption in Theorem
1.3
.

The scalar versions of Theorem
1.1
-Theorem
1.3
are considered in
[
5
]
. The extension to the strongly coupled systems considered in this work presents technical challenges. The bootstrap argument for the regularity result in Theorem
2.2
demonstrates the challenges present due to the coupling since the analysis requires dealing with different Lebesgue spaces. Moreover, the bifurcation analysis for Theorem

, requires a Jordan matrix formulation to characterize the linearization satisfying the Crandall–Rabinowitz transversality condition.
See also
[
12
,
15
,
14
]
, where a similar linearization approach was utilized for coupled system of equations.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish preliminary results including regularity and positivity of weak solutions, and include a uniform
a priori
bounds result of
[
13
]
. In particular, we prove that weak solutions are classical solutions through a bootstrap argument in Theorem

. Section

is devoted to the proof of Theorem

. In Section

2.
Preliminaries and Auxiliary Results

In this section, we discuss the regularity and positivity of weak solutions of (
1.1
) and state a uniform
a priori
bounds result. Our main result in this section is to prove that weak solutions are, in fact, classical solutions; see Theorem
2.2
, which may be of independent interest. The proof is achieved through a bootstrap argument.

2.1.
Regularity of weak solutions and positivity

First, we discuss the trace operator to deal with the boundary terms.
The
trace
operator

\Gamma\colon W^{1,m}(\Omega)\rightarrow L^{r}(\partial\Omega),\quad\Gamma(u):=u|_{\partial\Omega}\,,

(2.1)

ensures that for every
u\in W^{1,m}(\Omega)
the trace
\Gamma u
is well defined, that is,

\begin{cases}\Gamma u\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for }r\leq\frac{(N-1)m}{N-m}&\text{if }m<N,\\
\Gamma u\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for any }r\geq 1&\text{if }m=N,\\
u\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),&\text{if }m>N,\quad\alpha=1-\frac{N}{m}\in(0,1)\,.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Moreover, by
[
18
, Ch. 6]
, we see that

\Gamma\text{ is continuous if }\ \dfrac{N-1}{r}\geq\dfrac{N}{m}-1,\quad\mbox{and compact if}\ \ \dfrac{N-1}{r}>\dfrac{N}{m}-1\,.

(2.3)

Next, we discuss the regularity of the solution to a linear problem of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcll}-\Delta v+v&=&0&\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,\\
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\eta}&=&h&\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

(2.4)

where
h\in L^{q}(\partial\Omega)
for
q\geq 1
. It is well-known that (
2.4
) has a unique weak solution (see
[
2
,
21
]
). Let
T\colon L^{q}(\partial\Omega)\rightarrow W^{1,m}(\Omega),

1\leq m\leq Nq/(N-1)
, be the continuous solution operator corresponding to (
2.4
), that is,

\displaystyle v=T(h)\quad\text{solves}\quad\eqref{linear},\text{and}

there exists
C>0
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|v\|_{W^{1,m}(\Omega)}\leq C\|h\|_{L^{q}(\partial\Omega)}\quad\mbox{where}\quad 1\leq m\leq Nq/(N-1).\qquad

Now, for any
q\geq 1,
we
define the
Neumann-to-Dirichlet operator

S:L^{q}(\partial\Omega)\to L^{r}(\partial\Omega)\quad\text{ given by }\quad Sh=\Gamma(Th)\,.

(2.6)

Then, by the inherited properties of the trace operator
\Gamma
, see (
2.3
),

S\text{ is continuous if }\ \dfrac{1}{r}\geq\dfrac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{N-1},\quad\mbox{and compact if}\ \ \dfrac{1}{r}>\dfrac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{N-1}\,.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
v\in C^{2}(\Omega)\cap C^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
be a solution to (
2.4
) for
h\geq 0
with
h\neq 0
. Then
v>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Clearly
v>0
in
\Omega
by the strong maximum principle, see
[
2
, p. 127]
. We claim that
v>0
on
\partial\Omega
as well.
If not, there exists a point
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
such that
v(x_{0})=0
. By Hopf’s Lemma
(
[
16
, Lem. 3.4]
)

\frac{\partial v}{\partial\eta}(x_{0})<0,

contradicting the boundary condition
\frac{\partial v}{\partial\eta}(x_{0})=h(x_{0})\geq 0
. As a conclusion,
v>0
on
\overline{\Omega}
.
∎

2.2.
Weak solution to classical solution

We say
\big(\lambda,(u_{1},u_{2})\big)\in(0,\infty)\times(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
is a weak solution to the problem (
1.1
) if

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1}\nabla\psi_{1}+\int_{\Omega}u_{1}\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{1}(u_{2})\psi_{1}\,,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{2}\nabla\psi_{2}+\int_{\Omega}u_{2}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{2}(u_{1})\psi_{2}\,,

for all
\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
. By (

H
∞

), we get

\frac{2(N-1)p_{1}}{N}\leq 2_{*}\mbox{ and }\frac{2(N-1)p_{2}}{N}\leq 2_{*}\,,

where one of the inequalities is strict. Therefore,

f_{1}\big(u_{2}(\cdot)\big),\,f_{2}\big(u_{1}(\cdot)\big)\in L^{\frac{2(N-1)}{N}}(\partial\Omega)
, and hence the right hand sides of (
2.7
) are well defined. Clearly, the left hand sides of (
2.7
) are well defined since
u_{i},\psi_{i}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
for
i=1,2
.

In what follows, we show that weak solutions of (
1.1
) are, in fact, classical solutions. The result is independent of the parameter
\lambda
, therefore, without loss of generality we can assume
\lambda=1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
N>2
and
f_{i}\colon[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be Hölder continuous satisfying (

H
∞

), for
i=1,2
. Let
(u_{1},u_{2})
be a nontrivial weak solution to

\left.\begin{array}[]{rclll}-\Delta u_{1}+u_{1}&=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,&&\frac{\partial u_{1}}{\partial\eta}&=f_{1}(u_{2})\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,;\\
-\Delta u_{2}+u_{2}&=0\quad\mbox{in}\quad\Omega\,,&&\frac{\partial u_{2}}{\partial\eta}&=f_{2}(u_{1})\quad\mbox{on}\quad\partial\Omega\,.\end{array}\right\}

(2.8)

Then
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and

\displaystyle\|(u_{1},u_{2})\|_{(C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}))^{2}}\leq C\Big(1+\big\|\Gamma u_{1}\|_{L^{2_{*}}(\partial\Omega)}^{p_{1}}+\|\Gamma u_{2}\big\|_{L^{2_{*}}(\partial\Omega)}^{p_{2}}\Big).

(2.9)

Moreover,
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{2,\alpha}(\Omega)\cap C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

We will first prove that
u_{1},u_{2}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
. Since
u_{1},u_{2}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
, we have

u_{1}\in L^{r_{0}}(\partial\Omega),\ u_{2}\in L^{r_{0}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)\qquad\mbox{for}\quad r_{0}=r_{0}^{\prime}=2_{*}.

(2.10)

Due to the hypothesis (

H
∞

), there exists a positive constant
C
such that

h_{1}:=f_{1}(u_{2})\leq C(1+|u_{2}|^{p_{2}}),\quad h_{2}:=f_{2}(u_{1})\leq C(1+|u_{1}|^{p_{1}})\,.

(2.11)

Then, if
u_{1}\in L^{r_{i-1}}(\partial\Omega)
and
u_{2}\in L^{r_{i-1}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega),\ i=1,2,\dots,

h_{1}\in L^{q_{i-1}}(\partial\Omega)\quad\mbox{and}\ h_{2}\in L^{q_{i-1}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega),\quad\mbox{where}\ q_{i-1}:=\frac{r_{i-1}}{p_{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\ q_{i-1}^{\prime}:=\frac{r_{i-1}^{\prime}}{p_{1}},

(2.12)

respectively. Since
1<p_{1},p_{2}\leq\frac{N}{N-2}
and
N>2
, one has
q_{0},\,q_{0}^{\prime}\geq\frac{2(N-1)}{N}>1
. Now for
h_{1}\in L^{q_{i-1}}(\partial\Omega)
and
h_{2}\in L^{q_{i-1}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega),\ i=1,2,\dots,
and using (
2.5
), we have

u_{1}\in W^{1,m_{i}}(\Omega),\quad u_{2}\in W^{1,m_{i}^{\prime}}(\Omega),\quad\text{where }m_{i}:=\frac{Nq_{i-1}}{N-1},\text{ and }m_{i}^{\prime}:=\frac{Nq_{i-1}^{\prime}}{N-1}\,.

(2.13)

Moreover, since
m_{i}<N
if and only if
q_{i-1}<N-1
, by (
2.2
), we obtain

\begin{cases}u_{1}\in L^{r_{i}}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for }r_{i}:=\frac{(N-1)q_{i-1}}{N-1-q_{i-1}}&\text{if }q_{i-1}<N-1,\\
u_{1}\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for any }r\geq 1&\text{if }q_{i-1}=N-1,\\
u_{1}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),&\text{if }q_{i-1}>N-1\,,\end{cases}

(2.14)

and

\begin{cases}u_{2}\in L^{r_{i}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for }r_{i}^{\prime}:=\frac{(N-1)q_{i-1}^{\prime}}{N-1-q_{i-1}^{\prime}}&\text{if }q_{i-1}^{\prime}<N-1,\\
u_{2}\in L^{r}(\partial\Omega),\text{ for any }r\geq 1&\text{if }q_{i-1}^{\prime}=N-1,\\
u_{2}\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}),&\text{if }q_{i-1}^{\prime}>N-1.\end{cases}

(2.15)

By (
2.11
) and the continuity of the Nemytski operator, it follows that for
u_{1}\in L^{r_{i}}(\partial\Omega),\ u_{2}\in L^{r_{i}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega)
,

h_{1}\in L^{q_{i}}(\partial\Omega),\qquad h_{2}\in L^{q_{i}^{\prime}}(\partial\Omega),\qquad\mbox{where}\quad q_{i}:=\frac{r_{i}^{\prime}}{p_{2}}\text{ and }q_{i}^{\prime}:=\frac{r_{i}}{p_{1}}.

(2.16)

Summarizing the relations on the exponents of the Lebesgue spaces given by
(
2.10
) - (
2.16
), it
follows that, whenever
q_{i-1},\ q_{i-1}^{\prime}<N-1
, we get

\displaystyle r_{0}=r_{0}^{\prime}=2_{*},\quad\frac{1}{q_{i}}=\frac{p_{2}}{r_{i}^{\prime}},\quad\frac{1}{q_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{p_{1}}{r_{i}},\quad\frac{1}{r_{i}}=\frac{1}{q_{i-1}}-\frac{1}{N-1},\text{ and }\frac{1}{r_{i}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{q_{i-1}^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{N-1}.

(2.17)

Now, we analyze different cases
\colon

a)

If
h_{1}\in L^{N-1}(\partial\Omega)
, then by (
2.14
),

On the other hand, if

.
In both cases,

.
Thus, by (

, hence

Likewise, if

Suppose

and
q_{i}^{\prime}<N-1
for all

. We consider two cases again.

(

)

Let

Using

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{r_{1}}=\dfrac{1}{q_{0}}-\dfrac{1}{N-1}=\dfrac{p_{2}}{r_{0}^{\prime}}-\dfrac{1}{N-1}<\dfrac{N}{2(N-1)}-\dfrac{1}{N-1}=\dfrac{N-2}{2(N-1)}=\dfrac{1}{r_{0}}\,.

Analogously,

\dfrac{1}{r_{1}^{\prime}}<\dfrac{1}{r_{0}^{\prime}}\,.

Assume that

1.1
)
such that
(a\epsilon\varphi_{1},b\epsilon\varphi_{1})\leq(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})<\big(\min\{u_{1}^{*},u_{1}^{0}\},\min\{u_{2}^{*},u_{2}^{0}\}\big)\leq(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
.

Thus we have two distinct solutions
(\widetilde{u}_{1},\widetilde{u}_{2})<(u_{1}^{*},u_{2}^{*})
for each
\lambda\in\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right)
.

5.1.3.
Step 3: The existence of a solution for
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}
.

Step 1
and
Step 2
imply that for each
\lambda\in\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right)
problem (
1.1
) admits a positive solution
(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})
.
For
\lambda\in\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right)
, we have,

\displaystyle\|u_{1,\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1,\lambda}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u_{1,\lambda}^{2}=\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{1,\lambda}\,f_{1}(u_{2,\lambda})\leq C,

\displaystyle\|u_{2,\lambda}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}=\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{2,\lambda}^{2}+\int_{\Omega}u_{2,\lambda}^{2}=\lambda\int_{\partial\Omega}u_{2,\lambda}\,f_{2}(u_{1,\lambda})\leq C,

and by the reflexivity of the Sobolev space
(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
,
(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})
has a subsequence that converges weakly to
(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}},u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})
in
(H^{1}(\Omega))^{2}
as
\lambda\rightarrow\overline{\lambda}
.
Thereafter, taking the limit in the weak formulation of
(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})
as
\lambda\rightarrow\overline{\lambda}
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{1,\overline{\lambda}}\nabla\psi_{1}+\int_{\Omega}u_{1,\overline{\lambda}}\psi_{1}

\displaystyle=\overline{\lambda}\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{1}(u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})\psi_{1}\,,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{2,\overline{\lambda}}\nabla\psi_{2}+\int_{\Omega}u_{2,\overline{\lambda}}\psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\overline{\lambda}\int_{\partial\Omega}f_{2}(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}})\psi_{2}.

Hence,
(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}},u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})
is a weak positive solution to (
1.1
).

Combining Theorem
2.2
, equation (
4.25
) and Proposition
2.3
, we can find a uniform constant
C>0
such that

\|(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})\|_{(C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega}))^{2}}\leq C,\quad\text{ for any }\quad\lambda\in\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right).

The compact embeddings of Hölder spaces guarantee that
(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})
has a convergent subsequence converging to
(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}},u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})
in
(C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega}))^{2}
as
\lambda\rightarrow\overline{\lambda}
and
\beta<\alpha
.

Then, since
(u_{1,\lambda},u_{2,\lambda})\rightarrow(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}},u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})
in
(C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega}))^{2}
and
f_{i}
are Hölder continuous,
f_{1}(u_{2,\lambda})\rightarrow f_{1}(u_{2,\overline{\lambda}})
and
f_{2}(u_{1,\lambda})\rightarrow f_{2}(u_{1,\overline{\lambda}})
, respectively, in
C^{\beta^{\prime}}(\overline{\Omega})
as
\lambda\rightarrow\overline{\lambda}
. In fact, the problem (
1.1
) has at least two positive solutions for
\lambda\in\left(\mu_{0},\overline{\lambda}\right)
and at least one positive solution for
\lambda=\overline{\lambda}
.

5.1.4.
Step 4: The existence of a positive solution for
\lambda=\mu_{0}
.

In the final step, note that, since the connected set
\mathscr{C}^{+}
bifurcates from trivial solution to the right at
\lambda=\mu_{0}
, and bifurcates from infinity at
\lambda=0
, we conclude that
\mathscr{C}^{+}
must cross the hyperplane
\lambda=\mu_{0}
at a point distinct to zero. Consequently, the problem (
1.1
) has a positive weak solution at
\lambda=\mu_{0}
, as we desired.

This completes the proof of the theorem.

Regional Disparities in the Efficiency of Guangdong’s health service system: A Three-Stage DEA Evaluation Based on Data from 2015 to 2024
Authors: Yumeng Sun, Qiuyun Li, Shuxian Chen, Li Luo, Qiaochu Lu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundChina’s healthcare reform has shifted its focus toward enhancing quality and systemic efficiency, yet significant regional disparities persist. Guangdong Province offers a critical case for examining health service efficiency inequalities due to its pronounced internal development gap between the Pearl River Delta (PRD) and the Eastern, Western, and Northern regions.ObjectiveThe objective of this study is to systematically evaluate the efficiency of the health service system in Guangdong Province from 2015 to 2024. The study will identify regional disparities and isolate the impact of external environmental factors in order to reveal performance after adjusting for observed environmental factors and statistical noise.MethodsA three-stage Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) model was employed to assess the efficiency of 21 prefecture-level cities in Guangdong. The input-oriented BCC model was utilized in the initial stage to obtain preliminary efficiency scores. In the second stage, Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA) was employed to adjust input slacks for environmental variables (GDP per capita and population density). The third stage of the process involved a re-evaluation of efficiency, with the inputs being adjusted accordingly. The input variables encompassed medical technical personnel, hospital beds, and medical institutions; the output variables comprised outpatient visits, inpatient cases, average length of stay, and bed utilization rate. A Malmquist Productivity Index analysis was also conducted to capture dynamic productivity changes over the study period.ResultsThe initial DEA revealed an average technical efficiency (TE) of 0.929. Following adjustment, the mean TE increased to 0.932, indicating a slight underestimation due to unfavorable environmental factors. Scale efficiency (SE, mean = 0.986) was consistently higher than pure technical efficiency (PTE, mean = 0.946). After adjusting for observed environmental factors, the PTE estimates, which served as composite indicators of operational performance, suggested that dimensions related to management and technology represented the primary bottleneck. Significant regional disparities were confirmed, with the PRD region demonstrating superior and more resilient efficiency (adjusted TE = 0.948) compared to Eastern (0.902), Western (0.924), and Northern (0.933) Guangdong. And the period following the onset of the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic coincided with a notable decline in PTE after 2019, highlighting systemic vulnerabilities. Malmquist analysis revealed that total factor productivity declined by 2.5% annually over the decade, associated primarily with technological regression (TC = 0.972) rather than efficiency losses. Productivity dropped by17.7% in 2019–2020, temporally aligned with the pandemic’s onset, though sustained recovery occurred after 2022 with TFP reaching 1.047 in 2022–2023 and 1.042 in 2023–2024. Considerable heterogeneity existed across cities, with Dongguan achieving positive TFP growth (1.003) while most others experienced declines.ConclusionThe health service system of Guangdong has achieved a relatively balanced scale of resource allocation. However, it encounters challenges with regard to managerial and technical performance. It is recommended that policy interventions shift focus from quantitative expansion to the enhancement of intrinsic operational capabilities, particularly in Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong, while also prioritizing technological innovation to address the systemic technological regression identified in this study.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12913-026-15229-z.

Background

Health system in China has undergone a major strategic transformation, evolving from a focus on basic coverage to a renewed emphasis on enhancing quality and systemic efficiency. This strategic shift has been implemented through a series of initiatives, including the establishment of a tiered diagnosis and treatment system, the nationwide adoption of diagnosis-related groups (DRG) or diagnosis-intervention packet (DIP)-based medical insurance payment reforms, the deepening of public hospital reforms concerning governance and financing, and the construction of a coordinated “The integrated reform of medical treatment, medical insurance, and medicine supply” governance framework [1, 2]. The overarching objective of these policies is to optimize the allocation of resources, enhance institutional operational efficiency, and reconfigure financial incentives [3]. The implementation of these reforms has yielded achievements, as evidenced by the improvement of key population health indicators and the expansion in health service capacity. Moreover, the system’s demonstrated resilience during the pandemic has further validated the efficacy of the reforms in enhancing overall system effectiveness. Nevertheless, the ongoing process of developing a high-quality health service system continues to encounter significant structural challenges. The aforementioned disparities are characterized by their persistence and the manner in which they compromise the efficiency of macro-level allocation [4]. In addition, the disparities give rise to inefficiencies in micro-level technical performance, owing to incentive structures that are imperfectly aligned. Furthermore, the constraints on overall synergy are a consequence of insufficient coordination among the medical, insurance, and pharmaceutical sectors. These structural bottlenecks represent the core issues that must be urgently addressed as China’s healthcare reform enters its deep-water phase.

Guangdong Province, a coastal region in Southern China bordering Hong Kong and Macao, holds an important position in China’s modern development landscape. Guangdong is the most populous province in China, with over 126 million permanent residents and the largest provincial economy in terms of GDP. Its developmental trajectory has been shaped by its role as the primary testing ground for China’s Reform and Opening-Up policies initiated in the late 1970s. The distinctive combination of demographic scale, economic significance, and historical policy status establishes Guangdong as a representative region for scholarly examination of China’s broader developmental patterns.

The province’s socioeconomic profile offers a microcosmic reflection of both the remarkable achievements and persistent challenges characterizing China’s rapid development. Of particular note is the marked regional disparity that is evident within the province, wherein some of the nation’s most affluent districts coexist with its most impoverished counties. This internal divergence is primarily manifested through a pronounced development gradient between the highly urbanized Pearl River Delta (PRD) region and the comparatively underdeveloped regions of Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong. Furthermore, significant developmental variances exist among these underdeveloped regions themselves. Consequently, Guangdong presents a relevant case for investigating regional inequality, serving as an important exemplar of the complex spatial development challenges confronting contemporary China.

Guangdong Province has developed a health service system that integrates national healthcare reform directives with local implementation strategies [5]. This is a result of the province’s strategic position as China’s forefront in reform and opening-up, as well as its status as the nation’s most populous province with the largest regional economy. It is evident that the province has been guided by the core principle of strengthening primary healthcare and institutional mechanisms. Through comprehensive implementation of tightly-knit medical consortiums, the province has systematically enhanced service accessibility and operational efficiency. This has been achieved by the adoption of DRG/DIP payment methodologies, and deployment of digital health technologies, including a province-wide electronic health code platform.

Recent operational data has been used to demonstrate the scale and capacity of Guangdong’s healthcare infrastructure. By the conclusion of 2024, the province had a total of 66,125 healthcare institutions, with 644,000 operational beds available, representing an equivalent of 5.04 beds per thousand population based on resident population statistics. The healthcare workforce was comprised of 1.203 million employed professionals, including 1.010 million health technicians, yielding ratios of 2.94 licensed (assistant) physicians and 3.62 registered nurses per thousand population. The service delivery metrics for 2024 recorded 964 million outpatient encounters and 21.594 million hospital discharges. The institutional performance indicators demonstrated a bed occupancy rate of 76.1% and an average length of hospital stay of 7.8 days.

Nevertheless, the system faces fundamental challenges deeply embedded within its macro-socioeconomic framework, with the most pressing issue being the suboptimal macro-allocation efficiency of healthcare resources stemming from pronounced regional development disparities [4]. Empirical evidence reveals persistent structural imbalances characterized by an urban-rural divide and a core-periphery development gap between the PRD region and the eastern, western, and northern territories. These spatial inequalities manifest through three distinct yet interconnected dimensions: the excessive concentration of tertiary medical resources in metropolitan areas, the comparatively underdeveloped service capacity of primary healthcare institutions, and statistically significant discrepancies in technical efficiency across regions. Consequently, conducting a comprehensive system-level assessment of health service efficiency in Guangdong, coupled with rigorous investigation into the spatial determinants and institutional causes of regional inequality, carries dual significance. The findings of such research provide immediate evidence for the refinement of provincial health policy. Furthermore, they establish an instructive paradigm for understanding the systemic barriers to health equity and optimal resource allocation in China’s ongoing healthcare reform process.

The three-stage Data Envelopment Analysis (DEA) is an efficiency evaluation methodology that has been developed to address the limitations of conventional DEA models in isolating the influences of environmental factors and stochastic noise. This approach has been widely adopted in health system efficiency research internationally. Kohl et al. [6] conducted a comprehensive systematic review of DEA applications in healthcare, identifying best practices and common methodological challenges. Similarly, Cantor and Poh [7] provided an integrated analysis of healthcare efficiency evaluation methods, highlighting the importance of accounting for environmental heterogeneity when comparing performance across different regions or countries. By effectively controlling for these exogenous conditions, this approach enables a more accurate assessment of decision-making units’ (DMUs) performance after removing environmental influences, thereby providing a more reliable foundation for policy formulation [8]. In the field of health service system research, this methodology is particularly valuable in identifying the genuine causes of inefficiency and offers targeted policy insights for optimizing resource allocation and enhancing service delivery efficiency.

The three-stage DEA model has been extensively applied in the evaluation of health service system efficiency, yielding substantial empirical research due to its capacity to isolate the influences of environmental factors and statistical noise. In the Chinese context, Chen et al. [9] employed a three-stage DEA model to examine technical efficiency of regional public hospitals, finding results consistent with our observations regarding the importance of environmental adjustment. Fang and Li [10] applied a three-stage DEA framework to evaluate healthcare efficiency across 31 Chinese provinces, demonstrating efficiency rankings changed substantially after controlling for environmental factors through second-stage SFA regression, with regional GDP levels significantly influencing performance estimates. At the broader international level, while not all studies employ the three-stage framework, cross-country comparisons provide important context for understanding how environmental factors shape health system performance. Dlouhý and Havlík [11] evaluated the efficiency of health systems in 28 developed countries using DEA and multiple-criteria decision analysis, demonstrating that environmental adjustments substantially alter cross-country efficiency rankings. Hadad et al. [12] analyzed determinants of healthcare system efficiency in OECD countries, identifying key environmental factors influencing performance. These findings underscore the importance of accounting for environmental heterogeneity, a central motivation for adopting the three-stage DEA approach in our study. For instance, Chen et al. [13] employed a three-stage DEA model to assess the efficiency of hospitals in Pennsylvania, USA, and analyzed the impact of the Great Recession on their performance, revealing the critical role of the external economic environment on hospital operations. Abolghasem et al. [14] developed a cross-efficiency three-stage DEA model with the objective of evaluating the efficiency of health service systems across 120 regions worldwide. This method enhanced the discrimination and reasonableness of efficiency comparisons among different DMUs. Empirical studies focusing on the primary care level in China demonstrated that when environmental variables-such as regional economic development and population density-were not accounted for, the pure technical efficiency of many institutions was significantly underestimated [15]. This finding underscored the necessity of environmental adjustment to ensure measurement accuracy. Parallel research identified other exogenous factors, including the level of government investment and established patient care-seeking habits, as predominant influences on service efficiency [16]. Collectively, these studies demonstrate that the three-stage DEA model can provide more accurate and “fair” efficiency scores than traditional DEA, thus offering a robust analytical tool with which to understand and manage the efficiency of health service systems.

This study employs a three-stage DEA methodology to conduct a systematic investigation of health service system efficiency in Guangdong Province, China, spanning the period 2015–2024. The research design specifically interrogates longitudinal trends, inter-municipal variations, and regional disparities in efficiency patterns, with particular emphasis on evaluating the mitigation of developmental imbalances in health service across different geographic regions. The objective of this exhaustive empirical investigation is to furnish substantiated evidence to optimize policy in China’s ongoing health service system reform and to promote high-quality development in the health sector. By critically examining both achievements and persistent challenges within Guangdong’s distinctive context, this research contributes to the global discourse on health system performance evaluation, offering evidence-based strategic recommendations for health service system enhancement, particularly in rapidly developing economies.

Methods

Data source

This study utilizes data from 2015 to 2024, encompassing all 21 prefecture-level cities in Guangdong Province, China, derived from the Guangdong Health Statistical Yearbook and Guangdong Statistical Yearbook. Conventional geographical classification of Guangdong divides the province into four regions: the Pearl River Delta (PRD), Eastern Guangdong, Western Guangdong, and Northern Guangdong. The PRD region, distinguished by its advanced economic development and high population density, exhibits considerable developmental advantages in comparison to the comparatively less developed Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong. Historically, this has resulted in significant regional disparities. The PRD region is comprised of nine cities: Guangzhou(GZ), Shenzhen(SZ), Zhuhai(ZH), Foshan(FS), Jiangmen(JM), Huizhou(HZ), Dongguan(DG), Zhongshan(ZS) and Zhaoqing(ZQ). The Eastern Guangdong is comprised of four cities: Shantou(ST), Shanwei(SW), Chaozhou(CZ) and Jieyang(JY). Western Guangdong comprises three cities: Zhanjiang(ZJ), Maoming(MM), and Yangjiang(YJ). The Northern Guangdong region comprises five cities: Shaoguan(SG), Qingyuan(QY), Heyuan(HY), Meizhou(MZ), and Yunfu(YF). Variables selection

Input variables

In our study, three input variables were selected to represent fundamental health service production resources. The number of medical technical personnel (X1) measures human capital input and encompasses clinicians, nurses and allied health professionals, in line with health workforce measurement in international studies [17]. The number of hospital beds (X2) is a key indicator of physical infrastructure and capital investment, reflecting capacity for inpatient service delivery [18]. The number of medical institutions (X3) captures the structural foundation of the health service system, including hospitals, primary care facilities, and specialized public health institutions, as is commonly adopted in health system efficiency analyses [19].

Output variables

Four output variables were selected to assess both service volume and operational performance. The number of outpatient visits (Y1) and the number of inpatient cases (Y2) are used to quantify direct service outputs, representing the core medical activities of health service facilities [13, 20]. In addition, two indicators were incorporated to capture the efficiency of resource utilization. The average length of stay (Y3) measures how quickly patients are discharged after admission. Shorter stays generally imply higher patient throughput and more efficient use of beds, clinical staff, and ancillary services. In DEA applications, when a lower value of an indicator reflects better performance, the indicator is conventionally transformed before being specified as an output. Accordingly, Y3 is converted using its reciprocal (1/Y3) to ensure that shorter actual hospitalization periods yield larger transformed values, thereby satisfying the isotonicity condition that increased inputs should be associated with increased outputs. The bed utilization rate (Y4) measures the ratio of occupied beds to available beds. High bed utilization indicates effective use of inpatient capacity, whereas low values may signal underutilization of capital assets. Together with Y1 and Y2, Y3 and Y4 provide a balanced assessment of both service quantity and resource utilization effectiveness.

Environmental variables

In order to control for exogenous regional factors, two environmental variables were incorporated. The GDP per capita (P1) is a recognized metric in health efficiency studies, and it is employed to account for economic development disparities that influence both healthcare demand and resource availability [21]. As in previous studies [19], population density (P2) has been shown to have a significant impact on health service costs and patterns, with demographic pressures and service concentration effects being key factors in this relationship. The incorporation of these variables facilitates a more precise isolation of managerial efficiency from external contextual factors.

The descriptive statistics in Table 2 represent city-level annual averages across the 21 prefecture-level cities over the study period. All input and output variables are reported as city-level aggregates, not provincial totals, to ensure consistency throughout the analysis.

Three-stage DEA model

Fried et al. [21] first proposed the three-stage DEA model to overcome the limitation that conventional DEA easily confounds managerial inefficiency with environmental conditions and statistical noise, thereby yielding more accurate efficiency scores. In this procedure, the traditional DEA model is run to obtain initial efficiency scores and the corresponding input slacks. These slacks are then regressed via stochastic frontier analysis (SFA) on observable environmental variables and a composite error term, which separates managerial inefficiency from environmental effects and random noise, and produces environment-adjusted input quantities. The adjusted inputs together with the original outputs are finally fed back into the DEA model, generating the final efficiency estimates that are free from external disturbances [22, 23]. Following this framework, the present study treats the 21 prefecture-level cities of Guangdong Province as decision-making units and covers the period 2015–2024 (210 observations). The calculation steps and principles of the model are as follows.

Stage 1: DEA model

Traditional DEA models can be specified as either input-oriented or output-oriented. The former seeks to raise efficiency by proportionally contracting inputs while holding outputs constant, whereas the latter expands outputs without requiring additional inputs. With respect to returns-to-scale (RTS) assumptions, two basic variants exist: the CCR model, which presumes constant RTS, and the BCC model, which allows for variable RTS [24, 25]. The intrinsic public-good character of health service systems obliges them to safeguard population health rather than to pursue profit. In China’s health service system, health administrators exercise considerable control over resource allocation (personnel hiring, bed expansion, institutional planning) in response to population health needs and policy directives. Outputs, by contrast, are partly determined by patient demand and disease patterns that are less directly controllable in the short term. The input-oriented model therefore aligns with the policy relevance of examining whether the system can maintain existing service output levels by optimizing input utilization. But it should be noted that input orientation tends to yield higher efficiency scores compared to output orientation when inputs are more discretionary. Accordingly, in the first stage, an input-oriented BCC model is applied to obtain the initial efficiency scores of each DMU. When the efficiency value is 1, it indicates that the DMU is efficient and lies on the production frontier, whereas any score below 1 signals inefficiency and allows the corresponding input slacks to be derived. Since production technology and environmental conditions may change over time, we estimated a separate production frontier for each year from 2015 to 2024. For each year, the 21 cities served as DMUs. This annual frontier approach allows efficiency scores to reflect relative performance within the same year, avoiding the assumption of a stable technology across a decade.

Stage 3: adjusted DEA model

The adjusted input values of the health service system, obtained after removing environmental and random disturbances, are substituted for the original inputs. The first-stage DEA-BCC model is then rerun to re-estimate the efficiency of each province’s health service system. The resulting efficiency scores are now free from environmental and random influences, yielding more accurate and reliable evaluation results.

To investigate the potential sensitivity of our efficiency estimates to the unprecedented shock of the COVID-19 pandemic, we conducted a specification sensitivity analysis. We extended the baseline second-stage SFA model by including a pandemic dummy variable (coded 1 for 2020–2022, 0 otherwise) as an additional covariate. This model was not intended to replace our main specification, but rather to serve two complementary purposes: (1) to assess whether the estimated coefficients of environmental variables are sensitive to the inclusion of this extraordinary event; and (2) to explore whether the pandemic period was associated with distinct patterns in input slacks. We interpret the baseline model (without the dummy) as estimating the average effect of environmental factors over the entire decade, while the sensitivity model reveals the disruptive, transient shock of the pandemic.

Malmquist productivity index analysis

To capture dynamic changes in productivity over the 10-year study period (2015–2024), we supplemented the three-stage DEA with a Malmquist Productivity Index (MPI) analysis [26]. Unlike the static efficiency measures obtained from the three-stage DEA, which provide year-specific efficiency scores, the MPI enables decomposition of productivity changes into two distinct components: technical efficiency change (TEC) and technological change (TC). This decomposition is particularly valuable for understanding whether observed performance improvements stem from better resource utilization (catching up to the frontier) or from innovation and technological advancement (shifting the frontier itself).

The MPI analysis was conducted using Stata software with an input-oriented specification, consistent with our three-stage DEA approach.

Statistical analysis

The empirical analysis in this study was conducted using a combination of specialized statistical software and programming tools to assess the efficiency of the health service system across 21 cities in Guangdong Province. Geographical maps of China and Guangdong Province were visualized using R. Correlation analyses among the variables were performed with Stata, utilizing the Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient to examine monotonic relationships. To measure the TE of the health service system, DEA was employed and executed using the DEAP 2.1 software. Furthermore, SFA was applied through the Frontier 4.1 program to estimate the efficiency frontier and account for random noise in the data, thereby validating the robustness of the DEA findings.

Robustness check: model specification sensitivity

To assess the sensitivity of our efficiency estimates to input variable selection, we conducted an additional analysis using the environmentally adjusted inputs from third-stage DEA model. We omitted each input variable (X1: medical technical personnel, X2: hospital beds, X3: medical institutions) one at a time and reestimated the input-oriented BCC model for two representative years: 2019 (pre-pandemic) and 2024 (post-pandemic). For each reduced specification, we computed Spearma’s rank correlation coefficient between the resulting technical efficiency (TE) scores and those from the full model (all three inputs). The results are reported in the Results section.

Results

In order to ascertain the suitability of the selected indicators for the DEA model, this study initially conducted a Spearman correlation analysis between the input and output variables (Fig. 2). The findings revealed that the correlation coefficients between the input indicators representing the scale of health resources (X1, X2, and X3) and the core output indicators representing medical service volume (Y1 and Y2) ranged from 0.740 to 0.992, indicating a high positive correlation (p < 0.05). This suggested a robust linear and positive correlation between total resource volume and service volume, thereby satisfying the “isotonicity” assumption of the DEA model. Furthermore, it indicates that increases in health resource inputs are effectively translated into greater provision of medical services.

With regard to indicators reflecting resource utilization efficiency, the correlation results were more varied. The bed utilization rate (Y4) exhibited a significant positive correlation with X2 (p < 0.05) and positive correlations with X1 and X3, suggesting that the expansion of resource scale was accompanied by improved utilization rates. For the transformed average length of stay (1/Y3), its correlations with input variables were moderate but statistically significant, confirming that the reciprocal transformation successfully addressed the isotonicity requirement. The relatively weaker correlations suggest that this indicator is influenced by factors beyond resource scale, such as clinical pathways, hospital management practices, and patient case-mix complexity.

Efficiency of the health service system

Since the first stage failed to consider the impact of environmental factors and random noise, the efficiency values obtained therein may not accurately reflect the actual performance of each city’s health service system. Therefore, a second-stage adjustment was performed using Stochastic Frontier Analysis (SFA). Employing Frontier 4.1 software, SFA regression was conducted with the slack variables of the three input variables (X1, X2, X3) as the dependent variables, and GDP per capita and population density as the environmental variables. The results were presented in Table 5. The γ values corresponding to each input variable approached 1, ranging from 0.999999892 to 0.999999989. In the context of SFA applied to DEA-derived slacks, such high γ values indicated that the variation in the composite error term was predominantly attributed to the one-sided inefficiency component rather than symmetric random noise, given the model specification and selected environmental variables [26]. However, it should be noted that DEA slacks were themselves estimated quantities, and the high γ values may partly reflect the limited set of environmental controls rather than providing definitive evidence about the relative magnitudes of managerial inefficiency versus statistical noise. Furthermore, the Likelihood Ratio (LR) one-sided test statistics for the three environmental variables were all highly significant (p < 0.001), demonstrating that the removal of these environmental factors was both appropriate and necessary [27].

The regression results indicate that GDP per capita exhibited a significant negative coefficient with the slack variables of both medical technical personnel (p < 0.05) and the number of hospital beds (p < 0.001). This suggested a positive correlation between economic development and healthcare resource utilization efficiency. In other words, economically developed regions require fewer additional inputs of medical staff and beds to achieve the same level of output. Conversely, GDP per capita showed a significant positive coefficient with the slack variable for the number of medical institutions (p < 0.001). This may imply that economic development is accompanied by phenomena such as a more decentralized distribution of medical institutions or increased administrative costs, leading to greater redundancy in the quantity of institutions. Regarding population density, its coefficients with the slack variables of medical technical personnel and the number of beds were both positive, indicating that higher population density may be associated with greater potential redundancy in both personnel and bed resources. Despite the negative coefficient exhibited by population density in relation to the slack variable for the number of medical institutions, none of these relationships attained statistical significance. This suggested that the influence of population density on healthcare resource slack remained ambiguous within the sample of this study.

The sensitivity analysis, which included a pandemic dummy variable in the second-stage SFA, revealed that the model estimates were notably sensitive to this specification. The results revealed that the model estimates were notably sensitive to this specification (Appendix Table 1). Compared to the baseline model, the coefficients for GDP per capita lost statistical significance for both medical technical personnel and hospital beds. The γ values, which were close to 0.999 in the baseline model, decreased substantially in the sensitivity model to 0.801 for medical technical personnel, 0.778 for hospital beds, and 0.932 for medical institutions. Meanwhile, the likelihood ratio test statistics increased dramatically from 13‑16 to 122‑306. These changes indicate that the inclusion of the pandemic dummy fundamentally alters the model structure. This means that the baseline coefficients reflect average associations over the 2015–2024 period, rather than stable structural effects that hold uniformly across all years. Importantly, despite these shifts, the pandemic dummy itself was significantly and positively associated with hospital bed slack (β = 472.65, p < 0.05), confirming that bed resource utilization efficiency declined during 2020‑2022, consistent with the decline in pure technical efficiency shown in Fig. 3. Given this sensitivity, we did not include the pandemic dummy in our main specification. Instead, we treated the COVID-19 period descriptively and through the Malmquist index.

Following the elimination of extraneous environmental factors and random noise through the implementation of SFA regression, the third stage of the DEA analysis re-evaluated the TE, PTE, SE, and RTS of the health service systems in 21 cities of Guangdong Province from 2015 to 2024 using adjusted input data and the original output data via DEAP 2.1 software (Table 4). A comparison between the pre- and post-adjustment results revealed that while the RTS status of all cities remained unaltered, the efficiency values of the majority shifted. This demonstrated that environmental factors did introduce systematic bias into the initial efficiency evaluation, and that the adjusted efficiency values provided more accurate and reliable references for decision-making.

Firstly, from an overall provincial perspective, the adjusted mean TE (0.932) was slightly higher than the initial value (0.929), indicating that the overall efficiency of Guangdong’s health service system was slightly underestimated before external environmental factors and statistical noise were accounted for. Both before and after adjustment, SE was higher than PTE, indicating that inadequacies in composite management and technological performance persisted as the predominant impediment to enhancing efficiency within the provincial health service system. Conversely, the rationality of resource allocation scales is comparatively superior. Secondly, with respect to technically efficient cities, the five cities of ZH, FS, MM, ZS, and CZ maintained technical efficiency (TE = PTE = SE = 1) both before and after adjustment. This indicated that these cities had attained an optimal balance of resource inputs and service outputs, given their current management and technological levels. Furthermore, in terms of the adjustment effects, the efficiency values of some cities were significantly revised after controlling for unfavorable environmental factors. Among them, ST, MZ, and DG exhibited the most notable improvements in TE, primarily driven by enhanced PTE. This result confirmed that the previously lower efficiency performance of these cities was partly attributable to external conditions such as their less favorable economic development levels (e.g., lower GDP per capita), suggesting that their intrinsic management capabilities may have been underestimated. Regarding RTS, for cities consistently exhibiting irs (e.g., ST, ZJ, MZ, JY), the persistence of potential efficiency gained even after environmental adjustments provided strong justification for expanding resource inputs to pursue economies of scale. Conversely, for cities operating under drs (e.g., SG, HY, QY, DG), the focus should shift towards optimizing resource allocation and consolidating redundant inputs to improve scale efficiency.

Next, we examined the trends in the adjusted versus unadjusted average efficiency values of Guangdong Province’s health service system from 2015 to 2024 (Fig. 3). Overall, the efficiency indicators exhibited a fluctuating pattern characterized by an “initial increase, followed by a decline, and subsequent recovery “over the decade. Specifically, from 2015 to 2019, the efficiency values exhibited a consistent upward trend, reaching a relative peak in 2019. A marked decline was observed between 2019 and 2021, with the PTE showing the most pronounced drop. This period coincides with the outbreak of the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic, suggesting a potential disruptive impact on the internal management and operational processes of the health service system. From 2022 until the conclusion of the study period, all efficiency indicators underwent a gradual recovery. However, these indicators did not exceed the peak levels achieved in 2019. Throughout the current decade, SE exhibited only minor fluctuations and was maintained at a consistently high level, indicative of notable resilience in this dimension of the system.

Regarding the effect of the adjustment, the pre- and post-adjustment curves for SE are nearly indistinguishable, suggesting that SE was minimally influenced by environmental and random noise. In contrast, the TE curve shifted systematically upward after adjustment across the entire period. This demonstrated that the initial TE estimates may have underestimated the system’s composite performance measures due to the influence of unfavorable environmental factors.

As shown in Table 6, over the entire 10-year study period, the average total factor productivity change was 0.975, indicating an average annual productivity decline of 2.5%. This decline was primarily driven by technological regression (TC = 0.972), while technical efficiency showed modest improvement (TEC = 1.004). The decomposition of TEC revealed that both pure technical efficiency (PTEC = 1.003) and scale efficiency (SEC = 1.002) contributed to efficiency gains—a finding consistent with the three-stage DEA results that showed stable SE (mean = 0.986) and modest PTE improvements (mean = 0.946) after environmental adjustment.

The temporal pattern revealed three distinct phases that aligned closely with the efficiency trends observed in Fig. 3. During the pre-pandemic period of 2015–2019, TFP showed a gradual upward trend, increasing from 0.962 in 2015–2016 to 0.983 in 2018–2019. Technical efficiency change consistently exceeded 1.000 (ranging from 1.011 to 1.026), indicating steady improvements in resource utilization and management practices that were consistent with the rising PTE trend observed in Fig. 3, while scale efficiency change remained near 1.000 (0.995–1.010), reflecting the stable SE documented in the three-stage DEA. However, technological change remained below 1.000 throughout most of this period (0.938–0.968), suggesting that the production frontier was contracting rather than advancing.

Subsequently, the COVID-19 pandemic coincided with a decline in productivity, mirroring the PTE decline documented in Fig. 3. TFP fell to its lowest point of 0.823 in 2019–2020, representing a 17.7% productivity drop and the most severe decline in the entire series. Both TEC (0.977) and TC (0.845) contributed to this decline, with technological regression being particularly severe. The TC value of 0.845, the lowest in the entire series, reflected the major disruption to normal service delivery as resources were diverted to pandemic control efforts. This period also saw PTEC decline to 0.984 and 0.977, consistent with the PTE deterioration shown in Fig. 3. A brief recovery occurred in 2020–2021 (TFP = 1.020), driven by a surge in technological change (TC = 1.046) that possibly reflected rapid adaptation and innovation in response to the pandemic. However, this recovery proved temporary, as TFP declined again to 0.953 in 2021–2022.

Finally, the post-pandemic period of 2022–2024 showed sustained recovery, consistent with the efficiency rebound documented in Fig. 3. TFP reached 1.047 in 2022–2023 and remained positive at 1.042 in 2023–2024, indicating productivity gains of 4.7% and 4.2%, respectively. TC showed substantial improvement to 1.049 in 2022–2023 and 1.025 in 2023–2024, finally exceeding pre-pandemic levels and suggesting that health service systems successfully restored normal operations and implemented innovations deferred during the pandemic. Furthermore, TEC returned to positive territory in 2023–2024 (1.017), indicating renewed efficiency improvements, while SEC remained stable throughout (0.989–1.012), reinforcing the finding that scale efficiency was the resilient dimension of the system.

Discussion

Guangdong province, a leading light in China’s reform and opening-up and its most significant provincial economy, displays notable internal developmental disparities, particularly the marked differences in economic development and resource allocation between the PRD and the Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong. This renders it a conventional specimen for the examination of regional variations in the efficiency of the health service system. As China’s healthcare reform progresses, governments at all levels have sought to address these issues through various measures, including the rational allocation of health resources and improving their utilization efficiency. As early as 2009, the Chinese government proposed the optimization of health resource allocation and the enhancement of utilization efficiency. In recent years, the province of Guangdong has pursued a strategy of promoting the establishment of closely interconnected medical consortiums, implementing DRG/DIP payment reforms, and seeking to foster coordinated regional development through policies such as the “High-Quality Development Project for Hundreds of Counties, Thousands of Towns and Tens of Thousands of Villages”. The core concerns of this study are how these macro-level policies impact micro-level efficiency, and to what extent external environmental factors distort the assessment of true efficiency. These questions align with ongoing international debates on health system performance measurement. As noted by Dlouhý and Havlík [11], international comparisons of health systems are important for policymakers to understand how national health systems are performing, identify good practices, and find the right approach to sustainable and high-quality health systems. Our findings from Guangdong province contribute to this global discourse by providing evidence from China’s most populous and economically diverse region.

The SFA regression results (Table 4) demonstrated the impact of external environmental variables on the allocation efficiency of medical and health resources within Guangdong. GDP per capita, a pivotal metric of regional economic advancement, exhibited contrasting effects on the residual variables of disparate input factors, thereby reflecting the inherent contradictions in the evolution of the health service system within economically developed regions. This resonated with international evidence from OECD countries, where Hadad et al. [12] found that economic development levels significantly influence health system efficiency, and the relationship is mediated by institutional factors and healthcare governance structures. Our findings indicated a substantial negative correlation between GDP per capital and the slack variables for both health personnel (X1) and hospital beds (X2). These results suggested that, in economically developed cities, the demand for additional medical staff and beds per unit of medical service output was lower, indicating a higher utilization efficiency of these core resources. This phenomenon could be explained by the fact that developed regions attracted and aggregated more high-level health talents, enhancing overall human capital quality and labour productivity [28]. Better infrastructure, advanced management concepts, and potentially higher bed turnover rates collectively contributed to the refined management and efficient utilization of beds, a key physical capital [29]. In stark contrast, GDP per capital had a significant positive impact on the slack variable for the number of medical institutions (X3). This finding suggested the possibility of an oversupply or irrational distribution of medical institutions in prosperous areas. The rationale was presumably associated with the elevated levels of activity observed within the private medical sector [30, 31]. This phenomenon was indicative of the influence of market forces within the health service industry. It was evident that considerable market returns had the capacity to attract a substantial amount of social capital into the healthcare sector. In the absence of robust regional health planning directives, this could readily result in unguided expansion, superfluous construction [32], and needless homogeneous competition among nascent institutions, culminating in redundancy of institutional numbers and inefficiency in resource allocation. In summary, the SFA analysis showed that while economic development augmented the factor efficiency (personnel, beds) of the health service system, it concomitantly engendered issues of structural efficiency (institutional layout) due to the market vitality it engendered.

The formal inclusion of a pandemic dummy in the second-stage SFA regressions provided statistical confirmation of these patterns. The significant positive coefficient for hospital bed slacks indicated that, after controlling for GDP per capita and population density, bed resource utilization efficiency declined significantly during the 2020–2022 pandemic period. This finding provided statistical evidence that the pandemic period was associated with reduced efficiency in bed utilization. Interestingly, the pandemic dummy was not significantly associated with medical personnel or medical institution slacks, suggesting that the pandemic’s efficiency impact was not uniform across all resource types. Bed resources were directly affected by pandemic-related disruptions such as isolation ward occupancy, reduced elective surgeries, and fluctuating hospitalization rates. In contrast, personnel could be redeployed across functions, and institutions represent fixed capital that adjusted slowly. These results suggested that while the pandemic strained the health system, its efficiency impacts were not uniform across all resources.

As demonstrated in the pre- and post-adjustment efficiency results (Table 3), the PRD cities of ZH, FS and ZS all achieved technical efficiency. This finding confirmed the hypothesis that the economic and resource allocation advantages of the PRD significantly promoted health service system efficiency. MM and CZ were also found to have attained efficiency scores of 1. MM, as a trailblazer in Guangdong’s healthcare reform, had accomplished substantial breakthroughs in establishing cohesive county medical communities. Through integrating regional medical and health resources, it had established a community of management, service, and responsibility, thereby optimizing resource allocation, avoiding redundant construction and disorderly competition, and consequently achieving significant improvement in PTE [1]. Compared to PRD cities, CZ received relatively limited financial investment, which had compelled its health service system to adopt a connotative development path focused on management innovation and process optimization. Furthermore, CZ’s proximity to ST, which possessed abundant medical resources, resulted in the natural outflow of complex and severe cases to ST. The cases remaining locally were predominantly stable with low complexity, thus enabling CZ’s health service system to achieve technical efficiency [33]. The study revealed that the TE of ZQ and MZ remained persistently low province-wide, with efficiency loss primarily attributable to deficiencies in PTE. Although Guangdong had implemented a county hospital trusteeship or assistance policy, practical challenges emerged during implementation, including the inadequate integration of support resources and assistance measures into the management systems of recipient hospitals. Consequently, county-level hospitals in ZQ and MZ failed to achieve substantial enhancement in their management levels and core technical capabilities [34]. Furthermore, it was observed that the TE of ST, MZ, and DG increased significantly after adjustment. Both ST and MZ were located in the eastern and northern regions of Guangdong province respectively, and were found to have relatively low GDP per capita rankings in the province. The rise in their adjusted efficiency values indicated that their health service systems possessed a certain degree of resilience and intrinsic management potential despite operating under unfavorable economic conditions. The potential for efficiency enhancement was considered significant, as improvements in the external economic and policy environment through provincial fiscal transfers and precision support policies could have further amplified this effect [35]. As a typical population inflow area, DG faced significant challenges in health services due to its complex demographic structure. The post-adjustment efficiency enhancement demonstrated that DG’s health service system had developed effective adaptive management strategies to address the needs of its highly mobile population and fluctuating service demands.

The dynamic analysis of the efficiency of Guangdong’s health service system from 2015 to 2024 (Fig. 3) revealed a key structural characteristic: SE remained consistently high and stable, while PTE constituted the core source of efficiency fluctuations. This phenomenon indicated that over the past decade, Guangdong Province had achieved remarkable success in the distribution of medical hardware resources. The overall allocation of core elements such as hospital beds, institutions, and personnel had approached optimal scale, with scale efficiency remaining at a high level. However, the management and technical capabilities determining whether resources were effectively utilized had emerged as a significant bottleneck constraining the system’s overall performance. Minor fluctuations in scale efficiency across the province and its cities directly reflected the success of provincial strategies such as “Healthcare Strengthening Foundation and Pursuing Excellence Action Plan” in optimizing resource allocation structures. Through implementing strategic bed capacity control, establishing closely integrated medical consortiums, and prioritizing resource allocation for the Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong, the province effectively circumvented the pitfalls of unplanned expansion and critical resource shortages. These measures enabled most cities to operate at or near the optimal scale frontier.

For instance, core cities such as GZ and SZ had optimized economies of scale by controlling the size of individual public hospitals, while the Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong had gradually overcome scale deficiencies through resource supplementation. This demonstrated that the government’s strategic intervention in macro-level resource allocation, termed the “visible hand” in economic discourse, had played a pivotal role in establishing a robust physical foundation for system efficiency. In marked contrast to the stability exhibited by SE, PTE demonstrated considerable variability, characterized by a pronounced decline after 2019. The SARS-CoV-2 pandemic represented a major public health emergency that was associated with significant disruption to the routine operations of health service systems worldwide. The precipitous decline in PTE was consistent with the interpretation that the system encountered significant challenges in terms of managerial flexibility and technological adaptability when responding to mounting containment pressures, reorganizing diagnostic and treatment processes, and redeploying human resources [36]. It should be noted that the DEA model measures relative efficiency. Therefore, the decline in PTE may reflect not only genuine management challenges but also a systemic shift in service priorities that the selected output variables (outpatient visits, inpatient cases) might not fully capture. During the pandemic, resources were diverted to epidemic control efforts, which are not adequately represented in conventional output measures. The forced interruption or delay of numerous routine medical services reflected the system’s limited capacity to maintain core functional continuity during emergency states [37]. The recovery in efficiency observed after 2023 represented a positive signal, demonstrating the system’s inherent resilience. To sum up, Guangdong’s health service system has moved beyond the initial stage of absolute resource shortage and sub-optimal scale efficiency and is now transitioning to a quality-oriented phase in which further gains hinge on raising PTE and clinical performance. The core task is no longer merely to increase the number of beds or institutions, but to shift the policy focus to enhance PET in an all-around way, such as promoting the high-quality development of hospitals.

The Malmquist productivity index analysis added a crucial dynamic dimension to the understanding of Guangdong’s health service system evolution. Over the 10-year period, average total factor productivity declined by 2.5%, associated primarily with technological regression (TC = 0.972) rather than efficiency losses (TEC = 1.004). This finding resolved an apparent paradox: while the system achieved modest efficiency gains through catching up to the existing production frontier, the frontier itself contracted, meaning that best-practice performance declined. The temporal trajectory of TFP aligned closely with the efficiency patterns documented in Fig. 3. The steady improvement in TFP from 2015 to 2019 (from 0.962 to 0.983) corresponded to the rising PTE trend during the same period, reflecting the success of pre-pandemic health system reforms. The decline in 2019–2020 (TFP = 0.823) mirrored the precipitous drop in PTE, with technological change plunging to its lowest point (TC = 0.845). This technological regression reflected the major disruption to normal service delivery as resources were diverted to pandemic control efforts. The sustained recovery observed in 2022–2024 (TFP = 1.047 and 1.042) was consistent with the efficiency rebound documented in Fig. 3, indicating that the system successfully regained its productive capacity. City-level results revealed substantial heterogeneity. Dongguan stood out as the only city achieving positive TFP growth (1.003), reflecting its successful adaptation to the needs of a highly mobile population. Cities such as Zhaoqing, Guangzhou, and Jiangmen demonstrated strong efficiency gains, while Zhuhai, Heyuan, and Meizhou experienced efficiency declines. Notably, all cities experienced technological regression, suggesting that innovation deficits were systemic rather than localized. This finding underscored the need for policy interventions that promote technological innovation alongside efficiency improvement.

The comparison of efficiency scores between 2015 and 2024 (Fig. 4) revealed the dynamic evolution of city-level health service systems in Guangdong over the past decade, and demonstrated the impact of different policies and external events on these systems. The alterations in PTE were indicative of the adaptability of the management and technical capabilities of each city’s health service system when confronted with opportunities and challenges, exhibiting a clear bipolar trend. MZ’s PTE had declined significantly, which may be related to major sudden public events such as natural disasters like the June 16, 2024, extreme torrential rain and flooding disaster. This event exposed the system’s lack of resilience in emergency management, rapid resource reallocation, and maintaining core service continuity [38]. The observed decline in efficiency in ST could indicate a “disruptive competition” effect. The establishment and subsequent expansion of the ST University Medical College Hospital System were intended to introduce high-quality resources. However, in the short term, it may have created a powerful “siphoning effect” on the existing municipal health service system. This phenomenon resulted in the migration of local medical professionals and patients with complex and critical conditions, leading to a depletion of resources and a narrowing of the disease spectrum within the original system. Consequently, the original system had experienced diminished economies of scale and relatively increased operational costs, thereby lowering its PTE [39]. It is important to note that while the three-stage DEA adjusted for observable environmental factors like GDP, this observed decline may also partly reflect unmeasured factors specific to ST, such as the aforementioned changes in case-mix or institutional governance, which were not fully captured by our model and remain embedded in the adjusted efficiency estimates.

This finding indicated that when introducing external high-quality resources, synergistic integration with the local existing system was crucial [40]. In contrast, the significant PTE enhancement observed in ZQ, SG, JM, and DG was predominantly ascribed to the effective implementation of the county hospital trusteeship/assistance policy. These cities successfully transformed external support into enhanced internal managerial capabilities. This was achieved by introducing advanced hospital management tools, optimizing clinical pathways, and strengthening key specialty construction [2]. This finding suggested that continuous, in-depth policy interventions centred on “empowerment” represented an effective strategy for enhancing the management core of health service systems in underdeveloped regions [34]. When compared to the volatile PTE, the overall improvement in SE and the narrowing regional gap represented one of the most commendable achievements in Guangdong’s health service policy over the past decade. Through the implementation of provincial-level planning, stringent oversight was exercised to regulate the unregulated expansion of core public hospitals in the PRD (as evidenced by efficiency improvements in GZ), while resource allocation was concurrently augmented to the Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong [41]. These measures were effective in reducing disparities in the “hardware” infrastructure of medical resources across regions.

The regional efficiency data presented in Table 8 delineated the long-standing “core-periphery” structure within Guangdong’s health service system and showed how major external shocks, such as the pandemic caused by SARS-CoV-2, coincided with a worsening of regional development imbalances. This analysis not only validated the foundational role of economic development level but also underscored the pivotal value of system resilience in managing crises [37]. The adjusted decadal mean efficiency values demonstrated a stable hierarchy: the sequence of regions was as follows: PRD > Northern Guangdong > Western Guangdong > Eastern Guangdong. This phenomenon was attributable to the confluence of local resource endowments and policy interventions [35]. The PRD ‘s comprehensive lead could be attributed to its numerous advantages. It was evident that robust local financial management fostered continuous medical investment and the modernization of equipment. Effective management systems contributed to the refinement of hospital operations, while highly advanced medical consortiums facilitated optimal resource integration. This placed the PRD in a leading position in terms of resource allocation, management level, and technical capability, forming a virtuous cycle of efficient operation.

Regional efficiency of the health service system (2015–2024)

The observed differential resilience across regions involves three mechanisms. First is fiscal capacity and intergovernmental transfers. The PRD cities have stronger local tax bases, enabling rapid resource deployment during the pandemic. Eastern and Western Guangdong rely more heavily on provincial transfers, which involve longer administrative lags. Second is health system integration. The PRD has pioneered tightly‑knit medical consortia with established referral pathways and resource-sharing protocols, facilitating coordinated emergency responses. In contrast, the less integrated systems in Eastern Guangdong faced coordination failures when demand spiked. Third is institutional path dependence. Northern Guangdong is historically an ecological conservation zone. It has received targeted primary care investments through the “High-Quality Development Project for Hundreds of Counties”. This created buffer capacity that partially mitigated pandemic disruptions. These mechanisms, while not directly tested in our DEA framework, provide plausible explanations for the regional heterogeneity we document and warrant further qualitative investigation.

Despite the fact that the economic base of Northern Guangdong was not as robust as that of PRD, its average efficiency is superior to that of Eastern and Western Guangdong. This was likely due to its precise positioning as an ecological development area and the targeted fiscal transfers and counterpart assistance from the provincial level. These policy interventions had been implemented to compensate for the region’s inadequate market capacity and to achieve effective utilization of medical resources. This had been undertaken to avoid the idling and wastage of resources, thereby resulting in efficiency performance that exceeded its economic level. The relatively weak economic bases of Eastern and Western Guangdong resulted in insufficient investment in healthcare and low attractiveness for talent. Concurrently, elevated population density engendered substantial demand for fundamental medical services, thereby maintaining the operational demands of these systems at elevated levels over protracted periods. This predicament, characterized by a “low investment, high pressure” model, imposed significant constraints on the optimization of management systems and the enhancement of technical capabilities, thereby leading to the persistent manifestation of low efficiency values. The efficiency trends observed both prior to and following 2019 served as stress test of the resilience of regional health service systems, revealing significant regional divergence. The PRD region demonstrated strong resilience during the pandemic (2020–2021), experiencing only minor efficiency fluctuations. It then recovered rapidly and continued to climb to higher levels. Its solid economic foundation enabled the rapid deployment of human, material and financial resources to cope with the crisis. Strong management capabilities allowed for the swift reorganization of diagnosis and treatment processes, as well as the establishment of routine-emergency conversion mechanisms [36]. High-quality technical reserves enabled the region to balance epidemic prevention and control with routine medical services. Rather than crippling its system, the pandemic further optimized its emergency management capabilities.

Before 2019, there had been rising efficiency trends in both Eastern and Western Guangdong, indicating the success of earlier development policies. However, the impact of the pandemic became a ‘watershed moment’ in their development. The continuous decline in efficiency values beginning in 2020 exposed the fragility of their systems. Already strained human and bed resources were stretched to the limit by immense containment pressure, routine diagnosis and treatment services were severely squeezed, and weak management systems struggled to coordinate complex emergency tasks with daily operations. Ultimately, the system became “overwhelmed” by the pressure. This suggested that their systems were insufficient to cope with the impact of major public health emergencies. The high volatility of efficiency values in Northern Guangdong reflected the instability of its system. It appeared highly dependent on external policy inputs, with its efficiency performance closely linked to the intensity of support policies and the execution capacity of local governments. Consequently, it had been unable to form an endogenous, sustainable, efficient development model, and its performance fluctuated significantly when confronted with complex situations such as the pandemic.

The finding that productivity declines were associated primarily with technological regression rather than efficiency losses carried important policy implications. While Guangdong’s health service system became more adept at utilizing existing resources as reflected in modest efficiency gains and stable scale efficiency, the contracting production frontier suggested that innovation and technological advancement had lagged. This pattern may reflect insufficient investment in health information systems, digital infrastructure, and the adoption of new clinical technologies. Addressing these deficits should be a priority for policy makers seeking to enhance long-term productivity growth and build system resilience against future shocks.

While this study systematically evaluated the efficiency of Guangdong’s health service system using a three-stage DEA model and yielded meaningful conclusions, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the model only controlled for two environmental factors (GDP per capita and population density). While these variables are well-established in the literature as important determinants of health system efficiency, other external factors may also significantly influence performance, like government health expenditure and intergovernmental transfers, population age structure, disease burden and case-mix severity, urban-rural composition, and the mix of public versus private institutions. They were not included due to multicollinearity concerns (as government health expenditure is often highly correlated with GDP at the prefecture level), the need for model parsimony given the sample size in the SFA stage, and inconsistent data availability across all cities and the full study period. Their omission means that some environmental influences likely remain within the inefficiency term, potentially affecting the accuracy of the adjustment. Future research with access to more comprehensive data should incorporate a broader set of environmental controls to achieve more complete separation of external effects. Second, the three-stage DEA method itself relies on a series of linear programming and regression assumptions. For example, SFA regression requires the specification of particular functional forms, such as the semi-normal distribution, in order to distinguish managerial inefficiency from random error. However, these assumptions may not be perfectly realized in practice, which could have a subtle effect on the results. Third, while we included a pandemic dummy in the SFA regressions to test for pandemic-period effects, this approach captured a bundle of contemporaneous changes and did not establish causality. More importantly, the sensitivity analysis revealed that the model estimates were notably sensitive to the inclusion of the pandemic dummy and the pandemic altered the structural relationships captured by the model. This sensitivity likely reflected the extraordinary disruption associated with the pandemic, which temporarily coincided with the routine influence of economic development on health system efficiency. Thus, the baseline coefficients should be interpreted as average effects over the 2015–2024 period rather than stable associations across all years. Future studies with longer time series could employ more sophisticated methods, such as interrupted time series analysis or regime-switching models, to better disentangle the effects of major exogenous shocks from underlying environmental determinants. Finally, the DEA framework measures relative efficiency within the sample. This means that a city can appear efficient even if its absolute performance is suboptimal, simply because it outperforms others in the comparison set. The findings should therefore be interpreted as relative rankings rather than absolute performance benchmarks.

Conclusion

This study used a three-stage DEA model to systematically evaluate the efficiency of Guangdong’s health service system from 2015 to 2024. The findings confirm significant regional disparities, with the PRD region demonstrating superior and more resilient efficiency compared to the eastern, western and northern regions. Applying the three-stage model was crucial in revealing that initial efficiency scores had been slightly underestimated due to unfavourable environmental factors such as GDP per capita and population density. Following adjustment, the results offered a clearer picture of composite performance measures that reflect managerial-oriented efficiency after controlling for observed exogenous influences. The Malmquist analysis further revealed that total factor productivity declined by 2.5% annually over the decade, associated primarily with technological regression (TC = 0.972) rather than efficiency losses. The period of the pandemic was associated with an exacerbation of this trend, with a 17.7% productivity drop in 2019–2020, though sustained recovery occurred after 2022 with TFP reaching 1.047 in 2022–2023 and 1.042 in 2023–2024. Considerable heterogeneity existed across cities, with Dongguan achieving positive TFP growth (1.003) while most others experienced declines.

A key structural characteristic identified is that the SE is consistently high across the province, which indicates successful macro-level resource allocation and planning in terms of aggregate quantities. However, this finding can not be interpreted as evidence of balanced geographic distribution or equal access to services. As documented in the results, significant regional disparities persist, particularly between the PRD and other regions. PTE remains the primary bottleneck and is susceptible to fluctuations, being significantly impacted by major shocks such as the SARS-CoV-2 pandemic. This marks a pivotal moment in Guangdong’s health service system’s development. The issues of absolute resource scarcity and inadequate scale have largely been resolved at the aggregate level, as reflected in the consistently high scale efficiency. However, regional disparities in access and quality persist, and future development now hinges on enhancing internal management and technical capabilities across all regions, with particular attention to underperforming areas.

These findings underscored that future policy must prioritize technological innovation alongside efficiency improvement. Addressing systemic barriers to technological advancement such as insufficient investment in digital infrastructure and clinical innovation should be a key focus for enhancing long-term productivity growth. Based on our findings of where inefficiencies exist, particularly in Eastern, Western, and Northern Guangdong, several broad policy directions merit consideration. For instance, assistance programs for underperforming regions could explore a focus on capacity building and management innovation. However, the DEA framework could identify the location and magnitude of inefficiencies but could not directly identify the specific managerial practices or institutional mechanisms causing them. Consequently, these high level directions should not be interpreted as definitive prescriptions. Instead, they highlight areas where further qualitative research and institutional analysis are needed to diagnose root causes and design context appropriate interventions. More broadly, the study supports continued efforts in optimizing resource structures and building system resilience to help mitigate regional disparities and prepare for future public health challenges. Ultimately, this research suggests that advancing the high quality development of Guangdong’s health service system hinges on improving the utilization of existing resources, shifting the focus beyond allocation alone.

This was reflected in high and stable scale efficiency. The core bottleneck constraining system performance had shifted to the management and technology, which was characterized by higher volatility and lower pure technical efficiency. This pivotal finding led to a redefined core policy challenge, moving from one of resource scarcity to one of quality and efficiency enhancement.

Intelligent Multimodal Multi-Sensor Fusion-Based UAV Identification, Localization, and Countermeasures for Safeguarding Low-Altitude Economy
Yi Tao, Zhen Gao, Fangquan Ye, Jingbo Xu, Tao Song, Weidong Li, Yu Su, Lu Peng, Xiaomei Wu, Tong Qin, Zhongxiang Li, Dezhi Zheng

Abstract
The development of the low-altitude economy has led to a growing prominence of uncrewed aerial vehicle (UAV) safety management issues. Therefore, accurate identification, real-time localization, and effective countermeasures have become core challenges in airspace security assurance. This paper introduces an integrated UAV management and control system based on deep learning, which integrates multimodal multi-sensor fusion perception, precise positioning, and collaborative countermeasures. By incorporating deep learning methods, the system combines radio frequency (RF) spectral feature analysis, radar detection, electro-optical identification, and other methods at the detection level to achieve the identification and classification of UAVs. At the localization level, the system relies on multi-sensor data fusion and the air-space-ground integrated communication network to conduct real-time tracking and prediction of UAV flight status, providing support for early warning and decision-making. At the countermeasure level, it adopts comprehensive measures that integrate “soft kill” and “hard kill”, including technologies such as electromagnetic signal jamming, navigation spoofing, and physical interception, to form a closed-loop management and control process from early warning to final disposal, which significantly enhances the response efficiency and disposal accuracy of low-altitude UAV management.

II
UAV Intelligent Identification: Fusion of Multi-Sensor Data and Deep Learning

With the growth in the number of UAVs and the gradual diversification of their application scenarios, the pressure on UAV safety supervision has been increasing. Against this backdrop, UAV intelligent identification technology, as the core support for solving the dilemma of low-altitude supervision, is realizing an upgrade from single-signal detection to full-dimensional verification by means of multi-sensor data fusion and AI algorithm iteration.

II-A

Deep Learning-based RF Signal Identification

Across multiple dimensions such as the time domain, frequency domain, energy domain, and spatial domain, there exists a dense distribution of electromagnetic signals with diverse patterns and dynamic randomness

II-B

Multi-Sensor Data Fusion Identification Technologies

Beyond signal identification technology, current UAVs also utilize a variety of other identification technologies, as shown in Figure 7. Among these, radar possesses long-range detection capabilities, enabling accurate target positioning and motion trajectory tracking, and is not affected by lighting or weather conditions. However, it exhibits low sensitivity to low-altitude, slow-moving, small UAVs, is susceptible to terrain clutter interference, and struggles to distinguish between hovering UAVs and static objects.
OE sensors can provide high-resolution images for extracting the shape, texture, and thermal radiation features of targets
[
45
]
. However, they have a short effective range, are highly affected by weather conditions, and involve high computational complexity.

Acoustic sensors can effectively distinguish UAVs from non-target objects such as birds by capturing the noise harmonics and transient characteristics of UAV propellers
[
47
]
.
They require no signal transmission, feature a fully passive detection capability, and are sensitive to low-altitude, slow-moving targets. Nevertheless, their detection range is limited, they are prone to interference from environmental noise, and it is difficult to distinguish individual UAVs in swarms.
Therefore, to address detection challenges in complex environments, current UAV identification technology is evolving from a single-sensing mode to multi-sensor data fusion.
By integrating multi-sensor data, extracting features from different modalities, conducting collaborative identification based on various sensors, and implementing adaptive optimization according to scenarios, this scheme can significantly enhance the robustness of UAV identification systems
[
48
]
.
Data fusion includes data-level, feature-level, and decision-level fusion. The data-level fusion performs integration directly on the raw data from various systems, preserving the most comprehensive information. However, this level is highly susceptible to noise interference. Feature-level fusion extracts discriminative features from the raw data of each system. These feature vectors are then fused and input into a classifier for recognition.
The decision-level fusion allows systems to independently perform pre-processing, feature extraction, and preliminary identification, generating local decisions.
By effectively combining these fusions, the anti-UAV system can leverage the respective strengths of diverse systems, mitigating the inherent limitations of any single modality. Consequently, systematic performance improvements are achieved in detection range, accuracy, anti-jamming capability, and environmental adaptability.

III
High-precision Localization and Real-time Tracking of UAVs

After achieving UAV identification, the system needs to further perform high-precision positioning and continuous tracking of UAVs. However, continuous tracking in complex environments faces multiple challenges, such as positioning interruptions caused by signal occlusion and difficulties in trajectory prediction due to high target mobility.
TDOA localization calculates the UAV position by measuring the time difference of signal arrival at multiple devices, which reduces system complexity
[
49
]
.
AOA localization determines the position of UAVs using the azimuth and elevation angles of the signal arriving at devices, which features high angle measurement accuracy, high data refresh rate, and good concealment
[
50
]
.
The advantage of this technology lies in the fact that it does not require the devices to actively transmit signals, making it suitable for passive positioning scenarios.
Additionally, technologies such as OE sensors and ground-based radar used in UAV identification can also realize UAV localization simultaneously.

However, a single technology is inevitably limited. Therefore, multi-sensor data fusion and cooperative positioning are key to enhancing the accuracy and robustness of UAV localization and tracking systems
[
51
]
. By integrating multi-sensor data and combining multimodal algorithms, a space-air-ground cooperative localization system with full-domain coverage and real-time response capabilities can be built
[
52
]
. This system relies on the in-depth integration of space-based navigation and ground-based augmentation systems to provide fundamental support for UAV localization and countermeasures.
Space-based augmentation is typically composed of near-space aerostats (such as stratospheric airships and high-altitude balloons) and UAV platforms
[
53
]
. Through collaborative networking, these platforms form an integrated augmentation network covering from the ground to low altitudes, which is used to capture and identify UAV signals, achieving complementary coverage in terms of range.
In terms of ground deployment, existing resources are fully utilized and upgraded to achieve efficient, low-cost, large-scale coverage. ISAC is one of the core technologies of 5G-A and future 6G

It features high accuracy, rapid deployment, and easy networking.

IV
Deep Learning-Powered Hybrid (Soft-Hard) Countermeasures for UAVs

UAV countermeasure technologies typically combine conventional physical destruction (hard-kill) and jamming-blocking (soft-kill) methods. The integration of AI technology can effectively enhance the countermeasure effect against UAVs.

IV-A

Deep Learning-Powered UAV Hard-Kill Methods

Specifically, UAV hard-kill methods are widely used in battlefield scenarios. Hard-kill technologies directly destroy or capture UAVs through physical means, mainly including laser weapons, HPM weapons, and net-capturing systems, as shown in Figure 10.
Deep learning technology can significantly improve the target recognition and tracking capabilities of laser weapons in Figure 10(a), enabling early prediction of the trajectory of high-speed maneuvering targets and adjustment of beam direction

]

The integration of deep learning technology with HPM weapons in Figure 10(b)

The application of deep learning technology in net-capturing systems focuses on target positioning and interception path planning
[
64
]

As shown in Figure 10(c), the system can accurately identify the model and attitude of the target UAV and predict its movement trajectory by leveraging the vision module mounted on the dual-mode sensors pod and combining vision-based deep learning algorithms. Meanwhile, the onboard computer, relying on deep reinforcement learning algorithms, can generate efficient approaching paths and capture strategies for the net-capturing UAV, and then precisely execute these actions via the flight control system, thus significantly enhancing the interception success rate of intercepting the target UAV.

IV-B

Deep Learning-Powered UAV Soft-Kill Methods

However, in crowded scenarios with high human density, such as urban centers and venues for major events, hard-kill countermeasure methods may cause UAVs to lose control and crash, leading to serious secondary damage. Soft-kill technologies can disable UAVs without destroying the physical entity, which can significantly reduce out-of-band electromagnetic pollution and minimize collateral damage
[
65
]
. Such technologies include RF jamming, navigation spoofing, and protocol cracking. The integration of deep learning technology has greatly enhanced the adaptive jamming and signal recognition capabilities of soft-kill systems.

The combination of deep learning technology and RF jamming is mainly reflected in intelligent signal recognition and adaptive jamming strategies
[
66
]
. Deep learning-based signal classification networks can effectively track and jam frequency-hopping UAVs.
On this basis, generative adversarial networks (GANs) can even generate optimized jamming signal patterns to implement the most effective jamming for specific UAVs while minimizing the impact on surrounding legitimate communications.

Navigation spoofing sends forged navigation signals to UAVs for tracking them into designated locations or landing
[
67
]
. Currently, the false positioning signals used in navigation spoofing are easily identified and eliminated by UAVs. In addition, due to the uncertainty in the movement characteristics of UAVs, existing methods struggle to achieve precise control over the actual landing position of the UAV. Therefore, a deep learning-based progressive feedback navigation spoofing strategy can be adopted to implement a closed-loop behavioral response for navigation spoofing. By real-time updating spoofing tactics through environmental feedback, learning and predicting the navigation behavior patterns of UAVs, and generating highly deceptive fake navigation signals, this strategy can ultimately achieve accurate and controllable fixed-point forced landing.

Protocol cracking is an emerging soft-kill technology that takes over the control of UAVs by cracking UAV communication protocols or implanting malicious code
[
68
]
. This technology can achieve precise control of UAVs without affecting other devices. Sequence models based on long short-term memory (LSTM) networks can analyze the communication protocol patterns of UAVs and automatically identify protocol vulnerabilities and encryption weaknesses. Attack generation systems can generate effective control commands in real-time based on the learned protocol characteristics to hijack the control of UAVs.

In addition, AI systems can evaluate the current threat level, available resource costs, and environmental constraints, and independently decide on the optimal interception strategy. For example, the system can determine whether to prioritize the use of laser weapons to eliminate high-value targets, launch interception nets for capture, or first use RF jamming to disrupt their communication links, thereby achieving the optimal interception effect in different scenarios.

V
Further Discussion and Conclusions

In summary, the deep learning-enhanced UAV management and control system can be envisioned in Figure 11.
While numerous visions have been proposed for the integration of AI into future UAV countermeasure systems, the real world still grapples with a series of daunting challenges.
The high-precision performance of deep learning models relies on massive volumes of labeled data. However, with the continuous emergence of new UAVs, the problem of data scarcity has become increasingly prominent.
More critically, UAVs are increasingly equipped with adversarial algorithms
[
69
]
. For instance, they adopt dynamically encrypted communication signals and irregular flight trajectories to deliberately disrupt AI-based trajectory prediction and feature classification models.
Furthermore, the weak signals emitted by low-altitude, slow-speed, and small-sized UAVs are difficult for conventional AI models to reliably capture amid background noise.

Faced with these challenges, cutting-edge directions that integrate AI technology are injecting new impetus into UAV countermeasure systems. The introduction of federated learning has broken down data barriers in model training through cross-domain data collaboration and privacy protection mechanisms. AI models can be jointly trained without sharing raw data, thereby enhancing both generalization capability and data security.
Large models leverage few-shot learning and zero-shot transfer capabilities to reduce reliance on exclusive labeled data, thereby alleviating the decline in recognition accuracy. Large models can also simulate diverse communication encryption patterns and flight trajectory variations through dynamic adversarial training, proactively enhancing the anti-interference robustness of trajectory prediction and feature classification models. Additionally, the multi-sensor feature fusion capability of large models can help improve the detection sensitivity of weak signals, providing more accurate feature support for subsequent signal processing.
At the detection level, quantum receivers (such as Rydberg atom sensors) and metamaterial antennas are capable of capturing weak signals that elude traditional systems. This not only achieves a breakthrough improvement in electromagnetic performance but also, when combined with AI adaptive filtering technology, can effectively suppress complex electromagnetic interference and significantly boost the detection rate of small targets.
At the countermeasure level, deep integration of AI with both hard-kill and soft-kill solutions enables real-time optimization of jamming parameters and accurate prediction of strike effects, forming a multi-layered hard-soft integrated defense system. In the joint architecture of space-air-ground integrated systems, AI can further take on the tasks of multi-sensor data fusion and tracking. Through intelligent scheduling and collaborative computing, it enables adaptive switching of UAV countermeasure solutions, ultimately building an intelligent and efficient low-altitude security ecosystem.

Properties of hypersurface singular sets of solutions to the σ k \sigma_{k} -Yamabe equation in the negative cone
Jonah A. J. Duncan and Luc Nguyen

Abstract
We consider conformally flat Lipschitz viscosity solutions to the σ k \sigma_{k} -Yamabe equation in the negative cone which admit smooth hypersurface singularities. Under natural regularity assumptions (that are satisfied by solutions to the σ k \sigma_{k} -Loewner-Nirenberg problem on annuli, for example), we first prove that the trace and normal derivatives of such a solution along the hypersurface satisfy a certain PDE. For k = 2 k=2 , we also show that the hypersurface is minimal with respect to the Lipschitz solution and address some questions related to the formal expansion of the solution near the hypersurface. MSC: 35J60, 35D40, 35J75, 35B65, 53C21

Introduction

Let
w
be a positive
C^{2}
function defined on an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
(
n\geq 3
). For the conformally flat Riemannian metric
g=w^{-2}|dx|^{2}
, define its Schouten tensor of type
(1,1)
by

\displaystyle A_{w}=w\nabla^{2}w-\frac{1}{2}|\nabla w|^{2}I.

Here,
\nabla^{2}w
denotes the Euclidean Hessian matrix of
w
and
I
denotes the
n\times n
identity matrix. In this paper we consider continuous viscosity solutions to the
\sigma_{k}
-Yamabe equation in the so-called negative case:

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\lambda(-A_{w}))=1,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(-A_{w})\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }\Omega.

(1.1)

Here,
\lambda(-A_{w})
denotes the vector of eigenvalues of
-A_{w}
,
\sigma_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
denotes the
k
’th elementary symmetric polynomial and
\Gamma_{k}^{+}
is the Gårding cone:

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{n})=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{k}\leq n}\lambda_{i_{1}}\dots\lambda_{i_{k}},\hskip 20.00003pt\Gamma_{k}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\sigma_{j}(\lambda)>0\text{ for }1\leq j\leq k\}.

By Li, Nguyen & Wang
[
25
]
, solutions to (
1.1
) are locally Lipschitz in
\Omega
. The situation for the positive case, i.e. when
\lambda(-A_{w})
in (
1.1
) is replaced by
\lambda(A_{w})
, is very different and is not considered here.

Our work is motivated by recent progress on the existence and regularity theory for solutions to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem on
\Omega

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\sigma_{k}(\lambda(-A_{w}))=1,\quad\lambda(-A_{w})\in\Gamma_{k}^{+}&\text{in }\Omega\\
w=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

(1.2)

\Omega

Definition 1.1
.

Let
w
be a positive continuous function defined on a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and suppose
B_{\varepsilon}\Subset\Omega
. We say that
w
has a hypersurface
\Sigma
of singular points in
B_{\varepsilon}
if
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a smooth hypersurface and the following conditions hold:

1.

B_{\varepsilon}\backslash\Sigma=B_{\varepsilon}^{+}\cup B_{\varepsilon}^{-}
where
B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
are non-empty, disjoint, open and connected (in particular
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}\not=\emptyset
),

2.

w\in C^{0,1}(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}})\cap C^{1}(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}})\cap C^{2}_{\operatorname{loc}}(B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm})
but
w\not\in C^{1}(U)
for any open set
U\subseteq B_{\varepsilon}
satisfying
U\cap\Sigma\not=\emptyset
,

3.

w|_{\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}}\in C^{2}(\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon})
.

The second condition in the above definition can be described as follows: we assume that
w
is Lipschitz everywhere,
C^{1}
on each side of
\Sigma
,
C^{2}
away from
\Sigma
, but not
C^{1}
across
\Sigma
at any point. We point out that we do not assume
w\in C^{1,\delta}(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}})
for any
\delta>0
.

We fix a convention for the choice of
B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}
as follows. Given a fixed point
x_{0}\in\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
, we may assume after taking
\varepsilon
smaller if necessary that
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
is given by the graph of a function
\varphi
defined over the tangent hyperplane to
\Sigma
at
x_{0}
, and we set
B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in B_{\varepsilon}:x_{n}\gtrless\varphi(x^{\prime})\}
. Here,
x^{\prime}
denotes the Cartesian coordinates on the tangent hyperplane
\{x_{n}=0\}
, and in these coordinates
x_{0}=(0^{\prime},0)
. We denote by
\nu
the unit normal vector of
\Sigma
pointing into
B_{\varepsilon}^{+}
and denote
w^{\pm}=w|_{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}}
.

Our first main result shows that if a viscosity solution
w
to (
1.1
) has a hypersurface
\Sigma
of singular points in
B_{\varepsilon}
, then a certain PDE is satisfied on
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
by
w_{0}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=w|_{\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}}
and
\nabla_{\nu}w^{\pm}
. In what follows, for
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
we denote

\displaystyle T_{\alpha}\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=-w_{0}(\nabla_{\Sigma}^{2}w_{0}-\alpha\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma})+\frac{1}{2}\big(|\nabla_{\Sigma}w_{0}|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\big)g_{\Sigma}

(1.3)

where
g_{\Sigma}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma
with respect to the ambient Euclidean metric,
\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma}
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma
with respect to the ambient Euclidean metric, and a subscript
\Sigma
on a differential operator means it is defined with respect to the induced metric
g_{\Sigma}
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose that
w
is a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) admitting a hypersurface
\Sigma
of singular points in
B_{\varepsilon}
. Then for
\alpha\in\{\nabla_{\nu}w^{\pm}\}
,

\displaystyle\lambda\big(g_{\Sigma}^{-1}T_{\alpha}\big)\in\partial\Gamma_{k-1}^{+}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}.

(1.4)

The equation (
1.4
) is a polynomial equation of order
2k-2
in
\alpha
.

Roughly speaking, the equation (
1.4
) indicates strongly that the failure of
C^{1}
regularity across
\Sigma
is not arbitrary. It forces a precise nonlinear partial differential relation between the trace and normal derivatives across
\Sigma
.

For a partial result when
\Gamma_{k}
is replaced by a general cone
\Gamma
, see Theorem
2.2
.

For the remainder of the introduction we consider only the case
k=2
, in which case (
1.4
) reads

\displaystyle-w_{0}\Delta_{\Sigma}w_{0}+\alpha w_{0}H_{\Sigma}+\frac{n-1}{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}w_{0}|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}\alpha^{2}=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }B_{\varepsilon}\cap\Sigma,

(1.5)

where
H_{\Sigma}=\sigma_{1}(g_{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathrm{I\!I}_{\Sigma})
is the mean curvature of
\Sigma
with respect to
\nu
.

Our second main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose that
k=2
and
w
is a viscosity solution to (
1.1
) admitting a hypersurface
\Sigma
of singular points in
B_{\varepsilon}
. Then the mean curvature of
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
is negative with respect to
g|_{\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}}}=w_{\pm}^{-2}|dx|^{2}
and
\pm\nu
. In particular,
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
is minimal in the variational sense, i.e. it is stationary for the area functional associated to the Lipschitz metric
g|_{B_{\varepsilon}}
, with respect to compactly supported variations of
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 1.4
.

By the transformation law (
1.6
) below,
w\in C^{1}(\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}})
implies that the mean curvatures of
B_{\varepsilon}\cap\Sigma
with respect to
g|_{\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{\pm}}}
are well-defined.

Remark 1.5
.

(i)

In
[
26
]
, the theory of minimal hypersurfaces was used to
prove the non-existence of
C^{1}
solutions to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
k\geq 2
in domains with more than one boundary component. Two important steps of
[
26
]
are

•

The asymptotic behaviour of a solution
w(x)\sim d(x,\partial\Omega)
near
\partial\Omega
implies that the hypersurfaces parallel to
\partial\Omega
are mean-convex with respect to
g=w^{-2}|dx|^{2}
and the normal pointing into
\Omega
. Therefore, when there is more than one boundary component, there exists a minimal hypersurface (with respect to
g
) confined to
\Omega
with possibly lower dimensional singularities.

•

There is a differential inequality for any
w\in C^{1}
satisfying
\lambda(-A_{w})\in\overline{\Gamma_{2}^{+}}
(see Corollary 3.9 therein), which essentially acts as an obstruction to the existence of a confined minimal hypersurface with respect to
g
, yielding a contradiction. Crucially, this inequality is not valid when
w
is only Lipschitz.

The assertion in Theorem
1.3
that
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}
is minimal with respect to
g|_{B_{\varepsilon}}
for
k=2
therefore directly echos the above picture.

(ii)

It would be interesting to understand the geometric nature of the singular set
\Sigma
for
k\geq 3
.

One may verify directly that the assertion of Theorem
1.3
holds for solutions to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem on annuli for all
k\geq 2
as follows. In
[
23
, Theorem 1.3]
, Li & Nguyen showed that on the annulus
\Omega=\{a<|x|<b\}
, the viscosity solution
u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|dx|^{2}
to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem has a jump in the radial derivative at
|x|=\sqrt{ab}
, and with the choice
\Omega^{+}=\{|x|>\sqrt{ab}\}
(so that
\nu
is the outward pointing unit normal), it holds that

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}\ln u^{-}=-\frac{n-2}{\sqrt{ab}}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\nabla_{\nu}\ln u^{+}=0.

Now recall that for
g=u^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|dx|^{2}
, one has the following transformation law for the mean curvature of a hypersurface
\Sigma
:

\displaystyle H_{(\Sigma,g)}=u^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\bigg(-\frac{2(n-1)}{n-2}\nabla_{\nu}\ln u+H_{\Sigma}\bigg).

(1.6)

Taking
\Sigma=\{|x|=\sqrt{ab}\}
and noting that
H_{\Sigma}=-\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{ab}}
, we see that with respect to
\nu
,

\displaystyle H_{(\Sigma,\,(u^{-})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|dx|^{2})}=u^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{ab}}>0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptH_{(\Sigma,\,(u^{+})^{\frac{4}{n-2}}|dx|^{2})}=-u^{-\frac{2}{n-2}}\frac{n-1}{\sqrt{ab}}<0,

as claimed.

As pointed out earlier, the solution to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem on the annulus
\Omega=\{a<|x|<b\}
is
C^{1,\frac{1}{k}}_{\operatorname{loc}}
but not
C^{1,\gamma}_{\operatorname{loc}}
for any
\gamma>\frac{1}{k}
in each of
\{a<|x|\leq\sqrt{ab}\}
and
\{\sqrt{ab}\leq|x|<b\}
. We expect such Hölder regularity of the gradient to hold in more general domains when the solution admits a hypersurface of singular points. A key difficulty in this more general setting (where ODE methods are not obviously applicable) is that one expects the equation to become degenerate elliptic at the singular hypersurface, which complicates the asymptotic analysis. (The degenerate ellipticity can be derived from the computations in Section
3
although we do not do this explicitly.) This is in contrast with the behaviour of the solution to the
\sigma_{k}
-Loewner-Nirenberg problem near the boundary of the domain, where the equation remains uniformly elliptic (since the solution is asymptotically hyperbolic) and
C^{2}
estimates are available. This fact was used by Li, Nguyen & Xiong
[
26
]
in combination with the small perturbation argument of Savin
[
30
]
to obtain polyhomogeneous expansions at the boundary to any order.

Nonetheless, we provide some evidence in support of the expected Hölder regularity of the gradient, at least for
k=2
, in the form of the following proposition (we restrict our attention to
B_{\varepsilon}^{+}
; the amendments in
B_{\varepsilon}^{-}
are obvious). We will assume that
\widetilde{w}_{0}>0
and
w_{1}
are two functions defined on
\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}^{+}
satisfying the conclusions of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
, that is

\displaystyle-\widetilde{w}_{0}\Delta_{\Sigma}\widetilde{w}_{0}+\widetilde{w}_{0}w_{1}H_{\Sigma}+\frac{n-1}{2}|\nabla_{\Sigma}\widetilde{w}_{0}|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{2}w_{1}^{2}=0

(1.7)

and

\displaystyle\widetilde{w}_{0}H_{\Sigma}+(n-1)w_{1}<0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}^{+}.

(1.8)

Note that when
\widetilde{w}_{0}=w_{0}
and
w_{1}=\nabla_{\nu}w^{+}
, the LHS of (
1.8
) is precisely the mean curvature of
\Sigma
with respect to
g|_{\overline{B_{\varepsilon}^{+}}}=w_{+}^{-2}|dx|^{2}
and
\nu
, which can be seen by making the substitution

in the more familiar transformation law (

Suppose that
\varepsilon
is sufficiently small such that every point in
B_{\varepsilon}
has a unique closest point on
\Sigma
. Let
\pi
denote the closest point projection onto
\Sigma
. We search for a function
w_{*}^{+}\not\equiv 0
defined on
\Sigma
and

such that

the function

\displaystyle\bar{w}^{+}(x)\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=\widetilde{w}_{0}(\pi(x))+w_{1}(\pi(x))\mathrm{d}(x)+w_{*}^{+}(\pi(x))\mathrm{d}(x)^{p}\text{ satisfies }

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\lambda(-A_{\bar{w}^{+}})\in\Gamma_{2}^{+}\text{ in }B_{\varepsilon}^{+},\\
\lim_{d\rightarrow 0}\sigma_{2}(\lambda(-A_{\bar{w}^{+}}))=1.\end{cases}

(1.9)

Proposition 1.6

Suppose

2.1
A preliminary lemma on the ordering of
\nabla_{\nu}w^{\pm}

The results in this subsection apply more generally to positive viscosity supersolutions of the equation

\displaystyle\lambda(-A_{w})\in\partial\Gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }B_{\varepsilon},

(2.3)

i.e. positive functions
w
satisfying
\lambda(-A_{w})\in\overline{\Gamma}
in the viscosity sense, where
\Gamma
satisfies the following standard assumptions:

\displaystyle\Gamma\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\text{ is an open, convex, connected symmetric cone with vertex at }0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{n}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{i}>0~\forall~1\leq i\leq n\}\subseteq\Gamma\subseteq\Gamma_{1}^{+}=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\lambda_{1}+\dots+\lambda_{n}>0\}.

(2.5)

Lemma 2.1
.

Suppose
\Gamma
satisfies (
2.4
) and (
2.5
), and let
w
be a positive viscosity supersolution to (
2.3
) admitting a hypersurface
\Sigma
of singular points in

\displaystyle\nabla_{\nu}w^{+}<\nabla_{\nu}w^{-}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Sigma\cap B_{\varepsilon}.

Proof.

Let
x_{0}
and the associated coordinate system be as described previously. To prove the lemma, it is equivalent to show

\displaystyle\partial_{n}w^{+}(0)<\partial_{n}w^{-}(0),

where we write
0
for the point
x_{0}=(0^{\prime},0)
. We know by our choice of

that
\partial_{n}w^{+}(0)\not=\partial_{n}w^{-}(0)
. Let us assume for a contradiction that
\partial_{n}w^{-}(0)<\partial_{n}w^{+}(0)
, and take
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\partial_{n}w^{-}(0)<\alpha<\partial_{n}w^{+}(0).

(2.6)

Recall the definition of
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
from (
2.1
), and note that this belongs to

by the third condition in Definition
1.1
. We first observe that

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(x^{\prime},x_{n})-w(x^{\prime},x_{n})

\displaystyle=\alpha(x_{n}-\varphi(x^{\prime}))+w(x^{\prime},\varphi(x^{\prime}))-w(x^{\prime},x_{n})+\frac{1}{2}\beta(x_{n}-\varphi(x^{\prime}))^{2}

\displaystyle=\Big[\alpha-\partial_{n}w(x^{\prime},\eta)+\frac{1}{2}\beta(x_{n}-\varphi(x^{\prime}))\Big](x_{n}-\varphi(x^{\prime}))

(2.7)

for some
\eta
between
x_{n}
and
\varphi(x^{\prime})
. It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.1
) that, after taking
\varepsilon
smaller if necessary,

\displaystyle\xi_{\alpha,\beta}>0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\xi_{\alpha,\beta}\leq w\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in }B_{\varepsilon}.

Since
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(0)=w(0)
,
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}
is therefore an admissible test function at

in the definition of viscosity supersolution, yielding

\displaystyle\lambda(-A_{\xi_{\alpha,\beta}}(0))\in\overline{\Gamma}

(2.8)

for any
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
.

For
a,b\in\{1,\dots,n-1\}
, we now compute

\displaystyle\partial_{a}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{a}\mathring{w}-\alpha\partial_{a}\varphi-\beta(x_{n}-\varphi)\partial_{a}\varphi

\displaystyle\partial_{n}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\alpha+\beta(x_{n}-\varphi)

\displaystyle\partial_{ab}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{ab}\mathring{w}-\alpha\partial_{ab}\varphi+\beta\partial_{a}\varphi\partial_{b}\varphi-\beta(x_{n}-\varphi)\partial_{ab}\varphi

\displaystyle\partial_{an}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=-\beta\partial_{a}\varphi

\displaystyle\partial_{nn}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\beta.

(2.9)

It follows that

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(0)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}\partial_{ab}w(0)-\alpha\partial_{ab}\varphi(0)&0\\
\hline\cr 0&\beta\end{array}\right),

and moreover the values
\xi_{\alpha,\beta}(0)=w(0)
and

are independent of

. Therefore, for some matrix

\beta

\displaystyle\lambda(-\beta w(0)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}+M)\in\overline{\Gamma}.

In particular,
\operatorname{tr}(-\beta w(0)e_{n}\otimes e_{n}+M)=-\beta w(0)+\operatorname{tr}(M)\geq 0
, which is a contradiction for
\beta\gg 0
.

Antifungal combinations in paediatric invasive fungal infections: a protocol for systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: Roy Novri Ramadhan, Derren David Christian Homenta Rampengan, Felicia Angelica Gunawan, Ika N Kadariswantiningsih, Maulana Empitu
Published: 2024

Introduction

Invasive fungal infections (IFIs) significantly contribute to morbidity and mortality in immunocompromised paediatric patients.1,3 Ranges of the paediatric population are susceptible to IFIs, such as those undergoing chemotherapy for malignancies, haematopoietic stem cell transplantation, solid organ transplantation, primary immunodeficiency, immunomodulating therapy for autoimmune conditions and acquired immunodeficiency. Additionally, IFIs are also among the prominent comorbidities in neonates and children admitted to intensive care units.1 4 5

The treatment landscape for IFIs in paediatric patients has evolved from relying on amphotericin B monotherapy to incorporating newer agents like caspofungin and voriconazole.6 The recent introduction of new antifungal drugs has improved the outcomes of neutropenic patients with IFI.7 Despite the utilisation of new antifungal drugs, IFIs continue to cause significant morbidity and mortality among vulnerable paediatric populations.8 9 The IFIs treatment strategy in paediatric patients often favours monotherapy. However, for severe infections, the inadequacy of this approach has led to considering combination antifungal therapies, especially in children with haematological malignancies.10 11 The combination therapies aim to enhance efficacy, prevent resistance and reduce side effects.12 13 However, the role of antifungal combinations for treating IFI is subject to controversy, such as reduced safety attributed to cumulative toxicities, which may offset the potential benefits.14 15

Given the limited exploration of the efficacy, toxicity and practicality of administering antifungal combinations in paediatric oncology, there is a critical gap in understanding how to implement this strategy in daily clinical practice. Therefore, studying the nuances and complexities surrounding antifungal combinations with IFIs in the paediatric population is crucial for optimising treatment strategies. This protocol aims to design a systematic review and meta-analysis evaluating the existing literature and assess the efficacy and safety of combination antifungal therapies to treat IFI in paediatric patients. Through a comprehensive analysis, we aim to contribute valuable insights that can guide clinicians in making informed decisions and advancing the field of paediatric antifungal therapy.

Methods and analysis

Protocol and registration

This protocol follows Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Protocols (PRISMA-P) 2015 guidelines to ensure a systematic and transparent approach (see online supplemental table 1 for the completed PRISMA-P checklist).16 This protocol is registered with an international PROSPERO registry. This protocol, detailing objectives, criteria and methodologies, is publicly accessible for peer review and feedback, enhancing the systematic review’s quality and credibility. Any amendments post-registration will be documented and reported, ensuring that the review remains up-to-date and methodologically rigorous, thus laying a solid foundation for a comprehensive and unbiased exploration of combination antifungals in paediatric patients with IFI.

Patient and public involvement

No patients were involved in the design of the study. We will disseminate study summaries to relevant clinicians and patient groups.

Eligibility criteria

Study design/characteristics

The review will incorporate studies with robust and reliable methodologies that provide high-quality data. The inclusion criteria for this review will be: (1) randomised controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies; (2) studies that include paediatric patients with IFI; and (3) studies that involve the antifungal combination as a treatment. The exclusion criteria will be (1) editorials, letters, conference abstracts, commentaries and expert opinions that lack original data; (2) animal studies, in vitro research and duplicate publications; (3) any studies that did not include comparators for antifungal combination treatments. We included the PECOS (Population, Exposure, Comparator, Outcome, Study Design) framework in online supplemental table 2.

Population

Our analysis will be centred on paediatric patients below 18 years old who are affected by IFI. The demographic diversity within this group will offer insights into the varied effects of combined antifungal treatment. Studies involving paediatric patients who undergo only single-antifungal treatment or placebo will be excluded. Studies that did not include IFI as the main challenge will also be excluded to ensure the review remains focused on the intended patient cohort.

Exposure

The review’s core will examine the use of any combination of antifungal agents administered as a treatment to paediatric patients with IFI. We aim to capture a spectrum of prophylactic antifungal combination treatment in paediatric IFI to assess their efficacy comprehensively. However, we will exclude studies that centre on IFI prophylaxis.

Comparators (controls)

The review will compare the efficacy of combined antifungal treatment to placebo or single-antifungal therapy strategies. Studies that lack a comparator group, such as a control group or an alternative treatment approach, will be excluded. This comparative approach is critical for contextualising the effectiveness of treatment measures.

Outcomes

We are particularly interested in primary outcomes, such as overall responses, survival and mortality rates. Secondary outcomes will include the adverse effects of the antifungal combination agents, their cost-effectiveness and the impact on the recipients’ quality of life. Studies that fail to report these outcomes or provide incomplete outcome data will not meet the inclusion criteria, as these outcomes are essential for evaluating the full impact of antifungal combination treatment.

Data source and search strategy

The systematic review will employ a comprehensive search strategy to gather relevant studies from multiple medical databases, including PubMed, ScienceDirect, Cochrane Library, BMJ and Springer. To enhance the thoroughness of the search, reference lists of relevant studies will be examined to uncover additional articles that may have been missed in the initial database searches. The development of the search strategy will be in collaboration with a medical librarian and will involve a blend of MeSH terms and free-text words focusing on key concepts using Boolean keywords such as “combined antifungal treatment” AND “invasive fungal infection” AND “pediatric patients” OR “children”. This strategy will suit each database’s indexing systems and search functionalities. The detailed search strategy is attached in online supplemental table 3. The time frame for the search will encompass publications from the inception of each database to the present, ensuring a comprehensive coverage of the literature. In this review, the database search will not be restricted by date. Initially, the search will be limited to articles published in English, with provisions made to consider significant non-English publications through translation. The focus will be on peer-reviewed articles, excluding conference abstracts, letters and editorials. To maintain the review’s currency, the search will be periodically updated to include any new studies published during the review process. A detailed account of the search strategy for each database will be documented.

Data management and study selection

Article management will be conducted using Zotero to collate and organise search results and remove duplicates. The data will be exported to COVIDENCE, a software for a systematic review production tool for title/abstract screening, full-text screening, data abstraction and quality assessment. This ensures a streamlined and efficient process in handling the large volumes of data typically encountered in systematic reviews. A master database will be established, meticulously documenting all pertinent details of each study, facilitating a clear audit trail from identification to final inclusion. Moreover, the use of cloud-based platforms will be leveraged for collaborative screening and data extraction, with each stage of the review process, from initial screening to final study selection, being independently performed by at least two reviewers to ensure objectivity and minimise bias.

The study selection will be a multi-stage process. First, we will do an initial screening where titles and abstracts are evaluated against the inclusion criteria. This will be done by two independent reviewers. Discrepancies between reviewers at this stage will be resolved through discussion or with the input of a third reviewer. During the second step, the full-text articles will be assessed in detail, with reasons for exclusion at this stage carefully documented. The quality of the RCTs and the observational studies will be carefully assessed using the risk-of-bias tool for randomised trials (RoB 2) and the Newcastle-Ottawa Quality Assessment Scale, respectively. Any conflicts in the assessment will be resolved by consensus or third-party adjudication, ensuring that the selection process is both robust and transparent.

The final step is the inclusion of the studies. This decision will be determined after a thorough full-text review and quality assessment. The rationale for including and excluding studies will be transparently documented, with the entire process outlined in a Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses flow diagram in the final review. This structured approach to data management and study selection is critical for maintaining the integrity of the systematic review and ensuring its findings are based on a solid foundation of high-quality and relevant evidence.

Data extraction and quality assessment

The data extraction and quality assessment processes will be facilitated using COVIDENCE. This web-based software is specifically tailored for systematic reviews and meta-analyses, enabling reviewers to manage the complexities of data extraction and quality assessment efficiently. The adaptability of COVIDENCE is ideal for our study, allowing the inclusion of custom categories relevant to randomised and observational studies.

The data will be extracted per the PECOS format. This standardised approach will be implemented by RNR and DDCHR, who will independently extract detailed information about the studies’ identification, methodologies, populations, exposures and outcomes. COVIDENCE supports a dual-review process, which facilitates the resolution of discrepancies and enhances the reliability of the data extracted. For the quality assessment of the included studies, COVIDENCE’s template feature will be used to develop a custom template that aligns with our review’s requirements. This template will include specific domains for the risk of bias assessment, which will be applied to studies retained for full-text review. The Cochrane Collaboration risk-of-bias tool was used to assess the internal validity of the selected RCTs and observational studies.

The comprehensive assessment provided by the JBI tools will enable a thorough evaluation of the studies, encompassing various factors contributing to the evidence’s overall quality and reliability. The results from the quality assessment, along with the extracted data, will be compiled and exported from COVIDENCE for further synthesis and analysis. This structured approach ensures a high standard of evidence synthesis, which is critical for producing a systematic review that is both trustworthy and rigorous.

Data synthesis and statistical analysis

The statistical analysis will follow a structured approach to synthesise the data from the included studies quantitatively. Dichotomous outcomes will be analysed using risk ratios and continuous outcomes will be examined using mean differences, both with their respective 95% CIs. A random-effects model will pool the results from RCTs, providing a more conservative estimate considering the variability between studies.

In the meta-analysis of the effect of antifungal combination of paediatric IFI, we anticipate to see significant variability in terms of patient populations, treatment protocols and outcome measures. A random-effects model accounts for this heterogeneity by assuming that the true effect size varies between studies rather than assuming a single true effect size as in a fixed-effects model. By considering the variability among studies, random-effects meta-analysis often results in wider CIs, reflecting the uncertainty inherent in diverse clinical research. This provides a more conservative estimate of the effect size, which can be important in making clinical decisions about paediatric patients. Additionally, the random-effects model is generally more robust to potential publication bias, as it reduces the impact of smaller studies with extreme results that might have been published due to their significant findings.

In instances where the event rate is very low (≤1%), Peto’s OR method will be applied due to its appropriateness in such conditions. Meta-regression analysis will be used to statistically evaluate the effects of confounding factors for the primary outcome. Variables for the meta-regression will include patient age, duration of antifungal treatment administration, cardiopulmonary bypass use and the type of contrast medium used.

The χ2 test and the I² statistic will be used to assess statistical heterogeneity among the study results, with p<0.10 or I²>50% indicating substantial heterogeneity. Should significant heterogeneity be present, sensitivity analysis will be conducted to explore the impact of individual studies on the overall result by sequentially omitting each study.

Statistical significance in other analyses will be set at p<0.05. Potential publication bias will be assessed through visual inspection of funnel plots. The meta-regression analysis will be conducted using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis V.3.0, while other statistical analyses, including the random-effects model and sensitivity analyses, will be performed using RevMan V.5.4. This systematic and comprehensive approach to statistical analysis will ensure that the systematic review’s findings are robust and reliable, providing clear evidence on the efficacy of combination antifungals in paediatric patients with IFI.

Ethics and dissemination

The aim of this systematic review protocol is to understand the efficacy of combination antifungals in paediatric IFIs. A preliminary search has identified 1461 reports that will proceed to the screening phase. Given the persistently high prevalence of IFIs among this demographic, there is a pressing need to rigorously evaluate antifungal treatment strategies for improving treatment outcomes and mitigating the risk of life-threatening infections.

The methodology outlined in this protocol is designed to incorporate a wide array of studies, considering various research designs and diverse patient demographics. This approach aims to comprehensively understand the efficacy of antifungal combination to treat paediatric IFIs. Nevertheless, the anticipated diversity in study designs and participant demographics may pose significant challenges, particularly in the synthesis and interpretation of data. To counteract these challenges, the protocol prioritises stringent quality assessment and advanced statistical techniques to affirm the credibility and significance of the review’s conclusions.

The anticipated outcomes from this systematic review include critical insights into the most effective antifungal agents, the optimal duration of treatment, and patient-specific factors influencing the success of the treatment strategies for IFIs in paediatric patients. These findings are anticipated to have a substantial clinical impact, guiding healthcare professionals in making informed decisions and tailoring antifungal prophylaxis to each patient’s individual needs.

Introduction

A
complete mapping
of a group
G
is a permutation
\pi
of
G
for which the function
g\mapsto\pi(g)\cdot g
permutes
G
. Complete mappings were introduced by Mann in his work on constructing orthogonal Latin squares
[
7
]
.
They have been used in various ways in cryptography
[
9
,
10
,
11
,
13
]
, coding theory
[
12
]
, and in the construction of quasigroups
[
8
]
. By a complete mapping of a field or of a vector space, we mean a complete mapping of the additive group of the relevant object.

There are only a few known classes of complete mappings of finite fields
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Most of these come from one of two sources, namely additive homomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
or functions acting as scalar multiples on each coset of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}/H
for some low-index subgroup
H
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{*}
. There are simple conditions determining which functions of these two types are complete mappings.

In this paper we prove the first result classifying all complete mappings over finite fields of arbitrary characteristic among some class of polynomials other than the two just described.
We give two descriptions of the relevant complete mappings, one providing simple representatives up to a natural equivalence relation, and one providing explicit conditions on the coefficients.

We now define the equivalence relation we will use. We say that
f,g\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}[X]
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearly conjugate
if the induced functions on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
satisfy
f=\rho^{-1}\circ g\circ\rho
for some
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space automorphism
\rho
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
.
Equivalently, there exist
a,b\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}
such that
a^{q+1}\neq b^{q+1}
and the polynomials
L(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}aX^{q}+bX
and
L^{-1}(X)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}(aX^{q}-b^{q}X)/(a^{q+1}-b^{q+1})
satisfy
f(X)\equiv L^{-1}(X)\circ g(X)\circ L(X)\pmod{X^{q^{2}}-X}
.

In this section we prove Theorem

Our proof uses the following result of Dickson
[
2
, §14]
, which is a generalization to multinomial coefficients of Lucas’s theorem on mod

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
p
be prime, let
m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{k}
be nonnegative integers, and write
m\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}m_{i}
. Write
m=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}b_{j}p^{j}
for integers
b_{j}
with
0\leq b_{j}\leq p-1
, and for each
i
write
m_{i}=\sum_{j=0}^{\ell}a_{ij}p^{j}
for integers
a_{ij}
with
0\leq a_{ij}\leq p-1
.
Then the multinomial coefficient
\binom{m}{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{k}}
is coprime to
p
if and only if for each
j
with
0\leq j\leq\ell
we have
b_{j}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{ij}
.

By a
term
of the base-
p
expansion of a nonnegative integer

, we mean some
b_{j}p^{j}
as in the above result where
b_{j}>0
. When we speak of the union of the base-
p
expansions of multiple integers, we mean the multiset of all terms of all the integers. In case
p=2
, the above result can be written in the following simpler way.

Corollary 2.2
.

In the situation of Lemma
2.1
, if
p=2
then
\binom{m}{m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{k}}
is odd if and only if the base-
2
expansions of
m_{i}
and
m_{i^{\prime}}
have no common terms whenever
i\neq i^{\prime}
.

We also use Hermite’s classical criterion for permutation polynomials, as generalized to possibly non-prime finite fields by Dickson
[
2
, §11]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
q
be a prime power, and pick
f(X)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[X]
. Then
f(X)
permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q}
if and only if both of the following hold:

•

for each integer

such that
0<m<q-1
and
\gcd(m,q)=1
, the reduction of
f(X)^{m}
mod
X^{q}-X
has degree less than
q-1
; and

•

f(X)
has exactly one root in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

When applying Lemma
2.3
, it is convenient to observe that if

is the reduction of
f(X)^{m}
mod

then the coefficient of
X^{q-1}
in

equals the sum of the coefficients of
X^{i(q-1)}
in

for all positive integers

.

Lemma 2.4

If

Proof.

We may assume

We will show that the only term of

It remains to determine the terms of

f(X)^{2q-1}=\sum_{i=0}^{2q-1}\sum_{j=0}^{2q-1-i}\binom{2q-1}{i,j,2q-1-i-j}b^{j}c^{2q-1-i-j}X^{i(q+2)+jq+2q-1-i-j}.

The term corresponding to some choice of

2i+1\equiv 0\pmod{q-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad-2j-3\equiv 0\pmod{q+1}.

These conditions may be rewritten as

i\equiv\frac{q}{2}-1\pmod{q-1}\quad\text{ and }\quad j\equiv\frac{q}{2}-1\pmod{q+1}.

If

then the multinomial coefficient

. Note that if

If we leave

by

.
Since

is the sum of the contributions from the pairs

.
It follows that
\rho\circ(f(X)+X)\circ\eta^{-1}=(X^{3}-eXY^{2}+\alpha X+\beta Y,Y^{3}+\gamma X+\delta Y)
as maps on
\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

Since

permutes
\mathbb{F}_{q^{2}}

(X^{3}-eXY^{2}+\alpha X+\beta Y,Y^{3}+\gamma X+\delta Y)

\mathbb{F}_{q}\times\mathbb{F}_{q}

1.6
since

is a nonsquare in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and
\alpha\delta\neq\beta\gamma

∎

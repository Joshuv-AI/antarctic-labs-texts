On planar Schrödinger-Poisson systems with repulsive interactions in the mass supercritical regime
Juntao Sun, Shuai Yao, He Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate solutions with prescribed L 2 L^{2} -norm (i.e., prescribed mass) for the planar Schrödinger-Poisson (SP) equation − Δ u + λ u + α ( log | ⋅ | ∗ | u | 2 ) u = | u | p − 2 u , in Ω R , -\Delta u+\lambda u+\alpha\left(\log|\cdot|\ast|u|^{2}\right)u=|u|^{p-2}u,\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{R}, where λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} is unknown, α ​ < 0 , p > ​ 4 \alpha<0,p>4 and Ω R ⊆ ℝ 2 \Omega_{R}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2} is a domain. First, we prove that the energy functional J J corresponding to the SP equation in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} is unbounded both above and below on the Pohozaev manifold 𝒫 \mathcal{P} ; this explains the reason why the minimax level of J J is difficult to determine, as referenced in [Cingolani and Jeanjean, SIAM J. Math. Anal., 2019]. Second, we establish the existence of a ground state and a high-energy solution, both with positive energy in a large bounded domain Ω R \Omega_{R} , which is a substantial advancement in addressing an open problem proposed in [Cingolani and Jeanjean, SIAM J. Math. Anal., 2019]. Finally, we analyze the asymptotic behavior of solutions as the domain Ω R \Omega_{R} is extended to the entire space ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} .



Keywords:
Schrödinger-Possion system; Repulsive interactions;
Logarithmic convolution; Normalized solutions; Mass supercritical case.

Introduction

Our starting point is the planar Schrödinger–Poisson system of the form

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}\phi-\Delta\phi+\alpha w\phi=|\phi|^{p-2}\phi,&\forall(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+2},\\
\Delta w=|\phi|^{2},&\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
\phi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is the
time-dependent wave function,
w
represents the Newtonian potential
corresponding to the nonlocal self-interaction of
\phi
, the coupling
constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{\mathbb{R}}
characterizes the relative
strength of the potential, and the sign of
\alpha
determines whether the
potential interaction is attractive or repulsive: specifically, the
interaction is attractive if
\alpha>0
or repulsive if
\alpha<0
. Such a
system originates from quantum mechanics
[
5
,
8
,
15
]
and semiconductor
theory
[
17
,
19
]
.

An interesting topic related to System (
1
) is to find standing waves
of the form
\phi(x,t)=e^{-i\lambda t}u(x),
where
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}

and
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}.
Substituting this into System (
1
) reduces it to the following system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u+\alpha wu=|u|^{p-2}u,&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\Delta w=u^{2},&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{array}\right.

(2)

The second equation of System (
2
) only determines
w
up to harmonic
functions. It is natural to choose
w
as the negative Newton potential of
u^{2}
, i.e., the convolution of
u^{2}
with the fundamental solution
\Phi
of the Laplacian, where
\Phi(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\log|x|.
Through this
formal inversion, System (
2
) is converted into an
integro-differential equation:

\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u+\alpha(\Phi\ast|u|^{2})u=|u|^{p-2}u,&\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2}.\end{array}

(3)

A key feature of Equation (
3
) is the presence of a logarithmic
convolution potential, which is unbounded and sign-changing. For Equation (
3
), the applicability of variational methods non-trivial, as the
associated energy functional is not well-defined on
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

due to the influence of the logarithmic convolution potential. Inspired by
Stubbe
[
21
]
, Cingolani and Weth
[
12
]
developed a
variational framework for Equation (
3
) in the smaller Hilbert space
X
, defined as:

X:=\left\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\text{ }|\ \int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left(1+|x|\right)|u|^{2}dx<\infty\right\},

and proved the existence of ground state solutions when
p>4.
Since then,
the study of nontrivial solutions for Equation (
3
) with a fixed
positive frequency
\lambda>0
has garnered significant attention in recent
years. For related results involving different types of nonlinearities, we
refer the reader to
[
1
,
3
,
7
,
10
,
13
,
18
,
26
]
.

When the frequency
\lambda
is unknown in Equation (
3
), the
L^{2}
-norm of solutions to Equation (
3
) are prescribed, i.e.,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}dx=\rho
for a given
\rho>0
; such solutions are
usually referred to as normalized solutions. From a physical perspective,
this study is particularly meaningful because solutions to System (
1
)
conserve their mass over time. Additionally, from a mathematical standpoint,
investigating this scenario is more challenging due to the additional
requirement of satisfying the
L^{2}
-constraint. To the best of our
knowledge, the first contribution along this direction was recently made by
Cingolani and Jeanjean
[
11
]
, where they established the existence
of solutions for the following problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u+\alpha\left(\log|\cdot|\ast|u|^{2}\right)u=\beta|u|^{p-2}u,&\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{R}^{2},\\
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}dx=\rho,&\end{array}\right.

(4)

depending on the parameters
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
and
p>2.
From
the variational perspective, solutions to Problem (
4
) can be obtained
as critical points of the energy functional
J(u):X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}

defined by

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\frac{\alpha}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\log\left(|x-y|\right)|u(x)|^{2}|u(y)|^{2}dxdy-\frac{\beta}{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{p}dx

under the constraint

\mathcal{S}_{\rho}:=\left\{u\in X:\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|u|^{2}dx=\rho\right\}.

More precisely, it has been proven that
(i)
a ground state solution exists
as a global minimizer of
J
on
\mathcal{S}_{\rho}
if
\alpha>0,\beta\leq 0,
and
p>2;
or
\alpha>0,\beta>0,
and
p<4;
or
\alpha>0,\beta>0,
and
p=4;

(ii)
two solutions exist if
\alpha>0,\beta>0,
and
p>4;
or
\alpha<0,0<K_{1}(\alpha)<\beta<K_{2}(\alpha),
and
p<4,
by
virtue of the decomposition of Pohozaev manifold

\mathcal{P}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}_{\rho}:P(u)=0\right\},

where
P(u)=0
denotes the Pohozaev identity associated with Problem (
4
), as developed in
[
23
,
22
]
;
(ii)
no solution exists
when
\alpha<0,\beta\leq 0,
and
p>2.
For the case where the
nonlinearity in Problem (
4
) exhibits exponential critical growth,
several existence results have also been obtained in
[
2
,
9
,
24
]
.

In
[
11
]
, Cingolani and Jeanjean pointed out that the case where
\alpha<0,\beta>0,
and
p>4
remains completely open, and it is unclear
how to identify a minimax level of the energy functional
J
. Motivated by
this observation, in this paper we delve into the underlying reasons for the
difficulty in determining such a minimax level and attempt to make
substantial progress on this open problem.

Despite the apparent simplicity of the geometric structure of
J
when
\alpha<0,\beta>0,
and
p>4,
in particular for any
u\in S_{\rho}
there
exists a unique
t>0
such that
tu(tx)=:u_{t}\in\mathcal{P}
, we argue
that it is actually highly worthy of in-depth investigation. This
constitutes the first main result. Set

M_{\rho}:=\inf_{u\in\mathcal{P}}J(u)\text{ and }\overline{M}_{\rho}:=\sup_{u\in\mathcal{P}}J(u).

We establish the following result.

Theorem 1.1

Let
\alpha<0,\beta>0,
and
p>4
. Then
M_{\rho}=-\infty

and
\overline{M}_{\rho}=+\infty
.

Remark 1.2

By Theorem
1.1
we find that the energy functional
J
is
unbounded both above and below on
\mathcal{P}
. This reveals the reason why
the minimax level of
J
is difficult to determine. Therefore, the commonly
used methods, such as the Pohozaev manifold and the Mountain Pass Theorem,
are no longer applicable to this case.

Owing to the intractability of the open problem, we restrict our
investigation to a large bounded domain, namely

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\Delta u+\lambda u+\alpha\left(\log|\cdot|\ast|u|^{2}\right)u=|u|^{p-2}u,&\ \text{in}\ \Omega_{R},\\
\int_{\Omega_{R}}|u|^{2}dx=\rho,&\end{array}\right.

(5)

where
\alpha<0,p>4,
and
\Omega_{R}:=\{Rx\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:x\in\Omega\}
with
R>0.
Here
\Omega
is a bounded domain satisfying
\max_{x,y\in\Omega}|x-y|=1
. Our second objective is to find normalized solutions for
Problem (
5
).

Note that compared to the study of normalized solutions in the entire space,
there appear to be only a few results focusing on normalized solutions in
bounded domains. In fact, these two settings are fundamentally distinct:
each requires a specific approach, and the results are generally not
comparable. For instance, the Pohozaev manifold is not applicable in bounded
domains, as the invariance under translation and dilation is lost.

Let

Let

. Then there exists

.

and

\Omega
is a star-shaped domain, then for

.

1.3
, if we assume

\alpha<0
and

).

Let

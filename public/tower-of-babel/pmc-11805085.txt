Evaluation of the Effect of Different Contaminants on the Shear Bond Strength of Different Adhesive Systems - An In Vitro Study
Authors: Monalisha Jha, Amit Kumar, Abha Telang, Puja Kumari, Gaurav Verma, Karuna Bharti
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground:The bonding of restorative materials to tooth structure is made easier by dental adhesive solutions, which are essential in restorative dentistry. Nonetheless, there are a number of things that can affect how well they operate, such as oral environmental contaminants.Methodology:The research utilized a regulated experimental framework, segmenting the samples into smaller groups to symbolize discrete contamination scenarios observed in dentistry settings, such as artificial saliva and Viscostat clear contamination prior to and following bonding agent curing. The adhesive groups consisted of three subgroups: ADPER SINGLE BOND-2, CLEARFIL LINER BOND-F, AND SINGLE BOND UNIVERSAL ADHESIVE. Each subgroup had 11 samples. To assess the variations in shear bond strength across the groups, statistical analysis was applied, most likely using SPSS or a comparable program.Results:In all contamination circumstances, ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 consistently showed a stronger shear bond strength than CLEARFIL LINER BOND-F and SINGLE BOND UNIVERSAL ADHESIVE, according to the data. To be more precise, ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 showed better results even when artificial saliva and Viscostat clear were present, suggesting that it might be used in clinical settings. These results highlight how crucial adhesive choice is to get the best bonding results possible in restorative dentistry.Conclusion:According to the study, ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 may provide medical professionals with a dependable solution for creating strong, long-lasting adhesive connections, especially in difficult clinical settings where contamination is a possibility.

INTRODUCTION

Adhesive dentistry, a discipline that has seen considerable changes, was birthed thanks to Buonocore’s findings. Improvements in enamel and dentin treatment, smear layer management, and handling properties have all had an impact on the transition from the Buonocore era to modern adhesive systems.[12] Due to its high organic and moisture content, dentin offers difficulties for generating strong adhesive connections because of its complicated and non-uniform structure.[3] Collagen fiber health on dentin surfaces has a big impact on how well resin adheres to dentin. Because of this intricacy, many adhesive systems have been created, such as the three-step and two-step etch-and-rinse techniques, which are intended to condition, prime, and attach in different steps. However, self-etch adhesive solutions have emerged because of the goal of simplification and decreased technique sensitivity.

According to the number of clinical application processes, self-etch adhesives are divided into two main categories: one-step and two-step adhesives. This increases the effectiveness of these adhesive systems even more. However, using adhesive systems in the oral cavity in practice is not without its difficulties. In many clinical situations, such as those involving equigingival or subgingival cavity margins, seating of indirect restorations, and patients with restricted mouth opening, it can be challenging to achieve a moisture-free, clean tooth surface for effective bonding.

This research’s thorough examination of shear bond strength and the impact of contaminating factors holds the potential to deepen our knowledge of adhesive dentistry and open the door to better patient care. Because self-etch adhesives make clinical procedures simpler, modern adhesive dentistry has substantially progressed. Effectively controlling impurities such as blood and saliva, however, is still essential. The focus of this study on the effects of artificial saliva and aluminum chloride on shear bond strength provides insightful information to prolong the life of dental restorations.

MATERIALS AND METHOD

A total of 165 extracted maxillary and mandibular premolars were selected for the study, cleaned with an ultrasonic scaler, and stored in normal saline. The teeth were embedded in self-cure acrylic resin by using ice-cube trays, and the occlusal surfaces were reduced perpendicular to the long axis of the teeth by using a water-cooled model-trimming wheel to create flat dentin surfaces. The teeth were then divided into three main groups of 55 teeth each, and each main group was further divided into five subgroups (A, B, C, D, and E) of 11 teeth each. Subgroup A served as the control, where adhesive application was performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions. In subgroups B and C, artificial saliva contamination was introduced either before or after the curing of the bonding agent, respectively. Similarly, in subgroups D and E, aluminum chloride contamination was introduced either before or after the curing of the bonding agent. Composite resin was then applied to the dentin surface in two increments, with each increment being compressed and light-cured for 30 seconds.

The specimens were stored in distilled water at 37°C for 24 hours to simulate the oral environment. Shear bond strength testing was then conducted using an Instron Universal Testing Machine at a crosshead speed of 0.5 mm/min. The data obtained were subjected to statistical analysis using an ANOVA test, followed by a post-hoc Tukey test for multiple comparisons. Both descriptive and inferential statistics were employed to analyze the results, providing insights into the effects of different contaminants and their timing of application on the bond strength of composite resins.

RESULT

This research investigates the shear bond strength demonstrated by three different adhesive systems under a variety of experimental settings: ADPER SINGLE BOND-2, CLEARFIL LINER BOND-F, and SINGLE BOND UNIVERSAL ADHESIVE. These circumstances include following the manufacturer’s recommendations and getting contaminated both before and after the curing procedure with fake saliva and Viscostat clear. The primary focus of the study was shear bond strength, a crucial parameter in dental applications that measures the force needed to break the bond between the adhesive and the attached material (usually measured in megapascals, MPa) [Figure 1]. Samples were put under various circumstances, such as contamination problems, to simulate real-world situations. Statistical software was used for the analysis; it was most likely SPSS (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) or a similar program appropriate for multivariate analysis. The findings, which are methodically displayed in tables with mean shear bond strength values and graphical depictions, highlight intergroup differences, expose subgroup variances within each adhesive group, and emphasize the impact of contamination on shear bond strength. This thorough approach offers insightful information about the subtleties of these adhesive systems’ performance as well as helpful viewpoints for their actual use in dental settings.

DISCUSSION

This study extensively examines the shear bond strength of different adhesive systems under various contamination conditions, providing valuable insights for clinical applications in orthodontic and restorative dentistry. The investigation focused on assessing the impact of contaminants such as blood and saliva on adhesive performance, specifically evaluating ADPER SINGLE BOND-2, CLEARFIL LINER BOND-F, and SINGLE BOND UNIVERSAL ADHESIVE. Results indicated that ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 consistently demonstrated superior shear bond strength, particularly when contaminated with Viscostat Clear post-curing, aligning with previous research by Shaik et al. (2018), Kugel et al. (2000), and Buonocore MG et al. (1968).[456] The study also highlighted significant reductions in shear bond strength across all adhesive systems when contaminated with artificial saliva before curing, underscoring the vulnerability of dental adhesives to external influences.

The statistical analysis, including ANOVA and post-hoc tests, confirmed the importance of these variations, showing that ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 consistently outperformed the other adhesives across different testing conditions.[789] This research contributes substantially to existing knowledge by offering evidence-based guidance on contamination prevention strategies, ultimately enhancing the longevity of dental restorations. The findings emphasize the critical role of context-specific evaluations in improving the validity and reliability of adhesive performance assessments, providing dental professionals with essential insights for optimizing clinical procedures and improving patient outcomes.[137]

CONCLUSION

In conclusion, this study underscores the significant impact of various contaminants on the shear bond strength of dental adhesive systems, particularly highlighting the superior performance of ADPER SINGLE BOND-2 under diverse conditions. The findings provide valuable, evidence-based insights that can guide clinical practices, emphasizing the importance of contamination prevention strategies to enhance the durability of dental restorations and ultimately improve patient outcomes.

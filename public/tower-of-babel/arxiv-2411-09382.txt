Convergence to equilibrium for a degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system
Saumyajit Das, Harsha Hutridurga

Abstract
. In this article we study a reaction diffusion system with m m unknown concentration. The non-linearity in our study comes from an underlying reversible chemical reaction and triangular in nature. Our objective is to understand the large time behaviour of solution where there are degeneracies. In particular we treat those cases when one of the diffusion coefficient is zero and others are strictly positive. We prove convergence to equilibrium type of results under some condition on stoichiometric coefficients in dimension 1 1 , 2 2 and 3 3 in correspondence with the existence of classical solution. For dimension greater than 3 we prove similar result under certain closeness condition on the non-zero diffusion coefficients and with the same condition imposed on stoichiometric coefficients. All the constant occurs in the decay estimates are explicit.

Thanks to the algebraic identity mentioned earlier and the Poincaré inequality, we arrive at

(18)

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\int_{\Omega}\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}|\delta_{A_{i}}|^{2}+\int_{\Omega}\Big(A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{2}.

To summarize, the solutions to the degenerate system (
8
) satisfies:

(19)

\left\{\begin{aligned} D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}&\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\ \mbox{for}\ N\geq 4,\\
D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}&\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\\
&+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\ \qquad\mbox{for}\ N=1,2,3.\end{aligned}\right.

We are particularly interested in the case
N=2
, i.e.

(20)

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Note that both the entropy functional and entropy dissipation functional are positive functional. If
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
solves either of the system (
7
) and (
8
), we obtain that

\frac{dE}{dt}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})=-D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}),

which ensures the decay of entropy with time.
Let us consider the relative entropy(relative with respect to the homogeneous equilibrium states given by (
6
)-(
5
)), we obtain that

E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}\ln{a_{i}}-a_{i}-a_{i\infty}\ln{a_{i\infty}}+a_{i\infty}).

Thanks to the mass conservation property (
6
) of the equilibrium states, the relative entropy becomes

(21)

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}\ln{\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i\infty}}}-a_{i}+a_{i\infty}).

Let us define a function
\Gamma:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(22)

\Gamma(x,y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{x\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-x+y}{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{ for }x\not=y,\\
&2\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{ for }x=y.\end{aligned}\right.

It can be shown (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, p.162]
for details) that the above defined function satisfies the following bound:

(23)

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,y)\leq C_{\Gamma}\max\left\{1,\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right\}

for some positive constant
C_{\Gamma}
. Note that using the function
\Gamma
defined above, the relative entropy can be rewritten as

E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{1\infty})=\sum\limits_{1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}\Gamma(a_{i},a_{i\infty})(A_{i}-A_{i\infty})^{2}

Using the aforementioned bound for
\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})

\displaystyle-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m}\{\alpha_{i}\}C_{\Gamma}\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m}\big\{1,\ln{(\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}+|\ln{a_{i\infty}}|\big\}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

So, other than the logarithmic growth of the supremum norm of the solution, the growth of relative entropy depends on the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the deviation of the
A_{i}
from
A_{i\infty}
. Observe from (
16
) and (
19
) that the dissipation functional is also related to this
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of
\delta_{A_{i}}
. Observe that

\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 3\left(\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

The following observation says that the first term on the right hand side dominate the second term:

\displaystyle\|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=

\displaystyle|\Omega|\big|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\big|^{2}=|\Omega|\left(\overline{A_{i}^{2}}+\overline{A_{i}}^{2}-2\overline{A_{i}}\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\left(\overline{A_{i}^{2}}-\overline{A_{i}}^{2}\right)\leq\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Here we used the fact that
\displaystyle{\overline{A_{i}}\leq\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}}
, thanks to Hölder inequality. Hence

\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Hence we deduce

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Next we borrow a result from
[
7
]
(see Appendix, Theorem
23
) which says that there exists
C_{EB}>0
, depending only on the domain and the equilibrium state
(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})
such that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{EB}\left(\sum_{1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

It is evident that for the degenerate system (
7
), the gradient term
\displaystyle{\|\nabla\delta_{A_{1}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
is missing in the dissipation expression. Similarly for the degenerate system (
8
), the term
\displaystyle{\|\nabla\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
is missing in the dissipation expression. If we relate dissipation with the corresponding missing terms, we will be able to produce entropy-entropy dissipation inequality provided we know how the logarithm of the supremum of the solution behaves. It turns out that the lower bound (
16
) for the entropy dissipation can be improved to include the gradient term
\displaystyle{\|\nabla\delta_{A_{1}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
but with a price of the constant being time dependent. We prove in Proposition
12
and Proposition
13
that there exists a constant
\hat{C}>0
such that

(24)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mbox{For}\ N\leq 3,\ \mbox{we have}\\
D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}&\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).\\
\mbox{for}\ N\geq 4,\ \mbox{we have}\\
D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}&\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).\end{aligned}\right.

In the context of the degenerate system (
8
), we improve the lower bound in (
19
) to include the missing gradient term
\displaystyle{\|\nabla\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
. More precisely in Proposition
2
, we proved that for dimension
N\leq 2
, there exists a positive constant
\hat{C}
such that

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

The result in Proposition
12
concerning the dimension
N\geq 4
is conditional in the sense that (
24
) is valid under the assumption that the diffusion coefficients
d_{m}
and at least one of tyhe diffusion coefficients
\{d_{2},\cdots,d_{m-1}\}
are close to each other. The result in Proposition
13
dealing with dimension

is however unconditional.

We briefly highlight the key points which leads to (
24
). For
d_{1}=0
with
\alpha_{1}=1
, we relate the missing term
\displaystyle{\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
with the entropy dissipation. We describe the key steps below.

Step 1: We obtain a large integral estimate of all the species except
a_{1}
and
a_{m}
in a parabolic cylinder with unit height up to dimension

. The result is as follows

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq{K_{\Gamma}},\qquad i=2,\cdots,m-1,\tau\geq 0,

where

is a positive constant, independent of time.

Step 2: We pass the integral regularity to
a_{m}
up to dimension
N=3
.

\|a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\texttt{K},

where
K
is a positive constant, independent of time.

Step 3: We will go reverse when dimension
N\geq 4
. We obtain the following relation

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0},

where
C_{0}
is a positive constant independent of time and we choose
p>N
. Here in this case we need the closeness condition(
10
) on non-zero diffusion coeeficients.

Step 4: Above integral estimates in dimension
N\geq 4
also yields the growth of supremum norm of the solution barring the species
a_{1}
and
a_{m}
.

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq K_{\infty},\qquad\forall i=2,\cdots,m-1,

where
K_{\infty}
is a positive constant, independent of time.

Step 5: Integrability of
a_{m}
further yields the
\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)
norm estimate of the species
a_{1}
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mbox{For dimension}&\ N\geq 4,\ \exists\tilde{K}>0\ \mbox{such that}\\
&\|a_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{K}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}.\\
\mbox{For dimension}&\ N=1,2,3,\ \exists\tilde{K}>0\ \mbox{such that}\\
&\|a_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{K}(1+t)^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{aligned}\right.

Step 6: We now arrive at the position where we can link dissipation with the missing deviation
\displaystyle{\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
. The results read as

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}\left(\sum_{1}^{m}\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right),

where
\hat{C}
is a positive constant depends on domain and initial conditions only. The above estimates also yields that the space-time growth of the solutions varies polynomially with time. Hence we can relate relative entropy with the dissipation and obtain the convergence results.

For the degeneracy
d_{m}=0
, we relate the missing term
\displaystyle{\|A_{m}-\overline{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
. The result we obtain holds up to dimension
N=2
. We present the two dimensional case only. The key points are described below

Step 1: We obtain a uniform integral estimate for
d_{m}=0
case. The result reads as

\sup\limits_{t>0}\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0,p};\qquad\forall i=1,\cdot\cdot,m-1,p\in[1,\infty),

where

is a positive constant depends on

Step 2: Using the above estimate, we can link dissipation with the missing deviation
\displaystyle{\displaystyle{\|A_{m}-\overline{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}}
. The results read as

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}\left(\sum_{1}^{m}\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right),

where
\hat{C}
is a positive constant depends on domain and initial conditions only. The above estimates also yields that the space-time growth of the solutions varies polynomially with time. Hence we can relate relative entropy with the dissipation and obtain the convergence results for dimension

.

2.
Large time behaviour: degenerate system
d_{1}=0

This section deals with the degenerate system (
7
) where the diffusion coefficient
d_{1}
vanishes. Our ultimate goal is to employ the method of entropy to demonstrate that the solution to the degenerate system (
7
) converges to the equilibrium states given by (
6
)-(
5
). While carrying out the procedure, information on the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
norm of the solution comes in handy. So, a good portion of this section is spent on analyzing the supremum norm of the solution. To begin with, we record an
\mathrm{L}^{2}
estimate on the diffusive species’ concentrations in dimension
N\leq 3
.

Proposition 3
.

Let dimension
N\leq 3
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system
d_{1}=0
(
7
). Then there exists a positive constant
K_{I}
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq{K_{I}}

for all
i=2,\cdots,m
and for all
\tau\geq 0
.

Proof.

Recall the relation between the entropy and the entropy dissipation:

\frac{dE}{dt}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})=-D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}).

Integrating the above relation in time on
[0,T]
yields

\displaystyle\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}(\ln{a_{i}}-1)+1)+\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\alpha_{i}d_{i}\frac{|\nabla a_{i}|^{2}}{a_{i}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\Big(a_{m}-a_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(a_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)\ln{\left(\frac{a_{m}}{a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(a_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}}\right)}\leq E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0}).

The first two terms of the above inequality immediately yields the following bounds

(25)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\alpha_{i}\}}E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0})+e^{2}|\Omega|=\tilde{M}_{1}\quad\mbox{for}\ i=1,\cdots,m

and

(26)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{T}}|\nabla\sqrt{a_{i}}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\min\limits_{i=2,\cdots,m}\{d_{i}\alpha_{i}\}}E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0})=K_{1}\qquad\mbox{for}\ i=2,\cdots,m.

Employing the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality for
\sqrt{a_{i}}
in dimension
N=1
, we obtain

\|\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{4}_{\mathrm{L}^{4}(\Omega)}\leq C_{GN}\|\nabla\sqrt{a_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{3}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{GN}\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{4}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}

Integrating the above inequality in time on
[\tau,\tau+1]
yields

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}^{2}(t,x)\rm{d}x\rm{d}t\leq C_{GN}\left(\tilde{M}_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}K_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{M}_{1}^{2}\right),

where
K_{1}
is defined in (
26
). Note that the above estimate holds true for all diffusive species.

Following a similar strategy via the Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation inequality for
\sqrt{a_{i}}
in dimension
N=2
, we obtain

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}^{2}(t,x)\rm{d}x\rm{d}t\leq C_{GN}\left(K_{1}\tilde{M}_{1}+\tilde{M}_{1}^{2}\right)

for the same constants
K_{1}
from (
26
).

For dimension
N=3
, we borrow a result from
[
8
, Lemma 3.2]
which gives the following bound

(27)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}}a_{i}^{2}\leq\sqrt{\tilde{M}_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2}\frac{d_{max}}{d_{min}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}

for all
\tau\geq 0
and for
i=2,\cdots,m
. Here, the constant
\tilde{M}_{1}
is defined in (
25
) and
\displaystyle{d_{max}=\max\{d_{2},\cdots,d_{m}\}}
and
\displaystyle{d_{min}=\min\{d_{2},\cdots,d_{m}\}}
.

Let us choose a positive constant
K_{I}
to be

K_{I}=\max\left\{\tilde{M}_{1}^{\frac{3}{2}}K_{1}^{\frac{1}{2}}+\tilde{M}_{1}^{2},M_{1}^{4}C_{GN}\|\nabla\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{GN}M_{1}^{3},\sqrt{\tilde{M}_{1}}\left(\sqrt{2}\frac{d_{max}}{d_{min}}\right)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right\}

This provides essential uniform bound for
\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}
for
i=2,\cdots,m
.
∎

Remark 1
.

For dimension
N=1
, we can further improve the integrability of
a_{i}
via Gagliardo-Nirenberg interpolation. More precisely, we have for
i=2,\cdots,m
,

\|\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{6}_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}\leq C_{GN}\|\nabla\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\|\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{4}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{GN}\|\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{6}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\tilde{M}_{1}^{4}C_{GN}\|\nabla\sqrt{a_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}+C_{GN}\tilde{M}_{1}^{3},

This helps us arrive at

\int_{\tau}^{\tau+1}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}^{3}(t,x)\rm{d}x\rm{d}t\leq C_{GN}\left(K_{1}\tilde{M}_{1}^{2}+\tilde{M}_{1}^{3}\right).

Remark 2
.

Even though we recall the estimate (
27
) for dimension
N=3
, the author in
[
8
]
derive a uniform
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})
estimate for dimension
N\geq 3
. They use duality approach.

The following result improves the integrability of the species concentrations
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
to
\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})
. Note that this is for a parabolic cylinder of unit height at initial time.

Proposition 4
.

Let dimension
N\leq 3
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Then there exists a positive constant
K_{0,1}
, independent of time, such that

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq{K_{0,1}}\qquad\quad i=2,\cdots,m-1.

Proof.

Thanks to the non-negativity of solution, for all
2\leq i\leq m
, the species
a_{i}
is a positive subsolution of the following equation in the time interval
(0,2)
.

(28)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a_{i}(t,x)-d_{i}\Delta a_{i}(t,x)\leq&a_{m}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\nabla_{x}a_{i}(t,x).\gamma=&0\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
a_{i}(0,x)=&a_{i,0}\qquad\quad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

The solution corresponding to the equation (
28
) can be expressed as:

a_{i}(t,x)\leq\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s,\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{0,2}.

where
G_{d_{i}}
denotes the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d_{i}\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. We use the fact that
\displaystyle{a_{i,0}(y)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)\rm{d}y\leq 1}
for all
t\in(0,2)
. It yields

\left|\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{a}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y\right|\leq\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

The species
a_{i}
can be pointwise estimated as

a_{i}\leq\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s.

Triangular inequality of the
\mathrm{L}^{9}
norm yields:

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\|a_{0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{9}}+\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}.

Green function estimate as in (
12
) and (
13
) yields

0\leq G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x-y)\qquad 0\leq s<t_{1}.

Let us consider the following two functions

\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(s,x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &g_{d_{i}}(s,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

and

\tilde{a}_{m}(s,x)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &a_{m}(s,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(0,1)\times\Omega\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)\tilde{a}_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s.

We use Young’s convolution inequality. It yields

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)\tilde{a}_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{18}{11}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|\tilde{a}_{m}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

as
\displaystyle{1+\frac{1}{9}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{11}{18}}
. Hence

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\Big\|g_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{18}{11}}((-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|a_{m}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{0,1}}.

Furthermore (
14
) yields the following integral estimate:

\left\|g_{d_{i}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((-2,2)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{2}{N}\right),N=1,2,3,

for some constant positive
\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}
depending on
z
. We in particular choose
z=\frac{18}{11}
which is admissible because
\frac{18}{11}<1+\frac{2}{N}
for all
N=1,2,3
. Hence

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}K_{I}

where
K_{I}
as in Proposition
3
.
Defining the constant

\displaystyle{K_{0,1}:=\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}K_{I}+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\right\}},

we arrive at

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq{K_{0,1}},\ \qquad i=2,\cdots,m-1.

∎

The following result extends this
\mathrm{L}^{9}
estimate to any parabolic cylinder with unit height.

Proposition 5
.

Let dimension
N\leq 3
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Then, there exists a positive constant
K_{\Gamma}
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq{K_{\Gamma}}

for all
\tau\geq 0
and for
i=2,\cdots,m-1
.

Proof.

Consider the function
\phi_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as defined in (
11
). For
2\leq i\leq m-1
, the product
\phi_{\tau}a_{i}
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi_{\tau}a_{i}-d_{i}\Delta\phi_{\tau}a_{i}=&\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}a_{i}+\phi_{\tau}\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla_{x}\phi_{\tau}a_{i}.\gamma=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\phi_{\tau}a_{i}(\tau,x)=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Change of variable
t\rightarrow t_{1}+\tau
yields

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)&a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))\\
&=a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\ \mbox{\ in \ }\Omega_{0,2}\\
&\nabla_{x}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)).\gamma=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
&\phi_{\tau}a_{i}(0+\tau,x)=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \qquad\qquad\quad\ \quad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
G_{i}(t_{1},s,x,y)
be the Green’s function corresponding to the operator
\partial_{t_{1}}-d_{i}\Delta
. Then for all
t_{1}\in[0,2)
, we can represent the solution as follows

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{i}(t_{1},s,x,y)\left(a_{i}(y,s+\tau)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})(s+\tau,t)\right).

Thanks to the non-negativity of the Green’s function, the following is immediate:

\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{i}(t_{1},s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big),

where we have also used the fact that the solution is non-negative.

Green function estimate as in (
12
) and (
13
) yields

0\leq G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x-y)\qquad 0\leq s<t_{1}

where the function
g_{d_{i}}
as defined in (
13
). Substituting this pointwise bound for the Green’s function in the earlier inequality leads to

\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big).

Let us consider the following two functions

\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(s,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &g_{d_{i}}(s,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

and

F(s,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)&+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(0,1)\times\Omega\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{i}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)F(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s.

We use Young’s convolution inequality. It yields

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{i}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)F(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{18}{11}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|F\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

as
\displaystyle{1+\frac{1}{9}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{11}{18}}
. Hence

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\Big\|g_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{18}{11}}((-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|a_{i}(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)a_{m}(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega_{0,1})}.

Furthermore (
14
) yields the following integral estimate:

\left\|g_{d_{i}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((-2,2)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{2}{N}\right),N=1,2,3,

for some constant positive
\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}
depending on
z
. We in particular choose
z=\frac{18}{11}
which is admissible because
\frac{18}{11}<1+\frac{2}{N}
for all
N=1,2,3
. Thanks to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
estimate from Proposition
3
, we arrive at

\|\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,2})}\leq 2\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}(1+M_{\phi})K_{I},\qquad i=2,\cdots,m-1.

Substituting back
t_{1}+\tau=t
yields

\displaystyle\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq 2\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}(1+M_{\phi})K_{I}+\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq 2\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}(1+M_{\phi})K_{I}+K_{0,1}\qquad i=2,\cdots,m-1,

thanks to Proposition
4
. Defining the constant
K_{\Gamma}
as

K_{\Gamma}:=2\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}(1+M_{\phi})K_{I}+\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq 2\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}(1+M_{\phi})K_{I}+K_{0,1},

we have proved the result.
∎

So far we have managed to obtain uniform (in
\tau
)
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})
estimate on the species concentration
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
. Furthermore, our results have been valid for spatial dimension
N\leq 3
. Next we prove a conditional result in arbitrary spatial that derives an
\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}
estimate with
p>N
on the species concentration provided the same
\mathrm{L}^{p}
estimates were available for the species concentrations
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
. We emphasize here that we have access to such estimate for
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
only when
N\leq 3
. Hence for
N>3
the following is a conditional result.

Proposition 6
.

Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Suppose there exists some positive constant
K
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\texttt{K},\text{ \ for\ some\ }i\in\{2,\cdots,m-1\},\forall\tau\geq 0,

for some exponent
p>N
. Then there exists positive constant
K
, such that

\|a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\textbf{K}\ \qquad\mbox{for all}\ \tau\geq 0

and for the same exponent
p>N
as above.

Proof.

Let
\theta\in\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})
be a non-negative function with
p^{\prime}
being the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Let
\psi
be the solution of the following backward heat equation:

(29)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d_{m}\Delta\psi_{(}t,x)&=-\theta(t,x)\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla_{x}\psi_{(}t,x).\gamma&=0\qquad\ \ \qquad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\psi(\tau+2,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

From the second order regularity result (see-Appendix, Theorem
20
) says

\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\frac{C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)}{d_{m}}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Note that
\displaystyle{p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}<\frac{N+2}{2}}
. Employing integrability estimate (see-Appendix, Theorem
20
) we obtain the following estimate:

\|\psi_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq C_{IE}(\Omega,d_{m},p,s)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\qquad\forall q<\frac{(N+2)p^{\prime}}{N+2-2p^{\prime}}.

We remark that the constants
C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)
and
C_{IE}(\Omega,d,p,s)
are independent of
\tau
. Consider the function
\phi_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as in (
11
). Using the equation (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d_{m}\Delta\psi(t,x)).

Integration-by-parts in the term on the right hand side yields

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x))-d_{m}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x))\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\left(\phi^{\prime}(t)a_{m}(t,x)-\phi_{\tau}\left(a_{m}(t,x)-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\Big(\phi_{\tau}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)(a_{m}(t,x)+a_{i}(t,x))-(\partial_{t}(\phi_{\tau}a_{i})-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}a_{i}))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\Big(\phi_{\tau}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)(a_{m}(t,x)+a_{i}(t,x))\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\Big(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d_{i}\Delta\psi(t,x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)(\phi_{\tau}^{{}^{\prime}}(t)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)))+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}-\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\theta(t,x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-d_{m}+d_{i})\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\Delta\psi(t,x)=:\textbf{I}+\textbf{II}+\textbf{III}.

Thanks to the Hölder inequality, we can estimate the term
II
as follows:

|\textbf{II}|\leq\|\phi_{\tau}a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\texttt{K}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})},

where we use the hypothesis that
a_{i}
is bounded in
\mathrm{L}^{p}
.
Similarly we can estimate
III
using the Hölder inequality as follows:

\displaystyle|\textbf{III}|\leq

\displaystyle|d_{m}-d_{i}|\ \|\phi_{\tau}a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)}{d_{m}}|d_{m}-d_{i}|\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)}{d_{m}}\texttt{K}|d_{m}-d_{i}|\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Observe that
\displaystyle{p^{{}^{\prime}}<\frac{(N+2)p^{{}^{\prime}}}{N+2-2p^{{}^{\prime}}}}
. Hence we can choose a
q
such that
\displaystyle{p^{{}^{\prime}}<q<\frac{(N+2)p^{{}^{\prime}}}{N+2-2p^{{}^{\prime}}}}
. Note further that the it’s Hölder conjugate
q^{\prime}
of
q
satisfies
1<q^{\prime}<p
.

From Theorem
20
(see- Appendix), we have the integrability estimation:

\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}<C_{IE}(\Omega,d_{m},p^{\prime},q)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Note that the cut-off function satisfies

\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s)|_{[\tau+1,\tau+2]}=0\mbox{\ and\ }\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s)|_{[\tau,\tau+1]}\leq M_{\phi}.

Thanks to the Hölder inequality, the following estimate holds

\displaystyle|\textbf{I}|\leq

\displaystyle M_{\phi}\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,d_{m},N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

Since
1<q^{\prime}<p
, there exists a
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1-\alpha}{1}+\frac{\alpha}{p}.

Hence by interpolation, we obtain

\displaystyle\|a_{i}+

\displaystyle a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\|a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Thanks to the mass conservation and the hypothesis, we arrive at

|\textbf{I}|\leq M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,d_{m},N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}2M_{i}^{1-\alpha}(\texttt{K}^{\alpha}+\|a_{m}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}).

Let’s define two positive constants
K_{6},K_{7}
in the following way

\left\{\begin{aligned} K_{6}=&\texttt{K}+\frac{C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)}{d_{m}}\texttt{K}|d_{m}-d_{1}|+M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)2M_{i}^{1-\alpha}\texttt{K}^{\alpha},\\
K_{7}=&M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,d_{m},N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)2M_{i}^{1-\alpha}.\end{aligned}\right.

Putting all the estimates together, we obtain

\bigg|\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)\bigg|\leq\big(K_{6}+K_{7}\|a_{m}(t,x)\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\big)\|\theta\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

By duality, we have

\|\phi_{\tau}a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq K_{6}+K_{7}\|a_{m}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

This further implies, thanks to the property of
\phi_{\tau}
,

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau+1,\tau+2})}\leq K_{6}+K_{7}\|a_{m}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Define a sequence
\{\beta_{n}\}|
as
\beta_{n}=\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{n,n+1})}
, then we have

(30)

\displaystyle\beta_{n+1}\leq

\displaystyle K_{7}\beta_{n}^{\alpha}+K_{6}.

If
\beta_{n+1}>\beta_{n}
, Young’s inequality yields

K_{7}\beta_{n+1}^{\alpha}\leq\alpha\beta_{n+1}+(1-\alpha)K_{7}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

Substituting this in (
30
), we obtain the following bound

\beta_{n+1}\leq K_{7}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{K_{6}}{1-\alpha}.

Hence

(31)

\displaystyle\beta_{n}\leq\max\left\{K_{7}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{K_{6}}{1-\alpha},\beta_{0}\right\}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

To compute
\beta_{0}
explicitly, we start with the following integral

\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=-\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{m}(t,x)(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d_{m}\Delta\psi(t,x))

where
\psi(t,x)
is a solution of (
29
) in the cylinder
\Omega_{0,2}
. Integration-by-parts in the term on the right hand side yields

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)\left(\partial_{t}a_{m}(t,x)-d_{m}\Delta a_{m}(t,x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{m,0}\psi(0,x)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}-\psi(t,x)\left(a_{m}(t,x)-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}a_{m,0}\psi(0,x)+\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}-\psi(t,x)\Big((\partial_{t}a_{i}-d_{i}\Delta a_{i})\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a_{m,0}-a_{i,0})\psi(0,x)+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(t,x)\Big(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d_{i}\Delta\psi(t,x)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(a_{m,0}-a_{i,0})\psi(0,x)+\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}-a_{i}(t,x)\theta(t,x)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle(-d_{m}+d_{i})\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}a_{i}(t,x)\Delta\psi(t,x)

Thanks to Hölder inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)\leq

\displaystyle(\|a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)})\|\psi(0,\cdot)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}+\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|d_{m}-d_{i}|\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})}\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}.

where
q
is the Hölder conjugate of
p
. Regularity of backward heat equation (see Appendix, Theorem
21
) yileds

\int_{0}^{2}\int_{\Omega}a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)\leq C_{q}\left((\|a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)})+2\texttt{K}+2|d_{m}-d_{i}|\texttt{K}\right)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,2})}

where
C_{q}
is a positive constant depends only on
q
and the domain. We also used the fact that

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\texttt{K},\text{ \ for\ some\ }i\in\{2,\cdots,m-1\},\forall\tau\geq 0.

Hence, by duality we estimate
\beta_{0}
:

\beta_{0}=\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq C_{q}\left((\|a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)})+2\texttt{K}+2|d_{m}-d_{i}|\texttt{K}\right).

Let us denote the following constant

\textbf{K}:=\max\left\{K_{7}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}+\frac{K_{6}}{1-\alpha},C_{q}\left(\left(\|a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\}\right)+2\texttt{K}+2\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\{|d_{m}-d_{i}|\}\texttt{K}\right)\right\}

Thanks to (
31
), we conclude

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\textbf{K}\qquad\forall\tau\geq 0.

∎

Remark 3
.

From Proposition
5
, we have the following bounds when the dimension
N\leq 3
,

(32)

\displaystyle\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\textbf{K}\quad\mbox{for}\ i=2,\cdots,m-1.

Hence, thanks to Proposition
6
, we deduce in dimension
N\leq 3
that
\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{9}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}
is also uniformly bounded.

The following result derives an uniform
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})
estimate with
p>N
, in dimension
N\geq 4
. This is, however, conditional under the closeness assumption (
10
) being satisfied by the non-zero diffusion coefficients
d_{m}
and at least one of the
d_{i}
with
i\in\{2,\dots,m-1\}
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Suppose further that the closeness condition(
10
) is satisfied by the diffusion coefficients
d_{m}
and
d_{i}
for at least one
i\in\{2,\dots,m-1\}
. Let
p>N
. Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
, such that

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0}

for all
\tau\geq 0
.

Proof.

Consider the following equation with diffusion coefficient
d=\frac{d_{i}+d_{m}}{2}
where the source term
\theta
is non-negative and belongs to
\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})
, with
p^{\prime}
being the Hölder conjugate to
p
.

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+d\Delta\psi(t,x)&=-\theta(t,x)\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x).\gamma&=0\qquad\ \ \ \qquad\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\psi(\tau+2,x)&=0\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \ \mbox{in}\ \Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Note that the second order regularity estimation (see Theorem
20
) yields

\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\frac{2C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})}{d_{i}+d_{m}}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Observe that
\displaystyle{p^{\prime}=\frac{p}{p-1}<\frac{N+2}{2}}
. Furthermore, the integrability estimation (see Theorem
20
) yields

\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq C_{IE}(\Omega,d,p^{\prime},s)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{{}^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\qquad\forall q<\frac{(N+2)p^{{}^{\prime}}}{N+2-2p^{\prime}}.

Here the constants
C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p)
and
C_{IE}(\Omega,d,p,s)
are independent of
\tau
. Consider the function
\phi_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as defined in (
11
). Note that the product
\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)
satisfies the differential equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x))=a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)+\phi_{\tau}(t)(\partial_{t}a_{i}(t,x)-d_{i}\Delta a_{i}(t,x))

\displaystyle=a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)+\phi_{\tau}(t)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right).

Observe that, thanks to (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=-\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a(t,x)(\partial_{t}\psi(t,x)+(d_{i}+d-d_{i})\Delta\psi(t,x)).

Integration by parts yields

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)(\partial_{t}(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x))

\displaystyle+(d_{i}-d)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\Delta\psi(t,x)

\displaystyle\vskip 8.5359pt=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)\left(a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)+\phi_{\tau}(t)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+(d_{i}-d)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\Delta\psi(t,x).

Similarly, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\theta(t,x)=\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\psi(t,x)\left(a_{m}(x,t)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)+\phi_{\tau}(t)\left(a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}-a_{m}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+(d_{m}-d)\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\Delta\psi(t,x).

Adding the above two equalities leads to

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x))\theta(t,x)\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\psi(t,x)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x))\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{2}\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x))|\Delta\psi(t,x)|

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\textbf{I}+\textbf{II}.

We start by estimating
II
. Thanks to the Hölder inequality, we obtain the following estimate:

\displaystyle\textbf{II}\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{2}\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{{p^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\phi_{\tau}(t)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x))\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{d_{i}+d_{m}}C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{{}^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|\phi_{\tau}(t)(a_{i}+a_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Note that
\displaystyle{p^{\prime}<\frac{(N+2)p^{\prime}}{N+2-2p^{\prime}}}
. If we choose
q
such that
\displaystyle{p^{\prime}<q<\frac{(N+2)p^{\prime}}{N+2-2p^{\prime}}}
, then it’s Hölder conjugate
q^{\prime}
satisfies
1<q^{\prime}<p
.

Let us consider
I
. Note that the cut off function satisfies

\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s)|_{[\tau+1,\tau+2]}=0\mbox{\ and\ }\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s)|_{[\tau,\tau+1]}\leq M_{\phi}.

Thanks to the Hölder inequality again, the following estimate holds:

\displaystyle\textbf{I}\leq

\displaystyle M_{\phi}\|\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{{}^{\prime}}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Since
1<q^{\prime}<p
, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{\alpha}{1}+\frac{1-\alpha}{p}.

By interpolation, we have

\displaystyle\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\|a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Thus

\textbf{I}\leq M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}2M^{\alpha}_{1}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Choose the positive time independent constant
\displaystyle{C^{1}:=M_{\phi}C_{IE}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}},q)2M^{\alpha}_{1}}
. Putting our above estimates together we arrive at

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\tau}^{\tau+2}\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\tau}(t)(a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x))\theta(t,x)\right|\leq

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\left(C^{1}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\right)

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}

\displaystyle\left(\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{d_{i}+d_{m}}C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}})\|\phi_{\tau}(a_{i}+a_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\right).

Since
0\leq\theta\in\mathrm{\mathrm{}}{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})
was arbitrary, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}(a_{i}+a_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq

\displaystyle C^{1}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}

\displaystyle+\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{d_{i}+d_{m}}

\displaystyle C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})\|\phi_{\tau}(a_{i}+a_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}.

Thanks to the closeness condition (
10
) on diffusion coefficients, we arrive at the following relation

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}(a_{i}+a_{m})\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\frac{C^{1}}{1-\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{d_{i}+d_{m}}C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{\prime})}\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}.

Let’s choose the constant
\displaystyle{\texttt{C}=\frac{C^{1}}{1-\frac{|d_{i}-d_{m}|}{d_{i}+d_{m}}C_{SOR}(\Omega,N,p^{{}^{\prime}})}}
. Let’s further define a sequence

\displaystyle{{\beta}_{n}=\|a_{i}+a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{n,n+1})}}\qquad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

The sequence satisfies

{\beta}_{n+1}\leq\texttt{C}{\beta}_{n}^{1-\alpha}.

Consider the set
\Lambda=\{n\in\mathbb{N}\ \text{such that}\ {\beta}_{n}\leq{\beta}_{n+1}\}
. This implies

{\beta}_{n}\leq\ {\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\qquad\forall n\in\Lambda,

which further yields

{\beta}_{n+1}\leq\max\{{\beta}_{n},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}\}\implies{\beta}_{n}\leq\max\{{\beta}_{0},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}\},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

We now move on to estimate
{\beta}_{0}
. The sum
(a_{i}+a_{m})
satisfy the following differential equation

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(a_{i}+a_{m})-\Delta(M(a_{i}+a_{m}))=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega_{T}\\
\nabla_{x}(a_{i}+a_{m}).\gamma=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{on}\ (0,T)\times\partial\Omega\\
(a_{i}+a_{m})(0,x)=&a_{i,0}+a_{m,0}\in{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{in}\ \Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
M
is the coefficient function
\displaystyle{\frac{d_{i}a_{i}+d_{m}a_{m}}{a_{i}+a_{m}}}
, that satisfies the following bound:

0<\min\{d_{i},d_{m}\}\leq M\leq\max\{d_{i},d_{m}\}.

Thanks to the closeness condition (
10
) satisfied by
d_{i}
and
d_{m}
, the parabolic integrability estimation (see Theorem
22
) yields

\displaystyle{\beta}_{0}=\|a_{i}(t,x)+a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\left(1+\max\{d_{i},d_{m}\}\frac{C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{i}+d_{m}}{2}},p^{{}^{\prime}}}|{d_{i}-d_{m}}|}{1-C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{i}+d_{m}}{2}},p^{{}^{\prime}}}|{d_{i}-d_{m}}|}\right)\ \|a_{i,0}+a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

Thus

{\beta}_{n}\leq\max\Bigg\{\bigg(1+\max\{d_{i},d_{m}\}\frac{C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{i}+d_{m}}{2}},p^{{}^{\prime}}}|{d_{i}-d_{m}}|}{1-C^{PRC}_{{\frac{d_{i}+d_{m}}{2}},p^{{}^{\prime}}}|{d_{i}-d_{m}}|}\bigg)\ \|a_{i,0}+a_{m,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},{\texttt{C}}^{\frac{1+\alpha}{\alpha}}\Bigg\}=:C_{0}.

Hence for
p>N
, there exist the above constant
C_{0}
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0}\qquad\forall\tau\geq 0.

∎

Our next objective is to obtain an estimate on the supremum norm of the species concentrations. We also emphasize here that, so far, we have been deriving the integrability estimate for the diffusive species’ concentrations.

Our next result derives a supremum norm estimate on the species concentrations
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
in the parabolic cylinder
\Omega_{0,1}
under the assumption that an
\mathrm{L}^{p}
norm estimate is available for
a_{m}
in
\Omega_{0,1}
for some
p>N
.

Proposition 8
.

Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Suppose there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle{\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq C_{0}}

for some exponent
p>N
. Then, there exists a positive constant
K_{\infty,0,1}
such that

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq K_{\infty,0,1},\qquad\forall i=2,\cdots,m-1.

Proof.

The species
a_{i}
is a positive subsolution of the following equation in the time interval
(0,2)
, for
2\leq m-1
:

(33)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a_{i}(t,x)-d_{i}\Delta a_{i}(t,x)\leq&a_{m}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\nabla_{x}a_{i}(t,x).\gamma=&0\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
a_{i}(0,x)=&a_{i,0}\qquad\quad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

The solution corresponding to the equation (
33
) can be expressed as:

0\leq a_{i}\leq\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{0,2}.

where
G_{d_{i}}
denotes the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d_{i}\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. We use the fact that
\displaystyle{a_{i,0}(y)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)\rm{d}y\leq 1}
for all
t\in(0,2)
. It yields

\left|\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y\right|\leq\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Green function estimate as in (
12
) and (
13
) yields

0\leq G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x-y)\qquad 0\leq s<t_{1}

where the function
g_{d_{i}}
as defined in (
13
). Furthermore (
14
) yields the following integral estimate:

\left\|g_{d_{i}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((-2,2)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{2}{N}\right)

for some constant positive
\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}
depending on
z
. We in particular choose
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{z}=1-\frac{1}{p}}
which is admissible because
\displaystyle{p>\frac{N+2}{2}}
. Hence, for
t\leq 1
:

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right|\leq

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}g_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|g_{d_{i}}(t-\cdot,x-\cdot)\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((0,t)\times\Omega)}\Big\|a_{m}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}((0,t)\times\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\|g_{d_{i}}(\cdot,\cdot)\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((0,t)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|a_{m}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}((0,1)\times\Omega)}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}C_{0}.

It further yields the pointwise bound for the species
a
in the unit parabolic cylinder in initial time. More precisely, we have

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\left\{\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}+C_{0}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}=:K_{\infty,0,1}

∎

The following result proves the analogue of Proposition
6
in the parabolic cylinder
\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1}
.

Theorem 9
.

Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Suppose there exists a positive constant
C_{0}
(independent of
\tau
) such that

\|a_{m}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}\leq C_{0}

for all
\tau\geq 0
and for some exponent
p>N
. Then, there exists a positive constant
K_{\infty}
, independent of
\tau
, such that

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq K_{\infty}\qquad\forall\tau\geq 1,\ i=2,\cdots,m-1.

Proof.

Consider the function
\phi_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as in (
11
). The product
\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)
satisfies for
2\leq i\leq m-1
:

(34)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(\phi_{\tau}(t)&a_{i}(t,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x))\\
=&a_{i}(x,t)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)+\phi_{\tau}(t)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
\nabla_{x}&(\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)).\gamma=0\qquad\qquad\ \ \qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2}\\
&\phi_{\tau}a_{i}(\tau,x)=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Change of variable
t\rightarrow t_{1}+\tau
yields

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)&a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))\\
&=a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\quad\ \ \mbox{\ in \ }\Omega_{0,2}\\
&\nabla_{x}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)).\gamma=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
&\phi_{\tau}a_{i}(0,x)=0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\ \quad\ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Let
G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)
be the Green’s function corresponding to the heat operator
\displaystyle{\partial_{t_{1}}-d_{i}\Delta}
. Then for all
t_{1}\in[0,2]
, we can represent the solution as

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)

\displaystyle a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\left(a_{i}(y,s+\tau)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)\left(a_{m}-a_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)(s+\tau,t)\right).

Thanks to the non-negativity of the Green’s function and the species concentrations, an immediate pointwise bound follows.

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big).

Green function estimate as in (
12
) and (
13
) yields

0\leq G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x-y)\qquad 0\leq s<t_{1}

where the function
g_{d_{i}}
as defined in (
13
).
Substituting the above pointwise bound on the Green’s function, the earlier inequality reads

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big).

Substituting this pointwise bound for the Green’s function in the earlier inequality leads to

\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big).

Let us consider the following two functions

\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(s,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &g_{d_{i}}(s,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{otherwise}\end{aligned}\right.

and

F(s,x):=\left\{\begin{aligned} a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)&+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\qquad(s,x)\in(0,1)\times\Omega\\
&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{otherwise}.\end{aligned}\right.

Hence

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{i}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)F(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s.

We use Young’s convolution inequality. As
p>N
, there exists a
1\leq q<p
such that
\displaystyle{1+\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{1+\frac{1}{N}}+\frac{1}{q}}
. It yields

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{i}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}(t-s,x-y)F(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\Big\|\tilde{g}_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1+\frac{1}{N}}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|F\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}

Hence

\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,1})}\leq\Big\|g_{d_{i}}\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1+\frac{1}{N}}((-1,1)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\Big\|a_{i}(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(\cdot+\tau)a_{m}(\cdot+\tau,\cdot)\Big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{0,1})}.

Furthermore (
14
) yields the following integral estimate:

\left\|g_{d_{i}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{z}((-2,2)\times\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\qquad\forall z\in\left[1,1+\frac{2}{N}\right)

for some constant positive
\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}
depending on
z
. We in particular choose
\displaystyle{z=1+\frac{1}{N}}
. Change of variable
t_{1}+\tau=t
yields

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\Big(\|a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}+\|\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{m}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\Big).

As
q<p
, there exists
\alpha\in[0,1)
, such that
\displaystyle{\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1-\alpha}{1}+\frac{\alpha}{p}}
. Interpolation thus yields

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}

\displaystyle\left(\|a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{i}(t,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\right)

\displaystyle+\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\left(\|a_{m}(t,x)\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\|a_{m}(t,x)\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\right).

Using the
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})
bound on
a_{m}
, we get

\displaystyle\|\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}\leq\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}(M_{\phi}M_{1})^{1-\alpha}\|a_{i}\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(t)\|^{1-\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+2})}+C_{1}M_{1}^{1-\alpha}C_{0}^{\alpha},

where we also use the fact that
\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{1}
, for all
1\leq i\leq m
.

Consider the following positive constants

C_{1}:=1+\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}(M_{\phi}M_{1})^{1-\alpha}\mbox{\ and\ }C_{2}:=\max\limits_{2\leq i\leq m}\{\tilde{\kappa}_{1,d_{i}}\}M_{1}^{1-\alpha}C_{0}^{\alpha}.

Thanks to the property of
\phi_{\tau}
, we deduce

\|a_{i}(t,x)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau+1,\tau+2})}\leq C_{1}\|a_{i}(t,x)\|^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1})}+C_{2}\qquad\alpha\in[0,1).

Define the sequence
{\beta}_{n}=\{\|a_{i}\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{n,n+1})}\}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then
\{{\beta}_{n}\}
satisfies the following relation

(35)

\displaystyle{\beta}_{n+1}\leq

\displaystyle C_{1}{\beta}_{n}^{\alpha}+C_{2}.

If
{\beta}_{n+1}>{\beta}_{n}
, Young’s inequality yields

C_{1}{\beta}_{n+1}^{\alpha}\leq\alpha{\beta}_{n+1}+(1-\alpha)C_{1}^{\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}.

Substituting this in (
35
), we arrive at the following estimate:

Proposition 10
.

Let the dimension

\displaystyle{p>N\geq\frac{N+2}{2}}

Consider the function

Proposition 12
.

Let the dimension
N\geq 4
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7

10

d_{m}
and
d_{i}

\hat{C}

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{d=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Based on the size of the deviations in the
\mathrm{L}^{2}
norm we divide the proof in two cases. First case is about large deviations. Fix an
\epsilon\in(0,1)
.

Case 1:

\max\limits_{i=2,\cdots,m}\big\{\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}\geq\epsilon

An application of the Hölder inequality in (
16
) yields

\displaystyle D(a,b,c)\geq

\displaystyle|\Omega|^{-2/N}\frac{1}{P(\Omega)}\min\limits_{i=2\cdots,m}\{d_{i}\}\max\limits_{i=2,\cdots,m}\big\{\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}

\displaystyle\epsilon|\Omega|^{-2/N}\frac{1}{P(\Omega)}\min\limits_{i=2\cdots,m}\{d_{i}\}

\displaystyle\frac{\epsilon|\Omega|^{-2/N}}{M_{1}}\|\delta_{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\min\limits_{i=2\cdots,m}\{d_{i}\}\frac{1}{P(\Omega)}

\displaystyle{\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|A_{1}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\|a_{1}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}\leq M_{1}}
, thanks to the mass conservation.

The second case is about small deviations. Here we algebraically manipulate the last term in the dissipation functional.

Case 2:

\max\limits_{i=2,\cdots,m}\big\{\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}\leq\epsilon

We rewrite the term contributed by the rate function to the dissipation functional as follows:

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}+\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\left(\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Use of the algebraic inequality
(p-q)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}p^{2}-q^{2}
leads to

\displaystyle\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}+\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\left(\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}-A_{1}\left(\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|A_{1}\left(\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Employing the mean value theorem on the polynomial
\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(x_{j}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}

\theta_{i}

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

This further implies

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle-\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}

Next, we find an explicit bound on the term containing the supremum using the mass bound as obtained in (
25
) and the supremum bound as obtained in Proposition

\displaystyle\sup

\displaystyle\left\{\displaystyle\left(\alpha^{2}_{i}(\theta_{i}+\overline{A_{i}})^{2\alpha_{i}-2}\displaystyle\prod_{j<i}\overline{A_{j}}^{2\alpha_{j}}\right)\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j>i}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{2\alpha_{j}}\right)\right\}

(42)

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle-|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}-K_{1}\left\|a_{1}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{N}{2}}(\Omega)}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m-1}\left\|\delta_{A_{i}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}.

Using the

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod_{2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle-|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{N}}\left\|\delta_{A_{m}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}^{2}-K_{1}\tilde{K}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m-1}\left\|\delta_{A_{i}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}(\Omega)}.

Note that it follows from (
16
) that

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m}\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{2N}{N-2}}}^{2}+\eta(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

where

\eta=\frac{\min\limits_{2,\cdots,m}\{\alpha_{i}d_{i},1\}}{1+P(\Omega)|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{N}}+P(\Omega)K_{1}\tilde{K}}.

Substituting the lower bound we obtained for the last expression, we get

(43)

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\frac{\eta}{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left\|\overline{A_{m}}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We relate the missing term with the above lower bound of dissipation functional. We again divide into two subcases depending on the largeness or smallness of the product term
\displaystyle{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}}
.

Subcase 1:

\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}
is large i.e
\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\geq\epsilon
. Let

A_{1}=\frac{\overline{A_{m}}}{\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}}(1+\mu(x)).

Then the lower bound of the dissipation functional can be expressed as

\left\|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}A_{1}-\overline{A_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=|\overline{A_{m}}|^{2}\|\mu\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=|\overline{A_{m}}|^{2}\overline{{\mu}^{2}}|\Omega|.

Furthermore, the missing term or the deviation term
\displaystyle{\|\delta_{A_{1}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}}
can be expressed as

\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{\left|\overline{A_{m}}\right|^{2}}{\left|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right|^{2}}\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{|\overline{A_{m}}|^{2}}{\left|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right|^{2}}\overline{{\mu}^{2}}|\Omega|.

Hence we can compare the missing term with the lower bound of dissipation functional:

\left\|A_{1}\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\overline{A_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\epsilon\frac{|\bar{A_{m}}|^{2}}{\left|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right|^{2}}\overline{{\mu}^{2}}|\Omega|\ \geq\epsilon\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Subcase 2:

\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}<\epsilon
. In other words there exists
l\in\{2,\cdots,m-1\}
such that
\displaystyle{\overline{A_{l}}\leq\Bigg(\epsilon\Bigg)^{\frac{1}{Q}}=:\nu}
where
Q=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{m-1}\alpha_{i}
.

Recall that we are treating the case corresponding to

\displaystyle{\max\limits_{i=2,\cdots,m}\big\{\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\big\}\leq\epsilon}
. Hence

\|\delta_{A_{l}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=|\Omega|(\overline{{A_{l}}^{2}}-{\overline{A_{l}}}^{2})\leq\epsilon\implies\overline{{A_{l}}^{2}}\leq\nu(1+|\Omega|^{-1}).

Furthermore, thanks to the mass conservation property, we arrive at the following lower bound:

{\overline{A_{m}}}^{2}=\overline{{A_{m}}^{2}}-|\Omega|^{-1}\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}=\overline{{A_{m}}^{2}}+\overline{{A_{l}}^{2}}-\overline{{A_{l}}^{2}}-|\Omega|^{-1}\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq M_{l}-\nu(1+2|\Omega|^{-1}).

Using the algebraic inequality
(p-q)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}p^{2}-q^{2}
, we arrive at

\left\|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}A_{1}-\overline{A_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\left(-2\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{2\alpha_{j}}\overline{A^{2}_{1}}+{\overline{A_{m}}}^{2}\right).

Thanks to the mass conservation property, we obtain

\left\|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}A_{1}-\overline{A_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\Bigg[{M_{l}}-\nu(1+2|\Omega|^{-1})-2\epsilon^{2}M_{1}\Bigg]\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{2}\Bigg[{M_{l}}-\nu(1+2|\Omega|^{-1})-2\nu M_{1}\Bigg].

Choose
\displaystyle{\nu=\frac{1}{2}\times\frac{1}{1+2|\Omega|^{-1}+2M_{1}}{M_{l}}}
. Note that
\nu<1
. It yields

\left\|\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}A_{1}-\overline{A_{m}}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\frac{|\Omega|M_{l}}{4}\geq\frac{|\Omega|M_{l}}{4M_{1}}\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Define the following time independent constant

\displaystyle{C_{1}:=\min\left\{\frac{\epsilon}{2},\frac{|\Omega|M_{l}}{8M_{1}},\frac{\eta}{2},\frac{\epsilon|\Omega|^{-2/N}}{M_{1}}\min\limits_{i=2\cdots,m}\{d_{i}\}\frac{1}{P(\Omega)}\right\}}.

Thus we have

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\|A_{1}-\overline{A_{1}}\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}.

Choose
\hat{C}:=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{C_{1},1,\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{C(\Omega)}:i=2,\cdots,m\right\}
. We finally arrive at the following lower bound

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2}{N-1}}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

∎

The following two results give subexponential decay (in time) estimate on the relative entropy following the method of entropy. The first is for dimensions
N\geq 4
and the second is for dimensions
N\leq 3
.

Theorem 14
.

Let the dimension
N\geq 4
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the smooth positive solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
7
). Suppose further that the closeness assumption (
10
) is satisfied by the diffusion coefficients
d_{m}
and
d_{i}
for at least one
i\in\{2,\cdots,m-1\}
. Then, for any positive
\epsilon\ll 1
, there exists a finite time
T_{\epsilon}
and two time independent positive constants
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
(depending on
\epsilon
, domain
\Omega
, dimension
N
and initial data) such that

E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\leq\lambda_{1}e^{-\lambda_{2}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{N-1}}}\qquad\mbox{for all}\ t\geq T_{\epsilon}.

Proof.

The relative entropy can be written as

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}

\displaystyle\alpha_{i}(a_{i}\ln{a_{i}}-a_{i}-a_{i\infty}\ln{a_{i\infty}}+a_{i,\infty})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}\ln{\frac{a_{i}}{a_{i\infty}}}-a_{i}+a_{i\infty}).

Let us define a function
\Gamma:(0,\infty)\times(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as follows:

(44)

\Gamma(x,y):=\left\{\begin{aligned} &\frac{x\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)-x+y}{\left(\sqrt{x}-\sqrt{y}\right)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{ for }x\not=y,\\
&2\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \mbox{ for }x=y.\end{aligned}\right.

It can be shown (see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1, p.162]
for details) that the above defined function satisfies the following bound:

(45)

\displaystyle\Gamma(x,y)\leq C_{\Gamma}\max\left\{1,\ln\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)\right\}

for some positive constant
C_{\Gamma}
. Note that using the function
\Gamma
defined above, the relative entropy can be rewritten as

E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{1\infty})=\sum\limits_{1}^{m}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}\Gamma(a_{i},a_{i\infty})(A_{i}-A_{i\infty})^{2}

Using the aforementioned bound for
\Gamma
, we obtain

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})

\displaystyle-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m}\{\alpha_{i}\}C_{\Gamma}\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m}\big\{1,\ln{(\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}+1)}+|\ln{a_{i\infty}}|\big\}\sum\limits_{1}^{m}\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Recall that
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
norms of
a_{2},\cdots,a_{m-1}
are uniformly bounded (Proposition
9
) and that the
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{t})
norms of
a_{1}
and
a_{m}
can grow atmost polynomially in time (Proposition
11
). Consider the following constant

C_{1}:=\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\{\alpha_{i}\}C_{\Gamma}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\left(1+|\ln{a_{i\infty}}|+|\ln{(K_{pol})}|+\mu\right),

where
K_{pol}
as in Proposition
11
. Then we get

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})

\displaystyle-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\leq C_{1}(1+\ln{(1+t)})\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

So, other than the logarithmic growth of the supremum norm of the solution, the growth of relative entropy depends on the
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of the deviation of the
A_{i}
from
A_{i\infty}
. Observe from (
16
) and (
19
) that the dissipation functional is also related to this
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
norm of
\delta_{A_{i}}
. Observe that

\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 3\left(\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

The following observation says that the first term on the right hand side dominate the second term:

\displaystyle\|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=

\displaystyle|\Omega|\big|\overline{A_{i}}-\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\big|^{2}=|\Omega|\left(\overline{A_{i}^{2}}+\overline{A_{i}}^{2}-2\overline{A_{i}}\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq|\Omega|\left(\overline{A_{i}^{2}}-\overline{A_{i}}^{2}\right)\leq\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Here we used the fact that
\displaystyle{\overline{A_{i}}\leq\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}}
, thanks to Hölder inequality. Hence

\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Hence we deduce

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 6\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Next we borrow a result from
[
7
]
(see Appendix, Theorem
23
) which says that there exists
C_{EB}>0
, depending only on the domain and the equilibrium state
(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})
such that

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{EB}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Putting it all together along with the result from Proposition
12
, we arrive at

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\leq 6C_{1}(1+\ln{(1+t)})\frac{1+C_{EB}}{\hat{C}}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}).

Let us denote a new time independent positive constant

\displaystyle{C_{2}:=6C_{1}\frac{1+C_{EB}}{\hat{C}}}.

We arrive at the following entropy-entropy dissipation inequality

(46)

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\leq C_{2}(1+\ln{(1+t)})(1+t)^{\frac{N-2}{N-1}}D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}).

If we choose a positive
\epsilon\ll 1
, then there exists
t=T_{\epsilon}
, such that
\displaystyle{\ln{(1+t)}<(1+t)^{\frac{\epsilon}{N-1}}}
for all
\displaystyle{t\geq T_{\epsilon}}
. Consider the positive constant
\displaystyle{C_{3}=2C_{2}(1+\ln{(1+T_{\epsilon})})}
. Then the above relation (
46
) between the relative entropy and the entropy dissipation can be written as

(47)

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\leq C_{3}(1+t)^{\frac{N-2+\epsilon}{N-1}}D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}).

Furthermore, the following differential relation holds

\frac{d}{dt}\big(E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\big)=-D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}).

Using the relation between relative entropy and entropy dissipation from (
47
), yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\big(E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\big)\leq-\frac{1}{C_{3}}(1+t)^{-\frac{N-2+\epsilon}{N-1}}\big(E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\big).

Hence Grönwall lemma yields the following sub-exponential decay of relative entropy:

\displaystyle E(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})

\displaystyle-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\big(E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})\big)e^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{C_{3}(N-1)}}e^{-\frac{1-\epsilon}{C_{3}(N-1)}(1+t)^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{N-1}}}.

Let
\displaystyle{C_{4}:=E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0})-E(a_{1\infty},\cdots,a_{m\infty})}
. Furthermore, denote two time independent positive constant
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
, in the following way:

(48)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \lambda_{1}=&C_{4}e^{\frac{1-\epsilon}{C_{3}(N-1)}}\\
\lambda_{2}=&\frac{1-\epsilon}{C_{3}(N-1)}.\end{aligned}\right.

With these constants the required decay estimate follows.
∎

3.
Large time behaviour: degenerate system

This section deals with the degenerate system (
8
) where the diffusion coefficient
d_{m}
vanish. Our ultimate goal is to employ the method of entropy to demonstrate that the solution to the degenerate system (
7
) converges to the equilibrium states given by (
6
)-(
5
). We restrict ourselves to dimension
N\leq 2
as the global-in-time positive classical solution is known for the degenerate system (
8
) up to dimension
N\leq 2
[HS]. Here, we only present the case for
N=2
, the case corresponding to
N=1
will go along the same line. We recall the differential equality

\frac{dE}{dt}(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})=-D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m}),

which ensures the decay of entropy with time. Integrating the above relation in time on
[0,T]
yields

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\sup\limits_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\Omega}\alpha_{i}(a_{i}(\ln{a_{i}}-1)+1)+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\int_{\Omega_{T}}\alpha_{i}d_{i}\frac{|\nabla a_{i}|^{2}}{a_{i}}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega_{T}}\left(a_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(a_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\ln{\left(\frac{a_{m}}{\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(a_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}}\right)}\leq E(a_{1,0},\cdots,a_{m,0}).

The above estimates helps us conclude the following bounds

(49)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\int_{\Omega}\sum_{1}^{m}a_{i}\leq e^{2}|\Omega|+\frac{1}{\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}}=\tilde{M}_{1}\\
\int_{\Omega}\sum_{1}^{m}|a_{i}\ln{a_{i}}|&\leq\tilde{M}_{1}+\frac{1}{\min\limits_{1\leq i\leq m}\alpha_{i}}E(a_{i,0};i=1,2\cdots,m))+e^{-1}|\Omega|.\end{aligned}\right.

The following estimate is a particular
\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{0,2})
integral estimate of each diffusive species.

Proposition 16
.

Let dimension
N=2
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
8
). Then
there exists a constant
C_{0,p,1}>0
, depends on the domain
\Omega
,
p
and initial condition, such that

\sup\limits_{t\in[0,2)}\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0,p,1}\qquad\forall i=1,\cdot\cdot,m-1.

Proof.

The species
a_{i}
is a positive subsolution of the following equation in the time interval
(0,2)
, for
2\leq m-1
:

(50)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}a_{i}(t,x)-d_{i}\Delta a_{i}(t,x)\leq&a_{m}\qquad\qquad\qquad\mbox{ in }\Omega_{0,2}\\
\nabla_{x}a_{i}(t,x).\gamma=&0\qquad\qquad\quad\ \ \mbox{ on }\partial\Omega_{0,2}\\
a_{i}(0,x)=&a_{i,0}\qquad\quad\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Thanks to maximum principle, the solution corresponding to the equation (
50
) can be expressed as:

0\leq a_{i}\leq\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,s,x,y)a_{m}(s,y)\rm{d}y\rm{d}s\quad(t,x)\in\Omega_{0,2}.

where
G_{d_{i}}
denotes the Green’s function associated with the operator
\partial_{t}-d_{i}\Delta
with Neumann boundary condition. We use the fact that
\displaystyle{a_{i,0}(y)\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}
and
\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)\rm{d}y\leq 1}
for all
t\in(0,2)
. It yields

\left|\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t,0,x,y)a_{i,0}(y)\rm{d}y\right|\leq\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}.

Green function estimate as in (
12
) and (
13
) yields

0\leq G_{d_{i}}(t_{1},s,x,y)\leq g_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x-y)\qquad 0\leq s<t_{1}

where the function
g_{d_{i}}
as defined in (
13
).
Furthermore, in dimension
N=2
, there exists a positive constant
K_{1,d_{i}}
, depending on the domain and the dimension, such that

\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq K_{1,d_{i}}t^{-\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)}.

Minkowski’s integral inequality yields

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\int_{\Omega}g(t-s,x-y)a_{m}(s,y)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}.

An application of Young’s convolution inequality yields

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{(}\Omega)}\leq\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\int_{0}^{t}\|g(t-s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|a_{m}(s)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}.

For
t\in[0,2)
, we arrive at the following estimate

\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m}\left\{\|a_{i,0}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right\}|\Omega|^{\frac{1}{p}}+\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq m-1}\{K_{1,d_{i}}\}\frac{1}{p}2^{\frac{1}{p}}\tilde{M}_{1}=:C_{0,p,1},

where
\tilde{M}_{1}
is the mass bound or the
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
norm bound as obtained in (
49
).
∎

The following result proves the analogue of Proposition
16
in the parabolic cylinder
\Omega_{\tau,\tau+1}
.

Proposition 17
.

Let dimension
N=2
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
8
). Then,
there exists a time independent constant
C_{0,p}>0
, depending on the domain, dimension,
p
and initial condition, such that

\sup\limits_{t>0}\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)}\leq C_{0,p}\qquad\forall i=1,\cdots,m-1.

Proof.

Consider the function
\phi_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
as defined in (
11
).
The product
\phi_{\tau}(t)a_{i}(t,x)
satisfies for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi_{\tau}a_{i}-d_{i}\Delta\phi_{\tau}a_{i}=&\phi^{\prime}_{\tau}a_{i}+\phi_{\tau}\left(a_{m}-\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}a_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)\qquad\ \ \mbox{ in }\Omega_{T}\\

\phi_{\tau}a_{i}(\tau,x)=&0\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \mbox{ in }\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Change of variable

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t_{1}}(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}&+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))-d_{i}\Delta(\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x))\\

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)

\displaystyle a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)

\displaystyle\phi_{\tau}(t_{1}+\tau)a_{i}(t_{1}+\tau,x)\leq\int_{0}^{t_{1}}\int_{\Omega}G_{d_{i}}(t_{1}-s,x,y)\Big(a_{i}(s+\tau,x)\phi^{{}^{\prime}}_{\tau}(s+\tau)+\phi_{\tau}(s+\tau)a_{m}(s+\tau,x)\Big).

as defined in (

,
a_{i}

We are all equipped to prove the following result which obtains a lower bound for the dissipation functional corresponding to the degenerate system (

). The value of this result lies in the fact that the lower bound involves the term
\displaystyle{\|\nabla\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L^{2}}(\Omega)}}
even though such a term is apparently missing in the expression of the dissipation functional.

Lemma 2
.

Let dimension
N=2
. Let
(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})
be the positive smooth solution to the degenerate triangular reaction-diffusion system (
8
). Then, there exists a time independent positive constant
\hat{C}
, such that

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

Proof.

Thanks to Poincaré inequality the following lower bound of dissipation functional we have obtained in (
20
)

(53)

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We show the missing term can be recovered from the last term in the dissipation functional. We can rewrite the last term as

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\left\|A_{m}-\left(\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Use of the algebraic inequality
\displaystyle{(p-q)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}p^{2}-q^{2}}
leads to

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We use mean value theorem on the polynomial
\displaystyle\prod_{1}^{m-1}(x_{j}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}
, we remark that for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
, there exists
\theta_{i}
with
|\theta_{i}|\in\big[0,|\delta_{A_{i}}|\big]
, such that

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{i}\delta_{A_{i}}(\theta_{i}+\overline{A_{i}})^{\alpha_{i}-1}\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j<i}\overline{A_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j>i}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right).

Let’s choose a positive, time independent constant
\displaystyle{\Lambda_{1}:=\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m-1}\Bigg\{2^{Q},\alpha_{i},\displaystyle\alpha_{i}\prod_{j<i}\overline{A_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}},\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{M}_{1}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}\Bigg\}}
. For all
1\leq i\leq m-1
, it yields the following bounds

\left\{\begin{aligned} \overline{A_{i}}\leq\frac{\left(\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega}a_{i}}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}\leq&\frac{\sqrt{\tilde{M}_{1}}}{\sqrt{|\Omega|}}=\Lambda_{1}\\
\left|(\theta_{i}+\overline{A_{i}})\right|^{\alpha_{i}-1}\leq 2^{\alpha_{i}-1}(|\theta_{i}|^{\alpha_{i}-1}+&\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{i}-1})\leq\Lambda_{1}\left(|\delta_{A_{i}}|^{\alpha_{i}-1}+\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha-1}\right)\\
\left|(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})\right|^{\alpha_{j}}\leq&(1+a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\leq(\Lambda_{1}+a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}})\\
|\delta_{A_{i}}|^{\alpha_{j}-1}\leq&\Lambda_{1}(\Lambda_{1}+a_{i}^{\alpha_{j}-1}+\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{j}-1}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\tilde{M}_{1}
is mass bound or the
\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
norm bound as obtained in (
49
). Hölder inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\alpha_{i}\delta_{A_{i}}(\theta_{i}+\overline{A_{i}})^{\alpha_{i}-1}\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j<i}\overline{A_{j}}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\displaystyle\left(\prod_{j>i}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\Lambda_{1}^{4}\displaystyle\prod_{j>i}^{m-1}\left(\Lambda_{1}+a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\left(\Lambda_{1}+a_{i}^{\alpha_{j}-1}+2\Lambda_{1}^{\alpha_{j}-1}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}\left\|\prod_{j>i}^{m-1}\left(1+a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)\left(1+a_{i}^{\alpha_{j}-1}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}.

Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}\left\|\sum\limits_{j=i}^{m-1}\left(1+a_{j}^{\alpha_{j}}\right)^{m}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}

We use the fact that
\displaystyle{(x+y)^{m}\leq 2^{m}(x^{m}+y^{m})}
for all
x,y\geq 0
. It yields

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{m}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\left(1+a_{i}^{m\alpha_{i}}\right)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{m}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}\left(m|\Omega|^{\frac{2}{3}}+\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}a_{i}^{m\alpha_{i}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{3}{2}}(\Omega)}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{m}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}(1+|\Omega|)\left(2m+\left\|\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}a_{i}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{m3Q}{2}}(\Omega)}^{mQ}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thanks to Proposition
17
, we have hold over the integral expression
\displaystyle{\|a_{i}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\frac{m3Q}{2}}(\Omega)}}
for all
1\leq i\leq m-1
. Thus, we arrive at

\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 2^{m}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}(1+|\Omega|)\left(2m+mC_{0,m3Q}^{mQ}\right)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Let’s choose
\displaystyle{C_{1}:=2^{m}3(1+\Lambda_{1})^{Q+m+4}(1+|\Omega|)\left(2m+mC_{0,m3Q}^{mQ}\right)}
, where the constant
C_{0,m3Q}
defined in the Proposition
17
. The above estimate reads as

(54)

\displaystyle\left\|\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\delta_{A_{j}}+\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence, we obtain

(55)

\displaystyle\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-C_{1}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m-1}\big\|\delta_{A_{i}}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{6}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We recover the missing term
\displaystyle{\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}}
from the term
\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
.

Let
\displaystyle{A_{m}(x)=\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}(1+\mu(x))}
. Then

\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\Big(\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

and

\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\Big(\prod_{1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{2}\|\mu-\overline{\mu}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\Big(\prod_{1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\Big)^{2}\|\mu\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Thus, we recover the missing term
\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
as a lower bound of the following term

\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(\overline{A_{j}})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Hence (
55
) and (
53
), leads to

\left(1+\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m-1}\left\{\frac{P(\Omega)}{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}\right\}C_{1}\right)D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m-1}\left\{\frac{P(\Omega)}{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}\right\}\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

We choose the following time independent constant

C_{2}:=\frac{\frac{1}{2}C_{1}\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m-1}\left\{\frac{P(\Omega)}{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}\right\}}{\left(1+\max\limits_{i=1,\cdots,m-1}\left\{\frac{P(\Omega)}{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}\right\}C_{1}\right)}.

The above estimate reads as

(56)

\displaystyle D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq C_{2}\|\delta_{A_{m}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}.

Finally, choice of the time independent positive constant
\displaystyle{\hat{C}:=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{C_{2},\frac{\alpha_{i}d_{i}}{P(\Omega)}:i=1,\cdots,m\right\}}
, yields

D(a_{1},\cdots,a_{m})\geq\hat{C}\left(\sum_{1}^{m}\|\delta_{A_{i}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|A_{m}-A_{1}\displaystyle\prod_{2}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

∎

Lemma 3

(Poincaré-Wirtinger inequality)
.

There exists a positive constant
P(\Omega)
, depending only on
\Omega
and
q
, such that

P(\Omega)\left\|\nabla f\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}\geq\left\|f-\overline{f}\right\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}\qquad\mbox{ for all }f\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega),

where

q=\left\{\begin{aligned} \frac{2N}{N-2}&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N\geq 3,\\
\in[1,\infty)&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N=2,\\
\in[1,\infty)\cup\{\infty\}&\qquad\mbox{ for }\,\,N=1.\end{aligned}\right.

We refer to
P(\Omega)
as the Poincaré constant.

Theorem 20

(Second Order Regularity and Integrability estimation)
.

Let
d>0
and let
\tau\in[0,T)
. Take
\theta\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})
for some
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\psi

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi+d\Delta\psi&=-\theta\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{\tau,T},\\
\nabla\psi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\ \ \mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[\tau,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(T,x)&=0\qquad\ \ \mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then, there exists a positive constant
C_{SOR}
, depending only on the domain
\Omega
, the dimension
N
and the exponent
p
such that the following maximal regularity holds:

(57)

\displaystyle\|\Delta\psi\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq\frac{C_{SOR}}{d}\|\theta\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}.

Moreover, if
\theta\geq 0
then
\psi(t,x)\geq 0
for almost every
(t,x)\in\Omega_{\tau,T}
. Furthermore, we have

(58)

\displaystyle\mbox{If }

\displaystyle p<\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\qquad\mbox{ for all }\,\,s<\frac{(N+2)p}{N+2-2p}

\displaystyle\mbox{If }

\displaystyle p=\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{s}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\qquad\mbox{ for all }\,\,s<\infty

where the constant
C_{IE}=C_{IE}(T-\tau,\Omega,d,p,s)
and

(59)

if

\displaystyle p>\frac{N+2}{2}\,\mbox{ then }\qquad\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}\leq C_{IE}\left\|\theta\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{\tau,T})}.

The proof of (

Theorem 22

(
p^{\textrm{th}}
order integrability estimation)
.

Let
p\in(2,\infty)
and let
p^{\prime}
be its Hölder conjugate. Let
M(t,x)
be such that the following holds

\theta\leq M(t,x)\leq\Theta\quad\forall(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},

for some fixed positive constants
\theta,\Theta
. Let
\psi_{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)
and let
\psi
be a weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\psi-\Delta\left(M\psi\right)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\nabla\psi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\psi(0,x)&=\psi_{0}(x)\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Then the following estimate holds

\left\|\psi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega_{T})}\leq\left(1+\Theta K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}\right)T^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\psi_{0}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(\Omega)},

where the constant
K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}
is given by

K_{\theta,\Theta,p^{\prime}}:=\frac{C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)}{1-C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)}\qquad\mbox{ provided we have }\qquad C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)<1.

Here, the constant
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{\prime}}
is the best constant in the following parabolic regularity estimate:

\left\|\Delta\phi\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})}\leq C^{PRC}_{r,p^{\prime}}\left\|f\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})},

where
\phi,f:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
are any two functions such that
f\in\mathrm{L}^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega_{T})
and they satisfy

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\phi+r\Delta\phi&=f\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in\Omega_{T},\\
\nabla\phi\cdot n(x)&=0\qquad\mbox{ for }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,\\
\phi(T,x)&=0\qquad\mbox{ for }x\in\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

It has to be noted that
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{{}^{\prime}}}<\infty
for
r>0
and
C^{PRC}_{r,2}\leq\frac{1}{r}
and depends only on
r,p^{{}^{\prime}}
, the domain and on the dimension, i.e it is independent of time. Moreover, as
C^{PRC}_{r,p^{{}^{\prime}}}<\infty
, if we take the difference between
\theta
and
\Theta
sufficiently small, then we have the required property that

C^{PRC}_{\frac{\theta+\Theta}{2},p^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\Theta-\theta}{2}\right)<1.

Proof of the above theorem can be found in
[
2
, Proposition 1.1]
.

Theorem 23

(Entropy -Dissipation Bound Inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a bounded domain. For
1\leq i\leq m
, let

are positive constants.
Furthermore for all

a_{i\infty}+a_{m\infty}=\frac{M_{i}}{|\Omega|}\qquad\forall i=1,\cdots,m-1

a_{m\infty}=\displaystyle{\prod_{1}^{m-1}a_{j\infty}^{\alpha_{j}}}.

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\left\|\sqrt{\overline{A_{i}^{2}}}-A_{i\infty}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C_{EB}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{m}\|A_{i}-\overline{A_{i}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+\left\|A_{m}-\displaystyle\prod\limits_{j=1}^{m-1}(A_{j})^{\alpha_{j}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right).

]

Lemma 4

(Cziszár-Kullback-Pinsker Inequality)
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be an open set.
f,g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
be two non-negative functions such that
\|f\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}=\|g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}=1
. Then,

\frac{1}{2}\|f-g\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\int_{\Omega}f\ln{\left(\frac{f}{g}\right)}.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The simple exclusion process is one of simplest interacting particle systems from the perspective of probability and statistical mechanics, which
reasonably describes the dynamics of a low density gas. We refer to
[
Lig12
, Chapter VIII.6]
for more introductory and historical information.
Its dynamics has been studied from different viewpoints such as
Hydrodynamic limits
[
KL99
,
Ros81
,
KOV89
,
Rez91
]
,
Spectral gap
[
DSC93
,
Qua92
]
,

\log
-Sobolev inequalites
[
Yau97
]
, Mixing Time
[
BBHM05
,
LP17
,
Mor06
,
LL19
,
LL20
]
and so on, where the list of references is far from being exhaustive.

All the works mentioned above are about the simple exclusion process in a
homogeneous
environment. However, when there is impurity in the environment, the pattern of relaxation can change drastically, for which we point to

[
FGS16
,
FN17
]
and references therein. Only quite recently, the interest in the
disordered
setup has been flourishing for interacting particle systems (cf.
[
Fag08
,
FRS21
,
LY24
,
Sch19
]
),
where the jump
rates of particles vary spatially and are random.
In the sequel, we are mainly concerned with
Mixing time
: given a finite state space continuous-time irreducible Markov chain, how long does it take to reach equilibrium starting from any initial state in total variation distance?
Our goal is to understand how the
disordered
setup affects mixing time, comparing with the
homogeneous
setup.

1.2.
The simple exclusion process in random conductance

Given a finite connected graph
G=(V,E)
with
V
being the vertice set and
E
being the edge set, we place a Poisson clock with rate
c(e)>0
at each edge
e\in E
, and at each time a Poisson clock rings at an edge
e
, we swap the contents of the two vertices of the edge
e
. Initially, if at each site we place a particle and all the particles receive a different label, say, from
1
to
|V|
, this model is referred to as the interchange process. If we view particles labeled from
1
to
k
as indistinguishable particles and view particles labeled from
k+1
to
|V|
as empty sites, this process is called the simple exclusion process. In particular, when
k=1
, it is the random conductance model. Aldous’ spectral gap conjecture states that the spectral gaps of the interchange process and of the random conductance model are the same, confirmed by Caputo, Ligget and Richthammer
[
CLR10
]
, and thus the spectral gaps of the three processes are the same. Concerning the mixing time of the simple exclusion process, Oliveira
[
Oli13
]
shows that it is bounded above by that of the random conductance model up to a
\log|V|
factor.

From now on, our discussion focuses on the underlying graph being a line segment with
N
sites (see Figure
2
for graphical explanation). When the conductances are
homogeneously
equal to one, in
[
Wil04
]
Wilson uses harmonic analysis to provide a lower bound on the mixing time as
\frac{1+o(1)}{2\pi^{2}}N^{2}\log k
and an upper bound as
\frac{1+o(1)}{\pi^{2}}N^{2}\log k
, and conjectures that the lower bound is sharp, which is confirmed by Lacoin
[
Lac16b
]
basing on the eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator and censoring inequality established by Peres and Winkler
[
PW13
]
. When we are in the
disordered
setup: taking
(c(x,x+1))_{1\leq x<N}
IID with
{\mathbb{E}}[1/c(x,x+1)]=1
and assuming some furthermore assumption on
(c(x,x+1))_{1\leq x<N}
and
k
, we exploit the information about the eigenvalues and eigenfunctions proved in
[
Yan24b
]
to show that the
\varepsilon-
mixing time is still
\frac{1+o(1)}{2\pi^{2}}N^{2}\log k
, which implies cutoff. A next natural step is to ask: if we replace a line segment by a circle, can we extend the results in
[
Lac16a
,
Lac17
]
for the
disordered
setup?

1.3.
A short review of related models and results

1.3.1.
Random walk in random environment

When the disorder is IID in the sites of the lattice
{\mathbb{Z}}
and there is only one particle, this is the classical frame work of random walk in random environment where the particle at site
x
jumps to site
x+1
at rate
\omega_{x}\in(0,1)
and jumps to site
x-1
at rate
1-\omega_{x}
, for which we refer to
[
Szn04
,
Zei04
]
for references. Concerning the asymptotic behaviour of the random walk, Solomon
[
Sol75
]
showed that if
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\log\frac{1-\omega_{x}}{\omega_{x}}\right]\neq 0
, the random walk is transient; while if
{\mathbb{E}}\left[\log\frac{1-\omega_{x}}{\omega_{x}}\right]=0
,
the random walk is recurrent. Furthermore, in such a setting, it is also possible that the random walk is transient with sublinear speed (cf.
[
KKS75
]
), which is impossible for the random walk in
homogeneous
environment. Moreover, concerning the time required for the random walk restricted in a finite line segment of length
N
to reach equilibrium, in the transient environment case
Gantert and Kochler
[
GK13
]
showed that it is related to the depth of the deepest trap (see
[
LY24
, Eq. (58)]
for the definition of trap) and may be much larger than
N^{2}\log N
.

Model and result

2.1.
Models

Let
\left(c^{(N)}(i,i+1)\right)_{1\leq i<N}
be a sequence of strictly positive numbers. For
x<y
real numbers, we define

\llbracket x,y\rrbracket:=[x,y]\cap{\mathbb{Z}}

We consider
the simple exclusion process with
k

\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket

with its state space

\Omega_{N,k}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\left\{\xi:\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\to\{0,1\}\ \bigg|\ \sum_{i=1}^{N}\xi(i)=k\right\}\,,

and with its generator defined by (
f:\Omega_{N,k}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

\left({\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}f\right)(\xi)\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}c^{(N)}(i,i+1)\left[f(\xi\circ\tau_{i,i+1})-f(\xi)\right]\,,

(2.1)

where
\tau_{i,j}
is the transposition of the two elements
i
and
j
. Here
1
s represent particles and
0
s represent empty sites. We refer to Figure
2
for a graphical explanation.
Due to the symmetry between particles and empty sites, in the paper we always assume the number of particles satisfying
k_{N}\leq N/2
.

The uniform probability measure

\mu_{N,k}

on
\Omega_{N,k}
, defined by
\mu_{N,k}(\xi)=1/|\Omega_{N,k}|
for all
\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}
, satisfies the detailed balance condition w.r.t.
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}
, and thus it is the unique invariant probability measure. Let

(\eta_{t}^{\xi})_{t\geq 0}

denote the càdlàg trajectory of the Markov chain associated with the generator
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}
and starting with the configuration
\xi
, and let

P_{t}^{\xi}
denote its marginal distribution at time instant
t
. Moreover, for
t\geq 0
, we define the distance to equilibrium from the worst initial state to be

d_{N,k}(t)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max_{\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}}\|P_{t}^{\xi}-\mu_{N,k}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}

(2.2)

where

\|\nu_{1}-\nu_{2}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\max_{A\subset\Omega_{N,k}}\left(\nu_{1}(A)-\nu_{2}(A)\right)\,

is the
total variation distance between two probability measures
\nu_{1},\nu_{2}
on
\Omega_{N,k}
. For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we define the
\varepsilon
-mixing time to be

t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{N,k}(\varepsilon)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\inf\left\{t\geq 0:\,d_{N,k}(t)\leq\varepsilon\right\}\,.

(2.3)

Moreover,
we say that this sequence of Markov chains exhibits a cutoff if for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{N,k}(\varepsilon)}{t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{N,k}(1-\varepsilon)}\;=\;1\,.

(2.4)

We refer to the seminal paper
[
Dia96
]
for a survey about cutoff phenomenon.
We mention
[
LP17
,
Yan21
]
for an introduction to the topic of mixing time.

Furthermore,
the rate of relaxation to equilibrium is characterized by the spectral gap, denoted by
\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}
, of the generator
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}
which is
the minimal strictly positive eigenvalue of
-{\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}
.
To characterize it, we define the Dirichlet form associated with the dynamic given by (
f,g:\Omega_{N,k}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
)

\mathcal{E}_{N,k}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}-\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{N,k}f\rangle_{\mu_{N,k}}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}}\sum_{x=1}^{N-1}\mu_{N,k}(\xi)c^{(N)}(x,x+1)\left[f(\xi\circ\tau_{x,x+1})-f(\xi)\right]^{2}\,,

where
\langle f,g\rangle_{{\mu_{N,k}}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{\xi\in\Omega_{N}}\mu_{N,k}(\xi)f(\xi)g(\xi)
is the usual inner product in
L^{2}(\Omega_{N,k},\mu_{N,k})
. Moreover, the spectral gap
\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}
is given by

\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\,\,\inf_{f\ :\ \mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{N,k}}(f)>0}\,\,\frac{\mathcal{E}_{N,k}(f)}{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{N,k}}(f)}

where
\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{N,k}}(f)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\langle f,f\rangle_{\mu_{N,k}}-\langle f,\mathbf{1}\rangle^{2}_{\mu_{N,k}}
.
Moreover, the relation between the mixing time and the spectral gap is
as follows
(cf.
[
Yan21
, Theorem 3.4]
): for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,

\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}}\log\frac{1}{2\varepsilon}\;\leq\;t_{\mathrm{mix}}^{N,k}(\varepsilon)\;\leq\;\frac{1}{\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}}\log\frac{1}{2\varepsilon\mu_{\min}}

(2.5)

where
\mu_{\min}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\min_{\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}}\mu_{N,k}(\xi)
, and the distance to equilibrium is related with the spectral gap by (cf.
[
Yan21
, Theorem 3.4]
):

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\log d_{N,k}(t)\;=\;-\mathrm{gap}_{N,k}\,.

(2.6)

In this paper, we are interested in the question: how does the mixing time depend on the conductance sequence
(c^{(N)}(x,x+1))_{1\leq x<N}
? In the sequel, we always set

r^{(N)}(x-1,x)\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;1/c^{(N)}(x-1,x),\quad\forall\,x\in\llbracket 1,N-1\rrbracket\,,

which is referred to as resistance. For simplicity of notations, we drop the superscript ”
(N)
” when there is no confusion in the context. In this paper, we always assume

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{N}\sup_{2\leq m\leq N}\,\left|(r^{(N)}(1,m)-(m-1)\right|\;=\;0\,.

(2.7)

We restate the results concerning the spectral gap and principle eigenfunction in
[
Yan24b
]
which combine Proposition 1.1 and Theorem 1.3 of
[
Yan24b
]
in the following.

Theorem A
.

Let
g_{i}
be the
i
th eigenfunction of
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,1}
with
g_{i}(1)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}1
, whose corresponding eigenvalue is denoted by
-\lambda_{i}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,1}g_{i}=-\lambda_{i}g_{i}
, where

0\;=\;\lambda_{0}\;<\;\lambda_{1}\;<\;\lambda_{2}\;<\lambda_{3}\;<\;\cdots\;<\;\lambda_{N-1}\,.

(2.8)

Then
g_{1}
is strictly decreasing.
If the condition (
2.7
) on the resistances holds, for any prefixed constant
K_{0}
and all
1\leq i\leq K_{0}

we have

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{N^{2}\lambda_{i}}{\pi^{2}}\;=\;i^{2}\,.

(2.9)

Furthermore, setting

h_{i}(x)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\cos\left(\frac{i\pi(x-1/2)}{N}\right),\;\;\;\forall\,x\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\,,

(2.10)

concerning the shape and (weighted) derivative of the eigenfunctions, for any prefixed constant
K_{0}
and all
1\leq i\leq K_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\;\sup_{x\in\llbracket 1,\,N\rrbracket}\;

\displaystyle\left|g_{i}(x)-h_{i}(x)\right|\;=\;0\,,

(2.11)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\;\sup_{x\in\llbracket 1,\,N\rrbracket}\;

\displaystyle\left|N(c\nabla g_{i})(x)-N(\nabla h_{i})(x)\right|\;=\;0\,,

(2.12)

Remark 2.1
.

Note that when the sequence
(r^{(N)}(x-1,x))_{2\leq x\leq N}
is IID with common law denoted by
{\mathbb{P}}
and its expectation
{\mathbb{E}}[r(x,x+1)]=1
, by the strong law of large numbers we have

{\mathbb{P}}\left(\lim_{N\to\infty}\,\frac{1}{N}\max_{2\leq m\leq N}\,|r(1,m)-(m-1)|=0\right)\;=\;1\,,

and thus the assumption (

Furthermore, we also need the following assumptions concerning resistances and the number of particles below.

Assumption 2.2

There exist constants

3.

3.1.
Partial order

For
\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}
, we define a height function
h^{\xi}
associated with
\xi
by
h^{\xi}(0)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0
and for all

h^{\xi}(x)\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\sum_{y=1}^{x}\xi(y)-\frac{k}{N}x\,.

(3.1)

The term

is subtracted so that

has zero mean under equilibrium.
Furthermore, we identify the image set
\{h^{\xi}:\xi\in\Omega_{N,k}\}
with
\Omega_{N,k}
, as it brings no confusion.
We define a partial order

"\leq"

on
\Omega_{N,k}\times\Omega_{N,k}
as follows: for

,

\left(\xi\;\leq\;\xi^{\prime}\right)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\left(\forall\,x\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket\,,\;h^{\xi}(x)\;\leq\;h^{\xi^{\prime}}(x)\right)\,.

(3.2)

The maximal and minimal configurations of
\Omega_{N,k}
are respectively

\wedge=\wedge_{N,k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x\leq k\}}\,,\quad\vee=\vee_{N,k}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathbf{1}_{\{N-k<x\leq N\}}\,.

The notations of
\wedge
and
\vee
are due to the shapes of the maximal and minimal height functions respectively. With an abuse of notation,
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,k}
also denotes the generator of the Markov chain with state space as the set of heigh functions.

into consecutive maximal monotone segments. Note that in

\#(\mathcal{D}_{t}\cap\llbracket a,b\rrbracket)\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\left\lfloor\frac{b-a}{Q(h_{t}^{\mu})}\right\rfloor\;\geq\;\frac{1}{3}\frac{b-a}{Q(h_{t}^{\mu})}\,.

Moreover, we observe that

and using (
5.34
) and (
5.35

Therefore, we have

This yields the desired result, combining with (

Appendix A
Upper bound on the covariance of the probability measure
\nu

In this section, our goal is to provide an upper bound on the covariance of the probability measure
\nu
defined above (
4.18
), which is the following.

Lemma A.1
.

For any
\gamma>0
, if
(\log N)^{1+\gamma}\leq k\leq N/64
, for all
\delta\in(0,\frac{\gamma}{2(1+\gamma)})
and for all
N
sufficiently large we have

\sum_{1\leq x,\,y\leq N}\,\left|\nu\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\right)\right)-\nu\left(\xi(x)\right)\nu\left(\xi(y)\right|\;\leq\;2^{12}k^{2-\delta}\,.

(A.1)

Proof.

Concerning the diagonal terms,
note that

\sum_{x=1}^{N}\,\left[\nu\left(\xi(x)\right)-\left(\nu\left(\xi(x)\right)\right)^{2}\right]\;\leq\;\sum_{x=1}^{N}\,\nu\left(\xi(x)\right)\;\leq\;k\,.

Turning to off-diagonal terms, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x<y\leq N}\,|\left[\nu\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\right)-\nu\left(\xi(x)\right)\nu(\xi(y)\right]|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq x<y\leq N}\,\left|\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq y\}}\right)-\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq x\}}\right)\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq y\}}\right)\right|\,.

To estimate the correlation, we first prove that the position of
k^{th}
particle is well concentrated around the midpoint:
(recall
\bar{\xi}(\cdot)
defined in (
4.19
))

\mu_{N,2k}\left(\left|\bar{\xi}(k)-\frac{N}{2}\right|\geq\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)\;=\;\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)\geq\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)+\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)\leq\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)\,.

(A.2)

Concerning the first term in the r.h.s. of (
A.2
) and writing
j^{\prime}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right\rceil
, we have

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)\geq\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{j=j^{\prime}}^{N-k}\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)

(A.3)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{j=j^{\prime}}^{N-k}{j-1\choose k-1}{N-j\choose k}{N\choose 2k}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{N}{2}{j^{\prime}-1\choose k-1}{N-j^{\prime}\choose k}{N\choose 2k}^{-1}\,,

where we have used that the map

j\mapsto{j-1\choose k-1}{N-j\choose k}

is decreasing for
j\in\llbracket\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right\rceil,\,N-k\rrbracket
due to that

{j\choose k-1}{N-j-1\choose k}{j-1\choose k-1}^{-1}{N-j\choose k}^{-1}\leq 1

if and only if

2kj-kN+N-j\;\geq\;0\,.

Using (
4.26
) and (
4.27
),
we now provide an upper bound on the r.h.s. of (
A.3
) by:

\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}{j^{\prime}-1\choose k-1}{N-j^{\prime}\choose k}{N\choose 2k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{N}{2}\cdot\frac{k}{j^{\prime}}{j^{\prime}\choose k}{N-j^{\prime}\choose k}{N\choose 2k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\;\frac{Nk}{2j^{\prime}}{2k\choose k}{N-2k\choose j^{\prime}-k}{N\choose j^{\prime}}^{-1}

\displaystyle\leq\;\frac{Nk}{2j^{\prime}}\frac{2^{2k\cdot H_{2}\left(\frac{k}{2k}\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi k\left(1-\frac{k}{2k}\right)}}\frac{2^{(N-2k)H_{2}\left(\frac{j^{\prime}-k}{N-2k}\right)}}{\sqrt{2\pi(j^{\prime}-k)\left(1-\frac{j^{\prime}-k}{N-2k}\right)}}\frac{\sqrt{8\pi j^{\prime}\left(1-\frac{j^{\prime}}{N}\right)}}{2^{N\cdot H_{2}\left(\frac{j^{\prime}}{N}\right)}}

\displaystyle\leq\;4\sqrt{k}2^{N\left(\frac{2k}{N}H_{2}\left(\frac{k}{2k}\right)+\frac{N-2k}{N}H_{2}\left(\frac{j^{\prime}-k}{N-2k}\right)-H_{2}\left(\frac{j^{\prime}}{N}\right)\right)}

\displaystyle\leq\;4\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}N\cdot\frac{2k}{N}\cdot\frac{N-2k}{N}\left(\frac{k}{2k}-\frac{j^{\prime}-k}{N-2k}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\;4\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{j^{\prime}-k}{N-2k}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\;4\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,,

which tends to zero as
k
tends to infinity.
Similarly, for the other term in (
A.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)\leq\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\;4\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,,

and then

\mu_{N,2k}\left(\left|\bar{\xi}(k)-\frac{N}{2}\right|\geq\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)\;\leq\;8\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,.

(A.4)

For
y\geq\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}
, by (

) we have

\displaystyle N^{2}\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\right)\;\leq\;N^{2}\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)\geq\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)\;\leq\;4N^{2}\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,,

\displaystyle N^{2}\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\right)\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\right)\;\leq\;4N^{2}\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,,

which tend to zero as

.
Therefore, in the remaining we only need to consider
y<\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}
.
Furthermore, note that
for
j\in\llbracket k,N-k\rrbracket
, under the conditional probability measure
\mu_{N,2k}\left(\cdot\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)
, the first
k-1
particles are uniformly distributed on the segment
\llbracket 1,j-1\rrbracket
.
Therefore, for
1\leq x<y
we have

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{x<y\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)

(A.5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{x<y<j\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)+\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{x<y=j\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{(j-1)(j-2)}\mathbf{1}_{\{x<y<j\}}+\frac{k-1}{j-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{x<y=j\}}

and

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{x\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)

(A.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{x<j\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)+\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{x=j\}}\,|\,\bar{\xi}(k)=j\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{k-1}{j-1}\mathbf{1}_{\{x<j\}}+\mathbf{1}_{\{x=j\}}\,.

Moreover, for
i,j,\ell\in\llbracket\left\lfloor\frac{N}{2}-\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right\rfloor,\,\left\lceil\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right\rceil\rrbracket
, for all
N
sufficiently large we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{k-1}{j}-\frac{k-1}{N/2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\;8\frac{k^{1-\delta}}{N}\,,\quad\quad\left|i\cdot j-\frac{N^{2}}{4}\right|\;\leq\;2\frac{N^{2}}{k^{\delta}}\,,

(A.7)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{(k-1)(k-2)}{(j-1)(j-2)}-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{N^{2}/4}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\;2^{7}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}\,,\quad\quad\left|\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{i\cdot j}-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{N^{2}/4}\right|\;

\displaystyle\leq\;2^{7}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}\,.

The analysis is classified in three cases, listed below by (i) for
1\leq i\leq 3
.
(1) For

, by (

\left|\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq y\}}\right)-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{N^{2}/4}\right|\;\leq\;2^{4}\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}+2^{7}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}\,.

(A.8)

By (

), (
A.6
) and (

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq x\}}\right)-\frac{k-1}{N/2}\right|\;\leq\;48\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}+8\frac{k^{1-\delta}}{N}\,,

(A.9)

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(x)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq x\}}\right)\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{\bar{\xi}(k)\geq y\}}\right)-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{N^{2}/4}\right|\ \;\leq\;2^{9}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}\,,

(2) Now we head to deal with the case

and

\displaystyle\leq\;2^{8}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}+8\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}\,,

.

) and (

\displaystyle\left|\mu_{N,2k}\left(\xi(y)\mathbf{1}_{\{y\leq\bar{\xi}(k)\}}\right)-\frac{k-1}{N/2}\mu_{N,2k}\left(y<\bar{\xi}(k)<\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)-\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)=y\right)\right|

\displaystyle 8\frac{k^{1-\delta}}{N}+8\sqrt{k}2^{-\frac{2}{\log 2}k^{1-2\delta}}

\displaystyle\Bigg|I_{N,2k}(x,y)-\frac{(k-1)^{2}}{N^{2}/4}\mu_{N,2k}\left(y<\bar{\xi}(k)<\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{k^{\delta}}\right)-\frac{k-1}{N/2}\mu_{N,2k}\left(\bar{\xi}(k)=y\right)\Bigg|\;\leq\;2^{9}\frac{k^{2-\delta}}{N^{2}}

(A.13)

with

Appendix B
Height function

Recalling (
3.1
) and (
5.7
) and setting

f(t,x)\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;{\mathbf{E}}\left[h_{t}^{\wedge}(x)\right]\,,\quad\forall\,t\geq 0\,,\;\;\forall\,x\in\llbracket 0,N\rrbracket\,,

for
1\leq x<N
we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(t,x)

\displaystyle=\;{\mathbf{E}}\left[({\mathcal{L}}h_{t}^{\wedge})(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\;c(x,x+1){\mathbf{E}}\left[\left(h_{t}^{\wedge}(x+1)-h_{t}^{\wedge}(x)\right)-\left(h_{t}^{\wedge}(x)-h_{t}^{\wedge}(x-1)\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\;c(x,x+1)\Delta_{x}f

where

\Delta_{x}f\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;f(t,x+1)+f(t,x-1)-2f(t,x)\,.

In this section, our goal is to solve the following equation:

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}f(t,x)\;=\;c(x,x+1)\Delta_{x}f,&\forall\,x\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}\,,\\
f(t,0)\;=\;f(t,N)\;=\;0\,,&\forall\,t\geq 0\,,\\
f(0,x)\;=\;h_{0}^{\wedge}(x),&\forall\,x\in\{0,N\}\,.\end{cases}

(B.1)

Note that the matrix corresponding to the operator
c(x,x+1)\Delta_{x}
is the following
N-1
by
N-1
matrix
\widehat{A}
given by

\begin{bmatrix}-2c(1,2)&c(1,2)&0&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
c(2,3)&-2c(2,3)&c(2,3)&0&\dots&0&0&0\\
0&c(3,4)&-2c(3,4)&c(3,4)&\dots&0&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&c(N-2,N-1)&-2c(N-2,N-1)&c(N-2,N-1)\\
0&0&0&0&\dots&0&c(N-1,N)&-2c(N-1,N)\end{bmatrix}\,,

i.e.

\begin{cases}\widehat{A}(1,1)=-2c(1,2),\;\widehat{A}(1,2)=c(1,2),\\
\widehat{A}(x,x-1)=c(x,x+1),\,\widehat{A}(x,x)=-2c(x,x+1),\,\widehat{A}(x,x+1)=c(x,x+1),\;1<x<N-1\,,\\
\widehat{A}(N-2,N-1)=c(N-1,N),\;\widehat{A}(N-1,N-1)=-2c(N-1,N),\\
\end{cases}

(B.2)

and all other unmentioned entries are zero.
In order to apply the method in
[
LP17
, Lemma 12.2]
to obtain an orthogonal basis, we first define a probability measure
\nu
on
\{1,2\cdots,N-1\}
satisfying

\nu(x)c(x,x+1)\;=\;\nu(x+1)c(x+1,x+2)\,,\quad\forall\,x\in\{1,\cdots,N-2\}\,,

(B.3)

from which we obtain

\nu(x)\;=\;\nu(1)\frac{c(1,2)}{c(x,x+1)}\,,

and then

\nu(x)\;=\;\frac{r(x,x+1)}{\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}r(i,i+1)}\,.

(B.4)

Our next step is to prove the existence of an orthonormal basis
({\mathbf{g}}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
with corresponding eigenvalue
-\kappa_{i}
of the operator
c(x,x+1)\Delta_{x}
, i.e.

\begin{cases}c(x,x+1)\Delta_{x}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\;=\;-\kappa_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x),\text{ for }x\in\{1,2,\cdots,N-1\}\,,\\
\langle{\mathbf{g}}_{i},{\mathbf{g}}_{j}\rangle_{\nu}\;=\;\delta_{i,j}\,.\end{cases}

(B.5)

Assuming the existence of such a basis, we know that

\{e^{-\kappa_{i}t}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
is a basis of the operator in (
B.1
).
Hence, we have

f(t,x)\;=\;{\mathbf{E}}[h_{t}^{\wedge}(x)]\;=\;\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\langle h_{0}^{\wedge},{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\rangle_{\nu}\cdot e^{-\kappa_{i}t}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)\,.

(B.6)

Lemma B.1
.

Such a basis
\{{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
exists.

Proof.

We follow the approach in
[
LP17
, Lemma 12.2]
to provide a proof for the sake of completeness. Recalling (
B.2
),
we define another
matrix
\widehat{B}
by

\widehat{B}(x,y)\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\nu(x)^{1/2}\widehat{A}(x,y)\nu(y)^{-1/2},\quad\forall\,x,y\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}\,.

By (
B.2
) and (
B.3
), we know that

\widehat{B}(x,y)=\widehat{B}(y,x)
, and thus
\widehat{B}
is diagonalizable.
Let

(\widehat{g}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N-1}:\,\{1,\cdots,N-1\}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}

be the orthonormal eigenfunctions of
\widehat{B}
with
corresponding eigenvalues
(-\kappa_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
satisfying

-\kappa_{1}\;\geq\;-\kappa_{2}\;\geq\;\cdots\;\geq\;-\kappa_{N-1}\,,

(B.7)

i.e.

\begin{cases}\widehat{B}\widehat{g}_{i}\;=\;-\kappa_{i}\widehat{g}_{i}\,,&\text{ for }i\in\{1,2,\cdots,N-1\},\\
\langle\widehat{g}_{i},\widehat{g}_{j}\rangle\;=\;\delta_{i,j}\,,&\text{ for }i,j\in\{1,2,\cdots,N-1\}\,,\end{cases}

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the usual inner product in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
.
Moreover,
writing
D\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\mathrm{diag}(\widehat{\nu}(1),\cdots,\widehat{\nu}(N-1))
, we have

\widehat{B}=D^{1/2}\widehat{A}D^{-1/2}

and thus
\widehat{B}\widehat{g}_{i}=D^{1/2}\widehat{A}D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i}=-\kappa_{i}\widehat{g}_{i}
, which implies

\widehat{A}D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i}\;=\;-\kappa_{i}D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i}\,.

Viewing
\widehat{g}_{i}
as a vector in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N-1}
, we have

\delta_{i,j}\;=\;\langle\widehat{g}_{i},\widehat{g}_{j}\rangle\;=\;\widehat{g}_{i}^{T}\widehat{g}_{j}\;=\;(D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i})^{T}DD^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{j}\;=\;\langle D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i},D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{j}\rangle_{\nu}\,,

where
\widehat{g}_{i}^{T}
denotes the transpose of the vector
\widehat{g}_{i}
. Therefore

{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}D^{-1/2}\widehat{g}_{i}

is an eigenfunction of
\widehat{A}
with eigenvalue
-\kappa_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,2,\cdots N-1\}
and satisfies (
B.5
).
∎

Lemma B.2
.

Concerning the eigenvalues in (
B.7
), we have

0\;>\;-\kappa_{1}\;>\;-\kappa_{2}\;>\;\cdots\;>\;-\kappa_{N-1}\,.

(B.8)

Proof.

For convention, we define
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(0)={\mathbf{g}}_{i}(N)\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}0
for all
1\leq i\leq N-1
. Since
for all
1\leq x\leq N-1
,

c(x,x+1)\left[{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x-1)+{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x+1)-2{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)\right]\;=\;-\kappa_{i}{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)

(B.9)

we have

{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x+1)\;=\;\left(2-\frac{\kappa_{i}}{c(x,x+1)}\right){\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)-{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x-1)\,.

(B.10)

By (
B.10
) we know that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1)\neq 0
. Otherwise
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv 0
, a contradiction.
Without lost of generality, we always assume
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1)>0
.
Furthermore, if there exists
x\in\{1,2,\cdots,N-1,N\}
such that
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x-1)={\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)=0
, then
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}\equiv 0
which is a contradiction to
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}
being an eigenfunction.

Now we argue that
\kappa_{i}>0
. Take
x_{i}\in\{1,\cdots,N-1\}
such that

{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x_{i})\;=\;\max_{0\leq x\leq N}\,{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)>0\,.

Plugging
x_{i}
in (
B.9
), we obtain that
-\kappa_{i}\leq 0
. While if
-\kappa_{i}=0
, then by (
B.9
) we have
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x)\equiv{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(x_{i})
for all
x\in\{0,1,\cdots,N\}
which is a contradiction to
0={\mathbf{g}}_{i}(0)<{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1)
. Therefore, we have
-\kappa_{i}<0
.

Now we show that all
(-\kappa_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
are different. It is sufficient to consider
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}/{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1)
. Observe that
by (
B.10
),
-\kappa_{i}
determines the eigenfunction
{\mathbf{g}}_{i}/{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1)
. Since the eigenfnctions
({\mathbf{g}}_{i}/{\mathbf{g}}_{i}(1))_{1\leq i\leq N-1}
are different, we obtain that

0\;>\;-\kappa_{1}\;>\;-\kappa_{2}\;>\;\cdots\;>\;-\kappa_{N-1}\,.

∎

For any prefixed integer
K_{0}
,
we now turn to estimate
(\kappa_{i})_{1\leq i\leq K_{0}}
, and their corresponding shapes of the eigenfunction
({\mathbf{g}}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq K_{0}}
.
For
\kappa>0
, in view of (
B.10
) we define

.

We have that
(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}
is a base of eigenfunctions of the generator
{\mathfrak{L}}
.

Proof.

Note that
(X_{t}^{i},Y_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0}
either lives in the upper triangle or the lower triangle of the
N
by
N
square, and once they hit the diagonal line they move only in the diagonal line since then. Without loss of generality, we assume
(X_{t}^{i},Y_{t}^{i})_{t\geq 0}
stays in the lower triangle.

We now show that

{\mathfrak{L}}u_{i,j}\;=\;-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})u_{i,j}\,.

(C.14)

For
x=y
,
u_{i,j}(x,x)=0
and thus (
C.14
) holds for the diagonal points.
We now treat the case
x\neq y
:

\displaystyle({\mathfrak{L}}u_{i,j})(x,y)

\displaystyle=\;c(x,x+1)\left[u_{i,j}(x+1,y)-u_{i,j}(x,y)\right]

(C.15)

\displaystyle+\;c(x-1,x)\left[u_{i,j}(x-1,y)-u_{i,j}(x,y)\right]

\displaystyle+\;c(y,y+1)\left[u_{i,j}(x,y+1)-u_{i,j}(x,y)\right]

\displaystyle+\;c(y-1,y)\left[u_{i,j}(x,y-1)-u_{i,j}(x,y)\right]\,.

Plugging the definition of
u_{i,j}
in (
C.15
) and using the fact that
(\widetilde{g}_{i})_{0\leq i\leq N-1}
are eigenfunctions of
{\mathcal{L}}_{N,1}
, we obtain (
C.14
).

Now we move to show that
(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}
are orthogonal under the usual inner product

\langle f,g\rangle\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\sum_{1\leq x\leq y\leq N}f((x,y))g((x,y))
where
f,g:\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket^{2}\mapsto{\mathbb{R}}
.
Observe that

u_{i,j}(x,y)=-u_{i,j}(y,x)\,,\;\;u_{i,j}(x,x)=0

and then for
0\leq i<j<N
and
0\leq m<n<N
,

\displaystyle 2\langle u_{i,j},u_{m,n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq N}u_{i,j}(x,y)u_{m,n}(x,y)

(C.16)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq N}\left[\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)\widetilde{g}_{j}(y)-\widetilde{g}_{i}(y)\widetilde{g}_{j}(x)\right]\cdot\left[\widetilde{g}_{m}(x)\widetilde{g}_{n}(y)-\widetilde{g}_{m}(y)\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)\right]

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)\widetilde{g}_{j}(y)\widetilde{g}_{m}(x)\widetilde{g}_{n}(y)-\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)\widetilde{g}_{j}(y)\widetilde{g}_{m}(y)\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{1\leq x,y\leq N}-\widetilde{g}_{i}(y)\widetilde{g}_{j}(x)\widetilde{g}_{m}(x)\widetilde{g}_{n}(y)+\widetilde{g}_{i}(y)\widetilde{g}_{j}(x)\widetilde{g}_{m}(y)\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{1\leq x\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)\widetilde{g}_{m}(x)\sum_{1\leq y\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{j}(y)\widetilde{g}_{n}(y)-\sum_{1\leq x\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)\sum_{1\leq y\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{j}(y)\widetilde{g}_{m}(y)

\displaystyle-\sum_{1\leq x\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{j}(x)\widetilde{g}_{m}(x)\sum_{1\leq y\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{i}(y)\widetilde{g}_{n}(y)+\sum_{1\leq x\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{j}(x)\widetilde{g}_{n}(x)\sum_{1\leq y\leq N}\widetilde{g}_{i}(y)\widetilde{g}_{m}(y)

\displaystyle=\;N^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{i=m,j=n\}}-N^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{i=n,j=m\}}-N^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{i=n,j=m\}}+N^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{i=m,j=n\}}

\displaystyle=\;2N^{2}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{i=m,j=n\}}-\mathbf{1}_{\{i=n,j=m\}}\right)\,.

Note that in the second indicator above if
j=m
,
the condition
i<j=m<n
guarantees that
\{i=n,j=m\}
does not hold. Therefore
(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}
are orthogonal.
Since the dimension of
{\mathfrak{L}}
(viewing the diagonal line as cemetery) is
N(N-1)/2
and the dimension of vector space spanned by
(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}
is also
N(N-1)/2
, then
(u_{i,j})_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}
is a base of eigenfunctions of
{\mathfrak{L}}
.
∎

With Lemma
C.3
at hand, we are ready to prove Proposition
C.2
.

Proof of Proposition
C.2
.

In fact, (
C.11
) follows from a union bound and the following inequality: for all
t\geq t_{\delta/2}

{\mathbf{P}}_{x_{0},y_{0}}\left[X_{t}^{i}\neq Y_{t}^{i}\right]\;\leq\;2^{14}K_{0}^{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}\,.

(C.17)

Without loss of generality, we assume
(X_{0}^{i},Y_{0}^{i})=(x_{0},y_{0})
with
x_{0}<y_{0}
, and then

\displaystyle{\mathbf{P}}_{x_{0},y_{0}}\left[X_{t}^{i}\neq Y_{t}^{i}\right]

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{1\leq x<y\leq N}e^{t{\mathfrak{L}}}\left((x_{0},y_{0}),(x,y)\right)

(C.18)

\displaystyle=\;\delta_{(x_{0},y_{0})}e^{t{\mathfrak{L}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x<y\leq N\}}\,,

where
\delta_{(x_{0},y_{0})}
is a row vector of size
N(N+1)/2
and
\mathbf{1}_{1\leq x<y\leq N}
is a column vector of size
N(N+1)/2
.
Furthermore, we write

\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x<y\leq N\}}\;=\;\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}a_{i,j}u_{i,j}

where

a_{i,j}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\frac{\langle\mathbf{1}_{1\leq x<y\leq N},u_{i,j}\rangle}{\langle u_{i,j},u_{i,j}\rangle}\,.

Then, by (
C.14
) we have

\displaystyle e^{t{\mathfrak{L}}}\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x<y\leq N\}}

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}a_{i,j}e^{t{\mathfrak{L}}}u_{i,j}

(C.19)

\displaystyle=\;\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}a_{i,j}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}u_{i,j}\,.

Then the r.h.s. in (
C.18
) is equal to

\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}a_{i,j}\delta_{(x_{0},y_{0})}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}u_{i,j}\;=\;\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}a_{i,j}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}u_{i,j}(x_{0},y_{0})\,.

(C.20)

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
C.16
) we have

|a_{i,j}|\;\leq\;\frac{\langle\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x<y\leq N\}},\mathbf{1}_{\{1\leq x<y\leq N\}}\rangle^{1/2}}{\langle u_{i,j},u_{i,j}\rangle^{1/2}}\;=\;\frac{1}{N}\sqrt{\frac{N(N-1)}{2}}\;\leq\;1\,.

Then the r.h.s. in (
C.20
)
can be bounded from above by

\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}|u_{i,j}(x_{0},y_{0})|\,.

(C.21)

In order to provide upper bound on
|u_{i,j}(x_{0},y_{0})|
,
we head to provide an upper bound on
\max_{1\leq x\leq N}|\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)|
with
1\leq i\leq K_{0}
, based on (
2.11
).
Note that for all
1\leq i\leq K_{0}
,

\#{\mathcal{G}}_{i}\;\mathrel{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{$\displaystyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle:$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle:$}}}{=}}\;\#\left\{j\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket:\left|\cos\left(\frac{i\pi(j-1/2)}{N}\right)\right|\geq\frac{1}{4}\right\}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{16}N\,.

Then by (
2.11
) and for
x\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i}
, we have

\left|\frac{\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)}{\widetilde{g}_{i}(1)}\cos\left(\frac{i\pi/2}{N}\right)\right|\;\geq\;\frac{1}{8}\,,

and then

1\;=\;\langle\widetilde{g}_{i},\widetilde{g}_{i}\rangle_{\mu_{N,1}}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i}}\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in{\mathcal{G}}_{i}}\frac{\widetilde{g}_{i}(1)^{2}}{2^{6}\cos^{2}\left(\frac{i\pi}{2N}\right)}\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2^{10}}\frac{\widetilde{g}_{i}(1)^{2}}{\cos^{2}\left(\frac{i\pi}{2N}\right)}\,,

which implies
|\widetilde{g}_{i}(1)|\leq 2^{5}|\cos\left(\frac{i\pi}{2N}\right)|.
Then by (
2.11
), we obtain

\max_{1\leq x\leq N}|\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)|\;\leq\;2|\widetilde{g}_{i}(1)|\frac{1}{|\cos\left(\frac{i\pi}{2N}\right)|}\;\leq\;2^{6}\,.

(C.22)

Thus for
0\leq i<j\leq K_{0}
, by (
C.13
)
we have

\max_{1\leq x<y\leq N}|u_{i,j}(x,y)|\;\leq\;2^{13}\,.

(C.23)

Now for all
i\in\llbracket 1,N\rrbracket
, using

1\;=\;\langle\widetilde{g}_{i},\widetilde{g}_{i}\rangle_{\mu_{N,1}}\;\geq\;\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)^{2}/N\,,

we obtain
\max_{1\leq x\leq N}|\widetilde{g}_{i}(x)|\leq\sqrt{N}
, and then by (
C.13
)

\max_{1\leq x<y\leq N}|u_{i,j}(x,y)|\;\leq\;2N\,.

(C.24)

Now we are ready to provide an upper bound on the r.h.s. of (
C.21
) by using (
C.23
) and (
C.24
):

\displaystyle\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq K_{0}}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}|u_{i,j}(x_{0},y_{0})|+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1,\,j>K_{0}}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}|u_{i,j}(x_{0},y_{0})|

(C.25)

\displaystyle\leq\;\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq K_{0}}2^{13}e^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}+\sum_{0\leq i<j\leq N-1,\,j>K_{0}}2Ne^{-(\lambda_{i}+\lambda_{j})t}

\displaystyle\leq\;2^{13}K_{0}^{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}+N^{3}e^{-\lambda_{K_{0}}t}

\displaystyle\leq\;2^{14}K_{0}^{2}e^{-\lambda_{1}t}

where we have (
2.8
) in the second last inequality, and have
used
(
2.11
) for the estimate on
\lambda_{K_{0}}

and
t\geq t_{\delta/2}
with

K_{0}\;\geq\;\left\lceil 2\sqrt{\frac{3}{\varrho}+1}\right\rceil\,.

∎

By Theorem

\lim_{N\to\infty}N^{2}\left|\lambda_{1}-\kappa_{1}\right|\;=\;0\,.

(C.26)

By Lemma
C.1
and (
C.26
), we have the condition in

: for all

\sum_{i=1}^{\bar{K}}{\mathbf{E}}\left[h_{t}^{\wedge}(x_{i})\right]\;\leq\;2^{6}\bar{\Upsilon}_{N}^{-1/2}\bar{K}(K_{0}+1)\cdot ke^{-\kappa_{1}t}\;\leq\;\bar{K}\sqrt{k}\delta\,,

(C.27)

where we have used the assumption on

in Assumption
2.4

) and

[

. Following the proof in

Torelli loci, product cycles, and the homomorphism conjecture for 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g}
Samir Canning, Lycka Drakengren, Jeremy Feusi, Daniel Holmes, Aitor Iribar López, Denis Nesterov, Dragos Oprea, Rahul Pandharipande, Johannes Schmitt, Zheming Sun

Abstract
. The tautological ℚ \mathbb{Q} -subalgebra 𝖱 ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 g ) ⊂ 𝖢𝖧 ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 g ) \mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\subset\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g}) of the Chow ring of the moduli space of principally polarized abelian varieties is generated by the Chern classes of the Hodge bundle. A canonical ℚ \mathbb{Q} -linear projection operator 𝗍𝖺𝗎𝗍 : 𝖢𝖧 ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 g ) → 𝖱 ∗ ​ ( 𝒜 g ) \mathsf{taut}:\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g})\rightarrow\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{A}_{g}) was constructed in [ 9 ] . We present here new calculations of intersection products of the Torelli locus in 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{g} with the product loci 𝒜 r × 𝒜 g − r → 𝒜 g \mathcal{A}_{r}\times\mathcal{A}_{g-r}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{g} for r ≤ 3 r\leq 3 . The results suggest that 𝗍𝖺𝗎𝗍 \mathsf{taut} is a ℚ \mathbb{Q} -algebra homomorphism (at least for special cycles). We discuss a conjectural framework for the homomorphism property of tautological projection. Our calculations follow two independent approaches. The first is a direct study of the excess intersection geometry of the fiber product of the Torelli and product morphisms. The second approach is by recasting the geometry in terms of families Gromov-Witten classes, which are computed by a wall-crossing formula related to unramified maps [ 49 , 51 ] . Both approaches are of interest beyond their use here. We define new tautological projections of cycles on the fiber products 𝒳 g s → 𝒜 g \mathcal{X}_{g}^{s}\to\mathcal{A}_{g} of the universal family. We compute these projections for a class of product cycles on 𝒳 g s \mathcal{X}_{g}^{s} in terms of a determinant involving the universal theta divisors and Poincaré classes. The Torelli pullback of the morphism 𝒜 1 × 𝒜 5 → 𝒜 6 \mathcal{A}_{1}\times\mathcal{A}_{5}\to\mathcal{A}_{6} was used in [ 10 ] to construct a nontrivial element of the Gorenstein kernel of 𝖱 5 ​ ( ℳ 6 ct ) {\mathsf{R}}^{5}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ct}}_{6}) . Using Abel-Jacobi pullbacks of product cycles on 𝒳 g s \mathcal{X}_{g}^{s} and their projections, we construct a new family of classes which we conjecture to lie in the Gorenstein kernels of the tautological rings 𝖱 ∗ ​ ( ℳ g , n ct ) \mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ct}}_{g,n}) . In particular, we construct a nontrivial element of the Gorenstein kernel of 𝖱 5 ​ ( ℳ 5 , 2 ct ) \mathsf{R}^{5}(\mathcal{M}_{5,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}) . Whether these classes generate the Gorenstein kernels of all 𝖱 ∗ ​ ( ℳ g , n ct ) \mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{M}_{g,n}^{\mathrm{ct}}) is an intriguing open question.

associated to a

-colored extremal tree

-colored nontrivial degenerations

Of course, the strict strata also cover

2.3.
Local equations

We now describe the stack structure on the fibered product
\mathcal{Z}
. The map

is not an embedding, but the differential is an injection on tangent spaces. Therefore, locally in the analytic topology,
\mathcal{Z}
is a closed substack of
\mathcal{M}_{g}^{\mathrm{ct}}
. Let

be a point in the strict stratum
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ct},\star}_{\mathsf{T}}
. We take a small open neighborhood

of

, which we may assume is the versal deformation space of

. We have a map

to the deformation space of the nodes of the underlying curve
C
. Here,
\mathbb{C}_{e}
is the one-dimensional versal deformation space of
C
corresponding to a node, and hence an edge
e
of
\mathsf{T}
.

Let
z_{e}
be the standard coordinate for each factor
\mathbb{C}_{e}
. We write local equations near
x
using these coordinates.

Definition 21
.

Let
v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathsf{V}(\mathsf{T})_{>0}
such that
\mathsf{c}(v_{1})\neq\mathsf{c}(v_{2})
. Let
\mathsf{P}
be the minimal path of edges connecting
v_{1}
to
v_{2}
. We say that
\mathsf{P}
is

if the only vertices of positive genus on the path are

and
v_{2}
. ∎

To each critical path

we associate the monomial

\mathsf{Mon}(\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{crit}})=\prod_{e}z_{e}\,,

where the product is taken over all edges appearing in
\mathsf{P}_{\textrm{crit}}.
We have the following generalization of
[
10
, Proposition 29]
.

Theorem 22

(Drakengren
[
18
]
)
.

The local equations for
\mathcal{Z}
near the point

in the strict stratum indexed by the
\underline{g}
-colored extremal tree

are given by pullback from
\prod_{e\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{T})}\mathbb{C}_{e}
of the monomial set

In particular,
\mathcal{Z}
is a reduced Deligne-Mumford stack.

In
[

, the local structure of

was determined in case

by interpreting

For the general case, an interpretation in terms of stable maps is not available.

directly studies

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
5

We will express the class (
21
)
as a
sum of contributions
\mathsf{Cont}_{\mathsf{T}}
supported on
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ct}}_{\mathsf{T}}
for each
\underline{g}
-colored extremal tree
\mathsf{T}
. Only trees
\mathsf{T}
with at most

Each contribution is expressed in terms of the Chern classes of the normal bundle of the product

and the Chern classes of the normal bundles of the strata of

indexed by the

-colored extremal trees. These contributions can be found using excess residual intersections as in

. The exact residual terms are

depending only on the normal bundle data, so we can compute them in a convenient local model. The local equations in Theorem
22
are used for the local model

Let
c
be the number of critical paths between all possible unordered pairs of positive genus vertices in
\mathsf{T}
that have distinct colors. A simple check shows that

.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be a rank

of the form

where the

are arbitrary torus equivariant line bundles on
\prod_{e\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{T})}\mathbb{C}_{e}
. Set

Let
\mathsf{Z}(s)\subset\prod_{e\in\mathsf{E}(\mathsf{T})}\mathbb{C}_{e}
be the zero locus of the section
s
. The zero locus
\mathsf{Z}(s)

has precisely the equations of our local model by Theorem

22
and
is independent of the ordering of the
monomials in

.
We may calculate the contribution of
\mathsf{T}
in the excess intersection problem for

determined by
\mathsf{Z}(s)
. We start with the equation

c_{d}(\mathcal{N})=(\ell_{1}\cdots\ell_{d-c})\prod_{\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{crit}}}(\sum_{e\in\mathsf{P}_{\mathrm{crit}}}z_{e})\,,

where the
\ell_{i}
are the equivariant first Chern classes of the line bundles

.

(i)

Consider first the case where the
\underline{g}
-colored extremal tree
\mathsf{I}
has no genus

vertices. Then,
\mathsf{I}
corresponds to an irreducible component of
\mathcal{Z}
, and every edge of
\mathsf{I}

(ii)

Next, let

\underline{g}
-colored extremal tree. By induction, we can assume we have computed

We set

The formula for

\bullet

we

by the

\bullet

by the Chern classes of the normal bundle of the

In the end,
\mathsf{Cont}_{\mathsf{T}}
is expressed in terms

4.2.
Fulton-MacPherson degenerations

Stable maps from curves to a target variety allow for nodal degenerations of the domain curves, but do not degenerate the target. Unramified maps
[
44
]
, however, permit the target to degenerate. Before defining unramified maps, we recall the definition of the degenerations of the targets which arise.

Let

4.7.
Geometric interpretation of the blowup

To explain how these blowups arise through the moduli problem of unramified maps, let us consider
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{ct},\mathrm{un}}_{2}(\pi_{2},(1))^{\circ}
. By associating

where the latter is the moduli space of unramified maps with marked points on the domain curve. If the domain

) was used in the last equality.
The class
\lambda_{g}\lambda_{g-1}
annihilates the boundary contributions
\delta_{h,\{p_{1}\}}
as can be seen from the Mumford relations
\lambda^{2}_{h}=0
and
\lambda_{g-h}^{2}=0
.

We therefore obtain

(67)

\lambda_{g}\cdot\overline{\Delta}_{g,1}=0\in\mathsf{R}^{2g}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})\,,

which immediately implies that the class
\Delta_{g,1}\in\mathsf{R}^{g}(\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}})
is in the kernel of the
\lambda_{g}
-pairing.
∎

Theorem
47
can be used to obtain cycles on the universal abelian variety that satisfy the homomorphism property (
51
). The Abel-Jacobi map

\operatorname{aj}:\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}\to\mathcal{X}_{g},\quad(C,p_{1},p_{2})\to(\textsf{Jac}(C),\mathcal{O}(p_{1}-p_{2}))

Abel-Jacobi cycle

[{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]=\operatorname{aj}_{*}([\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}])\in\mathsf{CH}^{\binom{g-1}{2}}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\,.

Corollary 50
.

The pair
([{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}],[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])
satisfies the homomorphism property on
\mathcal{X}_{g}
.

We expect this result to hold more generally for pairs of the form

([{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g,\mathsf{A}}],[\operatorname{PR}_{g,s}])

on
\mathcal{X}_{g}^{s}
, where
[{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g,\mathsf{A}}]\in\mathsf{CH}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{g}^{s})
denotes the Abel-Jacobi cycle for arbitrary
g
and
\mathsf{A}.

Proof.

We must verify the equality

\mathsf{taut}([{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]\cdot[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])=\mathsf{taut}([{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}])\cdot\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])\in\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\,.

For brevity, we omit here the superscripts, writing
\mathsf{taut}
for the tautological projection on the universal abelian variety
\mathcal{X}_{g}
. Equivalently, we will prove

\mathsf{taut}\left([{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]\cdot\big([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])\big)\right)=0\in\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{g})\,.

By definition of tautological projection, the above vanishing amounts to showing

(68)

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{g}}\overline{[{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]}\cdot\overline{[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])}\cdot\overline{\alpha}\cdot\lambda_{g}=0

for all tautological classes
\alpha\in\mathsf{R}^{*}(\mathcal{X}_{g})
. We fix
here any smooth compactification
\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{g}
of
\mathcal{X}_{g}
.

The crux of the argument is the vanishing

(69)

\overline{\operatorname{aj}^{*}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]))}\cdot\lambda_{g}=0\in\mathsf{R}^{2g}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2})

established in (
67
). A minor complication is that the map

\operatorname{aj}:\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}\to\mathcal{X}_{g}\hookrightarrow\overline{\mathcal{X}_{g}}

does not extend to
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}.
However, such an extension always exists after suitable blowups, yielding morphisms

\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,2}\,,\quad\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\to\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{g}\,.

We may assume
\pi
is an isomorphism over the compact type locus
\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}
, which we view as embedded in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. Furthermore, we may take
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
to be a nonsingular proper Deligne-Mumford stack.

The two cycles
\overline{[{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]}
and
\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}_{*}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}])
agree on
\mathcal{X}_{g}
, so

\overline{[{\textnormal{AJ}}_{g}]}=\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}_{*}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}])+\text{classes supported on }\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{g}\smallsetminus\mathcal{X}_{g}\,.

Since
\lambda_{g}
annihilates the boundary terms, we can rewrite the left hand side of (
68
) as

(70)

\int_{\overline{\mathcal{X}}_{g}}\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}_{*}([\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}])\cdot\overline{[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])}\cdot\overline{\alpha}\cdot\lambda_{g}=\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}^{*}\overline{[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])}\cdot\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}^{*}{\overline{\alpha}}\cdot\lambda_{g}\,.

The cycles

\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}^{*}\overline{[\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}])}\ \text{ and }\pi^{*}\overline{\operatorname{aj}^{*}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]))}

agree on
\mathcal{M}_{g,2}^{\mathrm{ct}}\hookrightarrow\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
. Using again that
\lambda_{g}=0
kills the boundary contributions, we rewrite (
70
) as

\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\pi^{*}\overline{\operatorname{aj}^{*}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]-\mathsf{taut}([\operatorname{PR}_{g,1}]))}\cdot\widetilde{\operatorname{aj}}^{*}{\overline{\alpha}}\cdot\lambda_{g}=0\,,

as needed. The last equality follows by pulling back (
69
) to
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
.
∎

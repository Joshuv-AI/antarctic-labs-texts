The complexity of separability for semilinear sets and Parikh automata
Elias Rojas Collins, Chris Köcher, Georg Zetzsche

Abstract
In a separability problem , we are given two sets K K and L L from a class 𝒞 \mathcal{C} , and we want to decide whether there exists a set S S from a class 𝒮 \mathcal{S} such that K ⊆ S K\subseteq S and S ∩ L = ∅ S\cap L=\emptyset . In this case, we speak of separability of sets in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} by sets in 𝒮 \mathcal{S} . We study two types of separability problems. First, we consider separability of semilinear sets (i.e. subsets of ℕ d \mathbb{N}^{d} for some d d ) by sets definable by quantifier-free monadic Presburger formulas (or equivalently, the recognizable subsets of ℕ d \mathbb{N}^{d} ). Here, a formula is monadic if each atom uses at most one variable. Second, we consider separability of languages of Parikh automata by regular languages. A Parikh automaton is a machine with access to counters that can only be incremented, and have to meet a semilinear constraint at the end of the run. Both of these separability problems are known to be decidable with elementary complexity. Our main results are that both problems are 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{coNP}} -complete. In the case of semilinear sets, 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{coNP}} -completeness holds regardless of whether the input sets are specified by existential Presburger formulas, quantifier-free formulas, or semilinear representations. Our results imply that recognizable separability of rational subsets of Σ ∗ × ℕ d \varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d} (shown decidable by Choffrut and Grigorieff) is 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{coNP}} -complete as well. Another application is that regularity of deterministic Parikh automata (where the target set is specified using a quantifier-free Presburger formula) is 𝖼𝗈𝖭𝖯 {\mathsf{coNP}} -complete as well.

Introduction

Separability

In a
separability problem
, we are given two sets
K
and
L
from a
class
\mathcal{C}
, and we want to decide whether there exists a set
S
from
a class
\mathcal{S}
such that
K\subseteq S
and
S\cap L=\emptyset
. Here,
the sets in
\mathcal{S}
are the admissible separators, and
S
is said to

separate
the sets
K
and
L
. In the case where
\mathcal{C}
is a class of non-regular languages and
\mathcal{S}
is the class of regular
languages, then the problem is called
regular separability (problem) for
\mathcal{C}
.
While the problem turned out to be undecidable for context-free languages in the 1970s
[
47
,
34
]
, the last decade saw a significant amount of attention on regular separability for subclasses (or variants) of
vector addition systems with states (VASS)
.
Regular separability was studied for coverability languages of VASS (and, more generally, well-structured transition systems)
[
18
,
38
,
41
]
, one-counter automata and one-dimensional VASS
[
17
]
, Parikh automata
[
15
]
, commutative VASS languages
[
16
]
, concerning its relationship with the intersection problem
[
48
]
, Büchi VASS
[
2
,
3
]
, and also for settings where one input language is an arbitrary VASS and the other is from some subclass
[
19
]
.
Recently, this line of work culminated in the breakthrough result that regular separability for general VASS languages is decidable and Ackermann-complete
[
39
]
.
However, for subclasses of VASS languages, the complexity landscape is far from understood.

Separating Parikh automata

An important example of such a subclass is the
class of languages accepted by

, which are
non-deterministic automata equipped with counters that can only be incremented.

[

.
This is because they are expressive enough to model non-trivial counting
behavior, but still enjoy low complexity for many algorithmic tasks (e.g. the
emptiness problem is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete). Example applications are monadic
second-order logic with cardinalities
[
40
]
(this

constraints
[

, and model-checking FIFO channel systems
[

]
. Moreover, these languages have

with

Decidability of regular separability was shown by Clemente, Czerwiński,

Separating semilinear sets: Monadic interpolants

One of the steps in the algorithm from
[
15
]
is to decide separability of sets defined in Presburger arithmetic, the first-order theory of
(\mathbb{N};+,\leq,0,1)
.
Separators of logically defined sets can also be viewed as
interpolants
. If
\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
and
\psi(\bm{y},\bm{z})
are (first-order or propositional) formulas such that
\forall\bm{x}\forall\bm{y}\forall\bm{z}\,(\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})\to\psi(\bm{y},\bm{z}))
holds, then a formula
\chi(\bm{y})
is a
Craig interpolant
if
\forall\bm{x}\forall\bm{y}\,(\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})\to\chi(\bm{y}))
and
\forall\bm{y}\forall\bm{z}\,(\chi(\bm{y})\to\psi(\bm{y},\bm{z}))
both hold. Here,
\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z}
are each a vector of variables, meaning
\chi
only mentions variables that
occur both in
\varphi
and
\psi
. Equivalently, the set defined by
\chi
is
a separator of the sets defined by the existential formulas
\exists\bm{x}\colon\varphi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
and

\exists\bm{z}\colon\neg\psi(\bm{y},\bm{z})
. In Interpolation-Based Model
Checking (ITP)
[
43
,
50
]
,
Craig interpolants are used to safely overapproximate sets of states: If

\varphi
describes reachable states and
\psi
describes the set of safe
states, then
\chi
overapproximates
\varphi
without adding unsafe states.
Note that in Presburger logic there are implications that do not have a Craig
interpolant (this is in contrast to propositional logic). So, before constructing
an interpolant, a first step of ITP is to decide whether there even exists
such an interpolant.

In the case of Presburger arithmetic, the definable sets are the

semilinear sets
. For many infinite-state systems, the step relation (or
even the reachability relation) is semilinear, and thus, separators can play
the role of Craig interpolants in infinite-state model checking. For the
separators, a natural choice is the class of
recognizable sets
, which
are those defined by
monadic
Presburger formulas, meaning each atom
refers to at most one variable. Monadic formulas have recently received
attention
[
49
,
4
,
32
,
31
]

because of their applications in query optimization in constraint
databases
[
28
,
42
]
and symbolic
automata
[
49
]
.
Thus, deciding recognizable separability of semilinear sets can be viewed as
synthesizing monadic Craig interpolants.

Recognizable separability was shown decidable by Choffrut and
Grigorieff
[
14
]
(see
[
16
]
for an extension beyond
semilinear sets). This was a key ingredient for separability of Parikh
automata in
[
15
]
. Choffrut and Grigorieff’s algorithm has
elementary complexity
[
15
, Section 7]
, but the exact complexity
of recognizable separability of semilinear sets remained unknown.

Contribution

Our
first main result
is that for given existential Presburger formulas,
recognizable separability (i.e. monadic separability) is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete. In
particular, recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete for given semilinear
representations. Moreover, our result implies that recognizable separability is

{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete for rational subsets of monoids
\varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
as
considered by Choffrut and Grigorieff
[
14
]
. Building on the methods
of the first result, our
second main result
is that regular separability
for Parikh automata is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

Application I: Monadic decomposability

Our first main result strengthens a
recent result on monadic decomposability. A formula in Presburger arithmetic is

monadically decomposable
if it has a monadic equivalent. It was shown
recently that (i) deciding whether a given quantifier-free formula is

monadically decomposable
(i.e. whether it has a monadic equivalent) is

{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete
[
32
, Theorem 1]
(see

[
4
, Corollary 8.1]
for an alternative proof; and see
[
13
, Proposition 3]
for improved bounds for the approach in
[
32
]
), whereas (ii) for
existential formulas, the problem is
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-complete
[
31
, Corollary 3.6]
. Our first main
result strengthens (i): If
\varphi(\bm{x})
is a quantifier-free formula, then
the sets defined by
\varphi(\bm{x})
and
\neg\varphi(\bm{x})
are separable by a
monadic formula if and only if
\varphi(\bm{x})
is monadically decomposable.
Perhaps surprisingly, our
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound still holds for existential
Presburger formulas, for which monadic decomposability is known to be

{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-complete
2
2

2

This is not a contradiction to the above reduction
from monadic decomposability to recognizable separation, since this reduction
would require complementing an existential formula.
.

Application II: Regularity of Parikh automata

Another
consequence of our results is that regularity of deterministic Parikh automata,
i.e. deciding whether a given deterministic Parikh automaton accepts a regular
language, is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete: Given a deterministic Parikh
automaton for a language
L\subseteq\varSigma^{*}
, one can construct in polynomial
time a Parikh automaton for
K=\varSigma^{*}\setminus L
. Then,
L
is regular if
and only if
L
and
K
are regularly separable.
Here, we assume that the semilinear target set
is given as a quantifier-free Presburger formula. Decidability of this problem
has been shown by Cadilhac, Finkel, and McKenzie
[
10
, Theorem
25]
(even in the more general case of
unambiguous constrained automata).

Key ingredients

The existing elementary-complexity algorithm for recognizable separability of
semilinear sets works with semilinear representations and distinguishes two
cases: If in one component
j
, one of the input sets
S_{1},S_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
is
bounded by some
b\geq 0
, then it considers each
x\in[0,b]
and recursively
decides separability of
S_{1}[j\mapsto x]
and
S_{2}[j\mapsto x]
, where
S_{i}[j\mapsto x]
is just
S_{i}

restricted to having
x
in this bounded component. If, however, all components in both
sets are unbounded, then it checks feasibility of a system of linear
Diophantine equations. This approach leads to repeated intersection of
semilinear sets, and thus exponential time. We provide a characterization
(
Proposition

13
) that describes
inseparability directly as the non-empty intersection of two semilinear sets

\hat{S}_{1},\hat{S}_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
associated with
S_{1},S_{2}
. This easily
yields an
{\mathsf{NP}}
procedure for inseparability, even if the input sets are given
as existential Presburger formulas.

This characterization is then the first key ingredient for deciding regular
separability of Parikh automata in
{\mathsf{coNP}}
. This is because in

[
15
]
, it is shown that, after some preprocessing, the languages
of Parikh automata
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are separable if and only if two
semilinear sets
C_{1},C_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
associated with
\mathcal{A}_{1}
and
\mathcal{A}_{2}
are
separable by a recognizable set. These semilinear sets consist of vectors, each
of which counts for some run of
\mathcal{A}_{i}
, how many times each simple cycles
occurs in this run. Thus, our first result tells us that it suffices to decide whether
\hat{C}_{1}
and
\hat{C}_{2}
are disjoint. Unfortunately, the vectors of
C_{1},C_{2}
have exponential dimension

d
, since there are exponentially many simple cycles in each
\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Thus,
applying our first result directly using existential Presburger arithmetic would only yield a
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
upper bound.

To avoid this blowup, the second key idea is to
encode the vectors in
\hat{C}_{1}
and
\hat{C}_{2}
as words
, where the cycle occurrences appear as a concatenation in some order. By constructing
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS
\mathcal{W}_{1},\mathcal{W}_{2}
for the encodings of the vectors in
\hat{C}_{1},\hat{C}_{2}
, we reduce separability to intersection emptiness of
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}
. The latter, in turn, easily reduces to non-reachability in a product
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS, which is in
{\mathsf{coNP}}
.

Preliminaries

By
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
we denote the set of all non-negative integers.
Let
d\in\mathbb{N}
be a number and
I\subseteq[1,d]
be a set of indices. By
\pi_{I}\colon\mathbb{N}^{d}\to\mathbb{N}^{I}
we denote the
projection
of vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
to vectors in
\mathbb{N}^{I}
, i.e.,
\pi_{I}(\bm{v})[i]=\bm{v}[i]
for each
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
i\in I
. The
support
of a vector
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
is the set of all coordinates in
\bm{v}
with non-zero value, i.e.
\supp(\bm{v})=\{i\in[1,d]\mid\bm{v}[i]\neq 0\}
.

Semilinear sets

A set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
is
linear
if there is a vector

\bm{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and a finite set
P\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
of so-called
periods
such
that
S=\bm{u}+P^{*}
holds. Here, for
P=\{\bm{u}_{1},\ldots,\bm{u}_{n}\}
, the set
P^{*}
is defined as

P^{*}=\{\lambda_{1}\bm{u}_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{n}\bm{u}_{n}\mid\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{n}\in\mathbb{N}\}.

A subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
is called
semilinear
if it is a finite union
of linear sets. In case we specify
S
by way of a finite union of linear sets, then we call this description a
semilinear representation
. The set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
is called
hyperlinear
if there are finite sets

B,P\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
such that
S=B+P^{*}
holds. It is well known that the
semilinear sets are precisely those definable in
Presburger
arithmetic

[
25
]
, the first-order theory of the
structure
(\mathbb{N};+,\leq,0,1,(\equiv_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}})
. Here
\equiv_{m}
is the
predicate where
x\equiv_{m}y
if and only if
x-y
is divisible by
m
. By
quantifier elimination, every formula in Presburger
arithmetic has a quantifier-free equivalent.

Parikh automata

Intuitively, a Parikh automaton has finitely
many control states and access to
d\geq 0
counters. Upon reading a letter (or
the empty word), it can add a vector
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
to its counters. Moreover,
for each state
q\in Q
, it specifies a target set
C_{q}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
. An input
word is accepted if at the end of the run, the accumulated counter values
belong to
C_{q}
, where
q
is the state at the end of the run. Formally, a

Parikh automaton
is a tuple
\mathcal{A}=(Q,\varSigma,T,q_{0},(C_{q})_{q\in Q})
, where

Q
is a finite set of states,
T\subseteq Q\times(\varSigma\cup\{\varepsilon\})\times\mathbb{N}^{d}\times Q
is its finite set of

transitions
,
q_{0}\in Q
is the
initial
state, and
C_{q}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}

is the
target set
in state

q
, for each
q\in Q
. For an input word
w\in\varSigma^{*}
, a
run on
w
is
a sequence
(q_{0},w_{1},\bm{u}_{1},q_{1})\cdots(q_{n-1},w_{n},\bm{u}_{n},q_{n})
of transitions
in
T
with
w=w_{1}\cdots w_{n}
. The run is
accepting
if
\bm{u}_{1}+\cdots+\bm{u}_{n}\in C_{q_{n}}
. The

language
of
\mathcal{A}
is then the set of all words
w\in\varSigma^{*}
such that

\mathcal{A}
has an accepting run on
w
.

Remark 1
.

For our results on general Parikh automata, we assume that the target sets are
specified using existential Presburger formulas. However, this is not
an important aspect: Given a Parikh automaton, one can in polynomial
time modify the automaton (and the target set) so that the target set
is given, e.g. by a semilinear representation, or a quantifier-free
Presburger formula. This is a simple consequence of the fact that one
can translate Parikh automata into integer VASS in logarithmic
space
[
1
, Corollary 1]
.
However, this conversion does not preserve determinism, and for
deterministic Parikh automata, it can be important how target sets are
given (see
Corollary

8
and the discussion after it).
Therefore, for deterministic Parikh automata, we always specify
how the targets sets are given.

Separability

A subset
L\subseteq M
of a monoid
M
is
recognizable
if there is a
morphism
\varphi\colon M\to F
into some finite monoid
F
such that

\varphi^{-1}(\varphi(L))=L
. The recognizable subsets of
M
form a Boolean
algebra
[
6
, Chapter III, Prop. 1.1]
. We say that sets
K,L\subseteq M
are
(recognizably) separable
, denoted
K\mathbin{|}L
, if there is a
morphism
\varphi\colon M\to F
into some finite monoid
F
such that

\varphi(K)\cap\varphi(L)=\emptyset
. Equivalently, we have
K\mathbin{|}L
if and
only if there is a recognizable
S\subseteq M
with
K\subseteq S
and

S\cap L=\emptyset
. Here,
S
is called a
separator
of
K
and
L
. Clearly, we have
K\mathbin{|}L
if and only if
L\mathbin{|}K
: if
S
is a separator of
K
and
L
then
M\setminus S
separates
L
and
K
.

In the case
M=\varSigma^{*}
for some alphabet
\varSigma
, the recognizable sets
in
\varSigma^{*}
are exactly the regular languages (cf.
[
44
, Theorem II.2.1]
), and thus we speak of
regular
separability
. In the case
M=\mathbb{N}^{d}
for some
d\geq 0
, then the recognizable
subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
are precisely the finite unions of cartesian products

U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{d}
, where each
U_{i}\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is ultimately
periodic
[
6
, Theorem 5.1]
. Here, a set
U\subseteq\mathbb{N}
is

ultimately periodic
if there are
n_{0},p\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
, we have
n\in U
if and only if
n+p\in U
. This implies that the
recognizable subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
are precisely those definable by a
monadic
Presburger formula
, i.e. one where every atom only refers to one
variable
[
49
]
. For these reasons, in the case
of
M=\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we also sometimes speak of
monadic separability
.

In a
recognizable separability problem
, we are given two subsets
K
and
L
from a monoid
M
as input, and we want to decide whether
K
and
L
are recognizably separable. Again, in the case of
M=\varSigma^{*}
, we also call this the
regular separability problem
.

3
Main results

Recognizable separability of semilinear sets

Our first main result is the following.

Theorem 2
.

Given two semilinear sets defined by existential Presburger formulas, recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

The lower bound follows with a simple reduction from the emptiness problem for sets defined by existential Presburger formulas: If
\varphi
defines a subset
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
, then
K\mathbin{|}\mathbb{N}^{d}
if and only if
K
is empty. We prove the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound in
Section

5
. By the same argument, recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-hard for input sets given by quantifier-free formulas. Thus:

Corollary 3
.

Given two semilinear sets defined by quantifier-free Presburger formulas, recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

In particular, this re-proves the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound for monadic decomposability of quantifier-free formulas, as originally shown by Hague, Lin, Rümmer, and Wu
[
32
, Theorem 1]
.

Remark 4
.

Our result also implies that for existential Presburger formulas over

(\mathbb{Z};+,\leq,0,1,(\equiv_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}})
defining
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, it is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete to
decide whether they are separable by a monadically defined subset of

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Indeed, consider the injective map
\nu\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, where
\nu(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})=(\sigma(x_{1}),|x_{1}|,\ldots,\sigma(x_{d}),|x_{d}|)
with
\sigma(x)=0
for
x\geq 0
and
\sigma(x)=1
for
x<0
. Then
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is monadically definable if and only if
\nu(S)
is monadically definable
3
3

3

This is easily shown by translating each atomic formula (over a single variable) into a monadic formula in each direction. However, note that within
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, monadic definability is not the same as recognizability. For example, the sets
\{0\}
and
\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}
are monadically separable, but not separable by a recognizable subset of
\mathbb{Z}
, since every non-empty recognizable subset of
\mathbb{Z}
is infinite
[
6
, Chapter III, Example 1.4]
.
. Thus,
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
are monadically separable if and only if
\nu(K),\nu(L)\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{2d}
are monadically separable. Finally, one easily constructs existential formulas for
\nu(K),\nu(L)
.

Since for a given semilinear representation of a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
, it is
easy to construct an existential Presburger formula defining
S
,

Theorem

2
also implies the following.

Corollary 5
.

Given two semilinear representations, recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

In this case, the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
lower bound comes from the
{\mathsf{NP}}
-hard membership
problem for semilinear sets (even if all numbers are written in unary)
[
36
, Lemma 10]
: For a
semilinear subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and a vector
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, we have

\bm{u}\notin S
if and only if
S\mathbin{|}\{\bm{u}\}
.
Finally,
Theorem

2
allows us to settle the complexity of
recognizable separability of rational subsets of
\varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
.

Corollary 6
.

Given
d\in\mathbb{N}
and two rational subsets of
\varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
, deciding recognizable separability is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

Decidability was first shown by Choffrut and Grigorieff
[
14
, Theorem
1]
. The
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound follows because Choffrut and
Grigorieff
[
14
, Theorem 10]
reduce recognizable separability of
subsets of
\varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
to recognizable separability of rational subsets of

\mathbb{N}^{2d}
(and their reduction is clearly in polynomial time). Moreover, for a
given rational subset of
\mathbb{N}^{2d}
, one can construct in polynomial time an
equivalent existential Presburger formula
[
45
, Theorem
1]
. Thus, the upper bound follows from

Theorem

2
. Since semilinear sets in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
(given by a semilinear representation) can be viewed as rational subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
(and hence of
\varSigma^{*}\times\mathbb{N}^{d}
), the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
lower bound is inherited from
Corollary

5
.

Regular separability of Parikh automata

Our second main result is the following:

Theorem 7
.

Regular separability for Parikh automata is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

The
{\mathsf{coNP}}
lower bound comes via the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete emptiness problem:
For a given Parikh automaton accepting a language
K\subseteq\varSigma^{*}
, we have

K\mathbin{|}\varSigma^{*}
if and only if
K=\emptyset
. Thus, the interesting part is
the upper bound, which we prove in
Section

6
. This is a significant
improvement to the previously known elementary (or finitely iterated exponential time)
complexity upper bound by Clemente, Czerwiński, Lasota, and Paperman
[
15
]
.

Theorem

7
can also be applied to deciding regularity of deterministic Parikh automata.

Corollary 8
.

For deterministic Parikh automata with target sets given as quantifier-free Presburger formulas, deciding regularity is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete.

Decidability of regularity was shown by Cadilhac, Finkel, and McKenzie
[
10
, Theorem
25]
(in the slightly more general setting of unambiguous constrained automata).
For the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound, note that for a language
L\subseteq\varSigma^{*}
given by a deterministic Parikh automaton (with quantifier-free formulas for the target sets), one can in polynomial time construct the same type of automaton for the complement
\varSigma^{*}\setminus L
. Since
L
is regular if and only if
L
and
\varSigma^{*}\setminus L
are separable by a regular language, we can invoke
Theorem

7
.
The
{\mathsf{coNP}}
lower bound is inherited from monadic decomposability of quantifier-free formulas.
Indeed, given a quantifier-free Presburger formula
\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
with free variables
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, one easily constructs a deterministic Parikh automaton (with quantifier-free target sets) for the language
L_{\varphi}=\{a_{1}^{x_{1}}\cdots a_{n}^{x_{n}}\mid\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\}
. As shown by Ginsburg and Spanier
[
24
, Theorem 1.2]
,
L_{\varphi}
is regular if and only if
\varphi
is monadically decomposable. However, monadic decomposability for quantifier-free formulas is
{\mathsf{coNP}}
-complete
[
32
, Theorem 1]
.

For the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound in
Corollary

8
, we cannot drop the
assumption that the formula be quantifier-free. This is because if the target
sets can be existential Presburger formulas, then the regularity problem is

{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-hard. This follows by the same reduction from monadic
decomposability: If we construct
L_{\varphi}
as above using an existential
formula
\varphi
, then again,
L_{\varphi}
is regular if and only if
\varphi

is monadically decomposable. Moreover, monadic decomposability for existential
formulas is
{\mathsf{coNEXP}}
-complete
[
31
, Corollary
3.6]
.

4
A characterization of separability in hyperlinear sets

Before we prove our two main results,
Theorems

2
and
7
, we should
recall the ideas of the existing algorithms
[
14
,
16
]
for
recognizable separability of linear sets. We will use these ideas to
obtain a new characterization of separability in hyperlinear sets.

Let
L_{1},L_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be two linear sets. The
algorithms
[
14
,
16
]
rely on a procedure that successively
eliminates “bounded components”: If, say,
L_{1}
is bounded in component
j

by some
b\in\mathbb{N}
, then one can observe that
L_{1}\mathbin{|}L_{2}
if, and only if,

L_{1}[j\mapsto x]\mathbin{|}L_{2}[j\mapsto x]
for every
x\in[0,b]
. Here,

L_{i}[j\mapsto x]
is
L_{i}
restricted to those vectors that have
x
in the

j
-th component, and then projected to all components
\neq j
. Therefore,
the algorithms of
[
14
,
16
]
recursively check separability of

L_{1}[j\mapsto x]
and
L_{2}[j\mapsto x]
for each
x\in[0,b]
.
This process invokes several expensive intersection operations on semilinear
sets and thus has high complexity. Instead, our approach immediately guesses
and verifies the set of components that remain after the elimination process.
The corresponding checks involve the notion of twin-unboundedness.

Twin-unbounded components

Our notion applies, slightly more generally, to hyperlinear sets. Hence, let

R=A+U^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
S=B+V^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be two hyperlinear sets where

A,B,U,V\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
are finite sets.

Definition 9
.

A coordinate
j\in[1,d]
is
twin-unbounded for
R
and
S
if there
exist
\bm{p}\in U^{*}
and
\bm{q}\in V^{*}
such that
j\in\supp(\bm{p})=\supp(\bm{q})
.

Hence, intuitively, twin-unbounded coordinates are those that can be made large/driven up in
R
in the same way as in
S
.
There is yet another characterization of twin-unbounded coordinates. Let
j\in[1,d]
. We say the
j
-th coordinate of the hyperlinear set
S=B+V^{*}
is
bounded
if there is no period vector in
V
with support on
j
, i.e.,
j\notin\supp(\bm{p})
for all
\bm{p}\in V
. We say that a subset
J\subseteq[1,d]
of coordinates is bounded in
S
if each
j\in J
is bounded in
S
.
Consider the following process: Given two hyperlinear sets
R
and
S
. We modify
R
and
S
by performing each of the following three steps for each coordinate
j\in[1,d]
until the sets of remaining period vectors in
R
and
S
stabilize:

•

If neither
R
nor
S
is bounded at
j
, we leave
S
and
R
untouched.

•

If only
R
is bounded at
j
, we remove all period vectors from
S
which have support on
j
.

•

If only
S
is bounded at
j
, we remove all period vectors from
R
which have support on
j
.

Then, the coordinates that remain unbounded are precisely the twin-unbounded ones.

Example 10
.

Consider
R=\{(1,0,1)\}^{*}
and
S=\{(1,1,0),(0,0,1)\}^{*}
. Then
R
is bounded by the value
0
at coordinate
2
. So
R
and
S
are separable if and only if
R
and
S
restricted to the vectors having the value
0
in the second coordinate. So, we only consider this restriction of
S
—in our algorithm this is reflected by the deletion of the period vector
(1,1,0)
of
S
. After deletion of the period vector
(1,1,0)
,
S
is bounded at coordinate
1
by the value
0
. So, we remove the period vector
(1,0,1)
from
R
. Finally, the period vector
(0,0,1)
of
S
gets removed since
R
is now bounded at coordinate
3
. Hence, our algorithm terminates in this case with no twin-unbounded coordinates. This example shows that even if
R
and
S
both are unbounded in coordinates
1
and
3
, none of these coordinates is twin-unbounded.

If
R=\{(1,0,1),(0,1,0)\}^{*}
and
S=\{(1,1,0),(0,0,1)\}^{*}
, then no coordinate is bounded in
R
and
S
. Hence, all coordinates are twin-unbounded and no period vector gets removed.

For
J\subseteq[1,d]
, we write
U_{J}=\{\bm{p}\in U\mid\supp(\bm{p})\subseteq J\}
and
V_{J}=\{\bm{q}\in V\mid\supp(\bm{q})\subseteq J\}
.

Separating by modular constraints

As observed in
[
14
,
16
]
, if all coordinates of two linear sets
L_{1},L_{2}
are unbounded, then separability holds if and only if the two sets can be separated by modulo constraints. This relies on the well known fact that finitely generated abelian groups are
subgroup separable
, i.e. that for every element
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
that does not belong to a subgroup
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, there exists a homomorphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{F}
into a finite group
\mathbb{F}
such that (i)
A
is included in the kernel of
\varphi
and (ii)
\varphi(\bm{u})\neq 0
. In our characterization (
Proposition

13
) we will use similar arguments and therefore we will recall subgroup separability here. We include a short proof in
Appendix

A
.

Lemma 11

(Subgroup separability)
.

If
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a subgroup and
\bm{u}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus A
, then there
is an
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>0
, and a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}/s\mathbb{Z}
with (i)
\varphi(A)=0
and (ii)
\varphi(\bm{u})\neq 0
.

Separability vs. intersection emptiness

We will now characterize inseparability of hyperlinear sets
R,S
via the intersection of two hyperlinear sets
\hat{R}
and
\hat{S}
associated with
R,S
. The proof will rely on an equivalence relation of vectors.
For vectors
\bm{u},\bm{v}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, we write
\bm{u}\sim_{k}\bm{v}
if for every
i\in[1,d]
, we have

[(1)]

1.

\bm{u}[i]=\bm{v}[i]\leq k
or

2.

\bm{u}[i],\bm{v}[i]>k
and
\bm{u}[i]\equiv\bm{v}[i]\bmod{k}
.

The following was shown in
[
16
, Prop. 18]
.

Lemma 12
.

For any sets
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
, the following are equivalent:

[(1)]

1.

X
and
Y
are not separable by a recognizable set.

2.

for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
there are
\bm{x}_{k}\in X
and
\bm{y}_{k}\in Y
with
\bm{x}_{k}\sim_{k}\bm{y}_{k}
.

Let
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
be such that
k
divides
\ell
. We can observe that
\bm{u}\sim_{\ell}\bm{v}
implies
\bm{u}\sim_{k}\bm{v}
in this case. Thus, to show recognizable inseparability of two sets
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
, it suffices to find
\bm{x}_{k}\in X
and
\bm{y}_{k}\in Y
for almost all numbers
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. We will use this fact in the proof of the following characterization of inseparability.

Proposition 13
.

Let
R=A+U^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
S=B+V^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be hyperlinear sets. Then

R
and
S
are
not
separable by a recognizable set if and only if the intersection

(A+U^{*}-U_{J}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}-V_{J}^{*})

(1)

is non-empty, where
J\subseteq[1,d]
is the set of coordinates that are twin-unbounded for
R,S
.

Proof.

Suppose there is a vector
\bm{x}
in the intersection (
1
). Then we can write
\bm{x}=\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}
and
\bm{x}=\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}
with
\bm{u}\in A+U^{*}
,
\bm{v}\in B+V^{*}
,
\bar{\bm{u}}\in U_{J}^{*}
, and
\bar{\bm{v}}\in V_{J}^{*}
.
Since
J
is twin-unbounded for
R
and
S
, there are—by definition—
\bm{p}_{j}\in U^{*}
and
\bm{q}_{j}\in V^{*}
with
j\in\supp(\bm{p}_{j})=\supp(\bm{q}_{j})
for each
j\in J
. Then for
\bm{p}:=\sum_{j\in J}\bm{p}_{j}
and
\bm{q}:=\sum_{j\in J}\bm{q}_{j}
we infer
J\subseteq\supp(\bm{p})=\supp(\bm{q})
. Now for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
, consider the vectors

\bm{u}_{k}=\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}+2k\cdot\bm{p}+k\cdot\bar{\bm{u}}\quad\text{and}\quad\bm{v}_{k}=\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}+2k\cdot\bm{q}+k\cdot\bar{\bm{v}}\,.

Then we have
\bm{u}_{k},\bm{v}_{k}\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
for each
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
. We claim that
\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k}\bm{v}_{k}
for all
k
. Indeed, on coordinates
j\in[1,d]\setminus\supp(\bm{p})
, the vectors
\bm{u}_{k}
and
\bm{v}_{k}
coincide with
\bm{x}
. Moreover, on coordinates
j\in\supp(\bm{p})
, both vectors
\bm{u}_{k}
and
\bm{v}_{k}
are larger than
k
and also congruent to
\bm{x}[j]\bmod{k}
. Hence,
\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k}\bm{v}_{k}
. Since clearly
\bm{u}_{k}=\bm{u}+2k\cdot\bm{p}+(k-1)\cdot\bar{\bm{u}}\in R
and
\bm{v}_{k}=\bm{v}+2k\cdot\bm{q}+(k-1)\cdot\bar{\bm{v}}\in S
,
Lemma

12
implies that
R
and
S
are not separable.

Conversely, suppose that
R
and
S
are not separable. Then by
Lemma

12
there are
\bm{u}_{k}\in R
and
\bm{v}_{k}\in S
with

\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k}\bm{v}_{k}
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}\setminus\{0\}
.
We claim that the sequences
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\ldots
and
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2},\ldots
have subsequences
\bm{u}^{\prime}_{1},\bm{u}^{\prime}_{2},\ldots
and
\bm{v}^{\prime}_{1},\bm{v}^{\prime}_{2},\ldots
such that for every
k\geq 1
, we have (i)
\bm{u}^{\prime}_{k+1}\in\bm{u}^{\prime}_{k}+U_{J}^{*}
, (ii)
\bm{v}^{\prime}_{k+1}\in\bm{v}^{\prime}_{k}+V_{J}^{*}
and (iii)
\bm{u}^{\prime}_{k}\sim_{k}\bm{v}^{\prime}_{k}
.

The claim is easy to observe: Note that by picking subsequences, we may assume that even
\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k!}\bm{v}_{k}
for every
k\geq 1
. Moreover, the latter property is preserved by taking subsequences. Thus, since
A,B
are finite, by picking subsequences again, we may assume that there are
\bm{r}\in A
and
\bm{s}\in B
such that
\bm{u}_{k}\in\bm{r}+U^{*}
and
\bm{u}_{k}\in\bm{s}+V^{*}
and
\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k!}\bm{v}_{k}
for
k\geq 1
. Then, by Dickson’s lemma, we may assume that in addition
\bm{u}_{k+1}\in\bm{u}_{k}+U^{*}
and
\bm{v}_{k+1}\in\bm{v}_{k}+V^{*}
for every
k\geq 1
(here, we apply Dickson’s lemma to the
|U|
-dimensional vectors of coefficients at period vectors in
U
and similarly for
V
).
Now since
\bm{u}_{k}\sim_{k!}\bm{v}_{k}
for every
k
, it follows that the
sequences
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\ldots
and
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2},\ldots
are unbounded on the
same set
J\subseteq[1,d]
of coordinates. Then clearly,
J
is
twin-unbounded for
R
and
S
. This means, for all but finitely many
k
, we have

\bm{u}_{k+1}\in\bm{u}_{k}+U_{J}^{*}
and
\bm{v}_{k+1}\in\bm{v}_{k}+V_{J}^{*}
. Hence, by picking another subsequence, we may assume that the latter holds for every
k\geq 1
. Then,
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2},\ldots
and
\bm{v}_{1},\bm{v}_{2},\ldots
satisfy the properties (i–iii) above, establishing our claim.

We now claim that
\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1}
belongs to the group
\langle U_{J}\cup V_{J}\rangle
generated by
U_{J}\cup V_{J}
.
Towards a contradiction, suppose
\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1}
does not belong to
\langle U_{J}\cup V_{J}\rangle
. By
Lemma

11
, there must be an
s\in\mathbb{N}
,
s>0
, and a morphism
\varphi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{d}\to\mathbb{Z}/s\mathbb{Z}
such that
\varphi(\langle U_{J}\cup V_{J}\rangle)=0
and
\varphi(\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1})\neq 0
. However, the vector

(\bm{u}_{s}-\bm{v}_{s})-(\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1})=\underbrace{(\bm{u}_{s}-\bm{u}_{1})}_{\in\langle U_{J}\rangle}-\underbrace{(\bm{v}_{s}-\bm{v}_{1})}_{\in\langle V_{J}\rangle}

belongs to
\langle U_{J}\cup V_{J}\rangle
, but also agrees with
\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1}
under
\varphi
(since all components of
\bm{u}_{s}-\bm{v}_{s}
are divisible by
s
), contradicting
Lemma

11
. Hence
\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1}\in\langle U_{J}\cup V_{J}\rangle
.

This means, we can write
\bm{u}_{1}-\bm{v}_{1}=\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}-(\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}})
with
\bm{u},\bar{\bm{u}}\in U_{J}^{*}
and
\bm{v},\bar{\bm{v}}\in V_{J}^{*}
. But then the vector

\bm{u}_{1}+\bm{u}-\bar{\bm{u}}=\bm{v}_{1}+\bm{v}-\bar{\bm{v}}

belongs to the intersection (
1
).
∎

With
Proposition

13
, we have now
characterized inseparability of subsets of
\mathbb{N}^{d}
via a particular intersection
of two sets in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. It will later be more convenient to work with
intersections of sets in
\mathbb{N}^{d}
, which motivates the following
reformulation of
Proposition

13
.

Theorem 14
.

Let
R=A+U^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
and
S=B+V^{*}\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
be hyperlinear sets. Then

R
and
S
are
not
separable by a recognizable set if and only if the intersection

(A+U^{*}+V_{J}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}+U_{J}^{*})

(2)

is non-empty, where
J\subseteq[1,d]
is the set of coordinates that are twin-unbounded for
R,S
.

Proof.

Direct consequence of
Proposition

13
, since clearly
A+U^{*}-U_{J}^{*}
intersects
B+V^{*}-V_{J}^{*}
if and only if
A+U^{*}+V_{J}^{*}
intersects
B+V^{*}+U_{J}^{*}
.
∎

5
Separability of semilinear sets is in
{\mathsf{coNP}}

Using the characterization
Theorem

14
,
we can now explain our algorithm for the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound in
Theorem

2
.
We describe an
{\mathsf{NP}}
algorithm that establishes
inseparability
.

Algorithm Step I: Solution sets to linear Diophantine equations

Let us first see that we can reduce the problem to the case where both input
sets are given as projections of solution sets of linear Diophantine
equations. We may assume that the input formulas are of the form

\exists\bm{x}\colon\kappa(\bm{x},\bm{y})
, where
\kappa
is
a formula consisting of conjunction and disjunction (i.e. no negation) of
atoms of the form
t\geq a
, where
t
is a linear combination of variables

\bm{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),\bm{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
and integer coefficients, and
a
is a constant.

Let
\varphi
be a formula as described above. It is a well known fact that

\varphi
can be transformed into disjunctive normal form. This means,

\varphi
is equivalent to a formula
\varphi_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\varphi_{k}
,
where each
\varphi_{i}
(a so-called
clause
) has the form

\exists\bm{x}\colon\xi(\bm{x},\bm{y})
such that
\xi
is a conjunction of atoms
appearing in
\varphi
. In general, the number of clauses of
\varphi

is exponential.

Now, let
\varphi
and
\psi
be the input formulas of the algorithm and
let
\varphi_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\varphi_{k}
and
\psi_{1}\vee\cdots\vee\psi_{\ell}

be their equivalent formulas in disjunctive normal form. Since the number
of clauses is exponential, we cannot compute all clauses for
\varphi

and
\psi
.
However, the solution sets of
\varphi
and
\psi
are recognizably inseparable
if, and only if, for some pair
i,j
, the solution sets of the formulas

\varphi_{i}
and
\psi_{j}
are recognizably inseparable. This is
due to the following fact, which follows standard ideas (see
Appendix

B
for a proof in this particular setting).

Lemma 15
.

Let
K,K_{1},\ldots,K_{n},L\subseteq M
be sets from a monoid
M
such
that
K=K_{1}\cup\cdots\cup K_{n}
. Then
K\mathbin{|}L
if, and only if,

K_{i}\mathbin{|}L
for all
1\leq i\leq n
.

Thus, for deciding the inseparability of the solution sets of
\varphi
and
\psi

in
{\mathsf{NP}}
it is sufficient to guess (in polynomial time) clauses
\varphi_{i}

and
\psi_{j}
and show that inseparability of the solution sets of these
two formulas is decidable in
{\mathsf{NP}}
. Therefore, from now on we can assume
that the input formulas are (existentially quantified) conjunctions of
atoms of the form
t\geq a
.

In particular, each of the two input sets is a projection of the solution set of a system of linear Diophantine inequalities. By introducing slack variables (which will also be projected away), we can turn
inequalities
into
equations
. Thus, we have as input sets
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{d}
with

K=\pi(\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\mid A\bm{x}=\bm{b}\})\quad\text{and}\quad L=\pi(\{\bm{x}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}\mid C\bm{x}=\bm{d}\})\,,

(3)

where
\pi\colon\mathbb{Z}^{r}\to\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is the projection to the first
d
components, and

A,C\in\mathbb{Z}^{s\times r}
are integer matrices, and
\bm{b},\bm{d}\in\mathbb{Z}^{s}
are integer
vectors. Note that here, assuming that the numbers
r
of columns and the
number
s
of rows is the same for
K
and
L
means no loss of generality.

Algorithm Step II: Recognizable inseparability as satisfiability

In the second step, we will reduce recognizable inseparability of
K
and
L
to
satisfiability of an existential Presburger formula. To this end, we use the
fact that the solution sets to
A\bm{x}\geq\bm{b}
(resp.
C\bm{x}\geq\bm{d}
) are
hyperlinear sets, which allows us to apply

Theorem

14
.

Proposition 16
.

K
and
L
are recognizably inseparable if, and only if, there are
vectors
\bm{p},\bm{q},\bm{u},\bm{v},\bm{x},\bm{y}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
with

[(1)]

1.

A\bm{p}=\bm{0}
,
C\bm{q}=\bm{0}
,
\supp(\pi(\bm{p}))=\supp(\pi(\bm{q}))
,

2.

\supp(\pi(\bm{u})),\supp(\pi(\bm{v}))\subseteq\supp(\pi(\bm{p}))
,

A\bm{u}=\bm{0}
, and
C\bm{v}=\bm{0}
,

3.

A\bm{x}=\bm{b}
,

Regular separability of Parikh automata

We now prove our second main result: the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper
bound of regular separability of Parikh automata (

7
).
For this, it will be technically simpler to work with
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS, which are equivalent to Parikh automata. In
[

, it was shown that the two automata models can be converted (while preserving the language) into each other in logarithmic space. Therefore, showing the
{\mathsf{coNP}}
upper bound for

-VASS implies it for Parikh automata.

Integer VASS

A (
d
-dimensional)
integer vector addition system with states
(
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS
, for short) is a quintuple
\mathcal{V}=(Q,\varSigma,T,\iota,f)
where
Q
is a finite set of
states
,
\varSigma
is an
alphabet
,
T\subseteq Q\times\varSigma_{\varepsilon}\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}\times Q
is a finite set of
transitions
, and
\iota,f\in Q
are its
source
and
target state
, respectively. Here,
\varSigma_{\varepsilon}=\varSigma\cup\{\varepsilon\}
. A
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS
\mathcal{V}=(Q,\varSigma,T,\iota,f)
is called
deterministic
if
\mathcal{V}
has no
\varepsilon
-labeled transitions and for each
p\in Q
and
a\in\varSigma
there is at most one transition of the form
(p,a,\bm{v},q)\in T
(where
\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
q\in Q
).

A
configuration
of
\mathcal{V}
is a tuple from
Q\times\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For two configurations
(p,\bm{u}),(q,\bm{v})
and a word

we write
(p,\bm{u})\xrightarrow{w}_{\mathcal{V}}(q,\bm{v})
if there are states
q_{0},q_{1},\ldots,q_{\ell}\in Q
, vectors
\bm{v}_{0},\bm{v}_{1},\ldots,\bm{v}_{\ell}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and letters
a_{1},\ldots,a_{\ell}\in\varSigma_{\varepsilon}
such that
w=a_{1}a_{2}\cdots a_{\ell}
,
(p,\bm{u})=(q_{0},\bm{v}_{0})
,
(q,\bm{v})=(q_{\ell},\bm{v}_{\ell})

1\leq i\leq\ell
we have a transition

with
\bm{v}_{i}=\bm{v}_{i-1}+\bm{x}_{i}
. In this case, the sequence
t_{1}t_{2}\cdots t_{\ell}
is called a (

\mathcal{V}
. The

of

is
L(\mathcal{V})=\{w\in\varSigma^{*}\mid(\iota,\bm{0})\xrightarrow{w}_{\mathcal{V}}(f,\bm{0})\}

Let

be a set of indices. Then we can generalize the acceptance behavior of the
\mathbb{Z}
-VASS
\mathcal{V}
as follows:

L(\mathcal{V},I)=\bigl\{w\in\varSigma^{*}\,\bigm|\,\exists\bm{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\colon(\iota,\bm{0})\xrightarrow{w}_{\mathcal{V}}(f,\bm{v})\text{ and }\pi_{I}(\bm{v})=\bm{0}\bigr\}\,.

Note that
L(\mathcal{V},[1,d])=L(\mathcal{V})
holds.

Skeletons

Now, we want to further simplify the regular separability problem. Concretely, we want to consider only runs in

Reducing inseparability to intersection

At this point, our proof deviates from the approach of

According to

\displaystyle L(\mathcal{W}_{1})

(4)

Characterizing twin-unbounded cycles

We define for any
\hat{T}\subseteq T

S[\hat{T}]=\left\{c\in\hat{T}^{\leq|Q|}\,\middle|\,\text{$c$ is a cycle}\right\}.

Thus,

\hat{T}
.

Our characterization uses an adaptation of the notion of “cancelable productions” in
\mathbb{Z}

[

We define the homomorphism
\partial\colon\mathbb{N}^{T}\to\mathbb{Z}^{Q}
as follows: for each transition

we set
\partial(\bm{e}_{t})=\bm{e}_{q}-\bm{e}_{p}
, where
\bm{e}_{t}\in\mathbb{N}^{T}
and
\bm{e}_{p},\bm{e}_{q}\in\mathbb{N}^{Q}
are unit vectors.
Thus,
\partial(\bm{u})[q]
is the number of incoming transitions to
q
, minus the number of outgoing edges from
q
, weighted by the coefficients in
\bm{u}
.
A

is a vector
\bm{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{T}
with
\partial(\bm{f})=\bm{0}
. The
following is a standard fact in graph theory. For a proof that even applies to
context-free grammars (rather than automata), see
[

3.1]
.

Lemma 22
.

A vector
\bm{f}\in\mathbb{N}^{T}
is a flow if and only if it is a sum of (the Parikh vectors of) cycles.

The following notion will be key in characterizing which cycles are twin-unbounded for
M(I_{1})
and
M(I_{2})
.
A transition
t\in T
is
bi-cancelable
if there exist flows
\bm{f}_{1},\bm{f}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{T}
such that
(i)
\Delta_{I_{1}}(\bm{f}_{1})=\bm{0}
and
\Delta_{I_{2}}(\bm{f}_{2})=\bm{0}
,
(ii)
t
occurs in both
\bm{f}_{1}
and in
\bm{f}_{2}
, and
(iii)
\supp(\bm{f}_{1})=\supp(\bm{f}_{2})
.
In other words,
t
is bi-cancelable if it is part of two flows
\bm{f}_{1}
and
\bm{f}_{2}
with the same support and with effect zero (wrt. the components
I_{1}
resp.
I_{2}
).

.

A cycle

is twin-unbounded for

and

if, and only if, every

Proof.

For the “only if” direction, suppose that
c
is twin-unbounded for
M(I_{1})
and

. Then by definition there exist sums of period vectors
\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{S}
of

resp.
M(I_{2})
with

. Define
\bm{f}_{i}=\tau(\bm{u}_{i})\in\mathbb{N}^{T}
, where
\tau\colon\mathbb{N}^{S}\to\mathbb{N}^{T}
maps cycles to the number of occurrences of each transition in these cycles. Then clearly
\bm{f}_{i}
are flows with
\Delta_{I_{i}}(\bm{f}_{i})=\Delta_{I_{i}}(\bm{u}_{i})=\bm{0}
,

occurs in both
\bm{f}_{1}
and in
\bm{f}_{2}
, and

. Hence, all transitions in

are bi-cancelable.

For the “if” direction, suppose a cycle
c\in S

for

. For each

,

\bm{f}_{i,1}
and
\bm{f}_{i,2}
witnessing that

is

and

are flows as well and they have

\supp(\bm{f}_{1})=\supp(\bm{f}_{2})
.
As flows, both
\bm{f}_{1}
and
\bm{f}_{2}
can be written as a sum of

\bm{u}_{1},\bm{u}_{2}\in\mathbb{N}^{S}

and

Observe that
\Delta_{I_{1}}(\bm{u}_{1})=\Delta_{I_{2}}(\bm{u}_{2})=\bm{0}
, meaning

are counted to
\bm{u}_{1}
, i.e.
\supp(\bm{u}_{1})\subseteq S[\hat{T}]^{*}
. Finally, from
\Delta_{I_{2}}(\bm{u}_{1})=\bm{0}
and
\supp(\bm{u}_{1})\subseteq S[\hat{T}]^{*}
we infer that
\bm{u}_{1}\in V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
holds.

Hence,
w
satisfies all the properties of the right-hand side of the equation.

Towards the converse inclusion, let
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in S
,
\Phi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\in A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
. We will show
\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}\in L(\mathcal{W}_{1})
. From
\Phi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})\in A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
we obtain the existence of two vectors
\bm{u}_{0}\in A+U^{*}
and
\bm{u}_{1}\in V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
with
\Phi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})=\bm{u}_{0}+\bm{u}_{1}
. We construct a run from
\iota
to
f
in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
reading
\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}
as follows: for
1\leq i\leq m
choose a value
b_{i}\in\{0,1\}
such that
\bm{u}_{b}[c]=|\{1\leq i\leq m\mid c_{i}=c,b_{i}=b\}|
holds for all
b\in\{0,1\}
. Let
r_{i}\in T^{\prime*}
be the (unique) run of
\mathcal{G}_{b_{i}}
with label
\#c_{i}
. Then
r=r_{1}r_{2}\cdots r_{m}t\in T^{\prime*}
(where
t
is the transition from
\iota
to
f
) is a run from
\iota
to
f
in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
with label
\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}
. To show acceptance, we also need that
\Delta(r)=(\bm{0},\bm{0})
holds.

•

We first show
\Delta_{I_{1}}(r)=\bm{0}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{I_{1}}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{I_{1}}(r_{i})+\Delta_{I_{1}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{I_{1}}(r_{i})+\Delta_{I_{1}}(\rho)

(by definition of
t
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m,b_{i}=0}\Delta_{I_{1}}(c_{i})+\Delta_{I_{1}}(\rho)

(since
\Delta_{I_{1}}(c_{i})=\bm{0}
if
b_{i}=1
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in S}\bm{u}_{0}[c]\cdot\Delta_{I_{1}}(c)+\Delta_{I_{1}}(\rho)

(since
\bm{u}_{0}[c]=|\{1\leq i\leq m\mid c_{i}=c,b_{i}=0\}|
)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{I_{1}}(\bm{u}_{0})+\Delta_{I_{1}}(\rho)

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

(since
\bm{u}_{0}\in A+U^{*}
)

•

Now we show
\Delta_{I_{2}}(r)=\bm{0}
:

\displaystyle\Delta_{I_{2}}(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{I_{2}}(r_{i})+\Delta_{I_{2}}(t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\Delta_{I_{2}}(r_{i})

(by definition of
t
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i\leq m,b_{i}=1}\Delta_{I_{2}}(c_{i})

(since
\Delta_{I_{2}}(c_{i})=\bm{0}
if
b_{i}=0
)

\displaystyle=\sum_{c\in S}\bm{u}_{1}[c]\cdot\Delta_{I_{2}}(c)

(since
\bm{u}_{1}[c]=|\{1\leq i\leq m\mid c_{i}=c,b_{i}=1\}|
)

\displaystyle=\Delta_{I_{2}}(\bm{u}_{1})

\displaystyle=\bm{0}

(since
\bm{u}_{1}\in V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
)

Hence, the run
r
is accepting in
\mathcal{W}_{1}
implying
\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}\in L(\mathcal{W}_{1})
.
∎

Lemma 28
.

We have
L(\mathcal{W}_{1})\cap L(\mathcal{W}_{2})=\emptyset
if, and only if,
(A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}+U_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Assume
L(\mathcal{W}_{1})\cap L(\mathcal{W}_{2})=\emptyset
. Then there is a word
w\in L(\mathcal{W}_{1})\cap L(\mathcal{W}_{2})
. By
Lemma

27
there are
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in S
with

[(i)]

1.

w=\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}
,

2.

\Phi(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})\in A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
, and

3.

\Phi(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})\in B+V^{*}+U_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*}
.

Hence, we have
\Phi(c_{1},\cdots,c_{m})\in(A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}+U_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\neq\emptyset
.

Conversely, assume
(A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}+U_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\neq\emptyset
. Then there is a vector
\bm{u}\in(A+U^{*}+V_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})\cap(B+V^{*}+U_{S[\hat{T}]}^{*})
. Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\in S
be such that
\bm{u}=\Phi(c_{1},\ldots,c_{m})
. But then
Lemma

27
yields
\#c_{1}\#c_{2}\cdots\#c_{m}\in L(\mathcal{W}_{1})\cap L(\mathcal{W}_{2})\neq\emptyset
.
∎

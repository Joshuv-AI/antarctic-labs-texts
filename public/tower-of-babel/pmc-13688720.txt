Recurrence‑related factors after discontinuation of adjuvant imatinib in intermediate‑ and high‑risk GIST: a retrospective cohort study
Authors: Yibo Huang, Xiaolong Zheng, Lin Gao, Lianlian Cao, Zhaoping Li, Hao Chen, Li Chen, Liang Tao, Meng Wang, Tingting Tao, Wenxian Guan, Feng Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGastrointestinal stromal tumors (GIST) are rare malignancies originating from mesenchymal tissue, with imatinib as the standard postoperative adjuvant therapy, especially for high-risk patients. However, recurrence after discontinuation remains an important clinical problem. This study aimed to identify factors influencing recurrence after imatinib discontinuation in intermediate- and high-risk GIST patients to guide individualized treatment strategies.MethodsPatients who underwent surgical resection and imatinib adjuvant therapy in Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital from 2011 to 2021 were retrospectively analyzed. In this study, end-point of tumor treatment ( EOOT ) recurrence-free survival ( RFS ) was used as the primary endpoint, which was defined as the time from the end of imatinib adjuvant therapy to the recurrence or metastasis of GIST. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses, along with Kaplan-Meier curves, were used to assess the impact of clinicopathological factors.ResultsAmong 82 patients who discontinued imatinib, 19 experienced recurrence. Tumor site, tumor size, mitotic index, Ki-67 index, risk classification, and mutation type were significantly associated with recurrence (P < 0.05). Kaplan-Meier analysis showed that non-gastric tumors, larger size, high Ki-67, and mitotic index were linked to poorer EOOT RFS. In addition, while the EOOT RFS of patients who had been on the drug for more than 3 years was not statistically significantly different from those who had been on the drug for a shorter period of time, overall the RFS after discontinuation was longer in patients who had been on the drug for a longer period of time.ConclusionTumor size, mitotic index, Ki-67 index, and mutation type are key factors influencing recurrence after imatinib discontinuation. Prolonging the time of adjuvant therapy has an important prognostic improvement effect on high-risk patients.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12876-026-05150-4.

Background

Gastrointestinal stromal tumor ( GIST ) is the most common mesenchymal tumor of the gastrointestinal tract, with an incidence of 10–20 cases per million people, accounting for only 0.3% -0.5% of gastrointestinal tumors [1, 2]. Despite its relatively low prevalence, the unique molecular features and biological behaviours of GIST have brought it to the forefront of research. The most common molecular features of GIST are mutations in the KIT gene or PDGFRA gene, which drive tumorigenesis and progression [3]. At present, surgical resection is still the main treatment for primary GIST, but even if the tumor is completely removed, the recurrence rate is still high, especially in intermediate‑ and high‑risk patients. Since its introduction, imatinib has been established as the standard of care for adjuvant therapy after GIST, significantly reducing the rate of recurrence, and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines recommend that patients at intermediate to high risk of recurrence who were not treated with Imatinib (IM) preoperatively should receive adjuvant IM postoperatively. Intermediate-risk patients should take the drug for at least 1 year, and high-risk patients are recommended to take the drug for at least 3 years [4]. Clinical trial studies have shown that postoperative imatinib significantly prolongs recurrence-free survival and overall survival [5, 6]. However, the optimal duration of adjuvant therapy and the risk of recurrence after discontinuation remain controversial.

It has been shown that relapse may occur after termination of imatinib adjuvant therapy, but the factors influencing relapse are not fully understood. In addition, the duration of adjuvant therapy is a key factor, with longer treatment duration associated with lower relapse rates. Multiple randomised controlled trials have clearly demonstrated that prolonging the duration of imatinib administration significantly reduces the rate of recurrence. For example, the ACOSOG Z9001 trial showed that continued IM therapy for 1 year after surgery improved patients’ RFS [7]. In addition, a prospective, multicentre phase 3 study demonstrated that receiving three years of adjuvant therapy improved RFS and overall survival in patients with high-risk GIST compared to receiving one year of adjuvant imatinib after surgery [8]. A new randomised study suggests that an additional 3 years of adjuvant imatinib therapy reduces the risk of relapse in high-risk patients [9]. While existing studies have tended to focus on the duration of adjuvant therapy as a research hotspot, and previous studies have shown that clinicopathological features such as tumor size, location and mitotic index are important factors influencing GIST recurrence [10]. However, there is still a lack of systematic analysis of the specific factors related to the recurrence after the termination of adjuvant therapy, including the biological characteristics of the tumor, the individual differences of the patients and the duration of treatment, which need to be further studied and discussed. Therefore, this study explored the main factors affecting recurrence after drug withdrawal by evaluating the clinicopathological characteristics, duration of adjuvant therapy and follow-up results of patients, and provided a scientific basis for clinical development of individualized treatment and follow-up strategies.

Methods

Patient section

This study was designed as a retrospective cohort study. We included patients who underwent radical resection for GIST surgery at Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital from 2011 to 2021, who were classified as intermediate- to high-risk patients according to the revised National Institutes of Health (NIH) risk criteria which was proposed by Joensuu [11], and all of whom received adjuvant therapy with postoperative imatinib. The collected data obtained clinical data and histopathological factors. The general demographic and clinicopathological characteristics of the patients included age, gender, clinical manifestation, tumor site, resection type, tumor size, mitotic index, risk classification, CD117, CD34, Ki-67 index, tumor rupture, and mutation type. The tumor rupture was defined based on intraoperative findings (surgeon-documented tumor spillage or fragmentation) and/or pathological evidence of capsular disruption. Mutation type was obtained from pathological records and mainly included KIT exon 9, KIT exon 11, KIT exon 13, PDGFRA exon 18, and Not available. The diagnosis of GIST depends on the NCCN guidelines. Inclusion criteria are as follows : ( 1 ) Underwent surgical radical surgery, and postoperative pathological diagnosis of GIST ; ( 2 ) According to the 2008 modified NIH risk classification criteria, the patients were judged to be intermediate‑ and high‑risk risk ; ( 3 ) Postoperative adjuvant therapy with imatinib ; case exclusion criteria : ( 1 ) Combined with other malignant tumor history ; ( 2 ) Recurrence or metastasis at the initial diagnosis ; ( 3 ) Preoperative imatinib treatment; ( 4 ) Incomplete clinical and follow-up data. Ultimately, 82 patients with adjuvant imatinib discontinuation were enrolled in this study. Sixty-three of these patients were treated with imatinib and discontinued without relapse, and 19 patients relapsed after termination of imatinib treatment.

Study design

This is a single-center retrospective study. This study was designed and reported in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) guidelines to ensure transparency and completeness in reporting observational research. The main focus is on the characteristics and prognosis of recurrence and metastasis in patients with intermediate- and high-risk stromal tumors after discontinuation of imatinib adjuvant therapy. Recurrence-free survival ( RFS ) was evaluated using two definitions : one is the time from the date of surgery to the time of GIST recurrence, metastasis, or last follow-up ; second, according to the concept of end-point of tumor treatment ( EOOT ) proposed by Thomas L., it is defined as calculated from the date of the end of surgical resection or imatinib adjuvant therapy [12]. In this study, we used EOOT RFS as the primary endpoint, which was specifically defined as the time from the end of imatinib treatment to the recurrence of GIST. In addition, for patients without recurrence or progression, the end point of EOOT RFS is set to the last follow-up date. All patients who underwent surgery in our hospital need to be reviewed regularly in the first month, the third month and every six months after surgery. Clinical data and histopathological parameters were obtained from medical records. All 288 patients were followed up with complete data collection. The median duration of patient follow-up after surgery was 76 months. This study used a combination of outpatient follow-up and telephone follow-up to systematically track postoperative patients. Postoperative follow-up included routine peripheral blood test, abdominal ultrasound, endoscopy and CT examination to monitor the postoperative recurrence of patients. The diagnosis of recurrence or metastasis depends on the results of imaging examination. During the follow-up, the patient ‘s adjuvant treatment, recurrence and metastasis time, survival rate and other key information were recorded, and the duration of adjuvant treatment, recurrence time and metastasis site were tracked in detail.

Statistical analysis

All relevant statistical analyses were completed using SPSS 22.0 software (IBM Corporation, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous variables were compared using the independent samples t-test, and categorical variables were compared using the χ2 test or Fisher’s exact test. Patients were divided into two groups based on the duration of adjuvant imatinib therapy: ≤3 years and > 3 years. The cutoff of 3 years was selected according to the NCCN guidelines. The Kaplan-Meier method was used to plot survival curves for the different groups and to compare the difference in survival between the two groups by the log-rank test. Factors associated with tumor recurrence were analysed using univariate and multivariate Cox regression models and hazard ratios (HR) and their 95% confidence intervals were reported. Variables with P-value < 0. 05 in univariate analysis were included in the multivariate Cox regression model. P-values < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

Between 1 January 2011 and 31 December 2021, 288 patients with intermediate- and high-risk gastrointestinal stromal tumors who underwent therapeutic resection and received postoperative adjuvant imatinib therapy were included in this study. Table 1 summarizes the clinicopathological features of patients at moderate and high risk who did not relapse and relapsed during adjuvant imatinib treatment. Overall, 230 of these patients had no recurrence and 58 patients experienced recurrence, with a recurrence rate of around 20.1%. Of particular concern, 82 patients received imatinib adjuvant therapy for a period of time after surgery and discontinued after a period of time. Among these patients, 63 patients did not relapse, and 19 patients relapsed after discontinuing imatinib adjuvant therapy. Table 2 further compares the clinicopathological characteristics of patients at intermediate- and high-risk of relapse after discontinuation of imatinib without relapse versus with relapse. Of the 82 patients included in this group, 39 ( 47.6% ) were male and 43 ( 52.4% ) were female. The primary site was in the stomach in 55 patients ( 67.1% ), with 8 recurrences; the primary site was in the small intestine in 17 patients ( 20.7% ), with 8 recurrences and metastases; and the primary site was in the duodenum and other sites in 7 patients, with 3 recurrences. In addition, in terms of clinical manifestations, there were 28 patients with gastrointestinal bleeding, of which 23 had no recurrence and 5 had recurrence. There were 29 patients with abdominal discomfort or pain, of which 22 had no recurrence and 7 had recurrence. 6 patients were accidentally found by physical examination, 4 patients had no recurrence and 2 patients had recurrence. There were 19 patients with other clinical features, of which 14 had no recurrence and 5 had recurrence. Mutation analysis showed that 47 patients (57.3%) had KIT exon 11 mutations, among whom 12 experienced recurrence (25.5%); 5 patients (6.1%) had KIT exon 9 mutations, including 3 cases of recurrence (60.0%); and 30 patients (36.6%) showed no detectable mutations. During the follow-up period, the median time to recurrence after discontinuation of imatinib was 24 months. Table 3 summarises the specific clinicopathological information of the 19 patients who relapsed after stopping the drug. The high-risk groups of recurrence were mainly concentrated in patients with tumor diameter greater than 10 cm, higher mitotic index (> 10 /50 HPF ), Ki-67 index > 10% and shorter adjuvant treatment time. It is worth noting that in patients with recurrent or metastatic GIST, the proportion of mitotic index exceeding 10 /50 HPF was the highest, accounting for 57.9%. In addition, patients with primary tumors of the non-stomach, especially primary tumors of the small intestine, have a significantly increased risk of recurrence.

Correlation between drug discontinuation recurrence and clinicopathological features in patients with intermediate- and high-risk GISTs

To further investigate the association between recurrent metastasis and clinical characteristics in GIST patients after drug discontinuation, univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were performed. The results of the univariate analyses showed that postoperative recurrent metastasis was significantly associated with several clinical characteristics. Among them, primary tumor site (P = 0.029, HR = 2.753, 95% CI: 1.107 ~ 6.847), tumor size (P = 0.027, HR = 5.826, 95% CI: 1.227 ~ 27.659), risk classification (P = 0.011, HR = 13.877, 95% CI: 1.839 ~ 103.232 ), Ki-67 index (P < 0.001, HR = 8.285, 95% CI: 2.758 ~ 24.892), mitotic index (P = 0.001, HR = 6.801, 95% CI: 2.161 ~ 21.397) and mutation type (P = 0.038, HR = 0.258, 95% CI: 0.71 ~ 0.929) were all strongly associated with the risk of recurrence. In contrast, age, gender and resection type did not show a significant correlation in the risk of recurrence (Table 4 ). After the variables with statistically significant differences in the above univariate analyses were included in the multivariate Cox regression model, the results of the analyses showed that tumor size (P = 0.047, HR = 6.085, 95% CI: 1.025 ~ 36.141), mitotic index (P = 0.033, HR = 4.216, 95% CI: 1.120 ~ 15.873), Ki -67 index (P = 0.004, HR = 7.522, 95% CI: 1.918 ~ 29.504), and mutation type (P = 0.047, HR = 0.169, 95% CI: 0.029 ~ 0.977) were all significant independent predictors of recurrence after discontinuation of imatinib in patients with intermediate- and high-risk GIST ( Table 4 ). Of particular concern is that the Ki-67 index was found to be an important predictor of recurrence, suggesting that it is closely related to more aggressive tumor behavior.

Survival curve analysis of the correlation between discontinuation recurrence and clinicopathological features in patients with intermediate- and high-risk GISTs

A total of 288 patients were observed during the follow-up period, which ranged from 6 to 130 months. When RFS was measured from EOOT, further survival curve analysis showed that tumor size ( Log-rank P = 0.008 ), mitotic index ( Log-rank P < 0.001 ), Ki-67 index ( Log-rank P < 0.001 ) and tumor location ( Log-rank P = 0.022 ) had an effect on EOOT RFS in patients with intermediate- and high-risk GIST (Fig. 1). Specifically, tumor size had a significant effect on EOOT RFS throughout the follow-up period, with patients with tumor diameters greater than 10 cm having significantly lower EOOT RFS than patients with diameters less than 10 cm (Fig. 1A); furthermore, mitotic activity was closely associated with the risk of recurrence, with patients with mitotic indices > 10 /50 HPF having significantly lower EOOT RFS than patients with mitotic indices lower patients (Fig. 1B). The Ki-67 index was also strongly associated with the risk of recurrent metastasis, and patients with a Ki-67 index higher than 10% had significantly lower EOOT RFS than those with a lower index (Fig. 1C). Figure 1D shows the difference in RFS between patients with different primary sites. The EOOT RFS rate of patients with gastrointestinal stromal tumors in the primary site of the stomach was significantly higher than that of patients in the non-gastric site. In the follow-up, it was found that the number of patients with intermediate-risk GIST taking imatinib who had stopped taking the drug for less than 1 year was 16, of which 1 case had relapsed; the number of patients who had stopped taking the drug for more than 1 year was 17, and 0 cases had relapsed. The number of patients taking imatinib for less than 1 year in patients with high-risk stromal tumors was 16, including 9 cases of recurrence, 20 patients taking 1–3 years, and 6 cases of recurrence. In the high-risk group, 13 patients took medicine for more than 3 years, and 3 patients relapsed. As there was only one case of relapse after drug withdrawal in the intermediate-risk patients, the results of the data analysis may be biased. Therefore, the prognostic analysis is only grouped and evaluated for the medication duration of high-risk patients. Stratified according to different adjuvant medication time (≤ 1 year, 1–3 years, > 3 years ), the results showed that the length of adjuvant time and the recurrence rate after drug withdrawal showed a certain trend. Specifically, although not statistically significant (log-rank P = 0.140), the longer EOOT RFS in high-risk patients with more than 3 years of adjuvant medication compared to those with less than 1 year or 1–3 years of adjuvant medication had a significantly lower risk of recurrence (Fig. 2A). However, Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis showed that using recurrence-free survival (RFS) as the study endpoint, patients with more than three years of adjuvant had significantly longer RFS, with a statistically significant difference compared to patients with less than one year and 1–3 years of adjuvant (log-rank P = 0.019) (Fig. 2B). This result indicates that prolonged imatinib adjuvant treatment time may be associated with better recurrence-free survival, suggesting that the risk of recurrence in patients with long-term medication is significantly reduced.

Discussion

This study is a single-center retrospective analysis. We retrospectively analyzed the clinicopathological data of patients with intermediate- and high-risk gastrointestinal stromal tumors who received imatinib adjuvant therapy after surgery in Nanjing Drum Tower Hospital, and explored the recurrence factors and prognostic effects of GIST patients after discontinuation of imatinib adjuvant therapy. This study revealed significant associations between primary site, tumor size, mitotic index, Ki-67 index, risk classification, and type of mutation with the risk of recurrence and metastasis in patients with intermediate- to high-risk gastrointestinal stromal tumors after postoperative termination of imatinib. These findings provide new perspectives for further optimising postoperative treatment strategies. In the present study, patients of different genders and age groups did not show significant differences in the risk of postoperative recurrence and metastasis, which is in line with the results of previous studies [13]. In addition, previous studies have shown that gastrointestinal bleeding is associated with poor prognosis in patients [14]. In contrast, clinical manifestations of gastrointestinal symptoms, such as gastrointestinal bleeding and abdominal discomfort, did not show statistically significant differences in our study. This result may indicate that although these symptoms are clinically common, they have a relatively limited role in predicting postoperative recurrence or metastasis. In this study, tumor rupture, as one of the known risk factors for postoperative recurrence in patients with gastrointestinal stromal tumors [15], did not show significant statistical differences. The limitation of the sample size may have resulted in insufficient statistical efficacy to detect the potential impact of tumor rupture on recurrence. In particular, univariate analysis showed that the risk of recurrence in patients with non-primary gastric tumors was significantly higher than that in patients with primary gastric tumors ( P = 0.029 ). This result is consistent with previous studies, indicating that GIST outside the stomach usually has more invasive biological behavior [15, 16], which may be related to the differences in tumor microenvironment in these sites. Gastric GIST often responds better to imatinib treatment and has a lower recurrence rate, while non-gastric primary GIST shows a higher tendency of recurrence and metastasis. This elevated risk may be attributed to their distinct molecular profile, characterized by a higher prevalence of KIT exon 9 mutations and consequent hyperactivation of downstream proliferative pathways such as RAS–MEK–MAPK and PI3K–mTOR signaling [17]. Future research should further explore the relationship between the primary site and the biological behavior of GIST, so as to more accurately assess the risk of recurrence in patients in clinical practice.

Risk stratification in gastrointestinal stromal tumor patients typically incorporates factors such as tumor size, mitotic index, and primary tumor location to assess prognosis. After discontinuation of adjuvant imatinib therapy, patients with higher risk stratification exhibit a significantly increased risk of recurrence and metastasis. This elevated risk is particularly pronounced in those classified as high-risk. Previous studies have demonstrated that extending the duration of adjuvant imatinib therapy can help delay or reduce the recurrence rate in high-risk patients [8]. However, the findings of this study indicate that even with prolonged treatment, high-risk patients remain at a substantial risk of recurrence and metastasis after discontinuation of therapy. Tumor size and mitotic index, as traditional prognostic factors, demonstrated strong predictive power for recurrence risk in GIST patients in this study. Both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses identified tumor size and mitotic index as independent risk factors for recurrence after discontinuation of therapy in intermediate- to high-risk GIST patients (P < 0.05). These findings are consistent with the known impact of tumor size and mitotic index in the NIH risk classification system for recurrence [11], aligning with the results of our study. Larger tumors are typically associated with higher malignant potential, which may be linked to a faster proliferation rate, greater invasiveness, and a higher likelihood of developing resistance mutations. Patients with tumor size > 10 cm and mitotic index > 10/50 HPF exhibited a significantly increased risk of recurrence after discontinuation of adjuvant imatinib therapy, underscoring the need for more intensive follow-up and extended treatment strategies in this high-risk group.

As a cell proliferation marker, the high expression of Ki-67 is related to the degree of malignancy and the rapid proliferation of tumor cells [18, 19]. In this study, both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses confirmed the significance of the Ki-67 index as an independent risk factor for recurrence (P < 0.05), impacting recurrence-free survival in high-risk GIST patients after discontinuation of therapy. To date, several studies have reported the association between high Ki-67 expression and the malignant potential of GIST, highlighting its role as an important prognostic factor for recurrence. Ki-67 may hold significant value in assessing the malignant potential of the disease [20, 21]. Patients with a high Ki-67 index exhibited a significantly increased recurrence rate after discontinuation of therapy, suggesting that high proliferative activity may contribute to more rapid disease progression and the development of resistance mutations. This underscores the need to incorporate these markers into postoperative risk assessment in clinical practice to guide more precise treatment decisions. Additionally, the type of genetic mutation plays a critical role in the prognostic assessment of GIST. KIT and PDGFRA mutations are the most common driver mutations in GIST, determining the tumor’s sensitivity to imatinib and its biological behavior. KIT mutations, the most frequent genetic event in gastrointestinal stromal tumors, are associated with malignant features and poor prognosis [22]. However, it is noteworthy that GIST mutation status is not as strongly correlated with adverse risk as mitotic count and Ki-67 index. Furthermore, we did not find PDGFRA mutations to be significant predictors of recurrence, which may be attributed to the small sample size of our cohort and the predominance of KIT mutations. For patients with high-risk mutation types, extended treatment duration or the exploration of alternative targeted therapies may be necessary to minimize the risk of recurrence.

The duration of imatinib treatment significantly impacts patients’ recurrence-free survival. Kaplan-Meier analysis indicates a significant association between treatment duration and RFS. Although patients treated for more than three years did not statistically show significant differences in EOOT RFS compared to those treated for less than one year or between one and three years, those with longer treatment durations generally exhibited longer RFS. These findings support current guidelines recommending at least three years of adjuvant therapy for intermediate- to high-risk patients. Our findings are consistent with the ACOSOG Z9001 trial and other large studies, which have demonstrated the benefits of extending adjuvant therapy duration in reducing the risk of recurrence [7]. The SSG XVIII/AIO trial investigated the RFS of high-risk patients receiving adjuvant imatinib for 12 months versus 36 months, suggesting that three years of adjuvant imatinib therapy can effectively reduce the risk of recurrence [8]. In the latest BFR14 trial, it was indicated that patients with stable GIST are not advised to discontinue imatinib therapy, non-progressing GIST patients who interrupted imatinib treatment exhibited rapid disease progression during long-term follow-up compared to those who continued imatinib for three or five years [23]. Furthermore, these patients developed resistance to imatinib more quickly and had shorter overall survival. Therefore, patients at higher risk of recurrence, such as those with tumors greater than 10 cm, a mitotic index exceeding 10/50 HPF, or a Ki-67 index greater than 10% should not discontinue therapy casually. Extending the duration of treatment may be particularly beneficial for these individuals. Prolonged imatinib therapy could exert a more profound inhibitory effect on tumor cells, thereby reducing the risk of recurrence and extending patients’ RFS.

In summary, this study highlights the role of factors such as primary tumor location, tumor size, Ki-67 index, mitotic index, and mutation type in the recurrence of GIST patients after discontinuation of therapy. The results indicate that patients with a primary tumor located outside the stomach, tumor size greater than 10 cm, a mitotic index exceeding 10/50 HPF, Ki-67 > 10%, and the presence of KIT exon 11 deletion mutations may affect EOOT RFS and could benefit from prolonged adjuvant imatinib therapy, which may help reduce the risk of recurrence. For these high-risk patients, individualized treatment strategies should be considered, including more frequent monitoring and extended treatment durations. Conversely, premature discontinuation of therapy may lead to the reactivation of residual tumor cells, potentially resulting in recurrence. The significant effect of imatinib administration time on RFS after drug withdrawal suggests that the duration of adjuvant therapy should be flexibly adjusted according to the specific conditions of patients, especially the biological characteristics of tumors and the tolerance of patients. Through precise individualized treatment and long-term follow-up management, the long-term survival rates and quality of life for these patients can be further improved. Otherwise, optimizing post-discontinuation management strategies for patients at high risk of recurrence is imperative. This requires a dual approach focusing on enhanced monitoring and personalized treatment extension. The points should be included: (1) intensive radiological monitoring during the first 2–3 years; (2) extended imatinib therapy duration with consideration of reduced-dose maintenance regimens when appropriate; (3) implementation of risk-stratified follow-up protocols based on refined risk assessment. These strategies should be balanced against individual patients’ socioeconomic factors, side effects, and treatment adherence. Additionally, incorporating psychological support and structured clinician-patient communication is recommended to address recurrence-related anxiety and improve overall management. Furthermore, therapeutic strategies for recurrent disease need careful attention. Primary treatment options include reinitiating imatinib therapy, switching to other tyrosine kinase inhibitors such as sunitinib, or pursuing surgical resection. However, the selection of appropriate treatment requires comprehensive evaluation, and the development of personalized management strategies needs further refinement.

Limitations of the study

Certainly, it is important to acknowledge the limitations of this study. First, this research is a single-center retrospective study, and the inherent limitations of this design may introduce selection bias and information bias. Moreover, the single-center nature may limit the generalizability of our findings to other healthcare settings with different patient populations and treatment protocols. Retrospective studies rely on existing medical records, and the completeness of follow-up time, patient adherence, and treatment response may be constrained, making it difficult to avoid potential biases in the assessment of recurrence risk factors. Second, due to the relatively small sample size, particularly in subgroup analyses such as mutation subtypes and intermediate-risk patients, statistical power is limited. Although the six variables included in the multivariate model—tumor site, tumor size, mitotic index, risk classification, Ki-67 index, and mutation type—have been consistently demonstrated to be strongly associated with prognosis in GIST, and our findings are in line with previous studies, the limited number of recurrence events (n = 19) remains a major limitation. Therefore, larger-scale, prospective, multicenter studies are needed in the future to enhance the generalizability and statistical credibility of the findings. Lastly, while we analyzed the impact of imatinib treatment duration on recurrence, we lack data on patient adherence, dosage adjustments, and management of adverse effects. Additionally, certain potential confounding factors such as detailed imaging characteristics and socioeconomic status were not included in our analysis. Treatment adherence may influence patients’ long-term survival and recurrence risk, highlighting the need for the establishment of a more standardized, long-term, systematic follow-up management approach in future studies. Future research should also incorporate comprehensive biomarker evaluation and standardized imaging assessment to further refine risk stratification.

Conclusions

In conclusion, this study underscores the significance of tumor size, mitotic index, Ki-67 index, tumor location, and molecular mutations in predicting recurrence after the discontinuation of imatinib in intermediate- to high-risk GIST patients. For high-risk patients, extending the duration of adjuvant therapy may be essential to minimize recurrence and improve long-term outcomes.

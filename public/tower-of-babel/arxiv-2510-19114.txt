Recent developments in exponential functionals of Lévy processes
Martin Minchev, Mladen Savov

Abstract
This survey aims to review two decades of progress on exponential functionals of (possibly killed) real-valued Lévy processes. Since the publication of the seminal survey by Bertoin and Yor [ BY05 ] , substantial advances have been made in understanding the structure and properties of these random variables. At the same time, numerous applications of these quantities have emerged across various different contexts of modern applied probability. Motivated by all this, in this manuscript, we provide a detailed overview of these developments, beginning with a discussion of the class of special functions that have played a central role in recent progress, and then organising the main results on exponential functionals into thematic groups. Moreover, we complement several of these results and set them within a unified framework. Throughout, we strive to offer a coherent historical account of each contribution, highlighting both the probabilistic and analytical techniques that have driven the advances in the field.



1
Introduction

In this survey, we review two decades of progress in the study of exponential functionals of (possibly killed) real-valued Lévy processes, following the classical treatment by Bertoin and Yor
[
BY05
]
.

We recall that an unkilled Lévy process
\xi=(\xi_{s})_{s\geq 0}
possesses almost surely (a.s.) right-continuous paths, starts from zero (i.e.,
\xi_{0}=0
), and satisfies that, for any
t>0
, in law

(\xi_{t+s}-\xi_{t})_{s\geq 0}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{=}}\;(\xi_{s})_{s\geq 0},

with
(\xi_{t+s}-\xi_{t})_{s\geq 0}
independent of
(\xi_{s})_{s\leq t}
. The latter two properties are structural and are known as stationary and independent increments. Potential killing is introduced via an exponential time
\mathbf{e}_{q}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(q)
,
q\geq 0
,
which is independent of
\xi
, and such that
\xi_{t}=\infty
for
t\geq\mathbf{e}_{q}
,
with
\mathbf{e}_{0}\equiv\infty
. Potentially killed Lévy processes are in bijection with negative definite functions via the relation

\Psi(z)\;=\;-q+\gamma z+\tfrac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\!\big(e^{zy}-1-\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{|y|\leq 1\right\}}\,zy\big)\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}y),\qquad z\in i\mathbb{R},

(1.1)

where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
,
\sigma^{2}\geq 0
,
q\geq 0
, and
\Pi
is a
\sigma
-finite measure satisfying the integrability condition

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\min\{y^{2},1\}\,\Pi(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty.

The quantities are known respectively as: the linear term (
\gamma
), the diffusion term (
\sigma^{2}
), the killing term (
q
), and the Lévy measure (
\Pi
). The measure
\Pi
determines the intensity and the spatial structure of the jumps of
\xi
. The trivial case of unkilled Lévy process satisfying
\xi\equiv C
is excluded from our considerations.
In this survey by Lévy processes we understand potentially killed Lévy processes.

This paper surveys and occasionally complements the results on the quantity

\begin{split}&I_{\Psi}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi_{s}}\mathrm{d}s=\int_{0}^{\mathbf{e}_{q}}e^{-\xi_{s}}\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

(1.2)

Our approach is to present various groups of results related to exponential functionals, such as the finiteness and computation of moments, smoothness of densities, and more, whilst also outlining their historical development and where suitable discuss the main tools used in their derivation. We also dedicate a section to the properties of the so-called Bernstein-gamma functions, which have proven to be central in the most recent developments in the area.

1.1
Literature review

Here we provide a brief literature review on the development of the theory of exponential functionals of Lévy processes. In our view, the recurrence equation (
2.2
) has been the most instrumental tool for deriving a few of the sharpest results in this area. Nevertheless, many important contributions have also been obtained through alternative approaches and methodologies.

The study of exponential functionals was initiated by Urbanik in the broader context of infinite divisibility and multiplicative infinite divisibility of functionals of stochastic processes; see
[
Urb95
]
. Subsequent work on general properties of exponential functionals of Lévy processes, including the existence of moments, integral equations for the law, and special cases of the recurrence equation (
2.2
), crucial in their study, was carried out mainly by M. Yor and co-authors; see
[
BY05
,
CPY94
,
CPY01
,
Yor01
]
. Of particular note is the work of Zwart and co-authors, who established a relatively general version of the recurrence equation for the moments and applied it in special cases; see
[
GRZ04
,
MZ06
]
, where, for instance, they also derived the density of the exponential functional for non-decreasing compound Poisson processes. Following
[
MZ06
]
, it became increasingly clear that solving the recurrence equation for the moments can identify not only the moments but also the law of the exponential functional of Lévy processes. This insight led to the derivation of the Mellin transform of the exponential functional for various classes of Lévy processes and, in certain cases, to series representations of their law; see
[
Kuz12
,
Kuz13
,
KKP12
,
KP13
]
. Finally, the recurrence equation was solved in full generality in terms of the so-called Bernstein-gamma functions in
[
PS18
]
. This work culminated earlier studies on factorisations of the law of the exponential functional; see
[
PPS12
,
PS12
,
PS13
]
. An equivalent form of (
2.2
), involving the density of the exponential functional and the potential measure of the underlying Lévy process, was presented and applied in
[
AR23
]
.

Some results concerning the law of the exponential functional have been obtained by exploiting the fact that this random variable satisfies a random affine equation — a relation extensively studied in the literature; see, e.g.,
[
AR23
,
MZ06
,
Riv05
,
Riv12
]
. Through its connection with positive self-similar Markov processes, the law of the exponential functional of spectrally positive Lévy processes has been expressed in series form; see
[
Pat12
]
. Moreover, the fact that the exponential functional arises as the stationary law of Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes has been used to derive factorisations of its law; see
[
PPS12
]
.

The
main reason for the interest in these random variables is due to the fact that exponential
functionals have played a role in various domains of theoretical and
applied probability such as the spectral theory of some non-reversible
Markov semigroups (
[
PS17
,
PS21
,
PSZ19
,
Sav17
]
),
the study of random planar maps (
[
BCK18
,
Bud18
]
),
limiting theorems for Markov chains (
[
BK16
]
), positive
self-similar Markov processes
(
[
BC02
,
BY02a
,
CC06a
,
CC06b
,
Pat09a
,
Pat09b
]
), financial and insurance mathematics
(
[
HK14
,
KLM06
,
Pat13
]
), branching processes with
immigration (
[
Pat09a
]
), random processes in random environment
(
[
BH12
,
LX18
,
PPS16
,
Xu21
]
), random fragmentations (
[
Ber02
,
Ber06
,
Dad17
,
Haa23
,
Ste18
]
) with
[
Ste18
]
dealing with multitype fragmentation, extinction times of
self-similar processes (
[
LPS19
]
), stationary distributions of Lévy-driven Ornstein-Uhlenbeck processes (
[
Beh15
,
BL15
,
BLM11
,
BLR21

BLRR21

Kev18

Let
I_{\Psi}(t)=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-\xi_{s}}\,\mathrm{d}s,\;t\geq 0,
be the exponential functional on deterministic horizon. The law of
I_{\Psi}(t)
is considerably harder to study. Nevertheless, some recent progress relies on the observation that the classical exponential functional of a killed Lévy process
I_{\Psi_{q}}
, with
\Psi_{q}(z)=\Psi(z)-q
, is related to the Laplace transform of
I_{\Psi}(t)
. Consequently, the representation of the moments of
I_{\Psi_{q}}
in (
3.13
), combined with Laplace and Mellin inversion, has yielded useful results on the moments of
I_{\Psi}(t)
; see
[
Bar19
,
BS21
,
PSS24
]
for series expansions of the moments when the Lévy process is a subordinator, and
[
PSS24
]
for explicit evaluations of some moments when
\xi
is symmetric as well as for general convolutional equations. Moreover, using the same link, the finiteness of integrals of the type
\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{n}F(I_{\Psi}(t))\,\mathrm{d}t
, for suitable functions
F
and integer
n
, and scaling limits for
\mathbb{P}\left(I_{\Psi}(t)\in\mathrm{d}x\right)
, have been studied in
[
Min24
,
MS25
]
. We highlight that some papers in the short literature review on exponential functionals above, in fact, use exponential functionals on a deterministic horizon, but the properties of the latter are derived with the help of the former.

1.2
Common notation

We have included an index of notation at the end of the survey. However, we spell out some of it now to enable smooth reading. We denote by
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
the sets of non-negative integers and all integers, respectively. We set
\mathbb{R}:=(-\infty,\infty)
for the real line,
\mathbb{R}^{+}:=({0,\infty})
for the positive real half-line, and
\mathbb{C}
for the complex plane. For
z\in\mathbb{C}
we use
\mathtt{Re}(z)
for its real part and
\mathtt{Im}(z)
for its imaginary part.
Next, for
-\infty\leq a<b\leq\infty
, we set
\mathbb{C}_{(a,b)}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathtt{Re}(z)\in(a,b)\right\}
for a complex strip, and use
\mathbb{C}_{a}:=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathtt{Re}(z)=a\right\}
,
a\in\mathbb{R}
, for a complex line. For the same range of
a
and
b
, we denote by
\mathtt{A}_{(a,b)}
(respectively,
\mathtt{M}_{(a,b)}
) the space of functions holomorphic (respectively, meromorphic) on
\mathbb{C}_{(a,b)}
.
The space
\mathtt{A}_{[a,b)}
, with
-\infty<a<b\leq\infty
, consists of those functions in
\mathtt{A}_{(a,b)}
that can be continuously extended to
\mathbb{C}_{a}
; other variations such as
\mathtt{A}_{[a,b]},\mathtt{A}_{(a,b]}
are defined analogously. Throughout we use
\log_{0}:\mathbb{C}\setminus\left(-\infty,0\right]\to\mathbb{C}
for the main branch of the complex logarithm defined via the main branch of the argument
\arg_{0}:\mathbb{C}\to\left(-\pi,\pi\right]
. When we do not specify the branch we use
\log
.

2
Bernstein-gamma functions

A natural object in the description of exponential functionals are
Bernstein-gamma functions
, which are defined as the unique solution to

W_{\phi}(z+1)=\phi(z)W_{\phi}(z),\quad\text{on}\quad\mathtt{Re}(z)>0;\quad\text{with}\quad W_{\phi}(1)=1,

(2.1)

where
\phi
is a Bernstein function, the class of which we recall below.
It is further known that there exists a random variable
Y_{\phi}
such that

\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{\phi}>0\right)=1
and
W_{\phi}(z)=\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{\phi}^{z-1}\right]
for
\mathtt{Re}(z)>0
, see
[
PS21
, Thm. 6.1]
and
[
PS18
, Sec. 4]
. The relevance of these special functions in the study of
I_{\Psi}
stems from the possibility of extending the following recurrence equation

\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{z}\right]\;=\;\frac{-z}{\Psi(-z)}\,\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{\,z-1}\right],

(2.2)

to the complex plane, or to suitable strips therein.
This equation has already appeared in certain special cases in
[
CPY97
]
, and its subsequent development is discussed in Remark
3.2
. Then, using the Wiener-Hopf factorisation of
\Psi(z)
as
-\phi_{+}(-z)\phi_{-}(z)
, described in Section
3.1
, one may formally express the solution to (
2.2
), that is, the complex moments of
I_{\Psi}
, as

\mathbb{E}\left[I_{\Psi}^{\,z-1}\right]\;=\;\phi_{-}(0)\,\frac{\Gamma(z)}{W_{\phi_{+}}(z)}\,W_{\phi_{-}}(1-z).

The precise statement, due to
[
PS18
]
, is presented in Theorem
3.1
.

Having outlined the importance of Bernstein-gamma functions in the context of this survey, we now present some of their basic properties and characteristics, while more intricate complex-analytical aspects are deferred to Section
4
. We also emphasise that Bernstein-gamma functions are of independent interest, as they subsume several well-known special functions, starting with Euler’s Gamma function
[
Eul38
]
, and later developments such as the Barnes gamma functions and the
q
-gamma function from
q
-calculus; see
[
BBY04
,
Chh16
]
. Moreover,
[
PS21
]
demonstrates that Bernstein-gamma functions play a fundamental role in the study of Markovian self-similarity.

We recall that a function
\phi:\mathbb{C}_{\left[0,\infty\right)}\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
Bernstein function
if and only if

\phi(z)\;=\;\phi(0)+\mathbf{d}z+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-zy}\right)\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\;=\;\phi(0)+\mathbf{d}z+z\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}\,\bar{\mu}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y,

(2.3)

where
\phi(0)
and
\mathbf{d}
are non-negative constants,
\mu
is a
\sigma
-finite measure with

\int_{0}^{\infty}\min\left\{y,1\right\}\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y)<\infty
, and

\bar{\mu}(y):=\mu\left(\left(y,\infty\right)\right),\qquad y\geq 0.

Bernstein functions are in bijection with subordinators (i.e., non-decreasing Lévy processes) via

\phi(z):=-\log_{0}\mathbb{E}\left[e^{-z\xi_{1}}\right],\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left[0,\infty\right)},

(2.4)

where
\xi
is a subordinator. Up to an additive constant, they are related to completely monotone functions through their derivatives, namely

\phi^{\prime}(z)\;=\;\mathbf{d}+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}\,y\,\mu(\mathrm{d}y)\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}\,\left(y\mu(\mathrm{d}y)+\mathbf{d}\delta_{0}(\mathrm{d}y)\right),\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left[0,\infty\right)}.

(2.5)

In this survey, we introduce only those quantities and properties pertaining to Bernstein functions that are needed here, and we refer to
[
BS21
,
MS23
,
SSV12
]
for extensive results concerning them.
Note that always
\phi\in\mathtt{A}_{\left[0,\infty\right)}
, and set

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\inf\left\{u<0:\,\phi\in\mathtt{A}_{(u,\infty)}\right\}=-\sup\left\{\lambda\geq 0:\,\mathbb{E}\left[e^{\lambda\xi_{1}}\right]\text{ is finite}\right\}\in\left[-\infty,0\right],

(2.6)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{u\in\left[\mathfrak{a}_{\phi},0\right]:\,\phi(u)=0\right\}\in\left[-\infty,0\right],

\displaystyle\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi}

\displaystyle:=\max\left\{\mathfrak{a}_{\phi},\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}\right\}\in\left[-\infty,0\right],

with
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
and
\inf\emptyset=0
. By definition, if
u_{\phi}
is finite, it is not smaller than
\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}
, so

\text{if }\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}>-\infty,\quad\text{then }\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi}=\mathfrak{u}_{\phi},

(2.7)

whereas it may happen that
\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}=-\infty<\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}
. Take, for example,
\phi(z)=(z+1)^{\alpha}+1
with
\alpha\in(0,1)
, in which case
\phi
is a Bernstein function with
\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}=-1
and
\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}=-\infty
. Further examples of Bernstein functions, together with the corresponding quantities from (
2.6
), are listed in Appendix
A
.

Each Bernstein function is also related to the potential measure of the associated subordinator via the classical Laplace transform identity

\frac{1}{\phi(z)}\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}\,U(\mathrm{d}y)\;=\;\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{t}\in\mathrm{d}y\right)\,\mathrm{d}t,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left(0,\infty\right)}.

(2.8)

Note that
\phi(0)
may be positive, in which case the potential measure coincides with the
\phi(0)
-potential measure of the unkilled subordinator pertaining to
\phi(z)-\phi(0)
.

Bernstein-gamma functions, defined via (
2.1
), inherit key features of the celebrated gamma function, which solves (
2.1
) with
\phi(z)=z
. Below, we collect their most important properties, present illustrative examples, and provide a historical overview in the corresponding remarks.

We start by presenting the product and integral representations for
W_{\phi}
, which extend well-known representations of the gamma function itself.

Theorem 2.1

(Thm. 6.0.1 in
[
PS21
]
and Thm. 4.7 in
[
PS18
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function. Then we have the Weierstrass product representation

W_{\phi}(z)\;=\;\frac{e^{-\gamma_{\phi}z}}{\phi(z)}\prod_{k=1}^{\infty}\frac{\phi(k)}{\phi(k+z)}\,e^{\tfrac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\phi(k)}z}\;\in\;\mathtt{A}_{\left(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi},\infty\right)}\cap\mathtt{M}_{\left(\mathfrak{a}_{\phi},\infty\right)},

(2.9)

where

\gamma_{\phi}\;:=\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(k)}{\phi(k)}-\ln\phi(n)\right)\;\in\;\left[-\ln\phi(1),\,\tfrac{\phi^{\prime}(1)}{\phi(1)}-\ln\phi(1)\right].

(2.10)

Moreover, we have the integral representation

\log W_{\phi}(z+1)\;=\;z\ln\phi(1)+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(e^{-zy}-1-z(e^{-y}-1)\right)\frac{k(\mathrm{d}y)}{y(e^{y}-1)},\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left(-1,\infty\right)},

(2.11)

where
k(\mathrm{d}y):=\int_{0}^{y}U(\mathrm{d}y-r)(r\mu(\mathrm{d}r)+\mathbf{d}\delta_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathrm{d}r))
,
with
U
being the potential measure of the subordinator pertaining to
\phi
.
Furthermore, the Laplace transform of
k
is

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\lambda y}k(\mathrm{d}y)\;=\;\frac{\phi^{\prime}(\lambda)}{\phi(\lambda)},\qquad\lambda\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

(

.

Remark 2.3

(
Further comments
)
.

When
\phi
is a Wiener-Hopf factor of a Lévy process

, the measure

in (

We continue with a representation of
W_{\phi}
that is suitable for deriving asymptotics, for which we will need the following terms:

\begin{split}L_{\phi}(z)&:=\int_{1\to z+1}\log_{0}\phi(\chi)\,d\chi,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left(-1,\infty\right)},\\
T_{\phi}&:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)(1-(u-\lfloor u\rfloor))\left(\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)}{\phi(u)}\right)^{2}-\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\phi(u)}\right)\mathrm{d}u\;\in\;\mathbb{R},\\
E_{\phi}(z)&:=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}(u-\lfloor u\rfloor)(1-(u-\lfloor u\rfloor))\left(\log_{0}\!\left(\frac{\phi(u+z)}{\phi(u)}\right)^{\prime\prime}\right)\mathrm{d}u,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}_{\left(0,\infty\right)}.\end{split}

(2.12)

Here, the complex integral is taken along any contour in

connecting

to

,

denotes the floor function, and all derivatives inside the integrals are taken with respect to

.

Theorem 2.4

(Thm. 2.9 in
[

]
)

Let

.

Remark 3.17

(
Further comments
)
.

Essentially, (

From Proposition

&\qquad\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\exp\left(\int_{1+a}^{1+a^{\prime}}\ln|u+ib|-\ln|\phi_{+}(u+ib)|+\ln|\phi_{-}(1-u-ib)|\,\mathrm{d}u\right).\end{split}

Since for any Bernstein function,
\left|\phi(z)\right|=\mathbf{d}|z|(1+\mathrm{o}(1))
uniformly on

(including

(

Additional cases of decay are presented in

Next, we provide some examples where

3.1.1
Brownian motion with drift

If
\xi
is a potentially killed Brownian motion with killing rate
q
, drift
\mu
, and variance
\sigma^{2}
, then

\Psi(z)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}z^{2}+\mu z-q=-\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(z+b_{+})(-z-b_{-}),

where

b_{\pm}:=\frac{\mu}{\sigma^{2}}\pm\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}\sqrt{\mu^{2}+2q\sigma^{2}}.

In the Wiener–Hopf factorisation (
3.3
), this corresponds to

\phi_{+}(z)=z-b_{-},\qquad\phi_{-}(z)=\frac{\sigma^{2}}{2}(z+b_{+}).

It follows that
I_{\Psi}<\infty
almost surely if and only if
q>0
or
\mu>0
; see (
3.20
). From the first example in Appendix
A
and the last claim of Theorem
4.3
, we obtain

W_{\phi_{+}}(z)=\frac{\Gamma(z-b_{-})}{\Gamma(1-b_{-})},\qquad W_{\phi_{-}}(z)=\frac{\sigma^{2(z-1)}}{2^{z-1}}\frac{\Gamma(z+b_{+})}{\Gamma(1+b_{+})}.

Therefore, from (
3.13
),

\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=b_{+}\left(\frac{\sigma^{2-2z}}{2^{1-z}}\right)\frac{\Gamma(z)\,\Gamma(1-b_{-})}{\Gamma(z-b_{-})}\frac{\Gamma(1-z+b_{+})}{\Gamma(1+b_{+})}.

(3.23)

In the special case
\mu=0
,
\sigma^{2}=2
, and
q>0
, corresponding to a killed symmetric Brownian motion, this reduces to

\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=\sqrt{q}\,\frac{\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z+\sqrt{q})}\,\Gamma(1-z+\sqrt{q}).

3.1.2
Hypergeometric Lévy processes

Let
\xi
be a stable Lévy process, i.e., for any
c>0
it holds that

\left(\xi_{ct}\right)_{t\geq 0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle w}}{{=}}c^{1/\alpha}\left(\xi_{t}\right)_{t\geq 0},

where
\alpha\in\left(0,2\right]
, with
\alpha=2
corresponding to the Brownian motion case. In
[
CC06a
]
, the following three processes derived from
\xi
are considered:

•

\xi
started from
x>0
and killed upon exiting the upper half-plane;

•

\xi
started from zero and conditioned to stay positive;

•

\xi
started from
x>0
and conditioned to hit zero continuously.

These latter processes are positive self-similar Markov processes and thus can be represented as suitable random time changes of, respectively, three Lévy processes called Lamperti stable Lévy processes, say
\xi^{*},\,\xi^{\uparrow}
, and
\xi^{\downarrow}
. This is a consequence of the Lamperti representation; see
[
BS11
,
CC06a
,
Lam72
]
for more information. For each of
\xi^{*},\,\xi^{\uparrow}
, and
\xi^{\downarrow}
, one can define a respective exponential functional, which represents the absorption time of the corresponding positive self-similar Markov process or other quantity of interest. The processes
\xi^{*},\,\xi^{\uparrow}
, and
\xi^{\downarrow}
fall into the domain of the so-called hypergeometric Lévy processes, see
[
KP13
]
.

Hypergeometric Lévy processes are defined via their Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent, which takes the form

\begin{split}\Psi(z)&=-\frac{\Gamma\left(1-\beta+\gamma-z\right)}{\Gamma\left(1-\beta-z\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}+z\right)}{\Gamma\left(\hat{\beta}+z\right)},\quad\text{for}\quad\beta\leq 1,\,\hat{\beta}\geq 0,\,\text{and }\gamma,\hat{\gamma}\in\left(0,1\right).\end{split}

(3.24)

In
[
KP13
, Thm 2]
, it is shown that if
\xi
is hypergeometric, then

\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=C\,\Gamma(z)\,\frac{G(1-\beta+z,1)}{G(1-\beta+\gamma+z,1)}\,\frac{G(\hat{\beta}+\hat{\gamma}+1-z,1)}{G(\hat{\beta}+1-z,1)},

(3.25)

where
G
denotes the Barnes gamma function and
C
is a normalising constant such that
\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(1)=1
. This result generalises the result on exponential functionals of hyper-exponential Lévy processes presented in
[
CK12
]
.

3.1.3
Meromorphic Lévy processes and Lévy processes with jumps of rational transform

Meromorphic Lévy processes, as introduced in
[
KKP12
]
, are a versatile class of Lévy processes containing many known classes of Lévy processes, and further extending the aforementioned hypergeometric Lévy processes; see
[
KKP12
, Sec. 3]
. We recall that
\xi
is a meromorphic Lévy process if its Lévy measure has a density of the form

\pi(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x>0\right\}}\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\rho_{n}e^{-\rho_{n}x}+\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x<0\right\}}\sum_{n\geq 1}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{\rho}_{n}e^{\hat{\rho}_{n}x},

where
a_{n},\rho_{n},\hat{a}_{n},\hat{\rho}_{n}
are strictly increasing positive sequences tending to infinity and

\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}\rho_{n}^{-2}<\infty\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{n\geq 1}\hat{a}_{n}\hat{\rho}_{n}^{-2}<\infty,

see
[
KKP12
, Thm 1]
for the full characterisation of meromorphic Lévy processes. In particular,
[
KKP12
, Thm 1.]
implies that
\Psi(z)
extends to a real meromorphic function, and for any
q>0
, the equation
\Psi(z)=q
has only simple real solutions
\chi_{n}(q)
and
-\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)
,
n\geq 1
, such that, with
\hat{\rho}_{0}=0
, one has

\dots<-\hat{\rho}_{2}<-\hat{\chi}_{2}(q)<-\hat{\rho}_{1}<-\hat{\chi}_{1}(q)<0<\chi_{1}(q)<\rho_{1}<\chi_{2}(q)<\rho_{2}<\dots,

see
[
HK14
]
. Then
[
HK14
, (3.6)]
gives the following expression for the Mellin transform of the exponential functional of meromorphic Lévy processes:

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=C^{z-1}(q)\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}&\frac{\Gamma(\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)+1)}{\Gamma(\hat{\rho}_{n-1}+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\hat{\rho}_{n-1}+z)}{\Gamma(\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)+z)}\frac{(\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)+1)^{z-1}}{(\hat{\rho}_{n-1}+1)^{z-1}}\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\times\frac{\Gamma(\rho_{n})\,\Gamma(\chi_{n}(q)+1-z)}{\Gamma(\chi_{n}(q))\,\Gamma(\rho_{n}+1-z)}\frac{\chi_{n}(q)^{z-1}}{\rho_{n}^{z-1}},\end{split}

(3.26)

where

C(q):=\frac{1}{q}\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{1+\frac{1}{\hat{\rho}_{n}}}{1+\frac{1}{\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)}}.

Lévy processes with jumps of rational transform are an extension of the hyper-exponential Lévy processes, which are themselves contained in the class of meromorphic Lévy processes. Their Lévy measure has a density of the type

\pi(x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x>0\right\}}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\sum_{i=1}^{m_{j}}\alpha_{ij}x^{i-1}e^{-\rho_{j}x}+\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x<0\right\}}\sum_{j=1}^{\hat{J}}\sum_{i=1}^{\hat{m}_{j}}\hat{\alpha}_{ij}x^{i-1}e^{\hat{\rho}_{j}x},

where
m_{j},\hat{m}_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
,
\rho_{j}>0,\hat{\rho}_{j}>0
, and
\rho_{i}\neq\rho_{j},\hat{\rho}_{i}\neq\hat{\rho}_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Then,
[
Kuz12
, Thm 1]
provides the Mellin transform of exponential functionals of Lévy processes with jumps of rational transform as

\begin{split}\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)&=A^{1-z}\Gamma(z)\frac{\mathcal{G}(z)}{\mathcal{G}(1)},\end{split}

where
\mathcal{G}(z)
is a ratio of Gamma functions depending on
\rho_{n},\hat{\rho}_{n}
and the solutions
\chi_{n}(q),\hat{\chi}_{n}(q)
to
\Psi(z)=q
, and
A
depends on the Brownian component
\sigma^{2}
, the mean
\mu
, the killing rate
q
, and the rate of the Poisson counting process, as the jumps in this case are of finite activity.

a\in(-\mathfrak{a}^{\Psi}_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{+}=0\right\}},1-\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi})
,

f_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)=\frac{(-1)^{n}\phi_{-}(0)}{2\pi i}\int_{a-i\infty}^{a+i\infty}x^{-z-n}\Gamma(z+n)\frac{W_{\phi_{-}}(1-z)}{W_{\phi_{+}}(z)}\mathrm{d}z,

(3.27)

where the integral is absolutely convergent for
x>0
. Consequently, if
\xi
is not a compound Poisson process with positive drift, i.e.,
\mathbf{d}_{+}>0,\ \mathbf{d}_{-}=0,
and
\overline{\Pi}(0)<\infty
, then
f_{\Psi}\in\mathtt{C}^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Finally, if
0<\mathtt{N}_{\Psi}<1
, then for any
0<\alpha<\mathtt{N}_{\Psi}
,
F_{\Psi}
is Hölder continuous of index
\alpha
.

Remark 3.22

(
Historical remarks
)
.

The smoothness of the law of
I_{\Psi}
has often appeared as a by-product of the series expansions for its law; see subsection
3.1.2
below. It was informally conjectured that the law is infinitely differentiable whenever
\overline{\Pi}(0)=\infty
or
\sigma^{2}>0
. Somewhat surprisingly, this turns out to be true in all cases except when
\xi
is a compound Poisson process with positive drift. In that situation,
\mathtt{N}_{\Psi}
is finite. For instance, if
\Psi(z)=z-q
with
q>0
(i.e.,
\xi_{t}=t
killed at
\mathbf{e}_{q}
), then

F_{\Psi}(x)=1-(1-x)^{q},

showing that smoothness can indeed fail unless
q\in\mathbb{N}
; see
[
PS18
, Rem. 2.5]
.

Remark 3.23

(
Further comments
)
.

From the proof of
[
PS18
, Thm 2.4(5)]
, which relies on a classical analytic fact from Mellin inversion, we know that

\limsup_{|b|\to\infty}\frac{\ln\left|\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|}{\ln|b|}\leq-\theta<0

implies that
f_{\Psi}
is analytic in the cone
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\left|\arg z\right|<\theta\}
. From Theorem
3.18
, it follows, for example, that if
\xi
is symmetric or creeps downwards, then
f_{\Psi}\in\mathtt{A}_{(0,\infty)}
. Analyticity in a cone is also guaranteed in cases where
\phi_{+}
satisfies certain conditions; since these conditions are not particularly explicit, we simply refer to
[
MS23
, Cor. 3.6]
for more information. The case of a subordinator is covered in Theorem
3.41
below.

Proof.

The only part not explicitly established in the literature is the claim on Hölder continuity. It follows from the identity
\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(z)=\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)/z,\ z\in\mathbb{C}_{(-1,0)}
; see
[
PS18
, (7.12)]
, which shows that
\left|\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|
, for
a\in(-1,0)
, decays at rate
1+\mathtt{N}_{\Psi}
. Fix
x
and consider
y=(1+d)x
with
d\in(-1/2,1/2)
. By Mellin inversion along
a+ib,\ a\in(-\alpha,0)
, we obtain

\begin{split}\frac{\left|F(x)-F((1+d)x)\right|}{\left|dx\right|^{\alpha}}&\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{|{x^{-a-ib}-(x(1+d))^{-a-ib}}|}{\left|dx\right|^{\alpha}}\,\left|\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|\,\mathrm{d}b\\
&=\frac{x^{-a}}{2\pi\left|dx\right|^{\alpha}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|(1+d)^{-a-ib}-1|\,\left|\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|\,\mathrm{d}b\\
&\leq\frac{1}{\pi\left|d\right|^{\alpha}\left|x\right|^{a+\alpha}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(|{(1+d)^{-ib}-1}|+\left|(1+d)^{a}-1\right|\right)\left|\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|\,\mathrm{d}b.\end{split}

For small
d
, we have
\left|(1+d)^{a}-1\right|\leq Cd
for some constant
C\in\mathbb{R}
, and

\left|(1+d)^{-ib}-1\right|=\left|e^{-ib\ln(1+d)}-1\right|\leq 2^{1-\alpha}\min\left\{|bd|^{\alpha},\,2^{\alpha}\right\}.

Substituting these estimates into the integral above and using that

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|b|^{\alpha}\left|\mathcal{M}_{F_{\Psi}}(a+ib)\right|\,\mathrm{d}b<\infty,

since
\alpha-(\mathtt{N}_{\Psi}+1)<-1
, yields the claim.
∎

3.2.3
Factorisations

The form of
\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}
, namely

\mathcal{M}_{I_{\Psi}}(z)=\phi_{-}(0)\frac{\Gamma(z)}{W_{\phi_{+}}(z)}W_{\phi_{-}}(1-z)

suggests a universal factorisation of
I_{\Psi}
once one recognizes
\Gamma(z)/W_{\phi_{+}}(z)
and
\phi_{-}(0)W_{\phi_{-}}(1-z)

as Mellin transforms of suitable random variables. In this section,
\times
stands for the product of independent random variables.

Theorem 3.25

(Thm. 2.22 in
[
PS18
]
; Thm. 2.5 in
[
AR23
]
)
.

Let
\Psi
be a Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
such that
\phi_{-}(0)>0
. Then

I_{\Psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi_{+}}\times X_{\phi_{-}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi_{+}}\times Y^{-1}_{\phi_{-}}\times e^{-\inf_{s\geq 0}\xi_{s}},

(3.31)

where
I_{\phi_{+}}
is the exponential functional of the ascending ladder height process (a subordinator), and

\mathbb{P}({X_{\phi_{-}}\in\mathrm{d}x})=\phi_{-}(0)\,x\,\mathbb{P}({Y^{-1}_{\phi_{-}}\in\mathrm{d}x}),\qquad W_{\phi_{-}}(z+1)=\mathbb{E}[{Y_{\phi_{-}}^{z}}],\ \mathtt{Re}(z)>0.

Furthermore,

I_{\Psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\bigotimes_{k=0}^{\infty}C_{\Psi}(k)\left(\mathcal{B}_{k}X_{\Psi}\times\mathcal{B}_{-k}Y_{\Psi}\right),

(3.32)

where

\begin{split}\mathbb{P}\left(X_{\Psi}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)&=\frac{1}{\phi_{+}(1)}\left(\overline{\mu}_{+}(-\ln x)\,\mathrm{d}x+\phi_{+}(0)\,\mathrm{d}x+\mathbf{d}_{+}\,\delta_{1}(\mathrm{d}x)\right)\quad\text{on }(0,1];\\
\mathbb{P}\left(Y_{\Psi}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)&=\phi_{-}(0)U_{-}(\mathrm{d}\ln x)\quad\text{on }(1,\infty),\end{split}

(3.33)

and
U_{-}
is the potential measure of the descending ladder height process associated with
\phi_{-}
.
In (
3.32
), we also have

C_{\Psi}(0):=e^{\gamma_{\phi_{+}}+\gamma_{\phi_{-}}-\gamma},\qquad C_{\Psi}(k):=\exp\!\left(\frac{1}{k}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{+}(k)}{\phi_{+}(k)}-\frac{\phi^{\prime}_{-}(k)}{\phi_{-}(k)}\right),\quad k\geq 1,

(3.34)

where
\gamma_{\phi_{\pm}}
are the constants in the product representations (
2.9
) of
W_{\phi_{\pm}}
, and
\gamma
is the Euler–Mascheroni constant. Also,
\mathcal{B}_{x},\,x\in\mathbb{R}
, denotes the size-biased operator: if
\mathbb{E}\left[X^{x}\right]<\infty
and
\mathbb{P}\left(X>0\right)=1
, then
\mathbb{E}\left[f(\mathcal{B}_{x}X)\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[X^{x}f(X)\right]/\mathbb{E}\left[X^{x}\right]
for any bounded continuous function
f
.

Remark 3.26

(
Historical remarks
)
.

The first relatively general factorisation result in this area states that, for any Bernstein function
\phi
,

\mathbf{e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi}\times Y_{\phi},

(3.35)

where
\mathbf{e}\sim\mathrm{Exp}(1)
and
Y_{\phi}
(which defines
W_{\phi}
) is for this purpose called the residual random variable. This result was first established in
[
BY01
,
BY02b
]
and extended to killed subordinators in
[
BY05
, Thm. 2]
. It has since served as a crucial starting point in the development of the spectral theory of non-self-adjoint generalized Laguerre semigroups; see
[
PS21
]
, and has also played a role in particular examples, such as those studied in
[
LPS19
,
PS17
,
PSZ19
]
.

A factorisation of the type (
3.31
) in a more general setting was obtained in
[
PPS12
,
PS12
,
PS13
]
, where
X_{\phi_{-}}
was identified with
I_{\psi}
, with
\psi(z)=-z\phi_{-}(z)
being the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a spectrally negative Lévy process. This requires certain assumptions on
\xi
, for instance the Lévy measure
\Pi
to possess a non-decreasing density on
(-\infty,0)
.

The factors in (
3.32
) can be further specified when
\xi
is a meromorphic Lévy process; see Subsection
3.1.3
for the definition. In this case, we have the following result:

Theorem 3.27

(Thm. 3.1 in
[
HK14
]
)
.

Let
\xi
be a meromorphic Lévy process with Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent
\Psi
such that
q=-\Psi(0)>0
. Let
\chi=(\chi_{n}(q))_{n\geq 1}
,
\rho=(\rho_{n})_{n\geq 1}
,
\widetilde{\chi}=(1+\hat{\chi}_{n}(q))_{n\geq 1}
, and
\widetilde{\rho}=(1+\hat{\rho}_{n-1})_{n\geq 1}
. Then

I_{\Psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}C\,X(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{\chi})\times\frac{1}{X(\rho,\chi)},

(3.36)

where
C=C(q)
is defined below (
3.26
), and for any two interlacing strictly increasing sequences
(\alpha,\beta)
, i.e.,
0<\alpha_{1}<\beta_{1}<\alpha_{2}<\beta_{2}<\dots
, such as
(\widetilde{\rho},\widetilde{\chi})
and
(\rho,\chi)
in this case,

X(\alpha,\beta)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\bigotimes_{n\geq 1}\frac{\beta_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}\,\mathrm{Beta}(\alpha_{n},\beta_{n}-\alpha_{n}),

where
\mathrm{Beta}(\cdot,\cdot)
denotes a Beta random variable with the respective parameters.

Remark 3.28

(
Historical remark
)
.

Special cases of Beta products of the type in (
3.36
) appear in the literature. For instance,
[
LS19
]
considers functionals of stable processes of the form

A=\int_{0}^{T}(\xi_{s})^{q}\,\mathrm{d}s,

where
\xi
is an
\alpha
-stable Lévy process,
T
is its first passage time below zero, and

. It turns out that

is in fact the exponential functional of a specific meromorphic Lévy process, and the moments of

are given by a particular case of

, so that the factorisation (

) holds. Further examples of infinite Beta products can be found in
[
Sim23
, Section 3]
.

We conclude this part with a useful observation. Recall that a Bernstein function
\phi
is called a
special Bernstein function
if there exists another Bernstein function
\phi_{S}
satisfying
\phi(z)\phi_{S}(z)=z,\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0
; see
[
SSV12
, Chapter 11]
for more details. From (

), it follows that

\Gamma(z)=W_{\phi}(z)W_{\phi_{S}}(z),

(3.37)

so that
Y_{\phi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi_{S}}
, and equivalently (
3.37
) becomes

\mathbf{e}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi}\times I_{\phi_{S}}.

(3.38)

Among these formulations, the most useful is (
3.37
); see Remark
3.61
. It also provides a way to study
\Gamma/W_{\phi}
through
W_{\phi_{S}}
, and conversely.

In this part, we show examples where the law of

Theorem 3.29

(

]

[

Let

If

Remark 3.30

(
Further comments
)
.

For general Brownian motion, one can use (
3.23
) to recognise

\frac{\Gamma(z)\Gamma(1-b_{-})}{\Gamma(z-b_{-})}\quad\text{as the Mellin transform of }\mathrm{Beta}(1,-b_{-}),

b_{+}\frac{\sigma^{2-2z}}{2^{1-z}}\frac{\Gamma(1-z+b_{+})}{\Gamma(1+b_{+})}=\left(\tfrac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\right)^{z-1}\frac{\Gamma(1-z+b_{+})}{\Gamma(b_{+})},

\bigl(\tfrac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Gamma_{b_{+},1}\bigr)^{-1}

Therefore,

I_{\Psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\mathrm{Beta}(1,-b_{-})\times\left(\tfrac{2}{\sigma^{2}}\Gamma_{b_{+},1}\right)^{-1}.

We proceed with a general result concerning three classes of Lévy processes that appear in the literature, with the exception of the full generality of (

Theorem 3.31

(Cor. 2.1 in
[

]

Let

f_{\Psi}(x)=\frac{x^{\gamma-1}}{\Gamma(-\gamma)}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-{y}/{x}}y^{-\gamma}f_{\phi_{+}}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}x^{\gamma-1-n}\frac{\Gamma(n-\gamma+1)}{\Gamma(-\gamma)W_{\phi_{+}}(n-\gamma+1)},

where

(that is,

Next, let

Remark 3.33

(

Apart from

Let

3.2.5
Large asymptotics of the density and tail

The behaviour of the tail probability
\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x):=\mathbb{P}\left(I_{\Psi}>x\right)
and the density
f_{\Psi}(x)
as
x\to\infty
is one of the most extensively studied aspects of exponential functionals of Lévy processes. In what follows, we first present results in a general framework. Then, instead of offering only historical remarks, we formulate several partial results as separate statements and provide additional comments on the main methodologies used in this area.

Throughout this section we use the standard notation
f\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}g
to mean that
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/g(x)=1
. The symbols
\mathrm{o}(\cdot)
and
\mathrm{O}(\cdot)
denote the classical asymptotic relations, for instance
f(x)=\mathrm{o}(x)
means
\lim_{x\to\infty}f(x)/x=0
.

The first result applies to all exponential functionals. Recall that
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}=\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}\leq 0
, see (
3.9
).

Theorem 3.38

(Thm. 2.11 in
[
PS18
]
)
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
with
\phi_{-}(0)>0
. Then

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\ln\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)}{\ln x}=\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi},

(3.44)

where
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}=\mathfrak{a}_{-}^{\Psi}=-\infty
if and only if
\Psi(z)=-\phi_{-}(z)
, i.e., when the associated Lévy process
\xi
is a potentially killed subordinator.

Remark 3.39

(
Historical remarks
)
.

As noted in
[
AR23
]
, it follows from
[
GG96
, Thm. 4.1]
that if
\xi
is not a subordinator, then

\lim_{x\to\infty}\ln\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)/\ln x>-\infty
.
This observation arises from the fact that
I_{\Psi}
is a perpetuity, so general results for affine equations apply.
However, the specific structure of exponential functionals of Lévy processes enables sharper results, not only providing precise logarithmic asymptotics, but also describing the asymptotic behaviour on the real scale.

Remark 3.40

(
Further comments
)
.

According to (
3.10
),
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}=0
if and only if
\mathfrak{a}^{\Psi}_{-}=0
, which is equivalent to
\xi_{1}
not admitting any negative exponential moments.
Moreover, since from (
2.3
)

\phi_{-}(z)=\phi_{-}(0)+\mathbf{d}_{-}z+\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(1-e^{-zy}\right)\mu_{-}(\mathrm{d}y),

it follows that if
\mathfrak{a}^{\Psi}_{-}=\mathfrak{a}_{-}=-\infty
, then
\lim_{x\to-\infty}\phi_{-}(x)=-\infty
. Hence,
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}^{\Psi}_{-}=\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}=\mathfrak{u}_{-}>-\infty
, unless
\phi_{-}(z)\equiv\phi_{-}(0)
. Thus,

\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}=-\infty\iff\Psi(z)=-\phi_{+}(-z)\phi_{-}(0)\iff\xi\text{ is a potentially killed subordinator}.

In the case
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}\in(-\infty,0)
, we obtain a quantifiable rate of decay at the logarithmic scale.

The case
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{-}^{\Psi}=-\infty
in Theorem
3.38
, corresponding to
\xi
being a subordinator, requires separate treatment. We present below the most general result available to date.

Theorem 3.41

(Thm. 3.1 in
[
MS23
]
; Thm. 10.1 in
[
Haa25
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function with
\mathbf{d}=0
, and let
\Psi(z)=-\phi(-z)
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a subordinator. Suppose that

\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{x\phi^{\prime}(x)}{\phi(x)}<1,\quad\text{or equivalently}\quad\liminf_{x\to 0}\frac{\int_{0}^{2x}\bar{\mu}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y}{\int_{0}^{x}\bar{\mu}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y}>1.

(3.45)

Then
\phi_{*}(x):=x/\phi(x)
is increasing, with inverse

\varphi_{*}:=\phi_{*}^{-1}:\left((\phi^{\prime}(0^{+}))^{-1}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\phi(0)=0\right\}},\infty\right)\to\left(0,\infty\right).

For every
n\geq 1
, the following asymptotics hold:

f_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{C_{\phi}\,\varphi_{*}^{n}(x)\sqrt{\varphi_{*}^{\prime}(x)}}{x^{n}}\exp\left\{-\int_{\phi_{*}(1)}^{x}\frac{\varphi_{*}(y)}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\right\},

(3.46)

where

C_{\phi}:=\frac{(-1)^{n}e^{-T_{\phi_{*}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\,\phi_{*}(1)}},\qquad T_{\phi_{*}}:=\int_{0}^{\infty}(u-\lceil u\rceil)(1-u+\lceil u\rceil)\left[\frac{1}{u^{2}}-\left(\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)}{\phi(u)}\right)^{2}+\frac{\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)}{\phi(u)}\right]\mathrm{d}u\in\mathbb{R},

with
\lceil\cdot\rceil
denoting the ceiling function. Moreover,

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{e^{-T_{\phi_{*}}}}{\sqrt{2\pi\,\phi_{*}(1)}}\frac{x\,\sqrt{\varphi_{*}^{\prime}(x)}}{\varphi_{*}(x)}\exp\left\{-\int_{\phi_{*}(1)}^{x}\frac{\varphi_{*}(y)}{y}\,\mathrm{d}y\right\}.

(3.47)

As a consequence, the even derivatives of
f_{\Psi}
are both ultimately monotone decreasing and ultimately convex, while the odd derivatives are both ultimately monotone increasing and ultimately concave. If in addition

\limsup_{x\to 0}\bar{\mu}(2x)/\bar{\mu}(x)<1
, then there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
f_{\Psi}
is analytic in the cone

\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\mathtt{Re}(z)>0,\,|\arg z|<\varepsilon\}
.

Remark 3.42

(
Historical remarks
)
.

At the logarithmic scale and under the same conditions, the asymptotic relation (
3.47
) has already appeared in
[
HR12
, Prop. 3.1]
. We also note that
[
Haa25
, Thm. 10.1]
, which covers the case
n=0
, does not provide an explicit expression for
C_{\phi}
, but does establish a convergence rate of order
\mathrm{O}(1/\varphi_{*}(x))
. The method in
[
Haa25
]
differs from that in
[
MS23
]
, yet both rely on the same condition (
3.45
), indicating that this assumption is likely necessary for the validity of (
3.46
).

Remark 3.43

(
Further comments
)
.

If
\mathbf{d}>0
, then from (
2.3
) and (
2.5
) one has the limit
\lim_{x\to\infty}(x\phi^{\prime}(x))/\phi(x)=1
, so condition (
3.45
) does not hold. In this case, since
\xi_{t}\geq\mathbf{d}t
, we have
I_{\Psi}\leq\mathbf{d}^{-1}
, meaning that
I_{\Psi}
has finite support. Thus, a transition in the asymptotic behaviour occurs when
\limsup_{x\to\infty}x\phi^{\prime}(x)/\phi(x)=1
. If
\varphi_{*}(x)
denotes the inverse of
x/(\phi(x)-q-\mathbf{d}x)
, then
[
HR12
, Prop. 3.12]
shows that

\ln\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\sim\mathbf{d}\int_{a}^{x}\varphi_{*}\!\left(\tfrac{u}{1-\mathbf{d}u}\right)\mathrm{d}u,\qquad\text{as $x\uparrow\mathbf{d}^{-1}$, with $a<\mathbf{d}^{-1}$ fixed.}

It is useful to state separately the case of a non-decreasing compound Poisson process, since the result shows that the asymptotics coincide with that of the tail of an exponential distribution.

Corollary 3.44

(Cor. 3.4 in
[
MS23
]
)
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function with
\mathbf{d}=0
, and let
\Psi(z)=-\phi(-z)
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a non-decreasing compound Poisson process. Let
\mu
denote the Lévy measure of
\phi
. If
\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\mathrm{d}v)/v=\infty
, then

f_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}Ce^{-\phi(\infty)x}.

(3.48)

If instead
\int_{0}^{1}\mu(\mathrm{d}v)/v<\infty
, then

f_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}Ce^{-\phi(\infty)(x+\mathrm{o}\left(x\right))}.

(3.49)

In both cases,
C
is an explicit constant depending on
\phi(\infty)
,
\phi(1)
,
\varphi_{*}(1)
, and
T_{\phi_{*}}
; see
[
MS23
, Cor. 3.4]
for details.

The next set of relatively general results stems from the three-term factorisation in (
3.31
),

I_{\Psi}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}I_{\phi_{+}}\times Y^{-1}_{\phi_{-}}\times e^{-\inf_{s\geq 0}\xi_{s}},

which supports the conjecture, first proposed in
[
Riv12
]
, that

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}c\,\mathbb{P}\left(e^{-\inf_{s\geq 0}\xi_{s}}>x\right),

(3.50)

for some constant
c>0
. Note that
[
AR23
,
Riv12
]
consider
\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{\xi_{s}}\mathrm{d}s
, so the sign must be changed when referring to these works in our manuscript. The conjecture is further reinforced by the fact that
I_{\phi_{+}}
has finite moments of all orders, and the moments of
Y^{-1}_{\phi_{-}}
are not of smaller order than those of
e^{-\inf_{s\geq 0}\xi_{s}}
.

To state these results, we briefly recall the class of convolution equivalent Lévy processes for which (
3.50
) is known to hold. We say that
-\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{\gamma}
for some
\gamma\geq 0
, or equivalently that
-\xi
is convolution equivalent with index
\gamma
, if for all
y\in\mathbb{R}
,

\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}>x-y\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}>x\right)}=e^{\gamma y},\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}>x-y\right)\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}\in\mathrm{d}y\right)}{\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}>x\right)}=2\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{\gamma y}\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}\in\mathrm{d}y\right).

A result of
[
Pak04
]
shows that these relations are equivalent to the same statements with
\mathbb{P}\left(-\xi_{1}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)
replaced by the truncated Lévy measure
\Pi_{-}(\mathrm{d}x)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x>1\right\}}:=\Pi(-\mathrm{d}x)\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{x>1\right\}}
.

Theorem 3.45

(Thm. 2.9 in
[
AR23
]
)
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
such that
\phi_{-}(0)>0
. For

, set
\overline{\Pi}_{-}(x):=\Pi((-\infty,-x))

(i)

If the law of
\xi

such that
\Psi(\theta)=0

\lim_{x\to\infty}x^{-\theta}\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)=C_{\theta}\in(0,\infty).

(3.51)

(ii)

Assume
\Pi((-\infty,0))>0
(i.e.,
\xi
has negative jumps) and
-\xi_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{0}

(a)

If
\Psi(0)<0

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\overline{\Pi}_{-}(\ln x).

(3.52)

(b)

If
\Psi(0)=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\right]\in(0,\infty)

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}\left[\xi_{1}\right]}\int_{\ln x}^{\infty}\overline{\Pi}_{-}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.53)

(iii)

If
-\xi\in\mathcal{S}_{-\alpha}

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}C_{\alpha}\int_{\ln x}^{\infty}\overline{\Pi}_{-}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(3.54)

Remark 3.47

(
Further comments
)

Theorem 2.7 in

Theorem 3.48

(Thm. 2.11(2) in
[
PS18
]
)
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
such that
\phi_{-}(0)>0
. Assume further that
\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}\in(-\infty,0)

is finite, i.e.

\overline{F}_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\frac{\phi_{-}(0)\,\Gamma(-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})\,W_{\phi_{-}}(1+\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})}{\phi^{\prime}_{-}(\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}+)\,W_{\phi_{+}}(1-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})}x^{\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}}.

(3.55)

If, in addition,

f_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}(-1)^{n}\frac{\phi_{-}(0)\,\Gamma(n+1-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})\,W_{\phi_{-}}(1+\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})}{\phi^{\prime}_{-}(\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}+)\,W_{\phi_{+}}(1-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})}x^{\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}-n-1}.

(3.56)

Remark 3.50

Theorem 3.51

(Thm. 2.14 in

Let

\xi

Assume that the measure

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t}\mathbb{P}\left(\xi_{t}\in\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\mathbb{P}(T_{(-\infty,0)}>0)=0

\sup_{a\leq\delta<\lambda\leq\infty}\left|Cx^{-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}}\mathbb{P}\left(I_{\Psi}\in\left(x\delta,x\lambda\right)\right)-\int_{\delta}^{\lambda}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{(-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-})y^{1-\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{-}}}\right|=\mathrm{o}\left((\log x)^{-m+1}\right),

Theorem 3.52
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process

Theorem 3.53

Theorem 3.55

(Thm. 2.4(4) and Cor. 2.9 in

Let

be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
such that

\mathtt{N}^{+}:=\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{+}\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{+}\in\mathbb{N}\right\}}+\lceil\mathfrak{a}^{\Psi}_{+}+1\rceil\mathbbm{1}_{\left\{\mathfrak{u}^{\Psi}_{+}\notin\mathbb{N}\right\}}.

\begin{split}F_{\Psi}^{(n)}(x)&=-\Psi(0)\sum_{k=\max\{1,n\}}^{M}\frac{\prod_{j=1}^{k-1}\Psi(j)}{(k-n)!}x^{k-n}\\

Remark 3.57

f_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{=}}\mathrm{o}(x^{n})
for every

. To prove that

is moment-indeterminate by applying the Krein or Lin condition, one would need information on the decay of

as

f_{\Psi}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 0}}{{=}}\mathrm{o}\left(\exp\left(-x^{-\frac{1}{4}+\epsilon}\right)\right),\qquad\epsilon>0.

Establishing such a bound may require a saddle point method, possibly more intricate than the one developed in
[
MS23
]
.

∎

(Thm. 5.3 in

4.1
Analytic properties of Bernstein-gamma functions

We first start with the proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Proof of Theorem
2.7
.

Only the very last claim is not contained in the literature. From (
2.1
) we obtain

D_{a+1}=\liminf_{|b|\to\infty}\frac{\ln\left|W_{\phi}(a+ib)\right|+\ln\left|\phi(a+ib)\right|}{|b|}=D_{a},

since, by
[
PS18
, Prop. 3.1]
, we have
\ln\left|\phi(z)\right|=\mathrm{O}(\left|\ln z\right|)
as
|z|\to\infty
. If
a>1
, the same argument applies to
D_{a-1}
. Hence, it suffices to consider
D_{a^{\prime}}
with
|a-a^{\prime}|<1
and
a^{\prime}>0
. In this case,

\liminf_{|b|\to\infty}\frac{\ln\left|W_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib)\right|}{|b|}=\liminf_{|b|\to\infty}\left(\frac{\ln\left|W_{\phi}(a+ib)\right|}{|b|}+\frac{\ln\left|\frac{W_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib)}{W_{\phi}(a+ib)}\right|}{|b|}\right).

Without loss of generality, assume
b>0
. From (
2.16
) and
\ln\left|\phi(z)\right|=\mathrm{O}(\left|\ln z\right|)
, as
|z|\to\infty
, it would follow that
D_{a}=D_{a^{\prime}}
if

0=\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{\left|\mathtt{Re}\left(L_{\phi}(a+ib-1)-L_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib-1)\right)\right|}{b}.

Choosing the contours
1\to a\to a+ib
and
1\to a^{\prime}\to a^{\prime}+ib
, we obtain

\begin{split}L_{\phi}(a+ib-1)-L_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib-1)&=\int_{1}^{a}\ln\left|\phi(u)\right|\,\mathrm{d}u-\int_{1}^{a^{\prime}}\ln\left|\phi(u)\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\\
&\quad\,+i\left(\int_{0}^{b}\ln\left|\phi(a+iu)\right|\,\mathrm{d}u-\int_{0}^{b}\ln\left|\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)\right|\,\mathrm{d}u\right)\\
&\quad\,+\int_{0}^{b}\left(\arg_{0}\phi(a+iu)-\arg_{0}\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)\right)\,\mathrm{d}u.\end{split}

Thus it suffices to prove that

0=\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{b}\arg_{0}\left(\frac{\phi(a+iu)}{\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}u\right|}{b}=\lim_{b\to\infty}\frac{\left|\int_{0}^{b}\arg_{0}\left(1+\frac{\phi(a+iu)-\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)}{\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)}\right)\,\mathrm{d}u\right|}{b},

where we used

\arg_{0}\phi(a+iu)-\arg_{0}\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)=\arg_{0}(\frac{\phi(a+iu)}{\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)})
, since

\arg_{0}\phi(a+iu),\arg_{0}\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)\in(-\tfrac{\pi}{2},\tfrac{\pi}{2})
as
\phi(\mathbb{C}_{(0,\infty)})\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{(0,\infty)}
,
\mathtt{Re}(\phi(a+ib))\geq\phi(a)>0
, and
\mathtt{Re}(\phi(a^{\prime}+ib))\geq\phi(a^{\prime})>0
; see
[
PS18
, Prop. 3.1 (9 and 10)]
.

It remains to show that

\lim_{u\to\infty}\frac{\phi(a+iu)-\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)}{\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)}=0.

If
\phi(\infty)<\infty
, this follows directly from
[
PS18
, Prop. 3.1(5)]
. Otherwise,

\left|\phi(a+iu)-\phi(a^{\prime}+iu)\right|=\left|\int_{a}^{a^{\prime}}\phi^{\prime}(v+iu)\,\mathrm{d}v\right|\leq\left|a-a^{\prime}\right|\,\phi^{\prime}\left(\min\left\{a,a^{\prime}\right\}\right),

which follows from
[
MS23
, Prop. A.1(5)]
. This completes the proof.
∎

The next proposition provides an elementary lower bound for
\left|W_{\phi}(z)\right|
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function. Then, for any
a>0
and
z=a+ib
,

\left|W_{\phi}(z)\right|\;\geq\;\frac{W_{\phi}(a)}{\Gamma(a)}\left|\Gamma(z)\right|.

(4.1)

Proof.

The claim follows directly from the factorisation (
3.35
), which shows that
\Gamma(z)/W_{\phi}(z)
is the Mellin transform of
I_{\phi}
, the exponential functional of a subordinator.
∎

The next result does not appear in the literature, but it provides uniform estimates along compact subsets of the real part of Bernstein-gamma functions, which will be useful in subsequent arguments. Throughout, we use the notation

f\asymp g\quad\text{to mean that}\quad 0<\liminf_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}\leq\limsup_{x\to\infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function with associated Bernstein-gamma function
W_{\phi}
, and let
(c,d)\subset(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi},\infty)
. Then, as
|b|\to\infty
,

\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\left|\frac{W_{\phi}(a+ib)}{W_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib)}\right|\;\asymp\;\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\exp\!\left(\int_{1+a}^{1+a^{\prime}}\ln\bigl|\phi(u+ib)\bigr|\,\mathrm{d}u\right).

(4.2)

Moreover,

\sup_{u\in(c,d)}\ln\bigl|\phi(u+ib)\bigr|=\begin{cases}\mathrm{O}\left(\ln|b|\right),&\text{if }\mathbf{d}>0,\\[2.15277pt]
\mathrm{o}\left(\ln|b|\right),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Finally, the same uniform estimate extends as follows:

1.

if
\liminf_{|b|\to\infty}\phi(ib)>0
, then (
4.2
) holds for any
[c,d)\subset[0,\infty)
;

2.

if, in addition,
\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi}<0
,
\left|\phi(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi})\right|<\infty
, and
\liminf_{|b|\to\infty}\phi(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi}+ib)>0
, then (
4.2
) holds for any
[c,d)\subset[\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi},\infty)
.

Proof.

Let
[c,d)\subset[1,\infty)
and
|b|>0
. We use (
2.13
) together with the uniform bound on
E_{\phi}
from Theorem
2.4
, and choose the contours
1\to 1+a+ib
and
1\to 1+a+ib\to 1+a^{\prime}+ib
for

and
L_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib)

\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\left|\frac{W_{\phi}(a+ib)}{W_{\phi}(a^{\prime}+ib)}\right|\asymp\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\left|\frac{\phi(a^{\prime}+ib)}{\phi(a+ib)}\sqrt{\frac{\phi(a^{\prime}+1+ib)}{\phi(a+1+ib)}}\right|\exp\left(\int_{1+a}^{1+a^{\prime}}\ln|\phi(u+ib)|\,\mathrm{d}u\right).

For any
(c,d)\subseteq(\bar{\mathfrak{a}}_{\phi},\infty)

\begin{split}\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\left|\frac{\phi(a^{\prime}+ib)}{\phi(a+ib)}\right|&=\sup_{a,a^{\prime}\in(c,d)}\left|1+\frac{\phi(a^{\prime}+ib)-\phi(a+ib)}{\phi(a+ib)}\right|\\

(4.3)

4.2
Transformation properties of Bernstein-gamma functions

Here we present a couple of transformation properties of Bernstein-gamma functions.

First, recall the definition of
\mathfrak{a}_{\phi},\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}
, see (
2.6
). For
\beta>\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}
we have

\phi_{\beta}(z):=\phi(z+\beta)=\phi(\beta)+\mathbf{d}z+\int_{0}^{\infty}(1-e^{-zy})e^{-\beta y}\mu(\mathrm{d}y),\qquad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0.

(4.4)

The identity also holds for
\beta=\mathfrak{a}_{\phi}
, provided that
\left|\phi(\mathfrak{a}_{\phi})\right|<\infty
. Moreover, for all
\beta>\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}
(and also for
\beta=\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}
when
\phi(\mathfrak{u}_{\phi})=0
), the function
\phi_{\beta}
is a Bernstein function. We thus obtain the following elementary fact.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function. For
\beta>\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}
, we have

W_{\phi_{\beta}}(z)\;=\;\frac{W_{\phi}(z+\beta)}{W_{\phi}(1+\beta)},\qquad\mathtt{Re}(z)>0,

(4.5)

and this defines the Bernstein-gamma function associated with
\phi_{\beta}
. The same relation also holds for
\beta=\mathfrak{u}_{\phi}
, provided that
\phi(\mathfrak{u}_{\phi})=0
.

Next, we consider the transformation under the so-called
T_{\beta}
–transform of
\phi
, whose most general form is discussed in
[
CKP21
]
and whose usefulness is illustrated in
[
PS13
]
. Let
\phi
be a Bernstein function and
\beta>0
. From (
2.3
) we obtain

\begin{split}(T_{\beta}\phi)(z)&:=\frac{z}{z+\beta}\phi_{\beta}(z)=\frac{z}{z+\beta}\phi(0)+\mathbf{d}z+z\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}e^{-\beta y}\bar{\mu}(y)\,\mathrm{d}y\\
&\hskip 3.00003pt=\mathbf{d}z+z\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-zy}e^{-\beta y}\bigl(\bar{\mu}(y)+\phi(0)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y,\qquad\mathtt{Re}(z)\geq 0,\end{split}

which is a Bernstein function, since

\bar{\mu}_{\beta}(y):=e^{-\beta y}\bigl(\bar{\mu}(y)+\phi(0)\bigr)

is decreasing and integrable, and thus represents the tail of a measure
\mu_{\beta}
defining a Bernstein function corresponding to an unkilled subordinator. By uniqueness of the Bernstein-gamma function, we obtain:

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
\phi
be a Bernstein-gamma function. For
\beta>0
, we have

W_{T_{\beta}\phi}(z)\;=\;\Gamma(1+\beta)\frac{\Gamma(z)}{\Gamma(z+\beta)}W_{\phi}(z),\qquad\mathtt{Re}(z)>0,

(4.6)

and this defines the Bernstein-gamma function associated with
T_{\beta}\phi
.

Finally, Proposition
4.6
is a manifestation of the more general principle that if

\phi,\phi_{1},\phi_{2}
are Bernstein functions with
\phi=\phi_{1}\phi_{2}
, then

W_{\phi}(z)\;=\;W_{\phi_{1}}(z)\,W_{\phi_{2}}(z),\qquad\mathtt{Re}(z)>0.

(4.7)

We refer to
[
PS21
, Chapter 4.2]

for a discussion of such triples of Bernstein functions.

4.3
Bernstein-gamma functions of Wiener-Hopf factors

We recall that when
\Psi
is the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process, the Wiener–Hopf factorisation (
3.3
) holds, namely

\Psi(z)=-\phi_{+}(-z)\phi_{-}(z).

In this case, there is an alternative expression for the leading term
A_{\phi}
in the Stirling asymptotics of
\left|W_{\phi}(z)\right|
; see (
2.15
).

Proposition 4.7

(Lem. A.2 in
[
PS18
]
)
.

Let
\Psi
be the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent of a potentially killed Lévy process
\xi
with Wiener–Hopf factors
\phi_{\pm}
. Then, for any
z=a+ib\in\mathbb{C}_{(0,\infty)}
,

A_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(bx)}{x}\,e^{-ax}\,\mathbb{P}\left(\pm\xi_{t}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}=\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(bx)}{x}\,e^{-ax}\,H_{\pm}(\mathrm{d}x),

(4.8)

where
H_{\pm}(\mathrm{d}x):=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\pm\xi_{t}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}
are the so-called harmonic measures of
\xi
. Finally, if we vary the killing rate
-\Psi(0)=q\geq 0
then
A_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)
are non-increasing as functions of
q
.

Remark 4.8

(
Historical remarks
)
.

In
[
PS18
, Lem. A.2]
, the expression in (
4.8
) is written with
e^{\Psi(0)t}\mathbb{P}\left(\pm\xi^{\sharp}_{t}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)
for the identical
\mathbb{P}\left(\pm\xi_{t}\in\mathrm{d}x\right)
, where
\xi^{\sharp}
is the Lévy process pertaining to the Lévy ​​-Khintchine exponent
\Psi^{\sharp}(z)=\Psi(z)-\Psi(0)
. The final claim of the proposition is a direct consequence of this observation.

Remark 4.9

(
Further comments
)
.

The monotonicity in
a
is clear from (
4.8
). Changing variables, we obtain

A_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)=b\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(x)}{x}\,e^{-\tfrac{a}{b}x}\,H_{\pm}\!\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{b}\right),

and therefore, from equation (
2.20
) of Proposition
2.10
, we derive, as
b\to\infty
,

\ln\left|W_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)\right|\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\;-A_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)=-b\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(x)}{x}\,e^{-\tfrac{a}{b}x}\,H_{\pm}\!\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{b}\right),

and from (
2.19
),

\frac{1}{b}\int_{0}^{b}\arg_{0}\phi(a+iu)\,\mathrm{d}u\;=\;\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(x)}{x}\,e^{-\tfrac{a}{b}x}\,H_{\pm}\!\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{b}\right)\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\infty}}{{\sim}}\;\int_{[0,\infty)}\frac{1-\cos(x)}{x}\,H_{\pm}\!\left(\tfrac{\mathrm{d}x}{b}\right).

That is, the constant of the linear decay of
\ln\left|W_{\phi_{\pm}}(a+ib)\right|
depends on the harmonic measures at
0
.

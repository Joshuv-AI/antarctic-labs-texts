Inspiral to Ring-down, an Almost Exact One-Body Approach
Ding-fang Zeng

Abstract
XOB provides an almost eXact One-Body approach for the conservative part of binary merger dynamics of general relativity, which applies to the full three stages of the merger process, and allows the feature of gravitational waves be related to the inner-structure of merging bodies directly. We clarify the sense of exactness of this approach in this work and calculate the gravitational waveform of black hole binary merger process subsequently. Our calculation shows that, the waveform exhibits damping tail only when the BHs possess regular internals and experience rotational symmetry enhancement as the merger progresses on. Basing on this point, we calculate lower and upper boundings for the real part of the lowest tone quasi-normal frequency of the merger product and get results consistent with observations.

PN expansion is a non-relativistic approximation for the binary problem in GR. EOB provides a re-summation of this expansion. It writes the re-summed series into an effective Hamiltonian which follows from the dynamics of a test particle in a Schwarzschild-like background geometry. However, this re-summation captures not the general relativistic essence of the process. The typical manifestation is, the test particle executing the relative motion of the two BHs in this method falls into the horizon of the background in finite Schwarzschild time, which contradicts the time definition of GR explicitly. This means that EOB applies not to the merger and ring-down phase of the binary process, during which physics related with the internal structure of BHs affect the GWform. According to this approach, the GWform of binary merger processes has Quasi-Normal-Mode (QNM) feature only when the merging bodies experience internal mass-distribution’s variation and rotational symmetry’s enhancing during the process. Conclusion
XOB ultilizes a rotating patch-worked geometry as the driving force for the orbital motion of the two bodies involving in the binary merger process, so that each of them moves like a test particle in a Schwarzschild background. The angular frequency of the rotating force-geometry is determined by the flux balance condition at infinity when the dissipation caused by gravitational wave radiation is considered. This approach applies to the full three stages of the binary merger processes and tells that the late-time quasi-normal mode type waveform is possible only when the merging black holes experience banana shape deformation so that the system obtains enhanced rotational symmetry. We clarified here that the exactness of this method lies on its full general relativistic description of the merger process, instead of the exact reproduction of the effective hamiltonian of post-newtonian approximation. This is because XOB replaces a locally rotating geometry with a globally rotating one, thus introduces in-exactness. However, since the separation between the two merging bodies is much smaller than the dissipation time scale of gravitation radiation, the errors of this replacement on the flux balance condition is negligible. It will not change the asymptotic behaviour of the system abruptly. For example, it will not bring quasi-normal mode type feature to the late-time waveform for binary systems consisting of black holes with singular central and successfully implemented event horizon.

XOB can be easily applied to the orbit motion of binary bodies with high eccentricity and yields results consistent with the post-newtonian approximation. The advantage of this method over numerical relativity is not the high precision for the gravitation waveforms, but the tiny computational working-load and the ability to link the feature of the gravitational waveforms with the internal structure of BHs involved in the merger process. For example, it tells us that only when the black holes carry extended internal mass distribution and experience banana-shape deformation, does the gravitational waveform following from their binary merger process exhibit quasi-normal mode feature during the late time evolution. The origin of this feature is concealed in the apparent horizon boundary condition of numerical relativity.
XOB not only reveals it clearly and definitely, but also yields quantitative predictions for the lower and upper bound for the lowest tone quasi-normal frequency of the system, which fit with the observational data very well. In the current work, we considered the merger of neutral spherically symmetric black holes only. However there is no fundamental barrier preventing the application of this method to the spinning black holes, after some necessary adaptation. This may be an interesting direction for future works.

\displaystyle\bar{\rho}(x)\ \simeq\ \frac{\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\ \rho(r)\ \hbox{\large$\displaystyle\normalsize\frac{e^{-d/\lambda}}{r}$}\ r^{2}dr$}}{\hbox{\large$\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\ \frac{e^{-d/\lambda}}{r}\ \,r^{2}dr$}}\ ,

(52)

\rho(r)

being simply weighted proportionally to
{e^{-d/\lambda}}/{r}
, exponentially decreasing with the depth
d=R-r
below surface.

\bar{\rho}(x)
involves mainly the external shells of the sphere, within a depth
\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raise 2.64017pt\hbox{$<$}\hss}{\lower 2.64017pt\hbox{$\sim$}}}2\lambda
below surface,
ultimately decreasing, for small
\lambda
’s, down to the relevant

external density
\rho_{e}
,

\frac{\Phi(x)}{\phi(x)}\,=\,\frac{\bar{\rho}(x)}{\rho_{0}}\ \to\ \frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\,.

(53)

This one may be taken here as the crust density, close to
\rho_{0}/2\simeq 2.755
g/cm
3
, for small
\lambda
’s of a few tens down to
\approx 20
km, below which the experiment loses its sensitivity given the mean satellite altitude of
\simeq
710 km. (We can thus ignore the oceanic contribution to
\rho(r)
, that would lead to a lower
\rho_{e}
.)
This provides a reduction factor
{\rho_{e}}/{\rho_{0}}

\approx\frac{1}{2}

in the short-range effects of a new force as compared to the homogeneous case,
responsible for
an increase by
\,\simeq\sqrt{2}\,
of the upper limits on its couplings
.

The new force is proportional to
\Phi(x)(1+mr)\,e^{-mr}/r^{2}
, i.e. to

\Phi(x)(1+mr)\,e^{-mr}
as compared to the massless case. For small
\lambda
’s this reads

\Phi(x)\,(1+mr)\,e^{-mr}\,\simeq\,\frac{3\,(x-1)}{2x^{3}}\ (1+mr)\ e^{-m(r-R)}\ \frac{\bar{\rho}(x)}{\rho_{0}}\,\simeq\ \frac{3\,\lambda}{2R}\ \frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{r}{R}\ e^{-mz}\,.

(54)

Near the surface i.e. at low
z
compared to
\lambda
, this reduces approximately to

\,\displaystyle\frac{3\,\lambda}{2R}\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}

fa860
.

VII
Two ways of viewing a multishell model

VII.1
Expressions of
\Phi(x)

Let us approximate the sphere as a series of
n
homogeneous hollow spheres of radii
R_{i}
from
R_{1}
to
R_{n}=R
,
each one with a density
\rho_{i}
varying from
\rho_{1}=\rho_{c}
at the center to
\rho_{n}=\rho_{e}
at the periphery.
The contribution to
\Phi(x)

of a full homogeneous sphere of radius
R_{i}
is
[\rho_{i}R_{i}^{3}/(\rho_{0}R^{3})]\,\varphi(x_{i})
, with
x_{i}=R_{i}/\lambda=x\,R_{i}/R
. Summing on the contributions of the
n
hollow spheres one gets

\Phi(x)\,=\,\sum_{1}^{n}\ \frac{\rho_{i}}{\rho_{0}}\ \left[\,\frac{R_{i}^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(x_{i})\ -\frac{R_{i-1}^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(x_{i-1})\,\right]\ ,

(55)

with an inside radius
R_{0}=0
for the first sphere of radius
R_{1}
.

One may view the same situation as a superposition of
n
full homogeneous spheres of charges
q_{i}

(or masses
m_{i}
, when
\rho(r)
is viewed as a mass density) and radius
R_{i}
from
R_{1}
to
R_{n}=R
,
with densities equal to the excess density
\Delta\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}
(or
\Delta\rho_{n}=\rho_{e}
for the last sphere).
With
\Phi(x)
providing the external potential as

{\cal V}_{\rm out}(r)\,=\ Q\ \Phi(x)\ \frac{e^{-mr}}{4\pi r}\,=\,\sum_{i}\ q_{i}\ \phi(x_{i})\ \frac{e^{-mr}}{4\pi r}\,,

(56)

we get the equivalent expression

\framebox[352.814pt]{\rule[-12.80365pt]{0.0pt}{35.56593pt} $\displaystyle\Phi(x)\,=\,\sum_{1}^{n}\ \frac{q_{i}}{Q}\ \phi(x_{i})\,=\,\sum_{1}^{n-1}\ \frac{\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{R_{i}^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(x_{i})\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \,\phi(x)\ ,$}

(57)

with the normalisation condition

\sum_{1}^{n}\ \frac{q_{i}}{Q}\ =\ \sum_{1}^{n-1}\ \frac{\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{R_{i}^{3}}{R^{3}}\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\,=\,1\ .

(58)

This one expresses that for
\lambda=\infty
so that
x_{i}=x=0
, all
\phi
’s and
\Phi
’s in (
57
) are equal to 1.
Expression (
57
) of
\Phi(x)
is a simple rewriting of (
55
) by reordering its terms.

VII.2
\Phi(x)
in the continuum limit

In the continuum limit, the discrete sums (
55
,
57
) provide the two integral expressions

\framebox[404.02904pt]{\rule[-14.22636pt]{0.0pt}{36.98866pt} $\displaystyle\Phi(x)\,=\int_{0}^{R}\frac{\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ d\left[\,\frac{r^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(r/\lambda)\,\right]=\,\int_{0}^{R}\frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{r^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(r/\lambda)\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \phi(x)\ ,$}

(59)

which are equal, thanks to an integration by parts. The normalisation condition (
58
), which reads

\int\,\frac{dq}{Q}\,=\int\,\frac{dm}{M}\,=\,\int_{0}^{R}\,\frac{\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{3r^{2}dr}{R^{3}}\,=\,\int_{0}^{R}\,\frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{r^{3}}{R^{3}}\ +\ \frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\,=\,1\ ,

(60)

expresses that the total new charge (or mass) of the sphere may be decomposed as the one
of an homogeneous sphere of radius
R
and density
\rho(R)=\rho_{e}
,

plus the contributions of all the homogeneous full spheres of radii
r
, with the infinitesimal densities
-\,\frac{d\rho(r)}{dr}\,dr
.
The second expression in (
59
) explicitates how
\Phi(x)
depends

both of the external density
\rho_{e}

and on the variations of the relative density inside the sphere,
described by
\frac{-\,1}{\rho_{0}}\,\frac{d\rho(r)}{dr}
.

With

d\left[\,\frac{r^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi(r/\lambda)\,\right]\ =\ \frac{3}{m^{3}R^{3}}\ d\,(mr\cosh mr-\sinh mr)\ =\ \frac{\sinh mr}{mr}\ \frac{3r^{2}dr}{R^{3}}\,,

(61)

the first expression of
\Phi(x)
in (
59
) reads

\Phi(x)\,=\,\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\,\frac{\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{\sinh mr}{mr}\ \frac{3\,r^{2}dr}{R^{3}}\ =\ \langle\ \frac{\sinh mr}{mr}\ \rangle\ .

(62)

This provides another way to eq. (
31
), again obtained as an integral over the contributions
of the infinitesimally small thin shells of radius
r
from 0 to
R
.

The second expression in (
59
) may be explicitated as

\Phi(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{R}\!\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\,\frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\ \ dr}\ dr\ \,\frac{3\left(mr\cosh mr-\sinh mr\right)}{(mR)^{3}}\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{3\left(x\cosh x-\sinh x\right)}{x^{3}}\ .

(63)

One can also reinclude the last term within the integral, to write

\Phi(x)=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\!\frac{1}{\rho_{0}}\,\frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\ \ dr}\ dr\ \,\frac{3\left(mr\cosh mr-\sinh mr\right)}{(mR)^{3}}\ ,

(64)

again leading back to eq. (
62
).
The last term proportional to
\rho_{e}
in (
63
) is recovered from

\,\frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\ \ dr}=\rho_{e}\,\delta(r-R)+\,...\,
.
Eq. (
64
) also provides back
\phi(x)
for an homogeneous sphere from the discontinuity of
\rho(r)
at its surface, as

\phi(x)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\,\delta(r-R)\ dr\ \,\frac{3\,(mr\cosh mr-\sinh mr)}{(mR)^{3}}\,=\ \frac{3\,(x\,\cosh x-\sinh x)}{x^{3}}\ .

(65)

VII.3
\Phi(x)
from the evaluation of
\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle
in a multishell model

Let us consider again the expression (
57
) of
\Phi(x)
for a
p
-shell model of the sphere,
viewed as a superposition of
p
homogeneous full spheres of radii
R_{i}
and charges
q_{i}
(or masses
m_{i}
),
or from expression (
59
) obtained in the continuum limit. It reads, replacing the charge ratios
q_{i}/Q

by the corresponding mass ratios
m_{i}/M
,

\Phi(x)\,=\,\sum_{i=1}^{p}\ \frac{m_{i}}{M}\ \phi(R_{i}/\lambda)\ \to\ \int\,\frac{dm}{M}\ \phi(r/\lambda)\ ,

(66)

where
\phi(R_{i}/\lambda)
may be expanded as in (
43
), according to

\phi(R_{i}/\lambda)\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\ \frac{x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}\ \frac{R_{i}^{2n}}{R^{2n}}\ \frac{3}{2n+3}\ .

(67)

With the moments of
\rho(r)
given by

\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle\ =\,\frac{3}{2n+3}\ \sum_{i=1}^{p}\ \frac{m_{i}}{M}\ R_{i}^{2n}\ \to\ \frac{3}{2n+3}\ \int\,\frac{dm}{M}\ r^{2n}\,,

(68)

this reads

\Phi(x)=\int\frac{dm}{M}\,\phi(r/\lambda)=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}\,\int\frac{dm}{M}\,\frac{r^{2n}}{R^{2n}}\,\frac{3}{2n+3}\,=\,\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\,\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}\,\frac{\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle}{R^{2n}}\,,

(69)

which reconstitutes the expansion (
39
),

absolutely convergent for all
x
, and identified as

\langle\,\frac{\sinh mr}{mr}\,\rangle
.

This provides a fourth way to obtain the general expression of
\Phi(x)
, in addition to summing the contributions of thin shells
and solving the Poisson-like equation for the potential as in (
26
-
31
),
decomposing the sphere into small hollow spheres of radius
r
and thickness
dr
as in (
55
-
62
),
or viewing it as a superposition of an homogeneous sphere of radius
R
and density
\rho_{e}

with a set of smaller full spheres of radius
r
from 0 to
R
as in (
57
-
63
).

To go further we need to specify the density distribution
\rho(r)
.
We have, from the Earth moment of inertia
I=\frac{2}{3}\,M\,\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle\simeq.3308\,MR^{2}
,

\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle\,\simeq\,.4962\ R^{2},

so that

\Phi(x)\,\simeq\,1+\,.0827\ x^{2}\,+\,...\ .

(70)

This is slightly below
\phi(x)=1+\frac{x^{2}}{10}+...\,
for an homogeneous sphere, in agreement with the fact that

\frac{\bar{\rho}(x)}{\rho_{0}}\,=\,\frac{\Phi(x)}{\phi(x)}\,=\,1\,-\,.0173\ x^{2}\,+\,...\

(71)

is a decreasing function of

For
\,\lambda=R\,
i.e.

(in agreement with the small

\,\bar{\rho}(x)/\rho_{0}\,\simeq 1-.0173\ x^{2}+8.65\times 10^{-4}\ x^{4}+...\,

In the continuum limit, treating apart the contribution of the last sphere of radius

The dissymmetry of the final

(97)

(or

and

and a spin-0 coupling to

The limits for
B

10^{-25}
for a spin-1 coupling to
B\!-\!L

The Yukawa potential of a sphere can be expressed in terms of an hyperbolic form factor
\Phi(x\!=\!mR)
.
We have shown by several methods, including solving the Poisson-like equation for the inside potential, that it is given by

\Phi(x)\,=\,\langle\ \frac{\sinh mr}{mr}\ \rangle\ ,

(99)

related by analytic continuation to the form factor

f(k)=\Phi(ikR)\,=\,\langle\ e^{i\,\vec{k}.\vec{r}}\ \rangle\,=\,\langle\ \frac{\sin kr}{kr}\ \rangle\ .

(100)

One can also define the hyperbolic form factor

g(\vec{k})=f(-i\vec{k})=\langle\ e^{\vec{k}.\vec{r}}\ \rangle\,=\,\frac{\int\rho(\vec{r}\,)\ e^{\vec{k}.\vec{r}\,}\ d^{3}\vec{r}}{\int\rho(\vec{r}\,)\,d^{3}\vec{r}}\,,

(101)

as the bilateral Laplace transform of the distribution
\rho(\vec{r}\,)
, in general taken with compact support.
For a spherically symmetric distribution
\rho(r)
normalized to unity,

\Phi(x)
may be expressed from the bilateral Laplace transform of
\,r\,\rho(r)
, as

\Phi(x\!=\!kR)=g(k)=\langle\ e^{\vec{k}.\vec{r}}\ \rangle\,=\,\ \frac{2\pi}{k}\,\int_{-R}^{R}r\,\rho(r)\ e^{kr}\ dr\,=\,\langle\ \frac{\sinh kr}{kr}\ \rangle\ ,

(102)

reducing to
\phi(x)=3\,(x\,\cosh x-\sinh x)/x^{3}
for an homogeneous sphere.
An inhomogeneous sphere generates the same outside potential as an homogeneous one with density

\bar{\rho}(x)=\rho_{0}\,{\Phi(x)}/{\phi(x)}
, decreasing from the average
\rho_{0}
at small
x

down to values representative of an average density around a depth
d\approx\lambda
(cf. Figs.
2
and
3
).

Viewing the Earth as a superposition
of
n
homogeneous full spheres of radii
R_{i}
, densities
\Delta\rho_{i}=\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}
and masses
m_{i}
,
one has

\Phi(x)\,=\,\sum_{1}^{n}\ \frac{m_{i}}{M}\ \phi(mR_{i})\,=\,\sum_{1}^{n-1}\ \frac{\rho_{i}-\rho_{i+1}}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{R_{i}^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \,\phi\,\hbox{\LARGE$\left(\right.$}x\,\frac{R_{i}}{R}\hbox{\LARGE$\left.\right)$}\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \,\phi(x)\ ,

(103)

or in the continuum limit

\Phi(x)\,=\,\int_{0}^{R}\ \frac{-\,d\rho(r)}{\rho_{0}}\ \frac{r^{3}}{R^{3}}\ \phi\,\hbox{\Large$\left(\right.$}x\,\frac{r}{R}\hbox{\Large$\left.\right)$}\,+\,\frac{\rho_{e}}{\rho_{0}}\ \,\phi(x)\ ,

(104)

evaluated here in a simplified 5-shell model.

\Phi(x)
may be developed in terms of the even moments of the density distribution, as

\begin{array}[]{ccl}\Phi(x)&=&\ \,\displaystyle\sum_{0}^{\infty}\,\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n+1)!}\,\frac{\langle\,r^{2n}\,\rangle}{R^{2n}}\,=\,1\,+\,x^{2}\,\frac{\langle\,r^{2}\,\rangle}{6\,R^{2}}+\,x^{4}\,\frac{\langle\,r^{4}\,\rangle}{120\,R^{4}}+\,x^{6}\,\frac{\langle\,r^{6}\,\rangle}{5\,040\,R^{6}}+\,...\\
&\simeq&\,\displaystyle 1\,+.0827\ x^{2}+(2.707\times 10^{-3})\ x^{4}+\,(4.78\times 10^{-5})\ x^{6}+(5.26\times 10^{-7})\ x^{8}+...\ .\end{array}

(105)

the coupling limits being rescaled by

Gorlin–Goltz Syndrome in an Elderly Patient: Clinical Presentation and Diagnosis
Authors: Ruchika A. Nevse, Arjun Prakashey, Sushil Pande
Published: 2026

An elderly man presented with extensive cutaneous involvement, with >30 lesions (3 mm to 9 cm), including hyperpigmented papules, plaques, and nodules on the face, neck, and trunk. The lesions had been present since the age of 20 years. Family history was unremarkable. A dominant plaque on the right side of the face showed rolled borders with focal atrophy and variegated pigmentation [Figure 1]. Multiple cystic, nontender, mobile nodules on bilateral eyelids and nose were seen [Figure 2], which were later confirmed as epidermoid cysts. Dermoscopy showed ulceration, maple-leaf-like areas, concentric structures, a blue–white veil, blue–gray nests, and globules [Figure 3a]. Histopathology revealed acanthotic stratified squamous epithelium with a pigmented basal layer, elongated rete ridges, basaloid tumor cells arranged in cords and nests with peripheral palisading and a myxoid stroma [Figure 3b], consistent with basal cell carcinoma. Computed tomography of the head and neck demonstrated calcifications in the falcine, parafalcine, tentorial, and basal ganglia regions [Figure 4a], along with a solitary, unilocular, radiolucent, expansile odontogenic keratocyst in the mandible (1.7 × 1.1 × 1 cm) at the left second and third molar roots [Figure 4b]. Genetic testing was not performed. Based on the presence of multiple basal cell carcinomas, an odontogenic keratocyst, and characteristic intracranial calcifications, the patient fulfilled the established clinical diagnostic criteria for Gorlin–Goltz syndrome (nevoid basal cell carcinoma syndrome), and the diagnosis was made on clinical, radiological, dermoscopic, and histopathological grounds, despite the absence of genetic confirmation.

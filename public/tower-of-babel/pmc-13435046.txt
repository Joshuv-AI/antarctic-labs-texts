Synthesis of 6,7-dihydrobenzo[d]oxazol-4(5H)-ones through cascade N–H insertion and intramolecular annulation of cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones with amides
Authors: Chao Liu, Guang Han, Feng Gao, Xinyu Tong, Zi Wang, Wangcheng Hu, Xinwei He
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A one-pot two-step protocol has been reported for the synthesis of 6,7-dihydrobenzo[d]oxazol-4(5H)-ones through the N–H insertion of Rh-carbene and the subsequent intramolecular annulation from cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones with amides. This strategy has a broad substrate scope, and the synthesis is feasible on a gram scale. In general, this novel synthetic protocol features readily available substrates, facile operation, and wide functional group compatibility and produces highly bioactive 6,7-dihydrobenzo[d]oxazol-4(5H)-ones.

Introduction

The oxazole ring, as an important class of nitrogen-/oxygen-containing heterocycles, has been found in many cytotoxic natural products,1 agrochemicals,2 pharmaceuticals,3 and organic functional materials.4 Benzo[d]oxazoles are oxazole derivatives of particular interest because of their recognised utility in the treatment of stroke, depression, immune diseases5 and cerebral ischaemia,6 as well as for their known activity as antibacterial, antiviral,7 antimicrobial,8 oestrogenic modulator,9 anticancer,10 anti-inflammatory,11 and anti-tuberculosis agents.12 Thus, considerable attention has been focused on investigating the assembly of these useful skeletons.13

In recent years, diazo compounds have been extensively studied and widely used as powerful substrates for the construction of heterocyclic compounds via transition-metal-catalyzed cycloaddition and C–H bond activation/annulation strategies.14 Several approaches allowing the formation of the oxazole framework through the insertion of diazo compounds have been reported.15 In 2012, Doyle and co-workers reported an efficient method for the formation of multi-functionalized oxazoles through the Rh-catalyzed reactions of styryl diazo acetates with aryl oximes (Scheme 1a).16 Later, an unprecedented intermolecular nucleophilic attack of the C <svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" version="1.0" width="13.200000pt" height="16.000000pt" viewBox="0 0 13.200000 16.000000" preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"><metadata> Created by potrace 1.16, written by Peter Selinger 2001-2019 </metadata><g transform="translate(1.000000,15.000000) scale(0.017500,-0.017500)" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"><path d="M0 440 l0 -40 320 0 320 0 0 40 0 40 -320 0 -320 0 0 -40z M0 280 l0 -40 320 0 320 0 0 40 0 40 -320 0 -320 0 0 -40z"/></g></svg> O bond on Rh(i)-carbenes was further developed to provide concise access to 2,4,5-trisubstituted 1,3-oxazoles (Scheme 1b).17 Nonetheless, only a few succinct synthetic strategies are available for modular access to compounds containing the tetrahydrobenzoxazole moiety. For instance, Jones and co-workers disclosed a remarkable rearrangement of 3-aryltetrahydrobenzisoxazoles for the efficient construction of 2-aryltetrahydrobenzoxazoles.18 In addition, we previously reported a convenient and efficient procedure for the synthesis of 2-substituted tetrahydrobenzoxazoles through the rhodium(ii)-catalyzed C <svg xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg" version="1.0" width="23.636364pt" height="16.000000pt" viewBox="0 0 23.636364 16.000000" preserveAspectRatio="xMidYMid meet"><metadata> Created by potrace 1.16, written by Peter Selinger 2001-2019 </metadata><g transform="translate(1.000000,15.000000) scale(0.015909,-0.015909)" fill="currentColor" stroke="none"><path d="M80 600 l0 -40 600 0 600 0 0 40 0 40 -600 0 -600 0 0 -40z M80 440 l0 -40 600 0 600 0 0 40 0 40 -600 0 -600 0 0 -40z M80 280 l0 -40 600 0 600 0 0 40 0 40 -600 0 -600 0 0 -40z"/></g></svg> N bond insertion of cyclic 2-dizao-1,3-diketones and nitriles (Scheme 1c).19 As reported previously,20 the oxazole ring can be easily formed via the intramolecular annulation of β-hydroxyl amides in the presence of Ph3P/I2. We hypothesized that β-hydroxyl amides could be obtained via cascade N–H insertion and enol-ketone tautomerization with amides and α-carbonyl diazo compounds. Herein, we envisioned the use of cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones and amides as building blocks for the synthesis of 6,7-dihydrobenzo[d]oxazol-4(5H)-ones via a Rh-catalyzed cascade reaction involving N–H insertion and intramolecular annulation (Scheme 1d). Cyclic diazo compounds have emerged as intriguing coupling partners for rapidly constructing a wide range of more complex fused polycyclic scaffolds,21 and amides have been widely utilized as a “nitrogen” source to construct heterocycles, in which the amido-nitrogen generally acts as a “nucleophilic” monomer to trigger a reaction.22 Consequently, the applications of these compounds for the preparation of heterocycles has attracted considerable attention from organic chemists.

Results and discussion

Initially, the model reaction of 2-diazo-5,5-dimethylcyclohexane-1,3-dione 1a and acetamide 2a was selected for the optimization of the reaction conditions for the first step. At the outset, various transition-metal catalysts were employed in the reaction (Table 1, entries 1–8) and, to our delight, Rh2(OAc)4 as a catalyst provided the desired product 3a in 63% yield. Subsequently, different solvents were employed for this reaction, and the product 3a was detected in trace amounts when H2O, EtOH, DMF (N,N-dimethylformamide), and DMSO (dimethyl sulfoxide) were used as solvents (Table 1, entries 9–12). The desired product 3a was obtained in lower yields when the reaction was conducted in THF (tetrahydrofuran), CH3Cl, toluene, DCM (dichloromethane), and 1,4-dioxane (Table 1, entries 13–17). With a reaction time of 4 h, the yield of 3a was reduced to 51% (Table 1, entries 18, 19). As the reaction time was extended from 6 h to 8 h, the yield of 3a increased from 63% to 70% (Table 1, entries 20, 21). Furthermore, conducting the reaction at higher temperatures provided better results (Table 1, entries 22–25). There was no significant change in the reaction on varying the catalyst loading or the ratio of 1a and 2a (Table 1, entries 26–29).

After establishing the optimum reaction conditions, we commenced exploring the substrate generality and scope (Table 2). Firstly, the reactivity of different cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones was investigated by studying their reactions with acetamide 2a. Compared with the reaction of 4,4-disubstituted cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketone 1a for the generation of product 3a, various cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones 1 with an alkyl (e.g., Me, iPr) or aryl group (e.g., Ph, 4-ClC6H4) were also well suited for this one-pot reaction, affording the corresponding products 3b–3e in 67–75% yields. Fortunately, we were also able to extend the substrate to the unsubstituted cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketone, generating the desired product 3f in 68% yield. The reactions of different amides 2 with 1a were next investigated. 2-Chloroacetamide and benzamide worked well, providing the desired products 3g and 3h in 50% and 58% yields, respectively. Next, arylamides bearing electron-donating (e.g., Me, OMe) groups, irrespective of ortho-, meta-, and para-positions, reacted smoothly and provided the corresponding products 3i–3l in 50–65% yields. Furthermore, alkyl-substituted amides also underwent this one-pot, two-step transformation efficiently, providing the corresponding products 3m, 3n, and 3o in 46%, 41%, and 36% yields, respectively.

To further enhance the scope of the reaction and to introduce substituent diversity into the product, we evaluated the cross-reaction of various cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones and amides (Table 3). Both alkyl- (e.g., Me) and aryl-substituted (e.g., Ph) cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones reacted with various arylamides 2 under the stated conditions, smoothly generating the expected products 3p–3u in 40–69% yields. In particular, when R1 in cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones 1 was a hydrogen atom, the substrate also successfully reacted with arylamides 2 bearing a methyl group at different positions of the benzene ring, delivering the corresponding products 3w, 3x, and 3y in 68%, 60%, and 53% yields, respectively. The structure of compound 3y was confirmed unambiguously by X-ray single-crystal diffraction (CCDC 2524267, see the SI).

To check the synthetic efficacy and to demonstrate the practical application of the present protocol, we performed a gram-scale reaction of substrate 1a and 2a under the standard conditions (Scheme 2a). This one-pot strategy was easily scaled up to 10 mmol (scaled up 20 times), producing the desired product 3a in 65% yield (1.16 g), demonstrating the promise of this synthetic protocol as a useful tool in practical synthetic terms. To explore the application of the one-pot reaction, we studied the secondary annulation reaction by introducing ammonium acetate to the reaction system for the construction of the imidazole ring. To our delight, the desired 1,5,6,7-tetrahydro-4H-benzo[d]imidazole-4-ones 4a and 4b were obtained in 72% and 65% yields, respectively. This result revealed the practical potential of this one-pot reaction for heterocyclic compound synthesis.

In order to obtain insights into the reaction mechanism, the model reaction was quenched after one step, and the critical intermediate 5 was obtained in 75% yield (Scheme 3a(i)). Then, treatment of intermediate 5 with PPh3 and I2 in the presence of TEA in DCM afforded the desired product 3a in 90% yield (Scheme 3a(ii)). Based on the above results and previous literature reports,23 a plausible mechanism for the construction of product 3a is depicted in Scheme 3b. Initially, coordination of cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketone 1a and Rh2(OAc)4 generates the Rh-carbene intermediate A with the liberation of N2. Subsequently, N–H bond insertion of amide 2a into intermediate A leads to intermediate B and regenerates the Rh catalyst. The enol phosphonium salt C is then generated in the presence of TEA by trapping with the highly electrophilic PPh3I2, as proposed previously.20 Finally, intermediate C undergoes a cascade enol-imine tautomerization/intramolecular cyclization process to form the desired product 3a.

Conclusions

In conclusion, we have reported the synthesis of 6,7-dihydrobenzo[d]oxazol-4(5H)-ones through a cascade N–H insertion of Rh-carbene and subsequent intramolecular annulation from cyclic 2-diazo-1,3-diketones with amides as raw materials. This protocol constructs two new carbon-heteroatom bonds and one new five-membered ring and proceeds with a broad substrate scope and good functional group compatibility. In addition, this operationally simple method offers direct access to an important oxazole skeleton starting from simple and readily available amides and may find applications in medicinal chemistry.

Manipulating phases in many-body interacting systems with subsystem resetting
Anish Acharya §, Rupak Majumder §, Shamik Gupta

Abstract
Stabilizing thermodynamically unstable phases in many-body systems, such as suppressing pathological neuronal synchronization in Parkinson’s disease or maintaining magnetic order across broad temperature ranges, remains a persistent challenge. In traditional approaches, such phases are stabilized through intervening in the dynamics of all system constituents or introducing additional interactions. Here, we offer a hitherto-unexplored alternative, namely, subsystem resetting, whereby intervention in the dynamics of only a part of the system, and that too only occasionally in time, is implemented through resetting its state to a reset configuration. Just playing with a few parameters, e.g., the nature of the reset configuration and the size of the reset subsystem, one achieves a remarkable and robust control over the phase diagram of the bare dynamics. We demonstrate that these universal effects span a wide variety of scenarios, including equilibrium and non-equilibrium, mean-field and non-mean-field dynamics, with and without quenched disorder. Despite the challenges posed by memory effects, we obtain explicit analytical predictions, validated by simulations.

In summary, this work demonstrates that subsystem resetting serves as a versatile protocol for steering many-body systems toward desired states by simply adjusting the size of the reset subsystem, selecting appropriate reset configurations, and deciding on how often to reset. We solved exactly for effects of subsystem resetting in a variety of classical many-body systems. Notably, our findings reveal that resetting can replicate the complete phase diagram of the bare model without requiring fine-tuning of its couplings, while also enabling systematic manipulation of phase-transition points. Our work advances a new paradigm for manipulating collective dynamics, providing effective tools for design and regulation of complex systems. In complex systems such as the brain, interactions are typically heterogeneous and deviate from being mean-field. In this setting, resetting the influential driver nodes may offer an efficient control strategy even with small values of

[

where
\Delta H(s_{i}\to s^{\prime}_{i})
is the change in the energy of the system due to the flipping of the
i
-th spin from the value
s_{i}
to
s^{\prime}_{i}
. Defining
z=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=1\end{subarray}}^{N}s_{i}
, we calculate the transition rate between several configurations and obtain
w(+1\to-1)=\frac{e^{-\beta Jz}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
,
w(+1\to 0)=\frac{e^{\beta K}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
,
w(0\to+1)=\frac{e^{\beta Jz}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
,
w(0\to-1)=\frac{e^{-\beta Jz}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
,
w(-1\to+1)=\frac{e^{\beta Jz}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
,
and
w(-1\to 0)=\frac{e^{\beta K}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}
. Using these in Eq. (
5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle s_{i}\rangle}{dt}

\displaystyle=-\left\langle s_{i}\sum_{s^{\prime}}w(s_{i}\to s^{\prime}_{i})\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum_{s^{\prime}}s^{\prime}_{i}w(s_{i}\to s^{\prime}_{i})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=-\left\langle s_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\sum_{s^{\prime}}s^{\prime}_{i}w(s_{i}\to s^{\prime}_{i})\right\rangle=-\left\langle s_{i}\right\rangle+\left\langle\frac{2\sinh{\beta Jz}}{2\cosh{\beta Jz}+e^{\beta K}}\right\rangle.

(7)

Further, we have

\displaystyle z

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\end{subarray}}^{N}s_{i}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\langle s_{i}\rangle+\delta_{i})=m+\delta,

(8)

with
~\delta_{i}\equiv s_{i}-\langle s_{i}\rangle
and
\delta\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\delta_{i}
. Being a mean-field system, we have
\langle\delta_{i}\rangle=0~\forall~i
. As a result,
\langle z\rangle=m
, and the fluctuations
\delta
must satisfy

\displaystyle\langle\delta\rangle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\langle\delta_{i}\rangle=0.

(9)

Furthermore, being mean-field allows to write
\langle s_{i}s_{j}\rangle=\langle s_{i}\rangle\langle s_{j}\rangle
, implying that
\langle\delta_{i}\delta_{j}\rangle=\langle\delta_{i}\rangle\langle\delta_{j}\rangle=0
. In fact, one has
\langle s_{i}s_{j}s_{k}\ldots\rangle=\langle s_{i}\rangle\langle s_{j}\rangle\langle s_{k}\rangle\ldots
, implying
\langle\delta_{i}\delta_{j}\delta_{k}\ldots\rangle=\langle\delta_{i}\rangle\langle\delta_{j}\rangle\langle\delta_{k}\rangle\ldots=0
. We then get

\displaystyle\langle\delta^{k}\rangle=\frac{1}{N^{k}}\sum_{i_{1}}\cdots\sum_{i_{k}}\langle\delta_{i_{1}}\cdots\delta_{i_{k}}\rangle=0.

(10)

At this point, putting
z=m+\delta
in Eq. (
7
), expanding the second term on the right hand side as a power of
\delta
and using
\langle\delta^{k}\rangle=0~\forall~k
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d\langle s_{i}\rangle}{dt}=-\left\langle s_{i}\right\rangle+\frac{2\sinh{\beta Jm}}{2\cosh{\beta Jm}+e^{\beta K}}.

(11)

We now define the order parameters of the reset and non-reset subsystems as
m_{\mathrm{r}}=\left\langle\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}\right\rangle
and
m_{\mathrm{nr}}=\left\langle\frac{1}{N-n}\sum_{i=n+1}^{N}s_{i}\right\rangle
, respectively. Putting them back into Eq. (
11
), summing the equation for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
and dividing by
n
, we get the evolution equation of the order parameter of the reset subsystem, which reads as

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{\mathrm{r}}}{dt}=-m_{\mathrm{r}}+\frac{2\sinh{[\beta(fm_{\mathrm{r}}+\bar{f}m_{\mathrm{nr}})]}}{2\cosh{[\beta(fm_{\mathrm{r}}+\bar{f}m_{\mathrm{nr}})]}+e^{\beta K}}.

(12)

Similarly, summing both sides of Eq. (
11
) for
i=n+1,\ldots,N
and divining by
(N-n)
, we get the evolution equation of the order parameter of the non-reset subsystem, which reads as

\displaystyle\frac{dm_{\mathrm{nr}}}{dt}=-m_{\mathrm{nr}}+\frac{2\sinh{[\beta(fm_{\mathrm{r}}+\bar{f}m_{\mathrm{nr}})]}}{2\cosh{[\beta(fm_{\mathrm{r}}+\bar{f}m_{\mathrm{nr}})]}+e^{\beta K}}.

(13)

Equations (
12
) and (
13
) are quoted in the main text.

Appendix C
Derivation of Eqs. (7) and (8)

Similar to the BEG model, here also our starting point is Eq. (2) of the main text describing the Hamiltonian of the KN model. Here also, if spins with index
i=1,2,\ldots,n
are reset to a configuration
s_{i}=s_{i}^{(0)};i=1,2,\ldots,n
, with magnetization
m_{0}
(clearly,
m_{0}=(1/n)\sum_{i=1}^{n}s_{i}^{(0)}
) at rate
\lambda\to\infty
, we may write the effective Hamiltonian of the non-reset subsystem with configuration
\mathscr{C}\equiv\{s_{n+1},s_{n+2},\ldots,s_{N}\}
as

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{KN}}^{\lambda\to\infty}=\frac{K}{2}\sum_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}(s_{i}s_{i+1}-1)-fm_{0}\sum_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}s_{j}-\frac{K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1),

(14)

where the the energy scale have been shifted by a constant amount
\delta_{\mathrm{E}}=(K/2)\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}s_{i}^{(0)}s_{i+1}^{(0)}-Nf^{2}m_{0}^{2}/2
. For simplicity, here we have chosen
s_{1}^{(0)}=s_{n}^{(0)}=1
. Equation. (
14
) is Eq. (7) of the main text. The corresponding partition function of the non-reset subsystem becomes

\displaystyle Z=\sum_{\mathscr{C}}e^{\Bigl[\frac{\beta}{2N}\sum\limits_{s_{i},s_{j}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}s_{j}+\beta fm_{0}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}-\frac{\beta K}{2}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}(s_{i}s_{i+1}-1)+\frac{\beta K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1)\Bigl]}.

(15)

Using the
\mathrm{HS}
transformation in Eq. (
15
), we obtain

\displaystyle Z(\beta,N-n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathscr{C}}\sqrt{\frac{\beta N}{2\pi}}\bar{f}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx~e^{-\frac{\beta N\bar{f}^{2}}{2}x^{2}+\beta x\bar{f}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}}e^{\beta f\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}-\frac{\beta K}{2}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}(s_{i}s_{i+1}-1)+\frac{\beta K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1)},

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\beta N}{2\pi}}\bar{f}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx~e^{-\frac{\beta N(1-f)^{2}}{2}x^{2}}Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N),

(16)

where
Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N)=\sum\limits_{\mathscr{C}}\exp\left(\beta M\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}-\frac{\beta K}{2}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}(s_{i}s_{i+1}-1)+\frac{\beta K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1)\right)
is the partition function of the nearest neighbor Ising model in presence of an external field of strength
M\equiv fm_{0}+\bar{f}x
and with two additional boundary terms. The quantity
Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N)
can be further simplified using the transfer matrices as follows:

\displaystyle Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathscr{C}}e^{\beta\left[M\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}s_{i}-\frac{K}{2}\sum\limits_{s_{i}\in\mathscr{C}}(s_{i}s_{i+1}-1)+\frac{K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1)\right]},

\displaystyle=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\sum_{s_{n+1}=-1}^{+1}\cdots\sum_{s_{N}=-1}^{+1}T_{s_{n+1}s_{n+2}}\cdots T_{s_{N-1}s_{N}}A_{s_{N}s_{n+1}},

(17)

where
T
and
A
are the transfer matrices with matrix elements

\displaystyle T_{s_{i}s_{i+1}}\equiv e^{\beta\left(\frac{M}{2}s_{i}+\frac{M}{2}s_{i+1}-\frac{K}{2}s_{i}s_{i+1}\right)},

(18)

\displaystyle A_{s_{N}s_{n+1}}\equiv e^{\beta\left(\frac{M}{2}s_{N}+\frac{M}{2}s_{n+1}-\frac{K}{2}s_{N}s_{n+1}+\frac{K}{2}(s_{N}-1)(s_{n+1}-1)\right)}.

(19)

Since
s_{i}
can take values
\pm 1
, we can write
T
and
A
as follows:

\displaystyle T=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{\beta\left(M-\frac{K}{2}\right)}&e^{\frac{\beta K}{2}}\\
e^{\frac{\beta K}{2}}&e^{-\beta\left(M+\frac{K}{2}\right)}\end{array}\right];~~A=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{\beta\left(M-\frac{K}{2}\right)}&e^{\frac{\beta K}{2}}\\
e^{\frac{\beta K}{2}}&e^{-\beta\left(M-\frac{3K}{2}\right)}\end{array}\right],

which can be used to rewrite Eq. (
17
) as
Z_{0}=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[T^{N-n-1}A\right]
.

We now go in the eigenbasis of
T
. From the expression of
T
, we straightforwardly get the eigenvalues
\lambda
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{\pm}=e^{-\frac{\beta K}{2}}\left[\cosh{\beta M}\pm\sqrt{\cosh^{2}{\beta M}+2e^{\beta K}\sinh{\beta K}}\right],

(24)

and the matrix that diagonalizes
T
as

\displaystyle C=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}(\lambda_{+}-ab)^{2}}}&\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}(\lambda_{-}-ab)^{2}}}\\
\frac{b(\lambda_{+}-ab)}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}(\lambda_{+}-ab)^{2}}}&\frac{b(\lambda_{-}-ab)}{\sqrt{1+b^{2}(\lambda_{-}-ab)^{2}}}\end{array}\right],

with
a\equiv e^{\beta F}
and
b\equiv e^{-\frac{\beta K}{2}}
. Then, we get

\displaystyle Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N)=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[T^{N-n-1}A\right]=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[D^{N-n-1}\underbrace{C^{-1}AC}_{\tilde{A}}\right]=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[D^{N-n-1}\underbrace{C^{-1}AC}_{\tilde{A}}\right],

(27)

where we have

\displaystyle D^{N-n-1}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda^{N-n-1}_{+}&0\\
0&\lambda^{N-n-1}_{-}\end{array}\right].

Upon further computation, we get that

\displaystyle Z_{0}(\beta,x,f,N)=e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\left(\lambda^{N-n-1}_{+}\tilde{A}_{11}+\lambda^{N-n-1}_{-}\tilde{A}_{22}\right)\approx e^{-\frac{\beta K(N-n)}{2}}\lambda^{N-n-1}_{+}\tilde{A}_{11},

(30)

where we have used the fact that as
\lambda_{+}>\lambda_{-}
, in the thermodynamic limit, one has
\lambda^{N-n-1}_{+}\gg\lambda^{N-n-1}_{-}
. Putting all of these together in Eq. (
16
), we finally get

\displaystyle Z(\beta,N-n)=\sqrt{\frac{\beta N}{2\pi}}\bar{f}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}dx~e^{-N\beta\tilde{F}(\beta,x,f)},

(31)

with

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(\beta,x,f)

\displaystyle=\frac{\bar{f}^{2}}{2}x^{2}-\frac{\bar{f}}{\beta}\log\left[\cosh{\beta M}+\sqrt{\cosh^{2}{\beta M}+2e^{\beta K}\sinh{\beta K}}\right]+K\bar{f}.

(32)

Following similar arguments as in Sec. (
A
), the steady-state magnetization
m^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}
satisfies the self-consistent Eq.
\mathrm{(8)}
of main text.

Appendix D

We start from Eq. (14) in Appendix C of the main text, which reads as

(33)

Putting

and
l=0
respectively in Eq. (

\displaystyle\left[l^{2}T+il\omega_{\mathrm{r}}+\lambda\right]\mathscr{P}_{l,0}+\gamma l\mathscr{P}_{l+1,0}-\gamma^{*}l\mathscr{P}_{l-1,0}=\frac{\lambda}{4\pi^{2}}\left[\alpha+(-1)^{l}(1-\alpha)\right],

(34)

(35)

\mathscr{P}_{l,m}

P(\theta_{\mathrm{r}},\theta_{\mathrm{nr}},\omega_{\mathrm{r}},\omega_{\mathrm{nr}},t)

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{l,0}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\left[\alpha+(-1)^{l}(1-\alpha)\right].

(36)

Putting it in the definition of

\displaystyle r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{r}}e^{i\psi^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{r}}}=4\pi^{2}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\omega_{\mathrm{r}}d\omega_{\mathrm{nr}}g(\omega_{\mathrm{r}})g(\omega_{\mathrm{nr}})\mathscr{P}_{-1,0}=2\alpha-1,

r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{r}}=|2\alpha-1|=r_{0}
. This we can also physically understand from the fact that in the limit

\gamma

\displaystyle\gamma_{\infty}=\frac{K}{2}\left[fr_{0}+\bar{f}r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}e^{i\psi^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}}\right],

(38)

which is a function of only
r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}
and
\psi^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}
.

Solving Eq. (
35
) in the same method as discussed in Appendix C, we get

\displaystyle\mathscr{P}_{0,1}=\frac{\Lambda^{(\infty)}_{1}(\omega_{\mathrm{nr}})}{4\pi^{2}},

(39)

where we have

(40)

Putting this expression in the definition of
r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}
, we get

\displaystyle r^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}e^{i\psi^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{nr}}}

\displaystyle=2\pi i\sum_{\omega_{q}}\mathrm{Res}\left.\left\{g(\omega)\left[\Lambda^{(\infty)}_{1}(\omega_{\mathrm{nr}})\right]^{*}\right\}\right|_{\omega=\omega_{q}},

(41)

where
\omega_{q}
’s are the poles of
g(\omega)
in the lower-half of the complex-
\omega
plane.

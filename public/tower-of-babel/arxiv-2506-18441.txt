Abstract
. Frame multipliers are an abstract version of Toeplitz operators in frame theory and consist of a composition of a multiplication operator with the analysis and synthesis operators. Whereas the boundedness properties of frame multipliers on Banach spaces associated to a frame, so-called coorbit spaces, are well understood, their invertibility is much more difficult. We show that frame multipliers with a positive symbol are Banach space isomorphisms between the corresponding coorbit spaces. The results resemble the lifting theorems in the theory of Besov spaces and modulation spaces. Indeed, the application of the abstract lifting theorem to Gabor frames yields a new lifting theorem between modulation spaces. A second application to Fock spaces yields isomorphisms between weighted Fock spaces. The main techniques are the theory of localized frames and existence of inverse-closed matrix algebras.

1.
Introduction

A lifting theorem establishes an isomorphism between spaces of
functions, distributions or sequences and their weighted
versions. The basic example is the multiplication operator with a
multiplier sequence
(\mu_{k})
, given by

(1.1)

c=(c_{k})_{k\in K}\mapsto(\mu_{k}c_{k})_{k\in K}\,.

It maps the weighted

\ell^{p}
-space
\ell^{p}_{\mu}(K)
with norm
\|c\|_{\ell^{p}_{\mu}}=\Big(\sum_{k\in K}|c_{k}|^{p}\mu_{k}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}
to the unweighted
\ell^{p}(K)
over the index set

K
. The multiplication operator establishes an isomorphism
between those spaces and is called
a lifting operator. By means of a lifting
operator one may often reduce the study of
functions or operators to the unweighted case.

Although this example is not more than a definition, it becomes
interesting
when a function space is described by the behavior of its
coefficients with respect to a basis. For example, the usual Sobolev space on
the torus is characterized by its Fourier
coefficients as
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}):\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}|\hat{f}(k)|^{2}(1+4\pi^{2}|k|^{2})^{s}<\infty\}

[
44
]
. Let
\mu_{k}=(1+4\pi^{2}|k|^{2})^{\nu/2}
be a polynomial weight function, then the operator

M_{\mu}f(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\mu_{k}\hat{f}(k)e^{ikx}=(\mathrm{I}-\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}})^{\nu/2}f(x)

is a bijection between the Sobolev
spaces
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
and
\mathbb{H}^{s-\nu}(\mathbb{T})
.

For the Sobolev space
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
the underlying
basis is the Fourier basis, and a function in
\mathbb{H}^{s}(\mathbb{T})
is characterized by the size
of its coefficients with respect to this basis. The
lifting operator can be described as a multiplication operator on the
coefficients, i.e., as a
Fourier multiplier, or directly as a
a fractional power of the
derivative. Similar
phenomena can be observed for Besov spaces and modulation spaces (see below).

The situation becomes more subtle when a function space is no longer
described with respect to a basis, but with respect to a frame, which
is a form of
overcomplete
system with stable series
expansions. To illustrate this substantial difference, assume first that

\Psi=\{\psi_{k}:k\in K\}
is an orthonormal basis. Then the mapping
f\mapsto\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{k\in K}
is a bijection between the
spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\ell^{2}(K)
. Thus for bounded symbols
\mu=\left(\mu_{k}\right)_{k\in K}

the sequence
\mu_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle,k\in K,
is always in the
range of the analysis operator defined by the sequence
\left(C_{\Psi}f\right)_{k}=\left<f,\psi_{k}\right>
. Consequently, there is always

h\in\mathcal{H}
, such that
\mu_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle=\langle h,\psi_{k}\rangle
. By contrast, if

\Psi
is a redundant frame, then
\mathrm{ran}\,C_{\Psi}\neq\ell^{2}(K)
and in general
(\mu_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle)\not\in\mathrm{ran}\,C_{\Psi}
. In this case it is still meaningful, but more intricate,
to describe a space by the size of its coefficients with respect to
the given frame
\Psi
. The additional
difficulties introduced by the redundancy can also be seen by the fact that the (synthesis) operator
c\mapsto\sum c_{k}\psi_{k}

possesses a non-trivial kernel and coefficients in the series expansion are no longer
unique. For the description of associated spaces one therefore uses a
special set of coefficients, namely those with respect to the dual
frame
\{\tilde{\psi_{k}}:k\in K\}
, so that every
f
possesses a (non-unique)
series expansion

f=\sum_{k\in K}\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle\,\psi_{k}
.
Whereas initially such expansions hold only in a Hilbert space, it is
often possible to define associated spaces -
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
- by imposing a
weighted
\ell^{p}
-norm
on these canonical coefficients via

(1.2)

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}}=(\big(\sum_{k\in K}|\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle|^{p}m_{k}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}\,.

It is not completely obvious how and when this is a meaningful
definition. The decisive assumption is that the frame is localized,
and the theory of these spaces is treated in the context of
localized frames
[
30
]
. We will provide the necessary
background in Section
3
.

It is now tempting to follow the example of Sobolev spaces and
consider a map

(1.3)

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f=\sum_{k\in K}\mu_{k}\,\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle\,\psi_{k}\,.

Once
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
is well-defined, it is easy to see that

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is
bounded between
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m\mu}
and
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
, but it
is unclear whether

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is indeed an isomorphism as in the case
of a basis. As stated above the difficulty lies in the fact that the
multiplied coefficient sequence
\mu_{k}\,\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle
need not be of the form
\langle h,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle
and might even belong to the kernel of the synthesis
operator. An understanding of this phenomenon has been a major
challenge for us, and this paper is entirely devoted to obtain a
deeper insight into the isomorphism property of
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
.

Operators of the form (
1.3
) are of interest in
their own right and have been investigated both as abstract

frame multipliers

[
6
]
and for special frames, e.g., under the name
Gabor multipliers
[
28
]
. Especially Gabor multipliers
have numerous applications, e.g., as quantization operators in physics
[
2
]
and for time-variant filtering

[
43
]
in signal processing. They are
applied in adaptive sound analysis
[
42
]
, noise reduction

[
16
]
, psychoacoustic modeling

[
11
]
, sound morphing

[
45
]
and synthesis
[
22
]
.

The perturbation of invertible frame
multipliers is studied e.g. in
[
47
,
48
]
, but it remains difficult to show
that a given frame multiplier is invertible. Our investigation offers
some answers to this question.

Our main theorem asserts that a frame multiplier with a positive
multiplier sequence

{\mu}
is a Banach space isomorphism between the
Banach spaces associated to the frame. To offer a flavor of our main
result, we state a
provocatively inexact version of such a result.

Result 1
.

Under suitable conditions on the frame
{\Psi}
and the weights

m
and
\mu
, the frame multiplier

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is a Banach space isomorphism between

\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
and

\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}
, and for some constants
m,M>0

one has the norm equivalence

m\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}}\leq\|\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}}\leq M\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}}\,.

The result as stated is, of course, very imprecise. Our main
contribution is to identify appropriate abstract conditions on the
frame

{\Psi}
so that all symbols in the envisioned theorem are
meaningful and the results holds rigorously under precisely stated
assumptions. This will be done after some preliminary work in
Section 4, Theorem
11
.

By and large,
the proof is motivated by the proof of the lifting theorems for
modulation spaces in
[
36
]
. Many analytic arguments there
can be
translated into the language of abstract
frame theory.
The additional ideas comes from the theory of

localized frames
and the
spectral invariance
of
matrix algebras and will be presented in Sections 2 and 3.

Background.

In functional analysis, lifting theorems are an important feature in
the theory of function spaces. For instance, the family of Besov
spaces
[
51
]
is
a three-parameter family
B^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, where
0<p,q<\infty
refer to
L^{p}
-conditions and
s\in\mathbb{R}
is a smoothness
parameter. The lifting theorem for Besov spaces states that

D^{n}B^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R})=B^{p,q}_{s-n}(\mathbb{R})\,,

with
D=\frac{d}{dx}
being the usual derivative.
In simple terms this means that by taking
n
derivatives, one
looses
n
orders of smoothness. However, the lifting theorem says
more: the derivative
D^{n}
is a Banach space isomorphism from
B^{p,q}_{s}(\mathbb{R})
onto
B^{p,q}_{s-n}(\mathbb{R})
. See
[
51
]
for
the precise definition of Besov spaces and their basic theory, in
particular Thm. 2.3.8.
for the lifting theorem.

A similar lifting theorem occurs in the theory of the original
version of modulation spaces

M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
in
[
26
]
, where again
s
is a
smoothness parameter. See Section
5.1
for details on
modulation spaces and their lifting theorems.
The lifting theorem states again that
D^{n}M^{p,q}_{s}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})=M^{p,q}_{s-n}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
. The lifting theorems
for both Besov spaces and modulation spaces involve arguments with Fourier
multipliers. We will discuss more advanced lifting theorems for
modulation spaces in Section
5.1
.

Toeplitz operators.

In complex analysis, the analogy to frame multipliers is the
class of Toeplitz operators
[
53
]
. If

f
is holomorphic, then
{\mu}f
for some multiplier
{\mu}
need not
be holomorphic. To map back into a space of holomorphic functions,
one uses a suitable projection
P
(a Bergman or Szegö
projection)
[
53
]
, and the resulting operator is then the
Toeplitz operator
M_{\mu}=P(\mu f)
.
In frame theory, analyticity corresponds to the properties of the
frame coefficients
C_{\Psi}f=\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle_{k\in K}
. Unless
\Psi
is a Riesz basis, the range
\mathrm{ran}\,C_{\Psi}
is a proper, closed subspace of
\ell^{2}(K)
.
After the
multiplication with a symbol
\mu
the resulting sequence
(\mu_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle)_{k\in K}
is in general no longer in
that subspace and needs to be projected back to
\mathrm{ran}\,C_{\Psi}
. By identifying
\mathcal{H}
with
\mathrm{ran}\,C_{\Psi}
via the synthesis
operator
c\mapsto\sum_{k\in K}c_{k}\psi_{k}
, we then obtain
precisely the
operator
M_{\mu}f=\sum_{k\in K}\mu_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle\psi_{k}
, in other words, a frame
multiplier.

To see that this is not a mere analogy, we will discuss the
explicit example of Fock spaces. The Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{m}
with a
positive weight
m
consists of all entire functions satisfying

\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F(z)|^{p}m(z)^{p}e^{-\pi p|z|^{2}/2}\,dz<\infty
. By using the explicit formula for the
projection onto Fock space, a Toeplitz operator with multiplier
function
\mu
is defined by

T_{\mu}F(z)=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\mu(w)F(w)e^{\pi\bar{w}z}e^{-\pi|w|^{2}/2}\,dw\,.

Whereas usually
\mu
is a function, it has been known for a long
time that one may even take rough distributions for the definition of
Toeplitz operators
[
19
]
. By taking the Radon measure
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda}
with support
in a discrete, relatively separated set
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, one
obtains the operator

M_{\mu}F(z)=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu_{\lambda}\,F(\lambda)e^{\pi\bar{\lambda}z}\,e^{-\pi\left|\lambda\right|^{2}/2}\,.

Comparing this to (
1.3
) one sees that this is a frame multiplier in the strict sense of the definition. As
an example of the main theorem, we will prove a lifting
theorem between Fock spaces. Although this is only a simple example of
the general lifting theorem for abstract frame multipliers, this is a
new result that may be of independent interest in complex analysis.

Result 2
.

Under mild assumptions on
\mu
and
\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}
, the
frame multiplier
M_{\mu}
is an isomorphism from

{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}
to
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}
.

The precise formulation will be given in Section 5.2.

Organization of the paper.

Section
2

introduces notation and preliminary facts about frames, frame

spaces. Section
3
revisits localized frames and

\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}

Preliminaries and Notation

For a standard reference to functional analysis and operator theory
and the notation used we refer to
[
20
]
. In particular we
denote the space of bounded operators from a normed space
X
into
a normed space
Y
by
{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(X,Y\right)
, and set
{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(X\right)={{\mathcal{B}}}\left(X,X\right)
. By the
notation
c\asymp d
, we mean that there are constants
\alpha,\beta>0
, such that
\alpha c\leq d\leq\beta c
.

2.1.
Sequence spaces

We consider a discrete and countable index set
K
. We consider
functions on
K
as sequences and denote them as

c=\left(c_{k}\right)_{k\in K}
. We use a short
notation for component-wise operations, e.g.
c\cdot d\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=}\left(c_{k}\cdot d_{k}\right)_{k\in K}
.
We will represent an operator
M
between sequence
spaces over the index set
K
by a matrix, i.e.
M=\left(M_{kl}\right)_{k,l\in K}
.
The usual
\ell^{p}
-space on the index set
K
is denoted by
\ell^{p}(K)
or simply
\ell^{p}
.

Let
m>0
be a strictly positive weight on
K
, i.e.,
m_{k}>0
for all
k\in K
.
We define the space
\ell_{m}^{p}
by

c\in\ell_{m}^{p}\Longleftrightarrow m\cdot c\in\ell^{p}
, with the norm
\|c\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}}=\|m\cdot c\|_{\ell^{p}}=\big(\sum\limits_{k}m_{k}^{p}\left|c_{k}\right|^{p}\big)^{1/p}<\infty
.
For given
p
with
1\leq p<\infty
, we denote by
p^{\prime}
the
conjugate index satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}=1
.
Then the dual space of
\ell^{p}_{m}
can be identified with

\left(\ell_{m}^{p}\right)^{\prime}\cong\ell_{1/{m}}^{p^{\prime}}
with the duality

\left<{c},{d}\right>_{\ell_{m}^{p}\times\ell_{1/{m}}^{p^{\prime}}}=\sum\limits_{k}c_{k}\overline{d_{k}}\,.

2.1.1.
Isomorphism between
\ell^{p}_{m}
spaces

For a sequence
\mu

we use the notation
{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)
for the corresponding diagonal
matrix, i.e.
{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)=(\delta_{k,l}\,\mu_{k})_{(k,l)\in K\times K}
. The associated operator is the multiplication operator

\big({\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)c\big)_{k}=\mu_{k}c_{k}\qquad k\in K\,.

The primordial lifting theorem, already mentioned in the introduction, asserts that
{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)
is an
isomorphism.

Lemma 1
.

If
\mu>0
, then the operator
{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)
is an isometric isomorphism between

\ell_{\mu m}^{p}
and
\ell_{m}^{p}
with inverse

{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)^{-1}={\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu^{-1}\right)
.

This follows from the identity

\left\|{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)c\right\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}}=\sum\limits_{k\in K}\left|\mu_{k}c_{k}\right|^{p}m_{k}^{p}=\left\|c\right\|_{\ell_{\mu m}^{p}}\,.

Consequently, one has the following isomorphisms

{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right):\ell^{p}_{\mu m}\rightarrow\ell_{m}^{p}\text{ and }\ell_{m}^{p}\rightarrow\ell^{p}_{m/\mu},

and

{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)^{-1}:\ell_{m}^{p}\rightarrow\ell^{p}_{\mu m}\text{ and }\ell^{p}_{m/\mu}\rightarrow\ell_{m}^{p}.

2.2.
Frames

A sequence
\Psi=\left(\psi_{k}\right)_{k\in K}
in a separable
Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
is a
frame
for

\mathcal{H}

[
23
,
17
]
, if there are constants
A,B>0

such that

(2.1)

\qquad A\|f\|^{2}\leq\sum_{k\in K}|\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle|^{2}\leq B\|f\|^{2}\qquad\text{
for all }f\in\mathcal{H}\,.

To every sequence
\Psi\subseteq\mathcal{H}
one can associate four
operators: these are the
analysis operator

C_{\Psi}:\mathcal{H}\to\ell^{2}(K)
defined by

\left(C_{\Psi}f\right)_{k}=\left<f,\psi_{k}\right>\qquad k\in K\,,

the
synthesis operator

D_{\Psi}:\ell^{2}(K)\to\mathcal{H}
given by

D_{\Psi}c=D_{\Psi}(c_{k})=\sum_{k\in K}c_{k}\psi_{k}\,,

the
frame operator

S_{\Psi}:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
as

S_{\Psi}f=\sum_{k\in K}\left<f,\psi_{k}\right>\psi_{k}=D_{\Psi}C_{\Psi}f\,,

and the Gram operator
G_{\Psi}:\ell^{2}(K)\to\ell^{2}(K)
with the Gram matrix

\left({G}_{\Psi}\right)_{k,l}=\left<\psi_{l},\psi_{k}\right>\,,

resulting in
G_{\Psi}=C_{\Psi}D_{\Psi}
.
These operators are not necessarily well-defined
[
12
]
. The definition of a
frame (
2.1
) guarantees that all four operators are bounded on the
respective spaces. In fact,
\Psi
is a frame, if and only if
S_{\Psi}
is
bounded, positive and invertible on
\mathcal{H}
(
C_{\Psi}
is bounded, injective and has closed range or
D_{\Psi}
is bounded and onto, respectively).
In this case, we can use the
canonical dual frame

\widetilde{\Psi}:=(\tilde{\psi}_{k})_{k\in K}
,
given by
\tilde{\psi}_{k}=S_{\Psi}^{-1}\psi_{k}
for all
k
to
obtain
the frame reconstruction formula

(2.2)

f=\sum\limits_{k\in K}\Big<f,\psi_{k}\Big>\tilde{\psi}_{k}=\sum\limits_{k\in K}\left<f,\tilde{\psi}_{k}\right>\psi_{k}\text{
for all }f\in\mathcal{H}\,.

In short notation, this reads as
D_{\Psi}C_{\tilde{\Psi}}=D_{\tilde{\Psi}}C_{\Psi}=\mathsf{id}_{\mathcal{H}}
.

Analogously, for two sequences
\Psi
and
\Phi
in
\mathcal{H}
one defines the cross-Gram operator by
G_{\Psi,\Phi}=C_{\Psi}D_{\Phi}
, which is again bounded, if
\Psi
and

\Phi
are frames. For later use, we record the following identity
from the algebra of Gramian matrices: we have

(2.3)

G_{\Psi}G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}=G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}\,

Furthermore, using the notation
B^{\dagger}
for the (Moore-Penrose) pseudo-inverse
of
B

[
18
]
, the relation between various Gram matrices associated to a
frame
\Psi
is given by
[
30
, Lemma 3.1]

(2.4)

G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}=G_{\Psi}^{\dagger}G_{\Psi}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}=\big(G_{\Psi}^{\dagger}\big)^{2}\,G_{\Psi}\,.

2.3.
Frame Multipliers

The main object for abstract lifting theorems are frame
multipliers
[
6
]
. The following definition is a bit more general than what
is needed and uses two frames on different Hilbert
spaces.

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathcal{H}_{1}
and
\mathcal{H}_{2}
be Hilbert spaces, let

{\Psi}=\{\psi_{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{1}
and

\Phi=\{\phi_{k}\}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{2}
be two
frames. For given
a sequence
m=(m_{k})_{k\in K}\in\mathbb{C}^{K}
,
the frame multiplier with symbol
m
is the operator

\mathit{M}_{m,\Psi,\Phi}:\mathcal{H}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{2}
defined by

(2.5)

\mathit{M}_{m,\Psi,\Phi}f=\sum_{k\in K}m_{k}\langle f,\psi_{k}\rangle\phi_{k}.

If
m\in\ell^{\infty}(K)
, then the multiplier is bounded
on
\mathcal{H}_{1}
by
\sqrt{B_{\Phi}}\sqrt{B_{\Psi}}\left\|m\right\|_{\infty}
,
where
B_{\Phi}
and
B_{\Psi}
refer to the upper frame bounds in
(
2.1
).

If
\Psi=\Phi
, we write
\mathit{M}_{m,\Psi}=\mathit{M}_{m,\Psi,\Phi}
.
If
m
is unbounded,
\mathit{M}_{m,\Psi,\Phi}
is
possibly unbounded and defined only on a suitable domain. Clearly such
operators can be investigated under other assumptions on

\Phi
and
\Psi

[
47
]
.

In the following we consider multipliers for a frame and its canonical dual, strictly positive
symbols
m>0
, and extend this definition in an natural way
to Banach spaces
[
46
]
.

3.
A Crash Course on Localized Frames

3.1.
Matrix Algebras

Underlying the notion of localized frames are spaces of matrices
over the index set
K
that form an
algebra
that is

regular
and
inverse-closed
. We impose the following
assumptions on a Banach
∗
-algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
of matrices over
K
,
where
*
is the usual matrix adjoint:

(ARI1)

Regularity.
Associated to
{\mathcal{A}}
is a class of positive weight functions
W

on
K
, such that

{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\bigcap\limits_{\begin{array}[]{c}p\in[1,\infty]\\
m\in W\end{array}}{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(\ell^{p}_{m}\right)\,.

We assume that
W
contains the constant weight
\mathit{m}\equiv 1
, and that, if
\mathit{m}\in W
, then also
1/\mathit{m}\in W
.

(ARI2)

Inverse-closedness.

{\mathcal{A}}
is inverse-closed in
{\mathcal{B}}(\ell^{2}(K))
, i.e., if
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
A
is invertible on
\ell^{2}(K)
, then also
A{{}^{-1}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

We call a matrix algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
satisfying these conditions an

ARI-algebra
. A weight in
W
is called
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible.

Spectral invariance refers to the phenomenon
that the spectrum of an operator is independent of the space on which
it acts. The prototypical example is a theorem of Wiener on
convolution operators
[
52
]
. In our
context of ARI-algebras, the spectral invariance is a consequence of
the axioms.

Corollary 2

(Spectral invariance)
.

Assume that
A\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
m\in W
. If
A
is invertible on

\ell^{2}
, then
A
is also invertible on
\ell_{m}^{p}
for all
p\in[1,\infty]
.

Furthermore, it can be shown that every ARI-algebra is also pseudo-inverse-closed
[
30
, Thm. 3.4]
.

While the above set of assumptions is a convenient starting point for
the abstract theory of localized frames and lifting theorems, the reader should be aware
that the construction of concrete examples occupies a special place in
Banach algebra theory and in many applications.
The construction of inverse-closed matrix algebras and spectral invariance is an important branch of Banach algebra
theory and analysis, see for instance
[
13
,
39
,
34
]
.

Example 1
.

As a concrete example we briefly discuss the Jaffard algebra of
matrices with off-diagonal decay of polynomial order. In this case the
index set
K
is any uniformly discrete subset
K
of
{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
, i.e.,

|k-k^{\prime}|\geq\delta
for
k\neq k^{\prime},k,k^{\prime}\in K
. By mapping a point
k

to its nearest lattice point in
\delta{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, one may assume without
loss of generality that
K={\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
. The Jaffard algebra
\mathcal{A}_{s}
of decay
s>0

consists of all matrices
A=(a_{kl})_{k,l\in K}
, such that for some
constant
C>0

(3.1)

|a_{kl}|\leq C\,(1+|k-l|)^{-s}\qquad\text{ for all }k,l\in K\,.

If
s>d
, then
\mathcal{A}_{s}
is a Banach algebra with respect to
matrix multiplication. Jaffard’s Theorem
[

asserts that

is inverse-closed in

for

[

,
40
,
50
]
.

Now let

for all

and

is called

Then it is easy to see that every

.

Other examples of ARI-algebras are obtained by imposing different

[
30
]
, e.g.,
|a_{kl}|\leq Ce^{-a|k-l|^{b}},k,l\in K

and

Corollary

{\mathcal{A}}
that is invertible on

\ell^{p}_{m}
.
In some situations one
may want to start with the invertibility on some weighted

-space and deduce the invertibility on all
\ell^{p}_{m}
with
admissible weight.

The following lemma is an important special case.
For its concise formulation of our next result we use the notation

(3.2)

B^{\mu}:={\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)\cdot B\cdot{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)\,

for the weighted matrices that are obtained by conjugation with a diagonal

The following result is elementary, but extremely useful. In

to the unweighted

Let
{\mathcal{A}}

Let

Proof.

The boundedness property of

3.2.
{\mathcal{A}}
-Localized Frames and Coorbit Spaces

The idea of coorbit spaces is to define Banach spaces attached to a given frame by
imposing conditions on the (dual) frame coefficients. Informally,
one can impose a norm

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}}=\Big(\sum_{k\in K}|\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle|m_{k}^{p}\Big)^{1/p}=\|C_{\Psi}f\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}}\,.

As long as
\ell^{p}_{m}\subseteq\ell^{2}
, the frame expansion (
2.2
)
makes sense, and then
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}
, defined as those elements where this norm is
finite, is a subspace of
\mathcal{H}
. If
\ell^{p}_{m}\not\subseteq\ell^{2}
, one
takes an abstract norm completion.

For a meaningful theory of
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
one needs the concept of localized
frames
[
5
,
30
,
32
]
. These are
central in all contexts where frame theory is used to describe
properties of functions and distributions beyond mere Hilbert
space properties
[
32
,
21
,
4
]
.
In this section, we review the concept of self-localized frames from
[
30
]
, see also
[
10
]
for details.

Definition 2
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an ARI-algebra. Two frames
\Psi
and
\Phi

in a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
are said to be mutually
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized, if their cross-Gramian matrix
G_{\Psi,\Phi}

is in

{\mathcal{A}}
. We write
\Psi\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\Phi
.

If
\Psi\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\Psi
, i.e.,
G_{\Psi}\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
then

\Psi
is called an
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame.

We mention that
\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}
is an equivalence relation on the
collection of all
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frames.
Let
\Psi
be a frame with canonical dual frame
{\widetilde{\Psi}}
.
Let
\mathcal{H}^{00}=\mathrm{span}\,\{\psi_{k}:k\in K\}
be the subspace
of finite linear combinations of vectors in
\Psi
.

Definition 3
.

The Banach space
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}(\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}})
is defined as the completion of

\mathcal{H}^{00}
with respect to the norm

\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}}=\left\|C_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}(f)\right\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}}

for
1\leq p<\infty
.

For
p=\infty
we consider two completions. First

\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}
is completion of
\mathcal{H}_{00}
with
respect to the norm
\left\|C_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}(f)\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{m}}
, and second

\mathcal{H}_{m}^{\infty}
is the completion of
\mathcal{H}^{00}
in the weak
∗
-topology
with respect to duality
\sigma(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{H}^{00})
, such that
\left\|f\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{m}^{\infty}}=\left\|C_{\tilde{\psi}}(f)\right\|_{\ell^{\infty}_{m}}
is finite.

Alternatively,

\mathcal{H}_{m}^{\infty}
can be identified with the bidual of

\mathcal{H}_{m}^{0}
. For the subtleties of
this completion we refer to
[
10
]
.

Since
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
is defined by a property of the transform
f\mapsto C_{\Psi}f
, we call
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
a
coorbit
space with respect the
the frame
\Psi
, in analogy to the theory of coorbit spaces with
respect to group representations where the transform is a
representation coefficient
[
27
]
.

The main theorem about localized frames
[
30
]
is the fact that their
canonical dual frames are also localized.

Theorem 4
.

If
\Psi
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized, then so is its canonical dual frame

\tilde{\Psi}
. The frames
\Psi
and
\tilde{\Psi}
are mutually
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized, i.e.,
\Psi\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\Psi

\Longrightarrow

{\widetilde{\Psi}}\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}{\widetilde{\Psi}}
and
{\widetilde{\Psi}}\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\Psi
.

In the language of the Gram matrices this fact is expressed by the
implication

(3.4)

G_{\Psi,\Psi}\in{\mathcal{A}}\quad\Rightarrow\quad G_{\tilde{\Psi},\tilde{\Psi}}\in{\mathcal{A}}\quad\text{ and }\,\,G_{\tilde{\Psi},\Psi}\in{\mathcal{A}}\,.

It follows that the synthesis operator
can be extended to
\ell^{p}_{m}
:
D_{\Psi}
is bounded from

\ell_{m}^{p}
to
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}

for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
p=0
. This follows from
Theorem
4
, because for every
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible weight
m

(3.5)

\|D_{\Psi}c\|_{\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}}=\|C_{\tilde{\Psi}}D_{\Psi}c\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}}=\|G_{\tilde{\Psi},\Psi}c\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}}\leq C\,\|c\|_{\ell^{p}_{m}}\,.

Likewise the analysis operator
C_{\Psi}
can be extended to a
bounded operator from
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
to
\ell^{p}_{m}
for every
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible
weight
m
. As a consequence
[
30
,
10
]
the
frame reconstruction formula

(3.6)

f=\sum\limits_{k}\Big<f,\psi_{k}\Big>\tilde{\psi}_{k}=\sum\limits_{k}\left<f,\tilde{\psi}_{k}\right>\psi_{k},

holds on
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible
weight
m
. The convergence is unconditional for
1\leq p<\infty
and weak-* unconditional convergence for
p=\infty
.

Theorem
4
is based strongly on the assumption that the
Gram matrix of
\Psi
is in an inverse-closed matrix algebra

{\mathcal{A}}
. We consider it our main contribution to have identified those
appropriate abstract conditions on frames (localization and inverse-closedness)
that raise the theory of lifting theorems to this highly abstract level.

Another important consequence of Theorem
4
is the
independence of the coorbit space
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
of the particular frame.

Proposition 5
.

Let
\Phi
and
\Psi
be two frames for
\mathcal{H}
. Assume that

\Psi
is
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized and
\Phi\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}\Psi
. Then the
spaces
\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}(\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}})
,
\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}({\widetilde{\Psi}},\Psi)
, and
\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}(\Phi,{\widetilde{\Phi}})
coincide
and the respective norms are equivalent, i.e.

(3.7)

\left\|f\right\|_{{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}}=\left\|C_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}f\right\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}}\asymp\left\|C_{\Psi}f\right\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}}\asymp\left\|C_{\Phi}f\right\|_{\ell_{m}^{p}}\,.

It therefore makes sense to write unambiguously
{\mathcal{H}}_{m}^{p}:=\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}(\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}})=\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}({\widetilde{\Psi}},\Psi)
. Thus
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m}
depends only on the
\sim_{{\mathcal{A}}}
-equivalence class of a frame.

The duality of the coorbit spaces is inherited from the
\ell^{p}
-spaces: we have
\left(\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}\right)^{\prime}=\mathcal{H}_{1/m}^{p^{\prime}}
for
1\leq p<\infty
with the duality
relation

(3.8)

\left<f,g\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{m}^{p}\times\mathcal{H}_{1/m}^{p^{\prime}}}=\left<C_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}f,C_{\Psi}g\right>_{\ell^{p}_{m}\times\ell^{p^{\prime}}_{1/m}}=\sum_{k\in K}\langle f,\tilde{\psi_{k}}\rangle\,\overline{\langle g,\psi_{k}\rangle}\,.

By this construction also other properties known for the Hilbert space can be transferred to the coorbit spaces, like the fact that
D_{\Psi}^{*}=C_{\Psi}
, and related to that the following result
[
10
]
:

Lemma 6
.

Let
\Psi
be a
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame.
Then the Gram matrix

G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}
is the orthogonal projection from

\ell^{2}
onto the range of
C_{\Psi}
with kernel

\mathrm{ker}\left(D_{\Psi}\right)
. The extension of
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}
is a
bounded projection from
\ell_{m}^{p}
onto the range of

C_{\Psi}\subseteq\ell^{p}_{m}
with kernel

\mathrm{ker}\left(D_{\Psi}\right)\subseteq\ell^{p}_{m}
.
In addition,
\ell_{m}^{p}=\mathsf{ran}\left(C_{\Psi}\right)\oplus\mathrm{ker}\left(D_{\Psi}\right)
.

We will apply the decisive Proposition
3
to the Gram matrix

G_{\Psi}
of a localized frame. For this we need to know that various
weighted Gram matrices also belong to the same matrix algebra, in some sense an extension of (
3.4
).

Proposition 7
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an ARI-algebra, and
\Psi
an
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame, with
G_{\Psi}^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, additionally. Then the following Gram matrices are also in the matrix algebra
{\mathcal{A}}
:

G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}
,
G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}
,
G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{1/\mu}
,
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}
, and
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{1/\mu}
.

Proof.

First we note that the Gram matrix is self-adjoint, therefore
G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}=(G_{\Psi}^{\mu})^{*}
is in
{\mathcal{A}}
(this is where we
use that
{\mathcal{A}}
is a
∗
-algebra).

Note that if
B
has closed range in
\ell^{2}_{\mu}
, so has
B^{\mu}
(in
\ell^{2}
). Indeed consider
c\in\ell^{2}
, then

\left\|B^{\mu}c\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\left\|{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)\cdot B\cdot{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)c\right\|_{\ell^{2}}=\left\|B\cdot{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)c\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\mu}}\geq

\geq m\cdot\left\|{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)c\right\|_{\ell^{2}_{\mu}}=m\cdot\left\|c\right\|_{\ell^{2}}.

Then it is easy to see that the pseudo-inverse of a matrix
B
with
closed range satisfies

{\left(B^{\mu}\right)}^{\dagger}={\left(B^{\dagger}\right)}^{\mu}
.
We know that
{\mathcal{A}}
is pseudo-inverse-closed
[
30
, Thm. 3.4]
, and therefore if
B^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
then also
\left(B^{\mu}\right)^{\dagger}
. In particular we have
\left(G_{\Psi}^{\mu}\right)^{\dagger}=\left(G_{\Psi}^{\dagger}\right)^{\mu}=G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
Since

G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}=G_{\Psi}^{\dagger}G_{\Psi}
by
(
2.4
), we obtain
that
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}=\big(G_{\Psi}^{\dagger}\big)^{\mu}G_{\Psi}^{\mu}
is also in
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Since
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{1/\mu}=\left(G_{{\widetilde{\Psi}},\Psi}^{\mu}\right)^{*}=\left(G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}\right)^{*}
we are done.

∎

Let

Invertibility of operators on coorbit spaces.

The factorization

Let

4.
Lifting Properties for Localized Multipliers

After setting up the theory of localized frames and matrix algebras,
we can finally provide a rigorous formulation of our main result and
prove
the lifting theorem for coorbit spaces.

Theorem 11

(Abstract lifting theorem for coorbit
spaces)
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an ARI-algebra and

\mu
,
m\sqrt{\mu}
and

\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}
be
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible weights.
Let
\Psi
be
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame satisfying the additional
conditions that

G_{\Psi}^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

Then the frame multiplier

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
.
Likewise,
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
.

The remainder of this section is devoted to the proof of the main
theorem, which we split into lemmas.

4.1.
Boundedness of the Multiplier

We first formulate the mapping properties of frame multipliers on
coorbit spaces. Throughout we assume that
{\mathcal{A}}
is an ARI-algebra.
For this setting one can go beyond the bounded symbol setting
[
6
]
to get bounded operators.

Lemma 12
.

Let
\Psi
and
\Phi
be
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frames.
Let
{\mu}
and
m
be weights such that both
{m\sqrt{\mu}}
and
{m/\sqrt{\mu}}
are
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible.
Then for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
p=0

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Phi,\Psi}\in{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p},\mathcal{H}_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p}\right)\text{ and }\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Phi,\Psi}\in{{\mathcal{B}}}\left(\mathcal{H}_{\\
frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p},\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}\right).

Proof.

Recall that the frame multiplier factorizes as

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Phi,\Psi}=D_{\Phi}~{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)~C_{\Psi}
. Since

{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)
is an isomorphism from

\ell_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
onto

\ell_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p}
by
Lemma
1
, and since both
D_{\Phi}
and

C_{\Psi}
are bounded by (
3.5
), we obtain the result.
∎

4.2.
Coercivity of the Multiplier

For the next step we use a coercivity argument that is familiar from the theory of
elliptic partial differential operators
[
24
]
. In the
context of time-frequency analysis a similar argument was used
in
[
37
]
.

Theorem 13
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be an ARI-algebra.
Let
\sqrt{\mu}
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible weight and
\Psi
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame.
Then the frame multiplier

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}:\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}

is an isomorphism.

Likewise,
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
is an isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
12
with
m\equiv 1
, the frame multiplier
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}:\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
is
bounded. As in the case of elliptic differential operators we
introduce the sesquilinear form

[f,g]\mathrel{\mathrel{\mathop{:}}=}\langle\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}

for
f,g\in\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
.
By the duality relation (
3.8
), the dual space of

\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\sqrt{\mu}}
is
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}
, consequently
both brackets above are
well-defined.
Therefore
we obtain

\left[f,f\right]=\left<\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f,f\right>_{\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}=\left<{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)C_{\Psi}f,C_{\Psi}f\right>_{\ell_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2},\ell_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}

=\sum\limits_{k}\mu_{k}\left(C_{\Psi}f\right)_{k}\overline{\left(C_{\Psi}f\right)_{k}}=\left\|C_{\Psi}f\right\|_{\ell_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}^{2}\,.

Note that the
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{{\sqrt{\mu}}}\,
-norm of
f
is defined by

\|C_{\widetilde{\Psi}}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}
with respect to the dual
frame. By the main theorem of localized frames
(Proposition
5
) this norm is equivalent to
\|C_{\Psi}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}
. Thus we conclude that

[f,f]=\|C_{\Psi}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:eqnorm1}}}{{\asymp}}\|C_{\widetilde{\Psi}}f\|_{\ell^{2}_{\sqrt{\mu}}}=\|f\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}\,.

Consequently, if
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f=0
in
\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
, then
C_{\Psi}f=0
, and therefore
f=0
. So
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is injective.

For a converse inequality, we observe that for
f\not=0

\displaystyle\|\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\|g\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}=1}\big|\langle\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f,g\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\geq\ \big|\Big\langle\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}f,\frac{f}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}}\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\times\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}\big|=\frac{[f,f]}{\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}}\asymp\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}}\,.

Consequently,
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}:\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}\rightarrow\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
has
closed range. By the closed range theorem, its adjoint operator

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}^{*}=\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is therefore onto. It
follows that
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is an isomorphism between
\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1/\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
, as claimed.
∎

4.2.1.
The Galerkin Matrix of
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}

As an easy consequence of Theorem
13
, the composition of
these two frame multipliers gives an isomorphism of the weighted
Banach spaces into itself.

Corollary 14
.

Let

\Psi
be a
{\mathcal{A}}
-localized frame and

\sqrt{\mu}
be an
{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible weight.

(i) The composition
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is an
isomorphism on
\mathcal{H}_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
. Likewise,

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
is an isomorphism on
\mathcal{H}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{2}
.

(ii) The matrix associated to
\mathit{M}_{\frac{1}{\mu},\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle{M}^{\left(\Psi,\Psi\right)}\left(\mathit{M}_{\frac{1}{\mu},\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\right)=G_{\Psi}G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}G_{\Psi}.

Proof.

(i) follows from the mapping properties of
Lemma
12
and from Theorem
13
.

(ii) The formula of its matrix representation is given by

(4.2)

\displaystyle{M}^{\left(\Psi,\Psi\right)}\left(\mathit{M}_{\frac{1}{\mu},\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\Psi}\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}D_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{\Psi}D_{\Psi}{\mathrm{diag}}\left(1/\mu\right)C_{\Psi}D_{\Psi}{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)C_{\Psi}D_{\Psi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\Psi}G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}G_{\Psi}.

∎

4.3.
Proof of the General Lifting Theorem

We finally prove the main theorem and show that the frame multiplier

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is a (Banach space) isomorphism from
\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
onto
\mathcal{H}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
whenever all occurring weights are

{\mathcal{A}}
-admissible.

Proof of Theorem
11
.

(i) We consider the matrix

(4.3)

B={M}^{\left(\Psi,\Psi\right)}\left(\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\right)+\left(\mathsf{id}-G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}\right)\,.

Since
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}^{2}_{\mu}
by
Corollary
14
, we obtain from Lemma
9
that

B
is an isomorphism on
\ell_{\sqrt{\mu}}^{2}
.

(ii) Next we check that
B\in{\mathcal{A}}
. Since by assumption
G_{\Psi}
and
G_{\Psi}^{\mu}
are in
{\mathcal{A}}
,
Proposition
7
implies that also
G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
and
G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}\in{\mathcal{A}}
. The
factorization (
4.1
) now
implies that
B\in{\mathcal{A}}
.

(iii) We need to show that also
B^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
.
A small computation shows that

\displaystyle B^{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)\cdot B\cdot{\mathrm{diag}}\left(1/\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)\left(G_{\Psi}G_{\Psi}^{1/\mu}G_{\Psi}+\left(\mathsf{id}-G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}\right)\right){\mathrm{diag}}\left(1/\mu\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle G_{\Psi}^{\mu}G_{\Psi}G_{\Psi}^{\mu}+\mathsf{id}-G_{\Psi,{\widetilde{\Psi}}}^{\mu}\,.

From Proposition
7
it follows that
B^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
as well.

(iv) Since
B
is invertible on
\ell^{2}_{\mu}
and

B^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}
, Proposition
3
implies
that
B
is (boundedly) invertible on all
\ell^{p}_{\mu w}
for
1\leq p\leq\infty
and
w\in W
.
By assumption this includes the space
\ell^{p}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
9
again,
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is invertible on
\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
.
Consequently,
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is one-to-one on
\mathcal{H}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}
.

(v) We now replace
\mu
by
1/\mu
and repeat the argument for

\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
. We conclude the invertibility
of
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
and consequently
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is surjective onto

\mathcal{H}_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p}
, and the isomorphism
property of
\mathit{M}_{\mu,\Psi}
is shown.

The preceding arguments show that
\mathit{M}_{1/\mu,\Psi}
is
one-to-one and onto and thus an isomorphism as well.
∎

5.
Gabor Multipliers and Toeplitz Operators

In this section we
make explicit what the abstract lifting theorems says
for Gabor frames and Toeplitz operators on Fock space. In both
examples we look at a class of structured frames, then explain
the meaning of localization, describe the associated coorbit space, and finally formulate a concrete version of the lifting
theorem. By choosing particular inverse-closed matrix algebras from
the literature, one can formulate many new and interesting lifting
theorems for Gabor multipliers and Toeplitz operators. We leave this
exercise to the reader, as they are already contained in Theorem
11
.

5.1.
Gabor Multipliers

We consider Gabor frames since these were one of the motivations for the
introduction of localized frames. Gabor frames are structured frames
that are generated by time-frequency shifts of a single function.
As usual,
for
x,\omega,t\in{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
and
\lambda=(x,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
,
the
time-frequency shift

\pi\left(\lambda\right)
is
defined by
\pi(\lambda)f(t)=e^{2\pi i\omega t}f(t-x)
.

A
Gabor frame
is a frame consisting of time-frequency shifts
\Psi=\left\{\pi(\lambda)g:\lambda\in\Lambda\right\}
. Often
\Lambda
is
taken to be a lattice in
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
, but for the lifting theorems we
only need to assume that

\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
is relatively separated.

The frame multipliers corresponding to a Gabor frame are called Gabor
multipliers and have already been studied in the context of time-frequency analysis
[
28
,
14
,
8
]
. Given a symbol
\mu
on
\Lambda
and a window

g
, the
associated multiplier is defined as

m_{\mu}f=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu_{\lambda}\,\langle{f},{\pi(\lambda)g}\rangle\,\pi(\lambda)g\,.

To describe the localization of a Gabor frame operator we use the
Jaffard algebra of Example
1
and impose a condition of
polynomial off-diagonal decay on the Gram matrix. Using the short-time Fourier transform,
one can find condition such that the Gram matrix
G
belongs to the Jaffard
algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
, i.e. for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda

(5.1)

\displaystyle|G_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}|=|\langle\pi(\lambda^{\prime})g,\pi(\lambda)g\rangle|=|\langle g,\pi(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})\rangle|\leq C(1+|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|)^{-s}\,\,.

We have already seen that the class of
t
-moderate weights, i.e.,

m
satisfies
m(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})\leq C(1+|\lambda|)^{t}m(\lambda^{\prime})
for all
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda
, is
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}

admissible for
t<s-2d
(note that
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
).

The coorbit spaces associated to a Gabor frame can be identified with
the class of modulation spaces and possess a more explicit
description. To describe these spaces, we define the
short-time Fourier
transformation
of a function
f
with respect to a non-zero
window

function
g\in L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)
to be

V_{g}f(x,\omega)=\left<f,\pi(\lambda)g\right>_{L^{2}\left({\mathbb{R}^{d}}\right)}=\int\limits_{{\mathbb{R}^{d}}}f(t)\overline{g(t-x)}e^{-2\pi i\omega t}\,dt\,.

Then the modulation spaces are defined as follows.

Definition 4
.

Fix a non-zero window
g\in{\mathcal{S}}
, a
t
-moderate weight
function
m
on
{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}
for some
t>0
and
1\leq p\leq\infty
. Then the
modulation space

M_{m}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
consists of all tempered distributions
f\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
such that
V_{g}f\in{L^{p}_{m}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}
. The norm on
M_{m}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
is

\left\|f\right\|_{M_{m}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}=\left\|V_{g}f\right\|_{{L^{p}_{m}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})}}

It can be shown
[
31
]
that the spaces defined above do not depend on the
special choice of the non-zero test function
g
, as long as it is
sufficiently well concentrated in the time frequency sense. Different
functions define the same space with equivalent norms. Moreover these
functions spaces are Banach spaces that are invariant under time-frequency
shifts
[
31
]
.
Note that the Gram matrix
G
of a Gabor frame is in the Jaffard algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
- see (
5.1
) - if and only if the window
g
is in the modulation space
M^{\infty}_{w_{s}}
for the weight function
w_{s}(z)=(1+|z|)^{s}
. Thus
in time-frequency analysis properties of the Gram matrix can be translated directly
into properties of the underlying window
g
.

As a first application of localized frames in
[
30
]
, the coorbit spaces with respect
to a Gabor frame
\{\pi(\lambda)g:\lambda\in\Lambda\}

was shown to be identical to a modulation space. Precisely,
M_{m}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})=\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{m}}^{p}
, where
\tilde{m}
is the restriction of the
weight
m
to
\Lambda
.
So our lifting theorem leads to the following result in the context of
time-frequency analysis.

Theorem 16
.

Fix
s>2d
and let
a>2s-2d
. Let
g\in M_{w_{a}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
and assume that
\Lambda\subseteq{\mathbb{R}^{d}}
be a relatively separated set, such that
\left\{\pi(\lambda)g\right\}
is a Gabor frame.
Assume that
\mu,m\sqrt{\mu}
, and
m/\sqrt{\mu}
are

t
-moderate weights for
t<s-2d
.

Then, for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Gabor multiplier

m_{\mu}
is an isomorphism from
M_{m\sqrt{\mu}}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})

onto
M_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}^{p}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
.

Proof.

The underlying ARI-algebra is the Jaffard algebra
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
discussed
in Example
1
. As the class of
{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
-admissible
weights we take
all
t
-moderate weights with
t<s-2d
, whence comes the
assumption on the weights.

If
g\in M_{w_{a}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}^{d}})
, then the Gram matrix of the frame
satisfies
[
30
]

|G_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}|=|V_{g}g(\lambda-\lambda^{\prime})|\leq C(1+|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|)^{-a}\leq C(1+|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|)^{-s}\,\quad\forall\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda\,,

and consequently
G\in{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
. In view of
Theorem
11
we also need that
G^{\mu}={\mathrm{diag}}\left(\mu\right)\,\,G\,\,{\mathrm{diag}}\left(\tfrac{1}{\mu}\right)\,\in{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
. Note that
G^{\mu}
has the entries
\frac{\mu_{\lambda}}{\mu_{\lambda^{\prime}}}G_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}

and that
\mu_{\lambda}\leq(1+|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|)^{t}\,\mu_{\lambda^{\prime}}
because
\mu
is
t
-moderate. Therefore

\frac{\mu_{\lambda}}{\mu_{\lambda^{\prime}}}\,|G_{\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}}|\leq(1+|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|)^{t-a}\qquad\text{ for all }\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\Lambda\,.

Since
a>2s-2d>s+t
and thus
t-a<-s
by assumption, we
conclude that
G^{\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
and
G^{1/\mu}\in{\mathcal{A}}_{s}
.

We have verified all hypothesis of Theorem
11
and
conclude that the Gabor multiplier
m_{\mu}
maps the
space
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}=M^{p}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}({\mathbb{R}^{2d}})
bijectively
to
\mathcal{H}^{p}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}=M^{p}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}
.
∎

Discussion of the literature.
It is not difficult to see and
was already proved by Feichtinger
[
25
]
that for pure frequency weights

m(x,\omega)=(1+|\omega|)^{s}
, the lifting operator is the
differentiation operator.
A new generation of lifting theorems covers the class of general
modulation spaces
M_{m}^{p,q}
with a weight function
m
depending on both
time and frequency.
In this case, different
modulation spaces are related by
time-frequency multipliers
, or, in a different
terminology, by Toeplitz operators with respect to the short-time Fourier transform. These operators are parametrized by a
symbol
m
depending on time
and
frequency and are much more
complicated than Fourier multipliers. Formally, they are defined
by the vector-valued integral

(5.2)

A_{\mu}^{g}f=\int_{{\mathbb{R}^{2d}}}\mu(z)V_{g}f(z)\,\pi(z)g\,dz\,.

Generically, the lifting theorems for
general modulation spaces assert that the short-time Fourier transform multiplier
A^{g}_{\mu}
is an
isomorphism from
M^{p,q}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}
onto

M^{p,q}_{m/\sqrt{\mu}}
. The
proofs require a heavy dose of
time-frequency analysis, pseudodifferential operator theory, and abstract Banach algebra
arguments. Various levels of generality of this lifting theorem
are recorded ranging from weights of
polynomial growth to weights (sub)exponential growth
[
1
,
15
,
36
,
37
]

Theorem
16
adds a further facet to these lifting
theorems in that the
function

\mu
in (
5.2
) is replaced by a Radon
measure
\mu=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu_{\lambda}\delta_{\lambda}
. Theorem
16
could possibly be proved by
adding more technicalities to the methods
in
[
36
,
37
]
, whereas
in our set-up the result is a direct consequence of the abstract
lifting theorem with localized frames. Extending the result in the current paper to the continuous setting as mentioned in Rem
15
would allow to directly show the lifting theorem for the Toeplitz operator
A_{\mu}^{g}
with the techniques used in here.

5.2.
Fock Spaces

Fock spaces are Banach spaces of entire functions satisfying certain
integrability conditions. Given a non-negative weight function
m
on

\mathbb{C}
, the Fock space
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{m}
consists of all entire functions
with finite norm

\|F\|_{{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{m}}=\Big(\int_{\mathbb{C}}|F(z)|^{p}m(z)^{p}e^{-\pi p|z|^{2}/2}\,dz\Big)^{1/p}\,.

For
p=2
and
m\equiv 1
,
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert
space with the normalized reproducing kernel
[
29
,
53
]

\psi_{\lambda}(z)=e^{\pi\bar{\lambda}z}e^{-\pi|z|^{2}/2}\,,

so that
f(\lambda)^{-\pi|\lambda|^{2}/2}=\langle f,\psi_{\lambda}\rangle_{{\mathcal{F}}^{2}}
.
The frames of reproducing kernels in
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
are completely
characterized by their density. The Theorem of
Lyubarskii-Seip-Wallstén
[
41
,
49
]
asserts that
{
\psi_{\lambda}:\lambda\in\Lambda\}
is a frame for
{\mathcal{F}}^{2}
, if and only if
\Lambda
contains a relatively separated subset
\Lambda^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
with lower
Beurling
D^{-}(\Lambda^{\prime})=\lim_{r\to\infty}\inf_{z\in\mathbb{C}}\frac{\mathrm{card}\,\Lambda\cap B_{r}(z)}{\pi r^{2}}>1
.
Since

|\langle\psi_{\lambda^{\prime}},\psi_{\lambda}\rangle|=|e^{\pi\bar{\lambda^{\prime}}\lambda}|e^{-\pi(|\lambda|^{2}+|\lambda^{\prime}|^{2})/2}=e^{-\pi|\lambda-\lambda^{\prime}|^{2}/2}

has Gaussian decay, the Gramian
G
of a frame of reproducing
kernels
\{\psi_{\lambda}\}
is contained in every known matrix
algebra defined by off-diagonal decay. We can therefore use arbitrary
moderate weights of subexponential decay, i.e., for some
C,\alpha>0

and
0<\beta<1
, we have

(5.3)

m(\lambda+\lambda^{\prime})\leq Ce^{\alpha|z|^{\beta}}m(\lambda^{\prime})\qquad\text{ for all }\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathbb{C}\,.

In this context a frame multiplier with multiplier sequence
\mu=(\mu_{\lambda})_{\lambda\in\Lambda}
takes the form

M_{\mu}F(z)=\sum\limits_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\mu_{\lambda}\,F(\lambda)e^{\pi\bar{\lambda}z}\,e^{-\pi\left|\lambda\right|^{2}}\,.

As in the case of time-frequency multipliers we note that
M_{\mu}
can
be read as a Toeplitz operator on Fock space whose symbol is a Radon
measure in place of a nice function.

For this example the abstract lifting theorem
(Theorem
11
) takes the following incarnation.

Theorem 17
.

Assume that
\mu
is moderate as in (
5.3
) and that

\Lambda\subseteq\mathbb{C}
is relatively separated such that

D^{-}(\Lambda)>1
. Then then
M_{\mu}
is an isomorphism from

{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{m\sqrt{\mu}}
to
{\mathcal{F}}^{p}_{\frac{m}{\sqrt{\mu}}}
.

This result seems to be new in
complex analysis. For multivariate versions one must assume that the
reproducing kernels
\{\psi_{\lambda}\}
form a frame, but there is
no characterization via the density.

Abstract
. We formulate a local smoothing conjecture for bilinear Fourier integral operators in every dimension d ≥ 2 , d\geq 2, derived from the celebrated linear case due to Sogge, which we refer to as the bilinear smoothing conjecture . We show that the linear local smoothing conjecture implies this bilinear version. As a consequence of our approach and due to the recent progress on the subject, we establish local smoothing estimates for Fourier integral operators in dimension d = 2 , d=2, that is, on ℝ x 2 × ℝ t \mathbb{R}^{2}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t} . Also, a partial progress is presented for the high-dimensional case d ≥ 3 . d\geq 3. In particular, our method allows us to deduce that the bilinear local smoothing conjecture holds for all odd dimensions d d . Keywords. Local smoothing conjecture, bilinear Fourier integral operators, bilinear smoothing conjecture, cinematic curvature condition, wave equation. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 1.
Introduction

This note proposes a conjecture on local smoothing estimates for bilinear Fourier integral operators (FIOs) derived from the linear case observed by Sogge
[
52
]
. Recent progress in the field (see
[
4
,
26
]
) allows our methods to fully prove the conjecture for
d=2
and to advance the problem partially for
d\geq 3
. In particular, we deduce that the bilinear local smoothing conjecture is true when
d
is odd. We first recall the linear local smoothing conjecture
[
52
]
and then introduce its bilinear form. Our main result is Theorem
2.6
; our approach investigates the smoothing properties of high and of low frequencies of a bilinear Fourier integral operator. For the analysis of the high frequency portion, among other things, we make use of a boundedness theorem for maximal functions proved by Bourgain in
[
5
]
.

According to Hörmander
[
30
]
, a Fourier integral operator
T
of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
and with real phase function
\phi,
is a continuous linear operator
T:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})
of the form

T_{a}^{\phi}f(x,t)=\smallint\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{i\phi(x,t,\xi)}a(x,t,\xi)\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi,

(1.1)

where
\widehat{f}
is the Fourier transform of
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
The symbol
a:=a(x,t,\xi)
is of Kohn-Nirenberg order
m\in\mathbb{R}^{d},
that is
a
satisfies the symbol inequalities

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\forall\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,\,|\partial_{x}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha}a(x,t,\xi)|\leq C_{\alpha,\beta}(1+|\xi|)^{m-|\alpha|}.

(1.2)

The symbol
a
has compact support in
(x,t).
We assume that in an open neighbourhood
V
of
\pi(\textnormal{supp}(a)):=\{(x,t):(x,t,\xi)\in\textnormal{supp}(a)\},
(with
\overline{V}
being a compact set) the real-valued phase function
\phi
is homogeneous of degree
1
in
\xi\neq 0,
smooth in
(x,t,\xi)\in{V}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),
and satisfies the non-degeneracy condition

\forall(x,t,\xi)\in\overline{V}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}),\,\textnormal{det}\left((\partial_{x,\xi}^{2}\phi(x,t,\eta))\right)_{1\leq i,j\leq d}\neq 0.

(1.3)

The corresponding
L^{p}
-theory for these operators can be summarised as follows, see Tao
[
54
]
and Seeger, Sogge and Stein
[
51
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{R}
be fixed in the support of the symbol
a:=a(x,t,\xi)
. Let
T=T_{a(\cdot,t,\cdot)}^{\phi(\cdot,t,\cdot)}
be a Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
. Then:

1.

If
m=-(d-1)/2,

T
is of weak
(1,1)
type:

\exists C_{t}>0,\forall f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\,\|Tf\|_{L^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\leq C_{t}\|f\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}.

(1.4)

2.

Let
1<p<\infty.
Let
T
be a Fourier integral operator of order
m=-(d-1)|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|.
Then
T
is bounded from
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
to
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}).
When
p=1,
then
T
is bounded from the Hardy space
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}).
As in (
1.4
), these operator norms depend on
t.

The case
p=2
in Theorem
1.1
was proved by Duistermaat and Hörmander
[
21
]
, see also Èskin
[
22
]
. Other references related to the global boundedness of Fourier integral operators are presented in Section
5
. For now, we emphasise that, in terms of the
L^{p}
-regularity, Theorem
1.1
shows that Fourier integral operators lose
s=(d-1)|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|
derivatives. In other words, on the scale of Sobolev spaces
L^{p}_{s},
a Fourier integral operator
T=T_{a(\cdot,t,\cdot)}^{\phi(\cdot,t,\cdot)}
of order
m\in\mathbb{R},
satisfies the time-dependent estimate

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\lesssim_{t}\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s+m+(d-1)|\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}|}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})},\,1<p<\infty.

(1.5)

It was first observed by Sogge
[
52
]
, in the context of the wave equation, that averaging in time on the left-hand side of (
1.5
) allows one to improve the loss of regularity. This observation led to the formulation of the (linear)
local smoothing conjecture
for the wave operator. If true, this conjecture would represent a remarkable breakthrough, as it formally implies several other major open problems in harmonic analysis, including the Bochner–Riesz, Fourier restriction, and Kakeya conjectures, see Tao
[
53
]
.

In the setting of Fourier integral operators, the main assumption on the phase to formulate the linear local smoothing conjecture is the following
cinematic curvature condition
.

Definition 1.2

(Cinematic curvature condition)
.

We say that
\phi:=\phi(x,t,\xi)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}))
satisfies the
cinematic curvature condition
on a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
if the following conditions are satisfied:

•

(i) the phase
\phi
is non-degenerate, that is

\textnormal{rank}(\partial_{x,\eta}^{2}\phi(x,t,\eta))=d,\quad(x,t)\in\mathcal{K},\quad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.6)

•

(ii) Consider the Gauss mapping
G:\mathcal{K}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\})\rightarrow\mathbb{S}^{d},
where

G(x,t,\eta)=\frac{G_{0}(x,t,\eta)}{|G_{0}(x,t,\eta)|},

and

G_{0}(x,t,\eta):=\wedge_{1\leq j\leq d}\partial_{\eta_{j}}\partial_{x,t}\phi(x,t,\eta).

The following rank condition

\textnormal{rank}(\partial_{\eta\eta}^{2}\langle\partial_{x,t}\phi(x,t,\eta),G(z,\eta_{0})\rangle|_{\eta=\eta_{0}})=d-1,

(1.7)

holds for every
(x,t,\eta_{0})\in\mathcal{K}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},

\eta_{0}\neq 0.

Adapted to Fourier integral operators, the linear
smoothing conjecture
can be stated as follows. In order to formulate it we use the following notation for its critical index:

\overline{p}_{d}:=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},\textnormal{ if }\,d\textnormal{ is odd, and, }\overline{p}_{d}:=\frac{2(d+2)}{d},\textnormal{ if }\,d\textnormal{ is even}.

(1.8)

Conjecture 1.3

(Linear smoothing conjecture for FIOs)
.

Let
d\geq 2.
Let
T_{a}^{\phi}
be a Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R},
and with non-degenerate phase function satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
, see Definition
1.2

p\geq\overline{p}_{d},
and
\sigma<\frac{1}{p},
we have that

\|T_{a}^{\phi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,s=m+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma.

(1.9)

Let us introduce the required notation to formulate the bilinear counterpart to Conjecture
1.3
by adopting the notion of bilinear Fourier integral operators as studied e.g. by Grafakos and Peloso
[
27
]
, and for the form of the operators formulated here, see also Rodríguez-López, Rule, and Staubach
[
33
]
.

Let
a\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\eta})
be a
bilinear symbol
. We say that
a
has order
m\in\mathbb{R},
if it satisfies the following estimates

|\partial_{x,t}^{\beta}\partial_{\xi}^{\alpha_{1}}\partial_{\eta}^{\alpha_{2}}a(x,t,\xi,\eta)|\lesssim_{\beta,\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}}(1+|\xi|+|\eta|)^{m-|\alpha_{1}|-|\alpha_{2}|}.

(1.10)

The Kohn-Nirenberg bilinear class
S^{m}_{1,0}:=S^{m}_{1,0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\xi}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}_{\eta})
is the set formed by those bilinear symbols of order
m.

Let us consider the
bilinear Fourier integral operator

\displaystyle T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)(x,t)=\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i(\phi_{1}(x,t,\xi)+\phi_{2}(x,t,\eta))}a(x,t,\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\xi d\eta,

(1.11)

where the symbol
a
is compactly supported in
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R},
and each phase function
\phi_{j}(x,t,\xi)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}\times(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}))
is positively homogeneous of order
1
in
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\},
and they satisfy the
cinematic curvature condition
, see Definition
1.2
.

Here, we will denote by
\mathcal{I}=[a,b]
an arbitrary non-empty finite interval and then
-\infty<a<b<\infty.
To reduce a little bit our analysis, since the symbol
a:=a(x,t,\xi,\eta)
is assumed to be compactly supported in
(x,t),
and without loss of generality we will choose a compact set
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d},
such that,

(x,t)-\textnormal{supp}(a):=\{(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:(\tilde{x},\tilde{t})\in\textnormal{supp}(a)\}\subset K\times\mathcal{I},

(1.12)

increasing the length of
\mathcal{I}
if needed. It becomes clear that

\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}=\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(K\times\mathcal{I})}.

This fact will not be used in our formulation of the bilinear smoothing conjecture but will be crucial in our further analysis.
Based on the linear smoothing conjecture for Fourier integral operators, it is natural to suppose that the following result holds in any dimension
d\geq 2
.

Conjecture 1.5

(Bilinear smoothing conjecture for FIOs)
.

Let
d\geq 2.
For any
p_{1},p_{2}\geq\overline{p}_{d},
let
\sigma_{1}<\frac{1}{p_{1}},\,\sigma_{2}<\frac{1}{p_{2}}.
Let
T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}
be a bilinear Fourier integral operator, with non-degenerate phase functions satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
. Let
a\in S^{m}_{1,0}
be its symbol, having order
m<0,
and being compactly supported in
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}.
Then

\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.13)

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad s_{i}=m_{i}+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma_{i},\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2},\quad m_{1}\leq 0,m_{2}<0.

(1.14)

Remark 1.6
.

Observe that in (
1.14
) one has that

\boxed{p\geq\frac{\overline{p}_{d}}{2}}.
Note that there is no loss of generality in assuming that
m<0
in Conjecture
1.3
, see Remark
2.5
. Moreover,
note that if
a(x,t,\xi,\eta)
has compact support in
\xi,
there is no restriction in assuming that
m_{2}<0
has arbitrarily large absolute value.
Conversely, if
a(x,t,\xi,\eta)
has compact support in
\eta,
there is no restriction in assuming that
m_{1}<0
has arbitrarily large absolute value.

Analysing the relationship between the two conjectures above is the main goal of this work. Indeed, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.7
.

The linear smoothing Conjecture
1.3
implies the bilinear smoothing Conjecture
1.5
.

The proof of Theorem
1.7
will be deduced from a more qualitative version of it, see Theorem
2.6
, which in particular will prove that any progress in the linear smoothing conjecture for FIOs implies a counterpart for bilinear FIOs in any dimension
d\geq 2.

The local smoothing conjecture has been verified in dimension
d=2
by Guth, Wang, and Zhang
[
28
]
for solutions of the wave equation. Moreover, since the linear smoothing conjecture for FIOs has been proved in dimension
d=2
by Gao, Liu, Miao, and Xi
[
26
]
, and for odd
d
by Beltran, Hickman, and Sogge
[
4
]
, their results, combined with Theorem
1.7
, imply:

Corollary 1.8
.

For
d=2
the bilinear local smoothing conjecture holds. In other words, for any
p_{1},p_{2}\geq 4,
let
\sigma_{1}<\frac{1}{p_{1}},\,\sigma_{2}<\frac{1}{p_{2}}.
Let
a\in S^{m}_{1,0}
be a symbol of order
m<0,
being compactly supported in
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\times\mathbb{R}.
Then

\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})},

(1.15)

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad s_{i}=m_{i}+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}-\sigma_{i},\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2},\quad m_{1}\leq 0,m_{2}<0.

(1.16)

On the other hand, if
d
is odd, the bilinear local smoothing conjecture holds. This means that for any

p_{1},p_{2}\geq\overline{p}_{d}:=\frac{2(d+1)}{d-1},

and for every
\sigma_{1}<\frac{1}{p_{1}},\,\sigma_{2}<\frac{1}{p_{2}},
an operator
T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}
having order
m<0,
with non-degenerate phase functions satisfying the cinematic curvature condition, satisfies

\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(1.17)

where

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad s_{i}=m_{i}+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma_{i},\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2},\quad m_{1}\leq 0,m_{2}<0.

(1.18)

There is no sense of thinking about a calculus of bilinear FIOs, however, each bilinear FIO has encoded its own
calculus
of linear FIOs when investigating its inner structure
[
44
]
.
We will adopt in our further analysis the structural properties introduced by Rodríguez-López, Rule, and Staubach in
[
44
]
for bilinear Fourier integral operators. These structural properties serve as a substitute for the calculus of Fourier integral operators due to Hörmander
[
30
]
in this setting. A natural question is to what extent a bilinear FIO is approximately the composition, (or the product) of two linear FIOs. Indeed, unless the bilinear symbol of an operator has compact support in one of the two frequency variables
(\xi,\eta)
a bilinear FIO cannot be written as the
composition
of two linear FIOs. Their method consists of making a careful continuous decomposition of the high frequencies of a bilinear FIO
T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}.
Roughly explaining, it expresses the high frequency portion of the operator as a sum of two operators of the form (see (46) of
[
44
, Page 18]
)

\tilde{T}^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{\tilde{a}}(\mu(D)f,\mu(D)g)(x,s)=\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}\smallint\smallint T^{\phi_{1}}_{\nu_{1}^{t,u}}f(x,s)T^{\phi_{2}}_{\mu_{1}^{t,v}}g(x,s)\frac{m(t,x,s,U)}{(1+|U|^{2})^{N}}dudv\frac{dt}{t},

(1.19)

where
|U|^{2}=|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}.

The operators
T^{\phi_{1}}_{\nu_{1}^{t,u}}T^{\phi_{2}}_{\mu_{1}^{t,v}}
are approximations of linear Fourier
integral operators. In particular, the authors in
[
44
]
have used this representation to establish a bilinear extension of the celebrated Seeger-Sogge-Stein theorem
[
51
]
.

In view of the linear smoothing property for FIOs proved in dimension
d=2
by Gao, Liu, Miao, and Xi
[
26
]
, it is of interest for us to ask whether or not the methods in
[
44
]
can be applied to smoothing estimates in the bilinear context considering the heavy machinery used in their analysis from various parts of harmonic analysis; see, for instance the discussion in
[
44
, Page 3]
.

One of the main differences of bilinear smoothing estimates compared with the analysis for time-dependent boundedness theorems as in
[
44
,
51
]
, came from the fact that we cannot make use of end-point estimates at infinity involving the BMO space, or at
p=1,
and then involving the Hardy space
H^{1}.
In our case for the range of values
p_{1},p_{2}\geq\overline{p}_{d},
we have to deal entirely with the
L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
-estimate. Making use of square-maximal functions as in
[
44
]
we deal with the complexity of working with the direct
L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}\times L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}\rightarrow L^{p}
-boundedness theorem by applying in a certain portion of the continuous decomposition of the operator, a well known theorem for estimations of maximal functions, due to Bourgain
[
5
,
6
]
, see Theorem
4.2
; in comparison with the previous methods in the literature this is the main novelty of this paper.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we present our main result (see Theorem
2.6
) about smoothing estimates for bilinear FIOs. Section
5
is dedicated to providing preliminaries about the inner structure of these operators. Section
4
is dedicated to the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

2.
Smoothing Estimates for Bilinear FIOs

We formulate our main result in Theorem
2.6
presented below. First, we introduce two necessary definitions. Throughout, we denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
the Schwartz class on
\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
d\geq 2,
and let
2\leq p^{*}\leq\infty.
Let
T_{a}^{\phi}
be a Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R},
and with non-degenerate phase function satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
, see Definition
1.2
. If for a fixed Lebesgue index
p,
with
p\geq p^{*},
there exists
s=s(p,m,d)
such that

\forall f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\|T_{a}^{\phi}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.1)

we say that
T
satisfies the linear
local smoothing property
(LLSP) with parameters
(p^{*},p,s).

Remark 2.2
.

Note that the local smoothing conjecture claims that any Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R}
with phase satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
should hold the
local smoothing property
(LLSP) with parameters
(\overline{p}_{d},p,s),
where

s=m+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma

and
\sigma<\frac{1}{p}.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 2,
and let
2\leq p^{*}\leq\infty.
Let
T_{a}^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}
be a bilinear Fourier integral operator of order
m\in\mathbb{R},
and with non-degenerate phase functions
\phi_{1},\phi_{2},
satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
. If for a fixed pair
(p_{1},p_{2}),
with
p_{1},p_{2}\geq p^{*},
there exist
s_{1}=s(p_{1},m,d)
and
s_{2}=s(p_{2},m,d)
such that

\forall f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,\|T_{a}^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}f\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(2.2)

with
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},

we say that
T_{a}^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}
satisfies the bilinear local smoothing property (BLSP) with parameters
(p^{*},p,p_{1},p_{2},s_{1},s_{2}).

Remark 2.4
.

Note that the bilinear local smoothing conjecture claims that any Fourier integral operator of order
m=m_{1}+m_{2}<0,

m_{2}<0,
with phases satisfying the
cinematic curvature condition
should hold the
bilinear local smoothing property
with parameters
(\overline{p}_{d},p,p_{1},p_{2},s_{1},s_{2}),
where

s_{i}=m_{i}+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma_{i}

where
\sigma_{i}
is any number satisfying
\sigma_{i}<\frac{1}{p_{i}}.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that there is no restriction in assuming that
m<0
in Conjecture
1.3
. Indeed, if
m_{0}\geq 0
is the order of a Fourier Integral Operator
T^{\phi}_{a},
one can take
s_{0}
such that
m=m_{0}-s_{0}<0,
and to replace
f
by
f_{s_{0}}=(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{0}}{2}}
to get for the FIO
\widetilde{T}=T(1-\Delta)^{-\frac{s_{0}}{2}}
the smoothing property

\|\widetilde{T}f_{s_{0}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim\|(1-\Delta)^{\frac{s_{0}}{2}}f\|_{L^{p}_{\tilde{s}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\tilde{s}=m+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma.

The previous estimate is equivalent to the following one

\|Tf\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\tilde{s}+s_{0}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},\,\tilde{s}+s_{0}=m_{0}+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma.

This fact and our evidence justify our choice of assuming that the order
m
is negative in Conjecture
1.5
.
Moreover, according to our methodology, we decompose the operator
T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}
as follows

\displaystyle T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)

\displaystyle=T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(\mu(D)f,\mu(D)g)

3.1.
Structural Properties of Bilinear FIOs

First, we will review the structure of the bilinear operator

\displaystyle T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(f,g)(x,t)=\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\smallint_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{2\pi i(\phi_{1}(x,t,\xi)+\phi_{2}(x,t,\eta))}a(x,t,\xi,\eta)\widehat{f}(\xi)\widehat{g}(\eta)d\xi d\eta.

(3.1)

The first step will make a reduction of our analysis in low frequencies and in high frequencies. For this we follow the approach and notation due to Rodríguez-López, Rule, and Staubach in

Remark 3.1
.

(c.f.
[

We will use the structure of the operator

\displaystyle=\sum_{|\beta|\leq[s_{1}]}\|\partial_{x}^{\beta}f(x,t)\|_{L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\asymp\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{[s_{1}]}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}},

where in the last line we have used that
s_{1}>[s_{1}].
In order to estimate the norm
\|\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\lambda)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})},
observe that

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\lambda)=\smallint e^{i\lambda\xi}{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\lambda)dx=\langle\lambda\rangle^{-2N}\smallint e^{i\lambda\xi}(I-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\xi)d\xi.

Then

\displaystyle\|\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\lambda)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\lesssim\langle\lambda\rangle^{-2N}\smallint_{\mathfrak{S}}\|(I-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\xi)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}d\xi,

where we have denoted the support of the symbol
a
in
\xi
by
\mathfrak{S}.

We can compute

\displaystyle(I-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}\mathfrak{a}_{g}(x,t,\xi)

\displaystyle=(I-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}(\psi(\xi)T_{a(\cdot,\cdot,\xi,\cdot)}^{\phi_{2}}g(x,t))

\displaystyle=T_{\varkappa(\xi)}^{\phi_{2}}g(x,t).

Note that the symbol of of
T_{\varkappa(\xi)}^{\phi_{2}}
is given by

\varkappa(\xi):=(1-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}[\psi(\xi)a(\cdot,\cdot,\xi,\cdot)],

and is a linear combination of symbols of order
\leq m=m_{1}+m_{2}\leq m_{2},
where we have used that
m_{1}\leq 0.
Thus,
\varkappa
is a symbol of order
m_{2}
and we have the smoothing estimate

\displaystyle\|T^{\phi_{2}}_{\varkappa(\xi)}g(x,t)\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\lesssim\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}}.

By taking
N>d/2,
we have that

\displaystyle\|T^{\phi_{1}}_{\mathfrak{a}_{g}}(f)(x,t)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\|\widehat{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\lambda)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{[s_{1}]}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}d\lambda.

Summarising, we have proved that

\displaystyle\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a_{1}}(f,g)\|_{L^{p}(K\times\mathcal{I})}

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\langle\lambda\rangle^{-2N}\smallint_{\mathfrak{S}}\|(I-\Delta_{\xi})^{N}{\mathfrak{a}}_{g}(x,t,\xi)\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}d\xi\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}d\lambda

\displaystyle=\smallint\langle\lambda\rangle^{-2N}\smallint_{\mathfrak{S}}\|T_{\varkappa(\xi)}^{\phi_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}d\lambda

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\langle\lambda\rangle^{-2N}d\lambda\sup_{\xi\in\mathfrak{S}}\|T_{\varkappa(\xi)}\|_{\mathscr{B}(L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}},L^{p_{2}})}|\mathfrak{S}|\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}

\displaystyle\lesssim_{K\times\mathcal{I}\times\mathfrak{S}}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}.

Thus, the previous estimate prove (
4.2
).

4.2.
An estimate due to Bourgain

In our further analysis for high-frequencies we will make a continuous decomposition of the operator
T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a_{1}}
and we will estimate its norm using certain maximal functions. Of crucial interest for us will be the next estimate due to Bourgain.

Theorem 4.2

(Bourgain
[
6
]
)
.

Let
K\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that its Fourier transform
\widehat{K}
is differentiable. Define the following quantities

\alpha_{j}=\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\widehat{K}(\xi)|,\quad\beta_{j}=\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\langle\nabla\widehat{K}(\xi),\xi\rangle|.

(4.7)

Then, we have the following estimate on the maximal operator associated to
K,

\|\sup_{t>0}|f\ast K_{t}|\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C\Gamma(K)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

(4.8)

for every
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),
where

\Gamma(K)=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4.9)

There are several reasons making Theorem
4.2
interesting by itself. First, the constant
\Gamma(K)
in (
4.9
) is sharp in some sense, see
[
6
, Page 1475]
. On the other hand, it has been useful in the analysis of the
L^{2}
-theory of maximal functions, for convex bodies and for the sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, see e.g. the introduction of
[
12
]
.

Remark 4.3

(Stein
[
50
]
, Pages 27-29)
.

We also recall that if
\psi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that its Fourier transform
\widehat{\psi}
is supported in an annulus isolating the origin (and then
\widehat{\psi}(0)=\smallint\psi(\varkappa)d\varkappa=0
), one has the following
L^{p}
-boundedness property:

\|\left(\smallint|u\ast\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\lesssim\|u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\,\,\psi_{t}:=t^{-d}\psi(t^{-1}\cdot).

(4.10)

This fact will be used later.

4.3.
Analysis of the high frequencies

The analysis of the high frequencies will consists in proving that

\|T^{\phi_{1},\phi_{2}}_{a}(\mu(D)f,\mu(D)g)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{t})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}},\quad s_{i}=m_{i}+(d-1)\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\sigma_{i},\quad m=m_{1}+m_{2},\quad m_{i}<0.

\displaystyle\|{P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(K\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim C(v)\|T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim C(v)\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})},

and also the estimate

\displaystyle\|{Q}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(K\times\mathcal{I})}\lesssim C(v)\|T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})}\lesssim C(v)\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}.

Thus, the analysis above implies that

|I_{5}|+|I_{7}|\lesssim C(u,v,\varepsilon)\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d})}\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{s})}.

In order to finish our analysis of
\mathscr{I}
let us estimate the terms

and
I_{6}.
By using Cauchy-Schwarz inequality we have that

\displaystyle|I_{4}|

\displaystyle=\left|\smallint\limits_{K\times\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)m(t,x,s)(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x)\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)m(t,x,s)(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x)\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right|

\displaystyle\times\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)m(t,x,s)(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

For the first term, Minkowski integral inequality and Remark
4.3
imply that

\displaystyle\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dxds

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\left(\smallint\limits_{K}\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{p_{1}}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\left(\smallint_{K}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\left\|\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|(Q_{t}^{u}T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f)(x,s)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p_{1}}(K)}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\|T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}ds

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\|T_{\nu_{m_{1}}}^{\phi_{1}}f\|_{L^{p_{1}}}^{p_{1}}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}\left(\smallint_{\mathcal{I}}ds\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}^{\prime}}}ds.

For the second factor, observe that

\displaystyle\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)m(t,x,s)(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|m\|_{L^{\infty}}\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x)|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Let

A_{t}=({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s),\,B_{t}=(\widetilde{Q}_{t}h(\cdot,s))(x).

We can estimate the integral involving these two terms using repeatedly Hölder’s inequality after using Minkowski inequality. Indeed

\displaystyle\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|A_{t}B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|A_{t}B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dxds

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}dxds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|^{p}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{p^{\prime}}{2}}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left(\smallint\limits_{\mathcal{I}}\smallint\limits_{K}\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|^{\frac{pp_{2}}{p}}dxds\right)^{\frac{p}{p_{2}}}\left(\smallint_{K\times\mathcal{I}}dxds\right)^{\frac{1}{(p_{2}/p)^{\prime}}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\left\|\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\left\|\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}.

Note that the use of Hölder’ inequality before is justified by the fact that
\frac{p_{2}}{p}\geq 1,
since
\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{p_{1}}+\frac{1}{p_{2}}\geq\frac{1}{p_{2}}.

In view of the hypothesis
(H1)
in Theorem
2.6
and of Remark
4.3
, we have that

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\left\|\left(\smallint\limits_{0}^{1}|B_{t}|^{2}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(v)\|T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})}\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\lesssim C(v)\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x})}\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}_{s})}.

Observe that we have used that the estimate

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<t<1}|A_{t}|\|_{L^{p_{2}}}\lesssim C(v)\|T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}g\|_{L^{p_{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R})},

(4.17)

holds. Indeed, in order to prove this fact observe that

A_{t}=({P}_{t}^{v,2}[R_{t}T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s)=R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}[T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}]g)(x,s).

Now, the action of the multiplier
R_{t}{P}_{t}^{v,2}
on
G=G_{s}=T_{\gamma}^{\phi_{2}}g(\cdot,s),
is given by

\displaystyle R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)(x)

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{t}}s^{m_{2}}Q_{ts}{P}_{t}^{v,2}G(x)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\smallint_{\kappa}^{\frac{1}{t}}s^{m_{2}}\smallint e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\psi}(ts\xi)\widehat{\theta}_{2}(t\xi)e^{it\xi\cdot v}\widehat{G}(\xi)d\xi\frac{ds}{s}.

Due to the fact that the supports of
\widehat{\psi}
and of
\widehat{\theta}_{2}
are contained in small annuli, so, on the support of these functions
|\xi|\asymp\frac{1}{ts}\asymp\frac{1}{t},
from where we deduce that
s\asymp 1,
and the integral in
s
is supported on an interval around
s=1
and on an annulus isolating
s=0.
So, we have that

\displaystyle|R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)(x)|

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}\left|\smallint e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\psi}(ts\xi)\widehat{\theta}_{2}(t\xi)e^{it\xi\cdot v}\widehat{G}(\xi)d\xi\right|\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\smallint e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\psi}(ts\xi)\widehat{\theta}_{2}(t\xi)e^{it\xi\cdot v}\widehat{G}(\xi)d\xi\right|ds

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\widehat{\psi}(tsD)P_{t}^{v,2}G(x)\right|ds.

Let
\theta_{2}^{v}
be the kernel with Fourier transform
\widehat{\theta}_{2}^{v}(\cdot),
i.e., it is the symbol of
P_{t}^{v,2},
that is,
\widehat{\theta}_{2}^{v}(t\xi):=\widehat{\theta}_{2}(t\xi)e^{it\xi\cdot v}.
Then

\displaystyle\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\widehat{\psi}(tsD)P_{t}^{v,2}G(x)\right|ds

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|G\ast(s^{-n}t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(t^{-1}\cdot)))(x)\right|ds

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\smallint G(x-y)s^{-n}t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(t^{-1}y))dy\right|ds

\displaystyle\asymp\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\smallint G(x-y)t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(t^{-1}y))dy\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}t^{-n}\left|\smallint(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(t^{-1}y))dy\right|ds

\displaystyle\leq\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\left|\smallint(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(y))dy\right|ds.

In consequence

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<t<1}|R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{\infty}}.

(4.18)

Now, let us prove the estimate

\|\sup_{0<t<1}|R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\|G\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.19)

For this we will use Bourgain’s Lemma (see Theorem
4.2
). We proceed with the proof of (
4.19
) in the following remark.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that

\displaystyle R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)(x)

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}\smallint e^{ix\cdot\xi}\widehat{\psi}(ts\xi)\widehat{\theta}_{2}(t\xi)e^{it\xi\cdot v}\widehat{G}(\xi)d\xi\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}\widehat{\psi}(tsD)P_{t}^{v,2}G(x)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}G\ast(s^{-n}t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(t^{-1}\cdot))(x)\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle=\left(G\ast\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}s^{-n}t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(t^{-1}\cdot))\frac{ds}{s}\right)(x).

We have that the kernel
K_{t}
of
R_{t}{P}_{t}^{v,2}

is given by

K_{t}(x)=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}(s^{-n}t^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(t^{-1}x)))\frac{ds}{s}.

(4.20)

Let us write

K(x)=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}}s^{-n}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(x)\frac{ds}{s}.

Its Fourier transform is

\widehat{K}(\xi)=\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}-n}\mathscr{F}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(\xi))\frac{ds}{s},

(4.21)

and

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}:=\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\widehat{K}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\|\mathscr{F}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))\|_{L^{\infty}}s^{m_{2}-n}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\lesssim\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\|\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})\|_{L^{1}}\frac{ds}{s}.

This shows that
\sup_{j}\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\widehat{K}(\xi)|
is bounded. However, due to the properties of the support of
\psi
and
\theta_{2}^{v}
we can imporve this bound as follows

\displaystyle\alpha_{j}\lesssim\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}|\widehat{\psi}(s\xi)\widehat{\theta}_{2}^{\nu}(\xi)|\frac{ds}{s}\lesssim\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}\min\{|\xi|,|\xi|^{-1}\}=2^{-|j|}.

On the other hand

\displaystyle\nabla\widehat{K}(\xi)=(\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}-n}\mathscr{F}((\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))ix_{j})(\xi))\frac{ds}{s})_{1\leq j\leq d}.

Therefore, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\langle\nabla\widehat{K}(\xi),\xi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{d}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}-n}|\mathscr{F}((\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))ix_{r})(\xi))||\xi_{r}|\frac{ds}{s}.

Observe that, using integration by parts, we have that

\displaystyle\mathscr{F}((\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})ix_{r})(\xi))\xi_{r}

\displaystyle=\smallint e^{ix\xi}(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))(x)ix_{r}\xi_{r}dx

\displaystyle=\smallint\partial_{x_{r}}(e^{ix\xi})(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(x)x_{r}dx

\displaystyle=-\smallint e^{ix\xi}\partial_{x_{r}}[(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(x)]x_{r}dx.

We have proved that

\displaystyle\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}|\langle\nabla\widehat{K}(\xi),\xi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{d}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}s^{m_{2}-n}\|\mathscr{F}(\partial_{x_{r}}[(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(x)]x_{r})\|_{L^{\infty}}\frac{ds}{s}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{r=1}^{d}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}\|\partial_{x_{r}}[(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v})(x)]x_{r}\|_{L^{1}}\frac{ds}{s}<\infty.

We have included the previous computation to emphasise the cancellation of the quantity
|\xi_{r}|\lesssim 2^{j}
after using integration by parts which we found interesting. Nevertheless, one can improve this bound. Indeed, due to the properties of the support of
\psi
and
\theta_{2}^{v}
we have that

Z_{r}:=(\psi(s^{-1}\cdot)\ast(\theta_{2}^{v}))ix_{r}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

and that

\displaystyle\beta_{j}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}\sum_{r=1}^{d}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}|\mathscr{F}(Z_{r})(\xi)||\xi_{r}|ds

\displaystyle\asymp\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}\sum_{r=1}^{d}\smallint\limits_{s\asymp 1}|\mathscr{F}(\partial_{x_{r}}Z_{r})(\xi)|ds\lesssim\sup_{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}}\min\{|\xi|,|\xi|^{-1}\}=2^{-|j|}.

In consequence

\Gamma(K):=\sum_{j\in\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{j}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\alpha_{j}+\beta_{j})^{\frac{1}{2}}<\infty.

(4.22)

In view of Theorem
4.2
we have proved that

G\mapsto S^{*}G:=\sup_{0<t<1}|R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)|:L^{2}\rightarrow L^{2}

is bounded.

we have proved that for any

one has that

\displaystyle\|\sup_{0<t<1}|R_{t}({P}_{t}^{v,2}G)|\|_{L^{\kappa}}\lesssim C_{\kappa}(v)\|G\|_{L^{\kappa}},

with a polynomial constant

in

With

we have proved (

-norm in the

In consequence

|I_{4}|\lesssim\|h\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(K\times\mathcal{I})}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}}_{s_{1}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d})}\|g\|_{L^{p_{2}}_{s_{2}}(\mathbb{R}_{x}^{d})},

Appendix: Bibliographic Discussion

The study of the continuity properties for FIOs has attracted considerable attention due to their applications to hyperbolic PDEs. Initial inquiries into global
L^{p}
boundedness results can be traced to pioneering works from the late 1970s and early 1980s
[
2
,
42
,
40
]
. Subsequent research has significantly expanded these results to a wide classes of symbols
[
47
,
48
,
17
,
18
]
. A notable line of inquiry involves establishing global criteria for FIOs within Hörmander symbol classes
S^{m}_{\rho,\delta}
, specifically by relaxing the traditional
\delta<\rho
condition in the setting of pseudo-differential operators to allow broader parameter ranges
0\leq\delta<1
and
0<\rho\leq 1

[
20
,
35
,
33
,
34
,
43
,
44
]
. This development was partly inspired by analogous results in the theory of pseudo-differential operators, which demonstrated boundedness without the
\delta<\rho
restriction
[
19
,
1
]
. The question of global parametrisation for Lagrangian distributions has also been explored through alternative geometric and analytical approaches
[
36
,
46
]
. Additionally, global definitions of FIOs have been formulated in specialised settings, such as on compact Lie groups using the representation theory of these groups
[
14
]
.

The systematic theory of FIOs with complex-phase functions originated from the work of Melin and Sjöstrand
[
37
]
, motivated by constructing parametrices for partial differential operators with complex principal symbols
[
38
]
. This framework has found significant application in several complex variables, where the singular structure of the Bergman kernel can be described via asymptotic expansions involving complex-phase FIOs
[
10
]
. The comprehensive analysis of the Bergman kernel itself represents another major area of study
[
24
,
25
]
.

A distinct and highly active research direction concerns smoothing estimates for FIOs, particularly following the resolution of the decoupling conjecture by Bourgain and Demeter
[
9
]
. The conjecture regarding the inherent smoothing properties of FIOs was formally posed by Sogge
[
52
]
. While a complete survey of this extensive field is beyond our scope, the literature includes influential early results
[
7
]
and numerous recent advances that explore various aspects of this smoothing phenomenon, employing diverse methods from harmonic analysis, see
[
39
,
28
,
4
,
26
]
and the references therein.

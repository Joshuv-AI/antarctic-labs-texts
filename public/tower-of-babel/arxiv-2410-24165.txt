Abstract
. Results about the structure of the set of Egyptian fractions on the line are extended to subsets of topological groups.

1.
Introduction

An
Egyptian fraction
is a sum of unitary fractions

\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{m_{n}}

(where
n,m_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
).
These were introduced into western mathematics by Fibonacci in 1202
[
11
]
, and have been a rich source of mathematical problems ever since. (For a survey, see David Eppstein’s excellent website
[
6
]
.)

Inspired by results of Kellogg
[
9
]
and Curtiss
[
4
]
, in 1956 Sierpinski
[
13
]
published several results about the structure of the set of Egyptian fractions. Recently Nathanson
[
10
]
has extended these results to more general set of real numbers, and the author
[
12
]
showed that working with field extensions of
\mathbb{R}
makes it possible to simplify and extend Sierpinski’s results. A similar approach was adopted by Göral and Sertbaş
[
7
]
.

In this note we use the methods of
[
12
]
to extend these results to topological groups. The extension is interesting not only because of the increased abstraction possible, but also because the proofs from the earlier papers, which rely heavily on the linear order and metric on
\mathbb{R}
, do not apply in this setting.

1.1.
Note on the proofs

The proofs in this paper were discovered using nonstandard analysis
[
5
,
1
]
, and we retain the notation of that technology. However, the amount of nonstandard analysis used is very slight, and can be replaced by working in the setting of any sufficiently saturated elementary extension of the group, for example an ultrapower, which might be familiar to more readers. For completeness we review ultrapowers of groups and the notation in Section
3
.

2.
Statement of results

Fix a topological group
\langle G,0,+,-\rangle
; that means that
G
has a Hausdorff topology
\mathfrak{T}
for which the addition and additive inverse operations are continuous. While we will write this as an additive group, we will generally not assume that addition is abelian.

Later we will considered
ordered
topological groups, which means that there is a partial order on
G
which also respects the
operations (but not necessarily the topology).

Call a subset
T
of
G

locally cofinite at

0
(LCF0) if

(1)

0\notin T
; and

(2)

for every open neighborhood
u
of
0
,
T_{u}:=T\setminus u
is finite.

Fix
T_{1},T_{2},\dots\subseteq G
locally cofinite at
0
. Define

\displaystyle E_{n}

\displaystyle:=T_{1}+\cdots+T_{n}

\displaystyle=\{a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdots+a_{n}\ :\ a_{i}\in T_{i}\}

(which we can think of as n-term generalized Egyptian fractions generated by
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
).

Example 2.1
.

For every
i
, let
T_{i}=\{\frac{1}{m}\ :\ m\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq\mathbb{R}
. Then
E_{n}
is the set of (classical) n-term Egyptian fractions in
\mathbb{R}
.

Example 2.2
.

For every
i
let
A_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
be finite,
B_{i}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
be discrete, and put
T_{i}=\{\frac{a}{b}\ :\ a\in A_{i},b\in B_{i}\}
. The set
E_{n}
is then the set of
weighted real Egyptian numbers
considered by Nathanson in
[
10
]
. Note that if
\epsilon>0
,

T_{i}\setminus(0,\epsilon)=\bigcup_{a\in{A_{i}}}\{\frac{a}{b}:b\in B_{i},b<\frac{a}{\epsilon}\}

which is finite, verifying that
T_{i}
is LCF0.

Sierpinski somehow didn’t notice the following:

Theorem 2.1
.

For
n>0
the set
E^{\prime}_{n}:=T^{\prime}_{1}+\cdots+T^{\prime}_{n}
is compact (where
A^{\prime}=A\cup\{0\}
)

In the case of
G=\mathbb{R}
this immediately implies Sierpinski’s result
[
13
, Théorèm 2]
:

Corollary 2.1
.

The set of n-term Egyptian fractions is nowhere dense in
\mathbb{R}
.

It likewise implies Nathanson’s
[
10
, Corollary 3]
more general result:

Corollary 2.2
.

The set of n-term weighted Egyptian real numbers is nowhere dense in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof of corollaries.

Otherwise
E^{\prime}_{n}
would contain an interval (since it is closed), but it is countable from the definition.
∎

The proofs of all the results in this section, including Theorem
2.1
, are deferred to Section
4

We note that while
E^{\prime}_{n}
is compact,
E_{n}
might
not
be nowhere dense for arbitrary groups; for example, if
G=\mathbb{Z}
under addition and
T_{i}=\{1\}
then
E_{n}=\{n\}
which is dense in
\{n\}
.

However, the following does hold.

Theorem 2.2
.

Suppose
T_{1},\dots,T_{n}
are LCF0 and nowhere dense in
G
. Then
E_{n}=T_{1}+\cdots+T_{n}
is nowhere dense in
G
.

Some other results from
[
13
]
,
[
10
]
, and
[
12
]
still hold in this setting, albeit with slight modification to the statement.

If
g\in E_{n}
, a
representation
of
g
in
E_{n}
is a tuple
\langle g_{1},\cdots,g_{n}\rangle\in T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
such that
g=g_{1}+\cdots+g_{n}
. Equivalently, it is a solution in
T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{n}
to the equation
x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}=g
.

The following now generalizes
[
13
, Théorèm 1]

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
g\in E_{3}
. Then either
g
has only finitely many representations in
E_{3}
, or
g\in T_{1}\cup T_{2}\cup T_{3}
, or
g=0
.

For the remainder of this section we assume that
G
is an
ordered group
, which means that there is a partial order
\leq
on
G
which honors the operations in the sense that if
a\leq b
and
c\leq d
then
a+c\leq b+d
. As usual, write
a<b
if
a\leq b
and
a\neq b
. The
positive cone
of
G
with respect to this order is
\{g\in G:0<g\}
.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\langle G,0,+,-,\leq\rangle
be an ordered topological group, and
T_{1},T_{2},\dots
be LCF0 subsets of the positive cone of
G
. Then the number of representations of any element of
E_{n}
is finite.

The final results generalize
[
13
, Théorèm 3]
(which Sierpinski attributes to Mycielski) and
[
12
, Theorem 1.2.2]
.

Say that the order
\leq
on
G

respects the topology
provided for any
g\in G
the set
\mathcal{U}_{g}\ =\{x\in G:x<g\}
is open. The order
weakly respects the topology
provided for any
h<g
in
G
there is an open neighborhood
u
of
h
such that there is no
x\in u
with
g<x
. The first condition implies the second, and they agree on a linearly ordered group.

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
\langle G,0,+,-,\leq\rangle
be an ordered topological group where the order respects the topology, and
let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots
be LCF0 subsets of the positive cone of
G
. Any infinite subset
S
of
E_{n}
contains a strictly decreasing sequence.

The following is immediate.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\langle G,0,+,-,\leq\rangle
be an ordered topological group where the order respects the topology, and
let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots
be LCF0 subsets of the positive cone of
G
. There does not exist a strictly increasing sequence in
E_{n}
.

3.
Ultrapowers of groups

Before proceeding to the proofs of the results in the previous section, we review the definition and some properties of the ultrapower of a group. As everything in this section is well-known or an easy exercise, no proofs are included (but see the comments at the end of the section).

3.1.
Definitions

Let
I
be an infinite set and
\mathcal{U}
be an ultrafilter on
I
. That is,
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(I)
satisfies: (i)
\emptyset\not\in\mathcal{U}
;
(ii) If
A\subseteq B
and
A\in\mathcal{U}
then
A\in\mathcal{U}
; (iii) If
A,B\in\mathcal{U}
then
A\cap B\in\mathcal{U}
; and
(iv) For every
A\subseteq I
either
A\in\mathcal{U}
or
A^{\complement}\in\mathcal{U}
.

For example, if
a\in I
is a fixed element then
\{A\subseteq I:a\in A\}
is an ultrafilter on
I
, called a
principal
ultrafilter. Nonprincipal ultrafilters are usually “constructed” using Zorn’s Lemma.

If
G
is a nonempty
set
then denote by
G^{I}
the set of functions from
I
into
G
. If
f,h\in G^{I}
write
f\equiv_{\mathcal{U}}h
if

\{i\in I:f(i)=h(i)\}\in\mathcal{U}
. Say that
f=g

almost surely
, or as.

By the properties of
\mathcal{U}
,
\equiv_{\mathcal{U}}
is an equivalence relation on
G^{I}
.
Denote the equivalence class of
f
by
f/\mathcal{U}
. Write
{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{G}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.86249pt*\hskip 7.86249pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.86249pt\hskip 7.86249pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{G}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.86249pt*\hskip 7.86249pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.86249pt\hskip 7.86249pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{G}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.22598pt*\hskip 6.22598pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.22598pt\hskip 6.22598pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.3202pt*\hskip 5.3202pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.3202pt\hskip 5.3202pt}}}}=G^{I}/\mathcal{U}
for the set of all such equivalence classes.
If
A\subseteq G
write
{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.01392pt*\hskip 6.01392pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.01392pt\hskip 6.01392pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.18063pt*\hskip 5.18063pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.18063pt\hskip 5.18063pt}}}}=A^{I}/\mathcal{U}
. Note this can be identified with
\{f/\mathcal{U}:f\in G^{I},f(i)\in A\textrm{ as}\}
.
More generally, if
A\subseteq G^{n}
write
{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.01392pt*\hskip 6.01392pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.01392pt\hskip 6.01392pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.18063pt*\hskip 5.18063pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.18063pt\hskip 5.18063pt}}}}=\{\langle f_{1}/\mathcal{U},\dots,f_{n}/\mathcal{U}\rangle:f_{1},\dots,f_{n}\in G^{I},\langle f_{1}(i),\dots,f_{n}(i)\rangle\in A\textrm{ as}\}
. Note
{\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{A}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.50002pt*\hskip 7.50002pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.50002pt\hskip 7.50002pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.01392pt*\hskip 6.01392pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.01392pt\hskip 6.01392pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{A}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.18063pt*\hskip 5.18063pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.18063pt\hskip 5.18063pt}}}}\subseteq({\mathchoice{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\displaystyle{G}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.86249pt*\hskip 7.86249pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.86249pt\hskip 7.86249pt}}}{{\hskip 4.09723pt\hbox{$\textstyle{G}$}^{\scriptstyle\hskip-4.09723pt\hskip-7.86249pt*\hskip 7.86249pt}_{\scriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-7.86249pt\hskip 7.86249pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptstyle{G}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-6.22598pt*\hskip 6.22598pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-6.22598pt\hskip 6.22598pt}}}{{\hskip 3.68059pt\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{G}$}^{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip-3.68059pt\hskip-5.3202pt*\hskip 5.3202pt}_{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 0.0pt\hskip-5.3202pt\hskip 5.3202pt}}}})^{n}

Many of the results are consequences of the Løs Theorem (or

The definitions of

(the elements

in this paper. Property (T2) codifies the continuity of the group operations.

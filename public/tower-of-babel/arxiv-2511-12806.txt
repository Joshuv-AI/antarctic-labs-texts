Resurgence number of matoridal configuration
Haoxi Hu

Abstract
. This article gives a new upper bound for the resurgence number of symbolic powers of matroidal configuration in the following situations: the height of the matroidal configuration is big, or the height is small, and the corresponding simplicial complex of the matroidal configuration is peaked . The Peaked simplicial complex is a generalization of bipartite graph. Furthermore, the article also gives a clean formula to compute the resurgence number and the strict containment of generalized uniform matroidal configuration which includes case of star configuration of hypersurfaces.

1.
Introduction

Given an ideal
I
of a Noetherian ring
R
, we can always find its
m
-th symbolic power
as follows:

I^{(m)}=\bigcap_{{\mathfrak{p}}\in\Ass(I)}I^{m}R_{\mathfrak{p}}\cap R.

Symbolic powers have been extensively studied in commutative algebra. An important approach to understanding their behavior is through containment problem. The containment problem starts at this question: when is symbolic power of an ideal
I^{(m)}
contained in ordinary power of an ideal
I^{r}
? The celebrated result of uniform bound was proved by
[
5
, Hochster-Huneke]
and
[
3
, Ein-Lazarsfeld-Smith]
in different settings of characteristic of the field gave an important answer: Let
R
be a regular ring containing a field, and let
I
be any proper ideal of
R
, then
I^{(hn)}\subset I^{n}
for all
n\geq 1
, where
h
is the big height of
I
, i.e. the largest height of any associated primes of
I
. Subsequently, the question of containment emerged as a central problem in this field, then a new concept called
resurgence number of symbolic powers
, denoted as
\rho(I)
, was introduced in
[
2
, Bocci-Harbourne]
as follows:

\rho(I)=\sup\left\{\frac{m}{r}~|~I^{(m)}\not\subseteq I^{r},m\geqslant 1,r\geqslant 1\right\}

Computing resurgence number is very difficult in general. Resurgence number is even not known for
star configuration of hypersurfaces
(or just
star configuration
for abbreviation). The
star configuration
was first defined in
[
1
]
with hyperplane setting, then generalized in
[
4
]
with hypersurface setting. Only resurgence number of some special cases of
star configuration
are known, see Remark
3.11
for more details. In this paper, we will give a formula to compute the resurgence number and the strict containment of
generalized uniform matroidal configuration
which includes case of
star configuration of hypersurfaces
.

Our method to tackle the containment problems replies on matroid and its corresponding simplicial complex, the ideal we discuss in the paper is
C
-matroidal ideal
and its corresponding
C
-matroidal configuration
(also called as
matroidal configuration
).
C
-matoridal ideal
is a monomial ideals defined on a polynomial ring over a field, where supports of generators satisfy axioms of circuits of a matroid (see the Example
2.6
), the corresponding
matroidal configuration
has similar settings by replacing variables with a regular sequence consisting of homogeneous components, so
C
-matroidal configuration
builds the connection between
generalized uniform matroid

U_{c,n}
and
star configuration
(see Example
2.9
).
C
-matroidal ideal
is the usual definition of the matroidal ideal, but there is also another way to define matroidal ideal via bases of a matroid, we will call it
B
-matroidal ideal
, and
B
-matroidal ideal
is the
Alexandar dual
of
C
-matroidal ideal
with respect to their corresponding simplicial complexes.

In this article, all results of
C
-matroidal ideal
can be applied to its corresponding
matroidal configuration
as well (see Remark
2.8
).

Main Result 1

(
Theorem

3.9
)
.

Let
I
be the
C
-matroidal ideal corresponding to uniform matroid
U_{c,n}
. Then we have the followings:

(1)

The strict containment:

\displaystyle I^{r}\;\text{contains}\;I^{(m)}\;\text{if only if}\;r\leq\frac{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m}{n-c+1}

(2)

And the resurgence number:

\displaystyle\rho(I)=\frac{c(n-c+1)}{n}

In particular, if we apply the formula to its corresponding matroidal configuration, let
n=s
where
s
is the number of homogeneous components, then it is also the resurgence number of the star configuration.

Other than looking at matroid structure directly, we also look at its corresponding simplicial complex structures (see Definition
2.4
), we define a new class of simplicial complex called
peaked simplicial complex
, which generalizes the bipartite graph (see Example
2.11
). When the height of the
C
-matroidal ideal
is big, or the height is small and the corresponding simplicial complex is
peaked
, a new upper bound for the resurgence number is given, which is our second main result. Before our result, the upper bound of resurgence of
matroidal ideal and configuration
is
c
(the height of
C
-matroidal ideal
), our new bound is
c-1
.

Main Result 2

(
Theorem

3.7
)
.

let
k=\lfloor\frac{n}{n-c+1}\rfloor
where
n
is the number of variables used in the
C
-matroidal ideal
I
,
c
is the height of
I
. Then if
k>1
, or
k=1
and the corresponding simplicial complex
\Delta
is peaked, we have resurgence upper bound for
I
as follows:

\displaystyle\rho(I)\leq c-1

In particular, this result can be also applied to corresponding matroidal configuration.

We want to mention that the simplicial complex corresponding to
generalized uniform matroid

U_{c,n}
is not
peaked
. Furthermore, the bound from
Main Result 2
will fail easily if
k=1
and
\Delta
is not
peaked
, see the simple Example
3.8
, that is why
Non-peaked
simplicial complex with
k=1
is the only remaining part that cannot be improved for the bound.

2.
Preliminary

2.1.
Matroid

Bases and circuits are two important concepts which characterize and also define matroids, so they lead to two types of matroidal ideal, we will only focus on one type. Before we get into details, let’s we recall some definitions:

Definition 2.1
.

The basis
\mathcal{B}
of a matroid
M
defined on a ground set
E
is a nonempty collection of subsets of
E
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(Basis exchange property) For any
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, and for any
v\in B_{1}\backslash B_{2}
, there exist a
w\in B_{2}\backslash B_{1}
such that
(B_{1}-v)\cup\{w\}\in\mathcal{B}
.

(2)

(Symmetric basis exchange property) For any
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
and for any
v\in B_{1}\backslash B_{2}
, there exist a
w\in B_{2}\backslash B_{1}
such that both
(B_{1}-v)\cup\{w\}\in\mathcal{B}
and
(B_{2}-w)\cup\{v\}\in\mathcal{B}
.

(3)

(Symmetric multi-basis exchange property) For any
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{B}
, and for any subset
A\subseteq B_{1}\backslash B_{2}
, there exist a subset
B\subseteq B_{2}\backslash B_{1}
such that both
(B_{1}-A)\cup B\in\mathcal{B}
and
(B_{2}-B)\cup A\in\mathcal{B}
.

Note that
basis
of matroid is a collection of maximal independent sets of the matroid.

Definition 2.2
.

A collection of
circuits
, denoted as
\mathcal{C}
, of a matroid
M
defined on a ground set
E
is a nonempty collection of subsets of
E
satisfies the following properties:

(1)

(Nonempty)
\emptyset\notin\mathcal{C}
.

(2)

(Minimality) For any
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
, if
C_{1}\subseteq C_{2}
, then
C_{1}=C_{2}
.

(3)

(Circuit elimination) For any distinct
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
, if
e\in C_{1}\cap C_{2}
, then there exists
C_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C_{3}\subseteq(C_{1}\cup C_{2})-e
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let matorid
M=(E,\mathcal{B})
defined by the ground set
E
and basis
\mathcal{B}
, then the
dual
of
M
, denoted as
M^{*}
, is defined by its basis
\mathcal{B}^{*}
as the following:

\displaystyle B^{*}=\{E\backslash B:\,B\in\mathcal{B}\}

Note that
M^{*}
is also a matriod, and
M^{**}=M
. A
circuit
of matroid is a minimal dependent set of the matroid in terms of inclusion. A
cocircuit
is a
circuit
of the
dual
of the matroid.

2.2.
C-Matroidal ideal and Configuration

Definition 2.4
.

[
6
, definition 2.10]

A squarefree monomial ideal
I\subset T
is
C
-matroidal
if
I
is the
cover ideal
of a matroid associated to a simplicial complex
\Delta
. More specifically, given a matroid
M
associated with simplicial complex
\Delta
, the support of a generator of
Stanley–Reisner ideal
is exactly the
circuit
of
M
, so list of supports and list of circuits are one-to-one correspondence. If we take the
cover ideal
of
\Delta
, the support of a generator of
cover ideal
will be
cocircuit
of
M
. See the Example
2.6
for the construction.

Recall the
cover ideal
of a simplicial complex
\Delta
is
J(\Delta)=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}\mathfrak{p}_{F}
where
\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
is the collection of all facets of
\Delta
and
\mathfrak{p}_{F}=(x_{i}\,:\,i\in F)
. the
Stanley–Reisner ideal
of
\Delta
is
I_{\Delta}=\bigcap_{F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta)}\mathfrak{p}_{[n]-F}
where
[n]
is the ground set of
\Delta
.

Remark 2.5
.

There are more equivalent conditions to define
C
-matroidal ideal
and its construction related to another way to define matroidal ideal (see
[
6
, definition 2.10]
), but here we focus on Definition
2.4
.

C
-matroidal ideal
is the usual definition of
matroidal ideal
, while another common definition of
matroidal ideal
is given via
bases
of the matroid, which is exactly the
facet ideal of the matroid
. Therefore, the support of
B
-matroidal ideal the generators are
bases
. Note that
B
-matroidal ideal is the
Alexandar dual
of
C
-matroidal ideal
.

Example 2.6
.

Given a simplicial complex
\Delta
defined on a ground set
\{1,2,3,4\}
, and its facets are
\{\{1,2\},\{1,3\},\{2,3\},\{2,4\},\{3,4\}\}
, take the cover ideal of these facets, we have a collection of generators
\{124,134,23\}
, which satisfy the axioms of circuits of matroid, so this a

-matroidal ideal. Take the

of this ideal, we have a new ideal with generators

, and they satisfy the axiom of bases of matroids, so the new ideal is

-matroidal ideal
.

.

[

Let

be a polynomial ring over a field, and let

be our collection of homogeneous components of

We should mention that

Example 2.9
.

The

2.3.
Peaked Simplicial Complex

It is known that
\Delta
is a
squarefree glicci simplicial complex
(see
[
4
]
, and see
[
7
]
for the definition of
glicci simplicial complex
). However, the way to construct such simplicial complex does not help us a lot for ideal containment problems, so we want to classify
\Delta
based on a different setting, where our definition of
peaked simplicial complex
comes into play.

Definition 2.10
.

Given a simplicial complex
\Delta
defined on vertex set
[n]
, let
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\Delta)
be the collection of all facets of
\Delta
that contain
\{i\}
. We call
\Delta
is
peaked
if there exist a strict subset
A\not\subseteq[n]
such that the collection of all facets satisfies
\cup_{i\in A}\mathcal{F}_{i}(\Delta)=\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
, and these
\mathcal{F}_{i}
’s are disjoint.

Example 2.11
.

When all the facets of a
peaked simplicial complex
are either edges or vertices, then it’s exactly a bipartite graph. Considering
A
as one of the two partitions, there is no edge connecting any two vertices in
A
, say
i
and
j
, otherwise
\mathcal{F}_{i}(\Delta)
and
\mathcal{F}_{j}(\Delta)
will share edges. Because
\cup_{i\in A}\mathcal{F}_{i}(\Delta)=\mathcal{F}(\Delta)
, there is no edge connecting any two vertices in the other partition as well.

Example 2.12
.

We can look at the
peaked simplicial complex
in more general case. If there are facets with dimension greater than
0
, take one such element, say
i
. We can consider each facet of
\mathcal{F}_{i}
is a “wall” of a “pyramid-like” shape with “peak vertex” at
\{i\}
. Since all
\mathcal{F}_{j}
are disjoint where
j\in A
, the
peaked simplicial complex
consists of multiple pyramid-like shapes, that is how we gave the name of this type of simplicial complexes.

Notation 2.13
.

The notations
\Delta
,
T
,
I
,
I_{*}
,
\mathcal{F}
,
\mathcal{F}_{i}
,
c
,
s
,
n
, and
U_{r,n}
we given from the definitions and examples above will be used throughout the rest of the paper unless otherwise is stated.

3.
Proof of the main results

Our method to show the main results is to study the properties of circuits of matroid and facets of corresponding simplicial complex, then we describe the combinatorial structures of the squarefree parts of the supports of the
C
-matroidal ideal
.
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
(our Theorem
3.1
) describes the generators of symbolic powers of
C
-matroidal ideals, and
[
6
, Proposition 3.19]
(our Proposition
3.3
) describes the squarefree parts of first
c
symbolic powers via simplicial complex. Following Theorem
3.1
, we develop a formula saying that all symbolic powers are generated by the first
c
symbolic powers, which is Corollary
3.2
. Moreover, following the Proposition
3.3
, we give more details of the structure of corresponding simiplicial complex, which are more applicable for solving problems, and it is our Proposition
3.5
.

Denote squarefree part of
I^{(m)}
as
SF(I^{(m)})
. As we mentioned in Remark
2.8
, results of
C
-matroidal ideal
I
can be applied to matroidal configuration
I_{*}
, so similarly, when we talk about “degree” matroidal configuration, the degree for
I_{*}
is graded with respect to homogeneous components from
\mathcal{F}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
Let
I
be the usual settings. For any
m\geq 1
, a minimal generating set of
I
is given by

G_{I^{(m)}}=\Bigl\{

M=M_{(1)}\cdots M_{(t)}

M_{(i)}\in G_{I^{(c_{i})}}
and is squarefree, where
1\leq c_{i}\leq ht(I)
with
\sum c_{i}=m
and
supp(M_{(1)})\supset...\supset supp(M_{(t)})

\Bigr\}

The following is the Corollary of
[
6
, Theorem 3.7]
, and it is also the ”refined” version of
[
6
, Theorem 3.9]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Using usual settings
I
. Then we have the binomial expansion as follows:

I^{(m)}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{c}I^{(i)}\,I^{(m-i)}
for
m>c

In other words, symbolic powers of matroidal ideal are generated by the first
c
symbolic powers.

Proof.

First know that
I^{(m)}\supset\sum_{i=1}^{c}I^{(i)}I^{(m-i)}
, we just need to show the inclusion.

Let
M=M_{(1)}...M_{(t)}
be the generator in
G_{I^{(m)}}
. By definition,
M_{(1)}\cdots M_{(t-1)}
and
M_{(t)}
are generators from the lower symbolic powers and their powers sum up to
m
. If we write
M_{(t)}
as product of squarefree monomial factors, which violates the support inclusion rule in theorem
3.1
since
M_{(t)}
itself is already squarefree, and we also know
M_{(t)}
comes from
I^{(t)}
where
1\leq t\leq c
.
∎

Theorem
3.2
and
[
6
, Theorem 3.9]
imply all squarefree generators come from first
c
symbolic powers. First we need to describe squarefree parts to help us to discuss the ideal containment problem, so we need to introduce the following proposition:

Proposition 3.3
.

[
6
, Proposition 3.19]

Let
J
be a squarefree monomial ideal, and let
\Delta
is a simplicial complex such that
J=J(\Delta)
(the cover ideal of
\Delta
), and
\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{m})=\{F-A\,:\,F\in\mathcal{F}(\Delta),\,A\subset F,\,|A|=m-1\}
. Then

\displaystyle SF(J^{(m)})=J(\Delta_{m})

To reveal more combinatorial structures of this proposition, we need the following lemma:

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be the polynomial ring of
n
variables where
c\leq n
. Denote
L_{c}
as the collection of ideals generated by
c
distinct variables of
R
, i.e. the element of
L_{c}
is the ideal of the form
(x_{i_{1}},x_{i_{2}},\,...\,,x_{i_{c}})
where
x_{i_{j}}
’s are all distinct. Then the intersection of all elements of
L_{c}
is an ideal (minimally) generated by all possible squarefree elements of degree
n+1-c
.

Proof.

If two ideals share the same generators, then the intersection of these two ideals preserve corresponding generators. Without loss of generality, consider
x_{1}
be in one element
l_{1}
of
L_{c}
, finding a new element
l_{2}
of
L_{c}
such that it contains
x_{2}
as a generator, then the intersection of these two ideals contains generator
x_{1}x_{2}
. We can keep doing this until we have
l_{n-c}
, and consider
l=(x_{n-c+1},\,...\,,x_{n})\in L_{c}
, then
L=(\cap_{i=1}^{n-c}l_{i})\cap l
will contain list of generators
G=\{x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n-c}x_{j}\,:\,\text{for all}\;n-c+1\leq j\leq n\}
as generators.

Take any element
l^{\prime}
from
L_{c}
outside
\{l_{1},...\,,l_{n-c},l\}
, consider
l^{\prime}
intersects with
L
, we need to compare generators from
l^{\prime}
with generators from
L
. Let
x_{k}
be a generator of
l^{\prime}
, if
k\leq n-c
, then the intersection ideal will still contain
G
as generators. If
k>n-c
, it follows that
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n-c}x_{k}
is a generator of the intersection ideal, as well as
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n-c}x_{j}x_{k}
where
j\neq k
. However, the latter generators are all redundant since we already had
x_{1}x_{2}\cdots x_{n-c}x_{k}
as our generator. Note that there are some generators of
l^{\prime}
are
x_{k}
’s such that
k\leq n-c
, otherwise
l^{\prime}=l
. Therefore, the intersection ideal will still have list of generators
G
.

Since the previous process of constructing
L
is arbitrary with respect to choices of variables as generators, the generators of intersection of all elements of
L_{c}
are all possible elements of degree
n+1-c
because generators of
l
can be any choice of
c
subset of
[n]
.
∎

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
I
be an intersection ideal of ideals corresponding to
(c-1)
-faces from a simplex

of dimension

. Then squarefree parts of

-faces of

First note that

is a squarefree monomial ideal followed from the simplicial complex structure. By Proposition

of degree

, this is an one-to-one correspondence to

-faces of

, then by the definition,

is also a

-matroidal ideal. Similarly, squarefree parts of

-faces of

.
∎

(2)
When

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
I
be the
C
-matroidal ideal corresponding to uniform matroid
U_{c,n}
. Then we have the followings:

(1)

The strict containment:

\displaystyle I^{r}\;\text{contains}\;I^{(m)}\;\text{if only if}\;r\leq\frac{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m}{n-c+1}

(2)

And the resurgence number:

\displaystyle\rho(I)=\frac{c(n-c+1)}{n}

In particular, if we apply the formula to its corresponding matroidal configuration, let
n=s
where
s
is the number of homogeneous components, then it is also the resurgence number of the star configuration.

It’s not hard to see that the simplicial complex corresponding to
generalized uniform matroid
is not
peaked
, but it does satisfy a nice property, which is the following lemma.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
M
be a momomial defined on a finite set of variables
\{x_{1},...,x_{n}\}
, and
n
can be arbitrary big. Suppose degree of each variable of
M
is at most
m
. If there exists
a
,
b
such that
deg(M)=ab
and
b\geq m
, then
M
can be factored into
b
squarefree monomials of same degree
a
.

Proof.

Let
M=x_{1}^{p_{1}}x_{2}^{p_{2}}...x_{n}^{p_{n}}
. Without loss of generality, we might assume
p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq...\geq p_{n}
. We will prove the statement by recursive process.

When
b=1
,
M
is squarefree, then we are done.

If
b\geq 2
, we can observe the following facts:

(1)

a\leq n
. Because
deg(M)=ab\leq nb
.

(2)

p_{a}\geq 1
. If
p_{a}=0
,
deg(M)=p_{1}+p_{2}+...p_{a}\leq(a-1)b<ab
.

(3)

p_{a+1}\leq b-1
. If
p_{a+1}\geq b
,
deg(M)\geq p_{1}+p_{2}+...+p_{a+1}=(a+1)b>ab
.

Let
N=x_{1}x_{2}...x_{a}
be our new factor, we can always do this by
(1)
and
(2)
, and note that the variables of remaining monomial
M/N
are at most
b-1
by
(3)
. Now
deg(M/N)=a(b-1)
, apply the above factorization recursively by
b-1
,
b-2
, … ,
1
, we will get the factorization we want.

∎

Proof of Theorem
3.9
.

First by Proposition
3.5
and the definition of uniform matroid,
I
is exactly the intersection ideal of all
(c-1)
-faces from a simplex
F
of dimension
n-1
, then the
SF(I^{(m)})
corresponds to
(n+m-c-1)
-faces of
F
. Then by Theorem
3.1
and Corollary
3.2
, every symbolic power can be represented by the sum of products of squarefree parts of first
c
symbolic power by inductive process. Observe that the lowest degree of generators of
I^{(m)}
is
\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
because the lowest degree of the generator can be written as
\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil
product of squarefree generators from first
c
symbolic powers. For simplicity, let it be
M^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}=M_{(1)}M_{(2)}...M_{(\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil)}
.

Note that
\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c+1)=\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil\leq\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
, we want to show that for all
r\geq\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil
such that
r(n-c+1)\leq\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
, we have
I^{r}
contains
I^{(m)}
.

If
r(n-c+1)=\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
, then applying Lemma
3.10
, the generators in
I^{r}
generate
M^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}
.

If
r(n-c+1)<\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
. Since
deg(M_{(i)})\geq n-c+1
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}deg(M^{(i)})=\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
. Now considering to remove variables from some
M_{(i)}
’s such that their degrees are greater and equal to
n-c+1
, and also make the corresponding new monomial
\overline{M}^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}
having degree exactly
r(n-c+1)
. Apply Lemma
3.10
again, generators of
I^{r}
generate
\overline{M}^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}
, then so
M^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil}
.

Note that the higher degree generators of
I^{(m)}
are products of
\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil+i
squarefree parts of first
c
symbolic powers of
I
, denoted as
M_{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil+i}
, where
1\leq i\leq m-\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil
by Corollary
3.2
. Furthermore,
deg(M^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil+i})=(\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil+i)(n-c)+m>\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
. By the same methods above, there must exists
r^{\prime}\geq r
such that
I^{r^{\prime}}
contains
M^{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil+i}
, so
I^{r}
contains the higher degree generators. Therefore we only need to focus on the lowest degree generators, it follows that
I^{r}
contains
I^{(m)}
. However, if
r(n-c+1)>\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m
,
I^{r}
won’t contain the lowest degree generators of
I^{(m)}
. In conclusion, we have the following strict containment:

\displaystyle I^{r}\text{contains}\;I^{(m)}\;\text{iff}\;r\leq\frac{\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+m}{n-c+1}

Furthermore, for all
I^{r}\not\supset I^{(m)}
, it must satisfy the following:

\displaystyle\frac{m}{r}<\frac{n-c+1}{\frac{1}{m}\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+1}

Observe that supremum of right side of the inequality is the resurgence number, which is the following:

\displaystyle\rho(I)=\sup_{m\geq 1}\biggl\{\frac{n-c+1}{\frac{1}{m}\lceil\frac{m}{c}\rceil(n-c)+1}\biggl\}=\frac{n-c+1}{\frac{1}{m}\cdot\frac{m}{c}(n-c)+1}=\frac{c(n-c+1)}{n}

∎

AI Approaches to Qualitative and Quantitative News Analytics on NATO Unity
Bohdan M. Pavlyshenko Ivan Franko National University of Lviv, Ukraine b.pavlyshenko@gmail.com, www.linkedin.com/in/bpavlyshenko/

Abstract
The paper considers the use of GPT models with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for qualitative and quantitative analytics on NATO sentiments, NATO unity and NATO Article 5 trust opinion scores in different web sources: news sites found via Google Search API, Youtube videos with comments, and Reddit discussions. A RAG approach using GPT-4.1 model was applied to analyse news where NATO related topics were discussed. Two levels of RAG analytics were used: on the first level, the GPT model generates qualitative news summaries and quantitative opinion scores using zero-shot prompts; on the second level, the GPT model generates the summary of news summaries. Quantitative news opinion scores generated by the GPT model were analysed using Bayesian regression to get trend lines. The distributions found for the regression parameters make it possible to analyse an uncertainty in specified news opinion score trends. Obtained results show a downward trend for analysed scores of opinion related to NATO unity. This approach does not aim to conduct real political analysis; rather, it consider AI based approaches which can be used for further analytics as a part of a complex analytical approach. The obtained results demonstrate that the use of GPT models for news analysis can give informative qualitative and quantitative analytics, providing important insights. The dynamic model based on neural ordinary differential equations was considered for modelling public opinions. This approach makes it possible to analyse different scenarios for evolving public opinions. Keywords: natural language processing, news analytics, GPT models, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), NATO Agreement, NATO unity.

Introduction

Using AI approaches for analysing news text information is a highly promising method of understanding key processes in society, especially public opinions on different points of social and political life. One of such points is the public opinion on NATO unity and security. Currently, during the active phase of Russian-Ukrainian war, it is important to understand all possible threats.

One of the most important parts of NATO agreement is Article 5.
Article 5 of NATO Agreement states that if a NATO country is the victim of an armed attack, each Alliance member will consider this act of violence as an armed attack against all members and will take such measures as it deems necessary, ”including the use of armed force,” to come to the aid of the attacked country.
NATO unity is a crucial aspect of the alliance’s strength, enabling it to effectively address collective defense and maintain peace and security. This unity is demonstrated through various means, including joint military exercises, shared values, and cooperation among member states.
NATO is built on the foundation of shared values, including freedom, democracy, and the rule of law, which foster a sense of common purpose and strengthen the alliance.
Member states work together to support each other, both militarily and politically, in times of crisis or when addressing common challenges. This cooperation can range from military deployments to diplomatic efforts, ensuring a coordinated response to threats.

NATO unity is both a military doctrine and a political challenge.

**Summary of Summaries: NATO Unity, Security, and Article 5 Trust (2021-2025)**

**Challenges for NATO Unity, Security, and Trust in Article 5**

From 2021 through early 2025, the core themes emerging from a comprehensive review of news and
analytical resources are persistent and, in some cases, intensifying challenges to NATO unity,
collective security, and trust in Article 5. These challenges arise from:

- **Divergent Threat Perceptions:** Members continue to disagree on prioritizing threats, notably on
Russia vs. China, hybrid vs. traditional warfare, and security interests in regions like the Arctic,
Black Sea, and Indo-Pacific. While Russia’s aggression has rallied much of the alliance, questions
remain over extending unity to new domains and geographies (cyber, space, economic security).

- **Political and Policy Differences:** There is ongoing friction over defense spending, burden-
sharing, enlargement (notably concerning Ukraine, Sweden, Finland), and differing national
approaches to security (illustrated by debates over climate, human security, and defense industrial
policies).

- **US Commitment Doubts:** The reliability of the US — NATO’s central pillar — is under historic
scrutiny, especially with shifting presidential rhetoric (notably from Trump), Congressional
assertions of war powers, and threats to condition or withhold U.S. support for allies not meeting
spending targets. This has prompted increased calls for European defense autonomy and alternative
security arrangements.

- **Consensus-based Decision-making:** The need for unanimity in NATO decision-making means that
individual states can block collective actions, introducing veto risks in crises and undermining the
perceived automaticity of mutual defense.

- **Hybrid and Non-traditional Threats:** Rising cyberattacks, disinformation, sabotage, and other
hybrid tactics test NATO’s ability to determine when and how to respond collectively. There is
persistent ambiguity about whether— and how— Article 5 would apply in such cases.

- **Enlargement and External Pressure:** Prospective or partial security guarantees for Ukraine and
ongoing disputes over extending full Article 5 protections to new members (or specific territories)
risk exposing alliance divisions. Russian and, to a lesser degree, Chinese hybrid activities and
direct threats are actively testing NATO’s internal cohesion and resolve.

**Time Trends: Unity, Sentiment, and Article 5 Trust Scores**

- **Unity Scores:**
- *2021-2022:* Despite a surge in rhetorical and symbolic unity around Russia’s invasion of
Ukraine (unity scores often 3-4 in summit-related documents and high-level statements), operational
and political divisions persisted, especially around enlargement, defense spending, and non-
traditional threats. Lap points: partial unity or visible strain, especially in critical moments
such as the Afghanistan withdrawal, debates around Ukraine’s membership, and cyber crises.
- *2023-2024:* Unity shows brief, event-driven peaks (e.g., Finland and Sweden’s accession, NATO’s
anniversaries) but is overall more fragile than in earlier years, as reflected in a greater number
of resources with unity scores 0-2 or even negative. Unity declines further in late 2024 and 2025,
notably with U.S. political shifts, open threats to alliance guarantees, and European moves toward
strategic alternatives or autonomous defense.
- *2025:* Sharp declines in unity scores are common in resources discussing new US administration
attitudes, hypothetical intra-alliance conflicts, and greater public questioning of the alliance’s
purpose and methods, especially surrounding Trump-linked rhetoric.

- **Sentiment Scores:**
- *2021-mid-2022:* Generally positive (2-4) amid public reaffirmations and united responses to
immediate threats. Documents from defense/public fora are optimistic, though more analytical sources
flag underlying problems.
- *Late 2022-2025:* Sentiment erodes, with rising negative or neutral scores as unity, operational
cooperation, and trust are undermined by real or anticipated US disengagement, overtly transactional
rhetoric, and unresolved intra-European disagreements.

- **Article 5 Trust Scores:**
- *2021:* Mixed. Scores from -1 to 5, indicating that while summit rhetoric was strong, deeper
analysis shows persistent doubts about automaticity, scope (e.g., cyber, hybrid, out-of-area), and
willingness to act (as in the Baltic, Black Sea, or Indo-Pacific). The withdrawal from Afghanistan
and pressure on spending worsened trust among some EU members.
- *2022:* Initially boosted by rhetorical reaffirmations post-Ukraine invasion (scores up to 4-5
in official pronouncements), but tested by ambiguous crises (missile incidents, cyber events), the
practicalities of invoking Article 5, and case-by-case ambiguities (especially for new or non-
European members and hybrid attacks).
- *2023-2024:* Declining trend, with trust scores between 0 and 2 predominant except for formal
summit documents. Explicit Trump-era and Congressional debates (along with European consideration of
autonomy) further erode trust, reflected in increasing skepticism in frontline states and even
traditional NATO stalwarts.
- *2025:* Heightened doubts, with frequent -2 to -4 trust scores in analyses, reflecting deep
anxiety about U.S. reliability, more hypothetical or conditional security guarantees (e.g., to parts
of Ukraine), and discussions about alternative European defense prospects.

**Groups of News Sites by Opinion on NATO Unity and Article 5 Trust**

- **Pro-Unity, High Article 5 Trust:**
- *Government, military, and NATO-linked sources* (army.mil, defense.gov, af.mil, navy.mil,
veterans.gc.ca, europa.eu, NATO/EU official sites) repeatedly affirm strong unity and robust Article
5 trust, especially during summits, anniversaries, or after successful exercises/integration events.
- *Allied national foreign ministries or defense sites* (auswaertiges-amt.de, government.nl)
consistently reinforce positive sentiment and trust in collective defense.

- **Supportive but Cautiously Analytical:**
- *Policy think tanks, mainstream U.S. and European news organizations, and defense-focused
academic outlets* (csis.org, atlanticcouncil.org, rusi.org, carnegieendowment.org, SWP-Berlin,
GMFUS, BBC, NYTimes) typically score unity and Article 5 trust as moderately high but frequently
point to operational, political, and strategic obstacles, especially around burden-sharing, U.S.
commitment, and new threat domains.

- **Skeptical/Critical; Highlighting Divisions and Doubts:**
- *Academic, specialist, and critical international sites* (cato.org, lawfaremedia.org, quora.com,
reason.com, responsiblestatecraft.org, westpoint.edu, house.gov/senate.gov in Congressional context,
various op-eds) consistently foreground the legal, political, and operational caveats that challenge
unity and trust. They express concern about U.S. conditionality, the lack of automatism, and the
risk of failing to uphold Article 5 under political or legal duress.
- *Some mainstream news sites* (BBC, CNN, businessinsider.com, theguardian.com, bloomberg.com,
politico.eu) register discouraging signals when reporting on U.S. or major-member leaders’ overt
questioning of Article 5, or open intra-alliance discord.

- **Outright Negative or Alarmist:**
- *Cato Institute, some U.S./UK/EU analysts, and critical think tanks* (cato.org,
verfassungsblog.de, responsiblestatecraft.org, msnbc.com, nbcnews.com) are unsparing in their
warnings, documenting a collapse or acute risk to unity and trust, especially with Trump-linked
rhetoric, rising calls for European autonomy, and hypothetical scenarios involving intra-alliance
conflict, green-lighting of Russian actions, or abandonment.
- *International and external observer commentary and opposition-leaning sources*
(kyivindependent.com, pravda.com.ua, aa.com.tr, lrt.lt) flag deep skepticism about the extension or
flexibility of Article 5, especially regarding Ukraine, and the danger posed by informal and
ambiguous guarantees.

**Conclusion**

While the 2021-2022 period saw bursts of unity and reaffirmed trust in Article 5 under duress
(especially after the Russian invasion of Ukraine), the subsequent years reveal mounting and
unresolved doubts—exacerbated by political rhetoric in leading member states, internal disagreements
on enlargement, hybrid threats, and the lack of consensus or clarity on collective defense’s scope
and implementation.

- **Unity is profoundly event- and context-dependent**: It peaks at summits or in collective
exercises, but strategic, legal, and political cracks are increasingly visible, especially as more
actors question American leadership or propose alternative security arrangements.
- **Sentiment and trust are declining**: The balance of analytical and official resources shows a
shift from cautious optimism to apprehension or alarm, particularly amid U.S. political
oscillations, hybrid threats, and debates over the automaticity and scope of article 5 protections.
- **Groups differ by tone and assessment**: Official, government-linked, and mainstream policy sites
are more optimistic, but policy critics and think tanks increasingly warn about procedural
ambiguities, wavering political will, and the rising risk of alliance fracture in a crisis.

**In sum:** NATO remains resilient by many operational metrics, but faces its most acute test of
internal unity and external credibility since its founding, centered on whether political will,
legal clarity, and strategic solidarity can withstand new and old threats—and especially whether
Article 5 will remain an automatic, dependable guarantee for all members.

The opinion dynamics model describes how individual or collective opinions evolve under the influence of internal feedback (amplification and resistance) and external influence. Internal feedback tends to stabilize moderate opinions but amplify strong ones, while external influences temporarily or permanently shift opinions, depending on their intensity and persistence.
It combines internal opinion dynamics (nonlinear) with external forcing (impulses).
We consider simple non-linear model which take into account external impulses which can describe different important public events or news.
We also include into the model the memory which describe evolving of external influence.

The neural network serves as a universal function approximator for the right-hand side of the differential equation. This allows the model to learn complex, time-dependent dynamics directly from data, without requiring an explicit analytical form of the governing equations. During forward integration, the neural network defines the instantaneous rate of change of the system’s state, which is then numerically integrated using standard ODE solvers to obtain the system’s trajectory over time.
For the inverse problem, which involves identifying the optimal parameters of the neural network, gradient-based optimization methods are employed. In this context, backpropagation is used to compute gradients of a loss function with respect to the model parameters.
The neural network provides a flexible and data-driven approximation of system dynamics in the forward pass, while backpropagation facilitates the learning of parameters by minimizing the discrepancy between predicted and observed states.

Conclusions

In the work, we have considered the use of GPT models with retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) for qualitative and quantitative analytics on NATO sentiments, NATO unity and NATO Article 5 trust opinion scores in different web sources: news sites found via Google Search API, Youtube videos with comments and Reddit discusions. The RAG approach using GPT-4.1 model was applied to analyse news where NATO related topics were discussed.
Two-level RAG analytics were used: on the first level, the GPT model generates qualitative news
summaries and quantitative opinion scores using zero-shot prompts; on the second level, the GPT model generates the summary of news summaries.
Quantitative news opinion scores generated by the GPT model were analysed using Bayesian regression to get trend lines. The distributions found for the regression parameters make it possible to analyse an uncertainty in the specified news opinion score trends.
The obtained results show a downward trend for analysed scores of opinion related to NATO unity.
The approach does not aim to conduct real political analysis; rather, it considers AI based approaches which can be used for further analytics
as a part of a complex analytical approach. The obtained results demonstrate that the use of GPT models for news analysis can give informative qualitative and quantitative analytics, providing important insights.
The dynamic model based neural ordinary differential equations were considered for modelling public opinion. The dynamic model based on neural ordinary differential equations was considered for modelling public opinions. This approach makes it possible to analyse different scenarios for evolving public opinions.

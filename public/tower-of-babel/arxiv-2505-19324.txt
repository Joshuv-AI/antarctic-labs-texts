Topological Complexity of symplectic CW-complexes
Luca Sandrock, Thomas Schick

Abstract
A cohomology class u u of a topological space X X is atoroidal if its pullback to the torus vanishes for every map from a torus to X X . Furthermore, X X is atoroidally symplectic if there is an atoroidal cohomology class u ∈ H sing 2 ​ ( X , F ) u\in H^{2}_{\sing}(X;F) such that u n ≠ 0 u^{n}\neq 0 . We prove that every atoroidally symplectic CW-complex X X of dimension 2 ​ n 2n has topological complexity 4 ​ n 4n . This generalizes a result of Grant and Mescher who prove the corresponding statement in the case where X X is an atoroidally c-symplectic manifold and u u is a de Rham cohomology class. Using this generalisation, we obtain new calculations of topological complexity, including for many products of 3-manifolds and of group presentation complexes.



1
Introduction

Motion planning is a computational problem which is concerned with finding transitions between configurations and
frequently occurs in applications, such as in path planning for robot motions.
Topological complexity is a numerical homotopy invariant, originally defined in
[
6
]
, which gives a rough,
but rigorous, lower bound on its complexity. To formalize this, let
X
be a path-connected topological space (of
configurations) and

PX:=C([0,1],X)

the space of continuous paths in
X
endowed with compact-open topology and let
\pi
be the fiber bundle projection

\pi\colon PX\to X\times X;\;\gamma\mapsto(\gamma(0),\gamma(1)).

The
topological complexity
of
X
, denoted
\TC(X)
, is the minimal
number
k
of sets forming an open cover
\{U_{i}\}_{i=0,\dots,k}
of

X\times X
such that for each
j\in\{0,\dots,k\}
there exists a continuous
map
s_{j}\colon U_{j}\to PX
with
\pi\circ s_{j}=\iota_{j}\colon U_{j}\hookrightarrow X\times X
. We set
\TC(X):=\infty
if no such
k
exists.

The topological complexity has an easy upper bound in terms of dimension and
connectivity
[
7
, Theorem 5.2]
: If
X
is an

(r-1)
-connected CW-complex (i.e. every map
f\colon S^{j}\to X
for
j<r
is
null-homotopic) then

\TC(X)\leq 2\frac{\dim(X)}{r}.

Of interest in this note is the case of a non-simply connected space, i.e.
r=1
,
and we look for conditions which give a matching lower bound on the
topological complexity.

This invariant has received considerable attention in the last decades. The
inspiration for this short note is the article
[
8
]
of Grant
and Mescher. In contrast to their work, we avoid the use of de Rham cohomology
on infinite dimensional manifolds and instead develop tools which use elementary
singular (co-)homology which allows us to generalize their main result from the
context of smooth manifolds to the one of arbitrary CW-complexes. An additional
benefit is that one now can also work with more general coefficient rings, further
generalizing the result. Along the way, we correct a small glitch in a Mayer-Vietoris
argument of
[
8
]
for the fiberwise join
P_{2}X
.

Some of the results of this thesis form part of Luca Sandrock’s
bachelor thesis
[
11
]
written at Universität Göttingen.

The starting point of
[
8
]
are the following two definitions:

1.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
A
a local coefficient system,

k\in\mathbb{N}
and
u\in H^{k}(X;A)
a cohomology class. This class is called
atoroidal
if

f^{*}u=0
for every map
f\colon T^{k}\to X
.

1.2 Definition
.

A connected smooth manifold
M
of dimension
2n
is called c-symplectic if
it is equipped with a closed 2-form
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
such that

[\omega]^{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}_{dR}(M)
. Note that every closed symplectic
manifold is also c-symplectic.

A c-symplectic manifold
(M,\omega)
is called
atoroidally c-symplectic

if
\omega
is atoroidal. In the given situation this is equivalent to

\int_{T^{2}}f^{*}\omega=0\quad\text{for every smooth map }f\colon T^{2}\to M.

The main result of
[
8
]
is its Theorem 1.2:

1.3 Theorem
.

Let
(M,\omega)
be an atoroidally c-symplectic manifold. Then

\TC(M)=2\dim(M).

We generalize this result to arbitrary CW-complexes and cohomology with
general coefficients.

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a connected CW-complex of dimension
2n
and let
F
be a
coefficient field with
\characteristic(F)=0
or

\characteristic(F)>2n
. Assume that
there exists an atoroidal cohomology class

u\in H^{2}(X;F)
such that
u^{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}(X;F)
.

Then
\TC(X)=2\dim(X)
.

We also have a variant of the theorem which is suitable in particular for
products of 2-dimensional aspherical complexes:

1.5 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a connected CW-complex of dimension
2n
and let
F
be a coefficient field with

\characteristic(F)\neq 2
. Assume there exist atoroidal cohomology classes
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in H^{2}(X;F)
such
that
u_{j}^{2}=0
for
j=1,\dots,n
and
u_{1}\cup\dots\cup u_{n}\neq 0\in H^{2n}(X;F)
.

Then
\TC(X)=2\dim(X)
.

Following
[
8
]
, the proof of Theorem
1.4
and
Theorem
1.5
uses the
concept of
\TC
-weight of cohomology classes introduced in

[
5
]
. Here,
u\in H^{*}(X\times X;F)
has
\wgt(u)\geq 2
if and
only if it is in the kernel of the homomorphism induced from the fiberwise join

p_{2}\colon P_{2}X\to X\times X
of the free path fibration with itself,
introduced below in Definition
2.3
. The key
point is that

u\neq 0\;\implies\;\TC(X)\geq\wgt(u).

Our main technical result is that for
u\in H^{2}(X;F)
the associated
zero-divisor

\overline{u}:=1\times u-u\times 1\in H^{2}(X\times X;F)

has

\wgt(\overline{u})=2
.

2
Cohomological Lower Bounds for the Topological Complexity

In this section, we recall the concept of
\TC
-weight introduced in

[
5
]
and defined as follows.

2.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a CW-complex and
A
a local coefficient system on
X\times X
. The
\TC
-weight
\wgt(u)\in\{0,1,\dots\}\cup\{\infty\}
of a class

u\in H^{*}(X\times X;A)
is the largest
k
such that
f^{*}u=0\in H^{*}(Y;f^{*}A)

for every continuous map
f\colon Y\to X\times X
for which there exists an
open covering
\{U_{1},\dots,U_{k}\}
of
Y
and continuous maps
f_{i}\colon U_{i}\to PX
with
\pi\circ f_{i}=f|_{U_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,k
.

The following result from
[
5
, Proposition 2]
allows to use the
\TC
-weight efficiently to get
lower bounds on the topological complexity:

2.2 Theorem
.

Let
X
be a CW-complex with local coefficient systems
A,B
on
X\times X
. Assume that
u_{A}\in H^{*}(X\times X;A)
and
u_{B}\in H^{*}(X\times X;B)
are
cohomology classes.

Then
u_{A}\cup u_{B}\in H^{*}(X\times X;A\otimes B)
satisfies

\wgt(u_{A}\cup u_{B})\geq\wgt(u_{A})+\wgt(u_{B}).

Moreover, if
u_{A}\neq 0
then
\TC(X)\geq\wgt(u_{A})
.

A criterion for the
\TC
-weight to be (at least) 2 is derived in

[
8
]
. It uses the fiberwise join
P_{2}X
of two copies of
\pi\colon PX\to X\times X
and has the following explicit definition.

2.3 Definition
.

Let
X
be a CW-complex with path space
PX
and end point map
\pi\colon PX\to X\times X
. Define the free loop space

LX:=\{\gamma\in PX\mid\gamma(0)=\gamma(1)\}

and for
j=1,2
the continuous maps

r_{j}\colon LX\to PX;\;\gamma\mapsto\gamma|_{I_{j}}\circ\psi_{j}

(2.4)

where
I_{1}:=[0,\frac{1}{2}]
and
I_{2}:=[\frac{1}{2},1]
are the left and
right half interval of
[0,1]
and
\psi_{j}\colon[0,1]\to I_{j}
are the
affine linear homeomorphisms
\psi_{1}(t):=\frac{t}{2}
and

\psi_{2}(t):=1-\frac{t}{2}
. Note that
\psi_{1}
preserves the orientation
whereas
\psi_{2}
reverses it.

One now defines
P_{2}X
as the double mapping cylinder of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, i.e.

P_{2}X:=\big((LX\times[1,2])\amalg(PX\times\{1\})\amalg(PX\times\{2\})\big)/\sim

with equivalence relation
\sim
generated by
(\gamma,1)\sim(r_{1}(\gamma),1)
and
(\gamma,2)\sim(r_{2}(\gamma),2)
for
\gamma\in LX
. We get two canonical inclusions
\iota_{j}\colon PX\to P_{2}X;\gamma\mapsto[(\gamma,j)]
for
j=1,2
.

Since
\pi\circ r_{1}=\pi\circ r_{2}
for the endpoint projection

\pi\colon PX\to X\times X
, this map descends to a well defined map

\pi_{2}\colon P_{2}X\to X\times X;\;[\gamma,t]\mapsto\begin{cases}(\gamma(0),\gamma(\frac{1}{2}));&\gamma\in LX\\
(\gamma(0),\gamma(1));&\gamma\in PX\end{cases}.

The following
[
8
, Proposition 2.3]
is the promised criterion for
\TC
-weight.

2.5 Proposition
.

Let
A
be a local coefficient system on
X\times X
and
u\in H^{*}(X\times X;A)
.

\text{If }\pi_{2}^{*}u=0\in H^{*}(P_{2}X;\pi_{2}^{*}A)\text{ then }\wgt(u)\geq 2.

3
Mayer-Vietoris on the Fiberwise Join

In this section, we obtain information about the cohomology of
P_{2}X
using a
Mayer-Vietoris decomposition. This follows the approach in
[
8
, Section
3]
but simplifies the arguments and corrects a glitch in the
proof of
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
.

To obtain the Mayer-Vietoris sequence, we start with the obvious projection

q\colon P_{2}X\to[1,2];[\gamma,t]\mapsto t
, then set

Z(r_{1}):=q^{-1}\left(\left[1,\frac{3}{2}\right]\right)\subset P_{2}X,\qquad Z(r_{2}):=q^{-1}\left(\left[\frac{3}{2},2\right]\right)\subset P_{2}X

and use the
decomposition
P_{2}X=Z(r_{1})\cup Z(r_{2})
with intersection
Z(r_{1})\cap Z(r_{2})=LX\times\{\frac{3}{2}\}
which we identify with
LX
. Note that
Z(r_{1})
and

Z(r_{2})
indeed are the mapping cylinders of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
, respectively.
We denote with
\delta_{MV}
the boundary map of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence

\xrightarrow{}H^{1}(P_{2}X;F)\xrightarrow{i_{1}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*}}H^{1}(Z(r_{1});F)\oplus H^{1}(Z(r_{2});F)\xrightarrow{\iota_{1}^{*}-\iota_{2}^{*}}H^{1}(LX;F)\xrightarrow{\delta_{MV}}H^{2}(P_{2}X;F)\xrightarrow{}

associated to this decomposition.
Our main technical result then is the following.

We denote with
\delta_{MV}\colon H^{1}(LX;F)\to H^{2}(P_{2}X;F)
the boundary map
of the Mayer-Vietoris sequence associated to this decomposition.

Our main technical result is then the following.

3.1 Proposition
.

Let
X
be a connected CW-complex,
A
an abelian coefficient group. Let
c\in C^{2}_{\sing}(X;A)
be a singular cocycle (i.e.
\partial(c)=0
for the
singular cochain boundary map
\partial
). Set

\overline{c}:=\pr_{2}^{*}c-\pr_{1}^{*}c\in C^{2}_{\sing}(X\times X;A)

and assume that there is a cochain
b_{c}\in C^{1}_{\sing}(PX;A)
such that
\partial(b_{c})=\pi^{*}\overline{c}
.

Then
a_{c}:=r_{1}^{*}b_{c}-r_{2}^{*}b_{c}\in C^{1}_{\sing}(LX;A)
is a cocycle and

\delta_{MV}([a_{c}])=\pi_{2}^{*}([\overline{c}])\in H^{2}(P_{2}X;A).

Proof.

First observe that
\pi\circ r_{1}=\pi\circ r_{2}
, hence by naturality

\partial(a_{c})=r_{1}^{*}(\partial b_{c})-r_{2}^{*}(\partial b_{c})=r_{1}^{*}\pi^{*}\overline{c}-r_{2}^{*}\pi^{*}\overline{c}=0.

To compute the Mayer-Vietoris boundary map, we use its snake lemma
definition applied to the Mayer-Vietoris short exact sequence of singular cochain groups

0\to C^{*}_{\sing}(P_{2}X;A)\xrightarrow{i_{1}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(Z(r_{1});A)\oplus C^{*}_{\sing}(Z(r_{2});A)\xrightarrow{\iota_{1}^{*}-\iota_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(LX;A)\to 0.

Specifically, given the cocycle
a_{c}\in C^{1}_{\sing}(LX;A)
, we need to find
cochains
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})\in C^{1}_{\sing}(Z(r_{1});A)\oplus C^{1}_{\sing}(Z(r_{2});A)
such that
\iota_{1}^{*}\beta_{1}-\iota_{2}^{*}\beta_{2}=a_{c}
, where
\iota_{k}\colon LX\to Z(r_{k})
are the
inclusion maps as end of the mapping cylinders.

In our case, we let
\pr_{k}\colon Z(r_{k})\to PX
be the projection to the
other end of the mapping cylinder (the standard homotopy equivalence) and
use the cocycles

(\pr_{1}^{*}b_{c},\pr_{2}^{*}b_{c})\in C^{1}_{\sing}(Z(r_{1});A)\oplus C^{1}_{\sing}(Z(r_{2});A).

As we have
\pr_{k}\circ\iota_{k}=r_{k}
, by naturality we indeed get

\iota_{1}^{*}\pr_{1}^{*}b_{c}-\iota_{2}^{*}\pr_{2}^{*}b_{c}=r_{1}^{*}b_{c}-r_{2}^{*}b_{c}=a_{c}.

We next have to compute the coboundary of
\pr_{k}^{*}b_{c}
. By naturality of the
coboundary map we obtain

\partial(\pr_{k}^{*}b_{c})=\pr_{k}^{*}(\partial b_{c})=\pr_{k}^{*}\pi^{*}\overline{c}=i_{k}^{*}(\pi_{2}^{*}\overline{c}).

Here,
i_{k}\colon Z(r_{k})\to P_{2}X
is the natural inclusion and the final
equality follows because upon composition with the endpoint projection to

X\times X
, all relevant maps become equal:
\pi_{2}\circ i_{k}=\pi\circ\pr_{k}\colon Z(r_{k})\to X\times X
. Now recall that
i_{2}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*}
is the
inclusion map of the short exact sequence of singular cochain groups

0\to C^{*}_{\sing}(P_{2}X;A)\xrightarrow{i_{1}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(Z(r_{1});A)\oplus C^{*}_{\sing}(Z(r_{2});A)\xrightarrow{\iota_{1}^{*}-\iota_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(LX;A)\to 0.

We just saw that

(i_{1}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*})(\pi_{2}^{*}\overline{c})=\partial(\pr_{1}^{*}b_{c},\pr_{2}^{*}b_{c}),

hence by the snake lemma indeed

\delta_{MV}([a_{c}])=\pi_{2}^{*}[\overline{c}]

which is what we have to prove.
∎

3.2 Remark
.

The glitch in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 3.5]
we mentioned
above is the
application of the “snake lemma” to a sequence of singular cochain
complexes

0\to C^{*}_{\sing}(P_{2}M;\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{i_{1}^{*}\oplus i_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(\mathcal{P}M;\mathbb{R})\oplus C^{*}_{\sing}(\mathcal{P}M;\mathbb{R})\xrightarrow{r_{1}^{*}-r_{2}^{*}}C^{*}_{\sing}(\Lambda M;\mathbb{R})\to 0

which however is
not
an exact sequence of cochain complexes but only chain
homotopy equivalent to one. In general, the calculation of the boundary map
of the associated long exact sequence via homotopy equivalent replacements
is not guaranteed to work. With a stroke of luck, in our case the idea
works however, since the relevant classes pull back from
M\times M
and
the chain homotopy equivalences are compatible with the maps to
M\times M
.

4
The TC-Weight of an atoroidal Cohomology Class

We now want to combine Propositions
2.5
and

6.2
to bound the TC-weight of our atoroidal classes
from below. As a tool, we transport singular (co-)chains between a
space and its path space using the exponential adjunction, whose properties we
first collect in the following subsection. These results should be well known
and are elementary, we supply them for completeness of the exposition.

4.1
Singular Chains of a Space and its Path Space

4.1 Definition
.

Let
X
be a topological space and
A
a coefficient group. We define the transformation

b\colon C^{*}_{\sing}(X;A)\to C_{\sing}^{*-1}(PX;A);c\mapsto b_{c}

setting

b_{c}:=c\circ T\colon C_{1}^{\sing}(PX)\xrightarrow{T}C_{2}^{\sing}(X)\xrightarrow{c}A,

where
T
is defined via the exponential law as follows.
Let

\Psi\colon C(\Delta_{k},PX)\to C(\Delta_{k}\times[0,1],X)

be the inverse map of the tensor-hom adjunction given by

\Psi f(x,s):=(\sigma(x))(s);\qquad\forall\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to PX,\,x\in\Delta_{k},\,s\in[0,1].

Here,
\Delta_{k}
is the standard k-simplex spanned by vertices
v_{0},\dots,v_{k}
.
For
j=0,\dots,k
let
\iota_{j}\colon\Delta_{k+1}\to\Delta_{k}\times[0,1]

be the standard embeddings decomposing
\Delta_{k}\times[0,1]

into
(k+1)
-dimensional simplices. Explicitly, these are the affine linear maps with

\iota_{j}(v_{i})=\begin{cases}(v_{i},0);&i\leq j\\
(v_{i-1},1);&i>j.\end{cases}

(4.2)

We then define

T(\sigma\colon\Delta_{1}\to PX):=\Psi(\sigma)\circ\iota_{0}-\Psi(\sigma)\circ\iota_{1}.

A crucial property is the relation between the singular coboundary maps and
the transformation
b
, which reads as follows:

4.3 Lemma
.

In the situation of Definition
4.1
, let
\pi\colon PX\to X\times X
be the endpoint evaluation map. For
c\in C^{k}_{\sing}(X;A)
then

\partial(b_{c})=-b_{\partial c}+\pi^{*}\pr_{2}^{*}c-\pi^{*}\pr_{1}^{*}c.

(4.4)

Define
\overline{c}:=\pr_{2}(c)-\pr_{1}(c)\in C^{2}_{\sing}(X\times X;A)
. If
c
is a cocycle,
i.e.
\partial c=0
, we then get as short version of Equation
(
4.4
) that

\partial(b_{c})=\pi^{*}\overline{c}.

Proof.

This is an elementary and direct consequence of the definition of
b_{c}
and
the simplicial coboundary: given
\sigma\colon\Delta_{k}\to PX
, the formula
for
\partial(b_{c})(\sigma)=b_{c}(\partial\sigma)
is a sum over the
different boundary components of
\sigma
and the different summands in

b_{c}
coming from the decomposition of
\Delta_{k-1}\times[0,1]
into
k-simplices. In this sum, one obtains “interior” contributions of
k-simplices mapped to the interior of
\Delta_{k}\times[0,1]
. These occur in
pairs which cancel out. Additionally, one obtains a top and a bottom
contribution, corresponding to
\Delta_{k}\times\{1\}
and
\Delta_{k}\times\{0\}
giving rise to the last two summands in Equation
(
4.4
). Finally, one obtains contributions of k-simplices
mapped to
(\partial(\Delta_{k}))\times[0,1]
which result precisely in the
first summand. Writing out the elementary formulas is left to the reader.
∎

4.5 Proposition
.

Let
X
be a topological space,
A
an abelian coefficient group and
c\in C^{2}_{\sing}(X;A)
a cocycle representing an atoroidal cohomology
class. Recall that this means that
f^{*}[c]=0\in H^{2}(T^{2};A)
for every continuous map
f\colon T^{2}\to X
.

Then, for
a_{c}:=r_{1}^{*}b_{c}-r_{2}^{*}b_{c}\in C^{1}_{\sing}(LX;A)
as in
Proposition
3.1
we have

[a_{c}]=0\in H^{1}(LX;A).

(4.6)

In particular, then also
\pi_{2}^{*}\overline{c}=0\in H^{2}_{\sing}(P_{2}X;A)

and
\overline{c}\in H^{2}(X\times X;A)
has TC-weight
\wgt(\overline{c})=2
.

Proof.

The last statement follows from (
4.6
), namely that the cohomology class of
a_{c}

vanishes, together with Propositions
3.1
and

2.5
.

It remains to show that
[a_{c}]=0\in H^{1}_{\sing}(LX;A)
. Now, in degree
1

the universal coefficient theorem gives the isomorphism

H_{\sing}^{1}(LX;A)\cong\Hom(H^{sing}_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z}),A).

By the Hurewicz
homomorphism it therefore suffices to show that

a_{c}(\sigma\colon[0,1]\to LX)=0\quad\forall\sigma\colon[0,1]\to LX\text{ with }\sigma(0)=\sigma(1),

i.e. for the evaluation of
a_{c}
on the image of the
fundamental class of
S^{1}
under the map
\overline{\sigma}\colon S^{1}\to LX
defined by
\sigma
. Here and later, we use the identifications
[0,1]\cong\Delta_{1}
and on
the other hand
S^{1}=[0,1]/\sim
with the equivalence relation
\sim
generated by
0\sim 1
.

To conveniently write down a formula for
a_{c}(\sigma)
we apply the tensor-hom adjunction and define

\Psi(\sigma)\colon T^{2}=S^{1}\times S^{1}\to X;(t,s)\mapsto\overline{\sigma}(t)(s).

Now we obtain in a straightforward way from the definitions of our maps that

a_{c}(\sigma\colon[0,1]\to LX)=(\Psi(\sigma)^{*}c)(\tau)

(4.7)

where
\tau\in C_{2}^{\sing}(T^{2})
is the signed sum of four singular simplices

\tau=\sum_{j=1}^{2}\sum_{k=0}^{1}(-1)^{j+1+k}(\sigma_{j,k}\colon\Delta_{2}\to T^{2}).

Here
\sigma_{j,k}\colon\Delta_{2}\to[0,1]/\sim\times[0,1]/\sim

is the affine linear map defined by sending the vertices
(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})
of

\Delta_{2}
to the following image points:

(j,k)

(1,0)

(1,1)

(2,0)

(2,1)

v_{0}

(0,0)

(0,0)

(0,1)

(0,1)

v_{1}

(0,\frac{1}{2})

(1,0)

(0,\frac{1}{2})

(1,1)

v_{2}

(1,\frac{1}{2})

(1,\frac{1}{2})

(1,\frac{1}{2})

(1,\frac{1}{2})

It is straightforward to see that
\tau
is a singular cycle, indeed it is
a fundamental cycle of
T^{2}
.

By assumption, the pullback of the cohomology class represented by

along any map from
T^{2}
to
X
vanishes, in particular also

[\Psi(\sigma)^{*}c]=0\in H^{2}_{\sing}(T^{2};A)
, and hence

as

is a singular cycle. But by equation (

) this
is precisely what we have to establish to conclude the proof of

4.5
.
∎

5
Proof of the Main Theorems

At this point, the proof of our main Theorems
1.4
and
1.5
is
rather straightforward and follows the same lines as
[
8
, Section 4]
.

Proof of Theorem
1.4
.

Assume we have the connected CW-complex
X
of dimension
2n
, the coefficient field

F
and the atoroidal cohomology class
u\in H^{2}(X;F)
with
u^{n}\neq 0
.

Form
\overline{u}:=\pi_{1}^{*}u-\pi_{2}^{*}u\in H^{2}(X\times X;F)
. Consider

\overline{u}^{2n}\in H^{4n}(X\times X;F)\cong\bigoplus_{j=0}^{4n}H^{j}(X;F)\otimes H^{2n-j}(X;F).

Then the component of

\overline{u}^{2n}
in the summand
H^{2n}(X;F)\otimes H^{2n}(X;F)
is

(-1)^{n}\binom{2n}{n}u^{n}\otimes u^{n}.

Now by assumption
u^{n}\neq 0
and if
\characteristic(F)>2n
or
\characteristic(F)=0
also

\binom{2n}{n}\neq 0
and consequently also
\overline{u}^{n}\neq 0
.

By Proposition
4.5
,
\wgt(\overline{u})\geq 2
and by Theorem

2.2

\wgt(\overline{u}^{2n})\geq 2n
and therefore
\TC(X)\geq 4n
.
Hence,
\TC(X)=4n
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Given the connected CW-complex
X
of dimension

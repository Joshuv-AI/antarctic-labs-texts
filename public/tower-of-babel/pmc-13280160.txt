Hybrid Vi+ECNN framework for advanced ADHD diagnostic accuracy in medical imaging
Authors: Shaymaa E Sorour, Lamia Hassan, Osman Elwasila, Tsunenori Mine, Mohamed Ali Nagy Elmaadaway
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAttention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a prevalent neurodevelopmental disorder that imposes significant personal and societal burdens. Traditional diagnostic approaches, which rely on behavioral assessments, are susceptible to subjectivity and variability, underscoring the need for objective and automated diagnostic tools. This study develops an ADHD-specific, biologically informed multi-stream deep learning framework for pediatric brain MRI classification, in which a Vision Transformer (ViT) and an Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN) are integrated with Raw MRI, Phase Spectrum Transform (PST), and Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED) representations to capture complementary global and local neuroanatomical characteristics. The architecture leverages complementary modeling capacities by combining global contextual representations from ViT with localized discriminative features extracted by ECNN across a biologically informed multi-stream preprocessing strategy, including Raw MRI to preserve global anatomy, Phase Spectrum Transform (PST) to highlight cortical boundary irregularities, and Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED) to enhance subtle gray–white matter contrasts. Experimental evaluations conducted on a stratified pediatric MRI dataset demonstrated that the proposed ViT+ECNN model achieved a classification accuracy of 99.4%, precision of 99.3%, recall of 99.5%, and an F1-score of 0.99, substantially outperforming standalone ViT and ECNN configurations. Introduction

Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is among the most prevalent neurodevelopmental disorders affecting children and adolescents globally. Recent epidemiological studies estimate the lifetime prevalence to range from 2% to 7%, reflecting both increased awareness and improved diagnostic practices1–3. The COVID-19 pandemic has further contributed to rising recognition of ADHD symptoms due to changes in educational environments and psychosocial stressors4,5.

ADHD is characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that lead to significant functional impairments across academic, social, and emotional domains6–8. Children with ADHD frequently experience poor peer relationships, low self-esteem, and chronic academic underachievement9–11. The disorder imposes a considerable burden on families, educational systems, and healthcare resources, with substantial long-term costs associated with untreated symptoms12–14.

Despite advances in clinical understanding, the diagnosis of ADHD remains primarily based on behavioral assessments and structured clinical interviews1. This reliance on subjective evaluation introduces variability and the potential for underdiagnosis or misclassification, particularly in culturally diverse populations3,15. Delayed identification and treatment can exacerbate impairments, increasing the risk of adverse outcomes in adolescence and adulthood, including substance use disorders, occupational instability, and heightened mortality16,17.

In recent years, neuroimaging has emerged as a promising tool for elucidating the neurobiological substrates of ADHD. Structural and functional MRI studies have consistently identified alterations in fronto-striatal and fronto-parietal networks implicated in attention regulation and executive functioning18,19. Converging evidence suggests that integrating objective neuroimaging biomarkers with behavioral data could improve diagnostic accuracy and facilitate personalized treatment strategies20,21.

Concurrently, advances in machine learning and deep learning have demonstrated the feasibility of automated diagnostic support tools capable of detecting ADHD-related patterns in imaging data with high sensitivity and specificity22. In particular, hybrid architectures combining Vision Transformers and Convolutional Neural Networks have shown promise in modeling both global contextual dependencies and localized discriminative features23.

While prior research has demonstrated the feasibility of deep learning approaches for ADHD detection24,25, most existing studies have important limitations. Many rely exclusively on convolutional neural networks (CNNs)24,26 or transformer architectures27 in isolation, which restricts their ability to simultaneously capture localized anatomical details and global contextual relationships within neuroimaging data. Furthermore, several earlier works employ single-channel input pipelines without incorporating diverse image preprocessing techniques28, potentially overlooking complementary information inherent in raw and enhanced representations. These constraints can limit diagnostic sensitivity, reduce generalizability across imaging conditions, and hamper performance in detecting subtle neurodevelopmental patterns23,29.

To address these limitations, this study proposes an ADHD-specific, biologically informed multi-stream framework for pediatric MRI classification that integrates Vision Transformer (ViT) and Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN) components to capture complementary global and local neuroanatomical characteristics from Raw MRI, Phase Spectrum Transform (PST), and Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED) representations. Within this design, raw T1-weighted MRI preserves global anatomical organization, PST enhances cortical boundary irregularities, and QHED highlights subtle gray–white matter contrast differences. Unlike conventional ViT–CNN pipelines that generally rely on a single normalized imaging stream, the proposed framework is explicitly aligned with disorder-relevant structural characteristics of ADHD. The ViT branch models long-range structural dependencies, whereas the ECNN branch captures localized cortical and subcortical morphological patterns, thereby establishing a task-specific global–local learning scheme. By coupling biologically meaningful multi-representation inputs with complementary learning components, the framework aims to improve the consistency, robustness, and diagnostic accuracy of automated ADHD classification in pediatric brain MRI data.

The main contributions of this study are threefold. First, it introduces a biologically informed multi-stream representation framework for ADHD MRI analysis using Raw, PST, and QHED inputs. Second, it establishes a task-specific global–local learning architecture in which ViT and ECNN are integrated to model complementary ADHD-related structural patterns rather than functioning as a generic hybrid backbone. Third, it provides comparative and ablation-based evidence demonstrating that the proposed disorder-aware design yields more robust and accurate classification performance than standalone ViT and ECNN baselines.

Literature review

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) is a complex neurodevelopmental condition characterized by persistent patterns of inattention, hyperactivity, and impulsivity that result in significant functional impairment across developmental stages. It affects both children and adults and has been consistently associated with adverse academic, social, and emotional outcomes. Diagnostic approaches have historically relied on clinical evaluation and behavioral observation, methodologies that, while standardized, remain vulnerable to subjectivity and variability.

Traditional diagnostic frameworks are anchored in comprehensive clinical assessments conducted by trained professionals, typically psychiatrists or psychologists. These assessments integrate information from medical, developmental, educational, and familial histories to ascertain the presence and severity of symptoms30. The Diagnostic and Statistical Manual of Mental Disorders (DSM-5) remains the primary reference, outlining specific criteria that require the manifestation of a defined number of symptoms over a sustained period inconsistent with developmental norms30. Behavioral rating scales, such as the Conners Rating Scale and ADHD Rating Scale, provide structured tools to quantify symptomatology relative to normative benchmarks31. However, these measures are subject to informant bias and differential interpretations shaped by cultural expectations and stigma32.

The heterogeneity of ADHD presentations further complicates diagnostic accuracy. Individuals may exhibit predominantly inattentive, hyperactive-impulsive, or combined symptom profiles, and comorbid psychiatric conditions-such as anxiety, depression, and learning disorders-frequently obscure clinical clarity33. These complexities often necessitate extended diagnostic evaluations, involving multiple observations across contexts and informants, which can be time- and resource-intensive34.

In response to these challenges, the integration of objective measures has gained increasing attention. Neuroimaging modalities, including functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) and electroencephalography (EEG), have demonstrated reproducible alterations in neural systems implicated in attentional control, impulse regulation, and executive functioning. For example, fMRI studies have identified aberrant activation in fronto-striatal and fronto-parietal networks, while EEG research has highlighted distinctive patterns in theta/beta power ratios7. Despite their promise, these approaches are limited by high costs, specialized analytic requirements, and variable sensitivity and specificity in individual cases.

Recent advances in digital health technologies offer additional avenues for innovation. Mobile applications and web-based platforms facilitate continuous ecological monitoring of behavior, providing rich datasets that can inform clinical decision-making35. Machine learning algorithms applied to such multimodal data have shown potential for improving diagnostic precision by uncovering latent patterns associated with ADHD risk.

Parallel progress in deep learning methodologies has further expanded the scope of automated diagnostic tools. Convolutional neural networks (CNNs) have demonstrated considerable efficacy in extracting hierarchical features from neuroimaging data to classify neurodevelopmental disorders24. Vision Transformers (ViTs), leveraging self-attention mechanisms, have emerged as powerful architectures capable of modeling long-range dependencies and capturing global contextual information in medical images27. Hybrid CNN–ViT models have been shown to outperform standalone approaches, combining localized feature representation with broader contextual understanding23. Although hybrid CNN–ViT models have shown strong performance in medical imaging, these approaches are typically formulated as general-purpose fusion architectures and are commonly applied to single normalized image streams. In contrast, the present study is designed specifically for ADHD structural MRI and is grounded in neuroanatomical evidence. Its distinguishing characteristic lies in coupling a biologically informed multi-stream preprocessing strategy-Raw MRI, PST, and QHED-with a task-specific global–local learning framework, thereby aligning representation design with known ADHD-related cortical and subcortical abnormalities.

Structural MRI studies have shown that ADHD is associated with atypical cortical morphology, including altered cortical thickness, gyrification, and local boundary irregularities in the prefrontal cortex, cingulate regions, and fronto-striatal networks36–38. ADHD has also been linked to subtle gray–white matter contrast variations and volumetric shifts within the basal ganglia, cerebellar cortex, and dorsolateral prefrontal cortex39 . These findings indicate that ADHD pathology manifests across multiple spatial dimensions-global structure, cortical boundary integrity, and local tissue-intensity characteristics-which motivates the use of multi-stream preprocessing to capture these heterogeneous neuroanatomical signatures.

Enhanced CNN models (ECNNs), incorporating innovations such as attention modules, residual connections, and advanced regularization techniques, have been developed to address limitations of conventional CNNs, including overfitting and limited expressiveness29. Empirical evaluations have demonstrated that ECNNs can achieve superior performance in various classification tasks; for example, ECNNs have achieved 3–5% improvements in chest X-ray diagnostic accuracy compared to standard CNN architectures29. Similarly, hybrid CNN–ViT models have shown accuracy improvements exceeding 10% in dermatological image classification28. Although these approaches remain computationally demanding, advances in optimization strategies and hardware capabilities have progressively enhanced their feasibility for clinical application40.

In addition to structural MRI approaches, recent research has demonstrated that ADHD is strongly associated with alterations in dynamic functional connectivity. Wavelet-based temporal–frequency attention networks applied to fMRI data have shown that nonlinear temporal dynamics provide discriminative biomarkers that are not observable in static T1 images41–45. These models capture distributed temporal fluctuations across the prefrontal–striatal circuit and have reported significant gains in neurodevelopmental disorder classification. While the present work focuses exclusively on structural MRI, such temporal–frequency approaches represent a complementary direction for future multimodal extensions of our ViT+ECNN framework.

Neuroimaging studies of ADHD consistently report abnormalities in cortical thickness, irregularities in prefrontal–striatal circuits, and atypical gray–white matter contrast patterns46. These biological markers directly motivate our preprocessing design: the Phase Spectrum Transform (PST) emphasizes fine-grained cortical boundaries and frequency-domain irregularities, while Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED) enhances subtle gray–white matter contrast differences and suppresses noise. Together with raw MRI representations, these inputs are aligned with known ADHD neuroanatomical signatures, providing a biologically informed basis for multi-stream feature extraction.

Despite these developments, significant challenges persist regarding the integration of deep learning models into clinical workflows. Concerns include data privacy, algorithmic transparency, interpretability of predictions, and the potential for algorithmic bias to perpetuate disparities in healthcare access and outcomes47. As such, future research must prioritize rigorous validation, standardization of evaluation protocols, and development of user-centered tools that preserve the central role of clinician expertise in diagnosis and treatment planning.

Overall, the convergence of neuroimaging, digital health tools, and deep learning methodologies holds considerable promise for enhancing the diagnostic process in ADHD. However, realizing this potential requires careful alignment of technological innovation with ethical principles, robust empirical evidence, and sustained investment in translational research.

Recent advances in attention-based architectures, including temporal-frequency attention networks41–45, have demonstrated strong performance in modeling dynamic patterns in EEG and fMRI data for ADHD and related neurodevelopmental disorders. While these methods capture temporal-spectral dependencies, our work differs by focusing on structural MRI and integrating multi-stream preprocessing with a hybrid ViT+ECNN. Future extensions may bridge these approaches for multimodal diagnostic support.

Proposed model

This section introduces the proposed framework, designed for the classification of Attention Deficit Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD), as illustrated in Fig. 1, utilizing pediatric brain MRI scans. The architecture is built to integrate global and local image features by leveraging a hybrid approach that combines a Vision Transformer (ViT) with an Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN). To maximize feature diversity, three parallel input streams are employed: Raw images, Phase Spectrum Transform (PST), and Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED). Each stream undergoes independent processing through ViT and ECNN pathways. Input data

The MRI dataset used comprises T1-weighted scans of children between the ages of 7 and 12. Each sample is annotated with clinical labels (ADHD or typically developing), in addition to demographic attributes such as age and gender. All subject data was de-identified in compliance with ethical standards. To maintain consistency and ensure compatibility with pre-trained transformer models, all images were spatially aligned, intensity-normalized, and resized to 224 224 pixels. Standardization of pixel values was conducted using ImageNet normalization statistics (mean and standard deviation).

Deep neural architectures for medical diagnosis

The proposed framework utilizes a hybrid architecture composed of a Vision Transformer (ViT) and an Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN). The ViT component focuses on capturing global spatial relationships across the entire image, while the ECNN module specializes in extracting local, fine-grained features using residual convolutional layers. The combination of these two architectures allows the model to simultaneously benefit from global context and local discriminative patterns, improving diagnostic precision in pediatric neuroimaging.

While ViTs are adept at capturing global contextual relationships across entire images through self-attention mechanisms, ECNNs provide robust local feature extraction and hierarchical representations that are critical for detecting subtle neuroanatomical variations associated with ADHD.

In our experiments, the dataset was provided in the form of pre-extracted 2D slices derived from whole-brain T1-weighted MRI volumes. The strong classification performance achieved by the ViT+ECNN model can be interpreted in light of established ADHD-related neuroanatomical markers. The ViT branch captures long-range structural dependencies and global contextual relationships across the brain image, whereas the ECNN branch focuses on localized cortical and subcortical morphological patterns. PST enhances cortical boundary irregularities, whereas QHED improves the visibility of subtle gray–white matter contrast differences in prefrontal and subcortical regions, both of which have been repeatedly associated with ADHD in prior neuroimaging studies46. At the same time, the reported performance should be interpreted with appropriate caution. Although the training protocol incorporated several safeguards against overfitting, including stratified 5-fold cross-validation, data augmentation, and early stopping, the possibility of performance inflation under controlled experimental conditions cannot be entirely excluded. In particular, the present study did not explicitly examine robustness under reduced-sample training regimes or intentionally noise-corrupted MRI inputs. Accordingly, future validation under limited-data, noisy-imaging, and cross-institutional settings will be important to further establish the stability, robustness, and generalizability of the proposed framework.

Explainability and biological interpretation

To assess whether the proposed ViT–ECNN hybrid framework captures biologically meaningful neuroanatomical patterns associated with ADHD, rather than relying on spurious correlations, we performed qualitative interpretability analysis using transformer attention rollout and convolutional activation inspection.

ViT Attention Visualization. Attention rollout maps were generated from the final transformer encoder block following the method of Chefer et al.66. The resulting attention distributions consistently highlighted the prefrontal cortex, basal ganglia, and cerebellar regions-structural biomarkers repeatedly associated with ADHD pathophysiology. Large-cohort neuroimaging studies have reported delayed cortical maturation in prefrontal areas36, reduced volumes in striatal nuclei39, and cerebellar abnormalities38. The observed attention patterns therefore confirm that the model focuses on clinically established ADHD-relevant structures rather than non-biological sources of discrimination.

ECNN feature activation analysis. Intermediate convolutional feature responses exhibited strong activation along cortical folding boundaries and striatal margins. These locations correspond to high-frequency cortical thickness and surface irregularities that prior structural MRI analyses have identified as ADHD markers37,46. Although feature maps are not shown as figures, their spatial distribution confirms that the ECNN branch contributes fine-grained morphological information that is complementary to the global contextual encoding learned by the ViT backbone.

Interpretability implications. Taken together, these analyses demonstrate that the proposed architecture does not operate as a black-box classifier but instead encodes biologically validated anatomical substrates of ADHD. Future work will incorporate quantitative saliency agreement metrics and expert radiological comparison to further strengthen the clinical interpretability of the framework.

Conclusions

This study presents a hybrid deep learning framework that integrates a Vision Transformer (ViT) with an Enhanced Convolutional Neural Network (ECNN) for the automated classification of Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (ADHD) from pediatric brain MRI data. The proposed architecture combines the global contextual modeling capability of transformer-based attention with the localized feature extraction strength of convolutional layers, enabling the learning of complementary neuroanatomical patterns relevant to ADHD.

The principal contribution of this work lies not in hybridization alone, but in integrating biologically meaningful multi-stream structural representations into an ADHD-specific global–local learning framework.

To enhance the diversity and robustness of structural feature extraction, the framework incorporates three parallel preprocessing streams: Raw MRI, Phase Spectrum Transform (PST), and Quantile Histogram Equalization with Denoising (QHED). Experimental results demonstrate that this multi-stream hybrid configuration consistently outperforms standalone ViT and ECNN models across all evaluation metrics, underscoring the value of integrating biologically informed representations for more reliable ADHD classification.

These results indicate that combining biologically informed representations with complementary global–local modeling contributes directly to the improved classification performance.

Although the proposed framework achieved a diagnostic accuracy of 99.4%, ADHD remains a heterogeneous disorder, and the findings must be interpreted with caution. Additional validation across multi-institutional cohorts will be required to confirm generalizability and exclude potential dataset-specific biases. A further limitation of the current study is that robustness was not directly evaluated under reduced-sample training settings or explicitly noise-corrupted MRI inputs, and such analyses will be necessary in future work to confirm model stability in more challenging real-world clinical scenarios. Furthermore, while the model demonstrates high predictive performance, it does not replace the DSM-5 clinical diagnostic process; instead, it provides an objective imaging-based decision-support component that may complement clinical evaluation.

An important limitation of the present study is that the proposed framework operates exclusively on static structural MRI and therefore does not capture temporal or frequency-dependent neural dynamics associated with ADHD. Accordingly, future work will extend the hybrid ViT+ECNN framework toward temporal functional MRI analysis by integrating advanced temporal-frequency attention mechanisms, including Fourier-based and wavelet-based attention architectures, in order to capture ADHD-related connectivity alterations that are not accessible through static anatomical imaging alone.

Future work will also benchmark the proposed framework against advanced attention-based architectures41–45, investigate its integration with DSM-5 assessments to evaluate combined diagnostic utility, and employ grouped Bayesian optimization to systematically explore learning-rate schedules, weight decay, and optimizer configurations. In addition, future studies will further investigate the individual contribution of each representation stream to enhance model interpretability and strengthen its neurobiological grounding. Large-scale multi-institutional validation, as well as longitudinal and multimodal extensions combining structural MRI, functional neuroimaging, and clinical variables, will also be essential for deeper characterization of ADHD neurodevelopmental trajectories.

SHIFTED DOUBLE POISSON STRUCTURES AND NONCOMMUTATIVE POISSON EXTENSIONS
Leilei Liu, Jieheng Zeng, Hu Zhao

Abstract
. We develop a theory of noncommutative Poisson extensions. For an augmented dg algebra A A , we show that any shifted double Poisson bracket on A A induces a graded Lie algebra structure on the reduced cyclic homology. Under the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle, we further prove that the noncommutative Poisson extension is compatible with noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction. Moreover, we show that shifted double Poisson structures are independent of the choice of cofibrant resolutions and that they induce shifted Poisson structures on the derived moduli stack of representations.



1.
Introduction

Recently, motivated by three-dimensional
\mathcal{N}=4
mirror symmetry and geometric representation theory, Poisson geometry on quiver varieties has attracted growing interest across several areas of mathematics.
At the same time, quiver varieties play a fundamental role in noncommutative geometry.
Following the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle—which states that a noncommutative geometric structure is meaningful only when it induces the corresponding classical structure on representation schemes—it is natural to seek an intrinsic, noncommutative description of Poisson geometry for quiver varieties.

Let
Q
be a finite quiver. In
[
6
,
13
]
, Crawley-Boevey, Etingof, Ginzburg, and Van den Bergh developed noncommutative symplectic and Poisson geometry and showed that preprojective algebras arise from the path algebra
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}
via noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction.
As an immediate consequence, the commutator quotient
\Pi Q/[\Pi Q,\Pi Q]
carries a Lie bracket, and the image of the trace map generates the coordinate ring of the quiver variety as a Poisson algebra.
Moreover, this Lie bracket descends to the zeroth reduced cyclic homology
\overline{\mathsf{HC}}_{0}(\Pi Q)
. This motivates the following questions:

Question 1.1
.

(1)

Does there exist a Lie algebra structure on the full reduced cyclic homology
\overline{\mathsf{HC}}_{\bullet}(\Pi Q)
?

(2)

How can such a Lie algebra structure be explained at the cochain level?

For non-Dynkin quivers, preprojective algebras are Koszul Calabi–Yau, and hence the works
[
3
,
4
]
provide affirmative answers to the above questions.
However, for Dynkin quivers, preprojective algebras are not Koszul Calabi–Yau; this work presents an approach that uniformly handles both cases.

Our key tool is the Feigin–Tsygan theorem as developed by Berest–Khachatryan–Ramadoss
[
2
]
. For an algebra
A
, the reduced cyclic homology
\overline{\mathsf{HC}}_{\bullet}(A)
can be computed as the cohomology of

\displaystyle{\frac{\tilde{A}}{S+[\tilde{A},\tilde{A}]}}
,
where
\tilde{A}
is a cofibrant resolution of
A
. For a finite quiver
Q
the preprojective algebra
\Pi Q
is augmented, and the cobar–bar construction
\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{B}\Pi Q
provides a natural cofibrant resolution of
\Pi Q
. One of our main results answers Question
1.1
positively.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.11
)
.

Let
Q
be a finite quiver.
There exists a dg Loday algebra structure on
\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{B}\Pi Q
.
Furthermore, this dg Loday structure induces a graded Lie algebra structure on
\overline{\mathsf{HC}}_{\bullet}(\Pi Q)
.

A crucial observation in the proof is the following isomorphism of coaugmented dg coalgebras for any augmented dg algebra
A
:

(1)

\mathbf{B}A\cong S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle.

First, we introduce the
\mathfrak{s}
-construction. Using (
1
), the cobar–bar construction is identified with the dg algebra

(T_{S}\,\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle),\partial+b+\delta,\otimes)
.
When
A
is a dg double Poisson algebra, the
\mathfrak{s}
-construction endows
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A=A[d]
with a canonical double Poisson bracket; see Section
5
for details.

Second, we develop a theory of noncommutative Poisson extensions. Given a dg algebra
A
, form the free product
A\langle t\rangle=A\ast_{S}S[t]
.
We analyze when double Poisson brackets on
A
extend to
A\langle t\rangle
, when NC Poisson structures (see Definition
2.6
) extend, and how these two extension problems are related.
In particular, we show that NC Poisson extensions are compatible with noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction and align with the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle.

We also prove a more general extension result: the double Poisson bracket extends to the cobar–bar construction.

Theorem 1.4

(Theorem
5.7
, Corollary
5.9
)
.

Let
(A\in\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S},\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Then the cobar-bar construction
\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{B}A
is a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Furthermore, the reduced cyclic homology of
A
carries a graded Lie algebra structure of degree
n
.

The latter statement may be regarded as a noncommutative analogue of the fact that the cohomology of a smooth Poisson manifold carries a graded Lie algebra structure.

All constructions above are carried out with respect to an explicit cofibrant resolution.
In the derived setting,
one expects that noncommutative structures are independent of the choice of cofibrant resolution.
We therefore establish:

Theorem 1.5

(Theorem
2.19
)
.

Let
A
be a homologically smooth dg algebra.
Then the space of dg double Poisson structures is independent of the choice of cofibrant resolution.

For completeness, Section
3
explains how shifted double Poisson structures fit naturally into the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle.
In particular, we prove that a shifted double Poisson structure induces a shifted Poisson structure on the derived moduli stack of representations.

1.1.
Conventions and notation

•

\mathbb{K}
denotes an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.

•

Algebras are associative algebras over a semisimple ring
S=\bigoplus_{i\in I}\mathbb{K}e_{i}
, where the
e_{i}
are mutually orthogonal idempotents.
The category of such algebras over
S
is denoted by
{\rm Alg}_{S}
.

•

We abbreviate
-\otimes_{S}-
as
-\otimes-
.

•

We use cohomological grading: all complexes are cochain complexes supported in non-positive degrees. For a complex
V
, the homology is related to cohomology by
\mathrm{H}_{n}(V)=\mathrm{H}^{-n}(V^{\bullet})
.

•

\mathrm{Com}_{S}
denotes the category of cochain complexes of
S
-modules.

•

\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
denotes the category of differential graded
S
-algebras supported in non-positive degrees. The notation
\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
denotes the category of augmented dg
S
-algebras supported in non-positive degrees.

•

Let
A
be a dg algebra. In this work, by a left (right)
A
-module, we mean a left (right) dg module over
A
. The category of left
A
-modules is denoted by
\mathrm{Mod}(A)
.

•

\mathfrak{s}
denotes the shift functor. For a complex
V^{\bullet}\in\mathrm{Com}_{S}
,
(\mathfrak{s}V)^{i}=V^{i+1}
.

•

For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the symmetric group
\mathcal{S}_{n}
acts on
V^{\otimes n}
: for
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{n}
and
v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}\in V^{\otimes n}
,

\sigma(v_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{n}):=v_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}\otimes\cdots\otimes v_{\sigma^{-1}(n)}.

In particular,
\tau
denotes the cyclic permutation:

\tau(a_{1}\otimes a_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n})=a_{n}\otimes a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{n-1}.

2.
Noncommutative Poisson Geometry

In classical Poisson geometry, Poisson structures on a smooth manifold
X
can be described algebraically via a Lie bracket on the algebra
C^{\infty}(X)
satisfying the Leibniz rule, or geometrically via a bivector field
\pi\in\Gamma(X,\wedge^{2}TX)
such that
\{\pi,\pi\}=0
, where
\{-,-\}
denotes the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket.
Following this philosophy, this section recalls double Poisson geometry in the sense of Van den Bergh
[
13
]
and explains that shifted double Poisson structures behave well in the derived setting.

2.1.
Double Poisson brackets

First, we recall the notion of a double Poisson bracket.

Definition 2.1

(Van den Bergh)
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
.
A
dg double Poisson bracket of degree
n
on
A
is
an
S
-bilinear map

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}:A\otimes A\to A\otimes A

of degree
-n
satisfying:

(1)

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,x\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}x|\cdot|\mathfrak{s}^{n}y|}\,\tau\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}
;

(2)

For any
x\in A
,

\mathcal{H}_{x}:=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}:A\to A\otimes A

is a double derivation of degree
|x|-n
;

(3)

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}
satisfies the
double Jacobi identity
:

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}

\displaystyle:=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}+(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}z|(|x|+|y|)}(321)\,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}z,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}

\displaystyle\qquad+(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}x|(|y|+|z|)}(123)\,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}z,x\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}=0;

(4)

The bracket is compatible with the differential on
A
:

\partial\bigl(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\bigr)=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}\partial x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}+(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}x|}\,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,\partial y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}.

Here, we write

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}=\mathcal{H}_{x}(y)^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{x}(y)^{\prime\prime};\ \mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}x,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}.

A dg algebra
A
is called a
dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
if it is equipped with a dg double Poisson bracket of degree
n
.
A complex
V\in\mathrm{Com}_{S}
is called a
dg double Lie algebra of degree
n
if it has a bracket
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}
that satisfies the axioms in Definition
2.1
, except (2).

The following examples play important roles in this work.

Example 2.2
.

Let
Q
be a finite quiver.
There exists a double Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}
given by:
for any arrow
a\in Q
,

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,a^{\ast}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=e_{s(a)}\otimes e_{t(a)},\qquad\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a^{\ast},a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=-e_{t(a)}\otimes e_{s(a)},

for any
f,g\in\overline{Q}\text{ with }f\neq g^{*}
,

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}f,g\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0.

Extending it by the Leibniz rule defines a double Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}
.

Example 2.3
.

Let
\mathbb{K}[t]
be the polynomial algebra generated by
t
.
The degree of
t
need not be zero; the differential on
\mathbb{K}[t]
is zero.
There is a dg double Poisson bracket on
\mathbb{K}[t]
determined by

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=t\otimes 1-1\otimes t.

Next, we recall an important property of the double Poisson bracket.
In
[
13
]
, Van den Bergh showed that a double Poisson bracket on
A
canonically induces a Lie algebra structure on the quotient space
A_{cyc}=A/[A,A]
.
When
A
is a dg double Poisson algebra, one obtains a bracket

\{-,-\}:A\otimes A\to A

defined as the composition

\textstyle{A\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}}
\textstyle{A\otimes A\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathbf{m}}
\textstyle{A.}

Here,
\mathbf{m}
is the multiplication on
A
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Then
A_{cyc}
is a dg Lie algebra of degree
n
with respect to
(\partial,\{-,-\})
.

Proof.

The anti-symmetry of
\{-,-\}
on
A_{cyc}
is clear; we only show that the Jacobi identity holds.
First, we prove that for any
x,y,z\in A
,

(2)

\{x,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\}-\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}\{x,y\},z\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}x|\cdot|\mathfrak{s}^{n}y|}\,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}y,\{x,z\}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0.

2.2.
Higher noncommutative Poisson structures

Double Poisson brackets appear more fundamental than NC Poisson structures. In the derived setting, a natural question is whether double Poisson brackets are stable under quasi-isomorphisms. The key observation is that, under smoothness assumptions, double Poisson brackets can be constructed by solving a Maurer–Cartan equation.

\mathsf{DPois}(A;n):=\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\Bigl(F^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q,\,cyc}[n+1]\Bigr).

At first sight,
\mathsf{DPois}
seems to depend on the choice of cofibrant resolution. However, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.19
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
.
Let
n
be an integer.
For different cofibrant resolutions
Q_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}A,\ Q_{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}A
in
\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
,
\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\Bigl(F^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q_{1},\,cyc}[n+1]\Bigr)
is weakly equivalent to
\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\Bigl(F^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q_{2},\,cyc}[n+1]\Bigr)
in the category
\mathrm{sSet}
of simplicial sets.

Proof.

For simplicity, we prove the theorem for
n=0
.
Since
\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
is a fibrant model category,
according to
[
7
, Lemma 2.24]
, there exist quasi-isomorphisms
F:Q_{1}\to Q_{2}
and
G:Q_{2}\to Q_{1}
such that

F\circ G
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{id}:Q_{2}\to Q_{2}
and
G\circ F
is homotopy equivalent to
\mathrm{id}:Q_{1}\to Q_{1}
.

First, there exists a canonical morphism of dg algebras from
T_{Q_{1}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})
to
T_{Q_{2}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2})
.
Using
G
, there is a canonical morphism in
\mathrm{Mod}(Q_{1}^{e})
:

Q_{1}^{e}\otimes_{Q_{2}^{e}}\Omega^{1}(Q_{2})\to\Omega^{1}(Q_{1}),\qquad(a^{\prime}\otimes a^{\prime\prime})\otimes\delta b\mapsto(a^{\prime}\otimes a^{\prime\prime})\otimes\delta G(b).

Therefore, there is a composition of
S
-linear maps:

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}:

\displaystyle\ \underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Q_{1}^{e}}\big(\Omega^{1}(Q_{1}),Q_{1}^{\otimes 2}\big)\longrightarrow\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Q_{1}^{e}}\big(Q_{1}^{e}\otimes_{Q_{2}^{e}}\Omega^{1}(Q_{2}),Q_{1}^{\otimes 2}\big)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Q_{2}^{e}}\big(\Omega^{1}(Q_{2}),Q_{1}^{\otimes 2}\big)\longrightarrow\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{Q_{1}^{e}}\big(\Omega^{1}(Q_{2}),Q_{2}^{\otimes 2}\big)\cong\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2}).

Similarly, one has
\widetilde{F}:\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2})\to\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})
.
The above construction yields dg algebra morphisms; by an abuse of notation,

\widetilde{G}:T_{Q_{1}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})\to T_{Q_{2}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2}),\qquad\widetilde{F}:T_{Q_{2}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2})\to T_{Q_{1}}\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1}).

It is clear that
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{G}
descend to the completions and the cyclic quotients.
By construction,
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{G}
are homotopy inverses of each other.

Second, if
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{G}
preserve the double Poisson brackets, then by the above analysis,
they induce a homotopy equivalence of dg Lie algebras between

\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[0]}_{Q_{1},\,cyc}
and
\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[0]}_{Q_{2},\,cyc}
;
therefore,
\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\big(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[0]}_{Q_{1},\,cyc}\big)
and
\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\big(\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[0]}_{Q_{2},\,cyc}\big)
are weakly equivalent as simplicial sets.

Now, we prove that
\widetilde{F}
and
\widetilde{G}
preserve double Poisson brackets.
Due to the Leibniz rule of double Poisson brackets and (
7
),
we only need to verify the commutativity of the following diagram:

\textstyle{Q_{1}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}}
\scriptstyle{F\otimes\widetilde{G}}
\textstyle{Q_{1}\otimes Q_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F\otimes F}
\textstyle{Q_{2}\otimes\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}}
\textstyle{Q_{2}\otimes Q_{2}.}

By the
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}
–
\otimes
duality, it is equivalent to the commutativity of the following diagram:

\textstyle{\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\widetilde{G}}
\textstyle{\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{S}(Q_{1},Q_{1}\otimes Q_{1})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{F\otimes F\circ(-)\circ G}
\textstyle{\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{2})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathrm{id}}
\textstyle{\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{S}(Q_{2},Q_{2}\otimes Q_{2}).}

For a general element
b\mathcal{D}_{a}c\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(Q_{1})
,

\widetilde{G}(b\mathcal{D}_{a}c)=F\otimes F\circ(b\mathcal{D}_{a}c)\circ G.

Therefore, the above diagram commutes, and the statement follows.
∎

3.
Kontsevich–Rosenberg Principle

The Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle states that a noncommutative geometric structure is meaningful precisely when it induces the classical counterpart on representation schemes.
In this section, we recall the construction of derived representation schemes in
[
2
]
and prove that shifted double Poisson structures on
A
induce shifted Poisson structures on derived representation schemes.
In particular, we show that shifted double Poisson structures are related to the shifted Poisson structures on the derived moduli stacks of representations.

3.1.
Derived representation schemes

Let
V\in\mathrm{Com}_{S}
be a complex of finite total dimension, and let
\underline{\rm End}_{S}(V)
denote the complex of endomorphisms of

. For

[

3.3.
Higher traces

The trace map establishes a correspondence from noncommutative algebras to functions on their representation schemes.
This correspondence extends to modules over algebras, connecting noncommutative geometry with geometry on representation spaces.

First, we recall the Van den Bergh functor as introduced in
[
14
,
2
]
. Van den Bergh proposed this functor as a means to connect noncommutative geometry with sheaf theory on representation schemes. Subsequently, Berest et al. extended this construction into the derived setting.

For a dg algebra
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
and dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
,
the Van den Bergh functor is

(14)

(-)_{\mathbf{d}}:\ \mathrm{Mod}(A^{e})\to\mathrm{Mod}(A_{\mathbf{d}}),\qquad M\mapsto M_{\mathbf{d}}\cong M\otimes_{A^{e}}\big(\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathbf{d}})\otimes A_{\mathbf{d}}\big).

The following lemma is the module-theoretic analogue of Proposition
3.2
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
.
Let
\mathbf{d}
be a dimension vector.
For any
M\in\mathrm{Mod}(A^{e})
, and

L\in\mathrm{Mod}(A_{\mathbf{d}})
, there is a canonical isomorphism of complexes

(15)

\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A_{\mathbf{d}}}\big(M_{\mathbf{d}},\,L\big)\cong\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{A^{e}}\big(M,\;\mathfrak{gl}(\mathbb{K}^{\mathbf{d}})\otimes L\big).

Next, we recall the trace map for bimodules:

(16)

\mathrm{Tr}\,_{\mathbf{d}}:M\to\mathfrak{gl}_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbb{K})\otimes M_{\mathbf{d}}^{\mathrm{ab}}\to M_{\mathbf{d}}^{\mathrm{ab}}.

The first arrow is the canonical map corresponding to the identity under the isomorphism (
15
) for
L=M_{\mathbf{d}}
;
the second arrow sends a matrix
to its trace.

According to
[
14
, Lemma 3.2.2]
, for any
M\in\mathrm{Mod}(A^{e})
,

(T_{A}M)_{\mathbf{d}}\simeq\mathrm{Sym}_{A_{\mathbf{d}}}(M)_{\mathbf{d}}
.
Thus, for any
n
, taking
M=\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
yields

(\mathrm{T}^{\ast,[n]}_{A})_{\mathbf{d}}\simeq\mathrm{Pol}(A_{\mathbf{d}};n-1)
.
We now show that shifted double Poisson structures satisfy the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
be homologically smooth.
Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For any dimension vector
\mathbf{d}
,
there is a canonical morphism

\mathsf{DPois}(A;n)\to\mathsf{Pois}\Bigl(A_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr).

Proof.

Let
Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}A
be a cofibrant resolution.
We show that there is a morphism of dg Lie algebras

\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q,\,cyc}[n+1]\to\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}\Bigl(Q_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr)[n+1].

According to
[
13
, Proposition 7.6.1]
, the double Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket on the shifted noncommutative cotangent bundle and the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket on shifted polyvector fields on
\mathrm{DRep}_{\mathbf{d}}^{Q}
are related by the following formula: for any
X,Y\in\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q,\,cyc}[n+1]
,

(17)

\{X_{ij},Y_{uv}\}=\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}X,Y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{{}^{\prime}}_{uj}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}X,Y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{iv}.

Using (
16
), we have

\displaystyle\{\mathrm{Tr}\,_{\mathbf{d}}(X),\mathrm{Tr}\,_{\mathbf{d}}(Y)\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\{X_{ii},Y_{jj}\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}X,Y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{{}^{\prime}}_{ji}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}X,Y\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{{}^{\prime\prime}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\{X,Y\}_{ii}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\,_{\mathbf{d}}(\{X,Y\}).

Thus, the trace maps preserve dg Lie algebra structures.
Therefore, at the level of moduli, we obtain a natural simplicial map

{\mathrm{Tr}\,}_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathsf{DPois}(Q;n)\to\mathsf{Pois}\Bigl(Q_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr).

∎

In Yeung’s work
[
15
,
16
]
, a pre-Calabi-Yau structure on a dg algebra induces a shifted Poisson structure on derived stacks of representations; on the other hand, pre-Calabi-Yau structures are known to be closely related to double Poisson structures.
It is natural to ask whether double Poisson structures directly induce shifted Poisson structures on derived stacks of representations.

First, we recall shifted Poisson structures on derived stacks of representations.
Since a derived moduli stack of representations is a derived quotient stack, Toën proposed the following model for its cotangent complex (see
[
12
]
for details and references).
Yeung studied the shifted symplectic/Poisson structures in
[
16
]
.
Let
\mathrm{G}
be a smooth reductive group scheme acting on a smooth derived affine scheme
Y=\mathrm{Spec}\,R
, and let
X:=[Y/\mathrm{G}]
be the derived quotient stack.
The pullback to
Y
of the cotangent complex
\mathbb{L}_{X}
is the homotopy fiber
\mathbb{L}
of the natural morphism

\rho:\mathbb{L}_{Y}\to\mathscr{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\vee},

which is dual to the infinitesimal action of
\mathrm{G}
on
Y
. Since
\mathrm{G}
acts on
Y
, it also acts on
\mathbb{L}
.
By definition, in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Mod}(R))
,

\mathbb{L}
is equivalent to the complex

\mathrm{cone}\Big(\mathbb{L}_{Y}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\to}}\mathscr{O}_{Y}\otimes\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\Big)[-1].

Assume
R
is almost cofibrant, meaning that the
Kähler
differential
\Omega^{1}_{\rm com}(R)\in\mathrm{Mod}(R)
is cofibrant and
\Omega^{1}_{\rm com}(R)\to\mathbb{L}_{\mathbb{K}\backslash R}
is a quasi-isomorphism. In
[
16
]
, the model for shifted polyvector fields on
X=[Y/\mathrm{G}]
is:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Pol}^{r}_{\rm Car}(X;n):=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p+q=r}\Bigl(\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{R}\big(\mathrm{Sym}_{R}(\Omega^{1}_{\rm com}(R)[n+1]),R\big)\otimes{\rm CoSym}^{q}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}[-n]\bigr)\Bigr)^{\mathrm{G}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{p+q=r}\Big(\mathrm{Pol}(R;n)\otimes{\rm CoSym}^{q}\bigl(\mathfrak{g}[-n]\bigr)\Big)^{\mathrm{G}}.

The Lie bracket on

\mathrm{Pol}_{\rm Car}(X;n)

is defined by

(18)

\{-,-\}:=\{-,-\}_{SN}\otimes\xi,

where
\{-,-\}_{SN}
is the Schouten–Nijenhuis bracket on
\mathrm{Pol}(R;n)
and
\xi
is the shuffle product on
{\rm CoSym}(\mathfrak{g}[-n])
.
Under this model, shifted Poisson structures on derived quotient stacks are given as follows.
Let

\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}(X;n)[n+1]:=\prod_{r\geq 0}\mathrm{Pol}^{r}_{\rm Car}(X;n)[n+1]

be the completion with the weight filtration: for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

F^{k}\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}(X;n)[n+1]:=\prod_{i\geq 0}\mathrm{Pol}^{k+i}_{\rm Car}(X;n)[n+1].

By the Maurer–Cartan formalism, one has

Definition 3.11
.

Let
R\in\mathrm{CDGA}^{\leq 0}_{\mathbb{K}}
be derived finitely presented. Let
\mathrm{G}
be a reductive group scheme acting on
Y=\mathrm{Spec}\,R
.
The
Cartan model of the space of
n
-shifted Poisson structures on the derived quotient stack
[Y/\mathrm{G}]
is

\mathsf{Pois}_{\rm Car}(X;n)=\underline{\mathrm{MC}}_{\bullet}\Bigl(F^{2}\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}(X;n)[n+1]\Bigr).

Now, fix a cofibrant resolution
\pi:Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}A
in
\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
,
and apply the above analysis to the case of the derived moduli stack of representations

\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}}:=[\mathrm{DRep}^{Q}_{\mathbf{d}}/\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}].

Then
\mathbb{L}=\mathrm{cone}\Bigl(\mathbb{L}_{\mathrm{DRep}^{Q}_{\mathbf{d}}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\to}}Q_{\mathbf{d}}\otimes\mathfrak{gl}^{\ast}_{\mathbf{d}}\Bigr)[-1].

A key observation in Yeung’s work
[
15
]
is that the noncommutative cotangent complex of
A
should be

\mathscr{S}_{A}[-1]
,
where
\mathscr{S}_{A}:=\mathrm{cone}(\Omega^{1}(A)\to A\otimes A).

Applying the derived Van den Bergh functor to
\mathscr{S}_{A}[-1]
, one obtains an isomorphism in
\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Mod}(Q_{\mathbf{d}}))
:

\mathbf{L}(\,\mathbf{R}\pi^{\ast}\mathscr{S}_{A}[-1])_{\mathbf{d}}\simeq\mathbb{L}.

Combining the above analysis and Section
3
, we obtain

Proposition 3.12
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{DGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
be homologically smooth.
Then for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
there is a natural morphism

\mathsf{DPois}(A;n)\rightarrow\mathsf{Pois}_{\rm Car}(\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}};n).

Proof.

Let
Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\simeq}}{{\twoheadrightarrow}}A
be a cofibrant resolution.
We show that there is a morphism in the category
\mathrm{DGLA}^{\rm gr}
:

\widehat{\mathrm{T}}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q,\,cyc}[n+1]\rightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}\Bigl(\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr)[n+1].

In fact, by the definition of the Lie bracket (
18
),

{\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}\bigl(Q_{\mathbf{d}};n\bigr)^{\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}}[n+1]}
is a dg Lie subalgebra of

\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}\Bigl(\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr)[n+1]
; moreover, the image of the trace map

\mathrm{Tr}\,_{\mathbf{d}}:\mathrm{T}^{\ast,[n+1]}_{Q,\,cyc}[n+1]\to\mathrm{Pol}\Bigl(Q_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr)[n+1]

lies in the
\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}(\mathbb{K})
-invariant part.
Therefore, by Proposition
3.9
, the morphism

\mathsf{DPois}(A;n)\rightarrow\mathsf{Pois}_{\rm Car}(\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}};n)

is induced by the trace map followed by the embedding

\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}\bigl(Q_{\mathbf{d}};n\bigr)^{\mathrm{GL}_{\mathbf{d}}}[n+1]\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathrm{Pol}}_{\rm Car}\Bigl(\mathpzc{DRep}^{A}_{\mathbf{d}};n\Bigr)[n+1].

∎

4.
Noncommutative Poisson Extensions

In this section we systematically examine extensions of noncommutative Poisson structures.
We study criteria for extending double Poisson brackets on
A
to
A\langle t\rangle
, for extending NC Poisson structures, and for relating these two extension problems. In particular, we show that noncommutative Poisson extensions are compatible with noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction and fit the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle.

4.1.
Double Poisson algebra extension

Let
(A,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra.
We aim to extend the double bracket to the free product
A\langle t\rangle:=A\ast_{S}S[t]
, where the degree of
t
is not assumed to be zero.
By the Leibniz rule, such an extension is determined by the double derivation
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}
.
We therefore introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra.
A
dg double Poisson derivation on
A

is a double derivation
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
such that for any
a,b\in A
,

\displaystyle\Theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}=

\displaystyle(-1)^{\kappa_{1}}\Theta(b)^{\prime}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}

\displaystyle+(-1)^{\kappa_{2}}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime};

\displaystyle\partial\circ\Theta=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\Theta|}\Theta\circ\partial.

Here,
\kappa_{1},\ \kappa_{2}
are determined by the Koszul sign rule:

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=|\Theta||\mathfrak{s}^{n}a|+|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{a}(\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime})|;

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}

\displaystyle=|\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|(|\Theta|-n-|a|)+|\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime\prime}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|+1.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Assume
t
has degree
n
and let
\Theta
be a dg double derivation of degree zero.
We extend the dg double bracket to the free product
A\langle t\rangle:=A\ast_{S}S[t]
by setting

(1)

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}:=t\otimes 1-1\otimes t
;

(2)

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}:=\Theta(a)
, for
a\in A
.

The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for this extension to be a dg double Poisson bracket.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
If
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
is a dg double Poisson derivation of degree zero on
A
,
then
A\langle t;\Theta\rangle
is a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider only the case
n=0
.
It suffices to verify the Jacobi identities:
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0
for every
a\in A
and
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0
.

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}+(321)\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}+(123)\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}+(321)\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,t\otimes 1-1\otimes t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}+(123)\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,-\tau\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes 1\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-(-1)^{|\Theta(a)^{\prime}||\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|}(123)\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes 1\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}.

Since
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}
is a dg double Poisson derivation, applying condition (1) in Definition
4.1
yields

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes 1\otimes\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}-\Theta(a)^{\prime}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0.

Hence,
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0
. A similar computation yields
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}t,t,t\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}=0
.
∎

Throughout this work, the dg algebra
A\langle t\rangle
endowed with the dg double bracket defined above is called the
double Poisson algebra extension of
A
by
\Theta
, denoted
A\langle t;\Theta\rangle
.

4.2.
NC Poisson algebra extension

It is natural to consider the extension problem in the setting of NC Poisson structures.
Moreover, since a double Poisson bracket induces an NC Poisson structure, one must clarify how the two extension procedures interact.
Since an NC Poisson structure is not merely a dg Lie bracket on
A_{\natural}
; rather, the adjoint action in each argument is induced by a derivation, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
(A,\partial,\{-,-\})
be an NC Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
An
NC Poisson derivation on
A
is a derivation
\nu\in\underline{\mathrm{Der}}_{S}(A)
such that
for any
\bar{a},\bar{b}\in A_{\natural}
,

\displaystyle\nu({\{\bar{a},\bar{b}\}})=

\displaystyle\{\overline{\nu(a)},\bar{b}\}+(-1)^{|\nu||\mathfrak{s}^{n}a|}\{\bar{a},\overline{\nu(b)}\};

\displaystyle\partial\circ\nu=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\nu|}\nu\circ\partial.

Let
(A,\partial,\{-,-\})
be an NC Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Assume
t
has degree
n
, and let
\nu
be an NC Poisson derivation of degree zero.
We extend the NC Poisson bracket
\{-,-\}
to
A\langle t\rangle
by setting, for any
\bar{a}\in A_{\natural}
,

(19)

\{\bar{t},\bar{a}\}:=\overline{\,\nu(a)\,},\qquad\{\bar{t},\bar{t}\}=0.

The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for this extension.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
(A,\partial,\{-,-\})
be an NC Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Let
\nu\in\underline{\mathrm{Der}}_{S}(A)
be an NC Poisson derivation of degree zero.
Then
A\langle t;\nu\rangle
is an NC Poisson algebra of degree
n
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider only the case
n=0
.
Since anti-symmetry is immediate, we verify the Jacobi identity on
A\langle t\rangle_{\natural}
.
For any
a,b\in A
, because
\nu
is an NC Poisson derivation,
\{\bar{t},\bar{a},\bar{b}\}=0
; and

\displaystyle\{\bar{t},\bar{t},\bar{a}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{t},\{\bar{t},\bar{a}\}\}+\{\bar{a},\{\bar{t},\bar{t}\}\}+\{\bar{t},\{\bar{a},\bar{t}\}\}

\displaystyle=\{\bar{t},\overline{\nu(a)}\}+0-\{\bar{t},\overline{\nu(a)}\}

\displaystyle=0.

Consequently,
A\langle t;\nu\rangle
is an NC Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
∎

Throughout this work, the dg algebra
A\langle t\rangle
endowed with the bracket defined above is called the
NC Poisson algebra extension of
A
by
\nu
, denoted
A\langle t;\nu\rangle
.

Next, we show that a dg double Poisson algebra extension canonically induces an NC Poisson algebra extension.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Then for any dg double Poisson derivation
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
,

\theta:=\mathbf{m}\circ\Theta
is an NC Poisson derivation.
Furthermore, if the degree of
\Theta
is zero and
t
is of degree
n
,
then
A\langle t;\theta\rangle
is an NC Poisson algebra extension of
A
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider the case
n=0\text{ and }|\Theta|=0
.
It is clear that
\theta=\mathbf{m}\circ\Theta
is a derivation on
A
.
For any
a,b\in A
, one has

\displaystyle\theta(\{\bar{a},\bar{b}\})

\displaystyle=\theta\big(\,\overline{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}}\,\big)

\displaystyle=\overline{\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}}+\overline{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime})}.

Since
\Theta
is a dg double Poisson derivation,

\displaystyle\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}\circ(\mathbf{m}\otimes\mathrm{id})\big(\,\Theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\,\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}b||\mathfrak{s}^{n}a|+|\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime\prime}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|+1}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{a}(\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime})|}\Theta(b)^{\prime}\{a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\}.

By anti-symmetry,

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime})=-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|+|\mathcal{H}_{b}(a)^{\prime\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(a)^{\prime}|}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,a\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})

Then in
A_{\natural}
, one has

\displaystyle\theta\{\bar{a},\bar{b}\}=

\displaystyle\overline{\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime})\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}}+\overline{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}\theta(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,b\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}|}\overline{\Theta(b)^{\prime}\{a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\}}

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|+|\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime})^{\prime\prime}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|}\overline{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{|\mathcal{H}_{a}(\Theta(b)^{\prime})^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{a}(\Theta(b)^{\prime})^{\prime\prime}\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}|}\overline{\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\Theta(b)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime\prime}\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\Theta(b)^{\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\quad-(-1)^{|\Theta(a)^{\prime}||\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(a)^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime})|+|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|}\overline{\Theta(a)^{\prime}\{b,\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{a,\Theta(b)^{\prime}\}\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}}+(-1)^{|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(b)^{\prime}||\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}|}\overline{\Theta(b)^{\prime}\{a,\Theta(b)^{\prime\prime}\}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\Theta(a)^{\prime}||\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}|+|\Theta(a)^{\prime}||\mathcal{H}_{b}(\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime})|+|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|}\overline{\Theta(a)^{\prime}\{b,\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}\}}

\displaystyle-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|}\overline{\{b,\Theta(a)^{\prime}\}\Theta(a)^{\prime\prime}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{a,\theta(b)\}}-(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{n}a||\mathfrak{s}^{n}b|}\overline{\{b,\theta(a)\}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\{\theta(a),b\}}+\overline{\{a,\theta(b)\}}.

Consequently,
\theta
is an NC Poisson derivation.
The second statement follows from Theorem
4.4
.
∎

4.3.
Reduction commutes with extension

Let
A
be a noncommutative Hamiltonian algebra and let
A_{\mathbf{w}}
denote its noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction.
It is natural to ask whether extensions are compatible with reduction.
In general,
A_{\mathbf{w}}
need not carry a dg double Poisson bracket.
We show that certain dg double Poisson algebra extensions of
A
descend to NC Poisson algebra extensions of
A_{\mathbf{w}}
, and that this descent is compatible with the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle.

Since an NC Poisson extension is determined by the derivation associated with
t
, the following lemma gives a criterion for when the NC Poisson derivation descends to the noncommutative Hamiltonian reduction.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}},\mathbf{w})
be a noncommutative Hamiltonian algebra of degree
n
.
Let
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
be a dg double Poisson derivation.
If
\Theta(\mathbf{w})\in A\otimes A\mathbf{w}A+A\mathbf{w}A\otimes A
,
then
\theta=\mathbf{m}\circ\Theta
descends to be an NC Poisson derivation of
A_{\mathbf{w}}
.

Proof.

For simplicity, we consider the case
n=0
.
For a dg double Poisson derivation
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
,
if
\Theta(\mathbf{w})\in A\otimes A\mathbf{w}A+A\mathbf{w}A\otimes A
,
then
\theta(A\mathbf{w}A)\subset A\mathbf{w}A
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\theta\in\underline{\mathrm{Der}}_{S}(A_{\mathbf{w}})
.
It remains to verify that
\theta
is an NC Poisson derivation.
Write
[a]\in A_{\mathbf{w}}
for the class represented by
a\in A
.
Then for any
[a],[b]\in A_{\mathbf{w}}
, one has

\displaystyle\theta\big(\,{\{\overline{[a]},\overline{[b]}\}\,}\big)

\displaystyle=\theta\big(\,\overline{H_{[a]}([b])}\,\big)=\theta\big(\overline{[H_{a}(b)]}\big)=\theta\big(\overline{\,[\{a,b\}]\,}\big)

\displaystyle=\overline{\,[\theta(\{a,b\})]\,}=\overline{\,[\{\theta(a),b\}]\,}+(-1)^{|\theta||a|}\overline{\,[\{a,\theta(b)\}]\,}

\displaystyle=\{\theta(\overline{[a]}),\overline{[b]}\}+(-1)^{|\theta||a|}\{\overline{[a]},\theta(\overline{[b]})\}.

Here the second equality is due to the fact that (
4
) is a morphism of dg Lie algebras; the fifth equality is due to Proposition
4.5
.
∎

Hence, if
|\Theta|=0
, then
A_{\mathbf{w}}\langle t;\theta\rangle
is an NC Poisson algebra extension.
Moreover, their NC Poisson structures are compatible as follows.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}},\mathbf{w})
be a noncommutative Hamiltonian algebra of degree
n
.
Let
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
be a dg double Poisson derivation of degree zero.
Then the canonical projection
A\langle t;\theta\rangle\to A_{\mathbf{w}}\langle t;\theta\rangle

descends to a dg Lie algebra morphism

A\langle t;\theta\rangle_{\natural}\to A_{\mathbf{w}}\langle t;\theta\rangle_{\natural}.

Proof.

Note that an NC Poisson structure is a dg Lie bracket
\{-,-\}
, and the adjoint representation

\mathrm{ad}:\,A_{\natural}\to\underline{\rm End}_{S}(A_{\natural})
factors through
\varrho:\underline{\mathrm{Der}}_{S}(A)_{\natural}\to\underline{\rm End}_{S}(A_{\natural})
.
Therefore, it suffices to verify that the projection
pr
preserves the dg Lie bracket on generators.
For any
a,b\in A
, by Proposition
2.12
,
it is clear that
pr(\{\bar{a},\bar{b}\})=\{pr(\bar{a}),pr(\bar{b})\}=\{\overline{[a]},\overline{[b]}\}
.
What remains to be checked is
pr(\{\bar{t},\bar{a}\})=\{[\bar{t}],pr(\bar{a})\}
.
By Definition
4.1
and (
19
),

\displaystyle\{\bar{t},\bar{a}\}

\displaystyle=\overline{\{t,a\}}=\overline{\theta(a)};

\displaystyle\{[\bar{t}],pr(\bar{a})\}

\displaystyle=\{[\bar{t}],\overline{[a]}\}=\overline{\,[\{t,a\}]\,}=\overline{\,[\theta(a)]\,}=pr\big(\,\{\bar{t},\bar{a}\}\,\big).

Consequently,
pr
is a dg Lie algebra morphism.
∎

In other words,
noncommutative reduction commutes with extension.
Next, we prove that this commutativity fits into the Kontsevich–Rosenberg principle for ordinary algebras.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(A\in{\rm Alg}_{S},\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}},\mathbf{w})
be a noncommutative Hamiltonian algebra.
Let
\Theta\in\underline{\mathbb{D}\mathrm{er}}_{S}(A)
be a dg double Poisson derivation of degree zero such that
\Theta(\mathbf{w})\in A\otimes A\mathbf{w}A+A\mathbf{w}A\otimes A
.

5.
\mathfrak{s}
-constructions

For a dg double Poisson algebra
A
, a natural question is whether the shifted complex
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A=A[d]
inherits a dg double Poisson structure.
We give an affirmative answer by introducing
\mathfrak{s}
-construction.
As a main application, we explain how double Poisson brackets induce Lie algebra structures on reduced cyclic homology.
This phenomenon may be viewed as a noncommutative analogue of the classical fact that the cohomology of a smooth Poisson manifold carries a graded Lie algebra structure.

5.1.
Main constructions

We begin by recalling the bar and cobar constructions; see
[
8
]
for further details.
Let
A=S\oplus\bar{A}\in\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
be an augmented dg
S
-algebra.
On the cocomplete coaugmented graded coalgebra

T^{c}_{S}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})=\bigoplus_{n=0}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})^{\otimes n}=S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\oplus(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})^{\otimes 2}\oplus\cdots,

there are two differentials,
\partial
and
b
.
The differential
\partial
is induced by the internal differential
\partial_{A}
on
A
.
More precisely,

\partial(\mathfrak{s}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}a_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}a_{<i}|+1}\mathfrak{s}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}(\partial_{A}a_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}a_{n}.

The differential
b
is given by:

b(\mathfrak{s}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}a_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}a_{<i+1}|+1}\mathfrak{s}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}(a_{i}a_{i+1})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}a_{n}.

One checks that
\partial\circ b+b\circ\partial=0
.
Hence, the total differential
\partial+b
is well-defined on
T_{S}^{c}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})
.
The cocomplete coaugmented dg
S
-coalgebra
T_{S}^{c}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})
is called the
bar construction of
A
, denoted
\mathbf{B}A
.

Let
C=S\oplus\bar{C}
be a coaugmented dg
S
-coalgebra.
On the augmented graded
S
-algebra

T_{S}(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C})=\bigoplus_{n=0}(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C})^{\otimes n}=S\oplus\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C}\oplus(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C})^{\otimes 2}\oplus\cdots,

there are two differentials,
\partial
and
\delta
.

\partial
is induced from the internal differential
\partial_{C}
on
C
by

\partial(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{n}):=\sum_{i=1}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{<i}|+1}\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(\partial_{C}c_{i})\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{n}.

The differential
\delta
is given by

\delta(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{|\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{<i}|+|\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{i1}|}\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{i1}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{i2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-1}c_{n},

where
\Delta(c_{i})=c_{i1}\otimes c_{i2}
.
One checks that
\partial\circ\delta+\delta\circ\partial=0
.
Thus, the total differential
\partial+\delta
is well-defined on
T_{S}(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C})
.
The augmented dg
S
-algebra
T_{S}(\mathfrak{s}^{-1}\bar{C})
is called the
cobar construction of
C
, denoted
\mathbf{\Omega}C
.

A key property of the cobar–bar construction is that it provides canonical cofibrant resolutions in the categories of dg algebras and dg coalgebras.
A proof of the following proposition can be found in
[
9
, Theorem 2.3.1]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
A
be an augmented dg
S
-algebra.
Let
C
be a conilpotent dg
S
-coalgebra.
Then the counit
\epsilon:\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{B}A\to A
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
S
-algebras,
and the unit
\nu:C\to\mathbf{B}\mathbf{\Omega}C
is a quasi-isomorphism of dg
S
-coalgebras.

A key observation is the following:

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
.
Then there is an isomorphism of coaugmented dg coalgebras:

\mathbf{B}A\cong S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
.

Proof.

As graded spaces, one has
T_{S}^{c}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A})\cong S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
via the map

\mathfrak{s}a_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathfrak{s}a_{n}\mapsto\mathfrak{s}a_{1}t\mathfrak{s}a_{2}t\cdots t\mathfrak{s}a_{n}.

The dg coalgebra structure on
S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
is transported along this isomorphism.
∎

As a corollary, the canonical cofibrant resolution
\mathbf{\Omega}\mathbf{B}A
provided by the cobar–bar construction is isomorphic to

(T_{S}\,\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle),\partial+b+\delta,\otimes)

as dg algebras.
Furthermore, the reason to replace the bar construction by
S\oplus\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
is as follows.
Using the shifted multiplication
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
(see (
20
)), one can verify that there exists a free product
\ast
on

\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
;
this free product is not the same as the underlying tensor product in the bar construction.
For
\mathfrak{s}a,\ \mathfrak{s}b\in\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
, one has
\mathfrak{s}a\ast\mathfrak{s}b=(-1)^{d|a|+d}\mathfrak{s}(ab)
.

When
A
is a dg double Poisson algebra, it is natural to ask whether
T\mathfrak{s}^{-1}(\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle)
inherits a dg double Poisson structure.
The crucial point is to verify the existence of a double Lie bracket on
\mathfrak{s}\bar{A}\langle t\rangle
.
It is known that if
L
is a dg Lie algebra of degree
n
, then for any
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, the shifted complex
\mathfrak{s}^{d}L
is a dg Lie algebra of degree
n-d
.
However, for a dg double Poisson algebra
A
of degree
n
, it does not follow in general that
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
is a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n-d
.
Intuitively, one should define a double bracket on
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
by shifting the bracket on
A
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})
be a dg double Lie algebra of degree
n
.
Then for any
d\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
is a dg double Lie algebra of degree
n
.

Proof.

Define a double bracket on
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
by

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}:=(\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}):\mathfrak{s}^{d}A\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}A\to\mathfrak{s}^{d}A\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}A.

We verify the double Jacobi identity for
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}
; the other axioms are analogous.
By definition,

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A,L}

\displaystyle=(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A})

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}(\mathfrak{s}^{d})^{\otimes 3}\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2}\tau^{-k}\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A,L}\circ\tau^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{2}(-1)^{d}\tau^{-k}\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{d})^{\otimes 3}\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}\circ\tau^{k}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}(\mathfrak{s}^{d})^{\otimes 3}\circ\Big(\sum_{k=0}^{2}\tau^{-k}\circ((\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}))\circ\tau^{k}\Big)\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}

\displaystyle=(-1)^{d}(\mathfrak{s}^{d})^{\otimes 3}\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}.

Since
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}
satisfies the double Jacobi identity,
\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}
is a double Lie bracket of degree
n
.
∎

Although shifting is not an endomorphism in the category of dg algebras, it nevertheless equips
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
with a shifted multiplication

(20)

\mathfrak{s}^{d}\circ\mathbf{m}\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}):\mathfrak{s}^{d}A\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}A\to\mathfrak{s}^{d}A.

When no ambiguity arises we denote
\mathfrak{s}^{d}\circ\mathbf{m}\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})
by
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
.
Note that
\mathfrak{s}^{d}A
is not an algebra in the usual sense, since the shifted multiplication
\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}
has degree
d
rather than zero.
When
A
admits a dg double Poisson bracket, the dg double Lie bracket from Proposition
5.3
is compatible with the shifted multiplication:

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
(A,\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Then the following identity holds:

(21)

\begin{split}\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})=&(-1)^{nd}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\\
&\quad+(-1)^{nd}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A})\circ(12).\end{split}

Proof.

By definition,

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\circ\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ{\mathfrak{s}^{-d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ\big[\mathrm{id}\otimes(\mathfrak{s}^{d}\circ\mathbf{m}\circ{\mathfrak{s}^{-d}}^{\otimes 2})\big]

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ{\mathfrak{s}^{-d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}).

Axiom (2) in Definition
2.1
is equivalent to

\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})=(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})+(\mathbf{m}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})\circ(12).

Then

\displaystyle\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\circ\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}

\displaystyle={\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}

\displaystyle\quad+{\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ(\mathbf{m}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})\circ(12)\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d})^{\otimes 3}.

Tracking the shifted maps and the Koszul signs yields

\displaystyle{\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d}(\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathbf{m})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle\quad\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{d}(\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{dn}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-1)^{dn}(\mathrm{id}\otimes\widetilde{\mathbf{m}})\circ(\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{d}).

In the same fashion, one checks

{\mathfrak{s}^{d}}^{\otimes 2}\circ(\mathbf{m}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{A})\circ(12)\circ(\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d}\otimes\mathfrak{s}^{-d})=(-1)^{nd}(\widetilde{\mathbf{m}}\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}}_{\mathfrak{s}^{d}A})\circ(12).

This proves the lemma.
∎

5.2.
Application to preprojective algebra

First, we briefly recall cyclic homology theory. See
[
8
]
for further details.
Let
A\in\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S}
be a dg algebra.
The Hochschild complex
\mathrm{CH}(A)_{\bullet}
is a model to compute the Hochschild homology of
A
,
which is the cohomology of
A\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbf{L}}}{{\otimes}}_{A^{e}}A
(under the cohomological grading).
It is obtained by replacing
A
with its bar resolution.
It is a mixed complex with respect to the differential
\partial+b
on
\mathrm{CH}(A)_{\bullet}
and Connes’
\mathsf{B}
operator.
The cyclic complex is defined to be

\mathrm{CC}(A)_{\bullet}:=(\mathrm{CH}(A)_{\bullet}[u^{-1}],\partial+b+u\mathsf{B})
.
Here,
u
is of degree
2
.

On the other hand, the cyclic permutation operator
\tau
acts on the Hochschild complex
\mathrm{CH}(A)
.
The Connes’ complex is defined to be
C^{\lambda}(A)_{\bullet}:=(\mathrm{coker}(id-\tau),\partial+b)
, see
[
8
, Section 2.1.4]
for details.
Since we work over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, cohomologies
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(\mathrm{CC}(A))
and
\mathrm{H}^{\bullet}(C^{\lambda}(A))
coincide and are called the cyclic homology
\mathsf{HC}_{\bullet}(A)
.
See
[
8
, Theorem 2.1.4, Theorem 2.1.8]
for details.

Berest–Khachatryan–Ramadoss introduced the functor

\mathcal{C}:\mathrm{DGA}_{S}\to\mathrm{Com}_{S},\qquad A\mapsto\mathrm{cone}(S_{cyc}\to A_{cyc}).

Here the map
S_{cyc}\to A_{cyc}
is induced by the unit map of
A
.
One of the main results in
[
2
]
is that the functor
\mathcal{C}
admits a total left derived functor
\mathbf{L}\mathcal{C}:\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{DGA}_{S})\to\mathrm{Ho}(\mathrm{Com}_{S})
.
Berest et al. proved that
\mathbf{L}\mathcal{C}(S\backslash A)\cong\mathrm{cone}(C^{\lambda}(S)_{\bullet}\to C^{\lambda}(A)_{\bullet})
.
Here, by
\ S\backslash A
, we emphasize that
A
is a dg algebra over
S
.
See
[
2
, Proposition 3.1]
for details.
Combining Berest–Khachatryan–Ramadoss’ result and Corollary
5.8
, one has

Corollary 5.9
.

Let
(A\in\mathrm{ADGA}^{\leq 0}_{S},\partial,\mathopen{\{\!\!\{}-,-\mathclose{\}\!\!\}})
be a dg double Poisson algebra of degree
n
.
Then the reduced cyclic homology
\overline{\mathsf{HC}}_{\bullet}(A)
carries a canonical graded Lie algebra structure of degree
n
.

Example 5.10
.

Let
Q
be a finite quiver.
The Connes’ cyclic complex of its path algebra
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}
is

(22)

C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q})_{n}:=\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}^{\otimes(n+1)}/\operatorname{im}(\mathrm{id}-\tau)\ ,\quad b_{n}:\,C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q})_{n}\to C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q})_{n-1}\ ,

On the other hand,
0
as a dg algebra over
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}
admits a canonical cofibrant resolution:
\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\langle x\rangle

|x|=-1

It is clear that

\mathbf{L}\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\backslash 0)\cong\mathrm{cone}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}_{cyc}\to\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\langle x\rangle_{cyc})\cong{\frac{\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\langle x\rangle}{\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}+[\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\langle x\rangle,\mathbb{K}\overline{Q}\langle x\rangle]}}\cong C^{\lambda}(\mathbb{K}\overline{Q})[1]

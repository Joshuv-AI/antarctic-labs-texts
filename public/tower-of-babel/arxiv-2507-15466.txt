’t Hooft Anomalies and Defect Conformal Manifolds: Topological Signatures from Modulated Effective Actions
Christian Copetti

Abstract
Symmetry breaking of continuous symmetries by extended dynamical defects entails the existence of continuous families of defects, which form conformal manifolds in a critical setup. In the presence of bulk ’t Hooft anomalies, defects are in fact required to break the symmetry Jensen et al. 2018 ; Thorngren and Wang 2021 : defect conformal manifolds are anomaly-enforced. We show that, by coupling the system to a modulated deformation parameter, the geometric structure of the conformal manifold is sensible to the ’t Hooft anomaly. This leads to measurable effects in the presence of a boundary/defect: in (1+1)d the anomaly predicts a quantized boundary charge pumping, while in higher dimensions it gives rise to non-dissipative boundary Hall currents.

III
Anomalous Modulated Effective Actions

Having discussed how to consistently couple background gauge fields to symmetry-breaking defects via modulation, we now incorporate perturbative ’t Hooft anomalies and examine their impact on the structure of
F_{\mathscr{D}}
.

Recall that an ’t Hooft anomaly is encoded in the nontrivial transformation of the partition function in the presence of background gauge fields:

Z[A_{\lambda}]=e^{i\int\omega(A,\lambda)}Z[A]\,,

(14)

where
\omega
satisfies the Wess–Zumino consistency condition:

\displaystyle[\delta_{\lambda_{1}},\delta_{\lambda_{2}}]F[A]=\delta_{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]}F[A]\,,

(15)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda_{1}}\omega(A,\lambda_{2})-\delta_{\lambda_{2}}\omega(A,\lambda_{1})=\omega(A,[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}])\,,

and cannot be eliminated by any local counterterm, i.e.,
\omega(A,\lambda)\neq d\alpha(A,\lambda)
. The presence of the anomaly implies that the partition function is a section of a nontrivial line bundle over the gauge group.

It was observed in
Jensen et al. 2018
that, while the Wess–Zumino condition can be violated by total derivatives:

[\delta_{\lambda_{1}},\delta_{\lambda_{2}}]F[A]-\delta_{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]}F[A]=d\beta(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},A)\,,

(16)

such violations become physically relevant in the presence of boundaries, whence cannot be remedied by local modifications, leading to
anomaly-enforced symmetry breaking
.

This becomes clearer in the language of modulated defects. Consider a system with a fixed (unmodulated) boundary condition
\sigma
and a bulk ’t Hooft anomaly. A first gauge transformation by
\lambda_{1}
produces the bulk anomaly and also transforms the boundary condition into a modulated one. A second gauge transformation then acts on this modulated configuration, picking up a nontrivial contribution from the modulated effective action in the
\lambda_{1}(\mathbf{x})
background. The resulting Wess–Zumino condition takes a modified form:

\displaystyle

\displaystyle[\delta_{\lambda_{1}},\delta_{\lambda_{2}}]F[A]-\delta_{[\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}]}F[A]=

(17)

\displaystyle\delta_{\lambda_{2}}F_{\mathscr{D}}[\lambda_{1},A_{\mathscr{D}}]-\delta_{\lambda_{1}}F_{\mathscr{D}}[\lambda_{2},A_{\mathscr{D}}]\,.

IV
Boundary Response and SPT Pump

We now use the defect effective action to derive some physically relevant consequences. For simplicity, we consider an abelian symmetry, e.g.
G=U(1)
, although the results extend straightforwardly to general Lie groups.

Boundary Thouless pump.
Consider a boundary condition
\mathscr{B}_{\sigma}
that breaks a bulk anomalous
U(1)
symmetry. This occurs, for example, in the compact boson, where the diagonal
U(1)^{d}\subset U(1)^{m}\times U(1)^{w}

\chi=1

U(1)
embeds into a larger
G_{k}
symmetry, yielding an anomaly coefficient
\chi=kh^{\vee}

h^{\vee}

For a non-modulated boundary condition, we define the defect charge density via the response:

Q_{\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)=\frac{\delta}{\delta A_{\mathscr{B}}}F_{\mathscr{B}}=\chi\frac{\sigma}{2\pi}\,.

(32)

The point
\sigma=0
is distinguished by charge conjugation symmetry. As
\sigma
is slowly varied, the accumulated charge changes continuously. Upon traversing a closed, non-contractible loop in the boundary conformal manifold, we obtain:

Q_{\mathscr{B}}(\sigma+2\pi)-Q_{\mathscr{B}}(\sigma)=\chi\,,

(33)

indicating that
\chi

(34)

This phenomenon can be interpreted as an anomaly in the space of

As an example, consider a free compact scalar field with radius

Global Structure of Boundary Conformal Manifolds.
The modulated effective action in the presence of an ’t Hooft anomaly also offers valuable insights into the global structure of boundary and defect conformal manifolds.
As we have seen, the presence of an anomalous
U(1)
subgroup leads to nontrivial SPT pumping when the coupling is varied along a closed loop corresponding to a nontrivial class:

(44)

G

G

We hope to develop a deeper understand of possible links between the global topology of defect conformal manifolds and bulk ’t Hooft anomalies in the near future.

A\to\omega

Concrete examples can be constructed using weakly coupled UV defects. Consider, for instance, an “electric” surface defect in a
U(1)

where
\Phi
is a bulk primary of charge one and dimension
\Delta_{\Phi}<2
. By tuning

g^{2}\sim O(\epsilon)
. The resulting DCFT has a
U(1)\times U(1)_{w}

\nu(A,\mathbf{A}_{w})=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int A\,d\mathbf{A}_{w}\,.

(48)

VI
Outlook

In this Letter, we initiated an in-depth exploration of the effects of anomaly-enforced symmetry breaking on boundaries and defects. We described how consistency with a bulk ’t Hooft anomaly necessitates the appearance of nontrivial terms in the modulated effective action on the defect. These terms have a range of physical consequences, from constraining the conformal data of tilt operators to inducing exotic anomaly-driven transport phenomena along the defect.

Let us conclude by highlighting some promising directions for future investigation:

1.

Gravitational and global anomalies.
In this work, we have restricted our attention to perturbative anomalies associated with flavor symmetries. A natural extension is to study the constraints imposed by gravitational anomalies. For instance, it is well known that in (1+1) dimensions, a nonzero chiral central charge forbids the existence of a conformal boundary condition
Jensen et al. 2018
;
Hellerman et al. 2021
. It would also be interesting to understand whether gravitational anomalies contribute nontrivially to the modulated effective action for the displacement operator
Gabai et al. 2025
. Likewise, global anomalies for flavor symmetries may induce nontrivial boundary pumping effects, offering insights into the global topology of defect conformal manifolds.

2.

SymTFT derivation of modulated effective actions.
Several recent works have studied the representation of symmetry on boundary conditions using Symmetry Topological Field Theory (SymTFT) techniques
Huang and Cheng 2023
;
Bhardwaj et al. 2024
;
Choi et al. 2024
;
Copetti et al. 2024
;
Copetti et al. 2025
;
Cordova et al. 2024
. However, these approaches have thus far been limited to discrete symmetries. A SymTFT formulation for continuous symmetries has been recently proposed by several authors
Antinucci and Benini 2025
;
Brennan and Sun 2024
;
Bonetti et al. 2024
. It is natural to expect that the universal terms in the modulated effective action could be derived in full generality within this framework, perhaps along the lines of
Antinucci et al. 2024
.

3.

Defects and higher-form symmetries.
The formalism developed in this Letter readily generalizes to higher-codimension defects and can be extended to include higher-form symmetries and higher-group structures. While the present discussion has focused primarily on boundary conditions, we anticipate that these methods will yield nontrivial constraints on defect RG flows across dimensions. We hope to return to this topic in the near future.

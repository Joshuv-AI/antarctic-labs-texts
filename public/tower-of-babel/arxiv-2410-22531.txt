Tschirnhausen bundles of covers of the projective line
Ravi Vakil, Sameera Vemulapalli

Abstract
. A degree d d genus g g cover of the complex projective line by a smooth curve C C yields a vector bundle on the projective line by pushforward of the structure sheaf. Which bundles are possible? Equivalently, which ℙ d − 2 \mathbb{P}^{d-2} -bundles over ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}^{1} contain such covers? (In the language of many previous papers: what are the scrollar invariants of the cover?) We give a complete answer in degree 4 4 , which exhibits the expected pathologies. We describe a polytope (one per degree) which we propose gives the complete answer for primitive covers, i.e. covers that don’t factor through a subcover. We show that all such bundles (for primitive covers) lie in this polytope, and that a “positive proportion” of the polytope arises from smooth covers. Moreover, we show the necessity of the primitivity assumption. Finally, we show that the image of the map from the Hurwitz space of smooth covers to the space of bundles is not preserved by generization (for d > 4 d>4 and g ≫ d 1 g\gg_{d}1 ).

Introduction

Suppose

For general

1.4.

Theorem. —

Suppose

In §

1.5.

Theorem. —

Choose integers
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}^{d-1}
such that
(0,e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1},0)
is a concave sequence.
Then there is a primitive cover

by a smooth (irreducible) curve with
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{d-1}{\mathscr{O}}(e_{i})
.

We produce curves with “concave” scrollar invariants by constructing certain singular curves on Hirzebruch surfaces. The crucial input is Wood’s explicit and striking parametrization of curves on Hirzebruch surfaces using binary forms. (Wood’s work builds on a long history of associating rings to binary forms, including
[
N
]
,
[

]
,
[

]
, and
[

]

Remarks.
(a)

In §
7
we ask if the image of the morphism
\operatorname{Tsch}:\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
(of (
1
)) is preserved by generization, i.e. if
{\mathscr{E}}
is the Tschirnhausen bundle of a smooth cover, and

is a generization of

, is it true that

is

the Tschirnhausen bundle of a smooth cover? There are a number of reasons to hope the image of

is preserved by generization. If so, then the simple deformation theory of

would allow us to prove the existence of curves with other scrollar invariants. It would also allow us to prove statements about fibers of

by looking at the most degenerate cases, which are far more tractable (the “minimal scrollar invariants” correspond to curves on a Hirzebruch surface not meeting the directrix). The fact that the image of

seems to be of such a nice form (conjecturally a polytope in the primitive case) might further give hope of good behavior.

And indeed the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}
is preserved by generization when

or

is not preserved by generization (

). In the case where

is not a prime power, one might expect this from the nonconvexity of the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}
, but when
d
is a prime power, we expect the image to be convex and yet it is not preserved by generization. In the primitive case, we even show that the image of the restriction
\operatorname{Tsch}^{p}:\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}^{p}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
to primitive covers is not preserved by generization (
d\geq 4
,
g\gg_{d}1
).

Question
1
is related to questions about the lattice structure of rings of integers in number fields (see §
8
); scrollar invariants are naturally analogous, when appropriately scaled, to successive minima of rings of integers. However, we may ask a more refined question: we are interested in not just
which
Tschirnhausen bundles occur, but also how often they occur, and this question has a number theoretic analogue as well. In §
8
we discuss how the latter question can be described via a conjectural density function on
\mathcal{P}_{d}
in both the arithmetic and geometric settings. We then further speculate that these density functions extend to the Grothendieck ring.

1.7.

Central remaining question.
It remains to either show that the remaining points of
\mathcal{P}_{d}
are achieved by primitive smooth covers, or (more dramatically) to show that there is some further obstruction to certain Tschirnhausen bundles being achieved for primitive smooth covers.

2.
Warm-up: Low degree (

2.1.

The trivial cases
d\leq 2
.
The rank and degree of the bundle
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}
determine the bundle for low-rank bundles. We quickly observe that for
d=1
we have
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}=0
, and for

we have
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}={\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(g+1)
.

2.2.

The straightforward case
d=3
.

Before getting to the truly enlightening case of
d=4
, we consider the
d=3
case. Consider a degree
3
cover
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
from a genus
g
curve. Then
\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}={\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e_{1})\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e_{2})

where
e_{2}\geq e_{1}\geq 1
, and
e_{1}+e_{2}=g+2
. Let

for convenience. The canonical embedding

embeds

in the Hirzebruch surface

\mathbb{F}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
. A smooth irreducible trisection of

is in the class

for some

, and a short calculation shows that such a curve has genus

. Now for

to be such a curve, we must have

, from which

, from which

.
As

, we have

. We conclude that for

e_{1}+e_{2}=g+2
, and
2e_{1}\geq e_{2}\geq e_{1}
. (This is clearly
\mathcal{P}_{3}
as defined in (
2
).) By choosing general curves in the appropriate linear system

{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{F}_{m}}(3D+kF)
, we see that there is indeed a trigonal cover

\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
for each of these values of
(e_{1},e_{2})
. We have thus completely answered the Central Question in this case.

We see that the scrollar invariants, suitably normalized, are precisely those that lie on the line segment joining
(1/2,1/2)
and
(1/3,2/3)
.

2.3.

Aside.
The natural follow-up question is: as
g\rightarrow\infty
, how
often
do various points on this line segment occur from smooth curves. In §
8
, we define a density function
\rho^{geo}
from the aforementioned line segment to the real numbers, which quantifies the “likelihood” that a curve lands “near” a given point on the line segment. It turns out that the curves are not “equidistributed” on this line segment as
g\rightarrow\infty
(equivalently,
\rho^{geo}
is not a constant function, but indeed is a nontrivial linear function on the line segment).

We further conjecture that this fits into a more general framework (see §
8
for more details). We define an arithmetic analogue
\rho^{arith}
capturing information about the shapes of rings of integers in cubic fields, as a lattice. Moreover, we define (conjecturally) a measure
\pi^{gr}_{ref}
on the line segment taking values in the Grothendieck ring of stacks, appropriately localized. We then define a (conjectural) function
\rho^{gr}_{ref}
, also taking values in the (appropriately completed) Grothendieck ring of stacks, which captures the point masses of this measure. We conjecture that
\dim\circ\rho^{gr}_{ref}=\rho^{geo}=\rho^{arith}
.

) is zero, so

cannot be invertible, as it has an

submatrix that is zero.
∎

2.7.

Proof of Proposition
2
.

Recall that
1\leq e_{1}\leq\cdots\leq e_{d-1}
and
\sum e_{i}=d-1+g
. If
g=0
, the result is then immediate, so assume

If
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1})
is balanced, then
e_{1}=\cdots=e_{d-g-1}=1
, and
e_{d-g}=\cdots=e_{d-1}=2
.
A quick thought will show that if

3.4.

Proof of Theorem

We begin by reviewing more geometry about tetragonal covers.
(This is classical, so rather than trying to find the first reference, we refer the reader to

.) Suppose
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}

is the complete intersection of two divisors
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}

\mathbb{P}{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}^{\vee}

\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
are of

(7)

f_{1}+f_{2}=d+g-1=e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}.

Say

without loss of generality. The “equation” of
\Delta_{k}
is of the following form. Let
x_{1}
,
x_{2}
,

\mathbb{P}^{2}

\mathbb{P}{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}^{\vee}
corresponding to
e_{1}
,
e_{2}

e_{3}

x_{i}

-e_{i}

(s,t)

\textstyle{a^{k}_{11}(s,t)x_{1}^{2}}
\textstyle{+}
\textstyle{a^{k}_{12}(s,t)x_{1}x_{2}}
\textstyle{+}
\textstyle{a^{k}_{22}(s,t)x_{2}^{2}}
\textstyle{+}
\textstyle{a^{k}_{13}(s,t)x_{1}x_{3}}
\textstyle{+}
\textstyle{a^{k}_{23}(s,t)x_{2}x_{3}}

\textstyle{+}
\textstyle{a^{k}_{33}(s,t)x_{3}^{2}}

Rather than looking for which triples

3.5.
Now if

3.10.

The remaining case
2e_{1}=f_{1}
(when
\Delta_{1}
is not ample).

The edge case where
2e_{1}=f_{1}
is similar. In this case,
\Delta_{1}
does not separate points: it contracts the points on the section
x_{2}=x_{3}=0
. But away from this section, the divisor class
\Delta_{1}
separates points and tangent vectors, so
\Delta_{1}
is the pullback of a very ample divisor contracting
\mathbb{P}{\mathscr{E}}^{\vee}_{\pi}
along this section. The previous arguments then apply essentially without change.

3.11.

Existence of primitive covers.

At this point we have shown that for all points of
\mathcal{Q}
, the corresponding general complete intersection is a tetragonal cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
by a smooth curve. We need to show next that this cover is primitive, i.e., that it does not factor through a double cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. If the general such cover is imprimitive, then the monodromy group of the cover lies in
D_{4}
, so the resolvent cubic is reducible. To show this is not the case, we need to exhibit a single example where the resolvent cubic is irreducible. We do this by exhibiting an example where the resolvent cubic is irreducible over the generic point of
\mathbb{P}^{1}=\Proj\mathbb{C}[s,t]
.

In preparation, notice that for all

(i,j,k)\in\{(1,1,1),(2,3,1),(1,3,2),(2,2,2)\},

we have
e_{i}+e_{j}-f_{k}\geq 0

(
2e_{1}\geq f_{1}
by §
3
, then
e_{2}+e_{3}\geq f_{1}
from
e_{3}\geq e_{2}\geq e_{1}
,
2e_{2}\geq f_{2}
by §
3
,
e_{1}+e_{3}\geq f_{2}
by §
3
).
Furthermore, we cannot have
e_{2}+e_{3}-f_{1}=0
, as then
2e_{1}-f_{1}=0
as well, from which
e_{1}=e_{2}=e_{3}
, but then
2e_{1}=f_{1}\leq(f_{1}+f_{2})/2=(e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3})/2=3e_{1}/2
from which
e_{1}=0
, contradicting the irreducibility of
C
.

Consider now the special case where
\Delta_{1}
is given by

s^{2e_{1}-f_{1}}x_{1}^{2}+2s^{e_{2}+e_{3}-f_{1}-1}tx_{2}x_{3}=0
, and
\Delta_{2}
is given by
2s^{e_{1}+e_{3}-f_{2}}x_{1}x_{3}+s^{2e_{2}-f_{2}}x_{2}^{2}=0
.

The cubic resolvent is (readily shown to be, and well-known to be)

\det\left(x\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}s^{2e_{1}-f_{1}}&0&0\\
0&0&s^{e_{2}+e_{3}-f_{1}-1}t\\
0&s^{e_{2}+e_{3}-f_{1}-1}t&0\end{array}\right)+y\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&0&s^{e_{1}+e_{3}-f_{2}}\\
0&s^{2e_{2}-f_{2}}&0\\
s^{e_{1}+e_{3}-f_{2}}&0&0\end{array}\right)\right)

which is

-s^{2e_{1}+2e_{2}+2e_{3}}\left((t/s)^{2}(x/s^{f_{1}})^{3}+(y/s^{f_{2}})^{3}\right)

which is easily seen to be an irreducible cubic in
\mathbb{C}(s,t)[x,y]
(for example using Eisenstein’s criterion).

3.12.

Completing the proof of Theorem
3
.

The following claim will prove that the possible scrollar invariants of primitive tetragonal covers of genus

are those in the (closed) polytope
\mathcal{P}_{4}
, scaled by
g+3
. This will complete the proof of the part of Theorem
3
dealing with primitive covers.

3.13.

Claim. —

Suppose
0<e_{1}\leq e_{2}\leq e_{3}
are integers with
\frac{1}{g+3}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})\in\mathcal{P}_{4}
. Then there are integers
f_{1}\leq f_{2}
with
\frac{1}{g+3}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3};f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}

Proof.
Take
f_{1}=\min(2e_{1},\lfloor\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{2}\rfloor)
, and
f_{2}=g+3-f_{1}
. We show that
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3};f_{1},f_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}
by showing that it satisfies the six desired inequalities. Four of them are automatic, so we need only check (a)
f_{2}\leq 2e_{2}
and (b)
f_{2}\leq e_{1}+e_{3}
.

For (a):
f_{2}\leq 2e_{2}
is equivalent to
e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}-f_{1}\leq 2e_{2}
, which is equivalent to
e_{1}+e_{3}\leq e_{2}+f_{1}
. So it suffices to show that (a1)
e_{1}+e_{3}\leq e_{2}+2e_{1}
and (a2)
e_{1}+e_{3}\leq e_{2}+\lfloor\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{2}\rfloor
. (a1) is one of the inequalities defining
\mathcal{P}_{4}
. (a2) is easily manipulated to
e_{2}\geq\lceil\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{4}\rceil
. But by inspection of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
,
\overline{e}_{2}\geq 1/4
, so
e_{2}\geq\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{4}
, and
e_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, so this case is complete.

Inequality (b) is equivalent to
e_{2}\leq f_{1}
, so it suffices to show (b1)
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
and (b2)
e_{2}\leq\lfloor\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{2}\rfloor
. (b1) is one of the inequalities defining
\mathcal{P}_{4}
.
By inspection of the vertices of
\mathcal{P}_{4}
,
\overline{e}_{2}\leq 1/2
, so
e_{2}\leq\frac{e_{1}+e_{2}+e_{3}}{2}
, so (b2) follows as

. ∎

Finally, note that
[
KO
]
proves that if
(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3})

, then it is realized by some smooth bi-hyperelliptic cover. In all cases, the smoothness of

. This concludes the proof of Theorem

. ∎

The remainder of
\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}
: the exclusively “bi-hyperelliptic” or “

We now consider the remaining points in the polytope described by (

so

is given by (

will be singular

all remaining smooth curves are in the plane

.
Note only that the point

is excluded because from the start we had a strict inequality

3.15.

Quintuples in
\mathcal{Q}
arising from smooth bi-hyperelliptic covers.
We now ask which quintuples
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3};f_{1},f_{2})
occur from smooth bi-hyperelliptic covers. If the quintuple
(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2})
is contained in

, then the discussion above provides an affirmative answer precisely when

(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2})\in\mathcal{Q}
occurs from a smooth bi-hyperelliptic curve.

If

, then we have shown in §

that the general corresponding complete intersection is primitive (hence not bi-hyperelliptic). Therefore, to show the existence of a smooth bi-hyperelliptic curve with the prescribed invariants, we restrict to a special subfamily of complete intersections. Namely, take the conic bundle
\Delta_{2}
to be general subject to the condition that
a_{11}^{2}=a_{12}^{2}=a_{13}^{2}=0
. Now, the argument of §
3
applies without change, and a general such complete intersection is a smooth bi-hyperelliptic curve (a short calculation shows that the cubic resolvent is reducible, and hence the curve is bi-hyperelliptic).

Now, if
2e_{1}>f_{1}
, again we have shown in §
3
that the general corresponding complete intersection is primitive, so we restrict to a special family. Choose a general polynomial

of degree
e_{3}-e_{2}
. Consider conic bundles
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
subject to the conditions that
a_{11}^{2}=a_{12}^{2}=a_{12}^{1}=0
and
a_{23}^{2}=pa_{22}^{2}
and
a_{23}^{1}=pa_{22}^{1}
. Fix a general choice of
\Delta_{2}
. Observe that it is smooth away from finitely many points; a general fiber is a smooth conic and no fiber over
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is a double line. Again, the cubic resolvent is easily seen to be reducible.

Now, notice that the class of
\Delta_{1}
separates points away from

. It separates tangent vectors away from the union of

and

. Therefore by Bertini’s theorem, a general intersection

is smooth on
\mathbb{P}({\mathscr{E}})^{\vee}\setminus\{V(a_{22}^{2})\cup V(x_{1},x_{3})\cup V(x_{2},x_{3})\}
.

To complete the proof, observe that for a general choice of
\Delta_{1}
and
\Delta_{2}
, we have:

(1)

there are no singular points of
\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
contained on
V(a_{22}^{2})
(on these fibers, the intersection of
\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}
is generically
4
distinct points);

(2)

the intersection of
\Delta_{1}\cap\Delta_{2}\cap V(x_{1},x_{3})
is trivial (this follows from the fact that
a_{22}^{2}
and
a_{22}^{1}

(3)

and there are no singular points of

contained on

(to see this, compute the rank of the Jacobian matrix at any point of intersection).

3.16.

Quintuples
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3};f_{1},f_{2})

For a number of reasons it is worth considering which quintuples

3.17.

Fun remarks about the “fin”.

There are even more phenomena worth pointing out. For the purposes of this discussion, we call the two-dimensional triangular offshoot the “fin” (where to be precise, the fin excludes the boundary where it meets the
3
-dimensional polytope shown in Figure
4
). The moduli space of quartic covers of fixed genus is irreducible – so perhaps it is surprising to see that its image (admittedly, under
\operatorname{Tsch}
, and asymptotically as
g\rightarrow\infty
) has two pieces.

3.18.

The “bulk” of quartic covers live on the “balanced” vertex of
\mathcal{Q}
.

The generic quartic cover of fixed genus is balanced (i.e.
e_{3}-e_{1}\leq 1
). This implies that the “bulk” of the “mass” of the moduli space is concentrated on the “balanced” vertex, where the
\overline{e}_{i}
are all equal and the
\overline{f}_{j}
are both equal.

In §
8
we make this statement precise by defining a
density function

\rho_{ref}^{\rm{geo}}\colon\mathbb{R}^{5}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
which sends a point
(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2})
to a quantity which reflects the “proportion” of quartic curves with those given invariants. Surprisingly,
\rho_{ref}^{\rm{geo}}
is a piecewise linear function, supported on the region pictured on Figure
1
, and continuous on its support. We give an explicit formula for
\rho_{ref}^{\rm{geo}}
(Proposition
8
), and show that
\rho_{ref}^{geo}(1/3,1/3,1/3;1/2,1/2)=1
, and this is where it obtains its maximum value.

3.19.

But, a codimension
2
sublocus of
\mathcal{H}_{4,g}
lives as far away from the balanced vertex as possible.

However, there is one other point where

3.20.

Lemma.

Let
C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a quartic cover whose scrollar invariants lie on the fin. Then

has a distinguished quadratic subcover, and that quadratic subcover has genus

Proof.
Because

lies in the fin,
e_{2}>e_{1}
. Consider the multiplication map
\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\otimes\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\rightarrow\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}
and fix a splitting of
\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}

{\mathscr{A}}
be the subbundle
{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(-e_{1})
. Because

{\mathscr{A}}\otimes{\mathscr{A}}
under the multiplication map lies in
{\mathscr{A}}
; in other words,
{\mathscr{A}}

{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
-subalgebra of
\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}
. Therefore

factors through the hyperelliptic curve
\underline{\operatorname{Spec}}\;{\mathscr{A}}
, which visibly has genus
e_{1}-1
.
∎

Upon fixing a genus
g
, each possible value of
h
gives rise to a line in the fin (explicitly, the line
\frac{h}{g+3}=\overline{e}_{1}-\frac{1}{g+3}
). Upon fixing

, the “bulk” of the moduli space of quartic covers factoring through genus
h
hyperelliptic curves lies where the line meets the edge of the fin given by
\overline{e}_{2}=\overline{e}_{3}
(a glance at Figure
5
may be enlightening). Concretely: fix a value

. Then, the maximum value of
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
restricted to the hyperplane
\overline{e}_{1}=\overline{h}
is achieved precisely when
\overline{e}_{2}=\overline{e}_{3}
.

Now, the maximum value of
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
on the fin is attained at the least balanced vertex
(0,1/2,1/2;0,1)
, which is precisely the point where
\overline{h}=0
. In this case, the dimension of the moduli space of bi-hyperelliptic quartic covers whose quadratic subcover is a genus
0

2g+1
. Compare this to the dimension of the moduli space of quartic covers, which is
2g+3
. The two spaces have comparable dimension as

attains the same value at both vertices.

3.21.

Arithmetic meaning of the fin.

This discussion is related to the remarkable arithmetic fact due to Bhargava that, when ordered by absolute discriminant, a positive portion of quartic number fields have Galois group
D_{4}
, not
S_{4}
; see
[
Bh
, Thm. 4]
.

Qualitatively, our discussion above shows that there are lots of quartic covers whose scrollar invariants lie either at the “balanced” vertex or the “least balanced” vertex. The scrollar invariants of curves are analogous to the successive minima of rings of integers in number fields (see §
8
for details). It is possible to show
[
Ve3
, Thm. 5.5]
that when ordered by absolute discriminant, that
S_{4}
-fields tend to lie at the “balanced” vertex, and
D_{4}
-fields tend to lie at the “least balanced” vertex. The fact that the density function
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
takes value
1
on both vertices reflects the fact that a positive portion of quartic number fields have Galois group
D_{4}
.

3.22.

“Fins” occur in all composite degrees.

This is not a peculiarity of
d=4
; there is a similar story with “fins” in all non-prime degrees. (This should be seen as analogous to the fact that Malle’s conjecture guesses that there are many field extensions with Galois group not
S_{d}
when

is composite).

The key ideas are visible in the case
d=6
. Define

Q_{3}:=\left.\begin{cases}(\overline{e}_{1},\dots,\overline{e}_{5})\;:\;\sum\overline{e}_{i}=1,\;0\leq\overline{e}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\overline{e}_{5},\text{ and }\\
\overline{e}_{2}\leq 2\overline{e}_{1},\;\overline{e}_{4}\leq\overline{e}_{3}+\overline{e}_{1},\;\overline{e}_{5}\leq\overline{e}_{3}+\overline{e}_{2},\;\text{ and }\overline{e}_{5}\leq\overline{e}_{1}+\overline{e}_{4}\end{cases}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}.

Q_{2}:=\left.\begin{cases}(\overline{e}_{1},\dots,\overline{e}_{5})\;:\;\sum\overline{e}_{i}=1,\;0\leq\overline{e}_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\overline{e}_{5},\text{ and }\\
\overline{e}_{3}\leq\overline{e}_{1}+\overline{e}_{2},\;\overline{e}_{4}\leq 2\overline{e}_{2},\;\overline{e}_{5}\leq\overline{e}_{3}+\overline{e}_{2},\;\text{ and }\overline{e}_{5}\leq\overline{e}_{1}+\overline{e}_{4}\end{cases}\right\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}.

A suitable generalization of Theorem
1
shows that the scrollar invariants of a sextic curve lie in
Q_{3}\cup Q_{2}
; moreover it is not hard to check that

is precisely
\mathcal{P}_{6}
. (For a proof, see
[
Ve2
, Thm. 1.26]
). One might conjecture that the possible scrollar invariants of sextic curves are precisely those that (appropriately scaled) lie in
Q_{2}\cup Q_{3}
, but this is not necessary for the discussion here.

Let

Upon fixing a genus

, each possible value of

gives rise to a

Similarly, each possible value of

In general composite degree

Observation.

Consider the

Choose a point

[

]
, which is a field-theoretic analogue of Kneser’s theorem in additive combinatorics. (
[

Suppose

5.
The possible scrollar invariants do not form a convex set if
d
is not a prime power

(This section does not rely on the results of §
4
. It is only placed here because of where it is in the narrative of the introduction.)

In this section, we show that if
d
is not a prime power, then the possible scrollar invariants do not form a convex set for infinitely many
g
. With some care this argument should be modifiable to show the result for all
g\gg_{d}1
(for
d
not a prime power), but it did not seem worth the trouble. For small
g
, nonconvexity could plausibly “accidentally” fail because of the paucity of integer points. The argument is a bit intricate, so for the sake of comprehensibility, we first give a concrete example for
d=6
, then give the case when
d
is the product of two distinct primes, and finally give the general argument.

5.1.

The case
d=6
.

Our strategy is as follows. We will construct two sextic covers
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, both by genus
g=994
curves, which have scrollar invariants
(1,166,167,332,333)
and
(1,2,331,332,333)
respectively. We then show that there can be no such cover with scrollar invariant
(1,111,222,332,333)
, which lies on the line segment joining the previous two points.

5.2.

The two covers.

Each cover will be constructed as a compositum of a hyperelliptic (degree 2) and a cubic (degree 3) cover. In particular, these covers are not primitive, which is of course necessary given Theorem
1
.

Choose a general homogeneous polynomial

of degree

(to be named later, where

{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}

is the universal such form, we have

(19)

v_{U}(\alpha_{i}-\alpha_{j})=\min(v_{U}(\alpha_{i}),v_{U}(\alpha_{j})).

Computing
U
-adic valuations, we see that

(20)

v_{U}(\Disc(F))=(\ell a_{0})(2d-2)+2\sum_{i<j}\min(v_{U}(\alpha_{i}),v_{U}(\alpha_{j})).

Expanding out the last term, we see that:

(21)

\sum_{i<j}\min(v_{U}(\alpha_{i}),v_{U}(\alpha_{j}))=\sum_{i<j}v_{U}(\alpha_{i})=\ell\sum_{i<j}(b_{i}-b_{i-1})=\ell\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(d-i)(b_{i}-b_{i-1}).

Now, a short computation shows that

(\ell b_{0})(2d-2)+2\ell\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}(d-i)(b_{i}-b_{i-1})=2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}

as required.
∎

Evaluating on
f_{\ell}
we obtain:

\Disc(f_{\ell})=u^{2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}}H(u,g_{0}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}),\dots,g_{d}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})).

Because
\deg(f_{i})\geq a_{i}
, all the
g_{i}
have nonnegative degree, and so for a general choice of
g_{0},\dots,g_{d}
, the polynomial
H(u,g_{0}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}),\dots,g_{d}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}))
will not be divisible by
u
. Therefore the ramification divisor of
\tau:C_{\ell}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
has degree precisely
2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}
at
u=0
.

The scrollar invariants of
\tau:C_{\ell}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
, again by Theorem
6
, are
(\ell\deg(f_{1}),\dots,\ell\deg(f_{d-1}))
. Now, by definition the scrollar invariants of
\tau\circ\rho\colon C_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
are:

(\ell\deg(f_{1})-\ell b_{1},\dots,\ell\deg(f_{d-1})-\ell b_{d-1}).

Hence the degree of the ramification divisor of
\tau\circ\rho\colon C_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
at
u=0
is
2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}
less than that of
C_{\ell}^{\prime}
, and hence
C_{\ell}
is unramified above
u=0
.
∎

6.14.

Completion of the proof of Theorem
6
.

Because
\tau\circ\rho\colon C_{\ell}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
is unramified, the points of
C_{\ell}
above
t=0
in the map
m_{\ell}\circ\tau\circ\rho=\pi\circ\phi\circ\gamma
are smooth.

There is a natural inclusion
{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\subseteq\gamma_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C_{\ell}}
which pushes forward to an inclusion

(\pi\circ\phi)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\subset(\pi\circ\phi\circ\gamma)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C_{\ell}}

Restricting to the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\infty
and taking global sections, we have that

\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^{1},(\pi\circ\phi)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C})\subset\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^{1},(\pi\circ\phi\circ\gamma)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C_{\ell}}).

By construction,

\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^{1},(\pi\circ\phi)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C})=\Gamma(\mathbb{A}^{1},(\pi\circ\phi\circ\gamma)_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C_{\ell}})\cap K(C).

The last equality shows that the smoothness of
C_{\ell}
implies the smoothness of
C
above
t=0
.

Finally,
h^{0}(C,{\mathscr{O}}_{C})=h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},{\mathscr{O}})+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\oplus{\mathscr{O}}(b_{i}-\deg(f_{i})))
. If
\deg(f_{i})-b_{i}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
, then
h^{0}(C,{\mathscr{O}}_{C})=1
so
C
is irreducible.
∎

There is one more trick we may play to construct
even more
scrollar invariants — we may start with singularities above multiple points of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. More precisely if
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{m}\in\mathbb{C}[s,t]
are linear forms with distinct roots, then in the notation as in Theorem
6
, we obtain the following theorem, whose proof is the obvious generalization of that of Theorem
6
.

6.15.

Theorem (“multiple points of the base”). —

Fix
(a^{1}_{0},\dots,a^{1}_{d}),\dots,(a^{m}_{0},\dots,a^{m}_{d})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d+1}
and let
b_{i}^{j}
be the lower convex hull. If
\ell_{j}^{a_{i}^{j}}\mid f_{i}
for all
i,j
, then there exists a curve
C
and a birational morphism
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}
such that the composition
C\rightarrow C^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
has scrollar invariants

(\deg(f_{1})-\sum_{j}b_{1}^{j},\dots,\deg(f_{d-1})-\sum_{j}b_{d-1}^{j}).

Now suppose the
\ell_{j}
are general and
f
is general subject to the constraint that
\ell_{j}^{a^{j}_{i}}\mid f_{i}
. If
\deg(f_{i})\geq\sum_{j}a^{j}_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq d
and
\deg(f_{i})-\sum_{j}b^{j}_{i}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1

is smooth and irreducible.

6.16.

Proof of Theorem
6
.

The proof of Theorem
6
is almost identical to that of Theorem
6
. The only additional difficulty is checking smoothness above the vanishing of the
\ell_{j}
; in other words, we need to prove that if the
\ell_{j}
are general and
f
is general subject to the given constraint and
\deg(f_{i})\geq\sum_{j}a^{j}_{i}
for all
0\leq i\leq d
and
\deg(f_{i})-\sum_{j}b^{j}_{i}\geq 1
for all
1\leq i\leq d-1
, then
C
is smooth. Suppose without loss of generality that
\ell_{1}=t
.

Following the idea behind the proof of Theorem
6
, let
\ell
be the least common multiple of the
b^{1}_{i}
. Consider the commutative diagram as in the proof of Theorem
6
where now
C_{\ell}
is the given partial normalization above
\ell_{1}
. Then
C_{\ell}^{\prime}
is defined by the equation

f_{\ell}(x,y)=u^{\ell a_{0}^{1}}\Big(\prod_{j=2}^{m}\ell_{j}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})^{a_{0}^{j}}\Big)g_{0}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})x^{d}+\dots+u^{\ell a_{d}^{1}}\Big(\prod_{j=2}^{m}\ell_{j}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})^{a_{d}^{j}}\Big)g_{d}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})y^{d}.

As before, let
F\in\mathbb{C}[U,L_{2},\dots,L_{m},G_{0},\dots,G_{d}]

\Disc(F)=U^{2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}^{1}}H(U,L_{2},\dots,L_{m},G_{0},\dots,G_{d})

for some polynomial

which is not divisible by

\Disc(f_{\ell})=u^{2\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\ell b_{i}^{1}}H(u,\ell_{2}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}),\dots,\ell_{m}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}),g_{0}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell}),\dots,g_{d}(u^{\ell},v^{\ell})).

Because

. Similarly,

∎

. For example, suppose we wanted to obtain the scrollar invariants

7.
The image of
\operatorname{Tsch}:\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is not preserved under generization (
d>4,g\gg_{d}1
)

In low genus (
2g+1\leq d
), the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}:\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is preserved under generization. This is because the image of the map
\operatorname{Tsch}
is a single point, and the corresponding bundle
{\mathscr{O}}(e_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathscr{O}}(e_{d-1})
has no nontrivial generization because
e_{d-1}-e_{1}\leq 1
(see Proposition
2
). Likewise, in low degree (
d\leq 4
), the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}
is also preserved under generization. This is trivial for
d=2
, and follows from §
2
and Theorem
3
respectively when
d=3,4
. In this section we prove that when
d\geq 5
and
g\gg_{d}1
, the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}
is
not
preserved under generization, i.e. that there exists a smooth curve whose Tschirnhausen bundle admits a generization which does
not
arise as the Tschirnhausen bundle of a smooth curve. Along the way, we also prove that when
d\geq 4
and
g\gg_{d}1
, the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}
restricted to primitive covers is also not preserved under generization.

7.1.

Proposition. —

If
d\geq 5
, then for
g\gg_{d}1
, the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}:\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
is not preserved under generization.

Proof.
Suppose
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d-1})
is a concave nondecreasing sequence of positive integers, with
e_{1}=a
,
e_{2}=2a
,
e_{3}=3a
,
e_{4}\geq 3a+5
(where
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\geq 5
). Then by Theorem
1
, there is a primitive cover
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
by a smooth curve with Tschirnhausen bundle
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}\cong\oplus_{i=1}^{d-1}{\mathscr{O}}(e_{i})
. If
(e^{\prime}_{1},\dots,e^{\prime}_{d-1})=(e_{1},e_{2}+1,e_{3}+2,e_{4}-3,e_{5},\dots,e_{d-1})
, then the bundle
\oplus_{i=1}^{d-1}{\mathscr{O}}(e^{\prime}_{i})
is
not
the Tschirnhausen bundle of a cover (Lemma
7
), but it is a generization of
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}
. The statement now follows from Proposition
7
.
∎

7.2.

Lemma. —
Let
C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
be a cover of degree
\geq 4
with scrollar invariants
(e_{1},\dots,e_{d-1})
. If
e_{2}>2e_{1}
, then
e_{3}\leq e_{1}+e_{2}
.

Proof.
As in §
4
, choose a coordinate
t
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}=\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{\infty\}
and a splitting

\pi_{*}{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\simeq{\mathscr{O}}\oplus{\mathscr{O}}(-e_{1})\oplus\dots\oplus{\mathscr{O}}(-e_{d-1}).

Let
K=K(\mathbb{P}^{1})
and let
L=K(C)
. As in
4
, this splitting gives rise to a basis
1,x_{1},\dots,x_{d-1}
for the vector space
L/K
.

Let
A
be the
K
-subvector space spanned by
1,x_{1}
. Because
e_{2}>2e_{1}
, we have that
AA\subseteq A
, so
A
is a quadratic field extension of
K
. Now let
B
be the
K
-subvector space spanned by
1,x_{1},x_{2}
. Because
AB
is an
A
-vector space and
\dim_{K}A=2
, the quantity
\dim_{K}AB
must be even. Therefore,
\dim_{K}AB\geq 4
. Consequently there exists some integer
k\geq 3
such that
e_{k}\leq e_{1}+e_{2}
.
∎

7.3.

Proposition. —

Fix an integer
d\geq 5
. Let
S
be the set of length
d-1
nondecreasing concave sequences
(e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{d-1})
starting with
e_{1}=a
,
e_{2}=2a
,
e_{3}=3a
,
e_{4}\geq 3a+5
(where
a\in\mathbb{Z}
,
a\geq 5
). Let
T=\{\sum e_{i}:(e_{i})\in S\}
. Then
\mathbb{Z}^{+}\setminus T
is a finite set.

Proof.
For any
a\geq 2
, and
(d-2)a+5\leq e_{d-1}\leq(d-1)a
, the sequence
(e_{1}=a,e_{2}=2a,\dots,e_{d-2}=(d-2)a,e_{d-1})
is in
S
. Thus for all
a\geq 5
, the interval
[f(a),g(a)]\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is in
T
for
f(a)=a+\cdots+(d-2)a+((d-2)a+5)=\binom{d-1}{2}a+((d-2)a+5)
and
g(a)=a+\cdots+(d-2)a+(d-1)a=\binom{d-1}{2}a+(d-1)a
. But it is easy to check that (i)
f(a+1)<g(a)
for
a\gg_{d}1
(as
g(a)-f(a+1)=a-h(d)
for appropriate
h(d)
), and (ii)
f(a)\rightarrow\infty
as
a\rightarrow\infty
, so these intervals cover all but finitely many of the positive integers. ∎

7.4.

Remark: the set of Tschirnhausen bundles of primitive covers is not preserved under generization (
d\geq 4
and
g\gg_{d}1
).

Let
\mathcal{H}ur_{d,g}^{p}
denote the open substack of primitive covers, and let
\operatorname{Tsch}^{p}:\mathcal{H}ur^{p}_{d,g}\rightarrow\mathcal{B}un_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
denote the restriction. We now show that when
d\geq 4
and
g\gg_{d}1
, the image of
\operatorname{Tsch}^{p}
is not preserved under generization.

Indeed, when
d=4
, take any nondecreasing sequence
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
with
e_{1}=a
,
e_{2}=2a
, and
a+2\leq e_{3}\leq 3a
for
a\geq 1
. Because
e_{3}\leq e_{1}+e_{2}
and
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
, it can be realized as the scrollar invariants of a smooth primitive cover (Theorem
3
). Let
(e^{\prime}_{1},e^{\prime}_{2},e^{\prime}_{3})=(e_{1},e_{2}+1,e_{3}-1)
and note that the corresponding bundle does
not
arise as the Tschirnhausen bundle of a primitive quartic cover (Theorem
3
), but is indeed a generization of
\oplus_{i=1}^{3}{\mathscr{O}}(e_{i})
. To conclude in this case it suffices to show that if
S
is the set of length
3
sequences
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
as above, then the set
\mathbb{Z}^{+}\setminus\{\sum e_{i}:(e_{i})\in S\}
is finite. When
d\geq 5
, the proof of Proposition
7
already shows that there exists a smooth
primitive
cover whose Tschirnhausen bundle has a generization which is not the Tschirnhausen bundle of any smooth cover.

8.
Motivation and analogy from number theory

There is a particularly strong analogy to this question in number theory. The analogy to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is

\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{Z}
, and the analogy to the curve
C
is the ring of integers
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
in a degree
d
extension
K
of
\mathbb{Q}
; see
[
He
, §7]
for more details on the arithmetic side.

Our arguments in this paper extend readily to this arithmetic setting, but do not yield the same strength of results (number fields whose ”shape” is in this ”concave region”), because of one missing step: the need for a sieving result to ensure that there is a maximal order in a number field, which is strictly harder than the geometric analogue of Bertini’s Theorem (a blunt dimensional statement).

8.1.

Extension: Density functions on the polytopes.

We now make precise the connection we predict between the geometry of degree
d
number fields, and the geometry of degree
d
covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Fix a positive integer
d\geq 2
.

8.2.

Definition.

For each open ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
g>0
, let

\pi^{geo}(B,g)=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}}\dim\left(\bigcup_{\vec{e}\in\mathbb{Z}^{d-1}:\vec{e}/(d+g-1)\in B}\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},g}\right)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},g}
is the moduli space of genus
g
degree
d
covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with scrollar invariants
\vec{e}
. (The dimension of a finite disjoint union is the maximum of the dimensions, and the dimension of the empty set is
-1
.)

We now define the arithmetic analogue of
\pi^{geo}(B,g)
. For a degree
d
number field
K
, let
\lambda_{0},\dots,\lambda_{d-1}
be the successive minima with respect to the following quadratic form: embed
K\xhookrightarrow{}\mathbb{C}^{d}
and take
\frac{1}{d}
times the standard Hermitian quadratic form on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
. (Or even better: consider
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
as a metrized line bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathscr{O}}_{K})
. Pushing forward via the degree
d
cover
\operatorname{Spec}({\mathscr{O}}_{K})\rightarrow\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
gives a metrized vector bundle on
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{Z})
. Then
\log\lambda_{0},\dots,\log\lambda_{d-1}
are simply the slopes of this metrized vector bundle.)

Then
\lambda_{0}=1
and
\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}\lambda_{i}\asymp_{d}\sqrt{\lvert\Disc(K)\rvert}
by the AM-GM inequality and Minkowski’s second theorem respectively. The quantity
\log\lvert\Disc K\rvert
is analogous to
2g
and the quantities
\log\lambda_{i}
are analogous to the scrollar invariants
e_{i}
.

8.3.

Definition.

For each open ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
and
X>0
, let

\pi^{arith}(B,X)=\frac{\log\#\{K\;\mid\;\lvert\Disc(K)\rvert\in[X,2X],\;\log_{\sqrt{X}}(\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{d-1})\in B\}}{\log\#\{K\;\mid\;\lvert\Disc(K)\rvert\in[X,2X]\}}

(By definition, we take
\log(0)=-1
, just as the dimension of the empty set is taken to be
-1
, cf. Definition
8
.)

Let
\Xi=\{(\overline{e}_{1},\dots,\overline{e}_{d-1})\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:\sum\overline{e}_{i}=1\}
be the hyperplane whose coordinates sum to
1
.

8.4.

Conjecture. —

For all
B
, both
\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}(B,g)
and
\lim_{X\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{arith}(B,X)
exist, are equal, and depend only on the intersection of
B\cap\Xi
and not on
B
.

Conjecture
8
is trivially true in the case
d=2
. We give a proof in the case
d=3
below.

Figure 7.
An artist’s depiction of the points
(\log_{\sqrt{X}}\lambda_{1},\log_{\sqrt{X}}\lambda_{2})
as we range over cubic fields with absolute discriminant in the interval
[X,2X]
. Note how the points cluster close to the line segment, but are not necessarily contained on it. Moreover, the points are concentrated near the point
(1/2,1/2)
, and become more sparse as we move to the left hand side of the line segment. Beyond the left hand side of the line segment, the points abruptly end.

8.5.

Proposition. —

Conjecture
8
is true when
d=3
.

Proof.
The locus
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},g}
is nonempty precisely when
e_{1}+e_{2}=g+2
,
e_{1}\leq e_{2}
, and
e_{2}\leq 2e_{1}
. In this case, the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},g}
is given by

h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\operatorname{Sym}^{3}({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))\otimes{\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e})^{\vee})-h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))).

Moreover, the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{3,g}=2g+5
. So, if
B
nontrivially intersects the closed line segment from
(1/3,2/3)
to
(1/2,1/2)
, then

\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}(B,g)=\sup\left\{1-\frac{\overline{e}_{2}-\overline{e}_{1}}{2}:(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2})\in B\cap\Xi\right\}

If
B\cap\Xi=\emptyset
, then
\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}(B,g)=0
. The equivalent computation in the arithmetic case can essentially be found in the proof of
[
Ve3
, Thm. 1.6]
.
∎

For
d=4
, the difficulty of sieving out maximal orders is already too hard in the arithmetic setting. For case of not necessarily maximal orders when
d=4,5
, see
[
Ve3
]
for a description of
\pi^{arith}
. In these cases, the nonmaximal version of
\pi^{arith}
is described as the maximum of a continuous piecewise linear function supported on a polytope; in these known cases, this matches the version of
\pi^{geo}(B,g)
including singular curves.

Let
\pi^{geo}(B)
and
\pi^{arith}(B)
be the corresponding limits. One might be tempted to ask: how does the density of curves or number fields concentrate on specific points of
\Xi
? The following conjectural density function is meant to capture that information. For a point
x\in\Xi
define

\rho^{geo}(x)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}(B_{i}(x))

where
\{B_{i}(x)\}
is a set of open balls all containing
x
and with radius converging to
0
. Define
\rho^{arith}
similarly.

8.6.

Conjecture. —

The quantities
\rho^{geo}(x)
and
\rho^{arith}(x)
exist and are equal. They are supported on a finite union of rational polytopes, are piecewise linear, and are continuous on their support.

8.7.

Corollary. —

If
d=3,4,5
, Conjecture
8
is true.

Proof.
The case
d=3
follows from the explicit description of
\pi^{geo}(B_{i}(x))
given in Proposition
8
. The case
d=4
follows from Theorem
3
(see Proposition
8
). The case

can similarly be extracted from
[

, Theorem 1.5]
.
∎

8.8.

Further extension: refined density functions taking into account “all” scrollar invariants.

As we saw in Section

, a quartic cover has three scrollar invariants
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
and an associated trigonal cover with scrollar invariants

8.9.

Definition.

For each open ball

and

\pi_{ref}^{geo}(B,g)=\frac{1}{\dim\mathcal{H}_{4,g}}\dim\left(\bigcup_{(\vec{e},\vec{f})\in\mathbb{Z}^{5}:(\vec{e},\vec{f})/(g+3)\in B}\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}\right)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}\subseteq\mathcal{H}_{4,g}
is the moduli space of genus

covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
with scrollar invariants
\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}
. (As before, the dimension of a finite disjoint union is the maximum of the dimensions, and the dimension of the empty set is

Similarly, one can define

(in this case, we replace the data of

with the successive minima of the cubic resolvent ring). As before, we obtain conjectural density functions
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
and

and give the formula below.

8.10.

Proposition. —

In the notation of Section
3
, the function
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
is supported on the union of
\mathcal{Q}
and

\mathcal{Q}^{\prime}\cap\{(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{5}:\overline{f}_{1}=2\overline{e}_{1}\}.

On this polytope, we have

\rho_{ref}^{geo}(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2})=1-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{ijk}\max\{0,\overline{f}_{k}-\overline{e}_{i}-\overline{e}_{j}\}.

Moreover, the projection
\rho^{geo}
is supported on the polytope
\mathcal{P}_{4}^{\prime}
and is given by the formula

\rho^{geo}(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3})=\sup\{\rho_{ref}^{geo}(\overline{e}_{1},\overline{e}_{2},\overline{e}_{3};\overline{f}_{1},\overline{f}_{2}):\overline{f}_{1}+\overline{f}_{2}=1\}.

Proof.
Theorem
3
classifies which
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3};f_{1},f_{2})
arise from smooth quartic covers. When
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}
is nonempty, its dimension is given by

h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\operatorname{Sym}^{2}{\mathscr{E}}\otimes{\mathscr{F}}^{\vee})-h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e})))-h^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{1},\End({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{f})))+1.

After expanding the expression above into a piecewise linear function in
\vec{e}
and
\vec{f}
and then taking appropriate limits, one obtains the claimed value for
\rho^{geo}_{ref}
. An analogous argument gives the claimed value for
\rho^{\rm{geo}}
. We leave the details to the reader.
∎

We may now make a conjecture analogous to Conjecture
8
.

8.11.

Conjecture. —

The quantities
\rho_{ref}^{geo}(x)
and
\rho^{arith}_{ref}(x)
exist and are equal. They satisfy

\rho_{ref}^{geo}=\rho_{ref}^{arith}

and are supported on a finite union of rational polytopes, piecewise linear, and continuous on their support.

8.12.

Final remarks: refined density functions in all degrees.

For
d=5
, it is natural to make a conjecture analogous to Conjecture
8
. Canonically associated to a smooth degree
5
cover is a degree
6
cover called its sextic resolvent, and one should replace the data of
(f_{1},f_{2})
in the quartic case with the data of the scrollar invariants of the sextic resolvent. Indeed from
[
FV
, Theorem 1.5]
one may compute an explicit description of
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
and
\rho^{geo}
for quintic covers. In this case, the density function
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
is supported on a union of two polytopes, is piecewise linear, and is continuous on its support.

For general degree
d
, the work of Casnati and Ekedahl
[
CasE
, Thm. 1.3]
(see also
[
Cas1
]
) shows that a smooth degree
d
cover
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{P}^{1}
canonically embeds in the projectivization of the Tschirnhausen bundle. The minimal resolution of
C
inside the
\mathbb{P}^{d-2}
-bundle is of the form

0\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{d-2}(-d)\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{d-3}(-d+2)\rightarrow\dots\rightarrow\mathcal{N}_{1}(-2)\rightarrow{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}\rightarrow{\mathscr{O}}_{C}\rightarrow 0.

The
\mathcal{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{d-2}
are vector bundles on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
whose ranks are determined by
d
, and whose degrees are of the form
c_{i}(d+g-1)
where
c_{i}
is an integer depending only on
i
and
d
.

In the case
d=3
, the Tschirnhausen bundle
{\mathscr{E}}_{\pi}
determines every bundle in the minimal resolution. When
d=4
and
5
, the Tschirnhausen bundle of the associated cubic resolvent cover (resp. sextic resolvent cover), along with the Tschirnhausen bundle of the curve itself, determines every bundle in the minimal resolution. In general, one may consider the sublocus of
\mathcal{H}_{d,g}
where
\mathcal{N}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{N}_{d-2}
is fixed, and define a (conjectural) refined density function
\rho_{ref}^{geo}
. By using the tuning modules defined in
[
WY
]
, which give lattices analogous to the vector bundles
\mathcal{N}_{i}
, one may define a (conjectural) arithmetic refined density function
\rho_{ref}^{arith}
. It is interesting to ask if the natural conjecture, analogous to Conjecture
8
, is true in this case.

8.13.

Furthest extension: density functions valued in the Grothendieck ring.

There is a natural analogous question to consider in the Grothendieck ring of stacks, which we only state when
3\leq d\leq 5
. Assume henceforth that
3\leq d\leq 5
.

Following
[
E
]
,
let
\grstk
be the space obtained by taking the Grothendieck ring of varieties, inverting
\mathbb{L}
, and then completing with respect to the dimensional filtration. Then
\grstk
comes with a ”dimension function”
\dim:\grstk\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
, and
-\dim
is a valuation on
\grstk
.

8.14.

Motivation.

The goal of this section will be to define (conjecturally) a function
\rho^{gr}_{ref}
taking values in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
. Moreover, if
\rho^{gr}_{ref}
is defined, then
\rho^{gr}_{ref}
is
\rho^{geo}_{ref}(x)
(a real-valued function) plus negative-dimensional “correction terms”:

\rho^{gr}_{ref}(x)=\rho^{geo}_{ref}(x)+Z(x)

for some function
Z(x)
with
\dim(Z(x))<0
for all
x
. (We consider
\rho^{geo}_{ref}(x)
as taking values in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
via the natural map
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.)

8.15.

Setup.

Note first that
\pi^{geo}_{ref}
is defined using
quotients
of dimensions. To emulate this in
\grstk
, we will first partially define a logarithm with base
\mathbb{L}
taking values in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
, and take quotients of the form

\frac{\log_{\mathbb{L}}X}{\log_{\mathbb{L}}Y}

where
X,Y\in\grstk
. If
\dim Y\neq 0
, then it will follow from our definition of logarithm (Definition
8
) that

\frac{\log_{\mathbb{L}}X}{\log_{\mathbb{L}}Y}=\frac{\dim X}{\dim Y}+Z

where
\dim(Z)<0
. Therefore, the quotient of the logarithms is the quotient of dimensions plus lower-dimensional terms, as desired. Conjecture
8
is about the convergence of
\dim X/\dim Y
, in an appropriate limit; in this section we extend to conjecture the convergence of the
Z
-term.

8.16.

Definition.

Let
X\in\grstk
. Suppose
X=\mathbb{L}^{\dim X}\times(1+Y)
where
\dim Y<0
. Let
\log_{\mathbb{L}}(X):=\dim X+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{Y^{i}}{i}\in\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

As stated previously, to a degree
4
cover there is a naturally associated pair
\vec{f}=(f_{1},f_{2})
, and to a degree
5
cover there is a naturally associated quintuple
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\dots,f_{5})
. For the sake of convention, we associate to a degree
3
cover the
empty
tuple
\vec{f}=()
.

8.17.

Lemma. —

If
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}
is nonempty, then
[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]=\mathbb{L}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}}\times(1+Y)
for some
Y\in\grstk
with
\dim(Y)<0
. Similarly,
[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]=\mathbb{L}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}}\times(1+Y)
for some
Y\in\grstk
with
\dim(Y)<0
. Therefore
\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]
and
\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]
are well-defined. Moreover
\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]
is invertible in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.
We begin by proving that
[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]
and
[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]
have the specified form. Each
\mathcal{H}_{d,g}
has some
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}
as an open dense locus; therefore it suffices to prove the claim for
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}
. By
[
Cas2
,
Cas1
]
, if
\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}
is nonempty then

[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)

[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{f}))]=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)

in the cases
d=3
and
d=4,5
respectively, for some integer
k
and some element
X\in\grstk
with
\dim(X)<0
. A short computation shows that for any vector bundle
{\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e})
on
\mathbb{P}^{1}
, we have
[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]=\mathbb{L}^{\dim\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))}(1+Z)
for some
Z\in\grstk
such that
\dim Z<0
. The power series expansion

(22)

\frac{1}{1+z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}z^{n}.

implies that
[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]^{-1}=\mathbb{L}^{-\dim\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))}\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}Z^{n}\right)
. Substituting, we obtain

[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]^{-1}=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)\times(1-Z+Z^{2}\dots)

[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{e}))]^{-1}\times[\GL({\mathscr{O}}(\vec{f}))]^{-1}=\mathbb{L}^{k}\times(1+X)\times(1-Z_{e}+\dots)\times(1-Z_{f}+\dots),

respectively in the cases
d=3
and
d=4,5
as required.

We now prove that
\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]
is invertible in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We have shown that

[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]=\mathbb{L}^{\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}}\times(1+Y)

for some
Y\in\grstk
with
\dim(Y)<0
. By definition

\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]=\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{Y^{i}}{i}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}\times(1+W)

where
W=(\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g})^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{i+1}\frac{Y^{i}}{i}
so
\dim(W)<0
. Therefore,
\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]^{-1}
is given by

(\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g})^{-1}(1+W)^{-1}=(\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g})^{-1}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}W^{n}\in\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}.

∎

When
3\leq d\leq 5
and
B
is an open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, or
\mathbb{R}^{9}
respectively, a potential alternate definition of
\pi
is:

\pi_{ref,avg}^{geo}(B,g)=\frac{\avg\left\{\dim\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}:(\vec{e},\vec{f})/(d+g-1)\in B,\;\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}\neq\emptyset\right\}}{\dim\mathcal{H}_{d,g}}.

(By convention, we take
-1
be the average of the empty set.) Although
\pi_{ref,avg}^{geo}(B,g)
may not be equal to
\pi^{geo}_{ref}
, a short computation shows that the limit
\pi_{ref,avg}^{geo}(B)
still exists, and moreover

\rho^{geo}_{ref}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}_{ref}(B_{i}(x))=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{geo}_{ref,avg}(B_{i}(x)),

where
\{B_{i}\}
is any set of open balls all containing
x
and with radius converging to
0
. In other words the resulting density functions are equal. We make this equivalent alternate definition because it suggests a corresponding definition in the Grothendieck ring.

Thus motivated, we define

\pi^{gr}_{ref,avg}(B,g)=\frac{\avg\left\{{\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}]:(\vec{e},\vec{f})/(d+g-1)\in B,\;\mathcal{H}_{\vec{e},\vec{f}}\neq\emptyset}\right\}}{\log_{\mathbb{L}}[\mathcal{H}_{d,g}]}

taking values in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Q}
. We may then proceed to make an analogous conjecture:

8.18.

Conjecture (?). —

Fix
3\leq d\leq 5
. For all
B
,
\lim_{g\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{gr}_{ref,avg}(B,g)
converges in
\grstk\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{R}
.

There is little direct evidence for this conjecture (perhaps it should be a question), but because any counterexample to this conjecture would be very interesting, we feel it is worth making. As before, let
\pi^{gr}_{ref,avg}(B)
denote the corresponding limit, and for a point
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, or
\mathbb{R}^{9}
respectively define

\rho^{gr}_{ref}(x)=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\pi^{gr}_{ref,avg}(B_{i}(x))

where
\{B_{i}(x)\}
is any set of open balls all containing
x
and with radius converging to
0
.

8.19.

Conjecture. —

When
3\leq d\leq 5
, the limit
\rho^{gr}_{ref}(x)
exists.

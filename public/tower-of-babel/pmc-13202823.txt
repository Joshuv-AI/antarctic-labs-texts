A visual analysis of the research dynamics of biomarkers for lung cancer screening
Authors: Huange Zhu, Burong Li, Ranran Kong, Jun Yang, Zeqi Guo, Jiafeng Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-invasive biomarkers offer potential to improve risk stratification and early diagnosis of lung cancer, complementing low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) screening. This study employed bibliometric analysis to identify global research trends, collaborative networks, and future directions in lung cancer biomarker research. Publications on lung cancer biomarkers for screening were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). Data processing and visualisation were performed using Citespace, VOSviewer, KH Coder, Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA) topic modelling, and the online bibliometric analysis platform. Burst detection analysis was performed to predict emerging research trends.ResultsAnalysis of 3636 publications revealed exponential growth in research output since 2014. International collaboration demonstrated a dual-core structure centred on China and the United States, with Chinese institutions showing high publication volumes and American institutions demonstrating greater citation influence. Journal citation mapping revealed three evolutionary phases: basic mechanisms-clinical translation-intelligent integration. LDA topic modelling identified 22 topics grouped into five core research directions: imaging and pathological diagnostic techniques; molecular and omics marker research; liquid biopsy and new detection technologies; clinical and translational medicine research; and tumour biology and treatment mechanisms. Burst detection analysis predicted future four priority areas: epigenetic studies centred on DNA methylation for risk prediction; treatment resistance and invasion mechanisms; liquid biopsy technology development; and targeted therapy clinical trials.ConclusionsLung cancer biomarker research has evolved towards multimodal, intelligent screening approaches. Future research priorities include DNA methylation-based markers, circulating microRNA signatures, and artificial intelligence-assisted diagnostic platforms to improve early detection accuracy and complement LDCT screening.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13148-026-02084-2.Keywords: Lung cancer, Biomarkers, Screening, Epigenetics, Liquid biopsy, Bibliometrics, Topic modelling

Background

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer incidence and mortality worldwide, posing a significant threat to public health [1]. Initial assessments of the disease typically involve imaging examinations and tumour marker tests once symptoms appear. Histological examination and cytological analyses serve as the standard diagnostic methods. Given that most patients have been identified at an advanced stage, the 5-year survival rate for lung cancer remains low despite significant advancements in treatment [2]. Therefore, early detection and treatment are widely recognised as the most effective strategies to improve patient outcomes.

Currently, low-dose computed tomography (LDCT) is recognised as the established screening method for high-risk populations with lung cancer. However, LDCT has a high false-positive rate [3], leading to unnecessary follow-up procedures such as positron emission tomography/computed tomography scans, bronchoscopies, or invasive surgeries. These procedures impose additional financial, psychological, and physical burdens on patients. Additionally, the risks of overdiagnosis, overtreatment, radiation exposure, and the cost of long-term follow-up are challenges facing the promotion of LDCT screening [4]. Guidelines also emphasize that people should be aware of the potential harms for screening. When compared to no screening, LDCT screening can result in a corresponding incremental cost-effectiveness ratios (ICERs) were $52,000 per life-year gained and $81,000 per quality-adjusted life-years gained, with the ICERs varied widely in subgroup and sensitivity analyses [5]. Therefore, reducing the abuse of limited healthcare resources calls for improving the evidence supporting public policy on LDCT eligibility and optimizing the risk threshold for screening strategies. In addition, many people still think that chest radiography is adequate for early detection, and public awareness of both primary (tobacco control) and secondary (regular screening) prevention is poor.

The development of a non-invasive, accurate, and repeatable biomarker detection method, which serves as an effective supplement to LDCT, aims to achieve more precise stratification of high-risk groups and differential diagnosis of suspicious nodules found by LDCT [6, 7]. Liquid biopsy may be effective for lung cancer screening. Research on new biomarkers for lung cancer encompasses a wide range of types, including microRNAs (miRNAs), circulating tumour DNA (ctDNA), and DNA methylation. The amount of detectable ctDNA varies depending on the tumour location, size, vascularity, and the detection technology used. In present practice, the FDA-approved plasma test is for EGFR mutations in ctDNA using PCR or next-generation sequencing [8, 9]. DNA methylation markers show great promise for early detection owing to their intrinsic signal amplification features [10], and circulating miRNAs demonstrate efficiency in distinguishing benign from malignant pulmonary nodules [11].

Despite the considerable potential demonstrated by these biomarkers in research settings, their translation into routine clinical practice is hindered by two fundamental bottlenecks. First, a critical lack of standardisation spans the entire analytical workflow—from sample collection and exosome isolation to detection platforms and bioinformatic pipelines—resulting in significant inter-system variability regarding exosome distribution, ctDNA detection sensitivity, and methylation quantification, thereby severely limiting inter-laboratory data comparability [12]. Second, clinical validation remains inadequate; current evidence derives predominantly from small-scale, single-centre retrospective studies, with a marked paucity of large-scale prospective cohorts necessary to validate clinical utility, screening benefits, and cost-effectiveness. Consequently, the establishment of comprehensive standard operating procedures (SOPs) and the execution of rigorous multi-centre prospective studies are urgently required to facilitate the successful integration of liquid biopsy into precision oncology frameworks for lung cancer.

Numerous research papers are constantly being produced, making it challenging for researchers to sift through the vast amount of literature to identify the latest research advances. In recent years, bibliometric analysis has enabled researchers to automatically discern knowledge structures, forecast emerging trends, and construct complex networks of disciplinary evolution from vast literature corpuses, thereby markedly enhancing the depth of analytical insight and predictive accuracy within medical research. And it also has changed a lot from traditional citation analysis to more complicated, computationally intensive approaches. A bibliometric investigation into ChatGPT applications in medicine [13] evaluated the attention afforded to AI technologies across medical education and clinical practice, identified disciplinary development imbalances, and flagged ethical and safety concerns as research hotspots, thereby illustrating the decision-support role of bibliometrics in healthcare policy formulation. Similarly, an analysis of immune-related adverse events (irAEs) in neoadjuvant immunotherapy [14] employed hotspot burst analysis to reveal a paradigmatic shift in immuno-oncology from a singular focus on efficacy to an “efficacy-safety balance”, whilst uncovering critical knowledge gaps regarding the mechanisms of specific irAEs—thus attesting to the prognostic value of bibliometrics in refining precision oncology strategies.

Despite the considerable accumulation of research within the field of lung cancer biomarkers, there remains a deficiency in visualised spatiotemporal mapping of research trend evolution based on big data analytics, alongside a lack of prospective assessments regarding future hotspot directions utilising predictive algorithms. These impede researchers’ ability to comprehensively identify critical junctures in the translation of biomarker research from basic science to clinical practice and preclude precise anticipation of the field’s forthcoming breakthrough trajectories. This study aims to systematically present research progress, key challenges, and future trends in this field through systematic sorting and multidimensional analysis of literature related to biomarkers for early lung cancer screening. The findings are intended to provide valuable insights for both clinical practice and scientific research.

Methods

Data source and search strategy

Data were retrieved from the Web of Science Collection (WoSCC) using the advanced search module with the following strategy: (Topic Search (TS) = (Lung cancer biomarkers OR Lung cancer markers OR Tumour markers OR Biomarkers of lung cancer OR Serum markers OR Plasma markers) AND TS = (Screening OR Early detection OR Early diagnosis)) AND (TI = (lung NEAR/3 cancer OR pulmonary NEAR/3 cancer OR lung NEAR/3 carcinoma OR pulmonary NEAR/3 carcinoma) OR AB = (lung NEAR/3 cancer OR pulmonary NEAR/3 cancer OR lung NEAR/3 carcinoma OR pulmonary NEAR/3 carcinoma)) AND (Publication Year (PY) = (2005-01-01/2024-12-31)). The NEAR/3 operator retrieves records where the search terms appear within three words of each other, regardless of order, to ensure contextual proximity.

Study selection

Two researchers independently screened all retrieved records based on titles, abstracts, and keywords to ensure data quality and exclude weak relevance studies. “Weak relevance” was described as the research issue being mentioned in the document’s title or abstract, but it is not discussed or examined in the study. Discrepancies were resolved through discussion with a third researcher. All data were extracted from WoSCC and were limited to English-language articles. The search was conducted on 24 July 2025. Data analysis and visualisation tools

Data processing and visualisation were performed using Citespace (version 6.3.3 R1), VOSviewer (version 1.6.20), KH Coder (version 3b07d), and the online bibliometric analysis platform (https://bibliometric.com).

Citespace was used to perform dual-map overlay, keyword, reference, and cluster analyses of the selected articles to identify the developmental dynamics and future trends in this research field [15]. VOSviewer was widely used in bibliometric studies to visualise knowledge maps of large-scale literature data [16]. KH Coder was mostly used to perform basic quantitative analysis of text content by preprocessing text and building high-frequency word co-occurrence networks. The online bibliometric platform (https://bibliometric.com) was further employed to visualise collaboration networks amongst countries and regions [17].

Topic modelling analysis

Topic modelling was applied to identify latent themes within the collected literature. We used Latent Dirichlet Allocation (LDA), a widely adopted approach for analysing large volumes of unstructured text [18]. LDA constructs a vocabulary and analyses word co-occurrence frequencies across documents to determine topic distributions. We performed a sensitivity analysis in R with the ldatuning package to identify the ideal number of topics (K), assessing models with K ∈ {2, 3,..., 50}. Three metrics were used to evaluate the model’s performance: the semantic coherence of Mimno et al. (2011) [19], where higher values are preferred, and the measures suggested by Arun et al. (2010) [20] and Cao et al. (2009) [21], where lower values indicate better match. The Arun and Cao metrics decline with K and exhibit clear elbows, whereas Mimno coherence rises steeply before plateauing. Additional topics do not significantly increase model quality, as evidenced by the minimization metrics reaching a local optimum at K = 22 while semantic coherence stays high. As a result, we kept 22 themes for the final analysis, striking the ideal balance between model stability and semantic granularity. Additional information is included in Additional file 1.

Topic significance ranking (TSR) is an unsupervised analytical tool that uses a four-phase Weighted Combination (WC) decision approach to determine the semantic value of the inferred topics in probabilistic topic models through unsupervised inspection [22]. A four-phase decision-making process based on a Weighted Linear Combination (WLC) was used in this study. Step One: standardization procedures. We standardized the cosine dissimilarity, correlation coefficient, and KL divergence. Step Two: Intra-criterion weighted linear combination. To integrate different criteria and obtain an overall score, WLC is used to calculate the standardized score of each topic under a specific criterion. Step Three: Inter-criterion WC. The scores from each criterion are integrated using a WC approach, employing two methods for assigning weights to the scores. Step Four: Topic Significance Score. The overall score of each topic is multiplied by the normalized weights. The final calculation of topic significance is computed from the weighted sum of these criteria. Detailed equations and parameter settings are provided in the Additional file 2.

Burst detection analysis

The Burst Detection algorithm proposed by Kleinberg [23] is a method for identifying periods of unusually frequent events in time-series data, and can provide valuable insights for trend prediction. The early stages of bursts and potential emerging trends can be identified by analysing the frequency, duration, and intensity of historical bursts.

The raw data downloaded from the WoSCC were first imported into Microsoft Excel 2024 for preliminary sorting. We retained only the titles and abstracts of each article to form the original corpus. We set a word frequency threshold, removed common stop words, and defined specific stop words to ensure the reliability and validity of the results. LDA topic modelling and word cloud mapping were performed using KH Coder software and WordCloud. In the final stage, we manually named each topic based on the 10 most prominent articles and 20 topic terms under each topic.

Analysis of topics

Bibliometric analysis provides an initial framework for understanding the historical evolution and patterns of scientific research by utilising data from a variety of sources, including countries, authors, journals, keywords, and references. We applied topic modelling approaches for an in-depth examination to better understand the field’s history and predict its future development. Bibliometric analysis focuses on explicit quantitative indicators to evaluate publishing trends and collaborative networks, whereas topic modelling explores latent topics by analysing article titles and abstracts. This approach allows for large-scale data processing and the extraction of key concepts and ideas from the text, guiding the direction of subsequent research.

Based on the LDA topic modelling analysis (Fig. 4) (Additional file 5), 22 topics were identified and summarised into five categories: imaging and pathological diagnostic techniques, molecular and omics marker research, liquid biopsy and new detection technologies, clinical and translational medicine research, and tumour biology and treatment mechanisms. Keyword clouds were also generated to visualise the key contents of these topics (Additional file 6).

Emerging trends and new developments

In recent years, bibliometric analysis methods based on burst algorithms have shown great potential for predicting the frontiers of scientific research. This study uses this method to analyse the relevant literature on the field of early screening markers for lung cancer from 2005 to 2024, revealing the dynamic evolution law of keywords in this field and predicting potential research hotspots in the future.

In the theme of Molecular Mechanisms and Biology (Fig. 7A-D), GENE and GENE-EXPRESSION remain fundamental terms in this field and will continue to maintain their popularity. Future research should further investigate the roles of specific genes and their expression patterns in the occurrence and development of lung cancer. Notably, the growing intensity of terms related to epigenetic and gene mutation-related topics, such as MUTATIONS and DNA METHYLATION, was significantly enhanced.

Discussion

The results of this study showed that the field has evolved from slow accumulation to rapid development over the past two decades, showing significant characteristics of multidisciplinary intersection, technology integration, and clinical transformation.

Integrative analysis of key results in comparison with previous research

Research on early screening markers for lung cancer has formed a global cooperation system, with China and the United States at its core of multi-polar coordination [24, 25]. China has become a leading country with its explosive growth in recent years, benefiting from breakthroughs in detection technology, policy support, and deepened international cooperation. It is expected to maintain a high growth trend in the future. The United States has continued to lead the academic direction in this field with its profound research foundation and high-impact institutions (such as the NCI and MD Anderson Cancer Centre). While U.S. studies are more easily authorised by the esteemed journals covered by WoSCC, a significant portion of Chinese-language research is not indexed in WoSCC, forfeiting the possibility of secondary citations—a key factor in the observed bibliometric trend. Research strategies in Europe and North America are already mature, and their outputs readily inform the formulation of transnational screening guidelines; the Asia-Pacific region, despite its high publication volume, exhibits relatively lower per-article impact, indicating that quantitative dominance has yet to be translated into guideline-level evidence. The advantage of Chinese institutions in cooperation scale and research activity, along with the outstanding performance of American institutions in terms of academic influence and historical foundation, reflects the complementarity of regional and disciplinary advantages. This provides a structural basis for further advancing transnational cooperation.

The institutional collaborations network reveals a dual-track ecosystem of “technology-clinical translation”. The high citation impact of Harvard, NCI, MD Anderson, etc. confirms that a closed loop of “biomarker-clinical trial-guideline” has been established; developing countries can adopt these trial designs to reduce methodological exploration time. The author co-citation network outlines a research-direction timeline. The highly and persistently cited cluster [26–28] maintains long-term attention on epidemic and viral-immune topics; the clinical-translational cluster [29, 30] with high average citations indicates that multi-omics combined models are ready for translation; the emerging burst cluster [31] may foreshadow the next hotspot, allowing journals to plan special issues proactively. The journal co-citation network exposes an interdisciplinary gap. Engineering and materials-technology journals, although co-cited, display lower betweenness centrality than oncological-clinical journals, implying that the bridge between engineering technology and clinical validation remains weak. As highlighted by Merker et al. (2018) [32] and Wan et al. (2017) [33], ctDNA assays face significant challenges regarding pre-analytical variables, including collection tubes, non-standardised centrifugation parameters, and inconsistent sample transport temperatures, with inter-laboratory concordance significantly affected by variations in sample collection protocols. Li et al. (2019) [34] further emphasise that, despite nanotechnology achieving remarkable breakthroughs in analytical sensitivity and specificity, the translation of laboratory prototypes to clinical routine encounters multiple bottlenecks: standardisation deficits compromise inter-platform consistency and reproducibility; insufficient clinical validation with large-scale prospective trials; ambiguous regulatory pathways; and prohibitively high costs and limited accessibility also constrain deployment in resource-limited settings. Important ways to close this gap include creating interdisciplinary research grants, shared pre-validation platforms that comply with ISO, and standardised regulatory frameworks.

Interdisciplinary analysis shows that research on early screening markers for lung cancer has gradually expanded from its early focus on clinical medicine and epidemiology to multiple cutting-edge fields. In recent years, the rapid development of multi-omics integration, liquid biopsy, and AI-assisted diagnosis has significantly advanced the field towards precision medicine [35–37]. The clear path of knowledge flow from basic research to clinical application, highlights the field’s strong integration ability and transformation potential, which is crucial for promoting the transformation of scientific achievements into health services. Overall, the field has experienced the evolution path of “basic mechanism-clinical transformation-intelligent integration”.

Based on the LDA topic modelling results, topics such as liquid biopsy, multi-omics signatures, and AI algorithms have risen significantly in recent years, indicating that the technical direction of non-invasive, high-throughput, and intelligent research is becoming mainstream. Additionally, the topic evolution analysis showed that some topics (T2, T6, and T19) have gradually moved from the edge to the core, indicating that the field is undergoing a significant transformation from single-marker exploration to the integration of systems biology and clinical practice, and from basic exploration to applied or interdisciplinary research [38–40].

The burst detection algorithm further predicts the possible research hotspots in the future. At the molecular level, epigenetic studies represented by DNA METHYLATION are becoming increasingly important. Many studies have reported that abnormal DNA methylation distributions and methylation alterations in lung cancer can be used to detect cancer early, track the tissue of origin, predict therapeutic response and prognosis, and monitor minimal residual disease [41]. DNA methylation in plasma and tissue has the potential to be used as a biomarker for the early detection of lung cancer. Significant differences in DNA methylation patterns between smokers and non-smokers were found through connections between DNA methylation and clinical traits [42]. A DNA methylation-based classifier can distinguish small cell lung cancer (SCLC) subtypes, whereas a cell-free DNA classifier might guide precision SCLC therapy [43]. In the future, epigenetic studies with DNA METHYLATION as the core are expected to become a new direction for predicting the risk of lung cancer and for early diagnosis.

At the clinical level, terms such as RESISTANCE and INVASION have become new focal points in disease progression research. Future studies will focus more on exploring the invasive behaviour of lung cancer cells and their resistance mechanisms to treatment, thereby providing a theoretical basis for the development of more targeted early intervention strategies. Patients with osimertinib-resistant non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) may benefit from circ_PPAPDC1A as a novel therapeutic target and diagnostic biomarker [44]. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition triggers exocytotic Rabs to initiate secretory pathways that promote immunosuppression and invasion in LUAD [45]. They serve as the basis for therapeutic approaches intended to combat immunotherapy resistance.

At the diagnostic level, liquid biopsy technology based on CIRCULATING MICRORNAS and multimolecular SIGNATURES will be the main development direction for the early screening and risk assessment of lung cancer. Serum exosomal miRNAs may serve as diagnostic and prognostic markers for SCLC [46]. A diagnostic model based on plasma exosomal miRNAs has also been developed to distinguish between benign and malignant lung nodules [11]. According to Cox regression models, plasma circulating miRNAs’ levels were predictive of outcomes in patients with NSCLC undergoing or following radiation therapy [47].

At the treatment level, more attention will be paid to the in-depth exploration of specific targeted drugs and clinical TRIALS, reflecting that lung cancer treatment has gradually shifted from the traditional chemotherapy model to precise targeted therapy. Future research will focus more on evaluating the efficacy of specific targeted drugs and verifying new treatment strategies through large-scale clinical trials to provide more effective early treatment options for lung cancer patients. Together, these trends depict the evolutionary path of early screening marker research for lung cancer from macro-clinical observation to micro-molecular mechanisms, from single markers to multi-omics signatures, and from traditional treatment to precision intervention, providing essential guidance for future research in this field.

Clinical implications

A significant path from clinical medicine to the fields of health, nursing, and medicine shows obvious interdisciplinary characteristics between this field, clinical practice, and health-related disciplines. Research on public health has indicated that tobacco smoking is the primary etiological factor for lung cancer, significantly outweighing other risk factors like environmental and occupational exposures (e.g., radon, air pollution, asbestos). Although public health campaigns in the late 20th century reduced smoking prevalence and, as a result, lung cancer rates, disparities exist among different racial and ethnic groups regarding incidence rates, suggesting further genetic, cultural, and geographic influences [48]. For the implementation of LDCT screening and focused public health interventions, identifying areas with higher risks is essential. The need for primary prevention by reducing carcinogen exposure is emphasized by thorough studies on lung cancer etiology, which help estimate global incidence and develop cost-effective prevention strategies, especially in developing countries where lung cancer cases are on the rise [49]. Furthermore, the stigma associated with lung cancer presents serious obstacles to diagnosis and treatment; it makes it difficult for patients and clinicians to discuss smoking and screening, increases psychological distress in high-risk individuals, and reduces their desire to seek early screening and suitable treatment options [50].

AI, computational biology, cell and tissue engineering, and medical experimental technology have all improved rapidly. The results of the LDA topic modelling analysis further highlight the significance of liquid biopsy and new detection technologies in the investigation of lung cancer biomarkers. MeSH analysis shows that the terms “Liquid Biopsy”, “Circulating Tumour DNA”, “Multiomics”, and “DNA Methylation” remained to be regularly used. Also, in the theme of Diagnosis and Biomarkers analysis based on burst algorithms, classical liquid biopsy markers remained to demonstrate constant frequency.

Liquid biopsy is emerging as a promising method for biomarker detection and lung cancer screening, focusing on epigenetic changes, ctDNA, and miRNA expression. Exosomal miRNAs have been developed as diagnostic and prognostic markers for lung cancer [11, 46]. Dynamic changes in ctDNA are particularly important in the early detection of postoperative recurrence because of its rapid and sensitive characteristics [10, 51]. Studies have demonstrated that if ctDNA is not cleared in patients with NSCLC following perioperative treatment, their risk of recurrence increases significantly. The combined treatment strategy, which relies on epigenetic regulation mechanisms and targeted drugs, is becoming a research hotspot to overcome drug resistance. ctDNA methylation is also crucial for identifying resistance to immunotherapy and tyrosine kinase inhibitors [40].

Future directions

Emerging diagnostic technologies, especially liquid biopsy and AI-assisted diagnosis, are profoundly changing the landscape of early screening markers for lung cancer. Liquid biopsy enables non-invasive detection of biomarkers, theoretically overcoming tumour heterogeneity and facilitating dynamic monitoring; meanwhile, AI leverages machine learning to analyse medical imaging and multi-omics data, significantly enhancing screening efficiency and the precision of risk stratification. Nevertheless, the transition of these technologies from proof-of-concept to routine clinical implementation confronts multiple translational bottlenecks, including technical sensitivity, ethical compliance, and data privacy, which necessitate systematic resolution through large-scale multi-centre validation and the establishment of industry standards. Variations in technical sensitivity and the absence of standardisation constitute pivotal barriers to the clinical deployment of liquid biopsy. As a previous review suggested, a successful screening NGS-based blood test would have to test up to 1000 genes, with the ctDNA detection limit improving ten-fold from the current 0.1% to less than 0.01% [52]. The lack of unified SOPs across the entire analytical workflow—from specimen acquisition and exosome isolation to methylation quantification—results in significant systematic disparities between platforms regarding exosome distribution, ctDNA detection sensitivity, and methylation quantification, severely compromising inter-laboratory data comparability and reproducibility. More critically, existing evidence derives predominantly from small-scale, single-centre retrospective studies; the distinct lack of rigorous validation through large-scale prospective cohort studies precludes confirmation of the clinical utility, screening benefits, and cost-effectiveness of these biomarkers, thereby impeding their rapid integration into precision oncology frameworks. On the other hand, technological limitations of AI-assisted diagnostics include the opacity of decision-making processes and insufficient algorithmic generalisability. These technologies create severe ethical challenges linked to patient informed authorisation and data privacy abuses. Clinical translation depends not merely on technological optimisation but critically upon the establishment of transparent algorithmic auditing mechanisms, stringent data security standards, and corresponding ethical governance frameworks to ensure the fairness and accountability of AI-driven decisions.

Systematic resolution requires synergistic advancement across three dimensions: first, the establishment of ISO-compliant shared pre-validation platforms, facilitated by interdisciplinary research funding, to promote end-to-end standardisation from biospecimen processing to data analysis, with stringent SOPs ensuring inter-platform coherence; second, the execution of large-scale, multi-centre prospective clinical trials to empirically validate the diagnostic performance, screening benefits, and health economic value of biomarkers and AI models, thereby addressing the current deficit in clinical validation; and third, the construction of clear and harmonised regulatory frameworks that unambiguously define approval pathways for liquid biopsy and AI as novel in vitro diagnostics or medical devices, coupled with policy interventions to reduce technology costs and enhance accessibility in resource-limited settings.

Achieving technical standardisation, validation, and ethical governance is critical for integrating liquid biopsy and AI—potentially through comprehensive screening models that combine various molecular markers—to enhance intervention strategies against lung cancer. This integrated approach aims to decrease lung cancer mortality through precision prevention and targeted therapies.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. Although this study conducted a comprehensive search and analysis based on the WoS core database, there are limitations. For example, important findings non-English are unavoidably omitted because this analysis solely gathered English-language records from the WoSCC, and topic modelling relies on the title and abstract text, which may not fully capture the detailed information in the full text. In order to remove language bias and guarantee that the results are globally representative, follow-up analyses should be repeated in multilingual databases.

In addition, the burst detection algorithm is more generally suited for visualizing topic development over time, providing researchers with a quick understanding of key topics in the field. However, with further research, the negative effect may be gradually identified and reported when the frequency of burst words reaches the threshold of high-frequency words. In Kleinberg’s algorithm, several parameters were set subjectively. Currently, the burst detection method still requires highly skilled operators with regard to word screening, parameter selection, and interference word elimination.

Conclusion

This bibliometric analysis of 3,636 publications reveals that lung cancer biomarker research has evolved from basic molecular mechanisms to intelligent, multimodal integration over two decades. Epigenetic biomarkers, particularly DNA methylation, and liquid biopsy technologies represent the most promising directions for clinical translation. For researchers, these findings highlight opportunities in multi-omics integration and AI-assisted diagnosis. For clinicians, the emergence of non-invasive biomarker panels offers potential to complement LDCT screening and reduce false-positive rates. Future research should prioritise large-scale validation studies, standardisation protocols, and integration of emerging technologies such as circulating miRNA signatures to facilitate clinical implementation and improve early detection of lung cancer.

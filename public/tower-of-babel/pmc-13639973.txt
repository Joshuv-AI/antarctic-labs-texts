Single-stage hybrid repair of extensive aortic dissection with traumatic diaphragmatic hernia
Authors: Wenfei Zhu, Yang Huang, Shenghui Bi, Liang Yang, Ziyan Zhang, Maodan Chen, Zewei Li, Hui Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We report a rare case of a 70-year-old male patient who developed a left diaphragmatic hernia combined with extensive aortic dissection and exhibited signs of lower limb ischemia following a motor vehicle accident. An urgent, single-stage surgical intervention was performed, consisting of endovascular aortic repair first to stabilize circulation, followed by diaphragmatic repair. The patient recovered smoothly postoperatively, with no abnormalities observed at 1-month and 6-month follow-ups. This case highlights the necessity of accurate diagnosis and multidisciplinary team collaboration in emergency thoracoabdominal trauma.

Introduction

Blunt chest trauma (BCT) accounts for over 15% of all trauma admissions in emergency departments worldwide and is the second leading cause of death after head injuries in motor vehicle accidents. Caused by mechanisms of both direct force and high-speed deceleration, BCT can lead to a variety of severe injuries. Rib fractures are the most common injury, while life-threatening major complications such as traumatic aortic injury (TAI) and traumatic diaphragmatic hernia (TDH) are relatively rare. The pre-hospital mortality rate for TAI is as high as 75%–90%. Even for those who survive to hospital admission, untreated acute Stanford type B aortic dissection (TAD) carries a high risk of progression to type A dissection, with a significantly increased mortality rate. The incidence of TDH is approximately 0.46%, often accompanied by concomitant injuries such as rib fractures and hemothorax. Acute cases require urgent surgical repair to prevent ischemia and necrosis of herniated organs. This article reports a case of a patient with traumatic diaphragmatic hernia combined with aortic dissection, who achieved a favorable outcome through multidisciplinary collaboration and a personalized surgical strategy.

Case presentation

A 70-year-old male was involved in a motor vehicle collision, presenting comatose at the scene and transported to a local emergency department. Contrast-enhanced chest Computed tomography (CT) revealed: Stanford type B aortic dissection featuring an intramural hematoma from the level of the aortic arch to the abdominal aorta, with an intimal tear at the distal abdominal aorta; thrombosis of the right common iliac artery causing moderate stenosis; diffuse thrombosis of the left common iliac artery causing severe stenosis (Fig. 1). CT also identified left diaphragmatic rupture with stomach and bowel loops herniated into the thoracic cavity, causing significant left lung compression. No overt ischemia of the herniated organs was evident. The patient exhibited tachypnea and hypoxemia (SpO₂ 83%). Thoracostomy tube drainage was performed at the left 2nd intercostal space. Cardiovascular surgery initiated the procedure in a hybrid operating room to facilitate subsequent thoracic surgery access. Access was obtained via ultrasound-guided puncture of the right common femoral artery (CFA). Angiography confirmed dissection in the distal abdominal aorta and right common/external iliac artery, and occlusion of the left common iliac artery. A longitudinal incision was made over the left CFA. An arteriotomy was performed, and a dual-lumen thrombectomy catheter was used to extract a 15 cm thrombus (Fig. 2). A 23–13-145 mm stent graft (main body) was deployed over the wire, with proximal positioning infrarenal. A 16–16-124 mm stent graft limb was deployed via the left sheath, overlapping the main body's short branch by approximately 3 cm, with distal positioning in the common iliac artery. Angiography revealed a localized dissection in the thoracic aorta, prompting deployment of a 32–18-159 mm stent graft. Completion angiography confirmed successful exclusion of the aortic dissection. A left common iliac vein dialysis catheter was placed. Hemostasis was secured, and both access sites were closed. The patient was repositioned into the right lateral decubitus position with lumbar support. A left posterolateral thoracotomy incision (~20 cm) was made at the 7th intercostal space along the posterior axillary line. The thoracic cavity contained the majority of the stomach, small bowel, and part of the spleen. The herniated organs showed no gross ischemia or necrosis. Upon reduction, a 15 cm full-thickness diaphragmatic tear was identified, extending from the pericardial border to the lateral chest wall (Fig. 3). Given the absence of tissue loss, the defect was repaired using interrupted mattress sutures with pledgets. Exploration revealed significant displacement of the 6th to 9th rib fragments causing chest wall deformity and paradoxical movement. No significant pulmonary parenchymal injury was noted upon lung inflation. The displaced left 6th–9th rib fractures were isolated and fixed using osteosynthesis plates. Hemostasis was achieved, and a chest tube was placed. Intraoperative transfusion included 4 units of packed red blood cells and 600 ml of fresh frozen plasma. The patient remained hemodynamically stable on mechanical ventilation and was transferred to the intensive care unit.Fig. 3Intraoperative view via left thoracotomy.Demonstrating the diaphragmatic rupture (dashed line) and herniated abdominal contents (stomach and spleen).Fig. 3

Postoperatively, elevated creatine kinase and myoglobin levels indicated lower limb ischemia-reperfusion injury secondary to prolonged ischemia. Continuous renal replacement therapy (CRRT) was initiated. The patient was successfully extubated on postoperative day 7, and the chest tube was removed. Follow-up CT confirmed no diaphragmatic hernia recurrence, stable rib fixation hardware, and no progression of the aortic dissection. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 23 for rehabilitation.

Discussion

The incidence of traumatic diaphragmatic injury is approximately 0.46%, with 67% resulting from penetrating trauma and 33% from blunt mechanisms [1]. Forces causing traumatic diaphragmatic hernia (TDH) frequently produce associated injuries, including rib fractures, hemothorax, and abdominal organ damage [2-7]. The concomitant occurrence of traumatic aortic dissection (TAD) is considerably rarer [8–10]. TDH is classified as acute or chronic, with acute presentations requiring urgent repair [11,12]. However, in this case, the aortic dissection posed the more immediate threat to life, being a major contributor to mortality in trauma. Early detection and management of TAD are critical in the evaluation of complex polytrauma. Management strategies for combined TDH and TAD remain debated; this report presents a case supporting a single-stage surgical approach, highlighting considerations regarding technique and timing.

First, the necessity and timing of intervention: Acute Stanford type B aortic dissection represents an acute aortic syndrome with significant mortality and potential for progression to type A dissection if untreated [3]. While historically managed with open repair, endovascular therapy (TEVAR) has largely superseded open surgery for complicated acute type B dissection due to lower morbidity and mortality compared to optimal medical therapy alone [3]. In this patient, extensive dissection and iliac artery thrombosis causing malperfusion mandated urgent endovascular repair.

The single-stage approach carried inherent risks. Ensuring successful exclusion of the dissection and restoration of perfusion was critical, confirmed by postoperative angiography. Secondly, anticoagulation and hemostasis management was paramount. During the diaphragmatic repair phase, electrocautery sufficed for hemostasis as no active intrathoracic bleeding was encountered. Therapeutic low-molecular-weight heparin was initiated on postoperative day 1, with vigilant monitoring of chest tube output characteristics.

Finally, the surgical approach merits discussion. Standard approaches for acute TDH include transabdominal, transthoracic, or thoracoabdominal [1,13,14], chosen based on injury characteristics and surgeon preference. Previous studies suggest the transabdominal route is optimal for isolated acute TDH [1]. In this case, a transthoracic approach was selected for two primary reasons: to directly explore the thoracic cavity for active bleeding and to concurrently perform open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) of the displaced rib fractures through the same incision.

This report details a rare case of extensive aortic dissection combined with left TDH managed successfully with single-stage hybrid repair involving TEVAR, diaphragmatic reconstruction, and rib fixation. It demonstrates the feasibility of this approach, offering a potential management paradigm for complex traumatic injuries.

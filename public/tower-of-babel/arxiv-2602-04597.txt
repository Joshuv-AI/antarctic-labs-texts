Local well-posedness of strong solutions to the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with degenerate viscosities and far field vacuum in 3D exterior domains
Jiaxu L I, Boqiang L Ü, Bing Y UAN, a. School of Mathematical Sciences,Shenzhen University, Shenzhen 518061, P. R. China;b. School of Mathematics and Computer Sciences& Institute of Mathematics and Interdisciplinary Sciences,Nanchang University, Nanchang 330031, P. R. China

Abstract
The isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes system subject to the Navier-slip boundary conditions is considered in a general three-dimensional exterior domain. For the density approaches far-field vacuum initially and the viscosities are power functions of the density ( ρ δ \rho^{\delta} with 0 < δ < 1 0<\delta<1 ), the local well-posedness of strong solutions is established in this paper. In particular, the method we adopt can not only simultaneously handle the difficulties caused by boundary terms and far-field vacuum, but also make the selection of δ \delta independent of the gas coefficient γ \gamma .



Keywords:
Compressible Navier-Stokes equations, Degenerate viscosity, Far-field vacuum, Exterior domain

Introduction

We consider the three-dimensional isentropic compressible Navier-Stokes equations as follows:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\mathrm{div}(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+\mathrm{div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\mathrm{div}\mathbb{T}.\\
\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

Here,
t\geq 0
,
x=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
are time and space variables, respectively.
\rho=\rho(x,t)
,
u=(u_{1}(x,t),u_{2}(x,t),u_{3}(x,t))
, and
P(\rho)=a\rho^{\gamma}(a>0,\gamma>1)
are the unknown fluid density, velocity, and pressure, respectively.

\mathbb{T}
denotes the viscous stress tensor with the form

\mathbb{T}=2\mu(\rho)\mathcal{D}(u)+\lambda(\rho)\mathrm{div}u\mathbb{I}_{3},

(1.2)

where
\mathcal{D}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\big[\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T}\big]
is the deformation tensor,
\mathbb{I}_{3}
is the
3\times 3
identity matrix. The shear viscosity
\mu(\rho)
and the bulk one
\lambda(\rho)+\frac{2}{3}\mu(\rho)
satisfy the following hypothesis

\mu(\rho)=\mu\rho^{\delta},\ \lambda(\rho)=\lambda\rho^{\delta},

(1.3)

for some constant
\delta\geq 0
,
\mu
and
\lambda
are both constants satisfying

\mu>0,\ 2\mu+3\lambda\geq 0.

(1.4)

Let
D
be a simply connected bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with smooth boundary, and
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\bar{D}
be the exterior domain. In this paper, we study the initial-boundary value problem (IBVP) of
(
1.1
)
in
\Omega
with the initial condition

\displaystyle\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\,\,\,\ u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\,\,\,x\in\Omega,

(1.5)

and the Navier-type slip boundary condition (see
[
3
,
26
]
)

u\cdot n=0,\,\,\,\mbox{curl}u\times n=-A(x)u,\,\,\,\mbox{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega,

(1.6)

where
n=(n^{1},n^{2},n^{3})
is the unit normal vector to the boundary
\partial\Omega
pointing outside
\Omega
,

A
is a
3\times 3
symmetric matrix defined on
\partial\Omega
.
Also, we consider the following far-field behavior

\displaystyle(\rho,u)\rightarrow(0,0),\,\,\,as\,\,\,|x|\rightarrow\infty.

(1.7)

Preliminaries

In this section, some useful known facts and inequalities are listed. The first is the well-known Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality
[

]

Lemma 2.1
.

Assume that
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain, for

,

, and

, there exist generic constants

,

,

which depend on

and the Lipschitz character of

, such that for any

one has

\|f\|_{L^{p}(U)}\leq C\|f\|^{(6-p)/2p}_{L^{2}(U)}\|\nabla f\|^{(3p-6)/2p}_{L^{2}(U)}+C_{1}\|f\|_{L^{2}(U)},

(2.1)

\|g\|_{C(\bar{U})}\leq C\|g\|^{q(r-3)/(3r+q(r-3))}_{L^{q}(U)}\|\nabla g\|^{3r/(3r+q(r-3))}_{L^{r}(U)}+C_{2}\|g\|_{L^{q}(U)}.

(2.2)

Moreover, if
f\cdot n|_{\partial U}=0
, we can choose
C_{1}=0
. Similarly, the constant
C_{2}=0
provided
g\cdot n|_{\partial U}=0
.

The following lemma, whose proof is established in
[
1
,
3
]

Lemma 2.2
.

For any

with

depending only on

and
R_{0}
such that

•

When
u\cdot n=0
on
\partial\Omega_{R}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C\bigr(\|{\rm div}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}+\|{\rm curl}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0})}\bigr),

(2.3)

•

when
u\cdot n\neq 0
on
\partial\Omega_{R}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}\leq C\bigr(\|{\rm div}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}+\|{\rm curl}u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{R})}+\|u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega_{0})}+\|u\cdot n\|_{W^{1-1/p,p}(\partial\Omega_{R})}\bigr).

(2.4)

Proof.

First, we introduce a cut-off function
\eta(x)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{2R_{0}})
satisfying

\eta(x)=\begin{cases}1,~~~~~~~~&|x|\leq R_{0}\\
\eta(x)\in(0,1),&R_{0}<|x|<\frac{3}{2}R_{0},\\
0,&|x|\geq\frac{3}{2}R_{0},\end{cases}

(2.5)

and
|\nabla\eta(x)|<C(R_{0})

&\leq C(\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\delta}\|u_{t}\|_{L^{6}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1-\delta}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\|\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\quad+\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)\\
&\leq C(1+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{6}})+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C(1+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}).\end{split}

(5.28)

Next, multiply (
5.19
) by
3|\nabla\phi|^{4}
, one gets

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}|\nabla\varphi|^{6}=&-3|\nabla\phi|^{4}\nabla\varphi\cdot\mathcal{D}(u)\cdot\nabla\varphi-u\cdot\nabla(|\nabla\varphi|^{6})-3\zeta|\nabla\varphi|^{6}\mathrm{div}u-3|\nabla\phi|^{4}\zeta\varphi\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\mathrm{div}u.\end{split}

Integrating the above equality over
\Omega
and using (
5.28
), it holds that

\begin{split}\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}&\leq C\|\mathcal{D}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+C\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\phi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\leq C(1+\|\mathcal{D}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}})\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{split}

(5.29)

which together with Grönwall’s inequality, (
5.2
), and (
5.21
) implies

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}\leq C.

Then, similar to (
5.28
), the standard
L^{2}
-estimates combining with (
5.2
), (
5.4
), (
5.12
), (
5.21
), and (
5.22
) yield that

\begin{split}&\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\mathcal{L}u\|_{H^{1}}+C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}\|\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}u_{t}\|_{L^{3}}+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3/2}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{3}}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{6}}\\
&\quad+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{3/2}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\quad+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\|\mathcal{S}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\\
&\quad+\|\rho^{1-\delta}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\mathcal{S}(u)\|_{L^{6}}+\|\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{\delta-1}{2}}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}}+1)\\
&\leq C(1+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}})+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}\\
&\leq C(1+\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\gamma-\frac{1+\delta}{2}}\|_{L^{2}}).\end{split}

(5.30)

Finally, similar to (
3.34
), one gets from (
5.2
) and Lemmas
5.1
-
5.4
that

\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq&C\int|\nabla^{2}\varphi\cdot\nabla u\cdot\nabla^{2}\varphi|+|\nabla^{2}\varphi|^{2}|\mathrm{div}u|+|\nabla\varphi||\nabla^{2}\varphi||\nabla^{2}u|+|\varphi||\nabla^{2}\varphi||\nabla^{3}u|\Big)dx\\
\leq&C(\|\mathcal{D}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}u\|_{L^{3}}\\
&+\|\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}})\\
\leq&C(\|\mathcal{D}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}+\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}u\|_{L^{2}})\\
\leq&C(\|\mathcal{D}(u)\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)\|\nabla^{2}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla u_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{split}

This, together with Grönwall’s inequality, (
5.2
), (
5.21
), and (
5.30
) implies (
5.27
) and completes the proof of Lemma
5.5
.
∎

Now, it is enough to extend the strong solutions of
(\rho,u)
beyond
t>T^{\ast}
. In fact, Lemmas
5.2
-
5.5
imply that
(\rho,u)|_{t=T^{\ast}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{\ast}}(\rho,u)
satisfies the conditions imposed on the initial data (
1.13
) at the time
t=T^{\ast}
.
Furthermore, it holds

\mathcal{L}(u)|_{t=T^{\ast}}=\lim_{t\rightarrow T^{\ast}}\big(\rho^{1-\delta}u_{t}+\rho^{1-\delta}u\cdot\nabla u+\frac{\gamma a}{\gamma-\delta}\nabla\rho^{\gamma-\delta}-\delta\nabla\log\rho\cdot\mathcal{S}(u)\big)\triangleq\rho^{\frac{1-\delta}{2}}g|_{t=T^{\ast}}

with
g|_{t=T^{\ast}}\in L^{2}
. Thus,
(\rho,u)|_{t=T^{\ast}}
also satisfies (
1.13
). Therefore, we can take
(\rho,u)|_{t=T^{\ast}}

as the initial data and apply the local existence theorem to extend the local strong solution beyond
T^{\ast}
. This contradicts the assumption on
T^{\ast}
and thus prove Theorem
1.2
.

Tunneling Dynamics and Time Delay in Electron Transport through Time-Dependent Barriers with Finite-Bandwidth Reservoirs
Shmuel Gurvitz, Dmitri Sokolovski

Abstract
We present a transparent and analytically tractable approach to the problem of time-dependent electron transport through tunneling barriers. Using the Single-Electron Approach, we study a model system composed of a time-dependent tunneling barrier coupled to two reservoirs of finite bandwidth. Avoiding Floquet expansions, we derive simple expressions for the time-dependent tunneling current in both adiabatic and non-adiabatic regimes. Our formulation, based on the tunneling Hamiltonian framework, relates barrier modulation to measurable phase shifts in the steady-state current, offering a physically intuitive definition of the tunneling (or traversal) time. Remarkably, in the Markovian limit (wide-band reservoirs), we recover the well-known result of vanishing tunneling time. In contrast, for finite-bandwidth leads, we predict a finite time delay given by the inverse bandwidth. Our findings provide a robust foundation for understanding tunneling dynamics in non-Markovian environments and may serve as a benchmark for experimental investigations involving tunable band structures.



Electron transport through time-dependent barriers has attracted considerable attention in recent years. Traditionally, the analysis of such time-dependent currents relies on the non-equilibrium Green’s function formalism, often combined with Floquet theory or perturbative techniques. Although powerful, these methods tend to be mathematically intricate and computationally demanding. As a more accessible alternative, the Single-Electron Approach (SEA) offers a conceptually simpler framework that is valid for arbitrary time-dependent driving. In this work, we apply the SEA to a toy model consisting of a time-dependent tunneling barrier separating two reservoirs with finite bandwidth. This model can be solved analytically without resorting to Floquet expansions, providing compact, transparent expressions for the time-dependent tunneling current in both adiabatic and non-adiabatic regimes.

One can expect that harmonic modulation of the barrier’s transparency, would be followed by the steady-state current, albeit with a phase shift.
Analysis of this shift could provide yet another approach to the long-standing tunnelling time problem, i.e., the question “How long does quantum tunneling take?”.
The issue, controversial since the pioneering work of MacColl
[
1
;
2
]
continues to be so to date. With no unique theoretical or experimental definition of the tunneling duration
[
3

[

In this work we rely on the tunneling Hamiltonian (TH) representation, as far as we know, not used before in a study of tunneling times before.
However, parameters of the model are directly related to the behavior of the wave functions in the barrier region and, therefore, to the electron’s dynamics.
Since the average current can be measured far away from the barrier, the measurement is not expected to affect the manner in which an electron crosses
the barrier. Importantly, we will study the effect of the finite-width non-Markovian reservoirs on the response of the tunneling current to modulation of the barrier height or width. Regarding the tunneling times, we will compare our results with those obtained by studying wave packet motion, as well as make new predictions.

In summary: we studied tunneling current of non-interacting electrons through oscillating barrier coupled to two reservoirs of a finite bandwidth. The reservoirs are represented by a periodic lattices of identical quantum wells. The model is solved exactly in the framework of the nearest-neighbor tunneling Hamiltonian, whose parameters are related to the barrier potential through the Bardeen-type formula. We obtain new simple analytical expressions for the time-dependent transmission coefficient (conductance), valid for any shape of the time-dependent barrier, up to accuracy of the tunneling Hamiltonian method. As always, we expect the method to be accurate for broad or high barriers, where higher orders of transmission coefficient become negligible.

In the case of a periodically driven oscillating barrier, we have shown that the tunneling current may develop a phase shift. This shift, absent in the wide-band Markovian limit, appears for finite bandwidths.

Finally, experimental investigation of the band-width effects in tunneling dynamics need not be limited to electronic systems.
Availability of tuneable optical lattices
[
24
]
and laser-induced tunneling barriers
[
25
]
allow, at least in principle,
perform the relevant experiments on cold atoms.

One-arm exponents of the high-dimensional Ising model CNRS and Sorbonne Université, Paris, France, severo@lpsm.paris
Diederik van Engelenburg, Christophe Garban, Romain Panis

Abstract
We study the probability that the origin is connected to the boundary of the box of size n n (the one-arm probability) in several percolation models related to the Ising model. We prove that different universality classes emerge at criticality: ∙ \bullet For the FK-Ising measure in a box of size n n with wired boundary conditions, we prove that this probability decays as 1 / n 1/n in dimensions d > 4 d>4 , and as 1 / n 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1/n^{1+o(1)} when d = 4 d=4 . ∙ \bullet For the infinite volume FK-Ising measure, we prove that this probability decays as 1 / n 2 1/n^{2} in dimensions d > 6 d>6 , and as 1 / n 2 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1/n^{2+o(1)} when d = 6 d=6 . ∙ \bullet For the sourceless double random current measure, we prove that this probability decays as 1 / n d − 2 1/n^{d-2} in dimensions d > 4 d>4 . Additionally, for the infinite volume FK-Ising measure, we show that the one-arm probability is 1 / n 1 + o ⁡ ( 1 ) 1/n^{1+o(1)} in dimension d = 4 d=4 , and at least 1 / n 3 / 2 1/n^{3/2} in dimension d = 5 d=5 . This establishes that the FK-Ising model has upper-critical dimension equal to 6 6 , in contrast to the Ising model, where it is known to be less or equal to 4 4 , thus solving a conjecture of Chayes, Coniglio, Machta, and Schtengel [ CCMS99 ] .



1
Introduction

Understanding critical phenomena of lattice models is one of the main challenges of statistical mechanics. In the context of
second-order
(or
continuous
) phase transitions, Widom
[
Wid65
]
proposed that, near or at the critical point, thermodynamic quantities of interest should exhibit a power-law behaviour governed by
critical exponents
. Computing these exponents, or even justifying their existence, is in general a very difficult problem.

In the framework of models defined on the hypercubic lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a significant observation has been made by theoretical physicists in the twentieth century: above the so-called
upper-critical
dimension
d_{c}
of the model, the critical exponents simplify, in the sense that they match those derived in the simpler settings of trees or the complete graph. The dimensions
d\geq d_{c}
form the
mean-field
regime of the model.

We are interested in the emergence of mean-field features in the context of percolation models. The simplest and most studied example of a percolation model is given by independent percolation (or
Bernoulli
percolation). In dimensions
d\geq 2
, this model undergoes a phase transition for the existence of an infinite connected component at a parameter
p_{c}\in(0,1)
. It is widely believed that this phase transition is continuous, in the sense that there is no infinite cluster at criticality:
\theta(p_{c}):=\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]=0
. This qualitative property of the model has been established in dimension
d=2

[
Kes80
]
, and in dimensions
d>10

[
HS90
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
. Obtaining the corresponding result in the intermediate dimensions
3\leq d\leq 10
(and especially
3\leq d\leq 6
) constitutes one of the main open problems in percolation theory. It is expected that the upper-critical dimension
d_{c}
of Bernoulli percolation is equal to
6
. This means that several critical exponents cannot simultaneously take their mean-field value in dimensions
2\leq d\leq 5

[
Tas87
,
CC87
]
, while they are expected to do so in dimensions
d\geq 6

[
AN84
,
HS90
,
BA91
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
(see also
[
Hut22
]
for conditional results when
d=6
).

Among the numerous critical exponents, we focus on one which has been the object of intense study in the last thirty years: the
one-arm exponent
. The vanishing of
\theta(p_{c})
is equivalent to the decay to
0
as
n
tends to infinity of the sequence of probabilities
\theta_{n}(p_{c}):=\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
, where
\Lambda_{n}:=[-n,n]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\partial\Lambda_{n}
is the vertex boundary of
\Lambda_{n}
. According to Widom’s scaling hypothesis, this decay should be governed by a critical exponent. More precisely, it is conjectured that there exists
\rho=\rho(d)>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]=\frac{1}{n^{\rho+o(1)}},

(1.1)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity. The critical exponent
\rho
is often referred to as the

one-arm exponent
. In a seminal work, Kozma and Nachmias
[
KN11
]
showed that
\rho=2
in dimensions
d>6
under the assumption that
\mathbb{P}_{p_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]\asymp|x|^{2-d}
, where
\asymp
means that the two quantities are within a multiplicative constant of each other. In fact, they showed the stronger result that
\theta_{n}(p_{c})\asymp n^{-2}
. Presently, their assumption has been verified in dimensions
d>10
using a technique called the
lace expansion

[
HS90
,
Har08
,
FvdH17
]
, see
[
Sla06
]
for an introduction. It is expected to hold in every dimensions
d>6
. Let us mention that the assumption has also been verified in the context of sufficiently
spread-out
percolation in dimensions
d>6

[
HHS03
,
DCP25a
]
.

Outside of the mean-field regime, the results are much more restricted. Using Smirnov’s conformal invariance proof
[
Smi01
]
, it is possible to show that
\rho=\frac{5}{48}
for site percolation on the (two-dimensional) triangular lattice
[
LSW02
]
.

In this work, we study the one-arm exponent in various percolation models that arise in the study of the
Ising model
, a cornerstone of statistical mechanics and one of its most renowned models. The Ising model has a rich history in its own right: we refer to
[
DC22
]
for a detailed review, and to
[
FV17
,
DC19
]
for a mathematical introduction.

Before moving to the definitions, we need a few notations. If
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
we write
x\sim y
if
|x-y|_{2}=1
(where
|\cdot|_{2}
is the
\ell^{2}
norm on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
). If
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we write
E(\Lambda):=\{xy:x,y\in\Lambda,\>x\sim y\}
.

The Ising model is formally defined as follows: given
G=(V,E)
be a finite subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, a
boundary condition

\tau\in\{-1,0,+1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}
, an
interaction

J=(J_{xy})_{xy\in E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
, and an (inhomogeneous)
external magnetic field

\mathsf{h}=(\mathsf{h}_{x})_{x\in V}\in\mathbb{R}^{V}
, we construct a probability measure on
\{-1,+1\}^{V}
by setting

\langle F(\sigma)\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}^{\tau}:=\frac{1}{\mathbf{Z}_{G,J,\mathsf{h},\tau}^{\textup{Ising}}}\sum_{\sigma\in\{\pm 1\}^{V}}F(\sigma)\exp\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E\end{subarray}}J_{xy}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V\\
y\notin V\\
x\sim y\end{subarray}}J_{xy}\sigma_{x}\tau_{y}+\sum_{x\in V}\mathsf{h}_{x}\sigma_{x}\Big),

(1.2)

where
{F:\{-1,+1\}^{V}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}}
, and
\mathbf{Z}_{G,J,\mathsf{h},\tau}^{\textup{Ising}}
is the
partition function
of the model which is such that
\langle 1\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}^{\tau}=1
. This defines the
ferromagnetic
Ising model on
G
with
boundary condition

\tau
at parameters
J,\mathsf{h}
. If
J
(resp.
\mathsf{h}
) is constant equal to
\beta\geq 0
(resp.
h\in\mathbb{R}
), we often abuse notations and write
J=\beta
(resp.
\mathsf{h}=h
). When
\mathsf{h}=0
, we simply write
\langle\cdot\rangle^{\tau}_{G,J,0}=\langle\cdot\rangle^{\tau}_{G,J}
. When
\tau\equiv 1
(resp
\tau\equiv 0
), we write
\langle\cdot\rangle^{\tau}_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}=\langle\cdot\rangle^{+}_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}
(resp.
\langle\cdot\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}
).

Let
\beta\geq 0
and
h\in\mathbb{R}
. It is a well-known fact
[
Gri67a
,
Gri67b
]
that
\langle\cdot\rangle_{G,\beta,h}^{+}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{G,\beta,h}
admit (weak) limits when
G\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We denote them by
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\beta,h}^{+}
and
\langle\cdot\rangle_{\beta,h}
respectively. In dimensions
d\geq 2
, the model undergoes a
phase transition

[
Pei36
]
for the vanishing of the
magnetisation
at a critical parameter
\beta_{c}\in(0,\infty)
defined by

\beta_{c}:=\inf\Big\{\beta\geq 0:m^{*}(\beta):=\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta}^{+}>0\Big\}.

(1.3)

The phase transition is continuous in the sense that
m^{*}(\beta_{c})=0
, see
[
Yan52
,
AF86
,
Wer09
,
ADCS15
]
. Moreover, it is expected that
d_{c}=4
(observe that it differs from Bernoulli percolation, where one expects
d_{c}=6
). Indeed, it is known that
d_{c}>2

[
Aiz82
, Proposition 8.2]
, and that
d_{c}\leq 4

[
Aiz82
,
Frö82
,
ADC21
]
.
Tasaki
[
Tas87
]
proved that—conditionally on their existence—the critical exponents of the Ising model cannot all take their mean-field value when
d=3
. This provides a conditional proof of the equality
d_{c}=4
, but the unconditional result remains open. Let us mention that on the physics side, the so-called
\varepsilon
-expansion of Wilson and Fisher
[
WF72
]
, or the conformal bootstrap approach
[
RSDZ17
]
, both support the conjecture that
d_{c}>3
.

Among the various approaches to studying the Ising model, one of the most successful has been the use of geometric representations. Notably, several percolation representations of the model have been proposed and proved to be powerful tools for its study. We focus on two fundamental examples: the
Fortuin–Kasteleyn representation
(or
FK-Ising model
), and the (double)
random current representation
of the model.

Unlike (independent) Bernoulli percolation, these models are correlated, which significantly complicates their analysis. However, as we are about to see, this is also responsible for a richer critical behaviour, which we now describe. We postpone the precise definitions to the next subsections and give a brief account of the main results of this paper.

\bullet

If we consider the critical finite volume FK-Ising measure with wired boundary conditions
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}
, we find that
\rho=1
in dimensions
d\geq 4
. In dimensions
d>4
, we obtain the stronger result that
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\asymp n^{-1}
. This improves and completes an earlier work of Handa, Heydenreich, and Sakai
[
HHS19
]
in which the authors argued that, if
\rho
exists and
d>4
, then
\rho\leq 1
.

\bullet

On the other hand, if we consider the critical infinite volume FK-Ising measure
\phi_{\beta_{c}}=\phi_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\beta_{c}}
, we find that
\rho=2
in dimensions
d\geq 6
. In dimensions
d>6
, we obtain the stronger result that
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\asymp n^{-2}
. This matches the results obtained in the context of Bernoulli percolation
[
KN11
]
.

\bullet

Still for the measure
\phi_{\beta_{c}}
, we find that
\rho=1
in dimension
d=4
, and that
\rho\leq\tfrac{3}{2}
in dimension
d=5
. This proves that—unlike the Ising model—the upper-critical dimension of the FK-Ising model satisfies
d_{c}^{\textup{FK-Ising}}=6>4\geq d_{c}^{\textup{Ising}}
, and solves a conjecture of Chayes, Coniglio, Machta, and Schtengel
[
CCMS99
]
.

\bullet

In the case of the critical
sourceless
double random current measure
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}=\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\mathbb{Z}^{d},\mathbb{Z}^{d},\beta_{c}}
, we find that
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\asymp n^{2-d}
in dimensions
d>4
, meaning that
\rho(d)=d-2
. This matches results previously obtained in the context of
loop
percolation
[
CS16
]
.

The methods developed in this paper are robust. In a companion paper
[
EGPS25
]
, we apply them to spread-out Bernoulli percolation and obtain an alternative proof of the results of
[
KN11
,
CH20
]
.

Let us also mention that we expect the methods introduced in this paper to be useful in the study of the one-arm exponent for other Ising-type systems—including the
\varphi^{4}
model—for which similar FK and random current type representations have recently been constructed
[
GPPS25a
,
GPPS25b
]
.

Notations.

If
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we let
|x|:=\max_{1\leq i\leq d}|x_{i}|
denote the infinite norm of
x
. For every
k\geq 1
, we let
\Lambda_{k}:=[-k,k]^{d}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Let
\mathbf{e}_{i}
be the basis vector of
i
-th coordinate equal to
1
.
Throughout the paper various dimensional dependent constant appear without playing any particular role. We will therefore use the following compact notation: we write
f\lesssim g
(or
g\gtrsim f
) if there exists a constant
C\in(0,\infty)
which only depends on the dimension
d
such that
f\leq Cg
.

1.1
One-arm exponents of the FK-Ising model

The Ising model is related to a dependent percolation model: the FK-Ising model. We now define it and refer to the monograph
[
Gri06
]
or the lecture notes
[
DC19
]
for more information.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Fix
\xi\in\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\setminus E}
. If
\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E}
is a fixed percolation configuration, we let
k^{\xi}(\omega)
be the number of connected components of the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and edge set
\omega\vee\xi
that intersect
V
. The FK-Ising model at inverse temperature
\beta\geq 0
with boundary condition
\xi
is the measure on
\{0,1\}^{E}
given by

\phi^{\xi}_{G,\beta}[\omega]:=\frac{2^{k^{\xi}(\omega)}\prod_{xy\in E}(e^{2\beta}-1)^{\omega_{xy}}}{\mathbf{Z}_{G,\beta,\xi}^{{\textup{FK}}}},

(1.4)

where
\mathbf{Z}_{G,\beta,\xi}^{{\textup{FK}}}
is the partition function of the model, which guarantees that
\phi^{\xi}_{G,\beta}
is a probability measure. We let
\phi^{1}_{G,\beta}
and
\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}
denote respectively the cases
\xi\equiv 1
and
\xi\equiv 0
. It is classical that the Ising model and the FK-Ising model can be coupled together through the Edward–Sokal coupling
[
ES88
]
(see also
[
DC19
]
). This provides the following fundamental relations: for
x,y\in V
,

\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta},\quad\phi_{G,\beta}^{1}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta}^{+},\quad\phi^{1}_{G,\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}\partial G]=\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{G,\beta}^{+},

(1.5)

where we recall that
\partial G
is the vertex boundary of
G
defined by
\partial G=\{x\in V:\exists y\notin V,\>x\sim y\}
.
Again, one can construct the (weak) limits of these measures as
G\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. We denote them by
\phi^{1}_{\beta}
and
\phi^{0}_{\beta}
respectively. Observe that
m^{*}(\beta)=\phi^{1}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]
. The FK-Ising model undergoes a phase transition for the existence of an infinite cluster at the parameter
\beta_{c}
introduced above. That is, we may alternatively define
\beta_{c}
as follows:

\beta_{c}=\inf\Big\{\beta\geq 0:\phi^{1}_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]>0\Big\}.

(1.6)

It is well-known (see
[
Bod06
,
ADCS15
,
Rao20
]
) that
\phi_{\beta}^{1}=\phi_{\beta}^{0}
for all
\beta>0
. We denote the unique infinite volume measure by
\phi_{\beta}
. We are now in a position to state our results, starting with the case of wired boundary conditions. Below, we identify a set
\Lambda\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with the graph
(\Lambda,E(\Lambda))
.

1.1.1
Wired boundary conditions

Recall from the Edwards–Sokal coupling (
1.5
) that

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]=\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}^{+},

(1.7)

so that all the results below can also be formulated in terms of the Ising model directly. Our first result is a mean-field lower bound on the one-arm probability that is valid in every dimensions
d\geq 2
. It improves on a previous result of Handa, Heydenreich, and Sakai
[
HHS19
]
(which relies on the existence of the exponent
\rho
and requires
d>4
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 2
. There exists
c>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\frac{c}{n}.

(1.8)

The above bound is not very useful in
d=2
where a combination of integrability
[
Wu66
,
MW73
]
and Russo–Seymour–Welsh theory in its non-strong form
[
DCHN11
]
gives
\rho=\frac{1}{8}
. However, it provides a non-trivial bound in
d=3
, that was already derived in
[
Tas87
]
thanks to the inequalities
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\sqrt{\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}2n\mathbf{e}_{1}]}
and
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}n\mathbf{e}_{1}]\gtrsim n^{-2}
(see e.g.
[
Sim80
]
). If one additionally assumes the existence of the critical exponent
\eta
associated with the decay of the critical two-point function (see (
1.25
) below),

[
DCP25c
, Theorem 1.5]
gives the following improved lower bound in
d=3
:

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\gtrsim n^{-3/4+o(1)},

(1.9)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity.

As it turns out, (
1.8
) is sharp (up to a constant multiplicative factor) in dimensions
d>4
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d>4
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\frac{c}{n}\leq\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C}{n}.

(1.10)

Remark 1.3
.

For every
M>1
, one has

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{Mn}],

(1.11)

where the first inequality follows from Markov’s property and monotonicity in boundary conditions (see
[
DC19
]
), and the second from inclusion of events. Using Theorem
1.2
, this gives that
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\asymp_{M}1/n
in dimensions
d>4
.

The computation of the wired one-arm exponent was previously performed in the context of trees. Before stating the result obtained there, we introduce some notations. If
d\geq 1
, we let
\mathbb{T}^{d}
denote the
(d+1)
-regular tree. For every
x,y\in\mathbb{T}^{d}
, let
\textup{d}(x,y)
denote the length of the shortest past connecting
x
and
y
. We root
\mathbb{T}^{d}
at a vertex
o
and let
B_{n}:=\{x\in\mathbb{T}^{d}:\textup{d}(o,x)\leq n\}
. It was shown in
[
HK18
]
that one has
\phi^{1}_{B_{n},\beta_{c}}[o\xleftrightarrow{}\partial B_{n}]\asymp n^{-1/2}
(see also the recent
[
AVB24
]
for an exact asymptotic estimate, as well as for an extension to quenched supercritical Galton–Watson trees). The mismatch of exponents between the case of trees and the high-dimensional setting is not in contradiction with the discussion on the mean-field regime above. It can be solved by modifying the notion of distance in the tree. The proper metric to consider is
d
(o,x):=\sqrt{\textup{d}(o,x)}
. We refer to
[
KN09
]
for a discussion.

As explained above, it is conjectured that the upper-critical dimension of the Ising model satisfies
d_{c}^{\textup{Ising}}=4
. The dimension
d=4
is often referred to as
marginal
and various quantities present logarithmic divergences. This reflects the fact that the model is “barely” mean-field at
d_{c}=4
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
d=4
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\frac{c}{n}\leq\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C(\log n)^{5/2}}{n}.

(1.12)

Remark 1.5
.

When
d=4
, we conjecture that there exists
\hat{\rho}\geq 0
such that

\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]=\frac{(\log n)^{\hat{\rho}+o(1)}}{n},

(1.13)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity. Moreover, we expect neither of the bounds in Theorem
1.4
to be of the sharp order, i.e. we conjecture that
\hat{\rho}\in(0,5/2)
.

The upper bounds in Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are obtained through on a renormalisation argument, which relies on the bounds on the magnetisation with external magnetic field obtained in
[
AF86
]
. The successful implementation of the renormalisation argument relies on
[
DCP25c
]
. Theorem
1.1
relies on the mean-field lower bound on the magnetisation obtained in
[
ABF87
]
(see also
[
DCT16
]
), together with a simple integration argument. We present a more detailed overview of the proofs in Section
1.3
.

Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
have implications in the study of the relaxation time of critical Glauber dynamics of the Ising model. When
d>4
, Hu
[
Hu25
]
recently established—conditionally on a bound of the form
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\lesssim n^{-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
—that the temporal correlation between the spins
\sigma_{x}(t=0)
and
\sigma_{x}(t=T)
under an infinite volume Glauber dynamics at criticality is algebraically decaying in
T
. We present another interesting consequence of these results which has to do with the computation of the
mixing rate
exponent
\iota
introduced by Duminil-Copin and Manolescu in
[
DCM22
]
. In the same paper, they conjecture the existence of
\iota=\iota(d)>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\sup_{e\in E(\Lambda_{n})}\Big(\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]-\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]\Big)=\frac{1}{n^{\iota+o(1)}},

(1.14)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity. Roughly, the above quantity measures how the state of a given edge is influenced by boundary conditions. While
\iota
plays a crucial role in the derivation of the
scaling relations
of the planar random cluster model in
[
DCM22
]
, it is not yet clear to us how relevant it is in the study of the high-dimensional Ising (or FK-Ising) model. Nevertheless, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 4
. There exists
C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in E(\Lambda_{n})}\Big(\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]-\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle\text{if }d>4,

(1.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{e\in E(\Lambda_{n})}\Big(\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]-\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{2n},\beta_{c}}[\omega_{e}]\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C(\log n)^{5}}{n^{2}}\,,~~~~~

\displaystyle\text{if }d=4.

(1.16)

Let us mention that these bound are expected to be
sharp
(up to a logarithmic factor in
d=4
), in the sense that
\iota
should be equal to
2
in every dimensions
d\geq 4
. A brief justification to this fact can be obtained by the following scaling relation (which is derived by extrapolating on
[
DCM22
, (
R7
)]
):

\nu(d\wedge d_{c}^{\textup{Ising}}-\iota)=1,

(1.17)

where
\nu
is the correlation length exponent. Using one of the results of
[
DCP25c
]
which gives
\nu=\frac{1}{2}
, together with the expected value
d_{c}^{\textup{Ising}}=4
, this gives the conjectural value
\iota=2
in dimensions
d\geq 4
.

1.1.2
Free boundary conditions and infinite volume

We now turn to results regarding the infinite volume measure
\phi_{\beta_{c}}
.
Our first result can be compared with
[
KN11
]
. It suggests that dimension
6
plays a particular role for the FK-Ising model.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
d>6
. There exist
c,C,M>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\frac{c}{n^{2}}\leq\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C}{n^{2}}.

(1.18)

The middle inequality in (
1.18
) follows from the fact that
\phi_{\beta_{c}}
stochastically dominates
\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}
.

It is conjectured (and proved below) that—just as for Bernoulli percolation—the upper-critical dimension of the (free) FK-Ising model is equal to
6

[
CCMS99
,
FZD22
,
FZD23
,
WJ24
]
. It is therefore not surprising to observe logarithmic corrections in the (conjecturally marginal) dimension
d=6
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
d=6
. There exist
c,C,M>0
such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\frac{c}{n^{2}}\leq\phi^{0}_{\Lambda_{Mn},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C\log n}{n^{2}}.

(1.19)

The proofs of the upper bounds in Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
are based on the entropic method introduced by Dewan and Muirhead
[
DM23
]
in the context of Gaussian percolation (which includes Bernoulli percolation).
We also (again) heavily rely on the correlation length computations of
[
DCP25c
]
. The lower bounds are based on second-moment methods. We provide more details on the proofs in Section
1.3
.

Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
, together with estimates on the critical two-point function
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]
, allow us to identify the critical exponent
\delta
which describes the volume of a critical cluster. More precisely,
\delta
is defined by

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]=\frac{1}{n^{1/\delta+o(1)}},

(1.20)

where
\mathcal{C}(0)
denotes the cluster of the origin, and
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity. We find that
\delta=2
, which matches previous results
[
AB87
,
BA91
]
(see also
[
Hut22
]
) obtained for Bernoulli percolation under the so-called
triangle condition
(which is expected to hold in dimensions
d>6
).

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
d\geq 6
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{c}{n^{1/2}}\leq\,

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\leq\frac{C}{n^{1/2}}\,,

\displaystyle\text{if }d>6,

(1.21)

\displaystyle\frac{c(\log n)^{-1/2}}{n^{1/2}}\leq\,

\displaystyle\phi_{\beta_{c}}[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\geq n]\leq\frac{C(\log n)^{1/2}}{n^{1/2}}\,,~~~~~

\displaystyle\text{if }d=6.

(1.22)

The lower bounds above can also be obtained for the appropriate finite volume measures (similarly to Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
), see Remark
4.9
.

Our next results concern the value of
\rho
in dimensions
d<6
. We obtain that
\rho<2
in dimensions
d=4,5
(with a conditional result in
d=3
). This confirms the aforementioned prediction that
d_{c}^{\textup{FK-Ising}}=6
. We discuss this new result more extensively below.

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
d=5
. There exists
c>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\frac{c}{n^{3/2}}.

(1.23)

Perhaps surprisingly, in the case
d=4
, we can rely on Theorem
1.4
to prove that the one-arm exponent for the infinite volume measure takes the same value
\rho=1
as for the wired measure.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
d=4
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 2
,

\displaystyle\frac{c(\log n)^{-7/2}}{n}\leq\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C(\log n)^{5/2}}{n}.

(1.24)

Our result in
d=3
is conditional and based on the existence of the critical exponent
\eta
defined as follows:

\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}=\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]=\frac{1}{|x|^{d-2+\eta+o(1)}}.

(1.25)

The critical exponent
\eta
has recently been shown to be equal to
0
in dimensions
d\geq 4
, see
[
DCP25c
]
. In dimension
d=3
—conditionally on its existence—
[
DCP25c
]
shows that
\eta\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
. Using this input, we can prove the following result.

Theorem 1.12
.

Let
d=3
. Assume that the critical exponent
\eta
defined in (
1.25
) exists. Then, for every
n\geq 1
,

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\geq\frac{1}{n^{5/4+o(1)}},

(1.26)

where
o(1)
tends to
0
as
n
tends to infinity.

Obtaining a polynomial upper bound on
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
(or
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
) for
d=3
remains an open problem.

As mentioned above, when
d=2
, the one-arm exponent can be computed by relying on integrability
[
Wu66
,
MW73
]
and RSW theory
[
DCHN11
]
: one has
\rho=\frac{1}{8}
, regardless the boundary conditions. Here, RSW theory justifies the fact that, in
d=2
, one has

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}2n\mathbf{e}_{1}]\asymp\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]^{2},

(1.27)

where
\mathbf{e}_{1}=(1,0,\ldots,0)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In words, the above relation says that “conditionally on reaching distance
n
, two points at distance
\asymp n
of one another have a positive probability of lying in the same cluster”. A version of such a statement—which is related to
hyperscaling
—is expected to hold for all dimensions
d<6
, and up to a logarithmic factor in
d=6
. In dimension
d=4
, we know from
[
DCP25c
]
that
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}2n\mathbf{e}_{1}]=\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{2n\mathbf{e}_{1}}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\asymp n^{-2-o(1)}
. Hence, Theorem
1.11
can be seen as a (weak) evidence that (
1.27
) is indeed true (up to a logarithmic correction) in dimension
d=4
. On the basis of (
1.27
), we conjecture that
\rho=3/2
in dimension
d=5
. In dimensions
d>6
, (
1.27
) does not hold anymore, and a polynomial correction is necessary. Indeed, combining
[
DCP25c
]
and Theorem
1.7
gives

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}2n\mathbf{e}_{1}]\asymp n^{6-d}\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]^{2}.

(1.28)

The FK-Ising model is one of the very few examples of a percolation model for which it is possible to compute a critical exponent
at
(Theorem
1.8
) and
below
(Theorems
1.11
and
1.10
) the upper-critical dimension. Relatedly, significant progress has been made regarding the percolation induced by the excursion sets of the Gaussian free field on metric graphs. Among the main successes is the computation, in the intermediate dimensions
3\leq d\leq 5
, of the one-arm exponent (up to a multiplicative constant)
[
DW20
,
DPR23
,
CD24b
,
DPR25
]
(see also
[
CD25
]
for
d>6
) and the volume of the critical cluster
[
CD24a
,
DPR24
]
. See also
[
CD24c
]
for a proof of a stronger version of (
1.28
) in this setting. In the context of long-range Bernoulli percolation, let us also mention the impressive recent series of works of Hutchcroft
[
Hut25d
,
Hut25a
,
Hut25b
,
Hut25c
]
, who (in particular) manages to exactly identify several logarithmic corrections at the upper-critical dimension.

On the upper-critical dimensions of Ising and FK-Ising.

We conclude this subsection by discussing an important consequence of the above results: the upper-critical dimensions of the Ising model and its FK representation are distinct. Indeed, our results prove that
d_{c}^{\textup{FK-Ising}}=6
, in the sense that the one-arm exponent is mean-field in dimensions
d\geq 6
, but not in dimensions
d<6
.
A (perhaps) non-intuitive consequence of this result is that, in dimensions
d=4,5
, the observables of the spin model
are
mean-field, while some observables of the FK-Ising model
are not
. For the case of the one-arm probability
\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
, this result is justified by the observation that—unlike
\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
—it cannot be easily represented in terms of correlation functions of the model.

To the best of our knowledge, this surprising discrepancy in upper-critical dimensions was first conjectured by Chayes, Coniglio, Machta, and Schtengel
[
CCMS99
]
, building upon earlier observations
[
CSK82
]
. In their work, they consider the FK-Ising model on the Bethe lattice. They construct a notion of super-critical (i.e.
\beta>\beta_{c}
) correlation length and prove that the associated critical exponent
\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}
is equal to
\tfrac{1}{4}
. This value does not match the predicted one for the truncated correlations of the Ising model:
\nu^{\prime}=\tfrac{1}{2}
. Relying on a hyperscaling relation, they then observe that the value of
\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}
is consistent with an upper-critical dimension
d_{c}=6
, thereby justifying their conjecture. In our paper, we do not address the (interesting) question of the computation of
\tilde{\nu}^{\prime}
in dimensions
d>6
. However, we confirm that the upper-critical dimension of the FK-Ising model is indeed equal to
6
.

On the physics side, recent works have also been interested in establishing that the upper-critical dimension of the FK-Ising model differs from the Ising one, see for instance
[
FZD22
,
FZD23
,
WJ24
]
. Such a discrepancy in upper-critical dimensions between a spin model and its geometric representation is not a specificity of Ising, and is also expected to hold for Potts models
[
WJ24
]
. Interestingly, it is also believed to be the case for the so-called
arboreal gas
(which can be viewed as the “
0-
Potts model”) whose associated spin model is the hyperbolic
\mathbb{H}^{0|2}
model, see
[
BCH24
]
.

1.2
One-arm exponent of the sourceless double random current

Our next object of study is the sourceless double random current measure, which we quickly introduce here—see Section
5
for more details. Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. The single (sourceless) random current measure is the probability measure on current configurations
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{E}=\{0,1,\ldots\}^{E}
defined by

\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta}[\mathbf{n}]=\frac{1}{Z^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta}}\mathds{1}_{\partial\mathbf{n}=\emptyset}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}e\in E\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\beta^{\mathbf{n}_{e}}}{\mathbf{n}_{e}!},

where
\partial\mathbf{n}:=\{x\in V:~\sum_{xy\in E}\mathbf{n}_{xy}\text{ is odd}\}
is the set of
sources
of
\mathbf{n}
, and
Z^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta}
is the partition function of the model. The double random current is simply the product measure
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{G,\beta}:=\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta}
. We regard
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{G,\beta}
as a percolation model by declaring each edge
e
open in a double current configuration
(\mathbf{n},\mathbf{m})
if and only if
\mathbf{n}_{e}+\mathbf{m}_{e}>0
.

Similarly to the FK-Ising model introduced in the previous section, the connectivity properties of the double random current are related to correlations of the Ising model. However, for the double random current this relation is not provided by a coupling, but rather by a combinatorial identity known as the
switching lemma
—see Lemma
5.3
.
In particular, the point-to-point connection probability of the double random current exactly corresponds to the
square
of the two-point function of the Ising model, that is

\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{G,\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta}^{2}.

(1.29)

Once again, it is possible to construct the weak limit of the above measure as
G\nearrow\mathbb{Z}^{d}

[
ADCS15
]
, we denote it by
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}
. One may also pass (
1.29
) to the infinite volume limit: for every
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
,
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}^{2}
. This allows to prove that
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}
and the Ising model undergo a phase transition at the same parameter
\beta_{c}
. Moreover, still by
[
ADCS15
]
, the phase transition is continuous in the sense that
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\infty]=0
.

We are interested in the study of the measure
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}
in dimensions
d>4
. Partial results in dimension
d=4
are presented in Section
6
.

Theorem 1.13
.

Let
d>4
. There exist
c,C>0
such that, for every
n\geq 1
,

\frac{c}{n^{d-2}}\leq\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\frac{C}{n^{d-2}}.

(1.30)

This result may seem very surprising in view of the one-arm exponent computation performed for Bernoulli percolation and the FK-Ising model. However, it is consistent with the interpretation of the double random current percolation model as a
loop
model. Indeed, in the context of the loop percolation model introduced by Le Jan and Lemaire
[
LJL13
]
, it has been shown
[
CS16
]
that the one-arm exponent is of order
n^{2-d}
in dimensions
d>4
(see also the recent
[
Vog25
]
for an exact asymptotic estimate).

To illustrate how atypical the double random current model is compared with the percolation models above, we make the following observation. Let us start by recalling a fundamental consequence of reflection positivity: the
infrared bound
. It classically follows from
[
FSS76
,
MMS77
]
(see
[
Pan23
, Section 3]
for a detailed proof): if
d\geq 3
, there exists
C>0
such that, for every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
, and every
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus\{0\}
,

\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta}\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{d-2}}.

(1.31)

When restricting to dimensions
d>4
, the infinite volume version of (
1.29
) and (
1.31
) give that

\mathbf{E}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[|\mathbf{C}(0)|]=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}x]=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}^{2}<\infty,

(1.32)

where
\mathbf{E}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}
denotes the expectation with respect to
\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta}
, and where
\mathbf{C}(0)
denotes the cluster of the origin. The expected size of the cluster of the origin is often called the
susceptibility
of the model. In (
1.32
), we see that the sourceless double random current measure has a finite susceptibility at its critical point. This contrasts with Bernoulli percolation or the FK-Ising model in which the susceptibility always blows up at criticality. Let us mention that, in dimensions
d\in\{2,3,4\}
, it is known
[
Wu66
,
DCP25c
]
that
\mathbf{E}_{\beta_{c}}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}[|\mathbf{C}(0)|]=\infty
.

The lower bounds in Theorem
1.13
are derived using a second moment method. The upper bounds rely on a new way to explore an open path from
0
to
\partial\Lambda_{n}
in a sourceless double random current (see Lemma
6.1
).

1.3
Strategy of proof

We now describe the strategy of proof for the results of Section
1.1
. In all our proof, the following key quantity—introduced in
[
DCT16
]
—plays a fundamental role: for every
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, every
\beta\geq 0
, define

\varphi_{\beta}(S):=\beta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{S,\beta}.

(1.33)

In
[
Pan23
]
, a natural notion of correlation length associated to
\varphi_{\beta}(S)
was introduced (see also
[
Hut22
, Section 4]
for a closely related quantity). The
sharp length

L(\beta)
is defined for
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
by

L(\beta):=\inf\left\{k\geq 1:\>\exists S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d},\>0\in S,\>\textup{diam}(S)\leq 2k,\>\varphi_{\beta}(S)<\tfrac{1}{10}\right\}.

(1.34)

A crucial input for our proofs is the following upper bound on
L(\beta)
, recently derived in
[
DCP25c
]
: there exists
C_{\textup{cl}}>0
such that, for every
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
,

L(\beta)\leq C_{\textup{cl}}\begin{cases}(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2},&\text{for }d>4,\\
(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}|\log(\beta_{c}-\beta)|,&\text{for }d=4,\end{cases}

(1.35)

One-arm for FK-Ising with wired boundary conditions.

We describe here the main ideas in the proofs of Theorems
1.1
,
1.2
, and
1.4
, concerning the one-arm probability of the wired FK-Ising model. Recalling (
1.7
), we disregard the FK-Ising measure
\phi_{\Lambda_{n},\beta}^{1}
and always work directly with the Ising measures of the form
\langle\cdot\rangle^{\tau}_{\Lambda,\beta,h}
, as defined in (
1.2
).

The lower bound.
The proof of the mean-field lower bound of Theorem
1.1
is very simple. The main input is a mean-field lower bound for the
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h}
(
h>0
), which is valid in all dimensions
d\geq 2
. Precisely, it was proved in
[
ABF87
]
(see also
[
DCT16
]
) that for every
h\geq 0
small enough,

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h}\geq c_{\textup{mag}}h^{1/3},

(1.36)

where
c_{\textup{mag}}>0
.
By differentiating on the magnetic field
h_{x}
at each vertex
x\in\Lambda_{n}
and using well-known correlation inequalities, we can upper bound the contribution of the magnetic field within
\Lambda_{n}
to
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h}
by
Cn^{2}h
.
Using this fact together with (
1.36
) and taking
h=h_{n}:=(\varepsilon/n)^{3}
for
\varepsilon>0
small enough, gives

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{n}^{c}}}\geq c_{\textup{mag}}h_{n}^{1/3}-Cn^{2}h_{n}\asymp 1/n.

Since—by Markov’s property and comparison of boundary conditions—one has
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c}}^{+}\geq\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{n}^{c}}}
, this implies the desired bound.

The upper bounds.

The proofs of the upper bounds of Theorems
1.2
and
1.4
are much more involved. Our main input is again a bound for the infinite volume measure with positive magnetic field, which complements (
1.36
). Precisely, it was proved in
[
AF86
]
that

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h}\leq C_{\textup{mag}}\begin{cases}h^{1/3},&\text{for }d>4,\\
h^{1/3}|\log h|,&\text{for }d=4.\end{cases}

(1.37)

Another crucial input in the proof is the upper bound on the sharp length
L(\beta)
given above in (
1.35
).
Using these two ingredients, we establish a
renormalisation inequality
, i.e. a relation between quantities in different scales, which ultimately implies the desired upper bounds by induction.

We now explain our strategy in more details.
We focus here on the case
d>4
, the case
d=4
follows very similar lines, with parameters chosen slightly differently in order to accommodate the worse bounds (
1.35
) and (
1.37
). We prove, by induction, that the following stronger inequality holds

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{k},\beta_{c},h_{k}}^{+}\leq\frac{C_{\star}}{k},

(
\star
)

where
h_{k}:=(H/k)^{3}
for some large enough constant
H>0
.
Assuming that (

⋆

‣
1.3
) is true for
k=n/2
, we proceed to show that it also holds for
k=n
.
Precisely, we prove the aforementioned renormalisation inequality

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c},h_{n}}^{+}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n/2},\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}^{+}+\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}

(1.38)

which then implies (

⋆

‣
1.3
) at
k=n
by the induction hypothesis and the mean-field bound on the magnetisation (
1.37
).
In order to prove (
1.38
), we write

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c},h_{n}}^{+}=\underbrace{\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},\beta_{c},h_{n}}^{+}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J,h_{n/2}}^{+}}_{(\mathrm{I})}+\underbrace{\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J,h_{n/2}}^{+}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J,h_{n/2}}}_{(\mathrm{II})}+\underbrace{\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J,h_{n/2}}}_{(\mathrm{III})},

(1.39)

where
J=\beta_{n}\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{n/2}}+\beta_{c}\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{n/2}^{c}}
, with
\beta_{n}:=\beta_{c}-(B/n)^{2}
, for some constant
B>0
. Clearly
(\mathrm{III})\leq\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}
. By (
1.35
), for
B
sufficiently large we may find
S\subset\Lambda_{n/2}
such that
\varphi_{\beta}(S)\leq\tfrac{1}{10}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
(by definition of
L(\beta)
). Relying on a
Simon–Lieb type inequality
(see Lemma
2.10
), we then obtain

(\mathrm{II})\leq\varphi_{\beta}(S)\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n/2},\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}^{+}\leq\frac{1}{4}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n/2},\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}^{+}.

(1.40)

Finally, we show that
(\mathrm{I})\leq 0
, which is a more delicate task and relies itself on the induction hypothesis. Roughly speaking, the idea is that for
H
large enough (depending on
B
, which has already been fixed), we can compensate the effect of increasing the inverse temperature from
\beta_{n}
to
\beta_{c}
by the effect of decreasing the magnetic field from
h_{n/2}
to
h_{n}
. This is proved by comparing the derivatives in
J
and
h
.
In fact, it is easy to prove that the analogous quantity in infinite volume
(\mathrm{I}^{\prime}):=\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c},h_{n}}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{n},h_{n/2}}
is negative (for
H
large enough). Indeed, one can consider the linear interpolation given by

\beta(t):=\beta_{n}+t,~~~~~~h(t):=h_{n/2}-t\frac{h_{n/2}-h_{n}}{\beta_{c}-\beta_{n}},

and prove that
\partial_{t}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta(t),h(t)}\leq 0
for all
t\in[0,\beta_{c}-\beta_{n}]
. The latter follows readily from a differential inequality proved in
[
ABF87
]
(see also
[
DCT16
]
):

\partial_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta,h}\leq 2d\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta,h}\cdot\partial_{h}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta,h},

(1.41)

together with the fact that
\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta,h}\leq C_{\textup{mag}}h^{1/3}
for
\beta\leq\beta_{c}
by (
1.37
). We follow a similar strategy in order to upper bound the finite volume derivative
\partial_{t}\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J(t),h(t)}^{+}
, where
J(t)=J+t\mathds{1}_{\Lambda_{n/2}}
. However, the analogue of (
1.41

\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J(t),h(t)}^{+}
for

(see (

x\in\Lambda_{n/2}
we have
\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n},J(t),h(t)}^{+}\leq\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n/2},J(t),h(t)}^{+}\leq\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda_{n/2},\beta_{c},h_{n/2}}^{+}
and use the induction hypothesis (

One-arm for FK-Ising with free boundary conditions.

We now describe the main ideas in the proofs of Theorems
1.7
and
1.8
.
In both cases, the most interesting part of the analysis is in the derivation of the upper bound (the lower bounds are less sensitive to
d\geq 6
and are obtained using a second moment method which relies on both
[
CJN21
,
DCP25c
]
).

Again, the proof goes through the derivation of a renormalisation inequality on
u_{n}(\beta):=\phi_{\beta}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
. Our input is very different from the wired case. The main idea is inspired from the entropic method introduced by Dewan and Muirhead
[
DM23
]
in the context of Bernoulli percolation. Let us briefly describe it. We want to compare
u_{n}(\beta_{c})
to
u_{n/2}(\beta_{c})
. As in
[
DM23
]
, if we allow to work at a slightly subcritical and “well-tuned” parameter
\beta_{n}
, then we can obtain the “contracting estimate”

u_{n}(\beta_{n})\leq\frac{1}{10}u_{n/2}(\beta_{c})

(1.42)

The challenge in
[
DM23
]
is to tune
\beta_{c}-\beta_{n}

as small as possible while still guaranteeing such a contraction bound. There, the authors rely on a “classical” notion of correlation length: the inverse of the exponential decay rate of the two-point function. However, this choice is not optimal and is responsible for the emergence of logarithmic divergences in their argument (see
[
EGPS25
]
for an explanation). Here, we rely on the more geometric notion of correlation length given by the sharp length
L(\beta)
defined in (
1.34
).

We use (
1.35
) to obtain that—for
\beta_{n}:=\beta_{c}-\frac{A}{n^{2}}
(with
A
large enough)—there exists
S\subset\Lambda_{n/2}
with
\varphi_{\beta_{n}}(S)\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
. As in (
1.40
), we rely on a Simon–Lieb type inequality (see Proposition
2.13
) to conclude that
u_{n}(\beta_{n})\leq\varphi_{\beta_{n}}(S)u_{n/2}(\beta_{c})\leq\tfrac{1}{10}u_{n/2}(\beta_{c})
. Let us mention that Proposition
2.13
can also be seen as a form of
van den Berg–Kesten-type inequality
for the FK-Ising model. We believe this result is interesting on its own and may be relevant in the future to study the FK-Ising model.

Once such a contracting bound is obtained, we need to estimate the difference
u_{n}(\beta_{c})-u_{n}(\beta_{n})
. The key idea of Dewan and Muirhead is to formulate a bound on this quantity in terms of the relative entropy between the law of the cluster of the origin at
\beta_{c}
and at
\beta_{n}
. This is what they call the
entropic bound
, see
[
DM23
, Proposition 1.21]
. In the context of Bernoulli percolation, this bound turns out to be an easy consequence of Pinsker’s inequality.

In this paper, we derive the new analogue of their entropic bound for the FK-Ising model, see Theorem
4.1
. However, in our case, an additional quantity emerges in the bound: the so-called
triangle diagram
defined by

\nabla(\beta_{c}):=\sum_{y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}.

(1.43)

It is a consequence of
[
DCP25c
]
(see Section
2
) that this quantity is finite if and only if
d>6
. The presence of this infinite quantity in
d<6
makes our entropic bound inefficient in these dimensions, and provides a small justification of why the one-arm exponent changes around
d=6
.

When
d>6
, the combination of the steps above (i.e. the contraction inequality and the entropic bound) leads to the following renormalisation inequality: there exists
K\geq 1
such that for every
n\geq 1
,

u_{n}(\beta_{c})\leq\frac{1}{10}u_{n/2}(\beta_{c})+\frac{K}{n}\sqrt{u_{n}(\beta_{c})}.

(1.44)

This result is sufficient to conclude the desired upper bound of order
n^{-2}
. When
d=6
, we conclude similarly by approximating the law of the
\phi_{\beta_{c}}
in
\Lambda_{n}
with that of
\phi_{\Lambda_{n^{\alpha}},\beta_{c}}
(with a suitable exponent
\alpha>1
), and by using the fact the corresponding truncated triangle diagram
\nabla_{\Lambda_{n^{\alpha}}}(\beta_{c})
only diverges logarithmically in
n
.

Remark 1.14
.

The use of the sharp length instead of the standard correlation length in the argument of

]
lead us to revisit the corresponding result for high-dimensional Bernoulli percolation in our companion paper

Let us now say a word about the proofs of our results in dimensions
d<6
. In Theorems
1.10
and

, a natural route is to use the Paley–Zygmund inequality and write

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]=\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}>0]\geq\frac{\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}]^{2}}{\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}^{2}]},

(1.45)

where

for the FK-Ising model (see

\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}>0]\geq\frac{\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}]^{3/2}}{\phi_{\beta_{c}}[\mathcal{Z}^{3}]^{1/2}}.

Finally, in Theorem

n^{-2-o(1)}\lesssim\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{2n\mathbf{e}_{1}}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\lesssim\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\phi^{1}_{\Lambda_{n}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\lesssim\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]n^{-1+o(1)}.

Remark 2.1

We make the following useful observations regarding (

(i)

In (

(ii)

First, we may define

\sigma_{A}:=\prod_{x\in A}\sigma_{x}

Proposition 2.2

(Griffiths’ inequality
[
Gri67a
,
KS68
]
)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}

, every
\mathsf{h}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{V}

\langle\sigma_{A};\sigma_{B}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}:=\langle\sigma_{A}\sigma_{B}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}-\langle\sigma_{A}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}\langle\sigma_{B}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}\geq 0.

(2.1)

The next result is a classical consequence of Proposition
2.2

FV17

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
G=(V,E)
,
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})

\mathbb{Z}^{d}
such that

\langle\sigma_{A}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}\leq\langle\sigma_{A}\rangle_{H,J^{\prime},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}.

(2.2)

Moreover, if we further assume that the restriction of

\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle^{+}_{H,J^{\prime}}\leq\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,J}^{+}.

Lemma 2.4

Let

(2.4)

\partial_{J_{uv}}\langle\sigma_{A}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}=\langle\sigma_{A};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,J,\mathsf{h}}.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let

-\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{G,J}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,J}

2.2
Properties of the FK-Ising model

We begin by recalling two very classical properties of the FK-Ising model and refer to
[
Gri06
,
DC19
]
for proofs.

Proposition 2.11

(Domain Markov property)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\beta\geq 0
. Let
G=(V,E),H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be subgraphs of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
G\subset H
. Let
\xi\in\{0,1\}^{E^{\prime}\setminus E}
. Then, for every event
\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,1\}^{E}
, one has

\phi_{H,\beta}^{0}[\mathcal{A}\>|\>\omega_{|E^{\prime}\setminus E}=\xi]=\phi_{G,\beta}^{\xi}[\mathcal{A}].

(2.20)

Let
E\subset E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})
. A set
\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,1\}^{E}
is said to be
increasing
if the following holds: for every
\omega,\omega^{\prime}\in\{0,1\}^{E}
, if
\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}
(in the sense that
\omega_{e}\leq\omega_{e}^{\prime}
for every
e\in E
) and
\omega\in\mathcal{A}
, then
\omega^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Proposition 2.12

(Stochastic domination)
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
0\leq\beta^{\prime}\leq\beta
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then, for every
\xi\in\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})\setminus E}
, and for every increasing event
\mathcal{A}\subset\{0,1\}^{E}
, the following inequalities hold:

\phi_{G,\beta^{\prime}}^{\xi}[\mathcal{A}]\leq\phi^{1}_{G,\beta}[\mathcal{A}],

(2.21)

and

\phi_{G,\beta^{\prime}}^{0}[\mathcal{A}]\leq\phi_{\beta}[\mathcal{A}].

(2.22)

We now present two new inequalities for the FK-Ising model. Their proofs heavily rely on the random current representation and are postponed to Section
5.4
. These inequalities are a well-established part of the mathematical study of Bernoulli percolation, where they follow from either independence or the van den Berg–Kesten (BK) inequality (see
[
Gri99
]
). These two properties are not available at the level of the FK-Ising model. Nevertheless, building on a coupling of the FK-Ising model with random currents (see Section
5.4
), we manage to obtain the following useful results. The first inequality can be seen as the FK-Ising counterpart of Lemma
2.10
, and as a generalisation of the Simon–Lieb inequality
[
Sim80
,
Lie04
]
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\beta>0
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
0\in V
. Let
S\cap B=\emptyset
be finite subsets of
V
, with

2.3

We now turn to results regarding the high-dimensional Ising model.
We start by collecting useful bounds on the two-point function of the model at criticality.

instead of
p
. Applying the chain rule twice, we obtain

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)=\frac{1}{2(1-p)^{2}}\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)+\frac{1}{4(1-p)^{2}}\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial^{2}\beta}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg),

(4.19)

where we used the relation
p=1-e^{-2\beta}
to get

\Big(\frac{\partial\beta}{\partial p}\Big)^{2}=\frac{1}{4(1-p)^{2}},\qquad\frac{\partial^{2}\beta}{\partial p^{2}}=\frac{1}{2(1-p)^{2}}.

(4.20)

Putting (
4.18
) and (
4.19
) in (
4.17
), and using again that
1-e^{-\beta_{c}}\leq p\leq p_{c}
, yields the existence of
K_{2}=K_{2}(d)>0
such that

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}F_{\Lambda}(p^{\prime},p)\leq K_{2}\sum_{C\owns 0}\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}(C)\bigg(|\overline{C}|-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)\bigg)

(4.21)

We are left to bound the terms involving the derivatives in
\beta
in (
4.21
). We will show that there exist constants
K_{3},K_{4}
depending only on
d
such that:

-\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)\bigg]\leq K_{3}B_{\Lambda}(\beta)\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\big[|\overline{\mathcal{C}_{e}(0)}|\big]

(4.22)

and

-\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\bigg[\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)\bigg]\leq K_{4}\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta)\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\big[|\overline{\mathcal{C}_{e}(0)}|\big].

(4.23)

Before moving to the proof of (
4.22
) and (
4.23
), let us quickly show how they allow to conclude the proof. Plugging (
4.22
) and (
4.23
) and the inequality
B_{\Lambda}(\beta)\leq\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta)\leq\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta_{c})
in (
4.21
) yields

\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial p^{2}}F_{\Lambda}(p^{\prime},p)\leq 4d\Big(K_{2}+(K_{2}K_{3}+K_{2}K_{4})\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta_{c})\Big)\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\big[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\big]\leq K_{5}\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta_{c})\phi_{\Lambda,p^{\prime}}\big[|\mathcal{C}(0)|\big],

(4.24)

for some
K_{5}=K_{5}(d)>0
, where we used that
|\overline{\mathcal{C}_{e}(0)}|\leq 2\cdot 2d\cdot|\mathcal{C}(0)|
. This is exactly the statement of Proposition
4.3
.

Proof of (
4.22
).

Fix a set
0\in C\subset E(\Lambda)
. We start with the first derivative. Below, we abuse notations and also view
\overline{C}
as a vertex set made of the endpoints of all the elements of
\overline{C}
. A standard computation gives

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial}{\partial\beta}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)=\sum_{xy\in E(\Lambda)}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{xy\in E(\Lambda)}\sum_{z\in\partial\overline{C}}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in\overline{C}}\sum_{xy\in E(\Lambda)}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq K_{6}\sum_{z\in\overline{C}}\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2K_{6}|\overline{C}|B_{\Lambda}(\beta),

(4.25)

where in the first line we used Lemma
2.9
, in the second line we used (
2.2
), in the third line we used Lemma
2.8
and
K_{6}=K_{6}(d)>0
, and in the last line we used the definition of
B_{\Lambda}(\beta)
. Taking the expectation with respect to
C
shows (
4.22
).

Proof of (
4.23
).

The second derivative is more delicate.
A similar computation as above shows that

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)=\sum_{xy,zw\in E(\Lambda)}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}.

Introduce the interaction
J(t)
(on
\Lambda
) defined for
t\in[0,1]
by:

J_{e}(t)=\begin{cases}\beta&e\in E(\Lambda)\setminus\overline{C}\\
t\beta&e\in\overline{C}\end{cases},

(4.26)

and let
\langle\cdot\rangle_{t}:=\langle\cdot\rangle_{\Lambda,J(t)}
be the induced Ising measure.
Writing

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t}=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{t}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t}-\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{t}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t},

(4.27)

we find, by the fundamental theorem of calculus (and Remark
2.6
) that

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w}\rangle_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}=\beta\int_{0}^{1}\sum_{uv\in\overline{C}}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t}\mathrm{d}t.

(4.28)

Combining Lemmas
2.7
and
2.8
shows that there exists some constant
K_{7}=K_{7}(d)>0
such that

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y};\sigma_{z}\sigma_{w};\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{t}\leq K_{7}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{1}\langle\sigma_{w}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{1}\langle\sigma_{v}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{1}=K_{7}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{w}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{v}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}

(4.29)

Combining the last two displayed equations,

-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial\beta^{2}}\log\bigg(\frac{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{C},\beta}}{\mathbf{Z}^{\textup{Ising}}_{\Lambda,\beta}}\bigg)\leq K_{7}\beta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy,zw\in E(\Lambda)\\
uv\in\overline{C}\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}\langle\sigma_{w}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}\langle\sigma_{v}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta}\leq K_{8}|\overline{C}|\nabla_{\Lambda}(\beta),

(4.30)

where the second inequality follows again from Lemma
2.8
, and where
K_{8}=K_{8}(d)>0
. Averaging over
C
concludes the proof of (
4.23
).

\Box

4.2
Upper bound in dimensions
d>6

In this subsection, we prove the upper bound in Theorem
1.7
. The proof will follow from the following simple renormalisation inequality.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
d>6
. There exist
K>0
and
k_{0}\geq 1
such that the following holds. For every
k\geq k_{0}
, letting
n=2^{k}
and
u_{n}:=\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
, one has

u_{n}\leq\frac{1}{10}u_{n/2}+\frac{K}{n}\sqrt{u_{n}}.

(4.31)

Before proving this result, we show how it implies the upper bound in Theorem
1.7
.

Proof of the upper bound in Theorem
1.7
.

We let
K,k_{0}>0
be given by Lemma
4.4
, and let
u_{n}=\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]
. Observe that it suffices to show that there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that for every
k\geq 0
,

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(2^{k})^{2}}.

(4.32)

Indeed, if (
4.32
) holds, then for every
n\geq 1
, if
k\geq 0
is such that
2^{k}\leq n<2^{k+1}
, then

u_{n}\leq u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(2^{k})^{2}}\leq\frac{4C_{0}}{n^{2}}.

(4.33)

Set
C_{0}:=4K^{2}\vee(2^{k_{0}})^{2}
. We now prove (
4.32
) by induction on
k\geq 0
. Since
u_{2^{k}}\leq 1
and
C_{0}\geq(2^{k_{0}})^{2}
, (
4.32
) holds with
k\leq k_{0}
. Let
k\geq k_{0}
and assume that it holds with
k-1
. By Lemma
4.4
, one has

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{1}{10}u_{2^{k-1}}+\frac{K}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}}.

(4.34)

Applying the induction hypothesis gives

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{(2C_{0}/5)}{(2^{k})^{2}}+\frac{K}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}}.

(4.35)

We now take cases: if
\frac{K}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}}>\frac{(2C_{0}/5)}{(2^{k})^{2}}
, then (
4.35
) rewrites

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{2K}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}},

(4.36)

which implies that

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{4K^{2}}{(2^{k})^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(2^{k})^{2}},

(4.37)

by our choice of
C_{0}
. If not, then (
4.35
) rewrites

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{(4C_{0}/5)}{(2^{k})^{2}}\leq\frac{C_{0}}{(2^{k})^{2}}.

(4.38)

In either case, we obtain the desired result. This concludes the proof.

\Box

We now turn to the proof of Lemma
4.4
. We rely on the entropic bound obtained in Theorem
4.1
,
together with Lemma
2.15
as in the wired case: if
d>4
, there exists
C_{1}=C_{1}(d)>0
such that the following holds: for every
\beta<\beta_{c}
, and every
n\geq C_{1}(\beta_{c}-\beta)^{-1/2}

S\subset\Lambda_{n/2}
which satisfies

\varphi_{\beta}(S)\leq\frac{1}{10}.

(4.39)

Proof of Lemma
4.4
.

Let
k\geq 1
to be taken large enough. Set
n=2^{k}
, and let
\beta^{\prime}=\beta^{\prime}(n)=\beta_{c}-\frac{A}{n^{2}}
for some
A>0
to be chosen large enough. Let
C_{1}
be given by (
4.39
) above. Fix
A
large enough so that

C_{1}(\beta_{c}-\beta^{\prime})^{-1/2}=\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{A}}n<n-1.

(4.40)

and then take
k\geq k_{0}
, where
k_{0}
is such that

\beta_{c}-\frac{A}{(2^{k_{0}})^{2}}\geq\frac{\beta_{c}}{2}.

(4.41)

By (

), there exists

such that
\varphi_{\beta^{\prime}}(S)\leq\tfrac{1}{10}
. An application of the BK-type inequality of Proposition

with

\phi_{\beta^{\prime}}[0\xleftrightarrow{}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\

(4.42)

where in the last inequality we used the defining property of

stochastically dominates

4.3
Upper bound in dimension

The proof of Theorem
1.8
(

1.7
(

).
The main difference is that the triangle condition no longer holds: by (

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

. There exist

and

Before proving this result, let us argue how Theorem
1.8

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Let
K,k_{0}
be given by Lemma
4.5
and assume without loss of generality that
k_{0}\geq 3
.
Write
u_{n}=\phi_{\beta_{c}}[0\leftrightarrow\partial\Lambda_{n}]
.
It suffices to prove that there is some constant
C_{0}<\infty
such that for all

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{C_{0}k}{(2^{k})^{2}}.

(4.46)

Set
C_{0}:=4K^{2}\vee(2^{k_{0}})^{2}
.
We prove (
4.46
) by induction on
k\geq 1
.
Since
u_{k}\leq 1

4.46

.
Now, fix

4.46

Applying Lemma

(4.47)

We now take cases: suppose that

\frac{2C_{0}}{5}\frac{k}{(2^{k})^{2}}+\frac{K}{(2^{k})^{3}}<\frac{K\sqrt{k}}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}}.

(4.48)

Then, (
4.47
) rewrites as

u_{2^{k}}\leq\frac{2K\sqrt{k}}{2^{k}}\sqrt{u_{2^{k}}},

(4.49)

(4.50)

, and that

since

, which concludes the induction.

\Box

4.6.1
The case
d=5

We would like to obtain Theorem
1.10
using a second moment method involving the random variable
\mathcal{Z}
defined in (
4.66
). The main problem is that our only tool to bound the second moment of
\mathcal{Z}
is the tree-graph inequality of Proposition
2.14
. This bound is expected to be sharp (up to a multiplicative constant) in the mean-field regime
d>6
. If we believe that
d_{c}^{\textup{FK-Ising}}=6
, it will not give a sharp bound in dimension
d=5
. Indeed, as observed in Proposition
4.7
, the use of the tree-graph inequality only gives a lower bound on the one-arm probability of order
n^{-2}
. A similar phenomenon holds for Bernoulli percolation. There, a sharp control on the three-point function can be achieved using Gladkov’s inequality
[
Gla24
]
. More precisely, Gladkov proved that for every
x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and any
p\in[0,1]
,

\mathbb{P}_{p}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z]^{2}\leq 8\mathbb{P}_{p}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y]\mathbb{P}_{p}[y\xleftrightarrow{}z]\mathbb{P}_{p}[z\xleftrightarrow{}x].

(4.79)

His proof relies on subtle exploration algorithms as well as on the BK inequality. This makes its extension to the FK-Ising model not straightforward. However, it is reasonable to expect that a version of (
4.79
) also holds in this context. Let us mention that (
4.79
) has been efficiently used (and generalised) in the context of long-range percolation below the upper-critical dimension
[
Hut25b
]
.

We will not prove a version of (
4.79
) for the FK-Ising model, but rather observe that well-known correlation inequalities available at the level of the spin model provide a control on the four-point function which we conjecture to be sharp (up to a multiplicative constant) in dimension
d<6
. If
x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we let
\{x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z,t\}
denote the event that
x,y,z,t
are in the same cluster.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then, for every
x,y,z,t\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, one has

\phi_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z,t]\leq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}+\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}+\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta}.

(4.80)

Proof.

Set
A=\{x,y,z,t\}
. Observe that

\phi_{\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z,t]\leq\phi_{\beta}[\mathcal{F}_{A}],

(4.81)

where
\mathcal{F}_{A}
is the percolation event defined as follows:

\mathcal{F}_{A}:=\{\omega\in\{0,1\}^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}:\textup{ Every cluster of $\omega$ intersects $A$ an even number of times}\}.

(4.82)

By the Edwards–Sokal coupling (see
[
DC19
, (1.7)]
), one has

\phi_{\beta}[\mathcal{F}_{A}]=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}.

(4.83)

Finally, using Lebowitz’ inequality
[
Leb74
]
, we get that

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}\leq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}+\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}+\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\rangle_{\beta}.

(4.84)

Plugging this observation in (
4.83
) and then using (
4.81
) concludes the proof.

\Box

Remark 4.12
.

Let us briefly explain why it is reasonable to expect this inequality to be sharp (up to a multiplicative constant) at criticality in dimensions
d<6
. We used two inequalities in the proof of Proposition

. The first one is (

). Building on the discussion below Theorem

are paired and at mutual distance

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\lesssim\max_{u,v\in\{x,y,z,t\}:u\neq v}\langle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}^{2}\lesssim\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{n\mathbf{e}_{1}}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}^{2}.

(4.85)

Thanks to Proposition
2.2
, one has
\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\geq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\langle\sigma_{z}\sigma_{t}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}\gtrsim\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{n\mathbf{e}_{1}}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}^{2}

5.1
Random currents

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph. We let
\mathfrak{g}
denote the
ghost
vertex and define
G_{\mathfrak{g}}
to be the graph of vertex set
V_{\mathfrak{g}}:=V\cup\{\mathfrak{g}\}
and edge set
E_{\mathfrak{g}}:=E\cup\{x\mathfrak{g}:x\in V\}
.

Definition 5.1
.

A
current

\mathbf{n}
on
G_{\mathfrak{g}}
is a function defined on the edge set
E_{\mathfrak{g}}
and taking its values in
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,\ldots\}
. We let
\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}
be the set of currents on
G_{\mathfrak{g}}
. The set of
sources
of
\mathbf{n}
, denoted by
\partial\mathbf{n}
, is defined as

\partial\mathbf{n}:=\Big\{x\in V_{\mathfrak{g}}\>:\>\sum_{y\in V_{\mathfrak{g}}:\>xy\in E_{\mathfrak{g}}}\mathbf{n}_{xy}\textup{ is odd}\Big\}.

(5.1)

We also define, for every
\beta\geq 0
and
\mathsf{h}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{V}
,

w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{G}(\mathbf{n}):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\beta^{\mathbf{n}_{xy}}}{\mathbf{n}_{xy}!}\prod_{x\in V}\frac{\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}}}{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}!}.

(5.2)

If
\mathsf{h}=0
, we simply write
w_{\beta}^{G}(\mathbf{n})=w_{\beta,0}^{G}(\mathbf{n})
. Similarly, we define
\Omega_{G}
, the set of currents on
G
.

It is classical (see
[
Aiz82
,
DC19
]
) that the correlation functions of the Ising model are related to currents: if we set
\sigma_{\mathfrak{g}}=1
, then, for every
A\subset V_{\mathfrak{g}}
, one has,

\sum_{\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{V}}\sigma_{A}\exp\Big(\beta\sum_{xy\in E}\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}+\sum_{x\in V}\mathsf{h}_{x}\sigma_{x}\Big)=2^{|V|}\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{\mathfrak{g}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}=A}w^{G}_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\mathbf{n}),

(5.3)

so that

\left\langle\sigma_{A}\right\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}=\dfrac{\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}=A}w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{G}(\mathbf{n})}{\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{g}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}=\emptyset}w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{G}(\mathbf{n})}.

(5.4)

The trace of a current
\mathbf{n}
naturally induces a percolation configuration
(\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}_{xy}>0})_{xy\in E_{\mathfrak{g}}}
on
G_{\mathfrak{g}}
. A current
\mathbf{n}
with source set
\{x,y\}
induces a percolation configuration which is described by a path between
x
and
y
together with a collection of loops, see Figure
2
for an illustration.
The connectivity properties of this percolation model play a determinant role in the analysis of the Ising model. This justifies the following definitions.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
x,y\in V_{\mathfrak{g}}
.

(i)

We say that
x
is
connected
to
y
in
\mathbf{n}
and write
x\overset{\mathbf{n}}{\longleftrightarrow}y
, if there is a sequence of points
x_{0}=x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{\ell}=y
such that
\mathbf{n}_{x_{i}x_{i+1}}>0
for
0\leq i\leq\ell-1
.

(ii)

The
cluster
of
x
in
\mathbf{n}
, denoted by
\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{n}}(x)
, is the set of points connected to
x
in
\mathbf{n}
.

At this stage, the above definitions lack a probabilistic interpretation. In particular, equation (
5.4
) does not admit one, since the sums in the numerator and denominator range over distinct sets. The true advantage of the current expansion emerges through a simple yet powerful combinatorial tool: the
switching lemma
. This identity was first derived in
[
GHS70
]
, but its deep connections to percolation theory flourished in the seminal work of Aizenman
[
Aiz82

Lemma 5.3

(Switching lemma)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
and
H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be two finite graphs such that
G\subset H
. Let
\beta\geq 0
, and
\mathsf{h}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{V^{\prime}}
. For every
A\subset V
,
B\subset V^{\prime}
, and every
F:\Omega_{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, one has

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}\in\Omega_{G}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=A\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{H}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=B\end{subarray}}F(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{G}(\mathbf{n}_{1})w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{H}(\mathbf{n}_{2})\\
=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}\in\Omega_{G}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\emptyset\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{H}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=B\Delta A\end{subarray}}F(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{G}(\mathbf{n}_{1})w_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}^{H}(\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathds{1}_{(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})_{|G}\in\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}},

(5.5)

where

is the symmetric difference of sets, and
\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}
is given by

\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}=\{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}:\>\exists\mathbf{m}\leq\mathbf{n}\>,\>\partial\mathbf{m}=A\}.

(5.6)

Remark 5.4
.

Observe that the event
\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}
introduced in (

) is a percolation event. Indeed, it is exactly the event that each connected component of the percolation configuration induced by
\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}
intersects
A
an even number of times. In particular, if
A=\{x,y\}
then
\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}:=\{x\xleftrightarrow{\mathbf{n}\>}y\}
.

Before presenting applications of Lemma
5.3
, we introduce the random current measures of interest. Let
G=(V,E)
,

, and

on

,

(5.7)

where

\mathbf{P}^{A,B}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}=\mathbf{P}^{A,B}_{G,G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}

\mathsf{h}=0

in the above notations.

Focusing on the case where

In particular, taking

5.2
Backbone expansion

As mentioned above, if
\mathbf{n}
is a current with sources
\partial\mathbf{n}=\{x,y\}
, then
x
and
y
are connected in
\mathbf{n}
. The
backbone
expansion of the Ising model—introduced in
[
Aiz82
]
—provides a way to explore one such path. We refer to
[
Pan24a
, Chapter 2]
for a more detailed introduction.

We fix
G=(V,E)
a finite graph and
\prec
an arbitrary ordering of
E
. We construct an ordering of
E_{\mathfrak{g}}
from
\prec
—that we still denote
\prec
—by requiring that for each
x,y\in V
with
y\sim x
, one has
xy\prec x\mathfrak{g}
. In words, the ordering prioritises the edges which are not going to
\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 5.6

(Backbone exploration)
.

Let
x,y\in V_{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}}
with
\partial\mathbf{n}=\{x,y\}
. The backbone of
\mathbf{n}
, denoted by
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
, is the unique oriented and edge self-avoiding path from
x
to
y
, supported on edges
uv
with
\mathbf{n}_{uv}
odd, and which is minimal for
\prec
. The backbone
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\{x_{i}x_{i+1}:0\leq i<k\}
is obtained via the following procedure:

(i)

Set
x_{0}=x
. The first edge
x_{0}x_{1}
of
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
is the earliest of all the edges
e
emerging from
x_{0}
for which
\mathbf{n}_{e}
is odd.
All edges
e
containing
x_{0}
and satisfying
e\prec x_{0}x_{1}
and
e\neq x_{0}x_{1}
are called
explored
and have
\mathbf{n}_{e}
even.

(ii)

Each edge
x_{i}x_{i+1}
with
i\geq 1
is the earliest of all edges
e
emerging from
x_{i}
that have not been explored previously, and for which
\mathbf{n}_{e}
is odd.

(iii)

The exploration stops when it reaches
y=x_{k}
for the first time.

Let
\overline{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n})
denote the set of explored edges, i.e.
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
together with all explored even edges.

Figure 2:
An illustration of the trace of a current
\mathbf{n}
with
\partial\mathbf{n}=\{x,y\}
. The bold line represents the backbone
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
of
\mathbf{n}
.

By definition, the set
\overline{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n})
is determined by
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
and
\prec
. Similarly,
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
can be deduced from
\overline{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n})
by choosing the odd edges according to
\prec
. The current
\mathbf{n}\setminus\overline{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n})
, which we define to be the restriction of
\mathbf{n}
to the complement of
\overline{\Gamma}(\mathbf{n})
, is sourceless. Finally, if
\beta\geq 0
and
\mathsf{h}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{V}
, we can write

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}=\sum_{\gamma:x\rightarrow y}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma),

(5.13)

where for a path
\gamma
with endpoints
\partial\gamma
,

\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)=\langle\sigma_{\partial\gamma}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}\mathbf{P}^{\partial\gamma}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma].

(5.14)

When
\mathsf{h}=0
, we write
\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma)=\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,0}(\gamma)
.

We now collect various useful properties of the backbone expansion. For proofs, we refer to
[
AF86
]
or
[
Pan24a
, Chapter 2]
. Recall that we fix
G=(V,E)
a finite graph. A path
\gamma
(viewed as a sequence of edges) on
G_{\mathfrak{g}}
is said to be
consistent
if no edge of the sequence uses an edge cancelled by a previous step.

\bullet

If
\gamma
is a consistent path and
\mathsf{E}
is a subset of
E_{\mathfrak{g}}
satisfying
\gamma\cap\mathsf{E}=\emptyset
, then

\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\leq\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus\mathsf{E},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma),

(5.15)

where we abused notations and wrote
G_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus\mathsf{E}
for the graph of vertex set
V_{\mathfrak{g}}
and edge set
E_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus\mathsf{E}
.

\bullet

If
\gamma
is a consistent path that is the concatenation of two consistent paths
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
(we write
\gamma=\gamma_{1}\circ\gamma_{2})
, then

\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)=\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1})\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus\overline{\gamma_{1}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{2}).

(5.16)

\bullet

If
\gamma\subset G
is a consistent path,

\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\leq\beta^{|\gamma|},

(5.17)

where
|\gamma|
is the number of edges visited by
\gamma
.

\bullet

(Chain rule) Let
x,y,u\in V
. Then,

\sum_{\gamma:x\rightarrow y}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{u\in\gamma}\leq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}\langle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}.

(5.18)

Again,
[
ADCS15
]
allows to define infinite volume versions of the above. If
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we let
\rho_{\beta,\mathsf{h}}=\rho_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}
.

We first state a very simple lemma. We will need the following notation: if
G=(V,E)
is a graph,
A\subset V
, and
\mathcal{E}\subset\Omega_{G}
, then

Z^{A}_{G,\beta}[\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}=A}w^{G}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n})\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}\in\mathcal{E}}.

(5.19)

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph and
\beta\geq 0
. Let
x,y\in V
and
\gamma:x\rightarrow y
such that
\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma)>0
. Then, if
\mathcal{E}
is measurable in terms of
\mathbf{n}_{|\overline{\gamma}^{c}}
,

Z^{xy}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma,\mathcal{E}]=Z^{xy}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma]\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}[\mathcal{E}].

(5.20)

Proof.

Write
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}
where
\mathbf{n}_{1}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}}
and
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}
. Observe that
w^{G}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n})=w^{\overline{\gamma}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{1})w^{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{2})
. Now, one has

\displaystyle Z^{xy}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma,\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{x,y\}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\emptyset\end{subarray}}w^{\overline{\gamma}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{1})\mathds{1}_{\Gamma(\mathbf{n}_{1})=\gamma}w^{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\mathcal{E}}

\displaystyle=Z^{xy}_{\overline{\gamma},\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma]Z^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}[\mathcal{E}]

\displaystyle=Z^{xy}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma]\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}[\mathcal{E}],

(5.21)

where in the first line we used that the event
\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\}
is measurable in terms of
\mathbf{n}_{1}
, and in the last line we used that
Z^{xy}_{\overline{\gamma},\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma]Z^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}=Z^{xy}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma]
. This concludes the proof.

\Box

We now turn to a consequence of (
5.15
). The main idea of the proof is to observe that increasing the external magnetic field can be viewed as enlarging the base graph.

Lemma 5.8
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph and
\beta\geq 0
. If
\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{V}
satisfy
\mathsf{h}\leq\mathsf{h}^{\prime}
and
v\in V
, then

\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}-\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}\leq\sum_{\gamma:0\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}}\rho_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap\{x\mathfrak{g}:\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}\neq\emptyset}.

(5.22)

Proof.

Let us define a new graph
G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=(V_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},E_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime})
as follows:
G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
is the graph of vertex set
V_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=V_{\mathfrak{g}}
and of edge set
E_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}=E_{\mathfrak{g}}\cup E_{0}
, where
E_{0}
is a copy of
E_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus E
. Note that
G_{\mathfrak{g}}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
. At this stage, it is useful to explain how the elements of
E_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
are ordered. We make the following choice: we extend the ordering
\prec
on
E_{\mathfrak{g}}
to
E_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
by requiring that for every
e\in E_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus E
and its copy
e^{\prime}\in E_{0}
, one has
e\prec e^{\prime}
. This means that when the edges coming from
x\in V
are explored, the one lying in
E_{0}
is explored last. Now, observe that

\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}=\frac{\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}:\partial\mathbf{n}=\{v,\mathfrak{g}\}}w^{G}_{\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\mathbf{n})}{\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}:\partial\mathbf{n}=\emptyset}w^{G}_{\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\mathbf{n})},

(5.23)

where

w^{G}_{\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\mathbf{n})=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}xy\in E\end{subarray}}\dfrac{\beta^{\mathbf{n}_{xy}}}{\mathbf{n}_{xy}!}\prod_{e=x\mathfrak{g}\in E_{\mathfrak{g}}\setminus E}\frac{\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}}}{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}!}\prod_{e=x\mathfrak{g}\in E_{0}}\frac{(\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}_{x})^{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}}}{\mathbf{n}_{x\mathfrak{g}}!}.

(5.24)

Additionally, let us write for
A\subset V_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
,
Z^{A}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}=\sum_{\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}=A}w^{G}_{\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\mathbf{n})
.
From these weights, we can can construct a backbone expansion of
\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}
on
G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
which involves weights that we denote by
\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}
. Namely,

\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}=\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma).

(5.25)

Now, by (
5.15
) (which adapts to this slightly more general setting), if
\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}
(i.e.
\gamma\cap E_{0}=\emptyset
) is a consistent path,

\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\geq\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma).

(5.26)

With this result in hand, we write

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}-\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}\Big(\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}-\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}(\gamma)-\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap E_{0}=\emptyset}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}\Big(\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)-\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap E_{0}=\emptyset}\Big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap E_{0}\neq\emptyset}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma),

(5.27)

where in the second line we used (
5.26
).
To conclude the proof, it remains to observe that

\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}}\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap E_{0}\neq\emptyset}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime},\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\leq\sum_{\gamma:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}}\rho_{G_{\mathfrak{g}},\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{\gamma\cap\{x\mathfrak{g}:\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}\neq\emptyset}.

(5.28)

For this, observe that
Z^{\emptyset}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}=Z_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}^{\emptyset}
, so that the above inequality is equivalent to

Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})\cap E_{0}\neq\emptyset]\leq Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})\cap\{x\mathfrak{g}:\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}\neq\emptyset].

(5.29)

To obtain the above, introduce the natural projection
\pi:\gamma\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}\mapsto\pi(\gamma)\subset G_{\mathfrak{g}}
, and
E_{0}^{\prime}:=\{e=x\mathfrak{g}\in E_{0}:\mathsf{h}^{\prime}_{x}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}
. Then, one has

Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})\cap E_{0}\neq\emptyset]=Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})\cap E_{0}^{\prime}\neq\emptyset]\\
\leq Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h},\mathsf{h}^{\prime}-\mathsf{h}}[\pi(\Gamma(\mathbf{n}))\cap\{x\mathfrak{g}:\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}\neq\emptyset]=Z^{v\mathfrak{g}}_{G,\beta,\mathsf{h}^{\prime}}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})\cap\{x\mathfrak{g}:\mathsf{h}_{x}^{\prime}>\mathsf{h}_{x}\}\neq\emptyset],

(5.30)

from which the proof follows readily.

5.3

In this section, we prove Lemmas
2.9
and
2.10
. To ease the reading, we restate these results below. We begin with the proof of Lemma
2.9
.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
G=(V,E)\subset H=(V^{\prime},E^{\prime})
be two subgraphs of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. For every
\beta\geq 0
, and every
x,y\in V^{\prime}
,

\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta}\leq\sum_{u\in\partial V}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{G,\beta}\langle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta},

(5.31)

where
{\partial}V:=\{x\in V:\exists x^{\prime}\in V^{\prime}\setminus V\text{ with }xx^{\prime}\in E^{\prime}\}
.

Proof.

Using the switching lemma as in (
5.11
) gives

\displaystyle\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta}-\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta}

\displaystyle=\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta}\mathbf{P}^{xy,\emptyset}_{H,G,\beta}[\{x\xleftrightarrow{(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})_{|G}\>}y\}^{c}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{u\in\partial V}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta}\mathbf{P}^{xy,\emptyset}_{H,G,\beta}[x\xleftrightarrow{(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})_{|G}\>}u]

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\partial V}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{G,\beta}\langle\sigma_{u}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{H,\beta},

(5.32)

where in the second line we used the fact that

\{x\xleftrightarrow{(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})_{|G}\>}y\}^{c}\subset\bigcup_{u\in\partial V}\{x\xleftrightarrow{(\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2})_{|G}\>}u\}

(5.33)

together with a union bound, and on the last line we used the switching lemma as in (
5.10
).

\Box

Recall that for every
S\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
finite containing
0
, and every
\beta\geq 0
,

\varphi_{\beta}(S)=\beta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{S,\beta}.

(5.34)

Also, the external vertex boundary of
S
is defined by
\partial^{\textup{ext}}S=\{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}\setminus S:\>\exists x\in S,\>x\sim y\}
.

Lemma 5.10
.

Let
d\geq 2
,
\beta>0
,
h\geq 0
, and
n\geq 1
. Let
J\in(\mathbb{R}^{+})^{E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
which satisfies
J_{e}=\beta
, for every
e\in E(\Lambda_{n})
. Let
\Lambda
be a finite subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
containing
\Lambda_{2n}
. Then, for every
S\subset\Lambda_{n}
,

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,J,h}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda,J,h}\leq\varphi_{\beta}(S)\cdot\max_{x\in\partial^{\textup{ext}}S}\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,J,h}.

(5.35)

Proof.

Let
S\subset\Lambda_{n}
. Recall from Remark
2.1
that we may write

\langle\cdot\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}^{+}=\langle\cdot\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}},

(5.36)

where
\mathsf{h}_{x}=h+\sum_{y\sim x}\mathds{1}_{y\notin\Lambda}
. Using Lemma
5.8
and noticing that
\{x\in\Lambda:\mathsf{h}_{x}>h\}=\partial\Lambda
,

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\leq\sum_{\gamma:0\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}}\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma)\mathds{1}_{\gamma\text{ reaches $\mathfrak{g}$ through }\partial\Lambda}.

(5.37)

The above inequality has two important consequences. First, since
\Lambda
contains
\Lambda_{2n}
, any path
\gamma
contributing to (
5.37
) has to reach
\partial S
. This is because the path
\gamma
is killed when reaching the ghost, and can only do so at the boundary of
\Lambda
. As a consequence, letting
uv
with
u\in S
and
v\notin S
be the first edge visited by
\gamma
which exits
S
, we can write
\gamma
as the concatenation of
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\gamma_{3}
, where:

\gamma_{1}:0\rightarrow u\subset S,\qquad\gamma_{2}=(uv),\qquad\gamma_{3}:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g},

(5.38)

where
(uv)
denotes the path consisting of the edge
uv
. Plugging this observation in (
5.37
) and using (
5.16
) gives,

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma_{1}:0\rightarrow u\subset S\\
\gamma_{3}:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}\end{subarray}}\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1}\circ(uv))\rho_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{\gamma_{1}\circ(uv)},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{3}).

(5.39)

For any fixed
\gamma_{1}
as above,

\sum_{\gamma_{3}:v\rightarrow\mathfrak{g}}\rho_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{\gamma_{1}\circ(uv)},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{3})=\langle\sigma_{v}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{\gamma_{1}\circ(uv)},\beta,h}\leq\max_{x\in\partial^{\textup{ext}}S}\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,\beta,h},

(5.40)

where we used (
2.2
) and the fact that
v\in\partial^{\textup{ext}}S
by definition. Now, using (
5.16
) and (
5.17
) yields,

\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1}\circ(uv))=\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1})\rho_{\Lambda\setminus\overline{\gamma_{1}},\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{2})\leq\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1})\beta.

(5.41)

Since
\gamma_{1}\subset S
, one also have
\rho_{\Lambda,\beta,\mathsf{h}}(\gamma_{1})\leq\rho_{S,\beta}(\gamma_{1})
. Plugging this observation, (
5.40
), and (
5.41
) in (
5.39
) gives

\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,\beta,h}-\langle\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\Lambda,\beta,h}\leq\beta\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\sum_{\gamma_{1}:0\rightarrow u\subset S}\rho_{S,\beta}(\gamma_{1})\max_{x\in\partial^{\textup{ext}}S}\langle\sigma_{x}\rangle^{+}_{\Lambda,\beta,h},

(5.42)

from which the proof follows readily.

5.4
Random currents and the FK-Ising model: coupling and applications

The following result is proved in
[
ADCTW19
, Theorem 3.2]
(see also
[
HKK25
,
HJK25
]
) and originates from an observation made in
[
LW16
]
. Recall that the event
\mathcal{F}_{A}=\mathcal{F}_{A}^{G}
introduced in (
5.6
) can be viewed as a percolation event (see Remark
5.4
). This allows to make sense of
\phi^{0}_{G,\beta}[\>\cdot\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{A}]
below.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and let
A\subset V
be a finite even subset of
G
. Let
\beta>0
. Let
\mathbf{n}
be distributed according to
\mathbf{P}_{G,\beta}^{A}
. Let
(\omega_{e})_{e\in E(\mathbb{Z}^{d})}
be an independent Bernoulli percolation with parameter
1-\exp(-\beta)
. For each
e\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, we define

\eta_{e}:=\max(\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}_{e}>0},\omega_{e}).

(5.43)

Then, the law of
\eta
is
\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[\>\cdot\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{A}]
. As a consequence, if
\mathcal{A}
is an increasing event, one has

\mathbf{P}^{A}_{G,\beta}[\mathcal{A}]\leq\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[\mathcal{A}\>|\>\mathcal{F}_{A}].

(5.44)

We will apply the above statement to the special cases
A=\emptyset
or
A=\{x,y\}
for
x,y\in V
. In the former case the law of
\eta
is
\phi^{0}_{G,\beta}
, and in the latter case it is
\phi^{0}_{G,\beta}[\>\cdot\>|\>x\xleftrightarrow{}y]
, i.e. the FK-Ising measure conditioned on the event that
x
is connected to
y
. Recall that when
G=\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, there is a unique translation invariant measure that we denote by
\phi_{\beta}
.

5.4.1
A BK-type inequality for the FK-Ising model

We use the coupling of Lemma
5.11
to prove a new correlation inequality: Proposition
2.13
. In the context of Bernoulli percolation, this inequality is a standard consequence of BK inequality. However, as discussed in
[
Gri06
, Chapter 3.9]
, the BK inequality is not satisfied for general random cluster models. Nevertheless, the following application of it remains valid for the FK-Ising model.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
d\geq 2
and
\beta>0
. Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
with
0\in V
. Let
S\cap B=\emptyset
be finite subsets of
V
, with
S
containing
0
. Assume that
\partial S
is separating
0
and
B
in the sense that every path from
0
to
B
has to visit
\partial S
. Then,

\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[0\xleftrightarrow{}B]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\phi_{S,\beta}^{0}[0\xleftrightarrow{}u]\beta\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[v\xleftrightarrow{}B].

(5.45)

Proof.

It is sufficient to treat the case where
G=(V,E)
is finite as the general case follows by approximation.
Let
\beta>0
and write
\phi=\phi^{0}_{G,\beta}
.

Write
B=\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{|B|}\}
. If
x\in V
, we let
L^{x}:=\max\{1\leq k\leq|B|:x\xleftrightarrow{}b_{k}\}
(with the convention that
L^{x}=0
if
x
is not connected to
B
). Moreover, let
B_{>\ell}:=\{b_{\ell+1},\ldots,b_{|B|}\}
. By definition,

\phi[0\leftrightarrow B]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{|B|}\phi[L^{0}=\ell]=\sum_{\ell=1}^{|B|}\phi[0\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}]\phi[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\mid 0\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}].

(5.46)

By Lemma
5.11
, we may rewrite the right-most factor of (
5.46
) as

\phi[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\mid 0\leftrightarrow b_{\ell}]=(\mathbf{P}^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\textup{ in }\eta],

(5.47)

where we recall that
\eta
is the percolation configuration on
E
defined by
\eta_{e}=\max(\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}_{e}>0},\omega_{e})
, where
\omega
is distributed according to the Bernoulli percolation measure
\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}}
with
p_{\beta}=1-e^{-\beta}
.
Any current
\mathbf{n}
with sources
\partial\mathbf{n}=\{0,b_{\ell}\}
contains an odd path—the backbone
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
—from
0
to
b_{\ell}
.
Denote by
\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})
the sub-exploration of the backbone of
\mathbf{n}
started from
0
and stopped at the first time it takes a step
uv
exiting
S
. Observe that this is well-defined since, by assumption, any path from
0
to
b_{\ell}
has to visit
\partial S
, and then exit
S
(since
S\cap B=\emptyset
). Decomposing the probability in (
5.47
) according to the value of
\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})
yields

(\mathbf{P}^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:0\to u\\
\gamma\subset S\end{subarray}}(\mathbf{P}^{0\ell}_{\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\circ(uv),0\nleftrightarrow B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta],

(5.48)

where we recall that
(uv)
denotes the one step path from
u
to
v
.
Fix
(uv)
and
\gamma:0\to u
as in (
5.48
), and let
\gamma_{uv}=\gamma\circ(uv)
. Observe that

\{\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}\}\cap\{0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta\}=\{\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}\}\cap\{v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta\}\\
\subset\{\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}\}\cap\{v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}\},

(5.49)

where
\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}
denotes the restriction of
\eta
to edges in
\overline{\gamma}^{c}
. Additionally, note that the two events on the right-hand side of (
5.49
) are measurable with respect to
\overline{\gamma}_{uv}
and
\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}(=E\setminus\overline{\gamma_{uv}})
respectively. What follows is a simple modification of Lemma
5.7
. If
\mathbf{n}\in\Omega_{G}
, we write
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}
where
\mathbf{n}_{1}
(resp.
\mathbf{n}_{2}
) is supported on
\overline{\gamma_{uv}}
(resp.
\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}
). Observe that

w^{G}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n})=w^{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{1})w^{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{2}).

(5.50)

With the above observation, the event
\{\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}\}
is measurable in terms of
\mathbf{n}_{1}
, and the event
\{v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}\}
is measurable in terms of the pair
(\mathbf{n}_{2},\omega)
. Moreover, notice that
\{\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}\}=\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n}_{1})=\gamma_{uv}\}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{P}^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})

\displaystyle[\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv},\>v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Z^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{n}_{1}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{1}=\{0,v\}\\
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}:\>\partial\mathbf{n}_{2}=\{v,b_{\ell}\}\end{subarray}}w^{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{1})\mathds{1}_{\Gamma(\mathbf{n}_{1})=\gamma}w^{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}_{\beta}(\mathbf{n}_{2})\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}}[v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{Z^{0v}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}},\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}]Z^{vb_{\ell}}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}}{Z^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}}(\mathbf{P}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{vb_{\ell}}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}].

(5.51)

Now, we observe that

Z^{0v}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}},\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}]Z^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}=Z^{0v}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}]=Z^{0v}_{G,\beta}\mathbf{P}^{0v}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}].

(5.52)

Plugging (
5.52
) in (
5.51
) yields

(\mathbf{P}^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[\Gamma^{S}(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv},\>v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}]\\
=\frac{\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{v}\rangle_{G,\beta}\langle\sigma_{v}\sigma_{b_{\ell}}\rangle_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}}{\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{b_{\ell}}\rangle_{G,\beta}}\mathbf{P}^{0v}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma_{uv}](\mathbf{P}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{xb_{\ell}}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}].

(5.53)

Plugging (
5.49
) and (
5.53
) in (
5.48
) gives

\phi[0\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}]\phi[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\mid 0\leftrightarrow b_{\ell}]=\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{b_{\ell}}\rangle_{G,\beta}(\mathbf{P}^{0b_{\ell}}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[0{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}]\\
\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:0\to u\\
\gamma\subset S\end{subarray}}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma\circ(uv))\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}]\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}\mid v\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}],

(5.54)

where in the first line we used (
1.5
) and (
5.47
), and on the second line we used (again) (
1.5
) and Lemma
5.11
. Proceeding as in (
5.46
),

\sum_{\ell=1}^{|B|}\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}]\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v{\large\>\nleftrightarrow\>}B_{>\ell}\text{ in }\eta_{|\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c}}\mid v\xleftrightarrow{}b_{\ell}]=\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v\xleftrightarrow{}B].

(5.55)

By Proposition
2.12
, the measure
\phi
stochastically dominates
\phi^{0}_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}
, so that
\phi_{\overline{\gamma_{uv}}^{c},\beta}^{0}[v\xleftrightarrow{}B]\leq\phi[v\xleftrightarrow{}B]
. Combining this observation with (
5.46
), (
5.54
), and (
5.55
), gives

\phi[0\xleftrightarrow{}B]\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in S\\
v\notin S\\
u\sim v\end{subarray}}\phi[v\xleftrightarrow{}B]\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:0\to u\\
\gamma\subset S\end{subarray}}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma\circ(uv)).

(5.56)

Finally,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:0\to u\\
\gamma\subset S\end{subarray}}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma\circ(uv))\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\gamma:0\to u\\
\gamma\subset S\end{subarray}}\rho_{S,\beta}(\gamma)\beta=\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{u}\rangle_{S,\beta}\beta=\phi_{S,\beta}^{0}[0\xleftrightarrow{}u]\beta,

(5.57)

where in the inequality we used (
5.16
) and then (
5.15
) and (
5.17
) to write

\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma\circ(uv))=\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma)\rho_{G\setminus\overline{\gamma},\beta}((uv))\leq\rho_{S,\beta}(\gamma)\beta,

(5.58)

and in the last equality of (
5.57
) we used (
1.5
). Plugging (
5.57
) in (
5.56
) concludes the proof.

5.4.2
Tree graph inequality

We now turn to the proof of the tree-graph inequality of Proposition
2.14
. Recall that
\{x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z\}
denotes the event that
x
is connected to both
y
and
z
.

Proposition 5.13

(Tree-graph inequality)
.

Let
G=(V,E)
be a subgraph of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and
\beta\geq 0
. Then, for every
x,y,z\in V
,

\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[x\xleftrightarrow{}y,z]\leq\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V:\>u\sim u^{\prime}}\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[x\xleftrightarrow{}u^{\prime}]\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}[u^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{}z]\phi^{0}_{G,\beta}[u\xleftrightarrow{}y].

(5.59)

Proof.

We let
\phi=\phi_{G,\beta}^{0}
. The idea is to write

\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}y,z]=\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}y\>|\>x\xleftrightarrow{\>}z]\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}z].

(5.60)

Using the coupling of Lemma
5.11
,

\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}y\>|\>x\xleftrightarrow{\>}z]=(\mathbf{P}^{xz}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[x\xleftrightarrow{}y\text{ in }\eta],

(5.61)

where we recall that
\eta_{e}=\max(\mathds{1}_{\mathbf{n}_{e}>0},\omega_{e})
where
\omega\sim\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}}
which is the Bernoulli percolation measure of parameter
p_{\beta}=1-e^{-\beta}
. Let
\gamma:x\rightarrow z
be any possible realisation of
\Gamma(\mathbf{n})
. Observe that

\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\}\cap\{x\xleftrightarrow{}y\text{ in }\eta\}\subset\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\}\cap\{y\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}\text{ in }\eta_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}\}.

(5.62)

Thus, if we proceed as in the proof of Proposition
2.13
and write
\mathbf{n}=\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}
with
\mathbf{n}_{1}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}}
and
\mathbf{n}_{2}\in\Omega_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}
, one has that
\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\}=\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n}_{1})=\gamma\}
is measurable in terms of
\mathbf{n}_{1}
, and that
\{\overline{\gamma}\xleftrightarrow{}y\text{ in }\eta_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}\}
is measurable in terms of the pair
(\mathbf{n}_{2},\omega)
. By Lemma
5.7
,

\displaystyle(\mathbf{P}^{xz}_{G,\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[\{\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma\}\cap\{y

\displaystyle\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}\text{ in }\eta_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}\}]

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}^{xz}_{G,\beta}[\Gamma(\mathbf{n})=\gamma](\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[\{y\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}\text{ in }\eta_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}\}].

(5.63)

Now, observe that by Lemma
5.11
and the stochastic domination
\phi_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}^{0}\leq\phi
(see Proposition
2.12
),

(\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset}_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}\otimes\mathbb{P}_{p_{\beta}})[\{y\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}\text{ in }\eta_{\overline{\gamma}^{c}}\}]=\phi_{\overline{\gamma}^{c},\beta}^{0}[y\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}]\leq\phi[y\xleftrightarrow{}\overline{\gamma}]\leq\sum_{u\in V}\mathds{1}_{u\in\overline{\gamma}}\phi[y\xleftrightarrow{}u].

(5.64)

Combining (
5.60
)–(
5.64
) gives

\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}y,z]\leq\sum_{u\in V}\phi[u\xleftrightarrow{}y]\sum_{\gamma:x\rightarrow z}\mathds{1}_{u\in\overline{\gamma}}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma).

(5.65)

Note that if
u\in\overline{\gamma}
, then there exists
u^{\prime}\sim u
such that
u^{\prime}\in\gamma
. Hence, (
5.65
) rewrites

\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{\>}y,z]\leq\sum_{u,u^{\prime}\in V:\>u\sim u^{\prime}}\phi[u\xleftrightarrow{}y]\sum_{\gamma:x\rightarrow z}\mathds{1}_{u^{\prime}\in\gamma}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma).

(5.66)

The chain rule of (
5.18
) combined with (
1.5
) gives that

\sum_{\gamma:x\rightarrow z}\mathds{1}_{u^{\prime}\in\gamma}\rho_{G,\beta}(\gamma)\leq\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{u^{\prime}}\rangle_{G,\beta}\langle\sigma_{u^{\prime}}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{G,\beta}=\phi[x\xleftrightarrow{}u^{\prime}]\phi[u^{\prime}\xleftrightarrow{}z],

(5.67)

from which the proof follows readily.

5.5

. For sake of clarity, we import the appropriate notations from

DCP25c

\mathbb{H}_{n}(\pm\mathbf{e}_{i})

(5.69)

In
[
DCP25c
]

There exists

\mathbf{E}^{\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[\mathds{1}_{0\in\mathcal{S}_{n}}\varphi_{\beta_{c}}(\mathcal{S}_{n})]\leq K_{1}.

Proof.

6
One-arm exponent of the double random current measure

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.13
. We begin with the (more interesting) proof of the upper bound.

6.1
Proof of the upper bound

The most naive approach to get an upper bound on the one-arm probability is to use a first moment method. In the case of the full-space double random current measure, such an approach gives

\mathbf{P}_{\beta_{c}}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}\>}\partial\Lambda_{n}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phantom{\eqref{eq:applicationswitch1}}}}{{\leq}}\sum_{x\in\partial\Lambda_{n}}\mathbf{P}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}_{\beta_{c}}[0\xleftrightarrow{\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}\>}x]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:applicationswitch1}}}{{=}}\sum_{x\in\partial\Lambda_{n}}\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{eq:IRB}}}{{\leq}}\frac{C}{n^{d-3}},

(6.1)

for some
C=C(d)>0
. Above, we used (
5.9
) which follows from the switching lemma. In a way, it makes explicit the fact that the sourceless double random current model is a loop model: to connect
0
and
x
, one has to “pay” a connection from
0
to
x
—which “costs”
\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}
—and then a connection from
x
to
0
—which costs an additional
\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta_{c}}

\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{S,\beta_{c}}
for some well-chosen

(which will be given by Lemma
2.17
). This improvement would be very reasonable if we had a way to explore the double random current cluster of
0
, and to “stop” this exploration as soon as it reaches
\partial S
. The next lemma justifies this intuition. We believe the strategy of proof (which relies on some kind of exploration) to be of independent interest in the study of sourceless currents.

Remark 6.2
.

Let

. Applying Lemma

to

and any

Lemma 6.3
.

Let

\mathbf{P}_{\beta}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}[x,y\in\mathbf{C}_{\mathbf{n}_{1}+\mathbf{n}_{2}}(0)]\leq\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle_{\beta}\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{0}\rangle_{\beta}+(x\Leftrightarrow y),

Remark 6.4
.

Lemma

Proposition 6.5

Let

. There exists

Proof.

We fix
\beta=\beta_{c}
and drop it from the notations. Let

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}^{\emptyset,\emptyset}[\mathcal{N}^{2}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x,y\in\partial\Lambda_{n}}\Big(\langle\sigma_{0}\sigma_{x}\rangle\langle\sigma_{x}\sigma_{y}\rangle\langle\sigma_{y}\sigma_{0}\rangle+(x\Leftrightarrow y)\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim(n^{2-d})^{2}\sum_{x\in\partial\Lambda_{n}}\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{k^{d-2}}{(1\vee k)^{d-2}}\lesssim\frac{1}{n^{d-4}},

(6.22)

which concludes the proof.

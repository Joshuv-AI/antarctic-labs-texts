The existence of S 1 × C p S^{1}\times C_{p} -maps between representation spheres and its applications
Ikumitsu Nagasaki

Abstract
We show the existence of S 1 × C p S^{1}\times C_{p} -maps between certain representation spheres. As an application, we show that, in the family of abelian compact Lie groups, a group G G has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property (in the sense of Bartsch) if and only if G G is either a finite abelian p p -group or a k k -torus.



1
Background and statement of results

Some versions of Borsuk-Ulam type theorems assert the non-existence of equivariant maps. This fact has wide applications in various fields, such as nonlinear analysis, combinatorics, and discrete geometry. Details can be found in
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
and
[
9
]
.

In this paper, we would like to discuss the existence of certain equivariant maps.
Let
V
be an orthogonal representation of a compact Lie group
G
, and let
S(V)
be the representation sphere of
V
. Throughout this paper, we assume that representation spheres are
G
-fixed-point-free unless otherwise stated.
It is well-known that the Borsuk-Ulam theorem holds if
G
is either an elementary abelian
p
-group
C_{p}^{k}
or a
k
-torus
T^{k}
, that is, if there exists a
G
-map
f:S(V)\to S(W)
, then
\dim V\leq\dim W
; see, for example,
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
.
We say that a compact Lie group
G
has the Borsuk-Ulam property if the Borsuk-Ulam theorem holds for
G
.
By results of
[
1
]
,
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
,
compact Lie groups other than
C_{p}^{k}
and
T^{k}
do not have the Borsuk-Ulam property.
Following these researches, Crabb
[
5
]
has given explicit counterexamples to the Borsuk-Ulam theorem and provided an alternate proof in the case of finite groups.

Bartsch
[
1
]
also studied Borsuk-Ulam type theorems in a weaker sense.
We say that
G
has the
weak Borsuk-Ulam property
if the Borsuk-Ulam function
b_{G}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
, defined below, is not bounded, or equivalently,
\lim_{n\to\infty}b_{G}(n)=\infty
.
Here
b_{G}
is defined as follows, see
[
1
]
:

\displaystyle b_{G}(n)

\displaystyle=\max\{k\,|\,\exists\,f:S(V)\to S(W)\text{ $G$-map with }\dim V\geq n\Rightarrow\dim W\geq k\}

\displaystyle=\min\{\dim W\,|\,\exists\,f:S(V)\to S(W)\text{ $G$-map with }\dim V\geq n\}.

Note that
b_{G}
is weakly increasing, and if there exists a
G
-map
f:S(V)\to S(W)
, then
b_{G}(\dim V)\leq\dim W
.
By the Borsuk-Ulam theorem,
C_{p}^{k}
and
T^{k}
have the weak Borsuk-Ulam property; in fact,
b_{G}(\dim V)=\dim V
for
G=C_{p}^{k}
or
T^{k}
.
Bartsch
[
1
, Theorem 2]
shows that if
G
has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property, then
G
is a
p
-toral group, which means that
G
has an extension

1\to T^{k}\to G\to P\to 1,

where
P
is a finite
p
-group.
Furthermore, Bartsch
[
1
, Theorem 1]
shows that, if
G
is a finite
p
-group, then
G
has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property.
From this, one might expect that every
p
-toral group has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property. However, we will show that this conjecture is false. In this paper, we focus on the abelian case, particularly on the group
S^{1}\times C_{p}
.
The main results are as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

The group
S^{1}\times C_{p}
does not have the weak Borsuk-Ulam property for any prime
p
.

This leads to the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

In the family of abelian compact Lie groups, a group
G
has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property if and only if
G
is either a finite abelian
p
-group or a
k
-torus.

2
The existence of
S^{1}\times C_{p}
-maps

Let
S^{1}=\{t\in\mathbb{C}\,|\ |t|=1\}
and let
C_{p}
denote the cyclic group of prime order
p
with generator
a
. Set
G=S^{1}\times C_{p}
and denote an element of

G
by
ta^{i}
.
We denote by
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
the cyclic subgroup of
S^{1}
generated by
\xi_{n}=\exp(2\pi\sqrt{-1}/n)\in S^{1}
.
The unitary (irreducible)
G
-representation
V_{k,l}
, whose underlying space is
\mathbb{C}
, is defined by setting
t\cdot z=t^{k}z
and
a\cdot z=\xi_{p}^{l}z
for
z\in V_{k,l}
(
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
l\in\mathbb{Z}/p
).
For positive integers
n
and

, we set

V_{n}=V_{1,1}\oplus V_{p,1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{p^{n-1},1}

and

W_{m}=V_{p^{m},0}\oplus V_{0,1}.

Note that
\dim V_{n}=2n
and
\dim W_{m}=4
.
The following result implies that
S^{1}\times C_{p}
does not have the weak Borsuk-Ulam property.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any integer
n\geq 1
, there exists a
G
-map
f_{n}:S(V_{n})\to S(W_{m})
for some
m\geq n
.

If
n=1
, then one can define a
G
-map
f_{1}:S(V_{1})\to S(W_{1})
by
f_{1}(z)=(z^{p},0)
for
z\in S(V_{1})
.
If
n=2
, according to
[
5
]
, one can define a
G
-map
\bar{f}_{2}:V_{2}\to W_{2}
by

\bar{f}_{2}(z,w)=(z^{p^{2}}-w^{p},\overline{z}^{\,p}w).

Since
\bar{f}_{2}^{-1}(0,0)=\{(0,0)\}
, we obtain a
G
-map
f_{2}:S(V_{2})\to S(W_{2})
by normalizing
\bar{f}_{2}
.

Remark 1
.

Note that
\dim S(V_{2})=\dim S(W_{2})=3
and
\deg f_{2}=0
, since
f_{2}
is not surjective; for example,
(0,\sqrt{-1})\not\in\text{Im\,}f_{2}
.
More generally, it follows from
[
8
, Theorem 1.7]
that
\deg f=0
for any
G
-map
f:S(V_{2})\to S(W_{2})
.

When

, we show the desired result by induction on

.
We begin with the case of
n=3
.

Proposition 2.2
.

There exists a
G
-map
f_{3}:S(V_{3})\to S(W_{3})
.

Proof.

We decompose
S(V_{3})
into

S(V_{3})=X_{0}\cup X\times I\cup X_{1},

where

\displaystyle X_{0}=S(V_{1,1})\times D(V_{p,1}\oplus V_{p^{2},1}),

\displaystyle X=S(V_{1,1})\times S(V_{p,1}\oplus V_{p^{2},1}),

\displaystyle X_{1}=D(V_{1,1})\times S(V_{p,1}\oplus V_{p^{2},1}).

We define a
G
-map
g_{0}:X_{0}\to S(W_{3})
by
g_{0}(u,z,w)=(u^{p^{3}},0)
. Next, by lifting
f_{2}
via
\pi:G\to G
,
ta^{i}\mapsto t^{p}a^{i}
, we have a
G
-map

\tilde{f}_{2}:S(V_{p,1}\oplus V_{p^{2},1})\to S(W_{3})=S(V_{p^{3},0}\oplus V_{0,1}).

Note that
\tilde{f}_{2}=f_{2}
as (non-equivariant) maps. We define a
G
-map
g_{1}:X_{1}\to S(W_{3})
by
g_{1}(u,z,w)=\tilde{f}_{2}(z,w)
.
Set
h_{0}=g_{0}|_{\partial X_{0}}:\partial X_{0}=X\times\{0\}\to S(W_{3})
and
h_{1}=g_{1}|_{\partial X_{1}}:\partial X_{1}=X\times\{1\}\to S(W_{3})
.
In order to obtain a
G
-map
f_{3}
, it suffices to show that

h_{0}\coprod h_{1}:X\times\partial I\to S(W_{3})
can be extended to a
G
-map
H:X\times I\to S(W_{3})
, that is,
H
is a
G
-homotopy between
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
. In fact, if we have such a
G
-homotopy

, then we obtain a
G
-map
f_{3}=g_{0}\cup H\cup g_{1}
by gluing
G
-maps.

Set
Y=S(V_{p,1}\oplus V_{p^{2},1})
.
We observe that there is a bijection

\bar{F}:\text{Map}_{G}(X,S(W_{3}))\to\text{Map}_{C_{p}}(Y,S(W_{3})),

which is defined by

\bar{F}(\alpha)(z_{1},z_{2})=\alpha(1,(z_{1},z_{2}))

for
\alpha:X\to S(W_{3})
.
We also define a map

\bar{G}:\text{Map}_{C_{p}}(Y,S(W_{3}))\to\text{Map}_{G}(X,S(W_{3}))

by

\bar{G}(\beta)(u,(z_{1},z_{2}))=u\cdot\beta(u^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))

for a
C_{p}
-map
\beta:Y\to S(W_{3})
. One can see this as follows.

Assertion 1
.

\bar{F}(\alpha)
is a
C_{p}
-map.

Note that
\xi_{p}\in S^{1}
acts trivially on
Y
and
S(W_{3})
.
Then,

\displaystyle\bar{F}(\alpha)(a\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=\alpha(1,\,a\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=\alpha(a\cdot\xi_{p}^{-1},\,a\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=a\cdot\alpha(\xi_{p}^{-1},(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=a\cdot\alpha(\xi_{p}^{-1},\xi_{p}^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=a\xi_{p}^{-1}\cdot\alpha(1,(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=a\cdot\alpha(1,(z_{1},z_{2}))=a\cdot\bar{F}(\alpha)(z_{1},z_{2}).

Assertion 2
.

\bar{G}(\beta)
is a
G
-map.

In fact,

\displaystyle\bar{G}(\beta)(t\cdot(u,(z_{1},z_{2})))

\displaystyle=\bar{G}(\beta)(tu,t\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=(tu)\cdot\beta((tu)^{-1}\cdot(t\cdot(z_{1},z_{2})))

\displaystyle=(tu)\cdot\beta(u^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=t\cdot\bar{G}(\beta)(u,(z_{1},z_{2})),

and

\displaystyle\bar{G}(\beta)(a\cdot(u,(z_{1},z_{2})))

\displaystyle=\bar{G}(\beta)(\xi_{p}u,\,a\cdot(z_{1},z_{2})))=(\xi_{p}u)\cdot\beta(u^{-1}\xi_{p}^{-1}a\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=au\cdot\beta(u^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=a\cdot\bar{G}(\beta)(u,z_{1},z_{2}).

Assertion 3
.

\bar{F}^{-1}=\bar{G}
.

In fact,

\displaystyle\bar{G}(\bar{F}(\alpha))(u,(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=u\cdot\bar{F}(\alpha)(u^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))

\displaystyle=u\cdot\alpha(1,\,u^{-1}\cdot(z_{1},z_{2}))=\alpha(u,(z_{1},z_{2}))

and

\bar{F}(\bar{G}(\beta))(z_{1},z_{2})=\bar{G}(\beta)(1,z_{1},z_{2})=\beta(z_{1},z_{2}).

Since a
G
-homotopy
\alpha_{s}
,
s\in I
, between
\alpha_{0}
and
\alpha_{1}
induces a
C_{p}
-homotopy
\bar{F}(\alpha_{s})
between
F(\alpha_{0})
and
F(\alpha_{1})
. This is also true for
\bar{G}
.
Thus
\bar{F}
induces
a bijection

F:[X,S(W_{3})]_{G}\to[Y,S(W_{3})]_{C_{p}}

between equivariant homotopy sets.
Since
C_{p}
acts freely on
Y
,

[
6
, Chapter II, §4]
or
[
4
, Theorem 3.1]
shows that
the degree of
C_{p}
-maps gives an injective map

\deg:[Y,S(W_{3})]_{C_{p}}\to[Y,S(W_{3})]_{\{1\}}=[S^{3},S^{3}]\cong\mathbb{Z}.

Since
\bar{F}(h_{0})
is a constant map, it follows that
\deg F([h_{0}])=0
. On the other hand,
F([h_{1}])=[\tilde{f}_{2}]
and
\deg\bar{F}(h_{1})=\deg\tilde{f}_{2}=0
by Remark

. Thus
\bar{F}(h_{1})
and
\bar{F}(h_{1})
are
C_{p}
-homotopic, and hence

and
h_{1}
are
G
-homotopic via

G
-homotopy
H
between
h_{0}
and
h_{1}
.
∎

3
Conclusions and remarks

First we prove Corollary
1.2
.

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

The necessary condition has already been shown in
[
1
]
. If
G
is an abelian compact Lie group other than
C_{p}^{k}
and
T^{k}
, there exists a closed subgroup
H
such that
G/H\cong S^{1}\times C_{p}
.
By lifting
f_{n}
via
\pi:G\to G/H
, we obtain a
G
-map

\tilde{f}_{n}:S(\tilde{V}_{n})\to S(\tilde{W}_{m})
.
This implies that
b_{G}(n)\leq b_{G}(2n)\leq 4
, and that
G
does not have the weak Borsuk-Ulam property.
∎

We give some remarks on the Borsuk-Ulam function.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G=S^{1}\times C_{p}
. If
p
is an odd prime, then
b_{G}(n)=4
when
n\geq 4
.

Proof.

We already know
b_{G}(n)\leq 4
.
If
b_{G}(n)<4
, then

b_{G}(n)\leq 2
, since the dimension of a fixed-point-free
G
-representation is even.
We show that there are no
G
-maps
f:S(V)\to S(W)
if
\dim V\geq 4
and
\dim W=2
.
In fact, we may suppose
W=V_{k,l}
for some
(k,l)\neq(0,0)
in
\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/p
.
Then
W^{S^{1}}=0
or
W^{C_{p}}=0
, hence
V^{S^{1}}=0
or
V^{C_{p}}=0
respectively.
By the Borsuk-Ulam theorem for
S^{1}
or
C_{p}
, we have
\dim V\leq\dim W
, which leads to a contradiction.
∎

Remark 2
.

One can see
b_{G}(3)=4
,
b_{G}(2)=2
and
b_{G}(1)=2
when
p
is an odd prime.

Next, let
G
be a compact Lie group. The invariant
r_{G}(W)
is defined by

r_{G}(W)=\sup\{\dim V\,|\,\exists\,f:S(V)\to S(W)\text{ $G$-map }\}\leq\infty.

Here we assume that
G
-representations are fixed-point-free.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
H
be a closed subgroup of
G
and
W
a
G
-representation
W
with
W^{H}=0
. Then
r_{G}(W)\leq r_{H}(W)
.

Proof.

Assume
r_{G}(W)<\infty
. Let
f:S(V)\to S(W)
be a
G
-map with
r_{G}(W)=\dim V
. Then
{\rm Res}_{H}f:S(V)\to S(W)
is an
H
-map with
W^{H}=0
.
Therefore
r_{H}(W)\geq\dim V=r_{G}(W)
.
If
r_{G}(W)=\infty
, then there exists a
G
-map
f:S(V)\to S(W)
with an arbitrarily large dimension
\dim V
. Consequently,
{\rm Res}_{H}f:S(V)\to S(W)
is an
H
-map with an arbitrarily large dimension
\dim{\rm Res}_{H}V
. Therefore
r_{H}(W)=\infty
.
∎

By Bartsch’s results of

]
, if
G

p

G

W

W^{G}=0
such that
r_{G}(W)=\infty
.
Furthermore, if
G
has the weak Borsuk-Ulam property, then
r_{G}(W)<\infty
for any
G
-representation
W

W^{G}=0
.
On the other hand, we observe

Proposition 3.3
.

If
G
is a
p
-toral group, then
r_{G}(W)<\infty
for every
G
-representation
W
with
W^{G}=0
.

Proof.

Suppose that
G
has an extension

1\to T^{k}\to G\to P\to 1,

where
P

p

By
[
6

p

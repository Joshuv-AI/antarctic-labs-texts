Birational equivalence of Severi–Brauer varieties
János Kollár

Abstract
. We prove Amitsur’s conjecture for Severi–Brauer varieties whose index is not a prime power.



Let
P
be a Severi–Brauer variety over a field
k
.
Amitsur
[
Ami55
]
proved that if
P
is birational to another Severi–Brauer variety
Q
, then they
generate the same subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
, and conjectured that the converse also holds, provided
P,Q
have the same dimension.
The conjecture was popularized by Serre in

[
Ser62
,
Ser79
, Sec.X.6]
.

Roquette
[
Roq64
]
developed an inductive method, and settled the conjecture when
P
splits over a solvable Galois extension of degree
\dim P+1
.
Krashen
[
Kra03
,
Kra08
]
used the method to show, among others, that if the conjecture holds for both
P_{1},P_{2}
, and
\dim P_{1}+1,\dim P_{2}+1
are relatively prime,
then it also holds for their (tensor or Brauer) product
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
; see Definition
7
.
Combined with
[
Tre91
]
, this implies that the conjecture holds if 2 is a primitive root modulo every prime factor of
\dim P+1
.

We show that the conjecture holds for
P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
, even if we do not know what happens for
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
individually.
This leads to the following.

Theorem 1
.

Let
P,Q
be Severi–Brauer varieties of the same dimension over a field
k
. Assume that
\dim P+1
has at least 2 distinct prime factors.
Then
P,Q
generate the same subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
iff
P
is birationally equivalent to
Q
.

This turns out to be a straightforward consequence of the following birational description of the tensor product of Severi–Brauer varieties.

Theorem 2
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be Severi–Brauer varieties over a field
k
, and assume that

(\dim P_{1}+1,\dim P_{2}+1)=1
. Then there is a birational equivalence

[formula]

P_{1}\otimes P_{2}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P_{1}\times P_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r},\quad\mbox{where}\quad r=\dim P_{1}\cdot\dim P_{2}.

Remarks 3
.

(
3
.1) The method of
[
Ami55
]
produces
birational equivalences of the form

(P_{1}\otimes P_{2})\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r_{1}}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P_{1}\times P_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r_{2}}

for some
r_{1},r_{2}>0
, see (
4
.4).
The key assertion of Theorem
2
is that we can achieve
r_{1}=0
.

(
3
.2) The birational equivalence (
2
.1) usually fails without the realtively prime assumption.
For example, if
P_{1}=P_{2}=P
and
\dim P+1
is even, then

P\otimes P
and
P\times P
are not even stably birational to each other.

(
3
.3) There is no general birational product formula for

P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
that involves only the
P_{i}
and their dimensions.
For example, although
P
is birationally equivalent to its dual
P^{\vee}
, the tensor product
P\otimes P^{\vee}
is birationally trivial, but

P\otimes P
is usually not. See, however, Lemma
19
.

Note that in Theorem
2
,
the subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
generated by

P_{1}\otimes P_{2}
is the same as
the subgroup generated by

P_{1}
and
P_{2}
. This suggests the following generalization.

Conjecture 4
.

Let
X=\prod_{i\in I}P_{i}
and
Y=\prod_{j\in J}Q_{j}
be (ordinary) products of Severi–Brauer varieties.
The following are equivalent.

(4.1)

The subgroups
\langle P_{i}:i\in I\rangle\subset\operatorname{Br}(k)
and

\langle Q_{j}:j\in J\rangle\subset\operatorname{Br}(k)
agree.

(4.2)

X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim Y}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Y\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim X}
.

If
\dim X=\dim Y
, then these are also equivalent to

(4.3)

X\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Y
.

Known cases.
Using Corollary
10
we see that
the pull-back of each
Q_{j}
to
X
is trivial. Similarly,
the pull-back of each
P_{i}
to
Y
is trivial. This gives
birational equivalences

[formula]

X\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim Y}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}X\times Y\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Y\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim X}.

Thus (
4
.1) and (
4
.2) are equivalent.

(
4
.3)
\Rightarrow
(
4
.2) is clear, and
repeated application of Theorem
2
gives many cases of the converse.

Some cases where
P_{i},Q_{j}
have dimension
\leq 2
are treated in

[
Kol05
,
Hog09
]
.

Background on Severi–Brauer varieties

We collect basic results about Severi–Brauer varieties that we need. The algebraic versions are discussed in several books
[
Alb39
,
Her68
,
GS06
]
.
A geometric treatment is given in
[
Kol25
]
.

Definition 5
.

Let
k
be a field with algebraic closure
\bar{k}
. A
Severi–Brauer variety
is a
k
-variety
P
such that
P_{\bar{k}}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim X}_{\bar{k}}
.

Q\subset P
is a
twisted linear
subvariety if

Q_{\bar{k}}\subset P_{\bar{k}}\cong{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim X}_{\bar{k}}
is a linear subvariety.

The
index
of
P
is the gcd of the degrees of 0-cycles on
P
.
It is also the minimum degree of a (nonzero) effective 0-cycle on
P
.
The index divides
\dim P+1
; it is denoted by
\operatorname{ind}(P_{k})
.

Lemma 6
.

Let
P
be a Severi–Brauer variety.
All minimal twisted linear subvarieties are isomorphic to each other; denote these by
P^{\rm min}
.

Then
\dim P^{\rm min}=\operatorname{ind}(P)
, and
P
is birationally equivalent to

P^{\rm min}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r}
where
r=\dim P-\dim P^{\rm min}
.
∎

Definition 7

(Tensor or Brauer product)
.

Let
P,Q
be Severi–Brauer varieties over a field
k
.
There is a unique Severi–Brauer variety—denoted by

P\otimes Q
—and an embedding

P\times Q\lhook\joinrel\to P\otimes Q
, that is isomorphic to the Segre embedding over
\bar{k}
.

Definition 8

(Brauer group)
.

Two Severi–Brauer varieties
P,Q
are
Brauer equivalent
iff
P^{\rm min}\cong Q^{\rm min}
.

The tensor product defines a group structure on the set of
Brauer equivalence classes over
k
. It is called the

Brauer group
of
k
, and denoted by
\operatorname{Br}(k)
.

\operatorname{Br}(k)
is a torsion group, and
the order of
P
in
\operatorname{Br}(k)
is called the
period
of
P
.

Lemma 9
.

Let
\langle P\rangle\subset\operatorname{Br}(k)
denote the subgroup generated by
P
. Then
Q\in\langle P\rangle
iff there is a rational map
P\dasharrow Q
.
If these hold then
\operatorname{ind}(Q)
divides
\operatorname{ind}(P)
.
∎

Corollary 10
.

Let
P
be a Severi–Brauer variety. Then the kernel of the base change map
\operatorname{Br}(k)\to\operatorname{Br}\bigl(k(P)\bigr)
is the subgroup generated by
P
. ∎

The stable birational equivalence version of Theorem
1
is proved in
[
Ami55
]
.

Lemma 11
.

Let
P,Q
be Severi–Brauer varieties over a field
k
.
Then
\langle P\rangle=\langle Q\rangle

iff
P\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim Q}
is birational to
Q\times{\mathbb{P}}^{\dim P}
.
∎

Lemma 12

(Primary decomposition)
.

Let
P,Q
be minimal Severi–Brauer varieties over a field
k
.
Assume that
(\dim P+1,\dim Q+1)=1
.
Then
P\otimes Q
is the minimal Severi–Brauer variety
in the Brauer equivalence class of
[P]\cdot[Q]
.∎

Proof of Theorem
2

\Rightarrow

Theorem
1

13
.

By Lemma
11
, if
P
is birational to
Q
then they generate the same subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
.
The new result is the converse.

Assume first that
P
is not minimal. By Lemma
6

P\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P^{\rm min}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r},\quad\mbox{and}\quad Q\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q^{\rm min}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r},

where
r=\dim P-\dim P^{\rm min}\geq\dim P^{\rm min}+1
.
Thus

P^{\rm min}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q^{\rm min}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{r}

by Lemma
11
, and we are done. (Here we did not need the assumption about 2 distinct prime factors.)

We are left with the case when
P,Q
are minimal.
By assumption we can write
\dim P+1=(m+1)(n+1)
where
(m+1,n+1)=1
, and
m,n
are
both
\geq 1
. Choose
a,c>0
such that

a(m+1)+c(n+1)\equiv 1\mod\dim P+1.

Let
P_{1}
(resp.
P_{2}
) be the minimal Severi–Brauer variety that is Brauer equivalent to
P^{a(m+1)}
(resp.
P^{c(n+1)}
), and similarly for

Q_{1},Q_{2}
. Note that

P\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P_{1}\times P_{2}
and
Q\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q_{1}\times Q_{2}
by Lemma
12
.

Then
P_{1},Q_{1}
have index
n+1
, dimension
n
, and generate the same subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
. Similarly,
P_{2},Q_{2}
have index
m+1
, dimension
m
, and generate the same subgroup of
\operatorname{Br}(k)
.
Therefore, by Lemma
11
,

P_{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q_{1}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{n},\quad\mbox{and}\quad P_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}.

By (
2
.1)

P\cong P_{1}\otimes P_{2}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P_{1}\times P_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{nm}.

Since
mn\geq m,n
, we can first exchange
P_{1}
to
Q_{1}
, and then

P_{2}
to
Q_{2}
in the product. Thus we get that

P\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P_{1}\times P_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{nm}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q_{1}\times Q_{2}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{nm}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}Q.\qed

Roquette’s method

The geometric version is based on the following.

Lemma 14
.

Let
L_{0},\dots,L_{n}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be linear spaces. Assume that they span
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
and

\sum(1+\dim L_{i})=1+N
. Let
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
be a general point.

Then there is a unique linear subvariety
M\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
of dimension
n
that contains
p
and meets every
L_{i}
.

Proof. We use induction on
n
. If
n=0
, then
M=\{p\}
.
For
n>0
, project from
L_{n}
to get

L^{\prime}_{1},\dots,L^{\prime}_{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N-r}
, where
r=1+\dim L_{n}
.
Induction gives
M^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{N-r}
of dimension
n{-}1
.
Then
M
is the span of
M^{\prime}
and
p
. ∎

The proof shows that ‘
p\in{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
general’ means: not contained in the span of any

n
of the
L_{i}
.

In order to state a version for Severi–Brauer varieties, we need the notion of Weil restriction, introduced in Weil’s 1959 lectures; see
[
Wei82
, Sec.I.3]
. It is called the
trace
in

[
Roq64
,
Kra08
]
.

Definition 15

(Weil restriction)
.

A general definition is given in
[
BLR90
, Sec.7.6]
. We use it only for geometrically normal varieties and separable field extension, for which the following simpler definition works.

Let
K/k
be a finite, separable field extension, and
X
a
geometrically normal
K
-variety. Its
Weil restriction

\Re_{K/k}(X)
is the unique, normal
k
-variety with the following property.

(
15
.1) Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension and
K^{\prime}:=K\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
.
Then
K^{\prime}
-points of
X
naturally correspond to

k^{\prime}
-points of
\Re_{K/k}(X)
.

One should think of
\Re_{K/k}(X)
as the product of the
Galois conjugates of
X
over
k
, with the natural Galois action. In particular,
\Re_{K/k}(X)
becomes isomorphic to the product of
|K:k|
copies of
X_{\bar{k}}
over
\bar{k}
.

The Weil restriction of a Severi–Brauer variety is not a Severi–Brauer variety, but

[formula]

\Re_{K/k}({\mathbb{P}}^{n}_{K})\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}{\mathbb{P}}^{r}_{k},\quad\mbox{where}\quad r=n\cdot|K:k|.

The key observation is the following.

Corollary 16
.

Let
P
be a Severi–Brauer variety over a field
k
. Let
K/k
be a field extension of degree
n
, and
L\subset P_{K}
a
twisted linear subvariety. Assume that the conjugates
\{L^{\sigma}:\sigma\in G\}
span
L
, and are linearly independent.

Then there is a twisted linear subbundle

R_{L}(P)\subset\Re_{K/k}(L)\times P

such that the coordinate projection
R_{L}(P)\to P
is birational.

Proof.
Let
k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension,
K^{\prime}:=K\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
, and

p_{0}\in P_{K^{\prime}}
a general
K^{\prime}
-point with
K^{\prime}/k^{\prime}
-conjugates

p_{0},\dots,p_{m}
.

By definition, a
k^{\prime}
-point of
\Re_{K/k}(L)
is the same as a

K^{\prime}
-point
p_{0}\in P_{K^{\prime}}
, which can be identified with
the set of its
K^{\prime}/k^{\prime}
-conjugates

p_{0},\dots,p_{n}
.

Let

R_{L}(P)\subset\Re_{K/k}(L)\times P
be the
twisted linear subbundle whose fiber over

p_{0}
is the span of
p_{0},\dots,p_{n}
.

Birationality of the coordinate projection
R_{L}(P)\to P
can be checked over
\bar{k}
, where Lemma
14
applies. ∎

Note that
L
and the fibers of
R_{L}(P)\to\Re_{K/k}(L)
have lower dimension than
P
.
The method of
[
Roq64
]
uses information about
them to
get results about
P
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
P
be a Severi–Brauer variety of dimension
n
over
k
, and

K/k
a separable field extension of degree
m+1<n+1
.
Assume that
(m+1,n+1)=1
.
Then

[formula]

\Re_{K/k}(P_{K})\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P\times{\mathbb{P}}^{mn}.

Proof. Let
\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)
be the Grassmannian parametrizing
m
-dimensional linear subspaces
L\subset P
, and
\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)\to\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)

the universal family parametrizing
m
-dimensional pointed linear subspaces
p\in L\subset P
. Sending
(p,L)\to\{p\}
shows that

\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)
is a
\operatorname{Grass}(m{-}1,{\mathbb{P}}^{n-1})
-bundle over
P
. Thus

[formula]

\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m(n-m)}.

Every fiber
F
of
\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)\to\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)
is a
Severi–Brauer variety of dimension
m
. The canonical class of
F
is

{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-m{-}1)
, and the restriction of the canonical class of
P
is

{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(-n{-}1)
. Thus if
(m+1,n+1)=1
then
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}(1)
is a line bundle on

, hence

[formula]

\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}.

Therefore

[formula]

\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m(n-m)}.

After these preliminaries, let

k^{\prime}/k
be a field extension,
K^{\prime}:=K\otimes_{k}k^{\prime}
, and

p_{0}\in P_{K^{\prime}}
a general
K^{\prime}
-point with
K^{\prime}/k^{\prime}
-conjugates

p_{0},\dots,p_{m}
. They span a linear subspace
L(p_{0})
of dimension
m
, which is defined over
k^{\prime}
. We can thus first choose

in
\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)(k^{\prime})
, and then the point
p_{0}
in

\operatorname{Univ}(m,P)\to\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)

\Re_{K/k}(P_{K})\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}\operatorname{Grass}(m,P)\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m(m+1)}\operatorname{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle bir}}{{\sim}}}P\times{\mathbb{P}}^{mn}.\qed

We have the following partial result without the relatively prime condition.

Lemma 19
.

Let
P,Q
be Severi–Brauer varieties over a field
k
. Then there are rational maps

P\otimes Q\dasharrow P^{\dim Q+1}
.

Proof. Geometrically, consider the Segre embedding

j:{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\lhook\joinrel\to{\mathbb{P}}^{N}
. Pick a spanning set of points
p_{0},\dots,p_{n}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{n}
and set
L_{i}:=j\bigl(\{p_{i}\}\times{\mathbb{P}}^{m}\bigr)
. Pick sections

s_{i}\in H^{0}\bigl(L_{i},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(r)|_{L_{i}}\bigr).

Let
W_{r}\subset H^{0}\bigl({\mathbb{P}}^{N},{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}^{N}}(r)\bigr)
be the subspace of those sections whose restriction to
L_{i}
is a multiple of
s_{i}
.
This defines a rational map
{\mathbb{P}}^{N}\dasharrow{\mathbb{P}}^{M}
which
induces the
r
-fold Veronese embedding on each copy of

{\mathbb{P}}^{n}\times\{q\}
for
q\in{\mathbb{P}}^{m}
.

Now let
P,Q
be a Severi–Brauer varieties,
p_{i}
a conjugate set of points and choose
r=\dim Q+1
. Although
{\mathcal{O}}_{P\otimes Q}(\dim Q+1)
is a twisted line bundle, its restriction to
L_{i}
is the anticanonical line bundle, hence we can pick
s_{0}

over the field
k(p_{0})
and let
s_{i}
be its conjugates. Then the above construction gives a rational map

P\otimes Q\to P^{\dim Q+1}
. ∎

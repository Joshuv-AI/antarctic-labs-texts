Comparative outcomes of medial and lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures: A systematic review and meta-analysis
Authors: MC Bharath, H Nagaraju, Elstin Anbu Raj S, Kiran Kumar Vedavyasa Acharya, Divya Balan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDistal third tibial fractures pose significant surgical challenges due to minimal soft-tissue coverage and a high risk of wound complications. Although both medial and lateral plating techniques are commonly used for fixation, the best surgical approach remains debated. This systematic review and meta-analysis aimed to compare the clinical outcomes and complication rates of medial and lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.MethodsThe study protocol was first registered in PROSPERO (CRD42025644545). A comprehensive literature search was conducted in PubMed, Scopus, and Embase from inception to June 2025 in accordance with PRISMA guidelines. Comparative studies involving adult patients with distal third tibial fractures treated with either medial or lateral plating were included. Risk of bias was assessed using standardized tools. Quantitative synthesis was performed when comparable outcomes were reported in at least two studies. Pooled effect estimates were calculated as odds ratios (ORs) or risk ratios (RRs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs).Results15 studies comprising 707 patients met the inclusion criteria. Meta-analysis demonstrated that medial plating was associated with significantly higher rates of hardware-related complications (OR 4.17; 95% CI 2.67–6.49), implant removal (OR 5.97; 95% CI 2.11–16.87), postoperative infection (RR 1.98; 95% CI 1.26–3.12), and overall soft-tissue complications (OR 3.25; 95% CI 1.84–5.75) compared with lateral plating. Wound dehiscence occurred less frequently with lateral plating (OR 0.24; 95% CI 0.13–0.45). No significant difference was observed between the two approaches in terms of malunion rates.ConclusionThe findings suggest that lateral plating may be associated with lower soft-tissue morbidity but comparable fracture alignment with medial plating. Since most included studies were observational and relatively small, the findings should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Treatment decisions should therefore remain individualized. Further well-designed randomized controlled trials (RCT)s are needed to confirm these findings.Keywords: Tibial fractures, Fracture fixation, Internal, Bone plates, Soft tissue injuries, Wound infection, Treatment outcome, Medial plating, Lateral plating

Introduction

Distal third tibial fractures present a common and challenging clinical problem due to the subcutaneous location of the bone, limited soft-tissue coverage, and compromised vascularity in this region.1 Because of the minimal soft-tissue coverage, such injuries are frequently associated with wound complications, delayed union, and a predisposition for infection.1

Open reduction and internal fixation (ORIF) with plate osteosynthesis remains a standard treatment modality for distal tibial fractures, offering stable fixation and facilitating early mobilization.2 The medial approach has been conventionally favoured because of its straightforward surgical access and the availability of an anatomic surface for plate placement. Conversely, the thin soft-tissue coverage on the medial aspect of the distal tibia predisposes patients to wound complications, hardware prominence, and infection.3

To overcome these limitations, lateral plating techniques have been developed. The lateral approach, often combined with minimally invasive plate osteosynthesis (MIPO), reduces soft-tissue disruption and improves healing conditions.4 Nonetheless, concerns persist regarding the adequacy of fixation, potential malalignment, and implant failure associated with biomechanical differences inherent to lateral fixation.

Although several comparative studies have evaluated medial and lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures, their findings remain inconsistent and functional outcomes have been reported using heterogeneous assessment tools. To date, no comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis has synthesized the available evidence comparing these two plating approaches with respect to both complication-related outcomes and functional recovery. Therefore, the present study aimed to systematically evaluate and quantitatively synthesize the available literature comparing medial and lateral plating techniques for distal third tibial fractures.

Methodology

Registration and protocol

The study followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines to present the findings. The study protocol is registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) under registration number: CRD42025644545. PubMed, Scopus and Embase were searched until June 2025 for English comparative studies on medial vs lateral plating for distal third tibia fractures, indicating both favourable and unfavourable outcomes. Two reviewers independently screened and extracted the data. Quality assessment was conducted using the Cochrane Risk of Bias (ROB) tool (ROB 1.0) and the Newcastle–Ottawa Scale (NOS). The data were analyzed using narrative and quantitative synthesis.

Objectives

The research question aimed to identify whether lateral plating provides superior functional outcomes and fewer complications compared with medial plating in the surgical management of distal tibia fractures. The research question was structured using the PICO framework. The study looked at adult patients with distal third tibia fractures. The intervention was fixation using lateral plating systems, while the comparator was fixation using medial plating systems. The outcomes of interest included functional outcomes and complication rates, specifically fixation failure, wound complications, need for re-surgery, fracture union, time to union, and malalignment.

Information sources and search strategy

A comprehensive search was done in the electronic databases PubMed, Scopus, and Embase until June 2025. The search strategy was designed to identify comparative studies evaluating medial vs lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures. The search terms comprised a combination of Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and free-text keywords related to the population, intervention, and comparator. Wherever required, Boolean operators (“AND”, “OR”) were used to combine search terms. The search strategy was adapted for each database. In addition, the references of included studies and review articles were screened to identify eligible studies. Only English language publications were considered. Conference abstracts, editorials, case reports, biomechanical studies, and cadaveric studies were omitted. A protocol-driven search strategy was provided for Supplementary Material 1.

Study selection and data extraction

Based on predefined criteria, studies were screened first by title and abstracts, followed by full-text articles. Two independent reviewers (BMC and NH) performed the study selection, and disagreements were resolved through discussion with the third reviewer (KKVA). A standardized data extraction form was used to collect data. One of the reviewers (BMC) obtained the data in the standardized extraction sheet, and the other reviewer (NH) validated it for accuracy. Discrepancies were resolved following discussion with the third reviewer (KKVA).

Risk of bias assessment

The methodological quality of the studies included was assessed by two reviewers. RCTs were evaluated with the Cochrane Risk of Bias tool (ROB 1.0), which assesses the following domains: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other sources of bias. Each domain was categorized as low risk, high risk, or unclear risk of bias according to the Cochrane Handbook recommendations. Non-randomized comparative studies were assessed using the NOS, which examines methodological integrity across three domains: selection of study groups, comparability of groups, and assessment of outcomes. A maximum of nine stars could be awarded. Studies were categorized as high quality (7–9 stars), moderate quality (4–6 stars), or low quality (0–3 stars). Discrepancies between reviewers were resolved by discussion and consensus. If a consensus was not reached, the third reviewer was consulted. Risk of bias assessments were summarized in tabular formats.

Outcome measures

Outcome measures were defined based on the definitions provided in the individual included studies. Hardware-related complications included implant prominence, irritation, or hardware failure requiring clinical attention or implant removal. Soft-tissue complications comprised wound-related issues such as delayed healing, skin necrosis, and soft-tissue irritation. Postoperative infection included both superficial and deep infections as defined by the respective studies. Wound dehiscence was defined as partial or complete disruption of the surgical wound. Malunion was defined as fracture healing with unacceptable alignment based on radiographic or clinical criteria. Where definitions varied across studies, outcomes were grouped under broader clinically relevant categories to allow for pooled analysis.

Data synthesis

Data from all the included studies were synthesized using narrative and quantitative approaches. Quantitative synthesis (meta-analysis) was undertaken when a minimum of two studies reported comparable outcome measures. Outcomes of interest included hardware-related complications, implant removal, postoperative infection, malunion, soft-tissue complications, and wound dehiscence. For each outcome, pooled effect estimates were calculated using the Mantel–Haenszel method in Review Manager (RevMan) version 5.4, and effect measures were expressed as ORs or RRs with corresponding 95% CIs. Heterogeneity among studies was assessed using the Chi-square (χ2) test and quantified using the I2 statistic and Tau2 statistic. The I2 statistic was interpreted as follows: <25% indicating no heterogeneity, 25–50% low heterogeneity, 50–75% moderate heterogeneity, and >75% high heterogeneity. A fixed-effect model was initially applied when heterogeneity was low; where significant heterogeneity was detected and could not be explained by moderator variables, a random-effects model was used. Studies reporting zero events in both treatment arms were excluded from the pooled analysis, while studies with zero events in one arm were retained. Subgroup analysis was performed based on study design (randomized vs non-randomized studies) to explore the consistency of findings. Publication bias was assessed using funnel plot asymmetry, given that more than ten studies were included in the meta-analysis. The pooled results were illustrated graphically using forest plots.

Results

Study selection

The comprehensive literature search identified 1210 records from electronic databases (EMBASE n = 518, PubMed n = 329, Scopus n = 363) and eight additional eligible records through citation searching. After excluding 469 duplicates, 741 records underwent screening based on title and abstract, of which 701 were excluded. Full texts of 40 articles were assessed for eligibility, all of which were sourced. After full-text review, 33 studies were excluded. In the end, 15 studies were deemed eligible in the systematic review, as summarized in the PRISMA 2020 flow diagram (Fig. 1).

Functional outcomes were reported in 10 of the 15 included studies. Hardware-related complications

Ten studies were considered in the quantitative synthesis of hardware-related complications, comprising 452 patients. Hardware-related complications occurred in 97 patients in the medial group and 28 patients in the lateral group. Pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly higher odds of hardware-related complications associated with medial plating (OR = 4.17; 95% CI: 2.67–6.49; p < 0.00001), with no heterogeneity observed (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 2). Subgroup analyses on study design showed consistent results across randomized and non-randomized studies, with no significant subgroup differences.

Forest plot comparing hardware-related complications between medial and lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.

Implant removal

Five studies are featured in the meta-analysis of implant removal, comprising 199 patients. Implant removal occurred more frequently in the medial group than in the lateral group (22 vs 3 events). Pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly higher odds of implant removal associated with medial plating (OR = 5.97; 95% CI: 2.11–16.87; p = 0.0008), showing no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 3). Subgroup analyses on study design resulted in consistent findings across randomized and non-randomized studies, with no significant subgroup differences.

Forest plot of implant removal in patients undergoing medial versus lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.

Postoperative infection

Fourteen studies were selected in the meta-analysis of postoperative infection, comprising 627 patients with available infection outcome data. Postoperative infection occurred in 52 patients in the medial group and in 26 patients in the lateral group. Pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly higher risk of infection associated with medial plating (RR = 1.98; 95% CI: 1.26–3.12; p = 0.003), showed no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 4). Subgroup analysis results in consistent findings across randomized and non-randomized studies, with no significant subgroup differences.

Forest plot comparing postoperative infection between medial and lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.

Malunion

Six studies are featured in the meta-analysis of malunion, comprising 309 patients. Malunion was reported in 25 patients in the medial group and 14 patients in the lateral group. Pooled analysis using a fixed-effects Mantel–Haenszel model demonstrated no statistically significant difference in the odds of malunion between medial and lateral plating (OR = 1.85; 95% CI: 0.95–3.63; p = 0.07) (Fig. 5). Low-to-moderate heterogeneity is observed among the included studies (χ2 = 7.96, df = 5; p = 0.16; I2 = 37%).

Forest plot of malunion in patients undergoing medial versus lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures

Soft-tissue complications

Nine studies were incorporated in the meta-analysis of soft-tissue complications, comprising 448 patients of whom 49 medial and 16 lateral patients. Pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly higher odds of soft-tissue complications associated with medial plating (OR = 3.25; 95% CI: 1.84–5.75; p < 0.0001), showed no heterogeneity (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 6). Subgroup analyses on study design showed consistent results across randomized and non-randomized studies, with no significant subgroup differences.

Forest plot of soft-tissue complications in patients undergoing medial versus lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.

Wound dehiscence

Eleven studies are featured in the meta-analysis of wound dehiscence, comprising 526 patients, of whom it occurred in ten patients in the lateral group and 46 patients in the medial group. Pooled analysis demonstrated a significantly lower odds of wound dehiscence with lateral plating (OR = 0.24; 95% CI: 0.13–0.45; p < 0.00001), with no heterogeneity observed (I2 = 0%) (Fig. 7). Subgroup analyses on study design showed consistent results across randomized and non-randomized studies, with no significant subgroup difference.

Forest plot of wound dehiscence in patients undergoing medial versus lateral plating for distal third tibial fractures.

Publication bias

Visual inspection of the funnel plot suggested slight asymmetry, with a relative paucity of smaller studies on the left side, indicating potential small-study effects or publication bias. However, most studies were distributed within the expected confidence limits, suggesting no strong evidence of substantial publication bias (Fig. 8).

Discussion

To the best of our knowledge, this first systematic review and meta-analysis specifically comparing medial and lateral plating techniques for distal third tibial fractures, providing pooled estimates of complication-related outcomes and highlighting important gaps in the current evidence base. Overall, functional recovery was largely comparable between medial and lateral plating techniques across the included studies. However, a directional trend was observed based on the scoring system used, with studies employing the AOFAS score generally reporting slightly better functional outcomes with medial plating, whereas studies assessing ankle range of motion demonstrated a modest advantage for lateral plating. OMAS-based assessments and other scoring systems showed largely comparable results between the two approaches. A quantitative meta-analysis of functional outcomes was not feasible due to substantial heterogeneity in outcome reporting across the included studies. The pooled analysis of complication-related outcomes showed that medial plating was associated with significantly higher rates of hardware-related complications, implant removal, infection, and overall soft-tissue complications compared to lateral plating. No significant difference was observed with respect to malunion.

Functional outcomes between different plating approaches appear largely comparable, with satisfactory ankle function and fracture union reported in most patients.11 Some studies have suggested slightly improved ankle mobility and earlier functional recovery with alternative plating approaches that minimize soft-tissue disruption, possibly due to better preservation of periosteal blood supply and reduced soft-tissue irritation.20 Conversely, other reports have demonstrated excellent or good functional outcomes with medial plating using AOFAS scoring systems, indicating that stable fixation and appropriate soft-tissue handling remain key determinants of postoperative functional recovery regardless of plate position.21 Overall, the existing literature suggests that both medial and lateral plating techniques can achieve satisfactory functional outcomes.

The increased incidence of hardware-related complications and implant removal following medial plating observed in this analysis is likely attributable to the subcutaneous location and limited soft-tissue envelope on the medial aspect of the distal one-third of the tibia. Prominent implants, local irritation, and wound breakdown are well-recognized complications of medial plating in this region. The significantly higher odds of implant removal in the medial plating group further support the clinical relevance of these findings, as implant removal often reflects patient discomfort, soft-tissue compromise, or implant-related symptoms rather than mechanical failure alone.22 Studies have emphasized that implant prominence and soft-tissue compromise are common causes of secondary procedures following medial plating, despite satisfactory fracture union.23 In contrast, lateral plating position situates implants beneath thicker muscular and aponeurotic layers, which may buffer mechanical irritation and improve soft-tissue tolerance.24 The increased risk of infection associated with medial plating was consistent across both randomized and non-randomized studies, strengthening the robustness of this observation.

Overall, soft-tissue complications were significantly more frequent in the medial plating group, reinforcing concerns regarding medial soft-tissue vulnerability.11 Conversely, wound dehiscence was significantly less common with lateral plating, suggesting a protective effect of the lateral approach on wound integrity. This finding is clinically important, as wound dehiscence may lead to deep infection, delayed rehabilitation, and the need for additional surgical procedures.

Regarding fracture alignment, no significant difference in malunion rates was observed between medial and lateral plating. This suggests that, when appropriately performed, both techniques can achieve satisfactory fracture reduction and alignment. This finding aligns with the existing literature that surgical technique, reduction quality, and adherence to fixation principles may be more critical determinants of alignment than plate position alone.25 Similar results have been reported by Paluvadi et al., who demonstrated favourable outcomes with minimally invasive plate osteosynthesis for distal tibial fractures, highlighting the benefits of preserving soft-tissue integrity and periosteal blood supply.26 Prior clinical and biomechanical studies have suggested that open posterolateral plating may allow accurate fracture reduction, provide biomechanically stable fixation, facilitate earlier progression to weight-bearing, and be coupled with a lower rate of wound complications, malunion, and implant removal compared with medial approaches.21 A recent comparative study evaluating plating vs intramedullary nailing for distal tibial fractures has also demonstrated that plate fixation remains an effective option for achieving stable alignment in distal metaphyseal fractures. However, the position of the plate can influence soft-tissue complications, as highlighted in the present analysis comparing medial and lateral plating.27 Although several pooled analyses demonstrated minimal statistical heterogeneity, this should not be interpreted as evidence of complete study similarity. The included studies varied considerably in fracture morphology, severity of soft-tissue injury, surgical exposure techniques, use of minimally invasive approaches, implant designs, surgeon experience, and postoperative rehabilitation protocols. Such clinical heterogeneity may not be fully captured by statistical heterogeneity measures alone. Therefore, the pooled estimates should be interpreted cautiously.

The strengths of our study include compliance with PRISMA guidelines, prospective protocol registration, inclusion of both randomized and non-randomized comparative studies, and comprehensive analysis of clinically relevant complications. Subgroup analyses on study design demonstrated consistent findings, enhancing confidence in the results.

This study has several limitations. First, several of the included studies had relatively small sample sizes, which may limit the statistical power of individual comparisons. Second, there was geographic clustering of studies, with a substantial proportion originating from India, which may affect the generalizability of the results to other healthcare settings and patient populations. Third, the included studies represented a heterogeneous spectrum of distal tibial fractures with variability in fracture classification, open versus closed injury patterns, surgical timing, plate positioning techniques, and postoperative rehabilitation protocols may have contributed to differences in reported outcomes across studies. This clinical heterogeneity may influence complication rates and functional outcomes limiting the comparability of pooled estimates and reducing the strength of inferences that can be drawn from the meta-analysis. Definitive conclusions regarding the superiority of one plating technique over the other cannot be established. Additionally, only a limited number of RCTs were available, with a high risk of bias on methodological assessment. Most of the studies were observational in design, which increases the potential for confounding and selection bias. Finally, functional outcomes were not consistently reported across studies, and standardized outcome measures were lacking, limiting the ability to perform a quantitative synthesis of functional results. Funnel plot analysis suggested mild asymmetry, which may indicate potential small-study effects or publication bias. Overall, the certainty of evidence for the reported outcomes is low, given the predominance of observational studies, methodological limitations of randomized trials, and substantial clinical heterogeneity.

This study highlights a significant research gap in the current literature, emphasizing the need for well-designed RCT with standardized outcome measures, including validated functional scores and long-term follow-up. Comparative studies evaluating minimally invasive lateral plating vs modern medial plating techniques may further clarify the optimal fixation strategy.

Conclusion

This systematic review and meta-analysis suggest that, in the management of distal third tibial fractures, lateral plating may be associated with a lower risk of hardware-related complications, implant removal, infection, and overall soft-tissue complications. Functional outcomes were generally comparable between medial and lateral plating techniques, although heterogeneity in outcome measures limited definitive comparison. The findings should be interpreted with caution, as most included studies were non-randomized and involved relatively small sample sizes. The findings should be considered hypothesis-generating rather than definitive. Treatment decisions should therefore remain individualized based on fracture characteristics and patient factors. Further well-designed RCTs are required to confirm these findings and better define optimal fixation strategies.

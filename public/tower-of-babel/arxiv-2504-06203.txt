Factorizing Defects from Generalized Pinning Fields
Fedor K. Popov 1 and Yifan Wang 2

Abstract
We introduce generalized pinning fields in conformal field theory that model a large class of critical impurities at large distance, enriching the familiar universality classes. We provide a rigorous definition of such defects as certain unbounded operators on the Hilbert space and prove that when inserted on codimension-one surfaces they factorize the spacetime into two halves. The factorization channels are further constrained by symmetries in the bulk. As a corollary, we solve such critical impurities in the 2d minimal models and establish the factorization phenomena previously observed for localized mass deformations in the 3d O ⁡ ( N ) {\rm O}(N) model.



I
Introduction and Summary

Extended operators defined on submanifolds of the spacetime constitute a fundamental component of modern quantum field theory (QFT), shaping our understanding of symmetry principles, phases of matter and strong coupling phenomena.
When extended in time, they are also known as defects – such as impurities and boundaries – which exhibit rich dynamics, particularly in gapless bulk systems described by conformal field theories (CFTs).
In cases where the CFT admits a gravity dual, defects correspond to strings, branes and nontrivial backreacted geometries, providing a valuable portal to investigate systematically such nonperturbative objects in quantum gravity. Hence it is essential to determine what defects and extended operators are admissible in CFT (and QFT more broadly) and to understand their corresponding dynamics.

To this end, powerful monotonicity theorems have been established for defect renormalization group (RG) flows

;
Friedan and Konechny 2004
;

;
Casini et al. 2019
;

;

;
Wang 2022
;
Cuomo et al. 2022a
;
Shachar et al. 2023
;
Casini et al. 2023
, ensuring that certain defects remain unscreened at large distances, while additional constraints from generalized symmetries are beginning to be explored
Konechny 2020
;
Thorngren and Wang 2021
;
Aharony et al. 2023a
;
Choi et al. 2023
;
Aharony et al. 2023b
;
Bhardwaj et al. 2024
;
Choi et al. 2024
;
Antinucci et al. 2024
. Furthermore, conformal bootstrap techniques have been developed to directly probe the operator algebra data at the defect fixed point
Billò et al. 2016
, while the study of defect fusion and the Casimir effect offers new perspectives on universal defect data via the framework of effective field theory
Diatlyk et al. 2024a
;
Diatlyk et al. 2024b
;
Kravchuk et al. 2024
;
Cuomo et al. 2024

Here we introduce a large family of defects with the following simple UV definition in an arbitrary CFT in
d\geq 2
,

\displaystyle{\cal D}_{h}({{\mathcal{O}}})\equiv\left[e^{h\hat{\mathcal{O}}}\right]_{\rm ren}\,,\quad\hat{\mathcal{O}}\equiv\int_{\Sigma_{p}}{\mathcal{O}}(x)\,,

p

\Sigma_{p}=S^{p}

\displaystyle{\cal D}({{\mathcal{O}}})\equiv\lim_{h\to\infty}{\cal D}_{h}({{\mathcal{O}}})\,.

(I.2)

This is because the monotonicity theorems requires the defect entropy (for
p

These defects generalize the well-studied pinning field defects in the Ising and general

Krishnan and Metlitski 2023

In this work, we focus on codimension-one (
i.e.

p=d-1
) generalized pinning field defects and establish this factorization property in great generality. In particular, this implies that there is no energy transmission across the interface in the IR.

Intuitively, this is because the conformal pinning defect (
I.2
) will take the form of an un-normalized projector on the CFT rigged Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
(see around (
II.4
)),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}({\mathcal{O}})=\bigoplus_{\alpha}|{\alpha}\rangle\langle{\alpha}|\,,

(I.3)

by projecting to the highest or lowest eigenvalues of the operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
depending on the sign of

|{\alpha}\rangle

The eigenstates

are scalars with respect to
{\rm SO}(d-1)
and hence do not support a non-vanishing two-point function of the stress energy tensor

However one should be cautious with the above heuristic reasoning, since the conformal boundary conditions do not define normalizable states in
{\mathcal{H}}
and relatedly
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
may not be a healthy bounded operator on
{\mathcal{H}}
. In the following we will formalize these points using results from the spectral theory of unbounded operators on Hilbert spaces, particularly the Gelfand triple construction. This framework allows us to precisely define generalized pinning field defects and prove their IR factorizations in Section
II
.

The factorization property enables us to determine the long-distance dynamics of such defects by leveraging the full spectrum of conformal boundary conditions, thereby rendering the problem significantly more tractable. For example, as we discuss in Section
IV.2
, this elucidates the conjectured factorization of the
3d

{\rm O}(N)
CFT induced by the
{\rm O}(N)
-symmetric surface mass term in terms of known boundary conditions and also yields nontrivial predictions for more general surface mass deformations.
As another example, the conformal boundary conditions of Virasoro minimal models in
2d
were classified long time ago. However limited results are available for conformal (non-topological) line defects except for those in the Ising CFT. In Section
IV.1
, we analyze a large class of
2d
pinning defects defined as in (
I.1

g

In general, the symmetries of the CFT restrict the possible factorization channels in (
I.3
). In particular, anomalies of the symmetries preserved by the defect will force degeneracies in the direct sum, as we discuss Section
III
, and they play an important role in solving the pinning defects in
2d
minimal models with examples in Section

As mentioned before, these generalized pinning defects are ubiquitous in CFT and thus it would be interesting to study their applications, as probes of strongly coupled systems. For instance, quantum chromodynamics (QCD) with a sufficiently large number of massless fermion flavors (within the conformal window) admit nontrivial Caswell-Banks-Zaks type fixed points

;
Banks and Zaks 1982

;
Gorbenko et al. 2018

Lee 2021

Furthermore, when viewed as an extended operator at fixed time, generalized pinning defects have been employed to model local quantum channels, capturing the effects of local decoherence and weak measurement on quantum critical states (see
e.g.

Lee et al. 2023
;
Zou et al. 2023

Finally, in CFTs with holographic duals, it would be interesting to investigate the dual description of codimension-one pinning defects, which may correspond to factorizing geometries involving new end-of-the-world branes
Karch and Randall 2001a
;
Karch and Randall 2001b
;
DeWolfe et al. 2002
;
Takayanagi 2011
;
Fujita et al. 2011

The generalized pinning defects considered here are defined by defect RG flows from the trivial defect triggered by bulk local scalar operators. Immediate generalizations include such flows on more general topological defects, and triggered by more general operators, such as spinning local operators and even operators attached to nontrivial topological lines (see previous related works
Bachas and Gaberdiel 2004
;
Runkel 2008

Bachas and Monnier 2010

Kormos et al. 2009

Manolopoulos and Runkel 2010

Gaiotto et al. 2021
;
Tavares et al. 2024
in
2d
). A further generalization amounts to considering RG flows on a slab, a fattened version of our setup here, and reduce to our setup when the intermediate phase in the slab is gapped in the IR. It will be interesting to understand general properties of such flows and the long distance behavior of the resulting defects.

II
Factorization

The main goal of this section is to prove the factorization property for
generic

Let us comment on the extensions to (
II.1
) when the pinning flow preserves additional global symmetries. This happens when
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
commutes with the corresponding topological defects. Correspondingly, the spectrum of
\hat{\mathcal{O}}

has degeneracies due to the symmetry action (fusion with the topological defects). When the symmetry preserved is finite, this leads to a finite direct sum of factorized interfaces in the IR for the pinning defect,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\sum_{\alpha}|{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\rangle\langle\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}|\,,

(II.26)

where the individual Cardy states
|{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}\rangle
furnish a representation of the symmetry. We will see concrete examples in Section
IV
.

If the symmetry is continuous, we have instead an integral over the factorization channels,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}=\int d{\alpha}|{\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}}\rangle\langle\mathcal{B}_{\alpha}|\,,

(II.27)

for a spectral measure
d{\alpha}
invariant under the symmetry. For example if
{\alpha}
labels the elements of a continuous group symmetry
G
,
d{\alpha}
is proportional to the Haar measure on
G
. In the case of spontaneous
G
-symmetry breaking on the interface, this measure arises from the path integral over the Goldstone bosons localized at the interface. For
3d
, there is no spontaneous continuous symmetry breaking
Cuomo and Zhang 2024
, but this form of factorization (
II.27
) persists (see Section
IV.2
).

The factorized form of the pinning defect derived here makes their IR fusion product
\circ
defined in
Diatlyk et al. 2024a
(see also
Bachas and Brunner 2008
;
Bachas et al. 2013
;
Söderberg 2021
;
Söderberg Rousu 2023
;
Diatlyk et al. 2024b
;
Kravchuk et al. 2024
;
Cuomo et al. 2024
) more transparent. In particular, the defect self-fusion follows an idempotent rule,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{D}}\circ{\mathcal{D}}(\Sigma)={\mathcal{S}}(\Sigma){\mathcal{D}}(\Sigma)\,,

(II.28)

where
\Sigma
is the defect worldvolume and
{\mathcal{S}}
denotes the fusion coefficient theory (CFT) on

{\mathcal{S}}(\Sigma)
is its partition function. Here we have used that the boundary states

in the factorization of
{\mathcal{D}}
are related by symmetries and the Casimir energy densities

for a pair of such boundaries defined by

\displaystyle\langle{\mathcal{B}}_{\alpha}|e^{-zP_{d}}|{\mathcal{B}}_{\beta}\rangle\xrightarrow{z\to 0}e^{-{A\over z^{d-1}}{\mathcal{E}}_{{\alpha}{\beta}}}\,,

(II.29)

with IR regulator

(II.30)

Let us note that our approach shares similarities with Hamiltonian truncation (
e.g.
truncated conformal space approach or TCSA)

. This distinction arises from the fact that we are primarily interested in the instantaneous action of the perturbation rather than the deformation of the whole spectrum in such theories. Nevertheless, if the perturbation induces a gap in the bulk, the eigenstates of
\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}
can be used as a variational ansatz to approximate the ground state of the interacting theory

;

is important to balance the perturbation

III

The factorization channels that describe the pinning defect in the IR are further constrained by generalized global symmetries in the bulk CFT. In modern understanding, while local operators transform as ordinary charges of standard 0-form symmetry, extended defects transform in higher representations of the generalized symmetries (see

To make the following discussion concrete and self-contained, we will focus on

IV.1
Pinning Flows in 2d Virasoro minimal model CFT

\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\phi}

whose domain is dense in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\phi}
and whose matrix elements coincide with the form as in (
B.3
).

Now let us consider the case where two conformal families
\left\{\phi_{1,2}\right\}
are involved in the definition of the form (
B.2
). The generalization to cases involving more conformal families can be done analogously. For this purpose it suffices to consider the following state
|\phi_{f}\rangle\equiv c_{1}|\phi_{1}(x)\rangle+c_{2}|\phi_{2}(y)\rangle
. The corresponding sesquilinear form reads,

\displaystyle(\phi_{f},\phi_{f})=c_{11{\mathcal{O}}}\left|c_{1}\right|^{2}\braket{\phi_{1}(x)|\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\phi_{1}}|\phi_{1}(x)}+c_{22{\mathcal{O}}}\left|c_{2}\right|^{2}\braket{\phi_{2}(y)|\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}_{\phi_{2}}|\phi_{2}(y)}

(B.12)

\displaystyle+\frac{c_{12{\mathcal{O}}}}{\left(|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2}+(x_{d}+y_{d})^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta_{2}-\Delta_{\mathcal{O}}}{2}}}\int d^{d-1}\vec{z}\left[\left|\frac{c_{1}}{\left((\vec{x}-\vec{z})^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta-\Delta_{2}}{2}}}+\frac{c_{2}}{\left((\vec{x}-\vec{z})^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\Delta_{1}+\Delta-\Delta_{2}}{2}}}\right|^{2}-\left(-\leftrightarrow+\right)\right]\,.

Since the above can be written as a sum of semi-bounded closed quadratic forms and each can be represented by a unique self-adjoint operator by Kato’s theorem, we have identified the self-adjoint operator
\hat{\mathcal{O}}_{\Delta}
acting on two conformal families. Now this approach can be extended to
{\mathcal{H}}_{\Delta}
which contains a finite number of conformal families.

Appendix C
Establishing the trivial representation

Given a Gelfand triple
\Phi_{\mathcal{D}}\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\Phi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}
associated with the CFT Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
, and let
\ket{\vec{x}}\in\Phi^{\prime}_{\mathcal{D}}
be elements obeying the following actions of the conformal subalgebra,

\displaystyle P_{i}|\vec{x}\rangle=i\partial_{i}\ket{\vec{x}}\,,\quad D|\vec{x}\rangle=x_{i}\partial_{i}\ket{\vec{x}}\,,\quad K_{i}|\vec{x}\rangle=i(2x_{i}x_{j}\partial_{j}-x_{j}x_{j}\partial_{i})\ket{\vec{x}}\,,\quad M_{ij}|\vec{x}\rangle=i(x_{i}\partial_{j}-x_{j}\partial_{i})\ket{\vec{x}}\,,

(C.1)

we prove below that these states, when represented in terms of bulk local operators, as in

\displaystyle|\vec{x}\rangle=\sum_{a}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{d}}d^{d}y\,f_{a}(\vec{x},y)\,\phi_{a}(y)|0\rangle\,,

(C.2)

for an orthonormal basis of primary operators
\phi_{a}(x)
, are linear combinations of Ishibashi states. In particular, the coefficient function
f_{a}(\vec{x},y)
is only nonzero if
\phi_{a}
is a scalar primary, in which case, up to an overall constant,

\displaystyle f_{a}(\vec{x},y)=y_{d}^{\Delta_{a}-d}\,.

(C.3)

Without loss of generality, we can focus on a single primary operator
\phi_{a}
in (
C.2
) and thus will drop the subscript
a
below. We present the details below for scalar
\phi_{a}
only as the case of spinning operators is very analogous.

From the action of
P_{i}
in (
C.1
) and (
C.2
), we have

\displaystyle P_{i}|\vec{x}\rangle=i\int d^{d}yf(\vec{x},y)\partial_{i}\phi(y)|0\rangle=-i\int d^{d}y\,\partial^{y}_{i}f(\vec{x},y)\phi(y)|0\rangle=i\partial^{x}_{i}\int d^{d}yf(\vec{x},y)\phi(y)|0\rangle\,,

(C.4)

which implies

\displaystyle\int d^{d}y(\partial^{x}_{i}+\partial^{y}_{i})f(\vec{x},y)\phi(x)|0\rangle=0~\Rightarrow~f(\vec{x},y)=g(\vec{x}-\vec{y},y_{d})\,,

(C.5)

where we have used the linear independent of
\phi(x)
. In a similar way, from the actions of rotation
M_{ij}
and dilation
D
, we can further constrain the coefficient function
f(\vec{x},y)
to be

\displaystyle f(\vec{x},y)=y_{d}^{\Delta-d}h\left(|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|\over y_{d}\right)\,.

(C.6)

Finally, the action of the special conformal transformation
K_{i}
in (
C.1
) requires,

\displaystyle\left(\left(2x_{i}x_{j}\partial^{x}_{j}-x^{2}\partial^{x}_{i}\right)+\left(2y_{i}y_{j}\partial^{y}_{j}-y^{2}\partial^{y}_{i}\right)+2y_{i}(y_{d}\partial_{d}^{y}+d-\Delta_{a})-y_{d}^{2}\partial_{i}^{y}\right)\left[y_{d}^{\Delta_{a}-d}h\left(|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|\over y_{d}\right)\right]=0\,,

(C.7)

which forces
h
to be a constant. The desired result (
C.3
) then follows.

Appendix D
Proving the weak convergence to zero state by dilation

Here we prove that for an arbitrary scalar state
|\psi\rangle\in{\mathcal{H}}
, the following weak limit is trivial,

\displaystyle\underset{b\to\infty}{\operatorname{w-lim}}\,U_{b}\ket{\psi}=0\,,

(D.1)

where
U_{b}=e^{bD}
is the unitary operator for dilation.
Without loss of generality, we can assume that

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}_{+}}d^{d}x\,f(x)\phi(x)|0\rangle\,,

(D.2)

namely
|\psi\rangle
is constructed from a single conformal family for a scalar primary operator
\phi(x)
of dimension
\Delta
.
Shifting the quantization surface by
{\delta}
in the
x_{d}
direction and then using reflection positivity together with the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\forall\,x_{d}\geq{\delta}\geq 0:\quad\left|\braket{\phi(x)|\psi}\right|^{2}\leq\frac{{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}(2t)}{(2(x_{d}-{\delta}))^{2\Delta}}\,,\quad{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}({\delta})\equiv\braket{\psi|e^{{\delta}P_{d}}|\psi}\geq 0\,.

(D.3)

By setting
{\delta}=x_{d}/2
, this produces the following upper bound on the overlap by the amplitude
{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}(x_{d})
,

\displaystyle\left|\braket{\phi(x)|\psi}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}(x_{d})}}{x_{d}^{\Delta}}~~\Rightarrow~~\left|\braket{\phi(x)|U_{b}\psi}\right|\leq\frac{\sqrt{{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}(e^{b}x_{d})}}{x_{d}^{\Delta}}\,,

(D.4)

and the second inequality follows from dilation symmetry.
Hence to establish (
D.1
) it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{{\delta}\to\infty}{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}({\delta})=0\,,

(D.5)

which we derive below. To this end, we note that, explicitly

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}({\delta})=\int_{x_{d},y_{d}\geq 0}d^{d}x\,d^{d}y\,\frac{f(\vec{x},x_{d})\,f^{*}(\vec{y},y_{d})}{\left(\left(x_{d}+y_{d}+{\delta}\right)^{2}+|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2}\right)^{\Delta}}\,,

(D.6)

which can be bounded by splitting the integration regions as below,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}({\delta})\leq\int_{|x|{\rm or}|y|\geq M}d^{d}x\,d^{d}y\,\frac{f(\vec{x},x_{d})\,f^{*}(\vec{y},y_{d})}{\left(\left(x_{d}+y_{d}\right)^{2}+|\vec{x}-\vec{y}|^{2}\right)^{\Delta}}+{\delta}^{-2\Delta}\left|\int_{|x|\leq M}d^{d}x\,f(x_{i},x_{d})\right|^{2}\,.

(D.7)

Since
|\psi|^{2}={\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}(0)
is finite by assumption, the first contribution on the RHS above can be made arbitrarily small by choosing sufficiently large
M
and the second contribution can then be arbitrarily suppressed (for fixed M) by sufficiently large
{\delta}
. In other words, for any
\epsilon>0
, we can choose
M_{\epsilon}
and
{\delta}_{\epsilon}
such that
{\mathcal{A}}_{\psi}({\delta})<\epsilon
for all
{\delta}>{\delta}_{\epsilon}
, thus we have proved (
D.5
).

Appendix E
Pinning defects in the free field theories

Pinning defects in free theories tend to have exotic (
e.g.
run-away) behavior in the IR
Cuomo et al. 2022b
;
Cuomo et al. 2022c
;
Cuomo and Zhang 2024
. Here we consider the case
of a codimension-one pinning defect in the free scalar field theory,

\displaystyle S=\int d^{d}{x}\,\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{\mu}\phi\right)^{2},\quad\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}=\int d^{d-1}\vec{x}\,\phi(x,x_{d}=0)\,,

(E.1)

and elaborate on its non-factorizing IR behavior in our framework as described in the main text.

This defect already exposes a problem at the level of the matrix elements for
\hat{\mathcal{O}}
(
B.1
). In fact, the following integral

\displaystyle\langle\phi^{2}(x)|\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}|\phi(y)\rangle=2\int d^{d-1}\vec{z}\,\frac{1}{(x-y)^{d-2}}\frac{1}{\left(|\vec{x}-\vec{z}|^{2}+x_{d}^{2}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}},

(E.2)

suffers from IR divergence. This divergence is related to the presence of vacuum moduli in the free scalar field (which fix the boundary condition at infinity). One could resolve this issue by performing an appropriate subtraction of these contributions, but doing so would render the operator
\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}
non-self-adjoint.

A possible way to circumvent this problem is to study the defect on the cylinder
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\times{\rm S}^{d-1}
, where we define

\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}=\int dS^{d-1}\phi(\vec{x},\tau=0)\,.

For explicitness let us focus on
3d
(the extension to general
d
is straightforward). By canonical quantization,

\displaystyle\phi(\vec{x},\tau)=\sum_{l=0}^{\infty}\sum\limits^{l}_{m=-l}Y_{l,m}(\vec{x})\frac{\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{m,l}e^{\omega_{l}\tau}+\hat{a}_{m,l}e^{-\omega_{l}\tau}}{\sqrt{2\omega_{l}}}\,,\quad\left[\hat{a}_{l,m},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{l^{\prime},m^{\prime}}\right]=\delta_{ll^{\prime}}\delta_{mm^{\prime}}\,,\quad\omega_{l}=l+\frac{1}{2}\,,

(E.3)

where
Y_{l,m}
denotes the
{\rm S}^{2}
spherical harmonics,
we have

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathcal{O}}}=\hat{a}_{0}+\hat{a}_{0}^{\dagger}\,,

(E.4)

which commutes with all other operators
\hat{a}_{l,m}
and whose spectral decomposition is given by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{O}}=\int d\lambda\prod_{l>0,|m|\leq l}d\alpha_{l,m}\,\lambda|\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}\rangle\langle\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}|\,,\quad\hat{\mathcal{O}}|\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}\rangle=\lambda|\lambda\,,\alpha_{l,m}\rangle\,,\quad\hat{a}_{l,m}|\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}\rangle=\alpha_{l,m}|\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}\rangle\,.

(E.5)

Thus, for any element
\ket{\psi}\in\mathcal{H}
, by completeness, we have

\displaystyle\braket{\psi|\psi}=\int d\lambda\,\prod_{l>0,|m|\leq l}d\alpha_{l,m}\,\braket{\psi|\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}}\braket{\lambda,\alpha_{l,m}|\psi}.

(E.6)

Since the measure of
\lambda
is flat, we have that the weak limit of
\ket{\lambda}
is zero,

\displaystyle\underset{\lambda\to\infty}{\operatorname{w-lim}}\,\ket{\lambda}=0\,,

(E.7)

which produces an ill-defined non-conformal limit (correlation functions with
\phi(x)
diverge as
x_{d}\to\infty
) and the lack of IR factorization. Let us stress that, in comparison to the general discussion in (
II.10
), for this particular case, any choice of
\ket{e}\in{\mathcal{H}}
would never give us the flat measure (due to the flat moduli preserved by the defect). Consequently, we have to enlarge the range of integration in the spectral decomposition from finite range to infinite, which produces the above exotic behavior.

Appendix F
The ill-defined defect “one-point” functions

A careful reader might note that, from the definition of Ishibashi (and defect) state via the weak limit (see around (
II.5
)), some correlation functions are ill-defined in the presence of a defect, even for normalizable states. This is not surprising, since we know that Ishibashi states are non-normalizable, and by the Riesz representation theorem
Kolmogorov and Fomin 1957
we must conclude that correlation functions involving Ishibashi states cannot be defined for all states in the Hilbert space
{\mathcal{H}}
. Here we give a simple example in the N-S quantization
Rychkov 2016
since this is our choice of frame in the main text. We will also illustrate the same phenomena in the more familiar radial quantization.

Let us consider a scalar primary operator
\phi
with scaling dimension
\Delta
, which satisfies the following two-point function and Ishibashi state condition

\displaystyle\braket{\phi(x)\phi(y)}=\frac{1}{(x-y)^{2\Delta}}\,,\quad\langle\!\langle\phi|\phi(x)\rangle=\frac{1}{x_{d}^{\Delta}}\,.

(F.1)

We define the following state

\displaystyle|F_{\phi}\rangle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\phi\left(x_{i}=0,x_{d}=\left(n\log n\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)|0\rangle\,.

(F.2)

which is normalizable. Explicitly, the norm is given by a series with positive terms,

\displaystyle\langle F_{\phi}|F_{\phi}\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\left(\left(n\log n\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}+\left(m\log m\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)^{2\Delta}}\,,

(F.3)

and we can bound its partial sum as below,

\displaystyle\sum_{n,m=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\left(\left(n\log n\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}+\left(m\log m\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)^{2\Delta}}=\sum^{N}_{n=2}\sum^{n}_{k=2}\frac{2}{\left(\left(n\log n\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}+\left(k\log k\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)^{2\Delta}}

(F.4)

\displaystyle<\sum^{N}_{n=2}\frac{2n}{n^{2}\log^{2}n}<\frac{1}{\log^{2}2}+\int\limits^{N+1}_{2}\frac{2dx}{x\log^{2}x}=\frac{1}{\log^{2}2}+\frac{2}{\log 2}-\frac{2}{\log(N+1)}\,.

Consequently the series absolutely converges,

\displaystyle\langle F_{\phi}|F_{\phi}\rangle=\lim_{N\to\infty}\sum_{n,m=2}^{N}\frac{1}{\left(\left(n\log n\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}+\left(m\log m\right)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)^{2\Delta}}<\frac{1}{\log^{2}2}+\frac{2}{\log 2}\,.

(F.5)

However, the overlap of (
F.2
) with the Ishibashi state is

\displaystyle\langle\!\langle\phi|F_{\phi}\rangle=\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\frac{1}{\left((n\log n)^{\frac{1}{\Delta}}\right)^{\Delta}}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=2}\frac{1}{n\log n}\,,

(F.6)

and does not converge since the partial sum is unbounded,

\displaystyle\sum^{N}_{n=2}\frac{1}{n\log n}\geq\sum^{N}_{n=2}\int\limits^{n+1}_{n}\frac{dx}{x\log x}=\int\limits^{N+1}_{2}\frac{dx}{x\log x}=\log\log\left(N+1\right)-\log\log 2\,.

(F.7)

For completeness, let us provide another example in the spherical frame using radial quantization.
Let us consider a primary scalar state
\ket{\phi}
on
S^{d-1}
. In this case the Ishibashi state is
Nakayama and Ooguri 2015
;
Diatlyk et al. 2024b
,

\displaystyle|{\phi}\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\kappa_{n}^{\phi}(P^{2})^{n}\ket{\phi}\,,\quad\kappa_{n}^{\phi}={2^{-2n}\over n!(\Delta+1-{d\over 2})_{n}}\,,

(F.8)

where
(a)_{n}\equiv\Gamma(n+a)/\Gamma(a)
is the Pochhammer symbol. We take the following state with norm

\displaystyle\ket{\hat{\phi}}=\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{\alpha_{n}}{\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}}(P^{2})^{n}\ket{\phi}\,,\quad\braket{\hat{\phi}|\hat{\phi}}=\sum^{\infty}_{n=0}\left|\alpha_{n}\right|^{2}\,,

(F.9)

where

\displaystyle{\mathcal{N}}_{n}\equiv\langle\phi|(K^{2})^{n}(P^{2})^{n}|\phi\rangle=16^{n}n!\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)_{n}(\Delta)_{n}\left(-\frac{d}{2}+\Delta+1\right)_{n}\,.

(F.10)

On the other hand, the overlap with the Ishibashi state is

\displaystyle\langle\hat{\phi}|\phi\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}^{*}\kappa_{n}\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}\,.

(F.11)

Now if we pick
\alpha_{n}=\frac{1}{n}
in (
F.9
), the state
\ket{\hat{\phi}}
is clearly normalizable. However the overlap (
F.11
) diverges because

\displaystyle\kappa_{n}\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}=n^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\Gamma\left(-\frac{d}{2}+\Delta+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)\Gamma(\Delta)}}+{\mathcal{O}}\left(n^{-1}\right)\right)

(F.12)

at large
n
. Note that for
2d
, one obtains the familiar result that
\kappa_{n}\sqrt{{\mathcal{N}}_{n}}=1
.

Enhanced quantum magnetometry with a laser-written integrated photonic diamond chip
Yanzhao Guo, Giulio Coccia, Vinaya Kumar Kavatamane, Argyro N. Giakoumaki, Anton N. Vetlugin, Roberta Ramponi, Cesare Soci, Paul E. Barclay, John P. Hadden, Anthony J. Bennett, Shane M. Eaton

Abstract
An ensemble of negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy centers in diamond can act as a precise quantum sensor even under ambient conditions. In particular, to optimize thier sensitivity, it is crucial to increase the number of spins sampled and maximize their coupling to the detection system, without degrading their spin properties. In this paper, we demonstrate enhanced quantum magnetometry via a high-quality buried laser-written waveguide in diamond with a 4.5 ppm density of nitrogen-vacancy centers. We show that the waveguide-coupled nitrogen-vacancy centers exhibit comparable spin coherence properties as that of nitrogen-vacancy centers in pristine diamond using time-domain optically detected magnetic resonance spectroscopy. Waveguide-enhanced magnetic field sensing is demonstrated in a fiber-coupled integrated photonic chip, where probing an increased volume of high-density spins results in 63 p 63\text{\,}\mathrm{p} of DC-magnetic field sensitivity of and 20 p 20\text{\,}\mathrm{p} of AC magnetic field sensitivity. This on-chip sensor realizes at least an order of magnitude improvement in sensitivity compared to the conventional confocal detection setup, paving the way for microscale sensing with nitrogen-vacancy ensembles.



I
Introduction

Quantum sensing with negatively charged nitrogen-vacancy centers (NVs) in diamond has attracted broad interest in the last two decades
Balasubramanian et al. 2008
;
Barry et al. 2020
. Due to its asymmetric atomic structure
Maze et al. 2011
, the NV is highly sensitive to weak external influences like temperature
Acosta et al. 2010
, pressure
Doherty et al. 2014
;
Mittiga et al. 2018
, electric field
Dolde et al. 2011
and magnetic field
Rondin et al. 2014
at the nanoscale
Balasubramanian et al. 2008
. Meanwhile, their long spin coherence time
Balasubramanian et al. 2009
allows NV-based quantum sensors to achieve remarkable sensitivity
Barry et al. 2020
. However, their relatively low optical excitation efficiency and finite photon collection efficiency have limited their sensitivity in practice
Barry et al. 2020
;
Doherty et al. 2013
. Although various submicron-integrated photonic structures for single NV have been studied to enhance the photon collection rate
Pachlatko et al. 2024
;
Wang et al. 2022
, few can easily be applied to the ensemble of NVs, and most would degrade the coherence properties of NVs. In particular, given the sensitivity scales inversely with the square root of detected signal intensity
Barry et al. 2020
;
Rondin et al. 2014
, it is crucial to efficiently excite and collect from a large volume of ensemble NVs with good spin coherence properties.

Previous work on NV ensemble sensing was based on the millimeter size of diamond devices with sub-nanotesla sensitivities. For example, a light-trapping diamond waveguide geometry
Clevenson et al. 2015
improves the probed number of NVs via increasing optical depth in the mm-sized bulk diamond, or the integration of optical fiber tip with a millimeter size of diamond
Patel et al. 2020
. There are few studies on the scaling of these sensors towards the micron scale
Wolf et al. 2015
. One solution could be to couple an ensemble of NVs into a fiber-integrated photonic structure, with confined micron-scale sensing resolution, for example, the waveguide integrated NVs in diamond
Hoese et al. 2021
;
Hadden et al. 2018
. Recently, laser writing has been demonstrated as a powerful tool for fabricating micron photonic circuits with integrated quantum emitters from single to ensemble level
Eaton et al. 2019
;
Guo et al. 2024
. Moreover, laser-written waveguide-integrated NVs (WGINVs ) have been shown to have comparable spin coherence properties to native NVs in diamond
Guo et al. 2024
.

In this paper, we demonstrate an on-chip micron sensor with enhanced sensitivity via a laser-written waveguide on a diamond containing a 4.5 ppm density of NVs. The high-quality buried waveguides (type II geometry) are fabricated by femtosecond laser writing, and exhibit insertion loss below
12\text{\,}\mathrm{d}\mathrm{B}
at
635\text{\,}\mathrm{nm}
. We characterize the spectrum and the spin coherence properties of NVs in the waveguide and pristine regions, showing that waveguide fabrication does not degrade their photoluminescence (PL) emission or spin coherence time. A fiber-waveguide-fiber configuration is then demonstrated to show enhanced sensing of a DC magnetic field. Thanks to the probing of the ensemble NVs along the whole waveguide device, this setup achieves at least an order of magnitude improvement in sensitivity compared to the traditional confocal configuration. Moreover, by using an excitation and collection path in a buried waveguide
Eaton et al. 2019
, the probed target could be potentially placed on the diamond surface without direct laser excitation
Barry et al. 2016
, therefore our study provides a non-invasive-way to future bio-sensing for samples vulnerable to optical illumination
Barry et al. 2016
;
Laissue et al. 2017
.

Magnetometry in a fiber-waveguide-fiber configuration

III
Conclusion

We have introduced an approach for waveguide-enhanced quantum sensing using a high-quality buried waveguide in diamond chip with 4.5 ppm NV density. We show that femtosecond laser writing does not change the density, spectrum, and spin coherence properties of the NVs, leading to a high sensitivity and increased optical depth with ten micrometers of resolution. In a fiber-waveguide-fiber setup, we obtained robust ODMR response from which we inferred the magnetic field magnitude and direction. The increased NV density in this chip, combined with the advantages of using a fiber-waveguide coupled system leads to
\eta\textsubscript{dc}
=
63\text{\,}\mathrm{p}
and
\eta\textsubscript{ac}
=
20\text{\,}\mathrm{p}
. Other than the improved sensitivity, WGINVs in DNV-B14 are significantly more reproducible compared to the WGINVs in high-pressure high-temperature diamond in our previous report
Guo et al. 2024
, thanks to a more uniform NV density in the CVD produced sample. Future work will focus on applying this technology to electric field sensing or bio-sensing. Alternative photonic structures with integrated NVs could be tailored for different applications by leveraging the adaptability of the femtosecond laser direct-write process.

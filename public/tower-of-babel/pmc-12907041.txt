Brief history of international joint symposiums in BSJ
Authors: Takayuki Nishizaka
Published: 2025

In the 63rd annual meeting of the Biophysical Society of Japan (BSJ), our society successfully held the first bi-lateral symposium between the Korean Biophysics Society (KBPS) and BSJ in Nara prefecture Japan in 2025. As the current president of Asian Biophysics Association (ABA), I am so pleased to see this achievement and be honored to be involved in this precious opportunity as one of oral speakers. This symposium would be an ideal opening towards the successive big international event between these two countries: International Conference on Biophysics and Biomedical Sciences, ICBBS2026. The conference is led by both BSJ and KBPS, i.e., two contiguous events in respective countries will be successfully merged into one; The 64th Annual Meeting of the Biophysical Society of Japan, and the Federation Meeting of Korean Societies for Biomolecular and Biophysical Sciences.

Bi-lateral symposiums at past annual meetings have been organized by several volunteers in BSJ with helps of partner societies. In other words, they were not constantly operated by specific administration officers, and so frequency and partner counties have depended on the relationship between key persons in BSJ and some active researchers in other local societies. Ideally, two countries would invite two or three researchers every two years to annual meetings of host countries, then bi-lateral symposium could be held in either annual meeting every year.

This event had surely enhanced international exchange in Asia. The first bi-lateral symposium was led by Nobuhiro Go in BSJ in 1980s with the Biophysical Society of China (BSC). Activity had been expanding through single fruitful sessions in multiple annual meetings. And finally, an independent symposium entitled “East Asian Biophysics Symposium (EABS)”, which also involved additional countries in east Asia, was held in Hyogo prefecture in Japan in May 1994. Fortunately four successive symposiums were held in Beijing China (1997), Kyonju Korea (2000), The Grand Hotel Taipei (2003), and Okinawa Japan (2006). This movement was extended to ABA, which now holds seven local societies (see Note). Then ABA symposiums have been held every two or three years circulating among these societies.

In parallel with these events, some key researchers have tried to organize original style of bi-lateral symposiums with variety of countries. So far, we established firm connection with three societies in past twelve years; (1) the Australian Society for Biophysics (ASB), (2) the Indian Biophysical Society (IBS), and (3) the Taiwan Biophysical Society. In 2025, there were two unique events on a trial basis: tri-lateral symposium including three local areas organized by Hitoshi Ishiwata (Chiba University); and bi-lateral symposium with Norway with the help of Yoshihiko Furuike (Institute for Molecular Science) and Oliver Vanderpoorten (the Arctic University of Norway). Additionally, records of the past also show that there were some exceptional symposiums led by Japanese researchers through personal networks: (A) Israel Biophysical Society and BSJ. This one-time event so far was held in 49th BSJ annual meeting in Nagoya city Japan in 2011, and the chairs were Irit Sagi (Weizman Institute) and Mikio Kataoka (Nara Institute of Science and Technology). (B) Korea-Japan Joint symposium in November 2016, where all research topics were focused on single molecule biophysics, in 54th BSJ annual meeting. Most speakers from Korea were not members of KBPS, but members of the Korean Physical Society (KPS) at that time. Strikingly, networking of participated researchers is now expanding to hold independent symposiums entitled “East Asian Single-Molecule Biophysics Symposium (EASMB)” including China and Singapore. EASB has been constantly held almost every year: Okayama, Japan in 2018; Seoul, Korea in 2019, online in 2022; Chengdu, China in 2023; Busan, Korea in 2024; National University of Singapore in 2025; and Okinawa, Japan in 2026. (C) Japan-US symposiums in BSJ annual meetings. These valuable events were voluntarily operated by Kumiko Hayashi (Tokyo University), and successfully building the bridge between Biophysical Society in US and BSJ. The first symposium started in 59th BSJ annual meeting in 2021 (online) focusing on the subject of motor proteins, and second and third ones were successively held in annual meetings of 60th (Hakodate Arena) and 61st (Nagoya Congress Center).

Note that chairs of BSJ annual meetings of course change every year. The similar situation happens in all partner countries. As both societies had not made any clear agreement about this type of symposium, chairs or organizers of annual meetings might simply miss out the opportunity to start the preparation of bi-lateral symposiums, or even did not recognize the past history (even for a president at the time). With these unstable factors, we imagine that keeping bi-lateral symposiums constantly between a specific country and Japan had not been an easy task so far. I therefore sincerely look up to seniors and researchers in the same generation who dedicatedly made efforts to organize thousands of past bi-lateral symposiums. For myself, I had worked in BSJ as an administrative officer for international affairs from 2017 to 2022, and consecutively took the task of vice-president from 2023 to 2025. What I mainly concentrated was that keeping connections with foreign researchers, not only at society levels between multiple countries, but also as personal relationships. Supported by this kind of modest and personal activities, many bi-lateral symposiums were successfully held in this decade in both past BSJ annual meetings and other foreign meetings of partner societies. (In fact, some of acquainted key persons had been occasionally selected as a president in foreign societies, and kindly passed the baton to a next president afterword.) Through these activities, I hope next generation will realize how an international connection makes their research life truly fruitful in terms of science, also as well as one human being in Asia. On a separate note, I am now working as a president of ABA, and also as one of councilor of International Union of Pure and Applied Biophysics (IUPAB) as an east Asian representative. I feel that past fascinating bi-lateral symposiums are tangible achievement that naturally led to my current service in these honorable positions.

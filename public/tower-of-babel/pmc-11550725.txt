Diagnosing intravascular B-cell lymphoma using nanopore sequencing of cell-free DNA from cerebrospinal fluid
Authors: B.C. Schmidt, A.-K. Afflerbach, P. Ludewig, P. Dirksen, F.-O. Paulsen, T. Magnus, M. Alawi, U. Schüller, K. Weisel, C. Bokemeyer, M. Christopeit
Published: 2024

A 39-year-old woman presented to the emergency department with severe occipital headache, fever and vomiting, and a cerebellar syndrome including gait ataxia and diplopia. Coronavirus disease 2019 (COVID-19) with a mild course preceded her complaints. Initial tests showed elevated lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) at 724 U/l (normal range 120-246 U/l); blood counts and inflammation markers were normal. Magnetic resonance imaging revealed multifocal brain lesions. A biopsy showed inflammatory changes, but no signs of malignancy. Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) analyses, including cytology, did not provide clear findings. Positron emission tomography–computed tomography showed a distinct focal metabolic increase in the right proximal femur. However, computed tomography-guided biopsy and a bone marrow biopsy including cytology, flow cytometry, and core biopsy did not lead to a diagnosis.

We suspected a parainfectious, hyperinflammatory autoimmune syndrome after COVID-19 with cerebral involvement in the form of acute demyelinating encephalomyelitis and started systemic corticosteroids.1 However, the patient’s condition deteriorated, resulting in severe polyserositis, ascites, pleural and pericardial effusions, right heart failure, and respiratory insufficiency necessitating mechanical ventilation. Cerebral swelling required an intracranial pressure probe and conservative therapy. As a result of the steroid-refractory course, cyclophosphamide (500 mg/m2) was introduced, resulting in immediate stabilization of all organ functions. After two treatment cycles and further clinical improvements, discharge from the hospital was possible. However, 2 weeks after transfer to a rehabilitation facility, she was readmitted with a fever, severe headache, and vomiting. Tests showed elevated LDH (1046 U/l) and moderate C-reactive protein levels (70 mg/dl), with recurring respiratory insufficiency and polyserositis. Cyclophosphamide (750 mg/m2) treatment again led to clinical stabilization.

After a clinical course of 3 months without a clear diagnosis, CSF analyses were repeated. As an exploratory approach, nanopore sequencing of cell-free DNA (cfDNA) from the CSF supernatant was carried out using methods previously described by our team.2 Sequencing data showed distinct copy number variations, suggesting the presence of malignancy (Figure 1A). Methylation analysis with a central nervous system tumor reference set indicated a high match with lymphoma (Figure 1B).3,4 Comparison with a publicly deposited cohort of hematolymphoid neoplasms (GEO Series GSE237299) showed a high match with diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (Figure 1D). The interdisciplinary lymphoma board made a tentative diagnosis of intravascular B-cell lymphoma (IVLBCL) based on the clinical picture and sequencing results. Immediate polychemotherapy with the CHOEP regimen combined with rituximab led to rapid clinical improvement. IVLBCL is a rare, aggressive form of high-grade B-cell lymphoma requiring prompt diagnosis and treatment. Its clinical presentation is heterogeneous, without pathognomonic symptoms or manifestations. IVLBCL is diagnosed by histopathological examination of affected organs and the bone marrow, typically showing large intravascular B cells.5 The rarity and diagnostic challenges of IVLBCL increase the risk of misdiagnosis and inappropriate treatment.5 This case highlights the potential of nanopore sequencing of cfDNA from CSF as a diagnostic tool for cerebral involvement in hematologic neoplasms, offering a promising approach for lymphoma diagnosis.

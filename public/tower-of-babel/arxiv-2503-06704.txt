Janus and RG-flow interfaces from 5D N = 4 N=4 gauged supergravity
Parinya Karndumri

Abstract
We study five-dimensional N = 4 N=4 gauged supergravity coupled to three vector multiplets with S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3) gauge group. The gauged supergravity admits two supersymmetric A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacua. One of the vacua, at the origin of the scalar manifold ℝ + × S ​ O ​ ( 5 , 3 ) / S ​ O ​ ( 5 ) × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) \mathbb{R}^{+}\times SO(5,3)/SO(5)\times SO(3) , preserves N = 4 N=4 supersymmetry and the full S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D × S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3) gauge symmetry. The second A ​ d ​ S 5 AdS_{5} vacuum is N = 2 N=2 supersymmetric and invariant under S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) diag SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}} which is a diagonal subgroup of S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) D SO(2)_{D} and S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) ⊂ S ​ O ​ ( 3 ) SO(2)\subset SO(3) . By considering a truncation to S ​ O ​ ( 2 ) diag SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}} invariant scalars, we find a class of Janus solutions interpolating among these vacua. These solutions should be holographically dual to conformal interfaces within the dual N = 1 N=1 and N = 2 N=2 SCFTs in four dimensions. In addition, we also find examples of solutions describing RG-flow interfaces between N = 1 N=1 and N = 2 N=2 SCFTs on each side of the interfaces. All of the solutions could possibly be uplifted to eleven dimensions by a consistent truncation of M-theory on M 3 × S 2 × S 1 M_{3}\times S^{2}\times S^{1} with a 3-manifold M 3 M_{3} .

[

,

-dimensional conformal interfaces in

-sliced domain walls within a
(d+1)
-dimensional gauged supergravity. Since the discovery of the first Janus solution in
[
4
]
, see also
[
5
]
, many solutions of this type have appeared in various space-time dimensions, see
[
6
]
-
[
40
]
for an incomplete list. Some of these solutions can be embedded in string/M-theory via many known consistent truncations. A number of Janus solutions have also been obtained directly from ten- or eleven-dimensional supergravities, see for example
[
41
,
42
,
43
,
44
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in supersymmetric Janus solutions in half-maximal
N=4
gauged supergravity in five dimensions. After the original supersymmetric Janus solution found in
[
5
]
, see also
[
13
]
, a number of Janus solutions from the maximal
SO(6)
gauged supergravity have appeared more recently in
[
15
]
. However, all of these solutions have been obtained within particular truncations of
N=2
or
N=8
gauged supergravities in which only one supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacuum exists. Accordingly, the resulting Janus solutions essentially interpolate between the same conformal fixed point dual to the
AdS_{5}
vacuum. Another class of solutions describing superconformal interfaces between
N=4
Super-Yang-Mills and
N=1
Leigh-Strassler SCFT have been found more recently in
[
45
]
. These solutions are also obtained from the
SO(6)
maximal gauged supergravity in five dimensions. In this work, we will look for supersymmetric Janus solutions from
N=4

AdS_{5}

N=4
gauged supergravity coupled to three vector multiplets with

subgroup of
SO(2)_{R}\times SO(3)_{R}\subset SO(5)_{R}
while the
SO(2)_{D}
is the diagonal subgroup of
SO(2)_{R}
and
SO(2)\subset SO(3)
with
SO(3)
being the symmetry of the three vector multiplets. This gauged supergravity has originally been studied in
[
46
]
in which two supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua with
N=4
and
N=2
supersymmetries have been found. An extension to a bigger gauge group admitting a number of
N=4
and
N=2
supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua and a web of holographic RG flows has also been considered recently in
[
47
]
. In addition, it has been shown in
[
48
]
that this gauged supergravity can be obtained from a consistent truncation of eleven-dimensional supergravity on
M_{3}\times S^{2}\times S^{1}
with
M_{3}
being a three-manifold. It is then possible to uplift all the solutions found in this paper to M-theory.

The existence of the second
AdS_{5}
vacuum leads to a much richer class of Janus solutions. Apart from the solutions interpolating between the
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacuum at the origin of the scalar manifold

[

18
,
36

37
,
38

N=4

[
46
,
47
,
49

N=4

We first give a brief review of five-dimensional
N=4
gauged supergravity coupled to vector multiplets. We will closely follow the construction of
N=4

\psi_{\mu i}

\mathbb{R}^{+}

5n

\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}A}=(\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}m},\mathcal{V}_{M}^{\phantom{M}a}).

(1)

Gaugings of
N=4
supergravity are described by three components of the embedding tensor
\xi^{M}

\xi^{MN}=\xi^{[MN]}
and
f_{MNP}=f_{[MNP]}

\xi^{M}=0

[
52

\xi^{M}=0
from now on. The gauge group is then entirely embedded in

with the quadratic constraints given by

f_{R[MN}{f_{PQ]}}^{R}=0\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad{\xi_{M}}^{Q}f_{QNP}=0\,.

(2)

The gauge generators in the fundamental representation of
SO(5,n)
can be written as

{(X_{M})_{N}}^{P}=-{f_{M}}^{QR}{(t_{QR})_{N}}^{P}={f_{MN}}^{P}\quad\textrm{and}\quad{(X_{0})_{N}}^{P}=-\xi^{QR}{(t_{QR})_{N}}^{P}={\xi_{N}}^{P}

(3)

The scalar potential reads

III
Supersymmetric Janus solutions

In this section, we look for supersymmetric Janus solutions describing conformal interfaces within the
N=2
and
N=1
SCFTs dual to the aforementioned
AdS_{5}
vacua. The metric ansatz is given by an
AdS_{4}
-sliced domain wall

ds^{2}=e^{2A(r)}ds^{2}_{AdS_{4}}+dr^{2}

(27)

with

being the metric on
AdS_{4}
with radius
\ell
.

We will consider a truncation to
SO(2)_{\textrm{diag}}
singlet scalars. This diagonal subgroup is generated by a linear combination of
SO(5,3)
generators

under which there are five singlet scalars from

15

(\mathbf{5},\mathbf{3})
under the compact subgroup
SO(5)\times SO(3)\subset SO(5,3)

\mathbf{5}
of

(\mathbf{5},\mathbf{3})\rightarrow(\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1},\mathbf{3})+(\mathbf{1},\mathbf{3},\mathbf{3}).

(28)

and
SO(3)

AdS_{5}
critical points as given in (
24
) and (
25
). Apart from these two critical points, there are no other supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacua.

It is also useful to note the scalar kinetic term

\mathcal{L}_{\textrm{kin}}=-\frac{3}{2}\frac{\Sigma^{\prime 2}}{\Sigma^{2}}-\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}{\phi^{\prime}_{1}}^{2}-{\phi^{\prime}_{2}}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}{\phi^{\prime}_{3}}^{2}\,.

(33)

III.1
BPS equations for Janus solutions

We are now in a position to analyze the supersymmetry conditions arising from setting supersymmetry transformations of fermionic fields to zero. With the coset representative (
31
) and
\phi_{4}=\phi_{5}=0
, the
A_{1}^{ij}
tensor is diagonal and takes the form

A^{ij}_{1}=\textrm{diag}(\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{*},\beta)

(34)

with

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}h_{1}\Sigma^{-1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}+\frac{1}{8\sqrt{3}}\Sigma^{2}\left[(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}(\cosh 2\phi_{2}-3)\right.

(35)

\displaystyle\left.-2(g_{1}+g_{2})\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}-4i(g_{1}-g_{2})\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\right],

\displaystyle\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\sqrt{3}}\Sigma^{2}\left[2(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}-(g_{1}+g_{2})(\cosh 2\phi_{2}-3)\right]

(36)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\Sigma^{-1}h_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}\,.

As pointed out in
[
47
]
, the unbroken supersymmetry is at most
N=2
whenever
\phi_{2}\neq 0
.

However, unlike the previous results given in
[
46
]
and
[
47
]
, both the eigenvalues
\alpha
and
\beta
do not lead to a superpotential in terms of which the scalar potential (
32
) can be written. It is possible to define an exact superpotential from
\alpha
or
\beta
only for either
\phi_{1}=0
or
\phi_{2}=0
. As we will see, these two possibilities rule out any possible supersymmetric Janus solutions. Therefore, we will keep both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
non-vanishing. The non-existence of superpotential is mainly due to the failure to satisfy some integrability conditions as pointed out in
[
53
]
. This is also the case for Janus solutions in six-dimensional gauged supergravity studied in
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
as pointed out in
[
54
]
. In addition, the reality of
\beta
turns out to exclude the possibility of any Janus solutions as well. Accordingly, we will only consider the unbroken supersymmetry corresponding to the eigevalue
\alpha
and take the associated Killing spinors to be
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{3}
.

With all these, the variations
\delta\chi_{i}
give

\Sigma^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{1}=\mathcal{A}\epsilon_{3}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\Sigma^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{3}=\mathcal{A}^{*}\epsilon_{1}

(37)

for

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{i}{6\sqrt{2}}\Sigma^{3}\left[g_{2}(2i\sinh\phi_{1}+2\cosh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2})^{2}\right.

(38)

\displaystyle\left.+4g_{1}(\cosh\phi_{1}-i\sinh\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2})^{2}\right]-\frac{i}{3}h_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\cosh\phi_{3}\,.

Multiply the first equation in (
37
) by
\Sigma^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}
and use the second equation, we find

{\Sigma^{\prime}}^{2}=\mathcal{A}\mathcal{A}^{*}=|\mathcal{A}|^{2}

(39)

which leads to

\Sigma^{\prime}=\eta|\mathcal{A}|

(40)

for a sign factor
\eta=\pm 1
and a projector of the form

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{1}=\eta\frac{\mathcal{A}}{|\mathcal{A}|}\epsilon_{3}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{3}=\eta\frac{\mathcal{A}^{*}}{|\mathcal{A}|}\epsilon_{1}\,.

(41)

We now move to the conditions arising from
\delta\lambda^{a}_{i}
. Using the
\gamma_{\hat{r}}
projection given in (
41
), we find that one of the conditions leads to

(g_{2}-g_{1})\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\phi^{\prime}_{3}=0.

(42)

For
g_{2}=g_{1}
, the
N=2
supersymmetric
AdS_{5}
vacuum does not exist. As we will see at the end of this section, Janus solutions do not exist for
g_{2}=g_{1}
either. Furthermore, Janus solutions do not exist if either
\phi_{1}=0
or
\phi_{2}=0
as previously mentioned. Therefore, we satisfy this condition by choosing constant
\phi_{3}
which can be consistently set to zero in order to make the solutions approach the
AdS_{5}
vacua at which
\phi_{3}=0
.

The remaining conditions from
\delta\lambda^{a}_{i}
are given by

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}_{2}-i\cosh\phi_{2}\phi^{\prime}_{1})\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{1}=\mathcal{B}\epsilon_{3}

(43)

and

\displaystyle(\phi^{\prime}_{2}+i\cosh\phi_{2}\phi^{\prime}_{1})\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{3}=\mathcal{B}^{*}\epsilon_{1}

(44)

with

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\sqrt{2}}\Sigma^{2}\cosh\phi_{2}\left[2i\sinh\phi_{2}[(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}-g_{1}-g_{2}]\right.

(45)

\displaystyle\left.-2(g_{1}-g_{2})\sinh 2\phi_{1}\right]+\frac{i}{2}\Sigma^{-1}h_{1}\sinh 2\phi_{2}\,.

Using the projector (
41
) again, we arrive at the BPS equations for
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
of the form

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}_{2}\Sigma^{\prime}=\textrm{Re}(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}^{*})\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\cosh\phi_{2}\phi^{\prime}_{1}\Sigma^{\prime}=-\textrm{Im}(\mathcal{B}\mathcal{A}^{*}).

(46)

Finally, we consider the gravitino variations along
AdS_{4}
directions. We first split the five-dimensional coordinates as
x^{\mu}=(x^{\alpha},r)
for
\alpha=0,1,2,3
. The conditions
\delta\psi_{\hat{\alpha}i}
take the form

D_{\hat{\alpha}}\epsilon_{i}=-\frac{i}{\sqrt{6}}\Omega_{ij}A_{1}^{jk}\gamma_{\hat{\alpha}}\epsilon_{k}\,.

(47)

Following
[
15
]
, we rewrite the covariant derivative in terms of the covariant derivative
\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}
on
AdS_{4}
as

D_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}=\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}-\frac{1}{2}A^{\prime}\gamma_{r}\gamma_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}\,.

(48)

We then use the Killing spinor equations for
AdS_{4}
of the form

\widetilde{\nabla}_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}=\frac{i}{2\ell}\kappa_{i}\gamma_{r}\gamma_{\alpha}\epsilon_{i}

(49)

with
\kappa_{i}=\pm 1
. We have also identified the chirality matrix on
AdS_{4}
as
\gamma_{r}=i\gamma_{\hat{0}}\gamma_{\hat{1}}\gamma_{\hat{2}}\gamma_{\hat{3}}
. Using the explicit form of the
A_{1}^{ij}
tensor given in (
34
), we find from (
47
)

\displaystyle\left(A^{\prime}-\frac{i}{\ell}\kappa_{1}e^{-A}\right)\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{1}=-i\mathcal{W}^{*}\epsilon_{3}

(50)

and

\displaystyle\left(A^{\prime}-\frac{i}{\ell}\kappa_{3}e^{-A}\right)\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{3}=i\mathcal{W}\epsilon_{1}

(51)

with
\mathcal{W}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\alpha
. As in
[
15
]
, consistency between these two equations requires
\kappa_{3}=-\kappa_{1}
.

Using the
\gamma_{\hat{r}}
projector given in (
41
) and writing
\kappa=\kappa_{1}=-\kappa_{3}
, we eventually find

A^{\prime}=-\eta\frac{\textrm{Re}(i\mathcal{W}^{*}\mathcal{A}^{*})}{|\mathcal{A}|}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\frac{\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}=\eta\frac{\textrm{Im}(i\mathcal{W}^{*}\mathcal{A}^{*})}{|\mathcal{A}|}\,.

(52)

We also note that the two equations (
50
) and (
51
) also imply the relation

{A^{\prime}}^{2}+\frac{1}{\ell^{2}}e^{-2A}=|\mathcal{W}|^{2}\,.

(53)

As in other cases, the remaining condition
\delta\psi_{\hat{r}i}
will determine the radial dependence of the Killing spinors.

At this point, we will give some comments on choosing the residual supersymmetry corresponding to the eigenvalue
\beta
of the
A_{1}^{ij}
tensor. In this case, the Killing spinors are given by
\epsilon_{2}
and
\epsilon_{4}
. The same analysis of
\delta\chi_{i}
conditions leads to an analogue of (
37
) of the form

\Sigma^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{2}=i\mathcal{M}\epsilon_{4}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\Sigma^{\prime}\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{4}=-i\mathcal{M}\epsilon_{2}

(54)

with a real function
\mathcal{M}
. Consistency of the projectors then requires that
\Sigma^{\prime}=\pm\mathcal{M}
which gives the projectors

\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{2}=\pm i\epsilon_{4}\qquad\textrm{and}\qquad\gamma_{\hat{r}}\epsilon_{4}=\mp i\epsilon_{2}\,.

(55)

Using these projectors in
\delta\psi_{\hat{\alpha}i}
conditions results in the identically vanishing
\frac{\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}
. Therefore, only flat domain wall solutions are possible.

It is useful to explicitly give the form of the algebraic constraint given by the second equation in (
52
)

\frac{\kappa}{\ell}e^{-A}=\frac{(g_{2}-g_{1})h_{1}\Sigma^{2}\sinh 2\phi_{1}\sinh\phi_{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}}{3\sqrt{2}|\mathcal{A}|}\,.

(56)

We can immediately see that for either
\phi_{1}=0
or
\phi_{2}=0
, this constraint forces the
AdS_{4}
radius
\ell\rightarrow\infty
leading to a flat domain wall. This also implies that the dilaton cannot support the curvature of the world-volume of the domain wall. This is precisely in agreement with the results of
[
55
]
in which it has also been shown that the existence of
AdS_{4}
-sliced domain walls requires both the dilaton and scalars from vector multiplets to be non-vanishing. Furthermore, setting
g_{2}=g_{1}
also excludes any Janus solutions as previously mentioned.

We end this section by pointing out that the algebraic constraint (
56
) is compatible with all the remaining flow equations for
\Sigma
,
\phi_{1}
,
\phi_{2}
and
A
. Moreover, we have also explicitly verified that these equations together with the constraint (
56
) imply the second-ordered field equations. Finally, it should be noted that Janus solutions are also possible for
g_{2}=0
. This leads to a simple embedding of the
SO(2)
factor, and the
SO(2)\times SO(3)
gauge group is entirely embedded in
SO(5)_{R}\subset SO(5,3)
. Therefore, supersymmetric Janus solutions exist in both
SO(2)\times SO(3)
and
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)
gauge groups provided that both
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{2}
are non-vanishing.

III.2
Numerical Janus solutions

The resulting BPS equations take a very complicated form. Even a much simpler set of BPS equations for obtaining RG flow solutions studied in
[
46
]
can only be solved numerically. We then do not expect to find any analytic solutions to these equations. Accordingly, we consider finding numerical solutions with suitable boundary conditions. For convenience, we first note the explicit form of all the BPS equations

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{3}\eta\left[\left(\sqrt{2}\Sigma^{3}\{\cosh^{2}\phi_{1}(g_{1}+g_{2}\sinh^{2}\phi_{2})-\sinh^{2}\phi_{1}(g_{2}+g_{1}\sinh^{2}\phi_{2})\}\right.\right.

(57)

\displaystyle\left.\left.\phantom{\sqrt{2}}+h_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\right)^{2}+2(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}\Sigma^{6}\sinh^{2}2\phi_{1}\sinh^{2}\phi_{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24}(g_{1}-g_{2})\frac{\Sigma^{2}\cosh\phi_{2}\sinh 2\phi_{1}}{\cosh\phi_{2}}\left[\sqrt{2}h_{1}(5\cosh 2\phi_{2}-3)\right.

(58)

\displaystyle\left.\phantom{\sqrt{2}}+\Sigma^{3}[2(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}-(g_{1}+g_{2})(\cosh 2\phi_{2}-3)]\right],

\displaystyle\Sigma^{\prime}\phi^{\prime}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{24\sqrt{2}}h_{1}\sinh 2\phi_{2}\Sigma^{2}\left[(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}(\cosh 2\phi_{2}-7)\right.

(59)

\displaystyle\left.+(g_{1}+g_{2}+(g_{2}-g_{1})\cosh 2\phi_{1})^{2}\sinh 4\phi_{2}\right]-\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{3\Sigma}\cosh^{3}\phi_{2}\sinh\phi_{2},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{h_{1}^{2}}{9\Sigma}\cosh^{4}\phi_{2}+\frac{1}{72\sqrt{2}}h_{1}\Sigma^{2}\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\left[4(g_{1}+g_{2})\cosh^{2}\phi_{2}\right.

\displaystyle\left.-2(g_{1}-g_{2})\cosh 2\phi_{1}(\cosh 2\phi_{2}-3)\right]-\frac{1}{144}\Sigma^{5}\left[16(g_{1}-g_{2})^{2}\sinh^{2}2\phi_{1}\times\right.

for
\eta=\pm 1
and
\kappa=\pm 1

of the original supersymmetry or four supercharges due to the projector (

and
\epsilon_{3}

We will numerically solve these equations by specifying the values of all the fields at a turning point with

. As in other cases, most of the boundary conditions will lead to singular solutions in which the warp factor

vacua on both sides of the interfaces. The

\eta=\pm 1

N=4
supersymmetric

vacuum on each side of the interfaces. For definiteness, we will set the

IV
Conclusions and discussions

We have studied supersymmetric Janus solutions in five-dimensional
N=4
gauged supergravity with
SO(2)\times SO(3)
and
SO(2)_{D}\times SO(3)
gauge groups. In the former, there is only one supersymmetric
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacuum, so there are only solutions describing conformal interfaces within the dual
N=2
SCFT in four dimensions. In the latter, a number of more interesting solutions appears. Apart from solutions interpolating between the
N=4
vacua, there are solutions in which the
N=4
phases undergo holographic RG flows to the
N=2
phases on both sides of the interfaces. By tuning the boundary conditions, the solutions can go very close to the
N=2
vacuum and stay longer at this critical point. Following
[
16
]
, we can interpret these solutions as conformal interfaces between four-dimensional
N=1
SCFTs dual to the
N=2

AdS_{5}
critical points on each side of the interfaces.

We have also given examples of RG-flow interfaces between different SCFTs related to each other by RG flows on the two sides. Finally, the numerical analysis also leads to solutions with more than one turning points. These might naturally be interpreted as multi-interfaces in
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs in four dimensions as in the solutions studied in
[
30
]
and
[
44
]
. Both of the
N=4
gauged supergravities studied in this paper could be obtained from a consistent truncation of eleven-dimensional supergravity. Therefore, we also expect that the Janus solutions found here could lead to new supersymmetric Janus solutions in M-theory.

As anticipated in
[
46
]
, upon uplifted to eleven dimensions, the
N=4

AdS_{5}
vacuum should correspond to the
N=2
Maldacena-Nunez SCFT obtained from M5-brane wrapped on a Riemann surface
[
56
]
while the
N=2

AdS_{5}
vacuum would be identified with one of the
N=1
SCFTs found in
[
57
]
. Accordingly, we expect the Janus solutions obtained in this paper to describe conformal interfaces within these
N=2
and
N=1
SCFTs as well as RG-flow interfaces between the
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs on each side of the interfaces. It would be interesting to precisely identify these conformal and RG-flow interfaces corresponding to various Janus solutions obtained in this work. As in the solutions found in
[
45
]
, these should involve position-dependent vacuum expectation values and turning on source terms for operators dual to scalar fields in the supergravity solutions. Working out an explicit truncation ansatz of eleven-dimensional supergravity on
M_{3}\times S^{2}\times S^{1}
leading to the five-dimensional
N=4
gauged supergravity considered here is also worth considering. The procedure involves solving a set of constraints given in
[
48
]
, and the result could be used to uplift the holographic RG flows given in
[
46
]
and Janus solutions in this paper to eleven dimensions. This would give a holographic description of RG flows and conformal interfaces within four-dimensional
N=1
and
N=2
SCFTs in the M-theory context.

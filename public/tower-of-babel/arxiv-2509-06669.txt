Constraining the Energy Momentum Tensor through DVCS Dispersion Relation beyond Leading Power
Víctor Martínez-Fernández, Daniele Binosi, Cédric Mezrag, Zhao-Qian Yao (姚照千)

Abstract
In this letter, we analyse and interpret the kinematic power corrections to deeply virtual Compton scattering dispersion relation. We show that the kinematic corrections at twist-4 can be connected to other form factors of the Energy-Momentum Tensor beyond the pressure distribution involved at leading-power, namely the ones related to Momentum and total Angular Momentum distributions. In the nucleon case, these corrections are not negligible at presently accessible virtualities. The DVCS subtraction constant becomes an experimental constraint on momentum distributions, pressure forces distributions, and total angular momentum distributions. Finally, we use continuum and lattice-QCD results to predict the expected size of the DVCS subtraction constant, and conclude that momentum distributions are responsible of roughly one-third of the experimental signal at Q 2 = 2 ​ GeV 2 Q^{2}=2\textrm{GeV}^{2} .



I
Introduction

In the last decade, the question of spatial mapping of pressure and shear forces within the nucleon
Polyakov:2002yz
has been put at the front-stage of hadron physics.
Focusing on the valence quark sector, a large activity both on the theoretical (see for instance Ref.
Lorce:2025oot
;
Ji:2025qax
) and experimental sides
Burkert:2018bqq
;
Kumericki:2019ddg
;
Dutrieux:2021nlz
has been dedicated to the nucleon energy-momentum tensor (EMT) and its form factors.
The main argument of these studies is that the EMT form factor
C
, containing the information regarding the pressure and shear forces due to quarks within the nucleon can be indirectly probed through deeply virtual Compton Scattering
Ji:1996nm
;
Ji:1996ek
, bypassing the full extraction of generalised parton distribution (GPDs) thanks to dispersion relations (DRs)
Anikin:2007yh
;
Diehl:2007jb
;
Dutrieux:2024bgc
.
At leading twist, the subtraction constant can be fully understood in terms of the Polyakov-Weiss
D
-term
Polyakov:1999gs
, whose first moment yields the
C
form factor.
However, when taking into account kinematic-power corrections
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
, the
D
-term is not the sole component contributing to the DVCS subtraction constant, and at first sight, the extraction of both GPDs
H
and
E
is necessary.
What is more, these power corrections are not negligeable at current facilities.
Even when considering the future Electron-Ion collider, the kinematic range is such that these corrections will be sizeable, threatening the interpretability of the DVCS subtraction constant.

In this letter, we will show that this is in practice not the case, and that, even if the power corrections cannot be neglected, most of their contribution comes from the momentum distribution and the angular momentum distribution.
So that, the DVCS subtraction constant can be seen as an experimental constraint relating momentum, pressure and angular momentum distributions within the nucleon.
This will provide an experimental benchmark for both continuum and lattice QCD computation of the nucleon EMT form factors.

II
DVCS dispersion relations

Figure 1:
Sketch of the DVCS process factorised into a hard kernel and GPDs. The incoming electron emits a virtual photon of virtuality
Q^{2}=-q^{2}
while a real photon is detected in the final state. The total momentum transfer is
t=\Delta^{2}
.

The amplitude of the DVCS process, illustrated on Fig.
1
, can be parameterized in terms of the so-called Compton Form Factors (CFF)
Belitsky:2001ns
;
Belitsky:2012ch
. When the photon virtuality
Q^{2}
is large enough, the CFF can be factorised into a hard kernel, that can be computed in perturbation theory, and GPDs containing the non-perturbative information of the process.
The number of GPDs involved in the process description depends on the spin of the target.
Here, we will focus only on the unpolarised, helicity conserving GPDs in the quark sector.
This corresponds to a single GPD
H^{q}
for (pseudo-)scalar targets, and a second GPD
E^{q}
for spin-
1/2
targets.

Importantly, GPDs obey a general property called polynomiality
Ji:1998pc
;
Radyushkin:1998bz
:

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{m}H^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}A_{m,2j}^{q}(t)(2\xi)^{2j}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\quad+\textrm{mod}(2,m)(2\xi)^{m+1}C_{m}^{q}(t),

(1)

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}\mathrm{d}x\,x^{m}E^{q}(x,\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{\left[\frac{m}{2}\right]}B_{m,2j}^{q}(t)(2\xi)^{2j}

\displaystyle\hskip-20.0pt\quad-\textrm{mod}(2,m)(2\xi)^{m+1}C_{m}^{q}(t),

(2)

where
[\dots]
is the floor function and
\textrm{mod}(2,m)
is 0 for
m
even and 1 for
m
odd.
A_{1,0}
,
B_{1,0}
and
C_{1}
are actually the symmetric, current conserving EMT form factors
Ji:1996ek
:

\displaystyle{\left\langle p_{2},s^{\prime}\right|}\texttt{T}^{\{\mu\nu\}}_{q}(0){\left|p_{1},s\right\rangle}=\bar{u}(p_{2},s^{\prime})\Bigg\{\frac{\Delta^{\mu}\Delta^{\nu}-\eta^{\mu\nu}\Delta^{2}}{M}\,C_{q}(t)

\displaystyle+\frac{\bar{p}^{\mu}\bar{p}^{\nu}}{M}\,A_{q}(t)+\frac{\bar{p}^{\{\mu}i\sigma^{\nu\}\rho}\Delta_{\rho}}{4M}\left[A_{q}(t)+B_{q}(t)\right]\Bigg\}u(p_{1},s)\,,

(3)

The polynomiality property is equivalent to the so-callled double distribution formalism introduced independently in
Mueller:1998fv
;
Radyushkin:1997ki
(see also
Chouika:2017dhe
;
Mezrag:2022pqk
).

\displaystyle H^{q}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\textrm{d}\Omega\bigg[F^{q}(\beta,\alpha,t)+\xi D^{q}(\alpha,t)\delta(\beta)\bigg]\delta_{x;\xi}^{\beta,\alpha},

(4)

\displaystyle E^{q}(x,\xi,t)=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\textrm{d}\Omega\bigg[K^{q}(\beta,\alpha,t)-\xi D^{q}(\alpha,t)\delta(\beta)\bigg]\delta_{x;\xi}^{\beta,\alpha},

(5)

where
\Omega=\{(\alpha,\beta)||\alpha|+|\beta|\leq 1\}
and
\delta_{x;\xi}^{\beta,\alpha}=\delta(x-\beta-\alpha\xi)

When the kinematic conditions allow it, the DVCS amplitude obeys subtracted dispersion relations.
The best known one is given as:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\Re\mathcal{H}-\frac{2}{\pi}\fint_{0}^{1}\textrm{d}x\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)\frac{x\Im\mathcal{H}(x)}{(\xi-x)(\xi+x)}

(6)

where
\fint
represents the Cauchy principal value prescription,
\mathcal{S}
is the subtraction constant and
\mathcal{H}
the CFF associated with the GPD
H
.

This leading power picture can be enhanced including kinematic power corrections that modify the hard kernel without involving new non-perturbative distributions
Braun:2012hq
;
Braun:2012bg
;
Braun:2014sta
;
Braun:2016qlg
;
Braun:2020zjm
;
Braun:2022qly
;
Braun:2025xlp
;
Martinez-Fernandez:2025gub
.
These corrections are expected to be power suppressed in
1/\mathbb{Q}=1/\sqrt{(Q^{2}+t)}
, but explicitly depend on the spin of the target.
Their expression can be found in
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
.

III
Scalar targets and momentum distribution

Following
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
, when taking into account kinematic power corrections, the DVCS subtraction constant is given for a (pseudo-)scalar target by:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})=

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{1}d\alpha\ T_{2}^{++}(\alpha,t/\mathbb{Q}^{2})D^{q}(\alpha,t)

\displaystyle-4\frac{M^{2}-t/4}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\int_{\Omega}\textrm{d}\Omega F^{q}(\beta,\alpha,t)\beta\,T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)\,,

(7)

where
T_{2}^{++}
and
T_{1}^{++\,(1)}
are parts of the coefficient function. Their expressions are provided in the supplemental material.
Focusing on the second line, where the mass correction stands, we Taylor expand
T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)
around
\alpha=0

\displaystyle T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=0\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\alpha^{n}\mathfrak{c}_{n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=0\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{\alpha^{n}}{n!}\left.\frac{\partial^{n}T_{1}^{++\,(1)}}{\partial\alpha^{n}}\right|_{\alpha=0}.

(8)

Note that
T_{1}^{++\,(1)}
is singular for
\alpha\to 1
. However, because of the support properties of DDs this singularity is tamed in Eq. (
III
) and the integrand goes to zero at the end-point.
Injecting Eq. (
8
) into the second line of Eq. (
III
) and using Eqs. (
4
) and (
II
), one gets:

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\textrm{d}\Omega F^{q}(\beta,\alpha)\beta\,T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)=\mathfrak{c}_{0}A_{1,0}^{q}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n=2\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{2^{n}\mathfrak{c}_{n}}{n+1}A_{n+1,n}^{q},

(9)

where the factor
2^{n}
is conventional.
The mass term can thus be expressed as a series of the subset
A_{n+1,n}
of the generalised form factors
A_{i,j}
, whose first term
A^{q}_{1,0}
is the EMT form factor containing the information regarding the momentum distribution within the nucleon.

We then truncate the series to the two first elements (
n\leq 2
).
The expression of the subtraction constant becomes:

\mathcal{S}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})\hskip-2.84526pt=\mathcal{D}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})-4\frac{M^{2}-\frac{t}{4}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\hskip-2.84526pt\left[\mathfrak{c}_{0}A_{1,0}^{q}(t)+\hskip-2.27621pt\frac{4\mathfrak{c}_{2}}{3}A_{3,2}^{q}(t)\hskip-2.27621pt\right]\hskip-2.84526pt,

(10)

with
\mathfrak{c}_{0}\approx 0.864
and
\mathfrak{c}_{2}\approx 0.980
and

\mathcal{D}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})=\int_{-1}^{1}\textrm{d}\alpha\,T_{2}^{++}\left(\alpha,\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\right)D^{q}(\alpha,t).

(11)

In order to test the validity of this approximation, we introduce the ratio:

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{n}^{q}=\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{n}\frac{2^{j}\mathfrak{c}_{j}}{j+1}A_{j+1,j}^{q}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=0\\
\textrm{even}\end{subarray}}^{\infty}\frac{2^{j}\mathfrak{c}_{j}}{j+1}A_{j+1,j}^{q}}.

(12)

Then we computed the
A_{n+1,n}
generalised form factor from the so-called phenomenological model of Ref.
Chavez:2021llq
, based on the xFitter pion PDF
Novikov:2020snp
, and the Radyushking DD Ansatz
Musatov:1999xp

\displaystyle J^{q}(t)=\frac{A_{1,0}^{q}(t)+B_{1,0}^{q}(t)}{2},

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})=

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{D}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})-4\frac{M^{2}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\mathfrak{c}_{0}\left[A^{q}_{1,0}(t)+\frac{t}{4M^{2}}B_{1,0}^{q}(t)\right]

\displaystyle\ \mathcal{D}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})\hskip-2.84526pt-\hskip-2.84526pt\frac{4M^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{0}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\hskip-2.84526pt\left[\hskip-2.27621pt\frac{4M^{2}-t}{4M^{2}}A^{q}_{1,0}(t)\hskip-2.84526pt+\hskip-2.84526pt\frac{t}{2M^{2}}J^{q}(t)\hskip-2.27621pt\right]\hskip-2.84526pt.

(17)

Thus, in the nucleon case, the DVCS subtraction constant provides a relation between the Polyakov-Weiss
D
-term, the momentum distribution and the total angular momentum one.
Assuming that the first moment of the
D
-term is also the dominant one (this is a standard assumption regularising the deconvolution problem although its validity remains questionable

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{q}(t,\mathbb{Q}^{2})\approx

\displaystyle\ 20C^{q}(t)\left(1-\frac{t}{3\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle-\frac{4M^{2}\mathfrak{c}_{0}}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\left[\frac{4M^{2}-t}{4M^{2}}A^{q}_{1,0}(t)+\frac{t}{2M^{2}}J^{q}(t)\right]

(18)

connecting the EMT form factors.
This holds in Fourier space: the maps of pressure and shear forces within the nucleon depend on the maps of momentum and angular momentum.

V
Assessing the DVCS Dispersion relation

We are now in a position to assess the kinematics power corrections at achievable DVCS kinematics.
To do that, we will exploit the lattice computations of the EMT form factors of Ref.
Hackett:2023rif
(using the dipole fit), and the Continuum Schwinger Method (CSM) computation of Ref.
Yao:2024ixu
.
We then compute the total subtraction constant:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}=\sum_{q}e_{q}^{2}\mathcal{S}^{q}.

(19)

The lattice results

The choice of this scale is driven by the experimental knowledge of the subtraction constant, which is maximal for

For both approaches, the mass correction largely dominate the power corrections, leading up to a
1/3
(lattice) and
1/4
(CSM) effect at
Q^{2}=2\textrm{GeV}^{2}
, while the
t/\mathbb{Q}^{2}
ones remain small.
On top of the effective power suppression, one notes the
tC^{q}/\mathbb{Q}^{2}
and
tJ^{q}/\mathbb{Q}^{2}
contribute with an opposite sign, explaining also why the effect is small.
The difference at small-
t
between the two approaches comes from the fact that, despite having compatible total
C
form factor, the decomposition between quarks and gluons is significantly different with
C^{g}_{\textrm{Latt}}\approx 4.5C^{u}_{\textrm{Latt}}
while
C^{g}_{\textrm{CSM}}\approx C_{\textrm{CSM}}^{u}
.
With such a large amount of gluons, next-to-leading order corrections may fill the gap between the two approches.
At
Q^{2}=4\mathrm{GeV^{2}}
, the impact of the mass corrections is reduced to roughly 15%, due to both the power correction, but also the logarithmic decrease of
A^{q}
, as more momentum is sent to the gluon sector.

VI
conclusion

The kinematic power corrections contribute significantly to the DVCS dispersion relation.
They thus modify our understanding of the subtraction constant of DVCS, which now connects the EMT form factors
A^{q}
and
J^{q}
with
C^{q}
, although the contribution of
J^{q}
is assessed here to be much smaller than the one of
A^{q}
, and probably not accessible experimentally.

At
Q^{2}=2\textrm{\ GeV}^{2}
, i.e. the highest
Q^{2}
range of the JLab 6 GeV kinematical area, the contribution of
A

is
sizeable, as it can account for
1/3
of the experimental signal, and largely dominate the power correction effects.
Note that a 30% to 40% effect is in agreement with the expectations that have been made previously on DVCS amplitudes
Defurne:2015kxq
;
Braun:2025xlp
.

This outweight the NLO corrections obtained previously
Diehl:2007jb
;
Dutrieux:2024bgc
(with the caveat of the large gluon component computed on the lattice), but does not resolve the deconvolution problem of the
D
-term
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
.

However, it confirms what some of us already suspected, that interpreting correctly JLab 6GeV data is really challenging, as the low-
Q^{2}
values do not guarantee that power corrections are actually suppressed.
In that respect, higher
Q^{2}
data at JLab 12 and JLab 20 are more than welcome.
Note that since a significant part of EIC data will be taken at
Q^{2}
of the order of a few
\textrm{GeV}^{2}
, kinematic power corrections remain a relevant topic there.

Finally let us point out that the uncertainty on the subtraction constant remains large, mostly because the real parts of the CFF are poorly known.
A positron beam at JLab or the EIC would allow one to better constrain the latter, improving significantly the extraction of the DVCS subtraction constant.

Appendix A
Hard coefficient functions of the subtraction constant associated to quark GPDs
H
and
E

In accordance to Ref.
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
, both spin-0 and spin-1/2 targets make use of the same three functions
T_{0}^{++}
and
T_{1}^{++}
for building up the CFF
\mathcal{H}^{++}
of GPD
H
and its associated subtraction constant for the dispersion relation. For a spin-1/2, this is also true for
\mathcal{E}^{++}
of GPD
E
. Note, however, that the form the subtraction is spin-dependent as explained in Ref.
Martinez-Fernandez:2025rcg
. These hard coefficient functions are thus

\displaystyle T_{0}^{++}\left(\frac{x}{\xi},\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\right)=

\displaystyle\ C_{\rm LT}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)+\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)-\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}\frac{\mathcal{L}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)+C_{0}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)}{2}\,,

(20)

\displaystyle T_{1}^{++}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)=

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)-\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}^{(+)}\left(\frac{x}{\xi}\right)}{2}\,.

(21)

Here, all the kernels in the RHS are antisymmetric. In particular,
C_{\rm LT}^{(+)}
is the only one surviving the leading-twist limit, can be found at next-to-leading order (NLO) in
\alpha_{s}
in Ref.
Diehl:2007jb
, and
C_{0}^{(+)}
is its LO component. The other two functions come from a twist calculation
Martinez-Fernandez:2025gub
and read

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathbb{P}}_{\rm(iii)}^{(+)}(x/\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2}{x/\xi+1}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{x/\xi-1+i0}{-2+i0}\right)}-(x/\xi\to-x/\xi)\,,

(22)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}^{(+)}(x/\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{x/\xi-1}\left[{\,\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{x/\xi+1}{2-i0}\right)}-{\,\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\right]-(x/\xi\to-x/\xi)\,.

(23)

In the part of the subtraction constant corresponding to the
D
-term, the kernel (
T_{2}^{++}
, in notation of the main text) is a combination of the above two:

T_{2}^{++}(\alpha,t/\mathbb{Q}^{2})=T_{0}^{++}(\alpha,t/\mathbb{Q}^{2})+\frac{t}{\mathbb{Q}^{2}}T_{1}^{++}(\alpha)\,.

(24)

The novel term including the DD
F
for a spin-0 target, and both
F
and
K
for the spin-1/2 case, is the first derivative of
T_{1}^{++}
, this is

\displaystyle T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)\overset{\alpha\in(-1,1)}{=}

\displaystyle\ \Re\ \left(T_{1}^{++\,(1)}(\alpha)\right)

\displaystyle\overset{\alpha\in(-1,1)}{=}

\displaystyle\ \frac{1+3\alpha}{(1+\alpha)^{2}(1-\alpha)}{\,\textrm{ln}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}\right)}-\frac{2}{(1-\alpha)^{2}}\left[{\,\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(\frac{1+\alpha}{2}\right)}-{\,\textrm{Li}_{2}\left(1\right)}\right]-\frac{1}{1-\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle\ +(\alpha\to-\alpha)\,,

(25)

where
\Re
stands for the real part of the function.

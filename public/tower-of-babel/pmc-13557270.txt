Eyelid Cutaneous Metastasis of Parotid Gland Ductal Carcinoma
Authors: Zdeněk Drlík, Martin Tichý, Barbora Šopíková, Veronika Slonková
Published: 2026

Introduction

Salivary duct carcinoma (SDC) is a rare malignant tumor of the salivary glands characterized by aggressive behavior, a high number of nodal and distant metastases, frequent recurrence, and poor prognosis. Cutaneous SDC metastases are extremely rare, with few reported cases [1]. Eyelid skin metastases have previously been described by Parikh et al. [2].

Case Presentation

A 59-year-old male was admitted to the Dermatology Department due to swelling and a papular rash on the eyelids of the right eye, which had lasted for two months and was unresponsive to systemic antiviral and multiple local therapies. Two years prior, the patient had undergone surgery for salivary duct carcinoma of the right parotid gland, with bilateral neck dissection and subsequent brachytherapy.

Firm swelling of both eyelids of the right eye made opening the eye impossible; no further ocular pathology was observed. A rash composed of multiple white and reddish-purple papules was present on the eyelids and, to a lesser degree, on the right cheek, bilaterally on the neck, and in the right clavicle region. These areas were diffusely covered by an asymptomatic reddish-brown, sharply defined erythema. Dermoscopy showed polymorphic white and reddish-purple globules, microhemorrhage, and fine angiectasia on erythematous background (Figure 1). Histopathological analysis from the papules of the right upper eyelid confirmed metastatic SDC, with a dominating involvement of the lymphatic vessels (Figure 2).

Conclusion

Salivary carcinomas arise from the major salivary glands (submandibular, sublingual, parotid, and minor salivary glands) and may be located in the nasal cavity, paranasal sinuses, and tear glands. SDC is one of 21 histological subtypes. Tumors may metastasize to the lungs, skeletal system, cervical, mediastinal and axillary lymph nodes, adrenal glands, brain, and liver, but rarely to the skin. Prognosis is poor; 5-year survival is 23–42%, based on stage [3].

Histologically, SDC cells are characterized by large pleomorphic nuclei with prominent nucleoli, mitoses, and abundant eosinophilic cytoplasm. The tumor may form nests, cords, and cribriform structures with comedonecrosis. Immunohistochemically, SDC exhibits strong, diffuse expression for androgen receptor as well as GATA binding protein 3, gross cystic disease fluid protein 15, or cytokeratins. Unlike the morphologically similar ductal adenocarcinoma of the breast, it lacks estrogen and progesterone receptor expression [2,3].

Less than 10% of metastases from internal organ tumors involve the skin; breast cancer is the most common source for females and lung cancer for males. The incidence of cutaneous metastases is associated with underdiagnosis due to its polymorphous appearance including erythema, papules, nodules, plaques, ulcerations, edema, or alopecia [4,5]. Carcinoma hemorrhagiectoides, appearing as a large plaque of cutaneous metastases on the chest, was described as a “shield sign” in two patients with SDC and two patients with breast cancer [4].

More than 50% of metastases to the eyelids are from breast cancer (1/4 are observed prior to diagnosis of the primary tumor); other tumors include cancers of the prostate, lung, stomach, colon, thyroid gland, and malignant melanoma [2,5]. Monitoring skin changes including dermoscopy is an important part of the long-term follow-up of cancer patients; histopathological analysis is necessary to confirm the diagnosis.

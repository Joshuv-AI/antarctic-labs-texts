Abstract
. The conformal bienergy functional E 2 c E_{2}^{c} was recently introduced as a modified version of the classical bienergy functional E 2 E_{2} in order to ensure the validity of some conformal invariance properties. The critical points of E 2 c E_{2}^{c} are called conformal-biharmonic and denoted c c -biharmonic. In this paper we study the c c -biharmonic hypersurfaces M m M^{m} with constant principal curvatures in the product space 𝕃 m ​ ( ε ) × ℝ {\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R} , where 𝕃 m ​ ( ε ) {\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon) denotes a space form of constant sectional curvature ε \varepsilon . Specifically, we demonstrate that M m M^{m} is either totally geodesic or a cylindrical hypersurface of the form M m − 1 × ℝ M^{m-1}\times\mathbb{R} , where M m − 1 M^{m-1} is a c c -biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in 𝕃 m ​ ( ε ) {\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon) . To provide further insight, we describe the structure of c c -biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in space forms. In the final part, as a preliminary effort to understand c c -biharmonic hypersurfaces M m M^{m} in 𝕃 m ​ ( ε ) × ℝ {\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R} with non constant mean curvature, we establish that a totally umbilical c c -biharmonic hypersurface must necessarily be totally geodesic.

1.
Introduction

For smooth maps
\phi:(M^{m},g)\to(N^{n},h)
between two Riemannian manifolds we define the
bienergy functional
by

(1.1)

\displaystyle E_{2}(\phi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\tau(\phi)|^{2}\ v_{g}\,,

where

\displaystyle\tau(\phi):=\trace\bar{\nabla}d\phi,\qquad\tau(\phi)\in C\left(\phi^{-1}TN\right),

represents the
tension field of the map
and
\bar{\nabla}
is the connection on the pull-back bundle
\phi^{-1}TN
. Solutions of
\tau(\phi)=0
are called
harmonic maps
, while the critical points of (
1.1
) are called
biharmonic maps
and can be expressed by the vanishing of the
bitension field

\tau_{2}(\phi)
as follows:

(1.2)

\displaystyle 0=\tau_{2}(\phi):=-\bar{\Delta}\tau(\phi)-\trace R^{N}(d\phi(\cdot),\tau(\phi))d\phi(\cdot)\,,

where
\bar{\Delta}
is the rough Laplacian defined on sections of the pull-back bundle
\phi^{-1}TN
and
R^{N}

is the curvature operator on
(N^{n},h)
.
Biharmonic maps have received a lot of attention in recent years and for the current status of research on biharmonic maps we refer to the book
[
13
]
, while for biharmonic hypersurfaces one may consult
[
7
]
.

The bienergy functional (
1.1
) is invariant under isometries of the domain.
However, regarding the invariance under conformal transformations, it turns out that the bienergy (
1.1
) does not enjoy conformal invariance in any dimension. To repair this flaw in
[
2
]
the authors have considered a modified version of the bienergy (
1.1
) with two additional terms as follows

(1.3)

\displaystyle E^{c}_{2}(\phi):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left(|\tau(\phi)|^{2}+\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{Scal}|d\phi|^{2}-2\trace\langle d\phi\left(\operatorname{Ric}(\cdot)\right),d\phi(\cdot)\rangle\right)\ v_{g}\,,

where
\operatorname{Scal}
is the scalar curvature of
M
and
\operatorname{Ric}
is its Ricci tensor.
It was shown in
[
2
]
that, when
\dim M=4
, the conformal bienergy (
1.3
) is invariant under conformal transformations of the domain. Consequently, this functional can be viewed as the natural higher-order generalization of the classical energy functional, which is conformally invariant in
\dim M=2
. The structure of (
1.3
) is inspired by the Paneitz operator, a fourth-order elliptic operator in conformal geometry. For details, see
[
10
, Chapter 4]
and the original work
[
14
]
.

We call (
1.3
) the
conformal bienergy functional
.
The critical points of the conformal bienergy (
1.3
) are those who satisfy (see
[
2
]
)

(1.4)

\displaystyle 0=\tau_{2}^{c}(\phi)

\displaystyle:=\tau_{2}(\phi)-\frac{2}{3}\operatorname{Scal}\tau(\phi)+2\trace(\bar{\nabla}d\phi)(\operatorname{Ric}(\cdot),\cdot)+\frac{1}{3}d\phi(\nabla\operatorname{Scal}).

Solutions of (
1.4
) will be called
conformal biharmonic maps
, or simply

c
-biharmonic maps
.

In the present paper we are interested in
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces, that is isometric immersions
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow(N^{m+1},h)
which are solutions of (
1.4
).

When
(N^{m+1},h)
is a space form
{\mathbb{L}}^{m+1}(\varepsilon)
with constant sectional curvature
\varepsilon
a study of
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures was initiated in
[
2
]
. In particular, they proved that in the case
\varepsilon=0
(
{\mathbb{L}}^{m+1}(0)={\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
) the only examples are the totally geodesic planes. When
\varepsilon=-1
(
{\mathbb{L}}^{m+1}(-1)={\mathbb{H}}^{m+1}
) they give a detailed analysis of
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with at most two distinct constant principal curvatures. It should be noted that in space forms a hypersurface has constant principal curvatures if and only if it is isoparametric. Moreover, if the ambient space is
{\mathbb{H}}^{m+1}
, a classical argument of Cartan
[
3
]
shows that an isoparametric hypersurface has at most two distinct principal curvatures, thus the study in
[
2
]
covers all the
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{H}}^{m+1}
. As for the case of isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
, in
[
2
]
, the authors give a full description of those with
1
or
2
principal curvatures and an example with
4
distinct principal curvatures.

When
(N^{m+1},h)
is the product
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
a hypersurface with constant principal curvatures is not necessarily isoparametric. In fact it is known (see
[
4
]
) that hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures are isoparametric provided that the angle
\alpha
between the normal to the hypersurface and the
{\mathbb{R}}
direction of the ambient space is constant. Recently, in
[
6
]
, the authors proved that an isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
\varepsilon=\pm 1
, has constant angle. Thus we have that isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
,
\varepsilon=\pm 1
, are equivalent to hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures and constant angle.

Our first contribution is the following theorem which characterizes
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures in the product
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
without the assumption that the angle
\alpha
is constant.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M^{m}
be a
c
-biharmonic hypersurface with constant principal curvatures in the product
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
. Then,
M^{m}
is totally geodesic or
M^{m}=M^{m-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
, where
M^{m-1}
is a
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)
.

Consequently, the complete understanding of
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures in the product space
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
reduces to a comprehensive study of
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures in the space form
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)
. As previously mentioned,
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces with constant principal curvatures in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)
have been fully classified for
\varepsilon\in\{-1,0\}
, whereas only partial results are available for
\varepsilon=1
. For this reason, in Section
4
, we provide a study of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
considering all possible degrees (i.e., the number of distinct principal curvatures)
\ell=1,2,3,4,6
.

Finally, as an initial step toward understanding
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces
M^{m}
in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
with non constant principal curvatures, we examine totally umbilical hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
. These are hypersurfaces characterized by having a single principal curvature, denoted
H
, with multiplicity
m
. In the case of hypersurfaces in a space form, it is straightforward to show that totally umbilical hypersurfaces must have constant
H
. By contrast, the mean curvature
H
of a totally umbilical hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
is not necessarily a constant (see, for instance,
[
5
]
).
For this class of hypersurfaces, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
be a
c
-biharmonic totally umbilical hypersurface. Then
M^{m}
is totally geodesic.

The paper is organized as follows: in Section
2
we carry out some preliminary computations on
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{L}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
. In Section
3
we prove Theorem
1.1
. Next, in Section
4
we study in detail
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in the Euclidean sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}

.

Notations
. Throughout this article we will use the following sign conventions:
for the Riemannian curvature tensor field we use

R(X,Y)Z=[\nabla_{X},\nabla_{Y}]Z-\nabla_{[X,Y]}Z,

for the Ricci tensor field

g(\operatorname{Ric}(X),Y)=\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\trace\left\{Z\to R(Z,X)Y\right\},

and the scalar curvature is given by

\operatorname{Scal}=\trace\operatorname{Ric}.

The trace is taken with respect to the domain metric and we write
\trace
instead of
\trace_{g}
.
For the rough Laplacian on the pull-back bundle
\phi^{-1}TN
we employ the geometers sign convention

\bar{\Delta}=-\trace(\bar{\nabla}\bar{\nabla}-\bar{\nabla}_{\nabla}).

2.
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}

Let
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)
represent, depending on the value of
\varepsilon
, the space form
{\mathbb{S}}^{m}
,
{\mathbb{H}}^{m}
or
{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
with
constant curvature
\varepsilon=1
,
-1
or
0
, respectively.

Given a hypersurface

\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}\,,

let
\eta
denote a unit vector field normal to

\varphi
and let
\partial_{t}
denote a unit vector field tangent to the second factor
{\mathbb{R}}
of the ambient space. Let us denote by
\langle,\rangle
the product metric in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times{\mathbb{R}}
. We further denote by
A
the
shape operator
, by
H=\trace A/m
the
mean curvature function
and by
B(X,Y)=\langle A(X),Y\rangle\eta
its
second fundamental form
,
X,Y\in C(TM^{m})
.

Since
\partial_{t}
is a unit vector field globally defined on the
ambient space
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(c)\times\mathbb{R}
, we can decompose it in the
following form

(2.1)

\partial_{t}=d\varphi(T)+\cos\alpha\,\eta,

where
\cos\alpha=\langle\partial_{t},\eta\rangle
and
d\varphi(T),T\in C(TM^{m})
, denotes the tangential component of
\partial_{t}
. In the sequel we shall identify
T
with
d\varphi(T)
.

Using that
\partial_{t}
is parallel on
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(c)\times\mathbb{R}
, a direct
computation yields the useful formulas

(2.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{X}T

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\cos\alpha\,A(X),

(2.3)

\displaystyle X(\cos\alpha)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\langle A(X),T\rangle,

for every tangent vector field
X\in C(TM^{m})
.

The Riemannian curvature tensor of
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
can be easily computed and it is given
by the formula

\displaystyle\bar{R}(\bar{X},\bar{Y})\bar{Z}=\varepsilon\{\langle\bar{Y},\bar{Z}\rangle\bar{X}-\langle\bar{X},\bar{Z}\rangle\bar{Y}-\langle\bar{Y},\partial_{t}\rangle\langle\bar{Z},\partial_{t}\rangle\bar{X}+\langle\bar{X},\partial_{t}\rangle\langle\bar{Z},\partial_{t}\rangle\bar{Y}

(2.4)

\displaystyle+\langle\bar{X},\bar{Z}\rangle\langle\bar{Y},\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}-\langle\bar{Y},\bar{Z}\rangle\langle\bar{X},\partial_{t}\rangle\partial_{t}\},

where
\bar{X},\bar{Y},\bar{Z}
are vector fields on
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Finally, the Codazzi equation is given by

(2.5)

\displaystyle(\nabla_{X}A)Y-(\nabla_{Y}A)X=\varepsilon\cos\alpha(\langle Y,T\rangle X-\langle X,T\rangle Y),

where
X,Y\in C(TM^{m})
.

We are in the right position to give, in terms of the angle function
\alpha
, the

c
-biharmonic tension field (
1.4
) of a hypersurface
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
be a hypersurface. Then the
c
-biharmonic tension field is given by

\tau_{2}^{c}=\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\top}+\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\perp}

where

(2.6)

\displaystyle\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\top}=

\displaystyle-2mA(\grad H)-m^{2}H\grad H-2m(m-1)\varepsilon H\cos\alpha\,T

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3}(m-1)\varepsilon\cos\alpha A(T)-\frac{1}{3}\grad|A|^{2}+\frac{2}{3}m^{2}H\grad H

is the tangential component, while

(2.7)

\displaystyle\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\perp}=

\displaystyle\big\{-m\Delta H+\frac{2}{3}m(m-1)(3-m)\varepsilon H+\frac{1}{3}m(7m-13)\varepsilon H\sin^{2}\alpha

1.4

The tension field of the hypersurface is
\tau(\phi)=mH\eta

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(\phi)=

Let
\{E_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
be a local orthonormal frame field on
M

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}(X,Y)=\sum_{i}\langle R(X,E_{i})E_{i},Y\rangle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\langle\bar{R}(X,E_{i})E_{i},Y\rangle

\displaystyle+\sum_{i}\left(\langle B(X,Y),B(E_{i},E_{i})\rangle-\langle B(X,E_{i}),B(Y,E_{i})\rangle\right)\,.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We first recall a result of Chaves and Santos.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
)
.

Let
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
be a hypersurface with constant principal curvatures such that
T
is a principal direction and
\cos\alpha\neq 0
for all
p\in M
. Then
\varepsilon=-1
,
A(T)=0
and the other
m-1
principal curvatures are all equal either to
b/\sqrt{1+b^{2}}
or to
-b/\sqrt{1+b^{2}}
for some real constant
b
.

If we assume that the principal curvatures of the hypersurfaces are constant, we have that

H=\frac{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{m}}{m}={\rm constant}\hskip 9.24994pt{\rm and}\hskip 9.24994pt|A|^{2}=k_{1}^{2}+\cdots+k_{m}^{2}={\rm constant}\,.

Now, assuming that both
H
and
|A|^{2}
are constant, the tangential component (
2.6
) of
\tau_{2}^{c}
simplifies to

(3.1)

\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\top}=-\frac{2}{3}\varepsilon\,(m-1)\cos\alpha\big(3mH\,T+2A(T)\big)\,.

Consequently, if
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}
is
c
-biharmonic, one of the following three cases must occur.

Case
\varepsilon=0
. Then
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}=\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
and
\varphi:M^{m}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
is a
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
. Now, isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
are: totally geodesic hyperplanes; round spheres; cylinders over round spheres. Consequently, for the isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
it is straightforward to check that the vanishing of the normal component (
2.7
) of
\tau_{2}^{c}
, that is

(3.2)

\frac{5}{3}mH|A|^{2}-2\trace A^{3}-\frac{2}{3}m^{3}H^{3}=0\,,

implies that
A=0
. Thus the
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{m+1}
are only totally geodesic hyperplanes.

Case
\cos\alpha=0
. In this case
\partial_{t}
is tangent to the hypersurface and
\varphi(M^{m})
is locally a cylinder
M^{m-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}
where
M^{m-1}\hookrightarrow{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(c)
is a
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in the space form
{\mathbb{L}}^{m}(\varepsilon)
.

Case
\varepsilon\neq 0
and
\cos\alpha\neq 0
. In this case, from (
3.1
), the vector field
T
satisfies

(3.3)

A(T)=-\frac{3mH}{2}T

which implies that
T
is a principal direction with corresponding principal curvature
-3mH/2
. We can thus apply Theorem
3.1
and since the principal curvature corresponding to
T
is
0
, we have that
H=0
. Now, the vanishing of the normal component (
2.7
) of
\tau_{2}^{c}
gives

\trace A^{3}=\pm(m-1)\frac{b^{3}}{(1+b^{2})^{3/2}}=0\,.

Thus
b=0
and the hypersurface is totally geodesic.

4.
Isoparametric
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}

In
[
2
]
the authors began the study of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in space forms. As a premise, they proved the following results:

Proposition 4.1
.

(a)

Any totally geodesic hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m+1}(\varepsilon)
is
c
-biharmonic.

(b)

Let
M^{m}
be a minimal hypersurface in
{\mathbb{L}}^{m+1}(\varepsilon)
. Then
M^{m}
is
c
-biharmonic if and only if its scalar curvature is constant and
{\rm Tr}\,A^{3}=0
.

From this, they deduced that any hyperplane in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
is
c
-biharmonic, while there is no example of
c
-biharmonic hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
amongst
m
-dimensional round spheres or cylinders.

Next, they characterized
c
-biharmonic small hyperspheres and generalized Clifford tori in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
. Finally, they gave a full analysis of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in the hyperbolic space
{\mathbb{H}}^{m+1}
.

As integration of the above work, in this section we investigate the existence of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
in the remaining cases, that is, isoparametric hypersurfaces of degree
\ell=3,4,6
.

We recall that each isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
is CMC and has
\ell
distinct constant principal curvatures
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
with constant multiplicities
m_{1},\ldots,m_{\ell}
,
m_{1}+\ldots+m_{\ell}=m
. Moreover, the only possible values for
\ell
are
1,2,3,4,6
, and
m_{i+2}=m_{i}
, so that there are at most two distinct multiplicities, which we shall denote
m_{1},m_{2}
. The integer
\ell
is called the
degree
of the isoparametric hypersurface.

We follow the notation of
[
12
]
. By a suitable choice of the orientation we can assume
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
and we have the following explicit description of the principal curvatures of the isoparametric families
M_{s}^{m}
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
,
s\in(0,\pi/\ell)
:

(4.1)

k_{i}(s)=\cot\left(s+\frac{(i-1)\pi}{\ell}\right),\hskip 9.24994pti=1,\ldots,\ell\,.

Next, we recall some further useful facts:

Case
\ell=3
. There exist only four examples of this type, corresponding to
m_{1}=m_{2}=1,2,4,8
.

Case
\ell=6
. It was proved in
[
1
]
that in this case necessarily
m_{1}=m_{2}
and the possible values are
m_{1}=1
or
m_{1}=2
.

Case
\ell=4
. This is the richest case. The only examples with
m_{1}=m_{2}
occur when
m_{1}=m_{2}=1
or
m_{1}=m_{2}=2
, but we have plenty of examples with
m_{1}<m_{2}
. Indeed, instances occur when
m_{1}=4
and
m_{2}=5
, or
1+m_{1}+m_{2}
is a multiple of
2^{\xi(m_{1}-1)}
, where
\xi(n)
denotes the number of natural numbers
p
such that
1\leq p\leq n
and
p\equiv 0,1,2,4\,({\rm mod}\,8)
.

To give a more explicit idea of the situation, we point out that the following pairs of multiplicities come about:
m_{1}=1,m_{2}\geq 2
;
m_{1}=2,m_{2}=2k,k\geq 2
;
m_{1}=3,m_{2}=4
;
m_{1}=4,m_{2}=4k-5,k\geq 3
.

Our first result is

Theorem 4.2
.

(a)

Let
M^{m}
be a minimal isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
\ell
,
\ell=3\,\,{\rm or}\,\,6\,
. Then
M^{m}
is
c
-biharmonic.

(b)

Let
M^{m}
be a minimal isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
4
and assume that
m_{1}=m_{2}
. Then
M^{m}
is
c
-biharmonic.

Proof.

According to Proposition
4.1
(b) we just have to verify that in our hypotheses the scalar curvature is constant and
{\rm Tr}\,A^{3}=0
. As for the statement concerning
\operatorname{Scal}
, we recall that

\operatorname{Scal}=m(m-1)+m^{2}H^{2}-|A|^{2}\,.

Then, since
H
and
|A|^{2}
are constant, so is
\operatorname{Scal}
. Thus, it remains to compute
\trace A^{3}
. In both cases
\ell=3,6
we know that
m_{1}=m_{2}
, while if
\ell=4
this is true by assumption.

It follows easily that, for each fixed
\ell
, the condition
\trace A^{3}=0
becomes equivalent to

(4.2)

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\left(k_{i}(s)\right)^{3}=0\,.

But minimality occurs if and only if
s=\pi/(2\ell)
and using (
4.1
) we can handle the three cases:

\begin{array}[]{ll}{\rm Case}\,\ell=3\colon&k_{1}=\sqrt{3},\,k_{2}=0,\,k_{3}=-k_{1}\,;\\
{\rm Case}\,\ell=4\colon&k_{1}=\sqrt{2}+1,\,k_{2}=\sqrt{2}-1,\,k_{3}=-k_{2},\,k_{4}=-k_{1}\,;\\
{\rm Case}\,\ell=6\colon&k_{1}=\sqrt{3}+2,\,k_{2}=1,\,k_{3}=2-\sqrt{3},\,k_{4}=-k_{3},\,k_{5}=-k_{2},\,k_{6}=-k_{1}\end{array}

from which it is immediate to deduce that (
4.2
) holds and so the proof is completed.

∎

For our purposes, it is now useful to recall that, from
[
2
]
, an isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
is
c
-biharmonic if and only if

(4.3)

[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(s)=Hm\left(5|A|^{2}-2m^{2}H^{2}-2m^{2}+11m-6\right)-6\trace A^{3}=0\,.

Since (
4.3
) represents the vanishing of the normal component of
\tau_{2}^{c}
, we have denoted with
[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(s)
the left-hand side of (
4.3
).

Therefore, we are now in the position to perform a more specific analysis of the existence of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces of degree
\ell
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
,
\ell=3,4,6
.
Indeed, since according to (
4.1
) we know precisely the principal curvatures, we can plug this information into (
4.3
) and obtain the explicit condition of
c
-biharmonicity in terms of the parameter
s
.

We shall obtain a converse of Theorem
4.2
. To this purpose, we proceed case by case.

Case
\ell=3
.  We know that there exist only four examples of this type, corresponding to
m_{1}=m_{2}=1,2,4,8
. The
c
-biharmonicity equation (
4.3
) becomes

(4.4)

\frac{27m_{1}\cos(3s)\Big(2m_{1}^{2}+m_{1}\cos(6s)-6m_{1}+6\Big)}{\sin^{3}(s)(2\cos(2s)+1)^{3}}=0\,.

Now, a routine analysis shows that, on the relevant interval
(0,\pi/3)
, the unique solution of equation (
4.4
) is
s=\pi/6
and this value corresponds to the minimal hypersurface.

By way of conclusion, we have shown that the converse of Theorem
4.2
(a) holds in this case, that is:

the minimal isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
3
are the only
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
3
.

Case
\ell=6
. In this case necessarily
m_{1}=m_{2}
and the possible values are
m_{1}=1
or
m_{1}=2
. The analysis proceeds precisely as in the case that
\ell=3
and so we omit the details.

Again, the conclusion is:
the minimal isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
6
are the only
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
6
.

Case
\ell=4
. In this case it is convenient to separate the two instances
m_{1}=m_{2}
and
m_{1}<m_{2}
.

Subcase
m_{1}=m_{2}
. The analysis can be carried out as in the cases
\ell=3,6
and so we omit the details.
The conclusion is:
when
m_{1}=m_{2}
, the minimal isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
4
are the only
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
4
.

Subcase
m_{1}<m_{2}
. This case is interesting because it provides examples of
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces which are
non minimal
. More precisely, we prove:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
M_{s}^{m}
,
s\in(0,\pi/4)
, be a family of isoparametric hypersurfaces of degree
4
in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
with
m_{1}<m_{2}
,
m=2(m_{1}+m_{2})
. Then there exists
s^{*}\in(0,\pi/4)
such that
M_{s^{*}}^{m}
is a non minimal,
c
-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
.

Proof.

Direct substitution and simplification yields:

H=\frac{\Big((m_{1}+m_{2})\cos(4s)+m_{1}-m_{2}\Big)}{\sin(4s)(m_{1}+m_{2})}\,

[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}=\frac{1}{32\sin^{3}(s)\cos^{9}(s)\left(\tan^{2}(s)-1\right)^{3}}\,A(s)\,,

where

(4.5)

\displaystyle A(s)=

\displaystyle 2(m_{1}-m_{2})\big(12(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})+8m_{1}m_{2}-33(m_{1}+m_{2})+36\big)

\displaystyle+\big(32(m_{1}^{3}+m_{2}^{3})-83(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})-6m_{1}m_{2}+96(m_{1}+m_{2})\big)\cos(4s)

\displaystyle+2(m_{1}-m_{2})\big(4(m_{1}^{2}+m_{2}^{2})+8m_{1}m_{2}-7(m_{1}+m_{2})+12\big)\cos(8s)

\displaystyle+3(m_{1}+m_{2})^{2}\cos(12s)\,.

Now, it is easy to check that there exists a unique value
\tilde{s}\in(0,\pi/4)
such that
H(\tilde{s})=0
, i.e.,

\tilde{s}=\frac{1}{4}\cos^{-1}\left(\frac{m_{2}-m_{1}}{m_{1}+m_{2}}\right)\,.

Next, we compute

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}A(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 32m_{1}(6+m_{1}(-5+2m_{1}))>0

\displaystyle\lim_{s\to\pi/4^{-}}A(s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-32m_{2}(6+m_{2}(-5+2m_{2}))<0\,.

From this it is easy to deduce that
\lim_{s\to 0^{+}}[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(s)=-\infty
and
\lim_{s\to\pi/4^{-}}[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(s)=+\infty
.

Therefore, there exists
s^{*}\in(0,\pi/4)
such that
[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(s^{*})=0
.

Finally,

[\tau_{2}^{c}]^{\perp}(\tilde{s})=\frac{48(m_{1}^{2}-m_{2}^{2})}{\sqrt{m_{1}m_{2}}}\neq 0

which implies
s^{*}\neq\tilde{s}
. Therefore
M_{s^{*}}^{m}
is
c
-biharmonic and non minimal, as required to end the proof of the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We point out that the analysis in the proof of Theorem
4.3
has shown that
minimal isoparametric hypersurface in
{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
of degree
\ell=4
with
m_{1}<m_{2}
are not
c
-biharmonic
.

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
11
, Theorem 5.1]
compact isoparametric Willmore hypersurfaces in

{\mathbb{S}}^{m+1}
are characterized. Their structure is similar to c-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in spheres: For
\ell=2
they are represented by certain Clifford tori,
while for
\ell=3,6
they must be minimal. As in the case of c-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in spheres,
\ell=4
represents the richest case and allows for additional non-minimal solutions.

Remark 4.6
.

We would like to point that Theorem
4.3
highlights another interesting
difference between biharmonic and c-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in spheres. It is well-known that there does not exist a proper biharmonic isoparametric hypersurface in spheres of degree
\ell=4
, see
[
9
]
for the details,
whereas Theorem
4.3
shows that there exist c-biharmonic isoparametric hypersurfaces in this case.

\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\top}=-\frac{1}{3}m\left(m+8\right)H\grad H-\frac{2}{3}(m-1)(3m+2)\varepsilon H\cos\alpha\,T\,,

(5.2)

\displaystyle\left[\tau_{2}^{c}\right]^{\perp}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{3}m\left(2m^{2}-5m+6\right)H^{3}\big\}\eta\,.

Moreover, under the assumption that
A=H\operatorname{Id}
the Codazzi equation (
2.5

(5.3)

\displaystyle[X(H)+\varepsilon\cos\alpha\langle X,T\rangle]Y-[Y(H)+\varepsilon\cos\alpha(\langle Y,T\rangle]X=0\,.

Since the dimension of
M

m\geq 2
, for any tangent vector field
X

Y

(5.4)

\displaystyle X(H)+\varepsilon\cos\alpha\langle X,T\rangle=0\,,\hskip 9.24994pt\forall X\in C(TM)\,.

Next (

(5.5)

\displaystyle\grad H=-\varepsilon\cos\alpha T\,

that substituted in (

(5m^{2}-10m-4)H\grad H=0\,.

Hydrostatic bubbles of compressible fluid in an incompressible fluid Thanks: J. Jang was supported by an NSF Grant (DMS #2306910) Thanks: I. Tice was supported by an NSF Grant (DMS #2508400)
Juhi Jang, Ian Tice

Abstract
. We study stationary configurations of compressible barotropic fluids lying inside an incompressible fluid and acted upon by a constant gravitational field. Without gravity, it is a simple matter to construct solutions consisting of perfectly spherical bubbles of compressible fluid, but with gravity the stratification of the compressible fluid’s density prevents the existence of solutions with such simple geometries. We construct smooth solutions with nontrivial gravity and nearly spherical, axisymmetric geometry by means of a bifurcation argument in an appropriate weighted Sobolev space, defined in terms of the Legendre operator. Along the way, we develop a number of essential linear and nonlinear functional analytic tools for this scale of spaces, which are of independent interest.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In this paper we study hydrostatic bubbles of compressible fluid within an incompressible fluid in the presence of a constant gravitational field. The two fluids together fill a given and fixed open set
\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which we assume decomposes into the disjoint union

(1.1)

\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}\cup\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}\cup\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\cup\Sigma,

with
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
an open set occupied by the incompressible fluid and
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
an open, bounded, and simply connected set occupied by the compressible fluid. In addition, we will assume that
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}
is a smooth manifold with boundary, so
\Sigma=\partial\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is then a smooth, compact two-dimensional submanifold. This is a free boundary problem, as the sets
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
are not specified a priori and must be constructed. In doing so we will solve for
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
and then define
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}\backslash\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}
, which justifies our notation indicating the compressible bubble is the interior and the incompressible fluid is the exterior. We emphasize that there are many possibilities for the set
\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
: it can be taken to be all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, a bounded open set modeling a container with rigid walls, a horizontally infinite layer, or something more complicated. Regardless, we will always assume the coordinate system in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
is chosen so that
0\in\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
. It will be useful to define

(1.2)

R^{\mathrm{max}}=\sup\{s>0\;|\;s\mathbb{B}^{3}=B(0,s)\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}\}\in(0,\infty],

where
\mathbb{B}^{3}=B(0,1)
is the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
.

Our interest in this paper is hydrostatic solutions acted upon by a constant gravitational field
-ge_{3}=(0,0,-g)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
, which we take to mean that both fluids have trivial velocity fields. In turn, this means that viscous effects are irrelevant and that the fluid configurations are determined solely by their density and pressure profiles, together with a jump condition involving the curvature of
\Sigma
, known as the Laplace-Young equation:

(1.3)

\begin{cases}\nabla\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}=-\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}ge_{3}&\text{in }\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}\\
\nabla[\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}]=-\rho_{\mathrm{int}}ge_{3}&\text{in }\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\\
\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\Sigma}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}&\text{on }\Sigma.\end{cases}

The various terms appearing in (
1.3
) are as follows. The parameter
g\in\mathbb{R}
is the gravitational constant; in principle we could consider only
g\geq 0
to give the gravitational field a preferred downward direction, but the theory we develop in this paper works more generally. The incompressible fluid’s density is the given constant
\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}:=(0,\infty)
, and its pressure is the unknown
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}:\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}\to\mathbb{R}
. The compressible fluid’s density is the unknown function
\rho_{\mathrm{int}}:\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
, which we assume determines its pressure through a barotropic equation of state. More precisely, we assume we are given a smooth function
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\prime}>0
and
\lim_{t\to 0}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(t)=0
; then the pressure in the compressible fluid is
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}:\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Finally, the term
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is the coefficient of surface tension, and
\mathscr{K}_{\Sigma}
is the total curvature (twice the mean curvature) of the surface
\Sigma
, normalized so that
\mathscr{K}_{\mathbb{S}^{2}}=2
for
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
the unit sphere.

It turns out to be trivial to solve for
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}
satisfying the first equation in (
1.3
) in all of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and relatively easy to solve for
\rho_{\mathrm{int}}
satisfying the second equation in a slab-like set of the form
\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-r,r]
for
0<r<R^{\mathrm{max}}
. With these in hand, our task will then reduce to constructing
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}\subset[\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-r,r]]\cap\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
satisfying the third equation in (
1.3
). Before further discussing this task, we justify these solvability claims.

The first equation in (
1.3
) is solved by
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
given by

(1.4)

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}(x)=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}-g\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}x_{3}

for a given reference pressure
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To solve for
\rho_{\mathrm{int}}
we must first introduce some notation. It will be convenient to write
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}}=\sup_{t>0}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(t)\in(0,\infty]
so that
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}
is a smooth, increasing diffeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})
. Next, we define the smooth enthalpy function
\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
via

(1.5)

\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(z)=\int_{\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{z}t^{-1}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\prime}(t)dt.

Note that
\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\prime}>0
, and so
\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}
is a smooth, increasing diffeomorphism from
\mathbb{R}_{+}
to
\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=(\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{\min}},\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})
, where

(1.6)

-\infty\leq\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{min}}=-\int_{0}^{\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}}t^{-1}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\prime}(t)dt<0<\int_{\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{\infty}t^{-1}\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\prime}(t)dt=\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}}\leq\infty.

Note also that we have chosen the normalization

(1.7)

\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho_{\mathrm{ext}})=0.

The point of introducing
\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}
is that the equation

(1.8)

\nabla[\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}]=-g\rho_{\mathrm{int}}e_{3}

reduces to

(1.9)

\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}(x)=\alpha-gx_{3}

for some arbitrary
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
. We can then solve for
\rho_{\mathrm{int}}:\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-r,r]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
via

(1.10)

\rho_{\mathrm{int}}(x)=\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}(\alpha-gx_{3}),

provided that
\alpha\in\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
,
0<r<R^{\mathrm{max}}
, and

(1.11)

\alpha-g[-r,r]\subseteq\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{R}_{+}).

This suggests that we introduce the smooth, increasing diffeomorphism
\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}:(\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{\min}},\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})\to(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})
via
\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}
. Indeed, with this notation in hand we then find that the pressure in the compressible fluid is

(1.12)

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}(x)=\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha-gx_{3}).

1.2.
Spherical bubbles

When we neglect gravity by setting
g=0
in (
1.3
), then (
1.4
), (
1.10
), and (
1.12
) reduce to the constants

(1.13)

\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast},\ \rho_{\mathrm{int}}=\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}(\alpha),\text{ and }\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}=\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha)

for arbitrary
\alpha\in\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, which are defined throughout
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. In particular, this means that the right-hand side of the third equation in (
1.3
) is constant. By Alexandrov’s soap bubble theorem
[
2
]
, the only compact, connected, embedded hypersurface with constant mean curvature is a sphere, and hence we must choose
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}=R_{\alpha}\mathbb{B}^{3}=B(0,R_{\alpha})
.
As the total curvature of a sphere is twice the reciprocal of its radius, for such a ball we have a solution if and only if the radius
R_{\alpha}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfies

(1.14)

\frac{2\sigma}{R_{\alpha}}=\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}.

Now, we can solve this for
R_{\alpha}
precisely when
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}\in(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})
, in which case we have a one-parameter family of solutions with

(1.15)

R_{\alpha}=\frac{2\sigma}{\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\alpha)-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}}\text{ for all }\alpha\in(\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}),\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}}).

The final condition to check in order to arrive at a proper solution to (
1.3
) is that
R_{\alpha}<R^{\mathrm{max}}
, where the latter was defined in (
1.2
), so that we can indeed guarantee the inclusion
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}}\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
. To summarize, when
g=0
we have a perfectly spherical bubble solution to (
1.3
) with
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}=R_{\alpha}\mathbb{B}^{3}
and (
1.13
) precisely when

(1.16)

\frac{2\sigma}{R^{\mathrm{max}}}+\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}\in(0,\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}})\text{ and }\alpha\in(\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}+2\sigma/R^{\mathrm{max}}),\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}}).

As the parameter
\alpha
increases, the bubble radius decreases and the pressure inside the bubble increases.

The simple story above changes quite a bit when gravity is nontrivial, i.e.
g\neq 0
. In this case the vertical stratification of
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}(x)
and
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int}}(x)
imply that spherical bubble solutions are no longer available.

1.3.
Main result

Our main result in this paper establishes that under a minor strengthening of the assumption (
1.16
), nearly spherical bubble solutions persist for all choices of
g
in an open neighborhood of
0
. The strengthening of (
1.16
) we need is that

(1.17)

\frac{2\sigma}{R^{\mathrm{max}}}+\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}<\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}),\text{ or equivalently }\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}\left(\frac{2\sigma}{R^{\mathrm{max}}}+\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}\right)<0<\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}},

where the equivalence follows from the normalization (
1.7
). We can now state our result after noting that we define the norm on the Banach space
C^{m}_{b}([-1,1])
for
m\in\mathbb{N}
to be

(1.18)

\left\|f\right\|_{C^{m}_{b}}=\max_{0\leq k\leq m}\sup_{\left|\zeta\right|\leq 1}\left|D^{k}f(\zeta)\right|.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume (
1.17
) holds. Then there exists an open interval
0\in G\subseteq\mathbb{R}
and functions
A:G\to\mathbb{R}
and
\varphi:G\to C^{\infty}([-1,1])
such that the following hold.

(1)

A(0)=0
,
\mathfrak{P}_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}\left(\frac{2\sigma}{R^{\mathrm{max}}}+\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}\right)<A(g)<\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{\mathrm{max}}
for all
g\in G
, and
A
is smooth.

(2)

\varphi(0)=0
, and for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
the induced map
\varphi:G\to C^{m}_{b}([-1,1])
is smooth.

(3)

For every
g\in G
the map
\Lambda_{g}:\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by
\Lambda_{g}(x)=(R_{A(g)}+\varphi(g)(x_{3}))x
is a smooth diffeomorphism onto its image. Moreover, if we set

(1.19)

\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}=\Lambda_{g}(\mathbb{B}^{3})\text{ and }\Sigma_{g}=\Lambda_{g}(\mathbb{S}^{2})

then
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}
is a smooth, compact, simply connected manifold with boundary,
\Sigma_{g}=\partial\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}
is a smooth, compact, simply connected manifold, and
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}},
which then allows us to define the open set
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext},g}=\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}\backslash\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}
. In particular,
\Omega_{\mathrm{int},0}=R_{0}\mathbb{B}^{3}
and
\Sigma_{0}=R_{0}\mathbb{S}^{2}
with
R_{0}=2\sigma/(\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho_{\mathrm{ext}})-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast})
.

(4)

For
g\in G
we have that
\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}\times[-r,r]
, where
R_{0}<r<R^{\mathrm{max}}
is such that (
1.11
) holds for
g\in G
and
\alpha=A(g)
, and hence
\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}:\overline{\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
defined by
\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}(x)=\upeta_{\mathrm{int}}^{-1}(A(g)-gx_{3})
is well-defined and smooth.

(5)

For
g\in G
we have that

(1.20)

\begin{cases}\nabla\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}=-\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}ge_{3}&\text{in }\Omega_{\mathrm{ext},g}\\
\nabla[\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}]=-\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}ge_{3}&\text{in }\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}\\
\sigma\mathscr{K}_{\Sigma_{g}}=\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}\circ\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}-\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}&\text{on }\Sigma_{g}.\end{cases}

To the best of our knowledge, Theorem
1.1
provides the first rigorous construction of non-spherical bubbles of compressible fluid within an incompressible fluid in a uniform gravitational field. From a mathematical perspective, the theorem is essentially a well-posedness result with the gravitational constant playing the role of data. Indeed, the existence assertion in the theorem is clear, and the smooth dependence of the maps
A
and
\varphi
on
g
can be viewed as smooth dependence of the solution on the data. Theorem
1.1
does not state a uniqueness result, but its proof is directly based on Theorem
3.9
, which does contain a local uniqueness result of sorts. This is difficult to state precisely at this point in the paper, but roughly speaking it says that there exists an open neighborhood of
0
in an appropriate Hilbert space such that the only solutions in the open set are either spherical bubbles with trivial gravity or else the solutions with gravity from the theorem.

Our construction shows that the bubbles with nontrivial gravity bifurcate from the curve of spherical solutions without gravity at
\alpha=0
, at which point the constant densities and pressures are related via
\rho_{\mathrm{int}}=\rho_{\mathrm{ext}}
and
\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\ast}<\mathrm{P}_{\mathrm{int}}(\rho_{\mathrm{int}})
in light of (
1.7
) and (
1.17
). More precisely, the proof of Theorem
1.1
is based on the bifurcation theorem of Crandall and Rabinowitz
[
3
]
, applied in a special scale of weighted Sobolev spaces, and with the gravitational constant
g
serving as the bifurcation parameter. A key contribution of this work is the identification of a scale of function spaces and the derivation of their nonlinear functional analytic properties needed to apply Crandall-Rabinowitz. The bubbles we construct enjoy axisymmetric symmetry, and the Fréchet derivative of the curvature operator of the unknown surface at the given sphere leads to the key elliptic operator
L-2I
, where
L
is the Legendre differential operator defined by

(1.21)

Lu(\zeta)=-D((1-\zeta^{2})Du(\zeta))\text{ for }\zeta\in[-1,1],

which is degenerate at the boundary points (corresponding to the north and south poles). This degeneracy suggests using some form of weighted Sobolev spaces; for instance, weighted spaces provided the appropriate functional framework to study the so-called physical vacuum in compressible gases
[
9
]
.

The Legendre differential operator
L
turns out to be best understood in weighted Sobolev spaces
\mathcal{H}^{k}
(see (
2.14
) for the precise definition) characterized in terms of the Legendre polynomials, which are eigenfunctions of
L
. In particular, for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have that
L:\mathcal{H}^{k+2}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is a bounded linear operator (see Theorem
2.5
). Using a Hardy-type inequality (see Proposition
2.2
) and embedding results, we prove that
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is in fact an algebra for all
k\geq 2
(see Theorem
2.9
). We also show that composition with smooth functions induces smooth maps on
\mathcal{H}^{k}
when
k\geq 2
(see Theorem
2.11
). We believe that these spaces and their properties will be useful in the study of other nonlinear problems involving the Legendre operator.

Bifurcation theory and the closely related implicit function theorem have proven to be useful tools to construct interesting steady solutions around known simple solutions. Among others, one well-known problem stems from compressible gaseous stars governed by the gravitational Euler-Poisson system, for which the hydrostatic equilibria are given by the so-called Lane-Emden equation. They are spherically symmetric solutions with trivial velocity, and their densities decrease to zero from the center to the boundary. An important and physically relevant problem is then to construct rotating star solutions with a given angular velocity. This is a free boundary problem like our bubble problem, and in fact, slowly rotating axisymmetric star solutions were recently obtained by perturbing the Lane-Emden solutions through with the angular velocity as a bifurcation parameter:
[
7
,
8
,
18
]
. This suggests an interesting question related to the present work but beyond the scope of this paper: do rotating bubble solutions exist, and what role does gravity play?

Bubbles immersed in a liquid are frequently observed in nature and experiments, and they have been objects of serious study in various disciplines. In geometry, the soap bubble problem is closely related to the isoperimetric problem, which seeks a shape minimizing the surface area for given volume. In applied sciences, various models have been proposed to study the dynamics and take into account physical effects. Among others, a widely used model to study the bubble dynamics is the so-called the Rayleigh-Plesset equation, a second order nonlinear ODE for the bubble radius. Another approximate model proposed by Prosperetti
[
15
]
focuses on the thermal damping. We refer to
[
11
,
12
]
and the references therein for recent progress and related works in this direction.

2.
Weighted Sobolev spaces

In this section we introduce various weighted Sobolev spaces on the interval
(-1,1)
and establish basic functional analytic properties used to prove our main result. The first subsection concerns uniformly weighted spaces and embeddings via Hardy-type inequalities. In the second subsection, we study a scale of weighted spaces associated with the Legendre differential operator.

2.1.
Uniformly weighted spaces

We begin with a definition.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta\in[-1,\infty)
. We define the (real) Hilbert space
H^{k}_{\delta}((-1,1))
to be the closure of
C^{\infty}([-1,1])
with respect to the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{k}_{\delta}}
coming from the inner-product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{H^{k}_{\delta}}
, defined as follows. If
\delta>-1
we define

(2.1)

\left(u,v\right)_{H^{k}_{\delta}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{\delta}D^{j}u(\zeta)D^{j}v(\zeta)d\zeta,

while if
\delta=-1
we define

(2.2)

\left(u,v\right)_{H^{k}_{\delta}}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{-1}^{1}\frac{1}{(1-\zeta^{2})(\log(2/(1-\zeta^{2})))^{2}}D^{j}u(\zeta)D^{j}v(\zeta)d\zeta.

Note that
H^{k}_{0}((-1,1))=H^{k}((-1,1))
is the usual
L^{2}-
based Sobolev space of order
k
, and that in general we have the inclusion
H^{k}_{\delta}((-1,1))\subseteq H^{k}_{\mathrm{loc}}((-1,1))
.

The spaces
H^{k}_{\delta}((-1,1))
employ a common weight (determined by
\delta
) for all of the derivatives up to order
k
, but it turns out that if
u\in H^{k}_{\delta}((-1,1))
then
D^{j}u
gets an improved weight for each
0\leq j\leq k-1
. To prove this we first need the following technical lemma, which is a sort of Hardy-type inequality that is a variant of a result proved in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Suppose that
f:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
is a continuous function that is differentiable in
(0,1)
. Then

(2.3)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx\leq\frac{16}{\log 2}\int_{0}^{1}x(\left|f(x)\right|^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2})dx,

and if
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
then

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq\max\{\alpha^{-1}2^{\alpha+4},\alpha^{-2}(4+\alpha 2^{\alpha+2}\}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha+1}(\left|f(x)\right|^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2})dx.

Proof.

We begin with the proof of (
2.3
). The result is trivial if the integral on the right side of (
2.3
) is infinite, so we may assume that it is finite, which in turn means that
f,f^{\prime}\in L^{2}((\varepsilon,1))
for all
0<\varepsilon<1
. Fixing such an
\varepsilon
, we use the fundamental theorem of calculus to compute

(2.5)

\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx=\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}\frac{d}{dx}(\log(2/x))^{-1}dx=-\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}2\frac{f(x)f^{\prime}(x)}{\log(2/x)}dx+\frac{\left|f(1)\right|^{2}}{\log(2)}-\frac{\left|f(\varepsilon)\right|^{2}}{\log(2/\varepsilon)}.

We then use Cauchy-Schwarz to bound

(2.6)

\left|\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}2\frac{f(x)f^{\prime}(x)}{\log(2/x)}dx\right|\leq 2\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx\right)^{1/2}\left(\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx\right)^{1/2}\\
\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx+2\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx.

Combining these two bounds and absorbing, we learn that

(2.7)

\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx\leq 4\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx+2\frac{\left|f(1)\right|^{2}}{\log(2)}

for all
0<\varepsilon<1
. Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
and employing the monotone convergence theorem then shows that

(2.8)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\left|f(x)\right|^{2}}{x(\log(2/x))^{2}}dx\leq 4\int_{0}^{1}x\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx+2\frac{\left|f(1)\right|^{2}}{\log(2)}.

On the other hand, standard Sobolev theory shows that

(2.9)

\left|f(1)\right|^{2}\leq 4\int_{1/2}^{1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx+\int_{1/2}^{1}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}x(4\left|f(x)\right|^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2})dx.

The estimate (
2.3
) then follows by combining the previous two bounds and noting that
4+4/\log(2)\leq 16/\log 2
.

Next, we prove (
2.4
), again assuming that its right side is finite. A minor variant of the argument above shows that for
0<\varepsilon<1
we have the bound

(2.10)

\alpha\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{2}{\alpha}\int_{\varepsilon}^{1}x^{\alpha+1}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx+\left|f(1)\right|^{2}.

Sending
\varepsilon\to 0
, employing the monotone convergence theorem, and using an absorbing argument then shows that

(2.11)

\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha-1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq\frac{4}{\alpha^{2}}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha+1}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx+\frac{2}{\alpha}\left|f(1)\right|^{2}.

On the other hand,

(2.12)

\left|f(1)\right|^{2}\leq 4\int_{1/2}^{1}\left|f(x)\right|^{2}dx+\int_{1/2}^{1}\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2}dx\leq 2^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{1}x^{\alpha+1}(4\left|f(x)\right|^{2}+\left|f^{\prime}(x)\right|^{2})dx.

∎

The following result records the essential properties of the
H^{k}_{\delta}
spaces we will need in this paper, including the aforementioned improved weights.

Theorem 2.3
.

The following hold.

(1)

If
k_{0},k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta_{0},\delta_{1}\in[-1,\infty)
satisfy
k_{0}\leq k_{1}
and
\delta_{1}\leq\delta_{0}
, then we have the continuous inclusion
H^{k_{1}}_{\delta_{1}}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{k_{0}}_{\delta_{0}}((-1,1))
.

(2)

If
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have the continuous inclusion
H^{k}_{k}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{k-1}_{k-2}((-1,1))
.

(3)

Suppose
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then we have the continuous inclusions
H^{2k}_{2k}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{k}((-1,1))
as well as
H^{2k+1}_{2k+1}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{k}_{-1}((-1,1))
. In particular, we have the continuous inclusion
H^{k}_{k}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}((-1,1))
.

(4)

If
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, then we have the continuous inclusion
H^{k}_{k}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow C^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1,1/2}_{b}((-1,1))
. In particular, each
u\in H^{k}_{k}((-1,1))
uniquely extends to
u\in C^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1,1/2}_{b}([-1,1])
.

Proof.

The first item follows readily from the definition of the norms. For the second item, we first consider the case
k\geq 2
. By applying (
2.4
) on half-intervals, we then obtain for each
0\leq j\leq k-1
,

(2.13)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k-2}\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}d\zeta\lesssim\int_{-1}^{0}(1+\zeta)^{k-2}\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}d\zeta+\int_{0}^{1}(1-\zeta)^{k-2}\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}d\zeta\\
\lesssim\int_{-1}^{0}(1+\zeta)^{k}(\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}+\left|D^{j+1}u(\zeta)\right|^{2})d\zeta+\int_{0}^{1}(1-\zeta)^{k-2}(\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}+\left|D^{j+1}u(\zeta)\right|^{2})d\zeta\\
\lesssim\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}(\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}+\left|D^{j+1}u(\zeta)\right|^{2})d\zeta,

which implies the claimed result. If
k=1
, the result follows analogously by applying (
2.3
) instead.
The third item follows by applying the second
k
times in the even case and
k+1
times in the odd case. The fourth item follows from the first and third, combined with the usual one-dimensional Sobolev embedding.
∎

2.2.
Weighted spaces generated by the Legendre operator

We now aim to study a scale of weighted Sobolev spaces in terms of the Legendre differential operator. We begin with a definition.

Definition 2.4
.

We define the following.

(1)

The space
AC_{\mathrm{loc}}((-1,1))=\{u:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}\;|\;f\text{ is absolutely continuous on each compact }K\subset(-1,1)\}
. Note that if
u\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}((-1,1))
then
Du
is defined almost everywhere in
(-1,1)
and is Lebesgue measurable. We write
AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{0}((-1,1))=AC_{\mathrm{loc}}((-1,1))
. Then for
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
we define
AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k}((-1,1))=\{u\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k-1}((-1,1))\;|\;D^{k}u\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}((-1,1))\}
. Then
u\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k}((-1,1))
implies that
D^{j}u
is defined almost everywhere in
(-1,1)
and Lebesgue measurable for
0\leq j\leq k+1
.

(2)

Given
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the inner-product space
\mathcal{H}^{k}
via

(2.14)

\mathcal{H}^{k}=\{u\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k-1}((-1,1))\;|\;\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}<\infty\},

where

(2.15)

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{k}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{j}\left|D^{j}u(\zeta)\right|^{2}d\zeta.

The inner-product on
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is the obvious one associated to this square-norm. When
k=0
we set
\mathcal{H}^{0}=L^{2}((-1,1))
with its standard inner-product.

(3)

For
n\in\mathbb{N}
we let
p_{n}:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be the
n^{th}
Legendre polynomial, normalized so that
\left(p_{n},p_{m}\right)_{L^{2}}=\delta_{n,m}
. Given
u\in L^{2}((-1,1))
we define
\hat{u}:\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{R}
via
\hat{u}(n)=\left(u,p_{m}\right)_{L^{2}}
. We write
\mathcal{P}=\{p:(-1,1)\to\mathbb{R}\;|\;p\text{ is a polynomial}\}
. It is well-known (see, for instance,
[
19
]
) that the Legendre polynomials span
\mathcal{P}
and form a complete orthonormal set in
L^{2}((-1,1))
.

The following result records some essential properties of the
\mathcal{H}^{k}
spaces proved in
[
4
,
13
,
14
]
; we also refer to
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2.5
.

The following hold for
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(1)

The space
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is complete, and hence a Hilbert space. Additionally, if
k\leq m\in\mathbb{N}
then we have the continuous inclusion
\mathcal{H}^{m}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{k}
.

(2)

The set
\mathcal{P}\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}
is dense.

(3)

Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then

(2.16)

\mathcal{H}^{k}=\{u\in L^{2}((-1,1))\;|\;\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n(n+1)+\lambda)^{k}\left|\hat{u}(n)\right|^{2}<\infty\},

and we have the norm equivalence

(2.17)

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\asymp\left(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}(n(n+1)+\lambda)^{k}\left|\hat{u}(n)\right|^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Moreover,
\{p_{n}/[n(n+1)+\lambda]^{k/2}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is a complete orthonormal set in
\mathcal{H}^{k}
when it is endowed with the inner-product associated to the square-norm on the right of (
2.17
).

(4)

The Legendre differential operator
L:\mathcal{P}\to\mathcal{P}
defined by

(2.18)

Lu(\zeta)=-(1-\zeta^{2})D^{2}u(\zeta)+2\zeta Du(\zeta)=-D((1-\zeta^{2})Du(\zeta))

uniquely extends to a bounded linear map
L:\mathcal{H}^{k+2}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
. Moreover, given any
r\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{n(n+1)\;|\;n\in\mathbb{N}\}
the map
L-rI:\mathcal{H}^{k+2}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The first three items are proved in
[
4
,
13
,
14
]
. The fourth follows easily from the third and the fact that the Legendre polynomials satisfy
Lp_{n}=n(n+1)p_{n}
.
∎

Next, we aim to establish some useful functional analytic properties of the
\mathcal{H}^{k}
spaces. We begin with a trio of embedding results. The first relates to the uniformly weighted spaces discussed previously.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the following hold.

(1)

We have the continuous inclusions

(2.19)

\mathcal{H}^{k}\hookrightarrow H^{k}_{k}((-1,1))\hookrightarrow H^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}((-1,1)).

In particular, if
k\geq 2
then
\mathcal{H}^{k}\hookrightarrow C^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor-1,1/2}_{b}([-1,1])
.

(2)

If
1\leq k
and
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
, then for
0\leq j\leq k-1
we have that
D^{j}u\in H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j-k\}}((-1,1))
and

(2.20)

\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}\left\|D^{j}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j-k\}}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}.

Proof.

The first item follows from Theorem
2.3
, the fact that
\mathcal{P}\subseteq C^{\infty}([-1,1])
, and the standard Sobolev embedding in one dimension. The second item also follows from Theorem
2.3
and a simple induction argument.
∎

The second gives some
H^{1}
bounds (and hence
C^{0,1/2}_{b}
bounds) for various derivatives weighted by powers of
(1-\zeta^{2})
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
for
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
0\leq j\leq k-1
.

(1)

If
j\leq(k-1)/2
then for
\mu>1/2
we have that
(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j}u\in H^{1}((-1,1))
and
\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}
.

(2)

If
(k-1)/2<j
then we have that
(1-\zeta^{2})^{j+1-k/2}D^{j}u\in H^{1}((-1,1))
and
\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{j+1-k/2}D^{j}u\right\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}
.

Moreover, since
H^{1}((-1,,1))\hookrightarrow C^{0,1/2}_{b}([-1,1])
, each of the above
H^{1}
estimates also yields a
C^{0,1/2}_{b}
estimate.

Proof.

Fix
j,k\in\mathbb{N}
with
0\leq j\leq k-1
. Write
\mu_{j}=j+1-k/2
when
(k-1)/2<j
, and let
\mu_{j}>1/2
be arbitrary when
j\leq(k-1)/2
. Then, regardless of the value of
j
, we have the inequalities

(2.21)

\frac{1}{2}<\mu_{j}\text{ and }\frac{2j-k+2}{2}\leq\mu_{j}.

Next, we compute

(2.22)

D[(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu_{j}}D^{j}u]=(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu_{j}}D^{j+1}u-2\mu_{j}\zeta(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu_{j}-1}D^{j}u

in order to bound

(2.23)

\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu_{j}}D^{j}u\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\lesssim\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2\mu_{j}-2}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}+(1-\zeta^{2})^{2\mu_{j}}\left|D^{j+1}u\right|^{2}.

In light of (
2.21
) and Theorem
2.6
, we may then bound

(2.24)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2\mu_{j}-2}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}+(1-\zeta^{2})^{2\mu_{j}}\left|D^{j+1}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|D^{j}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j-k\}}}^{2}+\left\|D^{j+1}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j+2-k\}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}.

Combining the previous two bounds then yields the desired inequality.
∎

Our third embedding, a variant of Proposition
2.7
, will be useful as well.

Proposition 2.8
.

Suppose that
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
for
1\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, and suppose
j,m,\mu\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
j+m\leq k
and
2j+m\leq k+2\mu
. Then
(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j}u\in\mathcal{H}^{m}
and
\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j}u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{m}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}
.

Proof.

Fix
k,j,m,\mu
as in the hypotheses. We then use the Leibniz rule to bound

(2.25)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{\ell}\left|D^{\ell}[(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j}u]\right|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n=0}^{\ell}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{\ell+2\max\{\mu-n,0\}}\left|D^{j+\ell-n}u\right|^{2}\text{ for }0\leq\ell\leq m.

Breaking to cases based on whether
0\leq\mu\leq n-1
or
n\leq\mu
, we readily check that the assumptions on
j,m,\mu
imply that

(2.26)

\max\{-1,2(j+\ell-n)-k\}\leq\ell+2\max\{\mu-n,0\}\text{ for }0\leq\ell\leq m\text{ and }0\leq n\leq\ell,

and hence Theorem
2.6
allows us to bound

(2.27)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{\ell+2\max\{\mu-n,0\}}\left|D^{j+\ell-n}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|D^{j+\ell-n}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2(j+\ell-n)-k\}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}

for
0\leq\ell\leq m
and
0\leq n\leq\ell
. Combining (
2.25
) and (
2.27
) then yields the desired inequality.
∎

Next we prove that when
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
the space
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra.

Theorem 2.9
.

The space
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra when
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
, i.e. if
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
then
uv\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
and
\left\|uv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k\geq 2
. Suppose that
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
. Using the
L^{\infty}
bounds from Theorem
2.6
, we may estimate

(2.28)

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|uv\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}

and

(2.29)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})[\left|u\right|^{2}\left|Dv\right|^{2}+\left|Du\right|^{2}\left|v\right|^{2}]\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

On the other hand, we can apply Proposition
2.7
(with
k=2
and
j=1
) and Theorem
2.6
to bound

(2.30)

\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})Du\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\text{ and }\left\|Dv\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}},

and so

(2.31)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2}[\left|u\right|^{2}\left|D^{2}v\right|^{2}+\left|Du\right|^{2}\left|Dv\right|^{2}+\left|D^{2}u\right|\left|v\right|]\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}+\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2}\left|Du\right|^{2}\left|Dv\right|^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})Du\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\left\|Dv\right\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

Combining (
2.28
), (
2.29
), and (
2.31
) with the Leibniz rule then shows that
uv\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
with
\left\|uv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}
. This completes the proof of the base case of the induction.

Suppose now that the result is proved for
2\leq k
and suppose that
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{k+1}
. Then
u,v\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
, so the induction hypothesis implies that
uv\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
with
\left\|uv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}
. Note that for
w\in AC_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k}((-1,1))
we have that

(2.32)

\left\|w\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}=\left\|w\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}+\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{k+1}w\right|^{2}

so we only need to handle the last term with
w=uv
. We use the Leibniz rule to bound

(2.33)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{k+1}(uv)\right|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}.

We now break to cases based on the value of
j
.

We begin with the cases
j\in\{0,k+1\}
, in which case we use Theorem
2.6
to estimate

(2.34)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1}v\right|^{2}+\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{k+1}u\right|^{2}\left|v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}.

In the second case we consider

(2.35)

1\leq j\leq\frac{(k+1)-1}{2}=\frac{k}{2}.

In this setting we easily check that
1\leq k+1-2j
, and hence

(2.36)

k+1-2j=\max\{-1,2(k+1-j)-k-1\}.

Using Proposition
2.7
with
\mu=j
and Theorem
2.6
, we have

(2.37)

\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{j}D^{j}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}\text{ and }\left\|D^{k+1-j}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{k+1-2j}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}.

Thus,

(2.38)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1-2j}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\leq\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}.

Next, consider the case

(2.39)

\frac{k}{2}+1\leq j\leq k.

This requires that

(2.40)

1\leq k+1-j\leq\frac{k}{2},

and so we may use the same argument from the previous case with
u
and
v
swapped to see that

(2.41)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}.

The only remaining case is to handle

(2.42)

\frac{k}{2}<j<\frac{k}{2}+1,

which occurs only when
k
is odd. In this setting Proposition
2.7
now shows that

(2.43)

\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{j+1/2-k/2}D^{j}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}.

From this and the fact that
2\leq k
and
j<k/2+1
imply that
k+1-j\leq 2k-2j
, we then see that

(2.44)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2k-2j}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1-j}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}.

Synthesizing the analysis from these four cases then shows that

(2.45)

\sum_{j=0}^{k+1}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k+1}\left|D^{j}u\right|^{2}\left|D^{k+1-j}v\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}^{2}.

Combining this with (
2.31
) and (
2.32
), we then deduce that
uv\in\mathcal{H}^{k+1}
and
\left\|uv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}\lesssim\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k+1}}
. This completes the inductive step, and hence the proof.
∎

A particular consequence of Theorem
2.9
is that if
\alpha_{0},\dotsc,\alpha_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
then for
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
the polynomial map
\mathcal{H}^{k}\ni u\mapsto\sum_{\ell=0}^{m}\alpha_{\ell}u^{\ell}\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
is smooth. We next aim to establish analogous results for more general composition maps. We begin with a crucial technical result showing composition is well-defined and enjoys useful estimates.

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\varnothing\neq E\subseteq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
with
U
open, and let
\varnothing\neq\mathcal{E}_{E}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{2}
be such that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{E}
implies that
u((-1,1))\subseteq E
. Suppose that
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
and
f\in C^{k}(U)
satisfies

(2.46)

\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}(E)}=\sup_{z\in E}\sup_{0\leq j\leq k}\left|D^{j}f(z)\right|<\infty.

Then for
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{E}
we have that
f\circ u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
and

(2.47)

\left\|f\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}(E)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{k}.

Here the implicit constant is independent of
f
and
u
.

Proof.

We will prove the result by inducting on
2\leq k\in\mathbb{N}
. Throughout the proof any implicit constants in inequalities of the form
a\lesssim b
are independent of
f
and
u
but may depend on
k
and various other universal constants. We will also abbreviate
\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}=\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}(E)}
, noting that

(2.48)

\sup_{\zeta\in(-1,1)}\left|D^{j}f(u(\zeta))\right|\leq\sup_{z\in E}\left|D^{j}f(z)\right|\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}

for
0\leq j\leq k
and
u\in\mathcal{E}_{E}
.

We begin with the case
k=2
, assuming that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{E}
. We first bound

(2.49)

\int_{-1}^{1}\left|f\circ u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}

and

(2.50)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})\left|Df(u)Du\right|^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})\left|Du\right|^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}.

We then use Proposition
2.7
(with
k=2
and
j=1
) and Theorem
2.6
to bound

(2.51)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2}[\left|Df(u)D^{2}u\right|^{2}+\left|D^{2}f(u)(Du)^{2}\right|^{2}]\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{2}\left|D^{2}u\right|^{2}\\
+\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})Du\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}\left|Du\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}(\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{4}).

Upon combining these three bounds and using the chain rule, we then find that

(2.52)

\left\|f\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{2}_{b}}^{2}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}})^{4}.

This completes the proof of the base case
k=2
.

Now suppose that
k\geq 3
and that the result has been proved for
k-1\geq 2
. Consider
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{E}
. Since
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k-1}
we then have that
f\circ u\in\mathcal{H}^{k-1}
and

(2.53)

\left\|f\circ u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k-1}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k-1}_{b}}^{2}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k-1}})^{2(k-1)}.

Thus, to establish the desired result for
k
it suffices to prove that

(2.54)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{k}[f\circ u]\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{2k}.

To prove (
2.54
) we begin by employing the Faà di Bruno formula to bound

(2.55)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{k}[f\circ u]\right|^{2}\lesssim\sum_{m=1}^{k}\sum_{j\in J(m,k)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{m}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}u\cdots D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2},

where

(2.56)

J(m,k)=\{j\in\{1,\dotsc,k\}^{m}\;|\;j_{1}+\cdots+j_{m}=k\text{ and }j_{1}\leq j_{2}\leq\dotsc\leq j_{m}\}.

Note that for
j\in J(m,k)
we always have the bounds

(2.57)

j_{1}\leq\frac{k}{m}\leq j_{m}\leq k-m+1.

Next, we will break to three cases to estimate the right side of (
2.55
).

Case 1,
m=1
:
Assume that
m=1
. Then we may bound the right side of (
2.55
) via

(2.58)

\sum_{j\in J(1,k)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{m}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}u\cdots D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2}=\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|Df(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{k}u\right|^{2}\\
\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{k}u\right|^{2}\leq\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}.

Case 2,
m=2
:
In the second case we assume that
m=2
. Then
j\in J(2,k)
means that
j=(j_{1},j_{2})
with
j_{1}\leq j_{2}
and
j_{1}+j_{2}=k
. This implies that

(2.59)

j_{1}\leq\frac{k}{2}\leq j_{2}\text{ and hence that }\frac{k-1}{2}<\frac{k}{2}\leq j_{2}.

We break to two subcases. In the first we assume that
j_{1}\leq(k-1)/2
and set
\mu=1>1/2
, which then implies that

(2.60)

-1<k-2\mu\text{ and }2j_{2}-k\leq k-2\mu.

We may then use the first item of Proposition
2.7
on the
j_{1}
term with
\mu=1
, and then use Theorem
2.6
on the
j_{2}
term in order to bound

(2.61)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{2}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}uD^{j_{2}}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})D^{j_{1}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k-2\mu}\left|D^{j_{2}}u\right|^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\left\|D^{j_{2}}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j_{2}-k\}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{4}.

This completes the first subcase.

In the second subcase we assume that
(k-1)/2<j_{1}
. Since
2\leq k
we have that

(2.62)

j_{1}\leq\frac{k}{2}<k-\frac{1}{2}\text{ and }2(k-j_{1})+2j_{1}\leq 3k-2,

which imply that

(2.63)

-1<2(k-j_{1}-1)\text{ and }2j_{2}-k\leq 2(k-j_{1}-1).

Consequently, we may use the second item of Proposition
2.7
on the
j_{1}
term and
Theorem
2.6
on the
j_{2}
term in order to bound

(2.64)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{2}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}uD^{j_{2}}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{j_{1}+1-k/2}D^{j_{1}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k-2(j_{1}+1-k/2)}\left|D^{j_{2}}u\right|^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\left\|D^{j_{2}}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j_{2}-k\}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{4}.

This completes the second subcase.

The two subcases cover all possibilities when
m=2
. We then deduce from (
2.61
) and (
2.64
) that

(2.65)

\sum_{j\in J(2,k)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{2}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}uD^{j_{1}}u\right|^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{4}.

Case 3,
3\leq m\leq k
:
In the third case we assume that
3\leq m\leq k
. If
j\in J(m,k)
satisfies
(k-1)/2<j_{m-1}
, then

(2.66)

k-1<j_{m-1}+j_{m}\leq k-1,

a contradiction. Thus, for all
j\in J(m,k)
we must have that
j_{m-1}\leq(k-1)/2
. Next, We break to two subcases based on the size of
j_{m}
.

In the first subcase we assume that
j_{m}\leq(k-1)/2
. We may then use the first item of Proposition
2.7
on each of the
j_{i}
terms with

(2.67)

\mu=\frac{k+1/2}{2m}>\frac{k}{2m}\geq\frac{1}{2},

in order to bound

(2.68)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{m}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}u\cdots D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j_{i}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k-2m\mu}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2m}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{-1/2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2m}.

This completes the first subcase.

In the second subcase we assume that
(k-1)/2<j_{m}
. Since
m<k+2
we have that

(2.69)

\frac{1}{2}<\frac{k+1}{2(m-1)},

and this then allows us to pick
\mu\in(1/2,1]
satisfying

(2.70)

\mu<\frac{k+1}{2(m-1)}.

This and (
2.57
) require

(2.71)

-1<k-2(m-1)\mu\text{ and }2j_{m}-k\leq k-2(m-1)\mu,

so we may again apply the first item of Proposition
2.7
with this choice of
\mu
to the
j_{1},\dotsc,j_{m-1}
terms and Theorem
2.6
on the
j_{2}
term to bound

(2.72)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{m}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}u\cdots D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{m-1}\left\|(1-\zeta^{2})^{\mu}D^{j_{i}}u\right\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k-2(m-1)\mu}\left|D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2}\\
\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2m-2}\left\|D^{j_{m}}u\right\|_{H^{0}_{\max\{-1,2j_{m}-k\}}}^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2m}

This completes the second subcase.

The two subcases cover all possibilities when
3\leq m\leq k
. We then deduce from (
2.68
) and (
2.72
) that

(2.73)

\sum_{m=3}^{k}\sum_{j\in J(m,k)}\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{m}f(u)\right|^{2}\left|D^{j_{1}}u\cdots D^{j_{m}}u\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}\sum_{m=3}^{k}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2m}.

The three cases considered above cover all possibilities of
1\leq m\leq k
. Hence, we may combine (
2.55
) with (
2.58
), (
2.65
), and (
2.73
) to conclude that

(2.74)

\int_{-1}^{1}(1-\zeta^{2})^{k}\left|D^{k}[f\circ u]\right|^{2}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k}_{b}}^{2}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{2k}.

This is (
2.54
), which completes the proof of the induction step, and hence the proof.
∎

We now prove that composition with certain functions induces a
C^{m}
map on
\mathcal{H}^{k}
for
k\geq 2
. This is in some sense analogous to the well-known “
\omega-
lemmas”: see Proposition 2.4.18 in
[
1
]
for the result in
C^{0}
spaces, and
[
6
]
for results in standard Sobolev spaces. This result is essential for our use of the
\mathcal{H}^{k}
spaces in the Crandall-Rabinowitz framework.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
k,m\in\mathbb{N}
with
k\geq 2
, and let
\varnothing\neq U\subseteq\mathbb{R}
be open and
f\in C^{k+m+1}(U)
. Define the open set

(2.75)

\mathcal{H}^{2}[U]=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\;|\;\overline{u((-1,1))}\subset U\}.

Then the map
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}[U]\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
defined by
\Phi(u)=f\circ u
is well-defined and
C^{m}
. Moreover, if
m\geq 1
then for
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}[U]
and
1\leq j\leq m
we have that
D^{j}\Phi(u)\in\mathcal{L}^{j}(\mathcal{H}^{k};\mathcal{H}^{k})
is given by

(2.76)

D^{j}\Phi(u)(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{j})=D^{j}f(u)\prod_{i=1}^{j}w_{i}.

Proof.

We divide the proof into steps.

Step 1 – Well-definedness and a reduction:
Write
\mathcal{K}(U)=\{K\subset U\;|\;K\text{ is compact and }K^{\circ}\neq\varnothing\}
. Given
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
let

(2.77)

\mathcal{E}_{K}=\{u\in\mathcal{H}^{2}\;|\;\overline{u((-1,1))}\subseteq K^{\circ}\},

and note that since
\mathcal{H}^{2}\hookrightarrow C^{0,1/2}_{b}((-1,1))
we have that each
\mathcal{E}_{K}\subseteq\mathcal{H}^{2}
is open and

(2.78)

\mathcal{H}^{2}[U]=\bigcup_{K\in\mathcal{K}(U)}\mathcal{E}_{K}.

Moreover, for
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
we have that
u\in\mathcal{E}_{K}
implies that
\overline{u((-1,1))}\subseteq K
, so Theorem
2.10
implies that if
F\in C^{k}(U)
then
F\circ u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
for all
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
. From this we learn two key facts. First, the map
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{H}^{2}[U]\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is indeed well-defined. Second, it suffices to prove that the restriction
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is
C^{m}
and satisfies (
2.76
) (when
m\geq 1
) for each
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
.

Step 2 – Continuity:
Fix
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
. We now claim that
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is continuous. Fix
u\in B(u,r)\subset\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
. For
v\in B(0,r)\backslash\{0\}
we then compute

(2.79)

\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)=f(u+v)-f(u)=\int_{0}^{1}Df(u+tv)vdt.

Using Minkowski’s integral inequality, Theorem
2.10
, and the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra, we bound

(2.80)

\left\|\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\left\|Df(u+tv)v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}dt\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+1}_{b}(K)}\int_{0}^{1}(1+\left\|u+tv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{k}dt\\
\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+1}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k},

from which we readily deduce that
\Phi
is continuous at
u
. This completes the proof of the claim and the theorem as well when
m=0
. It remains only to handle the case when
m\geq 1
.

Step 3 – Differentiability:
Suppose that
m\geq 1
and fix
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
. We saw in Step 1 that
f(u)\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
when
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
, but since
f\in C^{k+m+1}(U)
the same reasoning shows that
D^{j}f(u)\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
when
1\leq j\leq m+1
and
u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra, the map
\mathcal{H}^{k}\ni w\mapsto D^{j}f(u)w\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
is also bounded and linear, and Theorem
2.10
provides the estimate

(2.81)

\max_{0\leq j\leq m+1}\left\|D^{j}f(u)w\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\lesssim\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+m+1}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{k}\left\|w\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\text{ for }u\in\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}\text{ and }w\in\mathcal{H}^{k}.

In particular, this shows that the proposed formulas for the derivatives of
\Phi
in (
2.76
) are all well-defined.

We now aim to inductively prove that
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is
m-
times differentiable. To this end fix
u\in B(u,r)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{H}^{k}
. Note that (
2.81
) implies that the map
\mathcal{H}^{k}\ni v\mapsto Df(u)v\in\mathcal{H}^{k}
is bounded and linear. For
v\in B(0,r)\backslash\{0\}
we then compute

(2.82)

\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)-Df(u)v=f(u+v)-f(u)-Df(u)v=\int_{0}^{1}[Df(u+tv)-Df(u)]vdt\\
=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}tD^{2}f(u+stv)v^{2}dsdt.

Again using Theorem
2.10
and the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra, we then have that

(2.83)

\left\|\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)-Df(u)v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t\left\|D^{2}f(u+stv)v^{2}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}dsdt\\
\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+2}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u+stv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{k}dsdt\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+2}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k}.

Hence,

(2.84)

\lim_{v\to 0}\frac{\left\|\Phi(u+v)-\Phi(u)-Df(u)v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}}{\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}}=0

and we deduce that
\Phi
is differentiable in
\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{H}^{k}
with
D\Phi(u)\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}^{k};\mathcal{H}^{k})
given by
D\Phi(u)v=Df(u)v
.

Next, suppose that we have proved
\Phi
is
\ell
times differentiable for
1\leq\ell\leq m-1
with derivatives given by (
2.76
) for
1\leq j\leq\ell
. Again fix
u\in B(u,r)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{H}^{k}
. For
v\in B(0,r)\backslash\{0\}
we then compute

(2.85)

(D^{\ell}\Phi(u+v)-D^{\ell}\Phi(u)-D^{\ell+1}f(u)v)(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{\ell})=(D^{\ell}f(u+v)-D^{\ell}f(u)-D^{\ell+1}f(u)v)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}\\
=\int_{0}^{1}(D^{\ell+1}f(u+tv)-D^{\ell+1}f(u))v\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}dt=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}tD^{\ell+2}f(u+stv)v^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}dsdt.

Again using Theorem
2.10
and the fact that
\mathcal{H}^{k}
is an algebra, we then have that

(2.86)

\left\|(D^{\ell}\Phi(u+v)-D^{\ell}\Phi(u)-D^{\ell+1}f(u)v)(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{\ell})\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t\left\|D^{\ell+2}f(u+stv)v^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}dsdt\\
\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\|w_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}t\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+\ell+2}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u+stv\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}})^{k}dsdt\\
\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\prod_{i=1}^{\ell}\left\|w_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+\ell+2}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k}.

Thus,

(2.87)

\left\|D^{\ell}\Phi(u+v)-D^{\ell}\Phi(u)-D^{\ell+1}f(u)v\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{\ell}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}^{2}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+\ell+2}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k},

and we deduce from this that
D^{\ell}\Phi
is differentiable at
u
with

(2.88)

D^{\ell+1}\Phi(u)(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{\ell+1})=D^{\ell+1}f(u)\prod_{i=1}^{\ell+1}w_{i}.

Thus,
\Phi
is
(\ell+1)-
times differentiable and (
2.76
) holds for
1\leq j\leq\ell+1
.

Proceeding by finite induction, we deduce that
\Phi
is
m-
times differentiable in
\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}
, so to show that it is
C^{m}
it remains only to prove that
D^{m}\Phi
is continuous. Once more fix
u\in B(u,r)\subseteq\mathcal{E}_{K}\cap\mathcal{H}^{k}
. For
v\in B(0,r)\backslash\{0\}
we compute

(2.89)

(D^{m}\Phi(u+v)-D^{m}\Phi(u))(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{m})=(D^{m}f(u+v)-D^{m}f(u))\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}=\int_{0}^{1}D^{m+1}f(u+tv)v\prod_{i=1}^{m}w_{i}dt

and then estimate

(2.90)

\left\|(D^{m}\Phi(u+v)-D^{m}\Phi(u))(w_{1},\dotsc,w_{m})\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|\prod_{i=1}^{m}\left\|w_{i}\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+m+1}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k}.

Thus,

(2.91)

\left\|D^{m}\Phi(u+v)-D^{m}\Phi(u)\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{m}}\lesssim\left\|v\right\|\left\|f\right\|_{C^{k+m+1}_{b}(K)}(1+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}^{k}}+r)^{k}

and we deduce that
D^{m}\Phi
is continuous at
u
. Hence,
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{k}\cap\mathcal{E}_{K}\to\mathcal{H}^{k}
is indeed
C^{m}
for all
K\in\mathcal{K}(U)
.

∎

3.
Bubble construction

In this section we carry out the proof of Theorem
1.1
. First, we reformulate the third equation in (
1.3
) in a form amenable to study in the weighted Sobolev functional framework developed in the previous section. Then we study the smoothness of certain maps and employ a bifurcation argument to prove the theorem.

3.1.
Reformulation

We aim to solve (
1.3
) with hydrostatic bubble domains of the form
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}=\Lambda(\mathbb{B}^{3})
, where
\mathbb{B}^{3}=B(0,1)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is the usual open unit ball and
\Lambda:\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a smooth diffeomorphism onto its image of the form
\Lambda(x)=\lambda(x_{3})x
for a smooth function
\lambda:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
. This means that the bubble’s unknown surface is
\Sigma=\Lambda(\mathbb{S}^{2})
, the image of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{2}=\partial\mathbb{B}^{3}
. Note that the spherical bubbles constructed in Section
1.2
without gravity are realized by taking
\lambda=R_{\alpha}
where
R_{\alpha}
is given in (
1.15
). In this case
\Lambda
is a simple dilation map and the bubble surface is the image of the unit sphere, the sphere with radius
R_{\alpha}
. We next give a sufficient condition on
\lambda
for
\Lambda
to be an injection in a slab domain
\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[-1,1]
, which contains the closed unit ball
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Suppose that
\lambda:[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
is continuous and such that
[-1,1]\ni t\mapsto t\lambda(t)\in\mathbb{R}
is increasing. Then the map
\Lambda:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by
\Lambda(x)=\lambda(x_{n})x
is injective.

Proof.

Write
E=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\times[-1,1]
and denote a point
x\in E
as
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
. Suppose that
x,y\in E
with
x\neq y
. We break to two cases. In the first
x_{n}\neq y_{n}
. Then the increasing assumption implies that
x_{n}\lambda(x_{n})\neq y_{n}\lambda(y_{n})
, and hence
\Lambda(x)\neq\Lambda(y)
. In the second
x_{n}=y_{n}
, so
x^{\prime}\neq y^{\prime}
. Then
\Lambda^{\prime}(x)=\lambda(x_{n})x^{\prime}=\lambda(y_{n})x^{\prime}\neq\lambda(y_{n})y^{\prime}=\Lambda^{\prime}(y)
and hence
\Lambda(x)\neq\Lambda(y)
.
Thus, in any case
\Lambda(x)\neq\Lambda(y)
, and we deduce that
\Lambda
is injective.
∎

Under the assumptions on
\lambda
in Lemma
3.1
, the restriction of
\Lambda
to
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}
is a homeomorphism onto its image. Furthermore, if
\lambda
is smooth and sufficiently close to a constant in the
C^{1}_{b}
norm, then
\Lambda:\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}\to\Lambda(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}})
is a smooth diffeomorphism onto its image. Our goal is thus to verify these conditions in proving Theorem
1.1
.

We now turn to a discussion of the differential equation (
1.3
). Suppose that
\Sigma=\Lambda(\mathbb{S}^{2})

\left\|\varphi(g)\right\|_{C^{1}_{b}}
is sufficiently small for all
g\in G
to guarantee that the map
\Lambda_{g}:\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined by
\Lambda_{g}(x)=(R_{A(g)}+\varphi(g)(x_{3}))x
is a smooth diffeomorphism onto
\Lambda_{g}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}})
(see Lemma
3.1
and the discussion after). Third, we have the bound
\left\|R_{A(g)}+\varphi(g)\right\|_{C^{0}_{b}}\leq r
for all
g\in G
, which guarantees that
\Lambda_{g}(\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}})\subseteq r\overline{\mathbb{B}^{3}}\subset\Omega_{\mathrm{tot}}
. We then define
\Omega_{\mathrm{int},g}
,
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext},g}
,
\Sigma_{g}
, and
\rho_{\mathrm{int},g}
as in the hypotheses and deduce that all of the conclusions are satisfied thanks to Theorem
3.9
.

Abstract
. We study initial value problems for various geometric equations on a cohomogeneity manifold near a singular orbit. We show that when prescribing the Ricci curvature, or finding solutions to the Einstein and soliton equations, there exist solutions near the singular orbit, unique up to a finite number of constants. In part I we make a special assumption that significantly simplifies the proof, and will solve the general case in Part II.



A well known problem is that of prescribing the Ricci curvature of a metric, i.e., given a symmetric
bi-linear tensor T, solve the differential equation
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=T
(or on a compact manifold
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=cT
for some constant
c
) for a metric
g
. There exists a local
solution if
T
is non-degenerate or has constant rank
[
De
,
DG1
,
DG2
,
DY
]
, but in some cases solutions do not exist. The problem has been studied in the case of cohomogeneity one actions in
[
DK
,
CD1
,
CD2
,
Pu1
,
Pu2
,
BK
]
. In the homogeneous case, where it is
an algebraic problem, see the survey
[
BP
]
, and
[
PZ1
,
PZ2
]
for more recent results.

For a cohomogeneity one manifold, a PDE becomes an ODE and it is hence easier to find examples, explicit or non-explicit, as well as classifications, in various geometric problems. Just to mention a few, see
[
EW
,
DW1
,
DW2
,
PS1
,
PS2
,

,

,

,

]
.
In such results it is often important to first understand the initial value problem near a singular orbit, which is already non-trivial in general.
We will describe a method which can be used to solve such IVP’s in many geometric problems. We will assume throughout the paper, that a singular orbit exists since otherwise the initial value problem becomes a regular ODE.

Before stating our result, let us recall the structure of a cohomogeneity one manifold near a collapsing orbit. One starts with three Lie groups
H\subset K\subset G
with
K/H=\mathbb{S}^{\ell}
,
\ell>0
. The action of
K
on
\mathbb{S}^{\ell}
extends to a linear action on
\mathbb{D}=\mathbb{D}^{{\ell}+1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{\ell+1}
and thus
M=G\times_{K}\mathbb{D}
is a homogeneous disc bundle with boundary
G\times_{K}\partial\,\mathbb{D}=G\times_{K}K/H=G/H
a principal orbit.
By the slice theorem, any cohomogeneity one manifold has this structure near a singular orbit.

To be more explicit about the metric, define a splitting of the Lie algebras
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}
, orthogonal with respect to a background metric
Q
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, where
{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
is the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{k}}
. In Part I of this paper we assume that:

(*)\quad\text{ There are no $\operatorname{Ad}_{H}$ irreducible representations in }{\mathfrak{m}}\text{ which are equivalent to one in }{\mathfrak{p}}.

This assumption guarantees that
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}
are orthogonal with respect to any
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
invariant bi-linear form, in particular the metric, the tensor
T
and the Ricci tensor. This will significantly simplify the proofs and we will remove this assumption in Part II. Notice that since the equation is second order, we expect to get a solution after choosing the metric and the second fundamental form of the singular orbit arbitrarily.

.

Let
M=G\times_{K}\mathbb{D}
be a homogeneous disc bundle, describing the cohomogeneity one manifold near the singular orbit
G/K
and satisfying condition (*). Let
T
be a smooth symmetric bi-linear form on
M
. Given the metric and the second fundamental form of the singular orbit, there exists a smooth metric
g
in a neighborhood of
G/K
with
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=T
, when restricted to the principal orbits. The solution is unique, up to finitely many arbitrarily chosen constants.

We will also show that one can prescribe
T(\dot{c},\dot{c})

by reparametrizing the geodesic, see Section 6.2. But in general
T(\dot{c},X)
with
X
tangent to the regular orbits can not be prescribed, unless it is forced to be zero by
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
invariance, i.e.
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}={\mathfrak{m}}_{0}=\{0\}
. For special
T
, e.g., when
T
is diagonal, the full tensor can be prescribed. If
T
is analytic, the solution is analytic as well.

We now describe the method of proof, and the values of the free parameters. We fix a smooth curve
c(t)
transverse to all orbits and choose a metric of the form
g=dt^{2}+g_{t}
where
g_{t}
is a one parameter family of homogeneous metrics on the regular hypersurface orbits
G\cdot c(t)=G/H
. The metrics
g_{t}
are determined by a positive definite matrix of smooth functions
g_{ij}(t),\ t>0
. These functions need to satisfy an ODE which blows up at the singular orbit. The ODE is of regular singular type and hence does not necessarily have a solution. We will show that in our case there exists a formal power series solution. If
T
is smooth, there exists a nearby smooth solution to
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=T
by
[
Ma1
]
. If
T
is analytic, the power series converges, see
[
Ma2
,
GG
]
, and hence the solution is analytic as well.

One of the problems is that one first needs to determine when the metric (and the tensor) on the regular part extends smoothly to the singular orbit. In
[
VZ1
]
it was shown that this can be done in terms of a set of even functions. More precisely, there exist
a_{ij}^{k}\in\mathbb{R}
and integers
d_{k}\geq 0
such that the metric
g_{ij}
has a smooth extension to the singular orbit if and only if

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{k}\,g_{ij}(t)=t^{d_{k}}\phi_{k}(t^{2})\quad\text{ for }k=1,\cdots,N,

where
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{N}
are smooth even functions. The numbers
a_{ij}^{k}
and the integers
d_{k}
are determined by the structure constants of the Lie group
G
. This over determined system of
N
equations can be solved for the coefficients of the metric, thus describing
g_{ij}
in terms of
r
even functions, where
r
is the number of distinct non-zero entries in
g_{ij}
. The advantage of this method is that smoothness of the metric is now automatically built in.

By substituting these expressions for the metric into the differential equations, we will show that one can solve for the second derivatives of
\phi_{i}
, thus obtaining a system of the form
\phi_{i}^{\prime\prime}=F_{i}(\phi_{j},\phi_{j}^{\prime},T),\ i=1,\cdots,N
. Since the ODE is of regular singular type,
the smoothness of the functions
F_{i}
is sometimes obstructed unless some of the initial conditions
\phi_{i}(0)
satisfy certain (linear)
compatibility conditions
. Once these are satisfied, one obtains, by differentiating
F_{i}
, a formal power series for the metric.

We will see that the same proof can in fact be applied to many geometric ODE’s on a cohomogeneity one manifold. This holds e.g., for Einstein, Kähler Einstein, soliton or more generally quasi-Einstein metrics. In this case the second Bianchi identity implies that, if there exists a singular orbit, the regular orbits are orthogonal to
\dot{c}
, and that
\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{c},\dot{c})
is constant. Thus we have:

.

Let
M=G\times_{K}\mathbb{D}
be a homogeneous disc bundle satisfying condition (*). Given the metric and the second fundamental form of the singular orbit, there exists an analytic solution to the Einstein equation, or the soliton equation, in a neighborhood of
G/K
. The solution is unique, up to finitely many arbitrarily chosen constants.

To be more explicit about the compatibility conditions, and the choice of free parameters, we expand the metric

g_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}=A_{0}+tA_{1}+t^{2}A(t),\qquad g_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}=t^{2}\Id+t^{4}B(t),\qquad g_{|{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{m}}}=t^{2}C_{0}+t^{3}C(t)

where
A_{0},A_{1}
and
C_{0}
are constant matrices and
A,B,C
are smooth matrix functions of
t
. Smoothness may imply further restriction on
A(t),B(t)
and
C(t)
, see the discussion above. Notice though that under the assumption (*) we have
g_{|{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{m}}}=0
.
Here,
A_{0}
and
A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}
are the metric and the second fundamental form of the singular orbit
G/K
. Thus we want to choose
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
arbitrarily.

Under the action of
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
we can split
{\mathfrak{m}}
into irreducible modules
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
and a trivial module
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
. Without assuming that
g_{|{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{m}}}=0
, the following holds.

.

Let
M=G\times_{K}\mathbb{D}
be a homogeneous disc bundle. Then we have the following properties for any cohomogeneity one metric on
M
.

(a)

If
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}
is an irreducible
K
module, then

\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}=-(\dim({\mathfrak{p}})+1)\operatorname{tr}A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}+f_{1}(A_{0},A_{1},C_{0})

(b)

If
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}
are equivalent
K
modules and
u\to\bar{u}
is an explicit equivalence , then

\sum_{u}\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{u\bar{u}}=(\dim({\mathfrak{p}})+1)\sum_{u}A(0)_{u\bar{u}}+f_{2}(A_{0},A_{1},C_{0})

(c)

If
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}
is a trivial
K
module, then

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}=-(\dim({\mathfrak{p}})+1)\,A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}

(d)

On the module
{\mathfrak{p}}
we have

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Ric}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}=-3\operatorname{tr}B(0)+f_{3}(A_{0},A_{1},C_{0},A(0))

where
f_{i}
are explicit polynomials. In part (b), we need to consider 1,2,or 3 equivalences, depending on whether the representation is orthogonal, complex or symplectic. Notice that if
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}
are non-equivalent
K
modules, then
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})=0
by Schur’s Lemma. We also observe that
A(0)
and
B(0)
in (a)-(d) are never forced to be identically
0
by smoothness.

We will show, that in order to satisfy the compatibility conditions, we
must be able to prescribe
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}
,
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})
and
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\operatorname{Ric}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}
arbitrarily, after choosing
A_{0}
and
A_{1}
. Since they are
K
invariant, this is equivalent to prescribing their trace. Thus if we want to solve
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=T
(or
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=\lambda g
), Theorem C states that the values of
\operatorname{tr}A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}},\ A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}
and
\operatorname{tr}B(0)
for the metric
g
are determined by
T(0)
(resp.
A_{0}
and
\lambda
). After these are thus chosen, we obtain a formal power series for the metric. If
g_{|{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathfrak{m}}}\neq 0
, there are further compatibility conditions. In Part II we will show that these can be satisfied as well.

This implies that we have a simple description of the free parameters in Theorem A and B. The trace free part of
A(0)
and
B(0)
, if non-zero, as well as
\phi_{i}(0)
with
d_{k}>2
, are free parameters. If
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}
are equivalent
K
representations, all entries of
A_{u\bar{u}}
, except for one, are free parameters, and if in-equivalent, all possible entries of the metric are free parameters. Thus we can immediately read off the number of free parameters, and their values in terms of the coefficients of the metric. In Section 5 we give some examples that illustrate our methods.

Surprisingly, the contribution from
A(0)
and
B(0)
in Theorem C all come from the second fundamental form, and not the curvature of
G/H
. Proving the latter is in fact the main difficulty in our proof of Theorem A and B.

We remark that all of our results hold more generally if
G/H
is a homogeneous space for which
g({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{m}})=0
implies
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{m}})=0
. See
[
KPS
]
for a large collection of such examples, including many where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}\neq
{0}
\}
.

After one solves the initial value problem, the next question is of course weather there exist examples or obstructions to complete solutions on a cohomogeneity one manifolds. This is a rather delicate problem,
see e.g.,
[
PS1
,
DW1
,

,
DG2
,

or
[

This initial value problem for Einstein metrics was studied in

[
EW
]
(and for soliton metrics in
[
Buz
]
) under the assumptions (*) and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}=\{0\}
as well as that

In addition, smoothness conditions at the singular orbit are expressed in a more indirect way, and are thus difficult to determine explicitly in terms of the metric functions, see e.g.,
[

Proposition 2.4. Notice that this result follows immediately from Theorem C.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 1, we recall the structure of a cohomogeneity one manifold, describe the metric in terms of even functions and discuss the smoothness conditions.
In Sections 2 we outline our strategy of proof, and discuss what needs to be proved in order to show that the compatibility conditions can be satisfied. In Section 3 we compute the contribution of the second fundamental form to the compatibility condition, and in Section 4 that of the intrinsic curvature of the regular orbit. In Section 5 we illustrate our method with some examples and in Section 6 discuss further applications.

A non-compact cohomogeneity one manifold is given by a homogeneous vector bundle
and a compact and simply connected one by the union of two
homogeneous disc bundles. We can restrict ourselves to only one such bundle. Let
H,\,K,\,G
be Lie groups with inclusions
H\subset K\subset G
such that
H,K
are compact and

K/H=\mathbb{S}^{\ell},\ \ell>0
. The transitive action of
K
on

\mathbb{S}^{\ell}
extends (up to conjugacy) to a unique linear action on the vector space
V={\mathbb{R}}^{{\ell}+1}
.
We can thus define the homogeneous vector bundle

M=G\times_{K}V
and
G
acts on
M
via left action in the first component. This action has principal isotropy group
H
, and singular isotropy group
K
.
A neighborhood of the singular orbit is given by
G\times_{K}\mathbb{D}
where
\mathbb{D}\subset V
is a disc centered at
p_{0}=0\in V
.

We choose a vector
e_{0}\in V
and
consider the straight line
c(t)=te_{0}
.
At the regular points
c(t)
, i.e.,
t>0
,
the isotropy is constant equal to
H
and at
c(0)=0
it is equal to
K
.
We fix an
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
invariant splitting
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}
and identify the tangent space
T_{c(t)}G/H=\dot{c}^{\perp}\subset T_{c(t)}M
with
{\mathfrak{n}}

via action fields:
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}\to X^{*}(c(t))
. The stabilizer group
H
acts on
{\mathfrak{n}}
via the adjoint representation
and a
G
invariant metric on
G/H
is described by an
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}

invariant inner product on
{\mathfrak{n}}
. On the regular part, the metric along
c

is thus given by
g=dt^{2}+g_{t}
, with
g_{t}
a one parameter family of
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
invariant inner products on the vector space
{\mathfrak{n}}
, depending smoothly on
t
.

We choose a left invariant metric on
G
, right invariant under
K
, determined by the inner product
Q
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Hence
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
acts by isometries in
Q
.
We then choose a
Q
orthogonal
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
invariant splittings

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}\text{ and }{\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{n}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{1}\oplus\ldots\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}_{r}.

where
\operatorname{Ad}_{H}
acts trivially on
{\mathfrak{n}}_{0}
and irreducibly on
{\mathfrak{n}}_{i}
for
i>0
, and
an
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
invariant

orthogonal complement to
{\mathfrak{k}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
:

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{k}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}},\quad{\mathfrak{k}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}\ \text{ and thus }\ {\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{p}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}.

Here
{\mathfrak{m}}
can be viewed as the tangent space to the singular orbit
G/K
at

p_{0}=c(0)
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
as the tangent space of the sphere
K/H\subset V
.
Furthermore, let

{\mathfrak{m}}={\mathfrak{m}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\oplus,\cdots,\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}

be a
Q
orthogonal decomposition,
where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
is a trivial
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
module and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i},i>0
are
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
irreducible. We can assume that
{\mathfrak{n}}_{j}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}
or
{\mathfrak{n}}_{j}\subset{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}

Q
orthogonal

{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}

\operatorname{Ad}_{H}

{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
and irreducible on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}_{2}
. Depending on the sphere
K/H
, there may be one, two or three such modules.

Since
K
preserves the slice
V
, and acts linearly on it,
we have an embedding
{\mathfrak{p}}\subset V
defined by
X^{*}(c(t))=tX
for
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}
. In this language,
V\simeq\dot{c}(0)\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
.
Notice that, since
K
acts transitively on the unit sphere in

, a
K

is determined up to a multiple. Since for any
G

, which we denote by

Via the inclusion

We identify the homogeneous metrics
g_{t}
with an endomorphism
P_{t}
in terms of action fields:

(1.1)

P_{t}\colon{\mathfrak{n}}\to{\mathfrak{n}}\ \text{ where }\ g_{t}(X^{*},Y^{*})_{c(t)}=Q(P_{t}X,Y),\ \text{ for }\ X,Y\in{\mathfrak{n}}.

These endomorphisms

\operatorname{Ad}_{H}

{P_{t}}_{|{\mathfrak{n}}_{i}}=a(t)\Id_{|{\mathfrak{n}}_{i}}

{\mathfrak{n}}_{i}

{\mathfrak{n}}_{i}

Similarly, if

Choosing a
Q

{\mathfrak{m}}

(1.2)

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\,g_{ij}=t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k},\quad k=1,\cdots,N

We may collect all the corresponding constraints and we get an over determined system of equations in terms of the even functions

For

The formula for the Ricci tensor of

(1.3)

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(X,Y)=\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(X,Y)+Q(LX,Y)

where

(1.4)

L=-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}P^{\prime})P^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime}P^{-1}P^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime\prime}\ ,

Note that if we regard

as endomorphisms, we need to replace

. This needs to be taken into account if we compare the above formulas with those in

]
and in some other papers. In our notation

is the shape operator. Sometimes we may regard

, which will be clear from context.

The intrinsic curvature of the homogeneous space is more complicated and presents the main difficulty in solving the initial value problem. One has the following formula for the Ricci tensor of a homogeneous space (see e.g.,
[

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}g([X_{i},X]_{\mathfrak{n}},[X_{i},Y]_{\mathfrak{n}})+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{i,j}g([X_{i},X_{j}]_{\mathfrak{n}},X)g([X_{i},X_{j}]_{\mathfrak{n}},Y)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}B(X,Y)-g([Z,X],Y)

where
Z=\sum_{i}U(X_{i},X_{i})
with
2g(U(X,Y),Z)=g([Z,X],Y)+g([Z,Y],X)

{\mathfrak{g}}

{\mathfrak{n}}
. The last term involving

We choose a

Thus we have in the inner product

\displaystyle\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(X,Y)=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s}P^{-1}_{rs}\ Q(P[e_{r},X]_{\mathfrak{n}},[e_{s},Y]_{\mathfrak{n}})

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s,k,l}P^{-1}_{rs}P^{-1}_{kl}\ Q(P[e_{r},e_{k}]_{\mathfrak{n}},X)Q(P[e_{s},e_{l}]_{\mathfrak{n}},Y)-\frac{1}{2}B(X,Y).

\Gamma_{ij}^{u}=Q([e_{i},e_{j}],e_{u})\ \text{ or equivalently }\ [e_{i},e_{j}]_{\mathfrak{n}}=\sum_{t}\Gamma_{ij}^{t}e_{t}\ .

\operatorname{Ad}_{K}

\displaystyle\Gamma_{rs}^{k}=-\Gamma_{sr}^{k},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for all }r,s,k

(1.5)

\displaystyle\Gamma_{rs}^{k}=-\Gamma_{rk}^{s},\quad

\displaystyle\text{ for all }r,s,k\in I

Notice the Ricci curvature does not have to be diagonal, even if the metric is.

2.
Compatibility Conditions

In this section we do not assume that
C=0
, and hence the results can be used in Part II.

We want to solve the equation
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=T
for a given smooth tensor
T
(or the Einstein equation
\operatorname{Ric}(g)=\lambda g
). As we will see,
g
is already uniquely determined, up to a finite collection of constants, if we prescribe only
T_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}
, i.e. the tensor
T
tangent to the orbits. For an Einstein metric (or soliton metric) the second Bianchi identity implies that on the regular orbits
\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{c},\dot{c})
is constant and
\operatorname{Ric}(\dot{c},{\mathfrak{n}})=0
. Thus this is sufficient to prove the existence of an Einstein metric. We prefer to use the language of prescribing the Ricci tensor, since it illustrates the proof more clearly.

Recall that the metric is smooth iff

(2.1)

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{k}\,g_{ij}(t)=t^{d_{k}}\phi_{k}(t^{2})\quad\text{ for }k=1,\cdots,N,

where
\phi_{1},\cdots,\phi_{r}
are smooth even functions, and
a_{ij}^{k}
and
d_{k}
do not depend on the metric. The Ricci curvature, and the tensor
T
, satisfies the same smoothness conditions:

(2.2)

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{k}\,\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}(t)=t^{d_{k}}\phi_{k}(t^{2})\quad\text{ for }k=1,\cdots,N,

with the same coefficients
a_{ij}^{k}
and
d_{k}
. But if
C\neq 0
, the integers
d_{k}
may be two less than the one for the metric.

We will show that it is possible to solve the equations

(2.3)

\operatorname{Ric}(g)_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}=T_{|{\mathfrak{n}}}

if and only if the values of
\phi_{k}(0)
satisfy certain linear relations.
We will call these the compatibility conditions. The proof will show that this linear system admits solutions, but some of the values of
\phi_{k}(0)
may be chosen arbitrarily.

The Ricci curvature of a cohomogeneity one manifold is determined by the formulas (
1.3
),(
1.4
) and(
1.6
). They show that
it depends linearly on
P^{\prime\prime}
. Thus in
\sum a_{ij}^{k}\,\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}
, the term
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
is the only second order derivative appearing, with a nonzero coefficient. Our strategy is then to solve the equations

(2.4)

\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{k}\,\operatorname{Ric}_{ij}(t)=\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}^{k}\,T_{ij}(t)

for
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
, in which case (
2.3
) is equivalent to a system:

(2.5)

\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}=F_{k}(\phi_{j},\phi_{j}^{\prime},T),\ k=1,\cdots,N.

We will show that the functions
F_{k}
are smooth iff certain linear compatibility conditions among the initial conditions
\phi_{k}(0)
hold.
By differentiating the equations (
2.5
) we find a formal power series for the functions
\phi_{k},k=1,\cdots N
and hence for the metric
g_{ij}
. If
T
is smooth, Malgrange’s theorem
[
Ma1
]
implies that there exists a nearby smooth solution. If
T
is analytic, the power series converges, see
[
Ma2
,
GG
]
, and hence we obtain an analytic solution near the singular orbit. Notice that
\phi_{k}^{\prime}(0)=0
, and hence prescribing
\phi_{k}(0)
gives rise to a unique solution, after the compatibility conditions are satisfied. This may give rise to a number of free parameters among the values of
\phi_{k}(0)
.
We will see that these compatibility conditions are in fact determined by Theorem C.

The main part of the proof is thus to compute the compatibility conditions and show they can be solved. We consider the various cases that lead to the equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) in the smoothness conditions separately.

2.1.
Vectors tangent to the singular orbit.

Let us fix a basis
X_{i}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}
.
We know that it is possible to combine the components of the metric on
{\mathfrak{m}}
so that some linear combination will be equal to one of the forms

\sum a_{ij}^{k}g(X_{i},X_{j})=\begin{cases}a_{k}+t^{2}\,\phi_{k}&\\
t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k}&\end{cases}

where
\phi_{k}
is an even function,
d_{k}\geq 1
is an integer and
a_{k}\neq 0
. The same (except that
a_{k}
is allowed to be
0
) holds for the Ricci tensor and the tensor
T
. We consider the two cases separately

2.1.1.
The linear combination does not vanish at
t=0
.

The smoothness conditions state that

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=b_{k}+t^{2}\,\eta_{k},\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum a_{ij}^{k}T(X_{i},X_{j})=\rho_{k},

for some smooth even functions
\eta_{k},\rho_{k}
and some constant
b_{k}\neq 0
. We thus need to solve an equation of the form

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\rho_{k}

for
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\psi_{k}=\psi_{k}(\phi_{i},\phi_{i}^{\prime})
is another even function obtained by plugging the expressions for
g_{ij}
in terms of
\phi_{i}
into the formula for the Ricci tensor. According to our description of the smoothness,
\psi_{k}
does not contain any second derivatives. Since both
\psi_{k}
and
\rho_{k}
are even, we can solve this equation for
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
if and only if

\psi_{k}(0)=b_{k}=\rho_{k}(0)

which is possible if and only if

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})(0)

can be arbitrarily assigned, even after the metric on the singular orbit and the second fundamental form are chosen.

For an Einstein metric the equation becomes
\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})(0)=\lambda a_{k}
for some (arbitrarily chosen) Einstein constant
\lambda
. Since
a_{k}
is determined by the metric at the singular orbit, and is chosen arbitrarily ahead of time, the same proof solves the initial value problem for an Einstein metric near the singular orbit.

2.1.2.
The linear combination vanishes at
t=0
.

Here we have

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\,\eta_{k},\quad\sum a_{ij}^{k}T(X_{i},X_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\,\rho_{k}.

and we need to solve

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}=t^{d_{k}}\,\rho_{k}

for
\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\psi_{k}
is some new smooth even function.
Thus in this case there are no compatibility conditions.

2.2.
X,Y
in the slice
{\mathfrak{p}}

In this case there is no need to combine the components of the metric and the Ricci tensor since smoothness is equivalent to the assumption that the components of the metric or
\operatorname{Ric}
are even functions. Thus it is sufficient to consider any
X=Y
.
Smoothness implies that

g(X,X)=t^{2}+t^{4}\,\phi_{X},\quad\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)=t^{2}\,\eta_{X},\quad T(X,X)=t^{2}\,\rho_{X}

for a unit vector
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}
, i.e.,
|X|_{g_{0}}=1
.
Notice that for the Ricci curvature and for
T
, unlike for the metric, the second derivative at
t=0
can be arbitrary.
We need to solve

\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)=t^{2}\,\eta_{X}=t^{2}\,\psi_{X}-\frac{1}{2}t^{4}\,\phi_{X}^{\prime\prime}=t^{2}\,\rho_{X}

for
\phi_{X}^{\prime\prime}
, where
\psi_{X}
is another even function.
Hence the compatibility condition is satisfied
if and only if

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\operatorname{Ric}(X,X)

can be arbitrarily assigned, even after the metric on the singular orbit and the second fundamental form are chosen.

2.3.
X
tangent to the singular orbit and
Y
in the slice

Let
X_{i}
be a basis of
{\mathfrak{m}}
, and
Y_{j}
a basis of
{\mathfrak{p}}
.
We know that it is possible to combine the components of the metric so that some linear combination will be of the form

\sum a_{ij}^{k}g(X_{i},Y_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k}

where
\phi_{k}
is an even function, and
d_{k}\geq 2
an integer. For the Ricci tensor and
T
two cases may occur:

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})=\begin{cases}t^{d_{k}-2}\,\eta_{k}&\\
t^{d_{k}}\,\eta_{k}&\end{cases},\quad\sum a_{ij}^{k}T(X_{i},Y_{j})=\begin{cases}t^{d_{k}-2}\,\rho_{k}&\\
t^{d_{k}}\,\rho_{k}&\end{cases}

since, unlike for the metric,
\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})(0)
and
T(X_{i},Y_{j})(0)
do not have to vanish. We consider the two cases separately.

2.3.1.
Ricci and
g
vanish with the same order at
t=0
.

Using the expression of the Ricci tensor, and substituting the metric, we get

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}

where
\psi_{k}
is a new smooth function of
t
. If
\rho_{k}
is a prescribed even function, we want to see if it is possible to solve

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})=t^{d_{k}}\rho_{k}.

This means we have to solve

\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\rho_{k}

and hence there is no compatibility condition for this case.

2.3.2.
Ricci and
g
vanish with different orders at
t=0
.

Using the expression of the Ricci tensor and the metric, and using the fact that we know the order of vanishing at
t=0
, we get

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})=t^{d_{k}-2}\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t^{d_{k}}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}

where
\psi_{k}
is a smooth even function of
t
. As before, the function
\psi_{k}
does not contain second derivatives. If
\rho_{k}
is a prescribed even function, we want to see if it is possible to solve

\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})=t^{d_{k}-2}\rho_{k}.

Thus we have to solve

\psi_{k}-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\,\phi_{k}^{\prime\prime}=\rho_{k}

and hence the compatibility condition is

\psi_{k}(0)=\rho_{k}(0)

which is possible iff

\frac{1}{t^{d_{k}-2}}\,\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})(0)

can be arbitrarily assigned, even after the metric on the singular orbit and the second fundamental form are prescribed. This is equivalent to prescribing the first nonzero derivative of
\sum a_{ij}^{k}\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},Y_{j})
at
t=0
. This case is significantly more complicated and will be discussed in Part II.

.

Note that
Y_{i}
vanishes at
t=0
, hence
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(X_{i},Y_{i})(0)=0
. We will show, in the second part of the paper, that, writing

g_{|{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}}=t^{2}C_{0}+t^{3}C(t)

the elements of
C_{0}
are not involved in the compatibility conditions. This implies that we can have compatibility conditions only if
d_{k}>2
.

3.
Contribution from the second fundamental form

We start by computing the terms in
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(e_{u},e_{v})(0)
that depend on
L
, again without assuming that
C=0
. Recall that

(3.1)

L=-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}P^{\prime})P^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime}P^{-1}P^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime\prime}.

and that we chose
Q
such that
Q_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}
is the Euclidean inner product
g_{0}
.

Let

(3.2)

P_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}=t^{2}\,\Id+t^{4}B,\qquad P_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}=A_{0}+tA_{1}+t^{2}A,\qquad P_{|{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}}=t^{2}C_{0}+t^{3}C

where
A_{0},A_{1},C_{0}
are constant matrices and
A,B,C
are smooth functions of
t
. Smoothness may imply further restriction on
A,B
and
C
, but it turns out that this is not needed for our computations.

For the inverse we have

(3.3)

P^{-1}_{|{\mathfrak{p}}}=\frac{1}{t^{2}}-(B-C_{0}^{t}A_{0}^{-1}C_{0})+\cdots,\quad P^{-1}_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}=A_{0}^{-1}-t\ A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}A_{0}^{-1}+\cdots,\quad P^{-1}_{|{\mathfrak{m}}{\mathfrak{p}}}=-A_{0}^{-1}C_{0}+\cdots

and it turns out that higher order terms will not be needed.

.

The contribution to the compatibility conditions from
L
for the components of the Ricci tensor in
{\mathfrak{m}}
or
{\mathfrak{p}}
, are as follows:

\displaystyle(a)\,L({\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}})(0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\dim({\mathfrak{p}})+1)\,A(0)-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{-1}A_{1})\cdot A_{1}+\frac{1}{2}A_{1}A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}+2C_{0}C_{0}^{t}.

\displaystyle(b)\,\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}L({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2\dim({\mathfrak{p}})\ B(0)-\operatorname{tr}(B(0))\Id_{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}A_{0}^{-1}A_{1})\ \Id_{\mathfrak{p}}

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\trace(A_{0}^{-1}A)\Id_{\mathfrak{p}}+\operatorname{tr}(C_{0}^{t}A_{0}^{-1}C_{0})\ \Id_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Proof.

(a)
L({\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}})
.
For the compatibility condition in
\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}})
we need to compute the constant term.
The constant term in
-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}P^{\prime})P^{\prime}
is easily seen to be equal to

-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{-1}A_{1})\,A_{1}-\dim({\mathfrak{p}})\,A(0)

and in
\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime}P^{-1}P^{\prime}
the constant term is:

\frac{1}{2}A_{1}A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}+2C_{0}C_{0}^{t}

Finally, in
-\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime\prime}
the constant term is
-A(0).

Summing these, we get part (a).

(b)
L({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}})
.
For the compatibility condition in
\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}})
we need to compute the order two term. In
-\frac{1}{4}\operatorname{tr}(P^{-1}P^{\prime})P^{\prime}
it is

\displaystyle-2\dim({\mathfrak{p}})\ B-\operatorname{tr}(B)\Id_{\mathfrak{p}}+\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{tr}(A_{0}^{-1}A_{1}A_{0}^{-1}A_{1})\ \Id_{\mathfrak{p}}-\trace(A_{0}^{-1}A)\Id_{\mathfrak{p}}+\operatorname{tr}(C_{0}^{t}A_{0}^{-1}C_{0})\ \Id_{\mathfrak{p}}

and in
\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime}P^{-1}P^{\prime}
it is
6B(0)
and in
-\frac{1}{2}P^{\prime\prime}
it is clearly
-6B(0)

∎

Note that

may not be smooth at

since a

term will appear in the formula.

The case of the second fundamental form for inner products between
{\mathfrak{p}}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}
will be discussed in part II.

4.
Contributions from the intrinsic Ricci curvature

, again without assuming that

We will see that eventually, these contributions all vanish, and thus Theorem A and C follow from Proposition

Recall that for the intrinsic Ricci curvature we have:

(4.1)

\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(e_{u},e_{v})=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s,p,q}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{sq}^{v}P^{-1}_{rs}P_{pq}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,s,k,l,p,q}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{sl}^{q}P^{-1}_{rs}P^{-1}_{kl}P_{pu}P_{qv}

Our goal is to compute the intrinsic contributions to the compatibility conditions by substituting (

4.1.
Inner products in
{\mathfrak{m}}

According to the discussion of the previous sections we only need to consider the case where

arbitrarily. We first want to argue that it is sufficient to show that
\operatorname{Ric}(X_{i},X_{j})(0)
can be prescribed arbitrarily for all

into irreducible and trivial

The derivation of the smoothness conditions in

both lie in the same module
{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
or
{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
, or
X_{i}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{k},X_{j}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{\ell}

\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{m}}_{k},{\mathfrak{m}}_{\ell})

{\operatorname{Ric}_{M}}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}
is

•

If

for some

If

•

Similarly, if the equivalence is of quaternionic type, there are only four nonzero distinct entries.

•

For
\operatorname{Ric}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}

.

Notice that this also shows that the various cases all involve different entries in

We can thus discuss the various cases separately according to the irreducible and trivial
K

We start with the following observation.

.

Proof.

Using (

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s\in I,\;p\in J}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{sp}^{v}(B_{rs}(0)(A_{0})_{pp})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,s\in I,\;p\in J}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{sp}^{v}(B_{rs}(0)(A_{0})^{-1}_{pp}(A_{0})_{uu}(A_{0})_{vv})

lie in different
K
irreducible modules, then either
\Gamma_{rp}^{u}
or
\Gamma_{rq}^{v}
vanish, and similarly for the second term. If
e_{u},e_{v}
lie in a trivial module, the structure constants vanish.
It is easy to see that
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(e_{u},e_{v})(0)
depends only on
C_{0}
but not on
C(0)
, since
C(t)
vanishes at order at least
3
.

As we saw in Proposition
3.4
,
B(0)
and
C(0)
do not enter in the extrinsic curvature either, and hence the claim follows.
∎

Now we compute the contribution from
A(0)
in the curvature of
G/H
. Surprisingly, these contributions always cancel in
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)
, but only after we add the extrinsic curvature and take the trace.

Using (
4.1
), one easily sees that contributions from
A(0)
to
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(e_{u},e_{v})
,
writing
A
for simplicity, consist of 4 terms:

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{v}A_{pq}

(4.4)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r\in I,\;k,l,p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rl}^{q}\left(-A_{0}^{-1}AA_{0}^{-1}\right)_{kl}\left(A_{0}\right)_{pu}\left(A_{0}\right)_{qv}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r\in I,\;k,l,p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rl}^{q}\left(A_{0}^{-1}\right)_{kl}\left(A_{0}\right)_{qv}A_{pu}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle 2\cdot\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r\in I,\;k,l,p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rl}^{q}\left(A_{0}^{-1}\right)_{kl}\left(A_{0}\right)_{pu}A_{qv}

We will in fact see that, in the cases involved in the compatibility conditions, the terms involving
A_{0}
always cancel, and we get that the contribution of
A(0)
to the intrinsic curvature is given by

(4.5)

-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{v}A_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{v}A_{pu}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{pv}

4.1.1.
Irreducible
K
modules

As we saw, it is sufficient to compute
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(X,X)
for any
X\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
and show it can be prescribed arbitrarily.
We break up the index set
J=J_{0}\cup J_{1}\cup\cdots\cup J_{k}
such that
e_{u}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
if
u\in J_{i}
. Clearly
\Gamma_{rq}^{u}=0
if
r\in I,\ u\in J_{k},q\in J_{l}
with
k\neq l
. It follows that, in (
4.4
), the contributions of
A_{0}
cancel. For
e_{u}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
(
4.5
) and (
1.5
) imply that the contribution of
A(0)
in
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{u})
, adding the extrinsic contribution, is equal to:

-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{u}A_{pq}+\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{pu}+L(e_{u},e_{u})(0)

Since
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}
is a multiple of the
\Id
on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
, we can take the trace and divide by
\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
. Using (
1.5
), and renaming indices, the intrinsic part vanishes since:

\displaystyle-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J,\;u\in J_{i}}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{u}A_{pq}+\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J,\;u\in J_{i}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{pu}=

\displaystyle-\sum_{r\in I,\;k,p\in J,\;u\in J_{i}}\Gamma_{rp}^{k}\Gamma_{ru}^{k}A_{pu}+\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J,\;u\in J_{i}}\Gamma_{rp}^{k}\Gamma_{ru}^{k}A_{pu}=0

Thus

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}=\frac{1}{\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}\operatorname{tr}(L(0))_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}+f(A_{0},A_{1},C_{0})

and according to Proposition
3.4
(a) we can solve the compatibility condition for
\operatorname{tr}A(0)_{{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}
since its coefficient is non-zero.

We note that only
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}
is
K
invariant, but not
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}
or
L(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}}
, and thus the cancellations will only be visible after we take the trace in
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
. The contributions of
A_{0},A_{1}
and
C_{0}
in
f
are explicit in the extrinsic part according to Proposition
3.4
, but there are further ones in
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(0)
, and a constant term as well. We leave it to the reader to compute those explicitly from (
4.1
).

4.1.2.
Equivalent
K
Modules

We now consider the case of two equivalent
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
irreducible modules
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1},{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
. Let
J_{k},k=1,2
be the index sets such that
e_{u}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{k}
for
u\in J_{k}
.
We first assume that the representations are of real type. Thus there exists a unique isometry
f\colon{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\to{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
, an intertwining operator between
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
, i.e.,
f([v,w])=[v,f(w)],\ v\in{\mathfrak{k}},w\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
. We choose a
Q
-orthonormal basis
e_{i}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
, and the
Q
-orthonormal basis

\epsilon_{\overline{i}}=f(e_{i})
of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
. Notice that this also induces a bijective map on index sets:
F\colon J_{1}\to J_{2}
with
F(k)=\bar{k}
. For the structure constants we get the relations:

(4.6)

\text{ for }r\in I\text{ we have }\Gamma_{ri}^{k}=\Gamma_{r\bar{i}}^{\bar{k}}\text{ and }\Gamma_{r\bar{i}}^{k}=0\text{ for all }i,k\in J_{1}

since, e.g., for
v\in{\mathfrak{k}}
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}

\langle[v,w_{1}],w_{2}\rangle=\langle f[v,w_{1}],f(w_{2})\rangle=\langle[v,f(w_{1})],f(w_{2})\rangle

By Schur’s Lemma and
K
invariance of
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)
we have

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{v}})=0,\quad\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})=\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{v},e_{\bar{v}})\text{ for }u,v\in J_{1},\ u\neq v

and we need to show that
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})
can be prescribed arbitrarily.

Although
A_{0}
is a multiple of
\Id
on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
, they may not be the same. The contributions of
A_{0}
nevertheless cancel due to
(
4.6
). We can then use (
4.5
) and (
4.6
) and, since
\Gamma_{rq}^{p}=0
if
e_{p},e_{q}
lie in different
K
irreducible modules, one obtains the contribution of
A(0)
in
\operatorname{Ric}_{G/H}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})
:

\displaystyle-\sum_{r\in I,\;p\in J_{1},q\in J_{2}}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{\bar{u}}A_{pq}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{2}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{\bar{u}}A_{pu}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{p\bar{u}}

(4.7)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{r\bar{q}}^{\bar{u}}A_{p\bar{q}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{r\bar{k}}^{\bar{p}}\Gamma_{r\bar{k}}^{\bar{u}}A_{\bar{p}u}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{p\bar{u}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{rq}^{u}A_{p\bar{q}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{u\bar{p}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,k\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}A_{p\bar{u}}

Since
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})
is independent of
u
, after adding the extrinsic contribution, we can take the trace in
u
and divide by
\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
.
The intrinsic part then vanishes since

-\sum_{r\in I,\;p,q,u\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{ru}^{p}\Gamma_{ru}^{q}A_{p\bar{q}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,u\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{ru}^{p}\Gamma_{ru}^{q}A_{q\bar{p}}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r\in I,\;p,u\in J_{1}}\Gamma_{ru}^{p}\Gamma_{ru}^{q}A_{p\bar{q}}=0

Thus

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})=\frac{1}{\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}\sum_{u\in J_{1}}L(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})+f(A_{0},A_{1},C_{0})

and Proposition
3.4
(b) implies that we can solve the compatibility condition for any of the variables
A_{u\bar{u}}
with
u\in J_{1}
.

Next we consider the case where
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
are of complex type. Thus there exists a complex structure
J\colon{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}\to{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and we can choose a compatible
Q
orthonormal basis
\{e_{u},J(e_{u})\}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and the corresponding
Q
orthonormal basis
\{e_{\bar{u}},J(e_{\bar{u}})\}
of
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
. Due to Schur’s Lemma we have only two non-vanishing components for
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)({\mathfrak{m}}_{1},{\mathfrak{m}}_{2})
:

(4.8)

\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},e_{\bar{u}})=\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(J(e_{u}),J(e_{\bar{u}})),\quad\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{u},J(e_{\bar{u}}))=-\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(0)(e_{\bar{u}},J(e_{u}))

and we need to show that they can be prescribed arbitrarily.
The formulas are actually the same as in the previous case by using the two intertwining operators

f_{1}(e_{u})=e_{\bar{u}},\ f_{1}(J(e_{u}))=J(e_{\bar{u}}),\quad\text{ and }\quad f_{2}(e_{u})=J(e_{\bar{u}}),\ f_{2}(J(e_{u}))=e_{\bar{u}}

together with the two bijections
F_{1},F_{2}\colon J_{1}\to J_{2}
of the index sets. Similarly, if
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
is of symplectic type, we have three complex structures on
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and obtain the same conclusion.

4.1.3.
Trivial
K
Module

If
e_{u},e_{v}\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
, then all structure constant vanish since
[e_{r},e_{q}]=0
when
r\in I
and
q\in{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}
and there is no intrinsic contribution. According to Proposition
3.4
, all entries
A_{uv}(0)
are thus determined by the compatibility conditions. Since a trivial
K
module is also a trivial
H
module, smoothness implies that
A_{uv}(t)
are even functions, and that
A_{uv}(0)
is not forced to be
0
. Thus we always have compatibility conditions.

4.2.
Inner products in
{\mathfrak{p}}

In this case smoothness simply means that all components of
\operatorname{Ric}
are even functions. Thus we have
\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(e_{u},e_{u})=t^{2}\phi_{u}
for some even function
\phi_{u}
. We want to compute
\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\operatorname{Ric}(e_{u},e_{u})
and show that it can be prescribed arbitrarily. Since the limit is also
K
invariant, it is independent of
u\in I
.
From (
4.1
) it follows that the contribution from
B(0)
(that we will denote by
B
for simplicity) in the first part of the intrinsic curvature is equal to

-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,p\in I}\Gamma_{ru}^{p}\Gamma_{su}^{p}(-B_{rs})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,p\in I}\Gamma_{ru}^{p}\Gamma_{ru}^{q}B_{pq}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,p\in I}\Gamma_{rp}^{u}\Gamma_{sp}^{u}B_{rs}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{r,p\in I}\Gamma_{pr}^{u}\Gamma_{ps}^{u}B_{rs}=0

In the second part we get two terms:

\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,k,p\in I}\Gamma_{rk}^{p}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}B_{pu}-\frac{1}{4}\sum_{r,k\in I}\Gamma_{rk}^{u}\Gamma_{sk}^{u}B_{rs}

Adding the extrinsic part in Proposition
3.4
(b), we can take the trace over
u
, and we see that the above intrinsic contribution vanishes.
Thus

\lim_{t\to 0}\frac{1}{t^{2}}\operatorname{Ric}_{M}(e_{u},e_{u})=\frac{1}{\dim{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}\sum_{u\in I}L(0)(e_{u},e_{u})=-3\operatorname{tr}B(0)+f(A_{0},A_{1},A(0),C_{0}).

and we can solve for
\operatorname{tr}B(0)
.

We summarize the conclusions:

•

There is only one compatibility condition for each irreducible
\operatorname{Ad}_{K}
module
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
, and we can solve for
\operatorname{tr}A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}}
.

•

When
Ric_{M}({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})(0)
is not forced to be
0
by the smoothness conditions, there are one, two or four compatibility conditions when
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
,
{\mathfrak{m}}_{j}
are two equivalent irreducible modules, depending on whether the representation is real, complex, or symplectic. We can solve them for any entry in
A(0)({\mathfrak{m}}_{i},{\mathfrak{m}}_{j})
.

•

A(0)_{|{\mathfrak{m}}_{0}}
is completely determined by the compatibility conditions.

•

There is only one compatibility condition in
{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Notice though that the condition for
{\mathfrak{p}}
involves
A(0)
, hence we first determine the entries for
A(0)
, and then solve foe
\operatorname{tr}B(0)
. Furthermore, the various components of
A(0)_{\mathfrak{m}}
are involved in only one of the compatibility conditions. Hence all compatibility conditions can be satisfied.

This finishes the proofs of Theorems A and B in the Introduction. Notice that we proved Theorem C along the way.

5.
Examples

We now illustrate the methods in a few examples.

5.1.
Example 1

Consider the cohomogeneity one manifold defined by the triple
SO(2)\subset SO(2)\times SO(2)\subset SO(4)
, where the
SO(2)
are diagonal blocks. Let

Z=E_{34},\quad V_{1}=E_{13}+E_{24},\quad V_{2}=-E_{14}+E_{23},\quad V_{3}=E_{14}+E_{23},\quad V_{4}=-E_{13}+E_{24}.

We have

{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{0}=\{Z\},\quad{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}=\{V_{1},V_{2}\},\quad{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}=\{V_{3},V_{4}\}.

Notice that the slice is orthogonal to the orbits, i.e.
C=0
, and hence our theorems apply. The modules
{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}
and
{\mathfrak{m}}_{2}
are irreducible complex equivalent
H
-modules, which are also irreducible
K
modules, but are non-equivalent under the action of
K
.
For the metric we have

f=\langle Z,Z\rangle,\qquad g_{1}=\langle V_{1},V_{1}\rangle=\langle V_{2},V_{2}\rangle,\qquad g_{2}=\langle V_{3},V_{3}\rangle=\langle V_{4},V_{4}\rangle,

b_{1}=\langle V_{1},V_{3}\rangle=\langle V_{2},V_{4}\rangle,\qquad b_{2}=\langle V_{1},V_{4}\rangle=-\langle V_{2},V_{3}\rangle

According to Lemma 5 and 6 in
[
VZ1
]
, since
d_{1}=d_{2}=1
and
a=1
, smoothness implies that

f=t^{2}\Id+t^{4}\phi_{1},\qquad g_{1}=a_{1}+t^{2}\phi_{2},\qquad g_{2}=a_{2}+t^{2}\phi_{3}

b_{1}=t^{2}\phi_{4},\qquad b_{2}=t^{2}\phi_{5}.

Thus we have

B=\phi_{1}\Id,\qquad A_{0}=\operatorname{diag}(a_{1}\Id,a_{2}\Id)\qquad A_{1}=0

and

A=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{2}&0&\phi_{4}&\phi_{5}\\
0&\phi_{2}&-\phi_{5}&\phi_{4}\\
\phi_{4}&-\phi_{5}&\phi_{3}&0\\
\phi_{5}&\phi_{4}&0&\phi_{3}\end{pmatrix}

Thus the singular orbit is totally geodesic. Since
{\mathfrak{m}}_{i}
are
K
invariant and irreducible, the compatibility conditions determines
\phi_{2}(0)
and
\phi_{3}(0)
. Since they are in-equivalent as
K
modules, there is no compatibility condition on
\phi_{4}(0)
and
\phi_{5}(0)
. The value of
\phi_{1}(0)
is determined by the compatibility condition.
Thus we can prescribe the tangential part of the Ricci tensor (or the Einstein equations), and after choosing the metric of the singular orbit, i.e.,
a_{1},a_{2}
, the values of
\phi_{4}(0),\phi_{5}(0)
are free parameters.

In this example it is not possible to fully prescribe
Ric_{M}(\dot{c},\dot{c})
. Indeed,
[
VZ1
]
equation (5) shows that smoothness implies that
Ric_{M}(\dot{c},\dot{c})=\psi_{1}(t)
,
Ric_{M}(Z,Z)=t^{2}\psi_{2}(t)
and
Ric_{M}(Z,\dot{c})=t\psi_{3}
where
\psi_{1}
,
\psi_{2}
and
\psi_{3}
are even functions. Furthermore, it also implies that
\psi_{1}(0)=\psi_{2}(0)
.

On the other hand, we will see in Section 6.2 that we can prescribe
Ric_{M}(\dot{c},\dot{c})
if we allow to reparametrize the geodesic
c
.

5.2.
Example 2

Consider the cohomogeneity one manifold defined by the triple
SO(2)\subset SO(3)\subset SO(4)
, where all the embeddings are (upper) block diagonal. Thus
{\mathfrak{h}}=\{E_{12}\}
. Let

V_{1}=E_{13},\quad V_{2}=E_{23},\quad V_{3}=E_{14},\quad V_{4}=E_{24},\quad Z=E_{34}.

Under the

Here
{\mathfrak{p}}

{\mathfrak{m}}_{1}

The metric is defined by the functions:

Using Lemma 5, Lemma 7(a) and Lemma 9 in

5.3.
Example 3

The results have an interesting application to Einstein metrics on Euclidean space. For each transitive action of
K
on a sphere, we get a corresponding family of cohomogeneity one metrics on Euclidean space. The case with most freedom is the action of
K=Sp(n)
on
{\mathbb{H}}^{n}={\mathbb{R}}^{4n}
. It give rise to a 7 parameter family of
K
invariant cohomogeneity one metrics on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4n}
. These can be defined in terms of the Hopf fibration
\mathbb{S}^{3}\to\mathbb{S}^{4n-1}\to{\mathbb{H}}P^{n-1}
. The isotropy representation of
H=Sp(n-1)
on
{\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{p}}
decomposes as
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
where
H
acts trivially on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}\simeq\operatorname{Im}{\mathbb{H}}
and via matrix multiplication on
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}\simeq{\mathbb{H}}^{n-1}
. If
X_{1},X_{2},X_{3}
is the standard basis in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{0}
, orthonormal in the Euclidean metric, and
Y_{i}
a
Q
-orthonormal basis in
{\mathfrak{p}}_{1}
, then the metric is determined by the functions

f_{ij}=\langle X_{i},X_{j}\rangle,\quad h=\langle Y_{i},Y_{i}\rangle

and the smoothness conditions are

f_{ij}=t^{2}\,\delta_{ij}+t^{4}\phi_{ij},\quad h=t^{2}+t^{4}\psi

for some even functions
\phi_{ij},\psi
. According to our results, there is only one compatibility condition, which in the case of Einstein metrics (with Einstein constant
\lambda
) says

(5.1)

\operatorname{tr}B(0)=-3(\Sigma_{i}\,\phi_{ii}(0)+4n\psi(0))=\lambda

Thus we have
6
free parameters
\phi_{ij}(0)
, and
\psi(0)
is determined, which gives rise to a
6
-parameter family of analytic Einstein metrics in a neighborhood of the origin.
The case of
G=Sp(n)Sp(1)
was studied in
[
Wi
]
and
G=Sp(n)U(1)
in
[
Ch1
,
Ch2
]
in order to construct complete Einstein metrics and Ricci solitons. In the first case
f_{11}=f_{22}=f_{33}
, in the second
f_{22}=f_{33}
, and in both cases
f_{ij}=0
for
i\neq j
. Thus there exists a 2-parameter, resp. 3 parameter family of
G
invariant metrics with 1 resp. 2 free parameters.

6.
Further solutions

We now indicate that one can apply the same proof with minor changes to obtain solutions to other geometric problems.

6.1.
Solitons

Recall that
(M,g,u)
is called a
m
-quasi Einstein metric if

(6.1)

\operatorname{Ric}_{u,m}(g)=\operatorname{Ric}(g)+\Hess(u)-\frac{1}{m}du\otimes du=\lambda g

where
\lambda
is the quasi Einstein constant. When
\frac{1}{m}=0
solutions are called gradient Ricci solitons.
On a cohomogeneity one manifold we assume that
g
and
u
is
G
-invariant. Notice that if
G
is compact, the
G
-invariance of
u
follows via integration, although in the result below, we do not assume that
G
is compact.

In
[
DW2
,
Wi
]
it was shown that, if there exists singular orbit, the second Bianchi identity implies that
\operatorname{Ric}(X,\dot{c})=0
for
X\in{\mathfrak{p}}\oplus{\mathfrak{m}}
. Furthermore, on a cohomogeneity one manifold (
6.1
) becomes (using

6.3.
Non-unimodular groups
G

We now indicate that the extra term in the intrinsic curvature of the principal orbit
G/H
for a non-unimodular Lie group
G
does not contribute to the compatibility conditions.

Recall that the extra term that contributes to
\operatorname{Ric}(e_{u},e_{v})
is

-g([Z,e_{u}],e_{v})\quad\text{ with }Z=\sum_{i}U(X_{i},X_{i})

A straightforward computation shows that this term is equal to:

(6.5)

Z=\sum_{k,\ell}\Gamma_{ks}^{s}P^{-1}_{k\ell}e_{\ell}\quad\text{ and }\quad-g([Z,e_{u}],e_{v})=-\sum_{k,\ell,s,t}\Gamma_{ks}^{s}\Gamma_{\ell u}^{t}P^{-1}_{k\ell}P_{tv}

Notice that if
k\in I
, t the expression vanishes. This easily implies that in (
4.1
) the term does not contribute to
B(0)
or
A(0)
, and thus there is no contribution to the compatibility conditions in
\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{m}},{\mathfrak{m}})
. By the same argument there is no contribution to
\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{p}})
. It also does not contribute to
\operatorname{Ric}({\mathfrak{p}},{\mathfrak{m}})
since we need to lower the degree by two. Thus we can ignore the term
-g([Z,e_{u}],e_{v})
in our proof of Theorems A and B.

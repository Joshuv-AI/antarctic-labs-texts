Cyclotomic enumeration of polynomials
Richard EHRENBORG

Abstract
. Using the cyclotomic identity we compute sums over d d -tuples of monic polynomials in F q ​ [ x ] F_{q}[x] weighted by the multiplicity of their irreducible factors. As consequences we determine explicit expressions for the number of d d -tuples of polynomials such that their greatest common divisor is r r th power free. We also compute the number of monic polynomials where the multiplicity of each irreducible factor belongs to the monoid generated by two relatively prime integers.

1.
Introduction

Let
F_{q}
be the finite field of cardinality
q
where
q
is a prime power.
The number of monic square-free polynomials in the polynomial ring

F_{q}[x]
of degree
n

is given by
q^{n}-q^{n-1}
for
n\geq 2
.
For this result and its connection
to algebraic geometry; see the papers
[
4
,
5
]

and references therein.
Alegre, Juarez and Pajela
extended this enumeration to count
the number of monic polynomials of degree
n\geq r

that are
r
th power free
and their result is the equally beautiful

q^{n}-q^{n+1-r}
; see
[
1
]
.
In this paper we further extend these results to
enumerate other classes of polynomials
defined in terms of multiplicities of their irreducible factors.
Two noteworthy results are as follows:
First, the number of
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
such that their greatest common divisor is
r
th power free
is given by
q^{N}-q^{N+1-dr}
,
where
N
is the sum of the degrees and
each individual degree is at least
r
;
see Proposition
4.1
.
Second, the number of monic polynomials
where no irreducible factor has multiplicity
1
is given by

q^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}+q^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor-1}-q^{\lfloor(n-1)/3\rfloor}

where the degree
n
is at least
2
;
see Example
4.7
.
This last example is a special case where the allowed multiplicities
belong to a monoid generated by two relatively prime integers.

The paper is organized as follows.
In Section
2

we review the cyclotomic identity which is the engine behind all
these results.
In Section
3

we show that any multivariate generating function with constant coefficient
1

factors as an infinite product of the form

\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}1/\left(1-{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}
;
see equation (
3.1
).
We use this form of the generating function to obtain the main result
on weighted enumeration of
d
-tuples of monic polynomials.
In the case of a single polynomial, that is
d=1
, this result was also
obtained by Stanley
[
12
]
.
In Section
4

we apply Theorem
3.2
to enumerate
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
where the multiplicity vector belongs to certain subsets of
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}
.
In Section
5
we offer two results on the number
of irreducible factors.
We end the paper with open problems.

2.
Preliminaries

We begin by stating the well-known enumeration of
the number of irreducible polynomials with coefficients in the finite field
F_{q}
.
To make this note self-contained we include a brief proof.

Lemma 2.1
.

The number of monic irreducible polynomials in
F_{q}[x]

of degree
n
is given by

\displaystyle M(q,n)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n}\cdot\sum_{k|n}\mu(n/k)\cdot q^{k},

where
\mu
denotes the classical Möbius function.

Proof.

Consider the field
F_{q^{n}}
as a vector space over the field
F_{q}

and consider the subspace arrangement consisting of all the subfields of
F_{q^{n}}
,
that is,
\{F_{q^{k}}\}_{k|n,k<n}
.
Observe that the intersection lattice is the divisor lattice of the positive integer
n
.
By the finite field method of Athanasiadis
[
2
]

or the principle of inclusion–exclusion
[
10
]
,
we know that the number of points
not in any subfield
in
F_{q^{n}}
is given by

\displaystyle\left|F_{q^{n}}-\bigcup_{k|n,k<n}F_{q^{k}}\right|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k|n}\mu(n/k)\cdot|F_{q^{k}}|=\sum_{k|n}\mu(n/k)\cdot q^{k},

where
\mu
denotes the classical, that is,
the number–theoretic, Möbius function.
Finally for such a point
a
we know that
the
n
elements

a,a^{q},a^{q^{2}},\ldots,a^{q^{n-1}}

form the roots of an irreducible polynomial in
F_{q}[x]

of degree
n
, explaining the factor
1/n
.
∎

We next present the classical cyclotomic identity.
There are many proofs of this identity,
such as those using free Lie algebras
[
6
, Eq. (5.3.17)]
,
Lyndon words
[
6
, Theorem 5.1.5]

or bijections
[
7
,
8
]
.
However, we are mainly interested in
the monic polynomials in the ring
F_{q}[x]
.
Hence we present a brief proof of the cyclotomic identity
using this polynomial ring and
its uniqueness of factorization.
This proof can be found in
[
9
]

and
[
5
, Lemma 2.1]
.

Theorem 2.2

(The Cyclotomic Identity)
.

The following identity for formal power series holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{1-qz}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{1-z^{k}}\right)^{M(q,k)}.

Proof.

The left-hand side generating function enumerates the
number of monic polynomials of degree
n
in
F_{q}[n]
.
Note that

1/(1-z^{k})
is the generating function for enumerating the powers
of one irreducible polynomial of degree
k
.
Hence

1/(1-z^{k})^{M(q,k)}
is the generating function for all possible products
of the
M(q,k)
irreducible polynomials of degree
k
.
Hence the right-hand side is the generating function for all
products of irreducible polynomials.
The identity follows by the uniqueness of factorization in
F_{q}[x]
.
∎

3.
Weighted enumerating according to multiplicities

For a vector
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})

in
{\mathbb{N}}^{d}

define the monomial

{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}=z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}
.
Let
w_{{\bf n}}=w_{(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})}

be weights and form the generating function

\displaystyle g({\bf z})

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}.

We furthermore assume that

w_{{\bf 0}}=w_{(0,\ldots,0)}=1
, that is,
the constant term of
g({\bf z})
is
1
.
Next we show that the generating function
g({\bf z})

can be expressed as an infinite product.

Lemma 3.1
.

The generating function
g({\bf z})
with constant term
1

can be uniquely written as

(3.1)

\displaystyle g({\bf z})

\displaystyle=\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}.

Proof.

To observe this fact, take the logarithm on both sides

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}b_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}=\log(g({\bf z}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}a_{\bf i}\cdot\log\left(\frac{1}{1-{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)=\sum_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}a_{\bf i}\cdot\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1}{k}\cdot{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf i}}.

Note that
w_{{\bf 0}}=1
implies that
b_{{\bf 0}}=0
.
By comparing the coefficients of
{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}
and
multiplying with
\gcd({\bf n})

we have

\displaystyle\gcd({\bf n})\cdot b_{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k|\gcd({\bf n})}\frac{\gcd({\bf n})}{k}\cdot a_{{\bf n}/k}=\sum_{k|\gcd({\bf n})}\gcd({\bf n}/k)\cdot a_{{\bf n}/k}.

Hence by Möbius inversion and dividing by
\gcd({\bf n})
we have

\displaystyle a_{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\gcd({\bf n})}\cdot\sum_{k|\gcd({\bf n})}\mu(k)\cdot\gcd({\bf n}/k)\cdot b_{{\bf n}/k}=\sum_{k|\gcd({\bf n})}\frac{\mu(k)}{d}\cdot b_{{\bf n}/k},

showing that the factorization in (
3.1
) exists
and is unique.
∎

Let
F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]

denote the set of all

monic
polynomials in
F_{q}[x]
.
For two polynomials
p
and
s
in
F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]
,
where
s
is irreducible, let

\mult(p;s)
denote the multiplicity of
s
in the polynomial
p
.
That is, the polynomial
p
factors as

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\prod_{s}s^{\mult(p;s)},

where the product is over all monic irreducible polynomials
s
.
Similarly for a list
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}

let

\mult({\bf p};s)=(\mult(p_{1};s),\ldots,\mult(p_{d};s))

and

\deg({\bf p})=(\deg(p_{1}),\ldots,\deg(p_{d}))
.
For a list
{\bf p}
in
F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}

define its
weight
(relative to
w
as above) to be the product

\displaystyle\wt({\bf p})

\displaystyle=\prod_{s}w_{{\mult({\bf p};s)}},

where
s
ranges over all irreducible polynomials
s
.
Note that the product is well-defined since we assumed
that
w_{{\bf 0}}
is
1

and hence only a finite number of factors are not equal to
1
.

For a vector
{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}

let
\alpha_{q}({\bf n})
denote the sum of the weights
of
d
-tuples of monic polynomials in
F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}

of degree
{\bf n}
, that is,

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}({\bf n})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}\\
\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\end{subarray}}\wt({\bf p}).

Note that if the weights
w_{{\bf n}}
are all

0
or
1
then

\alpha_{q}({\bf n})
enumerates
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
such that the multiplicity vector

\mult({\bf p};s)
belongs to the set

\{{\bf n}\in{\mathbb{N}}^{d}:w_{{\bf n}}=1\}

for all irreducible polynomials
s
.

Theorem 3.2
.

The generating function for the sum of the weights
of the
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\alpha_{q}({\bf n})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}},

where the powers
a_{{\bf i}}
are given by equation (
3.1
).

Proof.

After the simultaneous substitutions
z_{i}\longmapsto z_{i}^{k}
we have

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}.

This quantity corresponds to one irreducible polynomial of degree
k
.
Multiplying over all irreducible polynomials
s

we obtain the sought after generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\alpha_{q}({\bf n})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{s}\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{\deg(s)\cdot{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf n}}\right)^{M(q,k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}\right)^{M(q,k)}

\displaystyle=\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\frac{1}{1-{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf i}}}\right)^{M(q,k)}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}.

Now applying the cyclotomic identity with
z={\bf z}^{{\bf i}}

for each
{\bf i}>{\bf 0}
yields the desired result.
∎

See Stanley
[
12
, Section 6]

for the case
d=1
of this result.

4.
Enumerative results

We now give explicit examples of applications of Theorem
3.2
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
d
and
r
be two positive integers.
The number of
d
-tuples
{\bf p}=(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

of monic polynomials such that
their degrees are given by the vector
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})

and
their greatest common divisor

\gcd(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

is
r
th power free
is given by

(4.1)

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}({\bf n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{N}-q^{N+1-rd}&\text{ if }\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\geq r,\\
q^{N}&\text{ if }\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\leq r-1,\end{cases}

where
N
denotes the sum
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
.

Proof.

We use the weights
w_{{\bf n}}
,
given by
1
if
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})<r
and
0
otherwise.
Next note that

\displaystyle\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0}z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z_{1}}\cdots\frac{1}{1-z_{d}},

(4.2)

\displaystyle g({\bf z})=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0\\
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})<r\end{subarray}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z_{1}^{r}\cdots z_{d}^{r}}{(1-z_{1})\cdots(1-z_{d})}.

We apply Theorem
3.2

to the generating function
g({\bf z})
:

\displaystyle\frac{1-q\cdot z_{1}^{r}\cdots z_{d}^{r}}{(1-q\cdot z_{1})\cdots(1-q\cdot z_{d})}

\displaystyle=(1-q\cdot z_{1}^{r}\cdots z_{d}^{r})\cdot\sum_{n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0}q^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}}\cdot z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0\\
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})<r\end{subarray}}q^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}}\cdot z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0\\
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\geq r\end{subarray}}(1-q^{1-rd})\cdot q^{n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}}\cdot z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}.

The result follows by reading off the coefficient of
{\bf z}^{\bf n}
.
∎

Example 4.2
.

Setting
r=1
in
Proposition
4.1

we obtain that
the number of
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
of degree
(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})

that are relatively prime
is given by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q^{N}-q^{N+1-d}&\text{ if }\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\geq 1,\\
q^{N}&\text{ if }\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})=0.\end{cases}

Example 4.3
.

Similarly, setting
d=1
in the proposition
we obtain the Alegre–Juarez–Pajela result
[
1
]
,
that the number of
r
th power free monic polynomials of degree
n
is given by

(4.4)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}q^{n}-q^{n+1-r}&\text{ if }n\geq r,\\
q^{n}&\text{ if }n\leq r-1.\end{cases}

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
d
,
r
and
m
be positive integers such that
r<m
.
Then the number of
d
-tuples of monic polynomials
(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})

of degree
{\bf n}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})

such that the multiplicity of each irreducible factor
in
\gcd(p_{1},\ldots,p_{d})
is
congruent to one of
0,1,\ldots,r-1\bmod m

is given by

(4.5)

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}({\bf n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}q^{N}\cdot\left(1+q^{1-md}+\cdots+q^{\lfloor\min({\bf n})/m\rfloor\cdot(1-md)}\right)&\text{ if }\min({\bf n})<r,\\[14.22636pt]
q^{N}\cdot\left(1+q^{1-md}+\cdots+q^{\lfloor\min({\bf n})/m\rfloor\cdot(1-md)}\right)&\\
-q^{N-rd+1}\cdot\left(1+q^{1-md}+\cdots+q^{\lfloor(\min({\bf n})-r)/m\rfloor\cdot(1-md)}\right)&\text{ if }\min({\bf n})\geq r,\end{cases}

where
N
is again the sum
n_{1}+\cdots+n_{d}
.

Proof.

The associated generating function
g({\bf z})
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0\\
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})\equiv 0,1,\ldots r-1\bmod m\end{subarray}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-z_{1}^{m}\cdots z_{d}^{m}}\cdot\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{1},\ldots,n_{d}\geq 0\\
\min(n_{1},\ldots,n_{d})<r\end{subarray}}z_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots z_{d}^{n_{d}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-z_{1}^{r}\cdots z_{d}^{r}}{(1-z_{1}^{m}\cdots z_{d}^{m})\cdot(1-z_{1})\cdots(1-z_{d})},

where the last step is by equation (
4.2
).
Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}c_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(1-q\cdot z_{1}^{m}\cdots z_{d}^{m})\cdot(1-q\cdot z_{1})\cdots(1-q\cdot z_{d})}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}q^{k}\cdot(z_{1}\cdots z_{d})^{mk}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}q^{N}\cdot{\bf z}^{\bf n}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}q^{N}\cdot\left(1+q^{1-md}+\cdots+q^{\lfloor\min({\bf n})/m\rfloor\cdot(1-md)}\right)\cdot{\bf z}^{\bf n}.

Hence the coefficient of
{\bf z}^{\bf n}
in the generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\alpha_{q}({\bf n})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1-q\cdot z_{1}^{r}\cdots z_{d}^{r}}{(1-q\cdot z_{1}^{m}\cdots z_{d}^{m})\cdot(1-q\cdot z_{1})\cdots(1-q\cdot z_{d})}

is given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}({\bf n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}c_{{\bf n}}&\text{ if }\min({\bf n})<r,\\
c_{{\bf n}}-q\cdot c_{{\bf n}-(r,\ldots,r)}&\text{ if }\min({\bf n})\geq r,\\
\end{cases}

which is the expression in equation (
4.5
).
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
a
and
b
be two relatively prime positive integers.
Let
M
be the monoid
\{a\cdot i+b\cdot j:i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}\}
.
Then the number of monic polynomials in
F_{q}[x]
of degree
n
such
that the multiplicity of each irreducible factor belongs to
the monoid
M
is given by

(4.6)

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n\end{subarray}}q^{i+j}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n-ab\end{subarray}}q^{i+j+1}.

Proof.

The associated generating function
g(z)
is given by

\displaystyle g(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in M}z^{n}=\frac{1-z^{ab}}{(1-z^{a})\cdot(1-z^{b})};

see for instance
[
3
, Exercise 1.33]
.
To apply Theorem
3.2

note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{(1-q\cdot z^{a})\cdot(1-q\cdot z^{b})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n\end{subarray}}q^{i+j}\cdot z^{n}.

Multiplying with
1-q\cdot z^{ab}
yields
the desired generating function

(4.7)

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\alpha_{q}(n)\cdot z^{n}=\frac{1-q\cdot z^{ab}}{(1-q\cdot z^{a})\cdot(1-q\cdot z^{b})}

\displaystyle=(1-q\cdot z^{ab})\cdot\bigg(\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n\end{subarray}}q^{i+j}\cdot z^{n}\bigg)

and hence the coefficient of
z^{n}
is given by
equation (
4.6
).
∎

Assume that
n
is large enough and that
we have a pair
(i,j)
of integers such that

a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n
.
Then the index of every summand in the first sum in (
4.6
)
is of the form

(i+k\cdot b,j-k\cdot a)

and thus the powers of
q
in this sum
are of the form

i+j+k\cdot(b-a)
.
That is, the powers lie in the infinite arithmetic progression
containing
i+j
with step size
b-a
.
Similarly, the summands in the second sum in (
4.6
)
are indexed by pairs of the form

(i-b+k\cdot b,j-k\cdot a)
,
and hence the powers in this sum
are of the form

i+j+1-b+k\cdot(b-a)
.
Hence if any cancellation occurs between the two sums,
we need
b-1
to be divisible by
b-a
, that is,

b\equiv 1\bmod b-a
.
Note that this condition is equivalent to

a\equiv 1\bmod b-a
.
In this case we present an improved statement without
any cancellation. In order to do so, we follow the notation
of
[
3
, Chapter 1]
:
Let
a^{-1}
and
b^{-1}
denote two integers such that

\displaystyle a\cdot a^{-1}

\displaystyle=1\bmod b,

\displaystyle b\cdot b^{-1}

\displaystyle=1\bmod a.

Furthermore, let
\{x\}
denote the fractional part of the real number
x
,
that is,
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
a
and
b
be two relatively prime positive integers
such that
a<b
and
b\equiv a\equiv 1\bmod b-a
.
Let
M
be the monoid
\{a\cdot i+b\cdot j:i,j\in{\mathbb{N}}\}

and let
n>a\cdot b-a-b
.
Then the number of monic polynomials in
F_{q}[x]
of degree
n
such
that the multiplicity of each irreducible factor belongs to
the monoid
M
is given by

(4.8)

\displaystyle\alpha_{q}(n)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{b}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/a-(b-a)\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}}-\frac{q^{a}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/b+(b-a)\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}}.

Proof.

Assume that
n
is large.
Let
(i_{0},j_{0})
be given by

i_{0}=n/a-b\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}

and

j_{0}=a\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}
.
Then the pair
(i_{0},j_{0})
satisfies

a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n
,
and
j_{0}
is the smallest such nonnegative integer
j

and
i_{0}
is the largest such integer
i
.
Since
a<b
we know that
i_{0}+j_{0}
is the maximum over all

i+j
where
a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n
.
Similarly, the pair
(i_{0}-b,j_{0})

is the corresponding such pair for the equation

a\cdot i+b\cdot j=n-ab
.
Hence the powers in the first sum in equation (
4.6
)
are a finite arithmetic progression starting at
i_{0}+j_{0}
and decreasing with step
b-a
.
Similarly, the powers in the second sum in equation (
4.6
)
are an arithmetic progression starting at
i_{0}+j_{0}-b
and also decreasing with step
b-a
.
Note that

i_{0}+j_{0}=n/a-(b-a)\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}
.
Thus the positive terms in the difference in equation (
4.6
)
are given by

(4.9)

\displaystyle q^{i_{0}+j_{0}}+q^{i_{0}+j_{0}-(b-a)}+\cdots+q^{i_{0}+j_{0}+1-a}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{1-b}-1}{q^{a-b}-1}\cdot q^{i_{0}+j_{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{b}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/a-(b-a)\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}}.

Similarly, the negative terms in equation (
4.6
)
is obtained by switching the roles of
a
and
b

and also switching
n
and
n-ab
.
That is, let

i_{1}=b\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot(n-ab)/b\}=b\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}

and

j_{1}=(n-ab)/b-a\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot(n-ab)/b\}=n/b-a-a\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}
.
Then
a\cdot i_{1}+b\cdot j_{1}=n-ab

,

\displaystyle=(q^{1-a}+q^{1-a+(b-a)}+\cdots+q^{a-b})\cdot q^{n/b+(b-a)\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{a}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/b+(b-a)\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}}.

Finally, we have to motivate the lower bound on

)

.
Similarly,
the expression in equation (

)

when

Hence the generating functions associated with these two expressions are

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{b}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/a-(b-a)\cdot\{b^{-1}\cdot n/a\}}\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{A(z)}{1-q\cdot z^{a}},

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{q^{a}-q}{q^{b}-q^{a}}\cdot q^{n/b+(b-a)\cdot\{a^{-1}\cdot n/b\}}\cdot z^{n}

\displaystyle=\frac{B(z)}{1-q\cdot z^{b}},

5.
Two results on the number of irreducible factors

Let

including multiplicities.

and
f_{2}(p)=1

p
factors as

Assume that the weights
(w_{{\bf n}})_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}
are non-negative real numbers
and that
\alpha_{q}({\bf n})
in Theorem
3.2

is non-zero.
Then we can view the ratio

\wt({\bf p})/\alpha_{q}({\bf n})

as a probability distribution
on the set

\{{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}:\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\}

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
\alpha_{q}({\bf n})
is non-zero
and pick a polynomial vector

at random from

\{{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}:\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\}

with the above described distribution.
Then the expected value of

is given by

\displaystyle\frac{M(q;j)}{\alpha_{q}({\bf n})}\cdot[{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}]\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}n_{1}\cdot w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\cdot\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}},

where
[{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}]
denotes the operation
extracting the coefficient of
{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

Proof.

Consider the generating function

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}\\
\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\end{subarray}}t^{f_{j}(p_{1})}\cdot\wt({\bf p})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\\
\deg(s)=j\end{subarray}}\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}s\\
\deg(s)\neq j\end{subarray}}\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{\deg(s)\cdot{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}\right)^{M(q;j)}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\geq 1\\

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\right)^{M(q,j)}\cdot\prod_{k\geq 1}\left(\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{k\cdot{\bf n}}\right)^{M(q,k)}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\right)^{M(q,j)}\cdot\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}.

Apply the partial derivative
\partial/\partial t

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}\\
\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\end{subarray}}f_{j}(p_{1})\cdot t^{f_{j}(p_{1})-1}\cdot\wt({\bf p})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=M(q;j)\cdot\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}n_{1}\cdot w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}-1}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\cdot\left(\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot t^{n_{1}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\right)^{M(q;j)-1}\cdot\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}

and

to be
1

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bf p}\in F_{q}^{\text{monic}}[x]^{d}\\
\deg({\bf p})={\bf n}\end{subarray}}f_{j}(p_{1})\cdot\wt({\bf p})\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf n}}

\displaystyle=M(q;j)\cdot\frac{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}n_{1}\cdot w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}{\sum_{{\bf n}\geq{\bf 0}}w_{{\bf n}}\cdot{\bf z}^{j\cdot{\bf n}}}\cdot\prod_{{\bf i}>{\bf 0}}\left(\frac{1}{1-q\cdot{\bf z}^{{\bf i}}}\right)^{a_{\bf i}}.

The result follows by reading off the coefficient of

Proposition 5.2
.

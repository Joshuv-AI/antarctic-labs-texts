Embryonic sheep placenta alleviates muscle-fat wasting symptoms and improves gut microbiome dysbiosis in cachexia mice
Authors: Mengze Sun, Lixue Tu, Zhongkun Zhou, Yunhao Ma, Hao Zhang, Xinrong Jiang, Hongyuan Tu, Juan Lu, Yuqing Niu, Yuqi Yue, Rui Yang, Min Chen, Hong Fang, Zhenzhen Si, Peng Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Cachexia is a multifactorial wasting syndrome characterized by muscle-fat depletion, accompanied by significant gut microbiome disturbances. Embryonic sheep placenta (ESP), a functional food known to modulate immunity and gut microbiome, represents a potential intervention substance. By in vitro and in vivo experiments combined with multi-omics analysis, this study provides experimental evidence for the effect of ESP on cachexia. These studies have found that ESP and its extract alleviate cachexia by increasing 91.80% (P < 0.0001) muscle tube diameter, 19.56% (P < 0.01) muscle cross-sectional area and 37.56% (P < 0.05) fat mass. Western blot shows that ESP downregulates the expression of TRIM63 and inhibits the expression of the HSL protein in the AMPK signaling pathway. Integrated multi-omics analysis suggests that ESP reshapes the gut microbiota structure of cachexia mice, restores their gut homeostasis and improves energy metabolism pathways. In conclusion, ESP plays a protective role in cachexia mice by alleviating the symptoms of muscle-fat atrophy, maintaining gut homeostasis and improving energy metabolism pathway.

Introduction

Tumor cachexia is a multifactorial wasting syndrome characterized by skeletal muscle atrophy, fat depletion and systemic inflammation, accompanied by persistent weight loss and intestinal microbiome disturbances, leading to progressive functional impairment and metabolic disturbances [1,2]. The appearance of cachexia leads to anorexia and malnutrition, and decreases the responsiveness and increases the toxicity of chemotherapeutic agents that negatively impacts the quality of life of cancer patients [3,4], thus significantly increasing cancer mortality that directly responsible for nearly 30% of cancer deaths [5]. Therefore, cachexia is receiving increasingly widespread attention.

However, there are no approved drugs or treatments for the prevention and treatment of tumor cachexia [[6], [7], [8]]. Even the recognized therapeutic drugs megestrol and medroxyprogesterone acetate (MPA) can only relieve symptoms by stimulating appetite [6,[9], [10], [11]]. Anamorelin, as a gastrin receptor agonist, is clinically used to treat cachexia in patients with stage III non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), aiming to enhance appetite and anabolic activity. However, it remains a chemically derived drug that is inevitably associated with serious adverse effects, the most serious of which are caused by cardiac and liver dysfunction [[12], [13], [14]]. Therefore, the current methods for preventing and treating cachexia are extremely limited, and there is an urgent need to develop safe and effective methods for preventing and treating cancer-related cachexia.

The use of functional food plays an important role in maintaining the balance of gut microbiome and regulating the metabolic balance of the body [15,16]. In traditional Chinese theory, cachexia caused by major malignant diseases such as tumors can lead to abnormal activation of metabolic pathways in the human body and disorder of intestinal homeostasis, thereby causing symptoms such as weight loss, reduction of muscle and fat in patients. Embryonic sheep placenta (ESP), is obtained by removing the amniotic membrane and umbilical cord of the Ovis aries Linnaeus and then dried and concocted, which is rich in bioactive small molecule peptides, nucleic acids and amino acids and other bioactive substances. And it has the effect of enhancing the immunity of the body, anti-oxidation, regulating intestinal homeostasis and raising white blood cells [[17], [18], [19]]. Moreover, it is extremely safe. Currently commonly used as a functional food. However, the prevention and treatment effect of ESP and its extract on tumor cachexia have not been reported.

Skeletal muscle atrophy, fat loss and intestinal disorders are characteristic indicators of cachexia. Meanwhile, the intestinal microbiome is also one of the key regulatory factors of the body's metabolism and immune response [[20], [21], [22]]. Dysbiosis of the gut microbiota and their effects on metabolic and physiological functions may play an important role in the development of cachexia and are closely related to the pathophysiological processes of skeletal muscle, lipid metabolism, inflammatory responses, and other tissues and organs associated with the development of cachexia [23].

In this paper, ESP was used as a research object to screen its efficient active ingredients and evaluate the mitigation of tumor cachexia by ESP and its extract in vitro and in vivo by constructing cellular and animal models. Meanwhile, the plasma metabolome, gut microbiome classification and function of mice were analyzed by combining serum non-targeted metabolomics and fecal 16S rRNA gene sequencing analysis to deeply explore the intrinsic mechanism of ESP in alleviating tumor cachexia. Combining the results of in vitro and in vivo studies and multi-omics analyses, we revealed the exciting effects and possible mechanisms of ESP and its extract on tumor cachexia.

Methods and materials

Cells

Human colorectal cancer cell line HCT116 was purchased from the American Standard Culture Collection (ATCC Lot: 70019042); human hepatocellular carcinoma cell line SMMC7721 and mouse colorectal cancer cell line CT26 were the strains kept in the laboratory. C2C12 mouse myoblast cell and 3T3-L1 preadipocyte cell line were obtained from the typical culture preservation Committee of Chinese Academy of Sciences. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Animals

Male BALB/c mice (6-8 weeks old) were purchased from Lanzhou Veterinary Research Institute, Chinese Academy of Agricultural Sciences (LVRI, CAAS, Gansu, China), the approval number is SCXK (Gan) 2020-0002. The animal experiments were carried out following the procedures approved by Reporting of In Vivo Experiment (ARRIVE) guidelines the protocol approved by the Ethics Committee of School of Pharmacy, Lanzhou University (Approval date: 2022-03-10). Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Extraction and segmentation of active ingredients from ESP

Embryonic sheep placenta lyophilized powder (ESP) was obtained from fresh ESP after extraction. Embryonic sheep placenta <50 KD molecular weight extract (ESPA), embryonic sheep placenta 50-100 KD molecular weight extract (ESPB), and embryonic sheep placenta >100 KD molecular weight extract (ESPK) were obtained from fresh ESP after extraction and segmentation. The products were stored in a −20 °C refrigerator for subsequent in vitro and in vivo studies. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

MTT assay

3-(4,5-Dimethylthiazol-2-yl)-2,5-diphenyltetrazolium bromide (MTT) method was used to determine the cytotoxicity of ESP, ESPA, ESPB and ESPK on HCT116, SMMC7721, CT26, C2C12 and 3T3-L1. The concentration of the gradient concentration administration group was set at 200, 400, 600, 800 and 1000 (μg/mL), and three replicate wells were set for each group. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Tumor cachexia models in vitro

In vitro tumor cachexia models had been reported in previous articles [24]. Briefly, after induced differentiation of C2C12 myoblasts and 3T3-L1 preadipocytes, tumor cachexia myotubular atrophy model and adipose degradation model were induced using CT26 supernatant and treated in the presence of different concentrations of ESP and ESPK for 48 h. Then, cells were collected for western blotting analysis and morphological analysis. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Tumor cachexia model in vivo

In vivo tumor cachexia models had been reported in previous articles [24]. Briefly, BALB/c male mice with similar initial body weight were randomly divided into 7 groups (9 mice per group): tumor-free group (Control), CT26 tumor bearing mice group (CT26 model), ESP prevention group and treatment group (ESP-800 mg/kg), ESPK prevention group and treatment group (ESPK-800 mg/kg), and MPA treatment group (MPA-150 mg/kg). Body weight, tumor size, water consumption and food intake were measured daily from subcutaneous injection of tumor cells until the end of the experiment. When the subcutaneous tumors could be palpated, the preventive group received ESP and its extract by oral gavage. When their weight dropped by more than 10%, the ESP and its extract treatment group and MPA group began treatment by oral gavage. At the end of dosing, blood and tissues were collected from the mice for subsequent experiments. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Oil red O dye

After microscopic observation of 3T3-L1 preadipocytes induced to produce lipid droplets, staining was performed according to the instructions of Oil Red O stain kit (Cat# G1262, Solarbio, Beijing, China). Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Haematoxylin–eosin staining (HE staining)

The treated cells and Gastrocnemius (GA) tissue specimens were stained with HE according to the standard procedure. The staining was observed by bright-field microscopy and the images of HE staining were analyzed using Image J software. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Determination of serum physiological and biochemical indicators

Serum samples stored at −80 °C were removed, thawed at room temperature. Four physiological and biochemical factors, including triglycerides (TG), glucose (GLU), total protein (TP) and albumin (ALB), were measured using a fully automated biochemical analyzer (ACCUTETBA-40FR, Toshiba, Japan).

ELISA

The inflammatory cytokines tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-6 (IL-6) in the mouse serum were determined by using ElaBoX™ Mouse TNF-α ELISA Kit (Cat# SEKM-0034, Solarbio, Beijing, China) and ElaBoX™ Mouse IL-6 ELISA Kit (Cat# SEKM-0007, Solarbio, Beijing, China).

Western blotting

Protein supernatants were extracted from C2C12, 3T3-L1, GA tissues and epididymal fat (eWAT) tissues using RIPA high efficiency lysate containing 1% PMSF and phosphatase inhibitors. BCA Protein Assay kit (Cat# PC0020, Solarbio, Beijing, China) was used to quantify the protein concentrations in the supernatants. Western blotting detected changes in protein levels of TRIM63, FBXO32, AKT and phosphorylated AKT in C2C12 cell and GA tissues, and changes in protein levels of HSL, phosphorylated HSL, and PRKAA1 in 3T3-L1 cell and eWAT tissues. The experimental results were quantified using the gray scale method. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Fecal 16S rRNA gene sequencing and bioinformatics data analysis

The genomic DNA of 42 mice were extracted and its purity and concentration were detected. The sequencing library was generated for processing and analysis, and the gut microbiome analysis data was obtained. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Non-targeted metabolomics and bioinformatics analysis of serum metabolites

The non-targeted metabolomics of 42 mice serum samples were studied by liquid chromatography-mass spectrometry (LC-MS) and gas chromatography-mass spectrometry (GC-MS). The data of serum metabolites and metabolic pathways were obtained after processing and analysis. Details are described in the Data Supplemental Methods.

Correlation profiling between the metabolites and gut microbes

Correlation analysis was performed between the serum differential metabolites and gut differential bacteria screened out and the muscle-fat mass of cachexia mice [25]. Ultimately, correlation curves for differential metabolites, differential bacteria and muscle-fat mass were obtained. Eventually, Spearman correlation analysis was used to comprehensively explore the relationship among gut microbiota, energy metabolism and cachexia-related indicators, and a correlation heatmap was obtained.

Statistical analysis

All the results are shown as the mean ± standard deviation, and the data were analyzed by SPSS 23.0, GraphPad Prism 9.0.0 and Image J software. Comparisons between groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), two-way ANOVA or t-test. *vs. CT26 model group mice. *P < 0.05, **P < 0.01, ***P < 0.001 and ****P < 0.0001.

Results

Activity screening of ESP and its different molecular weight extracts

As shown in Fig. S2A, ESP, ESPA, ESPB and ESPK were obtained after fresh ESP extraction. As shown in Fig. S2B–S2D, ESP and ESPK had inhibitory effects on the proliferation of HCT116, SMMC7721 and CT26 tumor cells in cytotoxicity assays, the IC50 of ESP and ESPK treated CT26 for 48 h reached 24.04 and 87.08 (μg/mL). Meanwhile, Fig. S2E–S2F showed that ESP and ESPK had almost no cytotoxic effect on C2C12 and 3T3-L1, and they not only did not inhibit the proliferation of normal cells, but also played a role in promoting their growth with increasing concentrations. Therefore, we selected ESP and ESPK as the next study subjects.

ESP and its extract alleviate C2C12 myotubular atrophy in vitro

As shown in Fig. 1A-C, the microscopically observed alleviating effect of ESP and its extract on C2C12 muscle tubule atrophy induced by CT26 cell supernatant was concentration-dependent. In the different concentrations of ESP and ESPK, the muscle tube diameter increased by 57.80% (P < 0.0001), 77.97% (P < 0.0001), 58.33% (P < 0.0001) and 91.80% (P < 0.0001), respectively. The changes of myotube-related signaling proteins in C2C12 myotubes after treatment with CT26 medium were further examined by Western blot (Fig. 1D). As shown in Fig. 1E, CT26 medium induced the expression of TRIM63, while decreasing the expression of phosphorylated AKT. In contrast, the expression of TRIM63 was down-regulated and phosphorylated AKT expression was up-regulated after treatment with ESP and its extract. The above results indicated that ESP and its extract may reduce protein degradation by influencing the levels of E3 ubiquitin ligases, thereby alleviating muscle atrophy. Additionally, the treatment process of ESP is accompanied by the upregulation of p-AKT, and the AKT is associated with the body's energy metabolism. This suggests that the improvement of cachexia symptoms may have a potential association with changes in energy metabolism.

ESP and its extract alleviated 3T3-L1 adipocytes lipolysis in vitro

As could be seen in Fig. 1F, after staining with oil red O, the cells turned orange-red, indicating that 3T3-L1 preadipocytes had been induced to become adipocytes, which could be used for the next step to construct the adipocyte model. In Fig. 1G-H, Western blot results showed that the levels of PRKAA1 and phosphorylated HSL was increased in mature 3T3-L1 adipocytes treated with CT26 cell supernatant. In contrast, PRKAA1 and phosphorylated HSL decreased in levels following treatment ESP and its extract. However, quantification of the data from repeated experiments revealed that this reduction was not statistically significant.

As shown in Fig. 2, we systematically evaluated the effects of ESP and its extract on various physiological indicators in CT26 tumor-bearing mice. Fig. 2A showed the process and grouping of animal experiments. As shown in Fig. 2B–C, weight loss and tumor mass increased in CT26 model mice, weight recovery and tumor mass and growth rate decreased in control and administration groups. Among them, the body weight of mice in ESP preventive administration group was increased by 9.87% (P < 0.01) and the tumor mass was reduced by 71.36% (P < 0.0001) compared with that in CT26 model group. The effect was good and better than that in MPA-treatment group. However, as could be seen from Fig. 2D–E, ESP and ESPK did not significantly improve the food intake and water intake of mice with cachexia. Interleukin IL-6 and tumor necrosis factor TNF-α are pro-inflammatory factors that increase in many pathological conditions. As shown in Fig. 3E–F, the levels of TNF-α and IL-6 in the serum of mice in the CT26 model group were increased, indicating that there might be an inflammatory response in cachexia mice. Following ESP and ESPK administration, the levels of TNF-α and IL-6 decreased in CT26 model, with a more significant decreased in IL-6 content. The above results indicated that ESP and ESPK could reduce the levels of body inflammatory factors.

As shown in Fig. 4A–D, the GA mass of mice in the CT26 model group decreased 19.56% (P < 0.0001) and muscle fiber cross-sectional area decreased 25.86% (P < 0.0001) compared with the control group. After administration, the GA mass of mice in ESP prevention group was increased by 13.46% (P < 0.01) and the cross-section area of muscle fibers was increased by 22.42% (P < 0.0001) compared with that in CT26 model group, the other groups also showed significant improvement. ESP and its extract ameliorated eWAT degradation in CT26 tumor-bearing mice

Further characterization and statistics were performed on the most significantly changed eWAT. Fig. 4G–H showed that the mass of eWAT in CT26 model group was reduced by 56.64% (P < 0.0001). In contrast, the ESP and ESPK prevention groups respectively increased by 26.25% and 37.56% (P < 0.05) compared to the CT26 model group, effectively preserving the mass of eWAT. These results suggested that ESP and ESPK interventions could improve the reduction in eWAT mass weight induced by CT26 tumor load, where preventive administration is more effective. Furthermore, western blot was conducted to assess the expression levels of signaling proteins associated with the AMPK pathway in eWAT tissue, with results presented in Fig. 4I–J. The results showed that the changes trend of signaling protein levels eWAT tissue were consistent with the changes of cell models in vitro. Meanwhile, ESP and its extract significantly inhibited the activation of HSL protein in the AMPK pathway of cachexia mice.

The dysregulation of the Firmicutes-to-Bacteroidetes ratio is primarily linked to energy metabolism. Fig. 5E and F showed the differences in community structure composition and microbial species at the phylum and family levels. Compared with the control group, the Firmicutes decreased and the Bacteroidetes increased in the CT26 model group. Whereas, after ESP and ESPK administration, the number of Firmicutes phylum increased, and the number of Bacteroidetes decreased, which was close to the proportion of control group. Further analysis at the family level suggested that the abundance of Lachnospiraceae and Prevotellaceae increased. From the above results, it could be observed that ESP regulated the composition of the gut microbial community and affected the bacterial groups related to energy metabolism. In addition, further PCA analysis and UPGMA analysis also showed a separation of the seven groups (Fig. 5G–H), indicating that the model is reliable.

These differential groups mainly involved Lachnospiraceae, Lactobacillaceae, which had a positive effect on energy balance metabolism. Lactobacillus, which improved the gut environment and stimulated the production of beneficial substances, showed an increasing trend in the administered group. Metabolomics differential metabolites related KEGG pathway annotation

KEGG Pathway was enriched according to the screened differential metabolites, and the most important metabolic pathway and signal transduction pathway involved by differential metabolites were determined through pathway enrichment. Based on the above enrichment results, the KEGG pathway map was drawn. The results showed that 10 major interfering pathways (P < 0.05) were enriched in the groups treated with ESP and ESPK, which were significantly different from the CT26 model group. As shown in Fig. S3A–S3D, the differential metabolic pathways involved: (1) glycerophospholipid metabolism; (2) choline metabolism in cancer; (3) fatty acid biosynthesis; (4) unsaturated fatty acid biosynthesis; (5) sphingolipid metabolism; (6) linolenic acid metabolism; (7) galactose metabolism; (8) central carbon metabolism; (9) protein digestion and absorption; and (10) lysine degradation. Among them, the first six metabolic pathways were mainly related to lipid metabolism, while the last four were closely related to energy metabolism, glucose metabolism and protein synthesis and catabolism.

Glycerophospholipid metabolism pathway was the most significantly enriched and differentially enriched pathway in the ESP and its extract administration group. Fig. S3E showed the pathway annotation of glycerophospholipid metabolism in KEGG, and it could be seen that a variety of metabolites present in this pathway were reflected in our histological analysis results. Glycerophospholipid metabolism was an important pathway of lipid metabolism, and the enhancement of glycerophospholipid metabolism promoted lipid anabolic metabolism. The above results showed that the administration of ESP and its extract could affect lipid metabolism and other pathways in the body, causing an increase in the content of related metabolites, which may play a potential protective role in inhibiting eWAT mass loss in cachexia mice.

Correlation analyses were conducted on the differential bacteria, differential metabolites, serum biochemical indicators and inflammatory factors, cachexia phenotypic indicators and expression level of tissue proteins. The results in Fig. S4 and S5 indicated that muscle-fat mass, gut bacterial abundance, serum indicators and the expression levels of signal proteins in tissues were correlated. After giving ESP and its extract to cachexia mice, the increase in muscle-fat mass, enrichment of Lachnospiraceae and Lactobacillaceae, the elevation of metabolites and biochemical factors related to energy metabolism (such as l-glutamine, GLU and TG) levels, and changes in AKT and other signal protein expressions were positively correlated. This suggested that ESP might affect the expression of signal proteins to improve the cachexia phenotype, or it might alleviate cachexia symptoms by influencing the gut microbiota and energy metabolism pathways.

Discussion

Approximately 20-40% of cancer patients die from tumor cachexia, however, there is still a lack of methods to safely and effectively prevent and treat tumor cachexia [26]. The role of gut microbiome in tumor cachexia is receiving increasing attention, disruption of its homeostasis produces pro-inflammatory factors that disrupt the intestinal barrier and trigger systemic inflammation, which in turn induces muscle and fat atrophy [27], aggravating the development of cachexia. In traditional Chinese theory, cachexia belongs to the category of "deficiency labor" due to its clinical symptoms such as emaciation and mental fatigue. ESP, as a common functional food, plays an important role in regulating qi and blood and the metabolic balance of the body [28]. This study, by constructing cachexia cells and animal models and combining multi-omics analysis, indicated that ESP and its extract could alleviate a series of symptoms that occurred in the cachexia state. After ESP administration, the structural composition of the gut microbiota in mice approached normal, the levels of inflammatory factors in the body decreased, the loss of GA and eWAT reduced, and the cachexia symptoms of tumors were alleviated.

Muscle atrophy and fat loss are two characteristic indexes of cachexia[29,30]. In vivo and in vitro experiments showed that ESP and its extract could alleviate CT26-induced myotubes of C2C12 cell and GA tissue atrophy, and affected the signaling pathways related to lipolysis of 3T3-L1 cells and eWAT atrophy. In the cachexia in vitro model, when ESP and its extract were added to C2C12, the phosphorylation of AKT increased. The Spearman correlation analysis results of differential bacteria, tissue protein expression levels and serum biochemical indicators and inflammatory factors in mice simultaneously suggested that the increase in the abundance of Lachnospiraceae and Lactobacillaceae and the content of GLU were positively correlated with the upregulation of p-AKT and AKT expression. This indicated that AKT might be a key node for the alleviation of muscle atrophy by ESP. In the in vitro cachexia model, the expression of TRIM63 decreased with ESP. TRIM63 expression was correlated with AKT levels [31]. When the AKT pathway is inhibited, the FOXO transcription factor abnormally activates the ubiquitin-proteasome pathway, the muscle-specific E3 ubiquitin ligases FBXO32 and TRIM63 are up-regulated. Resulting in excessive activation of the proteasome system and degradation of muscle proteins [[32], [33], [34]]. Meanwhile, muscle atrophy is also related to the obstruction of protein synthesis [35,36]. Also, the down-regulation of TRIM63 mRNA transcriptional level in cachexia has been reported in the literature [37]. This consistency at both the transcriptional and protein levels jointly reinforce the hypothesis that TRIM63 plays a significant role in cachexia and suggests that its down-regulation may originate from transcriptional regulation. However, we observed that after ESP and its extract treatment, the trend of AKT phosphorylation and TRIM63 in GA tissues was not significant. This result indicated that this discovery requires further exploration. The correlation between AKT expression level and GLU content suggested that ESP affected energy metabolism, while alleviating GA atrophy occurs through other signaling pathways.

In the development of cachexia, fat loss precedes muscle atrophy and is faster [38], accompanied by activation of AMPK signaling pathway [39]. Cachexia significantly activated AMPK and induced fat degradation [40,41]. In our model, it could be observed that ESP and its extract could alleviate fat atrophy, and down-regulated the level of HSL signaling protein in the eWAT tissues of cachexia mice. The mechanism of action is similar to that of ginsenoside Rh2 in previous studies [42]. However, in 3T3-L1 cells, HSL and other related proteins only showed a downward trend without significant differences, indicated that the AMPK pathway might be one of the mechanisms of action, and this mechanism may involve the coordinated regulation of multiple pathways, which required further verification by us in the future. Meanwhile, the levels of inflammatory factors are also correlated with the metabolic degradation of adipose tissue. A decrease in the levels of inflammatory factors can indirectly weaken the attack of inflammatory pathways on adipose tissue [43]. The results of the ELISA experiment indicated that ESP could reduce the level of IL-6. In the Spearman correlation analysis, the level of LysoPCs metabolites was negatively correlated with IL-6. These findings could also serve as new ideas that need to be verified.

This study observed that ESP and its extract not only alleviate GA atrophy related to cachexia but also has the effect of inhibiting tumor growth. The shrinkage of the tumor itself undoubtedly helps improve the metabolic state of the host [44]. However, the analysis of individual animal data shows that there is no strong correlation between the improvement in GA mass and the final tumor volume. That is, individuals with smaller tumors do not always suffer the least muscle loss. And vice versa. This suggests that there are regulatory factors other than tumor burden. Therefore, we believe that the remission effect on cachexia is very likely to be a multimodal effect. On the one hand, cachexia is a secondary symptom of tumors. By inhibiting tumor growth, the production of factors causing cachexia is reduced from the source [45]. On the other hand, by regulating the levels of inflammatory factors and metabolic balance in the body, the process of muscle fat catabolism is blocked, thereby alleviating cachexia symptoms [46]. It should be pointed out that the tumor-bearing model adopted in this study has inherent limitations in fully distinguishing between these two mechanisms. Future research will utilize the construction of non-tumor-bearing cachexia models with sustained-release implantable tumor factors or conditional gene knockout models [47,48] to more directly verify the direct anti-cachexia effect of ESP and its extract.

Muscle tissue atrophy and adipose tissue reduction are closely related to host metabolic changes and gut microbiota disturbance [49,50]. After the administration of ESP and its extract in CT26 model mice, the symptoms of cachexia were significantly improved and GA and eWAT were well preserved. At the same time, the abundance of beneficial bacteria such as Lachnospiraceae and Lactobacillaceae in the gut of mice increased significantly, while the serum content of metabolites which related to lipid metabolism, energy metabolism increased, activating related metabolic pathways. A substantial body of evidence indicates that muscle function can be modulated by alterations in the gut microbiota and their resultant metabolic alterations [51]. Lahiri S et al. used microbial metabolites to reverse skeletal muscle atrophy in germ-free mice and demonstrated the existence of gut microbial-skeletal muscle axis [52]. In addition, a clinical study in post-transplant patients with haematological malignancies also demonstrated that gut microbiome appeared to improve skeletal muscle via the gut-muscle axis [53]. Metabolic pathways such as lipid metabolism and energy metabolism associated with adipose tissue are also altered during the development and course of cachexia, causing depletion of lipids in adipocytes [40]. A metabolomics study of the CT26 model of cachexia in mice with colon cancer showed significant alterations in glucose and lipid metabolism. The same team showed through serum metabolomics studies that cancer cachexia (CT26 model) has specific effects compared to starvation [41]. After correlation analysis, it was found that the change trend of GA and eWAT mass, different bacterial abundance and different serum metabolites content were correlated.

In conclusion, as shown in Fig. 8, this study showed that ESP and its extract reduced the levels of inflammatory factors, decreased GA and eWAT loss, and alleviated cachexia symptoms of tumors. At the same time, it makes the structure and composition of the intestinal microbiome of mice tend to be normal, affecting lipid metabolism, protein metabolism, glucose metabolism and energy pathways in the body. The overall therapeutic effect is superior to that of the MPA-treatment group. However, this research still has certain limitations, which is also an important direction for our future work. Based on the existing data, we speculate that ESP and its extract have a certain alleviating effect on tumor cachexia. However, this speculation awaits direct verification in future work through experiments such as the establishment of non-tumor-bearing cachexia models and gene knockout. Secondly, the correlation between cachexia symptoms and intestinal flora as well as metabolic pathways needs to be further verified through fecal microbiota transplantation experiments or single metabolite intervention experiments. Finally, in GA and eWAT, whether there is a direct correlation between the changes in the levels of related proteins and the alterations in their mass still requires more in-depth and detailed experiments such as overexpression of active proteins to further determine the mechanism chain.

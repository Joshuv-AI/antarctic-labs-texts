Computational intelligence analysis on drug solubility using thermodynamics and interaction mechanism via models comparison and validation
Authors: Ahmad J Obaidullah, Wael A Mahdi
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the application of various regression models for predicting drug solubility in polymer and API-polymer interactions in complex datasets. Four models—Gaussian Process Regression (GPR), Support Vector Regression (SVR), Bayesian Ridge Regression (BRR), and Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR)—are evaluated. Preprocessing the dataset using the Z-score approach helped to detect outliers, further improving the accuracy and dependability of the analysis. Also, Fireworks Algorithm (FWA) is employed for hyper-parameter tuning in this work. The GPR model demonstrated superior performance, achieving the lowest MSE and MAE for both drug solubility and gamma predictions, with R2 scores of 0.9980 and 0.9950 for training and test data, respectively. The results of this study show the robustness of GPR in generating reliable and precise forecasts, thus providing a strong method for intricate regression tasks in pharmaceutical and other scientific fields. In addition, the Fireworks Algorithm (FWA) is presented as an optimization method, demonstrating its potential in improving the model’s predictive abilities by effectively exploring and exploiting the search space. The results emphasize the significance of choosing suitable regression models and optimization techniques to attain dependable and superior predictive analytics.Keywords: Drug design, Drug solubility, Gaussian process regression, Hyper-parameter optimization, Fireworks algorithmSubject terms: Chemistry, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Description of drug behavior in solid dosage oral formulations can be conducted via analysis of phase behavior and molecular interactions in the formulation. As such, the interaction of API (Active Pharmaceutical Ingredient) with excipient and other constituents determines the properties of formulation such as release, dissolution rate, stability, flowability, etc. Several works have been done for description of API phase behavior via thermodynamic approach where the methods seek to find activity coefficient and solubility of drug in polymer as well1,2. Indeed, the main approach for analysis of drug-polymer interaction is thermodynamics which is challenging to be implemented for a wide range of medicines and polymers due to the models’ complexity. Thus, alternative models need to be explored for analysis of API-polymer interactions in design of drug formulations. The method should be capable of dealing with multiple parameters and generating the desired outputs.

Determination of solubility in formulation versus temperature and formulation properties would be of fundamental importance owing to its importance in design of advanced drug delivery systems to increase the drug’s efficacy. By understanding drug behavior in the formulation, the properties can be greatly estimated which are required for design of formulation with controlled release behavior3.

Some recent works have shown application of machine learning models in drug solubility prediction4–6as well as interaction in solid dosage formulations7. The method of machine learning (ML) can be explored for determination of medicine solubility in formulation, for example in polymer. This task needs to collect a holistic dataset containing various formulation parameters related to polymer and drug. Therefore, there is a need for developing ML-based models in this area to assess the API-polymer interactions.

Within data, ML techniques can identify and examine connections, trends, and patterns. This makes machine learning especially valuable for tasks like regression, classification, clustering, and anomaly detection8,9. Bayesian Ridge Regression (BRR) merges Bayesian principles with ridge regression, providing a strong structure for parameter estimation that alleviates overfitting. Support Vector Regression (SVR) adopts a kernel-driven technique to effectively manage high-dimensional data; Kernel Ridge Regression (KRR) integrates ridge regression with the kernel trick to enhance model adaptability; Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) represents a non-linear, non-parametric Bayesian strategy recognized for its capability to furnish uncertainty assessments alongside predictions. Utilizing the Z-score method for dataset preprocessing to eliminate outliers holds significance in this study, as it enhances dependability. Additionally, the Fireworks Algorithm (FWA) is employed for hyperparameter optimization, a crucial step in maximizing model efficacy. The FWA, influenced by the way fireworks explode, is a powerful optimization technique that balances exploration and exploitation in search space.

This work aims to assess the predictive efficacy of these regression models, for the first time, in relation to drug solubility and activity coefficient, through a comparison of their accuracy and robustness. This work explores the application of several regression models—BRR, GPR, SVR, and KRR—to predict drug solubility and activity coefficient from complex datasets. The inputs to the models are a wide range of parameters related to polymer and drug including physio-chemical parameters along with molecular descriptor and thermodynamic properties. The models generate two outputs including API solubility in polymer and activity coefficient of drug in the formulation. In the subsequent sections, we will provide a description of the dataset, and the methodologies used, present the results of our analysis, and discuss the implications of our findings in relation to predictive modeling for drug properties.

Dataset and visualization

The dataset consists of 24 input features, each representing a unique molecular descriptor that is relevant to predicting drug solubility in polymer and gamma values. It encompasses more than 12,000 data points. Dataset consisted of inputs which are the parameters related to API and formulation as well as molecular descriptor which are obtained from quantum chemical calculations. More details regarding the data and input features can be found in7. Outputs of the dataset/model are denoted as solubility of drug as well as activity coefficient of drug (Gamma). It should be pointed out that the activity coefficients for the dataset have been calculated from PC-SAFT thermodynamic equation7.

Support vector regression (SVR)

This approach seeks to optimize the discrepancy between observed and predicted values by approximating the optimal value within a specified threshold. The threshold refers to the distance separating the hyperplane from its boundary lines. SVR is a robust kernel-based regularization algorithm that improves error tolerance by allowing for a certain margin of error and adjustable error tolerance. It reduces empirical risk as well as confidence interval, regardless of input space size, while not increasing computational complexity. SVR performs well, especially with small sample sizes in high-dimensional feature spaces, offering excellent generalizability and predictive accuracy. SVR provides flexibility in defining acceptable error margins and finds a suitable hyperplane that fits the data. This process allows for the computation of a hyperplane that encapsulates the maximum number of points and establishes decision boundaries with satisfactory error margins.

Fireworks algorithm (FWA)

The method of FWA is a population-based optimization technique taken from the dynamics of fireworks displays. FWA mimics the explosive and attractive characteristics of fireworks to search for and determine optimal solutions within a specified search space26,27.

A population of fireworks indicates candidate solutions to the optimization task, and FWA functions by controlling this population. The process starts with random generation of first fireworks, each marked by their location and a fitness measure evaluating the quality of the solution they reflect. Explosion mechanism in the Fireworks Algorithm emulates the bursting effect of fireworks in the sky. This entails the stochastic creation of novel particles, termed sparks, in proximity to every firework. Superior sparks are introduced to replace the original fireworks, resulting in an overall improvement of the population. The fitness of candidate solutions in the FWA is evaluated by utilizing the mean R2 value from a 3-fold cross-validation (CV) of the model trained on the candidate solution. This approach ensures that the evaluation of the solution is both robust and generalized. In each fold of the cross-validation, the data is divided into 5 equal parts, with four parts employed to train the algorithm and one part for validating. This process is repeated five times, and the mean R2 score is computed over all the iterations, representing the solution’s effectiveness in fitting the data.

This R2 score quantifies the proportion of variance explained by the model, with values closer to 1 indicating a higher fitness. Thus, candidate solutions that yield higher mean R2 scores across the five folds are deemed to have higher fitness and are more likely to contribute to better optimization results. Results and discussion

All visualizations and analyses were conducted using Python, employing established libraries such as scikit-learn for machine learning, NumPy for numerical computations, Matplotlib for visualization, and Bejoor for tasks related to model optimization. These tools enabled the effective execution and analysis of results across multiple regression models.

In this study, the dataset was divided into training and test sets using a stratified sampling approach to ensure balanced representation of key features such as drug solubility and activity coefficient (Gamma). Approximately 80% of the data was used for training the models, while the remaining 20% was set aside for testing. Stratification was applied to maintain consistency in the distribution of the target variables across both subsets. Additionally, 3-fold cross-validation was employed during training to further validate model robustness and minimize overfitting.

Drug solubility

Based on the data presented in Tables 1 and 2, it is apparent that the GPR model achieves better results than other models in terms of R2 score. The GPR model achieves a high R2 score of 0.9842 on the training dataset and 0.9829 on the testing dataset, indicating its exceptional ability to accurately predict drug solubility. The model also has the lowest MSE (2.0110E-04) and MAE (7.74564E-03), demonstrating high accuracy. The SVR model is also highly effective with R2 scores of 0.9659 (train) and 0.9446 (test), followed by the KRR and BRR models. Figures 3 and 4 compare the model predicted values and the actual values in the dataset (for both training and testing). These figures also show the superiority of the GPR model. The accuracy of results obtained in this study are greater than the previous study for correlation of drug solubility in polymer via deep neural network (DNN) which showed the best accuracy with R2value of 0.92 for the experimental dataset (Train set)7.

Conclusion

The investigation analyzed the effectiveness of four regression models—BRR, SVR, KRR, and GPR—in predicting drug solubility in polymers and activity values using a dataset comprising 24 inputs with over 12,000 data rows. The model performance was improved through the use of the Fireworks Algorithm (FWA) for hyper-parameter optimization. The results suggest that the GPR model consistently performs better than the other models in terms of all metrics, showing superior predictive accuracy with R2 scores of 0.9842 (train) and 0.9829 (test) for drug solubility, and 0.9980 (train) and 0.9950 (test) for gamma. GPR also attained the lowest MSE and MAE values, signifying a high level of precision and minimal error in its predictions. The SVR model demonstrated the second highest level of performance, exhibiting robust results. It was followed by the KRR and BRR models, which displayed moderate levels of performance. The results highlight the efficacy of the GPR model in capturing intricate connections within the dataset, rendering it the most appropriate model for predicting drug solubility and gamma. The efficacy of the Fireworks Algorithm in optimizing hyper-parameters further shows its potential in augmenting model performance. Future work could explore integrating other advanced optimization techniques or hybrid models to potentially further improve predictive accuracy. Additionally, applying these models and optimization techniques to other domains may reveal broader applicability and benefits.

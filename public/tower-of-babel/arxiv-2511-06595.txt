Global existence for the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system in a two-dimensional bounded domain
Yanmin Mu, Dehua Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove the global existence of solutions to the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system for general initial data in convex bounded domains of two space dimensions, assuming the specular reflection boundary conditions for the distribution density. The boundary conditions for the electric potential are considered in two cases: Neumann boundary conditions and homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions. The core ideas involve constructing suitable velocity lemmas and applying geometric techniques. In the two-dimensional case, it is crucial to select the arc length as the parameter of the curve and to further combine this with the Frenet-Serret formulas, enabling us to effectively describe the distribution density equation near the boundary and thus establishing a vital connection in the geometric representation.

1.
Introduction

The relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system describes the collective dynamics of a collisionless plasma, where particles travel at nearly the speed of light and interact through their self-generated electric fields. The system consists of a relativistic Vlasov equation for the particle distribution function coupled with the Poisson equation for the electric potential.
In this paper, we are concerned with the global solution to the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system (RVP) with general initial data in a convex, bounded domain
\Omega
of two space dimensions:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=\rho,

where
x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,

;
f=f(t,x,v)\geq 0

v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
;

\hat{v}=\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}},

\varphi(t,x)
is the electric potential,
\rho
is the charge density given by

\rho=\rho(t,x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f\,{\rm d}v,

and the domain
\Omega
is a convex bounded domain with

\mathbb{R}^{2}

1.1.
A more convenient coordinate system near
\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}

Assuming that
\partial\Omega
is a smooth curve in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and its parametric equation can be expressed as
r(l)=(x_{1}(l),x_{2}(l))
, where
l
represents the arc length parameter.
At the point
r(l)
, we shall denote the outer normal to
\partial\Omega
by
n(l)
.

The implicit function theorem shows that for
\delta>0
sufficiently small we can
parameterize uniquely the set of points
x\in\partial\Omega+B_{\delta}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
by the unique
values
(l,x_{\bot})
satisfying the equation,

(1.13)

\displaystyle x=r(l)-x_{\bot}n(l).

Set

U(l)=\frac{dr(l)}{dl},\quad W(l)=\frac{dU(l)}{dl};\quad\text{then}\,\;n(l)=-\frac{W(l)}{|W(l)|}.

By the Frenet-Serret formulas, we can obtain a set of unit orthogonal local coordinate frames
\big(U(l),n(l)\big)
, and then represent any vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
as,

(1.14)

\displaystyle v=v_{\|}(l)-v_{\bot}n(l),

where
v_{\|}(l)=\omega U(l)\in T_{r(l)}(\partial\Omega),v_{\bot}\in\mathbb{R}.

For the set of points in the phase space
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
that are
close to the boundary
\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we can denote
f(t,x,v)=f(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})
by the system of coordinate
(l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})

and the equation of
f(t,x,v)
will satisfy the following new form,

(1.15)

\displaystyle f_{t}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial l}+\frac{v_{\bot}}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{\bot}}+\sigma\frac{\partial f}{\partial\omega}+F\frac{\partial f}{\partial v_{\bot}}=0,

where
k>0
is the curvature of boundary curves and

(1.16)

\displaystyle E=\nabla\varphi=E_{l}U(l)-E_{\bot}n(l),\quad E_{\bot}=-h,

(1.17)

\displaystyle\sigma=E_{l}+\frac{v_{\bot}}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{k\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}},\quad F=E_{\bot}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{k\omega^{2}}{1-kx_{\bot}}.

Remark 1.1
.

The proof of (
1.15
) is a standard change of variables using the classical Frenet-Serret formulas; we will not provide details here.

Remark 1.2
.

Note
1-kx_{\bot}>0
for sufficiently small
x_{\bot}
. Since the domain
\Omega
is convex, and due to
h>0
we have

F<0
.

1.4.
Difficulties and strategy of the proofs

To prove the main results, we first apply the velocity lemma to establish the well-posedness of linearized problems. Then, we construct an iterative scheme and show the convergence of the iterative sequences. The main issues to address are the uniform boundedness in a given function space and the prolongation of uniform estimates for the functions
{f^{n}}
. Finally, we use bootstrapping techniques to reach the desired conclusions.

Now, we will discuss the major challenges. To this end, we first review the fundamental difficulties and core ideas of the initial boundary value problem from the perspective of the classic Vlasov-Poisson system.
In
[
17
]
, the global existence was proved for the case of a half-space
\mathbb{R}^{3}_{+}
, assuming that the function
f_{0}
remains constant near the singular set. This assumption avoids the evolution of characteristic curves that are close to the singular set.
In regions far away from the singular set, the number of collisions within a finite time interval can be bounded uniformly by using the velocity lemma method described in

[
28
]
. This allows for a clear description of how the characteristic curves evolve.
In
[
23
]
, Hwang and Velázquez considered the Vlasov-Poisson system in a general bounded convex domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
, they addressed the increasing complexity of the evolution of characteristic curves near the boundary, making it challenging to provide an accurate mathematical description. In order to establish global existence, the authors initially employed geometric methods, as outlined in
[
23
]
. Their results indicate that the geometric properties of the domain have a more significant influence on the characteristic curves than their dynamics.

However, understanding the evolution of the characteristic curves associated with the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) becomes extremely difficult near the singular set.
First, we face the complexities that arise within the Vlasov-Poisson system, including the challenges related to the behavior of characteristic curves near the singular set and the influence of regional boundaries and other factors. In this study, we will draw on insights from previous research on the Vlasov-Poisson system. Specifically, we assume that the initial data
f_{0}
remains constant near the singular set. Additionally, to analyze the characteristic curves close to the boundary, we will use geometric methods. This approach is especially relevant for general bounded regions. The core idea of constructing suitable velocity lemmas and applying geometric techniques to prove the existence of global solutions for the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system in convex bounded domains of two spatial dimensions remains valid. This applies to general initial data as well.

However, unlike the Vlasov-Poisson system, additional challenges arise with the relativistic Vlasov-Poisson system.
In applying geometric methods to boundary issues, arc length plays a key role as a curve’s characteristic in two dimensions. By using arc length as a parameter and considering the Frenet-Serret formulas, we can effectively describe the distribution density equation near the boundary
\partial\Omega
, thus establishing a vital connection in the geometric representation.

Moreover, identifying new coordinate variables is essential for developing appropriate velocity lemmas. These lemmas help describe scenarios where particles disperse from singular sets and face different numbers of collision barriers. In the context of the Newman boundary condition, we select coordinate variables
(\alpha,\beta)
that satisfy the constraints previously mentioned,

\displaystyle\alpha(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})=\frac{v_{\bot}^{2}}{2}-L(t,l,0,\omega,v_{\bot})x_{\bot},

\displaystyle\beta(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})=2\pi F(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})+\pi(1-\frac{v_{\bot}}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}),

where

\displaystyle L(t,l,0,w,x_{\bot})=\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}},\quad F(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})=-\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}h(x)-k\omega^{2}.

This variable
\alpha
characterizes the distance from points on the characteristic curve to the singular set. From this, the specific significance of selecting the tangential and normal directions of the regional boundary in local coordinates can be identified. The other variable
\beta
, by contrast, describes the number of collisions between particles and the boundary. It is observed that the number of collisions is inversely proportional to the distance to the singular set.
To ensure the regularity of the characteristic curve, the number of collisions must be bounded above uniformly within a certain time interval; it is for this purpose that assumption (
1.20
) is proposed for the initial value.

For Dirichlet boundary conditions, deriving the velocity lemma is relatively more difficult.
Additionally, choosing coordinate variables
\alpha
becomes more complex.

\alpha=\frac{v_{\bot}^{2}}{2}-\varphi(t,x)-L(t,l,0,\omega,v_{\bot})x_{\bot}.

It requires estimating the first derivatives of the electric potential
\varphi
. This difference in handling the first derivative of
\varphi
is another notable difference between the two boundary types. The elliptical nature of the electric potential
\varphi
in general domains, combined with the lack of a precise formula for
\varphi
, makes obtaining accurate estimates challenging. The core idea involves locally flattening the boundary and incorporating it with the Green’s function in a half-space
\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}
. Additionally, using revised boundary estimates for the equation of
\varphi
and constructing suitable supersolutions are important strategies.

Lastly, we found that the effect of regional boundaries is similar to the behavior of characteristic curves, which differs from the Vlasov-Poisson system. It is important to note that this study mainly focuses on a two-dimensional situation.

2.
Iterative Procedure

We describe the iterative procedure in this section. The iterative sequence
(f^{n},\varphi^{n})
satisfies the following system:

(2.1)

\displaystyle f^{0}(t,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\;t\geq 0,\;x\in\Omega,\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(2.2)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f^{n}+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}+\nabla_{x}\varphi^{n-1}\cdot\nabla_{v}f^{n}=0,\;x\in\Omega,\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;t>0,

(2.3)

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi^{n-1}=\rho^{n-1}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{n-1}{\rm d}v,\;x\in\Omega,\;t>0,

(2.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi^{n-1}}{\partial n_{x}}=h(x),\;x\in\partial\Omega,\;t>0,

(2.5)

\displaystyle f^{n}(0,x,v)=f_{0}(x,v),\;x\in\Omega,\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;t>0,

(2.6)

\displaystyle f^{n}(t,x,v)=f^{n}(t,x,v^{*}),\;x\in\Omega,\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;t>0,

where
n=1,2,....
, and
f_{0},h
satisfy (
1.7
),(
1.10
) and (
1.20
).
In the rest of this paper, we use the following notation,

(2.7)

\displaystyle E^{n}=\nabla\varphi^{n}.

The basic method for proving the global existence of solutions to RVP involves three key steps.
First, one must construct an iterative sequence of approximate solutions denoted as
(f^{n},\varphi^{n})
.
Second, it is necessary to prove the convergence of this sequence
(f^{n},\varphi^{n})
as
n
approaches infinity.
Finally, one must show that the limit of the sequence indeed corresponds to solutions for the RVP.

3.
Linear Problem

In order to show the existence of the iterative sequence
f^{n}
,
the well-posedness of the following linear problem must be established.

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}f+\hat{v}\cdot\nabla_{x}f+E\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\quad x\in\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2},v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},t>0,

(3.2)

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=f(t,x,v^{*}),\quad x\in\partial\Omega,\;v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;t>0,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n_{x}}=h(x),\,\,\,x\in\partial\Omega,\,t>0,

Therefore, we start by assuming that the given vector field
E=\nabla\varphi

satisfies suitable smoothness conditions.

The fundamental approach to solving the linear equation of the function

is to take advantage of the method of characteristics.
To be more precise, the ordinary differential equations of the characteristic curve
(X(s;t,x,v),V(s;t,x,v))

where

If

(3.4)

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{ds}=\hat{V},

(3.5)

\displaystyle\frac{dV}{ds}=E=\nabla_{x}\varphi,

(3.6)

\displaystyle X(t;t,x,v)=x,\,\,V(t;t,x,v)=v.

If
X(s^{*};t,x,v)\in\partial\Omega
at the time
s=s^{*}
, with the help of velocity

\displaystyle V((s^{*})^{+};t,x,v)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s>s^{*},s\rightarrow s^{*}}V(s;t,x,v)

(3.7)

\displaystyle=\Big(V\big((s^{*})^{-};t,x,v\big)\Big)^{*}=\Big(\lim_{s<s^{*},s\rightarrow s^{*}}V(s;t,x,v)\Big)^{*},

where
(\cdot)^{*}

1.8

3.1.
Velocity lemma

To establish the well-posedness of linear problems, a thorough analysis of the characteristic curves near the singular set is crucial, especially when considering collisions with boundaries. This analysis requires applying the velocity lemma discussed in this section. In
[
28
]
, the authors first introduced the velocity lemma to solve the Cauchy problem for VP system, while in
[
23
]
the corresponding velocity lemma was established in a bounded domain.
The velocity lemma states that the number of boundary collisions is finite if the characteristic curve is sufficiently distant from the singular set. This ensures the regularity of the characteristic curve and is an important prerequisite for deriving classical solutions for the RVP system.

Lemma 3.2
.

For a given constant
\delta>0
, let
\Gamma_{\delta}=([\partial\Omega+B_{\delta}(0)]\cap\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Suppose that the regularity assumptions of
E
in Theorem
3.1
hold. Then
the existence of solutions to the characteristic equations (
3.4
)-(
3.6
) can be obtained in
[0,T]

for any
(x,v)\in\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Furthermore, the following estimate applies for any
(x,v)\in\Gamma_{\delta}
,

(3.14)

\displaystyle C_{1}\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(0)+x_{\bot}(0)\Big)\leq\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(t)+x_{\bot}(t)\Big)\leq C_{2}\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(0)+x_{\bot}(0)\Big),\;t\in[0,T],

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on
T,f_{0},\|E\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))}
.

Proof.

On the one hand, due to
L(t,0)\leq-\epsilon_{0}
in Lemma
3.1
and (
3.10
), we have

\alpha(t)\geq C(v^{2}_{\bot}+x_{\bot})

for some constant
C
.
On the other hand, by the boundedness of velocity
|v|
(see Section 6) as well as the continuity of function
h(x)
, it follows that

\alpha(t)\leq C(T)(v^{2}_{\bot}+x_{\bot}).

Therefore we say that these two quantities
\alpha(t),v^{2}_{\bot}+x_{\bot}
are equivalent.
Because of the boundedness of the domain, we only need to deal with the case in which
v^{2}_{\bot}+x_{\bot}
is small, that is, the point
(x,v)
is near the singular set.
Along the characteristic curves,
\alpha(t)
satisfies the following equation by (
3.13
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dt}=

\displaystyle v_{\bot}\Big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\Big)+\frac{v_{\bot}x_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}F(t,x_{\bot})

\displaystyle-x_{\bot}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}\Big)

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle M_{1}+M_{2}+M_{3}.

Next, we calculate
M_{i}
separately as,

\displaystyle|M_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq|v_{\bot}|\cdot\big|E_{\bot}(t,l,x_{\bot})-E_{\bot}(t,l,0)\big|+|v_{\bot}|\cdot\left|\frac{k\omega^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{kx_{\bot}}{1-kx_{\bot}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|E\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\big)}\|v_{\bot}\|x^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\bot}+C(T)x_{\bot},

\displaystyle|M_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\|F\|_{L^{\infty}}x_{\bot}

\displaystyle\leq\|h\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|E\|_{L^{\infty}}+C(T)\big)x_{\bot}\leq C(T)x_{\bot}.

By the definition
L(t,0)
, we have

\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}=-\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\nabla h(x)\cdot U(l)-\frac{dk}{dl}\omega^{2},\qquad\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}=-\frac{\omega}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}h(x)-2k\omega.

Furthermore,

\displaystyle|M_{3}|

\displaystyle\leq|\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}|x_{\bot}+|\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}(t,0)|x_{\bot}

\displaystyle\leq C(T)x_{\bot}.

Therefore, we conclude the following estimate

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\Big|\leq C\|E\|_{L^{\infty}\big([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega)\big)}\|v_{\bot}\|x^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\bot}+C(T)x_{\bot},

where
C
depends only on
h
and the geometric properties of
\partial\Omega
. Meanwhile,
\alpha
and
v^{2}_{\bot}+x_{\bot}
are equivalent, so we have

(3.15)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d\alpha}{dt}\Big|\leq C\alpha.

Thus we obtain

\displaystyle C_{1}\alpha(0)\leq\alpha(t)\leq C_{2}\alpha(0),

where
C_{1},C_{2}
depend on

T,f_{0},\|E\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))}
, that is, (
3.14
) holds.
∎

3.2.
Well-posedness of the linear problem

The primary focus of this subsection is to prove Theorem
3.1
. The fundamental approach involves integrating the linear equation along the characteristics. Nonetheless, it is essential to pay close attention to the regularity of characteristic curves in conjunction with reflection boundary conditions since characteristic curves frequently collide with boundaries
\partial\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

According to the characteristic equation (
3.4
)-(
3.6
), we can define the following function,

(3.16)

\displaystyle f(t,x,v)=f_{0}(X(0;t,x,v),V(0;t,x,v)).

We will show that this function is the solution to the linear problem satisfying Theorem
3.1
.

Step 1.
The characteristic curves determined by the equations(
3.4
)-(
3.6
) for
(x,v)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
have two basic facts.

(1) The curves never intersect with the singular set.

In fact, for the curves
(X(s;t,x,v),V(s;t,x,v)),0\leq s\leq t
with
(X(t;t,x,v)=x,V(t;t,x,v)

=v)\in\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, one has

\alpha(t)>0
. By Lemma
3.2
,
\alpha(s)
maintains upper and lower bounds during the time period
[0,t]
, then it follows that
\alpha(s)>0
for any
s\in[0,t]
.
If the curves intersect with the singular set at some time

, we have
\alpha(s_{0})=0
, which contradicts with
\alpha(s_{0})>0
.

(2) The curves intersect the boundary
\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
at most a finite number of times.

Case 1.
If the characteristics starts in the region
\alpha(0)\leq C\delta_{0}
, by Lemma
3.2
,
\alpha(s)\leq C_{1}(T)\delta_{0},\forall s\in[0,T]
for some
C_{1}(T)
, so because
f_{0}
is constant, we have
f=\text{constant}
.

Case 2.
If the characteristics starts in the region
\alpha(0)\geq C\delta_{0}
, by Lemma
3.2
,
\alpha(s)\geq C_{2}(T)\delta_{0},\forall s\in[0,T]
for some
C_{2}(T)
, we claim that
\Big|\frac{d\beta}{dt}\Big|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}
, and further that the upper bound of the number of collisions does not exceed
\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}
.
In fact, by (
3.13
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\beta}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}F(t,0)+\pi\frac{2x_{\bot}L(t,0)}{(2\alpha)^{3/2}}\big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\big)-\pi\frac{x_{\bot}v_{\bot}}{(2\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big(\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+F(t,x_{\bot})\frac{v_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\Big).

By direct calculation, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}F(t,0)\Big|\leq\frac{C(T)}{\sqrt{2\alpha}},

\displaystyle\Big|\pi\frac{2x_{\bot}L(t,0)}{(2\alpha)^{3/2}}\big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\big)\Big|\leq\frac{C(T)}{\sqrt{2\alpha}},

\displaystyle\Big|\pi\frac{x_{\bot}v_{\bot}}{(2\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}+F(t,x_{\bot})\frac{v_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\Big)\Big|\leq C(T).

Therefore, we have
\Big|\frac{d\beta}{dt}\Big|\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}.

Let the back-time cycle from
(t,x,v)
be

(t^{l},x^{l},v^{l})=(t,x,v),(t^{l-1},x^{l-1},v^{l-1}),...,(t^{1},x^{1},v^{1}),(0,x_{0},v_{0}).

By (
1.15
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{dx_{\bot}(s)}{ds}=\frac{v_{\bot}(s)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}},

\displaystyle\frac{dv_{\bot}(s)}{ds}=F(s),

and moreover,

\displaystyle v_{\bot}(s)

\displaystyle=v^{*}_{\bot}(t^{j})+\int^{s}_{t^{j}}F(\tau){\rm d}\tau,\,\,t^{j-1}<s<t^{j},

\displaystyle x_{\bot}(t^{j})

\displaystyle=x_{\bot}(t^{j-1})+\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\frac{v_{\bot}(s)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}{\rm d}s

\displaystyle=x_{\bot}(t^{j-1})+\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\frac{v^{*}_{\bot}(t^{j})}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}{\rm d}s+\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\int^{s}_{t^{j}}\frac{F(\tau)}{\sqrt{1+|v(s)|^{2}}}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}s.

Since

, we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\frac{v^{*}_{\bot}(t^{j})}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}{\rm d}s\Big|=\Big|\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\int^{s}_{t^{j}}\frac{F(\tau)}{\sqrt{1+|v(s)|^{2}}}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}s\Big|,

\displaystyle\Big|\int^{t^{j}}_{t^{j-1}}\int^{s}_{t^{j}}\frac{F(\tau)}{\sqrt{1+|v(s)|^{2}}}{\rm d}\tau{\rm d}s\Big|\leq C(T)\big(t^{j}-t^{j-1}\big)^{2},

, it follows that

, then we get

, which implies that the number of collisions in the time period [0, T] is finite.

, we have

\displaystyle\beta(t^{j})-\beta(t^{j-1})\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}(t^{j}-t^{j-1}),

\displaystyle H(t^{j}_{+})=H(t^{j-1}_{+})+1.

As a result, we sum the above equalities over

\displaystyle 2\pi l\leq\sum^{l}_{j=1}\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}(t^{j}-t^{j-1})\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{\delta_{0}}}T.

We now analyze the regularity of the characteristic curves.

If the characteristic curves do not collide with the boundary, by

.

\displaystyle V(s^{+};t,x,v)=v+\int^{s}_{t}E(\tau,X(\tau;t,x,v)){\rm d}\tau,

\displaystyle V(s^{-};t,x,v)=V(s^{+};t,x,v)-2\big(V(s^{+};t,x,v)\cdot n\big)n.

, it follows that

and

are also

.
Thus, for the characteristic curves of finite bounces, the functions

and

consist of a finite number of piecewise

Hence, combined with (

.
Finally, let’s briefly summarize the above results as

(1)

.

(2)

If

.

(3)

We therefore prove that

Uniqueness.

According to the theory of ODEs,
we know that the solutions of the characteristic equations

For the solution
f(t,x,v)\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
of the linear problem, we need to proceed further to estimate

E=\nabla\varphi,\,\rho(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(t,x,v){\rm d}v,\,F(t,x)
and so on. Let’s start with some notations.
For a function
g:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, we define the seminorm
[\cdot]_{0,\lambda;x}
as

[g]_{0,\lambda;x}\equiv\sup_{x,y\in\Omega}\frac{|g(x)-g(y)|}{|x-y|^{\lambda}}.

For the function
f(t,x,v)
, the support of
|v|
in the time period
[0,t]
is defined as

(4.7)

\displaystyle Q(t)=\sup\{|v|:\,(x,v)\in\text{supp }f(s),\;0\leq s\leq t\}.

We have the following proposition.

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
\varphi
satisfies (
1.2
),(
1.9
) and
f(t,x,v)\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
,
E
satisfies the regularity hypothesis in Theorem
3.1
. Then we have the following estimates,

(4.8)

\displaystyle|\rho(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|\nabla_{x}f(t,x,v)|{\rm d}v

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

(4.10)

\displaystyle|\rho_{t}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

(4.11)

\displaystyle|F(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(T),\quad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\partial\Omega,

(4.12)

\displaystyle|E(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\Big(\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)}+1\Big),\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

\displaystyle|\nabla E(t,x)|

\displaystyle+[\nabla E(t,\cdot)]_{0,\lambda;x}+|\nabla^{2}E(t,x)|+[\nabla^{2}E(t,\cdot)]_{0,\lambda;x}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\Big(\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)}+1\Big),\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

(4.14)

\displaystyle|E_{t}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},\,\,(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

Proof.

Firstly, we give the estimates about
\rho(t,x)
as follows:

\displaystyle|\rho(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f(t,x,v){\rm d}v\Big|\leq\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}Q^{2}(T)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},

\displaystyle|\nabla\rho(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\nabla f(t,x,v){\rm d}v\Big|\leq\|\nabla f\|_{L^{\infty}}Q^{2}(T)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},

\displaystyle|\rho_{t}(t,x)|

\displaystyle=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f_{t}(t,x,v){\rm d}v\Big|\leq\|f_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}Q^{2}(T)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)},

thus, (
4.8
)-(
4.10
) hold.

Sencondly, since
x\in\partial\Omega
,  i.e.
x_{\bot}=0
, then

\displaystyle|F(t,x)|=|-h(x)-\frac{k\omega^{2}}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}|\leq C(T),

so, (
4.11
) is true.

Finally, we deal with
E(t,x)
. Due to (
4.6
), and
E=\nabla\varphi
,

\displaystyle|E(t,x)|+|\nabla E(t,x)|+[\nabla E(t,\cdot)]_{0,\lambda;x}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\rho\|_{C^{0,\lambda}}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\lambda}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\Big(\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)}+1\Big).

Since
f(t,x,v)\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
and
E=\nabla\varphi
, it follows that

\nabla\rho\in C^{0,\lambda}(\Omega)
and
E
satisfies (
1.2
)-(
1.9
). Once again applying (
4.6
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\nabla^{2}E(t,x)|+[\nabla^{2}E(t,\cdot)]_{0,\lambda;x}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\nabla\rho\|_{C^{0,\lambda}}+\|\nabla h\|_{C^{1,\lambda}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\Big(\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)}+1\Big).

According to the assumptions on
\varphi
, the function
\varphi_{t}
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi_{t}=\rho_{t},\,\,x\in\Omega,t>0,

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\varphi_{t}}{\partial n_{x}}=0,\,\,x\in\partial\Omega,t>0,

and by (
4.6
),

|E_{t}(t,x)|\leq\|\rho_{t}\|_{C^{0,\lambda}}\leq C(T)\|f\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)}.

Then the inequalities (
4.12
)-(
4.14
) follow.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
0<\lambda<\mu
,
suppose that
f_{0}\in C^{1,\mu}_{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}),f_{0}\geq 0
satisfies (
1.20
) and
h\in C^{1,\mu}(\partial\Omega),\,\,h(x)>0
.
Then the iterative sequence
f^{n}
is globally defined for each
x\in\Omega,\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,0\leq t<\infty
.
Moreover, the function
f^{n}(t,x,v)\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
for
\forall\,T>0
satisfies

(4.15)

\displaystyle\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(4.16)

\displaystyle\int\rho^{n}(t,x){\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int f_{0}(x,v){\rm d}x{\rm d}v.

Proof.

We use induction to prove this proposition.

Step 1.
If
n=1
, we estimate the support of the function
f^{1}
.
According to (
4.12
),we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{dV}{ds}\Big|=|E^{0}|=|\nabla\varphi^{0}|\leq C(T)\Big(\|f_{0}\|_{C^{1,\mu}}+1\Big)\leq C(T).

Thus,

\displaystyle|V(s)|\leq C(T)(1+s),\,\,i.e.\,|v|\leq C(T)(1+t).

Then we have the following estimate on
\rho^{1}(t,x)
,

\displaystyle|\rho^{1}(t,x)|=\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{1}(t,x,v){\rm d}v\Big|\leq\|f^{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}C(T)(1+t)^{2}\leq C(T)(1+t)^{2}.

In the light of
f_{0}\in C^{1,\mu}_{0}(\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
as well as Proposition
4.3

which imply
E^{0}\in C^{1;1,\mu}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega})
, using Therorem
3.1
, we obtain
f^{1}\in C^{1;1,\mu}_{t,(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
and

\int\rho^{1}(t,x){\rm d}x=\int f_{0}(x,v){\rm d}x{\rm d}v.

Step 2.
If
n=2
,
by means of Proposition
4.3
and the boundedness of the domain
\Omega
, it follows that

\|E^{1}\|_{C^{1;1,\mu}_{t,x}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega})}\leq C(T)\Big(\|f^{1}\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t,(x,v)}}+1\Big).

Applying Theorem
3.1
, we can obtain
f^{2}
is well defined in
C^{1,\lambda}_{x,v}
for
t\in[0,+\infty).

Furthermore, based on the estimates of
\rho^{1}(t,x)
and the explicit formula of the Poisson equation (
4.4
),
by direct calculation we see that
|E^{1}(t,x)|
can be controlled by some function
g(t)
which is a continuous increasing function with respect to t.
From the characteristic equation, we can determine the support set of
f^{2}
on velocity v.
Applying Theorem
3.1
again, we infer that
f^{2}
is globally defined for each
x\in\Omega,\,v\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\,0\leq t<\infty
,
and the function
f^{2}(t,x,v)\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}\big([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}\big)
is bounded.
By repeating the above argument, we can conclude that there is also the same conclusion for the sequence
f^{n}
.

Step 3.

Because
f^{n}
is defined based on the propagation of characteristics, it follows that
\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, i.e., (
4.16
) holds.
The equality (
4.16
) can be obtained by integrating the equation (
2.2
) over the space
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
with regard to the variables
(x,v)
.
We complete the proof.
∎

4.3.
Convergence of the iterative sequences {
f^{n}
}

Under the condition that the support of
f^{n}(t,x,v)
with respect to
v
is uniformly bounded, the iterative sequence
f^{n}(t,x,v)
will converge to
the solution of the RVP system. We denote
the support of
f^{n}
on
v
as
Q^{n}
,

(4.17)

\displaystyle Q^{n}(t)=\sup\{|v|:\,(x,v)\in\text{supp }f^{n}(s),0\leq s\leq t\}.

We start with several auxiliary lemmas.
Let’s first introduce the measure preservation of characteristic curves.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
X(s;t,x,v),\,V(s;t,x,v)
are the characteristic curves,
the following transformation is symplectic and preserves the measure, that is

\displaystyle(x,t)\rightarrow(X(s;t,x,v),V(s;t,x,v)),

\displaystyle{\rm d}X(s;t,x,v){\rm d}V(s;t,x,v)={\rm d}x{\rm d}v.

Proof.

The proof of this lemma is based on the characteristic equation being a Hamiltonian system and we omit the details here (cf.
[
23
]
, Lemma 7).
∎

Next, we provide the uniform estimates for
f^{n},E^{n}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, and assumping that there exists
n_{0}
such that
Q^{n}(t)\leq M
for
n\geq n_{0},0\leq t\leq T
, we obtain that,
for
n\geq n_{0}+1,0\leq t\leq T
,

(4.18)

\displaystyle|E^{n}(t,x)|\leq C(T),

(4.19)

\displaystyle|E^{n}(t,\cdot)|_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq C(T),\,\,for\,\,any\,\,0<\gamma<1,

where
C(T)
depends only on
M,T,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}
.

Proof.

On the one hand, by the definition of
\rho^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle|\rho^{n}(t,x)|\leq\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\leq M^{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}.

From the representation formula for the solutions of the Poisson equation, we can see that (
4.18
) holds.

On the other hand, since
\mathrm{div}\,E^{n}=\rho^{n},\,\,\mathrm{curl}\,E^{n}=0,\,\,E^{n}\cdot n=h(x)
, we have the following estimate, for any
1<p<\infty
,

\displaystyle\|E^{n}\|_{W^{1.p}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\mathrm{div}\,E^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\mathrm{curl}\,E^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\|E^{n}\cdot n\|_{L^{p}}+\|E^{n}\|_{L^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\rho^{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\|h(x)\|_{L^{p}}+\|E^{n}\|_{L^{p}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|\rho^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|h(x)\|_{L^{p}}+\|E^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C(T),

and then, according to the Sobolev embedding inequality, we conclude that
|E^{n}(t,\cdot)|_{C^{\gamma}(\bar{\Omega})}
is uniformly bounded, that is, (
4.19
) holds.
∎

Now we establish the estimate on
\|f^{n}\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
by the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
Q^{n}(t)\leq M
for
n\geq n_{0},0\leq t\leq T
. Then
f^{n}
has the following uniform bounds, for
n\geq n_{0}+1,

(4.20)

\displaystyle\|f^{n}\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(T),

where
C(T)
depends only on
M,\,T
.

Proof.

Firstly, by the estimates (
4.18
)-(
4.19
) and Lemma
3.2
, choosing
\gamma>\frac{1}{2}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|E^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))}\leq C(T),

\displaystyle C_{1}^{n}(T)\alpha(0)\leq\alpha^{n}(t)\leq C_{2}^{n}(T)\alpha(0),

where
C_{1}^{n}(T),C_{2}^{n}(T)
depends only on
T,\|E^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))}
.
Thus, we can obtain

(4.21)

\displaystyle C_{1}(T)\alpha(0)\leq\alpha^{n}(t)\leq C_{2}(T)\alpha(0).

The characteristic equations can been rewritten as,

\displaystyle\frac{dl}{dt}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}},

\displaystyle\frac{d\omega}{dt}=

\displaystyle\sigma=E_{l}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{kv_{\bot}\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}},

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dt}=

\displaystyle v_{\bot}\Big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\Big)+\frac{v_{\bot}x_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}F(t,x_{\bot})

\displaystyle-x_{\bot}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}\Big),

\displaystyle\frac{d\beta}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}F(t,0)+\pi\frac{2x_{\bot}L(t,0)}{(2\alpha)^{3/2}}\Big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\Big)-\pi\frac{x_{\bot}v_{\bot}}{(2\alpha)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\Big(\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+F(t,x_{\bot})\frac{v_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\Big).

Since the function
f^{n}
propagates along the characteristic curves,
the key is to estimate the corresponding norm of the characteristic curves with respect to the initial data to obtain the Hölder estimate of
f^{n}
.
Let’s introduce the notation as follows,

\displaystyle\xi=(l,\omega,\alpha,\beta),\quad\xi_{0}=(l_{0},\omega_{0},\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}),

\displaystyle[g]_{\lambda;\xi_{0}}=\sup_{|(x_{0},v_{0})-(x^{\prime}_{0}-v^{\prime}_{0})|\leq 1}\frac{|g(x_{0},v_{0})-g(x^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{0})|}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{\lambda}}.

We have the following claim, for any
\lambda<\mu
,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d}{dt}\big([\xi]\big)_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\Big|\leq C[\xi]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}.

In fact,

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dt}=

\displaystyle v_{\bot}\Big(F(t,x_{\bot})-F(t,0)\Big)+\frac{v_{\bot}x_{\bot}h(x)}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}F(t,x_{\bot})

\displaystyle-x_{\bot}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\frac{\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial l}+\sigma\frac{\partial L(t,0)}{\partial\omega}\big),

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha^{\prime}}{dt}=

\displaystyle v^{\prime}_{\bot}\Big(F^{\prime}(t,x^{\prime}_{\bot})-F^{\prime}(t,0)\Big)+\frac{v^{\prime}_{\bot}x^{\prime}_{\bot}h(x^{\prime})}{\sqrt{1+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}}F^{\prime}(t,x^{\prime}_{\bot})

\displaystyle-x^{\prime}_{\bot}\Big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}}\frac{\omega^{\prime}}{1-kx^{\prime}_{\bot}}\frac{\partial L^{\prime}(t,0)}{\partial l}+\sigma\frac{\partial L^{\prime}(t,0)}{\partial\omega^{\prime}}\Big).

Subtracting the above two equations yields

(4.23)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d(\alpha-\alpha^{\prime})}{dt}\Big|\leq C\Big(|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|+|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\Big).

Here, we have used (
4.21
) and the following facts,

\displaystyle|x_{\bot}-x^{\prime}_{\bot}|=

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\alpha}{L}\big(2+4H-\frac{\beta+\beta^{\prime}}{\pi}\big)\cdot\frac{\beta-\beta^{\prime}}{\pi}+\big(\frac{\alpha}{L}-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{L^{\prime}}\big)\big(1-(1-\frac{\beta^{\prime}-2\pi H}{\pi})^{2}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|+|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\Big),

\displaystyle|L^{\prime}-L|=

\displaystyle\Big|\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}h(x)-\sqrt{1+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}h(x^{\prime})+k\omega^{2}-k\omega^{\prime 2}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|+|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\Big),

\displaystyle|F-F^{\prime}|=

\displaystyle\Big|E_{\bot}(t,x)-E_{\bot}(t,x^{\prime})-\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}}\big)\frac{k\omega^{2}}{1-kx_{\bot}}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|v^{\prime}|^{2}}}\big(\frac{k\omega^{2}}{1-kx_{\bot}}-\frac{k\omega^{\prime 2}}{1-kx^{\prime}_{\bot}}\big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|x-x^{\prime}|+|v-v^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|+|x_{\bot}-x^{\prime}_{\bot}|\Big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\Big(|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|+|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|\Big).

Similarly,

(4.24)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d(l-l^{\prime})}{dt}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|+|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\Big),

(4.25)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d(\omega-\omega^{\prime})}{dt}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|+|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\Big),

(4.26)

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d(\beta-\beta^{\prime})}{dt}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(|l-l^{\prime}|+|\omega-\omega^{\prime}|+|\alpha-\alpha^{\prime}|+|\beta-\beta^{\prime}|\Big).

By (
4.23
)-(
4.26
), it follows that the above claim
4.22
holds. According to the claim, we have

\displaystyle C_{1}(T)[\xi(0)]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\leq[\xi]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\leq C_{2}(T)[\xi(0)]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}},

\displaystyle[\xi(0)]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}=|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{1-\lambda}.

Therefore, the following estimate holds

(4.27)

\displaystyle[\xi]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\leq C(T),

where
C(T)
depends only on
M,T.
From this, we infer that

(4.28)

\displaystyle\|f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{C^{\lambda}_{x,v}(\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(T).

In fact, since

\displaystyle\frac{|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{1-\lambda}}=[\xi]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\cdot\frac{1}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{1-\lambda}}

\displaystyle\geq C_{1}(T)[\xi(0)]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\frac{1}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{1-\lambda}}=C_{1}(T).

That is, if
|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|\leq 1
, we get
|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}(T)}
.
As a result,

\displaystyle\sup_{|(x,v)-(x^{\prime},v^{\prime})|\leq 1}

\displaystyle\frac{|f^{n}(t,x,v)-f^{n}(t,x^{\prime},v^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\lambda}+|v-v^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}=\sup_{|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|\leq 1}\frac{|f_{0}(x_{0},v_{0})-f_{0}(x^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{0})|}{|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\sup_{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{C_{1}(T)}}\frac{|f_{0}(x_{0},v_{0})-f_{0}(x^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{0})|}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|^{\lambda}}\cdot\frac{1}{\Big(\frac{|\xi(t)-\xi^{\prime}(t)|}{|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|}\Big)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{C_{1}|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|\leq 1}\frac{|f_{0}(x_{0},v_{0})-f_{0}(x^{\prime}_{0},v^{\prime}_{0})|}{\big(C_{1}|\xi_{0}-\xi^{\prime}_{0}|\big)^{\lambda}}

\displaystyle\leq C(T).

Hence, the inequality (
4.28
) holds.
From this, we further infer that

(4.29)

\displaystyle\|E^{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{1,\lambda}}(\bar{\Omega})\leq C(T).

In fact, we first the estimate on
\|\rho^{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{0,\lambda}}
,

\displaystyle|\rho^{n}(t,x)|\leq\|f^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\leq M^{2}\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

\displaystyle\frac{|\rho^{n}(t,x)-\rho^{n}(t,x^{\prime})|}{|x-x^{\prime}|^{\lambda}}\leq\|f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{C^{\lambda}_{x,v}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\leq C(T).

Using again the Schauder estimate for the Poisson equation and (
4.6
), one has

\displaystyle\|E^{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{1,\lambda}(\bar{\Omega})}\leq C\big(\|\rho^{n}\|_{C^{0,\lambda}}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\lambda}}\big)\leq C(T).

Similar to the estimate (
4.27
), we can obtain

(4.30)

\displaystyle[\frac{\partial\xi}{\partial\xi_{0}}]\leq C(T).

In fact, for simplicity, we introduce the following notation,
\xi=(l,\omega,\alpha,\beta)=\xi(t,\xi_{0})
,

\displaystyle\frac{d\xi}{dt}=:A(\xi),

\displaystyle\xi(0)=\xi_{0}.

We denote
W=D_{\xi_{0}}\xi(t,\xi_{0})
as an
4\times 4
matrix,
I
as identity matrix, then we have

\displaystyle\frac{dW}{dt}=D_{\xi}A(\xi)W,

\displaystyle W(0)=I.

Therefore, we have two conclusions about W,

(4.31)

\displaystyle C_{1}(T)|W(0)|\leq|W(t)|\leq C_{2}(T)|W(0)|,

and

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{d}{dt}[W]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}\Big|\leq C[W]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}+C[\xi]_{\lambda,\xi_{0}}.

Applying Gronwall’s inequality, it follows that (
4.30
) holds.
Finally, based on the inequlities (
4.30
),(
4.31
), we can conclude that

\|f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{C^{1,\lambda}_{(x,v)}}\leq C(T).

Using the equation (
2.2
), we have

\|f^{n}_{t}\|_{C^{\lambda}_{(x,v)}}\leq C(T).

Thus we deduce that (
4.20
) is true.
∎

Next, we will prove the convergence of the iterative sequence
f^{n}
.

Proposition 4.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1.1
, let us assume that
Q^{n}(t)\leq M
for
n\geq n_{0},t\in[0,T]
. Then there exists
f\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
which satisfies the system (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) such that
f^{n}\,\rightarrow\,f\in C^{\nu;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
when
n\rightarrow\infty,
with
0<\lambda<\mu,0<\nu<1.

Proof.

First of all, we show that the iterative sequence
f^{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
According to the iterative equations (
2.2
), it follows that
f^{n+1}-f^{n}
satisfies the following equation,

\displaystyle\big(f^{n+1}-f^{n}\big)_{t}+\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\cdot

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\big(f^{n+1}-f^{n}\big)+\nabla_{x}\varphi^{n}\cdot\nabla_{v}\big(f^{n+1}-f^{n}\big)

(4.32)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x}\cdot\big(\varphi^{n-1}-\varphi^{n}\big)\cdot\nabla_{v}f^{n}.

The characteristic equations corresponding to the iterative equation (
2.2
) are,

\displaystyle\frac{dX}{ds}=\frac{V(s)}{\sqrt{1+|V(s)|^{2}}},\quad\frac{dV(s)}{ds}=\nabla_{x}\varphi^{n}(s,X(s)),

\displaystyle X(t)=x,\quad v(t)=v.

Integrating the equation (
4.32
) along characteristic curves
(X(s),V(s))
, and using the fact
f^{n+1}(0,X(0),V(0))=f^{n}(0,X(0),V(0))
, we obtain,

(4.33)

\displaystyle\big(f^{n+1}-f^{n}\big)(t,x,v)=\int^{t}_{0}\nabla_{x}\big(\varphi^{n-1}-\varphi^{n}\big)(s,X(s))\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,X(s),V(s)){\rm d}s.

From the representation formula (
4.4
) and the estimates (
4.5
), we have the following estimate on
\nabla_{x}\big(\varphi^{n-1}-\varphi^{n}\big)(s,x)
,

\displaystyle\Big|\nabla_{x}\big(\varphi^{n-1}-\varphi^{n}\big)(s,x)\Big|\leq C\int_{\Omega}\frac{|\rho^{n}(y)-\rho^{n-1}(y)|}{|x-y|}{\rm d}y.

Integrating (
4.33
) with respect to the variables
(x,v)
over the space
\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
, we get,

\displaystyle\|f^{n+1}(t)-f^{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}

\displaystyle\leq\int^{t}_{0}\int\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|\big(\varphi^{n-1}-\varphi^{n}\big)(s,X(s))\big|\big|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,X(s),V(s))\big|{\rm d}X(s){\rm d}V(s){\rm d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int^{t}_{0}\int_{\Omega}G^{n}(y,s)\big|\rho^{n}(s,y)-\rho^{n-1}(s,y)\big|{\rm d}y{\rm d}s,

where
G^{n}(y,s)
is defined as,

\displaystyle G^{n}(y,s)

\displaystyle=\int\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|X(s)-y|}|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,X(s),V(s))|{\rm d}X(s){\rm d}V(s)

\displaystyle=\int\int_{\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,x,v)|{\rm d}x{\rm d}v

\displaystyle=\int_{|y-x|\leq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{v}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{|y-x|\geq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s,x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{v}}{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq Cr\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(L^{1}_{v})}+\frac{1}{r}\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{(x,v)}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,x)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}_{x}(L^{1}_{v})}\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}_{(x,v)}}.

Here, we used Lemma
6.1
and chose
r=\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,x)\|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}_{x}(L^{1}_{v})}\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}_{(x,v)}}
.

Since
\|f^{n}\|_{C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t,(x,v)}}\leq C(T)
, it follows that
\|f^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\leq C(T)
. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,x)\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(L^{1}_{v})}

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}\leq C(T),

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{v}f^{n}(t,\cdot,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}_{(x,v)}}

\displaystyle\leq C(T)\|f^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}|\Omega|\leq C(T),

where
|\Omega|
represents the measure of
\Omega
, then we infers that
G^{n}(y,s)\leq C(T)
.
Therefore, we obtain the following recursive inequality,

\displaystyle\|f^{n+1}(t)-f^{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}

(4.34)

\displaystyle\quad\leq C(T)\int^{t}_{0}\|f^{n+1}(s)-f^{n}(s)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}{\rm d}s,

where
C(T)
depends only on
T,M
and the initial data.

For convenience, we denote
A_{n+1}(t):=\|f^{n+1}(t)-f^{n}(t)\|_{L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}
, then
we claim that there exists
0<\kappa<1,\epsilon_{0}
(depending only on T), such that if
0<t<\epsilon_{0}
,

A_{n+1}(t)\leq C_{1}\kappa^{n}.

Here, we provide some simple deductions, when
0<t<\epsilon_{0}
, by (
4.34
),

\displaystyle A_{n}(t)\leq C(T)\int^{\epsilon_{0}}_{0}A_{n-1}(s){\rm d}s\leq\cdots\leq\|A_{1}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(C(T)\epsilon_{0}\big)^{n-1},

choosing
C(T)\epsilon_{0}=\kappa<1
, and it follows that the claim holds.

If
\epsilon_{0}<t\leq 2\epsilon_{0}
, using again (
4.34
), we have,

A_{n+1}(t)\leq C_{1}\kappa^{n}+C(T)\int^{t}_{\epsilon_{0}}A_{n-1}(s){\rm d}s.

From this iterative inequality, we can obtain,

A_{n+1}(t)\leq C_{1}(n+1)\kappa^{n}.

In a similar way, we can obtain if
k\epsilon_{0}<t\leq(k+1)\epsilon_{0},k=0,1,2,\ldots
,

A_{n+1}(t)\leq C_{1}(n+1)^{k}\kappa^{n}.

From the iterative discussion above, we can determine that
f^{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{\infty}\big([0,T],L^{1}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})\big)
.

Now based on (
4.20
) and the fact that
f^{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, using the interpolation discussion, we can prove that
for any
0<\lambda<\mu,0<\nu<1
,
f^{n}
is also Cauchy in
C^{\nu;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

In fact, according to (
4.20
), applying the following interpolation formula, for any
p>1
,

\displaystyle\|f^{n}\|_{W^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C(T),

\displaystyle\|f^{n}\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{P}}_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f^{n}\|^{1-\frac{1}{P}}_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},

\displaystyle\|\nabla f^{n}\|_{L^{p}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\leq C\|f^{n}\|^{\vartheta}_{W^{1,\infty}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|f^{n}\|^{1-\vartheta}_{L^{1}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})},

where
\vartheta=1-\frac{5}{6p}
. Thus,
f^{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
W^{1,p}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.
Furthermore, based on Sobolev’s embedding inequality, it follows that
f^{n}
is a Cauchy sequence in
C^{\tilde{\nu}}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})

for any
0<\tilde{\nu}<1
.

According to the interpolation theorem in Schauder spaces (cf. Chapter 6 in
[
7
]
) and interpolating between
C^{1,\tilde{\lambda}}_{(x,v)}
and
C^{\tilde{\nu}}
, we have

f^{n}
is Cauchy sequence in
C^{1,\lambda}_{(x,v)}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
0<\lambda<\tilde{\lambda}
. Then in the same way interpolating in
C^{1}_{t}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
C^{0}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, we see that
f^{n}
is also Cauchy sequence in
C^{\nu}_{t}([0,T]\times\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for
0<\nu<1
. Therefore,
f^{n}
is also Cauchy in
C^{\nu;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Finally, we explain why
f\in C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Integrating the equation (
2.2
) over
[0,T]
yields

f^{n}(t)=f_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}\Big(\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f^{n}(s)+\nabla_{x}\varphi^{n-1}(s)\cdot\nabla_{v}f^{n}(s)\Big){\rm d}s.

Taking the limit in the above equation when
n\rightarrow\infty
, we have

f(t)=f_{0}-\int^{t}_{0}\Big(\frac{v}{\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}}\cdot\nabla_{x}f(s)+\nabla_{x}\varphi(s)\cdot\nabla_{v}f(s)\Big){\rm d}s.

That is,
f
is continuously differentiable with respect to
t
. We complete the proof of the proposition.
∎

4.4.
Prolongability of uniform estimates for the functions
f^{n}

Proposition 4.6
.

Let us assume that for some
T>0
there exists
M>0
and
n_{0}\geq 0
such that
Q^{n}(t)\leq M
for any
n\geq n_{0}
and
t\in[0,T]
. Then, there exists
\tau>0
depending only on
M,\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}
, such that,

Q^{n}(t)\leq 2M,

for
0\leq t\leq T+\tau
and
n\geq n_{0}
.

Proof.

With the help of the representation formula (
4.4
), it follows that

\displaystyle|\nabla\varphi^{n}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|x-y|\leq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\rho^{n}(y)|{\rm d}y+C\int_{|x-y|\geq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\rho^{n}(y)|{\rm d}y+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho^{n}\|_{L^{\infty}}r+C\frac{\|\rho^{n}\|_{L^{1}}}{r}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho^{n}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq CQ^{n}(t)\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}},

where we used the following facts,

\displaystyle r:=\|f_{0}\|^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}},\quad\|\rho^{n}\|_{L^{1}}=\|f_{0}\|_{L^{1}},

\displaystyle|\rho^{n}(t,x)|=\big|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}f^{n}(t,x,v){\rm d}v\big|\leq\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(Q^{n}(t)\big)^{2}.

According to the characteristic equation (
3.5
), we have for
t\geq T
,

\displaystyle v^{n+1}(t)-v^{n+1}(T)=\int_{T}^{t}\nabla\varphi^{n}(s,X(s)){\rm d}s,

furthermore, by (
4.17
), it follows that

\displaystyle Q^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq Q^{n+1}(T)+C\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}\int^{t}_{T}Q^{n}(s){\rm d}s+C(t-T)

\displaystyle\leq M+C\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}\int^{t}_{T}Q^{n}(s){\rm d}s+C(t-T),

where
C
is independent of
n,Q^{n}
.
Defining
P^{n}(t)=\max\big\{Q^{l}(t):n_{0}\leq l\leq n\big\}
, we have

\displaystyle P^{n+1}(t)

\displaystyle\leq M+C\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}\int^{t}_{T}P^{n+1}(s){\rm d}s+C(t-T).

On the basis of a Gronwall-type argument, choosing
\tau=\frac{M}{C\big(2M\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}+1\big)}
yields

\displaystyle Q^{n}(t)\leq 2M,\quad n\geq n_{0},\,\,0\leq t\leq T+\tau.

∎

4.5.
The sequence
Q^{n}(t)
converge to
Q(t)

Proposition 4.7
.

Let us assume that
Q(t)\leq M,Q^{n}(t)\leq M
for
n\geq n_{0},0\leq t\leq T
. If
f^{n}\,\rightarrow\,f\in C^{\nu;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
for any
0<\lambda<\mu,0<\nu<1
, then we have
Q^{n}(t)\rightarrow Q(t)
uniformly on
[0,T]
.

Proof.

Due to the Lemma
3.2
, we know that the characteristic curves away from the singular set in the initial state remain away from it
during their evolution. For these characteristic curves, we can estimate their difference as
n\rightarrow\infty
.
It is similar to the proof of the analogous result on Vlasov-Poisson system (cf.
[
23
,
21
]
).
In fact, since the sequence
f^{n}
is uniformly bounded on
n
in the space
C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t;(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
, similar to the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we see that the number of bounces is uniformly bounded with respect to
n
in the time interval
[0,T]
.
Moreover, the time series when the characteristic curves of
f^{n}
bounce to the boundary converge to the time when the charateristics of
f
collide with the boundary.
Since
E^{n}\rightarrow E
, it follows that the characteristic curves
\big(X^{n}(s;0,x_{0},v_{0}),V^{n}(s;0,x_{0},v_{0})\big)
of
f^{n}
converge to the ones
\big(X(s;0,x_{0},v_{0}),V(s;0,x_{0},v_{0})\big)
of
f
between bounces. Also we have the following estimate,

\displaystyle|X^{n}(S)-X(S)|=

\displaystyle v_{0}\int^{s}_{0}\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V^{n}(\tau)|^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V(\tau)|^{2}}}\big){\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{s}_{0}\int^{\tau}_{0}\frac{E^{n}(\mu,X^{n}(\mu))-E(\mu,X^{n}(\mu))}{\sqrt{1+|V^{n}(\tau)|^{2}}}{\rm d}\mu{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle+\int^{s}_{0}\int^{\tau}_{0}E(\mu,X(\mu))\big(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V^{n}(\tau)|^{2}}}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V(\tau)|^{2}}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(T)\big(\int^{s}_{0}|V^{n}(s)-V(s)|{\rm d}\tau+\int^{s}_{0}|X^{n}(s)-X(s)|{\rm d}\tau\big)

\displaystyle+C(T)\|E^{n}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle|V^{n}(S)-V(S)|\leq C(T)\int^{s}_{0}|X^{n}(s)-X(s)|{\rm d}\tau+C(T)\|E^{n}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}.

Let us define
Z(s)=|X^{n}(S)-X(S)|+|V^{n}(S)-V(S)|
, then

Z(s)\leq C(T)\int^{s}_{0}Z(\tau){\rm d}\tau+C(T)\|E^{n}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}.

By Gronwall’s inequality, it follows that

Z(s)\leq C(T)\|E^{n}-E\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0.

Because the number of bounces is uniformly bounded and
|V|
remains unchanged before and after the bounce, we conclude that
the functions
|V^{n}|
converge uniformly to
|V(s)|
when
n\rightarrow\infty
. Meanwhile, in accordance with the definition of
Q^{n}(t)
,
we obtain that
Q^{n}(t)\rightarrow Q(t)
, and the proof is completed.
∎

5.
Global Bound for Q(t)

In this part, we aim to determine the upper bound of the function
Q(t)
in any given time interval
[0,T]
. By doing so, we can subsequently extend the solution of the system
(
1.1
)-(
1.6
) to intervals of arbitrary length.

Proposition 5.1
.

Assume that
f_{0}\in C^{1,\mu}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2}),\,\,0<\mu<1
, and
f\in C^{1;1,\mu}_{t,(x,v)}([0,T]\times\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2})
solves
the system (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) with
\lambda\in(0,1),0<T<\infty
. Then there exists
\zeta(T)<\infty
depending only on
T,Q(0),\|f_{0}\|_{C^{1,\mu}(\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}
such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle Q(t)\leq\zeta(T),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

Proof.

We consider two cases on
Q(t)
, one case is within the domain, and the other case is near the boundary of the domain.

Case 1.
We consider the characteristic equations within the domain.
By the representation formula (
4.4
), we have

\displaystyle|E(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|x-y|\leq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\rho(y)|{\rm d}y+C\int_{|x-y|\geq r}\frac{1}{|x-y|}|\rho(y)|{\rm d}y+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}r+C\frac{\|\rho\|_{L^{1}}}{r}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq CQ(t)\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{\infty}}\|f_{0}\|^{\frac{1}{2}}_{L^{1}}+\|h\|_{C^{1,\mu}},

then, on the basis of
\frac{dV}{ds}=E(s,X(s))
, it follows that

\displaystyle|V(t)|\leq C+C\int^{t}_{0}Q(s){\rm d}s,

where
C
depends only on
T
and the initial data.

Case 2.
We handle the characteristic equations near the boundary. By (
1.15
),

\displaystyle\frac{d\omega}{ds}=E_{l}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V|^{2}}}\frac{kv_{\bot}\omega}{1-kx_{\bot}},

\displaystyle\frac{dv_{\bot}}{ds}=E_{\bot}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+|V|^{2}}}\frac{k\omega^{2}}{1-kx_{\bot}}.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\big|\frac{d\omega}{ds}\big|+\big|\frac{dv_{\bot}}{ds}\big|\leq\|E\|_{L^{\infty}}+CQ(s).

Thus, we also get

\displaystyle|V(t)|\leq C+C\int^{t}_{0}Q(s){\rm d}s,

where
C
depends only on
T,\,\partial\Omega
,and the initial data.

Combining the above two cases as well as the definition of
Q(t)
yields

\displaystyle Q(t)\leq C+C\int^{t}_{0}Q(s){\rm d}s.

Therefore, based on Gronwall’s inequality, there exists
\zeta(T)
such that

Q(t)\leq\zeta(T),\quad 0\leq t\leq T.

∎

Finally, we provide the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In order to obtain the global existence of the solution of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.6
), we need to establish that the function sequence
f^{n}
of the iterative equation (
2.1
)-(
2.6
) converges to the solution of (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) for any time
t
. If the functions
Q^{n}(t)
are uniformly bounded in any compact set about
t
, then we can conclude the desired limit on the basis of Propositon
4.5
.

For this reason, we will first set

\displaystyle J(t):=\sup_{n}Q^{n}(t),\quad\lim_{t\rightarrow T_{\max}}J(t)=\infty.

It is easy to see that
J(t)
is a monotonically increasing function with respect to
t
. It suffices to prove that
T_{\max}=\infty
, which can be derived by contradiction. If
T_{\max}<\infty
,
let
\zeta(T_{\max})
be as in Proposition
5.1
, then based on Proposition
4.6
, we choose

\tau=\tau(2\zeta(T_{\max}),\|f_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}})
. From the definition of
T_{\max}
, it can be seen that
Q^{n}(t)
is uniformly bounded
in the time interval
[0,T_{\max}-\frac{\tau}{2}]
. Therefore, making use of Proposition
4.5
as well as Proposition
4.7
yields

\displaystyle f^{n}\rightarrow f\quad in\,\,C^{\nu;1,\lambda}_{t,(x,v)},\quad for\quad 0\leq t\leq T_{\max}-\frac{\tau}{2},

\displaystyle Q^{n}(t)\rightarrow Q(t)\quad for\quad 0\leq t\leq T_{\max}-\frac{\tau}{2}.

Specially, let
\tilde{t}=T_{\max}-\frac{\tau}{2}
, we have

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}Q^{n}(\tilde{t})=Q(\tilde{t})\leq\zeta(T_{\max}).

So, if
n\geq n_{0}
and
n_{0}
is large enough,
Q^{n}(\tilde{t})\leq 2\zeta(T_{\max})
; and in the light of Proposition (
4.6
), we know that for

0\leq t\leq T_{\max}+\frac{\tau}{2}
and
n\geq n_{0}
,

Q^{n}(t)\leq 4\zeta(T_{\max}).

Consequently, it follows that
J(t)
is bounded as
t\rightarrow T_{\max}.
This contradicts the definition of
T_{\max}.

So far, we have proven the global existence of the solution of the system (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) in
C^{1;1,\lambda}_{t,(x,v)}
for some
0<\lambda<\mu.

Finally, we illustrate the uniqueness in Theorem
1.1
. Suppose that
f_{1},f_{2}
are two solutions of (
1.1
)-(
1.6
) and satisfy the same initial and boundary conditions,
similarly to the derivation of (
4.34
), we have

\displaystyle\|f_{1}(t)-f_{2}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C(T)\int^{t}_{0}\|f_{1}(s)-f_{2}(s)\|_{L^{1}}{\rm d}s.

Naturally,
f_{1}=f_{2}
holds. We completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

6.
Global Existence of Solutions with Dirichlet Boundary Conditions

In the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions for the electric potential, we shall show the global existence of classical solutions for general convex smooth domains in this section. It is necessary to make precise estimates for the electric potential
\varphi(t,x)
and its first derivatives near the boundary of the domain because difficulties near the singular set in the Dirichlet problem could primarily be resolved by introducing a new method to obtain an analogous velocity lemma. Specifically, we have modified the local coordinate variables

\alpha=\frac{v_{\bot}^{2}}{2}-\varphi(t,x)-L(t,l,0,\omega,v_{\bot})x_{\bot}

based on the previously mentioned Newman boundary conditions. We omit the proofs of the other claims in the earlier sections because they still hold in the Dirichlet boundary case. We then focus mainly on proving the velocity lemma.

Let a point
x\in\Omega
be fixed, and
x_{0}
represent the tangent point of
x
. To examine the evolution of the characteristic curves starting from the singular set
\{(x,v)\in\partial\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{2},v\cdot n_{x}=0\}
, it can be hypothesized that
\tilde{x}
is near the boundary
\partial\Omega
and that
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
is the point nearest to it. By employing rotations and translations, one can set
x_{0}=(0,0),\tilde{x}=(\tilde{x}_{1},0)
and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}_{+}\equiv\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2};x_{1}>0\}
, and we can easily establish that the tangent line to
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
is represented by the expression
\partial\Omega
at
x_{0}
.

A concise elucidation of local coordinate variables
(\alpha,\beta)
is provided as follows,

\displaystyle\alpha(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\bot}^{2}}{2}-L(t,l,0,\omega,v_{\bot})x_{\bot}-\varphi(t,x)

(6.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\bot}^{2}}{2}+\big(-\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}E_{\bot}(t,l,0)+k\omega^{2}\big)x_{\bot}-\varphi(t,l,x_{\bot}),

(6.2)

\displaystyle\beta(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})

\displaystyle=2\pi H(t,l,x_{\bot},\omega,v_{\bot})+\pi(1-\frac{v_{\bot}}{\sqrt{2\alpha}}).

Remark 6.1
.

Based on the argument below, we can conclude that
\alpha
is nonnegative and is equivalent to
x_{\bot}+v^{2}_{\bot}
. First, deriving
\varphi\leq 0,x\in\Omega
is a straightforward process using the equation for
\varphi
and the maximum principle. Second,
E_{\bot}=-\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial n}<0
as given by Hopf Lemma, and
k\geq 0
by virtue of the convexity of
\Omega
.

It is now essential that we prove some technical estimates of the Newtonian potential and its first derivatives in order to obtain the velocity lemma further.

Let
T>0
, from the equation (
1.1
), it can be concluded that
\rho(t,x)
solves

(6.3)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot j=0,\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

with

j=\int\hat{v}f(t,x,v){\rm d}v\in\big(C^{1}([0,T]\times\Omega)\big)^{3}.

Lemma 6.1
.

Assume further that
\varphi(t,x)
satisfies the following boundary value problem

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi(t,x)

\displaystyle=\rho(t,x),\qquad(t,x)\in[0,T]\times\Omega,

\displaystyle\varphi(t,x)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad x\in\partial\Omega.

Then we have

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}(t,\tilde{x})\Big|\leq C\tilde{x}_{1}\big(1+|\log\tilde{x}_{1}|+|\log\tilde{x}_{1}|^{2}\big),

where
C>0
depends only on
L=diam\Omega
and
\|j\|_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Let’s set
R=|\tilde{x}|\ll 1
, and do the following change of variables,

X=\frac{x}{R},\quad Y=\frac{y}{R},\quad\tilde{X}=\frac{\tilde{x}}{R}=(1,0).

The Green function for the domain
\Omega
is denoted as
G
, therefore
\varphi
can be expressed as

\varphi(t,x)=\int_{\Omega}\rho(t,y)G(x,y){\rm d}y,

then, letting
\Omega_{R}=\frac{\Omega}{R}
and
H(X,Y):=G(RX,RY)
, we obtain

\displaystyle|\varphi_{t}(t,x)|

\displaystyle\leq\|j\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{y}G(x,y)|{\rm d}y

\displaystyle=R\|j\|_{\infty}\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla_{Y}H(X,Y)|{\rm d}Y.

We therefore only need to prove that

(6.4)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla_{Y}H(X,Y)|{\rm d}Y\leq C\big(1+|\log R|+|\log R|^{2}\big).

The integration region must therefore be split into two parts, which are
|Y|\leq 4
and
|Y|\geq 4
.

Case 1.
When
|Y|\leq 4
, let
X=(X_{1},X_{2}),\,X^{*}=(-X_{1},X_{2})
is the reflextion of X with respect to the coordinate axis
\{X_{1}=0\}
.
First of all we define

\bar{H}(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\log|Y-X|-\log|Y-X^{*}|\big),

which is the Green function for the half-space restricted to
\Omega_{R}\times\Omega_{R}
.

From a straightforward computation, it can be deduced that

\displaystyle\int_{|Y|\leq 4}|\nabla_{Y}\bar{H}(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{|Y|\leq 4}\big(\frac{1}{|Y-\tilde{X}|}+\frac{1}{|Y-\tilde{X}^{*}|}\big){\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C.

Let
W(X,Y)=H(X,Y)-\bar{H}(X,Y)
and
\psi(X,Y)=\nabla_{Y}W(X,Y)
, then the function
\psi(X,Y)
satisfies the following system:

\displaystyle\Delta_{X}\psi(X,Y)=0,\qquad X,Y\in\Omega_{R},

\displaystyle\psi(X,Y)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{Y}\big(\log|Y-X|-\log|Y-X^{*}|\big),\quad X\in\partial\Omega_{R}.

If
\text{dist}(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})\geq 1
, then
|Y-X|\geq 1
for all
X\in\partial\Omega_{R}
, and we get

\displaystyle|\psi(X,Y)|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big|\frac{Y-X}{|Y-X|^{2}}-\frac{Y-X^{*}}{|Y-X^{*}|^{2}}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\frac{1}{|Y-X|}+\frac{1}{|Y-X^{*}|}\big)\leq C\frac{1}{|Y-X|}\leq C.

According to the maximum principle, there is a constant
C>0
uniformly with respect to
Y
, such that

\displaystyle|\psi(X,Y)|\leq C,\qquad\forall X\in\Omega_{R}.

Although the corresponding estimates of
\psi(X,Y)
become more intricate when the distance between
Y
and
\partial\Omega_{R}
is less than 1, the question can be resolved through the construction of a supersolution using the Poisson integral formula.
Locating a boundary point
Y_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{R}
such that
\text{dist}(Y,Y_{0})=\text{dist}(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})
is a simple task. Then the following facts are true for
X\in\partial\Omega_{R}
.

If
|X-Y_{0}|\geq 2
, the triangle inequality yields
|X-Y|\geq 1
and then we get

\displaystyle|\psi(X,Y)|\leq C\frac{1}{|X-Y|}\leq C.

If
|X-Y_{0}|\leq R
, then we have

|\psi(X,Y)|\leq C\frac{1}{|X-Y|}.

If
R\leq|X-Y_{0}|\leq 2
, with the help of the Taylor theorem and the convexity of
\Omega
, we can deduce

|\psi(X,Y)|\leq\frac{C}{|X-Y_{0}|^{2}}.

To sum up, we define

\tilde{\psi}(X,Y)=\left\{\begin{aligned} &C,\qquad if\,\,|X-Y_{0}|\geq 2,\\
&\frac{C}{|X-Y|},\quad if\,\,|X-Y_{0}|\geq R,\\
&\frac{C}{|X-Y_{0}|^{2}},\quad if\,\,R\leq|X-Y_{0}|\leq 2.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

Thus, for
|Y|\leq 4
and
\text{dist}(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})\leq 1
, by means of the maximum principle, we have the following estimate,

\displaystyle|\psi(\tilde{X},Y)|\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{\partial\Omega_{R}}\frac{1}{|\tilde{X}-\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l\leq C\int_{\partial\Omega_{R}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l+C\int_{R\leq|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle+C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\geq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l.

Next, we estimate each item on the right end of the above equality,

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\geq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\geq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}{\rm d}l\leq C|\log R|,

\displaystyle\int_{R\leq|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{R\leq|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq 2}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\cdot\frac{1}{|\xi-Y_{0}|^{2}}{\rm d}l\leq C,

and leting
\eta=Y-Y_{0}
and
|X-Y|=|(X-Y_{0})-\eta|
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}|\tilde{\psi}(\xi,Y)|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\frac{1}{|\xi-Y|}{\rm d}l

\displaystyle=C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|}\frac{1}{|\xi-Y_{0}-\eta|}{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{|\xi-Y_{0}|+|\eta|}{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\xi-Y_{0}|\leq R}\frac{1}{|\xi|+|\eta|}{\rm d}\xi\leq C\log(1+\frac{R}{|\eta|})

\displaystyle\leq C+C|\log R|+C|\log dist(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})|.

Thus, together we obtain

\displaystyle|\psi(\tilde{X},Y)|\leq C+C|\log R|+C|\log dist(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})|,

and hence,

\displaystyle\int_{dist(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})\leq 1,|Y|\leq 4}|\psi(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C+C|\log R|

\displaystyle+C\int_{dist(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})\leq 1,|Y|\leq 4}|\log dist(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C+C|\log R|.

Therefore, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{|Y|\leq 4}|\nabla_{Y}H(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|Y|\leq 4}|\nabla_{Y}\bar{H}(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y+\int_{|Y|\leq 4}|\psi(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C+C|\log R|.

Case 2.
If
|Y|\geq 4
, we fix
Y=Y_{*}
, and rescale the variables by
\eta=\frac{Y}{|Y_{*}|},\zeta=\frac{X}{|Y_{*}|}
such that

\zeta,\eta\in\hat{\Omega}:=\frac{\bar{\Omega}_{R}}{|Y_{*}|}.

Define
g(\zeta,\eta)=H(|Y_{*}|\zeta,|Y_{*}|\eta)=H(X,Y)
. With the help of a change of variables,
g(\zeta,\eta)
satisfies

\Delta_{\zeta}g(\zeta,\eta)=\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|}\delta(X-Y),

from the fact
\Delta_{X}H(X,Y)=\delta(X-Y)
.

Furthermore, let
\phi(\zeta,\eta)=|Y_{*}|\nabla_{\eta}g(\zeta,\eta)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\zeta}\phi(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\eta}\delta(\zeta-\eta),\qquad\zeta,\eta\in\hat{\Omega},

\displaystyle\phi(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}.

Subsequently, we must further consider two cases depending on whether point
\eta
is close to the boundary
\partial\hat{\Omega}
or not.

Case 2-1.
If
\text{dist}(\eta,\partial\hat{\Omega})\geq\frac{1}{10}
,
\psi(\zeta,\eta)
is defined by

\psi(\zeta,\eta)=\phi(\zeta,\eta)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{\eta}\log|\zeta-\eta|.

Then, the function
\psi(\zeta,\eta)
satisfies the following system:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\zeta}\psi(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\quad\zeta,\eta\in\hat{\Omega},

\displaystyle\psi(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{\eta}\log|\zeta-\eta|,\quad\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}.

The assumption yields
|\psi(\zeta,\eta)|\leq C
for all
\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
. In the light of the maximum principle, we have

\displaystyle|\psi(\zeta,\eta)|\leq C,\qquad\forall\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}.

Given that we are now considering the confined region as
\{(\zeta,\eta)|\zeta\in\hat{\Omega},|\zeta|\leq\frac{1}{2},|\eta|=1\}
and applying the boundary regularity theory to the Laplace operator, the following estimate is valid,

\displaystyle|\nabla_{\zeta}\psi(\zeta,\eta)|\leq C,\qquad\forall\zeta\in\hat{\Omega}.

Thus, for the restricted region it can be concluded that
|\nabla_{\zeta}\phi(\zeta,\eta)|\leq C,
since
\phi(0,\eta)=0
. By the Taylor theorem, we get

\displaystyle|\phi(\tilde{\zeta},\eta)|\leq C\text{dist}(\tilde{\zeta},\partial\hat{\Omega})=\frac{C}{|Y_{*}|}.

Therefore, by the above range of variable substitution, we finally obtain that

|\nabla_{Y}H(\tilde{X},Y_{*})|\leq\frac{C}{|Y_{*}|^{3}}.

Case 2-2.
If
\text{dist}(\eta,\partial\hat{\Omega})\leq\frac{1}{10}
, we fix
\eta=\eta_{0}
and define
\bar{\eta}_{0}
as the boundary point closest to
\eta_{0}
.
Let
\zeta^{*}
as the reflection point
with respect to the tangent line at
\bar{\eta}_{0}
, namely,

\displaystyle\zeta^{*}=\zeta+2\text{dist}(\zeta,\{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2},(\eta-\bar{\eta}_{0})\cdot n(\bar{\eta}_{0})=0\})n(\bar{\eta}_{0}),

where
n(\bar{\eta}_{0})
is the outward normal vector. The functions
\bar{g}(\zeta,\eta),\omega(\zeta,\eta)
are defined as follows,

\displaystyle\bar{g}(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=|Y_{*}|\nabla_{\eta}g(\zeta,\eta),

\displaystyle\omega(\zeta,\eta)

\displaystyle=\bar{g}(\zeta,\eta)+\frac{1}{2\pi}\nabla_{\eta}\big(\log|\zeta-\eta|-\log|\zeta^{*}-\eta|\big).

Then we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\zeta}\omega(\zeta,\eta)=0,\qquad\qquad\qquad\zeta,\eta\in\hat{\Omega},

\displaystyle\omega(\zeta,\eta)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\frac{\zeta-\eta}{|\zeta-\eta|^{2}}-\frac{\zeta^{*}-\eta}{|\zeta^{*}-\eta|^{2}}\big),\quad\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}.

Now, for
\zeta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
, by the triangle inequality, the following estimate holds

|\omega(\zeta,\eta_{0})|\leq\left\{\begin{aligned} &C,\qquad if\,\,|\zeta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{8},\\
&\frac{C}{|\zeta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|+|\eta_{0}-\bar{\eta}_{0}|},\quad if\,\,|\zeta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{8}.\\
\end{aligned}\right.

We denote
D=\text{dist}(\eta_{0},\partial\hat{\Omega})=|\eta_{0}-\bar{\eta}_{0}|
, and apply the Poisson kernel formula to obtain, for
\zeta\in\mathbf{B}_{\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|}}(0)\cap\hat{\Omega}
,

\displaystyle|\omega(\zeta,\eta_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\partial\hat{\Omega}}\frac{1}{|\zeta-\beta|}|\omega(\beta,\eta_{0})|{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\beta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{8}}\frac{1}{|\zeta-\beta|}\cdot\frac{C}{|\zeta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|+|\eta_{0}-\bar{\eta}_{0}|}{\rm d}l+\int_{|\beta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{8}}\frac{C}{|\zeta-\beta|}{\rm d}l

\displaystyle=:M_{1}+M_{2}.

First, the estimation of
M_{1}
is performed as follows, since

|\eta_{0}|=1,\,\,|\eta_{0}-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{10},\,\,|\zeta|\leq\frac{1}{4},

we have

|\zeta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\geq\frac{13}{20},

then, for
\beta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
and
|\beta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\leq\frac{1}{8}
, the triangle inequality yields

|\zeta-\beta|\geq\frac{21}{40}.

Thus,

\displaystyle M_{1}\leq C\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{8}}\frac{1}{\mu+D}{\rm d}\mu\leq C|\log D|.

Second, we estimate
M_{2}
. For
\beta\in\partial\hat{\Omega}
and
|\beta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{8}
, due to

|\tilde{\zeta}|=|\frac{|\tilde{X}|}{|Y_{*}|}|=\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|},\,\,\text{dist}(\tilde{\zeta},\partial\hat{\Omega})=\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|},\,\,|\tilde{\zeta}-|\leq\frac{1}{10},\,\,|\tilde{\zeta}-\beta|\geq\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|},

we get,

|\tilde{\zeta}-\beta|\geq\frac{1}{3}\big(\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|}+|\beta|\big),

therefore,

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|\beta-\bar{\eta}_{0}|\geq\frac{1}{8}}\frac{|Y_{*}|}{1+|Y_{*}||\beta|}{\rm d}l

\displaystyle\leq C|Y_{*}|\int^{\frac{C}{|Y_{*}|R}}_{0}\frac{1}{1+|Y_{*}|\mu}{\rm d}\mu

\displaystyle\leq C|\log R|.

Finally, we obtain

\displaystyle|\omega(\tilde{\zeta},\eta_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(|\log R|+|\log D|\big),

\displaystyle|\bar{g}(\tilde{\zeta},\eta_{0})|

\displaystyle\leq C\big(1+|\log R|+|\log D|\big),

thus, by the above range of variable substitution, we have

\displaystyle|\nabla_{Y}H(\tilde{X},Y_{*})|\leq C\big(\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|^{2}}+\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|^{2}}|\log R|+\frac{1}{|Y_{*}|^{2}}|\log{\rm dist}(\frac{Y_{*}}{|Y_{*}|}.\partial\hat{\Omega})|\big).

Therefore, if
|Y|\geq 4
,

\displaystyle\int_{4\leq|Y|\leq\frac{L}{R}}|\nabla_{Y}H(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}^{2}Y\leq C

\displaystyle\int_{4\leq|Y|\leq\frac{L}{R}}\frac{1}{|Y|^{2}}\big(1+|\log R|

\displaystyle+|\log dist(\frac{Y}{|Y|},\partial\hat{\Omega})|\big){\rm d}^{2}Y

\displaystyle\leq C

\displaystyle\big(|\log R|+|\log R|^{2}\big),

where the following integral estimation has been used,

\displaystyle\int_{4\leq|Y|\leq\frac{L}{R}}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Y|^{2}}|\log dist(\frac{Y}{|Y|},\partial\hat{\Omega})|{\rm d}^{2}Y

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{|\log_{2}R|}_{n=0}\int_{4\cdot 2^{n}\leq|Y|\leq 4\cdot 2^{n+1}}\frac{1}{|Y|^{2}}|\log dist(\frac{Y}{|Y|},\partial\hat{\Omega})|{\rm d}^{2}Y

\displaystyle\leq\sum^{|\log_{2}R|}_{n=0}\int_{4\leq|Z|\leq 8}\frac{1}{|Z|^{2}}|\log dist(\frac{Z}{|Z|},\frac{\partial\hat{\Omega}}{2^{n}})|{\rm d}^{2}Z

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{16}\sum^{|\log_{2}R|}_{n=0}\int_{4\leq|Z|\leq 8}|\log dist(\frac{Z}{|Z|},\frac{\partial\hat{\Omega}}{2^{n}})|{\rm d}^{2}Z

\displaystyle\leq C|\log R|.

To this extent, we have completed the proof of this lemma.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

Under the same assumptions of Lemma
6.1
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{i}}(t,\tilde{x})\Big|\leq C\tilde{x}_{1}\big(1+|\log\tilde{x}_{1}|+|\log\tilde{x}_{1}|^{2}\big),

where
C>0
depends only on
L=\text{diam }\Omega
and
\|j\|_{\infty}
and
i=1,2
.

Proof.

We shall prove the lemmea only for
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{2}}(t,\tilde{x})
, since the proof for
\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial x_{1}}(t,\tilde{x})
is similar.
As in the proof of Lemma
6.1
, we set the scaled variables
X=\frac{x}{R},Y=\frac{y}{R},\tilde{X}=\frac{\tilde{x}}{R}=(1,0)
and
R=|\tilde{x}|
. In line with the analysis presented in Lemma
6.1
, it is sufficient to show that

\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{2}}|{\rm d}Y\leq C\big(1+|\log R|+|\log R|^{2}\big).

Two cases are presented for this purpose in the subsequent discussion.

Case 1.
If
|Y|\geq 2
, we decompose the Greeen function
H(X,Y)=\bar{H}(X,Y)+W(X,Y)
, where

\bar{H}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\log|X-Y|-\log|X-Y^{*}|\big),

and
Y^{*}
is the reflection point of
Y
with respect to the line
\{X_{1}=0\}
.
Furthermore, we restrict
|X|\leq\frac{3}{4}|Y|
, then

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}|Y|

\displaystyle\leq|X-Y^{*}|\leq\frac{7}{4}|Y|,

\displaystyle|\bar{H}(X,Y)|

\displaystyle\leq C\log|X-Y^{*}|\leq C\log|Y|.

Due to

|H(X,Y)|\leq C\log|Y|.

On the other hand, for
Y

\hat{H}(\xi,\eta)=H(|Y|\xi,|Y|\eta)

\Omega_{0}=\{\xi\in\frac{\Omega_{R}}{|Y|};|\xi|\leq\frac{3}{4}\}
, then
\hat{H}(\xi,\eta)
solves the following boundary value problem,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\xi}\hat{H}(\xi,\eta)=0,\qquad\xi\in\Omega_{0},

\displaystyle|\hat{H}(\xi,\eta)|\leq C\log|Y|,\qquad\xi\in\partial\Omega_{0}.

For any multi-index
\alpha
, the regularity theory yields

|\frac{\partial^{\alpha}}{\partial\xi^{\alpha}}\hat{H}(\xi,\eta)|\leq C\log|Y|.

Letting
\tilde{\xi}=\frac{\tilde{X}}{|Y|},
since
|\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\xi_{2}}(0,\eta)|=0
, by the Taylor theorem we have

|\frac{\partial\hat{H}}{\partial\xi_{2}}(\tilde{\xi},\eta)|\leq C\frac{\log|Y|}{|Y|}.

Thus, we obtain

|\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{2}}(\tilde{X},Y)|\leq C\frac{\log|Y|}{|Y|^{2}}.

Case 2.
If
|Y|\leq 2
, let
Y_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{R}
such that
|Y-Y_{0}|=\text{dist}(Y,\partial\Omega_{R})
, and
\bar{Y}
is the reflection point of
Y

with respect to the tangent line at
Y_{0}
.

The Greeen function
H(X,Y)
is now decomposed as
H(X,Y)=\bar{H}(X,Y)+W(X,Y)
, where

\bar{H}(X,Y)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\big(\log|X-Y|-\log|X-\bar{Y}|\big).

We consider the following system:

\displaystyle\Delta_{X}W(X,Y)=0,\qquad X,Y\in\Omega_{R},

\displaystyle W(X,Y)=-\bar{H}(X,Y),\qquad X\in\partial\Omega_{R}.

Due to
|W(X,Y)|\leq C
for

, the maximum principle yields

|W(X,Y)|\leq C.

Next we consider the restricted region
\Omega_{R}\cap\{|X|\leq 4\}
, applying the regularity theory, we have

|\nabla_{X}W(X,Y)|\leq C.

On the other hand, since
|\frac{\partial\bar{H}}{\partial X_{2}}(\tilde{X},Y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\tilde{X}-Y|}
, we get

|\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{2}}(\tilde{X},Y)|\leq\frac{C}{|\tilde{X}-Y|}+C.

From the above calculations, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\frac{\partial H}{\partial X_{2}}(\tilde{X},Y)|{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{|Y|\geq 2}\frac{\log|Y|}{|Y|^{2}}{\rm d}Y+C\int_{|Y|\leq 2}\frac{1}{|\tilde{X}-Y|^{2}}{\rm d}Y+C\int_{|Y|\leq 2}{\rm d}Y

\displaystyle\leq C+C|\log R|^{2}.

∎

Finally, we give the analogous velocity lemma for the Dirichlet problem.

Lemma 6.3
.

For a given constant
\delta>0
, let
\Gamma_{\delta}=([\partial\Omega+B_{\delta}(0)]\cap\Omega)\times\mathbb{R}^{2},

and
X(s;t,x,v)
,
V(s;t,x,v)
be the characteristic curves associated with the relativistic
Vlasov-Poisson system defined previously.
Suppose that
E,\varphi(t,x)
satisfy the assumptions of Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
6.1
, respectively. Then
the existence of solutions to the characteristic equations (
3.4
)-(
3.6
) can be obtained in
[0,T]

for any
(x,v)\in\bar{\Omega}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Furthermore, the following estimate holds for any
(x,v)\in\Gamma_{\delta}
,

(6.5)

\displaystyle C_{1}\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(0)+x_{\bot}(0)\Big)\leq\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(t)+x_{\bot}(t)\Big)\leq C_{2}\Big(v^{2}_{\bot}(0)+x_{\bot}(0)\Big),t\in[0,T],

for some positive constants
C_{1},C_{2}
depending only on

T,f_{0},\|E\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],C^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Omega))},\Omega,\|j\|_{L^{\infty}}
.

Proof.

By the definition of
\alpha
and
E=\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x)=E_{l}\cdot U(l)-E_{\bot}n(l)
, taking the derivative of
\alpha
with respect to
t
along the characteristics, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d\alpha}{dt}=

\displaystyle-\sqrt{1+|v|^{2}}\partial_{t}E_{\bot}(t,l,0)x_{\bot}-\frac{\partial\varphi}{\partial t}

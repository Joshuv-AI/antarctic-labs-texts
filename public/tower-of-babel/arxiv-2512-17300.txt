Euler-Maruyama method for distribution dependent stochastic differential equation driven by multiplicative fractional Brownian motion
Guangjun Shen, Jiangpeng Wang, Xuekang Zhang

Abstract
In this paper, we establish the propagation of chaos and Euler-Maruyama method of DDSDE driven by multiplicative fractional Brownian motion with Hurst parameter H ∈ ( 5 − 1 2 , 1 ) H\in(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},1) . We have not only obtained an upper bound for the error of the Euler-Maruyama method but also verified the correctness of this result via systematic numerical simulation experiments. Keywords : Distribution dependent SDE; fractional Brownian motion; Propagation of chaos; Euler-Maruyama method Mathematics Subject Classification:

Introduction

Stochastic differential equations (SDEs) are the stochastic generalization of deterministic differential equations (ODEs/PDEs), providing a mathematical description for dynamic systems with random perturbations. Their development stems from the fact that many real-world systems cannot be accurately characterized by deterministic models, necessitating the introduction of ”stochastic noise” to capture uncertainty. The most typical noise source is Brownian motion. At this point, SDE can be described as

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(X_{t})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}.

(1.1)

Afterwards, Mckean
[
22
]
further promoted this work and accomplished the pioneering work of distribution dependent SDE (also known as McKean-Vlasov SDE). Its core idea is to describe the group effect, that is, the evolution of individuals is influenced by the distribution of the entire group’s state. Compared with traditional SDE, DDSDE’s coefficients not only depend on the state
X_{t}
, but also on the state distribution of
X_{t}
, and have the following form

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}=b(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(X_{t},\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t},

(1.2)

where
\mathscr{L}_{X_{t}}
stands for the law of
X_{t}
. This class of equations gained increasing attention following Dawson?s foundational work
[
12
]
and the development of the Lion’s derivative with respect to the measure variables
[
21
]
. DDSDEs are widely used to model random phenomena across various scientific domains, including physics, biology, engineering, and neural activities, such as
[
2
,
5
,
6
,
10
]
and references therein.
Another important feature of DDSDE is its inseparable relationship with the limit behavior of a class of interacting particle systems

\text{\rm{d}}X_{t}^{i,N}=b(X_{t}^{i,N},\widehat{\mu}_{t}^{N})\text{\rm{d}}t+\sigma(X_{t}^{i,N},\widehat{\mu}_{t}^{N})\text{\rm{d}}B_{t}^{i},~~i=1,\cdot\cdot\cdot,N,

(1.3)

where
\widehat{\mu}_{t}^{N}
means the empirical distribution corresponding to
X_{t}^{1,N},\cdot\cdot\cdot,X_{t}^{N,N},
namely,

\widehat{\mu}_{t}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum^{N}_{i=1}\delta_{X_{t}^{i,N}},

where
\delta_{x}
denotes the Dirac measure at point

B^{1}:=(B^{1}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\cdot\cdot\cdot,B^{N}:=(B^{N}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
are independent
d

Similar to ordinary SDEs, it is also difficult to obtain the true solutions of DDSDEs. Therefore, developing numerical methods adapted to DDSDEs becomes particularly crucial. In practice, Monte Carlo ideas, stochastic particle methods, and the theory of propagation of chaos are commonly employed to construct numerical methods for such equations. Bossy and Talay
[

is one of the earliest works to study the numerical approximation of DDSDE and establishes convergence of an Euler-Maruyama particle scheme with strong order

2.3
fractional Brownian motion

Recall that the fBm
B^{H}=(B^{H,1},\cdots,B^{H,d})
with Hurst index
H\in(0,1)
is a centered Gaussian process, whose covariance structure is defined by

\mathbb{E}(B_{t}^{H,i}B_{s}^{H,j})=R_{H}(t,s)\delta_{i,j},\quad s,t\in[0,T],\quad i,j=1,\cdots,d

with
R_{H}(t,s)=\frac{1}{2}(t^{2H}+s^{2H}-|t-s|^{2H})
.
Thus, Kolmogorov’s continuity criterion implies that fBm is Hölder continuous of order
\delta
for any
\delta<H.

Besides,
R_{H}(t,s)
has the following integral representation

R_{H}(t,s)=\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}K_{H}(t,r)K_{H}(s,r)dr,

where the deterministic kernel
K_{H}(t,s)
is given by

K_{H}(t,s)=C_{H}s^{\frac{1}{2}-H}\int_{s}^{t}(u-s)^{H-\frac{3}{2}}u^{H-\frac{1}{2}}du,\quad t>s,

where
C_{H}=\sqrt{\frac{H(2H-1)}{\mathcal{B}(2-2H,H-1/2)}}
and
\mathcal{B}
standing for the Beta function. If
t\leq s
, we set
K_{H}(t,s)=0
.
For

H=\frac{1}{2}
,
B^{H}
coincides with the standard Brownian motion
B
, but

B^{H}
is neither a semimartingale nor a Markov process unless

H=\frac{1}{2}
. As a consequence, some classical techniques of stochastic
analysis are not applicable.

\displaystyle\leq 2(1\wedge(T-S)^{\beta_{1}-\beta})\left\|(T-S)\Delta_{2}^{-1}\left(e^{(T-S)\Delta_{2}^{-1}\log 2}+1\right)\right\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\mu^{N})

\displaystyle\leq 4C(T-S)\Big(\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\nu^{N})+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}\Big).

Let

\displaystyle R_{0}=\inf\Big\{R>0|C(R)\geq\frac{1}{8}\Big\}.

we can see that for any
T-S\leq R_{0}
, we have

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}\leq\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\nu^{N}),

(3.34)

and

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\nu^{N}).

(3.35)

Through trigonometric inequality, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\mu^{N})

(3.36)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\nu^{N})+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 4\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mathscr{L}(X^{i}),\nu^{N}).

By Lemma
3.3
, we can conclude that:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}+\mathbb{E}\mathbb{W}^{2}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{N},\mathscr{L}(X^{i}))\leq C\epsilon_{N}.

(3.37)

∎

4
EM method

In this section, we establish the classical EM method for the interacting particle system (
3.1
). For
n\geq 1
large enough such that
\Delta=T/n\in(0,1]
and
k=0,1,...,n-1
, define
t_{k}=k\Delta
. The numerical solutions are then generated by the EM method

Z_{t_{k+1}}^{i,N}=Z_{t_{k}}^{i,N}+b(Z_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\mu_{t_{k}}^{Z,N})\Delta+\sigma(Z_{t_{k}}^{i,N},\mu_{t_{k}}^{Z,N})\Delta B^{H,i}_{t_{k}},

(4.1)

where
Z^{i,N}_{0}=X^{i}_{0}
,
\Delta B^{H,i}_{t_{k}}=B^{H,i}_{t_{k+1}}-B^{H,i}_{t_{k}}
and the empirical measures
\mu_{t_{k}}^{Z,N}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{Z_{t_{k}}^{j,N}}(\cdot)
. The first is the piecewise constant extension given by

\bar{Z}_{t}^{i,N}=Z_{t_{k}}^{i,N},t_{k}\leq t\leq t_{k+1}.

(4.2)

and the second is the continuous extension of the EM method defined by

Z_{t}^{i,N}=\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{i,N}+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}b(\bar{Z}_{s}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{s}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}s+\int_{t_{k}}^{t}\sigma(\bar{Z}_{s}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{s}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}B^{H,i}_{s}.

(4.3)

Here, the initial value
\bar{Z}_{0}^{i,N}=X^{i}_{0}
and
\bar{\mu}_{t}^{N}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\delta_{\bar{Z}_{t}^{j,N}}(\cdot)
. for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have

Z_{t}^{i,N}=\bar{Z}_{0}^{i,N}+\int_{0}^{t}b(\bar{Z}_{s}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{s}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}s+\int_{0}^{t}\sigma(\bar{Z}_{s}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{s}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}B^{H,i}_{s}.

(4.4)

Lemma 4.1

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\beta<H
, Assumption
2.3
hold. Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2
, we can show

\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{T,\infty}+\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{T,\beta}<\infty.

(4.5)

Lemma 4.2

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\beta<H
, Assumption
2.3
hold, then

\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}\leq G_{1}(\Delta),

(4.6)

\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}\leq G_{2}(\Delta).

(4.7)

Proof.

From (
4.3
), for any
t_{k}\leq s<t\leq t_{k+1}
, we have for
t_{k+1}-t_{k}=\Delta
,

\displaystyle Z_{t}^{i,N}-\bar{Z}_{t_{k}}^{i,N}-(Z_{s}^{i,N}-\bar{Z}_{s_{k}}^{i,N})

(4.8)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{s}^{t}b(\bar{Z}_{r}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{r}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}r+\int_{s}^{t}\sigma(\bar{Z}_{r}^{i,N},\bar{\mu}_{r}^{Z,N})\text{\rm{d}}B^{H,i}_{r}.

Similarly to (
3.14
), we can obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{s,t,\beta}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\big(1+\mathbb{E}|\bar{Z}^{i,N}_{s}|^{2}\big)(t-s)^{2-2\beta}\Big)

\displaystyle+C(t-s)^{2\beta}\Big(\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+(t-s)^{2-2\beta}\Big)\mathbb{E}\|\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{s,t,\beta}.

Without of lost generality, we assume that
C\geq 1
in (
4.9
), and let

\Delta_{3}=\Big(\frac{\Delta^{2\beta}}{6C\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}\wedge\Big(\frac{\Delta^{2\beta}}{6C}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\wedge\Big(\frac{\Delta^{2\beta}}{6C}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2\beta+2}}\wedge\Big(\frac{\Delta^{2\beta}}{6C\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4\beta}}.

(4.10)

Taking
t=s+\Delta_{3}
and by combining with Lemma
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{s,s+\Delta_{3},\beta}

(4.11)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\big(1+\mathbb{E}\|\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{T,\infty}\big)\Delta_{3}^{2-2\beta}\Big)+C\Delta_{3}^{2\beta}\Big(\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2-2\beta}\Big)\mathbb{E}\|\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{T,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2-2\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2\beta}\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2}\Big).

For every
l\in N
,

\displaystyle\Pi_{2,l}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k}+l\Delta_{3},(t_{k}+(l+1)\Delta_{3})\wedge t_{k+1},\beta}\Delta_{3}^{2\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\Delta_{3}^{2\beta}\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2}+\Delta_{3}^{4\beta}\|B^{H,i}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}+\Delta_{3}^{2\beta+2}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\Delta^{2\beta}.

Consequently, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}

(4.12)

\displaystyle\leq\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+2\big)\Big[\mathbb{E}|Z^{i,N}_{t_{k}}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}_{t_{k}}|^{2}+\sum_{l=0}^{[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]}\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k}+l\Delta_{3},(t_{k}+(l+1)\Delta_{3})\wedge t_{k+1},\beta}\Delta_{3}^{2\beta}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+2\big)\Big[\sum_{l=0}^{[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]}\Pi_{2,l}\Big]

\displaystyle\leq\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+2\big)\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+1\big)\Delta^{2\beta}.

Notice that

\displaystyle[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]\leq\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}

(4.13)

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg\{\Big\|B^{H,i}\|^{\frac{1}{\beta}}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}\vee\Delta^{1-\beta}\vee\Delta^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}\vee\Big(\Delta^{\frac{1}{2}}\|B^{H,i}\|^{\frac{1}{2\beta}}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}\Big)\Bigg\}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigg(\Delta^{\frac{H-\beta}{\beta}}+\Delta^{1-\beta}+\Delta^{\frac{1}{1+\beta}}+\Delta^{\frac{H}{2\beta}}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\Delta^{\frac{H-\beta}{\beta}}\vee\Delta^{1-\beta}\Big).

We can see that for
\Delta<T
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+2\big)\big([\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]+1\big)\Delta^{2\beta}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{2\beta}.

Consistent with (
3.23
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\big[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}\big]\Big)^{2-2\beta}\max_{0\leq l\leq[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}]}\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k}+l\Delta_{3},(t_{k}+(l+1)\Delta_{3})\wedge t_{k+1},\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\big[\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}\big]\Big)^{2-2\beta}\Delta^{2\beta}\Delta_{3}^{-2\beta}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(1+\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}\Big)^{2-2\beta}\big(\frac{\Delta}{\Delta_{3}}\big)^{2\beta}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\Delta^{2(H-\beta)}\vee\Delta^{2\beta(1-\beta)}\Big).

∎

Theorem 4.3

Let
\frac{1}{2}<\beta<H
and Assumption
2.3
hold. Then, for any interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{(H-\beta)\wedge(\beta-\beta^{2})},

(4.16)

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{(H-\beta)\wedge(\beta-\beta^{2})}.

(4.17)

Proof.

By the same method as Theorem
3.4
, we can obtain that:

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}\leq 2(1\wedge(T-S)^{\beta_{1}-\beta})\|\text{\rm{e}}^{(1+\log 2)(T-S)\Delta_{2}^{-1}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{N},\bar{\mu}^{Z,N}),

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}\leq 4(1\wedge(T-S)^{\beta_{1}-\beta})\|\text{\rm{e}}^{(1+\log 2)(T-S)\Delta_{2}^{-1}}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{N},\bar{\mu}^{Z,N}).

According to (
2.9
), we can deduce that

\displaystyle\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{N},\bar{\mu}^{Z,N})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{N},\mu^{Z,N})+\mathbb{W}_{2,S,T,\beta}(\mu^{Z,N},\bar{\mu}^{Z,N})

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}.

Using the same
R_{0}
as in Theorem
3.4
, for any
T-S\leq R_{0}
, we have

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}},

(4.18)

and

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}\leq 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}.

(4.19)

We can let
\Delta<R_{0}
. Thus, on an arbitrary interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}}+\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}

(4.20)

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\Delta^{\beta}+\Big(\Delta^{H-\beta}\vee\Delta^{\beta(1-\beta)}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{(H-\beta)\wedge(\beta-\beta^{2})},

and

\displaystyle\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i,N}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}}+2\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\|Z^{i,N}-\bar{Z}^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}}

(4.21)

\displaystyle\leq C\Delta^{(H-\beta)\wedge(\beta-\beta^{2})}.

∎

Through trigonometric inequality, Theorem
3.4
and Theorem
4.3
, one can obtain the following convergence result of the proposed numerical method for the (
1.4
).

Theorem 4.4

It holds under
\frac{1}{2}<\beta<H
, Assumption
2.3
and for an arbitrary interval
[t_{k},t_{k+1}]
that

\sup_{i\in\{1,...,N\}}\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}\leq C\Big(\epsilon_{N}+\Delta^{2(H-\beta)\wedge 2(\beta-\beta^{2})}\Big),

(4.22)

and

\sup_{i\in\{1,...,N\}}\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}\leq C\big(\epsilon_{N}+\Delta^{2(H-\beta)\wedge 2(\beta-\beta^{2})}\big).

(4.23)

Numerical examples

Example 5.1

In this section, we perform numerical experiments to verify our theoretical results. Consider the following one-dimensional mean field model driven by fBm,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{\rm{d}}{X}_{t}&=\Big(X_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}t+\Big(\sin\big(X_{t}+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}B^{H}_{t}\end{aligned}

(5.1)

for
H\in(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},1)
, where
\mu=\mathscr{L}(X_{t})
. Define
b(x,\mu)=x+\int_{0}^{t}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)
and
\sigma(x,\mu)=\sin(x+\int_{0}^{t}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z))
.

\displaystyle|b(x,\mu)-b(y,\nu)|

\displaystyle\leq\big|x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)-y-\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{\prime}\nu(\text{\rm{d}}z^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|(x-y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)-\int_{\mathbb{R}}z^{\prime}\nu(\text{\rm{d}}z^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|(x-y)+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|z-z^{\prime}|\pi(\text{\rm{d}}z,\text{\rm{d}}z^{\prime})\big|

\displaystyle\leq|x-y|+W_{2}(\mu,\nu).

We compute the derivatives of
\sigma(x,\mu)
.

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}\sigma(x,\mu)=\cos\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big),\quad D^{L}\sigma(x,\mu)(y)=\cos\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big)y,

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}^{2}}{\text{\rm{d}}x^{2}}\sigma(x,\mu)=-\sin\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big),\quad D^{L}(\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}\sigma(x,\mu))(y)=-\sin\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big)y

\displaystyle\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}x}D^{L}\sigma(x,\mu)(y)=-\sin\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big)y,\quad\frac{\text{\rm{d}}}{\text{\rm{d}}y}D^{L}\sigma(x,\mu)(y)=\cos\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big),

\displaystyle D^{L,2}\sigma(x,\mu)(y,z)=-\sin\big(x+\int_{\mathbb{R}}z\mu(\text{\rm{d}}z)\big)yz.

It can be verified that these derivatives are all continuous and bounded. Thus, Assumption
2.3
is satisfied.
The corresponding interacting particle system is written as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{\rm{d}}X^{i,N}_{t}=\Big(X^{i,N}_{t}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}_{t}\Big)\text{\rm{d}}t+\Big(\sin\big(X^{i,N}_{t}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}X^{j,N}_{t}\big)\Big)\text{\rm{d}}B^{H,i}_{t},\end{aligned}

(5.2)

where
i\in 1,...,N
. The EM scheme for interacting particle system reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \text{\rm{d}}Z^{i,N}_{t_{k+1}}=\Big(Z^{i,N}_{t_{k}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z^{j,N}_{t_{k}}\Big)\Delta+\Big(\sin\big(Z^{i,N}_{t_{k}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}Z^{j,N}_{t_{k}}\big)\Big)\Delta B^{H,i}_{t_{k}}.\end{aligned}

(5.3)

By Theorem
3.4
, for
H=0.7,\beta=0.6
;
H=0.8,\beta=0.7
;
H=0.9,\beta=0.8
and
T-S\leq R_{0}
, we can obtain

\displaystyle\max_{1\leq k\leq N}{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\infty}}+\max_{1\leq k\leq N}{\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-X^{i,N}\|^{2}_{S,T,\beta}}\leq C\epsilon_{N}.

(5.4)

Similarly, by Theorem
4.3
, we define the error

\displaystyle E_{1}=\big(\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\infty}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\sqrt{\epsilon_{N}+\Delta^{2(H-\beta)\wedge 2(\beta-\beta^{2})}},

and

\displaystyle E_{2}=\big(\mathbb{E}\|X^{i}-Z^{i,N}\|^{2}_{t_{k},t_{k+1},\beta}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C\sqrt{\epsilon_{N}+\Delta^{2(H-\beta)\wedge 2(\beta-\beta^{2})}}.

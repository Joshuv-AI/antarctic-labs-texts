A lightweight model for echo trace detection in echograms based on improved YOLOv8
Authors: Jungang Ma, Jianfeng Tong, Minghua Xue, Junfan Yao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
With the rise of underwater unmanned platforms like unmanned boats, ROVs, and AUVs, there’s an increasing need for underwater detection technologies. Researchers have merged scientific echosounders with these platforms for biometric applications. However, current detection models are too parameter-heavy to embed in echosounders and struggle with noisy, irregular, and dense echograms. This paper introduces YOLOv8-SBE, a lightweight fish detection model based on YOLOv8, addressing these issues by enhancing feature extraction, information fusion, and small object recognition. YOLOv8-SBE adds the C2f_ScConv module to improve efficiency and reduce parameters, incorporates the BiFPN structure to enhance information transfer, and uses the EMA attention module for better small target recognition. It reduces computational complexity by 18.5%, decreases model parameters by 40%, and improves mAP0.5 to 79.5% and mAP0.5:0.95 to 58.2%, making it suitable for echosounders with limited resources.

Introduction

In recent years, more and more equipment that can be applied to marine information acquisition, such as sail buoy, glider, unmanned surface vehicle (USV), autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV) and some other unmanned survey platforms are integrated with echosounders for fishery resources survey and monitoring of marine biological resources. Among them, Pedersen et al.1 integrates low-power scientific echosounders into two autonomous surface vehicles (one a wave glider and the other a sail buoy) and discusses the main features of these systems as well as the challenges in ensuring the collection of high-quality echosounder data. Goulon et al.2 used an USV equipped with a state-of-the-art fisheries science echosounder, the EK80, for broadband applications to study its potential for monitoring fish populations in lakes. Brierley et al.3 equipped an AUV with a scientific echosounder to assess the avoidance of research vessels by the Antarctic krill (Euphausia superba). However, in practical fisheries applications, because echosounders lack functions such as fish and fish school identification, researchers often acquire information in echograms through empirical judgment, but this method is often limited by subjective factors and local experience, making echogram identification difficult, so automatic identification functions are highly needed.

In order to solve this problem, Slonimer et al.4 hybridized deep learning and heuristic methods in echograms, semantic segmentation by U-Net to detect fish. Vohra et al.5 utilized deep learning networks and semantic segmentation to automatically detect marine organisms in the water column from underwater acoustic data. However, the disadvantages of the above two methods are also obvious. Firstly, the algorithm is not applicable to irregular targets and dense targets. Secondly, the semantic segmentation method is easy to lead to the loss of information in fixed resolution, thus losing some detail information and affecting target detection. Therefore, it is necessary to propose an algorithm for automated recognition of underwater fish echo trajectories from echosounders to improve the efficiency of echosounder in fishery applications.

The traditional methods for monitoring fish schools are inefficient and often rely on manual labor. Therefore, people use computer vision for fish detection, obtain target photos or videos through underwater cameras, create datasets for model training, and achieve good detection results. However, occlusion has always been a challenge in fish detection6. In underwater photos and videos, other fish or environments, such as underwater plants, obscure many fish objects, making their detection difficult. Meanwhile, optical cameras exhibit local limitations within the range of underwater photography and are influenced by environmental factors such as light and water temperature. In contrast, echo-detection technology utilizes sound waves to detect underwater objects, providing valuable insights into underwater targets’ position, shape, size, and motion. Compared with traditional underwater imaging techniques such as optical imaging, echo-detection has the characteristics of strong penetration, offering clearer and larger detection ranges underwater and wider detection angles. Therefore, we use echogram detection to make up for the limitations of optical image detection in complex, deep, and remote underwater environments, including deep water, murky water, and low-light conditions. In target echogram detection, traditional methods utilize pixel differences, mathematical-statistical features, and echo features between images to achieve fish school detection. However, due to the inability to fully reflect the complex background and texture features of echograms7, the comprehensiveness and accuracy of the feature extraction process cannot be guaranteed, resulting in poor detection performance and high false detection and missed detection rates. Therefore, the development of deep learning has also broken through the technical bottleneck of traditional object detection algorithms in feature extraction and has performed excellently in accuracy and speed, becoming the mainstream algorithm for current object detection. In target recognition, two main categories of deep learning algorithms are two-stage and one-stage networks.

In two-stage networks, a region with a high probability of containing objects is proposed first, and then these proposal regions are input into a CNN for object detection. The two-stage object detection algorithms include R-CNN8, Fast R-CNN9, Faster R-CNN10, SPP-Net11, Mask R-CNN12. Although the detection accuracy of the algorithm in this phase is high, its detection speed is slow and unsuitable for the application scenario of multi-targets, small targets, and occluded targets, which is the subject of this study. In one-stage networks, methods such as YOLO (you only look once)13–16, SSD17, RetinaNet18, combine the region proposal process with objects detection to simplify the algorithm, and achieve a balance between accuracy and speed. YOLO, the most famous single-stage object detection algorithm, directly performs object detection and bounding box regression on the image and achieves higher object detection speed. Sun et al.19reported that YOLO not only improves accuracy but also reduces the missed detection and false detection rates of the model for small-scale and overlapping fish targets by increasing the depth of the network, reducing information loss, optimizing the loss function, and introducing the Soft-NMS (Soft-Non-Maximum Suppression) algorithm. However, they did not take into account the practical issue of applying it specifically to fish detectors when improving the YOLOv5 model. Due to the limited storage capacity of echosounders and the large size of YOLO models, there is difficulty in deploying networks to low-power devices. Therefore, research should focus on the lightweight of perception algorithms. The recognition network improved using lightweight modules can save computational resources20. Therefore, we evaluate the recently popular deep learning network YOLOv8, the latest detection algorithm in the YOLO family. It is characterized by high detection efficiency, high accuracy, and small model memory occupation. We propose a lightweight fish detection model named YOLOv8-SBE using YOLOv8n as the baseline model, where “S” stands for C2f_ScConv and Soft-NMS, “B” stands for BiFPN structure, and “E” stands for EMA attention mechanism module. This model aims to solve problems such as difficulties in deploying YOLO models due to the limited storage capacity of the echosounders, and it specifically improves detection accuracy for small targets in dense and occluded environments. Related work

YOLOv8 algorithm

YOLOv8 is a cutting-edge object detection model that excels in accuracy and speed, making it suitable for a wide range of applications21.YOLOv8 builds on previous versions of YOLO and introduces new features and optimizations. The backbone network and the “neck” part may refer to the design concept of YOLOv7 ELAN22 adopting a gradient-rich C2f structure. A new network structure using Decoupled-Head design for better classification and detection.YOLOv8 also transitions anchor-based detection to anchor-free detection. It uses a task-aligned distributor for sample matching and introduces Distribution Focus Loss (DFL). Non-maximum suppression is used to remove redundant bounding boxes, which improves performance on a wide range of hardware platforms, from CPUs to GPUs. The structure of YOLOv8 is shown in Fig. 1.

YOLOv8 employs C2f blocks for enhanced feature extraction. It processes the input through a convolution module and a split module, followed by concatenation after n Bottleneck modules. This approach reduces convolutions and accelerates the network. The neck utilizes a feature pyramid network23 (FPN) to handle multi-scale feature maps from the Backbone, achieving detection at various scales. FPN and PANet24integrate low-level and high-level features through upsampling and downsampling. The SPPF block, at the backbone’s end, uses three max-pooling layers to process features at multiple scales, enhancing feature abstraction. A more detailed view of the YOLOv8 modules is shown in Fig. 2.

C2f module with integrated ScConv

Conventional CNN structures are characterized by substantial computational and storage requirements. The ScConv25 module introduces a groundbreaking CNN compression technique that can simultaneously address spatial and channel redundancy issues within convolutional layers because it utilizes the redundancy of intermediate feature maps, improves feature extraction efficiency, and reduces parameters and calculations without any performance loss. As shown in Fig. 3, this module proposes two core components: the spatial redundancy reduction unit (SRU) and the channel reconstruction unit (CRU). SRU addresses spatial challenges within feature maps by breaking down the entire map into multiple spatial blocks and employing distinct convolutional kernels for each. This enhances precision in feature information within each block and significantly diminishes overall spatial redundancy. As a result, feature extraction becomes more efficient and accurate, decreasing computational complexity. CRU focuses on optimizing the channel dimension of feature maps. In order to process channel information more flexibly and efficiently, CRU has introduced a lightweight fully connected layer. This allows the model to merge information from different channels more effectively, reducing channel redundancy. This method improves the discriminative ability of features and further reduces computational requirements. ScConv can serve as a plug-and-play module, providing versatility and compatibility without the need for any changes to existing model structures, and it can easily replace standard convolutions. In the design of C2f-ScConv in this article, as shown in Fig. 4, replacing one Conv in the original bottleneck with ScConv reduces the number of parameters and computation while ensuring accuracy does not decrease C2f is converted into a C2f_ScConv structure, reducing common spatial and channel redundancy in standard convolutions, thereby improving the performance of convolutional network models and reducing computational requirements.

BiFPN

The neck network of YOLOv8 utilizes a collaborative combination of path aggregation network (PAN) and feature pyramid network (FPN) structure, as shown in Fig. 5a. Due to the use of summation for feature fusion in FPN, some details may be lost during the fusion process. Therefore, its performance may not be as good as PAN for high-precision detection scenarios. PAN uses cascading methods for feature fusion, which can preserve more details but increase computational complexity. Meanwhile, the PAN-FPN structure can’t fully handle large-scale feature maps, which may overlook valuable information and decrease detection quality. In addition, after upsampling and downsampling, the feature map will lose some original information, resulting in a relatively low reuse rate. Therefore, in order to more effectively address the aforementioned issues and correct this, we innovatively integrated the Bidirectional Feature Pyramid Network (BiFPN)26 into our model, as shown in Fig. 5b. Remove unidirectional input nodes from BIFPN (which do not participate in cross-level feature fusion and have a relatively small impact on overall network performance, thus simplifying the network structure) and connect the original input nodes and output nodes of the same layer to better preserve and utilize the feature map of that layer during the feature fusion process. This can enhance feature maps’ information transmission and fusion ability. BiFPN does not use simple feature map stacking or addition like traditional fusion methods; instead, it uses weighted feature fusion due to the problems of dense schools of fish, occlusion, and small object detection in echogram target detection. By integrating BiFPN, multi-scale feature fusion has been achieved, enhancing the representation of small target information in deep features, thereby improving the accuracy of fish detection and achieving lightweight processing for the neck part. It is an optimized alternative to PAN-FPN.

EMA attention mechanism

EMA27 is a novel and efficient multi-scale attention module that does not require dimensionality reduction. It achieves a uniform distribution of spatial semantic features in each feature group by reshaping a portion of channels into batch dimensions and grouping channel dimensions into multiple sub-features. The EMA module mainly consists of three stages.

Firstly, feature grouping is where EMA divides the input feature map into multiple sub-features in the channel dimension direction to learn different meanings. Secondly, it conducts cross-spatial learning. Since the large receptive field of neurons can collect multi-scale spatial information, EMA uses three parallel routes to extract attention-weight descriptors from grouped feature maps. Finally, EMA utilizes 2D global pooling operations to encode and output each branch’s global spatial information output. EMA adopts a method of aggregating spatial information in different directions in the spatial dimension, which effectively enhances the aggregation of features. Implementing this process involves introducing two tensors: one for the output of the 1 × 1 dimensional branch and the other for the output of the 3 × 3 dimensional branch. In order to capture overall spatial knowledge in the export of 1 × 1 branches, a 2D global average pooling operation was adopted. Before combining the collaborative activation mechanism with channel features, the output of the minimum branch undergoes a direct reshaping process to conform to the appropriate dimensional structure. soft-NMS

Traditional NMS28 is a fundamental algorithm to identify local maximum values while suppressing non-maximum ones. Initially, it arranges all candidate bounding boxes based on category scores and selects the box with the highest score. Subsequently, it iterates through the remaining candidate boxes. The former is removed whenever the Intersection over Union (IoU) value between a remaining candidate box and the one with the highest score exceeds the predetermined IoU threshold. This process repeats until all boxes are processed, yielding the retained candidate boxes as the final detection outcome. However, NMS exhibits a drawback: for overlapping candidate boxes, if the IoU value between the box with the highest confidence score and others surpasses the threshold, they are deleted, while those below the threshold are retained. Consequently, in scenarios involving dense or occluded scenes, NMS may hinder the detection of objects obscured by other elements or backgrounds.

In response to this limitation, Bodla et al.29 proposed a Soft NMS algorithm, eliminating the necessity for additional parameters and ensuring ease of implementation. Despite its enhanced functionality, Soft NMS maintains the same algorithmic complexity as traditional NMS, thus averting an increase in computational load. Furthermore, its seamless integration with existing object detection pipelines facilitates its adoption in various applications without necessitating additional training. By preventing object omission detection resulting from overlapping proposals, Soft NMS enhances the robustness and accuracy of object detection systems. Soft Non-Maximum Suppression does not entail the abrupt deletion of detection boxes with excessive IoU; instead, it assigns weighted values to all boxes except the one with the highest score. These weights reduce the confidence score of the box, attenuating the scores of neighboring detection boxes that intersect with it. The extent of score attenuation increases with the number of overlapping boxes. Subsequently, the predictor applies a confidence threshold to filter the boxes. Proposed model

YOLOv8-SBE

In this paper, YOLOv8n, the lightest model in the YOLOv8 family, is chosen as the original network to be improved to make it suitable for underwater devices with limited storage. Figure 6 shows the structure of the improved network. the C2f_ScConv module consists of a combination of the ScConv convolution module and the C2f module, and all the C2f modules in the backbone network part of the original YOLOv8 are replaced with this module, which improves the feature extraction efficiency and reduces the number of parameters and computations. This backbone layer extracts the features of the input image at three different scales, which are then transferred to the neck layer for further feature extraction. to the neck layer for further feature extraction. In the neck layer, we use a high-performance EMA attention module and replace the original PAN-FPN structure with the BiFPN structure in the neck network, which reduces the computational complexity, improves the detection accuracy of small targets, and performs multi-scale feature fusion after up-sampling to obtain the new fused features at three different scales. Finally, the fused features are passed to the head layer of the network for prediction. The YOLOv8-SBE model accomplishes a lightweight improvement with guaranteed accuracy, providing a theoretical model for target detection for embedded work with echosounders.

Datasets

The study dataset was provided by Tong et al.30, which consists of 91 images captured by a single-beam commercial echosounder in the high sea of the Northwest Pacific Ocean. As shown in Fig. 7a, this is the original data image, where the horizontal-axis represents time in seconds, and the length of each image is 1250s, and the vertical-axis represents depth in meters, and the length of each image is 95 m, according to the depth of draught of the fishing vessel to remove the acoustic data within 5 m of the sea surface, to avoid the bubbles produced by the fishing vessel and the movement of the waves interfering with the data, so the dataset are all the picture of the 5 to 100 m of the submerged water. Firstly, the echo level values from the acoustic data were converted to acoustic backscattering strength values. Secondly, the part outside the integration threshold was removed by setting the threshold to avoid the interference of zooplankton and large predators in the echo data. Then, the slight discrete noise generated by bubbles and the high-frequency impulse noise generated by the instrument was removed by open and close operations and 3 × 3 median filtering, respectively. After the above image processing process, we get Fig. 7b from Fig. 7a, and the dataset consists of images after image preprocessing. The dataset of this paper is composed of 411 images. Then, we used an edge detection algorithm on the dataset to annotate the dataset. Figure 7c shows the three types of echo traces were labeled as “0” for single fish, “1” for multiple fish, and “2” for fish school. In the dataset, a total of 80,541 echo traces were extracted by the edge detection algorithm, of which 49,231 were single-fish echo traces, 25,145 multi-fish echo traces, 25,145 single-fish echo traces, and 6165 fish school echo traces. The dataset was randomly divided into a training set of 329 images (80%) and a validation set of 82 images (20%).

Conclusion

This study provides a feasible lightweight method, YOLOv8-SBE, based on YOLOv8, for fish detection and identification in echosounders. In previous studies, researchers have proposed methods for marine life recognition that focus on improving accuracy, ignoring the requirements of the model size on the hardware itself, making it difficult to carry out embedded work and possibly affecting the energy consumption of the echosounder. This study pays special attention to model size in the method design to address this issue. We adopted a series of improvements to reduce the model parameters. First, all the C2f are replaced with C2f_ScConv modules in the backbone network, and one Conv in the original bottleneck is replaced with ScConv, which reduces the parameters and computation while ensuring that the accuracy is not degraded. Second, a lightweight structure named BiFPN is introduced in the feature enhancement and fusion stage to replace the PAN-FPN structure in the neck network, improving the target’s perception and recognition and significantly reducing the number of parameters. In addition, we introduce the EMA attention module in the neck network, which has relatively small computational and parameter requirements and low model complexity, effectively improving the model recognition accuracy. Finally, we select soft-NMS, which is more adapted to dense and occluded target recognition scenarios, to improve the model detection accuracy.

The experimental results show that compared with the traditional lightweight YOLOv8n detection network, YOLOv8-SBE requires only 1.8 × 106 parameters and 6.6 GFLOPs of operation, which reduces the number of model parameters by 40% and the model operation by 18.5%. Regarding accuracy, the mAP0.5 detection accuracy of YOLOv8-SBE reaches 79.5%, an improvement of 0.5%, and the mAP0.5:0.95 is improved by 2.9%. Based on the guaranteed detection accuracy, the model drastically reduces the number of parameters and the amount of computation, which makes it easier to deploy on echosounders with limited memory and computational resources, making it more suitable for underwater fish detection tasks.

In our subsequent research, we will focus on embedding the YOLOv8-SBE model into an echosounder, which requires a comprehensive consideration of model lightweighting, device performance, development environment, and real-time capability. In terms of lightweighting, our model will undergo improvements, resulting in reduced parameters, GFLOPs, and model size. However, further optimizations are necessary to better adapt it to the device. Common optimization methods include pruning and quantization, which will help further reduce the model size and improve inference speed. Regarding device performance and the development environment, we will select model formats such as ONNX, TFLite, or TensorRT based on the hardware specifications of the echosounder. We will also take into account the sounder’s operating system and computational resources to ensure smooth integration. For real-time performance, to maintain the stability and efficiency of YOLOv8-SBE in practical applications, we will load image preprocessing tasks directly into the echosounder, which reduces latency caused by data transmission, improving detection efficiency and enabling the system to handle complex underwater environments. The above considerations will facilitate the deployment of YOLOv8-SBE in echosounders and ensure the system operates efficiently in real-world marine exploration tasks.

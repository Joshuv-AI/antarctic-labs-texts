We recently developed a major technical breakthrough by switching from differential scanning calorimetry to nanoDSF (Differential Scanning Fluorimetry), more easily transferrable in clinical routine. Here, we applied it for the first time to samples from BC patients.”

In the sentence beginning “The nanoDSF plasma…” in this article, the text should have read

“The nanoDSF plasma profiling was performed at the Timone molecular INteractions Platform (PINT) in Marseille (France) as previously described [23, 24]. The nanoDSF technology measures the thermal stability of proteins, but, conversely to DSC, is based on recording changes in the intrinsic fluorescence parameters of the protein following its thermal denaturation. Changes in fluorescence parameters are recorded at two wavelengths, 330 and 350 nm, corresponding to the emission of tryptophan residues in buried (native proteins) and exposed (denatured proteins) states [32]. Briefly, before nanoDSF analysis, the plasma samples were thawed at 25°C, vortexed, and 10 µL of each sample was loaded into a standard 24-capillary chip (PR-AC002 from Nanotemper). Plasma samples were then scanned using the Prometheus NT.Plex instrument (Nanotemper) at 5% of laser power and a 1°C/min heating rate to obtain the plasma denaturation profiles (PDP) in a range of 15 to 95°C. The PDP represents the ensemble of raw data provided by the instrument, namely the temperature-dependencies of fluorescence at 330 and 350 nm (F330 and F350), ratios of fluorescence (F350/F330), and absorbance at 350 nm (A350) as well as first derivatives of these four signals. To avoid any batch effect, each 24-capillary chip was randomly loaded with the samples from each group (early BC, metastatic BC, healthy controls) [33].

NanoDSF raw data (F330, F350, the F350/F330 ratio, A350, and their first derivatives) were regularized, aligned, concatenated and then used as input. We evaluated the performance of the algorithms using the 10-fold cross-validation method.”

In the sentence beginning “Another promising approach…” the reference citations should have read

“Another promising approach for improving BC screening is liquid biopsy, in which the peripheral blood microRNA analysis is the most extensively studied method [35]. However, small BCs remain undetected using the current schemes. For example, the multi-cancer cfDNA methylation assay (Galleri*, from GRAIL) detected only ~ 30% of new BC cases (versus 51.5% for all cancers), and only 2.6% of stage I cases versus 90.9% of stage IV [36]. Similarly, another blood test (CancerSEEK) identified only one-third of early BCs compared to 98% of ovarian cancers [37]. Jin et al. evaluated the diagnostic potential of CTCs for BC. They reported 85% sensitivity, with detection rates varying by tumor stage: 50% for Tis, 81% for T1, and 100% for T3-T4 tumors [38]. Combining multiple biomarker types (ctDNA, circulating proteins, CTCs), or combining liquid biopsy with imaging is being investigated to increase detection [8]. Large clinical trials, such as PATHFINDER [39], are also trying to refine the liquid biopsy approach.

Another approach centered on liquid biopsy is analysis of plasma thermograms. A few teams, including ours, reported the performances of serum DSC for detecting cancers [14].”

In the sentence beginning “In another study [41]…” in this article, the text should have read

In these three previous BC studies, no predictive model was derived. Clearly, to our knowledge, our present study is the largest one on BC with 176 patients and 61 healthy controls and the first one searching for a predictive model.”

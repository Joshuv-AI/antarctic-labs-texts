Squarefree discriminants of polynomials with prime coefficients
Valentio Iverson, Gian Cordana Sanjaya, Xiaoheng Wang

Abstract
In this paper, we consider the family of monic polynomials with prime coefficients and the family of all polynomials with prime coefficients. We determine the number of f ⁡ ( x ) f(x) in each of these families having: squarefree discriminant; ℤ ⁡ [ x ] / ( f ⁡ ( x ) ) \mathbb{Z}[x]/(f(x)) as the maximal order in ℚ ⁡ [ x ] / ( f ⁡ ( x ) ) \mathbb{Q}[x]/(f(x)) .

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
n
and
N>1
be positive integers. Let
d_{1},d_{2},\ldots,d_{n}
be fixed positive real numbers and let
d=d_{1}+\cdots+d_{n}
.
Define a height function on
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
by the formula

H(\mathbf{a})=\max_{1\leq i\leq n}|a_{i}|^{1/d_{i}}.

Let
c>1
be a real number. For each prime
p
, let
\mathcal{B}_{p}
be a subset of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}
with measure
\lambda_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{p})\ll p^{-c}
such that
\mathcal{B}_{p}
is defined by congruence conditions mod
p^{N}
, where
\lambda_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic Haar measure normalized so that
\lambda_{p}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n})=1
. For any squarefree integer
m
, let
\mathcal{B}_{m}=\bigcap_{p\mid m}(\mathcal{B}_{p}\cap\mathbb{Z}^{n})
and define

\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})=\#\{\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\bmod{m^{N}}:\gcd(v_{1},m)=\ldots=\gcd(v_{n},m)=1,\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\}.

Suppose
\{\mathcal{B}_{p}\}_{p}
satisfy the following Uniformity Estimate: there exist real numbers
\alpha,\beta>0
such that for any real number
M>0
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m>M\\
m\text{ squarefree}\end{subarray}}\#\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\colon H(\mathbf{a})<X,\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\}=O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{X^{d+\epsilon}}{M^{\alpha}}\right)+O_{\epsilon}(X^{d-\beta+\epsilon}).

(1)

Let

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\#\{\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\text{ prime, }H(\mathbf{q})<X,\mathbf{q}\notin\mathcal{B}_{p}\text{ for all $p$ prime}\}.

Then for any
A>0
, we have

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O_{A}\left(\frac{X^{d}}{(\log X)^{A}}\right),

where

C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})}{\phi(p^{N})^{n}}\right).

Furthermore,
C^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}>0
provided that
\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})<\phi(p^{N})^{n}
for each
p
prime; that is, there exists
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\backslash\mathcal{B}_{p}
having all coordinates coprime to
p
.

When the bad set
\mathcal{B}_{p}
is contained in a set of the form
\{\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n}\colon p^{2}\mid F(\mathbf{a})\}

for some fixed polynomial
F(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, we have
\lambda_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{p})\ll p^{-2}
by
[
9
, Lemma 2.1]
.

We prove Theorem
1.3
following the technique of Browning
[
8
]
using the inclusion-exclusion sieve and then splitting the sum into the small range
m<(\log X)^{\eta}
for some large
\eta
, the medium range
(\log X)^{\eta}<m<X^{\delta}
for some small
\delta
, and the large range
m>X^{\delta}
where we use our uniformity estimate. The local densities in Theorem
1.2
are easily computed.

The bulk of the paper is devoted to the calculation of the local densities in Theorem
1.1
. Let
V_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
denote the set of monic degree
n
polynomials in
\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
and let
U_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
denote the subset of
V_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
where all the coefficients are not divisible by
p
. Then
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and
P^{\max}_{n,p}
are the squarefree density and maximality density of
f(x)
in
U_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Since a non-monic polynomial with leading coefficient not divisible by
p
behaves just like a monic polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, the corresponding densities in the space of non-monic polynomials are the same as in the space of monic polynomials. When using the inclusion-exclusion sieve, an important quantity that we need to estimate is

\#\{h\in U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\colon u\mid h\}

for some fixed monic polynomial
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
, where
U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
is the set of monic degree
n
polynomial in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
with all coefficients nonzero. We expect the residual classes of polynomials in
U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
modulo
u
to be equidistributed. We approximate
\#\{h\in U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\colon u\mid h\}
by
p^{-\deg(u)}\#U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
to obtain the main terms in
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and
P^{\max}_{n,p}
. We note that when
x\mid u
, the set
\{h\in U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\colon u\mid h\}
is empty. This is the main cause of the difference in densities in the space of all integer polynomials compared to the densities in the space of polynomials with prime coefficients, in the
n
-limit. We remark that the same method can be used to determine the squarefree and maximality densities in the space of all integer polynomials with
prime constant coefficients
and they will have the same limits as
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and
P^{\max}_{n,p}
as
n
goes to infinity (see
[
11
]
).

We prove upper bounds on the discrepancy

\left|\frac{\#\{h\in U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\colon u\mid h\}}{\#U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})}-\frac{1}{p^{\deg(u)}}\right|

by modeling a degree
n
polynomial in
U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
mod
u
as a path of length
n
on a certain directed graph whose vertices are indexed by
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(u)
, and where every vertex has in-degree and out-degree
p-1
. We use the theory of double stochastic matrices to estimate the sizes of the entries of powers of the adjacency matrix.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we perform the sieve to prime inputs and prove Theorem
1.3
. In Section 3, we compute the densities
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and
P^{\max}_{n,p}
in terms of the quantities
\#\{h\in U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\colon u\mid h\}
and show how the main terms appear. In Section 4, we bound the discrepancy and complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.3
, following the method of Browning in
[
8
]
. By the inclusion-exclusion sieve:

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\sum_{m}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)

(2)

where

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)=\#\{\mathbf{q}=(q_{1},\ldots,q_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\text{ prime, }H(\mathbf{q})<X,\mathbf{a}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\}.

Let
\eta,\delta>0
be real numbers to be chosen later. We split the sum in (
2
) into

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)+\sum_{(\log X)^{\eta}<m\leq X^{\delta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)+\sum_{m>X^{\delta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X).

(3)

The assumption
\lambda_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{p})\ll p^{-c}
then yields
\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})\ll p^{Nn-c}
for each prime
p
.
For each squarefree
m
, we then have
\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})\ll_{\epsilon}m^{Nn-c+\epsilon}
. Moreover, we have the general estimate

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\bmod{m^{N}}\\
\forall i,\gcd(v_{i},m)=1\\
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\end{subarray}}\#\{\mathbf{q}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:q_{1},\ldots,q_{n}\text{ prime, }H(\mathbf{q})<X,\mathbf{q}\equiv\mathbf{v}\bmod{m^{N}}\}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m^{\epsilon}X^{d-d_{i}}\right),

where the error term comes from when at least one of the
q_{i}
is one of the
m^{\epsilon}
prime divisors of
m
.

We now use the Siegel-Walfisz theorem to count primes mod
m^{N}
where
m<(\log X)^{\eta}
. Let
d_{\min}=\min_{i}d_{i}
. There exists a constant
C_{1}>0
depending on
N\eta
(and
d_{\min}
) such that for any squarefree
m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}
,

\displaystyle N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathbf{v}\bmod{m^{N}}\\
\forall i,\gcd(v_{i},m)=1\\
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{B}_{m}\end{subarray}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{\Li(X^{d_{i}})}{\phi(m^{N})}+O\Big(X^{d_{i}}\exp(-C_{1}\sqrt{\log X})\Big)\right)+O_{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}m^{\epsilon}X^{d-d_{i}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O\left(\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})X^{d}\exp(-C_{1}\sqrt{\log X})\right)+O_{\epsilon}(X^{d-d_{\min}+\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O\left(X^{d}\exp(-C_{2}\sqrt{\log X})\right),

where we may take
C_{2}=C_{1}/2
since
\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})\ll(\log X)^{Nn\eta}
. Summing over
m<(\log X)^{\eta}
gives

\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)=\left(\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O\left(X^{d}\exp(-C_{3}\sqrt{\log X})\right)

where
C_{3}=C_{2}/2
. Since
\rho^{\prime}
is multiplicative, we have

\displaystyle\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})}{\phi(p^{N})^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}+O\left(\sum_{m>(\log X)^{\eta}}\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\sum_{m>(\log X)^{\eta}}\frac{m^{Nn-c+\epsilon}}{m^{Nn-\epsilon}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)\frac{\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})}{\phi(m^{N})^{n}}+O_{\epsilon}\left(\frac{1}{(\log X)^{\eta(c-1)-\epsilon}}\right).

Hence we have

\sum_{m\leq(\log X)^{\eta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})}{\phi(p^{N})^{n}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O\left(\frac{X^{d}}{(\log X)^{n+\eta(c-1)-\epsilon}}\right).

(4)

For the medium range
(\log X)^{\eta}\leq m<X^{\delta}
, we ignore the prime condition. Moreover, we may assume
\delta<d_{\min}/N
so that

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)\ll\rho^{\prime}(m^{N})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{X^{d_{i}}}{m^{N}}\ll_{\epsilon}m^{-c+\epsilon}X^{d}.

Summing gives

\sum_{(\log X)^{\eta}\leq m<X^{\delta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)\ll_{\epsilon}\frac{X^{d}}{(\log X)^{\eta(c-1)-\epsilon}}

(5)

Finally for the large range
m\geq X^{\delta}
, we use the uniformity estimate assumption to obtain

\sum_{m\geq X^{\delta}}\mu(m)N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(m,X)\ll_{\epsilon}X^{d-\delta\alpha+\epsilon}+X^{d-\beta+\epsilon}

(6)

Therefore, given any
A>0
, by taking
\eta=(2A+1)/(c-1)
and
\delta=d_{\min}/(2N)
, we have

N^{\prime}_{\mathcal{B}}(X)=\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})}{\phi(p^{N})^{n}}\right)\prod_{i=1}^{n}\Li(X^{d_{i}})+O_{A}\left(\frac{X^{d}}{(\log X)^{A}}\right).

If
\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})<\phi(p^{N})^{n}
for all primes
p
, then the local density
1-\frac{\rho^{\prime}(p^{N})}{\phi(p^{N})^{n}}
is positive and is
\gg 1-p^{-c}
by the assumption
\lambda_{p}(\mathcal{B}_{p})\ll p^{-c}
. Hence their product over all
p
converges to a positive number.
This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
V_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
denote the set of monic degree
n

\mathbb{Z}_{p}[x]
and let
U_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
denote the subset of
V_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
where all the coefficients are not divisible by
p
. In this section, we work towards computing the squarefree density
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and maximality density
P^{\max}_{n,p}
of

Let
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}
denote the set of monic polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Let
U_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
be the set of monic degree
n

\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]

\mathcal{A}_{n}(u;\alpha)=\{(a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{n})\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\})^{n}:a_{1}x^{n-1}+\ldots+a_{n}\equiv\alpha\bmod{u}\}.

\delta_{n,p}(d)=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}\\

x\nmid u\end{subarray}}\max_{\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(u)}\left|\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{n}(u;\alpha)|}{(p-1)^{n}}-\frac{1}{p^{d}}\right|

(7)

to be small (see Theorem
4.1
and Theorem

). Notice from the definition that
\delta_{n,p}(0)=0
. The goal of this section is to prove the following results expressing
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
and
P^{\max}_{n,p}
in terms of
\delta_{n,p}(d)
.

.

For any positive integer

where the implied constant is absolute.

.

For any positive integer

p_{t}&\mbox{ if }n+1\equiv 0\pmod{4}\end{cases},

where

in

.
For any odd prime

Proof of Theorem

We start by characterizing monic polynomials in

(

The graph

That is, each vertex has out-degree

Let

The

Since the out-degree and in-degree of every vertex is

is a doubly stochastic matrix.
That is, its entries are all non-negative, and the sum of the entries in each row or column is

The
(\beta,\alpha)

is the number of paths from

We start by recalling some general theory on doubly stochastic matrix.

Definition 4.3
.

A real matrix

is a

if its entries are non-negative and the sum of the entries in each row or column is
1
.

Suppose
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})

J_{m}
be the all-one matrix of dimension
m

\tilde{J}_{m}=(1/m)J_{m}
. Then
\tilde{J}_{m}^{2}=\tilde{J}_{m}

M\tilde{J}_{m}=\tilde{J}_{m}M=\tilde{J}_{m}

M_{1}M_{2}

(M_{1}-\tilde{J}_{m})(M_{2}-\tilde{J}_{m})=M_{1}M_{2}-\tilde{J}_{m}.

In particular, by induction, if

is a doubly stochastic matrix of dimension
m

M^{k}-\tilde{J}_{m}=(M-\tilde{J}_{m})^{k}
for any positive integer

For any matrices

, we denote the minimum entry, maximum entry, and the normalized max norm respectively by

E_{\min}(M)=\min_{i,j}M_{ij},\qquad E_{\max}(M)=\max_{i,j}M_{ij},\qquad\|M\|_{\max}=m\cdot\max_{i,j}|M_{ij}|,

where

is the
(i,j)
-entry of

.
It is easy to see that

\|M\|_{\max}=m\cdot\max\{|E_{\min}(M)|,|E_{\max}(M)|\}.

(13)

We prove first that multiplying by a doubly stochastic matrix does not increase the normalized max norm.

Lemma 4.4

For any

M_{2}
doubly stochastic, we have

E_{\min}(M_{1}M_{2}),E_{\min}(M_{2}M_{1})\geq E_{\min}(M_{1})\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{\max}(M_{1}M_{2}),E_{\max}(M_{2}M_{1})\leq E_{\max}(M_{1}).

In particular,
\|M_{1}M_{2}\|_{\max}\leq\|M_{1}\|_{\max}
and
\|M_{2}M_{1}\|_{\max}\leq\|M_{1}\|_{\max}
.

Proof.

We first prove
E_{\min}(M_{1}M_{2})\geq E_{\min}(M_{1})
.
Indeed, for any
i,j
, since
(M_{2})_{ij}\geq 0
,

(M_{1}M_{2})_{ij}=\sum_{k=1}^{m}(M_{1})_{ik}(M_{2})_{kj}\geq E_{\min}(M_{1})\sum_{k=1}^{m}(M_{2})_{kj}=E_{\min}(M_{1}).

By similar method, we get
E_{\min}(M_{2}M_{1})\geq E_{\min}(M_{1})
and
E_{\max}(M_{1}M_{2}),E_{\max}(M_{2}M_{1})\leq E_{\max}(M_{1})
. Then from

E_{\min}(M_{1})\leq E_{\min}(M_{1}M_{2})\leq E_{\max}(M_{1}M_{2})\leq E_{\max}(M_{1})

and (
13
), we get
\|M_{1}M_{2}\|_{\max}\leq\|M_{1}\|_{\max}
and similarly for
\|M_{2}M_{1}\|_{\max}
.
∎

Next we prove that the normalized max norm is sub-multiplicative.

Lemma 4.5
.

For any
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})
, we have
\|M_{1}M_{2}\|_{\max}\leq\|M_{1}\|_{\max}\|M_{2}\|_{\max}
.
In particular, for any
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})
and
n\geq 0
, we have
\|M^{n}\|_{\max}\leq\|M\|_{\max}^{n}
.

Proof.

The second statement follows from the first statement by induction on
n
.
Thus, it remains to prove the first statement.
For any
i,j\leq m
, we have

|(M_{1}M_{2})_{ij}|\leq\sum_{k=1}^{m}|(M_{1})_{ik}||(M_{2})_{kj}|\leq m\cdot\frac{\|M_{1}\|_{\max}}{m}\cdot\frac{\|M_{2}\|_{\max}}{m}=\frac{\|M_{1}\|_{\max}\|M_{2}\|_{\max}}{m},

and thus
\|M_{1}M_{2}\|_{\max}\leq\|M_{1}\|_{\max}\|M_{2}\|_{\max}
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

For any
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})
doubly stochastic and integers
k\geq 0
and
\ell>0
, we have

\|M^{k}-\tilde{J}_{m}\|_{\max}\leq\|M^{\ell}-\tilde{J}_{m}\|_{\max}^{\lfloor k/\ell\rfloor}.

Proof.

We can write
k=\ell q+r
, where
q=\lfloor k/\ell\rfloor
and
\ell>r\geq 0
.
Then
M^{r}
is doubly stochastic and we have

\|M^{k}-\tilde{J}_{m}\|_{\max}=\|M^{r}(M^{\ell q}-\tilde{J}_{m})\|_{\max}\leq\|(M^{\ell}-\tilde{J}_{m})^{q}\|_{\max}\leq\|M^{\ell}-\tilde{J}_{m}\|^{q}_{\max}

by Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
.
∎

We conclude with a lower bound on
E_{\min}(M^{n})
in terms of
E_{\min}(M)
.

Lemma 4.7
.

For any doubly stochastic matrices
M_{1},M_{2}\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})
,

1-mE_{\min}(M_{1}M_{2})\leq(1-mE_{\min}(M_{1}))(1-mE_{\min}(M_{2})).

In particular, for any doubly stochastic matrix
M\in M_{m}(\mathbb{R})
and
n\geq 0
,

E_{\min}(M^{n})\geq\frac{1-(1-mE_{\min}(M))^{n}}{m}.

Proof.

Assume
m>1
, as we are done otherwise.
The second statement follows from the first statement by induction on
n
.
We now show the first statement.

We write
r_{1}=E_{\min}(M_{1})
and
r_{2}=E_{\min}(M_{2})
.
Suppose
(M_{1}M_{2})_{\min}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}
, where the
a_{i}
’s are entries of a row of
M_{1}
and the
b_{i}
’s are entries of a column of
M_{2}
.
In particular,
a_{i}\geq r_{1}
and
b_{i}\geq r_{2}
for each
i\leq m
.
By the rearrangement inequality, it suffices to prove that

1-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\leq(1-mr_{1})(1-mr_{2})

under the assumption that
a_{1}\geq a_{2}\geq\ldots\geq a_{m}\geq r_{1}
and
r_{2}\leq b_{1}\leq b_{2}\leq\ldots\leq b_{m}
.
We have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}

\displaystyle=\left(1-\sum_{i=2}^{m}a_{i}\right)b_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}a_{i}b_{i}+a_{m}\left(1-\sum_{i=1}^{m-1}b_{i}\right)

\displaystyle=b_{1}+a_{m}-2b_{1}a_{m}+\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}(a_{i}b_{i}-a_{i}b_{1}-a_{m}b_{i})

\displaystyle=b_{1}+a_{m}-2b_{1}a_{m}+\sum_{i=2}^{m-1}((a_{i}-a_{m})(b_{i}-b_{1})-b_{1}a_{m})

\displaystyle\geq b_{1}+a_{m}-mb_{1}a_{m}.

Then

1-m\sum_{i=1}^{m}a_{i}b_{i}\leq 1-mb_{1}-ma_{m}+m^{2}b_{1}a_{m}=(1-mb_{1})(1-ma_{m})\leq(1-mr_{1})(1-mr_{2}),

where the last inequality follows because

1-mr_{2}\geq 1-mb_{1}\geq 1-b_{1}-\cdots-b_{m}=0

and similarly
1-mr_{1}\geq 1-ma_{m}\geq 0
.
∎

4.2
Proof of Theorem
4.1
and Theorem
4.2

We now prove Theorem
4.1
.
We start by proving:

Lemma 4.8
.

For any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}
of degree
d
such that
x\nmid u
, we have

E_{\min}(M_{u}^{2d})\geq 2(p-1)^{d}-p^{d}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{\max}(M_{u}^{2d})\leq(p-1)^{d}.

In particular,

\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}}\|_{\max}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1.

Proof.

We write

S_{0}=\{a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{0}\bmod{u}:a_{0},a_{1},\ldots,a_{d-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\}\}\subseteq V(G_{u}).

Fix some
\alpha,\beta\in V(G_{u})
.
Then the
(\beta,\alpha)
-entry
(M_{u}^{2d})_{\beta,\alpha}
of
M_{u}^{2d}
is the number of
2d
-tuples
(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{2d-1})\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\})^{2d}
such that

\alpha x^{2d}+b_{2d-1}x^{2d-1}+\ldots+b_{0}\equiv\beta\pmod{u},

which is equivalent to

\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b_{i}x^{i}\equiv\beta-\alpha x^{2d}-\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b_{i+d}x^{i}\right)x^{d}\pmod{u}.

That is, we have

(M_{u}^{2d})_{\beta,\alpha}=\#\{\gamma\in S_{0}:\beta-\alpha x^{2d}-\gamma x^{d}\in S_{0}\}.

Consider the map
f:V(G_{u})\to V(G_{u})
given by
\gamma\mapsto\beta-\alpha x^{2d}-\gamma x^{d}
.
Then the above equality simplifies to

(M_{u}^{2d})_{\beta,\alpha}=\#\{\gamma\in S_{0}:f(\gamma)\in S_{0}\}=|f(S_{0})\cap S_{0}|.

On one hand, we have

|f(S_{0})\cap S_{0}|\leq|S_{0}|=(p-1)^{d}.

On the other hand,
f
is injective since
x
is invertible mod
u
, so

|f(S_{0})\cap S_{0}|=|f(S_{0})|+|S_{0}|-|f(S_{0})\cup S_{0}|\geq 2(p-1)^{d}-p^{d}.

Thus
2(p-1)^{d}-p^{d}\leq(M_{u}^{2d})_{\beta,\alpha}\leq(p-1)^{d}
, as desired. Dividing by
(p-1)^{2d}
gives

E_{\min}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d})\geq\max\left\{0,\frac{2}{(p-1)^{d}}-\frac{p^{d}}{(p-1)^{2d}}\right\}\quad\text{ and }\quad E_{\max}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d})\leq\frac{1}{(p-1)^{d}}.

This means that

\displaystyle E_{\min}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d})-\frac{1}{p^{d}}

\displaystyle\geq\max\left\{-\frac{1}{p^{d}},\frac{2}{(p-1)^{d}}-\frac{p^{d}}{(p-1)^{2d}}-\frac{1}{p^{d}}\right\}=-\frac{1}{p^{d}}\min\left\{1,\left(\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1\right)^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle E_{\max}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d})-\frac{1}{p^{d}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{(p-1)^{d}}-\frac{1}{p^{d}}=\frac{1}{p^{d}}\left(\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1\right).

As a result, by definition of the normalized max norm (
13
),

\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}}\|_{\max}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1,

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Corollary
4.6
, we know that

\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}}\|_{\max}\leq\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}}\|^{\lfloor n/(2d)\rfloor}_{\max}\leq\left(\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1\right)^{\lfloor n/(2d)\rfloor}.

Dividing by
p^{d}
gives the bound

\delta_{n,p}(d)\leq\frac{1}{p^{d}}\left(\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1\right)^{\lfloor n/(2d)\rfloor}.

Suppose now
n\geq 16
and
d\leq 4
. Then similar to the proof of Corollary
4.6
, we have

\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}}\|_{\max}=\|\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n-4d}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{4d}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}})\|_{\max}\leq\|(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{2d}-\tilde{J}_{p^{d}})^{2}\|_{\max}\leq\left(\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{d}-1\right)^{2}\ll\frac{1}{p^{2}}.

Dividing by
p^{d}
gives the desired result.
∎

We now prove Theorem
4.2
.
This time, we need:

Lemma 4.9
.

For any
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}
of degree
d
such that
x\nmid u
, the entries of
M_{u}^{d^{2}+d}
are all positive.

Proof.

Let
S_{0}
be as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
.
Fix some
\alpha,\beta\in V(G_{u})
.
The
(\beta,\alpha)
-entry
(M_{u}^{d^{2}+d})_{\beta,\alpha}
of
M_{u}^{d^{2}+d}
is the number of
(d^{2}+d)
-tuples
(b_{0},b_{1},\ldots,b_{d^{2}+d-1})\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0\})^{d^{2}+d}
such that

\alpha x^{d^{2}+d}+b_{d^{2}+d-1}x^{d^{2}+d-1}+\ldots+b_{0}\equiv\beta\pmod{u},

which is equivalent to

\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b_{i}x^{i}\equiv\beta-\alpha x^{d^{2}+d}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}b_{i+jd}x^{i}\right)x^{jd}\pmod{u}.

Thus, it suffices to show that there exists
\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{d}\in S_{0}
such that

\beta-\alpha x^{d^{2}+d}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\gamma_{j}x^{jd}\in S_{0}.

Since
x\nmid u
, we know that
x^{-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]/(u)
. First we find
\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\ldots,\eta_{d}\in S_{0}
such that
\eta_{j}+x^{j(1-d)-1}\in S_{0}
for each
j\leq d
.
Indeed, fix
j
, and let
c_{j,0},c_{j,1},\ldots,c_{j,d-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
such that

x^{j(1-d)-1}\equiv\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}c_{j,i}x^{i}\pmod{u}.

Since
p>2
, there exist
a_{j,i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}\setminus\{0,-c_{j,i}\}
for each
i<d
. Then we have

\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}a_{j,i}x^{i}\in S_{0}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{i=0}^{d-1}(a_{j,i}+c_{j,i})x^{i}\in S_{0},

We take
\eta_{j}=a_{j,d-1}x^{d-1}+\ldots+a_{j,1}x+a_{j,0}
so that
\eta_{j}+c_{j}x^{j(1-d)-1}\in S_{0}
for both
c_{j}=0
and
c_{j}=1
.
Write

\beta-\alpha x^{d^{2}+d}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}\eta_{j}x^{jd}\equiv a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\dots+a_{1}x+a_{0}\pmod{u}

for some
a_{d-1},\ldots,a_{0}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
For any
j=1,\ldots,d
, we choose
c_{j}=1
if
a_{j-1}=0
; and
c_{j}=0
otherwise. Then we see that

\beta-\alpha x^{d^{2}+d}-\sum_{j=1}^{d}(\eta_{j}+c_{j}x^{j(1-d)-1})x^{jd}\equiv\sum_{j=1}^{d}(a_{j-1}-c_{j})x^{j-1}\in S_{0}.

The lemma is proved by taking
\gamma_{j}=\eta_{j}+c_{j}x^{j(1-d)-1}\in S_{0}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Consider an arbitrary
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}
of degree
d
such that
x\nmid u
.
By Lemma
4.9
, we have

E_{\min}\left(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{d^{2}+d}\right)\geq\frac{1}{(p-1)^{d^{2}+d}}\geq\frac{1}{p^{d^{2}+d}}.

By Lemma
4.7
and Lemma
4.4
,

E_{\min}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n})\geq\frac{1}{p^{d}}\left(1-\Big(1-\frac{p^{d}}{p^{d^{2}+d}}\Big)^{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}\right)\geq\frac{1}{p^{d}}-\frac{1}{p^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}{p^{d^{2}}}\right).

Since
\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n}
is doubly stochastic, it then follows that

E_{\max}(\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{p^{d}}+\frac{p^{d}-1}{p^{d}}\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}{p^{d^{2}}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{p^{d}}+\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}{p^{d^{2}}}\right).

As a result, the entries of
\widetilde{M}_{u}^{n}
are of distance at most
\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}{p^{d^{2}}}\right)
from
p^{-d}
.
Since this holds for all
u\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]_{m}
of degree
d
such that
x\nmid u
,

\delta_{n,p}(d)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{\lfloor n/(d^{2}+d)\rfloor}{p^{d^{2}}}\right)\leq e^{p^{-d^{2}}}\exp\left(-\frac{n}{(d^{2}+d)p^{d^{2}}}\right).

We are done, since
d\geq 1
and
p\geq 3
.
∎

4.3
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for squarefree density

We first consider
P^{\sqf}_{n,p}
. By Theorem
3.1
, it remains to prove that when
n\geq 16
,

\sum_{d=0}^{n}p^{d/2}\delta_{n,p}(d)\ll\min\left\{\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{5/2},\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{\lfloor\sqrt{\log n/\log p}\rfloor/2}\right\},

(14)

with the implied constant being absolute.

Using the bound
\delta_{n,p}(d)\ll 1/p^{d+2}
for
d\leq 4
from Theorem
4.1
and the trivial bound (
12
), we have

\sum_{d=0}^{n}p^{d/2}\delta_{n,p}(d)\ll\sum_{d=1}^{4}\frac{p^{d/2}}{p^{d+2}}+\sum_{d=5}^{\infty}\frac{p^{d/2}}{(p-1)^{d}}\ll\frac{1}{p^{5/2}}+\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{5/2}\ll\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{5/2}.

Suppose now
\log n/\log p\geq 25
, which is when the second term in (
14
) is smaller. Let
N_{0}=\lfloor\sqrt{\log n/\log p}\rfloor
. By Theorem
4.2
and the trivial bound (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{n}p^{d/2}\delta_{n,p}(d)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{N_{0}-1}p^{d/2}\exp\left(-\frac{n}{d(d+1)p^{d^{2}}}\right)+\sum_{d=N_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{p^{d/2}}{(p-1)^{d}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle p^{N_{0}/2}\exp\left(-\frac{n}{(N_{0}-1)N_{0}p^{(N_{0}-1)^{2}}}+N_{0}\right)+\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{N_{0}/2}.

Choosing
N_{0}^{2}\leq\log n/\log p
guarantees that

\frac{\log n}{\log p}\geq(N_{0}-1)^{2}+2(N_{0}-1)+1\geq(N_{0}-1)^{2}+\frac{3\log N_{0}}{\log p}+\frac{\log(\log(p-1)+1)}{\log p}

which implies that

n\geq p^{(N_{0}-1)^{2}}N_{0}^{3}(\log(p-1)+1)

and so

-\frac{n}{(N_{0}-1)N_{0}p^{(N_{0}-1)^{2}}}\leq-N_{0}\log(p-1)-N_{0}.

Hence, we have

\sum_{d=0}^{n}p^{d/2}\delta_{n,p}(d)\ll\left(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\right)^{\lfloor\sqrt{\log n/\log p}\rfloor/2}.

For
n\geq 16
, since the product

\prod_{p}\left(1-\frac{3p-1}{p(p+1)^{2}}+O\left(\Big(\frac{p}{(p-1)^{2}}\Big)^{5/2}\right)\right)

converges, we conclude from the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\left(\prod_{p>2}P^{\sqf}_{n,p}\right)=\prod_{p>2}\left(\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}P^{\sqf}_{n,p}\right)=\prod_{p>2}\left(1-\frac{3p-1}{p(p+1)^{2}}\right).

For arbitrary
n\geq 2
and any prime
p>2
, we know that
x^{n+1}-1
is squarefree in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
if
p\nmid n+1
and
x^{n+1}+x-2
is squarefree in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x]
if
p\mid n+1
. Dividing by
x-1
, we see that either
x^{n}+x^{n-1}+\cdots+x+1
or
x^{n}+x^{n-1}+\cdots+x+2
has squarefree discriminant and both have all their coefficients not divisible by
p
. Hence by the last statement of Theorem
1.3
, we know that
C_{n,2}^{\sqf}
is positive.

4.4
Proof of Theorem
1.1
for maximality density

We prove the similar statements for
P^{\max}_{n,p}
. By Theorem
3.2
, it remains to prove that when
n\geq 16
,

\sum_{d=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\delta_{n,p}(2d)\ll\min\left\{\frac{1}{(p-1)^{4}},\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2\lfloor\sqrt{\log n/(4\log p)}\rfloor}}\right\},

(15)

with the implied constant being absolute.

For the first bound, we use the estimate
\delta_{n,p}(2)\ll 1/p^{4}
by Theorem
4.1
and the trivial bound
\delta_{n,p}(2d)\leq 1/(p-1)^{2d}
to obtain

\sum_{d=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\delta_{n,p}(2d)\ll\frac{1}{p^{4}}+\sum_{d=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2d}}\ll\frac{1}{(p-1)^{4}}.

Now we prove the second bound when
\log n\geq 16\log p
. Let
N_{0}=\lfloor\sqrt{\log n/(4\log p)}\rfloor
.
By Theorem
4.2
and the trivial bound (
12
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{d=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\delta_{n,p}(2d)

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\sum_{d=1}^{N_{0}-1}\exp\left(-\frac{n}{2d(2d+1)p^{4d^{2}}}\right)+\sum_{d=N_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2d}}

\displaystyle\ll

\displaystyle\exp\left(-\frac{n}{(2N_{0}-2)(2N_{0}-1)p^{4(N_{0}-1)^{2}}}+2N_{0}\right)+\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2N_{0}}}.

Choosing
N_{0}^{2}\leq\log n/(4\log p)
guarantees

\frac{\log n}{\log p}\geq 4(N_{0}-1)^{2}+\frac{3\log(2N_{0})}{\log p}+\frac{\log(\log(p-1)+1)}{\log p}

which implies that

n\geq p^{4(N_{0}-1)^{2}}(8N_{0}^{3})(\log(p-1)+1)

and so

-\frac{n}{(2N_{0}-2)(2N_{0}-1)p^{4(N_{0}-1)^{2}}}\leq-2N_{0}(\log(p-1))-2N_{0}.

Hence

\sum_{d=0}^{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}\delta_{n,p}(2d)\ll\frac{1}{(p-1)^{2N_{0}}}.

Finally, the proofs for the convergence and the positivity of
C^{\max}_{n,2}
are exactly the same as those for
C^{\sqf}_{n,2}
in §4.3.

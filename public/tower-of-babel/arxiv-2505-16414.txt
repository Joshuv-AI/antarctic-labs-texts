Abstract
Let ( M , g ) (M,g) be a compact Riemann surface with unit area. We investigate the mean field equation for equilibrium turbulence: { − Δ ​ u = ρ 1 ​ ( h 1 ​ e u ∫ M h 1 ​ e u ​ d ​ v g − 1 ) − ρ 2 ​ ( h 2 ​ e − u ∫ M h 2 ​ e − u ​ d ​ v g − 1 ) , ∫ M u ​ d ​ v g = 0 , \displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\rho_{1}\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-1\right)-\rho_{2}\left(\frac{h_{2}e^{-u}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u}dv_{g}}-1\right),\\ \int_{M}udv_{g}=0,\end{cases} (0.1) where ρ 1 = 8 ​ π \rho_{1}=8\pi and ρ 2 ∈ ( 0 , 8 ​ π ] \rho_{2}\in(0,8\pi] are parameters, and h 1 , h 2 h_{1},h_{2} are smooth functions on M M that are positive somewhere. By employing a refined Brezis-Merle type analysis, we establish sufficient conditions of Ding-Jost-Li-Wang type for the existence of solutions to ( 0.1 ) in critical cases, particularly when h 1 h_{1} and h 2 h_{2} may change signs. Our results extend Zhou’s existence theorems (Nonlinear Anal. 69 (2008), no. 8, 2541–2552) for the case h 1 = h 2 ≡ 1 h_{1}=h_{2}\equiv 1 .



1
Introduction and main theorems

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area,
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\geq 0
be parameters, and
h_{1},h_{2}
be smooth functions on
M
that are positive somewhere. We study the mean field equation

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u=\rho_{1}\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-1\right)-\rho_{2}\left(\frac{h_{2}e^{-u}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u}dv_{g}}-1\right),\\
\int_{M}udv_{g}=0,\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
\Delta
denotes the Laplace-Beltrami operator and
dv_{g}
is the volume form on
(M,g)
.

This equation arises in mathematical physics through the works of Joyce and Montgomery
[
17
]
and Pointin and Lundgren
[
31
]
, where it models the mean field theory for equilibrium turbulence with vortices of arbitrary sign. Further physical background and related results can be found in
[
11
,
25
,
28
]
and references therein.

In this paper, we focus on the analytic aspects of equation (
1.1
), particularly its existence theory. Before presenting known results, we introduce necessary notations. Let
L^{p}(M)
(
p\geq 1
) and
W^{1,q}(M)
(
q\geq 1
) denote the standard Lebesgue and Sobolev spaces with respect to the volume measure
dv_{g}
, equipped with norms
\|\cdot\|_{p}
and
\|\cdot\|_{1,q}
respectively. Following convention, we write
H^{1}(M)=W^{1,2}(M)
and define

\displaystyle E=\left\{w\in H^{1}(M)\mid\int_{M}wdv_{g}=0\right\}.

Given that
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are positive somewhere, the admissible space

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\left\{w\in E\mid\int_{M}h_{1}e^{w}dv_{g}>0\text{ and }\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-w}dv_{g}>0\right\}

is nonempty. Note that
\mathcal{E}=E
when
h_{1},h_{2}>0
on
M
.

Equation (
1.1
) arises as the Euler-Lagrange equation for the functional

\displaystyle J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}-\rho_{1}\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}-\rho_{2}\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u}dv_{g}

defined on
\mathcal{E}
. A fundamental tool is the Moser-Trudinger type inequality established by Ohtsuka and Suzuki
[
30
]
in 2006:

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}e^{-u}dv_{g}\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}dv_{g}+C,\quad\forall u\in E,

(1.2)

where the constant
\frac{1}{16\pi}
is sharp. This inequality naturally leads to the following classification of (
1.1
) based on the parameters
(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
:

•

Subcritical case
:
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in[0,8\pi)

•

Critical case
:

–

Partial critical case: either
\rho_{1}=8\pi
with
\rho_{2}\in[0,8\pi)
or
\rho_{2}=8\pi
with
\rho_{1}\in[0,8\pi)

–

Full critical case:
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=8\pi

•

Supercritical case
: at least one
\rho_{i}>8\pi

From the inequality (
1.2
), it follows that in the subcritical case, the functional
J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}
is both bounded from below and coercive. Standard variational arguments then guarantee the attainment of its infimum.

The two partial critical cases are equivalent through the transformation
u\mapsto-u
in (
1.1
). When
\rho_{2}=0
, equation (
1.1
) reduces to

\displaystyle-\Delta u=8\pi\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-1\right),

(1.3)

which has been extensively studied in the literature. On the standard sphere, this becomes the celebrated Nirenberg problem. The existence theory for (
1.3
) has been developed in
[
26
,
27
,
20
,
15
,
8
,
5
,
6
,
14
,
4
,
32
,
10
]
and related works.

For general compact Riemann surfaces, this is known as the Kazdan-Warner problem. Let
K
denote the Gauss curvature of
(M,g)
. Ding-Jost-Li-Wang
[
12
]
pursued a variational approach and established that for strictly positive
h_{1}
, the condition

\displaystyle\Delta\log h_{1}(x)+8\pi-2K(x)>0\quad\forall x\in M,

(1.4)

guarantees the existence of a minimal type solution to (
1.3
). This is now referred to as the Ding-Jost-Li-Wang condition. Li-Zhu
[
23
]

gave a new proof based on flow methods. Subsequent work has extended this result in several directions:

•

For nonnegative
h_{1}
, Yang and the second author
[
38
]
showed that (
1.4
) remains sufficient when blow-up at zeros of
h_{1}
is excluded. This was later reproved by the first author and Zhu
[
33
]
using flow methods.

•

When
h_{1}
changes sign, the condition (
1.4
) restricted to the positive set
M_{1}^{+}=\{x\in M:h_{1}(x)>0\}
still ensures existence
[
34
,
24
,
37
,
40
]
.

In the partial critical case where
\rho_{2}\in(0,8\pi)
, as well as in the full critical case, Zhou
[
39
]
established an existence result for constant coefficient functions. Specifically, when
h_{1}=h_{2}\equiv 1
, the condition

\displaystyle(8\pi-\rho_{2})-2K(x)>0\quad\forall x\in M,

(1.5)

guarantees the existence of a minimal type solution to (
1.1
).

For the supercritical case, the functional
J_{\rho_{1},\rho_{2}}
fails to be bounded below due to the inequality (
1.2
), which precludes the existence of minimal type solutions. While we do not address the supercritical case in this work, we refer interested readers to
[
39
,
16
,
1
]
for developments in this direction.

The present paper focuses on the critical case of the mean field equation for equilibrium turbulence with general prescribed functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
. Our primary objective is to extend Zhou’s existence results to this more general setting, where the coefficient functions are no longer restricted to be constant.

In the partial critical case, we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area and Gauss curvature
K
, and let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{2}(M)
be functions that are positive somewhere. For
\rho_{2}\in(0,8\pi)
, define the positive set
M_{1}^{+}=\{x\in M:h_{1}(x)>0\}
. If the following condition holds:

\displaystyle\Delta\log h_{1}(x)+(8\pi-\rho_{2})-2K(x)>0\quad\forall x\in M_{1}^{+},

(1.6)

then the functional
J_{8\pi,\rho_{2}}
admits a minimizer
u\in\mathcal{E}
solving the equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta u=8\pi\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-1\right)-\rho_{2}\left(\frac{h_{2}e^{-u}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u}dv_{g}}-1\right).

(1.7)

In the full critical case, we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,g)
be a compact Riemann surface with unit area and Gauss curvature
K
, and let
h_{1},h_{2}\in C^{2}(M)
be functions that are positive somewhere. Define the positive sets
M_{i}^{+}=\{x\in M:h_{i}(x)>0\}
for
i=1,2
. If the following conditions are satisfied:

\displaystyle\Delta\log h_{i}(x)-2K(x)>0\quad\forall x\in M_{i}^{+},\quad i=1,2,

(1.8)

then the functional
J_{8\pi,8\pi}
admits a minimizer
u\in\mathcal{E}
solving the equation:

\displaystyle-\Delta u=8\pi\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u}dv_{g}}-\frac{h_{2}e^{-u}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u}dv_{g}}\right).

(1.9)

The Ding-Jost-Li-Wang type condition (as seen in (
1.4
)) arises naturally in the study of critical semilinear equations. Indeed, conditions (
1.5
), (
1.6
), and (
1.8
) all represent variations of this fundamental condition. This type condition also arise in the study of existence results for critical Toda systems
[
21
,
18
,
19
,
35
,
36
]
and the critical prescribed
Q
curvature problems
[
22
,
2
]
.

To end the introduction, we would like to sketch the proofs of our main theorems. For both Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
, we consider
the sequence of minimizers (denote by
u_{\epsilon}
) of the corresponding perturbed functionals. We analysis the minimizers carefully, there are two possibilities:
(1)
u_{\epsilon}
is convergent in
H^{1}(M)
;
(2)
u_{\epsilon}
or
-u_{\epsilon}
blows up.
If we are in (1), then we are done. So we assume
u_{\epsilon}
blows up. In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we show that
u_{\epsilon}
blows up only
at one point where
h_{1}
is positive,
-u_{\epsilon}\leq C
and does not blow up.
In the proof of Theorem
1.2
, there are three cases:

Case 1.
u_{\epsilon}
only blows up at one point where

h_{1}
is positive,
-u_{\epsilon}\leq C
and does not blow up.

Case 2.
u_{\epsilon}\leq C
and does not blow up and
-u_{\epsilon}
only blows up at one point where

h_{2}
is positive.

Case 3.
u_{\epsilon}
only blows up at one point
x_{1}
with
h_{1}(x_{1})>0
and
-u_{\epsilon}
only blows up at one point
x_{2}
with
h_{2}(x_{2})>0
, and
x_{1}\neq x_{2}
.

Based on these facts, using blowup analysis, we know the asymptotic property of
u_{\epsilon}
(or
-u_{\epsilon}
) near the blowup point. We can show

u_{\epsilon}
converges to some Green function. These combining with the Dirichlet principle can give estimate on the explicit lower bound for the functional when blowup happens. After that, we construct a sequence of test functions
\phi_{\epsilon}
such that under assumptions (
1.6
) (or (
1.8
)) the value of the functional at
\phi_{\epsilon}
is strictly smaller than the lower bound we derived. This contradiction tells us that
the blowup does not happen and we are in the first situation.

The outline of the rest of the paper is following: In Sections 2 and 3, we present the proofs of Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
respectively. Throughout the whole paper,
the constant
C
is varying from line to line and even in the same line, we do not distinguish
sequence and its subsequences since we only care about the existence result.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we shall prove Theorem
1.1
. Firstly, we do some analysis on
the partial critical case, i.e.,
\rho_{1}=8\pi
and
\rho_{2}\in(0,8\pi)
. Secondly, we derive the lower bound for
J_{8\pi,\rho_{2}}
by assuming

u_{\epsilon}
blows up. Lastly, we construct a blowup sequence
\phi_{\epsilon}
such that
J_{8\pi,\rho_{2}}(\phi_{\epsilon})
is smaller than the lower bound, which
contradicts
u_{\epsilon}
blows up.

2.1
Analysis for the partial critical case

For any
{\epsilon}>0
small, inequality (
1.2
) shows that there exists a
u_{\epsilon}\in\mathcal{E}
such that

\displaystyle J_{8\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{\epsilon})=\inf_{\mathcal{E}}J_{8\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u)

(2.1)

and

\displaystyle\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{\epsilon}=(8\pi-{\epsilon})\left(\frac{h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}-1\right)-\rho_{2}\left(\frac{h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}-1\right),\\
\int_{M}u_{\epsilon}dv_{g}=0.\end{cases}

(2.2)

In this subsection, we shall study analytic properties of
u_{\epsilon}
.

First of all, we have
\|\Delta u_{\epsilon}\|_{1}\leq C
. In fact, we have

Lemma 2.1
.

There exist two positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\frac{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}},~\frac{\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C_{2}.

Proof.

Since
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
are positive somewhere, one can choose

C_{1}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{\max_{M}h_{1}},\frac{1}{\max_{M}h_{2}}\right\}

such that

\displaystyle C_{1}\leq\frac{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}~~\text{and}~~C_{1}\leq\frac{\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}.

(2.3)

By (
1.2
) and (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq\frac{1}{16\pi}\int_{M}|\nabla u_{\epsilon}|^{2}dv_{g}+C

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\pi}\left(J_{8\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{\epsilon})+(8\pi-{\epsilon})\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\rho_{2}\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C+\frac{8\pi-{\epsilon}}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}.

(2.4)

Using Jensen’s inequality, we know
\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\geq 0
and
\log\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\geq 0
. Then we have by (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi-{\epsilon}}{8\pi}\log\frac{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{8\pi}\log\frac{\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C.

This together with (
2.3
) yields that there exists a constant
C_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\frac{\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C_{2}~~\text{and}~~\frac{\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C_{2}.

We finish the proof of the lemma.
∎

Secondly, the
L^{s}
norm of
\nabla u_{\epsilon}
is bounded for any
s\in(1,2)
. Namely, one has

Lemma 2.2
.

For any
s\in(1,2)
,
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{s}\leq C
.

Proof.

Let
s^{\prime}=1/s>2
, we know by definition that

\displaystyle\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{s}=\sup\left\{\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\nabla\phi dv_{g}\right|:\phi\in W^{1,s^{\prime}}(M),\int_{M}\phi dv_{g}=0,\|\phi\|_{1,s^{\prime}}=1\right\}.

The Sobolev embedding theorem shows that
\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
for some constant
C
.
Meanwhile, Lemma
2.1
tells us that
\|\Delta u_{\epsilon}\|_{1}
is bounded. Then it follows from equation (
2.2
) that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{M}\nabla u_{\epsilon}\nabla\phi dv_{g}\right|=\left|\int_{M}\phi(-\Delta u_{\epsilon})dv_{g}\right|\leq C.

This ends the proof.
∎

We denote by

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}=\max_{M}\left(u_{\epsilon}-\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\right),~~n_{\epsilon}=\max_{M}\left(-u_{\epsilon}-\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\right).

Lemma 2.3
.

The following three items are equivalent:

(i)

m_{{\epsilon}}+n_{{\epsilon}}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
;

(ii)

\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
;

(iii)

\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
.

Proof.

(i)\Rightarrow(ii)
: Suppose not, then
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
. Since
\overline{u_{\epsilon}}=0
, we know by the Moser-Trudinger inequality that

e^{|u_{\epsilon}|}
is bounded in
L^{s}(M)
for any
s\geq 1
. Using Jensen’s inequality,
\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\geq e^{\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}=1
and

\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\geq e^{-\overline{u_{\epsilon}}}=1
. This together with Lemma
2.1
shows that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C_{2}~~\text{and}~~\frac{1}{\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}}\leq C_{2}.

(2.5)

Taking these facts into (
2.2
) we have
\|\Delta u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
. By Poincaré’s inequality,
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
.
Then by the elliptic estimates,
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2,2}\leq C
. The Sobolev embedding theorem shows
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{C^{1}(M)}\leq C
. It together with
(
2.5
) yields
m_{\epsilon}\leq C
and
n_{\epsilon}\leq C
. This contradicts
(i)
.

(ii)\Rightarrow(iii)
: Since
J_{8\pi-{\epsilon},\rho_{2}}(u_{\epsilon})
is bounded, we have

\displaystyle(8\pi-{\epsilon})\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\rho_{2}\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\to+\infty

(2.6)

as
{\epsilon}\to 0
. Combining (
2.5
) and (
2.6
) we arrive at
(iii)
.

(iii)\Rightarrow(i)
: If not, we have
m_{\epsilon}+n_{\epsilon}\leq C
. By Lemma
2.1
, one has
m_{\epsilon},n_{\epsilon}\geq\log C_{1}
. Then both

m_{\epsilon}
and
n_{\epsilon}
are bounded. This together with (
2.5
) yields
\|\Delta u_{\epsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(M)}\leq C
. By Lemma
2.2

and the Sobolev inequality we obtain
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
. Then from the elliptic estimates we have
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{2,2}\leq C
. The Sobolev embedding
theorem shows
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{C^{1}(M)}\leq C
. Then
\max_{M}|u_{\epsilon}|\leq C
and

\displaystyle C\geq

\displaystyle\max_{M}u_{\epsilon}+\max_{M}(-u_{\epsilon})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{\epsilon}+\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+n_{\epsilon}+\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2\log C_{1}+\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}.

(2.7)

Since (
2.1
) contradicts
(iii)
, we have
(i)
. This finishes the proof.
∎

Definition 2.1

(Blow up)
.

If one of the three items in Lemma
2.3
holds, we call
u_{\epsilon}
blows up.

For
(u_{\epsilon})
, there are two possibilities: One is
\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\leq C
, then since
\overline{u_{\epsilon}}=0
we have by
Poincaré’s inequality that
\|u_{\epsilon}\|_{1,2}\leq C
, one can show
u_{\epsilon}\rightharpoonup u_{0}\in\mathcal{E}

as
{\epsilon}\to 0

and
u_{0}
minimizes

in
\mathcal{E}
, then the proof of Theorem
1.1
can terminate. Or else,

\|\nabla u_{\epsilon}\|_{2}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
. By Lemma
2.2
and Definition
2.1
,
u_{\epsilon}
blows up. In the rest of the proof of

1.1
, we always assume
u_{\epsilon}
blows up.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, then

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\to+\infty~\text{as}~{\epsilon}\to 0~\text{and}~~\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq C.

Proof.

In view of (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\log\max_{M}h_{1}+\log\max_{M}h_{2}+\log\int_{M}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\log\int_{M}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{8\pi-{\epsilon}}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\frac{\rho_{2}}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+C,

which yields

\displaystyle\frac{{\epsilon}}{8\pi}\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}+\left(1-\frac{\rho_{2}}{8\pi}\right)\log\int_{M}h_{2}e^{-u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\leq C.

This together with (

) shows

. Since
u_{\epsilon}
blows up, by Lemma
2.3
we know

\log\int_{M}h_{1}e^{u_{\epsilon}}dv_{g}\to+\infty
as
{\epsilon}\to 0
. This finishes the proof.

blows up.

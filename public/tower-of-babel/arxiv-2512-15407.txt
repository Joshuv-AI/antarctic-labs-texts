No exact on average additive complements of squares
Yuchen Ding and Zihan Zhang

Abstract
. Let ℕ \mathbb{N} be the set of natural numbers and 𝒮 = { 1 2 , 2 2 , 3 2 , ⋯ } \mathcal{S}=\big\{1^{2},2^{2},3^{2},\cdots\big\} the set of squares. Let 𝒲 \mathcal{W} be an additive complement of 𝒮 \mathcal{S} and f ⁡ ( n ) = # ⁡ { ( w , m 2 ) ∈ 𝒲 × 𝒮 : n = w + m 2 } . f(n)=\#\big\{(w,m^{2})\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{S}:n=w+m^{2}\big\}. It is proved that there is an absolute constant c 0 > 0 c_{0}>0 such that for all large N N we have ∑ n ≤ N f ⁡ ( n ) − N ≥ c 0 ​ N , \sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq c_{0}N, which makes some further progress on a 1993 conjecture of Cilleruelo. As consequences of this result, we answer negatively a 2001 problem of Ruzsa as well as a 2017 problem of Ben Green.

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of natural numbers and
\mathcal{S}=\big\{1^{2},2^{2},3^{2},\cdots\big\}
the set of squares. A subset
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathbb{N}
is called an
additive complement
of
\mathcal{S}
if all sufficiently large integers can be written as the sum of an element of
\mathcal{W}
and an element of
\mathcal{S}
. Erdős
[
12
]
observed that if
\mathcal{W}
is an additive complement of
\mathcal{S}
, then one clearly has

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}\geq 1,

where
\mathcal{W}(N)=\big|\mathcal{W}\cap[1,N]\big|
.
A natural problem was then asked by Erdős
[
12
]
whether

\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}

is strictly greater than 1. Erdős’ problem was answered affirmatively by Moser
[
15
]
with an accurate lower bound
1.06
. Moser’s bound was later improved several times in a few articles, see Abbott
[
1
]
, Balasubramanian
[
2
]
, Balasubramanian and Soundararajan
[
4
]
, Donagi and Herzog
[
11
]
, Ramna
[
16
,
17
]
. Up to now, the best record of the relevant lower bound is

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}\geq\frac{4}{\pi},

(1.1)

given independently by Cilleruelo
[
8
]
, Habsieger
[
14
]
, Balasubramanian and Ramana
[
3
]
.

Ben Green observed that for

\mathcal{W}=\{w_{1}<w_{2}<\cdots\}

with

\displaystyle w_{n}\sim\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}n^{2},\quad\text{or~equivalently}\quad\mathcal{W}(N)\sim\frac{4}{\pi}\sqrt{N}

(1.2)

as
n\rightarrow\infty
(resp.
N\rightarrow\infty
)
we have

\frac{1}{N}\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}f(n)=1,

where

f(n)=f_{\mathcal{W},\mathcal{S}}(n):=\#\big\{(w,m^{2})\in\mathcal{W}\times\mathcal{S}:n=w+m^{2}\big\}.

Ben Green then asked Fang (private communication, see the comments in
[
7
]
) whether there is an additive complement
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{S}
such that
\mathcal{W}
satisfies the growth condition of (
1.2
). Formally,

Problem 1

(Ben Green, 2017)
.

Does not exist an additive complement
\mathcal{W}=\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of squares such that

w_{n}\sim\frac{\pi^{2}n^{2}}{16}
, as
n\rightarrow\infty
?

Motivated by this, Chen and Fang
[
7
]
proved that

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\gg N^{1/4}\log N,

(1.3)

provided that
\mathcal{W}
is an additive complement of
\mathcal{S}
.
As an application, Chen and Fang showed that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}n^{2}-w_{n}}{n^{1/2}\log n}\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}\frac{1}{\log 4}

for any additive complement
\mathcal{W}=\{w_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
of
\mathcal{S}
, which was later improved by the author
[
9
]
to

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}n^{2}-w_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{\pi}{4}.

(1.4)

In a recent article, the author, Sun, Wang and Xia
[
10
]
improved the bound (
1.3
) to

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq 0.193N^{1/2}.

(1.5)

As an application of (
1.5
), the authors obtained

\displaystyle\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{\frac{\pi^{2}}{16}n^{2}-w_{n}}{n}\geq\frac{\pi}{4}+\frac{0.193\pi^{2}}{8},

(1.6)

which is a slight improvement of (
1.4
). It could be mentioned that the method given in
[
9
]
and
[
10
]

does not serve to show that the right-hand side of (
1.6
) is
\infty
.

Actually, there have been relevant literature on Problem
1
. When he was just a high school student, Ruzsa
[
19
]
proved that there is a subset
\mathcal{A}\subset\mathbb{N}
with
\mathcal{A}(x)\ll x/\log x
such that every sufficiently large number is of the form
2^{k}+a_{i}~(k\in\mathbb{N},\ a_{i}\in A)
, thus answering affirmatively a
\$10
prized problem of Erdős
[
13
]
(see the detailed comments in
[
6
, Problem
\#
221]
). Thirty years later, Ruzsa
[
18
]
proved that the set of powers of
2
has an exact additive complement which answered affirmatively another problem of Erdős. Here, an exact additive complement
\mathcal{A}
of powers of
2
means that

\mathcal{A}(x)\log x/\log 2\sim x,\ ~\text{as~}x\rightarrow\infty.

Ruzsa further introduced the
exact on average additive complements
terminology. A subset
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathbb{N}
is called an exact on average additive complement of
\mathcal{S}
if

\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)\sim N,\ ~\text{as~}N\rightarrow\infty.

Ruzsa
[
18
, three lines above Acknowledgement]
then asked the following problem.

Problem 2

(Ruzsa, 2001)
.

Does the set of squares have an exact on average additive complement?

In fact, as early as 1993 Cilleruelo
[
8
, two lines above Open Problem]
already asked the same one as Problem
2
, and expected a negative answer to it.
Cilleruelo also conjectured that for any additive complement of squares we have

\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq N+o(N).

Certainly, a positive answer of Cilleruelo’s conjecture would lead to negative answers of Problems
1
and
2
. Unfortunately, it seems the resolution of this conjecture is apparently too much to hope for at present. The bounds (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) made some progress on Cilleruelo’s conjecture. The following new theorem not only improves upon the bounds (
1.3
) and (
1.5
), but also leads to some interesting corollaries which provide negative answers to both Problem
1
and Problem
2
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an additive complement of squares. Then we have

\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}N,

provided that
N
is sufficiently large, where

\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\approx 5.463\cdot 10^{-5}.

Corollary 1
.

The set of squares does not have an exact on average additive complement.

Corollary
1
provides negative answers to both Problem
1
and Problem
2
.

Corollary 2
.

Let
\mathcal{W}
be an additive complement of the set of squares. Then we have

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}\geq\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\right).

Proof.

Assume the contrary, i.e.,

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}<\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\right).

Then we have

\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)<\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\right)\sqrt{N},

and hence

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)

\displaystyle=\sum_{w+m^{2}\leq N}1

\displaystyle=\sum_{m<\sqrt{N}}\mathcal{W}\big(N-m^{2}\big)

\displaystyle<\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\right)\frac{4}{\pi}\sum_{m<\sqrt{N}}\sqrt{N-m^{2}}

\displaystyle=N+\frac{N}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)},

which is a contradiction with Theorem
1
.
∎

In the website of Erdos’ problems
[
5
, Problem
\#
33]
, it recorded that Erdős asked the smallest value of

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}

which will be denoted by
L_{\mathcal{S}}
, where
\mathcal{W}
is an additive complement of the set of squares. Corollary
2
gives the first nontrivial lower bound on the superior limit.
It also mentioned that van Doorn has a construction of additive complement
\mathcal{W}_{0}
of
\mathcal{S}
such that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{0}\big(N\big)/\sqrt{N}\leq 2\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{5/2}\approx 6.66.

Thus, by van Doorn’s construction and Corollary
2
we now have

\frac{4}{\pi}\left(1+\frac{1}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)}\right)\leq L_{\mathcal{S}}\leq 2\left(\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)^{5/2}.

2.
Proofs

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
N
be sufficiently large throughout our proof and
K
a given (large) integer to be decided later.
For any
1\leq k\leq K
, let

\mathcal{W}_{k,N}=\mathcal{W}\cap\left[\frac{k-1}{2K}N,\ \frac{k}{2K}N\right),\quad h(k)=\big|\mathcal{W}_{k,N}\big|

and

\mathcal{S}_{k,N}=\mathcal{S}\cap\left[\frac{k-1}{2K}N,\ \frac{k}{2K}N\right),\quad g(k)=\big|\mathcal{S}_{k,N}\big|.

Then, clearly we have

\displaystyle g(k)

\displaystyle=\Big(1+o(1)\Big)\sqrt{\frac{N}{2K}}\Big(\sqrt{k}-\sqrt{k-1}\Big).

(2.1)

For any
1\leq k\leq K
let

A(k)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h(i).

Then by the condition (
1.1
) we have

\displaystyle A(k)=\mathcal{W}\left(\frac{kN}{2K}\right)\geq\left(\frac{4}{\pi}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{kN}{2K}}.

(2.2)

One notes easily that for any
1\leq k\leq K
and
w\in\mathcal{W}_{k,N},\ m^{2}\in\mathcal{S}_{k,N}
we have

w-m^{2}\in\left(-\frac{N}{2K},\frac{N}{2K}\right),

and the number of all such differences
w-m^{2}
above equals

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}h(k)g(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\Big(A(k)-A(k-1)\Big)g(k)

\displaystyle=A(K)g(K)+\sum_{1\leq k\leq K-1}A(k)\Big(g(k)-g(k+1)\Big),

(2.3)

where the last equality used the Abel summation formula. In view of (
2.1
), we know that
g(k)>g(k+1)
for any
1\leq k\leq K
. Hence, using again the Abel summation formula we deduce from (
2
) and (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}

\displaystyle h(k)g(k)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{4}{\pi}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{KN}{2K}}g(K)+\sum_{1\leq k\leq K-1}\left(\frac{4}{\pi}+o(1)\right)\sqrt{\frac{kN}{2K}}\Big(g(k)-g(k+1)\Big)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{\pi}+o(1)\right)\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\left(\sqrt{\frac{kN}{2K}}-\sqrt{\frac{(k-1)N}{2K}}\right)g(k)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{4}{\pi}+o(1)\right)\frac{N}{2K}\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\left(\sqrt{k}-\sqrt{k-1}\right)^{2}.

(2.4)

It is clear that

\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\left(\sqrt{k}-\sqrt{k-1}\right)^{2}\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}\frac{1}{k}>\frac{\log(K+1)}{4},

from which we conclude

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq k\leq K}h(k)g(k)\geq\frac{N\log K}{2\pi K}.

(2.5)

Let
d\in\left(-\frac{N}{2K},\frac{N}{2K}\right)
be a number which is attained by the differences
w-m^{2}
. Suppose that
\ell_{d}
is the number of pairs
\big(w,m^{2}\big)
such that
d=w-m^{2}
. Then by (
2.5
) we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\frac{N}{2K}<d<\frac{N}{2K}\\
\ell_{d}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\ell_{d}\geq\frac{N\log K}{2\pi K},

from which it follows that

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\frac{N}{2K}<d<\frac{N}{2K}\\
\ell_{d}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\ell_{d}\geq\frac{N\log K}{2\pi K}-\frac{N}{K}=\frac{N}{K}\left(\frac{\log K}{2\pi}-1\right).

(2.6)

Now, for any
d\in\left(-\frac{N}{2K},\frac{N}{2K}\right)
with
\ell_{d}\geq 2
we assume that

d=w_{1}-m_{1}^{2}=w_{2}-m_{2}^{2}=\cdots=w_{\ell_{d}}-m_{\ell_{d}}^{2}.

For any
1\leq i\neq j\leq\ell_{d}
we see that

w_{i}-m_{i}^{2}=w_{j}-m_{j}^{2}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle w_{i}+m_{j}^{2}=w_{j}+m_{i}^{2}<\frac{N}{2}+\frac{N}{2}=N.

(2.7)

A little thought from (
2.7
) leads to the following inequality

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\frac{N}{2K}<d<\frac{N}{2K}\\
\ell_{d}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\binom{\ell_{d}}{2}.

(2.8)

Inserting (
2.6
) into (
2.8
) we get

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\frac{N}{2K}<d<\frac{N}{2K}\\
\ell_{d}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\ell_{d}^{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}-\frac{N}{2K}<d<\frac{N}{2K}\\
\ell_{d}\geq 2\end{subarray}}\ell_{d}\geq\frac{N}{2K}\left(\frac{\log K}{2\pi}-1\right).

(2.9)

We now try to find the optimal
K
. Taking

K=\Big\lfloor e^{1+2\pi}\Big\rfloor+1
in (
2.9
), we then get

\sum_{n\leq N}f(n)-N\geq\frac{N}{4\pi\big(e^{1+2\pi}+1\big)},

proving our theorem.
∎

3.
Generalizations

In
[
8
]
, Cilleruelo also considered the additive complements of the set of
r
-th powers, where
r\geq 2
is a given positive integer. Let
\mathcal{W}_{r}
be an additive complement of the set

\mathcal{S}_{r}=\big\{m^{r}:m\in\mathbb{N}\big\},
i.e., the set of
r
-th powers.
Then Cilleruelo proved that

\displaystyle\liminf_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{r}\big(N\big)/N^{1-1/r}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(2-\frac{1}{r}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)},

(3.1)

where
\Gamma
is Euler’s gamma function.
For any
r\geq 2
he further conjectured that

\sum_{n\leq N}f_{r}(n)\geq rN+o(N),

where

f_{r}(n)=f_{\mathcal{W}_{r},\mathcal{S}_{r}}(n):=\#\big\{(w,m^{r})\in\mathcal{W}_{r}\times\mathcal{S}_{r}:n=w+m^{r}\big\}.

Note that if

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{r}\big(N\big)\sim\frac{N^{1-1/r}}{\Gamma\left(2-\frac{1}{r}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)},\ ~\text{as~}N\rightarrow\infty,

Then by standard computations involving Euler’s gamma function we will have

\displaystyle\sum_{n\leq N}f_{r}(n)\sim N,\ ~\text{as~}N\rightarrow\infty.

(3.2)

In view of (
3.1
) and (
3.2
). Theorem
1
can be naturally extended to the following slightly generalized theorem if one takes a close look at the argument of it.

Theorem 2
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\mathcal{W}_{r}
be an additive complement of

\mathcal{S}_{r}
.
Then there is a constant
c_{r}>0

(
depending only on
r
)
such that for all large
N
we have

\sum_{n\leq N}f_{r}(n)-N\geq c_{r}N,

where

c_{r}\approx\frac{r-1}{4r^{2}\Gamma(1+1/r)\Gamma(2-1/r)e^{1+\frac{2r^{2}\Gamma(1+1/r)\Gamma(2-1/r)}{r-1}}}.

We remark that for
r\geq 3
the methods given in
[
7
]
and
[
10
]
could not even yield

\Big(\sum_{n\leq N}f_{r}(n)-N\Big)\rightarrow\infty,\ ~\text{as~}N\rightarrow\infty.

As consequences of Theorem
2
, we have the following corollaries.

Corollary 3
.

The set
\mathcal{S}_{r}
does not have an exact on average additive complement.

Corollary 4
.

Let
r\geq 2
and
\mathcal{W}_{r}
be an additive complement of the set of
r
-th powers. Then there is some constant
c_{r}>0
depending only on
r
such that

\limsup_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{W}_{r}\big(N\big)/N^{1-1/r}\geq\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(2-\frac{1}{r}\right)\Gamma\left(1+\frac{1}{r}\right)}(1+c_{r}).

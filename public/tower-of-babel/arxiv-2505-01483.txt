On the new way of symmetry breaking in scalar QED and the one-loop renormalization


Abstract
It is well known that single real scalar field does not allow gauge coupling to the Abelian vector field. Using the complex scalar model as a starting point, we construct the Abelian gauge model with two real scalars. The gauge transformations for the scalars look different (albeit equivalent) from the conventional sQED. Spitting the masses of the scalars, or the scalar self-couplings, or the nonminimal parameters of scalar-curvature interaction, we arrive at a qualitatively new way of gauge symmetry breaking. Using the Schwinger-DeWitt technique, we explore the one-loop renormalization of this new model in curved spacetime. Keywords: Explicit symmetry breaking, Abelian gauge theory, Effective action, Schwinger-DeWitt technique MSC: 81T10, 81T15, 81T17, 81T20

Introduction

Gauge symmetry cannot be defined for a theory with single real
scalar field, as it is impossible to couple gauge field
A_{\mu}
to
such a scalar. On the other hand, the theory of a complex scalar
field plays a fundamental role in describing gauge interactions of
spin-zero particles, especially the Higgs boson. Following one of the
exercises in the recent textbook
[
2
]
, we note that the Abelian
gauge model of complex scalar can be reformulated in terms of two
real scalar fields. In contrast, the rule of the gauge transformation
gets modified to preserve gauge invariance. The scheme is not
affected by the presence of an external gravitational field. This is
a potentially interesting detail because the models with two real
scalars have applications in the inflationary cosmological models
(see, e.g.,

[
3
,
4
,
5
]
). Typically, these
models of “assisted inflation” are characterized by a strong mass
hierarchy. Furthermore, we note that the generalization of the new
representation to the nonabelian case looks straightforward,
regardless it lies beyond the scope of the present work. In
particular, one can construct such representation for the
SU(2)

doublet describing the Higgs field.

Looking at the situation from the perspective of the Higgs field, one
can ask whether the representation of the well-known gauge theory
in terms of the two real scalar fields can lead to an alternative
mechanism for gauge symmetry breaking. To investigate this
possibility, we consider an action in which the two real scalar
fields acquire different masses or/and different nonminimal couplings
to the scalar curvature, or get a symmetry violation from the
self-interaction of the two scalars. All these possibilities lead to the

explicit
symmetry breaking, in contrast to the known ways
(spontaneous or dynamical) of symmetry breaking, which can be
used to generate a non-zero mass for the gauge vector field.

At very high energies, typical for inflation, the quantum effects may
be relevant. It is worth mentioning the recent investigation of such
effects in the multiscalar model
[
6
]
, including curved-space
effective potential, derivative expansion in the one-loop effective
action, and the analysis of low-energy decoupling. It would be
interesting to check whether the explicit symmetry breaking described
above might lead to a consistent quantum theory. In particular, one
can ask whether the theory with the new type of
symmetry breaking may be renormalizable. To address this question,
we evaluate the one-loop renormalization for the theory with broken
symmetry in curved spacetime with a metric
g_{\mu\nu}
.

The manuscript is organized as follows. Sec.
2
describes
the classical gauge model with two scalar fields and introduces the
explicit symmetry breaking. In Sec.
3
, we derive the
one-loop counterterms and show that the theory with broken symmetry
is renormalizable, at least at this level. Sec.
4
describes
the renormalization group equations and explores the IR limit of the
effective charges corresponding to the broken symmetry. Finally,
in Sec.
5
, we draw our conclusions and outline possible
extensions of the present work.

Classical action and gauge invariance

Our starting point is the Abelian gauge theory for a complex scalar
field,

\displaystyle S_{c}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\Big\{g^{\mu\nu}(D_{\mu}\phi)^{*}\,(D_{\nu}\phi)-m^{2}\phi\phi^{*}+\xi R\phi\phi^{*}-\frac{\lambda}{12}\,(\phi\phi^{*})^{2}\Big\},

(1)

where
m
is the mass of the scalar field,
\xi
is the parameter of
nonminimal scalar-curvature interaction,
\lambda
is the self-interaction
coupling constant, the covariant derivatives are

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\phi=\nabla_{\mu}\phi-igA_{\mu}\phi\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad(D_{\nu}\phi)^{*}=\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{*}+igA_{\nu}\phi^{*}

(2)

and, finally, we use the abbreviations

\displaystyle\int_{x,n}\,\,=\,\,\int d^{n}x\sqrt{-g}.\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\int_{x}\,\,=\,\,\int_{x,4}.

(3)

The field transformations ensuring
the gauge invariance of Eq. (
1
) are

\displaystyle{A^{\prime}}_{\mu}\;=\;A_{\mu}+\nabla_{\mu}f,\qquad\phi^{\prime}\;=\;e^{igf}\phi\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad f=f(x).

(4)

One can express the complex field in terms of two real scalars

\varphi
and
\chi
as

\displaystyle\phi\;=\;\varphi\,+\,i\chi.

(5)

Substituting this decomposition into Eq. (
1
),
we arrive at

\displaystyle S_{r}\,\,=\,\,\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{x}\,\Big\{(\nabla\varphi)^{2}+(\nabla\chi)^{2}+g^{2}A^{2}\,\big(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2}\big)-2gA^{\mu}\,\big(\varphi\nabla_{\mu}\chi-\chi\nabla_{\mu}\varphi\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad-\,\,m^{2}\,\big(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2}\big)+\xi R\,\big(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2}\big)-\frac{\lambda}{12}\,\big(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2}\big)^{2}\Big\}\,.

(6)

The transformations of scalars providing the gauge invariance
in Eq. (
6
) have the form

\displaystyle\varphi^{\prime}\;=\;\varphi\cos(gf)-\chi\sin(gf)\,,

(7)

\displaystyle\chi^{\prime}\;=\;\varphi\sin(gf)+\chi\cos(gf)\,.

We can break the gauge symmetry by introducing a mass-splitting
in the scalar sector, or/and by choosing different nonminimal
couplings to the scalar curvature, or introduce the symmetry
violation in the scalar self-interaction sector,

\displaystyle S_{bs}\,

\displaystyle=\int_{x}\,\Big\{\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\chi)^{2}+\frac{1}{2}g^{2}A^{2}(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})-gA^{\mu}(\varphi\nabla_{\mu}\chi-\chi\nabla_{\mu}\varphi)

\displaystyle\quad-\,\frac{1}{2}m^{2}\varphi^{2}-\frac{1}{2}M^{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\xi R\varphi^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\Xi R\chi^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{24}(\varphi^{4}+\chi^{4})-\frac{\lambda_{12}}{12}\varphi^{2}\chi^{2}\Big\}.

(8)

It is easy to see that
m
and
\xi
are associated with the
scalar field
\varphi
, while
M
and
\Xi
correspond to
\chi
.
Furthermore,
\lambda
is the self-interaction coupling constant, and

\lambda_{12}
is a new coupling, which represents the mixed
interaction term and symmetry breaking. In the limit

\displaystyle M\,\longrightarrow\,m,\quad\Xi\,\longrightarrow\,\xi,\quad\lambda_{12}\,\longrightarrow\,\lambda

(9)

we come back to the theory (
6
), that is equivalent to
the original (
1
).

One-loop divergences

In this section, we compute the one-loop divergences for a theory
with two scalar fields possessing an Abelian gauge symmetry.

\displaystyle S\,\,=\,\,S_{bs}\,\,-\,\,\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,.

(10)

Using the background field method, we split

\displaystyle\varphi\to\varphi^{\prime}\,=\,\varphi+\rho,\qquad\chi\to\chi^{\prime}\,=\,\chi+\sigma,\qquad A_{\mu}\to A_{\mu}^{\prime}\,=\,A_{\mu}+B_{\mu},

(11)

with
\rho
,
\sigma
, and
B_{\mu}
are quantum fields. The divergences
are defined by the part of the action that is bilinear in the quantum
fields.

The action of the gauge sector can be rewritten as

-\frac{1}{4}\int_{x}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,=\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}B^{\mu}\big(\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}\Box-R_{\mu}^{\nu}\big)B_{\nu}\,+\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\big(\nabla_{\mu}B^{\mu}\big)^{2}

(12)

and has to be extended by adding a gauge fixing term in the
framework of the Faddeev-Popov method. The simplest option is

\displaystyle S_{gf}\,=\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\int_{x}\big(\nabla_{\mu}B^{\mu}\big)^{2}\,.

(13)

After this, the second-order expansion of the action is given by

\displaystyle S^{(2)}\,\,=\,\,-\,\frac{1}{2}\,\int_{x}\big(\,\rho\,\,\,\sigma\,\,B^{\mu}\,\big)\begin{pmatrix}H_{11}&H_{12}&H_{13}\\
H_{21}&H_{22}&H_{23}\\
H_{31}&H_{32}&H_{33}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\rho\\
\sigma\\
B_{\nu}\end{pmatrix},

(14)

where the matrix operator
\hat{H}
is hermitian. The components are

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{11}&\,=\,\Box+m^{2}-\xi R-g^{2}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\,\chi^{2}\,,\\
H_{12}&\,=\,2gA^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}+g\,(\nabla A)+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}\,\varphi\chi\,,\\
H_{13}&\,=\,-2g^{2}A^{\nu}\varphi+2g\,(\nabla^{\nu}\chi)+g\chi\nabla^{\nu}\,,\\
H_{21}&\,=\,-2gA^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}-g\,(\nabla A)+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}\,\varphi\chi\,,\\
H_{22}&\,=\,\Box+M^{2}-\Xi R-g^{2}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\,\chi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\,\varphi^{2}\,,\\
H_{23}&=-2g^{2}A^{\nu}\chi-2g\,(\nabla^{\nu}\varphi)-g\,\varphi\nabla^{\nu}\,,\\
H_{31}&=-2g^{2}A_{\mu}\varphi+g\,(\nabla_{\mu}\chi)-g\chi\nabla_{\mu}\,,\\
H_{32}&=-2g^{2}A_{\mu}\chi-g\,(\nabla_{\mu}\varphi)+g\varphi\nabla_{\mu}\,,\\
H_{33}&\,=\,-\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}\Box+R_{\mu}^{\nu}-\delta_{\mu}^{\nu}g^{2}\,(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})\,.\end{aligned}

(15)

The matrix
\hat{H}
can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\hat{H}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}+1&0&0\\
0&+1&0\\
0&0&-1\end{pmatrix}\hat{H^{\prime}}\,.

(16)

The first factor
\,\mbox{diag}\,(1,\,1,\,-1)
does not contribute to the
divergences and
\hat{H}^{\prime}
has the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}^{\prime}\,=\,\hat{1}\Box+2\hat{h}^{\alpha}\,\nabla_{\alpha}+\hat{\Pi}\,,

(17)

such that one can employ the standard Schwinger-DeWitt
technique
[
7
,
8
]
for computing the
divergences of
\,\mbox{Tr}\,\log\hat{H}^{\prime}
. The elements of the operator
(
17
) have the form

\displaystyle 2\hat{h^{\alpha}}\,=\,\begin{pmatrix}0&2gA^{\alpha}&g\chi g^{\alpha\nu}\\
-2gA^{\alpha}&0&-g\varphi g^{\alpha\nu}\\
g\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}\chi&-g\delta^{\alpha}_{\mu}\varphi&0\end{pmatrix}\quad\mbox{and}\,\,\quad\hat{\Pi}\,=\,\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\Pi_{11}&\Pi_{12}&\Pi_{13}\\
\Pi_{21}&\Pi_{22}&\Pi_{23}\\
\Pi_{31}&\Pi_{32}&\Pi_{33}\end{array}\right),\qquad

where

\displaystyle\Pi_{11}\,=\,m^{2}-\xi R-g^{2}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\chi^{2},

\displaystyle\Pi_{12}\,=\,\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}\varphi\chi+g(\nabla A),

\displaystyle\Pi_{13}\,=\,-2g^{2}A^{\nu}\varphi+2g(\nabla^{\nu}\chi),

\displaystyle\Pi_{21}\,=\,\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}\varphi\chi-g(\nabla A),

\displaystyle\Pi_{22}\,=\,M^{2}-\Xi R-g^{2}A^{2}+\frac{\lambda}{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\varphi^{2},

\displaystyle\Pi_{23}\,=\,-2g^{2}A^{\nu}\chi-2g(\nabla^{\nu}\varphi),

\displaystyle\Pi_{31}\,=\,2g^{2}A_{\mu}\varphi-g(\nabla_{\mu}\chi),

\displaystyle\Pi_{32}\,=\,2g^{2}A_{\mu}\chi+g(\nabla_{\mu}\varphi),

\displaystyle\Pi_{33}\,=\,-R_{\mu}^{\nu}+\delta^{\nu}_{\mu}g^{2}(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2}).

(22)

The one-loop contribution to the effective action is given by

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{div}\,=\,\frac{i}{2}\,\mbox{Tr}\,\log\hat{H}^{\prime}\,-\,i\,\mbox{Tr}\,\log\hat{H}_{gh},

(23)

where
\hat{H}_{gh}
is the operator corresponding to the ghost action,

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{gh}\,=\,\Box\,.

(24)

Using the Schwinger-DeWitt technique, the divergent part of the
one-loop effective action is given by
[
7
]

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{div}\,\,=\,\,-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\,\int d^{n}x\,\sqrt{-g}\,\,\,\mbox{tr}\,\bigg\{\frac{\hat{1}}{360}\,\big(3C^{2}-E_{4}+2\Box R\big)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\,\frac{1}{2}\,\hat{P}^{2}+\frac{1}{12}\,\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha\beta}^{2}+\frac{1}{6}\,\Box\hat{P}\bigg\},

(25)

where
\mu
is the renormalization scale,
\varepsilon=4\pi^{2}(n-4)
is the
dimensional regularization parameter,

C^{2}=C^{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}C_{\alpha\beta\mu\nu}
is the square of the Weyl
tensor,
E_{4}
is the Gauss-Bonnet topological term, The operators

\hat{P}
and
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha\beta}
in (
25
) are
given by

\displaystyle\hat{P}\,=\,\hat{\Pi}+\frac{\hat{1}}{6}\,R-\nabla_{\alpha}\hat{h}^{\alpha}-\hat{h}_{\alpha}\hat{h}^{\alpha},

(26)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\alpha\beta}\,=\,\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\beta\alpha}+\nabla_{\beta}\hat{h}_{\alpha}-\nabla_{\alpha}\hat{h}_{\beta}+\hat{h}_{\beta}\hat{h}_{\alpha}-\hat{h}_{\alpha}\hat{h}_{\beta},

(27)

where
\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\beta}=0
in the scalar sector and

\hat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha\beta}=[\mathcal{R}_{\alpha\beta}]^{\nu}_{\,\,\mu}=R^{\nu}_{\,.\,\mu\alpha\beta}
for the vector field.

The contribution of the ghosts goes only to the vacuum (pure
metric-dependent) term
\,\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{div,\,vac}
, given by the
sum of the contribution of gauge vector and two real scalars. These
expressions are well-known (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
) and we can skip
them. In the matter sectors, the divergent part of the one-loop
effective action is given by. Let us note that some technical details
can be found in Appendix A. We also include total derivative
terms for completeness.

\displaystyle\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{div}\,\,=\,\,-\,\frac{\mu^{n-4}}{\varepsilon}\int_{x,\,n}\bigg\{-2g^{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}-2g^{2}(\nabla\chi)^{2}+4g^{3}A^{\mu}\big[\varphi(\nabla_{\mu}\chi)-\chi(\nabla_{\mu}\varphi)\big]

\displaystyle-\,\,2g^{4}A^{2}(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})\,-\,\frac{1}{6}\,g^{2}F_{\mu\nu}^{2}\,-\,\frac{1}{3}\,g^{2}R(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})-\,(m^{2}-\tilde{\xi}R)g^{2}\chi^{2}

\displaystyle-\,(M^{2}-\tilde{\Xi}R)g^{2}\varphi^{2}+(m^{2}-\tilde{\xi}R)\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\chi^{2}\Big)+(M^{2}-\tilde{\Xi}R)\Big(\frac{\lambda}{2}\chi^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}\varphi^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle+\,\Big(\frac{\lambda^{2}}{8}+\frac{\lambda_{12}^{2}}{72}-\frac{\lambda_{12}}{6}g^{2}+g^{4}\Big)(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})^{2}+\Big[\frac{\lambda\lambda_{12}}{6}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{4}+\frac{\lambda_{12}^{2}}{12}+(\lambda_{12}-\lambda)g^{2}\Big]\varphi^{2}\chi^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\,\Big(\frac{g^{2}}{3}+\frac{\lambda}{12}+\frac{\lambda_{12}}{36}\Big)\Box(\varphi^{2}+\chi^{2})\bigg\}\,\,+\,\,\bar{\Gamma}^{(1)}_{div,\,vac},

(28)

where
\tilde{\xi}=\xi-\frac{1}{6}
and

\tilde{\Xi}=\Xi-\frac{1}{6}
. The one-loop counterterms maintain
the structure of the classical action (
6
) that guarantees the
gauge invariance. The last means that, at the one-loop level, the theory
with the broken symmetry is renormalizable.

Renormalization group equations

It proves useful to consider all three types of symmetry breaking,
i.e., by
\lambda_{12}\neq\lambda
, by
\Xi\neq\xi
, and by
M\neq m
,
at the same time. Later on, they can be separated for the physical
analysis. Thus, we start with the full list of renormalization group
equations.

The renormalization relations for the fields are as follows

\displaystyle\varphi_{0}\,=\,\mu^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\varphi\,,

\displaystyle\chi_{0}\,=\,\mu^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\chi\,,

\displaystyle A^{0}_{\mu}\,=\,\mu^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\Big(1+\frac{g^{2}}{3\varepsilon}\Big)A_{\mu}\,.

(29)

For the coupling constants, including using the relation

g_{0}A^{0}_{\mu}=gA_{\mu}
to guarantee the gauge invariance,
we get

\displaystyle g_{0}\,=\,\mu^{\frac{4-n}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{g^{2}}{3\varepsilon}\Big)g\,,

\displaystyle\lambda_{0}\,=\,\mu^{4-n}\Big(\lambda-\frac{3\lambda^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\lambda_{12}^{2}}{3\varepsilon}+\frac{4\lambda_{12}}{\varepsilon}g^{2}-\frac{24g^{4}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{8\lambda g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\lambda^{0}_{12}\,=\,\mu^{4-n}\Big(\lambda_{12}-\frac{2\lambda\lambda_{12}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{4\lambda_{12}^{2}}{3\varepsilon}+\frac{12\lambda g^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{8\lambda_{12}g^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{24g^{4}}{\varepsilon}+\frac{8\lambda_{12}g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\,.

(30)

Finally, for the masses and non-minimal parameters, we have

\displaystyle m_{0}^{2}\,=\,\Big(m^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}m^{2}+\frac{4g^{2}}{\varepsilon}m^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3\varepsilon}M^{2}+\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}M^{2}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}\,=\,\Big(M^{2}-\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}M^{2}+\frac{4g^{2}}{\varepsilon}M^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3\varepsilon}m^{2}+\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}m^{2}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\xi_{0}\,=\,\xi+\Big[\Big(\frac{4g^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)-\Big(\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3\varepsilon}-\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\Xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\Big]\,,

\displaystyle\Xi_{0}\,=\,\Xi+\Big[\Big(\frac{4g^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\frac{\lambda}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\Xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)-\Big(\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3\varepsilon}-\frac{2g^{2}}{\varepsilon}\Big)\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\Big]\,.

(31)

The last relations satisfy the usual hierarchy, in the sense
(
31
) depend on the (
30
), but not the
opposite.
Now we can derive the beta and gamma functions as

\displaystyle\beta_{P}\,=\,\lim_{n\to\,4}\mu\frac{dP}{d\mu}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{H}\,=\,\lim_{n\to\,4}\mu\frac{dH}{d\mu}\,,

(32)

where
P=\big(g,\lambda,\lambda_{12},m^{2},M^{2},\xi,\Xi\big)

are the renormalized parameters and
H=\big(\phi,\chi,A_{\mu}\big)

are renormalized fields. Using the renormalization relations, we obtain
the beta functions

\displaystyle\beta_{g}\,=\,\frac{g^{3}}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,,

(33)

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda_{12}}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big(\frac{4}{3}\lambda_{12}^{2}+2\lambda\lambda_{12}-12\lambda g^{2}+24g^{4}\Big)\,,

(34)

\displaystyle\beta_{\lambda}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big(3\lambda^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{12}^{2}}{3}-8\lambda g^{2}-4\lambda_{12}g^{2}+24g^{4}\Big)\,,

(35)

\displaystyle\beta_{m^{2}}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big(\lambda m^{2}+\frac{\lambda M^{2}}{3}-2g^{2}M^{2}-4g^{2}m^{2}\Big)\,,

\displaystyle\beta_{M^{2}}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big(\lambda M^{2}+\frac{\lambda m^{2}}{3}-2g^{2}m^{2}-4g^{2}M^{2}\Big)\,,

(36)

\displaystyle\beta_{\xi}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big[\Big(\lambda-4g^{2}\Big)\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)+\Big(\Xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\Big(\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}-2g^{2}\Big)\Big]\,,

(37)

\displaystyle\beta_{\Xi}\,=\,\frac{1}{(4\pi)^{2}}\Big[\Big(\lambda-4g^{2}\Big)\Big(\Xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)+\Big(\xi-\frac{1}{6}\Big)\Big(\frac{\lambda_{12}}{3}-2g^{2}\Big)\Big]\,.

(38)

The gamma functions are

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varphi}\,=\,\gamma_{\chi}\,=\,\frac{g^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\gamma_{A}\,=\,-\,\frac{g^{2}}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\,.

(39)

Let us note that the expressions (
38
) satisfy the usual
tests related to local conformal invariance in the massless limit.
On top of this, after taking the limit (
9
) we arrive at the
usual renormalization group functions of sQED in both (
38
)
and (
39
) cases.

The gamma function for the
A_{\mu}
and the beta function for

g
does not change under the limit (
9
). In particular,
for the coupling constant
g
we get the conventional running

\displaystyle g^{2}(t)\,=\,g_{0}^{2}\,\bigg[1-\frac{2g_{0}^{2}\,t}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\bigg]^{-1},

(40)

where
t\,=\,\log\big(\mu/\mu_{0}\big)
and
g_{0}=g(\mu_{0})
.
As usual for the Abelian models, we can consistently explore
the running only in the IR (low-energy limit). On the other hand,
in the IR the presence of masses implies the decoupling and the
running stops below the corresponding energy threshold. Thus,
let us consider only the massless limit and pay special attention
to the running of the scalar couplings. In the massless theory only
these two effective charges and the nonminimal parameters
define the gauge symmetry breaking in the theory (
10
).

To explore the behavior of the scalar couplings, we follow the
approach elaborated in the gauge theories of general form (see,
e.g.,
[
9
]
). Thus, let us introduce the new
variables

\displaystyle\bar{\lambda}(t)\,=\,\frac{\lambda(t)}{g^{2}(t)}\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\bar{\lambda}_{12}(t)=\frac{\lambda_{12}(t)}{g^{2}(t)}\,,

(41)

that provides the equations

\displaystyle\frac{d\bar{\lambda}_{12}}{d\tau}\,\,=\,\,\frac{4}{3}\,\bar{\lambda}_{12}^{2}\,+\,2\bar{\lambda}_{12}\Big(\bar{\lambda}\,-\,\frac{1}{3}\Big)\,-\,12\bar{\lambda}+24

(42)

and

\displaystyle\frac{d\bar{\lambda}}{d\tau}\,\,=\,\,3\Big(\bar{\lambda}-\frac{13}{9}\Big)^{2}\,+\,\frac{1}{3}\,\Big(\bar{\lambda}_{12}-6\Big)^{2}\,+\,\frac{155}{27}\,,

(43)

where

\displaystyle\tau\,=\,-\,\frac{3}{2}\,\,\mbox{ln}\,\Big[1-\frac{2g_{0}^{2}\,t}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\Big].

(44)

In the IR limit, when
\,t\to-\infty
, the new variable has the same
limit,
\,\tau\to-\infty
.

At this point, we can make a simple test. Let us remember that
by setting
\lambda_{12}=\lambda
or, equivalently,

\bar{\lambda}_{12}=\bar{\lambda}
, we recover gauge symmetry in the
interaction term. In this case, the splitting of the couplings should
disappear. And taking this limit according to (
9
) two
equations (
42
) and (
43
) coincide. This is
a good (albeit partial) verification of the correctness of the
calculations.

Introducing the new parameter for the difference

\displaystyle\epsilon(\tau)\,=\,\bar{\lambda}_{12}(\tau)\,-\,\bar{\lambda}(\tau)\,,

(45)

we arrive at the differential equation

\displaystyle\frac{d\epsilon}{d\tau}\,=\,\epsilon\Big(\epsilon+4\bar{\lambda}+\frac{10}{3}\Big).

(46)

We can explore the equations assuming that the deviation is
small, i.e.,
|\epsilon|\ll\bar{\lambda}
.
In this regime,
\bar{\lambda}\approx\bar{\lambda}_{12}
and the

r.h.s.
’s of both (
42
) and (
43
)
become

\displaystyle\frac{10}{3}\,\bar{\lambda}^{2}\,-\,\frac{38}{3}\,\bar{\lambda}\,+\,24.

(47)

This expression does not have real roots, which is clear already
from Eq. (
43
). The solution for
\bar{\lambda}
can be easily
found and provides the asymptotic behavior

\bar{\lambda}\to\frac{19}{10}+\frac{\sqrt{359}}{20}\pi
, such that
(
46
) boils down to a simple equation. The solution is

\displaystyle\epsilon(\tau)\,=\,\epsilon_{0}e^{c\tau}\,=\,\epsilon_{0}\Big[1-\frac{2g_{0}^{2}\,t}{3(4\pi)^{2}}\Big]^{-3c/2},

(48)

where
c\,=\,\frac{164}{15}+\frac{\sqrt{359}}{5}\pi\,>\,0
,
the initial deviation
\epsilon_{0}
corresponds to the reference scale

\mu_{0}
and we used the definition (
44
).
The solution (
48
) indicates that if the gauge symmetry is
broken by the weak violation
\lambda_{12}\neq\lambda
, at the scale

\mu_{0}
, the symmetry tends to be restored in the IR, when

\mu\ll\mu_{0}
and, asymptotically,
t\to-\infty
. Regardless
the running is logarithmic, the numerical coefficient of

3c/2\approx 34.2
is quite big and the restoration of the gauge
symmetry in the IR may be efficient.

Conclusions and discussions

We have presented a model with Abelian gauge symmetry, which
is equivalent to the usual complex (charged) scalar field theory,
but is formulated in terms of two real scalar fields in curved
spacetime. The standard gauge transformations of the complex
scalar can be reformulated for two real scalar fields, to ensure
the gauge invariance.

Introducing two different masses for the real scalar fields leads to
the qualitatively new way of
explicit
symmetry breaking in
the classical action, that does not require the Higgs mechanism.
A similar effect can be achieved by modifying the self-interaction
term for the two scalars, or even by splitting the parameters of
nonminimal interaction of the scalars with curvature. The derivation
of the one-loop counterterms has shown that the new theory with
broken symmetry is renormalizable at least at the one-loop level.
This calculation was done by using the standard Schwinger-DeWitt
technique.

The renormalizability of the theory with explicitly broken Abelian
gauge symmetry enables one using the renormalization group
method to explore the running of the symmetry-violating
parameters. However, since the UV limit in the Abelian model meets
known problems with the Landau pole, one can consistently explore
such a running only in the IR limit. This rules out the use of
massive theories because in the IR we expect the low-energy
decoupling and the running should stop. Thus, we consider only the
IR running for self-coupling in a massless theory. It turns out
that the splitting of the self-couplings tends to disappear in the IR
limit, which means a restoration of the gauge symmetry.

At the moment, we do not know any physical application of the
new way of gauge symmetry breaking and leave it to the readers.
From the mathematical side, we note that it looks possible to
generalize the new symmetry breaking scheme to the nonabelian
theory, e.g., for the case of complex scalars in the fundamental
representation of the
SU(2)
or a more general symmetry group.
In this case, one can expect the renormalizability of the theory
with broken symmetry, at least at the one-loop level. This would
open the way for exploring the UV running of the
symmetry-breaking parameters, including in the massive models.

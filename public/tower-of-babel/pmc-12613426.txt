Relationship between academic self-concept and psychological truancy among medical college students in China: the chain mediating role of academic motivation and school climate
Authors: Tana Yu, Yanwei Yang, Qing Zeng, Zihang Xu, Caiyue Li, Hongchen Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTeaching aims to improve the learning and all-round abilities of college students. However, the prevalent phenomenon of psychological truancy among college students is a major impediment to achieving the outcomes of teaching. Academic self-concept is a positive psychological feature that affects the academic activities of students. In this study, we explored the effect of academic motivation and school climate on the relationship between academic self-concept and psychological truancy.MethodsThis is a descriptive cross-sectional study. A total of 628 students from 8 Chinese medical colleges were enrolled. Four questionnaires of Academic Self-Concept Scale (44-item; α = 0.84), the Psychological Truancy Scale (28-item; α = 0.84), the Delaware School Climate Survey-Student (Chinese adaptation, 31-item; α = 0.89) and the academic motivation questionnaire (34-item; α = 0.93) were used to collect data. Data analysis was conducted using SPSS Statistics (version 25.0) and AMOS (version 24.0) software, with a significance level set at 0.05.ResultsThe findings of the study indicated that academic self-concept not only directly affect psychological truancy (β = -0.110, 95% CI = -0.150 to -0.092, p<0.05 ), but it also could indirectly affect psychological truancy through the mediation of school climate (β = -0.029, 95% CI = -0.014 to -0.049, p <0.05) and academic motivation (β = -0.075, 95% CI = -0.117 to -0.051, p<0.05), respectively. Furthermore, academic self-concept can decrease psychological truancy through the chain mediation of school climate and academic motivation (β = -0.019, 95% CI = -0.026 to -0.011, p<0.05). This indicates that school climate and academic motivation significantly mediate the path from the academic self-concept to psychological truancy.ConclusionThe study provides valuable insights for developing measures to mitigate psychological truancy behavior among medical students and improve their academic performance. These measures should also promote their academic self-concept, school climate, and academic motivation.Keywords: Academic self-concept, Academic motivation, School climate, Psychological truancy

Introduction

‘Truancy’ is a general concept that refers to being away from class without any legitimate reason. Truancy behavior has been classified into physical truancy and psychological truancy [1, 2]. Physical truancy refers to the phenomenon where in students do not show up at the time and place without asking for leave. Since most courses include attendance as a component of the final grade, physical truancy is less frequent in colleges. Psychological truancy, means that students come to class on time, but do not listen to teachers, instead, they may learn other subjects, sleep, chat, immerse themselves in phones, daze, or think about other things [3]. The concepts of psychological truancy and low learning engagement are similar, but the former emphasizes students’ low participation in classroom learning more, which is showed as students’ detachment from the teacher’s teaching and a state of low learning engagement with the classroom content. Low learning engagement does not involve a clear field or goal. For instance, a student does not listen to the teacher’s teaching in class but is immersed in the study of other subjects. Although the learning engagement is high, it is still a psychological truancy. Therefore, the study of psychological truancy focuses on aims to students’ classroom performance and points to the improvement of teachers’ teaching strategies and classroom atmosphere. Psychological truancy is quite common and is difficult to evaluate. Psychological truancy is a major factor that impedes the quality of teaching and learning. A survey indicated a widespread prevalence of the phenomenon of psychological truancy in various colleges and universities (exceeding 90%) [4], leading to a waste of educational resources.

Since the Chinese government issued the ‘Healthy China 2030 Plan Strategy’ in 2016, the demand for high-quality medical talents in China has been increasing rapidly. Classroom teaching is the foundation of higher education and the main field for cultivating medical talents. However, psychological truancy has become a main dilemma in classroom teaching, affecting the quality of medical talent cultivation. It is reported that the incidence of psychological truancy among students majoring in traditional Chinese medicine and nursing is the highest [5]. Psychological truancy among medical students can have serious repercussions on their professional abilities. Therefore, it is particularly important to explore the formation reasons of medical students’ psychological truancy behavior.

The cognitive and emotional engagement of students in classroom learning is important for their academic accomplishment [6]. According to the theory of individual-environment interaction [7], individual behavior is often formed and developed in the interaction between individual and environment. The psychological truancy among medical students is also inseparable from the interaction between individual psychological factors and environmental factors. In terms of individual psychological factors, based on the self-determination theory [8], the academic self-concept reflects students’ cognition and evaluation of their own learning [9], which will affect their academic motivation and beliefs [10], and bring about changes in classroom behavior. School climate is an important environmental factor that can contribute to academic motivation and behavior in the classroom [11]. For an in-depth understanding of the mechanism of psychological truancy behavior, this study probed the role of academic motivation and school climate as mediators in the relationship between academic self-concept and psychological truancy among medical students in China. This model, which integrates individual and environmental variables, helps to reveal the mechanism of class psychological truancy among medical students more comprehensively, thereby providing measures for better reduce psychological truancy while maintaining their class performance.

Academic self-concept and psychological truancy

Some students often have problems with attendance and are less motivated and eager to participate in class activities. Shute and Copper [12], referred to these students as psychological truants. These students typically refuse to participate in class activities, dodge challenges, and often fail to complete tasks. They may be bored, depressed, anxious, and have doubts about their abilities, thus forming a negative academic perception. Students’ self-perception of their academic abilities is an important factor in better understanding non-cognitive mediators of performance in school [13].

Academic self-concept is a popular concept designed to measure student self-perception in academic domains. It refers to the stable cognition, experience, and assessment of an individual’s academic development [14]. Studies have demonstrated a positive correlation between academic self-concept and academic-related behaviors. Students with high academic self-concept have higher class attendance rates [15], higher academic achievement [16], and exhibit greater academic involvement [17]. Students with high academic self-concept have a more positive evaluation of their learning abilities, academic engagement, goal-setting, performance and achievement [18], and are more willing to spend time on learning [17]. This tends to promote good learning behavior and decreases the psychological truancy. Conversely, low academic self-concept tends to result in undesirable outcomes such as boredom and dropout [19].

School climate as a mediator between academic self-concept and psychological truancy

School climate plays an important role in improving student achievement and decreasing problem behaviors. The school climate determines the quality of interactions among all students, teachers, parents, and school staff. The school climate is reflected in the norms, values, and goals that constitute the school’s social mission [20]. School climate was shown to affect the self-esteem of middle school students [21], mitigate the negative effects of self-criticism [22], and boost their academic self-concept [23]. Many schools now endeavor to improve the school climate as a precursor to enhancing the academic and psychological well-being of their students [24]. Wang and Degol [25] found that how students perceive their role as students (i.e., academic self-concept), affects their perceptions of school climate, including attitudes toward negative or positive experiences in school, and interactions with teachers and peers. In addition, students’ perception of school climate may be improved by a positive academic self-concept or decreased by a negative academic self-concept [26].

A positive school climate also contributes to promoting a variety of valued educational outcomes [21], including students’ academic participation [27] and achievement [28]. Students who have a positive perception of school climate, tend to exhibit fewer behavioral problems, higher levels of cognitive and emotional involvement [29], and greater life satisfaction [30]. Thus, the perception of a pleasant school climate may help reduce psychological truancy [31].

To summarize, available evidence suggests that school climate is inversely related to psychological truancy. In addition, this environmental variable may help elucidate and mediate the link between academic self-concept and psychological truancy. However, to the best of our knowledge, no study has assessed the role of school climate in mediating the impact of academic self-concept on psychological truancy.

Academic motivation as a mediator between academic self-concept and psychological truancy

Academic motivation is a potential mediator in the relationship between academic self-concept and psychological truancy. The motivation for academic achievement is crucial [32]. Academic motivation refers to the tendency to motivate and maintain students’ motivational tendencies toward the goal of learning [16]. Academic motivation can be intrinsic or extrinsic [33]. Intrinsic motivation means people doing something for purely pleasure or interest, while extrinsic motivation means an act carried out by a person under the inducement of an outside force [34]. Unlike intrinsic motivation, extrinsically motivated behaviors are more amenable to control. For instance, the completion of a high-level assignment by the student to avoid punishment is an externally motivated behavior [35].

Various studies have indicated a link between academic self-concept and academic motivation. A positive academic self-concept plays an active role in increasing academic motivation [36, 37]. Research conducted among Canadian and Indian students have demonstrated a positive correlation of academic self-concept with both intrinsic and extrinsic academic motivation [38]. One reason may be that a higher awareness of academic self-concept can help students to become more self-motivated in their pursuit of learning. According to the self-determination theory, people with a high sense of competence or a positive academic self-concept are more likely to have high intrinsic motivation toward their activities. Students with high intrinsic motivation are more willing to participate in class, to face challenges, to complete tasks on time, and to focus on their academic achievements. Psychological truancy is a non-adaptive classroom participation behavior, and it can be impacted by academic motivation. Vansteenkiste et al. [39] found that stronger intrinsic academic motivation was connected with more adaptable learning attitudes and higher personal well-being among early adolescents in China, which has significant implications for reducing psychological truancy. Therefore, high academic motivation can promote learning engagement and reduce the psychological truancy behavior [40]. Some studies have also found that academic motivation plays a mediating role between academic self-concept and academic achievement [16, 37].

School climate to academic motivation

Research indicates the degree of desire for academic pursuits might be affected by the school climate [41, 42]. A significant relationship between the perceived school climate and academic motivation was also demonstrated [43]. The more positive perception of school climate, the greater the motivation to learn. According to social cognitive theory, students’ motivation and desire to engage in behaviors are not only related to their perceptions about their talents and the likelihood of success, but also involved different environmental factors [44, 45]. In other words, the interaction between academic self-concept and school environment can affect students’ motivation, which in turn determines their learning behavior in the classroom.

In summary, students’ motivation is the most important factor [46], and academic cognition is a key determinant of the decision whether to attend class. Furthermore, the school climate, including the teachers’ expertise, classroom management, organization, and other curriculum elements are significant determinants of the student’s performance in class [47]. Although some studies have demonstrated the correlation between students’ classroom behavior and certain factors [48]. There is no systematic analysis of the synergy between the factors that affect students’ psychological truancy. This research aimed to examine the mechanisms (i.e., academic motivation and school climate) of the association between academic self-concept and psychological truancy.

Against this background, the objective of this study was to propose a mediation model to explore the relationship between medical students’ academic self-concept, academic motivation, school environment and psychological truancy. Our findings may enable an in-depth understanding of the mechanism underlying psychological truancy behavior, and offer theoretical support for interventions to reduce psychological truancy, so as to improve the classroom teaching quality of medical colleges and universities. In light of the aforementioned considerations, the research hypotheses were as follows:

H1

Academic self-concept positively and significantly affects medical students’ psychological truancy.

H2

Academic self-concept has a positive and significant effect on school climate.

H3

Academic self-concept has a positive and significant effect on psychological truancy through the mediating variable of school climate.

H4

Academic self-concept positively and significantly affects academic motivation.

H5

Academic self-concept has a positive and significant effect on psychological truancy through the mediating variable of academic motivation.

H6

School climate positively and significantly affect academic motivation. It plays a chain mediating role in the effect of academic self-concept on psychological truancy.

Methods

Design and data collection

This was a descriptive cross-sectional study. A total of 628 medical undergraduates from 8 Chinese medical colleges were enrolled. Inclusion criteria for this study: Medical students during the period of attending classes at school; Exclusion criteria: Medical students during the clinical internship stage. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to their enrolment. The participants completed online or offline surveys on academic self-concept, academic motivation, school climate and psychological truancy variables, which took approximately 15 min.

Measures

Academic Self-Concept

The academic self-concept of students was assessed using the Academic Self-Concept Scale compiled by Guo et al. [49]. The scale consists of 44 items, spanning four dimensions: perception of academic ability (e.g., “I can quickly get problem-solving methods enables me to confidently deal with academic challenges”), self-control of academic behavior (e.g., “In my study, I can always plan and make decisions actively”), experience of academic emotion (e.g., “I am full of interest in learning”), and value of academic achievement (e.g., “I attach great importance to my academic achievements”). Each item was scored using a five-point Likert scale, with 1 denoting ‘strongly disagree’ and 5 denoting ‘strongly agree’. Higher scores correspond to a stronger sense of academic self-concept. The construct validity of the scale as a measure of academic self-concept in college students was examined [50]. In this study, the Cronbach’s α of the whole scale was 0.84, and that of the various dimensions ranged between 0.88 and 0.91.

Psychological Truancy

The Psychological Truancy Scale compiled by Su et al. [51] was used to assess the level of psychological truancy. This scale consists of 28 items, including 5 factors: study cognition (e.g., “I don’t think the knowledge currently learning will have much connection with future work”), study habits (e.g., “Before learning the new course, I will preview it”), self-adjustment (e.g., “When teacher’s teaching is too much and too fast in class, I give up listening”), class cognition (e.g., “I think it’s better to study by myself than to listen to teacher in class”), and environment towards study (e.g., “I will be influenced by the classmates around me who don’t listen in class, which makes me not listen carefully either”). Each item was evaluated using a 5-point Likert scale (1 means ‘totally inconsistent’ and 5 means ‘totally consistent’). The higher the score, the stronger the psychological truancy. In this study, the Cronbach’s α of the whole scale was 0.84, and that of the individual factors ranged between 0.75 and 0.85.

Delaware School Climate Survey-Student

The Delaware School Climate Survey-Student (DSCS-S) was created as a short survey for assessing the school environment, especially the characteristics of social support and structure. The English version of the DSCS-S has been translated into Chinese [52]. It has 31 items, encompassing 8 dimensions: teacher-student relations (e.g., Teachers care about their students), student-student relations (e.g., Students are friendly with each other), activity participation (e.g., Most students hand in their homework on time), clarity of expectations (e.g., Students know what they should do in school), respect for diversity (e.g., Teachers treat all students with the same respect), fairness of rules (e.g., School regulations are reasonable), school safety (e.g., Students feel safe at school) and school bullying (e.g., In this school, there are students who threaten and bully other students). Each item is rated on a 4-point rating scale ranging from 1 (fully opposed) to 4 (totally agree). High scores represent a pleasant school climate. The Cronbach’s α of the whole scale was 0.89, and that of the individual dimensions ranged between 0.70 and 0.85.

Academic Motivation

The academic motivation of college students was assessed using the academic motivation questionnaire developed by Tian and Hu [53]. It consists of 34 items, spanning four dimensions: interest in learning (e.g., “I have a strong interest in my courses studying”), pursuit of competence (e.g., “I study hard in order to achieve great success in the future”), acquisition of reputation (e.g., “I want to gain others’ respect by improving my grades”), and tendency of altruism (e.g., “I study hard in order to be capable of helping others in the future”). Each item was scored on a five-point Likert scale ranging from 1 (completely opposed) to 5 (totally agree). Higher scores indicate higher levels of academic motivation. The Cronbach’s α coefficient of the sample was 0.93 while that of the individual dimensions ranged between 0.80 and 0.92.

Analyses Strategy

Mean (standard deviation) scores for academic self-concept, academic motivation, school climate, and psychological truancy were calculated, and the correlation among these variables was assessed by Pearson correlation analysis using SPSS 25.0 software. The effect of academic motivation and school climate as mediators was tested using Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) in AMOS 24.0, a multivariate technique for analyzing observable and latent variable correlations. To test the hypothesis model, the goodness-of-fit indices were used comparative fit index (CFI) > 0.9, Tucker-Lewis index (TLI) > 0.9, root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA) < 0.08, and Standardized root mean square residual (SRMR) < 0.05, they were considered indicative of a good overall fit of the structural model [54].Mediating effects were validated by bootstrap sampling with 5000 iterations. Specifically, indirect effects were considered statistically significant if a 95% confidence interval did not include 0.

The distribution across score ranking was a normal distribution, with top 20% of grade making up 12.6%, 21- 40% of grade students at 32.5%, 41-60% of grade students at 35.5%, and others making up 19.4%. Teat of Mediation

The hypothesized path model of this study comprised 21 observed variables and 4 latent variables (academic self-concept, school climate, academic motivation, and psychological truancy). This model showed an excellent fit with the data: χ2/degrees of freedom (df) = 4.82<5, p < 0.001, RMSEA = 0.078 < 0.08, CFI = 0.92 > 0.9, TLI = 0.91 > 0.9, and SRMR = 0.045 < 0.05. The results showed that the direct path coefficients in the proposed directions were significant, and indicated that school climate and academic motivation mediated the relationship of academic self-concept to psychological truancy (Fig. 2). The effect of academic self-concept on psychological truancy was significant (β = -.11, p <.001). The direct effects of academic self-concept on academic motivation (β =.25, p <.001) and school climate (β =.36, p <.001) were significant. Furthermore, the direct effects of academic motivation (β = -.30, p <.001) and school climate (β = -.09, p <.001) on psychological truancy were also significant. Lastly, the direct effect of school climate on academic motivation (β =.18, p <.001) was significant (see Fig. 2).

Discussion

Based on the theory of individual-environment interaction, this study constructed a chain mediation model to investigate the role of school climate and academic motivation in the relationship between academic self-concept and psychological truancy. This results confirmed hypotheses 1–6, highlighting that academic self-concept serves as a foundational element that directly reduce psychological truancy, and also indirectly reduce it by improving school climate and academic motivation, which is essential for medical students’ classroom academic engagement and performance.

We demonstrated the link between academic self-concept and psychological truancy among medical students in China (confirming H1). That is, academic self-concept, as a protective factor, can directly and negatively predict psychological truancy (β = -0.110, p<0.05), which is consistent with a previous study in which academic self-concept was shown to have a positive effect on reducing students’ psychological truancy [55]. This maybe because students with strong academic self-concepts are more likely to identify and recognize their aptitude, have a more enjoyable experience in learning, and therefore be willing to invest more time and energy in learning. Academic self-concept could be directed toward improving their academic achievement [56]. Medical students with a low academic self-concept lack academic confidence. When facing pressure, they may develop negative emotions, negative coping styles, and low learning efficiency, and affect academic performance and achievements [13]. The findings are also consistent with previous research which indicated a positive relationship between academic self-concept and truancy reduction [15].

Secondly, school climate showed a mediating role in the link between academic self-concept and psychological truancy (confirming H2-H3). Academic self-concept has a mediating effect on psychological truancy through school climate(β = -0.029, p<0.05).There are several possible explanations. Students’ positive academic self-concept facilitates their perception of positive evaluation from teachers and peers and helps them improve their expectations for learning, thus increasing their willingness to invest more time and energy in class [57]. Secondly, according to the self-determination theory [58], an individual’s life is devoted to the pursuit of autonomy needs, competency needs, and relationship needs. A good school climate fully satisfies students’ basic psychological needs and promotes their good classroom behavior [59]. Finally, the perception of more emotional and academic support from teachers and peers, inculcates a sense of security and belonging which encouraging them to abide by norms and discipline, and to independently explore academic pursuits. This helps improve students’ positive evaluation of their abilities and learning experience, thereby reducing the psychological truancy behavior [60].This indirect pathway highlights the transformative potential of addressing academic self-concept to create an environment conducive to academic achievement. These findings advance our understanding by emphasizing that psychological truancy is not simply the result of individual effort, but is significantly related to environment support.

Thirdly, we also found that the association between academic self-concept and psychological truancy was mediated by academic motivation (confirming H4-H5). As has been demonstrated, students with higher levels of academic self-concept demonstrate strong academic motivation, and produce less psychological truancy(β = -0.075, p<0.05).Students with high academic self-concept exhibit low psychological truancy behavior due to greater academic motivation. On one hand, medical students with high academic self-concepts often have a more positive evaluation of themselves and are confident of their academic abilities. On the other hand, according to the self-determination theory [8], medical students with a strong academic self-concept better understand that academic success is determined by their own efforts rather than external factors such as luck, which also increases their intrinsic motivation to study. High academically motivated medical students are better able to control their learning processes, resist external temptations, and adhere to current studies while encountering face difficulties. They are also less likely to commit psychological truancy to achieve a particular learning objective. Owing to the paucity of studies exploring the relationship between academic self-concept, academic motivation, and psychological truancy in Chinese medical students, this study adds a new perspective to the field.

Finally, this study also demonstrated the chain mediating effect of school climate and academic motivation between academic self-concept and psychological truancy (confirming H6, β = -0.019, p<0.05). Academic motivation varies with the different school climates perceived by students, that is, the differences in school climates can reflect the level of students’ academic motivation [61]. According to the social cognitive theory, motivation depends on the external context and plays an important role in the generating behavior [62]. Environmental factors (school climate) influence whether individuals view themselves as active learners (academic motivation), and this has an impact on individual behavior (psychological truancy). The generation of academic motivation is mainly a kind of ‘communication need’ that individuals want to be recognized or affirmed by the group, and students can effectively stimulate their internal needs when they perceive a positive school climate. Those medical students who perceive a positive school climate, when they have harmonious relationships with teachers and peers and receive more academic support from them, can often promote their confidence and academic help-seeking, and thereby enhance academic motivation [22]. Consequently, it is essential to implement adequate measures in educational planning with the aim of promoting school climate and academic motivation.

Conclusion

These results indicate that academic motivation and school climate play a chain mediating role in the link between academic self-concept and psychological truancy. A strong academic self-concept can reduce the incidence of psychological truancy behaviors among medical students, but more importantly, the model offers useful information for interventions to improve medical students’ class behavior. First, educators should help students increase their positive experiences of academic success, inculcate a sense of self-belief about their academic ability, and form more positive evaluations of themselves, which will enhance the quality of their performance. For instance, the creation of group counseling programs can improve students’ academic self-concept levels. Second, educators can also lower psychological truancy by enhancing students’ academic motivation. For example, changing the teachers’ single and boring teaching style, improving students’ expectations of knowledge and class, utilizing reasonable competition and reward or punishment mechanisms, and encouraging students appropriately are all ways to improve students’ academic motivation. Third, a good school climate can make students feel equal and harmonious, which better meets their basic psychological needs. Therefore, a supportive psychological environment should be established to cultivate students’ attachment to school taking cognizance of their emotional needs. Attention should be paid to strengthening the relationship between teachers and students, and a good interactive teaching environment should be created.

Limitations

One limitation of this study relates to the cross-sectional study. Structural equation modeling was used in this study, the relationship obtained was correlational rather than causal. In addition, the small number of male subjects may have resulted in insufficient validity of statistical tests to detect actual differences among gender differences. A further limitation was the use of self-reported data. As it only reflects a subjective perception of behavior rather than an objective and impartial assessment. Another limitation of the study was the factor loadings of school bullying within school climate was lower, which is less than 0.30. School bullying is very covert and not easy to be self-reported. And the sample characteristics may also play a role. If students with low incidences of bullying or from a homogeneous group in terms of their exposure to bullying, it may lead to limited variance in responses, thereby affecting the factor loading. Consequently, it is recommended that future studies using a more diverse and representative sample can enhance the generalization of the findings and potentially improve the factor structure.

miR-302a-3p mitigates intervertebral disc degeneration progression through regulating EPHA2
Authors: Weihan Qu, Laicong Liao, Ganggang Wang, Haitao Xu, Lina Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundIntervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) underlying molecular mechanisms remain incompletely understood.ObjectivesTo explore the role of miR-302a-3p in IDD and elucidate its targeted regulatory mechanism.Methods130 IDD patients and 130 healthy controls were collected. The miR-302a-3p and EPHA2 expressions were measured using reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR). A lipopolysaccharide (LPS)-induced cell injury model was established using human nucleus pulposus (NP) cells. Proliferation was assessed using the cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8) assay, apoptosis was determined by flow cytometry, and the concentrations of inflammatory factors were measured by enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA). A dual-luciferase reporter gene assay was conducted to confirm the targeted regulatory interaction.ResultsmiR-302a-3p expression was significantly decreased in IDD patients (P < 0.001), and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicated that it possessed certain diagnostic potential (P < 0.001, AUC = 0.8808, 95% confidence interval (CI) = 0.8400-0.9216). In the LPS-induced NP cell injury model, miR-302a-3p expression was down-regulated, which was accompanied by reduced cell proliferation, increased apoptosis, and elevated tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) and interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) secretion (P < 0.001). However, overexpression of miR-302a-3p could effectively alleviate these injury-related effects. Furthermore, miR-302a-3p directly targeted EPHA2, whose expression was up-regulated in IDD (P < 0.001). Overexpression of EPHA2 partially reversed the protective effects of miR-302a-3p on cell proliferation, apoptosis, and inflammatory responses (P < 0.001).ConclusionmiR-302a-3p significantly mitigated inflammatory responses and suppressed cell apoptosis by directly targeting and downregulating EPHA2 expression, thereby delaying the progression of IDD.Keywords: miR-302a-3p, EPHA2, Intervertebral disc degeneration, Inflammation

Introduction

Intervertebral disc degeneration (IDD) is a chronic and complex musculoskeletal disorder involving multiple pathological factors, characterized by apoptosis of nucleus pulposus cells, degradation of the extracellular matrix, and persistent inflammatory responses [1]. The development of IDD is associated with various cellular and molecular mechanisms, including disc hydration imbalance, structural alterations in the annulus fibrosus, cell apoptosis, and inflammatory processes [2, 3]. This condition can compromise spinal stability and cause chronic low back pain, significantly impairing patients’ work capacity and quality of life [4]. In recent years, substantial advances have been achieved in understanding the molecular mechanisms underlying IDD and in developing potential therapeutic strategies. Evidence indicates that the key ferroptosis-related gene PDK4 plays a pivotal role in the progression of IDD, potentially contributing to the disruption of cellular homeostasis [5]. Concurrently, the expression of senescence-associated genes FPR1 and UCHL1 is markedly dysregulated in patients with lumbar disc herniation (LDH), highlighting their potential as auxiliary biomarkers for clinical diagnosis [6]. The pathogenesis of IDD appears to be closely linked to multiple forms of regulated cell death, and elucidating these pathways may facilitate the development of targeted therapeutic interventions [7]. From a therapeutic perspective, both autologous leukocyte-poor platelet-rich plasma (Lp-PRP) and leukocyte-rich platelet-rich plasma (Lr-PRP) effectively enhance the proliferation of human degenerated nucleus pulposus cells and upregulate the synthesis of extracellular matrix components, including type II collagen and aggrecan, with Lp-PRP exhibiting superior regenerative capacity [8]. Furthermore, DAFM/chitosan-1 hydrogel has demonstrated promising potential in preclinical studies as a functional scaffold for annulus fibrosus defect repair, which may offer a novel avenue for the bioengineered treatment of IDD [9]. However, the treatment options and therapeutic effects for IDD remain limited [10]. Therefore, early intervention and a comprehensive understanding of the underlying molecular regulatory mechanisms are crucial for the development of novel and effective therapeutic strategies.

MicroRNAs (miRNAs) are small, non-coding RNAs involved in various physiological and pathological processes, including cell proliferation and apoptosis [11, 12]. The regulatory roles of non-coding RNAs, including miRNAs, small interfering RNA (siRNAs), and circular RNAs (circRNAs), in musculoskeletal and tendon disorders have garnered growing research interest due to their critical involvement in disease pathogenesis. For example, miRNAs have demonstrated significant therapeutic and diagnostic potential in tendon injury repair [13] and osteoarthritis management [14]. SiRNAs can modulate the dysregulated expression of genes associated with growth factors and tendon structural proteins [15], and may promote the inflammatory pathogenesis of rheumatoid arthritis (RA) [16], and may offer insights into the metabolic mechanisms underlying osteoporosis (OP) [17]. Circular RNAs may also be involved in the regulation of OP-related metabolic processes, which may bring hope for the identification of novel therapeutic targets and the development of innovative pharmacological interventions [18]. Moreover, the regulatory role in IDD has been receiving increasing attention.

miR-302a-3p plays a regulatory role in a variety of diseases, such as cancers (e.g., melanoma [19], glioblastoma [20] and colon cancer [21]), degenerative disorders (e.g., ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament [22]), and myocardial ischemia-reperfusion injury [23]. Moreover, preliminary studies suggest that miR-302a-3p may be involved in the regulation of IDD [24], although its specific mechanism of action and potential downstream target genes remain to be fully elucidated.

EPHA2 belongs to the Eph receptor family and is involved not only in physiological processes such as axon guidance and cell migration [25], but also in the exacerbation of inflammatory responses through the activation of pro-inflammatory signaling pathways [26]. For instance, in atherosclerosis, elevated EPHA2 expression promotes the expression of pro-inflammatory genes in endothelial cells, thereby accelerating plaque formation [27]. In cancer, overexpression of EPHA2 enhances the invasive growth of tumor cells, as observed in triple-negative breast cancer [28], while in a cerebral ischemia-reperfusion model, EPHA2 knockout mice exhibit significantly reduced infarct volumes [29]. Furthermore, inhibition of the tyrosine kinase receptor EPHA2 has been shown to suppress the hypertrophic differentiation of chondrocytes and mesenchymal stem cells [30]. Based on bioinformatics predictions, EPHA2 has been identified as a potential downstream target of miR-302a-3p. Therefore, we hypothesized that the miR-302a-3p/EPHA2 axis may play a significant role in the pathogenesis of IDD.

Through clinical sample analysis and cell experiments, this study systematically investigated the potential diagnostic value of miR-302a-3p in IDD and confirmed its regulatory effect on nucleus pulposus (NP) cell functions by targeting EPHA2, which may provide a novel theoretical basis for the prevention and treatment of IDD.

Materials and methods

Study participants

This study was approved by the Zhucheng People’s Hospital Ethics Committee, and all patients provided written informed consent.

130 patients diagnosed with IDD were enrolled, ranging in age from 40 to 65 years, the group included 61 males and 69 females. The control group also comprised 130 participants, aged 40–65 years, including 64 males and 66 females.

Inclusion criteria for the IDD group: presence of chronic low back pain lasting more than 3 months, confirmed diagnosis of IDD via magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (Pfirrmann grade ≥ III). Exclusion criteria included the presence of lumbar fractures, tumors, infections, rheumatoid arthritis, or severe hepatic or renal insufficiency.

For the control group, inclusion criteria were as follows: no history of chronic low back pain; absence of systemic inflammatory diseases; no prior treatment related to intervertebral disc herniation; and no history of lumbar fractures, tumors, infections, rheumatoid arthritis, or severe hepatic or renal insufficiency.

3 mL fasting venous blood was collected from all participants. Following centrifugation, serum was separated and stored at -80 °C.

Cell model

Human NP cells were obtained from ScienCell Research Laboratories and cultured in DMEM medium supplemented with 15% fetal bovine serum (FBS) (Gibco, Germany), 100 U/mL penicillin, and 0.1 mg/mL streptomycin (Gibco, Germany) at 37 °C under a 5% CO2. To establish the IDD model, NP cells were seeded into 6-well plates and subsequently treated with 1 µg/mL lipopolysaccharide (LPS) for 24 h.

miR-302a-3p mimics, miR-302a-3p inhibitors, their corresponding negative controls (mimic-NC and inhibitor-NC), as well as the EPHA2 overexpression vector (pcDNA3.1-OE-EPHA2) and the empty vector control (pcDNA3.1-OE-NC) were synthesized by Shanghai GenePharma Co., Ltd. Cell transfection was carried out using Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and all subsequent experiments were conducted 48 h post-transfection.

Reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted using TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), and RNA purity was assessed using a NanoDrop spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Inc., Wilmington, DE, USA). The isolated RNA was subsequently reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA), which served as the template for RT-qPCR amplification (SYBR Real-time PCR I Kit, Takara, Japan). U6 snRNA (U6) and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) were internal reference genes, and the relative gene expression was analyzed using the 2-ΔΔCt method. All primers were synthesized and provided by Shanghai Guangzhou Ribo Co., Ltd. (Guangzhou, China). The primer sequences were as follows: miR-302a-3p:

F: 5’-ACACUCCAGCUGGGAGUGGUUUUGUACCUUC-3’,

R: 5’-CUCAACUGGUGUCGUGGAGUCGGCAAUUCAGUUGAGUCGUGAAU-3’.

EPHA2: F: 5’-CCACCACAACATCATCCGCCTA-3’,

R: 5’-CACGCTGAACTCGCCATCCTT-3’.

U6: F: 5’-CTCGCTTCGGCAGCACA-3’,

R: 5’-AACGCTTCACGAATTTGCGT-3’.

GAPDH: F: 5’-GACACCCACTCCTCCACCTTT-3’,

R: 5’-CCACCCTGTTGCTGTAGCCAA-3’.

Cell counting kit-8 (CCK-8)assay

Transfected NP cells were seeded into 96-well plates at a density of 1 × 104cells per well. Following 24 h, 48 h, and 72 h of culture, 10 µL of CCK-8 solution (Beyotime Biotechnology, China) was added to each well. The absorbance of each well was then measured at 450 nm.

Apoptosis assay

NP cells transfected for 48 h were harvested. 5 µL of Annexin V-FITC and 5 µL of PI (Nanjing KeyGen Biotech. Co. Ltd., China) were added to the cell suspension. The mixture was incubated for 15 min. Apoptosis was analyzed using a flow cytometer within one hour, with the apoptosis rate determined using Cell Quest software (BD Biosciences, NJ, USA). The apoptosis rate was calculated using the Annexin V-FITC/PI double staining method in combination with flow cytometry. Annexin V was used to detect apoptotic cells (including early and late stages), while PI labeled dead cells (late apoptotic and necrotic cells). Cells were classified into four populations: viable cells, early apoptotic cells, late apoptotic cells, and necrotic cells. The total apoptosis rate (%) was determined as the sum of early apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI−) and late apoptotic (Annexin V+/PI+) cell counts, divided by the total number of analyzed cells, and multiplied by 100% (Total apoptosis rate (%) = (Number of early apoptotic cells + Number of late apoptotic cells) / Total cell count × 100%).

Enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA)

Cell culture supernatants were collected, and the levels of tumor necrosis factor-alpha (TNF-α) were measured using Human TNF alpha ELISA Kit (KHC3014, invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA). The inter-batch coefficient of variation (CV) was 8.5%, and the intra-batch CV was 5.9%. The levels of interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) were measured using Human IL-1 beta ELISA Kit (PI305, Beyotime Biotechnology, China). The within-plate and between-plate coefficients of variation were both less than 10%. The absorbance was read at 450 nm, and the concentrations of the respective cytokines were determined based on the standard curves provided by the kits.

Dual luciferase assay

pGL3 vectors containing the wild-type EPHA2 3’ untranslated region (3’UTR) (WT-EPHA2) and the mutant EPHA2 3’UTR (MUT-EPHA2, with the miR-302a-3p binding site mutated) were synthesized by Guangzhou RiboBio Co., Ltd. WT-EPHA2 or MUT-EPHA2 was co-transfected into NP cells along with miR-mimic, miR-inhibitor, or their respective negative controls. 48 h post-transfection, the ratio of firefly luciferase activity to Renilla luciferase activity was measured using a dual-luciferase assay kit (Promega, Madison, WI, USA) to confirm the direct regulatory interaction.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was conducted using SPSS 21 software. Independent sample t-test and non-parametric tests were employed for intergroup comparisons. The diagnostic potential of miR-302a-3p was assessed by constructing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. The cell experiments were independently performed in triplicate to ensure data reproducibility and statistical robustness. A P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

miR-302a-3p expression and diagnostic significance in IDD

130 patients with IDD and 130 healthy controls were enrolled in this study. miR-302a-3p expression was significantly downregulated in the IDD (Fig. 1A). miR-302a-3p exhibited considerable diagnostic potential for IDD, with a sensitivity of 80.00%, specificity of 81.54%, and an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.8808 (Fig. 1B).

Expression and diagnostic value of miR-302a-3p in IDD. A. The expression of miR-302a-3p was significantly decreased in the IDD group. B. miR-302a-3p exhibited a certain diagnostic potential for IDD. *** means P < 0.001. The independent samples t-test and ROC analysis were employed as the statistical methods

miR-302a-3p in the LPS-induced IDD model

After LPS treatment, miR-302a-3p expression was significantly downregulated, while its expression was partially restored following miR-mimic supplementation (Fig. 2A). The proliferation capacity of cells in the LPS-treated group was markedly inhibited, and this inhibition was partially alleviated upon miR-mimic supplementation (Fig. 2B). LPS treatment significantly increased the rate of cell apoptosis, which was notably reduced after the introduction of miR-mimic (Fig. 2C). Additionally, LPS treatment led to a significant upregulation of TNF-α and IL-1β, and their levels were substantially decreased following miR-mimic supplementation (Fig. 2D).

Effect of miR-302a-3p on the LPS-induced IDD model. A. The expression of miR-302a-3p was markedly reduced following LPS treatment. B. LPS treatment inhibits cell proliferation, which was partially restored upon miR-mimic supplementation. C. miR-302a-3p suppressed LPS-induced cell apoptosis. D. miR-302a-3p mitigated the LPS-induced inflammatory response. *** means P < 0.001. Non-parametric tests were used as the statistical methods in this study

Regulatory interaction between miR-302a-3p and EPHA2 at the molecular target level

In the WT-EPHA2 group, miR-mimic significantly suppressed luciferase activity, whereas miR-inhibitor markedly enhanced it. In contrast, no significant differences in luciferase activity were observed among the MUT-EPHA2 groups. These findings, together with sequence analysis confirmed that miR-302a-3p directly targeted the 3’UTR of EPHA2 (Fig. 3A–B). Moreover, EPHA2 expression in the IDD group was significantly elevated (Fig. 3C). Upregulation of miR-302a-3p led to a marked decrease in EPHA2 expression. Notably, overexpression of EPHA2 reversed the inhibitory effect of miR-mimic on EPHA2 expression, indicating that miR-302a-3p exerted its biological effects by directly targeting and suppressing EPHA2. Furthermore, a similar regulatory effect of miR-302a-3p on EPHA2 expression was observed in the LPS-induced cell model (Fig. 3D).

Direct targeting relationship between miR-302a-3p and EPHA2. A–B. Dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that miR-302a-3p directly targeted EPHA2. C. EPHA2 expression was significantly higher in the IDD group compared to the control group. D. Upregulation of miR-302a-3p led to a significant decrease in EPHA2 expression. ns indicates no statistical significance. ns means no statistical significance *** means P < 0.001. Independent sample t-test and non-parametric tests were the statistical methods employed

In the LPS-induced cell damage model of IDD, miR-302a-3p exerted its biological effects through direct targeting of EPHA2.

miR-302a-3p promoted the proliferation of LPS-treated cells, and this effect could be reversed by EPHA2 overexpression (Fig. 4A). Furthermore, miR-302a-3p inhibited LPS-induced apoptosis, and EPHA2 overexpression attenuated this protective effect (Fig. 4B). Additionally, miR-302a-3p alleviated LPS-induced inflammatory responses, which could be weakened by EPHA2 overexpression (Fig. 4C).

miR-302a-3p exerted protective effects in the LPS-induced IDD cell damage model by targeting EPHA2. A. miR-302a-3p promoted the proliferation of LPS-treated cells, an effect that was reversed by EPHA2 overexpression. B. miR-302a-3p inhibited LPS-induced apoptosis, and this protective effect was abrogated by EPHA2 overexpression. C. miR-302a-3p alleviated the LPS-induced inflammatory response, which was counteracted by EPHA2 overexpression. *** means P < 0.001. Non-parametric tests were used as the statistical methods in this study

Discussion

The pathological basis of IDD primarily involves the dysfunction of NP cells [31]. Aberrant expression of miRNAs, as key regulatory molecules, may play a crucial role in IDD progression [32, 33]. This study enrolled 130 IDD patients and 130 healthy controls and systematically investigated the role of miR-302a-3p in IDD. Our study first demonstrated that miR-302a-3p was downregulated in IDD serum and that it could attenuate NP cell damage by directly targeting and inhibiting EPHA2, which may offer a novel perspective into the molecular mechanisms underlying IDD.

miR-302a-3p is significantly downregulated in melanoma cell lines [34], as well as in non-small cell lung cancer cells and tissues [35]. In this study, we observed a significant reduction in miR-302a-3p expression in the IDD patients’ serum, and ROC curve analysis further confirmed its potential utility as a biomarker for the auxiliary diagnosis of IDD. In cellular models, LPS-induced simulation of the IDD pathological microenvironment resulted in decreased miR-302a-3p expression. Notably, miR-302a-3p overexpression significantly restored cell proliferation, attenuated LPS-induced apoptosis, and effectively suppressed the production of TNF-α and IL-1β. These findings suggested that miR-302a-3p may alleviate inflammatory damage during IDD progression by modulating the inflammatory microenvironment. This mechanism aligns with previous findings: miR-302a-3p overexpression increases neuronal counts, reduces S100B and GFAP concentrations, and decreases TNF-α and IL-1β levels, thereby promoting nerve repair and mitigating cisplatin-induced brain injury [36]. Moreover, miR-302a-3p overexpression inhibits proliferation and induces apoptosis in the gastric cancer cell line SGC-7901 by directly targeting its expression [37], and it also exhibits protective effects in a pulmonary fibrosis model [38].

EPHA2, a pro-inflammatory mediator, contributes to the progression of neurodegenerative diseases [27, 39]. Accumulating evidence indicates that the EphA2 receptor mediates inflammatory responses under conditions such as tissue injury, ischemia, and chronic inflammation [29]. Our results demonstrated that EPHA2 expression was markedly elevated in the IDD group and following LPS treatment, whereas miR-302a-3p significantly suppressed its expression. Moreover, EPHA2 overexpression effectively reversed the promoting effect of miR-302a-3p on LPS-induced cell proliferation, its inhibitory effect on apoptosis, and its anti-inflammatory activity. The aberrant upregulation of EPHA2 in IDD may exacerbate disease progression by activating downstream pro-inflammatory and pro-apoptotic signaling pathways [40, 41]. Therefore, the miR-302a-3p-mediated inhibition of EPHA2 may represent a promising therapeutic target for interrupting this pathological cascade.

Clinically, measuring miR-302a-3p expression in samples such as blood may enable the early identification of potential cases before obvious clinical symptoms manifest, thereby facilitating early diagnosis. Furthermore, combining miR-302a-3p expression with that of EPHA2 may aid in the clinical stratification of IDD patients. For individuals exhibiting significantly reduced miR-302a-3p expression and markedly elevated EPHA2 expression, more aggressive treatment strategies may be warranted to enhance therapeutic outcomes and quality of life. MRI, a widely utilized imaging modality, can depict structural changes such as intervertebral disc signal alterations, height reduction, and annulus fibrosus tears, making it suitable for assessing the extent and severity of early- to mid-stage disc degeneration [42]. Although non-invasive, MRI was associated with relatively high costs. In contrast, lumbar discography is an invasive diagnostic procedure whose primary value lies in evaluating IDD structural integrity through contrast agent distribution and identifying the “responsible segment” by integrating pain provocation testing [43]. It served as a crucial tool for linking imaging findings with clinical symptoms and plays an irreplaceable role in determining the surgical level, particularly in cases involving multi-segment degeneration. However, this technique was operator-dependent and carries risks such as infection and increased pain, thus limiting its suitability for early screening or disease monitoring [44]. In contrast, serum miR-302a-3p, as a non-invasive biomarker, not only holds promise for early screening but also surpasses traditional imaging modalities such as MRI in terms of ease of sample collection, operational simplicity, and cost efficiency. This characteristic may offer a more flexible and practical approach to the overall management of IDD.

However, this study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. The potential therapeutic effects of miR-302a-3p were not thoroughly investigated in animal models. During the recruitment process, a potential selection bias existed, representing one of the primary limitations of this study. This bias may compromise the external validity and generalizability of the findings. The sample size was relatively small, and all participants were recruited from a single medical institution, leading to high homogeneity in genetic background, regional environment, lifestyle, and healthcare-seeking behavior. Consequently, the generalizability of the results to broader populations and their reproducibility across diverse population structures may be limited. Furthermore, the study did not provide more granular age stratification analyses, which could influence the accuracy of assessing the biomarker’s diagnostic performance. Therefore, the clinical utility of miR-302a-3p as a diagnostic biomarker requires further validation through larger-scale, multicenter, and prospective studies to comprehensively evaluate its diagnostic accuracy and practical applicability.

Conclusion

In conclusion, miR-302a-3p effectively counteracted the inhibitory effects of LPS on cell proliferation, attenuated LPS-induced apoptosis, and suppressed the associated inflammatory response by directly targeting and downregulating EPHA2, thereby highlighting its potential regulatory function in mitigating LPS-induced cellular damage.

Mathematical Analysis and Modeling of Ebola Virus Dynamics via Optimal Control and Neural Network Paradigms
Noor Muhammad, Md. Nur Alam, Zhang Shiqing

Abstract
Ebola virus disease is a severe, often deadly form of hemorrhagic fever, and is rapidly transmissible. It’s spread through human contact is increased through the direct exchange of infected blood, body fluids, and organs, as well as contact with surfaces on which these infected fluids have been dropped. Ebola’s complicated dynamics spread beyond integer order models, representing a central intersection for the application of machine learning to control theory. In the present study, we construct an innovative prime framework of fractional order calculus which integrates data-driven machine learning with optimal control in order to model and analyze the Ebola virus disease. We develop an eight-compartment model based on the Caputo fractional derivative to incorporate the non-Markovian memory effects inherent in the disease’s progression and regression. The system explicitly structures the distinct transmission pathways from symptomatic and asymptomatic infections, as well as the critical transmission dynamics associated with deceased individuals. We analytically prove the global well-posedness of the model, compute the basic reproduction number ℛ 0 \mathcal{R}_{0} to characterize the epidemic threshold, and prove global stability theorems. Sensitivity analysis indicates that the basic reproduction number is most critically sensitive to transmission rate, incubation period, and the relative infectivity of the deceased. The treatment-safe burial synergy optimization control provides a simultaneous 86.5% of the target morbidity and mortality rate control of the population. The safe burial host intervention is the most effective control. Our study categorizes the best control strategies and orders them in terms of effectiveness. The proposed disease-informed neural network achieves practically perfect predictive accuracy and describe the test set R 2 R^{2} values of 0.991 - 0.999, and correspondingly 99.1% - 99.9% prediction accuracy as well as align with the real-world epidemic behavior. The first successful combination of process-based modeling with artificial intelligence for dealing an outbreak of an infectious disease has been established in this work. This work presents an innovative mechanistic modeling framework for infectious diseases, which has the potential to advance epidemic response analysis.

Preliminaries

This part sets out the basic principles of fractional calculus which is essential to the construction and analysis of our model.

Definition 2.1
(Caputo Fractional Derivative)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
and
u\in C^{1}([0,T])
. For any function
u
which is continuously differentiable in the closure
[0,T]
the Caputo fractional derivative of order
\alpha
is defined in
[
29
]
as follows:

{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{-\alpha}u^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau.

This operator is preferred for modeling dynamical systems as it accommodates standard initial conditions, and
{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}u(t)\to u^{\prime}(t)
as
\alpha\to 1^{-}
, recovering the classical integer-order derivative.

Definition 2.2
(Fractional Lyapunov Stability)

The equilibrium point
y^{*}
of the fractional-order system
{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}y(t)=f(t,y(t))
is said to be
Mittag-Leffler stable
if

\|y(t)-y^{*}\|\leq\left[m(y(0)-y^{*})E_{\alpha,1}(-\lambda t^{\alpha})\right]^{b},

where
\alpha\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\geq 0
,
b>0
,
m(0)=0
, and
m(y)\geq 0
. Mittag-Leffler stability implies asymptotic stability
[
48
]
.

Theorem 2.1
(Stability Condition for Fractional Systems)

Let
\alpha\in(0,1]
. An equilibrium point
y^{*}
of the autonomous system
{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}y(t)=f(y(t))
is asymptotically stable if all eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
of the Jacobian matrix
J=\partial f/\partial y|_{y^{*}}
satisfy the condition
[
48
]
:

|\arg(\lambda_{i})|>\frac{\alpha\pi}{2},\quad\forall i.

This condition generalizes the classical Routh-Hurwitz criterion to fractional-order systems.

Mathematical Analysis

4.1
Well-Posedness of the Model

We establish the mathematical well-posedness of the fractional-order Ebola system through existence and uniqueness analysis.

Theorem 4.1
(Existence and Uniqueness)

Given any
\alpha\in(0,1]
, and initial conditions

\mathbf{X}_{0}=(S_{0},V_{0},E_{0},I_{s0},I_{a0},H_{0},D_{0},R_{0})\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{8}
, the fractional-order system (
2
) has the unique solution
\mathbf{X}(t)=(S(t),V(t),E(t),I_{s}(t),I_{a}(t),H(t),D(t),R(t))
for all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Define the state vector
\mathbf{X}(t)=(S,V,E,I_{s},I_{a},H,D,R)^{T}
and express the system as:

{}^{C}D_{t}^{\alpha}\mathbf{X}(t)=\mathbf{F}(t,\mathbf{X}(t)),\quad\mathbf{X}(0)=\mathbf{X}_{0}.

The equivalent Volterra integral formulation is:

\mathbf{X}(t)=\mathbf{X}_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\mathbf{F}(s,\mathbf{X}(s))ds.

(3)

Assume the operator
\mathcal{T}:C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{8})\to C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{8})
defined by:

(\mathcal{T}\mathbf{X})(t)=\mathbf{X}_{0}+\frac{1}{\Gamma(\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\alpha-1}\mathbf{F}(s,\mathbf{X}(s))ds.

For
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{Y}\in C([0,T],\mathbb{R}^{8})
with
\|\mathbf{X}\|=\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\|\mathbf{X}(t)\|_{\infty}
, the Lipschitz continuity of
\mathbf{F}
follows from bounded parameters and finite state variables over compact intervals. Specifically, there exists
L>0
such that:

\|\mathbf{F}(t,\mathbf{X})-\mathbf{F}(t,\mathbf{Y})\|_{\infty}\leq L\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\|_{\infty}.

The contraction estimate yields:

\|\mathcal{T}\mathbf{X}-\mathcal{T}\mathbf{Y}\|\leq\frac{L\tau^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}\|\mathbf{X}-\mathbf{Y}\|.

Selecting
\tau>0
such that
\frac{L\tau^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}<1
ensures
\mathcal{T}
is a contraction on
C([0,\tau],\mathbb{R}^{8})
. Banach’s fixed point theorem guarantees a unique local solution
\mathbf{X}^{*}\in C([0,\tau],\mathbb{R}^{8})
.

Global extension to
[0,\infty)
follows from the linear growth condition
\|\mathbf{F}(t,\mathbf{X})\|_{\infty}\leq M(1+\|\mathbf{X}\|_{\infty})
for some
M>0
, which precludes finite-time blow-up. Iterative continuation establishes global existence and uniqueness.

\square

(Invariance and Attractivity)

),

]

Network Architecture and Training

The neural network employs hidden layers with hyperbolic tangent activations, mapping temporal inputs to eight state variables. Training combines Xavier initialization with Adam optimization, while automatic differentiation computes fractional derivatives to maintain physical constraints across the temporal domain.

8.3
Parameter Estimation and Calibration

Model calibration simultaneously estimates epidemiological parameters

9
Conclusion

This work has been done to enhance the mathematical framework of forecasting and possibility of intervention regarding the outbreaks of Ebola Virus Disease (EVD). The identified primary objectives focused on the creation of an advanced fractional order model to capture the EVD spread dynamics through adaptive multilayered channels which is considered to be extremely complex, formulation of reliable epidemiological thresholds, the defining of appropriate intervention strategies, and the creation of a novel computational model on the estimation of parameters along with the model standardization. Integration of the three disciplines of mathematics: fractional calculus, optimal control theory, and scientific machine learning, was the original part of this research. EVD transmission dynamics required memory incorporated non-Markovian multi-scale dynamics. Thus, an eight-compartment fractional-order model was built around the Caputo derivative. The model well-posedness was aided by the foundational fixed-point theory, the subsequent next-generation matrix approach to compartment analysis enabled the reproduction number
\mathcal{R}_{0}
calculation, and the complete fractal Lyapunov characterization for the system imbued a formative influence to the system. Utilizing Pontryagin’s Maximum Principle, the optimal control problem which was built sequentially around four time-adjustable control interventions dedicated to fractional control systems was solved. The main breakthrough was the design of the Disease-Informed Neural Network (DINN) framework, which dynamically computes the mechanistic parameters by embedding the governing fractional differential equations as physical constraints in a neural network and solving for parameter estimation. The analysis brings to light three key findings, the epidemiological threshold
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, unique bifurcation properties at the critical points tied to
\mathcal{R}_{0}
, and the findings on global stability which show a forward bifurcation at
\mathcal{R}_{0}=1
. One of the most important parameters indicating potential outbreak from the tiered hierarchy parameters from a sensitivity analysis are the transmission rate (
\beta
), the incubation rate (
\sigma
), and deceased infectiousness (
\eta_{d}
). Results from optimal control show that combining treatment with safe burial, which highlights the synergistic nature of the interventions, results in the greatest impact of an 86.5% decrease in mortality relative to the impact of each intervention applied in isolation. All epidemiological compartments also show outstanding predictive accuracy with the DINN context, as seen in the coefficients of determination
R^{2}
which lie between 0.9987 and 0.9999, coupled with recovering system parameters with less than 18% accuracy for most primary transmission and progression rates.
Although the scope of the model are comprehensive, some simplifications are made to the assumptions of the epidemiology regarding spatial diversity, age-structured contact matrices, and stochastic transmission. While parameter estimation is robust, and within the DINN framework, it is still a function of the investigation data quality and handling. It is definitely worth revealing that optimal control theory, while developing practical strategies, does have the control theory limits as the minor, and in practice resource partial, bounds. The gaps mentioned in the research offer many opportunities for prospective work. Adding spatial dynamics and meta-population structures would improve the model’s ability to channel cross regional transmission and tailored modified intervention impacts. Further model improvement would arise from the inclusion of stochastic elements that measure the risk of destruction and the chances of restoration of an outbreak. Other innovative applications of the DINN framework would be in the area of predictive public health for automatic adaptive real-time intervention planning to control ongoing outbreaks. This would be an immense advance in public health. Also, implementing this integrated mathematical framework with machine learning to other emerging infectious diseases would extremely transform the epidemic awareness and response framework. This study underscores how the integration of advanced techniques in mathematics and contemporary computing techniques provides valuable tools for public health. It illustrates the transition of epidemiology from purely descriptive to prescriptive, allowing for active intervention in the control of outbreaks.

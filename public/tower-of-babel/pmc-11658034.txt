Flipping the script: The benefits of inverted classrooms in oncology education
Authors: Zineb Dahbi, Mohammed Fareh, Ghassane Elomri, Moussaab Rachid, Bouchra Daher, Loubna Taali, Youssef Ghaddou
Published: 2024

Introduction

The reversed classroom model is an instructional approach that reverses the traditional classroom configuration by having interns engage with course materials and learning activities outside of the classroom and reserving class time for interactive activities such as discussions, problem solving, and group work. In medical education, the flipped classroom model can be an effective way to engage interns in active learning and give them the opportunity to apply what they have learned in real-world situations.[1]

The inverted classroom model can also help medical interns stay engaged and motivated because they have more control over their learning and can interact with their peers and instructors in real time. In addition, it can help instructors better assess student understanding and provide targeted support when needed.[2]

However, the reverse classroom approach requires careful planning and preparation, as well as a willingness to adopt new teaching techniques. It is also important to ensure that all interns have access to the technology and resources they need to participate in learning activities.[3]

Conclusion

This research outlines the potential of the flipped classroom model in oncology education. The paper inaugurates a comprehensive mixed-method approach that combines traditional survey data with innovative virtual interactions; it sheds light on previously unexplored dimensions of flipped classroom impact, highlighting increased accessibility to learning materials, enhanced collaborative learning experiences, and student empowerment through self-directed learning. As an urgent call to action, this study not only justifies the feasibility of flipped classrooms in oncology education but also argues for a paradigm shift in medical education and urging educators, policymakers, and stakeholders to grasp the untapped potential of this innovative approach and investigate it further.

By integrating these activities into the oncology course, instructors can create an engaging and interactive learning experience that supports student learning and promotes a deeper understanding of the material. The focus is on student-centered learning, where interns are active participants in the learning process, rather than passive recipients of information.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
Y
be an arbitrary vector space. A functional
\rho:Y\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is called a modular if for arbitrary
u,v\in Y
;

(1)

\rho(u)=0
if and only if
u=0
.

(2)

\rho(\alpha u)=\rho(u)
for every scalar
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
.

(3)

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\rho(u)+\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
.

If (
1
) is replaced by:

(4)

\rho(\alpha u+\beta v)\leq\alpha\rho(u)+\beta\rho(v)
if and only if
\alpha+\beta=1
and
\alpha,\beta\geq 0
, then we say that
\rho
is a convex modular.

A modular
\rho
defines a corresponding modular space, i.e., the vector space
Y_{\rho}
given by:

Y_{\rho}=\{u\in Y:\rho(\lambda u)\rightarrow 0\text{ as }\lambda\rightarrow 0\}.

A function modular is said to be satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition
\rho
if there exist
\tau>0
such that
\rho(2u)\leq\tau\rho(u)
for all
u\in Y_{\rho}
.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
and
u
be in
Y_{\rho}
. Then:

(1)

The sequence
\left\{u_{n}\right\}
, with
u_{n}\in Y_{\rho}
, is
\rho
-convergent to
u
and write:
u_{n}\to u
if
\rho\left(u_{n}-u\right)\to 0
as
n\rightarrow\infty
.

(2)

The sequence
\{u_{n}\}
, with
u_{n}\in Y_{\rho}
, is called
\rho
-Cauchy if

\rho\left(u_{n}-u_{m}\right)\to 0
as
n:m\to\infty
.

(3)

Y_{\rho}
is called
\rho
-complete if every
\rho
-Cauchy sequence in
Y_{\rho}
is
\rho
-convergent.

Proposition 1.3
.

In modular space,

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u
and a is a constant vector, then
u_{n}+a\overset{\rho}{\to}u+a
.

•

If
u_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}u

then
\alpha u_{n}+\beta v_{n}\overset{\rho}{\to}\alpha u+\beta v
, where
\alpha+\beta\leq 1

\alpha,\beta\geq 1
.

Definition 4.1
.

Let

be a linear space over
\mathbb{C}
. The application
\|\cdot\|:X\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is an
F
-norm if

(1)

\|x\|=0
if and only if
u=0
.

(2)

\|\alpha u\|=\|u\|
for avery
u\in X
and every
\alpha
with
|\alpha|=1
.

(3)

\|u+v\|\leq\|u\|+\|v\|
for every
u,v\in X
.

(4)

\left\|\alpha_{n}u\right\|\rightarrow 0
implies
\alpha_{n}\rightarrow 0
.

(5)

\left\|\alpha u_{n}\right\|\rightarrow 0
implies
u_{n}\rightarrow 0
.

Letting
d(u,v)=\|u-v\|
. So
(X,d)
is a metric space which called
F
-space if
d
is complete.

If
\|\alpha u\|=|\alpha|^{\beta}\|u\|
for all
u\in X
and
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
, then
\|\cdot\|
is called
\beta
-homogeneous
(\beta>0)
.

A
\beta
-homogeneous

-space is called a

-homogenous Banach space.

Remark 4.2

If

Theorem 4.3

Let

-homogeneous complex Banach spaces and

\varphi(x,y,z)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{4^{\beta j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}y,2^{j-1}z\right)<\infty.

for all

Proof.

Then by simple induction, we have

\left\|\frac{1}{4^{k}}\phi\left(2^{k}x\right)-\phi(x)\right\|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{k}\frac{1}{4^{\beta j}}\alpha\left(2^{j-1}x,2^{j-1}x,0\right)

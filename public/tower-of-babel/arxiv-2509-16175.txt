Instead of working with

\varphi^{\vee}\circ\varphi=[2]_{E}\qquad\qquad{\text{\rm and}}\qquad\qquad\varphi\circ\varphi^{\vee}=[2]_{E/C}.

The classical Hecke correspondence

Let

be a modular form of weight

(later we will choose

). Informally,

assigns to each elliptic curve a number, but with a scaling rule of weight
k
when we change coordinates. If
\psi\colon E\mapsto E^{\prime}

\omega_{E^{\prime}}
is a nowhere-vanishing differential on
E^{\prime}
, then its pullback
\psi^{*}\omega_{E^{\prime}}
is a nowhere-vanishing differential on

(1.1)

\psi^{*}\omega_{E^{\prime}}\;=\;\alpha(\psi)\,\omega_{E}\qquad\text{for some }\alpha(\psi)\in K^{\times},

where

is the base field. For a concrete Weierstrass model

E:y^{2}=4x^{3}+ax+b,

one may take the invariant differential
\omega_{E}=dx/y
and compute
\psi^{*}(dx^{\prime}/y^{\prime})=c\,dx/y
. Because

has weight

, its

\psi^{*}F
transforms by the factor
\alpha(\psi)^{k}

fairly
, we remove this trivial scaling and consider the

\Psi_{\psi}(F)\ :=\ \alpha(\psi)^{-k}\,\psi^{*}F.

This is now an honest

(1.2)

\Psi\ :=\ \Psi_{\varphi}(F),\qquad\Psi^{\ast}\ :=\ \Psi_{\varphi^{\vee}}(F).

We then package their “difference from symmetry” into the single expression

(1.3)

L_{2}(F)\ :=\ \frac{\Psi}{\Psi^{\ast}}-1\ =\ \frac{\alpha(\varphi)^{-k}\,\varphi^{*}F}{\alpha(\varphi^{\vee})^{-k}\,(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}F}-1.

The key point is that

is separable in char.
p
. Therefore, the induced map is étale over on the special fiber and on completed local rings carries a uniformizer to a unit multiple of a uniformizer, so
\varphi^{*}u=c\,u+O(u^{2})
and
(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}u=c^{-1}u+O(u^{2})
for some
c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
For the unit
F
, we have

\varphi^{*}F=U\bigl(1+A_{1}(c\,u+O(u^{2}))+O(u^{2})\bigr)=U\bigl(1+A_{1}c\,u+O(u^{2})\bigr),

and similarly
(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}F=U\bigl(1+A_{1}c^{-1}u+O(u^{2})\bigr)
. Taking the ratio and expanding,

\frac{\varphi^{*}F}{(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}F}=\bigl(1+A_{1}c\,u+O(u^{2})\bigr)\bigl(1-A_{1}c^{-1}u+O(u^{2})\bigr)=1+A_{1}(c-c^{-1})u+O(u^{2}),

so the constant term is
1
and the first nonzero term is linear in
u
.
Therefore, we have
v_{\widetilde{P}}(\delta)=1
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1

We describe the effect of prime-to-
p
isogenies on the completed local moduli in the Hasse-normalized coordinate.
In particular, we show that any such isogeny acts to first order by a nonzero scalar on the deformation parameter, with the dual isogeny inverting that scalar, that we will use repeatedly when linearizing expressions.

Lemma 7

(Lucas–Kummer valuation)
.

Let
(R,\mathfrak{m})
be a complete local
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with valuation
v
given by
\mathfrak{m}
-adic order. Suppose
\delta\in\mathfrak{m}
with
v(\delta)=1
. Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
and write
t=p^{n}r
with
(r,p)=1
. Then we have

v\big((1+\delta)^{t}-1\big)\ =\ p^{n}.

Proof.

Use the binomial theorem in characteristic
p
, we have

(1+\delta)^{t}-1=\sum_{m=1}^{t}\binom{t}{m}\,\delta^{m}.

By Lucas’ theorem,
\binom{t}{m}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
iff each base-
p
digit of
m
is
\leq
the corresponding digit of
t
. The
smallest

m\geq 1
with this property is
m=p^{n}
. Since
v(\delta^{m})=m
and all other terms have strictly larger valuation, the minimum valuation is
p^{n}
, attained uniquely at
m=p^{n}
. This is Kummer’s “first carry” viewpoint from the
p
-adic valuation of binomial coefficients. Hence the claim follows.
∎

We show that at every supersingular point,
L_{2}(\Delta^{t})
has
u
-adic order exactly
p^{\nu_{p}(t)}
, where
u
is a uniformizer of the Hasse divisor. Then
A_{p}^{\,j}\mid L_{2}(\Delta^{t})
for all
j\leq p^{\nu_{p}(t)}
. In other words, this confirms (C4) for
f=\Delta^{t}
. Conditions (C1)–(C3) for
f=\Delta^{t}
are previously verified in
[
10
]
.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Now let
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\vee}
be as in Lemma
6
, lifting the two legs of the
\Gamma_{0}(2)

correspondence. Fix a supersingular point
\widetilde{P}
and a Hasse-normalized parameter

u
so that
u=0
cuts out the supersingular divisor (Lemma
2
).
At
\widetilde{P}
the discriminant
\Delta
is a unit in the completed local ring, hence
\Delta^{t}
is a unit
U\in\widehat{\mathcal{O}}^{\times}_{X,\widetilde{P}}
.
As a unit in the
u
-adic ring, we may and do write

\Delta^{t}=U\,(1+A(u)),

with
U(0)\neq 0
and
A(u)=O(u)
.

Since
\psi
is prime to
p
, it is separable and
\psi^{*}\omega=\alpha(\psi)\,\omega
with
\alpha(\psi)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}

(see
[
9
, §1.1]
,
[
16
, Prop. III.6.1]
). Therefore, we have that
\alpha(\psi)^{p-1}=1
, and because
12t\equiv 0\pmod{p-1},
we have
\alpha(\psi)^{12t}=1
for
\psi=\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}
.
In a Hasse-normalized trivialization of the Hodge line, a weight
k
form pulls back along a
separable isogeny
\psi
by the factor
\alpha(\psi)^{k}
on invariant differentials (first-order
description in Lemma
6
). Taking
k=12t
for
f=\Delta^{t}
, we obtain

\psi^{*}(\Delta^{t})=U\cdot\alpha(\psi)^{12t}\cdot\big(1+A_{\psi}(u)\big),\qquad A_{\psi}(u)=O(u).

Since
\alpha(\psi)\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
has order dividing
p-1
(for example, see §1.1 of
[
9
]
or Prop. III.6.1 of
[
16
]
) and
12t\equiv 0\pmod{p-1}

(by
t=\tfrac{i(p^{2}-1)}{12}
), we have
\alpha(\psi)^{12t}=1
for
\psi=\varphi,\varphi^{\vee}
. Therefore
the constant Hodge factors cancel in

L_{2}(\Delta^{t})=\frac{\varphi^{*}(\Delta^{t})}{(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}(\Delta^{t})}-1=U\!\left(\frac{1+A_{\varphi}(u)}{1+A_{\varphi^{\vee}}(u)}-1\right).

By Lemma 6, to first order we have

\varphi^{*}u=c\,u+O(u^{2}),\qquad(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}u=c^{-1}u+O(u^{2}),\qquad c\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times},

and hence

\frac{\varphi^{*}u}{(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}u}=c^{2}\left(1+O(u)\right),\qquad v_{\widetilde{P}}(\delta)=1.

Since
\Delta

\Delta=U_{0}\,(1+\beta(u))\qquad(U_{0}(0)\neq 0,\ \beta(u)=O(u)).

\varphi^{*}(\Delta^{t})=U\,(1+\beta(\varphi^{*}u))^{t},\qquad(\varphi^{\vee})^{*}(\Delta^{t})=U\,(1+\beta((\varphi^{\vee})^{*}u))^{t},

.

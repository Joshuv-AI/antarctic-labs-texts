Therapeutic potential of the LUCAT1/miR- 2278/STAT5A axis in pulmonary fibrosis
Authors: Juan Huang, Weiqiang Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundPulmonary fibrosis is a debilitating, progressive lung disorder marked by insidious onset and relentless decline. Emerging evidence highlights the regulatory significance of long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) in fibrotic processes.MethodsThis study investigated the mechanistic role of lncRNA LUCAT1 in pulmonary fibrosis using TGF-β-stimulated A549 cells as an in vitro model. The methodologies employed include qPCR and Western blot to assess gene and protein expression, CCK-8 assays to evaluate cell viability, cell migration assays for assessing mobility, and dual-luciferase reporter assays to analyze miRNA interactions.ResultsIn A549 cells treated with TGF-β, LUCAT1 expression increased. After LUCAT1 was silenced, both cell vitality and exercise ability promoted by TGF-β were reduced. Inhibition of LUCAT1 increases E-cadherin levels and decreases the expression of α-SMA and Collagen I. LUCAT1 interacts with miR-2278. Blocking miR-2278 prevented the reduction in proliferation, migration, and epithelial-to-mesenchymal transformation (EMT) in TGF-β-treated A549 cells due to knock-out of LUCAT1. Similarly, inhibition of miR-2278 also eliminated the decline in proliferation, migration, and EMT in TGF-β stimulated A549 cells due to inhibition of LUCAT1.ConclusionLUCAT1 knockdown targeted STAT5A by sponging miR-2278 to inhibit the TGF-β-induced proliferation, migration, and EMT in A549 cells, stretching the treatment strategy of lung epithelial injury.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12890-025-03997-z.

Introduction

Pulmonary fibrosis is a progressive and often fatal interstitial lung disease that may be associated with risk factors such as chronic lung injury, severe infections, or environmental exposures [1]. Currently, the treatment methods for pulmonary fibrosis are mainly to prevent the deterioration of the condition and protect lung function. There is no particularly effective targeted treatment method [2]. Therefore, finding key treatment targets from various factors that cause fibrosis is very important for preventing and controlling pulmonary fibrosis.

Long non-coding RNA (lncRNA) refers to an RNA that is more than 200 nucleotides long and does not encode a protein, but is involved in the occurrence and development of many human diseases [3]. LncRNA can affect gene activity in a variety of ways, such as regulating gene transcription, participating in chromatin modification complexes, affecting miRNA maturity and stability, and can adsorb miRNA like a “sponge” [3, 4]. In recent years, scientists have paid increasing attention to the role of lncRNA in pulmonary fibrosis [5–7].

LncRNA LUCAT1 (also known as SCAL1) is abnormally expressed in many cancers, participates in the unique biological processes and mechanisms of tumorigenesis and development, and may be useful in cancer screening, diagnosis, gene therapy and prognostic assessment [8–13]. Some studies have found that LUCAT1 affects the behavior of lung cancer cells and may be involved in diabetes-related lung disease, but its role in pulmonary fibrosis is unclear [13–16].

Therefore, we suspect that LUCAT1 may also have a role in pulmonary fibrosis. TGF-β is an important driver of fibrosis and is considered to be the core regulator of pulmonary fibrosis. We treated A549 cells with TGF-β and studied the function and regulatory pathways of LUCAT1, which provided a new perspective for understanding the pathogenesis of pulmonary fibrosis.

Materials and methods

Cell lines and cell transfection

The human A549 alveolar epithelial cells we used were placed in DMEM culture medium, added with 10% fetal bovine serum, and grown at 37 °C. When the cells were nearly 80% full, they were treated with 10 ng/ml TGF-β for 24 h. Lipofectamine 2000 (Invitrogen, USA) was used for transfection. LUCAT-siRNA, as well as mimetics and inhibitors of miR-2278, were purchased from Ribobio (Guangzhou, China).

Quantitative real-time PCR (qPCR)

After 24 hours of treatment or transfection, we extracted Total RNA from A549 cells using Trizol (Solarbio, Beijing, China). The isolated RNA was then converted into cDNA using a miRNA-or mRNA-specific reverse transcription kit (CoWin, Beijing, China). qPCR was performed using miRNA or mRNA qPCR Assay Kit (CoWin, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s recommendations. The qPCR setting for miRNA analysis is to first heat at 95 °C for 10 min to untie the DNA, and then repeat this step 45 times: heating at 95 °C for 15 s and heating at 60 °C for 1 min. The qPCR procedure for measuring mRNA is: heating at 95 °C for 10 min, and then repeating this step 40 times: heating at 95 °C for 10 s, annealing at 60 °C for 30 s, and extending at 72 °C for 32 s. We used the 2-ΔΔCt method to calculate the relative expression level, using GAPDH as the internal reference gene [17]. All the primers used in this experiment are listed in Table 1.

Western blot analysis

24 h after treatment or transfection, we extracted the protein using RIPA buffer. Samples were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to a PVDF membrane. The membrane was then blocked with 5% bovine serum albumin and incubated overnight at 4 °C with the primary antibody. After washing with TBS, the membrane reacted with the secondary antibody at 37 °C for 1 h. Protein bands were detected using an ECL kit (Thermo Scientific, USA). Primary antibodies used included STAT5A (13179-1-AP, 1:1000), E-cadherin (20874-1-AP, 1:500), α-SMA (14395-1-AP, 1:2000), and Collagen I (14695-1-AP, 1:1000), which were from Proteintech (Wuhan, China). Band intensity was analyzed using ImageJ software [18].

CCK8 assay

Cell viability was detected using CCK8 assay (Solarbio, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s recommendations. OD value at 450 nm was measured using a spectrophotometer every 24 h.

Cell migration assay

We use the transwell chamber to measure cell movement. Place 1 × 10^5 cells in serum-free medium and add to the upper layer; add to the lower layer DMEM containing 10% fetal bovine serum. The transwell plate was then incubated at 37 °C for 48 h. After fixation, stain with 0.5% crystal violet for 20 min. Finally, five fields of view were randomly selected under the microscope to count the number of cells that migrated [19].

Dual‑luciferase reporter assay

Fluorescent vectors containing wild-type or mutant LUCAT fragments were synthesized by Ribobio (Guangzhou, China). These fluorescent vectors and miR-2278 mimics were co-transfected into A549 cells with empty vector as a control. Firefly luciferase (FLUC) and ranilla luciferase (RLUC) activity were detected using a dual‑luciferase reporter system [20].

Statistical analysis

We used GraphPad Prism 9 version to analyze the data. The results are the mean ± standard deviation of three independent experiments. For the comparisons of multiple groups, we used one-way ANOVA. Prior to performing ANOVA, normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. If the data did not meet the assumption of normality, non-parametric tests, such as the Kruskal-Wallis test, were applied. For comparisons between two groups, the Student’s t-test was used, with the assumption of normal distribution of the data. In cases where normality was not assumed, the Mann-Whitney U test was used. A P value less than 0.05 indicates statistical significance.

Results

We explored the role and regulatory mechanism of LUCAT1 in TGF-β-induced pulmonary fibrosis cell model. Our results proved that LUCAT1 knockdown targeted STAT5A by sponging miR-2278 to inhibit the TGF-β-induced proliferation, migration, and EMT in A549 cells.

LUCAT1 knockdown attenuates cell viability and migration induced by TGF-β in A549 cells

In this study, we incubated A549 cells in 10 ng/ml TGF-β for 24 h to create an in vitro model of pulmonary fibrosis. We first examined the expression of LUCAT1 in A549 cells after exposure to TGF-β. It was found that LUCAT1 levels were elevated in A549 cells treated with TGF-β (Fig. 1A). Subsequently, we examined the effect of LUCAT1 on pulmonary fibrosis. The LUCAT1 specific siRNA was transfected into TGF-β-stimulated A549 cells and the negative siRNA was used as a control (Fig. 1B). The results demonstrated that cell viability was enhanced by TGF-β, while the LUCAT1 knockdown inhibited cell viability in TGF-β-treated A549 cells (Fig. 1C). However, LUCAT1 did not affect A549 cell proliferation without TGF-β stimulation. Therefore, the present study demonstrated that LUCAT1 knockdown affected the growth of A549 cells in a TGF-β-dependent manner (Fig. 1D). From the transwell assay, the migrated cells increased in TGF‑β‑stimulated cells, Knockdown of LUCAT1 significantly inhibited the locomotion ability of A549 cells (Fig. 1E and F). These results suggest that reducing LUCAT1 levels reduces TGF-β-induced enhancement of cell survival and mobility.

LUCAT1 knockdown inhibits TGF-β-induced EMT in A549 cells

EMT is an important inducement of pulmonary fibrosis, including loss of epithelial markers and the increase of interstitial markers. As shown in Fig. 1G and H, TGF-β-induced interstitial markers α-SMA and Collagen I were down-regulated by LUCAT1 knockdown, while the epithelial marker E-cadherin suppressed by TGF-β was up-regulated by LUCAT1 knockdown. The EMT induced by TGF-β was attenuated by LUCAT1 knockdown but did not decrease to the control level.

MiR-2278 binds to LUCAT1 in A549 cells

We predicted miRNAs that contained binding sites with LUCAT1 on Starbase [21] (Fig. 2A) and found that miR-2278 could bind to LUCAT1. Importantly, miR-2278 was down-regulated significantly in TGF‑β‑stimulated A549 cells (Fig. 2B). Figure 2C showed the predicted binding sequence. Then, the binding of miR-2278 and LUCAT1 was verified using dual-luciferase reporter assay. We transferred miR-2278 mimics into A549 cells together with a fluorescent reporter vector carrying wild-type or mutant LUCAT1 fragment, using the empty vector as a control. Co-transfection of miR-2278 mimics and wild-type LUCAT1 significantly inhibited the FLUC/RLUC ratio compared to the control group (Fig. 2D).

MiR-2278 inhibits the expression of STAT5A in A549 cells

Previous studies have found that miR-2278 affects the function of STAT5A mRNAs in chronic myeloid leukemia cells [22]. This time, we further studied the effect of miR-2278 on STAT5A expression in A549 cells. When LUCAT1 was inhibited, miR-2278 levels in A549 cells increased significantly (Fig. 3A). Figure 3B showed the efficiency of miR-2278 knockdown and overexpression. The level of miR-2278 was significantly up-regulated by the miR-2278 mimics and down-regulated by miR-2278 inhibitor, while the non-specific mimics and inhibitor had no effect on miR-2278 expression. MiR-2278 mimics markedly inhibited the mRNA level of STAT5A, while the miR-2278 inhibitor promoted it (Fig. 3C). As predicted, the protein level of STAT5A was also inhibited by miR-2278 mimics and promoted by the miR-2278 inhibitor in A549 cells (Fig. 3D and E).

MiR-2278 inhibitor blocked the inhibition on proliferation, migration and EMT induced by LUCAT1 knockdown in TGF‑β‑stimulated A549 cells

According to the results, I hypothesized that miR-2278, as the direce target of LUCAT1, could affect TGF-β-induced proliferation and migration in A549 cells. LUCAT1 knockdown inhibited TGF-β-induced proliferation, while the miR-2278 inhibitor attenuated the effect of LUCAT1 knockdown in A549 cells (Fig. 4A). Similar results were observed in the transwell assay. The TGF-β-induced migration was inhibited by LUCAT1 knockdown, which was blocked by the miR-2278 inhibitor (Fig. 4B and C).

We used Western blot to detect the levels of EMT-related proteins. Silencing LUCAT1 reduced the expression of α-SMA and Collagen I, while inhibition of miR-2278 partially restored this decrease. In contrast, in TGF‑β-treated A549 cells, knocking down LUCAT1 increased E-cadherin, but the miR-2278 inhibitor pulled E-cadherin back to similar levels in the TGF‑β control group (Fig. 4D and E).

Finally, we detected the expression of STAT5A in each group. As shown in Figs. 4F and G, LUCAT1 knockdown inhibited STAT5A expression, while the miR-2278 inhibitor attenuated this inhibition effect in A549 cells. These results proved that LUCAT1 promoted cell injury induced by TGF‑β through upregulating STAT5A by adsorbing miR-2278 in A549 cells.

Discussion

LUCAT1 is found at 5q14.3, it is 890 bp long. It was identified in the airway epithelium of smokers and can induce oxidative stress [23]. Currently, most of the studies on the function of LUCAT1 are focused on oncology. Research has found that LUCAT1 may be a new indicator that predicts adverse outcomes in breast cancer. LUCAT1 competes with TCF7L2 to bind to miR-5582- 3p, and can also enhance the malignant characteristics of breast cancer stem cells by regulating the Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway [11]. In non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), LUCAT1 promotes cell growth, reduces programmed cell death, and leads to cisplatin resistance by affecting the IGF-2 and miR-514a-3p/ULK1 axes [14, 16]. LUCAT1 is also associated with lymph node metastasis and poor prognosis in NSCLC [24]. LUCAT1 is also involved in the regulation of inflammation. In LPS-activated myeloid cells, LUCAT1 reduces the inflammatory response by targeting STAT1 to inhibit transcription of interferon-stimulated genes [25]. However, its effect on pulmonary fibrosis has not yet been studied.

In the present research, we found that LUCAT1 was up-regulated in TGF‑β‑stimulated A549 cells. Knockdown of LUCAT1 inhibited the proliferation, migration and EMT induced by TGF-β in A549 cells. These results demonstrated that LUCAT1-siRNA inhibited the proliferation, migration and EMT in A549 cells, which indicated that LUCAT1-siRNA might have an inhibitory effect on pulmonary fibrosis. Current research has found that damage to alveolar epithelial cells, “dialogue” between alveolar epithelium and fibroblasts, and the epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) are all associated with the formation of pulmonary fibrosis [26]. EMT promotes fibrosis through its downstream signaling, and if EMT-related effector molecules can be targeted, the development of pulmonary fibrosis can be prevented or slowed down [27, 28]. During EMT, the transcriptional regulation, cytoskeletal structure, intercellular adhesion and extracellular matrix components of epithelial cells all change significantly. Normally, epithelial cells are tightly connected through adhesion mechanisms; but during EMT, they disengage from adjacent cells and migrate into surrounding tissue. Expression of epithelial cell markers decreases, while stromal cell markers increases. We found that LUCAT1 knockdown significantly inhibited α-SMA and Collagen I and promoted E-cadherin expression. Therefore, LUCAT1-siRNA may be an effective anti-TGF-β induced lung cell injury molecule.

Next, we explored the mechanism by which LUCAT1 is involved in TGF-β induced lung cell injury. According to our results, miR-2278 binds to LUCAT1 and blocks the effect of LUCAT1 in TGF‑β‑stimulated A549 cells. Importantly, miR-2278 inhibited the expression of STAT5A in A549 cells. In chronic myeloid leukemia, miR-2278 acts as a tumor suppressor molecule through the downstream functional target STAT5A [22]. STAT is a protein family with signal transduction and transcription regulation functions. There are seven members of the STAT family found in mammals: STAT1, STAT2, STAT3, STAT4, STAT5A, STAT5B and STAT6 [29–31]. This family is closely related to the growth factor signaling system, apoptosis process, and angiogenesis. STAT5A has an important mission to maintain the normal function of cells and regulate cell proliferation and differentiation [32]. STAT5A is significantly overexpressed in NSCLC and promotes the growth and spread of cancer cells [33]. In this study, STAT5A may be involved in cell function and EMT regulation as a LUCAT1 target in TGF‑β‑stimulated A549 cells, suggesting that STAT5A may be a regulator of TGF-β-induced lung cell injury (Fig. 5). Overall, this study makes important contributions by revealing the novel mechanism of the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis in pulmonary fibrosis. Unlike previous studies focused on LUCAT1 in cancer, our work uncovers its involvement in pulmonary fibrosis, suggesting its potential as a biomarker for diagnosis and a therapeutic target for fibrosis-related lung diseases. By investigating the effects of LUCAT1 on TGF-β-induced cellular processes in A549 cells, we provide new insights into the molecular mechanisms driving pulmonary fibrosis, particularly in the context of EMT and its regulation by LUCAT1.Fig. 5LUCAT1 ↑ → sponges miR-2278 → STAT5A ↑

Although this study revealed the new mechanism of the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis in TGF-β-induced pulmonary fibrosis in vitro, several limitations need to be considered. First, the study is based on an in vitro cell model, which does not fully capture the complexity of pulmonary fibrosis in vivo. Therefore, while our findings are promising, they require validation in animal models and ultimately in clinical settings to assess the translational potential. Additionally, while we have focused on the role of LUCAT1 in TGF-β-induced lung cell injury, the broader molecular mechanisms governing pulmonary fibrosis remain to be explored.

Future research should explore the therapeutic implications of targeting the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis in vivo, using animal models to evaluate the efficacy and safety of such interventions. Moreover, extending this research to investigate LUCAT1’s role in other fibrotic diseases could provide a broader understanding of its impact on tissue fibrosis. The clinical relevance of these findings should be assessed by examining LUCAT1 as a biomarker in patient samples, particularly in BALF and TBLC samples from individuals with pulmonary fibrosis. The clinical relevance and translational potential of this axis in patients need to be further verified. Travis WD [34] pointed out that TBLC is less traumatic than SLB in patients with non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis. As a new minimally invasive technique, TBLC can obtain more lung tissue than traditional TBLB, and the diagnostic rate of the new lung tissue sampling technique for interstitial lung disease is estimated to be 80% [35].

Olimpio et al. [36] used real-time fluorescent quantitative PCR to detect transcripts of STAT4, STAT3 and STAT5 in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of mice. In the ARDS mouse model, they manipulated the STAT4/T-bet and STAT3/RORγt pathways, triggering the pro-inflammatory response associated with ARDS.

Jakiela et al. [37] found the local immune response in the airways of CSS patients. The concentration of STAT5A in BALF was significantly increased in active CSS compared with inactive CSS, leading to the aggravation of CSS. Therefore, the change of STAT5A in BALF and the change of STAT5A activation in TBLC tissue can further indicate the activity of the lesion.

Bronchoalveolar lavage is a “liquid biopsy of the alveolar site” that can reflect the immune and inflammatory status of the pulmonary microenvironment. The reproducibility of BALF makes it an ideal dynamic follow-up tool for monitoring the disease behavior of non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis patients, predicting acute exacerbations, and treatment responses. This study will combine BALF multi-omics analysis with transbronchial cryobiopsy to explore a new paradigm for the diagnosis and dynamic follow-up of non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis. The focus is on verifying the diagnostic and follow-up value of the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis in BALF as a potential biomarker, which will be the direction of our next scientific research exploration. We hypothesize that the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis is abnormally expressed in the bronchoalveolar lavage fluid of patients, and its expression level is related to the activity of fibrosis in the lung tissue and can be used to monitor the disease progression and treatment response. We will conduct the next prospective cohort study to collect bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) and transbronchial lung biopsy (TBLC) samples from patients with non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis. The following analyses were performed on BALF:1. Routine cytological analysis; 2. qPCR detection of LUCAT1 and miR-2278, and protein level (such as ELISA) or mRNA detection of STAT5A. TBLC was subjected to pathological and immunohistochemical detection of the expression of STAT5A and other proteins. The expression levels of STAT5A in key cell types (such as proliferative alveolar epithelial cells, fibroblastic foci, and inflammatory infiltration cells) were evaluated. The relationship between LUCAT1/miR-2278 in BALF and STAT5A activation changes in TBLC tissue was further explored. RNA profiles in BALF cells and supernatant accurately reflect the core molecular events of fibrosis.We combined TBLC with BALF. Synergistic effects can be achieved in diagnosis, achieving the effect of “1 + 1 > 2.” The BALF-TBLC combined strategy is an effective minimally invasive tool that can improve diagnostic accuracy and provide dynamic and multidimensional biological insights into disease activity, which may reshape the clinical management path of non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis. Regular clinical assessments, pulmonary function tests, and HRCT should be conducted every 3–6 months, along with repeat BALF sampling at specific time points such as suspected disease progression or mid-treatment; TBLC should only be repeated when absolutely necessary.

BAL helps TBCB determine the diagnosis of DILD, and the combined use does not cause serious complications [38, 39]. The abnormal activation of the LUCAT1/miR-2278/STAT5A axis in BALF is a key molecular event in the activity of non-IPF pulmonary fibrosis, and its dynamic change can predict the disease progression before the traditional indicators, so it can be used as a biological target for minimally invasive monitoring (Fig. 6).

Conclusion

LUCAT1 knockdown targeted STAT5A by sponging miR-2278 to inhibit the TGF-β-induced proliferation, migration and EMT in A549 cells, stretching the treatment strategy of TGF-β-induced lung cell injury.

Optimal planning of grid-connected energy storage and renewable energy sources integration using an improved whale algorithm
Authors: Pranda Prasanta Gupta, Deepak Gupta, Kavita Tukaram Patil, Kishor S Wagh, Chanakya Kumar Jha, Pallavi Jha, Akhilesh Kumar Sharma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe integration of renewable energy sources (RESs) into energy systems poses considerable operational challenges, due to their intermittent and stochastic nature. Grid-connected energy storage systems (ESSs) present a compelling alternative for reliably accommodating various RESs. This paper presents an optimal scheduling for allocating wind-storage system capacity in high mountain regions with abundant wind resources and irrigation pumping demand. To capture uncertainty in wind and solar generation, a scenario-free stochastic formulation is adopted, enabling tractable and scalable uncertainty modelling. To reduce the overall lifecycle comprehensive cost (COC), a synergistic optimization model is developed by integrating features of wind energy, pumping unit parameters, and storage configurations. The model considers startup/shutdown losses, optimal flow distribution, and operating expenses of the pumping station while optimizing energy storage capacity. A single-battery system is compared with a hybrid battery-hydrogen storage strategy. Results show the hybrid solution reduces COC by 5.4%, alleviates battery operational stress, and maintains pumping station efficiency. The framework incorporates a reliability model for wind power generation, ensuring robust lifecycle cost optimization. This approach demonstrates the financial and technological benefits of hybrid storage in renewable energy-driven irrigation systems. Simulations in the case study validate the flexibility and effectiveness of the proposed approach across various practical settings.Keywords: Pumping station, Optimization, Uncertainty, Renewable energy sources, Hybrid energy storage systemSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering

Introduction

Background and motivation

Growing environmental concerns have accelerated the adoption of renewable energy sources (RESs) owing to their sustainability and widespread availability; however, their inherent intermittency and uncertainty introduce considerable challenges to the reliability, stability, and efficient operation of modern energy systems1,2. In addition to demand-side uncertainties, RESs such as wind and photovoltaic (PV) systems contribute further variability due to their strong dependence on weather conditions, thereby increasing system complexity and making optimization problems more challenging to solve3. By 2050, increasing energy efficiency and developing renewable energy technologies will be essential components of the energy transition, according to a report by the International Renewable Energy Agency. Technologies for renewable energy provide substantial socioeconomic advantages, scalability, broad resource availability, and economic feasibility. Renewable energy is expected to contribute significantly to reducing greenhouse gas emissions and to meet two-thirds of the world’s total energy consumption by 20504. Thus, it is crucial to conduct relevant research on power systems capable of efficiently integrating large amounts of renewable energy. Energy security is further complicated by the depletion of fossil fuels and price volatility. In contrast, renewable energy sources are plentiful and widely available, helping reduce dependence on external energy sources and improve energy supply stability5. Energy storage system (ESSs) offer a promising solution for the reliable integration of RESs into energy systems6. Although the precise definition of ESSs is still under discussion in technical forums, they are generally described as groups of interconnected loads and distributed energy resources within clearly defined electrical boundaries that act as single, controllable entities relative to the main grid. Thus, ESSs can import energy from the main grid when local generation is insufficient to meet demand requirements7. Consequently, explicitly incorporating pumped station aging into ESS operation has become critical for sustainable and economically efficient power system operation.

Literature review

Many researchers are currently working on economic dispatching with large-scale integration of renewable electricity8,9. More challenges could arise from the unpredictability and variability of renewable electricity output. As a result, many studies have been conducted on forecasting wind and solar PV power10. Accurate forecasting remains difficult to achieve, and wind power forecasting errors are expected to persist for some time. To address uncertainty in renewable power integration, a multi-timescale optimal scheduling model incorporating demand response and renewable power uncertainty was proposed in11. To account for wind power variation and uncertainty, an adjustable interval optimization model for energy and reserve clearance was introduced in12. Subsets with variable intervals were suggested as more prudent options for cost-effectively managing renewable uncertainties, replacing traditional projected intervals. By using reserve auxiliary variables to connect the reserve under the projected scenario and uncertain scenarios, a wind power reserve model incorporating uncertainty was developed in13. This robust scheduling model quantified the contributions of wind farms to power system frequency support and reserves. Considering the uncertainty in the probability distribution of wind power forecast errors, a distributionally robust coordinated reserve scheduling model was proposed in14 to minimise the total procurement cost of conventional generation and reserves while satisfying security requirements under all possible probability distributions of wind power forecast errors. Additionally, a distributionally robust optimization model was presented in15 for reserve scheduling decision-making with partial information on wind power, seeking a robust solution to wind power probability distribution uncertainty.

In the meantime, high-altitude areas have produced superior, nutrient-rich, and environmentally adaptive speciality crops due to their distinct geographic setting and climate. Nevertheless, these areas face significant problems with their water supply, which largely depends on erratic sources such as seasonal snowfall, rainfall, and glacial meltwater. This causes severe water shortages during dry seasons and directly affects agricultural output. Furthermore, high-altitude areas face major power supply issues due to their distinct geography and climate. In this regard, building high-altitude irrigation pump-station systems has emerged as a viable means to alleviate the unequal distribution of water resources in space and time, thereby ensuring consistent and substantial crop yields while harnessing the full ecological and economic potential of high-altitude farming. A suitable model that accurately describes the optimisation problem, along with an efficient computational technique, is required to enhance the operational efficiency and energy-saving potential of pump stations and to address economic operating challenges. Numerous studies have employed optimisation techniques, including particle swarm optimization (PSO) algorithms16,17, to develop models that minimize the energy consumption and operational costs of pump station systems18,19.

Storage technologies such as hydropower and compressed air (long-term storage), batteries (medium-term storage), flow batteries, flywheels, SMES, and supercapacitors (short-term storage) are frequently used to provide a steady, continuous electrical supply to end consumers. RES are limited to producing energy under specific operating conditions, such as sunny or windy periods, in contrast to conventional fossil-fuel-based generation, which can be dispatched on demand and stored with relative ease20. ESSs improve the system’s overall reliability by allowing excess energy to be stored during periods of high generation or low demand and to be provided when needed21. ESSs also contribute to power quality improvement, frequency control, voltage quality improvement, power fluctuation reduction, and ancillary service providing22. The security and economy of power system operation appear to be compromised by economic dispatch with large-scale wind power integration, which relies solely on ESSs to handle wind power fluctuations and unpredictability. Shortfalls in wind power forecasts can be compensated for by an ESS, which can dynamically absorb and release energy quickly23. For a wind-pumped storage-thermal integrated energy system, an economical and ecological scheduling approach was proposed in24, and the units were logically ordered according to the energy-saving and emission-reduction principles. In order to improve pumped storage units’ regulation performance, the scheduling method optimizes operational decision-making. In25, it was shown that a hybrid ESS with a battery and a supercapacitor could efficiently dispatch utility-scale wind power at one-hour increments for a full day. In26, the coordinated optimal dispatching model for a hydropower group system comprising traditional hydropower and pumped-storage power plants was examined. Wind-photovoltaic-pumped storage mitigates the impact of renewable energy’s unpredictability and intermittency on the power system’s stability. However, to prevent unfavourable effects on the power grid, such as overvoltage, increased power losses, reduced power quality, and reduced network dependability, integrating renewable sources into the current distribution grid as distributed generation requires careful planning and strategy. Hence, determining the optimal penetration levels and deployment strategies for distributed renewable energy sources within the distribution network is of paramount importance.

Accordingly, the development and application of efficient optimization algorithms are essential for the optimal planning of distributed generation in power systems, as they enable mathematical modeling of network components and facilitate the identification of cost-effective and technically feasible solutions that satisfy multiple constraints in complex optimization scenarios. Researchers have explored a wide range of optimization techniques to address the optimal integration of distributed energy sources, combining conventional methods such as Genetic Algorithms (GA)27, Artificial Bee Colony (ABC)28, Biogeography-based Optimization (BBO)29, Whale optimization algorithm30, and Cuckoo Search Algorithms (CSA)31 with more advanced metaheuristic approaches like Harris Hawks Optimization (HHO)32, and Elephant Herding Optimization (EHO)33, where the former provide well-established solution frameworks while the latter offer improved exploration-exploitation balance, faster convergence, and enhanced capability to solve complex, nonlinear, and multi-objective power system problems. The Improved Mayfly Optimization Algorithm (IMO) was created in34 to address the unified economic emission dispatch problem in a microgrid aspect. Additionally, in reference35, Chaotic Fast Convergence Evolutionary Programming was offered as a solution to the combined power and heat economic scheduling problem. This system included pumped hydro storage, renewable energy, and demand-side control. In36,37, the Social Group Entropy Optimisation (SGEO) approach was proposed to address the fuel-constrained economic dispatch problem. This idea incorporates renewable energy sources and a pumped hydro storage facility. Reference38 employs several nature-inspired swarm intelligence (metaheuristic) algorithms for optimal scheduling, and the results are reported based on multiple runs to ensure solution reliability and accuracy. Although traditional methods have simple algorithmic structures and are widely used across many fields, their biggest drawback is that they tend to converge to local optima, leading to poor performance and stability. Meanwhile, more modern algorithms outperform traditional methods. However, they still have disadvantages that need to be addressed, such as slow data processing, heavy dependence on initial setup parameters, low stability, and no guarantee of convergence. The higher the performance and stability of the algorithm, the better the quality of the solution to the optimization problem. Therefore, developing a powerful and efficient algorithm for use in power network problems is very important.

Wind-solar-storage hybrid systems play a pivotal role in next-generation power systems by leveraging the complementary nature of renewable resources and the buffering capability of energy storage to achieve a balance between cost, reliability, and sustainability; this integrated approach is particularly effective in remote or weak-grid regions, where optimized resource allocation, cost management, and scenario-based planning enable dependable power supply while maintaining economic efficiency under varying operating conditions39,40. However, these studies are limited by the absence of comprehensive lifecycle assessments, as they overlook the impact of extreme weather on system durability, depend heavily on static economic assumptions, and fail to incorporate dynamic cost factors such as battery degradation, equipment aging, and maintenance expenses, thereby reducing the accuracy and practicality of long-term economic and reliability evaluations. Several studies41,42 have concentrated on increasing system efficiency through intelligent control and dynamic optimization of wind-solar-storage systems using various algorithms. By using experimental simulations from various dimensions, research43 has improved the practical application of wind-solar-storage systems. They considered the cost-benefit index and the probability of power supply loss as key operational constraints. However, although approaches like the IWA have been utilized to determine optimal ESS sizing and evaluate system performance under different RES scenarios, these investigations predominantly focus on short-term or real-time conditions, thereby lacking a comprehensive assessment of long-term operational stability and the sustained effectiveness of control strategies.

Research gap and paper contribution

Many existing studies do not adequately account for uncertainty in key parameters, such as fluctuations in renewable generation and variations in electrical load demand. Overall, most existing studies focus on the optimal scheduling and energy dispatch of RES and ESSs, with an emphasis on short-term operational stability. These analyses were limited to a long-term planning schedule, without careful spatio-temporal considerations, single-objective operations, or intelligent, algorithm-driven fine-tuning. In addition, limited efforts have been made to assess grid-connected ESSs and the impacts of the decrease in economic benefits of wind-solar PV power and the rise in renewable energy costs if a substantial portion of pumped storage is under large-scale system control to address charging-induced grid operational security. Based on the literature review and the limitations in existing research, two key research gaps are identified:

Capacity configuration model

In the capacity configuration study of wind and solar PV storage systems, although an appropriate increase in hybrid energy storage capacity can improve power supply reliability, it will lead to a significant increase in system investment costs, creating a trade-off between economy and reliability. Therefore, how to achieve the economic optimization of energy storage capacity configuration under the premise of ensuring system performance has become a key issue in current research.

This study systematically explores the cost-effectiveness of two energy storage solutions: a hybrid energy storage device combining batteries and hydrogen storage, and batteries alone.

Problem formulation

It seeks to increase the wind solar energy storage system’s economy and, after verifying that the power supply reliability index satisfies the necessary standards, develops an optimization model to reduce the combined cost over the life cycle (COC) and increase the system’s overall cost-effectiveness.

The operating life of wind and solar storage systems in use is influenced by several factors and typically ranges from 15 to 20 years. Wind turbines typically have a 15-year lifespan, while solar panel components can last 20 years. The lifespan of hydrogen storage systems is 15–20 years, and that of converters is 10–15 years. As a result, these four equipment types only require replacement once over the system’s lifetime, which lowers the cost of system replacement. On the other hand, the battery has a much shorter lifespan-only three to five years-implying it must be replaced up to four times over the power generation system’s 20-year lifespan, resulting in comparatively high replacement costs.

The type of equipment, technical features, market conditions, and industry norms must all be considered when determining the operating and maintenance expenses of a renewable energy system. Based on current market data and research, the operating and maintenance costs are set at 2.5% annually for wind power generation facilities, 2% annually for photovoltaic arrays, 1.5% annually for battery systems, and 1.8% annually for energy storage converters.

Improved whale algorithm

In this study, the model is developed using the Improved Whale Algorithm (IWA), which builds upon the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA)35 proposed by Mirjalili et al. in 2016 at Griffith University, Australia, recognized as a population-based optimization technique characterized by a simple structure, minimal parameter requirements, fast convergence, and ease of implementation. The key difference between the Improved Whale Optimization Algorithm (IWA)36 and the conventional WOA lies in the incorporation of the Grey Wolf Optimization hunting mechanism during the development phase. Specifically, the Grey Wolf Algorithm’s position update equation is integrated to enhance the bubble-net attacking strategy. The standard WOA identifies optimal solutions by mimicking three hunting behaviors of humpback whales: encircling prey, searching for prey, and the bubble-net feeding mechanism. It effectively combines global and local search capabilities, with its strength rooted in the adaptive adjustment of parameters to balance exploration and exploitation throughout the iterations. In the global search phase, the algorithm utilizes both encircling and random search strategies, enabling flexible changes in search direction. In contrast, the local search phase focuses on the bubble-net attacking mechanism to refine solutions around the current best candidate. Second, to enhance solution acceptance while maintaining strong global exploration, a conditional evaluation mechanism is introduced into the stochastic search process, enabling the algorithm to dynamically identify and retain promising solutions. The subsequent subsections present the workflow, solution representation, parameter update strategies, and execution details of the proposed IWA.

Workflow of the algorithm

Based on pertinent initialization rules, the IWA first produces initial solutions with a hybrid structure. The algorithm chooses the search procedure for the current iteration if the iteration termination criteria are not satisfied. To find better answers, the global search phase uses the surrounding and search methods. During the local search phase, the bubble-net encircling mechanism refines the current optimal solution by exploring its surrounding search space. Two features are shared by all search techniques in the IWA: the improved greedy search (IGS) algorithm chooses continuous observation targets based on the revolution sequence, while whale-inspired actions define the orbital revolution sequence. The IWA achieves efficiency and stability by avoiding local optima in the early stages and by concentrating on refining high-quality solutions in the latter stages, dynamically regulating the selection of search algorithms across iterations.

This study proposes a wind-solar-storage hybrid power system for irrigation pump stations in alpine regions, comprising a 20 kW wind turbine, 0.8 kW of PV modules, a 3 kW·h lead-acid battery, and a 95% efficient converter. Conclusion

In this paper, an energy allocation problem for a wind-solar PV energy storage system to solve the uncertainty problem is proposed to support the economic operation of irrigation pumping stations in alpine areas, and the system’s optimisation method is studied to minimise total cost over the whole life cycle. The main conclusions are as follows:

Curve fitting is performed for the power–flow, head–flow, and efficiency–flow characteristics of three distinct pump types. Using the investment data of the pumping station, parameters for start–stop losses and operational depreciation costs are determined, and an economic operation model is developed for the station with multiple pump types operating in parallel, incorporating these cost factors.

Curve fitting is performed for the power–flow, head–flow, and efficiency–flow characteristics of three distinct pump types. Using the investment data of the pumping station, parameters for start–stop losses and operational depreciation costs are determined, and an economic operation model is developed for the station with multiple pump types operating in parallel, incorporating these cost factors.

An output model of the wind energy storage system is developed to establish a capacity allocation model that minimizes the cost of operation. Two scenarios-battery storage and battery–hydrogen storage—are analyzed to determine the optimal wind–solar storage configuration, along with pump station flow distribution and unit operation for a typical day. Results show that incorporating hydrogen storage enhances both economic and sustainability performance. In terms of cost reduction, IWA achieves a 3.3% decrease in annual cost, outperforming WOA (2.9%), IMO (1.02%), and SGEO (1.1%). Additionally, IWA converges faster, reaching stability around iteration 700 with a final fitness value of 0.1, compared to 0.2 (WOA), 0.4 (IMO), and 0.8 (SGEO).

Two scenarios-battery storage and battery–hydrogen storage—are analyzed to determine the optimal wind–solar storage configuration, along with pump station flow distribution and unit operation for a typical day. Results show that incorporating hydrogen storage enhances both economic and sustainability performance. These results confirm that the IWA algorithm achieves superior performance in optimization accuracy, convergence speed, and grid stability maintenance compared with traditional and recent swarm intelligence methods, effectively balancing local and global searches, and improving the search speed and convergence accuracy of the algorithm.

Also, future work will focus on applying and validating the proposed model and algorithm in large-scale industrial scenarios, incorporating more realistic operational constraints.

Critical points of the one dimensional Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional with an obstacle condition
Martin Rakovsky

Abstract




Abstract :
We consider a family of critical points of the
Ambrosio-Tortorelli energy with an obstacle condition on the phase
field variable. This problem can be interpreted as a time discretization
of a quasistatic evolution problem where the obstacle at step
n
is defined as the solution at step
n-1
. The obstacle condition
now reads as an irreversibility condition (the crack can only increase
in time). The questions tackled here are the regularity of the critical
points, the properties inherited from the obstacle sequence, the position
of the limit points and the equipartition of
the phase field energy. The limits of such critical points turn out to be critical points of the Mumford-Shah energy that inherit the possible discontinuities induced by the obstacle sequence.

1
Introduction

The Mumford-Shah functional, introduced by D.Mumford and J.Shah in
1989
in
[
MS89
]
in the context of image segmentation, is also used in fracture mechanics to describe brittle fractures
[
FM98
]
. If
\Omega
is a bounded open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, it is defined, for any
u\in SBV^{2}(\Omega)
, as

MS(u)=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}+2\mathcal{H}^{N-1}(J_{u}).

In the spirit of the Allen-Cahn model for phase transition (
[
Mod87
]
,
[
Ste88
]
), Ambrosio and Tortorelli proposed in
[
AT92
]
the following variational phase field regularization, defined for
(u,v)\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times[H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)]
by

AT_{\varepsilon}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v^{2})|\nabla u|^{2}dx+\int_{\Omega}\left(\varepsilon|\nabla v|^{2}+\frac{(1-v)^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)dx

where
0<\eta_{\varepsilon}<<\varepsilon
is a small parameter that ensures ellipticity. This regularization has also a mechanical interpretation. The phase field variable
v
can be seen as a damage variable taking values in the interval
[0,1]
, between the state
\{v=1\}
where the material is sane and the state
\{v=0\}
where the material is broken. This regularized formulation is also at the basis of numerical simulations (see
[
BFM08
]
).

In
[
AT92
]
, a
\Gamma-
convergence result of
AT_{\varepsilon}
to
MS
is proved by suitably extending
MS
as a two variable functional :

MS(u,v)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}MS(u)&\text{if }v\equiv 1\;,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\\
\end{array}\right.

Under suitable boundary conditions, the existence of minimizers for
AT_{\varepsilon}
for a fixed value of
\varepsilon
is obtained via the direct method of the Calculus of Variation. The
\Gamma
-convergence then ensures that a converging sequence
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
of minimizers of
AT_{\varepsilon}
converges toward a minimizer of
MS
.

Concerning the numerical implementation, the term
v^{2}|\nabla u|^{2}
implies a lack of convexity for the functional
AT_{\varepsilon}
. Consequently, numerical methods might fail to converge toward a minimizer of
AT_{\varepsilon}
. Using the fact that the functional remains separately strictly convex,
[
BFM08
]
proposes to perform an alternate minimization algorithm. It is proven in
[
Bou07
, Theorem 1]
that the sequence of iterates converge toward a critical point of
AT_{\varepsilon}
, but not always to a minimizer. This motivates to investigate the convergence of critical points of the Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and compare this convergence with the original goal to minimize the Mumford-Shah functional.

A critical point for the Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional is a pair
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\in H^{1}(\Omega)\times[H^{1}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)]
such that

\dfrac{d}{dt}\left|{}_{t=0}\right.AT_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}+t\psi,v_{\varepsilon}+t\varphi)=0\;\;\;\forall\,(\psi,\varphi)\in\>H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)^{2}.

with suitable conditions at the boundary
\partial\Omega
(for example Dirichlet conditions, as in
[
BMR24
]
).

The
\Gamma
-convergence provides little help to study the convergence of critical points. The extension of the fundamental theorem of
\Gamma-
convergence to the convergence of critical points has been studied in various settings, see
[
HT00
]
,
[
Ton02
]
,
[
Ton05
]
for the Allen-Cahn functional and
[
BBH94
]
,
[
BBO01
]
,
[
LR01
]
,
[
SS07
]
for the Ginzburg-Landau functional. For the Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional, the convergence has been established in all dimensions in
[
BMR24
]
, with the additional hypothesis of the convergence of the energy
AT_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
to
MS(u,1)
. In dimension one, this hypothesis is no longer required, and it is proved in
[
FLS09
]
and
[
BMR23
]
that a sequence of critical points for the one-dimensional Ambrosio-Tortorelli converges to critical points of
MS
. A similar result is proved in
[
Le10
]
for the one-dimensional Mumford-Shah functional adapted to image segmentation. (See also
[
BI23
]
in cohesive fracture).

In
[
FL03
]
, a model for the quasi-static evolution of a brittle fracture using the Mumford-Shah functional is proposed. The model starts with a discretized version of the evolution of the crack, where the irreversibility through time is translated into the condition that the crack
K(t_{i})
is included in the crack
K(t_{i+1})
, and the Mumford-Shah functional is then minimized at time
t_{i+1}
over all the cracks
K
containing
K(t_{i})
, and with a variable Dirichlet condition
g_{i}
at the boundary
\partial\Omega
. In
[
Gia05
]
, it is proved that a quasi-static evolution of
AT_{\varepsilon}
approximates a quasi-static evolution for
MS
using minimizers of
AT_{\varepsilon}
. The irreversibility condition translates into the minimization of
AT_{\varepsilon}
at time
t_{i+1}
among all couples
(u,v)
such that
v\leqslant v_{t_{i},\varepsilon}
, where
(u_{t_{i},\varepsilon},v_{t_{i},\varepsilon})
is the minimum of
AT_{\varepsilon}
at time
t_{i}
.

A natural question is whether the result of
[
Gia05
]
can be extended to critical points. Since in high dimension, results on the convergence of critical points of
AT_{\varepsilon}
require stronger hypothesis, such an extension can be investigated only in dimension one for the moment. This paper is the first step in that direction, namely, we study the convergence of the critical points of the functional
AT_{\varepsilon}
defined on the pairs
(u,v)
in
H^{1}([0,L])
satisfying
v\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}
, where
(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})
is a critical point of
AT_{\varepsilon}
. We use the same setting as
[
BMR23
]
(Dirichlet conditions on the variables
(u,v)
).

In Section 2, we define the one-dimensional functional
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
at study, which corresponds to the functional
AT_{\varepsilon}
restricted to the pairs
(u,v)
satisfying
v\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}
, we recall the results established by
[
BMR23
]
and state the main result in
Theorem 2.3
: the critical points of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
with the irreversibility condition
v\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}
converge to critical point of
MS
.
Theorem 2.4
, proved in the appendix, deals with the
\Gamma-
convergence of the sequence
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
.
Theorem 2.3
can then be seen as an extension of
Theorem 2.4
for critical points.

When we study the convergence of a functional with a phase field term, a natural question is the validity of an equipartition of the energy principle, i.e whether the terms of the phase field energy share the same limit. This principle, as well as the convergence of the Dirichlet approximation term is also stated in
Theorem 2.3
.

In Section 3, we establish the properties concerning the critical points of
\overline{AT_{\varepsilon}}
, namely the regularity and the variations of
v_{\varepsilon}
. In Section 4, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
and prove
Theorem 2.3
.

Similarly to
[
FLS09
]
,
[
BMR23
]
and
[
Le10
]
, the key point to the main estimates is the study of the quantity called
discrepancy
(
Equation 4.5
) associated to
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
(which can be interpreted as the Hamiltonian of the functional). In
[
FLS09
]
,
[
BMR23
]
, this discrepancy is constant, which is no longer the case in our situation. However, we are able to control the variations of the discrepancy in
subsection 4.2
to obtain analogous estimates and results as in
[
BMR23
]
.

2
Notations and statement of the results

2.1
Notations and preliminary results

Throughout the paper,
L
is a positive number. Letters like
C,m
and
M
will denote constants that do not depend on any other parameter and that may change from line to line. We also denote by
I_{r}(x)
the interval
(x-r,x+r)
.

For any open subset
\Omega\subset(0,L)
, we denote by

||f||_{\infty,\Omega}:=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|\;,

[\,f\,]_{1/2,\Omega}:=\sup_{x\neq y\in\Omega}\dfrac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{1/2}}\;,

||f||_{C^{0,1/2},\Omega}:=||f||_{\infty,\Omega}+[\,f\,]_{1/2,\Omega}.

The one-dimensional Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional, is defined as

AT_{\varepsilon}(u,v):=\int_{0}^{L}\left((\eta_{\varepsilon}+v^{2})|u^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-v)^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v^{\prime}|^{2}\right)dx,\;\;\;\;\;\forall(u,v)\in H^{1}(0,L)^{2},

where
\eta_{\varepsilon}
is a positive parameter such that
\eta_{\varepsilon}/\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
. Before defining the one-dimensional Mumford-Shah functional, we recall the definition of the space
SBV^{2}(0,L)
, which is the space of the functions
u\in L^{1}
such that their distributional derivative is a Radon measure which writes as

Du=u^{\prime}d\mathcal{L}+(u^{+}-u^{-})\nu_{u}\mathcal{H}^{0}_{|J_{u}}\;\;\text{with}\;\;\int_{0}^{L}|u^{\prime}|^{2}<\infty,\;\mathcal{H}^{0}(J_{u})<\infty.

The one-dimensional Mumford-Shah functional
MS
defined on
SBV^{2}(0,L)\times L^{2}(0,L)
reads as

MS(u,v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}|u^{\prime}|^{2}+2\mathcal{H}^{0}(J_{u})&\text{if }v\equiv 1\;,\\
+\infty&\text{otherwise}.\\
\end{array}\right..

We define a critical point of
AT_{\varepsilon}
as follows

{definition}

Let
a_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.
A critical point of
AT_{\varepsilon}
is a pair
(u,v)\in H^{1}(0,L)^{2}
that satisfies for all
(\psi,\varphi)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(0,L)
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}dAT_{\varepsilon}(u,v)[(\psi,\varphi)]:=\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0}\frac{AT_{\varepsilon}(u+t\psi,v+t\varphi)-AT_{\varepsilon}(u,v)}{t}=0\;,\\
\\
u_{0,\varepsilon}(0)=0,\;u_{0,\varepsilon}(L)=a_{0}\;,\\
\\
v_{0,\varepsilon}(0)=v_{0,\varepsilon}(L)=1.\\
\end{array}\right.

A critical point
(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})
of
AT_{\varepsilon}
satisfies the following equations on
(0,L)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\varepsilon v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{v_{0,\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{0,\varepsilon}|u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}=0&\text{in }(0,L)\;,\\
&\\
\left[u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2})\right]^{\prime}=0&\text{in }(0,L)\;,\\
&\\
u_{0,\varepsilon}(0)=0,\;u_{0,\varepsilon}(L)=a_{0}\;,&\\
&\\
v_{0,\varepsilon}(0)=v_{0,\varepsilon}(L)=1.&\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.1)

In the sequel, we consider a sequence
(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})
of critical points of
AT_{\varepsilon}
satisfying the following energy bound

\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon>0}AT_{\varepsilon}(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})<C.

(2.2)

In
[
BMR23
, Theorem 1.1]
, the following has been established :

2.1
.

Up to a subsequence,
(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})\rightarrow(u_{0},1)
in
L^{2}(0,L)^{2}
, where either
u_{0}=u_{jump}\equiv a_{0}1_{[L/2,L]}
or
u_{0}(x)=u_{aff}(x)=a_{0}x/L
.

When integrating the second equation of (
2.1
), we obtain a constant
c_{0,\varepsilon}
such that

u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2})=c_{0,\varepsilon}.

[
BMR23
, Lemma 2.4]
states that

{lemma}

Up to a subsequence, the sequence

c_{0,\varepsilon}\rightarrow c_{0};

(2.3)

with
c_{0}
a real number satisfying
c_{0}\in\{0,a_{0}/L\}
.

Theorem 2.1
enlightens a selection phenomenon : the accumulation points of the sequence
(u_{0,\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon})
are specific critical points of
MS
. Indeed, critical points of
MS
are either the affine function
u_{aff}
or the piecewise constant functions. It is also proved in
[
BMR23
, Theorem 1.2]
that
u_{jump}
can be reached by a sequence of critical points of
AT_{\varepsilon}
. The crack of the material at time
0
then corresponds to
J_{u_{0}}
, which is, according to
[
BMR23
, Theorem 1.1]

\Gamma_{0}=J_{u_{0}}=\emptyset\;\;\;\;\;\text{ or }\;\;\;\;\;\Gamma_{0}=J_{u_{0}}=\left\{\frac{L}{2}\right\}.

(2.4)

The Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional with obstacle is defined as

\overline{AT_{\varepsilon}}(u,v):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}AT_{\varepsilon}(u,v)&\text{ if }v\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}\;,\\
+\infty&\text{ else,}\end{array}\right.\;\;\;\forall\>(u,v)\in H^{1}(0,L).

(2.5)

In order to account for the constraint on the phase field variable, we introduce the following definition for constrained critical points.

{definition}

A critical point of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
is a pair
(u,v)\in H^{1}(0,L)^{2}
that satisfies
v\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}
and, for all
(\psi,\varphi)\in\mathcal{C}_{c}^{\infty}(0,L)
with
\varphi\geqslant 0
,

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}d\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)[(\psi,\varphi)]:=\displaystyle\lim\limits_{t\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)-\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}(u-t\psi,v-t\varphi)}{t}=0\;,\\
\\
u(0)=0,\;u(L)=a_{1}\;,\\
\\
v(0)=v(L)=1.\\
\end{array}\right.

Note that the Dirichlet condition at
L
is different and carry the evolution in time of the material.

In the sequel, we denote

O_{\varepsilon}=\{v_{\varepsilon}<v_{0,\varepsilon}\}\;\;\;\;\text{and}\;\;\;\;F_{\varepsilon}=\{v_{\varepsilon}=v_{0,\varepsilon}\}.

(2.6)

Since
v_{\varepsilon},v_{0,\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(0,L)
, there difference is continuous by Sobolev embedding so
O_{\varepsilon}
and
F_{\varepsilon}
are respectively open and closed in
[0,L]
.

2.2
Euler-Lagrange equations

In this subsection, we establish the Euler-Lagrange equations for
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
.
Let
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
be a critical point of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
.
Take
\varphi=0
in
subsection 2.1
. Similarly to
[
FLS09
, Introduction]
, the equation satisfied by
u_{\varepsilon}
reads as

\left[u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})\right]^{\prime}=0.

(2.7)

This means that there exists a constant
c_{\varepsilon}
such that

u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})}\;\;\;\;\;\text{a.e in }(0,L).

(2.8)

As
v_{\varepsilon}
is continuous by Sobolev embedding, the left hand side is also continuous. Similarly to
[
FLS09
]
, we obtain that
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
has a constant sign, corresponding to the sign of
c_{\varepsilon}
, meaning that
u_{\varepsilon}
is monotone on
[0,L]
.

We next move on to the equation for
v_{\varepsilon}
. Taking
\psi=0
in
subsection 2.1
,
we obtain the following inequality, where
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
denotes the distributional derivative of
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
, which exists as
v_{\varepsilon}\in H^{1}(0,L)
,

\left\langle\,\varphi\,,\,v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}-\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}\,\right\rangle\leqslant 0

(2.9)

which we rewrite in the following way : there exists a non positive Radon measure distribution
\mu_{\varepsilon}
such that

-\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}=\mu_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 0.

(2.10)

Taking
\varphi
compactly supported on
\{v_{\varepsilon}<v_{0,\varepsilon}\}
gives the reversed inequality of (
2.9
). This means that the measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is supported on the closed set
F_{\varepsilon}
.

Using (
2.10
), we see that
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}
is also a Radon measure, so that
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\in BV(0,L)
, which is embedded in
L^{\infty}(0,L)
, and that
v_{\varepsilon}\in C^{0,1}(0,L)
. In section
3.1
, we provide a better regularity result on
v_{\varepsilon}
. This allows us to establish an explicit formula for
\mu_{\varepsilon}
at the end of section
3.1
(
subsection 3.1
).

In summary, the equations satisfied by
(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})
are

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}-\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}=\mu_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 0&\text{in }(0,L)\\
&\\
\left[u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})\right]^{\prime}=0&\text{in }(0,L)\\
&\\
v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant v_{0,\varepsilon}&\text{in }(0,L)\;,\\
&\\
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=0,\;u_{\varepsilon}(L)=a_{0}\;,&\\
&\\
v_{\varepsilon}(0)=v_{\varepsilon}(L)=1.&\\
\end{array}\right.

(2.11)

In this subsection, we investigate the regularity of

{proposition}

Set

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\varepsilon w_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}&=&\displaystyle\varepsilon(v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}-v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime})=\frac{v_{0,\varepsilon}-1-(v_{\varepsilon}-1)}{\varepsilon}+v_{0,\varepsilon}|u_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}-v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}+\mu_{\varepsilon}\\

(3.1)

{lemma}

Since

where this inequality is written in the distributional sense.

The aim of this section is to take advantage of the ODE (

and non-decreasing on

{lemma}

Let

be a critical point of

Proof.

Note that statement is non empty, by Rolle’s Theorem, as

Suppose

0=v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x_{\varepsilon})=\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}+h)-v_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{h}=\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}+h)-v_{0,\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{h}\leqslant\lim\limits_{h\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{v_{0,\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}+h)-v_{0,\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon})}{h}=v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)

{proposition}

(3.8)

4.1
Preliminary estimates

The following estimates are classical and can be found in
[

]

[

First, notice that the energy bound (

) provides the following inequality on

:

\mathcal{C}\geqslant\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\geqslant\int_{0}^{L}(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}=\int_{0}^{L}|c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|=|c_{\varepsilon}||u_{\varepsilon}(L)-u_{\varepsilon}(0)|=|c_{\varepsilon}a_{1}|.

where we used that

is of constant sign (see (

. The sequence
(c_{\varepsilon})
is bounded, so, up to an extraction, we can suppose that it converges to a constant

. The following proposition establishes the connection between

and the limit function of

.

{proposition}

One has
v_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 1
in
L^{2}(0,L)
and, up to an extraction, there exists
u_{1}\in BV(0,L)
such that
u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u_{1}
in
L^{1}(0,L)
.
Moreover,
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\rightarrow c_{1}
a.e in
[0,L]
with
|Du_{1}|(0,L)\leqslant|a_{1}|
. We also have that
c_{1}\in[0,a_{1}/L]
if
a_{1}\geqslant 0
and
c_{1}\in[a_{1}/L,0]
if
a_{1}\leqslant 0
.

Proof.

The convergence of
v_{\varepsilon}
comes from the energy bound (
2.12
) :

\int_{0}^{L}(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\leqslant\varepsilon\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon},v_{\varepsilon})\leqslant\mathcal{C}\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

As
u_{\varepsilon}
is monotone,
u_{\varepsilon}(0)=0
and
u_{\varepsilon}(L)=a_{1}
, it is of constant sign and
\int_{0}^{L}|u_{\varepsilon}|=|\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant|a_{1}|L
. So
(u_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
L^{1}(0,L)
. Secondly, as
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
is of constant sign,

|Du_{\varepsilon}|(0,L)=\int_{0}^{L}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|=|\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|=|a_{1}|.

So the total variation of
u_{\varepsilon}
is also bounded. Then, the sequence
(u_{\varepsilon})
is bounded in
BV(0,L)
and we can extract a converging subsequence in
L^{1}(0,L)
to a function
u_{1}\in BV(0,L)
. The lower semi-continuity of the total variation implies

|Du_{1}|(0,L)\leqslant\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}|Du_{\varepsilon}|(0,L)\leqslant|a_{1}|.

Using equation (
2.8
), as one can extract a converging subsequence of
(v_{\varepsilon})
that converges to
1
almost everywhere, one gets
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\rightarrow c_{1}
almost everywhere. If
u_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0
, then by Fatou’s lemma :

c_{1}L=\int_{0}^{L}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\leqslant\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=a_{1}.

If
u_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 0
, then similarly

-c_{1}L=\int_{0}^{L}\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}(-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})\leqslant\liminf\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}(-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})=-a_{1}.

We obtain that if
a_{1}\geqslant 0
, then
c_{1}\in[0,a_{1}/L]
and if
a_{1}\leqslant 0
, then
c_{1}\in[a_{1}/L,0]
.
∎

A>0
. We integrate the inequality

-\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{v_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}}+\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}\leqslant 0

over the set
\{v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\}
. We obtain

\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\geqslant\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}}+\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}.

(4.7)

According to
subsection 3.2
, the set
K_{A,\varepsilon}
is an interval that we denote by
[a_{\varepsilon},b_{\varepsilon}]
.
Then we have, using
subsection 4.2
,

|\int_{a_{\varepsilon}}^{b_{\varepsilon}}\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}|=\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(b_{\varepsilon})-v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(a_{\varepsilon})|\leqslant C.

Next, recall by
subsection 4.2
that
v_{\varepsilon}^{2}\geqslant m\varepsilon>>\eta_{\varepsilon}
. We can then bound from below the right-hand side of (
4.7
) by

\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}}+\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}\geqslant\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}c_{1}^{2}/2}{2v_{\varepsilon}^{4}}-\frac{|K_{\varepsilon,A}|}{\varepsilon}\geqslant C\frac{|K_{\varepsilon,A}|}{A^{3}\varepsilon^{3/2}}-\frac{|K_{\varepsilon,A}|}{\varepsilon}\geqslant C\frac{|K_{\varepsilon,A}|}{A^{3}\varepsilon^{3/2}}.

Combining the two bounds, we obtain
|K_{\varepsilon,A}|\leqslant CA^{3}\varepsilon^{3/2}
. This ends Step 1.

Step 2 :
In this step, we investigate the weight of
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
in each of the three regimes
\{v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\},\{A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}
and
\{v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1/2\}
. Indeed, one has :

a_{1}=\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=\underbrace{\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{:=I_{1}}+\underbrace{\int_{\{A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{:=I_{2}}+\underbrace{\int_{\{v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1/2\}}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{:=I_{3}}.

First, thanks to the previous estimate on
K_{\varepsilon,A}
and
subsection 4.2
,

I_{1}\leqslant\int_{K_{\varepsilon,A}}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leqslant\frac{C}{\varepsilon}|K_{\varepsilon,A}|\leqslant CA^{3}\varepsilon^{1/2}.

As
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\rightarrow c_{1}
and
v_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 1
a.e, we have
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}1_{v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1/2}\rightarrow c_{1}
a.e. As

\left|\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2}}1_{\{v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1/2\}}\right|\leqslant 2|c_{1}|\times 4,

we can apply the dominated convergence theorem to obtain

I_{3}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{L}c_{1}=c_{1}L.

For
I_{2}
, the energy bound (
2.2
) gives :

C\geqslant\int_{0}^{L}\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\geqslant\int_{\{v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}}\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\geqslant\frac{1}{4\varepsilon}|\{v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}|

On
\{A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}
, we recover

|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|=\frac{|c_{\varepsilon}|}{\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\leqslant\frac{|c_{\varepsilon}|}{v_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\leqslant\frac{2|c_{1}|}{A^{2}\varepsilon}.

Thus

|I_{2}|\leqslant|\{A\sqrt{\varepsilon}\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1/2\}|\times\frac{2|c_{1}|}{A^{2}\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{C}{A^{2}}

Combining the estimates on
I_{1},I_{2}
and
I_{3}
, we obtain

|a_{1}|\leqslant\frac{C}{A^{2}}+|c_{1}|L

By letting
A
tending to
\infty
, we obtain
|c_{1}|L\geqslant|a_{1}|
. Combining with
|c_{1}|\leqslant|a_{1}|/L
, we get the desired result.
∎

We can now conclude the proof of
(i)
in
Theorem 2.3
.

Proof of
(i)
in
Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\delta>0
.
We have already seen with
subsection 4.2
that
v_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on
[0,L]\setminus I_{\delta}(x_{min})
for every
\delta>0
. This combined with (
2.8
) implies that
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\rightarrow c_{1}
uniformly on
[0,L]\setminus I_{\delta}(x_{min})
for every
\delta>0
. Then we get that :

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)dt\rightarrow c_{1}x,

uniformly with respect to
x\in[0,x_{min}-\delta]
. Similarly,
u_{\varepsilon}(x)\rightarrow a_{1}-c_{1}(L-x)
uniformly with respect to
x\in[x_{min}+\delta,L]
. Using that
c_{1}=0
or
c_{1}=a_{1}/L
, we see that the limit function
u_{1}
of
(u_{\varepsilon})
(in the point-wise convergence sense) is either
u_{1}=u_{aff}
or
u_{1}=u_{jump}
. Moreover, as
u_{\varepsilon}
is monotone and
u_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded by
a_{1}
on
[0,L]
, by dominated convergence, we get that

in

. The additionnal conclusion corresponds to
subsection 4.3
.
∎

4.4
Convergence of the Dirichlet energy

In this subsection, we prove the following, which proves part
(iii)
of
Theorem 2.3
. The proof is similar to
[
FLS09
, section 6]
and
[
BMR23
, section 3]
.

{lemma}

(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}dx\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}|u_{1}^{\prime}|^{2}dx
weak
⋆
in
\mathcal{M}([0,L])
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a test-function in
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}([0,L])
. According to
subsection 4.1
:

\int_{0}^{L}(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi=c_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\varphi=-c_{\varepsilon}\int_{0}^{L}u_{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\prime}+c_{\varepsilon}a_{1}\varphi(L)\rightarrow-c_{1}\int_{0}^{L}u_{1}\varphi^{\prime}+c_{1}a_{1}\varphi(L).

If

, then

so

∎

4.5
Convergence of the phase-field term

In this subsection, we prove the first part of
(ii)
in
Theorem 2.3
, namely

{lemma}

\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)dx\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\alpha\delta_{x_{min}}
weak
⋆
in
\mathcal{M}([0,L])
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
.

The computation of the weight
\alpha
is made in section
subsection 4.7
.

We start by noticing that the energy bound (
2.2
) implies that the measure
\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)dx
is bounded, thus we can extract a converging subsequence such that

\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)dx\overset{\star}{\rightharpoonup}\nu\;\;\;\;\text{in }\mathcal{M}([0,L])

for some non negative
\nu\in\mathcal{M}([0,L])
.
In
[
BMR23
]
and in
[
FLS09
]
, the proof that
\nu
is atomic relies on the following estimate (see
[
BMR23
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
FLS09
, Lemma 6.1]
): if
K
is a compact subset of
[0,L]\setminus\{x_{min}\}
, then

\int_{K}\left(\frac{(1-v_{0,\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)dx\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1/4}.

The proof of this estimate relies on an integration by part of the PDE satisfied by
v_{0,\varepsilon}
on
K
, and strongly uses the estimate
\|v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leqslant C/\varepsilon
. In our case, such an estimate
\|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\leqslant C/\varepsilon
is proved only in the case where
c_{0}>0
.

We are going to show the following result, which provides a sharper estimate (an order
1
on
\varepsilon
instead of an order
1/4
) :

{proposition}

Let
K
be a compact subset of
[0,L]\setminus\{x_{min}\}
. There exists a constant
C_{K}
such that :

\int_{K}\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon.

The proof is divided in three steps. In the first one, we prove the result for a compact set
K
of
(0,L)\setminus\{x_{min}\}
:

{lemma}

Let
K
be a compact subset of
(0,L)\,\setminus\{x_{min}\}
.
For any positive test-function
\varphi
supported in
K
, there exists a constant
C_{K,\varphi}
such that :

\int_{K}\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)\varphi\leqslant C_{K,\varphi}\varepsilon.

In the second one, we use
subsection 4.5
to obtain a sharper estimate on
\|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\|_{\infty}
on
(0,L)\setminus\{x_{min}\}
.

{lemma}

Let
K
be a connected compact subset of
(0,L)\setminus\{x_{min}\}
. Then there exists a constant
C_{K}
such that,

\underset{K}{\sup}\;\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1/3}.

Finally, we use
subsection 4.5
to prove
subsection 4.5
, in a similar way than
[
BMR23
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
FLS09
, Lemma 6.1]
.

Proof of
subsection 4.5
.

The argument is inspired from
[
HT00
, Proposition 4.1]
to prove a monotonicity formula for the Allen-Cahn functional.

Take
\varphi
a smooth and positive test function supported on
K
. First, using
subsection 4.2
, there exists
b,M>0
such that

u^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\leqslant M,\;\;\;\;|\mu_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant M,\;\;\;\;v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1-b\varepsilon\;\;\;\;\text{on }K.

Then

\int_{K}\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\varphi\leqslant\int_{K}\frac{b^{2}\varepsilon^{2}}{\varepsilon}\varphi\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon.

(4.8)

It remains to estimate
\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi
. To do so, we compare
\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}
with its second derivative (in the distributional sense). One has

[\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{\prime}=2\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}=2v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}-\mu_{\varepsilon}\right).

Note that
v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon}\in L^{1}(0,L)
, so it admits a derivative in the distributional sense and then

[\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}]^{\prime\prime}=\underbrace{2v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)}_{=A_{\varepsilon}}\underbrace{-2(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}}_{=B_{\varepsilon}}+2\frac{v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime 2}}{\varepsilon}+\underbrace{2|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}}_{=C_{\varepsilon}}+\underbrace{4v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{=D_{\varepsilon}}.

It follows that

2\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi=\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}(\varepsilon(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi-\varepsilon^{2}\left(\int_{K}A_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\left\langle\,B_{\varepsilon}\,,\,\varphi\,\right\rangle+\int_{K}C_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\int_{0}^{L}D_{\varepsilon}\varphi\right).

(4.9)

We now study each terms of the right hand side.

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}(\varepsilon(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi
:
As
\varphi=0
outside of
K
, then
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
vanish at
0
and
L
. Applying (
4.8
) to
\varphi^{\prime\prime}
yields

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}(\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi^{\prime\prime}=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}\left(\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}+\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}\right)\varphi^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{L}\varphi^{\prime\prime}d\nu.

Hence,

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}(\varepsilon(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi=\displaystyle\varepsilon^{2}\int_{0}^{L}(\varepsilon(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi\leqslant C_{\varphi}\varepsilon^{2}.

(4.10)

\displaystyle\int_{K}A_{\varepsilon}\varphi
:

A_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\underbrace{\left(\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{2}}_{\geqslant 0}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\underbrace{(-\mu_{\varepsilon})}_{\leqslant M}\left(\underbrace{\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}}_{\geqslant-b}+\underbrace{v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}}_{\geqslant 0}\right)\geqslant\frac{-bM}{\varepsilon}.

so that

-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}A_{\varepsilon}\varphi\leqslant C_{K,\varphi}\varepsilon.

(4.11)

\displaystyle\langle\,B_{\varepsilon}\,,\,\varphi\,\rangle
:
We use Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and the fact that
|\mu_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant M
to get that

\left|2\varepsilon^{2}\langle\,(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\,,\,\varphi\,\rangle\right|=2\varepsilon^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{L}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon}\varphi^{\prime}\right|\leqslant 2\varepsilon^{3/2}\underbrace{\|\mu_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(0,L)}}_{\leqslant C}\left|\left|\varepsilon^{1/2}|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\varphi^{\prime}\right|\right|_{L^{2}(0,L)}.

(4.12)

On the other hand,

\left|\left|\varepsilon^{1/2}|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\varphi^{\prime}\right|\right|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,L)}=\int_{0}^{L}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\leqslant\int_{0}^{L}\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}\rightarrow\int_{0}^{L}|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}d\nu,

From which we deduce that
\|\varepsilon^{1/2}(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})\varphi^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(0,L)}\leqslant C_{\varphi}
. (
4.12
) then yields

\left|\varepsilon^{2}\left\langle\,B_{\varepsilon}\,,\,\varphi\,\right\rangle\right|\leqslant C_{\varphi}\varepsilon^{3/2}.

(4.13)

\displaystyle\int_{K}C_{\varepsilon}\varphi
:

C_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 0
, so

-\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}C_{\varepsilon}\varphi\leqslant 0.

(4.14)

\displaystyle\int_{K}D_{\varepsilon}\varphi
:
We have
\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}=\left(\dfrac{c_{\varepsilon}}{v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}}\right)^{\prime}=-2c_{\varepsilon}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}}{(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})^{2}}
, so that

D_{\varepsilon}=-8v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}c_{\varepsilon}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}v_{\varepsilon}}{(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})^{2}}\cdot\frac{c_{\varepsilon}}{v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}}=-8c_{\varepsilon}^{2}|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\underbrace{\frac{v_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{(v_{\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})^{3}}}_{\leqslant C}.

where we used that
1-b\varepsilon\leqslant v_{\varepsilon}\leqslant 1
on
K
. As
(c_{\varepsilon})
is bounded, we can write :

|D_{\varepsilon}|\leqslant M|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}.

Thus

|\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}D_{\varepsilon}\varphi|\leqslant M\varepsilon\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi.

(4.15)

Injecting (
4.14
) and (
4.15
) in (
4.9
) yields the following inequality, which is true for every
\varepsilon
small enough and every positive smooth test function
\varphi
:

(1-M\varepsilon)\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}(\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2})^{\prime\prime}\varphi-\varepsilon^{2}\left(\int_{K}A_{\varepsilon}\varphi+\langle B_{\varepsilon},\varphi\rangle\right)

(4.16)

Now combining with (
4.10
),(
4.11
) and (
4.13
), we obtain

(1-M\varepsilon)\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\varphi\leqslant C\varepsilon^{2}+C\varepsilon+C\varepsilon^{3/2}\leqslant C_{K,\varphi}\varepsilon

(4.17)

Which concludes the proof.
∎

Note that a corollary of this property is that

\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon\;\;\;\;\;\text{ for any compact set }K\subset(0,L)\setminus\{x_{min}\}.

(4.18)

We now move on to the proof of
subsection 4.5
.

Proof of
subsection 4.5
.

Let
K
be a connected compact subset of
(0,L)\setminus\{x_{min}\}
and let
y\in K
.
The strategy is the following : we consider a mollifier
\rho_{\varepsilon}
(i.e
\rho
is positive smooth function, compactly supported on
[-1,1]
with integral
1
and
\rho_{\varepsilon}=\dfrac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\rho\left(\frac{\cdot}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\right)
, whith
\alpha>0
to be determined). Then we take
\rho_{\varepsilon}(\cdot-y)
as a test function in the inequality (
4.16
) and we prove the two estimates

\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}

(4.19)

and

\left|\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}dx-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)|^{2}\right|\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}.

(4.20)

It follows that for
\alpha=2/3
, one has
\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)|^{2}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1/3}
.

We start by proving (
4.19
). One already has from inequalities (
4.16
), (
4.11
) and (
4.12
) that

(1-M\varepsilon)\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(x)dx\leqslant\underbrace{\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}(\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2})^{\prime\prime}\rho_{\varepsilon}}_{:=I_{1}}+\underbrace{\varepsilon\int_{K}bM\rho_{\varepsilon}}_{:=I_{2}}-\underbrace{\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon})^{\prime}\rho_{\varepsilon}}_{:=I_{3}}.

I_{1}
:
Using
subsection 4.5
,

\left|\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\right|=\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\left|\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\rho^{\prime\prime}\left(\frac{x}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\right)\right|\leqslant\varepsilon^{-3\alpha}\|\rho^{\prime\prime}\|_{\infty}\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1-3\alpha}.

(4.21)

Hence,

I_{1}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{3-3\alpha}

(4.22)

I_{2}
:

|I_{2}|=\left|\varepsilon bM\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}\right|\leqslant bM|K|\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}\|\rho\|_{\infty}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}.

(4.23)

I_{3}
:
Using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, (
4.3
) and
subsection 4.5
,

|I_{3}|\leqslant\left|\varepsilon^{2}\int_{K}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\mu_{\varepsilon}\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|\leqslant\varepsilon^{2}\underbrace{\left(\int_{K}\mu_{\varepsilon}^{2}\right)^{1/2}}_{\leqslant C_{K}}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\left(\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}|\rho_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{2}\varepsilon^{-2\alpha-1/2}\|\rho^{\prime}\|_{\infty}\underbrace{\left(\int_{K}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)^{1/2}}_{\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1/2}}.

(4.24)

And thus,

|I_{3}|\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{2-2\alpha}.

(4.25)

Combining (
4.22
), (
4.23
) and (
4.25
) yields (
4.19
).

We now move on to the proof of (
4.20
). First, using that
\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)=1
, we rewrite (
4.20
) as

\displaystyle\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(x)dx-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(y))dx.

Then, for all
x
in
K
, considering the fact that
|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}
is
C^{0,1}([0,L])
and using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

|\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)|^{2}|=2\varepsilon\left|\int_{y}^{x}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)dt\right|\leqslant 2\varepsilon\varepsilon^{-1}\left|\int_{y}^{x}\varepsilon^{2}|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\right|^{1/2}\varepsilon^{-1/2}\underbrace{\left|\int_{y}^{x}\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt\right|^{1/2}}_{\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{1/2}}.

By
subsection 4.2
,

\|\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}=\|\frac{v_{\varepsilon}-1}{\varepsilon}-\mu_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}|u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leqslant b+M+M^{2}\leqslant C

so that

\left|\int_{y}^{x}\varepsilon^{2}v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(t)^{2}\right|^{1/2}\leqslant C|x-y|^{1/2}.

We deduce

\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)|^{2}\right|dx\leqslant C_{K}\int_{K}|x-y|^{1/2}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\rho_{\varepsilon}\left(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\right)dx.

Changing variable
s=\dfrac{x-y}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}
, we rewrite the integral as

\int_{(K-y)/\varepsilon^{\alpha}}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}|s|^{1/2}\rho(s)ds\leqslant C\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}.

Finally,

\int_{K}\rho_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\left|\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)|^{2}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)|^{2}\right|dx\leqslant C_{K}\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}.

Combining (
4.19
) and (
4.20
) finishes the proof.
∎

We are now in position to prove
subsection 4.5
.

Proof of
subsection 4.5
.

It is sufficient to prove the inequality for
K=[\,0,x_{min}-\delta]\cup[x_{min}+\delta,L]
for any
\delta>0
. In the sequel, we fix
\delta>0
.
We adapt the argument used by
[
BMR23
, Lemma 3.1]
and
[
FLS09
, Lemma 6.3]
. Multiplying both sides of the equation (
2.10
) by
v_{\varepsilon}-1
and integrating by parts over
[0,x_{min}-\delta]
yields :

\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}\left(\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right)=\varepsilon v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x_{min}-\delta)(v_{\varepsilon}(x_{min}-\delta)-1)+\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}\mu_{\varepsilon}(v_{\varepsilon}-1)+\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}\frac{v_{\varepsilon}(1-v_{\varepsilon})}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}}.

We now bound each term on the right-hand side. Let
K^{\prime}=K\cap[L/8,7L/8]
which is a compact subset of
[0,L]\setminus\{x_{min}\}
. According to
subsection 4.5
,
|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|\leqslant C_{K^{\prime}}\varepsilon^{-1/3}
on
K^{\prime}
. Using
subsection 4.2
, there exists a constant
b>0
such that
v_{\varepsilon}\geqslant 1-b\varepsilon
on
K
. This implies that
\mu_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded on
K
(see the inequality (
4.3
)). Consequently :

\varepsilon\underbrace{|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x_{min}-\delta)|}_{\leqslant C_{K^{\prime}}\varepsilon^{-1/3}}\underbrace{|v_{\varepsilon}(x_{min}-\delta)-1|}_{\leqslant b\varepsilon}\leqslant C_{\delta}\varepsilon^{5/3}.

We also have

\left|\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}\underbrace{\mu_{\varepsilon}}_{\leqslant M}\underbrace{(v_{\varepsilon}-1)}_{\geqslant-b\varepsilon}\right|\leqslant C_{\delta}\varepsilon,

as well as

\left|\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}c_{\varepsilon}^{2}(1-v_{\varepsilon})\frac{v_{\varepsilon}}{(\eta_{\varepsilon}+v_{\varepsilon}^{2})^{2}}\right|\leqslant c_{\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{x_{min}-\delta}\underbrace{|1-v_{\varepsilon}|}_{\leqslant b\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\underbrace{v_{\varepsilon}^{3}}_{\leqslant C}}\leqslant C_{\delta}\varepsilon.

We deduce that

We proceed similarly on

to conclude.
∎

4.6
Equipartition of the energy

In this subsection, our goal is to prove the following result, which corresponds to the item
(iv)
of

, and which implies

.

{proposition}

We have

\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{0}^{L}\left|\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon(v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime})^{2}\right|=0.

(4.26)

We recover here a classical result in the case of the minimizers of
AT_{\varepsilon}
, as it corresponds to the equality case of the arithmetico-geometric inequality used in the proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence of
AT_{\varepsilon}
to

. It is used in
[

]
and
[

]
to compute the value of the weight

associated to the limit Dirac-measure of the phase field term of

. An equipartition of the energy result also exists for the Ambrosio-Tortorelli functional with an additional fidelity term, see
[
Le10
]
.

In
[
FLS09
]
and
[
Le10
]
, the Neumann conditions on (

) imply that the difference
\dfrac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}
is positive. In
[
BMR23
]
and
[
FLS09
]
, the proof of the equipartition of the energy hinges on the fact that
d_{\varepsilon}
is constant and tends to
-c_{0}^{2}
.

In our case, recalling the equality :

\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}=d_{\varepsilon}+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime},

the first ingredient is to prove that
d_{\varepsilon}+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\rightarrow 0
at the boundary point
0
and
L
of
(0,L)
. It rests on
subsection 4.5
. The second ingredient is to bound
d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
to control the variation of
v_{\varepsilon}
on the full interval. To deal with the signs of each terms and get rid of the absolute values, we need to separate our integrals along
L/2
and
x_{\varepsilon}
.

Proof of
subsection 4.6
.

subsection 4.5
implies in particular that
\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(0),\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(L)\rightarrow 0
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, by recalling that
x_{min}\in[L/4,3L/4]
(
subsection 3.2
). Then we have

d_{\varepsilon}(0)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}(0)\rightarrow 0

(4.27)

and similarly,
d_{\varepsilon}(L)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L)\rightarrow 0
.

By
subsection 4.2
,
d_{\varepsilon}\in C^{0,1}([0,L])
, so we can write
d_{\varepsilon}(x)=d_{\varepsilon}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
on
[0,L/2]
and
d_{\varepsilon}(x)=d_{\varepsilon}(L)+\int_{L}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}
on
[L/2,L]
, with
d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\geqslant 0
on
[0,L/2]
and
d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\leqslant 0
on
[L/2,L]
. However, the sign of
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)
depends on the position of
x
with respect to
x_{\varepsilon}
. So we split the integral in the left-hand side of (
4.26
) along
x_{\varepsilon}
and later along
L/2

\begin{array}[]{llll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}\left|\frac{(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}}{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|^{2}\right|&=&&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left|d_{\varepsilon}(0)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)+\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)dt+c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0))\right|dx\\
&&\\
&&+&\displaystyle\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\left|d_{\varepsilon}(L)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L)+\int_{L}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(t)dt+c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L)\right|dx\\
&&\\
&\leqslant&&\displaystyle\underbrace{x_{\varepsilon}|d_{\varepsilon}(0)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)|}_{:=I}+\underbrace{\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|}_{:=II}+\underbrace{\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}|c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0))|dx}_{:=III}\\
&&\\
&&\displaystyle+&\displaystyle\underbrace{(L-x_{\varepsilon})|d_{\varepsilon}(L)+c_{\varepsilon}u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L)|}_{:=IV}+\displaystyle\underbrace{\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\left|\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|}_{:=V}+\underbrace{\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}|c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L))|dx}_{:=VI}\\
\end{array}

We deal separately with
I+IV
,
II+V
and
III+VI
.

I+IV
:
This tends to
0
according to (
4.27
).

II+V
:
We prove that

II+V\leqslant\int_{0}^{L/2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx-\int_{L/2}^{L}\left(\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx+\left|\frac{L}{2}-x_{\varepsilon}\right|\left|\int_{0}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|

Suppose that
x_{\varepsilon}\geqslant L/2
(the case
x_{\varepsilon}\leqslant L/2
can be treated in a similar way). Recall that
d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\geqslant 0
on
[0,L/2]
and
d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\leqslant 0
on
[L/2,L]
. Then
II+V
can be rewritten as

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|dx+\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\left|\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|dx&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L/2}\left|\int_{0}^{x}\underbrace{d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{\geqslant 0}\right|dx+\int_{L/2}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{0}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}-\int_{x}^{L}\underbrace{d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{\leqslant 0}\right|dx+\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\left|\int_{x}^{L}\underbrace{d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}}_{\leqslant 0}\right|dx\\
&&\\
&\leqslant&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L/2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx-\int_{L/2}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx+\int_{L/2}^{x_{\varepsilon}}\left|\int_{0}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|-\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\left(\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx\\
&&\\
&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L/2}\left(\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx-\int_{L/2}^{L}\left(\int_{x}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right)dx+\left|\frac{L}{2}-x_{\varepsilon}\right|\left|\int_{0}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|.\\
\end{array}

Now, as
u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0)=u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L)=\dfrac{c_{\varepsilon}}{\eta_{\varepsilon}+1}
, by (
4.27
)

and

both converge to
-c_{1}^{2}
. Then

\left|\frac{L}{2}-x_{\varepsilon}\right|\left|\int_{0}^{L}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}\right|=\left|\frac{L}{2}-x_{\varepsilon}\right||d_{\varepsilon}(L)-d_{\varepsilon}(0)|\rightarrow 0.

We study the remaining terms separately, recalling the expressions (

\begin{array}[]{lllll}\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{x}d_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}dx&\leqslant&\displaystyle 2(c_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}-c_{\varepsilon}^{2})\int_{0}^{L/2}\int_{0}^{x}-\frac{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{\prime}v_{0,\varepsilon}}{(v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon})^{2}}dx&=&\displaystyle(c_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}-c_{\varepsilon}^{2})\int_{0}^{L/2}\left[\frac{1}{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}+\eta_{\varepsilon}}\right]_{0}^{x}dx\\
&&\\
&\leqslant&\displaystyle c_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}\int_{0}^{L/2}\left(\frac{1}{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}(x)+\eta_{\varepsilon}}-\frac{1}{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}(0)+\eta_{\varepsilon}}\right)dx&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L/2}\frac{c_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}}{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}(x)+\eta_{\varepsilon}}dx-\frac{c_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}}{v_{0,\varepsilon}^{2}(0)+\eta_{\varepsilon}}\cdot\frac{L}{2}\\

Similarly, one has

where we used that

(see

, Lemma 2.4]
).
We conclude that

.

III+VI
:
Recall that
v_{\varepsilon}
is non-increasing on

and non-decreasing on

\begin{array}[]{lll}III+VI&=&\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(0))dx+\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}c_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(x)-u_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(L))dx\\

&&\\
&\rightarrow&c_{1}(a_{1}-c_{1}L)=0\\
\end{array}

where we used
subsection 4.3
. In conclusion, we get

.

This completes the proof of the equipartition of the energy.
∎

4.7
Computation of the weight of the limit measure

In this section, we compute the weight

of the Dirac mass

To do so, we use the equipartition principle, as it is done in

and
[

Proof of
(ii)
.

The equipartition principle ensures that :

. Recall that

is non-increasing on

and non-decreasing on

. We have :

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{L}2|v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}|(1-v_{\varepsilon})=\displaystyle\int_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}-2v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(1-v_{\varepsilon})+\int_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}2v_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(1-v_{\varepsilon})=\displaystyle\left[(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}+\left[-(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}=2(1-v_{\varepsilon}(x_{\varepsilon}))^{2}.

. Then

. According to

Hence,

and

\left[(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]_{0}^{x_{\varepsilon}}+\left[-(1-v_{\varepsilon})^{2}\right]_{x_{\varepsilon}}^{L}\rightarrow 2

We conclude that if
\alpha\neq 0
, then

.
∎

5.3
Critical Points of
\overline{MS}

Theorem 2.4
ensures that the minimizers of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
converge to a pair
(1,u)
that minimizes
\overline{MS}
if we have the additional assumption of an energy bound on
(AT_{\varepsilon})
.

A natural extension is to ask whether the critical points of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
also converge to critical points of
\overline{MS}
. Here, we prove that the critical points of
\overline{MS}
are either affine or piecewise constant.

Take
u
a minimizer of
\overline{MS}
. First,
u
has a finite number of discontinuity points, that we denote by
\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}\}
. Let
\varphi
in
SBV^{2}(0,L)
with
J_{\varphi}\subset J_{u}
and take
u+t\varphi
as a competitor for
t
a real number. Then,
J_{u+t\varphi}=J_{u}
so one has

\int_{0}^{L}|u^{\prime}|^{2}\leqslant\int_{0}^{L}|u^{\prime}+t\varphi^{\prime}|^{2},

which rewrites, after dividing by
t
, letting
t
tend successively to
0^{+}
and
0^{-}
, as :

\int_{0}^{L}u^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}=0.

So
u
is affine on each interval
(x_{i},x_{i+1})
. In the case where
u
admits at least one discontinuity point, say
x_{i}
, and take
\varphi
equal to
0
outside
(x_{i},x_{i+1})
and equal to
\dfrac{x-x_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}
on
(x_{i},x_{i+1})
(where
x_{i+1}
can be replaced by
L
). Then
\varphi\in SBV^{2}(0,L)
with
\varphi^{\prime}=1_{(x_{i},x_{i+1})},J_{\varphi}=\{x_{i}\}
and

u(x_{i+1})-u(x_{i})=\int_{x_{i}}^{x_{i+1}}u^{\prime}=\int_{0}^{L}u^{\prime}\varphi^{\prime}=0,

so that
u(x_{i})=u(x_{i+1})
, i.e
u
is piecewise constant.

Hence, critical points of
\overline{AT}_{\varepsilon}
converge to critical points of
\overline{MS}
. However, there is a selection criterion as limit functions of critical points of
AT_{\varepsilon}
must be non decreasing and admit at most one discontinuity point.

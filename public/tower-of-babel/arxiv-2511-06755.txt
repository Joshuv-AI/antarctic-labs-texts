Redshift Frame Systematics Account for Less than 1% of the Hubble Tension: A Full-Sky Covariance-Weighted Test with Pantheon+
Said Laaroua

Abstract
We perform full-sky covariance-weighted quantification of redshift-frame systematics in the Pantheon+ Type Ia supernova sample with the explicit goal of determining whether such effects can meaningfully bias the inferred Hubble constant. Using 1,543 unique SNe with redshifts heliocentric and CMB-frame, we detect statistically significant but physically small residuals: a monopole offset ⟨ z CMB − z HEL ⟩ = ( − 3.8 ± 0.1 ) × 10 − 4 \langle z_{\rm CMB}-z_{\rm HEL}\rangle=(-3.8\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-4} and a kinematic dipole of amplitude A = ( 1.5 ± 0.1 ) × 10 − 3 A=(1.5\pm 0.1)\times 10^{-3} aligned to within 1 ∘ 1^{\circ} of the CMB dipole. When propagated through the full Pantheon+ STAT+SYS covariance matrix, these residuals shift the inferred Hubble constant by only Δ ​ H 0 = 0.07 ​ km ​ s − 1 ​ Mpc − 1 \Delta H_{0}=0.07~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}} , corresponding to ≈ 1 % \approx 1\% of the present 5 5 – 6 ​ km ​ s − 1 ​ Mpc − 1 6~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}} Hubble tension. This result provides a direct quantitative demonstration that redshift-frame inconsistencies in Pantheon+ cannot contribute more than a percentage level fraction of the H 0 H_{0} discrepancy. We therefore rule out a class of proposed explanations attributing the Hubble tension to SN redshift-frame errors, local-flow contamination, or frame-transformation inaccuracies, and establish a precision benchmark for next-generation cosmological surveys.



I
Introduction

Accurate determination of the Hubble constant
H_{0}
from Type Ia supernovae (SNe Ia) requires precise measurement of the redshift of each object and the reference frame in which it is defined.
Standard cosmological analyses, such as Pantheon+
Scolnic2022
and SH0ES
Riess2022
, convert heliocentric redshifts (
z_{\mathrm{HEL}}
) into the cosmic microwave background (CMB) frame (
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}
) to remove the effects of the motion of the solar system. These corrections are small—of order
v_{\mathrm{CMB}}/c\simeq 1.2\times 10^{-3}
—but essential for anchoring the local Hubble flow to the cosmic rest frame.
Despite their ubiquity, the numerical precision and internal consistency of these transformations have not been systematically tested across the full Pantheon+ dataset.
Residuals at the level of
\Delta z\sim 10^{-3}
could, in principle, produce sub-percent biases in
H_{0}
, particularly in the low-redshift regime (
z<0.1
) used to calibrate the distance ladder.
Moreover, coherent directional patterns in
\Delta z
may trace the projection of our motion with respect to the CMB or indicate subtle anisotropies in the data reduction process
Kenworthy2023
;
Horstmann2022
;
Colin2019
;
Kogut1993
.
Several works have speculated that the Hubble tension may be largely or wholly due to redshift-frame or dipole-related effects—including heliocentric vs CMB-frame redshift choices
Colin2019b
, dipole anisotropies of local-motion
Krishnan2020
;
Wu2017
and mis-modeling of peculiar-velocity
Odderskov2014
;
Carr2022
. In this work we demonstrate using the full Pantheon+ SH0ES dataset and covariance matrix that such effects contribute
<1\%
to the inferred
H_{0}
and therefore cannot account for the
\approx 6~$\mathrm{k}\mathrm{m}\,\mathrm{s}^{-1}\,\mathrm{M}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{c}^{-1}$
discrepancy.

This analysis addresses two essential questions for precision cosmology:

1.

Are the heliocentric and CMB redshift frames in the Pantheon+ compilation numerically self-consistent at the level required for sub-percent cosmology?

2.

Do any remaining residuals exhibit a sky-dependent structure that could mimic or bias local measurements of the Hubble flow?

By answering these questions, our aim is to benchmark the numerical fidelity of widely used supernova catalogs and quantify a subtle but previously untested systematic relevant to next-generation
H_{0}
measurements. Thus, this work presents the first covariance-weighted, full-sky quantification of redshift-frame systematics in the Pantheon+ sample. We statistically compare
z_{\mathrm{HEL}}
,
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}
, and the Hubble-diagram redshift (
z_{\mathrm{HD}}
); identify any redshift- or direction-dependent residuals; and evaluate their effect on the inferred Hubble constant.

To isolate the impact of the redshift-frame choice on the inferred value of
H_{0}
, we performed a dedicated one-parameter likelihood analysis based on the Pantheon+ SH0ES dataset. After deduplication, the full sample contains 1543 SNe Ia; restricting to the Hubble-flow range
0.023<z<0.15
yields 431 objects. For these SNe we extract both heliocentric and CMB-frame redshifts as provided by Pantheon+, as well as the corrected apparent magnitudes
m_{B,\mathrm{corr}}
.

We use the full
1543\times 1543
Pantheon+ STAT+SYS covariance matrix, restricted to the 431 Hubble-flow SNe. The matrix is symmetrized and regularized to ensure positive-definiteness, and its inverse is evaluated via a Cholesky decomposition. The theoretical distance modulus is modeled using a second-order low-
z
expansion of the luminosity distance,

\mu_{\rm th}(z;H_{0})=5\log_{10}\!\left[\frac{c\,z}{H_{0}}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{2}(1-q_{0})\,z\right)\right]+25,

(1)

with deceleration parameter fixed to
q_{0}=-0.55
.

To avoid degeneracies between
H_{0}
and the absolute magnitude
M
, we fix
M
to the value obtained from the full two-parameter fit in the CMB frame, namely
M_{\rm abs}=-19.2403
. The likelihood for
H_{0}
is then

\chi^{2}(H_{0})=\Delta\mu^{\mathsf{T}}\,C^{-1}\,\Delta\mu,

(2)

\Delta\mu=m_{B,\mathrm{corr}}-M_{\rm abs}-\mu_{\rm th}(z;H_{0}),

(3)

where
C
is the Hubble-flow covariance submatrix.

Uncertainties on
H_{0}
are obtained non-parametrically from the
\Delta\chi^{2}=1
criterion: the
1\sigma
interval is defined by
\chi^{2}(H_{0,\pm})=\chi^{2}_{\rm min}+1
. This approach is robust to Hessian conditioning and yields a stable, frame-independent error estimate. The procedure is applied identically to the CMB-frame and heliocentric redshift columns, enabling a direct comparison of the inferred
H_{0}
values.

II
Data and Methodology

This work revisits the Pantheon+ dataset to
(1) test the statistical consistency of the redshift frames;
(2) determine whether discrepancies exhibit redshift- or sky-dependent structure; and
(3) quantify their effect on
H_{0}
when full covariance information is included.

We use the publicly available Pantheon+ SH0ES compilation containing 1,701 SNe Ia spanning
0.001<z<2.3
.
Each entry provides heliocentric, CMB, and Hubble-diagram redshifts (
z_{\mathrm{HEL}}
,
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}
,
z_{\mathrm{HD}}
), apparent magnitudes, and light-curve corrections.
We remove duplicate entries by supernova identifier (CID), yielding 1,543 unique objects. The complete Pantheon+ STAT+SYS covariance matrix is loaded and reshaped into its
(1543\times 1543)
form, aligned with the deduplicated dataset.
All reported statistical and systematic uncertainties, including peculiar-velocity errors, are propagated through this matrix.

For each supernova, we compute

\Delta z_{\mathrm{CMB-HEL}}=z_{\mathrm{CMB}}-z_{\mathrm{HEL}}.

(4)

We test the null hypothesis
\langle\Delta z_{\mathrm{CMB-HEL}}\rangle=0
using a one-sample
t
-test, and compare low- and high-redshift subsets (
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}<0.01
vs.
z_{\mathrm{CMB}}>0.03
) using Welch’s test for unequal variances.

The distance modulus in a flat
\Lambda
CDM universe at low
z
is modeled as

\mu(z;H_{0})=5\log_{10}\!\left[\frac{cz}{H_{0}}\left(1+\frac{1}{2}(1-q_{0})z\right)\right]+25,

(5)

where
q_{0}=-0.55
and
c
is the speed of light.
The best-fit
H_{0}
is obtained by minimizing

\chi^{2}(H_{0})=(\bm{\mu}-\bm{\mu}_{\mathrm{mod}})^{\!T}\mathbf{C}^{-1}(\bm{\mu}-\bm{\mu}_{\mathrm{mod}}),

(6)

where
\mathbf{C}
is the full covariance matrix.
The fits are restricted to the SH0ES-defined Hubble-flow range
0.023<z<0.15
, and uncertainties on
H_{0}
are derived from the curvature of
\chi^{2}
at its minimum.

To test for anisotropy, we model the sky variation of
\Delta z
as

\Delta z(\hat{n})=A\cos\theta+B,

(7)

where
A
is the dipole amplitude,
B
the offset of the monopole, and
\theta
the angle between each supernova and the dipole axis
(\mathrm{RA}_{0},\mathrm{Dec}_{0})
. The parameters
(A,B,\mathrm{RA}_{0},\mathrm{Dec}_{0})
are obtained by minimizing
\sum(\Delta z-A\cos\theta-B)^{2}
.
We then compare the best-fit dipole with the known CMB dipole and visualize
\Delta z
across the sky using a Mollweide projection.

A closely related line of work is the redshift-consistency analysis performed by Carr et al. (2021)
Carr2022
, who reviewed heliocentric–to-CMB-frame transformations and examined the impact of coherent local flows on low-redshift SNe Ia. Their study provided updated redshift values and highlighted the need for precise treatment of Solar System motion when anchoring the local Hubble flow.

Whereas Carr et al. focused on improving the underlying redshift corrections, our approach evaluates whether the existing transformations introduce coherent residual patterns large enough to bias Hubble-flow inferences.
In this sense, the two analyses are methodologically distinct but address the same overarching question: whether redshift-frame systematics could meaningfully affect the determination of the Hubble constant.

IV
Discussion

Our analysis reveals coherent redshift-frame residuals in Pantheon+ that are statistically significant but cosmologically negligible. The measured dipole (
A=1.48\times 10^{-3}
) aligns within
3^{\circ}
of the CMB apex, with a weak peculiar velocity correlation (
r=0.15
), confirming a kinematic origin rather than data systematics.

The redshift-dependent sign reversal—positive at
z<0.01
, negative at
z>0.03
—marks the transition from local velocity dominance to Hubble flow. When propagated through covariance-weighted Hubble fits, these effects shift
H_{0}
by only
\Delta H_{0}=0.07~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
(
\sim 1.3\%
of the Hubble tension), definitively excluding frame systematics as a significant contributor.

Secondary numerical effects arise from finite precision (
\Delta z\sim 10^{-6}
) in redshift transformations, producing coherent offsets comparable to our measured amplitude. While negligible for current cosmology, these residuals establish a precision floor for next-generation surveys like
Roman
and LSST, requiring
\geq 10^{-8}
redshift precision for sub-percent measurements.

Our covariance-weighted methodology demonstrates Pantheon+’s internal consistency and provides a benchmark for future frame-level validation in precision cosmology.

The fixed-
M
one-parameter analysis provides a clean measurement of the effect of redshift-frame choice on the Pantheon+ determination of
H_{0}
. Using the full covariance matrix and the 431 Hubble-flow SNe, we obtain

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\mathrm{CMB}}

\displaystyle=72.998\pm 0.304~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},

(8)

\displaystyle H_{0}^{\mathrm{HEL}}

\displaystyle=73.116\pm 0.304~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}},

(9)

corresponding to a difference of

\Delta H_{0}\equiv H_{0}^{\mathrm{CMB}}-H_{0}^{\mathrm{HEL}}=-0.118~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}.

(10)

The relative shift is therefore only
\simeq 0.16\%
, more than an order of magnitude smaller than the
\sim 5
–
6~\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}\,Mpc^{-1}}
Hubble tension. Crucially, although the heliocentric-frame fit yields a significantly worse goodness-of-fit (with
\Delta\chi^{2}\approx 31
), the best-fit value of
H_{0}
is almost unchanged.

These results demonstrate that the choice of redshift frame—heliocentric versus CMB—has a negligible impact on the inferred Hubble constant when the full Pantheon+ covariance is used. Redshift-frame systematics therefore cannot provide a resolution of the Hubble tension, nor do they produce a meaningful bias in the determination of low-redshift of
H_{0}
. This constitutes a quantitative and model-independent exclusion of an entire class of proposed explanations that attribute the tension to mis-specified local-frame redshifts or large-scale dipolar flows.

V
Conclusion

In this work, we subjected the Pantheon+ redshift frames to a rigorous, full-covariance stress test. Our detection of a kinematic dipole aligned with the Solar motion serves not merely as a calibration check, but as a physical validation of the catalog’s integrity: the data successfully recovers the observer’s motion through the CMB rest frame.

By constraining the impact of frame-dependent systematics to
\Delta H_{0}=0.07
km s
-1
Mpc
-1
, we provide evidence that the Hubble tension is robust against redshift-level numerical artifacts. While recent literature has rightfully explored local bulk flows as a potential source of bias, our results demonstrate that the Pantheon+ implementation of these corrections is internally consistent and cosmologically neutral.

Consequently, this analysis effectively closes the ”redshift systematic” loophole. The discrepancy between early- and late-universe measurements cannot be resolved by re-examining frame transformations or local kinematic corrections. This nullifies a specific class of proposed solutions to the
H_{0}
crisis, narrowing the remaining explanatory space to photometric calibration issues or, increasingly likely, non-standard physics in the pre-recombination era. As the community prepares for the LSST and Roman era, this work establishes a systematic floor, confirming that current redshift precision is already sufficient for sub-percent cosmology.

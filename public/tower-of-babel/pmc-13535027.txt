More precise risk stratification for de novo acute myeloid leukemia with double-mutation CEBPA
Authors: Jing jing Liu, Hai ping Yang, Meng Hu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundCEBPA double-mutation (CEBPAdm) defines a favorable-risk subgroup of acute myeloid leukemia (AML), but marked heterogeneity exists, and relapse remains common. More precise prognostic stratification is urgently needed for this population.ObjectivesTo identify reliable prognostic factors and establish a refined risk stratification model for de novo AML with CEBPAdm.DesignSingle-center retrospective observational cohort study.MethodsSamples were collected from AML patients at diagnosis for analysis. The proportions of different surface antigens in the bone marrow were evaluated via flow cytometry. The detection of mutations in AML patients was based on next-generation sequencing (NGS). Multiparametric flow cytometry (MFC) can detect minimal residual disease (MRD). The data were analysed via Statistical Program for Social Sciences Version 27.0 (SPSS 27.0).Results①Univariate analysis revealed that high expression of cluster of differentiation antigen 33 (CD33)was unfavourable prognostic factor (P=0.028). ②There was a statistically significant difference between patients with more than 2 companion gene mutations (χ2=9.868, P=0.002). ③Notably, patients who became MRD-negative after induction chemotherapy had more favourable outcomes (χ2=3.847, P=0.05). ④Overall survival (OS) was worse in the high expression of CD33 group (43.00 months vs. 39.00 months, Log Rank P=0.024).ConclusionsThese findings provide a more precise restratification for CEBPAdm patients on the basis of the expression of CD33.Keywords: acute myeloid leukemia, double-mutation CEBPA, measurable residual disease, allogeneic stem cell transplantation, chemotherapy

Introduction

Acute myeloid leukaemia (AML) is characterized by the rapid proliferation of abnormal myeloid cells, leading to the suppression of normal haematopoiesis. 1 Patients with de novo AML are monitored myeloid fusion and mutant genes for prognostic stratification. Chemotherapy regimens are individualized based on genetic results, so that patients can achieve better outcomes and longer survival.

The identification of CCAAT/enhancer binding protein-a (CEBPA) mutations represents a breakthrough in understanding the molecular basis of AML, 3-15% newly diagnosed AML patients with CEBPA double mutations (CEBPAdm). Patients with CEBPAdm are associated with a favourable prognosis.2–4 However, AML patients with CEBPAdm have a 30–50% relapse rate. The heterogeneity of AML in patients with CEBPAdm has been reported in many studies. 5 CEBPAdm occurs most frequently in the FAB M2 subtype and is closely related to the expression of CD13, CD33, and myeloperoxidase (MPO) on leukemic cells. 6 Concurrent gene mutations such as FLT3-ITD, GATA2, and CSF3R are commonly observed in CEBPAdm AML, which may contribute to its prognostic heterogeneity. 7 Multiparameter flow cytometry (MFC) is a rapid, sensitive, and widely available technique for evaluating immunophenotypic characteristics and measurable residual disease (MRD) in AML. Surface antigen expression profiles detected by flow cytometry are closely associated with leukemic cell biology and clinical prognosis, providing feasible and practical indicators for prognostic stratification. Among these antigens, CD33 is stably expressed on myeloid leukemic blasts and serves as a key immunotherapeutic target, making it a promising candidate for risk restratification of CEBPAdm AML.8,9

Although CEBPAdm is recognized as a favorable prognostic marker in AML, 30–50% of CEBPAdm AML patients still experience relapse, indicating significant clinical and molecular heterogeneity within this subgroup. 10 Reliable prognostic factors are lacking to distinguish high-risk patients who may benefit from intensified therapy or allogeneic stem cell transplantation. Thus, a more precise risk stratification model is urgently needed to guide personalized treatment and improve long-term survival for de novo AML patients with CEBPAdm. Therefore, treatments and monitoring strategies for these patients need to consider a variety of factors comprehensively. For de novo AML, rapid identification of poor survival patients with high risk factors, and preemptive treatment with more aggressive therapy, such as haematopoietic stem cell transplant (HSCT), may improve survival (6-8).11–13

Methods

Patients

We conducted a retrospective analysis of patients diagnosed de novo AML with CEBPAdm at our institution (Department of Hematology, First Affiliated Hospital of Henan University of Science and Technology) between November 2017 and February 2023. All patients were adults (age≥18 years); no pediatric patients were included in this study.

Inclusion criteria: (1) Age≥18 years; (2) newly diagnosed de novo AML; (3) confirmed CEBPAdm by next-generation sequencing; (4) complete clinical and follow-up data. Exclusion criteria: (1) Secondary or therapy-related AML; (2) combined with other malignant tumors; (3) incomplete clinical or molecular data; (4) loss to follow-up.

Eighty-six patients with complete information were included in the study. All patients were diagnosed, evaluated and treated according to the National Comprehensive Cancer Network (NCCN) guidelines. 14 Patients with CEBPAdm were further stratified using a combination of clinical, molecular, immunophenotype, cytogenetic parameters, and MRD. In the report, we conduct traditional induction chemotherapy, such as standard IA (Idarubicin+cytarabine), DA (Daunorubicin+cytarabine), TA (Pirarubicin+cytarabine), or CAG (cytarabine+aclamycin+granulocyte colony stimulating factor) with or without the decitabine regimen.11,15 Consolidation chemotherapy was given after complete remission (CR). If the patients did not acquire CR, we gave another induction chemotherapy different from the first one. Consolidation chemotherapy usually included cytarabine. But different regimens were used according to the results. For patients with a moderate or poor prognosis, allogeneic haematopoietic stem cell transplantation were performed. 13 In the retrospective analysis, the risk stratification and definitions of CR, no remission (NR), OS (overall survival), etc., were obtained from the NCCN guidelines for AML. 14 Follow-up was performed by consulting medical records or via telephone. The follow-up deadline was April 01, 2024.

All clinical, laboratory, and follow-up data were collected retrospectively from electronic medical records and paper medical documents.

Methods

To determine the expression ratio of cell surface antigens, including CD17, CD13, CD33, and MPO, and detect MRD status, a multi-colour immunolabelling method was used via FACS analysis (FACS Navios, Beckman Coulter), as described previously.13,15–17 For flow cytometric analysis, multi-color immunolabelling was performed using commercially available monoclonal antibodies (Beckman Coulter, USA) and analyzed on a FACS Navios flow cytometer (Beckman Coulter, Brea, CA, USA). CD33 expression was evaluated as the percentage of CD33-positive leukemic blasts in bone marrow mononuclear cells. The optimal cutoff value of 64.16% was determined by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis to stratify patients into low-CD33 and high-CD33 groups. Measurable residual disease (MRD) was detected by 8-color multiparametric flow cytometry (MFC) according to the European LeukemiaNet (ELN) guidelines, with a threshold of < 0.1% leukemic cells defined as MRD negativity. 18 Besides, we got the result of bone marrow chromosome karyotype analysis by the R-banding method. Specific chromosomal abnormalities including +8, −7, −5, 11q23 rearrangement, t (9; 22), complex karyotype were recorded in detail. Fusion genes were detected by Real-time quantitative PCR (RT-PCR) using an ABI 7500 PCR system (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA).11,15 Genetic testing was performed on the Illumina NovaSeq sequencing platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). COSMIC, dbSNP, PolyPhen, SIFT and other databases and software to determine pathogenic gene mutations. 15

Statistical analysis

The data were analysed via Statistical Program for Social Sciences Version 27.0 (SPSS 27.0). The probability of OS, from the time of diagnosis to all-cause death, was calculated via the Kaplan‒Meier method and compared via the log-rank test, generating survival curves. The chi-square test (2-tailed) was used to compare the categorical variables between the different groups. For univariate and multivariate analyses, log-rank tests were applied to analyse differences in OS between groups. Estimation was limited to the greatest survival if it was censored. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant. This was a retrospective study including all consecutive eligible patients with CEBPAdm AML at our center during the study period. No formal sample size calculation or power analysis was performed.

The reporting of this study conforms to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) statement. 19

Results

Our study included 86 patients with CEBPAdm AML. We identified distinct subgroups within this population, with varying clinical characteristics. Patients with concurrent mutations in genes such as FLT3-ITD presented a greater incidence. However, GATA2 and CSFR3 co-mutations are usually reported.6,12,20,21 Most patients (73.26%) have more than one co-mutated gene, and among them, 24 patients (38.10%) had no less than three co-mutated genes. The results showed recurrence in 29 (33.72%) patients consistent with what was reported in the literature.5,13 CEBPAdm appear most usually in patients with the FAB M2 subtype (58.14%), and are closely associated with the expression of CD13, CD33, and MPO on the surface of leukemic cells. 5

We found patients who the age<46.6 years old, WBC<39.9×109/l at diagnosis, without relapse, MRD negative after induction chemotherapy and less than two concurrent mutations had a better prognosis. The univariate analysis results showed that low level of CD33 expression (<64.16%) at diagnosis was a favourable factor for OS(HR=2.089, P=0.028). Discussion

In recent years, a growing number of studies have shown that CEBPAdm AML is a heterogeneous group of diseases. Therefore, several studies have attempted to restratify the prognosis of such patients, in which a combination of genetic mutations and MRD may be potential prognostic indicators. 22 Although CEBPAdm is favorable prognostic factor in AML patients, our retrospective study showed that 29 patients (33.72%) still relapsed. Therefore, more precise stratification is needed for de novo AML with CEBPAdm to achieve better prognosis and avoid relapse. Our results showed that MRD negative after two courses of chemotherapy in 73 patients (84.88%) and had no influence on OS in univariate analysis. In our study, patients who relapsed had poor OS, gave intensified chemotherapy or underwent transplant procedures.22,23

Our findings underscored the heterogeneity of CEBPAdm AML and highlighted the need for a personalized approach to risk assessment. By integrating data, including clinical characteristics and cooccurring genetic alterations, we proposed a refined stratification model that may better predict patient’ outcomes and inform for treatment decisions. According to the previous literature, co-mutated genes may have prognostic restratification value, including GATA2, WT1, TET2, and CSF3R.6,12,20 However, this retrospective study revealed a higher rate of the FLT3 mutation (24.42%), which did not affect OS. Previous results have shown that FLT3-ITD was an independent poor prognostic factor for OS. 24 Although co-mutation was found to have no effect on OS, the number of co-mutation genes was associated with patient prognosis.

There have been a number of reports confirming that CD33 is a feasible target for immunotherapy of AML. 15 As immunophenotype in AML patients, CD33 can be used to restratify CEBPAdm patients according to the proportion of CD33 expression. This allows for a more individualized treatment strategy.

In multivariate Cox regression analysis, high CD33 expression showed a hazard ratio of 1.899 (95% CI: 0.969–3.720, P=0.062), which did not reach strict statistical significance but showed a strong trend toward an adverse prognostic effect. This marginal significance may be related to the relatively small sample size of this study. Nevertheless, combined with the univariate analysis and survival curve results, CD33 expression level still provides valuable information for risk stratification in CEBPAdm AML and deserves further validation in larger cohorts.

This study revealed that transplantation, 7 patients among them, had no effect on patient outcomes. Consolidation and maintenance therapy after remission has been recommended in some studies; however, recurrence rates are high, and some studies have shown a decrease in recurrence rates in patients after transplantation. 13 Perhaps more clinical studies are needed to obtain more reliable data.

Although our prognostic model is based on a relatively small sample and needs to be expanded or validated by prospective clinical trials, the model integrates pre-treatment and post-treatment parameters, which has certain reference significance for the establishment of prediction models in follow-up studies.

Limitations

First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size (n=86). Second, no formal sample size calculation or power analysis was performed, which may reduce the statistical power of some analyses. Third, the median follow-up duration was moderate. Fourth, the multivariate effect of CD33 was marginally significant. Finally, the results require prospective validation in multi-center cohorts.

Conclusion

This study presented a comprehensive analysis of de novo AML with CEBPAdm, advocating for risk stratification. These indicators can be combined for more precise stratification and assessment of prognosis in CEBPAdm patients. However, except for the level of CD33, other indicators did not impact patient’ OS in the retrospective analysis study. In the future, CD33-targeted therapy may become one of the treatment strategies for de novo AML with CEBPAdm.

Enhanced Early Stabilization and Improved Clinical Outcomes by Transforaminal Lumbar Interbody Fusion With Contralateral Facet Fusion Compared With Conventional Posterior Lumbar Interbody Fusion
Authors: Tae Hoon Kang, Seok-In Jang, Minjoon Cho, Jae Hyup Lee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThis study introduces and evaluates the transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion technique with contralateral facet fusion (TLIFcFF), aiming to improve fusion rates and clinical outcomes compared to conventional posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF).MethodsA retrospective case-control study was conducted on patients undergoing TLIFcFF or PLIF between July 2009 and November 2020. Patients with one- or two-level lumbar fusion were included, excluding cases involving long-level fusions, revision surgeries, or other diseases. Perioperative parameters, radiological outcomes, fusion rates, and clinical outcomes were analyzed.ResultsThe study included 101 patients in the TLIFcFF group and 89 in the PLIF group, with a mean follow-up of 57 months. TLIFcFF demonstrated higher fusion rates at six months (84.9% vs. 66.4%, P < 0.001) but comparable fusion rates at one year. TLIFcFF showed significant improvements in low back pain and radiating pain scores at 3, 6, and 12 months postoperatively (P < 0.05). There were no significant differences in complications, operation time, or functional outcomes (Oswestry Disability Index, Functional Rating Index) between groups.ConclusionTLIF with contralateral facet fusion represents a viable alternative to conventional PLIF, demonstrating advantages in early fusion rates and reduced pain within the first year. Further prospective studies are warranted to validate these findings.

INTRODUCTION

Lumbar interbody fusion is an effective treatment for spinal stenosis and intervertebral instability causing neural compression.1 Traditional approaches include anterior, posterior, transforaminal, lateral, and oblique lateral techniques. The posterior approach, posterior lumbar interbody fusion (PLIF), allows for thorough neural decompression via bilateral facetectomies and enables bilateral cage insertion. While this extensive removal of posterior structures facilitates segmental lordosis restoration and scoliosis correction, it may compromise spinal stability and increases the risk of neural injury—such as incidental durotomy or permanent nerve damage—due to dural retraction during bilateral cage insertion.234567 To enhance stability, PLIF is often combined with posterolateral fusion (PLF); however, this necessitates wide exposure of the transverse processes and results in additional paraspinal muscle damage.8

Transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion (TLIF) was developed to address these limitations by using a paramedian approach with unilateral facetectomy, thereby reducing neural traction and preserving contralateral posterior elements.791011 Nevertheless, open TLIF still requires bilateral muscle detachment for pedicle screw insertion.6 Moreover, performing additional PLF demands even further muscle dissection.

To overcome these challenges, we developed a modified technique called “transforaminal lumbar interbody fusion with contralateral facet fusion (TLIFcFF),” which combines open TLIF with preservation of contralateral facet and lamina using unilateral laminotomy and bilateral decompression (ULBD), along with contralateral facet fusion performed without exposing the transverse process. This approach aims to achieve sufficient neural decompression and stabilization than conventional PLIF while minimizing muscle damage typically associated with PLF. We hypothesized that TLIFcFF would yield higher fusion rates and better clinical outcomes compared to conventional PLIF.

METHODS

Study design and material

This study is a retrospective case-control study. The TLIFcFF technique has been implemented at our institution since 2011. To evaluate its efficacy, we sought to compare it with conventional PLIF performed during the same period. Therefore, the material for this study includes patients who underwent either TLIFcFF or conventional PLIF between July 2009 and November 2020. All surgeries were performed by a single senior surgeon who had more than 10-year experiences of spine operation on 2009. There were no specific criteria established for determining which surgical technique to use. The selection between TLIFcFF and PLIF was based on the surgeon's clinical judgment at the time of surgery.

Indications and contraindications of TLIFcFF

The indications and contraindications for the TLIFcFF are consistent with those for conventional one-level or two-level PLIF. The primary indications include degenerative spondylolisthesis with spinal stenosis or degenerative spondylosis with foraminal stenosis that has not responded to conservative treatment. Absolute contraindications for the procedure include patient refusal of surgical treatment and relative contraindications include cases where general anesthesia is not feasible due to poor general health, suspected systemic infection.

Study inclusion and exclusion criteria

To evaluate the fusion rate, only patients with a minimum follow-up period of one year were included in the study. Considering that the TLIFcFF was performed only in patients requiring less than three-level fusion, PLIF cases more than three levels were excluded. Additionally, cases for metabolic bone diseases (Paget’s disease), infection, trauma, tumors were excluded.

Surgical procedures of TLIFcFF and conventional PLIF

TLIFcFF

The surgery is performed under general anesthesia, and the preparation follows the standard protocol for posterior spinal surgeries. Once the patient is placed in the prone position on a spine table or Wilson frame, standard sterilization and draping procedures are performed. A midline incision is made, with confirmation using C-arm or X-ray to ensure exposure from the uppermost to the lowermost spinous processes of the surgical segments. A conventional midline approach is conducted by detaching the paraspinal muscles. Bilateral pedicle screws are inserted at the target segment. The spinous process and interspinous ligament are partially removed to expose the disc level, while preserving the uppermost and lowermost spinous processes. Laminotomy is performed on the narrower foramen side or the concave side in cases of scoliosis. On the opposite side, saving laminar as much as possible, sublaminar decompression is performed. The ligamentum flavum is removed to secure the course of the bilateral traversing nerve roots (ULBD). On the ipsilateral side, the remaining inferior articular process (IAP) and medial half of superior articular process (SAP) is removed to expose the Kambin’s triangle. After exposing it, discectomy is performed, and endplate preparation follows. Sufficient bone grafting is performed using autologous bone from laminectomy and facetectomy mixed with allogenic cancellous bone. Less than 30cc of allogeneic cancellous bone per patient was used as a supplement routinely. No additional materials like demineralized bone matrix were used and no iliac bone grafting was performed. One single TLIF cage was inserted with gentle retraction of dura (Bullet type, PEEK, no lordotic angle, length: 30–32 mm, width: 10 mm, height: 9–13 mm). On the contralateral facet joint and remaining lamina, decortication is performed, followed by the placement of residual bone grafts. No additional bone grafting was performed on the facet joints or transverse processes. A drain is placed, and the surgery is concluded (Figs. 1 and 2).

Conventional PLIF

A midline approach was used with bilateral exposure. The IAPs and spinous process were removed bilaterally, while the bilateral SAPs were removed partially depending on the decompression needs. Bilateral discectomy was performed, followed by endplate preparation. Two cages were inserted bilaterally with adequate bone grafting within the disc space (Bullet type, PEEK, no lordotic angle, length: 22–26 mm, width: 9 mm, height: 9–13 mm). About bone fusion materials, the same protocol is applied with TLIFcFF. No additional bone grafting was performed on the facet joints or transverse processes.

Comparison between two groups

To ensure the equivalence except the operation method between the two groups, demographic factors, including age, sex, surgical levels, body mass index (BMI), bone mineral density (BMD), and operation time, were compared.

Perioperative factors (operation time, blood loss, hospital days, complications)

Operation times, measured from skin incision to closure, were recorded. Blood loss was assessed using estimated blood loss (EBL) from surgical records and by evaluating the hemoglobin (Hb) drop, defined as the difference between preoperative and immediate postoperative Hb levels. The presence and volume of transfusions were also compared. The duration of the hospital stay was documented. Considering the potential for neural tissue damage due to dural retraction during cage insertions, we assessed and compared the incidence of intraoperative durotomy or permanent dural damage between groups. Finally, reoperation rates were reviewed.

Radiological evaluation (fusion rates and alignment correction)

Radiological evaluations consisted of two points, fusion rates and spine alignment correction. First, fusion rates, the primary outcomes, were analyzed at 6 months by flexion and extension gab of X-rays and at 1 year by the gaps of X-rays and CT images. CT scans were limited to the discharge and 1-year timepoints to minimize radiation exposure to patients. Successful fusion on X-rays was defined as translation < 3 mm and angular change < 3 on flexion-extension films.12 On CT, fusion was defined as the existence of bony trabeculae between the upper and lower vertebral endplates on 1mm thin cut 3D CT scans (Fig. 3).13 To ensure the accuracy of the results in X-ray and CT results, two orthopedic surgeons checked independently and the intraclass correlation coefficient was 0.92. If there is a difference in measurement, they reviewed it, and the result was reached by agreement. Considering the TLIFcFF is done with single facetectomy and single cage while PLIF is done with bilateral facetectomies and bilateral cages insertion, radiographic alignment assessments were added. Segmental lumbar lordosis (SL), total lumbar lordosis (L1-S1, TL), segmental scoliosis (SS), and total lumbar scoliosis (L1-S1, TS) at preoperative and postoperative 1-year standing whole spine X-rays were measured.

Computed tomography images taken 1 year postoperatively. (A) Coronal cut showing bony trabeculae in the intervertebral space. (B) Sagittal cut demonstrating bony trabeculae in the intervertebral space. (C) Axial cut revealing the fusion status of the right facet. (D) Coronal cut confirming the fusion status of the right facet.

Clinical outcomes

Clinical parameters contain pain scores of low back and radiculopathy and functional scorings. Low back pain (LBP) and radiating pain (RP) intensity were assessed using the Visual Analog Scale, recorded on a scale of 0–10. Functional recovery between the two groups was compared using the Oswestry Disability Index (ODI) and Functional Rating index.14 Data were collected preoperatively and at 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year postoperatively.

Statistical analysis

All continuous variables were analyzed using Student’s t-test, while categorical variables were compared using the chi-square test. All statistical analyses were performed using Rex software (Rex, Seoul, Korea, based on R). A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Ethics statement

This retrospective case series study, reviewing medical records post-surgery, was conducted in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and the 1964 Helsinki Declaration. Consent was exempted, and the study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of SMG-SNU Boramae Medical Center (No. 20-2022-46).

RESULTS

One hundred one patients (165 levels) underwent TLIFcFF, while 89 patients (133 levels) underwent PLIF. The minimum follow-up period was 1 year, and the maximum was 13 years 10 months. The mean follow-up duration was 57 months ± 38 months. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups in age, sex distribution, BMI, BMD, or segmental distribution (Table 1).

Perioperative factors (operation time, blood loss, hospital days, complications)

There was no significant difference in operation time between the two groups. Regarding blood loss, TLIFcFF group demonstrated more EBL in one-level operations and more Hb drop in two-level operations. However, no differences were observed between the two groups in terms of transfusion rates or volume. Unlikely to blood loss, the TLIFcFF group demonstrated shorter hospital stays compared to the PLIF group.

With respect to neurological complications, there were no significant differences between the groups. In terms of reoperation cases, there were three reoperation cases due to one wound dehiscence and two epidural hematomas in the TLIFcFF group. Meanwhile, there were two reoperation cases involving one epidural hematoma and one posterior migration of the bone graft (Table 2).

Clinical outcomes

Both groups demonstrated a reduction in LBP and RP postoperatively. However, the TLIFcFF group consistently recorded significantly lower LBP scores at 3 months, 6 months, and 1 year. Similarly, the TLIFcFF group showed significantly lower RP scores at 3 months and 1 year postoperatively. Both groups showed notable improvements in functional scores. Although the TLIFcFF group demonstrated consistently lower pain scores, the ODI scores remained slightly higher than the PLIF group at all timepoints, though this difference was not statistically significant (Fig. 4).

DISCUSSION

Our hypothesis was that TLIFcFF would offer advantages in fusion rate and clinical outcomes compared to conventional PLIF. From our data, contralateral facet fusion improved the early stabilization, observed at six months postoperatively.

As far as many surgeons have performed PLIFs combining with PLF to increase the fusion rate. That’s because there was no residual bone bed except the pedicle areas and transverse processes after both facetectomies, which leads more paraspinal muscle detachments and retractions.151617 With advancements in fixation devices, recent efforts to minimize muscle detachment, aimed at alleviating pain and improving recovery outcomes, have led to a decreased necessity for additional PLF procedures.1118 However, in the setting of mid-line aproach with bilateral pedicle screw fixations, bone grafting over decorticated lamina and contralateral facet joint in TLIF can be performed without additional muscle detachment. However, no prior studies have evaluated the effect of contralateral facet fusion in TLIF compared to conventional PLIF without PLF. This study is, as we know, the first to demonstrate the effectiveness of contralateral facet fusion in achieving early fusion outcomes during TLIF.

The most remarkable finding was the quicker fusion observed in the TLIFcFF group. Numerous studies have demonstrated that PLIF and TLIF show no significant differences in fusion rates at one year.1920 However, in our data, while the one-year fusion rates were comparable between the groups, the TLIFcFF group exhibited significantly higher fusion rates at six months postoperatively, particularly in two-level fusions. While flexion-extension radiographs cannot assess fusion status as accurately as CT scans, we were able to confirm a clear difference in 6 months. Previous studies have indicated that combining PLF with posterior interbody fusion can promote earlier fusion, and our findings align with this rationale.2122 This accelerated fusion process in the TLIFcFF may be attributed to several factors. First, preservation of the contralateral facet joint which reduces micromotion and may enhance fusion. Second, Additional decortication and bone grafting to the contralateral facet joint provides an additional fusion surface beyond the interbody space. Finally, saving the contralateral facet joint could contain saving articular arteries and other vessels, which is important to bone fusion.

In the same context of the early fusion rates, our data revealed lower LBP scores in the TLIFcFF group, which could result from earlier vertebral stabilization.23 Interestingly, RP scores were also lower in the TLIFcFF group. Previous studies have shown that delayed fusion could be related to RP.24 However, there were no significant differences in functional scores between the groups. It’s because both surgical techniques were effective for degenerative spine diseases and showed substantial improvements in functional outcomes. Meanwhile, preoperative function scores were higher in the TLIFcFF, this apparent discrepancy may be attributed to demographic differences between the groups, as the TLIFcFF group had a higher proportion of patients with two-level fusions (43.6% vs. 38.2%, P = 0.053), which means higher tendency of lumbar degeneration.25

Our data revealed no significant difference in operation time between the two groups. Only single facetectomy and cage insertion were performed in TLIFcFF contrast to PLIF, additional contralateral decortication, and bone grafting took time and the tendency of two-level operations affected. In terms of blood loss, we hypothesized that only one facetectomy in the TLIFcFF would result in less blood loss compared to bilateral facetectomies. However, it looked like the additional decortication work and two-level operations contributed to more blood loss. Despite this, there was no significant difference in transfusion rates or volumes between the groups, indicating that blood loss differences did not impact clinical outcomes. Regarding hospital stay, patients in the TLIFcFF group were discharged more quickly. It may be related to the early stabilization of the spine leading to a tendency to discharge earlier.26 Regarding complications, neural complications showed no differences between the two groups. Although TLIF theoretically carries lower risk of such complications compared to PLIF, the surgeon’s extensive experience with PLIF led to the absence of differences. Similarly, reoperation rates were comparable between groups.

Previous studies have suggested that bilateral facetectomies are necessary to achieve SL and TL during TLIF, similar to a posterior column osteotomy.2728 However, our data revealed no significant differences in SL or TL between the two groups. We acknowledge that our study did not include comprehensive spinopelvic parameter analysis, which represents a limitation in fully assessing sagittal balance. However, there was no tendency of less correction in sagittal balance in TLIFcFF and this might be due to the use of bilateral pedicle screw fixation and correction of lordosis. Interestingly, the TLIFcFF group demonstrated less TS at one year postoperatively. This finding may be attributed to the single facetectomy and unilateral cage insertion to the foraminal stenosis side or concave side of degenerative scoliosis.29

Regarding surgical considerations, the TLIFcFF technique does not require specific additional preparations, which is one of its advantages. However, table tilting plays a critical role during ULBD. Without bilateral facetectomies, contralateral decompression must be achieved through undermining, making proper table tilting essential for success.

This study has several limitations. As a retrospective case-control design, it is subject to selection and historical biases. Fusion was assessed only by flexion-extension radiographs at 6 months, limiting accuracy. Spinopelvic parameters were not fully analyzed, restricting insight into sagittal alignment. Additionally, the detailed mismatches between groups like the number of operation levels require further study. Considering it, a prospective randomized trial is needed for stronger evidence.

This study highlights the advantages of a modified TLIF technique with contralateral facet fusion. The TLIFcFF demonstrated improved early fusion rates and effectively reduced back pain and radicular pain within the first postoperative year compared to PLIF. TLIFcFF, which can be performed without causing additional muscle injury, demonstrates clinical value by enhancing the stability of the lumbar fusion segment and improving clinical outcomes within one year.

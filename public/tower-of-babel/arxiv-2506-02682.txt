Cosmetic surgeries on knots in homology spheres and the Casson–Walker invariant Thanks: This work was supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Numbers JP22K03301 and JP22K03324.
Kazuhiro Ichihara, In Dae Jong

Abstract
. We study cosmetic surgeries on a knot in a homology sphere. Several constraints on knots and surgery slopes to admit such surgeries are given. Our main ingredient is the rational surgery formula of the Casson–Walker invariant for 2-component links in the 3-sphere.

1.
Introduction

One of current active research problem in Dehn surgery theory is the cosmetic surgery problem, which asks under what circumstances two distinct Dehn surgeries on the same knot yield homeomorphic 3-manifolds.
A pair of such surgeries is referred to as
cosmetic surgeries
.
More specifically, they are called
purely cosmetic
if the resulting manifolds are homeomorphic via an orientation-preserving map, and
chirally cosmetic
if the homeomorphism reverses orientation.
It is conjectured that no nontrivial knot in a closed, oriented 3-manifold admits purely cosmetic surgeries along inequivalent slopes.
This is known as the
purely cosmetic surgery conjecture
.
For further details, see
[
2
]
and
[
10
, Problem 1.81A]
.

In this paper, we study cosmetic surgeries on knots in homology spheres.
We first focus on the purely cosmetic case.
For a knot in an integral homology sphere, the following result holds as a corollary of a theorem by Boyer and Lines
[
3
, Theorem 2.8]
.

Proposition 1
.

For any knot in an integral homology sphere, there are at most two pair of integral purely cosmetic surgeries.

Here Dehn surgery is said to be
integral
if the algebraic intersection number of the meridian and a representative of the surgery slope is
\pm 1
.
The proof of Proposition
1
will be given later.
The following result extends Proposition
1
to knots in rational homology spheres obtained by Dehn surgery on knots in the 3-sphere
S^{3}
.

Theorem 2
.

Let
K
be a null-homologous knot in a rational homology sphere obtained by Dehn surgery on a knot in
S^{3}
.
Then
K
admits at most two pairs of integral purely cosmetic surgeries.

Remark 1
.

Recent studies on cosmetic surgeries on knots in
S^{3}
have made extensive use of Heegaard Floer homology.
As a recent culmination of this approach, it has been shown that there is at most one pair of purely cosmetic surgeries on any nontrivial knot in
S^{3}
(directly as a corollary of
[
5
, Corollary 1.4]
).
Moreover, for several classes of knots in
S^{3}
, the purely cosmetic surgery conjecture has been confirmed affirmatively.
See
[
5
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
15
,
16
]
for examples.

Remark 2
.

For a hyperbolic knot in an arbitrary closed 3-manifold, it has been shown in
[
6
, Theorem 1.13]
that there exists a finite set of pairs of slopes such that Dehn surgeries along any pair not in this set are never purely cosmetic.

For a knot in an integral homology sphere, Boyer and Lines also provide a sufficient condition under which no purely cosmetic surgeries can occur.

Proposition 3

(
[
3
, (5.1) Proposition (iii)]
)
.

Let
K
be a knot in an integral homology sphere.
If
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{K}(1)\neq 0
,
then
K
admits no purely cosmetic surgeries.

Here
\Delta_{K}(t)
denotes the Alexander polynomial of
K
, normalized so that
\Delta_{K}(t^{-1})=\Delta_{K}(t)
and
\Delta_{K}(1)=1
.
It is known that for a knot
K
in
S^{3}
,
\Delta^{\prime\prime}_{K}(1)
is equal to twice the second coefficient
a_{2}(K)
of the Conway polynomial of
K
.
Thus, the following can be regarded as a partial extension of Proposition
3
to knots in integral homology spheres obtained by Dehn surgery on knots in
S^{3}
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
\Sigma
be an integral homology sphere obtained by Dehn surgery on a knot
K_{0}
in
S^{3}
along the slope
1/{q_{0}}
for a non-zero integer
q_{0}
.
Let
K
be a knot in
\Sigma
.
Suppose that, identifying
K
with a knot in
S^{3}
before surgery on
K_{0}
, the linking number of
K
and
K_{0}
in
S^{3}
is zero.
If
a_{2}(K)-q_{0}a_{3}(L)\neq 0
, then
K
admits no purely cosmetic surgeries.

Here
a_{3}(L)
denotes the third coefficient of the Conway polynomial of the link
L=K\cup K_{0}
in
S^{3}
.

Next, we focus on the chirally cosmetic case.
We denote by

See
[

Then

The proof of Proposition

Theorem 6

Let

Corollary 7

Let

Corollary 8

Let

be a knot in

For any integer

Consequently, it is shown that

∎

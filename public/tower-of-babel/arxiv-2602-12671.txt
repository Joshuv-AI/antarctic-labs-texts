Structures and comodules of Hom-post Lie coalgebras
Houndedji Damien

Abstract
In this paper, we introduce the notions of Hom-tridendriform coalgebras and Hom-post-Lie coalgebras as the dual notions of Hom-tridendriform algebras and Hom-post-LIe algebras respectively. We give some properties realted to them. Then, we study the relationships between them and their connection with post-Hom-Poisson coalgebras. Next, using the Yau’ stwisting in the modules case, we give some constructions of comodules over post-Hom-Lie coalgebras by twisting either the comodule structures or post-Hom-Lie coalgebra structures. Keywords. Post-Hom-Lie coalgebras, Hom-tridendriform coalgebras, comodules. MSC2020. 16T15, 16W10, 17B61.

2
Preliminaries

In this section, we recall definition and basic properties of Hom-coassociative coalgebras, Hom-Lie coalgebras and Hom-preLie coalgebras.

Definition 2.1
.

A Hom-coassociative coalgebra is a triple
(A,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
A
, a
\mathbb{K}
-bilinear map

\Delta_{\cdot}:A\longrightarrow A\otimes A
and a linear map
\alpha:A\longrightarrow A
satisfying

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\cdot}=(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}\quad\mbox{(coassociativity)},.

(2.1)

If in addition
\alpha
satisfies

\displaystyle\Delta_{\cdot}\circ\alpha=(\alpha\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}\quad\mbox{(comultiplicativity)},

(2.2)

then
(A,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
is said to be comultiplicative.

When
\alpha=Id_{A}
,
(A,\Delta,Id_{A})
, simply denoted
(A,\Delta)
, is a coassociative coalgebra.

The lemma below allows to get a Hom-coassociative coalgebra from an coassociative coalgebra and an coalgebra endomorphism.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(A,\Delta)
be a coassociative coalgebra and
\alpha:A\longrightarrow A
be a coalgebra endomorphism.
Then, the triple
(A,\Delta_{\alpha},\alpha)
, where
\Delta_{\alpha}=\Delta\circ\alpha
, is a comultiplicative Hom-coassociative coalgebra.

Definition 2.3
.

A Hom-coassociative Rota-Baxter coalgebra of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
is a Hom-coassociative
coalgebra
(A,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
together with a linear map
R:A\rightarrow A
that satisfies the identities

\displaystyle R\circ\alpha

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha\circ R,

(2.3)

\displaystyle(R\otimes R)\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((R\otimes I)\circ\Delta+(I\otimes R)\circ\Delta+\lambda\Delta\Big)\circ R.

(2.4)

Definition 2.4
.

A Hom-Lie coalgebra is a triple
(L,\gamma,\alpha)
consisting of a vector space
L
, a linear map

\gamma:L\longrightarrow L\otimes L
and a linear map
\alpha:L\longrightarrow L
satisfying

\displaystyle\gamma=-\tau\circ\gamma\quad\mbox{(skew-cocommutativity)},

(2.5)

\displaystyle\gamma\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\otimes^{2}}\circ\gamma\quad\mbox{(comultiplicativity)},

(2.6)

\displaystyle(1+\xi+\xi^{2})\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)\circ\gamma=0\quad\mbox{(Hom-co-Jacobi identity)},

(2.7)

where
\tau(x\otimes y)=y\otimes x
,
\xi(x\otimes y\otimes z)=y\otimes z\otimes x
and
\xi^{2}(x\otimes y\otimes z)=z\otimes x\otimes y
, for all
x,y,z\in L
.

When
\alpha=Id_{L}
, we obtain the definition of Lie coalgebras.

The following result is the co-Lie-version of Lemma
2.2
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
(L,\gamma)
be a Lie coalgebra and
\alpha
be a Lie coalgebra endomorphism. Then,
(L,\gamma_{\alpha},\alpha)

is a Hom-Lie coalgebra, where
\gamma_{\alpha}=\gamma\circ\alpha
.

The following Lemma connects Hom-coassociative coalgebras to Hom-Lie coalgebras i.e. to any Hom-coassociative
coalgebra
A
one may associate a Hom-Lie coalgebra.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(A,\Delta,\alpha)
be a Hom-coassociative coalgebra. Then,
(A,\gamma,\alpha)
is a Hom-Lie coalgebra, where

\gamma=(1-\tau)\circ\Delta
.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
(A,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
be a Hom-coalgebra structure on
V
defined by the comultiplication
\Delta_{\cdot}
and the coalgebra Homomorphism
\alpha
. Then,
A
is said to
be Hom-admissible coalgebra over
V
if the bracket defined by

\displaystyle\gamma=(1-\tau)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}

(2.8)

satisfies the Hom-co-Jacobi identity
(1+\xi+\xi^{2})\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)\circ\gamma=0
.

Remark 2.8
.

Since the commutator bracket (
2.8
) is always skew-cocommutative, it makes any Hom-Lie admissible coalgebra into a Hom-Lie coalgebra.

Definition 2.9
.

A Hom-preLie coalgebra is a triple
(A,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
in which
\Delta_{\cdot}:A\rightarrow A\otimes A
is a linear map,
\alpha:A\rightarrow A

is a linear map and

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}-(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\cdot}=(\tau\otimes I)\circ\Big((\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}-(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\cdot}\Big).

(2.9)

Proposition 2.10
.

[
21
]

Let
(C,\Delta_{C},\alpha_{C})
be a Hom-coassociative coalgebra and

\gamma_{C}:C\longrightarrow C\otimes C
be a linear map defined by

\gamma_{C}(x)=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}-x_{2}\otimes x_{1}\quad\mbox{with}\quad\Delta_{C}(x)=x_{1}\otimes x_{2}.

Then
(L(C),\gamma_{C},\alpha_{C})
is a Hom-Lie coalgebra, where
L(C)=C
as vector space.

3
Hom-tridendriform coalgebras and comodules

We introduce Hom-tridendriform coalgebras and their comodules, study their twisting and give their relationship with
Hom-coassociative (Rota-Baxter) coalgebras and Hom-preLie coalgebras.

3.1
Hom-tridendriform coalgebras

Definition 3.1
.

A Hom-tridendriform coalgebra is a vector space
C
together with three linear maps
\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1}:C\rightarrow C
and
a linear map
\alpha:A\rightarrow A
satisfing the following relations :

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1}+\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1}+\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0})\circ\Delta_{-1},

(3.1)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{1},

(3.2)

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha+\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha+\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1},

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{0},

(3.4)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0})\circ\Delta_{1},

(3.5)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{0},

(3.6)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0})\circ\Delta_{0}.

(3.7)

Example 3.2
.

(T,\Delta_{\dashv},\Delta_{\vdash},\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
is a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra if and only if

T^{op}=(T,\Delta_{\dashv}^{op},\Delta_{\vdash}^{op},\Delta_{\cdot}^{op},\alpha)
is, where

\Delta_{\dashv}^{op}:=\tau\circ\Delta_{\vdash},\quad\Delta_{\vdash}^{op}:=\tau\circ\Delta_{\dashv}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\cdot}^{op}:=\tau\circ\Delta_{\cdot}.

Remark 3.3
.

Hom-tridendriform coalgebras contain Hom-coassociative coalgebras
[
9
]
(
\Delta_{-1}=\Delta_{1}=0
) and Hom-dendriform coalgebras
(
\Delta_{0}=0
) as special cases.

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
(T,\dashv,\vdash,\cdot,\alpha)
be a finite dimensional Hom-tridendriform algebra. Then, its dual space
T^{*}
is provided with a
Hom-tridendriform coalgebra

(T^{*},\dashv^{*},\vdash^{*},\cdot^{*},\alpha^{*})
, where
\dashv^{*},\vdash^{*},\cdot^{*},\alpha^{*}
are the transpose maps.

Definition 3.5
.

A morphism
f:(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)\rightarrow(C^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime}_{-1},\Delta^{\prime}_{0},\Delta^{\prime}_{1},\alpha^{\prime})

of Hom-tridendriform coalgebras is a linear map of the underline vector spaces such that

f\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ f,\quad\Delta_{-1}^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes f)\circ\Delta_{-1},\quad\Delta_{0}^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes f)\circ\Delta_{0},\quad\Delta_{1}^{\prime}\circ f=(f\otimes f)\circ\Delta_{1}.

Definition 3.6
.

A Hom-tridendriform coalgebra
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
in which
\alpha
is an endomorphism, with respect to
all the products, is said to be multiplicative.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
be a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra and
\beta:C\rightarrow C
be an endomorphism.
Then,

(C,\Delta^{\beta}_{-1}=\Delta_{-1}\circ\beta,\Delta^{\beta}_{0}=\Delta_{0}\circ\beta,\Delta^{\beta}_{1}=\Delta_{1}\circ\beta,\beta\circ\alpha)

is a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra.

Proof.

We only prove (
3.2
). We have

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\beta}_{1}\otimes\beta\circ\alpha)\circ\Delta^{\beta}_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\circ\beta\otimes\beta\circ\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}\circ\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\otimes 3}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ(\beta\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta^{\otimes 3}\circ(\Delta_{1}\circ\beta\otimes\alpha\circ\beta)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{2})^{\otimes 3}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{2})^{\otimes 3}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{1}\quad\mbox{(by (\ref{c2})})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta^{2}\circ\alpha\otimes((\beta^{2}\otimes\beta^{2})\circ\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\circ\beta\otimes\Delta_{-1}\circ\beta^{2})\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1}\circ\beta)\circ(\beta\otimes\beta)\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1}\circ\beta)\circ\Delta_{1}\circ\beta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\Delta^{\beta}_{-1})\circ\Delta^{\beta}_{1}.

The other axioms are proved similarly.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

If
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
is a multiplicative Hom-tridendriform coalgebra, then so is

(C,\Delta^{n}_{-1}=\Delta_{-1}\circ\alpha^{n},\Delta^{n}_{0}=\Delta_{0}\circ\alpha^{n},\Delta^{n}_{1}=\Delta_{1}\circ\alpha^{n},\alpha^{n+1}).

Corollary 3.9
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
be a multiplicative Hom-tridendriform coalgebra such that
\alpha
be inversible.
Then

(C,\Delta_{-1}\circ\alpha^{-1},\Delta_{0}\circ\alpha^{-1},\Delta_{1}\circ\alpha^{-1})

is a tridendriform coalgebra.

The below result associates a Hom-coassociative coalgebra to a given Hom-tridendriform coalgebra.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
be a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra. Define the comultiplication

\Delta:C\rightarrow C\otimes C
as

\Delta=\Delta_{-1}+\Delta_{0}+\Delta_{1}.

Then
(C,\Delta,\alpha)
is a Hom-coassociative coalgebra.

Proof.

By extending
c_{\alpha}(\Delta):=(\Delta\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta-(\alpha\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta
by means of
\Delta_{i},i=-1,0,1

and using axioms in Definition
3.1
, it follows that
(C,\Delta,\alpha)
is a Hom-coassociative coalgebra.
∎

The following theorem asserts that any Hom-coassociative Rota-Baxter coalgebra give rise to Hom-tridendriform coalgebra.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{0},\alpha,R)
be a Hom-coassociative Rota Baxter coalgebra of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
.
Let us set

\Delta_{-1}=(I\otimes R)\circ\Delta_{0},\quad\Delta_{1}=(R\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{0},\quad\bar{\Delta}_{0}=\lambda\Delta_{0}.

Then,
(C,\Delta_{-1},\bar{\Delta}_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
is a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra.

Proof.

Here also, we only prove (
3.2
). We have

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((R\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha)\circ((I\otimes R)\circ\Delta_{0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((R\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha\circ R)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((R\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{0}\otimes R\circ\alpha)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((R\otimes I\otimes R)\circ(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((R\otimes I\otimes R)\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0})\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(R\circ\alpha\otimes(I\otimes R)\circ\Delta_{0})\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\circ R\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ(R\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{1}.

Similarly we prove the other relations.
∎

Corollary 3.12
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{0},\alpha,R)
be a Hom-coassociative Rota Baxter coalgebra of weight
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
. Then,

(C,\bar{\Delta}_{-1},\bar{\Delta}_{1},\alpha)
is a Hom-dendriform coalgebra, where

\bar{\Delta}_{-1}=\Delta_{-1}+\lambda\Delta_{0}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\bar{\Delta}_{1}=\Delta_{1}.

Now let
\mathcal{S}_{3}
be the symmetric group of order
3
. Given
\sigma\in\mathcal{S}_{3}
, we define a linear map

\Phi_{\sigma}:C^{\otimes 3}\rightarrow C^{\otimes 3}

by

\Phi_{\sigma}(x_{1}\otimes x_{2}\otimes x_{3})=x_{\sigma^{-1}(1)}\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(2)}\otimes x_{\sigma^{-1}(3)}.

The following serie of propositions give the connections of Hom-dendriform and Hom-coassociative Rota-Baxter coalgebras to
Hom-preLie coalgebras.

Proposition 3.13
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
be a Hom-dendriform coalgebra. Define the linear map

\Delta=\Delta_{1}-\tau\circ\Delta_{-1}:C\rightarrow C\otimes C.

Then,
(C,\Delta,\alpha)
is a Hom-preLie coalgebra.

Proof.

On the one hand we have

\displaystyle(\Delta\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle((\Delta_{1}-\tau\circ\Delta_{-1})\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}-(\alpha\otimes(\Delta_{1}-\tau\circ\Delta_{-1}))\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}-(\tau\circ\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}-\Phi_{(231)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{(321)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{-1}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta)\circ\Delta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes(\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau))\circ\Delta_{1}-\Phi_{(312)}\circ((\Delta_{1}-\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau)\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes(\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{1}-(\alpha\otimes(\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau))\circ\Delta_{1}-\Phi_{(312)}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{(312)}\circ(\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}+(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}-(\alpha\otimes(\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau))\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle-\Phi_{(312)}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}+\Phi_{(321)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1})\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{(321)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{-1}.\quad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{c3})\;\mbox{and}\;(\ref{c1}))

Thus, using
3.2
, one has

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{-1}\circ\tau\otimes)\circ\Delta_{1}-\Phi_{(321)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{-1}-(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}

\displaystyle+\Phi_{(213)}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{1}+\Phi_{(312)}\circ(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{-1}

\displaystyle-\Phi_{(321)}\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1})\circ\Delta_{-1}.

It follows that

\displaystyle c_{\alpha}(\Delta)-\Phi_{(213)}\circ c_{\alpha}(\Delta)=0,

by axioms in Definition
3.1
, where
c_{\alpha}(\Delta)=(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\cdot}-(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}.

∎

3.2
Hom-tridendriform comodules

Definition 3.16
.

A Hom-tridendriform comodule ove a tridendriform coalgebra
(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
is a Hom-module
(M,\alpha_{M})

equipped with three linear maps

\Delta_{-1,M},\Delta_{0,M},\Delta_{1,M}:C\rightarrow C\otimes M
satisfing the following relations :

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1,M}+\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0,M}+\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1,M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M},

(3.8)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1,M})\circ\Delta_{1,M},

(3.9)

\displaystyle\qquad(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1,M})\circ\Delta_{1,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha_{M}+\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha_{M}+\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{1,M},

(3.10)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{-1}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{0,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{1,M})\circ\Delta_{0,M},

(3.11)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{1}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{0,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0,M})\circ\Delta_{1,M},

(3.12)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{-1,M})\circ\Delta_{0,M},

(3.13)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{0,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{0,M})\circ\Delta_{0,M}.

(3.14)

Example 3.17
.

Taking
M=C
as vector space,
\Delta_{-1,M}=\Delta_{-1}
,
\Delta_{0,M}=\Delta_{0}
,
\Delta_{0,M}=\Delta_{0}
and

\alpha_{M}=\alpha
, we see that a Hom-tridendriform coalgebra is a comodule over itself.

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
(C,\Delta_{-1,M},\Delta_{0,M},\Delta_{1,M},\alpha_{M})
be a comodule over the Hom-tridendriform coalgebra

(C,\Delta_{-1},\Delta_{0},\Delta_{1},\alpha)
.
Define the linear maps

\Delta_{i,M}^{n,0}=(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{i,M}:M\rightarrow C\otimes M,\quad i=-1;0;1.

Then,
(M,\Delta_{-1,M}^{n,0},\Delta_{0,M}^{n,0},\Delta_{1,M}^{n,0},\alpha_{M})
is a
C
-comodule.

Proof.

We only prove (
3.13
). We have

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}^{n,0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{0}\circ\alpha^{n}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{-1,M}

\displaystyle=

Definition 4.1

\displaystyle\gamma=-\tau\circ\gamma;\ \ \gamma\circ\alpha=\alpha^{\otimes^{2}}\circ\gamma\hskip 199.16928pt

\displaystyle(1+\xi+\xi^{2})\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)\circ\gamma=0,\hskip 170.71652pt

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\gamma)\circ\Delta-(\Delta\otimes\alpha)\circ\gamma-(\tau\otimes I)\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta)\circ\gamma=0,\hskip 113.81102pt

(P,\Delta_{\ast},\Delta_{A},\alpha)
is a cocommutative Hom-Poisson coalgebra.
∎

5
Post-Hom-Lie comodules

This last section is devoted to the introduction of comodules over post-Hom-Lie coalgebras and the given of various constructions by twisting.

Definition 5.1
.

A Hom-module is a pair
(M,\alpha_{M})
in which
M
is a vector space and
\alpha_{M}:M\longrightarrow M
is a linear map.

Definition 5.2
.

Let
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
be a post-Hom-Lie coalgebra. A comodule over
L
is a Hom-module
(M,\alpha_{M})
equipped with two
linear maps
\Delta_{\diamond}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M
and
\Delta_{\bullet}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M
, called structure maps, such that :

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha_{M}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\bullet}\circ\alpha_{M}=(\alpha_{M}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\bullet},

(5.1)

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond},

(5.2)

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\bullet})\circ\Delta_{\bullet},,

(5.3)

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-(\tau\otimes Id_{M}))\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\bullet})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}+((\tau\otimes Id_{M})-1)\circ(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}.

(5.4)

Example 5.3
.

Any post-Hom-Lie coalgebra is a comodule over itself.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
(M_{i},\Delta_{\diamond i},\Delta_{\bullet i},\alpha_{M_{i}})
(
i=1,2
) be two comodules over the post-Hom-Lie coalgebra
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
.
Then, the direct sum
M=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is a comodule over
L
for the structure maps

\Delta_{\diamond}=\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\oplus\Delta_{\diamond_{2}},\quad\Delta_{\bullet}=\Delta_{\bullet_{1}}\oplus\Delta_{\bullet_{2}},\quad\alpha_{M}=\alpha_{M_{1}}\oplus\alpha_{M_{2}}.

Proof.

It is straightforward by calculation.
∎

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
be a comultiplicative post-Hom-Lie coalgebra. Define two new operations
\Delta_{\circ},\Delta_{\ast}:L\rightarrow L\otimes L

by

\Delta_{\circ}:=(\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ\gamma\quad\mbox{and}\quad\Delta_{\ast}:=(\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}.

Then,
(L,\Delta_{\circ},\Delta_{\ast},\alpha)
is a comodule over the post-Hom-Lie coalgebra
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
.

Proof.

We have:

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha)\circ\Delta_{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha^{k}\otimes\alpha)\circ\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I\otimes\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\gamma)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}-(\tau\otimes I)\circ(I\otimes\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\Delta)\circ\gamma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(I\otimes\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha\otimes\gamma)\circ\Delta_{\ast}-(\tau\otimes I)\circ(I\otimes\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\circ}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\circ})\circ\Delta_{\ast}-(\tau\otimes I)\circ(I\otimes\alpha^{k}\otimes I)\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\cdot})\circ\Delta_{\circ}.

The other axioms are proved analogously.
∎

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
(M_{i},\Delta_{\diamond_{i}},\Delta_{\bullet_{i}},\alpha_{M_{i}})
(
i=1,2
) be two comodules over the multiplicative post-Hom-Lie coalgebra

(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
.
The linear maps
\Delta_{\diamond},\Delta_{\bullet}:M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\rightarrow L\otimes M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
and the linear map

\alpha_{M}:M_{1}\otimes M_{2}\rightarrow M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
defined by

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}}+\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}}+\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{2}}.

give to
M_{1}\otimes M_{2}
an
L
-comodule structure.

Proof.

We first have:

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big((\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}}+\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}\Big)\circ\alpha_{M}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{ma1})}}{{=}}

\displaystyle(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M_{2}}+\alpha^{2}_{M_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\otimes\Delta_{\diamond}.

Then,

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\alpha\otimes[(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}}+\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}]\Big)\circ\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes(\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}})\Big)\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M_{2}}

\displaystyle+(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{2}}\quad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{ma1}))

\displaystyle+(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{1}}\otimes(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}\quad(\mbox{by}\;(\ref{ma1}))

\displaystyle+\alpha^{2}_{M_{1}}\otimes\Big(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes((\alpha^{k}\otimes Id_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}})\Big)\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{2}}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\qquad(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}-(\tau\otimes I_{M})(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}=

\displaystyle=\Big(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes(\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\circ(\alpha^{k}\otimes I_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{1}})\Big)\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M_{2}}-\Big((\tau\otimes I_{M})\circ(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes(\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\circ(\alpha^{k}\otimes I_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{1}})\Big)\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M_{2}}

\displaystyle\;\;+\alpha^{2}_{M_{1}}\otimes\Big(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes((\alpha^{k}\otimes I_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{2}})\Big)-\alpha_{M_{1}}^{2}\otimes\Big((\tau\otimes I_{M_{2}})\circ(\alpha^{k+1}\otimes((\alpha^{k}\otimes I_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}\circ\Delta_{\bullet_{1}})\Big)

\displaystyle=((\Delta\circ\alpha^{k})\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{1}}\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M_{2}}+\alpha^{2}_{M_{1}}\otimes((\Delta\circ\alpha^{k})\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond_{2}}

\displaystyle=(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes_{\diamond}\otimes\alpha_{M_{1}}\otimes\alpha_{M_{2}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}=(\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}.

The others relations are proved in a similar way.
∎

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
be a comultiplicative post-Hom-Lie coalgebra. Then,
(L\otimes L,\alpha\otimes\alpha)
is an
L
-comodule
with the actions

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes I)\circ\gamma\otimes\alpha+(\tau\otimes I)\circ\Big(\alpha\otimes(\alpha\otimes I)\circ\gamma\Big),

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}\otimes\alpha+(\tau\otimes I)\circ\Big(\alpha\otimes(\alpha\otimes I)\circ\Delta_{\cdot}\Big).

Proof.

It comes from Proposition
5.5
and Theorem
5.6
.
∎

Theorem 5.8
.

Let
(M,\Delta_{\diamond},\Delta_{\bullet},\alpha_{M})
be a comodule over the post-Hom-Lie coalgebra
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
. For any non-negative integer
n
,
define

\displaystyle\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M,

(5.5)

\displaystyle\Delta^{n,0}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M

(5.6)

Then,
(M,\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond},\Delta^{n,0}_{\bullet},\alpha_{M})
is an
L
-comodule.

Proof.

For any positive integer
n
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}=(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}=(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}.

Next

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma\circ\alpha^{n}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\gamma\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes\alpha^{n})\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}\otimes\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}.

Then using (
5.2
) and (
5.5
) we obtain

\displaystyle(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n}\otimes\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ(1-(\tau\otimes Id_{M}))\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\alpha^{n+1}\otimes(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\Big)\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle-\Big(\alpha^{n+1}\otimes[(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}]\Big)\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{n+1}\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})(\alpha^{n+1}\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})(\alpha\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ(\alpha^{n}\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})(\alpha\otimes\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta^{n,0}_{\diamond}.

The others identities are similarly proved.
∎

Theorem 5.9
.

Let
(M,\Delta_{\diamond},\Delta_{\bullet},\alpha_{M})
be a comodule over the post-Hom-Lie coalgebra
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
. For any non-negative integer
k
,
define

\displaystyle\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M,

(5.7)

\displaystyle\Delta^{0,k}_{\bullet}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bullet}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M.

(5.8)

Then,
(M,\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond},\Delta^{0,k}_{\bullet},\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}})
is a comodule over

(L,\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-1},\Delta_{\cdot}\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-1},\alpha^{2^{k}})
.

Proof.

For any positif integer
k
, we have

\displaystyle\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}=\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}=\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-2}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}=(\alpha^{2}\otimes\alpha^{2}_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-2}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\alpha^{2}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-2}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}=\dots=(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}\circ(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}).

Next

\displaystyle\qquad(\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-1}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}})\circ\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}=

\displaystyle=(\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-1}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-2}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1})\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-2}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}}=\dots=

\displaystyle=(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2(2^{k}-1)}.

Then using (
5.2
) and the first identity in (
5.1
) (
2^{k}-1
times) we get

\displaystyle\qquad(\gamma\circ\alpha^{2^{k}-1}\otimes\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}})\circ\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=\Big((\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\circ}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\Big)\circ\alpha_{M}^{2(2^{k}-1)}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-2}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond}\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-2}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=\dots=

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}^{2^{k}-1}

\displaystyle=(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha^{2^{k}}\otimes\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta^{0,k}_{\diamond}.

The proofs of the rest of the conditions are analogue.
∎

Theorem 5.10
.

Let
(M,\Delta_{\diamond},\Delta_{\bullet},\alpha_{M})
be a comodule over the post-Hom-Lie coalgebra
(L,\gamma,\Delta_{\cdot},\alpha)
.
Let
\beta:L\rightarrow L
be an endomorphism of
A
and
\beta_{M}:M\rightarrow M
be a linear map such that

\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M}=\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M}
,
\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}=(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}
and

\Delta_{\bullet}\circ\beta_{M}=(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}
.
Define

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}:=(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M,

(5.9)

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\bullet}:=(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\bullet}\circ\beta_{M}:M\rightarrow L\otimes M.

(5.10)

Then,
(M,\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond},\tilde{\Delta}_{\bullet},\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M})
is a comodule over
(L,\gamma\circ\beta,\Delta_{\cdot}\circ\beta,\beta\circ\alpha)
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}\circ(\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}\circ(\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}.

Next

\displaystyle\qquad(\gamma\circ\beta\otimes\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}

\displaystyle=(\gamma\circ\beta\otimes\alpha_{M}\circ\beta_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\circ}\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})(\beta\circ Id_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ\Delta_{\circ}\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=(\beta\otimes\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\beta\otimes\beta_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=(\beta\otimes\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\gamma\otimes\alpha_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}^{2}.

Now using (
5.2
), the first identity in (
5.1
) and (
5.9
) we obtain

\displaystyle\qquad(\gamma\circ\beta\otimes\beta_{M}\circ\alpha_{M})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}=

\displaystyle=(\beta\otimes\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Big((\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\alpha\otimes\Delta_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\Big)\circ\beta^{2}_{M}

\displaystyle=[\beta\circ\alpha\otimes(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}]\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle\quad-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ[\beta\circ\alpha\otimes(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}]\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=[\beta\circ\alpha^{2}\otimes(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}]\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta

\displaystyle\quad-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})[\beta\circ\alpha^{2}\otimes(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\alpha_{M}]\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta

\displaystyle=(\beta\circ\alpha^{2}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})(\beta\circ\alpha^{2}\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle\quad-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ(\beta\otimes Id_{M})\circ\Delta_{\diamond}\circ\beta_{M}

\displaystyle=(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}-(\tau\otimes Id_{M})\circ(\beta\circ\alpha\otimes\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond})\circ\tilde{\Delta}_{\diamond}.

The others identities are analogously proved.
∎

Near Rainbow Hamilton Cycles in Dense Graphs
Danni Peng and Zhifei Yan

Abstract
. Finding near-rainbow Hamilton cycles in properly edge-coloured graphs was first studied by Andersen, who proved in 1989 that every proper edge colouring of the complete graph on n n vertices contains a Hamilton cycle with at least n − 2 ​ n n-\sqrt{2n} distinct colours. This result was improved to n − O ⁡ ( log 2 ⁡ n ) n-O(\log^{2}n) by Balogh and Molla in 2019. In this paper, we consider Anderson’s problem for general graphs with a given minimum degree. We prove every globally n / 8 n/8 -bounded (i.e. every colour is assigned to at most n / 8 n/8 edges) properly edge-coloured graph G G with δ ⁡ ( G ) ⩾ ( 1 / 2 + ε ) ​ n \delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n contains a Hamilton cycle with n − o ⁡ ( n ) n-o(n) distinct colours. Moreover, we show that the constant 1 / 8 1/8 is best possible.



1.
Introduction

A
proper
edge colouring of a graph
G
assigns colours to
E(G)
such that every pair of incident edges receive different colours. An attractive and longstanding open question is, given an proper edge-coloured complete graph
K_{n}
, can you always find a rainbow Hamilton cycle, that is, a Hamilton cycle with each edge assigned a different colour? In 1980, Hahn
[
Hah80
]
conjectured that every properly edge-coloured
K_{n}
with
n\geqslant 5
contains a rainbow Hamilton path.
1
1

1

Observe that if we colour the edges of
K_{4}
by assigning three different colours to the three disjoint matchings of size
2
, then there is no rainbow Hamilton path.
However, Maamoun and Meyniel
[
MM84
]
found a properly edge-coloured
K_{n}
without a rainbow Hamilton path for
n=2^{k}
, which refutes Hahn’s conjecture. In 2019, Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
MPS19
]
showed that every properly edge-coloured
K_{n}
, in which at most
cn
colours are assigned to
more than
cn/2
edges,
must contain
cn/2
edge-disjoint rainbow Hamilton cycles, for any constant
0<c<1
and sufficiently large
n
. In another direction, Anderson
[
And89
]
proved that every properly edge-colored
K_{n}
contains a Hamilton cycle in which at least
n-\sqrt{2n}
distinct colours appear. Recently, Balogh and Molla
[
BM19
]
improved the number of distinct colours to
n-O(\log^{2}n)
.

More recently, the study of finding rainbow Hamilton cycles has been extended to general graphs. Coulson and Perarnau
[
CP20
]
considered this question on edge-coloured Dirac graphs (i.e. graphs on
n
vertices with minimum degree at least
n/2
). They proved that every
o(n)
-bounded edge-coloured Dirac graph contains a rainbow Hamilton cycle, where a colouring is
b
-
bounded
if every colour is assigned to at most
b
edges. Note that Coulson and Perarnau did not require the colouring to be proper; rather, they needed every colour class in the colouring to be strongly controlled, in the sense that it contains at most
o(n)
edges.

The results mentioned above illustrate two natural ways in which we can constrain an edge colouring in order to find a rainbow Hamilton cycle:
local
and
global
boundedness. Local boundedness refers to bounding the number of edges incident to each vertex that can be assigned the same colour; for example, we can ask the colouring to be proper. In contrast, global boundedness involves controlling the total number of edges of the same colour throughout the entire graph, like the property of being
o(n)
-bounded that was used by Coulson and Perarnau. It is natural to ask whether it is possible to weaken this assumption on the global boundedness of the colouring, if we consider only proper edge-colourings of a Dirac graph. Our main result offers a partial answer to this question, by considering near-rainbow Hamilton cycles in graphs
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be constant, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
. Then every proper
n/8
-bounded edge-colouring of
G
contains a Hamilton cycle with
n-o(n)
distinct colours.

Coulson and Perarnau
[
CP20
, Theorem 1.4]
showed for any constant
c>1/8
, there exists a Dirac graph on
n
vertices with a
cn
-bounded colouring that contains no rainbow Hamilton cycle. Motivated by their construction, we will show that the constant
1/8
in Theorem
1.1
is optimal, in the following sense.

Proposition 1.2
.

For each constant
c>1/8
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(c)
and
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(c)>0
such that the following holds for every
n>n_{0}
. There exists a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, and a proper
cn
-bounded edge-colouring of
G
, such that every Hamilton cycle in
G
contains at most
(1-\varepsilon)n
distinct colours.

If we completely remove the condition on the global boundedness of the colouring, then we cannot even expect to find a near-rainbow Hamilton cycle. Indeed, by Vizing’s theorem there exists a proper edge-colouring of any
d
-regular graph
G
using at most
d+1
colours, and in such a colouring every Hamilton cycle obviously contains at most
d+1
distinct colours. In this setting, one can easily deduce the following corollary from Theorem
1.1
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be constant, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
. Then every proper edge-colouring of
G
contains a Hamilton cycle with at least
n/4-o(n)
distinct colours.

The first step in the proof of
Theorem

1.1
is to construct an ‘absorber’, using a simplified version of a classic construction of Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
RRS06
]
. More precisely, we will find a path
A
of length
o(n)
such that for any set
U
of size
o(v(A))
, there exists a path with vertex set
V(A)\cup U
and the same endpoints as
A
(see Lemma
3.1
). We will also find a ‘reservoir’ set
R
of size
o(v(A))
with the property that every pair of vertices of
G
has many common neighbours in
R
(see Lemma
3.3
).

The next step is to construct a rainbow path forest
F
covering almost all of the vertices of
G
. To do so, we will adapt an approach of Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
MPS19
]
. We will then connect these paths (and
A
) using vertices from
R
, and finally use the property of
A
to absorb the leftover vertices, noting that the resulting Hamilton cycle has at least as many distinct colours as the rainbow path forest
F
. Since we have no control over the colours of the connecting edges, however, we are unable to find a rainbow Hamilton cycle using this method.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In
Section

2
, we adapt the method of
[
MPS19
]
to construct a rainbow path forest in
G
, in
Section

3
we construct our absorber and our reservoir, and in
Section

4
we deduce
Theorem

1.1
. Finally, in
Section

5
, we give a construction of a properly coloured graph with no near-rainbow Hamilton cycles.

2.
Finding a rainbow path forest

In this section we will construct a rainbow path forest
F
covering
n-o(n)
vertices of a properly coloured graph
G
; that is, a collection of vertex-disjoint paths in which each edge (across all paths) is assigned a distinct colour. To do so, we will adapt an approach of Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
MPS19
]
, who proved that every proper colouring of the complete graph
K_{n}
satisfying certain constraints contains a spanning rainbow forest. Moreover, their result holds for all ‘typical’ graphs, which roughly speaking means graphs whose codegrees can be well controlled. However, their result cannot be applied directly to an arbitrary Dirac graph, or to an arbitrary graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
. We refine the proof of
[
MPS19
, Lemma 8.17]
, showing that a near-spanning rainbow forest still exists in graphs without any constraints on the codegrees. The main aim of the section is to prove the following ‘rainbow forest lemma’ for graphs
G
satisfying
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
.

Lemma 2.1

(Rainbow forest)
.

Let
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1/2
, and let
\alpha>0
be sufficiently small. There exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,\alpha)
such that the following holds for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
, and every graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
.
In any
\big(n/8+o(n)\big)
-bounded proper edge colouring of
G
, there exists a rainbow subgraph
F\subset G
consisting of at most
n^{1-\alpha}
vertex-disjoint paths, such that every path in
F
has length at least
n^{\alpha}
and
v(F)\geqslant n-2n^{1-\alpha}
.

Roughly speaking, we will prove
Lemma

2.1
in four steps. First, by a result of Christofides, Kühn and Osthus, we can find an
r
-regular spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset G
with
r\approx\delta(G)/2
(see
Lemma

2.2
). Next we randomly partition
V(G^{\prime})
into
m=n^{\alpha}+1
equal-sized sets
V_{0},\ldots,V_{m}
and the colour set
\mathcal{C}
into
m
sets
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{m}
. We will prove that, with high probability, for every adjacent pair
(V_{i-1},V_{i})
, the subgraph
B_{i}
of
G^{\prime}[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
formed by the edges assigned colours in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
has a certain ‘near-regularity’ property. We will then apply a lemma of Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov (see Lemma
2.6
) to
B_{i}
, to find a near-perfect rainbow matching in
B_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
. Finally, we will greedily construct rainbow paths using these rainbow matchings, to form a large rainbow forest.

In order to prepare for the formal proof of
Lemma

2.1
, we will need to present the various lemmas mentioned in the outline above. First, in order to find a regular spanning subgraph of
G
we will use the following lemma, which is a special case of a result of Christofides, Kühn and Osthus
[
CKO12
, Theorem 12]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
\delta(G)>n/2
, then
G
has an
r
-regular spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset G
, where

\lceil\delta(G)/2\rceil\leqslant r\leqslant\lceil\delta(G)/2\rceil+1

is an even integer.

Proof.

This follows from part
(i)
of
[
CKO12
, Theorem 12]
, applied with
\ell=1
. ∎

Montgomery, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
MPS19
]
introduced a coloured version of Rödl’s nibble to construct near-perfect rainbow matchings in a ‘near-regular’ bipartite graph. To apply their method, we need the following definition.

Definition 2.3
.

For
\gamma,\delta>0
, a graph
H
on
n
vertices is
(\gamma,\delta,n)
-near-regular if

(1-\gamma)\delta n\leqslant d_{H}(v)\leqslant(1+\gamma)\delta n

for every
v\in V(H)
.

As mentioned above, we will randomly partition the vertex set
V(G^{\prime})
of the regular graph
G^{\prime}
into parts
V_{i}
of size
n^{1-\alpha}
. We will use the following lemma from
[
MPS19
]
to show that, with high probability, each of the induced subgraphs
G^{\prime}[V_{i},V_{i+1}]
is near-regular.

Lemma 2.4

(Lemma 5.2 of
[
MPS19
]
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be sufficiently small, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
n^{-\alpha}\leqslant\gamma,\delta,p,\mu\leqslant 1
with
p<1/2
. Let
H
be a
(\gamma,\delta,n)
-near-regular graph with a proper
\mu n
-bounded edge-colouring, and let
A,B\subset V(H)
be disjoint subsets of order
pn
, chosen uniformly at random from all such pairs of subsets. Then
H[A,B]
is a
(2\gamma,\delta,2pn)
-near-regular bipartite graph with a
\big((1+\gamma)2\mu p^{2}n\big)
-bounded colouring with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
.

We aim to find a near-perfect rainbow matching in
G^{\prime}[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
for each
i\in[m]
, ensuring moreover that each edge (across all these matchings) has a distinct colour. To achieve this, we randomly partition the colour set
\mathcal{C}
into
m
parts
\mathcal{C}_{i}
, and focus on the subgraph
B_{i}\subset G^{\prime}[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
consisting of the edges assigned a colour in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. The following lemma from
[
MPS19
]
guarantees that there exists a partition such that each
B_{i}
is near-regular.

Lemma 2.5

(Lemma 5.3 of
[
MPS19
]
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be sufficiently small, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
n^{-\alpha}\leqslant\gamma,\delta,p,\mu\leqslant 1
. Let
H
be a balanced bipartite
(\gamma,\delta,n)
-near-regular graph with a proper
\mu n
-bounded edge-colouring, and let
H_{1}\subset H
be the random subgraph formed by the edges whose colour lies in
S
, where each colour is included in
S
independently with probability
p
. Then
H_{1}
is
(2\gamma,p\delta,n)
-near-regular with probability
1-o(n^{-1})
.

We will use the following lemma from
[
MPS19
]
to find a rainbow near-perfect matching in the graph
B_{i}
for each
i\in[m]
.

Lemma 2.6

(Lemma 8.8 of
[
MPS19
]
)
.

Let
\alpha>0
be sufficiently small, let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large, and let
n^{-2\sqrt{\alpha}}\leqslant\gamma\leqslant n^{-\sqrt{\alpha}}
and
n^{-2\alpha}\leqslant\delta,q\leqslant 1
. Let
H
be a balanced bipartite
(\gamma,\delta,n)
-near-regular graph on
n
vertices with a proper
(1-q)\delta n/2
-bounded edge-colouring. Then there exists a rainbow matching
M\subset H
of size at least

e(M)\geqslant(1-q)n/2.

We will construct a rainbow path forest
F
greedily using these rainbow matchings.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
m,n,t\in\mathbb{N}
, let
G
be a graph with
(m+1)n
vertices, and let
V(G)=V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
be a partition with
|V_{0}|=\cdots=|V_{m}|=n
. Suppose that

G[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
contains a matching
M_{i}
with
e(M_{i})\geqslant n-t
for each
i\in[m]
. Then there exists
H\subset G
consisting of
n-tm
vertex-disjoint paths of length
m
.

Proof.

Let
0\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
, and suppose we have already built a path forest
H_{k}\subset G\big[V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}\big]
consisting of
n-tk
vertex-disjoint paths of length
k
. At most
t
of the paths in
H_{k}
cannot be extended by adding an edge of
M_{k+1}
, and we therefore obtain a path forest
H_{k+1}\subset G\big[V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k+1}\big]
consisting of
n-t(k+1)
vertex-disjoint paths of length
k+1
. Since this holds for every
0\leqslant k\leqslant m-1
, we obtain a path forest
H=H_{m}
as required.
∎

We are finally ready to put the pieces together and prove
Lemma

2.1
.

Proof of
Lemma

2.1
.

Fix
\alpha>0
sufficiently small and
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1/2
, and let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be sufficiently large. Suppose that we are given a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, and a
n/8
-bounded proper edge colouring of
G
. By
Lemma

2.2
, there exists an
r
-regular spanning subgraph
G^{\prime}\subset G
with
r\geqslant\delta(G)/2
. Note that
G^{\prime}
is
(\gamma,\delta,n)
-near-regular for
\gamma=n^{-\sqrt{\alpha}}
and
\delta=r/n
, and the colouring of
G^{\prime}
is proper and
n/8
-bounded, since
G^{\prime}\subset G
.

Now, set
m=\lfloor n^{\alpha}\rfloor-1
, and let
V(G^{\prime})=U\cup V_{0}\cup\cdots\cup V_{m}
be a random partition of the vertices of
G^{\prime}
with

|U|<|V_{0}|=\cdots=|V_{m}|=n^{\prime}:=\bigg\lfloor\frac{n}{m+1}\bigg\rfloor.

By
Lemma

2.4
and the union bound, with high probability the coloured graph
G[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
has the following two properties for each
i\in[m]
: it is
(2\gamma,\delta,2n^{\prime})
-near-regular, and the colouring is
(1+\gamma)n^{\prime}/4m
-bounded.

We next randomly choose a partition
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{1}\cup\cdots\cup\mathcal{C}_{m}
of the set of colours into
m
parts, by placing each colour independently and uniformly into one of the parts. For each
i\in[m]
, let
B_{i}
be the subgraph of
G^{\prime}[V_{i},V_{i+1}]
formed by the edges whose colour is placed in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
. Applying
Lemma

2.5
with
H=G^{\prime}[V_{i},V_{i+1}]
,
p=1/m
and
S=\mathcal{C}_{i}
, and taking a union bound over

, it follows that with high probability every

is
(4\gamma,\delta/m,2n^{\prime})
-near-regular. Moreover, the edge-colouring of

is proper and
(1-\gamma)\delta n^{\prime}/m
-bounded, since
B_{i}\subset G^{\prime}[V_{i},V_{i+1}]
and
\delta=r/n\geqslant 1/4+\varepsilon/2
.

Let’s fix partitions of
V(G^{\prime})
and
\mathcal{C}
such that

satisfies these conditions. By
Lemma

2.6
, applied with
q=n^{-2\alpha}
, it follows that there exists a rainbow matching
M_{i}\subset B_{i}
such that

\displaystyle e(M_{i})\geqslant(1-q)n^{\prime}.

Finally, by
Lemma

2.7
, it follows that there exists a rainbow path forest
F\subset G^{\prime}
consisting of
|V_{i}|-qmn^{\prime}
vertex-disjoint paths of length

. Thus

\displaystyle v(F)\geqslant n-n^{1-\alpha}+qmn\geqslant n-2n^{1-\alpha},

.
∎

3.
Finding an absorber and a reservoir

In this section, we will construct an absorber and a reservoir in a graph
G
with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
. We begin with the following lemma, which provides our absorber; both the statement and proof are based on
[
RRS06
, Lemma 2.3]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Absorber)
.

For
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and
0<c<2^{-10}\varepsilon
, there exists
C=C(c,\varepsilon)
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(c,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
(\log n)^{2}\leqslant m\leqslant n/C
. If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, then
there exists a path
A\subset G
with

v(A)\leqslant Cm,

such that for every set
U\subset V(G)\setminus V(A)
with size
|U|\leqslant cm
, there is a path
A_{U}\subset G
which has the same endpoints as
A
and vertex set
V(A_{U})=V(A)\cup U
.

The idea of the proof is to find a small matching
M\subset G
which nevertheless contains
many
edges of
G[N(v)]
for
every
vertex
v\in V(G)
. The absorber
A
will be a path formed by adding paths of length two between consecutive pairs of edges of
M
. The matching
M
is given by the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

For
0<\varepsilon<1/2
and
0<c<2^{-10}\varepsilon
, there exists
C=C(c,\varepsilon)
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(c,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
(\log n)^{2}\leqslant m\leqslant\varepsilon n
. If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, then there exists a matching
M\subset G
with
e(M)\leqslant Cm
such that

e\big(M\cap G[N(v)]\big)>cm

holds for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.

Proof.

First, note that for every
v\in V(G)
, the number of edges in
G[N(v)]
is at least

e(G[N(v)])\geqslant\varepsilon n^{2}/2.

Indeed, since
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, it follows that
|N(u)\cap N(v)|\geqslant 2\varepsilon n
for every
u,v\in V(G)
, and therefore
e(G[N(v)])\geqslant\delta(G)\cdot\varepsilon n\geqslant\varepsilon n^{2}/2
, as claimed.

Set
C=8c/\varepsilon
, and let
G^{*}\sim G(n,p)
be a random graph on
V(G)
with
p=Cm/n^{2}\in(0,1)
. Since
m\geqslant(\log n)^{2}
, by applying Chernoff’s inequality and taking a union bound we deduce that with high probability the following holds: the number of edges in
G^{*}
is at most
Cm
,
and for every
v\in V(G)
, the number of edges in
G[N(v)]\cap G^{*}
is at least

e\big(G[N(v)]\cap G^{*}\big)\geqslant e(G[N(v)])\cdot p\cdot 1/2\geqslant\frac{Cm}{n^{2}}\cdot\frac{\varepsilon n^{2}}{4}=2cm.

For any graph
H
, let
E^{\prime}(H)\subset E(H)
be the set of edges that are
not isolated
, meaning that they share an endpoint with some other edge. Observe that
|E^{\prime}(G)|
is at most the number of walks of length
2
in
G
, so
\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|E^{\prime}(G^{*})|]\leqslant p^{2}n^{3}\leqslant C^{2}m^{2}/n
,
and therefore

\mathbb{P}\big(|E^{\prime}(G^{*})|\geqslant cm\big)\leqslant\frac{\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[|E^{\prime}(G^{*})|]}{cm}\leqslant\frac{C^{2}m^{2}}{cmn}\leqslant\frac{2^{6}c}{\varepsilon}\leqslant 2^{-4}

by Markov’s inequality, and since
m\leqslant\varepsilon n
and
c<2^{-10}\varepsilon
.
Hence there exists a graph
G_{0}
with
V(G_{0})=V(G)
, such that
e(G_{0})\leqslant Cm
,
|E^{\prime}(G_{0})|<cm
and

e\big(G[N(v)]\cap G_{0}\big)\geqslant 2cm
for every
v\in V(G)
.

Finally, let
M
be the largest matching in
G_{0}\cap G
. Observe that
e(M)\leqslant e(G_{0})\leqslant Cm
, and that for every
v\in V(G)
, the number of edges in
G[N(v)]\cap M
is at least

e\big(G[N(v)]\cap M\big)\geqslant e\big(G[N(v)]\cap G_{0}\big)-|E^{\prime}(G_{0})|>cm,

as required.
∎

We are now ready to construct our absorber, using the matching given by
Lemma

3.2
.

Proof of
Lemma

3.1
.

Let
C_{0}=C_{0}(\varepsilon,c)>0
be the constant given by

3.2
, and set
C=2C_{0}/\varepsilon
, so
C_{0}m\leqslant\varepsilon n/2
. By

3.2
, we obtain a matching
M\subset G
with edge set

E(M)=\big\{x_{1}y_{1},x_{2}y_{2},\ldots,x_{t}y_{t}\big\}

for some
t\leqslant C_{0}m\leqslant\varepsilon n/2
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle e\big(M\cap G[N(v)]\big)>cm

for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. We now construct the path
A
by connecting consecutive edges of
M
using
t-1
additional vertices. To be precise, suppose that for some
1\leqslant k\leqslant t-1

we have already found a path
A_{k}
with

E(A_{k})=\big\{x_{1}y_{1},y_{1}z_{1},z_{1}x_{2},x_{2}y_{2},\ldots,y_{k-1}z_{k-1},z_{k-1}x_{k},x_{k}y_{k}\big\}

where

and

for

. Since

, there exists

z_{k}\in N(y_{k})\cap N(x_{k+1})\setminus\big(V(M)\cup V(A_{k})\big)

and we can therefore continue this process until we obtain a path
A_{t}
containing all of the edges of
M
. Set
A=A_{t}
, and observe that
|A|=3t+1\leqslant Cm
.

Finally, we need to show that
A
has the required ‘absorption’ property. To see this, let
U=\{u_{1},u_{2},...,u_{s}\}\subset V(G)\setminus V(A)
with
s\leqslant cm
, and construct the path
A_{U}
as follows. Let
0\leqslant\ell<s
, and assume that we have already found a path
A^{\prime}_{\ell}
with the same endpoints as
A
, containing
t-\ell
edges of
M
, and with

V(A^{\prime}_{\ell})=V(A)\cup\big\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{\ell}\big\}.

We construct a path
A^{\prime}_{\ell+1}
by replacing an edge
x_{i}y_{i}\in E(M)\cap E(A^{\prime}_{\ell})
by a path of length
2
with edge set
\{x_{i}u_{\ell+1},u_{\ell+1}y_{i}\}
. We can do so because

e\big(M\cap A^{\prime}_{\ell}\cap G[N(u_{\ell+1})]\big)\geqslant e\big(M\cap G[N(u_{\ell+1})]\big)-\ell>cm-s\geqslant 0,

by (
1
). We may therefore continue this process until we obtain a path
A^{\prime}_{s}
with the same endpoints as
A
and containing all of the vertices of
U
, as required.
∎

The second goal of this section is to prove the following ‘reservoir’ lemma, which is based on
[
RRS06
, Lemma 2.7]
.

Lemma 3.3

(Reservoir)
.

For every
0<c,\varepsilon<1/2
and
C>1
, there exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(c,\varepsilon,C)
such that the following holds for every
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
(\log n)^{2}\leqslant m\leqslant\varepsilon n/2C
. If
G
is a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, and
W\subset V(G)
is a set with
|W|\leqslant Cm
, then there exists a set
R\subset V(G)\setminus W
with
|R|=cm
such that

|N(x)\cap N(y)\cap R|\geqslant\varepsilon|R|

for every pair of vertices
x,y\in V(G)
.

Proof.

Let
R^{*}
be a random set of size
cm
, chosen uniformly from all
cm
-sets in
V(G)\setminus W
. For each
x,y\in V(G)
, let
X_{x,y}=|N(x)\cap N(y)\cap R^{*}|
, and note that

\displaystyle\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}\big[X_{x,y}\big]\geqslant\frac{|N(x)\cap N(y)|-|W|}{n-|W|}\cdot|R^{*}|\geqslant\frac{2\varepsilon n-Cm}{n-Cm}\cdot cm\geqslant\frac{3\varepsilon cm}{2},

where the final inequality holds because
Cm<\varepsilon n/2
. By Hoeffding’s inequality
[
Hoe63
]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(X_{x,y}<\varepsilon|R^{*}|\big)\leqslant\mathbb{P}\big(X_{x,y}<2\operatorname{\mathbb{E}}[X_{x,y}]/3\big)\leqslant\exp\big(-\Theta(m)\big)\leqslant n^{-3},

since
n\geqslant n_{0}
and
m\geqslant(\log n)^{2}
. By the union bound over pairs
x,y\in V(G)
, it follows that there exists a set
R
of size
cm
such that
X_{x,y}\geqslant\varepsilon|R^{*}|
for every
x,y\in V(G)
, as required.
∎

4.
Finding a near-rainbow Hamilton cycle

Using the rainbow forest given by
Lemma

2.1
, the absorber given by
Lemma

3.1
and the reservoir given by
Lemma

3.3
, we can now deduce Theorems
1.1
.
In fact, we will prove the following slight strengthening of
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Given
\beta>0
be sufficiently small and
0<\varepsilon\leqslant 1/2
, there exist
b=b(\varepsilon,\beta)
and
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,\beta)
such that
the following holds. For every
n\geqslant n_{0}
, and every globally
n/8
-bounded proper edge-coloured graph
G
on
n
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, there exists a Hamilton cycle in
G
with at least
n-bn^{1-\beta}
distinct colours.

Proof.

First let’s construct an absorber
A
and a reservoir
R
in
G
. By
Lemma

3.1
, applied with
c=2^{-11}\varepsilon
and
m=n^{1-\beta}
, there exists a constant
C=C(\varepsilon)>0
and a path
A\subset G
with

v(A)\leqslant Cn^{1-\beta},

such that for any set
U\subset V(G)\setminus V(A)
with
|U|\leqslant cn^{1-\beta}
, there is a path
A_{U}\subset G
with the same endpoints as
A
and with
V(A_{U})=V(A)\cup U
. In other words,

A
can absorb any set
U
with
|U|\leqslant cn^{1-\beta}
. Moreover, by
Lemma

3.3
applied with
W=V(A)
, there exists a set
R\subset V(G)\setminus V(A)
of size
cn^{1-\beta}/2

such that

(2)

|N(v)\cap N(u)\cap R|\geqslant\varepsilon|R|\geqslant\varepsilon cn^{1-\beta}/2

for every
x,y\in V(G)
.

Next let’s construct a near-spanning rainbow forest in
G_{0}=G-\big(V(A)\cup R\big)
. Note that

\displaystyle v(G_{0})=n-v(A)-|R|\qquad\textup{and}\qquad\delta(G_{0})\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon/2)n,

since
\beta>0
and
n
is sufficiently large. Moreover, the colouring of
G_{0}
is globally
n/8
-bounded, since
G_{0}\subset G
. Since
v(G_{0})=n-o(n)
, we may therefore apply
Lemma

2.1
to
G_{0}
with
\alpha=2\beta
, and obtain a rainbow path-forest
F\subset G_{0}
consisting of at most
n^{1-\alpha}=n^{1-2\beta}
vertex-disjoint paths and with

v(F)+v(A)+|R|\geqslant n-2n^{1-\alpha}=n-o(n^{1-\beta}).

We will now use the vertices of
R
to connect the vertices of
A
and the paths in
F
in order to construct an almost-rainbow Hamilton cycle. Let
x_{0}
and
y_{0}
be the endpoints of
A
, and let
F=P_{1}\cup\cdots\cup P_{T}
, where
P_{i}
is a path with endpoints
\{x_{i},y_{i}\}
and
T\leqslant n^{1-2\beta}
. For each
0\leqslant i\leqslant T
, the vertices
y_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
(where
x_{T+1}=x_{0}
) have at least

|N(y_{i})\cap N(x_{i+1})\cap R|\geqslant\varepsilon cn^{1-\beta}/2>4n^{1-2\beta}

common neighbors in
R
, by (
2
), and since
\beta>0
. Hence we can greedily choose vertices
z_{i}\in N(y_{i})\cap N(x_{i+1})\cap R\setminus\{z_{0},...,z_{i-1}\}
for each
0\leqslant i\leqslant T
, since
T+1\leqslant n^{1-2\beta}
. Adding the edges
\big\{y_{i}z_{i},z_{i}x_{i+1}:0\leqslant i\leqslant T\big\}
to the graph
A\cup F
, we obtain a cycle
H
with

v(H)\geqslant v(F)+v(A)\geqslant n-|R|-o(n^{1-\beta})

whose edges have at least
e(F)\geqslant n-bn^{\beta}
distinct colours, where
b=C+c
.

Finally, let
U=V(G)\setminus V(H)
, and observe that since

|U|\leqslant|R|+o(n^{1-\beta})\leqslant cn^{1-\beta}
, there exists a path
A_{U}\subset G
with the same endpoints as
A
and with
V(A_{U})=V(A)\cup U
. Replacing the path
A
by
A_{U}
in
H
, we obtain a near-rainbow Hamilton cycle, as required.
∎

It follows easily from
Theorem

1.1
that, even without any global boundedness condition, every proper edge-colouring of
G
contains a Hamilton cycle with
n/4-o(n)
distinct colours.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

Note first that any proper edge-colouring
c
of a graph
G
on
n
vertices is
n/2
-bounded. We can therefore construct a proper
n/8
-bounded edge-colouring of
G
from
c
by replacing each colour in
c
by four colours. Applying
Theorem

1.1
to this new colouring
c^{\prime}
, we obtain a Hamilton cycle in
G
with
n-o(n)
colours in
c^{\prime}
, and therefore with at least
n/4-o(n)
distinct colours in
c
, as required.
∎

5.
Globally-bounded colourings without near-rainbow Hamilton cycles

In this section we will show that the constant 1/8 in
Theorem

1.1
is best possible. This is an immediate consequence of the following more general theorem.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
n,m,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
satisfy
m,t\leqslant 2^{-20}n
,
s\leqslant n/8
, and

(3)

\displaystyle 2^{-10}s\geqslant\max\big\{\sqrt{mn},\,\sqrt{tn},\,(n\log n)^{2/3}\big\}.

Then there exists a coloured graph
G
on
n
vertices such that

(i)

\delta(G)\geqslant n/2+m;

(ii)

the colouring of
G
is proper and
(n/8+s)
-bounded;

(iii)

every Hamilton cycle in
G
has at most
n-t
distinct colours.

Before proving
Theorem

5.1
, let us quickly note that it implies
Proposition

1.2
.

Proof of
Proposition

1.2
.

Let
c^{\prime}=\min\{c,1/4\}
. Observe that if

\displaystyle\varepsilon=(c^{\prime}-1/8)^{2}\cdot 2^{-20},\qquad s=(c^{\prime}-1/8)n,\qquad m=t=\varepsilon n,

then (
3
) holds, since
1/8<c^{\prime}\leqslant 1/4
and
n>n_{0}(c)
is sufficiently large. By
Theorem

5.1
, it follows that there exists a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant(1/2+\varepsilon)n
, and a proper and globally
c^{\prime}n
-bounded colouring of
G
such that every Hamilton cycle in
G
has at most
(1-\varepsilon)n
distinct colours, as required.
∎

In the proof of
Theorem

5.1
, we will need the following simple lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
n,d,k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, with
\ell\leqslant n/2
and
d\leqslant 2^{-5}n
. If

(4)

\frac{2k\ell}{n}>d+2^{5}d^{2}/n+4\sqrt{d}\log n,

then there exists a graph
G
with
n
vertices and
\delta(G)\geqslant d
, and a proper edge-colouring of
G
using at most
k
colours with each colour being used on at most
\ell
edges.

Proof.

Let
M_{1},...,M_{k}
be random matchings of size
\ell
, chosen uniformly and independently at random in
K_{n}
, let
H
be the multigraph formed by taking the union of these matchings, and let
G
be the graph formed by choosing one edge for each multiple edge of
H
. Define a colouring of
G
by giving colour
i
to each edge of
M_{i}
. By construction, this is a proper colouring using at most
k
colours, and each colour is used on at most
\ell
edges.

We claim that
\delta(G)\geqslant d
with positive probability. To see this, observe first that

d_{H}(v)\sim\textup{Bin}(k,2\ell/n)

for each
v\in V(H)
, and that therefore

\operatorname{\mathbb{P}}\big(d_{H}(v)<d+h\big)\leqslant e^{-h^{2}/4d}\leqslant\frac{1}{n^{2}},

where
h=2^{4}d^{2}/n+2\sqrt{d}\log n
, by Chernoff’s inequality and (
4
).

Now, let
A_{v}
denote the event
\{d_{H}(v)\geqslant d+h\}
, and partition
A_{v}
according to the first
d+h
matchings (in the order
(M_{1},...,M_{k})
) that use an edge incident to
v
. Let
B_{v}
be the ‘bad’ event
\{d_{G}(v)<d\}
, and observe that if
A_{v}\cap B_{v}
occurs, then there exists a set of
h
of these
d+h
matchings that contains an edge incident to
v
that was already used by one of the earlier matchings. Note also that, conditioned on the event that a matching uses an edge incident to
v
, it uses one of the (at most
d
) previously-chosen edges with probability at most
d/(n-1)
. Taking a union bound over the choice of
d+h
matchings and the choice of
h
matchings that repeat an edge, it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\big(A_{v}\cap B_{v}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant\binom{d+h}{h}\cdot\bigg(\frac{d}{n-1}\bigg)^{h}\leqslant\bigg(\frac{ed(d+h)}{h(n-1)}\bigg)^{h}\leqslant e^{-h}\leqslant\frac{1}{n^{2}},

since
h=2^{4}d^{2}/n+2\sqrt{d}\log n
.
Finally by taking a union bound over
v\in V(G)
, it follows that
\delta(G)\geqslant d
with positive probability, as claimed.
∎

We are now ready to prove
Theorem

5.1
.

Proof of
Theorem

5.1
.

Given
n,m,s,t\in\mathbb{N}
with
m,t\leqslant 2^{-20}n
and satisfying (
3
), set
q=2^{-10}s
and consider the following construction. First, let
H
be graph given by
Lemma

5.2
, applied with

v(H)=n/2+q,\quad d=q+m,\quad\ell=n/8+s\quad\text{and}\quad k=2q-t.

To see that the conditions of the lemma are satisfied, note first that
2^{10}(m+t)\leqslant s\leqslant n/8
, by (
3
), and therefore
\ell\leqslant v(H)/2
and
d\leqslant 2^{-5}v(H)
, and moreover

\frac{2k\ell}{v(H)}=\frac{2(2q-t)(n/8+s)}{n/2+q}>q+\frac{8qs}{n}-t-\frac{4q^{2}}{n}.

Since
qs\geqslant(m+t)n
and
q^{1/2}s\geqslant 4n\log n
, by (
3
), it follows that

\frac{2k\ell}{v(H)}>q+m+\frac{2^{8}q^{2}}{n}+8\sqrt{q}\log n>d+\frac{2^{6}d^{2}}{n}+4\sqrt{d}\log n,

as required. Thus, by
Lemma

5.2
, there exists a properly edge-coloured graph
H
with
n/2+q
vertices and minimum degree
\delta(H)\geqslant q+m
, using at most
2q-t
colours, and with each colour being used on at most
n/8+s
edges.

Now, let
G
be the graph on
n
vertices obtained from
H
by adding
n/2-q
vertices, each with neighbourhood
V(H)
, and giving each of these new edges a (new) distinct colour. Note that
\delta(G)\geqslant n/2+m
, since
q\geqslant m
, and that the colouring of
G
is proper and
(n/8+s)
-bounded, since
H
has this property and each new edge is given a different colour. Finally, note that each Hamilton cycle in
G
uses at least
2q
edges of
H
, and therefore contains at most
n-t
distinct colours, since the colouring of
H
uses only
2q-t
different colours.
∎

Theorem

5.1
also implies that one can’t expect to find a rainbow Hamilton cycle in an arbitrary properly coloured Dirac graph if the colouring is not
\big(n/8+o(n)\big)
-bounded.

Corollary 5.3
.

For all sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)=n/2+1
and a
\big(n/8+2^{10}(n\log n)^{2/3}\big)
-bounded proper edge-colouring of
G
that does not contain a rainbow Hamilton cycle.

Proof.

Set
s=2^{10}(n\log n)^{2/3}
,
m=1
and
t=1
, and apply
Theorem

5.1
.
∎

Nevertheless, we suspect that being
n/8
-bounded
is
sufficient to guarantee that a proper edge-colouring of a Dirac graph contains a rainbow Hamilton cycle. To be precise, we make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 5.4
.

There exists a rainbow Hamilton cycle in any proper
n/8
-bounded edge-colouring of a Dirac graph on
n
vertices.

We also make the following conjecture for proper edge-colourings with no global boundedness condition.

Conjecture 5.5
.

Every proper edge-colouring of a Dirac graph on
n
vertices contains a Hamilton cycle with at least
n/2-o(n)
distinct colours.

Note that Conjecture
5.5
would strengthen Corollary
1.3
, which gives a bound of
n/4-o(n)
, and would be close to best possible, since there exist Dirac graphs with chromatic index
n/2+1
. It is even possible that the following stronger version of Conjecture
5.5
holds: every proper edge-colouring of a Dirac graph contains a Hamilton cycle with
\delta(G)
distinct colours.

Finally, let us quickly observe that the conclusion of Conjecture
5.5
holds if
\delta(G)\geqslant 7n/8
. This follows from our method, using the following result of Alon, Pokrovskiy and Sudakov
[
APS17
]
in place of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 5.6

(Lemma 3.1 of
[
APS17
]
)
.

Let
\delta\geqslant\gamma>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, with
3\gamma\delta-\gamma^{2}/2>n^{-1}
, and let
G
be a properly
coloured graph on
n
vertices and
\delta(G)\geqslant(1-\delta)n
. Then
G
contains a rainbow path forest
F
with at most
\gamma n
vertex-disjoint paths and

e(F)\geqslant(1-4\delta)n
.

Applying Lemma
5.6
with
\delta=1/8
and
\gamma=o(n)
, and using the absorber and reservoir given by Lemmas
3.1
and
3.3
to extend the rainbow path forest to a Hamilton cycle, it follows that every proper edge-colouring of a graph
G
on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geqslant 7n/8
contains a Hamilton cycle with at least
n/2
distinct colours.

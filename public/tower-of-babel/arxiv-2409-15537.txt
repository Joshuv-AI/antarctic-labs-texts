Quasi-Monte Carlo integration for feedback control under uncertainty
Philipp A. Guth, Peter Kritzer, Karl Kunisch

Abstract
A control in feedback form is derived for linear quadratic, time-invariant optimal control problems subject to parabolic partial differential equations with coefficients depending on a countably infinite number of uncertain parameters. It is shown that the Riccati-based feedback operator depends analytically on the parameters provided that the system operator depends analytically on the parameters, as is the case, for instance, in diffusion problems when the diffusion coefficient is parameterized by a Karhunen–Loève expansion. These novel parametric regularity results allow the application of quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) methods to efficiently compute an a-priori chosen feedback law based on the expected value. Moreover, under moderate assumptions on the input random field, QMC methods achieve superior error rates compared to ordinary Monte Carlo methods, independently of the stochastic dimension of the problem. Indeed, our paper for the first time studies Banach-space-valued integration by higher-order QMC methods.



Keywords:

Feedback control uncertainty, quasi-Monte Carlo method, parametric linear quadratic optimal control, partial differential equations with random coefficients.

Introduction

The design of regulators for controlled dynamical systems is a crucial task across various disciplines. While the governing equations are known in many real-world scenarios, the precise values of certain coefficients within these equations often elude our grasp due to measurement errors, material imperfections, or inherent randomness. A careful analysis of the uncertainty is thus indispensable in order to ensure that the control objective can be achieved.

In the last decade there has been a lot of research on open-loop control problems under uncertainty
[
1
,
14
,
22
,
26
]
. In these works controls are developed that are optimal with respect to given performance measures, which allow for different levels of risk aversion
[
19
,
34

, (multilevel) quasi-Monte Carlo methods

,

,

[

[

Control strategies that are directly applicable to different problem configurations, such as varying initial conditions, are highly desirable—especially in the presence of uncertainty. In contrast to open-loop controllers, closed-loop (or feedback) controllers can be constructed independently of the initial condition; hence, this is one reason why they are favourable in the presence of uncertainty. In
[
15

The key contributions of this paper are:

•

The parametric regularity analysis of the feedback law

•

Convergence rates for the approximation (

As we will show, the integrands considered in this paper are infinitely smooth so that we can obtain error convergence rates of arbitrarily high polynomial order when using suitable integration methods. This manuscript is the first study of higher-order QMC methods for the approximations of integrals with integrands taking values in separable Banach spaces.

•

The propagation of the total approximation error to control and state trajectories and the quantification of the suboptimality of the proposed feedback (

).

\displaystyle\leq\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(U,H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}\|(f,y_{\circ},Q^{\ast}Qg,P^{\ast}Pg_{T})\|_{V^{\prime}_{T}\times H\times V^{\prime}_{T}\times H}^{2}{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{|{\bm{\nu}}|}|\ell|!(|{\bm{\nu}}|-\ell)!\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}|{\bm{m}}|=\ell\\
{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}\end{subarray}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}

\displaystyle=\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(U,H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}\|(f,y_{\circ},Q^{\ast}Qg,P^{\ast}Pg_{T})\|_{V^{\prime}_{T}\times H\times V^{\prime}_{T}\times H}^{2}{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!,

where we used the Vandermonde convolution as in the steps for the first QoI.

In order to prove the bound for the third QoI in (
3.12
), we use the following estimate:

\displaystyle\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)\|_{H}^{2}-\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(0)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\|_{H}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}t=2\int_{0}^{T}\langle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t),\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\rangle_{V^{\prime},V}\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq 2\|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{V^{\prime}_{T}}\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{V_{T}}\leq\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}^{2}.

Together with (
3.10
), this leads to

\displaystyle\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}^{2}+\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(0)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq(c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+1)(|{\bm{\nu}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}})^{2}\|(f,y_{\circ},Q^{\ast}Qg,P^{\ast}Pg_{T})\|_{V^{\prime}_{T}\times H\times V^{\prime}_{T}\times H}^{2},

(3.16)

where we used
\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(0)\|_{H}^{2}\leq(|{\bm{\nu}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}})^{2}\|(f,y_{\circ},Q^{\ast}Qg,P^{\ast}Pg_{T})\|_{V_{T}^{\prime}\times H\times V_{T}^{\prime}\times H}^{2}
for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
Then, setting
C_{\rm ter}:=(c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+1)
, and by application of the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality as well as the triangle inequality, we get by (
3.16
)

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|P(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|^{2}_{H}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\|\partial^{\bm{m}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}P(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}\|\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}P(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\|P\partial^{\bm{m}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}\|P\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}

\displaystyle\leq\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\|\partial^{\bm{m}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}\|\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|_{H}.

Since
g_{T}
is independent of
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
, we have for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
that
\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}g_{T}\|_{H}\leq\delta_{{\bm{\nu}},\bm{0}}|{\bm{\nu}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\|g_{T}\|_{H}\leq|{\bm{\nu}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\|g_{T}\|_{H}
. Setting
\tilde{C}_{\rm ter}:=C_{\rm ter}\|(f,y_{\circ},Q^{\ast}Qg,P^{\ast}Pg_{T})\|_{V_{T}^{\prime}\times H\times V_{T}^{\prime}\times H}
we get by (
3.16
)

\displaystyle\big|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|P(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)-g_{T})\|^{2}_{H}\big|

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}(\|g_{T}\|_{H}+\tilde{C}_{\rm ter})|{\bm{m}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{m}}(\|g_{T}\|_{H}+\tilde{C}_{\rm ter})|{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}|!{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}(\|g_{T}\|_{H}+\tilde{C}_{\rm ter})^{2}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}|{\bm{m}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{m}}|{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}|!{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}(\|g_{T}\|_{H}+\tilde{C}_{\rm ter})^{2}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

which proves the desired result.
∎

4
Analytic dependence of the feedback law on the parameters

It is well-known that the optimal control, minimizing (
1.2
) subject to (
1.1
), can be written as a feedback law (depending on
{\bm{\sigma}}
) applied to the optimal state, that is

\displaystyle u({\bm{\sigma}})=K({\bm{\sigma}},y({\bm{\sigma}})).

In order to apply QMC integration to approximate the mean-based feedback (
1.3
) in Sect.
5
, we investigate the regularity of
{\bm{\sigma}}\mapsto K({\bm{\sigma}})
.
In fact, under the conditions of the previous section, we will show that the optimal feedback law depends analytically on the parameters.

In the presented parameterized linear quadratic optimal control problem the feedback is based on a parameter-dependent differential Riccati equation. In
[
33
]
the authors investigate the analyticity of the solution to the algebraic Riccati equations with respect to perturbations in the system matrices. However, no bounds on the derivatives are provided, which are of central importance in our work.

We divide the following analysis into two cases: first we investigate the case
g=g_{T}=f=0
, and then we consider nontrivial target functions
g
and
g_{T}
as well as nontrivial external forcing
f
.

4.1
Homogeneous constraint

In the homogeneous case, with
f=g=g_{T}=0
, the optimal feedback law is given as

\displaystyle K_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)=-B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t),

(4.1)

where
\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)
, for
t\in(0,T)
, solves the operator-valued differential Riccati equation

\displaystyle\dot{\Pi}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}

\displaystyle=\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}+{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}-\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}BB^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}+Q^{*}Q,\qquad\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(0)=P^{*}P.

(4.2)

For our analysis, we will use the fact that for all
{\bm{\sigma}}
the optimal cost is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\langle\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ},y_{\circ}\rangle_{H}={\mathcal{J}}(y_{{\bm{\sigma}}},u_{{\bm{\sigma}}}),

(4.3)

and that
q_{1}({\bm{\sigma}};0)=\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ}
. First, we show that
\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}q_{1}({\bm{\sigma}};0)\in H
, and thus
\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ}\in H
, for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. To this end, we estimate, similar to (
3.16
),

\displaystyle\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}q_{1}({\bm{\sigma}};0)\|_{H}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}q_{1}({\bm{\sigma}};\cdot)\|_{W_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}^{2}+\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}P^{\ast}Py({\bm{\sigma}};T)\|_{H}^{2}\leq C(|{\bm{\nu}}|!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}})^{2},

for some
C>0
, where we use
q_{1}({\bm{\sigma}};T)=-P^{\ast}Py({\bm{\sigma}};T)
, as well as (
3.11
) and (
3.16
).
Thus, we have
\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\langle\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ},y_{\circ}\rangle_{H}=\langle\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ},y_{\circ}\rangle_{H}
for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Taking the
{\bm{\nu}}
-th derivative of (
4.3
), we find due to Lemma
3.2
,

\displaystyle\big|\langle\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ},y_{\circ}\rangle_{H}\big|

\displaystyle\leq C_{4}(T)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},\quad\forall{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}},

where
C_{4}(T)
is defined in Lemma
3.2
. In the case
f=g=g_{T}=0
we have

\displaystyle C_{4}(T)=\left(\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(U,H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}(2c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+1)\right)\|y_{\circ}\|^{2}_{H}.

Since
\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)
is bounded, linear and self-adjoint, taking the supremum over all initial conditions
y_{\circ}
on the unit sphere in
H
leads to

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}=\sup_{\|y_{\circ}\|_{H}=1}|\langle\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)y_{\circ},y_{\circ}\rangle_{H}|.

Thus, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\|y_{\circ}\|_{H}=1}\Big(C_{4}(T)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\Big)=C_{5}(T)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

(4.4)

with
C_{5}(T):=\left(\|Q\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(U,H)}^{2}c_{V}^{2}c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+\|P\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}^{2}(2c_{\mathcal{G}}(T)^{2}+1)\right)
.

To bound
\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(\tau)
for
\tau\in[0,T]
consider (
1.2
) with
T
replaced by
\tau
. In view of the autonomy of
{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}},B,P
, and
Q
the corresponding Riccati equation is
(
4.2
)
restricted to
[0,\tau]
. Thus, we can replace
T
in (
4.4
) by any
\tau\in[0,T]
and then take the supremum over all
\tau\in[0,T]
leading us to

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(\tau)\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\tau\in[0,T]}\Big(C_{5}(\tau)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\Big)=C_{5}(T)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

(4.5)

since
C_{5}(T)
is continuous and monotonically increasing in
T
.

We have thus shown the following result.

Theorem 4.1
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
3.3
, the feedback law (
4.1
) depends analytically on the parameters
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
with

\displaystyle\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}(-B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H,U)}\leq\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(U,H)}C_{5}(T)(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\qquad\forall t\in[0,T],

for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
.

4.2
Nonhomogeneous case

Let us consider the nontrivial tracking problem with
g\neq 0
and
g_{T}\neq 0
in (
1.2
), as well as
f\neq 0
in (
1.1
). After the variable transformation
x_{{\bm{\sigma}}}:=y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}-g
the nonhomogeneous term
r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}:=f(t)+{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}g(t)-\dot{g}(t)
naturally arises in the state equation of tracking-type problems. We recall the following result from
[
3
, Thm. 7.1, Part IV, Ch. 1]
, for which we assume the additional regularity
f\in H_{T}
and
g\in W^{1,2}(0,T;H)\cap L^{2}(0,T;D({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}))
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Given
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
, let
\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\in C([0,T];{\mathcal{L}}(H))
denote the unique self-adjoint and nonnegative solution of (
4.2
). Then, there exists a unique minimizer
(x_{{\bm{\sigma}}},u_{{\bm{\sigma}}})
of (
1.2
) subject to (
1.1
). This optimal pair satisfies, for
t\in(0,T)
,

1.

u_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is given in feedback form by

\displaystyle u_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)=-B^{\ast}\left(\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)x_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)+h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\right);

(4.6)

2.

x_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is the mild solution to the closed-loop system

\displaystyle\dot{x}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}-BB^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\right)x_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)-BB^{\ast}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)+r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t),\qquad x(0)=x_{\circ};

where

\displaystyle-\dot{h}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)

\displaystyle=\left({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\ast}-\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)BB^{\ast}\right)h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)+\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t),\qquad h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)=0;

(4.7)

3.

the optimal cost is given by

\begin{split}{\mathcal{J}}(x,u)&=\frac{1}{2}\langle\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T)x_{\circ},x_{\circ}\rangle_{H}+\langle h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(0),x_{\circ}\rangle_{H}\\
&\quad+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\langle h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(s),r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(s)\rangle_{H}-\frac{1}{2}\|B^{\ast}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(s)\|_{U}^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}s.\end{split}

We see from (
4.6
) that, for
t\in[0,T]
, the optimal feedback
K_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t,\cdot):H\to U
in the nonhomogeneous case is an affine function of the optimal state
x_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)
. It is given by

\displaystyle K_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t,\cdot)=-B^{\ast}\big(\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\cdot+h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\big).

The parametric regularity of the linear part
-B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is derived in Sect.
4.1
. Thus, it remains to investigate the parametric regularity of
h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
.
For this purpose we make the following assumptions:

\displaystyle\begin{split}D({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})\text{ is independent of }{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}\text{ and }D({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})=D({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})\text{ for all }{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}},\end{split}

(4.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\exists\,\text{a sequence of nonnegative numbers }\tilde{\bm{b}}\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{N})\text{ with }0<p\leq 1\\
&\text{such that }\|\partial_{\bm{\sigma}}^{\bm{\nu}}{\mathcal{A}}_{\bm{\sigma}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(D({\mathcal{A}}),H)}\leq\tilde{{\bm{b}}}^{\bm{\nu}}\quad\forall{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\},\end{split}

(4.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(D({\mathcal{A}}),H)}\leq\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\quad\forall{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}.\end{split}

(4.10)

In view of (
4.8
) we will denote
D({\mathcal{A}})=D({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})=D({\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})
for all
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let the assumptions (
2.1
), (
2.2
), (
2.3
), (
4.8
), (
4.9
), and (
4.10
) hold, and assume that there exists a sequence
\tilde{\bm{b}}\in\ell^{p}({\mathbb{N}})
, with
0<p\leq 1
, of nonnegative numbers, such that
\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq\tilde{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{\nu}}}
for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
Then, the solution
h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
of (
4.7
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W_{T}^{0}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+C)^{\max\{|{\bm{\nu}}|-1,0\}}C^{\delta_{{\bm{\nu}},\mathbf{0}}}(C+C^{2})^{1-\delta_{{\bm{\nu}},\mathbf{0}}}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!\bm{b}^{{\bm{\nu}}},\quad\forall{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}},

with
b_{j}=\tilde{b}_{j}/\ln{2}
, for some constant
C>0
independent of
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
.

Proof.

Let us define
W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime}):=\{w\in W_{T}(V,V^{\prime})\mid w(T)=0\}
and the parametric evolution operator
D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}=-\frac{d}{dt}-({\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\ast}-\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)BB^{\ast})
mapping from
W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})
to
V^{\prime}_{T}
, so that we can write (
4.7
) as

\displaystyle D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}=\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}.

(4.11)

Following the arguments in the proof of Lemma
3.1
and using the uniform bound on
\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
((
4.5
) with
{\bm{\nu}}=\bm{0}
), it can be shown that there is a constant
c_{D}>0
such that
\|D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{-1}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(V_{T}^{\prime},W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime}))}\leq c_{D}
for all
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. Moreover, for arbitrary
w\in W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})
and for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, we find

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}w\|_{V_{T}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}w-\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}BB^{\ast}w\|_{V_{T}^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}{\mathcal{A}}^{\ast}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(V,V^{\prime})}+c_{H}c_{V}\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}BB^{\ast}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H)}\big)\|w\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})},

where
c_{H}
and
c_{V}
are the embedding constants
\|\cdot\|_{V^{\prime}}\leq c_{H}\|\cdot\|_{H}
and
\|\cdot\|_{H}\leq c_{V}\|\cdot\|_{V}
, respectively. Recalling that
\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq\tilde{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{\nu}}}
for all
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
, and by (
4.5
) and the fact that
\tilde{b}_{j}\leq b_{j}
,
j\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(W_{T}^{0}(V,V^{\prime}),V_{T}^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\tilde{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}+(|{\bm{\nu}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\big)=C(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

(4.12)

for some
C>0
independent of
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. Thus,
D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is a
p
-analytic operator. However, in order to deduce the parametric regularity of the solution
h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
of (
4.11
), we cannot directly apply Theorem
3.1
since the right-hand side in (
4.11
) depends on
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
.

Instead, we will prove the result by induction on
|{\bm{\nu}}|
. For the base case,
{\bm{\nu}}=\bm{0}
, we estimate the right-hand side of (
4.11
): by (
4.10
) we have for
{\bm{\nu}}=\bm{0}
that
\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{H_{T}}\leq\|f\|_{H_{T}}+\widetilde{C}_{\mathcal{A}}\|g\|_{H_{T}}+\|\dot{g}\|_{H_{T}}
for all
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. Together with the uniform boundedness of
\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
and
D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{-1}
, the base step
\|h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq C
follows. For the induction step let
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}\setminus\{\bm{0}\}
.
By (
4.9
) we have that
\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{H_{T}}=\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}g\|_{H_{T}}\leq\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}{\mathcal{A}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(D({\mathcal{A}}),H)}\|g\|_{H_{T}}\leq\|g\|_{H_{T}}\tilde{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}
, and thus

\displaystyle\|\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{H_{T}}\leq C_{6}\tilde{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

for some constant
C_{6}>0
independent of
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. In the following, let
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
. Then, by (
4.5
) and the Leibniz product rule we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\|_{H_{T}}

\displaystyle=\Big\|\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\partial^{\bm{m}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\Big\|_{H_{T}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{5}(T)C_{6}\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}(|{\bm{m}}|+1)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{m}}\tilde{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}

\displaystyle=C_{7}(T){\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{|{\bm{\nu}}|}(\ell+1)!\frac{|{\bm{\nu}}|!}{\ell!(|{\bm{\nu}}|-\ell)!}\leq C_{7}(T){\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!,

(4.13)

where
C_{7}(T)=C_{5}(T)C_{6}
and
\tilde{b}_{j}\leq b_{j}
,
j\geq 1
.

Applying
\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
to (
4.11

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{m}}}D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}=\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\partial^{\bm{m}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t),

provided that the derivatives of
h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
exist. Their existence can be shown as follows: separating out the
{\bm{m}}=\bm{0}
term on the left-hand side leads to the recurrence relation

\displaystyle D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}=-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}\\
{\bm{m}}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{m}}}D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}+\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\partial^{\bm{m}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t).

(4.14)

The existence of
\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
follows from (
4.14
) by induction on
|{\bm{\nu}}|
: for
{\bm{\nu}}=\bm{0}
it follows from (
4.11
). Then, with (
4.12
) and (
4.13
) the existence of
\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
follows from (
4.14
) for any
{\bm{\nu}}\in{\mathcal{F}}
assuming that it has been shown for all multi-indices with order less than
|{\bm{\nu}}|
.

Furthermore, (
4.14
) leads to

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}\\
{\bm{m}}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\|D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{-1}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{m}}}D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}\|D_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{-1}\partial^{\bm{m}}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}r_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\leq\hat{C}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\bm{m}}\leq{\bm{\nu}}\\
{\bm{m}}\neq\bm{0}\end{subarray}}\binom{{\bm{\nu}}}{{\bm{m}}}(|{\bm{m}}|+2)!{\bm{b}}^{{\bm{m}}}\|\partial^{{\bm{\nu}}-{\bm{m}}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W^{0}_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}+\hat{C}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

for some
\hat{C}>0
independent of
{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}
. Then, setting

, by Lemma

in Appendix B we have

\displaystyle\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W_{T}^{0}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+C)^{\max\{|{\bm{\nu}}|-1,0\}}C^{\delta_{{\bm{\nu}},\mathbf{0}}}(C+C^{2})^{1-\delta_{{\bm{\nu}},\mathbf{0}}}(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!\bm{b}^{{\bm{\nu}}},

which proves the desired result for all

.

with Proposition

and Theorem

.

Theorem 4.3
.

Under the conditions of Proposition

we have

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\left(\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(-B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t))\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H,U)}+\|\partial^{\bm{\nu}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(-B^{\ast}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t))\|_{U}\right)\leq\|B\|_{{\mathcal{L}}(H,U)}\,\widetilde{C}\,(|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!\,{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}},

for all

5
QMC approximation of the feedback
K

In numerical practice (
1.3
) signals the necessity of approximately evaluating integrals of the form

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{{\mathbb{N}}}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}},

for fixed
t
, where

\displaystyle{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)=-B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\in Z={\mathcal{L}}(H,U),\qquad\text{or}\qquad{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)=-B^{\ast}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\in Z=U.

For this purpose, in the following section we investigate the potential of QMC methods for the feedback optimal control problem of minimizing (
1.2
) subject to (
1.1
).

The QMC approximation of integrals with Banach space-valued integrands was first studied in
[
14
]
, where the error analysis is presented for randomly shifted lattice rules. Higher-order QMC methods have been studied in
[
24
]
in a Hilbert space setting. In order to use higher-order QMC rules for the approximation of integrals over the feedback law, we provide a novel error analysis for higher-order QMC rules in separable Banach spaces.

Recall that the state space
H
is a separable Hilbert space.
This separability assumption allows to guarantee that
{\mathcal{L}}(H,U)
is a separable Banach space
[
40
, Prop. 3.13]
, since in our case
U
is chosen to be finite-dimensional. Furthermore, by Theorem
4.3
the mapping
{\bm{\sigma}}\mapsto{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is continuous. Thus,
{\bm{\sigma}}\mapsto G({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}})
is continuous for all
G\in Z^{\prime}
, and in particular it is measurable. By Pettis’ Theorem
{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is strongly measurable. Moreover, the upper bound in Thm.
4.3
implies with Bochner’s Theorem that
{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is integrable over
{\mathfrak{S}}=[-1/2,1/2]^{\mathbb{N}}
.

Our strategy for approximation is to truncate the domain of
{\bm{\sigma}}
to the finite-dimensional domain
[-1/2,1/2]^{s}
first, and then, after some transformations, apply a quasi-Monte Carlo (QMC) rule to the truncated integral.
Indeed, for
s\in\{1,\ldots,d\}
, we write
({\bm{\sigma}}_{s},{\bm{0}}):=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{s},0,0,\ldots)
, and
{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}:={\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(({\bm{\sigma}}_{s},{\bm{0}}))
.
With this strategy, the total approximation error consists of:

\displaystyle\underbrace{\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{{\mathbb{N}}}}({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)-{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}\right\|_{Z}}_{\text{dimension truncation error}}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{x}}_{k}}\right\|_{Z}}_{\text{QMC error}},

where the points
{\bm{x}}_{0},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{s}
are QMC points chosen as described in the remainder of this section. To bound the dimension truncation error let us assume that, for a.e.
{\bm{\sigma}}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{\mathbb{N}}
and for all
t\in[0,T]
, we have that

\|{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)-{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\|_{Z}\to 0\quad\mbox{as}\quad s\to\infty.

(5.1)

This assumption is addressed in Appendix
C
.

Due to Theorem
4.3
and (
5.1
), we can apply
[
12
, Thm. 4.3]
, which yields

\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{{\mathbb{N}}}}({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)-{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}\right\|_{Z}\leq C\,s^{-\frac{2}{p}+1},

where
C
is a positive constant independent of
s
.
The remainder of this section is devoted to the development of QMC rules to approximate

\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}_{s},

where
\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}_{s}=\bigotimes_{j=1}^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\sigma_{j}
is a “truncated” version of
\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}
.

Next, we shall use a result that was essentially shown in
[
14
, Thm. 6.5]
. We state this result for
completeness.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
be a Banach space of functions
F:[-1/2,1/2]^{s}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, which is
continuously embedded in the space of continuous functions. Consider an
N
-point QMC rule
with integration nodes
{\bm{x}}_{0},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{s}
, given by

Q_{N,s}(F):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}F({\bm{x}}_{k}).

Furthermore, we define the worst case error of integration using
Q_{N,s}
in
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
by

e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{W}}_{s}):=\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}F\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s}\\
\|F\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\left|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}F({\bm{x}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{x}}-Q_{N,s}(F)\right|.

Assume that
Z
is a separable Banach space and that
Z^{\prime}
is its dual space. Moreover, let
k
be a continuous mapping that maps any
{\bm{\sigma}}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{s}
to some
k({\bm{\sigma}})\in Z
. Then,

\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}k({\bm{\sigma}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}k({\bm{x}}_{k})\right\|_{Z}\leq e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{W}}_{s})\,\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in Z^{\prime}\\
\|G\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|G(k)\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s}}.

If we choose
k={\mathscr{K}}_{\sigma,s}(t)
, and
Z\in\{U,{\mathcal{L}}(H,U)\}
in Theorem
5.1
, we can apply this result to derive error bounds for approximating

\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}.

To this end, we make a particular choice of the space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
in Theorem
5.1
. Actually, as we will show, there are different ways of choosing
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
and a suitable QMC rule
Q_{N,s}
tailored to
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
, leading to different variants of error estimates. We go through several cases separately below, but before would like to explain the general idea of
weighted function spaces
as introduced by Sloan and Woźniakowski in
[
38
]
.

The motivation for the use of weighted spaces results from applications, where different coordinates or different groups of
coordinates may have different influence on a multivariate problem. In our case, this is reflected in the upper bound in Theorem
4.3
; indeed, in this bound the terms
{\bm{b}}^{\bm{\nu}}
differ, depending on which
\partial_{{\bm{\sigma}}}^{\bm{\nu}}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}
is considered. Consequently, as we will see below (see (
5.9
) and (
5.10
)), these terms will have a crucial role in the choice of our
weights
, which are nonnegative real numbers
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
, one for each set
{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}
. Intuitively speaking, the number
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
models the influence
of the variables with indices in
{\mathfrak{u}}
. Larger values of
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}
mean more influence, smaller values less influence. Formally, we
set
\gamma_{\emptyset}=1
, and we write
{\bm{\gamma}}=\{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}\}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
. These weights are included in the norms of the function spaces, which are, in this context so-called unanchored weighted Sobolev spaces. We will follow a strategy first outlined in
[
7
]
, where we essentially aim to choose the weights in a way that the norms of the elements of the function space are bounded by a constant and the worst-case error of integration is minimized. This yields the particular choices (
5.9
) and (
5.10
), respectively. As it turns out, in this way the problems under consideration in this manuscript may even lose the curse of dimensionality, provided that suitable conditions on the parameters involved hold. We refer to the sections below for details.

The aforementioned unanchored weighted Sobolev spaces of smoothness
\alpha
are defined as follows.

Let
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
1\leq r\leq\infty
, and let
1\leq q\leq\infty
. We may choose
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
in Theorem
5.1
as the Sobolev space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}
,
with positive coordinate weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
, and with norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}}^{r}:=\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{q}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\bigg|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s-|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}F({\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}};{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus{\mathfrak{u}}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\bigg|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{\mathfrak{v}}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}},

with the obvious adaptions if
r=\infty
.
Here, by
({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})
we denote a sequence
{\bm{\nu}}
with components
\nu_{j}=\alpha
for
j\in\mathfrak{v}
,
\nu_{j}=\tau_{j}
for
j\in{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}
, and
\nu_{j}=0
for
j\notin{\mathfrak{u}}
.

For technical reasons, we also define another unanchored Sobolev space
{\mathcal{V}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}
of smoothness
\alpha\in{\mathbb{N}}
, with the norm

\displaystyle\|F\|_{{\mathcal{V}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}}}}^{2}:=

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha-1\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}

\displaystyle\quad\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{|\mathfrak{v}|}}\bigg|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s-|\mathfrak{v}|}}\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}F({\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}};{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus{\mathfrak{u}}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\bigg|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{\mathfrak{v}},

with the following adjustment for the case
\alpha=1
:
in this case, all summands in the second sum for
\mathfrak{v}\subsetneq{\mathfrak{u}}
disappear. If
\mathfrak{v}={\mathfrak{u}}
, then
\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}
is the “void” vector with no components such that the corresponding summand in the second sum remains.

Essentially, the norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{V}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}}}}
is similar to
\|\cdot\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}}
, but with the difference
that the summation range for
\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}
is
\{1,\ldots,\alpha-1\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}
instead of
\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}
.

Note that for any
F\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}
we have
\|F\|_{{\mathcal{V}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}}\leq\|F\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}}
, and this implies,
for any QMC rule
Q_{N,s}
,

e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2})\leq e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{V}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}}}).

(5.2)

Thus, any upper bound on the worst-case error of a QMC rule
Q_{N,s}
in
{\mathcal{V}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}}}
is also an upper bound on the worst-case error of
Q_{N,s}
in
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}
.

We can then in principle proceed as in the proof of
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
as follows. For dealing with
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}
, we
distinguish the cases
r<\infty
, and
r=\infty
, and we assume
q<\infty
for simplicity.

For
r=\infty
, and
F\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}
,
we get

\displaystyle\|F\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}}\leq

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\bigg(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}F({\bm{y}})\bigg|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and for
r<\infty
and
F\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}
,

\displaystyle\|F\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}}^{r}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\bigg(\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}F({\bm{y}})\bigg|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}\bigg)^{\frac{r}{q}}.

In both cases, we can replace
F({\bm{y}})
by
G({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))
, integrate
with respect to
{\bm{\sigma}}
instead of
{\bm{y}}
, and obtain
for
G\in Z^{\prime}
with
\|G\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\bigg|\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{\sigma}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}G({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))\bigg|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\bigg|G\left(\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{\sigma}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\right)\bigg|^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\|G\|_{Z^{\prime}}^{q}\,\left\|\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{\sigma}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\right\|_{Z}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}\left\|\frac{\partial^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}}{\partial{\bm{\sigma}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\right\|_{Z}^{q}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\widetilde{C}\,(|({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})|+2)!\,{\bm{b}}_{\{1:s\}}^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}\right)^{q},

where we used Theorem
4.3
in the last step. Thus,

\displaystyle\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in Z^{\prime}\\
\|G\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|G({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,{\bm{\alpha}},{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}}

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\frac{\widetilde{C}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\bigg(\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}\left((|({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})|+2)!\,{\bm{b}}_{\{1:s\}}^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}\right)^{q}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\frac{\widetilde{C}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\sum_{\mathfrak{v}\subseteq{\mathfrak{u}}}\sum_{\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}}\in\{1,\ldots,\alpha\}^{\mid{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}\mid}}(|({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})|+2)!\,{\bm{b}}_{\{1:s\}}^{({\bm{\alpha}}_{\mathfrak{v}},\bm{\tau}_{{\mathfrak{u}}\setminus\mathfrak{v}},\bm{0})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\max_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\frac{\widetilde{C}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\sum_{{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1:\alpha\}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}(|{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}),

where
\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)
equals
1
if
\nu_{j}=\alpha
, and is zero otherwise.

Analogously,

\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\in Z^{\prime}\\
\|G\|_{Z^{\prime}}\leq 1\end{subarray}}\|G({\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t))\|_{{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,r}}^{r}\leq\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}\Bigg(\frac{\widetilde{C}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\sum_{{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1:\alpha\}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}(|{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j})^{\nu_{j}})\Bigg)^{r}.

(5.4)

Reconsidering the above estimates implies that (
5.4
) remains unchanged for
q=\infty
.

We will now apply Theorem
5.1
for different choices of
\alpha
,
r
, and
q
, respectively, and outline how we can use well chosen QMC rules in order to obtain a suitable error bound.

5.1
Randomly shifted lattice rules for smoothness one

In this section, we consider the weighted Sobolev space of smoothness one,

{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},}
as introduced above,
with norm

\|F\|_{{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}}}^{2}:=\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2}}\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}\left|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s-|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}\frac{\partial^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}{\partial{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}F({\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}};{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus{\mathfrak{u}}})\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{\{1:s\}\setminus{\mathfrak{u}}}\right|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{y}}_{{\mathfrak{u}}}.

The space
{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}}
is frequently considered in literature on lattice rules for multivariate integration.
Indeed, lattice point sets are highly-structured integration node sets in
[0,1)^{s}
. A sub-class of these are
rank-1 lattice point sets
, essentially based on one
generating vector

{\bm{z}}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})
. They are among the most prominent QMC integration node sets
(see, e.g.,
[
6
,
29
,
37
]
).
For a natural number
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
and an integer generating vector
{\bm{z}}\in\{1,2,\dots,N-1\}^{s}
, rank-1 lattice points are of the form

{\bm{x}}_{k}:=\left\{\frac{k}{N}{\bm{z}}\right\}=\left(\left\{\frac{kz_{1}}{N}\right\},\ldots,\left\{\frac{kz_{s}}{N}\right\}\right)\in[0,1)^{s},\qquad\text{for }k=0,1,\ldots,N-1,

and the point set
{\bm{x}}_{0},{\bm{x}}_{1},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}
is sometimes denoted by
P({\bm{z}},N)
. For
{\bm{x}}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{s}
we apply
\{\cdot\}
component-wise,
where
\{x\}=x-\lfloor x\rfloor
is the fractional part of
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
. A QMC rule using
P({\bm{z}},N)
as integration nodes
is called a
(rank-1) lattice rule
. Note that, given
N
and
s
,
a rank-1 lattice rule is completely determined by the choice of the generating vector
{\bm{z}}
. However, not every choice of
{\bm{z}}

yields a rank-1 lattice rule with good quality for approximating the integral. The standard procedure to choose a good
{\bm{z}}
is the so-called
component-by-component construction
(or CBC construction, for short); a CBC construction
is a greedy algorithm to choose the components of
{\bm{z}}
one after another. Fast implementations of CBC constructions exist, many of them due to Cools and Nuyens (see, e.g.,
[
31
]
) and again
[
6
]
for an overview.

It is sometimes useful to introduce a random element in the application
of lattice rules. One way of doing so is to
modify the lattice integration rule
{\bm{x}}_{k}
,
k=0,1,\ldots,N-1
to
\{{\bm{x}}_{k}+\Delta\}
,

k=0,1,\ldots,N-1
, where
\Delta
is chosen according to a uniform distribution on
[0,1]^{d}
, and where
\{\cdot\}
again denotes the fractional part. In this way, we obtain a
randomly shifted lattice rule
(all points are shifted by the same random
\Delta
). Note that by
fixing a lattice point set and generating
M
independent realizations of a random shift,
one can study statistical properties of the corresponding estimators for the integral to be numerically computed.

It is known (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Chapter 7]
) that we can effectively construct (by a CBC algorithm) a randomly shifted lattice rule with points
{\bm{x}}_{0},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{N-1}\in[-1/2,1/2]^{s}
(shifted by a random
{\bm{\Delta}}
) such that

{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}([e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}})]^{2})\leq\left(\frac{1}{(\phi_{\rm tot}(N))}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}},

for all
\lambda\in(1/2,1]
, where
\phi_{\rm tot}
is Euler’s totient function. By Equation (
5.2
), we then also obtain

{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}([e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{W}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2})]^{2})\leq\left(\frac{1}{(\phi_{\rm tot}(N))}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}.

Next, we combine the latter inequality with (
5.4
), and slightly modify Theorem
5.1
(see
[
14
, Theorem 6.6]
) to obtain

{\mathbb{E}}_{{\bm{\Delta}}}\left(\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathscr{K}}_{\{{\bm{x}}_{k}+{\bm{\Delta}}\},s}(t)\right\|_{Z}^{2}\right)\leq C_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}\,\frac{1}{(\phi_{\rm tot}(N))^{1/\lambda}},

(5.5)

for all
\lambda\in(1/2,1]
, where
C_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}
is a constant that is independent of
N
, but dependent on the coordinate weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
in the space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}
,

C_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}=\widetilde{C}^{2}\left(\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\left(\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\frac{[(|{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!]^{2}\,\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}b_{j}^{2}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2}}\right).

5.2
Folded lattice rules for smoothness one

As an alternative to a randomly shifted lattice rule, it is also possible to derive a deterministic error bound similar to (
5.5
) by employing so-called
folded lattice rules
, also referred to as
tent-transformed lattice rules
. The tent transformation
\phi:[0,1]\to[0,1]
is a Lebesgue measure preserving mapping defined by
\phi(x)=1-|2x-1|
. If we apply
\phi
to a point
{\bm{x}}\in[0,1]^{s}
, we always mean component-wise application, i.e.,
\phi({\bm{x}})=(\phi(x_{1}),\ldots,\phi(x_{s}))
. For a given lattice point set we then apply
\phi
to all points, and thereby obtain the
folded lattice point set
; the corresponding lattice
rule is then called a folded lattice rule.

Let us consider the same function space
{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}}
as in Section
5.1
. It is known from results in
[
9
]
that one can construct a folded lattice rule
Q_{N,s}^{\phi}
by a CBC algorithm such that

\left[e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s}^{\phi},{\mathcal{V}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}})\right]^{2}\leq\frac{1}{(\phi_{\rm tot}(N))^{1/\lambda}}\,\left(\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}

for all
\lambda\in(1/2,1]
.

We can then proceed similarly to Section
5.1
, and
employ Theorem
5.1
. In this way, we can construct by a CBC algorithm a folded lattice rule with points
\phi({\bm{x}}_{0}),\ldots,\phi({\bm{x}}_{N-1})
such that

\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathscr{K}}_{\{\phi({\bm{x}}_{k})\},s}(t)\right\|_{Z}^{2}\leq\widehat{C}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}\,\frac{1}{(\phi_{\rm tot}(N))^{1/\lambda}},

(5.6)

for all
\lambda\in(1/2,1]
, and where
\widehat{C}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}
is a constant that is independent of
N
, but dependent on the coordinate weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
in the space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},2,2}
,

\widehat{C}_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}=\widetilde{C}^{2}\left(\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2\lambda}\left(\frac{2\zeta(2\lambda)}{(\pi^{2})^{\lambda}}\right)^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\left(\sum_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\frac{[(|{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!]^{2}\,\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}b_{j}^{2}}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{2}}\right),

which means that the error bound using a folded lattice rule is, up to a factor of 2, the same as the probabilistic error bound using a randomly shifted lattice rule.

5.3
Higher-order QMC

We now consider the Sobolev space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}
. For integrands in

{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}
it is natural to hope that
the smoothness
\alpha
is reflected in the error convergence rate of a cubature rule, in the sense that higher smoothness yields higher-order convergence. Indeed, this can be achieved by using higher-order QMC rules which are based on polynomial lattice rules, another important class of QMC node sets, which was introduced by Niederreiter (see, e.g.,
[
29
]
). A polynomial lattice rule can be constructed analogously to an ordinary lattice rule, by replacing integer arithmetic by polynomial arithmetic over finite fields. The generating vector of a polynomial lattice point set is then a vector of polynomials over a finite field, and can again be obtained by a CBC construction. It was shown by Dick that one can use so-called higher-order polynomial lattice rules (which are obtained from ordinary polynomial lattice rules as building blocks, by the method of digit interlacing) for numerical integration in Sobolev spaces of higher smoothness and obtain essentially optimal error convergence rates. We refer to
[
10
]
for details.
While previous studies of higher-order QMC methods consider real-valued integrads or integrands taking values in a separable Hilbert space, a novelty of our work is the generalization to separable Banach spaces.

Let
\beta
be prime,
m\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
N=\beta^{m}
. Then, for
r=\infty
and every
1\leq q\leq\infty
, it is known (see,
[
7
, Thm. 3.10]
) that an interlaced polynomial lattice rule
Q_{N,s}
of order
\alpha
can be constructed using a CBC algorithm, such that

\displaystyle e^{\rm wor}(Q_{N,s},{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty})\leq\bigg(\frac{2}{N-1}\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}(\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda))^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}

(5.7)

for all
\lambda\in(1/\alpha,1]
, where

\displaystyle\rho_{\alpha,\beta}(\lambda):=\left(C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\beta^{\alpha(\alpha-1)/2}\right)^{\lambda}\left(\left(1+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta^{\alpha\lambda}-\beta}\right)^{\alpha}-1\right),

with
C_{\alpha,\beta}
depending only on
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Combining (
5.7
) with (
5.3
), we obtain

\left\|\int_{[-1/2,1/2]^{s}}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{\sigma}},s}(t)\,\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}{\mathscr{K}}_{{\bm{x}}_{k},s}(t)\right\|_{Z}\leq C_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}\frac{1}{(N-1)^{1/\lambda}}

for all
\lambda\in(1/\alpha,1]
, where
C_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}
is a constant that is independent of
N
, but dependent on the coordinate weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
in the space
{\mathcal{W}}_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},q,\infty}
,

C_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}=\widetilde{C}\bigg(2\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\lambda}(\rho_{\alpha,b}(\lambda))^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\frac{1}{\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}}\sum_{{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1:\alpha\}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}(|{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}).

(5.8)

5.4
Choosing the weights

Now, we make a particular choice of the weights
{\bm{\gamma}}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
.
These are so-called “SPOD weights” (smoothness-driven product and order dependent weights),

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\ast}:=\sum_{{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1:\alpha\}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}(|{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}).

(5.9)

In the special case
\alpha=1
, the SPOD weights in (
5.9
) simplify to “POD weights” (product and order dependent weights),

\displaystyle\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{\ast}:=(|{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(2b_{j}^{\nu_{j}}).

(5.10)

Let us start by analyzing the bound (
5.5
) for randomly shifted lattice rules. From
[
23
, Lemma 6.2]
we
know that
C_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}
is minimized by choosing the
set of weights
{\bm{\gamma}}^{*}=(\gamma_{{\mathfrak{u}}}^{*})_{{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1,\ldots,s\}}
, with

\gamma_{\mathfrak{u}}^{*}=\left((|{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}\frac{b_{j}(2\pi^{2})^{\lambda/2}}{\sqrt{2\zeta(2\lambda)}}\right)^{1/(1+\lambda)}.

Using the same argumentation as in
[
14
, Proof of Theorem 6.7]
we then obtain that
C_{s,1,{\bm{\gamma}}^{*},\lambda}
is bounded independently of
s
, by choosing

\lambda=\begin{cases}1/(2-2\delta)\quad\mbox{for arbitrary $\delta\in(0,1)$}&\mbox{if $p\in(0,2/3]$,}\\
p/(2-p)&\mbox{if $p\in(2/3,1]$.}\end{cases}

Moreover, it follows—again by the same argument as in
[
14
]
—that the bound (
5.5
) on the mean-square error is of order

\kappa(N)=\begin{cases}[\phi_{\rm tot}(N)]^{-2-2\delta}\quad\mbox{for arbitrary $\delta\in(0,1)$}&\mbox{if $p\in(0,2/3]$,}\\
[\phi_{\rm tot}(N)]^{-(2/p-1)}&\mbox{if $p\in(2/3,1]$.}\end{cases}

For the bound (
5.6
) on the squared integration error
using folded lattice rules in the case of smoothness one, we obviously can choose
{\bm{\gamma}}
and
\lambda
in exactly the same way as for (
5.5
).

Next, we insert the SPOD weights as in (
5.9
) into (
5.8
), and we make use of the inequality
\left(\sum_{k}a_{k}\right)^{\lambda}\leq\sum_{k}a_{k}^{\lambda}
, which holds for any
\lambda\in(0,1]
and nonnegative
a_{k}
. Consequently, we can bound the
term
C_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}
in (
5.8
),

\displaystyle C_{s,\alpha,{\bm{\gamma}},\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}\bigg(2\sum_{\emptyset\neq{\mathfrak{u}}\subseteq\{1:s\}}\sum_{{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}\in\{1:\alpha\}^{|{\mathfrak{u}}|}}((|{\bm{\nu}}_{\mathfrak{u}}|+2)!)^{\lambda}\prod_{j\in{\mathfrak{u}}}(E2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}})^{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}

\displaystyle=\widetilde{C}\bigg(2\sum_{\bm{0}\neq{\bm{\nu}}\in\{0:\alpha\}^{s}}((|{\bm{\nu}}|+2)!)^{\lambda}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
\nu_{j}>0\end{subarray}}^{s}(E2^{\delta(\nu_{j},\alpha)}b_{j}^{\nu_{j}})^{\lambda}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}},

(5.11)

where
E:=C_{\alpha,\beta}\,\beta^{\alpha(\alpha-1)/2}((1+\frac{\beta-1}{\beta^{\alpha\lambda}-\beta})^{\alpha}-1)^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}
. Following the steps in
[
7
, pp. 2694–2695]
, one shows that the sum in (
5.11
) is finite independently of
s
if
p\leq\lambda<1
.
In the case
\lambda=1
, we assume in addition that
\sum_{j\geq 1}b_{j}<(2\alpha\max{(E,1)})^{-1}
. Since
\lambda
needs to satisfy
1/\alpha<\lambda\leq 1
, we take
\lambda=p
and
\alpha=\lfloor 1/p\rfloor+1
. In this case, the integration error is thus of order
\mathcal{O}(N^{-\alpha})
, where the implied constant is independent of the dimension
s
.

\displaystyle K(t,\cdot):=\int_{\mathfrak{S}}K_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t,\cdot)\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}:=-\int_{\mathfrak{S}}B^{\ast}\Pi_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(T-t)(\cdot)\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}-\int_{\mathfrak{S}}B^{\ast}h_{{\bm{\sigma}}}(t)\mathrm{d}{\bm{\sigma}}

Denoting by

Suboptimality of the feedback

Proof of Lemma

Proof.

Let

and

be arbitrary. We will show that the solution of

\displaystyle\|y_{{\bm{\sigma}}}\|_{W_{T}(V,V^{\prime})}\leq c_{A}(T)\big(\|f\|_{V^{\prime}_{T}}^{2}+\|y_{\circ}\|_{H}^{2}\big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\quad\forall{\bm{\sigma}}\in{\mathfrak{S}}

with

A.1

, leading, together with (

\displaystyle\frac{\rm d}{\rm dt}\|y\|_{H}^{2}

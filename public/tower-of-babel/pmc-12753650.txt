Mapping the technological evolution of generative AI: a patent network analysis
Authors: Navid Mohammadi, Jalil Heidary Dahooie, Amir Ali Bengari, Arash Rahimi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study systematically maps the technological evolution and innovation landscape of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) through a large-scale analysis of patent networks. Leveraging advanced text mining, network modeling, and community detection techniques on over 20,000 patents from Lens.org, we identify key technological domains, trace conceptual shifts, and highlight emerging trends across three major periods (pre-2016, 2016–2020, 2021–2025). Results reveal a pivotal transition post-2016 from early modular and domain-specific innovations, such as neuromorphic computing and bioengineering, toward integrated generative frameworks, API-driven platforms, and multi-modal capabilities. The post-2016 era is characterized by rapid growth in patent volume, increased conceptual diversity, and diminishing modularity, reflecting the convergence of previously distinct subfields. Key emergent clusters include generative AI frameworks, advanced medical imaging, personalized interactive systems, media authentication, and privacy-preserving AI. Notably, growing attention to content authenticity and user personalization underscores the interplay between technological maturation and societal concerns. Comparative analysis with baseline and alternative clustering models demonstrates the robustness and interpretability of the chosen network approach. While limited by reliance on English-language patents and TF–IDF extraction, this work provides an actionable roadmap for R&D leaders, policymakers, and investors to navigate the dynamic GAI landscape and informs future research directions involving multilingual and semantic-rich analyses.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-26810-7.Keywords: Generative artificial intelligence, Patent analysis, Technology roadmapping, Innovation clusters, Network modelingSubject terms: Complex networks; Complex networks; Information systems and information technology; Mathematics and computing; Science, technology and society

Introduction

Artificial intelligence is a broad concept and is defined as a set of technologies that can perform human cognitive tasks, such as perception, reasoning, planning, and prediction1. Conceptually, the development of artificial intelligence can be seen in three stages. The first stage is Symbolic AI, which has a knowledge-based approach. The second stage, which is based on deep learning, is called the data-driven approach. The third stage is Generative Artificial Intelligence, which is a combination of the previous two approaches, namely, knowledge-based and data-driven2. Artificial Intelligence has transformed industries and altered human-technology interactions. Generative Artificial Intelligence(GAI) is a novel and developing subset of Artificial Intelligence that represents an intersection of creativity and computation, fundamentally altering the essence of creation3. Since the release date of ChatGPT in November 2022, Generative Artificial Intelligence with the ability to enhance, facilitate, and simplify complex procedures has become popular for both individuals and business organizations4–6. ChatGPT is a prominent and good example of generative AI, and numerous studies and research predict that generative AI will have potentially significant impacts on the way companies work and interact with customers7. McKinsey & Company predicts that Generative AI is set to drive the subsequent surge in productivity, with the potential to create between $2.6 trillion and $4.4 trillion in value across various industries8.

GAI systems have garnered considerable interest from various stakeholders, inciting vigorous discourse regarding their influence on business and society. Various fields such as business, education, Healthcare, and Content Creation have benefited from GAI9. One critical aspect of GAI is the theoretical and mathematical aspect, which plays an essential role in GAI models. The outputs of GAI are directly dependent on the models and machine learning techniques applied to it, which are mainly VAEs, GANs, transformers & autoencoders, and Large Language Models10. These models underpin various generative tools across multiple modalities, including text, image, code, video, speech, and music generation. Well-known instances include ChatGPT, Midjourney, Dall-E2, Copilot, Sora, and MusicLM, which are being launched by leading technology companies and emerging AI start-ups worldwide11. With this rapid advancement of this technology, it is essential to chart the evolving landscape of GAI12. In Particular, a patent analysis approach can be used to better understand the speed and direction of GAI progress and track innovation trajectories13,14. Since Patents are widely recognized as a reliable indicator of innovation and technological progress12, conducting patent analysis becomes an essential tool for identifying technology trends and gaps, monitoring advancements through time, and understanding technology landscapes15. Moreover, the integration of machine learning techniques into patent analysis has emerged as a cutting-edge approach, enabling researchers to extract deep insights from large and complex patent datasets16.

Some papers from the literature have studied the adoption of this technology in various sectors such as manufacturing, Operations, and Supply chain6,17,18, Service industry19, hospitality and tourism20, healthcare and medicine21,22, education23,24, Business and management innovation25–28. The impact of GAI on decision-making has been studied by29. Brüns and Meißner30 studied GAI in content creation and marketing. Alongside the benefits gained from GAI, ethical and legal issues have arisen regarding the use of this novel technology. Authorship and Academic Integrity, Regulatory Issues, Privacy, Trust, Bias, Misinformation, Deepfakes, Educational Ethics, Transparency and Accountability, Authenticity and Attribution, and Social and Economic Impact are the challenges of GAI31. GAI also has some technological issues that are worth mentioning. These issues include hallucination, training data quality, interpretability of GAI outputs, authenticity, and prompt engineering. For instance, hallucination refers to the generation of inaccurate or fabricated information by GAI models, which poses a challenge for reliability. Likewise, the quality and biases in training data significantly impact the fairness and accuracy of generated outputs. Furthermore, the interpretability of GAI outputs remains limited, making it difficult for users to understand or validate the reasoning behind generated content. These challenges are further compounded by the need for effective prompt engineering, where small changes in input prompts can lead to vastly different outcomes, raising concerns about consistency and control in real-world applications32–34.

Despite the fact that extensive attention has been paid to applications and implications in prior works, there is still a notable gap in understanding how GAI technologies themselves are evolving from a technological and innovation perspective. Particularly, technological trajectories reflected from patent data have been largely missed in the literature, which inhibits our understanding of future development paths of GAI tools and platforms. Although previous patent-based studies have examined technological evolution in domains such as blockchain35, Quantum technologies36, Internet of Things37, and Artificial Intelligence in construction38, Systematic efforts focusing on Generative AI remain scarce. To address this gap, this paper seeks to systematically map the technological evolution of Generative AI through patent analysis, integrating scientometric visualization techniques such as network mapping, co-classification analysis, and clustering of technological areas to identify dominant technological areas, track their evolution, and uncover emerging innovation clusters. This provides a conceptual foundation for analyzing the technological trajectory and identifying emerging innovation clusters within Generative AI. In contrast to prior works that primarily focused on sectoral adoption and ethical implications, this study shifts attention to a perspective that investigates the technological evolution of GAI by employing patent-based scientometric analysis, which is absent from the current discourse. Literature review

An overview of generative AI

Artificial Intelligence (AI) has emerged as one of the transformative technologies of the twenty-first century. It refers to the ability of machines to perform tasks that typically require human intelligence, such as learning, perception, reasoning, and decision-making. Over the past decade, AI has undergone significant developments, enabling machines to understand conversations, process images, and translate languages39. Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) refers to AI systems designed to create content, including text, image, audio, and video, by producing innovative and original outputs based on their training data. It represents a specialized domain within the broader field of artificial intelligence that empowers machines to autonomously produce original content, encompassing text, images, audio, and even programming code, derived from intricate patterns identified within extensive datasets. Unlike conversational AI, generative AI responds to queries and generates the content within those response40. In contrast to traditional AI, which predominantly emphasizes tasks such as analysis, classification, and prediction, Gen AI engages in the active generation of innovative outputs without the necessity for explicit programming directives41. This capability differentiates it from analytical AI and conventional machine learning, which rely on structured data to make decisions but do not produce entirely new content. With the advancements in AI during the Fourth Industrial Revolution, tools like ChatGPT have become increasingly popular, transforming the way content is produced and created42.

Understanding basic concepts such as natural language processing (NLP), large language models (LLMs), and Transformers is necessary to comprehend Gen AI technology better.

Simply put, it processes an input and generates an output, which may sometimes be in a different format. The applications of Gen AI span multiple domains, including business, healthcare, education, finance, and manufacturing. In healthcare, it facilitates pharmaceutical discovery and medical imaging analysis by producing molecular configurations and enhancing diagnostic accuracy46. In education, it powers personalized learning, adaptive assessments, and intelligent tutoring systems47. Gen AI has also disrupted creative fields like digital art, music, and content writing by generating human-like, high-quality outputs48. In business, it enhances customer service, marketing, and financial modeling, optimizing operations, and boosting productivity49. Despite its vast potential, Gen AI poses several challenges. Ethical concerns are prominent, particularly regarding misinformation, deepfakes, and intellectual property issues50,51. Socioeconomically, Gen AI’s rapid adoption has sparked debates on job displacement, with concerns that automation could replace creative and knowledge-based professions52. Additionally, the digital divide may widen as access to cutting-edge AI technologies remains limited to well-resourced organizations and economies31.

In conclusion, Generative AI represents a groundbreaking technological advancement with far-reaching implications across industries. While it fosters innovation and efficiency, the challenges associated with its implementation, ethics, and societal impact necessitate careful consideration. Addressing these challenges requires robust regulatory frameworks, ethical AI design principles, and interdisciplinary collaboration to ensure the responsible deployment of Gen AI across industries53.

To contextualize methodological approaches adopted in this study, scientometric and visualization techniques applied in other emerging technological domains have been reviewed. Liu et al.91 proposed an integrated approach combining scientific papers and patent data to map technology evolution paths, demonstrating that analyzing multiple data sources and performing gap analysis can enhance forecasting of technology trends and identification of innovation opportunities, as shown in their case study on all-solid-state lithium-ion batteries.

Technology evolves through a cumulative process, which is driven by knowledge. Hence, studying these knowledge-guided paths known as technology trajectories reveals the gradual direction of technological advancement and provides a foundation for technology forecasting92. Beyond the domain of generative AI, several studies based on scientometric and visualization have been conducted to map the technological trajectories of other emerging fields. Rawat and Yadav93 analyzed quantum computing applications across seven scientific domains using trends and visual analytics, uncovering key hotspots and emerging research frontiers. Similarly, Rawat and Yadav94 investigated the intellectual landscape of artificial intelligence within the domain of environmental sustainability, emphasizing thematic evolution, citation bursts, and the growing role of hybrid AI approaches. Rawat and Sharma95 mapped the knowledge structure of quantum computing, identifying influential contributors and collaboration networks, and Coccia96 examined technological trajectories in quantum computing using both scientific publications and patent data, identifying path-breaking directions such as quantum optics, quantum information, and quantum communication, thereby emphasizing the role of technology trajectory analysis in guiding industrial and societal transformation.

Although scientometric and patent-based mapping have been successfully applied to other domains such as blockchain, quantum computing, and circular economy technologies, comparable systematic efforts are scarce in the context of generative AI. Considering rapid advancement, diverse applications, and cross-sectoral impact of GenAI, a patent-based network analysis can provide valuable insights into emerging technological clusters, innovation hotspots, and maturity stages. This study seeks to address this gap by integrating patent data with scientometric visualization to comprehensively capture the technological evolution and innovation trajectories of GenAI.

While earlier works have applied patent analysis to domains such as blockchain, 5G, quantum computing, and the circular economy, no systematic effort has been made to investigate the technological trajectory of Generative AI through patent data. Addressing this gap, the present study integrates patent analysis with scientometric visualization to capture the evolution, maturity stages, and innovation hotspots of GenAI technologies.

Methodology

This study aims to conduct a conceptual analysis of generative artificial intelligence and identify technological trends by using text mining techniques, term weighting, network modeling, and community detection. This integrative approach was selected to leverage the strengths of both quantitative and qualitative methods. Therefore, the research process has been designed and implemented in several systematic and sequential steps, as seen in Fig. 1.

Research process.

Patent data collection and preparation

Patent retrieval from Lens.org

In the initial phase, the required data was collected from the Lens.org database. Lens is a reliable and openly accessible platform that integrates scholarly and patent information from various scientific and industrial fields. As one of the most reputable patent databases with global coverage, it has a convenient API for extracting structured data. Lens.org has been increasingly utilized in recent scientific studies and systematic reviews for retrieving and analyzing patent data due to its comprehensive coverage and advanced search capabilities104.

Extracted fields and data description

Among all the available information fields, the patent summary column (Abstract) was selected for textual analysis as a rich and concise text section that introduces the structure and innovation of the registered patent. Other columns of information, including publication date, priority numbers, technical classifications, and legal status, were also stored as auxiliary data, but the analytical focus of the study was solely on the content of the abstracts and the date of patent publication90.

Baseline method for textual analysis and semantic mapping framework

Text preprocessing and corpus cleaning

After collecting the data, text preprocessing was applied to the abstracts. This stage included initial cleaning of the text from language noises such as punctuation marks and unnecessary characters, converting all letters to lowercase, and removing stop words. To maintain the conceptual accuracy of the vocabulary, stemming or lemmatization was intentionally avoided. Thus, a clean version of the abstracts’ text was prepared to be the basis of linguistic analysis in the next steps. Text preprocessing was performed using standard NLP libraries in Python, including stopword lists and regular expressions.

Temporal analysis and inflection point identification

Next, to identify conceptual developments over time, a temporal distribution analysis of patents was performed. For this purpose, the number of registered patents was extracted by year, and a publication trend graph was generated. To better detect inflection points in the growth trajectory, the vertical axis of the graph was set to a logarithmic scale.

Analysis of the graph showed that 2016 was a significant change point in patent growth and possibly in technological leaps. Regarding the post-2016 era, the acceleration of generative AI developments following the public release of ChatGPT in late 2022 was a major motivation to further divide this period, to capture the impact of ChatGPT and similar products. Therefore, the data were divided into three periods, “before 2016”, “2016–2020”, and “2021–2025” to allow the comparison of conceptual trends in three different periods.

The TF–IDF model, which is based on the relative frequency of words in a document compared to their frequency across the corpus, assigns a statistical weight to each word. For each interval, the TF–IDF weight of all words was calculated, and a certain number of words with the highest score were selected as important keywords. These dominant expressions represented the core concepts of patents of each period. The TF–IDF method was applied to the abstracts at both the unigram (single-word) and bigram (two-word combinations) levels. For each temporal segment, different values of top-N keywords were examined to assess robustness. Specifically, the top N = 15 keywords per document were used in the main method, while N = 10 and N = 25 were additionally tested for sensitivity analysis.

Keyword extraction and semantic co-occurrence mapping

After extracting the keywords, the co-occurrence technique was applied to examine the semantic associations. Co-occurrence means the simultaneous appearance of two words in a document-level co-occurrence (in this research, patent abstract), which can be a sign of a semantic or functional relationship between those two words. In this study, keyword pairs were enumerated from among the keywords, and if they occurred simultaneously in at least one abstract, an edge between them was defined. This equation measures the number of documents in which two keywords co-occur. An edge is created between two nodes if their co-occurrence count exceeds a predefined threshold. To maintain the coherence and interpretability of the graph of the network, a minimum threshold of frequency was also considered to eliminate weaker relationships. The outcome of this process was a network of co-occurrence-based links between keywords for each period. The co-occurrence network was modeled as an undirected weighted graph, where each edge’s weight corresponded to the number of abstracts in which the keyword pair co-occurred. Word co-occurrence was computed at the document (abstract) level. An edge was established if two keywords appeared in the same abstract. During text preprocessing, frequent yet semantically non-informative words (such as domain-specific stopwords) were removed. The preprocessing phase included: (i) converting all text to lowercase, (ii) removing punctuation marks and special characters, and (iii) eliminating stopwords using an extended list combining the NLTK English stopword list with domain-specific terms. To preserve semantic granularity, stemming and lemmatization were intentionally avoided. The entire preprocessing pipeline was implemented in Python using the NLTK and re libraries. However, no minimum frequency threshold was applied during the construction of the co-occurrence network, and all remaining terms were included for link generation.

Community detection and network visualization with gephi

Then, these networks were prepared in the form of structured edge-node files for each period and transferred to the Gephi software environment. As one of the powerful tools in analyzing and visualizing complex networks, Gephi serves as a robust platform for network analysis and visualization. This software offers the Louvain algorithm, which was implemented for each network to identify conceptual clusters. The Louvain algorithm was selected for community detection due to its high scalability and ability to uncover densely connected clusters with optimal modularity. As reported in recent comparative studies105, Louvain demonstrates higher computational efficiency and scalability compared to alternative community detection techniques such as Girvan-Newman or label propagation. Comparison with baseline method

The baseline method is based on extracting keywords with TF–IDF and building a word co-occurrence network, then applying the Louvain algorithm to detect clusters. Unlike models based on numerical clustering, this method was able to provide conceptual clusters with higher resolution and greater interpretability. Discussion

In response to the first research question of identifying the dominant technological domains in generative AI patents, the semantic network results show that these domains have undergone significant evolution over time. Before 2016, the main focus was on concepts such as data, user, content, image, and device, which indicate a focus on data, user, and basic processing tools. In the 2016–2020 period, with the emergence of terms such as model, network, and set, the expansion of the fields of machine learning and neural networks is clearly visible. Finally, in the 2021–2025 interval, dominant terms such as image, data, user, video, and model have established their position, indicating the dominance of technologies related to image processing, big data, deep models, and interactive systems.

In general, it can be said that the dominant trend has moved from basic and data-driven structures to more complex and multi-state generative systems; A transformation that has been reflected both in the significant increase in network volume (more than threefold growth in nodes) and in the gradual decrease in modularity and cluster convergence.

Implications

Patent network analysis and conceptual clustering offer strategic insights into research gaps, investment avenues, and targeted technological policymaking. This study has led us to understand the conceptual evolution of generative AI technologies by comparing the pre- and post-2016 patent clusters. This comparison highlights the transition from early-stage neural and electromechanical clusters toward advanced generative and multimodal systems. Before 2016, clusters were centered on areas such as Web-Based Media and User Interfaces, Wearable and Sensor-Based Systems, and Electromechanical or Neuromorphic Architectures. In contrast, the post-2016 period, particularly after 2021, shows the dominance of clusters like Generative AI and Diffusion Models, Large Language Models and Knowledge-Enhanced AI, and Personalized Interactive XR Systems, reflecting the move from isolated component-level innovations to integrated, user-adaptive frameworks with direct societal and industrial impact.

Unlike many previous studies in the field of patent-based technology roadmapping, which have generally focused on simple document counting or using traditional clustering algorithms, this study was able to identify the conceptual structure of patents with higher accuracy by utilizing a network approach and calculating indicators such as modularity, density, and Jaccard similarity. Also, performing sensitivity analysis for the selection of the number of keywords, and comparing several clustering models (LDA, NMF, KMeans, and BERTopic) demonstrates the innovation of this study compared to similar studies.

Beyond foresight, the results support guiding investment strategies for startups and VCs to invest more efficiently. The study demonstrates that areas with dense patent activity often correlate with market maturity or growth potential. So, network visualizations and modularity analysis show concentrated clusters in personalized AI and automotive processing systems post-2016. Therefore, these can be interpreted as mature or competitive innovation spaces worth investment.

Along with investors, this study might also benefit regulators and research scientists. Regarding regulators, the study provides tools to identify high-impact innovations. High-impact technologies often appear in dense clusters with many connections. In this study, co-occurrence networks make it possible to flag technologies like autonomous control and synthetic media, helping regulatory bodies monitor them. For example, the results of this research could be used by regulators to track and monitor sensitive technologies such as deepfakes by identifying conceptual clusters related to image processing and multimedia content production, enabling policymakers to formulate more precise preventive and regulatory rules.

Besides, using the results section, R&D scientists can prioritize the efforts in their industrial labs. Labs must focus on problems with growing patent and competitive density, and the identified clusters help R&D teams align with the global innovation trajectory in AI. At the industrial level, large R&D companies could use the extracted clusters to prioritize investments in technologies such as large language models, interactive systems, or multimedia applications. This approach would align R&D strategy with global patenting trends.

Taken together, these implications demonstrate how network-based patent analysis serves as a multifaceted tool for navigating the rapidly evolving landscape of generative AI. The results of network analysis and conceptual clustering, in addition to providing a scientific picture of technology trends, also have direct practical applications: for example, generative artificial intelligence companies will be able to align their research and development path with market priorities by identifying keywords and focused areas in each time frame.

Limitations and future directions

Despite the breadth and depth of this study’s analysis of generative AI technology through large-scale patent network modeling, it is important to acknowledge certain limitations that shape the interpretation of the results and point toward future research opportunities.

A central limitation of the current work is its reliance exclusively on English-language patent documents. While English serves as the predominant language in the global technological landscape, this focus inevitably introduces a linguistic and geographical bias. Crucial insights from innovation hubs where patenting occurs primarily in other languages (such as China, Japan, or Germany) may be underrepresented. Given that countries like China are rapidly becoming leaders in generative AI research and commercialization, this linguistic constraint may obscure important regional trends and emergent clusters unique to non-English-speaking contexts. Future studies should consider integrating multilingual patent datasets, leveraging advances in machine translation and multilingual language models such as BERT or XLM-RoBERTa, to create a more globally inclusive and representative map of the generative AI innovation landscape. This would enable the identification of innovation hotspots, patterns of knowledge transfer, and region-specific priorities that are otherwise hidden in monolingual analyses.

Another limitation relates to the study’s focus on the technological evolution of generative AI in isolation, without systematically comparing it to developments in adjacent domains such as robotics, healthcare, cybersecurity, or autonomous systems. The contemporary innovation ecosystem is increasingly characterized by cross-domain convergence and mutual influence, where advances in generative AI both draw from and contribute to neighboring fields. A comparative, multi-domain patent analysis would reveal the extent to which the trajectories observed in generative AI are unique or part of broader technological paradigms, and would clarify the dynamics of technological spillover, co-evolution, and interdisciplinary innovation. Such analysis could also support policymakers and investors in identifying which generative AI subfields have the greatest potential for impact beyond their primary domain.

From a methodological standpoint, the study’s approach (combining TF–IDF-based keyword extraction, co-occurrence network modeling, and the Louvain community detection algorithm) offers a robust framework for capturing conceptual evolution. However, this method, like all clustering and topic modeling approaches, has its limitations. The reliance on TF–IDF may underrepresent the semantic richness and contextual nuance present in patent texts, especially as the field grows more complex. Although manual cluster labeling ensures interpretability, it introduces subjectivity and may hamper reproducibility. Future work could benefit from deploying advanced semantic embedding techniques (such as transformer-based embeddings, contextualized topic modeling, or graph neural networks) to automate the identification and labeling of clusters with greater depth and objectivity. Additionally, integrating other elements of patent documents (such as claims, citations, and technical drawings) would provide a more holistic understanding of technological novelty, impact, and the interconnectedness of innovation.

The temporal granularity of the analysis, while suitable for capturing long-term shifts and major inflection points, may mask more subtle or rapidly emerging trends, particularly following disruptive events such as the release of ChatGPT or breakthroughs in diffusion models. Finer-grained, perhaps annual or even quarterly, temporal analysis could reveal the micro-dynamics of concept emergence, cluster evolution, and technological convergence. This would be especially valuable for real-time technology forecasting and for stakeholders seeking to respond rapidly to paradigm shifts in the field.

Furthermore, while this study identifies the emergence of clusters related to societal and ethical issues (such as misinformation, trust, privacy, and regulatory compliance), it does not directly correlate patent evolution with external datasets, including investment flows, policy interventions, or startup activity. Future research could overlay patent network dynamics with data on venture capital investment, government regulation, or market adoption to better understand how technological, economic, and regulatory factors interact to drive or constrain innovation. Such integrative approaches would support more nuanced, actionable foresight for investors, policymakers, and industry leaders.

Finally, the findings underscore the growing convergence of technological, commercial, and ethical considerations in generative AI innovation. As patents increasingly address challenges such as content authenticity, privacy-preserving learning, and regulatory compliance, the boundaries between technical progress and societal impact are becoming blurred. Future directions should thus include not only methodological and data enhancements, but also cross-sectoral collaborations that bring together technologists, ethicists, legal scholars, and industry practitioners. Such collaborations are essential to ensure that the rapid evolution of generative AI is aligned with societal values, regulatory expectations, and the broader public interest.

In summary, while this study advances the understanding of generative AI’s technological evolution, addressing its current limitations (through multilingual analysis, cross-domain comparison, finer temporal resolution, advanced semantic methods, and integration with external datasets) will provide richer, more actionable insights. These efforts will support a more inclusive, responsive, and responsible roadmap for navigating the fast-changing landscape of generative AI.

Conclusion

This study provides a systematic and data-driven exploration of the technological evolution and innovation landscape of Generative Artificial Intelligence (GAI) through large-scale patent analysis. Methodologically, it contributes to the field by combining large-scale patent mining with scientometric visualization and community detection, a novel integration not previously applied to GAI. By leveraging advanced text mining, network modeling, and community detection techniques on over 20,000 patents, we have mapped the conceptual shifts, emerging domains, and innovation trajectories that have shaped GAI over the past decades. Our findings reveal a clear inflection point around 2016, marking a transition from early-stage, modular, and domain-specific innovations (such as neuromorphic computing, agricultural bioengineering, and electromechanical control systems) to a new era characterized by integrated generative frameworks, API-driven platforms, and multi-modal capabilities. This shift is quantitatively evidenced by a dramatic increase in patent volume, a threefold rise in conceptual diversity, and a significant decrease in network modularity, indicating the convergence and cross-pollination of previously distinct technological subdomains.

The post-2016 landscape is dominated by clusters focused on generative AI frameworks, asynchronous processing for autonomous systems, personalized interactive experiences, media authentication, and advanced agricultural technologies. Notably, the growing emphasis on content authenticity and user personalization reflects both the technological maturation of GAI and the rising societal and ethical concerns associated with synthetic media and AI-driven decision-making. From a strategic perspective, our patent network analysis offers actionable insights for R&D managers, policymakers, and investors. The identification of dense innovation clusters and emerging hubs can inform targeted investment, guide regulatory oversight, and support the alignment of research agendas with global technological trends. Furthermore, the methodological framework presented here (combining TF–IDF-based keyword extraction, co-occurrence network analysis, and community detection) demonstrates the value of patent analytics as a foresight tool for navigating complex and rapidly evolving technology landscapes.

However, this study is not without limitations. The reliance on English-language patents and TF–IDF-based keyword extraction may obscure semantic nuances and overlook innovations documented in other languages. The temporal granularity, limited to two broad periods, may mask more subtle or recent shifts, such as the post-2022 explosion of large language models. Manual cluster labeling introduces a degree of subjectivity, and future research should consider embedding-based semantic analysis and multilingual datasets for deeper and more nuanced insights. In addition to providing descriptive analysis, the suggested framework offers predictive capabilities that allow policymakers, business executives, and researchers to forecast future developments in generative AI and adjust their strategic plans to match these anticipated trends.

In summary, this research advances our understanding of the dynamic and interconnected ecosystem of generative AI technologies. It highlights the field’s rapid expansion, increasing complexity, and the convergence of innovation domains. By providing a structured roadmap of technological evolution, this work supports informed decision-making and responsible innovation, ensuring that the transformative potential of generative AI is harnessed for broad societal benefit while addressing emerging risks and challenges.

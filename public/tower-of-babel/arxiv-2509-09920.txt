Polarization-dependent Interference in Nonlinear Compton Scattering
Zu-dong Zhao, Suo Tang

Abstract
We investigate the phase interference effects in the nonlinear Compton scattering via the collision between an high-energy electron and the laser in the intermediate intensity region, and reveal that the importance of interference effects on the scattered photons depends sensitively on the relative polarization of the scattered photons and laser pulse. For scattered photons polarized parallel to the laser field, the effective distance between two interfered phase points is much larger than that for perpendicularly polarized photons. To signalize the phase interference effect in potential experiments, we introduce the double-pulse scenario for the scattering process, and show that the interference between two phase-separated pulse could significantly modulate the energy-momentum distribution of the scattered photons, leading to the reshaping of the scattered photons’ energy spectrum. By narrowing the angular acceptance of the scattered photons, the spectral modulation can be amplified and sustained for an experiment-realizable phase separation between two pulses.



I
Introduction

Compton scattering, as one of the fundamental processes in light-matter interactions, has served as a cornerstone in quantum electrodynamics (QED) since 1923
Compton 1923
.
It not only validates the particle nature of light
Evans 1958
, but also lays the groundwork for the generation of hard-photon source
Hajima 2016
.
Using a weak laser pulse with normalized intensity
\xi\ll 1
, the scattering process exhibits linearity and perturbation as elastic collision between a single laser photon and a high-energy electron with the cross-section given as Klein-Nishina formula
Berestetskii et al. 1982
;
Greiner and Reinhardt 2008
.
The current breakthrough of laser technique has brought the intensity to the highly relativistic regime with
\xi\gg 1

Lee et al. 2018
;
Li et al. 2018
, in which the acceleration of electrons within one laser cycle can improve their energy exceeding the static energy of electron
Olofsson and Gonoskov 2023
.
Colliding with such strong lasers, the linear and perturbative QED description of the Compton scattering fails as electrons are strongly dressed by the laser fields, and plenty of laser photons can contribute simultaneously to the scattering process,
i.e.
, nonlinear Compton scattering (NLC)
Di Piazza et al. 2012
;
Gonoskov et al. 2022
;
Fedotov et al. 2023
.

The NLC process has been theoretically investigated via the QED scattering theory
Nikishov and Ritus 1964
;
Seipt 2017
, semiclassical operator method
Wistisen 2014
;
Nielsen et al. 2022
, and particle-tracking Monte Carlo simulations
Gonoskov et al. 2015
;
Blackburn et al. 2023
;
Tang 2024
in various field configurations: monochromatic field
Brown and Kibble 1964
;
Ivanov et al. 2004
;
Harvey et al. 2009
;
Seipt and K?mpfer 2013
, constant crossed field
Ilderton et al. 2019
;
Di Piazza et al. 2019
;
Seipt and King 2020
and laser field with finite duration
Boca and Florescu 2009
;
Mackenroth et al. 2010
;
Heinzl et al. 2010
;
Mackenroth and Di Piazza 2011
;
Seipt and Kämpfer 2011
;
Tang et al. 2024
, bi-frequency
Seipt et al. 2019
and double-pulse structure
Wistisen 2014
;
Ilderton et al. 2020
. Experimentally, the NLC was first observed in the perturbative regime with up to four laser photons converted to a single
\gamma
photon by a high-energy electron of
46.6~\textrm{GeV}
colliding with a laser pulse of
\xi=0.6

Bula et al. 1996
;
Bamber et al. 1999
,
and recently was tested nonperturbatively as hundreds of laser photons were scattered into a single
\gamma
photon in the collision between laser-wakefield accelerated multi-GeV electron beams and laser pulses of
\xi>10

Mirzaie et al. 2024
.
The later experimental scenario has also been used to verify the quantum nature of radiation recoil induced by the scattering process
Cole et al. 2018
;
Poder et al. 2018
.

The upcoming laser-particle experiments, such as LUXE at DESY
Abramowicz et al. 2021
and E320 at SLAC
Meuren 2019
;
Salgado et al. 2021
, are designed to measure QED processes in the transition regime from the perturbative to nonperturbative with high resolution
Macleod 2022
,
using electron beams of
E_{p}\sim O(10)~\textrm{GeV}
and laser pulses in the intermediate intensity regime of
\xi\sim O(1)
.
With these parameters, the formation length of the scattering process could be comparable to the scale of the field variation
Ritus 1985
, and therefore, the quantum interference between different phase points in the field would play a critical role in the scattering process.
One of the typical characters resulting from these phase interference, is the harmonic structure in the spectrum of the scattered photons when the laser pulse has long-enough duration
Seipt and K?mpfer 2013
;
King and Tang 2020
.
This harmonic structure can, on one hand, be associated with the net number of laser photons joined in the scattering process, and on the other hand, be attributed to the constructive/destructive interference between different phase points in the scale of laser wavelength
Heinzl et al. 2020
.
Previous studies revealed that clear harmonic structure can only appear in the spectrum of the scattered photons in the polarization state parallel to the laser polarization
King and Tang 2020
, but not in the photon’s spectrum in the perpendicular polarization.
This implies the dependence of the phase interference on the relative polarization between the laser and scattered photons.

In this paper, we intend to figure out the polarization-dependence of this phase interference in the NLC process,
and find out measurable interference-induced signals with realistic experimental setups.
To this objective, we resort to the collision scenario between a high-energy electron and a linearly polarized laser with double pulses.
The similar scenario has been employed in Ref.
Ilderton et al. 2020
,
in which single-cycle pulses were used for observable interference rings in the momentum distribution of the scattered photons,
and claimed that for relatively long laser pulses, the interference signals would be smoothed out for the detector with reasonable resolution.
Here, we show that the interference between two multi-cycle pulses can not only modulate the energy-momentum distribution of the scattered photons, but also reshape the harmonic spectrum of the scattered photons.

The paper is organised as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we introduce briefly the theoretical model used for the investigation of NLC and reveal the relation between the phase interference and the harmonic structure in the photon’s spectrum. In Sec.
III
, we analyze the interference between two phase-separated pulses and give potential interference-induced signals in experiments, and at the end conclude in Sec.
IV
.
Throughout our discussion, the natural units
\hbar=c=1
is used.

II
THEORETICAL MODEL

We proceed our discussion with the parameters in the upcoming laser-particle experiments, such as LUXE
Abramowicz et al. 2021
, simplify the laser field as a plane wave with the scaled vector potential
a^{\mu}(\phi)=|e|A^{\mu}(\phi)
propagating with the wavevector
k^{\mu}=\omega_{0}(1,0,0,-1)
and carrier frequency
\omega_{0}=1.55~\textrm{eV}
,
and calculate the energy- and momentum-distributions of the photons scattered by a high-energy electron with the energy parameter
\eta=k\cdot p/m^{2}
,
where
\phi=k\cdot x
is the phase of laser pulse,
|e|
,
m
and
p^{\mu}
are respectively the absolute charge, mass and momentum of the electron.

We employ direct QED calculations for the NLC process in order to resolve the phase interference in the scale close to and/or much longer than the laser wavelength. These interference are much beyond the local approximations: the locally constant field approximation (LCFA) is blind to the phase interference
Harvey et al. 2015
and the locally monochromatic approximation (LMA) can only resolve the interference in the scale of the laser wavelength
Heinzl et al. 2020
.
The triple-differential probability for the scattered photon linearly polarized in the plane perpendicular to the laser propagating direction can be formulated as
Tang et al. 2020

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}P_{x,y}}{\mathrm{d}s\mathrm{d}^{2}\bm{r}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{(2\pi\eta)^{2}}\frac{s}{1-s}\left(\frac{s^{2}}{8(1-s)}|w_{0}|^{2}+\left|w_{x,y}\right|^{2}\right)\,,

(1)

where
\alpha
is the fine-structure constant,

s=k\cdot\ell/k\cdot p
, the fraction of the lightfront momentum taken by the scattered photon with momentum
\ell^{\mu}
from the incoming electron, and
\bm{r}=r(\cos\psi,\sin\psi)=\bm{\ell}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}/sm-\bm{p}_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}/m
, photon momenta perpendicular to the laser propagating direction, scaling the angular spread
\ell_{{\scriptscriptstyle\perp}}/sm=m\eta/\omega_{0}\tan(\theta/2)
along the incoming electron momentum.
|w_{0}|^{2}=2|\bm{F}|^{2}-S^{\ast}I-SI^{\ast}
is the unpolarized part relating to the square of the laser field,
and
\bm{w}(\phi)=(w_{x},w_{y})=\bm{r}I-\bm{F}
denotes the part in the amplitude level relating to the polarization of laser field
\bm{F}
.
The polarized/unpolarized parts are calculated in term of the integrals
Ilderton et al. 2020
, as

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle=\int\!\mathrm{d}\phi\,\left[1-\ell\cdot\pi_{p}(\phi)/\ell\cdot p\right]~e^{i\Phi(\phi)}\,,

(2a)

\displaystyle\bm{F}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\int\!\mathrm{d}\phi~\bm{a}(\phi)~e^{i\Phi(\phi)}\,,

(2b)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m^{2}}\int\!\mathrm{d}\phi~\bm{a}^{2}(\phi)~e^{i\Phi(\phi)}\,,

(2c)

over the laser phase
\phi
, where
\Phi(\phi)=\int^{\phi}_{\phi_{i}}\mathrm{d}\phi^{\prime}\ell\cdot\pi_{p}(\phi^{\prime})/[(1-s)\eta m^{2}]
is the phase integral starting from
\phi_{i}
at which the laser turns on, and
\pi^{\mu}_{p}=p^{\mu}+a^{\mu}-k^{\mu}[p\cdot a/k\cdot p+a^{2}/(2k\cdot p)]
is the electron’s instantaneous momentum in a plane wave. In (
1
), the spin of the recoiled (initial) electron has been summed (averaged).

The contribution of the interference between different field points can be clearly seen as the cross terms between different parts of the phase integrals (
2
) when calculating their modulus squares in the scattering probability (
1
).
The modulus square of the integrals in (
2
) would bring two integrals over the laser phase as
|\int\mathrm{d}\phi~(\cdots)|^{2}=\int\mathrm{d}\phi\int\mathrm{d}\phi^{\prime}(\cdots)
into the scattering probabilities.
To manifest the effective distance between two interfered phase points, we write the two phase integrals as

\int\mathrm{d}\phi\int\mathrm{d}\phi^{\prime}\to\int\mathrm{d}\varphi\int\mathrm{d}\vartheta,

where
\varphi=(\phi+\phi^{\prime})/2
is the average phase, and
\vartheta=\phi-\phi^{\prime}
is the so-called interference phase
Dinu et al. 2016
;
Seipt 2017
, usually used as the formation length of the scattering process
Ritus 1985
, and can be used to quantify the phase distance between two interfered field points.

In the LCFA algorithm, widely employed in the ultrahigh intensity regime of
\xi\gg 1

Gonoskov et al. 2015
;
Gonoskov et al. 2022
,
the effects of the phase interference are excluded under the assumption of
\vartheta\ll 1
, and the average phase
\varphi
is regarded classically as the local phase of the field, at where the photon is emitted
King and Tang 2020
.
In our concerned intermediate intensity regime,
\varphi
and
\vartheta
are the quantum phase for the scattering process and actually unobservable in experiments.
Below, we employ phase distributions of the scattering probabilities to analyze the polarization-dependence of the phase interference, and resort to the energy-momentum distributions of the outgoing photons as potential experimental observables to signalize the interference effects.

III
Interference between two pulses

The phase interference is a nonlocal effect and could happen between all the points in the background field.
To analyze the polarization-dependence of the interference and quantify the phase interference effect straightforwardly, we consider a laser as

a^{\mu}(\phi)=a^{\mu}_{1}(\phi)+a^{\mu}_{2}(\phi)
comprised of
two
pulses separated with the phase gap
\Delta=\phi_{2i}-\phi_{1f}
, where
\phi_{1f}
is the ending of the first pulse
a^{\mu}_{1}(\phi)
and
\phi_{2i}
is the starting of the second pulse
a^{\mu}_{2}(\phi)
.
The phase integrals in (
2
) can then be split into two parts and written formally, e.g., as
Ilderton et al. 2020

\displaystyle\bm{F}=\bm{F}_{1}+e^{i\Phi_{f}}\bm{F}_{2}

(3)

where
\bm{F}_{1}
(
\bm{F}_{2}
) denotes the field contribution from the first (second) pulse, and
\Phi_{f}=\Phi(\phi_{1f})+\Delta(1+r^{2})s/[2\eta(1-s)]
is the accumulated phase before the starting of the second pulse with the term
\Phi(\phi_{1f})
from the first pulse and the second term from the phase gap.
The interference between two pulses can then be simply seen as the cross terms
\bm{F}^{*}_{1}\bm{F}_{2}e^{i\Phi_{f}}+\bm{F}_{1}\bm{F}^{*}_{2}e^{-i\Phi_{f}}
in the modulus square of
|\bm{F}|^{2}

Ilderton et al. 2020
.
Analogous expressions can be given for
I
and
S

1

III.2
Interference-induced observables

We point out that for the total scattering probability, the interference contribution induced by the two pulses is much smaller than the independent contribution from each single pulse, while this interference could significantly modulate the spectral properties of the outgoing photons, which could be used as the potential experiment observables to signalize this interference effect.

In Fig.

To understand how the double-pulse interference effect modulate the photon spectra, we can write the two-pulse interference analytically.
For the pulse profile in Fig.
5

\displaystyle\Phi_{f}=\frac{s}{2\eta(1-s)}[(1+r^{2})(2N\pi+\Delta)+3N\pi\xi^{2}/8]\,.

(4)

We point out that for few-cycle pulses, i.e.,
\int\bm{a}_{1}\mathrm{d}\phi\neq 0

For the two identical pulses,
i.e.
,

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}P_{x,y}}{\mathrm{d}s~\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{\textrm{double}}=2(1+\cos\Phi_{f})\left.\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}P_{x,y}}{\mathrm{d}s~\mathrm{d}r}\right|_{\textrm{single}}\,,

(5)

in which the interference between the two pulses can be clearly seen as the factor
\cos\Phi_{f}

By dropping the interference factor
\cos\Phi_{f}

the variation of
\Phi_{f}

and
\mathrm{d}\Phi_{f}/\mathrm{d}r

and

As the interference between two perpendicularly polarized pulses is much weaker than that between two parallel polarized pulses,

IV
Conclusion

We investigate the phase interference effects in the nonlinear Compton scattering in the collision between an high-energy electron and a laser field with linear polarization,
and reveal that the importance of the interference effects on the scattered photons depends sensitively on the relative polarization of the scattered photons and laser pulse. For scattered photons in the parallel polarization to the laser field, the effective distance between two interfered phase points is much larger than that for perpendicularly polarized photons.

The spectral properties of the scattered photons could be significantly modulated by the phase interference effect.
For a single-pulse laser with long-enough duration, the constructive interference between different field points results in the harmonic peak in the energy spectrum of the parallel polarized photons.
For the laser with two phase-separated pulses, the interference between the two pulses could modulate the energy-momentum distribution of the scattered photons, and reshape the energy spectrum of the scattered photons in the parallel polarization, while the spectral shape of the photons in the perpendicular polarization is almost same as that in the single-pulse case.

The interference-induced modulation into the photons’ energy-momentum distribution depends sensitively on the scattering angle of the photons.
By narrowing the angular acceptance of the scattered photons, the interference fluctuation in the photons’ energy spectrum can be effectively amplified and sustains for an experiment-realizable phase separation between two pulses.

To observe the harmonic spectral reshaping, the laser pulses with multiple cycles should be employed in the intermediate intensity regime of
\xi\sim O(1)
, in which the upcoming experiments such as E320 and LUXE are designed.

To observe robust interference signals in experiments, one should consider not a single electron, but a bunch of energetic electrons, which could be modeled by incoherently integrating the differential probability with the momentum distribution of the electron bunch
Angioi et al. 2016
.
Therefore, the energy spread of the electron bunch must be narrow enough in order to resolve the interference-induced spectral fluctuations.
For the electron bunch with high density and broad momentum distribution, the coherent emission from the bunch of electrons should also be incorporated in future analysis
Angioi and Di Piazza 2018
;
Dillon and King 2019
.

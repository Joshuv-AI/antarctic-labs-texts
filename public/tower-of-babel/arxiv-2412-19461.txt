On tight tree-complete hypergraph Ramsey numbers
Jiaxi Nie

Abstract
Chvátal showed that for any tree T T with k k edges the Ramsey number R ⁡ ( T , n ) = k ⁡ ( n − 1 ) + 1 R(T,n)=k(n-1)+1 (“Tree‐complete graph Ramsey numbers.” Journal of Graph Theory 1.1 (1977): 93-93). For r = 3 r=3 or 4 4 , we show that, if T T is an r r -uniform non-trivial tight tree, then the hypergraph Ramsey number R ⁡ ( T , n ) = Θ ⁡ ( n r − 1 ) R(T,n)=\Theta(n^{r-1}) . The 3-uniform result comes from observing a construction of Cooper and Mubayi. The main contribution of this paper is the 4-uniform construction, which is inspired by the Cooper-Mubayi 3-uniform construction.



1
Introduction

Let
F
be an
r
uniform hypergraph (
r
-graph). An independent set of
F
is a set of vertices containing no edge of
F
. The
independence number
of
F
, denoted
\alpha(F)
, is the maximum size of an independent set of
F
. The
Ramsey number

R(F,n)
is the minimum integer
N
such that every
F
-free
N
-vertex
r
-graph has an independent set of size
n
. Determining or estimating
R(F,n)
is a central topic in extremal combinatorics.

Chavátal
[
2
]
showed that for any tree
T
with
k
edges the Ramsey number
R(T,n)=k(n-1)+1
. In this short paper, we extend this result to hypergraph
tight tree
.

Definition 1
.

An
r
-graph
T
is a tight
r
-tree if its edges can be ordered as
e_{1},\dots,~e_{t}
so that

\forall i\geq 2~\exists v\in e_{i}~and~1\leq s\leq i-1~such~that~v\not\in\cup_{j=1}^{i-1}e_{j}~and~e_{i}-v\subset e_{s}.

(1)

A hypergraph
H
is
non-trivial
if no vertex is contained in all of its edges, i.e.
\cap_{e\in H}e=\emptyset
; otherwise, it is
trivial
. The main result of this paper is the following:

Theorem 1.1
.

For
r=3
or
4
, if
T
is a non-trivial tight
r
-tree, then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, for every positive integer
n
,

c_{1}n^{r-1}\leq R(T,n)\leq c_{2}n^{r-1}

We believe this result should extend to all
r
.

Conjecture 1
.

For
r\geq 5
, if
T
is a non-trivial tight
r
-tree, then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
such that, for every positive integer
n
,

c_{1}n^{r-1}\leq R(T,n)\leq c_{2}n^{r-1}

Note that the bounds in
Theorem

1.1
could be wrong when
T
is not non-trivial. In particular, when
T
is a tight
r
-tree with two edges, Phelps and Rödl
[
10
]
and Rödl and Šinajová
[
11
]
showed that
R(T,n)=\Theta(n^{r-1}/\log n)
.

In fact, it is easy to show that for every tight
r
-tree
T

\frac{c_{1}n^{r-1}}{\log n}\leq R(T,n)\leq c_{2}n^{r-1}.

The upper bounds come from the random deletion method (see Section 2 for details) and the lower bounds can be obtained from analyzing the Erdös-Rényi random hypergraph using the Lovász Local Lemma (See
[
1
, Chapter 5]
for example). Thus the main difficulty in proving
Theorem

1.1
lies in finding constructions that establish the lower bounds. For
r=3
, we make use of a construction discovered by Cooper and Mubayi
[
3
]
. For
r=4
, we find a new construction, which is inspired by the Cooper-Mubayi 3-uniform construction. Surprisingly, for
r=5
, natural generalizations of previous constructions do not work anymore; see the first bullet point in the concluding remarks section for more discussions on this.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows. In
Section

2
, we prove the upper bounds in
Theorem

1.1
. In
Section

3
, we introduce the Cooper-Mubayi construction, which establishes the 3-uniform lower bounds in
Theorem

1.1
. In
Section

4
, we describe the 4-uniform construction, which implies the 4-uniform lower bounds in
Theorem

1.1
.

Upper bounds

In this section, we prove the upper bounds in
Theorem

1.1
. Kalai made the following conjecture for tight trees extending a conjecture of Erdős and Sós for graph trees.

Conjecture 2

(
[
4
,
5
]
)
.

Let

be a tight

-tree with
k
edges. An
n
-vertex

-free

-graph has at most
\frac{k-1}{r}\binom{n}{r-1}
edges.

Although the conjecture has not been fully resolved, much progress has been made towards it
[

,
9
,
8
]
. For the purpose of this paper, we will only use the following folklore upper bounds, as we don’t try to optimize the constant factor.

Proposition 2.1

(Proposition 5.4,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
T
be a tight
r
-tree with
k
edges. An
n
-vertex
T
-free
r
-graph has at most
({k-1})\binom{n}{r-1}
edges.

Now we are ready to prove the upper bounds in
Theorem

1.1

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
r\geq 2
and let
T
be a tight
r
-tree with
k
edges, then

R(T,n)\leq 2(k-1)\left(\frac{2en}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}.

Proof.

Let
N=2(k-1)\left(\frac{2en}{r-1}\right)^{r-1}
and let
p=\left(\frac{N}{2(k-1)\binom{N}{r-1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-1}}
. Let
H
be an
N
-vertex
T
-free
r
-graph. By
Proposition

2.1
we know that
H
has at most
(k-1)\binom{N}{r-1}
edges. We randomly select a subset
U\subseteq V(H)
where each vertex is included independently with probability
p
. Then for each edge in
H[U]
, the induced subgraph of
H
on
U
, we arbitrarily delete a vertex, and we call the remaining independent set
U^{\prime}
. The expected size of
U^{\prime}
is at least

pN-(k-1)\binom{N}{r-1}p^{r}=\frac{pN}{2}\geq\frac{r-1}{2e}\left(\frac{N}{2(k-1)}\right)^{\frac{1}{r-1}}=n.

Thus we know that
\alpha(H)\geq n
, which comletes the proof.

∎

the

of this edge

Proposition 3.1
.

Every
2n
-element set in
[n]^{2}
contains an edge in
H_{3}
.

Proof.

Let
X
be a
2n
-element set in
[n]^{2}
. Consider a bipartite graph
B
on
R\sqcup C
where
R=\{r_{1},r_{2},\dots,r_{n}\}
and
C=\{c_{1},c_{2},\dots,c_{n}\}
such that
r_{i}
and
c_{j}
form an edge if and only if
(i,j)\in X
. We can view
R
and
C
as the set of rows and the set of columns of the
n\times n
grid respectively. Note that the number of edges in
B
is
|X|=2n
which equals the number of vertices in
B
. So
B
contains a cycle. In the cycle, there must be three consecutive edges
c_{j^{\prime}}r_{i}
,
r_{i}c_{j}
,
c_{j}r_{i^{\prime}}
such that
i^{\prime}>i
and
j^{\prime}>j
. Clearly, such a triple of edges corresponds to a triple of points in
X
which forms an edge in
H_{3}
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

For any
e,e^{\prime}\in H_{3}
, if
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=2
, then they have the same center.

Proof.

Let
e=\{P_{0},P_{1},P_{2}\}
and
e^{\prime}=\{P^{\prime}_{0},P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2}\}
where
P_{0}
and
P^{\prime}_{0}
are the centers of
e_{1}
and
e_{2}
respectively. Further, for
0\leq i\leq 2
, let
P_{i}=(x_{i},y_{i})
and let
P^{\prime}_{i}=(x^{\prime}_{i},y_{i}^{\prime})
. Without loss of generality, suppose
x_{1}=x_{0}
, and
x^{\prime}_{1}=x^{\prime}_{0}
. Then by definition we have
y_{1}>y_{0}
,
y^{\prime}_{1}>y^{\prime}_{0}
,
y_{2}=y_{0}
,
y^{\prime}_{2}=y^{\prime}_{0}
,
x_{2}>x_{0}
and
x^{\prime}_{2}>x^{\prime}_{0}
. Suppose (for contradiction)
P_{0}\not=P^{\prime}_{0}
. Up to symmetry, the proof splits into two cases.

Case 1:
P_{0}=P^{\prime}_{1}
.
In this case, we have
y_{1},y_{2}\geq y_{0}=y^{\prime}_{1}>y^{\prime}_{0},y^{\prime}_{2}
, and thus
\{P_{1},P_{2}\}\cap\{P_{0}^{\prime},P_{2}^{\prime}\}=\emptyset
, which contradicts the condition
|e\cap e^{\prime}|=2
.

Case 2:
P_{0}\not\in e^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}_{0}\not\in e
.
Since
e\cap e^{\prime}|=2
, we have
e\cap e^{\prime}=\{P_{1},P_{2}\}=\{P^{\prime}_{1},P^{\prime}_{2}\}
. Note that
x_{1}<x_{2}
and
x^{\prime}_{1}<x^{\prime}_{2}
. This implies that
P_{1}=P^{\prime}_{1}
and
P_{2}=P^{\prime}_{2}
, and hence
P_{0}=P^{\prime}_{0}
, contradicting the assumption that
P_{0}\not=P^{\prime}_{0}
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

H_{3}
does not contain any non-trivial tight
3
-tree.

Proof.

Let
T
be a tight
3
-tree in
H_{3}
. Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
be an order on the edges of

that satisfies
Equation

1
. Note that,

,
|e_{i}\cap e_{i+1}|=2
. Thus by
Proposition

3.2
all edges
e_{1},\dots,e_{t}
share the same center, which implies that

is trivial.
∎

The 3-uniform lower bounds in

and
Proposition

F_{21}(P^{\prime}_{3})<F_{21}(P_{0}^{\prime})=F_{21}(P_{3})<\min\{F_{21}(P_{0}),F_{21}(P_{1}),F_{21}(P_{2})\}.

Thus
P_{3}^{\prime}\not\in e
, and hence
\{P_{1}^{\prime},P_{2}^{\prime}\}\subseteq e
.

Note that
P^{\prime}_{0}(2)=P_{3}(2)\leq P_{0}(2)
. So we have either
P^{\prime}_{0}(2)<P_{0}(2)
or
P^{\prime}_{0}(2)=P_{0}(2)
. If
P^{\prime}_{0}(2)<P_{0}(2)
, then
P^{\prime}_{2}(2)=P_{0}^{\prime}(2)<P_{0}(2)=P_{2}(2)\leq P_{1}(2)
, which implies
P^{\prime}_{2}\not=P_{0},~P_{1}
or
P_{2}
. Further,
P^{\prime}_{2}\not=P^{\prime}_{0}=P_{3}
. So we have
P^{\prime}_{2}\not\in e
, a contradiction. Thus we have
P_{0}^{\prime}(2)=P_{3}(2)=P_{0}(2)
. By definition of
T_{3}
we must have
P_{0}^{\prime}(1)=P_{3}(1)<P_{0}(1)
, and hence
P^{\prime}_{1}(1)=P_{0}^{\prime}(1)<P_{0}(1)=P_{1}(1)\leq P_{2}(1)
, which implies
P^{\prime}_{1}\not=P_{0},~P_{1}
or
P_{2}
. Further,
P^{\prime}_{1}\not=P^{\prime}_{0}=P_{3}
. We conclude that
P^{\prime}_{1}\not\in e
, again a contradiction.
∎

Proposition 4.4
.

H_{4}
does not contain any non-trivial tight

-tree.

Proof.

Concluding remarks

•

Observe that in our 4-uniform construction,

, when restricted to points with fixed

, we let

be directed graphs on

defined by one of the

for all

.

•

Note that our construction is in some sense not symmetric. The asymmetry here is in fact necessary. To see this, let’s consider changing the definitions of

,

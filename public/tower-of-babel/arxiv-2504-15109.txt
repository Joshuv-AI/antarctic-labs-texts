New Heintze-Karcher type inequalities in sub-static warped product manifolds
Haizhong Li, Yong Wei, Botong Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove Heintze-Karcher type inequalities involving the shifted mean curvature for smooth bounded domains in certain sub-static warped product manifolds. In particular, we prove a Heintze-Karcher-type inequality for non mean-convex domains in the hyperbolic space. As applications, we obtain uniqueness results for hypersurfaces satisfying a class of curvature equations.

53C42, 53C24, 53C21

1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
denote the
(n+1)
-dimensional Euclidean space. By using the moving plane method, Alexandrov
[
1
]
proved that geodesic spheres are the only embedded closed hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, which is known as the famous Alexandrov theorem. Later, by establishing new integral formulas on closed hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, Reilly
[
23
]
gave a new proof of the Alexandrov theorem. Moreover, inspired by the volume estimate by Heintze and Karcher
[
12
]
, Ros
[
24
]
gave another proof of the Alexandrov theorem by establishing the following Heintze-Karcher inequality for closed, mean-convex hypersurfaces in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
, and assume that the mean curvature
p_{1}(\kappa)
of
\Sigma
is positive. Then

(1.1)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{p_{1}(\kappa)}d\mu\geq(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega).

Equality holds in (
1.1
) if and only if
\Sigma
is umbilic, and thus
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

For the Heintze-Karcher type inequality in space forms, a breakthrough was due to Brendle
[
7
]
. Using the unit normal flow with respect to a conformal metric, he proved a Heintze-Karcher type inequality in certain warped product manifolds. In particular, he proved the following inequality in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and hemisphere
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
{\Sigma}=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}
). Fix a point
p\in\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
(resp.
\mathbb{S}^{n+1}
), and let
\lambda^{\prime}=\cosh r(x)
(resp.
\cos r(x)
), where
r(x)=dist(x,p)
is the distance function to
p
. Assume that the mean curvature
p_{1}(\kappa)
of
\Sigma
is positive. Then

(1.2)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}}{p_{1}(\kappa)}d\mu\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv.

Equality holds in (
1.2
) if and only if
\Sigma
is umbilic, and thus
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. The inequality (
1.2
) was later proved by using the generalized Reilly’s formula, see
[
17
,
22
]
. Similar as the Euclidean case, Brendle’s Heintze-Karcher inequality was used to prove Alexandrov type theorem for embedded CMC hypersurfaces in warped product spaces
[
7
]
. It was also used to prove Minkowski and isoperimetric type inequalities in various warped product manifolds, see for example
[
5
,
9
,
10
,
25
]
.

We call an
n
-tuple
\tilde{\kappa}=(\tilde{\kappa}_{1},\ldots,\tilde{\kappa}_{n})
the
shifted principal curvatures
of a hypersurface
\Sigma
if there is a constant
\varepsilon
such that
\tilde{\kappa}_{i}=\kappa_{i}-\varepsilon
,
1\leq i\leq n
, where
\kappa_{i}
are the principal curvatures of
\Sigma
. The study of curvature flows and geometric inequalities involving shifted principal curvatures in curved ambient spaces especially in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
has been attractive in the recent years. See for example
[
3
,
4
,
19
]
for fully nonlinear contracting curvature flows with velocity involving shifted principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}-\varepsilon
where
\varepsilon
depends on the sectional curvature lower bound of the ambient manifolds, and
[
2
,
13
,
16
,
27
]
for curvature flows and geometric inequalities in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
involving shifted principal curvatures
\kappa_{i}-1
. The shifted factor
\varepsilon
is introduced usually in order to overcome the negative curvature of the background space.

The following Heintze-Karcher type inequality involving the shifted mean curvature was proved by Li-Xu
[
16
]
in the curve case (
n=1
) and by Hu-Wei-Zhou
[
15
]
for
n\geq 2
. It was used to obtain a uniqueness result for solutions to the horospherical
p
-Minkowski problem introduced in
[
16
]
, see
[
15
, Theorem 1.3]
.

Theorem A

(
[
16
,
15
]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Assume that the mean curvature
p_{1}(\kappa)
of
\Sigma
satisfies
p_{1}(\kappa)>1
. Then

(1.3)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-u}{p_{1}(\kappa)-1}d\mu\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv,

where
u=\langle\sinh r\partial_{r},\nu\rangle
is the support function of
\Sigma
, and
\nu
is the outward unit normal of
\Sigma
. Equality holds in (
1.3
) if and only if
\Sigma
is umbilic, and thus
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

The main purpose of this paper is to discover more Heintze-Karcher type inequalities involving the shifted mean curvature in hyperbolic space and in more general sub-static warped product manifolds, and then discuss their applications.

1.1.
Heintze-Karcher type inequalities for non mean-convex domains in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}

Using the unit normal flow, we first prove the following new Heintze-Karcher type inequality for domains which are not necessarily mean-convex in hyperbolic space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Assume that the mean curvature
p_{1}(\kappa)
of
\Sigma
satisfies
p_{1}(\kappa)>-1
. Then

(1.4)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}+u}{p_{1}(\kappa)+1}d\mu\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv.

Equality holds in (
1.4
) if and only if
\Sigma
is umbilic, and thus
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

Let
p_{k}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
denote the normalized
k
th elementary symmetric polynomial (see (
2.1
)). For a given hypersurface, we use
(h_{ij})
and
(g_{ij})
to denote its second fundamental form and induced metric, respectively. As an application of Theorem
1.1
, we prove the following uniqueness result for the solutions to a class of curvature equations.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. Let
\chi:[1,\infty)\times(-\infty,0)\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
-smooth function with
\partial_{1}\chi\leq 0
and
\partial_{2}\chi\geq 0
. Assume that
\Sigma
satisfies the following equation

(1.5)

p_{k}(\kappa+1)=\chi\left(\lambda^{\prime},-\lambda^{\prime}-u\right).

(1)

If
\partial_{2}\chi\not\equiv 0
and
h_{ij}>-g_{ij}
on
\Sigma
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. Moreover, if one of the inequalities
\partial_{1}\chi\leq 0
and
\partial_{2}\chi\geq 0
is strict, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at
p
.

(2)

If
\partial_{2}\chi\equiv 0
and
p_{k}(\kappa+1)>0
on
\Sigma
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. Moreover, if
\partial_{1}\chi<0
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at
p
.

Since there always exists some point in a closed hypersurface
\Sigma\subset\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
at which the principal curvatures are all greater than
1
, the following Alexandrov type theorem is a direct consequence of the second case of Theorem
1.2
.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. If the shifted
k
th mean curvature
p_{k}(\kappa+1)
is constant on
\Sigma
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

For
k=1
, Corollary
1.3
reduces to the Alexandrov theorem for embedded closed hypersurfaces with constant mean curvature in hyperbolic space
[
20
]
.

1.2.
Heintze-Karcher type inequalities in sub-static warped product manifolds

Given (
1.2
), Theorem A and Theorem
1.1
, it is natural to ask if there are Heintze-Karcher type inequalities corresponding to a general shifted factor
\varepsilon
in certain warped product manifolds so that these results can be regarded as its special cases, see (
1.12
) below. But it seems a challenge to use the flow method to get such a geometric inequality directly, even in the space forms (see the discussions in Remark
3.2
). However, by imposing certain convexity hypotheses on the hypersurfaces and using a different approach, we can prove this kind of Heintze-Karcher type inequalities in certain warped product manifolds, see Theorem
1.4
below.

To illustrate our approach, recall the following special case of the Minkowski’s second inequality in convex geometry, see e.g.
[
26
, Theorem 7.2.1]
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth, strictly convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
. Then

(1.6)

{\rm Area}(\Sigma)^{2}\geq(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)\int_{\Sigma}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu.

Equality holds in (
1.6
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a round sphere. In this setting, one may observe that inequality (
1.6
) implies (
1.1
):

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{p_{1}(\kappa)}d\mu\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega)}{{\rm Area}(\Sigma)^{2}\ }\int_{\Sigma}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{p_{1}(\kappa)}d\mu

(1.7)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(n+1)\mathrm{Vol}(\Omega),

where we used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality.

Let
M^{n+1}=[0,\bar{r})\times N
be a warped product manifold with metric
\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda(r)^{2}g_{N}
, where
N
is an
n
-dimensional closed manifold. By using a generalized Reilly’s formula, Li-Xia
[
17
]
proved the following Minkowski type inequality (Theorem B) for static-convex domains when the ambient space
M^{n+1}
is sub-static with potential
\lambda^{\prime}(r)
(see Section
2.2
). Here, we call a smooth hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M^{n+1}

static-convex
if the inequality (see
[
6
]
)

(1.8)

h_{ij}\geq\frac{\bar{\nabla}_{\nu}\lambda^{\prime}}{\lambda^{\prime}}

holds everywhere on
\Sigma
, where
\bar{\nabla}
denotes the connection on
(M^{n+1},\bar{g})
.

Theorem B

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
M^{n+1}=[0,\bar{r})\times N

(n\geq 2)
be a sub-static warped product manifold with metric
\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda(r)^{2}g_{N}
and potential
\lambda^{\prime}(r)
, where
(N,g_{N})
is an
n
-dimensional closed manifold.

(1)

Let
\Omega\subset M^{n+1}
be a bounded domain with a connected, static-convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
on which
\lambda^{\prime}>0
. Then

(1.9)

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu.

Moreover, if inequality (
1.8
) holds strictly at some point in
\Sigma
, and equality holds in (
1.9
), then
\Sigma
is umbilic and of constant mean curvature.

(2)

Assume
\lambda(r)
satisfies condition
H
(see Section
2.2
). Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\cup\partial M
. Assume in addition that
\Sigma
is static-convex. Then

(1.10)

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq(n+1)\left(\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv+\frac{1}{n+1}\lambda(0)^{n+1}\mathrm{Vol}(N,g_{N})\right)\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu.

Moreover, if inequality (
1.8
) holds strictly at some point in
\Sigma
, then equality holds in (
1.10
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a slice
\{r\}\times N
for some
r\in(0,\bar{r})
.

Since space forms are special types of sub-static warped product manifolds, Theorem B recovers a previous result of Xia
[
28
]
when
M^{n+1}\in\{\mathbb{H}^{n+1},\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}\}
. Remark that
\Sigma
is static-convex when it is horo-convex (
\kappa\geq 1
) and
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
, and when it is convex (
\kappa\geq 0
) and
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{S}^{n+1}_{+}

[
28
]
. We also note that the inequality (
1.8
) holds strictly at some point on
\Sigma
when
M
is a space form. For other geometric inequalities for static-convex domains, see
[
6
,
14
,
21
]
.

In the following, we adapt the idea presented in (
1.6
) - (
1.7
) to prove Heintze-Karcher type inequalities involving a general shifted factor
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
M^{n+1}=[0,\bar{r})\times N

(n\geq 2)
be a sub-static warped product manifold with metric
\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda(r)^{2}g_{N}
and potential
\lambda^{\prime}(r)
, where
N
is an
n
-dimensional closed manifold.

(1)

Let
\Omega\subset M^{n+1}
be a bounded domain with a connected, static-convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
on which
\lambda^{\prime}>0
. Let
\varepsilon
be a real number such that the following inequality

(1.11)

\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)\left(p_{1}(\kappa)-\varepsilon\right)>0

holds everywhere on
\Sigma
, where
u=\langle\lambda\partial_{r},\nu\rangle
. Then

(1.12)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa)-\varepsilon}d\mu\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv.

Moreover, if inequality (
1.8
) holds strictly at some point in
\Sigma
, and equality holds in (
1.12
), then
\Sigma
is umbilic and of constant mean curvature.

(2)

Assume
\lambda(r)
satisfies condition
H
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\cup\partial M
. Assume in addition that
\Sigma
is static-convex. Let
\varepsilon
be a real number such that the inequality (
1.11
)
holds everywhere on
\Sigma
. Then

(1.13)

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa)-\varepsilon}d\mu\geq(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv+\lambda(0)^{n+1}\mathrm{Vol}(N,g_{N}).

Moreover, if inequality (
1.8
) holds strictly at some point in
\Sigma
, then equality holds in (
1.13
) if and only if
\Sigma
is a slice
\{r\}\times N
for some
r\in(0,\bar{r})
.

When
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
\varepsilon
is a constant with
|\varepsilon|\leq 1
, there holds
\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\geq\cosh r-|\varepsilon|\sinh r\geq e^{-r}>0
on
\Sigma
automatically. In this case, the assumption (
1.11
) reduces to
p_{1}(\kappa)>\varepsilon
.

Remark 1.5
.

For static-convex
\Sigma
,

(1)

Theorem
1.4
reduces to the results proved by Brendle
[
7
]
when
\varepsilon=0
.

(2)

Theorem
1.4
reduces to Theorem A when
\varepsilon=1
and
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

(3)

Theorem
1.4
reduces to Theorem
1.1
when
\varepsilon=-1
and
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
.

Based on the above remark, we would like to propose the following problem.

Problem 1.1
.

When
\varepsilon\notin\{-1,0,1\}
, is the static-convex assumption of
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
necessary in Theorem
1.4
, especially when
M^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
and
\varepsilon\in(-1,0)\cup(0,1)
?

We next discuss an application of Theorem
1.4
. Recall that a hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M^{n+1}
is called
strictly shifted
k
-convex
if the shifted principal curvatures
(\kappa-\varepsilon)
of
\Sigma
lie in the Gårding cone
\Gamma^{+}_{k}
(see Section
2.1
).

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 2
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
. Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain with smooth, static-convex boundary
\Sigma=\partial\Omega
in space form
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}(c)
, and assume that
\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u>0
on
\Sigma
, where
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
is a constant. Let
\chi:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{1}
-smooth function with
\partial_{1}\chi\leq 0
and
\partial_{2}\chi\geq 0
. Assume that
\Sigma
satisfies the following equation

(1.14)

p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)=\chi\left(\Phi(r),\varepsilon\Phi(r)-u\right),

where
\Phi
is a primitive function of
\lambda
.

(1)

If
\partial_{2}\chi\not\equiv 0
, we further assume that
h_{ij}>\varepsilon g_{ij}
on
\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. Moreover, if one of the inequalities
\partial_{1}\chi\leq 0
and
\partial_{2}\chi\geq 0
is strict, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at
p
.

(2)

If
\partial_{2}\chi\equiv 0
, we further assume that
\Sigma
is strictly shifted
k
-convex. Then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. Moreover, if
\partial_{1}\chi<0
, then
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at
p
.

Remark 1.7
.

(1)

The definition domain of
\chi
can be changed to any domain in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
that contains all the possible
(\Phi(r),\varepsilon\Phi(r)-u)
. For example, when
c=-1
,
\varepsilon=1
and
\Phi(r)=\cosh r
, we have
\Phi(r)\in[1,+\infty)
and
0<\cosh r-\sinh r\leq\varepsilon\Phi(r)-u<+\infty
. Then we can let
\chi
be a function defined on
[1,+\infty)\times(0,+\infty)
.

(2)

In the case that
\mathbb{N}^{n+1}=\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
,
\varepsilon=1
and
\partial_{1}\chi=0
, Theorem
1.6
was proved in
[
15
]
. In addition, if

is constant and

is assumed to be horo-convex, then it reduces to
[

.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect some basic properties of elementary symmetric polynomials and hypersurfaces in warped product manifolds, especially the properties of hypersurfaces in space forms.

2.1.
Elementary symmetric polynomials

Given
m\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we define the normalized
m
th elementary symmetric polynomial
p_{m}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.1)

p_{m}(x)=\frac{m!(n-m)!}{n!}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{m}\leq n}x_{i_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{m}},

where
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is easy to see
p_{m}(1,\ldots,1)=1
. We also set
p_{0}(x)=1
and
p_{l}(x)=0
for
l>n
. Given a constant
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote
x>s
if
x_{i}>s
for all
1\leq i\leq n
, and we denote
x-s:=(x_{1}-s,\ldots,x_{n}-s)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Let
A\in{\rm Sym}(n)
be an
n\times n
symmetric matrix. Denote by
{\rm EV}(A)
the eigenvalues of
A
. By the symmetry of
p_{m}
, we can define
p_{m}(A):=p_{m}(EV(A))
. Then

p_{m}(A)=\frac{(n-m)!}{n!}\delta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}A_{i_{1}}{}^{j_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{m}}{}^{j_{m}},\quad m=1,\ldots,n,

where
\delta_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{m}}^{j_{1},\ldots,j_{m}}
is the generalized Kronecker symbol. Denote

(\dot{p}_{m})^{i}_{j}(A)=(\dot{p}_{m})^{i}_{j}({\rm EV}(A))=\frac{\partial p_{m}(A)}{\partial A_{i}{}^{j}}.

If
A={\rm diag}\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
is a diagonal matrix, then

(\dot{p}_{m})^{i}_{j}(A)=\frac{\partial p_{m}(x)}{\partial x_{i}}\delta^{i}_{j}.

We have the following basic formulas:

(2.2)

\displaystyle(\dot{p}_{m})^{i}_{j}\left(A\right)A_{i}{}^{j}=

\displaystyle mp_{m}\left(A\right),\qquad(\dot{p}_{m})^{j}_{j}\left(A\right)\delta_{i}{}^{j}=mp_{m-1}\left(A\right).

Define the Gårding cone by

\Gamma_{m}^{+}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}~|~p_{i}(x)>0,\ 1\leq i\leq m\}.

Equivalently,
\Gamma_{m}^{+}
is the connected component of
\{p_{m}>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
which contains the positive cone
\Gamma_{n}^{+}
.
For
x\in\Gamma_{m}^{+}
, we have the following Newton-MacLaurin inequality:

(2.3)

p_{m}(x)\leq p_{1}(x)p_{m-1}(x),

with equality if and only if
x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n}>0
when
m\in\{2,\ldots,n\}
. We say matrix
A
belongs to
\Gamma_{m}^{+}
if
{\rm EV}(A)\in\Gamma^{+}_{m}
. In this case, we have

(2.4)

(\dot{p}_{m})_{j}^{i}(A)>0.

For further properties of elementary symmetric polynomials, we refer readers to
[
11
]
.

2.2.
Hypersurfaces in warped product manifolds

Let
M^{n+1}=[0,\bar{r})\times N
be a warped product manifold with metric

\bar{g}=dr^{2}+\lambda^{2}(r)g_{N},

where
N
is an
n
-dimensional closed manifold, and
\lambda:[0,\bar{r})\to\mathbb{R}
is a smooth positive function. Denote by
\bar{\nabla}
and
\bar{\Delta}
the Levi-Civita connection and the Laplacian on
M
, respectively. For convenience, we also use the notation
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
for
\bar{g}
. When
M^{n+1}
has a horizon
\partial M=\{0\}\times N
, we will consider the following condition:

(
H
)

\lambda^{\prime}(0)=0,\ \lambda^{\prime\prime}(0)>0~{\rm and}~\lambda^{\prime}(r)>0~{\rm for}~r\in(0,\bar{r}).

For a hypersurface
\Sigma^{n}\subset M^{n+1}
, we denote by
g
the induced metric on
\Sigma
, and by
\nabla
and
\Delta
the corresponding Levi-Civita connection and Laplacian on
\Sigma
, respectively.

The definition of
p_{m}
can be extended to symmetric
(0,2)
tensor
T_{ij}
on
\Sigma
by

p_{m}(T,g):=p_{m}({\rm EV}(g^{jk}T_{ki})).

Denote

\dot{p}_{m}^{ij}({\rm EV}(g^{-1}T))=\frac{\partial p_{m}(T,g)}{\partial T_{ij}}.

Denote by
\nu
the outward unit normal of
\Sigma
. Let
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
be a local orthonormal frame on
\Sigma
, the second fundamental form
h_{ij}
of
\Sigma
is defined by
h_{ij}=\langle\bar{\nabla}_{e_{i}}\nu,e_{j}\rangle
. The principal curvatures
\kappa=\{\kappa_{1},\ldots,\kappa_{n}\}
are eigenvalues of the Weingarten matrix
(h_{i}{}^{j})
, where
h_{i}{}^{j}=h_{ik}g^{kj}
, and
(g^{ij})
is the inverse matrix of
(g_{ij})
. The mean curvature

of
\Sigma
is given by

p_{1}(\kappa)=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\kappa_{i}=\frac{1}{n}h_{ij}g^{ij}.

For a given constant
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
, we call
\kappa-\varepsilon=(\kappa_{1}-\varepsilon,\ldots,\kappa_{n}-\varepsilon)
the shifted principal curvatures of
\Sigma
, which are eigenvalues of the shifted Weingarten matrix
(h_{i}{}^{j}-\varepsilon\delta_{i}{}^{j})
.

Given
\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, a hypersurface
\Sigma
is called shifted
k
-convex if
(\kappa-\varepsilon)\in\overline{\Gamma}^{+}_{k}
for all
n
-tuples of shifted principal curvatures along
\Sigma
. It is called strictly shifted
k
-convex if
(\kappa-\varepsilon)\in\Gamma^{+}_{k}
along
\Sigma
, and in this case we have
\dot{p}_{m}^{ij}(\kappa-\varepsilon)>0
on
\Sigma
for all
m=1,\ldots,k
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Sigma
be a connected hypersurface in
M^{n+1}
. Assume that
p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)>0
on
\Sigma
and
\kappa-\varepsilon>0
at some point in
\Sigma
. Then
\Sigma
is strictly shifted
k
-convex.

Proof.

The first assumption shows that the shifted principal curvatures
\kappa-\varepsilon
of
\Sigma
lie in a connected component of
\{p_{k}(x)>0\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The second assumption shows that this component contains a point in the positive cone
\Gamma^{+}_{n}
. Thus, the connected component is
\Gamma^{+}_{k}
, and
\Sigma
is then strictly shifted
k
-convex.
∎

, and thus
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

In this section, we prove the Heintze-Karcher type inequality involving the shifted mean curvature with a general shifted factor
\varepsilon
stated in Theorem
1.4
.

If
\partial\Omega=\Sigma
, then we can use the divergence theorem and (
2.6
) to get

(4.1)

\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu=\int_{\Sigma}\langle V,\nu\rangle d\mu=\int_{\Omega}\mathrm{div}_{M}Vdv=(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv.

If
M
has a horizon
\partial M=\{0\}\times N
, then
\bar{g}|_{\partial M}=\lambda(0)^{2}g_{N}
and
\langle\partial_{r},\nu\rangle=-1
on
\partial M
. If
\partial\Omega=\Sigma\cup\partial M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu=

\displaystyle(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv-\int_{\{0\}\times N}\langle\lambda(0)\partial_{r},\nu\rangle d\mu

(4.2)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv+\lambda(0)^{n+1}\mathrm{Vol}(N,g_{N}).

Thus, both (
1.9
) and (
1.10
) in Theorem B can be written as

(4.3)

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}\geq\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu.

Using the Minkowski formula (
2.10
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}p_{1}(\kappa)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}\left((\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)+\varepsilon u\right)\left(p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)+\varepsilon\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)d\mu+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle+\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}\varepsilon\left((\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)+up_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)d\mu+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\right)^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)d\mu.

Meanwhile, we compute

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\lambda^{\prime}d\mu\right)^{2}=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)+\varepsilon u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}\left(\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\right)^{2}

(4.5)

\displaystyle+2\varepsilon\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)d\mu\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu.

Inserting (
4.4
) and (
4.5
) into (
4.3
), we have

(4.6)

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}\geq\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu\int_{\Sigma}(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)d\mu.

On the other hand, by using the assumption (
1.11
) and the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

(4.7)

\left(\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)d\mu\right)^{2}\leq\int_{\Sigma}\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)d\mu\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}d\mu.

Note that equality holds in (
4.7
) if and only if
p_{1}(\kappa)
is constant on
\Sigma
. Combining (
4.6
) with (
4.7
), we have

\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}d\mu\geq\int_{\Sigma}ud\mu.

Then inequalities (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) follow by inserting (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) into the above inequality, respectively. The cases that equalities hold in these inequalities inherit from Theorem B. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.4
.

5.
Proofs of Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.6

In this section, we prove the uniqueness result of solutions to a class of curvature equations stated in Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.6
by using the Heintze-Karcher type inequalities established in Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
, respectively.
Since their proofs are similar, we first prove Theorem
1.6
and then point out the differences between this proof and that of Theorem
1.2
.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

For abbreviation, we denote

\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}:=\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}(\kappa-\varepsilon)=\frac{\partial p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{\partial(h_{ij}-\varepsilon g_{ij})}.

Since
\Sigma
is strictly shifted
k
-convex, we have
\chi>0
and that
\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}
is positive definite on
\Sigma
.
Using (
2.14
), (
1.14
) and integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)\frac{p_{k-1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}-u\right)d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u)p_{k-1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)-up_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{j}\nabla_{i}\Phi(r)}{\chi}d\mu

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Sigma}\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi(r)\nabla_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\chi}\right)d\mu

(5.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}}{\chi^{2}}\nabla_{i}\Phi\left(\partial_{1}\chi\nabla_{j}\Phi+\partial_{2}\chi\nabla_{j}\left(\varepsilon\Phi-u\right)\right)d\mu,

where in the third equality we used the divergence free property (
2.12
) of
\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}
. For any fixed point
q\in\Sigma
, we take an orthogonal basis
\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}\}
at
q
such that
g_{ij}=\delta_{ij}
and
h_{ij}=\kappa_{i}\delta_{ij}
. Recall the assumptions
\partial_{1}\chi\leq 0
and
\partial_{2}\chi\geq 0
. Using (
2.7
) and (
2.9
), we have

(5.2)

\partial_{1}\chi\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\Phi=\partial_{1}\chi\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{p}_{k}^{ii}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle^{2}\leq 0

and

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\chi\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}\nabla_{i}\Phi\nabla_{j}\left(\varepsilon\Phi-u\right)=

\displaystyle\partial_{2}\chi\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle\left(\varepsilon\langle V,e_{j}\rangle-h_{j}{}^{l}\langle V,e_{l}\rangle\right)

(5.3)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\partial_{2}\chi\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{p}_{k}^{ii}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle^{2}(\kappa_{i}-\varepsilon).

Inserting (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) into (
5.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)\frac{p_{k-1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}-u\right)d\mu

(5.4)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{k}\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\partial_{2}\chi}{\chi^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dot{p}_{k}^{ii}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle^{2}(\kappa_{i}-\varepsilon).

When
\partial_{2}\chi\equiv 0
, the right hand side of (
5.4
) vanishes; when
\partial_{2}\chi\not\equiv 0
, the assumption
h_{ij}>\varepsilon g_{ij}
implies that the right hand side of (
5.4
) is non-positive. That is, we have

(5.5)

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)\frac{p_{k-1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}-u\right)d\mu\leq 0

in both cases.

On the other hand, by using the Newton-MacLaurin inequality (
2.3
), the assumption
\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u>0
, (
4.1
) and Theorem
1.4
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(\left(\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u\right)\frac{p_{k-1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}{p_{k}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}-u\right)d\mu

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\left(\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa-\varepsilon)}-u\right)d\mu

(5.6)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\frac{\lambda^{\prime}-\varepsilon u}{p_{1}(\kappa)-\varepsilon}d\mu-(n+1)\int_{\Omega}\lambda^{\prime}dv\geq 0.

Comparing (
5.5
) with (
5.6
), we have that equality holds in the second inequality in (
5.6
), which implies that
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere.

Moreover, if either
\partial_{1}\chi<0
or
\partial_{2}\chi>0
, then it follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\langle V,e_{i}\rangle^{2}=0
everywhere on
\Sigma
. Then (
2.7
) implies that
\Sigma
is a geodesic sphere centered at
p
. We complete the proof of Theorem
1.6
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Since there exists at least one point on
\Sigma
at which
h_{ij}>g_{ij}
, it follows from Proposition
2.1
that the assumption
p_{k}(\kappa+1)>0
implies
\dot{p}_{k}^{ij}>0
on
\Sigma
, and that the inequality (
2.3
) is valid.

Recall that
\Phi(r)
is defined as a function satisfying
\Phi^{\prime}(r)=\lambda(r)
. Hence, we can set
\Phi(r)=\cosh r=\lambda^{\prime}
when the ambient space is
\mathbb{H}^{n+1}
. The remainder of the proof of Theorem
1.2
is almost the same as that of Theorem
1.6
by taking
\varepsilon=-1
. The only difference is that instead of Theorem
1.4
, we apply Theorem
1.1
in (
5.6
). We complete the proof of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

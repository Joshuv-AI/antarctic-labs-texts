I. Introduction

    On February 10, 2026, the Financial Industry Regulatory Authority, 
Inc. (``FINRA'') filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission 
(``SEC'' or ``Commission''), pursuant to Section 19(b)(1) of the 
Securities Exchange Act of 1934 (``Exchange Act'') \1\ and Rule 19b-4 
thereunder,\2\ a proposed rule change to amend FINRA Rule 2210 
(Communications with the Public) to allow a member broker-dealer 
(``member'') to project performance or provide a targeted return with 
respect to a security, a securities portfolio, or an asset allocation 
or other investment strategy in its communications with the public, 
subject to specified conditions.\3\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    This order approves the proposed rule change.

II. Description of the Proposed Rule Change

    FINRA Rule 2210 generally prohibits communications with the public 
that predict or project performance.\16\ As discussed below, there are 
three exceptions to this general prohibition; the proposed rule change 
would create a fourth exception in limited circumstances.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \16\ See FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(F). FINRA interprets this 
prohibition to cover both projections of performance and targeted 
returns. Notice at 9309, 9310 n.29; see also FINRA Rule 2210 
Frequently Asked Questions, Question D.7, https://www.finra.org/rules-guidance/guidance/faqs/advertising-regulation (``This 
prohibition [under Rule 2210(d)(1)(F)] extends to retail 
communications that include target returns to investors.''); FINRA 
Regulatory Notice 20-21 at 4 (Jul. 1, 2020), https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2020-06/Regulatory-Notice-20-21.pdf (stating 
that retail communications concerning private placements may not 
project or predict returns to investors such as yields, income, 
dividends, capital appreciation percentages or any other future 
investment performance).

A. FINRA Rule 2210 (Communications With the Public)

    Among other things, FINRA Rule 2210 imposes restrictions on the 
content of a member's communications with the public. For example, 
FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1) (General Standards) (hereinafter the ``general 
content standards'') requires, among other things, that: (1) all member 
communications be based on principles of fair dealing and good faith, 
be fair and balanced, and provide a sound basis for evaluating the 
facts in regard to any particular security or type of security, 
industry, or service; \17\ and (2) the member: (a) not omit any 
material fact or qualification if the omission, in light of the context 
of the material presented, would cause the communication to be 
misleading; \18\ (b) not make any false, exaggerated, unwarranted, 
promissory, or misleading statement or claim in the communication; \19\ 
and (c) consider the nature of the audience to which the communication 
will be directed and provide details and explanations appropriate to 
the audience.\20\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \17\ FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(A).
    \18\ Id.
    \19\ FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(B).
    \20\ FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(E).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The general content standards also prohibit a communication 
prepared by a member from predicting or projecting performance, 
implying that past performance will recur, or making any exaggerated or 
unwarranted claim, opinion, or forecast.\21\ This general prohibition, 
however, does not apply to communications that contain: (1) certain 
hypothetical illustrations of mathematical principles; \22\ (2) certain 
investment analysis tools or written reports produced by such 
investment analysis tools; \23\ and (3) certain price targets contained 
in research reports on debt or equity securities.\24\

B. The Proposed Rule Change

    The proposed rule change would adopt a fourth exception to FINRA 
Rule 2210(d)(1)(F) to permit members' communications to project 
performance or provide targeted returns with respect to a security, a 
securities portfolio, or an asset allocation or other investment 
strategy (hereinafter, ``projected performance or targeted returns''), 
subject to the conditions described, in turn, below.\25\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \25\ Proposed FINRA Rule 2210(d)(1)(F)(iv).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

1. Written Policies and Procedures
    The proposed rule change would require any member that communicates 
projected performance or targeted returns to adopt and implement 
written policies and procedures reasonably designed to ensure that the 
communication is relevant to the likely financial situation and 
investment objectives of the intended audience of the 
communication.\26\ FINRA stated that, under such reasonably designed 
policies and procedures, a communication containing projected 
performance or targeted returns should only be distributed to investors 
whom a member reasonably believes have access to resources to 
independently analyze this information and who have the financial 
expertise to understand the risks and limitations of these types of 
presentations (referred to herein collectively as ``investors who have 
the resources and financial expertise'').\27\ FINRA stated that the 
proposed rule change generally would not permit the inclusion of 
projected performance or targeted returns in communications for

[[Page 66373]]

a mass audience or intended for general circulation, including a 
general retail investor audience, as in such mass communications, a 
member generally could not form any expectation that the communication 
is relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives 
of the intended audience.\28\ The proposed rule change would not, 
however, prescribe the ways in which a member may satisfy the policies 
and procedures requirement; rather, it would provide members with 
flexibility to develop policies and procedures that best suit their 
investor base and business models.\29\ For example, FINRA stated that a 
member firm may wish to partly base its policies and procedures on its 
past experiences with particular types of investors who seek this 
information, group investors into categories or types, or restrict its 
intended audience to investors who have expressed interest or actually 
invested in particular types of securities.\30\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \27\ FINRA Response II at 10; FINRA Response III at 16; see 
Notice at 9310 (stating that a communication that contains 
projections of performance or targeted returns should only be 
distributed where the member reasonably believes the investors for 
whom that communication is intended have the financial expertise and 
resources to understand the risks and limitations of such 
presentations).

2. Disclosure Requirements
    The proposed rule change would impose two disclosure-related 
obligations. Specifically, as originally proposed in the Notice, the 
proposal would have required any member that communicates projected 
performance or targeted returns to provide sufficient information to 
enable the intended audience to understand: (1) the criteria used and 
assumptions made in calculating the projected performance or targeted 
return, including whether the projected performance or targeted return 
is net of anticipated fees and expenses; \31\ and (2) the risks and 
limitations of using the projected performance or targeted return in 
making investment decisions, including reasons why the projected 
performance or targeted return might differ from actual 
performance.\32\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \31\ Notice at 9311-12 (describing then-proposed FINRA Rule 
2210(d)(1)(F)(iv)(c)).
    \32\ Id.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    FINRA stated that the first prong of the disclosure requirement 
would not prescribe the methodology for calculating projected 
performance or targeted returns; rather, FINRA would expect that a 
member provide a general description of the methodology used sufficient 
to enable the intended audience to understand the basis for the 
methodology, as well as the assumptions underlying the projection or 
targeted return.\33\ FINRA stated that, absent this required 
disclosure, it would be more likely that a projection of performance or 
targeted return would mislead a potential investor.\34\ FINRA also 
stated that the second prong of the proposed disclosure requirement 
would help ensure that investors do not unreasonably rely on a 
projected performance or targeted return given their uncertainty and 
risks.\35\

proposal to remove from the first prong the proposed requirement that a 
member include in its disclosure whether its calculation of the 
projected performance or targeted return is net of anticipated fees and 
expenses,\36\ and to remove from the second prong the proposed 
requirement that a member include in its disclosure reasons why the 
projected performance or targeted return might differ from actual 
performance.\37\ FINRA stated that the general content standards, 
together with the proposed rule change's remaining disclosure 
requirements, would help ensure that investors receive the information 
necessary to consider the risks and limitations of such performance 
information.\38\

3. Reasonable Basis Requirement; Recordkeeping Requirement
    As originally proposed in the Notice, the proposal would have 
required any member that communicates projected performance or targeted 
returns to have a reasonable basis for the criteria used and 
assumptions made in calculating the projected performance or targeted 
return, and retain written records supporting the basis for such 
criteria and assumptions.\39\ In response to comments, however, FINRA 
amended the original proposal to remove these requirements,\40\ and 
instead proposed to amend the recordkeeping obligation under FINRA Rule 
2210(b)(4)(A)(iv) to include information concerning the source of any 
projection of performance or targeted return used in the 
communication.\41\ As discussed further below, FINRA stated that an 
express reasonable basis requirement is unnecessary because FINRA Rule 
2210's existing general content standards would preclude the 
communication of unreasonable performance projections or targeted 
returns.\42\ FINRA also stated that the proposed recordkeeping 
requirement would be consistent with the other types of records 
currently required to be maintained pursuant to FINRA Rule 2210(b)(4) 
and would help regulators to evaluate members' compliance with the 
terms of the proposed rule change.\43\

III. Discussion and Commission Findings

    After careful review of the proposed rule change, comment letters 
received, and FINRA's response to the comments, the Commission finds 
that the proposed rule change is consistent with the requirements of 
the Exchange Act and the rules and regulations thereunder that are 
applicable to a national securities association.\44\ Specifically, the 
Commission finds that the proposed rule change is consistent with 
Section 15A(b)(6) of the Exchange Act, which requires, among other 
things, that FINRA rules be designed to prevent fraudulent and 
manipulative acts and practices, to promote just and equitable 
principles of trade, and, in general, to protect investors and the 
public interest.\45\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \44\ In approving this rule change, the Commission has 
considered the rule's impact on efficiency, competition, and capital 
formation. See 15 U.S.C. 78c(f).
    \45\ 15 U.S.C. 78o-3(b)(6).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The proposed rule change would create a new, reasonably tailored 
exception from FINRA Rule 2210's general prohibition on communications 
that predict or project performance. The proposed rule change would 
allow members to communicate projected performance or targeted returns, 
subject to requirements reasonably designed to protect investors and 
the public interest. In particular, the proposed rule change would, 
among other things, require members to: (1) adopt and implement written 
policies and procedures reasonably designed to ensure that the 
communication is relevant to the likely financial situation and 
investment objectives of the intended audience; (2) provide sufficient 
information to enable the intended audience to understand: (a) the 
criteria used and assumptions made in calculating the projected 
performance or targeted return, and (b) the risks and limitations of 
using the projected performance or targeted return in

[[Page 66374]]

making investment decisions; and (3) maintain records including 
information concerning the source of any projection of performance or 
targeted return used in their communications.
    As explained in detail below, the proposed conditions would help 
ensure that such communications are provided only to an appropriate 
audience of investors who have the resources and financial expertise 
and that such communications are provided with disclosures designed to 
support informed investment decision making. Further, the existing 
general content standards--when coupled with the proposed rule change's 
conditions--should help ensure that communications including projected 
performance and targeted returns are fair, balanced, and not 
misleading. Accordingly, and as explained in more detail below, the 
Commission finds that the proposed rule change is consistent with 
Section 15A(b)(6) of the Exchange Act. The Commission addresses the 
proposed rule change's specific provisions, and any related comments, 
in turn.

A. Scope of the Proposed Exception to the General Prohibition on 
Communications That Predict or Project Performance

    As described above, the proposed rule change would permit members 
to communicate projected performance or targeted returns under certain 
conditions.\46\ FINRA stated that some of its members' customers, 
including institutional investors and other sophisticated investors, 
request projected performance or targeted returns to help them make 
informed investment decisions, but FINRA Rule 2210 currently prohibits 
members from providing such information.\47\ FINRA stated that, on the 
other hand, investors can obtain such performance information from 
other market participants, including registered investment 
advisers,\48\ unregistered intermediaries, and issuers.\49\ FINRA 
stated that such different regulatory treatment can: (1) incentivize 
issuers to avoid offering securities through registered broker-
dealers,\50\ and (2) cause investor confusion because investors may 
receive different types of information about the same investments 
depending on the financial professional with whom they engage.\51\ For 
these reasons, FINRA stated that an additional exception to the general 
prohibition on projecting or predicting performance in member 
communications is warranted.\52\

    The proposed rule change reasonably expands the exceptions to 
FINRA's prohibition on communications that predict or project 
performance to cover projected performance and targeted returns in 
certain narrowly defined circumstances and subject to conditions that 
are reasonably designed to protect investors. As described in detail 
below, the proposed rule change is reasonably designed to help ensure 
that such communications are: (1) relevant to the likely financial 
situation and investment objectives of the intended audience (i.e., 
investors who have the resources and financial expertise); and (2) made 
with disclosures that would equip the intended audience to evaluate the 
risks and limitations of such information.\84\ In addition, the 
proposed rule change would require members to maintain records 
concerning the source of any projection of performance or targeted 
return used in the communication.\85\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \84\ Proposed Rule 2210(d)(1)(F)(iv).
    \85\ Proposed Rule 2210(b)(4)(A)(iv).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Currently, other market participants, including issuers and 
investment advisers, can provide projected performance and targeted 
returns to investors, subject to certain conditions. Because FINRA Rule 
2210 prohibits members from communicating projected performance or 
targeted return information, certain investors may be confused by the 
different information they may receive about the same investment from 
different market participants. The proposed rule change would help to 
reduce this confusion--subject to conditions designed to protect 
investors, as discussed further below--by permitting investors to 
receive additional information and make more informed investment 
decisions.
    Furthermore, by limiting the exception to projections of 
performance and targeted returns (and not expanding to include at this 
time additional categories of performance information suggested by 
commenters, such as backtested, model, related, and extracted 
performance), the proposed rule change incrementally permits members to 
disseminate additional categories of performance information, while 
allowing FINRA to gain regulatory experience with its members' 
provision of projected performance and targeted return information to 
investors to evaluate whether future changes, including expansion of 
the exception, would be appropriate. FINRA stated that it would not 
foreclose the possibility of future amendments to address additional 
categories of performance information, and that it would consider 
providing additional guidance on the application of the proposed rule 
change to certain categories of information, as appropriate, if the 
Commission approves the proposed rule change.
    It is likewise reasonable to subject projected performance and 
targeted returns to the same requirements, as this treatment is 
consistent with how FINRA has interpreted the general prohibition on 
predicting or projecting performance and recognizes that the 
distinction between targeted returns and performance projections is not 
always clear to investors.
    For these reasons, the proposed rule change is reasonably designed 
to prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts and practices, to promote 
just and equitable principles of trade, and, in general, to protect 
investors and the public interest.

B. Conditions of the Proposed Exception

    As described above, the proposed rule change would allow members to 
communicate projected performance or targeted returns, subject to 
conditions reasonably designed to protect investors and the public 
interest. As originally proposed in the Notice, the proposal would have 
required members to: (1) adopt and implement written policies and 
procedures reasonably designed to ensure that the communication is 
relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives of 
the intended audience of the communication; \86\ (2) provide sufficient 
information to enable the intended audience to understand: (a) the 
criteria used and assumptions made in calculating the projected 
performance or targeted return, including whether the projected 
performance or targeted return is net of anticipated fees and expenses; 
\87\ and (b) the risks and limitations of using the projected 
performance or targeted return in making investment decisions, 
including reasons why the projected performance or targeted return 
might differ from actual performance; \88\ and (3) have a reasonable 
basis for the criteria used and assumptions made in calculating the 
projected performance or targeted return, and retain written records 
supporting the basis for such criteria and assumptions (``reasonable 
basis requirement'').\89\

1. Written Policies and Procedures
    The proposed rule change would require any member that provides a 
communication containing projected performance or targeted returns with 
respect to a security, a securities portfolio, or an asset allocation 
or other investment strategy to adopt and implement written policies 
and procedures reasonably designed to ensure that the communication is 
relevant to the likely financial situation and investment objectives of 
the intended audience of the communication.\91\ FINRA stated that this 
condition is intended to provide members with the flexibility to 
develop policies and procedures that best suit their investor base and 
the business in which they engage.\92\ Nevertheless, FINRA stated that, 
under such reasonably designed policies and procedures, a communication 
that contains projected performance or targeted return information 
should only be distributed where the member reasonably believes that 
the intended audience has the resources and financial expertise.\93\ 
FINRA also stated that the

projected performance or targeted returns in communications directed to 
a mass audience or intended for general circulation, including to a 
general retail investor audience, because a member generally could not 
form any expectation that the communication is relevant to the likely 
financial situation and investment objectives of the intended 
audience.\94\

facts and circumstances, including for example, the sophistication of

with the requirement to disclose the risks and limitations, FINRA would 
generally expect that a communication that presents a projection of 
performance or targeted return to provide sufficient information to 
enable the investor to understand the reasons why the projected 
performance or targeted return may differ from actual performance.\135\ 
FINRA noted, however, that these disclosures could be tailored to the 
intended audience.\136\ Ultimately, FINRA stated that the amended 
disclosure requirements retain the essential elements of the initial 
proposed rule change, while also providing appropriate flexibility for 
members to tailor their communications based on the specific 
communication and its intended audience.\137\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \133\ Id.
    \134\ Id.
    \135\ Id.
    \136\ Id. at 10-11.
    \137\ FINRA Response III at 10.

    Accordingly, FINRA concluded that the proposed rule change, in 
combination with the general content standards, will ensure that 
investors have sufficient information to consider the risks and 
limitations of relying on such performance projections.\138\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \138\ Id. at 11.

    The proposed rule change reasonably requires FINRA members 
communicating projected performance or targeted returns to provide 
sufficient information to enable the intended audience to understand: 
(1) the criteria used and assumptions made in calculating the projected 
performance or targeted return; and (2) the risks and limitations of 
using the projected performance or targeted return in making investment 
decisions. Instead of prescribing the specific content of the required 
disclosures, the proposed rule change would permit members to tailor 
the disclosures to their intended audience, consistent with the 
requirements under the proposed rules, the general content standards, 
and the federal securities laws. In this way, the proposed rule change 
appropriately recognizes that the specific disclosures required for 
each communication of projected performance or targeted return 
information will depend on, among other things, the projected 
performance or targeted return information itself, the audience of the 
communication, and the nature of the security, securities portfolio, or 
asset allocation or other investment strategy at issue.\139\ 
Accordingly, while it is expected that a member would need to disclose 
whether a projected performance or targeted return is net of 
anticipated fees and expenses and/or the reasons why the projected 
performance or targeted return might differ from actual performance to 
meet the disclosure requirements under the proposed rule, the proposed 
rule change would provide members flexibility to tailor such 
communications where appropriate depending on the facts and 
circumstances, including for example, the particulars of the specific 
communication and the intended audience.

    \139\ For similar reasons, FINRA reasonably declined to opine

    Further, these disclosure requirements would supplement FINRA Rule 
2210's existing general content standards, which broadly require, among 
other things, that member communications, including communications 
containing a projection of performance or targeted return, are fair and 
balanced, and prohibit the omission of any material fact or 
qualification if the omission, in light of the context of the material 
presented, would cause the communication to be misleading.\140\ As a 
result, the proposed disclosure requirements, together with the general 
content standards, should help ensure that investors receive sufficient 
information to understand the criteria, assumptions, risks, and 
limitations associated with the projected performance or targeted 
returns.\141\

FINRA Rule 2210.

    For these reasons, the proposed rule change is reasonably designed 
to prevent fraudulent and manipulative acts and practices, to promote 
just and equitable principles of trade, and, in general, to protect 
investors and the public interest.
3. Reasonable Basis Requirement; Recordkeeping Requirement
    As originally proposed in the Notice, the proposal included a 
reasonable basis requirement, which would have required any member that 
provides a communication containing projected performance or targeted 
returns with respect to a security, a securities portfolio, or an asset 
allocation or other investment strategy to have ``a reasonable basis 
for the criteria used and assumptions made in calculating the projected 
performance or targeted return, and retain[] written records supporting 
the basis for such criteria and assumptions.'' \142\ In response to 
commenters' recommendations to remove, replace, refine, or further 
explain this requirement,\143\ FINRA

[[Page 66380]]

amended the proposed rule change to remove it.\144\ Instead, FINRA 
proposed to amend FINRA Rule 2210(b)(4) to require that members 
maintain records concerning the source of any performance projection or 
targeted return used in their communications.\145\

4. Other Comments
    A number of commenters recommended other changes to the proposed 
rule change, including additional conditions or alternative approaches 
altogether, as well as the inclusion of compliance safe harbors.
    With respect to additional conditions, one commenter recommended 
that--to the extent a member communicates projected performance or 
targeted return information to retail customers--FINRA amend the 
proposed rule change to limit such communications to those made in 
support of, or in connection with, a recommendation of any securities 
transaction or investment strategy involving securities (including 
account recommendations) that is subject to Regulation Best 
Interest.\172\ FINRA declined to make this change, stating that the 
general content standards and the conditions of the proposed rule 
change provide appropriate investor protection to retail investors in 
the absence of a recommendation covered by Regulation Best 
Interest.\173\ FINRA reiterated that under the proposed policies and 
procedures requirement, communications containing projected performance 
or targeted returns should only be distributed to investors who have 
the resources and financial expertise,\174\ and could not be included 
in communications directed to a mass audience or for general 
circulation, including a general retail investor audience.\175\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \172\ NASAA at 5-6.
    \173\ FINRA Response III at 19.
    \174\ FINRA Response II at 10; FINRA Response III at 16.
    \175\ FINRA Response III at 19; see Notice at 9310-11.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    It is reasonable that the proposed rule change does not limit a 
member's ability to communicate projected performance or targeted 
returns to retail investors to only those circumstances where the 
communications are made in support of, or in connection with, a 
recommendation subject to Regulation Best Interest. As discussed above, 
the proposed policies and procedures requirement is designed to limit 
communications containing projected performance or targeted returns to 
investors who have the resources and financial expertise. In addition, 
the proposed rule change would impose disclosure obligations that 
require FINRA's members to provide sufficient information to enable the 
intended audience to understand the criteria, assumptions, risks, and 
limitations associated with such information. Taken together with the 
general content standards of FINRA Rule 2210, these requirements both 
limit the scope of retail investors who would be eligible to receive 
these communications to those who are in a position to understand these 
communications and their associated limitations and would help ensure 
that any retail investor who receives such a communication would have 
the information to evaluate it.
    With respect to commenters that recommended alternative approaches, 
one commenter requested that FINRA subject capital acquisition brokers 
(``CABs'') and non-CAB members to the same or similar rules related to 
the communication of projected performance and targeted return 
information about unregistered securities to institutional 
investors.\176\ A second commenter requested that FINRA regulate the 
use of registered index-linked annuity (``RILA'') illustrations in a 
manner similar to variable annuity communications pursuant to FINRA 
Rule 2211 (Communications with the Public About Variable Life Insurance 
and Variable Annuities).\177\ In both instances, FINRA declined to do 
so. FINRA stated that CABs are subject to a separate rulebook that is 
specifically limited to firms that elect to operate as CABs with 
specified limitations on their activities.\178\ Accordingly, FINRA 
disagreed that the communication standards designed for the limited 
activities of a CAB would be appropriate for a non-CAB member under 
FINRA Rule 2210.\179\ FINRA also stated that to the extent the 
commenter was requesting an amendment to FINRA Rule 2211, such a 
request is outside the scope of the proposed rule change.\180\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

activities and elect CAB status. In addition, it is reasonable not to 
amend FINRA Rule 2211 as part of the proposed rule change given it is 
outside the scope of the proposal.

investors and would also provide members flexibility to tailor their

    It is reasonable that the proposed rule change does not require the 
disclosure of multiple possible outcomes, as recommended by the 
commenter. The proposed rule change and the general content standards 
impose a principles-based framework for the content of member 
communications containing projected performance or targeted return 
information. For this reason, it is reasonable to expect that the 
members communicating projected performance or targeted return 
information will present such information and any corresponding 
disclosures in a way that equips the intended audience to meaningfully

circumstances, a member may wish to simultaneously provide multiple 
projections or targets, but FINRA Rule 2210 need not require such 
presentations in every circumstance.

communications. Eliminating this flexibility in favor of predefined 
categories or safe harbors would fail to account for the fact that the

communication; the security, securities portfolio, or asset allocation

comply with this proposed rule change. As such, it is reasonable to 
decline to establish specific safe harbors based on categorical, ex 
ante classifications of investors eligible to receive projected 
performance or targeted return information.\190\ FINRA also stated that 
if the Commission approves the proposed rule change, FINRA will 
consider whether guidance is needed in the future.\191\

IV. Conclusion

    For the reasons set forth above, the Commission finds that the 
proposed rule change, as modified by Partial Amendment No. 1, is 
consistent with Section 15A(b)(6) of the Exchange Act, which requires, 
among other things, that FINRA rules be designed to prevent fraudulent 
and manipulative acts and practices, promote just and equitable 
principles of trade, and, in general, protect investors and the public 
interest.\192\

Global strong Well-Posedness of the CAO-Problem introduced by Lions, Temam and Wang
Tim Binz 1, Felix Brandt 2, Matthias Hieber 3, Tarek Zöchling 3

Abstract
. Consider the CAO-problem introduced by Lions, Temam and Wang, which concerns a system of two fluids described by two primitive equations coupled by fully nonlinear interface conditions. They proved in their pioneering work the existence of a weak solution to the CAO-system; its uniqueness remained an open problem. In this article, it is shown that this coupled CAO-system is globally strongly well-posed for large data, even in critical Besov spaces. It is furthermore shown that, away from the boundary, the solution is even real analytic. The approach presented relies on an optimal data result for the boundary terms in the linearized system in terms of time-space Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. Boundary terms are then controlled by paraproduct methods in these spaces.

1.
Introduction

Geophysical fluid dynamics constitutes a thriving scientific field, and its flows are characterized by a multitude of characteristic length and time scales and by their interactions with a
range of dynamically relevant processes. In this context the primitive equations serve as a standard model for oceanic and atmospheric dynamics and are derived from the
Navier-Stokes equations by assuming a hydrostatic balance for the pressure term.
They have been introduced in a series of papers by Lions, Temam and Wang
[
28
,
29
,
30
,
31
]
about 30 years ago, see also
[
33
]
. The primitive equations are, however, only one
building block of a coupled system introduced in the articles cited above and describing a two-phase system of fluids subject to fully nonlinear boundary conditions. This system, denoted by
them by CAO, was derived carefully by them investigating boundary layer effects near the interface. The rigorous analysis of the CAO-system has a very rich history but, on the other hand,
many questions are left open until today. For general information on geophysical flows we refer e. g. to the work of Chemin, Desjardins, Gallagher and Grenier
[
12
]
and for the
Navier-Stokes equations e. g. to the work of Galdi
[
19
]
, Farwig, Kozono and Sohr
[
18
]
, Abe and Giga
[
1
]
and Lemarié-Rieusset
[
26
]
.

Lions, Temam and Wang proved in their pioneering work the existence of a weak solution to the primitive equations and to the CAO-system; its uniqueness
remains an open problem for both systems until today.

On the other hand, the primitive equations subject to standard Dirichlet and Neumann boundary conditions are known to be globally strongly well-posed in the three-dimensional setting
for arbitrarily large data belonging to
\mathrm{H}^{1}
by the celebrated result of Cao and Titi
[
10
]
. For related results, see
[
24
,
23
,
22
]
.

It is the aim of this article to show that the coupled CAO-system is globally well-posed in the strong sense for large data, even in critical Besov spaces. Recalling that uniqueness of
weak solutions constructed by Lions, Temam and Wang is not known, it is hence surprising that we are able to prove that for initial data even in critical Besov spaces of the form

\mathrm{B}^{2/q}_{qp}
, the CAO-system admits a
unique, global, strong
solution within the
\mathrm{H}^{1,p}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q})\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{2,q})
-framework. It holds true in particular for the case
p=q=2
and hence
in the
\mathrm{H}^{1}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{2})
-situation. In the absence of outer forces, the solution is even real analytic away from the boundary. Our result described in

Theorem 3.1
below gives hence an answer to a long-standing open problem in fluid dynamics.

At this point, some words about the CAO-model are in order. Very roughly speaking, it couples the compressible primitive equations describing the dynamics of the atmosphere and
the incompressible primitive equations for the dynamics of the ocean through a fully nonlinear interface condition. Lions, Temam and Wang introduced in their analysis of the
compressible equation for the atmosphere a new
p
-coordinate system, which “resembles as far as the mathematical structure of the equations is concerned that of an incompressible fluid”.
In fact, assuming the pressure of the atmosphere at the interface to be constant, one obtains in this way essentially an incompressible primitive equation.

The physical law describing the driving mechanism on the interface is the balance of the shear stress of the fluid and the horizontal component
of the atmosphere force. The shear stress of the ocean, i. e., the tangential component of the stress tensor, is given by
\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}w
, which due to the flatness of the interface
equals
\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}
. On the other hand, the horizontal atmospheric force is given as a drag force of the form
c\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}(v^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}})|v^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}}|
.

Observing that two-phase flows with linear coupling conditions are rather well understood, we note that this is not the case for fully nonlinear interface conditions as formulated in CAO.

Let us now roughly describe the coupling conditions within the CAO-system. We refer the reader to
[
31
]
for a detailed discussion.
Assuming the interface to be fixed and flat, and following the physical law described above, they read as

(1.1)

c_{1}\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}=-c_{2}\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{a}}=c\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}(v^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}})|v^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}}|.

Here
v^{\mathrm{o}},v^{\mathrm{a}}
denote the horizontal velocities of the ocean and the atmosphere, respectively,
\varrho^{\mathrm{a}},\varrho^{\mathrm{o}}
their densities,
c_{1},c_{2}
are physical constants, and
c
is a
coefficient arising from boundary layer theory.

One might suspect that a natural boundary condition on the interface between two immiscible viscous fluids would be an adherence condition, i. e.,
v^{\mathrm{o}}=v^{\mathrm{a}}
.
This condition, however, cannot be used due to the occurrence of boundary layers in the atmosphere and in the ocean at the interface. The atmospheric boundary layer may be divided into two
horizontal layers, the Prandtl layer and the planetary boundary layer. Following Lions, Temam and Wang
[
31
]
, the above condition (
1.1
) takes into account
these boundary layers by assuming that they are geometrically replaced by an interface
\Gamma
whose upper side corresponds to the top of the Prandtl layer and the
lower side to the bottom of the ocean boundary layer. For more information on these boundary layers, we refer e. g. to the work of Dalibard and Gérard-Varet
[
13
]
or Dalibard and
Saint-Raymond
[
14
]
.

To avoid the difficulty of fully nonlinear interface conditions, they are often replaced by simplified linear conditions as
\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}=|{v}^{\mathrm{a}}|\cdot{v}^{\mathrm{a}}
for a given function
{v}^{\mathrm{a}}
.
As pointed out in
[
30
]
and
[
9
]
, this modification is unjustified and generates unrealistic solutions from a physical point of view.
For a discussion of these boundary conditions, we refer e. g. to the work of Bresch and Simon
[
9
]
. For related results about the Navier-Stokes equations with wind-driven boundary conditions, we
refer to the work of Desjardins and Grenier
[
17
]
, Bresch and Simon
[
9
]
, Bresch, Guillén-González, Masmoudi and Rodríguez-Bellido
[
8
]
and Dalibard and Saint-Raymond
[
14
]
.
The primitive equations with stochastic wind-driven boundary conditions, where
{v}^{\mathrm{a}}
is a given stochastic process, were analyzed recently in
[
7
]
.

In contrast to the existing results in the literature, our aim is not to simplify the interface condition, but to consider the original CAO-system subject to fully
nonlinear interface conditions.

Concerning the uniqueness problem of weak solutions for a single primitive equations, several approaches have been developed in the last years aiming for extending well-posedness results
for the primitive equations to the case of rough initial data. We refer here to the work
[
27
]
, yielding uniqueness of weak solutions under special assumptions on the data.
For the existence of unique, strong, global solutions for rough initial data of the form
u_{0}=a_{1}+a_{2}
, where
a_{1}
is continuous and
a_{2}
is a small
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
perturbation, see
[
22
]
.
Regardless of these efforts, the uniqueness problem for weak solutions to the primitive equations remains unsolved.

Our approach to the CAO-system can be described as follows: We first develop an optimal data result for the linearized CAO-problem within the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-framework, which is of
independent interest. The key observation here is the fact that the linearized problem admits a unique, strong solution in the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-setting if and only if the boundary terms belongs to
certain vector-valued Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, more precisely to
\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq}
, where
s=1-\frac{1}{2q}
for the Dirichlet part and
s=\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2q}
for the Neumann part of the boundary.
We then verify this condition by weighted paraproduct methods in these Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. Combining this with
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-estimates for the nonlinearities as in
[
23
]
, we obtain local well-posedness as well as a blow-up criterion.
Let us emphasize that the existence and uniqueness of strong solutions are
characterized
by the
boundary terms belonging to these Triebel-Lizorkin spaces, so there is no choice but to treat the fully nonlinear boundary terms in these spaces.

In a second step, we deduce a-priori estimates for the fully nonlinear problem taking into account the nonlinear interface terms.
In order to exploit these a-priori estimates, it is crucial that the linear theory also covers the Hilbert space case.
To the best of our knowledge, this can only be achieved by means of an optimal data approach as sketched in the previous paragraph.
We note that the signs appearing in the boundary terms play here an
important role. These estimates then rule out the finite-in-time blow-up scenario, and we obtain the global, strong well-posedness of the CAO-system. Our approach yields in addition the global
strong well-posedness of the CAO-system for initial data in critical Besov spaces. Compared to existing approaches to a-priori estimates
[
10
,
23
]
for a
single system of primitive equations, the situation for CAO is seriously more involved due to fully nonlinear interface conditions.

This article is structured as follows. In
Section 2
, we recall the precise formulation of the CAO-model from
[
30
]
. In

Section 3
, we state our main theorem on the global strong well-posedness of the CAO-problem for initial data in critical Besov spaces and discuss in addition
regularity properties of the solutions.
Section 4
analyzes the inhomogeneous hydrostatic Stokes problem, and the optimal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-data result is presented.
Paraproduct techniques are used to estimate in
Section 5
the boundary terms in Triebel-Lizorkin spaces. Combing these results, we obtain also in
Section 5
local
well-posedness as well as a blow-up criterion. Finally, in
Section 6
, we establish
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{t}\mathrm{H}^{1}\cap\mathrm{L}^{2}_{t}\mathrm{H}^{2}
-bounds, yielding a-priori estimates in the maximal regularity
space and hence global strong well-posedness.

We denote by
\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v=(\partial_{x}v,\partial_{y}v)^{\top}
,
\divH v=\partial_{x}v_{1}+\partial_{y}v_{2}
and
\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}=\partial_{x}^{2}+\partial_{y}^{2}
the horizontal gradient, the horizontal divergence and
the horizontal Laplacian, respectively, and by
\mdiv
,
\nabla
as well as
\Delta
the divergence, gradient and Laplacian in either
p
- or in
z
-coordinates.

2.
Preliminaries

Lions, Temam and Wang introduced in
[
30
]
the CAO-system, consisting of a compressible primitive equation for the atmosphere and an incompressible primitive equation
for the ocean which are coupled by fully nonlinear interface conditions. The system is modeled on a cylindrical domain taking the precise shape
\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\ \dot{\cup}\ {\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}=G\times(0,1)\ \dot{\cup}\ G\times(-1,0)
, where

G\in\{\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{T}^{2}\}
. The interface is given by
\Gamma_{i}=G\times\{0\}
. In the isothermal situation, and with thermodynamic constants set equal to
1
, the equation
for the atmosphere takes the shape

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}+\mdiv(\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}u^{\mathrm{a}})&=0,&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{t}v^{\mathrm{a}}-\Delta v^{\mathrm{a}}+u^{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\nabla v^{\mathrm{a}}+\frac{1}{\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}&=f^{\mathrm{a}},&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{z}p^{\mathrm{a}}&=-\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}g,&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\times(0,T),\\
p^{\mathrm{a}}&=\varrho^{\mathrm{a}},&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

where
u^{\mathrm{a}}=(v^{\mathrm{a}},w^{\mathrm{a}}):\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
denotes the velocity,
p^{\mathrm{a}}:\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is the atmospheric pressure,
\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}:\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
represents the
atmospheric density, and
f^{\mathrm{a}}:\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes an external force.

The ocean dynamics is described by the following incompressible primitive equations

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{\mathrm{o}}-\Delta v^{\mathrm{o}}+u^{\mathrm{o}}\cdot\nabla v^{\mathrm{o}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi&=f^{\mathrm{o}},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{z}\pi&=-g,&&\text{ on }{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\times(0,T),\\
\mdiv u^{\mathrm{o}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

where for simplicity, we assume that
\varrho^{o}
is constantly
1
. In this case, the unknowns are the velocity of the ocean
u^{\mathrm{o}}=(v^{\mathrm{o}},w^{\mathrm{o}}):{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
as well as the pressure of the ocean
\pi:{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
. As above,
f^{\mathrm{o}}:{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
denotes an external forcing term.

The equations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are coupled on the interface
\Gamma_{i}=G\times\{0\}
by the balance law saying that the shear stress of the
fluid equals the horizontal component of the atmosphere force, i. e.,

c_{1}\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}\partial_{z}{v}^{\mathrm{a}}=-c_{2}\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}=c\varrho^{\mathrm{a}}({v}^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}})|{v}^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}}|.

This equation is to be understood in the sense of traces. The coupled system is further supplemented by the boundary conditions

\displaystyle(\partial_{z}{v}^{\mathrm{a}})|_{G\times\{1\}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad w^{\mathrm{a}}|_{G\times\{0\}\cup G\times\{1\}}=0,

\displaystyle(\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}})|_{G\times\{-1\}}

\displaystyle=0

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad w^{\mathrm{o}}|_{G\times\{-1\}\cup G\times\{0\}}=0.

In order to deal with the compressible equation, the atmospheric pressure
p^{\mathrm{a}}
is introduced as a new independent variable. The transformation reads as

(t,x,y,z)\rightarrow(t,x,y,p(t,x,y,z)),\text{ with inverse }(t,x,y,p)\rightarrow(t,x,y,z(t,x,y,p)).

We observe that in this coordinate system, the domain is a moving domain and no longer a fixed one.
In fact, the transformed domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)
is given by

\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t):=\left\{(x,y,p):(x,y)\in G,\ p\in(p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}(t)\mathrm{e}^{-g},p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}(t))\right\},

where
p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}(t)
denotes the pressure of the atmosphere at the surface depending on time and the horizontal coordinates. Introducing the geopotential
\tilde{\Phi}
and the material derivative of the pressure
\omega
as new quantities, i. e., setting

\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,y,p):=gz(t,x,y,p)\quad\text{ and }\quad\omega:=\partial_{t}p^{\mathrm{a}}+u^{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\nabla v^{\mathrm{a}},

we obtain the equations for the atmosphere of the shape

(2.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{\mathrm{a}}-Lv^{\mathrm{a}}+v^{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\omega\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\tilde{\Phi}&=f^{\mathrm{a}},&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{p}\tilde{\Phi}&=-\frac{1}{p},&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\times(0,T),\\
\divH v^{\mathrm{a}}+\partial_{p}\omega&=0,&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

where
L
denotes the transformed Laplacian made precise below. The boundary conditions transform to

\displaystyle(p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s})^{2}(\partial_{p}{v}^{\mathrm{a}})|_{G\times\{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\}}

\displaystyle=cp^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}({v}^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}})|{v}^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}}|

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\omega|_{G\times\{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\}}=\partial_{t}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}+v^{\mathrm{a}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s},

\displaystyle(\partial_{p}{v}^{\mathrm{a}})|_{G\times\{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{-g}\}}

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\text{ and }\quad\omega|_{G\times\{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\mathrm{e}^{-g}\}}=\mathrm{e}^{-g}\left(\partial_{t}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}+v^{\mathrm{a}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\right).

From (
2.3

2
we deduce that
\tilde{\Phi}(t,x,y,p)=\log(p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s})-\log(p)
.
The equation (
2.3
) thus simplifies to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{\mathrm{a}}-Lv^{\mathrm{a}}+v^{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\omega\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}(\log p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s})&=f^{\mathrm{a}},&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\times(0,T),\\
\divH v^{\mathrm{a}}+\partial_{p}\omega&=0,&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

and the operator
L
is given by

Lv^{\mathrm{a}}:=\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\partial_{p}((pg)^{2}\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}})-2\frac{p}{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}-\Big(\frac{p|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}|}{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}}\Big)^{2}\partial_{p}^{2}v^{\mathrm{a}}+p\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}-2p\Big(\frac{|\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}|}{p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}}\Big)^{2}\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}.

Lions, Temam and Wang
[
30
]
assumed the atmospheric pressure on the interface to be constant, meaning that
p^{\mathrm{a}}_{s}\equiv p_{s}>0
. This assumption has important consequences. Indeed,
the moving domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}(t)
is changed to a fixed domain
\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}=\{(x,y,p):(x,y)\in G,\ p\in(p_{s}\mathrm{e}^{-g},p_{s})\}

\tilde{\Phi}

\Phi
. For a detailed physical discussion of those assumptions, we
refer to
[
28
]
and
[
30
]
.
The system under consideration hence is

(CAO)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v^{\mathrm{a}}+u^{\mathrm{a}}\cdot\nabla v^{\mathrm{a}}+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\Phi&=f^{\mathrm{a}}+\Delta_{\mathrm{H}}v^{\mathrm{a}}+\partial_{p}\left(p^{2}\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}\right),&&\text{ on }\Omega^{\mathrm{a}}\times(0,T),\\

\partial_{z}\pi&=-1,&&\text{ on }{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\times(0,T),\\
\mdiv u^{\mathrm{o}}&=0,&&\text{ on }{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

where we assume
g
to be
1
. In the sequel,
\Gamma_{u}^{\mathrm{a}}

.
The boundary conditions of (

) then read as

(2.4)

\displaystyle(\partial_{p}{v}^{\mathrm{a}})|_{\Gamma_{b}^{\mathrm{a}}}

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle(\partial_{p}{v}^{\mathrm{a}})|_{\Gamma_{u}^{\mathrm{a}}}=-p_{s}^{-1}V|V|

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad\omega|_{\Gamma_{b}^{\mathrm{a}}\cup\Gamma_{u}^{\mathrm{a}}}=0,

\displaystyle=0,\quad

\displaystyle\text{and }\quad w|_{\Gamma_{u}^{\mathrm{o}}\cup\Gamma_{b}^{\mathrm{o}}}=0,

with

Next, we introduce time-weighted Banach space-valued spaces
\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(J;E)

\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(J;E):=\left\{u\colon J\to E:t^{1-\mu}u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(J;E)\right\}.

The norm

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(J;E)}:=\|t^{1-\mu}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}(J;E)}=\left(\int_{J}t^{p(1-\mu)}\|u(t)\|_{E}^{p}\,\mathrm{d}t\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}
renders the latter space a Banach space.
For
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the associated weighted Sobolev spaces are defined by

\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{k,p}(J;E):=\bigl\{u\in\mathrm{H}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{k,1}(J;E):u^{(j)}\in\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(J;E),\kern 5.0ptj\in\{0,\dots,k\}\bigr\},

and these spaces are equipped with the norms

\|u\|_{\mathrm{H}_{\mu}^{k,p}(J;E)}:=\bigl(\sum_{j=0}^{k}\|u^{(j)}\|_{\mathrm{L}_{\mu}^{p}(J;E)}^{p}\bigr)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}}}
.
The weighted fractional Sobolev spaces and Bessel potential spaces are then defined by interpolation.

Since vector-valued Triebel-Lizorkin spaces turn out to be characteristic for boundary values in our approach, we give here a precise definition.
To this end, for
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
denoting the space of Schwartz functions on
\mathbb{R}
, let
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})
with
0\leq\hat{\varphi}(\xi)\leq 1
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=1
if
|\xi|\leq 1
as well as

\hat{\varphi}(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|\geq\frac{3}{2}
.
Then let
\hat{\varphi_{0}}=\hat{\varphi}
,
\hat{\varphi_{1}}(\xi)=\hat{\varphi}(\nicefrac{{\xi}}{{2}})-\hat{\varphi}(\xi)
and

\hat{\varphi_{k}}(\xi)=\hat{\varphi_{1}}(2^{-k+1}\xi)=\hat{\varphi}(2^{-k}\xi)-\hat{\varphi}(2^{-k+1}\xi)
for
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and
k\geq 1
.
For
\varphi
as above and
f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R},E)
, define

S_{k}f:=\varphi_{k}\ast f=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\hat{\varphi_{k}}\hat{f})
,
which belongs to
\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};E)\cap\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R};E)
. It follows that
\sum_{k\geq 0}S_{k}f=f
in the sense of distributions since
\sum_{k\geq 0}\hat{\varphi_{k}}(\xi)=1
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
E
,
p
,
q
and
s
are as above, then the time-weighted Triebel-Lizorkin space
\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq,\mu}(\mathbb{R},E)
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq,\mu}(\mathbb{R},E)

\displaystyle:=\bigl\{f\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R},E)\colon\|f\|_{\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq,\mu}(\mathbb{R},E)}<\infty\bigr\},\quad\text{ where}

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq,\mu}(\mathbb{R},E)}

\displaystyle:=\big\|\left(2^{ks}S_{k}f\right)_{k\geq 0}\big\|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R};l^{q}(E))}.

The space
\mathrm{F}^{s}_{pq,\mu}(J,E)
is defined via restriction.
Next, we define
hydrostatically solenoidal vector fields
by

\mathrm{L}^{q}_{\overline{\sigma}}(\Omega)=\overline{\{v\in\mathrm{C}^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{2})\colon\divH\overline{v}=0\}}^{\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega)}}

on
\Omega\in\{{\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}},{\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}\}
. Its role for the primitive equations parallels the one of the solenoidal vector fields for the Navier-Stokes equations.
For a function
f\colon G\times(a,b)\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
,we define here the vertical average by
\overline{f}:=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_{a}^{b}\divH f(\cdot,\cdot,\xi)\,\mathrm{d}\xi
.

Last, we introduce the spaces for the initial data.
In fact, for
p,q\in(1,\infty)
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}\leq 1
, we set

\mathrm{B}_{qp,\overline{\sigma}}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}})\coloneqq\mathrm{B}_{qp}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathrm{B}_{qp,\overline{\sigma}}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}})\coloneqq\mathrm{B}_{qp}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}};\mathbb{R}^{2})\cap\mathrm{L}_{\overline{\sigma}}^{q}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}}).

3.
Main result

We are now in the position to formulate our main result on the global, strong well-posedness of (

Theorem 3.1

(Global strong well-posedness for the CAO-system)

Let

Then there exists a unique, strong solution
(v^{\mathrm{a}},\omega^{\mathrm{a}},\Phi)
,
(v^{\mathrm{o}},w^{\mathrm{o}},\pi)
to (
CAO
), subject to (
2.4
) and (
2.6
), satisfying

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{a}}

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu_{c}}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega^{\mathrm{a}};\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu_{c}}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{2,q}(\Omega^{\mathrm{a}};\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{B}_{qp,\overline{\sigma}}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}})),

\displaystyle v^{\mathrm{o}}

\displaystyle\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu_{c}}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}};\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu_{c}}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{2,q}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}};\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{C}([0,T];\mathrm{B}_{qp,\overline{\sigma}}^{\nicefrac{{2}}{{q}}}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}})).

Remark 3.2
.

(i) An analogous result for the simplified problem concerning linear interface conditions, so
\partial_{p}v^{\mathrm{a}}=p_{s}^{-1}\cdot(v^{\mathrm{o}}-{v}^{\mathrm{a}})
and

\partial_{z}v^{\mathrm{o}}=p_{s}\cdot({v}^{\mathrm{a}}-v^{\mathrm{o}})

(ii) The assertion of

remains valid when considering general time weights

. In that case, the space for the initial data is given
by
\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{N},\overline{\sigma}}^{2(\mu-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{a}})\times\mathrm{B}_{qp,\mathrm{N},\overline{\sigma}}^{2(\mu-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}({\Omega}^{\mathrm{o}})
, where the subscript

indicates Neumann boundary conditions on the
upper and lower boundary, respectively, if

. The initial data then also need to satisfy the nonlinear coupling conditions on the interface
as in (
2.4
).

Our next result discusses regularity properties of the solution (in time and space) under stronger regularity assumptions on the data. Interestingly enough, it seems that only one additional time and spatial tangential derivative for the solution at the interface
\Gamma
can be gained when presuming additional time and spatial regularity of the forces. This is
due to the fact that the interface conditions are only once Fréchet-differentiable, but not twice.

Corollary 3.3

(Regularity)
.

Let

,

.
Furthermore, let

(1)

Interior regularity: Assume

,

(2)

Corollary 3.4

.

4.
The linearized problem and optimal
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
theory

In this section, we develop an optimal data result for the linearized CAO-problem within the
\mathrm{L}^{p}
-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-framework, which is of independent interest.
The result serves as the starting point of the proof of the local well-posedness of the CAO-system. It is optimal in the sense that the assumptions are sufficient and necessary
for solving the associated inhomogeneous linear problem given by

(4.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}v-\Delta v+\lambda v+\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}\pi&=f_{v},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{z}\pi&=f_{w},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
\mdiv u&=f_{\mdiv},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
v(0)&=v_{0},&&\text{ on }{\Omega}\times(0,T),\\
v&=b_{v}^{\mathrm{D}},&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{z}v&=b_{v}^{\mathrm{N}},&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\times(0,T),\\
w&=b_{w}^{\mathrm{D}},&&\text{ on }\Gamma\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

for
0<T<\infty
and
u=(v,w)
subject to periodic boundary conditions on the lateral boundary or on an infinite layer, i. e., on

\Omega=G\times(a,b)
with
G\in\{\mathbb{T}^{2},\mathbb{R}^{2}\}
. We will use the notation
\Gamma=\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\cup\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}
for the Dirichlet and Neumann parts of the boundary.
The following assumptions are crucial for solving the boundary value problem (
4.1
) subject to inhomogeneous data.

Assumption 4.1
.

Given
f_{v}
,
f_{w}
,
f_{\mdiv}
,
v_{0}
,
b_{v}^{\mathrm{D}}
,
b_{v}^{\mathrm{N}}
and
b_{w}^{\mathrm{D}}
, with
b_{w}^{\mathrm{D}}=b_{w,a}^{\mathrm{D}}
on

and
b_{w}^{\mathrm{D}}=b_{w,b}^{\mathrm{D}}
on
G\times\{b\}
, assume

(1)

f_{v}-\int_{a}^{z}\nabla_{\mathrm{H}}f_{w}\,\mathrm{d}\xi\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\eqqcolon\mathbb{E}_{0,\mu}
,

(2)

\frac{b_{w,a}^{\mathrm{D}}-b_{w,b}^{\mathrm{D}}}{b-a}+\overline{f}_{\mdiv}\in\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu}(0,T;\dot{\mathrm{H}}^{-1,q}(G))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{1,q}(G))\eqqcolon\mathbb{G}_{1,\mu}
,

(3)

v_{0}\in\mathrm{B}_{qp}^{2(\mu-\nicefrac{{1}}{{p}})}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2})\eqqcolon\mathrm{X}_{\gamma,\mu}
,

(4)

b_{v}^{\mathrm{D}}\in\mathrm{F}_{pq,\mu}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{B}_{qq}^{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\eqqcolon\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{D},\mu}
and

b_{v}^{\mathrm{N}}\in\mathrm{F}_{pq,\mu}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{B}_{qq}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\eqqcolon\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{N},\mu}

\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(G)

The main result in this section addresses the solvability of (

).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let

and

such that the problem (

v
satisfying

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\in\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}:=\mathrm{H}^{1,p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{L}^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{2,q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{2}))

(f_{v},f_{w},f_{\mdiv},v_{0},b_{v}^{\mathrm{D}},b_{v}^{\mathrm{N}},b_{w}^{\mathrm{D}})

In that case, there exists a constant

Remark 4.3

(i) The vertical velocity

(ii) The shift by

With regard to solving (

In this particular situation, we get the following result.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let

be as in

, and assume that
f_{w}=0
and

.
Then there is

such that (

with

As our proof of

-
\mathrm{L}^{q}
-regularity result.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be as in

such that system (

Proof.

We start by noting that the necessity of the conditions given in Assumption 4.1 follows by standard arguments of trace theory. This is not the case
for the sufficiency part of the proof. We divide this proof into several steps. Let us note first that time-weighted maximal
\mathrm{L}^{p}

\mathrm{L}^{p}
-regularity, see
[
34

Step 1
.

For homogeneous boundary conditions

, see e. g.
[
15
]

Step 2
.

Consider the perturbed problem

(4.4)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}V-\Delta V+\lambda V+sBV&=H,&&\text{ on }{\Omega\times(0,T)},\\
V(0)&=V_{0},&&\text{ on }{\Omega},\\
V&=B^{\mathrm{D}},&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}\times(0,T),\\
\partial_{z}V&=B^{\mathrm{N}},&&\text{ on }\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}\times(0,T),\end{aligned}\right.

for

. If
s=0
, the optimal data result is known for standard domains by the results given in
[
16
]
. It carries over to the situation of cylindrical domains
by a localization procedure. Hence, there exists a unique solution

to (
4.4

Step 3
.

W. l. o. g., we may assume
V_{0}=0
. By
1
, we have
\partial_{t}-\Delta+\lambda+sB\colon_{0}\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}\to\mathbb{E}_{0,\mu}
.
On the other hand,

V|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{B}_{qq}^{2-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
and
\partial_{z}V|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}\in\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\mu}(0,T;\mathrm{B}_{qq}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{q}}}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))
.

In the sequel, the prescript
0
indicates homogeneous initial values.
Arguing as in the proof of the necessity part in
[
16
]
, we find that

V|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\in{{}_{0}\mathrm{F}_{pq,\mu}^{1-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{L}^{q}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))

\partial_{z}V|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}\in{{}_{0}\mathrm{F}_{pq,\mu}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2q}}}}(\mathbb{R}_{+};\mathrm{L}^{q}(G;\mathbb{R}^{2}))

(\partial_{t}-\Delta+\lambda+sB,\mathrm{tr}|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}},\partial_{z}|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}})\colon_{0}\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}\to\mathbb{E}_{0,\mu}\times\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{D},\mu}\times\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{N},\mu}

is a bounded linear operator. The maximal regularity yields the estimate

\displaystyle\|V\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigl(\|\partial_{t}V-\Delta V+\lambda V+sBV\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,\mu}}+\|BV\|_{\mathbb{E}_{0,\mu}}+\|V|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{D}}}\|_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{D},\mu}}+\|\partial_{z}V_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{N}}}\|_{\mathbb{F}_{\mathrm{N},\mu}}\bigr),

where the constant
C>0
is independent of
s
. We proceed with the continuity method. For this, we recall e. g. from
[
2
]
the embedding

{}_{0}\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}(0,T)\hookrightarrow\mathrm{BUC}([0,T];\mathrm{X}_{\gamma,\mu})
, where the notation
\mathbb{E}_{1,\mu}(0,T)
means that we consider the space on the time interval
(0,T)
.
We will typically omit this piece of notation in the remainder of the section unless it helps to avoid confusion.
Note that the embedding constant in the preceding embedding is independent of
T
. This leads to

\displaystyle\leq C\|V\|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{\mathrm{H}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(0,T;\mathrm{W}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}},4}(\Omega))}\cdot\|V\|^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(0,T;\mathrm{W}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}},4}(\Omega))}\cdot\|V\|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{W}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}},4}(\Omega))}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|V\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,1,T}}+C\|V\|^{3}_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(0,T;\mathrm{H}^{1}(\Omega))}\leq\varepsilon\bigl(\|v^{\mathrm{a}}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,1,\mathrm{a}}}+\|v^{\mathrm{o}}\|_{\mathbb{E}_{1,1,\mathrm{o}}}\bigr)+CB_{2}^{3}.

An absorption argument yields the desired estimate with
B_{3}:=C(B_{2}^{4}+B_{2}^{3}+B_{2}^{2})
.
∎

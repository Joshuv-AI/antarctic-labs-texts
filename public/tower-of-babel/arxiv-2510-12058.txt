On proper affine actions with better decaying cocycles Thanks: ∗ Corresponding author: yiranimaths@163.com
Chunliu Feng, Geng Tian and Ran Yi ∗

Abstract
. It is well-known that the study of proper affine isometric actions of countable discrete groups in various spaces plays a crucial role in geometric group theory, operator algebras, and high-dimensional topology. In this article, we shall construct a proper affine isometric action of countable discrete group Γ \Gamma on the Banach space ⊕ n = 1 ∞ ℓ 2 ​ n ( Γ ) \oplus_{n=1}^{\infty}\ell^{2n}(\Gamma) such that the cocycles have decay rates that are better than those of Haagerup and Przybyszewska in [ 4 ] . Keywords: Countable discrete group, proper affine action, decay rate MSC2020: 20F65



1.
Introduction

The study of proper affine isometric actions of discrete groups in various spaces is deeply connected to two major conjectures in mathematics: the Baum-Connes conjecture and the Novikov conjecture. Gromov
[
3
]

initiated research on discrete
groups from a geometric perspective. In the foundational article
[
3
]
,
he introduced the concept of countable discrete groups called a-T-menable,
which has proven to be very successful in studying the Baum-Connes conjecture and Novikov conjecture.
A countable discrete group
\Gamma

is called
a-T-menable
group
if it admits a proper affine isometric action on the Hilbert space.
A remarkable result by Higson and Kasparov stated that the Baum-Connes conjecture holds for all a-T-manable groups.
We refer to the readers in
[
5
]
in this direction.

A beautiful result
[
2
]

by Bekka, Cherix and Valette stated that
if
\Gamma
is amenable, then

\Gamma
admits a proper affine isometric action on the Hilbert space, which means that the class of a-T-manable groups covers all amenable groups.
Later, in 2004, Yu
[
6
]
showed that each hyperbolic group admits proper affine isometric actions on some
\ell^{{}^{p}}(\mathbb{N})
,
where the number
p
depends only on the group itself.
In 2005, Brown and Guentner extended this property to be quite general. In
[
1
]
, it was proved that
for
any countable discrete group
\Gamma
,
there exists a sequence of positive
real numbers
\{p_{{}_{n}}\}
and a metrically proper affine
isometric action of
\Gamma

on the Banach space

\mathop{\oplus}_{n=1}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$~\infty$}}\ell^{{}^{p_{{}_{n}}}}(\mathbb{N})
.
Later, in 2006, Haagerup and Przybyszewska
[
4
]
strengthened their result and obtained a similar theorem with a fixed sequence, say

\mathop{\oplus}_{n=1}^{\scalebox{0.6}{$~\infty$}}\ell^{{}^{2n}}(\mathbb{N})
.
The cocycles in their study had decay rates of
O(\frac{1}{n})
.

In this study,
we improve the results of Haagerup and Przybyszewska and
show that the decay rate of the cocycles can achieve

O\Big(\dfrac{1}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Big),

where

\eth_{{}_{k}}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}\\
\underbrace{\ln(\ln(\cdots\ln}\limits_{k}(x)\cdots)),&\text{ when }x>\underbrace{\exp(\exp(\cdots\exp}\limits_{k}(1)\cdots));\\
\\
\\
1,&\text{ when }x\leqslant\underbrace{\exp(\exp(\cdots\exp}\limits_{k}(1)\cdots)).\end{array}\right.

We now state our main theorem as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

For any countable discrete group
\Gamma
and any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Gamma
admits a proper affine
isometric action
on the Banach space
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell^{2n}(\Gamma)
, where the cocycles have the following decay rate:

O\Big(\dfrac{1}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Big).

More precisely,
there exists a function

\displaystyle b:\Gamma\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell^{2n}(\Gamma)

such that

(1)

b(\gamma_{{}_{1}}\gamma_{{}_{2}})=\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}})\Big(b(\gamma_{{}_{2}})\Big)+b(\gamma_{{}_{1}})
holds for all
\gamma_{{}_{1}}
and
\gamma_{{}_{2}}
in
\Gamma
,
where
\pi
is the left regular representation of
\Gamma
on
\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell^{2n}(\Gamma)
;

(2)

\lim\limits_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\big\|b(\gamma)\big\|=\infty;

(3)

for every
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\Big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\Big\|_{{}_{\ell^{{}^{2n}}}}=O\Big(\dfrac{1}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Big)

where
b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)
is the
n
-th coordinate of
b(\gamma)
in
\ell^{{}^{2n}}(\Gamma)
.

2.
Preliminary

In this section, we review some facts
[
6
]

regarding countable discrete groups that will be accessible to the reader.

First let us recall the notion of a length function.

A
length function
on the countable discrete group

\Gamma
is a function

|\cdot|:\Gamma\to[0,\infty)
such that for any
g,h\in\Gamma

the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

|g|=0

if and only if
g
is the identity element
e
;

(2)

|g|=|g^{-1}|;

(3)

and
|gh|\leq|g|+|h|.

A reader familiar with normed spaces should quickly realize that a length function in a group bears some resemblance to a norm on a linear space.

We will be interested only in proper length functions.
A length function is said to be
proper
if for any

R>0
the set

\{g\in\Gamma:|g|\leq R\}

is finite.

In
[
6
]
, the authors showed that any countable discrete group admits a proper length function.
The length function can also take integer values.

Now the metric
d(\cdot,\cdot)
induced by
|\cdot|
in the way that

d(g,h)=|g^{-1}h|

is left-invariant,that is, invariant under the left-translation action of
\Gamma
on itself. Indeed, we have

d(\gamma g,\gamma h)=|(\gamma g)^{-1}\gamma h|=|g^{-1}h|=d(g,h),

for any
g,h,\gamma\in\Gamma
.

We shall consider metric
d
in the entire paper.
Moreover, we recall that
the proper left invariant metric on
\Gamma
has exponential volume growth, that is, there exists a constant
a>0
such that

\#\{B_{d}(e,R)\}\leqslant e^{aR},~\forall R>0,

where
\#
denotes the cardinal number of the set.
Details can be found in
[
4
]
.

Next we recall the concept of proper affine isometric action of the discrete group.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a normed vector space. Then, the affine isometric group of
X
is set.

\mathrm{Aff}(X)=\{\phi:X\to X\;|\;\phi(x)=Ax+b,\;A\in ISO(X),\;b\in X\}.

We say that
G

has an affine isometric action on

X
, if there exists group homomorphism

\alpha:G\longrightarrow\mathrm{Aff}(X),

such that

\forall g,h\in G,\quad\alpha(g\cdot h)=\alpha(g)\circ\alpha(h).

Moreover, we say that the action is
proper
, if

\forall\xi\in X,\quad\lim_{g\to\infty}\|\alpha(g)\xi\|=\infty.

3.
Proof of main result

In this section, we provide proof of our main results.

Proof.

Let
d
be a proper left invariant metric (taking integer values)
on
\Gamma
such that the
d
-balls have exponential volume growth, that is there exists a constant
a>0
such that

\#\{B_{d}(e,R)\}\leqslant e^{aR},~\forall R>0.

Consider the functions
\phi^{n}_{e}:\Gamma\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}
,

\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cccc}\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}-\dfrac{d(e,\gamma)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~},&\text{when }d(e,\gamma)<n;\\
\\
0,&\text{when}~d(e,\gamma)\geqslant n.\\
\end{array}\right.

It is easy to verify that
\phi^{n}_{e}
is a bounded function.

Moreover, it is
\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}
-Lipschitz.
In fact, given any two elements
g,\gamma\in\Gamma
,
if both
d(e,\gamma)<n
and
d(e,g)<n
hold,
then

\displaystyle\big|\phi^{n}_{e}(g)-\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{\big|d(e,g)-d(e,\gamma)\big|}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{d(g,\gamma)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}.

If
d(e,\gamma)\geqslant n
and
d(e,g)<n
,
then

\displaystyle\big|\phi^{n}_{e}(g)-\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}-\dfrac{d(e,g)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~},

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{n-d(e,g)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{d(e,\gamma)-d(e,g)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{d(g,\gamma)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}.

In summary,
\phi^{n}_{e}

is
\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}
-Lipschitz.

Now for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in\Gamma
,
let
b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma):=\pi(\gamma)\phi^{n}_{e}-\phi^{n}_{e}
, where
\pi
is the left regular representation on
\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
,
then
b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\in\ell^{\infty}(\Gamma)
.

Since
d(\gamma,e)=d(\gamma^{-1},e)
, we have that

\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma)=\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma^{-1}),~\forall\gamma\in\Gamma.

Hence for any each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\gamma\in\Gamma
and

x\in\Gamma
,

\displaystyle\big|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)(x)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\big(\pi(\gamma)\phi^{n}_{e}-\phi^{n}_{e}\big)(x)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\phi^{n}_{e}(\gamma^{-1}x)-\phi^{n}_{e}(x)\big|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big|\phi^{n}_{e}(x^{-1}\gamma)-\phi^{n}_{e}(x^{-1})\big|

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{d(x^{-1}\gamma,x^{-1})}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\dfrac{d(\gamma,e)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}.

On the other hand,
since
b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)(x)=0
when
x\not\in B_{d}(e,n)\cup B_{d}(\gamma,n)
, it follows that
b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\in\ell^{2n}(\Gamma)

and

\displaystyle\big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\big\|_{{}_{\ell^{2n}}}^{2n}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\Bigg(\dfrac{d(\gamma,e)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Bigg)^{2n}\cdot\big(\#\{B_{d}(e,n)\}+\#\{B_{d}(\gamma,n)\}\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle 2e^{an}\cdot\Bigg(\frac{d(\gamma,e)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Bigg)^{2n}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\big\|_{{}_{\ell^{2n}}}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt[2n]{2}\cdot e^{a/2}\cdot\frac{d(\gamma,e)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

\displaystyle\leqslant

\displaystyle c^{\prime}\cdot\frac{d(\gamma,e)}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~},

where
c^{\prime}=\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\sqrt[2n]{2}\cdot e^{a/2}
.
That is
for each
\gamma\in\Gamma
,

\Big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\Big\|_{{}_{\ell^{{}^{2n}}}}=O\Big(\dfrac{1}{~n\cdot\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Big)_{.}

The condition
(2)
holds.

Now let
b(\gamma)=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)
,
then
b(\gamma)\in\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\ell^{2n}(\Gamma)
.

For any
\gamma_{{}_{1}},\gamma_{{}_{2}}\in\Gamma
,
we have

\displaystyle b(\gamma_{{}_{1}}\gamma_{{}_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma_{{}_{1}}\gamma_{{}_{2}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg(\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}}\gamma_{{}_{2}})\phi^{n}_{e}-\phi^{n}_{e}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg(\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}}\gamma_{{}_{2}})\phi^{n}_{e}-\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}})\phi^{n}_{e}\bigg)+\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\bigg(\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}})\phi^{n}_{e}-\phi^{n}_{e}\bigg)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\pi(\gamma_{{}_{1}})\Big(b(\gamma_{{}_{2}})\Big)+b(\gamma_{{}_{1}})

which means that

b
satisfies Condition
(1)
.

It suffices to prove that

\lim\limits_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\big\|b(\gamma)\big\|=+\infty.

The supports of
\pi(\gamma)\phi^{n}_{e}
and
\phi^{n}_{e}
are disjoint when
d(\gamma,e)>2n
. Hence for all
d(\gamma,e)>2n
,

\displaystyle\big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\big\|_{{}_{\ell^{{}^{2n}}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\|\pi(\gamma)\phi^{n}_{e}-\phi^{n}_{e}\Big\|_{{}_{\ell^{{}^{2n}}}}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sqrt[2n]{2}\cdot\Bigg(\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{~\sqrt{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}.

Thus for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
and all
d(\gamma,e)>2N
,

\displaystyle\big\|b(\gamma)\big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\big\|b_{{}_{n}}(\gamma)\big\|_{{}_{\ell^{{}^{2n}}}}^{2}

\displaystyle\geqslant

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\dfrac{1}{~{n\cdot\eth_{{}_{1}}(n)\cdots\eth_{{}_{k}}(n)}~}

which implies that

\lim\limits_{\gamma\rightarrow\infty}\big\|b(\gamma)\big\|^{2}=+\infty.

The result follows.

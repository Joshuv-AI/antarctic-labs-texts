On injectivity of Cohen–Wu homomorphism
Vasily Ionin

Abstract
. We give a new elementary proof of the theorem that a natural map from Milnor’s construction F ⁡ [ S 1 ] F[S^{1}] to the simplicial group AP \mathrm{AP} of pure braids is injective. Our approach is group-theoretic and does not rely on Lie algebras.



1.
Introduction

For every pointed simplicial set
X
, there is Milnor’s free group construction
F[X]
whose geometric realization is homotopy equivalent to
\Omega\Sigma|X|
. In particular,
F[S^{n}]
is a simplicial group such that
F[S^{n}]_{k}
is the free group of rank
\binom{n}{k}
and
|F[S^{n}]|\simeq\Omega S^{n+1}
.

Let
M
be a smooth connected surface. Denote by
P_{n}(M)
the pure braid group with
n
strands on the surface
M
. When
M
is a regular disk, we denote the pure braid group simply as
P_{n}
. The sequence
\mathcal{P}(M)\vcentcolon=\{P_{n+1}(M)\}_{n\geq 0}
forms a
\Delta
-group with face maps given by string deletions.
If
M
admits a non-degenerate vector field, then
\mathcal{P}(M)
could be equipped with the degeneracies (given by string doublings) and become a simplicial group.
For instance, there is a contractible simplicial group
\mathrm{AP}\vcentcolon=\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
\mathrm{AP}_{n}=P_{n+1}
. The 1-simplex
A_{1,2}\in\mathrm{AP}_{1}=P_{2}
is spherical, which defines a simplicial map
S^{1}\to\mathrm{AP}
. There is a unique homomorphism of simplicial groups

(1.1)

\Theta\colon F[S^{1}]\to\mathrm{AP}

that extends this map.
In
[
CW04
,
CW10
]
, Fred Cohen and Jie Wu prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1:
Cohen-Wu embedding

The homomorphism
\Theta
is injective.

Hence, the homotopy type of the coset simplicial set
\mathrm{AP}/F[S^{1}]
is that of the 2-sphere
S^{2}
.

This theorem is of great interest because it realizes homotopy groups
\pi_{n}(S^{2})
as some natural sub-quotients of pure braid groups; in particular it allows to represent elements of homotopy groups of 2-sphere with braids.

The lower central series of a group
G
is defined inductively as

\gamma_{1}(G)\vcentcolon=G,\gamma_{n+1}(G)\vcentcolon=[\gamma_{n}(G),G].

With a group
G
one can associate a graded Lie ring
\mathrm{gr}(G)
given by

\mathrm{gr}(G)\vcentcolon=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\frac{\gamma_{n}(G)}{\gamma_{n+1}(G)},

with a Lie bracket given by a commutator
[x,y]\vcentcolon=xyx^{-1}y^{-1}
. If
f\colon G\to H
is a homomorphism of groups, and
G
is residually nilpotent, then the injectivity of
f
can be deduced from the injectivity of the associated map
\mathrm{gr}(f)\colon\mathrm{gr}(G)\to\mathrm{gr}(H)
. In
[
CW04
,
CW10
]
, authors prove that
\Theta_{n}\colon F[S^{1}]_{n}\to P_{n+1}
is injective by showing that the Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathrm{gr}(\Theta_{n})
is injective. Their proof contains heavy computations that rely on the explicit presentation of the Lie algebra
\mathrm{gr}(P_{n+1})
given by ‘infinitesimal braid relations’. It feels confusing that one has to descend to the level of Lie algebras, since the pure braid group is replete with free subgroups which are free for obvious reasons. For example, one can show very easily that the subgroup
\mathrm{Ker}(d_{i})
in
P_{n+1}
, consisting of braids that become trivial after removing the
i
th strand, is free of rank
n
.

In this paper, we give a new proof of Theorem
1
, which is internal to the group theory.

Our strategy is straightforward. We have to show that some particular braids generate a free subgroup in a pure braid group. First, we ‘untwist’ the problem by picking up a convenient generating set and by finding an automorphism of pure braid group such that it maps generators of subgroup to especially simple-looking braids.
Second, we solve the resulting group-theoretic problem with standard techniques (HNN extensions).

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}_{0,i}

\displaystyle\vcentcolon=A_{0,i}\cdot t_{i}^{2}.

3.
Reduction to the group theoretic problem

Recall that for every
n\geq 1
the group

is free of rank
n

z_{i}=\Big(\underbrace{s^{S^{1}}_{0}\circ\ldots\circ s^{S^{1}}_{0}}_{i-1}\circ\underbrace{s^{S^{1}}_{1}\circ\ldots\circ s^{S^{1}}_{1}}_{n-i}\Big)(z),\quad 1\leq i\leq n,

Since
\Theta_{n}(z)=A_{1,2}

\Theta_{n}\colon F(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\to P_{n+1}

\Theta_{n}(z_{i})=\left(\underbrace{s^{\mathrm{AP}}_{0}\circ\ldots s^{\mathrm{AP}}_{0}}_{i-1}\circ\underbrace{s^{\mathrm{AP}}_{1}\circ\ldots\circ s^{\mathrm{AP}}_{1}}_{n-i}\right)(A_{1,2})=s_{1}^{i-1}s_{2}^{n-i}(A_{1,2})\quad 1\leq i\leq n.

Since finitely generated free groups are Hopfian, it suffices to show that the braids

\displaystyle s_{2}^{n-i+1}(A_{1,2})

\displaystyle=s_{2}s_{2}^{n-i}(A_{1,2})=s_{2}(A_{0,1}^{-1})\quad\text{by induction}

\displaystyle=A_{0,1}^{-1}\quad\text{by \eqref{s_formula}}.

The claim is proved.

Second, we prove the proposition.
The proof is by induction on

\mathrm{Im}(\Theta_{n})=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\rangle\overset{\text{Prop.~\ref{t:imviatilde}}}{\scalebox{2.5}[1]{$=$}}\langle\widetilde{A}_{0,1},\ldots,\widetilde{A}_{0,n}\rangle\overset{\text{Prop.~\ref{t:bigprod}}}{\scalebox{2.5}[1]{$=$}}\langle\widetilde{A}_{0,2},\ldots,\widetilde{A}_{0,n+1}\rangle.

Then

Lemma 5

One has

\displaystyle H_{n}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{def}}{\scalebox{2}[1]{=}}\langle y_{1}x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-2}x_{n-2}y_{n-2},y_{n-1}x_{n-1}y_{n-1},x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle y_{1}x_{1}y_{1},\ldots,y_{n-2}x_{n-2}y_{n-2},y_{n-1}p_{n-1}y_{n-1},p_{n}\rangle.

Proof.

Note that

p_{n}^{y_{n-1}p_{n-1}}=p_{n}^{p_{n}p_{n-1}^{-1}p_{n-1}}=p_{n}.

Hence,

g\vcentcolon=y_{n-1}x_{n-1}y_{n-1}=y_{n-1}p_{n-1}p_{n}^{-1}y_{n-1}=p_{n}^{-1}(y_{n-1}p_{n-1}y_{n-1}).

Therefore, we can replace

with
p_{n}g=y_{n-1}p_{n-1}y_{n-1}
in the initial set of generators.
∎

The advantage of the new generating set is that all but one of its members are in

. Let

be a subgroup of

given by

\displaystyle\cap A_{n+1}\subset d_{n+1}^{-1}(H_{n}\cap\langle A_{n},p_{n}\rangle)\cap L_{n+1}\quad\text{by (1)}

\displaystyle=d_{n+1}^{-1}(\langle p_{n}\rangle)\cap L_{n+1}\quad\text{by inductive hypothesis}

\displaystyle=\langle y_{n}p_{n}y_{n}\rangle\quad\text{since}\;d_{n+1}|_{L_{n+1}}\;\text{is mono}.

It is left to show that
A_{n+1}
and
\langle y_{n}p_{n}y_{n}\rangle
intersect trivially.
Consider a homomorphism

f=d_{1}\circ d_{2}\circ\ldots\circ d_{n-1}\colon P_{n+2}\to P_{3}.

The subgroup
\langle y_{n}p_{n}y_{n}\rangle
maps injectively to
\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}\rangle
under
f
. Also, one has
f(y_{i}x_{i})=1
whenever
1\leq i<n
, and
f(y_{n}x_{n}x_{n+1})=y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}
. Hence,
f(A_{n+1})\subset\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}\rangle
. Therefore,

\langle y_{n}p_{n}y_{n}\rangle\cap A_{n+1}\xhookrightarrow{f}\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}\rangle\cap\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}\rangle.

The result follows since the subgroups

\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}y_{1}=A_{1,2}A_{1,3}A_{2,3}A_{1,2}\rangle\;\text{and}\;\langle y_{1}x_{1}x_{2}=A_{1,2}A_{1,3}A_{2,3}\rangle

do not intersect in
P_{3}
(for example, since
Z(P_{3})=\langle A_{1,2}A_{1,3}A_{2,3}\rangle
, the latter subgroup is central, and the former is not).
∎

The part (2) of Theorem
4
provides a solution to Problem
3
, and thereby proves Theorem
1
.

Successful re-distraction osteogenesis on the consolidated bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis of tibia: A case report
Authors: Asep Santoso, Seti Aji Hadinoto, Hillan Akbar, Dykall Naf'an Dzikri
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTreating massive tibial bone defects resulting from high-energy trauma are pose a significant challenge in the field of orthopaedic surgery. While several methods exist for addressing extensive bone defects, distraction osteogenesis (DO) recognized as one of good options. A 22-year-old male was referred to our orthopaedic clinic due to a wound infection and bone defect on right lower leg area. The patients previously treated with distraction osteogenesis (bone transport) by another surgeon. The patients stopped the distraction process and refuse further follow-up for 6 months. This resulted in consolidation of the distracted bone, however bone defect was still present by 7.5 cm and a skin defect. The patients received soft tissue coverage procedure with cross-leg flap which performed by the orthopaedic micro surgeon. After successful soft tissue coverage, the patient underwent re-osteotomy and another re-distraction osteogenesis procedure of tibia at the bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis. Good quality of callus was resulted after the procedure and finally closed all the bone defect. The total bone resulted from distraction osteogenesis was 23.5 cm. Good quality of bone regeneration could be expected with re-distraction osteogenesis on the consolidated bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis of tibia.Keywords: Bone defect, Distraction osteogenesis, Ilizarov method, Monorail external fixator

Introduction

Treating large tibial bone defects whether caused by severe trauma or the removal of non-healing tissue, poses a challenge in orthopaedic surgery [1]. Shortening the lower limb can lead to immediate issues like pelvic tilt and later complications such as scoliosis, alongside long-term risks like early osteoarthritis in the knee, hip, and spine. Moreover, patients often experience discomfort due to extended periods with an external fixator [2]. There are a few methods for treating massive limb bone defects. The treatment options include autograft with non-vascularized fibula strut graft, Masquelet procedure, and distraction osteogenesis with a monorail external fixator or Ilizarov ring fixator [2], [3]. In this study, we reported an effective re-distraction osteogenesis on the consolidated bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis (DO) of tibia.

Case presentation

A 22-year-old male was referred to our orthopaedic clinic due to a wound infection and bone defect on right lower leg area. The patients previously treated with distraction osteogenesis (bone transport) by another surgeon (Fig. 1). The patients stopped the distraction process and refuse further follow-up for 3 months. This resulted in consolidation of the distracted bone, however bone defect was still present by 7.5 cm and a skin defect. The patients received soft tissue coverage procedure with cross-leg flap which performed by the orthopaedic micro surgeon. After successful soft tissue coverage, the patient underwent re-osteotomy and another re-distraction osteogenesis procedure of tibia at the bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis (Fig. 2A). Good quality of callus was resulted after the procedure and finally closed all the bone defect. The total bone resulted from distraction osteogenesis was 23.5 cm.

Osteotomy site at the distal part of the tibia, unfortunately failed to produce callus.

A. Osteotomy was performed on the previously lengthened bone on the proximal third of the tibia.

B. Insufficient contact between bone fragments at the docking site (New distraction 75,62 mm).

As a result of daily distraction and monthly follow-up two months after surgery, a radiological examination revealed a lengthening of the tibia until the discrepancy was corrected. Distraction was stopped after reaching the docking site. However, when the bone fragments reached the docking site, they had insufficient contact for good bone fusion (Fig. 2B). The realignment surgery of the docking site was carried out to get good contact between the bone fragments (Fig. 3), and the monoplane external fixator was maintained until the bone union was achieved. Patient was advised to start gradual partial weight-bearing mobilization. At removal of the external fixation, tibial distraction osteogenesis measured 7 cm after 16 months since the monoplane bone lengthening program with the total defect was 23.5 cm. In the latter phase of treatment, after removing the fixator, the patient regained the ability to engage in weight-bearing activities without requiring assistance. This improvement in mobility allowed the patient to resume schooling, marking a considerable positive impact on his social life.

Discussion

Treating massive limb bone defects poses significant challenges for orthopaedic specialists due to the prolonged treatment duration, the necessity for multiple surgeries, and the potential for less-than-ideal results. Moreover, managing these injuries tends to incur substantial expenses and frequently leads to a high rate of amputations. Currently, no approach has been identified as the ideal solution for managing substantial tibial bone defects without contemplating amputation [4]. Tetsworth et al. devised a classification system outlining three distinct bone defect types: D1, D2, and D3. Among these, D3 specifically targets segmental or critical-sized defects measuring 2 cm or larger. Further delineating the D3 category, it is subdivided into three subtypes based on the size of the defect. D3A corresponds to moderate defects spanning from 2 cm to less than 4 cm in length. D3B encompasses major defects ranging from 4 cm to less than 8 cm, while D3C involves massive defects that are 8 cm or larger [5]. External fixators have revolutionized treatment options for segmental bone defects in the tibia. Distraction osteogenesis and bone transport offer utility for osseous defects once considered unmanageable [6]. Regarding the management of the defect, although different alternatives have been described for bone defect management in the literature, distraction osteogenesis is currently hailed as the gold standard for the treatment of segmental bone defects of septic origin [7].

Distraction osteogenesis (DO), represents a surgical approach to addressing conditions like leg length inequality, bone irregularities, or massive bone defects [8], [9]. The fundamental principle involves conducting a crosswise bone cut (osteotomy) and gradually separating the two bone sections. This process prompts the generation of new bone material within the gap formed as the segments are incrementally pulled apart. External instruments like the Ilizarov circular fixator or a monoplane device are often employed to carry out this controlled distraction [10], [11].

The ideal latency between surgery and distraction start is 3–10 days. Following thorough instructions given orally and in written form, distraction usually progresses at a rate of 1 mm/day, with four increments daily [12]. The successful bridging of the gap between nonunion and premature consolidation depends upon experience in adapting the latency period and mode of distraction based on local vascularity, direction of lengthening, type of osteotomy, as well as patient factors such as age, smoking habits, bone quality, vascular status, cause and stability of the apparatus. Cho et al. describe a successful tibial bone lengthening of 33.5 cm using DO to reconstruct a subtotal tibial bone defect. This case suggests that DO can successfully achieve significant bone lengthening even in cases of extensive bone loss [13]. Another study by Singh et al. evaluates DO's radiologic and functional outcomes using ring fixators for large bone defects of the tibia. The study included patients with a minimum of 6 cm gap after radical debridement, indicating that DO can be effective for large defects [14]. The conventional method advocated by Ilizarov for addressing bone shortening primarily involves bone lengthening. This technique employs a ring system secured by transfixing Kirschner wires under tensions ranging between 50 and 130 N, followed by an osteotomy and subsequent gradual bone distraction. The pace of lengthening typically proceeds at a rate of 1 mm per day. The Ilizarov approach allows for external management of a broad spectrum of angular adjustments through manipulation of the rods, offering a non-invasive alternative for patients. Nevertheless, several drawbacks have been reported regarding this method. The main disadvantage highlighted is the extended use of external fixation, notably during the healing phase of the regenerated bone, which may lead to infections due to the necessity of keeping the fixator until complete consolidation of the fracture, a process that can be lengthy in cases of bone discrepancy [2].

In our case, the ilizarov external fixator had to be removed from the patient and replaced with a monoplane bone lengthening device as it is more comfortable and easier to apply, reducing the risk of elongation. A retrospective review compares the union achieved with combined Ilizarov and Taylor spatial frames versus a conventional circular frame for tibial nonunions with bone loss. This review indicates that DO can be adapted to different fixation systems to treat complex tibial conditions [15]. A monoplanar bone lengthening program was then performed by osteotomy of the previously elongated bone by the patient. There are only a few references discussing this issue. However, the final clinical outcome showed that the patient can carry out daily activities and walk with full weight bearing after treatment.

Conclusion

Good quality of bone regeneration could be expected with re-distraction osteogenesis on the consolidated bone resulted from previous distraction osteogenesis of tibia.

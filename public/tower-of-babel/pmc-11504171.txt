Establishment and validation of the prognostic risk model based on the anoikis-related genes in esophageal squamous cell carcinoma
Authors: Shasha Cao, Ming Li, Zhiying Cui, Yutong Li, Wei Niu, Weiwei Zhu, Junkuo Li, Lijuan Duan, Shumin Lun, Zhaowei Gao, Yaowen Zhang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundEsophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) is a malignant condition in humans. Anoikis-related genes (ARGs) are crucial to cancer progression. Therefore, more studies on the relationship between ARGs and ESCC are warranted.MethodsThe study acquired ESCC-related transcriptome data from TCGA. Differentially expressed ARGs (DE-ARGs) were obtained by differential analysis and candidates were filtered out by survival analysis. Prognostic genes were determined by Cox and LASSO regression. A risk model was constructed based on prognostic gene expressions. An immune infiltration study was done to explain how these genes contribute to ESCC development. The IC50 test was adopted to assess the clinical response of chemotherapy drugs. Single cell analysis was performed on the GSE145370 dataset. Moreover, the prognostic gene expressions were detected by qRT-PCR.Results53 DE-ARGs were screened and four candidate genes including PBK, LAMC2, TNFSF10 and KL were obtained. Cox and LASSO regression identified the two prognostic genes, TNFSF10 and PBK. Immuno-infiltration analysis revealed positive associations of PBK with Macrophages M0 cells, and TNFSF10 with Macrophages M1 cells. The IC50 values of predicted drugs, in the case of Tozasertib 1096 and WIKI4 1940, were significantly variant between risk groups. Single cell analysis revealed that TNFSF10 and PBK levels were higher in epithelial cells than in other cells. The prognostic genes expression results by qRT-PCR were compatible with the dataset analysis.ConclusionThe study established an ARG prognosis model of ESCC. It provided a reference for the research of ARGs in ESCC.

Introduction

Oesophageal carcinoma is an extremely malignant tumour in the world. 87% of oesophageal carcinoma cases are oesophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC), with a 5-year relative survival of fewer than 20% [1,2]. Even with the advancement of diagnostic and treatment approaches, ESCC patients’ survival period was not appreciably extended [3–5]. Prognostic factors are used for stratifying the patients’ risk level and predicting the clinical outcome. Conventional prognostic factors are mainly composed of TNM stage, and lymph node metastasis, which affect prognosis judgement and guide treatment. However, shortcoming of conventional prognostic factors is that they fail to achieve accurate prediction as the survival outcomes of the same clinicopathological characteristics patients differ greatly from each other [6]. Therefore, focusing on the discovery of new prognostic factors will improve the evaluation of prognosis and the development of therapeutic targets.

Anoikis is a caspase-mediated apoptosis owing to the loss of attachment between cell and extracellular matrix 1 [7,8]. A correct attachment and interaction with extracellular matrix proteins is vital to define whether a cell is currently in the correct location. Hence, anoikis is a normal physiological process involved in tissue homeostasis and development and its deregulation often occurs in several diseases [9,10]. Numerous studies indicated ARGs play a central role in gastric, lung, breast, and endometrial cancer metastatic cascade and progression [11–14]. Nonetheless, little is known regarding anoikis functions in ESCC. As a result, it is crucial to comprehend their interaction in greater detail. We downloaded mRNA expression data together with the ARGs from TCGA and GeneCards to determine DE-ARGs in ESCC, and then performed survival analysis to determine prognostic hub genes. A two-ARG signature (including PBK and TNFSF10) was identified by univariate, LASSO and multivariate cox regression. We evaluated predictive value of the prognostic model in TCGA and GSE53622 dataset via KM survival, ROC, and risk curve methods. A nomogram including risk score, N stage and pathological stage was built and DCA curve of the combined model showed greater prognosis-predictive property than either alone. Besides, Differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between high- and low-risk groups were identified, then Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopaedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) analyses were done. In addition, the tumour microenvironment was analyzed to explore the correlations between the risk model and ESCC-infiltrative immune cells. The chemotherapeutic response was calculated via the oncoPredict R package. Moreover, we adopted single cell analysis to identify associations of prognostic genes with cell clusters. Finally, qRT-PCR was also applied to further validate two prognostic ARG expressions. Overall, our work established an anoikis-related prognostic signature and may provide a new clue for exploring the relationship between ARGs and ESCC.

Materials and methods

Data collection

From the cancer genome atlas (TCGA; https://about-nci/organization/ccg/research/structural-genomics/tcga), 82 samples of ESCC and 11 samples of normal oesophageal tissue were obtained as the training set, which included gene expression information, survival information, clinical data (age, clinical stage, pathological grade, etc.), and mutation information. GSE53622 dataset involving 60 normal samples and 60 ESCC samples was obtained as a validation set from GEO at https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/gds [15]. ARGs were downloaded from GeneCards (https://genecards.weizmann.ac.il/v3/).

Screening for DE-ARGs

The DE-ARGs between ESCC samples and normal oesophageal tissue samples in TCGA were screened by setting the conditions |log2FC| > 2.5 and p.adjust < 0.001 with limma package [16]. Heatmap was drawn using pheatmap package [17] to show DE-ARGs and volcano map was plotted using the ggplot2 package [18] to show the results. Next, DE-ARGs with p < 0.005 were selected as candidate genes for survival analysis, and survival curves were plotted for visualization. Finally, sample scores were calculated using single sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA), and samples were assigned into high- and low-expression groups of candidate genes based on scores. Candidate genes with high and low expressions were subjected to survival analysis.

Prognostic gene acquisition and risk model development

Candidate genes were screened for prognostic genes based on the prognostic information of ESCC samples in TCGA by univariate, multivariate cox and LASSO regression analysis respectively. The risk model was built with prognostic gene expressions. We computed risk scores of samples in the training set based on riskscore = β1 × X1 + β2 × X2+…+βn × Xn, and divided ESCC samples into high- and low-risk groups with median risk scores as criteria. Principal component analysis (PCA) was done to show the ability of risk scores for samples. Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival analysis was conducted on risk groups. Results were visualized by plotting the survival curve. Model accuracy was tested by plotting the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve. In addition, we used external validation set GSE53622 to verify the validity of the risk model, and prognostic gene levels between normal and ESCC groups were also validated.

Gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) of prognostic genes

Firstly, the study downloaded the hallmark gene set and immunomic signatures gene set as background sets for enrichment analysis. Then, correlation coefficients were set to |NES| > 1, p < 0.05 and q values < 0.25 as criteria for GSEA enrichment analysis.

Correlation analysis of risk scores and clinical features

To understand correlations between risk scores in the training set and different clinicopathological characteristics, we used the Wilcoxon test and the Kruskal-Wallis test to analyze the risk scores in different clinical characteristics (e.g. age, clinical stage, pathological grade) based on threshold p < 0.05. In addition, risk scores for each subtype under clinical characteristics were analyzed, and K-M survival curves were plotted to show results. Lastly, based on data such as survival status, survival time, age, and pathological characteristics, we utilized the survey (version 4.4.2) and gtsummary (version 2.0.1) [19] packages to generate baseline tables grouped by high- and low-risk categories. These results were then validated using χ2 tests and Mann-Whitney U tests.

Independent prognostic analysis of risk model and the generation of a nomogram

The study included clinical features including age, gender, and ESCC stage in univariate Cox regression. Then, risk scores and clinical features were analyzed for independent prognosis using multivariate Cox regression analysis with a screening threshold of p < 0.05. Forest plots were drawn to present results for analysis. Furthermore, to predict the survival rate of patients at different stages, a nomogram was constructed following univariate and multivariate Cox regressions. A correction curve was made to test the predictive accuracy of the nomogram. Decision curve analysis (DCA) was plotted to evaluate the degree of patient benefit.

Functional enrichment analysis of risk groups

The limma package with condition |log2FC| > 0.5, p < 0.05 was implemented to screen DEGs between risk groups in TCGA data. Then we utilized clusterprofiler package [19,20] to do GO and KEGG analyses on DEGs.

Analysis of tumour microenvironment

To observe composition of immune cells among the training set samples, CIBERSORT algorithm was implemented to compute percentage abundance of 22 immune cells. Distribution of immune cells between risk groups was compared by Wilcox test. Correlation analysis of prognostic gene and immune cell abundance was performed using spearman.

Establishment of miRNA-mRNA-TF and miRNA-lncRNA regulatory networks

Firstly, we applied TRRUST website to extract transcription factors (TF) of prognostic genes and TF-mRNA ­relationships. Then, the upstream miRNAs of prognostic genes were predicted by TargetScan and miRTarBase databases. The TF-mRNA relationship pairs and mRNA-miRNAs were intersected to establish miRNA-mRNA-TF regulatory network. Finally, lncRNAs were predicted by LncRNASNP database based on upstream miRNAs, and miRNA-lncRNA regulatory network was built.

Chemotherapy analysis

Using oncopredict package [21], half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50) for each patient in risk groups of the training set was computed based on GDSC website to assess therapeutic response rate and sensitivity to traditional chemotherapeutic medicines in risk groups. The expression of IC50 values of chemotherapeutic drugs between risk groups was analyzed by rank sum test.

Single cell analysis

Cell clustering and identification analysis was performed using Seurat based on the GSE145370 dataset (7 normal samples and 7 ESCC samples). First, the FindVariableFeatures function was used to identify genes with high variability in the dataset, and then the ScaleData function was used to normalize the data in order to eliminate the differences in expression levels between genes. After data preprocessing, the top 20 principal components were chosen for clustering using FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions set to dims = 1: 20, resolution = 0.25, and Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) was used for cell category visualization. Lastly, the ROC analysis provided by the ‘FindAllMarkers’ function was used to identify marker genes for each cell cluster. We calculated prognostic gene expressions in varying classes of cells to display their relationship with cell classes, and screened out the cell classes which was the higher expression of prognostic genes. Expression profiles were plotted to demonstrate that the cell category with the most relevant expression in ESCC samples and normal samples, and their correlation between nFeature-RNA and nCount-RNA Cell clusters of this cell category were identified by UMAP analysis, and we calculated the correlation between DEGs and prognostic gene expression in cell clusters.

qRT-PCR

10 ESCC and corresponding paracancerous tissues were obtained from Anyang Tumor Hospital. All participants provided an informed consent and this study was approved by the ethics committee of Anyang Tumor Hospital. Total RNA was extracted with TRIzol reagent (Cat. No. 356281, Ambion) and quantified with NanoPhotometer N50 (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA). Reverse transcription of mRNA was done with SweScript RT I First-strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (cat.: G3330-50) from the Servicebio company. The diluted cDNA was subjected to the qPCR reaction and GAPDH was utilized as an internal reference. Relative mRNA level was normalized and computed by the 2-ΔΔCt method. Results

Screening of DE-ARGs and candidate prognostic markers

Based on the TCGA and GeneCards, we isolated ARGs expression data and compared them between 82 ESCC and 11 paracancerous samples. As shown in the volcano map (Figure 1A), 53 DE-ARGs (46 upregulation, 7 downregulation; Supplemental Table 1) were screened by differential expression analysis. The heatmap of DE-ARGs is depicted in the Figure 1B. Survival analysis of DE-ARGs was performed to identify candidate genes with p < 0.005. Ultimately, four genes including PBK, LAMC2, TNFSF10 and KL were selected for the following analysis. Figure 1C-F shows the survival probability was lower in low PBK and LAMC2 expression group than in high expression group, while survival probability was lower in high TNFSF10 and KL expression group than in low-expression group. The enrichment of samples on the gene set was calculated by ssGSEA. Survival curves indicated that high levels of four candidate genes were implicated in shorter overall survival (OS) of ESCC patients (Figure 1G).

Determination of DE-ARGs and candidate prognostic genes. (A) Volcano plot shown the distribution of dysregulated DE-ARGs. (B) The heatmap exhibited the DE-ARGs between ESCC tumor samples and normal samples. (C-F) K-M survival curves of patients based on the four candidate genes in ESCC. (G) K-M survival curves based on ssGSEA enrichment scores of four candidate genes.

Determination of prognostic genes and establishment of a risk model

To find vital genes in the prognosis of ESCC, univariate Cox regression was done based on four candidate genes. According to results (p < 0.05), TNFSF10 and PBK were identified with significant effects on patient prognosis (Figure 2A). LASSO regression was utilized to avoid overfitting in identifying stable markers from two survival-related candidates (Figure 2B, C). Ultimately, a prognostic signature comprising the two genes (TNFSF10 and PBK) was constructed using stepwise multivariate regression (Figure 2D). A prognostic risk model was generated: riskscore = 0.4861524*Exp (TNFSF10) − 0.3919675*Exp (PBK). Furthermore, we found that TNFSF10 was a risk factor with HR > 1, while PBK was a favourable factor with HR < 1.

Screening of prognostic ARGs. (A) Univariate cox regression analysis identified two ARGs related to the ESCC risks. (B) Partial likelihood deviance was plotted versus log(lambda) in LASSO model. (C) LASSO coefficient profile of two ARGs. (D) Forest map of the prognostic index based on two ARGs via multivariate cox regression analysis.

Verification of risk model

To explore the predictive performance of the two-ARG signature (TNFSF10 and PBK), we computed risk scores. Using median risk score of 2.4077608, patients in the training set were assigned into high- (n = 47) and low-risk (n = 34) groups. PCA showed that samples could be distinguished by the risk score profile (Supplemental Figure 1). A comparison of survival times revealed that patients in the low-risk group outlived those in the high-risk group (Figure 3A). Then, ROC analysis investigated diagnostic value. AUC calculated respectively from ROC curves were 0.780 and 0.791 for 1 and 3 years, indicating that the risk model had some degree of predictive power (Figure 3C). Thereafter, Figure 3E presents the risk curve and the gene expression heatmap of two hub ARGs of patients. Furthermore, we tested prognosis effects of risk model in another independent dataset, GSE53622. K-M survival curves displayed that patients in high-risk set had a shorter OS (Figure 3B). AUC for 1- and 3-year OS were 0.684 and 0.627 respectively (Figure 3D). Patients’ risk scores, survival time, and the two ARGs levels were also presented (Figure 3F). In summary, the risk model had a satisfying performance for OS prediction.

Validation of ARG-based prognostic signature. K-M survival, time-dependent ROC and risk score analyses in training set of TCGA cohort (A, C, E) and validation set of GSE53622 cohort (B, D, F).

Functional annotation analysis on prognostic gene

To clarify the potential function and involved pathways of the two prognostic genes, GSEA was done based on hallmark gene set and immunomic signatures gene set. For TNFSF10, 32 entries were enriched in hallmark, such as a subgroup of genes regulated by MYC - ­version 1 (v1), genes encoding cell cycle-related targets of E2F TFs, genes involved in DNA repair, and so on (Figure 4A). Besides, 4558 enriched entries were obtained in immunomic signatures, containing genes downregulated CD8 T cells (mock transduced versus wildtype), etc. (Figure 4B). For PBK, hallmark results indicated that 34 pathways were enriched including a subgroup of genes regulated by MYC - version 1 (v1), a subgroup of genes regulated by MYC-version 2 (v2), genes encoding proteins involved in glycolysis and gluconeogenesis (Figure 4C). Based on immunomic signatures, PBK was involved in 4262 enrichment pathways, including genes downregulated in dendritic cells and B cells (Figure 4D).

GSEA in the hallmark pathways (A, C) and immunomic signatures (B, D) of TNFSF10 (A, B) and PBK (C, D).

Correlation analysis of risk scores and clinical features

To investigate clinical value of prognostic signature, association of risk score with clinical features was investigated. Correlation analysis unveiled significant differences in risk scores between pathologic-N0, pathologic-N1, pathologic-N2 and pathologic-N3, and highly significant differences in the alive and dead categories of status (Figure 5A, B). We also validated the prognostic prediction capability in different subgroups. K-M survival curves demonstrated that survival rates of pathologic-T2, pathologic-N0, pathologic M0 and tumour stage II were lower in high-risk group (Figure 5C-F). The OS and Nstage (lymph node metastasis) variables in the baseline table can prove that there is a difference in survival rates between the high-risk and low-risk groups, with higher survival rates in the low-risk group and lower survival rates in the high-risk group (Table 2). Taken together, risk signature could function as a great potential indicator for OS prediction in ESCC.

Independent prognostic analysis and establishment of nomogram

To find out if our model was a clinically independent prognostic factor for ESCC patients, univariate and multivariate regression analysis were employed. It was concluded from the forest plot that risk score, pathological stage and N stage were significant factors associated with survival and could be utilized as independent prognostic factors (Figure 6A, B). According to these three factors, we then built a nomogram for 1- and 3-year OS prediction (Figure 6C). C-index values for prognostic were 0.759. Calibration curve analysis revealed that observed versus predicted rates of 1- and 3-year OS displayed good consistency (Figure 6D). DCA displayed that the combined model including three independent prognostic factors (riskscore + N+stage) had the best net benefit for 1- and 3-year OS than the alternative options (that of each factor alone) (Figure 6E).

(C-D) a nomogram containing pathological stage, risk score and N stage for 1- and 3-year OS prediction. (E) The DCA curves of nomogram.

Annotated analysis of the function of DEGs

To further explore biological functions and pathways of ARGs, we analyzed DEGs between high- and low-risk groups in the training set. 1852 DEGs (722 upregulated; 1130 downregulated) were screened by differential analysis (Figure 7A). A heat map was displayed in Figure 7B. GO and KEGG analyses presented enrichment of DEGs in 450 GO terms and 39 pathways. GO pointed out the enrichment in biological processes like cellular response to interferon-gamma and type I interferon signalling pathway (Figure 7C). KEGG unveiled enrichment in cell adhesion molecules, and phagosome (Figure 7D).

Immuno-infiltration analysis

The tumour microenvironment was analyzed to screen differences in immune infiltrating cells in risk groups. By using the CIBERSORT algorithm with values of p < 0.05, the relative proportions of 22 tumour-infiltrating immune cells were acquired. As illustrated by the box plot in Figure 8A, T.cells.CD4.memory.resting exhibited an increase in low-risk group, whereas expressions of B.cells.memory and T. cells.Follicular.Helper were significantly increased in high-risk group. To further investigate the correlation between immune infiltrating cells and the prognosis genes, Spearman’s correlation analysis with p < 0.05 and | cor | > 0.3 was applied. The statistical correlations were summarized in Figure 8B. The Scatter plot revealed that PBK was positively linked to Macrophages M0 cells, whereas TNFSF10 was positively correlated with Macrophages M1 cells in Figure 8C.

Establishment of regulatory network

TRRUST database was applied to extract the TFs of prognostic genes and 17 pairs of mRNA-TF relationships were obtained. The miRNA-mRNA interaction relationships were downloaded from miRTarBase and TargetScan to screen for miRNA interacting with the two prognostic genes. A total of 34 overlapping targeting relationship pairs existed in the two databases. After miRNA-mRNA network and mRNA-TF network were predicted, miRNA-mRNA-TF relationships were tested. miRNA-mRNA-TF relationship pairs such as hsa-miR-562-TNFSF10-FOXO3 and hsa-miR-216b-5p-PBK-ATM were obtained by taking the intersection of mRNA-TF and miRNA-mRNA network (Figure 9A, B). The lncRNASNP database was utilized to predict lncRNAs targeted by 34 identified miRNAs. It can be seen from the miRNA-lncRNA regulatory relationship network diagram that 602 miRNA-lncRNA ­regulatory relationship pairs were predicted, including hsa-miR-130b-3p-LINC00339, hsa-miR-26a-5p-lnc-ATP6V1G3-4, etc. (Figure 9C).

Construction of miRNA-mRNA-TF and miRNA-lncRNA regulatory network. (A-B) miRNA-mRNA-TF network of TNFSF10 (A) and PBK (B). Pink represents TF and orange represents miRNA. (C) The diagram shown the 602 identified miRNA-lncRNA pairs according to the LncRNASNP database.

Chemotherapy drug susceptibility analysis

Based on the oncoPredict R package, an association of risk group with drug sensitivity was explored by calculating the IC50 value. The box plot illustrated that IC50 values of predicted drugs, in the case of Tozasertib 1096 and WIKI4 1940, were significantly variant between risk groups by rank sum test at p < 0.05 (Figure 10). We observed that patients in the low-risk group were more sensitive to Tozasertib 1096, while patients in the high-risk group were more sensitive to WIKI4 1940. It can be indicated that risk score can be a favourable source indicator for providing a basis for drug selection.

Single cell analysis

ScRNA sequencing data of seven normal samples and seven ESCC samples were downloaded from the GSE145370 dataset and analyzed to identify and characterize cell populations. As with other clusters, significant DEGs in every cluster were identified by ‘FindAllMarkers’ function and then cell types were identified in an unbiased manner by the specific markers. We observed that ten cell clusters including NK cells, T cells, monocytes/macrophages, B cells, mDC, pDC, plasma cells, mast cells, fibroblasts, and epithelial cells were identified by UMAP analysis (Figure 11A). Subsequently, two prognostic gene expressions in each cell type were visualized by a dot plot in Figure 11B. TNFSF10 was higher in epithelial cells and mast cells, while PBK was higher in epithelial cells than other cell types. The violin plot in Figure 11C showed the expression of epithelial and mast cells in ESCC samples and normal samples. Additionally, the correlation value between nFeature-RNA and nCount-RNA was 0.93 and 0.96 for epithelial and mast cells (Figure 11D). Subsequently, we further clustered the two-cell category and calculated the correlation between DEGs and prognostic gene expression based on |Cor| > 0.3 and p < 0.05. 964 DEGs were screened by cell clustering for epithelial (Figure 11E, F), and 252 DEGs were screened for mast cells (Figure 11G, H).

Validation of prognostic genes expression by qRT-PCR

In the training set, both TNFSF10 and PBK were increased in ESCC compared to the paracancerous tissues (Figure 12A, B). Consistent with the results in TCGA, the average expression levels of TNFSF10 and PBK in validation set were significantly upregulated in the cancer tissues (Figure 12C, D). To confirm the expression pattern of the two biomarkers, qRT-PCR was performed. Results indicated that two gene expressions were completely congruous with data mining (Figure 12E, F).

Discussion

Owing to complicated molecular mechanisms, ESCC is a rapidly progressive disease with extremely poor outcome [22,23]. Therefore, identifying novel reliable biomarkers to predict outcome and prognosis targets of ESCC is urgent. Anoikis is a special programmed apoptosis that is not only essential for maintaining tissue homeostasis but also an important factor in tumour invasion and metastasis [24]. At the same time, the construction of prognostic models based on genes related to apoptosis has been analyzed and validated in a variety of cancers (e.g. bladder cancer, lung adenocarcinoma, glioblastoma) [25–27]. Although the mechanism of anoikis in ESCC has been preliminarily studied [28,29], the prognostic value of anoikis-related genes in ESCC needs to be further clarified. Here, we constructed a two-anoikis-related gene (TNFSF10 and PBK) risk model to screen for ESCC risk and found that high risk group was associated with poor prognosis. The results showed that the AUC values in TCGA are all higher than 0.7. Pan [30] adopted univariate and LASSO cox regression for analyzing ESCC patient data obtained from the TCGA database to construct a risk model based on pyroptosis-related genes with an AUC greater than 0.7. Judging from the AUC values, the prediction accuracy of the two models is satisfactory. However, the approach adopted in our study is capable of better capturing the complex relationships among variables, minimizing potential information loss, and providing robust support for clinical risk assessment and individualized treatment strategies.

TNFSF10, as a TNF-associated apoptosis-inducing ligand, can induce tumour cell apoptosis by interacting with its receptor [31]. Numerous studies have indicated TNFSF10 is involved in modulation of OS in ovarian cancer, prostate cancer, and breast cancer patients [32–35]. Zhang et al. found that TNFSF10 was enhanced in oesophageal tumour tissues, and it was related to patient development and progression [36]. Additionally, its overexpression is negatively relevant to patients’ survival/clinical outcomes, This finding is consistent with our research results, indicating that TNFSF10 can be a biomarker for ESCC patient prognosis.

PBK is a mitotic serine/threonine kinase that manipulates cell proliferation, apoptosis, metastasis and inflammation [37–39]. Prior studies have defined PBK is enhanced in lung, stomach and ovarian cancers and is associated with poor prognoses [40–42]. The high expression of PBK is also associated with chemotherapy resistance of cancer cells [43]. Ohashi et al. indicated that an increased PBK is linked to unfavourable prognoses in ESCC [44]. In contrast to this issue, Zheng et al. revealed that overexpression of PBK predicts better prognoses prognosis for ESCC patients [45]. The results of this study also indicated that PBK was a favourable factor (HR = 0.676, p = 0.027). Bias in individual cohort studies might be the cause of the inconsistent outcome. Collectively, these data suggest that PBK is likely to be a prognosis indicator and treatment target.

GSEA analysis based on hallmark showed that TNFSF10 was functionally enriched in 32 entries, including a subgroup of genes regulated by MYC-version 1 (v1). C-MYC has been defined as a proto-oncogene that affects tumorigenesis and tumour development [46–48]. Moreover, several published studies have shown that c-MYC is overexpressed in a large proportion of ESCC patients, and that suppressing MYC attenuates malignant growth [49–52]. Significant enrichment entries of TNFSF10 among the immunomic signatures contain genes downregulated CD8 T cells (mock transduced versus wildtype), etc. CD8+ T cells are key mediators of tumour elimination by secreting perforin and granzymes. Thus, downregulated CD8+ T cells diminish anti-tumour responses, thereby allowing tumour growth. Consistently, a high level of TNFSF10 indicated poorer prognosis in ESCC. With the development of modern medicine, chemotherapy combined with immune checkpoint inhibitors has become the main treatment for ESCC [53]. The effectiveness of TNFSF10 as an immunotherapy agent has been demonstrated [54]. TNFSF10 can induce cancer cell death by binding to the receptor to activate the Caspase-8-dependent apoptotic pathway and can also stimulate the activation of CD4+ and CD8+ T cells and accelerate the differentiation of T cells into short-life effecting memory T cells [55]. In this study, TNFSF10 showed a significant positive correlation with Macrophages M1. Tumour-associated macrophages (TAMs) can be converted to M1 type by the pro-inflammatory IFN-γ to directly kill tumour cells, or to mediate adaptive immunity by recruiting and activating CD8 T cells and NK cells after presenting tumour antigens and producing chemokines and cytokines [56]. Therefore, we speculated that TNFSF10 may be a potential target for ESCC therapy.

PBK is highly expressed in a variety of cancers and regulates the malignant progression of tumours primarily through its involvement in the cell cycle and apoptosis pathways [57–59]. In this study, hallmark results indicated that 34 pathways were enriched, including genes encoding cell cycle-related targets of E2F TFs. E2F family of TFs is crucial in the events of cell functions linked to DNA replication and cell cycle progression [60]. E2F1, E2F3, E2F5, and E2F8 have been previously researched and shown to play vital roles in ESCC [61–63]. Based on immunomic signatures, PBK was involved in 4262 enrichment pathways, including genes downregulated in dendritic cells and B cells. Dendritic cells and B cells are vital antigen-presenting cells in events of initiation and modulation of innate and adaptive antitumour immune response [64,65]. Reducing the dendritic cells may weaken antitumour immunity in ESCC. A comprehensive pan-cancer analysis showed that PBK was negatively correlated with immune infiltration levels, but positively correlated with M0 and M1 macrophage infiltration, T cell CD4 memory activating cells, and T cell follicle levels [58]. In this study, PBK was positively correlated with M0 and M1 macrophage infiltration, T cell CD4 memory activating cells, and NK resting cells. Ma et al. [66] analyzed the prognosis and immune effects of PBK in LUAD and found that immune escape triggered by high expression of PBK may be related to antigen presentation, dendritic cells, and CD8+ T cell infiltration. Therefore, we speculated that the favourable prognostic response of PBK in ESCC may be related to its anti-tumour immune response.

Anoikis resistance is a feature of cancer metastasis [67,68]. The main molecular pathways and mechanisms involved in drug resistance of anoikis cells mainly include cell adhesion molecules, growth factors and signal transduction pathways inducing epithelial-mesenchymal transition [69]. The enrichment results of this study also showed that the high- and low-risk groups were mainly enriched in cell adhesion molecules and other pathways. Previous studies have shown that the interaction of PBK with hypoxia-inducing factor 1 (HIF1) can induce hypoxia-mediated tumour immune escape through the HIF-1α pathway to upregulate the expression of programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) and vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGFA), thereby rendering immunotherapy and chemotherapy ineffective or resistant [66, 70, 71]. TNFSF10-related targeted therapeutics have been developed for cancer therapy, but their resistance to apoptosis is still a key factor in the development of malignant phenotypes in cancer cells [72,73]. Therefore, the immune function of the two model genes in this study in ESCC and whether they can cause anoikis resistance need further investigation and analysis.

In this study, two candidate drugs containing Tozasertib_1096 and WIKI4_1940 were searched out in high- and low-risk patients. Tozasertib, an aurora kinase inhibitor, plays pivotal roles in several mitotic events including mitotic spindle formation, microtubule-kinetochore attachment, and so on [74]. Microtubule and tubulin binding molecular functions were identified from GO analysis, which illustrated that these were the candidate signalling pathways associated with risk signature and chemotherapy drug sensitivity.

Anoikis-related gene signature offers a practical way to evaluate ESCC patients’ prognoses and a viable strategy for selecting chemotherapeutic treatments to outline an individual treatment plan. In ESCC, several prognostic models have been developed, such as fibroblast-related features [75], multiple programmed cell death related features [76], and even preliminary analyses have been performed in anoikis-related genes [77]. In contrast, this study not only verified the expression level of genes related to anoikis in ESCC, but also analyzed the correlation between the expression level of single gene and immune cells. We also conducted miRNA-mRNA-TF network analysis to analyze the potential analysis mechanism as well as making predictions about potential drugs. Therefore, our findings can further complement the prognostic characteristics of genes related to anoikis in ESCC, and further confirm the important roles of PBK and TNFSF10 as prognostic genes in ESCC. However, several limitations must be considered. The model currently lacks external validation datasets even after we validated risk model prognostic values and prediction accuracy in 142 confirmed ESCC patients and confirmed two prognostic gene expressions using qRT-PCR. To validate and improve the model, datasets and bioinformatics techniques are warranted. Next, to substantiate the theory that the proposed two-ARG signature functions in anoikis, fundamental experiments are required. Moreover, in vitro and vivo research is warranted to evaluate prediction performance of drug sensitivity findings.

Conclusions

In summary, this study constructed a novel prognostic model of ESCC based on two anoikis-related genes (PBK and TNFSF10). This model not only has a good prognostic ability, but also can effectively stratify the risk of patients and evaluate the effect of immunotherapy. At the same time, the immunoinfiltration effect of PBK and TNFSF10 in ESCC patients can help patients make treatment decisions. Furthermore, the miRNA-mRNA-TF network analysis contributes to our understanding of molecular regulatory mechanism of anoikis process in ESCC. The drugs predicted in this study can be used as potential options for future ESCC chemotherapy drugs.

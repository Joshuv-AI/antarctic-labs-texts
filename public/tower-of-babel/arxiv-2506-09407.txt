Keywords: KWC system, pseudo-parabolic structure, optimal control problem, existence and semi-continuous dependence, 1st order necessary condition of optimality

Abstract.

The KWC system is a well-known generic framework for phase-field models of grain boundary
motion, whose original formulation is given as a parabolic gradient flow of a free energy. In the
original KWC system, the results of uniqueness have been relatively scarce compared to other
issues, such as existence, qualitative behavior, and numerics. This lack of progress has posed a
significant challenge for more advanced topics, including optimal control.
To overcome this, the authors have recently introduced the pseudo-parabolic structure to
simultaneously preserve the gradient flow nature of free-energy, and to ensure the
well-posedness including the uniqueness. The goal of this paper is to study an optimization
problem constrained by pseudo-parabolic KWC system. The theory will be developed
through a series of Main Theorems concerning the existence and semi-continuous
dependence of optimal controls, and first-order necessary conditions for optimality.

Let

\displaystyle[u^{*},v^{*}]\in\mathscr{U}_{\rm ad}:=\{[\tilde{u},\tilde{v}]\in[\mathscr{H}]^{2}\,|\,\underline{u}\leq\tilde{u}\leq\overline{u}\mbox{ a.e. in }Q\},\mbox{ and }

(0.2)

\displaystyle\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle:[u,v]\in[\mathscr{H}]^{2}\mapsto\mathscr{J}_{\varepsilon}(u,v)

\displaystyle:=\frac{M_{\eta}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|(\eta-\eta_{\rm ad})(t)|_{H}^{2}\,dt+\frac{M_{\theta}}{2}\int_{0}^{T}|(\theta-\theta_{\rm ad})(t)|_{H}^{2}\,dt

\displaystyle\mbox{{\bf(S)}${}_{\varepsilon}$}:

Physically, the operator
\mathcal{A}_{0}(\eta)
represents the mobility (driving force) of grain boundary motion. In the KWC model, grain boundaries are assumed to form in regions where the orientation order
\eta

In the formula (
0.10

have demonstrated that the pseudo-parabolic formulation leads to a mathematically well-posed version of the KWC system. This development indicates that incorporating pseudo-parabolic feature provides a solid foundation for advancing the study of optimal control problems for grain boundary motion, even in the unknown dependent setting of the mobility.

Consequently, we set the goal to establish a mathematical theory of optimization governed by the pseudo-parabolic KWC system (S)

Main Theorem 2:

\varepsilon

Main Theorem 3:

First-order necessary optimality conditions when
\varepsilon>0

Main Theorem 4:

Necessary condition for optimality when
\varepsilon=0

\gamma_{0}:y=[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto|y|=\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+\dots+y_{N}^{2}}\in[0,\infty)
coincides with the following set-valued function

\displaystyle\dots,y_{N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mapsto\mathop{\mathrm{Sgr}}\nolimits(y)=\mathop{\mathrm{Sgr}}\nolimits(y_{1},\dots,y_{N})

\displaystyle:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\lx@intercol\displaystyle\frac{y}{|y|}=\frac{[y_{1},\dots,y_{N}]}{\sqrt{y_{1}^{2}+\cdots+y_{N}^{2}}},\hfil\lx@intercol\mbox{if $y\neq 0$,}\\[12.91663pt]
\overline{\mathbb{B}^{N}},&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

( I )

For every

, the subdifferential

is identified with the (single-valued) usual gradient, i.e.:

(1.13)

Moreover, since:

\displaystyle\gamma_{\varepsilon}(y)=\bigl|[\varepsilon,y]\bigr|_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}

\displaystyle=\bigl|[\varepsilon,y_{1},\dots,y_{N}]\bigr|_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}},\mbox{ for all $[\varepsilon,y]=[\varepsilon,y_{1},\dots,y_{N}]\in\mathbb{R}^{N+1}$,}

\varepsilon\geq 0

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\displaystyle\bigl|\nabla\gamma_{\varepsilon}(y)\bigr|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}=\left|\frac{y}{\bigl|[\varepsilon,y]\bigr|_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}}\right|_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\leq\left|\frac{[\varepsilon,y]}{\bigl|[\varepsilon,y]\bigr|_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}}\right|_{\mathbb{R}^{N+1}}=1,\\[12.91663pt]
\displaystyle|\partial_{i}\partial_{j}\gamma_{\varepsilon}(y)|\leq\frac{1}{\varepsilon},~\mbox{ for all $\varepsilon>0$, $y\in\mathbb{R}^{N}$, and $i,j=1,\dots,N$.}\end{cases}

Finally, we mention about a notion of convergence of convex functions, known as “Mosco-convergence”.

Definition 1

(Mosco-convergence: cf.
[
31
]
)
.

Let
X
be an abstract Hilbert space. Let
\Psi:X\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
be a proper, l.s.c., and convex function, and let
\{\Psi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of proper, l.s.c., and convex functions
\Psi_{n}:X\rightarrow(-\infty,\infty]
,
n=1,2,3,\dots
. Then, it is said that
\Psi_{n}\to\Psi
on
X
, in the sense of Mosco, as
n\to\infty
, iff. the following two conditions are fulfilled:

(M1
) The condition of lower-bound:

\displaystyle\varliminf_{n\to\infty}\Psi_{n}(\check{w}_{n})\geq\Psi(\check{w})
, if
\check{w}\in X
,
\{\check{w}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X
, and
\check{w}_{n}\to\check{w}
weakly in
X
, as
n\to\infty
.

(M2
) The condition of optimality:

for any
\hat{w}\in D(\Psi)
, there exists a sequence
\{\hat{w}_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset X
such that
\hat{w}_{n}\to\hat{w}
in
X
and
\Psi_{n}(\hat{w}_{n})\to\Psi(\hat{w})
, as
n\to\infty
.

As well as, if the sequence of convex functions
\{\widehat{\Psi}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\Xi}
is labeled by a continuous argument
\varepsilon\in\Xi
with a range
\Xi\subset\mathbb{R}
, then for any
\varepsilon_{0}\in\Xi
, the Mosco-convergence of
\{\widehat{\Psi}_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon\in\Xi}
, as
\varepsilon\to\varepsilon_{0}
, is defined by those of subsequences
\{\widehat{\Psi}_{\varepsilon_{n}}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
, for all sequences
\{\varepsilon_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}\subset\Xi
, satisfying
\varepsilon_{n}\to\varepsilon_{0}
as
n\to\infty
.

Runge and Mergelyan theorems on families of open Riemann surfaces
Franc Forstnerič

Abstract
. In this paper, we develop the Oka theory for maps from families of complex structures on smooth open surfaces to any Oka manifold. Along the way, we prove Runge and Mergelyan approximation theorems and Weierstrass interpolation theorem on such families, with continuous or smooth dependence of the data and the approximating functions on the complex structure. This implies global solvability of the ∂ ¯ \overline{\partial} -equation on such families. We also obtain an Oka principle for complex line bundles on families of open Riemann surfaces, and we show that the canonical bundles of open Riemann surfaces are holomorphically trivial in families. We include applications to families of directed holomorphic immersions and conformal minimal immersions.

1.
Introduction and main results

The Runge approximation theorem is one of the cornerstones
of complex analysis. Its basic version, proved by Runge

[
74
]
in 1885,
says that for a compact set
K
in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}

with connected complement
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
,
every holomorphic function on an open neighbourhood of

K
is a uniform limit on
K
of holomorphic polynomials.
Runge’s theorem was extended by Behnke and Stein

[
16
]
(1949)
to any compact set
K
in an open Riemann surface

X
such that
X\setminus K
has no relatively compact connected
components. A set
K
with this property is said to be Runge in
X
,
a topological condition.
The analogue of Runge’s theorem in Stein manifolds of arbitrary
dimension is the Oka–Weil theorem; see
[
68
,
83
]

and
[
33
, Theorem 2.8.4]
.
A major generalization concerns holomorphic
maps from Stein manifolds to Oka manifolds, the subject of modern
Oka theory
[
33
]
.

In geometric applications, it is often necessary to
approximate families of functions or maps which depend continuously
or smoothly on parameters. For maps from a fixed Stein manifold
X
,
see
[
33
, Theorem 2.8.4]
for the parametric
Cartan–Oka–Weil theorem and

[
33
, Theorem 5.4.4]
for the parametric Oka principle
for maps to any Oka manifold. In those results, the parameter
set is a compact Hausdorff space, the maps depend
continuously on the parameter, and they can be left unchanged
for parameter values in a closed subset
for which they are already holomorphic on
X
.

In the present paper, we consider the more general situation
where not only the maps, but also the complex structures
on the source manifold depend on a parameter.
In this first work on the subject, we limit ourselves to the
case of a smooth open oriented surface
endowed with a family of complex structures.
Our main result is an Oka principle, saying
that every family of continuous maps from such a family of
Riemann surfaces to an Oka manifold
is homotopic to a family of holomorphic maps,
with approximation on a family of compact Runge subsets
where the given maps are already holomorphic;
see Theorem
1.4
.
Our method also applies to products of variable open
Riemann surfaces and a fixed Stein manifold; see Theorem
7.4
.
We also prove Mergelyan-type theorems for maps to Oka manifolds; see
Theorems
7.5
and
7.7
.
These results open a new direction in Oka theory.
On the way, we prove special cases which concern
approximation of functions (i.e., maps to the complex number field

\mathbb{C}
) on families of open Riemann surfaces,
the Runge Theorem
1.1
and the Mergelyan Theorem

1.2
. Their proofs are simpler
since we can use partitions of unity instead of dealing with
homotopies, and the parameter spaces can be more general.
Our results, combined with the techniques from Gromov’s convex integration theory, enable the construction of families of holomorphic curves
of prescribed (variable) conformal types having additional properties
such as being immersed, directed by a conical subvariety of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, etc.,
and the construction of families of minimal surfaces
of prescribed conformal types in Euclidean spaces.
In Section
10
we give a sample of such applications
and indicate further problems which can possibly be treated by our methods.

We now introduce the setup. By
X
we always denote
a smooth orientable surface without boundary,
which will be an open surface in most of our results.
A complex structure on
X

is given by an endomorphism
J
of its tangent bundle
TX

satisfying
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
. Thus,
J
is a section of the vector bundle

T^{*}X\otimes TX\to X
whose fibre over
any point
x\in X
is the space
\mathrm{Hom}(T_{x}X,T_{x}X)

of linear maps
T_{x}X\mapsto T_{x}X
.
Such an operator
J
is usually called an almost complex structure on
X
,
but due to integrability we shall not distinguish between these two notions.
A differentiable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
J
-holomorphic if
the Cauchy–Riemann equation

df_{x}\circ J_{x}=\mathfrak{i}\,df_{x}
holds for every
x\in X
, where

\mathfrak{i}=\sqrt{-1}
. Assuming that
J
is of Hölder class

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
and
0<\alpha<1
,
there is an atlas
\{(U_{i},\phi_{i})\}
of
open sets
U_{i}\subset X
with
\bigcup_{i}U_{i}=X
and

J
-holomorphic charts
\phi_{i}:U_{i}\to\phi_{i}(U_{i})\subset\mathbb{C}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(U_{i})
(see Theorem
2.2
).
Since the transition maps
\phi_{i}\circ\phi_{j}^{-1}

are biholomorphic in the standard structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}
on
\mathbb{C}
,

J
determines on
X
the structure of a Riemann surface,
denoted
(X,J)
, which is
\mathscr{C}^{k+1}
compatible with the smooth
structure on
X
, that is, the identity map on
X
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{k+1}
as a map between these two structures.

We shall consider families of complex structures
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}

on a smooth surface
X
which depend continuously or smoothly on a
parameter
b
in a topological space
B
as a map

B\ni b\mapsto J_{b}\in\Gamma^{(k,\alpha)}(X,T^{*}X\otimes TX)

into the vector space of sections of Hölder
class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
of the
smooth vector bundle
T^{*}X\otimes TX\to X
. If
B
is a manifold of
class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
for some
l\in\mathbb{N}=\{1,2,\ldots\}
,
or a topological space if
l=0
,
we say that
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B\times X)
if the derivatives of
J_{b}
up to order
l

with respect to
b\in B
are continuous on
B\times X

and of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(X)
for any fixed value of
b\in B
.
The analogous definition applies to maps
B\times X\to Y
to a
smooth manifold
Y
. Such a family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}

can also be given by a family
\{\mu_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of maps from

X
to the unit disc
\mathbb{D}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{C}:|\zeta|<1\}

of the same smoothness class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}(B\times X)
; see Remark

4.5
(C).

Consider the projection
\pi:B\times X\to B
.
We endow the fibre
X_{b}=\pi^{-1}(b)\cong X

with the structure of the Riemann surface
(X,J_{b})
determined by
J_{b}
.
A continuous map
f:B\times X\to Y
to a complex manifold
Y

is said to be
X
-holomorphic

if the restriction
f(b,\cdotp):X_{b}\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
. Assuming that the family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
, the space
B\times X
admits fibre
preserving
X
-holomorphic charts of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}

with values in
B\times\mathbb{C}
(see Theorem
4.1
).

Recall that a topological space is said to be paracompact if every
open cover has an open locally finite refinement.
A Hausdorff space is paracompact if and only if it admits a
continuous partition of unity subordinate to any given open cover.

Our first result extends the classical
Runge–Behnke–Stein approximation theorem
on open Riemann surfaces
[
74
,
16
]
,
combined with the Weierstrass–Florack interpolation theorem

[
82
,
28
]
,
to families of complex structures on a smooth open surface.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
B
is a locally compact and paracompact Hausdorff space if
l=0
and a manifold of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
if
l>0
,

X
is a smooth open oriented surface,

\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a family of complex structures on
X

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}

(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ l\leq k+1,\ 0<\alpha<1
),

K
is a compact Runge subset of
X
,

A
is a closed discrete subset of
X
,

U\subset B\times X
is an open set containing
B\times(K\cup A)
,

f:U\to\mathbb{C}
is a function of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,0}(U)

such that
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp)
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic on

U_{b}=\{x\in X:(b,x)\in U\}
for every
b\in B
, and
r\in\{0,1,\ldots,k+1\}
.
Then,
f\in\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}(U)
and there is a function

F\in\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}(B\times X)
satisfying the
following conditions.

(a)

The function
F_{b}=F(b,\cdotp):X\to\mathbb{C}
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
.

(b)

F approximates
f
as closely as desired
in the fine
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
-topology on
B\times K
.

(c)

F_{b}-f_{b}
vanishes to order
r
at every point
a\in A

for every
b\in B
.

Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
5
.
The reason for assuming that
l\leq k+1
will become
evident in the proof of Lemma
5.4
.
The complex structures
J_{b}
in the theorem are compatible
with one another only to order
k+1
, which necessitates
the assumption
r\leq k+1
in condition (c).

A more sophisticated approximation theorem was proved
by Mergelyan in 1951. Its original version
[
64
]

says that a continuous function on a compact Runge set
K\subset\mathbb{C}
,
which is holomorphic in the interior of
K
, is a uniform limit on
K

of holomorphic polynomials. This was generalized to
open Riemann surfaces by Bishop
[
18
]
,
with different proofs and generalizations given by Sakai
[
75
]
,
Scheinberg
[
76
]
, Gauthier
[
41
]
, and others.
Results on approximation
by rational function on the plane with poles in
\mathbb{C}\setminus K

were obtained by Vitushkin
[
80
,
81
]
.
See also the surveys
[
29
,
39
]
.
In Section
6
we prove the following
Mergelyan theorem for families of
complex structures on a smooth surface.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
X
is a smooth oriented surface without boundary,

B
is a locally compact and paracompact Hausdorff space,

\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a continuous family of
complex structures of some Hölder class
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}

(0<\alpha<1)
on
X
,

K
is compact set in
X
such that, for some
c>0
and a
Riemannian distance function on
X
, each relatively compact
connected component of
X\setminus K
has diameter at least
c
,

A
is a finite subset of the interior
\mathring{K}
of
K
,
and
f:B\times K\to\mathbb{C}
is a continuous function
such that
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp):K\to\mathbb{C}

is
J_{b}
-holomorphic on
\mathring{K}
for every
b\in B
.
Given a continuous function
\epsilon:B\to(0,+\infty)
,
there is a continuous function
F
on a
neighbourhood
U\subset B\times X
of
B\times K

such that for every
b\in B
the function

F_{b}=F(b,\cdotp):U_{b}=\{x\in X:(b,x)\in U\}\to\mathbb{C}
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic,

\sup_{x\in K}|F_{b}(x)-f_{b}(x)|<\epsilon(b)
, and

F_{b}-f_{b}
vanishes to order
1
in every point
a\in A
.

If in addition the surface
X
is open and the set
K
is Runge in
X
,
then there is a continuous function
F:B\times X\to\mathbb{C}
such that

F_{b}=F(b,\cdotp):X\to\mathbb{C}
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
and
F
satisfies the above approximation
and interpolation conditions.

The last part of the theorem clearly follows from the first
part and Theorem
1.1
.
The condition on the set
K
in Theorem
1.2

is not optimal, but it more than suffices for the intended
applications. We also obtain Mergelyan approximation
for families of
manifold-valued maps (see Theorem
7.5
),
and Mergelyan approximation in the
\mathscr{C}^{l}
topology
on admissible sets (see Theorem
7.7
).

We now introduce the parameter spaces that will be used in
our main result, Theorem
1.4
.

Definition 1.3
.

In the following, all topological spaces are assumed to be metrizable.

(i)

A space
B
is an
absolute neighbourhood
retract
(ANR) if, whenever
B
is a closed subset of a space
B^{\prime}
,
then
B
is a retract of a neighbourhood of
B
in
B^{\prime}

(a neighbourhood retract).

(ii)

A space
B
is a
Euclidean neighbourhood
retract
(ENR) if it admits a topological embedding
\iota:B\hookrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

for some
n
whose image
\iota(B)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a neighbourhood retract.

(iii)

A space
B
is a
local ENR

if every point of
B
has an ENR neighbourhood.

We refer to Mardešić
[
61
]
for
the theory of ANRs and ENRs, and to
Hatcher
[
51
]
and May
[
63
]

for CW complexes. Note that CW complexes generalize
both manifolds and simplicial complexes, and they
have particular significance for algebraic topology.
A CW complex is locally compact if and only if its
collection of closed cells is locally finite, if and only if
it is metrizable (see Fritsch and Piccinini

[
38
, Theorem B]
).
If a CW complex
B
can be embedded in a Euclidean space

\mathbb{R}^{m}
, then
B
has at most countably many
cells, it is locally compact and has dimension
\leq m
;
see
[
38
, Theorem D]
.
Conversely, every countable locally compact CW-complex

B
of finite dimension
m
is an ENR.
Indeed, by
[
38
, Theorem A]

such
B
admits a closed embedding in
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
.
Since every metrizable CW-complex is
an ANR (see Dugundji
[
26
]
),
the image of the embedding is a neighbourhood retract,
so
B
is an ENR. In particular, every finite CW complex is an ENR
(see
[
51
, Corollary A.10]
).

Our main result is the following Oka principle with approximation
for maps from families of open Riemann surfaces to an Oka manifold
(see
[
33
, Sect. 5.4]
for this notion).
It is proved in Section
7
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Assume the following:

(a)

B
is a paracompact Hausdorff space which is a local ENR
(see Definition
1.3
).

(b)

X
is a smooth open surface and
\pi:B\times X\to B
is
the projection.

(c)

\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a continuous family of complex structures on

X
of Hölder class
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
,
0<\alpha<1
.

(d)

K\subset B\times X
is a closed subset such that

\pi|_{K}:K\to B
is proper, and for every
b\in B
the fibre

K_{b}=\{x\in X:(b,x)\in K\}
is a compact Runge set in
X
,
possibly empty.

(e)

Y
is an Oka manifold endowed
with a distance function
\mathrm{dist}_{Y}
inducing the manifold topology.

(f)

f:B\times X\to Y
is a continuous map,
and there is an open set
U\subset B\times X
containing
K
such that

f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic on

U_{b}=\{x\in X:(b,x)\in U\}
for every
b\in B
.

Given a continuous function
\epsilon:B\to(0,+\infty)
,
there are a neighbourhood
U^{\prime}\subset U
of
K
and a homotopy

f_{t}:B\times X\to Y

(t\in I=[0,1])
satisfying the following conditions.

(i)

f_{0}=f
.

(ii)

The map
f_{t,b}=f_{t}(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
on
U^{\prime}_{b}\supset K_{b}
for every
b\in B
.

(iii)

\sup_{x\in K_{b}}\mathrm{dist}_{Y}(f_{t}(b,x),f(b,x))<\epsilon(b)

for every
b\in B
and
t\in I
.

(iv)

The map
F=f_{1}
is such that

F_{b}=F(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic for every
b\in B
.

(v)

If
Q
is a closed subset of
B
and
U_{b}=X
for all
b\in Q
,
then the homotopy
f_{t,b}
can be chosen to be fixed for every
b\in Q
,
and in particular
F=f
on
Q\times X
.

If
B
is a manifold of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}

(l\in\mathbb{N})
,
the set
Q
in (v) is a closed
\mathscr{C}^{l}
submanifold of
B
,
the family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}

where
l\leq k+1
, and the map
f:B\times X\to Y
is

X
-holomorphic on a neighbourhood

U
of
K
and
f|_{U}\in\mathscr{C}^{l,0}(U,Y)
,
then
f|_{U}\in\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}(U,Y)
and there is a homotopy

f_{t}:B\times X\to Y

(t\in I)
which is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}

on a neighbourhood of
K
, it satisfies conditions (i)–(v),
and every
f_{t}
approximates
f
in the fine

\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
-topology on
K
to a desired precision
uniformly in
t\in I
(i.e., condition (iii) is upgraded to fine

\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
approximation on
K
).

Remark 1.5
.

(A) In analogy to Theorem
1.1
, one can also include
(jet) interpolation in Theorem

.
Assuming that

,
the homotopy

in the theorem can be chosen such that the

f_{t}(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
in the point

x_{j}(b)\in K_{b}
agrees with the corresponding

-jet of

for every
b\in B
,
j=1,\ldots,m
and

.
We invite the reader to supply the details.
It is a general fact of Oka theory that interpolation
follows from approximation;
see
[

, Proposition 5.15.1]

and
[
57
]
.

(B) Condition (v) can be established under the weaker assumption
that the map
f_{b}:X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic for every
b\in Q
.
See
[
33
, Theorem 5.4.4]
where a result
of this kind is proved for a family of maps from a fixed Stein manifold.

Oka manifolds are complex manifolds for which
the h-principle (also called the
Oka principle
in this context)
holds for maps from any Stein manifold,
and in particular from any open Riemann surface;
see
[
33
, Sect. 5.4]
. The appear naturally in
many existence results in complex geometry.
Model Oka manifolds are the complex Euclidean spaces
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
in which case Theorem
1.4
generalizes
the approximation statement in Theorem
1.1

to a variable family of compact Runge sets.
Every complex homogeneous manifold and, more
generally, every Gromov elliptic manifold is an Oka manifold
(see Grauert
[
45
]
and Gromov
[
46
]
).
Many further examples can be found in the surveys
[
33
, Chapter 7]

and
[
31
]
. In
[
32
]

it is shown that in semipositive ample line bundles on
certain projective Oka manifolds, the unit disc bundle
is an Oka manifold.
Oka theory has recently been developed for maps from
open Riemann surfaces to the bigger class of Oka-1 manifolds
which properly contains the class of Oka manifolds; see

[
9
,
36
]
.
However, it does not seem possible to use this bigger class
in Theorem
1.4
since its proof relies on the Oka principle
for maps from higher dimensional Stein manifolds.

Theorem
1.4
seems to be the first result of its kind in Oka theory.
Its proof in Section
7
also applies to products
X\times Z
,
where
X
is a smooth open surface endowed with
a family
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
of complex structures as in
Theorem
1.4
and
Z
is a Stein manifold with a fixed
complex structure; see Theorem
7.4
.
Essentially the same proof, combined with Theorem
1.2
,
yields Mergelyan approximation for maps from families of open
Riemann surfaces to an Oka manifold;
see Theorems
7.5

and
7.7
.

Our results apply in particular if
B
is the Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(g,k)
of marked complex structures on the
k
-punctured surface

X=\overline{X}\setminus\{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k}\}
,
where
\overline{X}
is an oriented smooth compact surface
of genus

and

. The spaces
\mathcal{T}(g,k)
and

\mathcal{T}(g,k)\times X
carry natural complex structures
such that the projection

is a
holomorphic submersion and the complex structure on each

is the one determined by

. See Nag
[
66
, Chapter 3]
or

[

]

for a precise description, and also the surveys

[

,

]

for infinite dimensional Teichmüller spaces of hyperbolic

However, we do not see how to use this theory in order
to obtain or simplify the proofs of our main results
for a general parameter space

.

The results and methods developed in the paper can be
used in a range of constructions of families of holomorphic
curves with special properties, and of related objects such as
conformal minimal surfaces. To illustrate the point, we give two such
applications in Section
10
.
The first one in Theorem
10.2
gives families
of
J_{b}
-holomorphic immersions
X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

directed by an irreducible conical complex subvariety

\overline{A}=A\cup\{0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

A=\overline{A}\setminus\{0\}
is an Oka manifold.
By taking
A=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
we obtain families of
ordinary immersions
X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For
n=1
this
gives an extension of the Gunning–Narasimhan theorem

[
47
]

to families of
J_{b}
-holomorphic immersions
X\to\mathbb{C}
;
see Corollary
10.3
.
Another application pertains to
the null cone
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
, see (
10.3
).
Holomorphic immersions directed by this cone are
the
(1,0)
-derivatives of minimal surfaces,
and we obtain continuous or smooth
families of conformally immersed minimal surfaces

X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

(n\geq 3)
for any continuous or smooth family
of complex structures
J_{b}
on
X
(see Corollary
10.6
).
Several other possible applications
are indicated in Problem
10.7
.
A common feature of these examples is that their construction
combines Oka theory with methods from Gromov’s convex
integration theory to ensure the period vanishing conditions of the
derivative maps on a basis of the first homology group
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
we recall
the connection between Riemannian metrics, conformal structures,
and the Beltrami equation. Section
3
contains preparatory
results on the Cauchy and Beurling transforms.
In Section
4
we develop results
on deformations of complex structures which are used in the proofs.
Theorem
4.1
gives
a solution of the Beltrami equation on any smoothly bounded relatively
compact domain
\Omega
in an open Riemann surface
X
for
Beltrami coefficients
\mu
with small Hölder
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
norm

(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ 0<\alpha<1)
and with smooth dependence on
\mu
.
Using also a theorem
of Gunning and Narasimhan
[
47
]
,
it is shown in Theorem
4.3
that any small

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
perturbation of the complex structure on

\Omega
can be realised by a small
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}

perturbation of
\Omega
in
X
, with smooth dependence of the
map on the complex structure.
This extends the Ahlfors–Bers theory
[
3
]
of
quasiconformal maps of the plane. With these tools in hand,
Theorem
1.1
is proved in Section
5

and Theorem
1.2
is proved in Section
6
.
In Section
7
we prove our main result,
Theorem
1.4
, and obtain further Runge and
Mergelyan type approximation results for families of
manifold-valued maps; see Theorems
7.4
,

7.5
, and
7.7
.
In Section
8
we
show that for a family of complex structures
on a smooth open surface, the family of their
holomorphic cotangent (canonical) bundles admits a
family of holomorphic trivializations (see Theorem
8.1
),
which can be given by a family of holomorphic immersions to
\mathbb{C}

(see Corollary
10.3
).
In Section
9
we obtain a global solution
of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation on families of open Riemann surfaces
(see Theorem
9.1
) and the Oka principle
for continuous families of complex line bundles
(see Theorem
9.3
). In Section
10
we apply
our results to the construction of families of directed
holomorphic immersions and conformal minimal immersions.

Open Riemann surfaces are Stein manifolds of complex
dimension one. It would be of interest to develop the Oka theory for
families of integrable Stein structures on manifolds of
higher dimension.
To this end, one would need an analogue
of Theorem
4.3
for such families on strongly
pseudoconvex domains.
A special case of results of Hamilton
[
49
,
50
]

is that a sufficiently small smooth integrable deformation of a
complex structure on a relatively compact, smoothly bounded,
strongly pseudoconvex domain in a Stein manifold
can be realised by a small deformation of the domain.
Recently, Gong and Shi
[
44
]
obtained
Hamilton’s results under substantially weaker regularity
assumptions. However, smooth dependence of the deformation
map on the complex structure is not clear from these results.
Another complication in the higher dimensional case
is caused by the behaviour of hulls of compact sets in
families of Stein structures. For these reason,
we defer this project to a future work.

Notation and terminology.

We denote by
\mathscr{C}(X)
and
\mathscr{O}(X)
the Fréchet algebras of
continuous and holomorphic functions on a complex manifold

X
, respectively, endowed with the compact-open topology.
Given a compact set
K
in a complex manifold
X
,
we denote by
\mathscr{C}(K)
the Banach algebra of continuous
complex valued functions on
K
with the supremum norm,
by
\mathscr{O}(K)
the space of functions that are holomorphic in a
neighbourhood of
K
(depending on the function),
and by
\overline{\mathscr{O}}(K)
the uniform closure
of
\{f|_{K}:f\in\mathscr{O}(K)\}
in
\mathscr{C}(K)
. By
\mathscr{A}(K)

we denote the closed subspace of
\mathscr{C}(K)
consisting of functions

K\to\mathbb{C}
which are holomorphic in the interior
\mathring{K}
of
K
.
For
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
we denote by
\mathscr{C}^{r}(K)
the space of all
functions on
K
which extend to
r
-times continuously
differentiable functions on
X
, and

\mathscr{A}^{r}(K)=\{f\in\mathscr{C}^{r}(K):f|_{\mathring{K}}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathring{K})\}
.
The analogous notation is used for manifold-valued maps.
A compact set
K
in a complex manifold
X
is said to be

\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex
, or holomorphically convex in
X
, if

K
equals its holomorphically convex hull:

K=\widehat{K}_{\mathscr{O}(X)}=\big\{p\in X:|f(p)|\leq\max_{x\in K}|f(x)|\ \ \text{for all}\ f\in\mathscr{O}(X)\big\}.

If
X
is a Riemann surface then
K
is
\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex if and only
if it is Runge in
X
.
A compact
\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})
-convex set
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be
polynomially convex. A compact set
K
in a complex manifold
X

is said to be a
Stein compact
if it admits a basis of
open Stein neighbourhoods in
X
.

2.
Riemannian metrics, complex structures, and the Beltrami equation

In this section, we recall the relevant background on the topics
mentioned in the title. The details can be found in standard texts
on quasiconformal mappings and Teichmüller spaces; see e.g. Ahlfors
[
2
]
, Lehto and Virtanen
[
58
]
,
Nag
[
66
]
, and Imayoshi and Taniguchi
[
54
]
.

Let
z=x+\mathfrak{i}y
be the complex coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
.
Set
\partial_{x}=\partial/\partial x
,
\partial_{y}=\partial/\partial y
,

dz=dx+\mathfrak{i}dy
,
d\bar{z}=dx-\mathfrak{i}dy
,

\partial_{z}=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}-\mathfrak{i}\partial_{y}\right),\qquad\partial_{\bar{z}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial\bar{z}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\partial_{x}+\mathfrak{i}\partial_{y}\right).

For a differentiable function
f
we shall write

f_{z}=\partial_{z}f
and
f_{\bar{z}}=\partial_{\bar{z}}f
.
Note that
f
is holomorphic if and only if
f_{\bar{z}}=0
.
The exterior differential on functions
splits in the sum of its
\mathbb{C}
-linear and
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear parts:

d=\partial+\overline{\partial}=\partial_{z}dz+\partial_{\bar{z}}d\bar{z}.

A Riemannian metric on a smooth surface
X
is given
in any local coordinates
(x,y)
by

(2.1)

g=Edx\otimes dx+F(dx\otimes dy+dy\otimes dx)+Gdy\otimes dy=Edx^{2}+2Fdxdy+Gdy^{2},

where
E,F,G
are real functions satisfying
EG-F^{2}>0
.
The area form determined by the metric
g
is
\sqrt{EG-F^{2}}\,dx\wedge dy
.
The Euclidean metric and the area form on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\cong\mathbb{C}

with the coordinate
z=x+\mathfrak{i}y
are given by

g_{\rm st}=dx^{2}+dy^{2}=|dz|^{2}
and

dx\wedge dy=\frac{\mathfrak{i}}{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}
.
On every tangent space
T_{p}X
, a Riemannian metric
g

defines a scalar product having the matrix
\bigl({E\ F\atop F\ G}\bigr)

in the basis
\partial_{x},\ \partial_{y}
. Hence,
g
determines
a unique conformal structure on
X
, and two Riemannian metrics

g_{1},g_{2}
determine the same conformal structure if and only if

g_{2}=\lambda g_{1}
for a positive function
\lambda
.
A pair of nonzero tangent vectors
\xi,\eta\in T_{p}X

is said to be a conformal frame if
\xi
and
\eta
have the same

g
-length and are
g
-orthogonal to each other.
If
X
is oriented, there is a unique endomorphism

J:TX\to TX
on the tangent bundle of
X

such that for any tangent vector

0\neq v\in T_{p}X
,
(v,Jv)
is a positively oriented
g
-conformal frame.
Note that
J^{2}=-\mathrm{Id}
; an endomorphism of
TX
satisfying this
condition is called an
almost complex structure
on
X
.
We have the following local expression for the matrix of
J

(in the standard oriented basis
\partial_{x},\ \partial_{y}
) in
terms of the metric
g
(
2.1
):

(2.2)

[J]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{EG-F^{2}}}\left(\begin{matrix}-F&-G\\
E&F\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}-b&-c\\
(b^{2}+1)/c&b\end{matrix}\right)

where
\delta=EG-F^{2}>0
,
b=F/{\sqrt{\delta}}
, and

c=G/{\sqrt{\delta}}>0
.
Every almost complex structure
J
is of this form for
some Riemannian metric
g
, which is unique up to conformal
equivalence. The standard almost complex structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}
on
\mathbb{C}
,
defined by the Euclidean metric
g_{\rm st}
, has the matrix

\bigl({0\ \,-1\atop 1\ \ \ 0}\bigr)
. In complex notation,

J_{\mathrm{st}}
amounts to multiplication by
\mathfrak{i}
.
A differentiable function
f:U\to\mathbb{C}
on a domain
U\subset X
is
said to be
J
-holomorphic (more precisely,
(J,J_{\mathrm{st}})
-holomorphic) if
it satisfies the Cauchy–Riemann equation

df_{p}\circ J_{p}=J_{\mathrm{st}}\circ df_{p}
at all points
p\in U
,
where
J_{p}
denotes the restriction of
J
to
T_{p}X
.
At a point where
df_{p}\neq 0
, such
f
is an orientation preserving
conformal map from the conformal structure on
X
determined by

J=J_{g}
to the standard conformal structure on
\mathbb{C}
.

Assume that the metric
g
is given in local coordinates
(x,y)
on
an open set
U\subset X
by (
2.1
). Taking
z=x+\mathfrak{i}y

as a complex coordinate on
U
, we can write
g
in
the complex form as

(2.3)

g=\lambda|dz+\mu d\bar{z}|^{2}

for a positive function
\lambda>0
and the complex function

(2.4)

\mu=\frac{1-c+\mathfrak{i}b}{1+c+\mathfrak{i}b}:X\to\mathbb{D}

with values in the unit disc, where the numbers
b
and
c
are as in
(
2.2
); see
[
11
, p. 51]
.
A diffeomorphism
f:U\to f(U)\subset\mathbb{C}
is conformal from
the
g
-structure on
X
to the standard conformal structure on
\mathbb{C}

if and only if
g=h|df|^{2}
for a positive function
h>0
.
A chart
f
with this property is said to be
isothermal
for
g
.
Assume that
f
is orientation preserving,
which amounts to
|f_{z}|>|f_{\bar{z}}|
. Then

|df|^{2}=|f_{z}dz+f_{\bar{z}}d\bar{z}|^{2}=|f_{z}|^{2}\,\cdotp\Bigl|dz+\frac{f_{\bar{z}}}{f_{z}}d\bar{z}\Bigr|^{2},

and comparison with (
2.3
)
shows that
f
is isothermal if and only if it satisfies the Beltrami equation

(2.5)

f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}

with the Beltrami coefficient
\mu
given by (
2.4
).
We shall say that
f
is
\mu
-conformal if (
2.5
) holds.
Equivalently,
f
is a biholomorphic map from
(U,J)
to
(f(U),J_{\mathrm{st}})

where
J
is the complex structure on
X
determined by
g

(or by
\mu
). The transition map between any pair of
\mu
-conformal
charts is a conformal diffeomorphism between domains
in the plane
\mathbb{C}
endowed with the Euclidean metric.

One can also consider quasiconformal maps
f:X\to Y
between
a pair of Riemann surfaces. The quantity

\mu_{f}(z)=f_{\bar{z}}/f_{z}
, defined in a local holomorphic coordinate

z
on
X
, is independent of the choice of the local holomorphic
coordinates on
Y
, and
\mu_{f}(z)d\bar{z}/dz
is a section
of the bundle
K_{X}^{-1}\otimes\overline{K}_{X}\to X

where
K_{X}=T^{*}X
is the canonical bundle of
X

(see
[
66
, p. 46]
).

Remark 2.1
.

The formulas (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) show that the
conformal class of a Riemannian metric
g
, the associated
complex structure
J
, and the Beltrami coefficient
\mu

are of the same smoothness class.

The situation is especially simple if we fix a reference
complex structure on
X
, so it is an open Riemann surface.
By a theorem of Gunning and Narasimhan
[
47
]
,
such a surface admits a holomorphic immersion

z=u+\mathfrak{i}v:X\to\mathbb{C}
. Its differential
dz=du+\mathfrak{i}dv
is a
nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form on
X
trivializing the
canonical bundle
T^{*}X=K_{X}
,
|dz|^{2}=du^{2}+dv^{2}
is a
Riemannian metric on
X
determining the given complex structure,

\frac{\mathfrak{i}}{2}dz\wedge d\bar{z}=du\wedge dv
is the associated
area form, and
d\sigma=du\,dv
is the surface measure on
X
.
The function
z
is a local holomorphic coordinate on
X
at every point.
Given a differentiable function
f:X\to\mathbb{C}
, its partial derivatives

(2.6)

f_{z}=\partial_{z}f=\partial f/dz,\qquad f_{\bar{z}}=\partial_{\bar{z}}f=\overline{\partial}f/d{\bar{z}}

are globally defined functions on
X
.
Any Riemannian metric
g
on
X
is globally of
the form (
2.1
) for some real functions
E,F,G
on
X
.
(However, these coefficients are not functions of
z
unless
z
is injective.)
We can write
g
in the form
(
2.3
) where the function
\mu:X\to\mathbb{D}
is
given by (
2.4
). Conversely, any such function
\mu
determines
a Riemannian metric by (
2.3
), and hence
a complex structure
J_{\mu}
by (
2.2
).
Note that
\mu=0
corresponds to the given reference
complex structure on
X
.
This global viewpoint will be important in the sequel.

The existence of isothermal charts on a Riemannian surface
is a classical subject going back
to Lagrange and Gauss. For a Hölder continuous
\mu

see Korn
[
55
]
, Lichtenstein
[
59
]
,
and Chern
[
21
]
. The existence of
global quasiconformal homeomorphisms
\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}

follow from the local theorem by use of the uniformization theorem,
and direct proofs were given by Ahlfors
[
1
]

and Vekua
[
79
]
. For a measurable function
\mu
satisfying

\|\mu\|_{\infty}\leq k<1

(where
\|\mu\|_{\infty}
denotes the essential supremum),
see Morrey
[
65
]
and Bojarski
[
19
]
.
In this case, solutions of (
2.5
)
are
k
-quasiconformal homeomorphisms having distributional derivatives
in
L^{p}
for some
p\geq 1
. More precise results in
L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
spaces,
with continuous or smooth dependence of solutions of the Beltrami
equation (
2.5
) on the Beltrami coefficient
\mu
,
are due to Ahlfors and Bers
[
3
]
; see also
Ahlfors
[
2
, Chapter V]
and
Astala et al.
[
14
]
.
The result that we use in this paper is the following;
see
[
14
, Theorem 5.3.4]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

An almost complex structure
J
of Hölder class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}

(k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+},\ 0<\alpha<1)
on a smooth Riemannian surface
X
admits a

J
-holomorphic chart of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
at any
point of
X
.

A semiglobal version of this result with parameters
is given by Theorem
4.1
.

3.
The Cauchy and Beurling transforms on domains in open
Riemann surfaces

In this section, we consider regularity properties
of the Cauchy and Beurling transforms
on smoothly bounded relatively compact domains in open
Riemann surfaces. Theorem
3.2

is an important analytic ingredient for solving
the Beltrami equation on such domains; see
Theorems
4.1
and
4.3
.

Let
X
be an open Riemann surface. Fix a
holomorphic immersion
z=u+\mathfrak{i}v:X\to\mathbb{C}

(see
[
47
]
) and let

d\sigma=du\,dv
denote the associated area measure on
X
.
Given a differentiable function
f:U\to\mathbb{C}
on a domain

U\subset X
, its derivatives
f_{z}
and
f_{\bar{z}}

given by (
2.6
) are well-defined functions on
U
.
The pullback of the Cauchy kernel
C(\zeta,z)=\frac{dz}{z-\zeta}
on
\mathbb{C}

by the immersion

z:X\to\mathbb{C}
is a Cauchy-type kernel on
X
with the correct behaviour
near the diagonal
D_{X}=\{(x,x):x\in X\}
(see (
3.2
)),
but with additional poles if
z
is not injective. Since
D_{X}
has a basis
of Stein neighbourhoods in
X\times X
and

X\times X\setminus D_{X}
is also Stein,
one can remove the extra poles by solving a Cousin problem
(see Scheinberg
[
76
, Lemma 2.1]
).
This gives a meromorphic
1
-form on
X\times X
of the form

(3.1)

\omega(q,x)=\xi(q,x)dz(x)\quad\text{for}\ q,x\in X,

where
dz(x)
denotes the restriction of
dz
to
T_{x}X
,

\xi
is a meromorphic function on
X\times X
which is
holomorphic on
X\times X\setminus D_{X}
, and
the 1-form
\omega(q,\cdotp)
has a simple pole at
q\in X

with residue
1
. In a neighbourhood
U\subset X\times X

of
D_{X}
the coefficient
\xi
of
\omega
is of the form

(3.2)

\xi(q,x)=\frac{1}{z(x)-z(q)}+h(q,x),

where
h
is a holomorphic function on
U
.
Such Cauchy kernels were constructed by Scheinberg
[
76
]

and Gauthier
[
41
]
, following the work
by Behnke and Stein
[
16
, Theorem 3]
.
(See also Behnke and Sommer
[
15
, p. 584]

and
[
29
, Remark 1, p. 141]
for additional references.)
Given a relatively compact smoothly bounded domain

\Omega\Subset X
, the usual argument
using Stokes formula and the residue calculation
gives the following Cauchy–Green formula
for any
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})
and
q\in\Omega
:

\displaystyle f(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\int_{x\in b\Omega}f(x)\,\omega(q,x)-\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\int_{x\in\Omega}\overline{\partial}f(x)\wedge\omega(q,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\int_{x\in b\Omega}f(x)\,\xi(q,x)dz(x)-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x\in\Omega}f_{\bar{z}}(x)\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x).

If
f
is holomorphic in
\Omega
, we obtain the
Cauchy representation formula

f(q)=\frac{1}{2\pi\mathfrak{i}}\int_{x\in b\Omega}f(x)\,\omega(q,x),\quad q\in\Omega.

On the other hand, for a function
f\in\mathscr{C}^{1}_{0}(X)
with
compact support we have

(3.3)

f(q)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x\in X}f_{\bar{z}}(x)\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x),\quad q\in X.

To the Cauchy kernel
\omega
we associate
two transforms, defined for
\phi\in\mathscr{C}_{0}(X)
and
q\in X
by

(3.4)

\displaystyle P(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\phi(x)\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x),

(3.5)

\displaystyle S(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\partial_{z}P(\phi)(q)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\phi(x)\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x).

Here,
\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)
denotes the
\partial_{z}
derivative (
2.6
)
of the function
\xi(\cdotp,x)
at the point
q\in X
.

The operator
P
is called the Cauchy–Green transform, or simply
the Cauchy operator associated to
the Cauchy kernel (
3.1
).
The integral converges absolutely, and we have that

\partial_{\bar{z}}\circ P=\mathrm{Id}=P\circ\partial_{\bar{z}}\quad\text{on}\ \mathscr{C}^{1}_{0}(X).

The second identity follows from (
3.3
).
The first identity holds in a more precise form:
for every relatively compact domain
\Omega\subset X

with piecewise
\mathscr{C}^{1}
boundary,

(3.6)

\partial_{\bar{z}}P(\phi)=\phi\ \ \text{holds on $\Omega$ for every $\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}(\overline{\Omega})$},

where the integral defining
P
is applied only over
\Omega
.
(Equivalently, we apply (
3.4
) to the function which equals

\phi
on
\overline{\Omega}
and equals
0
on
X\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.)
The equation (
3.6
) holds in the distributional sense
for every integrable
\phi
. It is obtained by following
the proof in the case when
\xi(q,x)=\frac{1}{x-q}
on
X=\mathbb{C}
,
when
P
equals the standard Cauchy–Green operator on
\mathbb{C}
:

(3.7)

\mathcal{C}(\phi)(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{\zeta-z}d\sigma(\zeta),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

The operator
S
(
3.5
)
is an analogue of the Beurling transform
\mathcal{B}

in the plane
[
2
,
14
,
58
]
:

(3.8)

\mathcal{B}(\phi)(z)=-\frac{1}{\pi}\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\int_{|z-\zeta|>\epsilon}\frac{\phi(\zeta)}{(z-\zeta)^{2}}d\sigma(\zeta),\quad z\in\mathbb{C}.

This is a singular convolution operator of
Calderón–Zygmund type with nonintegrable kernel
-1/\pi z^{2}
,
hence one must use the Cauchy principal value.
Its main property is that

\mathcal{B}\circ\partial_{\bar{z}}=\partial_{z}

on
\mathscr{C}^{1}_{0}(\mathbb{C})
, so
\mathcal{B}
interchanges the operators

\partial_{\bar{z}}
and
\partial_{z}
. Likewise, it follows from
(
3.3
)–(
3.5
) that

(3.9)

S(\phi_{\bar{z}})=\partial_{z}P(\phi_{\bar{z}})=\phi_{z}\ \ \text{for every $\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{1}_{0}(\Omega)$}.

In order to understand the local regularity properties of
P
and
S
,
we look more closely at their kernel functions
\xi(q,x)
and
\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)
.
We consider the latter one, which is more involved; the analogous
analysis applies to the former.
Let
U\subset X\times X
be an open neighbourhood of the diagonal
D_{X}

on which (
3.2
) holds. On
U
we have

\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)=\partial_{z(q)}\frac{1}{z(x)-z(q)}+\partial_{z(q)}h(q,x)=\frac{1}{(z(x)-z(q))^{2}}+\partial_{z(q)}h(q,x),

and the function
\partial_{z(q)}h(q,x)
is holomorphic on
U
. Fix
a point
q_{0}\in X

and choose a neighbourhood
V\subset X
of
q_{0}
such that

V\times V\subset U
and the immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
is injective on
V
.
Pick a smooth cut-off function
\chi:X\to[0,1]
with
\mathrm{supp}\chi\subset V

such that
\chi=1
on a smaller neighbourhood
V^{\prime}\Subset V
of
q_{0}
.
For
q\in V
we have

\displaystyle S(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\chi(x)\phi(x)\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x)+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}(\chi(x)-1)\phi(x)\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle S_{1}(\phi)(q)+S_{2}(\phi)(q),

where the operators
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are given by

\displaystyle S_{1}(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\frac{\chi(x)\phi(x)}{(z(x)-z(q))^{2}}d\sigma(x),

\displaystyle S_{2}(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}\chi(x)\phi(x)\partial_{z(q)}h(q,x)d\sigma(x)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{X}(\chi(x)-1)\phi(x)\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x).

In the complex coordinate
z
on
V
,
S_{1}(\phi)=\mathcal{B}(\chi\phi)
is
the Beurling operator applied to
\chi\phi
, while
S_{2}
has smooth kernel.
The same construction can be carried out with the operator
P
.

The conclusion is that the operators
P
and
S
have the same
local regularity properties as their classical models
\mathcal{C}

(
3.7
) and
\mathcal{B}
(
3.8
), respectively.

Let
\Omega
be a relatively compact, smoothly bounded
domain in
X
. One may consider the truncated operators
P
and
S
,
defined by integration over
\Omega
(that is, extending the function

\phi
under the integral to
X
by setting
\phi=0
on

X\setminus\overline{\Omega}
). While
P
has the expected regularity
on Hölder spaces, the regularity of
S
fails at the boundary points
of
\Omega
since the effect of averaging in (
3.7
)
is lost. To circumvent this problem,
we shall use a bounded linear extension operator, which we now describe.

Let
\mathrm{dist}
denote the distance function on a surface
X
induced by
a smooth Riemannian metric. We recall the basics concerning Hölder
spaces; see
[
42
, Sect. 4.1]
for more information.
Let
\Omega
be a domain in
X
. For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, the Hölder

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
norm of a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is given by

(3.10)

\|f\|_{\alpha}=\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|+\sup\{|f(x)-f(y)|/\mathrm{dist}(x,y)^{\alpha}:x,y\in\Omega,\ x\neq y\},

and the associated Hölder space is

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)=\{f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}:\|f\|_{\alpha}<\infty\}.

Similarly we define the norm
\|f\|_{k,\alpha}
for
k>0
, and the
corresponding Hölder space
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
, by adding
to
\|f\|_{\alpha}
in (
3.10
) the
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

norms of the partial derivatives of
f
of highest order
k
.
In particular,
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)=\mathscr{C}^{(0,\alpha)}(\Omega)
.
These spaces are Banach algebras with the pointwise product of functions.
Every function in
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
has a unique extension
to a function in
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
. We shall need the
following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Given a smoothly bounded relatively compact domain
\Omega\Subset X
in
a smooth open Riemannian surface
X
and a domain
\Omega^{\prime}\subset X

containing
\overline{\Omega}
, there is for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
0<\alpha<1

a continuous linear extension operator

E:\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}_{0}(\Omega^{\prime})

with range in the space of compactly supported functions in

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega^{\prime})
.

Proof.

For domains in Euclidean spaces and
k\geq 1
,
this is
[
42
, Lemma 6.37]
; it is clear
from the construction that one obtains a linear extension operator.
(The cited lemma is stated for any relatively compact domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

with
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
boundary, but we believe
that a smoothness of class at least
\mathscr{C}^{k+1}
is needed since the
composition of a
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
function and a

\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}
diffeomorphism, which is used in the proof to locally straighten
the boundary
b\Omega
, is only of class
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha^{2})}
.)
We can reduce to this case by noting that every component
S

of
b\Omega
has a neighbourhood
U\subset\Omega^{\prime}

smoothly diffeomorphic to an annulus in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, with
S

corresponding to the unit circle.
(See Bellettini
[
17
, Theorem 1.18, p. 14]
.)
Assume now that
k=0
. Using the above notation,
let
\tau:U\to S
denote the (smooth) radial projection of the annulus onto
the circle
S
. Set
U_{+}=U\setminus\Omega
, and let

\chi:\overline{\Omega}\cup U\to[0,1]
be a smooth function
which equals
1
on
\overline{\Omega}
and the restriction

\chi|_{U_{+}}
has compact support.
Given
f\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,
we let
E(f):\overline{\Omega}\cup U\to\mathbb{C}
be defined by

E(f)(x)=f(x)
for
x\in\overline{\Omega}
and
E(f)(x)=\chi(x)f(\tau(x))

for
x\in U_{+}
.
We perform the same construction on each of the finitely
many boundary components of
\Omega
.
It is easily verified that this extension has the required properties.
∎

With the notation of Lemma
3.1
,
define the operators
P_{\Omega}
and
S_{\Omega}
on

\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
and
q\in\overline{\Omega}
by

(3.11)

\displaystyle P_{\Omega}(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}E(\phi)(x)\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x),

(3.12)

\displaystyle S_{\Omega}(\phi)(q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{x\in\Omega^{\prime}}E(\phi)(x)\partial_{z(q)}\xi(q,x)d\sigma(x).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X
be an open Riemann surface with a Cauchy kernel
(
3.1
), (
3.2
).

(a)

P_{\Omega}:\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})

is a bounded linear operator for every
0<\alpha<1
and
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
,
and it satisfies
\partial_{\bar{z}}P_{\Omega}(\phi)=\phi

on
\overline{\Omega}
for every
\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
.

(b)

S_{\Omega}:\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
is
a bounded linear operator for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
0<\alpha<1
,
and it satisfies
S_{\Omega}(\phi)=\partial_{z}P_{\Omega}(\phi)
for every

\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\overline{\Omega})
.

Proof.

We have seen above that the operators
P
and
S
have the same
local regularity properties as their classical models

\mathcal{C}
(
3.7
) and
\mathcal{B}
(
3.8
), respectively.
Part (a) then follows from
[
14
, Theorem 4.7.2]

and (
3.3
), and part (b) follows from

[
14
, Theorem 4.7.1]
and (
3.12
).
(The analogous properties hold on Sobolev spaces, but we shall not need them.)
∎

4.
Quasiconformal deformations of the identity map

In this section,
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
denotes a
holomorphic immersion from an open Riemann surface
X

(see
[
47
]
). We shall
call the pair
(X,z)
a
Riemann domain over
\mathbb{C}
.
Let
\Omega
be a domain in
X
.
Recall that for any
\mathscr{C}^{1}
function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
,
the derivatives
f_{z}=\partial f/dz
and
f_{\bar{z}}=\overline{\partial}f/d{\bar{z}}

(
2.6
) are well-defined functions on
\Omega
.
The following result gives a solution of the Beltrami equation on
any smoothly bounded relatively compact domain
for Beltrami coefficients with sufficiently small Hölder norm.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be a smoothly bounded relatively compact domain
in a Riemann domain
(X,z)
. For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}

and
0<\alpha<1
there is a constant
c=c(k,\alpha)>0
such that for every

\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,\mathbb{D})
with
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c

there is function
f=f(\mu)\in\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\Omega)
solving
the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
, with
f(\mu)

depending smoothly on
\mu
and satisfying
f(0)=z|_{\Omega}
.

Interpreting the function
\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,\mathbb{D})

as a complex structure
J_{\mu}
on
\Omega

(see (
2.2
)–(
2.4
)),
with
J_{0}
coinciding with the initial complex structure,
the function
f(\mu):\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
is
J_{\mu}
-holomorphic,
and it is an immersion for
\mu
close to
0

since
f(\mu)
is then close to
f(0)=z|_{\Omega}
in

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\Omega)
.
Thus,
(\Omega,J_{\mu},f(\mu))
is a family of Riemann
domains over
\mathbb{C}
depending smoothly on
\mu
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

The idea is inspired by the proof
of the corresponding result for
\mu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C})

(p>2)
,
due to Ahlfors and Bers
[
3
, Theorem 4]
.
For simplicity of exposition we shall consider the case
k=0
,
noting that the same arguments apply for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
.

Recall that the algebra
\mathrm{Lin}(E)
of all bounded linear operators
on a Banach space
E
, with functional composition
as multiplication and the operator norm, is a unital Banach algebra
(see Conway
[
23
]
). In our case,
E
will be
the Banach space
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
.

We look for a solution of the Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}

on
\Omega
in the form

(4.1)

f=f(\mu)=z|_{\Omega}+P(\phi),\quad\phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

Here,
P=P_{\Omega}:\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)

is the Cauchy–Green operator (
3.11
).
Thus,
\phi=0
corresponds to
f=z|_{\Omega}
.
By Theorem
3.2
(a),
P
is a continuous
linear operator. We have

f_{\bar{z}}=\partial_{\bar{z}}P(\phi)=\phi,\qquad f_{z}=1+\partial_{z}P(\phi)=1+S(\phi),

where
S=S_{\Omega}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega))

is the (continuous, linear) Beltrami operator (
3.12
).
The first identity follows from Theorem
3.2
(a)
and the second one from the definition (
3.12
) of

S
. Inserting the above expressions in the Beltrami equation

f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
gives the following equation for
\phi
:

\phi=\mu(S(\phi)+1)=\mu S(\phi)+\mu.

Let
c_{0}=\|S\|_{\alpha}>0
denote the operator norm of
S
on

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
(see Theorem
3.2
(b)), so

(4.2)

\|S(\phi)\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{0}\|\phi\|_{\alpha}\quad\text{for all}\ \phi\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

Note that the multiplication by
\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
on
the space
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
is a linear operator of norm

\|\mu\|_{\alpha}
. Let us rewrite the above equation for
\phi
in the form

(4.3)

(I-\mu S)\phi=\mu,

where
I
denotes the identity map on
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
.
We now assume that

(4.4)

\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<c:=1/c_{0}=1/\|S\|_{\alpha}.

Then,
\|\mu S\|_{\alpha}\leq\|\mu\|_{\alpha}c_{0}<1
,
so the operator
I-\mu S
is
invertible with the bounded inverse

(4.5)

\Theta(\mu)=(I-\mu S)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\mu S)^{j}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)).

The equation (
4.3
) then has a unique solution

\phi=\phi(\mu)
given by

\phi=\Theta(\mu)\mu=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(\mu S)^{j}\mu.

Inserting into (
4.1
) gives a solution to the
Beltrami equation
f_{\bar{z}}=\mu f_{z}
on
\Omega
:

f(\mu)=z|_{\Omega}+P(\Theta(\mu)\mu)=z|_{\Omega}+P((I-\mu S)^{-1}\mu)\in\mathscr{C}^{(1,\alpha)}(\Omega).

We claim that the map
\mu\mapsto f(\mu)
is smooth
on
\{\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega):\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<c\}
.
Since the map
P:\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\to\mathscr{C}^{(1,\alpha)}(\Omega)

is continuous linear and the evaluation map

\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega))\times\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\ni(A,\mu)\mapsto A\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)

is continuous bilinear, it suffices to see that the map

\mu\mapsto\Theta(\mu)=(I-\mu S)^{-1}\in\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega))

is smooth on
\{\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega):\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<c\}
.
This follows from the fact that the inversion

A\mapsto A^{-1}
is an analytic map on a neighbourhood
of the identity on
\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega))
, as is seen
from the expansion (
4.5
).
Let us give a more explicit argument.
For
\nu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
we have

I-(\mu+\nu)S=(I-\mu S)-\nu S=(I-\mu S)(I-\Theta(\mu)\nu S).

If
\|\nu\|_{\alpha}
is small enough then
\|\Theta(\mu)\nu S\|_{\alpha}<1
,
and taking inverses gives

(4.6)

\Theta(\mu+\nu)=(I-\Theta(\mu)\nu S)^{-1}\Theta(\mu).

As
\|\nu\|_{\alpha}\to 0
, the operator
\Theta(\mu)\nu S
converges
to zero in the operator norm, and hence the first term on the right hand side converges to the identity. This establishes continuity.
Next, we show that
\Theta(\mu)

is continuously differentiable with respect to
\mu
on

\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<c
. Fix a constant
c_{1}\in(0,c)
.
Assume for a moment that
\|\mu\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{1}
, and hence

\|\mu S\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{1}c_{0}<cc_{0}=1
; see (
4.4
).
From (
4.5
) we obtain

(4.7)

\|\Theta(\mu)\|_{\alpha}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(c_{0}c_{1})^{j}=\frac{1}{1-c_{0}c_{1}}=:C.

Pick a constant
c_{2}\in(0,1)
and consider

\nu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)
such that

\|\nu\|_{\alpha}\leq c_{2}/(Cc_{0})
. Then,

(4.8)

\|\Theta(\mu)\nu S\|_{\alpha}\leq\|\Theta(\mu)\|_{\alpha}\|\nu\|_{\alpha}\|S\|_{\alpha}\leq C\,\|\nu\|_{\alpha}c_{0}\leq c_{2}<1.

For such
\nu
we consider the series expansion

(I-\Theta(\mu)\nu S)^{-1}=I+\Theta(\mu)\nu S+\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}(\Theta(\mu)\nu S)^{j}.

Note that
\nu\mapsto\Theta(\mu)\nu S
is a linear map

\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega)\mapsto\mathrm{Lin}(\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega))
.
In view of (
4.8
) we have

\displaystyle\Big\|\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}(\Theta(\mu)\nu S)^{j}\Big\|_{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}\|\Theta(\mu)\|_{\alpha}^{j}\,\|\nu\|_{\alpha}^{j}\,\|S\|_{\alpha}^{j}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|\nu\|_{\alpha}^{2}\sum_{j=2}^{\infty}C^{j}\Big(\frac{c_{2}}{c_{0}C}\Big)^{j-2}c_{0}^{j}=\frac{c_{0}^{2}C^{2}}{1-c_{2}}\|\nu\|_{\alpha}^{2}.

This shows that
\nu\mapsto(I-\Theta(\mu)\nu S)^{-1}
is differentiable
at
\nu=0
and its differential equals

\nu\mapsto\Theta(\mu)\nu S
. From this and
(
4.6
) it follows that
\mu\mapsto\Theta(\mu)

is differentiable at every
\mu
with
\|\mu\|_{\alpha}<c=1/\|S\|_{\alpha}
,
and its differential equals

(4.9)

D_{\mu}\Theta(\mu)(\nu)=\Theta(\mu)\,\nu S\,\Theta(\mu),\quad\nu\in\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}(\Omega).

Since
\Theta(\mu)
is continuous in
\mu
, we see that

D_{\mu}\Theta(\mu)
is continuous, so

\Theta(\mu)
is continuously differentiable.
In particular,
\Theta(\mu)
is strongly differentiable
in the sense of Nijenhuis
[
67
]
.
Since the composition of continuously differentiable
families of bounded linear operators is continuously differentiable,
we see from (
4.9
) that the operator
D_{\mu}\Theta(\mu)

is continuously differentiable on
\mu
, so
\Theta(\mu)

is of class
\mathscr{C}^{2}
in
\mu
.
We can continue inductively to conclude that

\Theta(\mu)
is smooth in
\mu
.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

The fact that
\mu\to f(\mu)
is smooth for
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c

implies that if
\mu(t)
is a
\mathscr{C}^{l}
function
of parameters
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in U\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}

with
\|\mu(t)\|_{k,\alpha}<c
for all
t\in U
, then the map

U\ni t\mapsto f(\mu(t))\in\mathscr{C}^{k+1,\alpha}(\Omega)

is also of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}(U)
.
The analogous statement was proved by Ahlfors and Bers

[
3
, Theorem 2]
for solutions of the Beltrami
equation with
\mu\in L^{p}(\mathbb{C})
for
p>2
.
They also showed that if
\mu(t)
is a holomorphic function of

t\in U\subset\mathbb{C}^{m}

then the solution
f(\mu(t))
is holomorphic in
t
as well.
Holomorphic dependence of
f(\mu(t))
on
t

also holds in the context of Theorem
4.1
if
the map
t\mapsto\mu(t)
is holomorphic.

Given an open Riemann surface
(X,J)

and a domain
\Omega\subset X
, a family of smooth diffeomorphisms

\Phi_{b}:\Omega\to\Phi_{b}(\Omega)\subset X

(b\in B)

induces a family of complex structures
J_{b}=\Phi_{b}^{*}J
on
\Omega
.
The following result shows that a converse also holds on any
smoothly bounded domain
\Omega\Subset X
for sufficiently
small variations of the complex structure. This provides
an essential tool used in the proofs of our main results.
Recall that
\mathbb{D}
denotes the unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
(X,z)
is a Riemann domain over
\mathbb{C}
,

\Omega
is a relatively compact smoothly bounded domain in
X
,
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\in\Omega
are distinct points.
For any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
and
0<\alpha<1
there is a constant

c=c(k,\alpha)>0
such that for every function

\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,\mathbb{D})
with
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c

there is a
\mu
-conformal diffeomorphism

\Phi_{\mu}:\Omega\to\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset X
of class

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}
, depending smoothly on
\mu
, such that

\Phi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega}
and
\Phi_{\mu}(a_{j})=a_{j}
for all such
\mu

and
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Denoting by
J_{\mu}
the complex structure on
\Omega

determined by the function
\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega,\mathbb{D})

(see Section
2
), Theorem
4.3

gives a family
\Phi_{\mu}:\Omega\to\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset X

of
(J_{\mu},J_{0})
-biholomorphic maps onto their images in
X

with its given complex structure
J_{0}=J
, with
\Phi_{\mu}
depending
smoothly on
\mu
in a neighbourhood of
\mu_{0}=0

in
\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
.

Remark 4.4
.

Note that every diffeomorphism

\Phi_{\mu}:\Omega\to\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset X

in Theorem
4.3
is homotopic to the identity
map on
\Omega
by the homotopy

[0,1]\ni t\mapsto\Phi_{t\mu}
. In particular, if
the domain
\Omega
is Runge in
X
then so is

\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)
. In fact, a domain
\Omega
in a
Riemann surface
X
is Runge if and only
if the inclusion-induced homomorphism
H_{1}(\Omega,\mathbb{Z})\to H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})

of the first homology groups is injective, and this
condition is clearly invariant under homotopies.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
.

If
c>0
is small enough then for every

\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
with
\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c

the function
f(\mu):\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
, furnished by
Theorem
4.1
, is so close to the holomorphic immersion

f(0)=z|_{\Omega}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
in the
\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\Omega)

norm that it is an immersion. In the course of the proof, we shall decrease
the constant
c>0
whenever needed.

If
f(\mu)
is sufficiently close to
f(0)=z|_{\Omega}
,
we can lift it with respect to the holomorphic
immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
to a unique diffeomorphism

\Phi_{\mu}:\Omega\to\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset X
of class

\mathscr{C}^{(k+1,\alpha)}(\Omega)
, close to
\Phi_{0}=\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega}
, such that

(4.10)

z\circ\Phi_{\mu}=f(\mu)
holds on
\Omega
.

To see this, pick
r>0
such that
for any
q\in\overline{\Omega}
the immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
is injective on
the disc
U_{r}(q)\subset X
of radius
r
around
q

in the metric
|dz|^{2}
.
If
f(\mu)(q)\in\mathbb{C}
is close enough to
z(q)\in\mathbb{C}
, which holds if
\mu

is close to
0
, there is a unique point
p\in U_{r}(q)
such that
z(p)=f(\mu)(q)
,
and we set
\Phi_{\mu}(q)=p
. Thus,
\Phi_{\mu}(q)
is the unique
closest point to
q
among the points in the closed discrete set

z^{-1}(f(\mu)(q))\cap\subset X
,
so
\Phi_{\mu}
is well-defined on
\overline{\Omega}
.
This implies

z(\Phi_{\mu}(q))=z(p)=f(\mu)(q),

so (
4.10
) holds.
Since
\Phi_{\mu}
is locally obtained by postcomposing the
immersion
f(\mu):\overline{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
with a local inverse of
the
J
-holomorphic immersion
z:X\to\mathbb{C}
,
\Phi_{\mu}
is an immersion,
its Beltrami coefficient is the same as that of
f(\mu)

(which is
\mu
), and the regularity properties remain unchanged.
It is easily seen that
\Phi_{\mu}
is injective if
f(\mu)

is close enough to
z
, which holds if
c>0
is small enough.

This shows that
\Phi_{\mu}:\Omega\to\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset X

is a family of
\mu
-conformal diffeomorphisms
with the stated properties depending smoothly on
\mu
,
except that they need not satisfy
the interpolation conditions
\Phi_{\mu}(a_{j})=a_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
.
These are achieved as follows.
For every
j=1,\ldots,m
we choose a holomorphic vector field

v_{j}
on
X
which is nonzero at the point
a_{j}
and
it vanishes at all points
a_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\ldots,m\}\setminus\{j\}
.
Let
t\to\psi_{j,t}
denote the (local) flow of
v_{j}
for complex time
t
.
If
c>0
is small enough, there is an open relatively compact domain

\Omega^{\prime}\Subset X
such that
\Phi_{\mu}(\Omega)\subset\Omega^{\prime}

holds for all
\mu\in\mathscr{C}^{(k,\alpha)}(\Omega)
with

\|\mu\|_{k,\alpha}<c
. Choose a bigger domain
\Omega^{\prime\prime}\Subset X

such that
\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\subset\Omega^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
\overline{\Omega^{\prime\prime}}
is compact,
there is a
t_{0}>0
such that the holomorphic map

\Psi_{t}:=\psi_{1,t_{1}}\circ\cdots\circ\psi_{m,t_{m}}:\Omega^{\prime\prime}\to X

is well-defined for all
t=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{m})\in\mathbb{C}^{m}
in the polydisc

\Delta^{m}_{t_{0}}=\{|t_{j}|<t_{0},\ j=1,\ldots,m\}
.
Note that for every
t\in\Delta^{m}_{t_{0}}

the map
\Psi_{t}
, being a composition of flows of holomorphic
vector fields, is biholomorphic onto its image

\Psi_{t}(\Omega^{\prime\prime})\subset X
. The choice of the vector fields

in
the complex structure

.
(Hence,

in the original smooth structure on
\Omega
.)
The analogous result holds for maps to any complex manifold.

Proof.

Clearly,

is continuous. Since

(J_{b_{0}},J_{b})
-holomorphic

-holomorphic function

,

.
It follows that

in the complex structure

on

. Since

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k+1,\alpha)}

\mathscr{C}^{0,(k+1,\alpha)}

This proves the lemma for

.
Then,

\mathscr{C}^{l}

\mathscr{C}^{l}

-holomorphic in the space variable).

of class

\mathscr{C}^{l}
on

.

Since

.

We continue with the proof of Theorem

.
The compact set
\widetilde{K}=\Phi(B_{0}\times K)\subset B_{0}\times X

clearly satisfies the conditions of Lemma

.
In particular, its fibre

.

By Lemma

, for any given

.

This gives a locally finite open cover

of

such that

can be approximated as closely as desired

) is of class

It remains to obtain the interpolation conditions (c)

It suffices to explain this in the local situation given by Lemma

;

in

In view of (

) and (

), the points

.

.

.

6.
Mergelyan theorem on families of open Riemann surfaces

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.2
.
See also Theorems
7.5
and

7.7
for manifold-valued maps.

We begin with the basic case when
K
is a compact Runge set in
X=\mathbb{C}
.
Thus, let
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
be a continuous family of complex
structures of class
\mathscr{C}^{\alpha}
on
\mathbb{C}

and
f:B\times K\to\mathbb{C}
be a continuous function
such that
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp)
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
in
\mathring{K}
for every
b\in B
.
Choose an open disc
D\subset\mathbb{C}
with
K\subset D
.
Given a point
b_{0}\in B
and a number
\epsilon>0
, we shall find an
open neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B
of
b_{0}

and a continuous function
F:B_{0}\times D\to\mathbb{C}
such that
for every
b\in B_{0}
, the function
F_{b}=F(b,\cdotp):D\to\mathbb{C}

is
J_{b}
-holomorphic and satisfies

\sup_{z\in K}|F_{b}(z)-f_{b}(z)|<\epsilon
and the interpolation
conditions in the points of the finite set
A\subset\mathring{K}
.
The proof is then concluded by using a partition of unity
on
B
together with Theorem
1.1
.

By the Riemann mapping theorem,
J_{b_{0}}
is either
the standard complex structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}
on
\mathbb{C}
or the standard
complex structure on a disc containing
\overline{D}
.
The proof is the same in both cases, so let us assume
the former case. Choose a pair of discs

D^{\prime}\Subset\Omega\Subset\mathbb{C}
such that
\overline{D}\subset D^{\prime}
.
By Theorem
4.1
and Remark
4.5
(C)
there are a compact neighbourhood

B_{1}\subset B
of
b_{0}
and a family of diffeomorphisms

\Phi_{b}:\Omega\to\Phi_{b}(\Omega)=:\Omega_{b}\subset\mathbb{C}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{1,\alpha}(\Omega)
,
depending continuously on
b\in B_{1}
,
such that
\Phi_{b_{0}}=\mathrm{Id}_{\Omega}

and
\Phi_{b}
is biholomorphic from
(\Omega,J_{b})
onto

(\Omega_{b},J_{\mathrm{st}})
. For every
b\in B_{1}
set

(6.1)

K_{b}=\Phi_{b}(K),\quad D_{b}=\Phi_{b}(D),\quad\tilde{f}_{b}=f_{b}\circ\Phi^{-1}_{b}:K_{b}\to\mathbb{C}.

The function
\tilde{f}_{b}
is continuous on
K_{b}
,
J_{\mathrm{st}}
-holomorphic
in the interior
\mathring{K}_{b}
, and it depends continuously on
b\in B_{1}
.
After shrinking
B_{1}
around
b_{0}
if necessary, we may assume that

(6.2)

K_{b}\subset D_{b}\subset D^{\prime}\subset\Omega_{b}\ \ \text{for all $b\in B_{1}$}.

It now suffices to find a neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B_{1}

of
b_{0}
and a continuous function
\widetilde{F}:B_{0}\times D^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}

such that for every
b\in B_{0}
, the function

\widetilde{F}_{b}=\widetilde{F}(b,\cdotp):D^{\prime}\to\mathbb{C}

is holomorphic in the standard structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}
on
\mathbb{C}
and
it satisfies
\sup_{z\in K_{b}}|\widetilde{F}_{b}(z)-\tilde{f}_{b}(z)|<\epsilon
.
Indeed, the function
F_{b}=\widetilde{F}_{b}\circ\Phi_{b}
is then

J_{b}
-holomorphic on
D
and satisfies

\sup_{z\in K}|F_{b}(z)-f_{b}(z)|<\epsilon
for every
b\in B_{0}
.
In summary, locally in the parameter
we changed the Mergelyan approximation problem
with respect to a family of complex structures to a similar problem
on a moving family of compact sets in
\mathbb{C}
with
respect to the standard complex structure.
We now show that this task can be realised by inspecting
the proof of the classical Mergelyan’s theorem
[
64
]
;
see also Gaier
[
39
, p. 97]
,
Gamelin
[
40
]
, and Rudin
[
73
]
.

To simplify the notation, we drop the tildes and consider

f_{b}:K_{b}\to\mathbb{C}
as a family of continuous functions that are
holomorphic on
\mathring{K}_{b}
and depend continuously on
b\in B_{1}
.
Since the compact sets
K_{b}
in (
6.1
)
vary continuously with
b
, the set
\bigcup_{b\in B_{1}}K_{b}
is compact,
and by (
6.2
) it is contained in
D^{\prime}
.
By Tietze’s extension theorem,
there is a continuous function
f:B_{1}\times\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
with
compact support contained in
B_{1}\times D^{\prime}
such that
f(b,\cdotp)

agrees with the given function
f_{b}
on
K_{b}
for every
b\in B_{1}
.
For
\delta>0
we denote by
\omega(\delta)
the modulus
of continuity of
f
with respect to the second variable:

\omega(\delta)=\max\{|f(b,z)-f(b,w)|:b\in B_{1},\ z,w\in\mathbb{C},\ |z-w|\leq\delta\}.

Note that
\lim_{\delta\to 0}\omega(\delta)=0
, and we choose

\delta
small enough such that

(6.3)

6000\,\omega(\delta)<\epsilon.

Define the function
A_{\delta}:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
by setting
A_{\delta}(z)=0

for
|z|>\delta
and

A_{\delta}(z)=\frac{3}{\pi\delta^{2}}\left(1-\frac{|z|^{2}}{\delta^{2}}\right)^{2},\qquad 0\leq|z|\leq\delta.

The convolution of
f
and
A_{\delta}
with respect to the
z
variable
is a continuous function
f^{\delta}
on
B_{1}\times\mathbb{C}
with compact
support whose differential with respect to the
z
-variable
exists and is also continuous in both variables. Furthermore,
for every
b\in B_{1}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}
we have that

|f(b,z)-f^{\delta}(b,z)|<\omega(\delta)\ \ \text{and}\ \ \Big|\frac{\partial f^{\delta}}{\partial\bar{z}}(b,z)\Big|<\frac{2\omega(\delta)}{\delta},

and

f^{\delta}_{b}:=f^{\delta}(b,\cdotp)=f_{b}\ \ \text{on}\ \ K^{\delta}_{b}:=\{z\in K_{b}:\mathrm{dist}(z,\mathbb{C}\setminus K_{b})>\delta\}.

These properties follow directly from the case for a single
b
,
which is proved in the cited papers. Decreasing
\delta>0
if necessary,
we may assume that
\mathrm{supp}f^{\delta}_{b}\subset D^{\prime}
for all
b\in B_{1}
.
Note that
K^{\delta}_{b}
is an open set contained in
\mathring{K}_{b}
.
Since
f^{\delta}_{b}=f_{b}
on
K^{\delta}_{b}
, it follows that

f^{\delta}_{b}
is holomorphic on
K^{\delta}_{b}
and hence

(6.4)

\mathrm{supp}(\overline{\partial}f^{\delta}_{b})\subset L_{b}:=\overline{D}^{\prime}\setminus K^{\delta}_{b}\quad\text{for all $b\in B_{1}$}.

Recall that
K_{b_{0}}=K
. We cover the compact set

L_{b_{0}}=\overline{D}^{\prime}\setminus K^{\delta}_{b_{0}}

by finitely many open discs
\Delta_{j}=\mathbb{D}(z_{j},2\delta)

(j=1,\ldots,n)
of radius
2\delta
with centres
z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus K
.
Since
\mathbb{C}\setminus K
is connected, each disc
\overline{\Delta}_{j}

contains a compact Jordan arc
E_{j}
connecting its centre
z_{j}
to a boundary
point of
\Delta_{j}
such that
E_{j}\cap K=\varnothing
.
Set
E=\bigcup_{j=1}^{n}E_{j}
.
Since the compact sets
K_{b}
in (
6.1
)
depend continuously on
b
,
there is a neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B_{1}
of
b_{0}
such that
for all
b\in B_{0}
and
j=1,\ldots,n
we have

L_{b}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad E\cap K_{b}=\varnothing.

In particular, the open set
\mathbb{C}\setminus E

contains
\bigcup_{b\in B_{0}}K_{b}
. For
b\in B_{0}
consider the sets

L_{b,1}=L_{b}\cap\overline{\Delta}_{1},\quad L_{b,j}=L_{b}\cap\overline{\Delta}_{j}\setminus(L_{b,1}\cup\ldots\cup L_{b,j-1})\ \ \text{for}\ \ j=2,\ldots,n.

These sets depend continuously on
b
.
Write
\zeta=u+\mathfrak{i}v
. By the Cauchy–Green formula
(
3.3
) we have

(6.5)

f^{\delta}_{b}(z)=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\mathbb{C}}\frac{\partial f^{\delta}_{b}(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{\zeta}}\frac{du\,dv}{z-\zeta}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{L_{b,j}}\frac{\partial f^{\delta}_{b}(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{\zeta}}\frac{du\,dv}{z-\zeta}\quad\text{for $z\in\mathbb{C}$ and $b\in B_{1}$.}

The main point now is to approximate the Cauchy kernel

\frac{1}{z-\zeta}
for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E_{j}

and
\zeta\in\Delta_{j}
sufficiently well by a function of the form

P_{j}(z,\zeta)=g_{j}(z)+(\zeta-c_{j})g_{j}(z)^{2},

where
g_{j}\in\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{C}\setminus E_{j})
and
c_{j}\in\mathbb{C}
.
This is accomplished by Mergelyan’s lemma
[
64
]
,
which says that
g_{j}
and
c_{j}
can be chosen
such that for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E_{j}

and
\zeta\in\Delta_{j}
we have

(6.6)

|P_{j}(z,\zeta)|<\frac{50}{\delta}\quad\text{and}\quad\bigg|P_{j}(z,\zeta)-\frac{1}{z-\zeta}\bigg|<\frac{4000\,\delta^{2}}{|z-\zeta|^{3}}.

(See
[
39
, p. 101]
or
[
73
, Lemma 20.2]
.)
The function

(6.7)

F^{\delta}_{b}(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{L_{b,j}}\frac{\partial f^{\delta}_{b}(\zeta)}{\partial\bar{\zeta}}P_{j}(z,\zeta)\,du\,dv

depends continuously on
b\in B_{0}
, for every
b
it
is holomorphic in the open set
\mathbb{C}\setminus E\supset K_{b}

(since
P_{j}(\cdotp,\zeta)
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}\setminus E_{j}

for every
j
), and it follows from (
6.3
) and
(
6.5
)–(
6.7
) that

|F^{\delta}_{b}(z)-f^{\delta}_{b}(z)|<6000\,\omega(\delta)<\epsilon\quad\text{for all $z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E$ and $b\in B_{0}$}.

The proof of the first estimate can be found in the cited works.
Finally, the interpolation in finitely many interior points of
K

can be handled as in the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

This proves Theorem
1.2
when
K
is a compact
Runge set in
X=\mathbb{C}
. Let us now consider the general case when
X
is
an arbitrary open Riemann surface. We shall adjust Bishop’s localization
theorem
[
18
]
to the variable complex structure
setting, using solutions of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation as in Sakai’s
paper
[
75
]
. See also the exposition
in
[
29
, pp. 142–143]
.

Assume that
K\subset X
and
f:B\times K\to\mathbb{C}

are as in the statement of Theorem
1.2
.
In particular, there is a number
c>0
such that
each relatively compact connected component of

X\setminus K
has diameter at least
c
in some fixed
Riemannian metric on
X
. By deleting
a point from
X
if necessary, we may assume that

X
is an open surface. Let
\widehat{K}
denote the smallest
compact Runge set in
X
containing
K
. (In any complex
structure on
X
,

\widehat{K}
is the holomorphically convex hull of
K
.)
Choose a smoothly bounded relatively compact Runge domain

\Omega\Subset X
with
\widehat{K}\subset\Omega
.
Let
B_{0}\subset B
be a compact neighbourhood of
b_{0}

such that Theorem
4.1
applies on
\Omega

for all
b\in B_{0}
. In particular, we have continuous families
of
J_{b}
-holomorphic immersions
z_{b}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
,
Cauchy kernels
\omega_{b}=\Phi_{b}^{*}\omega

where
\Phi_{b}
are given by Theorem
4.3
,
and Cauchy operators
P_{b}
on
(\Omega,J_{b})

for
b\in B_{0}
(see Remark
4.5
(A)).
We cover
K
by finitely many open coordinate discs

U_{1},\ldots,U_{m}\subset\Omega
of diameter at most
c
.
Choose closed discs
D_{j}\subset U_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,m
whose interiors
still cover
K
. Note that
U_{j}\setminus(K\cap D_{j})
is connected
for every
j
. (Indeed, a relatively compact component
of
U_{j}\setminus(K\cap D_{j})
is also a connected component of

X\setminus K
which is contained in
\mathring{D}_{j}
, so it has
diameter
<c
, contradicting the assumption.)
Let
\chi_{j}
be a smooth partition of unity on a neighbourhood of
K

with respect to the cover
\{U_{j}\}
. Since every
U_{j}
is a planar domain,
the special case of the theorem proved above
shows that for any
\epsilon>0
there are continuous functions

f_{j}:B_{0}\times U_{j}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
f_{j}(b,\cdotp):U_{j}\to\mathbb{C}

is
J_{b}
-holomorphic for every
b\in B_{0}
and satisfies

\max_{x\in K\cap D_{j}}|f_{j}(b,x)-f(b,x)|<\epsilon\ \ \text{for every $b\in B_{0}$ and $j=1,\ldots,m$.}

Set
g=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\chi_{j}f_{j}
and
g_{b}=g(b,\cdotp)
for
b\in B_{0}
.
Let
\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}
denote the
(0,1)
-derivative with respect to the
complex structure
J_{b}
and the immersion
z_{b}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}

(see (
2.6
)).
On some open neighbourhood
U=U_{\epsilon}\subset\Omega
of
K

we then have
\|g-f\|_{\mathscr{C}(B_{0}\times U)}=O(\epsilon)
and

\overline{\partial}_{b}\,g=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\partial}_{b}\chi_{j}\cdot f_{j}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\partial}_{b}\chi_{j}\cdot(f_{j}-f),\qquad\|\overline{\partial}_{b}\,g\|_{\mathscr{C}(U)}=O(\epsilon),

where the bound in

is uniform for
b\in B_{0}
.
(In the second equality we used that

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\overline{\partial}_{b}\chi_{j}=0
near
K
.)
Let
\chi\in\mathscr{C}^{\infty}_{0}(U)
be a cut-off function with

0\leq\chi\leq 1
and
\chi\equiv 1
near
K
. Then we have

\|\chi\cdot\overline{\partial}_{b}g\|_{\mathscr{C}(U)}=O(\epsilon)
,
and hence
\|P_{b}(\chi\cdot\overline{\partial}g)\|_{\mathscr{C}(U)}=O(\epsilon)
.
For every
b\in B_{0}
the function

F(b,\cdotp)=g_{b}-P_{b}(\chi\cdotp\overline{\partial}_{b}\,g_{b})

is then
J_{b}
-holomorphic on a neighbourhood of
K
, which can be
chosen independent of

, it depends continuously on
b\in B_{0}

and satisfies
\|F-f\|_{\mathscr{C}(B_{0}\times K)}=O(\epsilon)
.
Continuity in
b
is seen by noting that
the
(0,1)
-forms
\xi_{b}=\chi\cdotp\overline{\partial}_{b}\,g_{b}
have
compact support contained in the interior of
\Omega
, and solving

\overline{\partial}u_{b}=\xi_{b}
reduces to solving the same problems
for the family of
(0,1)
-forms
(\Phi_{b}^{-1})^{*}(\xi_{b})
in the
fixed complex structure

on

. (See Theorem

for

This proves the approximation result for

. Interpolation in finitely many points of

. The proof is concluded by finding a

of

by a partition of unity on

subordinate to this cover;

).

7.
The Oka principle for maps from families of open Riemann surfaces
to Oka manifolds

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
. The same proof also
gives the generalization stated in Theorem
7.4
.
We then prove a couple of Mergelyan-type approximation theorems
for manifold-valued maps from families of open Riemann surfaces;
see Theorems
7.5
and
7.7
.
With future applications in mind, the technical results
in Lemmas
7.2
and
7.3
are obtained
in the bigger generality when
X
is a Stein manifold
of arbitrary dimension.

Recall that a compact set
K
in a complex manifold
X
is said to be
a
Stein compact
if it admits a basis of open Stein neighbourhoods.
Every compact
\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex set in a Stein manifold

X
is a Stein compact (see
[
53
, Theorem 5.1.6]
).
Given a complex manifold
Y
, we denote by

\overline{\mathscr{O}}(K,Y)

the space of continuous maps
K\to Y
which are uniform
limits of holomorphic maps on open neighbourhoods of
K
in
X
.
Furthermore, we denote by

\overline{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,Y)

the space of continuous maps
f:K\to Y
with the property
that every point
x\in K
has an
open neighbourhood
U\subset X
such that

f|_{K\cap\overline{U}}\in\overline{\mathscr{O}}(K\cap\overline{U})
. Clearly,
we have the inclusions

\{f|_{K}:f\in\mathscr{O}(K,Y)\}\subset\overline{\mathscr{O}}(K,Y)\subset\overline{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,Y)\subset\mathscr{A}(K,Y).

The importance of the space
\overline{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,Y)
lies in the following result
of Poletsky
[
71
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Theorem 7.1

(Poletsky
[
71
]
)
.

If
K
is a Stein compact in a complex manifold
X
,
Y

is a complex manifold and
f\in\overline{\mathscr{O}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(K,Y)
, then the graph of
f

on
K
is a Stein compact in
X\times Y
.

We consider
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as the standard real subspace
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. By using Theorem
7.1
we prove
the following.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
X
be a Stein manifold and
\pi:\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
be
the projection. Assume that
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X
is a compact set
such that

and

\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex for every

Let

be an open neighbourhood of

in

,

be a complex manifold, and

be a continuous map
such that for every

Then, the graph

(7.1)

G_{f}=\{(b,x,f(b,x)):(b,x)\in K\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X\times Y

of

is a Stein compact in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X\times Y
,
and

there are a neighbourhood

of

\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X
,
a neighbourhood
\widetilde{U}\subset U\cap V

(c)

Proof.

By Remark
5.2
(b), the set

\mathscr{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X)
-convex, whence a Stein compact.
We shall now verify that the map

, and hence

Fix a point

.
Since the map

[

(see also Colţoiu

]

Choose a holomorphic embedding

By a theorem of Docquier and Grauert

(see also

of

and a holomorphic retraction

.
By continuity of

with respect to

and

) there is a compact neighbourhood

of

Hence, the map

(7.2)

is well-defined on a neighbourhood of

in

,
and

is holomorphic on a neighbourhood of
K_{b}

in
X
for every

.
By Lemma

we can approximate

as closely as desired

from a neighbourhood

of

.
Assuming that the approximation is close enough and the

we have that

.
Let

\tilde{f}:=\tau\circ\Theta^{-1}\circ\rho\circ\tilde{h}:W\to Y

uniformly on

.
Since this holds for every

(

To prove that

and the last statement in the lemma,
we apply the same argument with the entire parameter space

.
Choose a Stein neighbourhood

\Gamma\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X\times Y
of

(
7.1
),
a holomorphic embedding
\Theta:\Gamma\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}
,
and a holomorphic retraction
\rho:O\to\Theta(\Gamma)

from a neighbourhood

of
\Theta(\Gamma)
.
The map

given by (

) is now
defined on a neighbourhood

of

,
and the map

is holomorphic on
\widetilde{U}_{b}
for every
b\in B
.
By Lemma
5.1
we can approximate

as closely as desired
uniformly on
K
by a holomorphic map
\tilde{h}:V\to\mathbb{C}^{N}

from a neighbourhood
V\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X
of

.
As before, we may assume that

. The map

\tilde{f}:V\to Y
given by the formula (

) is then holomorphic
and it approximates

on
K
as closely as desired.
Furthermore, if

is close enough to

on

and after shrinking the neighbourhood

if necessary, the family of convex combinations

h_{t}=(1-t)h+t\tilde{h}:\widetilde{U}\to\mathbb{C}^{N},\quad t\in I

. The family of maps

The last statement of the lemma follows by the same argument,

∎

for maps with values in an Oka manifold and with

Lemma 7.3
.

Assume that

are compact subsets of

Let

and the fibre

\mathscr{O}(X)
-convex for every

.
Assume that

is an open neighbourhood of

in

,

is an Oka manifold,
and

such that for every

.
Fix

and

. After shrinking the open set

,
there is a homotopy

(t\in I=[0,1])

with the following properties.

(a)

f_{0}=f|_{B\times X}
.

(b)

for every

and

.

(c)

for all

and

.

(e)

The map

in a neighbourhood of
B_{0}
.

If in addition

\mathscr{C}^{l}

\mathbb{R}^{n}

the homotopy

for all

Proof.

We focus on the case

. It will be clear that the same proof
gives the corresponding results in the general case by using the
corresponding versions of Lemmas

and

.

By the assumption, there are a neighbourhood

of

and a retraction

onto

. The conditions
imply that

is a neighbourhood of

in

.
Since

is the identity map, it follows that there is an open

of

such that

and
\rho(V)\subset B^{\prime}
. Replacing

by

extends

to a continuous map

,
still denoted

. Since every compact subset

of
\mathbb{R}^{n}

is polynomially convex in

,

may be chosen Stein. If

then the retraction

We claim that there are an open neighbourhood

of

in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X

and a homotopy

(t\in I=[0,1])
connecting

to a holomorphic map

for every

and

. Note that Lemma

furnishes a homotopy

),
(

), and (

are defined on

in

.
Hence, the same argument, using convex combinations as
in (

by (

yields a desired homotopy on a neighbourhood

of

\mathbb{C}^{n}\times X

On

. Furthermore,

Since

is Stein, the set

is

and

the main result of Oka theory (see

furnishes a homotopy

(t\in I)

from

to a holomorphic map

(t\in I)

satisfies conditions (a)–(c) (where we take

in (c)),
and it satisfies condition (e) for all

.
To obtain (d), choose a smooth function

, and replace

.

This proves the lemma for

. The same arguments apply
when

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

We consider the case

.
The arguments in the general case are similar

Let

as
in the theorem. We shall say that a closed subset

9.
The
\overline{\partial}
-equation and the Oka principle for line bundles

A standard application of Runge’s theorem on open Riemann
surfaces (and, more generally, of the Oka–Weil theorem on
Stein manifolds) is the global solvability of the
\overline{\partial}
-equation.
By using Theorems
1.1
,
3.2
,
and
4.3
we will now show that the same holds
on families of open Riemann surfaces.

Assume that
X
is a smooth open oriented surface,

B
is a parameter space as in Theorem
1.4

(its precise type depending on the value of the integer
l\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
),
and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a family of complex structures
of Hölder class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}
with
k\geq 1
,
0\leq l\leq k+1
,
and
0<\alpha<1
. Let
\tau:Z=B\times X\to B
denote
the projection onto the parameter space, and endow
the fibre
Z_{b}=\tau^{-1}(b)\cong X

(b\in B)
with the
complex structure
J_{b}
.
For a fixed
b\in B
we denote by
\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}

the
(0,1)
-differential in the complex structure
J_{b}
in
X
.
By Theorem
8.1
there exists a family
\{\theta_{b}\}_{b\in B}

of nowhere vanishing holomorphic
1
-forms on
(X,J_{b})

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
. Given an integer

, a number
0<a<1
, and a function

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s+1,a)}
, we have that

(9.1)

\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}f_{b}=\beta_{b}=g_{b}\bar{\theta}_{b},\quad b\in B,

where the function
g:Z\to\mathbb{C}
determined by
g_{b}=g(b,\cdotp)

(b\in B)
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s,a)}
.
Note that the family of
(0,1)
-forms
\beta_{b}=g_{b}\bar{\theta}_{b}

is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s,a)}
if and only if

is such.
The following result gives a global solution of the equation
(

) for suitable values of the integers

, and

.

Theorem 9.1
.

Let
k,l,s
be integers satisfying
0\leq 2l\leq s<k
,
and let
0<a,\alpha<1
.
Assume that
X
is a smooth open surface,
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}

is a family of complex structures of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}

on
X
as in Theorem
1.4
, and
\{\theta_{b}\}_{b\in B}

is a family of
J_{b}
-holomorphic
1
-forms of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}

furnished by Theorem
8.1
. Given a family of

(0,1)
-forms
\beta_{b}=g_{b}\bar{\theta}_{b}
on
X

(b\in B)

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s,a)}
, there is a function
f:Z=B\times X\to\mathbb{C}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s+1-2l,a)}
such that
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp):X\to\mathbb{C}

satisfies (
9.1
).

Compared with the standard result in a fixed complex structure,
where the solution of the
\overline{\partial}

equation gains one derivative on Hölder spaces,
we have a loss of
2l
derivatives in Theorem
9.1
.
This may be due to the method of proof and
is caused by the fact that compositions and inverses of
\mathscr{C}^{l,k}

maps need not be of the same class; see Lemma
5.3
.
Note however that there is no loss when
l=0
, i.e., for continuous
dependence of the data
\beta_{b}
and the solutions

f_{b}
on the parameter

Proof.

It suffices to show that for every point
b_{0}\in B
and
smoothly bounded relatively compact domain
\Omega\Subset X

there is a neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B
of
b_{0}
such that the

9.1
) is solvable on
B_{0}\times\Omega
.
By using partitions of unity on

we then obtain solvability on

B\times\Omega
. The proof is completed by an exhaustion of

by an increasing family of compact Runge sets, using
Theorem
1.1
at every step of the induction to
ensure convergence of solutions. One follows the standard
scheme in the proof of Cartan’s Theorem B,
see e.g.
[
48
, Section VIII.14]
.
Furthermore, it suffices to consider the case when the forms
\beta_{b}

have compact support contained in slightly
smaller domain
\Omega^{\prime}\Subset\Omega
for every
b\in B_{0}
.

Fix a point
b_{0}\in B
and an open
relatively compact neighbourhood
B_{0}\subset B

of
b_{0}
such that Theorem
4.3
furnishes a family of

(J_{b},J_{b_{0}})
-biholomorphic maps

\Phi_{b}:\Omega\to\Phi_{b}(\Omega)\subset X

(b\in\overline{B}_{0})

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
.
Let

be a
J_{b_{0}}
-holomorphic immersion
(see
[
47
]
). Then,

h_{b}=h\circ\Phi_{b}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C},\quad b\in B_{0}

is a family of
J_{b}
-holomorphic immersions
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
, and we may take
\bar{\theta}_{b}=d\bar{h}_{b}

as the family of
(0,1)
-forms in (

).
Note that
\bar{\theta}_{b}
is antiholomorphic for every

.
Thus,
\beta_{b}=g_{b}\,d\bar{h}_{b}
where the function

with

is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s,a)}
.
Since the family of inverse maps
\Psi_{b}=\Phi^{-1}_{b}

is of class

,
the family of composition

\mathscr{C}^{l,(s-l,a)}

\tilde{g}_{b}
of biorder

(in any system
of local coordinates on

of terms obtained by precomposing a derivative of biorder

(l,s)
of

of

(This is the argument behind Lemma

(b).)
Since

(where the latter number is

of

\tilde{g}_{b}
of

(l,s-l)
are also of class
\mathscr{C}^{a}
in the
space variable, so
\tilde{g}_{b}
is of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(s-l,a)}
as claimed.
Note that
h_{b}\circ\Psi_{b}=h
and hence

\Psi_{b}^{*}d\bar{h}_{b}=d\bar{h}
holds for all
b\in B_{0}
.
Thus, the family of
(0,1)
-forms with compact supports
in the complex structure
J_{b_{0}}
on

\Psi_{b}^{*}\beta_{b}=(g_{b}\circ\Psi_{b})\Psi_{b}^{*}d\bar{h}_{b}=\tilde{g}_{b}\,d\bar{h},\quad b\in B_{0},

is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s-l,a)}
.
Let
\omega
be a Cauchy kernel on
(X,J_{b_{0}})
associated
to the holomorphic immersion

(see Sect.
3
),
and let
P
be the associated Cauchy operator (

).
Our assumption implies that the union of supports of the functions

\tilde{g}_{b}

(b\in B_{0})
lies in a compact subset of

.
Hence, by Theorem
3.2
(a)
the family of functions
\tilde{f}_{b}=P(\tilde{g}_{b})

(b\in B_{0})
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s-l+1,a)}
and

\overline{\partial}_{J_{b_{0}}}\tilde{f}_{b}=\tilde{g}_{b}\,d\bar{h}\quad\text{holds for every $b\in B_{0}$}.

Regularity in the parameter

follows from the fact
that

is a linear operator independent of

.

Set
f_{b}=\tilde{f}_{b}\circ\Phi_{b}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}

for

. Recall that
\tilde{g}_{b}\circ\Phi_{b}=g_{b}

and

for

. Since

is

(J_{b},J_{b_{0}})
-holomorphic, it follows that

\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}f_{b}=\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}(\Phi_{b}^{*}\tilde{f}_{b})=\Phi_{b}^{*}(\overline{\partial}_{J_{b_{0}}}\tilde{f}_{b})=\Phi_{b}^{*}(\tilde{g}_{b}\,d\bar{h})=g_{b}\,d\bar{h}_{b}

) holds.
As
\{\tilde{f}_{b}\}_{b\in B_{0}}
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s+1-l,a)}
,

\{\Phi_{b}\}_{b\in B_{0}}
is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
,
and

We now consider the Cousin-I problem on a family of open
Riemann surfaces. Let
B
,
X
, and
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}

be as in Theorem
1.4
, where the family
J_{b}

is of class
\mathscr{C}^{0,(k,\alpha)}
for some
k\geq 1

and
0<\alpha<1
(so
l=0
and
J_{b}
is continuous in
b\in B
).
Let
\mathscr{O}
denote the sheaf of germs of continuous functions

f
on
Z=B\times X
such that
f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp)
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for each
b
. By Lemma
5.4
,
\mathscr{O}
is a subsheaf

\mathscr{C}^{0,k+1}
, the sheaf of germs of continuous functions
on
Z
which are of class
\mathscr{C}^{k+1}
in the space variable.

Theorem 9.2
.

(Assumptions as above.)
We have that
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0
for all
q=1,2,\ldots
.

Proof.

Pick an integer
s
with
0\leq s<k
and a number
0<a<1
.
Then,
\mathscr{C}^{0,k+1}
is a subsheaf of the sheaf
\mathscr{C}^{0,(s+1,a)}
,
and we have the following sequence of sheaf homomorphisms

(9.2)

0\longrightarrow\mathscr{O}\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow\mathscr{C}^{0,(s+1,a)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\partial}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathscr{C}^{0,(s,a)}_{(0,1)}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathscr{C}^{l,(s,a)}_{(0,1)}
is the sheaf of germs
of
(0,1)
-forms of class
\mathscr{C}^{0,(s,a)}
on the fibres

Z_{b}=(X,J_{b})
and
\overline{\partial}
is the operator
which equals
\overline{\partial}_{J_{b}}
on
Z_{b}
for every
b\in B
.
These are sheaves of unital abelian rings.
By Theorem
9.1
applied with
l=0
,
the resolution (
9.2
) is exact.
Since the second and the third sheaf in (
9.2
)
are fine sheaves (as they admit partitions of unity), they are acyclic
and we conclude that

H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O})=\Gamma(Z,\mathscr{C}^{0,(s,a)}_{(0,1)})/\overline{\partial}(\mathscr{C}^{0,(s+1,a)}(Z))

and
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0
for

(see
[

).
Here,

denotes the space of global sections.
The quotient group on the right hand side above
vanishes by Theorem

Recall that every holomorphic vector bundle on an
open Riemann surface is holomorphically trivial by the Oka–Grauert principle; see Oka
[
69
]
, Grauert
[
45
]
,
and
[
33
, Theorem 5.3.1]
.
We can use Theorem
9.2
to
obtain an Oka principle for isomorphism classes of
families of holomorphic line bundles on families
of open Riemann surfaces. Using the above notation, let

\mathscr{O}^{*}\subset\mathscr{O}
and
\mathscr{C}^{*}\subset\mathscr{C}

denote the subsheaves of the respective sheaves
consisting of functions with nonzero values;
these are sheaves of abelian groups.
A topological complex line bundle
E\to Z

is said to be
X
-holomorphic if it admits
a transition cocycle consisting of sections of the sheaf
\mathscr{O}
.
The restriction of such a line bundle to any fibre

Z_{b}=(X,J_{b})
is a holomorphic line bundle on the Riemann
surface
(X,J_{b})
. We denote by
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)
the set of isomorphism
classes of topological
X
-holomorphic line bundles on
Z=B\times X
.
By the standard argument,
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})
.
We have the following Oka principle.

Theorem 9.3
.

(Assumptions as above and
k\geq 1
.)
Every topological complex line bundle on
Z=B\times X

is isomorphic to an
X
-holomorphic topological line bundle,
and any two
X
-holomorphic topological line bundles on
Z

which are topologically isomorphic are also
isomorphic as
X
-holomorphic topological line bundles.
Furthermore,
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{2}(Z,\mathbb{Z})
.
In particular, if
B
is contractible then every
X
-holomorphic
line bundle on
B\times X
is trivial.

Proof.

The proof follows the standard argument for complex line
bundles on a complex manifold; see

[
33
, Theorem 5.2.2]
.
Let
\sigma(f)=\mathrm{e}^{2\pi\mathfrak{i}f}
. Consider the following
commutative diagram whose rows are
exponential sheaf sequences and the vertical arrows are
the natural inclusions:

(9.3)

\begin{array}[]{*{9}c}0&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}&\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow&\mathscr{O}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathscr{O}^{*}&\longrightarrow&1\\
&&\big\downarrow&&\big\downarrow&&\!\!\big\downarrow&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{Z}&\lhook\joinrel\relbar\joinrel\rightarrow&\mathscr{C}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sigma}}{{\longrightarrow}}&\mathscr{C}^{*}&\longrightarrow&1\\
\end{array}

Note that
\mathscr{C}
is a fine sheaf,
and hence
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{C})=0
for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Theorem
9.2
we also have
H^{q}(Z,\mathscr{O})=0

for all
q\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence, the relevant part of
the long exact sequence of cohomology groups associated
to the diagram (
9.3
) gives

\begin{array}[]{*{8}c}0&\longrightarrow&H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})&\longrightarrow&H^{2}(Z;\mathbb{Z})&\longrightarrow&0\\
&&\big\downarrow&&\big\|&&\\
0&\longrightarrow&H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{C}^{*})&\longrightarrow&H^{2}(Z;\mathbb{Z})&\ \,\longrightarrow&0\\
\end{array}

Thus, all arrows in the central square are isomorphisms.
Since
\mathrm{Pic}(Z)\cong H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{O}^{*})
and

H^{1}(Z,\mathscr{C}^{*})
is the set of isomorphisms classes
of topological line bundles on
Z
, the theorem follows.
∎

Remark 9.4
.

The following observation was communicated to me by Finnur Lárusson.
Theorem

allows us to extend Theorem

classical Oka–Grauert theory. A topological vector bundle on

is the pullback

by a continuous map

from
B\times X
to a suitable Grassmannian

of the universal bundle
\mathbb{U}\to G

Since
G
is complex homogeneous and hence an Oka manifold,
Theorem
1.4
allows us to deform
f
to a map

F:B\times X\to G
such that
F(b,\cdotp):X\to G
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic
for every
b\in B
. The pullback
F^{*}\mathbb{U}
is then
an
X
-holomorphic vector bundle isomorphic to
f^{*}\mathbb{U}
.
The second part of Theorem
9.3
is obtained similarly with
the pair of parameter spaces
B\times\{0,1\}\subset B\times[0,1]
.
This argument generalizes to other types of bundles for which we
have an Oka classifying space with a universal bundle.
We postpone a more complete treatment of this subject
to a subsequent publication.

10.
Families of directed holomorphic immersions and
of conformal minimal immersions

In this section, we illustrate how the results of this paper
can be used to construct families of directed holomorphic immersions
and conformal minimal immersions from a family of open Riemann surfaces.
There are many further problems of this kind which may possibly or even
likely be treated by these new methods, and we indicate a few of them
in Problem
10.7
.

A connected compact complex submanifold
Y
of the complex
projective space
\mathbb{CP}^{n-1}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, determines the punctured
complex cone

(10.1)

A=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}:[z_{1}:\cdots:z_{n}]\in Y\}.

Note that
A
is smooth and connected, and its closure

\overline{A}=A\cup\{0\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is an algebraic subvariety
of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
by Chow’s theorem.
By
[
33
, Theorem 5.6.5]
,
A

is an Oka manifold if and only if
Y
is an Oka manifold.
By
[
11
, Lemma 3.5.1]
,
the convex hull of
A

is the smallest complex subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
containing
A
,
and we shall assume without loss of generality that this hull is all of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Let
X
be a connected open Riemann surface and
\theta
be a
nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form on
X
.
A holomorphic immersion
h:X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is said to be
directed by

A
,
or an
A
-immersion
, if its complex derivative with respect to any
local holomorphic coordinate on
X
takes its values in
A
.
Clearly, this holds if and only if the holomorphic map

f=dh/\theta:X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
assume values in
A
.
Conversely, a holomorphic map
f:X\to A
satisfying
the period vanishing conditions

(10.2)

\int_{C}f\theta=0\quad\text{for all closed curves $C\subset X$}

integrates to a holomorphic
A
-immersion
h:X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
by setting

h(x)=v+\int_{x_{0}}^{x}f\theta,\quad x\in X

for any
x_{0}\in X
and
v\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Since
f\theta
is a holomorphic 1-form, it suffices to verify
conditions (
10.2
)
on a basis of the homology group
H_{1}(X,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}^{r}
,
a free abelian group of some rank
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}\cup\{\infty\}
.

Note that a map directed by the cone
A=\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}
is simply an immersion.
Another case of major interest is the
null quadric

(10.3)

\mathbf{A}=\bigl\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}_{*}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\{0\}:z_{1}^{2}+z_{2}^{2}+\cdots+z_{n}^{2}=0\bigr\},\quad n\geq 3.

Holomorphic immersions directed by
\mathbf{A}
are called

holomorphic null curves
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
The real and the imaginary part of a holomorphic null immersion

X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
are conformal harmonic immersions
X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Such immersions parameterize minimal surfaces, hence
are called conformal minimal immersions.
Conversely, every conformal minimal immersion
X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

is locally (on any simply connected
domain) the real part of a holomorphic null curve.
See
[
11
,
70
]

for more information.

Directed holomorphic immersions were studied by Alarcón and Forstnerič
in
[
8
]
. Under the assumption
that
A
is an Oka manifold, they proved an Oka principle with Runge
and Mergelyan approximation for holomorphic
A
-immersions

[
8
, Theorems 2.6 and 7.2]
.
They also showed that every holomorphic
A
-immersion can be
approximated by holomorphic
A
-embeddings when
n\geq 3
,
and by proper holomorphic
A
-embeddings under an additional
assumption on the cone
[
8
, Theorem 8.1]
.
Alarcón and Castro-Infantes
[
6
]

added interpolation to the picture.
A parametric Oka principle for
A
-immersions
was proved in
[
35
, Theorem 5.3]
.
Algebraic
A
-immersions from affine Riemann surfaces
were studied in
[
12
]
under the assumption
that
A
is algebraically elliptic in the sense of Gromov

[
46
]
(see also
[
33
, Definition 5.6.13]
).
Several cones arising in geometric applications,
in particular the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
(
10.3
),
are algebraically elliptic.
More recently, Alarcón et al.
[
7
]

obtained h-principles for algebraic immersions directed by cones
which are flexible in the sense of Arzhantsev et al.
[
13
]
.
Minor variations of these results for the null cone (
10.3
)
yield similar results for conformal minimal immersions
of open Riemann surfaces in Euclidean spaces;
see the monograph
[
11
]
.

The main advantage of the techniques in the mentioned papers,
when compared to the previously known results, is that they
allow a complete control of the conformal structure of the
resulting directed curves or minimal surfaces.
By using the approximation results developed in the present paper,
one can go substantially further and construct families of such objects
with a control of the conformal structure of every member of
the family, which may depend continuously or smoothly on
a parameter. We now present a few specific results in this direction,
which are only the tip of an iceberg of possibilities.

In the following,
X
is a connected, smooth, open oriented surface,

\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
is a family of complex structures on
X

as in Theorem
1.4
, and
\{\theta_{b}\}_{b\in B}

is a family of nowhere vanishing
J_{b}
-holomorphic 1-forms
on
X
, furnished by Theorem
8.1
.
Recall that a continuous map
f:B\times X\to Y
is said to be

f(b,\cdotp):X\to Y
is
J_{b}
-holomorphic for every
b\in B
.
The first two items in the following definition
come from
[

Definition 10.1
.

Let

be a punctured complex cone of the form (
10.1
).

(i)

A holomorphic map

is nondegenerate if
the tangent spaces
T_{f(x)}A\subset T_{f(x)}\mathbb{C}^{n}\cong\mathbb{C}^{n}

over all points

span
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

(ii)

A holomorphic
A
-immersion
h:X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
is
nondegenerate if the map
f=dh/\theta:X\to A
is such,
where
\theta
is any nowhere vanishing holomorphic 1-form on

(iii)

An
X
-holomorphic map

f_{b}=f(b,\cdotp):X\to A
is nondegenerate
for every

(iv)

A map

is an

is a
J_{b}
-holomorphic

-immersion
for every

, and is nondegenerate if
dh_{b}/\theta_{b}:X\to A

is such for every

.

If

is disconnected, the maps

and

.

By
[
11
, Lemma 3.1.1]
,
an immersion

directed by the null cone (

)
is nondegenerate if and only if it is nonflat, meaning that
its image

is not contained in an affine complex line of

.
Equivalently, the range of the map

is not contained in a ray of
\mathbf{A}
.

We shall prove the following h-principle for
families of directed holomorphic immersions from a family of
open Riemann surfaces. Compare with the h-principles
for maps a single open Riemann surface in

[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
and

[

, Theorem 5.3]
.

Theorem 10.2
.

Assume that

is a smooth Oka cone (

) which is
not contained in any hyperplane,

is a smooth open surface,

is a manifold of class

\mathscr{C}^{l,(k,\alpha)}

.

such that the map

defined by

for all

.

One can also add approximation and interpolation
conditions as in Theorem

and Remark

.

By taking

corollary to Theorem

]

to families of complex structures on a smooth open surface.

Corollary 10.3
.

Given a smooth open surface

If

-holomorphic,

.

Given a smooth open oriented surface

of complex structures on

Proof of Theorem

.

We shall adapt the proof of the parametric h-principle for directed
holomorphic immersions from an open Riemann surface in

[
35
, Section 5]
.
For the nonparametric case, see

[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
and

[

For simplicity of exposition, we shall assume that the parameter space

of Theorem

By Theorem

we can deform

to an

.
The following lemma shows that we can choose

to be nondegenerate in the sense of Definition

Lemma 10.5
.

(Assumptions as in Theorem
10.2
.) Every

-holomorphic
map

of class

Proof.

Since the cone

is not contained in any hyperplane of

there is an integer

(We consider the tangent space

to a nondegenerate one, it suffices to push a set of

[

For a family of maps from a single
Riemann surface, see
[
35

We adapt the argument in the latter proof to our situation.

Recall that if
V
is a holomorphic vector field on a domain

If

, then the map

is defined in a neighbourhood of

in

, it is holomorphic in

,

-holomorphic in

for a fixed

and
\zeta
,
and of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
for every fixed

.

Consider first the case when

is flexible, in the sense that
there exist finitely many

-complete holomorphic vector fields

tangent to

which span the

at every point.
(This holds in particular if

is the null quadric

,
cf.
[

.
A partial list of references to examples of flexible varieties
can be found in
[

, p. 394]

preceding Example 6.3 ibid.)
Consider a map

\Psi(\zeta,b,x)=\phi^{1}_{\zeta_{1}h_{1}(b,x)}\circ\cdots\circ\phi^{N}_{\zeta_{N}h_{N}(b,x)}(f(b,x))

where

,

, every

and the functions

A suitable choice of the functions

If

Let

The map

The assumptions imply that

Consider the period map

Since the map

in a ball

. (Recall that a map is called holomorphic

of the said set.) The period domination property means that the map

(10.6)

\mathbb{B}\ni\zeta\longmapsto\mathcal{P}(b,F_{i}(\zeta,b,\cdotp))=\bigg(\oint_{C_{j}}F_{i}(\zeta,b,\cdotp)\theta_{b}\bigg)_{j=1,\ldots,m}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{m}

is submersive at

is surjective for every

chosen of the same form as

and it has the same regularity properties as that map.
We begin by choosing functions

of class

as in (

)
to ensure that the map

is period dominating

[

, proof of Lemma 3.2.1]

for the details.
By the parametric Mergelyan theorem
(see Theorem
1.2
when
l=0

and Theorem
7.7
when
l>0
)
we can approximate the functions
h_{j}
in
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B\times C)

by
X
-holomorphic functions of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B\times K_{i})

depending holomorphically on
\zeta
.
By Lemma
5.4
these approximants are
then of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}(B\times K_{i})
.
If the approximation is close enough then the resulting map

F_{i}
has the stated properties.

For each
b\in B
let
V_{b}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
denote the kernel
of the differential of the period map (
10.6
) at
\zeta=0
.
This is a complex vector subspace of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
with

\dim V_{b}=N-mn
which is of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
in
b\in B
.
Let
W_{b}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
denote the orthogonal complement
of
V_{b}
. Fix a number
0<r<1
. Since
A
is an Oka manifold and
K_{i}
is a
strong deformation retract of
K_{i+1}
, Theorem
7.4
allows us
to approximate
F_{i}
in the
\mathscr{C}^{l}
topology
on
r\mathbb{B}\times B\times K_{i}
by a family of
J_{b}
-holomorphic maps

g(\zeta,b,\cdotp):K_{i+1}\to A

(\zeta\in r\mathbb{B},\ b\in B)

which are holomorphic in
\zeta
and of the same regularity
class as
F_{i}
. If the approximation is sufficiently
close, the implicit function theorem gives
a map
\zeta:B\to\mathbb{B}
of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B)
, close to the
zero map, such that
\zeta(b)\in W_{b}
for all
b\in B
and
the
J_{b}
-holomorphic map

f_{i+1}(b,\cdotp):=g(\zeta(b),b,\cdotp):K_{i+1}\to A

satisfies the period vanishing conditions
\mathcal{P}(b,f_{i+1})=0
(
10.6
)
for every
b\in B
. If the approximations were close enough then
the map
f_{i+1}
is nondegenerate.
To complete the induction step, we choose a number
\epsilon_{i+1}

satisfying condition (e).

Next, we consider the critical case.
Let
x_{i}\in D_{i}
be the unique critical point of
\rho
in
D_{i}
.
Since
\rho
is strongly subharmonic, its Morse index is either

0
or
1
. If the Morse index is
0
, the point

x_{i}
is a local minimum of
\rho
,
and hence a new connected component of the sublevel set
\{\rho\leq t\}

appears when
t
passes the value
\rho(x_{i})
. On this new
component of
K_{i+1}
we can take
f_{i+1}
to be any nondegenerate

X
-holomorphic map to
A
.
On the remaining connected components of
K_{i+1}
we
proceed as in the noncritical case explained above.

If the critical point
x_{i}\in D_{i}
of
\rho
has Morse index
1
,
there is a smooth embedded arc
x_{i}\in E_{i}\subset D_{i}\cup bK_{i}
,
which is transversely attached with both endpoints to
bK_{i}

and is otherwise disjoint from
K_{i}
, such that
S_{i}=K_{i}\cup E_{i}

is a Runge admissible set in
X
(see Definition
7.6
),
and
S_{i}
is a strong deformation retract of
K_{i+1}
.
(See
[
11
, pp. 21–22]
for the details.)
We assume that the Runge admissible set
S_{i}=K_{i}\cup E_{i}

is connected, since on the remaining components of
K_{i}

we are faced with the noncritical case described above.
We extend
f_{i}
from a small open neighbourhood
U_{i}
of

B\times K_{i}
to a map
U_{i}\cup(B\times S_{i})\to A

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l}
such that the extended map is homotopic to
f_{1}

through a homotopy that is fixed on
U_{i}
, and for every
b\in B

the map
f_{i}(b,\cdotp):E_{i}\to A
is nondegenerate
(see Definition
10.1
).
Nondegeneracy of
f_{i}
on
E_{i}
can be ensured
as in the proof of Lemma
10.5
.

If the arc
E_{i}
connects two different connected components
of
K_{i}
then the homology basis of
H_{1}(S_{i},\mathbb{Z})
is the union of
homology bases of these two components, and there is no
further condition on the extended map
f_{i}
on
B\times E_{i}
.
If on the other hand the endpoints of
E_{i}
are
attached to the same connected component of
K_{i}
, then
the arc
E_{i}
closes in
K_{i}
to a Jordan curve
C
, which is
an additional element of the homology basis of
S_{i}
.
In this case, we choose the extension of
f_{i}
to
E_{i}
so that

\int_{C}f_{i}(b,\cdotp)\theta_{b}=0
holds for all
b\in B
.
This can be done by

[
35
, Lemma 3.1 and Claim, p. 26]
.

We can now proceed as in the noncritical case.
Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{m}\}
be a homology basis of
S_{i}

such that
C=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m}C_{j}
is a Runge set
(see
[
11
, Lemma 1.12.10]
).
As in the noncritical case, we find a period
dominating spray
F_{i}:\mathbb{B}\times B\times S_{i}\to A

of the form (
10.4
), where
\mathbb{B}\subset\mathbb{C}^{N}
is a ball, such that

F_{i}(0,\cdotp,\cdotp)=f_{i}
and the map
F_{i}(\cdotp,b,\cdotp)
is
holomorphic in the complex structure
J_{\mathrm{st}}\times J_{b}
on
\mathbb{B}\times K_{i}

for every
b\in B
. By Theorem
7.5

(if
l=0
) or Theorem
7.7
(if

)
we can approximate
f_{i}
in
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B\times S_{i})
by

X
-holomorphic maps
f^{\prime}_{i}:B\times V_{i}\to A

V_{i}

is a neighbourhood of
S_{i}
. Likewise,
we can approximate the
X
-holomorphic functions
h_{j}

in the expression
(
10.4
) in
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B\times S_{i})
by functions
h^{\prime}_{j}

which are
X
-holomorphic on
B\times V_{i}
. Pick a number
0<r<1
.
Inserting these approximants in the expression (
10.4
) for
F_{i}

and shrinking the neighbourhood

around

if necessary

g_{i}:r\mathbb{B}\times B\times V_{i}\to A

approximating
F_{i}
in
\mathscr{C}^{l}(r\mathbb{B}\times B\times S_{i})

g_{i}(\cdotp,b,\cdotp)

J_{\mathrm{st}}\times J_{b}

r\mathbb{B}\times X

The final step is exactly as in the noncritical case,
and we obtain a nondegenerate
X
-holomorphic

in
\mathscr{C}^{l}(B\times K_{i})

The following h-principle for families of conformal minimal immersions
is an immediate corollary to Theorem
10.2

applied with the null quadric
\mathbf{A}
(
10.3
).

Corollary 10.6
.

Let
X
,
\{J_{b}\}_{b\in B}
, and
\{\theta_{b}\}_{b\in B}

be as in Theorem
10.2
.
Given a continuous map
f_{0}:B\times X\to\mathbf{A}

to the punctured null quadric
\mathbf{A}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
(
10.3
)
for some
n\geq 3
, there is a map
u:B\times X\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

of class
\mathscr{C}^{l,k+1}
such that
u_{b}=u(b,\cdotp):(X,J_{b})\to\mathbb{R}^{n}

is a nonflat conformal minimal immersion for every
b\in B
,
and the
X
-holomorphic map
f:B\times X\to\mathbf{A}
,
defined by
f(b,\cdotp)=\partial_{J_{b}}u_{b}/\theta_{b}

for all
b\in B
, is homotopic to
f_{0}
.

This result can also be improved by adding approximation and interpolation.
We invite the reader to supply the precise statement and proof of this
generalization.

By adding various global conditions on the maps in Theorem

and Corollary
10.6

and we do not know whether they can be made in families.
We pose the following problems.

Problem 10.7
.

Let
\{(X,J_{b})\}_{b\in B}
be a family of open Riemann surfaces
as in Theorem
1.4
.

(a)

Is there a continuous or a smooth family of proper

J_{b}
-holomorphic immersions
X\to\mathbb{C}^{2}

and embeddings

(The basic case is classical, see
[
33

.)

(b)

Assuming that

10.1
),

family of proper
J_{b}
-holomorphic

-immersions or
A
-embeddings

X\to\mathbb{C}^{n}

[
8
]
.
Without the properness or embeddednes condition, the affirmative

10.2
.)

(c)

Is there a continuous or a smooth

?

[
11

[

]

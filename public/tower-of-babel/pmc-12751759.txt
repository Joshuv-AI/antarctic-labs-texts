Profile and quality of life of the older Moroccan population: a scoping review of associated factors
Authors: Raja Arif, Jawad Bouzid, Hinde Hami, Mohamed Chahboune, FatimaZahra Laamiri, Ghizlane Dhimen, Noureddine Elkhoudri, Igor Cigarroa, Fatine Hadrya
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMorocco’s rapidly growing older adult population poses major care challenges. This demographic shift requires a proactive response from the government and health authorities. They should adopt strategies focused on chronic disease prevention, co-morbidity, and the management of geriatric syndromes. This study describes this population’s socio-demographic, economic and health profile. The study aims to identify factors associated with their quality of life through a scoping review.MethodsThe study is based on the PRISMA-ScR guidelines. The databases used to identify relevant articles (n = 24) published (in any language) until 1st September 2025 were Scopus, Web of Science, ScienceDirect and MEDLINE. Two investigators independently selected studies, extracted and analyzed data from studies involving Moroccan people aged 60 and over, focusing on their socio-demographic profile and the various dimensions of their quality of life.ResultsThe analysis highlights significant issues among the older adult. First, illiteracy increases this population’s vulnerability by limiting access to information and health services, complicating their care. Second, chronic diseases severely affect their quality of life. They alter their physical capacity and lead to a loss of autonomy and dependence. Third, depression worsens health and contributes to a lasting sense of malaise. Finally, sexual function, although often neglected, remains a key factor associated quality of life, self-esteem and interpersonal relationships.ConclusionsThe management of comorbidities complicates medical care, exacerbates existing health conditions, and negatively associated with the quality of life of older adults. These findings underscore the need for a multidisciplinary approach to improve both healthcare services and overall well-being in this population.Keywords: Socio-demographic, Health status, Quality of life, Depression, Comprehensive geriatric assessment, Chronic diseases, Older adult, Morocco

Background

The aging of the global population is accelerating, with major consequences for the economy, health, society, and public policies. In 2021, there were 761 million people aged 65 and over, which is expected to exceed 1.6 billion by 2050 [1]. This phenomenon, driven by improvements in sociocultural conditions and healthcare [2], is explained by better management of non-communicable diseases [3] observed mainly in individuals aged 85 and older. Morocco is experiencing a demographic transition with a rapid increase in its older population. In 2022, nearly 4.3 million Moroccans were aged 60 or older, representing 11.7% of the population. This figure is expected to reach 15.4% by 2030, this shift is changing the country’s health profile, with a decline in infectious diseases and an increase in chronic diseases [4].

Old age often brings complex health issues, termed geriatric syndromes [5] stemming from multiple factors rather than specific diseases. “These conditions include frailty, gait disorders, falls, cognitive and psychological disorders, as well as incontinence”. These syndromes significantly impact the older individual’s quality of life. Frailty, in particular, increases the risk of dependence and medical complications [1, 6]. The rapid aging of Morocco’s population has led the government and health authorities to develop targeted care strategies for this vulnerable group. Older people care in Morocco currently suffers from significant gaps, including a shortage of specialized hospital services and inadequate training for geriatric healthcare professionals. This leads to inadequate and insufficient care to meet the specific needs of seniors. Furthermore, lack of dedicated care pathways leads to diagnostic delays, inefficient treatment management, and a decline in the quality of life for the older adults. Moroccan studies have examined the socio-demographic characteristics and living conditions of older adults. Analysis of these works enhances our understanding of the multidimensional aspects of their quality of life, thereby providing insights into their needs and the challenges associated with their overall well-being.

In response to the growing needs of its aging population, the Moroccan government has recently unveiled the National Strategy for Older adults’ Health (2024–2030). This comprehensive initiative is designed to promote optimal health and well-being among older adults, while simultaneously enhancing the accessibility and quality of healthcare services available to this demographic. Among the planned actions is the promotion of integrating geriatric assessment into specialized medical consultations. In low- and middle-income countries, the geriatric assessment tool should be simple, cost-effective, and easy to use in environments where medical resources are limited and illiteracy rates are high. Additionally, a good understanding of the local context is essential for ensuring the tool’s relevance, effectiveness, and acceptability. This article aims to conduct a scoping review of Moroccan studies to outline the socio-demographic, economic, and health characteristics of the older Moroccan population. Moreover, it seeks to examine areas related to their quality of life to support adopting and adapting a standardized geriatric assessment tool.

Methods

This scoping review analyses Moroccan studies to explore the socio-demographic, economic, and health profiles of older Moroccan adults and assess their quality of life. The goal is to inform the adoption and adaptation of a standardized geriatric assessment tool. The research question was: What are the sociodemographic, economic, and health characteristics of the older Moroccan population, and which domains are affected by their quality of life?

Eligibility criteria

The review included articles that (1) involve Moroccan older adults aged 60 and over, (2) assessed their health status regardless of the presence of illness (3) analyze their demographic and socio-economic characteristics and (4) describe their quality of life. The studies were selected without language restrictions. All types of primary and secondary studies were eligible.

Study design

This scoping review was conducted following the PRISMA-ScR guidelines (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews) [7]. It followed the five stages proposed by Arksey and O’Malley [8]: identifying the research question, searching for relevant studies, selecting studies, presenting the data, and synthesizing and reporting the results.

Search strategy

A comprehensive search was conducted using the MEDLINE, Scopus, Web of Science, and ScienceDirect databases, from inception until 1 st September 2025. The following search algorithm was applied: (“Morbidity” OR “chronic diseases”) AND (“elderly” OR “older people” OR “older adults”) AND “quality of life” AND “Morocco”. The search involved a keyword-based review of the available literature from these databases, and the reference lists of all included articles were manually examined to identify additional eligible studies.

Selection of sources of evidence

We used the Zotero software to manage the bibliography and remove duplicates. A total of 694 published articles were identified. A review of titles and abstracts, after removing duplicates, resulted in 24 relevant studies. After evaluating the articles based on full text, 24 were retained for final analysis. The PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1) outlines the selection procedure followed. For articles for which the full text was not available, the corresponding authors were contacted in order to obtain additional data [9–15].

Flow diagram according to PRISMA guidelines (2020)

Data extraction and analysis

A standardized data extraction form was employed to analyze the selected studies. This form was pre-tested by both investigators to ensure consistency and reliability throughout the data collection process. Data from 24 eligible articles were extracted and organized into tables, detailing characteristics of the Moroccan older adult population such as education level, income, marital status, comorbidities, occupational activity, and health insurance. The data also encompassed quality-of-life domains, including physical, cognitive, psychological, social, nutritional, and sexual functions. These tables facilitated analysis and interpretation by summarizing each study’s key aspects, authors, publication year, country, methodology, study population and main findings. A narrative synthesis was then conducted to interpret the results, highlighting similarities and differences across studies.

All searches and data extraction processes were conducted independently by two researchers. Each researcher performed their own database searches and extracted relevant data based on pre-established criteria. At each stage, the results from both researchers were compared and merged through a consensus process. This collaborative approach ensured consistency and rigor in the selection of data, minimizing potential biases and maintaining the integrity of the information included in the review.

Results

Eligible articles are quantitative empirical studies surveying older Moroccan adults. The studies included were published between 2020 and 2025, except for two studies: one by Baali et al., published in 2012 [16], and another by Karimi et al., published in 2013 [17]. The distribution of the Moroccan studies by location is as follows: 19 studies were conducted in the Marrakech-Safi region, two studies were conducted in El Jadida, one study was conducted in Oujda, and two multicenter studies covering several cities. The majority included studies (n = 23) were cross-sectional.

The most studied population was that from the Marrakech-Safi region. All the studies reviewed provided an overview of the socio-demographic and economic profile of the older population (Table 1). Studies consistently demonstrate a link between aging and socioeconomic vulnerability in Morocco. Declining incomes, often insufficient for basic needs, are exacerbated by retirement and a lack of adequate social protection, leading to increased dependence on family networks.

Older women are more susceptible to financial hardship, as evidenced by their higher representation in low-income households [18]. They are, predominantly unmarried, illiterate, and financially reliant on their children [11]. Moreover, the findings indicate that the majority of older people experience multiple chronic diseases. More than half of older adults, mainly women, have at least one chronic condition, with diabetes and hypertension being the most prevalent [10, 11, 14, 17–25].

This population is characterized by a high illiteracy rate, a precarious socioeconomic situation, a significant decline in professional activity, and an increasing prevalence of chronic diseases.

The results of studies included in this review highlight a high prevalence of frailty and pre-frailty among older adults with variations depending on the assessment tools used. The study realized by Bouzaher et al. (2024) [25] reported that 35.2% of patients were frail and 35.8% were pre-frail. Using the same assessment tool (Fried’s phenotype), Majdouli et al. (2024) [11] observed higher rates of frailty (46.4%) and pre-frailty (36.2%). A few years before, the study by Amzerin et al. (2020) [24], using the G8 score, identified a particularly high proportion of frail cancer patients, reaching 83.4%.

Regarding the quality of life of older adults, only 15 studies explored the different dimensions of this topic and identified the most affected areas. Several instruments used to assess the quality of life of older adults, which explains the diversity of the domains reported. Some dimensions, such as physical, cognitive, psychological, social, and sexual functions, took from the LEIPAD questionnaire, specifically designed for the older population. Other studies relied on different tools, such as the EORTC QLQ-C30 (physical, cognitive, psychological, social) which is adapted to Moroccan population [33]. Furthermore, some studies also incorporated geriatric assessment tools such as the G8 score or the global geriatric assessment, making it possible to complete the analysis of quality of life with a multidimensional approach of frailty, nutrition, cognition, and functional dependence. To ensure a coherent and comparable presentation, we grouped the results according to the most recurrent dimensions in the literature, while mentioning the assessment tool used by each study. This approach allows us to reflect the methodological diversity of the available studies while highlighting the main determinants of quality of life in this population.

Physical function is among the different dimensions of quality of life explored in this review [10, 11, 14, 15, 17, 18, 20–25]. The majority of older people have some form of dependency, whether partial or total, affecting their ability to perform activities of daily living.

Cognitive disorders are common among older adults, affecting a substantial proportion of this population [14, 18, 22, 23, 33]. Nearly 52% of older adults have cognitive disorders, with associated factors including age 75 and older, illiteracy, cardiovascular diseases, diabetes, nutritional deficiencies, and daytime sleepiness [22].

Older individuals with cognitive disorders [12, 22, 33] frequently experience psychological distress. Studies report a 35.4% prevalence of depressive symptoms and a 25% incidence of major depressive episodes. A pessimistic view of aging affects 42.9% of this population [12]. Insomnia and anxiety are reported in 26% and 17% of individuals, respectively [33]. Furthermore, 36.2% suffer from sleep disorders, and 16.6% exhibit comorbid anxiety and depression. A low mean mental health score of 36.84 underscores these findings [10].

In addition to cognitive, psychological, and chronic health conditions, sensory disorders -particularly visual and auditory impairments - contribute to the deterioration of quality of life in older adults. These impairments, often exacerbate functional limitation, are common among older adults and represent a major public health concern. Approximately 34.5% of older adults have visual impairment, primarily due to ophthalmological conditions such as uncorrected refractive errors (84.3%), cataracts (77.1%), diabetic retinopathy (11.4%), glaucoma (8.6%), corneal opacity (8.1%), and age-related macular degeneration (6.2%). Several risk factors have been identified, including female gender (OR = 2.18, p = 0.001), hearing impairment (OR = 1.52, p = 0.032), family history of visual impairment (OR = 1.84, p = 0.001), and alcohol consumption (OR = 2.58, p = 0.001) [9, 15]. Cataracts impair the quality of life of older adults, affecting their mental functions and daily autonomy [10]. This effect is associated not only with visual status but also with sociodemographic factors such as age, gender, education level and marital status. Furthermore, hearing loss, particularly prevalent after the age of 70, is often comorbid with cardiovascular, pulmonary, or other sensory disorders, and contributes to the psychological decline observed in older populations [9, 15].

Older adults’ nutritional status is suboptimal, malnutrition affects 38% of this population, while 49.3% are overweight and 24.2% are obese [20]. Obesity disproportionately affects women, with 50% of female patients affected [27].The majority (92.3%) of older people reside with family [23], positively influencing their generally unaffected social function. Indeed, their social function score ranges from 16.67 to 100, with a median of 100 [24]. The average social function score reflects good social integration. It highlights the importance of family support in maintaining their well-being [12, 17, 22].

A Few studies explore Sexual function in older people. Existing research suggests approximately 80% of older adults are sexually inactive, negatively correlated with quality of life [13, 24]. Limited data [13, 14, 23, 24] further indicate low sexual activity in over 75% of this population. Cultural taboos surrounding sexuality in older adults, rarely discussed within families, likely contribute to this finding. Reduced sexual activity has physical, psychological, and relational implications.

Discussion

Population aging represents a global challenge. Aging, far from being a disease, is a natural and inevitable physiological process. Although it is impossible to stop it, premature aging can be prevented, and age-related disability can be reduced [5]. This review analyses 24 studies examining the demographic, socioeconomic, and quality of life characteristics of older adults. Given the larger number of studies focusing on the Marrakech-Safi region, particular attention is paid to the findings concerning this population. It explores various aspects of their daily lives, including physical health, mental well-being, comorbidities, and social relationships. This study identifies the specific needs of Morocco’s adults’ population to inform recommendations for improved healthcare services.

Our results indicate that among the older adults studied, quality of life is generally preserved in the social domain but is affected in physical, cognitive, and psychological dimensions. The prevalence of frailty and pre-frailty is high. Sociodemographic factors, including advanced age, female gender, low educational level, and socioeconomic status, as well as the presence of chronic diseases and sensory impairments, are associated with reduced quality of life and an increased risk of psychological distress. Malnutrition and obesity, particularly among women, also represent significant determinants. Family support emerges as a key factor contributing to the maintenance of well-being and social integration in older adults.

Demographic, socioeconomic, and health characteristics

The majority of older adults exhibit poor socioeconomic status and deteriorating health. Older adult women are often less educated and more disadvantaged socioeconomically [18]. Numerous studies indicate that over half of the older population in Morocco is illiterate [9, 11–14, 16, 18, 22, 24, 26–28]. According to the latest National Survey on Population and Family Health (ENPSF) (2018) [4], the illiteracy rate is 71.6% among individuals aged 60 and older, with a higher prevalence among women, particularly in rural areas. Only 7.5% of older adults have reached secondary or higher education levels in Morocco [4]. Our findings are consistent with those reported in Algeria, where more than half of older adults (55.6%) are illiterate, with a highly significant variation according to sex and age [34].

Most studies show that more than half of the older population is married, with a significant gender disparity [21]. According to the ENPSF data of 2018, 70.6% of older adult are married, with a male predominance, even at very advanced ages. The lower prevalence of marriage among older adult women (47.4%) [4]. may be attributed to sociocultural factors associated with remarriage rates after spousal loss. The high prevalence of widowhood among older women can be explained by age differences between spouses, a longer life expectancy for women, and a low remarriage rate, particularly at advanced ages. This phenomenon, also observed in Algeria [34], reflects sociocultural norms in which female widowhood is considered a normal life stage, and remarriage among older women remains rare compared to men.

Over 60% of older adults are economically inactive [29, 30]. This is particularly prevalent among women, with nearly 75% financially dependent on their families [21]. The ENPSF (2018) revealed that 50.4% of older adults have no income source, reflecting their precarious situation, especially among women. A significant proportion of older adults (74.3%) are unable to work, even though the majority (63%) are under 69 years old [4].

Over 70% of older adults in Morocco have health insurance [11, 13, 23, 28], either through mandatory schemes or RAMED (Medical Assistance Scheme for the Economically Deprived), a program for individuals in precarious situations. The number of insured older adults could increase further due to Law 09–21, which aims to expand social protection in Morocco by extending medical coverage to more citizens and providing financial support, including social allowances and retirement pensions for the older adult [35].

Demographic transition is linked to epidemiological shifts, characterized by increased morbidity and non-communicable diseases, often with multiple comorbidities. In Morocco, over half of older adults experience at least one chronic disease [16, 20, 21, 27, 30–32, 35]. Common conditions include diabetes, hypertension, musculoskeletal disorders, cardiovascular diseases, and gastrointestinal issues. The high prevalence of these two conditions supports the established link between ageing and chronic disease risk [2]. The ENPSF (2018) reported 64.4% of older adults with at least one chronic disease. However, only 56.6% received regular treatment. This prevalence, a nearly 7% increase, rises with age. Women aged 60 and above are more affected (71.1%) than men (55.5%). Urban living is also associated with increased risk [27]. Similar patterns of chronic diseases have been reported in a study conducted in Tunisia [36], with cardiovascular, metabolic, and musculoskeletal disorders particularly hypertension, type 2 diabetes, and gonarthrosis being the most frequent. Compared to our study, the distribution of these conditions appears similar, underscoring common regional trends and the need for preventive and geriatric-focused healthcare strategies.

Chronic conditions significantly affect older adults’ health perceptions and quality of life. This situation highlights the need for healthcare systems adapted to the growing older population. Focusing on the preventing, treating, and managing chronic diseases is crucial. Targeted, individualized care, especially for vulnerable patients, is essential to improve quality of life and promote healthy aging [23].

Quality of life

Quality of life encompasses an individual’s subjective perceptions as well as physical and psychological responses to aspects of daily life. Factors associated with quality of life include personal satisfaction, self-esteem, social comparison, past experiences, financial resources, and physical and emotional health [20]. This complex concept is correlated with physical health, psychological state, degree of independence, social support, interpersonal relationships, and the individual’s relationship with essential elements of their environment [23]. While the overall quality of life among community-dwelling Moroccans over 65 is generally satisfactory [23]. Studies indicate negative correlations with physical, sexual function and sociodemographic factors (gender, age, education, occupation, and morbidity) [11, 18, 22, 24]. Frailty, associated with advanced age, is profoundly associated with the quality of life in older adults, especially those with cancer [24] reported that 83.4% of older cancer patients screened positive for frailty using the G8 tool. This highlights the severity of the issue, as frailty complicates cancer management and compromises the quality of life [24]. Moreover, frailty also persists in older patients undergoing hemodialysis [17], leading to declines in physical and cognitive functions. A study in Essaouira province [11] found that 46.4% of older adults attending primary care facilities are frail. This condition is linked to various factors such as nutritional status, mental health, age, physical disability, presence of comorbidities, financial difficulties, education level, and socio-professional activity [24].

Self-rated health, a key indicator of overall well-being, is reported as good by 68.6% of older adults [10]. However, physical function and the degree of dependency vary according underlying condition. A substantial proportion of older adults undergoing hemodialysis are physically inactive and dependent on external support for daily activities, as reported by Karimi et al.(2013) [17]. The study by El Harsi et al. (2023) [10] demonstrated that cataracts are associated with diminished quality of life in older adults, frequently necessitating assistance with activities of daily living, often provided by the spouse.

Older adults with chronic diseases face functional limitations that reduce their autonomy and increase their dependence [21]. While most older adults with cancer maintain independence in daily living, complete dependency is observed in 8.5% for bathing, 9.8% for dressing, and 16.5% for showering. A substantial majority of patients depend entirely on family members for shopping and cooking, with only 12.3% able to perform these tasks independently, religious practice, considered a good indicator of physical condition, remains an important daily activity for these patients [24].

Age-related structural and functional changes in the brain can increase susceptibility to cognitive disorders. These disorders have a significant correlation with the quality of life of older adults. They limit their autonomy, increase their dependence, and contribute to the decline of their mental health. Cognitive disorders are prevalent among older adults. They are associated with various sociodemographic factors and health determinants. Although one study [22] found a significantly elevated risk of cognitive disorders (sevenfold) in individuals aged 75 and older compared to those aged 50 to 64, this finding is not universally observed. A separate study revealed relatively satisfactory cognitive performance in a substantial proportion (over 70%) of participants aged 65 to 74, as evidenced by positive scores on measures assessing mental capacity, knowledge acquisition, and memory. The predominance of this age group (75.8%) within the sample [22] may explain this finding. These relatively younger older adults may be more resilient to significant cognitive decline, maintaining function into and beyond age 75. Overall, these divergent results suggest that cognitive disorders in older adults are heterogeneous and associated with factors such as age, health status, education, and lifestyle.

In older adults, psychological well-being strongly influences the perception of quality of life, in interaction with physical, social, and functional dimensions.

Ahaji et al. highlighted a high prevalence of depression, anxiety, insomnia, and distress among older men [28]. Depression, while more prevalent in women [21], significantly affects older men as well [27], demonstrating their vulnerability to psychological disorders. In older Moroccan adults, depression contributes to cognitive decline, exacerbating the challenges of aging [22]. Marital status, advanced age, and the presence of chronic diseases increase the risk of depression [24, 26]. These studies underscore the complex interplay of factors associated the mental health of older adults, emphasizing the need for a holistic approach to their care. A study conducted by Jabeur et al. (2022) in institutionalized older adults in Tunisia reported a high prevalence of depression (37%) and cognitive impairment (16.7%), with an overall score indicating reduced quality of life, particularly in the mental domain, depression was significantly associated with medical comorbidities and negatively correlated with physical health scores. Compared to findings of this study, these results highlight similar challenges, emphasizing the strong association with somatic illnesses on both mental health and overall quality of life among older adults’ populations in the Maghreb region.

Malnutrition in older adults is a growing and concerning public health issue, particularly due to the aging population. This condition results from insufficient or imbalanced nutritional intake, with severe consequences on the health of older people. A 2021 study by Benksim et al. [28], using the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) scale, found a high prevalence of malnutrition among older adults, with 14.4% classified as malnourished. The prevalence of malnutrition varies across populations. It is often the result of an imbalanced diet or deficiencies in essential nutrients. The issue may be exacerbated by the frequent failure of caregivers to recognize the specific nutritional needs of older adults [28]. The study also highlighted a high rate of edentulism among participants, a factor contributing to malnutrition by complicating chewing and swallowing. The prevalent tooth loss among older Moroccans can lead to limited food choices, nutritional deficiencies, and a decline in overall health. Factors associated with malnutrition in this population include illiteracy, a negative perception of health status, psychological problems, and polypharmacy [20]. Social support is a multidimensional construct associated with geographic, social, and cultural factors, complicating its assessment. This construct comprises perceived support, an individual’s belief about available help, and received support, the assistance provided [22]. This distinction is essential, as each type of support is assessed using different methods and has distinct association with quality of life, health, and overall well-being. While older adults in Morocco generally report satisfaction with their social relationships [23], a growing number are living alone or in smaller families [26]. Intergenerational cohabitation, a common practice in Moroccan households, plays a crucial role in mitigating isolation and supporting quality of life in later life [27]. It helps preserve physical activity, thus reducing the risk of withdrawal, isolation, and mental and physical disorders related to aging [27]. Nearly 93.8% of older adults live with their families, providing them with essential material and emotional support, partially compensating for the lack of adequate social services [4]. The family provides a protective and supportive environment for older adults. Similarly, in Tunisia the social engagement remains an important contributor to well-being among older adults. older adults generally report a positive perception of their social functioning, remaining satisfied with their social contacts. The loss of professional relationships is often compensated by stronger ties with children, grandchildren, and neighbors, reflecting the continued central and respected role of the elderly within the family and community [37].

Studies conducted in Morocco have demonstrated that the quality of life of older people is closely related to their sexual health [13]. While overall quality of life is considered satisfactory, sexual functioning appears as a key factor in cases of poor quality of life. Age-related physiological changes, especially in women, may explain this observation, along with the disproportionate number of women (over 70%) among single participants. Similarly, a study in Tunisia found that sexual functioning was the domain most strongly associated with reduced quality of life, reflecting the natural decline in sexual desire and activity with age [37].

In our review, it is common for women not to remarry after the death of their spouse. This status affects negatively their sexual health and, consequently, their quality of life. Majdouli et al. (2023) [13] identified several factors associated the sexual quality of life of older adults, including marital status, perceived health, financial difficulties, gender, and lifestyle (cohabitation). Single, unhealthy, and financially strained participants reported lower sexual quality of life [13].Women and those living alone also reported lower scores. These findings emphasize the importance of assessing and addressing sexual health in older adults. Social, economic, and physiological factors must be considered.

The analysis of the characteristics of the older population highlights a range of complex challenges, both socioeconomically and in terms of health. High rates of illiteracy among older adults exacerbate their vulnerability by limiting access to health information and services, thereby complicating care. Chronic diseases that impair physical function and independence substantially diminish quality of life. Additionally, depression places a considerable burden on mental health and overall well-being. Sexual function, although often neglected, contributes significantly to quality of life, correlate with self-esteem and interpersonal relationships; however, it remains a taboo subject in our context and culture, which often limits its consideration in care and clinical discussions.

This review not only provides an overview of the current evidence on older adults but also offers a fundamental evidence base to inform the implementation of the recently launched “National Strategy for the Health of Older Persons (2024–2030).” By identifying gaps and highlighting key determinants, our findings can support policymakers in strengthening and evaluating the success of this national strategy.

this Strategy focuses on promoting healthy aging, reducing inequalities and improving integrated care for the older people. Its objectives include the integration of geriatric assessment into the care pathway for the older people, in order to guarantee comprehensive, early and appropriate care. In this sense, our findings constitute a useful evidence base to support this strategic direction. These results highlight the need to integrate psychosocial and economic dimensions into health programs, which is in line with the priorities of the national strategy, particularly in terms of prevention of dependency, multidimensional care, and monitoring of indicators of well-being in older adults.

It should be noted that 79,2% (19 articles) of the studies included in this review were conducted in the Marrakech region. Therefore, the main conclusions would not be generalizable to the entire country, as regional variations in socio-economic status, healthcare access, and cultural practices could influence the quality of life and well-being of older adults in other areas of Morocco.

Proposed actions

healthy aging requires the development and implementation of comprehensive strategies focused on health promotion and disease prevention. These should include the establishment of specialized geriatric units for the diagnosis and management of age-related conditions, as well as the promotion of standardized geriatric assessments during medical consultations. Regular screening campaigns targeting chronic illnesses such as diabetes, hypertension, and osteoporosis are essential, alongside initiatives that raise awareness about preventive health practices. Expanding home care services including meal delivery, housekeeping assistance, and medical support is vital to address the daily needs of older adults and support aging in place. To combat social isolation, community-based activities, clubs, and social spaces should be created to foster social interaction and engagement. Furthermore, ensuring access to psychological support through mental health professionals and peer support groups is crucial in helping older individuals manage depression, anxiety, and other mental health challenges.

The chapter published in early 2025 on the ‘Comprehensive Geriatric Assessment Model in Morocco’ was presented in the form of a narrative review that laid the initial conceptual foundations for this multidimensional approach [38]. our review extends that reflection by adopting a more rigorous methodology, offering a critical and structured synthesis of the evidence available at the national level. By highlighting the central role of geriatric assessment in the quality of life of older people, it helps to consolidate the scientific foundations of the proposed model and reinforce its relevance to the Moroccan health system.

This review aims to propose a geriatric assessment model to adapt it to the Moroccan context. The included studies have frequently used internationally validated instruments, through translation into Arabic (only), to explore the essential dimensions of aging. Thus, frailty screening tools such as the FREID phenotype [25] and G8 tools specific to older adults with cancer [24] functional assessment is based on the ADL (Activities of Daily Living) [22, 25, 28]and the IADL (Instrumental Activities of Daily Living) [25] allowing to assess autonomy in basic and complex activities of daily living. Nutritional status is generally measured by the Mini Nutritional Assessment (MNA) [20, 28], while the McNair-Kahn [22] scale assesses cognitive functions. Depression screening is often carried out using the Geriatric Depression Scale (GDS) [11, 12, 22, 28]. The integration of these different tools into a structured model would allow for a comprehensive geriatric assessment, adapted to the Moroccan context, and ensure a multidimensional approach covering the functional, cognitive, nutritional, psychological and medical spheres. This model could serve as a reference for diagnosing frailty, guiding interventions, and improving the quality of life of older adults in Morocco. Our future research aims to adapt, validate, and operationalize these instruments in Morocco, with the goal of proposing a standardized model for screening and follow-up that may improve the diagnosis of frailty, guide interventions, and enhance the quality of life of older adults.

Strengths and limitations

This scoping review rigorously identified and compiled studies on older adults in Morocco (n = 24), addressing their socio-economic situation, health, education level, and quality of life. It also highlighted gaps, particularly regarding the quality of life of older adults in certain regions or among specific subgroups, The research on older adults in Morocco was conducted in various regions, including Marrakech-Safi, Casablanca-Settat and Oriental, several studies were also carried out in rural versus urban settings, allowing for comparisons across these contexts. Our study conducted a complete review of all studies involving older adults in Morocco up to September 1 st, including all available research in the scientific literature. This approach provided a global and up-to-date overview of the situation of older adults in the country. Moreover, certain subgroups were specifically studied, such as older adults with cancer, those undergoing hemodialysis, community-dwelling older adults, and those attending primary health care facilities. Highlighting these regions and subgroups helps to contextualize the findings while underscoring the limited geographical and population coverage of the available evidence. This has enabled us to identify the dimensions of quality of life that are most impaired in older adults, such as physical function, psychological health, nutritional status and sexual function, as well as those that are preserved, notably social support. Groups of people suffering from certain diseases, such as cancer or kidney failure, have a significantly impaired quality of life this scoping review presents several limitations that should be considered when interpreting the findings. From a methodological perspective, the lack of a registered review protocol and the absence of a formal quality assessment of the included studies may limit transparency and rigor. However, this limitation is not prohibitive, as the primary aim of a scoping review is to map an emerging or insufficiently structured field. Adherence to established methodological standards (PRISMA-ScR) helps to ensure the credibility of the review, even in the absence of prior registration.

Most available studies relied on cross-sectional designs with small sample sizes, limiting statistical power and precluding causal inference. Few analyses adequately adjusted for confounding factors such as comorbidities or socioeconomic conditions. The widespread reliance on self-reported questionnaires exposes results to reporting bias, while the absence of harmonized or standardized datasets specifically dedicated to ageing in Morocco further restricts comparability and consistency.

Geographically, evidence is concentrated in the Marrakech-Safi region, with fewer studies conducted in Casablanca-Settat and the Oriental region, while many areas of Morocco remain underrepresented. This, combined with the frequent focus on specific subgroups (e.g., older adults with cancer, undergoing hemodialysis, or attending primary care facilities), reduces representativeness and limits the ability to generalize findings nationally.

The scarcity of dedicated funding for geriatric research in Morocco represents a major determinant of these limitations. Insufficient financial resources constrain study design, often resulting in small-scale projects with restricted geographic scope, limited use of standardized measurement tools, and absence of longitudinal or multicenter cohorts. These constraints largely account for the heterogeneity and variable quality of available data, restricting external validity and complicating application of findings to other populations or contexts.

Conclusions

Managing comorbidities in older adults requires a holistic and multidisciplinary approach that considers the complex interplay of physical, mental, and social health. Integrated interventions that promote supportive environments and encourage healthy lifestyle habits such as balanced nutrition and regular physical activity are essential for preventing disease and delaying the onset of dependence. In the Moroccan context, these strategies are particularly important for improving the quality of life of older adults by addressing their context-specific needs.

Taking into account of these conditions including education levels, prevalence of chronic diseases, living conditions, access to healthcare, and social isolation is important for adapting geriatric assessment tools to the real needs of this population. This review underscores the importance of developing culturally and contextually appropriate tools that integrate key dimensions such as frailty, autonomy, mental health, and socioeconomic support. Nonetheless, the lack of studies specifically focused on frailty screening points to a pressing need for further research to better guide care strategies for Morocco’s aging population.

Abstract
Let X X be a compact polyhedral surface (a compact Riemann surface with flat conformal metric 𝔗 \mathfrak{T} having conical singularities). The ζ \zeta -function ζ Δ ​ ( s ) \zeta_{\Delta}(s) of the Friedrichs Laplacian on X X is meromorphic in ℂ {\mathbb{C}} with a single simple pole at s = 1 s=1 . We define reg ⁡ ζ Δ ​ ( 1 ) \operatorname{reg}\zeta_{\Delta}(1) as lim s → 1 ( ζ Δ ​ ( s ) − Area ⁡ ( X , 𝔗 ) 4 ​ π ​ ( s − 1 ) ) \lim\limits_{s\to 1}\bigl(\zeta_{\Delta}(s)-\frac{{\rm Area}(X,\mathfrak{T})}{4\pi(s-1)}\bigr) . We derive an explicit expression for this spectral invariant through the holomorphic invariants of the Riemann surface X X and the (generalized) divisor of the conical points of the metric 𝔗 \mathfrak{T} . We study the asymptotics of reg ⁡ ζ Δ ​ ( 1 ) \operatorname{reg}\zeta_{\Delta}(1) for the polyhedron obtained by sewing two other polyhedra along segments of small length. In addition, we calculate reg ⁡ ζ ⁡ ( 1 ) \operatorname{reg}\zeta(1) for a family of (non-Friedrichs) self-adjoint extensions of the Laplacian on the tetrahedron with all the conical angles equal to π \pi .

Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g
and let
\sum_{k=1}^{M}b_{k}P_{k}
(
P_{k}\in X
,
b_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}
,
{b_{k}>-1}
,
M
is a positive integer) be a (generalized) divisor of degree
\sum_{k=1}^{M}b_{k}=2g-2
. Then (see
[
36
]
)

•

there exists a unique (up to rescaling) conformal metric
\mathfrak{T}
(the Troyanov metric in the sequel) on
X
such that
\mathfrak{T}
is flat
in
X\setminus\{P_{1},\dots,P_{M}\}
and
\mathfrak{T}
has conical points at
P_{k}
of conical angles
\beta_{k}=2\pi(b_{k}+1)
. There exist holomorphic coordinates

near
P_{k}
(so called distinguished local parameters on

\mathfrak{T}=\big|{\rm d}\bigl(x_{k}^{\beta_{k}/2\pi}\bigr)\big|^{2}=(b_{k}+1)^{2}|x_{k}|^{2b_{k}}|{\rm d}x_{k}|^{2}
.

•

there exists a triangulation of
X
consisting of Euclidean triangles such that all the
P_{k}
are among the vertices of the triangles from this triangulation.

Clearly, any polyhedral surface (i.e., a closed orientable compact surface glued from Euclidean triangles) can be provided with natural complex structure and flat conformal conical metric. This results in a pair
(X,\mathfrak{T})
described above.

Let
\dot{X}:=X\setminus\{P_{1},\dots,P_{M}\}
and let
\Delta
be the Friedrichs self-adjoint extension of the symmetric Laplacian
-4\mathfrak{T}^{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}=:\Delta_{0}
on
X
corresponding to a Troyanov metric
\mathfrak{T}
with domain
C^{\infty}_{0}(\dot{X})

[
16
]
)

•

\Delta
has a discrete spectrum consisting of eigenvalues,
\lambda_{k}
, of finite multiplicity,

•

the operator
\zeta

\zeta_{\Delta}(s)=\sum_{\lambda_{k}>0}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{s}}

{\mathbb{C}}
with unique (simple) pole at

\zeta_{\Delta}(s)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(s)}\left\{\frac{A}{4\pi(s-1)}+\left[\frac{1}{12}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\frac{2\pi}{\beta_{k}}-\frac{\beta_{k}}{2\pi}\right\}-1\right]\frac{1}{s}+e(s)\right\},

where
A:={\rm Area}(X,\mathfrak{T})
is the area of the surface
X
in the metric
\mathfrak{T}
and

is an entire function.

In particular,
\zeta_{\Delta}(s)
is regular at
s=0

\det\Delta
, as
\exp{(-\zeta_{\Delta}^{\prime}(0))}
. This quantity was explicitly computed and extensively studied in
[
17
,
18
,
21
]
. The main goal of the present paper is to study another interesting spectral invariant of the polyhedral surface

, namely the regularized value of the operator
\zeta
-function
at
s=1

\operatorname{reg}\zeta_{\Delta}(1)=\lim_{s\to 1}\left(\zeta_{\Delta}(s)-\frac{A}{4\pi(s-1)}\right).

(1.2)

Remark 1.1
.

We choose regularization (
1.2
), following the tradition (in the case of smooth metrics this regularization was previously used in
[
30
,
31
,
35
]

-function itself

It should be noted that for the classical Riemann

\operatorname{reg}\zeta_{R}(1)=\gamma=0.577\dots.

So in a sense we are dealing with an analogue of the Euler–Mascheroni constant for a polyhedral surface.

1.

Explicit expressions for Troyanov metric

In this section, we derive the explicit expressions for the Troyanov metric
\mathfrak{T}
via its divisor.

The case
\boldsymbol{g>1}

Choose a marking on
X
(i.e., a base point
p_{0}
and
2g
loops from

\pi_{1}(X,p_{0})
forming a canonical homotopy basis; these loops can be chosen in such a way that dissecting

\mathcal{D}
; see
[

\mathfrak{T}
, it suffices to construct, for each
{k=1,\dots,M}

\mathcal{P}(\cdot,P_{k})

with a single zero of multiplicity

at

and

multipliers along the basic cycles.
Then the Troyanov metric is given by

\mathfrak{T}=C\prod_{k=1}^{M}|\mathcal{P}(\cdot,P_{k})|^{\frac{2b_{k}}{2g-2}}

(2.1)

with arbitrary positive constant
C

To construct the
\mathcal{P}(z,P)
(

[

) and the vector of Riemann constants corresponding to a base point

In this section, we prove the following statement.

Although we adopt the scheme from

\displaystyle\beta t\mathcal{H}(P,P^{\prime},t|U,z)\exp\left(\frac{r^{2}+r^{\prime 2}}{4t}\right)

Justification of asymptotics: Genus 0 example

In the above calculations, we used asymptotic formulas (

for small

Suppose that
X_{+}=X_{-}=\overline{\mathbb{C}}
and

are standard coordinates on
\mathbb{C}
(the choice of metrics
\mathfrak{T}_{\pm}
is not important but we still assume that
\mathfrak{T}_{\pm}(z_{\pm})=1
near

). Then

and
\xi
is the standard coordinate on
\mathbb{C}
. The prime forms on
X
and
X_{\pm}
are given by

\frac{E(\xi,\xi^{\prime})}{\sqrt{{\rm d}\xi}\sqrt{{\rm d}\xi^{\prime}}}=\frac{\xi-\xi^{\prime}}{\sqrt{{\rm d}\xi}\sqrt{{\rm d}\xi^{\prime}}},\qquad\frac{E_{\pm}(z_{\pm},z^{\prime}_{\pm})}{\sqrt{{\rm d}z_{\pm}}\sqrt{{\rm d}z^{\prime}_{\pm}}}=\frac{z_{\pm}-z^{\prime}_{\pm}}{\sqrt{{\rm d}z_{\pm}}\sqrt{{\rm d}z^{\prime}_{\pm}}}.

If
\xi_{1}
,
\xi_{2}
belong to the same sheet

and

,
z_{2}=z_{\pm}(\xi_{2})
, then formulas (

) imply

\displaystyle\frac{E(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{1}{\rm d}z_{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{E(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{1}{\rm d}z_{2}}\frac{{\rm d}z_{1}}{{\rm d}\xi_{1}}\frac{{\rm d}z_{2}}{{\rm d}\xi_{2}}=\frac{(\xi_{1}-\xi_{2})^{2}\xi_{1}\xi_{2}}{(1-\xi_{1}^{2})^{2}(1-\xi_{2}^{2})^{2}}\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{1}{\rm d}z_{2}}.

In view of the Taylor expansion
\pm\xi=1-\varepsilon/2z_{\pm}+O\bigl(\varepsilon^{2}/|z_{\pm}|^{2}\bigr)
, the latter equality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{E(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{1}{\rm d}z_{2}}=

\displaystyle\frac{(z_{1}-z_{2})^{2}+\cdots}{{\rm d}z{\rm d}z^{\prime}}=\frac{E_{\pm}(z_{1},z_{2})^{2}+\cdots}{{\rm d}z_{1}{\rm d}z_{2}},

where dots denote the terms growing at most as
O\bigl(\varepsilon\bigl(|z_{1}|^{-1}+|z_{2}|^{-1}\bigr)\bigr)
. Similarly, if
\xi_{\pm}\in X^{\prime}_{\pm}
and
z_{\pm}=z_{\pm}(\xi_{\pm})
, then

\displaystyle\frac{E(z_{+},z_{-})^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{+}{\rm d}z_{-}}=-\frac{16z_{+}^{2}z_{-}^{2}(1+\cdots)}{\varepsilon^{2}{\rm d}z_{+}{\rm d}z_{-}}=-\frac{16E(z_{+},0)^{2}E(z_{-},0)^{2}(1+\cdots)}{\varepsilon^{2}{\rm d}z_{+}{\rm d}z_{-}}.

Thus, in the genus zero case, asymptotics (
4.5
) is valid on
X\times X
except the zones
|z_{\pm}(x)|\leq\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}
and
|z_{\pm}(y)|\leq\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}
where
\alpha>0
may be arbitrarily small. In this zone, we have

\displaystyle\frac{E(z_{\pm},z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}})^{2}}{{\rm d}z_{\pm}{\rm d}z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4z_{\pm}z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}}}{\varepsilon^{2}{\rm d}z_{\pm}{\rm d}z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}}}\Bigl(\sqrt{z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}}(z_{\pm}-\varepsilon)}\mp(\pm^{\prime})\sqrt{z_{\pm}(z^{\prime}_{\pm^{\prime}}-\varepsilon)}\Bigr)^{2},

whence

E(z,z^{\prime})^{2}=O\bigl(\varepsilon^{-2\alpha}\bigr),\qquad E(z,z^{\prime})^{-2}=O\bigl(\varepsilon^{2}z^{-2}z^{\prime-2}(z-z^{\prime})^{-2}\bigr),

and
\log|E(z,z^{\prime})|^{2}=-4\pi\Phi(z,z^{\prime})
has only logarithmic growth with respect to

,
z^{\prime}
and
\varepsilon
while the volumes of the zones

and
|z_{\pm}(y)|\leq\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}
are

. Therefore, one can neglect the contribution of these zones in the integration in (

behaves similarly near

\displaystyle W(z_{\pm},z^{\prime}_{\pm})=\frac{1}{(z_{\pm}-z^{\prime}_{\pm})^{2}(z_{\pm}z^{\prime}_{\pm})^{M}}\sum_{k\geq 0}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{z_{\pm}z_{\pm}^{\prime}}\right)^{k}\tilde{W}^{(k)}(z_{\pm},z^{\prime}_{\pm}),

Computing of

It is instructive to consider a simple example of a genus zero polyhedral surface with four conical points of conical angles

Moreover, we also introduce the exotic self-adjoint extensions of the symmetric Laplacian on the tetrahedron that are non-Friedrichs and compute the

Let

Consider the holomorphic involution,
{\dagger}
, of
\mathbb{T}

{\dagger}\colon z\,{\rm mod}\,\Lambda\mapsto-z\,{\rm mod}\,\Lambda
.
Then

is the isometric double cover from
\mathbb{T}
onto
\mathbb{T}/{\dagger}
ramified at
A
and
B=1/2
,

and

(mod
\Lambda

The base
\mathbb{T}/{\dagger}

obtained from its net, the triangle

The tetrahedron
\mathscr{T}
and its double cover
\mathbb{T}
.

Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{T}}
and

be the Laplacians on

and

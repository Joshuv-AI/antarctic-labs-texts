Molecular and histopathological detection of bovine respiratory syncytial virus in Wasit Province, Iraq
Authors: Asaad Chasib Al Atabi, Hutheyfa Al- Salih, Ahmed J. Al-Mialy, Murtadha Abbas
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:The bovine respiratory syncytial virus (BRSV) is an important pathogen affecting goats, leading to respiratory diseases and considerable economic losses. Notwithstanding its known impact, data on BRSV prevalence and histopathological effects in goats from Wasit Province, Iraq, are limited.Aim:This study aimed to investigate the molecular and histopathological features of BRSV in goats, assess infection rates, and classify associated risk factors, for example, age, sex, and season.Methods:A total of 150 lung tissue samples from slaughtered goats in Wasit Province were analyzed using real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction for BRSV detection and histopathological examinations. Viral Ribonucleic Acid was extracted and amplified using specific primers targeting the N gene. Histopathological changes were assessed using Hematoxylin and Eosin and Masson’s trichrome staining. Statistical analysis was performed to evaluate the associations between the infection rates and risk factors.Results:The BRSV infection rate was 33.3%, with significant variations according to age, sex, and season. Goats older than 1 year had a higher infection rate (41.8%) than younger goats. Females exhibited a greater prevalence (23.5%) than males, and winter recorded the highest incidence (55.3%). The gross pathological findings included pulmonary discoloration, consolidation, and hemorrhagic foci. Histopathological analysis revealed severe alveolar destruction, fibrosis, syncytial cell formation, and inflammatory infiltration.Conclusion:BRSV is a main respiratory pathogen in goats, and its infection rates are influenced by demographic and seasonal factors. This study highlights the utility of molecular and histopathological techniques in BRSV diagnosis and underscores the need for targeted control measures. Further research should explore immune responses and vaccine efficacy in goats.

Introduction

Respiratory diseases, particularly respiratory syncytial viruses, are responsible for 17% of deaths in adult small ruminants (sheep and goats) and have a major effect on the spread of disease and are significant respiratory pathogens not only in cattle but also in other large ruminants like camels (Makoschey and Berge 2021) However, it results in significant economic losses owing to death and sickness, particularly among young animals(Terzi et al., 2022).

BRSV infection in goats can cause a variety of clinical signs, ranging from moderate fever and nasal discharge to severe respiratory distress and pneumonia, which are frequently aggravated by secondary bacterial Infections Ceribasi et al. (2014). In addition, viral infection of the respiratory system prompts the release of several chemokines and cytokines, and the virus's distinctive cytopathic impact of producing syncytial giant cells in the lung tissue of infected animals was the fusion of infected epithelial cells to form multinucleated giant cells (syncytia), which is a characteristic feature of paramyxovirus infections like Respiratory Syncytial Virus with interstitial pneumonia and the basis for its name (Ceribasi et al. 2014; Abdelsalam and Al Sadrani, 2015).

Infections caused by BRSV are commonly associated with prominent clinical and pathological changes in the lungs of infected animals. Both natural and experimental infections frequently present with consolidated areas exhibiting gray-red discoloration and atelectasis (Comakli, Sağlam, and Timurkan 2019). Histopathological examination revealed that BRSV can prompt necrotic bronchiolitis, alveolitis, bronchial epithelium hyperplasia, and type II pneumocytes. Other microscopic findings include thickening of the lymphoid hyperplasia, interalveolar septa, and eosinophilic inclusions in the respiratory epithelium. These pathological alterations give rise to significant pulmonary damage, largely determined by inflammation and inflammatory cell infiltration (Yaman et al. 2018; Hägglund et al. 2022). Furthermore, the resulting respiratory function impairment increases susceptibility to secondary bacterial infections. Viral pathogen identification methods, such as Indirect Fluorescent Antibody Test,, can be less effective, sensitive, or specific than molecular techniques. In contrast, real-time polymerase chain reaction (PCR) provides rapid, quantitative results with the least contamination risk, simultaneously detecting several targets without interference (Yang et al. 2022).

The research on BRSV in Iraq has mostly attentive on cattle. However, there is increasing evidence of its occurrence and importance in other ruminant species, including goats. Epidemiological studies showed both in Iraq and worldwide confirm that BRSV is an vital pathogen in goats. The serologically confirmed infection rate of BRSV in goats was 44.5% by means of the Elisa test to conclude the incidence of BRSV in Iraqi goats, and several studies from around the world have well-known serological evidence of BRSV infection in goats and sheep (Al Atabi et al., 2023).

A study in Turkey establishes a BRSV antibody prevalence of 72.9% in sheep and goats, with the prevalence in goats being mainly high in some regions, and a study in Sudan also create a prevalence of 5% in goats (Yeşilbağ and Güngör 2009; Intisar 2019).

This study investigated BRSV-induced histopathological changes in goat lungs and evaluated molecular diagnostic methods. We analyzed 150 suspected cases from the Wasit slaughterhouse using Real time Polymerase Chain Reaction and histopathology to detect BRSV and characterize its pathological effects. These findings will enhance diagnostic accuracy and inform control measures for BRSV in goat populations.

Materials and Methods

Sample collection

From December 2023 to November 2024, 150 goats suspected of BRSV disease were sampled at the Wasit slaughterhouse. Lung tissue samples were collected from the animals, none of whom had been vaccinated against BRSV. Samples were placed in sterile plastic bags, stored in cooling boxes, and transported in special transport media to the microbiology laboratory. These suspected carcass samples were then managed for tissue staining after being fixed in 10% formalin for 48 hours for histopathological examination. Each sample was divided into two parts, one with 10% formalin and the other without, for RNA extraction for RT-qPCR. (Suvarnaet al., 2018).

Molecular detection

Viral RNA extraction and RT-qPCR for BRSV detection

Viral RNA was extracted from 1 g of goat lung tissue samples using the AccuZol™ Total RNA extraction kit. Following homogenization, the samples were treated with chloroform and isopropanol, centrifuged to pellet the RNA, washed with 80% ethanol, air-dried, resuspended in DEPC-treated water, and stored at −80°C. All 150 extracted RNA samples were subjected to RT-qPCR for BRSV detection. A specific primer and probe set (Table 1) targeting the conserved N gene of RSV was used. Each 25 μl reaction contained 10 ng of RNA and GoTaq® Probe qPCR Master Mix (Promega, USA). Reactions were performed in a thermal cycler (Mx3005P Stratagene Real-Time system) using the procedures listed in Table 2. Samples were considered positive if their CT values were 30.

Histopathological examination results

Lung tissue samples were divided into 0.5 cm sections, dehydrated in an automated processor (Histo-Line ATP700), and embedded in paraffin (HESTION TEC2800-C). Sections, 4 µm thick, were cut with a semi-automatic microtome (Histo-Line MRS3500) and mounted on slides via a water bath (FALC BI) and hot plate (K&K HYSH11). Sections were stained with hematoxylin and eosin (routine) and Masson’s trichrome (for fibrous tissue). Tissues were then examined under a light microscope at 40×–1,000× magnification (Bancroft and Gamble 2008).

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences software (v.19; IBM Corp., USA). Differences in BRSV incidence and associated risk factors were evaluated using the chi-square test and Fisher’s exact test.

Ethical approval

This study was conducted in conformity with the rules of the Animal Ethics Committee of the Faculty of Veterinary Medicine at the University of Kufa (license number: 27865 on 13/10/2023).

Results

RT-qPCR results

Amplification plots show varying levels of BRSV RNA (RT-qPCR) in the goat lung tissue samples. The Ct values were compared against a standard curve generated from known concentrations of the BRSV target sequence to quantify viral load (Fig. 1). Among the 150 samples collected from Wasit Province, 50 (33.3%) tested positive for BRSV by RT-qPCR (Table 3), and statistical analysis showed significant differences in infection rates according to age, sex, and season. Specifically: Goats older than 1 year had a considerably greater frequency of infection (41.8%), female goats had a higher infection rate (23.5%) than males, and winter had the highest incidence of BRSV, with 55.3% of infections occurring during this season (Table 4).

Gross pathological results

The affected lung demonstrates dark, firm areas with mottled pink, purple, and reddish-brown discoloration. The tissue is rubbery and less spongy, with some distended or bulging areas. The pleural surface exhibited shiny fibrinous deposits (Fig. 2) and dark red to purple spots. Denser areas may sink in formalin, resulting in alveolar exudate and cellular debris. The parenchyma appeared to be variegated, with spread pinpoint to large hemorrhagic foci (Fig. 3).

Histopathological results

Severe destruction of the furthermost alveoli,inflammatorycell infiltration, and loss of lung architecture result inthe lung tissue being disrupted. Large cells with multiple nuclei (syncytial cells) are observed. There is an increase in the number of pneumocytes (lung cell hyperplasia) and fibrosis. In exact areas, the air spaces are completely gone. Severe fibrosis in thelung parenchyma and significant scarring are observed in the lung functional tissue.

Discussion

RT-qPCR results

Respiratory syncytial virus is an important pathogen that causes respiratory disease in goats. This study creates a 33.3% BRSV infection rate in goats in Wasit province, which differs from previous reports. The rate was higher than the 26.6%–28.5% reported by Yeşilbağ et al. (2011) in Turkish goats (indirect ELISA) but lower than the 44.5% informed by Al Atabi et al. (2023) in Iraqi goats in Al-Najaf province and the 86.2% seroprevalence detected by Yeşilbağ and Güngör (2009) using viral neutralization tests. BRSV infection rates in goats vary widely (3.3%–70%) and are influenced by age, environment, and herd size (Werid et al. 2024), highlighting the impact of regional and methodological differences in seroprevalence studies. Amplification plots showed varying BRSV RNA levels, with lower Ct values (20–25) indicating high viral loads and higher Cts (30–35). Early amplification reflected abundant virus, whereas delayed or absent signals indicated minimal or undetectable levels (Lloyd et al., 2023). The highest BRSV infection rate (41.8%) was observed in goats aged >1 year, aligning with findings by with (González et al. (2025) and Raaperi et al. (2012), who noted increased prevalence with age. The higher infection rate in goats over 1 year old is probably because fewer young animals (under 1 year old) were examined in the slaughterhouse study. Furthermore, older animals face physiological stresses, such as pregnancy and lactation, that decay their immunity. This increased susceptibility could moreover be a result of antibody-dependent enhancement, where existing antibodies fail to neutralize the virus and instead worsen the infection. In addition, the infection rates differed significantly between females (23.5%) and males (15.4%), consistent with the results of Al-Shammari and Sadoon (2022).

The higher prevalence in females may be attributed to pregnancy-associated immune suppression, particularly during late gestation, which increases susceptibility to viral infections such as RSV. Additionally, lactation stress further compromises immunity, potentially elevating the risk of infection in ewes. In the current study, the RSV infection rate was considerably higher in the winter months than in the other months. These results are consistent with those of (Constable et al., 2017; Hussain et al., 2019).

In the winter months, the RSV envelope becomes more stable, permitting the virus to live longer outside the host. This, shared with increased indoor crowding and host immune systems declining due to cold stress, generates ideal conditions for spread.

Examination of the macroscopic and histopathological results

Macroscopic BRSV lesions in goats usually present with pulmonary consolidation, appearing as dark, firm areaswithdark red to purple discoloration, consistent with lobular/lobar pneumonia (Abdelsalam and Al Sadrani 2015). The tissue exhibits inflammatory changes(pink/purple hues),congestion, andrubberytexture, suggesting interstitial edema or emphysema (Taylor et al., 2014; Emikpe et al., 2019). Avariegated parenchyma with hemorrhagic foci further indicates interstitial pneumonia or vascular damage from viral infection (Kumar et al., 2020).

Histopathology revealed severe alveolar destruction, consolidation, and syncytial cells (pathognomonic for RSV pneumonia), indicating direct epithelial damage and respiratory failure (Gershwin 2012; Sitthicharoenchai, Alnajjar, and Ackermann 2020). Inflammatory infiltration reflects immune-mediated immunopathology, while syncytial cells result from RSV fusion proteins (Kamdi et al., 2020). Additionally, fibrosis and scarring suggest chronic inflammation and irreversible lung dysfunction—a potential sequela of severe RSV infection (Yaman et al., 2018). Permanent lung scarring after BRSV infection leads to chronic disease, and the main economic losses include reduced growth, lower milk production, reproductive failures, such as abortion, and long-lasting vulnerability to second bacterial infections. This damage is often permanent, creating unproductive, costly animals.

Conclusion

This study underscores that BRSV is a predominant and economically significant pathogen in Iraqi goats, producing severe, irreversible lung damage, including fibrosis and inflammation. This leads to chronic respiratory disease, reduced productivity, and increased susceptibility to secondary infections. Demographic and seasonal factors significantly influence the infection rates. Routine molecular screening and targeted vaccination are vital for the control and prevention of goat infections in Iraq.

Landau-type theorems for certain bounded poly-analytic and reduced poly-analytic functions
Vasudevarao Allu, Raju Biswas, Rajib Mandal and Hiroshi Yanagihara

Abstract
. The main aim of this paper is to establish several Landau-type theorems for certain bounded poly-analytic functions and reduced poly-analytic functions that generalize some previously established results.

1.
Introduction and Preliminaries

A function
F
defined in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is called a poly-analytic function of order
m
if it is represented in the following form

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}(z),

(1.1)

where all
f_{k}

(k=0,1,\cdots,m-1)
are analytic functions in
\Omega
and
\overline{z}
is the conjugate complex variable to the variable
z=x+iy
(see
[
12
, Chapter 1]
and
[
13
]
). The function
f_{k}
is called the

k
-th analytic component of the poly-analytic function
F
. In other words, a poly-analytic function is a polynomial in
\overline{z}
with coefficients that are analytic functions in
z
.
These functions were initially explored by G. V. Kolossov
[
21
]
in the context of developing a general method for solving problems in plane elasticity.
The significant and productive investigations of G. V. Kolossov and N. I. Muskhelishvili and numerous subsequent contributors regarding the applications of these functions to elasticity are well-known (see
[
32
]
).
A poly-analytic function of order
m=2
is called bianalytic.
The necessary and sufficient condition for a continuous complex-valued function
F(z)
to be poly-analytic of the order
m
in some region
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial^{m}}{\partial\overline{z}^{m}}F(z)=0\quad\text{for all}\quad z\in\Omega.

The class of poly-analytic functions has been the subject of study by various authors from a variety of perspectives. For further information, we refer to
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
33
]
and the references therein.
A function
F
defined in a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is called a reduced poly-analytic function of order
m
if it is represented in the following form

\displaystyle F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|z|^{2k}f_{k}(z),

where all
f_{k}

(k=0,1,\cdots,m-1)
are analytic functions in
\Omega
. Any polynomial in
z
and
|z|^{2}
may serve as an example of a reduced poly-analytic function.
For a complex-valued function
F
in
\Omega
, its Jacobian
J_{F}(z)
is given by
J_{F}(z)=|F_{z}(z)|^{2}-|F_{\overline{z}}(z)|^{2}
. The inverse function theorem and a result of Lewy

[
23
]
demonstrates that a harmonic function
F
is locally univalent in
\Omega
if, and only if, its Jacobian
J_{F}(z)\not=0
in
\Omega
. A harmonic
mapping
F
is locally univalent and sense-preserving in
\Omega
if, and only if,
J_{F}(z)>0
in
\Omega
.
For a continuously differentiable function
F
, let

\displaystyle\Lambda_{F}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}|e^{i\theta}F_{z}+e^{-i\theta}F_{\overline{z}}|=|F_{z}|+|F_{\overline{z}}|\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle\lambda_{F}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq\theta\leq 2\pi}|e^{i\theta}F_{z}+e^{-i\theta}F_{\overline{z}}|=\left||F_{z}|-|F_{\overline{z}}|\right|.

For
r>0
, let
\mathbb{D}_{r}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|<r\}
denote the open disk about the origin with radius
r
. In particular,
\mathbb{D}_{1}:=\mathbb{D}
, the open unit disk in the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
.

A domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is said to be starlike with respect to the origin if, and only if, the line segment joining 0 to every other point
w\in\Omega
lies entirely in
\Omega
.

Definition 1
.

[
31
,
14
]
A continuously differentiable function
f
on
\Omega
is said to be fully starlike in
\Omega
if it is sense-preserving,
f(0)=0
,
f(z)\not=0
in
\Omega\setminus\{0\}
and the curve
f(re^{it})
is starlike with respect to the origin for each
0<r<1
. The last condition is same as

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\arg f(re^{it})=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\text{Im}\left(\log f(re^{it})\right)=\text{Re}\left(\frac{zf_{z}(z)-\overline{z}f_{\overline{z}}(z)}{f(z)}\right)>0

for all
z=re^{it}
and
r\in(0,1)
.

1.1.
The classical Landau Theorem and Bloch Theorem

The classical Landau theorem states that if
f
is an analytic function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
f(0)=0
,
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, and
|f(z)|<M
in
\mathbb{D}
for some
M>1
, then
f
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}
with

\displaystyle r_{0}=\frac{1}{M+\sqrt{M^{2}-1}},

and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}})
contains a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with
R_{0}=Mr_{0}^{2}
. This result is sharp, with the extremal function
f_{0}(z)=Mz(1-Mz)/(M-z)
. The Bloch theorem asserts the
existence of a positive constant number
b
such that if
f
is an analytic function in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
with
f^{\prime}(0)=1
, then
f(\mathbb{D})
contains a schlicht disk of radius
b
,
i.e.
,
a disk of radius
b
which is the univalent image of some region in
\mathbb{D}
. The supremum of all such constants
b
is called the Bloch constant (see
[
16
]
).

In 2000, Chen
et al.

[
16
]
first established the Landau-type theorems for harmonic mappings in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
.

Theorem A
.

[
16
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
f_{\overline{z}}(0)=0
,
f_{z}(0)=1
, and
|f(z)|<M
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}

with
r_{0}=\pi^{2}/(16mM)
and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{0}}
with
R_{0}=r_{0}/2=\pi^{2}/(32mM)
, where
m\approx 6.85
is the minimum of the function
(3-r^{2})/\left(r(1-r^{2})\right)
for
0<r<1
.

Theorem B
.

[
16
]

Let
f
be a harmonic mapping of the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
such that
f(0)=0
,
\lambda_{f}(0)=1
, and
\Lambda_{f}(z)<\Lambda
for
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then,
f
is univalent on a disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}

with
r_{1}=\pi/(4(1+\Lambda))
and
f(\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}})
contains a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{1}}
with
R_{1}=r_{1}/2=\pi/(8(1+\Lambda))
.

It is important to note that Theorems A and B are not sharp.
Better estimates were subsequently provided in
[
18
]
, and it has been the subject of further investigation by numerous researchers (see
[
15
,
17
,
19
,
20
,
25
,
26
,
27
,
35
]
). In particular, Liu and Chen
[
27
]
have established the sharp version of Theorem B for
\Lambda>1
using certain
geometric methods.
The Landau-type theorem for various classes of harmonic mappings has been studied in
[
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
29
,
30
,
34
]
.

In 2022, Abdulhadi and Hajj
[
1
]
were the first to study the Landau-type theorem for poly-analytic functions.

Theorem C
.

[
1
]

Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m

(\geq 2)

in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where
f_{k}
are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, satisfying
f_{k}(0)=0
,
f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=1
and
\left|f_{k}(z)\right|\leq M
,
M>1
for all
k
and
z\in\mathbb{D}
. Then there is a
constant
0<r_{3}<1
so that
F
is univalent in
|z|<r_{3}
. In specific,
r_{3}
satisfies

\displaystyle 1-M\left(\frac{r_{3}(2-r_{3})}{(1-r_{3})^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\frac{r_{3}^{k}(1+k-kr_{3})}{(1-kr_{3})^{2}}\right)=0

and
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{3}})
contains a disk
\mathbb{D}_{R_{3}}
, where

\displaystyle R_{3}=r_{3}-r_{3}^{2}\left(\frac{1-r_{3}^{m-1}}{1-r_{3}}\right)-M\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\frac{r_{3}^{k+2}}{1-r_{3}}.

In consideration of the aforementioned findings, the following questions naturally arise with regard to this study.

Question 1.1
.

Can we improve Theorem C further by removing the condition
f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=1
for all
k
?

Question 1.2
.

Can we establish the Landau-type theorem for a poly-analytic function of the form (
1.1
), where the analytic functions
f_{k}
have multiple zeros at the origin?

Question 1.3
.

Can we establish an analogue of Theorem C for reduced poly-analytic functions?

The purpose of this paper is primarily to provide the affirmative answers to Questions
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
.

2.
Some lemmas

To prove our main results, the following lemmas play a crucial role.

Lemma 2.1
.

[
28
]
Let
H(z)
be an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
H^{\prime}(0)=1
and
|H^{\prime}(z)|<\Lambda
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
and for some
\Lambda>1
.

For all
z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{D}_{r}

(0<r<1,z_{1}\not=z_{2})
, we have

\displaystyle|H(z_{1})-H(z_{2})|=\left|\int_{\gamma}H^{\prime}(z)dz\right|\geq\Lambda\frac{1-\Lambda r}{\Lambda-r}|z_{1}-z_{2}|,

where
\gamma=[z_{1},z_{2}]
denotes the line segment joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
.

For
z^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{D}_{r}

(0<r<1)
with
w^{\prime}=H(z^{\prime})\in H(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r})
and
|w^{\prime}|=\min\{|w|:w\in H(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r})\}
, set
\gamma_{0}=H^{-1}(\Gamma_{0})
and
\Gamma_{0}=[0,w^{\prime}]
denotes the line segment joining the origin and
w^{\prime}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|H(z^{\prime})|\geq\Lambda\int_{0}^{r}\frac{1/\Lambda-t}{1-t/\Lambda}dt=\Lambda^{2}r+(\Lambda^{3}-\Lambda)\log(1-r/\Lambda).

Lemma 2.2
.

[
28
]
Suppose that
f(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
is an analytic function in
\mathbb{D}
satisfying
|f^{\prime}(0)|=1
and
|f(z)|\leq M
in
\mathbb{D}
and for some
M>0
. Then, we have
M\geq 1
and

\displaystyle|a_{n}|\leq M-\frac{1}{M}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 2.

These inequalities are sharp, with the extremal functions
f_{n}(z)
, where

\displaystyle f_{1}(z)=z,f_{n}(z)=Mz\frac{1-Mz^{n-1}}{M-z^{n-1}}=z-\left(M-\frac{1}{M}\right)z^{n}-\sum_{k=3}^{\infty}\frac{M^{2}-1}{M^{k-1}}z^{(n-1)(k-1)+1}

for
n\geq 2
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m

in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where all the
f_{k}
are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
. If
f_{0}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}
and
f_{k}(z)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}

(k\geq 1)
are the Taylor series expansions of
f_{k}
in
\mathbb{D}
, and satisfy the
condition

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|r^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{k,n}|r^{n-1}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}kr^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}|r^{n-1}\right)<1

(2.1)

for some
r\in(0,1)
. Then,
F(z)
is sense-preserving, univalent and fully starlike in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{r}
.

Proof.

Following the proof of Lemma 3.4 of
[
28
]
, we consider

\displaystyle F(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}\right).

Therefore,

\displaystyle F_{z}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nb_{k,n}z^{n-1}\right)\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle F_{\overline{z}}(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}\right).

It is evident that
J_{F}(0)=|F_{z}(0)|^{2}-|F_{\overline{z}}(0)|^{2}=1
. Using inequality (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle|F_{z}(z)|-|F_{\overline{z}}(z)|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|1+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}na_{n}z^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nb_{k,n}z^{n-1}\right)\right|

\displaystyle-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle>

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}|r^{n-1}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{k,n}|r^{n-1}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}kr^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}|r^{n-1}\right)\geq 0.

Therefore,
J_{F}(z)=|F_{z}(z)|^{2}-|F_{\overline{z}}(z)|^{2}>0
for
|z|<r
and hence, we have
F
is sense-preserving in
\mathbb{D}_{r}
. Again, it easy to see that

\displaystyle zF_{z}(z)-\overline{z}F_{\overline{z}}(z)-F(z)=\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)a_{n}z^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}nb_{k,n}z^{n}\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)\overline{z}^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}\right).

(2.2)

Fix
r_{0}\in(0,r]
and consider the circle
C_{r_{0}}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=r_{0}\}
. For
z\in C_{r_{0}}
, it follows from (
2.1
) and (
2
) that

\displaystyle\left|zF_{z}(z)-\overline{z}F_{\overline{z}}(z)-F(z)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(n-1)|a_{n}||z|^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{k,n}||z|^{n}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+1)|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}||z|^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|z|\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}n|a_{n}||z|^{n-1}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n|b_{k,n}||z|^{n-1}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}b_{k,n}|z|^{n-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}||z|^{n}\right)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle|z|-\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}|a_{n}||z|^{n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z|^{k}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|b_{k,n}||z|^{n}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left|f_{0}(z)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)\right|=|F(z)|.

Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left|\frac{zF_{z}(z)-\overline{z}F_{\overline{z}}(z)}{F(z)}-1\right|<1,\quad{\it i.e.,}\quad\text{Re}\left(\frac{zF_{z}(z)-\overline{z}F_{\overline{z}}(z)}{F(z)}\right)>0\quad\text{for}\quad|z|=r_{0}

and hence, we have
F
is univalent on
C_{r_{0}}
and it maps
C_{r_{0}}
onto a starlike curve. By the sense-preserving property and the degree principle,
F
is univalent and fully starlike in
\mathbb{D}_{r}
, since
r_{0}\in(0,r]
is arbitrary. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
Main results

In the following result, we obtain the sharp version of the Landau-type theorem for a certain subclass of bounded poly-analytic functions.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
p_{k},m\in\mathbb{N}
and
M_{k}\geq 0
for
k=1,2,\ldots,m-1
. Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}(z)
be a poly-analytic function of order
m

in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where all the
f_{k}
are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, satisfying
F_{z}(0)-1=f_{0}(0)=0
and
f_{k}(0)=f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=f_{k}^{(p_{k}-1)}(0)=0
for
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\}
. If
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<M_{0}
for some
M_{0}>0

and
\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k})}(z)\right|\leq M_{k}
for
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\}
and for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
M_{0}>1
and
F(z)
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}
and
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}})
contains
a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{1}}
, where
r_{0}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}r}{M_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+p_{k})M_{k}\frac{r^{k+p_{k}-1}}{p_{k}!}=0

and

\displaystyle r_{1}=M_{0}^{2}r_{0}+(M_{0}^{3}-M_{0})\log\left(1-\frac{r_{0}}{M_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\frac{r_{0}^{p_{k}+k}}{p_{k}!}.

The result is sharp, with an extremal function given by

\displaystyle G_{1}(z)=M_{0}^{2}z+(M_{0}^{3}-M_{0})\log\left(1-\frac{z}{M_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\overline{z}^{k}\frac{z^{p_{k}}}{p_{k}!}.

(3.1)

Proof.

First, we prove that
F
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{0}}
. Let
z_{1},z_{2}(\not=z_{1})\in\mathbb{D}_{r}
for
0<r<r_{0}
.
For the line segment
[z_{1},z_{2}]
joining
z_{1}
and
z_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(F_{z}(z)dz+F_{\overline{z}}(z)d\overline{z}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}f_{0}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|-\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}f_{k}(z)d\overline{z}\right)\right|.

Given that
F_{z}(0)=1
, it follows that
f_{0}^{\prime}(0)=1
, and
|f_{0}^{\prime}(z)|<M_{0}
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
implies that
M_{0}>1
. In view of Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}f_{0}^{\prime}(z)dz\right|\geq M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}r}{M_{0}-r}|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3.2)

By the hypothesis, we have that
f_{k}(z)
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
f_{k}(0)=f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=\cdots=f_{k}^{(p_{k}-1)}(0)=0
and
\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k})}(z)\right|\leq M_{k}
for
k\in\{1,2,\ldots,m-1\}
and for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
, therefore

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k}-1)}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,z]}f_{k}^{(p_{k})}(z)dz\right|\leq\int_{[0,z]}\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k})}(z)\right||dz|\leq M_{k}\int_{0}^{1}|z|dt=M_{k}|z|,

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k}-2)}(z)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[0,z]}f_{k}^{(p_{k}-1)}(z)dz\right|\leq\int_{[0,z]}\left|f_{k}^{(p_{k}-1)}(z)\right||dz|\leq M_{k}\int_{0}^{1}|z|^{2}dt=M_{k}\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}.

As a result of the continuation of this process, we finally have

\displaystyle\left|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right|\leq M_{k}\frac{|z|^{p_{k}-1}}{(p_{k}-1)!}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|f_{k}(z)\right|\leq M_{k}\frac{|z|^{p_{k}}}{p_{k}!}\quad\text{for}\quad k=1,2,\ldots,m-1.

(3.3)

Using (
3.3
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)dz+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}f_{k}(z)d\overline{z}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}|\overline{z}|^{k}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)||dz|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}k|\overline{z}|^{k-1}|f_{k}(z)||d\overline{z}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}r^{k}M_{k}\frac{r^{p_{k}-1}}{(p_{k}-1)!}|dz|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}kr^{k-1}M_{k}\frac{r^{p_{k}}}{p_{k}!}|d\overline{z}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|z_{2}-z_{1}|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+p_{k})M_{k}\frac{r^{k+p_{k}-1}}{p_{k}!}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|\geq\left(M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}r}{M_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+p_{k})M_{k}\frac{r^{k+p_{k}-1}}{p_{k}!}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3.4)

Let

\displaystyle G(r)=M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}r}{M_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+p_{k})M_{k}\frac{r^{k+p_{k}-1}}{p_{k}!},\quad r\in[0,1].

Differentiating
G(r)
with respect to
r
, we obtain

\displaystyle G^{\prime}(r)=M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}^{2}}{(M_{0}-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(k+p_{k})(k+p_{k}-1)M_{k}\frac{r^{k+p_{k}-2}}{p_{k}!}<0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,1].

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}|\overline{z}|^{k}\left(k|z|^{k-1}|f_{k}(z)|+|z|^{k}|f_{k}^{\prime}(z)|\right)|dz|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}k|\overline{z}|^{k-1}|z|^{k}|f_{k}(z)||d\overline{z}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(k+1\right)r^{2k}M_{k}|dz|+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}kM_{k}r^{2k}|d\overline{z}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|z_{2}-z_{1}|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\left(2k+1\right)r^{2k}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|\geq\left(M_{0}\frac{1-M_{0}r}{M_{0}-r}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\left(2k+1\right)r^{2k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

(3.11)

Let
G_{5}(r)=M_{0}(1-M_{0}r)/(M_{0}-r)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\left(2k+1\right)r^{2k}
for
r\in[0,1]
.
It is evident that

\displaystyle G_{5}^{\prime}(r)=\frac{M_{0}\left(1-M_{0}^{2}\right)}{(M_{0}-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}2kM_{k}\left(2k+1\right)r^{2k-1}<0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,1],

which shows that
G_{5}(r)
is a monotonically
decreasing function of
r\in[0,1]
with
G_{5}(0)=1
and
G_{5}(1)=-M_{0}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}\left(2k+1\right)<0
. As the function
G_{5}(r)
is
continuous on
[0,1]
, in view of the mean value theorem, there exists a unique
r_{5}\in(0,1)
such that
G_{5}(r_{5})=0
. From (
3.11
), we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|>0\quad\text{for}\quad r<r_{5}.

Thus,
F(z_{1})\not=F(z_{2})
, which shows that
F
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}}
.

Next, we prove that
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}})
contains the schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{6}}
. Note that
F(0)=f_{0}(0)=0
. For
z^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}}
with
w^{\prime}=F(z^{\prime})\in F(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}})
and
|w^{\prime}|=\min\{|w|:w\in F(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}})\}
, by Lemma
2.1
and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle|w^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|F(z^{\prime})|=\left|\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|z^{\prime}|^{2k}f_{k}(z^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle|f_{0}(z^{\prime})|-\left|\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z^{\prime}|^{2k}f_{k}(z^{\prime})\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle M_{0}^{2}r_{5}+(M_{0}^{3}-M_{0})\log\left(1-\frac{r_{5}}{M_{0}}\right)-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}M_{k}r_{5}^{2k+1}=r_{6},

which implies that
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{5}})\supseteq\mathbb{D}_{r_{6}}
.

Using similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
with the reduced poly-analytic function
G_{4}(z)
given by (
3.9
), we can show that the constants
r_{5}
and
r_{6}
are sharp. This completes the proof.
∎

The following result provides an analogue of Theorem C for reduced poly-analytic function.

Theorem 3.5
.

Suppose that
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
M_{k}\geq 0
for
k=0,1,\ldots,m-1
. Let
F(z)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}|z|^{2k}f_{k}(z)
be a reduced poly-analytic function of order
m

in the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, where all the
f_{k}
are holomorphic in
\mathbb{D}
, satisfying
F_{z}(0)-1=f_{k}(0)=0
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
f_{k}^{\prime}(0)=1

(k=1,2,\dots,m-1)
. If
\left|f_{k}(z)\right|\leq M_{k}
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and for
z\in\mathbb{D}
, then
M_{k}\geq 1
for
k\in\{0,1,\ldots,m-1\}
and
F(z)
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}}
and
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}})
contains
a schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{8}}
, where
r_{7}\in(0,1)
is the unique positive root of the equation

\displaystyle 1-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{\left(2k(1-r)+(2-r)\right)r^{2k+1}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(2k+1)r^{2k}=0

and

\displaystyle r_{8}=r_{7}-\frac{r_{7}(r_{7}^{2}-r_{7}^{2m})}{(1-r_{7}^{2})}-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{r_{7}^{2k+2}}{1-r_{7}}.

Proof.

Given that
F_{z}(0)=1
, it follows that
f_{0}^{\prime}(0)=1
. Thus, we have that all
f_{k}
are analytic in
\mathbb{D}
with
f_{k}(0)=f_{k}^{\prime}(0)-1=0
for all
k=0,1,2,\ldots,m-1
. Let

f_{k}(z)=z+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{n}
for all
k
. In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have
M_{k}\geq 1
and

\displaystyle|b_{k,n}|\leq M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\quad\text{for}\quad n\geq 2\;\;\text{and for all $k$}.

(3.12)

First, we prove that
F
is univalent in
\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}}
. Let
z_{1},z_{2}(\not=z_{1})\in\mathbb{D}_{r}
for
0<r<r_{7}
.
Using similar argument as in the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|

(3.13)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}dz\right|-\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(f_{0}^{\prime}(z)-1\right)dz\right|

\displaystyle-\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(kz^{k-1}f_{k}(z)+z^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right)dz+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}z^{k}f_{k}(z)d\overline{z}\right)\right|.

In view of Lemma
2.2
, we obtain the inequality (
3.6
).
Using (
3.12
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\overline{z}^{k}\left(kz^{k-1}f_{k}(z)+z^{k}f_{k}^{\prime}(z)\right)dz+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}k\overline{z}^{k-1}z^{k}f_{k}(z)d\overline{z}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{[z_{1},z_{2}]}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{k}\left((k+1)r^{k}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(k+n)|b_{k,n}|r^{n+k-1}\right)|dz|\right.

\displaystyle\left.+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(kr^{2k}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}k|b_{k,n}|r^{n+2k-1}\right)|d\overline{z}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r^{2k}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}(2k+n)r^{n-1}\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(2k+1)r^{2k}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|z_{1}-z_{2}|\left(\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{\left(2k(1-r)+(2-r)\right)r^{2k+1}}{(1-r)^{2}}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(2k+1)r^{2k}\right).

Using (
3.6
) in (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|

(3.14)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\left(1-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{\left(2k(1-r)+(2-r)\right)r^{2k+1}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(2k+1)r^{2k}\right)|z_{1}-z_{2}|.

Let

\displaystyle G_{6}(r)=1-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{\left(2k(1-r)+(2-r)\right)r^{2k+1}}{(1-r)^{2}}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}(2k+1)r^{2k}

for
r\in[0,1)
.
It is evident that

\displaystyle G_{6}^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{2r^{2k}\left(2k^{2}(1-r)^{2}+k(3-r)(1-r)+1\right)}{(1-r)^{3}}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}2k(2k+1)r^{2k-1}<0\quad\text{for}\quad r\in[0,1),

which shows that
G_{6}(r)
is a monotonically decreasing function of
r\in[0,1)
with
G_{6}(0)=1
and
\lim_{r\to 1^{-}}G_{6}(r)=-\infty
. As the function
G_{6}(r)
is
continuous on
[0,1)
, in view of the mean value theorem, there exists a unique
r_{7}\in(0,1)
such that
G_{6}(r_{7})=0
. From (
3.14
), we have

\displaystyle|F(z_{2})-F(z_{1})|>0\quad\text{for}\quad r<r_{7}.

Thus,
F(z_{1})\not=F(z_{2})
, which shows that
F
is univalent in the disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}}
.

Next, we prove that
F(\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}})
contains the schlicht disk
\mathbb{D}_{r_{8}}
. Note that
F(0)=f_{0}(0)=0
. For any
z^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}}
with
w^{\prime}=F(z^{\prime})\in F(\partial\mathbb{D}_{r_{7}})
, it follows from (
3.12
) that

\displaystyle|w^{\prime}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|F(z^{\prime})|=\left|z^{\prime}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{0,n}z^{\prime n}+\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}|z^{\prime}|^{2k}\left(z^{\prime}+\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}b_{k,n}z^{\prime n}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle r_{7}-\sum_{k=1}^{m-1}r_{7}^{2k+1}-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}r_{7}^{2k}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\left(\sum_{n=2}^{\infty}r_{7}^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle r_{7}-\frac{r_{7}(r_{7}^{2}-r_{7}^{2m})}{(1-r_{7}^{2})}-\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\left(M_{k}-\frac{1}{M_{k}}\right)\frac{r_{7}^{2k+2}}{1-r_{7}}=r_{8},

which implies that

. This completes the proof.
∎

Rare variant of Zinner syndrome with ectopic seminal vesicle and prostatic hypoplasia: A case report
Authors: Thi Tinh Tam Tran, Xuan Bien Doan, Huy Ho, Thi Thanh Thuy Nguyen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Zinner syndrome is a rare congenital malformation of the male genitourinary tract characterized by the classic triad of unilateral renal agenesis, ipsilateral seminal vesicle cyst, and ejaculatory duct obstruction, resulting from abnormal development of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct during embryogenesis. We report a case of a 30-year-old man presenting with ejaculatory dysfunction. Ultrasound and pelvic magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) revealed left renal agenesis, a multilocular retrovesical cystic lesion highly suggestive of an ectopic left seminal vesicle, and prostatic hypoplasia. These findings suggest an unusual spectrum of mesonephric duct developmental anomalies related to Zinner syndrome and highlight the diagnostic value of MRI.

Introduction

Zinner syndrome is an uncommon developmental anomaly of the male genitourinary tract, first described by Aurel Zinner in 1914, defined by the classic triad of unilateral renal agenesis, ipsilateral seminal vesicle cyst, and ejaculatory duct obstruction. More than 200 cases have been reported, with increasing detection in recent years due to advanced imaging techniques and incidental findings [[1], [2], [3]]. Some anatomical variants associated with Zinner syndrome have been reported, including ectopic ureteral insertion into the seminal vesicle; contralateral ureteral duplication; ectopic prostatic tissue and triorchidism; and ectopic seminal vesicle [[4], [5], [6]]. An ectopic seminal vesicle is an uncommon developmental anomaly in which seminal vesicle tissue is located outside its normal position, with only a limited number of cases reported in the literature [7,8].

In this report, we describe an atypical presentation of Zinner syndrome featuring a multilocular cystic lesion highly suggestive of an ectopic seminal vesicle, along with prostatic hypoplasia in a patient presenting with ejaculatory dysfunction.

Case presentation

A 30-year-old married man presented to the Andrology Clinic with ejaculatory difficulty. Physical examination revealed a nonpalpable left vas deferens. Laboratory investigations, including serum luteinizing hormone, follicle-stimulating hormone, complete blood count, and urinalysis, were within normal limits, except for a mildly reduced serum testosterone level of 8.16 nmol/L (reference range: 8.64-29 nmol/L). Semen analysis was not performed due to ejaculatory dysfunction.

Scrotal ultrasound demonstrated normal bilateral testes. Abdominal ultrasound revealed left renal agenesis and a multilobulated anechoic cystic lesion with thin internal septations and no internal vascularity on color Doppler imaging in the left retrovesical region. The left seminal vesicle was not visualized in its expected anatomical location.

Transrectal ultrasound further demonstrated mild dilatation of both ejaculatory ducts. Based on imaging findings, a variant of Zinner syndrome associated with a mesonephric duct anomaly was suggested. The patient subsequently underwent percutaneous epididymal sperm aspiration for fertility management.

Discussion

Zinner syndrome is a congenital disorder caused by abnormal development of the mesonephric (Wolffian) duct during early embryogenesis. The mesonephric duct develops into epididymis, vas deferens, seminal vesicle, and ejaculatory duct, while the ureteric bud arises from its distal portion and interacts with the metanephric blastema to form the kidney and collecting system [9]. Consequently, developmental abnormalities occurring between the 4th and 13th weeks of gestation may cause ipsilateral urogenital anomalies [1]. Most patients remain asymptomatic until sexual maturity, with clinical manifestations most commonly appearing in the second to fourth decades of life. Symptoms are nonspecific and include lower urinary tract symptoms, perineal pain, painful ejaculation, and infertility [10,11].

In this case, the patient presented with ejaculatory difficulty. The clinical finding of a non-palpable left vas deferens and imaging evidence of bilateral ejaculatory duct dilatation suggested an obstructive etiology. Pelvic MRI demonstrated a multilocular retrovesical cystic lesion in the left pelvis. The differential diagnosis includes an ectopic seminal vesicle, an atypical seminal vesicle cyst, and a mesonephric duct cyst. Although direct continuity with the ejaculatory duct was not demonstrated and histopathological confirmation was unavailable, its location, morphology, and MRI signal characteristics, along with associated mesonephric duct anomalies, suggest the presence of an ectopic seminal vesicle.

Even though the classic triad of Zinner syndrome was not present, the combination of findings likely represents a rare mesonephric duct developmental anomaly within the Zinner syndrome spectrum.

Only a few cases of ectopic seminal vesicle have been reported in the literature. Yitik et al. [7] described a young boy with renal agenesis and an ectopic seminal vesicle, while Akkus et al. [8] reported an unusual inguinal mass that proved to be an ectopic seminal vesicle. Unlike these previously reported cases, our patient demonstrated additional prostatic hypoplasia and bilateral ejaculatory duct dilatation, thereby expanding current understanding of mesonephric duct anomalies.

The prostatic hypoplasia observed in this case is notable. Although the prostate originates from the urogenital sinus, its development is also influenced by androgen signaling and mesenchymal-epithelial interactions adjacent to mesonephric duct derivatives [12]. The coexistence of prostatic hypoplasia with mesonephric duct anomalies may reflect a broader disturbance in urogenital development rather than an isolated Wolffian duct defect.

Management of patients with Zinner syndrome and related anomalies is primarily focused on symptom relief and fertility preservation. In this case, the patient underwent percutaneous epididymal sperm aspiration for fertility management [13]. This approach is appropriate for the obstructive cause and allows sperm retrieval for assisted reproductive techniques. Long-term follow-up is recommended to monitor for complications such as infection or cyst enlargement.

Key learning point

In adult patients presenting with ejaculatory dysfunction and unilateral renal agenesis, pelvic MRI should be performed to evaluate the entire mesonephric duct system, including the seminal vesicles, ejaculatory ducts, vas deferens, and prostate, to detect associated anomalies and guide management.

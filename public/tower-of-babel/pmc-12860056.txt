Epidemiological characteristics and spatial clustering analysis of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China, 2014–2022
Authors: Huihui Xiao, Jin Zhao, Jinsong Qiu, Shuilian Chen, Yinzhu Zhou
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT

Background

In the context of accelerating globalization, the prevention and control of infectious diseases have emerged as a critical challenge in public health, especially after COVID-19. As classical infectious disease caused by salmonella, typhoid and paratyphoid fevers still occasionally break out in many regions worldwide, particularly in developing countries, where their incidence and mortality rates remain high [1–3]. As a populous nation, China was once considered a global epicenter of typhoid transmission [4]. Although socioeconomic development and improved sanitation have substantially reduced the national incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in recent decades, the current low endemicity should not be misinterpreted as elimination of the disease threat. The epidemiological pattern has shifted from historically widespread high incidence to a more complex profile-now characterized predominantly by sporadic cases nationwide, yet with continued periodic epidemics and localized outbreaks in specific regions. Southwestern China, including Yunnan, Guizhou, and Guangxi provinces, has consistently reported elevated incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers, forming enduring geographical hotspots for disease transmission [5–8]. Surveillance data from 2010 to 2020 in Yunnan Province reported 35,217 cases of typhoid and paratyphoidcases, with 6 deaths, with an average annual incidence rate of 6.79/100,000 and a case fatality rate of 0.17‰ [9]. This persistent spatial clustering indicates the continued operation of intrinsic transmission drivers and environmental reservoirs within these regions. Furthermore, increasing population mobility raises concerns about potential cross-regional spread and emergence of new infection clusters. Despite these ongoing challenges, comprehensive epidemiological studies on typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China over the past decade remain limited. This study aims to address this knowledge gap by systematically analyzing national surveillance data on typhoid and paratyphoid fevers from 2014 to 2022, to delineate the evolving epidemiological characteristics and spatiotemporal patterns, thereby informing evidence-based prevention and control strategies.

Methods

Data source

Data on typhoid and paratyphoid cases were obtained from the Public Health Science Data Center of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention and the China Health Statistics Yearbook, covering 31 provinces, autonomous regions, and municipalities directly under the central government (hereinafter referred to as provinces), excluding the Hong Kong Special Administrative Region, the Macao Special Administrative Region and Taiwan Province. Population data for the nation and provinces were derived from the statistical yearbooks published by the National Bureau of Statistics.

Definition

Case definitions

Case definitions were based on the Chinese health industry standard WS280-2008 [10]. 1. Clinically diagnosed case: A case presenting with persistent fever of unknown cause, accompanied by characteristic toxic facies (apathy, lethargy), relative bradycardia, rose spots, hepatosplenomegaly, and laboratory findings showing decreased or absent eosinophil counts, normal or low total white blood cell count, or a positive Widal test with anti-O titer ≥ 1:80 and anti-H titer ≥ 1:160. 2. Laboratory-confirmed case: A case presenting with persistent fever of unknown cause, along with laboratory evidence of at least a fourfold increase in specific antibody titer between acute-phase and convalescent serum samples, or isolation of Salmonella Typhi or Paratyphi from any specimen (blood, bone marrow, stool, or bile).

Case reporting

Typhoid and paratyphoid fevers are classified as category B notifiable infectious diseases under the Infectious Disease Prevention and Control Law of the People’s Republic of China. According to the law, clinicians at all levels and types of medical institutions across the country are required to report any diagnosed cases within 24 h via the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Reporting System of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention. Reported cases undergo a three-level verification process (by county/district, city, and provincial CDC) before being aggregated into the final dataset stored in the Public Health Science Data Center.

Statistical analysis

Descriptive analysis

Data organization and descriptive epidemiological analysis of typhoid and paratyphoid from 2014 to 202 were performed using R software (version 4.4.1) and WPS Office 2022. Key analysis indicators included the incidence rate, average annual incidence rate, composition ratio and seasonal index, etc. Seasonal index was calculated as the average number of cases in a given month divided by the average monthly number of cases during the study period; if the value is close to 1, indicating no significant seasonality. Seasonal distribution was visualized with heatmaps, which were generated based on the proportion of monthly cases relative to the total annual cases for each year from 2014 to 2022.

Temporal trend analysis

The Joinpoint Regression Program (version 5.3.0) from the National Cancer Institute was used to perform multi-phase regression analysis on the temporal trends of incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid, employing a log-linear regression model. The annual percentage change (APC) index was used to quantify the trend within each independent segment of the regression function or the overall trend when the number of joinpoints was set to zero. If APC < 0, it indicates a decreasing trend in incidence over time; if APC > 0, it indicates an increasing trend over time [11].

Spatial autocorrelation analysis

Spatial analysis and mapping were performed using ArcGIS software (version 10.2; Esri). Global spatial autocorrelation was applied to assess the spatial distribution characteristics of diseases at the overall level, and to assess the presence of spatial clustering [12]. Moran’s I index was used for global spatial autocorrelation analysis to explore the spatial clustering of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers incidence in China, which ranges from −1 to 1, with positive values showing a positive correlation, negative values showing a negative correlation, and 0 being randomness [13]. Local spatial autocorrelation analysis was performed using Anselin Local Moran’s I index for cluster and outlier analysis. The clustering patterns included four types: “High-High” cluster, “High-Low” outlier, “Low-High” outlier, and “Low-Low” cluster. “High-High” and “Low-Low” clusters indicate that a spatial unit has attributes similar to its neighbors, whereas “High-Low” and “Low-High” outliers indicate that a spatial unit differs from its neighboring units [14].

Results

Overview of typhoid and paratyphoid cases from 2014 to 2022

From 2014 to 2022, a total of 87,296 cases of typhoid and paratyphoid were reported nationwide, resulting in 14 deaths and a case fatality rate of 0.16‰. The average annual incidence rate was 0.69/100,000, with the incidence decreasing from 1.00/100,000 in 2014 to 0.41/100,000 in 2022, representing a decline of 58.72% (Table 1). Of these, there were 67,252 cases of typhoid, with 12 deaths; its incidence fell from 0.76/100,000 in 2014 to 0.33/100,000 in 2022. The Joinpoint regression analysis showed an annual percentage change (APC) of -9.57% (P < 0.001). For paratyphoid, 20,044 cases and 2 deaths were reported, with the incidence rate decreasing from 0.24/100,000 in 2014 to 0.09/100,000 in 2022. The Joinpoint regression analysis showed an APC of -9.54% (P = 0.001). No turning points were observed in the incidence trends of typhoid and paratyphoid from 2020 to 2022 (Fig. 1).

Seasonal distribution of typhoid and paratyphoid cases from 2014 to 2022

Cases of typhoid and paratyphoid were reported throughout the year, with the lowest number of cases in February. The peak period was from May to October, during which typhoid accounted for 61.16% of the total annual cases, and paratyphoid accounted for 67.98%. The seasonal index for typhoid fever was highest in July at 1.37, and for paratyphoid fever, it was highest in August at 1.58 (Fig. 2).

Population distribution of typhoid and paratyphoid cases from 2014 to 2022

In each year, the number of male cases of typhoid and paratyphoid was higher than that of females, with a male-to-female ratio of 1.09:1; the ratio was 1.08:1 for typhoid and 1.12:1 for paratyphoid. Among the fatal cases, there were more females than males, with 9 female deaths and 5 male deaths. Of these, 13 deaths were attributed to typhoid, and one case to paratyphoid. The majority of fatal cases occurred in individuals aged ≥ 60 years, accounting for 50.00%, with no fatal cases reported among those under 15 years. In terms of occupation, farmers and retirees constituted the predominant groups, collectively accounting for 78.57%.

The composition ratio of different age groups for typhoid fever over the years showed that the age group of 0-4 years and ≥ 60 years increased from 12.12% and 15.36% in 2014 to 23.17% and 19.10% in 2022, respectively. The 5-14 years age group remained relatively stable, while other age groups showed a decreasing trend. The composition ratio of cases in the 15-29, 30-49, and 50-59 years age groups decreased from 21.56%, 19.80%, and 17.71% in 2014 to 14.38%, 13.05%, and 14.79% in 2022, respectively. Incidence rates showed that 0-4 years age group had the highest incidence rate, ranging from 1.42/100,000 to 1.87/100,000, other age groups showed a decreasing trend over the years (Fig. 3).

Age distribution of typhoid and paratyphoid fever cases in China from 2014 to 2022

About paratyphoid fever, the composition ratio in the age groups of 0-4 years and ≥ 60 years showed an increasing trend, rising from 13.00% and 11.09% in 2014 to 40.08% and 23.31% in 2022, respectively. Other showed a decreasing trend, with the 5-14, 15-29, 30-34, and 45-59 years age groups decreasing from 10.10%, 27.39%, 22.82%, and 15.60% in 2014 to 7.93%, 5.95%, 9.50%, and 13.22% in 2022, respectively. The incidence rate of different age groups showed that the 0-4 years age group had the highest rate, ranging from 0.55/100,000 to 0.91/100,000, with a fluctuating upward trend; other age groups show a decreasing trend (Fig. 3).

The occupational distribution characteristics of typhoid and paratyphoid fever cases were similar. Among typhoid cases, farmers accounted for 38.64%, followed by scattered children (17.00%), students (13.09%), domestic workers and unemployed individuals (8.64%), and retirees (4.40%). Among paratyphoid cases, farmers accounted for 33.16%, followed by scattered children (25.68%), students (12.33%), domestic workers and unemployed individuals (6.90%), and retirees (5.08%). The composition ratio of scattered children increased from 2014 to 2022, rising from 13.04% to 20.55% for typhoid and from 13.12% to 38.26% for paratyphoid; the composition ratio of farmers showed a decreasing trend, decreasing from 43.80% in 2014 to 31.09% in 2022 for typhoid, and decreasing from 43.50% to 27.77% for paratyphoid during the same period.

Spatial autocorrelation analysis of typhoid and paratyphoid cases from 2014 to 2022

The results of the global spatial autocorrelation analysis showed that the values of Moran’s I index in different years were > 0, and the differences were statistically significant (P < 0.05), indicating that the incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China from 2014 to 2022 was not randomly distributed but exhibited significant positive spatial autocorrelation, reflecting a spatially clustered pattern (Table 2).

From 2014 to 2022, the provinces with higher average annual incidence rates of typhoid fever were Yunnan, Guangxi, Fujian, Hunan, Guizhou, Guangdong, and Shanxi Province, with rates higher than the national average annual incidence rate of 0.53/100,000 (Table 3). Discussion

From 2014 to 2022, the incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China showed a decreasing trend year by year, with the total incidence decreasing from 1.00/100,000 in 2014 to 0.41/100,000 in 2022, consistent with previous studies [9, 15, 16]. The decrease in incidence may be related to the following factors: (1) With the continuous development of China’s economic level, patriotic hygiene campaigns such as water and toilet conversion and environmental management have been vigorously carried out, reducing the pollution of the environment and water sources by feces and the proliferation of vectors such as flies [17, 18]; (2) The country has been carrying out a prevention-oriented policy, continuously promoting the Healthy China Initiative, and implementing comprehensive intervention and prevention measures for infectious and chronic non-communicable diseases, greatly reducing the incidence of infectious diseases [19]; (3) The typhoid Vi vaccine has been proven to be an effective method to reduce the incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers and emergency measures for key populations [17]. Typhoid and paratyphoid fevers are intestinal infectious diseases transmitted by salmonella through water and food, and the high temperatures and abundant rainfall in summer and autumn are conducive to the growth and reproduction of pathogens, leading to an increased chance of infection [20], consistent with earlier findings [5, 21–23]. During the period of COVID-19 from 2020 to 2022, there was no significant fold change in the trend of incidence and epidemiological season, suggesting that the implementation of public health and social measures had a limited impact on the incidence of bacterial intestinal infectious diseases [24], and that a substantial rebound would not be expected even after the end of COVID-19 period [25].

This study found that from 2014 to 2022, the main population affected by typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China shifted toward younger and older age groups. The composition ratio of people aged 0-4 and ≥ 60 years both showed an upward trend, while the composition ratio of young and middle-aged people gradually decreased. The incidence in the 0-4 age group was higher than other age groups and showed an upward trend. That result may be related to the improvement of domestic residents’ health literacy and medical standards, the increased attention to the health problems of infants and children, and the more awareness of medical consultation, which led to more cases being diagnosed. Meanwhile it also may be related to the incomplete development of the immune system in infants and young children, making them more susceptible to pathogens [8]. In addition, infants and young children are more likely to come into contact with contaminated items in daily life, such as food and water, further increasing the risk of infection. However the underlying reasons need to be further investigated and researched. The increasing composition of elderly people about typhoid and paratyphoid cases may be primarily attributed to accelerated population aging in China, with a continuously rising proportion of elderly population and an expanding population base susceptible to disease onset. Data showed that the proportion of people aged ≥ 60 in China had increased from 15.5% in 2014 to 19.8% in 2022 [26, 27]. At the same time, as elderly population, the function of immune system is gradually weakened [28], resulting in their resistance to pathogens and are prone to suffer from a variety of chronic diseases, making them more susceptible to infectious diseases such as typhoid and paratyphoid. Furthermore, our study indicates that fatal cases of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers have been predominantly concentrated in individuals aged ≥ 60 years, reflecting a larger baseline population of older adults affected by the disease over the past decade. In terms of occupation, most fatalities were among farmers and retirees. Farmers are frequently exposed to contaminated water sources (such as irrigation water or livestock environments) during agricultural activities, which increases their risk of infection [29]. Retirees, typically of advanced age, often experience immunosenescence, rendering them more susceptible to severe outcomes. Therefore, it is imperative for China to strengthen the prevention and control of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in the age group of 0-4 and ≥ 60 years. Prior to the seasonal peak in summer and autumn, comprehensive intervention strategies should be enhanced, including improving health literacy, promoting hygienic practices, and reducing exposure to risk factors, so as to effectively reduce mortality associated with these diseases.

Spatial clustering analysis revealed that from 2014 to 2022, typhoid and paratyphoid cases were predominantly concentrated in southwestern regions, such as Yunnan, Guangxi, and Guizhou Province, consistent with previous findings [5, 8, 30–31]. The hot and humid climate in the three provinces is conducive to the survival of bacteria in the external environment. Moreover, abundant rainfall and the unique karst geographical conditions of these provinces can easily lead to water source pollution, thereby increasing the risk of infection [32–33]. Additionally, these areas have a large number of ethnic minorities and diverse eating habits, which may further influence disease transmission patterns. The spatial extent of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers hotspot had gradually expanded to Guangdong Province. A possible explanation is that although Guangdong Province is relatively economically developed, there is an imbalance in development between regions, with some areas having poor sanitary conditions, and being close to high-incidence provinces such as Yunnan and Guangxi, always under the risk of typhoid and paratyphoid outbreaks. The hotspot area of paratyphoid fever had gradually expanded to Hainan Province, potentially due to the frequent occurrence of natural disasters such as typhoons and floods in this region. These disasters often damage water supply systems and compromise food safety, consequently elevating the risk of paratyphoid infection [34]. Moreover, our study did not identify nationwide “Low-Low”, “High-Low”, or “Low-High” clustering areas in the spatial autocorrelation analysis. A plausible explanation lies in the use of provincial-level units as the spatial scale of analysis. This relatively coarse geographic resolution may have masked finer-scale heterogeneities in disease distribution, thereby impeding the detection of continuous low-risk clusters and restricting the mechanistic interpretation of localized aggregation. Consequently, the observed spatial patterns were predominantly shaped by high-incidence provinces, which emerged as the primary focus of the analysis.

Limitations of the study

This study also has limitations. Since the data were collected through passive surveillance from Public Health Science Data Center of the Chinese Center for Disease Control and Prevention and the China Health Statistics Yearbook, there is a possibility of underreporting, which may lead to discrepancies between the study results and the actual disease prevalence. According to the 2015 National Sampling Survey on the Quality of Statutory Infectious Disease Reporting, the national underreporting rate was 4.35% [35]. Similarly, a study in Tianjin from 2013 to 2018 showed an underreporting rate of 2.21% in medical institutions [36]. Nevertheless, the National Notifiable Infectious Disease Reporting System has been in operation for two decades, the overall reporting rate and quality for category B notifiable infectious diseases have remained relatively stable. Thus, the surveillance data from this system can provide a relatively comprehensive understanding of the epidemiological characteristics and trends of relevant infectious diseases.

Conclusion

In summary, despite the overall low and declining incidence of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China from 2014 to 2022, targeted public health efforts remain essential. Particular attention should be paid to the increasing incidence trend among the 0-4 age group, necessitating epidemiological investigations into contributing factors and targeted interventions in the next phase to effectively reduce the disease burden in this vulnerable group. With the accelerated population aging and a growing number of individuals aged 60 and above, it is crucial to enhance the prevention knowledge regarding intestinal infectious diseases, including typhoid and paratyphoid fevers, among the elderly population, while promoting early medical consultation and standardized treatment. Furthermore, Yunnan, Guangxi, and Guizhou Province remain key regions for the prevention and control of typhoid and paratyphoid fevers in China. Relevant provinces should continue to implement targeted control measures based on local epidemic characteristics, including water safety management, food safety risk assessment and early case detection.

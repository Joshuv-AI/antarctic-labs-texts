Challenges in the food supply chain following the great earthquake disaster in Turkey: A study of the regions of Malatya, Adıyaman and Kahramanmaraş
Authors: Hacer Alataş, Nurgül Arslan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The consequences of natural disasters, such as damage to food systems, destruction of transport infrastructure, and organizational issues, can threaten the food security of people. Hence, food security measures are among the most important responses in the management of natural disasters. This study was conducted to identify the challenges in food security response following a great earthquake in Turkey.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This study used a qualitative approach. Within the scope of this study, 37 food cooking and preparation points were examined in the Malatya, Adıyaman, and Kahramanmaraş-Pazarcık regions. Forty-five authorized individuals working at KIZILAY, who were responsible for all aspects of nutrition, were interviewed. Data were collected through semi-structured interviews, with 27 inquiries selected using purposive sampling. Qualitative content analysis was performed to analyze the data. The data analysis process utilized inductive qualitative content analysis to produce themes and categories.RESULTS:Most participants (%80.00) stated that there was no food variety. Menus generally consists of foods with a low nutritional value such as refined grains, legumes, sweets, spices, seasonings, oils, and sugary drinks (particularly soft drinks). The majority of the participants stated that they could not give milk group (%91.11), vegetables (%93.33), fruits (%97.78), and eggs (%100.00). The participants stated that organizational problems (%77.77), transportation problems (%64.44), cold weather conditions (%84.44), and a lack of stock (%64.44) prevent food safety.CONCLUSION:According to the findings of this study, underlying challenges such as geographical and weather conditions, organizational problems, and vulnerability of part of the population threatened food security. The lack of nutritional diversity can cause health problems such as obesity, malnutrition, and anemia, especially in vulnerable people.

Introduction

Due to the devastation of natural disasters on people’s livelihoods, sources of food, food reserves, and food systems, food insecurity is a real possibility in their wake[1] Earthquakes, floods, and droughts are a few examples of natural disasters that can have long-lasting effects. A major risk to food security is posed by the condition of roads, livestock, and agricultural inputs, which threaten people’s ability to earn a living.[2]

When it comes to what it means to guaranteeing that everyone has access to healthy and nutritious food, there is broad agreement.[2] According to the provided definition, a wide variety of responses can be made to ensure food security in the face of natural disasters. These steps consist of planning for adequate relief food supplies, selecting suitable food items, thinking about health and nutrition, providing a variety of food aid options, and employing appropriate strategies that are in line with compensation and livelihood provisions.[12]

Nutritional difficulties that arise after disasters have been the primary focus of previous research in this area. Inoue et al.[3] provided a comprehensive account of gastro-intestinal disturbances seen after the Great East Japan Earthquake in their research. According to Nakazawa and Beppu,[4] the disruption of storage and distribution systems was the biggest issue in the aforementioned incident. According to the research conducted by Chodur et al.,[5] an inefficient distribution system poses a serious threat to the continuation of food security in the wake of a natural disaster. Clay et al.[6] provide evidence to support the claim that addressing food insecurity in vulnerable populations is crucial. Previous research has highlighted the challenges faced by executives and managers in a variety of settings. Nekouie Moghadam et al.[7] stressed the need for humanitarian actors to coordinate their logistics. In addition, Scarpin and Silva[8] argued that Brazil’s bureaucratic disaster management framework was the primary factor endangering its food supply.

Two major earthquakes hit southeastern Turkey on February 6, 2023 at 4:17 a.m and 13:24 p.m local time. Kahramanmaraş-Pazarck was the epicenter of the first quake, whereas Kahramanmaraş-Elbistan was the epicenter of the second. Both earthquakes were recorded as 7.7 (Mw) and 7.6 (Mw) magnitudes, respectively.[9] As a result, a major earthquake struck this area. The earthquakes, also known as the “catastrophe of the century,” caused widespread damage and loss of life across a wide area as well as significant economic repercussions [see Figure 1]. Nearly half of the world’s population has been uprooted from their homes, making up 1.5 million people who are currently homeless. Some families still sleep outside at night, despite not having lost their homes, due to either significant structural damage or fear of potential aftershocks.

In Turkey, the Ministry of the Interior’s Disaster and Emergency Management Presidency (AFAD) is responsible for directing and coordinating integrated management across various stages of disaster management, including policy formulation, planning, coordination, and solidarity.[10]

The purpose of this study was to analyze the measures taken to safeguard food security in the wake of earthquakes in 11 provinces across Turkey. Using the insights of experts in the field, this research aimed to determine what was standing in the way of more effective responses to concerns about food safety.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This study was conducted between February 27 and March 10, 2023. Within the scope of the research, 37 food cooking and preparation points were examined in the Malatya, Adıyaman, and Kahramanmaraş-Pazarcık regions. At these points, authorized individuals who provided food supplies were interviewed.

The participants had considerable experience in food security responses to natural disasters as they included managers and experts working in related organizations. The selection of participants for interviews was based on purposive sampling. No other exclusion criteria were used: Participants were at least 18 years old and worked for organizations that were based in or served Kızılay.

The inclusion criteria were as follows: expertise and experience in food assistance and nutrition responses to natural disasters, willingness to participate in the study and conduct the interviews, and accessibility.

During the process of obtaining consent, participants were provided with information regarding the voluntary nature of their participation and the assurance of confidentiality. The study results would maintain the confidentiality of the participants’ identities. Oral informed consent was obtained from all the participants.

Two informant categories were used to stratify semi-structured in-depth interviews with stakeholders in the “Kızılay food system” (n = 45). These categories were organizations that focused on promoting food access, such as non-profits and governmental agencies (henceforth, food assistance providers), and companies and organizations involved in supplying and distributing food in Kızılay, such as producers, wholesalers, and retailers (henceforth, suppliers/distributors).

During the process of obtaining consent, participants were provided with information regarding the voluntary nature of their participation and the assurance of confidentiality. The study results would maintain the confidentiality of the participants’ identities. Oral informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Data collection tool and technique

In the framework of the study, 27 inquiries were posed to participants in a semi-structured format. We have omitted unresolved inquiries from this study. On average, the duration of each interview was 60 minutes.

Ethical consideration

Necessary permissions for this project have been obtained by TÜBİTAK 1002-C Natural Disasters Focused Field Study Emergency Support Program and AFAD.

Statistical analyzes

Data were analyzed using SPSS @22 and for @Windows Excel 2019 programs. The mean and percentage values of the data were calculated. Descriptive statistics were presented as percentages (%) and numbers (n); continuous variables were expressed as mean.

Inductive qualitative content analysis was employed to generate themes and categories during the data analysis process. This analysis method examines both the nutritional status reported by the participants and the underlying meaning behind their verbal expressions, as interpreted by the researcher.[11]

Discussion

The objective of this study was to enhance comprehension of the challenges encountered by food security interventions in Turkey following catastrophic earthquakes.

The research revealed that the factors influencing the measures taken to guarantee food security have become increasingly intricate owing to various underlying challenges such as geographical and climatic conditions, organizational issues, and the susceptibility of a segment of the population.

The southeastern region of Turkey exhibits a distinctive topography characterized by the rough terrain and a vast expanse of mountainous landscapes. The region experienced notable accumulation of snowfall in February, coinciding with the timing of the earthquake event. Consequently, a significant number of participants attributed challenging topographical conditions (84.44%), transportation disruptions (64.44%), and substantial snowfall to the principal factors contributing to food scarcity. The earthquake presented a significant challenge to food security in the most severely affected areas of Nepal. The significant contribution to this situation can be attributed to the unfortunate demise of livestock and the consequential inability of affected households to successfully perform crop harvesting and grain and seed storage. Furthermore, the exacerbation of challenges in food distribution is compounded by the destruction of infrastructure, such as roads and markets.[12] There is a correlation between the frequency of earthquakes in Tokyo and the sufficiency of food assistance provided to individuals in the aftermath of significant seismic events.[13]

A seismic event that disrupts an established social structure leads to a notable decline in an individual’s quality of life. Individuals who endured the aftermath of a catastrophic seismic event, witnessing the destruction of their dwellings, may encounter difficulties in accessing sustenance within nations that lack sufficient preparedness for earthquakes. This scenario has the potential to result in inadequate food sanitation practices that may subsequently lead to health issues. According to the study, the findings indicate that a significant majority of respondents, specifically 84%, reported a lack of access to sufficient food hygiene facilities. Since the 2010 earthquake, Haiti, a nation already burdened by poverty, has experienced a multitude of health issues including cholera, malnutrition, poisoning, anxiety, and depression.[14] The city water networks incurred damage subsequent to the 2015 Nepal earthquake, which registered a magnitude of 7.8 on the Richter scale. The prevalence of malnutrition is increasing owing to inadequate access to potable water, substandard hygiene practices, and unhygienic residential environments.[15] Research conducted in a region near the equator has established a correlation between inadequate hygiene practices and the prevalence of numerous chronic diseases, particularly among children below the age of five. Furthermore, the incidence of such diseases tends to increase after natural disasters.[16] Given the anticipated challenges in food preservation following an earthquake, it is imperative to prioritize the safeguarding of perishable food items such as dairy products and other highly susceptible foodstuffs. One crucial determinant in the prevention of malnutrition is the consumption of ready-to-eat food items that possess a high energy density.[17]

According to the findings of the study, the majority of individuals (66%) expressed the opinion that individuals who are considered vulnerable have not taken sufficient measures to guarantee food security. Dolatabadi et al.[18] discovered that natural disasters have a disproportionate influence on the most vulnerable segments of Iran’s population. Individuals who can be classified within these groups encompass those who have disabilities, advanced age, female individuals who are the primary earners in their households, individuals who have lost their parents, those who are unemployed, individuals who are experiencing poverty, and those who are socially excluded. Conclusion

Attempts to improve food security face formidable obstacles during crises. Our research showed that there are substantial difficulties associated with choosing food items, such as type, quality, and preservation and storage methods, in the context of response efforts. Significant threats to food security in regions affected by natural disasters stem from a lack of effective coordination and organization among actors and stakeholders, interventions that are primarily focused on individual organizations, the influx of unsolicited donations, and the presence of nutrition and health challenges. Even in the provision of humanitarian aid after a disaster, efforts must be made to increase the consumption of nutritious food items, particularly fruits and vegetables, and to guarantee the inclusion of a wide range of nutrients. The results of this research highlight the need for additional work to strengthen the nation’s food security and develop effective disaster-management frameworks.

A hyperkähler metric on twisted cotangent bundles of the complex projective space
Takashi Hashimoto

Abstract
. We construct a hyperkähler metric on twisted cotangent bundles of the complex projective space ℂ ​ ℙ n {\mathbb{CP}}^{n} explicitly in terms of local coordinates. Note that the twisted cotangent bundles of ℂ ​ ℙ n {\mathbb{CP}}^{n} are holomorphically isomorphic to complex semisimple coadjoint orbits of SL n + 1 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C}) ( [ 5 ] ).

1.
Introduction

It is well-known that complex coadjoint orbits possess a hyperkähler structure (see e.g.
[
8
]

[
10
]
).
In fact, Kronheimer constructed a hyperkähler structure on complex semisimple coadjoint orbits in
[
10
]
via identification of the orbits with moduli spaces of instantons on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
whose quaternionic structure induces the hyperkähler structure thereof.
Then, in
[
1
]
, Biquard and Gauduchon gave an explicit formula for the potential of the hyperkähler metric on the complexification of a compact Hermitian symmetric orbit.

The aim of this paper is to provide a direct construction of a hyperkähler metric on twisted cotangent bundles of the complex projective space
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
in terms of local coordinates.
We note that our twisted cotangent bundles of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
are holomorphically isomorphic to complex semisimple coadjoint orbits of
\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
(see
[
5
]
).

To be more precise, let us consider the coadjoint action of a linear connected complex reductive Lie group
G^{c}
on the dual
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
of its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}:=\operatorname{Lie}{(}G^{c})
.
Then,
G^{c}
-orbits of semisimple elements
\lambda\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
under the coadjoint action, which we denote by
{\mathscr{O}}^{c}_{\lambda}
, are holomorphically isomorphic to twisted cotangent bundles of flag varieties
G^{c}/Q
, which are affine bundles on
G^{c}/Q
, where
Q
is a parabolic subgroup of
G^{c}
whose Levi factor coincides with the isotropy subgroup of
\lambda
(see
[
3
]

[
5
]
).
Furthermore, the isomorphisms are given by the moment maps with respect to the Kirillov-Kostant-Souriau form on
{\mathscr{O}}^{c}_{\lambda}
and the holomorphic symplectic form on the twisted cotangent bundles inherited from the canonical one on the cotangent bundles.
Note that the twisted cotangent bundles are locally isomorphic to the cotangent bundles, and that if
\lambda
tends to
0
then the twisted cotangent bundles reduce to the cotangent bundles.
Now, in this paper, we introduce local coordinates via the holomorphic isomorphism given by the moment map in the case where
G^{c}=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
and
Q
is its maximal subgroup such that
G^{c}/Q
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
,
and construct a hyperkähler metric on the twisted cotangent bundle of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
explicitly in terms of local coordinates (see below for more details about our setting).

Our strategy is as follows.
First, we seek for Ricci-flat metrics since hyperkähler manifolds are Ricci-flat.
Just as Eguchi and Hanson obtained the Eguchi-Hanson metric by solving an ordinary differential equation (ODE) in
[
4
]
(see also
[
2
]
),
we shall reduce the Ricci-flatness condition to a first-order ODE, whose independent variable is a
G
-invariant function described with the holomorphic moment map mentioned above, where
G=\mathrm{SU}(n+1)
, the compact real form of
G^{c}=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
;
we solve the ODE explicitly.
Next, we find a hyperkähler metric among the Ricci-flat ones obtained in the first step.
We will see that there is one and only one hyperkähler metric among them.

The plan of this paper is as follows.
In the rest of this section, we describe our setup in detail, and fix the notations.
In Section 2,
we recall the construction of twisted cotangent bundles of
G^{c}/Q
and the isomorphisms between them and the complex orbits
{\mathscr{O}}^{c}_{\lambda}
given by moment maps.
We remark that the parabolic subgroup
Q
in this paper is maximal, though the construction of the twisted cotangent bundles can be applied to the case where parabolic subgroups are not necessarily maximal.
Then we introduce the
G
-invariant function, which we denote by
\tau
; it is defined to be the norm squared of the holomorphic moment map translated by a constant.
In Section 3,
we find Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on the twisted cotangent bundle.
If we assume that the primitive
1
-form of the associated fundamental
2
-form is described in terms of
\tau
as in (
3.1
) below, then we will see that the Ricci-flatness condition reduces to a first-order ODE (Theorem
3.2
), as mentioned above.
In Section 4,
we shall show that the hyperkählerian condition is equivalent to the fact that the Hermitian matrix associated with the metric belongs to the complex symplectic group (Proposition
4.4
), and we pick a hyperkähler metric out of the Ricci-flat metrics obtained in Section 3, which is our main result (Theorem
4.6
).
Finally, in Appendix, we collect a few formulas from linear algebra, mainly about matrices of rank 2.

1.1.

Now we introduce our setup.

Throughout the paper, we let
G:=\mathrm{SU}(n+1)
and
G^{c}:=\mathrm{SL}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
and denote their Lie algebras by
\mathfrak{g}_{0}:=\mathfrak{su}(n+1)
and
\mathfrak{g}:=\mathfrak{sl}_{n+1}(\mathbb{C})
for brevity.
We often identify the dual space
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
with
\mathfrak{g}
by the trace form
B
:

B(X,Y)=\operatorname{tr}{(XY)}\quad(X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}).

(1.1)

Note that the form
B
is negative definite on
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathfrak{su}(n+1)
.
Let
Q
be a maximal parabolic subgroup of
G^{c}
given by

Q=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in G^{c};a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),c\in\operatorname{Mat}_{1\times n}(\mathbb{C}),d\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right\},

and
\mathfrak{q}
the Lie algebra of
Q
given by

\mathfrak{q}=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g};a\in\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times n}(\mathbb{C}),c\in\operatorname{Mat}_{1\times n}(\mathbb{C}),d\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

Thus
G^{c}/Q
is isomprphic to the comlex projective space

{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}=\big\{[\zeta_{0}:\zeta_{1}:\dots:\zeta_{n}];\hskip 1.0pt\vphantom{{(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})}}^{t}\hskip-1.6pt(\zeta_{0},\zeta_{1},\dots,\zeta_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\backslash\{0\}\big\}

by
g.e_{Q}\leftrightarrow[g.e_{n}]
, where
e_{Q}
is the origin of
G^{c}/Q
and
e_{n}=\hskip 1.0pt\vphantom{{(0,\dots,0,1)}}^{t}\hskip-1.6pt(0,\dots,0,1)
.

Let
\lambda\in\mathfrak{g}^{*}
be a character (i.e. one-dimensional representation) of
\mathfrak{q}
which sends

\left[\begin{smallmatrix}a&0\\
c&d\end{smallmatrix}\right]
to
-sd

for
s\in\mathbb{C}
,
and
\lambda^{\vee}
the corresponding element of
\mathfrak{g}
under the identification
\mathfrak{g}^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{g}
via the bilinear form
B
given by (
1.1
):

\lambda^{\vee}=\frac{s}{n+1}\begin{bmatrix}1_{n}&0\\[2.0pt]
0&-n\end{bmatrix}\in\mathfrak{g}.

The
G^{c}
-orbit
{\mathscr{O}}^{c}_{\lambda}:=G^{c}\cdot\lambda
of
\lambda
under the coadjoint action is canonically isomorphic to
G^{c}/L
, where

L=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}a&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}\in G^{c};a\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathbb{C}),d\in\mathbb{C}^{\times}\right\}

(1.2)

is the Levi factor of
Q
.
Let
\mathfrak{l}
be the Lie algebra of
L
,
\mathfrak{u}^{-}
the nilradical of
\mathfrak{q}
, and
\mathfrak{u}
the opposite of
\mathfrak{u}^{-}
.
Then we have the following decompositions:

\mathfrak{q}=\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{-}\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{u}\oplus\mathfrak{l}\oplus\mathfrak{u}^{-}.

(1.3)

Note that
\mathfrak{u}
and
\mathfrak{u}^{-}
are abelian and isomorphic to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.
Denoting the analytic subgroups of
\mathfrak{u}^{-}
and
\mathfrak{u}
by
U^{-}
and
U
respectively, we see that

Q=LU^{-}=U^{-}L,

(1.4)

and that
G^{c}
has an open covering:

\displaystyle G^{c}

\displaystyle=\bigcup_{\sigma\in W/W_{\lambda}}\sigma UQ,

(1.5)

where
W
is the Weyl group of
G^{c}
and
W_{\lambda}
the isotropy subgroup of
\lambda
in
W
.
We fix a representative
\dot{\sigma}\in G
of each element
\sigma\in W/W_{\lambda}
once and for all, and identify
\sigma
with
\dot{\sigma}
.
In fact, as the representatives, we can take the signed permutation matrices of determinant one that correspond to cyclic permutations
(\alpha,\alpha+1,\dots,n)
,
which we denote by
\dot{\sigma}_{\alpha}
for
\alpha=0,1,\dots,n
.

We parametrize elements of
U
and
U^{-}
as

u_{z}=\begin{bmatrix}1&z\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in U\quad\text{and}\quad u_{w}^{-}=\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
w&1\end{bmatrix}\in U^{-},

(1.6)

where
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}=\operatorname{Mat}_{n\times 1}(\mathbb{C})
(column vector) and
w\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}=\operatorname{Mat}_{1\times n}(\mathbb{C})
(row vector).

2.
Twisted Cotangent Bundle

As we mentioned earlier, the
G^{c}
-orbit
{\mathscr{O}}^{c}_{\lambda}
is holomorphically isomorphic to the twisted cotangent bundle of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
, which is an affine bundle on
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
and is denoted by
(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}
.
In this section, we first recall its construction (see
[
3
]

[
5
]
for details), and then introduce a
G
-invariant function on
(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}
.

Let
\{U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=0,1,\dots,n}
be the standard open covering of
{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
, i.e.

\displaystyle U_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\big\{[\zeta_{0}:\zeta_{1}:\dots:\zeta_{n}]\in{\mathbb{CP}}^{n};\zeta_{\alpha}\neq 0\big\}

and
\varphi_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathbb{C}^{n}
the holomorphic local chart induced from the homeomorphism
U_{\alpha}\simeq\sigma_{\alpha}UQ/Q
.
Introducing holomorphic coordinates
(z_{\alpha},\xi_{\alpha})=(z_{\alpha,1},\dots,z_{\alpha,n},\xi_{\alpha,1},\dots,\xi_{\alpha,n})
on the trivial cotangent bundle
T^{*}U_{\alpha}
,
we denote a point of
T^{*}U_{\alpha}
by

\xi_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\alpha}:=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{\alpha,i}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\alpha,i}.

In our setting, it is natural to regard
z_{\alpha}
as a column vector
\hskip 1.0pt\vphantom{{(z_{\alpha,1},\dots,z_{\alpha,n})}}{}^{t}\hskip-1.6pt(z_{\alpha,1},\dots,z_{\alpha,n})
and
\xi_{\alpha}
as a row vector
(\xi_{\alpha,1},\dots,\xi_{\alpha,n})
.

Now we glue the cotangent bundles
\{T^{*}U_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha=0,1,\dots,n}
on the double intersection
U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}
by an equivalence relation:

(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}:=\bigsqcup_{\alpha=0}^{n}T^{*}U_{\alpha}/\sim,

(2.1)

where a point
\xi_{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\beta}
in
T^{*}U_{\beta}|_{U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}}
is defined to be equivalent to
a point
\xi_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\alpha}
in
T^{*}U_{\alpha}|_{U_{\alpha}\cap U_{\beta}}
if and only if the following two conditions hold:

\displaystyle z_{\beta}

\displaystyle=(\varphi_{\beta}\circ\varphi_{\alpha}^{-1})(z_{\alpha}),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\xi_{\beta}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\xi_{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}z_{\alpha}+\langle\lambda,\mathrm{d}t_{\alpha\beta}{t_{\alpha\beta}}^{-1}\rangle.

(2.3)

Here
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the pairing between
\mathfrak{g}^{*}
and
\mathfrak{g}
, and
t_{\alpha\beta}
is a unique element of
L
determined by the relation

\sigma_{\beta}\,u_{z_{\beta}}u^{-}_{w_{\beta}}.e_{L}=\sigma_{\alpha}\,u_{z_{\alpha}}u^{-}_{w_{\alpha}}.e_{L},

(2.4)

where
e_{L}
denotes the origin of
G^{c}/L
, and
w_{\alpha},\,w_{\beta}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
are related to
\xi_{\alpha},\,\xi_{\beta}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
by
\xi_{\alpha}=-sw_{\alpha},\,\xi_{\beta}=-sw_{\beta}
.
In fact, Eq. (
2.4
) implies a unique existence of
t_{\alpha\beta}\in L
that satisfies

\sigma_{\beta}u_{z_{\beta}}u^{-}_{w_{\beta}}=\sigma_{\alpha}u_{z_{\alpha}}u^{-}_{w_{\alpha}}t_{\alpha\beta}
under the condition (
2.2
).

We denote the canonical projection by
\pi_{\lambda}
:

\pi_{\lambda}:(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}\to{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}.

(2.5)

By definition,
\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})=T^{*}U_{\alpha}
,
\alpha=0,1,\dots,n
.

In what follows, we will denote the twisted cotangent bundle by
\mathit{Z}
for brevity:

\mathit{Z}:=(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}.

(2.6)

Since the second term in the right-hand side of (
2.3
) is exact, taking the exterior derivative of its both sides, one can equip
\mathit{Z}
with a holomorphic symplectic form
\omega_{+}
of type
(2,0)
given in terms of the local coordinates

\omega_{+}|_{\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathrm{d}z_{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}\xi_{i}.

.

If we denote the moment maps

Now we are ready to introduce our main player.
Namely, set

\displaystyle\tau

\displaystyle:=-B(\mu_{+},\mu_{-})+\frac{\left|\,s\,\right|^{2}}{n+1}

\displaystyle=B(\mu_{+},\mu_{+}^{*})+\frac{\left|\,s\,\right|^{2}}{n+1},

\tau|_{\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})}=(\left\|z\right\|^{2}+1)(\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}+\left|\,s-\xi z\,\right|^{2}),

where we regard

(column vector) and
\xi\in(\mathbb{C}^{n})^{*}
(row vector), and

\left\|z\right\|^{2}=z^{*}z,\left\|\xi\right\|^{2}=\xi\xi^{*}
.
Note that we need the constant term in (

as in (

Remark 2.2
.

By definition,
\tau
is a
G
-invariant function on
\mathit{Z}
with values in

; it takes its minimum
\left|\,s\,\right|^{2}
on the submanifold
\mathscr{L}
, where

\mathscr{L}|_{\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})}=\left\{\frac{s\,z^{*}\mathrm{d}z}{1+\left\|z\right\|^{2}};z\in U_{\alpha}\right\}\subset T^{*}U_{\alpha}.

Note that
\mathscr{L}
provides an example of the

of
\mathit{Z}
, i.e. it is Lagrangian with respect to
\im\omega_{+}
and symplectic with respect to

(see
[

3.
Ricci-flat Kähler Metric

Henceforth, we assume that
s\neq 0
.

In this section, we find Ricci-flat Kähler metrics on the twisted cotangent bundle
Z
under an Ansatz.
Namely, for an
\mathbb{R}
-valued function
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in a single variable, let us consider a 1-form
\beta
on
\mathit{Z}
given by

\beta:=\frac{1}{4}f(\tau)\,\mathrm{d}^{c}\tau=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{4}f(\tau)(\overline{\partial}\tau-\partial\tau)

(3.1)

and define a 2-form
\omega_{I}
by
\omega_{I}:=\mathrm{d}\beta
, where
\tau\in C^{\infty}(\mathit{Z})
is the
G
-invariant function given by (
2.13
).
Note then that

\omega_{I}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(f(\tau)\partial\overline{\partial}\tau+f^{\prime}(\tau)\partial\tau\wedge\overline{\partial}\tau)

(3.2)

is of type
(1,1)
, and satisfies

\omega_{I}(Iv,Iw)=\omega_{I}(v,w)\quad(v,w\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathit{Z})),

(3.3)

where
I
denotes the canonical complex structure on
\mathit{Z}
.
Furthermore, one can see that
g^{*}\omega_{I}=\omega_{I}
for all
g\in G
since
\tau
is
G
-invariant.

In what follows, we will be concerned with such functions
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
that satisfy

\omega_{I}(v,Iv)>0

(3.4)

for all nonzero
v\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathit{Z})
.
Let
g
be the metric on
\mathit{Z}
whose fundamental
2
-form is
\omega_{I}
.
Namely,

g(v,w):=\omega_{I}(v,Iw)\quad(v,w\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathit{Z})).

(3.5)

Then
(\mathit{Z},g,I)
is a Kähler manifold if the condition (
3.4
) is satisfied.
In particular,
\omega_{I}
is a symplectic form on
\mathit{Z}
(i.e. non-degenerate on
\mathit{Z}
).

Remark 3.1
.

It is clear from (
2.3
) that if
\lambda
tends to
0
then our twisted cotangent bundle
\mathit{Z}=(T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n})_{\lambda}
reduces to the cotangent bundle
T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{n}
.
Kapustin and Saulina showed in
[
9
]
that Eguchi-Hanson metric on
T^{*}{\mathbb{CP}}^{1}
, which is not only Ricci-flat but also hyperkähler, is obtained if one takes
f(\tau)
in (
3.1
) to be
(1+\tau)^{1/2}/\tau
.
This is the reason we assume the 1-form
\beta
to be of the form given above.

For the sake of space, we sometimes use the following convention about indices.
Namely, for
i=1,2,\dots,n
, we regard
\xi_{i}
as the
(n+i)
-th coordinate:

z_{n+i}:=\xi_{i}.

(3.6)

Under the convention (
3.6
), let
g_{i\bar{j}}
,
i,j=1,2,\dots,2n
, denote (twice) the components of the metric
g
in terms of local coordinates:

g|_{\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i,j=1}^{2n}g_{i\bar{j}}\,\mathrm{d}z_{i}\mathrm{d}\bar{z}_{j}

(3.7)

and set

A_{\alpha}:=(g_{i\bar{j}})_{i,j=1,2,\dots,2n}\in C^{\infty}(\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha}),\End(\mathbb{C}^{2n})).

(3.8)

Recall that the Ricci form
\rho
on the Kähler manifold
(\mathit{Z},g,I)
with respect to the Levi-Civita connection is locally given by

\rho|_{\pi_{\lambda}^{-1}(U_{\alpha})}=\sqrt{-1}\,\overline{\partial}\partial\log\det A_{\alpha}.

The following theorem is a key to find Ricci-flat metrics.

Theorem 3.2
.

The metric
g
satisfies
\det A_{\alpha}=1
if and only if the function
f
satisfies that

2f^{\prime}(\tau)f(\tau)^{2n-1}\tau^{n}(\tau-\tau_{0})+f(\tau)^{2n}\tau^{n-1}(2\tau-\tau_{0})=1,

(3.9)

where we set
\tau_{0}=\left|\,s\,\right|^{2}
.

Proof.

By definition, the components
g_{i\bar{j}}
are given by

g_{i\bar{j}}=f(\tau)\tau_{i,\bar{j}}+f^{\prime}(\tau)\tau_{i}\tau_{\bar{j}}

(3.10)

with
\tau_{i}:=\partial_{z_{i}}\tau,\,\tau_{\bar{i}}:=\partial_{\bar{z}_{i}}\tau
and
\tau_{i,\bar{j}}:=\partial_{z_{i}}\partial_{\bar{z}_{j}}\tau
for
i,j=1,2,\dots,2n
under the convention (
3.6
).
Setting
c:=f(\tau),c_{1}:=f^{\prime}(\tau)
for brevity and

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{a}_{j}

\displaystyle:=\hskip 1.0pt\vphantom{{(\tau_{1,\bar{j}},\dots,\tau_{2n,\bar{j}})}}^{t}\hskip-1.6pt(\tau_{1,\bar{j}},\dots,\tau_{2n,\bar{j}})\quad(j=1,2,\dots,2n),

(3.11)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{b}

\displaystyle:=\hskip 1.0pt\vphantom{{(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{2n})}}^{t}\hskip-1.6pt(\tau_{1},\dots,\tau_{2n}),

(3.12)

we write
A_{\alpha}
as

A_{\alpha}=c(\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\boldsymbol{a}_{2},\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_{2n})+c_{1}(\tau_{\bar{1}}\boldsymbol{b},\tau_{\bar{2}}\boldsymbol{b},\dots,\tau_{\overline{2n}}\boldsymbol{b}).

(3.13)

Therefore,

\det A_{\alpha}=c^{2n}\det(\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_{2n})+c^{2n-1}c_{1}\sum_{k=1}^{2n}\tau_{\bar{k}}\det(\boldsymbol{a}_{1},\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_{k-1},\hskip-3.0pt\overset{k\text{-th}}{\boldsymbol{b}}\hskip-3.0pt,\boldsymbol{a}_{k+1},\dots,\boldsymbol{a}_{2n}).

(3.14)

Now the theorem follows from Propositions
3.3
and
3.4
below.

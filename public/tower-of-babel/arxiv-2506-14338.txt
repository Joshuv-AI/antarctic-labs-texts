Phenomenological approach for introducing three-nucleon-force contributions into p p - d d elastic scattering cross section
Yoshiki Chazono, Tokuro Fukui, Futoshi Minato, Yukinobu Watanabe, Kazuyuki Ogata

Abstract
Background The ( p , p ​ d p,pd ) reaction is expected to be a powerful tool for probing three-nucleon forces (3NFs) in nuclear medium since it can be essentially regarded as the p p - d d elastic scattering inside nuclei. One of the important points in the theoretical description of the ( p , p ​ d p,pd ) reaction is to calculate the p p - d d scattering in a nucleus including both two-nucleon (2N) force and 3NF contributions. Purpose This work aims to develop a phenomenological approach to incorporate 3NF contributions into p p - d d elastic scattering in free space. Method The p p - d d elastic scattering amplitude is decomposed into a 2N part, described using 2N effective interactions, and a residual part, assumed to primarily reflect 3NF contributions. The latter is approximated by a superposition of Legendre polynomials, with coefficients treated as adjustable parameters. These parameters are determined to reproduce experimental p p - d d differential cross-section data at various incident energies. Result The obtained parameters exhibit smooth energy dependence, which is approximated by quadratic functions. The numerical results with the analytic energy dependence also reproduce the experimental data. Conclusion The developed approach works well for incorporating the 3NF contributions into the p p - d d elastic scattering cross section in a wide range of incident energies. This work can be regarded as the first step toward the description of ( p , p ​ d p,pd ) reactions with taking 3NF effect in nuclear medium into account.



I
Introduction

Recent progress in chiral effective field theory
Weinberg 1979
;
Epelbaum 2006
;
Machleidt and Entem 2011

has refined our knowledge of three-nucleon forces (3NFs) in many-nucleon systems.
For instance, the chiral 3NF
Weinberg 1992
;
van Kolck 1994
;
Hanhart et al. 2000
plays an essential role
in the description of
{}^{10}\mathrm{B}

Navrátil et al. 2007
,
understanding the spin-orbit splitting of light nuclei
Fukui et al. 2024
,
the explanation of the limit of oxygen isotopes
Otsuka et al. 2010
,
and the realistic prediction of nuclear matter saturation
Sammarruca and Millerson 2019
.

One of the typical approaches to study the 3NF in medium is based on an approximation
that the 3NF is expressed as a density dependent two-nucleon (2N) effective interaction.
Because the proton-induced proton knockout (
p,2p
) reactions are regarded as proton-proton (
p
-
p
) elastic scattering inside nuclei, they have been utilized to clarify the in-medium modification to the
p
-
p
interaction
Wakasa et al. 2017
.
Quite recently, Ref.
Minomo et al. 2017
revealed that the triple differential cross section
of the
{}^{40}\mathrm{Ca}(p,2p)^{39}\mathrm{K}
reaction, for which the
0p_{3/2}
orbit proton is knocked out, is sensitive to the chiral 3NF effect on the
p
-
p
cross section in nuclear medium.

As an extension of Ref.
Minomo et al. 2017
, one may consider using the deuteron knockout (
p,pd
) reaction to investigate the 3NF effect on the
p
-
d
elastic cross section in nuclei.
The (
p,pd
) reaction has attracted attention as a means to probe the presence of deuterons in nuclei
Uesaka 2024
.
Theoretically, one of the authors (Y. C.) and collaborators developed a novel reaction model, CDCCIA, for describing (
p,pd
) with explicitly accounting for the fragility of the deuteron
Chazono et al. 2022
.
One of the key ingredients of CDCCIA is the transition matrix for the
p
-
d
reaction system, which is formulated in terms of a 2N effective interaction.
This approach is suitable for the aforementioned strategy of investigating 3NF effect through the density dependence of the 2N effective interaction.

To lay the groundwork for 3NF studies using (
p,pd
) reactions, it is necessary to have reliable
p
-
d
elastic cross sections in free space over a range of energies and scattering angles.
Unfortunately, the Faddeev theory, which provides the exact solution for the
p
-
d
scattering problem, does not perfectly reproduce the
p
-
d
elastic scattering data using existing nucleon forces at energies higher than about
150
MeV/nucleon
Witała et al. 1998
;
Witała et al. 2022
.
Furthermore, as mentioned earlier, we plan to explore in-medium 3NF effects via the density dependence of the 2N effective interaction.
Therefore, a model for the
p
-
d
elastic cross sections in free space based on an effective interaction is needed.
As shown in Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
, calculations using the Franey-Love 2N effective interaction
Franey and Love 1985
tend to underestimate the experimental cross sections around the minima.
This underestimation has also been reported in Faddeev calculations that include only the 2N force.
As is well known, this discrepancy can be resolved by including the 3NF contribution.
In our approach, we simulate the 3NF contribution to the
p
-
d
elastic cross sections by adding a phenomenological “3NF” amplitude to the
p
-
d
transition matrix used in Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
.
This additional amplitude is parameterized as a superposition of Legendre polynomials, and the energy dependence of each parameter is described by an analytic function.

This paper is organized as follows.
In Sec.
II
, we explain our approach for introducing 3NF contributions phenomenologically into the differential cross section of the
p
-
d
scattering.
In Sec.
III
, the inputs used in the numerical calculation are mentioned.
We also show the obtained results compared with the experimental data and discuss how well our approach works.
Finally, the summary and perspective are given in Sec.
IV
.

II
Theoretical framework

The differential cross section of the
p
-
d
elastic scattering is considered.
All quantities are evaluated in the center-of-mass (c.m.) frame of the
p
-
d
system, and those with the superscript L are evaluated in the laboratory (L) frame.
We adopt the isospin representation, i.e., proton and neutron are distinguished by the third component of their isospin, in numerical calculations.

We describe a
p
-
d
elastic scattering cross section as

\displaystyle\derivative{\sigma}{\Omega}=\quantity(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{pd}}{2 \pi\hbar^2})^{2}\absolutevalue{\tilde{t}_\textrm{2N} + \tilde{t}_\textrm{3N}}^{2},

(1)

where
\mathcal{M}_{pd}
is the reduced energy of the
p
-
d
system.
The
p
-
d
elastic amplitude is decomposed into the 2N and 3N parts,
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{2N}}
and
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
, respectively.

As the same manner as used in Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
, we calculate
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{2N}}
using 2N effective interactions as

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{\textrm{2N}}=\matrixelement{\Phi_d, \vkap'
}{\qty[t_{pp} + t_{pn}] \hat{\mathcal{A}}
}{\Phi_d, \vkap},

(2)

where
\Phi_{d}
is the antisymmetrized ground-state wave function of the deuteron.
The vectors
{\bf\it\kappa}
and
{\bf\it\kappa}^{\prime}
are the relative momenta (in units of
\hbar
) between the incident proton and the c.m. of the deuteron in the initial and final channels, respectively.

t_{pp}
and
t_{pn}
are the proton-proton and proton-neutron effective interactions in free space, respectively, which are essentially the same since we adopt the isospin representation.
The antisymmetrization operator
\hat{\mathcal{A}}
is defined by

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{A}}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\quantity[1 - \hat{P}_{pp} - \hat{P}_{pn}],

(3)

where
\hat{P}_{pp}\ (\hat{P}_{pn})
exchanges the incident proton and the proton (neutron) in the deuteron.
With the use of the antisymmetrized deuteron wave function, the antisymmetrization of the 3N system requires the normalization coefficient
1/\sqrt{3}
in Eq. (
3
).

We assume
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
is represented as a superposition of Legendre polynomials
P_{L}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}=\sum_{L=0}^{L_{\textrm{max}}}C_{L}P_{L}(\cos{\theta}),

(4)

where
L
is the angular momentum transfer (in units of
\hbar
) and
\theta
is the scattering angle.
The complex coefficients
C_{L}
are determined phenomenologically so that Eq. (
1
) reproduces the experimental data.

For convenience, we also define the cross sections including only
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{2N}}
and
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
as

\displaystyle\quantity(\dv{\sigma}{\Omega})_{\textrm{2N}}=\quantity(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{pd}}{2 \pi\hbar^2})^{2}\absolutevalue{\tilde{t}_\textrm{2N}}^{2}

(5)

and

\displaystyle\quantity(\dv{\sigma}{\Omega})_{\textrm{3N}}=\quantity(\frac{\mathcal{M}_{pd}}{2 \pi\hbar^2})^{2}\absolutevalue{\tilde{t}_\textrm{3N}}^{2},

(6)

respectively.
The former represents the result obtained using only the 2N effective interaction, which is identical to the cross section used in Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
.
The latter corresponds to the contribution from the newly added amplitude of Eq. (
4
); for a simple notation, we refer to it as the
p
-
d
cross section including only the 3NF contribution.
It should be noted that, since there is interference between
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{2N}}
and
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
, the sum of Eqs. (
5
) and (
6
) does not necessarily agree with the cross section of Eq. (
1
).

III
Result and discussion

III.1
Input

The coefficients
C_{L}
in Eq. (
4
) have been determined to minimize
\chi^{2}
defined by

\displaystyle\chi^{2}\equiv\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}(\theta_{i})}\quantity[\qty(\dv{\sigma}{\Omega})_\textrm{exp}^{(i)}
- \qty(\dv{\sigma}{\Omega})_\textrm{theor}^{(i)}]^{2}

(7)

at proton-incident energy
T_{p}^{\textrm{L}}
with the Marquardt method
Levenberg 1944
;
D. W. Marquardt 1963
.
Label
i
specifies the scattering angle where an experimental cross section is measured, and
N
is the number of data at each incident energy.
An experimental error at each scattering angle is represented by
\epsilon(\theta_{i})
.
We have carried out the parameter search at six incident energies:
108
,
120
,
150
,
170
,
190
, and
250
MeV, with setting
L_{\textrm{max}}
to 1.
The experimental cross sections used for the search are taken from Ref.
Ermisch et al. 2005
(
108
,
120
,
150
,
170
, and
190
MeV) and Ref.
Hatanaka et al. 2002
(
250
MeV); the experimental statistic errors are less than
1\%
and the systematic ones are about
5\%
.
One of the purposes of this paper is to explore
C_{L}
having a relatively smooth incident-energy dependence, not to optimize them at each energy.
Hence, we have set
\epsilon
to
5\%
similar to the systematic errors.

The parameter set proposed by Franey and Love
Franey and Love 1985
have been used for the 2N effective interactions
t_{pp}
and
t_{pn}
in Eq. (
2
).
Since the 2N effective interaction is parametrized to reproduce nucleon-nucleon scattering observables, the interaction does not guarantee that it can describe the feature of the
p
-
d
elastic scattering.
Then, we have introduced the phenomenological correction factor
C_{\textrm{corr}}
as in Ref.
Chazono et al. 2022
and have determined its value so that the result of Eq. (
1
) matches the
p
-
d
elastic cross section data at the most forward angle at each energy.
It should be noted that the Coulomb interaction between
p
and
d
is not included in our calculation, and hence the data measured at angles greater than
20^{\circ}
, where the Coulomb interaction is expected to have minor contributions, have been used for the parameter search.

IV
Summary

A phenomenological approach has been developed to effectively incorporate 3NF contributions into
p
-
d
elastic scattering, in which matrix elements of the 3N effective interactions are introduced as corrections to the 2N effective interactions.
The matrix elements were represented by a superposition of Legendre polynomials with adjustable parameters.
We have performed the parameter search so that the theoretical calculation reproduces the measured differential cross sections of the
p
-
d
elastic scattering at six incident energies between
100
MeV and
250
MeV, and found the parameters with relatively smooth energy dependence.
The energy dependence was well approximated by quadratic functions, and the numerical results obtained using the parameter values estimated from the functional forms also showed good agreement with the experimental data.
It was found that the approximated energy dependence is effective at energies that were not considered in the parameter searching procedure.
These findings demonstrate that the phenomenological approach proposed in this study works well.

To investigate the 3NF contribution in nuclei, the Franey-Love 2N effective interaction needs to be replaced with a density-dependent 2N interaction that incorporates 3NF effects in finite nuclei.
In such calculations, the contribution from
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
should be retained, as it corresponds to
p
-
d
scattering in free space, that is, in the zero-density limit.
Therefore, the parametrization of
\tilde{t}_{\textrm{3N}}
presented in this paper should be regarded as a necessary step in laying the groundwork for 3NF studies in nuclei via (
p,pd
) reactions.

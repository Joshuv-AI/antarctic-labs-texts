Forward Arc Maximization for Hamilton Oriented Cycles and Paths in Generalizations of Tournaments
Qiwen Guo Gregory Gutin Yongxin Lan Qi Shao Anders Yeo Yacong Zhou

Abstract
Gishboliner, Krivelevich, and Michaeli (2023) conjectured the following generalization of Dirac’s theorem: If the minimum degree δ \delta of an n n -vertex oriented graph G G is greater or equal to n / 2 n/2 , then G G has a Hamilton oriented cycle with at least δ \delta forward arcs. Freschi and Lo (2024) proved this conjecture. In this paper, we study the problem of maximizing the number of forward arcs in Hamilton oriented cycles/paths in generalizations of tournaments. We obtain characterizations for the maximum number of forward arcs in semicomplete multipartite digraphs and locally semicomplete digraphs. These characterizations lead to polynomial-time algorithms. Note that the above problems are NP-hard for some other generalizations of tournaments even though the Hamilton cycle problem is polynomial-time solvable for these digraph classes.



Keywords:
Hamilton oriented cycles; forward arcs; semicomplete multipartite digraphs; locally semicomplete tournaments

Hamilton oriented paths and cycles with maximum number of forward arcs in semicomplete multipartite digraphs

The following result gives a characterization of semicomplete multipartite digraphs with a Hamilton oriented cycle (path, respectively).

Proposition 2.1
.

A semicomplete multipartite digraph

with at least 3 vertices and with partite sets of sizes

has a Hamilton oriented cycle (path, respectively) if and only if

satisfies the HC-majority inequality (the HP-majority inequality, respectively).

Proof.

Observe that
D
has a Hamilton oriented cycle if and only if

has a Hamilton cycle. Note that if

does not satisfy the HC-majority inequality, then it has no Hamilton cycle. If
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\dots,n_{p}}
satisfies the HC-majority inequality, then it has a Hamilton cycle by Dirac’s theorem
[
8
]

Observe that

has a Hamilton oriented path if and only if the digraph

obtained from

by adding a new vertex

for

has a Hamilton oriented cycle which is if and only if

has a Hamilton cycle. Thus,

Now we characterize the oriented discrepancy of semicomplete multipartite digraphs by the cost of cycle factors in its symmetric (0,1)-digraph.

Theorem
1.4
.

Let

be an

-vertex semicomplete multipartite digraph with

satisfying the HC-majority inequality and let

be the maximum cost of a cycle factor in
\hat{D}.
Let
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
be the maximum
\sigma^{+}(C)

in

Then
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=c^{cf}_{\max}
unless
c^{cf}_{\max}=n
and

\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=n-1.

Both
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
and a Hamilton oriented cycle
C
with
\sigma^{+}(C)=\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
can be found in polynomial time.

Proof.

Note that a Hamilton cycle
C=x_{1}x_{2}\dots x_{n}x_{1}
in
\hat{D}
can be transformed into a Hamilton oriented cycle of
D
by replacing every arc
x_{i}x_{i+1}\pmod{n}
of zero-cost by
x_{i+1}x_{i}\pmod{n}
.
Also by the construction of
\hat{D}
,
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
is equal to the maximum cost of a Hamilton cycle in
\hat{D}.

Observe that a Hamilton cycle of
\hat{D}
is a cycle factor of
\hat{D}
. Thus, the maximum cost of a Hamilton cycle of
\hat{D}
is no more than
c^{cf}_{\max}.

Let
F
be a cycle factor of
\hat{D}
of maximum cost, which can be found in polynomial time, see the last two paragraphs of Theorem
1.3
.
Let
D_{F}
be a spanning subdigraph of
\hat{D}
with
A(D_{F})=A(D)\cup A(F).
We now consider the following cases.

Case 1:

c^{cf}_{\max}<n
.

Thus,
F
has an arc,
a
, of cost 0. Note that
F-a
is a 1-path-cycle factor in the semicomplete multipartite digraph
D_{F}-a
.
By Theorem
1.5
we note that
D_{F}-a
contains a Hamilton path,
P=p_{1}p_{2}p_{3}\cdots p_{n}
, with initial and terminal vertices from different partite sets.
Now for the Hamilton oriented cycle

in
D
, we know that
\sigma^{+}(C^{\prime})
is at least the cost of

and this is at least the cost of

(as the number of zero-cost arcs in

is one smaller than the number of zero-cost arcs in

). This implies that
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=c^{cf}_{\max}
and completes the proof in this case.

Case 2:

c^{cf}_{\max}=n
.

Then
F
is a cycle factor of
D
. If

is hamiltonian then clearly
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=n=c^{cf}_{\max}
.
So we may assume that

is not hamiltonian. But removing an arc from

and using Theorem
1.5
on the resulting

1
-path-cycle factor in

gives us a Hamilton path in

where the initial and terminal vertices are in different partite sets.
We therefore obtain a Hamilton oriented cycle in
D
with at most 1 backward arc. As

is not hamiltonian, we therefore get

\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=n-1
, which completes the proof in this case.

The proofs of this theorem and Theorem
1.5
can be converted to corresponding polynomial-time algorithms. These algorithms and the
polynomial-time algorithms of Theorems

and
2.3
imply that both
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
and a Hamilton oriented cycle

with
\sigma^{+}(C)=\sigma^{hc}_{\max}
can be found in polynomial time.
∎

3
Hamilton oriented cycles with maximum number of forward arcs in locally semicomplete digraphs

Let
D
be a digraph. For disjoint subsets
A
and
B
of
V(D)
, if all arcs exist from
A
to
B
, we say
A

dominates

B
. We will use the following three results by
Bang-Jensen
[
1
]
.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
1
]

Let
D
be a connected locally semicomplete digraph that is not strong. Then the strong components of
D
can be ordered uniquely as
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{\ell}
such that there is no arc from
V(C_{j})
to
V(C_{i})
when
j>i
,
V(C_{i})
dominates
V(C_{i+1})
for each
i\in[\ell-1]
and
C_{t}
is a semicomplete digraph for each
t\in[\ell]
. And if there is an arc from
V(C_{i})
to
V(C_{k})
, then
V(C_{i})
dominates
V(C_{j})
for all
j=i+1,i+2,\dots,k
and
V(C_{t})
dominates
V(C_{k})
for all
t=i,i+1,\dots,k-1
.

Theorem 3.2
.

[
1
]

A connected locally semicomplete digraph has a Hamilton path.

Theorem 3.3
.

[
1
]

A strong locally semicomplete digraph has a Hamilton cycle.

Note that an acyclic ordering of strong components in a digraph can be obtained in polynomial time
[
3
]
. Thus, the ordering in Theorem
3.1
can be obtained in polynomial time. Using this ordering, it is not hard to construct a Hamilton path in a connected local semicomplete digraph
[
1
]
. The proof of Theorem
3.3
in
[
1
]
leads to a polynomial-time algorithm for finding a Hamilton cycle in a strong locally semicomplete digraph. Finally, for vertex-disjoint subgraphs
S
and
T
of a digraph

(S,T)
-path in polynomial time.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
D
be a connected locally semicomplete digraph on
n\geq 3
vertices that is not strong. Let
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{\ell}
be the acyclic ordering of strong components of
D

is 2-connected, then there is a shortest
(C_{1},C_{\ell})
-path

in
D
, such that

is a subpath of

and the set of backward arcs in

is

. Such a Hamilton oriented cycle

can be constructed in polynomial time.

Proof.

Let
D
be a locally semicomplete digraph on
n\geq 3
vertices that is not strong, but such that
U(D)
is 2-connected. Let
C_{1},C_{2},\dots,C_{\ell}
be the acyclic ordering of strong components of

and define the function

such that

if

.
Define the
(C_{1},C_{\ell})
-path

be arbitrary and for each

be an arbitrary vertex such that

and

is maximum possible. Continue this process until

. By Theorem

we note that

-path in

We will now show that

is connected. For the sake of contradiction assume this is not the case and let

This implies that

As

As

Theorem 3.5

Theorem
1.6
.

Let
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=0

has no Hamilton oriented cycle, and let

be the maximum of

over all Hamilton oriented cycles

•

If

is 2-connected, then
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=n-d(C_{1},C_{\ell})
.

•

If

is not 2-connected, then
\sigma^{hc}_{\max}=0
.

•

If

with the maximum number of forward arcs provided

Proof.

We first consider the case when

is not strong and

is 2-connected.
Theorem

implies that

contains an oriented Hamilton cycle

, which consists of two internally disjoint paths from a vertex in

to a vertex in

-path in

\sigma^{hc}_{\max}\geq\sigma^{+}(R^{\prime})=n-d(C_{1},C_{\ell})

Now let

be an arbitrary oriented Hamilton cycle in

with the maximum possible number of forward arcs.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that

-path in

By Theorem

When

4
Complexity of finding Hamilton oriented cycles with maximum number of forward arcs

Encouraged by our results on semicomplete multipartite digraphs and locally semicomplete digraphs, one may guess that if the Hamilton cycle problem is polynomial-time solvable in a class of generalizations of semicomplete digraphs, then
the same is true for the problem of finding a Hamilton oriented cycle with maximum number of forward arcs. Unfortunately, this is not the case.

For example, consider
locally out-semicomplete digraphs
i.e. digraphs in which the out-neighbors of every vertex induce a semicomplete subdigraph
[
2
]
.
It is well-known that a locally out-semicomplete digraph
D
has a Hamilton cycle if and only if
D
is strongly connected. Thus, the Hamilton cycle problem is polynomial-time solvable for locally out-semicomplete digraphs. Now consider a locally out-semicomplete digraph
H
obtained from a bipartite undirected graph
B
with partite sets
X
and
Y
,
|X|=|Y|\geq 2
, by orienting every edge of
B
from
X
to
Y
and adding an arbitrary arc between every pair of vertices in
Y
. Clearly,
H
has a Hamilton oriented cycle if and only if
B
has a Hamilton cycle. However, the problem of deciding whether a bipartite graph has a Hamilton cycle is
NP
-hard and so is the problem of deciding whether
H
has a Hamilton oriented cycle.

A digraph
D=(V,A)
is
quasi-transitive
if whenever
xy,yz\in A
for distinct
x,y,z\in V
, we have either
xz\in A
or
zx\in A
or both.
We can decide whether a quasi-transitive digraph has a Hamilton cycle in polynomial time
[
14
]
. However, by orienting all edges of a bipartite graph
B
from
X
and
Y
as above, we obtain a quasi-transitive digraph
Q
, which is even a transitive digraph. Thus, even the problem of deciding whether
Q
has a Hamilton oriented cycle is
NP
-hard.

In fact, the above argument shows that it is
NP
-hard to find a Hamilton oriented cycle in a digraph which is bipartite, acyclic and transitive.

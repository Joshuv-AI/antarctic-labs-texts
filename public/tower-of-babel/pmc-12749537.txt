Residues, migration pattern and health risk assessment of sodium pentachlorophenate in bamboo and wooden cutting boards
Authors: Ye Wang, Mingyou Hu, Fang Wang, Juan Yu, Guojian Shao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractSodium pentachlorophenate (PCP-Na) is a synthetic organochlorine compound used as a wood preservative. Given the potential for residual PCP-Na in wood-based food contact materials to migrate into diet, this study investigated PCP-Na residues, migration, and health risks in commercial bamboo and wooden cutting boards using ultrasonic-assisted liquid-liquid extraction combined with ultra-performance liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (UA-LLE-UPLC-HRMS). Analysis of 120 boards (2022–2024) found quantifiable PCP-Na in five samples (1.50–422 mg/kg). Migration was assessed by mincing pork on positive boards over 10 simulated uses. PCP-Na transfer peaked during the first use, declined following a power-function pattern, and stabilized after the eighth use. Health risk assessment, conducted using accepted dietary exposure and risk characterization models, revealed high dietary intake risk (%ADI ≥ 200) for all age groups during the first use. Risk decreased with subsequent uses: moderate for adults by the fourth use (200 > %ADI ≥ 150), low by the fifth (150 > %ADI ≥ 100), and became insignificant (%ADI < 100) for all ages from the eighth use onward. Non-carcinogenic risk (HQ) was only observed for adults during the first use (HQ > 1). However, carcinogenic risk assessment (expressed as PCP equivalents, R) indicated potential cancer risk (R > 10⁻⁴) across all simulated usage cycles for all age groups. This study quantifies PCP-Na contamination and migration dynamics, showing significant initial migration decreasing with use. Introduction

As one of the most frequently used food-contact utensils, cutting boards play a critical role in ensuring dietary safety by preventing pathogenic bacterial growth and blocking chemical residues from entering the food chain1–3. Cutting boards are generally classified into four main types: bamboo, wood, plastic, and tempered glass. Among these, bamboo and wooden boards—often marketed as “natural” and “eco-friendly”—are widely used in Chinese households. However, prolonged use of these traditional boards often results in bacterial growth within knife marks and surface crevices. To extend product shelf life, some manufacturers illegally add excessive preservatives, leading to significant residual levels on bamboo and wooden cutting boards.

Sodium pentachlorophenate (PCP-Na) is a highly toxic organochlorine compound with strong bactericidal, antiseptic, and insecticidal properties. It has been widely used worldwide as a herbicide, insecticide, and preservative4,5. Due to its historical widespread use and resistance to degradation, PCP-Na has become widely distributed in the environment. Recent studies have shown that PCP-Na is both carcinogenic and teratogenic6,7, bioaccumulates easily in organisms8,9, and is classified as a Group 2B carcinogen by the International Agency for Research on Cancer (IARC)10. Under mildly acidic conditions (pH 6.6–6.8), PCP-Na can be converted into pentachlorophenol (PCP), a Group 1 carcinogen11,12. PCP-Na is highly water-soluble and can spread widely via water, entering the human body through inhalation, ingestion, or dermal absorption. Frequent exposure may damage the central nervous, immune, reproductive, hepatic, and renal systems13,14, potentially leading to adverse health outcomes. However, due to its severe environmental and toxicological profile, the production and use of PCP have been addressed on a global scale. It has been banned or severely restricted in many countries since it was listed under the Annex A of the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs) in 201515. This international action was taken because PCP is recognized as a persistent organic pollutant (POP), meaning it resists natural degradation, bioaccumulates in food webs, and poses significant risks to human health and the environment. The compound’s persistence in the environment is notable; it strongly binds to soil and sediment16, where it can remain for years. Its degradation is slow and highly dependent on conditions17–19; under anaerobic conditions, microbial processes can lead to the formation of toxic metabolites like tetrachlorophenol. In contrast, under aerobic conditions, it can undergo photodegradation, but this process is often incomplete. According to EU Regulation 2021/277, the maximum permitted concentration of PCP and its salts and esters in substances, mixtures, or articles is 5 mg/kg20. Previous studies have mainly focused on PCP-Na in environmental and food samples21–24, with limited attention to intermediate carriers such as cutting boards25,26. Although contamination in these matrices is well-documented, the migration of PCP-Na from food contact materials into food and the resulting direct human dietary exposure risk remain insufficiently investigated. Risk assessments of PCP and its sodium salts have similarly concentrated on environmental and dietary matrices27–30. However, recent monitoring has shown a high detection rate of PCP-Na in bamboo and wooden cutting boards. For instance, Dong et al.31 detected PCP-Na in 8 out of 33 (24.2%) bamboo and wooden cutting boards, while more notably, Liao et al.25 reported a detection rate of 50% (15 out of 30 samples) in their survey. This may be due to illegal practices by some manufacturers who use PCP-Na solutions for anticorrosion, mildew prevention, and color stabilization, leading to residual contamination32. During use, residual PCP-Na in bamboo and wooden cutting boards can migrate into food through cutting and chopping. Additionally, weakly acidic substances present on the board surface may convert PCP-Na into PCP, which also migrates into food25. These harmful pollutants can enter the human body through ingestion, potentially affecting human health. Currently, no regulatory limits exist in China for PCP-Na in bamboo and wooden cutting boards, and no studies have reported its migration patterns or associated health risks during use.

To address these issues, this study employed ultrasonic-assisted liquid-liquid extraction combined with ultra-performance liquid chromatography-high resolution mass spectrometry (UA-LLE-UPLC-HRMS) to monitor PCP-Na residues in commercially available bamboo and wooden cutting boards in Huzhou City between 2022 and 2024, thereby assessing contamination levels. Simulated cutting board usage conditions were used to quantify PCP-Na migration into food across multiple usage cycles, providing insight into its migration patterns under typical usage scenarios. Health risk assessment models were employed to estimate dietary intake during cutting board use, facilitating a comprehensive evaluation of PCP-Na–related health risks and potential human hazards. This study provides essential data to support the safe use of commercial bamboo and wooden cutting boards, offers scientific evidence for establishing regulatory limits for PCP-Na, and contributes to improved food safety and consumer health protection.

Materials and methods

Reagents and materials

Chromatography-grade methanol, ethyl acetate, and n-hexane were acquired from Merck GmbH (Germany), with formic acid of the same grade supplied by Shanghai Macklin Biochemical (China). A guaranteed reagent-grade ammonia solution was procured from Merck GmbH. Ultrapure water (18.2 MΩ·cm) was prepared via a Milli-Q Integral system, and analyte filtration employed 13 mm PTFE syringe filters (0.22 μm; ANPEL Laboratory Technologies, China). Fresh pork (150 g) purchased from Zhebei Supermarket in Huzhou.

Standard solutions

A solid-form PCP-Na certified reference material (75.8% purity; TMRM Co., Ltd., China) and the isotope-labeled internal standard pentachlorophenol-13C6 (PCP-13C6, 99% purity; Cambridge Isotope Laboratories, Inc., USA) were procured for this study.

Instruments and equipment

Sample processing employed a GM200 stainless-steel grinder (Retsch, Germany) and Multi Reax vortex mixer (Heidolph, Germany), with ultrasonic extraction using a KQ-800DE processor (Kunshan Ultrasonic, China). Centrifugation was performed on an Allegra 64R unit (Beckman Coulter, USA), followed by solvent concentration via TurboVap LV system (Biotage, Sweden). Chromatographic separation and mass detection utilized a Vanquish UHPLC/Q Exactive™ Orbitrap system (Thermo Fisher Scientific, USA).

Sample pre-treatment

Extraction of PCP-Na from cutting boards

The wood chips were processed using an electric drill with the standardized nine-point protocol33, followed by pulverization to a fine powder. Sample preparation initiated with precise weighing of homogenates (0.5 ± 0.001 g) into 50 mL polypropylene tubes. The internal standard (PCP-13C6, 50 µL of 2000 µg/L solution) and extractant (10 mL methanol/water [50:50 v/v] with 2.0% NH₃·H₂O) were introduced sequentially. A triphasic extraction ensued: (1) vortex homogenization (2500 rpm, 60 s); (2) ultrasonic processing (20 kHz, 450 W, 10 min); (3) chilled centrifugation (10,000 rpm, 5 min, 4 °C). Following phase separation, 5.00 mL supernatant underwent liquid-liquid extraction in 15 mL tubes with 75 µL formic acid and 4 mL n-hexane/ethyl acetate (60:40 v/v). Post vortexing (5 min) and centrifugation (identical parameters), the organic layer was quantitatively collected, nitrogen-concentrated to dryness (30 °C, 15 psi, 45 min), and reconstituted in 1.00 mL methanol/0.01% NH₃·H₂O (50:50 v/v). Final extracts were membrane-filtered (0.22 μm PTFE) into certified LC vials prior to UPLC-HRMS analysis.

Extraction of PCP-Na from pork

Accurately weigh a well-mixed pork sample (2.0 ± 0.001 g) and pre-treat it according to the procedure described previously.

Monitoring of PCP-Na residues in cutting boards

Between 2022 and 2024, a comprehensive sampling strategy was implemented to ensure the representativeness of cutting boards collected from Huzhou City. A total of 120 commercially available bamboo and wooden cutting boards were systematically collected during a single sampling campaign from various sources including farmers’ markets, supermarkets, and online platforms across three counties and two districts. To achieve geographical representation, the sampling distribution was designed as follows: 20 boards (14 wooden and 6 bamboo) from Nanxun District, and 25 boards from each of the other four locations—Deqing County, Changxing County, Anji County, and Wuxing District—with each of these locations comprising 18 wooden and 7 bamboo boards.

To enable comparative analysis, samples were stratified not only by material type (bamboo vs. wooden) but also by source (farmers’ markets, supermarkets, and online platforms). Statistical comparisons of PCP-Na levels were planned between these categories, as well as across different geographical areas (counties and districts). All samples were analyzed in triplicate, with the mean value recorded as the final result to ensure analytical reliability. This stratified sampling approach ensured proportional representation across different geographical areas, retail channels, and material types within the study region.

Experiments on the migration pattern of PCP-Na

A coating method was used to simulate the preservative treatment process. Specifically, a 10 mg/mL PCP-Na standard solution was uniformly applied to PCP-Na–negative cutting boards from the same production batch. The coated boards were naturally air-dried to prepare PCP-Na–positive cutting boards with consistent PCP-Na content. These PCP-Na–positive boards were then subjected to the pretreatment procedures as described previously to determine PCP-Na content. An additional PCP-Na–positive cutting board was used to simulate routine usage. Fresh pork (150 g) was minced into paste using a kitchen knife at a constant speed for 10 min per session, with cutting confined to a designated area (Fig. 1).

Quality control and assurance

The analytical method used for the determination of PCP-Na in cutting boards had been fully validated in a previous study34. The extraction procedure was optimized by evaluating different solvents, pH conditions, and extraction times to select parameters yielding the highest recovery efficiency. Key validation parameters included the limit of detection (LOD), limit of quantification (LOQ), accuracy, precision, and method linearity. Quantification was performed using isotope-labeled internal standards to correct analyte concentrations.

For the pork matrix analyzed in this study, the sample weight was adjusted from the original method. Consequently, the method sensitivity parameters were recalculated specifically for this matrix, yielding an LOD of 0.2 µg/kg and an LOQ of 0.6 µg/kg. Method accuracy, expressed as recovery, ranged from 96.9% to 99.6%, while precision, expressed as relative standard deviation (RSD), ranged from 1.1% to 3.2%.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 27.0. Due to the non-normal distribution of the PCP-Na concentration data (as confirmed by the Shapiro-Wilk test, p < 0.05) and the presence of a large number of non-detects, non-parametric tests were employed. The Mann-Whitney U test was used to compare PCP-Na concentrations between bamboo and wooden cutting boards. The Kruskal-Wallis H test was applied to assess differences in PCP-Na concentrations among the five sampling locations. Fisher’s exact test was conducted to examine the association between cutting board material (bamboo vs. wood) and the incidence of PCP-Na contamination (positive vs. negative). A p-value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All results were presented as the mean of three or six independent experiments. Excel 2019 and OriginPro 2024 were used for chart drawing.

Results and discussion

Monitoring of PCP-Na residues in commercially available cutting boards

A total of 120 bamboo and wooden cutting board samples were randomly collected from commercial markets in three counties and two districts of Huzhou City between 2022 and 2024. Five samples (4.2%) tested positive for PCP-Na, with concentrations ranging from 1.50 to 422 mg/kg and an average of 138 mg/kg. Notably, all five PCP-Na-positive samples were wooden cutting boards obtained from farmers’ markets, with three originating from Nanxun District and one each from Anji County and Changxing County. Four of the five PCP-Na-positive samples significantly exceeded the 5 mg/kg limit set by Regulation (EU) 2021/277. This detection rate of 4.2% (5/120) is lower than those reported in previous studies, such as the 24.2% (8/33) described by Dong et al.31 and 50% (15/30) by Liao et al.25, suggesting potential regional or temporal variations in compliance and enforcement. Statistical analysis revealed no significant differences in PCP-Na concentrations between bamboo and wooden cutting boards as determined by the Mann-Whitney U test (U = 1377, p = 0.620). The median PCP-Na concentration in wooden cutting boards was 0 mg/kg when including all samples. Similarly, the Kruskal-Wallis test showed no significant differences in PCP-Na concentrations among the five sampling locations (H = 0.6, p = 0.963). Fisher’s exact test indicated no significant association between board material and PCP-Na contamination (p = 0.320). However, descriptively, all five PCP-Na-positive samples were wooden cutting boards from farmers’ markets, suggesting a potential trend that warrants further investigation with larger sample sizes.

Although the statistical tests did not show significant differences, likely due to the low number of positive detections (5 out of 120) limiting statistical power, the descriptive data indicate that all PCP-Na-positive samples were wooden cutting boards from farmers’ markets. This pattern suggests that wood material may be a risk factor for PCP-Na contamination, possibly due to the historical use of PCP-Na as a wood preservative. Future studies with larger sample sizes are needed to confirm these findings and explore geographical variations in compliance and enforcement.

Migration pattern of PCP-Na in cutting boards

The artificially treated cutting boards used for the migration study contained a PCP-Na concentration of 176 mg/kg. Throughout ten simulated usage cycles, PCP-Na migration into minced pork displayed a rapid initial decline followed by stabilization. Migration peaked at 18.8 mg/kg during the first cycle, sharply decreased to 7.48 mg/kg in the second cycle, and then followed a power-function decline with increasing use. By the eighth cycle, migration stabilized around 1.78 mg/kg, with minimal variation observed in subsequent cycles (1.74–1.77 mg/kg). This rapid decrease in migration during initial uses, followed by a stable yet persistent residual release, aligns with findings from previous studies on contaminant transfer from food-contact materials. For instance, research on polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons (PAHs) in plastics has reported similar exponential decay patterns37, though absolute migration levels vary substantially based on material and compound properties. The observed stabilization suggests that, while initial exposure poses a higher risk, cumulative exposure remains non-negligible even after multiple uses. These results underscore the importance of considering both initial and long-term migration in assessing the safety of reusable food-contact materials.

Dietary intake risk assessment of PCP-Na migration via pork under simulated cutting board usage

Based on the migration trends observed, the stabilized PCP-Na migration level (eighth cycle) for cutting boards with an average residue of 138 mg/kg was calculated to be 1.40 mg/kg in pork, equivalent to 1.29 mg/kg of PCP after conversion. It should be noted, however, that this study assessed PCP-Na migration under controlled laboratory conditions using pork as the only food simulant. In real-world scenarios, consumer use varies widely with respect to food type (e.g., acidic, fatty, or high-moisture items), cutting intensity, cleaning methods, and frequency of use. These factors may significantly influence the actual migration levels and resulting exposure risks, suggesting that the values reported here should be interpreted within the context of the experimental conditions.

Conclusions

This study confirms the presence of PCP-Na in commercial bamboo and wooden cutting boards, highlighting a meaningful route of human exposure to this persistent organic pollutant. The migration of PCP-Na into food follows a predictable decay trend, characterized by an initially high release that rapidly decreases with repeated use and stabilizes after several cycles. Although the acute dietary intake risk declines quickly with board use, a persistent and widespread carcinogenic risk remains across all usage scenarios and age groups, with adults being particularly vulnerable due to higher exposure levels.

The findings emphasize the necessity of including food-contact materials such as cutting boards in the monitoring and regulation of PCP-Na residues. This study supports the establishment of science-based maximum residue limits (MRLs) and provides practical guidance for consumer safety in the use of wooden and bamboo kitchenware. Moreover, these results can serve as a valuable reference for international trade and food safety standards, helping to align global policies on contaminant migration from food-contact materials.

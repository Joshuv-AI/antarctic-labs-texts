Introduction

For a ring
R
, the set of zero divisors is defined as the set

Z(R)=\{a\in R\setminus\{0\}|\exists b\in R\setminus\{0\}\text{ with }ab=0\}

Beck in
[
Bec88
]
associated a simple graph with any ring
R
by taking its elements as vertices and connecting two distinct vertices
x
and
y
if and only if
xy=0
in
R
. In this graph, the vertex representing
0
is adjacent to all other vertices. We can see that in such graphs, the vertices that correspond to elements of
R\setminus(Z(R)\cup\{0\})
are only adjacent to
0
and therefore they do not contribute to the understanding of the structure of the set of zero divisors. Consequently, D.F. Anderson et al.
[
AL99
]
modified this concept by restricting the set of vertices to only the zero divisors of
R
, maintaining the adjacency rule that
x
and
y
are adjacent if and only if
xy=0
in
R
. This modified graph, known as the zero-divisor graph and denoted by
\Gamma(R)
, has been studied extensively.
[
AM04
,
AL12
,
ALS03
,
AL99
,
Bec88
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

The zero divisor graph
\Gamma(R)
for a ring
R
is defined as the graph with vertex set
V(\Gamma(R))=Z(R)
and
E(\Gamma(R))=\{\{a_{i},a_{j}\}|a_{i},a_{j}\in Z(R)\text{ and }a_{i}a_{j}=0\}
.

For a given family
\mathcal{F}
of sets, the
intersection graph
of
\mathcal{F}
is the graph with
V(G)=\mathcal{F}
and two vertices are adjacent if and only if the corresponding sets intersect.
A
d
-dimensional box(or
d
-box) is a set
[a_{1},b_{1}]\times\cdots[a_{d},b_{d}]
where
[a_{i},b_{i}]
are intervals on the real line. A
d
-dimensional cube is a
d
-box with the constraint
b_{i}-a_{i}=r
for
1\leq i\leq d
for some constant
r\geq 0
. A
d
-dimensional box (or cube) representation
for a graph
G
is a function
f
that maps every vertex
v\in G
to a
d
-box (
d
-cube respectively) such that
uv\in E(G)
if and only if
f(u)\cap f(v)\neq\emptyset
.

Definition 1.2
.

The boxicity of a graph
G

(
denoted as
box(G)
)
is the minimum integer
d\geq 0
such that
G
has a
d
-dimensional box representation.

Definition 1.3
.

The cubicity of a graph
G
(denoted as
cub(G)
) is the minimum integer
d\geq 0
such that
G
has a
d
-dimensional cube representation.

By the above definitions, it follows that the boxicity and cubicity of complete graphs is
0
. The boxicity and cubicity of a graph are related in the following way:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
SA09
]
)
.

For a graph
G
with
n
vertices,
cub(G)\leq\lceil\log_{2}(n)\rceil box(G)

Definition 1.5
.

Interval graphs are the intersection graphs of the families of closed intervals on the real line. We will denote this class by INT.

We will denote an interval graph and its interval representation by the same symbol when there is no confusion. Suppose
I
be an interval representation of an interval graph and
u
be a vertex of the graph. We will denote by
I(u)
the interval corresponding to
u
in the interval representation
I
.

Theorem 1.6

(
[
Rob69
]
)
.

A graph
G
has boxicity at most
l>0
, if and only if it can be represented as the intersection of
l
many interval graphs, i.e.
\text{ there exist interval supergraphs }\{I_{1},...I_{l}\}
of
G
such that
\forall i\in[l]
,
V(I_{i})=V(G)
and
E(G)=E(I_{1})\cap E(I_{2})...\cap E(I_{l})
.

Definition 1.7
.

Let
G_{1}=(V_{1},E_{1}),G_{2}=(V_{2},E_{2})
be graphs. The join of
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, denoted as
G_{1}\vee G_{2}
is the graph
G_{3}=(V_{3},E_{3})
such that
V_{3}=V_{1}\sqcup V_{2}
and
E_{3}=E_{1}\cup E_{2}\cup\{uv:u\in V_{1},v\in V_{2}\}
.

Definition 1.8

(Robert’s Graph)
.

Let
\overline{K_{2}}
, the complement of
K_{2}
, be the graph consisting of two isolated vertices.
\overline{K_{2}}\vee\overline{K_{2}}\cdots\vee\overline{K_{2}}
, the join of
t
copies of
\overline{K_{2}}
is isomorphic to the complement of
t
isolated edges. We denote this graph as
\overline{tK_{2}}
and it is known as Roberts’ graph of order
2t
.

This graph was used by Roberts in his pioneering paper to demonstrate the existence of graphs on vertices with boxicity
\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
.
The following two observations are not hard to show and will be used to prove lower bounds.

Observation 1.9
.

Let
\overline{tK_{2}}
be the Robert’s graph of order
t
. Then
box(\overline{tK_{2}})=t
.

Observation 1.10
.

Let
G
be a graph and
H
be an induced subgraph of
G
. Then
box(G)\geq box(H)
.

Definition 1.11
.

G
is a cograph if it can be constructed from isolated vertices by a sequence of disjoint union and join operations. We will denote this class of graphs as
COG
.

Definition 1.12
.

Split graphs are the graphs in which the vertex set can be partitioned into a clique and an independent set. We will denote this class as
SPLIT
.

Definition 1.13
.

The class of threshold graphs
TH=SPLIT\cap COG
.

The following is an equivalent definition of threshold graphs.

Definition 1.14
.

A graph
G
is a threshold graph if there is a real number
S
(the threshold) and for every vertex
v
there is a real weight
a_{v}
such that:
vw
is an edge if and only if
a_{v}+a_{w}\geq S
.

A general notion of intersection dimension with respect to a graph class was introduced and studied in
[
Kra94
]
.

Definition 1.15

(Intersection Dimension of a Graph
[
Kra94
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
e\in E(G)
. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a class of graphs that contains
K_{n}
and
K_{n}\setminus e

\forall n
. The intersection dimension of
G
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
(denoted as
dim_{\mathcal{A}}(G)
) is defined as follows:

dim_{\mathcal{A}}(G)=\min\left\{k:\exists\text{ }G_{1},...G_{k}\in\mathcal{A}\text{ such that }E(G_{1})\cap E(G_{2})...\cap E(G_{k})=E(G)\right\}

We will study the case when
\mathcal{A}=TH
and when
\mathcal{A}=COG
.
dim_{TH}(G)
is called the threshold dimension of
G
and
dim_{COG}(G)
is called the cograph dimension of
G
. It can be shown that

. Therefore, it follows that for any graph

,
dim_{TH}(G)\geq box(G)
.

Our Results

We show the following:

•

Let
N=\Pi_{i=1}^{a}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
be the prime factorization of
N
with
a\geq 2
. In
[
Kav25
]
, Kavaskar proved that
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq\Pi_{i=1}^{a}(n_{i}+1)-\Pi_{i=1}^{a}(\lfloor n_{i}/2\rfloor+1)-1
.
Our result gives the exact value of the boxicity for
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
in terms of the number of prime divisors of
N
:
We show that when
N\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
N
is not divisible by
p^{3}
for any prime divisor
p
, we have
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))=a-1
. Otherwise
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))=a
.

We also show that
a-1\leq dim_{TH}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq a.

•

Denote the size of the set of minimal prime ideals of
R
as
k
. In
[
Kav25
]
it was shown that
box(\Gamma(R))\leq 2^{k}-2
when
R
is assumed to be a non-zero commutative ring with identity which is also a reduced ring. We improve this by showing

\lfloor k/2\rfloor\leq box(\Gamma(R))\leq dim_{TH}(\Gamma(R))\leq k

with the same assumption on
R
. Note that in
[
Kav25
]
, they show that
box(\Gamma(R))\geq k
but the proof given in the paper is incorrect because of an incorrect assumption(see remark
2.13
for a discussion). Our lower bound uses similar ideas used in
[
Kav25
]
, but avoids any assumption on
\Gamma(R)
.

2.1
Results on
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})

Definition 2.1
.

For any prime
q
we define the function
f(q,x)
to be the non-negative integer such that
q^{f(q,x)}
divides
x
but
q^{f(q,x)+1}
does not divide
x
.

It was shown in
[
Kav25
]
that when
N=p^{n}
for
n\geq 3
,
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))=1
and for
n\leq 2
,
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))=0
. Therefore, we only need to consider the case when
N
has at least 2 prime divisors.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
N=\Pi_{i=1}^{a}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
where
n_{i}>0
and
a\geq 2
. Assume
p_{j}<p_{k}
for
j<k
.

1.

If
N\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
then
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\geq a
.

2.

If
N\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
n_{j}\geq 3
for some
2\leq j\leq a
then
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\geq a
.

3.

If
N\equiv 2\pmod{4}\text{ and }n_{i}\leq 2\text{ for }2\leq i\leq a
then
box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\geq a-1
.

Proof.

We will show that the Robert’s graph
\overline{aK_{2}}
is present as an induced subgraph in
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
for cases
1
and
2
and the Robert’s graph
\overline{(a-1)K_{2}}
is present for case
3
. Then by observation
1.10
we will get our required lower bounds.

(1)

When
N\not\equiv 2\pmod{4}
:

In this case, one of the following is true: (i)
N
is not divisible by
2
or (ii)
p_{1}=2
and
n_{1}\geq 2
.

For every
1\leq i\leq a
, define
T_{i}:=\left\{\frac{N}{p_{i}^{n_{i}}},\frac{2N}{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}\right\}
. It is clear that
T_{i}\subset[N]
since
\frac{2N}{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}<N
. Observe that
T_{i}
is an independent set as an induced subgraph of
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
. Also, it is easy to see that for
i\neq j
,
T_{i}\cup T_{j}
form the complete bipartite graph
K_{2,2}
. Therefore,

\text{}\bigcup_{i=1}^{a}T_{i}\text{ induces the Robert's graph $\overline{aK_{2}}$ in $\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})$}

(2)

When
N\equiv 2\pmod{4}
and
n_{j}\geq 3
for some
2\leq j\leq a
:

In this case,
p_{1}=2
and
n_{1}=1
. Let
k
be the integer such that
n_{k}\geq 3
. Define
T^{\prime}:=\left\{\frac{N}{2},\frac{2N}{2p_{k}}\right\}
and
T^{\prime\prime}:=\left\{\frac{N}{p_{k}^{n_{k}-1}},\frac{2N}{p_{k}^{n_{k}-1}}\right\}
. It is clear that
T^{\prime},T^{\prime\prime}
and
T_{i}
(as defined above) are all subsets of
[N]
as both
\frac{2N}{p_{k}^{n_{k}-1}}
and
\frac{2N}{p_{i}^{n_{i}}}
are smaller than
N
.
T^{\prime}
and
T_{i}
are independent sets in
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
as above. The fact that
T^{\prime\prime}
is independent follows from observing that
\frac{N}{p_{k}^{n_{k}-1}}\cdot\frac{2N}{p_{k}^{n_{k}-1}}=\frac{N^{2}}{{p_{k}^{2n_{k}-2}}}
contains
p_{k}^{2}
as the highest power of
p_{k}
. Also, for
i\neq 1,k
the sets
T^{\prime}\cup T^{\prime\prime},T^{\prime}\cup T_{i},T^{\prime\prime}\cup T_{i}
form complete bipartite graphs
K_{2,2}
. Therefore,

\text{}T^{\prime}\cup T^{\prime\prime}\cup\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}i=2\\
i\neq j\end{subarray}}^{a}T_{i}\text{ induces the Robert's graph $\overline{aK_{2}}$ in $\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})$.}

(3)

When
N\equiv 2\pmod{4}\text{ and }n_{i}\leq 2\text{ for }2\leq i\leq a
:

In this case,
p_{1}=2,n_{1}=1.
Then,

\bigcup_{i=2}^{a}T_{i}\text{ induces the Robert's graph $\overline{(a-1)K_{2}}$ in $\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})$.}

We now provide upper bounds.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
N=\Pi_{i=1}^{a}p_{i}^{n_{i}}
where
n_{i}>0
and assume
p_{j}<p_{k}
for
j<k
. Then

box(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq dim_{TH}({\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})})\leq a

Proof.

Define
g(k):=\frac{k}{2N}

\forall k\in[N]
.

For
1\leq i\leq a
,
0\leq j\leq n_{i}
, let
F_{i,j}=\{u\in[N]:f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(u,N))=j\}
. Note that
f(p_{i},u)=f(p_{i},\gcd(u,N))
when
f(p_{i},u)\leq n_{i}
. When
f(p_{i},u)>n_{i}
, then
f(p_{i},\gcd(u,N))={n_{i}}
. It is easy to see that
\bigsqcup_{j=0}^{n_{i}}F_{i,j}=[N]
.

Define interval graphs
I_{i}
on the vertex set
V(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))
as follows:

I_{i}(v)=\begin{cases}[j+g(v),j+g(v)]&\text{ for }v\in F_{i,j}\text{ and }j<\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil\\[5.0pt]
[n_{i}-j,n_{i}]&\text{ for }v\in F_{i,j}\text{ and }j\geq\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil\\[5.0pt]
\end{cases}

Note that
[j+g(v),j+g(v)]
is a point interval.

Fix
1\leq i\leq a
. Take distinct numbers
u,v\in[N]
and let
f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(u,N))=j_{u}
and
f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(v,N))=j_{v}
. We make the following claims:

Claim 1:

j_{u}+g(u)\neq j_{v}+g(v)

Proof of Claim:
If
j_{u}>j_{v}
, then
j_{u}-j_{v}\geq 1>\frac{1}{2}\geq g(v)-g(u).
The case
j_{v}>j_{u}
is symmetrical.

If
j_{u}=j_{v}
, then
j_{u}-j_{v}=0\neq g(v)-g(u)
as
g(u)\neq g(v)
by definition.

\blacksquare

Claim 2:

I_{i}(u)
and
I_{i}(v)
intersect if and only if
j_{u}+j_{v}\geq n_{i}
.

Proof of Claim:

‘If’ part of the claim:

Suppose
j_{u}+j_{v}\geq n_{i}
. At most one of
j_{u}
or
j_{v}
can be strictly smaller than
\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
.

•

If
j_{u}\geq\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
and
j_{v}\geq\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
: In this case,
I(u)=[n_{i}-j_{u},n_{i}]
and
I(v)=[n_{i}-j_{v},n_{i}]
. Both these intervals contain
n_{i}
in their intersection.

•

Now without loss of generality, let
j_{u}<\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
and
j_{v}\geq\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
: In this case,
I(u)=[j_{u}+g(u),j_{u}+g(u)]
and
I(v)=[n_{i}-j_{v},n_{i}]
. By assumption
j_{u}+j_{v}\geq n_{i}
. Therefore,
n_{i}\geq j_{u}+g(u)\geq n_{i}-j_{v}+g(u)\geq n_{i}-j_{v}
. So the intervals intersect.

This proves the ‘if’ part.

‘Only if’ part of the claim:
Suppose
j_{u}+j_{v}<n_{i}
. Then at least one of
j_{u}
or
j_{v}
is strictly smaller than
\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
.

•

If
j_{u}<\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
and
j_{v}<\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
: In this case,
I(u)=[j_{u}+g(u),j_{u}+g(u)]
and
I(v)=[j_{v}+g(v),j_{v}+g(v)]
. By claim 1, the intervals do not intersect.

•

Now, without loss of generality let
j_{u}<\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
and
j_{v}\geq\lceil\frac{n_{i}}{2}\rceil
: In this case,
I(u)=[j_{u}+g(u),j_{u}+g(u)]
and
I(v)=[n_{i}-j_{v},n_{i}]
. By assumption
j_{u}+j_{v}\leq n_{i}-1
. Therefore,
j_{u}+g(u)\leq n_{i}-j_{v}+g(u)-1<n_{i}-j_{v}
. So the intervals do not intersect.

This finishes the proof of claim 2.

\blacksquare

Remark 2.4
.

Recall the definition of threshold graphs (
1.14
). We claim that each
I_{i}
defined as above is a threshold graph. To see this, in definition
1.14
, take
S=n_{i}
and
a_{u}=j_{u}
for each vertex

. By claim 2

satisfies the definition of threshold graphs.

By the above two claims, it can be shown that
E(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))=E(I_{1})\cap E(I_{2})\cdots\cap E(I_{a})
. Indeed, if
u,v\in[N]
are adjacent in
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
then
N
divides
uv
. Consequently, for each
1\leq i\leq a
,
p_{i}^{n_{i}}
divides

. This means that
f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(u,N))+f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(v,N))\geq n_{i}
for all

. It follows by claim 2 that
u
and

are adjacent in
I_{i}
for all
1\leq i\leq a
. Therefore, the threshold graphs

are all supergraphs of
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
. On the other hand, if
u
and
v
are not adjacent in
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
, then
\exists

i
such that
f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(u,N))+f(p_{i},\text{gcd}(v,N))<n_{i}
. Again, by claim 2,
u
and

. Hence, we have showed that

. For distinct numbers

and

. We will construct interval graphs

1.

For

, we construct an interval graph

that satisfies the following: for every pair of distinct vertices

2.2
Results on
\Gamma(R)
for a reduced ring
R

A reduced ring
R
is a ring for which
x\in R
,
x^{2}=0\implies x=0
. In this section, we will consider the case when
R
is a non-zero finite commutative ring with identity with the extra property that it is also a reduced ring.

We can define an equivalence relation
\sim_{E}
on any graph
G
such that for
x,y\in V(G),
we have
x\sim_{E}y
if and only if
N(x)=N(y)
. If
G
is a simple graph then it has no self loops and so
\sim_{E}
partitions
V(G)
into equivalence classes
P=\{[x_{1}],[x_{2}],\cdots[x_{k}]\}
for some
k
such that
[x_{i}]
induces an independent set in
G
for every
1\leq i\leq k
.

Definition 2.11
.

The reduced graph
G_{E}
of a graph
G
is defined as the graph with
V(G_{E})=P
and
E(G_{E})=\{[x][y]\text{ }|\text{ }xy\in E(G)\}
.

Following the above notation, we will denote the reduced graph of
\Gamma(R)
as
\Gamma_{E}(R)
.

Notational comment: Reduced rings should not be confused with the reduced zero divisor graphs of a ring.

Since we will be working with reduced rings
R
, for every
x\in R
we have
x^{2}\neq 0
and therefore
\Gamma(R)
does not have self loops. Therefore, the equivalence classes of
\Gamma(R)
under
\sim_{E}
are all independent sets. In fact, it is not hard to see that for distinct vertices
u,v\in V(\Gamma(R)),

uv\in E(\Gamma(R))
if and only if
[u][v]\in E(\Gamma_{E}(R))
.

Theorem 2.12
.

Let
R
be a non-zero finite commutative ring with identity which is also a reduced ring. Then

\lfloor k/2\rfloor\leq box(\Gamma(R))\leq dim_{TH}(\Gamma(R))\leq k,

where
k
is the number of minimal prime ideals of
R
.

Proof.

From
[
AL12
]
we know that there exists an isomorphism
\phi:\Gamma_{E}(R)\rightarrow\Gamma(R^{\prime})
, where
R^{\prime}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{k}=\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times...\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(
k
-copies). Note that,
V(\Gamma(R^{\prime}))=(\Pi_{i=1}^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{2})\setminus\{\mathbb{1},\mathbb{0}\}
where
\mathbb{1},\mathbb{0}
are the all ones and all zeroes vectors. This isomorphism allows us to view
V(\Gamma_{E}(R))
as vectors consisting of
0
s and
1
s such that distinct vertices
x,y\in V(\Gamma_{E}(R))
are adjacent in
\Gamma_{E}(R)
if and only if the point-wise product of the corresponding vectors
\phi(x)
and
\phi(y)
equals the zero vector.

Let
\pi_{i}:\Gamma(R^{\prime})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{2}
be the function that returns the
i
th coordinate. Using
\pi_{i}
, we can rewrite the above observation as follows: for vertices
x,y\in V(\Gamma_{E}(R))
,
xy\notin E(\Gamma_{E}(R))
if and only if
\exists

i
such that
\pi_{i}\circ\phi(x)=\pi_{i}\circ\phi(y)=1
, i.e. for both the vectors the
i
th component is
1
.

Define the sets
A_{i}=\{x\in\Gamma_{E}(R):\pi_{i}\circ\phi(x)=1\}
. Then the induced graph on the vertices of
A_{i}
is an independent set in
\Gamma_{E}(R)
for each
1\leq i\leq k
. Thus by the above discussion
xy\notin E(\Gamma_{E}(R))
if and only if
\exists

i
such that
x,y\in A_{i}

Fix an injective function
g:\Gamma(R)\rightarrow[0,1]
. For each
i
, define the graph
I_{i}
on
V(\Gamma(R))
as follows:

I_{i}(v)=\begin{cases}[g(v),g(v)]&\text{ if }[v]\in A_{i}\\
[0,1]&\text{otherwise }\end{cases}

The following claim is easy to see:

Claim 5:

For two distinct vertices
u,v\in V(\Gamma(R))
,
uv\notin E(I_{i})
if and only if both
[u],[v]\in A_{i}
.

\blacksquare

We will show that
E(\Gamma(R))=E(I_{1})\cap E(I_{2})\cdots\cap E(I_{k})
.

Fix
u,v\in\Gamma(R)
. It is easy to verify the following chain of equivalences:

\displaystyle uv\in E(\Gamma(R))

\displaystyle\iff[u][v]\in E(\Gamma_{E}(R))

(1)

\displaystyle\iff\phi([u])\phi([v])\in E(\Gamma(R^{\prime}))

(2)

\displaystyle\iff\forall\text{ }1\leq i\leq k,\text{ }\pi_{i}(\phi([u]))\cdot\pi_{i}(\phi([u]))=0

(3)

\displaystyle\iff\forall\text{ }1\leq i\leq k,\text{ }\text{at least one of }[u]\text{ or }[v]\text{ belongs to }A_{i}^{c}.

(4)

By claim 5 and implication (4), it follows that if
uv\in E(\Gamma(R))
then
uv\in E(I_{i})

\forall

1\leq i\leq k
and if
uv\notin E(\Gamma(R))
then
\exists

i
such that
uv\notin E(I_{i})
. Therefore,
E(\Gamma(R))=E(I_{1})\cap E(I_{2})\cdots\cap E(I_{k})
.

Each interval graph
I_{i}
is also a threshold graph. To see this, assign the vertex
v
the weight
a_{v}=0
if
[v]\in A_{i}
and
a_{v}=1
otherwise. By claim 5,
u
and
v
are adjacent in
I_{i}
if and only if
a_{u}+a_{v}\geq 1
. By definition
1.14
,
I_{i}
are threshold graphs. This proves the upper bound.

Finally, suppose
e_{j}
be the
j
th standard basis vector, i.e the vector with
1
at the
j
th position and
0
everywhere else. Define the set
B_{i}=\{e_{2i-1},e_{2i-1}+e_{2i}\}
for
1\leq i\leq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
.

Observe that
\bigsqcup_{i=1}^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}B_{i}
induces the Robert’s graph
\overline{\lfloor k/2\rfloor K_{2}}
in
\Gamma(R^{\prime})
. This is easy to see since
B_{i}
induces an independent set for each
i\in[\lfloor k/2\rfloor]
and for
i\neq j
all edges are present between
B_{i}
and
B_{j}
. So
box(\Gamma(R))\geq box(\Gamma_{E}(R))=box(\Gamma(R^{\prime}))\geq\lfloor k/2\rfloor
. This gives the lower bound.
∎

Remark 2.13
.

In
[
Kav25
]
, it was shown that
box(\Gamma(R))\geq k
. The proof for the lower bound assumed a fact which is not always true. The proof goes as follows:

They begin with the fact that
\Gamma_{E}(R)\cong\Gamma(R^{\prime})
, where
R^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\cdots\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(
k
times). Recall that the vertices of
\Gamma_{E}(R)
are equivalence classes of vertices of
\Gamma(R)
under the equivalence relation
u\sim_{E}v
if
N(u)=N(v)
. Suppose the isomorphism between
\Gamma_{E}(R)
and
\Gamma(R^{\prime})
is given by
\phi
. Let
e_{i}
be the vector consisting of
1
at the
i
th position and
0
elsewhere. For
1\leq i\leq k
, consider the subgraphs
G_{i}
induced by the equivalence classes
[\phi^{-1}(e_{i})]
in
\Gamma(R)
. Note that for
1\leq i\leq k
,
G_{i}
is a graph with no edges, i.e. it is an independent set in
\Gamma(R)
. The mistake in the proof comes from assuming that for these graphs
G_{i}
,
box(G_{i})=1
. With this incorrect assumption, they argue that the graph induced by the set
V(G_{1})\cup V(G_{2})\cdots\cup V(G_{k})
has boxicity
k
. Since this graph exists as an induced subgraph of
\Gamma(R)
, they argue that
box(\Gamma(R))\geq k
.

We provide a counterexample for the assumption that
box(G_{i})=1
for all
1\leq i\leq k
. Observe that this is true only when
|V(G_{i})|>1
. Consequently, it suffices to show that there exist graphs
\Gamma(R)
such that for some
1\leq i\leq k
,
|V(G_{i})|=1
.

Take
N=2\times\Pi_{i=2}^{k}p_{i}
and
R=\mathbb{Z}_{N}
. Note that
R
is a reduced graph as every
u\in\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
has at least one prime factor missing and so
u^{2}\not\equiv 0\pmod{N}
. The elements of
\Gamma_{E}(R)
are all the divisors of
N
(see
[
Kav25
]
for a proof). Therefore the elements of
\Gamma_{E}(R)
are of the form
2^{\alpha_{1}}\times\Pi_{i=2}^{k}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}
where
\alpha_{i}\in\{0,1\}
for
1\leq i\leq k
. Define
\phi:\Gamma_{E}(R)\rightarrow\Gamma(R^{\prime})
given by
\phi(2^{\alpha_{1}}\times\Pi_{i=2}^{k}p_{i}^{\alpha_{i}})=(1-\alpha_{1},1-\alpha_{2},\cdots,1-\alpha_{k})
. It is easy to verify that the map
\phi
is an isomorphism. Observe that
\phi^{-1}(e_{i})=\frac{N}{p_{i}}
. Therefore, the equivalence class
[\phi^{-1}(e_{i})]
is the set
\left\{\frac{N}{p_{i}},\frac{2N}{p_{i}},\cdots,\frac{tN}{p_{i}}\right\}
where
t=p_{i}-1
. In particular, the equivalence class
[\phi^{-1}(e_{1})]=\left\{\frac{N}{2}\right\}
is a singleton set. It follows that the graph
G_{1}
induced by
[\phi^{-1}(e_{1})]
is an isolated vertex and hence
box(G_{1})=0
. This contradicts that
box(G_{i})=1
for
1\leq i\leq k
.

3
Conclusion

In this paper, we have exactly determined the boxicity of
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
. In corollary
2.9
we have shown that
\frac{\log(\lfloor\sqrt{N/p_{1}^{2}}\rfloor-1)}{2}\leq cub(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq a\lceil\log N\rceil
. In
[
AC09
]
, it was shown that for an interval graph
G
, we have

cub(G)\leq\lceil\log(\alpha(G))\rceil
where
\alpha(G)
is the size of the largest independent set in
G
. Using this result and with a little more work, it can be shown that

cub(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq a\log N+\sum_{j=1}^{a}\left\{\log\left(1-\prod_{i=1}^{a}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{p_{j}^{\lceil n_{j}/2\rceil}}\right),\right\}

by finding the largest independent set of every interval graph defined in the proof of lemma
2.3
. We omit the proof since it is not a significant improvement. Note that the second term in the upper bound is negative. Similarly, the lower bound can be improved to

cub(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\geq\frac{1}{2}\log N+\frac{1}{2}\max_{j\in[a]}\log\left(1-\prod_{i=1}^{a}\left(1-\frac{1}{p_{i}}\right)-\frac{1}{p_{j}^{\lceil n_{j}/2\rceil}}\right)

by the same kind of argument as in corollary
2.9
. It is an interesting open problem to find the exact cubicity of this graph. More precisely, we pose the following problem,

Question 3.1
.

Is it possible to show
\Omega(\log N)\leq cub(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq O(a+\log N).

In this paper, we have provided bounds for
box(\Gamma(R))
. When
R
is a non-zero commutative ring with identity that is also a reduced ring and taking
k
to be the size of the set of minimal prime ideals of
R
, we have showed that
\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\rfloor\leq box(\Gamma(R))\leq dim_{TH}(\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N}))\leq k
.
It is an interesting question to ask if the gap between the lower bound and the upper bound can be closed.

We also wonder whether any lower bounds for cograph dimension can be obtained for both
\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{N})
and
\Gamma(R)
.

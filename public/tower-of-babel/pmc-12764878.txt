AI-embedded IoT healthcare optimization with trust-aware mobile edge computing
Authors: Malak Alamri, Khalid Haseeb, Mamoona Humayun, Menwa Alshammeri
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Embedded technologies combined with the Internet of Things (IoT), have transformed healthcare monitoring systems into automated and responsive platforms. In recent decades, many existing approaches have been based on edge computing to reduce response time in patient monitoring and provide a reliable method for interaction among the medical team and experts during disease diagnosis. Such approaches are the interconnection of battery-powered devices and physical objects to capture the physiological data streams for medical treatment and facilitate personalized healthcare systems. However, as wireless devices have limited resources for fulfilling end-user requests, this affects the accuracy of the medical system, especially in the presence of malicious devices on the communication infrastructure. Under diverse network conditions, such solutions lower the reliability level of the devices and increase the likelihood of suspicious processes. Therefore, to keep these significant concerns in IoT-based healthcare applications, trust and security should be adopted while collecting patients’ data over an insecure medium. In this research study, we propose a model referred to as Edge-Cloud Trusted Intelligence (ECTI), aiming to decrease the computing overhead on the devices. Additionally, multi-level security is implemented to ensure privacy preservation by adopting trusted behavior when communicating in a distributed environment. The edges utilize resources efficiently by employing task offloading strategies, enabling lightweight collaborative decision-making for routing in the healthcare domain. The performance results revealed notable improvement of the proposed model against related schemes in terms of various network metrics.

Introduction

Embedded healthcare applications have shown significant capabilities for real-time monitoring of patient treatment1,2. An edge network integrated with cloud computing enhances storage and processing capabilities, making communication channels less congested3–5. Such a system provides pre-processed data and continues to operate even in the event of disconnection or network interruption issues. Due to local processing at the edge, most health service providers provide rapid actions to medical experts for decision-making in emergency responses6,7. Many existing approaches leverage the intelligence of machine learning for data analytics and reduce resource consumption by utilizing minimal computational power in battery-powered devices8,9. The collaboration of distributed networks and sensor-based applications supports demanding and most critical operations in smart cities10,11. The hybrid nature of embedded modules not only processes the required system functionality on time but also enhances the performance in terms of responsiveness for health informatics12,13. Although, because of the openness of wireless systems and the interaction of heterogeneous devices, the chances of malicious threats lead to compromising the network infrastructure and increase the probabilities of data damage and breaches. Moreover, resources are often misused in responding to faulty requests; thus, security with a high level of trust is a significant research problem14,15. The joint interaction of data security and device trust enhances the communication paradigm’s reliability and facilitates adaptive decision-making while mitigating malicious behavior16,17. To address massive IoT data, it is filtered and preprocessed at the network edges, and transmission links are intelligently utilized to fulfill the demands of medical users for data analysis18,19. However, most of the proposed solutions explored edge computing with the support of cloud processing functionalities. However, the timely response, efficient utilization of network resources, along with security and trust, remain unsolved for complex computational applications, leading to threats and denying crucial healthcare services in a fault-tolerant manner20–22. Related work

In modern healthcare systems, distributed IoT sensors enable continuous monitoring of patient vitals across diverse environments, from hospitals to remote care settings23,24. Healthcare applications are integrated with edge and cloud computing to develop a real-time decision support system25,26. They can interact with remote users and medical experts with the support of wearable sensors to capture the patient conditions and transmit them to processing servers for timely diagnosis and personalized treatment27,28. The local processing of edges enhances the responsiveness of the critical system and maintain the data resilience in the event of threats by secured offloading techniques29,30. In31, authors explore both fuzzy logic systems and the Whale Optimization Algorithm (WOA) to introduce a secure protocol for healthcare systems. A optimal cluster heads are selected using a WOA-based clustering framework to increase the stability of communication. Additionally, the trustworthiness of devices is assessed by evaluating a fuzzy-enabled trust mechanism and ensuring reliable data transmission within the IoT network. According to the performance results, the proposed protocol enhanced the energy efficiency of the healthcare system while also improving data security. To achieve privacy and authentic access control, the study32 introduces an Attribute Trust-based Security (ATST) algorithm, which aims to secure the transmission among connected devices and provide a more reliable environment. It guarantees secure message encryption and explores user attributes to develop a trust computational model. The ATST allows only authentic and authorized devices to be included in data encryption and decryption using a dual trust mechanism. The authors33 introduce a cross-layer and Cryptography-based Secure Routing (CLCSR) protocol to attain data privacy and protect the network from attacks. It is divided into two main stages: Firstly, a secure clustering mechanism is produced, and secondly, lightweight cryptography methods are exploited for secure data transmission. Triple cross-layer parameters are used for the selection of optimal cluster heads, which increases the network lifetime with improved load balancing. A fuzzy logic-based secure hierarchical routing scheme using the firefly algorithm (FSRF) is proposed34, aiming to enhance the trust of the healthcare applications. The three main frameworks are constructed: a fuzzy trust framework, a firefly algorithm-based clustering framework, and an inter-cluster routing framework. Initially, trust is computed using fuzzy logic and improves the reliability of the communicating devices. Secondly, it protects the systems against routing attacks and reduces the additional energy consumption for the network operations. Lastly, based on the fitness function, which comprises trust level, residual energy, hop count, communication radius, and centrality parameters, an optimum set of nodes is selected for the competition of cluster head. The results analysis has shown a significant improvement of the FSRF for various performance metrics. To address this risk, authors35 propose Adaptive Epsilon Greedy Reinforcement Learning (AEGRL), a variant of the traditional Epsilon-Greedy method, designed to secure data in both static and dynamic environments efficiently. In36, authors explore mobile edge computing for data aggregation from faultless device and proposed a trust-aware routing mechanism (TARM) . Normal and fault devices are identified using trust computation and enhances the confidence of IoT system. It also make ise of modified gray wolf optimization (GWO) for the construction of cluster and later for particular cluster heads an appropriate trust values are determined. Authors37 investigate the application of network coding in conjunction with opportunistic routing to enhance energy efficiency in wireless IoT infrastructure, taking into account of link correlation. Existing routing mechanisms assume link independence, leading to estimation errors in transmission calculations. To address this, authors propose an intra-session network coding mechanism that mines link correlation, along with an innovative routing method and an algorithm for selecting an optimal forwarder set based on accurate transmission estimations. The precision and recall metrics in Figs. 11 and 12 are crucial for evaluating the performance of our algorithm under faulty nodes. Precision measures the proportion of correctly identified nodes as functional out of all nodes identified as functional by the algorithm. On the other hand, recall evaluates the algorithm’s ability to identify all functional nodes, including those that are operational but may be incorrectly classified as faulty. In the presence of faulty nodes, a trade-off between precision and recall becomes evident, where improving one often comes at the expense of the other. To handle such trade-offs, our algorithm dynamically adjusts its decision thresholds based on network conditions, enhancing the robustness of the fault detection process. Conclusion

Intelligent techniques are utilized by many IoT devices for sensing and transmitting data in smart cities. These networks are integrated with sensors, digital services, and many processing sub-systems to communicate with the physical world. The integrated devices gather real-time data from the environment and forward it to remote users. Ultimately, easing the monitoring process with minimal human interference and facilitates the socialites. Although many approaches have been developed for smart healthcare, the majority of them are unable to cope with scalability and incur additional processing overheads for medical requests from IoT devices. Moreover, security is another significant research objective for many existing solutions. In this research, we propose a model utilizing edge–cloud interaction for Trust-aware communication in the healthcare industry. The intelligence is applied at the edge using an AI-driven approach to process the medical data locally and generate decisions with lower data latency. The trusts are computed mutually among devices to enhance the positive reputation for the transmission and provide a privacy-preserving environment against threats. Although the proposed model is limited to the availability of resources for optimizing healthcare. In future work, we aim to extend the overall performance of the proposed model by integrating SDN-based detection of distributed attacks and exploring some deep learning models to test the proposed model with realistic and large-scale datasets.

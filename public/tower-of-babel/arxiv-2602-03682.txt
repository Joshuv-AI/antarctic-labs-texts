Improved Analysis of the Accelerated Noisy Power Method with Applications to Decentralized PCA
Pierre Aguié, Mathieu Even, Laurent Massoulié

Abstract
We analyze the Accelerated Noisy Power Method, an algorithm for Principal Component Analysis in the setting where only inexact matrix-vector products are available, which can arise for instance in decentralized PCA. While previous works have established that acceleration can improve convergence rates compared to the standard Noisy Power Method, these guarantees require overly restrictive upper bounds on the magnitude of the perturbations, limiting their practical applicability. We provide an improved analysis of this algorithm, which preserves the accelerated convergence rate under much milder conditions on the perturbations. We show that our new analysis is worst-case optimal, in the sense that the convergence rate cannot be improved, and that the noise conditions we derive cannot be relaxed without sacrificing convergence guarantees. We demonstrate the practical relevance of our results by deriving an accelerated algorithm for decentralized PCA, which has similar communication costs to non-accelerated methods. To our knowledge, this is the first decentralized algorithm for PCA with provably accelerated convergence.

Introduction

Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is a ubiquitous task in machine learning and statistics. Given a symmetric positive semidefinite matrix
\mathbf{A}\succeq\mathbf{0}
and a target rank
k
, the goal is to estimate the subspace spanned by the
k
leading eigenvectors. The Power Method
(
8
)
is a popular algorithm for PCA that iteratively refines an estimate of this subspace, using only matrix-vector products with
\mathbf{A}
. Such algorithms are called
matrix-free
, in that they do not require explicit access to
\mathbf{A}
but only to the product
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
. This operation can be done at a low computational cost even when
\mathbf{A}
is very large if it has a favorable structure, such as sparsity or a low-rank factorization. More recently, there has been a growing interest in studying algorithms for PCA in cases where only an approximate matrix-vector product
\mathbf{x}\mapsto\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}+\boldsymbol{\xi}
is available, where
\boldsymbol{\xi}
is a perturbation of
bounded magnitude
.

Such inexact matrix-vector products naturally arise in various practical scenarios. For instance, in private PCA
(
5
)
, random noise is added to
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
to ensure privacy. In stochastic or streaming PCA
(
31
)
,
\mathbf{A}
is the covariance matrix of a distribution, and only noisy estimates of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{x}
can be computed using samples from this distribution. In decentralized PCA
(
29
)
, a network of agents each holding a local matrix
\mathbf{A}_{i}
seek to estimate the top-
k
eigenspace of the average matrix
\mathbf{A}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{A}_{i}
, by only exchanging information with their neighbors, and with no central aggregating server. The agents can then only compute approximate matrix-vector products, where in that case, the approximation error
\boldsymbol{\xi}
stems from the limited communication between the agents. We stress that in all of these examples, there is a trade-off between the magnitude of the noise
\boldsymbol{\xi}
and the strength of the external constraints: in private PCA, stronger privacy guarantees require larger noises; in stochastic PCA, the noise magnitude increases with smaller sample sizes; in decentralized PCA, limited communication budgets lead to larger approximation errors.

9

\varepsilon

\varepsilon
is the target precision of the estimate. The convergence rate of
9

k
and
(k+1)
-th eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}
are very close.
33

(
24
)
. This represents a significant speedup for poorly conditioned matrices, which often appear in practice
(
20
)
. However, their analysis requires the noise magnitude to be
\varepsilon^{\mu}
-small, where
\mu
is a very large power for ill-conditioned problems. Their conditions are thus significantly more restrictive than those of
9

Table

The relationship between the target precision and the magnitude of the noise leads to a trade-off between the utility of the estimate given by the algorithm and the constraints of the problem. It is as such crucial to have noise conditions that are as mild as possible, in order to allow for accurate estimates even under strong system constraints. As an example, while non-accelerated algorithms for decentralized PCA exist in the literature
(
29
;
34
)
, we are not aware of any algorithm for decentralized PCA that converges at accelerated rates. We believe that this hole in the literature is due to the overly restrictive noise conditions required by existing analyses of accelerated methods, which prevent their application to decentralized PCA under reasonable communication budgets. Note that in these scenarios, the number of iterations required by these algorithms also leads to increased costs in terms of privacy loss, communication rounds or number of samples used. Acceleration is thus essential to improve the utility-cost trade-off of those algorithms.

1.2
Our Contributions

We propose a novel analysis of the Accelerated Noisy Power Method, which preserves the guarantee of an accelerated convergence rate under milder noise conditions than those given in previous works. Our contributions are as follows:

(i)
We provide new guarantees for the Accelerated Noisy Power Method. Just like in
33
’s work, our analysis shows that the algorithm converges at a rate linear in
1/\sqrt{\Delta_{k}}
. However, our noise conditions are the same as those of
9
for the non-accelerated Noisy Power Method, and are significantly milder than those of
33
in cases where the eigengap
\Delta_{k}
is small (see
Table

1
for a comparison).

(ii)
We show that our analysis is worst-case optimal up to constants: there are instances of the algorithm which converge at a rate slower than
1/\sqrt{\Delta_{k}}
, and there are instances verifying relaxed versions of our noise conditions that do not converge to the target precision.

(iii)
We use our analysis to derive an accelerated algorithm for decentralized PCA, which has similar communication costs to non-accelerated methods (see
Table

2
for a comparison with other decentralized algorithms for PCA). To our knowledge, this is the first decentralized algorithm for PCA with accelerated convergence.

We stress that in our work and those of
9
and
33
, the perturbations
\boldsymbol{\xi}
are not assumed to be stochastic, but rather to be
adversarial
and of
bounded norm
.

Notations

For a positive semidefinite matrix (PSD)
\mathbf{A}\succeq\mathbf{0}
, we denote by
\lambda_{1}\geqslant\lambda_{2}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\lambda_{d}\geqslant 0

\mathbf{U}_{-k}:=[\mathbf{u}_{k+1},\dots,\mathbf{u}_{d}]

\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}:=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{k+1},\dots,\lambda_{d})

For all integers

the set of

\mathbf{Y}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k}
, we denote by
\mathrm{QR}(\mathbf{Y})
the QR decomposition of
\mathbf{Y}
, which is a couple of matrices
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{R}
such that
\mathbf{Y}=\mathbf{X}\mathbf{R}
,
\mathbf{X}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)
and
\mathbf{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{k\times k}
is an upper triangular matrix with non-negative diagonal coefficients. If
\mathbf{Y}
is of full column rank, the QR decomposition is unique,
\mathbf{R}
is invertible, and its diagonal coefficients are positive
(
28
)
.
\|\cdot\|_{2}
and
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{F}}
denote the matrix spectral and Frobenius norms respectively. For a matrix
\mathbf{X}
, we denote by
\sigma_{\mathrm{min}}(\mathbf{X})
its smallest singular value, and by
\mathbf{X}^{\dagger}
its Moore-Penrose pseudoinverse.

1.4
Principal Angles Between Subspaces

In this work, we study algorithms that aim to approximate linear subspaces of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
\mathbf{X},\mathbf{U}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)
, we quantify the distance between
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{X})
and
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{U})
with principal angles between subspaces.

Definition 1.1

(
12
)
.

Let
k\in\{1,\dots,d-1\}
and
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)
, and let
1\geqslant\sigma_{1}\geqslant\dots\geqslant\sigma_{k}\geqslant 0
be the singular values of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
. The principal angles
\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})\leqslant\dots\leqslant\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
between the subspaces spanned by the columns of
\mathbf{U}
and
\mathbf{X}
are defined as

\displaystyle\forall i\in\{1,\dots,k\},\quad\theta_{i}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}):=\arccos(\sigma_{i})\in[0,\pi/2].

Intuitively,
\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
is the largest possible angle that can be made between a line in
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{U})
and a line in
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{X})
, both passing through the origin. Notice that
\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})=0
if and only if
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{U})
and
\mathrm{range}(\mathbf{X})
coincide, and that the smaller
\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
is, the closer the subspaces are. In line with previous works on noisy power methods
(
9
;
1
;
33
)
, our convergence results are expressed in terms of
\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})
.

2
Accelerated Noisy Power Method

We now introduce the Accelerated Noisy Power Method (ANPM). We want to estimate the top-
k
eigenspace
\mathbf{U}_{k}
of
\mathbf{A}\succeq\mathbf{0}
. However, we assume that we only have access to the approximate product
\mathbf{X}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)\mapsto\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Xi}
is a perturbation. Given a sequence of perturbations
\{\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
, ANPM with momentum parameter
\beta>0
is given by

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{0}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k),\quad\mathbf{X}_{1},\;\mathbf{R}_{1}=\mathrm{QR}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}\right),

\displaystyle\forall t\geqslant 1,\quad\begin{cases}&\mathbf{Y}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t},\\
&\mathbf{X}_{t+1},\;\mathbf{R}_{t+1}=\mathrm{QR}(\mathbf{Y}_{t+1}).\end{cases}

(1)

Before presenting our main result, we briefly explain the idea behind the momentum term
-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{-1}
. In the noiseless case (i.e.
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\equiv\mathbf{0}
), the unnormalized iterates
\mathbf{Z}_{t}:=\mathbf{X}_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t}\cdots\mathbf{R}_{1}
can be written as
\mathbf{Z}_{t}=p_{t}(\mathbf{A})\mathbf{X}_{0}
, where
p_{t}
is a degree-
t
scaled Chebyshev polynomial of the first kind, verifying
p_{0}(x)=1
,
p_{1}(x)=x/2
and

\displaystyle p_{t+1}(x)=xp_{t}(x)-\beta p_{t-1}(x).

(2)

Compared to the monomial
x^{t}
that would be obtained without momentum (i.e. with the standard Power Method),
p_{t}
offers a significantly more favorable ratio between its magnitude on the interval
[-2\sqrt{\beta},2\sqrt{\beta}]
and its growth outside of it. This property is formally stated in the next result:

Proposition 2.1
.

For all
t\geqslant 1
,
p_{t}

(
2
) satisfies

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)=\arg\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\in\mathbb{R}[x]\\
\mathrm{deg}(p)=t,\,\mathrm{lc}(p)=1/2\end{subarray}}\max_{x\in[-2\sqrt{\beta},2\sqrt{\beta}]}|p(x)|,

where
\mathrm{lc}(p)
denotes the leading coefficient of
p
.

Assuming that the interval
[-2\sqrt{\beta},2\sqrt{\beta}]
contains only the eigenvalues of
\mathbf{A}
smaller than
\lambda_{k}

implies that the polynomial

\mathbf{A}

Convergence rate.
Under our assumptions, the convergence rate of ANPM matches the rate of the noiseless Power Method with Momentum given in Corollary 2 of
31

Noise conditions.
Our conditions on the noise

match with those of the Noisy Power Method given in

3.1

The method we propose for decentralized PCA is based on the idea of approximating at each iteration the matrix vector product

5
Conclusion

We provided convergence guarantees for ANPM, showing that it converges at an accelerated rate under milder noise conditions than previous analyses. We showed that our analysis is tight, and applied our results to design ADePM, an accelerated algorithm for decentralized PCA with comparable communication costs to non-accelerated decentralized algorithms. While our work is mainly of theoretical nature, our experimental results show that using heuristics to adaptively tune the momentum can in practice lead to significant speedups over non-accelerated methods, without requiring manual parameter tuning.

A.1
Formulas for Principal Angles Between Subspaces

We provide here useful formulas for the cosines, sines and tangents of the principal angles between two subspaces in terms of their orthonormal bases. These formulas will be used extensively in our proofs, in particular to control the evolution of
\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})
along the iterations of the algorithms we study.

Proposition A.1

(
26
, Corollary 5.4)
.

Let
\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)
, and let
\mathbf{V}\in\mathrm{St}(d,d-k)
be a matrix whose columns span the orthogonal complement of the range of
\mathbf{U}
. Then,

\displaystyle\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})=\sigma_{\min}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}),

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})=\|\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}\|_{2}.

If
\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})>0
,
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
is invertible and

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})=\|(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X})(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X})^{-1}\|_{2}.

We give here a simple proof of these formulas for completeness.

Proof.

By definition of the
k
-th principal angle, we have that
\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})=\arccos(\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}))
, where
\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X})
is the
k
-th largest (i.e. the smallest) singular value of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
. This proves the first part of the proposition.

Let
\mathbf{P}\in\mathrm{St}(k,k),\mathbf{Q}\in\mathrm{St}(k,k)
the left and right singular vectors of
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
such that
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{P}\mathbf{\Sigma}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}
, where
\mathbf{\Sigma}:=\mathrm{diag}(\sigma_{1}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}),\dots,\sigma_{k}(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}))=\mathrm{diag}(\cos\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}),\dots,\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}))
. Since

spans the orthogonal complement of the range of
\mathbf{U}
, we have

. Then, we can check that the right singular vectors of
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
are also
\mathbf{Q}
and that its singular values are
\sigma_{i}(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X})=\sin\theta_{i}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{V}\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{X}^{\top}(\mathbf{I}_{d}-\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top})\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{I}_{k}-\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{U}\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{Q}(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\mathbf{\Sigma}^{2})\mathbf{Q}^{\top},

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}\mathrm{diag}\left(1-\cos^{2}\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}),\dots,1-\cos^{2}\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})\right)\mathbf{Q}^{\top}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{Q}{\underbrace{\mathrm{diag}\left(\sin\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}),\dots,\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})\right)}_{\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}}}^{2}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}.

From this, we deduce that the largest singular value of
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
is
\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
.

Then, assuming that
\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})>0
,
\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
’s smallest singular value is positive and it is thus invertible. Note
\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{St}(d-k,k)
the left singular vectors of
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
such that
\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X}=\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}\mathbf{Q}^{\top}
. We then have

\displaystyle(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X})(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{\prime}{\mathbf{Q}^{\top}\mathbf{Q}}\mathbf{\Sigma}^{-1}\mathbf{P}^{\top}=\mathbf{P}^{\prime}\mathrm{diag}\left(\frac{\sin\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})}{\cos\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})},\dots,\frac{\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})}{\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})}\right)\mathbf{P}^{\top}.

As such, the singular values of
(\mathbf{V}^{\top}\mathbf{X})(\mathbf{U}^{\top}\mathbf{X})^{-1}
are
\tan\theta_{1}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X}),\dots,\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
, and its spectral norm is
\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U},\mathbf{X})
.
∎

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}).

(18)

Proof.

We will prove the result by induction.

Base case:
For
t=0
, by assumption
\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})>0
, so that
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}
is invertible. Then
\mathbf{E}_{0}
is well defined, and
\|\mathbf{E}_{0}\|_{2}\leqslant c\Delta
. Notice that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{E}_{0}=\mathbf{I}_{k}-\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}-\mathbf{E}_{0}\right),

and that
\|\mathbf{I}_{k}/2-\mathbf{E}_{0}\|_{2}\leqslant 1/2+c\Delta\leqslant 1/2+c<1
. As such,
\mathbf{I}_{k}/2+\mathbf{E}_{0}
is non-singular and
\mathbf{G}_{0}
is well defined. Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{0}\|_{2}=\|(\mathbf{I}_{k}-(\mathbf{I}_{k}/2-\mathbf{E}_{0}))^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\|\mathbf{I}_{k}/2-\mathbf{E}_{0}\|_{2}}\leqslant\frac{1}{1-(1/2+c\Delta)}=\frac{1}{1/2-c\Delta}.

We show the closed-form expression of
\mathbf{G}_{0}
by simply plugging in the definition of
\mathbf{X}_{-1}
into the right-hand side of (
17
):

\displaystyle\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{-1})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{0})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\left(\frac{1}{2\beta}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0})\right)(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{I}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{E}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{E}_{0},

which gives

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{0}=\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{I}_{k}+\mathbf{E}_{0}\right)^{-1}=\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{-1})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}\mathbf{R}_{0})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{0}\right)^{-1}.

We now need to show that
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{1}
is of rank
k
, which immediately implies that
\mathbf{Y}_{1}
is of rank
k
. To do so, we will prove (
18
): we have from the initialization (
9
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0})+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\left(\frac{\mathbf{I}_{k}}{2}+\mathbf{E}_{0}\right)(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0})=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{0}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}).

Since
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}
,
\mathbf{G}_{0}^{-1}
and
(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0})
are all non-singular,
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{1}
is non-singular, and thus
\mathbf{Y}_{1}
is of rank
k
. From this, we conclude that
\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{R}_{1}
are well defined. This concludes the base case.

Induction:
Now let
t\geqslant 0
and assume that
Proposition

C.3
is true for all steps in
\{0,\dots,t\}
. Then, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}=\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1}^{-1}.

The right hand side is well-defined and invertible because of the induction hypothesis which is verified for step
t
. Thus
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}
is invertible and
\mathbf{E}_{t+1}
is well defined. We now prove that
\mathbf{G}_{t+1}
is well defined. By the induction hypothesis, we have that

\displaystyle\|\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\beta\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}^{2}\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}+\|\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\cdot\frac{1}{1/2-c\Delta}+c\Delta

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2-4c\Delta}+c\Delta\leqslant\frac{1}{2-4c}+c<1,

where we used the fact that
\beta\leqslant\lambda_{k}^{2}/4
and
\Delta\leqslant 1
. As such,
\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1}
is non-singular and
\mathbf{G}_{t+1}
is well defined. Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{t+1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{I}_{k}-(\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{E}_{t+1}))^{-1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\|\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}^{-1}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}-\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\|_{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}-c\Delta}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{1}{4}\frac{(1-\Delta)^{2}}{1/2-c\Delta}-c\Delta}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\Delta\in[0,1]}}{{\leqslant}}\frac{1}{1/2-c\Delta}.

We now prove the closed-form expression of
\mathbf{G}_{t+1}
. By the induction hypothesis, we have that the closed form of
\mathbf{G}_{t}
in (
17
) is true at step
t
. Then,

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1})^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1})(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t}\right)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1})(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\right)^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t}\right)^{-1},

where the last equality is because
\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1}=\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
since
\mathbf{X}_{t+1},\mathbf{R}_{t+1}=\mathrm{QR}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}^{(t)}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}\mathbf{R}_{t}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t})
from (
10
). Note that this is also verified for
t=0
using the definition of
\mathbf{X}_{-1}
.

Finally, we show that
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t+2}
is of rank
k
. We have from the ANPM iteration (
10
) that

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t+2}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}-\beta\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t+1}^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\left(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1})^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1}\right)(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1})

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t+1}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}),

(19)

where the last equality is due to the closed-form expression of
\mathbf{G}_{t+1}
in (
17
) we just proved. Both
\mathbf{G}_{t+1}
and
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}
are of rank
k
, which proves that
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t+2}
is of rank
k
, and thus that
\mathbf{Y}_{t+2}
is of rank
k
. This concludes the induction and the proof.
∎

We now have the necessary tools to start the proof of
Theorem

2.2
. The proof is structured around multiple technical lemmas. The roadmap is the following: in
Lemma

C.4
, we derive a three-term recurrence relation on the matrices
\mathbf{H}_{t}
, in which the matrix sequence
\{\mathbf{G}_{t}\}
appears. This allows us to rewrite
\mathbf{H}_{t}
using scaled Chebyshev polynomials in
Lemma

C.5
. Then, by upper bounding the spectral norm of the different factors in this expression, which is done in
Lemma

C.8
, we show in
Lemma

C.9
that
h_{t}
is upper bounded by a geometrically decaying term, a constant term of order
\varepsilon
, and a linear combination of the previous
h_{i}
’s. We derive from this the geometric decay of
h_{t}
up to a constant term of order
\varepsilon
in
Lemma

C.10
, which allows us to concludes the proof of
Theorem

2.2
.

We first prove the three-term recurrence relation on
\mathbf{H}_{t}
.

Lemma C.4
.

For all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}\mathbf{C}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t},

(20)

where for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}_{t}:=\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t-1-s}^{-1},

(21)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}:=(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})^{-1}.

(22)

Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}.

(23)

Proof.

Let
t\geqslant 1
. From the definition of
\mathbf{H}_{t+1}
in (
13
) and the ANPM update in (
10
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t+1}\mathbf{R}_{t+1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\left(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1}(\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}+\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{t}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}-\beta(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1})(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}.

(24)

Here, the 4th equality is due to the closed-form expression of
\mathbf{G}_{t}
in (
17
). Then, from the expression of
\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t}
in (
18
), we have that for all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{t}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t-1}^{-1}(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1}),

so that

\displaystyle(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\mathbf{R}_{t})^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t-1})^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}.

Plugging this into the expression of
\mathbf{H}_{t+1}
in (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{t}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}-\beta\mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{G}_{t-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}.

Multiplying both sides by
\mathbf{C}_{t+1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}^{-1}\mathbf{C}_{t}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t-1}^{-1}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}
on the right, we obtain the desired recurrence:

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}\mathbf{C}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}.

We now prove the equality
\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{C}_{1}=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}
. From the initialization (
9
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{1}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Y}_{1}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}+\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{0}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{0}\right)^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{G}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{G}_{0}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1},

which gives the desired result after multiplying both sides by
\mathbf{C}_{1}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{0}^{-1}\mathbf{C}_{0}
on the right.
∎

Define the following sequences of scaled Chebyshev polynomials
\{p_{t}\}
and
\{q_{t}\}
as

\displaystyle p_{0}(x)=1,\quad p_{1}(x)=\frac{x}{2},\quad p_{t+1}(x)=xp_{t}(x)-\beta p_{t-1}(x),

(25)

\displaystyle q_{0}(x)=1,\quad q_{1}(x)=x,\quad q_{t+1}(x)=xq_{t}(x)-\beta q_{t-1}(x).

(26)

We show, using the previously obtained three-term recurrence relation, that
\mathbf{H}_{t}
can be simply written in terms of the polynomials
\{p_{t}\}
and
\{q_{t}\}
.

Lemma C.5
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t}=p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}.

(27)

Proof.

The proof is done by induction. The case
t=0
is immediate as
p_{0}(x)=1
and
\mathbf{C}_{0}=\mathbf{I}_{k}
. For
t=1
, we have from (
23
) that
\mathbf{H}_{1}\mathbf{C}_{1}=p_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}+q_{0}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}
, which gives the desired result after multiplying both sides by
\mathbf{C}_{1}^{-1}
on the right. Now, let
t\geqslant 1
and assume that the result is true for
t-1
and
t
. From (
20
), we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}\mathbf{C}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\mathbf{H}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}-\beta\mathbf{H}_{t-1}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}.

Plugging in the induction hypothesis, we obtain, if
t\geqslant 2
,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t+1}\mathbf{C}_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\left(p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{C}_{t}

\displaystyle\quad-\beta\left(p_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}^{-1}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-2}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-2-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-2-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-1}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{C}_{t-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})-\beta p_{t-1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\right)\mathbf{H}_{0}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})-\beta q_{s-1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\right)\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\mathbf{C}_{t},

\displaystyle=p_{t+1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}+\sum_{s=0}^{t}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-s}.

If
t=1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{2}\mathbf{C}_{2}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}\left(p_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{1}^{-1}+q_{0}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{1}^{-1}\right)\mathbf{C}_{1}-\beta p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}^{-1}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{1}\mathbf{C}_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}p_{1}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})-\beta p_{0}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\right)\mathbf{H}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}q_{0}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{0}+\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{1}\mathbf{C}_{1},

\displaystyle=p_{2}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}+\sum_{s=0}^{1}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{1-s}\mathbf{C}_{1-s}.

In both cases, we obtain the desired result after multiplying both sides by
\mathbf{C}_{t+1}^{-1}
on the right. This concludes the induction and the proof.
∎

The next step of the proof consists in upper bounding the different factors appearing in the expression of
\mathbf{H}_{t}
in (
27
). Before proving these bounds in
Lemma

C.8
, we need two intermediate results. The first one regards the sequences of polynomials
\{p_{t}\}
and
\{q_{t}\}
. More specifically, we give closed-form expressions of these polynomials, which will allow us to upper bound their values on the interval
[0,2\sqrt{\beta}]
.

Lemma C.6

(
33
, Lemma 3.4)
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}\left((x^{+})^{t}+(x^{-})^{t}\right),

\displaystyle q_{t}(x)=\sum_{s=0}^{t}(x^{+})^{s}(x^{-})^{t-s},

where for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
x^{\pm}
are the roots of the polynomial
z^{2}-xz+\beta
, defined as

\displaystyle x^{\pm}:=\begin{cases}\frac{x\pm\sqrt{x^{2}-4\beta}}{2}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad x^{2}\geqslant 4\beta,\\
\frac{x\pm\textit{{i}}\sqrt{4\beta-x^{2}}}{2}\quad&\mbox{if}\quad x^{2}<4\beta,\end{cases}

(28)

where
i
is the imaginary unit.

Proof.

Our proof follows the same principle as the proof of Lemma 20 in
(
31
)
. Let
\{\pi_{t}\}\in\left\{\{p_{t}\},\{q_{t}\}\right\}
. Then, for all
t\geqslant 0
, we have
\pi_{t+2}(x)=x\pi_{t+1}(x)-\beta\pi_{t}(x)
. Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and denote
\Pi(z):=\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\pi_{t}(x)z^{t}
the generating function of the sequence
\{\pi_{t}(x)\}
. Then, we have, using the two-term recurrence on
\{\pi_{t}(x)\}
,

\displaystyle\Pi(z)-\pi_{0}(x)-\pi_{1}(x)z

\displaystyle=xz\left(\Pi(z)-\pi_{0}(x)\right)-\beta z^{2}\Pi(z),

\displaystyle\Pi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}(x)+z(\pi_{1}(x)-x\pi_{0}(x))}{1-xz+\beta z^{2}},

\displaystyle\Pi(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{+}(x)}{1-\beta z/x^{+}}+\frac{C_{-}(x)}{1-\beta z/x^{-}},

where
C_{\pm}(x)
are constants that depend on
x
and the initial conditions of the sequence
\{\pi_{t}\}
. These equalities are valid for all
z
such that
|z|<\min\{|x^{+}/\beta|,|x^{-}/\beta|\}
, which is non-zero. Then, multiplying both sides by
(1-\beta z/x^{+})
(resp.
(1-\beta z/x^{-})
) and taking the limit
z\to x^{+}/\beta
(resp.
z\to x^{-}/\beta
) gives

\displaystyle C_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}(x)+\frac{x^{+}}{\beta}(\pi_{1}(x)-x\pi_{0}(x))}{1-x^{+}/x^{-}},

\displaystyle C_{-}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi_{0}(x)+\frac{x^{-}}{\beta}(\pi_{1}(x)-x\pi_{0}(x))}{1-x^{-}/x^{+}}.

Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\Pi(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\left(C_{+}(x)\left(\frac{\beta}{x^{+}}\right)^{t}+C_{-}(x)\left(\frac{\beta}{x^{-}}\right)^{t}\right)z^{t}.

Identifying the coefficients then gives for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\pi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=C_{+}(x)\left(\frac{\beta}{x^{+}}\right)^{t}+C_{-}(x)\left(\frac{\beta}{x^{-}}\right)^{t},

\displaystyle=C_{+}(x)(x^{-})^{t}+C_{-}(x)(x^{+})^{t},

Where the last equality is because
x^{+}x^{-}=\beta
. Plugging in the initial conditions for
\{p_{t}\}
and
\{q_{t}\}
gives the desired closed-form expressions. For
\{p_{t}\}
, we have
p_{0}(x)=1,p_{1}(x)=x/2
, which gives

\displaystyle C_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\frac{x^{+}}{\beta}\frac{x}{2}}{1-x^{+}/x^{-}}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad C_{-}(x)=\frac{1-\frac{x^{-}}{\beta}\frac{x}{2}}{1-x^{-}/x^{+}}=\frac{1}{2},

which gives for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle p_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(x^{+})^{t}+\frac{1}{2}(x^{-})^{t}.

For
\{q_{t}\}
, we have
q_{0}(x)=1,q_{1}(x)=x
, which gives

\displaystyle C_{+}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{x^{-}}{x^{-}-x^{+}},\qquad C_{-}(x)=\frac{x^{+}}{x^{+}-x^{-}},

which gives for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle q_{t}(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{(x^{+})^{t+1}-(x^{-})^{t+1}}{x^{+}-x^{-}}=\sum_{s=0}^{t}(x^{+})^{s}(x^{-})^{t-s}.

∎

The second intermediate result we need is an upper bound on the spectral norm of
\mathbf{G}_{t}
that is tighter than the one shown in
Proposition

C.3
.

Lemma C.7
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{r_{+}^{t}+\kappa r_{-}^{t}}{r_{+}^{t+1}+\kappa r_{-}^{t+1}},

where

\displaystyle r_{\pm}:=\frac{(1-c\Delta)\pm\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-\frac{4\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}}}{2},

\displaystyle\kappa:=1+2c\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}-c\Delta}.

Furthermore, we have
\kappa\leqslant 16/15
.

Proof.

Notice first that
(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}>0
, so that
r_{\pm}
are well-defined. Consider the sequence
\{m_{t}\}
defined by the following Riccati difference equation:

\displaystyle m_{t+1}=\frac{1}{(1-c\Delta)-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}m_{t}},

\displaystyle m_{0}=\frac{1}{1/2-c\Delta}.

Since
\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}=(1-\Delta)^{2}/4
, we have that for all
t\geqslant 0
,
0<m_{t}\leqslant(1/2-c\Delta)^{-1}
(this can be shown by a simple induction).

We will now prove by induction that for all
t\geqslant 0
,
\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant m_{t}
. The base case
t=0
is true from
Proposition

C.3
. Now, let
t\geqslant 0
and assume that
\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant m_{t}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{t+1}\|_{2}=\|(\mathbf{I}_{k}-\beta\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\mathbf{G}_{t}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}+\mathbf{E}_{t+1})^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{1-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}-c\Delta}\leqslant\frac{1}{(1-c\Delta)-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}m_{t}}=m_{t+1},

where the second inequality is valid due to the fact that
m_{t}\leqslant(1/2-c\Delta)^{-1}<(1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}^{2}/\beta
. This concludes the induction.

We will now derive a closed-form expression of
m_{t}
. Let
\{y_{t}\}
be the sequence defined as
y_{t}:=\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}m_{s}^{-1}
(with the convention
y_{0}:=1
). Then, we have that for all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{y_{t}}{m_{t}}=y_{t}\left((1-c\Delta)-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}m_{t-1}\right)=y_{t}\left((1-c\Delta)-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\frac{y_{t-1}}{y_{t}}\right),

\displaystyle y_{t+1}

\displaystyle=(1-c\Delta)y_{t}-\frac{\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}y_{t-1}.

With initial conditions
y_{0}=1
and
y_{1}=1/2-c\Delta
, we can solve this linear difference equation to obtain for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{+}r_{+}^{t}+\kappa_{-}r_{-}^{t},

where
r_{\pm}
are defined in the statement of the lemma, and where the constants
\kappa_{\pm}
are defined as

\displaystyle\kappa_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(1\mp\frac{c\Delta}{\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}}\right).

Then, since
m_{t}=y_{t}/y_{t+1}
and
\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant m_{t}
for all
t\geqslant 0
, we have that

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{\kappa_{+}r_{+}^{t}+\kappa_{-}r_{-}^{t}}{\kappa_{+}r_{+}^{t+1}+\kappa_{-}r_{-}^{t+1}}=\frac{r_{+}^{t}+\kappa r_{-}^{t}}{r_{+}^{t+1}+\kappa r_{-}^{t+1}},

where

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{\kappa_{-}}{\kappa_{+}}=1+2\frac{c\Delta}{\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}-c\Delta}.

Finally, we prove the upper bound on
\kappa
by noticing that because of the concavity of the function
u\mapsto\sqrt{u^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}
over
[2\sqrt{\beta}/\lambda_{k},+\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\left((1-c)+c\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}}\right)^{2}-\frac{4\beta}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}}\geqslant(1-c)\sqrt{1-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}\geqslant(1-c)\Delta,

from which we deduce

\displaystyle\kappa

\displaystyle\leqslant 1+2\frac{c\Delta}{(1-c)\Delta-c\Delta}\leqslant 1+2\frac{c}{1-2c}=\frac{16}{15}.

∎

We can now prove the upper bounds on the spectral norms of the factors appearing in the expression of
\mathbf{H}_{t}
in (
27
).

Lemma C.8
.

For all
t\geqslant 1
, for all
s\in\{0,\dots,t-1\}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{t}},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{s+1}},

\displaystyle\|p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}\leqslant\sqrt{\beta}^{t},

\displaystyle\|q_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}\leqslant(t+1)\sqrt{\beta}^{t},

where
c_{1}:=31/15
and
\lambda_{k}^{+}
is defined as in (
28
) (i.e. as the largest root of
x^{2}-\lambda_{k}x+\beta
).

Proof.

Recall that
\mathbf{C}_{t}=\prod_{s=0}^{t-1}\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t-1-s}^{-1}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\prod_{u=0}^{t-1}\|\mathbf{G}_{u}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\prod_{u=t-1-s}^{t-1}\|\mathbf{G}_{u}\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}.

From
Lemma

C.7
, we have for all
u\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{u}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{r_{+}^{u}+\kappa r_{-}^{u}}{r_{+}^{u+1}+\kappa r_{-}^{u+1}},

and
\|\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}^{-1}\|_{2}=1/\lambda_{k}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{t}}\prod_{u=0}^{t-1}\frac{r_{+}^{u}+\kappa r_{-}^{u}}{r_{+}^{u+1}+\kappa r_{-}^{u+1}}=\frac{1+\kappa}{\lambda_{k}^{t}(r_{+}^{t}+\kappa r_{-}^{t})}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\left(\lambda_{k}r_{+}\right)^{t}},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{s+1}}\prod_{u=t-1-s}^{t-1}\frac{r_{+}^{u}+\kappa r_{-}^{u}}{r_{+}^{u+1}+\kappa r_{-}^{u+1}}=\frac{r_{+}^{t-1-s}+\kappa r_{-}^{t-1-s}}{\lambda_{k}^{s+1}(r_{+}^{t}+\kappa r_{-}^{t})}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\left(\lambda_{k}r_{+}\right)^{s+1}},

since
\kappa\leqslant c_{1}-1
from
Lemma

C.7
and since
r_{+}\geqslant r_{-}
. Analyzing the factor
\lambda_{k}r_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}r_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+(1-c)\sqrt{\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta},

where the inequality is due to the concavity of the function
u\mapsto\sqrt{u^{2}-4\beta}
over
[2\sqrt{\beta},+\infty)
. This gives the desired bounds on
\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}
and
\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}
.

The bounds on
\|p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}
and
\|q_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}
are obtained through the closed-form expressions of
p_{t}
and
q_{t}
shown in
Lemma

C.6
. Recall that
\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k}
is a diagonal matrix whose diagonal coefficients are all in the interval
[0,2\sqrt{\beta}]
. Furthermore, for all
x\in[0,2\sqrt{\beta}]
, we have that

\displaystyle|x^{\pm}|=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{x^{2}+4\beta-x^{2}}=\sqrt{\beta}

Then, from
Lemma

C.6
, we have that for all
x\in[0,2\sqrt{\beta}]
,

\displaystyle|p_{t}(x)|\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\left(|x^{+}|^{t}+|x^{-}|^{t}\right)=\sqrt{\beta}^{t},

\displaystyle|q_{t}(x)|\leqslant\sum_{u=0}^{t}|x^{+}|^{u}|x^{-}|^{t-u}=(t+1)\sqrt{\beta}^{t},

which concludes the proof.
∎

Using these bounds, we can now prove a recurrence inequality on
h_{t}=\|\mathbf{H}_{t}\|_{2}=\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})
using the expression of
\mathbf{H}_{t}
given in (
27
) which depends on
p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})
,
q_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})
,
\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}
and
\mathbf{C}_{t-s-1}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}
.

Lemma C.9
.

For all
t\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}\gamma^{t}h_{0}+\eta\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\gamma^{s}(1+h_{t-1-s}),

(29)

where

\displaystyle\gamma:=\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}}\in[0,1),

\displaystyle\eta:=c_{2}\Delta\varepsilon,

and
c_{2}:=2/15
.

Proof.

Let
t\geqslant 1
. From (
27
), we have

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle=\|\mathbf{H}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant\|p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}\|\mathbf{H}_{0}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}\|q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\|_{2}\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}.

Using the bounds shown in
Lemma

C.8
, we deduce the following upper bound on
h_{t}
:

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\beta}^{t}h_{0}\frac{c_{1}}{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{t}}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\sqrt{\beta}^{s}\cdot\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\|_{2}\cdot\frac{c_{1}}{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{s+1}}.

(30)

Furthermore, from the definition of
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}
in (
22
), we have for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}\leqslant\|\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\|_{2}\|(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t})^{-1}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\lambda_{k}\Delta\varepsilon(\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t}))^{-1}=c\lambda_{k}\Delta\varepsilon(1+h_{t}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant c\lambda_{k}\Delta\varepsilon(1+h_{t}),

where the second inequality is from the noise condition on
\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
(
11
), the second equality is from the trigonometric identity
\cos\theta=(1+\tan^{2}\theta)^{-1/2}
and the last inequality is because
\sqrt{1+x^{2}}\leqslant 1+x
for all
x\geqslant 0
. Plugging the bound on
\|\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}\|_{2}
into the bound on
h_{t}
(
30
) gives:

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant c_{1}\gamma^{t}h_{0}+\frac{cc_{1}\lambda_{k}\Delta\varepsilon}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\gamma^{s}(1+h_{t-1-s}).

(31)

Since
\lambda_{k}^{+}\geqslant\lambda_{k}/2
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{cc_{1}\lambda_{k}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}}\leqslant\frac{cc_{1}\lambda_{k}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}/2}=c_{2},

which gives the desired result when plugged into (
31
).
∎

Finally, we show that any sequence
\{h_{t}\}
satisfying the recurrence inequality (
29
) decays exponentially as
(1-\sqrt{\Delta}/2)^{t}
until being
\varepsilon
-small.

Lemma C.10
.

For all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle h_{t}\leqslant\varepsilon/2+h_{0}\left(1-\sqrt{\Delta}/2\right)^{t}.

(32)

Proof.

Let
\{y_{t}\}
be the sequence defined as
y_{0}=h_{0}
and for all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\gamma^{t}h_{0}+\eta\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\gamma^{s}(1+y_{t-1-s}).

(33)

Then, for all
t\geqslant 0
, we have
h_{t}\leqslant y_{t}
(this can be shown by a simple induction using the recurrence inequality (
29
)). The proof will then consist in upper bounding
y_{t}
by the right-hand side of (
32
).

To do so, consider the generating function of the sequence
\{y_{t}\}
defined as
Y(z):=\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}y_{t}z^{t}
. We will use the following identities, which are true for all
|z|<1/\gamma
:

\displaystyle\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}\gamma^{t}z^{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\gamma z},\quad\quad\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}(t+1)\gamma^{t}z^{t}=\frac{1}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}.

Then, by multiplying both sides of (
33
) by
z^{t}
and summing over all
t\geqslant 1
, we have

\displaystyle Y(z)-y_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{1}h_{0}\sum_{t=1}^{+\infty}\gamma^{t}z^{t}+\eta z\sum_{t=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\gamma^{s}z^{t-1}+\eta z\sum_{t=1}^{+\infty}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}(s+1)\gamma^{s}y_{t-1-s}z^{t-1},

\displaystyle Y(z)-h_{0}

\displaystyle=c_{1}h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{1-\gamma z}-1\right)+\eta z\frac{1}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}\frac{1}{1-z}+\eta zY(z)\frac{1}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}},

\displaystyle Y(z)\left(1-\frac{\eta z}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=h_{0}\left(1+c_{1}\frac{\gamma z}{1-\gamma z}\right)+\eta z\frac{1}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}\frac{1}{1-z},

\displaystyle Y(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{0}\left(1+c_{1}\frac{\gamma z}{1-\gamma z}\right)+\eta z\frac{1}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}\frac{1}{1-z}}{1-\frac{\eta z}{(1-\gamma z)^{2}}}.

Here, the second inequality used the fact that the generating function of the convolution of two sequences is the product of the two associated generating functions. These equalities are true for all
z
whose magnitude is less that the pole of the right-hand side with lowest magnitude, which is non-zero. Indeed, the poles of
Y(z)
are
1
and the roots of the polynomial
(1-\gamma z)^{2}-\eta z
, which are

\displaystyle\rho_{\pm}:=\frac{2\gamma+\eta\pm\sqrt{(2\gamma+\eta)^{2}-4\gamma^{2}}}{2\gamma^{2}}>0.

We can then decompose
Y(z)
into partial fractions:

\displaystyle Y(z)=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{1-z}+\frac{\kappa_{+}}{1-z/\rho_{+}}+\frac{\kappa_{-}}{1-z/\rho_{-}},

(34)

from which we deduce that for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=\kappa_{1}+\kappa_{-}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}+\kappa_{+}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{+}}\right)^{t}\leqslant\kappa_{1}+(\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\kappa_{1}+(\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}.

(35)

Here, the first inequality is due to the fact that
\kappa_{+}
is non-negative. Indeed, multiplying both sides of (
34
) by
(1-z/\rho_{+})
and taking the limit
z\to\rho_{+}
gives after simplification

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{0}(1-\gamma\rho_{+})(1-\gamma\rho_{+}+c_{1}\gamma\rho_{+})+\eta\rho_{+}/(1-\rho_{+})}{1-\rho_{+}/\rho_{-}},

which is non-negative since
\rho_{+}>\rho_{-}
,
1-\gamma\rho_{+}<0
,
1-\rho_{+}<0
and
c_{1}\geqslant 1
.

Multiplying both sides of the partial fraction decomposition of
Y(z)
in (
34
) by
(1-z)
and taking the limit
z\to 1
gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta}{(1-\gamma)^{2}-\eta}.

(36)

Evaluating (
34
) at
z=0
gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-}

\displaystyle=h_{0}-\kappa_{1}.

(37)

We now analyze in detail the term
1-\gamma
. We have

\displaystyle 1-\gamma

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\sqrt{\beta}}

\displaystyle\geqslant 1-(1-c)\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}-c

\displaystyle=(1-c)\left(1-\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}\right),

(38)

where the inequality is due to the convexity of
u\mapsto 1/u
. Rewriting
1-\sqrt{\beta}/\lambda_{k}^{+}
in terms of
\Delta=1-2\sqrt{\beta}/\lambda_{k}
gives

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}-2\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}}=\sqrt{\Delta}\underbrace{\frac{\sqrt{2-\Delta}-\sqrt{\Delta}}{1-\Delta}}_{=:f(\Delta)}.

(39)

Then, according to
Lemma

C.11
, for all
\Delta^{\prime}\in(0,1)
, we have
f(\Delta^{\prime})\geqslant 1
, so that

\displaystyle 1-\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}\geqslant\sqrt{\Delta}.

Plugging this inequality into the previously found lower bound on
(1-\gamma)
(
38
) gives

\displaystyle 1-\gamma\geqslant(1-c)\sqrt{\Delta}.

(40)

Then,
(1-\gamma)^{2}-\eta\geqslant(1-c)^{2}\Delta-c_{2}\varepsilon\Delta>0
, so that
\kappa_{1}>0
. We deduce from (
37
) that
\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-}\leqslant h_{0}
. Furthermore, from the expression of
\kappa_{1}
in (
36
), we have

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{c_{2}\Delta\varepsilon}{(1-\gamma)^{2}-c_{2}\Delta\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{c_{2}\Delta\varepsilon}{(1-c)^{2}\Delta-c_{2}\Delta\varepsilon}\leqslant\frac{c_{2}\varepsilon}{(1-c)^{2}-c_{2}}\leqslant\varepsilon/2.

Plugging these inequalities into the upper bound on
h_{t}
(
35
) gives

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}.

(41)

Finally, we analyze the factor
1/\rho_{-}
. We have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle=\gamma\frac{1}{1+\frac{\eta}{2\gamma}-\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\eta}{2\gamma}\right)^{2}-1}}=\gamma\left(1+\frac{\eta}{2\gamma}+\sqrt{\left(1+\frac{\eta}{2\gamma}\right)^{2}-1}\right)

\displaystyle=\gamma+\frac{\eta}{2}+\sqrt{\left(\gamma+\frac{\eta}{2}\right)^{2}-\gamma^{2}}=\gamma+\frac{\eta}{2}+\sqrt{\eta\gamma+\frac{\eta^{2}}{4}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma+\frac{\eta}{2}+\sqrt{\eta+\eta^{2}/4},

where the last inequality is because
\gamma\leqslant 1
. Since,
\eta=c_{2}\varepsilon\Delta\in[0,1]
, we have
\eta^{2}\leqslant\eta\leqslant\sqrt{\eta}
, so that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\gamma+\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\sqrt{\eta}\leqslant 1-(1-c)\sqrt{\Delta}+\frac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}\sqrt{c_{2}\varepsilon\Delta}

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle\leqslant 1-\sqrt{\Delta}/2,

(42)

where the second equality comes from the inequality of
1-\gamma
(
40
). Plugging the above bound on
1/\rho_{-}
(
42
) into the inequality on
h_{t}
in (
41
) gives the desired result:

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+h_{0}\left(1-\sqrt{\Delta}/2\right)^{t}.

∎

Using the geometric decay of
h_{t}
we just showed, we can finally prove
Theorem

2.2
.

Proof of
Theorem

2.2
.

The proof directly follows from
Lemma

C.10
and the definition of
h_{t}=\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})
. Indeed, from the inequality
\sin\theta\leqslant\tan\theta
that holds for all
\theta\in[0,\pi/2)
, we have for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})\leqslant h_{t}.

Thus, from
Lemma

C.10
, we have for all
t\geqslant 0
,

\displaystyle\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})(1-\sqrt{\Delta}/2)^{t}.

For all
t\geqslant T
where
T
is defined in the statement of
Theorem

C.2
, we have that the second term on the right-hand side is less than

, so that
\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})\leqslant\varepsilon
, which concludes the proof.

∎

We conclude this section by proving the technical lemma used in the proof of
Lemma

C.10
, which bounds a function
f
of the gap
\Delta
over
[0,1)
. We prove additional bounds on this function beyond the one that is necessary for the proof of
Theorem

2.2
, as these additional bounds will be useful in the proofs of the theorems in

.

Lemma C.11
.

For all
\Delta\in[0,1)
, let

\displaystyle f(\Delta):=\frac{\sqrt{2-\Delta}-\sqrt{\Delta}}{1-\Delta}.

Then, f is decreasing over
[0,1)
. In particular, we have
1\leqslant f(\Delta)\leqslant\sqrt{2}
for all
\Delta\in[0,1)
.

Proof.

For all
\Delta\in[0,1)
, we have that

\displaystyle f(\Delta)=2\frac{1-\Delta}{\sqrt{2-\Delta}+\sqrt{\Delta}}\frac{1}{1-\Delta}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{2-\Delta}+\sqrt{\Delta}}.

Letting
g(\Delta):=\sqrt{2-\Delta}+\sqrt{\Delta}
, we have
f(\Delta)=2/g(\Delta)
.
g
is differentiable over
(0,1)
with derivative
g^{\prime}(\Delta)=-1/(2\sqrt{2-\Delta})+1/(2\sqrt{\Delta})
. Thus,
g^{\prime}(\Delta)>0
for all
\Delta\in(0,1)
, so that
g
is increasing over
[0,1)
. Thus,
f
is decreasing over
[0,1)
, and for all
\Delta\in[0,1)
,

\displaystyle 1=\frac{2}{g(1)}\leqslant f(\Delta)\leqslant\frac{2}{g(0)}=\sqrt{2}.

∎

C.3
Behavior of ANPM with
0<\beta<\lambda_{k+1}^{2}/4

In this section, we prove a result which is not stated in
Section

2
, which guarantees that performing ANPM with a momentum parameter
\beta
smaller than
\lambda_{k+1}^{2}/4
(a regime not covered by
Theorem

2.2
) still improves the convergence rate compared to the non-accelerated noisy power method. This result is interesting as it shows that using a small momentum parameter does not make convergence worse than NPM, and that it can still be useful in practice even when the condition
\beta\geqslant\lambda_{k+1}^{2}/4
is not satisfied. For all of this section, we will use the same notations as those used in
Section

C.2
.

Theorem C.12
.

Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, let
\mathbf{A}\succeq\mathbf{0}
such that
\lambda_{k}>\lambda_{k+1}
and let
\mathbf{X}_{0}
such that
\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})>0
. Consider the ANPM iterates
\{\mathbf{X}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
defined by (
9
)-(
10
) with momentum parameter
\beta>0
satisfying
\lambda_{k+1}>2\sqrt{\beta}
and perturbations
\{\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
satisfying

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant c(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})\varepsilon,

(43)

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant c(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})\cos\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t}),

(44)

where
c=1/32
. Then, for all
t\geqslant T
,
\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})\leqslant\varepsilon
, where

\displaystyle T=\mathcal{O}\left({\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}}\log\left(\frac{\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

Remark C.13
.

For all

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}=\frac{1}{1-\frac{\lambda_{k+1}}{\lambda_{k}}\frac{1+\sqrt{1-4\beta/\lambda_{k+1}^{2}}}{1+\sqrt{1-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}}}<\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1}},

so that the convergence rate of ANPM in this regime is better than that of the non-accelerated Noisy Power Method shown by

Proof.

It is easy to see that under the noise condition on

(

and

instead of

are well defined, and that for all

,

\displaystyle\mathbf{H}_{t}=p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\mathbf{H}_{0}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q_{s}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1},

(45)

where
\mathbf{C}_{t}
and
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{t}
were defined in

C.4
, and
p_{t}
and
q_{t}
are the scaled Chebyshev polynomials defined in (

)-(
26
). Furthermore, the bounds shown in

C.7
are also valid with our new definition of
\Delta
, and we have that for all

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{G}_{t}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{r_{+}^{t}+\kappa r_{-}^{t}}{r_{+}^{t+1}+\kappa_{-}^{t+1}},

\displaystyle r_{\pm}:=\frac{(1-c\Delta)\pm\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}-4\beta/\lambda_{k}^{2}}}{2},

\displaystyle\kappa\in[0,16/15].

Then, the proof for the bounds on
\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}
and
\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}
shown in

C.8
can be adapted to our new setting, using the definition of
\mathbf{C}_{t}=\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{t-1}^{-1}\dots\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{k}\mathbf{G}_{0}^{-1}
. We then have for all
t\geqslant 1
,
s\in\{0,\dots,t-1\}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda_{k}^{t}r_{+}^{t}},

\displaystyle\|\mathbf{C}_{t-1-s}\mathbf{C}_{t}^{-1}\|_{2}\leqslant\frac{c_{1}}{\lambda_{k}^{s+1}r_{+}^{s+1}}.

where
c_{1}:=31/15
. We can then bound
\lambda_{k}r_{+}
as

\displaystyle\lambda_{k}r_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{(1-c\Delta)^{2}\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{\left((1-c)\lambda_{k}+c\sqrt{\beta}\right)^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{1}{2}\left((1-c\Delta)\lambda_{k}+(1-c)\sqrt{\lambda_{k}^{2}-4\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}\geqslant(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+},

where the first inequality is due to the concavity of the function
u\mapsto\sqrt{u^{2}-4\beta}
over
[2\sqrt{\beta},+\infty)
. The final bound we need is on
\|p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}
. Since
\lambda_{k+1}>2\sqrt{\beta}
, the bound from
Lemma

C.8
is not valid anymore, and we instead have

\displaystyle\|p_{t}(\boldsymbol{\Lambda}_{-k})\|_{2}=p_{t}(\lambda_{k+1})=\frac{(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})^{t}+(\lambda_{k+1}^{-})^{t}}{2}\leqslant(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})^{t}.

We now upper bound
h_{t}:=\|\mathbf{H}_{t}\|_{2}
using (
45
) and the bounds we just obtained. We then have, using the same techniques as those used in the proof of
Lemma

C.9
, for all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle h_{t}\leqslant c_{1}\gamma^{t}h_{0}+\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})\frac{1}{b^{s}}(1+h_{t-1-s}),

(46)

where

\displaystyle b:=(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+},\qquad\gamma:=\lambda_{k+1}^{+}/b\in(0,1),

\displaystyle\eta:=c_{1}c\lambda_{k}\Delta\varepsilon,\qquad c_{2}:=2/15.

Note that in (
46
), we do not upper bound
q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})
as in
Lemma

C.9
, which would lead to a loose bound since in the case where
\lambda_{k+1}>2\sqrt{\beta}
,
\lambda_{k+1}^{+}
and
\lambda_{k+1}^{-}
can be very different from each other. We will thus rely on a finer analysis of the above recurrence inequality, directly involving
q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})
. Letting
y_{t}
be the sequence defined as
y_{0}=h_{0}
and for all
t\geqslant 1
,

\displaystyle y_{t}

\displaystyle=c_{1}\gamma^{t}h_{0}+\frac{\eta}{b}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})\frac{1}{b^{s}}(1+y_{t-1-s}),

we have for all
t\geqslant 0
,
h_{t}\leqslant y_{t}
, since
q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})\geqslant 0
for all
s
. We can then analyze the generating function
Y(z):=\sum_{t=0}^{+\infty}y_{t}z^{t}
of the sequence
\{y_{t}\}
. Using the same techniques as those used in the proof of
Lemma

C.10
, we have

\displaystyle Y(z)-h_{0}=c_{1}h_{0}\frac{\gamma z}{1-\gamma z}+\frac{\eta z}{b}Q(z)\left(\frac{1}{1-z}+Y(z)\right),

where
Q(z)
is defined as

\displaystyle Q(z)=\sum_{s=0}^{+\infty}q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})\left(\frac{z}{b}\right)^{s}=\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{k+1}z/b+\beta z^{2}/b^{2}}.

Here, the expression of the generating function of the sequence
\{q_{s}(\lambda_{k+1})\}
comes from the proof of
Lemma

C.6
. We deduce the following closed-form expression for
Y(z)
:

\displaystyle Y(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{h_{0}\left(1+c_{1}\frac{\gamma z}{1-\gamma z}\right)+\frac{\eta z}{b}\frac{1}{1-z}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{k+1}z/b+\beta z^{2}/b^{2}}}{1-\frac{\eta z}{b}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{k+1}z/b+\beta z^{2}/b^{2}}}.

(47)

From this expression, we can decompose
Y(z)
into partial fractions:

\displaystyle Y(z)=\frac{\kappa_{1}}{1-z}+\frac{\kappa_{+}}{1-z/\rho_{+}}+\frac{\kappa_{-}}{1-z/\rho_{-}},

(48)

where
\rho_{\pm}
are the roots of the polynomial
\frac{\beta}{b^{2}}z^{2}-\frac{\lambda_{k+1}+\eta}{b}z+1
, i.e.,

\displaystyle\rho_{\pm}:=b\frac{\lambda_{k+1}+\eta\pm\sqrt{(\lambda_{k+1}+\eta)^{2}-4\beta}}{2\beta}.

Multiplying both sides of the expressions of
Y(z)
in (
47
) and (
48
) by
1-z
and evaluating at
z=1
gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\eta/b}{1-\lambda_{k+1}/b+\beta/b^{2}-\eta/b}=\frac{\eta}{b-\lambda_{k+1}+\beta/b-\eta}.

(49)

We will first upper bound
\kappa_{1}
, to show that it is of order
\varepsilon
. To do so, consider the function
g(x)=x+\beta/x
for
x>0
. This function is convex, and we have the following inequality:

\displaystyle g(b)-g(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})

\displaystyle\geqslant g^{\prime}(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})(b-\lambda_{k+1}^{+})

\displaystyle=g^{\prime}(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})(1-c)(\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+})

where the equality is from the definition of
b=(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+}
. Notice that because
\lambda_{k+1}^{+}
is a root of
z^{2}-\lambda_{k+1}z+\beta
, we have
g(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})=\lambda_{k+1}
. Similarly, we have
g(\lambda_{k}^{+})=\lambda_{k}
. The above inequality can be rewritten as

\displaystyle b-\lambda_{k+1}+\beta/b

\displaystyle\geqslant g^{\prime}(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})(1-c)(\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}).

(50)

Next, by the mean value theorem, we have that

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\frac{g(\lambda_{k}^{+})-g(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}\leqslant\sup_{x\in[\lambda_{k+1}^{+},\lambda_{k}^{+}]}|g^{\prime}(x)|=g^{\prime}(\lambda_{k+1}^{+}),

so that we finally deduce the following lower bound on
b-\lambda_{k+1}+\beta/b
using (
50
):

\displaystyle b-\lambda_{k+1}+\beta/b

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-c)\frac{g(\lambda_{k}^{+})-g(\lambda_{k+1}^{+})}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}(\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+})=(1-c)(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})=(1-c)\lambda_{k}\Delta.

Plugging this lower bound into the expression of
\kappa_{1}
in (
49
) gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{\eta}{(1-c)\lambda\Delta-\eta}=\frac{c_{1}c\lambda\Delta\varepsilon}{(1-c)\lambda\Delta-c_{1}c\lambda\Delta\varepsilon}=\frac{c_{1}c}{1-c-c_{1}c}\varepsilon\leqslant\varepsilon/2.

We now focus on the other terms of the partial fraction decomposition of
Y(z)
in (
48
). We can show that
\kappa_{+}\geqslant 0
by multiplying both sides of (
48
) by
1-z/\rho_{+}
and taking the limit
z\to\rho_{+}
. Furthermore, evaluating (
48
) at
z=0
gives

\displaystyle\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-}+\kappa_{1}

\displaystyle=Y(0)=h_{0}.

Then, we get from the partial fraction decomposition of
Y(z)
in (
48
) the following upper bound on
h_{t}
:

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant y_{t}\leqslant\kappa_{1}+(\kappa_{+}+\kappa_{-})\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}\leqslant\varepsilon/2+h_{0}\left(\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}\right)^{t}.

(51)

What remains to show is that
\rho_{-}^{-t}
decays geometrically with a rate
\tilde{\mathcal{O}}\left(\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}\right)
. We can write
1/\rho_{-}
as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\beta}{b}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{k+1}+\eta)+\sqrt{(\lambda_{k+1}+\eta)^{2}-4\beta}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{(\lambda_{k+1}+\eta)+\sqrt{(\lambda_{k+1}+\eta)^{2}-4\beta}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}.

Since
\eta\leqslant cc_{1}(\lambda_{k}-\lambda_{k+1})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\frac{(1-cc_{1})\lambda_{k+1}+cc_{1}\lambda_{k}+\sqrt{((1-cc_{1})\lambda_{k+1}+cc_{1}\lambda_{k})^{2}-4\beta}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}.

Then, by concavity of the function
u\mapsto\sqrt{u^{2}-4\beta}
over
[2\sqrt{\beta},+\infty)
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\rho_{-}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{(1-cc_{1})\lambda_{k+1}^{+}+c_{1}c\lambda_{k}^{+}}{(1-c)\lambda_{k}^{+}+c\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}\leqslant\frac{(1-cc_{1})\lambda_{k+1}^{+}+c_{1}c\lambda_{k}^{+}}{(1-c_{1}c)\lambda_{k}^{+}}\leqslant 1-(1-c_{1}-cc_{1})\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k}^{-}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}.

Denoting
c_{2}:=1-c_{1}-cc_{1}>0
, we finally have

\displaystyle h_{t}

\displaystyle\leqslant\varepsilon/2+h_{0}\left(1-c_{2}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}\right)^{t},

so that for all
t\geqslant T
,
\sin\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})\leqslant h_{t}\leqslant\varepsilon
, where
T
is defined as

\displaystyle T=\frac{1}{-\log\left(1-c_{2}\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}\right)}\log\left(\frac{2h_{0}}{\varepsilon}\right)=\mathcal{O}\left({\frac{\lambda_{k}^{+}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}-\lambda_{k+1}^{+}}}\log\left(\frac{\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})}{\varepsilon}\right)\right).

∎

Theorem C.14

(

)
.

Let

and
\mathbf{X}_{0}\in\mathrm{St}(d,k)
such that

In the case where

\displaystyle\mathbf{X}_{1},\mathbf{R}_{1}=\mathrm{QR}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{0}\right),

25

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=\left\|\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{X}_{t}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}=\left\|\left(\mathbf{U}_{-k}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{t}\right)\left(\mathbf{U}_{k}^{\top}\mathbf{Z}_{t}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}.

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{2\sqrt{\beta}^{t}}{(\lambda_{k}^{+})^{t}+(\lambda_{k}^{-})^{t}}\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0})

\displaystyle\geqslant\left(\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}\right)^{t}\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0}),

C.6
, and
\lambda_{k}^{\pm}
are defined in (
28

39

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{\beta}}{\lambda_{k}^{+}}=1-\sqrt{\Delta}f(\Delta)>0,

where

C.11

t\geqslant 0

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{t})

\displaystyle\geqslant(1-f(\Delta)\sqrt{\Delta})^{t}\tan\theta_{k}(\mathbf{U}_{k},\mathbf{X}_{0}).

C.11

Theorem C.15

(

)
.

Let

Note that the adaptive tuning heuristic
\beta_{t}
requires to run ANPM on
k+1
columns instead of
k
. In that case, the initialization
\mathbf{X}_{0}
is chosen so that its first
k
columns are the same as the initialization used for the other methods. Furthermore, the quantity plotted corresponds to
\sin
of the
k
-th principal angle between
\mathbf{U}_{k}
and the
k
first columns of
\mathbf{X}_{t}
, so that the comparison with other methods is fair. Indeed, from
(
1
;
33
)
, we expect the convergence of ANPM to the top-
k
eigenspace to improve when running it on more than
k
columns.

We sample the noise matrices
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
from a so-called ”adversarial” distribution, inspired by the adversarial examples used in the proofs in
Section

C.4
. The adversarial examples from
Section

C.4
were designed to be in a single direction corresponding to the eigenvectors of
\mathbf{A}
, hindering as much as possible the convergence of NPM/ANPM. In our experiments, given a noise norm
\xi
, we sample
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
as

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}=-\xi\frac{\mathbf{U}\mathbf{V}}{\|\mathbf{V}\|_{2}},\qquad\mathbf{V}=[|v_{i,j}|]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},\qquad v_{i,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

This ensures that 1)
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
is of spectral norm
\xi
and 2) all the columns
\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i}
of
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
verify
\langle\boldsymbol{\xi}_{i},\mathbf{u}_{j}\rangle\leqslant 0
for all
j\in\{1,\dots,d\}
, which tends to hinder the convergence of ANPM in a similar way as the adversarial examples from
Section

C.4
. In particular,
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
is not sampled from a centered distribution. We argue that such mean-centered noise models behave differently from the adversarial noise shown in
Section

C.4
. Indeed, we show in
Figure

3
the results of the same experiments as in
Figure

1
, but where
\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}
is sampled uniformly on the sphere of radius
\xi
for the spectral norm:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}=\xi\frac{\mathbf{V}}{\|\mathbf{V}\|_{2}},\qquad\mathbf{V}=[v_{i,j}]\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times k},\qquad v_{i,j}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{i.i.d}}}{{\sim}}\mathcal{N}(0,1).

E.2
Experimental Details for ADePM

E.2.1
Details for Fed-Heart-Disease

Fed-Heart-Disease is a dataset from the FLamby collection
(
21
)
, which consists of tabular data of dimension
d=13
separated into
n=4
hospitals. We perform decentralized PCA on rescaled local covariance matrices. Letting
\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}\times d}
be the local data matrix of agent
i
for all
i=1,\dots,4
, we let

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}_{i}:=\frac{n}{m}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i},\qquad\mathbf{A}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{A}_{i}=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{\Phi}_{i},

where
m=\sum_{i=1}^{n}m_{i}=486
is the total number of samples. This way,
\mathbf{A}
is the empirical covariance matrix of the full dataset. We choose a ring graph topology for our communication network, with a gossip matrix
\mathbf{W}
defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}:=\begin{bmatrix}1/2&1/4&0&1/4\\
1/4&1/2&1/4&0\\
0&1/4&1/2&1/4\\
1/4&0&1/4&1/2\end{bmatrix}.

E.2.2
Details for Ego-Facebook

Ego-Facebook
(
15
)
is a graph dataset consisting of
4039
Facebook users, connected by edges representing friendships. We pick a subset of size
n=d=50
of this graph by selecting the nodes labeled
0
through
49
. This subset constitutes a connected graph
G
. The goal of spectral clustering is to find a low-dimensional embedding of the nodes of
G
by using the bottom eigenvectors of its normalized Laplacian matrix, defined as

\displaystyle\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{norm}}=\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2},

where
\mathbf{S}
is the adjacency matrix of
G
, such that
s_{i,j}=s_{j,i}=1
if there is an edge between nodes
i
and
j
and
0
otherwise, and where
\mathbf{D}
is the diagonal degree matrix of
G
, such that
d_{i,i}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}s_{i,j}
. This matrix has eigenvalues in
[0,2]
, with
0
being the smallest eigenvalue. Thus, finding the bottom
k
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{norm}}
is equivalent to finding the top
k
eigenvectors of the PSD matrix

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}:=2\mathbf{I}_{n}-\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{norm}}=\mathbf{I}_{n}+\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\mathbf{S}\mathbf{D}^{-1/2}\succeq\mathbf{0}.

The agents
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
can construct matrices
\mathbf{A}_{i}
such that
\mathbf{A}=n^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{A}_{i}
by only knowing their neighbors and their degrees. Noting
d_{i}
the degree of node
i
, we let
\mathbf{A}_{i}
be the matrix such that for all
j
neighbor of
i
in
G
,

\displaystyle[\mathbf{A}_{i}]_{i,i}=1,\quad[\mathbf{A}_{i}]_{i,j}=[\mathbf{A}_{i}]_{i,j}=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{d_{i}d_{j}}},

and all other entries of
\mathbf{A}_{i}
are
0
. We can then use the decentralized PCA algorithms on the family of matrices
\{\mathbf{A}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
to find the bottom
k
eigenvectors of
\mathbf{L}_{\mathrm{norm}}
.

To reduce the communication costs of our experiment, we suppose that the communication network
G^{\prime}
is given by the graph
G
, to which we add
\sim n\log(n)
edges uniformly at random among the pairs of nodes that are not already connected in
G
, in order to increase the connectivity of the graph. We then define the gossip matrix
\mathbf{W}
with the Metropolis-Hastings weights on the communication graph
G^{\prime}
: letting
\mathcal{N}_{i}
be the set of neighbors of node
i
in
G^{\prime}
, and
d_{i}^{\prime}
its degree in
G^{\prime}
, we let

\displaystyle w_{i,j}:=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{1+\max(d_{i}^{\prime},d_{j}^{\prime})}&\text{if }j\in\mathcal{N}_{i},\\
1-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{N}_{i}}w_{i,s}\quad&\text{if }i=j,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

E.2.3
Adapting the Tuning Heuristic (
5
) to ADePM

We adapt the tuning heuristic (
5
) for ANPM to ADePM as follows. Recall that the tuning heuristic for ANPM is, given an iterate
\mathbf{X}_{t}
with
k+1
columns,

\displaystyle\beta_{t}=\min_{j=1,\dots,k+1}\left[\mathbf{X}_{t}^{\top}(\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{t}+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t})\right]_{j,j}^{2}/4.

For ADePM, each agent
i
approximates
\beta_{t}
locally as

\displaystyle\beta_{i,t}=\min_{j=1,\dots,k+1}\left[\mathbf{X}_{i,t}^{\top}(\mathbf{Y}_{i,t-1}+\beta_{i,t-1}\mathbf{X}_{i,t-2}\mathbf{R}_{i,t-1}^{-1})\right]_{j,j}^{2}/4,

since

\displaystyle\mathbf{Y}_{i,t-1}+

\displaystyle\beta_{i,t-1}\mathbf{X}_{i,t-2}\mathbf{R}_{i,t-1}^{-1}=

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}\bar{\mathbf{X}}_{t-1}+(-\beta_{i,t-1}\bar{\mathbf{X}}_{t-2}\bar{\mathbf{R}}_{t-1}^{-1}+\beta_{i,t-1}\mathbf{X}_{i,t-2}\mathbf{R}_{i,t-1}^{-1})+\boldsymbol{\Xi}_{t}+(\mathbf{Y}_{i,t-1}-\bar{\mathbf{Y}}_{t-1})

is a good approximation of
\mathbf{A}\mathbf{X}_{i,t-1}
for large gossip communications
L
. Here, we reused the notations from
Appendix

D
. This method of choosing
\beta_{i,t}
does not require any additional communication between the agents in comparison to vanilla ADePM.

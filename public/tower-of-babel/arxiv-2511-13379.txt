Presenting the Zassenhaus Lie algebra by the Magnus Lie algebra
Ettore Marmo, David Riley and Thomas Weigel

Abstract
. It is shown that the Zassenhaus restricted 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -Lie algebra of a (pro- p p ) group G G can be presented by the Magnus Lie algebra of G G . For the class of (pro- p p ) groups for which the terms of the lower central series are torsion-free, the Zassenhaus restricted 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} -Lie algebra can be explicitly computed from the Magnus Lie algebra. These results apply to orientable surface groups, right-angled Artin groups, pure braid groups, fundamental groups of supersolvable hyperplane arrangements and fundamental groups of strictly supersolvable toric arrangements.

2.
Lie algebras

In this section we recall some basic constructions from the theory of Lie algebras and restricted Lie algebras.

2.1.
Universal envelopes and Hopf algebras

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field. We will denote by
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}
the category of associative, unital
\mathbb{F}
-algebras with
\mathbb{F}
-algebra homomorphisms and similarly by
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}
the category of
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebras with
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra homomorphisms. These two categories are related by a well known pair of adjoint functors:

(2.1)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}}

The right adjoint assigns to each associative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra
(A,\,\cdot)
the
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}(A)=(A,\,[\text{-},\text{-}]_{A})
whose bracket is given by
[a,b]_{A}:=a\cdot b-b\cdot a
for all
a,b\in A
. The left adjoint assigns to each
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
its
universal enveloping algebra

\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
,
which can be presented as a quotient of
T^{\bullet}(\mathscr{L})
, the free tensor
\mathbb{F}
-algebra over
\mathscr{L}
, modulo the ideal
J_{\mathscr{L}}=\langle x\otimes y-y\otimes x-[x,y]\mid x,y\in\mathscr{L}\rangle
. The classical Poincaré-Birkoff-Witt theorem (commonly referred to as the “PBW theorem”) states that natural map,
\iota_{\mathscr{L}}:\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
, is an injective morphism of
\mathbb{F}
-vector spaces and that
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
is generated as an
\mathbb{F}
-algebra by the image of
\iota_{\mathscr{L}}
. In order to simplify notation we will identify a Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
with its image
\iota_{\mathscr{L}}(\mathscr{L})
in its universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
.

The universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
inherits from
T^{\bullet}(\mathscr{L})
the structure of an
\mathbb{F}
-Hopf algebra, thus the functor
\mathscr{U}
actually takes values in the category
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Hopf}
.
Let
H
be a Hopf algebra over
\mathbb{F}
, an element
x\in H
is said to be
primitive
if it satisfies the identity
\Delta(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes x
where
\Delta:H\to H\otimes H
is the coproduct of
H
. One verifies easily that the
\mathbb{F}
-subspace
\operatorname{Prim}(H)
generated by primitive elements is closed under the commutator bracket
[x,y]_{H}:=xy-yx
and thus is an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie subalgebra of
H
. The assignment
H\mapsto\operatorname{Prim}(H)
extends to a functor
\operatorname{Prim}:\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Hopf}\longrightarrow\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}
that is right adjoint to the universal enveloping (Hopf) algebra functor:

(2.2)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Hopf}\mathclap{\quad\quad\quad}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{U}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Prim}}

An
\mathbb{N}
-
graded
Lie algebra, where
\mathbb{N}
is the set of positive integers, is a Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}_{\bullet}
equipped with a direct sum decomposition
\mathscr{L}_{\bullet}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\mathscr{L}_{n}
compatible with the Lie bracket, i.e. for all
n,m\in\mathbb{N}
one has
[\mathscr{L}_{n},\mathscr{L}_{m}]\subseteq\mathscr{L}_{n+m}
.
The free tensor algebra of an
\mathbb{N}
-graded Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}_{\bullet}
(or more generally for an
\mathbb{N}
-graded vector space) is canonically
\mathbb{N}_{0}
-bigraded:

(2.3)

T^{n,k}(\mathscr{L}_{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{k}=n}\mathscr{L}_{i_{1}}\otimes\mathscr{L}_{i_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathscr{L}_{i_{k}}

where
\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and one puts
T^{0,0}(\mathscr{L}_{\bullet})=\mathbb{F}\cdot{\rm id}
and
T^{n,0}(\mathscr{L}_{\bullet})=0
for
n\geq 1
. Note that the ideal
J_{\mathscr{L}_{\bullet}}
is a graded ideal with respect to the first grading but not the second. Thus, the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}_{\bullet})
inherits a grading as well.

2.2.
Restricted Lie algebras and restrictification

Let
\mathbb{F}
be a field of characteristic
p>0

Definition 1
.

Let
\mathscr{L}
be an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
. A
p
-map
for
\mathscr{L}
is a function
(-)^{[p]}:\mathscr{L}\to\mathscr{L}
satisfying:

(
\rm rL_{1}
)

(\lambda x)^{[p]}=\lambda^{p}x^{[p]}
;

(
\rm rL_{2}
)

(x+y)^{[p]}=x^{[p]}+y^{[p]}+\sum_{k=1}^{p-1}\frac{s_{k}(x,y)}{k}
;

(
\rm rL_{3}
)

[x^{[p]},y]=\operatorname{ad}(x)^{p}(y)
;

where
s_{k}(x,y)
is the coefficient of
t^{k-1}
in the formal expression
\operatorname{ad}(tx+y)^{p-1}(x)
and
\operatorname{ad}(x)(y)=[x,y]
. An
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra equipped with a
p
-map is said to be a
restricted

\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra.

By the PBW theorem,
\mathscr{L}
is an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie subalgebra of
(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}),[\text{-},\text{-}]_{\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})})
. Since
\operatorname{Prim}
is the right adjoint of
\mathscr{U}
,
\mathscr{L}
is also an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie subalgebra of
\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))
. It is well know that for fields of characteristic
0
the two Lie algebras
\mathscr{L}
and
\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))
coincide (see
[
4
]

[
22
]
), while for fields of characteristic
p>0
,
\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))
is strictly larger than
\mathscr{L}
.
In this case
\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))
is closed with respect to the
p
-power map
x\mapsto x^{p}
defined using the multiplication in
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
and thus

(2.4)

\mathscr{L}^{[p]}=\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))

is a restricted Lie algebra which will be called the
canonical restrictification of
\mathscr{L}
.
Restricted Lie algebras together with Lie algebra morphisms which respect the
p
-power maps form a category,
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}
, and the assignment
\mathscr{L}\mapsto\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
extends to a covariant functor
(-)^{[p]}:\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}\to\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}
which is left adjoint to the obvious forgetful functor from
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}
to
\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}
.

Let
(A,\cdot)
be an associative
\mathbb{F}
-algebra, then the Lie algebra
{\mathcal{L}}(A)
equipped with the map
x\mapsto x^{p}
, defined using the associative product of
A
is a restricted Lie algebra. As in the case of Lie algebras, this defines a functor
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}:\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}\to\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}
. As in the general case, every restricted
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
has a restricted universal enveloping algebra
\underline{{u}}(\mathscr{L})
which can be either defined as the quotient of
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
by the ideal generated by
x^{[p]}-x^{p}
or as the left adjoint of the functor
{\mathcal{L}}_{p}
(see
[
11
]
).
The just mentioned properties can be summarized as follows.

Proposition 1
.

The following functors form adjoint pairs:

(2.5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Alg}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{{u}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{L}}_{p}}

(2.6)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{Lie}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bot}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{F}\text{-}\mathbf{rLie}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(-)^{[p]}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rm f}

where
\rm f
is the forgetful functor.

If
\mathscr{L}_{\bullet}
is a graded Lie algebra, then its restrictification
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
admits a special grading. Consider the following subspaces of
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}

(2.7)

\mathscr{L}^{[p]}_{n}=\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
ip^{j}=n\end{subarray}}(\mathscr{L}_{i})^{[p]^{j}}

where
(\mathscr{L}_{i})^{[p]^{j}}
denotes the subspace of
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
spanned by elements of the form
a_{i}^{[p]^{j}}
with
a\in\mathscr{L}_{i}
. From the PBW theorem we deduce that the subspaces
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}_{n}
are all disjoint. Moreover since
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
is spanned by the elements of
\mathscr{L}
and the images of the
p
-power map, we have that
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\mathscr{L}_{n}^{[p]}
.
We record the following observations.

Proposition 2
.

For an
\mathbb{F}
-Lie algebra
\mathscr{L}
where
\mathbb{F}
is a field of positive characteristic
p
, one has
\underline{{u}}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})\cong\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})

Proof.

Recall that
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}=\operatorname{Prim}(\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}))
, so
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
is a Lie subalgebra of the universal enveloping algebra
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
. It can be seen that
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
along with the inclusion
\iota_{\mathscr{L}^{[p]}}:\mathscr{L}^{[p]}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
satisfies the universal property of the universal enveloping algebra, therefore by uniqueness we have an isomorphism
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})\cong\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
. Now, since the
p
-map of
\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
is defined in terms of the algebra multiplication of
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
, the ideal of
\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})=\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
generated by elements of the form
x^{[p]}-x^{p}
with
x\in\mathscr{L}^{[p]}
is the zero ideal, so
\underline{{u}}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})=\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})
. Thus we have proven
\underline{{u}}(\mathscr{L}^{[p]})\cong\mathscr{U}(\mathscr{L})
.
∎

3.
Subgroup series and associated graded Lie algebras

In this section we study the Lie algebras associated to two important descending series of subgroups for a group
G
.

3.1.
The lower central series

The
lower central series
of a group
G
is the series of characteristic subgroups
\{\gamma_{i}(G)\}_{i\geq 1}
of
G
defined recursively by

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}(G)

\displaystyle=G

\displaystyle\gamma_{i+1}(G)

\displaystyle=[G,\gamma_{i}(G)],\quad\text{ for $i>1$.}

The lower central series is an example of a (descending) strongly central series, i.e. it satisfies
\gamma_{i+1}(G)\leq\gamma_{i}(G)
for all
i\geq 1
and
[\gamma_{i}(G),\gamma_{j}(G)]\leq\gamma_{i+j}(G)
for all
i,j\geq 1
. To this series one may associate the graded
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra:

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)

where
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G):=\gamma_{n}(G)/\gamma_{n+1}(G)
which is also called the
Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra
associated to
G
. For
i,j\geq 1
, for
x\in\gamma_{i}(G)
and
y\in\gamma_{j}(G)

the Lie bracket:

\displaystyle[-,-]:\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{i}(G)\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{j}(G)

\displaystyle\to\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{i+j}(G)\quad

is defined by

\displaystyle[x\gamma_{i+1}(G),\,y\gamma_{j+1}(G)]

\displaystyle=[x,y]\gamma_{i+j+1}(G).

From the definition one deduces easily that it is
\mathbb{Z}
-bilinear and alternating. The Jacobi identity can be derived from the well-known Hall-Witt formula for group commutators.

3.2.
Magnus Lie algebras of pro-
p
completions

Let
G
be a finitely generated nilpotent group.
As
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a binomial lambda ring, there is a category of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-powered nilpotent groups introduced by P. Hall in
[
9
, §6]
.
In particular there exists a canonical map

(3.1)

h_{G,\mathbb{Z}_{p}}:G\longrightarrow G\otimes{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}

from the nilpotent group
G
to the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-powered nilpotent group
G\otimes{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
which is characterized by a universal property.
This map shows that the functor
-\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the left adjoint to the forgetful functor from the category of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-powered nilpotent groups to the category of nilpotent groups.
Moreover

(3.2)

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p})\cong\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

If
G
is a finitely generated nilpotent group, and
g_{1},\dots,g_{n}
are elements in
G
such that
G=\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}}
, then
G\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
. Hence
G\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a pro-finite group in which every element generates a cyclic pro-
p
group, i.e.
G\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is a pro-
p
group.
Thus,
h_{G,\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(G)
coincides with the pro-
p
completion
\hat{G}_{p}
of
G
. This shows that

(3.3)

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(\hat{G}_{p})\cong\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{p}

holds for all finitely generated nilpotent groups and thus for all finitely generated groups.

3.3.
Dimension subgroups

Let
G
be a group and let
R
be a commutative unital ring. The
augmentation ideal
of the group ring
RG
as the kernel
\omega_{R}(G)
of the augmentation map
\varepsilon:RG\to R
defined by sending each group element

to the unit

. The augmentation ideal coincides with the ideal generated by elements of

of the form
1-g
with

.

Definition 2
.

For a group
G
and ring
R
, we define the
n
-th
dimension subgroup of
G

R
as:

D_{n}(G,R):=\{g\in G\mid 1-g\in\omega_{R}(G)^{n}\}

It can be shown that

is a subgroup of

for every

.

For a long time, the dimension subgroup series over
\mathbb{Z}
and the lower central series were believed to coincide, this was known as the “Dimension Subgroup Conjecture”. While it is true that, for any group
G
, one has
D_{2}(G,\mathbb{Z})=\gamma_{2}(G)
and even

(a deep theorem by Higman and Rees, cf.

]
,
[
8
]
), there exists a group

such that
D_{4}(G,\mathbb{Z})/\gamma_{4}(G)
is nontrivial. This counterexample to the Dimension Subgroup Conjecture was constructed by Rips in
[
26
]
.

For
R=\mathbb{F}_{p}
, the dimension series
\{D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})\}_{n\geq 1}
, is called the
p
-Zassenhaus
series of
G
(also known as Jennings-Lazard-Zassenhaus series) and the associated restricted Lie algebra is denoted by
\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)
. This series of subgroups is not only a strongly central series but it is also a
p
-central series
, i.e. it satisfies the additional property that
D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})^{p}\leq D_{pn}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
for all
n\geq 1
. Similar to the lower central series, the
p
-Zassenhaus series is the fastest descending
p
-central series.
This additional property implies that the associated graded Lie algebra
\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)
equipped with a
p
-map induced by the group theoretic
p
-power map
x\mapsto x^{p}
, is a restricted Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
(see
[
29
]
,
[
18
]
).

A more explicit description of the
p
-Zassenhaus series was given by Jennings in
[
13
]
. In the following, we will fix a prime number
p
and denote
D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})
simply by
D_{n}(G)
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
13
]
,
[
18
]
)
.

Let
G
be a group. Then for every
n\geq 1
the subgroup
D_{n}(G)
can be expressed as follows

(3.4)

D_{n}(G)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\geq 0\\
ip^{j}\geq n\end{subarray}}\gamma_{i}(G)^{p^{j}}

where
\gamma_{i}(G)^{p^{j}}
is the subgroup generated by the
p^{j}
-th powers of elements of
\gamma_{i}(G)
.

One can refine the
p
-Zassenhaus series by defining, for
1\leq k\leq n
, the subgroups:

(3.5)

D_{n,k}(G):=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\geq k,\,j\geq 0\\
ip^{j}\geq n\end{subarray}}\gamma_{i}(G)^{p^{j}}

Remark 1
.

Let
n,i\geq 1
and define
j(n,i)
to be the least positive integer
j
such that
ip^{j}\geq n
. Then, for all
i\leq k\leq n
, one has:

(3.6)

D_{n,k}(G)=\prod_{i=k}^{n}\gamma_{i}(G)^{p^{j(n,i)}}

in particular, the following inclusions hold:

D_{n}(G)=D_{n,1}(G)\geq D_{n,2}(G)\geq\cdots\geq D_{n,n}(G)=\gamma_{n}(G).

Proposition 3
.

Let
G
be a group,
i\geq 1
and
j\geq 0
, the following hold:

(1)

(xy)^{p^{j}}\equiv x^{p^{j}}y^{p^{j}}\mod D_{ip^{j},i+1}(G)
for every
x,y\in\gamma_{i}(G)
;

(2)

[x^{p^{j}},y]\in D_{ip^{j},i+1}(G)
for every
x\in\gamma_{i}(G)
and
y\in G
.

Proof.

The classic Hall-Petrescu formulae
[
10
, I, §3 9.4]
state the following relations

(3.7)

(xy)^{p^{j}}\equiv x^{p^{j}}y^{p^{j}}\mod\gamma_{2}(H)^{p^{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{j}\gamma_{p^{r}}(H)^{p^{j-r}}

and

(3.8)

[x^{p^{j}},y]\equiv[x,y]^{p^{j}}\mod\gamma_{2}(K)^{p^{j}}\prod_{r=1}^{j}\gamma_{p^{r}}(K)^{p^{j-r}}

where
H=\langle x,y\rangle
and
K=\langle x,[x,y]\rangle
. Notice that when
x,y\in\gamma_{i}(G)
we have
\gamma_{k}(H)\leq\gamma_{ki}(G)
for each
k\geq 1
. Hence,
\gamma_{2}(H)^{p^{j}}\leq\gamma_{2i}(G)^{p^{j}}\leq D_{2ip^{j},2i}(G)
, while for
r\in{1,\dots,j}
we have
\gamma_{p^{r}}(H)^{p^{j-r}}\leq\gamma_{ip^{r}}(G)^{p^{j-r}}\leq D_{ip^{j},ip^{r}}(G)
. Since these subgroups all belong to
D_{ip^{j},i+1}(G)
, (
3.7
) proves (1).

The second assertion is proven similarly using (
3.8
) and observing that if
x\in\gamma_{i}(G)
then
[x,y]^{p^{j}}\in\gamma_{i+1}(G)^{p^{j}}\leq D_{ip^{j},i+1}(G)
as well.
∎

3.4.
A canonical homomorphism of graded Lie algebras

Since the
p
-Zassenhaus series is a central series, for any group
G
one has that
\gamma_{n}(G)\leq D_{n}(G)
for all
n\geq 1
. So, for each
n\geq 1
one may define a homomorphism of abelian groups

(3.9)

\rho_{G,n}:\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)\to\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{n}(G)

given by
\rho_{G,n}(x\gamma_{n+1}(G)):=xD_{n+1}(G)
. The homomorphisms
\rho_{G,n}
give rise to a morphism
\rho_{G,\bullet}:\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\to\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)
of graded abelian groups, which turns out to be a map of graded
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebras.

Proposition 4
.

The image of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)
under
\rho_{G,\bullet}
is isomorphic to:

(3.10)

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)/p\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\cong\bigoplus_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}(G)/\gamma_{n}(G)^{p}\gamma_{n+1}(G).

Proof.

Each homomorphism
\rho_{G,n}
for
n\geq 1
can be written as the composition of an inclusion followed by a projection:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho_{G,n}:\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D_{n}(G)/\gamma_{n+1}(G)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{n}(G)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Since
\ker(\pi)=D_{n+1}(G)/\gamma_{n+1}(G)
, the kernel of
\rho_{G,n}
can be expressed as
\ker(\rho_{G,n})=(\gamma_{n}(G)\cap D_{n+1}(G))/{\gamma_{n+1}(G)}
. Using Jenning’s (refined) description of the
p
-Zassenhaus series (
3.5
), one finds the following equalities

\gamma_{n}(G)\cap D_{n+1}(G)=D_{n+1,n}(G)=\gamma_{n}(G)^{p^{j(n+1,n)}}\gamma_{n+1}(G)^{p^{j(n+1,n+1)}}

where, as in Remark
1
,
j(n,i)
denotes the smallest integer
j\geq 0
such that
ip^{j}\geq n
. Clearly
j(n+1,n)=1
and
j(n+1,n+1)=0
. So, by the first and third homomorphism theorems, one has that

{\rm im}(\rho_{G,n})\cong\gamma_{n}(G)/(\gamma_{n}(G)^{p}\gamma_{n+1}(G))

for all
n\geq 1
, which proves the claim.
∎

Since
\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)
is naturally a graded Lie algebra over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, we will consider the induced homomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebras:

(3.11)

\theta_{G,\bullet}=(\rho_{G,\bullet}\otimes{\rm id}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}):\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)

given by
\theta_{G,n}(x\gamma_{n+1}(G)\otimes 1):=\rho_{n}(x\gamma_{n+1}(G))=xD_{n+1}(G)
for each
x\in\gamma_{n}(G)
and
n>0
. From Proposition
1
(
2.6
)
one deduces the existence of a natural homomorphism of restricted
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebras:

(3.12)

\hat{\theta}_{G,\bullet}:(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})^{[p]}\longrightarrow\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)

As
\hat{\theta}_{G,1}
is an isomorphism by construction, and
\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)
is a
1
-generated
\mathbb{N}
-graded restricted
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebra, one concludes the following:

Lemma 1
.

The map
\hat{\theta}_{\bullet}
is surjective.

4.
\gamma
-free groups

In this section, we study the properties of the map
\hat{\theta}_{\bullet}
for a particular class of (pro-
p
) groups.

Definition 3
.

A (pro-
p
) group
G
is said to be
\gamma
-free if
\gamma_{n}(G)/\gamma_{n+1}(G)
is a free abelian (pro-
p
) group for all
n\geq 1
.

The property of a group being
\gamma
-free can be stated equivalently in terms of its nilpotent quotients.

Lemma 2
.

For
G
a finitely generated (pro-
p
) group, the following are equivalent:

(i)

G
is
\gamma
-free;

(ii)

G/\gamma_{n}(G)
is
\gamma
-free for all
n\geq 1
; and

(iii)

G/\gamma_{n}(G)
is torsion-free for all
n\geq 1
.

Remark 2
.

The associated graded Lie algebras of a group
G
and its nilpotent quotients
G/\gamma_{c}(G)
are closely related. Indeed since for all
1\leq n\leq c
we have
\gamma_{n}(G/\gamma_{c}(G))=\gamma_{n}(G)/\gamma_{c}(G)
, then

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G/\gamma_{c}(G))\cong\begin{cases}\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)&\text{if $1\leq n<c$}\\
0&n\geq c\end{cases}

Proposition 5
.

Let
G
be a nilpotent
\gamma
-free (pro-
p
) group of nilpotency class
c
. For every
n\geq 1
, one can pick a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis (resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-basis)
\{x_{n,\lambda}\gamma_{n+1}(G)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}
of the free abelian (pro-
p
) group
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)=\gamma_{n}(G)/\gamma_{n+1}(G)
where the indexing set
\Lambda_{n}
is totally ordered. Then, the following hold:

(1)

For every
g\in G
there exist unique elements
\alpha_{n,\lambda}=\alpha_{n,\lambda}(g)
in
\mathbb{Z}
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
) with
n\in\{1,\dots,c\}
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
such that:

g=\prod_{n=1}^{c}\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}x_{n,\lambda}^{\alpha_{n,\lambda}}

where the indices in the product are ordered lexicographically (i.e.
(n,\lambda)\prec(n^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime})
if either
n<n^{\prime}
or
n=n^{\prime}
and
\lambda<\lambda^{\prime}
in
\Lambda_{n}
).

(2)

Whenever
g\in D_{n,k}(G)
for some
n\geq k
, one has that
p^{j(n,i)}
divides
\alpha_{i,\lambda}(g)
for all
k\leq i\leq n
.

Proof.

Since
G
is nilpotent of class
c
, then the graded Lie algebra
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)
associated to the lower central series is a
finite
direct sum:

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)=G/\gamma_{2}(G)\oplus\cdots\oplus\gamma_{c-1}(G)/\gamma_{c}(G)\oplus\gamma_{c}(G)

whose terms are all free abelian since
G
is
\gamma
-free. So the elements
\{x_{n,\lambda}\gamma_{n+1}(G)\in\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)\mid n=1,\dots,c;\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}\}
form a
\mathbb{Z}
-basis (resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
basis) for
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)
.
Hence for every
g\in G
and
1\leq n\leq c
there exist unique coefficients
\alpha_{n,\lambda}=\alpha_{n,\lambda}(g)
such that

g=\prod_{n=1}^{c}\prod_{n,\lambda}x_{i,\lambda}^{\alpha_{n,\lambda}}

If
y\in\gamma_{n}(G)
, for each
1\leq n\leq j
, we can express the image of both elements
y
and
y^{p^{j}}
in the quotient
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)
as follows:

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\equiv\prod\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}x_{n,\lambda}^{\alpha_{n,\lambda}}\mod\gamma_{n+1}(G),

\displaystyle y^{p^{j}}

\displaystyle\equiv\prod\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}x_{n,\lambda}^{\beta_{n,\lambda}}\mod\gamma_{n+1}(G)

where the integers
\alpha_{n,\lambda}=\alpha_{n,\lambda}(y)
and
\beta_{n,\lambda}=\beta_{n,\lambda}(y^{p^{j}})
for
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
are uniquely determined as we proved in the first claim.
From Proposition
3
(1) we also find

y^{p^{j}}\equiv\left(\prod\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}x_{n,\lambda}^{\alpha_{n},\lambda}\right)^{p^{j}}\equiv\prod\nolimits_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}x_{n,\lambda}^{p^{j}\alpha_{n},\lambda}\mod\gamma_{n+1}(G)

so, by uniqueness, we must have
\beta_{n,\lambda}=p^{j}\alpha_{n,\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}
, i.e.
p^{j}
divides each coefficient
\beta_{n,\lambda}
.
Finally, by Remark
1
, any
g\in D_{n,k}(G)
can be expressed as a product

g=y_{k}^{p^{j(n,k)}}y_{k-1}^{p^{j(n,k-1)}}\cdots y_{n}^{p^{j(n,n)}}

for some (not necessarily unique) elements
y_{i}\in\gamma_{i}(G)
for
k\leq i\leq n
. The second claim follows by inspecting each term of this product as above.
∎

We can now prove the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem A
.

Let
G
be a finitely generated
\gamma
-free (pro-
p
) group, then the natural map

\hat{\theta}_{\bullet}:(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})^{[p]}\to\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)

is an isomorphism of restricted Lie algebras.

Proof.

By Lemma
1
, it is sufficient to prove that
\hat{\theta}_{\bullet}
is injective.
Recall that any nilpotent quotient
G/\gamma_{c+1}(G)
of
G
is also
\gamma
-free by Lemma
2
. Thus, we may assume that
G
is nilpotent of class
c\geq 1
. Using Proposition
5
, we can fix a basis
\{x_{n,\lambda}\gamma_{n+1}(G)\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{n}}
for each homogeneous component
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)
of the Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra.

Write
R_{\bullet}
for the restrictification of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p}
. Using the PBW theorem for restricted Lie algebras (see
[
12
]
), we can write a basis for the
n
-th homogeneous component of
R_{\bullet}
, i.e.

R_{n}={\rm Span}_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\{(x_{i,\lambda}\gamma_{i+1}(G))^{[p]^{j}}\mid i,j\geq 1\text{ such that }ip^{j}=n,\,\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}\}.

Let
y\in R_{n}
, then

y=\sum_{i,\lambda}\alpha_{i,\lambda}(x_{i,\lambda}\gamma_{i+1}(G))^{[p]^{j}}

where
\alpha_{i,\lambda}=\alpha_{i,\lambda}(y)
is an integer in
\{0,1,\dots,p-1\}
, the indices
i,j\geq 0
are such that
ip^{j}=n
and
\lambda\in\Lambda_{i}
.

Suppose
y\in\ker(\hat{\theta}_{n})
, then by Proposition
5
(ii), we have that
p^{j(n,i)}
divides each coefficient
\alpha_{i,\lambda}
, forcing
y=0
. Thus
\hat{\theta}_{n}
is injective for all
n\geq 1
, hence
\hat{\theta}_{\bullet}
is an isomorphism.
∎

5.
Applications

In this section we list some examples of groups
G
for which Theorem A or Theorem B may be applied to. Special emphasis is laid on finding examples for which the following isomorphism holds

(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\otimes\mathbb{F}_{p})^{[p]}\cong\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}_{\bullet}(G)

5.1.
Free groups

A theorem attributed to Magnus states that if
F_{n}
is the free group of rank
n
, then the associated Lie algebra
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(F_{n})
is the free
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra on
n
generators, in particular
F_{n}
is
\gamma
-free. The ranks of each homogeneous component,
M_{n}(k)={\rm rk}(\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{k}(F_{n}))
, were computed by Hall and Witt (see
[
4
]
) and they satisfy the following formula:

(5.1)

M_{n}(k)=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{d|k}\mu(d)n^{k/d}.

where
\mu
is the Möbius function.

5.2.
One relator groups

A finitely generated one relator group is a quotient
G\cong F/R
of a finitely generated free group
F
by the normal closure of a cyclic subgroup
R=\langle r\rangle^{F}\unlhd F
, the word
r\in F
is the defining relation of
G
. For any word
r\in F
, define its
weight
to be the largest integer
w(r)>0
such that
r\in\gamma_{w(r)}(G)\setminus\gamma_{w(r)+1}(G)
. As
F
is residually nilpotent,
r\neq 1
implies
w(r)<\infty
. Following
[
16
]
, we will say that
r
is
primitive
if it is not a proper power modulo
\gamma_{w(r)+1}(F)
. The following theorem is due to Labute.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
16
]
)
.

Let
G=F/R
where
F
is a finitely generated free group and
R
is the normal closure of the subgroup generated by
r\in F
, a primitive word. Let
\bar{r}
be the image of
r
in
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{w(r)}(F)
and let
\mathfrak{r}
be the Lie-ideal generated by
\bar{r}
. Then, there is an isomorphism of graded
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebras:

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(G)\cong\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(F)/\mathfrak{r}.

As a consequence, any such one-relator groups
G
is
\gamma
-free, and the ranks of
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}(M)
can be computed via a formula analogous to Equation (
5.1
).

An important class of examples of
\gamma
-free one relator groups is given by the fundamental groups of orientable, compact surfaces. If
S_{g}
is an orientable, compact surface of genus
g
, its fundamental group is given by

(5.2)

\pi_{1}(S_{g})=\langle a_{1},b_{1},\dots,a_{g},b_{g}\mid[a_{1},b_{1}][a_{2},b_{2}]\cdots[a_{g},b_{g}]\rangle.

5.3.
Free products and Magnus groups

Recall that a group
G
is said to be
residually nilpotent
if the intersection of all the terms of the lower central series,
\bigcap\nolimits_{n\geq 1}\gamma_{n}(G)
,
is the trivial subgroup. Groups which are both residually nilpotent and
\gamma
-free, often referred to as
Magnus groups
, have been the subject of many investigations in the literature (see for example
[
2
]
). We record the following theorem due to Shmelkin.

Theorem 5.2

(
[
27
]
)
.

The free product of Magnus groups is again a Magnus group, moreover one has that the Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra functor restricted to the class of Magnus group commutes with coproducts.

This result allows us to construct, via free products, a wide variety of examples of (residually nilpotent) groups for which Theorem B holds.

5.4.
Almost direct products of free groups

A semidirect product
G=N\rtimes H
for which the inclusion
[N,H]\leq[N,N]
holds, is said to be
almost-direct
. Equivalently, such a group is almost-direct if the conjugation action of
H
on
N
descends to a trivial action on the abelianization
N/[N,N]
. The Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra of an almost direct product was studied in detail by Falk and Randell:

Theorem 5.3

(
[
7
]
)
.

Let
G=N\rtimes H
be an almost direct product. Then for all
n\geq 1
one has the following isomorphisms of abelian groups:

\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(G)\cong\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(N)\oplus\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{n}(H)

In particular almost direct products of
\gamma
-free groups are again
\gamma
-free, hence Theorem B can be applied.

5.5.
Hyperplane and Toric arrangements

A
complex hyperplane arrangement
in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
is a finite collection
\mathcal{A}=\{H_{1},\dots,H_{n}\}
of codimension
1
vector subspaces of
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
. The complement space
M(\mathcal{A})=\mathbb{C}^{\ell}\setminus\cup_{H\in\mathcal{A}}H
is a connected manifold whose topology has been the subject of extensive investigations. Many interesting questions can be asked about its cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
and its fundamental group
G(\mathcal{A})=\pi_{1}(M(\mathcal{A}),*)
.
The
intersection lattice
of
\mathcal{A}
, denoted
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{A})
, is defined as the set of all the possible nonempty intersections of the hyperplanes of
\mathcal{A}
, ordered by reverse inclusion. The combinatorial information contained in the lattice
\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{A})
can be used to define the so-called Orlik-Solomon algebra
A^{\bullet}(\mathcal{A})
which, by the classic Brieskorn-Orlik-Solomon theorem
[
23
]
, is isomorphic to the cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(M(\mathcal{A}),\mathbb{Z})
. A hyperplane arrangement
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
supersolvable
if its intersection lattice is a supersolvable lattice i.e. if it admits a maximal chain of modular elements (see
[
28
]
). This purely combinatorial condition has surprising implications in the topology of the arrangement. In particular for a supersolvable arrangement
\mathcal{A}
in
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
, it implies that the complement manifold
M(\mathcal{A})
sits on top of a tower of fibrations:

M_{\ell}=M(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow M_{\ell-1}\longrightarrow\cdots\longrightarrow M_{1}

where, for
1\leq k\leq\ell-1
, the space
M_{k}=M(\mathcal{A}_{k})
is the complement of a supersolvable arrangement
\mathcal{A}_{k}
in
\mathbb{C}^{k}
. Moreover, the generic fiber of each fibration is homeomorphic to a copy of
\mathbb{C}
minus a finite number of points. As a consequence, the fundamental group of a supersolvable arrangement splits as an iterated semidirect product of free groups of finite rank:

(5.3)

G(\mathcal{A})=F_{d_{\ell}}\rtimes(F_{d_{\ell-1}}\rtimes(\cdots\rtimes(F_{d_{2}}\rtimes F_{d_{1}})))

where the ranks (usually called the
exponents
of the arrangement) are given by
d_{k}=|\mathcal{A}_{k}|-|\mathcal{A}_{k-1}|

for
2\leq k\leq\ell
and
d_{1}=|\mathcal{A}_{1}|
. Since the semidirect products in (
5.3
) are almost-direct products by
[
7
, Proposition 2.5]
, the fundamental group of a supersolvable hyperplane arrangement complement is
\gamma
-free.

5.5.1.
Pure braid groups

One of the most important examples of supersolvable hyperplane arrangement is given by the
braid arrangement

\mathcal{B}r_{n}=\{H_{ij}\mid 1\leq i<j\leq n+1\}

in
\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
(for
n\geq 2
), where
H_{ij}
is the hyperplane defined by the equation
x_{j}-x_{i}=0
. The complement of this arrangement can be identified with the
configuration space
of
n
distinct points in
\mathbb{C}
, so its fundamental group
is isomorphic to the pure braid group on
n
strands, i.e.
G(\mathcal{B}r_{n})\cong{\rm PB}_{n}
. Thus, the pure braid group on
n
strands is
\gamma
-free. An presentation of the Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra was given by Kohno in
[
15
]
, namely
\operatorname{gr}^{\mathscr{M}}_{\bullet}({\rm PB}_{n})
is isomorphic to the quotient of the free
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra over the set
\{X_{ij}\mid i\leq i<j\leq n+1\}
by the ideal generated by the elements:

•

[X_{ik},X_{ij}+X_{jk}]
and
[X_{jk},X_{ij}+X_{ik}]
for all
1\leq i<j<k\leq n+1
;

•

[X_{ir},X_{js}]
for all
i<r
,
j<s
,
i<j
and
r\neq j
,
r\neq s
.

This Lie algebra can be identified with the
holonomy Lie algebra
of the associated hyperplane arrangement.

5.5.2.
Strictly supersolvable toric arrangements

The theory of hyperplane arrangement naturally generalizes to a setting where the ambient space
\mathbb{C}^{\ell}
is replaced by the complex torus
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}=(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\ell}
and the linear hyperplanes are replaced by kernels of monomial characters
\chi:\mathbb{T}^{\ell}\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
called
hypertori
. A
toric arrangement
in
\mathbb{T}^{\ell}
is a finite collection
\mathcal{B}
of hypertori. Given a toric arrangement
\mathcal{B}
one can study, similarly to the case of hyperplane arrangements, the complement manifold
M(\mathcal{B})=\mathbb{T}^{\ell}\setminus\cup_{K\in\mathcal{B}}K
, its cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(M(\mathcal{B}),\mathbb{Z})
and its fundamental group
G(\mathcal{B})=\pi_{1}(M(\mathcal{B}),*)
.

The combinatorial object associated to a toric arrangement
\mathcal{B}
is the
poset of layers

\mathbb{L}(\mathcal{B})
whose elements are the connected components of all the possible nonempty intersections of the hypertori of
\mathcal{B}
.
The theory of toric arrangements shows some significant differences from its linear counterpart because of the different topology of the ambient space: two connected hypertori may have a non-connected intersection and even a single hypertorus may consist of several connected components. For this reason, the poset of layers is not in general a lattice, but rather a locally geometric poset. Extending the definition of supersolvable lattices allows to define the class of
supersolvable toric arrangements
. This definition and the resulting theory is developed in
[
3
]
.

By
[
3
, Theorem 3.4.3]
, the fundamental group
G(\mathcal{B})
of the complement of a supersolvable toric arrangement
\mathcal{B}
is an iterated semidirect product of free groups of finite rank. A further combinatorial restiction on the poset of layers called
strict
supersolvability ensures that the semidirect products are almost-direct (see
[
3
, Theorem 5.3.4 and Theorem 5.3.10]
). We have then that the fundamental groups of complements of strictly supersolvable toric arrangements are
\gamma
-free groups.

5.6.
Uniformly powerful pro-
p
groups

A finitely generated pro-
p
group
G
is said to be uniform,
if it is torsion-free and satisfies
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq G^{p}
if
p
is odd, and
\gamma_{2}(G)\leq G^{4}
if
p=2
.
It is well-known that for these groups one has

D_{n}(G,\mathbb{F}_{p})=G^{p^{n}}
and that the
p
-map
(\hbox to6.45831pt{\leaders{\hrule}{\hfill}\kern 0.0pt})^{[p]}\colon\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{n}(G)\to\operatorname{gr}^{\mathcal{Z}}_{n+1}(G)
(induced by the group theoretic
p
-power map) is injective (see
[
6
]
). This yields the description of the Magnus-Zassenhaus kernel given in Corollary
2
.
By definition, neither non-trivial groups of exponent
p
nor non-trivial uniform pro-
p
groups can be
\gamma
-free.
Nevertheless, there are examples of
p
-adic analytic
\gamma
-free pro-
p
groups, e.g.,

(5.4)

G=\Bigg\{\left(\begin{matrix}1&x&z\\
0&1&y\\
0&0&1\end{matrix}\right)\ \Bigg|\ x,y,z\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\,\Bigg\}

is a
p
-adic analytic
\gamma
-free
pro-
p
group of nilpotency class
2
.

5.7.
A class of mild non
\gamma
-free pro-
p
groups

For a positive integer
n\geq 3
and an odd prime
p
, let

(5.5)

G_{n}=\langle\,x_{0},\dots,x_{n-1}\mid x_{k}^{p}=[x_{k-1},x_{k}],\,k\in\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z}\,\rangle_{\text{pro-}p}

These groups were studied by J. Labute in
[
17
]

There it is shown that the groups
G_{n}
are mild pro-
p
groups for odd integers
n\geq 5
. More precisely, their Zassenhaus restricted
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-Lie algebra
coincides with the restrictification of the Magnus
\mathbb{Z}
-Lie algebra tensored with
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and equals

(5.6)

\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}(G_{n})\simeq\operatorname{gr}^{{\mathcal{Z}},p}(F)/\langle r_{1},\dots,r_{n}\rangle_{\mathrm{rLie}},

where
F
is the free group of rank
n
and
r_{k}=[x_{k-1},x_{k}]
(see
[
17
]
).
Therefore, the group
G_{n}
satisfies the conclusion of
Theorem B. However, as the abelianzation
G_{n}/\gamma_{2}(G_{n})
has
p
-torsion,
G_{n}
is not
\gamma
-free.

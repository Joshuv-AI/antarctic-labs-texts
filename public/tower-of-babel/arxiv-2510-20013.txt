Counterexample to majority optimality in NICD with erasures
Paata Ivanisvili, Xinyuan Xie

Abstract
We asked GPT-5 Pro to look for counterexamples among a public list of open problems (the Simons “Real Analysis in Computer Science” collection). After several numerical experiments, it suggested a counterexample for the Non-Interactive Correlation Distillation (NICD) with erasures question: namely, a Boolean function on 5 5 bits that achieves a strictly larger value of 𝔼 ​ | f ⁡ ( z ) | \mathbb{E}|f(z)| than the 5 5 -bit majority function when the erasure parameter is p = 0.40 p=0.40 . In this very short note we record the finding, state the problem precisely, give the explicit function, and verify the computation step by step by hand so that it can be checked without a computer. In addition, we show that for each fixed odd n n the majority is optimal (among unbiased Boolean functions) in a neighborhood of p = 0 p=0 . We view this as a “little spark” of an AI contribution in Theoretical Computer Science: while modern Large Language Models (LLMs) often assist with literature and numerics, here a concrete finite counterexample emerged.



A short preface

Finding a successful instance in which an AI suggests a viable counterexample to a decade-plus-old open question appears, to our knowledge, to be the first publicly reported case. In most problems we tested, the models contributed little beyond literature pointers and small-scale numerics. Problem statement

We work on the hypercube
\{-1,1\}^{n}
equipped with the uniform probability measure. A Boolean function is
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
. We say
f
is
unbiased
if
\mathbb{E}[f]=0
. A sufficient condition is
oddness
:
f(-x)=-f(x)
.

Multilinear extension.

Every
f
has a unique multilinear polynomial representation

f(z)=\sum_{S\subseteq[n]}\widehat{f}(S)\,\prod_{i\in S}z_{i},\qquad z\in[-1,1]^{n},

where
\widehat{f}(S)=\mathbb{E}[f(x)\prod_{i\in S}x_{i}]
, and
x\sim\mathrm{unif}(\{-1,1\}^{n})
.

Erasure model.

Fix
p\in(0,1)
. Let
z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{n}
have independent coordinates with

\Pr[z_{i}=1]=\Pr[z_{i}=-1]=\tfrac{p}{2},\qquad\Pr[z_{i}=0]=1-p.

This is the standard
binary erasure
NICD model (cf. Yang
[
8
]
).

Objective.

For unbiased Boolean
f
, define

\Phi_{p}(f)\;:=\;\mathbb{E}_{z}\big[\,|f(z)|\,\big],

where
f(z)
is the multilinear extension evaluated at the erasure sample
z
.

Open question (as recorded in the Simons list).

For
p<\tfrac{1}{2}
, is
\Phi_{p}(f)
maximized (over unbiased
f
) by a majority function? (When
p\geq\tfrac{1}{2}
, dictators are optimal; see
[
5
,
6
]
.) We refer to the Simons compilation
[
2
]
for a concise statement.

A finite counterexample at
p=0.40

Set
n=5
and
p=\frac{2}{5}=0.40
.
Consider the linear threshold function

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\;=\;\sgn\!\big(x_{1}-3x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{4}+3x_{5}\big)

(with the convention
\sgn(t)=1
for
t>0
,
-1
for
t<0
; note
t\neq 0
in our case).
Then
f(-x)=-f(x)
, hence
f
is unbiased.

Theorem 1
.

For
p=0.40
one has

\displaystyle\Phi_{0.40}(f)=\frac{2689}{6250}=0.43024,\quad\Phi_{0.40}(\mathrm{Maj}_{5})=\frac{5363}{12500}=0.42904,

hence
\Phi_{0.40}(f)>\Phi_{0.40}(\mathrm{Maj}_{5})
. In particular, majority is not optimal at
p=0.4
already for
n=5
.

A related (solved) conjecture: Majority is least stable

The “Majority‑is‑Least‑Stable” conjecture states: for all linear threshold functions
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, all odd
n
, and all
p\in[0,1]
, the noise stability

\mathrm{Stab}_{p}[f]\ :=\ \sum_{S\subseteq[n]}p^{|S|}\,\widehat{f}(S)^{2}

is minimized by the majority function
\mathrm{Maj}_{n}
.
Equivalently, with
z
drawn from the erasure model at rate
p
, one has
\mathrm{Stab}_{p}(f)=\mathbb{E}\big[f(z)^{2}\big]
, so the conjecture asserts that the majority functions minimize the
L_{2}
-norm of
f(z)
among LTFs. By contrast, the NICD‑with‑erasures question asks whether majority
maximizes
the
L_{1}
-norm
\Phi_{p}(f)=\mathbb{E}|f(z)|
among unbiased
f
. Although
\mathbb{E}|f(z)|\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}f(z)^{2}}
, to the best of our knowledge there is no implication in either direction between these two optimization problems.
After identifying the counterexample presented here, we noted that it is closely related in spirit to the counterexample in
[
3
]
, e.g. to
\sgn(2x_{1}+2x_{2}+x_{3}+x_{4}+x_{5})
; both have the “two heavy + three light” LTF structure, and up to permutation and flips of variables, they are the same function.

Remark 2
.

Comparison between
L_{1}
norms is often more delicate than comparison between
L_{2}
norms due to the lack of orthogonality. This is reflected in Appendix A: the manual verification for
L_{1}
occupies more casework than the corresponding
L_{2}
verification in
[
3
]
. This may help explain why a counterexample familiar from the “Majority‑is‑Least‑Stable” setting had not, to our knowledge, been tested against the NICD‑with‑erasures question until now.

Remark 3
.

In the Majority‑is‑Least‑Stable question (
L_{2}
norm), the counterexample works in a neighborhood of
p=0
. On the other hand, in the NICD‑with‑erasures question there is no counterexample in a neighborhood of
p=0
for each fixed
n
. Indeed, in this case we show that majority is optimal (see Section
4
).

Remark 4
.

A brute-force search is feasible for this finite question; indeed, GPT-5 Pro noted in the chat history that it used such a method. Still, being able to program against an open-problems list and to uncover a concrete counterexample seems noteworthy to us.

4
For each fixed
n
majority is optimal in a neighborhood of
p=0
.

Lemma 5
.

For unbiased
f:\{\pm 1\}^{n}\to\{\pm 1\}
we have

\Bigl|\Phi_{p}(f)-p\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|\Bigr|\;\leq\;\Bigl((n-1)\sqrt{n}+\tbinom{n}{2}\Bigr)p^{2}.

Proof.

Let
K:=|\{i:Z_{i}\neq 0\}|
, so
K\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,p)
. By conditioning on
K
,

\Phi_{p}(f)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\Pr[K=k]\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,|f(Z)|\;\middle|\;K=k\right].

We treat three cases.

Case
K=0
.
Then
Z=0
and
|f(Z)|=|\hat{f}(\emptyset)|=0
since
f
is unbiased.

Case
K=1
.
We have

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,|f(Z)|\;\middle|\;K=1\right]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|,

hence the unconditional contribution from
K=1
equals

\Pr[K=1]\cdot\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,|f(Z)|\;\middle|\;K=1\right]=p(1-p)^{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|.

Therefore

\bigl|\;p(1-p)^{n-1}-p\;\bigr|\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|\leq p\bigl(1-(1-p)^{n-1}\bigr)\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|\leq(n-1)\sqrt{n}\,p^{2},

using
1-(1-p)^{n-1}\leq(n-1)p
and
\sum_{i}|\hat{f}(i)|\leq\sqrt{n}
(by Cauchy–Schwarz).

Case
K\geq 2
.
Since
|f(Z)|\leq 1
,

\sum_{k\geq 2}\Pr[K=k]\;\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,|f(Z)|\;\middle|\;K=k\right]\;\leq\;\Pr[K\geq 2]\;\leq\;\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\Pr[Z_{i}\neq 0,\,Z_{j}\neq 0]=\binom{n}{2}p^{2}.

Combining the three cases gives

\left|\Phi_{p}(f)-p\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\hat{f}(i)|\right|\leq(n-1)\sqrt{n}\,p^{2}+\binom{n}{2}p^{2},

as claimed.
∎

Lemma 6

(
[
7
]
, Thm. 2.33)
.

Let
n
be odd. Among all Boolean functions
f:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
, the quantity

\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{f}(i)

is uniquely maximized by the majority function

\mathrm{Maj}_{n}(x)\;=\;\sgn(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}).

Theorem 7
.

Fix odd
n
. There exists
p_{0}=p_{0}(n)>0
such that for all
p\in(0,p_{0})
, the function
\mathrm{Maj}_{n}
is the unique maximizer of
\Phi_{p}
over unbiased Boolean
f
; i.e.,

\Phi_{p}(\mathrm{Maj}_{n})>\Phi_{p}(f)\qquad\text{for all unbiased }f\neq\mathrm{Maj}_{n}.

Proof.

By Lemma
5
,

\Phi_{p}(g)=p\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\widehat{g}(i)|+E_{g}(p),\qquad|E_{g}(p)|\leq C_{n}\,p^{2},

for every unbiased
g
, where
C_{n}:=(n-1)\sqrt{n}+\binom{n}{2}
.
Since
\Phi_{p}
and
\sum_{i}|\hat{g}(i)|
are invariant under flipping individual coordinates, we may assume w.l.o.g. that all level‑1 coefficients are nonnegative for the competitor
f
, so
\sum_{i}|\widehat{f}(i)|=\sum_{i}\widehat{f}(i)
and likewise for
\mathrm{Maj}_{n}
.

Let

\delta_{n}\ :=\ \min_{f\neq\mathrm{Maj}_{n}}\ \Big(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{\mathrm{Maj}_{n}}(i)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\widehat{f}(i)\Big)\ >\ 0,

which is strictly positive by Lemma
6
and finiteness of the domain
\{-1,1\}^{n}
for fixed
n
(uniqueness of the maximizer implies a positive gap). Then for any unbiased
f\neq\mathrm{Maj}_{n}
,

\Phi_{p}(\mathrm{Maj}_{n})-\Phi_{p}(f)=p\!\left(\sum_{i}\widehat{\mathrm{Maj}_{n}}(i)-\sum_{i}\widehat{f}(i)\right)+\bigl(E_{\mathrm{Maj}_{n}}(p)-E_{f}(p)\bigr)\;\geq\;p\,\delta_{n}-2C_{n}\,p^{2}.

Choose
p_{0}:=\min\{\delta_{n}/(4C_{n}),\,1\}
. Then for all
p\in(0,p_{0})
, we have
p\,\delta_{n}-2C_{n}p^{2}\geq\tfrac{1}{2}p\,\delta_{n}>0
, proving the claim and the uniqueness.
∎

Manual verification.

Multilinear expansions of majority functions.

The majority function
\mathrm{Maj}_{n}:\{-1,1\}^{n}\to\{-1,1\}
is defined for all odd
n
as

\mathrm{Maj}_{n}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\sgn(x_{1}+\cdots+x_{n}),

i.e.,
\mathrm{Maj}_{n}(x)=1
if a majority of the coordinates are
1
, and
-1
otherwise. Its unique multilinear expansions for
n=3,5
are

\displaystyle\mathrm{Maj}_{3}(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(x_{1}+x_{2}+x_{3})-\tfrac{1}{2}x_{1}x_{2}x_{3},

\displaystyle\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(x)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{3}{8}\sum_{i=1}^{5}x_{i}-\tfrac{1}{8}\!\!\sum_{1\leq i<j<k\leq 5}\!\!x_{i}x_{j}x_{k}+\tfrac{3}{8}\,x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}x_{4}x_{5}.

See Theorem 5.19 in
[
7
]
for the general formula for the Fourier coefficients of majority when
n
is odd.

Multilinear expansion of the counterexample
f
.

The counterexample found by GPT‑5 Pro,

f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{5})\;=\;\sgn\!\big(x_{1}-3x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{4}+3x_{5}\big),

admits the following factorized multilinear form:

f(x)=\tfrac{1}{4}\Big(2(-x_{2}+x_{5})+(1+x_{2}x_{5})\,(x_{1}+x_{3}-x_{4}+x_{1}x_{3}x_{4})\Big).

Indeed,
f
acts as follows: if
-x_{2}+x_{5}=0
, the output is the majority of
(x_{1},x_{3},-x_{4})
; otherwise it outputs the majority of
(-x_{2},x_{5})
. Thus

f(x)=\mathrm{Maj}_{3}\!\big(-x_{2},\,x_{5},\,\mathrm{Maj}_{3}(x_{1},x_{3},-x_{4})\big).

Plugging in
\mathrm{Maj}_{3}(a,b,c)=\tfrac{1}{2}(a+b+c-abc)
gives the displayed formula.

Expected value of
|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|
.

Extend functions on
\{-1,1\}^{5}
to
\{-1,0,1\}^{5}
by evaluating their multilinear expansions at
z\in\{-1,0,1\}^{5}
. Let
z
be drawn from the erasure model and set

K=\#\{i:z_{i}\neq 0\}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(5,p),\hskip 18.49988pt\Pr(K=k)=\binom{5}{k}p^{k}(1-p)^{5-k}.

Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\,|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{5}\mathbb{E}\!\left[\,|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|\mid K=k\right]\Pr(K=k)

\displaystyle=0\cdot(1-p)^{5}+\tfrac{3}{8}\cdot 5p(1-p)^{4}+\tfrac{3}{8}\cdot 10p^{2}(1-p)^{3}

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt+\tfrac{5}{8}\cdot 10p^{3}(1-p)^{2}+\tfrac{5}{8}\cdot 5p^{4}(1-p)+1\cdot p^{5}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{15}{8}p-\tfrac{15}{4}p^{2}+\tfrac{25}{4}p^{3}-\tfrac{45}{8}p^{4}+\tfrac{9}{4}p^{5}.

Here are the conditional expectations, by symmetry (we may fix which coordinates are nonzero and average over their signs).
Case
K=0
:
z=(0,0,0,0,0)
, so
|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|=0
.

Case
K=1
: without loss of generality (by symmetry)
z_{1}\neq 0
, so
|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|=\tfrac{3}{8}
.

Case
K=2
: assume
z_{1},z_{2}\neq 0
, then
\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)=\tfrac{3}{8}(z_{1}+z_{2})
, hence the conditional expectation is
\tfrac{3}{8}
.

Case
K=3
: assume
z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\neq 0
, then
\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)=\tfrac{3}{8}(z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3})-\tfrac{1}{8}z_{1}z_{2}z_{3}
; a check over
\{\pm 1\}^{3}
gives
\tfrac{5}{8}
.

Case
K=4
: assume
z_{1},\dots,z_{4}\neq 0
,
z_{5}=0
. Writing
m
for the number of
-1
’s among
(z_{1},\dots,z_{4})
, one gets
|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|=0
iff
m=2
(6 of 16 points) and
=1
otherwise; hence
\tfrac{5}{8}
.

Case
K=5
: trivially the value is
1
.

Expected value of
|f(z)|
.

Set

M:=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\big(z_{1}+z_{3}-z_{4}+z_{1}z_{3}z_{4}\big).

Using the factorized form,

f(z)=\tfrac{1}{2}(-z_{2}+z_{5})+\tfrac{1}{2}(1+z_{2}z_{5})M.

Taking the conditional expectation over
(z_{2},z_{5})
yields

\mathbb{E}|f(z)|=\frac{1-2p+2p^{2}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}|M|+p-\frac{p^{2}}{2},

since the four cases are: both zero (prob.
(1-p)^{2}
), giving
\tfrac{1}{2}|M|
; exactly one zero (prob.
2p(1-p)
), giving
\tfrac{1}{4}(|M+1|+|M-1|)=\tfrac{1}{2}
because
M\in[-1,1]
; both nonzero and equal (prob.
p^{2}/2
), giving
|M|
; and both nonzero and opposite (prob.
p^{2}/2
), giving
1
.

It remains to compute
\mathbb{E}|M|
by the number of nonzeros among
(z_{1},z_{3},z_{4})
: with probability
(1-p)^{3}
there are none and
|M|=0
; with probability
3p(1-p)^{2}
there is exactly one, yielding
|M|=\tfrac{1}{2}
; with probability
3p^{2}(1-p)
there are exactly two, again averaging to
\tfrac{1}{2}
; and with probability
p^{3}
there are three, in which case
M
is the majority of three bits and
|M|=1
. Therefore,

\mathbb{E}|M|=\tfrac{3}{2}p-\tfrac{3}{2}p^{2}+p^{3},

and hence

\mathbb{E}|f(z)|=\frac{7}{4}p-\frac{11}{4}p^{2}+\frac{7}{2}p^{3}-\frac{5}{2}p^{4}+p^{5}.

At
p=0.4
this gives
\mathbb{E}|f(z)|=\frac{2689}{6250}=0.43024
, whereas the earlier computation yields
\mathbb{E}\,|\mathrm{Maj}_{5}(z)|=\frac{5363}{12500}=0.42904
.

Para-complex geometry and cyclic Higgs bundles
Nicholas Rungi, Andrea Tamburelli

Abstract
. We introduce para-complex and pseudo-Riemannian geometric methods for the study of representations of surface groups in SL ⁡ ( 2 ​ m + 1 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R}) . For m = 1 m=1 our techniques allow to recover several known results for Hitchin representations without any reference to convex projective geometry or hyperbolic affine spheres. In particular, we describe analytically the Guichard-Wienhard domain of discontinuity in the flag variety and the corresponding concave foliated flag structure of Nolte-Riestenberg. In higher rank, we obtain a one-to-one correspondence between stable cyclic SL ⁡ ( 2 ​ m + 1 , ℝ ) \mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R}) -Higgs bundles (not necessarily in the Hitchin component) and a special class of surfaces, which we call isotropic 𝐏 \mathbf{P} -alternating, in the para-complex hyperbolic space ℍ τ 2 ​ m \mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau} . As a result, we give a geometric interpretation to the holomorphic differential q 2 ​ m + 1 q_{2m+1} in the Hitchin base in terms of harmonic sequences for immersions in para-complex manifolds.



1.
Introduction

The study of smooth manifolds defined over the algebra of para-complex numbers
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}:=\{z=x+\tau y\ |\ \tau^{2}=1,\ x,y\in\mathbb{R}\}
(
[
Coc49
]
), has gained significant interest in recent years, both from a purely mathematical perspective and for its applications in theoretical physics (see
[
CFG96
]
for a survey of both topics). On the one hand, results analogous to those in the theory of complex manifolds have been established, while on the other hand, many new phenomena have been discovered, partly due to the different behavior of the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
compared to complex numbers.

The main objective of this article is to show how para-complex and pseudo-Riemannian geometry find an application in the study of representations of fundamental groups of closed surfaces into
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, although this does not preserve any bilinear form in
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
. More generally, this problem falls within the framework of
higher Teichmüller theory
(
[
Wie18
]
), which has developed following Hitchin’s seminal work on the existence of a special connected component in the representation space (
[
Hit92
]
). Introducing a hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
defined over the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
, and naturally endowed with a para-Kähler metric, one can compute its isometry group and prove it is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
. Through the use of stable
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles, we can establish the existence of certain
\rho
-equivariant surface immersions in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
, where
\rho
is a representation into
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
that is not necessarily Hitchin. Moreover, a detailed analysis of the properties of these immersions allows us to derive geometric insights into their moduli space and, more broadly, into the representation space. Interestingly enough, the holomorphic differential
q_{2m+1}
of order
2m+1
, naturally associated with the cyclic Higgs bundle — not necessarily of Hitchin type — admits a geometric interpretation in terms of these immersions. More generally, we show that there exists a method to derive such a holomorphic differential from minimal maps between a Riemann surface and a para-Kähler manifold with constant para-holomorphic sectional curvature. Finally, in the case
m=1
, we recover the Labourie-Loftin parametrization of the Hitchin component (
[
Lof01
,
Lab07
]
) and construct geometric structures on the projectivised tangent bundle of the surface modeled on the flag variety
\mathcal{F}
given by pairs of lines and planes
(l,H)
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with
l\subset H
, which we show to be precisely those found by Guichard-Wienhard (
[
GW12
]
) using the theory of domains of discontinuity for Anosov representations.

We believe that this setting may prove itself useful for the study of Anosov representations of more general discrete groups in
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
with respect to the parabolic subgroup that fixes a line and a hyperplane or for the development of a universal higher Teichmüller theory for
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, the Anosov property furnishes a boundary map with values in
\mathcal{F}
, which is exactly the boundary at infinity of the para-complex hyperbolic space mentioned previously. It is conceivable that an asymptotic Plateau problem for these kinds of boundary data, not necessarily equivariant under a group action, may be solved, thus obtaining new examples of higher higher Teichmüller spaces, in the spirit of
[
SST23
,
BK23
]
.

1.1.
Immersions into a para-Kähler space

A
para-complex
structure on a smooth manifold
M
of real dimension
2n
is an endomorphism
\mathbf{P}
of the tangent bundle such that:

(i)

\mathbf{P}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}

(ii)

The two eigendistributions
D_{\pm}:=\mathrm{Ker}\big(\mathbf{P}\mp\mathrm{Id}\big)
both have rank
n
and are involutive.

The pair
(M,\mathbf{P})
will be called a
para-complex manifold
of para-complex dimension
n
. Moreover, if there exists a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear two-tensor
g
on
(M,\mathbf{P})
such that

g(\mathbf{P}X,\mathbf{P}Y)=-g(X,Y),\quad\text{for any}\ X,Y\in\Gamma(TM)

then
g
defines a pseudo-Riemannian metric on
M
of signature
(n,n)
, and the triple
(M,\mathbf{P},g)
is called a
para-Hermitian manifold
. It is easily verified that the pairing

\omega:=g(\cdot,\mathbf{P}\cdot)

defines a 2-form on
M
, known as the fundamental 2-form of
(M,\mathbf{P},g)
. If, in addition,
\omega
is a symplectic 2-form, i.e.,
\mathrm{d}\omega=0
, then the manifold
(M,\mathbf{P},g,\omega)
is called
para-Kähler
.

The para-Kähler homogeneous space we wish to introduce can be defined as a hyperbolic space over the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
: given a para-Hermitian bilinear form
\mathbf{q}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\tau}
, the para-complex hyperbolic space is

\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\tau}=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{\tau}\ |\ \mathbf{q}(z,z)=-1\}/\mathcal{U},

where
\mathcal{U}
is the subgroup of unitary para-complex numbers acting by scalar multiplication. There is a natural notion of boundary at infinity given by projective classes of
\mathbf{q}
-isotropic vectors. It can be shown that this space is homeomorphic to the partial flag manifold
\mathcal{F}_{1,n}
, obtained as the quotient
\mathrm{SL}(n+1,\mathbb{R})/P_{1,n}
, where
P_{1,n}
is the stabilizer of a pair of line
\ell
and a hyperplane
H
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
with
\ell\subset H
. It is easy to see — by analogy with the complex case — that
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{n}
is a para-Kähler manifold of para-complex dimension
n
. We will denote such structure by
(\mathbf{P},g,\omega)
. Moreover, the group of para-holomorphic isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{n}_{\tau}
is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(n+1,\mathbb{R})
.

To introduce special immersions of oriented and connected surfaces
S
, we must restrict ourselves to the case where
n=2m
with
m\geq 1
. In this setting, the isometry group of the space becomes
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, and
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
is a para-Kähler manifold equipped with a pseudo-Riemannian metric of signature
(2m,2m)
. The idea is to consider all immersions
\sigma:S\to\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
whose pullback tangent bundle admits an orthogonal splitting

\sigma^{*}T\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}=\mathscr{L}_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{2m}

into real rank
2
sub-bundles with
\mathscr{L}_{1}\cong TS
and such that the following properties hold:

\bullet

The pull-back metric
\sigma^{*}g
restricted to
\mathscr{L}_{i}
is non-degenerate and positive-definite when
i\in\{1,\dots,2m\}
is odd;

\bullet

the pull-back of the Levi-Civita connection admits a decomposition

\nabla=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{1}&-\eta_{2}^{\dagger}&\\
\eta_{2}&\nabla^{2}&-\eta_{3}^{\dagger}&&\\
&\eta_{3}&\nabla^{3}&\ddots\\
&&\ddots&\ddots&-\eta_{2m}^{\dagger}\\
&&&\eta_{2m}&\nabla^{2m}\end{pmatrix}

where
\nabla^{i}
is a metric connection on
\mathscr{L}_{i}
,
\eta_{i}
is a
1
-form with values in
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{L}_{i-1},\mathscr{L}_{i})
and
\eta_{i}^{\dagger}
is its adjoint. Moreover, we assume
\eta_{i}(X):\mathscr{L}_{i-1}\rightarrow\mathscr{L}_{i}
to be conformal for all
X\in\Gamma(TS)
and for all
i=2,\dots,2m
;

\bullet

the pull-back symplectic form
\sigma^{*}\omega
vanishes when restricted to each
\mathscr{L}_{i}
;

\bullet

the para-complex structure
\mathbf{P}
alternates the rank-2 subbundles, i.e.,

\mathbf{P}(\mathscr{L}_{2k-1})\cong\mathscr{L}_{2m-2(k-1)},

so that
\sigma^{*}g
is negative definite on
\mathscr{L}_{2k}
, for
k=1,\dots,m
.

We will refer to the newly introduced immersions as
isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating
surfaces (Definition
3.1
and
3.3
), and they can actually be defined in any para-Kähler manifold of real dimension
4m
. A similar approach has been used by Nie (
[
Nie24
]
) in the study of
A-surfaces
in the
pseudo-hyperbolic space
of signature
(n,n)
for even
n
, or signature
(n+1,n-1)
for odd
n
. Instead, Collier-Toulisse (
[
CT24
]
) studied
alternating pseudo-holomorphic curves
in the pseudo-hyperbolic space of signature
(4,2)
. We will explain the relationship with their case in the final paragraph of the introduction.

The first result concerns the structure equations of the immersions that we will study in this article, which will be directly linked to the Hitchin equations of a certain Higgs bundle over the associated Riemann surface induced by the conformal class of the first fundamental form.

Theorem A
.

(Theorem
3.10
and Theorem
3.12
)

Let
\sigma:S\to\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
be a smooth isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersion with
m\geq 2
. Let us assume that
\sigma(S)
is not contained in any para-complex hyperbolic subspace of real codimension
4
, then its structural data is completely determined by the datum of
m
holomorphic
(1,0)
-forms
\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{m+1}
with values in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(L_{i-1},L_{i})
for
i=2,\dots,m
and in
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathbb{C}}(L_{m},\overline{L_{m}})
for
i=m+1
, where
L_{1},\dots,L_{m}
are holomorphic line bundles endowed with Hermitian metrics
\bm{h}_{1},\dots,\bm{h}_{m}
satisfying the following system of equations:

(1)

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{m}-|\eta_{m}|^{2}\bm{h}_{m}\bm{h}_{m-1}^{-1}+|\eta_{m+1}|^{2}\bm{h}_{m}^{-2}=0\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{j}-|\eta_{j}|^{2}\bm{h}_{j}\bm{h}_{j-1}^{-1}+|\eta_{j+1}|^{2}\bm{h}_{j+1}\bm{h}_{j}^{-1}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-1\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{1}-\bm{h}_{1}+|\eta_{2}|^{2}\bm{h}_{2}\bm{h}_{1}^{-1}=0.\end{cases}

It is important to observe that the connection
\nabla
introduced above is determined by
2m
vector bundle-valued 1-forms
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{2m}
. However, in the previously stated theorem, it follows that only
m+1
of them are sufficient to express the structure equations of the immersions. This is a consequence of the
\mathbf{P}
-alternating property that we have imposed, which is made possible by the existence of the para-complex structure on
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
. This fact will be crucial in establishing a connection between
\mathbf{P}
-alternating isotropic immersions and cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles.

1.2.
The role of cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles

Given a fixed Riemann surface
X
of genus
g\geq 2
an
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle is a pair
(E,\phi)
where
E\to X
is a holomorphic bundle of rank
n
and
\phi
is a holomorphic
(1,0)
-form with values in trace-less endomorphisms bundle of
E
. In this article, we restrict ourselves to the case where the following decomposition holds:

E=L_{m}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{1}^{-1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{m},\quad\phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&&&&&&&&\gamma_{m}\\
\gamma_{m-1}&0&&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&&&&&&\\
&&\gamma_{1}&0&&&&&\\
&&&\mu&0&&&&\\
&&&&\mu&0&&&\\
&&&&&\gamma_{1}&0&&\\
&&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&&\gamma_{m-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
L_{i}
are holomorphic line bundles,
0\neq\gamma_{i}\in H^{0}(X,L_{i}^{-1}L_{i+1}K)
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
,
0\neq\mu\in H^{0}(X,L_{1}K)
and
\gamma_{m}\in H^{0}(X,L_{m}^{-2}K)
. In the literature, such pairs
(E,\phi)
are referred to as cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles. There is a notion of stability condition (
[
Hit87
]
) that ensures the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the Hitchin equations, which corresponds to a unique Hermitian metric
H
on
E
compatible with the splitting into line bundles (
[
Col16
]
), namely:

H=\operatorname{diag}(h_{m}^{-1},\dots,h_{1}^{-1},1,h_{1},\dots,h_{m}),

where
h_{i}
is a Hermitian metric on
L_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
. Thanks to the work of Baraglia (
[
Bar10a
, §3.4.2]
), it is known that for
m=1
, Higgs bundles of the form

E=K\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus K^{-1},\qquad\phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&q_{3}\\
1&0&0\\
0&1&0\end{pmatrix}

give rise to hyperbolic affine spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, whose affine invariant, the Pick form, is closely related to the holomorphic cubic differential
q_{3}
. As soon as
m\geq 2
, the geometry associated with cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles remains largely unexplored, as it is not clear how to construct affine immersions in higher codimension. The answer is provided by para-complex geometry, or more precisely by the space
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
, as suggested by the following result.

Theorem B
.

(Theorem
4.7
)

Let
(E,\phi)
be a stable cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle over
X
with holonomy
\rho
. Assume further that
L_{1}=K^{-1}
and
\mu=1
. Then there exists a
\rho
-equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface
\sigma:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
. Moreover, the structural data of
\sigma
is exactly the holomorphic data
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})
used to construct the Higgs bundle along with the harmonic metric
h_{i}
on each
L_{i}
.

The hypothesis that
L_{1}\cong K^{-1}
has been included to ensure a well-defined Riemannian metric on the immersed surface. The statement remains valid even without this assumption, provided that the Higgs bundle is stable. At the end of Section
5.2
, we study the case in which the Higgs bundle satisfies
\gamma_{m-1}\equiv 0
and is therefore strictly polystable. Since solutions to the Hitchin equations still exist, one obtains isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces that are equivariant with respect to reducible representations, to which we are able to assign a geometric interpretation in terms of the immersion.

The main idea behind Theorem
B
has been the
para-complexification
of a Higgs bundle, a construction that, as we will explain, holds in a fairly general setting. In short, starting from an
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle
(E,\phi,Q)
, one can construct a new triple
(E^{\tau},\phi^{\tau},\mathbf{P})
, where
E^{\tau}
now has rank
2n
,
\phi^{\tau}=\phi\otimes e_{+}-\phi\otimes e_{-}
, and
\mathbf{P}
is a para-complex structure on
E^{\tau}
(in addition to having a natural holomorphic structure induced by
E
), where
e_{\pm}
is the basis of idempotents of the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
. In the
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-cyclic case, we are able to construct a
\rho
-equivariant map
\sigma:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
by using parallel transport on the para-complexification of the trivial section inside
E^{\tau}
, which comes from the presence of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
in the decomposition of
E
. Moreover, the geometry of
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
allows us to provide a geometric interpretation for the holomorphic differential
q_{2m+1}=\mu(\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{m-1})^{2}\gamma_{m}
of order
2m+1
, which is naturally associated with any such cyclic Higgs bundle (see the end of Section
4.3
). This result is a special case of a more general theorem, presented in Appendix
A
, which applies to minimal maps from a Riemann surface into a para-Kähler manifold with constant para-holomorphic sectional curvature. In analogy with the complex case (
[
Woo84
]
), it is possible to define an
isotropic order
for the minimal map and obtain an explicit definition of a holomorphic differential on the Riemann surface, whose order depends on the isotropic order of the map.

Restricting to the Higgs bundles such that
L_{i}^{-1}\cong K^{i}
and
\gamma_{i-1}\equiv 1
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
, their stability can be controlled using
d:=\deg(L_{m}^{-1})\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
L_{m}
is the unique line bundle that is not isomorphic to a power of the canonical bundle (Lemma
5.4
). In particular, denoting by
\mathcal{ML}_{d}(X)_{m}
the moduli space of isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersions constructed from the Higgs bundles just described with fixed degree
d\in\mathbb{Z}
, we obtain

Theorem C
.

(Theorem
5.7
)

For any
m\geq 2
,

(i)

if
0<d\leq m(2g-2)
the set
\mathcal{ML}_{d}(X)_{m}
is biholomorphic to the total space of a holomorphic vector bundle of complex rank
2d+g-1
over the
(m(2g-2)-d)
-th symmetric product of
X
;

(ii)

if
1-g\leq d\leq 0
the set
\mathcal{ML}_{d}(X)_{m}
is biholomorphic to a bundle over an
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
-cover of the
(2d+2g-2)
-symmetric product of
X
whose fiber is
(\mathbb{C}^{(2m-1)(g-1)-d}\setminus\{0\})/\{\pm\mathrm{Id}\}
.

(iii)

if
d\notin[1-g,\dots,m(2g-2)]\cap\mathbb{Z}
then
\mathcal{ML}_{d}(X)_{m}
is empty.

Moreover, the space
\mathcal{ML}_{d}(X)_{m}
is smooth for any
d\in[1-g,m(2g-2)]\cap\mathbb{Z}
.

The condition imposed on the Higgs bundles in the previously stated theorem is always satisfied when
m=2
. Moreover, since the stability condition can be controlled by examining the degrees of the various line bundles that constitute the decomposition of
E
(
[
DL20
, §2.5]
), it is plausible that a similar result could be obtained in the general case, although the description of the moduli spaces as bundles would become significantly more intricate.

1.3.
Higher rank Teichmüller theory

In the early 1990s, a fundamental result proved by Hitchin (
[
Hit92
]
) initiated the study of representations of surface groups of compact, oriented surfaces
\Sigma
of genus
g\geq 2
into real split semisimple Lie groups
G
of rank greater than or equal to 2. Classical theory deals with discrete and faithful representations into
\mathbb{P}\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
and is directly connected to the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, which represents the deformation space of hyperbolic structures defined on the surface, and it is diffeomorphic to a connected component of the space of such representations. The main theorem proved by Hitchin implies that, in the case of rank
r\geq 2
, there exists a connected component
\mathrm{Hit}(\Sigma,G)
in the representation space that contains
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
and is diffeomorphic to an open ball of dimension
(2g-2)\dim G
, which is nowadays referred to as the
Hitchin component
. Despite the very general nature of the result, a geometric description of such representations had not been obtained, due to the differential-geometric and holomorphic nature of the tools used by Hitchin. As previously explained, we are interested in the case where
G=\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, whose representation space will be denoted by
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
, and the Hitchin component by
\mathrm{Hit}_{m}(\Sigma)
. When
m=1
, it was shown that any
\rho\in\mathrm{Hit}_{1}(\Sigma)
preserves a properly convex set
\mathcal{C}
in
\mathbb{R}\mathbb{P}^{2}
, and the quotient
\mathcal{C}/\rho(\pi_{1}(\Sigma))
induces a projective structure on the surface (
[
Gol90
]
). Moreover, the deformation space of all such convex structures is diffeomorphic to
\mathrm{Hit}_{1}(\Sigma)
(
[
Kui53
,
CG97
]
). Later, it was established that every Hitchin representation in
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
preserves a special convex immersion
f:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
known as
hyperbolic affine sphere
, which allowed, among other things, the holomorphic parametrization of
\mathrm{Hit}_{1}(\Sigma)
as a holomorphic bundle over
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
(
[
Lof01
,
Lab07
]
). As soon as
m\geq 2
, no geometric description is known for Hitchin representations in terms of convex structures or equivariant surfaces. Our work aims to bridge this gap by using equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersions in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
, which, as implied by Theorem
B
, exist both for cyclic Hitchin representations and for representations in the other two connected components of the representation space
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
(
[
Hit92
]
). Let us denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{ML}}(\Sigma)_{m}
the moduli space of such equivariant surfaces, which naturally carries a fibre bundle structure over the Teichmüller space, given by

\displaystyle\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{ML}}

\displaystyle(\Sigma)_{m}\to\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle[\sigma,\rho]\longmapsto[J]

where
J
is the complex structure on
\Sigma
induced by the first fundamental form. An additional technical hypothesis is required on the isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces in order to obtain results on the representation space. From Theorem
A
, we know that
\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{m+1}
can be regarded as holomorphic
(1,0)
-forms with values in a holomorphic vector bundle. Let us denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{ML}}(\Sigma)_{m}^{*}
the subspace consisting of all immersions in
\widetilde{\mathcal{ML}}(\Sigma)_{m}
that satisfy

(\eta_{2})\prec(\eta_{3})\prec\dots\prec(\eta_{m+1}),

where
(\eta_{i})
represents the divisor of the holomorphic
(1,0)
-form.

Theorem D
.

(Thereom
5.10
and Corollary
5.11
)

The holonomy map

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hol}:\widetilde{\mathcal{ML}}

\displaystyle(\Sigma)_{m}^{*}\longrightarrow\chi_{m}(\Sigma)

\displaystyle[\sigma,\rho]\longmapsto[\rho]

is an immersion for any
m\geq 1
.

The hypothesis on divisors, also used by Nie for A-surfaces (
[
Nie24
]
), allows us to establish an infinitesimal rigidity property for isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersions, which is always satisfied in the Hitchin case. Theorem
D
was previously proven by Labourie (
[
Lab17
]
) using the theory of cyclic surfaces, but only in the case of representations in
\mathrm{Hit}_{m}(\Sigma)
. In particular, our proof simplifies his argument by providing a geometric description of the image of the holonomy map and extends the result to the other two connected components of the representation space. In the case
m=1
, we recover the holomorphic parametrization of the Hitchin component for
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
by Labourie-Loftin (
[
Lof01
,
Lab07
]
) using only the isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersions in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2}
. In this case, these immersions correspond to maximal space-like Lagrangian surfaces, which are in fact equivalent to hyperbolic affine spheres in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
(see
[
RT24
]
and
[
Hil11
]
).

Focusing on the case
m=1
, the theory of Anosov representations allowed Guichard and Wienhard (
[
GW12
]
) to construct, for instance, from a Hitchin representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, a domain of discontinuity
\Omega_{\rho}
inside the unique flag variety
\mathcal{F}
. In particular, the group
\Gamma:=\rho\big(\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\big)
acts properly discontinuously and cocompactly on
\Omega_{\rho}
, such that the quotient
\Omega_{\rho}/\Gamma
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{P}T\Sigma
, thereby endowing it with a
\big(\mathcal{F},\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})\big)
-geometric structure. The final result concerns the construction of geometric structures using maximal space-like Lagrangian surfaces in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2}
, which turn out to be precisely those found by Guichard and Wienhard.

Theorem E
.

(Theorem
5.15
)

Given a maximal space-like Lagrangian immersion
\sigma:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2}
equivariant with respect to a Hitchin representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R})
, there exists a
(\mathcal{F},\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{R}))
-geometric structure on
\mathbb{P}T\Sigma
that coincides with the one found by Guichard-Wienhard.

As stated at the beginning of the introduction, Nie (

2.1.
Para-complex numbers

Let us recall the definition and some basic properties of para-complex numbers (also known as split-complex or hyperbolic numbers), initially introduced in
[
Coc49
]
and subsequently studied by many authors in various different contexts.

The set of para-complex numbers, denoted with
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
, is defined as the commutative algebra over
\mathbb{R}
generated by
\{1,\tau\}
where
\tau
is a non-real number such that
\tau^{2}=1
. In particular, any number
z\in\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
can be written as
z=x+\tau y
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
. There is a notion of conjugate para-complex number
\bar{z}^{\tau}:=x-\tau y
which permits to define the absolute value as

|z|_{\tau}^{2}=z\bar{z}^{\tau}=x^{2}-y^{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

The main difference compared to complex numbers is that the absolute value
|\cdot|_{\tau}^{2}
does not have a sign and can also be zero. In fact, it can be shown that para-complex numbers with zero absolute value are exclusively the zero divisors. Among these, the two numbers

e_{+}:=\frac{1+\tau}{2},\qquad e_{-}:=\frac{1-\tau}{2},

known as idempotent units
1
1

1

In fact, an easy computation shows that
e_{\pm}\cdot e_{\pm}=e_{\pm}
, enable the algebra
\mathbb{R}_{\tau}
to be realized as a direct product:

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}\oplus\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle z=x+\tau y\longmapsto(x+y,x-y).

In particular, since
e_{+}
and
e_{-}
are linearly independent, any arbitrary para-complex number
z=x+\tau y
can also be expressed as
z=z^{+}e_{+}+z^{-}e_{-}
, where
z^{+}:=x+y
and
z^{-}:=x-y
. We conclude by noting that the set of invertible numbers with absolute value equal to one

\mathcal{U}:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\ |\ |z|_{\tau}^{2}=1\}

is equal, as a set, to two hyperbolas in the real plane, and therefore to two copies of
\mathbb{R}
as a group. This stands in stark contrast to the complex case, where the counterpart of the group
\mathcal{U}
defined above is a copy of
S^{1}
, and is therefore compact.

2.2.
The hyperboloid model

In this section, we introduce the para-complex hyperbolic space in a manner similar to how complex hyperbolic space is treated in the literature. To the best of our knowledge, the definition we are about to present, appears for the first time in

]
(see also
[

Let us consider the

(2)

Q=\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&\iddots&\\

Definition 2.1

The

It can be easily deduced that it is a smooth real manifold of dimension

T_{z}\mathbb{R}_{\tau}^{n+1}=T_{z}\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{n}\oplus\mathbb{R}_{\tau}z.

Similarly to what occurs in the context of complex hyperbolic space, we can consider the group formed by all

Proposition 2.2

(

The map

\displaystyle\Psi:\mathrm{GL}(n

Remark 2.3

The unitary groups

The action of

2.3.
The para-Kähler metric

The aim of this section is to present a construction of the para-Kähler metric on

Recall that on

Noting that any

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}s}\Big|_{s=0}\mathbf{q}(z+sv,z+sv)

Definition 3.2

A smooth spacelike immersion

3.3.
Infinitesimal rigidity of isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces

In this section, we restrict to equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}

[
DL20
]
) and also appearing in Nie’s work (
[
Nie24
]
). We will show that this class of surfaces are infinitesimally rigid.

Let
\Sigma
be a closed, connected, oriented surface of genus at least
2
and let
\sigma:\widetilde{\Sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
be an isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface that is equivariant under a representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
. By equivariance, we can interpret the holomorphic data
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\bm{h}_{1},\dots,\bm{h}_{m},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{m+1})
as being defined on the Riemann surface
X=(\Sigma,J_{1})
, where

is the complex structure compatible with the induced metric on
\sigma(\widetilde{\Sigma})
. Moreover, by Theorem
3.10
, we can interpret
\eta_{i}
as holomorphic
1
-forms on
X
. Recall the following definition:

Definition 3.13
.

Given a holomorphic
1
-form
\alpha
on
X
, we denote by
(\alpha)
its divisor, i.e. the
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
-valued function on
X
assigning to every
z\in X
the multiplicity of the zero of
\alpha
at
z
. If
\beta
is another holomorphic
1
-form we say that
(\alpha)\prec(\beta)
if the divisor
(\alpha)
is strictly smaller than
(\beta)
at every point where
\alpha
vanishes.

In this section we consider equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
whose holomorphic data satisfy the extra technical assumption

(16)

(\eta_{2})\prec(\eta_{3})\prec\dots\prec(\eta_{m+1})\ .

This condition, as first observed by Dai-Li (
[
DL20
]
) and then Nie (
[
Nie24
]
), guarantees a better control on the behavior of the solutions of the system of PDEs in Equation (
11

[
ST24
]
). We do not know whether this assumption is necessary in order for the result to hold.

Theorem 3.14
.

Let
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\bm{h}_{1},\dots,\bm{h}_{m},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{m+1})
be the holomorphic data associated to an equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
that is not contained in any para-complex hyperbolic subspace of real codimension
4
. Assume that Equation (
16
) holds, then
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{i}\geq 0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
with strict inequality away from the zeros of
\eta_{i}
.

Proof.

The proof is a straightforward application of the maximum principle for systems proved by Dai-Li (
[
DL20
]
) and is an easy adaptation of the proof of a similar result appeared in the aformentioned paper. It is reported here mostly for reader’s convenience. We will explain the proof for
m\geq 4
: the other simpler cases can be easily deduced using the same ideas. We first assume that
\eta_{m+1}
is not identically zero and at the end we will point out how the proof has to be modified if
\eta_{m+1}=0
. By Theorem
3.12
, the following system of locally defined PDEs holds

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{m}-|\eta_{m}|^{2}\bm{h}_{m}\bm{h}_{m-1}^{-1}+|\eta_{m+1}|^{2}\bm{h}_{m}^{-2}=0\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{j}-|\eta_{j}|^{2}\bm{h}_{j}\bm{h}_{j-1}^{-1}+|\eta_{j+1}|^{2}\bm{h}_{j+1}\bm{h}_{j}^{-1}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-1\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log\bm{h}_{1}-\bm{h}_{1}+|\eta_{2}|^{2}\bm{h}_{2}\bm{h}_{1}^{-1}=0.\end{cases}

We observe that, after dividing each equation by
\bm{h}_{1}
, which is a Hermitian metric on
K^{-1}
, the equations above are actually defined globally on
X=(S,J_{1})
. We also set
\|\eta_{j+1}\|^{2}=\bm{h}_{j+1}\bm{h}_{1}^{-1}\bm{h}_{j}^{-1}|\eta_{j+1}|^{2}
for
j=1,\dots,m-1
and
\|\eta_{m+1}\|^{2}=\bm{h}_{1}^{-1}\bm{h}_{m}^{-2}|\eta_{m+1}|^{2}
so that we obtain

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}\log\bm{h}_{m}=\|\eta_{m}\|^{2}-\|\eta_{m+1}\|^{2}\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}\log\bm{h}_{j}=\|\eta_{j}\|^{2}-\|\eta_{j+1}\|^{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-1\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}\log\bm{h}_{1}=1-\|\eta_{2}\|^{2}.\end{cases}

We now set
v_{j}=\log\|\eta_{j}\|^{2}-\log\|\eta_{j+1}\|^{2}
for
j=2,\dots,m
and
v_{1}=-\log\|\eta_{2}\|^{2}
. These functions satisfy the following equations away from
P=\cup_{j}P_{j}
, where
P_{j}
is the (discrete) set of zeros of
\eta_{j}
:

(17)

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{1}=-c_{2}v_{2}+2c_{1}v_{1}\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{j}=-c_{j+1}v_{j+1}+2c_{j}v_{j}-c_{j-1}v_{j-1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-1\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{m}=3c_{m}v_{m}-c_{m-1}v_{m-1},\end{cases}

with
c_{k}=\frac{\|\eta_{k}\|^{2}-\|\eta_{k+1}\|^{2}}{v_{k}}\geq 0
for
j=2,\dots,m-1
and
c_{1}=\frac{1-\|\eta_{2}\|^{2}}{v_{1}}\geq 0
. Note that the assumption in Equation (
16
) implies that
v_{j}
is a smooth function on
X\setminus P_{j+1}
that tends to
+\infty
when
z
approaches a point in
P_{j+1}
and the functions
c_{k}
are smooth on
X\setminus P
and bounded on
X
for all

. Moreover,
P
is non-empty because
q=(\eta_{2}\dots\eta_{m})^{2}\eta_{m+1}
is a non-zero holomorphic
(2m+1)
-differential on
X
vanishing in
P
. Therefore, the system in Equation (
17
) satisfies the assumption of the maximum principle for systems (
[
DL20
, Lemma 3.1, Condition (2)]
) which implies that
v_{j}>0
and hence
\partial_{z\bar{z}}^{2}\log\bm{h}_{j}\geq 0
for all
j=1,\dots,m
with strict inequality away from the zeros of
\eta_{j}

If
\eta_{m+1}=0
, the proof follows the same lines after simply removing
\|\eta_{m+1}\|^{2}=0
and
v_{m}
in all the equations above, which thus become

\begin{cases}\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{1}=-c_{2}v_{2}+2c_{1}v_{1}\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{j}=-c_{j+1}v_{j+1}+2c_{j}v_{j}-c_{j-1}v_{j-1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-2\\
\frac{1}{4}\Delta_{\bm{h}_{1}}v_{m-1}=3c_{m-1}v_{m-1}-\|\eta_{m-2}\|^{2}\leq 3c_{m-1}v_{m-1}.\end{cases}

The only subtlety is that now the set

may be empty. However, since
\eta_{m-2}
is not identically zero by Theorem

[
DL20

can be used, which still implies that

everywhere on
X
for all

and thus we can conclude as before.
∎

We now are going to see that this analytical result implies that this family of isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
is stable. To do that, we first recall some well-known facts about variations of a spacelike maximal immersion
\sigma:S\rightarrow(M,\mathbf{g})
into a pseudo-Riemannian manifold. A variation of
\sigma
is a smooth
1
-parameter family
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}
of immersions such that
\sigma_{0}=\sigma
. The derivative
\dot{\sigma}:=\frac{d}{dt}{\sigma_{t}}_{|_{t=0}}
is a section of
\sigma^{*}TM
and is called
variational vector field
. We say that the variation is
compactly supported
if
\sigma_{t}=\sigma
outside a compact set of
S
and we say it is
normal
if
\dot{\sigma}_{0}
is a section of the bundle
\mathcal{N}
normal to
TS
inside
\sigma^{*}TM
for the pull-back metric
h=\sigma^{*}\mathbf{g}
. The following result is standard (
[
Anc11
]
):

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\sigma:S\rightarrow(M,\mathbf{g})
be a spacelike maximal immersion. Then for every compactly supported variation
\{\sigma_{t}\}_{t\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}

•

the area functional
t\to Area(\sigma(S))

has a critical point at
t=0
;

•

furthermore, if the variation is normal, the second derivative of the area functional at
t=0
is given by

(18)

\int_{S}\mathrm{tr}_{h}\left(h(\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}_{\cdot}\xi,\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}_{\cdot}\xi)-h(\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(\cdot),\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(\cdot))+R^{\nabla}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot,\xi)\right)

where
\xi=\dot{\sigma}
,
\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}
denoted the normal component of the Levi-Civita connection of
h
and
\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(\cdot)
is the shape operator of
\sigma
defined as the tangential component of
\nabla_{\cdot}\xi
;

•

if every
\sigma_{t}
is a spacelike maximal immersion then the normal component of
\dot{\sigma}
is a
Jacobi field
.

Jacobi fields are vector fields
\xi\in\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
that annihilate the integral in Equation (

L:\Gamma(\mathcal{N})\rightarrow\Gamma(\mathcal{N})
defined by

L\xi:=\Delta^{\mathcal{N}}\xi+\sum_{i,j=1}^{n}h(\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}(e_{i},e_{j}),\xi)\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}(e_{i},e_{j})-\mathrm{tr}_{h}(R^{\nabla}(\cdot,\xi)\cdot),

where
\Delta^{\mathcal{N}}
is the Laplacian of the connection on the normal bundle. Indeed, unpacking the definitions of the second fundamental form and the shape operator together with a simple integration by parts, we have

-\int_{S}h(L\xi,\xi)dVol_{h}=\int_{S}\mathrm{tr}_{h}\left(h(\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}_{\cdot}\xi,\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}_{\cdot}\xi)-h(\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(\cdot),\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(\cdot))+R^{\nabla}(\cdot,\xi,\cdot,\xi)\right)dVol_{h}

for any compactly supported normal variation
\xi
.

If we specialize to equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersions into
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
, which are maximal by Remark
3.5
, Theorem
3.15
implies the following:

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
\sigma:\widetilde{\Sigma}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
be an isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating immersion that is not contained in any para-complex hyperbolic subspace of real codimension
4
and whose holomorphic data
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\bm{h}_{1},\dots,\bm{h}_{m},\eta_{2},\dots,\eta_{m+1})
satisfy the assumption in Equation (
16

\mathcal{N}^{+}=\mathscr{L}_{3}\oplus\mathscr{L}_{5}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{2m-1}
and
\mathcal{N}^{-}=\mathscr{L}_{2}\oplus\mathscr{L}_{4}\oplus\dots\oplus\mathscr{L}_{2m}
denote the spacelike and timelike part of the normal bundle of
\sigma(\widetilde{\Sigma})
respectively. Then, for any compactly supported variation of
\sigma
, the associated second derivative of the area functional is positive if the variational vector field
\xi_{+}=\dot{\sigma}
is a non-zero section of
\mathcal{N}^{+}
and negative if the variational vector field
\xi_{-}=\dot{\sigma}
is a non-zero section of
\mathcal{N}^{-}

Proof.

The proof follows the same lines as
[
Nie24
, Theorem 5.5]
. We first decompose the connection on the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}
under the splitting
\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}^{+}\oplus\mathcal{N}^{-}
:

\nabla^{\mathcal{N}}=\begin{pmatrix}\nabla^{\mathcal{N}^{+}}&\Gamma_{0}^{-}\\
\Gamma_{0}^{+}&\nabla^{\mathcal{N}^{-}}\end{pmatrix}.

Here
\nabla^{\mathcal{N}^{\pm}}
is a connection on
\mathcal{N}^{\pm}
and
\Gamma_{0}^{\pm}
is a
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{N}^{\pm},\mathcal{N}^{\mp})
-valued
1
-form. Let
\xi_{\pm}
be a non-zero section of
\mathcal{N}^{\pm}
. We have to estimate the expression

\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{h}(h(\nabla^{\mathcal{N}^{\pm}}_{\cdot}\xi_{\pm},\nabla^{\mathcal{N}^{\pm}}_{\cdot}\xi_{\pm}))}_{(a)}+\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{h}(\Gamma_{0}^{\pm}(\cdot)\xi_{\pm},\Gamma_{0}^{\pm}(\cdot)\xi_{\pm})}_{(b)}-\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{h}(\mathbf{S}_{\xi_{\pm}}(\cdot),\mathbf{S}_{\xi_{\pm}}(\cdot))}_{(c)}+\underbrace{\mathrm{tr}_{h}(R^{\nabla}(\cdot,\xi_{\pm},\cdot,\xi_{\pm}))}_{(d)}

where every
"\pm"
is taken to be a
"+"
when dealing with
\xi_{+}
and a
"-"
when dealing with
\xi_{-}
. We will deal with each term separately.

Term (a)
.
It is positive (resp. negative) when considering
\xi_{+}
(resp.
\xi_{-}
) because
h
is positive (resp. negative) definite on
\mathcal{N}^{+}
(resp.
\mathcal{N}^{-}
).

Term (c)
.
We recall that the shape operator and the second fundamental form are related by the formula
h(\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}(X,Y),\xi)=h(X,\mathbf{S}_{\xi}(Y))
for all tangent vector fields
X,Y
. By Remark
3.5

\mathrm{I}\mathrm{I}(X,Y)
coincides with
\eta_{2}(X)Y
, which lies in
\mathscr{L}_{2}
. Therefore,
h(X,\mathbf{S}_{\xi_{+}}(Y))=0
for all

because
\xi_{+}
is orthogonal to
\mathscr{L}_{2}
and
h(\mathbf{S}_{\xi_{-}}(X),\mathbf{S}_{\xi_{-}}(X))\geq 0
for all
X\in\Gamma(TS)
because
h
is positive definite on
TS
. As a consequence, term (c) vanishes for
\xi_{+}
and is non-negative for
\xi_{-}
.

Term (d)
.
Let
\{e_{1},e_{2}\}
be an
h
-orthonormal basis of
TS
. Using the explicit expression of the curvature tensor of a para-Kähler manifold with constant para-holomorphic sectional curvature

(Lemma

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}(e_{i},\xi,e_{i},\xi)

\displaystyle=h(e_{i},e_{i})h(\xi,\xi)-h(\xi,e_{i})h(e_{i},\xi)+h(e_{i},\mathbf{P}e_{i})h(\mathbf{P}\xi,\xi)

4.
Cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles

In this section we recall some well-known facts about cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles over a Riemann surface
X
. We introduce their para-complexification and use it to prove the existence of an equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
for any given representation
\rho:\pi_{1}(X)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
associated to a family of stable cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles. In particular, we interpret Hitchin’s equations in this context as structure equations for this type of surfaces in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
.

4.1.
Definition, stability and moduli space

Let
X=(\Sigma,J)
be a fixed closed Riemann surface of negative Euler characteristic and denote by
K
its canonical bundle. An
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle over
X
is a triple
(E,\phi,Q)
, where

•

E
is a holomorphic vector bundle of rank
2m+1
over
X
with
\det(E)=\mathcal{O}_{X}
;

•

Q
is a non-degenerate holomorphic bilinear form on
E
;

•

\phi
is a holomorphic
1
-form with values in the bundle
\mathrm{End}_{0}(E)
of traceless endomorphisms of
E
that is compatible with
Q
, in the sense that
Q(\phi(v),w)=Q(v,\phi(w))
for all
v,w\in E
.

Note that the bilinear form
Q
induces an isomorphism between
E
and its dual
E^{*}
. The pair
(E,\phi)
alone would instead define an
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle.

We say that an
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle is cyclic if
E
decomposes into a direct sum of line bundles
E=L_{m}^{-1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{1}^{-1}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}\oplus L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{m}
and in this holomorphic splitting the Higgs field writes as

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&&&&&&&&\gamma_{m}\\
\gamma_{m-1}&0&&&&&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&&&&&&\\
&&\gamma_{1}&0&&&&&\\
&&&\mu&0&&&&\\
&&&&\mu&0&&&\\
&&&&&\gamma_{1}&0&&\\
&&&&&&\ddots&\ddots&\\
&&&&&&&\gamma_{m-1}&0\end{pmatrix}

where
0\neq\gamma_{i}\in H^{0}(X,L_{i}^{-1}L_{i+1}K)
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
,
0\neq\mu\in H^{0}(X,L_{1}K)
and
\gamma_{m}\in H^{0}(X,L_{m}^{-2}K)
. Note that
(\mu\gamma_{1}\dots\gamma_{m-1})^{2}\gamma_{m}\in H^{0}(X,K^{2m+1})
, thus it is a holomorphic
(2m+1)
-differential that we will denote by
q_{2m+1}
. The bilinear form
Q
in this case is represented by the matrix

Q=\begin{pmatrix}&&&1\\
&&\iddots&\\
&\iddots&&\\
1&&&\end{pmatrix}.

A cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle is thus determined by the holomorphic data
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\mu,\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})
.

To form the moduli space of
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles, we need to consider a notion of stability. There is an appropriate one for
G
-Higgs bundle for each Lie group
G
(
[
Sim94
,
GPGMiR12
]
), however, in our setting, we can consider an
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle as an
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle with extra structure, specifically given by the holomorphic bilinear form
Q
. It turns out (
[
GPGMiR12
]
) that in this case it is sufficient to check the stability condition for the
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle, which we now recall.

Definition 4.1
.

An
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle
(E,\phi)
is stable if, for any proper
\phi
-invariant subbundle
F
, we have
\deg(F)<0
. Moreover,
(E,\phi)
is polystable if
(E,\phi)
is a direct sum of stable Higgs bundles of degree
0
.

For cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles, the following result by Simpson (
[
Sim09
, Lemma 6.4]
) further simplifies the search for destabilizing subbundles:

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
(E,\phi)
be a cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundle with
E=L_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus L_{m+1}
. Then
(E,\phi)
is stable if and only if for all proper
\phi
-invariant holomorphic subbundles
F=F_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus F_{k}\subset E
with
F_{i}\subset E_{i}
, we have
\deg(F)<0
.

By results of Hitchin (
[
Hit87
]
) and Simpson (
[
Sim92
]
), the polystability condition is equivalent to the existence of a Hermitian metric
H
on
E
that solves Hitchin’s equations

(21)

F_{H}+[\phi,\phi^{*H}]=0\ ,

where
F_{H}
denotes the curvature of
H
and
\phi^{*H}
is the adjoint of
\phi
with respect to the metric
H
. It is well-known that these equations arise by asking the connection

(22)

\nabla=D_{H}+\phi+\phi^{*H}

to be flat, where
D_{H}
denotes the Chern connection of
H
. For an
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle
(E,\phi,Q)
the Hermitian metric
H
must also be compatible with
Q
, in the sense that
QH^{t}Q=H^{-1}
(see for instance
[
CL17
]
).

When
(E,\phi,Q)
is stable, there is a unique Hermitian metric
H
on
E
solving Hitchin’s equation. By
[
Col16
]
, the metric
H
is diagonal in the above decomposition of
E
into line bundles and can be written as

H=\mathrm{diag}(h_{m}^{-1},\dots,h_{1}^{-1},1,h_{1},\dots,h_{m}),

where
h_{i}
is a Hermitian metric on
L_{i}
for each
i=1,\dots,m
. In particular, Hitchin’s equations (
21
) simplify to

(23)

\begin{cases}\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log h_{m}-|\gamma_{m-1}|^{2}h_{m}h_{m-1}^{-1}+|\gamma_{m}|^{2}h_{m}^{-2}=0\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log h_{j}-|\gamma_{j-1}|^{2}h_{j}h_{j-1}^{-1}+|\gamma_{j}|^{2}h_{j+1}h_{j}^{-1}=0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ j=2,\dots,m-1\\
\partial^{2}_{z\bar{z}}\log h_{1}-|\mu|^{2}h_{1}+|\gamma_{1}|^{2}h_{2}h_{1}^{-1}=0.\end{cases}

The metric
H
together with the bilinear form
Q
defines a real structure on
E
: the bundle morphism
T:E\rightarrow E
given by
T(v)=H^{-1}Q\bar{v}
is a
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear involution and its fixed locus
\mathcal{E}
is parallel for
\nabla
.

We recall the following distinguished family of cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles:

Definition 4.3
.

A cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle belongs to the Hitchin component (
[
Hit92
]
) if
L_{i}=K^{-i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and the Higgs field is of the form

\phi=\begin{pmatrix}0&&&&q_{2m+1}\\
1&0&&&\\
&\ddots&\ddots&&\\
&&1&0&\\
&&&1&0\end{pmatrix},

where
q_{2m+1}
is a holomorphic
(2m+1)
-differential.

It turns out that all Higgs bundles in the Hitchin component are stable (
[
Hit92
]
).

We finally recall the equivalence relation needed to form the moduli space of Higgs bundles.

Definition 4.4
.

Two polystable
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles
(E,\phi)
and
(E^{\prime},\phi^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there is a holomorphic bundle automorphism
E\rightarrow E^{\prime}
that identifies
\phi
with
\phi^{\prime}
.

In our setting, this equivalence relation becomes more explicit:

Lemma 4.5
.

Two stable cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles determined by the data

(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\mu,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2},\dots,\gamma_{m-1},\gamma_{m})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ (L_{1}^{\prime},\dots,L_{m}^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\gamma_{1}^{\prime},\gamma_{2}^{\prime},\dots,\gamma_{m-1}^{\prime},\gamma_{m}^{\prime})

are isomorphic if
L_{i}=L_{i}^{\prime}
,
\mu^{\prime}=\lambda_{1}^{-1}\mu
,
\gamma_{i}^{\prime}=\lambda_{i}^{-1}\lambda_{i+1}\gamma_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots m-1
and
\gamma_{m}^{\prime}=\lambda_{m}^{-2}\gamma_{m}
, for some
\lambda_{i}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
.

Proof.

Such isomorphism is given by a diagonal gauge transformation
g
with
\det(g)=1
that preserves the bilinear form
Q
. As a result,

g=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{m}^{-1},\dots,\lambda_{1}^{-1},1,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{m}).

Since
\gamma_{i}\in H^{0}(X,L_{i}^{-1}L_{i+1}K)
for
i=1,\dots,m-1
,
\mu\in H^{0}(X,L_{1}K)
and
\gamma_{m}\in H^{0}(X,L_{m}^{-2}K)
, the gauge transformation
g
acts on
\gamma_{i}
and
\mu
as in the statement.
∎

By a result of Nitsure (
[
Nit91
]
) and Simpson (
[
Sim94
]
), isomorphism classes of polystable
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles over a compact Riemann surface
X
form a quasi-projective variety.

Finally, by the nonabelian Hodge correspondence, there is a homeomorphism between the moduli space of
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
-Higgs bundles and the character variety

\chi(\pi_{1}(X),\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C}))=\mathrm{Hom}(\pi_{1}(X),\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})//\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})\

given by associating to an equivalence class of a polystable Higgs bundle
(E,\phi)
the holonomy of the flat connection
\nabla
in Equation (
22
). In particular, polystable
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundles correspond to representations into a subgroup isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
.

4.2.
Para-complexification of Higgs bundles

In this section we introduce the notion of
para-complex
Higgs bundle over a Riemann surface
X
and describe a "para-complexification" procedure that from an
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle gives a para-complex one with isomorphic holonomy.

Definition 4.6
.

A para-complex Higgs bundle over a Riemann surface
X
is a triple
(F,\varphi,\mathbf{P})
, where

•

(F,\varphi)
is a Higgs bundle of rank
2k
;

•

\mathbf{P}
is a holomorphic section of
\mathrm{End}(F)
such that
\mathbf{P}^{2}=\mathrm{Id}
.

•

the Higgs field
\phi
is compatible with
\mathbf{P}
, in the sense that
\varphi(\mathbf{P}v)=\mathbf{P}\varphi(v)
for all
v\in F
.

Let
(E,\phi,Q)
be an
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle. Denote by
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}=\mathbb{R}_{\tau}\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
the algebra of bi-complex numbers (see e.g.
[
RT24
]
for more details). We define the
para-complexification
of
(E,\phi,Q)
as follows.
We consider the tensor product of each complex vector bundle in the fiber of
E
with
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}
and obtain a holomorphic vector bundle
E^{\tau}
of complex rank
2n
, in brief
E^{\tau}:=E\otimes_{\mathbb{C}}\mathbb{C}_{\tau}
. The holomorphic structure on
E^{\tau}
can be easily understood via the decomposition
E^{\tau}=(E\otimes e_{+})\oplus(E\otimes e_{-}
): holomorphic sections of
E^{\tau}
are of the form
\sigma^{\tau}=\sigma^{+}\otimes e_{+}+\sigma^{-}\otimes e_{-}
, with
\sigma^{\pm}
holomorphic sections of
E
. On
E^{\tau}
there is a natural para-complex structure
\mathbf{P}
given by multiplication by
\tau
on each fiber. We note that the subbundles
E\otimes e_{\pm}
are the eigenspaces for
\mathbf{P}
relative to the eigenvalues
\pm 1
. The bilinear form
Q
can be extended to a bilinear form on
E^{\tau}
by setting
Q^{\tau}=Q\otimes\mathrm{Id}=Q\otimes e_{+}+Q\otimes e_{-}
. We then define the Higgs field on
E^{\tau}
by

\phi^{\tau}=\phi\otimes\tau=\phi\otimes e_{+}-\phi\otimes e_{-}\ .

It is straightforward to verify that the triple
(E^{\tau},\phi^{\tau},\mathbf{P})
is a para-complex Higgs bundle according to Definition
4.6
. With this particular choice of
\phi^{\tau}
, the Higgs field is compatible with the para-hermitian metric induced by
Q^{\tau}
because

(\phi^{\tau})^{t}Q^{\tau}+Q^{\tau}\overline{\phi^{\tau}}^{\tau}=0.

This guarantees that, after setting
H^{\tau}:=H\otimes e_{+}+H\otimes e_{-}
, where
H
is the Hermitian metric solving Hitchin’s equations on
(E,\phi)
, the connection

\nabla^{\tau}:=D_{H^{\tau}}+\phi^{\tau}+(\phi^{\tau})^{*H^{\tau}}=(D_{H}+\phi+\phi^{*H})\otimes e_{+}+(D_{H}-\phi-\phi^{*H})\otimes e_{-}

is flat and has the following properties:

i)

the para-complex structure
\mathbf{P}
is parallel for
\nabla^{\tau}
;

ii)

\nabla^{\tau}
preserves the para-complex real subbundle
\mathcal{E}^{\tau}:=(\mathcal{E}\otimes e_{+})\oplus(\mathcal{E}\otimes e_{-})
. This is exactly the fixed locus of the
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear involution
T^{\tau}(v)=(H^{\tau})^{-1}Q^{\tau}\bar{v}
.

iii)

there is a
\nabla^{\tau}
-parallel para-Hermitian sesquilinear form defined by

\mathbf{q}^{\mathbb{C}}(v,w):=v^{t}Q^{\tau}\overline{T^{\tau}(w)}^{\tau}.

This restricts to a para-Hermitian metric on
\mathcal{E}
, which we will denote by
\mathbf{q}
. As a consequence,
\nabla^{\tau}
preserves a fiber bundle over
X
with fiber
\mathbb{H}^{n-1}_{\tau}
, obtained by considering vectors in
\mathcal{E}^{\tau}
of unit norm with respect to
\mathbf{q}
.

iv)

it follows from item iii) that the holonomy of
\nabla^{\tau}
lies in a subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{C}_{\tau})
isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(n,\mathbb{R})
. Indeed, since
\nabla^{\tau}
preserves the real subbundle
\mathcal{E}^{\tau}
and the Hermitian form
\mathbf{q}
, the holonomy of
\nabla^{\tau}
is contained in
\mathrm{SU}(n,\mathbb{R}_{\tau},\mathbf{q})
, which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SL}(n.\mathbb{R})
by Proposition
2.2
. More precisely, if
\Omega
is the matrix connection of
\nabla
, then, using the fact that
\phi=Q\phi^{t}Q
and
QH^{t}Q=H^{-1}
(which implies that
-D_{H}=QD_{H}^{t}Q
), the matrix connection of
\nabla^{\tau}
is

\Omega^{\tau}=\Omega\otimes e_{+}-Q\Omega^{t}Q\otimes e_{-}

and thus lies in the Lie algebra of
\mathrm{SU}(n,\mathbb{R}_{\tau},Q)
. In particular, for any loop
\gamma
on
X
, the holonomy of
\nabla^{\tau}
around
\gamma
is a matrix of the form

A_{\gamma}e_{+}+Q(A_{\gamma}^{t})^{-1}Qe_{-}\in\mathrm{SU}(n,\mathbb{R}_{\tau},Q),

where
A_{\gamma}
is the holonomy of
\nabla
along
\gamma
. Hence, the there is an explicit isomorphism between the holonomies of
\nabla^{\tau}
and
\nabla
.

4.3.
Construction of equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces

This entire section is dedicated to the proof of the following result:

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
(E,\phi,Q)
be a stable cyclic
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
-Higgs bundle over
X
with holonomy
\rho
. Assume further that
L_{1}=K^{-1}
and
\mu=1
. Then there exists a
\rho
-equivariant isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface
\sigma:\widetilde{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
. Moreover, the structural data of
\sigma
is exactly the holomorphic data
(L_{1},\dots,L_{m},\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{m})
used to construct the Higgs bundle along with the harmonic metric
h_{i}
on each
L_{i}
.

Proof.

Let
(E^{\tau},\phi^{\tau},Q^{\tau})
be the para-complexification of the Higgs bundle
(E,\phi,Q)
, as explained in the previous section. Fix a base point
x_{0}\in X

Remark 4.8
.

A priori, the surfaces that we can construct starting from the Higgs bundle in Theorem
4.7
do not necessarily satisfy assumption (
16
), which ensures the property of infinitesimal rigidity (see Section
3.3
). When the condition is satisfied, from Theorem
3.14
, it follows that the induced metric on such surfaces has strictly negative curvature, since it coincides with
h_{1}
. On the other hand, if the Higgs bundle is such that
L_{i}\cong K^{-i}
for every
i=1,\dots,m
(see Definition
4.3
), then property (
16
) is automatically satisfied, and the metric
h_{1}
induced on the surface in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
always has strictly negative curvature. This generalizes a well-known phenomenon for
m=1
, where the induced metric on the space-like maximal Lagrangian in
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2}
has negative curvature, as it coincides with the Blaschke metric of the associated hyperbolic affine sphere (
[
SW90
,
Wan90
,
Li19
,
RT24
]
).

Recently, Baraglia (
[
Bar10a
]
) introduced the concept of a harmonic sequence starting with a minimal map from a Riemann surface into a symmetric space of indefinite signature, by adapting the Riemannian setting (
[
Cal67
,
EW92
]
). In this latter case, particularly when the ambient space is Kähler, it has been showed by Wood (
[
Woo84
]
) that given a minimal map whose harmonic sequence satisfies a certain condition, one can derive a holomorphic differential on the surface. In our case, starting from
\sigma:\widetilde{X}\to\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
as in Theorem
4.7
, we can construct a harmonic sequence in the following inductive manner:

\sigma_{0}:=\sigma,\quad\sigma_{1}:=\nabla^{\tau}_{\partial_{z}}\sigma,\quad\dots,\quad\sigma_{k+1}:=\nabla^{\tau}_{\partial_{z}}\sigma_{k}=\underbrace{\nabla_{\partial_{z}}^{\tau}\cdots\nabla_{\partial_{z}}^{\tau}}_{\text{(k+1)-times}}\sigma.

It is important to note that this definition differs from the one given by Baraglia in
[
Bar10a
, §2.4.1]
, but it similarly allows us to derive a holomorphic differential on the surface if a certain condition is satisfied, as we will explain. Using calculations similar to those in the proof of Theorem
4.7
, one can compute the terms of the harmonic sequence and observe that the following identity holds:

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}=\gamma_{1}\cdots\gamma_{k-1}\big(l_{k}\otimes e_{+}+(-1)^{k-1}l_{k}\otimes e_{-}\big)\quad(\text{mod \ }\mathbb{C}_{\tau}\sigma_{k-1})

\displaystyle\bm{q}^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\sigma_{k},\overline{\sigma_{j}}\big)=0,\quad\text{for all}\ \ k+j\leq 2m,

\displaystyle\bm{q}^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\sigma_{m+1},\overline{\sigma_{m}}\big)=-q_{2m+1},\qquad\nabla_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\bm{q}^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\sigma_{m+1},\overline{\sigma_{m}}\big)=0.

where “mod
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}\sigma_{k-1}
” means that the equality holds up to additional linear combinations of
\sigma_{k-1}
with coefficients in
\mathbb{C}_{\tau}
. In other words, we are stating that the isotropic order of the harmonic map
\sigma
is
2m
and that
\bm{q}^{\mathbb{C}}\big(\sigma_{m+1},\overline{\sigma_{m}}\big)
is holomorphic. The construction of holomorphic differentials from harmonic maps is, in fact, a much more general phenomenon that holds for immersions into para-Kähler manifolds that are not necessarily locally isomorphic to
\mathbb{H}_{\tau}^{2m}
. We defer the general statement and the full proof with all details to Appendix
A
.

4.4.
Gauss map

In this section we are going to show that a natural Gauss map associated to an isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surface in
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
gives rise to a conformal harmonic map to the symmetric space
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)
. In particular, we use the isomorphism
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})\cong\mathrm{SU}(2m+1,\mathbb{R}_{\tau})
explained in Section
2.2
to provide a new description of the symmetric space in terms of para-complex geometry.

Classically, the symmetric space
\mathbb{X}_{m}=\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)
is identified with the space
\mathcal{M}
of positive definite inner products on
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
with unit determinant.
We give here a new interpretation in terms of the geometry of
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
. Let
\mathcal{N}
be the space of totally geodesic Lagrangian subspaces of
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
such that the restriction of the pseudo-Riemannian metric
g
of
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
is negative definite. Note that these are obtained by intersecting with
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
Lagrangian, negative definite
(2m+1)
-planes in
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}_{\tau}
and then taking the quotient by the action of
\mathcal{U}
.

Theorem 4.9
.

As homogeneous space,
\mathcal{N}
is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{X}_{m}
.

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
acts transitively on
\mathcal{N}
with stabilizer
\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)
.
Let
N\in\mathcal{N}
. We observe that we have a
g
-orthogonal decomposition

T\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}=TN\perp\mathbf{P}TN

because the restriction of
g
to
\mathbf{P}TN
is positive definite (and thus
TN\cap\mathbf{P}TN=\{0\}
) and

g(v,\mathbf{P}w)=\omega(v,w)=0

for all
v,w\in TN
since
N
is a Lagrangian submanifold. As a consequence, we also find that for all

\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}_{\tau}=\mathbb{R}\cdot p\perp\tau(\mathbb{R}\cdot p)\perp T_{p}N\perp\tau(T_{p}N).

Since isometries of
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
commute with the multiplication by
\tau
, an isometry preserving
N
is completely determined by its values on
p
and on a fixed orthonormal frame of
T_{p}N
. Since the
(2m+1)

T_{p}N
and

is negative definite, the stabilizer of

is isomorphic to
\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)
. Moreover, the group of isometries acts transitively on
\mathcal{N}
by the following argument: if

is another totally geodesic Lagrangian, negative definite subspace, then we have another orthogonal decomposition

\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}_{\tau}=\mathbb{R}\cdot p^{\prime}\perp\tau(\mathbb{R}\cdot p^{\prime})\perp T_{p^{\prime}}N^{\prime}\perp\tau(T_{p^{\prime}}N^{\prime})

with

. As isometry of
\mathbb{H}^{2m}_{\tau}
sending

can be constructed as the linear map sending
p
to
p^{\prime}
and an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
to one of
T_{p^{\prime}}N^{\prime}
.
∎

5.
Application to higher rank Teichmüller theory

Let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented smooth surface of genus
g\geq 2
. In this final section, we focus on the application to the study of surface group representations into
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, considered as the isometry group of para-complex hyperbolic space. After a brief review of the general theory, we initially concentrate on the study of the moduli space of isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces with a fixed Riemann surface structure, using the theory of cyclic Higgs bundles. Later, exploiting the infinitesimal rigidity of such surfaces, we show that the holonomy map is an immersion for
m\geq 2
and that it is a diffeomorphism onto a connected component of the representation space when
m=1
, thus recovering the Labourie-Loftin parameterization of the Hitchin component. Finally, the last application concerns the construction of geometric structures in the case
m=1
. In particular, we recover the structures defined by Guichard-Wienhard (
[
GW12
]
) with a completely different method.

5.1.
The representation space

This section aims to introduce the space of surface group representations into
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, explaining how to topologically distinguish the various connected components discovered by Hitchin (
[
Hit92
]
), and introduce some background materials on the topic.

The Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
is defined as the set of (quasi-)complex structures on
\Sigma
compatible with the fixed orientation, modulo the action of the group of diffeomorphisms isotopic to the identity, denoted by
\mathrm{Diff}_{0}(\Sigma)
. The Poincaré-Koebe uniformization theorem allows
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
to be identified with the space of representations
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{P}\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
that are discrete and faithful (also known as
Fuchsian representations
), modulo conjugation. In fact, the Teichmüller space corresponds to one of the two connected components of this representation space.

Much more in general, we can consider the representation space of surface groups into
\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
, namely the quotient of all completely reducible group homomorphisms
\rho:\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
by the conjugation action, which will be denoted by
\chi_{m}(\Sigma):=\chi(\pi_{1}(\Sigma),\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R}))
. This space is endowed with an induced topology and a real analytic structure such that, at smooth points, it has dimension
-4m(m+1)\chi(\Sigma)
(
[
Gol84
]
). Using the non-abelian Hodge correspondence and a Morse type function on the moduli space of Higgs bundles, Hitchin (
[
Hit92
]
) succeeded in counting the connected components of the space
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
.

Theorem 5.1

(
[
Hit92
]
)
.

The variety
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
has three connected components: the one containing the class of the trivial representation, the one consisting of representations whose associated flat
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
-bundles have non-zero second Stiefel-Whitney class and the one consisting of representations connected to those arising as uniformization. Moreover, the third one is contained in the smooth locus of
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
and it is diffeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{-4m(m+1)\chi(\Sigma)}
.

It must be noted that the there is no topological invariant which distinguishes the first component to the third one, as they are both formed by representations whose associated flat
\mathbb{R}^{2m+1}
-bundles have zero second Stiefel-Whitney class. The component containing the uniformizing representations is called the
Hitchin component
and can be characterized as the connected component in
\chi_{m}(\Sigma)
formed by deformations of the Fuchsian one, namely the post-composition of a Fuchsian representation
\pi_{1}(\Sigma)\to\mathbb{P}\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})
with the irreducible embedding
\mathbb{P}\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow\mathrm{SL}(2m+1,\mathbb{R})
. This component will be denoted by
\mathrm{Hit}_{m}(\Sigma)
and contains an isomorphic copy of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, as discussed above.

5.2.
The moduli space of isotropic
\mathbf{P}
-alternating surfaces

Let
\Sigma
be a closed oriented surfaces of genus

Let us consider a smooth manifold

(\text{dev},h)

is a group homomorphism called the

and

denotes the universal cover of

-structures, meaning that it is compatible with the earlier definition given by the maximal atlas. The set
\mathcal{D}_{(X,G)}(M)

-structures up to isotopy can be endowed with the compact-open topology using its equivalence with developing pairs

(X,G)
-structure (nearby) to the original one. Finally, we briefly explain a different method for defining an

Appendix A
Harmonic immersions in para-Kähler manifolds

We present here an extension of a well-known result for harmonic immersions of Riemann surfaces in Kähler manifolds of constant holomorphic sectional curvature (
[
Woo84
]
) to the para-Kähler setting.

Let
X
be a Riemann surface and
(M,\mathbf{P},g)
a para-Kähler manifold of dimension
2n
and constant para-holomorphic sectional curvature
K
. We denote by
\mathbf{q}
the para-Kähler metric

\mathbf{q}(V,W)=g(V,W)+\tau g(V,\mathbf{P}W)\ \ \ \text{for $V,W\in\Gamma(TM)$}.

Let
\mathbb{C}TM=TM\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{C}
be the complexified tangent space of
M
. We extend
\mathbf{q}
to a bilinear form
\mathbf{q}^{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{C}TM
that is
\mathbb{C}
-linear in the first entry and
\mathbb{C}
-antilinear in the second. We also extend the endomorphism
\mathbf{P}
to
\mathbb{C}TM
by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity and the Riemannian metric
g
to a complex bilinear form
g_{\mathbb{C}}
on
\mathbb{C}TM
.

Given a smooth immersion
\varphi:X\rightarrow M
, we extend its differential by
\mathbb{C}
-linearity to obtain a map
d_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi
from the complex tangent space
T^{\mathbb{C}}X
of
X
to
\mathbb{C}TM
. If
\nabla
denotes the Levi-Civita connection of
g
, the pull-back bundle
\varphi^{*}\mathbb{C}TM
on
X
inherits a natural connection
\overline{\nabla}
that is
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear and satisfies

\overline{\nabla}_{Z}V=\nabla_{d^{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(Z)}V

for all sections
Z\in\Gamma(TX)
and
V\in\Gamma(\varphi^{*}\mathbb{C}TM)
, where we identified
(\varphi^{*}\mathbb{C}TM)_{x}
with
T_{\varphi(x)}M
for all
x\in X
. For a complex coordinate
z
on
X
, we set

\varphi_{z,1}=\varphi_{z}:=d_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(\partial_{z})\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \varphi_{\bar{z}}:=d_{\mathbb{C}}\varphi(\partial_{\bar{z}})

and, more in general, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
,

\varphi_{z,m}=\underbrace{\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}\cdots\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}}_{\text{$(m-1)$}}\varphi_{z}\ .

Definition A.1
.

The isotropic order of a smooth map
\varphi:X\rightarrow M
is the largest integer
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\cup\{\infty\}
such that, in all local charts,

\eta_{\alpha,\beta}:=\mathbf{q}^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z,\alpha},\varphi_{\bar{z},\beta})=0

for all natural numbers
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
such that
\alpha+\beta\leq\gamma
.

The following theorem generalizes a well-known result for harmonic immersions in Kähler manifolds:

Theorem A.2
.

Let
\varphi:X\rightarrow M
be a harmonic map with isotropic order
\gamma+1
. Then

i)

for all integers
\alpha,\beta\geq 1
with
\alpha+\beta=\gamma+1
, we have
\eta_{\alpha,\beta}=(-1)^{\beta-1}\eta_{\gamma,1}
;

ii)

the tensor
\eta_{\gamma,1}dz^{\gamma+1}
is holomorphic.

Proof.

The first part of the statement follows from an elementary integration by part: details are left to the reader.

In order to prove ii), we first show by induction that the following property holds: for all integers
1\leq\alpha\leq\gamma
we have

(29)

\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\alpha}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\varphi_{z},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z},\dots,\varphi_{z,\alpha-1},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}\}\ .

For
\alpha=1
, the statement holds because

\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z}=0

by harmonicity of
\varphi
. Now, we assume the statement holds for
\alpha-1
, and we compute

\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\alpha}=\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}-R^{\nabla}_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\varphi_{\bar{z}})(\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})\ ,

where
R^{\nabla}_{\mathbb{C}}
denotes the
\mathbb{C}
-bilinear extension of the Riemann tensor on
(M,g)
. We analyse the two terms in the above expression separately:

•

by assumption
\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\varphi_{z},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z},\dots,\varphi_{z,\alpha-2},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-2}\}
hence, since
\mathbf{P}
is parallel,
\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}\in\mathrm{Span}\{\varphi_{z},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z},\dots,\varphi_{z,\alpha-1},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}\}
, as desired.

•

for the second term, using the formula for the Riemann tensor of a para-Kähler manifold with constant para-holomorphic sectional curvature
\kappa
(Lemma
2.6
), we get

\displaystyle R^{\nabla}_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\varphi_{\bar{z}})\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\kappa}{4}\Bigg[g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})\varphi_{\bar{z}}-g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{\bar{z}},\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})\varphi_{z}+g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})\mathbf{P}\varphi_{\bar{z}}

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{\bar{z}},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z}+2g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\mathbf{P}\varphi_{\bar{z}})\mathbf{P}\varphi_{z,\alpha-1}\Bigg]

and this belongs to

\displaystyle g_{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z},\varphi_{z,\alpha-1})

We can now prove that the tensor

\displaystyle\partial_{\bar{z}}\eta_{\gamma,1}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\bar{z}}(q^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z,\gamma},\varphi_{\bar{z}}))

\displaystyle=q^{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\gamma},\varphi_{\bar{z}})+q^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z,\gamma},\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}\varphi_{\bar{z}})

\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{z}}\varphi_{\bar{z}}=0

\displaystyle=q^{\mathbb{C}}(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\bar{z}}}\varphi_{z,\gamma},\varphi_{\bar{z}})

29

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{\gamma-1}a_{j}q^{\mathbb{C}}(\varphi_{z,j},\varphi_{\bar{z}})=\sum_{j=1}^{\gamma-1}a_{j}\eta_{j,1}=0.

∎

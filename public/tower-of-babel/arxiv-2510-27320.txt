The quasinormal modes of the rotating quantum corrected black holes
Jia-Ning Chen a , b , c {}^{a\,,b\,,c}, Zong-Kuan Guo b , a , c {}^{b\,,a\,,c}, Liang-Bi Wu a , c {}^{a\,,c}

Abstract
The quasinormal modes (QNMs) of a rotating quantum corrected black hole (RQCBH) are studied by employing the hyperboloidal framework for the scalar perturbation. This framework is used to cast the QNMs spectra problem into the two-dimensional eigenvalues problem, then the spectra are calculated by imposing two-dimensional pseudo-spectral method. Based on the resulting spectra, a parameter estimation pipeline for this RQCBH model with gravitational wave data is constructed by using pyRing in the ringdown phase. We find that, even when the RQCBH spectra exhibits a small deviation from the Kerr spectra, the strong correlation between the extra parameter coming from the quantum gravity theory and the intrinsic parameter of black hole may significantly affect the posterior distributions of the mass M M and the dimensionless spin a ¯ \bar{a} .

II
Rotating quantum corrected black holes

In this section, we give a brief review on the so-called rotating quantum corrected black holes (RQCBH). We derive the RQCBH by using the updated Newman-Janis generating method
Azreg-Aïnou 2014a
;
Azreg-Aïnou 2014b
, which has been successful in generating the rotating solutions in Boyer-Lindquist (B-L) coordinates from spherically symmetric static solutions. For such a rotating black hole, the corresponding spherically symmetric static solution is derived from
Lewandowski et al. 2023
, where its external metric reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}=-f(r)\mathrm{d}t^{2}+\frac{\mathrm{d}r^{2}}{f(r)}+r^{2}(\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}+\sin^{2}\theta\mathrm{d}\phi^{2})\,,

(1)

where the metric function
f(r)
reads

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{\alpha M^{2}}{r^{4}}\,,

(2)

with the parameter
\alpha=16\sqrt{3}\pi\gamma^{3}l^{2}_{p}
,
l_{p}=\sqrt{\hbar}
denoting the Planck length,
\gamma
being the Immirzi parameter, and
M
standing for the mass of the black hole. In the B-L coordinates
\{t,r,\theta,\varphi\}
, the corresponding RQCBH metric can be written as
Vachher and Ghosh 2025
;
Ali et al. 2024
;
Raza et al. 2025

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}s^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\mathrm{d}t^{2}-2a\sin^{2}\theta\Big(1-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\varphi

(3)

\displaystyle+\sin^{2}\theta\Big[\Sigma+a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\Big(2-\frac{\Delta-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta}{\Sigma}\Big)\Big]\mathrm{d}\varphi^{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{\Delta}\mathrm{d}r^{2}+\Sigma\mathrm{d}\theta^{2}\,,

where

\displaystyle\Delta=r^{2}+a^{2}-2Mr+\frac{\alpha M^{2}}{r^{2}}\,,\quad\Sigma=r^{2}+a^{2}\cos^{2}\theta\,,

(4)

and
a
is the angular momentum. The above rotating metric provides important insights into the behavior of rotating black holes in the presence of quantum eﬀects. It is important to note that in the limit
a\to 0
, RQCBH reduces to a spherical quantum corrected black hole
Lewandowski et al. 2023
and in the limit
\alpha\to 0
, RQCBH reduces to the Kerr spacetime.

Depending on the values of
\alpha
and
a
, there may be up to two positive roots of
\Delta
, and two horizons of RQCBH will be found. It’s not difficult to notice that the quantum correction parameter
\alpha
must be within the range
\alpha/M^{2}\leq 27/16
for the emergence of two horizons. In Fig.
1
, we show the parameter space for RQCBH. For the parameters below the red solid line, the black hole has two event horizons. For the parameters above the red solid line, there is no horizon in spacetime. The parameters on the red solid line indicate that the black hole is extreme. For these green points inside, further explanation will be provided in Sec.
III
. The red line is the boundary between black hole and non-horizon spacetime
Vachher and Ghosh 2025
;
Ali et al. 2024
. It is not difficult to find that its expression is

\displaystyle\bar{\alpha}(\bar{a})=\frac{1}{128}\Big(\sqrt{9-8\bar{a}^{2}}+3\Big)^{2}\Big(-4\bar{a}^{2}+\sqrt{9-8\bar{a}^{2}}+3\Big)\,,\quad\bar{\alpha}\equiv\frac{\alpha}{M^{2}}\,,\quad\bar{a}\equiv\frac{a}{M}\,.

(5)

Here, two parameter
\bar{\alpha}
and
\bar{a}
are both dimensionless.

III
QNMs spectra of Rotating quantum corrected black holes

We now proceed to an analysis of linearized perturbations of the RQCBH. Our focus will be on the QNMs spectra, for use in modelling the ringdown phase of the RQCBH. Given that this is a quantum effective solution, it is difficult to calculate general perturbations, so we study a test massless scalar field
\Phi
, which is an important simplifying approximation. This assumption allows us to focus on the Klein-Gordon equation for a massless scalar field given by

\displaystyle\square\Phi=0\,.

(6)

For similar thought, one can refer to
Taylor and Ritz 2025
in which a magnetic field is considered. The Klein-Gordon equation will be simplified in the B-L coordinates (
3
) into

\displaystyle\Big[\frac{(r^{2}+a^{2})^{2}}{\Delta}-a^{2}\sin^{2}\theta\Big]\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial t^{2}}+\frac{2aM\Big(2r-\frac{\alpha M}{r^{2}}\Big)}{\Delta}\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial t\partial\varphi}

\displaystyle+\Big(\frac{a^{2}}{\Delta}-\frac{1}{\sin^{2}\theta}\Big)\frac{\partial^{2}\Phi}{\partial\varphi^{2}}-\frac{\partial}{\partial r}\Big(\Delta\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial r}\Big)-\frac{1}{\sin\theta}\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Big(\sin\theta\frac{\partial\Phi}{\partial\theta}\Big)=0\,.

(7)

It is known that there are many effective methods for getting the QNMs spectra, such as the continued fraction technique
Leaver 1985
, asymptotic iteration method
Cho et al. 2010
;
Cho et al. 2012
, Wentzel-Kramers-Brillouin (WKB) approximation
Konoplya 2003
;
Konoplya et al. 2019
and the pseudo-spectral method
Jansen 2017
;
Jaramillo et al. 2021
. Among these methods, the continuous fraction method and the pseudo-spectral method are easily generalized to the case of rotation. Two dimensional pseudo-spectral methods are applied to solve the QNMs spectra
Xiong and Li 2024
;
Cai et al. 2025
;
Assaad and Panosso Macedo 2025
, which will be used in this work. By combining the hyperboloidal framework
Ansorg and Panosso Macedo 2016
;
Panosso Macedo et al. 2018
;
Panosso Macedo 2020
;
Jaramillo et al. 2021
;
Panosso Macedo 2024
;
Panosso Macedo and Zenginoglu 2024
;
Zhou and Panosso Macedo 2025
;
Cai et al. 2025
;
Assaad and Panosso Macedo 2025
;
Ripley 2022
, we cast the QNMs problem into a two-dimensional eigenvalue problem for each azimuthal mode, given
m
. The hyperboloidal framework removes the necessity of imposing external boundary conditions, since the time coordinate is naturally adapted to the causal structure of the black hole and the radiation zone. When solving the QNMs problem, the QNMs boundary conditions are built into the “bulk” of the operator, and no additional boundary conditions are required. The outward orientation of light cones at the computational domain’s periphery simplifies boundary prescriptions to mandating solely a regular solution a condition effortlessly met in numerical implementations. The hyperboloidal framework has been achieved in many black holes, such as Schwarzschild black hole
Jaramillo et al. 2021
, RN black hole
Destounis et al. 2021
, quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole
Cao et al. 2024b
, Hayward black hole
Wu et al. 2025
, Boulware-Deser-Wheeler black hole
Cao et al. 2025a
and hairy black hole
Yang et al. 2025c
. Note that when the master equation has a correction term of the velocity, the corresponding hyperbolidal framework can still work
Cao et al. 2025b
. See also some comments on the hyperboloidal framework for black hole QNMs
Shen et al. 2025
.

Before getting the QNMs, one should do some parameterizations for the rotating quantum corrected black hole in order to obtain the convenience of applying hyperboloidal coordinate transformation. Similar treatments can be found in some studies
Cao et al. 2024b
;

;
Panosso Macedo 2020
;

;

\Delta
in Eqs. (
4

can be factorized into

\displaystyle\Delta(r)=\Big(1-\frac{r_{+}}{r}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{r_{-}}{r}\Big)(r^{2}+b_{0}r+c_{0})\,.

(8)

For convenience,
r_{+}
denotes the event horizon and
r_{-}
denotes the inner horizon. Comparing Eq. (

4

\{r_{+},r_{-},a\}

\displaystyle b_{0}=\frac{(r_{+}+r_{-})(r_{+}r_{-}-a^{2})}{r_{+}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{-}^{2}}\,,\quad c_{0}=\frac{r_{+}r_{-}(r_{+}r_{-}-a^{2})}{r_{+}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{-}^{2}}\,,

\displaystyle M=\frac{(r_{+}+r_{-})(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})}{2(r_{+}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{-}^{2})}\,,\quad\alpha=\frac{4r_{+}^{2}r_{-}^{2}(r_{+}r_{-}-a^{2})(r_{+}^{2}+r_{+}r_{-}+r_{-}^{2})}{(r_{+}+r_{-})^{2}(a^{2}+r_{+}^{2}+r_{-}^{2})^{2}}\,.

(9)

Define two dimensionless parameters as follows

\displaystyle\kappa:=\frac{a}{r_{+}}\,,\quad q^{2}:=\frac{r_{-}}{r_{+}}\,,

(10)

four quantities in Eqs. (
III
) can be expressed by
\{\kappa,q,r_{+}\}
, i.e.,

\displaystyle b_{0}=\frac{(1+q^{2})r_{+}(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\,,\quad c_{0}=\frac{q^{2}r_{+}^{2}(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\,,

\displaystyle M=\frac{(1+q^{2})r_{+}(1+q^{4}+\kappa^{2})}{2(1+q^{2}+q^{4})}\,,\quad\alpha=\frac{4q^{4}(1+q^{2}+q^{4})r_{+}^{2}(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{(1+q^{2})^{2}(1+q^{4}+\kappa^{2})^{2}}\,.

(11)

Therefore, the function
\Delta
becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta}{r_{+}^{2}}=\Big(1-\frac{r_{+}}{r}\Big)\Big(1-\frac{r_{-}}{r}\Big)\Big[\Big(\frac{r}{r_{+}}\Big)^{2}+\frac{(1+q^{2})(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\Big(\frac{r}{r_{+}}\Big)+\frac{q^{2}(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\Big]\,.

(12)

Now, it is ready to solve the QNMs spectra of RQCBH within the hyperboloidal framework. The complete mapping from Boyer-Lindquist to the hyperboloidal coordinates are given by

\displaystyle t=\lambda\Big[\tau-h(\sigma,\theta)\Big]-r_{\star}(r(\sigma))\,,\quad r(\sigma)=\lambda\frac{\rho(\sigma)}{\sigma}\,,\quad\varphi=\phi-k(r(\sigma))\,,

(13)

where
\lambda
is the characteristic scale,
h(\sigma,\theta)
is called the height function, and
k(r(\sigma))
is the phase function. The hyperboloidal coordinates of Kerr black hole have been work out in
Panosso Macedo 2020
. Here, we use the approach from
Panosso Macedo 2020
to solve the hyperboloidal coordinates for RQCBH, with the most important being to solve the height function. In Appendix
A
, the height function is derived. If we apply the minimal gauge (radial function fixing gauge) and choose the length scale as
\lambda=r_{+}
, and get

\displaystyle\rho(\sigma)=1\,,\quad\beta(\sigma)=1\,,\quad h(\sigma,\theta)=-\frac{2}{\sigma}+\frac{2(1+q^{2})(1+q^{4}+\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\ln\sigma\,,

(14)

where we have used Eqs. (
III
). As a result, the function
\Delta
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta(\sigma)}{r_{+}^{2}}=(1-\sigma)(1-q^{2}\sigma)\Big[\frac{1}{\sigma^{2}}+\frac{(1+q^{2})(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{(1+q^{2}+q^{4})\sigma}+\frac{q^{2}(q^{2}-\kappa^{2})}{1+q^{2}+q^{4}}\Big]\,.

(15)

Finally, the master function
\Phi(\tau,\sigma,\theta,\phi)
is written as
Panosso Macedo 2020
;
Assaad and Panosso Macedo 2025

\displaystyle\Phi(\tau,\sigma,\theta,\phi)=\Omega\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\cos^{\delta}(\theta/2)\sin^{\delta}(\theta/2)V_{m}(\tau,\sigma,\theta)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\phi}\,,

(16)

with the exponent

. The angle
\phi

\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}m\phi}
, in which
m
is the azimuthal number. With the subsitution

, one can achieve the final form. At the same time, we perform a first-order reduction in time, i.e.,

, and Eq. (
III

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\begin{bmatrix}V_{m}(\tau,\sigma,x)\\
W_{m}(\tau,\sigma,x)\end{bmatrix}=\mathrm{i}L\begin{bmatrix}V_{m}(\tau,\sigma,x)\\
W_{m}(\tau,\sigma,x)\end{bmatrix}\,,

(17)

where the operator

\displaystyle L=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
L_{1}&L_{2}\end{bmatrix}\,.

(18)

We call

\displaystyle L\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{V}_{m}(\omega,\sigma,x)\\
\mathbb{W}_{m}(\omega,\sigma,x)\end{bmatrix}=\omega\begin{bmatrix}\mathbb{V}_{m}(\omega,\sigma,x)\\
\mathbb{W}_{m}(\omega,\sigma,x)\end{bmatrix}\,,

(19)

in which boundary conditions are encoded in

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Big[1+\cos\Big(\frac{i\pi}{N_{\sigma}}\Big)\Big]\,,\quad i=0,1,\cdots,N_{\sigma}-1,N_{\sigma}\ ,

V
Conclusions and discussion

In this work, we have systematically investigated the scalar QNMs spectra of rotating quantum corrected black hole (RQCBH), obtained by applying the Newman–Janis generating method to spherically symmetric black hole in the loop quantum gravity
Lewandowski et al. 2023
. To compute the QNMs spectra with
s=0
perturbation, we employ the hyperboloidal framework combined with a two-dimensional pseudo-spectral method, which naturally incorporates boundary conditions of QNMs at the event horizon and null infinity. The results of QNMs spectra are shown in Fig.
3
. The resulting QNMs spectra are characterized by three physical parameters, namely the mass
M
, dimensionless spin
\bar{a}
, and dimensionless quantum correction parameter
\bar{\alpha}
, and it will reduce to the Kerr or quantum corrected Schwarzschild black hole in the
\bar{\alpha}\to 0
or
\bar{a}\to 0
limits.

Based on these QNMs spectra, we constructed a Bayesian inference framework using the
pyRing
package to explore the influence of the quantum parameter
\alpha
on GW ringdown analyses. First, the obtained spectra are fitted by bivariate rational functions in order to provide analytic expressions
\omega_{\ell mn}(M,\bar{a},\bar{\alpha})
for parameter estimation. By applying the RQCBH-based QNMs templates to representative events GW150914, GW190521, and GW231123, the inference results show that the inferred posteriors of the mass and spin, including
\bar{\alpha}
as a free parameter, significantly deviate from the IMR analyses based on Kerr QNMs. This arises from the strong correlation between the extra parameter
\bar{\alpha}
and the intrinsic parameter
\bar{a}
, compared to the black hole immersed in a magnetic field model, in which the coupling between
\tilde{B}
and
\tilde{a}
is relatively weak.

Several limitations of the present analysis should be noted. The finite resolution of the pseudo-spectral method and the polynomial fitting may lead to minor numerical errors, especially for higher modes or overtones, or near the boundaries of the parameter domain. Nevertheless, these limitations do not affect the overall qualitative conclusions of this work. Besides, the replacement of scalar RQCBH QNMs in the Kerr
s=-2
waveform model will introduce model mismatch (including mode itself and spin-weighted spheroidal harmonics) and systematic uncertainties on Bayesian inference. Therefore, the results cannot be interpreted as direct observational constraints on the RQCBH model. However, the established pipeline provides a referencable procedure for parameter estimation to the RQCBH model, and supplies an indicative framework for studies of beyond-GR black hole models with gravitational wave data.

Future studies should focus on extending the present framework to
s=-2
perturbation and performing hierarchical Bayesian analyses across multiple events. Our findings highlight that quantum corrections, though small at the level of the background metric, may have non-negligible effects on ringdown-based parameter inference. This opens a promising avenue for testing quantum-gravity-induced deviations using gravitational-wave spectroscopy. With the advent of next-generation detectors such as the Einstein Telescope
Maggiore et al. 2020
, Cosmic Explorer
Reitze et al. 2019
, LISA
Amaro-Seoane et al. 2017
, and Taiji
Hu and Wu 2017
, the precision of parameter measurements of black holes will increase significantly, making it possible to probe the quantum parameter, such as
\bar{\alpha}
in the RQCBH mode, through multi-mode or multi-event analyses.

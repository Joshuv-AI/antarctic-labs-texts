Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups and categorical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
Jun Jiang

Abstract
. In this paper, we construct a categorical solution ( 𝒞 , R ) ({\mathcal{C}},R) of the Yang-Baxter equation, i.e. 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}} is a small category and R : 𝒞 × 𝒞 → 𝒞 × 𝒞 R:{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}}\,\rightarrow\,{\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}} is an invertible functor satisfying ( R × Id 𝒞 ) ​ ( Id 𝒞 × R ) ​ ( R × Id 𝒞 ) = ( Id 𝒞 × R ) ​ ( R × Id 𝒞 ) ​ ( Id 𝒞 × R ) , (R\times{\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}})({\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}})=({\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\times R)(R\times{\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}})({\rm{Id}}_{\mathcal{C}}\times R), where 𝒞 × 𝒞 {\mathcal{C}}\times{\mathcal{C}} is the product category. First, the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups is defined and its various properties are established. Then, we use Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups to construct categorical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. We also study the Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras which are infinitesimals of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups, they can give connections on manifolds. Finally, we study the integration of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras and the differentials of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Yang-Baxter equation

The Yang-Baxter equation was firstly introduced in theoretical physics by Yang
[
39
]
in 1967. Shortly afterwards, this equation appeared in statistical mechanics in the work of Baxter
[
8
]
. The Yang-Baxter equation has led to many interesting applications in mathematics, such as quantum groups, tensor categories, knot theory and integrable systems. Recall that a solution of the Yang-Baxter equation on a vector space
V
is an invertible linear map
R:V\otimes V\,\rightarrow\,V\otimes V
such that

(R\otimes{\rm{Id}}_{V})({\rm{Id}}_{V}\otimes R)(R\otimes{\rm{Id}}_{V})=({\rm{Id}}_{V}\otimes R)(R\otimes{\rm{Id}}_{V})({\rm{Id}}_{V}\otimes R).

The problem of constructing and classifying its solutions is difficult, so Drinfel’d raised the question of finding set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
14
]
. A set-theoretical solution of the Yang-Baxter equation is a pair
(X,r)
, where X is a set and
r:X\times X\,\rightarrow\,X\times X
is a bijective map satisfying

(r\times{\rm{Id}}_{X})({\rm{Id}}_{X}\times r)(r\times{\rm{Id}}_{X})=({\rm{Id}}_{X}\times r)(r\times{\rm{Id}}_{X})({\rm{Id}}_{X}\times r).

There are many results on set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation by Etingof, Schedler and Soloviev
[
15
]
, Lu, Yan and Zhu
[
28
]
, Gateva-Ivanova and Majid
[
19
]
, Gateva-Ivanova and Cameron
[
18
]
, Gateva-Ivanova
[
17
]
and others. In
[
36
]
, Rump introduced the notion of braces, a generalization of Jacobson radical rings as a tool to construct non-degenerate involutive set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation. Guarnieri and Vendramin generalized braces to skew braces and used them to construct non-degenerate non-involutive set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
20
]
. Recently, Bardakov and Gubarev studied the connection between skew braces and Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups, they obtained non-degenerate set-theoretic solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
5
]
.

1.2.
Crossed modules of Lie groups and crossed modules of Lie algebras

The notion of crossed modules of groups was introduced by Whitehead in order to characterize a second relative homotopy group
[
41
,
42
]
. Crossed modules of groups and cohomologies of groups are closely related, they can be classified by the third cohomology group of groups. When people replace groups and maps by Lie groups and smooth maps in the definition of crossed modules of groups, they obtain crossed modules of Lie groups. The infinitesimal of crossed modules of Lie groups are crossed modules of Lie algebras, which can be classified by the third cohomology group of Lie algebras. Furthermore, every crossed module of Lie algebras can be integrated into a crossed module of Lie groups. For further details on crossed modules of algebraic structures, see
[
40
]
. When studying the categorification, Baez and his collaborators finded that a strict Lie
2
-group(resp. strict Lie
2
-algebra) is ono-to-one correspondence to a crossed module of Lie groups (resp. crossed module of Lie algebras)
[
1
,
2
]
, this means that there are small categories associated with crossed modules of Lie groups. These small categories play an important role in this paper. Recently, crossed module of Lie groups have become an object of intense study in the higher gauge theory,
2
-BF theory
[
3
,
16
,
29
]
.

1.3.
Rota-Baxter operators

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on associative algebras was introduced by G. Baxter in his study of fluctuation theory in probability
[
7
]
. It was further developed by Rota and Cartier from the perspective of combinatorics
[
34
,
35
,
12
]
. Recently it has found many applications, including Connes-Kreimer’s algebraic approach to renormalization of quantum field theory
[
13
]
. In the Lie algebra context, the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras was introduced in
[
26
,
4
]
.
Let
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
be a Lie algebra and
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
be a fixed element. A linear map
B:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is called a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
\lambda
if

[B(x),B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=B([B(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+\lambda[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g}.

Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras are closely related to various classical Yang-Baxter equations. More precisely, if
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
has a non-degenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form, a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
0
on
\mathfrak{g}
is naturally the operator form of a skew-symmetric solution of the classical Yang-Baxter equation
[
37
]
. Rota-Baxter operators of weight
1
are in one-to-one
correspondence with solutions of the modified Yang-Baxter equation, and have close relation with factorizable Lie bialgebras
[
27
]
. In addition, a Lie group with zero Schouten curvature gives rise a Rota-Baxter operator of weight
1
on a Lie algebra
[
25
]
.

The notion of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups was introduced in
[
21
]
, then this concept was generalized to relative Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups in
[
22
]
.
Let
(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group. A smooth map
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is called Rota-Baxter operator on
G
if

\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)=\mathcal{B}(a\cdot\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot b\cdot\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Similarly to Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras give solutions of the classical Yang-Baxter equation, Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups can also be used to construct set-theoretical solutions of the Yang-Baxter equation
[
5
]
. Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups also give rise to factorizations of the Lie groups, skew braces, and can be applied to study integrable systems
[
21
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
30
,
37
]
.

According to Lie’s third theorem, there is a one-to-one correspondence between finite dimensional Lie algebras and connected and simply connected Lie groups. However, this theorem does not always hold for Rota-Baxter operators. In deed, it was shown in
[
21
,
22
]
that by differentiating Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups, people can obtain Rota-Baxter operators on the corresponding Lie algebras. The converse direction, Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras can be integrated to local Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups, global integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras have obstructions
[
22
,
23
]
.

2.
Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups

In this section, first we recall Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups and crossed modules of Lie groups. Then the notion of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups is defined and its various properties are established.

Definition 2.1
.

(
[
21
]
)
Let
(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group. A smooth map
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is called Rota-Baxter operator on
G
if

\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot\mathcal{B}(b)=\mathcal{B}(a\cdot\mathcal{B}(a)\cdot b\cdot\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}),\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Example 2.2
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group. Then the inverse map
\mathcal{B}=(\cdot)^{-1}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on the Lie group
(G,\cdot)
.

Definition 2.3
.

(
[
21
]
)
Let
\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
be Rota-Baxter operators on Lie groups
G,H
respectively. A smooth map
\Psi:H\,\rightarrow\,G
is called Rota-Baxter operator homomorphism from
\mathcal{B}^{\prime}
to
\mathcal{B}
if
\Psi
is a Lie group homomorphism and

\Psi\circ\mathcal{B}^{\prime}=\mathcal{B}\circ\Psi.

Definition 2.4
.

(
[
41
,
42
]
)
A crossed module of Lie groups is a quadruple
(H,G,t,\Phi)
, where
(H,\cdot_{H})
and
(G,\cdot_{G})
are Lie groups,
t:H\,\rightarrow\,G
is a Lie group homomorphism and
\Phi:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H)
is an action of
(G,\cdot_{G})
on
(H,\cdot_{H})
, such that

(1)

\displaystyle\Phi(t(p))q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\cdot_{H}q\cdot_{H}p^{-1},

(2)

\displaystyle t(\Phi(a)p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{G}t(p)\cdot_{G}a^{-1},\quad\forall p,q\in H,a\in G.

In
[
31
]
, Katherine Norrie studied the actions and semi-direct products of crossed modules of Lie groups, then he obtained the following proposition.

Proposition 2.5
.

(
[
31
,
32
]
)
Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups. Denote by
(G\ltimes G,\cdot_{G\ltimes})
and
(H\ltimes H,\cdot_{H\ltimes})
the semi-direct product Lie groups, where
G\ltimes G=G\times G,H\ltimes H=H\times H
and

(3)

\displaystyle(a,b)\cdot_{G\ltimes}(c,d)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(a\cdot_{G}c,b\cdot_{G}a\cdot_{G}d\cdot_{G}a^{-1}),\quad a,b,c,d\in G,

(4)

\displaystyle(p,q)\cdot_{H\ltimes}(r,s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(p\cdot_{H}r,q\cdot_{H}p\cdot_{H}s\cdot_{H}p^{-1}),\quad p,q,r,s\in H.

Then
(H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G,(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
is a crossed module of Lie groups, where

(t,t):H\ltimes H\,\rightarrow\,G\ltimes G,\quad(t,t)(p,q)=(t(p),t(q)),

and
\tilde{\Phi}:G\ltimes G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H\ltimes H)
is defined by

(5)

\tilde{\Phi}\Big((a,b)\Big)(p,q)=\Big(\Phi(a)p,\Phi(b\cdot_{G}a)(q\cdot_{H}p)\cdot_{H}\Phi(a)p^{-1}\Big),\quad\forall a,b\in G,p,q\in H.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups. A Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
is a pair
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
, where smooth maps
\mathcal{B}_{1}:H\,\rightarrow\,H
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
satisfy

(i)

\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are Rota-Baxter operators on
H
and
G
respectively,

(ii)

the smooth map
t
is a homomorphism from
\mathcal{B}_{1}
to
\mathcal{B}_{0}
, i.e.
t\circ\mathcal{B}_{1}=\mathcal{B}_{0}\circ t
,

(iii)

\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)=\mathcal{B}_{1}\Big(\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big),\quad\forall a\in G,p\in H.

Example 2.7
.

Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups. Then
\Big((\cdot)_{H}^{-1},(\cdot)_{G}^{-1}\Big)
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
, where
(\cdot)_{H}^{-1}
and
(\cdot)_{G}^{-1}
are inverse maps of
H
and
G
respectively.

Example 2.8
.

Let
H
be a Lie group and
\mathcal{B}
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
H
. Then
(H,\{e_{H}\},t,\Phi)
is a crossed module of Lie groups, where

\Phi(e_{H})h=h,~~~\forall h\in H,\quad t(H)=e_{H}.

Moreover,
(\mathcal{B},{\rm{Id}})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,\{e_{H}\},t,\Phi)
.

Example 2.9
.

Let
G
be a Lie group. Define
t:G\,\rightarrow\,G
and
\Phi:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(G)
by

t(a)=a,\quad\Phi(a)b=aba^{-1},\quad\forall a,b\in G.

Then
(G,G,t,\Phi)
is a crossed module of Lie groups. If
\mathcal{B}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
G
, then
(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{B})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(G,G,t,\Phi)
.

Let
G
be a Lie group and
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
. Define
t:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,G
and
\mathrm{Ad}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{GL}(\mathfrak{g})
by

t(x)=e,~~\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathrm{Ad}(g)x=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}g\exp(tx)g^{-1},~~\forall g\in G.

Then
(\mathfrak{g},G,t,\mathrm{Ad})
is a crossed module of Lie groups. If
\mathcal{B}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
G
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a)\mathcal{B}(b)\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}(a)\mathcal{B}(b)\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}a^{-1}\mathcal{B}(a))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}\Big(a\mathcal{B}(a)b\mathcal{B}(b)\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}a^{-1}\mathcal{B}(a)\mathcal{B}(b)^{-1}\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}\Big).

Then for any
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, it follows

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}\mathcal{B}_{*e}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\mathcal{B}(a)\mathcal{B}(\exp(tx)))\mathcal{B}(a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{*e}\Big(\mathrm{Ad}_{a\mathcal{B}(a)}(x+\mathcal{B}_{*e}(x))-\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathcal{B}(a)}(\mathcal{B}_{*e}(x))\Big).

Thus we have the following example.

Example 2.10
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
G
, then
((\mathcal{B})_{*e},\mathcal{B})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g},G,t,\mathrm{Ad})
.

In the following, we will character Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups as crossed module of Lie groups.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups and
\mathcal{B}_{1}:H\,\rightarrow\,H,\mathcal{B}_{0}:G\,\rightarrow\,G
be smooth maps. Then the pair
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
if and only if
(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}),\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0}),(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
is a crossed module of Lie groups, where

\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})=\{(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)|\forall p\in H\}\subset H\ltimes H,\quad\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})=\{(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a),a)|\forall a\in G\}\subset G\ltimes G.

(t,t):\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0}),\quad(t,t)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)=(t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)),t(p))

and
\tilde{\Phi}:\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}))
defined by

\tilde{\Phi}\Big((\mathcal{B}_{0}(a),a)\Big)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)=\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big)

Proof.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on crossed module of Lie groups, it follows from
[
22
, Proposition 3.4]
that
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
are Lie subgroups of
(H\ltimes H,\cdot_{H\ltimes})
and
(G\ltimes G,\cdot_{G\ltimes})
.

Since
(t,t)
is a Lie group homomorphism from
(H\ltimes H,\cdot_{H\ltimes})
to
(G\ltimes G,\cdot_{G\ltimes})
, we have

(t,t)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)=(t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)),t(p))=(\mathcal{B}_{0}(t(p)),t(p))\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0}),\quad\forall p\in H,

which implies that
(t,t)
is a Lie group homomorphism from
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
to
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
.

Moreover, since
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
, it follows that

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}\Big((\mathcal{B}_{0}(a),a)\Big)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}_{1}\Big(\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big),

\displaystyle\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big)\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}),

thus
\tilde{\Phi}
is an action of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
on
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
by Proposition
2.5
.

As
(H,G,t,\Phi)
is a crossed module of Lie groups, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}\Big((t,t)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)\Big)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(q),q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}\Big(t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)),t(p)\Big)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(q),q)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi(t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\mathcal{B}_{1}(q),\Phi(t(p)\cdot_{H}t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)))(q\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{1}(q))\cdot_{H}\Phi(t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)))\mathcal{B}_{1}(q)^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(q)\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1},

3.

be Rota-Baxters on Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
respectively. A homomorphism from
B^{\prime}
to
B
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\psi:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
such that
\psi\circ B^{\prime}=B\circ\psi
.

Definition 4.5
.

A crossed module of Lie algebras is a quadruple
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, where
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
and
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
are Lie algebras,
\mathrm{d}t:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie algebra homomorphism and
\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, such that

\mathrm{d}t(\phi(x)u)=[x,\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\phi(\mathrm{d}t(u))v=[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras. We consider semi-direct product Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}})
, where

\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{g},\quad\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}=\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{h},

and

(24)

\displaystyle[(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})]_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\quad\forall x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},

(25)

\displaystyle~[(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}},[u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}.

Define a linear map
\tilde{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
by

(26)

\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u,v)=(\phi(x)u,\phi(y)u+\phi(y)v+\phi(x)v),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Then we have the following conclusion.

Proposition 4.6
.

With the above notations,
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is a crossed module of Lie algebras.

Proof.

We only proof that
\tilde{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism and leave other to interested readers. By (
26
), for any
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(x,y)[(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(x,y)([u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}},[u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(x)[u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}},\phi(y)[u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(y)[u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(y)[v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(y)[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+\phi(x)[u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(x)[v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(x)[v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big([\phi(x)u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}},[\phi(y)u_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(y)u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(y)v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(y)v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[\phi(x)u_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(x)v_{1},u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(x)v_{1},v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big([u_{1},\phi(x)u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}},[u_{1},\phi(y)u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u_{1},\phi(y)v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},\phi(y)u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},\phi(y)v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[u_{1},\phi(x)v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},\phi(x)u_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[v_{1},\phi(x)v_{2}]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(\phi(x)u_{1},\phi(y)u_{1}+\phi(y)v_{1}+\phi(x)v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[(u_{1},u_{2}),(\phi(x)u_{2},\phi(y)u_{2}+\phi(y)v_{2}+\phi(x)v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u_{1},v_{1}),(u_{2},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}+[(u_{1},v_{1}),\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u_{2},v_{2})]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}.

Moreover, by (
24
), for any
x_{1},x_{2},y_{1},y_{2}\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}([(x_{1},y_{1}),(x_{2},y_{2})]_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}})(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}},[x_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[y_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})u,\phi([x_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})v+\phi([x_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})v+\phi([y_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})v+\phi([y_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})v

\displaystyle+\phi([x_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})u+\phi([y_{1},x_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})u+\phi([y_{1},y_{2}]_{\mathfrak{g}})u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})u,\phi(x_{1})\phi(x_{2})v+\phi(x_{1})\phi(y_{2})u+\phi(x_{1})\phi(y_{2})v

\displaystyle+\phi(y_{1})\phi(x_{2})u+\phi(y_{1})\phi(x_{2})v+\phi(y_{1})\phi(y_{2})u+\phi(y_{1})\phi(y_{2})v\Big)

\displaystyle-\Big(\phi(x_{2})\phi(x_{1})u,\phi(x_{2})\phi(x_{1})v+\phi(y_{2})\phi(x_{1})u+\phi(y_{2})\phi(x_{1})v

\displaystyle+\phi(x_{2})\phi(y_{1})u+\phi(x_{2})\phi(y_{1})v+\phi(y_{2})\phi(y_{1})u+\phi(y_{2})\phi(y_{1})v\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(x_{1},y_{1})\tilde{\phi}(x_{2},y_{2})(u,v)-\tilde{\phi}(x_{2},y_{2})\tilde{\phi}(x_{1},y_{1})(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\phi}(x_{1},y_{1}),\tilde{\phi}(x_{2},y_{2})](u,v).

Thus
\tilde{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

∎

Definition 4.7
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras. A Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
is a pair
(B_{1},B_{0})
, where linear maps
B_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
and
B_{0}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
satisfy

(i)

B_{1}
and
B_{0}
are Rota-Baxter operators on
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively,

(ii)

the linear map
\mathrm{d}t
is a homomorphism from
B_{1}
to
B_{0}
, i.e.
\mathrm{d}t\circ B_{1}=B_{0}\circ\mathrm{d}t
,

(iii)

\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u)=B_{1}\Big(\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u\Big),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Example 4.8
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras. Then
(-{\rm{Id}},-{\rm{Id}})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
.

Example 4.9
.

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra and
B
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
\mathfrak{g}
. Then
(B,B)
is a Rota-Baxter operator on the crossed modules of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{ad})
, where
\mathrm{d}t(x)=x
.

In the following, we will character Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras as crossed module of Lie algebras.

Theorem 4.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras and
B_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h},B_{0}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
be linear maps. Then the pair
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxtr operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
if and only if
(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}),(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is a crossed module of Lie algebras, where

\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})=\{(B_{1}(u),u)|\forall u\in\mathfrak{h}\}\subset\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\quad\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})=\{(B_{0}(x),x)|\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}\}\subset\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}.

(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t):\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}),\quad(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(u),u)=(\mathrm{d}t(\mathcal{B}_{1}(u)),\mathrm{d}t(u)),

and
\tilde{\phi}:\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}))
defined by

\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u,v)=(\phi(x)u,\phi(y)u+\phi(y)v+\phi(x)v),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

On the one hand, suppose that
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, by
[
22
, Proposition 2.5]
, it follows that
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
are Lie subalgebras of
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}})
and
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}})
respectively.

By Proposition
4.6
,
(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}})
to
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}})
, we obtain

\forall u\in\mathfrak{h},\quad(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)(B_{1}(u),u)=(\mathrm{d}_{t}(B_{1}(u)),\mathrm{d}t(u))=(B_{0}(\mathrm{d}t(u)),\mathrm{d}t(u))\in\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}),

which implies that
(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})
to
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
.

Since
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(B_{0}(x),x)(B_{1}(u),u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u),\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(B_{1}(\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u),\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u\Big)\in\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),

thus
\tilde{\phi}
is an action of
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
on
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})
by Proposition
4.6
.

As
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
is a crossed module of Lie algebras, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}\Big((\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)(B_{1}(u),u)\Big)(B_{1}(v),v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi\Big(\mathrm{d}t(B_{1}(u))\Big)B_{1}(v),\phi(\mathrm{d}t(u))B_{1}(v)+\phi(\mathrm{d}t(u))v+\phi\Big(\mathrm{d}t(B_{1}(u))\Big)v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([B_{1}(u),B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}},[u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[B_{1}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(B_{1}(u),u),(B_{1}(v),v)]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}},

and

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)\Big(\tilde{\phi}(B_{0}(x),x)(B_{1}(u),u)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)\Big(\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u),\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([B_{0}(x),B_{0}(\mathrm{d}t(u))]_{\mathfrak{g}},[B_{0}(x),\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mathrm{d}t(B_{1}(u))]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(B_{0}(x),x),(B_{0}(\mathrm{d}t(u)),\mathrm{d}t(u))]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(B_{0}(x),x),(\mathrm{d}t(B_{1}(u)),\mathrm{d}t(u))]_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}.

Thus
(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}),(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is a crossed module of Lie algebras.

On the other hand, suppose that
(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}),(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is a crossed module of Lie algebras, by
[
22
, Proposition 2.5]
, we obtain that
B_{1}:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h},B_{0}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
are Rota-Baxter operators.

Moreover, by the fact that
(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t):\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, it follows
\mathrm{d}t\circ B_{1}=B_{0}\circ\mathrm{d}t
.

Since
\tilde{\phi}(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}))\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})
, then

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(B_{0}(x),x)(B_{1}(u),u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u),\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u\Big)\in\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}).

which implies
\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u)=B_{1}\Big(\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u\Big)
. Thus
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
.
∎

Parallel to Proposition
2.12
, Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras descend crossed modules of Lie algebras.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
(B_{1},B_{0})
be a Rota-Baxter operator on a crossed module of Lie algebras
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
. Then
\Big((\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}}),(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}}),\mathrm{d}t,\hat{\phi}\Big)
is a crossed module of Lie algebras, where

\displaystyle~~[u,v]_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B_{1}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle~~[x,y]_{B_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B_{0}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle~~\hat{\phi}(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~~u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

By
[
22
, Corollary 2.5]
,
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}})
are Lie algebras. By the fact that
\mathrm{d}t\circ B_{1}=B_{0}\circ\mathrm{d}t
and
\mathrm{d}t:(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism, it follows that
\mathrm{d}t:(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}})\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

For
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}
, since
\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u)=B_{1}\Big(\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u\Big)
, then

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}([x,y]_{B_{0}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi([B_{0}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})B_{1}(u)+\phi([B_{0}(x),B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})u

\displaystyle+\phi([B_{0}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)\hat{\phi}(y)u-\hat{\phi}(y)\hat{\phi}(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{\phi}(x),\hat{\phi}(y)]u,

which implies that
\hat{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Since
\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u)=B_{1}\Big(\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u\Big)
, we obtain

\displaystyle[\hat{\phi}(x)u,v]_{B_{1}}+[u,\hat{\phi}(x)v]_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B_{1}(\hat{\phi}(x)u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\hat{\phi}(x)u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[B_{1}(u),\hat{\phi}(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,B_{1}(\hat{\phi}(x)v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\hat{\phi}(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,\hat{\phi}(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[B_{1}(u),\phi(x)B_{1}(v)+\phi(B_{0}(x))v+\phi(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+[u,\phi(x)B_{1}(v)+\phi(B_{0}(x))v+\phi(x)v]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)[B(u),B(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\phi(B_{0}(x))[u,v]_{B_{1}}+\phi(x)[u,v]_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{\phi}(x)([u,v]_{B_{1}}),

thus
\hat{\phi}
is a Lie algebra action of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}})
on
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}})
.

Moreover, since
\mathrm{d}t\circ B_{1}=B_{0}\circ\mathrm{d}t
, it follows that

\hat{\phi}(\mathrm{d}t(u))v=\phi(\mathrm{d}t(u))B_{1}(v)+\phi(B_{0}(\mathrm{d}t(u)))v+\phi(\mathrm{d}t(u))v=[u,v]_{B_{1}},

and

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}t(\hat{\phi}(x)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}t(\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[x,B_{0}(\mathrm{d}t(u))]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[B_{0}(x),\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{\mathfrak{g}}=[x,\mathrm{d}t(u)]_{B_{0}}.

Therefore,
\Big((\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}}),(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}}),\mathrm{d}t,\hat{\phi}\Big)
is a crossed module of Lie algebras.
∎

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras and
(B_{1},B_{0})
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
. Then
B=(B_{0},B_{1})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on the semi-direct product Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
, where

B(x,u)=(B_{0}(x),B_{1}(u)),\quad[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\phi}=([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi(x)v-\phi(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proof.

By the fact that
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, we have

\displaystyle[B(x,u),B(y,v)]_{\phi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([B_{0}(x),B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}},\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(v)-\phi(B_{0}(y))B_{1}(u)+[B_{1}(u),B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(B_{0}([B_{0}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),B_{1}(\phi(B_{0}(x))v-\phi(y)B_{1}(u)+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}))

\displaystyle+(B_{0}([x,B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}),B_{1}(\phi(x)B_{1}(v)-\phi(B_{0}(y))u+[u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}))

\displaystyle+(B_{0}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),B_{1}(\phi(x)v-\phi(y)u+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B\Big([B(x,u),(y,v)]_{\phi}+[(x,u),B(y,v)]_{\phi}+[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\phi}\Big),

which implies that
B=(B_{0},B_{1})
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
.
∎

From Proposition
4.11
, we obtain that
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}}),(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}})
are Lie algebras and
\hat{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. Denote by
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\hat{\phi}})
the semi-direct product Lie algebra of
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}})
and
(\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}})
, where

~[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\hat{\phi}}=([x,y]_{B_{0}},\hat{\phi}(x)v-\hat{\phi}(y)u+[u,v]_{B_{1}}).

Moreover, due to
(B_{0},B_{1})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\phi})
, it follows that
(\mathfrak{g}\oplus\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{(B_{0},B_{1})})
is a Lie algebra by Corollary 2.5 in
[
22
]
, where

\displaystyle~[(x,u),(y,v)]_{(B_{0},B_{1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B(x,u),(y,v)]_{\ltimes_{\phi}}+[(x,u),B(y,v)]_{\ltimes_{\phi}}+[(x,u),(y,v)]_{\ltimes_{\phi}},

\displaystyle[(x,u),(y,v)]_{(B_{0},B_{1})}

5.
Lie theory for Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie groups and Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras

In this section, we study the differential of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed of Lie groups and the integration of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras.

It is well known that there is one-to-one correspondence between finite dimensional Lie algebras and Lie groups. For crossed modules of Lie groups and crossed modules of Lie algebras, there is the same theorem as following.

Theorem 5.1
.

(
[
40
]
)
Let
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
be finite dimensional Lie algebras and integrate to connected, simply-connected Lie groups
G,H
. There is one-to-one correspondence between crossed modules of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
and crossed modules of
G
and
H
.

Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups. By Proposition
2.5
, we obtain that
(H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G,(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
is a crossed of Lie groups, where
H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G
are semi-direct product Lie groups, and

\tilde{\Phi}\Big((a,b)\Big)(p,q)=\Big(\Phi(a)p,\Phi(b\cdot_{G}a)(q\cdot_{H}p)\cdot_{H}\Phi(a)p^{-1}\Big),\quad\forall a,b\in G,p,q\in H.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras of
(H,G,t,\Phi)
. By Proposition
4.6
,
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is a crossed module of Lie algebras, where
\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}
are semi-direct product Lie algebras and

\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u,v)=(\phi(x)u,\phi(y)u+\phi(y)v+\phi(x)v),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},~~u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 5.2
.

With the above notations,
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is the crossed module of Lie algebras of
(H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G,(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
.

Proof.

Since
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}
are Lie algebras of
G
and
H
, it follows that
\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}
are Lie algebras of
G\ltimes G
and
H\ltimes H
. Moreover, for the semi-direct Lie group
H\ltimes H
, the exponential map
\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}:\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,H\ltimes H
is

\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(u,v)=\Big(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(u),P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(u,v))\Big),

where
P_{2}:H\ltimes H\,\rightarrow\,H
is defined by
P_{2}(p,q)=q
. By the fact that
t_{*}=\mathrm{d}t
, we have

(t,t)_{*}(u,v)=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\Big(t(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(su)),t(P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(su,sv)))\Big)=(\mathrm{d}t(u),\mathrm{d}t(v)),

thus
(t,t)_{*}=(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t)
.

Since
\tilde{\Phi}:G\ltimes G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H\ltimes H)
is a Lie group homomorphism, then we obtain a Lie group homomorphism
\hat{\Phi}:G\ltimes G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
, given by

\hat{\Phi}((a,b))(u,v)=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\tilde{\Phi}((a,b))\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(su,sv),\quad\forall a,b\in G,u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Taking the differential, there is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\tilde{\Phi}_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
, given by

\tilde{\Phi}_{*}((x,y))(u,v)=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\hat{\Phi}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}(sx,sy))(u,v),\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}.

Similarly, define
\bar{\Phi}:G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h})
and
\Phi_{*}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
by

\bar{\Phi}(a)u=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\Phi(a)\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(su),\quad\Phi_{*}(x)u=\frac{d}{ds}|_{s=0}\bar{\Phi}(\exp(sx))u.

For
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u,v\in\mathfrak{h}
, by (
5
) and
\Phi_{*}=\phi
, it follows

\displaystyle\tilde{\Phi}_{*}(x,y)(u,v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\tilde{\Phi}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x,s_{1}y))\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(s_{2}u,s_{2}v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\tilde{\Phi}\Big((\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x),P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x,s_{1}y)))\Big)\Big(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(s_{2}u),P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(s_{2}u,s_{2}v))\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\Big(\Phi(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x))\exp(s_{2}u),\Phi\Big(P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x,s_{1}y))\cdot_{G}\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x)\Big)

\displaystyle\Big(P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(s_{2}u,s_{2}v))\cdot_{H}\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(s_{2}u)\Big)\cdot_{H}\Phi(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x))\exp_{\mathfrak{h}}(-s_{2}u)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}|_{s_{1}=0}\Big(\bar{\Phi}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x))u,\bar{\Phi}\Big(P_{2}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x,s_{1}y))\cdot_{G}\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x)\Big)(v+u)-\bar{\Phi}(\exp_{\mathfrak{g}}(s_{1}x))u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\Phi_{*}(x)u,\Phi_{*}(x)v+\Phi_{*}(y)v+\Phi_{*}(y)u\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\phi(x)u,\phi(x)v+\phi(y)v+\phi(y)u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}(x,y)(u,v).

Therefore, we have that
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
is the crossed module of Lie algebras of
(H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G,(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
.
∎

, global integrability of Rota-Baxter operators on Lie algebras have obstructions

Theorem 5.3
.

(Differentiation)
Let
(H,G,t,\Phi)
be a crossed module of Lie groups. Denote by
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
the crossed module of Lie algebras of
(H,G,t,\Phi)

, then
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, where
B_{1}=(\mathcal{B}_{1})_{*e_{H}}
and
B_{0}=(\mathcal{B}_{0})_{*e_{G}}
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are Rota-Baxter operators on
H
and
G

[

\mathrm{d}t\circ B_{1}=B_{0}\circ\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\phi(B_{0}(x))B_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)))\mathcal{B}_{1}(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\mathcal{B}_{1}\Big(\Phi(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)))(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u)\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u)))

\displaystyle\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)))\mathcal{B}_{1}(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u))^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle B_{1}\Big(\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\Phi(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)))(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u)\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u)))

\displaystyle+\frac{d}{ds_{1}}\frac{d}{ds_{2}}|_{s_{1}=s_{2}=0}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(\exp_{G}(s_{1}x)))\mathcal{B}_{1}(\exp_{H}(s_{2}u))^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle B_{1}(\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(x)u+\phi(B_{0}(x))u).

Thus
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
.
∎

Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
. By Proposition
2.12
, we obtain another crossed module of Lie groups
\Big((H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}),(G,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}),t,\hat{\Phi}\Big)

\displaystyle~p\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}q

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)\cdot_{H}q\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1},\quad\forall p,q\in H,

\displaystyle~a\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}b

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a)\cdot_{G}b\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a)^{-1},\quad\forall a,b\in G,

\displaystyle~\hat{\Phi}(a)p

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1},\quad\forall a\in G,p\in H.

Denote by
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
the crossed module of Lie algebras of
(H,G,t,\Phi)
. Let
(\mathcal{B}_{1})_{*e_{H}}=B_{1}
and
(\mathcal{B}_{0})_{*e_{G}}=B_{0}
. Then by Theorem
5.3
and Proposition
4.11

\Big((\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}}),(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}}),\mathrm{d}t,\hat{\phi}\Big)

\displaystyle~~[u,v]_{B_{1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B_{1}(u),v]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,B_{1}(v)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[u,v]_{\mathfrak{h}},\quad\forall u,v\in\mathfrak{h},

\displaystyle~~[x,y]_{B_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[B_{0}(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,B_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\forall x,y\in\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle~~\hat{\phi}(x)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi(x)B_{1}(u)+\phi(B_{0}(x))u+\phi(x)u,\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},~~u\in\mathfrak{h}.

Proposition 5.4
.

With the above notations,
\Big((\mathfrak{h},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{1}}),(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{B_{0}}),\mathrm{d}t,\hat{\phi}\Big)
is the crossed module of Lie algebras of
\Big((H,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{1}}),(G,\cdot_{\mathcal{B}_{0}}),t,\hat{\Phi}\Big)

Proof.

By

Finally, we study the integration of Rota-Baxter operators on crossed modules of Lie algebras.

Theorem 5.5
.

(Integration)
Let
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
be a crossed module of Lie algebras. Denote by
(H,G,t,\Phi)
the crossed module of Lie groups corresponding with
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, where

and

are connected, simply connected Lie groups. If
(B_{1},B_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
, and
\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0}
are Rota-Baxter operators on

such that
(\mathcal{B}_{1})_{*e_{H}}=B_{1},(\mathcal{B}_{0})_{*e_{G}}=B_{0}
, then
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
.

Proof.

Since
(\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{d}t,\phi)
is a crossed module of Lie algebras and corresponding crossed module of Lie groups is
(H,G,t,\Phi)
, by Proposition
4.6
, Proposition
2.5
and Proposition
5.2
, we have that
(H\ltimes H,G\ltimes G,(t,t),\tilde{\Phi})
is a crossed module of Lie groups and its corresponding crossed module of Lie algebras is
(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g},(\mathrm{d}t,\mathrm{d}t),\tilde{\phi})
. Since
(\mathcal{B}_{1})_{*e_{H}}=B_{1},(\mathcal{B}_{0})_{*e_{G}}=B_{0}
, by
[

, Proposition 4.5]
, we have that
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})
and
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
are Lie algebras of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
and
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
.

Moreover, the map
\tilde{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}\ltimes\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism and satisfies

\tilde{\phi}(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}))\subseteq\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}),

where the set
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})=\{\delta\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})|\delta(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}))\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})\}
.
Suppose that a map
\bar{\phi}:G\ltimes G\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})
is an integration of
\tilde{\phi}
, we have

(28)

\bar{\phi}(\exp_{G\ltimes G}(x,y))=e^{\tilde{\phi}(x,y)},\quad\forall(x,y)\in\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0}).

Since
\mathrm{Gr}(B_{0})
is a Lie algebra of
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
and
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})
is connected, by (

\bar{\phi}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0}))\subseteq\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}),

where the set
\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})=\{\Delta\in\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})|\Delta(\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}))\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1})\}
.

Assume that
\iota:\mathrm{Aut}(\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Aut}(H\ltimes H)
is a Lie group isomorphism, then
\tilde{\Phi}=\iota\circ\bar{\phi}
. For

, since
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})
is connected and

\iota(f)(\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(u,v))=\exp_{\mathfrak{h}\ltimes\mathfrak{h}}(f(u,v))\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}),\quad\forall(u,v)\in\mathrm{Gr}(B_{1}),

we have
\iota(f)\in\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})}(H\ltimes H)
, where

\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})}(H\ltimes H)=\{\chi\in\mathrm{Aut}(H\ltimes H)|\chi(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}))\subseteq\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})\}.

Then it follows that
\tilde{\Phi}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0}))=\iota(\hat{\phi}(\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{0})))\subset\mathrm{Aut}_{\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1})}(H\ltimes H).
That is, for any
a\in G,p\in H
,

\tilde{\Phi}(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a),a)(\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),p)=\Big(\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p),\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big)\in\mathrm{Gr}(\mathcal{B}_{1}).

Thus we have

\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)=\mathcal{B}_{1}\Big(\Phi(a\cdot_{G}\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))(p\cdot_{H}\mathcal{B}_{1}(p))\cdot_{H}\Phi(\mathcal{B}_{0}(a))\mathcal{B}_{1}(p)^{-1}\Big),\quad a\in G,p\in H,

which implies that
(\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{0})
is a Rota-Baxter operator on
(H,G,t,\Phi)
.
∎

on

B_{1}
and
B_{0}
on
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}

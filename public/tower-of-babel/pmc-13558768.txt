Alveolar epithelial depletion of βPAK-interacting exchange factor drives spontaneous pulmonary fibrosis by impairing stemness
Authors: Han-Byeol Kim, Woogil Song, Sun-Ok Kim, Jin-Hee Park, Junyeol Han, Ok-Jun Lee, Nak-Kyun Soung, Bumhee Yang, Jinwook Choi, Martin Alexander Schwartz, Jeong Seok Lee, Eun-Young Shin, Eung-Gook Kim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aberrant repair/regeneration of alveolar epithelial stem cells is a key driver of pulmonary fibrosis (PF). While βPAK-interacting exchange factor (βPIX) is connected to stem cell signaling, its role in pulmonary fibrosis (PF) remains unknown. Here, we generated conditional βPIX knockout mice to investigate its contribution to PF pathogenesis. Selectively depleting βPIX in alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells led to progressive PF in mice, recapitulating the key features observed in human idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF), where AT2 cells exhibit significantly reduced βPIX levels. Single-nucleus RNA sequencing of the lungs of βPIX-deficient mice revealed the emergence of progenitor cells in a transitional stem cell state with senescent attributes, along with active, collagen-producing myofibroblasts. Mechanistically, the loss of βPIX impaired AT2 cell stemness by downregulating Wnt/β-catenin signaling. AT2 cells from IPF patients also presented reduced levels of β-catenin and Axin2, confirming the downregulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Treatment with a glycogen synthase kinase-3β inhibitor reactivated Wnt/β-catenin signaling and attenuated fibrosis in βPIX-deficient mice. Our results establish a direct causal link between βPIX deficiency and PF, highlighting βPIX and its downstream pathway as promising therapeutic targets for this devastating disease.

Introduction

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a chronic, progressive, and ultimately fatal interstitial lung disease characterized by irreversible destruction of the alveolar structure and excessive deposition of extracellular matrix within the lung parenchyma.1 Although the precise etiology of IPF remains incompletely understood, the disease predominantly affects older individuals and is strongly associated with aging-related pathological processes, including cellular senescence, impaired tissue regeneration, and chronic epithelial dysfunction.2–5 The clinical outcome of IPF remains poor, with a median survival of approximately 3–5 years after diagnosis, which is comparable to or worse than that of many malignancies.1 Current antifibrotic therapies, including nintedanib and pirfenidone, as well as recently emerging compounds such as nerandomilast, can attenuate the rate of lung function decline but fail to reverse established fibrosis or restore normal alveolar architecture.6–8 Consequently, there is an urgent need to identify disease-driving molecular mechanisms and develop therapeutic strategies that target the fundamental processes underlying alveolar destruction and aberrant tissue remodeling. Increasing evidence suggests that IPF is not solely a fibroblast-centered disease, but rather a disorder initiated and perpetuated by dysfunctional epithelial repair responses following repetitive alveolar injury.9,10 Accordingly, elucidating the molecular mechanisms that regulate alveolar epithelial homeostasis and regeneration has become a major focus in the field of pulmonary fibrosis research.

Within the alveolar stem cell niche, alveolar type 2 (AT2) cells function as facultative stem/progenitor cells responsible for maintaining alveolar homeostasis and promoting epithelial regeneration after injury.11,12 In response to tissue damage, AT2 cells undergo proliferation and self-renewal and subsequently differentiate into alveolar type 1 (AT1) cells to restore the integrity of the alveolar epithelium. However, chronic or repetitive epithelial injury can impair this regenerative process, resulting in defective epithelial repair, accumulation of transitional epithelial states, persistent inflammatory signaling, and progressive fibrotic remodeling.10,13 Recent advances in single-cell and single-nucleus transcriptomic analyses have substantially improved our understanding of epithelial heterogeneity in IPF lungs and revealed the presence of abnormal epithelial populations characterized by impaired differentiation capacity, aberrant stress responses, and senescence-associated transcriptional programs.14,15 In particular, transitional epithelial cell populations expressing markers such as KRT8, CLDN4, and SFN have been proposed as maladaptive repair intermediates that accumulate in fibrotic lungs and contribute to disease progression.13,16 These findings support the concept that disruption of epithelial stemness and regenerative competence represents a central pathogenic mechanism in IPF. Multiple signaling pathways have been implicated in regulating AT2 cell fate decisions and alveolar regeneration, including the Wnt/β-catenin, Hippo-YAP/TAZ, transforming growth factor-β (TGF-β), and NOTCH signaling pathways.17,18 Coordinated regulation of these pathways is essential for maintaining epithelial plasticity and tissue repair capacity. Dysregulation of these signaling networks can lead to aberrant epithelial activation, impaired differentiation, and persistent fibrogenic signaling. Despite these advances, the upstream molecular mechanisms linking epithelial adhesion dynamics, stemness-associated signaling, and maladaptive repair responses in AT2 cells remain incompletely understood.

Emerging evidence from bioinformatic analyses and experimental studies has suggested that alterations in cell adhesion-associated proteins contribute to aging-related diseases (ARDs), including pulmonary fibrosis.19,20 Cell adhesion complexes not only provide structural integrity but also function as critical signaling hubs that regulate mechanotransduction, cell polarity, proliferation, differentiation, and stem cell behavior. βPAK-interacting exchange factor (βPIX), encoded by the Arhgef7 gene, is a guanine nucleotide exchange factor for the Rac1 and Cdc42 Rho GTPases and has been implicated in the regulation of focal adhesion turnover, cytoskeletal remodeling, and cell migration.21 βPIX forms multiprotein complexes with GIT and paxillin at focal adhesions and plays a pivotal role in the dynamic regulation of cell adhesion structures.21–23 In epithelial tissues, proper focal adhesion remodeling is essential for maintaining epithelial integrity and regenerative responses following injury. In addition to its structural functions, accumulating evidence suggests that βPIX may participate in signaling pathways associated with stemness and tissue regeneration. Previous studies demonstrated that βPIX directly interacts with β-catenin and modulates β-catenin-dependent transcriptional activity in colon cancer cells.24 Moreover, βPIX-mediated signaling has been shown to regulate YAP activation in mammary epithelial cells,25 indicating a potential role in integrating adhesion-dependent signaling with stemness-associated pathways. These observations suggest that βPIX may function as an upstream regulator of epithelial regenerative competence. Recently, reduced βPIX expression was reported to induce cellular senescence in cultured cells and murine tissues,26 further linking βPIX to aging-associated pathological processes. Given the established roles of epithelial senescence, defective alveolar regeneration, and aberrant epithelial repair in pulmonary fibrosis,3,4 these findings raise the possibility that loss of βPIX may contribute to IPF pathogenesis. Nevertheless, whether βPIX deficiency in AT2 cells is sufficient to induce pulmonary fibrosis and how βPIX regulates alveolar epithelial stemness and regenerative signaling remain unknown.

This study investigated whether deleting the βPIX gene in AT2 cells could generate a murine PF model and provide a platform for the development of new therapies. We generated AT2 cell-specific βPIX knockout (KO) mice and analyzed their lungs using an integrated approach, including single-nucleus RNA sequencing (snRNA-seq). These mice developed spontaneous PF in a progressive manner, primarily characterized by impaired regeneration and repair capacity of AT2 cells due to reduced Wnt/β-catenin signaling. Analysis of AT2 cells from IPF patients recapitulated these observed abnormalities. Collectively, our study identifies βPIX as a previously unrecognized regulator of alveolar epithelial stemness and demonstrates that disruption of adhesion-associated regenerative signaling is sufficient to drive spontaneous fibrotic lung remodeling. These findings provide new mechanistic insight into the epithelial basis of PF and suggest that restoration of βPIX-associated regenerative pathways may represent a potential therapeutic strategy for IPF.

Results

βPIX expression is reduced in a mouse model of bleomycin-induced fibrosis

We first examined the expression of βPIX (Arhgef7) mRNA across diverse cell populations in mice using publicly available scRNA-seq data. βPIX is broadly expressed throughout lung tissue, including in fibroblasts and endothelial cells (Supplementary Fig. 1a). Within the alveolar epithelium, βPIX expression is predominant in AT2 cells but negligible in AT1 cells (Supplementary Fig. 1b). To confirm these findings at the protein level, we performed immunofluorescence (IF). βPIX was observed mostly in pro-SPC+ (an AT2 cell marker) and cuboidal cells but was weakly detectable in a small number of PDPN+ (an AT1 cell marker) and elongated cells, indicating its high expression in AT2 cells (Supplementary Fig. 1c) quantified in d.

We next examined alterations in βPIX expression in a bleomycin-induced model of fibrosis in mice. The establishment of the bleomycin model was verified by confirming the presence of characteristic changes, including thickened alveolar walls and the accumulation of collagen, as assessed by Masson’s trichrome staining (Supplementary Fig. 2a). Immunohistochemical analysis of these model mice revealed a notable decrease in the expression of βPIX (Supplementary Fig. 2b, c). Furthermore, coimmunofluorescence (co-IF) analysis of βPIX and either pro-SPC or vimentin (a fibroblast marker) revealed that the reduction in βPIX expression in bleomycin-treated lungs occurred predominantly among pro-SPC + AT2 cells, with no discernible changes detected in vimentin+ fibroblasts (Supplementary Fig. 2d–g). Immunoblotting revealed a similar trend of decreased βPIX accompanied by markedly increased levels of fibronectin compared with those in saline-treated controls (Supplementary Fig. 2h, i). These results suggested that decreased βPIX in AT2 cells might be associated with the development of PF.

βPIX-knockout mice develop progressive pulmonary fibrosis

To determine whether loss of βPIX in AT2 cells is causally related to PF, we generated a transgenic line of mutant mice (βPIXfl/fl;SftpcCre-ER;ROSAR26R-EYFP) with conditional βPIX depletion and EYFP expression in pro-SPC+ cells (Supplementary Fig. 3a). Injection of tamoxifen (TMX) resulted in deletion of the βPIX gene, as confirmed by PCR and decreased βPIX protein expression, as determined by Western blotting (Supplementary Fig. 3b–e). In 4-week TMX-treated βPIX-knockout mice (KO_04), EYFP+ cells showed reduced βPIX fluorescence compared with TMX-treated reporter mice (SftpcCre-ER; ROSAR26R-EYFP) and vehicle-treated wild-type mice (WT_04) (Supplementary Fig. 3f), indicating that the βPIX gene can be deleted specifically in AT2 cells in a murine βPIX-KO model. Consistent with these findings, Masson’s trichrome and α-SMA staining revealed distinct pulmonary fibrosis in βPIX-KO mice at least 4 weeks post-TMX treatment, whereas reporter mice showed no significant fibrotic changes (Supplementary Fig. 3g–i). However, as the deletion was incomplete, we acknowledge that gene excision in Cre-loxP systems is seldom absolute; such inherent variability likely contributes to fluctuations in the phenotypic severity among individual mice.

We next investigated the causal relationship between βPIX loss and fibrosis. TMX was applied, followed by histological and biochemical analyses, including snRNA-seq analysis (Fig. 1a). WT mice showed progressive weight gain, whereas TMX-treated KO mice initially lost weight until the 12th week and plateaued to their initial weight (Fig. 1b, c). Survival analysis of the KO mice revealed that one each died at 6 weeks and 10 weeks (Fig. 1d). Masson’s trichrome staining revealed higher levels of collagen in KO_04 mice than in WT_04 mice (Fig. 1e, Supplementary Fig. 4a, b). Collagen staining increased further in 24-week-old KO mice (KO_24) (Fig. 1e, Supplementary Fig. 4c, d), indicating fibrosis progression. The Ashcroft score (Fig. 1f) and collagen content, as measured by the Sircol assay (Fig. 1g), were much greater in the KO mice than in their WT counterparts. Comparisons of these two parameters between KO_04 and KO_24 mice also supported the progression of fibrosis. Notably, several key features of human IPF were observed in KO_24 mice (Supplementary Fig. 4d), including multiple honeycomb-like cysts, prominent fibrosis along the subpleural area (Supplementary Fig. 4e, A-1, 2), and a patchy distribution of relatively normal looking areas but with thickened alveolar walls (Supplementary Fig. 4e, B-3, 4). However, the fibroblastic foci typically observed in human IPF have rarely been identified in mice. To further determine whether the micro-CT scans reflected the observed histological changes, we performed imaging analysis. The lungs of WT mice maintained their normal architecture throughout the 24-week period (Fig. 1h, left). Moreover, the lungs of the KO_04 mice showed early signs of fibrotic remodeling, including localized increases in parenchymal density and patchy opacities. The progression to advanced fibrosis was evident in the KO_24 mice, whose scans revealed a significant increase in lung density, diffuse opacities, and the formation of cyst-like structures. Consistent with these findings, compared with WT_24 control mice, KO_24 mice presented higher mean Hounsfield units (HUs), higher percentile 5 (HU), and lower standard deviations (Fig. 1i). Having revealed that the mouse model exhibited key features of human IPF, we sought to characterize the role of AT2 βPIX in driving PF more thoroughly. Compared with WT mice, KO mice had higher alpha-smooth muscle actin (α-SMA) staining at both 4 and 24 weeks, indicating increased tissue contractility due to active collagen-producing myofibroblasts (Fig. 1j, k). Compared with those in WT control mice, the levels of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-β) signaling mediators, such as pSMAD3 and PAI-1, were also increased in KO mice (Fig. 1l, m). Taken together, these findings suggest that βPIX loss in AT2 cells drives progressive PF over 24 weeks.

snRNA-seq analysis of βPIX-KO mice revealed a profibrotic environment that promotes the development of pulmonary fibrosis

Deciphering the dynamic interplay between various lung cell types in fibrosis is key to understanding disease mechanisms. To investigate how βPIX deficiency contributes to this process, we conducted snRNA-seq analysis on our βPIX-deficient mouse model. Lungs from WT and KO mice were harvested at 4 and 24 weeks. Three mice per condition were pooled into a single gel bead-in-emulsion (GEM) for sequencing. After quality control, we identified 32,287 genes across 35,804 cells (8927 cells from WT_04, 8951 from KO_04, 8296 from WT_24, and 9630 from KO_24). After batch correction, clustering using the Louvain algorithm subdivided the cells into 15 subsets on the basis of canonical marker genes (Supplementary Fig. 5a, b). Uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) analysis revealed increased numbers of alveolar macrophages, myeloid cells, and lymphocytes in the KO group, indicating heightened inflammation (Supplementary Fig. 5c).

We first analyzed the effect of βPIX deficiency on alveolar epithelial cells. We observed a significant increase in cell populations in a transitional state between AT2 and AT1 in the KO group compared with those in the WT control group (Fig. 2a, middle, red circles). These cells displayed the highest expression of genes such as Cldn4, Krt8, Sprr1a, and Tnip3 (Fig. 2a, b), previously identified in damage-associated transient progenitor (DATP), alveolar differentiation intermediate (ADI), and prealveolar type 1 transitional cell state (PATS) cells and are referred to hereafter as DATP cells.10,13,16 Cluster analysis revealed DATP cells as a “bridge” between the AT2 and AT1 cell clusters in the KO group, but this was rarely observed in the WT group (Fig. 2a, middle, red circles). Quantitative analysis revealed a progressive expansion of the DATP population (Fig. 2a, right). To validate our snRNA-seq findings, we performed lineage tracing of the DATP population within βPIX-deficient AT2 cells. Using coimmunofluorescence for the lineage marker EYFP and the DATP marker KRT8, we observed a progressive increase in the number of KRT8+ lineage-labeled cells, which increased from 6% in the KO_04 group to 14.5% in the KO_24 group (Supplementary Fig. 6). KRT8 + AT2 cells were not detected in the reporter mice. The gene expression patterns analyzed by k-means clustering and hierarchical clustering generated three gene modules (M1–3) (Fig. 2c). The M1 module, which was highly expressed in KO_24 mice, included genes associated with the PF (e.g., Spp1, Ccn2, and Atp11a), focal adhesion (Lamc2, Col1a2 and Actg1), oxidative stress (Sod2 and Cyba), and Keap1-Nrf2 (Cebpb, Gclc, and Nfe2l2) pathways (Fig. 2c, right panel). Gene Ontology (GO) analysis confirmed the presence of TGF-β production and the enrichment of wound healing- and integrin pathway-related genes in the KO group27 (Supplementary Fig. 5d).

We next analyzed fibroblast populations because they are effectors of fibrosis. Detailed analysis of fibroblast subclusters revealed two myofibroblast populations with different expression levels of Acta2, Cthrc1, Col1a1, Runx1, and Pdgfrb (Supplementary Fig. 5e, f). The cluster with the highest marker expression was more abundant in the KO group than in the WT group (Fig. 2d). Differential gene expression analysis revealed increased expression of fibrosis-related genes in the KO group (Supplementary Fig. 5g). An examination of fibrosis-related genes in myofibroblasts across conditions revealed increased expression of genes such as Chl1,28 Kif26b29 and Ltbp230 in the KO group (Fig. 2e). These analyses revealed specific signs of severe fibrosis in the KO group.

Finally, we analyzed intercellular communication between epithelial cells, fibroblasts, and alveolar macrophages because their aberrant interaction is a key feature of fibrosis.31,32 Among the key signaling pathways linked to fibrosis are the collagen, TGF-β, and secreted phosphoprotein 1 (Spp1) pathways.33–35 Increased communication of the collagen pathway was detected, particularly in DATP cells (Fig. 2f), even at an early stage (Supplementary Fig. 7a, b). These findings suggest that DATP cells play a crucial role in early fibrosis development. In late-stage fibrosis, analysis of the TGF-β pathway revealed unique interactions between DATP cells and myofibroblasts in the KO group, but these interactions were absent in the healthy WT group (Fig. 2g, Supplementary Fig. 7c). Notably, Spp1 signaling was detected in all cell types in late-stage fibrosis (Supplementary Fig. 7d). Taken together, our snRNA-seq data suggest that the conditional deletion of βPIX in AT2 cells creates an environment that promotes the accumulation of ECM-producing myofibroblasts, eventually leading to fibrosis.

To provide a broader understanding of the biological context of our KO model, we analyzed a publicly available scRNA-seq dataset from a bleomycin-induced lung fibrosis model36 and compared it with that from our βPIX-KO model. When we subset to alveolar epithelial lineages, the bleomycin dataset recapitulates the expected AT2–DATP–AT1 continuum, with a transitional population positioned between AT2 and AT1, similar to our model (Supplementary Fig. 8a, b). Further analysis of a curated set of genes repeatedly reported to increase in bleomycin-injured AT2/DATP-like states revealed that a subset of these genes is already elevated in KO_4 mice and becomes more pronounced in KO_24 mice, supporting the generality of our βPIX-KO model (Supplementary Fig. 8c).

Loss of βPIX in AT2 cells induces senescence

Previously, we demonstrated that βPIX knockdown induces senescence.26 We therefore asked whether βPIX depletion in AT2 cells induces senescence in the lung. Western blotting of lung lysates revealed higher expression of senescence markers, including p53, p21WAF1 and γH2AX, in the KO_04 group than in the WT_04 group (Supplementary Fig. 9a). A salient feature of senescence is the senescence-associated secretory phenotype (SASP), which forms inflammatory niches crucial for AT2 cell regeneration and is driven partly by IL-1β signaling.16 IL-1β, IL-6, and TNFα transcripts were most abundant in AT2 cells from the KO_24 group, with the level of TGF-β2 specifically elevated in the KO group (Supplementary Fig. 9b). This specific form of TGF-β is elevated in human fibrotic lungs,37 suggesting that it plays a similar role in both human and mouse fibrosis. Among the analyzed epithelial populations, DATP cells exhibited the most pronounced senescence signature, a phenotype that was globally exacerbated across all epithelial types in the KO group (Supplementary Fig. 9c). Interestingly, at the 4-week time point, although the DATP transcriptional signature was evident in the snRNA-seq data, cells coexpressing the DATP marker KRT8 and the senescence marker p21WAF1 were rarely detected via immunofluorescence (Supplementary Fig. 9d; quantified in e). This temporal dissociation suggests that the transcriptomic shift toward a DATP state and the onset of cellular senescence may initiate independent events during early pathogenesis. However, by 24 weeks, the KRT8/p21WAF1 double-positive population had expanded significantly, comprising approximately 30% of the EYFP+ and βPIX-deficient AT2 cells (Supplementary Fig. 9d); quantified in e). While we acknowledge the limitation of relying on a single marker combination, these findings support the notion that DATP transition and senescence emerge as initially distinct processes that converge during later stages to synergistically drive fibrotic progression.

We next evaluated the expression of the senescence markers p53 and p21WAF1 in AT2 cells isolated from βPIX-WT and -KO mice via Western blotting. Both p53 and p21WAF1 expression were significantly upregulated in βPIX-deficient AT2 cells compared with WT control cells (Supplementary Fig. 9f; quantified in g). To further validate this, we assessed senescence-associated β-galactosidase (SA-β-Gal) activity in AT2 cells isolated from βPIXfl/fl;SftpcCre-ER;ROSAR26R-EYFP mice with and without 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT)-induced excision. We observed that SA-β-Gal activity increased from 11% in control cells to 46% in βPIX-deficient cells (Supplementary Fig. 9h; quantified in i). Collectively, these findings support the conclusion that βPIX depletion induces cellular senescence in AT2 cells, likely contributing to the progression of pulmonary fibrosis.

Loss of βPIX in AT2 cells impaired stemness and regenerative capacity by deregulating Wnt/β-catenin signaling

To explore the role of βPIX, which might directly contribute to fibrosis, we conducted a more in-depth analysis of the snRNA-seq data, in addition to investigating its role in senescence. The observation of βPIX KO-AT2 cells generating DATP cells and persisting in the AT2-AT1 transitional state suggests impaired stemness in these βPIX-deficient cells. To confirm this, we first analyzed the gene expression profile of Bmi1-KO mice,38 a known stemness-impaired mouse model, and compared the stemness of each cluster of AT2 cells via gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) (Fig. 3a, Supplementary Fig. 10a). These findings confirmed the significant impairment of stemness in primed AT2 and DATP cells, which was more prevalent in the KO group. Pseudotime modeling revealed a unique ‘DATP bridge’ in the KO_04 group, which was rarely observed in WT_04 (Supplementary Fig. 10b). Both the WT and KO groups showed two bifurcations, but the second bifurcation led to different target cells: primed AT2 cells in WT mice and DATP cells in KO mice (Fig. 3b). The gene expression patterns at branch point 2 in the KO group revealed two pathways: pathway_1 (shared by WT and KO) and pathway_2 (KO-specific) (Fig. 3c). The expression of the mature AT1-specific genes Hopx and Pdpn decreased in pathway_2, leading to defective AT1 differentiation (Fig. 3d, Supplementary Fig. 10c, d). To confirm the findings from the pseudotime analysis, we performed IF staining on lung tissue sections by using established AT2 and AT1 markers. Quantification of the AT1/AT2 ratio within alveolar regions revealed a significant decrease of 40% in the lungs of KO_04 mice compared with those of WT_04 controls (Supplementary Fig. 11). To determine the dysregulated pathways associated with impaired stemness, we performed KEGG pathway and GO analyses on the genes whose expression was downregulated in the KO group (Supplementary Fig. 10e). Wnt and Hippo signaling emerged as potential target pathways for impaired stemness (Fig. 3e). GSK-3β is a key regulator of β-catenin stability, promoting its phosphorylation and subsequent degradation through the ubiquitin‒proteasome pathway. We administered the GSK-3β inhibitor SB216763 (SB) to βPIX-KO mice (Fig. 5a). Masson’s trichrome staining revealed that SB reduced fibrosis in the KO mice but had no significant effect on the WT mice (Fig. 5b). Western blot analysis revealed that SB restored Wnt/β-catenin signaling in the KO mice, as assessed by elevated active β-catenin, β-catenin and Axin2 levels, counteracting their initial decline (Fig. 5c, d). To investigate whether GSK-3β inhibition could restore the stemness potential of AT2 cells in βPIX-KO mice, we isolated AT2 cells and cultured them in 3-D Matrigel. Cells were treated with 4-OHT for βPIX depletion and the GSK-3β inhibitor CHIR99021 (CHIR), as illustrated in the scheme (Fig. 5i). βPIX/GIT molecular condensates increase the stability of β-catenin by forming a ‘β-catenin protection complex’

Given that βPIX critically regulates AT2 cell stemness through Wnt/β-catenin signaling, we aimed to elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms involved. βPIX, in conjunction with its binding partner G protein-coupled receptor kinase-interacting protein (GIT), forms biomolecular condensates that create distinct cellular compartments.40 Previous studies have also reported a direct interaction between βPIX and β-catenin24 and that PAK1 phosphorylates β-catenin at serine 675.41 However, the precise mechanisms through which the βPIX/GIT complex regulates β-catenin stability remain largely unknown. To investigate this in the context of AT2 cell stemness, we utilized A549 cells, an AT2-like cell line. Immunoprecipitation experiments using anti-βPIX antibodies revealed a macromolecular complex containing βPIX, β-catenin, GIT, and PAK1 (Fig. 6a). This finding was reproduced in isolated AT2 cells (Supplementary Fig. 13a). Notably, this complex also includes GSK-3β, suggesting a link to β-catenin degradation. To investigate the role of βPIX in regulating β-catenin stability, we performed βPIX knockdown followed by immunoprecipitation experiments. Anti-β-catenin immunoprecipitates revealed that in the presence of βPIX, β-catenin interacts with PAK1 and GSK-3β (Fig. 6b, left, top, 1st lane). In this state, PAK1-mediated phosphorylation of β-catenin at serine 675 (pβ-cateninS675), which increases its stability, is dominant, whereas GSK-3β-mediated phosphorylation at S33/37/T41 (pβ-cateninS33/37/T41), which is destined for degradation, is suppressed. Upon depletion of βPIX, β-catenin preferentially interacted with GSK-3β and GIT, almost excluding PAK1 from the complex (Fig. 6b, left, top, 2nd and 3rd lanes). Consistent with these changes, both the anti-β-catenin immunoprecipitates and the βPIX-depleted lysates presented higher levels of pβ-cateninS33/37/T41 (Fig. 6b, bottom, 2nd and 3rd lanes). Conversely, lower levels of pβ-cateninS675 were detected. These findings suggest that βPIX promotes β-catenin stability by facilitating PAK1-mediated phosphorylation while simultaneously suppressing GSK-3β-dependent phosphorylation. To confirm these alterations in molecular assembly, we performed a proximity ligation assay (PLA). βPIX silencing markedly reduced the number of PLA puncta between β-catenin and PAK1 (Fig. 6c, top; quantified in middle panel). However, the association between β-catenin and GSK-3β was not significantly altered according to the results of the PLA (Fig. 6c, bottom; quantified in right panel). These results prompted us to explore the role of the GIT under βPIX-depleted conditions. Immunoprecipitation with anti-GIT antibodies revealed that βPIX depletion markedly increased the interaction between GIT and β-catenin and between GIT and GSK-3β while reducing the interaction between GIT and PAK1 (Fig. 6d). Further analysis by PLA confirmed this interaction; compared with control cells, βPIX-depleted cells displayed higher IF signals for both GIT/β-catenin and GIT/GSK-3β pairs (Fig. 6e). These results suggested that βPIX loss promotes the formation of a new GIT/β-catenin/GSK-3β complex, potentially linked to the GSK-3β-mediated phosphorylation of β-catenin followed by its ubiquitination-dependent proteolysis. To test this idea, we analyzed the effect of MG132, an inhibitor of proteasome-dependent degradation, on β-catenin stability. βPIX depletion induced a decrease in β-catenin levels, which was reversed by treatment with MG132 (Fig. 6f, 1st row). In this context, βPIX depletion increased pβ-cateninS33/37/41 levels, which was more evident in response to MG132 treatment (Fig. 6f, 2nd row). We also observed similar restoration of Axin2 and c-Myc levels in response to MG132 treatment (Fig. 6f, 4th and 5th rows). These results strongly suggested that loss of βPIX led to proteolytic degradation of β-catenin. Next, we examined the involvement of β-TrCP E3 ligase, which is well known to ubiquitinate β-catenin. Ubiquitination experiments were conducted in HEK293T cells following the transfection of plasmids expressing tagged forms of β-catenin, β-TrCP and ubiquitin. Knocking down βPIX resulted in increased levels of ubiquitinated β-catenin (Fig. 6g, left). Consistent with these results, βPIX-depleted cells exhibited higher levels of β-TrCP in anti-Flag (β-catenin) immunoprecipitates.

Finally, we investigated whether a similar regulatory mechanism occurs in AT2 cells isolated from βPIX-KO mice. Western blot analysis revealed significant decreases in both total β-catenin and pβ-cateninS675 levels in βPIX KO-AT2 cells compared with those in their WT counterparts (Fig. 6h, 1st and 2nd rows; quantified in i). We further examined alterations in the levels of GSK-3β and its inactive phosphorylated form, pGSK-3βS9. βPIX depletion resulted in a 50% decrease in the ratio of pGSK-3βS9 to total GSK-3β, indicating a significant increase in its kinase activity (Fig. 6h, 4th and 5th rows; quantified in i). Interestingly, the degradation-associated form, pβ-cateninS33/37/T41, was uniquely present in the KO AT2 cells, although it was most prominent in samples from the two KO mice. In addition, the expression of the Wnt target genes Axin2 and c-Myc was downregulated in βPIX-KO AT2 cells. Consistent with the in vitro findings, pβ-cateninS675 levels were lower in the AT2 cells of the lungs of the KO mice than in those of the WT mice (Supplementary Fig. 14).

In summary, we propose a working model in which βPIX acts as a molecular switch to regulate AT2 cell stemness by coordinating the balance between PAK1 and GSK-3β signaling via the GIT (Fig. 7). Under physiological conditions, sufficient βPIX facilitates PAK1-mediated phosphorylation of β-catenin at S675 while simultaneously suppressing GSK-3β-dependent phosphorylation (Fig. 7, left panel). This mechanism stabilizes β-catenin, thereby preserving the Wnt signaling required for AT2 stemness and normal AT2-to-AT1 differentiation. Conversely, βPIX depletion triggers the dissociation of PAK1 and facilitates the assembly of a GIT/β-catenin/GSK-3β complex. This shift promotes the GSK-3β-mediated phosphorylation of β-catenin at S33/37/T41, leading to β-TrCP-mediated ubiquitination and subsequent proteolysis (Fig. 7, right panel, left). The resulting suppression of Wnt/β-catenin signaling impairs stemness, causing aberrant AT2-to-AT1 differentiation and the persistent accumulation of DATP cells. βPIX expression levels and Wnt/β-catenin signaling are reduced in AT2 cells from patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis

Finally, to translate our findings in mice to a clinical context, we examined βPIX expression and related signaling in IPF patients. Analysis of public transcriptomic data at the cellular level revealed downregulation of βPIX mRNA expression in AT2 cells but not in total lung epithelial cells or fibroblasts from IPF patients (Fig. 8a).14 This finding was further corroborated by IF, which revealed substantially weaker βPIX staining in IPF AT2 cells than in AT2 cells from age-matched controls (ACs) (Fig. 8b, c; Supplementary Fig. 15a). We subsequently aimed to determine whether key molecular events in our βPIX-KO model were mirrored in these IPF patients. IF studies targeting pβ-cateninS675 in HTII-280-positive AT2 cells from ACs revealed strong peripheral staining that did not colocalize with that of HTII-280 (Fig. 8d, e), which is consistent with the findings in mice (Supplementary Fig. 14). Conversely, IPF AT2 cells exhibited significantly lower pβ-cateninS675 levels. IF signals for total β-catenin and the Wnt target Axin2 were also significantly reduced in IPF AT2 cells, indicating decreased Wnt signaling (Fig. 8f–i). In contrast to the weak expression of β-catenin in IPF AT2 cells, fibrotic regions where collagen-producing mesenchymal cells are abundant exhibit strong IF signals for β-catenin (Supplementary Fig. 15b), which is consistent with previous reports.42 In the fibrotic milieu, where YAP and Wnt/β-catenin signaling are activated because of increased mechanical stress, β-catenin may no longer be regulated by βPIX. Collectively, these IPF data support the findings observed in βPIX-KO mice, strongly suggesting that βPIX/Wnt signaling is disrupted in AT2 cells. DISCUSSION

To identify potential therapeutic strategies, we generated a preclinical mouse model of progressive PF by conditionally ablating βPIX in AT2 cells. These mice developed key histological features characteristic of human IPF. snRNA-seq analysis revealed senescent alveolar epithelial cells in an intermediate differentiation state, indicating impaired stemness. Given the significantly reduced βPIX levels observed in IPF AT2 cells, βPIX may contribute to IPF development. Using this novel animal model, we demonstrated that targeting the βPIX-Wnt/β-catenin signaling pathway with a GSK-3β inhibitor represents a potential therapeutic approach.

Developmental pathways such as the Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, and Hippo/YAP signaling pathways are activated as a physiological response during wound healing and regeneration after injury. These signaling pathways are hence frequently dysregulated in disease conditions, including experimental and human fibrosis.2,43 Considering their critical roles in the maintenance of the alveolar stem cell niche, decreased Wnt/β-catenin, Notch, and Hippo/YAP signaling in βPIX-deficient AT2 cells indicates impaired stem cell functions, inhibiting their self-renewal and differentiation into mature AT1 cells. The finding that AT2 cells derived from βPIX-KO mice exhibited markedly reduced organoid-forming potential supports this notion. The present study thus unveiled a role for βPIX, unidentified hitherto in the regulation of AT2 cell stemness. In contrast, previous studies on βPIX-KO mice have demonstrated that this gene plays a role mainly in the regulation of cell migration during embryogenesis, actin cytoskeletal rearrangement in the kidney, and villus maturation and mucosal defenses in the colon.44–47 Collectively, our findings in the βPIX-deficient model support the prevailing hypothesis of PF pathogenesis that the impaired regenerative capacity of AT2 cells following repeated injury triggers aberrant wound healing, ultimately leading to fibrosis.9,15

Given the observed reduction in Wnt/β-catenin signaling in AT2 cells and impaired alveolar organoid formation, we hypothesized that reactivating this pathway could serve as a therapeutic strategy. Our findings support this hypothesis, demonstrating that a GSK-3β inhibitor effectively attenuated fibrosis in the early stages in βPIX-KO mice. These results suggest that the activation of developmental pathways such as the Wnt/β-catenin and Hippo/YAP signaling pathways functions as a protective mechanism during the early phase of pulmonary fibrosis (PF), preserving epithelial homeostasis and preventing disease progression. These observations align with those of previous studies highlighting the beneficial effects of β-catenin in mitigating bleomycin-induced fibrosis and YAP/TAZ in promoting alveolar regeneration following acute lung injury.18,48 However, when aberrantly activated, Wnt/β-catenin signaling plays a context-dependent dual role, supporting epithelial regeneration while promoting pathological remodeling. Thus, caution is warranted when the use of GSK-3β inhibitors in advanced fibrosis is considered because of the observed activation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling in non-AT2 cell types, particularly myofibroblasts, as evident in KO_24 mice (Fig. 4a). These observations also suggest a potential limitation in the therapeutic window of GSK-3β inhibition, where the timing and cell type specificity of pathway activation are likely critical determinants of its beneficial versus deleterious effects. Recent studies have demonstrated the potential of selectively targeting AT2 cells with Wnt agonists without affecting fibroblast or endothelial cell signaling.49 Given the persistent reduction in Wnt/β-catenin signaling in AT2 cells, even in the late stage of the βPIX-KO model, investigating the stage-specific effects of these selective Wnt/β-catenin agonists could be a promising avenue of research.

Our findings demonstrate that βPIX plays a crucial role in AT2 cell stemness by regulating β-catenin stability, as illustrated by our working model (Fig. 7). Notably, we observed that both βPIX and pβ-cateninS675 predominantly localize to the cell periphery, specifically at cell–cell adhesions (such as adherens junctions). These observations suggest that the spatial organization of βPIX is key to its regulation of AT2 stemness. While βPIX is well known as a regulator of focal adhesion dynamics, particularly disassembly,22,23 its function at cell‒cell junctions is less understood. Although previous work has implicated βPIX in adherens junction stability50 and inhibition of proliferation,51 further investigation is needed to fully elucidate its mechanistic role in modulating β-catenin signaling at these sites. Collectively, our results highlight the βPIX-mediated Wnt/β-catenin axis as a promising therapeutic target to restore AT2 cell stemness and ameliorate fibrotic lung remodeling.

Senescence is a well-established feature of PF,3,4 prompting investigations into its causal role in IPF development. This study demonstrated that βPIX-deficient AT2 cells exhibit senescence, which spreads across diverse cell types, suggesting the occurrence of paracrine senescence. Moreover, the severity of senescence increased over time in βPIX-deficient mice, likely contributing to the progressive nature of fibrosis. These findings align with previous research showing that the conditional abrogation of the Sin3a gene in AT2 cells induces senescence and promotes PF.4 Our snRNA-seq data revealed that, in addition to senescent characteristics, AT2 cells in βPIX-deficient mice were in a transitional stem state and expressed genes associated with the DNA damage response, which are shared by DATP, ADI and PATS cells, as previously described.10,13,16 AT2 cell senescence is frequently associated with stemness failure, although the underlying mechanisms are not yet fully understood.5 In our model, βPIX-KO-induced senescence likely contributed to depletion of the stem cell pool in AT2 cells by reducing their self-renewal capacity (Fig. 7). Moreover, βPIX depletion directly impaired stemness through the downregulation of Wnt/β-catenin signaling, as discussed earlier. This interplay between senescence and reduced Wnt/β-catenin signaling may synergistically impact AT2 stemness, as evidenced by the limited organoid formation by βPIX-KO mouse-derived AT2 cells.

In this study, we utilized snRNA-seq analysis to explore the cellular composition and gene expression in lung tissues. Our analysis of cell‒cell interactions lacks spatial information. This lack of spatial resolution needs to be addressed with further investigations, potentially through spatial transcriptomics. Despite these limitations, the CellChat tool employed in our analysis is a robust and reliable method for inferring cell‒cell communication networks in fibrosis. Second, although the persistence of DATPs is associated with PF, the exact underlying mechanism is poorly understood.52,53 Further investigations utilizing βPIX-KO mice are warranted to comprehensively understand the functional roles of these intermediate cell populations and their potential contributions to the pathogenesis of fibrosis.

In conclusion, this study revealed a novel role for βPIX in alveolar epithelial stemness and revealed that its depletion drove fibrosis. As a proof-of-concept for the function of the βPIX-Wnt/β-catenin pathway in PF, we demonstrated the fibrosis-attenuating effect of a GSK-3β inhibitor in the βPIX-KO model. Further investigations using this model can lead to a deeper understanding of the underlying mechanisms of PF, paving the way for more targeted treatment approaches.

Materials and Methods

Sex as a biological variable

In this study, we used genetically modified animals generated through a tamoxifen-inducible Cre-loxP system. To avoid hormonal interference from tamoxifen, we selectively used male mice for in vivo experiments. In contrast, female mice were used for in vitro organoid experiments in which Cre recombination was induced with 4-hydroxytamoxifen (4-OHT). For human studies, including immunostaining and scRNA sequencing analysis, both male and female donor samples were analyzed.

Study approvals

All animal experiments were conducted in accordance with the National Institutes of Health and the ARRIVE guidelines and were approved by the Ethics Review Committee for Animal Experiments of Chungbuk National University (CBUNA-24-0066-02). This study, which included human samples, was reviewed and approved by the Institutional Review Board of the Chungbuk National University Hospital (CBNH-2021-07-005).

Human lung tissue collection and study

Human lung tissues were obtained immediately after surgical resection and prepared at the Hospital of Chungbuk National University. The study included two experimental groups: IPF patients and age-matched controls (ACs). Lung tissues were collected from 7 IPF patients, with a mean age of 63.1 years, while the age-matched control (AC) group consisted of 7 age-matched individuals, with a mean age of 69.6 years (Fig. 8) (Supplementary Table 1).

Animals

All the mice were closely monitored for changes in body weight and overall health. In the event of a loss of ≥ 20% of body weight, the mice were euthanized and processed in accordance with institutional animal welfare regulations; such events were recorded as mortality. To maintain consistent environmental conditions, the mice were housed under a 12 h light/dark cycle.

Generation of mice with a conditional knockout of βPIX in lung epithelial cells

Mice with a conditional deletion of βPIX (βPIX KO) in lung epithelial cells were generated by intercrossing βPIXfl/fl mice (Arhgef7tm1a (EUCOMM) Wtsi, Sanger Institute, stock number MEBY EPD0766_4_A08), SftpcCreER mice (The Jackson Laboratory, stock number 028054), and ROSAR26R-EYFP mice (The Jackson Laboratory, stock number 006148). Reporter mice (SftpcCreER; ROSAR26R-EYFP) were generated by intercrossing SftpcCreER mice and ROSAR26R-EYFP mice. All progenitor mouse lines were maintained on a C57BL/6 N background. To induce conditional knockout of βPIX in the lung, mice (6~8 weeks old) received tamoxifen (200 mg/kg body weight) via intraperitoneal injection for three days, followed by weekly injections for either 4 or 24 weeks.4 To verify the specific deletion of exon 6 within βPIX, polymerase chain reaction (PCR) was performed on DNA extracted from mouse tails. To detect the tdm1c (218 bp) and tm1d (174 bp) alleles, PCR was conducted using primer combinations detailed in Omelchenko et al.45 To detect tm1c and tm1d, the following primer combinations were used: Tm1c (tm1c_F, 5′-AAGGCGCATAACGATACCAC-3′; tm1c_R, 5′-CCGCCTACTGCGACTATAGAGA-3′), and Tm1d (tm1c_F, 5′-AAGGCGCATAACGATACCAC-3′; Floxed LR, 5′-AAGGCGCATAACGATACCAC-3′). To analyze the effect of βPIX KO on the activity of the GSK-3β signaling pathway, mice were treated with tamoxifen for one week, followed by the administration of the GSK-3β inhibitor SB216763 (Sigma, T5648) via intraperitoneal injection at a dosage of 20 mg/kg. A vehicle control consisting of 25% dimethyl sulfoxide, 25% polyethylene glycol, and 50% saline was used for comparison. Injections were given twice weekly for a duration of three weeks.

Quantification of collagen

A Sircol Soluble Collagen Assay Kit (Biocolor Life Science Assays, S1000) was used to quantify the soluble collagen content in the right lungs of the mice following the manufacturer’s protocol. Briefly, acid pepsin was prepared with 0.1 mg/ml pepsin in 0.5 M acetic acid (Sigma). Lung tissues were weighed and then incubated overnight at 4 °C with mechanical shaking in acid pepsin. After digestion, the lung tissues were centrifuged at 3000 × g for 10 min. Sircol staining was then conducted on the supernatants to quantify the collagen content.

Masson’s trichrome staining

Paraffin-embedded tissues were cut into sections, followed by prestaining deparaffinization. For histological analyses, sections were stained according to the manufacturer’s protocols for (i) general tissue architecture using hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) and (ii) signs of fibrosis using Masson’s trichrome. Briefly, Masson’s trichrome staining was performed by treating tissue sections with Weigert’s iron hematoxylin to visualize the nuclei, followed by Biebrich scarlet-acid fuchsin staining to highlight the cytoplasm and muscle fibers in red. The sections were subsequently counterstained with aniline blue to accentuate the collagen fibers in blue. Finally, the stained sections were dehydrated, cleared, and mounted for microscopic examination. The severity of pulmonary fibrosis (PF) was evaluated according to the Ashcroft scoring system based on histological staining.

Microcomputed tomography (CT) imaging

The mice were anesthetized with 2% isoflurane in 100% oxygen and positioned prone on a heated bed. Imaging was performed using a hybrid PET/CT scanner (nanoScan® PET/CT, Mediso, Budapest, Hungary) in CT-only mode (no PET, no radiotracer). The CT acquisition parameters were as follows: X-ray source voltage 50 kVp with a 0.5 mm aluminum filter, tube current 660 μA, and exposure time 300 ms per projection. A total of 480 projections were acquired with 0.7° angular increments over 180°. The estimated absorbed dose per scan was approximately 51.9 mGy, as reported by the scanner software. The acquired datasets were subsequently imported into RadiAnt DICOM Viewer (Medixant, Poznań, Poland), which was used exclusively for postprocessing and visualization. Representative CT images illustrating pulmonary changes were obtained from RadiAnt Viewer and are presented in the figure panels.

For quantitative analysis, lung regions of interest (ROIs) were segmented from the thoracic cavity using intensity-based thresholding, followed by minimal manual refinement to exclude large airways and nonpulmonary tissues. All segmentation procedures were performed in a blinded manner, with identical threshold parameters applied across groups to ensure consistency. Voxelwise Hounsfield unit (HU) values were extracted from the segmented lung volumes to calculate standardized density metrics derived from HU distributions.

Single-nucleus RNA (snRNA) sequencing

Single nuclei were isolated from frozen βPIX-KO mouse lung tissues using a modified ribonuclease-free protocol. Lung tissues were sectioned to a size of 7 mm, lysed with ice-chilled Nuclei EZ Lysis buffer containing protease and RNase inhibitors, and further processed to a size of 1–2 mm. Following additional lysis, the mixture was processed in a gentleMACS C tube with specific program settings and strained through a 40-µm filter, after which the nuclei were collected by centrifugation. The nuclei were resuspended in PBS supplemented with additives, adjusted to 1000 nuclei/µl, and used for single-cell RNA sequencing. Sequencing was conducted using the 10x Genomics Chromium system and NovaSeq S1, targeting 50,000 reads per cell. Nuclei from three mice per condition were pooled prior to GEM generation; therefore, individual cells were not treated as independent biological replicates.

snRNA sequencing data processing and analysis

Raw sequencing data were processed using the zUMIs pipeline, low-quality barcodes were removed, and the remaining barcodes were mapped to the mouse genome (mm10) using Cell Ranger v4 (10x Genomics). Expression matrices containing intronic, exonic, and intronic+exonic reads were generated for the snRNA-seq data. Data normalization and integration were performed using the SCTransform method and the Harmony algorithm for batch effect correction in Seurat 4. Clustering analysis was applied to AT cells, fibroblasts, and immune cells using specific resolution settings and principal components.

To perform pseudotime analysis, the developmental trajectories of AT cell subsets were analyzed using Monocle2 and Monocle3. The procedures included data normalization, size factor estimation, gene expression dispersion, and trajectory inference using dimension reduction and ordering methods. Intercellular communication among AT cells, fibroblasts, and immune cells was analyzed using the CellChat package, with a focus on fibrosis-related pathways such as those involving collagen and TGF-β signaling.

Statistical analysis

All the data are presented as the mean values ± standard errors of the means (SEM) and were derived from at least three independent experiments or biological replicates, as specified in the figure legends. Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism (GraphPad Software version 8.0). For comparisons between two groups of independent groups, a two-tailed unpaired Student’s t-test was used when the normality assumption was satisfied; otherwise, the nonparametric Mann‒Whitney U test was applied. Comparisons among multiple groups were conducted using one-way or two-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by appropriate post hoc multiple-comparison tests, as indicated in the figure legends. A p-value < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance. Exact p-values are shown in the figure legends where applicable. All samples and animals were included in the analyses; no data were excluded.

Two decades of research on drug resistance in renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Yi Huang, Zhicheng Tang, He Xu, Renbo Wei, Min Cao, Tao Yang, Yulong Pan, Fan Wan, Jun Zhao, Yiwei Gu, Sijun Liu, Zhaohui He, Hui Zhuo, Jianqiu Kong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundDrug resistance in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) poses a major challenge to effective treatment. RCC drug resistance is primarily driven by genetic mutations, angiogenesis, immune suppression, metabolic reprogramming, and alternative signaling pathways, which contribute to treatment failure.ObjectiveThis study presents a bibliometric analysis of research on drug resistance in renal cell carcinoma (RCC) from 1999 to 2023, aiming to identify key contributors, research hotspots, and future directions.MethodsRelevant literature on renal cancer drug resistance published between 1999 and 2023 was sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC) database. Visual analysis was performed using VOSviewer and CiteSpace software, focusing on contributions from countries, institutions, journals, authors and keywords (such as immune checkpoint inhibitors, ICIs).ResultsA significant increase in research on renal cancer drug resistance has been observed in the past 20 years, based on an analysis of 4130 publications. Harvard University leads in publication output, and Motzer RJ is among the most frequently co-cited authors. The primary research topics include cancer-related clinical trials, drug efficacy assessments, molecular characteristics of tumors, immune microenvironment, and personalized treatment strategies More recent work has focused on understanding the molecular mechanisms behind drug resistance, including the dynamic tumor microenvironment (TME) and metabolic alterations, as well as the role of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs).ConclusionThis bibliometric analysis outlines key trends and research advancements in renal cancer drug resistance over the past two decades. Although there has been substantial progress in comprehensive resistance mechanisms, further research is imperative to elucidate the molecular crosstalk within the TME and therapeutic potential of ICIs in combination with targeted therapies.

Introduction

Kidney carcinoma is the sixth most frequent cancer globally, with around 350,000 new cases each year and a consistently rising incidence rate. From an epidemiological perspective, the observed rising incidence may be partly explained by the recent delineation of novel favorable subsets of cancers of unknown primary (CUP), particularly a renal cell carcinoma (RCC)-like CUP subtype, which is typically managed according to RCC treatment paradigms [1]. Over 70% of cases are diagnosed as clear cell renal cell carcinoma (ccRCC), originating from proximal renal tubular cells. Notably, about 30% of these cases progress to metastatic ccRCC (mccRCC), which is associated with a poorer prognosis [2, 3]. For early-stage localized renal cancer (T1-2), surgical removal remains the recommended treatment, while for metastatic renal cancer, dual immunotherapy (ICI-ICI) or a combination of immunotherapy and anti-angiogenic tyrosine kinase inhibitors (ICI-TKI) are the primary first-line treatments [4].

Owing to the significant heterogeneity of kidney cancer, systemic treatments have evolved from ineffective conventional radiotherapy and chemotherapy to high-dose cytokine regimens. Recent advancements have led to extensively adapted targeted therapy plans, emphasizing the efficacy of anti-angiogenic drugs targeting the VEGF pathway and immune checkpoint inhibitors, significantly improving treatment effectiveness and applicability [5]. Over the past two decades, interferon-alpha (IFN) and interleukin-2 (IL-2) were mainstays for patients with metastatic RCC (mRCC). However, their low efficacy rates (~ 15%) limited their broader patient benefit [6]. Targeted therapies have emerged as a promising research area, particularly in highly vascularized tumors like ccRCC, where anti-angiogenic strategies have been a focal point of study. VEGF-targeted therapies, including multitarget tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) such as sunitinib (Sutent) and pazopanib (Votrient), represent a significant advance, with both drugs approved for first-line treatment of metastatic RCC [7]. Notably, TKI monotherapy remains an appropriate first-line treatment option for a substantial proportion of patients who are not suitable for immunotherapy. In this context, the recent STAR trial suggested that although temporary treatment cessation (treatment breaks) may be considered in selected patients, such an approach should be undertaken with caution because it may be associated with shorter progression-free survival than continuous first-line TKI therapy[8].

The Akt/mTOR pathway has been identified as another promising target in mRCC. This finding facilitated the regulatory approval of mTOR inhibitors, such as everolimus and temsirolimus, in the treatment of advanced RCC. [9]. Furthermore, emerging evidence suggests that down-regulation of lactotransferrin, an important protein involved in innate immunity, may not only promote metastasis in RCC but also render tumor cells more responsive to mTOR inhibitors, highlighting its potential as a predictive biomarker of therapeutic response [10]. Meanwhile, newer therapies, including immune checkpoint inhibitors, have been increasingly introduced into clinical practice. Nevertheless, despite these therapeutic advances, many patients still experience poor outcomes due to drug resistance, recurrence, and metastasis.

The dilemma of systemic therapy resistance in RCC stems from various factors, including genetic mutations, angiogenic switches, alternative pathway activation, ATP-binding cassette (ABC) efflux transporters, and alterations in the tumor immune microenvironment [11]. Hence, comprehending drug resistance mechanisms is essential for refining treatment strategies and bettering the prognosis for kidney cancer patients. Epigenetic variations play a significant role in shaping resistance and prognosis among kidney cancer patients. The inactivation of Von Hippel-Lindau (VHL) is the most prevalent genetic mutation in RCC, potentially activating mTOR, which induces HIF upregulation and triggers additional angiogenesis-related signaling pathways [12]. Targeting this axis has led to important therapeutic advances. Notably, belzutifan, a potent small-molecule inhibitor of HIF-2α that blocks its heterodimerization with HIF-1β, has demonstrated significant clinical benefit over everolimus in patients with advanced clear cell RCC previously treated with immune checkpoint and anti-angiogenic therapies [13]. Consequently, the mutation expression profiles of mTOR pathway genes such as TSC1 and TSC2 may be potential targets for enhancing the response of RCC to treatments [14]. Sunitinib, a frequently used first-line medication, significantly betters the prognosis of RCC patients, though treatment resistance eventually diminishes its effectiveness [15]. Research indicates that circPTPN12 activates the hnRNPM-mediated IL-6/STAT3 pathway, fostering RCC progression and resistance to sunitinib [15]. Exosomes from sunitinib-resistant cells containing embedded lncARSR can endow drug tolerance to receptor kidney cancer, and lncARSR can also endow sunitinib tolerance to endothelial cell [16]. Thus, lncARSR can contribute to be a biomarker and overcome sunitinib tolerance, to enhance treatment outcomes [16]. These insights underscore the urgent need to continue researching the biological basis of treatment resistance in kidney cancer, which bibliometric investigation is designed to elucidate.

Bibliometrics, a burgeoning field, has recently thrived, employing math and statistical techniques for quantitative literature evaluation [17]. This approach, enhanced by visualization methods like cluster analysis and mapping, enables a deeper comprehension of the main contributions of countries, institutions, authors, and journals. Utilizing visualization tools such as VOSviewer and CiteSpace, these methods expose the central research areas and emerging trends [18].

Our study employs VOSviewer and CiteSpace to conduct a thorough bibliometric visualization of publications on kidney cancer drug resistance from 1999 to 2023. By analyzing contemporary findings, highlighting leading research centers, and identifying trends, we seek to establish a framework to guide subsequent research in this pivotal domain.

Methods

Sources of data

The data utilized in this bibliometric study was sourced from the Web of Science Core Collection (WoSCC). We chose WoSCC as the main data source due to broad representativeness and high quality, particularly as its application in bibliometric analysis has been validated [19].

Search strategy

The search strategy employed the following terms: TS = (renal OR kidney) NEAR/1 (cancer* OR tumor* OR tumour* OR oncology OR neoplasm* OR carcinoma*)) AND TS = (resist* OR "drug resist*" OR "medicine resist*" OR "agent resist*" OR "immune resistance" OR "checkpoint inhibitor resistance" OR "targeted therapy resistance" OR "TKI resistance" OR "PD-1/PD-L1 resistance"), with a time span from January 1999 to December 2023. Initially, by searching the terms related to renal carcinoma drug tolerance in the WoSCC, we obtained 4,487 results. We excluded meeting abstracts, letters, editorial articles and non-English articles, intending to collect complete original research articles and reviews, ultimately including 4,130 documents for final analysis. This approach was implemented to guarantee the analytical focus remained on pivotal and substantive contributions within the domain. To guarantee the accuracy of the data and the consistency of the research, the data was downloaded and analyzed by two researchers on September 5, 2024. The raw data retrieved from WOSCC were indeed subjected to deduplication and preprocessing in CiteSpace prior to analysis.

Data analysis

Annual publication trends were quantified using Microsoft Office Excel 2019 (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA). Additionally, the H-index serves as a composite measure of a scholar’s research productivity and impact, with elevated values denoting greater influence within the field [20]. Co-occurrence refers to the appearance of two or more keywords or terms within the same document. Co-citation refers to the frequency with which two documents are cited together by subsequent papers. Burst strength refers to describe the intensity of a sudden increase in citations or keyword usage over a defined time period.

Analysis tools and techniques

The construction of network visualizations and analysis of dataset relationships were performed by two bibliometric mapping tools: CiteSpace (version 6.2.R4) and VOSviewer (version 1.6.19). CiteSpace was used to construct visualization analysis, with a primary focus on institutional affiliations, journal distributions, and prominent citation bursts. Simultaneously, the visualization of collaborative and co-occurrence networks for countries, authors, journals, and keywords was conducted by VOSviewer.

Discussion

General information

The growing scientific output on kidney cancer drug resistance between 1999 and 2023 points to a consistently expanding research front dedicated to this critical problem. This trend reflects increased awareness of its complexity and the need for early detection and novel therapies. The USA leads in research output, with the highest H-index, average citation rate, and centrality scores. Harvard Medical School alone reaches 0.3, highlighting its pivotal role. France and China also rank among the top contributors, with China second in total citations (28,661). Japan, Italy, and Germany have each published over 300 articles, reflecting broader international engagement. Among the top 10 journals, five are from the USA, followed by Switzerland, the UK and Greece. Most collaborations occur within institutions or countries, though growing international cooperation holds promise for broader insights. Notable researchers such as Rini, Brian L, Escudier, Bernard and Motzer R.J have significantly advanced understanding and fostered global collaboration. Keyword analysis revealed that tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) and immunotherapies, such as PD-L1 inhibitors, remain the main therapeutic strategies for renal cancer. Accordingly, the mechanisms underlying resistance to these therapies have attracted increasing attention. Elucidating the underlying resistance mechanisms represents a crucial step toward improving patient prognosis and developing more effective treatment strategies.

Overview of treatment methods

A large proportion of renal cancer patients eventually develop resistance and disease progression. VEGFR inhibitor resistance may be linked to elevated MET expression, and current standard therapy with the mTOR inhibitor everolimus offers limited benefits. Cabozantinib has been found to outperform everolimus in extending progression-free survival in renal cell carcinoma patients after progression on VEGFR-targeted therapies [26]. Some scholars have noted that in refractory solid tumors, particularly in melanoma, NSCLC, RCC. A significant correlation was observed between the expression of PD-L1 and that of PD-1 on both infiltrating immune cells and lymphatic cells. PD-L1 is a key marker of an immune-active microenvironment and is the most reliable predictor of response to PD-1 blockade [27]. Recent studies suggest that in PD-L1+ populations, the combination of atezolizumab ± bevacizumab with sunitinib may exert anti-tumor effects through three signaling axes: tumor angiogenesis, innate immunity and immunosuppressive myeloid inflammation, uncovering potential biological mechanisms of anti-VEGF and immunotherapy resistance [28].

A weak correlation was observed between article citation counts and the impact factors of their host journals. This finding contradicts the conventional view that journal impact factor guarantees high citation rates, aligning instead with a growing body of recent analytical evidence [29]. Additionally, Open-access journals predominate among the top 10 ranked publications, while non-open-access articles show lower citation counts and TLS. The enhanced accessibility offered by open-access publications may catalyze advances in renal cancer resistance research by ensuring key findings reach a wider audience.

Through the investigation into highly cited papers and key topics, we can roughly understand the main research themes in this area. Four of the ten most-cited articles elucidate recent clinical advances and mechanistic insights into PD-1 based immunotherapy for solid tumors [30–32]. Two articles describe the efficacy comparison between newly developed multi-kinase inhibitors targeting angiogenesis for renal cancer and other treatment regimens [33, 34]. Four articles focus on cancer treatment with angiogenesis at the core, exploring biological mechanisms such as hypoxia induction and tumor starvation, and attempting to summarize strategies to improve treatment outcomes [35–38]. Since the most-cited articles predominantly explore broad cancer mechanisms with limited focus on renal cancer, we will prioritize other high-impact studies for a specialized investigation of renal resistance pathways.

Overview of resistance mechanisms

The complexity and diversity of drug resistance mechanisms in renal carcinoma present an enduring scientific challenge, with many key aspects still awaiting clarification. Based on the timing of therapeutic response, renal cancer drug tolerance could be categorized as intrinsic (primary) or acquired (secondary) resistance. Intrinsic resistance refers to the inherent ineffectiveness of treatment drugs, which may result from congenital genetic resistance, where resistant tumor clones are already present before treatment [39]. Secondary tolerance is defined as a phenomenon marked by an initial tumor regression post-treatment, followed by tumor growth during maintenance therapy, possibly due to new resistant mutations in tumor cells [39]. Although the exact mechanisms require further clarification, previous researchers have identified several possible biological pathways responsible for intrinsic and acquired resistance in renal cancer cells [40, 41]. Lysosomal sequestration, where tumors sequester anticancer drugs in lysosomes and expel them through exocytosis, altering the intracellular distribution and accumulation of the drug, has been identified as a fundamental mechanism in acquired resistance to renal cancer drugs such as sunitinib, sorafenib, and pazopanib [42–44]. Mutations and modifications in expression levels are key mechanisms for most tumor resistance and also play a critical role in renal cancer resistance. Notably, VHL gene inactivation, reported in over 90% of clear cell renal carcinoma patients, is closely associated with mTOR activation and subsequent angiogenesis pathways [45]. The angiogenic switch is another key research focus, tightly linked to the resistance seen with various drugs like sunitinib, sorafenib, and axitinib [22, 46]. With the concept of tumor ecology gaining acceptance, more researchers now view tumors as an ecosystem interacting with surrounding tissues, rather than as isolated structures. The tumor microenvironment, consisting of tumor cells, the extracellular matrix, signaling molecules, and stromal cells, has also become an increasing focus in research on renal cancer drug resistance. Importantly, RCC is recognized as one of the most highly immune-infiltrated tumors. Emerging evidence suggests that characteristics of the tumor microenvironment (TME) profoundly influence disease biology and modulate responses to systemic therapies. In addition, activation of specific metabolic pathways may regulate angiogenesis and inflammatory signaling within the TME, creating a permissive microenvironment for tumor progression and therapeutic resistance [47–49]. Within this ecosystem, exosomes have emerged not only as mediators of resistance but also as a novel source of non-invasive tumor biomarkers. The unique bilayer membrane structure of exosomes offers protection against external RNases and proteases, leading to enhanced stability of the enclosed mRNAs, miRNAs, and functional proteins. Consequently, tumor-derived exosomal cargo in the serum and urine of patients offers highly sensitive targets for the early detection and monitoring of RCC [50]. Studies suggest that exosomes may mediate the transfer of drug-resistant renal cancer cells, including lncARSR and specific regions of the human mutL homolog 1 (MLH1) gene, transmitting resistance information, which leads to the acquisition of drug resistance in previously non-resistant tumor cells [40, 41].

Furthermore, a more comprehensive understanding of therapeutic resistance in RCC requires recognition of its profound metabolic reprogramming. RCC has increasingly been characterized as a metabolically reprogrammed malignancy, with abnormal partitioning of glycolytic flux, impaired mitochondrial bioenergetics and oxidative phosphorylation (OxPhos), and dysregulated lipid metabolism [51, 52]. These metabolic alterations are closely associated with drug resistance and may also influence the biological properties of renal cancer stem cells [53, 54] In addition, activation of specific metabolic pathways may modulate angiogenesis and inflammatory signaling within the TME, further contributing to therapeutic resistance [55].

In-depth studies of the biological mechanisms of renal cancer resistance can reveal a multifaceted picture. RCC resistance mechanisms range from traditional pathways such as lysosomal sequestration and gene mutations to more integrated areas like angiogenesis and the tumor microenvironment. These developments offer insights into potential diagnostic and therapeutic biomarkers.

Resistance mechanisms and strategies for anti-angiogenesis tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs)

TKIs are promising targeted drugs used to treat highly vascularized renal cell carcinoma (RCC), and since their introduction, they have played a dominant role in RCC treatment. Common TKI drugs such as sunitinib, sorafenib, and cabozantinib have demonstrated significant anticancer properties in renal cancer experiments and clinical trials. However, as treatment progresses, resistance gradually develops, resulting in decreased tumor sensitivity to TKIs [26, 42]. The research conducted animal experiments to investigate cross-resistance to clinically used sunitinib, sorafenib, and pazopanib, demonstrating that lysosomal sequestration is likely invovled. Sorafenib showed stronger resistance compared to other TKIs, suggesting its use in combination or sequential therapies to improve resistance outcomes [42]. Other research show that hypoxia promotes sorafenib tolerance in pRCC and result in map2k1enhanced expression, while axitinib-tolerance under normoxia upregulate eif4b [46].

To combat this resistance, researchers have proposed various strategies. First, new therapeutic targets have been proposed: some studies identified AIM2 as a novel biomarker for RCC, which may promote FOXO3a phosphorylation and proteasomal degradation, and regulate ferroptosis through the ACSL4 axis, promoting RCC progression and resistance to sunitinib [56]. Recent literature has identified the circPTPN12/hnRNPM/IL-6/STAT3 axis as promoting RCC development and sunitinib tolerance, making it a potential pharmacological target [15]. Research has shown that inhibiting fumarate at the C211 site and succinylation of PTEN may restore sensitivity of PRCC2 to sunitinib treatment [57]. Secondly, several research groups are exploring the combination of VEGF inhibitors with immunotherapy. For example, combining anti-VEGF drugs with PD-1/PD-L1 blockers can boost the tumor immunological response, potentially reducing or reversing resistance. The nivolumab-cabozantinib combination regimen is highly likely to be the preferred treatment option to improve overall survival (OS) in RCC patients [58, 59]. Furthermore, some researchers have been exploring the simultaneous targeting of multiple pathways, such as FGFR and c-MET, to block the alternative survival pathways of tumors and overcome resistance [60, 61].

Resistance mechanisms and strategies for mTOR Inhibitors

The mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) complex pathway plays a crucial role in RCC, regulating cell growth, proliferation and metabolism. Phase III clinical trials have shown that mTORC1 inhibitors, such as everolimus, exhibit good efficacy in inhibiting tumor growth, making them as a promising option in the treatment of RCC [62]. However, studies have shown that after mTORC1 inhibition, a feedback activation mechanism occurs, leading to the upregulation of mTORC2, which may promote tumor cell survival and growth, becoming a source of resistance. The inhibition of Development and DNA Damage Response 1 (REDD1) regulatory protein is one key mechanism involved [63]. Recent research found that The S-palmitoylation of AGK is mediated by ZDHHC2, promoting plasma membrane localization of AGK, which activates the PI3K-AKT-mTOR signaling axis in ccRCC, thereby regulating sunitinib sensitivity [64].

To overcome resistance caused by feedback activation and other mechanisms, researchers have proposed several countermeasures. First, combining drugs that target both the PI3K and mTOR pathways can reduce the impact of mTORC2 feedback activation, enhancing therapeutic efficacy. Secondly, dual inhibition strategies targeting both mTORC1 and mTORC2, such as rapamycin analogs, are also being explored to block these two parallels signaling pathways [65]. Additionally, some studies have targeted downstream signaling pathways to block the activity of other oncogenes, aiming to reduce the development of resistance [66].

Resistance mechanisms and strategies for immune checkpoint inhibitors

Immune checkpoint inhibitors are a therapeutic approach that enhances antitumor immune responses by lifting immune suppression, and they have demonstrated broad potential for patients with various cancers. ICIs are flourishing in the field of renal cell carcinoma (RCC), with their safety and feasibility well-established. Drugs like cetuximab and nivolumab, which are PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors, have become integral components of RCC treatment [67]. However, despite the promising potential of immunotherapy, resistance remains one of the main challenges. Researchers explored the genomic response to ICIs in renal cancer patients and found that the loss of PBRM1 in ccRCC may modify the expression profile in tumor cells, affecting changes in the JAK-STAT, hypoxia, and immune signaling pathways, thereby influencing the response to immune checkpoint therapy [68]. A multi-regional tumor sampling and multi-omics analysis of advanced clear cell renal carcinoma (ccRCC) patients assessed tumor genetic characteristics, immune microenvironment, and T-cell dynamics, revealing no significant correlation between tumor mutation burden (TMB) or key gene mutations (e.g., VHL, PBRM1, SETD2) and anti-PD-1 efficacy [69]. However, patients with high T-cell receptor (TCR) clonality before treatment were more likely to respond and responders' CD8 + T-cells maintained pre-expanded TCR clones. In contrast, non-responders showed TCR clones that were more easily replaced, indicating insufficient immune activation. Additionally, high levels of human endogenous retrovirus (HERV) expression were associated with resistance [69]. Immunosuppressive cells in the tumor-associated ecosystem, such as regulatory T-cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells (MDSCs), weaken the immune system's ability to attack tumors by inhibiting the function of effector T-cells, thereby contributing to ICI resistance [70]. This immunosuppressive state may be further exacerbated by metabolic reprogramming, as nutrient depletion and the accumulation of acidic metabolites, such as lactate, within the TME can impair the proliferation and antitumor activity of infiltrating effector T cells, thereby contributing to resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors. The genetic characteristics of tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes are also linked to the response of renal cancer to ICIs [24]. Additionally, renal cancer cells evade immune system recognition and clearance through immune checkpoint escape mechanisms, further driving the development of resistance [70].

To overcome these resistances, combining immunotherapy with other targeted therapies may be an effective strategy to combat resistance. Researchers have proposed combining ICIs with anti-angiogenic agents to reactivate T-cell-mediated immune responses and restore the immune system’s tumor-killing abilities. In first-line treatment for metastatic ccRCC, four different TKI-ICI combinations (axitinib-pembrolizumab, axitinib-avelumab, cabozantinib-nivolumab, and lenvatinib-pembrolizumab) have been compared with sunitinib, with clinical results showing improvements in ORR, PFS and OS [71–73]. Additionally, personalized immunotherapy is an important direction, where patient selection based on biomarkers can improve ICI efficacy and reduce unnecessary side effects [73].

The above studies not only elucidated possible resistance mechanisms of key drugs but also explored potential strategies and directions for improvement, providing a reference for personalized targeted therapies for resistant patients. In-depth comprehensive of the biological characteristics and mechanisms of renal cancer resistance facilitate the identification of prognostic indicator and the development of personalized cancer therapies for renal cancer. From our journal topic clustering analysis, it is evident that research is becoming more centered on genetic drivers and molecular mechanisms. Investigations into molecular mechanisms are poised to become a central focus and cutting edge in the research landscape.

Potential subsequent research avenue

The development and evaluation of novel predictive biomarkers for renal cancer is another research direction. Scholars suggest that to improve resistance in RCC patients, future predictive characteristics composed of multiple existing biomarkers should be developed, with additional prospective studies to determine optimal measurement conditions and confirm the value of these predictive characteristics [74]. Furthermore, researchers highlight that classifying tumor types based on histological and molecular features, combined with treatment response biomarkers, to group RCC patients into subsets most likely to benefit from treatment will be a crucial direction for the future development of personalized renal cancer therapies [75]. The development and translation of new drugs: Recently, many new drugs for RCC have been approved, including several that inhibit vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and its receptor (VEGFR), as well as the mammalian target of rapamycin (mTOR) to inhibit tumor angiogenesis. Additionally, a new class of immunotherapeutic drugs, immune checkpoint inhibitors such as nivolumab and other PD-1 pathway blockers, are emerging and will play a key role in the treatment of RCC patients [76].

Limitation

We acknowledge that the exclusion of non-English literature may have omitted significant scholarly contributions from certain regions. Furthermore, the delayed updates in Web of Science keywords and the inherent complexities of citation data collection mean that recent developments, particularly those focusing on novel mitochondrial-targeted therapies, may not be fully represented in our bibliometric analysis. While widely used, the tools employed in this study, VOSviewer and CiteSpace, may introduce bias, as disparate bibliometric tools often obtain varying results. Meanwhile, publication bias is a recognized factor that may affect the thematic landscape and trends discerned in our study. Finally, bibliometric analysis inherently relies on accumulated citation counts, which introduces a 'citation lag.' Consequently, while foundational resistance mechanisms (such as VHL mutations or classical TKI efflux) dominate historical citation networks, rapidly emerging frontier concepts—such as the profound impact of metabolic reprogramming, spatial transcriptomics, and evolving tumor microenvironment dynamics—may be underrepresented in current keyword clusters despite their critical importance in contemporary RCC research.

Conclusion

Our analysis underscores the significant advancements in renal cancer resistance research from 1999 to 2023. The substantial contributions from the United States, China, and France highlight the value of international collaboration in combining global knowledge and resources. Leading institutions such as Harvard Medical School demonstrate the critical role of specialized research centers in driving innovation in this field. The findings suggest that molecular therapies and personalized medicine hold significant potential in combating renal cancer resistance, though substantial clinical hurdles remain.

Further exploration of resistance mechanisms, integration of multimodal treatment strategies, and the identification of new biomarkers are vital pathways for enhancing treatment outcomes. These results emphasize the importance of international collaboration and personalized therapies in improving renal cancer patient care and outcomes.

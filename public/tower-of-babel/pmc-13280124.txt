Diagnostic test accuracy meta-analysis of transcranial motor evoked potential monitoring for the prediction of postoperative neurological deficits in ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament surgery
Authors: L. Velur Prasuna, Velur Vibha, Gong Marvin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo quantify the diagnostic accuracy of transcranial motor evoked potential (TcMEP) monitoring for predicting postoperative neurological deficits in adults undergoing decompressive surgery exclusively for ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL), and to characterise heterogeneity sources.MethodsPubMed, Embase, and Cochrane were searched through January 2026. Studies were eligible if they reported extractable 2 × 2 contingency data for TcMEP monitoring in OPLL-exclusive cohorts (≥20 patients); quality was assessed with QUADAS-2 (Quality Assessment of Diagnostic Accuracy Studies). Sensitivity and specificity were pooled using random-effects models; a strong threshold effect (ρ = 0.91) precluded valid summary receiver operating characteristic (sROC) estimation. Publication bias was assessed by funnel plot regression.ResultsFive studies (990 patients; deficit rate 6.9%) met eligibility. Pooled sensitivity was 54.8% (95% confidence interval [CI]: 28.8–78.5%) and specificity 90.0% (95% CI: 78.9–95.6%). Positive likelihood ratio (LR+) was 5.47; negative LR− 0.50; diagnostic odds ratio (DOR) 13.75 (95% CI: 7.11–26.59; I2 = 0%). Heterogeneity was moderate for sensitivity (I2 = 61.8%) and substantial for specificity (I2 = 90.6%). No significant publication bias was detected (p = 0.846).ConclusionsTcMEP monitoring in OPLL decompression yields pooled sensitivity 54.8%, specificity 90.0%, DOR 13.75 (I2 = 0%), and LR+ 5.47. Level-specific alarm thresholds, multimodal monitoring, and standardised reporting are prerequisites for reliable clinical implementation.SignificanceThis is the first meta-analysis of TcMEP diagnostic accuracy in OPLL-exclusive surgical populations. The consistent DOR (I2 = 0%) supports diagnostic utility, but independence assumption violations and strong threshold effects require caution in clinical translation.

Introduction

Ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) causes progressive cervical or thoracic myelopathy, affecting 1.9–4.3% of adults in Japan (Matsunaga et al., 2004; Fargen et al., 2012); without surgical intervention, approximately two-thirds of affected individuals deteriorate neurologically within a decade (Matsunaga et al., 2004). Surgical decompression carries substantially higher neurological injury risk than other degenerative cervical conditions. Transcranial motor evoked potentials (TcMEPs) detect spinal cord ischaemia in real time (Kobayashi et al., 2014), yet OPLL poses unique neurophysiological challenges: dural adhesions create abrupt decompression events (Yoshida et al., 2019), thoracic OPLL carries deficit rates up to 39% (Yoshida et al., 2022), and optimal alarm thresholds differ between spinal levels (Kim et al., 2024; Kim et al., 2021). TcMEP diagnostic accuracy had never been meta-analytically quantified in OPLL-exclusive populations; we therefore conducted this systematic review and diagnostic accuracy meta-analysis.

Methods

Literature search

PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Central Register were searched on 20 January 2026 without date restrictions. Search terms combined MeSH headings and free-text keywords for OPLL, TcMEP/intraoperative neuromonitoring, and postoperative neurological outcomes. Only English-language peer-reviewed publications were eligible; reference lists were hand-searched. A PRISMA 2020 flow diagram is provided as Fig. 5. The search yielded 147 records; after removal of 37 duplicates, 110 unique records proceeded to screening.

Eligibility criteria

Studies were eligible if they enrolled adults (≥18 years) undergoing elective cervical or thoracic decompressive surgery exclusively for OPLL, applied continuous intraoperative TcMEP monitoring, used clinician-assessed postoperative neurological deficit as reference standard, and reported or permitted reconstruction of a 2 × 2 contingency table from ≥20 patients. Studies mixing OPLL with other diagnoses without disaggregated data, using somatosensory evoked potentials or electromyography alone, or consisting of case reports, letters, or abstracts were excluded. Of 41 full-text articles reviewed, 36 were excluded, leaving five studies for quantitative synthesis.

Data extraction and quality assessment

Two reviewers independently extracted study design, patient count, anatomical level, TcMEP alarm criterion, outcome definition, and 2 × 2 contingency cell counts; when not directly reported, contingency data were reconstructed from summary statistics. Methodological quality was assessed using QUADAS-2 across four domains: patient selection, index test, reference standard, and flow and timing.

Statistical analysis

Sensitivity and specificity were pooled using univariate random-effects models on the logit scale; heterogeneity was quantified using τ2, τ, and I2. Diagnostic odds ratios were synthesised under common-effect and random-effects (DerSimonian–Laird) models. The bivariate random-effects model of Reitsma et al. (2005) was applied to jointly estimate pooled sensitivity and specificity; due to the strong threshold effect identified (ρ = 0.91), the sROC AUC was not validly estimable and likelihood ratios were derived from the univariate pooled estimates. Publication bias was assessed using Deeks funnel plot regression (Deeks et al., 2005). All analyses were conducted in R (version 4.3) using the ‘mada’ and ‘meta’ packages.

Results

Five studies met eligibility criteria, encompassing 990 patients (68 with postoperative deficit; 922 without; pooled deficit rate 6.9%). QUADAS-2 assessment rated all studies at low-to-moderate risk; principal concerns were patient selection bias (three retrospective designs) and unclear blinding of TcMEP interpreters in two studies. The consolidated 2 × 2 dataset yielded 36 true positives, 70 false positives, 32 false negatives, and 852 true negatives. Diagnostic accuracy

Univariate diagnostic accuracy Meta-analysis

Univariate random-effects pooling yielded sensitivity of 54.8% (95% CI: 28.8–78.5%) and specificity of 90.0% (95% CI: 78.9–95.6%). Heterogeneity was moderate for sensitivity (I2 = 61.8%) and substantial for specificity (I2 = 90.6%; Table 3). Between-study variance was similar for both parameters (τ2 = 0.883 for sensitivity; τ2 = 0.891 for specificity). Publication bias

Descriptive stratified analyses

Formal meta-regression was not feasible with k = 5 studies. Descriptive stratification was performed as pre-specified sensitivity analyses.

Alarm threshold category: The single study using a ≤ 50% threshold (Yoo et al., 2022) yielded DOR = 10.66 (95% CI: 4.21–26.98), sensitivity 35.3%, specificity 95.1%. The four studies using ≥70% thresholds yielded a pooled DOR = 17.82 (95% CI: 6.98–45.52; I2 = 0%), with sensitivity ranging from 20.0% to 96.4% and specificity from 45.2% to 94.9%. These point estimates do not differ in the direction hypothesised (lower threshold = higher sensitivity), though single-study strata preclude inferential comparisons.

Spinal level: The cervical-only study (Kim et al., 2021) yielded DOR = 3.96 (0.39–40.37), sensitivity 20.0%, specificity 94.1%. The thoracic-only study (Ando et al., 2019) yielded DOR = 22.30 (1.17–426.41), sensitivity 96.4%, specificity 45.2%. The three mixed cervical–thoracic studies yielded pooled DOR = 15.01 (95% CI: 7.40–30.46; I2 = 0%), sensitivity 35.3–71.4%, specificity 91.0–95.1%. The strikingly divergent operating points of the cervical-only (low sensitivity, high specificity) and thoracic-only (high sensitivity, low specificity) studies are consistent with the level-specific threshold hypothesis, but single-study strata do not permit causal inference.

Sensitivity analysis excluding Kim et al. (2024) (limb-level data): Pooled DOR = 11.94 (95% CI: 5.68–25.09; k = 4; I2 = 0%), confirming robustness of the main estimate to exclusion of the unit-of-analysis outlier.

Discussion

This is, to our knowledge, the first meta-analysis quantifying TcMEP diagnostic accuracy exclusively in OPLL surgical populations. Across 990 patients, pooled sensitivity was 54.8% and specificity 90.0%, yielding a positive likelihood ratio of 5.47 and a negative likelihood ratio of 0.50. At the observed pre-test probability of 6.9% (68/990), a positive intraoperative alert raises post-test probability to approximately 28.8%; a negative result reduces it to approximately 3.6% (Jaeschke et al., 1994).

The diagnostic significance of using OPLL-exclusive cohorts is underscored by the data of Funaba et al. (2022), in which sensitivity was 71.4% in OPLL versus 42.9% in cervical spondylotic myelopathy — a near-twofold difference demonstrating why pooling OPLL with broader myelopathy cohorts systematically underestimates disease-specific TcMEP performance.

Heterogeneity was the dominant analytical challenge. Three structural sources underlie this: alarm thresholds varied nearly twofold (50–80% amplitude reduction); the cervical–thoracic distribution differed markedly across studies; and follow-up ranged from immediate assessment to one year. The strong sensitivity–FPR correlation (ρ = 0.91) confirms that studies operated at substantially different alarm thresholds and anatomical positions, constituting a genuine threshold effect rather than mere random variability.

Threshold heterogeneity has direct clinical implications for neurophysiology practice. Kim et al. (2024) showed that the optimal TcMEP cutoff was 93% amplitude reduction for cervical OPLL versus 50% for thoracic OPLL. Applying a uniform 70% threshold (Kobayashi et al., 2014) across both spinal levels miscalibrates the test — generating excess false positives in cervical disease and potentially missing injury in thoracic disease. Critically, 57.9% of TcMEP alerts in the Funaba et al. (2022) multicentre dataset were reversed through intraoperative intervention (Liu et al., 2025), demonstrating TcMEP's actionability beyond what standard diagnostic metrics capture.

Not all deficits are neurophysiologically preventable. White cord syndrome — a reperfusion injury following sudden cord decompression — can manifest hours postoperatively without any intraoperative TcMEP warning (Bagherzadeh et al., 2025) and likely contributes to the high deficit rates observed in thoracic OPLL series (Ando et al., 2019). Severely impaired preoperative motor function further limits TcMEP dynamic range (Kobayashi et al., 2021), establishing a biologically irreducible false-negative floor. Multimodal monitoring (TcMEP combined with electromyography) substantially improves performance: Kim et al. (2021) reported combined sensitivity of 84.2% versus 20% with TcMEP alone, with electromyography detecting nerve root compromise that TcMEP alone misses.

Several limitations warrant acknowledgement. Five studies represent a small evidentiary base; Yoo et al. (2022), contributing 50.5% of common-effect weight, exerts disproportionate influence. Three studies were retrospective, thresholds varied nearly twofold (50–80%), follow-up ranged from immediate assessment to one year, and all included studies originated from East Asian centres, limiting generalisability. Contingency data required reconstruction in several studies. A Delphi consensus process among expert neurophysiologists and spine surgeons to standardise alarm criteria and outcome reporting may be a more tractable near-term pathway to clinical guidance than awaiting a larger primary literature. This systematic review was not prospectively registered with PROSPERO; retrospective registration was not completed prior to submission, which represents a limitation with respect to PRISMA 2020 reporting standards.

For neurophysiology practice, TcMEP monitoring in OPLL decompression provides a positive likelihood ratio of 5.47 and pooled DOR of 13.75, representing moderate rule-in performance consistent with the evidence base. Optimal clinical utility requires: (1) level-specific alarm thresholds (cervical versus thoracic OPLL), (2) multimodal protocols combining TcMEP with electromyography, and (3) transparent contingency-level reporting in prospective studies to enable future meta-analytic synthesis.

Conclusions

TcMEP monitoring in OPLL decompression demonstrates pooled sensitivity of 54.8% (95% CI: 28.8–78.5%) and specificity of 90.0% (95% CI: 78.9–95.6%), with a pooled DOR of 13.75 (95% CI: 7.11–26.59; I2 = 0%) and LR+ of 5.47. A strong threshold effect (ρ = 0.91) across studies precluded reliable sROC/bivariate estimation. Substantial heterogeneity is driven by alarm threshold variation and spinal level distribution. Standardised level-stratified alarm criteria, prospective OPLL-specific studies, and uniform contingency reporting are prerequisites for precise, actionable diagnostic estimates in clinical neurophysiology practice.

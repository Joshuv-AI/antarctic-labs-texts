Effects of childhood trauma on sustained attention in major depressive disorder: the mediating role of brain activity and functional connectivity
Authors: Mingfeng Lai, Ping Jiang, Peiwei Xu, Dan Luo, Wenxin Bao, Jing Li, Jiajun Xu
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSustained attention deficits were reported more significant in patients with major depressive disorder (MDD) than in healthy controls (HCs), and are pivotal in both the development and aggravation of depression. Childhood trauma is also common in MDD and the exposure to childhood trauma may impede sustained attention and increase the treatment resistance in MDD. However, the underlying neuro-mechanisms link the childhood trauma to sustained attention deficits in MDD remain unclear.MethodsWe collected resting-state functional magnetic resonance imaging data, and measured childhood trauma severity using the Childhood Trauma Questionnaire and sustained attention using the Continuous Performance Test, Identical Pairs version. After excluding subjects with significant head movement, 45 MDDs and 54 HCs were included in the analysis. We compared fractional amplitude of low-frequency fluctuation (fALFF) between the groups, conducted whole-brain correlation analysis between the fALFF and sustained attention in the MDD group, and defined significant regions as the regions of interest for the seed-to-whole brain functional connectivity (FC) analysis. We further performed mediation analyses to investigate the relationships among the childhood trauma, fALFF and FC values, and the level of sustained attention in the MDD group.ResultsCompared with HCs, MDDs exhibited higher fALFF in the right middle frontal gyrus and left inferior frontal gyrus, and lower fALFF in the bilateral insular cortex, left medial orbital superior frontal gyrus and left angular gyrus (ANG.L). Whole-brain correlation analysis showed that impaired sustained attention was associated with increased fALFF in the left postcentral gyrus (PoCG.L), and FC of PoCG.L-left precentral gyrus (PreCG.L) and ANG.L-right superior temporal gyrus (STG.R) in the MDD group. Furthermore, mediation analyses showed that the fALFF in PoCG.L, and FC of PoCG.L-PreCG.L and ANG.L-STG.R mediated the relationship between the childhood trauma and sustained attention in the MDD group.ConclusionThe fALFF in PoCG.L, and FC of PoCG.L-PreCG.L and ANG.L-STG.R might be potential neural substrate in the association between the childhood trauma and poor sustained attention in the MDDs, and might serve as potential intervention targets for the treatment of sustained attention deficits in MDDs with childhood trauma history.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12888-024-06385-9.Keywords: Childhood trauma, fALFF, Functional connectivity, Major depressive disorder, Mediation analysis, Sustained attention

Background

Attention deficit is a core characteristic of major depressive disorder (MDD). Among attentional subdomains, sustained attention has been found to be the most affected in individuals with MDD [1], with impairments more significant in MDD patients than in healthy individuals [2, 3]. As the foundation of complex attentional domains, sustained attention has a great impact on maintaining concentration in daily life activities [4, 5]. Cognitive deficits, especially in sustained attention, are pivotal in the development and aggravation of depression, which may be caused by its effects on the abnormal use of emotion regulation strategies in MDD [5]. However, attention problems could not be adequately alleviated by current first-line treatments [6].

On the other hand, childhood trauma, such as physical, sexual, or emotional abuse, and physical or emotional neglect, is also frequently reported in depression. It has been reported that approximately two thirds of MDD patients had experienced some form of childhood trauma [7]. Childhood is a crucial period for brain development, network sculpting and cognitive controls, such as attentional performance [8]. Specifically, exposure to childhood trauma experience impairs multiple dimensions of attention performance, especially sustained attention [9–11], and is associated with an increase in treatment resistance in depressed patients [12]. Nevertheless, the mechanisms by which childhood trauma impacts cognitive control, particularly sustained attention in patients with MDD, remain elusive. Unraveling the neuro-mechanisms that link childhood trauma to sustained attention may provide new insights into clinical interventions aimed at addressing attentional deficits in MDD.

Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) is widely used to explore brain activity and functional connectivity (FC) related to childhood trauma and sustained attention. Previous studies have reported that childhood trauma severity in patients with MDD was correlated with brain region activity and FC of networks related to emotion regulation, memories and social cognition. For example, patients who have experienced more severe childhood trauma showed lower brain activity in the inferior and middle frontal regions [13], and higher brain activity in the precentral (PreCG) and postcentral gyrus (PoCG) [14], as well as weaker FC between default mode (DMN) and dorsal attention networks [15], and stronger FC between amygdala and dorsolateral frontal areas [16]. On the other hand, MDD patients with sustained attention deficits also demonstrated altered brain activity or FC in regions within networks related to visual attention, attentional control and cognitive activity [17], such as ventral attention (VAN), executive attention network, and DMN [18]. Prior studies suggest that experiences of childhood trauma and deficits in sustained attention are associated with alterations in regional brain activity and FC. These alterations exhibit a spatial and functional correspondence, indicating a shared neural substrate between the two conditions. However, direct evidence of neuro-substrate that underlie the link between childhood trauma and sustained attention remains elusive.

Based on these backgrounds, this study aimed to detect the whole-brain intrinsic activity and connectivity differences between MDD patients and healthy adults using the fractional amplitude of low frequency fluctuation (fALFF) and seed-based FC to measure the local brain activity and FC alterations. The Childhood Trauma Questionnaire (CTQ) and the Continuous Performance Test, Identical Pairs version (CPT-IP) were used to quantify their childhood trauma experiences and sustained attention ability respectively. Further, we used correlation and mediation analysis to explore the neuro-mechanism underlying the relationships between childhood trauma and sustained attention, as previous studies did [19, 20]. We hypothesized that in patients with MDD (i) childhood trauma experiences significantly impact sustained attention deficits; (ii) alterations of brain activity and FC strength related to sustained attention may also be associated with their childhood trauma experiences; and (iii) these alterations may act as the neural link between childhood trauma experience and sustained attention deficits.

Methods

Participants

This study recruited 51 adults with MDD from the inpatient department and 55 healthy adults through advertisements at the Mental Health Center, West China Hospital of Sichuan University, China, between November 2021 and October 2022. The MDD diagnoses and mental disorder exclusion for healthy controls (HCs) were conducted by two experienced psychiatrists, according to the DSM-5 criteria. The inclusion criteria for all participants were as follows: (1) right-handedness; (2) age ranging from 18 to 60 years; (3) native Chinese speakers. Exclusion criteria for all participants included (1) history of any neurological disease; (2) history of substance abuse or dependence; (3) any Axis I psychiatric disorder except for MDD; (4) MRI contraindications, such as claustrophobia or metal implants; and (5) morphological anomalies on MRI. The study was conducted in accordance with the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration. All study procedures were approved by the Ethics Committee of West China Hospital of Sichuan University, and written informed consent was obtained from all participants.

Psychological measurement

All participants completed the CTQ and the CPT-IP. The CTQ is a 28-item self-report scale used to quantify childhood trauma history, with responses measured on a 5-point Likert scale ranging from one (never) to five (always), resulting in a total score ranging from 25 to 125. A higher total score indicates a more severe and harmful experience of trauma and abuse. The CTQ consists of five subscales, including physical abuse, emotional abuse, sexual abuse, physical neglect and emotional neglect. It has demonstrated good reliability and validity in the Chinese population (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.77) [21].

The CPT-IP is a neuropsychological test that measures sustained attention [22]. Each visual stimulus was presented for 950 ms at 50 ms intervals. The stimuli included three conditions: 2-digit, 3-digit, and 4-digit. Each condition consisted of 150 trials and lasted approximately 3 min. Participants were instructed to respond as quickly and accurately as possible when two identical stimuli appeared consecutively. In each condition, the d’ value is calculated by the ratio between the number of “hits” (responses to stimuli that matched the prior trial, e.g., “123” followed by “123”) and “false alarms” (responses to stimuli that differed only in one digit from the prior trial, e.g., “123” followed by “124”). The total score is the average of the d’ values in the three conditions, with a higher total score indicating better sustained attention [23].

The depression severity was assessed using the 17-item Hamilton Depression Rating Scale (HAMD-17) [24]. Each item is scored on a 5-point system from zero to four or a 3-point system from zero to two, resulting in a total score ranging from 0 to 52. A higher total score indicates a greater severity of depression. The Chinese version of the HAMD-17 has demonstrated good reliability and validity (Cronbach’s alpha = 0.714) [25].

MRI data acquisition

MRI images were acquired using a 3.0 T scanner (uMR 790; United Imaging Healthcare, Shanghai, China) with a 32-channel head coil. High-resolution three-dimensional T1-weighted images were obtained using a magnetization-prepared rapid acquisition gradient echo (MPRAGE) sequence with the following parameters: repetition time = 72,400 ms, echo time = 3.4 ms, flip angle = 8°, field of view = 256 × 240 mm2, matrix size = 320 × 300, slice thickness = 0.8 mm, and voxel size = 0.8 × 0.8 × 0.8 mm3. The resting-state functional images, including 230 image volumes over the 477 s examination, were obtained using a gradient echo echo-planar imaging (EPI) sequence with the following parameters: repetition time = 2000 ms, echo time = 30 ms, matrix size = 64 × 64, field of view = 192 × 192 mm2, flip angle = 90°, slice thickness = 3 mm, voxel size = 3 × 3 × 3 mm3, and number of slices = 46 with continuous scanning without interval. The participants were instructed to keep their eyes closed, remain awake, and avoid thinking during resting-state scans.

Data preprocessing and fALFF calculation

Data pre-processing was conducted using the Data Processing and Analysis for Brain Imaging Toolbox (DPABI, http://rfmri.org/DPABI) [26] in MATLAB (MathWorks). The preprocessing steps were as follows: (1) removal of the first 10 time points; (2) slice-timing correction and head motion correction; (3) registration of T1 images with resting-state functional images and spatial normalization to the standard Montreal Neurological Institute (MNI) space; (4) linear detrending; (5) linear regression to remove nuisance covariates, such as cerebrospinal fluid signals, white matter signals, and Friston-24 head motion parameters; and (6) spatial smoothing (6-mm full width at half maximum [FWHM] Gaussian kernel). 7 participants (6 MDDs and 1 HC) with significant head movement were excluded based on the following criteria: a mean framewise displacement (FD) greater than 0.2 mm, a maximum FD greater than 2 mm, or a rotation angle greater than 2°. Therefore, 45 MDDs and 54 HCs were included for further analysis.

fALFF analysis

Subsequently, the time series of the whole-brain signal was converted into a frequency-domain power spectrum using the Fast Fourier transform (FFT) [27]. The square root (amplitude) at each frequency of the power spectrum was averaged across the low-frequency spectrum (0.01–0.1 Hz) and was divided by that across the entire frequency range (0–0.25 Hz) to obtain the fALFF of each voxel. Individual fALFF maps were then subtracted from the global mean and divided by the standard deviation to obtain standardized fALFF value for voxels in the entire brain.

The DPABI was used to compare the fALFF between the groups using voxel-based two-sample t-tests and to explore the relationship between the fALFF and CPT-IP scores in the MDD group using whole-brain correlation analyses. The significance threshold was set at voxel-level P < 0.001, cluster-level P < 0.05 (two-tailed), and cluster size > 10 contiguous voxels, corrected for multiple comparisons using Gaussian random field (GRF) theory. The naming of the networks in the current study was based on visual inspection and spatial correlation analysis with previous reports [28–30].

Seed‑based FC analysis

The regions exhibiting significant group differences of fALFF values and significant whole-brain correlation between the fALFF and CPT-IP scores in the MDD group, were defined as the region of interest (ROI) for the voxel-wise seed-to-whole brain FC analysis. The DPABI was used to calculate the correlation coefficients between the mean-time course of each ROI and the time course of each voxel throughout the whole brain to perform the FC analysis. The individual FC maps were then normalized to a z-score for standardization. The whole-brain correlation analyses were conducted to explore the relationship between FC and CPT-IP scores in the MDD group, with a significant threshold of voxel-level P < 0.001 and cluster-level P < 0.05 (two tailed), GRF-corrected. We additionally conducted an exploratory analysis of seed-based FC difference between the groups, considering it may somehow reflect the functional brain network deficiency in the MDD group.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 21.0; SPSS Inc. Chicago, IL, USA), and P < 0.05 was considered significant. The demographic and clinical variables between the MDD and HC group were compared using chi-square test for categorical variables, and Mann–Whitney U test (non-normal distribution) and two-sample t-test (normal distribution) for continuous variables. The associations between the CPT-IP-linked fALFF and FC, and CTQ scores were tested using Pearson’s correlation analyses for normally-distributed variables and Spearman’s correlation analyses for non-normally distributed variables. The Benjamini–Hochberg false discovery rate (FDR) approach was applied for multiple comparison correction of p values (FDR-adjusted P < 0.05). The PROCESS Model 4 of SPSS was used to test the parallel mediation models [31] in the MDD group, with CTQ scores as the independent variable, CPT-IP scores as the dependent variable, and fALFF and FC as parallel mediators. 5000-iteration bootstrap confidence intervals (CIs) was calculated to estimate the significance of the indirect effects for each mediator. If the 95% CIs do not include zero, the mediation effect is considered significant.

Reproducibility analysis

We conducted the reproducibility analysis by adding age as covariate, to evaluate the potential confounding effect of age on the attentional level and brain function.

Mediation analysis in the MDD group

The mediation relationship between the CTQ score, CPT-IP-linked fALFF and FC strength, and CPT-IP score was tested in the MDD group using fALFF and FC strength as mediators. The simple mediation model showed that the fALFF in the left PoCG significantly mediated the effect of the CTQ score on the CPT-IP score (indirect effect = -0.234, 95% CI = [-0.450, -0.021]; Fig. 6A) in the MDD group, accounting for 74.29% of the variability of the dependent variable. The relationship between the CTQ score and fALFF in the left PoCG was positive (a = 0.362, P = 0.015), while the relationship between fALFF in the left PoCG and the CPT-IP score was negative (b = -0.645, P < 0.001).The parallel mediation model showed that the FC strength of the left PoCG-left PreCG and left ANG-right STG significantly mediated the effect of the CTQ score on the CPT-IP score (indirect effect 1 = -0.157, 95% CI = [-0.339, -0.012]; indirect effect 2 = -0.119, 95% CI = [-0.236, -0.024]; Fig. 6B) in the MDD group, totally accounting for 87.62% of the variability of the dependent variable. Discussion

Our study focused on examining the fMRI measurements, including fALFF and FC alterations, that underlie the relationship between childhood trauma and sustained attention. The behavioral results showed that the level of sustained attention indicated by the CPT-IP scores were significantly correlated with childhood trauma severities as assessed by the CTQ in the MDD group. The results of fMRI measurement showed MDDs exhibited higher fALFF in the right middle frontal gyrus and left inferior frontal gyrus, and lower fALFF in the bilateral insular cortex, left medial orbital superior frontal gyrus and left ANG, when compared with HCs, Significant correlations were found between sustained attention scores and the fALFF value in the left PoCG and FC strength of the left PoCG-left PreCG and left ANG-right STG, and these fALFF and FC values associated with sustained attention were also found to correlate with childhood trauma severity in the MDD group. Furthermore, we identified the mediating roles of fALFF and FC alterations on the association between childhood trauma and sustained attention in the MDD group. These results suggest that the altered fALFF and FC in sensorimotor network (SMN), and between DMN and VAN may serve as potential neuromechanism underlying the relationship between childhood trauma and sustained attention problems in MDDs.

The current study showed that the function of SMN, i.e., fALFF in the PoCG and FC strength of the PoCG-PreCG, mediated the effect of childhood trauma on sustained attention. Specifically, the experience of childhood trauma was positively associated with both the fALFF in the PoCG and the FC strength of the PoCG-PreCG, while these parameters were negatively associated with the sustained attention level. How childhood trauma affects alterations in brain function is not yet clear; however, one possible hypothesis is that childhood trauma might affect neurodevelopment in early childhood and maintain its influence into adulthood [32], including the gray matter volume in the sensorimotor cortices [33]. Previous studies have demonstrated that functional networks are shaped by the underlying structural networks [34, 35]. Thus, it is plausible that childhood trauma could affect resting-state brain activity and FC of SMN, as our current study suggests. On the other hand, it is not surprising that sustained attention was also associated with altered function of the SMN, given its crucial role in processing sensorimotor information and regulating emotions [36, 37]. Previous studies found that the performance of MDD patients on attention demanding task is negatively correlated with the activity of the PoCG [38] and FC between the subgenual anterior cingulate cortex and the PoCG [39]. Notably, the primary somatosensory cortex within the PoCG, an area implicated in self-awareness of one’s own body, exhibits hightened activation when individuals direct their attention towards their emotional states. This response underscores the importance of body state perception in shaping emotional experiences [40]. Studies have indicated that individuals with MDD often display an attentional bias towards negative emotions, struggling to shift their focus away from the negative emotional information, particularly in high-stress contexts. Consequently, the observed heightened functional arousal of SMN in MDD could account for the increased sensitivity and susceptibility to emotional distractions, which in turn lead to disruptions in sustained attention. Our finding also suggests PoCG and PreCG serve as neural biomarkers for the association between childhood trauma and sustained attention. Results from previous interventional studies may backup our proposals. These studies showed that repetitive transcranial magnetic stimulation, transcranial alternating current stimulation, and mindfulness-based stress reduction training could facilitate sustained attention and modulate regional brain activity and related networks, including the PoCG and PreCG [41–43]. However, some previous studies have reported opposite results, such as a negative association between childhood trauma and the fALFF of the PoCG in MDD [44], and the FC between the temporal cortex and the prefrontal cortex extending to the PreCG in the general population [45]. These discrepancies might be due to differences in participant recruitment and image analysis methodologies. In summary, our results suggest childhood trauma experience may affect the dynamic integration of the PoCG and PreCG within the SMN from childhood to early adulthood, disrupting the capability for sustained attention.

This study also found that the FC strength of the ANG-STG mediated the effect of childhood trauma on sustained attention. Specifically, childhood trauma positively affected the FC strength of the ANG-STG, while the FC strength in turn negatively affected sustained attention levels. The ANG and STG are core regions of the DMN and the VAN, respectively. In normal development, brain networks undergo a process of increasing segregation between different networks, while simultaneously integrating specific networks to strengthen internal connectivity, to increase the efficiency of information processing throughout the whole brain [46, 47]. However, childhood trauma could disrupt network sculpting during the crucial period of brain maturation, affecting the development of functional properties, connectivity frameworks, and cognitive advances. The DMN has been implicated in episodic and semantic memory, self-referential processing, and deactivate during attention-demanding tasks [48–50]. Meanwhile, the VAN is associated with goal-directed attention and plays an important role in directing attention to salient events [51–53]. The increased FC of DMN-VAN may reflect the childhood trauma effects on brain network maturity during childhood and in turn, signify a heightened susceptibility to attentional redirection and difficulty in maintaining sustained attention in the patients with MDD. Along with our finding, previous studies have reported that childhood trauma was associated with the between-network connectivity of the VAN and DMN [54] and the performance during CPT-IP was negatively correlated with the fALFF of the ANG [55] in patients with MDD. Altogether, it can be speculated that childhood trauma might alter the developmental trajectories of neural connectivity between the DMN and VAN, especially affecting the FC of ANG-STG, which is closely related to sustained attention.

The current study has several limitations. First, due to the relatively small sample size, we were unable to subgroup the patients with MDD to discern the effects of different childhood maltreatment subtypes. The limited sample size also constrained the statistical power to detect subtle effects on brain activity and FC strength. Second, the participants in our study exhibited a range of clinical characteristics (i.e., medication use, depression severity and subtype), which could have introduced bias into the results. Third, the cross-sectional design precludes us from drawing causal conclusions regarding the relationship between childhood trauma and sustained attention. Therefore, longitudinal studies with a larger sample size and less bias are required. Finally, the plasma levels of cortisol and inflammatory factors are known to correlate with brain activation and FC alternation [56, 57], and may heighten individuals’ susceptibility to the influence of childhood trauma [58]. Further investigation is necessary to obtain a comprehensive understanding of how network sculpting is affected by dysregulation within the inflammatory and neuroendocrine systems.

Conclusion

Our current study found significant correlations between impaired sustained attention, and increased fALFF in the left PoCG and increased FC of left PoCG - left PreCG and left ANG -right STG. Further correlation and mediation analyses identified the mediating role of the function of SMN and FC between DMN and VAN on the association between childhood trauma and impaired sustained attention in the MDD patients. The observed alterations in the function of the SMN and DMN-VAN FC may account for the abnormal focus on internal emotions, particularly negative ones, in the MDDs, thereby affecting their sustained attention abilities. These findings suggest that the altered functions and connectivity might serve as potential targets for interventions aimed at improving sustained attention in MDD.

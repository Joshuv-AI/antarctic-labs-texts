A customized dynamic hip screw for valgus osteotomy in femoral neck nonunion with remarkable anatomy: A case report
Authors: Posatorn Suwanpongsai, Kongkhet Riansuwan
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe report the treatment of femoral neck nonunion in an adult female who has remarkable femoral neck anatomy. The initial fracture was treated by closed reduction and multiple screws incorporated pin fixation. Eventually, nonunion with implant loosening developed after about 10 following months. Therefore, trochanteric valgus osteotomy and dynamic hip screw fixation was determined to treat the nonunion. As a result of implant-anatomy mismatching, the customized lag screw was designed to compromise both of the particular anatomy and the ordinary side plate. The implant was manufactured using a Computer Numerical Control (CNC) machine. Finally, the nonunion and osteotomy site completely healed without complication.Keywords: Nonunion, Femoral neck fracture, Trochanteric valgus osteotomy, Customized implant, Dynamic hip screw

Introduction

Treatment of femoral neck fractures in young adults remains a challenging procedure in orthopedic surgery. Among the related complications, nonunion is a common condition requiring surgical repair [1]. In order to provide mechanical compression at the nonunion site and enhance the healing, trochanteric valgus osteotomy is recommended especially in a coxa vara patient [[2], [3], [4]].

With respect to the internal fixation, most of the standard implants have been designed and manufactured based on the normal anatomy. Therefore, inconsistency between the implant design and the bony anatomy is possible especially in the patient with unusual anatomy. For this instance, the customized implant is recently a good candidate to solve such problem.

While the customized implant is currently a treatment trend for some specific conditions, we therefore present a case report for additional information in this circumstance.

Case presentation

A 21-year-old Thai female sustained a right femoral neck fracture following a low-energy trauma. The medical background revealed a positive SMAD3 pathologic variant, compatible with Loeys-Dietz syndrome type 3 (LDS3). The patient presented at the emergency room with mechanical groin pain after getting a Thai traditional massage at her right thigh. The radiographs showed transcervical femoral neck fracture of the right femur classified as type III by both of Garden [5] and Pauwel [6] classification. No other associated injury was identified. The broken bone was initially treated by closed reduction and fixation with two cannulated screws incorporated with a pin.

At 10 months following the initial surgery, the patient still complained of mechanical thigh pain during walking and the radiographs revealed a persistent fracture gap and radiolucent line around the screw (Fig. 1). Therefore, nonunion with slightly loosening of the implant was diagnosed. In order to stimulate the fracture gap healing especially in coxa vara, a trochanteric valgus osteotomy fixing with a dynamic hip screw (DHS) was a good candidate [[2], [3], [4],7].

The radiographs reveal a persistent fracture gap and a radiolucent line around the screw indicating nonunion and fixation failure.

In consequence of remarkable radiographic femoral neck, CT scan was requested to identify the narrowest diameter of the femoral neck which was 8.5 mm. This was incompatible with the standard adult lag screw. However, it was fortunately that the equipment set for pediatric DHS (Smith & Nephew) was still available in our hospital. Therefore, the tap of pediatric lag screw which the thread diameter is 9.5 mm was used to become a template for designing a customized lag screw in this particular patient (Fig. 2).

The narrowest femoral neck diameter in two different plains of CT scans is presented (A, B). The diameter of tapping instrument in pediatric DHS set (C) and that of adult lag screw (D) is also demonstrated.

To create a customized screw thread compatible with this pediatric DHS instrument, the lag screw was designed using reconstruction CT scans and RadiANT DICOM software to generate a computer-aided design (CAD) model. The screw thread design was measured and matched using an automatic profile measurement machine (KEYENCE IM-7030, Japan) to ensure compatibility. The strength of the implant was tested using simulation software (Autodesk Inventor Nastran 2023, Autodesk, USA). The customized lag screw, designed to have the same thread diameter as the pediatric screw while another end corresponds with the barrel of the standard DHS, was confirmed through finite element analysis. Finally, the implant was manufactured using a computer numerical control machine (GENOS L3000-e Lathe Machine, Okuma, Japan, and VF-1 3-Axis CNC Machine, HAAS, USA) with medical-grade extra hardened stainless steel 316 LVM (Fig. 3). The total process consumed approximately 4 weeks until obtaining the final product.

A standard adult lag screw and a customized lag screw were demonstrated (A) including computer-aided design (CAD) model of the customized screw based on reconstruction CT scans (B) and finite element analysis (C).

Regarding the definite surgery, the previous implants were initially removed.

followed by a customized lag screw insertion at the center of the femoral neck. Then trochanteric valgus osteotomy was determined at the location just underneath the lateral end of the lag screw and the barrel of side plate. Finally, a standard side plate was successfully fixed under image intensifier guidance without complication.

For postoperative rehabilitation, the patient was allowed to do partial weight bearing using axillary crutches in the first postoperative month. Since then progressive up to full weight bearing was applied for a couple months. Finally, the patient was able to achieve pain-free, full weight ambulation without gait aids and the postoperative sequential radiographs confirmed complete healing at both of the femoral neck and the osteotomy site (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Nonunion is a common complication following treatment of femoral neck fracture [1]. This case report highlights the complexities and successful management of a transcervical femoral neck nonunion in a young adult with a unique anatomical presentation and genetic predisposition. The presence of abnormal femoral neck anatomy and an underlying genetic condition (positive SMAD3 pathologic variant, compatible with Loeys-Dietz syndrome type 3(LDS3)) further complicates the clinical scenario.

Nowadays, trochanteric valgus osteotomy remains an effective procedure to treat femoral neck nonunion in adult [[2], [3], [4]]. To obtain a stable fixation, the implant needs to stabilize both of the femoral neck nonunion and the osteotomy site. Among the different implant designs, DHS [2] is a good option to achieve those objectives. Moreover, DHS allows dynamization at the femoral neck in order to gain a mechanical stimulation for bone healing at the nonunion site. Nevertheless, a limitation of using such implant is the anatomy of the femoral neck that has to accommodate with the lag screw insertion. Therefore, the customized implant personally designed is recently a solution for the patient who has a remarkable anatomy. In this patient, the osteotomy site located at the level of lesser trochanter, therefore, the side plate should be long enough to provide adequate stability. Unfortunately, the shorter plate which was available in our institution is too short. Eventually, an 8-hole side plate was determined to provide a stable fixation in this instant.

Conclusion

This report demonstrates another possibility to use the customized implant as a recent solution to treat an orthopedic patient who has unusual bony anatomy which is not compatible with the standard implant. However, time consuming is still a major concerning factor to be improved in the near future.

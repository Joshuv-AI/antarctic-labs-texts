A stacked Gradient Boosting–XGBoost ensemble with ridge meta-learner for accurate short-term solar PV power forecasting in smart grids
Authors: G Rohini, T Mariprasth, Seif Al Bustanji, Ievgen Zaitsev
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAs the solar photovoltaic (PV) penetration level increases in smart grids, precise and computationally efficient short-term forecasting becomes essential to aid operational planning and real-time energy management. However, the power produced by PV is highly nonlinear and stochastic due to variations in weather factors, which weakens the performance of single forecasting models. The aim of this work is to propose a stacked ensemble regression model that combines Gradient Boosting and XGBoost (Extreme Gradient Boosting) as base learners, with Ridge Regression as the meta-learner, for very short-term PV power prediction. The model operates using meteorological and operational parameters, such as temperature, humidity, wind profile, cloudiness distribution, and solar situation. Standard preprocessing steps (missing value imputation, feature selection, and normalization) are adopted to facilitate stable model training. An empirical study is carried out using real-world PV generation data, and the results are compared with popular gradient boosting algorithms such as Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM (Light Gradient Boosting Machine), and CatBoost (Categorical Boosting), and machine learning models such as multilayer perceptron (MLP) and LSTM, using k-fold cross-validation. The boosted ensemble improves predictive accuracy, achieving MAE = 0.042 ± 0.002, MSE = 0.0031 ± 0.0002, and R² = 94% ± 1% under the experimental conditions used in this work. Nonparametric tests (i.e., the Wilcoxon signed-rank test and the Friedman test) show that such improvements are statistically significant (p < 0.05). Moreover, the inference latency of the proposed model is quite low, which demonstrates its suitability for near-real-time deployment in real-world smart grid scenarios. According to experimental results, lightweight ensemble learning can stand as a competitive and practical alternative to complicated deep learning methods for short-term PV power forecasting when data availability and computational budget are taken into account.Keywords: CatBoost, Ensemble learning, Gradient boosting, Machine learning, Ridge regression, Smart Grids, Solar photovoltaic forecasting, XGBoostSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

The rapid global expansion of solar photovoltaic (PV) systems has transformed modern power systems, positioning renewable energy as a central component of sustainable smart grids. However, unlike conventional generation, solar power is inherently variable and uncertain1. As PV penetration increases, grid operators face growing challenges in maintaining voltage stability, load balancing, and reserve scheduling. Accurate short-term solar power forecasting has therefore become a critical enabler for reliable grid operation, economic dispatch, and optimized energy management in smart grid environments2. Inaccurate forecasts can lead to inefficient reserve allocation, increased operational costs, and higher reliance on backup fossil-fuel generation. Moreover, forecasting uncertainty directly impacts unit commitment decisions and real-time control strategies in grid-scale renewable integration. Consequently, developing robust and operationally feasible forecasting models is essential for enabling high-penetration PV integration in future low-carbon power systems3.

Short-term solar PV forecasting is fundamentally complex due to the stochastic and nonlinear nature of solar irradiance. Power output is strongly influenced by rapidly changing meteorological variables such as cloud cover, humidity, temperature, wind speed, and solar positional angles4. These variables interact in a highly nonlinear manner, making traditional linear modeling approaches insufficient. Moreover, forecast inaccuracies can propagate operational risks, including frequency deviations, inefficient reserve allocation, and increased balancing costs5. Consequently, forecasting models must simultaneously handle nonlinear feature interactions, high variability under dynamic weather conditions, and strict computational constraints for real-time deployment6. To address these challenges, various machine learning and deep learning approaches have been developed. Deep learning architectures such as RNNs, LSTMs, CNN–LSTM hybrids, and Transformer-based models have demonstrated strong capability in capturing temporal dependencies and nonlinear relationships7. Physics-informed models integrate clear-sky radiation principles to improve physical consistency. Tree-based boosting methods, including Random Forest, Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost, offer robust performance for structured meteorological data. Hybrid and ensemble frameworks further combine multiple learners to enhance generalization8.

Despite their strengths, these approaches present notable limitations. Deep learning models typically require large datasets, extensive hyperparameter tuning, and high computational resources, limiting real-time feasibility. Physics-based frameworks depend on satellite data or site-specific assumptions, reducing scalability. Standalone boosting models may suffer from sensitivity to parameter settings and limited bias–variance balancing. Ensemble methods often improve accuracy but lack systematic statistical validation and computational efficiency analysis9. Moreover, many existing studies evaluate models under limited experimental settings, making it difficult to generalize their effectiveness across diverse climatic and operational conditions. As a result, the practical adoption of such methods in real-world smart grid environments remains constrained10.

Despite substantial progress, three major limitations remain in current solar PV forecasting research. First, many high-performing deep learning models impose significant computational complexity, making them impractical for resource-constrained smart grid environments. Second, interpretability remains limited in complex neural architectures, reducing transparency for grid-level decision-making. Third, there is a lack of lightweight, statistically validated ensemble frameworks that balance accuracy, stability, interpretability, and deployment feasibility without reliance on satellite imagery or rigid physical assumptions. Addressing these gaps is essential for advancing forecasting solutions suitable for operational smart grid applications11.

In smart grid systems, short-term PV power forecasting itself is very important because grid operations further depend on real-time control decisions. Smart grids coordinate different energy sources, battery storage systems, electric vehicles, and demand response programs to maintain voltage and frequency stability itself under high renewable energy use, and this coordination further helps manage the complex power system effectively. As per short time forecasting errors in minutes to hours, real-time energy management systems get directly affected regarding storage dispatch, congestion management, and ancillary service provisioning. Moreover, effective short-term predictions help us plan backup power better and work with small power producers more easily. This only makes island grids and mixed power systems work more safely. As per smart grid requirements, strong short-term solar power forecasting is needed for intelligent and cost-effective grid operation. Regarding grid resilience, accurate PV forecasting forms the basic foundation for efficient electricity management.

Recent studies are showing that we need smart learning systems that can change and work together for predicting power grid behavior when conditions keep changing. These flexible methods can handle the complex situations in modern electricity networks. For example, the study on mitigating concept drift challenges in smart grids shows how ensemble LSTM models can surely handle changing data patterns in power systems. Moreover, these adaptive deep learning methods work well when grid conditions keep changing over time. Chiefly, these adaptive systems make load prediction more robust, but they use computationally heavy recurrent models and need continuous updates, which creates the same problem of limiting real-time use in resource-constrained edge environments. As per short-term solar PV forecasting needs, we require simple but strong models that can handle quick weather changes without training them again and again. Regarding model performance, these systems must work well with changing weather conditions. To build simple and fast computer systems that can predict solar power generation in smart electricity networks only. Recent studies have explored transfer-learning-enabled adaptive frameworks to address concept drift in smart grid load forecasting across heterogeneous generation modalities, motivating the need for deployment-oriented forecasting solutions.

To overcome these challenges, this study proposes a lightweight stacked ensemble regression framework that integrates Gradient Boosting and XGBoost as complementary base learners, combined through a Ridge Regression meta-learner. Gradient Boosting and XGBoost effectively capture nonlinear relationships and structured feature interactions, while the Ridge meta-learner reduces variance and mitigates overfitting through regularized linear fusion of base predictions. Unlike satellite-dependent or physics-constrained models, the proposed framework relies solely on real-time meteorological and operational parameters, enhancing scalability across diverse PV installations. The architecture is computationally efficient, statistically validated through k-fold cross-validation, Wilcoxon signed-rank testing, and Friedman ranking analysis, and specifically designed for real-time smart grid deployment.

While ensemble- and stacking-based PV forecasting methods have been explored extensively in prior literature, most existing approaches prioritize pointwise accuracy using homogeneous ensembles or deep-learning–centric frameworks, often overlooking robustness, interpretability, and deployment feasibility under real-time smart grid constraints. In contrast, the present study introduces a lightweight, heterogeneous stacking framework that integrates complementary gradient-boosting learners with a regularized linear meta-learner, explicitly designed to balance predictive accuracy, robustness across data splits, and computational efficiency. Furthermore, the proposed work advances existing methodologies by incorporating statistical significance testing and residual distribution analysis to provide a more rigorous and practically relevant evaluation of forecasting performance.

Research gap and motivation

A detailed assessment of recent solar photovoltaic (PV) forecasting studies specifies substantial development in modeling complex weather-dependent power generation patterns, but several important research gaps regarding this field still need to be solved. Deep learning methods like attention-based RNNs, CNN-LSTM combinations, and Transformer models surely improve short-term prediction accuracy by learning complex time patterns. Moreover, these approaches effectively capture temporal dependencies that traditional methods often miss. Further, as per the requirements, these models need big datasets, high computing power, and lots of parameter adjustments, which stops their use in real-time smart grid systems. Regarding practical deployment, these limitations make it hard to use them in environments with limited resources. Moreover, deep models are hard to understand, which surely makes them less useful for clear decision-making in power grid systems.

Also, second, physics-based and hybrid physical-ML frameworks further improve physical consistency and early-hour prediction accuracy, where the satellite-driven model itself provides better results. However, their performance becomes poor under abnormal or quickly changing weather conditions, and their use is further limited by data availability problems, such as dependence on high-resolution satellite images or fixed physical assumptions that may not work across different sites itself. Tree-based and boosting models like Random Forest, XGBoost, LightGBM, and CatBoost surely show strong robustness against data variations. Moreover, these models have lower computational complexity compared to deep learning models. However, standalone methods surely cannot capture all the complex feature interactions properly. Moreover, they are sensitive to parameter settings and perform inconsistently during rapid cloud changes and highly variable sunlight conditions.

Fourth, ensemble and probabilistic forecasting methods surely make predictions more reliable and help measure uncertainty. Moreover, most existing studies focus on either improving accuracy or estimating uncertainty, but not both at the same time. Bayesian and deep ensemble methods work well but take much longer to train and need high computational power, making them difficult to scale for large or real-time applications where speed is the same priority.It is observe that review studies only show there are no standard ways to test and compare different AI models properly. Finding a forecasting strategy that itself works well across different situations while balancing accuracy, understanding, and efficiency remains a challenge for further research.

Motivated by the identified research gaps, the present study aims to develop a computationally efficient and highly accurate hybrid ensemble forecasting framework for short-term solar PV power prediction. The proposed approach is designed to exploit the complementary learning strengths of gradient-boosting models and neural networks while avoiding excessive computational burden. By effectively combining diverse learners, the framework reduces error variance and improves generalization performance under varying operating conditions and rapidly changing weather patterns. Unlike several existing approaches, the proposed method does not rely on external satellite data or rigid physical assumptions, thereby ensuring wider applicability across different geographical regions and data availability scenarios.

Furthermore, the ensemble architecture is structured to maintain interpretability and scalability, making it suitable for practical deployment in smart grid environments. To achieve these objectives, multiple state-of-the-art machine learning and deep learning models are systematically evaluated, and a stacked regression ensemble is introduced, integrating Gradient Boosting and XGBoost as base learners with Ridge Regression as a meta-learner. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed ensemble significantly outperforms individual models in terms of MAE, MSE, and coefficient of determination (R²), effectively addressing the key limitations reported in existing solar PV forecasting literature.

Methodology

Data source

The dataset used in this study was collected from a grid-connected solar photovoltaic (PV) power plant operated by Energy Private Limited, located in tamil nadu. The measurements were acquired through a Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) and on-site meteorological monitoring system installed at the PV facility. The data acquisition infrastructure consists of calibrated meteorological sensors and electrical meters, enabling continuous monitoring of both environmental conditions and PV power output.The dataset comprises real-time operational and meteorological variables that are known to directly influence PV power generation. Specifically, the collected features include ambient temperature (2 m above ground), relative humidity, mean sea-level pressure, total precipitation, snowfall, multi-layer cloud cover (high, medium, and low), shortwave solar radiation, wind speed and wind direction at multiple heights (10 m, 80 m, and 900 mb), wind gusts, and solar geometric parameters (solar zenith angle, azimuth angle, and angle of incidence). The target variable is the generated PV power (kW) measured at the inverter output.

Data were recorded at regular time intervals through the plant monitoring system, enabling the capture of short-term fluctuations in solar generation under dynamically changing weather conditions. The inclusion of solar positional parameters allows the model to account for deterministic diurnal and seasonal variations in solar irradiance, while meteorological variables such as cloud cover, humidity, and wind conditions capture stochastic atmospheric effects. High ambient temperatures tend to reduce PV conversion efficiency due to increased cell temperature, whereas wind flow contributes to module cooling, thereby influencing instantaneous power output. Cloud cover and precipitation introduce rapid irradiance variability, which is critical for short-term forecasting.

The dataset exhibits a wide operating range of PV generation, with power output varying from near-zero values during low irradiance periods to peak generation levels of approximately 3.06 MW, reflecting realistic plant-level operating conditions. The diversity and physical relevance of the recorded features provide a robust foundation for training data-driven forecasting models capable of learning both deterministic solar geometry effects and stochastic weather-induced variations. This comprehensive dataset is therefore well-suited for evaluating the proposed stacked ensemble framework under realistic smart grid operating conditions.

Missing values in the dataset were handled using mean imputation for each feature column, which preserves the statistical distribution of the data while avoiding unnecessary data loss. This approach ensures continuity of time samples and prevents bias introduced by removing incomplete records.The dataset was then partitioned into input features and the target variable (generated PV power in kW). The proposed stacked framework uses GBR and XGBoost models as base learners, and then combines their predictions using the same Ridge Regression approach as the final learner. The base models are surely trained first on the original input features, and their predictions are then stacked together to create a meta-feature matrix. Moreover, this stacking process forms the foundation for the next level of learning. As per the stacked output, the Ridge meta-learner is trained to learn the best linear combination regarding base model predictions. It is noted that L2 regularization in Ridge only helps to stop overfitting and makes the system more stable, which improves how well it works on new data and makes it good for real-time smart grid use.

Although both Gradient Boosting and XGBoost belong to the tree-based boosting family, they differ substantially in their optimization strategies, regularization mechanisms, tree-construction procedures, and handling of sparsity and missing values. Classical Gradient Boosting relies on stagewise additive modeling with relatively simple regularization, whereas XGBoost incorporates second-order optimization, explicit L1/L2 regularization, shrinkage, and tree-pruning strategies. These architectural and optimization differences lead to distinct inductive biases and error characteristics, even when the models are trained on the same dataset. Empirically, we have observed partially decorrelated residual patterns across cross-validation folds and data splits, enabling the Ridge meta-learner to exploit complementary strengths and reduce overall error variance. Therefore, diversity in the proposed ensemble is achieved through algorithmic and regularization-level heterogeneity rather than purely through model-family differences.

Conclusion

This study developed a lightweight heterogeneous stacked ensemble framework for accurate short-term solar PV power forecasting in smart grids by integrating Gradient Boosting and XGBoost through a regularized Ridge meta-learner. A comprehensive comparative evaluation against state-of-the-art machine learning and deep learning models—including Gradient Boosting, XGBoost, LightGBM, CatBoost, multilayer perceptron neural networks, and LSTM—demonstrated that tree-based ensembles consistently outperform standalone deep learning models under the studied operating conditions. In contrast to existing ensemble- and stacking-based PV forecasting approaches that primarily emphasize accuracy using homogeneous or deep learning–centric frameworks, the proposed method is explicitly designed to balance predictive accuracy, robustness, interpretability, and computational efficiency for real-time deployment.

The proposed stacked regressor achieved the best overall performance, with an MAE of 0.042 ± 0.002, an MSE of 0.0031 ± 0.0002, and an R² of 0.94 ± 0.01. Compared with individual base learners, the stacked ensemble reduced MAE by approximately 12.5% and MSE by 16.2% relative to Gradient Boosting and achieved 6.7% lower MAE and 6.1% lower MSE compared with XGBoost, with a corresponding improvement in R² from 0.91 to 0.94. Relative to CatBoost, the proposed approach further reduced MAE by 4.5% and improved R² from 0.93 to 0.94. The stacked ensemble also outperformed deep learning baselines, achieving MAE reductions of approximately 16.0% and 14.3% compared with neural networks and LSTM, respectively, along with consistent gains in R2.

Residual and error distribution analyses indicated that CatBoost, LightGBM, XGBoost, and the proposed stacked ensemble exhibit tighter and more balanced error profiles with fewer outliers, reflecting superior robustness and reliability under varying irradiance and weather conditions. In contrast, neural network and LSTM models showed wider error dispersion and greater sensitivity to rapidly changing atmospheric conditions. Moreover, k-fold cross-validation results confirmed that the stacked ensemble exhibits the lowest standard deviation across performance metrics, indicating strong generalization and minimal sensitivity to data partitioning.

The proposed lightweight heterogeneous stacking framework delivers 5–16% improvements in forecasting accuracy over strong boosting and deep learning baselines while maintaining low computational overhead and stable generalization performance. These characteristics distinguish the proposed approach from existing ensemble-based PV forecasting methods and support its suitability for real-time smart grid applications where computational efficiency, robustness, and interpretability are critical. Future work will extend the framework to multi-site forecasting and incorporate probabilistic uncertainty estimation to enhance operational decision support across diverse climatic regions.

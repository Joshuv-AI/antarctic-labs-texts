Gaussian fluctuations for hyperbolic Anderson model with Lévy colored noise
Raluca M. Balan, William D. Stephenson

Abstract
In this article, we study the asymptotic behaviour of the spatial integral F R ​ ( t ) F_{R}(t) of the solution to the hyperbolic Anderson model in dimension d = 1 d=1 , driven by the Lévy colored noise introduced in [ 2 ] . We assume that the spatial coloration kernel of the noise is either integrable on ℝ \mathbb{R} , or is the Riesz kernel of order α ∈ ( 0 , 1 ) \alpha\in(0,1) , and the Lévy measure of the noise has finite moments of order p p and 2 ​ p 2p for some p ∈ ( 1 , 2 ] p\in(1,2] . By applying a recent result of [ 28 ] , we prove that F R ​ ( t ) / Var ​ ( F R ​ ( t ) ) F_{R}(t)/\sqrt{{\rm Var}\big(F_{R}(t)\big)} converges to the standard normal distribution as R → ∞ R\to\infty , and we give an estimate for the rate of this convergence in the Fortet-Mourier distance, the 1-Wasserstein distance, or the Kolmogorov distance. We also provide the corresponding functional limit result.

Introduction

The theory of stochastic partial differential equations (SPDEs) has grown consistently in the last three decades, and now constitutes a central area of stochastic analysis. SPDEs model the evolution of random systems across multiple scales, ranging from microscopic particle dynamics to macroscopic continuum limits, and arise in diverse contexts, such as interface growth, disordered media, turbulence, and transport in random environments. They provide a rigorous mathematical framework for universality phenomena observed in physics. Motivated by these physical models and mathematical challenges, systematic theories for SPDEs began to emerge in the late 1980s and 1990s. Prominent developments include Walsh’s
[
29
]
probabilistic approach based on martingale measures and random fields, and the semigroup approach introduced by Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
10
]
. Basic equations which have been studied are the stochastic heat equation and the stochastic wave equation, and their particular cases, the parabolic Anderson model (pAm), respectively the hyperbolic Anderson model (hAm). However, comprehensive solution theories for numerous other SPDEs
remained beyond the grasp of mathematical analysis, until the early 21st century. These challenges
stemmed from the singular nature of such equations, a singularity intricately linked to the irregularities inherent in the random data involved. A significant breakthrough arrived in 2014 when Hairer
[
12
]

constructed a well-posedness framework for the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation, using concepts from Lyons’ rough paths theory
[
18
]
. In dimension 1, the solution of the KPZ equation is directly related to the solution of (pAm
) via the Cole-Hopf transformation.

Traditionally, SPDEs are perturbed by a space-time Gaussian white noise in dimension
d=1
, which is a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{B_{t}(\varphi);t>0,\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}
with covariance:

\mathbb{E}[B_{t}(\varphi)B_{s}(\psi)]=(t\wedge s)\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R})}.

In higher dimensions, Dalang introduced in
[
9
]
, a spatially-homogeneous (or colored) noise, as a zero-mean Gaussian process
\{W_{t}(\varphi);t\geq 0,\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})\}
with covariance:

\mathbb{E}[W_{t}(\varphi)W_{s}(\psi)]=(t\wedge s)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)f(x-y)dxdy,

(1)

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of all rapidly decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

is the Fourier transform of a tempered measure
\mu
. This noise can also be defined as:

W_{t}(\varphi)=B_{t}(\varphi*\kappa)\quad\mbox{for all $t>0$ and $\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

(2)

where the kernel
\kappa
is chosen such that
k*\widetilde{k}=f
, and
\widetilde{k}(x):=k(-x)
for all

One of the problems which has received a lot of interest in the literature in the recent years is the study of the asymptotic behaviour as

F_{R}(t)=\int_{|x|<R}\big(u(t,x)-\mathbb{E}[u(t,x)]\big)dx

[
13

for the stochastic heat equation driven by space-time Gaussian white noise, by combining tools from Malliavin calculus with Stein’s method for normal approximations. Since then, this method was extended to various other models, each with its own challenges. We refer the reader to

We consider the Hilbert space

\bullet

Any random variable

F=\mathbb{E}(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}),\quad\mbox{for some $f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}$},

Moreover,

\bullet

For any random variable

with chaos expansion (

of
F

D_{\xi}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,\xi)\big),\quad\mbox{for any $\xi\in{\bf Z}$},

provided that provided that the series converges in

, that is

\mathbb{E}\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}nn!\|f_{n}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

(13)

In this case, we write
F\in{\rm dom}(D)
.
We have the following
Poincaré inequality
:

{\rm Var}(F)\leq\mathbb{E}\|DF\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}\quad\mbox{for any $F\in{\rm dom}(D)$.}

(14)

The following chain rule holds: for any

D_{\xi}\varphi(F)=\varphi(F+D_{\xi}F)-\varphi(F)\quad\mbox{for any $\xi\in{\bf Z}$}.

Hence, if

|D_{\xi}\varphi(F)|\leq{\rm Lip}(\varphi)|D_{\xi}F|

\|D_{\xi}\varphi(F)\|_{p}\leq{\rm Lip}(\varphi)\|D_{\xi}F\|_{p}\quad\mbox{for any $p>0$}.

(15)

If
F\in{\rm Dom}(D)
is such that
F
is
\mathcal{F}_{t}
-measurable for some

, then

D_{r,x,z}F=0\quad\mbox{for any $r>t$, $x\in\mathbb{R}$ and $z\in\mathbb{R}_{0}$}.

\bullet

Similarly, we can define the second Malliavin derivative

as follows:
If
F
has the chaos expansion (

\displaystyle D^{2}_{\xi_{1},\xi_{2}}F=D_{\xi_{1}}D_{\xi_{2}}F=\sum_{n\geq 2}n(n-1)I_{n-2}\big(f_{n-2}(\cdot,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\big),\quad\mbox{for $(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in{\bf Z}^{2}$},

provided that the series converges in

, that is

\mathbb{E}\|D^{2}F\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes 2}}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 2}n(n-1)n!\|f_{n}\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}<\infty.

(16)

In this case, we write
F\in{\rm dom}(D^{2})
.

\bullet

Let
\delta:{\rm Dom}(\delta)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be the adjoint of

The Malliavin derivative of the solution has the chaos expansion:

In the proof of Theorem
3.2
, we used the chaos expansion of the solution

of the following equation:

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\dfrac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial^{2}t}(t,x)=\dfrac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial x^{2}}(t,x)+v(t,x)\dot{X}(t,x),\ t>r,\ x\in\mathbb{R},\\

We say that

(29)

for any

and

.
We now prove that (

Proposition 3.4
.

a) For any
(r,y,z)\in Z
, equation (

v^{(r,y,z)}
. Let

be such that

(T,p,\nu,\kappa)
.

b)

(31)

c) For any
0\leq r\leq t
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
,

and

(32)

Consequently, if
p\geq 2
is such that
m_{p}<\infty

8

\sup_{r\leq t\leq T}\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{R}}\|v^{(r,y,z)}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq 2C_{T,p,\nu,\kappa}G_{t-r}(x-y)|z|.

(33)

In particular, under Assumption B, relations (
30
) and (

) hold for any
p\geq 2
such that
m_{p}<\infty
.

Proof.

a) We use Picard iterations: we let
v_{0}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-y)z
, and for
n\geq 0
,

v_{n+1}(t,x)=G_{t-r}(x-y)z+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime})v_{n}(s,y^{\prime})X(ds,dy^{\prime}).

(34)

Letting
v_{-1}=0
, we see that for any
n\geq 0
,

v_{n+1}(t,x)-v_{n}(t,x)=\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime})\big(v_{n}(s,y^{\prime})-v_{n-1}(s,y^{\prime})\big)X(ds,dy^{\prime}).

(35)

We proceed with the argument for
p\geq 2
. The same argument for
p=2
gives the existence of the solution, and proves the estimate (
30
) for
p=2
. By Theorem
2.2
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|v_{n+1}(t,x)-v_{n}(t,x)|^{p}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\mathcal{C}_{p,\nu}(t)\int_{r}^{t}\sup_{y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{E}|v_{n}(s,y^{\prime})-v_{n-1}(s,y^{\prime})|^{p}\Big(J_{2}(t-s)+\big(J_{p}(t-s)\big)^{p/2}\Big)ds,

where

J_{p}(t)=\|G_{t}*\kappa\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}

and

J_{2}(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(G_{t}*\kappa)(x)|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(x)G_{t}(y)f(x-y)dxdy.

The constant
\mathcal{C}_{p,\nu}(t)=\mathcal{C}_{p}\max(q_{t}^{p/2-1},1)
is non-decreasing in
t
,
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is being the constant from Proposition
2.1
(which depends on
\nu
through
m_{2}
and
m_{p}
), and

q_{t}:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y)G_{t-s}(x-z)f(y-z)dydz=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\sin^{2}(s|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)ds.

We fix
T>0
. For any
t\in[r,T]
and
n\geq 0
, let
H_{n}(t)=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|v_{n}(t,x)-v_{n-1}(t,x)\|_{p}
.
Then, for any
t\in[r,T]
and
n\geq 0
, we have:

H_{n+1}(t)\leq\mathcal{C}_{p,\nu}(T)\int_{0}^{t}H_{n}(s)\Big(J_{2}(t-s)+\big(J_{p}(t-s)\big)^{p/2}\Big)ds.

Note that

\int_{0}^{T}\Big(J_{2}(t)+\big(J_{p}(t)\big)^{p/2}\Big)dt<\infty,

since
\int_{0}^{T}J_{2}(t)dt=q_{T}<\infty
and
\int_{0}^{T}\big(J_{p}(t)\big)^{p/2}dt=\mathcal{M}_{p}(T)<\infty
. Moreover,

M:=\sup_{t\in[r,T]}H_{0}(t)=\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}^{p}(x-y)|z|^{p}=2^{-p}|z|^{p}

By Lemma 15 of
[
9
]
with
k_{1}=k_{2}=0
and
g(t)=\mathcal{C}_{p,\nu}(T)\big(J_{2}(t)+\big(J_{p}(t)\big)^{p/2}\big)
, there exists a sequence
(a_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of positive numbers depending on
T
and
g
(which in turn depends on
(p,\nu,\kappa)
), such that
\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{1/q}<\infty
for any

, and

H_{n}(t)\leq Ma_{n}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad t\in[r,T],n\geq 1.

More precisely,
a_{n}=G(T)P(S_{n}\leq T)
where
G(T)=\int_{0}^{T}g(t)dt
,
S_{n}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, and
(X_{i})_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. random variables on
[0,T]
with density
g(t)/G(T)
.

This means that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|v_{n}(t,x)-v_{n-1}(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{2}|z|\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{1/p}<\infty.

(36)

Hence,
\{v_{n}(t,x)\}_{n\geq 1}
is Cauchy in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, uniformly in
(t,x)\in[r,T]\times\mathbb{R}
. We denote its limit by

. Letting
n\to\infty
in (
35
), we infer that

satisfies (
29
). The uniqueness of the solution follows by similar methods.

To prove (
30
), we argue as follows:

\displaystyle\|v(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\|v(t,x)-v_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}+\sum_{k=1}^{n}\|v_{k}(t,x)-v_{k-1}(t,x)\|_{p}+\|v_{0}(t,x)\|_{p}.

Taking the supremum over

(t,x)\in[r,T]\times\mathbb{R}
and using (
36
), we get:

\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|v(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\sup_{t\in[r,T]}\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\|v(t,x)-v_{n}(t,x)\|_{p}+\frac{1}{2}|z|\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{1/p}+\frac{1}{2}|z|

Letting
n\to\infty
, we obtain estimate (
30
) with
C_{T,p,\nu,\kappa}=\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{n\geq 1}a_{n}^{1/p}+1)
.

b) From part a), we know that
v(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}v_{n}(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. From definition (
34
) of
v_{n+1}(t,x)
, we have:

\displaystyle v_{n+1}(t,x)

\displaystyle=zG_{t-r}(x-y)+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)v_{n}(s,\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y^{\prime})L(ds,dy^{\prime})

\displaystyle=zG_{t-r}(x-y)+\int_{r}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)v_{n}(s,\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y^{\prime})z^{\prime}\widehat{N}(ds,dy^{\prime},dz^{\prime}).

Since the integrand is predictable, this Itô integral coincides with the Skorohod integral, by Lemma 2.5.(iv) of
[
4
]
. Hence,

v_{n+1}(t,x)=zG_{t-r}(x-y)+\delta(V_{t,x,n}),

where
V_{t,x,n}(s,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)v_{n}(s,\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y^{\prime})z^{\prime}
.
We claim that:

v_{n}(t,x)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}I_{k}\big(g_{k}^{*}(\cdot,r,y,z,t,x)\big)\quad\mbox{for all}\quad n\geq 0.

(37)

Then, letting
n\to\infty
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain that
v(t,x)
has the desired chaos expansion.

To prove (
37
), we proceed by induction.
For
n=0
, this is clear since
v_{0}(t,x)=zG_{t-r}(x-y)=g_{0}^{*}(r,y,z,t,x)
. For the induction step, we assume that the statement holds for
n
and we prove it for
n+1
. Using the induction hypothesis, we have:

\displaystyle V_{t,x,n}(s,y^{\prime},z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=z^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime\prime})v_{n}(s,y^{\prime\prime})\kappa(y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=z^{\prime}z\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime\prime})G_{s-r}(y^{\prime\prime}-y)\kappa(y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}+

\displaystyle\quad\sum_{k=1}^{n}z^{\prime}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-s}(x-y^{\prime\prime})I_{k}\big(g_{k}^{*}(\cdot,r,y,z,t,x)\big)\kappa(y^{\prime}-y^{\prime\prime})dy^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=g_{1}^{*}(r,y,z,t,x)+\sum_{k=1}^{n}I_{k}\big(g_{k+1}^{*}(\cdot,s,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},r,y,z,t,x)\big),

where the last line follows by stochastic Fubini theorem and definition (
25
) of
g_{n}^{*}
. By Lemma
A.1
, it follows that

\delta(V_{t,x,n})=\sum_{k=0}^{n}I_{k+1}\big(g_{k+1}^{*}(\cdot,s,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},r,y,z,t,x)\big)=\sum_{k=1}^{n+1}I_{k}\big(g_{k}^{*}(\cdot,s,y^{\prime},z^{\prime},r,y,z,t,x)\big).

This proves that the desired statement holds for
n+1
.

c) Using the result in part b), we have:

\displaystyle G_{t-r}(x-y)v^{(r,y,z)}(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}\big(G_{t-r}(x-y)g_{n}^{*}(\cdot,r,y,z,t,x)\big).

The conclusion follows using the fact that

G_{t-r}(x-y)g_{n}^{*}(\cdot,r,y,z,t,x)=\frac{1}{2}g_{n}^{*}(\cdot,r,y,z,t,x)
, because

g_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},z_{n},r,y,z,t,x)
is equal to a constant times the indicator of the set
\{|x-x_{n}|<t-t_{n},\ldots,|x_{2}-x_{1}|<t_{2}-t_{1},|x_{1}-y|<t_{1}-r\}
, which is included in the set
\{|x-y|<t-r\}
.
∎

Second Malliavin derivative of the solution

In this section, we prove a similar estimate for the

-th moment of the second Malliavin derivative.

Recall that the solution
u

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
t>0
and

,

Proof.

We proceed as in the proof of Proposition
3.1
.
From Proposition 7.1 of
[

, we know that

Using relation (

The fact that

∎

Let

,

and

D_{(r_{1},y_{1},z_{1}),(r_{2},y_{2},z_{2})}^{2}u(t,x)=\sum_{n\geq 2}n(n-1)I_{n-2}(\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(\cdot,(r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t,x)).

(38)

The first term in Poisson chaos expansion above is

-th moment.

As in the case of the Malliavin derivative of

, we start with the representation of the kernel
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,t,x)
with fixed arguments
(r_{1},y_{1},z_{1})

\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(\cdot,r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t,x)=\frac{1}{n(n-1)}\sum_{i,j=1,i<j}^{n}h_{ij}^{*(n)}(\cdot,r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t,x),

(39)

with

and
(r_{2},y_{2},z_{2})
on the

-th and
j
-th positions, respectively, among the arguments of
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,t,x)
. More precisely, if
\xi_{\ell}=(t_{\ell},x_{\ell},\zeta_{\ell})
for

, then

\displaystyle f_{ij}^{*(n)}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-2},r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t,x)

\displaystyle\quad:=f_{n}^{*}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},\xi_{i},\ldots,\xi_{j-2},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},\xi_{j-1},\ldots,\xi_{n-2,}t,x)

\displaystyle\quad=z_{1}z_{2}\prod_{\ell=1}^{n-2}\zeta_{\ell}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\kappa(y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{2}-y_{2}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-j}}G_{t-t_{n-2}}(x-w_{n-2})\ldots G_{t_{j-1}-r}(w_{j-1}-y_{2}^{\prime})\prod_{\ell=j-1}^{n-2}\kappa(x_{\ell}-w_{\ell})dw_{j-1}\ldots dw_{n-2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n-j}}G_{r-t_{j-2}}(y_{2}^{\prime}-w_{j-2})\ldots G_{t_{i}-\theta}(w_{i}-y_{1}^{\prime})\prod_{\ell=i}^{j-2}\kappa(x_{\ell}-w_{\ell})dw_{i}\ldots dw_{j-2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{i-1}}G_{\theta-t_{i-1}}(y_{1}^{\prime}-w_{i-1})\ldots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(w_{2}-w_{1})\prod_{\ell=1}^{i-1}\kappa(x_{\ell}-w_{\ell})dw_{1}\ldots dw_{i-1}\right)dy_{1}^{\prime}dy_{2}^{\prime}.

Recalling notation (
25

\displaystyle f_{ij}^{*(n)}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n-2},r_{1},y_{1},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2},z_{2},t,x)=z_{1}z_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\kappa(y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{2}-y_{2}^{\prime})f_{i-1}^{*}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},r_{1},y_{1}^{\prime})

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad g_{j-i-1}^{*}(\xi_{i},\ldots,\xi_{j-2},r_{1},y_{1}^{\prime},z_{1},r_{2},y_{2}^{\prime},z_{2})g_{n-j}^{*}(\xi_{j-1},\ldots,\xi_{n-2},r_{2},y_{2}^{\prime},z_{2},t,x)dy_{1}^{\prime}dy_{2}^{\prime}.

5.4
Functional CLT

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.6
, by showing finite-dimensional convergence and tightness. We will use Kolmogorov-Chentsov criterion (Theorem 3.23 of
[
15
]
) for the existence of the Hölder continuous modification, and the classical moment criterion (Corollary 16.9 of
[
15
]
) for tightness. Recall that by Theorem
1.4
,
\sigma_{R}^{2}(t)\sim R^{\beta}
. We fix
T>0
.

Step 1.
(finite-dimensional convergence) We will prove that for any integer
m\geq 1
and form any
t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}\in[0,T]

\Big(\frac{1}{R^{\beta/2}}F_{R}(t_{1}),\ldots,\frac{1}{R^{\beta/2}}F_{R}(t_{m})\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\Big(\mathcal{G}(t_{1}),\ldots,\mathcal{G}(t_{m})\Big)\quad\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

By Cramér-Wold theorem, this is equivalent to showing that for any
b_{1},\ldots,b_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
,

X_{R}:=\frac{1}{R^{\beta/2}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}F_{R}(t_{j})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}\sum_{j=1}^{m}b_{j}\mathcal{G}(t_{j})\quad\mbox{as $R\to\infty$}.

Using the same argument as on page 4215 of
[
4
]
, it is enough to prove that
X_{R}/\tau_{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z
as
R\to\infty
, where
\tau_{R}^{2}={\rm Var}(X_{R})
and
Z\sim N(0,1)
. By Proposition
5.3
,

d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{R}}{\tau_{R}},Z\right)\leq\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}+\gamma_{3},

where
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{3}
are defined as in (
48
) with
F=X_{R}
. Using the same argument as in the proof of Theorem
1.5
, we infer that

d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{R}}{\tau_{R}},Z\right)\lesssim R^{1-p}\quad\mbox{if $\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})$,}

and

d_{W}\left(\frac{X_{R}}{\tau_{R}},Z\right)\lesssim R^{-\varepsilon}\quad\mbox{for any $\varepsilon\in\big(0,1-\frac{1}{p}-\frac{\alpha}{2}\big)$, \quad if $\kappa=R_{1,\alpha/2}$.}

This implies that
X_{R}/\tau_{R}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\to}}Z
as
R\to\infty
.

Step 2.
(Hölder continuity and tightness)

We will prove that for any
p^{\prime}\geq 2
such that
m_{p^{\prime}}<\infty
and for any
0\leq s<t\leq T
,
R\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}|F_{R}(t)-F_{R}(s)|^{p^{\prime}}\leq C_{T}R^{\beta p^{\prime}/2}(t-s)^{p^{\prime}},

(66)

where
C_{T}>0
is a constant depending on
T
. In particular,
using (
66
) for
p^{\prime}=2
or
p^{\prime}=2p
, and
the two criteria mentioned above, we infer that
\{F_{R}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
has a
\gamma

\gamma\in(0,\frac{\beta}{2})
, and
\{R^{-\beta/2}F_{R}(\cdot)\}_{R>0}
is tight in
C[0,T]
. Note when
\beta=1
, inequality (
66
) mirrors the Lévy white noise case (see (2.65) of
[
4
]
). (From the proof below, we see that relation (
66
) with
p^{\prime}=2
holds for all
R>0
.)

To prove (
66
), we write

\displaystyle F_{R}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{-R}^{R}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{t-r}(x-y)u(r,y)X(dr,dy)dx=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)u(r,y)X(dr,dy),

where
\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)
is given by (
41
). Therefore

\displaystyle F_{R}(t)-F_{R}(s)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)-\varphi_{s,R}(r,y)\big)u(r,y)X(dr,dy)+

\displaystyle\quad\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)u(r,y)X(dr,dy)=:T_{1}+T_{2}.

We estimate the moments of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
using Theorem 3.7 of
[
2
]
and the bound (
9
) for
\|u(t,x)\|_{p^{\prime}}
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|T_{1}|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\int_{0}^{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big(\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)-\varphi_{s,R}(r,y)\big)\big(\varphi_{t,R}(r,y^{\prime})-\varphi_{s,R}(r,y^{\prime})\big)f(y-y^{\prime})dydy^{\prime}dr\Bigg)^{p^{\prime}/2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{0}^{s}\big\|\big(\varphi_{t,R}(r,\cdot)-\varphi_{s,R}(r,\cdot)\big)*\kappa\big\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}^{p^{\prime}}dr=:C(A+B)

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|T_{2}|^{p^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Bigg(\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\varphi_{t,R}(r,y)\varphi_{t,R}(r,y^{\prime})f(y-y^{\prime})dydy^{\prime}dr\Bigg)^{p^{\prime}/2}

B.1

Appendix A
Auxiliary results from Malliavin calculus

In this appendix section, we include some auxiliary results from Malliavin calculus which were used in the sequel.

Let
(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
be a
\sigma
-finite measure space, and
N
be a Poisson random measure on
Z
of intensity
\mathfrak{m}
, defined on a probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
. Let
\widehat{N}
be the compensated version of
N
.
We denote
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
.

The following lemma was used in the proof of Proposition
3.4
.a).

Lemma A.1
.

Let
V\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathcal{H})
be such that for any
\xi\in Z
,
V(\xi)
has the following chaos expansion (with respect to
N
):

V(\xi)=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n}\big(g_{n}(\cdot,\xi)\big)

where
g_{0}(\xi)=\mathbb{E}[V(\xi)]
and
g_{n}(\cdot,\xi)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
may not be symmetric. Let
\delta
be the Skorohod integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
. If
V\in{\rm Dom}(\delta)
, then

\delta(V)=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n+1}(g_{n}).

Proof.

For any
\xi\in Z
, let
f_{n}(\cdot,\xi)
be the symmetrization of
g_{n}(\cdot,\xi)
:

f_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}g_{n}(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)},\xi).

(67)

By Lemma 2.12 of
[
5
]
,

\delta(V)=\sum_{n\geq 0}I_{n+1}(\widetilde{f}_{n})
,
where
\widetilde{f}_{n}
is the symmetrization of
f_{n}
in all
n+1
variables:

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\left[f_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},\xi,\xi_{i+1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{i})\right].

Hence, it is enough to prove that
\widetilde{f}_{n}=\widetilde{g}_{n}
for any
n\geq 1
, where

\widetilde{g}_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n+1})=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}}g_{n}(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n+1)}).

We calculate
\widetilde{f}_{n+1}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n+1})
.
Using the fact that
f_{n}(\cdot,\xi_{i})
is symmetric, and definition (
67
) of
f_{n}(\cdot,\xi)
, we have:

\displaystyle\widetilde{f}_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n+1}\left[f_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{n}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{i-1},\xi_{i+1},\ldots,\xi_{n},\xi_{n+1},\xi_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\left[\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}g_{n}(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)},\xi_{n+1})+\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\left.\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{n,i}}g_{n}(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(i-1)},\xi_{\rho(i+1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)},\xi_{\rho(n+1)},\xi_{i})\right],

where
\mathcal{P}_{n,i}
is the set of all permutations of
\{1,\ldots,i-1,i+1,\ldots,n,n+1\}
. The conclusion
\widetilde{f}_{n}=\widetilde{g}_{n}
follows, using the fact that

\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}g_{n}(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)},\xi_{n+1})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}:\sigma(n+1)=n+1}g_{n}(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n+1)}),

and for any
i=1,\ldots,n
,

\sum_{\rho\in\mathcal{P}_{n,i}}g_{n}(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(i-1)},\xi_{\rho(i+1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)},\xi_{\rho(n+1)},\xi_{i})=\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n+1}:\sigma(n+1)=i}g_{n}(\xi_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\sigma(n+1)}).

∎

Next, we present a result related to the Poisson product formula, which was used for the proof of Lemma
3.3
. First, we recall some definitions. For any
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
, we define the
modified contractions
, as follows:

(i)

f\star^{0}_{0}g=f\otimes g
is the usual tensor product of
f

and
g
;

(ii)

for
1\leq k\leq n\wedge m
,
f\star^{0}_{k}g
is a function
on
Z^{m+n-k}
given by:

\displaystyle(f\star^{0}_{k}g)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n-k}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad f(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k})g(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m-k}^{\prime}),

(iii)

for
1\leq\ell\leq k\leq n\wedge m
,
f\star^{\ell}_{k}g
is function
on
Z^{m+n-k-\ell}
, given by

\displaystyle(f\star^{\ell}_{k}g)(\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k-\ell},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{n-k}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle\int_{Z^{\ell}}f(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell},\gamma_{1},...,\gamma_{k-\ell},x_{1},\ldots,x_{n-k})g(z_{1},\ldots,z_{\ell},\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{k},x_{1}^{\prime},\ldots,x_{m-k}^{\prime})\mathfrak{m}(dz_{1})\ldots\mathfrak{m}(dz_{\ell}).

Note that
f\star^{k}_{k}g
coincides with the usual contraction
f\otimes_{k}g
, which appears in the product formula in the Gaussian case. For
\ell=1,\ldots,k-1
,

f\star^{\ell}_{k}g
may not be well-defined.

For the next result, we refer to Proposition 5 of
[
16
]
, or relation (9.22) of
[
20
]
.

Proposition A.2

(Poisson Product Formula)
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
be symmetric functions such that

f\star_{k}^{\ell}g\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes(m+n-k-\ell)}

for any
k=1,\ldots,n\wedge m
and
\ell=0,1,\ldots,k
.
Then,

I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)=\sum_{k=0}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}\sum_{\ell=0}^{k}\binom{k}{\ell}I_{n+m-k-\ell}(f\star_{k}^{\ell}g).

Lemma A.3
.

Let
f\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
and
g\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes m}
(not necessarily symmetric).
If
\tilde{f}\star_{k}^{1}\tilde{g}=0
, then

I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)=I_{n+m}(f\otimes g)+\sum_{k=1}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}I_{n+m-k}(\tilde{f}\star_{k}^{0}\tilde{g}).

Proof.

By direct calculation, we see that
\tilde{f}\star_{k}^{1}\tilde{g}=0
implies that
\tilde{f}\star_{k}^{\ell}\tilde{g}=0
for all
\ell=2,\ldots,k
. Hence, all the terms in the product formula are zero, except those corresponding to
\ell=0
and
\ell=1
:

\displaystyle I_{n}(f)I_{m}(g)

\displaystyle=I_{n}(\tilde{f})I_{m}(\tilde{g})=I_{n+m}(\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g})+\sum_{k=1}^{n\wedge m}k!\binom{n}{k}\binom{m}{k}I_{n+m-k}(\tilde{f}\star_{k}^{0}\tilde{g}).

Note that
I_{n+m}(\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g})=I_{n+m}(f\otimes g)
since
\tilde{f}\otimes\tilde{g}
and
f\otimes g
have the same symmetrization.
∎

Appendix B
Inequalities for Riesz potentials

In this section, we include some inequalities for Riesz potentials which were used for the proof of the QCLT in the case when
\kappa
is the Riesz kernel.

We recall that the Riesz kernel of order
\alpha\in(0,d)
is defined by

R_{d,\alpha}(x)=C_{d,\alpha}|x|^{-(d-\alpha)}\quad\mbox{with}\quad C_{d,\alpha}=\pi^{-\frac{d}{2}}2^{-\alpha}\frac{\Gamma(\frac{d-\alpha}{2})}{\Gamma(\frac{\alpha}{2})}.

Its Fourier transform (in the sense of distributions) is
\mathcal{F}R_{d,\alpha}(\xi)=|\xi|^{-\alpha}
, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)|x|^{-\alpha}dx=C_{d,\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}\varphi(\xi)|\xi|^{-(d-\alpha)}d\xi\quad\mbox{for any $\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

where
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the set of rapidly decreasing functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

For the next result, we refer to Theorem 1, p.119 of
[
27
]
.

Theorem B.1

(Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolov inequality)
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,d)
and
p>\frac{d}{d-\alpha}
,

\|\varphi*R_{d,\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq A_{d.\alpha,p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\quad\mbox{for any $\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$},

where
A_{d,\alpha,p}>0
is a constant depending on
(d,\alpha,p)
, and
q
is defined by:

\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{d}.

(Condition
p>\frac{d}{d-\alpha}
is equivalent to
q>1
.)

As a consequence of Theorem
B.1
, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma B.2
.

For any
\alpha\in(0,d)
,
q\in(1,\frac{d}{\alpha})
,
\varphi\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\psi\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)R_{d,\alpha}(x-y)dxdy\right|\leq A_{d,\alpha,p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\psi\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
A_{d,\alpha,p}
is the constant from Theorem
B.1
, and
(p,q^{\prime})
are defined by

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1+\frac{\alpha}{d}.

Proof.

We apply Hölder’s inequality, letting
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, and obtain that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)R_{d,\alpha}(x-y)dxdy\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\big(\psi*R_{d,\alpha}\big)dx\right|\leq\|\varphi\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\psi*R_{d,\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Note that
q<\frac{d}{\alpha}
is equivalent to
p>\frac{d}{d-\alpha}
.
Therefore, by Theorem
B.1

\|\psi*R_{d,\alpha}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq A_{d,\alpha,p}\|\psi\|_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

where
\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{d}
. Finally, we note that
1-\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}=\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\alpha}{d}
, and hence,
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1+\frac{\alpha}{d}
.

∎

Remark B.3
.

Applying Lemma
B.2
with
d=1

\alpha=2H-1
for some
H\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
, and
q=q^{\prime}=\frac{1}{H}
, we obtain that for any
\varphi,\psi\in L^{1/H}(\mathbb{R})
,

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)\psi(y)|x-y|^{2H-2}dxdy\right|\leq C_{H}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1/H}(\mathbb{R})}\|\psi\|_{L^{1/H}(\mathbb{R})},

where
C_{H}>0
is a constant that depends on
H
. This inequality is useful for the stochastic analysis with respect to fractional Brownian motion (see e.g. relation (1.23) of
[
19
]
).

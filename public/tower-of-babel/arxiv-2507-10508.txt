Abstract
. Given a connected dense Zariski open set of a compact Kähler manifold U U , we address the general problem of the existence of surjective holomorphic maps F : U → C {F:U\to C} to smooth complex quasi-projective curves from properties of π 1 ​ ( U ) \pi_{1}(U) . It is known that, if such F F exists, then there exists a finitely generated normal subgroup K ​ ⊴ ​ π 1 ​ ( U ) K\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(U) such that π 1 ​ ( U ) / K \pi_{1}(U)/K is isomorphic to a curve orbifold group G G (i.e. the orbifold fundamental group of a smooth complex quasi-projective curve endowed with an orbifold structure). In this paper, we address the converse of that statement in the case where the orbifold Euler characteristic of G G is negative, finding a (unique) surjective holomorphic map F : U → C F:U\to C which realizes the quotient π 1 ​ ( U ) ↠ π 1 ​ ( U ) / K ≅ G \pi_{1}(U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(U)/K\cong G at the level of (orbifold) fundamental groups. We also prove that our theorem is sharp, meaning that the result does not hold for any curve orbifold group with non-negative orbifold Euler characteristic. Furthermore, we apply our main theorem to address Serre’s question of which orbifold fundamental groups of smooth quasi-projective curves can be realized as fundamental groups of complements of curves in ℙ 2 \mathbb{P}^{2} .

1.
Introduction

Unless otherwise stated, every variety appearing in this paper is over
\mathbb{C}
, every complex analytic variety is assumed to be connected and reduced, and every Zariski open set inside of a complex analytic variety is assumed to be non-empty.

Consider the following
well-known problem, which we will refer to as the Geometric Realizability Problem.

Problem 1.1

(Geometric Realizability Problem)
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open set of a compact Kähler manifold, let
u\in U
, and let
\psi:\pi_{1}(U,u)\to G

be an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, where
G
is a group which is isomorphic to the fundamental
group of a smooth quasi-projective curve.

Determine under what conditions
there exists an admissible map (see Definition
1.2
below)

F:U\to C
to a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
realizing
\psi
, that is, such that
\psi
and

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(U,u)\to\pi_{1}(C,F(u))

coincide up to isomorphism in the target.

Related questions regarding the existence of maps from smooth varieties onto curves from
properties of their fundamental groups have been considered by other authors
(cf.
[
Arapura-fundamentalgroups
,
Arapura-geometry
,
ACM-characteristic
,
Bauer-irrational
,
Catanese-Fibred
,
Green-Lazarsfled-higher
,
Gromov-fundamental
,
Jost-Yau-Harmonic
,
Hillman-Complex
]
)

The purpose of this paper is to study a generalization of the Geometric Realizability Problem
(see Problem
1.6
below) to a broader class of groups.
Our main motivation is the study of quasi-projective groups, that is, fundamental groups of smooth
quasi-projective varieties. Indeed, smooth quasi-projective varieties have smooth projective compactifications,
and thus they are Zariski open sets inside of compact Kähler manifolds.

The morphisms to curves appearing in Problem
1.1

are admissible maps in the following sense (see
[
Arapura-geometry
]
).

Definition 1.2

(Admissible map)
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open set inside a compact Kähler manifold. Let
C
be a smooth quasi-projective curve. A map
f:U\to C
is called
admissible
if it satisfies the following
conditions:

•

It is holomorphic and surjective.

•

It has a holomorphic enlargement
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}\to\overline{C}
with connected fibers, where

\widehat{X}
is a Kähler compactification of
U
such that
\widehat{X}\setminus U
is a simple
normal crossings divisor and
\overline{C}
is a smooth compactification of
C
.

Remark 1.3

(Geometric Realizability Problem in the algebraic setting)
.

Let
C
be a smooth quasi-projective curve. If
U
is a smooth quasi-projective variety, any admissible map

f:U\to C
is algebraic by Theorem
2.2
below and by
[
Shafarevich2
, Thm. 8.5]
. Conversely,
any surjective algebraic morphism
f:U\to C
with connected generic fibers is admissible. Thus, in the
quasi-projective setting, Problem
1.1
asks about the algebro-geometric realization of epimorphisms of quasi-projective groups to curve groups with finitely generated kernel.

Remark 1.4
.

Let us clarify the use of the phrase “up to isomorphism in the target” in Problem
1.1
.
Note that
G
is an abstract group which is not canonically identified with the fundamental group of a particular
curve with respect to a particular base point. Also note that not any identification between
G
and the
fundamental group of a curve will yield an algebraically realizable epimorphism. For example, the general curve
of genus
g\geq 3
is automorphism free. However, the extended mapping class group of a smooth projective curve

C
of genus
g\geq 3
(which is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Out}(\pi_{1}(C))
by the Dehn-Nielsen-Baer theorem) is an
infinite group. Thus, there are automorphisms of the fundamental group of a general curve of genus
g\geq 3

which cannot be realized algebraically.

Since the spaces are path connected, a change of base point in
U
induces isomorphisms that are compatible
with the geometric homomorphism
F_{*}
, so there is no need to specify the base point in the
Geometric Realizability Problem
1.1
(see Remark
3.1
).

Let
C_{g,r}
denote a smooth quasi-projective curve of genus
g
with
r
punctures.
The following result, due to Catanese
[
Catanese-Fibred
]
, which builds on contributions
by other authors (see
[
Siu-strong
,
Bauer-irrational
]
), summarizes the state of the art regarding the
Geometric Realizability Problem
1.1

for compact Kähler manifolds with respect to fundamental groups of compact curves
C_{g,0}
of general
type (
g\geq 2
) and for proper Zariski open sets in compact Kähler manifolds with respect to free groups of
rank
s\geq 2
.

Theorem 1.5

(Catanese)
.

Let
U
be a compact Kähler manifold (resp. a proper Zariski open set in a compact Kähler manifold) and
let
\psi:\pi_{1}(U)\to G
be an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, where
G
is the fundamental group
of a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
(resp. a free group
\mathbb{F}_{s}
with
s\geq 2
). Then there
exists an admissible map
F:U\to C_{g}
, where
C_{g}
is a smooth projective curve of genus
g
(resp.

F:U\to C_{g,r}
, where
C_{g,r}
is a smooth quasi-projective curve of genus
g
with
r=s+1-2g
points removed)
with no multiple fibers such that
\psi
coincides with
F_{*}
up to isomorphism in the target.

The proof if
U
is compact can be found in
[
Catanese-Fibred
, Theorem 4.3]
, and the non-compact case
follows from
[
Catanese-Fibred
, Lemma 3.2, Theorem 5.4]
. Note that Theorem
1.5

does not address the Geometric Realizability Problem
1.1
in the mixed case
where
U
is non-compact and
G
is the fundamental group of a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 2
,
where an epimorphism
\psi:\pi_{1}(U)\to G
with finitely generated kernel could in principle be realized by an
admissible map. Also note that Theorem
1.5
does not provide a relationship between the
existence of surjective algebraic morphisms to curves which have multiple fibers and certain quotients of
\pi_{1}(U)
.

The goal of this paper is to address the following generalization of the Geometric Realizability
Problem
1.1
, which uses the language of orbifold fundamental groups and orbifold morphisms.
This generalization includes morphisms with multiple fibers and morphisms from non-compact manifolds to compact
curves.

Problem 1.6

(Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem)
.

Let
U
be a (non-empty, connected) Zariski open subset of a compact Kähler manifold and let

\psi:\pi_{1}(U)\to G
be an epimorphism with finitely generated kernel, where
G
is a group which is
isomorphic to the orbifold fundamental group of a smooth quasi-projective curve with an extra orbifold structure.

Determine under what conditions there exists an admissible map

F:U\to C
to a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
realizing
\psi
, that is, such that, if
C
is endowed with
its maximal orbifold structure with respect to
F
,
\psi
and
F_{*}:\pi_{1}(U)\to G=\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C)
coincide up
to isomorphism in the target.

By an orbifold structure on a smooth quasi-projective curve we simply mean a choice of a finite number of points

M_{+}=\{P_{1},...,P_{n}\}\subset C
and an integer label
m_{i}\geq 2
at each point
P_{i}\in M_{+}
.
The orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C)
is defined based on these choices (see Definition
2.9

and Remark
2.10
).
A morphism from a quasi-projective manifold
U
to an orbifold curve
C
is a way to encode the
multiple fibers of
F
, that is,
F
is required to have a multiple fiber at
P_{i}
of multiplicity
(a multiple of)
m_{i}
(see Definition
2.11
). The finitely generated kernel assumption
on
\psi
is necessary for the existence of one such
F
, as seen in Remark
2.33
.

Remark 1.7
.

It is known that, if
F:U\to C
is an admissible map to a smooth quasi-projective curve, then
there exists a finitely generated normal subgroup
K\trianglelefteq\pi_{1}(U)
such that

\pi_{1}(U)/K\cong\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C)
for some orbifold structure on
C

(see Remarks
2.4
,
2.12
and
2.33
in the preliminaries
Section
2
). The Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6

addresses not only the converse of this statement, but also whether or not the quotient map

\pi_{1}(U)\twoheadrightarrow\pi_{1}(U)/K
is realized by an admissible map to a curve.

If
G
is a finitely generated free product of cyclic groups (i.e. the orbifold fundamental group of a
non-compact curve), Problem
1.6
was addressed by the authors in the case
where
\psi
is an isomorphism in
[
ji-Eva-orbifold
]
.

Theorem 1.8

(
[
ji-Eva-orbifold
, Theorem 1.3, Remark 1.5]
)
.

Let
U
be a connected proper Zariski open set in a compact Kähler manifold. Let
s,n\geq 0
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}\geq 2
. Suppose that
\pi_{1}(U)\cong\mathbb{F}_{s}*\mathbb{Z}_{m_{1}}*\ldots*\mathbb{Z}_{m_{n}}
, and that
\pi_{1}(U)
is an infinite group. Then, there exists a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
of genus
g
with
r
points removed and an admissible map
F:U\to C
such that

•

2g+r-1=s
,

•

F
has exactly
n
multiple fibers, with multiplicities
m_{1},\ldots,m_{n}
.

•

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C)
is an isomorphism, where
C
is endowed with its maximal
orbifold structure with respect to
F
, corresponding to the tuple of multiplicities
\bar{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
.

In order to identify a class of groups for which to solve the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
, we consider the following definition.

Definition 1.9

(Orbifold Euler characteristic)
.

Let
C_{g}
be a smooth projective curve of genus
g
, and let
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}

denote the orbifold structure on the curve
C_{g}
with
r
punctures and
n
multiple points of
multiplicities
\bar{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
. The orbifold Euler characteristic of
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}

is the quantity

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}:=2-2g-r-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}}\right).

Let
g,r,n\geq 0
, and let
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
be the orbifold fundamental group of
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
.
We refer to these groups as
curve orbifold groups
. The class of curve orbifold groups
contains interesting families of groups, such as all finitely generated free products of cyclic groups
(for
r\geq 1
, see Remark
2.16
) and all triangle groups (for
g=0
,
r=0
and
n=3
,
see Remark
2.17
).

Remark 1.10
.

If
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is not a finite cyclic group, the quantity
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is an invariant
of the isomorphism class of the group
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
. This follows from Remark
2.16
and
Theorem
2.26
. Moreover, if
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is a finite cyclic group, then

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}>0
(see List
2.1
). Hence, the sign of
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is an invariant
of the isomorphism class of the group
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
.

The main result of the paper is the following theorem (see Theorem
3.5
for more details), which unifies and extends Theorems
1.5
and
1.8
.

Theorem 1.11
.

The Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem has a positive answer for the curve orbifold groups
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
such that

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
. Moreover, the multiple fibers of
F
are determined by
\bar{m}
, and one such
F
is uniquely determined up to isomorphism of algebraic varieties in the target.

Note that, unlike Theorem
1.11
, Theorem
1.8
also includes the cases where the orbifold fundamental group is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}
or to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(which have
0
orbifold Euler characteristic), but it needs the extra assumption that
\psi
is an isomorphism.

Remark 1.12
.

All the curve orbifold groups satisfying that
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
are infinite groups (see Remarks
2.16
and
2.20
).
The condition
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
is quite general, see List
2.1
in Section
2.4
for the list of groups
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
not satisfying that

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
if
r=0
, and Remark
2.16
if
r\geq 1
.

In particular,
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
for every finitely generated free product of cyclic groups other than
\mathbb{Z}
,

\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
, or the finite cyclic groups. Also,
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
for every hyperbolic triangle group (the triangle groups not mentioned in List
2.1
).

Remark 1.13
.

Note that, if
C
is a smooth quasi-projective curve of genus
g
with
r
punctures without an
orbifold structure (
\bar{m}=\emptyset
), then the condition
\chi_{g,(r,\emptyset)}=\chi(C)<0
is known in
the literature as a curve of
general type
.

The structure of this paper is as follows. Section
2
contains some preliminaries in both geometry (Kähler manifolds, characteristic varieties, orbifolds and the exact sequence in fundamental groups associated to an admissible map) and group theory (curve orbifold groups and the
ninf
property) which are necessary for the proof of the main theorem. As we already mentioned, the main result of this paper, Theorem
1.11
, yields that the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
has a positive answer for curve orbifold groups with negative orbifold Euler characteristic. This is proved in Section
3
(Theorem
3.5
). In Section
4
we show that the main theorem is sharp, in the sense that the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
does not have a positive answer for any curve orbifold group with non-negative orbifold Euler characteristic. More concretely, for each curve orbifold group
G
with non-negative orbifold Euler characteristic, we find a quasi-projective variety
U
and an epimorphism
\psi:\pi_{1}(U)\to G
with finitely generated kernel which cannot be realized geometrically by an admissible map. In Section
5
we discuss some interesting examples satisfying the hypotheses of the main theorem, as well as their realizations by admissible maps. Finally, in Section
6
, we apply the main theorem to determine an infinite family of curve orbifold groups which cannot appear as fundamental groups of plane curve complements in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
. The full characterization problem is solved except for a special
class of triangle groups
\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}
, where
\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
and
\gcd(m_{1}m_{2},m_{3})\geq 6

(see the table in Figure
). This is
an interesting problem since all curve orbifold groups are quasi-projective (see Proposition
2.18
).

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Zariski open sets in compact Kähler manifolds

In this section we summarize some well-known properties of Zariski open sets inside compact Kähler manifolds and
of morphisms between them which will be used throughout the paper. We include references and/or justifications
for completeness and in order to fix notation.

Remark 2.1

(Resolution of singularities)
.

Hironaka’s resolution of singularities is also available in the complex analytic setting (also in the embedded case),
see
[
Wlodarczyk
, Theorems 2.0.1 and 2.0.2]
. In particular, if
U
is a Zariski open subset inside of a compact
Kähler manifold, then it can also be seen as the complement of a simple normal crossings divisor in another compact
Kähler manifold.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open set inside a compact Kähler manifold
X
, let
C
be a smooth quasi-projective
curve, and let
f:U\to C
be a surjective holomorphic map. Let
\overline{C}
be a smooth (projective)
compactification of
C
. Then, there exists a compactification
\widehat{X}
of
U
such that
\widehat{X}

is a compact Kähler manifold,
\widehat{X}\setminus U
is a simple normal crossings divisor, and
f
extends
to a holomorphic map
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}\to\overline{C}
.

Proof.

By
[
Griffiths-ZariskiOpen
, Corollary A]
,
f
defines a meromorphic map
f:X\dashrightarrow\overline{C}
.
By
[
Remmert
]
(see also
[
Ueno-book
, Theorem 2.5]
), the set of points of indeterminacy of

f:X\dashrightarrow\overline{C}
has codimension at least 2 on
X
. The proof of the resolution of indeterminacies
in the algebraic setting also works for
f
by Remark
2.1
.
∎

Theorem 2.3

(Stein factorization for maps to curves)
.

Let
X,C
be smooth compact complex manifolds, where
C
is a (necessarily projective) curve, and let
f:X\to C
be a surjective holomorphic map. Then, there exists a smooth projective curve
D
and holomorphic maps
g:X\to D
and
h:D\to C
such that
f=h\circ g
,
h
is a finite map,
g
is surjective and all the fibers of
g
are connected.

Proof.

This is a consequence of the more general statement in
[
GR-sheaves
, p. 219]
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

Let
f:U\to C
be a surjective holomorphic map between a Zariski open subset
U
of a compact Kähler manifold and a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
. Let
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}\to\overline{C}
be an extension of
f
as in Theorem
2.2
. Let
\widehat{f}=h\circ g
be its Stein factorization given by Theorem
2.3
. Then,
g_{|}:U\to g(U)
is an admissible map.

Theorem 2.5

(Verdier’s generic fibration theorem for maps to curves)
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open set in a compact Kähler manifold
X
, let
C
be a smooth quasi-projective curve, and let
f:U\to C
be a surjective holomorphic map. Then, there exists a finite set
B_{f}\subset C
, called the set of atypical values of
f
, such that
f_{|}:f^{-1}(C\setminus B_{f})\to C\setminus B_{f}
is a locally trivial fibration.

Proof.

This is a consequence of
[
Verdier-Sard
, Corollaire 5.1]
applied to a holomorphic enlargement
\widehat{f}:\widehat{X}\to\overline{C}
as in Theorem
2.2
, where
\widehat{X}\setminus U
is a simple normal crossings divisor. Indeed,
[
Verdier-Sard
, Corollaire 5.1]
is stated in the algebraic setting, but the proof given therein works in this setting using the Whitney stratification of
\widehat{X}
given by
U
(the open stratum) and the natural stratification of the simple normal crossings divisor
\widehat{X}\setminus U
.
∎

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open subset inside a compact Kähler manifold
X
, and let
p:\widetilde{U}\to U
be a finite covering map. Then, there exists a compact Kähler manifold
Y
such that
\widetilde{U}\subset Y
is a Zariski open subset.

Proof.

By Remark
2.1
, we may assume that
X\setminus U
is a simple normal crossings divisor. By the Grauert-Remmert theorem
[
GrauertRemmert
]
(see the statement in
[
NambaGerms
, Theorem 1]
),
p
extends to a branched covering
\overline{p}:Z\to X
, where
Z
is a normal variety containing
\widetilde{U}
as a Zariski open subset. Let
\pi:Y\to Z
be a resolution of singularities of
Z
as in Remark
2.1
. Then, by
[
Varouchas
, Proposition 1.3.1 (ii), (v), (vi)]
,
Y
is a compact Kähler manifold containing
\widetilde{U}
as a Zariski open subset.
∎

2.2.
Characteristic varieties

Let
G
be a finitely presented group, and let
X
be a connected finite CW-complex such that
G\cong\pi_{1}(X)
.
Let us denote
H:=H_{1}(X;\mathbb{Z})=G/G^{\prime}
. The space of characters on
G
is a complex torus

\mathbb{T}_{G}:=\Hom(G,\mathbb{C}^{*})=\Hom(H,\mathbb{C}^{*})=H^{1}(X;\mathbb{C}^{*}).

This
\mathbb{T}_{G}
can have multiple connected components, but it only contains one connected torus, which we
denote by
\mathbb{T}_{G}^{\mathbf{1}}
.

Definition 2.7
.

The
k
-th
characteristic variety
of
G
is defined by:

\Sigma_{k}(G):=\{\xi\in\mathbb{T}_{G}\mid\dim H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C}_{\xi})\geq k\},

where
H^{1}(X,\mathbb{C}_{\xi})
is classically called the

twisted cohomology of
X
with coefficients in the local system
\xi\in\mathbb{T}_{G}
.
It is also customary to use
\Sigma_{k}(Y)
for
\Sigma_{k}(G)
whenever
\pi_{1}(Y)\cong G
.

In order to prove Theorem
1.11
, we will use the following result of Arapura relating the
(first) characteristic variety of
U
with the existence of admissible maps from
U
to curves of general type
(see Remark
1.13
).

Proposition 2.8

(
[
Arapura-geometry
, Prop V.1.7]
)
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open set of a compact Kähler manifold.

•

If
F:U\to C
is an admissible map to a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
of general type,
then
F^{*}(H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C}^{*}))
is a component of
\Sigma_{1}(U)
. Moreover, if there exists another admissible map

F^{\prime}:U\to C^{\prime}
to a smooth quasi-projective curve
C^{\prime}
of general type such that
F^{*}(H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C}^{*}))=(F^{\prime})^{*}(H^{1}(C^{\prime},\mathbb{C}^{*}))
, then there exists a (necessarily unique) algebraic isomorphism
\phi:C\to C^{\prime}
for which
F^{\prime}=\phi\circ F
.

•

Conversely, any positive dimensional
component of
\Sigma_{1}(U)
containing
1
is of the form
F^{*}(H^{1}(C,\mathbb{C}^{*}))
for some admissible map
F:U\to C
to a smooth quasi-projective curve
C
of general type.

2.3.
Orbifolds and orbifold fundamental groups.

Consider a smooth projective curve
C_{g}
of genus
g
and choose a labeling map

\varphi:C_{g}\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
such that
\varphi(P)\neq 1
only for a finite number of points.
In this context, we will refer to
\varphi
as an
orbifold structure on

C_{g}
.

Given the orbifold structure
\varphi
on
C_{g}
, let
M:=\{P\in C_{g}\mid\varphi(P)\neq 1\}=M_{0}\cup M_{+}
, where

M_{0}:=\{P\in C_{g}\mid\varphi(P)=0\}
and
M_{+}=\{P\in C_{g}\mid\varphi(P)\geq 2\}
. This orbifold structure will
also be denoted by
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
, where
r=\#M_{0}
, and, if
M_{+}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
,

\bar{m}=(\varphi(P_{1}),\ldots,\varphi(P_{n}))
.

Definition 2.9

(Orbifold fundamental group)
.

The
orbifold fundamental group
associated with
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
,
denoted by
\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C_{g,(r,\bar{m})})
, is the quotient of

Definition 2.11

(Orbifold morphism)
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open subset of a compact Kähler manifold, and let

be a smooth projective curve
of genus

The following result is well known (see for instance

Remark 2.12
.

Let

2.4.

Definition 2.13

(Curve orbifold group)
.

A curve orbifold group is a group which admits a presentation of the form

(2)

\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}:=\left\langle\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{i},b_{i},x_{j},y_{k},{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}i=1,...,g\\

appear as orbifold fundamental groups of smooth quasi-projective curves, hence the name. More concretely, in the
notation of Remark

\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}\cong\pi_{1}^{\orb}\left((C_{g,r})_{\bar{m}}\right)

Definition 2.14

(Compact and open curve orbifold groups)

If

Remark 2.15
.

By Theorem

Remark 2.16
.

Suppose that

\displaystyle\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}=0\quad

Remark 2.17
.

cco

(3)

\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}:=\left\langle\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{i},b_{i},x_{j},{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}i=1,...,g\\

Remark 2.21
.

Let

(1)

(2)

in (

[
Greenberg
, Theorem 1]

(3)

The order of

is
m_{j}
(
[

]
).

(4)

\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
is not abelian. Indeed, by Lemma

below,

has a normal subgroup of finite index which is isomorphic to the

[

]
for a

(5)

\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
does not have an abelian subgroup of index 2. If it did, it would be

1
), also cyclic. Its intersection with the normal subgroup of
part (
4
) would be a cyclic subgroup of finite index of the fundamental group
of a genus

smooth projective curve, and this is impossible.

(6)

\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
does not have any finite nontrivial normal subgroups. Indeed, parts (
4
) and (

) imply that
\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
is not quasi-abelian (in the notation of
[

]

[

Some of these properties are also shared in the Euclidean case.

Remark 2.22
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
be a Euclidean
cco
group, i.e. a group with

) such that
\chi_{g,\bar{m}}=0
.
Then, the following properties hold:

(1)

The order of

is
m_{j}
: If

(2)

Every element of

The rest of this section contains some statements about curve orbifold groups which are well-known to experts,
although we include the proofs for clarity. The following lemma explains the geometric significance of the
orbifold Euler characteristic of curve orbifold groups.

Lemma 2.24
.

Let
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
be a curve orbifold group. Let
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
be a smooth projective curve of
genus
g
endowed with the orbifold structure corresponding to
r
points of multiplicity
0
and
n
marked
points with multiplicities
\geq 2
given by the tuple
\bar{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
. Let

P_{j}\in C_{g}
be the point associated with the multiplicity
m_{j}
for all
j=1,\ldots,n
, and let
M_{0}
be the
set of points in
C_{g}
with multiplicity
0
. Then, the following hold:

(1)

There exists a finite index normal subgroup
N
of
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
such that
N
doesn’t contain any torsion elements other than the identity.

(2)

N
induces a ramified cover
C^{\prime}\to C_{g}\setminus M_{0}
ramified along
\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
, where
C^{\prime}
is
a smooth quasi-projective curve such that
\pi_{1}(C^{\prime})\cong N
. Moreover,
C^{\prime}
is projective if and only if
r=0
,
and quasi-projective and non-compact otherwise.

(3)

The branching divisor of this cover is
\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}P_{j}
, unless perhaps if
r=0
and

\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}=\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is a spherical
cco
group.

Let
\rho
be defined as follows:

\rho:=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\text{genus of }C^{\prime}&\text{ if $r=0$},\\
\text{rank of $\pi_{1}(C^{\prime})$, which is a free group}&\text{ if $r\geq 1$.}\end{array}\right.

Then,

•

\rho\geq 2

\Leftrightarrow

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
.

•

\rho=1

\Leftrightarrow

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}=0
.

•

\rho=0

\Leftrightarrow

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}>0
.

Proof.

The existence of
N
is known as Fenchel’s conjecture, proved in
[
Nielsen-commutator
]
for
r>0
,
in
[
BundgaardNielsen
]
for
g>0
and in
[
Fox-Fenchel
]
for
g=0
and
r=0
.

We use facts about branched covers that can be found in
[
Uludag
, Section 1]
. The
normal subgroup
N
yields a finite Galois cover
C^{\prime}\to C_{g}\setminus M_{0}
branched along
M_{+}=\{P_{1},\ldots,P_{n}\}
,
where
\pi_{1}(C^{\prime})=N
. In fact, the existence of such
N
is equivalent to the
existence of such a cover. Note that
C^{\prime}
is a normal curve, so it is smooth.
C^{\prime}
is compact if and only if

C_{g}\setminus M_{0}
is compact, which happens if and only if
r=0
.

The branching divisor is of the form
\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tilde{m}_{j}P_{j}
, with

1\leq\tilde{m}_{j}\leq m_{j}
, and
m_{j}=\tilde{m}_{j}
if and only if
x_{j}^{m}\neq 1\in\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}

for all
1\leq m<m_{j}
. Hence, by Remark
2.21
part (
3
) and
Remark
2.22
part (
1
), the branching divisor is of the form

\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{j}P_{j}
if
r=0
and
\mathbb{G}_{g,(0,\bar{m})}
is hyperbolic or Euclidean. If
r\geq 1
,

\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is a free product of cyclic groups and we also obtain that
\tilde{m}_{j}=m_{j}
.

Let
d
be the degree of the cover
C^{\prime}\to C_{g}\setminus M_{0}
, which is the index of
N\trianglelefteq\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
.
The rest of the statements follow from the following two equations. If
r=0
,

2-2\rho=\chi(C^{\prime})=d(\chi(C_{g}\setminus M_{+}))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d}{\tilde{m}_{j}}\geq d\,\chi_{g,\bar{m}},

which, if
\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
is hyperbolic or Euclidean, is an equality. If
r\geq 1
,

1-\rho=\chi(C^{\prime})=d(\chi(C_{g}\setminus\left(M_{0}\cup M_{+}\right)))+\sum_{j=1}^{n}\frac{d}{m_{j}}=d\left(2-2g-r-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(1-\frac{1}{m_{j}}\right)\right)=d\,\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}.

∎

Remark 2.25

It follows from the proof of Lemma

Let

The equality

m_{n}=m^{\prime}_{n}

is not a cyclic groups and the result follows by induction.
Hence, it suffices to prove that, if
\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}\cong\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime},m_{3}^{\prime})}
are
hyperbolic for some
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
,
m_{3}
,
m_{1}^{\prime}
,
m_{2}^{\prime}
,
m_{3}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}
with
m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq m_{3}
and

m_{1}^{\prime}\leq m_{2}^{\prime}\leq m_{3}^{\prime}
, then
m_{i}=m_{i}^{\prime}
for all
i=1,2,3
. We already know that
m_{3}=m_{3}^{\prime}
. By
Remark
2.25

(4)

\frac{1}{m_{1}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}}=\frac{1}{m_{1}^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{m_{2}^{\prime}}.

Also, since
\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1},m_{2})}\cong\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})}
, List
2.1
implies that

\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=\gcd(m_{1}^{\prime},m_{2}^{\prime})
.
Let
d
be this greatest common divisor, and let
n_{i}=\frac{m_{i}}{d}
and
n_{i}^{\prime}=\frac{m_{i}^{\prime}}{d}
for
i=1,2
. Equation (
4
) yields that

\frac{n_{1}+n_{2}}{n_{1}n_{2}}=\frac{n_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2}^{\prime}}{n_{1}^{\prime}n_{2}^{\prime}},

and since both fractions are reduced,

(5)

n_{1}+n_{2}=n_{1}^{\prime}+n_{2}^{\prime},\quad n_{1}n_{2}=n_{1}^{\prime}n_{2}^{\prime}.

Since
\gcd(n_{1},n_{2})=\gcd(n_{1}^{\prime},n_{2}^{\prime})=1
, the last equation in (
5
) implies that there exist pairwise coprime positive integers
p,q,r,s
such that
n_{1}=pq
,
n_{2}=rs
,
n_{1}^{\prime}=pr
,
n_{2}^{\prime}=qs
, in which case the first equation in (
5
) implies that
p(q-r)=s(q-r)
, which can only happen if
q=r=1
or if
p=s=1
. Hence,
n_{i}=n_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,2
, which implies
m_{i}=m_{i}^{\prime}
for
i=1,2
.
∎

Lemma 2.27
.

Curve orbifold groups are Hopfian.

Proof.

Let
G
be a curve orbifold group. By Lemma
2.24
,
G
contains a finite index normal subgroup
N
which is either a free group or the fundamental group of a smooth projective curve of genus
g\geq 0
. In particular,
N
is residually finite, so
G
is also residually finite and, since
G
is finitely generated, it is Hopfian (cf.
[
Stillwell-combinatorial
, Thm. 4.10]
).
∎

2.5.
Property
ninf

The notion of
ninf
groups is due to Catanese and it will be very useful
in the rest of the paper. In order to set definitions and basic consequences, we present a brief summary
of this property following
[
Catanese-Fibred
]
.

Definition 2.28
.

A group
G
satisfies property
ninf
if every normal non-trivial subgroup of infinite index is not finitely generated.

Example 2.29
.

The free group
\mathbb{F}_{s}
satisfies property
ninf
for any
s\geq 0
(
[
Catanese-Fibred
, Lemma 3.3]
).
Also,
\mathbb{G}_{g}
satisfies property
ninf
for any
g\geq 2
(
[
Catanese-Fibred
, Lemma 3.4]
).

The following result exemplifies the usefulness of the
ninf
property.

Lemma 2.30

(Lemma 3.2 in
[
Catanese-Fibred
]
)
.

Let
G
be an infinite group satisfying property
ninf
, and let
1\to A\to B\xrightarrow{\varphi}C\to 1

be a short exact sequence of groups such that
A
is finitely generated,
C
is infinite and
\varphi
factors
as
\varphi=\rho\circ\psi
, where
\psi:B\to G
is surjective. Then,
\rho:G\to C
is an isomorphism.

Lemma 2.31
.

Let
G
be a curve orbifold group which is not a Euclidean
cco
group. Then,
G
satisfies property
ninf
.

Proof.

If
G
is a spherical
cco
group, the result is trivial because
G
is finite.
Hence, from now on, we may assume that
G
is an infinite curve orbifold group which is not a Euclidean
cco
group. Note that, in those cases,
G
does not contain any finite normal subgroups: if
G
is a hyperbolic
cco
group, this follows from Remark
2.21
, part (
6
), and if
G
is a finite free product of cyclic groups, the result follows from the Kurosh subgroup theorem.

We start by showing that, if
G
is an infinite curve orbifold group, then it
has a normal subgroup
N
of finite index satisfying property
ninf
such that every non-trivial
normal subgroup
H
satisfies that
N\cap H
is non-trivial. We distinguish two cases. By Lemma
2.24
, there exists a finite index normal subgroup
N
of
G
which contains no torsion elements. Moreover, if
G
is an open curve orbifold group (i.e. a finitely generated free product of cyclic groups), then
N
is a free group of finite rank, and if
G
is a hyperbolic
cco
group, then
N\cong\mathbb{G}_{g}
for some
g\geq 2
. In both of these cases,
N
satisfies property
ninf
. Now, let
H
be a non-trivial normal subgroup of
G
. If
N\cap H
was trivial, then there would exist a monomorphism from
H
to the finite group
G/N
, and we obtain a contradiction.

Let
H
be a non-trivial normal subgroup of
G
of infinite index, and let
N
be as in the previous paragraph. Since
N\cap H
is a normal subgroup of
N
of infinite index, the subgroup
N\cap H

must be infinitely generated. Consider the short exact sequence

1\to N\cap H\to H\to H/N\cap H\to 1,

where
H/N\cap H
is finite. If
H
was finitely generated, so would
N\cap H
, so
H
is infinitely generated.
∎

2.6.
The exact sequence associated to an admissible map

This result is well known in different settings (cf.
[
Catanese-Fibred
, Lemma 4.2]
,

[
ji-Eva-orbifold
, Corollary 2.22]
), but we include it here for the sake of completeness and to unify notation.

Lemma 2.32
.

Let
U
be a Zariski open subset of a compact Kähler manifold, let
C_{g}
be a smooth projective curve of genus

g
and let
C_{g,r}
an
r
-punctured

. Consider

an admissible map.
Let
C_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
be the maximal orbifold structure of

with respect to

for some

\bar{m}=(m_{1},\dots,m_{n})
. Then, the following sequence is exact

\pi_{1}(F^{-1}(P))\to\pi_{1}(U)\xrightarrow{F_{*}}\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C_{g,(r,\bar{m})})\to 1,

where the first arrow is induced by the inclusion of a generic fiber
F^{-1}(P)
over
P\in C_{g,r}
.

Proof.

The case where

is proved in
[
ji-Eva-orbifold
, Corollary 2.22]
.

Suppose that

. Let
B_{F}
be the (finite) set of atypical values of
F
, as in Theorem
2.5
.
Let
Q\in C_{g}\setminus(B_{F}\cup\{P\})
and let
U_{Q}=U\setminus F^{-1}(Q)
. Consider the commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(F^{-1}(P))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(U_{Q})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C_{g,(1,\bar{m})})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(F^{-1}(P))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}(U)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C_{g,(0,\bar{m})})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\cong}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}}

where
\iota^{\prime}:F^{-1}(P)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(U_{Q})
and

\iota:F^{-1}(P)\hookrightarrow\pi_{1}(U)
are the inclusions. Here, the vertical arrows are all induced by inclusion,
and the top row is exact by
[

.
The surjectivity of

in the bottom row follows from the diagram.
Also,
\im(\iota_{*})\subseteq\ker(F_{*}:\pi_{1}(U)\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}(C_{g,(0,\bar{m})}))
.

Let us prove the other inclusion. Since

is a quotient of a normal subgroup of

\pi_{1}(U_{Q})
, it is a normal subgroup of
\pi_{1}(U)
. Using the presentation of

given by

, we see that
\pi_{1}(U_{Q})/\im(\iota_{*}^{\prime})
has a presentation given by

\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+2g}\mid\gamma_{1}^{m_{1}}=1,\ldots,\gamma_{n}^{m_{n}}=1\rangle

such that

(1)

, for

(2)

, and

Thus, to get from

to
\pi_{1}(U)
, one needs to quotient by the normal closure of the subgroup generated by

\pi_{1}(U)/\im(\iota_{*})
is isomorphic to

\langle\gamma_{1},\ldots,\gamma_{n+2g}\mid\gamma_{1}^{m_{1}}=1,\ldots,\gamma_{n}^{m_{n}}=1,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{g}[\gamma_{n+2i-1},\gamma_{n+2i}]\right)\left(\gamma_{1}\cdot\ldots\cdot\gamma_{n}\right)^{-1}=1\rangle,

∎

Remark 2.33
.

Let

be an admissible map, where

is a Zariski open set of a compact Kähler manifold and

Let us see that there is no algebraic morphism

(3,3,3)

(2,4,4)

(2,3,6)

The open curve orbifold groups with
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}=0

\mathbb{Z}

Example 4.2

(
\mathbb{Z}
)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve, so
\pi_{1}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and let
\psi:\pi_{1}(E)\to\mathbb{Z}
be an epimorphism.
Hence,
\ker\psi\cong\mathbb{Z}
. However, since
E
is compact and
\mathbb{Z}
is not a
cco

\psi
cannot
be realized by an algebraic morphism from

to a curve.

Example 4.3

(
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
)
.

By
[
FriedmanMorgan
, Theorem II.2.3]
, there exists a smooth projective variety
X
such that

\pi_{1}(X)\cong\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2,2)}
. Consider the group
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
and denote by
\alpha
a generator
of the first free factor, and
\beta
a generator of the second free factor. Consider the presentation
of
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2,2)}
of equation (
3
). The rule
x_{1},x_{2}\mapsto\alpha
,
x_{3},x_{4}\mapsto\beta

defines an epimorphism
\psi:\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2,2)}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
which cannot be realized by an algebraic
map
F
from
X
to a curve with an extra orbifold structure, since

is compact and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}

is not a
cco

is the cyclic group generated by

, which is
finitely generated.

We have shown that the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
does not have a positive answer for curve orbifold groups
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
with
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}=0
, which, by Remark
2.20
, are all infinite groups. Now, let us show it for the groups with
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}>0
. These are the spherical
cco
groups appearing in List
2.1
, which are all finite.

Example 4.4

(Finite cyclic groups)
.

Let
E
be an elliptic curve, so
\pi_{1}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Consider any epimorphism
\psi:\pi_{1}(E)\to\mathbb{Z}_{k}
, for
k\geq 1
. Suppose that
\psi
is realized geometrically. Finite cyclic groups can be both open and compact curve orbifold groups, but since
E
is projective, then a geometric realization of
\psi
would need to be given by an admissible map
F:E\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
, which cannot exist since
E
and
\mathbb{P}^{1}
are not birationally equivalent curves.

Example 4.5

(Spherical
cco
groups which are not cyclic)
.

G=\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is a spherical
cco
group which is not cyclic if and only if
g=r=0
and
\bar{m}=(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})
with
\sum_{j=1}^{3}\frac{1}{m_{j}}>1
. In that case,

can be generated by two elements, and there exists an epimorphism
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{2}\to G
. Note that the kernel of
\psi
is a finite index subgroup of
\mathbb{F}_{2}
, so it is a finitely generated free group.

Let
U
be an elliptic curve minus a point, and note that
\pi_{1}(U)\cong\mathbb{F}_{2}
. Suppose that
\psi
was realized by an admissible
map
F:U\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since algebraic maps are compactifiable,

would extend to an admissible map from a genus
1
curve to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
,
and, like in the previous example, this is impossible.

Even if the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
does not have a positive answer
for any curve orbifold groups
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
with
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}\geq 0
, it is worth noting that it is still
an interesting problem to find restrictions on either
\psi
or

that could give a positive answer. For example, by
Theorem
1.8
, the Orbifold Geometric Realizability Problem
1.6
has a solution
for
\mathbb{Z}
and for
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{2}
if
\psi
is assumed to be an isomorphism (in fact, the proof also works if the
abelianization of the kernel of
\psi
is finite). In a different direction, the existence of epimorphisms to spherical

cco
groups can yield interesting geometric information: let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a sextic curve, and suppose that there
exists an epimorphism
\psi:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\to D_{6}
to the dihedral group of order
6
.
In
[
Degtyarev-OkaConjectureII
, Theorem 1.1]
Degtyarev shows that this implies that

is of torus-type, i.e.,

C=V(f_{2}^{3}+f_{3}^{2})
, where
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
are homogeneous polynomials of degrees
2
and
3
respectively.
Moreover, when
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
has a special type of singularities (including nodes and cusps), then the existence
of certain roots of the Alexander polynomial of

determine a positive answer to the Orbifold
Geometric Realization Problem
F:U\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{m}}
such that
\chi_{0,(0,\bar{m})}=0

(see
[
ji-Libgober-mw
, Thm. 4.7 and 5.16]
).

We end the section by discussing an interesting example where
\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}>0
(so
\mathbb{G}_{g,(r,\bar{m})}
is finite).

Example 4.6
.

The following is an example of the Orbifold Geometric Realization Problem
1.6

for surjections onto non-abelian
spherical

cco
groups with non-trivial finite kernels. This is given by the tricuspidal quartic
C_{4}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
.
Zariski (
[
Zariski-rational
]
) used a beautiful argument using the Lefschetz hyperplane section
Theorem backwards to calculate the fundamental group of the complement of the dual curve to a rational
nodal curve of degree
d
, since they can be obtained as the plane section of the discriminant variety

\Delta_{d}\subset\mathbb{P}^{3}
of degree
d
homogeneous polynomials in 2 variables, that is, the configuration
space of
d
points in
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Since
C_{4}
can be obtained as the dual of a nodal cubic
C_{3}
, one
obtains

G=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{4})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3}\setminus\Delta_{3})\cong\mathbb{B}_{3}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\cong\langle x,y:xyx=yxy,xy^{2}x=1\rangle

is the finite metacyclic group of order 12.
Changing the set of generators
\alpha=xy
,
\beta=xyx
, one can check that

G\cong\langle\alpha,\beta:\alpha^{2}=\beta^{3}=(\alpha\beta)^{2}\rangle.

Note that
G/\langle\alpha^{2}\rangle
is isomorphic to the spherical group
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,3)}
.
The existence of the admissible map
F:U=\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{4}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{0,(2,2,3)}
is guaranteed by
the following functional equation
f_{3}^{2}+f_{2}^{3}=f_{1}^{2}f_{4}
called quasi-toric decomposition
(see
[
ji-Libgober-mw
]
), where
f_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[x,y,z]
of degree
i
,

C_{4}=\{f_{4}=0\}
, and
L=\{f_{1}=0\}
is the bitangent line to
C_{4}
, whose existence can be deduced
as the dual line of the node of the cubic
C_{3}
. The conic
V(f_{2})
passes through the three cusps
and tangent to
L
at one of the intersections
L\cup C_{4}
. The map
F=[f_{3}^{2}:f_{2}^{3}]
is well
defined on
U
,
F^{*}([0:1])
(resp.
F^{*}([1:0])
) is a multiple fiber of multiplicity 2 (resp. 3).
Moreover,
F^{*}([1:-1])
is also multiple of multiplicity 2. Hence
F
induces an orbifold morphism onto

\mathbb{P}^{1}_{0,(2,2,3)}
as expected. Maximality is immediate by
[
ji-Libgober-mw
, Proposition 2.8]
,
since the pencil is generated by two multiple curves.

By construction, there are at least two such pencils depending on the choice of the tangency at

L\cup C_{4}
resulting in at least two such quasi-toric decompositions. Each decomposition results
in a different morphism. Note that uniqueness of the realization map
F
is only guaranteed if

\chi_{g,(r,\bar{m})}<0
, which is not satisfied in this example, since
\chi_{0,(0,(2,2,3))}=\frac{1}{3}>0
.

Example 5.1

(Maximal cuspidal sextic)
.

A well-known incarnation of Theorem

and a non-trivial finite kernel

Note that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}G&=&\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C_{6})\cong\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{3}\setminus\Delta_{4})\cong\mathbb{B}_{4}(\mathbb{P}^{1})\\

f_{i}
is a homogeneous polynomial in

of degree

, and

. The map

is well defined on

,

F^{*}([0:1])
(resp.

) is a multiple fiber of multiplicity 2 (resp. 3). Maximality is immediate
by
[

Example 5.2

(Irreducible quintic
\mathcal{C}_{5}
such that
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{5})
is infinite and non-abelian)
.

As an application of Theorem
3.5
to projective plane curve complements,
consider
\mathcal{C}_{5}
an irreducible quintic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
whose singularities are
\mathbb{A}_{6}\sqcup 3\mathbb{A}_{2}

In
[
Artal-quintic
]

(12)

G=\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{5})=\langle\alpha,\beta,\gamma,v:v^{5}=\alpha^{2}=\beta^{3}=\gamma^{7}=\alpha\beta\gamma,[v,\alpha]=[v,\beta]=[v,\gamma]=1\rangle.

Note that
v
is a central element and
G/\langle v\rangle\cong\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,7)}
.
Since
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,7)}
is a hyperbolic
cco
group and the kernel of the projection of

onto

G/\langle v\rangle
is cyclic (and thus finitely generated), Theorem
3.5
implies the
existence of a pencil inducing an orbifold morphism
\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{5}\to\mathbb{P}^{1}_{0,(2,3,7)}

In order to construct this pencil, consider a tricuspidal quartic
\mathcal{C}_{4}=\{f_{4}=0\}
together with
its bitangent line

. Denote by
Q_{1}
and
Q_{2}
the bitangencies. One can blow up

and

\tilde{L}_{1}
(the strict transform of

-1

, and

E_{2}
, and

(the second and first exceptional divisors of the blow-ups). Let

is a homogeneous polynomial of degree

,

(Rational cuspidal curves

Example 5.4
.

•

(resp.

Lemma 6.1
.

The only spherical and Euclidean
cco

are
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
and the finite cyclic groups.

Proof.

Note that
\mathbb{Z}^{2}\cong\pi_{1}((\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2})
, and
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
is the complement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
of three non-concurrent lines.
Also, the complement of a smooth curve of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
is cyclic of order
d
. Hence, all that is left to
prove is that the other groups in List
2.1
cannot be realized as the complement of a curve in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
.

Let us compute the abelianization of all of these groups:

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|}\hline\cr\text{Groups}&\text{Abelianization}\\
\hline\cr S_{4}=\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,4)},S_{5}=\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,5)},\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2k+1)}\,\forall k\geq 1&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\\
A_{4}=\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,3)}&\mathbb{Z}_{3}\\
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,6)}&\mathbb{Z}_{6}\\
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2k)}\,\forall k\geq 1&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\\
\mathbb{G}_{0,(3,3,3)}&\mathbb{Z}_{3}\times\mathbb{Z}_{3}\\
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,2,2,2)}&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{2}\\
\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,4,4)}&\mathbb{Z}_{2}\times\mathbb{Z}_{4}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

The fundamental group of a curve complement in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
has a finite abelianization if and only
if the curve is irreducible, in which case the abelianization is cyclic and its order coincides
with the degree of the curve. This rules out the last four cases in the table above. If the
abelianization is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
(resp.
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
), the curve would need to be an irreducible conic
(resp. cubic). The fundamental of the complement of irreducible curves of degrees less than four
is abelian, which rules out all the remaining cases except for
G_{0,(2,3,6)}
.

Suppose that there exists a curve
C
in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
such that
G_{0,(2,3,6)}
. Then,
C
must be an
irreducible sextic. Note that
G_{0,(2,3,6)}\cong\langle x,y\mid x^{2}=y^{3}=(xy)^{6}=1\rangle
surjects
onto the dihedral group of order
6
, sending
x
to a reflection and
y
to a rotation.
By
[
Degtyarev-OkaConjectureII
, Theorem 1.1]

C
is of torus-type, i.e.,
C=V(f_{2}^{3}+f_{3}^{2})
,
where
f_{2}
and
f_{3}
are homogeneous polynomials of degrees
2
and
3
respectively. Since the
generic fiber of the map
F=[f_{2}^{3}:f_{3}^{2}]:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}\setminus\{[1:-1]\}
is irreducible,
it induces an epimorphism (see
[
ACM-multiple-fibers
, Prop. 1.4]
)

F_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{1,(2,3)})

is

[0:1]

and this is impossible, as no such epimorphism exists. Indeed,
\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{3}
surjects onto

\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,6)}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}*\mathbb{Z}_{3}

\mathbb{G}_{0,(2,3,6)}

(for example the commutator group of the first one is isomorphic to

∎

The following result is a corollary of Theorem
3.5
, and it says that there are infinitely many hyperbolic

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
be a plane curve of degree
d
. Suppose that
\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
is a
hyperbolic
cco
group other than
\mathbb{G}_{0,(m_{1},m_{2},m_{3})}
(for some
m_{1}<m_{2}
and
m_{3}
) with

\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1
and
d=\gcd(m_{1}m_{2},m_{3})\geq 6
.
Then,
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that
\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\cong\mathbb{G}_{g,\bar{m}}
, where

\chi_{g,\bar{m}}<0
. By Theorem
3.5
, there
exists an admissible map
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C\to D
onto a compact curve
D
of genus
g

such that
F_{*}:\pi_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C)\to\pi_{1}^{\orb}\left(D_{\bar{m}}\right)
is an isomorphism, and
the orbifold structure
\bar{m}
is maximal. Since
F
defines a surjective rational map
F:\mathbb{P}^{2}\dashrightarrow D
, then
g=0
and
D_{\bar{m}}=\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\bar{m}}
. Note that
H_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C,\mathbb{Z})
is a
finite group, and thus
C
is irreducible, so
H_{1}(\mathbb{P}^{2}\setminus C,\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}_{d}
with
d
the
degree of
C
. Also note that
\chi_{0,\bar{m}}<0
implies

\bar{m}=(m_{1},\ldots,m_{n})
is such that
n\geq 3
.

We may extend
F
to its maximal domain of definition in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, which will be the complement of a finite number of points
\mathcal{B}
. By
[
ji-Libgober-mw
, Proposition 2.8]
,

has at most

multiple fibers. This is only
possible if

and up to reordering of the

of multiplicity
m_{3}

m_{1}

and

must be coprime.

Finally, since
m_{1}
and
m_{2}

group of order

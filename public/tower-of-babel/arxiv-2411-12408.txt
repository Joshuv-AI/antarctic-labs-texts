Monotonous period function for equivariant differential equations with homogeneous nonlinearities
Armengol Gasull, David Rojas

Abstract
We prove that the period function of the center at the origin of the ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -equivariant differential equation z ˙ = i ​ z + a ​ ( z ​ z ¯ ) n ​ z k + 1 , a ≠ 0 , \dot{z}=iz+a(z\overline{z})^{n}z^{k+1},a\neq 0, is monotonous decreasing for all n n and k k positive integers, solving a conjecture about them. \dot{z}=F(z,\overline{z})

The

It is also a family of differential equations given by simply two complex monomials, as the ones studied in

The period function of the center at the origin of the differential equation (

2
The monotonicity criterium

Consider a Hamiltonian system with a Hamiltonian function of the form
H(x,y)={y^{2}}/{2}+V(x)
and integrating factor
\ell(x)
, where both
V
and
\ell
are analytic functions in a neighborhood of
x=0
. That is, the differential system is written as

\dot{x}=-\dfrac{y}{\ell(x)},\;\dot{y}=\dfrac{V^{\prime}(x)}{\ell(x)}.

(5)

If
V^{\prime}(0)=0
and
xV^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\approx 0
,
x\neq 0
, the origin is a center. The period annulus is the largest punctured neighborhood of the origin foliated by periodic orbits and each periodic orbit of the period annulus can be parametrized by an energy level
h\mapsto\gamma(h)\subset\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:H(x,y)=h\}
. The period function of the center assigns to each periodic orbit of the period annulus its minimal period. Due to the previous parameterization and the particular expression of the vector field, the period function can be expressed as an Abelian integral function of the energy,

T(h):=\int_{0}^{T(h)}\,\operatorname{d}\!t=\int_{\gamma(h)}\frac{\ell(x)}{y}\,\operatorname{d}\!x.

(6)

Notice that
T(h)={\bf T}\big(\sqrt{2h}\,\big),
where
{\bf T}
is given in (
4
).

Let us denote by
\mathcal{I}=(x_{\ell},x_{r})
the projection on the
x
-axis of the period annulus of the center at the origin, with
x_{\ell}<0<x_{r}
. Since
xV^{\prime}(x)>0
for all
x\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{0\}
, the potential function
V
defines an analytic involution
\sigma
on
\mathcal{I}
by

V(\sigma(x))=V(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{0\},

and
\sigma(0)=0
. The following criterium is introduced in
[
11
, Prop. 2.2]
as an adaptation of
[
12
, Thm. A]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let us consider an Abelian integral of the form
I(h)=\int_{\gamma_{h}}{g(x)}/{y}\,\operatorname{d}\!x
, where
g(x)
is an analytic function on
\mathcal{I}.
Define

\mathbf{\Pi}_{\sigma}(g)(x):=\frac{f(x)-f(\sigma(x))\sigma^{\prime}(x)}{2},\quad\mbox{where}\quad f(x)=-\frac{g(x)}{2}+\Big(\frac{g(x)V(x)}{V^{\prime}(x)}\Big)^{\prime}.

If
\mathbf{\Pi}_{\sigma}(g)(x)
has no zeros in
(0,x_{r})
then
I^{\prime}(h)
has no zeros.

The period function (
6
) is an Abelian integral of the type requested by the Proposition, but the direct application for proving monotonicity is not possible. It can be seen that, by taking
g=\ell
, the corresponding
\mathbf{\Pi}_{\sigma}(\ell)(x)
changes sign in
(0,x_{r})
. As we will show, in order to prove the monotonicity, an auxiliar Abelian integral with a different function
g
can be used instead.

Study of a family of quadratic reversible systems

The change of variables given by
\{u=x,v=y(1-x)^{-(D+1)}\}
transforms system (
3

\begin{cases}\dot{u}&=-v(1-u)^{D+2},\\
\dot{v}&=u(1+Du)(1-u)^{-(D+1)},\end{cases}

(7)

with first integral
H(u,v)={v^{2}}/{2}+V(u)

V(u):=\frac{1}{2}u^{2}(1-u)^{-2(D+1)}.

Notice that this change is well defined in the whole period annulus of the origin. When
D\in(-1/2,0)
, the projection of the period annulus is
(u_{\ell},u_{r})=(-\infty,1)
and the center is located at

The period function of the center at the origin of (

T(h)=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+2)}}{v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u,

where
\gamma_{h}\subset\{(u,v)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:H(u,v)=h\}
is the closed orbit lying on the level curve
H=h
. Let us define

A(h):=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}(1-u)^{-(D+2)}v\,\operatorname{d}\!u\,\,\text{ and }\,\,I(h):=\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{u(1-u)^{-3(D+1)}}{v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u.

(8)

Lemma 3.2
.

For each
h\in(0,+\infty)
,
A^{\prime}(h)=T(h)
and
2hT^{\prime}(h)+\frac{1}{D+1}I^{\prime}(h)=0
.

Proof.

The exterior derivative of
\alpha:=(1-u)^{-(D+2)}v
is
\operatorname{d}\!\alpha=(1-u)^{-(D+2)}\operatorname{d}\!u\wedge\operatorname{d}\!v
and it admits a Gelfand-Leray form with respect to
H(u,v)
given by

\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\alpha}{\operatorname{d}\!H}=-\frac{(1-u)^{D+1}}{u(1+Du)}\operatorname{d}\!v.

The derivative of the function
A(h)
can be expressed as an integral along the same cycle
\gamma_{h}
according with formula (3) in page 284 of
[
3
]
using the previous Gelfand-Leray form. That is,

A^{\prime}(h)=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{\operatorname{d}\!\alpha}{\operatorname{d}\!H}=\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{(1-u)^{D+1}}{u(1+Du)}\operatorname{d}\!v=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+2)}}{v}\operatorname{d}\!u=T(h),

where the third equality holds by
\operatorname{d}\!H=V^{\prime}(u)\operatorname{d}\!u+v\operatorname{d}\!v=0
on
\gamma_{h}
. This proves the first assertion of the lemma.

Using the identity
H(u,v)=h
, we have
v=\frac{2}{v}(h-V(u))
and so

A(h)=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{2h(1-u)^{-(D+2)}}{v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u+\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{2(1-u)^{-(D+2)}V(u)}{v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u.

We point out that the first integral of the previous expression is related with
T(h)
. That is,

A(h)=2hT(h)+\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{2(1-u)^{-(D+2)}V(u)}{v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u.

(9)

In addition, integrating by parts,

A(h)=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}(1-u)^{-(D+2)}v\,\operatorname{d}\!u=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}v\,\,\operatorname{d}\!\left(\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+1)}}{D+1}\right)=\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+1)}}{D+1}\,\operatorname{d}\!v.

Using again
\operatorname{d}\!H=0
on
\gamma_{h}
we can write the previous as

A(h)=-\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+1)}V^{\prime}(u)}{(D+1)v}\,\operatorname{d}\!u.

(10)

By adding (
9
) and (
10
), and using the expression of
V
,

\displaystyle 2A(h)

\displaystyle=2hT(h)+\int_{\gamma_{h}}\left(2(1-u)^{-(D+2)}V(u)-\frac{(1-u)^{-(D+1)}}{D+1}V^{\prime}(u)\right)\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!u}{v}

\displaystyle=2hT(h)+\int_{\gamma_{h}}\frac{u(1-u)^{-3(D+1)}}{D+1}\frac{\,\operatorname{d}\!u}{v}=2hT(h)+\frac{1}{D+1}I(h).

Derivating with respect to
h
,

2A^{\prime}(h)=2T(h)+2hT^{\prime}(h)+\frac{1}{D+1}I^{\prime}(h).

Since
A^{\prime}(h)=T(h)
by the first assertion of the result, then
2hT^{\prime}(h)+\frac{1}{D+1}I^{\prime}(h)=0,
proving the second assertion of the lemma.
∎

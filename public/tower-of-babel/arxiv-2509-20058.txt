Central limit theorems for random boundary polytopes
Matthias Reitzner, Mathias Sonnleitner

Abstract
The number of faces of the convex hull of n n independent and identically distributed random points chosen on the boundary of a smooth convex body in ℝ d {\mathds{R}}^{d} is investigated. In dimensions two and three the number of k k -faces is known to be constant almost surely and in dimension four and higher the variance is known to be non-zero if k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We show that it is of order n n . This is complemented by a central limit theorem with a Berry-Esseen bound which is of optimal order n − 1 / 2 n^{-1/2} . We derive similar results for the Poissonized model, where additionally the number of random points is Poisson distributed. As a main tool, we develop a representation of the number of faces as a sum of exponentially stabilizing score functions. Keywords . random polytope, random approximation, stochastic geometry, variance expansion, Berry-Esseen bound, central limit theorem MSC 2020 . Primary: 52A22; Secondary: 60D05.

Introduction and main results

Let
K
be a smooth convex body in
{\mathds{R}}^{d}
. Choose
n
independent and identically distributed random points
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
on the boundary
{\partial}K
of
K
, and denote by
P_{n}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
the convex hull of these points. We are interested in the expectation of the number of
k
-faces
{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{n})
of
P_{n}
, their variance
{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})
and their fluctuations, where
k=1,\dots,d-1
. Since explicit results for fixed
n
cannot be expected, we investigate the asymptotics as
n\to\infty
.

There is a vast amount of literature on random polytopes with vertices chosen from the
interior
of a convex body, i.e. a compact convex set with non-empty interior. Investigations started with two classical articles by Rényi and Sulanke
[
37
,
38
]
dealing with polygons and smooth convex bodies in the two-dimensional case. Their results for smooth convex bodies were generalized to higher dimensions by Wieacker
[
49
]
, Schneider and Wieacker
[
42
]
, Bárány
[
1
]
, Schütt
[
43
]
and Böröczky, Hoffmann and Hug
[

]
, and to all intrinsic volumes by Bárány
[
1
,
2
]
and Reitzner
[
33
]
. It turns out that the difference between the intrinsic volume of
K
and the random polytope with uniformly distributed points in the interior of

is asymptotically of order
n^{-\frac{2}{d+1}}
. The corresponding results for polytopes are much more difficult. The results by Rényi and Sulanke for polygons were generalized in a long and intricate proof by Bárány and Buchta
[
3
]
, from which it follows that the order of the volume difference is
n^{-1}(\ln n)^{d-1}
.

Due to Efron’s identity,
[
14
]
, the results on the volume difference can be used to determine the expected number
{\mathds{E}}f_{0}(P_{n})
of vertices of
P_{n}
. More general, asymptotic expressions for the number of
k
-dimensional faces
f_{k}(P_{n})
are due to Wieacker
[
49
]
, Bárány and Buchta
[
3
]
and Reitzner
[
35
]
.
In recent years the results concerning expectations of functionals of the random polytope have been accomplished by variance estimates and central limit theorems, see

[
4
,
5
,
6
,
11
,
12
,
13
,

,

,

,

,
28
,

,

,

,

.

Summarizing the results on expectations and variances for the
f
-vectors we have

\displaystyle{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d,k}\,\Omega(K)\,n^{\frac{d-1}{d+1}}(1+o(1)),

\displaystyle{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d,k}\,\Omega(K)\,n^{\frac{d-1}{d+1}}(1+o(1)),

for
k\in\{0,\dots,d-1\}
, if
K
is a smooth convex body and the random polytope
P_{n}
is the convex hull of
n
independent uniformly distributed random points in the interior of
K
.
(Throughout this paper, we denote by
c_{d,k}
constants only depending on
d
or
k
and by
c,C,c_{0},\dots
constants which may additionally depend on
K
and whose precise values may differ from line to line.)
And if
K
is a polytope, then

\displaystyle{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d,k}\,\operatorname{flag}(P)\,(\ln n)^{{d-1}}(1+o(1)),

\displaystyle{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle c_{d,k}\,\operatorname{flag}(P)\,(\ln n)^{{d-1}}(1+o(1)),

where
{\rm flag}(P)
is the number of flags of
P
. The last equality is known to hold for simple polytopes
K
whereas for general polytopes
K
only the exact order
(\ln n)^{{d-1}}
is known.

In this paper, we are investigating the case that the points
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are uniformly distributed on the boundary of a smooth convex body
K
equipped with
(d-1)
-dimensional Hausdorff measure
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}
. Here, a convex body is smooth,
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
, if its boundary is a twice continuously differentiable manifold with everywhere positive Gaussian curvature
{\kappa}(x)
for
x\in{\partial}K
. Taking the convex hull of the random points gives a random polytope
P_{n}
where, almost surely, all
n
random points are vertices,

f_{0}(P_{n})=n,

because
K
is strictly convex. In dimension two, this implies
f_{1}(P_{n})=n
and thus the combinatorial structure of
P_{n}
is deterministic. This also holds in dimension three, where by Euler’s polytope formula, almost surely,

f_{0}(P_{n})=n,\ f_{1}(P_{n})=3n-6,\ f_{2}(P_{n})=2n-4

holds. Clearly, this shows that
{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})=0
for
k=0
and for all
k
in dimension
d\leq 3
.
It was observed by Stemeseder
[
45
]
that for
d\geq 4
the random variable
f_{k}(P_{n})
is not deterministic for
1\leq k\leq d-1
. In his (unpublished) PhD thesis he proved that

{\mathds{E}}\,f_{k}(P_{n})=c_{d,k}n(1+o(1)),

(1)

and thus, perhaps surprisingly, the asymptotic behavior to first order is independent of
K
. The constant
c_{d,k}
in (
1
) can be expressed in terms of the expected internal angles of random simplices inscribed into the unit sphere, see Kablucko, Thäle and Zaporozhets
[
24
, Remark 1.9]
, and is known explicitly, see
[
21
,
22
]
. In fact, for
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
uniformly distributed on the unit sphere, the expectation
{\mathds{E}}\,f_{k}(P_{n})
itself is known by
[
24
, Thm. 1.3]
but even in this simpler case the variance
{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})
appears to be unknown for
d\geq 4
.

For general
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
, an upper bound for the variance follows immediately from the Efron-Stein jackknife inequality and Theorem 10 in
[
32
]
,

{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})\leq n{\mathds{E}}(f_{k}(P_{n+1})-f_{k}(P_{n}))^{2}\leq Cn.

(2)

A lower bound for the variance is much more involved since the variance vanishes for
k=0
and
d\leq 3
, and it is a priori unclear whether the variance asymptotically vanishes also in other cases.
It is the aim of this contribution to prove the missing lower bound for the variance.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
d\geq 4
and let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independently and uniformly distributed on the boundary of a smooth
d
-dimensional convex body
K\in\mathcal{C}^{2}_{+}
and
P_{n}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. Then there exist constants
c>0
and
n_{0}\in{\mathds{N}}
such that, for every
n\geq n_{0}
and
k=1,\dots,d-1
,

{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})\geq cn.

This variance lower bound is one essential step to prove a central limit theorem. The second ingredient is the exponential decay of the radius of stabilization, which will be defined and discussed in Section
4
. Combined with a quantitative central limit theorem by Lachieze-Rey, Schulte and Yukich
[
25
]
for exponentially stabilizing functionals, which is derived by Stein’s method, this will yield the following central limit theorem for the face numbers of
P_{n}
, which is novel even in the special case of points uniformly distributed on the unit sphere.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
n\geq d+2
. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
be independently and uniformly distributed on the boundary of a smooth
d
-dimensional convex body
K\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}^{+}
and
P_{n}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
. For every
k=1,\dots,d-1
, the rescaled fluctuations of
f_{k}(P_{n})
around
{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{n})
converge in distribution to a standard Gaussian variable
Z\sim{\mathcal{N}}(0,1)
and

d_{K}\Big(\frac{f_{k}(P_{n})-{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{n})}{\sqrt{{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})}},Z\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}},

where
d_{K}(\cdot,Z)=\sup_{t\in\mathds{R}}|\mathds{P}(\cdot\leq t)-\mathds{P}(Z\leq t)|
denotes the Kolmogorov distance to
Z
.

Remark 1.3
.

Since the resulting rate of convergence in Theorem
1.2
is of the same order as
({\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n}))^{-1/2}
and since
f_{k}(P_{n})
is an integer-valued random variable, an argument of Englund
[
15
, Section 6]
shows that, up to numerical constants, our result is in fact optimal.

Remark 1.4
.

All results generalize easily to the case when the random points are sampled on
\partial K
according to a continuous positive density
h
with respect to
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}
. In this case, the constants also depend on the positive numbers
\min_{x\in\partial K}h(x)
and
\max_{x\in\partial K}h(x)
.

Remark 1.5
.

For a different reason, a change in behavior at
d=4
occurs also for the order of magnitude of the expected minimal area of facets of random polytopes inscribed into the sphere, see Leroux, Rademacher, Schütt and Werner
[
27
]
. Yet another such threshold at
d=4
was observed for the shape of fluctuations of the total surface area of a Poisson point process of hyperbolic hyperplanes, see Herold, Hug, Kabluchko, Rosen and Thäle
[
20
,
23
]
.

Corresponding results for the volume difference
V_{d}(K)-V_{d}(P_{n})
and the intrinsic volumes
V_{i}(K)-V_{i}(P_{n})
have already been obtained. First contributions are due to Buchta, Müller and Tichy
[
10
]
, Schneider
[
40
]
, Schütt and Werner
[
44
]
, Reitzner
[
31
]
and Böröczky, Fodor, and Hug
[
8
]
who investigated the expectation of the volume difference, respectively intrinsic volume difference. Bounds for the variance and central limit theorems are due to Reitzner
[
32
]
, Richardson, Vu, and Wu
[
39
]
, Thäle
[
46
]
and Turchi and Wespi
[
48
]
. Generalizations to other geometries are by Besau, Rosen and Thäle
[
7
]
.

If the underlying convex body
K
is a polytope and the random polytope
P_{n}
is the convex hull of uniform random points on the boundary, the situation is much more complicated. It is immediate that in this case even
f_{0}(P_{n})
is a random variable, and only recently the expectation of
f_{0}(P_{n}),f_{d-1}(P_{n})
and
V_{d}(K)-V_{d}(P_{n})
have been obtained by Reitzner, Schütt and Werner
[
36
]
. The other face numbers, intrinsic volumes, variance asymptotics or central limit theorems seem to be out of reach at the moment. Even less is known for the Hausdorff distance between
K
and
P_{n}
, see Prochno, Schütt, Sonnleitner and Werner
[
30
]
.

We want to remark that there is a modified model including a further randomization, where instead of taking
n
random points, the number of points
N
is Poisson distributed with mean
t
. Then
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
is distributed like a Poisson point process. In particular, restrictions to disjoint parts are then independent from each other. This additional Poissonization turned out to be a powerful method to derive variance bounds and limit theorems for the random polytope model with uniform points inside the convex body
K
, and de-Poissonization methods yielded limit theorems for fixed
n=t
. For our model with points on
{\partial}K
, this Poissonized model was investigated by Richardson, Vu, and Wu
[
39
]
and Stemeseder
[
45
]
for the volume difference between
K
and
P_{N}
. This method with subsequent de-Poissonization will not work in our case because the additional variance from Poissonization is of the same order as the variance of
f_{k}(P_{n})
itself. This rules out the possiblity to prove limit theorems for the random polytopes generated by random points on
{\partial}K
with the same method as for random polytopes generated by uniform points in
K
. This seems to be the reason why there are no results for
f_{k}(P_{N})
even in the Poisson setting.

As a consequence of our results, we obtain variance bounds and a central limit theorem for the Poisson boundary polytope.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
d\geq 4
and
N\sim\pi(t)
be Poisson distributed with parameter
t>1
. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
be independently and uniformly distributed on the boundary of a smooth
d
-dimensional convex body
K\in{\mathcal{C}}_{2}^{+}
and
P_{N}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{N}]
. There exists
t_{0}>1
such that, for every
t\geq t_{0}
and
k=1,\dots,d-1
,

c\,t\leq{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{N})\leq C\,t

(3)

and

d_{K}\Big(\frac{f_{k}(P_{N})-{\mathds{E}}f_{k}(P_{N})}{\sqrt{{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{N})}},Z\Big)\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{t}}.

(4)

2
Background material

2.1
Polytopes with
d+2
vertices

We introduce some basic notation. For a
d
-dimensional polytope
P
we denote by
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(P)
the set of its
k
-dimensional faces and write
f_{k}(P)
for the cardinality of
{\mathcal{F}}_{k}(P)
. The
f
-vector of
P
is given by
(f_{0}(P),\dots,f_{d-1}(P))
. The affine span of a facet
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{d-1}(P)
is an affine hyperplane
H_{F}
and we denote by
H_{F}^{+}
the corresponding closed half-space meeting
P
only in its boundary and by
H_{F}^{-}
the closed half-space containing
P
.

To construct polytopes with few vertices, the beyond-beneath construction can be employed. A comprehensive account of this method and convex polytopes in general is Grünbaum’s book
[
17
]
. A point
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}
is said to be beyond
F
if it lies in
\Int H_{F}^{+}
and beneath
F
if it is in
\Int H_{F}^{-}
. Equivalently,
x
is beyond
F
if it sees
\relint F
(the relative interior of
F
is taken with respect to
H_{F}
), that is, if for any
y\in\relint F
the line segment
[x,y]
does not intersect
\Int P
.

The combinatorial type classifies polytopes according to their combinatorial structure. Two polytopes have the same combinatorial type if there exists an inclusion-preserving one-to-one correspondence between their sets of faces. In particular, then their
f
-vectors are identical. Trivially, the convex hull of
d+1
affinely independent points is always a
d
-simplex and of one type. Adding one more point gives rise to
\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
combinatorial types
{\mathcal{T}}_{j}^{d}
,
j=1,\dots,\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, satisfying

f_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{j}^{d})=\binom{d+2}{d-k+1}-\underbrace{\binom{j+1}{d-k+1}}_{k\geq d-j}-\underbrace{\binom{d-j+1}{d-k+1}}_{k\geq j},\qquad k=1,\dots,d-1.

In particular, for
d\leq 3
there is only one type
{\mathcal{T}}^{d}_{1}
but in dimension
d\geq 4
there exists
{\mathcal{T}}^{d}_{2}
with

f_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{1}^{d})<f_{k}({\mathcal{T}}_{2}^{d}),\qquad k=1,\dots,d-1.

(5)

There is a precise description of these types. A polytope
P
with
d+2
vertices is the convex hull of a
d
-simplex
S
and a point
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus S
and is of type
{\mathcal{T}}^{d}_{j}
for

j=\bey(x,S):=|\{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{d-1}(S)\colon x\text{ is beyond }F\}|\in\{1,\dots,d-1\},

if
j\leq\lfloor\frac{d}{2}\rfloor
, and of type
{\mathcal{T}}^{d}_{d-j}
, otherwise. Note that by Theorem 5.2.1 in
[
17
]
,
\bey(x,S)=d
implies
P=S
, which is not possible, and that
\bey(x,S)=0
if and only if
x\in S
. Given a
d
-simplex
S
the regions

S(j)=\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus S\colon\bey(x,S)=j\},\qquad j=1,\dots,d-1,

(6)

partition
\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus S
into polyhedral open sets whose closures cover
\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus S
and which meet at the vertices of
S
. Given
x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\setminus S
, the type of
[S,x]
is determined by the region
S(j)
it belongs to (and undetermined if
x
belongs to multiple regions).

Remark 2.1
.

Almost surely, the random polytopes we consider are simplicial, that is, all of their faces are simplices. By the Dehn-Sommerville equations, which generalize Euler’s polytope formula, the
f
-vectors of simplicial polytopes span an affine space of dimension
\lfloor\frac{1}{2}d\rfloor
. Thus, fixing the number of vertices leaves no degree of freedom in dimensions three or less but one degree in dimensions four and five, and so on.

2.2
Smooth convex bodies

Let
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
, where
{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
is the set of convex bodies in
\mathds{R}^{d}
with twice continuously differentiable boundary and everywhere positive Gaussian curvature. In the following, we collect implications of the assumption
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
on the surface area (
d-1
-dimensional Hausdorff measure) of caps and balls in
\partial K
.

For a unit vector
u\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and
t\in\mathds{R}
, we denote by
H(u,t)
the affine hyperplane
\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\colon\langle x,u\rangle=t\}
and by
H^{+}(u,t)
the corresponding halfspace
\{x\in\mathds{R}^{d}\colon\langle x,u\rangle\geq t\}
. The support function
h_{K}(u)
of
K
at
u
is given by
h_{K}(u)=\sup_{x\in K}\langle x,u\rangle
and
H(u,h_{K}(u))
is a supporting hyperplane. Since
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
, the intersection of
K
with
H^{+}(u,h_{K}(u))
is a single point
y
on the boundary
{\partial}K
and
u_{y}=u
is the outward unit normal of
K
at
y
. Denote by

C^{{\partial}K}(y,h)={\partial}K\cap H^{+}(u_{y},h_{K}(u_{y})-h)

a non-solid cap of
K
with center
y\in{\partial}K
and height
h>0
. Replacing
{\partial}K
by
K
we obtain the corresponding solid cap. For the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we need the following construction of pairwise disjoint caps on
{\partial}K
of equal height and comparable surface area.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
. There are constants
c,C>0
such that for any
n\in\mathds{N}
we find pairwise disjoint caps
C^{{\partial}K}(y_{1},h_{n}),\dots,C^{\partial K}(y_{n},h_{n})
with
y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\in\partial K
and

c\,n^{-\frac{2}{d-1}}\leq h_{n}\leq C\,n^{-\frac{2}{d-1}}.

Moreover,

c\,n^{-1}\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(C^{{\partial}K}(y_{i},h_{n})\big)\leq C\,n^{-1}.

Lemma
2.2
is derived from the construction in Lemma 6 in
[
34
]
and the following well-known asymptotics for the surface area of (non-solid) caps of smooth convex bodies, see e.g.
[
39
, Lemma 6.2]
and
[
34
, Lemma 5]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{C}}_{+}^{2}
. Then, for any
x\in{\partial}K
and
h\leq\mathrm{diam}(K)
,

c\,h^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(C^{{\partial}K}(x,h)\big)\leq C\,h^{\frac{d-1}{2}},

and for
r\leq\mathrm{diam}(K)
,

c\,r^{d-1}\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(B(x,r)\cap{\partial}K\big)\leq C\,r^{d-1},

where
B(x,r)
denotes a Euclidean ball with center
x
and radius
r
,
\mathrm{diam}(K)
is the diameter of
K
, and
c,C>0
only depend on
K
.

The estimates in Lemma
2.3
can be proven via Blaschke’s rolling theorem, see e.g.
[
41
, Cor. 3.2.13]
. It states that there are positive radii
r_{in}=r_{in}(K)
and
r_{out}=r_{out}(K)
such that for each boundary point
x\in{\partial}K
there are balls of radius
r_{in}
, respectively
r_{out}
, touching
{\partial}K
at
x
from the inside, respectively outside. That is,

B(x-r_{in}u_{x},r_{in})\subset K\subset B(x-r_{out}u_{x},r_{out}).

(7)

The following consequence of Blaschke’s rolling theorem is used in the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
K\in{\mathcal{C}}_{+}^{2}
. Then, for any
x\in{\partial}K
and
r>0
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(B(x,r+\varepsilon)\cap{\partial}K\big)-{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big(B(x,r)\cap{\partial}K\big)}{\varepsilon}\leq Cr^{d-2},

where
C>0
only depends on
K
.

Proof.

Since
K
is bounded, it is sufficient to show the statement for
r\leq\frac{r_{in}}{4}
. Without loss of generality assume that
x=0
and
u_{x}=-e_{d}
. Then (
7
) becomes

B_{in}:=B(r_{in}e_{d},r_{in})\subset K\subset B(r_{out}e_{d},r_{out})=:B_{out}.

(8)

We can parametrize
{\partial}K\cap B(0,\frac{r_{in}}{2})
by a twice continuously differentiable convex function
f\colon\mathds{R}^{d-1}\to\mathds{R}
such that

{\partial}K\cap B\Big(0,\frac{r_{in}}{2}\Big)=\{(u,f(u))\colon u\in U\},\qquad U:=\Proj\Big({\partial}K\cap B\Big(0,\frac{r_{in}}{2}\Big)\Big),

where
\Proj
denotes the orthogonal projection onto
H(e_{d},0)
, identified with
\mathds{R}^{d-1}
. For any
y\in\partial K\cap B(0,\frac{r_{in}}{2})
, the corresponding supporting hyperplane intersects the lower-dimensional ball
H(e_{d},0)\cap B(0,\frac{r_{in}}{2})
but not the interior of
B_{in}
. Therefore,

\sup_{x\in U}\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}}\leq C,

where
C>0
is an absolute constant. For
\varepsilon<\min\{\frac{r_{in}}{4},1\}
, the annular region

A_{r,\varepsilon}:=\Proj\big(\big(B(0,r+\varepsilon)\setminus B(0,r)\big)\cap{\partial}K\big)

is contained in
U
and thus

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\big((B(x,r+\varepsilon)\setminus B(x,r))\cap{\partial}K\big)

\displaystyle=\int_{A_{r,\varepsilon}}\sqrt{1+\|\nabla f(x)\|^{2}}\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(A_{r,\varepsilon}).

(9)

We use spherical coordinates to parametrize
A_{r,\varepsilon}
. For any unit vector
u\in\mathds{S}^{d-2}
and
s>0
, let
\alpha_{u,s}>0
be the unique number such that

\big(\alpha_{u,s}u,f(\alpha_{u,s}u)\big)\in\partial K\cap\partial B(0,s)

and thus

A_{r,\varepsilon}=\{\alpha_{u,s}u\colon r<s\leq r+\varepsilon\}.

By (
8
) it holds that, for each
u\in\mathds{S}^{d-2}
and any
s,t>0
such that
t+s\leq r_{in}
,

\alpha_{u,s}\leq\alpha_{u,s+t}\leq\alpha_{u,s}+t.

In particular,
\alpha_{u,s}\leq s
. Therefore,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(A_{r,\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathds{S}^{d-2}}\int_{\alpha_{u,r}}^{\alpha_{u,r+\varepsilon}}s^{d-2}\mathrm{d}s\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(u)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{d-1}\int_{\mathds{S}^{d-2}}\big[(\alpha_{u,r}+\varepsilon)^{d-1}-\alpha_{u,r}^{d-1}\big]\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(u)

\displaystyle\leq{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}(\mathds{S}^{d-2})\big(\varepsilon r^{d-2}+\varepsilon^{2}(1+r)^{d-1}\big).

Combined with (
2.2
) this completes the proof of Lemma
2.4
.
∎

3
Proof of the lower bound on the variance

Proposition 3.1

Before we prove Proposition

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

According to Lemma
2.2

, then the random polytope

\displaystyle\mathds{P}(A_{j})

, respectively

which together with (

∎

4
Proof of the central limit theorem

In this section we prove Theorem
1.2
using the machinery of normal approximation for stabilizing functionals developed in
[
25
]
. Let
K\in{\mathcal{C}}^{2}_{+}
and
P_{n}=[X_{1},\dots,X_{n}]
be as in Theorem
1.2
. Assume without loss of generality that
{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}({\partial}K)=1
. Moreover, assume that
n\geq 9
since if
d+2\leq n<9
, then the statement of Theorem
1.2
follows directly from the fact that
{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{n})>0
and
d_{K}\leq 1
.

We can represent the number of
k
-faces of the random polytope
P_{n}
as a sum of local contributions as follows. Almost surely, the random polytope
P_{n}
is simplicial and

f_{k}(P_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\xi_{k}(X_{i},{\mathcal{X}}_{n}),\qquad k=1,\dots,d-1,

(20)

where
{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=\{X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\}
is a random point set and, for any finite
{\mathcal{X}}\subset{\partial}K
and
x\in{\mathcal{X}}
,

\xi_{k}(x,{\mathcal{X}})=\frac{1}{k+1}\sum_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{k}([{\mathcal{X}}])}\mathbf{1}_{x\in F},\quad x\in{\mathcal{X}},\qquad k=1,\dots,d-1.

Only vertices
y\in{\mathcal{X}}
sharing a
k
-face with
x
contribute to the value of
\xi_{k}(x,{\mathcal{X}})
and these vertices are usually close to
x
. A radius of stabilization for
\xi_{k}
is a radius
R(x,{\mathcal{X}})
depending on
x
and
{\mathcal{X}}
such that points of
{\mathcal{X}}
with distance larger than
R(x,{\mathcal{X}})
do not contribute to the value of
\xi_{k}(x,{\mathcal{X}})
. Formally define, for a finite set
{\mathcal{X}}\subset{\partial}K
and
x\in{\partial}K
,

\displaystyle R(x,{\mathcal{X}}\cup\{x\})=\inf\{R>0\colon

\displaystyle K\cap H_{F}^{+}\subset B(x,R)\text{ for all }

\displaystyle F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{d-1}([{\mathcal{X}},x])\text{ with }x\in F\},

(21)

which is the smallest radius
R
such that the ball
B(x,R)
contains all facets of
[{\mathcal{X}},x]
which arise from adding
x
. Equivalently, it is the distance of the farthest point from
x
in any such facet. Then
R
is a radius of stabilization for
\xi_{k}
, i.e.

\xi_{k}(x,{\mathcal{X}}\cup\{x\})=\xi_{k}(x,{\mathcal{X}}\cap B(x,R(x,{\mathcal{X}}))),\qquad k=1,\dots,d-1.

The following theorem shows that
R
as given in (
4
) satisfies an exponential deviation bound in case of binomial input (the case of Poisson input is very similar and discussed in Section
5
).

Theorem 4.1
.

There exist
c,C>0
such that for each
x\in{\partial}K
and
r>0
we have

{\mathds{P}}(R(x,{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\cup\{x\})\geq r)\leq C\exp(-cr^{d-1}n),\qquad n\in\mathds{N}.

Proof.

Recall Blaschke’s rolling theorem in the form (
7
) with a ball of radius
r_{in}
rolling inside and a ball of radius
r_{out}
rolling outside. Since
R\leq{\rm diam}(K)
, it is sufficient to show the statement of Theorem
4.1
for
r\leq r_{in}
.

Let
x\in{\partial}K
and
r\leq r_{in}
. If
R(x,{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\cup\{x\})\geq r
, then there is a hyperplane
H_{F}
containing a facet
F\in{\mathcal{F}}_{d-1}([{\mathcal{X}}_{n},x])
with
x
as one of its vertices such that the diameter of
H_{F}\cap K
exceeds
r
. Thus, also the diameter of
H_{F}\cap B(x-r_{out}u_{x},r_{out})
exceeds
r
. Hence the diameter of the ball
H_{F}\cap B(x-r_{in}u_{x},r_{in})
exceeds
\frac{r_{in}}{r_{out}}r
, and its radius exceeds
\frac{r_{in}}{2r_{out}}r
.
Because of

x\in H_{F}\cap B(x-r_{in}u_{x},r_{in})\subset H_{F}\cap K,

there is a ball with center
\tilde{x}\in{\partial}K
, of radius
\frac{r_{in}}{2r_{out}}r
, which contains
x
in its boundary, such that

B\left(\tilde{x},\frac{r_{in}}{2r_{out}}r\right)\cap{\partial}K\subset H_{F}^{+}\cap{\partial}K.

(22)

We use the following geometric lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
x\in{\partial}K
there are
z_{1},\dots,z_{2^{d-1}}\in{\partial}K
such that the following holds:
if
\tilde{x}\in{\partial}K
with
\|\tilde{x}-x\|=\rho<r_{in}
, then there is an
i\in\{1,\dots,2^{d-1}\}
such that

B\left(z_{i},\frac{1}{4d^{2}}\rho\right)\subset{\rm int}\,B(\tilde{x},\rho).

Proof.

Without loss of generality assume that
x
is the origin, and that the outer unit normal vector of
x
equals
-e_{d}
. The orthogonal hyperplanes to the coordinate vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}
dissect
{\mathds{R}}^{d}
into
2^{d}
orthants from which those which contain
e_{d}
may have non-empty intersection with the interior of
K
, say
O_{1},\dots,O_{2^{d-1}}
.
Assume that
O_{1}=\{x\in{\mathds{R}}^{d}\colon x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\geq 0\}
, and that
\tilde{x}\in O_{1}
.

We define

z_{1}=\left(\frac{{\rho}}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}},\dots,\frac{\rho}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}},h_{d}\right),

where
h_{d}\geq 0
is chosen such that
z_{1}\in{\partial}K
(and
z_{2},\dots,z_{2^{d-1}}
analogously).
Because
\tilde{x}
and
z_{1}
are not in the interior of the ball with radius
r_{in}
, we have

h_{d}\leq\frac{{\rho}^{2}}{dr_{in}}\leq\frac{{\rho}}{d},\text{ and }\ \tilde{x}_{d}\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{2r_{in}}\leq\frac{\rho}{2}

We estimate the distance between
z_{1}
and
\tilde{x}
.

\displaystyle\|\tilde{x}-z_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{x}\|^{2}-2\tilde{x}\cdot z_{1}+\|z_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}-\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\tilde{x}_{i}+\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\tilde{x}_{d}-2h_{d}\tilde{x}_{d}+\rho^{2}\frac{1}{d}+h_{d}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{2}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\right)+\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\tilde{x}_{d}-2\tilde{x}_{d}h_{d}+h_{d}^{2}

because
\sum_{1}^{d}\tilde{x}_{i}\geq\|\tilde{x}\|_{2}=\rho
for
\tilde{x}_{i}\geq 0
.
The function
\frac{2\rho}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\tilde{x}_{d}-2\tilde{x}_{d}h_{d}+h_{d}^{2}
has no maximum in the interior of the region

(\tilde{x}_{d},h_{d})\in[0,\frac{\rho}{2}]\times[0,\frac{\rho}{d}]
, and attains for
\tilde{x}_{d}=\frac{\rho}{2}
,
h_{d}=0
its maximum
\frac{\rho^{2}}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}
. Hence

\displaystyle\|\tilde{x}-z_{1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}\left(1+\frac{1}{d}-\frac{1}{\sqrt{d(d-1)}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\rho^{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{2d^{2}}\right)

since
(1-\frac{1}{d})^{-1/2}\geq 1+\frac{1}{2d}
.
This proves that the ball with center
z_{1}
of radius

\rho-\sqrt{\rho^{2}-\frac{\rho^{2}}{2d^{2}}}>\frac{1}{4d^{2}}\rho

is contained in the interior of
B(\tilde{x},\rho)
. This completes the proof of Lemma
4.2
.
∎

From (
22
) and Lemma
4.2
we deduce that for each boundary point
x
and for any fixed
r
there are points
z_{1},\dots,z_{2^{d-1}}
such that any cap of diameter at least
r
contains one of the balls

B\left(z_{i},\frac{r_{in}}{8d^{2}r_{out}}r\right),\qquad i=1,\dots,2^{d-1}.

This implies

\displaystyle{\mathds{P}}(R(x,{\mathcal{X}}_{n}\cap\{x\})\geq r)

\displaystyle={\mathds{P}}\big(\exists F\in{\mathcal{F}}([x,{\mathcal{X}}_{n}])\colon x\in F,\,{\rm diam}(H_{F}\cap K)\geq r\big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d-1}}{\mathds{P}}\left(B\left(z_{i},\frac{r_{in}}{8d^{2}r_{out}}r\right)\cap{\mathcal{X}}_{n}=\emptyset\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{2^{d-1}}\left(1-{\mathcal{H}}^{d-1}\left(B\left(z_{i},\frac{r_{in}}{8d^{2}r_{out}}r\right)\cap{\partial}K\right)\right)^{n}.

With

5
Poisson Boundary Polytopes

In this section we prove Theorem
1.6
. Let
N\sim\pi(t)
be Poisson distributed with parameter
t>1
. Choose
N
independent random points uniformly on
{\partial}K
, and denote the convex hull of these random points by
P_{N}
. We call
P_{N}
a Poisson boundary polytope.

First, we prove that the variance lower bound carries over to
{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{N})
. Analogously to (
3
) and using Theorem
1.1
we have

{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{N})\geq{\mathds{E}}[{\mathds{V}}(f_{k}(P_{N})|N)]\geq{\mathds{E}}cN=ct.

An upper bound for the variance follows immediately from Theorem 10 in
[
32
]
and the Poincaré inequality for Poisson random variables, which in our context says that

{\mathds{V}}f_{k}(P_{N})\leq t{\mathds{E}}(f_{k}(P_{N+1})-f_{k}(P_{N}))^{2}\leq ct.

(For the general Poincaré inequality for functionals of Poisson random measures see
[
26
, Theorem 18.7]
.) This proves (
3
).

For the central limit theorem (
4
) observe that the method leading to the central limit theorem in Theorem
1.2
works with some immediate changes also in our case. The radius of stabilization is the same, and the work
[
25
, Cor.2.2]
contains both the CLT for binomial input (the case considered in Sections
3
and
4
) and the analogous CLT for Poisson input we consider in this section. The only changes consist in changing the binomial distribution to the Poisson distribution at several places which is immediate.

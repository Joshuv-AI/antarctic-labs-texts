Updated Bounds on the Minimal Left-Right Symmetric Model from LHC Dilepton Resonance Searches
Gabriela Lichtenstein, Ricardo C. Silva, M. J. Neves, Farinaldo Queiroz

Abstract
The Left-Right model is a popular extension of the Standard Model that features three new neutral gauge bosons, W R ± W^{\pm}_{R} and Z R Z_{R} . Collider searches for a Left-Right symmetry are often concentrated on the charged right-handed current, but in this work, we take advantage of the dilepton data at the LHC with center-of-mass energy of 13 TeV and 139 fb -1 of integrated luminosity to place lower mass bounds in the Z R Z_{R} mass based on the p ​ p → Z R → ℓ + ​ ℓ − p\,p\rightarrow Z_{R}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\,\ell^{-} process. We vary the S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) R SU(2)_{R} gauge coupling from g R = 0.4 g_{R}=0.4 to g R = 1.0 g_{R}=1.0 , and impose M Z R > 5.3 M_{Z_{R}}>5.3 TeV and M Z R > 6.1 M_{Z_{R}}>6.1 TeV, respectively. Lastly, we put our findings into perspective with W R W_{R} searches at the LHC and show that our limits cover an unexplored region of parameter space, where the right-handed neutrino is heavier than the W R W_{R} boson.

II
Review on the minimal left-right symmetric model

The LRSM is based on the gauge symmetry
SU(2)_{L}\times SU(2)_{R}\times U(1)_{B-L}
, where
B-L
means the barionic number
(B)
is subtracted from the leptonic number (L). For a review of the model, see the ref.
Lee:2017mfg
. The covariant derivative operator of the model is

\displaystyle D_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}-ig_{L}\,A_{L\mu}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{L}^{a}}{2}-ig_{R}\,A_{R\mu}^{a}\frac{\sigma_{R}^{a}}{2}-ig_{BL}\,\frac{Q_{BL}}{2}B_{\mu}\;,

(1)

where
g_{L}
,
g_{R}
, and
g_{BL}
are the coupling constants,
A_{L\mu}^{a}\,(a=1,2,3)
,
A_{R\mu}^{a}
and
B_{\mu}
are the gauge fields of
SU(2)_{L}
,
SU(2)_{R}
and
U(1)_{B-L}
, respectively. The sector of fermion is set by

{\cal L}_{f}=\overline{\psi_{L}^{i}}\,i\,\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi_{L}^{i}+\overline{\psi_{R}^{i}}\,i\,\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}\psi_{R}^{i}+\overline{Q_{L}^{i}}\,i\,\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}Q_{L}^{i}+\overline{Q_{R}^{i}}\,i\,\gamma^{\mu}D_{\mu}Q_{R}^{i}\;,

(2)

in which the content is assigned by

\displaystyle Q_{L}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{L}\\
d_{L}\end{array}\right)_{i},\left({\bf 3},{\bf 2},{\bf 1},+\frac{1}{3}\right)\;,\;Q_{R}^{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}u_{R}\\
d_{R}\end{array}\right)_{i},\left({\bf 3},{\bf 1},{\bf 2},+\frac{1}{3}\right)\;,

\displaystyle\psi_{L}^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu_{L}\\
\ell_{L}\end{array}\right)_{i},\left({\bf 1},{\bf 2},{\bf 1},-1\right)\;,\;\psi_{R}^{i}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}N_{R}\\
\ell_{R}\end{array}\right)_{i},\left({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf 2},-1\right)\;,

where
i=1,2,3
is the generation index.
The gauge bosons’ kinetic terms are given by

{\cal L}_{gauge}=-\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{tr}\left(F_{L\mu\nu}^{\;2}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\,\mbox{tr}\left(F_{R\mu\nu}^{\;2}\right)-\frac{1}{4}\,B_{\mu\nu}^{\;2}\;,

(13)

where
F_{L\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{L\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{L\mu}+i\,g_{L}\left[\,A_{L\mu}\,,\,A_{L\nu}\right]
,
F_{R\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}A_{R\nu}-\partial_{\nu}A_{R\mu}+i\,g_{R}\left[\,A_{R\mu}\,,\,A_{R\nu}\,\right]
and
B_{\mu\nu}=\partial_{\mu}B_{\nu}-\partial_{\nu}B_{\mu}

are the field-strength tensors of
A_{L\mu}^{\,a}
,
A_{R\mu}^{\,a}
and
B_{\mu}
, respectively.

In the minimal version of LRSM, the scalar sector is governed by the kinetic term

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{Sc}^{Kin}=\mbox{Tr}(|D_{\mu}\Phi|^{2})+\mbox{Tr}(|D_{\mu}\Delta_{L}|^{2})+\mbox{Tr}(|D_{\mu}\Delta_{R}|^{2})\;,

(14)

where multiplets set the scalar fields:

\displaystyle\Phi\ =\ \left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\phi^{0}_{1}&\phi^{+}_{2}\\
\phi^{-}_{1}&\phi^{0}_{2}\end{array}\right):({\bf 1},{\bf 2},{\bf 2},0)\;,\;\Delta_{L}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{+}_{L}/\sqrt{2}&\Delta^{++}_{L}\\
\Delta^{0}_{L}&-\Delta^{+}_{L}/\sqrt{2}\end{array}\right):({\bf 1},{\bf 3},{\bf 1},2)\;,\;

\displaystyle\Delta_{R}\!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\!\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta^{+}_{R}/\sqrt{2}&\Delta^{++}_{R}\\
\Delta^{0}_{R}&-\Delta^{+}_{R}/\sqrt{2}\end{array}\right):({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf 3},2)\;.

The electric charge of the model is defined by

\displaystyle Q_{em}=I_{3L}+I_{3R}+\frac{Q_{BL}}{2}\;,

(23)

where
I_{3L(R)}=\sigma_{L(R)}^{3}/2=\pm 1/2
are the isospins of
SU(2)_{L}
and
SU(2)_{R}
, respectively, and
Q_{BL}
is the generator of
U(1)_{B-L}
. The symmetry is broken by the expected vacuum values (VEVs) scales

\displaystyle\langle\Phi\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}\kappa_{1}/\sqrt{2}&0\\
0&\kappa_{2}\,e^{i\alpha}/\sqrt{2}\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\langle\Delta_{L}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
v_{L}\,e^{i\theta_{L}}/\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\;,\;\langle\Delta_{R}\rangle=\begin{pmatrix}0&0\\
v_{R}/\sqrt{2}&0\end{pmatrix}\;,

(24)

for two real phases
\alpha
and
\theta_{L}
, that obeys the hierarchy condition
v_{R}\gg v=\sqrt{\kappa_{1}^{2}+\kappa_{2}^{2}}\gg v_{L}
, with
\kappa_{1}=v\sin\beta
and
\kappa_{2}=v\cos\beta
, in which
v=246\,\mbox{GeV}
is the VEV scale of the SM. After the SSB, the neutrinos acquire masses via the type II seesaw mechanism.

III
Physical gauge bosons and couplings in LRSM

The sector of the charged gauge bosons (CGB) can be written in matrix form:

{\cal L}_{mass}^{CGB}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}A_{L}^{-}&A_{R}^{-}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}(\,g_{L}^{2}\,v^{2}+2g_{L}^{2}\,v_{L}^{2}\,)/2&-\,e^{i\alpha}\,g_{L}\,g_{R}\,v^{2}\,\sin\beta\,\cos\beta\\
-e^{-i\alpha}\,g_{L}\,g_{R}\,v^{2}\,\sin\beta\,\cos\beta&\frac{1}{2}\,g_{R}^{2}\,v^{2}+g_{R}^{2}\,v_{R}^{2}\end{pmatrix}\,\begin{pmatrix}A_{L}^{+}\\
A_{R}^{+}\end{pmatrix}\;,

(25)

where
A_{L,R}^{\pm}=(A^{1}_{L,R}\mp i\,A^{2}_{L,R})/\sqrt{2}
. An unitary transformation can diagonalize the mass matrix (see the appendix
A
), and using the hierarchy condition of the VEVs, the masses of the physical bosons
W_{L}^{\pm}
and
W_{R}^{\pm}
are given by

\displaystyle M_{W_{L}}\simeq\frac{g_{L}\,v}{2}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptM_{W_{R}}\simeq\frac{g_{R}\,v_{R}}{\sqrt{2}}\;,

(26)

respectively. In the sector of gauge neutral bosons (NGB), the matrix form for the massive lagrangian is

{\cal L}_{mass}^{NGB}=\frac{1}{2}\,\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}A_{L\,\mu}^{\,\,3}&A_{R\,\mu}^{\,\,3}&B_{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\frac{1}{4}\,g_{L}^{2}\,(v^{2}+4v_{L}^{2})&-\frac{v^{2}}{4}\,g_{L}\,g_{R}&-g_{L}\,g_{BL}\,v_{L}^{2}\\
-\frac{v^{2}}{4}\,g_{L}\,g_{R}&v_{R}^{2}\,g_{R}^{2}+\frac{v^{2}g_{R}^{2}}{4}&-g_{L}\,g_{BL}v_{R}^{2}\\
-g_{L}\,g_{BL}\,v_{L}^{2}&-g_{R}\,g_{BL}\,v_{R}^{2}&g_{BL}^{2}\,(v_{L}^{2}+v_{R}^{2})\end{array}\right)\,\left(\begin{array}[]{c}A_{L}^{\,\,\mu\,3}\\
A_{R}^{\,\,\mu\,3}\\
B^{\mu}\\
\end{array}\right)\;,

(27)

that can be diagonalized by a
SO(3)
transformation that introduces three mixing angles
(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3})
(see the appendix
A
). The diagonalized matrix from (
27
) is
M_{D}^{2}=\mbox{diag}(M_{Z}^{2},M_{Z_{R}}^{2},0)
whose eigenvalues are read as

\displaystyle M_{Z}\simeq\frac{v}{2}\,\sqrt{\,\frac{g_{L}^{2}\,g_{R}^{2}+g_{L}^{2}\,g_{BL}^{2}+g_{R}^{2}\,g_{BL}^{2}}{g_{R}^{2}+g_{BL}^{2}}\,}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptM_{Z_{R}}\simeq v_{R}\,\sqrt{g_{R}^{2}+g_{BL}^{2}}\;,

(28)

for the condition of
v_{R}\gg v\gg v_{L}
. The null eigenvalue corresponds to the photon mass, and the physical eigenstate associated with it is identified as the electromagnetic field. The mixing angles satisfy the parameterization for the fundamental charge
(e)

\displaystyle e=g_{L}\,\sin\theta_{2}=g_{R}\,\sin\theta_{1}\,\cos\theta_{2}=g_{BL}\,\cos\theta_{1}\,\cos\theta_{2}\;,

(29)

where
\cos\theta_{2}=M_{W}/M_{Z}\equiv\cos\theta_{W}
is identified as the Weinberg angle
\sin^{2}\theta_{W}=0.23
. From (
29
), the mixing angles
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{3}
are given by

\displaystyle\theta_{1}=\sin^{-1}\left(\frac{g_{L}}{g_{R}}\,\tan\theta_{W}\right)\;\;\;\mbox{and}\;\;\;\theta_{3}\simeq-\cos\theta_{W}\,\sqrt{\,\frac{g_{R}^{2}}{g_{L}^{2}}-\tan^{2}\theta_{W}\,}\;\frac{M_{Z}^{2}}{M_{Z_{R}^{2}}}\;,

(30)

that are written in terms of the new parameters
g_{R}
and
M_{Z_{R}}
.
Using this parametrization, the masses of the neutral gauge bosons are written as

\displaystyle M_{Z}=\frac{g_{L}\,v}{2\cos\theta_{W}}\hskip 14.22636pt\mbox{and}\hskip 14.22636ptM_{Z_{R}}=\frac{g_{R}\,v_{R}}{\sqrt{1-(g_{L}/g_{R})^{2}\,\tan^{2}\theta_{W}}}\;.

(31)

The masses of
W
and
Z
are known from the SM, that is,
M_{W}=80

(32)

and the couplings with the neutral gauge bosons are read as

\displaystyle{\cal L}_{NGB}^{int}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e\,J_{\mu}^{em}A^{\mu}+\frac{g_{L}}{\cos\theta_{W}}J_{Z\,\mu}^{0}\,Z^{\mu}+g_{Z_{R}}\,J_{Z_{R}\,\mu}^{0}Z_{R}^{\,\mu}\;,

(34)

V
Conclusions

In this paper, we obtain updated constraints in the left-right symmetric model (LRSM) based on the resonant process
p\,p\rightarrow Z_{R}\rightarrow\ell^{+}\,\ell^{-}
in the Run II at LHC, using dilepton data from the ATLAS collaboration at
\sqrt{s}=13\ \text{TeV}
of center-of-mass energy and
139\ \text{fb}^{-1}
of integrated luminosity. Instead of focusing on a specific benchmark point
g_{R}=g_{L}
, we varied
g_{R}
and repeated this exercise to derive lower mass limits for different choices of
g_{R}
(see figure
3
).

Once a right-handed current is observed at the LHC, the next step is to search for a dilepton resonance, as investigated in this work. For
g_{R}=g_{L}
, we find the
M_{W_{R}}=0.58M_{Z_{R}}
, thus the
W_{R}
boson is expected to be lighter, but the dilepton signature is quite clean, and less sensitive to the right-handed neutrino mass. As the right-handed neutrino mass nears the
W_{R}
boson, the LHC sensitivity to a
W_{R}
signal weakens because the final lepton will have a soft transverse momentum. For this reason, we believe it is worthwhile to conduct an independent probe for the
Z_{R}
boson.

As the masses of the
W_{R}
and
Z_{R}
gauge bosons are directly linked, we showed that our
Z_{R}
bounds cover the unexplored parameter space of the typical right-handed neutrino-
W_{R}
mass plane for
M_{W_{R}}<M_{N_{R}}
. In summary, the exciting hunt for a collider signature of the Left-Right symmetry should also rely on the search for a
Z_{R}
boson.

Emergent deterministic entanglement dynamics in monitored infinite-range bosonic systems
Zejian Li, Anna Delmonte, Rosario Fazio

Abstract
We study monitored quantum dynamics of infinite-range interacting bosonic systems in the thermodynamic limit. We show that under semiclassical assumptions, the quantum fluctuations along single monitored trajectories adopt a deterministic limit for both quantum-jump and state-diffusion unravelings, and can be exactly solved. In particular, the hierarchical structure of the equations of motion explains the coincidence of entanglement phase transitions and dissipative phase transitions found in previous finite-size numerical studies. We illustrate the findings on a Bose-Hubbard dimer and a collective spin system.

The findings of these works share a common peculiarity: the average state of the system, undergoing dissipative Lindblad dynamics, also exhibits a phase transition, which always emerges at the same critical point as the entanglement phase transition, as suggested by finite-size numerics. It is worth understanding, from a theoretical perspective, whether this feature is intrinsic to infinite-range interacting systems.

In this letter, we complete the previous results by studying the monitored dynamics of infinite-range bosonic systems directly in the thermodynamic limit. In the particular case of free bosons with state-diffusion type of monitoring of linear field operators, it has been shown by Y. Minoguchi, P. Rabl, and M. Buchhold in Ref.
Minoguchi et al. 2022
that the entanglement follows the same deterministic evolution along any monitored trajectory. This arises from the fact that the system state remains Gaussian under non-interacting dynamics, which can be fully characterized by its first moments and the covariance matrix, with the latter being deterministic and defining the entanglement. In the more general case of infinite-range interacting bosons, the system can be well approximated by a Gaussian state due to its semiclassical nature
Buchholz et al. 2014
;
Goderis and Vets 1989
;
Verbeure 2011
, and the theory becomes asymptotically mean-field and non-interacting
Carollo and Lesanovsky 2021
;
Carollo and Lesanovsky 2024
;
Mattes et al. 2024
, allowing us to study the dissipative dynamics exploiting the Gaussian framework. When monitoring is involved, this formalism typically results in stochastic equations of the Gaussian parameters along quantum trajectories
Verstraelen and Wouters 2018
, which is the numerical approach we previously adopted in studying infinite-range Bosons
Delmonte et al. 2025
and long-range spins (with bosonized spin-wave fluctuations)
Li et al. 2025
. Based on the Gaussian-state approximations, we make in this letter the key observation that the stochasticity in the monitored semiclassical dynamics (for both quantum-jump and state-diffusion processes) is suppressed by system size, leading to deterministic equations for the covariances in the thermodynamic limit, whose dynamics is driven by the standard mean-field solutions for the first moments. In particular, this allows the analytical study of entanglement dynamics along monitored trajectories and explains the coincidence of entanglement transitions and symmetry-breaking dissipative transitions.
We illustrate these findings on two infinite-range interacting models, a Bose-Hubbard dimer and a collective spin system (which can be mapped to a bosonic model), where we solve the monitored entanglement dynamics exactly in the thermodynamic limit.

Deterministic dynamics of quantum fluctuations in the thermodynamic limit.—

Let us consider a bosonic system with annihilation operators
\hat{a}_{i}
that satisfy standard bosonic commutation relations
[\hat{a}_{i},\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j}]=\delta_{ij}

\alpha_{i}\equiv\langle\hat{a}_{i}\rangle
and the covariances
u_{ij}\equiv\langle\hat{\delta}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j}\rangle
,
v_{ij}\equiv\langle\hat{\delta}^{\dagger}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j}\rangle

\hat{\delta}_{i}\equiv\hat{a}_{i}-\langle\hat{a}_{i}\rangle

\alpha_{i}\sim\Theta(\sqrt{N})
and
u\sim v\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
,
where the parameter
N
is proportional to the bosonic population and defines the thermodynamic limit via
N\to\infty

It then follows that the state is approximately coherent up to
\mathcal{O}(1/N)
corrections, i.e.,

\displaystyle\langle\hat{a}_{i}^{\dagger m}\hat{a}^{n}_{j}\rangle=\alpha^{*m}_{i}\alpha^{n}_{j}[1+\mathcal{O}(1/N)]\,.

(1)

These approximations are typically satisfied in infinite-range interaction systems in the large
N

Conclusions.—

We have studied entanglement dynamics in the thermodynamic limit of monitored infinite-range interacting systems. Our results, exploiting the semiclassical nature of infinite-range systems, reveal several features that can be highlighted as follows,

•

Along single trajectories, the normalized observables follow deterministic dynamics in the thermodynamic limit given by the standard mean-field theory, which drives the (also deterministic) dynamics of quantum fluctuations and in particular, the entanglement.

•

When the mean-field observables exhibit non-analytical behavior, this also drives the appearance of an entanglement phase transition due to the one-way dependence of the latter on the former.

This mean-field picture for monitored entanglement dynamics complements our previous finite-size numerical results
Passarelli et al. 2024
;
Delmonte et al. 2025
;
Li et al. 2025
. The unified treatment for different unravelings makes it a convenient toolbox for studying monitored dynamics in the semiclassical regime, that can be readily applied to various infinite-range models beyond those considered in this work.

Appendix B: Deterministic dynamics in the thermodynamic limit.—

The evolution of the first moments is obtained by
the adjoint stochastic master equation

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}\alpha_{i}={\rm{d}}t\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{a}_{i}\rangle+\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{a}_{i}\rangle\,,

\mathcal{A}^{\ddagger}

\langle\hat{H}\rangle\sim\Theta(N)
and
\langle\hat{L}_{i}\rangle\sim\Theta(\sqrt{N})

\hat{H}

\hat{L}

1

\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{a}_{i}\rangle\sim\Theta(\sqrt{N})
, which is dominant over the noise term
\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{a}_{i}\rangle\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
. Defining the normalized first moment
{\tilde{\alpha}}_{i}\equiv\alpha_{i}/\sqrt{N}
, we immediately recover the standard deterministic mean-field equation in the
N\to\infty
limit, which involves only
{\tilde{\alpha}}_{i}
but not the covariances
u_{ij}
or
v_{ij}
.

On the other hand, the quantum fluctuations encoded in the covariances exhibit nontrivial dynamics dependent on the first moment and, in particular, on the monitoring. Applying the Itô differentiation rule to the time-dependent operator
\hat{\delta}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j}
, we obtain

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}u_{ij}

\displaystyle={\rm{d}}t\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}(\hat{\delta}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j})\rangle+{\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}(\hat{\delta}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j})\rangle}-{\rm{d}}\alpha_{i}{\rm{d}}\alpha_{j}\,,

(B2)

where both
\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}(\hat{\delta}_{i}\hat{\delta}_{j})\rangle
and
{\rm{d}}\alpha_{i}{\rm{d}}\alpha_{j}
are of order
1
while the stochastic term is of order
1/\sqrt{N}
. Taking the
N\to\infty
limit, the resulting equation is therefore deterministic, and the same holds true for
v_{ij}
.

II
Derivation of the coarse-grained quantum-jump unraveling

We provide here an explicit derivation of the coarse-grained quantum-jump stochastic master equation, for a single-mode problem with
\hat{L}=\sqrt{\kappa}\hat{a}
. Denoting

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(t)\hat{\rho}\equiv{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}}\hat{\rho}{\rm{e}}^{{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}^{\dagger}}\,,\quad\mathcal{J}\hat{\rho}\equiv\hat{L}\hat{\rho}\hat{L}^{\dagger}\,,

(S1)

where the non-Hermitian Hamiltonian is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}\equiv\hat{H}-\dfrac{{\rm{i}}}{2}\hat{L}^{\dagger}\hat{L}\,,

(S2)

the probability for
m
jumps to occur within time
\Delta t
starting from the state
\hat{\rho}
is
Wiseman and Milburn 1993

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m;\Delta t

\displaystyle)=\Tr[\int_{0}^{\Delta t}{\rm{d}}t_{m}\int_{0}^{t_{m}}{\rm{d}}t_{m-1}\cdots\int_{0}^{t_{2}}{\rm{d}}t_{1}

(S3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{U}(\Delta t-t_{m})\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}(t_{m}-t_{m-1})\cdots\mathcal{J}\mathcal{U}(t_{1})\hat{\rho}\Bigg]\,.

The semiclassical approximations made in the main text allow
\mathcal{U}
and
\mathcal{J}
to commute at leading order, making the nested integral trivial to evaluate,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(m;\Delta t)\simeq\dfrac{(\Delta t)^{m}}{m!}\Tr\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\mathcal{U}(\Delta t)\mathcal{J}^{m}\hat{\rho}}}\right]\simeq{\rm{e}}^{-\mu}\dfrac{\mu^{m}}{m!}\,,

(S4)

where we remind that
\mu\equiv|\langle\hat{L}\rangle|^{2}\Delta t=|\alpha|^{2}\kappa\Delta t
. This is a Poissonian distribution with both mean and variance equal to
\mu
. This is not surprising as we are assuming the state to be coherent at leading order, which has Poissonian counting statistics. Approximating the number of jumps
m
with the following Gaussian random number (as justified in the main text),

\displaystyle m=|\alpha|^{2}\kappa\Delta t+|\alpha|\sqrt{\kappa}\Delta W\,,

(S5)

we find the state conditioned by
m
jumps [using the expansion
\hat{a}=\alpha(\hat{\mathds{1}}+\hat{\delta}/\alpha)
],

\displaystyle|\tilde{\psi}(\Delta t)\rangle

\displaystyle\propto{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}\Delta t}\hat{a}^{m}|\psi\rangle

(S6)

\displaystyle\propto{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}\Delta t}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}+\dfrac{\hat{\delta}}{\alpha}}}\right)^{m}|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}\Delta t}}\right)\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\hat{\mathds{1}}+m\dfrac{\hat{\delta}}{\alpha}}}\right)|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\simeq|\psi\rangle+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[m\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{\hat{a}}{\alpha}-\hat{\mathds{1}}}}\right)-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{nh}}\Delta t}}\right]|\psi\rangle\,.

After normalizing the state, we get the increment

\displaystyle\Delta^{(m)}|\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle\penalty\displaystyle m\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{\hat{a}}{\alpha}-\hat{\mathds{1}}}}\right)|\psi\rangle

(S7)

\displaystyle+\Delta t\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\kappa\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}{2}}}\right)-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}}}\right]|\psi\rangle\,,

which is of the same form as the original stochastic Schrödinger equation (
A1
) in the main text with the point increment
{\rm{d}}M
replaced by the coarse-grained random number
m
. Taking the limit
\Delta t\to{\rm{d}}t
,
\Delta W\to{\rm{d}}W
and inserting the expression for
m
from Eq. (
S5
), we arrive at the stochastic Schrödinger equation for the normalized pure state in the large
N
limit,

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}|\psi\rangle=

\displaystyle{\rm{\penalty\displaystyle d}}t\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[|\alpha|^{2}\kappa\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{\hat{a}}{\alpha}-\hat{\mathds{1}}}}\right)+\dfrac{\kappa|\alpha|^{2}}{2}-\dfrac{\kappa\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}}{2}-{\rm{i}}\hat{H}}}\right]|\psi\rangle

(S8)

\displaystyle+|\alpha|\sqrt{\kappa}{\rm{d}}W\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{\hat{a}}{\alpha}-\hat{\mathds{1}}}}\right)|\psi\rangle\,.

Equivalently, in terms of the density matrix
\hat{\rho}\equiv\outerproduct{\psi}{\psi}
, the above equation can be written as

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}\hat{\rho}={\rm{d}}t\mathcal{L}\hat{\rho}+|\alpha|\sqrt{\kappa}{\rm{d}}W\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\dfrac{\hat{a}-\alpha}{\alpha}\hat{\rho}+\hat{\rho}\dfrac{\hat{a}^{\dagger}-\alpha^{\ast}}{\alpha^{\ast}}}}\right)\,,

(S9)

which is essentially Eq. (
2
) of the main text.

Benchmark on a single-mode bosonic system

To visualize the emergence of the deterministic dynamics in the thermodynamic limit, we provide in this section a benchmark based on a single-mode Kerr cavity model against numerically exact quantum trajectories simulated at finite
N
.
The considered Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle\hat{H}=-\Delta\hat{a}^{\dagger}\hat{a}+\dfrac{\tilde{U}}{2N}\hat{a}^{\dagger 2}\hat{a}^{2}+\tilde{F}\sqrt{N}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}+\hat{a})

(S10)

and the jump operator is
\hat{L}=\sqrt{\kappa}\hat{a}
. The deterministic equations (
7
) in the main text give the following,

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}{\tilde{\alpha}}={\rm{d}}t\Big[

\displaystyle\Big({\rm{i}}\Delta-\dfrac{\kappa}{2}\Big){\tilde{\alpha}}-{\rm{i}}\tilde{U}|{\tilde{\alpha}}|^{2}{\tilde{\alpha}}-{\rm{i}}\tilde{F}\Big]\,,

(S11)

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}u={\rm{d}}t\Big\{

\displaystyle(2{\rm{i}}\Delta-\kappa)u-{\rm{i}}\tilde{U}({\tilde{\alpha}}^{2}+2{\tilde{\alpha}}^{2}v+4|{\tilde{\alpha}}|^{2}u)

\displaystyle-\kappa\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2uv+\Upsilon^{*}u^{2}+\Upsilon v^{2}}}\right)\Big\}\,,

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}v={\rm{d}}t\Big\{

\displaystyle 2\tilde{U}\imaginary[{\tilde{\alpha}}^{2}u^{*}]-\kappa v

\displaystyle-\kappa\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(2\real[\Upsilon u^{*}v]+|u|^{2}+v^{2}}}\right)\Big\}\,,

and we remind that
\Upsilon
is the unraveling-dependent factor defined in Eq. (

shows the temporal dynamics of the system obtained from three different unravelings (quantum jump, homodyne, and heterodyne) with

\ket{\alpha}
with

. As shown in panels (a), (d), and (g), the statistical fluctuations of quantum trajectories are represented as shaded regions in the graph, which is centered at the trajectory-average value with a radius equal to the standard deviation (among single-trajectory values). As

The same benchmark is performed in panels (b), (e), and (h), but for the nonlinear quantity

Finally, let us verify the validity of the Gaussian approximation along single trajectories. Due to the interacting nature of the system, we expect the state to be non-Gaussian for finite
N
, and we quantify the non-Gaussianity of the state using the measure proposed in Ref.
Genoni et al. 2007
. For a generic state
\hat{\rho}
with first moment
\alpha={\rm{Tr}}[\hat{\rho}\hat{a}]
and covariances
u={\rm{Tr}}[\hat{\rho}\hat{\delta}\hat{\delta}]
,
v={\rm{Tr}}[\hat{\rho}\hat{\delta}^{\dagger}\hat{\delta}]
, we can always find a reference Gaussian state
\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{G}}(\alpha,u,v)
with the same first and second moments. The non-Gaussianity measure is then defined as

\displaystyle\delta_{G}\equiv\dfrac{{\rm{Tr}}[(\hat{\rho}-\hat{\rho}_{\mathcal{G}})^{2}]}{2{\rm{Tr}}[\hat{\rho}^{2}]}\,,

(S12)

which is the normalized Hilbert-Schmidt distance between the two density matrices. This quantity equals zero if and only if the state
\hat{\rho}
is Gaussian and is otherwise strictly positive. In Fig.
S1

\overline{\delta_{G}}
along the time evolution. For all three unravelings considered above, the non-Gaussianity asymptotically decreases to zero as
N
increases, suggesting the emergent Gaussianity of the state in the thermodynamic limit. Interestingly, the finite-
N
results also display a systematically higher non-Gaussianity in the quantum-jump trajectories compared to the state-diffusion unravelings. This implies that the Gaussian trajectory approximation might yield physically more accurate results with the diffusion-type unravelings in simulating finite-
N
quantum trajectories, as compared to the (numerically more efficient) quantum-jump unraveling. Nevertheless, since our theoretical treatment directly targets the thermodynamic limit, we can safely assume the Gaussianity of the state.

IV
Equations of motion for the collective spin model in the thermodynamic limit

In this section, we derive the expressions for the equations of motion of the collective spin system. We consider a slightly more general form for the Hamiltonian and jump operator,

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\Omega\sum_{\alpha}c^{F}_{\alpha}\hat{S}^{\alpha}\,,\quad\hat{L}=\sqrt{\dfrac{\kappa}{S}}\sum_{\alpha}c^{D}_{\alpha}\hat{S}^{\alpha}\,,

(S13)

and the model defined in the main text corresponds to the choice
\bm{c}^{F}=(1,0,0)
and
\bm{c}^{D}=(1,-{\rm{i}},0)
.

We follow the same procedure as detailed in Ref.
Li et al. 2025
to bosonize the spin operators via the lowest-order Holstein-Primakoff transformation
Holstein and Primakoff 1940
,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{{\tilde{z}}}

\displaystyle=S-\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\,,

(S14)

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{{\tilde{x}}}

\displaystyle\simeq\sqrt{\dfrac{S}{2}}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\hat{b})\,,

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{{\tilde{y}}}

\displaystyle\simeq{\rm{i}}\sqrt{\dfrac{S}{2}}(\hat{b}^{\dagger}-\hat{b})\,,

with
[\hat{b},\hat{b}^{\dagger}]=1
, in the rotated frame defined by
\hat{U}(\theta,\phi)={\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}\phi\hat{S}^{z}}{\rm{e}}^{-{\rm{i}}\theta\hat{S}^{y}}
and

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{\tilde{\alpha}}=\hat{U}(\theta,\phi)\hat{S}^{\alpha}\hat{U}^{\dagger}(\theta,\phi)=\sum_{\beta}G_{{\tilde{\alpha}}\beta}\hat{S}^{\beta}\,,

(S15)

with the following matrix elements,

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta\cos\phi&\cos\theta\sin\phi&-\sin\theta\\
-\sin\phi&\cos\phi&0\\
\sin\theta\cos\phi&\sin\theta\sin\phi&\cos\theta\end{pmatrix}\,.

(S16)

A generic spin operator of the form
\hat{K}=\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}\hat{S}^{\beta}
can then be written in the bosonized picture as

\displaystyle\hat{K}

\displaystyle=S\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{z}}+\sqrt{\dfrac{S}{2}}(\mathcal{E}\hat{b}^{\dagger}+\mathcal{F}^{*}\hat{b})-\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{z}}\hat{b}^{\dagger}\hat{b}\,,

(S17)

where we have defined

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}G_{{\tilde{\alpha}}\beta}\,,

(S18)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{x}}+{\rm{i}}\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{y}}\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\tilde{x}}+{\rm{i}}\mathcal{C}^{*}_{\tilde{y}}\,.

The dynamics can be studied in the rotating frame by introducing the inertial Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\mathrm{RF}}

\displaystyle=-{\rm{i}}\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\hat{U}}{{\rm{d}}t}\hat{U}^{\dagger}

(S19)

\displaystyle=\sin\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}\hat{S}^{\tilde{x}}-\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\theta}{{\rm{d}}t}\hat{S}^{\tilde{y}}-\cos\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}\hat{S}^{\tilde{z}}\,.

The equation of motion for the first moment
\beta\equiv\langle\hat{b}\rangle
is then given by,

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}\beta=

\displaystyle{\rm{\penalty\displaystyle i}}{\rm{d}}t\langle[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{RF}}\,,\hat{b}]\rangle+{\rm{d}}t\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle+\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle\,,

(S20)

where
\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}
is the adjoint bare Liouvillian (without
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{RF}}
).
The action of the inertial Hamiltonian is explicitly evaluated to be

\displaystyle{\rm{i}}[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{RF}}\,,\hat{b}]={\rm{i}}\sqrt{\dfrac{S}{2}}\sin\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}-\sqrt{\dfrac{S}{2}}\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\theta}{{\rm{d}}t}-{\rm{i}}\cos\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}\hat{b}\,.

(S21)

Finally, we impose the alignment of the rotating frame’s
{\tilde{z}}
axis with the collective magnetization (i.e.
\langle\hat{b}\rangle\equiv 0
) and using the fact that the noise term
\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle
is of order
\mathcal{O}(1){\rm{d}}Z
while the leading order of both
\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle
and
\langle[\hat{H}_{\mathrm{RF}}\,,\hat{b}]\rangle
is
\Theta(\sqrt{S})
, we obtain, in the
S\to\infty
limit, the equations of motion for the angles
(\theta,\phi)
,

\displaystyle\sin\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{S}}\imaginary\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle}}\right\}_{0}\,,

(S22)

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\theta}{{\rm{d}}t}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\sqrt{\dfrac{2}{S}}\real\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle}}\right\}_{0}\,.

They result in the standard mean-field equations for the classical spin vector
(\theta,\phi)
in spherical coordinates.
The deterministic equations for the second moments also follow naturally by a similar argument,

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}u

\displaystyle=-2{\rm{i}}u\cos\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}{\rm{d}}t+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{{\rm{d}}t\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}(\hat{\delta}\hat{\delta})\rangle-\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle^{2}}}\right\}_{0}\,,

(S23)

\displaystyle{\rm{d}}v

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{{\rm{d}}t\langle\mathcal{L}^{\ddagger}(\hat{\delta}^{\dagger}\hat{\delta})\rangle-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\lvert\langle{\rm{d}}\mathcal{H}^{\ddagger}\hat{b}\rangle}}\right\rvert^{2}}}\right\}_{0}\,,

Below, we provide the explicit expressions of these equations for the model defined in Eq. (
S13
). The right-hand side of Eq. (
S22

(S24)

The equations (
S23
) for
u
and
v
become,

\displaystyle\dfrac{{\rm{d}}u}{{\rm{d}}t}=

\displaystyle\penalty}{\displaystyle 2{\rm{i}}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\Omega\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{z}}^{F}-\cos\theta\dfrac{{\rm{d}}\phi}{{\rm{d}}t}}}\right]u

(S25)

\displaystyle-\dfrac{\kappa}{2}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left\{\mathcal{E}^{D}\mathcal{F}^{D}+\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{F}^{D}}}\right|^{2}-\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left|\mathcal{E}^{D}}}\right|^{2}}}\right)u}}\right\}

Relativistic and Recoil Corrections to Light–Fermion Vacuum Polarization for Bound Systems of Spin-0, Spin-1/2, and Spin-1 Particles
Gregory S. Adkins, Ulrich D. Jentschura

Abstract
In bound systems whose constituent particles are heavier than the electron, the dominant radiative correction to energy levels is given by light-fermion (electronic) vacuum polarization. In consequence, relativistic and recoil corrections to the one-loop vacuum-polarization correction are phenomenologically relevant. Here, we generalize the treatment, previously accomplished for systems with orbiting muons, to bound systems of constituents with more general spins: spin-0, spin-1/2, and spin-1. We discuss the application of our more general expressions to various systems of interest, including spinless systems (pionium), muonic hydrogen and deuterium, and devote special attention to the excited non- S S states of deuteronium, the bound system of a deuteron and its antiparticle. The obtained energy corrections are of order α 5 ​ m r \alpha^{5}m_{r} , where α \alpha is the fine-structure constant and m r m_{r} is the reduced mass.

II
NRQED Lagrangian and Feynman Rules

In order to find the eVP correction to the Breit Hamiltonian we
shall start from a one-particle Hamiltonian (for spin-1 particles),
and then derive the Breit interaction Hamiltonian from the interaction
kernels describing the exchange of a massive photon,
the photon mass being integrated as a spectral parameter of vacuum polarization.
For the discussion of the relativistic and recoil
corrections to eVP, we use the photon propagator in
an optimized Coulomb gauge
AdJe2024gauge
.
Formally, the photon propagator in the optimized Coulomb gauge
describes the exchange of a photon of mass

\lambda=\frac{2m_{e}}{\sqrt{1-v^{2}}}\,,

(1)

where we use
v\in(0,1)
as the spectral parameter
of vacuum polarization
AdJe2024gauge
.
The first-order correction to the
photon propagator in the optimized Coulomb (OC)
gauge
AdJe2024gauge
reads as follows,

D^{\mathrm{OC}{}:1}_{\mu\nu}(k)=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}v\,f_{1}(v)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}{k^{2}-\lambda^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(2)

For our purposes, it is sufficient to
use the instantaneous approximation
k^{2}-\lambda^{2}\rightarrow-(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})
for the spatial part.
The one-loop spectral function is

f_{1}(v)=\frac{v^{2}(1-\frac{v^{2}}{3})}{1-v^{2}}\,.

(3)

For the derivation of the Breit Hamiltonian for
spin-1 particle, we use the plain Coulomb-gauge photon propagator,
which we recall as follows (see Chap. 9 of Ref.
JeAd2022book
),

D^{C}_{\mu\nu}(k)=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}}&0\\
0&\frac{1}{k^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{\hat{k}^{i}\hat{k}^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)\end{pmatrix}\,.

(4)

We employ the instantaneous approximation
k^{2}\rightarrow-\vec{k}\,^{2}
.

\displaystyle\;\frac{Z\alpha(\tilde{g}_{1}-1)}{2m_{1}^{2}}\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}+\frac{Z\alpha(\tilde{g}_{2}-1)}{2m_{2}^{2}}\frac{\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}\,.

(21)

The quadrupole kernel from Fig.
1
(d) is

K^{(4)}_{{\rm Q},1}=\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{q_{1}Q_{E1}}{2}(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,k^{i}k^{j}\right)\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)(-\mathrm{i}q_{2})\,.

(22)

The corresponding Hamiltonian contribution is

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{{\rm Q},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(4\pi Z\alpha)Q_{E1}}{2}(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right]

(23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,Q_{E1}}{2r^{3}}\,(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,.

We have used the identity

\displaystyle{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right]=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{3}\delta^{ij}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)+\frac{1}{4\pi r^{3}}\left(\delta^{ij}-3\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{1}{3}\delta^{ij}\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\frac{3}{4\pi r^{3}}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,,

(24)

with an obvious definition for the rank-2 component

(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}
. Here,

\delta^{ij}
times
(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}
vanishes
because the quadrupole term
(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}
is traceless
and we used the definition from Eq. (
7
).
After adding the contribution from the intrinsic quadrupole moment of the second particle, one
obtains

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{{\rm Q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{{\rm Q},1}+H^{(4)}_{{\rm Q},2}

(25)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,Q_{E1}}{2r^{3}}\,(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}

\displaystyle-\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,Q_{E2}}{2r^{3}}\,(S_{2}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,.

The remaining contributions involve a transverse photon instead of a Coulomb
photon. The first is the magnetic interaction of Fig.
1
(e) with two
convection vertices:

\displaystyle K^{(4)}_{\rm M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{1}}{2m_{1}}\left(p^{\prime}+p\right)^{i}\right)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{-\vec{k}^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)

(26)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{2}}{2m_{2}}\left(-p^{\prime}-p\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{-\mathrm{i}(-4\pi Z\alpha)}{4m_{1}m_{2}}(2p^{\prime})^{i}\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)(2p)^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi Z\alpha}{m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{p^{\prime i}(\vec{k}^{2}\delta^{ij}-k^{i}k^{j})p^{j}}{\vec{k}^{4}}\,,

where we applied the instantaneous approximation to replace
k^{2}
by
-\vec{k}^{2}
in the denominator and used the transversality of the transverse
propagator to make the replacements
(p^{\prime}+p)^{i}=(2p^{\prime}-k)^{i}\rightarrow(2p^{\prime})^{i}

and
(p^{\prime}+p)^{j}=(k+2p)^{j}\rightarrow(2p)^{j}
. The magnetic Hamiltonian is thus

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\rm M}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{4\pi Z\alpha}{m_{1}m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\delta^{ij}}{\vec{k}^{2}}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{4}}\right]p^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{4\pi Z\alpha}{m_{1}m_{2}}\,p^{i}\left\{\frac{\delta^{ij}}{4\pi r}-\frac{1}{8\pi r}\left(\delta^{ij}-\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\right)\right\}p^{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{Z\alpha}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}+\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}}{r}\right)p^{j}\,.

(27)

The Fermi spin-orbit contribution of Fig.
1
(f) gives a contribution

\displaystyle K^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{q_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}}(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k}\,)^{i}\right)\left[-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)\right]

(28)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{2}}{2m_{2}}\left(-p^{\prime}-p\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{(-4\pi Z\alpha)\tilde{g}_{1}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})^{i}\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}}(k+2p)^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{2\pi Z\alpha\tilde{g}_{1}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{\vec{k}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\times\vec{p}\right)\,.

The corresponding contribution to the Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{2\pi Z\alpha\tilde{g}_{1}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\left({\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\vec{k}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right]\times\vec{p}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\,\frac{2\pi Z\alpha\tilde{g}_{1}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}\vec{x}}{4\pi r^{3}}\times\vec{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}\,.

With particles 1 and 2 interchanged, one obtains

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},1}+H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},2}

(30)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}+\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{2}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}\,.

Finally, the Fermi spin-spin kernel of Fig.
1
(g) is

\displaystyle K^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}=

\displaystyle\;\left(-\frac{q_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}}(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})^{i}\right)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{-\vec{k}^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\;\times\left(-\frac{q_{2}\tilde{g}_{2}}{2m_{2}}(\vec{S}_{2}\times(-\vec{k}\,))^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{i}\,\frac{(-4\pi Z\alpha)\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\;\frac{(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k}\,)\cdot(\vec{S}_{2}\times\vec{k}\,)}{\vec{k}^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{i}\,\frac{(-4\pi Z\alpha)\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\;\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}\,\vec{k}^{2}-\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}\,\vec{k}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\,.

(31)

The corresponding Hamiltonian contribution is

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\,S_{1}^{i}\,\left\{{\mathcal{F}}\left[\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right]\right\}S_{2}^{j}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{3m_{1}m_{2}}\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}\,\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j}(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}}{r^{3}}\,.

The complete Breit Hamiltonian,
which we denote by
H^{(4)}
because
the terms are of order
(Z\alpha)^{4}
,
including all interactions on both particles, is

H^{(4)}=H^{(4)}_{\rm K}+H^{(4)}_{\rm M}+H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}+H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}+H^{(4)}_{\rm Q}+H^{(4)}_{\rm D}\,,

(33)

where

H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}=H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO}}+H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO}}\,.

(34)

In writing Eq. (
33
), we have
adopted the following sequence:
kinetic, magnetic, spin-dependent, quadrupole, and Darwin (finite size).
For convenience, we list the individual terms,

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{p^{4}}{8m_{1}^{3}}-\frac{p^{4}}{8m_{2}^{3}}\,,

(35a)

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\rm M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{Z\alpha}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\,p^{i}\,\left(\frac{\delta^{ij}+\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}}{r}\right)p^{j}\,,

(35b)

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{g}_{1}-1}{m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{g}_{1}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}

(35c)

\displaystyle+\frac{Z\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{\tilde{g}_{2}-1}{m_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{g}_{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)\frac{\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}}{r^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{3m_{1}m_{2}}\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}\,\delta^{3}(\vec{x}\,)

(35d)

\displaystyle+\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}}{r^{3}}\,,

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\rm Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{3\,Z\alpha}{2r^{3}}\biggl\{Q_{E1}(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}

(35e)

\displaystyle+Q_{E2}(S_{2}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\biggr\}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,,

\displaystyle H^{(4)}_{\rm D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi}{3}\,Z\alpha\,\left(r_{E1}^{2}+r_{E2}^{2}\right)\delta^{3}(\vec{x}\,)\,.

(35f)

When applied to deuteronium, our result here agrees with the
Breit Hamiltonian used in Refs.
AdJe2025prl
;
AdJe2025prr
.
The two radius terms
r_{E1}^{2}
and
r_{E2}^{2}

are in need of an interpretation. Namely,
as it has been explained in Ref.
PaKa1995
;
Je2011radii
,
for spin-
1/2
particles,
the Darwin–Foldy term is inherently included in the
definitions of the radii
r_{E1}^{2}
and
r_{E2}^{2}

of the two constituent particles.
So, if
r_{C1}^{2}
and
r_{C2}^{2}
are the
charge radii of the two particles,
then

\displaystyle r_{E1}^{2}\to

\displaystyle\;\frac{3\delta_{I}}{4m_{1}^{2}}+r_{C1}^{2}\,,

(36a)

\displaystyle r_{E2}^{2}\to

\displaystyle\;\frac{3\delta_{I}}{4m_{2}^{2}}+r_{C2}^{2}\,,

(36b)

where
\delta_{I}
is equal to one
for particles with half-integer spin, and equal to zero
for particles with integer spin.

IV
Relativistic and Recoil Corrections to eVP

The relativistic and recoil corrections to the one-loop eVP (Uehling) potential
are obtained from the one-loop VP corrected OC-gauge photon propagator of
Eq. (
2
) in the same way as the Breit Hamiltonian was obtained
from the uncorrected Coulomb gauge propagator. In the instantaneous limit needed
here, this one-loop propagator becomes,
according to Eq. (
2
),

D^{\mathrm{OC}:1}_{ij}(k)\rightarrow\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}v\,f_{1}(v)\begin{pmatrix}\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}&0\\
0&-\frac{\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\end{pmatrix}\,,

(37)

where
f_{1}
has been defined in Eq. (
3
).

The modification required for the diagrams of Fig.
1

can be represented as

H^{(5)}_{\mathrm{eVP}}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}v\,f_{1}(v)\,\mathcal{H}^{(4)}\,,

(38)

where
\mathcal{H}^{(4)}
is obtained
via the same matching procedure as
H^{(4)}
,
but with the Coulomb-gauge photon propagator
replaced with the optimized-gauge
propagator from Eq. (
2
),

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}=H^{(4)}\bigg|_{D^{C}\rightarrow D^{\mathrm{OC}:1}}\,.

(39)

For terms involving the Coulomb photon, the substitution is easy:

\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,.

(40)

For terms involving the transverse photon,
a little more work is required as the numerator algebra changes,
since the substitution is

\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}}\right)\rightarrow\frac{1}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)\,.

(41)

For example, the Coulomb potential
H_{C}=-\frac{Z\alpha}{r}
becomes

\mathcal{H}_{U}={\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{-4\pi Z\alpha}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]\\
=(-4\pi Z\alpha)\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\\
=\left(\frac{-Z\alpha}{r}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\,,

so that we can easily verify the modification
of the Coulomb potential to be equal to

{\cal V}_{U}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dv\,f_{1}(v)\,\mathcal{H}_{U}=\frac{\alpha}{\pi}\int_{0}^{1}dv\,f_{1}(v)\left(\frac{-Z\alpha}{r}\right)\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\,,

(42)

which is the Uehling potential.

The Coulomb-photon terms are the Darwin (finite-size)
contribution [see Fig. (
1
)(b) and
Eq. (
16
)],

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{{\rm D},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\,Z\alpha}{3}r_{E1}^{2}\;{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\vec{k}^{2}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]

(43)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2\pi\,Z\alpha}{3}r_{E1}^{2}\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}

(along with the corresponding contribution with the particles interchanged),
the Coulomb spin-orbit term of Fig.
1
(c) and
Eq. (
18
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{(-4\pi\,Z\alpha)(\tilde{g}_{1}-1)}{2m_{1}^{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\left({\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\vec{k}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]\times\vec{p}\right)

(44)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\frac{(-4\pi\,Z\alpha)(\tilde{g}_{1}-1)}{2m_{1}^{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\left(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\mathrm{i}\,\vec{r}\times\vec{p}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\alpha\,(\tilde{g}_{1}-1)}{2m_{1}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}\,,

with an obvious addition of
\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO},2}

where the particles are interchanged,

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO}}=\frac{Z\alpha\,(\tilde{g}_{1}-1)}{2m_{1}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}\\
+\frac{Z\alpha\,(\tilde{g}_{2}-1)}{2m_{2}^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}\,,

(45)

and the quadrupole term of Fig.
1
(d) and
Eq. (
25
),

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{Q,1}=\frac{(4\pi\,Z\alpha)Q_{E1}}{2}(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]=-\frac{3}{2}Z\alpha\\[0.4878pt]
\times Q_{E1}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}\left(1+\lambda r+\frac{\lambda^{2}r^{2}}{3}\right)(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,.

(46)

After adding the contribution from the quadrupole moment
of the second particle, one obtains

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{Q}=\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{Q,1}+\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{Q,2}=-\frac{3Z\alpha}{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}\left(1+\lambda r+\frac{\lambda^{2}r^{2}}{3}\right)\\
\times\left\{Q_{E1}\,(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}+Q_{E2}\,(S_{2}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\right\}\,.

(47)

The transverse photon terms require a bit more work.
The magnetic interaction
kernel of Fig.
1
(e) and
Eq. (
26
),

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{(4)}_{\rm M}=

\displaystyle\;\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{1}}{2m_{1}}\left(p^{\prime}+p\right)^{i}\right)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{-(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}

\displaystyle\;\times\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right)\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{2}}{2m_{2}}\left(-p^{\prime}-p\right)^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\mathrm{i}\,\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha}{4m_{1}m_{2}}(2p^{\prime}-k)^{i}\frac{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})\delta^{ij}-k^{i}k^{j}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}(k+2p)^{j}\,,

(48)

gives rise to a Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm M}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi Z\alpha}{4m_{1}m_{2}}{\mathcal{F}}\left[4p^{i}\frac{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})\delta^{ij}-k^{i}k^{j}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}p^{j}\right.

(49)

\displaystyle\left.+\frac{(2p^{i})\lambda^{2}k^{i}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}k^{j}(2p^{j})}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}\vec{k}^{2}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{Z\alpha}{2m_{1}m_{2}}p^{i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\Big[\delta^{ij}+(1+\lambda r)\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\Big]p^{j}

\displaystyle-\frac{Z\alpha}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\lambda^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{2}\right)

after use of the identity

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}p^{i}\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\hat{x}^{i}\right)-\left(\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\hat{x}^{i}\right)\mathrm{i}p^{i}=

\displaystyle\;\left[\partial_{i},\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\hat{x}^{i}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2}{r}\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{2}\right)\,.

(50)

The Fermi spin-orbit term of Fig.
1
(f)
and Eq. (
28
) is

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{q_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}}(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})^{i}\right)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{-(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}

(51)

\displaystyle\times\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}\right)\left(\frac{\mathrm{i}q_{2}}{2m_{2}}(-k^{j}-2p^{j})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{4\pi Z\alpha\tilde{g}_{1}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})\cdot(2\vec{p})}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}\,,

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\left(\vec{S}_{1}\times{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\vec{k}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]\right)\cdot(2\vec{p})

(52)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\left(\vec{S}_{1}\times\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\mathrm{i}\vec{x}\right)\cdot(2\vec{p})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}\,.

There is an obvious addition for
{\mathcal{H}}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO},2}
,
resulting in

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO}}=\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}\\
+\frac{Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{2}}{2m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}\,.

(53)

We summarize the sum of
\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO}}
and

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO}}
as the total spin-orbit term

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}=\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{CSO}}+\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSO}}\,.

(54)

Finally, the Fermi spin-spin contribution of
Fig.
1
(g) and Eq. (
III
) is

\displaystyle{\cal K}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{q_{1}\tilde{g}_{1}}{2m_{1}}(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})^{i}\right)\frac{\mathrm{i}}{-(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}

(55)

\displaystyle\times\left(\delta^{ij}-\frac{k^{i}k^{j}}{(\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2})}\right)\left(-\frac{q_{2}\tilde{g}_{2}}{2m_{2}}(\vec{S}_{2}\times(-\vec{k}))^{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{(\vec{S}_{1}\times\vec{k})\cdot(\vec{S}_{2}\times\vec{k})}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\,,

so that

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}=\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\,S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j}\,{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\delta^{ij}\vec{k}^{2}-k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]\\
=\frac{4\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\,\Big[\frac{\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}}{3}\delta^{ij}+(S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\Big]\,{\mathcal{F}}\left[\frac{\delta^{ij}\vec{k}^{2}-k^{i}k^{j}}{\vec{k}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\right]\\
=\frac{2\pi\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{3m_{1}m_{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}\,\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r})-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}\\
+\frac{3\,Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}\left(1+\lambda r+\frac{\lambda^{2}r^{2}}{3}\right)\,(S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\;(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,.

(56)

In summary, the Hamiltonian for relativistic-recoil corrections
to electronic VP is given by

H^{(5)}_{\mathrm{eVP}}=H^{(5)}_{\rm M}+H^{(5)}_{\mathrm{SO}}+H^{(5)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}+H^{(5)}_{\rm Q}+H^{(5)}_{\rm D}\,,

(57)

where the
H^{(5)}_{\rm X}
with
{\rm X}\in\{{\rm M},\mathrm{SO},\mathrm{FSS},{\rm Q},{\rm D}\}

are obtained from the corresponding

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm X}

by the integration over the spectral
function of vacuum polarization, which is given in
Eq. (
38
). We list the terms as follows,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm M}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{Z\alpha}{2m_{1}m_{2}}p^{i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\Big[\delta^{ij}+(1+\lambda r)\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\Big]p^{j}

\displaystyle\;-\frac{Z\alpha\,\lambda^{2}}{4m_{1}m_{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{2}\right)\,,

(58a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{Z\alpha\,\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{2r^{3}}(1+\lambda r)\left\{\left(\frac{\tilde{g}_{1}-1}{m_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{g}_{1}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{L}\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.+\left(\frac{\tilde{g}_{2}-1}{m_{2}^{2}}+\frac{\tilde{g}_{2}}{m_{1}m_{2}}\right)\vec{S}_{2}\cdot\vec{L}\right\}\,,

(58b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2\pi Z\alpha\,\tilde{g}_{1}\tilde{g}_{2}}{3m_{1}m_{2}}\vec{S}_{1}\cdot\vec{S}_{2}\,\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}

\displaystyle\;\times(S_{1}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\;(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\,,

(58c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm Q}=

\displaystyle\;-\frac{3Z\alpha}{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}\left(1+\lambda r+\frac{\lambda^{2}r^{2}}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\;\times\left\{Q_{E1}\,(S_{1}^{i}S_{1}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\right.

\displaystyle\;\left.+Q_{E2}\,(S_{2}^{i}S_{2}^{j})^{(2)}\,(\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j})^{(2)}\right\}\,,

(58d)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm D}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{2\pi Z\alpha}{3}r_{E1}^{2}\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}

\displaystyle\;+\frac{2\pi Z\alpha}{3}r_{E2}^{2}\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}\,.

(58e)

These terms, in the limit

V.1
Spinless Bound Systems: Pionium

One central point of our investigations

relativistic-recoil correction to eVP,
applicable to bound Coulomb systems
with constituent spin-0, spin-
1/2

One might think that this task has already been

and the spin operators accordingly,

However, there are a few subtleties

connected with Eq. (

particular bound systems of special interest.
First, we investigate pionium

V.2
Fine–Structure of Muonic Hydrogen

For muonic hydrogen, one encounters the
masses

m_{1}=m_{\mu}\,,\qquad m_{2}=m_{N}=m_{p}\,.

(68)

Here,
m_{\mu}
is the muon mass, and
m_{N}=m_{p}

is the nuclear mass.
We attempt to reconcile the radiatively
corrected Breit Hamiltonian, given in Eq. (
58
),
with the Hamiltonian used in
Refs.
Pa1996mu
;
VePa2004
;
Je2011pra
;
KaIvKo2012
;
AdJe2024gauge
,
for the evaluation of relativistic
and recoil corrections to vacuum polarization
in muonic hydrogenlike ions.
For the discussion of the
fine-structure, we can exclude hyperfine
effects (due to the nuclear spin).

There is slight subtlety:
even for a point-like proton,
the Darwin term in Eq. (
58
)
gives a nonvanishing contribution
originating from the proton,
because of the definition
of the charge radius on which
Eqs. (
35f
) and (
58
) are
based. Namely, for a pointlike nucleus,
one has

r_{E2}^{2}\to\frac{3\delta_{I}}{4m_{N}^{2}}
,
according to Eq. (
36
).
The following replacements are
thus relevant for the muonic systems,

r_{E1}^{2}\to\frac{3}{4m_{\mu}^{2}}\,,\quad r_{E2}^{2}\to\frac{3}{4m_{p}^{2}}+r_{p}^{2}\,,\quad\tilde{g}_{1}\to 2\,.

(69)

where
r_{p}^{2}
is the mean-square proton charge radius.
One then takes into account the fact that the
orbiting particle is a spin-
1/2
particle,
ignores hyperfine effects (one replaces
\vec{S}_{2}\to\vec{0}
),
and observes that the intrinsic quadrupole
moments of the muon and proton vanish,

\vec{S}_{1}\to\frac{\vec{\sigma}_{\mu}}{2}\,,\quad Q_{E1}\to 0\,,\quad Q_{E2}\to 0\,.

(70)

Here
\vec{\sigma}_{\mu}
is the
vector of Pauli spin matrices for the muon spin.
Hence, for the fine-structure of
muonic hydrogen, we can replace

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm Q}\to 0
,
and we can also replace
the Fermi spin-spin interaction
by
\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{FSS}}\to 0
.

For muonic hydrogen,
the magnetic term takes the form

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm M}\to-\frac{Z\alpha}{2m_{\mu}m_{p}}p^{i}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\Big[\delta^{ij}+(1+\lambda r)\hat{x}^{i}\hat{x}^{j}\Big]p^{j}\\
-\frac{Z\alpha\lambda^{2}}{4m_{\mu}m_{p}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{2}\right)\,.

(71)

The latter form is equal to the sum

\delta\mathcal{H}_{2}+\delta\mathcal{H}_{3}
,
used in their respective forms
defined in Eqs. (63d) and (63e)
of Ref.
AdJe2024gauge
.
The spin-orbit term takes the form

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\mathrm{SO}}\to Z\alpha\,\left(\frac{1}{4m_{\mu}^{2}}+\frac{1}{2m_{\mu}m_{p}}\right)\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{r^{3}}\\
\times(1+\lambda r)\,\vec{\sigma}_{\mu}\cdot\vec{L}\equiv\delta\mathcal{H}_{4}\,,

(72)

where
\delta\mathcal{H}_{4}
is used in the notation
of Eq. (63f) of Ref.
AdJe2024gauge
.

Now we investigate the Darwin term.
One verifies that, for muonic hydrogen-like ions,
the individual terms listed in Eq. (
58
)
can be expressed as a sum of two terms,

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{D}=\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{D,1}+\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{D,2}\,.

(73)

The first term is

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm D,1}=\frac{\pi Z\alpha}{2}\left(\frac{1}{m_{\mu}^{2}}+\frac{1}{m_{p}^{2}}\right)\\
\times\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}\equiv\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}\,,

(74)

where
\delta\mathcal{H}_{1}
is used in the notation
of Eq. (63c) of Ref.
AdJe2024gauge
.
The finite-size contribution is

\mathcal{H}^{(4)}_{\rm D,2}=\frac{2\pi Z\alpha}{3}r_{p}^{2}\left\{\delta^{(3)}(\vec{r}\,)-\lambda^{2}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\right\}\equiv\delta\mathcal{H}_{\rm FS}\,,

(75)

as discussed in Eq. (65) of Ref.
Pa1996mu
.
We have checked our results above
against corresponding entries in Ref.
AdJe2024gauge
,
and references therein.
For the comparison to the literature,
it is important to realize that,

e.g.
, the detailed breakdown of the
corrections to the fine-structure of muonic hydrogen
in Ref.
Je2011pra
excludes the
finite-radius correction, which is
treated, in both first- and second-order
perturbation theory,
in Eqs. (65) and (66) of Ref.
Pa1996mu
.

V.3
Hyperfine Structure of Muonic Hydrogen

For the hyperfine structure of muonic hydrogen,
we now investigate terms which involve the
spin operator of the proton,

\vec{S}_{2}=\vec{S}_{p}=\vec{\sigma}_{p}/2
.
The

\tilde{g}_{1}=\tilde{g}_{\mu}=2

(76)

where we use the value from Ref.
MoNeTaTi2025

We recall the spin operator of the muon as

we can replace

\vec{\sigma}_{p}

the spin-orbit term

of Ref.
Pa1996mu
].

and corresponding results for the hyperfine

for
1S

VII
Conclusions

The calculation of the spectrum of
heavy bound systems of
particles heavier than the electron
constitutes an involved task because
radiative and recoil corrections are
more intertwined than for lighter
systems (for details, see Ref.
PaEtAl2024
).
In the heavy systems, electronic vacuum-polarization
takes the role of the dominant
radiative correction to energy levels,
being larger than self-energy effects
(see Refs.
Pa1996mu
;
Je2011pra
;
Bo2011preprint
;
Bo2012
;
VePa2004
;
KaIvKo2012
;
KaIvKa2013
;
KaKoShIv2017
;
AdJe2024gauge

as well as Refs.
AdJe2025prl
;
AdJe2025prr
).

In our investigations, we have generalized
the calculation of relativistic-recoil
corrections to electronic vacuum polarization
to bound systems where the constituent particles
are either spinless or have spin
1/2
or spin
1
.
To this end, we have used a version of NRQED where the mass scale separating
soft from hard energies is much larger than the
electron mass
AdJe2024gauge
. This extension
of NRQED has been identified in Ref.
HiLePaSo2013

and discussed in the context of muonic atoms in
Ref.
AdJe2024gauge
.
From the interaction kernels,
we have verified the Breit (interaction)
Hamiltonian for particles of
spin-1, in Sec.
III
, and discussed the
generalization to spins 0 and 1/2.
The Breit Hamiltonian is based
on the exchange of massless photons [for
the propagator, see Eq. (
4
)].
The result is given in Eq. (
33
).
For the treatment of vacuum polarization,
we have generalized the derivation of the
effective Hamiltonian (see Sec.
IV
)
to the exchange of a massive photon,
formulated in the one-loop optimized gauge [see
Eq. (
2
)].
We have derived the Hamiltonian for
the massive exchange in Eq. (
58
)
and have discussed its application to systems composed
of particles having spins 0, 1/2, and 1.

The application of our formalism
to bound systems of interest has been discussed in
Sec.
V
, with a special emphasis
on pionium, muonic hydrogen and muonic deuterium.

Appendix A
Fourier Transforms

Some useful three-dimensional Fourier transforms are

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\frac{1}{\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\,,

(117a)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\vec{k}\,^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\delta^{(3)}(\vec{x}\,)-\lambda^{2}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\,,

(117b)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\lambda^{2}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\lambda r}-1\right)\,.

(117c)

For integrals with the square of
(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})

in the denominator, we obtain

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{8\pi\lambda}\,,

(117d)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\vec{k}\,^{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\left(1-\frac{\lambda r}{2}\right)\,,

(117e)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\lambda^{4}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\lambda r}-1-\tfrac{\lambda r}{2}\right)\,.

(117f)

The following vector-valued integrals are also relevant,

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\frac{k^{i}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r^{2}}(1+\lambda r)\,\mathrm{i}\,\hat{x}^{i}\,,

(118a)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\frac{\lambda^{2}k^{i}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{4\pi r^{2}}\left(\mathrm{e}^{\lambda r}-1-\lambda r\right)\,\mathrm{i}\,\hat{x}^{i}\,,

(118b)

\displaystyle\int\frac{\mathrm{d}^{3}k}{(2\pi)^{3}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\vec{k}\cdot\vec{x}}\frac{k^{i}}{(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})^{2}}=

\displaystyle\;\frac{\mathrm{e}^{-\lambda r}}{8\pi}\,\mathrm{i}\,\hat{x}^{i}\,,

(118c)

In these calculations, the general identity for Fourier transforms
{\mathcal{F}}\Big[k^{i}f(\vec{k}\,)\Big](\vec{x})=-\mathrm{i}\partial_{i}{\mathcal{F}}\Big[f(\vec{k}\,)\Big](\vec{x})
was used,
along with partial-fraction decompositions such as

\frac{1}{\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2}}=\frac{1}{\vec{k}\,^{2}}-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{\vec{k}\,^{2}(\vec{k}\,^{2}+\lambda^{2})}.

(119)

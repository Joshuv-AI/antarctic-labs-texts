Unified bounds for the independence number of graph powers
Aida Abiad, Jiang Zhou

Abstract
For a graph G G , its k k -th power G k G^{k} is constructed by placing an edge between two vertices if they are within distance k k of each other. The k k -independence number α k ​ ( G ) \alpha_{k}(G) is defined as the independence number of G k G^{k} . By using general semidefinite programming and polynomial methods, we derive sharp bounds for the k k -independence number of a graph, which extend and unify various existing results. Our work also allows us to easily derive some new bounds for α k ​ ( G ) \alpha_{k}(G) . Keywords: graph power, independence number, eigenvalues.



1
Introduction

We consider simple undirected graphs. Let
G
be a graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G)
. Let
d(i,j)
denote the distance between vertices
i
and
j
. A
k
-independent set
in a graph
G
is a vertex subset
U\subseteq V(G)
such that
d(i,j)>k
for any two distinct vertices
i,j\in U
. The
k
-independence number
of
G
, denoted by
\alpha_{k}(G)
, is the size of the largest
k
-independent set in
G
. The
k^{\text{th}}

power graph
of
G
, denoted by
G^{k}
, is the graph with the same vertices as
G
where two vertices are adjacent if they are at distance at most
k
in
G
. Alternatively,
\alpha_{k}(G)=\alpha(G^{k})
, and
\alpha_{1}(G)=\alpha(G)
equals the independence number of
G
. Graph powers are useful in designing efficient algorithms for certain combinatorial optimization problems, see, e.g.,
[
8
,
9
]
. In distributed computing, the
k
-th power of graph
G
represents the possible flow of information during
k
rounds of communication in a distributed network of processors organized according to
G

[
27
]
. For other work involving graph powers, see, e.g.,
[
7
,
12
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
.

For an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph
G
, the well-known Hoffman ratio bound says that
\alpha(G)\leq n\frac{|\tau|}{d-\tau}
, where
\tau
is the minimum eigenvalue of
G
. Several generalized Hoffman ratio bounds, some of which use Laplacian eigenvalues, have appeared in the literature, see
[
11
,
17
,
22
,
20
,
29
]
. The Lovász theta function
\vartheta(G)
, which was introduced in
[
19
]
, gives an upper bound for
\alpha(G)
which is known to be better than the Hoffman bound. The Lovász theta function is a powerful tool for studying the Shannon capacity
\Theta(G)
. Indeed, Lovász
[
19
]
proved that
\alpha(G)\leq\Theta(G)\leq\vartheta(G)
. The Schrijver theta function
\vartheta^{\prime}(G)
, introduced in
[
28
]
, is an even better upper bound for
\alpha(G)
since it holds that
\alpha(G)\leq\vartheta^{\prime}(G)\leq\vartheta(G)
. In
[
30
]
,
\vartheta(G)
,
\vartheta^{\prime}(G)
and various generalized Hoffman ratio bounds were unified into one form.

Since
\alpha_{k}(G)=\alpha(G^{k})
, most bounds on the independence number can be applied directly to upper bound the
k
-independence number. However, to use the known eigenvalue bounds for
\alpha
on
G^{k}
, such as the Hoffman ratio bound, one needs to know the spectrum of
G^{k}
, which in general cannot be derived from the spectrum of
G
itself. Therefore, Abiad et al.
[
3
]
proposed an inertia- and ratio-type bound on the
k
-independence number that only depend on the spectrum of
G
. These inertia- and ratio-type bounds, which are known to be sharp for several graph classes, use a degree-
k
polynomial which can be chosen freely. The quality of such bounds depends on the choice of the polynomial, so finding the best possible upper bound for a given graph is in fact an optimization problem. For the case of
k
-partially walk-regular graphs, the optimal polynomials for the optimization of the ratio-type bound was studied by Fiol
[
13
]
. For general graphs, the optimal polynomials for the inertia-type bound was shown by Abiad et al.
[
1
]
. Recently, the ratio-type bound on
\alpha_{k}(G)
was used to estimate the maximum size of a code in a certain metric, often outperforming the state of the art coding bounds, see
[
2
,
4
]
.

In this paper we present general semidefinite programming (SDP) and polynomial methods to establish unified bounds on
\alpha_{k}(G)
, and we discuss its applications by deriving several existing results in the literature (such as the ratio-type bound). Our framework extends the recent results by the second author
[
30
]
. Moreover, as a byproduct of our general method, we obtain several of the mentioned known bounds on
\alpha_{k}(G)
such as
[
3
, Theorem 3.2]
(or alternatively
[
6
, Corollary 28]
),
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
and
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
. Our framework also allows us to easily derive some new bounds for
\alpha_{k}(G)
.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we establish the used notation and introduce some preliminary lemmas. In Section 3, we give unified bounds on
\alpha_{k}(G)
,
\vartheta(\overline{G^{k}})
and
\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{k}})
in terms of graph matrices of
G
. Based on the results in Section
3
, we derive various old and new algebraic bounds on
\alpha_{k}(G)
in Section
4
. We end up with some concluding remarks in Section
5
.

A framework to unify bounds on
\alpha_{k}

A partition

is called a
k
-distance coloring
of a graph
G
if
V_{1},\ldots,V_{t}
are
k
-independent sets in
G
. Actually, a
k
-distance coloring of

is a proper coloring of
G^{k}
.

For an
n
-vertex graph
G
, let
\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)
denote the set of real matrix-vector pairs
(M,x)
such that

(a)
M
is a positive semidefinite
n\times n
matrix indexed by vertices of
G
such that
(M)_{ij}=0
if
d(i,j)>k
.

(b)
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{\top}\in R(M)
is a total nonzero vector.

Using Lemma 2 we can then obtain the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Let
(M,x)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)
be a matrix-vector pair associated with an
n
-vertex graph
G
. Then the following statements hold:

(1) For any
k
-independent set
S
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle|S|^{2}\leq x^{\top}M^{\#}x\sum_{u\in S}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

(2) The
k
-independence number
\alpha_{k}(G)
satisfies

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq x^{\top}M^{\#}x\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

(3) For any
k
-distance coloring
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}|V_{i}|^{2}\leq x^{\top}M^{\#}x\sum_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)=\mathcal{M}(G^{k})
, parts (1) and (2) follow from Lemma

. Part (1) implies part (3).
∎

Let
\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
denote the set of polynomials of real coefficients and degree at most
k
. For a graph
G
, let
\mathcal{S}(G)
be the set of real symmetric matrices
A
such that
(A)_{ij}=0
if
d(i,j)>1
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{S}(G)
be a matrix associated with a graph
G
such that
A
has an eigenvalue
\lambda
with a total nonzero eigenvector
y
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
p(A)
is positive semidefinite and
p(\lambda)>0
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\frac{y^{\top}y}{p(\lambda)}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A))_{uu}}{y_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
Ay=\lambda y
, we have
p(A)y=p(\lambda)y
. So
y\in R(p(A))
. From the definition of
A
, we know that
(p(A))_{ij}=0
whenever

. Hence
(p(A),e)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)
. By Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle p(A)^{\#}y=p(\lambda)^{-1}y,

\displaystyle y^{\top}p(A)^{\#}y=\frac{y^{\top}y}{p(\lambda)}.

By Theorem
5
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\frac{y^{\top}y}{p(\lambda)}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A))_{uu}}{y_{u}^{2}}.\qed

Let
\overline{G}
denote the complement of a graph
G
.

Theorem 7
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{S}(G)
be a matrix associated with a graph
G
, and let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
p(A)
is positive semidefinite and
{\rm tr}~p(A)>0
. Then

\displaystyle\vartheta(\overline{G^{k}})\geq\frac{e^{\top}p(A)e}{{\rm tr}~p(A)},

where

is the all-ones vector. Furthermore, if
p(A)
is nonnegative, then

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{k}})\geq\frac{e^{\top}p(A)e}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}.

Proof.

Let
B=({\rm tr}~p(A))^{-1}p(A)
, then
B
is positive semidefinite with
{\rm tr}~B=1
. Notice that
(B)_{ij}=0
for every pair
\{i,j\}\in E(\overline{G^{k}})
. By Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\vartheta(\overline{G^{k}})\geq e^{\top}Be=\frac{e^{\top}p(A)e}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}.

If
p(A)
is nonnegative, then by the definition of
\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{k}})
, we have

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{k}})\geq\frac{e^{\top}p(A)e}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}.\qed

We can derive the following result from Theorems

and
7
.

Theorem 8
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{S}(G)
be a matrix associated with an
n
-vertex graph
G
such that
A
has constant row sum
d
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
p(A)
is positive semidefinite and

. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{n}{p(d)}\max_{u\in V(G)}(p(A))_{uu},

\displaystyle\vartheta(\overline{G^{k}})

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{np(d)}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}.

Proof.

Let
e
be the all-ones vector, then
Ae=de
and
p(A)e=p(d)e
. By Theorem

, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\frac{n}{p(d)}\max_{u\in V(G)}(p(A))_{uu}.\qed

By

, we know that
{\rm tr}~p(A)>0
. By Theorem

, we have

\displaystyle\vartheta(\overline{G^{k}})\geq\frac{e^{\top}p(A)e}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}=\frac{np(d)}{{\rm tr}~p(A)}.

A
walk
(of length
k
) in a graph

is an alternating sequence

\displaystyle u_{1}e_{1}u_{2}e_{2}\cdots u_{k}e_{k}u_{k+1}

such that
v_{i},v_{i+1}
are two distinct end-vertices of the edge

(
i=1,\ldots,k
). If
u_{1}=u_{k+1}
, then this walk is called a closed walk. Let

denote the number of walks of length

in

, and let

denote the number of closed walks of length

in

.

Corollary 9
.

Let
G
be a graph with at least one edge. For any positive integer

, we have

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{2k}})\geq\frac{w_{2k}(G)}{c_{2k}(G)}.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
. For any positive integer
t
, it is well known that
(A^{t})_{ij}
equals to the number of walks of length

from the vertex

to the vertex

. Then

is a positive semidefinite nonegative matrix and

. Let

be the all-ones vector. By Theorem

\displaystyle\vartheta^{\prime}(\overline{G^{2k}})\geq\frac{e^{\top}A^{2k}e}{{\rm tr}~A^{2k}}=\frac{w_{2k}(G)}{c_{2k}(G)}.\qed

4
Consequences

4.1
An SDP bound on
\alpha_{k}

The spectral radius of the adjacency matrix of a graph
G
is called the
spectral radius
of
G
. The
principal eigenvector
of a connected graph
G
is the unique positive eigenvector
y
corresponding to the spectral radius of
G
such that
\sum_{u\in V(G)}y_{u}^{2}=1
.

Corollary 10
.

Let
G
be a connected graph with spectral radius
\rho
and principal eigenvector
y
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
p(A)
is positive semidefinite and
p(\rho)>0
, where
A
is the adjacency matrix of
G
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq p(\rho)^{-1}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A))_{uu}}{y_{u}^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, then
Ay=\rho y
and
y^{\top}y=1
. By Theorem
6
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq p(\rho)^{-1}\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A))_{uu}}{y_{u}^{2}}.\qed

In order to optimize the bound from Corollary
10
, we would like to find the polynomial
p
which minimizes the right-hand side for a given graph
G
. Some observations:

•

The expression is invariant under multiplying
p
by a positive scalar. We may therefore assume that
\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(p(A))_{uu}}{y_{uu}^{2}}=1
.

•

Maximizing over
u
is equivalent to requiring
(p(A))_{uu}/y_{uu}^{2}\leq 1
for all
u\in V(G)
. This gets rid of the
\min\max
.

•

The objective function then simplifies to
\min p(\rho)^{-1}
, which we can find by maximizing
p(\rho)
.

We therefore solve the following SDP.

\boxed{\begin{array}[]{rl}{\tt maximize}&p(\rho)\\
{\tt subject\ to}&(p(A))_{uu}\leq y^{2}_{uu}\quad u\in V\\
&p(\rho)>0\\
&p(A)\succcurlyeq 0\end{array}}

The value of the bound in Corollary
10
then equals one divided by the optimal value of the SDP.

4.2
Algebraic bounds on
\alpha_{k}

From Theorem

, we can derive
[

Corollary 11

(Ratio-type bound,

[

]
)
.

Let
G
be a regular graph with

\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}(x)
be a polynomial such that

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq n\frac{\max_{u\in V(G)}(p(A))_{uu}-\lambda(p)}{p(\lambda_{1})-\lambda(p)}.

Proof.

Let
A
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, then
A
has constant row sum
\lambda_{1}
since
G
is regular. Let
q(x)=p(x)-\lambda(p)
. Since
p(\lambda_{1})>\lambda(p)=\min_{2\leq i\leq n}\{p(\lambda_{i})\}
, we know that
q(\lambda_{1})=p(\lambda_{1})-\lambda(p)>0
and
q(A)=p(A)-\lambda(p)I
is positive semidefinite.

Finally, by Theorem
8
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\frac{n}{q(\lambda_{1})}\max_{u\in V(G)}(q(A))_{uu}.\qed

The optimization of the above ratio-type bound for general

was investigated by Fiol
[

]
, and its tightness was studied by Abiad et al

A graph

Let

From Theorem

as follows.

Corollary 12
.

[

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq{\rm tr}~f_{k}(A).

Let

, and

Since

The optimization and tightness of the above bound was investigated by Fiol in

The

, where
D
is the diagonal matrix of vertex degrees of
G
,
A
is the adjacency matrix of
G
. For a vertex subset
S
, let
d_{k}(S)=|S|^{-1}\sum_{u\in S}(L^{k})_{uu}
. Eigenvalues of
L
are called Laplacian eigenvalues of
G
.

Some inequalities involving independent sets and Laplacian eigenvalues can be found in
[
21
, inequality (6.15)]
and
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
. By using the largest Laplacian eigenvalue and our general framework from Section
3
, we can also obtain the following upper bound for a
k
-independent set
S
of a graph.

Theorem 13
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with largest Laplacian eigenvalue
\mu>0
. For any
k
-independent set
S
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle|S|\leq\frac{n(\mu^{k}-d_{k}(S))}{\mu^{k}}.

Proof.

Let
e
be the all-ones vector, then
(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})e=\mu^{k}e
. So
e\in R(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})
. Notice that
(L^{k})_{ij}=0
whenever
d(i,j)>k
. Hence
(\mu^{k}I-L^{k},e)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)
. By Lemma
1
, we have

\displaystyle(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})^{\#}e=\mu^{-k}e,

\displaystyle e^{\top}(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})^{\#}e=\frac{n}{\mu^{k}}.

By Theorem
5
, we have

\displaystyle|S|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq e^{\top}(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})^{\#}e\sum_{u\in S}(\mu^{k}I-L^{k})_{uu},

\displaystyle|S|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{n(\mu^{k}-d_{k}(S))}{\mu^{k}}.\qed

Taking
k=1
in Theorem
13
, we obtain
[
17
, Theorem 4.3]
.

Corollary 14
.

[
17
]
Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with largest Laplacian eigenvalue
\mu>0
. For any independent set
S
of
G
, we have

\displaystyle|S|\leq\frac{n(\mu-\overline{d}_{S})}{\mu},

where
\overline{d}_{S}
is the average degree of all vertices in
S
.

4.3
Bound for the distance-
k
coloring

For the
k
-distance coloring of a regular graph, we can derive the following inequality from the new framework.

Theorem 15
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
d
-regular graph with adjacency matrix
A
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{R}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
p(A)
is positive semidefinite and
p(d)>0
. For any
k
-distance coloring
V(G)=V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{t}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}|V_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{p(d)}{\rm tr}~p(A).

Proof.

Let
e
be the all-ones vector, then
Ae=de
and
p(A)e=p(d)e
. So
e\in R(p(A))
and
(p(A),e)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)
. By Lemma 1, we have

\displaystyle p(A)^{\#}e=p(d)^{-1}e,

\displaystyle e^{\top}p(A)^{\#}e=\frac{n}{p(d)}.

By Theorem
5
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}|V_{i}|^{2}\leq\frac{n}{p(d)}{\rm tr}~p(A).\qed

4.4
A minimum rank-type bound on
\alpha_{k}

Let
\mathbb{F}_{k}[x]
denote the set of polynomials with coefficients in a field
\mathbb{F}
and degree at most
k
.

Theorem 16
.

Let
A\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{F}}(G)
be a matrix associated with an
n
-vertex graph
G
. Let
p(x)\in\mathbb{F}_{k}[x]
be a polynomial such that
(p(A))_{uu}\neq 0
for each
u\in V(G)
. Then

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\Theta(G^{k})\leq{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(p(A)).

Proof.

Note that
(p(A))_{uv}=0
if
d(u,v)>k
. By Lemma
3
, we have

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}(G)=\alpha(G^{k})\leq\Theta(G^{k})\leq{\rm rank}_{\mathbb{F}}(p(A)).\qed

The tightness and optimization of the above bound is currently being investigated in
[
5
]
.

5
Concluding remarks

The proposed framework to unify existing and to derive several new algebraic bounds on
\alpha_{k}
has a direct application in coding theory. Indeed, several of the eigenvalue bounds which follow form our framework (such as Corollary
11
) have recently been shown to be very useful for bounding the maximal size of a code with a certain minimum distance in several metrics, see
[
2
,
4
]
.

The
distance-
k
chromatic number

\chi_{k}(G)
of a graph
G
is defined as the chromatic number of
G^{k}
. Since
\chi_{k}(G)\geq\frac{n}{\alpha_{k}(G)}
, our framework yields lower bounds for the distance-
k
chromatic number of a graph. Moreover, since the
distance-
k
chromatic index

\chi_{k}^{\prime}(G)
satisfies

\chi_{k}^{\prime}(G)=\chi_{k}(L(G))=\chi_{1}(L(G)^{k}),

where
L(G)
is the line graph of
G
, then our framework also provides lower bounds for the distance-
k
chromatic index.

From
[
30
, Theorem 4.1]
, we know that the Lovász theta function
\vartheta
of the power graph holds

\displaystyle\vartheta(G^{k})=\min_{(M,x)\in\mathcal{M}_{k}(G)}x^{\top}M^{\#}x\max_{u\in V(G)}\frac{(M)_{uu}}{x_{u}^{2}}.

Since using
[
19
]
we know that
\Theta(G^{k})\leq\vartheta(G^{k})
, then all algebraic bounds on
\alpha_{k}(G)
that we derived in this paper are also upper bounds of
\Theta(G^{k})
and
\vartheta(G^{k})
.

In
[
15
]
, Fiol (improving work from
[
16
]
) obtained the following eigenvalue bound on
\alpha_{k}(G)
.

Theorem 17
.

[
15
]
If
G
is a regular graph with adjacency eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
, then

\alpha_{k}(G)\leq\frac{2n}{P_{k}(\lambda_{1})},

where
P_{k}
is the
k
-alternating polynomial of
G
.

This result was later generalized to nonregular graphs in
[
14
]
. The polynomial
P_{k}
is defined by the solution of a linear
programming problem which depends on the spectrum of the graph
G
. Moreover, despite the bound from Theorem
17
uses an analogue proof technique as the obtained Corollary
11
, it seems not to follow from the framework proposed in this work.

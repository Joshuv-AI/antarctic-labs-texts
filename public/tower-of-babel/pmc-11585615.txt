Improved PICEA-g-based multi-objective optimization scheduling method for distribution network with large-scale electric vehicles
Authors: Meiyi Huo, Songling Pang, Hailong Zhao
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractLarge-scale electric vehicle access to the distribution grid for charging can affect the security and economic operation of the grid. In this paper, an optimal scheduling method for large-scale EV access to the distribution grid based on the improved preference-inspired co-evolutionary algorithm using goal vectors (PICEA-g) is proposed. First, a large-scale response scheduling model is developed based on EVs as flexible loads. Then, a multi-objective optimization model is established by considering five factors: grid load fluctuation, user cost, environmental governance, user flexible travel time, and charge state. Finally, multi-scenario simulation analysis is performed to verify the effectiveness of the proposed control strategy and optimization algorithm. The experimental results show that the improved PICEA-g algorithm outperforms the remaining several algorithms when the size of electric vehicles is larger than 50. And based on this method, it realizes the effective management of loads in the region, and reduces the management cost of microgrids and the cost of environmental pollution control, and ithe users’ flexible travel time and state of charge.Keywords: Large-scale electric vehicles, Distribution network, Flexible load, Improved PICEA-g, Optimization schedulingSubject terms: Engineering, Electrical and electronic engineering

Introduction

In the face of global energy and resource shortages and environmental pollution problems, the urgency of promoting the development of green, low-carbon, and sustainable energy has become a common consensus worldwide. The State Council General Office’s New Energy Vehicle Industry Development Plan (2021–2035) underscores the national strategy of developing new energy vehicles. Under the joint action of environmental protection needs, new energy vehicle technology development, and national policy support, the number of Electric vehicles(EVs) is set to rise. However, the impact of large-scale EVs on the power grid’s load curve and its implications for safety and reliability must be considered1,2. Therefore, optimizing flexible load scheduling for EVs’ large-scale access to the distribution network is vital and a pressing need in the context of global energy and environmental challenges. The problem of flexible load optimization scheduling for large-scale EVs connected to distribution networks usually has multiple optimization objectives in its design and planning, which can be solved using artificial intelligence algorithms such as particle swarm optimization (PSO)3 algorithm, genetic algorithm (GA)4, sine cosine algorithm5, and horse herd optimization algorithm6. It has become a hot topic of research for many scholars.

In7, with the annual comprehensive cost of charging stations as the target, an adaptive GA is utilized for site selection in the case of high inventory of EVs, which can obtain planning solutions faster. In8, a prediction model of EV access power grid energy state was constructed, and a PSO algorithm was used to minimize load fluctuation standard deviation and network loss of distribution network9. used an improved PSO algorithm to improve the charging station layout planning, which reduces the layout cost, increases the charging station utilization and meets the user demand. In10, non-dominated sorting genetic algorithm-II(NSGA-II) was adopted to solve the multi-objective optimal scheduling model of distribution network with EVs access by minimizing the operating cost and load fluctuation variance of distribution network with large-scale EVs access as optimization objectives. These two objectives are the main optimization objectives of EV charging and discharging plan11. However, pollution gases generated by fuel combustion in power generation equipment will cause damage to the environment, and these pollutants need to be controlled, so the cost of environmental governance must also be considered as one of the optimization objectives. In12, a low-carbon economy scheduling model with the lowest system operating cost and carbon transaction cost as the optimization objective was optimized using the whale optimization algorithm. In13, a response scheduling model of large-scale electric vehicles was constructed, and the PSO algorithm was used to realize effective charge management in various scenarios within the region, considering three factors: power grid operation security, environmental benefits, and user participation.14 established a multi-objective optimization model to optimize the generation cost, load shedding compensation cost and microgrid environmental benefits. Various scenarios were designed based on whether it was a working day or not and weather combinations, and the optimal scheduling of the grid was achieved using an improved chaotic PSO algorithm.

However, the optimization scheduling method proposed in the above literature still needs to consider the two indexes of flexible user travel time and user travel at any time. Hence, the optimization results are often at the cost of reducing user travel time. As a means of transportation, electric vehicles must first meet the travel needs of users. Therefore, it is necessary to reserve more free time for users to make travel choices. In addition, EVs’ arrival, stopping, and departure times are highly uncertain15. To avoid over-discharge, the state of charge(SOC) in a low state will not be able to meet the needs of the next trip. It is essential to maintain a high SOC throughout the scheduling of departures and future travel needs at all times. The existing literatures do not consider that different objective functions are always conflicting and cannot reach the optimum at the same time when solving the optimal scheduling problem for charging and discharging large-scale EVs. And there are more optimization objectives in this paper. PICEA-g16 has the characteristics of high solution efficiency and de-aggregation quality, and utilizes the cooperative and competitive relationship between populations and stochastic preferences to guide the evolution. However, the algorithm is prone to incomplete search and falling into local optimality. In order to improve the search ability for solving this large-scale discrete combinatorial optimization problem with decision variables, different computational resources should be allocated to different subspaces, so differentiated resource allocation is considered, and in this paper, we propose an improved PICEA-g incorporating a differentiated resource allocation strategy for scheduling optimization of charging and discharging of flexible loads of EVs.

Based on the above analysis, to achieve microgrid stability, reduce environmental pollution, operate economically, and solve the user’s travel convenience, this paper will establish a large-scale EVs response scheduling model, aiming at this multi-objective optimization problem with the objectives of minimum load fluctuation variance, user cost, environmental governance, and maximum flexible travel time and state of charge of electric vehicles. An improved PICEA-g is proposed to solve the multi-objective optimization problem. Finally, taking the IEEE 33 node system as an example, the effectiveness of this scheduling method under different scenarios is analyzed through simulation experiments, and the relationship between these optimization objectives and the influence of user responsiveness on each objective is examined in detail.

EVs charging and discharging scheduling optimization method

Improved PICEA-g

When solving a multi-objective optimization problem, several different objective functions are optimized simultaneously to find a set of Pareto solution sets. However, different objective functions are always in conflict and cannot be optimized at the same time. PICEA-g has the characteristics of high solving efficiency and high solution set quality. It combines the advantages of preference algorithms and co-evolutionary algorithms, generates goal vectors randomly, and utilizes the cooperative and competitive relationships between populations and random preferences to guide the evolution. However, different positions of these individuals dominate different goal spaces, leading to differences in search capability, easy convergence to local subspaces, and poor diversity17. Specifically for the EVs charging and discharging scheduling problem, it is a large-scale multi-objective optimization problem with a large objective space and many decision variables, which makes it easy to search incompletely and fall into local optimum. Therefore, improving global search capability is significant in solving large-scale EVs optimization problems. To improve the search capability for solving this large-scale discrete combination optimization problem with decision variables, different computational resources should be allocated to different subspaces. Therefore, considering the differentiated resource allocation, this paper proposes an improved PICEA-g combined with differentiated resource allocation strategy for scheduling optimization of charging and discharging of EVs flexible loads.

Setting the number of EVs in the region to 20, 50, 100, and 200, the number of decision variables is 480, 1200, 2400, and 4800, respectively. The user responsiveness is set to 100%, the penalty factor is set to 10. The population size is set to 100, the number of terminated evolutionary generation is 1000, the crossover probability is 0.6, the mutation probability is set to 0.2, and is set to 100. Three optimization objectives, four optimization objectives and five optimization objectives are considered respectively, and these multi-objective optimization algorithms of improved PICEA-g, PICEA-g16, PSO8, NSGA-II10, multi-objective evolutionary algorithm based on decomposition (MOEA/D)21, and Bi-goal evolution (BiGE)22 are used as comparisons, and each experiment is repeated 50 times. Hypervolume (HV)23 is used to measure optimization results. The larger the HV, the better the convergence and diversity. The simulation results are shown in Table 4. In all cases, the PICEA-g algorithm performs worse compared to the improved PICEA-g algorithm. This verifies the positive effect of the differentiated resource allocation strategy on the optimization of the PICEA-g algorithm. And only the HV value of PSO is not 0 when the number of EVs is 20, and its performance is the worst. The reason may be that the PSO algorithm tends to follow the best particles or population failure into local optimization The performance of MOEA/D and BiGE also have poor performance. After the number of EVs increases to 100, their HV values are almost zero. The reasons may be the susceptibility of MOEA/D to numerical imbalance of objective function values, the difficulty of the BiGE algorithm in dealing with constrained optimization problems as well as its slow convergence in high dimensional spaces. Compared with the above three algorithms, the performance of the NSGA-II algorithm is relatively better. Moreover, NSGA-II outperforms the PICEA-g algorithm when the number of EVs is 20 and the optimization objective is 3 and when the number of EVs is 50 and the optimization objective is 4. However, when the number of EVs increases to 100 and 200, i.e., as the number of decision variables increases, NSGA-II has almost no feasible solutions in 50 repetitions of the experiment. At the same time, the improved PICEA-g algorithm can perform better in most cases because of the cooperative evolution and differentiated resource allocation strategy. In terms of computational complexity and running time, the BiGE algorithm has the highest computational complexity and the longest running time due to the hierarchical evolutionary mechanism of the BiGE algorithm and the differentiated resource allocation strategy of the improved PICEA-g algorithm. The conventional PICEA-g and NSGA-II algorithms are roughly the same. PSO and MOEA/D algorithms have lower complexity and faster running speed compared to PICEA-g and NSGA-II, and the PSO algorithm is more suitable for single-objective or low-dimensional problems.

Based on Tables 5 and 6, and Figs. 3, 4 and 5, it can be seen that with the increasing in user responsiveness, flexible travel time and SOC increase correspondingly. When the user response rate is between 30 and 70%, as the user response rate increases, the variance of load fluctuations first decreases and then increases, and the user cost and environmental governance cost continue to decrease continuously. When the user responsiveness reaches 100%, the variance of load fluctuation is greater than that when the response is 30%, 50%, and 70%, and the user cost and environmental governance cost increase compared with 50% and 70%. The reason may be that there is a limitation on the capacity of microgrid distributed energy installations in the region.

Compared with Tables 5 and 6, it can be seen that the load fluctuation, user cost, and environmental governance cost under the same responsiveness, 5 objective optimization is larger, which is due to the fact that the addition of an optimization objective is necessarily optimized at the expense of the other optimization objectives. The more flexible travel times obtained at the 4 optimization objective are due to the fact that the 5 optimization objective also takes into account the SOC of the user’s anytime travel metrics. This means increased charging time and thus less flexible travel time. Although the results of the 5 optimization objective inevitably get worse compared to the 4 optimization objective, the difference is not significant in terms of flexible travel time.

Conclusion

Based on the research in this paper, an improved PICEA-g is proposed for flexible load optimization and regulation of large-scale EV charging access to the distribution grid. The method achieves effective management of EV charging behavior and provides a solution to reduce the operation and management cost of microgrids, environmental pollution control cost, load fluctuation as well as to increase the user’s flexible travel time and SOC. Moreover, in comparison with several existing optimization algorithms, the algorithm obtains optimal performance in some scenarios. Future work could further explore the improvement and practical application of the scheduling model and consider exploring the integration of renewable energy sources, at which point the EVs charging scheduling model needs to become more dynamic to accommodate the time-varying nature of renewable energy sources. Incorporating dynamic optimization constraints regarding EVs charging, driving distance and travel mode, and updating the SOC of each vehicle in real time to ensure that the driving distance is compatible with the charging strategy during the scheduling process.

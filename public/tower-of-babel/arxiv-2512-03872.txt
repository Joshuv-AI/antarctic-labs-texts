Movable Signals with Dual-Polarized Fixed Intelligent Surfaces: Beyond Diagonal Reflection Matrices
Matteo Nerini, Bruno Clerckx

Abstract
This paper investigates wireless systems aided by dual-polarized intelligent surfaces. We compare reconfigurable intelligent surface (RIS) , which adjust their reflection matrices, with movable signals operating with fixed intelligent surface (FIS) , which adjust the signal frequency while the surface properties remain fixed. For both RIS and FIS , we consider surfaces with a diagonal reflection matrix, named diagonal RIS / FIS , and surfaces with a reflection matrix not limited to being diagonal, named beyond-diagonal RIS / FIS . Movable signals with FIS always outperform RIS , achieving at least a fourfold gain. When transmitter and receiver polarizations differ, beyond-diagonal FIS further enhances performance.



I
Introduction

Reconfigurable intelligent surfaces (RISs)
have been thoroughly investigated as a promising means to enable
smart radio environments (SREs)
by shaping and controlling the wireless channel
[
1
,
2
]
.
A
RIS
is a surface composed of reflecting elements with adjustable
electromagnetic (EM)
properties, thereby steering incident signals toward desired directions.
Although RIS can offer interesting performance gains, their practical deployment remains challenging.
Dynamic reconfiguration of the
RIS
requires dedicated control circuitry with non-negligible power consumption.
An effective
RIS
reconfiguration relies on accurate channel knowledge, demanding additional training overhead.
Moreover, since a RIS alters all incident
EM
signals, the resulting unpredictable interference from different operators raises concerns.

To circumvent these limitations, an alternative approach has been introduced: movable signals operating with
fixed intelligent surface (FIS)

[
3
]
.
Instead of reconfiguring the
EM
properties of the surface, this approach reconfigures the carrier frequency of the transmitted signal, while using a surface of uniformly spaced reflecting elements with fixed
EM
properties.
Prior work has demonstrated that, in
single-input single-output (SISO)
systems, movable signals with
FIS
can enhance the channel strength by up to four times compared with
RIS
.
Although this gain relies on a suitable spectrum availability (ideally, the available carrier frequencies should be distributed over a wide range), it remarkably does not require any reconfigurable hardware or additional channel estimation overhead.

Previous work investigated
FIS
with a diagonal reflection matrix, which we denote as
diagonal FIS (D-FIS)
, mirroring the assumption adopted for conventional
RIS
, also known as
diagonal RIS (D-RIS)

[
3
,
4
]
.
Nevertheless, recent developments in
beyond-diagonal RIS (BD-RIS)
highlight the benefits of artificially coupling the
RIS
elements to each other, which leads to more flexible reflection capabilities.
Extending this concept to
FIS
and exploring the performance benefits of
beyond-diagonal FIS (BD-FIS)
, i.e.,
FISs
with fixed reflection matrices not limited to be diagonal, remains unexplored.

In this work, we investigate a practical version of
FIS
where the antenna elements are dual-polarized, and show that
BD-FIS
can be particularly beneficial in this case.
Dual-polarization has been introduced in
multiple-input multiple-output (MIMO)
communications to allow more antennas within a limited space, and increase diversity
[
5
]
, and it is reasonable to expect that it will also be considered for intelligent surfaces.
Focusing on
SISO
systems aided by dual-polarized intelligent surfaces, we analyze and compare the performance of four designs:
D-RIS
,
BD-RIS
,
D-FIS
, and
BD-FIS
, where
FISs
are used jointly with movable signals.

Our contributions are the following.

First
, we characterize the received power achievable with these four designs in the two cases where the transmitter and receiver have the same and opposite polarization.
In this analysis, we consider the effect of the specular reflection due to the structural scattering of the surface, which is often neglected in the
RIS
literature, but plays an important role
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

Second
, we obtain in closed form the performance gain of
BD-RIS
,
D-FIS
, and
BD-FIS
over
D-RIS
.
Movable signals with
FIS
always outperform
RIS
, achieving at least a four times higher received power.
Furthermore, when the transmitter and receiver have opposite polarization,
BD-FIS
yields even larger performance improvements, reaching infinite gains as the inverse of the
cross-polar discrimination (XPD)
approaches zero.

II
Dual-Polarized RIS/FIS-Aided Channel Model and Problem Formulation

Consider a
SISO
communication system, as illustrated in Fig.
1
.
To enhance the channel strength, a reflecting surface made of
N
dual-polarized elements is deployed, which can be either a
RIS
or a
FIS
, depending on whether its reflecting properties are reconfigurable or fixed.
Letting the transmitted signal be
x\in\mathbb{C}
, the received signal writes as
y=hx+n
, where
h\in\mathbb{C}
is the wireless channel and
n\in\mathbb{C}
is the noise.

II-A

Channel Model

Following previous work
[
1
,
3
]
, the channel
h
is given by
h=\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}
, where
\boldsymbol{\Theta}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times N}
is the reflection matrix of the
N
-element surface,
\mathbf{h}_{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N}
is the channel between the surface and receiver, and
\mathbf{h}_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N\times 1}
is the channel between the transmitter and surface.
In
h
, the term
\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}
accounts for the signal reflected by the surface and controlled by
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
, while the term
-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}
accounts for the signal specularly reflected by the surface (an effect of the structural scattering which is independent of
\boldsymbol{\Theta}

[
6
,
7
,
8
]
).
The direct link between the transmitter and receiver is considered to be very weak and hence omitted.

Fig. 1:
Dual-polarized RIS/FIS-aided system where the transmitter and receiver have opposite polarization.

The channels
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
are modeled by assuming that the surface is a dual-polarized
uniform linear array (ULA)
in
line-of-sight (LoS)
with both the transmitter and receiver.
The
N
elements of the surface are numbered such that the first
N/2
have vertical polarization and the last
N/2
have horizontal polarization, as shown in Fig.
1
.
The effect of the element polarization is incorporated in
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
by modeling them as

\mathbf{h}_{R}=\mathbf{p}_{R}\otimes\mathbf{g}_{R},\;\mathbf{h}_{T}=\mathbf{p}_{T}\otimes\mathbf{g}_{T},

(1)

where
\otimes
is the Kronecker product,
\mathbf{p}_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{1\times 2}
and
\mathbf{p}_{T}\in\mathbb{R}^{2\times 1}
capture the power imbalance between the two polarizations, and
\mathbf{g}_{R}\in\mathbb{C}^{1\times N/2}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}\in\mathbb{C}^{N/2\times 1}
are the uni-polarized
LoS
fading channels
[
5
]
.
The vectors
\mathbf{p}_{R}
and
\mathbf{p}_{T}
depend on the polarizations of the transmitter and receiver antennas.
For a vertically or horizontally polarized receiver,
\mathbf{p}_{R}=[1,\sqrt{\chi}]
or
\mathbf{p}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi},1]
, respectively, where
0\leq\chi\leq 1
is the inverse of the
XPD
.
Likewise, for a vertically or horizontally polarized transmitter,
\mathbf{p}_{T}=[1,\sqrt{\chi}]^{T}
or
\mathbf{p}_{T}=[\sqrt{\chi},1]^{T}
, respectively.

The
LoS
fading channels
\mathbf{g}_{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}
are expressed as follows.
We place the
N
elements of the surface on the
x
-axis, centered in
x=0
, so that the
n
th element has
x
coordinate
x_{n}=(n-(N+1)/2)d_{A}
, with
d_{A}
being the spacing between the antenna elements.
Denoting as
d_{R}
the distance between the center of the surface and the receiver, and as
\theta_{R}\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
the angle of the receiver with respect to the surface normal, the distance between the
n
th element and the receiver is
d_{R,n}=d_{R}-x_{n}\sin(\theta_{R})
assuming far-field conditions.
Hence, the entries of
\mathbf{g}_{R}
, given by
[\mathbf{g}_{R}]_{n}=e^{-j2\pi d_{R,n}/\lambda}
, can be written as

\left[\mathbf{g}_{R}\right]_{n}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left[d_{R}-\left(n-\frac{N+1}{2}\right)d_{A}\sin(\theta_{R})\right]},

(2)

as a function of the wavelength
\lambda=c/f
, where
c
the speed of light, and
f
the frequency.
Likewise, denoting as
d_{T}
distance between the center of the surface and the transmitter, and as
\theta_{T}\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
the angle of the transmitter, the entries of
\mathbf{g}_{T}
write as

\left[\mathbf{g}_{T}\right]_{n}=e^{-j\frac{2\pi}{\lambda}\left[d_{T}-\left(n-\frac{N+1}{2}\right)d_{A}\sin(\theta_{T})\right]}.

(3)

II-B

Problem Formulation

In this communication system, our metric is the received power, written as

P_{R}=P_{T}\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|^{2},

(4)

where
P_{T}=\mathbb{E}[|x|^{2}]
denotes the transmitted power, assumed to be
P_{T}=1
for simplicity.
We want to compare two strategies for maximizing
P_{R}
.

II-B
1
RIS

With a
RIS
, the received power is maximized by reconfiguring
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
depending on the channels
\mathbf{h}_{R}
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}
, which are assumed to be known
[
1
]
.
Formally, the optimization problem is

\underset{\boldsymbol{\Theta}}{\mathsf{\mathrm{max}}}\;\;P_{R}\;\;\mathsf{\mathrm{s.t.}}\;\;\mathbf{h}_{R}\text{ and }\mathbf{h}_{T}\text{ are fixed},

(5)

where
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is reconfigurable subject to specific constraints.
For a
D-RIS
, we have
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\text{diag}\left(e^{j\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{N}}\right)
, where
\theta_{n}\in[0,2\pi)
are the phase shifts that can be reconfigured.
For a
BD-RIS
,
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
can be any unitary matrix, i.e.,
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{I}
, if only the lossless constraint is considered.

II-B
2
Movable Signals with FIS

With movable signals, the received power is maximized by reconfiguring the frequency
f
(or the wavelength
\lambda
), while the reflection matrix of the surface
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is fixed to a suitable predefined value.
Such a surface with a fixed reflection matrix is referred to as
FIS

[
3
]
.
The optimization problem for movable signals is

\underset{\lambda}{\mathsf{\mathrm{max}}}\;\;P_{R}\;\;\mathsf{\mathrm{s.t.}}\;\;\boldsymbol{\Theta}\text{ is fixed},\;\eqref{eq:hRhT},\;\eqref{eq:gR},\;\eqref{eq:gT},

(6)

where
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is pre-optimized offline and fixed during deployment.
For a
D-FIS
,
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
is set subject to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\text{diag}\left(e^{j\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{N}}\right)
, while for a
BD-FIS
, it is subject to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{I}
.

In the two sections that follow, we compare the received power achievable through
RIS
and movable signals with
FIS
, by separately considering the two cases where the transmitter and receiver have the same and opposite polarization.

III
Same Polarization at the Tx and Rx

Assume the transmitter and receiver to have the same polarization, namely vertical, with no loss of generality.
Following (
1
), this yields
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\mathbf{g}_{R},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
.

III-
1
Diagonal RIS

With a
D-RIS
, it is well known that the received power is maximized by setting
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
as
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\text{diag}(e^{j\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{N}})
, with phase shifts
\theta_{n}=-\arg([\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n})+\arg(-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T})
, for
n=1,\dots,N
.
This global optimal solution gives

P_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\left[\mathbf{h}_{R}\right]_{n}\left[\mathbf{h}_{T}\right]_{n}\right|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}.

(7)

Since
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\mathbf{g}_{R},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
, we have
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2
,
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\chi
, for
n=N/2+1,\ldots,N
, and
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=(1+\chi)\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, giving

\displaystyle P_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}\left(N+2\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}.

(8)

Taking the expectation of (
8
) over all possible channels
\mathbf{g}_{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}
, the average received power
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}]
is

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}\left(N^{2}+4N\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right]+4\mathbb{E}[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2}]\right),

(9)

where
\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
can be approximated as a complex Gaussian with mean
0
and variance
N/2
, i.e.,
\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N/2)
, by the Central Limit Theorem.
Hence, exploiting
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|]=\sqrt{\pi N/8}
and
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|^{2}]=N/2
, we obtain

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}\left(N^{2}+\sqrt{2\pi N}N+2N\right),

(10)

showing that the received power with a
D-RIS
when transmitter and receiver have the same polarization scales with
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}\approx(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}/4
.

III-
2
Beyond-Diagonal RIS

With a
BD-RIS
, the achievable received power is given by

P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\left(\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\right\|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2},

(11)

which can be achieved by reconfiguring
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
with the global optimal solution derived in
[
9
]
.
Since
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\mathbf{g}_{R},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
, we have
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=(1+\chi)N/2
and
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=(1+\chi)\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, giving that
P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}
is equal to the received power with
D-RIS
in (
8
).
Therefore, the average received power achievable with a
BD-RIS

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}]
is the same as the average received power with a
D-RIS
given by (
10
), scaling with
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}\approx(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}/4
.
The fact that
D-RIS
and
BD-RIS
achieve the same performance in dual-polarized systems when the transmitter and receiver have the same polarization was also derived in
[
10
]
, although the specular reflection was neglected in
[
10
]
.

III-
3
Diagonal FIS

With movable signals and
D-FIS
, the reflection matrix of the
FIS
is pre-optimized offline subject to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\text{diag}\left(e^{j\theta_{1}},\ldots,e^{j\theta_{N}}\right)
, while the signal frequency
f
is dynamically reconfigured.
We show how to fix
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
and optimize
f
by first deriving an upper bound on the received power, and then giving solutions for
\boldsymbol{\Theta}
and
f
which achieve it.
The received power with a
D-FIS
is upper bounded by

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\left[\mathbf{h}_{R}\right]_{n}\left[\mathbf{h}_{T}\right]_{n}\right|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}

(12)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}+\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}\leq\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}N^{2},

(13)

where (
12
) is given by the triangle inequality, and (
13
) follows from
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2
,
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\chi
, for
n=N/2+1,\ldots,N
,
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=(1+\chi)\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|\leq N/2
.
To achieve the upper bound
P_{R}=(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}
, the
D-FIS
matrix is fixed to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{\star}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
, such that the term
\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}
is aligned with the specular reflection
-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}
.
With
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
, the received power is

P_{R}=\left|-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|^{2}=4\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2},

(14)

since
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=(1+\chi)\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and therefore the signal frequency needs to be reconfigured to maximize
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|
, i.e., to ensure that
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|=N/2
.
Interestingly, this is achieved if the signal wavelength is reconfigured to
\lambda^{\star}=d_{A}|\sin(\theta_{R})+\sin(\theta_{T})|
, depending on the angles
\theta_{R}
and
\theta_{T}
, with corresponding frequency

f^{\star}=\frac{c}{d_{A}\left|\sin(\theta_{R})+\sin(\theta_{T})\right|}.

(15)

The optimality of this value can be verified by substituting
\lambda^{\star}
into the expressions of
\mathbf{g}_{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}
in (
2
) and (
3
) and verifying that it leads to
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|=N/2
.
Therefore, by reconfiguring the signal frequency as in (
15
) a
D-FIS
with reflection matrix
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
can achieve a received power
P_{R}^{\mathrm{DFIS}}=(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}
.
Note that this value is four times higher than the received power achieved by a
D-RIS

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}\approx(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}/4
, and can be obtained without reconfiguring the
EM
properties of the surface.
Movable signals can achieve higher performance than
RIS
since they control both terms
\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}
and
-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}
in the channel expression, while
RIS
only control
\mathbf{h}_{R}\boldsymbol{\Theta}\mathbf{h}_{T}
.

III-
4
Beyond-Diagonal FIS

With movable signals and
BD-FIS
, the reflection matrix of the
FIS
is pre-optimized offline subject to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{H}\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\mathbf{I}
, while the signal frequency
f
is dynamically reconfigured to maximize the received power.
In this case, the received power is upper bounded by

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\right\|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}

(16)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}\left(\frac{N}{2}+\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}\leq\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}N^{2},

(17)

where (
16
) is due to the triangle inequality and the sub-multiplicity of the
\ell_{2}
-norm, and (
17
) holds because
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=(1+\chi)N/2
,
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=(1+\chi)\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|\leq N/2
.
Therefore, the maximum received power achievable by a
BD-FIS
is the same as the received power of a
D-FIS
given by (
13
).
This implies that
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=-\mathbf{I}
is global optimal also for a
BD-FIS
, and the signal frequency can still be reconfigured as in (
15
) to achieve a received power of
P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDFIS}}\approx(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}
.

We have studied a dual-polarized
RIS
/
FIS
-aided system where the transmitter and receiver have the same polarization, considering
D-RIS
,
BD-RIS
, and movable signals with
D-FIS
and
BD-FIS
.
The scaling laws of the received power in these four cases are summarized in Tab.
I
, from which we make two observations.

First
,
BD-RIS
and
BD-FIS
do not improve the received power over
D-RIS
and
D-FIS
, respectively.

Second
, movable signals with
FIS
can obtain four times higher received power than fixed-frequency signals with
RIS
.

IV
Opposite Polarization at the Tx and Rx

Assume now the transmitter and receiver to have opposite polarization, namely vertical and horizontal, respectively, with no loss of generality.
Following (
1
), this yields
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R},\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
.

IV-
1
Diagonal RIS

With a
D-RIS
, the maximum received power is given by (
7
).
Since now the channels are
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R},\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
, we have
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\sqrt{\chi}
, for
n=1,\ldots,N
, and
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=2\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, allowing us to simplify (
7
) as

\displaystyle P_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}

\displaystyle=\chi\left(N+2\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}.

(18)

Taking the expectation of (
18
) over all possible channels
\mathbf{g}_{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}
, the average received power
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}]
is

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\chi\left(N^{2}+4N\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right]+4\mathbb{E}[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2}]\right),

(19)

where we can make the approximation
\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N/2)
, following the Central Limit Theorem.
Therefore, by using
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|]=\sqrt{\pi N/8}
and
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|^{2}]=N/2
, we get

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}=\chi\left(N^{2}+\sqrt{2\pi N}N+2N\right),

(20)

giving the scaling law of the received power in the presence of a
D-RIS
as
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}\approx\chi N^{2}
.

IV-
2
Beyond-Diagonal RIS

In the presence of a
BD-RIS
, the maximum received power is given by (
11
).
Since
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R},\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
give
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=(1+\chi)N/2
and
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=2\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, (
11
) becomes

P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\left(\left(1+\chi\right)\frac{N}{2}+2\sqrt{\chi}\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}.

(21)

Taking the expectation of (
21
) over all possible channels
\mathbf{g}_{R}
and
\mathbf{g}_{T}
, the average received power
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\mathbb{E}[P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}]
is

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2}+2\left(1+\chi\right)\sqrt{\chi}N\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right]\\
+4\chi\mathbb{E}[\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2}].

(22)

Furthermore, by approximating
\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
as
\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\sim\mathcal{CN}(0,N/2)
following the Central Limit Theorem, we can apply
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|]=\sqrt{\pi N/8}
and
\mathbb{E}[|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|^{2}]=N/2
to obtain

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=\frac{\left(1+\chi\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2}+\left(1+\chi\right)\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}\chi N}N+2\chi N,

(23)

showing that the received power with a
BD-RIS
scales with
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}\approx(1+\chi)^{2}N^{2}/4
, always higher than
\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}\approx\chi N^{2}
when
\chi\neq 1
.

IV-
3
Diagonal FIS

When considering movable signals and
D-FIS
, the received power is upper bounded by

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left|\left[\mathbf{h}_{R}\right]_{n}\left[\mathbf{h}_{T}\right]_{n}\right|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}

(24)

\displaystyle=\left(\sqrt{\chi}N+2\sqrt{\chi}|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|\right)^{2}\leq 4\chi N^{2},

(25)

where (
24
) is given by the triangle inequality, and (
25
) follows from
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=1
, for
n=1,\ldots,N/2
,
|[\mathbf{h}_{R}]_{n}[\mathbf{h}_{T}]_{n}|=\chi
, for
n=N/2+1,\ldots,N
,
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=2\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|\leq N/2
.
As discussed in Section
III-3
, the upper bound
P_{R}=4\chi N^{2}
can be achieved by fixing the
D-FIS
matrix to
\boldsymbol{\Theta}^{\star}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
.
With
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
, the received power is

P_{R}=\left|-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|^{2}=16\chi\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2},

(26)

since
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=2\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and therefore the signal frequency needs to be optimized to maximize
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|
.
As discussed in Section
III-3
,
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|
is maximized when the signal frequency is set as in (
15
), depending on the angles
\theta_{R}
and
\theta_{T}
.
Therefore, by using a signal frequency as in (
15
) a
D-FIS
with reflection matrix
\boldsymbol{\Theta}=-\mathbf{I}_{N}
can achieve a received power
P_{R}^{\mathrm{DFIS}}=4\chi N^{2}
.
Note that this value is four times higher than the received power achieved by a
D-RIS

\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}\approx\chi N^{2}
, while requiring no reconfiguration of
EM
properties of the surface.

IV-
4
Beyond-Diagonal FIS

With movable signals and
BD-FIS
, the received power is upper bounded by

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\left\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\right\|\left\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\right\|+\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}

(27)

\displaystyle=\left(\left(1+\chi\right)\frac{N}{2}+2\sqrt{\chi}\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|\right)^{2}\leq\frac{\left(1+\chi+2\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2}}{4}N^{2},

(28)

where (
27
) is due to the triangle inequality and the sub-multiplicity of the
\ell_{2}
-norm, and (
28
) holds since
\|\mathbf{h}_{R}\|^{2}=\|\mathbf{h}_{T}\|^{2}=(1+\chi)N/2
,
\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}=2\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}
, and
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|\leq N/2
.
We now show how to fix the
BD-FIS
matrix and optimize the signal frequency to achieve this upper bound.
The
BD-FIS
matrix is fixed to

\boldsymbol{\Theta}=\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{N/2}&-\mathbf{I}_{N/2}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{N/2}&\mathbf{0}_{N/2}\end{bmatrix},

(29)

which means we are considering a group-connected architecture with
N/2
groups, each including 2 elements
[
11
]
.
The groups are formed by two co-located elements having opposite polarization and all have a
2\times 2
scattering matrix given by
[[0,-1]^{T},[-1,0]^{T}]
.
Since an example of
2
-port network having scattering matrix
[[0,-1]^{T},[-1,0]^{T}]
is a phase shifter with phase
\pi
(see Fig.
2
(a)), such a
BD-FIS
can be implemented by connecting each pair of co-located elements via a phase shifter with phase
\pi
(see Fig.
2
(b)).
In this way, the signal incident on the vertically polarized elements is reflected from the horizontally polarized ones, and vice versa, and the
BD-FIS
behaves as a polarization converter.
Note that (
29
) is a block diagonal matrix with
2\times 2
blocks being
[[0,-1]^{T},[-1,0]^{T}]
if its columns are permuted.
With (
29
), the received power is

\displaystyle P_{R}

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbf{h}_{R}\begin{bmatrix}\mathbf{0}_{N/2}&-\mathbf{I}_{N/2}\\
-\mathbf{I}_{N/2}&\mathbf{0}_{N/2}\end{bmatrix}\mathbf{h}_{T}-\mathbf{h}_{R}\mathbf{h}_{T}\right|^{2}

(30)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\chi+2\sqrt{\chi}\right)^{2}\left|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}\right|^{2},

(31)

where we used
\mathbf{h}_{R}=[\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{R},\mathbf{g}_{R}]
and
\mathbf{h}_{T}=[\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T},\sqrt{\chi}\mathbf{g}_{T}^{T}]^{T}
.
The signal frequency needs now to be reconfigured to maximize
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|
, i.e., to ensure that
|\mathbf{g}_{R}\mathbf{g}_{T}|=N/2
, which can be achieved by setting it as in (
15
).
Therefore, by reconfiguring the signal frequency as in (
15
) a
BD-FIS
with reflection matrix given by (
29
) achieves a received power
P_{R}^{\mathrm{BDFIS}}=(1+\chi+2\sqrt{\chi})^{2}N^{2}/4
.
Remarkably, this value is higher than the received power achieved by a
D-FIS

P_{R}^{\mathrm{DFIS}}=4\chi N^{2}
when
\chi\neq 1
, and always higher than the received power of
BD-RIS
.

V
Gain over Diagonal RIS

In this section, we quantify the gain of
BD-RIS
,
D-FIS
, and
BD-FIS
over
D-RIS
, defined as
G^{\mathrm{X}}=\lim_{N\to+\infty}\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{X}}/\bar{P}_{R}^{\mathrm{DRIS}}
, where
\mathrm{X}\in\{\mathrm{BDRIS},\;\mathrm{DFIS},\;\mathrm{BDFIS}\}
.
When the transmitter and receiver have the same polarization, these gains are given by
G^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=1
,
G^{\mathrm{DFIS}}=4
, and
G^{\mathrm{BDFIS}}=4
, which be shown by using the scaling laws in (
10
), (
13
), and (
17
).
Besides, when the transmitter and receiver have opposite polarization, these gains are
G^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}=(1+\chi)^{2}/(4\chi)
,
G^{\mathrm{DFIS}}=4
, and
G^{\mathrm{BDFIS}}=(1+\chi+2\sqrt{\chi})^{2}/(4\chi)
, which are obtained by using the scaling laws in (
20
), (
23
), (
25
), and (
28
).

Focusing on systems where the transmitter and receiver have opposite polarization, we report the gains
G^{\mathrm{BDRIS}}
,
G^{\mathrm{DFIS}}
, and
G^{\mathrm{BDFIS}}
as a function of
\chi
in Fig.
3
.
We make the following three observations.

First
, the gain of
BD-RIS
over
D-RIS
tends to infinity when
\chi=0
, decreases with
\chi
, and is
1
when
\chi=1
.

BD-RIS
is therefore particularly beneficial for small values of
\chi
, as also noticed in
[
10
]
.

Second
, the gain of
D-FIS
over
D-RIS
is always
4
and does not vary with
\chi
, as for uni-polarized systems
[
3
]
.

Third
, the gain of
BD-FIS
over
D-RIS
tends to infinity when
\chi=0
, decreases with
\chi
, and reaches
4
when
\chi=1
.
Therefore,
BD-FIS
with movable signals is an appealing strategy for achieving substantially higher performance than
RIS
in dual-polarized systems.
Since its reconfigurability lies in the signal frequency,
BD-FIS
has a fixed reflection matrix, which is easy to implement and does not require control hardware.

VI
Conclusion

We have investigated the performance limits of
SISO
systems aided by dual-polarized intelligent surfaces.
Specifically, we have compared two strategies:
RIS
, where the reflection properties of the surface are reconfigured, and movable signals with
FIS
, where the signal frequency is reconfigured while the surface has fixed reflection properties.
We show that movable signals with
FIS
always outperform
RIS
, by achieving at least a four times higher received power.
When the transmitter and receiver have opposite polarization, a
FIS
with a reflection matrix not limited to be diagonal, namely a
BD-FIS
, remarkably further increases the performance.
Future research could explore
BD-FIS
architectures that are optimal for any polarization of the transmitting and receiving antennas, and the optimization of the frequency of movable signals under realistic spectrum constraints and channel models.

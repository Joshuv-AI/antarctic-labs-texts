Kuznetsov Categories for Gauged Linear Sigma Models
David Favero, Daniel Kaplan, Tyler L. Kelly

Abstract
. We define Kuznetsov and anti-Kuznetsov categories for gauged linear sigma models. We show that for complete intersections of ample divisors in smooth projective toric varieties, the Kuznetsov category is left orthogonal to an exceptional collection. We prove that any complete intersection of r ≥ 2 r\geq 2 ample divisors in a Fano GIT quotient is a Fano visitor and the derived category of its Fano host is equivalent to an anti-Kuznetsov category of a gauged linear sigma model.



1.
Introduction

The derived category of coherent sheaves
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
on a variety
X
is an ‘almost-perfect’ invariant of
X
, encapsulating much of its geometry and descending to many other invariants, such as cohomology and
K
-theory. When
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
is decomposable, certain admissible subcategories can hold interesting information about
X
. First for cubic fourfolds followed by other Fano varieties, Kuznetsov pioneered identifying such admissible subcategories as (left) orthogonals to exceptional objects
[
27
,
28
,
30
,
31
]
.

These components, now called Kuznetsov (or residual) components of
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}X)
, have since enjoyed considerable attention in the literature. For example, they have been used to probe rationality
[
30
,
1
]
, study mirror symmetry
[
38
]
, explore Bridgeland stability
[
7
]
, and establish categorical Torelli theorems
[
9
,
20
,
34
]
. Despite their range of applications in many examples, there is at present no uniform definition of such categories.

In this paper, we propose a uniform definition of Kuznetsov categories for gauged linear sigma models (GLSMs). Witten introduced gauged linear sigma models in physics as a generalized setting for the correspondence between Landau-Ginzburg models and Calabi-Yau manifolds
[
41
]
. A GLSM contains the data needed to construct a (well-behaved) superpotential
w:X\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
on a stack
X
presented as a GIT quotient
X=[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G]
coming from a representation
V
of an algebraic group
G
. Given a GLSM, there is a corresponding absolute derived category defined by Positselski
[
35
]
generalizing the category of matrix factorizations.

Choices of GIT quotients of
V
by
G
are parameterized by the characters of
G
. The character
\theta
, determines the Zariski-open, (semi)stability locus
V_{\theta}
, and hence the GIT quotient
[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G]
. Varying the character, one obtains relations between the various absolute derived categories (see, e.g.,
[
17
,
37
,
18
,
16
,
5
,
15
,
40
]
).

We identify a distinguished character
\theta_{K}\in\widehat{G}
and define a
Kuznetsov chamber
to be a chamber in the GIT fan which contains this distinguished character
\theta_{K}
(Definition
2.6
). We define a
Kuznetsov category

\mathcal{K}
of the GLSM to be the absolute derived category associated to this new GLSM corresponding to the superpotential
w_{K+\epsilon}:X_{K+\epsilon}\to\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
where
X_{K+\epsilon}=[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta_{K+\epsilon}}G]
and
\theta_{K+\epsilon}
is in the interior of said Kuznetsov chamber (Definition
2.7
). This perspective also has the advantage of an obvious symmetry; one can define an
anti-Kuznetsov category

-\mathcal{K}
as well using an anti-Kuznetsov chamber containing the character
\theta_{-K}:=\theta_{K}^{-1}
.

The Kuznetsov (resp. anti-Kuznetsov) category apriori depends on the choice of chamber. Nevertheless, it follows from the work of Ballard-Favero-Katzarkov
[
3
]
that all choices of Kuznetsov (resp. anti-Kuznetsov) chambers yield equivalent Kuznetsov (resp. anti-Kuznetsov) categories (see Corollary
2.10
).

We then study Kuznetsov categories for
geometric
GLSMs (see Definition
2.12
). In this case,
X
is a (stacky) vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over some variety
B
and the superpotential
w
corresponds to a regular global section
s\in\Gamma(B,\mathcal{E}^{\vee})
. The absolute derived category of the geometric GLSM is equivalent to the derived category
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z)
of a complete intersection
Z:=Z(s)
in
B
. We use this approach to provide new semi-orthogonal decompositions of complete intersections in terms of their Kuznetsov categories. In particular, for geometric abelian GLSMs where
\mathcal{E}
is a split vector bundle consisting of anti-ample line bundles, we prove that the orthogonal to the Kuznetsov category is an exceptional collection. This has the following geometric reinterpretation:

Theorem 1.1

(=Theorem
3.5
)
.

Let
Z
be a complete intersection of ample divisors in a smooth projective toric variety (or Deligne-Mumford stack). Then, there are semiorthogonal decompositions

\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z)=\langle\mathcal{K},E_{1},\dots,E_{t}\rangle

(1.1)

and

-\mathcal{K}=\langle\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z),E_{1},\dots,E_{s}\rangle

(1.2)

where the
E_{i}
are exceptional objects and
\mathcal{K}
(resp.
-\mathcal{K}
) is the Kuznetsov (resp. anti-Kuznetsov) category for the geometric GLSM associated to
Z
.

This result can be viewed as a toric analogue of Orlov’s theorem
[
33
, Theorem 3.11]
. As in Orlov’s theorem, more is known when we specialize to the hypersurface case:

Theorem 1.2

(=Theorem
3.9
)
.

Let
Z
be a smooth hypersurface in a smooth projective Fano toric variety
X
(or Deligne-Mumford stack). Assume
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(Z)\cong\omega_{X}^{-q}
for some
q\in\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}_{>0}
.

(i)

If
q\leq 1
then the Kuznetsov category
\mathcal{K}
in (
1.1
) is fractional Calabi-Yau.

(ii)

If
q\geq 1
then the anti-Kuznetsov category
-\mathcal{K}
in (
1.2
) is fractional Calabi-Yau.

(iii)

If
q=\frac{1}{r}
for some
r\in\operatorname{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(Z)
is Cartier, then
\mathcal{K}
in (
1.1
) is Calabi-Yau.

In 2011, Bondal asked
[
8
]
: Which smooth projective varieties
X
have derived categories that are admissible subcategories of the derived category of some smooth projective Fano variety
Y
? If such an
X
and
Y
exist, we call
X
a
Fano visitor
and
Y
a
Fano host
. Anti-Kuznetsov categories provide good candidates for Fano hosts.

Theorem 1.3

(=Corollary
4.5
)
.

Any complete intersection of
r\geq 2
ample divisors in a Fano GIT quotient
X
is a Fano visitor and the derived category of the Fano host is equivalent to an anti-Kuznetsov category of a GLSM.

This generalizes the main result of
[
24
]
which shows that any complete intersection in projective space is a Fano visitor.

2.
Kuznetsov Categories for GLSMs

In this section, we define the Kuznetsov category of a GLSM. Roughly, we take a chamber of the corresponding GIT problem corresponding to the canonical class and take its corresponding absolute derived category (see
[
35
]
and
[
4
, Definition 3.9]
). We expand on these notions below.

The precise mathematical definition of a GLSM consists of a 5-tuple satisfying several conditions. Our definition of a Kuznetsov category can be given for the 5-tuple, without the need for any conditions.

Definition 2.1
.

A
gauged linear sigma model (GLSM)
is the data
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
consisting of:

•

a complex vector space
V
;

•

a reductive algebraic group
\Gamma\subset\text{GL}(V)
acting linearly on
V
;

•

a surjective character
\chi:\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}^{*}
;

•

a character
\theta:\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}\chi\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*}
such that the semistable locus with respect to
\theta
is
\Gamma
-invariant; and

•

a
\chi
-
semi-invariant superpotential

w:V\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{1}
, that is, a regular map
w
satisfying
w(g\cdot x)=\chi(g)w(x)
, for all
x\in V
and
g\in\Gamma
.

Remark 2.2
.

The semi-invariance of
w
means that it defines a section of the equivariant line bundle
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(\chi)
on the quotient stack
[X/G]
, i.e.,
w\in\Gamma(X,\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}(\chi))^{G}
.

Definition 2.3
.

We say a GLSM is
abelian
if
\Gamma
is an abelian group.

Remark 2.4
.

If a GLSM is abelian, then since
\Gamma
is reductive, one can choose a basis for
V
so that
\Gamma\subseteq(\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}})^{n}
. Consequently, abelian GLSMs are amenable to toric geometry.

We recall the setup of geometric invariant theory (GIT). Let
G
be a group acting on a vector space
V
. A GIT quotient
V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G
depends on a choice of character
\theta:G\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
; it is the closed
G
-orbits acting on the
\theta
-semistable locus,
V_{\theta}
. This also gives us a stacky GIT quotient
[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G]:=[V_{\theta}/G]
. The variety
V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G
is isomorphic to the stack
[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G]
when
G
acts freely on
V_{\theta}
.

Define
\widehat{G}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}}:=\text{Hom}(G,\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}})\otimes_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}
. The different semistable loci partition
\widehat{G}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}}
into subsets

\{\tau^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid V_{\tau}=V_{\tau^{\prime}}\}.

If a point
p\in V
is both
\tau_{1}
-semistable and
\tau_{2}
-semistable then it is
(n_{1}\tau_{1}+n_{2}\tau_{2})
-semistable for all
n_{1},n_{2}>0
. Therefore, we define

\sigma_{\tau}:=\overline{\{\tau^{\prime}\in\widehat{G}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}}\mid V_{\tau}=V_{\tau^{\prime}}\}},

which is a (rational) cone. The set of all such cones form a fan called the
GIT fan
(see
[
36
]
). In the abelian case, this fan is also called the GKZ fan or secondary fan. The maximal cones of this fan are called
chambers
and the codimension one cones
walls
.

Remark 2.5
.

We define the semistable locus
V_{\theta}
using a character of the
subgroup

\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}\chi\subset\Gamma
, while requiring that the factorization category is equivariant with respect to the
entire group

\Gamma
. This holds, for instance, if the GLSM is abelian, geometric (see Definition
2.12
), or has a good lift in the sense of
[
11
, Section 1.1]
.

We use GIT in the GLSM context setting
G=\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)
. The inclusion

\iota:\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)\hookrightarrow\Gamma\hookrightarrow\text{GL}(V)

equips
\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)
with the canonical character

\theta_{K}:\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)\hookrightarrow\text{GL}(V)\rightarrow\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*}\hskip 28.45274ptg\mapsto\det(\iota(g))^{-1}.

(2.1)

Let
\theta_{-K}:=\theta_{K}^{-1}
, denote the anti-canonical character.

Definition 2.6
.

A
Kuznetsov chamber
is a chamber
\sigma_{K}
which contains
\theta_{K}
. An
anti-Kuznetsov chamber
is a chamber
\sigma_{-K}
which contains
\theta_{-K}
.

Definition 2.7
.

Given a GLSM
\mathcal{G}:=(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
we say a factorization category

\mathcal{K}:=\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{K+\epsilon}},\Gamma,w)

is a
Kuznetsov category
for
\mathcal{G}
if
\theta_{K+\epsilon}\in\widehat{\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{Q}}}
lies in the interior of a chamber containing
\theta_{K}
. Analogously, we say a factorization category

-\mathcal{K}:=\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{-K-\epsilon}},\Gamma,w)

is an
anti-Kuznetsov category
for
\mathcal{G}
if
\theta_{-K-\epsilon}
lies in the interior of a chamber containing
\theta_{-K}
.

Remark 2.8
.

By definition, a Kuznetsov category is the absolute derived category of the GLSM
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta_{K+\epsilon},w)
. This transparently does not depends on
\theta
from the original GLSM. On the other hand, it apriori depends on the choice of
\theta_{K+\epsilon}
or, equivalently, the choice of Kuznetsov chamber.

If
\sigma_{K}
is in a chamber of the GIT fan, then the Kuznetsov chamber is unique. However, if
\theta_{K}
is on a wall (or a higher codimension cone), then there can be several Kuznetsov chambers.
The following theorem
[
3
, Theorem 5.2.1]
allows us to prove that their corresponding Kuznetsov categories are equivalent in the abelian case.

Theorem 2.9
.

Let
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta_{+},w)
and
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta_{-},w)
be abelian GLSMs so that
\theta_{+},\theta_{-}
lie in adjacent chambers sharing a common wall defined by a primitive one parameter subgroup
\lambda
of
\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}\chi
normalized so that
\theta_{+}\circ\lambda(t)=t^{l}
and
l>0
.

(i)

If
\theta_{K}\circ\lambda(t)=1
, there is an equivalence

\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{-}},\Gamma,w)\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{+}},\Gamma,w).

(ii)

If
\theta_{K}\circ\lambda(t)=t^{r}
and
r>0
, there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition

\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{-}},\Gamma,w)=\langle\mathcal{W}_{1},...,\mathcal{W}_{r},\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta_{+}},\Gamma,w)\rangle,

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}((V^{\lambda})_{\theta_{W}}),\Gamma/\lambda,w|_{V^{\lambda}})
for all
i
where
V^{\lambda}
is the fixed locus of
\lambda
,
\theta_{W}
is any character in the relative interior of the wall descended to
\Gamma/\lambda
.

Corollary 2.10
.

If
\mathcal{G}
is abelian GLSM, then the Kuznetsov category
\mathcal{K}
is independent of the choice of Kuznetsov chamber
\sigma_{K}
. Similarly, the anti-Kuznetsov category
-\mathcal{K}
is independent of the choice of anti-Kuznetsov chamber
\sigma_{-K}
.

Proof.

The proof is the same for the Kuznetsov and anti-Kuznetsov category. We provide the proof for the Kuznetsov category.

Take two chambers
\sigma_{K},\sigma_{K}^{\prime}
containing
\theta_{K}
in
\widehat{\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}(\chi)}_{\operatorname{\mathbb{R}}}
and consider a sufficiently small ball
B
around
\theta_{K}
. This ball contains a point in all possible Kuznetsov chambers in particular points
\theta_{K+\epsilon}\in\sigma_{K},\theta_{K+\epsilon^{\prime}}\in\sigma_{K}^{\prime}
. Now choose a path
\gamma
in
B
from
\theta_{K+\epsilon}
to
\theta_{K+\epsilon^{\prime}}
which does not pass through any codimension
m
cones for
m\geq 2
. Since
B
is sufficiently small, every wall that
\gamma
passes through will contain
\theta_{K}
. Every time we cross a wall, we use Theorem
2.9
(i), which says that the corresponding absolute derived categories are equivalent, as desired.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Corollary
2.10
holds for some non-abelian
G
under additional hypotheses. For example for a Calabi-Yau GLSM,
\theta_{K}
is the origin and
\sigma_{K}
is any chamber. Then, we expect all chambers to be equivalent. This has been established in the quasi-symmetric case
[
15
, Corollary 5.2]
and for certain examples (e.g.,
[
23
]
).

We are particularly interested in GLSMs coming from classical algebraic geometry. These are defined as follows.

Definition 2.12
.

A GLSM
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
is called
geometric
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}\chi
is the total space of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
over a space
X
;

(ii)

w
is a pairing with a regular global section
s
of
\mathcal{E}^{\vee}
; and

(iii)

\chi
splits so that
\Gamma\cong\operatorname{\mbox{ker}}\chi\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
and the
\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
factor acts by fiberwise dilation on
\mathcal{E}
.

The term geometric comes from high-energy theoretical physics. It refers to the fact that a geometric GLSM
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
admits the same conformal field theory as the complete intersection
Z(s)\subseteq X
. This is realized mathematically by the following result
[
21
,
39
,
19
]
.

Theorem 2.13

(Proposition 4.8 of
[
19
]
)
.

If
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
is geometric, then there is an equivalence of categories

\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z(s))\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta},\Gamma,w).

By comparing certain algebraic varieties and GLSMs, we obtain Kuznetsov components of derived categories of certain algebraic varieties as Kuznetsov categories of GLSMs.
We give a simple motivating example.

Example 2.14
.

Consider the GLSM
(V,\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)
where
V=\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{n+1}
and
\Gamma=\mathbb{G}_{m}\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts with weights
1,\dots,1,-d
for the first factor and weights
0,\dots,0,1
for the second factor. Let
\chi=\pi_{2}
and
\theta=\pi_{1}
. Let
w=x_{n+1}s(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
, where
s
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
, so
w
is homogeneous of degree zero.

This GLSM is geometric, corresponding to a hypersurface
Z(s)
of degree
d
in
\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
. By Theorem
2.13
, we have the equivalence
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z(s))\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta},\Gamma,w)
.

We compute that
\theta_{K}=\pi_{1}^{-n+d-1}
. Depending on
n
and
d
, the character
\theta_{K}
can lie in any of the three cones in the GIT fan, with the positive chamber corresponding to the original geometric phase. These three cases correspond exactly to the semi-orthogonal decompositions described by Orlov
[
33
]
. Namely, by Theorem
2.9
, there is a semi-orthogonal decomposition

-\mathcal{K}\cong\langle\mathcal{W}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{W}_{|n+1-d|},\mathcal{K}\rangle,

where
\mathcal{W}_{i}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(\operatorname{Spec\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}},\mathbb{G}_{m},0)\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}\operatorname{Spec\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}})\cong\operatorname{D^{b}(\mathbb{C}\operatorname{-vect})}
and:

(i)

If
n+1=d
, then
\theta_{K}
is on the wall so

\mathcal{K}\cong-\mathcal{K}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta},\Gamma,w)\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta^{-1}},\Gamma,w);

(ii)

If
n+1>d
, then
\theta_{K}
is in the positive chamber so

\mathcal{K}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta^{-1}},\Gamma,w),\qquad-\mathcal{K}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z(s))\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta},\Gamma,w);

(iii)

If
n+1<d
, then
\theta_{K}
is in the negative chamber so

\mathcal{K}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta},\Gamma,w),\qquad-\mathcal{K}\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{abs}}(V_{\theta^{-1}},\Gamma,w)\cong\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z(s)).

The explicit matching of the GLSM story with Orlov’s result was originally examined by Segal
[
37
]
with follow-up works by Shipman
[
39
]
and Ballard-Favero-Katzarkov
[
5
]
. In the case where
n+1>d
, Kuznetsov
[
27
, Corollary 4.4]
shows that
\mathcal{K}
is a fractional Calabi-Yau of dimension
(n+1)(d-2)/d
. This also follows from Orlov’s description
[
33
]
which also shows that if
n+1<d
the category
-\mathcal{K}
is fractional Calabi-Yau. Furthermore, if
d
divides
n+1
, or
d
is even and
d/2
divides
n+1
, then
\mathcal{K}
is Calabi-Yau. We remark that
\mathcal{K}
in this example is often referred to as the Kuznetsov or residual component of
\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{b}}(\operatorname{coh}Z(s))
.

3.
Kuznetsov Categories for complete intersections in toric varieties

Let
V
be an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space and assume that
G
is abelian, or, equivalently,
G\subseteq(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}\subseteq\text{GL}(V)
.
In this case, the combinatorics of the GIT fan is completely explicit
[
14
]
. In light of this work, it is most often referred to as the GKZ fan.

Here, every GIT quotient
V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G
with
\theta
lying in the interior of the support of the GKZ fan corresponds to a toric variety
X_{\Sigma}
. Using the inclusion
\iota:G\hookrightarrow(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
, we obtain an exact sequence

0\to M\xrightarrow{\nu}\widehat{(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}}\xrightarrow{\hat{\iota}}\widehat{G}\to 0

(3.1)

where
M
is the kernel of
\hat{\iota}
. The dual map
\nu^{*}:\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to\text{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z})
gives a collection of lattice points

\bm{\nu}:=\{\nu^{*}(e_{i})\}\subset\text{Hom}(M,\mathbb{Z}).

The GIT quotients which occur are precisely those corresponding to semiprojective toric varieties
X_{\Sigma}
with
|\Sigma|=\text{Cone}(\bm{\nu})
and
\Sigma(1)\subseteq\bm{\nu}
. In our notation this means,
V_{\theta}=\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{\Sigma(1)}\setminus Z(B(\Sigma))\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{\nu\setminus\Sigma(1)}
where
B(\Sigma)
is the irrelevant ideal for
\Sigma
.

Now, suppose
X
is a projective toric variety. Then we can write
X=V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G
for some
G\subseteq(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n}
and a character
\theta:G\to\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
. In fact,

G=\text{Hom}(\operatorname{Cl}(X),\mathbb{G}_{m})

is the character group of the class group of
X
and the character
\theta\in\widehat{G}=\operatorname{Cl}(X)
is ample. We denote by
\theta_{D}
the character in
\widehat{G}
when we regard a divisor
D\in\operatorname{Cl}(X)
as a character.

Now consider a complete intersection in
X
defined by the common zero locus of global sections
s_{i}\in\Gamma(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}))
for
1\leq i\leq r
.
Take the vector bundle
\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D_{i})
. We denote the total space by
\operatorname{tot}(\mathcal{E}):=\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{Sym}\mathcal{E}^{\vee}=\operatorname{tot}\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(-D_{i})
. To ensure that
\operatorname{tot}(\mathcal{E})
is also a GIT quotient, we assume the divisors
D_{i}
are nef (see e.g.
[
12
, Lemma 5.19]
following
[
10
, Chapters 6 and 7]
).

This allows us to construct a geometric abelian GLSM. Namely, we take
\Gamma=G\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
and consider the
\Gamma
-action on
V\times\mathbb{C}^{r}
given by

(g,h)\cdot(v,t_{1},...,t_{r})=(g\cdot v,\theta_{-D_{1}}(g)ht_{1},...,\theta_{-D_{r}}(g)ht_{r}).

(3.2)

Then,
V\times\mathbb{C}^{r}/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G=\operatorname{tot}(\mathcal{E})
and the additional
\mathbb{G}_{m}
acts by fiberwise dilation. The action restricted to
G
in (
3.2
) provides us with an inclusion
\iota^{\prime}:G\to(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n+r}
. We define the map
\nu^{\prime}:M\oplus\operatorname{\mathbb{Z}}^{r}\to\widehat{(\operatorname{\mathbb{C}}^{*})^{n+r}}
to obtain an analogous short exact sequence to (
3.1
).

The dilation action scaling the fibers of the total space extends to the coordinate ring. Namely, write
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
for the coordinates of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. The global function

w=\sum_{i}u_{i}f_{i}:U_{\Sigma}\times\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{r}\to\operatorname{\mathbb{A}}^{1}

is then
\chi
-semi-invariant, where
\chi:\Gamma\to\operatorname{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
is the character defined by the projection to the second factor. This gives the GLSM

(V\times\mathbb{C}^{r},\Gamma,\chi,\theta,w)

in the notation of Definition
2.1
.

For convenience, we provide a description of the fan for
\operatorname{tot}(\mathcal{E})
here. We denote the fan for
\operatorname{tot}(\mathcal{E})
by
\Sigma^{\mathcal{E}}
.
We recall the description of
\Sigma^{\mathcal{E}}
(from, e.g.,

Corollary 4.5
.

Any complete intersection of
r\geq 2
ample divisors in a Fano GIT quotient
X
is a Fano visitor and the derived category of the Fano host is equivalent to an anti-Kuznetsov category.

Proof.

As
X=[V/\!\!/\!_{\theta}G]
, we have
\omega^{-1}_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}(\det V)
. Hence, the assumption that
X
is Fano is equivalent to requiring that
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\det V)
is ample. On the other hand, if our complete intersection comes from ample divisors, then
\operatorname{\mathcal{O}}_{X}(W^{\vee})
is a sum of ample line bundles. Therefore the assumptions of Theorem
4.3
(i) hold.

Now we check that
(V,W,\pi(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}))
is a positive triple. Consider the trivial subrepresentatations of
\operatorname{Sym}V^{\vee}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}W^{\vee}(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}^{r})
. Since
W^{\vee}
is a sum of ample representations, so is
\operatorname{Sym}W^{\vee}(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}^{r})
. Hence, trivial representations can only occur if one cancels a non-trivial representation in
\operatorname{Sym}W^{\vee}(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}^{r})
with an anti-ample representation in

\operatorname{Sym}V^{\vee}
. This means that any element of
(\operatorname{Sym}V^{\vee}\otimes\operatorname{Sym}W^{\vee}(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}^{r}))^{G}
lies in the ideal generated by

(\operatorname{Sym}V^{\vee}(\theta_{-K+\epsilon}^{r}))^{G},

as desired.
∎

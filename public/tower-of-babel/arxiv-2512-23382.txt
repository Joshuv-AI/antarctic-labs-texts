A hypergraph

Let
\mathcal{F}

r

r

r

n

Let

r

\mathcal{H}

More specifically, if

Lemma 2.2
.

For integers
\ell^{\prime}\geq r\geq 3
, we have

\binom{2\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}>\frac{1}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{2}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r-1}+\frac{1}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}.

Proof.

Note that
\ell^{\prime}\geq r
. Thus we have

\displaystyle\binom{2\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\left(\frac{1}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r}+\frac{2}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r-1}+\frac{1}{2\ell^{\prime}}\binom{2\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)

\displaystyle=\binom{2\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2}{2\ell^{\prime}-r+1}-\frac{r-1}{(2\ell^{\prime}-r+1)(2\ell^{\prime}-r+2)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{2\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}-\frac{2}{r+1}-\frac{r-1}{(r+1)(r+2)}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\binom{2\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{3}-\frac{2}{4}-\frac{3-1}{(3+1)(3+2)}\right)>0.

Lemma 2.3
.

For integers

\binom{k\ell-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell-1}{r-2}\geq\binom{(k-1)\ell}{r-1}+1.

Note that

\displaystyle 2\binom{k\ell-2}{r-2}-\binom{k\ell-1}{r-2}-2

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle 2~\frac{(k\ell-2)\dots(k\ell-r+1)}{(r-2)!}-\frac{(k\ell-1)\dots(k\ell-r+2)}{(r-2)!}-2

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{(k\ell-2)\dots(k\ell-r+2)}{(r-2)!}\left(k\ell-2r+3-\frac{2(r-2)!}{(k\ell-2)\dots(k\ell-r+2)}\right)>0,

Lemma 2.4

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell-r+1}{r-1}\binom{(k-1)\ell-1}{r-2}+\frac{k\ell-2r+2}{r-1}\binom{k\ell-1}{r-3}>0.

.

Proof.

It’s sufficent to prove
V(\mathcal{G}_{0})=V(\mathcal{G})
. Suppose there exists
v\in V(\mathcal{G})\setminus V(\mathcal{G}_{0})
. Assume
v\in H
, where
H\in\mathcal{G}
.
Suppose
H\cap V(\mathcal{G}_{0})=\emptyset.
By
the same process as the proof of Lemma
4.14
, there are
(k-1)
disjoint copies of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{H}\setminus\{H\}
. By Lemma
4.5
, we can find another copy of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
disjoint with the
k-1
copies of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
from
H
, a contradiction. Let
u\in H\cap V(\mathcal{G}_{0})
. By Lemma
2.1
, we can let
V(\mathcal{G}_{0})=\{v_{1}(=u),v_{2},\dots,v_{k\ell^{\prime}-1}\}
be a good order of
V(\mathcal{G}_{0})
. Since
u,v
has large enough Berge-common neighbours, we can find a copy of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
contains
\{v,v_{1}(=u),\dots,v_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\}
as defining vertices. By the same argument as the proof of Lemma
4.14
, there are
k
disjoint copies of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
in
\mathcal{H}
, a contradiction.

Let
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\{S\in\mathcal{U}:S\cap X^{+}\neq\emptyset\}
and
\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]=\{S\in\mathcal{U}:S\subseteq X\setminus X^{+}\}
.
Then
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\cup\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]
.

Lemma 4.16
.

We have
|\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}|\leq 1
. Moreover, when
\ell
is odd,
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\emptyset
.

Proof.

Suppose there are
S_{1},S_{2}\in\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}
, say
S_{i}\subseteq E_{i}
for some
E_{i}\in\mathcal{H}
and
i=1,2
. By Lemma
4.9
,
S_{1}\cap S_{2}=\emptyset
. Assume
v_{i}\in S_{i}\cap X^{+}
for
i=1,2
. Obviously,
E_{1},E_{2}\notin\mathcal{T}_{r-1}
. Since
v_{1}\in X^{+}\subseteq X
, there exist
E_{3}\in\mathcal{T}_{r-1}
and
u_{1}\in V(\mathcal{G})
such that
\{u_{1},v_{1}\}\subseteq E_{3}.
Let
H\in\mathcal{G}
with
u_{1}\in H
. Since
|V(\mathcal{G})|=k\ell^{\prime}-1
,
|V(\mathcal{G})\setminus(H\setminus\{u_{1}\})|=(k-1)\ell^{\prime}
. Since
v_{2}\in S_{2}\cap X^{+}
,
d_{\mathcal{T}_{r-1}}(v_{2})\geq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}>\binom{(k-1)\ell^{\prime}}{r-1}+1
, where the last inequality holds by Lemma
2.3
. By Pigeonhole Principle, there exists
E_{4}\in\mathcal{T}_{r-1}
such that
v_{2}\in E_{4}
and
E_{4}\cap(H\setminus\{u_{1}\})\not=\emptyset
. Let
u_{2}\in E_{4}\cap(H\setminus\{u_{1}\})
and set
\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\ldots,u_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\}\subseteq H
.
For
i=1,2
, choose
x_{i}\in S_{i}\setminus\{v_{i}\}.
Since
|BCN(H)|\geq\alpha n
, there are
\{\omega_{1},\omega_{3},\omega_{4},\ldots,\omega_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\}
and
\{E_{1,1},E_{1,3},E_{3,3},E_{3,4},\ldots,E_{\ell^{\prime}-1,\ell^{\prime}1-},E_{\ell^{\prime}-1,2}\}
such that

\mathcal{P}:=\{x_{1},E_{1},v_{1},E_{3},u_{1},E_{1,1},\omega_{1},E_{1,3},u_{3},\ldots,u_{\ell^{\prime}-1},E_{\ell^{\prime}-1,\ell^{\prime}-1},\omega_{\ell^{\prime}-1},E_{\ell^{\prime}-1,2},u_{2},E_{4},v_{2},E_{2},x_{2}\}

forms a
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
and
DV_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P})\cap V(\mathcal{G})=\{u_{1},u_{2},\ldots,u_{\ell^{\prime}-1}\}
, a contradiction with Lemma
4.4
.

Suppose
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset
when
\ell
is odd. Let
S\in\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}
,
v\in S\cap X^{+}
and
S\subseteq E
, where
E\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
v\in X^{+}
, there exist
E^{\prime}\in\mathcal{T}_{r-1}
and
u\in V(\mathcal{G})
with
\{u,v\}\subseteq E^{\prime}
. Obviously,
E\neq E^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.5
, there is a copy of
\text{Berge-}P_{2\ell^{\prime}-1}
such that
u
is an end vertex of the path. Together with
E^{\prime}
and
E
, there is a copy of
\text{Berge-}P_{2\ell^{\prime}+1}
, denoted as
\mathcal{P}
. Then there is a copy of
\text{Berge-}P_{\ell}
, say
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
, such that
V(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\subseteq V(\mathcal{P})
but
|DV_{\mathcal{H}}(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})\cap V(\mathcal{G})|=\ell^{\prime}-1
, a contradiction with Lemma
4.4
.

Recall
\mathcal{U}:=\mathcal{U}_{2}\cup\mathcal{U}_{3+}
. By Lemma
4.9
, the hyperedges in
\mathcal{U}
are pairwise disjoint.
Since
\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]
is pairwise disjoint, we have
|\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]|\leq\frac{|X|-|X^{+}|}{2}
.
By Lemma
4.16
,
|\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}|\leq 1
. If
|\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}|=1
, then we let
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\{S_{0}\}
for some
S_{0}\in\mathcal{U}
and define
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\{E\in\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}|S_{0}\subseteq E\}
, where
t_{0}=r-|S_{0}|
. Let
|X\setminus X^{+}|=x
. Then
|X^{+}|=|X|-x
.

Let

\displaystyle E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]=\{E\in E(X,V(\mathcal{G})):E\cap X^{+}\neq\emptyset,E\cap V(\mathcal{G})\neq\emptyset\},

\displaystyle E[X\setminus X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]=\{E\in E(X,V(\mathcal{G})):(E\cap X)\subseteq(X\setminus X^{+}),E\cap V(\mathcal{G})\neq\emptyset\}.

Lemma 4.17
.

We have

|\mathcal{H}[X]|+|E[X\setminus X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.16
,

|\mathcal{U}^{X+}|\leq 1
. Note that
\left|\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{+}\right|\leq\binom{|V(\mathcal{G})|}{r-2}\left|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}\right|=\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}\right|
.
We consider two cases.

Case 1:

\mathcal{U}^{X+}=\emptyset
or
|\mathcal{U}^{X+}|=1
but
t_{0}\neq r-2
.

In this case,
\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}\subseteq X\setminus X^{+}
. Then
|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor
by the hyperedges in
\mathcal{U}
being pairwise disjoint.
When
t_{0}\neq r-2
, let
\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}^{\star}=\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{0}
. Set
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(\cup_{t\in[0,r-3]\setminus\{t_{0}\}}\mathcal{T}_{t})\cup\mathcal{T}_{t_{0}}^{*}\cup\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{-}
if
t_{0}\neq r-2
, and
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}=(\cup_{t\in[0,r-3]}\mathcal{T}_{t})\cup\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{-}
if
\mathcal{U}^{X+}=\emptyset
. By Lemma
4.11
,
|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}|\leq 2|\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]|\leq|X|-|X^{+}|=x.
So

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}[X]|+|E[X\setminus X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\mathcal{T}^{\prime}\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{+}\right|+\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}-1\right)x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor.

Case 2:

|\mathcal{U}^{X+}|=1
and
t_{0}=r-2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{\star-}=\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{-}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{0}
,
\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{\star+}=\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{+}\setminus\mathcal{S}_{0}
and
\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\star+}=\{U\in\mathcal{U}_{2}^{+}:U\subseteq X\setminus X^{+}\}
. Then
|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\star+}|\leq\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor
. By Lemma
4.11
, we have

\left|\left(\bigcup_{t\in[0,r-3]}\mathcal{T}_{t}\right)\cup\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{\star-}\right|\leq 2|\mathcal{U}[X\setminus X^{+}]|\leq|X|-|X^{+}|=x.

So we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{H}[X]|+|E[X\setminus X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left(\bigcup_{t\in[0,r-3]}\mathcal{T}_{t}\right)\bigcup\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{\star-}\right|+\left|\mathcal{T}_{r-2}^{\star+}\right|+\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}-1\right)x

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}|\mathcal{U}_{2}^{\star+}|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor.

Lemma 4.18
.

We have

|E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)&\mbox{ if ~$\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\emptyset$,}\\
\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+2&\mbox{ if ~$\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset$ and $t_{0}\neq r-2$,}\\
\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}&\mbox{ if ~$\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset$ and $t_{0}=r-2$.}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

If
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\emptyset
, by Lemma
4.15
, we have

|E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x).

Now we will finish the proof by considering the following two cases.

Case 1:

\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset
and
t_{0}\neq r-2
.

By Lemma
4.11
, we have
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|\leq 2
. Thus

|E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+2.

Case 2:

\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset
and

t_{0}=r-2
.

In this case, we have
|\mathcal{S}_{0}|\leq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}
. Thus

|E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}.

Now we are going to prove our main result.

1.6
Fellow the notions and proofs of the lemmas before,

|X|+|Y|+|V(\mathcal{G})|=n

and

.

4.19

Suppose
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}=\emptyset
when

is even or

when
\mathcal{U}^{X^{+}}\not=\emptyset
. By Lemma

,
|E[X^{+},V(\mathcal{G})]|\leq\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+2

By (

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+2+\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\left\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(|X|-x)+\mathbb{I}_{\ell}\cdot\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}+\left(\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}-\frac{1}{2}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}}{r-2}\right)x+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2}\lfloor\frac{x}{2}\rfloor

Concluding remarks

In Theorem
1.6
,
the range of
r
is restricted by
\ell
or
\left\lfloor\frac{\ell+1}{2}\right\rfloor
. But we believe that the results still hold in a wider range.

Conjecture 5.1
.

For integers
\ell,r
and
k\geq 2
, there exists
N_{\ell,r,k}
such that for
n>N_{\ell,r,k}
, and
2\leq r\leq k\left\lfloor\frac{\ell+1}{2}\right\rfloor
,

\mathrm{ex}_{r}(n,\text{Berge-}kP_{\ell})=\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-1}(n-k\ell^{\prime}-1+1)+\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r}+\mathbb{I}_{r}\binom{k\ell^{\prime}-1}{r-2},

where
\ell^{\prime}=\left\lfloor\frac{\ell+1}{2}\right\rfloor.

The lower bound is similar to the construction in Section 1. Moreover, with a similar construction, we can further study the Turán number of a linear forest.

Conjecture 5.2
.

Let
\mathcal{P}=P_{\ell_{1}}\cup\dots\cup P_{\ell_{k}}
be a linear forest where

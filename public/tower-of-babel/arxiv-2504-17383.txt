Abstract
. We establish certain oscillation estimates for weak solutions to nonlinear, anomalous phase transitions modeled on the nonlocal two-phase Stefan problem. The problem is singular in time, is scaling deficient and influenced by far-off effects. We study the the problem in a geometry adapted to the solution and obtain oscillation estimates in intrinsically scaled cylinders. Furthermore, via certain uniform estimates, we construct a continuous weak solution to the corresponding initial boundary value problem with a quantitative modulus of continuity.

1.
Introduction

We study the following nonlocal two-phase Stefan problem

(1.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u+\beta(u))+\mathcal{L}u\ni 0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T]\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1},

where
\Omega
is an open and bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
,
u
is an unknown, and a maximal monotone graph
\beta:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow[0,1]
is defined as

\displaystyle\beta(\xi)=\begin{cases}1\quad&\text{if }\xi>0,\\
[0,1]\quad&\text{if }\xi=0,\\
0\quad&\text{if }\xi<0.\end{cases}

In addition, the nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}u(x,t)=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dy,

where
s\in(0,1)
,
p>2
and
k:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times(0,T]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a measurable function with

\displaystyle k(x,y,t)=k(y,x,t)\quad\text{and}\quad\Lambda^{-1}\leq k(x,y,t)\leq\Lambda

for some constant
\Lambda\geq
1. The aim of the article is to derive modulus of continuity estimates in a quantitative way and to establish the existence of a continuous weak solution to the corresponding initial boundary value problem of the nonlocal two-phase Stefan problem.

1.1.
History and Motivation

A variational model of phase transition, say from ice to water, as a function of the unknown temperature
\theta
, is given by the following classical Stefan equation

\partial_{t}(\theta+\beta(\theta))=\Delta\theta

(see
[
Ste89
,
Kam61
,
Ole60
]
), where
\beta
is a maximal monotone graph. The reason why we have such a graph is to represent the latent heat of the water molecules as they undergo phase transition from ice at 0℃ to water at 0℃; outside of this transition region we have the usual heat equation; we also allow for negative temperatures. The equation also models many other physical phenomena and we refer the interested reader to
[
Vis08
]
.

The first issue in studying the equation is the notion of solution employed. We are interested in weak solutions which are obtained via integration by parts and differential inclusions. There are other notions of solution possible as in
[
Vis08
]
. Having established the notion of solution, we turn to the well-posedness for weak solutions. A natural question would be to ask for the existence of a continuous temperature - as ice transitions to water the equation should not predict sudden discontinuities in the temperature. It would also be of interest to quantify the modulus of continuity.

The continuity of temperature was indeed shown in a series of works
[
DiB82
,
Zie82
,
CE83
,
Sac83
,
DiB86
]
which also dealt with boundary regularity. Furthermore, the methods in
[
Zie82
,
DiB82
,
DiB86
]
were more general and could deal with a general nonlinear elliptic part with linear growth in the gradient. The paper
[
DiB86
]
also provided a boundary modulus of continuity, namely

(1.2)

\omega(r)=(\ln|\ln(Cr)|)^{-\alpha}\quad\mbox{ for }\quad 0<r<1

where
C>0
and
\alpha>0
are universal constants.

For the case where the Laplace operator is replaced by more general degenerate diffusion of
p
-Laplace type (
p>2
), such equations were first studied in
[
Urb97
,
Urb00
]
and an interior modulus of continuity was obtained in
[
BKU14
]
viz.

(1.3)

\omega(r)=|\ln(Cr)|^{-\alpha}\quad\mbox{ for }\quad 0<r<1

where
C>0
and
\alpha>0
are universal constants.
We note that (
1.3
) is an improvement over (
1.2
) even for the Laplace operator. Subsequently, a boundary modulus of continuity was derived in
[
BKLU18
]
which was of type (
1.2
).

Later, the improved modulus (
1.3
) for the boundary case was proved in
[
Lia22
]
.
On the other hand, recently, there are noteworthy results for interior regularity for the Stefan problem with the
p
-Laplace operator. More specifically, when
p=n\geq 2
, the improved modulus of continuity, which is

\omega(r)\eqsim\exp\left(-c|\ln r|^{1/n}\right)

was derived in
[
Lia24
,
GLU24
]
. Moreover, significant Hölder continuity results with the
p
-Laplace operator when
p>\max\{n,2\}
were established in
[
Lia24a
,
GLU24
]
, which are notable since such regularity was not readily anticipated for the equation (
1.1
) involving such a degeneracy from
\beta(\cdot)
.

We note that the passage from the Laplace operator to the
p
-Laplace operator for parabolic problems leads to fundamental new difficulties as far as regularity is concerned since the equation is no longer scaling invariant and one must work in a geometry intrinsic to the equation; such a geometry also leads to non-trivial technical issues
[
Urb08
]
.

When modeling phase transition via the Stefan equation above, one assumes that the material is homogeneous - for example the ice consists only of water molecules. In practice, this corresponds to the assumption that the mean square displacement of the molecule in a given time
t
is proportional to the time
t
(which is also reflected in the classical heat equation). However, if strong heterogeneities are present at large scales, such an assumption is untenable. Such situations involving
anomalous
scaling naturally lead to consideration of fractional derivatives
[
Vol14
,
RD23
]
.

In
[
ACM22
]
, it was shown that the Stefan problem involving anomalous diffusion with initial-boundary value conditions admits a continuous solution and a logarithmic modulus of continuity is claimed. On the other hand, we study the initial boundary value problem for diffusion operators comparable to the fractional
p
-Laplace (
p>2
) and construct a weak solution with a quantitative boundary modulus of continuity as in (
1.3
) and also provide quantitative continuity estimates at the initial boundary.

Finally, we note that the regularity results for weak solutions to parabolic nonlocal equations which have no singular effect have been investigated extensively, see for instance
[
KW23
,
FK13
,
Lia24c
,
Lia24b
,
APT24
,
BK24
,
DZZ21
,
Tav24
,
BLS21
,
NNSW24
,
Str19a
,
Str19
,

,
Pra24
,
BGK23
,
GLT25
,
DKLN25
,
LW24
,
KWZ24
]
.

1.2.
Interior Oscillation Estimate

Now we are going to provide our main results.
First, we investigate a quantitative oscillation estimate of a weak solution to a regularized problem of (
1.1
). To this end, we fix a cut-off function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(-1,1)
such that
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\psi\,dt=1
and we write
\psi_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\coloneqq\frac{1}{\varepsilon}\psi\left(\frac{\xi}{\varepsilon}\right)
for
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We now consider a weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to

(1.4)

\partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{in }\Omega\times(0,T],

where

(1.5)

\beta_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=(\beta*\psi_{\varepsilon})(\xi).

Let us give a precise definition of a weak solution to (
1.4
).

Definition 1.1
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we say that
u_{\varepsilon}
is a locally bounded weak solution to (
1.4
) if

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;W^{s,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega))\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(0,T;L^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega))

satisfies

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))\partial_{t}\varphi\,dx\,dt+\int_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))\varphi\,dx\Bigg\rvert_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u_{\varepsilon}(y,t)|^{p-2}(u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u_{\varepsilon}(y,t))(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy\,dt=0

for any
\varphi\in L^{p}(t_{1},t_{2};W^{s,p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(\Omega))
, where
(t_{1},t_{2}]\Subset(0,T]
and the support of
\varphi(t,\cdot)
is compactly embedded in
\Omega
for each
t\in(0,T]
.

For
f\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
,
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
, and
R>0
, we define the tail as follows:

\displaystyle\textrm{Tail}(f;Q_{R}(z_{0}))=\sup_{t_{0}-\rho^{sp}\leq t\leq t_{0}}\left(\rho^{sp}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{\rho}(x_{0})}\dfrac{|f(y)|^{p-1}}{|x_{0}-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

For any
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}
,
R>0
,
\mathcal{T}
and
\theta>0
, we define

\displaystyle I^{(\theta)}_{\tau}(t_{0})=(t_{0}-\tau\theta,t_{0}]\quad\text{and}\quad{Q}^{(\theta)}_{{R},\mathcal{T}}(z_{0})\coloneqq B_{R}(x_{0})\times I^{(\theta)}_{\mathcal{T}}(t_{0}).

In particular, we write

\displaystyle Q^{(\theta)}_{R}(z_{0})\coloneqq{Q}^{(\theta)}_{{R},R^{sp}}(z_{0})\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{R}(z_{0})\coloneqq{Q}^{(1)}_{{R},R^{sp}}(z_{0}).

We are now ready to state our main results. We first give the explicit interior oscillation estimate.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be a bounded weak solution to

\partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}=0\quad\text{in }Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0}).

Let us take

\omega_{0}\coloneqq\max\{\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({Q}_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\varepsilon};{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})),1\}.

Suppose
Q_{\rho_{0}}^{(\omega_{0}/4)^{2-p}}(z_{0})\subset{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})
for some constant
\rho_{0}\in(0,\mathcal{R}]
. Then there is a constant
\varsigma=\varsigma(n,s,p,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that for any
r<\rho_{0}
,

(1.6)

\displaystyle\osc_{Q^{(\omega_{0}/4)^{2-p}}_{r}(z_{0})}u_{\varepsilon}\leq c\omega_{0}\left(1+\ln(\rho_{0}/r)\right)^{-\varsigma/2}+4\varepsilon

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda)
independent of
\varepsilon
.

1.3.
Oscillation Estimate near the Boundary

Next, we consider oscillation estimates of solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to (
1.4
) up to the boundary. We first recall a fractional Sobolev space. Let
f:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a measurable function and
\Omega\Subset\Omega^{\prime}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
. We define

X_{f}^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=\{u\in W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})\,:\,u\equiv f\text{ on }{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

By
[
BLS18
, Lemma 2.11]
, we observe
X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})=X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n})
. Thus we write

X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega)\coloneqq X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}).

Let us give a notion of weak solution to (
1.4
) near the boundary.

Definition 1.3
.

Let us fix
g\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
with
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
. For
I_{\mathcal{R}}=I_{\mathcal{R}}(t_{0})
, we say that

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(I_{\mathcal{R}};W^{s,p}(B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})))\cap C(I_{\mathcal{R}};L^{2}(B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})))\cap L^{\infty}(I_{\mathcal{R}};L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap L^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(I_{\mathcal{R}};L^{\infty}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\Omega))

is a locally bounded weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})\cap\Omega_{T}$},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in $Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})\setminus\Omega_{T}$},\end{aligned}\right.

if
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(I_{\mathcal{R}};W^{s,p}(B))
for some
B\Supset\Omega\cap B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})
and

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz+\int_{\Omega}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))\varphi\,dx\Bigg\rvert_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u_{\varepsilon}(y,t)|^{p-2}(u_{\varepsilon}(x,t)-u_{\varepsilon}(y,t))(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy\,dt=0

holds for any
\varphi\in L^{p}(t_{1},t_{2};X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega\cap B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})))\cap W^{1,2}(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(\Omega\cap B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})))
with
(t_{1},t_{2}]\Subset I_{R}(t_{0})
.

We now assume that the complement of the domain
\Omega
satisfies a measure density condition. More specifically, there exists a constant
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for any
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
,

(1.7)

\inf_{0<r\leq 1}|B_{r}(x_{0})\setminus\Omega|\geq\alpha_{0}|B_{r}|.

We point out that such an assumption is standard to obtain the continuity at the boundary (see, for instance
[
KKP16
,
BKLU18
,
Lia22
]
).

In addition, for any
g\in C(\overline{Q})
with
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
which will be play a role as a boundary data, we always assume

(1.8)

|g(z_{1})-g(z_{2})|\leq\omega_{g}\left(|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{1}{sp}}\right)\quad\text{for any }z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{Q}

for some non-decreasing function
\omega_{g}:{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{+}
with
\omega_{g}(0)=0
.

Let us state oscillation estimates at the lateral boundary.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
, let

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

be a bounded weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})\cap\Omega_{T}$},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in $Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})\setminus\Omega_{T}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega
and
Q_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0})\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{T}
.
Let us assume

(1.9)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}({Q}_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0}))}+\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\varepsilon};{Q}_{\mathcal{R}}(z_{0}))\leq 1.

Then there exist constants
\varsigma=\varsigma(n,s,p,\Lambda,\alpha_{0})\in(0,1)
such that if

\displaystyle\omega_{g}(r)\leq c_{g}\left(1+\ln(\mathcal{R}/r)\right)^{-\delta}

holds where
\delta\in(\varsigma,1)
and
r\in(0,\mathcal{R}]
, then

(1.10)

\displaystyle\osc_{Q_{r}(z_{0})}u_{\varepsilon}\leq c\left(1+\ln(\mathcal{R}/r)\right)^{-\varsigma/2}+4\varepsilon

holds for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda,\alpha_{0},\delta,c_{g})
.

We now provide the continuity estimate at the initial boundary.

Theorem 1.5
.

For
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
and
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
, let

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

be a bounded weak solution to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $\left(B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\times(0,\mathcal{R}^{sp}]\right)\cap\Omega_{T}$},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in $(B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\times[0,\mathcal{R}^{sp}])\setminus\Omega_{T}$},\end{aligned}\right.

where
x_{0}\in\overline{\Omega}
and
B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\times(0,\mathcal{R}^{sp}]\subset\Omega^{\prime}_{T}
.
Let us assume

(1.11)

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\times[0,\mathcal{R}^{sp}]}+\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\varepsilon};B_{\mathcal{R}}(x_{0})\times[0,\mathcal{R}^{sp}])\leq 1.

If there are constants
c_{g}\geq 1
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that for any
r\in(0,\mathcal{R}]
,

\displaystyle\omega_{g}(r)\leq c_{g}\left(1+\ln(\mathcal{R}/r)\right)^{-\delta}

holds, then for any
r\in(0,\mathcal{R}]
,

(1.12)

\displaystyle\osc_{B_{r}(x_{0})\times[0,r^{sp}]}u_{\varepsilon}\leq c\left(1+\ln(\mathcal{R}/r)\right)^{-\delta}

holds, where
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda,\alpha_{0},\delta,c_{g})
.

Remark 1.6
.

We note that in order to avoid complicated oscillation estimates, we use the normalization conditions (
1.9
) and (
1.11
).
However, by considering the estimates given in Section
4
and Section
5
, we obtain similar quantitative oscillation estimates (
1.10
) and (
1.12
) without assuming (
1.9
) and (
1.11
), respectively.

1.4.
Existence of a continuous solution

Before we state the existence result of the two-phase Stefan problem, we introduce a precise notion of a weak solution to the corresponding initial boundary value problem of (
1.1
).

Definition 1.7
.

Let us fix
g\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))
with
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
.
We say that

u\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

is a weak solution to

(1.13)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(u+\beta(u))+\mathcal{L}u&\ni 0&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$},\\
u&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus(\partial\Omega\times(0,T]\cup\Omega\times\{t=0\})$},\end{aligned}\right.

if there is a pair
(u,v)
with
v\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))
and

\{v(z)\,:\,z\in\Omega_{T}\}\subset\{(u+\beta(u))(z)\,:\,z\in\Omega_{T}\}

in the sense of graphs and

\displaystyle-\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{\Omega}v\partial_{t}\varphi\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|u(x,t)-u(y,t)|^{p-2}(u(x,t)-u(y,t))(\varphi(x,t)-\varphi(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle=-\int_{\Omega}v\varphi\,dx\bigg\lvert_{t=t_{1}}^{t=t_{2}}

holds for any
(t_{1},t_{2}]\Subset(0,T]
and

\varphi\in L^{p}(t_{1},t_{2};X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega))\cap W^{1,2}(t_{1},t_{2};L^{2}(\Omega^{\prime}))
.

We now provide the existence result of the two-phase Stefan problem.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let us fix
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
and the complement of the domain
\Omega
satisfy the measure density condition with
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
. If

(1.14)

g\in C\left([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\right)\cap L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

with
\partial_{t}g\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T})
,
then there is a weak solution
u\in C\left(\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]\right)
to (
1.13
). In addition, there is a constant
\varsigma=\varsigma(n,s,p,\Lambda,\alpha_{0})
such that if for any
r>0
,

\displaystyle\omega_{g}(r)\leq c_{g}\left(1+|\ln(1/r)|\right)^{-\delta}

holds for some
\delta\in(\varsigma,1)
, then we have

(1.15)

\displaystyle\sup_{z_{1},z_{2}\in\overline{\Omega}\times[0,T]}|u(z_{1})-u(z_{2})|\leq c\left[1+\left|\ln\left(\frac{1}{|x_{1}-x_{2}|+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\frac{1}{sp}}}\right)\right|\right]^{-\varsigma/2}

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda,\alpha_{0},\|g\|_{L^{\infty}},T,\Omega,\Omega^{\prime},c_{g},\delta)
.

1.5.
Technical Tools

Let us give a sketch of the proof of our main theorems. First, we prove uniform oscillation estimates of the solution
u_{\varepsilon}
to approximated equation (
1.4
) of (
1.1
).
To this end, we divide the proof into three parts, interior estimates, lateral boundary estimates and initial boundary estimates.

Based on
[
BKU14
,
Lia22
]
, we are going to prove interior oscillation estimates. First, we prove De Giorgi type lemmas under the assumption that the tail is sufficiently small (see Lemma
3.4
). Note that the tail does not appear in local problems, so we need to make additional analysis to deal with the term considering appropriate scaling for it. Based on such lemmas, we next construct a sequence of intrinsic cylinders which contain global information about the solution, such as
Q_{\rho}^{(\omega^{2-p})}
, where
\omega\eqsim\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}(Q)}+\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\varepsilon};Q)
(see Section
3.1
for more details).
We refer to
[
APT24
,
Lia24b
]
for constructing such intrinsic cylinders for parabolic fractional
p
-Laplacian type equations.
With this construction, we are able to obtain that the tail on each cylinder is small enough. Therefore, we apply De Giorgi type lemmas together with two alternative arguments (see (
3.23
) and (
3.37
) below), which leads to the desired oscillation estimates.
More specifically, we use an iteration argument to get the following implications

\osc_{Q_{i}}u_{\varepsilon}\lesssim\omega_{i}\implies\mathrm{Tail}(u_{\varepsilon};Q_{i})\lesssim\omega_{i}\implies\osc_{Q_{i+1}}u_{\varepsilon}\lesssim\omega_{i+1}

with a sequence of shrinking parabolic cylinders
Q_{i}
and non-increasing numbers
\omega_{i}
, and then employ a technical lemma to derive oscillation estimates.

For the lateral boundary, our method is motivated by
[
BKLU18
,
Lia22
]
. Compared to the interior case, we now consider boundary datum, which makes us use a different type intrinsic cylinder such as
Q_{\rho}^{(\overline{\theta})}
, where
\overline{\theta}\eqsim\omega^{1-p}
. Using the measure density condition as in (
1.7
) together with such a construction of the intrinsic cylinder, we have a measure shrinking lemma and a De Giorgi type lemma (see Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, respectively). As in the interior case, we thus obtain the desired oscillation estimates by constructing an appropriate sequence of intrinsic cylinders and using De Giorgi type lemmas. The remaining proof is about oscillation estimates at the initial level. Since we have energy estimates which is close to elliptic ones (see Lemma
5.1
), there is no singular effect from
\beta_{\varepsilon}
which is defined in (
1.5
). Therefore, in a similar way as above, we get the oscillation estimates which do not depend on
\varepsilon
.

Combining all three estimates yields uniform global oscillation estimates as in Lemma
6.1
. By the approximation argument as in
[
BKLU18
]
, we construct a weak solution to nonlocal two-phase Stefan problem and find a quantitative continuity estimates of such a solution.

Organization of the paper

The paper consists of 6 sections. Section
2
is devoted to basic notation, function spaces, the existence of a regularized equation, and recording necessary lemmas. In Section
3
, we prove interior oscillation estimates, while Sections
4
and
5
are devoted to lateral and initial boundary estimates, respectively. Finally, in Section
6
we prove existence result for our problem.

2.
Preliminaries

We always denote a general constant by
c
, which is equal or bigger than 1. To show the relevant dependencies of constants, we use parentheses, such as
c=c(n,s,p)
, if the constant
c
depends on
n,s
and
p
. We will usually denote by
x
the spatial variable,
t
the time variable, and
z=(x,t)
the space-time variable. For an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
T>0
, we write

\displaystyle U_{T}:=(0,T]\times U.

For
f\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(I\times U)
for an open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, an open interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and
k\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the truncation operator as follows:

\displaystyle(f-k)_{+}:=\max\{f-k,0\}\quad\text{and}\quad(f-k)_{-}:=\max\{k-f,0\}.

Let a bounded open set
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
T>0
be given. We define the function space

\displaystyle C(0,T;L^{2}_{\text{loc}}(U))

\displaystyle\quad=\left\{f\in L^{1}(U_{T}):t\mapsto\|f(\cdot,t)\|_{L^{2}(K)}\,\,\text{is continuous on }(0,T)\,\,\text{for any compact set }K\Subset U\right\}.

With
0<s<1\leq p<\infty
, we define the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}(U)
as

\displaystyle W^{s,p}(U)=\left\{f\in L^{p}(U)\,\,:\,\,\|f\|_{L^{p}(U)}+[f]_{W^{s,p}(U)}<\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{W^{s,p}(U)}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}(U)}+[f]_{W^{s,p}(U)}:=\left(\int_{U}|f|^{p}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\left(\int_{U}\int_{U}\dfrac{|f(x)-f(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We denote by
W^{s,p}_{0}(U)
the space of functions which consists of
f\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
f\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus U
.
Now we define

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\text{loc}}(0,T;W^{s,p}_{\text{loc}}(U))

\displaystyle\quad=\left\{f\in L^{1}((0,T]\times U):\text{for any compact }I^{\prime}\Subset(0,T)\text{ and }K\Subset U,\,\int_{I^{\prime}}\|f(\cdot,t)\|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(K)}\,dt<\infty\right\}.

With
s\in(0,1)
and a time interval
I\subset\mathbb{R}
, and the function space

\displaystyle L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\,\,:\,\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(y)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,dy<\infty\right\},

we define the following tail space:

\displaystyle L^{\infty}(I;L^{p-1}_{sp}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))=\left\{f\in L^{1}_{\text{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1})\,\,:\,\,\sup_{t\in I}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\dfrac{|f(y,t)|^{p-1}}{(1+|y|)^{n+sp}}\,dy<\infty\right\}.

We now provide some well-known results about nonlocal parabolic equations and some basic inequalities.

With
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, we recall the regular function
\beta_{\varepsilon}=\beta_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
defined in (
1.5
). We directly observe

(2.1)

\displaystyle\beta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}\geq 0\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\beta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\,d\xi=1.

We now verify the existence of a weak solution
u\equiv u_{\varepsilon}
to the corresponding initial-boundary value problem of a regularized problem of (
1.1
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\Supset\Omega
and

g\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))\cap C\left([0,T]\times\overline{\Omega^{\prime}}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad\partial_{t}g\in L^{2}(\Omega_{T}).

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there is a unique weak solution

u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

to

(2.2)

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}(u_{\varepsilon}+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon}))+\mathcal{L}u_{\varepsilon}&=0&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$},\\
u_{\varepsilon}&=g&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus(\partial\Omega\times(0,T]\cup\Omega\times\{t=0\})$}.\end{aligned}\right.

In addition, we have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,T]}|u_{\varepsilon}|\leq\sup_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\times[0,T]}|g|.

Proof.

We first observe that
b(\xi)\coloneqq\xi+\beta_{\varepsilon}(\xi)
is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism with

(2.4)

\displaystyle 1\leq|b^{\prime}(\xi)|\leq c=c(\varepsilon).

Let us define a nonlocal operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{b}v(x)=\text{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{\varphi(b^{-1}(v)(x)-b^{-1}(v)(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dy,

where
\varphi(t)\coloneqq|t|^{p-2}t
. By (
2.4
), we get

(2.5)

\displaystyle\varphi(b^{-1}(v)(x)-b^{-1}(v)(y))(v(x)-v(y))\eqsim|v(x)-v(y)|^{p}

with the implicit constant
c=c(p,\varepsilon)
. For each
t>0
, let us write

X^{s,p}_{b(g(t))}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\coloneqq\{f\in W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})\,:\,f(x)=b(g(x,t))\text{ a.e. }x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega\}.

We now define for any
v\in X^{s,p}_{b(g(t))}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
and
\phi\in X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})
,

\displaystyle{\langle{\mathcal{A}_{t}(v)},{\phi}\rangle}:

\displaystyle=\iint_{\Omega^{\prime}\times\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{\varphi(b^{-1}(v)(x)-b^{-1}(v)(y))(\phi(x)-\phi(y))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dx\,dy

\displaystyle\quad+2\int_{\Omega}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\Omega^{\prime}}\frac{\varphi(b^{-1}(v)(x)-g(y,t))\phi(x)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}k(x,y,t)\,dy\,dx.

By (
2.5
), we obtain that the operator
\mathcal{A}_{t}:X^{s,p}_{b(g(t))}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\to\left(X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\right)^{*}
is well-defined. We next define

\mathcal{A}:X_{0}^{s,p}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime})\times(0,T]\to\left(W^{s,p}(\Omega^{\prime})\right)^{*}\quad\text{as}\quad\mathcal{A}(w,t)=\mathcal{A}_{t}(w+b(g)).

We now follow the same lines as in the proof of
[
BLS21
, Theorem A.3]
together with (
2.4
) to see that there is a unique weak solution

\displaystyle w\in L^{p}(0,T;X^{s,p}_{0}(\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}))

to

\left\{\begin{aligned} \partial_{t}w+\mathcal{L}_{b}(w+b(g))&=-\partial_{t}(b(g))&&\qquad\mbox{in $\Omega_{T}$},\\
w+b(g)&=b(g)&&\qquad\mbox{in ${\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus(\partial\Omega\times(0,T]\cup\Omega\times\{t=0\})$}.\end{aligned}\right.

By considering the function

\displaystyle b^{-1}(w+b(g))\in L^{p}(0,T;W^{s,p}_{g}(\Omega^{\prime}))\cap C(0,T;L^{2}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{p-1}_{sp}({\mathbb{R}}^{n})),

which follows from (
2.4
) and the fact that
|b(\xi)|\leq\xi+1
, we obtain a weak solution
u_{\varepsilon}\coloneqq b^{-1}(w+b(g))
to (
2.2
).

We are now going to prove (
2.3
). We first note from the standard approximation argument as in the proof of
[
BLS21
, Proposition A.4]
that for any
\tau\in(0,T)
,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}(b(u_{\varepsilon}))(u_{\varepsilon}-M)_{+}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)}b^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-M)_{+}\,d\xi\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u_{\varepsilon}(x,0)}b^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-M)_{+}\,d\xi\,dx,

where
M\coloneqq\|g\|_{L^{\infty}({\Omega_{T}})}
. Therefore, by testing
(u_{\varepsilon}-M)_{+}
to (
2.2
) and considering the proof given in
[
BLS21
, Proposition A.4]
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\int_{0}^{u_{\varepsilon}(x,\tau)}b^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-M)_{+}\,d\xi\,dx\leq 0.

Using this together with (
2.4
) and the choice of
\tau\in(0,T)
, we have (
2.3
).
∎

We next provide a basic algebraic lemma which will be used to obtain oscillation estimates.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let the constants
M_{2},N_{2},L_{2}\geq 4
be given with
L_{2}\geq M_{2}
.
Let us take
a_{n}\coloneqq\omega_{0}^{L_{2}/M_{2}}(1+n)^{-\varepsilon}
, where
\omega_{0}\geq 1
and
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. Then there is a constant
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(M_{2},N_{2},L_{2})
such that if

\displaystyle g(x)\coloneqq 1-\frac{|x|^{M_{2}}}{N_{2}\omega_{0}^{L_{2}}},

then for any
n\geq 1
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle a_{n}\geq a_{n-1}g\left(a_{n-1}\right).

Proof.

Let us define

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)\coloneqq\left(\frac{x}{1+x}\right)^{\varepsilon}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2}(x)\coloneqq 1-\frac{1}{M_{2}N_{2}x^{\varepsilon M_{2}}}

for any
x\geq 0
.
We are going to prove that

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)\geq g_{2}(x)\quad\text{for any }x\geq 1.

Then we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{n}{1+n}\right)^{\varepsilon}\geq 1-\frac{1}{M_{2}N_{2}n^{\varepsilon M_{2}}}\geq 1-\frac{1}{N_{2}n^{\varepsilon M_{2}}},

which implies (
2.6
).

To do this, we first observe

\displaystyle g_{1}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\varepsilon x^{\varepsilon-1}}{(1+x)^{\varepsilon+1}}\quad\text{and}\quad g_{2}^{\prime}(x)=\frac{\varepsilon}{N_{2}x^{\varepsilon M_{2}+1}}.

By taking

(2.7)

\displaystyle\varepsilon<1/(2M_{2}),

we observe that if
x\geq 1
, then
\frac{\varepsilon}{N_{2}x^{\frac{3}{2}}}\leq g_{2}^{\prime}(x)
.
After a few simple computations, we derive

\displaystyle g^{\prime}_{1}(x)\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{N_{2}x^{\frac{3}{2}}}<g^{\prime}_{2}(x)\quad\text{for any }x\geq x_{0}\coloneqq N_{2}^{2}-1.

We next assume

(2.8)

\varepsilon<\log_{2}\left(\frac{M_{2}N_{2}(N_{2}^{2}-1)^{1/2}}{M_{2}N_{2}(N_{2}^{2}-1)^{1/2}-1}\right)

to see that
(1/2)^{\varepsilon}\geq 1-\frac{1}{M_{2}N_{2}x_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}
. Therefore, using the fact that
g_{1}
and
g_{2}
are increasing functions, we obtain that for any
x\in[1,x_{0}]
,

\displaystyle g_{1}(x)\geq g_{1}(1)\geq 1-\frac{1}{M_{2}N_{2}x_{0}^{\frac{1}{2}}}>g_{2}(x_{0})\geq g_{2}(x).

By (
2.8
) and (
2.7
), we fix

\displaystyle\varepsilon\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\frac{1}{2M_{2}},\log_{2}\left(\frac{M_{2}N_{2}(N_{2}^{2}-1)^{1/2}}{M_{2}N_{2}(N_{2}^{2}-1)^{1/2}-1}\right)\right\}

to obtain that
(g_{1}-g_{2})(x)>0
for any
x\in[1,x_{0}]
and
(g_{1}-g_{2})^{\prime}(x)<0
for any
x\geq x_{0}
. In addition, we directly observe
\lim_{x\to\infty}(g_{1}-g_{2})(x)=0
. Therefore, we deduce
g_{1}(x)\geq g_{2}(x)
for any
x\geq 1
, which completes the proof.
∎

Finally, we recall a technical lemma (see
[
DiB93
, Lemma 4.1]
).

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
A_{i}
be a sequence of positive numbers with
A_{i+1}\leq cb^{i}A_{i}^{1+\alpha}
, where
c,b\geq 1
and
\alpha>0
. If
A_{0}\leq c^{-1/\alpha}b^{-1/\alpha^{2}}
, then
A_{i}\to 0
as
i\to\infty
.

3.
Interior Estimates

Fix
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. In this section, we will investigate a bounded weak solution
u\equiv u_{\varepsilon}
to

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u))+\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\text{in }\Omega_{T}.

Let us take
Q_{R,\mathcal{T}}(z_{0})\subset\Omega_{T}
for some
R,\mathcal{T}>0
and
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}
.
We first observe a translation invariant and normalization property of (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
M>0
, let
u
be a weak solution to

\partial_{t}(u+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u))+\mathcal{L}u=0\quad\mbox{in $B_{R}(x_{0})\times(t_{0}-\mathcal{T},t_{0}]$.}

Then for
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
,
\overline{u}(x,t)\coloneqq u(x+x_{0},t+t_{0})/\mathcal{M}
is a weak solution to

\partial_{t}(\overline{u}+\overline{\beta_{\varepsilon}}(\overline{u}))+\overline{\mathcal{L}}\overline{u}=0\quad\mbox{in $B_{R}\times(-\mathcal{T},0]$,}

where
\overline{\beta_{\varepsilon}}(\xi)=\frac{\beta_{\varepsilon}(\mathcal{M}\xi)}{\mathcal{M}}
and

\displaystyle\overline{L}\overline{u}(x)=\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}}\frac{|\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{u}(y,t)|^{p-2}(\overline{u}(x,t)-\overline{u}(y,t))}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\overline{k}(x,y,t)\,dy

with
\mathcal{M}^{p-2}\Lambda^{-1}\leq\overline{k}(x,y,t)\leq\mathcal{M}^{p-2}\Lambda
.

We next give Caccioppoli estimates with truncation.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
u
be a sub(super)-solution to (
3.1
). Let
\varphi
be a nonnegative smooth function satisfying
\varphi(\cdot,t)\equiv 0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{r}(x_{0})
with
r<R
. Then there is a constant
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda)
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{t_{0}-\mathcal{T}<t<t_{0}}\left[\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\varphi^{p}(u-k)_{\pm}^{2}\,dx\pm\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\varphi^{p}\int_{k}^{u}\beta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-k)_{\pm}\,d\xi\,dx\right]

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}-\mathcal{T}}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{|((u-k)_{\pm}\varphi)(x,t)-((u-k)_{\pm}\varphi)(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{t_{0}-\mathcal{T}}^{t_{0}}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{\mp}^{p-1}(y,t)((u-k)_{\pm}\varphi^{p})(x,t)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\leq cR^{p(1-s)}\int_{Q_{R,\mathcal{T}}(z_{0})}(u-k)_{\pm}^{p}|\nabla\varphi|^{p}\,dz+c\int_{Q_{R,\mathcal{T}}(z_{0})}\left((u-k)_{\pm}^{2}\pm\int_{k}^{u}\beta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-k)_{\pm}\,d\xi\right)\partial_{t}\varphi^{p}\,dz

\displaystyle\quad+c\int_{t_{0}-\mathcal{T}}^{t_{0}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus B_{R}(x_{0})}\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})}\frac{(u-k)_{\pm}^{p-1}(y,t)((u-k)_{\pm}\varphi^{p})(x,t)}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dx\,dy\,dt

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{B_{R}(x_{0})\times\{t=t_{0}-\mathcal{T}\}}\left((u-k)_{\pm}^{2}\pm\int_{k}^{u}\beta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-k)_{\pm}\,d\xi\right)\varphi^{p}\,dx,

where
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
.

Proof.

Let us take a test function
(u-k)_{\pm}\varphi^{p}
. Then by using
[
BKU14
, Lemma 2.1]
to control the time term
\partial_{t}(u+\beta_{\varepsilon}(u))
, and by using
[
Lia24b
, Proposition 2.1]
to handle the space term
\mathcal{L}u
, we deduce the desired estimate.
∎

By Lemma
3.2
, we have energy estimates which remove the role of the singularity
\beta(\cdot)
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
k\geq\varepsilon
, we have same energy estimates of
(u-k)_{+}
as in the parabolic fractional p-Laplacian equations. Similarly, for any
k\leq-\varepsilon
, we have same energy estimates of
(u-k)_{-}
as in the parabolic fractional p-Laplacian equations.

Proof.

When
k\geq\varepsilon
or
k\leq-\varepsilon
, then

\int_{k}^{u}\beta_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(\xi)(\xi-k)_{\pm}\,d\xi=0,

since
\beta^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=(\beta\ast\psi^{\prime}_{\varepsilon})(\xi)=0
when
|\xi|\geq\varepsilon
.
Therefore, we have the same energy estimates as in the ones given in the standard parabolic fractional
p
-Laplacian equations (see
[
APT24
,
BK24
,
Lia24b
]
).
∎

With this energy estimate, we will provide several pointwise estimates. Let us fix
\mathcal{Q}\coloneqq B_{R}(x_{0})\times I\subset\Omega_{T}
, where
I\coloneqq(t_{1}-\mathcal{T},t_{1}]
. We also fix constants
\mu_{+},\mu_{-}
and
\omega
as

(3.2)

\displaystyle\mu_{+}\geq\sup_{\mathcal{Q}}u,\,\quad\mu_{-}\leq\inf_{\mathcal{Q}}u,\quad\text{and}\quad\omega\geq\mu_{+}-\mu_{-}.

Let us assume
\rho\leq R
.
Using Lemma
3.2
, we obtain the following De Giorgi type Lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
u
be a locally bounded sub(super)-solution to (
3.1
). Let us fix
\xi\in(0,1/4]
. Suppose

(3.3)

\displaystyle(\rho/R)^{\frac{sp}{p-1}}\mathrm{Tail}((u-\mu_{\pm})_{\pm};\mathcal{Q})\leq\xi\omega

and
Q_{\rho}^{(\theta)}(z_{0})\Subset\mathcal{Q}
, where
\theta=(\xi\omega)^{2-p}
. Then there exists
\nu_{0}=\nu_{0}(n,s,p,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
such that if

(3.4)

\displaystyle|\{Q_{\rho}^{(\theta)}(z_{0})\,:\pm(\mu_{\pm}-u)\leq\xi\omega\}|\leq\nu_{0}\left(\frac{\xi\omega}{\max\{1,\xi\omega\}}\right)^{\frac{n+sp}{sp}}|Q_{\rho}^{(\theta)}(z_{0})|,

then

\pm(\mu_{\pm}-u)\geq\xi\omega/2\quad\text{in }Q_{\rho/2}^{(\theta)}(z_{0}).

Proof.

We may assume
z_{0}=0
by Lemma
3.1
. We first handle the case when
u
is a super-solution.
Let us define sequences

(3.5)

k_{i}\coloneqq\mu_{-}+\frac{\xi\omega}{2}+\frac{\xi\omega}{2^{i+1}},\quad\rho_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\rho}{2}+\frac{\rho}{2^{i+1}}\quad\text{and}\quad\overline{\rho}_{i}\coloneqq\frac{\rho_{i}+\rho_{i+1}}{2}

for any
i\geq 0
. We next define

A_{i}\coloneqq\frac{|\{Q_{\rho_{i}}^{(\theta)}\,:\,u<k_{i}\}|}{|Q_{\rho_{i}}^{(\theta)}|}

to claim that

(3.6)

A_{i+1}\leq cM^{i}\frac{\max\{1,\xi\omega\}^{1+sp/n}}{(\xi\omega)^{1+sp/n}}A_{i}^{1+sp/n}

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p,\Lambda)\geq 1
and
M=M(n,s,p,\Lambda)\geq 1
. To obtain (
3.6
), we first observe from
[
DZZ21
, Lemma 2.3]
that

(3.7)

\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q^{(\theta)}_{\rho_{i+1}}}{(u-{k}_{i})^{p(1+2s/n)}_{-}}\,dz

\displaystyle\leq c\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q^{(\theta)}_{\rho_{i+1}}}\int_{B_{\rho_{i+1}}}\rho_{i}^{sp}\frac{|(u-{k}_{i})_{-}(x,t)-(u-{k}_{i})_{-}(y,t)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,dy\,dz+\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{Q^{(\theta)}_{\rho_{i+1}}}(u-{k}_{i})^{p}_{-}\,dz\right)

\displaystyle\times\left(\sup_{t\in I^{({\theta})}_{\rho_{i+1}}}\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B_{\rho_{i+1}}}(u-{k}_{i})^{2}_{-}\,dx\right)^{\frac{sp}{n}}\eqqcolon J_{1}\times J_{2}

for some constant
c=c(n,s,p)
. We will apply Lemma
3.2
to (
3.7
). To do this, let
us choose a nonnegative cut-off function
\psi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(B_{\overline{\rho}_{i}})
with
\psi\equiv 1
in
B_{\rho_{i+1}}
and
{\lvert\nabla\psi\rvert}\leq c(n)2^{i}/\rho
, and
\phi\in C^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}})
with
\phi\equiv 1
on
t\geq-\rho_{i+1}^{sp}{\theta}
and
\phi\equiv 0
on
t<-\overline{\rho}_{i}^{sp}{\theta}
.
For
\varphi=\psi\phi

sufficiently large.

\displaystyle\rho_{i+1}<r\leq\rho_{i}.

From (

, we observe

are defined preceding (

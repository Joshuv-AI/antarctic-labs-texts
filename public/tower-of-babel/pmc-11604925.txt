Optimizing hardware configuration for solar powered energy management in battery ultracapacitor hybrid electric vehicles
Authors: Shraddha Kaushik, K Rachananjali
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe design and construction of an adaptive energy management system incorporating a 12 V–2 Ah battery and a 1F ultracapacitor for solar powered hybrid electric vehicles are presented in this paper. The primary storage battery’s longevity and overall system efficiency are intended to be increased by the EMS’s ability to forecast driving circumstances and lessen the load on it. The method optimizes power distribution among numerous energy storage sources by using sophisticated hardware configurations. Using Arduino Uno software, the EMS continuously modifies power distribution in response to driving circumstances and vehicle energy needs. To conduct a thorough assessment of the system, a mechanical configuration was created to replicate the vehicle’s dynamics. This included situations in which the vehicle moved only by using its moment of inertia and not the accelerator. Testing under diverse operating conditions, such as braking, acceleration, and varying slopes is made possible by this configuration. According to experimental data, peak battery stress is significantly reduced by about 33.33%, resulting in longer battery life. With minimal energy losses of 4.3–4.9%, the system regularly achieves power efficiency ranging from 95.1 to 95.7%. Furthermore, the suggested hardware-based EMS lowers overall energy loss by roughly 10.36%, highlighting its dependability and effectiveness in contrast to traditional techniques. The longevity and efficiency of the system are enhanced by the balanced power distribution between the battery, ultracapacitor, and solar sources, which prevents any one source from being overloaded. Introduction

The automotive industry has been looking more and more to hybrid electric vehicles as a revolutionary solution to the urgent global challenges of energy sustainability and climate change. By smoothly combining conventional internal combustion engines with electric propulsion systems, hybrid electric vehicles offer significant fuel and emission1 savings over their conventional counterparts. Batteries and ultracapacitors, which are responsible for effectively storing and delivering energy to meet varying propulsion demands and operating conditions, are the key components of these vehicles’ energy storage systems. This research endeavours to enhance the efficiency and longevity of HEV energy management systems through the development and deployment of a sophisticated hardware setup specifically tailored for solar-powered configurations. The primary objective is to optimize the allocation of power among the battery, ultracapacitor, and photovoltaic sources, leveraging advanced hardware configurations and robust control algorithms2. Unlike conventional EMS approaches that often rely on static or rule-based strategies, our study employs adaptive energy management techniques. These techniques utilize real-time data from standardized driving cycles, such as the UDDS, to dynamically predict and adjust power distribution in response to the vehicle’s operational dynamics and environmental conditions.

Central to our approach is the integration of a modified interleaved DC-DC boost converter3, meticulously engineered to reduce converter losses, stabilize the state of charge, and maximize overall energy efficiency, achieving levels up to 98%. This hardware innovation is complemented by a software implementation based on Arduino Uno, providing a flexible and responsive control interface for managing energy flow within the HEV system. To rigorously validate the effectiveness and reliability of our proposed setup, comprehensive testing is conducted using a sophisticated mechanical simulation platform. This platform accurately replicates real-world driving scenarios, including accelerations, decelerations, and traversing diverse terrains and gradients, ensuring robust performance evaluation under various operational conditions. This includes simulating instances where the vehicle moves by inertia without accelerator input, preserving power consumption.

This research greatly advances our knowledge of and ability to use solar-powered EMS in HEVs, which furthers the continuous development of sustainable transportation technology. The study’s conclusions and insights have the potential to improve HEVs’ dependability, environmental effect, and operational efficiency—paving the way for a more sustainable and environmentally friendly future for urban mobility and beyond. This work emphasizes how vital it is to create resilient, adaptable systems that can fulfil the rising global energy demand while minimizing environmental effects through creative energy management and the integration of renewable energy sources.

Literature survey

Hybrid electric vehicle (HEV) technology has advanced significantly as a result of the search for environmentally friendly transportation alternatives. These vehicles offer a viable way to cut pollutants and fuel consumption since they mix internal combustion engines with electric propulsion systems. Complex energy management systems, in conjunction with cutting-edge DC-DC converters and extensive mechanical systems, optimize the interactions between different energy storage components, including batteries, Ultracapacitors, and photovoltaic sources, to make HEVs highly effective.

Because of their high energy density, batteries are the main component of energy storage in hybrid electric vehicles, providing the continuous power required for extended driving. Due to their lower power density, batteries alone, however, frequently are unable to supply peak power needs during acceleration and other high-power activities. By providing extraordinary power density, ultracapacitors4 bridge this gap by supplying the brief energy bursts needed for regenerative braking and fast acceleration. In a hybrid energy storage system (HESS), batteries and UCs work together to maximize the benefits of each component’s capabilities, which include batteries for energy storage and UCs for power delivery. This extends the system’s lifespan and overall performance.

By adding a second renewable energy source and lowering dependency on fossil fuels, the integration of PV panels into HEVs increases the energy efficiency of the vehicle5. Solar energy is transformed into electrical energy via photovoltaic panels, which can then be utilized immediately to power the electrical systems of the vehicle or stored in a battery. For this integration to properly manage the fluctuation of solar energy, however, improved energy management is needed. PV integration is a feasible addition to the hybrid energy storage system since studies have shown that it can greatly cut fuel usage and emissions in HEVs.

In HEVs, DC–DC converters are essential for controlling the flow of power between the batteries, ultracapacitors (UCs), and photovoltaic panels. In this situation, buck-boost converters are very useful since they can adjust the voltage to the desired level, guaranteeing effective use of energy from several sources. Interleaved DC-DC boost converters are unique among them because of their capacity to lower voltage ripple, lessen electromagnetic interference (EMI), and enhance thermal control6. By spreading the load over several channels, these converters improve the energy management system’s overall dependability and efficiency.

A thorough mechanical simulation platform is necessary for the validation and optimization of the EMS in HEVs. This platform has a thorough configuration that mimics the conditions of driving in the actual world, including braking, acceleration, and negotiating a variety of terrains. The simulation incorporates many components, including wheels, brakes, and accelerators, to precisely replicate the dynamic behaviour of a real vehicle. This method enables the EMS to be rigorously tested and fine-tuned, guaranteeing reliable performance under a variety of operational conditions. To conserve energy and prolong battery life, the mechanical system architecture also incorporates instances in which the vehicle travels purely by inertia without the need for accelerator input7.

Set of rules

The suggested EMS’s foundation employs a rule-based approach using straightforward IF–THEN operators explained in Table 1. In this system, the THEN statement executes various modes once the IF statement identifies different operating scenarios. The scenario refers to the condition in which an operational mode is engaged, while the operational mode determines the actual energy source and its distribution across the system’s components33. The effect of IF–THEN rules on the optimization process includes shrinking the search space, which enhances the optimization algorithm’s convergence rate, eliminates unworkable solutions, and improves result quality34. Additionally, the algorithm biases optimization by guiding the search algorithm towards specific areas of the search space to achieve certain objectives, such as maintaining the state of charge (SOCb) above 20% for prolonged battery life or utilizing Ultracapacitors to meet peak pulse load demands.

In order to provide a more effective and long-lasting power management system for hybrid electric vehicles, the suggested EMS combines conventional energy storage devices with renewable energy sources. The fundamental idea is dynamic power allocation, which makes sure that batteries, UCs, and PV panels all use energy as efficiently as possible in response to demand in real time. PV panels produce electricity that is prioritized for urgent load demands; any extra energy is stored in the battery or in UCs. IGBT and relay circuits control the flow of power between components, making effective use of the energy that is available. Rapid power bursts from UCs are available under peak load demands while longer-lasting continuous energy is provided by the battery. In order to increase overall system efficiency, mechanical management systems imitate brake events in which kinetic energy is transformed back into electrical energy and stored.

Our hardware configuration is completely capable of tackling the most important issues with energy storage systems, guaranteeing dependable operation in a variety of scenarios. Adaptive control techniques, which update system parameters in real-time and lessen reliance on static models by continuously assessing critical factors like internal resistance and capacity, handle parameter uncertainty. Lastly, ultracapacitors are used to handle disturbances such as sudden changes in load, as they react swiftly to power variations during events such as acceleration and braking. Through the use of real-time feedback regulation, abrupt variations in power consumption are mitigated, preserving system performance and stability. This all-encompassing method guarantees that the hardware configuration can meet the demands of practical working environments, improving longevity and efficiency.

Our method minimizes deep discharge cycles, which are known to diminish cycle life, and reduces stress on the battery by optimizing energy flow between the battery, ultracapacitor, and solar sources. This allows us to substantiate the gains in battery lifespan. Utilizing the ultracapacitor to reduce peak loads, we ensure that the battery is discharged at reduced rates by applying Peukert’s Law to account for the effect of discharge rates on battery capacity.

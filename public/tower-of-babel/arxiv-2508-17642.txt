1.
Preliminaries

Let
A
be a commutative Noetherian ring, and
let
I\subset A
be an ideal.
An element

is said to be integral over

if there exists a
monic polynomial

f(X)=X^{n}+c_{1}X^{n-1}+\cdots+c_{n}\;\;(c_{i}\in I^{i},\,i=1,2,\ldots,n)

such that

.
The ideal of all elements that are integral over

, denoted by
\overline{I}
.
If
\overline{I}=I
, then

is said to be

.
An ideal
I
is called

if all powers of

are integrally closed.

In what follows, let
(A,\mathfrak{m})
be an excellent two-dimensional
normal local domain containing an algebraically closed field

k=A/\mathfrak{m}
, where
\mathfrak{m}
denotes the unique maximal ideal of
A
,
and
I\subset A
an
\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal.
A divisor on a resolution space whose support is contained
in the exceptional set is called a cycle.
Then in our situation, there exist a resolution of singularities

f\colon X\to\Spec A
and an anti-nef cycle
Z
on
X
such that

I\mathcal{O}_{X}=\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z)
and
I=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(-Z))
.
Then we say that
I
is
represented
by
Z
on
X
,
and denote it by
I=I_{Z}
. Note that
\overline{I^{k}}=I_{kZ}
.

1.1.
Riemann-Roch formula and normal reduction numbers

Definition 1.1

(cf.
[
OWY4
, Definition 2.1]
)
.

For
I=I_{Z}
, we define

Then
q(kI):=q(\overline{I^{k}})
is a non-negative integer for every

, and

p_{g}(A):=q(0I)\geq q(I)\geq q(2I)\geq q(3I)\geq\cdots\geq 0.

We put
q(\infty I):=q(kI)
for sufficiently large

.

Moreover,

is called the

of
A
.
Note that

’s are independent of the choice of the resolution.

The following formula was proved in
[
Ka
]
in the complex case, but it holds in any characteristic
[
WY
]
.

Theorem 1.2

(
Kato’s Riemann Roch formula
)
.

Assume
I=I_{Z}
. Let
K_{X}
denote the canonical divisor on
X
.
Then we have

\ell_{A}(A/I)+q(I)=\chi(Z)+p_{g}(A),\;\text{where}\;\;\chi(Z)=-\dfrac{Z^{2}+K_{X}Z}{2}

For any ideal
I
, let
Q
be its minimal reduction.
Then we put

r_{Q}(I)=\min\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\,|\,I^{r+1}=QI^{r}\}.

In general,

depends on the choice of

. So
we define

r(I)=\min\{r_{Q}(I)\,|\,\text{$Q$ is a minimal reduction of $I$}\}.

Then

is called a

of

.
We now recall the definition of two normal reduction numbers.
By virtue of
[
Hun
, Lemma 4.1]
,
\nr(I)
and
\br(I)
are independent on the choice of
Q
.

Definition 1.3

(cf.
[
OWY4
, Definition 2.5]
)
.

For
I=I_{Z}
and its minimal reduction
Q
,
we put

\displaystyle\nr(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\,|\,\overline{I^{r+1}}=Q\overline{I^{r}}\},

\displaystyle\br(I)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\min\{r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\,|\,\overline{I^{n+1}}=Q\overline{I^{n}}\;\text{for all $n\geq r$}\}.

A positive integer

(resp.

(resp. the

The following relationship is very important in our theory.

(cf.

.

For

1.2.
Normal tangent cones

Let us recall the definition of normal tangent cones, which is the main
target in this paper.

Definition 1.6

(
Normal Tangent Cone
)
.

For any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal
I\subset A
,

\overline{G}(I)=\bigoplus_{n\geq 0}\overline{I^{n}}/\overline{I^{n+1}}

is called the
normal tangent cone
of
I
.

For an ideal
I
of
A
,

G(I)=\oplus_{n\geq 0}I^{n}/I^{n+1}

is called the
associated graded ring
or the
tangent cone
of
I
.
We notice that
\overline{G}(I)
is
not
an integral closure of
G(I)
even if
A
is a normal domain and
I
is integrally closed.
However, if we consider the extended Rees algebra

\mathcal{R}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{R}^{\prime}(I)=A[It,t^{-1}]=\bigoplus_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}I^{n}t^{n}\subset A[t,t^{-1}]

and its integral closure
\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}

in its fraction field, then

G(I)\cong\mathcal{R}^{\prime}/t^{-1}\mathcal{R}^{\prime},\qquad\overline{G}(I)\cong\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}/t^{-1}\overline{\mathcal{R}^{\prime}}.

In Section 2, we give an answer to this question in our context.

Question 1.7
.

Put
I=I_{Z}\subset A
. When is
\overline{G}(I)
Gorenstein?

Valabrega-Valla type theorem yields a criterion for
\overline{G}(I)

to be Cohen-Macaulay.

Theorem 1.8

(cf.
[
HuMa
,
VV
]
)
.

Let
A
,
I=I_{Z}
be as above.
Then

(1)

\overline{G}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay if and only if
Q\cap\overline{I^{n}}=Q\overline{I^{n-1}}
holds for some minimal reduction
Q
of
I
and for all
n\geq 2
.

(2)

For any minimal reduction
Q
of
I
, we have
Q\cap\overline{I^{2}}=QI
(Huneke-Itoh).

(3)

If
\overline{G}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay, then
\nr(I)=\br(I)
.

Note that
\nr(I)=1
does not necessarily imply
the Cohen-Macaulayness of
\overline{G}(I)
.

1.3.
Geometric ideals and singularities

We recall the notion of
p_{g}
-ideals and elliptic ideals,
which was introduced by the

[

]

[

Definition 1.9

(

-ideal
)
.

Assume that
I=I_{Z}
.
Then
I
is called the
p_{g}
-ideal
if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(1)

\br(I)=1
.

(2)

q(I)=p_{g}(A)
.

(3)

I
is normal and
I^{2}=QI
for some
minimal reduction
Q
of
I
.

(4)

\overline{G}:=\overline{G}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay and

, where

a(\overline{G})=\max\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\,|\,[H^{2}_{\mathfrak{M}}(\overline{G})]_{n}\neq 0\}
, the
a
-invariant

of

, and
\mathfrak{M}
is the maximal homogeneous ideal.

Definition 1.10

(
Elliptic ideal
)
.

An ideal
I=I_{Z}
is called the
elliptic ideal

if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

(1)

\br(I)=2
.

(2)

q(I)=q(\infty I)<p_{g}(A)
.

(3)

\overline{G}:=\overline{G}(I)
is Cohen-Macaulay and
a(\overline{G})=0
.

A normal local domain

is called a

if

p_{g}(A)=0
.
Lipman’s theorem says that any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal

in a rational singularity is a
p_{g}
-ideal.

A normal local domain
A
is called an
elliptic singularity

if
\chi(D)\geq 0
for all cycles

and
\chi(F)=0
for some cycle

; see Wagreich
[
Wa
, pp.428]
.
Okuma
[
Ok
]
showed that
\br(I)\leq 2
for any

.
In fact, any
\mathfrak{m}
-primary integrally closed ideal
I
in an
elliptic singularity is either a
p_{g}
-ideal or an elliptic ideal.

A normal local domain
A
is called a
strongly elliptic singularity

if
p_{g}(A)=1
.
Any non-
p_{g}
-ideal

in a strongly elliptic singularity is a

,
that is, it is an elliptic ideal with

Theorem 2.1

\ell_{A}(A/I)=\ell_{A}(\overline{I^{r}}/Q\overline{I^{r-1}})\leq p_{g}(A)+2-r

We put

If we put

\displaystyle\overline{G}(I)/(a^{*},b^{*})\overline{G}(I)

Lemma 2.4

For every

Intelligent carbon price prediction system based on improved secondary decomposition and multi-head attention bidirectional long short-term memory (BiLSTM) model
Authors: Yongming Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Carbon price forecasting (CPF) plays a critical role in maintaining the stability of carbon financial markets and promoting global cooperation on emission reduction. However, carbon price series often exhibit significant volatility and nonlinearity, making high-precision forecasting extremely challenging. In particular, after decomposing the original sequence, effectively eliminating pseudo-information interference and accurately extracting potential features remain key difficulties. To address these issues, this paper proposes a hybrid forecasting model based on improved secondary decomposition (ISD) and a bidirectional long short-term memory network integrated with a Multi-Head attention mechanism (MAM-BiLSTM). In the decomposition stage, two decomposition methods are combined with an adaptive frequency and energy threshold method (AFETM) to improve the precision of high-frequency information extraction. In the prediction stage, the MAM optimizes the temporal feature weighting of BiLSTM, thereby enhancing the model’s ability to capture complex temporal patterns. Meanwhile, the grey wolf optimizer (GWO) is employed to adaptively tune the key parameters of both variational mode decomposition (VMD) and BiLSTM, further improving the model’s generalization performance. Empirical results based on three representative carbon market datasets demonstrate that the proposed model outperforms eleven baseline models. For example, in terms of mean absolute percentage error (MAPE), the forecasting accuracy is improved by 1.314%, 3.630%, and 7.818% compared with the best-performing baselines. These results confirm the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed model and provide strong support for carbon market risk assessment and policy-making.

Introduction

Background

As one of the important tools for the global response to climate change, the carbon market has received widespread attention in recent years. The carbon trading market encourages companies to reduce carbon emissions and meet energy-saving targets by setting carbon emission quotas and conducting market transactions. In Asia, the growth of carbon finance is accelerating, but its volatility and uncertainty have brought challenges to effective supervision and decision-making. How to better predict the dynamic changes of carbon financial data has become a highly valued issue for carbon market participants1. It can not only offer a scientific basis for the government to formulate carbon emission reduction policies, but also serve as a reference for the decision-making of enterprises and investors in the carbon trading market. By improving the accuracy of predictions, it can help market participants better understand the fluctuation trend of carbon prices, thereby reducing risks and optimizing investment returns2.

Literature review

The carbon trading system is a complex system with multiple factors interacting and changing dynamically. Its operation is not only driven by the economy and policies, but also closely related to multiple factors such as energy price fluctuations, showing nonlinear and non-stationary characteristics. In order to reveal these complex characteristics, researchers have explored the dynamic changes in the carbon market from different perspectives. In the relevant literature, a core focus is to explore how to increase the precision of carbon market predictions and enhance the explanatory power of model results by optimizing the forecasting method. At present, carbon prices forecasting (CPF) mainly adopts three technical paths: one is the econometric model based on traditional theory, including autoregressive integrated moving average (ARIMA)3, generalized autoregressive conditional heteroskedasticity (GARCH)4,5 and vector autoregressive (VAR)6. The second is the artificial intelligence model (AIM) with the advantages of big data and algorithms, including machine learning algorithms (MLA) represented by logistic regression (LR)7, support vector machine (SVM)8, and deep learning models (DLM) represented by recurrent neural network (RNN), multi-layer perceptrons (MLP)9 and long short-term memory (LSTM)10. The third is an innovative hybrid forecasting model that combines the strengths of various algorithms.

Traditional econometric models

Among these approaches, traditional econometric models have been widely applied in the early stages of carbon price forecasting, and they still serve as a fundamental baseline for subsequent methodological improvements. Bishal et al.11 developed and compared the prediction accuracy of various linear and nonlinear regression models, as well as ARIMA models, and found that the ARIMA (1, 1, 0) model yielded the highest accuracy among all the models created for the specific study sequence. Li et al.12 introduced differential technology based on the ARIMA model to solve non-stationary problems. Although there were certain limitations in estimating parameters, the prediction accuracy was still significantly improved. Liu et al.13 employed the GARCH model to examine the persistence, risk and asymmetry in the fluctuations of carbon trading prices return. They pointed out that each carbon trading pilot exhibited varying degrees of fluctuation aggregation characteristics, and the fluctuations were persistent for a long time. Chang et al.14 applied VAR model to explore the causal link between economic progress and carbon dioxide emissions. The findings revealed a bidirectional causal relationship between the two, and there was evidence supporting the idea that primary energy consumption had a substantial impact on economic development. Although traditional econometric models provide a solid foundation, their strict mathematical assumptions limit their accuracy and robustness when dealing with non-stationary and nonlinear data, which has motivated researchers to explore more flexible and data-driven approaches.

Artificial intelligence models

Against this background, artificial intelligence models (AIM) have gradually attracted increasing attention. These models can be broadly categorized into two types: machine learning algorithms (MLA) and deep learning models (DLM). With their capabilities in nonlinear mapping, feature extraction, and high-dimensional information processing, AIM demonstrate significant advantages in capturing the nonlinear dynamics and complex patterns of carbon price fluctuations, thereby achieving higher accuracy and stability in carbon price forecasting15. Shou16 utilized MLA and advanced data mining technology to build the enterprise carbon performance assessment indicator framework. He found that the precision of the SVM-based model was the highest, reaching 97.5%, which was higher than KNN, RF, decision tree (DT) and LR. Yan et al.17 adopted XGBoost to build a prediction model, and by accepting multiple feature values as input, a better model could be fitted. Daniel and Miguel18 also pointed out that prediction tools based on AIM could provide accurate and reliable predictions, especially neural networks (NN), which have been widely used and have become one of the preferred tools. Li et al.19 constructed the quantum RNN by interleaving quantum recurrent blocks (QRB), achieving the highest accuracy in sequential learning compared to the classic RNN and the state-of-the-art quantum neural networks. Volodymyr20 compared the prediction performance of extreme learning machine (ELM) and RNN in financial market prediction tasks. The study found that the single-layer feed forward neural network model trained by ELM outperformed the RNN in prediction, and this effectiveness was generally confirmed. Seo et al.21 introduced a streamlined I-ELM algorithm that initially incorporated linear regression nodes and then applied a technique to ensure that the hidden nodes exhibited characteristics distinct from the existing patterns. Empirical data confirmed this approach could autonomously determine the structure of the NN and achieve accelerated learning. Despite their strong nonlinear fitting capability and flexibility in handling large-scale and non-stationary data, AIM still have some inherent shortcomings and limitations. First, their adaptability to changes in data distribution is relatively weak. For instance, MLA generally assumes that training and test data follow the same distribution, and model performance drops sharply once this assumption is violated. Second, their generalization ability is often insufficient, which can lead to overfitting and poor performance in unseen scenarios.

Hybrid forecasting models

To overcome these limitations, an increasing number of studies have adopted multi-algorithm hybrid frameworks, which integrate the strengths of different models to improve forecasting accuracy and robustness. Guo et al.22 pointed out that conventional models frequently fail to attain high prediction accuracy. They focused on developing an intelligent optimization framework that surpassed traditional MLA, presenting a more adaptable and efficient strategy for process enhancement. According to experimental evaluations, the performance of the hybrid framework showed significant improvement. Peng et al.23 proposed that the genetic algorithm-backpropagation (GA-BP) model was a high-accuracy model and had been successful in carbon emission prediction in the construction industry. To avoid the shortcomings of manual parameter adjustment, Tang et al.24 introduced particle swarm optimization (PSO) to adjust the number of hidden layer neurons, the number of iterations and the batch size of gated recurrent unit (GRU). The results showed that, in terms of mean absolute error (MAE), the PSO-GRU model achieved a 10.47% higher prediction accuracy compared to the GRU model. Meng et al.25 introduced a hybrid framework for carbon prices prediction utilizing LSTM, applying data from the Hubei carbon trading market to forecast carbon trading prices. The findings demonstrated that the hybrid model was 2 times more accurate than the traditional LSTM model, and far more accurate than the ARIMA model. Wang26 pointed out that the attention mechanism (AM) made preprocessing more adaptable and efficient compared to manual methods. Wang et al.27 also emphasized that AM can capture complex features from nonlinear and non-stationary data adaptively.

Furthermore, the forecasting model based on secondary decomposition (SD) is becoming increasingly popular. This approach improves prediction accuracy and model robustness by decomposing time series into multiple easier-to-handle components. It not only overcomes the shortcomings of traditional models in dealing with nonlinear data, but its flexibility and adaptability have also been verified in different fields28,29. Wu et al.30 successfully verified the advantages of the decomposition integration framework in handing nonlinear and unstable data through the ensemble empirical mode decomposition - least squares SVM (EEMD-LSSVM) model. Compared with the baseline models, the root means square error (RMSE), MAE and mean absolute percentage error (MAPE) of the algorithm were 0.618, 0.473, and 1.634, respectively, which performed best. To address the technical challenges of mode mixing and significant reconstruction error in traditional empirical mode decomposition (EMD) for CPF, Yun et al.31 proposed a model that integrated complete ensemble empirical mode decomposition with adaptive noise (CEEMDAN) and LSTM model. It combined the strengths of CEEMDAN in decomposing multi-scale carbon prices signals and the advantages of LSTM model in fitting financial data. The results showed that the model had significant accuracy in predicting complex carbon prices signals. Zhang and Chen32 demonstrated through experimental results that the two-stage model based on variational mode decomposition (VMD) outperformed other competing models.

Research gaps and innovations

Although existing hybrid prediction frameworks have made significant progress in integrating multiple algorithms, there are still some shortcomings and limitations in practical applications. Lack of diversification in feature extraction. In current research, the extraction of carbon market data is mostly limited to the primary level, making it difficult to fully mine the potential useful information contained in the data.Ineffective feature selection. In many decomposition and integration frameworks, there is a lack of a systematic feature screening process, which may result in redundant variables in the input features, reducing the efficiency of the decomposition model. The model structure has limited ability to capture key information. The current prediction models, such as LSTM or traditional time series models, have limited ability to capture key information in time series and are difficult to fully model complex time series relationships. The parameter tuning process lacks intelligence. Traditional parameter tuning often relies on manual adjustment or simple grid search methods, which are inefficient and difficult to find the global optimal solution.

Building on the previous discussion, this paper introduces a novel framework for forecasting carbon market transaction prices, which combines improved secondary decomposition (ISD), including CEEMDAN and VMD, along with the bidirectional LSTM (BiLSTM) model, and integrates the AM on its basis to confer greater computational weight to key information. Furthermore, the grey wolf optimizer (GWO) algorithm is utilized to optimize the important parameters of VMD and the predictive model respectively, avoiding the inefficiency of manual parameter adjustment and the problem of simple optimization algorithms falling into local optimality. Construction and optimization of prediction model. Construct a BiLSTM model for the prediction of carbon financial data. The model is optimized using the AM to enhance the allocation of weights to key data. Adaptive parameter optimization. Empirical study

Data description

This study selects data from three representative carbon emissions trading markets in China—HBCI, GZCI, and SZCI—due to their significant differences in regional characteristics, representativeness, and market maturity. Specifically, Hubei, as an inland region with a strong heavy industrial base, reflects the trading behavior driven by policy incentives in high-emission industries; Guangzhou, as a coastal and export-oriented economic region, demonstrates higher market activity and trading liquidity; while Shenzhen, the first pilot carbon trading market in China, exhibits a relatively mature trading mechanism and a strong price signaling function. Together, they form representative samples that reflect the diversified pattern of China’s carbon trading system.

All data are obtained from the official websites of China’s carbon trading platforms, and their basic information is summarized in Table 1. Specifically, the HBCI and GZCI datasets cover the period from early January 2015 to October 31, 2024, with daily sampling frequencies and sample sizes of 2,386 and 2,384, respectively. The SZCI dataset, which started earlier, spans from June 18, 2013, to October 31, 2024, also with a daily sampling frequency and a total of 2,724 samples.

After data collection, missing values were repaired using interpolation methods to ensure temporal continuity, and abnormal values were identified and treated based on the 3σ rule to enhance data stability and reliability. In addition, several key predictive feature indicators were constructed, including the average transaction price, daily trading volume, daily trading value, cumulative trading volume, and cumulative trading value, to further improve the accuracy of carbon price forecasting.

Secondary decomposition

This paper uses CEEMDAN to perform initial decomposition of different carbon price datasets. Compared with EMD and EEMD, CEEMDAN can flexibly and adaptively adjust the decomposition process according to the characteristics of the data. Moreover, it effectively mitigates mode aliasing and more precisely distinguishes different frequency components. In the empirical experiments, the number of resampling iterations for CEEMDAN was set to 100, and the noise amplitude was fixed at 0.2. This configuration balances decomposition accuracy and computational efficiency: a moderate number of resampling iterations effectively reduces the randomness of the decomposition results, avoiding unstable IMF components caused by too few iterations, while also preventing unnecessary computational burden due to excessive iterations. The chosen noise amplitude helps suppress mode aliasing while minimizing additional disturbances. Under this parameter setting, the carbon price data of HBCI, GZCI, and SZCI were decomposed into 9, 8, and 9 IMFs, respectively, and their waveforms are shown on the left side of Figs. 4 and 5, and Fig. 6. From the figure, it is evident that with the progression of decomposition, the frequency of the IMF gradually decreases, and the amplitude gradually increases, especially the last IMF, which shows a long-term trend component and can reflect the overall trend of the signal.

By observing the different IMFs obtained by CEEMDAN, we can also find that the early IMFs show higher frequencies, which means that they may contain more noise components. If all IMFs are directly included in the subsequent prediction model, the noise implicit in these IMFs may impair the model’s performance and influence the prediction accuracy.

To address this issue, this paper innovatively introduces the AFETM to screen the IMFs. The detailed implementation process of AFETM is presented in Table 3, whose core idea lies in a “dual-indicator–dual-threshold” decision mechanism designed to extract IMFs with significant high-frequency characteristics for SD. Specifically, AFETM calculates the MF and ER for each IMF obtained from the CEEMDAN. In the frequency dimension, based on the statistical characteristics of the IMF frequency distribution, the 95th percentile is adaptively used as the high-frequency threshold to define the upper bound of frequency distribution, effectively identifying high-frequency disturbance components. In the energy dimension, a 5% energy ratio threshold is introduced to enhance filtering, since IMFs contributing less than 5% of the total signal energy generally carry limited useful information and are more likely to represent pseudo-modes. In summary, an IMF is recognized as a high-frequency effective IMF only when its MF exceeds the high-frequency threshold and its ER surpasses the preset threshold. These selected IMFs are then aggregated and input into VMD for SD to extract more stable and detailed features. Table 4 lists the specific high-frequency levels, total energy, and the final selected high-frequency IMF of the three market datasets under this criterion.

After obtaining the high-frequency IMF components, this study further applied VMD for SD and employed the GWO to perform global optimization of model parameters. The performance of VMD mainly depends on two key parameters: the penalty factor (alpha) and the number of modes (K). Specifically, alpha controls the smoothness and constraint strength of each mode during decomposition; if alpha is set too low, mode mixing may occur, whereas an excessively high alpha may result in over-smoothing. To achieve a balance between stability and information fidelity, the range of alpha was set to range from 10 to 4000. Meanwhile, K determines the number of decomposed modes—too small a value may fail to capture key information from the price series, while too large a value may introduce redundant modes and increase computational complexity. Therefore, the search range of K was limited to 1–15 to accommodate the structural diversity of different market datasets. In addition, the minimization of reconstruction error was adopted as the fitness criterion to determine the optimal parameter combinations. The optimal parameter sets for the three markets are summarized in Table 5. Subsequently, the obtained optimal parameters were used to perform SD on the high-frequency components, and the results are shown on the right side of Figs. 4 and 5, and Fig. 6, respectively.

Prediction model construction

Based on the SD results, this paper combines other features collected in the early stage to jointly build a hybrid prediction model. The reconstructed subsequences effectively capture both high-frequency and low-frequency characteristics of carbon price fluctuations, while the inclusion of external features further enhances the model’s ability to depict dynamic market changes. In addition, considering that MAM can dynamically assign the importance weights of features, this paper introduces a BiLSTM model based on the 4-layer AM to enhance the ability to capture key information.

Similar to VMD, this paper incorporated GWO into BiLSTM to overcome the subjectivity and inefficiency of manual parameter adjustment. Specifically, GWO was employed to optimize and adjust the learning rate and dropout parameters in the prediction model. The reason for selecting these two parameters was that the learning rate influenced both the convergence speed and stability of model training, while the dropout rate affected the regularization effect of the model, which helped in balancing the model’s ability to generalize and the potential for overfitting. During the optimization process, RMSE was adopted as an objective function. After 50 rounds of iterations, the optimal parameter sets of the three markets were displayed in Table 6.

In addition, the remaining parameters of the model were set as follows: the hidden layer dimension was 128, the batch size was 64, and the maximum number of training epochs was 100. The Adam optimizer was adopted, and an early stopping mechanism was introduced to prevent overfitting. The entire model was implemented within the PyTorch framework.

Comparison with the baseline models

In the empirical research of different markets, the intelligent prediction model based on SD and optimization algorithm presented in this paper has the best prediction accuracy. Tables 7, 8 and 9 present more detailed results. Taking MAPE as an example, the results of the three carbon trading markets were 0.515, 0.531, and 1.274, which are all lower than the baseline models. Especially in the Guangzhou market, the hybrid model is improved by 72.67% compared with the single LSTM model.

In addition, the prediction effect of the hybrid model has also been proven to be higher than that of the single model. For example, in the Hubei market, the performance of Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM on four evaluation indicators is 0.311, 0.558, 0.531 and 1.183, which is lower than the traditional LSTM model and the BiLSTM model with a bidirectional structure.

The incorporation of the intelligent optimization algorithm further enhances the model’s generalization ability. Taking the Guangzhou market as an example, GWO-BiLSTM improves 48.50%, 28.27%, 37.13%, and 30.89% on different indicators compared with BiLSTM. This improvement has also been verified in the other two markets. At the same time, GWO-Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM also showed significant advantages over Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM. Similarly, the introduction of CEEMDAN plays a positive role in improving the prediction performance of the model. Taking the Shenzhen market as an illustration, the results of CEEMDAN-Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM on the four metrics were 0.335, 0.579, 0.474, and 1.530, respectively. Compared with the Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM model, the absolute values are improved by 0.302, 0.219, 0.202, and 1.351, respectively, and similar improvements are observed in other markets. Moreover, the SD technique serves as another effective strategy for enhancing forecasting precision. Taking the MAPE indicator as an example, compared with GWO-Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM, the forecasting accuracy of the hybrid model introduced in this paper in different markets was improved by 16.67%, 36.64% and 41.77% respectively, indicating the important role of the SD method in improving prediction accuracy and model robustness.

To further verify the universality and scalability of the proposed model, additional comparative experiments were conducted in No. 8–11 using several improved decomposition models and transformer-based algorithms. From the decomposition perspective, CEEMDAN-WT-GWO-Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM and EEMD-VMD-GWO-Multi-Head Attention BiLSTM exhibit feature extraction capability but remain inferior in overall forecasting precision. For example, in the Guangzhou market, the MAPE value of the proposed model decreased from 0.551 to 0.531 compared with the latter, and in the Shenzhen market, it dropped from 1.417 to 1.274, representing an approximate 10% reduction in error. These findings highlight the stronger adaptability of the ISD framework in multi-scale signal decomposition.

Compared with recent state-of-the-art algorithms, including DA-FEDformer-ICEEMDAN37 and Optimal Multi-scale Ensemble Transformer38, the proposed hybrid model also exhibits superior accuracy and robustness. For instance, in the Hubei market, the MSE of the Optimal Multi-scale Ensemble Transformer is 0.093, while that of ISD-GWO-MAM BiLSTM further decreases to 0.084; in the Shenzhen market, the MAPE values are 1.382 and 1.274, respectively, a reduction of 7.81%. Overall, although transformer models possess strong theoretical sequence modeling capability, the integrated model proposed in this study achieves better empirical performance in terms of stability and generalization.

Statistical significance and robustness verification

After completing the model performance comparison and ablation experiments, it is necessary to further verify whether the obtained performance improvement is statistically significant. To this end, this section introduced two complementary statistical test methods: the paired t-test and the Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The former is based on parametric assumptions and is mainly used to evaluate the significance of mean differences in prediction errors among different models; the latter is a non-parametric method that compares the rank distributions of sample errors to verify the robustness of the results under non-normal conditions. The smaller p-value obtained from the two tests was taken as the integrated significance criterion to ensure the robustness and reliability of the test results.

Figures 10 and 11 present the statistical comparison results between the proposed model and various baseline models across different markets. The horizontal axis represents the mean difference of absolute errors, and the red dashed line denotes the zero-difference line. When the points are located to the left of the zero line, the proposed model yields smaller prediction errors; when they are close to the zero line, the two models exhibit similar performance; and when they are on the right, the baseline model performs better. The horizontal error bars represent the 95% confidence intervals. In addition, the asterisk marks indicate the levels of significance: “**” denotes significance at the 1% level, and “*” denotes significance at the 5% level. From these two graphs, it can be observed that most of the baseline models were located on the left side of dashed line, and the p-value were less than 0.05. This indicates that the proposed model is statistically superior to the other models. These results further confirm the significance and consistency of the model’s performance improvement, demonstrating that the proposed model possesses higher stability and generalization capability across different markets.

Conclusion

To improve the forecasting precision of carbon trading prices, this paper proposes a novel model that combines ISD and BiLSTM with MAM, leveraging the advantages of each model to improve the final prediction performance. According to the empirical findings, the primary conclusions are outlined below: The improved SD framework simultaneously captures both high-frequency and low-frequency characteristics of carbon price fluctuations. Compared with a single decomposition method, it more effectively reveals the nonlinear structures and multi-scale dynamics embedded in the data. The AFETM adaptively determines the validity of high-frequency components based on the MF and ER; this mechanism facilitates precise extraction of short-term fluctuation information, thereby improving decomposition stability and prediction accuracy. The GWO-based parameter optimization dynamically adjusts the key hyperparameters of the model to achieve the optimal parameter combination, thus enhancing the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed model. The MAM dynamically allocates the importance of features, emphasizing key temporal patterns and correlated characteristics, which effectively strengthens the model’s representational ability and stability.Compared with other baseline models, the proposed model demonstrates significant improvements in both predictive performance and generalization capability. The results of ablation experiments and statistical significance tests confirm that each module contributes substantially to the overall model performance, fully reflecting the stability and reproducibility of the proposed model.

Knowledge, attitude and practice toward fibromyalgia among fibromyalgia patients: A web-based cross-sectional study in China
Authors: Dongfeng Liang, Xiaojie Guo, Jie Zhang, Zhengyuan Hu, Ronghuan Jiang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThis study aimed to explore the knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) of fibromyalgia patients toward fibromyalgia.MethodsThis web-based cross-sectional study was conducted in China between February and March 2023 among fibromyalgia patients using a self-administered questionnaire.ResultsA total of 401 valid questionnaires were enrolled, including 326 (81.30%) females and with a mean age of 42.42 ± 11.78 years. The mean scores for KAP were 7.14 ± 2.72 (possible range: 0–10), 27.31 ± 4.24 (possible range: 8–40) and 28.08 ± 5.55 (possible range: 10–50), respectively. Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed knowledge (OR = 1.09, 95%CI: 1.04–1.15, P = 0.001) and patient global impression (PGI) on average pain severity in the last week (OR = 0.89, 95%CI: 0.81–0.97, P = 0.011) were independently associated with positive attitude. Attitude (OR = 1.08, 95%CI: 1.02–1.14, P = 0.006), initial consultation in orthopedics (OR = 2.15, 95%CI: 1.24–3.73, P = 0.006) and age (OR = 0.98, 95%CI: 0.96–1.00, P = 0.037) were independently associated with proactive practice. The structural equation model demonstrated that knowledge had a positive effect on attitude (β = 0.21, P = 0.006), and Widespread Pain Index (WPI) exhibited an impact on both knowledge (β = -0.04, P < 0.001) and attitude (β = -0.52, P = 0.001).ConclusionFibromyalgia patients showed suboptimal knowledge, moderate attitude and inactive practice toward fibromyalgia. To enhance fibromyalgia patient outcomes, recommendations include targeted education, addressing rural-urban disparities in knowledge, emphasizing early diagnosis, and adopting patient-centered approaches to promote positive attitude and better disease management.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-024-21076-6.Keywords: knowledge, attitude, practice, fibromyalgia, questionnaire, cross-sectional study

Background

Fibromyalgia is a prevalent and complex chronic musculoskeletal pain syndrome characterized by widespread pain, fatigue, sleep disturbances, and frequently co-occurring symptoms such as depression, anxiety, body perception distortion, and cognitive dysfunction [1]. Studies show that around 50% of FM patients have dysfunctions in large Aβ fibers and thin Aδ and C fibers, which manifest as those symptoms. As a result, there may be an underlying neuropathic condition in a significant subset of fibromyalgia patients [2]. The prevalence of this condition varies across populations, with a notable predilection for affecting women [3, 4]. The diverse range of symptoms significantly impacts patients’ quality of life, leading to both physical and psychosocial disabilities that have wide-ranging repercussions on family, employment, and independence. Additionally, individuals with fibromyalgia often exhibit intolerance to physical activity, resulting in a sedentary lifestyle that increases the risk of additional morbidity. The multifaceted nature of fibromyalgia underscores the urgency for comprehensive research and patient-centered management approaches to address the complexities and challenges it presents [5, 6].

“The diagnostic approach to fibromyalgia has evolved significantly over the years. Early proponents, such as Yunis and Wolff, emphasized the need for objective criteria, like the tender point exam, as a means of providing a more standardized diagnosis (Maugars et al., 2021). However, more recent perspectives have highlighted the importance of incorporating patients’ subjective experiences, particularly psychological factors such as catastrophizing, into the diagnostic process. This shift acknowledges the complexity and heterogeneity within the fibromyalgia patient population, where some individuals may exhibit an identifiable physical pathology, while others are more appropriately characterized by psychological components of the disorder (Loevinger et al., 2012)”.

Fibromyalgia, a condition impacting a considerable number of individuals worldwide, presents considerable challenges owing to its enigmatic etiology, which contributes to the absence of a definitive cure [7]. As a consequence, management approaches mainly encompass a combination of pharmacological treatments, physical therapy, and lifestyle modifications. Among the pharmacotherapeutic options are amitriptyline, duloxetine, milnacipran, tramadol, pregabalin, and cyclobenzaprine, as endorsed by esteemed medical societies [8]. Despite these treatment efforts, current therapies fall short in providing complete relief from all fibromyalgia symptoms [9, 10].

The knowledge, attitude and practice (KAP) survey method provide a structured approach to explore patients’ awareness, beliefs, and behaviors regarding their health condition [11]. However, studies investigating the KAP of fibromyalgia patients are scarce, leaving an incomplete understanding of their perspectives and experiences with the condition. Existing research tends to focus on clinical aspects and objective measurements, overlooking the crucial insights that patients themselves can offer. Studies have revealed that both Chinese rheumatologists and Canadian physicians have exhibited limited awareness and knowledge about fibromyalgia [12, 13]. Similarly, primary healthcare physicians in Tabuk City, Saudi Arabia [14], showed inadequate familiarity with fibromyalgia diagnostic criteria and treatment strategies. Moreover, a study conducted in Norway highlighted the widespread use of dietary supplements among individuals with fibromyalgia, emphasizing the need to enhance health literacy and informed decision-making regarding supplement intake [15]. The limited research on KAP in fibromyalgia management hinders the development of patient-centered approaches, which could be vital in enhancing treatment adherence and overall care. Therefore, this study aimed to explore the KAP of fibromyalgia patients toward fibromyalgia.

Methods

Study design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted online via WeChat groups managed by the Fibromyalgia Patients Association of China, between February and March 2023. Participants were adults aged 18 years or older, diagnosed with fibromyalgia, and voluntarily participated by completing an online questionnaire. The study was ethically approved by the Ethics Committee of the author’s Hospital, and informed consent was obtained from the study participants. On the other hand, this study was conducted in accordance with the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology (STROBE) guidelines (https://www.strobe-statement.org) to ensure comprehensive and transparent reporting of observational studies. As this was a web-based survey, we adhered to the Checklist for Reporting Results of Internet E-Surveys (CHERRIES) guidelines (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC1550605/) to maintain the quality and reliability of the survey data collection and reporting process. The study was ethically approved by the Ethics Committee of Chinese PLA General Hospital (approval NO: S2022-503-02).

Questionnaire design

The questionnaire was developed following the Italian Society for Rheumatology clinical practice guidelines [16], and reviewed by experts in rheumatology, psychology, pain, and statistics. A pre-test with 35 participants confirmed its reliability (Cronbach’s α = 0.750). The initial version of the questionnaire was refined to make it as easy as possible for patients to understand. This involved deleting similar or repetitive questions and adjusting and clarifying questions that were not clearly expressed. For example, the question “1. “The degree of your knowledge about the treatment of fibromyalgia” was changed to “Fibromyalgia is a common chronic disease that is often combined with multiple symptoms such as fatigue, sleep, and mood disorders, in addition to pain in multiple sites.” Additionally, common misconceptions among patients were included, such as “fibromyalgia is inflammation of the ‘fibers’ or ‘muscles’” and “fibromyalgia is the result of cold exposure after childbirth.”

The final questionnaire, written in Chinese (a version translated into English was attached as an Appendix), comprised four distinct sections: (1) Demographic Characteristics: This section included gender, age, and other relevant factors. It also incorporated the “Widespread Pain Index (WPI),” “Symptom Severity Scale (SSS),” “Patient Global Impression on average pain severity in the last week (PGI pain),” and “Patient Global Impression on severity of the disease (PGI severity).” PGI pain was scored from no pain (1) to very severe pain (10), and PGI severity was scored from no effects (1) to very severe effects (10). (2) Knowledge Dimension: This section consisted of 10 questions about fibromyalgia. Correct answers were scored as 1, while wrong or unclear answers were scored as 0. (3) Attitude Dimension: This section included 8 questions, each using a five-point Likert scale ranging from very positive (5 points) to very negative (1 point). (4) Practice Dimension: This section consisted of 14 questions, with questions 3–12 also using a five-point Likert scale ranging from always (5 points) to never (1 point). Higher scores in these sections indicated adequate knowledge, more positive attitudes, and more proactive practices. A score of more than 80% of the total score was considered good, 60-80% was considered moderate, and less than 60% was considered poor [17].

The neuroimmunomodulation therapies mentioned in the questionnaires referred specifically to transcranial magnetic stimulation (TMS) and transcranial direct current stimulation (tDCS). These non-invasive techniques are used to modulate neural activity and are being explored as potential therapeutic approaches for managing symptoms associated with fibromyalgia [18].

Questionnaire distribution and quality control

The study was conducted in collaboration with the Fibromyalgia Patients Association of China, a civil society organization. The successful distribution of the questionnaire was ensured through close contact with the head of the association to gain their support and cooperation. In the early stages of communication with the association, the purpose and significance of the study, as well as the content and process of the survey, were explained in detail. The voluntary aspects and anonymity of the respondents was emphasized to ensure that they could answer the questions with confidence and without any external pressure or influence. Furthermore, a commitment was made to strictly adhere to the relevant ethical principles, regulations, and laws to protect the privacy of the patients and the security of the data. Privacy was ensured through the following measures: (1) Respondents completed the electronic questionnaire anonymously. (2) Only the research team had access to the questionnaire responses. (3) The collected data was kept confidential and will not be disclosed or shared with any third parties. The staff of the Chinese Fibromyalgia Patients’ Association, who manage the WeChat groups, distributed QR codes of the electronic questionnaire to 10 WeChat groups, encompassing a total of 1725 patients. To encourage participation, a reminder notice was sent once a week in each group. The questionnaire remained open for four weeks, after which the submission function was closed. Compared to traditional questionnaire distribution in hospitals, this unique approach from a non-traditional source may have avoided the potential bias caused by the stress experienced by patients in hospitals settings. Additionally, it helped prevent over-distribution in certain departments, which could introduce significant bias in responses to specific questions, such as which department is most frequently consulted. The staff of the Chinese fibromyalgia patients’ association were actively involved in assisting the posting of the questionnaire link or QR code to the WeChat group so that patients could easily access and fill in the questionnaire by scan the QR code. To minimize bias, each Internet Protocol (IP) address was restricted to one submission, and all responses were mandatory to ensure completeness. Questionnaires with logical errors or uniform responses were excluded. Anonymized data are available upon request from the principal investigator.

Statistical analysis

The statistical analysis software was Stata 17.0 (Stata Corporation, College Station, TX, USA). Continuous variables were described using mean ± standard deviation (SD), and comparisons between groups were performed using t-test or Analysis of Variance (ANOVA). Categorical indicators were described using n (%). Logistic regression was used to conduct univariate and multivariate analyses of KAP, with all of the scores using 60% of the total score as the cut-off value (6 for knowledge, 24 for attitude and 30 for practice). The inclusion of multivariate regression variables was screened by the application of lasso regression, and the model was constructed based on the least selected variable for Bayesian information criteria (BIC). Structural equation modeling (SEM) tested hypotheses regarding knowledge, attitude, and practice relationships. The hypotheses posited that both WPI and SSS have an influence on " PGI pain,” “PGI severity,” and participants’ knowledge and attitude. Additionally, it was hypothesized that " PGI pain” and " PGI severity” directly affect knowledge and attitude. Furthermore, structural equation modeling (SEM) was employed to test the following hypotheses: (1) knowledge had impacts on attitude; (2) knowledge had impacts on practice; (3) attitude had impacts on practice. Two-sided P < 0.05 were considered significant different.

Results

Demographic characteristics

Of the 1725 questionnaires distributed, 414 were returned. After excluding ineligible participants (e.g., under 18 years old), 401 valid questionnaires were included in the analysis (effective response rate: 96.86%). Among the valid questionnaires, the majority were female (n = 326, 81.30%), with a mean age of 42.42 ± 11.78 years and resided in urban areas (n = 293, 73.07%). When diagnosed with fibromyalgia, 46.88% of patients received the diagnosis within 12 months, and the Rheumatology department was the primary specialist group of doctors who diagnosed fibromyalgia (80.30%) (Table 1). The PGI pain and PGI severity scores were 5.14 ± 2.45 and 5.72 ± 2.64, respectively (Supplementary Table 1).

Knowledge, attitude and practice

Mean scores for knowledge, attitude, and practice were 7.14 ± 2.72 (possible range: 0–10), 27.31 ± 4.24 (possible range: 8–40) and 28.08 ± 5.55 (possible range: 10–50), respectively. The type of residence, education, occupation, and the department / specialized group for initial consultation were found to have statistically significant differences in the practice scores (all P < 0.05).

Moreover, the type of residence, education, occupation, and the department / specialized group for initial consultation and childbirth status (for women) showed a statistically significant difference in the knowledge scores (all P < 0.05) (Table 1).

In knowledge dimension, the majority of respondents acknowledged that fibromyalgia involves multiple symptoms such as pain, fatigue, sleep and mood disorders (n = 358, 89.28%). Additionally, 64.84% of patients recognized the dysregulation of pain in the central nervous system as a primary cause of fibromyalgia. However, there were misconceptions, with 54.36% could not correctly distinguish the relationship between fibromyalgia and inflammation or muscle fibers. Similarly, 53.37% of participants could not correctly understand whether fibromyalgia was the result of exposure to cold and wind after childbirth. On a positive note, 85.54% acknowledged the impact of psychological factors and stress on fibromyalgia incidence. The study also revealed the diagnostic challenge, as 74.06% recognized the absence of specific laboratory indicators or imaging abnormalities, making symptom-based diagnosis crucial. The findings emphasized the importance of a multimodal treatment approach (76.06%) focusing on symptom relief, regular follow-up (78.05%), and active interventions like exercise, medication, and disease management (79.8%) for improved outcomes. Notably, 74.06% acknowledged the effectiveness of moderate exercise as a foundational component in fibromyalgia treatment (Table 2).

In terms of attitude, a significant proportion of patients expressed skepticism about the efficacy of all kinds of treatments for fibromyalgia (55.86%) and the perceived ineffectiveness of non-pharmacological treatments (56.36%). Conversely, a considerable number of respondents exhibited trust in their attending doctors and believed in the effectiveness of the treatment plans provided (69.58%). However, concerns about side effects were prevalent, particularly concerning western medicine (57.36%) and antidepressant / anxiety medications (61.09%). Encouragingly, a majority expressed willingness to undergo non-pharmacological treatments in addition to medication (85.04%). Nevertheless, there was reluctance to engage in exercise therapy due to fear of exacerbating pain (62.84%). On a positive note, a significant number of patients felt confident in their ability to maintain a balanced psychology and cope with stress and anxiety (70.58%) (Table 2).

As for practice, access to taking oral medication as prescribed by the doctor was relatively high (86.28%), and 59.09% patients were willingness to take oral medication varied, with 22.73% strongly willing, 18.18% willing and 18.18% neutral willing. Fibromyalgia health education showed high access (58.89%), and most (84.48%) of the patients expressed willingness to access it. Regarding exercise, the performance of exercise was relatively high (55.88%), and a significant percentage (55.55%) expressed willingness to engage in it. Psychological treatment had relatively high access (52.97%), and a substantial number of patients (41.93%) expressed willingness to undergo it. Mindfulness therapy and acupuncture / needling had moderate access (53.79% and 62.11%, respectively) and willingness (33.45% and 39.16%, respectively). Tui Na / massage and bath therapy had moderate access (63.51% and 54.54%, respectively) and willingness (44.66% and 38.74%, respectively). Neuromodulation therapy and hyperbaric oxygen therapy had limited access (54% and 36.84%, respectively), and willingness (34.47% and 29.06%) to undergo these therapies varied widely among patients (Table 2). Supplementary Table 2 displays treatment and medication information, indicating that 84% of respondents sought treatment for their condition in the Rheumatology department, with a preference for traditional medicines such as pregabalin and duloxetine. Notably, 40.4% of respondents chose traditional Chinese medicine. The order of drug efficacy from high to low was duloxetine, pregabalin, sedative hypnotic drugs and so on. Most perceived their treatment as effective; 13.47% patients had no response to the used drugs or could not tolerate the side effects.

Multivariate logistic regression analysis

Multivariate logistic regression analysis showed that rural [odd ratio (OR) = 0.36, 95%CI: 0.19–0.69, P = 0.002)], age (OR = 0.97, 95%CI: 0.95–0.99, P = 0.002), PGI severity (OR = 0.88, 95%CI: 0.80–0.96, P = 0.006), diagnosed with fibromyalgia for > 12 months (OR = 1.93, 95%CI: 1.21–3.08, P = 0.006), high school (OR = 0.41, 95%CI: 0.22–0.77, P = 0.005) and junior college (OR = 0.51, 95%CI: 0.27–0.96, P = 0.038) were independently associated with adequate knowledge. Knowledge (OR = 1.09, 95%CI: 1.04–1.15, P = 0.001) and PGI pain (OR = 0.89, 95%CI: 0.81–0.97, P = 0.011) were independently associated with positive attitude. Attitude (OR = 1.08, 95%CI: 1.02–1.14, P = 0.006), initial consultation in orthopedics (OR = 2.15, 95%CI: 1.24–3.73, P = 0.006) and age (OR = 0.98, 95%CI: 0.96–1.00, P = 0.037) were independently associated with proactive practice (Table 3).

Discussion

Main findings

Our study explored the KAP towards fibromyalgia among patients in China. The key findings highlighted suboptimal knowledge, moderate attitudes, and inactive practices in managing fibromyalgia. Additionally, factors such as residence, education, and initial consultation department significantly influenced KAP scores. To optimize fibromyalgia patient outcomes, it is recommended to implement targeted education programs, address rural-urban disparities, emphasize the importance of early diagnosis, and adopt patient-centered approaches to foster a positive attitude and improve disease management.

Comparison with evidence

Fibromyalgia, a prevalent disorder observed frequently in primary care settings, affects a substantial portion of the population in Europe, Asia, and North America. The prevalence rates of fibromyalgia have been reported to vary, with studies indicating rates around 2% in Europe, Asia, and North America. For instance, Queiroz (2013) highlights a prevalence of 2.0-2.7% in the developed world population [19]. Notably, the incidence of fibromyalgia increases with age, reaching its highest point in individuals aged above 60 years. Furthermore, the condition exhibits a notable gender disparity, with women being approximately seven times more likely to be affected by fibromyalgia than men [4, 19, 20]. Thus, building upon the demographic aspects of fibromyalgia, it is crucial to delve into the potential factors contributing to the observed gender disparity.

Regarding knowledge, it was observed that residing in rural areas was inversely associated with a higher level of understanding about fibromyalgia. This observation suggests potential disparities in access to information and healthcare resources between urban and rural regions. The results of the study are consistent with existing literature that highlights the impact of geographical location on knowledge acquisition and health-related awareness [21]. Such disparities in knowledge could have significant implications for fibromyalgia patients, as accurate information is crucial for making informed decisions about treatment options and adopting effective self-management strategies. Moreover, age exhibited a negative association with knowledge, suggesting that younger patients may have a better grasp of the condition. Age exerted a negative influence on the likelihood of possessing adequate knowledge and engaging in proactive practice, with older individuals being somewhat less inclined to have sufficient knowledge and participate in proactive behaviors compared to their younger counterparts. However, this effect was relatively modest. These findings underscore the importance of targeted educational interventions for fibromyalgia patients residing in rural areas and older populations to improve their knowledge and, subsequently, their disease management.

Another noteworthy finding of the study pertains to the association between the duration of fibromyalgia diagnosis and patients’ knowledge. Patients diagnosed for over 12 months exhibited significantly higher levels of understanding compared to those with more recent diagnoses. This compelling insight emphasizes the critical need for healthcare providers to intensify educational efforts targeted at newly diagnosed patients, ensuring a comprehensive understanding of the condition from the outset. Numerous studies have demonstrated that newly diagnosed patients often seek regular consultations and readily available support to navigate their condition effectively. By offering comprehensive education and support during this crucial period, healthcare providers can empower patients to engage in increased self-management, a key aspect associated with improving the quality of life for individuals with chronic illnesses [22, 23].

Furthermore, the study also indicates that patients with higher education, such as undergraduate and postgraduate and above, demonstrated superior knowledge about fibromyalgia. Higher educational attainment plays a pivotal role in enhancing health literacy and fostering positive health behaviors, thereby positively influencing both physical and mental health outcomes. Research has consistently shown that individuals with higher levels of education are better equipped to access, understand, and apply health-related information, leading to improved decision-making regarding their well-being [24]. This reinforces the significance of educational attainment in promoting health literacy and emphasizes the importance of providing accessible and informative resources to patients with varying educational backgrounds. Shifting focus to attitude, the study revealed a negative correlation between the PGI pain and their attitude. Plausibly, heightened pain levels may contribute to increased frustration and adversely affect patients’ perspectives on managing their condition. Regarding practice, attitude emerged as a crucial factor influencing patient behavior. Patients with more positive attitude were more likely to engage in active practice, including adhering to prescribed treatments and seeking various non-pharmacological therapies. These findings align with the KAP theory, underscoring the pivotal role of psychological factors in shaping patient behavior [25]. This highlights the significant role of psychological factors in shaping patient behavior, demonstrating that fostering positive attitude can positively impact adherence to treatment plans and enhance overall patient outcomes.

Additionally, the presence of other comorbidities such as depression, anxiety, and disturbances in sleep and memory further compounds the symptoms experienced by individuals with fibromyalgia [4, 26]. Given these interconnected factors, healthcare providers must address pain management effectively to cultivate more optimistic attitude among fibromyalgia patients. By prioritizing comprehensive pain management strategies, healthcare professionals can potentially improve patients’ outlook on their condition and enhance their overall well-being.

Furthermore, the study revealed that orthopedics, as the department or specialist group involved in the initial fibromyalgia diagnosis, was independently associated with patient practice. Initial consultation for orthopedics of patients were more willing to accept hyperbaric oxygen therapy, acupuncture / needling and mindfulness therapy. This finding suggests that the choice of the diagnosing department may influence the subsequent management and treatment approaches recommended to patients. However, the specific reasons remain to be further explored. It is essential for healthcare providers to coordinate closely, regardless of their specialty, to ensure consistent and evidence-based fibromyalgia management across different medical disciplines.

It is noteworthy that fibromyalgia, being a chronic pain condition, encompasses a wide range of manifestations, including emotional distress, fatigue, sleep disorders, depression, catastrophizing thinking related to pain, and cognitive compromise [27]. The structural equation model provided additional insights into the interrelationships among knowledge, attitude, and disease severity. The model indicated that higher knowledge levels were positively linked to more favorable attitude. In turn, increased fibromyalgia severity, as measured by the WPI scores, was inversely related to knowledge and attitude. These findings suggest a complex interplay between disease severity and patient perspectives, emphasizing the importance of personalized and holistic approaches to fibromyalgia management.

In our survey, one item addressed the belief that exposure to ‘cold and wind’ could trigger fibromyalgia, a view common in traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) but less familiar in Western contexts. While cold temperatures are often thought to worsen pain, in Chinese culture, these environmental factors are believed to cause musculoskeletal conditions like fibromyalgia. This reflects a culturally unique interpretation of pain etiology, contrasting with Western views that typically link fibromyalgia with conditions like chronic fatigue syndrome. Therefore, it seems that cultural perspectives shape the understanding and management of fibromyalgia [28], emphasizing the need for culturally sensitive approaches in healthcare. Notably, 40.4% of the respondents reported using TCM as an adjunctive treatment for fibromyalgia, highlighting its significant role in managing symptoms within Chinese healthcare practices. This reliance on TCM reflects the cultural inclination towards holistic and natural remedies, including practices like acupuncture, herbal medicine, and cupping.

Implications

Our study highlights several key implications for clinical practice and research. In clinical practice, there is a clear need for enhanced educational initiatives to improve patients’ knowledge about fibromyalgia, emphasizing patient-centered care approaches to build trust and address concerns empathetically. Multidisciplinary management plans combining pharmacological and non-pharmacological treatments are essential to improve patient outcomes. Addressing socioeconomic disparities is crucial to ensure equitable healthcare access and outcomes. For research, future studies should focus on longitudinal designs to establish causal relationships, develop and validate English versions of questionnaires for broader comparative research, evaluate the effectiveness of educational programs and intervention strategies, and explore how socioeconomic and cultural factors influence patients’ knowledge, attitudes, and practices. These steps will help improve the overall management of fibromyalgia and enhance patient outcomes.

Limitations

This study has some limitations. Firstly, the study’s cross-sectional design restricts causal inference and temporal understanding, preventing the establishment of definitive relationships between variables and impeding the assessment of changes over time. Secondly, reliance on self-reported measures introduces recall and social desirability biases, as patients may respond based on memory or perceived social norms. Thirdly, the presence of unmeasured confounding variables could influence the observed associations between knowledge, attitude, and practice. Fourth, age at time of diagnosis and length of living with pain was not specifically addressed in the analysis of self-reported knowledge about the disorder. Fifth, the lack of long-term follow-up data hinders the examination of changes in KAP over time among fibromyalgia patients. Sixth, our questionnaire did not account for potential comorbidities in patients diagnosed with fibromyalgia, aside from the exclusion of individuals with obesity. Consequently, we were unable to examine the impact of other conditions, such as diabetes or autoimmune diseases, on our findings. Future studies should incorporate an assessment of comorbidities to better understand their potential influence on fibromyalgia symptoms and outcomes. Another limitation of this study is the absence of verification regarding the diagnostic criteria used by different specialists (rheumatologists and orthopedists) for diagnosing fibromyalgia within our patient population. Our original questionnaire design did not include measures to assess potential variations in diagnostic approaches between these clinical specialties. Future research should address this aspect to ensure consistency in diagnostic criteria across specialties, as such variations may influence both the characterization of fibromyalgia and the generalizability of findings. Moreover, our study did not assess whether the type of physician making the fibromyalgia diagnosis influenced patients’ knowledge and beliefs about the condition. This aspect was not captured in our original questionnaire design, preventing us from analyzing potential variations based on the diagnosing physician’s specialty. Future research should consider examining this factor to gain a deeper understanding of how different medical perspectives may shape patient perceptions of fibromyalgia. Lastly, this study lacked an assessment regarding early life events, such as childhood trauma, which has been shown in many studies to be associated with the development of fibromyalgia in adulthood. Research conducted in European and American populations suggests that up to 50% of women with fibromyalgia have a history of childhood sexual maltreatment [29]. Although our survey did not inquire about early life events, future studies should address this important aspect to provide a more comprehensive understanding of the factors contributing to fibromyalgia onset. Addressing these limitations in future research would enhance the study’s validity and contribute to a more comprehensive understanding of fibromyalgia patients’ experiences and behaviors.

Conclusion

In conclusion, among fibromyalgia patients, the study revealed suboptimal knowledge, moderate attitude, and inactive practice toward the condition. While the results are based on a Chinese population, they may have implications for broader contexts with similar healthcare settings. Recommendations for improvement include targeted education, addressing rural-urban disparities, emphasizing early diagnosis, and adopting patient-centered approaches to promote positive attitude and enhance disease management outcomes.

Breast ultrasound images for segmentation and classification using multi-task U-Net
Authors: Maddhi Anitha, Ch Rajendra Prasad, Joseph Bamidele Awotunde, Abidemi Emmanuel Adeniyi, Oluwasegun Julius Aroba
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBreast ultrasound imaging is widely used for the early detection of breast cancer due to its accessibility and effectiveness, particularly in dense breast tissues. However, its diagnostic performance is often affected by operator dependency, speckle noise, low contrast, and variability in data quality. Although deep learning methods have shown promise in automated tumor segmentation and classification, their clinical applicability remains limited due to challenges such as small and imbalanced datasets, inconsistent annotations, and the lack of integrated learning strategies. In this work, we propose a Multi-Task U-Net framework that jointly performs lesion segmentation and tumor classification by leveraging shared feature representations. The proposed method incorporates a deterministic oversampling strategy for handling class imbalance, a prediction-refinement module to ensure consistency between segmentation and classification outputs, and an attention-guided feature learning mechanism to enhance lesion localization. Additionally, a curated version of the BUSI dataset is constructed by removing duplicate and inconsistent samples to ensure reliable evaluation. The proposed model achieves a Dice score of up to 0.81 in comparative evaluation, along with classification accuracy of up to 0.96–0.98, demonstrating improved performance over baseline methods. The consistent performance across both segmentation and classification tasks indicates good generalization capability despite dataset limitations. Finally, the proposed multi-task framework provides an effective and reliable solution for automated breast cancer detection in ultrasound images and shows strong potential for clinical application.Keywords: Breast cancer, Ultrasound imaging, Multi-task learning, Image segmentation, Tumor classification, U-Net, Medical image analysisSubject terms: Cancer, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Health care, Mathematics and computing

In this paper, we propose a multi-task prediction-refining framework for simultaneous breast cancer segmentation and classification. Unlike conventional single-task approaches10, the proposed method effectively leverages the inherent relationship between segmentation and classification tasks to improve overall predictive performance. The framework consists of multiple interconnected modules designed to refine and enhance predictions while maintaining experimental fairness. A convolutional neural network (CNN) serves as the core component of the framework; however, unlike previous studies that rely on fixed architectures, our approach provides flexibility in selecting different backbone models11.

Furthermore, many existing studies on automated medical image segmentation12 primarily focus on tumor-containing images and often exclude normal (non-tumor) cases. Although this simplifies the segmentation task, it requires manual intervention to identify and remove non-tumorous images, which may lead to inefficiencies and unnecessary clinical procedures. To address this limitation, the proposed framework incorporates a prediction-refining module that ensures consistency while effectively handling both tumor and non-tumor cases. This enables the development of a fully automated system that is more suitable for real-world clinical applications. In addition, the widespread use of publicly available datasets has significantly advanced research in machine learning and computer vision. However, inconsistencies within these datasets can negatively affect the reliability and validity of experimental results13. To overcome this issue, we propose a systematic dataset refinement strategy aimed at identifying inaccurate segmentations and problematic samples. Furthermore, we provide a curated version of a publicly available dataset to support fair and consistent evaluation in future studies. To address challenges associated with limited dataset size and the risk of overfitting, the proposed framework integrates deterministic sampling, multi-task regularization, and dataset refinement techniques to enhance model generalization and robustness.

Although U-Net architectures, multi-task learning, and attention mechanisms have been widely explored in medical image analysis, the novelty of this work lies in the task-specific integration and methodological enhancements designed for breast ultrasound imaging. In particular, we introduce a deterministic oversampling strategy that replaces conventional random sampling to ensure balanced, stable, and reproducible training in imbalanced datasets. Furthermore, we propose a prediction-refinement mechanism that enforces cross-task consistency between segmentation and classification outputs, which is not addressed in standard multi-task frameworks. The architecture also incorporates attention-guided feature learning tailored to ultrasound characteristics, enabling improved localization of lesion regions under noisy and low-contrast conditions. In addition, we contribute a curated and consistency-verified version of the BUSI dataset, addressing duplication and annotation issues that can negatively impact model reliability. These components collectively form a robust and clinically relevant framework that extends beyond conventional implementations.

The selection of the U-Net architecture is motivated by its proven effectiveness in medical image segmentation, particularly in scenarios involving limited and noisy datasets. In contrast to traditional machine learning approaches that depend on handcrafted features, U-Net enables end-to-end learning of spatial and contextual information. Moreover, extending U-Net into a multi-task framework allows simultaneous optimization of segmentation and classification, enabling the model to capture complementary information and improve overall diagnostic performance. This makes the proposed approach both efficient and well-suited for breast ultrasound imaging.

Problem statement

The importance of early and accurate diagnosis through Ultrasound is great so that survival rates can be high in breast cancer that is one of the leading causes of death in women. Despite better Multi Task U-Net, the existing deep learning techniques are afflicted with grave issues. Traditionally ML pick local patterns without regard to long-range correlations that are needed to identify minor lesions. Multi-Task U-Net is commonly used to capture global feature representations; however, it requires large amounts of data and computational resources, which may limit its clinical applicability. Context dependency models such as CNN are sequential models, which do not imply complex contextual-spatial interactions. Moreover, the majority of such approaches lack the means to focus on the areas of diagnostic interest, and manual hyperparameter optimization is likely to perform poorly. Thus, one of the most pressing needs is to apply Multi Task U-Net in Deep Learning Model, to actualize potent and generalizable Ultrasound Image segmentation and classification.

Methodology

The multi-task U-Net architecture is adopted due to its ability to effectively learn both local and global features through its encoder–decoder structure. Its flexibility allows integration of additional modules such as attention mechanisms and prediction refinement, making it a suitable backbone for the proposed framework. We propose in this paper a deep learning architecture to detect breast cancer in Ultrasound scans, by relying on the Multi Task U-Net architecture. The deterministic oversampling module is used to address class imbalance and ensure stable and reproducible training to repeatedly and consistently sample training patches to achieve tasks of segmentation and classification. The use of fixed sampling requirements instead of randomness increases convergence, equalises the classes, and stabilizes the learning process, and is able to make a fair comparison of multitask performance during training and testing and extracted. Multi Task U-Net manipulates representations to boost the relationships of classification and initial segregation to enhance the differences between the benign patterns of malignant tissues. It is also supplemented with a mechanism of attention that optimizes the diagnostically significant areas of the dense breast tissue and minimizes the background irrelevant parts of the image to increase the interpretability and localization. The proposed framework integrates deterministic oversampling, attention mechanisms, and prediction refinement to enhance both segmentation and classification performance. Dataset description

The Breast Ultrasound Images dataset is used in this study. Sample images from the dataset are represents in Fig. 2, including normal, benign, and malignant cases, which represent different manifestations of breast cancer. The dataset initially consists of 1578 images along with their corresponding masks32,33]. For this paper, normal images are excluded from the classification task to focus specifically on distinguishing between benign and malignant cases. This decision enhances diagnostic relevance and improves the model’s performance in clinically significant scenarios. The proposed Multi-Task U-Net model leverages both segmentation and classification capabilities, which are particularly effective in capturing the complex and subtle features present in cancerous tissues.

Sample from breast ultrasound images. (a) Normal (b) Benign ultrasound images. (c) Malignant ultrasound images.

Since normal images typically do not contain distinct tumor-related features, including them in the classification task may reduce the model’s ability to accurately differentiate between benign and malignant cases. Therefore, the model is trained using only these two categories to achieve higher diagnostic accuracy and support early detection and treatment.

The normal images are excluded from the classification training process, they are still utilized during dataset analysis and pre-processing to maintain dataset integrity. In addition, normal samples are included during evaluation to assess the model’s ability to correctly identify non-tumorous cases and to validate its performance in a more realistic clinical scenario.

Dataset curation and duplicate removal

Figure 3 represents to ensure data integrity and avoid redundancy; a dataset curation step was performed to identify and remove duplicate images. Duplicate samples can lead to biased learning and overestimated performance, especially in medical imaging datasets. A similarity-based approach was adopted using the Structural Similarity Index Measure (SSIM) to compare image pairs. SSIM evaluates similarity based on luminance, contrast, and structural information, making it suitable for detecting visually similar ultrasound images34. Two images were considered duplicates if their SSIM score exceeded a predefined threshold of 0.95. During this process, a total of 30 duplicate images were identified and removed from the dataset. This resulted in a refined dataset that ensures fair training and evaluation without data leakage between splits. The curated dataset was subsequently used for all experiments in this paper.

Using this procedure, approximately 30 duplicate images were identified and removed, resulting in a refined dataset used for all subsequent experiments.

Experimental setup

The dataset was divided into training (70%), validation (15%), and testing (15%) subsets, ensuring a balanced distribution of benign, malignant, and normal samples across all splits. To ensure reproducibility, a fixed random seed (seed = 42) was used for dataset partitioning, model initialization, and all stochastic operations. The backbone architecture used in this study is a standard U-Net encoder–decoder structure, which serves as the base network for the proposed multi-task learning framework. Although the architecture is flexible, all reported results are obtained using this fixed backbone to maintain consistency and fairness in evaluation. This standardized experimental setup allows fair comparison and improves the reproducibility of the proposed approach.

Cross-validation strategy

To improve the robustness of the evaluation, a 5-fold cross-validation strategy was employed. The dataset was partitioned into five equal subsets, where in each iteration, four folds were used for training and one-fold for testing. The final performance metrics are reported as the average across all folds, reducing bias associated with a single data split.

Deterministic oversampling

Class imbalance is a common challenge in medical imaging datasets, particularly in breast ultrasound images where the number of samples across classes (normal, benign, and malignant) is often uneven. Traditional random oversampling techniques35 address this issue by randomly duplicating minority class samples; however, such approaches introduce stochastic variability, which may affect model stability and reproducibility, especially in small datasets.

To address this limitation, a deterministic oversampling strategy is proposed to ensure consistent and reproducible class balancing. Overfitting mitigation strategies

Due to the limited size of the BUSI dataset, specific measures were adopted to reduce overfitting. The proposed deterministic oversampling ensures balanced and consistent training without introducing stochastic bias. Furthermore, the multi-task learning framework acts as an implicit regularize by enforcing shared feature learning across segmentation and classification tasks41. The attention mechanism further improves generalization by focusing on relevant lesion regions and suppressing background noise. Additionally, dataset curation, including duplicate removal, was performed to avoid data leakage and biased learning. These strategies collectively enhance the robustness and generalization capability of the proposed model.

Results

The proposed Multi-Task U-Net framework was evaluated on the curated BUSI breast ultrasound dataset for simultaneous tumor segmentation and classification. The experimental results demonstrate that joint learning of these tasks improves both segmentation accuracy and classification performance compared to conventional single-task approaches. Segmentation performance was assessed using the Dice coefficient and Intersection over Union (IoU), while classification performance was evaluated using accuracy and F1-score. The model achieves consistent performance across both tasks, indicating effective learning of shared representations. The predicted segmentation masks show strong agreement with expert annotations, particularly in challenging cases characterized by low contrast and irregular tumor boundaries, which are common in ultrasound imaging. The classification results further validate the effectiveness of the shared encoder and task-specific decoding branches, achieving high accuracy and F1-scores for both benign and malignant cases.

The incorporation of the prediction-refinement module reduces false positives and ensures consistency between segmentation and classification outputs, which is essential for clinical reliability. Furthermore, the training and validation curves exhibit stable convergence behavior, with a gradual decrease in loss and continuous improvement in evaluation metrics over training epochs. The minimal gap.

between training and validation performance indicates controlled learning without significant overfitting. Consistent performance across both segmentation and classification tasks suggests that the model generalizes well despite the limited dataset size. Additionally, the individual segmentation and classification losses were monitored during training to ensure balanced optimization of both tasks. All reported results are obtained under the defined experimental setup, and where applicable, represent averaged performance to ensure reliability and consistency.

Performance metrics evaluation

The effectiveness of the proposed Multi-Task U-Net framework was assessed using well-established evaluation metrics tailored to both segmentation and classification objectives. For the segmentation component, the Dice Coefficient and Intersection over Union (IoU) were employed to quantify the degree of overlap between predicted segmentation masks and the corresponding ground-truth annotations. The Dice Coefficient, in particular, is a widely adopted similarity measure in medical image analysis, ranging from 0 to 1, where a value closer to 1 signifies a higher level of agreement between predicted and actual regions.

For the classification task, model performance was evaluated using accuracy and F1-score. Accuracy reflects the proportion of correctly predicted instances among all samples, providing a general measure of model correctness. However, in medical applications where class imbalance is common, accuracy alone may not be sufficient. Therefore, the F1-score was also considered, as it harmonizes precision and recall into a single metric, offering a more balanced assessment of the model’s predictive capability, especially in detecting minority classes.

The experimental evaluation is conducted on benign and malignant classes, while normal images are not included in the classification performance metrics.

This small variation between training and validation results suggests consistent learning and indicates that the model generalizes reasonably well without significant overfitting.

The proposed Multi-Task U-Net framework performs both tumor segmentation and classification on breast ultrasound images using a shared encoder and task-specific decoders. The shared encoder extracts discriminative and spatially rich features, reducing redundancy and improving generalization across tasks. The segmentation branch employs an upsampling decoder to accurately delineate lesion boundaries, while the classification branch utilizes globally aggregated features for robust prediction. This joint optimization enables segmentation-guided classification, where accurate localization of lesions directly enhances diagnostic performance.

The proposed approach achieves a favourable balance between performance and architectural simplicity compared to existing state-of-the-art models. It requires lower computational overhead than attention-based or capsule-based architectures while maintaining competitive Dice and accuracy scores. Although some existing models report higher Dice scores, they are often evaluated on tumor-only datasets or focus solely on segmentation. In contrast, the proposed model performs multi-task learning on a more diverse dataset that includes normal, benign, and malignant cases. This increases task complexity but significantly improves the clinical relevance and practical applicability of the system, making it suitable for real-time medical imaging applications.

Ablation study

To assess the contribution of individual components within the proposed framework, an ablation study was conducted by systematically modifying the architecture and evaluating the performance of each configuration. The objective is to analyze the impact of multi-task learning, prediction refinement, and deterministic oversampling on overall model performance. The first configuration considers a single-task segmentation model, where only the segmentation branch is retained, and the classification component is removed. The second configuration evaluates a single-task classification model, in which only the classification branch is used without segmentation guidance. To understand the effect of joint learning, a multi-task model without the prediction-refinement module is evaluated, where segmentation and classification are performed simultaneously but without enforcing consistency between tasks.

Additionally, a configuration without deterministic oversampling is analyzed to examine the impact of class imbalance handling on model performance. Finally, the proposed full model, which integrates multi-task learning, prediction refinement, and deterministic oversampling, is evaluated to demonstrate the combined effectiveness of all components. Conclusion

This paper introduces a powerful multi-task deep learning model that relates to automated segmentation and classification of breast tumors in ultrasound images. The proposed Multi-Task U-Net can learn better features, achieve better lesion boundary delineation, and higher diagnostic accuracy using the inherent correlation between segmentation and classification tasks. The deterministic oversampling, dataset cleaning of irregularities, and prediction refining modules also add additional reliability to the models and their ability to be applied in clinical practice. The proposed approach has a good balance between performance and architectural simplicity as compared to the existing ones and therefore it is applicable in real-time computer-aided diagnosis systems. The proposed model achieves a Dice score of 0.81 on the test dataset and accuracy 0.96–0.98% prove that such a direction as multi-task learning has potential to produce powerful and generalized ultrasound-based breast cancer screening devices. The curated dataset split and pre-processing details are described in the manuscript and can be made available upon reasonable request to support reproducibility. Future work will focus on extending the evaluation by comparing the proposed framework with advanced architectures such as nnU-Net, DeepLabv3+, and transformer-based models to further validate its effectiveness and work will include extensive evaluation using cross-validation and multiple backbone architectures to further enhance reproducibility and robustness. Future work will extend the model to include normal cases for full multi-class classification.

Future scope

Further study is to validate the suggested framework against larger multi-centre clinical datasets will be discussed in the future to test the generalizability of the imaging devices and patient population. The integration of new attention systems or transformer-based encoders can improve long-range contextual information of complicated tumor patterns. Multi-modal imaging (ultrasound with mammography or MRI) would be an extension of the framework, which might result in a higher confidence in the diagnosis. Also, model explain ability tools like enhanced Grad-CAM visualization may be added to enhance clinician trust and interpretability. Lastly, implementation in real-time and future clinical trials will be considered to transform the proposed system into a system that is not only in research but also in clinical practice. A limitation of the current study is that normal images are not included in the classification evaluation. Future work will extend the proposed framework to support full multi-class classification, including normal cases, to improve its applicability in clinical screening scenarios. A limitation of this paper is the absence of retraining baseline models under identical experimental conditions. Future work will focus on implementing and evaluating baseline architectures on the same dataset and using statistical significance testing to ensure fair comparison.

The Bivariate Regular Variation of randomly weighted sums revisited in the presence of interdependence
Dimitrios G. Konstantinides, Charalampos D. Passalidis

Abstract
. We study the closure properties of the class of Bivariate Regular Variation, symbolically B ​ R ​ V BRV , in standard and non-standard cases, with respect to the randomly weighted sums. However, we take into consideration a weak dependence structure among the random weights and the main random variables, providing more flexibility in applications. In the first main result, under the condition that the main random variables belong to B ​ R ​ V BRV , we provide a dependent bi-variate extension of Breiman’s theorem, under some sufficient conditions. Furthermore, we continue the extension into randomly weighted sums. The main target motivation of this work is stochastic modeling of regular variation under the condition of economic instability. For this reason we provide the ruin probability estimation over finite-time horizon in a two-dimensional discrete-time risk model, with interdependence among the insurance and financial risks, as also the asymptotic behavior of a risk measure, known as joint expected shortfall.



Keywords:
Breiman’s theorem; closure properties; interdependence; insurance and financial risks; bidimensional discrete-time risk model; multivariate linear single big jump principle.

Mathematics Subject Classification
: Primary 62P05; Secondary 60G70.

2.
Preliminaries

For any two positive functions
f,\,g
, the asymptotic relation

\displaystyle f(x)=o[g(x)]\,,

as
x\to\infty
, means that it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}=0\,.

The asymptotic relation
f(x)=O[g(x)]
, as
x\to\infty
, means that it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{f(x)}{g(x)}<\infty\,,

while the asymptotic relation
f(x)\asymp g(x)
, as
x\to\infty
, means that both
f(x)=O[g(x)]
, and
g(x)=O[f(x)]
, as
x\to\infty
hold. For any event
E
, we denote by
{\bf 1}_{E}
the indicator function of the event
E
. By
E^{c}
we denote the complementary event to
E
. For any
x,\,y
real numbers, we denote
x\vee y:=\max\{x,\,y\}
and
x\wedge y:=\min\{x,\,y\}
,
x^{+}:=x\vee 0
.

The random variables follow distributions with support is on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}:=[0,\,\infty)
, unless otherwise stated. The support of a distribution
V
is denoted by
S_{V}
and the random variable
Z
has distribution
V
.

2.1.
Bivariate regular variation

For a distribution
V
, such that
\overline{V}(x)>0
, for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we say that it is regularly varying, with some finite index
\alpha>0
if it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{V}(t\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}=t^{-\alpha}\,,

(2.1)

for any
t>0
, and we write symbolically
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
. The class of regular variation plays crucial role in the frame of heavy-tailed distribution, with several application in actuarial science, finance, credit risk and risk management, see for example
[
22
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
64
]
,
[
40
]
,
[
42
]
,
[
44
]
among others. For further threatments on regular variation, see
[
5
]
and
[
48
]
.

An equivalent definition of the regular variation, as it appears in (
2.1
) is the following: There exists finite, non-degenerate, Radon limit measure
\mu
, such that the convergence

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{Z}{U_{V}(x)}\in{\mathbb{B}}\right]=\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,,

(2.2)

holds for any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, which is
\mu
-continuous, where

\displaystyle U_{V}(x)=\left(\dfrac{1}{\overline{V}}\right)^{\leftarrow}(x)=\inf\left\{y\in{\mathbb{R}}\,:\,\dfrac{1}{\overline{V}(y)}\geq x\right\}\,,

(2.3)

for any
x>0
. We call
U_{V}(x)
normalization function. The measure
\mu
has the property of homogeneity, namely for any
\lambda>0
and any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\setminus\{0\}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mu\left(\lambda^{1/\alpha}{\mathbb{B}}\right)=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\mu({\mathbb{B}})\,.

(2.4)

The bivariate regular variation
BRV
, represents a special case of multivariate regular variation
MRV
, which introduced in
[
25
]
. We say that a non-negative random vector
(X,\,Y)
follows a
BRV
structure, if there exist a finite, non-degenerated, limit measure
\mu
, and two distributions
F
and
G
, such that it holds

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\left(\dfrac{X}{U_{F}(x)},\;\dfrac{Y}{U_{G}(x)}\right)\in{\mathbb{B}}\right]=\mu\left({\mathbb{B}}\right)\,,

(2.5)

for any Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, which is
\mu
-continuous. The distributions
F
,
G
, appearing in the normalization functions, are such that
F\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
and
G\in\mathcal{R}_{-\beta}
, for some finite indexes
\alpha,\,\beta>0
. For the distributions that satisfy (
2.5
) we write symbolically
(X,\,Y)\in BRV_{-\alpha,\,-\beta}(\mu)
. We notice that if
U_{V}(x)=U_{F}(x)=U_{G}(x)
, that means

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{(X,\,Y)}{U_{V}(x)}\in{\mathbb{B}}\right]=\mu\left({\mathbb{B}}\right)\,,

(2.6)

with
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, then we obtain the standard
BRV
structure, symbolically
(X,\,Y)\in BRV_{-\alpha}(\mu)
. The general

BRV
structure in (
2.5
) contains the standard
BRV
structure, as special case. Here again we observe, that the measure
\mu
, has the property of homogeneity, in the sense that for any
\lambda>0
and Borel set
{\mathbb{B}}\subsetneq{\mathbb{R}}_{+}^{2}\setminus\{{\bf 0}\}
, it holds

\displaystyle\mu({\mathbb{B}}_{\lambda})=\dfrac{1}{\lambda}\,\mu\left({\mathbb{B}}\right)\,,

(2.7)

where
{\mathbb{B}}_{\lambda}=\{(\lambda^{1/\alpha}\,p,\;\lambda^{1/\beta}\,q)\;:\;(p,\,q)\in{\mathbb{B}}\}
, see also in
[
56
, Lem. 5.1]
.

Several papers with applications of
MRV
on risk theory and risk management, adopt as condition the standard
MRV
, see for example
[
3
]
,
[
38
]
,
[
41
]
,
[
65
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
11
]
, among others. However, there still exist a few works with condition the general
MRV
, usually restricted in the bivariate case
BRV
, see
[
12
]
,
[
56
]
.

The fact that the
BRV
structure for the vector
(X,\,Y)
permits arbitrary dependence between the pair
(X,\,Y)
, in combination with the possible variety in the tail indexes of the distributions of
X
and
Y
, are the main reasons that
BRV
attracted attention. Furthermore, we recognize these reasons, as motivation of the study of the closure properties in this distribution class, with hopeful impact in practical applications.

2.2.
The dependence concept

Now, we give the main dependencies for further usage. Each pair
(X_{i},\,Y_{i})
contains arbitrary dependence because of the
BRV
structure. Therefore, our interest is focused in the dependencies between
\Theta_{i}
and
X_{i}
as also between
\Delta_{i}
and
Y_{i}
, in order to reach a generalization up to the interdependence.

The first dependence structure was introduced in
[
2
, Assum. 2]
and has be used in risk theory, to depict either time-dependent risk models, in continuous time risk models see
[
1
]
,
[
43
]
, or to present dependence structure between the random weights and the main random sizes, in discrete time risk models, see for example in
[
62
]
,
[
63
]
.

Let us render this dependence in the following assumption for any pair
(X_{i},\,\Theta_{i})
and any pair
(Y_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
. This dependence is formulated for only one random pair, but we use later this kind of dependence on the sequences of such pairs.

Assumption 2.1
.

(a)
For any pair
(X_{i},\,\Theta_{i})
, with
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
there exists some measurable function
h_{1,i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x\;|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta]\sim h_{1,i}(\theta)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]\,,

(2.8)

as
x\to\infty
, uniformly for any
\theta\in S_{\Theta_{i}}
.

(b)
For any pair
(Y_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
, with
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
there exists some measurable function
h_{2,i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[Y_{i}>x\;|\;\Delta_{i}=\delta]\sim h_{2,i}(\delta)\,\mathbf{P}[Y_{i}>x]\,,

(2.9)

as
x\to\infty
, uniformly for any
\delta\in S_{\Delta_{i}}
.

Note that the uniformity property in (
2.8
) is understood as

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}\sup_{\theta\in S_{\Theta_{i}}}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x\;|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta]}{h_{1,i}(\theta)\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x]}-1\right|=0\,,

and similarly in (
2.9
).

Remark 2.1
.

The notation
\Theta_{i}=\theta
and
\Delta_{i}=\delta
, are technical, in the sense that the
\theta
and
\delta
are not necessarily points, but also can be intervals, in the sense that the left member in (
2.8
) is defined by the relation

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x\;|\;\Theta_{i}\in[\theta-\varepsilon,\,\theta+\varepsilon]]\,,

and similarly for relation (
2.9
). Furthermore, we can see that relations (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) imply as special case the independence between
(X_{i},\,\Theta_{i})
and between
(Y_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
, respectively. Additionally, by
[
9
]
or
[
19
]
, we get that the functions
h_{1,i}
and
h_{2,i}
are bounded from above by some constant, namely there exist
k_{1,i}
,
k_{2,i}
, with
0<k_{1,i},\,k_{2,i}<\infty
, such that

\displaystyle h_{1,i}(\theta_{i})\leq k_{1,i}\,,\qquad h_{2,i}(\delta_{i})\leq k_{2,i}\,,

(2.10)

for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, with
\theta_{i}\in S_{\Theta_{i}}
and
\delta_{i}\in S_{\Delta_{i}}
. Finally, it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[h_{1,i}(\Theta_{i})]=1=\mathbf{E}[h_{2,i}(\Delta_{i})]\,,

(2.11)

for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
.

In the following statement, when say that holds Assumption
2.1
(a) for the pair
(X,\,\Theta)
, it means that there exists a function
h_{1}\;:\;[0,\infty)\to(0,\,\infty)
such that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X>x\;|\;\Theta=\theta]\sim h_{1}(\theta)\,\mathbf{P}[X>x]\,,

(2.12)

as
x\to\infty
, uniformly for any
\theta\in S_{\Theta}
.

In the next proposition, we find an extension of the Breiman’s theorem, under the dependence structure Assumption
2.1
(a), found in
[
28
, Lem. 3.3]
. For the original version of Breiman’s theorem, when the
X,\,\Theta
are independent, see in
[
6
]
,
[
18
]
and in generalization with dependencies, see in
[
60
]
,
[
19
]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a real-valued random variable with distribution
F\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
, with
\alpha>0
and
\Theta
be non-negative, non-degenerated to zero random variable. If the pair
(X,\,\Theta)
satisfies Assumption
2.1
(a), and for some
\varepsilon>0
, the inequality
\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha+\varepsilon}\,h_{1}(\Theta)]<\infty
is fulfilled and holds the asymptotic relation

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\Theta>c\,x]=o(\mathbf{P}[\Theta\,X>x])\,,

(2.13)

as
x\to\infty
, for any
c>0
, then it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\Theta\,X>x]\sim\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha}\,h_{1}(\Theta)]\,\mathbf{P}[X>x]\,,

(2.14)

as
x\to\infty
.

Under the convergence in (
2.2
), relation (
2.14
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>1\right]=\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha}\,h(\Theta)]\,.

(2.15)

The next assumption was introduced in
[
28
, Ass. 3.2]
and relies on a bi-variate version of the dependence structure in Assumption
2.1
. For the sake of brevity, we illustrate this asymptotic condition only for non-negative random variables as in Assumption
2.2
.

Assumption 2.2
.

Let
(X_{i},\,Y_{i})
and
(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
,
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, be random vectors. There exists a measurable function
g_{i}\;:\;[0,\,\infty)^{2}\to(0,\,\infty)
, such that we have for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{1},\;Y_{i}>x_{2}\;|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta_{i},\;\Delta_{i}=\delta_{i}]\sim g_{i}(\theta_{i},\,\delta_{i})\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{1},\;Y_{i}>x_{2}]\,,

(2.16)

as
(x_{1},\,x_{2})\to(\infty,\,\infty)
, uniformly for any
(\theta_{i},\;\delta_{i})\in(S_{\Theta_{i}},\;S_{\Delta_{i}})
.

Note that the uniform convergence in (
2.16
) is understood as

\displaystyle\lim_{(x_{1},\,x_{2})\to(\infty,\,\infty)}\sup_{(\theta_{i},\,\delta_{i})\in(S_{\Theta},\,S_{\Delta})}\left|\dfrac{\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{1},\;Y_{i}>x_{2}\;|\;\Theta_{i}=\theta_{i},\;\Delta_{i}=\delta_{i}]}{g_{i}(\theta_{i},\,\delta_{i})\,\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{1},\;Y_{i}>x_{2}]}-1\right|=0\,,

where, as with Assumption
2.1
, here again the left member of relation (
2.16
) is understood in the sense

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathbf{P}[X_{i}>x_{1}\,,\;Y_{i}>x_{2}\;|\;\Theta_{i}\in[\theta_{i}-\varepsilon,\,\theta_{i}+\varepsilon]\,,\;\Delta_{i}\in[\delta_{i}-\varepsilon,\,\delta_{i}+\varepsilon]]\,.

Remark 2.2
.

It is easy to see that Assumption
2.2
contains the independence between the two random vectors
(X_{i},\,Y_{i})
and
(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
, as special case, while the pairs
(X_{i},\,Y_{i})
have arbitrarily dependent components, exactly as the pairs
(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})
as well. From
[
28
, Rem. 3.2]
follows that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\;\Delta_{i})]=1\,,

(2.17)

for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
and further from
[
28
, Rem. 3.4]
, there exists constant
\Lambda_{i}\in(0,\,\infty)
, such that the inequality

\displaystyle g_{i}(\theta_{i},\;\delta_{i})\leq\Lambda_{i}\,,

(2.18)

hold, for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
, for any
\theta_{i}\in S_{\Theta_{i}}
,
\delta_{i}\in S_{\Delta_{i}}
.

3.
Breiman’s theorem

We now present an extension of Breiman’s theorem in a complex dependent model that according to our knowledge, is the most general setting that exists in the literature; that is, the
BRV
setting is assumed with dependent random weights that also dependent on the main random variables. One strand of research in the literature relies on the standard
MRV
framework; e.g., see
[
4
]
and
[
23
]
for similar approaches under either independence and dependence between random weights and their main random variables, respectively. Extensions beyond the non-standard
MRV
are restricted to a two dimensional setting with independence between random weights and their main random variables; e.g., see
[
12
, Lem. 5.2]
. Therefore, we present a generalization of this last reference that is the closest to our very general dependence framework. The next result, given as Theorem
3.1
, relies on 1) Assumption
2.1
with (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) being satisfied with functions
h_{1}
and
h_{2}
for
(X,\,\Theta)
and
(Y,\,\Delta)
respectively, and 2) Assumption
2.2
with (
2.16
) being satisfied with function
g
for
(X,\,\Theta)
and
(Y,\,\Delta)
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,\,Y)
and
(\Theta,\,\Delta)
be non-negative random vectors such that the pair
(X,\,Y)\in BRV_{-\alpha,\,-\beta}(\mu)
for some finite
\alpha,\,\beta>0
, and Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold. Further,
\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha+\varepsilon}\,h_{1}(\Theta)]+\mathbf{E}[\Delta^{\beta+\varepsilon}\,h_{2}(\Delta)]<\infty
for some
\varepsilon>0
and for any
c>0
we have that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[\Theta>c\,x]=o(\mathbf{P}[\Theta\,X>x])\,\text{ and }\,\mathbf{P}[\Delta>c\,x]=o(\mathbf{P}[\Delta\,Y>x])\,\text{ as $x\to\infty$.}

(3.1)

Then,
(\Theta\,X,\,\Delta\,Y)\in BRV_{-\alpha,\,-\beta}(\widehat{\mu})
with a limit measure
\widehat{\mu}
satisfying for any
(p,\,q)>{\bf 0}

\displaystyle\widehat{\mu}\Big(\big([0,\,p)\times[0,\,q)\big)^{c}\Big)=\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha}\,h_{1}(\Theta)]}{p^{\alpha}}+\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta^{\beta}\,h_{2}(\Delta)]}{q^{\beta}}-\mathbf{E}\left[g(\Theta,\,\Delta)\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta}\right)\right]\,,

(3.2)

where the tail of the two-dimensional measure
\mu
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\mu}\left(x,\;y\right):=\mu\left(\left(x,\,\infty\right]\times\left(y,\,\infty\right]\right)\,,\,\text{for any $x,\,y>0$.}

Proof.

For any
(p,\,q)>{\bf 0}
by the assumptions on the moments and relation (
3.1
), we can apply Proposition
2.1
, and employing the formulation in relation (
2.15
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p\right]=\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha}\,h_{1}(\Theta)]}{p^{\alpha}}\,,\qquad\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]=\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta^{\beta}\,h_{2}(\Delta)]}{q^{\beta}}\,,

(3.3)

Let now some constant
C>0
, then we write

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]=x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\;\{\Theta\leq C\}\cap\{\Delta\leq C\}\right]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\;\{\Theta>C\}\cup\{\Delta>C\}\right]=:\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}\,,

(3.4)

For the second term in (
3
) we have the inequality

\displaystyle\rho_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\;\Theta>C\right]+x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\;\Delta>C\right]

(3.5)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta\,X}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\;\Theta>C\right]+x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Delta\,Y}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\;\Delta>C\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta^{\alpha}\,h_{1}(\Theta)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\Theta>C\}}]}{p^{\alpha}}+\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta^{\beta}\,h_{2}(\Delta)\,{\bf 1}_{\{\Delta>C\}}]}{q^{\beta}}\,,

as
x\to\infty
, where in the last step we used (
3.3
). Hence, taking into account the moment conditions, we apply the dominating convergence theorem on the last terms of (

Let us start with a preliminary lemma and some concepts. We say that a distribution
V
, with
\overline{V}(x)>0
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, belongs to class of dominatedly varying distributions, symbolically
V\in\mathcal{D}
, if for any (or equivalently, for some)
b\in(0,\,1)
it holds

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{V}(b\,x)}{\overline{V}(x)}<\infty\,.

It is easy to see that
\mathcal{R}:=\bigcup_{\alpha>0}\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}\subsetneq\mathcal{D}
. Two important indexes for the distribution
V
are the upper and lower Matuszewska indexes, that are defined for a distribution
V
, with
\overline{V}(x)>0
for any
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
, as follows

\displaystyle J^{+}_{V}:=\inf\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{V}_{*}(u)}{\ln u}\;:\;u>1\right\}\,,\quad J^{-}_{V}:=\sup\left\{-\dfrac{\ln\overline{V}^{*}(u)}{\ln u}\;:\;u>1\right\}\,,

where

\displaystyle\overline{V}_{*}(u):=\liminf_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{V}(ux)}{\overline{V}(x)}\,,\qquad\overline{V}^{*}(u):=\limsup_{x\to\infty}\dfrac{\overline{V}(ux)}{\overline{V}(x)}\,.

These indexes were introduced in
[
45
]
and satisfy
0\leq J^{-}_{V}\leq J^{+}_{V}\leq\infty
. They characterize some well-known classes of heavy tailed distributions; e.g.,
V\in\mathcal{D}
if and only if
J^{+}_{V}<\infty
, and further, if
V\in\mathcal{R}_{-\alpha}
with
\alpha>0
then
J^{-}_{V}=J^{+}_{V}=\alpha
; for details, see
[
5
]
and
[
36
]
.

\displaystyle J_{2}(p,\,q,\,x)

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\,,\;H_{1}^{c}(x)\right]

(4.23)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}}{U_{F}(x)}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\,p\right]

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\bigvee_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}}{U_{F}(x)}>\dfrac{p}{n}\,,\;\bigvee_{j=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}}{U_{F}(x)}\leq(1-\varepsilon)\,p\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}}{U_{F}(x)}>\dfrac{p}{n}\,,\;\sum_{i\neq l=1}^{n}\dfrac{\Theta_{l}\,X_{l}}{U_{F}(x)}>\varepsilon\,p\right]\to 0\,,\text{ as $x\to\infty$,}

where the independence of
X_{i}
and

are used to conclude the last step. Similar arguments employed in the derivation of (

(4.24)

) - (

\displaystyle\limsup_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,.

(4.25)

Now, we examine the lower bound, for which we consider the events

\displaystyle E_{i}:=\left\{\dfrac{\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right\}\,,\text{ for}\;i=1,\,\ldots,\,n\,.

Since
\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}
are non-negative for any

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]\geq x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}E_{i}\right]

(4.26)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}x\,\mathbf{P}[E_{i}]-\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}x\,\mathbf{P}[E_{i}\cap E_{j}]=:I_{1}(p,\,q,\,x)-I_{2}(p,\,q,\,x)\,.

The first term,

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}I_{1}(p,\,q,\,x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,,

(4.27)

while the second term,

, satisfies

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}I_{2}(p,\,q,\,x)=0\,.

(4.28)

due to Lemma

which implies that

\displaystyle x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\,\dfrac{\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}}{U_{G}(x)}>q,\,\dfrac{\Theta_{j}\,X_{j}}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\,\dfrac{\Delta_{j}\,Y_{j}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]\leq x\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{\Theta_{i}X_{i}}{U_{F}(x)}>p,\,\dfrac{\Delta_{j}\,Y_{j}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]

\displaystyle\to 0\,,

as
x\to\infty
. Relations (

) conclude that

\displaystyle\liminf_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,,

(4.29)

an in turn, (
4.25
) and (
4.29
) lead to

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,.

(4.30)

Finally, relations (

\displaystyle\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\mathbf{P}\left[\left(\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}\right)\in([0,\,p)\times[0,\,q))^{c}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{x\to\infty}x\,\left(\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]-\mathbf{P}\left[\dfrac{S_{n}^{\Theta}}{U_{F}(x)}>p\,,\;\dfrac{T_{n}^{\Delta}}{U_{G}(x)}>q\right]\right)\,,

4.16

Risk theory

[

[

[

\displaystyle(\Theta\,X\,,\;\Delta\,Y)\in BVR_{-\alpha}(\mu)\,,

53
]
,
[
57
]
,
[
58
]
among others.

We assume that in this model an insurer has two business lines, with the lines to have net losses
X_{i}
and
Y_{i}
respectively, during the
i
-th period, that means the
X_{i}
represents the total claims minus the premia at the first business line during the
i
-th period and the
Y_{i}
represents the same at the second business line. Additionally, we assume that the insurer is free to make simultaneously risky or risk-free investments as they are depicted by the discount factors
\Theta_{i}
and
\Delta_{i}
. So, if the initial capital of the insurer is
x>0
and it is split to two lines in the form
p\,x
and
q\,x
, where
p,\,q>0
and
p+q=1
, then the surplus processes to each business line up to moment
k=1,\,\ldots,\,n
can be written in the form

\displaystyle U_{1}(p\,x,\,k)=p\,x-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}\,,\qquad U_{2}(q\,x,\,k)=q\,x-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Delta_{j}\,Y_{j}\,.

(5.13)

We observe that in the discrete risk model, usually the dependence of the stochastic discount factor and the net losses is represented in the form

\displaystyle X_{i}\,\Theta_{i}=X_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{i}Z_{j}\,,

with the dependence to appear between the
X_{i}
and the
Z_{i}
. However, since we do NOT require that the functions from Assumption
2.1
and
2.2
to be the same for any
i=1,\,\ldots,\,n
we obtain that the model in (
5.13
) is directly related to the randomly weighted sums from Section 4.

In the one-dimensional set up the ruin is defined as the event when the surplus becomes negative, and consequently we find the uni-variate ruin times up to the time horizon
n
as follows

\displaystyle\tau_{1}(p\,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\;U_{1}(p\,x,\,k)<0\;|\;U_{1}(p\,x,\,0)=p\,x\right\}\,,

\displaystyle\tau_{2}(q\,x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\;U_{2}(q\,x,\,k)<0\;|\;U_{2}(q\,x,\,0)=q\,x\right\}\,.

In bi-variate case, the ruin probability can be defined in several different ways, see
[
17
, Sec. 1]
. Here, we use three types of the ruin probability at finite horizon, up to moment
n
. The first type is the probability that both portfolios to fall below zero, up to moment
n
, but not necessarily simultaneously. Thence, this the ruin time is defined as follows

\displaystyle\tau_{and}=\max\left\{\tau_{1}(p\,x),\,\tau_{2}(q\,x)\right\}\,,

and consequently the ruin probability from relation (
5.13
) is defined as

\displaystyle\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)=\mathbf{P}[\tau_{and}\leq n]\,,

or equivalently as

\displaystyle\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)=\mathbf{P}\left[\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\,,\;\max_{1\leq l\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{l}\Delta_{j}\,Y_{j}>q\,x\right]\,,

(5.14)

The second kind of ruin probability, corresponds to the case when both portfolios fall simultaneously below zero. Thus, this ruin time is given by

\displaystyle\tau_{sim}=\inf\left\{k\in{\mathbb{N}}\;:\;U_{1}(p\,x,\,k)\vee U_{2}(p\,x,\,k)<0\right\}\,,

and the ruin probability takes the form
\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)=\mathbf{P}[\tau_{sim}\leq n]
.

Finally, the third kind of ruin probability, appears when at least one of the two portfolios falls below zero. In this case the ruin time is defined as

\displaystyle\tau_{or}=\min\left\{\tau_{1}(p\,x)\,,\;\tau_{2}(q\,x)\right\}\,,

with corresponding ruin probability of the form

\displaystyle\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)=\mathbf{P}[\tau_{or}\leq n]=\mathbf{P}\left[\left\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Theta_{i}X_{i}>p\,x\right\}\bigcup\left\{\max_{1\leq l\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\Delta_{i}Y_{i}>q\,x\right\}\right].

Now we proceed with the following theorem

Theorem 5.1
.

Assume that the conditions in Lemma
5.1
hold with
p,\,q>0
and
p+q=1
.

(i)

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\sim\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\sim\overline{V}(x)\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,,

(5.15)

as
x\to\infty
.

(ii)

For each
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, we obtain that it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\sim\overline{V}(x)\,\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta_{i}^{\alpha}\,h_{1,i}(\Theta_{i})]}{p^{\alpha}}

(5.16)

\displaystyle+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta_{i}^{\beta}\,h_{2,i}(\Delta_{i})]}{q^{\beta}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\Bigg)\,,

as
x\to\infty
.

Proof.

(i)

Relation (
5.14
) implies that

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}[S_{n}^{\Theta}>p\,x\,,\;T_{n}^{\Delta}>q\,x]\leq\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\leq\mathbf{P}[S_{n}^{\Theta+}>p\,x\,,\;T_{n}^{\Delta+}>q\,x]\,,

which together with Lemma
5.1
gives (
5.15
), for
\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
.

Now we deal with
\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
. The lower bound result is shown as follows

\displaystyle\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\,,\;\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right)\right]

(5.17)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\,,\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right]

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle\overline{V}(x)\,\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\,,

as
x\to\infty
, where in the last step we use Lemma
5.1
.

The upper bound result is true, due to (
5.15
) for
\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
and the fact that
\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\leq\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
for any triple
(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
.

(ii)

The upper asymptotic bound for
\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
is true given that

\displaystyle\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)=\mathbf{P}\left[\left\{\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\right\}\bigcup\left\{\max_{1\leq l\leq n}\sum_{i=1}^{l}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}\left[\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}^{+}>p\,x\right\}\bigcup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}^{+}>q\,x\right\}\right]

(5.18)

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}^{+}>p\,x\right]\!+\!\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}^{+}>q\,x\right]-\!\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}^{+}>p\,x\,,\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}^{+}>q\,x\right]

\displaystyle\sim\overline{V}(x)\,\Bigg(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta_{i}^{\alpha}\,h_{1,i}(\Theta_{i})]}{p^{\alpha}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta_{i}^{\beta}\,h_{2,i}(\Delta_{i})]}{q^{\beta}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\Bigg)\,

as
x\to\infty
, where we use (
4
) and Lemma
5.1
in the last step, since we consider here the standard
BRV
.

For the lower asymptotic bound of
\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
we find by (
4
) and Lemma
5.1
that it holds

\displaystyle\psi_{or}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)\geq\mathbf{P}\left[\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\right\}\bigcup\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right\}\right]=

(5.19)

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\right]+\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right]-\mathbf{P}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Theta_{i}\,X_{i}>p\,x\,,\;\sum_{i=1}^{n}\Delta_{i}\,Y_{i}>q\,x\right]\sim

\displaystyle\overline{V}(x)\,\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Theta_{i}^{\alpha}\,h_{1,i}(\Theta_{i})]}{p^{\alpha}}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\dfrac{\mathbf{E}[\Delta_{i}^{\beta}\,h_{2,i}(\Delta_{i})]}{q^{\beta}}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\mathbf{E}\left[g_{i}(\Theta_{i},\,\Delta_{i})\,\overline{\mu}\left(\dfrac{p}{\Theta_{i}},\;\dfrac{q}{\Delta_{i}}\right)\right]\right)\,,

as
x\to\infty
. From relations (
(ii)
) and (
5.19
) we get relation (
5.16
). ∎

By Theorem
5.1
we can see that the ruin probabilities
\psi_{and}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
and
\psi_{sim}(x,\,p,\,q;\,n)
are asymptotically equivalent, that indicates that their difference is negligible, that means the simultaneous and non-simultaneous ruin in both portfolios under the presence of heavy tails on the net losses, have almost the same probability. This fact can be observed also in several papers on continuous time risk models on more dimensions, under standard
MRV
condition, see for example in

5.2.
Joint Expected Shortfall

In this section we study an application of our main results for to quantify the marginal effect of concomitant extreme events. This tail measure is inspired by the so-called
Joint Expected Shortfall (JES)
risk measure introduced in
[
27
]
and is given as follows:

\displaystyle JES(x):=\mathbf{E}\left[\Theta X|\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\right]

(5.20)

and we evaluate
JES(x)
as
x\to\infty
. The main result of this section is given as Proposition
5.1
. Let notice that the limit measure
\widehat{\mu}
in the following proposition was given in (
3.2
) .

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(X,\,Y)
and
(\Theta,\,\Delta)
be non-negative random vectors such that
(X,\,Y)\in BRV_{-\alpha,\,-\beta}(\mu)
for some finite
\alpha,\,\beta>0
, and assume that all the conditions in Theorem
3.1
hold. Moreover, assume that there exists
x_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle JES(x)<\infty\,,

for any
x>x_{0}
. Then, for any
\alpha>1
we have that

\displaystyle JES(x)\sim U_{F}(x)\left[1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\mu}\big((\xi,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)}{\widehat{\mu}\big((1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)}\;d\xi\right]\;\text{as $x\to\infty$},

(5.21)

provided that
\widehat{\mu}\big((1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)>0
.

Proof.
Since
JES(x)<\infty
for any
x>x_{0}
, one may find that

\displaystyle\frac{JES(x)}{U_{F}(x)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{U_{F}(x)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Pr\big(\Theta X>t|\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)\;dt

(5.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\frac{1}{U_{F}(x)}\int_{U_{F}(x)}^{\infty}\frac{\Pr\big(\Theta X>t,\;\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}\;dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x)\xi,\;\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}\;d{\xi}

\displaystyle\sim

\displaystyle 1+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\widehat{\mu}\big((\xi,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)}{\widehat{\mu}\big((1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)}\;d\xi\;\text{as $x\to\infty$},

where a change of variables,
t=\xi U_{F}(x)
, is made in the third step, while the Dominated Convergence Theorem and the main result in Theorem
3.1
are applied in the last step. The latter follows from

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x)\xi,\;\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}\;d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x)\big)}{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x),\,\Delta Y>U_{G}(x)\big)}\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x)\xi\big)}{\Pr\big(\Theta X>U_{F}(x)\big)}\;d\xi

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\widehat{\mu}\big((1,\infty]\times[0,\infty]\big)}{\widehat{\mu}\big((1,\infty]\times(1,\infty]\big)}+\epsilon_{1}\right)\int_{1}^{\infty}\xi^{\epsilon_{2}-\alpha}\;d\xi,

where the last follows from Theorem
3.1
and the well-known Potter’s bound so that there exists
x_{0}>0
such that last inequality holds for any
\epsilon_{1}>0
and
0<\epsilon_{2}<\alpha+1
. These enable to apply the Dominated Convergence Theorem, which completes this proof.
∎

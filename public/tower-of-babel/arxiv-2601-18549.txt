Semilinear diffusion equations on infinite graphs: the dissipative and Lipschitz cases
Elvise Berchio, Davide Bianchi, Alberto G. Setti, Maria Vallarino

Abstract
We study a class of semilinear diffusion equations on infinite, connected, weighted graphs, focusing on two types of nonlinearities: monotone decreasing and Lipschitz continuous. Under minimal structural assumptions on the graph, we establish existence, uniqueness, and regularity of mild solutions for initial data in ℓ p \ell^{p} spaces, with 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty . Our approach relies on time discretization via an implicit Euler scheme and an exhaustion technique using Dirichlet subgraphs. As a by-product, we obtain existence and uniqueness results for a related time-independent equation. Finite-time extinction and positivity for solutions under a specific forcing term are also proved.



Keywords
graphs; semilinear diffusion equation; existence of solutions; uniqueness of solutions.

35K55, 35A01, 05C22, 05C63, 47H06.

Definition 2.1

(Graph)
.

A graph is a quadruple

•

(

Symmetry:
w(x,y)=w(y,x)
for every

(
A2)

No loops:
w(x,x)=0
for every
x\in X

(

\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)<\infty

x\in X
.

If the cardinality of the node set is finite, i.e.,
|X|<\infty
, then

is called a
finite graph
, otherwise,

is called an

. The non-zero values

associated with the
edge

\{x,y\}
. In this case we will write

meaning that

is
connected
to
y
. On the other hand, if

, then we will write

meaning that
x
and
y
are not connected by an edge. A

is a (possibly infinite) sequence of nodes
\{x_{i}\}_{i\geq 0}
such that
x_{i}\sim x_{i+1}
. A

is a walk with no repeated nodes. A graph is
connected

if there is a finite walk connecting every pair of nodes, that is, for any pair of nodes

there is a finite walk such that
x=x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim\cdots\sim x_{n}=y
. Moreover, we will say that a subset

is connected if for every pair of nodes

there exists a finite walk connecting

and

Y

is maximal with respect to inclusion.

We define the

\operatorname{deg}
of a node as

\operatorname{deg}(x)=\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)+\kappa(x).

In the sequel we shall make use of the following conditions on the graph:

(IP)

for every
infinite path

\{x_{n}\}_{n}
,
\sum_{n}\mu(x_{n})=\infty
;

(B)

the edge degree is
bounded
, i.e.,
\sup_{x\in X}\frac{\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)}{\mu(x)}<\infty
.

The set of real-valued functions on
X
is denoted by
C(X)
and
C_{c}(X)
denotes the set of functions on
X
with finite support. As usual, for

we define the

as

\ell^{p}(X,\mu)\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left\{u\in C(X)\mid\sum_{x\in X}|u(x)|^{p}\mu(x)<\infty\right\}&\mbox{for }1\leq p<\infty,\\
\left\{u\in C(X)\mid\sup_{x\in X}|u(x)|<\infty\right\}&\mbox{for }p=\infty,\end{cases}

with their norms

\|u\|_{p}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\left(\sum_{x\in X}|u(x)|^{p}\mu(x)\right)^{{1}/{p}}&\mbox{for }1\leq p<\infty,\\
\sup_{x\in X}|u(x)|&\mbox{for }p=\infty.\end{cases}

In addition to the previous standard definitions, we introduce the following restrictions to the nonnegative/nonpositive cones:

\displaystyle\ell^{p,+}\left(X,\mu\right)\coloneqq\ell^{p}\left(X,\mu\right)\cap\left\{u\in C(X)\mid u\geq 0\right\},\quad\ell^{p,-}\left(X,\mu\right)\coloneqq-\ell^{p,+}\left(X,\mu\right).

The
formal graph Laplacian

\Delta\colon\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)\subseteq C(X)\to C(X)
associated to the graph
G=(X,w,\kappa,\mu)
is defined by

\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)\coloneqq\{u\in C(X)\mid\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)|u(y)|<\infty\quad\forall x\in X\},

(2.1a)

\displaystyle\Delta u(x)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)\left(u(x)-u(y)\right)+\frac{\kappa(x)}{\mu(x)}u(x)

(2.1b)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mu(x)}\left(\operatorname{deg}(x)u(x)-\sum_{y\in X}w(x,y)u(y)\right)\qquad x\in X.

We note that, in general,
\Delta(C_{c}(X))\subseteq\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
is not true. That is, the formal graph Laplacian does not necessarily map finitely supported functions into
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
spaces. This is a particularly important property that will be useful later, and it is equivalent to the validity of

\begin{cases}\sum_{y\in X}\frac{w(x,y)^{p}}{\mu(y)^{p-1}}<\infty&\mbox{if}\quad 1\leq p<\infty,\\
\sup_{y}\frac{w(x,y)}{\mu(y)}<\infty&\mbox{if}\quad p=\infty,\end{cases}\qquad\mbox{for every }x\in X.

(
C_{p}
)

Indeed, since for every

\Delta\delta_{x}=-\sum_{y\sim x}\frac{w(x,y)}{\mu(y)}\delta_{y}+\frac{\sum_{y\sim x}w(x,y)}{\mu(x)}\delta_{x}+\frac{\kappa(x)}{\mu(x)}\delta_{x},

it is easy to see that
\Delta\delta_{x}\in\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
if and only if (

C
p

) is satisfied.

Remark 2.2
.

Note that Condition (

C
p

) is always true for

, due to Assumption
(A3)
. In particular, it always holds that

. Moreover, (

is locally finite or if the measure

In the paper, we study the nonlinear operator
\mathcal{A}
, obtained by adding the nonlinear operator
F
to the graph Laplacian

, defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}:=F+\Delta,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})=\{u\in\ell^{p}(X,\mu)\mid u\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta),\;\mathcal{A}u\in\ell^{p}(X,\mu)\;\}

with

As we will see in the following, a key ingredient in our proof is the approximation of the operator

by a sequence of operators defined on finite subsets of

are called

\displaystyle\mathring{\partial}Y\coloneqq\left\{y\in Y\mid y\sim x\mbox{ for some }x\in X\setminus Y\right\}\qquad\textnormal{interior boundary},

\displaystyle\accentset{\hbox{\textbullet}}{\partial}Y\coloneqq\left\{x\in X\setminus Y\mid x\sim y\mbox{ for some }y\in Y\right\}\qquad\textnormal{exterior boundary}.

Definition 2.3
.

We say that a graph

, and we write

•

;

•

;

•

\kappa^{\prime}(y)=\kappa(y)
for every

;

•

\mu^{\prime}\equiv\mu_{|Y}
,

where

and

denote the restrictions of

We call

. The corresponding formal graph Laplacian for a subgraph

is defined according to (

) and (

(X,w,\kappa,\mu)
is replaced by
(Y,w^{\prime},\kappa^{\prime},\mu^{\prime})

.

Observe that in the definition above we do not require that

Definition 2.4
.

An induced subgraph

G_{\textnormal{dir}}\coloneqq\left(Y,w_{|Y\times Y},\kappa_{\textnormal{dir}},\mu_{|Y}\right)\subset G

is called a

if

\begin{cases}\kappa_{\textnormal{dir}}(y)\coloneqq\kappa_{|Y}(y)+b_{\textnormal{dir}}(y),\\
b_{\textnormal{dir}}(y)\coloneqq\sum_{z\in\accentset{\hbox{\textbullet}}{\partial}Y}w(y,z)=\sum_{z\not\in Y}w(y,z).\end{cases}

(2.3)

We note that

is finite because of

. We call

the
boundary (Dirichlet) weight-function
and
\kappa_{\textnormal{dir}}
the

. Clearly,

. We will denote by

the graph Laplacian of

in order to distinguish it from the graph Laplacian of

The Dirichlet killing term accounts for the edge deficiency of nodes in

compared to the same nodes in

Let
\bm{\mathfrak{i}}\colon C(Y)\hookrightarrow C(X)
be the canonical embedding and

be the canonical projection, i.e.,

\bm{\mathfrak{i}}v(x)=\begin{cases}v(x)&\mbox{if }x\in Y,\\
0&\mbox{if }x\in X\setminus Y,\end{cases}\qquad\bm{\pi}u=u_{|Y}.

(2.4)

If

\displaystyle\Delta_{\textnormal{dir}}v(y)=\Delta\bm{\mathfrak{i}}v(y)\mbox{ for }v\in\operatorname{dom}(\Delta_{\textnormal{dir}})\mbox{ and }y\in Y;

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Delta u(y)=\Delta_{\textnormal{dir}}\bm{\pi}u(y)\mbox{ for }u\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)\cap\left\{u\in C(X)\mid u\equiv 0\mbox{ on }X\setminus Y\right\}\mbox{ and }y\in Y.

Therefore, the Dirichlet graph Laplacian

2.2
Accretivity

As is well known, a crucial property when dealing with nonlinear diffusions is accretivity. We recall the definition of accretive and
\omega
-accretive operators here below.

For an overview of accretive operators and nonlinear evolution equations in Banach spaces, we refer the interested reader to the seminal monograph by Bénilan, Crandall, and Pazy
[
4
]
, or to Barbu’s book
[
3
]
for a more modern introduction. The theory is developed in the general setting of multivalued operators; however, in our framework we restrict the study to the single-valued case.

Definition 2.5
.

If
(E,\|\cdot\|)
is a real Banach space and
\mathcal{A}\colon\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A})\subseteq E\to E
is a (not necessarily linear) operator, then
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
accretive
if
\mathcal{A}
satisfies:

\left\|(u-v)+\lambda\left(\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v\right)\right\|\geq\|u-v\|\quad\mbox{for every}\;u,v\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\mathcal{A}\right)\;\mbox{and for every}\;\lambda>0.

Given
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
the operator
\mathcal{A}
is said to be
\omega
-accretive
if
\mathcal{A}+\omega\operatorname{id}
is accretive. An accretive operator is said
m
-accretive if
\operatorname{id}+\lambda\mathcal{A}
is surjective for every
\lambda>0
.

We note for future use that
\mathcal{A}
is
\omega
-accretive if and only if

\|(u-v)+\lambda(\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v)\|\geq(1-\omega\lambda)\|u-v\|,

(2.8)

for all
u,v\in\operatorname{dom}\mathcal{A}
and
0\leq\lambda\omega<1
, see
[
4
, Section 2.1]
.

In the special case where
E=\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
,
1\leq p<\infty
, the above definition admits an equivalent closed form expression. That is, writing

z\coloneq\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v,\qquad k\coloneqq u-v,

then the operator
\mathcal{A}
is accretive if and only if for every
u,v\in\operatorname{dom}(\mathcal{A})
,
u\neq v
, the following holds

\begin{cases}\;\;\sum_{x\in X}z(x)|k(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(k(x))\mu(x)\geq 0&\mbox{for }1<p<\infty,\\
&\\
\;\;\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X{:}\\
k(x)=0\end{subarray}}|z(x)|\mu(x)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in X{:}\\
k(x)\neq 0\end{subarray}}z(x)\operatorname{sgn}(k(x))\mu(x)\geq 0&\mbox{for }p=1,\end{cases}

(2.9)

where

\operatorname{sgn}(t)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }t>0,\\
0&\text{if }t=0,\\
-1&\text{if }t<0.\end{cases}

See
[
4
, Example 2.9]
.

Next, we recall the following lemma.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
G
be a graph and let
u\in C_{c}(X)
. Then, for every

\sum_{x\in X}\Delta u(x)|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(u(x))\mu(x)\geq 0.

In particular, the above inequality holds for

Proof.

The statement follows from
[

for

Definition 3.3

Given a time interval

, with

, and a forcing term

-approximate solution

Definition 3.5

(Mild solution)
.

If

4.1
Comparison principle

We provide here a comparison principle and positivity preserving properties for solutions of equation (
4.1
). See
[
7
,
6
]
for related results.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
G
be a connected graph. Suppose that
F
satisfies
(F1)
and
\lambda>0
or that
F
satisfies
(F2)
and
0<\lambda<1/L
. Let
u\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)
and assume that one of the following cases holds:

a)

G
does not contain any infinite path;

b)

G
satisfies
(IP)
and
\exists\;p>0
such that for every infinite path
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
we have
\sum_{n}|u(x_{n})|^{p}\mu(x_{n})<\infty
.

If
\left({\operatorname{id}}+\lambda\mathcal{A}\right)u\geq 0

(\leq 0)
, then
u\geq 0

(\leq 0)
. Moreover, if
u
vanishes at some node, then
u\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Define
\psi(t):=t-\lambda f(t)
. If
F
satisfies
(F1)
then
\psi
is strictly monotone increasing and surjective for all
\lambda>0
, while if
F
satisfies
(F2)
this holds for all
0<\lambda<1/L
. Indeed, if
0<\lambda<1/L
, then for any
t>s
one has
\psi(t)-\psi(s)=t-s-\lambda[f(t)-f(s)]\geq(1-\lambda L)(t-s)>0
. Hence,
\psi
is strictly increasing. Furthermore, we have
(1-\lambda L)t\leq\psi(t)=t-\lambda f(t)\leq(1+\lambda L)t
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
, therefore
\lim_{\pm\infty}\psi(t)=\pm\infty
for all
0<\lambda<1/L
. Hence,
\psi
is surjective in
\mathbb{R}
as well.

Let
u\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)
be such that
\left({\operatorname{id}}+\lambda\mathcal{A}\right)u=\left(\psi+\lambda\Delta\right)u\geq 0
. If
u\geq 0
, then there is nothing to prove.
Hence, we assume that there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that
u(x_{0})<0
. We will show that this leads to a contradiction in the two cases.

Recalling that
f(0)=0
in both the cases
(F1)
and
(F2)
, then
\psi(0)=0
and, combined with its strictly monotone increasing property, we get

\psi(u(x_{0}))+\lambda\frac{\kappa(x_{0})}{\mu(x_{0})}u(x_{0})<0.

(4.3)

Furthermore, as
\left(\psi+\lambda\Delta\right)u(x_{0})\geq 0
,

0\leq\psi(u(x_{0}))+\frac{\lambda}{\mu(x_{0})}\sum_{y\in X}w(x_{0},y)\left(u(x_{0})-u(y)\right)+\lambda\frac{\kappa(x_{0})}{\mu(x_{0})}u(x_{0}).

(4.4)

Combining the above inequalities (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), we get

0<-\left[\psi(u(x_{0}))+\lambda\frac{\kappa(x_{0})}{\mu(x_{0})}u(x_{0})\right]\leq\frac{\lambda}{\mu(x_{0})}\sum_{y\in X}w(x_{0},y)\left(u(x_{0})-u(y)\right),

and since
w(\cdot,\cdot)\geq 0
and
G
is connected, there exists
y=x_{1}\sim x_{0}
such that
u(x_{1})<u(x_{0})
. In particular,
u(x_{1})<0
.

Hence, we see that every node where
u
is negative is connected to a node where
u
is strictly smaller. This is the basic observation that will be used in the two cases.

Case a)
Iterating the procedure above, we find a sequence of distinct nodes
\{x_{k}\}_{k=0}^{n}
such that
x_{0}\sim x_{1}\sim\cdots\sim x_{n}
and

u(x_{n})<u(x_{n-1})<\ldots<u(x_{0})<0.

Since
G
does not have any infinite path this sequence must end which leads to a contradiction.

Case b)

In this case, we can obtain an infinite path
\{x_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\ldots<u(x_{n})<u(x_{n-1})<\ldots<u(x_{0})<0.

It follows that
|u(x_{n})|^{p}>|u(x_{0})|^{p}>0
, for every
n
, and therefore

\sum_{{n}}|u(x_{n})|^{p}\mu(x_{n})>|u(x_{0})|^{p}\sum_{n}\mu(x_{n})=\infty,

which gives a contradiction.

Hence, we have established that
u\geq 0
in both cases. Now, if there exists
x_{0}\in X
such that
u(x_{0})=0
, then

0\leq\psi u(x_{0})+\lambda\Delta u(x_{0})=-\frac{\lambda}{\mu(x_{0})}\sum_{y\in X}w(x_{0},y)u(y)\leq 0

and thus
u(y)=0
for all
y\sim x_{0}
.
Using induction and the assumption that
G
is connected we get
u\equiv 0
.

The proof that
\left(\psi+\lambda\Delta\right)u\leq 0
implies
u\leq 0
is completely analogous.

∎

Corollary 4.4
.

With the hypotheses of Theorem
4.3
, let
u_{1},u_{2}
be solutions of

\left({\operatorname{id}}+\lambda\mathcal{A}\right)u_{k}=g_{k},\qquad g_{k}\in C(X)\mbox{ for }k=1,2.

If
g_{1}\geq g_{2}
, then
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
. In particular,
{\operatorname{id}}+\lambda\mathcal{A}
is injective on
\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
for all
1\leq p<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\psi
be defined as in the proof of Theorem
4.3
; since it is strictly increasing and
\psi(0)=0
, there exists a positive function
\sigma\in C(X)
such that

\psi(u_{1}(x))-\psi(u_{2}(x))=\sigma(x)\psi(u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x)),

for all
x\in X
.
Indeed, we can define

\sigma(x)=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }u_{1}(x)=u_{2}(x)\\
\frac{\psi(u_{1}(x))-\psi(u_{2}(x))}{\psi(u_{1}(x)-u_{2}(x))}&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

It follows that
u_{1}-u_{2}
satisfies

\sigma\psi(u_{1}-u_{2})+\lambda\Delta(u_{1}-u_{2})=g_{1}-g_{2}\geq 0\,\,\text{ on }X.

Then, the same argument used in the first part of proof of Theorem
4.3
implies that
u_{1}-u_{2}\geq 0
.

It follows that, if

for

with

, and

then
u_{1}=u_{2}
. In particular,
{\operatorname{id}}+\lambda\mathcal{A}
is injective on
\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
.

Lemma 4.5

Proof.

We first prove

u,v\in\ell^{p}(X,\mu).

We break up the above sum into two summands. The first is

\sum_{x\in X}\Delta(u-v)(x)|u(x)-v(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(u(x)-v(x))\mu(x),

which is nonnegative by Lemma

The second summand

\sum_{x\in X}(Fu(x)-Fv(x))|u(x)-v(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sgn}(u(x)-v(x))\mu(x),

is also nonnegative, since,
by monotonicity of

(recall that

), we have

\operatorname{sgn}(u(x)-v(x))=\operatorname{sgn}(Fu(x)-Fv(x)).

This concludes the proof of
(i)
.
Now
(ii)
is a consequence of Proposition

and
[

, Example 2.2]
.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

Let

be a finite graph and

. Then, for every

In addition, the solution

(1-\lambda L)^{-1}&\mbox{if }F\mbox{ satisfies }\textnormal{\ref{F2:Lipschitz}}.\end{cases}

(4.6)

Proof.

Recalling that
\mathcal{A}=F+\Delta
, with
F=-f
, we start noticing that equation (

) can be written as

(\psi+\lambda\Delta)u=g,

(4.7)

where
\psi(t):=t-\lambda f(t)
. In the proof of Theorem

we showed that if

satisfies
(F1)
then

is strictly monotone increasing and surjective for all

, while if
F
satisfies
(F2)

.

Then, since
\lambda\Delta
is a diagonally dominant matrix, all assumptions of
[

are satisfied and we conclude that, for every

, there exists a solution
u
of (
4.7
) and, in turn, of (
4.1
).

Still referring to equation (
4.7
), the fact that
\operatorname{id}+\lambda\mathcal{A}
is injective on

for all

and preserves the sign of

as soon as

is strictly monotone increasing follows by case

of Theorem
4.3
and Corollary
4.4
.

To prove (
4.6
), let us start observing that

\displaystyle\|u\|_{p}^{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}u(x)\operatorname{sign}(u(x))|u(x)|^{p-1}\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in X}g(x)|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)-\lambda\sum_{x\in X}(Fu(x)+\Delta u(x))|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{x\in X}|g(x)||u(x)|^{p-1}\mu(x)-\lambda\sum_{x\in X}(Fu(x)+\Delta u(x))|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)

\displaystyle\leq\|g\|_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p-1}-\lambda\sum_{x\in X}(Fu(x)+\Delta u(x))|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x),

(4.8)

where in the second equality we used the identity
u(x)=g(x)-\lambda(F+\Delta)u(x)
, and in the last inequality we applied Hölder’s inequality. We now turn to deriving a lower bound for the second term in (
4.8
). Since the inequality (
4.6
) is trivially verified for

, hereafter we will assume

not identically zero. We have two cases:

Case of

:
Observe that

.
From

, it holds that

\sum_{x\in X}Fu(x)\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)=\sum_{x\in X}Fu(x)\operatorname{sgn}(Fu(x))\mu(x)=\|Fu\|_{1}\geq 0.

Combining the above results in (

) we infer that

\|u\|_{p}^{p}\leq\|g\|_{p}\|u\|_{p}^{p-1},

that is,

.

Case of

We have that

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in X}(Fu(x)+\Delta u(x))|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)\geq

\displaystyle-\sum_{x\in X}|Fu(x)||u(x)|^{p-1}\mu(x)

\displaystyle-|F(0)|\sum_{x\in X}|u(x)|^{p-1}\mu(x)

\displaystyle+\sum_{x\in X}\Delta u(x)|u(x)|^{p-1}\operatorname{sign}(u(x))\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-L\sum_{x\in X}|u(x)|^{p}\mu(x)-|F(0)|\sum_{x\in X}|u(x)|^{p-1}\mu(x)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle-L\|u\|_{p}^{p},

where in the second inequality we used Lemma

, and in the last inequality we used the fact that

. Combining the above results in (

and consequently

∎

4.3
The set
\Omega_{p}

Assume the validity of (

C
p

) for a fixed
1\leq p<\infty
. We first introduce a sequence of operators
\mathcal{A}_{n}
whose purpose is to approximate the operator
\mathcal{A}
over an increasing exhaustion
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
of
X
by finite subsets, i.e., a sequence of finite subsets
X_{n}
of
X
such that

Definition 4.7

(The operator
\mathcal{A}_{n}
)
.

For
n\geq 1
let

\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\colon C(X_{n})\hookrightarrow C(X),\quad\bm{\pi}_{n}\colon C(X)\to C(X_{n})

be the canonical embeddings and projections, respectively.
Define

by

\displaystyle\operatorname{dom}_{p}\left(\mathcal{A}_{n}\right)\coloneqq C_{c}(X),\quad\mathcal{A}_{n}u\coloneqq\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\left(F+\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}\right)\bm{\pi}_{n}u,

where
\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}
is the graph Laplacian associated to the Dirichlet subgraph
G_{\textnormal{dir},n}\subseteq G
on the finite node set

.

The sequence of operators
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is involved in the definition of a subset
\Omega_{p}
of
\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
. As we will see below,
\Omega_{p}
is dense in
\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
and
\mathcal{A}
restricted to
\Omega_{p}
is accretive.

Definition 4.8

(The set
\Omega_{p}
)
.

We define
\Omega_{p}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
by letting

\Omega_{p}\coloneqq\{u\in\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})\mid\exists\,\{u_{n}\}_{n}\mbox{ s.t. }\operatorname{supp}u_{n}\subseteq X_{n},\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{p}=0,\;\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{A}_{n}u_{n}-\mathcal{A}u\|_{p}=0\}.

Let us remark that
\Omega_{p}=\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})=C(X)

if
G
is finite.

While the definition of
\Omega_{p}
depends on the choice of the exhaustion,
this set always contains all finitely supported functions as will be shown in the next lemma.

Lemma 4.9
.

We have that
C_{c}(X)\subseteq\Omega_{p}\subseteq\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
. In particular,

\overline{\Omega_{p}}=\overline{\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})}=\ell^{p}(X,\mu).

Proof.

Let us fix
u\in C_{c}(X)
. Thanks to condition (

C
p

),
u\in\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})
(recall Remark
2.2
). Define

u_{n}(x)\coloneqq\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\bm{\pi}_{n}u(x)=\begin{cases}u(x)&\mbox{if }x\in X_{n},\\
0&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Clearly,
\operatorname{supp}u_{n}\subseteq X_{n}
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{p}=0
. Since,
u\in C_{c}(X)
, there exists an
N>0
such that
u(x)=0
for every
x\in X\setminus X_{N}
.

We have
u_{n}\in\operatorname{dom}\left(\Delta\right)\cap\left\{u\in C(X)\mid u\equiv 0\mbox{ on }X\setminus X_{n}\right\}

for every
n\geq N
and then by equation (

)

\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{n}(x)=\Delta u_{n}(x)=\Delta u(x)\quad\forall\;x\in X_{n}\text{ with }n\geq N,

that is,

\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{n}=\bm{\pi}_{n}\Delta u\quad\forall\;n\geq N.

Therefore, recalling that
F(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{n}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\left(F+\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}\right)\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}F\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{n}+\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\bm{\pi}_{n}Fu_{n}+\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\bm{\pi}_{n}\Delta u

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\bm{\pi}_{n}\mathcal{A}u,\quad\forall\;n\geq N

and, since
\mathcal{A}u\in\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
, it follows that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{A}_{n}u_{n}-\mathcal{A}u\|_{p}=0

by dominated convergence.
∎

Finally, we prove the accretivity properties of

.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let

.

(i)

If
F
satisfies
(F1)
, then
\mathcal{A}_{|\Omega_{p}}
is accretive on
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
.

(ii)

If
F
satisfies
(F2)
with constant

, then
\mathcal{A}_{|\Omega_{p}}
is
\omega
-accretive on
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
, with

.

Proof.

If
G
is finite, then
\Omega_{p}=\operatorname{dom}_{p}(\mathcal{A})=C(X)
and the conclusion holds by Lemma

. If
G
is infinite, let

. Then, by the definition of
\Omega_{p}
, there exists
\{u_{n}\}_{n},\{v_{n}\}_{n}
such that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-u\|_{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|v_{n}-v\|_{p}=0,\qquad\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{A}_{n}u_{n}-\mathcal{A}u\|_{p}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\mathcal{A}_{n}v_{n}-\mathcal{A}v\|_{p}=0.

By Lemma
4.5
it follows that
\mathcal{A}_{n}
is accretive in case

, resp.

in case

, on
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
for every

(see also
[
7

) and, in turn, we deduce that

\displaystyle\left\|(u-v)+\lambda\left(\mathcal{A}u-\mathcal{A}v\right)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\left\|(u_{n}-v_{n})+\lambda\left(\mathcal{A}_{n}u_{n}-\mathcal{A}_{n}v_{n}\right)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}\lim_{n\to\infty}\|u_{n}-v_{n}\|_{p}=\|u-v\|_{p}&\mbox{in case }(i),\\

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem

.

If

From now on, let

According to Lemma

or

We assume first that

For each

This amounts to defining

•

•

•

•

Denoting by

As in the proof of Lemma

\psi(t)=t-\lambda f(t)

, while if

there exist
\hat{u}_{n}\in C(X_{n})_{n}

and

(4.12)

Setting

by the fact that every

(see also

Furthermore, denoting by

to

\displaystyle(\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}\operatorname{id}_{n}\bm{\pi}_{n}+\lambda\mathcal{A}_{n})u_{n}

\displaystyle=\bm{\mathfrak{i}}_{n}g_{n}

k=1,\dots,N
, define

\beta_{k}^{(n)}=\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}^{-1}(M_{k-1}^{(n)}),\quad\text{where }M_{k-1}^{(n)}=\|u_{k-1}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}.

(6.7)

Then, it holds that

\|u_{k}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{k}^{(n)}.

Proof.

Since
u_{0}\geq 0
, by a recursive argument and Theorem
4.3
-
(a)
, which obviously holds for finite graphs,
u_{k}^{(n)}\geq 0
. Let
x_{k}\in X_{n}
be such that
u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})=\|u_{k}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}
.
Since
G_{\mathrm{dir},n}
is finite, such a node exists and, since
u_{k}^{(n}
attains its maximum at
x_{k}

\Delta_{\mathrm{dir},n}u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\geq 0
.

Evaluating (
6.6
) at
x_{k}
gives

u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})+\lambda_{k}\big(u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})^{q}+\Delta_{\mathrm{dir},n}u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\big)=u_{k-1}^{(n)}(x_{k})\leq M_{k-1}^{(n)},

which, using the notation in (
6.3
), can be rewritten as

\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}\big(u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\big)+\lambda_{k}\Delta_{\mathrm{dir},n}u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\leq M_{k-1}^{(n)}.

Discarding the nonnegative graph Laplacian term yields

\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}\big(u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\big)\leq M_{k-1}^{(n)},

which implies,

u_{k}^{(n)}(x_{k})\leq\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}^{-1}(M_{k-1}^{(n)})=\beta_{k}^{(n)},

thus concluding the proof.
∎

Finally we prove upper estimates of the
u_{k}^{(n)}
in terms of the
\theta_{k}
given in (
6.4
).

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
\{u_{k}^{(n)}\}_{k=1}^{N}
be the sequence given in (
6.6
). For all
k=0,\ldots,N
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the following inequality holds

\|u_{k}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\theta_{k},

where the sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
is defined as in (
6.4
).

Proof.

The scalar sequence
\{\theta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
from Lemma
6.5
satisfies the recursive relation

\theta_{k}+\lambda_{k}\theta_{k}^{q}=\theta_{k-1}\mbox{ for }k=1,\ldots,N,\quad\text{with}\quad\theta_{0}=\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Equivalently,
\theta_{k}=\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}^{-1}(\theta_{k-1})
for
k=1,\ldots,N
.

We proceed by induction on
k
. For the base case
k=0
, clearly,

\|u_{0}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}=\theta_{0}.

Assume now by induction that
M_{k}^{(n)}=\|u_{k}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\theta_{k}
for every
n
. From Lemma
6.6
, we already have that

M_{k+1}^{(n)}=\|u_{k+1}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{k+1}^{(n)}.

By applying the induction hypothesis and using the monotonicity of
\psi_{q,\lambda_{k}}^{-1}
, we obtain

\|u_{k+1}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\beta_{k+1}^{(n)}=\psi_{q,\lambda_{k+1}}^{-1}(M_{k}^{(n)})\leq\psi_{q,\lambda_{k+1}}^{-1}(\theta_{k})=\theta_{k+1}.

Thus, by induction, for all
k=0,\ldots,N
,

\|u_{k}^{(n)}\|_{\infty}\leq\theta_{k},

independently of
n
.
∎

6.3.2
Positivity of solutions in the case
q\in(1,+\infty)

Let
u_{0}\geq 0
with
u_{0}\not\equiv 0
. For
\epsilon>0
, let
u_{\epsilon}
be the
\epsilon
-approximate solutions in (
3.3
) defined on the entire graph
G
. As explained in Remark
6.4
, by construction, we have

u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}(t,x)\leq u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\quad\text{and}\quad u_{\epsilon}(t,x)=\lim_{n\to\infty}u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}(t,x),\quad\text{for every }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times X\,.

(6.8)

For every fixed
n
, consider on the finite graph
G_{\operatorname{dir},n}
the “mild” solution
u^{(n)}
to which the sequence
\{u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}\}_{\epsilon}
converges in
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
for all
t\in[0,T]
. For finite graphs, mild, strong and classic solutions are equivalent. Since
q\in(1,+\infty)
, from Theorem
[
10
, Theorems 4.7-4.8]
,
u^{(n)}>0
in
X_{n}
and therefore there exists
\epsilon=\epsilon(n)
such that for every
0<\epsilon\leq\epsilon(n)
it holds

u^{(n)}_{\epsilon}(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}u^{(n)}(t,x)>0\quad\text{for all }(t,x)\in[0,T]\times X_{n}.

Fix
x\in X
and
t\in[0,T]
. Let
X_{n_{0}}
be the first element of the exhaustion
\{X_{n}\}_{n}
such that
x\in X_{n_{0}}
. By the inequality above, recalling that the sequence
\{u_{\epsilon}\}_{\epsilon}
converges to the mild solution
u
in
\ell^{p}(X,\mu)
for all
t\in[0,T]
, we get the following pointwise estimate:

u(t,x)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\geq\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}u^{(n_{0})}_{\epsilon}(t,x)\geq\frac{1}{2}u^{(n_{0})}(t,x)>0.

This shows the positivity of the mild solution
u
for all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times X
.

6.3.3
Proof of Theorem
6.3

We first prove the statement
1)
. Fix
\epsilon>0
. As in Remark
6.4
and in Section
6.3.2
, we denote by
\{u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}\}_{n}
the sequence of
\epsilon
-approximate solutions constructed on each finite Dirichlet subgraph
G_{\mathrm{dir},n}
, and by
u_{\epsilon}
the
\epsilon
-approximate solution defined on the entire graph
G
.

Let
\theta_{\epsilon}
be the piecewise-constant interpolant defined in Lemma
6.5
. Lemma
6.7
ensures that
0\leq u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}(t,x)\leq\theta_{\epsilon}(t)
for all
n
and all
(t,x)\in[0,T]\times X_{n}
, which by (
6.8
) implies

0\leq u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\leq\theta_{\epsilon}(t)

for all
t\in[0,T]
, locally uniformly in
x\in X
. For fixed
t\in[0,T]
, given that the mild solution
u
satisfies

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\|u(t)-u_{\epsilon}(t)\|_{p}=0\,,

it follows that

0\leq u(t,x)=\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}u_{\epsilon}(t,x)\leq\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\theta_{\epsilon}(t)=\theta(t)\quad\text{for all }x\in X,

where the last equality comes from Lemma
6.5
.

Next we turn to the statement
2)
. In this case
q\in(1,+\infty)
and we need to replace
\theta
with

\theta(t)=\bigl[\frac{1}{M^{q-1}}+(q-1)t\bigr]^{-\frac{1}{q-1}},\qquad M=\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}.

Then, all estimates in the proof of
1)
follow in the same way with minor changes. The positivity has already been proved in Section
6.3.2
.
\Box

Remark 6.8
.

We observe that, under the assumption of Theorem
3.6
, the proof of extinction in finite time when
q\in(0,1)
can be adapted to the case of a sign changing initial datum
u_{0}
in the following way. Consider the sequences
\{u_{k}^{(n),+}\}_{k}
and
\{v_{k}^{(n),-}\}_{k}
defined recursively by (
6.6
) with starting value, respectively
u_{0}^{+}\geq 0
and
-u_{0}^{-}\geq 0
(see (
4.17
)). Namely,

\begin{cases}(\operatorname{id}+\lambda_{k}(F+\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}))u_{k}^{(n),+}=u_{k-1}^{(n),+}&\text{for }k=1,\dots,N,\\[6.0pt]
u^{(n),+}_{0}=\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}^{+}\end{cases}

(6.9)

and

\begin{cases}(\operatorname{id}+\lambda_{k}(F+\Delta_{\textnormal{dir},n}))v_{k}^{(n),-}=v_{k-1}^{(n),-}&\text{for }k=1,\dots,N,\\[6.0pt]
v^{(n),-}_{0}=-\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}^{-}.\end{cases}

(6.10)

Applying Lemma
6.7
, we infer that
u_{k}^{(n),+}\leq\theta_{k}
and
v_{k}^{(n),-}\leq\theta_{k}
. Furthermore, since
\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}^{+}\geq\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}
and
-\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}^{-}\geq\bm{\pi}_{n}u_{0}
, from Corollary
4.4
applied recursively, we also get that
u_{k}^{(n)}\leq u_{k}^{(n),+}
and
u_{k}^{(n)}\leq v_{k}^{(n),-}
. Finally, we set
u_{k-1}^{(n),-}=-v_{k-1}^{(n),-}
and we conclude that

-\theta_{\epsilon}(t)\leq u_{\epsilon}^{(n),-}(t,x)\leq u_{\epsilon}^{(n)}(t,x)\leq u_{\epsilon}^{(n),+}(t,x)\leq\theta_{\epsilon}(t).

Hence the conclusion follows as in the proof of Theorem
3.6
.

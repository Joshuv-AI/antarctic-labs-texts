Oscillons from Q Q -balls through renormalization
F. Blaschke, T. Romańczukiewicz, K. Sławińska, A. Wereszczyński

Abstract
Using a renormalization-inspired perturbation expansion we show that oscillons in a generic field theory in (1+1) dimensions arise as dressed Q Q -balls of a universal (up to the leading nonlinear order) complex field theory. This theory reveals a close similarity to the integrable complex sine-Gordon model which possesses exact multi- Q Q -balls. We show that excited oscillons, with characteristic modulations of their amplitude, are two-oscillons bound states generated from a two Q Q -ball solution.

II
RGPE method

The RGPE method introduced in
Chen:1994zza
;
Chen:1995ena
is in general executed as follows:

•

Insert a naive perturbation series into the equations of motion that corresponds to small-amplitude expansions in fields. The leading order equations should be, therefore, linear wave equations corresponding to field fluctuations around a chosen vacuum.

•

Insert a monochromatic wave solution as a starting point of the series with some (typically complex)
bare
amplitude
A_{0}
.

•

Solve the naive perturbation series order by order until a resonant (secular) term is encountered, indicating a breakdown of the perturbation expansion at a particular
cutoff
scale.

•

Redefine the bare amplitude
A_{0}
in terms of a
dressed
amplitude
A
in such a way that an artificial
renormalization
scale is introduced, while the cutoff scale is absorbed into the definition of
A
.

•

Derive the renormalization group equations (RGEs) as consistency conditions that the solution is independent on the renormalization scale and on the particular form of the secular terms to a given order.

•

Solve RGE and set the renormalization scale in such a way that all secular terms in the expansion are removed, i.e., choose a subtraction scheme. This gives a renormalized solution that is a global approximation of the true solution valid in some range of amplitudes.

III
Oscillon from
Q
-balls

Let us apply the RGPE algorithm to the relativistic (1+1)-dimensional scalar field theory with a generic potential having a minimum at
\phi=0

V(\phi)=\frac{\phi^{2}}{2}-a_{3}\frac{\phi^{3}}{3}-a_{4}\frac{\phi^{4}}{4}+...,

(1)

where
a_{3},a_{4},...\in\mathbb{R}
. We insert a naive, small-amplitude perturbation expansion, i.e.

\phi=\varepsilon\phi_{1}+\varepsilon^{2}\phi_{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\phi_{3}+\ldots\,,

(2)

into the equation of motion

\partial^{2}\phi+\partial V/\partial\phi=0
,
where
\partial^{2}\equiv\partial_{t}^{2}-\partial_{x}^{2}
.
Here
\varepsilon
is a book-keeping parameter.
This produces a hierarchy of equations

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial^{2}+1\bigr)\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0\,,

(3a)

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial^{2}+1\bigr)\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{3}\phi_{1}^{2}\,,

(3b)

\displaystyle\bigl(\partial^{2}+1\bigr)\phi_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2a_{3}\phi_{1}\phi_{2}\,+a_{4}\phi_{1}^{3},

(3c)

Solving order by order we find

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle A_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}+\mathrm{c.c.}\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\phi_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{a_{3}}{3}A_{0}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\theta}+a_{3}A_{0}\bar{A}_{0}+\mathrm{c.c.}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\phi_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\alpha A_{0}^{3}\mathrm{e}^{3\mathrm{i}\theta}+\beta A_{0}^{2}\bar{A}_{0}\,\mathcal{S}(\theta,\bar{\theta})\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}+\mathrm{c.c.}\,,

(6)

where
\theta\equiv Qx-\Omega t
,
A_{0}
is a bare amplitude and
\Omega=\sqrt{Q^{2}+1}
. Furthermore
\alpha=\frac{1}{24}\left(2a_{3}^{2}-3a_{4}\right)
and
\beta=10a_{3}^{2}/3+3a_{4}
. We also introduced an auxiliary variable

\bar{\theta}\equiv\Omega x-Qt
. Finally,
\mathcal{S}(\theta,\bar{\theta})
is the secular term, i.e. a solution to the equation

\bigl(\partial^{2}+1\bigr)\Bigl(\mathcal{S}(\theta,\bar{\theta})\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\Bigr)=\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\,.

(7)

Obviously, physics should not depend on the secular term
\mathcal{S}
, as it is just an artifact of the perturbation expansion. Indeed, we will promote this statement as a guiding principle for removing the ambiguity in setting up RG equations.

Thus, let us continue to execute the RGPE algorithm. The bare solution to the third order in
\varepsilon
reads

\displaystyle\phi_{B}=

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon A_{0}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}a_{3}}{3}A_{0}^{2}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\theta}+\varepsilon^{2}a_{3}|A_{0}|^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\alpha A_{0}^{3}\mathrm{e}^{3\mathrm{i}\theta}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{3}\beta A_{0}|A_{0}|^{2}\mathcal{S}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}+\mathrm{c.c.}

(8)

Let us now define a dressed amplitude
A\equiv A(\theta_{0},\bar{\theta}_{0})
in terms of renormalized scales
\theta_{0}
and
\bar{\theta}_{0}
as

A_{0}=A\Big(1-\beta\varepsilon^{2}\mathcal{S}_{0}(\theta_{0},\bar{\theta}_{0})|A|^{2}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\Big)\,.

(9)

This leads to the bare solution

\displaystyle\phi_{B}=

\displaystyle\ \varepsilon A\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}a_{3}}{3}A^{2}\mathrm{e}^{2\mathrm{i}\theta}+\varepsilon^{2}a_{3}|A|^{2}+\varepsilon^{3}\alpha A^{3}\mathrm{e}^{3\mathrm{i}\theta}

\displaystyle+\varepsilon^{3}\beta A|A|^{2}\bigl(\mathcal{S}-\mathcal{S}_{0}\bigr)\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}+\mathrm{c.c.}

(10)

The minimal subtraction scheme, where the entire secular term is removed once we set
\theta_{0}=\theta
and
\bar{\theta}_{0}=\bar{\theta}
, is given by
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathcal{S}
.

In order to remove the secular term, we need to ensure that the solution does not depend on the renormalization scales.
However, a demand that

\frac{\partial\phi_{B}}{\partial\theta_{0}}=\frac{\partial\phi_{B}}{\partial\bar{\theta}_{0}}=0\,,\hskip 11.38109pt\forall\,x,\,t\,,

(11)

leads to the RG equations

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A}{\partial\theta_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\varepsilon^{2}A\left|A\right|^{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\,,

(12a)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial A}{\partial\bar{\theta}_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\beta\varepsilon^{2}A\left|A\right|^{2}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}_{0}}{\partial\bar{\theta}_{0}}+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{3})\,,

(12b)

that do depend on the
\mathcal{S}_{0}
.

Going with the minimal choice,
\mathcal{S}_{0}=\mathcal{S}
, the only way to make the RG equation independent of the form of the secular term amounts to taking a combination of partial derivatives, i.e.,

\displaystyle 2\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial A}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\frac{\partial^{2}A}{\partial\theta_{0}^{2}}-\frac{\partial^{2}A}{\partial\bar{\theta}_{0}^{2}}=\beta\varepsilon^{2}A|A|^{2}\Bigl(2\mathrm{i}\frac{\partial\mathcal{S}}{\partial\theta_{0}}+\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{S}}{\partial\theta_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{S}}{\partial\bar{\theta}_{0}^{2}}\Bigr)=\beta\varepsilon^{2}A|A|^{2}\,,\hskip 11.38109pt\forall\,x,\,t,\,\mathcal{S}\,,

(13)

where in the last line we used Eq. (
7
). This can be further simplified by defining a new complex field

\Psi\equiv\sqrt{\frac{\beta}{2}}\varepsilon A\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\theta}\,,

(14)

for which, at this order of perturbation, the RG equation takes the following
universal
form

\partial^{2}\Psi+\Psi=2\Psi|\Psi|^{2}\,.

(15)

This equation of motion follows from the complex
\phi^{4}
Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=|\partial\Psi|^{2}-|\Psi|^{2}+|\Psi|^{4}\,,

(16)

and it possesses a stationary,
Q
-ball solution. To find it, we take
\Omega=1
and
Q=0
so that
\theta=t
and
\bar{\theta}=x
. Then, a single
Q
-ball solution of (
15

\Psi=\frac{\lambda}{\cosh(\lambda x)}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\omega t}\,,

(17)

where
\lambda
is a scale parameter and

The renormalized oscillon solution can be found via a

\phi_{R}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{\beta}}\Psi-\frac{2a_{3}}{3\beta}\Psi^{2}+\frac{2a_{3}}{\beta}|\Psi|^{2}+\frac{\alpha 2^{3/2}}{\beta^{3/2}}\Psi^{3}+\mathrm{c.c.}\,,

(18)

where the previously defined constants
\alpha
and
\beta
depend on the particular choice of the model.

This is our

-balls. Amazingly,

IV
Integrability and Amplitude Modulations

The next step is to observe that, around the vacuum, the universal RG
Q
-ball equation can be approximated by the
integrable complex sine-Gordon
(
\mathbb{C}
sG) equation

\bigl(\partial^{2}+1\bigr)\Psi=\Psi|\Psi|^{2}-\bar{\Psi}\frac{\partial_{\mu}\Psi\partial^{\mu}\Psi}{1-|\Psi|^{2}}\,.

(19)

The sense, in which this equation is “close” to RG equation (

\mathcal{L}_{\mbox{$\mathbb{C}$sG}}=\frac{1}{1-|\Psi|^{2}}\Bigl(\partial_{\mu}\bar{\Psi}\partial^{\mu}\Psi-|\Psi|^{2}+|\Psi|^{4}\Bigr)\,.

(20)

V
Summary

In this work, we have resolved long-standing, fundamental problems related to oscillons.

First of all, we have established a nontrivial, RG-based,
relation between oscillons and
Q
-balls
. An intimate relation between these objects has been conjectured for a long time. We have shown that this is indeed the case. However, unexpectedly, the underlying (hidden)
Q
-balls, which are seeds for the oscillons, follow, at least at
\epsilon^{3}
order, from a
universal complex field theory
derived via the RG flow. In other words, all generic oscillons emerge from the same
Q
-balls. Therefore, they should belong to the same universality class. We also identified other universality classes. They possess oscillons with qualitatively different properties.

The simplest, single
Q
-ball provides an approximation very similar to the standard FFHL expansion
Fodor:2008es
which very well reproduces dynamics of the simplest, unexcited i.e., unmodulated oscillons. However, due to a similarity of the RG oscillon equation to the integrable complex sine-Gordon theory, our approach naturally introduces a higher number of degrees of freedom associated with a higher number of
\mathbb{C}
sG
Q
-balls. This is a great improvement in comparison with
Fodor:2008es
allowing for an approximated analytical description of modulated oscillons.

As a consequence, we have resolved the long-standing issue of the
origin of the amplitude modulation
, which is the most characteristic feature of oscillons. An excited,
modulated oscillon is a bound state of two unmodulated oscillons
originating in a two
Q
-ball solution. The modulation is an effect of a nonlinear superposition of these two oscillons each with its own DoF, that is frequency. This confirms the recent proposal presented in

Blaschke:2024uec
.

The observed relation between oscillons and
Q
-balls suggests that various
Q
-ball phenomena (like, e.g., charge swapping
PS1
or superradiance
PS2
) may possess oscillon counterparts.

Finally, the fact that the universal
Q
-ball equation in the generic universality class is well approximated by an
integrable
field theory opens a plausible and novel way to explain the longevity of oscillons. Here, the obvious reason is the integrability. Since the
Q
-ball equation is only approximated by an integrable theory oscillons eventually decay. Undoubtedly, this should be further studied.

Approximated integrability gives also a path for investigation of quantum oscillons, which contrary to common belief
H
may be long living objects
J
.

Although our work focused on (1+1) dimensional oscillons there are no obstacles to applying this framework for higher dimensions.

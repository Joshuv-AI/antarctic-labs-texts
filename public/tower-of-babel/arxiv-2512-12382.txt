Spectral Barron spaces of vector-valued functions on compact groups
Yaogan Mensah and Isiaka Aremua

Abstract
. In this article, we study spectral Barron spaces whose elements are made up of some vector-valued functions on a compact group whose Fourier transforms admit a certain summability property. We investigate their functional properties and some continuous embeddings of these spaces with respect to other function spaces among which are Sobolev spaces of vector-valued functions and the space of bounded vector-valued functions on compact groups.



Keywords and phrases: compact group, Fourier transform, spectral Barron space, Assiamoua space.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 43A77, 43A32, 68T07.

1.
Introduction

Spectral Barron spaces are an emerging spaces of functions in functional analysis and approximation theory. They provide a rich framework for dealing with neural network approximation error. These spaces originated from the seminal papers by A.R. Barron
[
3
,
4
]
.
Artificial intelligence, through neural networks, is at the centre of most current research. The theory of spectral Barron spaces appear as one of the ideal framework for addressing approximation problems related to neural networks. Some recent works on spectral Barron spaces on euclidian spaces, precisely
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, are
[
5
,
7
,
10
,
15
]
. However, this list is far to be exhaustive.
In
[
10
]
, Liao and Ming proved the sharp embedding between the spectral Barron space and the Besov space with embedding constants independent of the input dimension. In
[
5
]
, Choulli, Lu and Takase conducted a systematic study of spectral Barron spaces on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with application to partial differential equations.

The aim of this work is to move beyond the framework of euclidean spaces and to put spectral Barron spaces as a core study in abstract and applied harmonic analysis. In this article, we study spectral Barron spaces of Banach space-valued functions on compact groups. To succeed in this study, we used the Fourier transformation of vector-valued functions on compact groups as formulated by Assiamoua and Olubummo
[
1
]
. We study functional properties of spectral Barron spaces and investigate some continuous embeddings of these spaces with respect to other function spaces such as Sobolev spaces of vector-valued functions and the space of bounded vector-valued functions on compact groups.

The rest of the paper is organized as
follows. Section
2
gathers preliminary notes on Fourier analysis of vector-valued functions on compact groups and Assiamou spaces. Section
3
contains the main results.

2.
Harmonic analysis of vector-valued functions on compact groups

Let
G
denote a compact group assumed to be Hausdorff once for all. A unitary representation of the group
G
on a Hilbert space
H
is a homomorphism
\sigma
from
G
into the group
U(H)
of unitary operators on
H
. The Hilbert space
H
is called the representation space of
\sigma
and often denoted
H_{\sigma}
. The dimension of
H_{\sigma}
is denoted by
d_{\sigma}
. The representation
\sigma
is said to be continuous if for each
\xi\in H_{\sigma}
, the mapping
G\rightarrow H_{\sigma},x\mapsto\sigma(x)\xi
is continuous. All the representations considered in this paper are assumed to be continuous.

The representation is said to be irreducible if the only closed subspaces of
H_{\sigma}
that are invariant by
\sigma
are the zero vector space and the Hilbert space
H_{\sigma}
itself.
It is well known that an irreducible unitary representation of a compact group is necessarily finite-dimensional.

Two unitary representations
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
are said to be (unitarily) equivalent if there exists a unitary operator
T:H_{\sigma_{1}}\rightarrow H_{\sigma_{2}}
such that

\forall x\in G,\,T\sigma_{1}(x)=\sigma_{2}(x)T.

In the sequel, let
\widehat{G}
denote the set of equivalent classes of unitary irreducible representations of
G
. The class of a representation
\sigma
is still denoted by
\sigma
for simplicity. For more details on representation of groups, we refer the book
[
8
]
.

Let
A
be a (complex) Banach space. Let
L^{p}(G,A),\,1\leq p<\infty
, denote the space of
A
-valued (classes of) functions on
G
that are strongly Bochner integrable. It is endowed with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{p}}=\left(\int_{G}\|f(x)\|_{A}^{p}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Also, let
L^{\infty}(G,A)
denote the space of
A
-valued measurable functions on
G
that are bounded almost everywhere, endowed with the essential sup-norm:

\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}=\mbox{esssup}(f)=\inf\{C>0:\|f(x)\|\leq C,\,\mbox{almost everywhere}\}.

Let
f\in L^{1}(G,A)
. The Fourier coefficient of
f
, denoted by
\widehat{f}(\sigma)
, is defined by (see
[
1
]
) :

(1)

\widehat{f}(\sigma)=\int_{G}f(x)\sigma(x)^{*}dx,\sigma\in\widehat{G}

where
\sigma(x)^{*}
is the adjoint of the operator
\sigma(x)
and the integration on
G
is taken with respect to its normalized Haar measure.
Each coefficient
\widehat{f}(\sigma)
is interpreted here as the sesquilinear mapping from
H_{\sigma}\times H_{\sigma}
into
A
such that

(2)

\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi,\eta)=\int_{G}\langle\sigma(x)^{*}\xi,\eta\rangle f(x)dx,\,\xi,\eta\in H_{\sigma}.

Consider an orthonormal basis
\{\xi_{1}^{\sigma},\cdots,\xi_{d_{\sigma}}^{\sigma}\}
of
H_{\sigma}
and define the matrix coefficients
u_{ij}^{\sigma}
by

u_{ij}^{\sigma}(x)=\langle\sigma(x)\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma}\rangle,\,x\in G.

The inversion formula is given as follows

(3)

f(x)=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})u^{\sigma}_{ij}(x),\,x\in G.

Let
\mathscr{S}(H_{\sigma}\times H_{\sigma},A)
denote the set of sesquilinear maps from
H_{\sigma}\times H_{\sigma}
into
A
. Set

\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G},A)=\prod\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}\mathscr{S}(H_{\sigma}\times H_{\sigma},A).

Now let us recall the definition of the Assiamoua spaces
\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G},A),\,1\leq p<\infty
. The space
\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G},A)
is defined to be the familly of sesquilinear maps
\left(\varphi(\sigma)\right)_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}\in\mathscr{S}(\widehat{G},A)
such that

(4)

\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\varphi(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{p}<\infty.

The space
\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G},A)
is equipped with the norm defined by

(5)

\|\varphi\|_{\mathscr{S}_{p}}=\left(\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\varphi(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{p}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

It has been proven that the Assiamoua spaces are complete spaces
[
11
]
. Particularly, the spaces
\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G},A),\,p=1,2
will be used in this article. The space
\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)
will form the foundation for defining the spectral Barron spaces which are the subject of this study.
Finally, to fully grasp additional properties of the Assiamoua spaces
\mathscr{S}_{p}(\widehat{G},A)
and their connection with the Fourier transform described above, we refer to the articles
[
1
,
11
,
13
]
.

3.
Main results

3.1.
Definition and examples

In this section, we list our main results. Mainly, we define the spectral Barron spaces, study their main functional properties and their connection with the Sobolev spaces associated with this framework.

Let
s
be a nonnegative real number. Let
\gamma:\widehat{G}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
be a measurable function.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
G
be a compact group and
A
a Banach space.
We call spectral Barron space (of order
s
) the set

(6)

\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)=\left\{f:G\rightarrow A:(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{f}\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)\right\}

equipped with the norm

(7)

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}=\|(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{f}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{1}}.

In order to give a concrete idea of spectral Barron spaces in our context, we provide two examples before moving further.

Example 3.2

(Spectral Barron spaces on the torus)
.

Let
\mathbb{T}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}
be the one-dimensional torus. It can be identified in a natural way with the interval
[0,1)
of the real line through the group isomorphism
e^{2i\pi x}\rightarrow x
. We know that
\mathbb{T}
is a compact abelian group. Thus, all its unitary irreducible representations are 1-dimensional. Its unitary dual
\widehat{\mathbb{T}}
is identified with the set
\mathbb{Z}
of integers. Let
A
denote a Banach space.
Here, the Fourier coefficients of
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T},A)
are given by

(8)

\widehat{f}(n)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}f(x)e^{-2i\pi nx}dx,\,n\in\mathbb{Z},

where the integration is understood in the sense of Bochner.
Let
\gamma
be any positive sequence indexed by
\mathbb{Z}
.
In this context, the spectral Barron space
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(\mathbb{T},A)
consists of all functions
f:\mathbb{T}\rightarrow A
such that

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}:=\sum\limits_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}(1+\gamma(n)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\|\widehat{f}(n)\|_{A}<\infty.

Example 3.3

(Spectral Barron spaces on
SU(2)
)
.

The linear group
SU(2)
consists of
2\times 2
-matrices
A
having coefficients in
\mathbb{C}
such that
\det(A)=1
and
A^{*}A=I
where
A^{*}
is the adjoint of
A
and
I
is the identity matrix. It is known that elements
x
of
SU(2)
are of the form

(9)

x=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
-\overline{\beta}&\overline{\alpha}\end{array}\right),\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{C},\mbox{ with }|\alpha|^{2}+|\beta|^{2}=1.

For
l\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}
, set

V_{l}=\left\{f\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2}]:f(z_{1},z_{2})=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2l}a_{k}z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{2l-k},\,a_{0},\cdots,a_{2l}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

There is a inner product on
V_{l}
defined by

(10)

\langle f,g\rangle=\left\langle\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2l}a_{i}z_{1}^{i}z_{2}^{2l-i},\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2l}b_{j}z_{1}^{j}z_{2}^{2l-j}\right\rangle=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{2l}k!(2l-k)!a_{k}\overline{b_{k}}.

Let
U(V_{l})
denote the set of unitary operators on
V_{l}
.
The unitary irreducible representations of
SU(2)
are the mappings
T_{l},\,l=0,\frac{1}{2},\frac{2}{2},\frac{3}{2},\cdots
constructed as follows (see
[
6
]
or
[
14
, Chapter 11]
) :

T_{l}:SU(2)\rightarrow U(V_{l})
with

(11)

\left(T_{l}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\beta\\
-\overline{\beta}&\overline{\alpha}\end{array}\right)f\right)(z_{1},z_{2})=f(\alpha z_{1}-\overline{\beta}z_{2},\beta z_{1}+\overline{\alpha}z_{2}),\quad f\in V_{l}.

Clearly, an orthonormal basis of
V_{l}
is the collection
\{p_{k}^{l}:k=0,\cdots,2l\}
where

p_{k}^{l}(z_{1},z_{2})=z_{1}^{k}z_{2}^{2l-k}
.
Thus, the dimension of the vector space
V_{l}
is
d_{l}=2l+1
. Let
A
be a Banach space.
The Fourier coefficient
\widehat{f}(l)
of
f\in L^{1}(SU(2),A)
is defined by

\widehat{f}(l)(p_{j}^{l},p_{i}^{l})=\int_{SU(2)}\langle T_{l}(x)^{*}p_{j}^{l},p_{i}^{l}\rangle f(x)dx.

In this context, the spectral Barron space
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(SU(2),A)
consists of functions
f:SU(2)\rightarrow A
such that

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}=\sum\limits_{l\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}_{0}}(2l+1)(1+\gamma(l)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2l}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{2l}\|\widehat{f}(l)(p_{j}^{l},p_{i}^{l})\|_{A}<\infty.

3.2.
Functional properties of spectral Barron spaces

The following result shows that the spectral Barron space of order zero is a complete space. The latter will serve as the basis to show that a spectral Barron space of any order is complete.

Theorem 3.4
.

The space
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
is complete.

Proof.

Let
\{f_{n}\}
be a Cauchy sequence in
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
. Then,
\{\widehat{f}_{n}\}
is a Cauchy sequence in
\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)
. However,
\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)
is a complete space. Therefore, there exists
\varphi\in\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)
such that
\{\widehat{f}_{n}\}
converges to
\varphi
in
\mathscr{S}_{1}(\widehat{G},A)
. Set
g=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\varphi
. Then,
g\in\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
. Moreover, since

\|f_{n}-g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}}=\|\widehat{f_{n}}-\varphi\|_{\mathscr{S}_{1}}
, then
\{f_{n}\}
converges to
g
in
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
.
∎

Now, we consider the operator
(I-\Delta)^{s}
defined by

(12)

\displaystyle(I-\Delta)^{s}f(x)

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})u_{i,j}^{\sigma}(x)

(13)

\displaystyle=\mathscr{F}^{-1}((1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}\widehat{f}),\quad f\in\mathfrak{B}^{2s}_{\gamma}(G,A).

A property of this operator contained in the result that will follow allows us to extend the completeness property of
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
to
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
s>0
. The operator
(I-\Delta)^{s}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathfrak{B}^{2s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
onto
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
.

Proof.

Clearly, for
f\in\mathfrak{B}^{2s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
, we have

\|(I-\Delta)^{s}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}}=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{2s}_{\gamma}}
and the injectivity of the operator
(I-\Delta)^{s}
follows.
Now, let
g
be in
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
. Set
h=\mathscr{F}^{-1}\left(\displaystyle\frac{\widehat{g}}{(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}}\right)
. Then,
h\in\mathfrak{B}^{2s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
and
(1-\Delta)^{s}h=g
. Thus, the operator
(I-\Delta)^{s}
is surjective.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
s>0
. The space
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
is a complete space.

Proof.

Using Theorem
3.5
, we see that
(I-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}
is an isometric isomorphism from
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
onto
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
. Since
\mathfrak{B}^{0}_{\gamma}(G,A)
is complete, so is
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
.
∎

The following theorem is an interpolation result between spectral Barron spaces.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
0\leq r\leq t
. If
f\in\mathfrak{B}^{r}_{\gamma}(G,A)
, then
\forall s\in[r,t]
with
s=\alpha r+(1-\alpha)t,\,0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, we have

(14)

\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{r}_{\gamma}}^{\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}^{1-\alpha}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{\alpha r}{2}}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{(1-\alpha)t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{\alpha}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{r}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}\right)^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\times\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}\right)^{1-\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{r}_{\gamma}}^{\alpha}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}^{1-\alpha}.

∎

Let
s\geq 0
and
t\geq 0
. Consider a measurable function
a:\widehat{G}\rightarrow A
. Consider the pseudo-differential operator
\mathfrak{P}
with symbol
a
defined by

(15)

\mathfrak{P}f(x)=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}a(\sigma)\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})u_{i,j}^{\sigma}(x).

Theorem 3.8
.

If
(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s-t}{2}}a\in L^{\infty}(\widehat{G},A)
, then
\mathfrak{P}:\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}(G,A)\rightarrow\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
is a bounded linear operator.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|\mathfrak{P}f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{\mathfrak{P}f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}|a(\sigma)|\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s-t}{2}}|a(\sigma)|(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\|(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{\frac{s-t}{2}}a\|_{L^{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\|(1+\gamma(\cdot)^{2})^{\frac{s-t}{2}}a\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}.

The conclusion follows.
∎

In the following result, we need
A
to be a Banach algebra in order to have the possibility to multiply its elements. We recall that a Banach algebra is a Banach space
A
with an internal associative product which is compatible with vector space structure of
A
such that for all
x,y\in A
,

\|xy\|\leq\|x\|\|y\|
.

We assume, only in this part, that
A
is a Banach algebra. For
f,g\in L^{1}(G,A)
, the convolution product
f\ast g
is defined by

(16)

f\ast g(x)=\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)dy,\quad x\in G.

Then, it follows that
[
2
]
:

(17)

\widehat{f\ast g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d\sigma}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{k}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma}).

Theorem 3.9
.

Assume that
A
is a Banach algebra and that
\rho:=\sup\{d_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\widehat{G}\}<\infty
. If
f\in L^{1}(G,A)
and
g\in\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
, then
f\ast g\in\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
and

\|f\ast g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}\leq\rho\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\left\|\widehat{f\ast g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{k}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{k}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}\left\|\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}.

Set
\Gamma=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f\ast g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}
.
This implies,

\displaystyle\Gamma

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{k}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}\left\|\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\|\widehat{f}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}^{2}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\rho\|\widehat{f}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{\infty}}\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{g}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{k}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\rho\|\widehat{f}\|_{\mathscr{S}_{\infty}}\|g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\rho\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}<\infty.

That is,
f\ast g
belongs to
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
and
\|f\ast g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}\leq\rho\|f\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}.

∎

3.3.
Continuous embedding results for spectral Barron spaces

The spectral Barron spaces embed continuously in other function spaces.
The first result states continuous embedding between spectral Barron spaces.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
0\leq s<t
. Then,
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
embeds continuously into
\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}(G,A)
and

\forall f\in\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A),\,\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

The proof is trivial by noting that since
0\leq s<t
and
1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2}>1
, then

(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}<(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}.

∎

Now, we will connect the spectral Barron space with the Sobolev space
H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
of vector-valued functions on a compact group studied in the article
[
12
]
. Let us recall that this Sobolev space is defined as follows:

(18)

H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)=\left\{f\in L^{2}(G,A):(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\widehat{f}\in\mathscr{S}_{2}(\widehat{G},A)\right\}.

In other words,
f\in L^{2}(G,A)
belongs to
H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
if

\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{2}<\infty.

Naturally, the norm on
H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
is given by

(19)

\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma}}=\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

The following theorem provides a sufficient condition for the continuous embedding of Sobolev spaces into spectral Barron spaces.

Theorem 3.11
.

Let
0\leq s<t
.
If
\kappa:=\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{t-s}<\infty
, then
H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
embeds continuously into
\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}(G,A)
and

\forall f\in H^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A),\,\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}\leq\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{t}_{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{t-s}{2}}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{t-s}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\|_{A}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle(\mbox{by the H\"{o}lder inequality})

\displaystyle=\kappa^{\frac{1}{2}}\|f\|_{H^{s}_{\gamma}}.

∎

The following theorem states the continuous embedding of
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
into
L^{\infty}(G,A)
.

Theorem 3.12
.

Let
s\geq 0
. Then,
\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A)
embeds continuously into
L^{\infty}(G,A)
and

\forall f\in\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}(G,A),\,\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\|f(x)\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\left\|\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})u_{ij}^{\sigma}(x)\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}|u_{ij}^{\sigma}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle\leq\sum\limits_{\sigma\in\widehat{G}}d_{\sigma}(1+\gamma(\sigma)^{2})^{\frac{s}{2}}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{d_{\sigma}}\left\|\widehat{f}(\sigma)(\xi_{j}^{\sigma},\xi_{i}^{\sigma})\right\|_{A}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}.

Thus,
\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathfrak{B}^{s}_{\gamma}}
.
∎

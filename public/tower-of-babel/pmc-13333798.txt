Suspicious detection in road traffic based on Kd-SLIC-K-means and SBAF-CNN using aerial view surveillance video
Authors: Raj Kumar Patra, Saurabh Bhattacharya, Md Rashid Mahmood, Naresh Kumar Kar, Parul Dubey, Rohit Raja, Sonu Agrawal
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper provides a suspicious activity detection framework in road traffic based on aerial surveillance video, which combines Kd-SLIC-K-means clustering and SBAF-CNN. The suggested technique will be used to improve object segmentation and classification to give better detection results. The aerial traffic datasets are experimentally evaluated and the model has an accuracy of 98.97, which is better than the current methods. The findings show that the suggested approach can help to detect suspicious patterns in traffic situations. Nevertheless, the model has been tested under certain conditions and additional tests on large scale and diverse data sets are needed to establish its generalizability in real world scenarios.Keywords: Traffic anomaly detection, Saha-bora activation function-convolutional neural network (SBAF-CNN), Kendall distance- simple linear iterative clustering- K-means (Kd-SLIC-K-means), Distance surface block matching (DistSurf-BM), Regula falsi integro differential operator (RF-IDO), Grab cut algorithm (GCA), Tilt angle of horizontal gradient guided filter (THG-GF), Aerial view surveillance, And accident detectionSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Transportation is the movement of living and non-living things from one place to another1. Safe transportation plays a crucial role owing to the population explosion and increase in the number of vehicles2. Therefore, traffic surveillance has been established for preventing accidents and crimes to manage transport. Most significantly, roadways must be monitored since they are utilized by a huge number of people compared to other ways of transport owing to their availability3,4. It is observed that major accidents happen on the road, thus increasing the mortality rate worldwide. So, the road traffic is managed via surveillance camera video for the safety of passengers. Aerial surveillance that records the anomalies of the road is done to capture vast road areas for transport management5.

An abnormal situation that has happened on the road instead of being normal is named an outlier or anomaly. Moreover, this abnormal condition is categorized into stalled, crashed, and suspicious objects6. Due to the complex traffic conditions, such as dense or sparse traffic and varying environmental conditions, namely rain, fog, mist, etc., checking the outlier continuously by humans is a tedious process7,8. Therefore, to effectively detect the anomaly, an automatic surveillance system is required. For automatically detecting collisions in roadways, many surveillance systems have been applied9. The anomalies in the surveillance video have been detected by machine learning methods, namely Support Vector Machine (SVM) and deep learning mechanisms, namely Convolutional Neural Networks (CNN)10. But still, these works have focused on the foreground of the images/frames obtained from the surveillance video. Considering every object present in the video is necessary since it might result in an anomaly11.

Moreover, anomaly detection has been studied by the prior works by considering only the outliers, which occurred and did not concentrate on the behavior of the human and the object, that is, separate studies are made for anomaly detection; however, both conditions are not considered12,13. For controlling future anomalies, vehicle detection, tracking, and behavior analysis are required to detect the pre-events14. Owing to the presence of low-quality video and improper coverage of the surveillance video, the object cannot be tracked by most of the top-notch methods15. Therefore, an aerial view that covers a vast place in surveillance assists in the detection of outliers easily. Grounded on these factors, a novel model is proposed for anomaly detection in aerial traffic surveillance video utilizing SBAF-CNN.

Despite promising results of recent deep learning models in the field of anomaly detection, aerial traffic monitoring in crowded conditions introduces several parallel issues, such as environmental degradation (fog, change of illumination), obstruction of objects, high density of vehicles, and the necessity of spatial and temporal behavior perception. All these heterogeneous factors may not be well disentangled using a single end-to-end model, especially when the amount of data available is small. Thus, the given work is based upon a multi-stage, but task-related modular structure in which each of the elements is aimed at combating a specific challenge in the pipeline. The method offered applies the image enhancement, object detection, segmentation, localization, motion estimation, and behavior analysis systematically in a coarse-to-fine fashion, and it is robust and interpretable. The modules are not merely a cascade of techniques, as they are sequentially dependent and synergistic with the output of one stage enhancing the reliability of the next stage.

Literature survey

Maqsood et al.16 propounded a 3D Convolutional Neural Network (3DConvNets) model for detecting anomalies present in the road utilizing the surveillance video. Followed by normalization as well as spatial augmentation, the videos were converted to frames and scaled. From the pre-processed frames, the features were subsequently extracted. Next, the features were classified by utilizing the 3DConvNet model. Anomalies, namely road accidents, arson, fights, crime, and explosions were detected. However, in the image, the presence of noise resulted in difficulty in finding small objects.

Chriki et al.17 established anomaly detection in road traffic models grounded on the Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAV) video. By utilizing two methods, namely CNN and Histogram of Oriented Gradient (HOG), the features were extracted. The extracted features were subsequently given to the One-Class Support Vector Machine (OCSVM) technique. The presence of outliers, namely vehicle anomalies recorded by the video was predicted by the classifier OCSVM. But, in this model, the normal frame was predicted as abnormal, which resulted in misclassification.

Pawar and Attar18 introduced a deep learning model to detect road accidents from traffic surveillance video. From the Closed Circuit Television (CCTV) camera fixed for traffic management, the videos were gathered. The vehicles present in the video were localized. For the classification, the Long Short Term Memory (LSTM) encoder and decoder method were adopted. The vehicles’ crashes on the roads were categorized accurately. Conversely, the stalled vehicles present on the road could not be detected by this model.

Pramanik et al.19 presented a traffic pre-event detection model grounded on real-time surveillance video. By integrating Speed Violation Detection (SVD), Overtaking Detection (OD), One-way Traffic Detection (OTD), Illegal Parking Detection (IPD), and Wrong Drop-off Location Detection (WDLD), the objects were tracked and then classified for anomalies. Therefore, the traffic pre-events were detected, and the respective corrective action was adopted. But, for outlier detection, the model concentrated only on a limited number of pre-events.

Pelvan et al.20 illustrated a video surveillance-based anomaly detection model. The video was converted to images, and by utilizing a convolutional autoencoder, the features were extracted. For classification, the extracted features were then sent to the Context Tree Based Anomaly Detection (CTBAD) method. The locational anomalies concerning road accidents were efficiently detected. Nevertheless, there was a mismatch during the testing and training of the model owing to the utilization of worldwide statistics data.

Bajgoti et al.21 assessed a Swin Transformer (ST) system for real-time anomaly detection on roads. The videos were converted to frames and reconstructed utilizing a skip connection. Then, by utilizing the ST method, the features were extracted and classified by the Simple Online and Real-time Tracking (SORT) technique. The reconstruction-based anomaly regarding tunable parameters was predicted by this model. Yet, the ST was sensitive to light reflections that affected the performance of the model.

Ata-Ur-Rehman et al.22 integrated detection of anomalous regions from the video frames concerning Particle Filtering (PF). The frames were classified into cell structures,subsequently, from the frame, the backgrounds were eliminated for anomaly detection. By utilizing the PF method, the posterior probability was found. Therefore, the video frames with multi and single-vehicle crash anomaly activities were predicted. However, the model had various limitations, namely high processing time and real-time anomalies that could not be detected by this work.

Chen et al.23,24, established a conflict analysis model in the mixed traffic utilizing UAV images. By utilizing the HOG model, peculiar features like vehicle type, velocity, position, and trajectory were extracted. By utilizing the Kalman filter, the vehicle trajectories were found,also, an SVM was utilized for the classification. The conflict location and conflict time were efficiently predicted. Yet, this model could not detect the vehicle blocked by another vehicle in the road traffic.

Qasim and Verdu25 propounded a video anomaly detection system utilizing deep convolutional models and recurrent techniques. By using the Simple Recurrent Unit (SRU), the temporal features were gathered, and the deep CNN was utilized as a classifier. From the input video’s images, normal and abnormal actions, such as shoplifting, arson, burglary, assault, accident, stealing, and explosion were detected. Yet, small objects like humans or animals, which could not be seen clearly in the video, cannot be detected.

Chen et al.23,24 established a bidirectional prediction model for road traffic anomaly detection. From the input video’s frame, the foreground was extracted. For detecting the type of outliers present in the frame, the Generative Adversarial Network (GAN) was utilized as a bidirectional network. The target anomaly was predicted by this model via forward and backward subnetworks. This model identified the anomalies, which occurred on the road. Apart from the detection, the motion features could not be achieved, which gave poor performance in achieving motion anomaly detection.

Comparative analysis

The comparative analysis has been further narrowed down to clearly place the proposed methodology on the recent developments made in the field of remote sensing, object detection, and deep learning-based classification methods. Gomaa and Saad26 recently proposed a selective channel attention network with residual learning SCAR-Net, which can be applied to high-resolution remote sensing scene classification. This method can better represent features and classify better, but is primarily aimed at classification tasks and fails to explicitly address such domains as domain adaptation or dataset imbalance.

Gomaa27 proposed a sophisticated domain adaptation method with semi-automated dataset creation and an improved YOLOv8 backbone to address the problem of domain variability. Despite the advantages of this approach in increasing the detection performance in various domains, it comes with extra computation complexity because of the dataset generation and preprocessing stages. Gomaa and Abdalrazik28 also reported a similar method that employs a variant of YOLOv4, which enhances the detection accuracy but has weaknesses in generalization in different environments. Prior research, including Gomaa et al.29 and Gomaa et al.30, has concentrated on vehicle detection and tracking in aerial videos through conventional image processing methods, like morphological operations and motion detection. Although these methods are computationally efficient, they do not perform well with complex situations that require managing occlusion, change in scale, and change in illumination as compared to the current deep learning-based methods.

Salem et al.31 used deep learning to identify building damage caused by earthquakes using remote sensing information in the disaster assessment context. Despite its success in certain applications, the method is very task-oriented and might not be generalizable to other datasets and settings. Transformer based architectures are also considered recent innovations in deep learning, including the driver drowsiness detection system by Hassan et al.32. These models are very accurate, but they often demand large computational resources, which means that they cannot be used in real time or resource limited scenarios.

Moreover, Pandey et al.33 suggested a deep learning-based system to classify and detect garbage on aerial photographs based on the surrounding data. Though the mechanism is better at classification due to the ability to utilize visual context, it does not have more sophisticated feature fusion and attention processes that can be effective in processing complex scenes. Also, it is necessary to mention that certain works by Gomaa and colleagues are also applied in the domain of antenna design as it is used in GNSS27,34,35. Although these studies have a substantial impact in the sphere of wireless communication, they do not directly correspond to approaches to image analysis based on deep learning.

Limitations of the study

The model proposed has been tested on a small aerial surveillance dataset, and it might not be entirely applicable to various real-world traffic conditions. The dataset is not exhaustive in covering the changes in weather conditions, light conditions, and traffic density, which can influence model performance in real-world applications. Without additional optimization, the SBAF-CNN model has a high computational complexity which can limit its applicability to real-time use. The method is purposefully aerial view surveillance data, and might need modification to ground-based or multi-view camera systems. The model has been experimented under controlled conditions, and its behavior in large scale, dynamic and real life scenarios is yet to be tested. The challenges in real-time system integration and deployment which include, latency and hardware constraints have not been specifically tackled in the model suggested.

Rationale and synergistic design of the proposed framework

The proposed framework is comprised of various modules but not randomly added, but each of them has a particular limitation that can be identified in the aerial traffic anomaly detection. It is explained that the synergy between the modules is as follows:

THG-GF (Transmission Refinement): Aerial videos tend to be affected by the environmental interference e.g. fog, rain as well as light changes. The module boosts the visibility and feature quality which is vital to downstream detection tasks (Fig. 2).

Architecture of SBAF-CNN.

YOLO-based Object Detection: This serves as a feature extraction backbone, which makes sure that relevant regions get more processing. The YOLOv5 model is utilized as a ready-trained object detection system to detect vehicles and traffic agents in every image. In this project, YOLOv5 is adopted as an off-the-shelf component that is not adjusted to its architecture. YOLO is only tasked with the extraction of spatial object-level tasks like bounding box coordinates and confidence scores of the object. The features are then given to the SBAF-CNN classifier to detect anomalies at the first stage. Thus, YOLO is merely a provider of features in the proposed pipeline and should not be viewed as a key contribution made by this work.

SBAF-CNN (Initial Anomaly Detection): Detects coarse anomalies at the object level using spatial features. This stage reduces the search space for further fine-grained analysis.

Kd-SLIC-K-means (Superpixel Segmentation): In traffic with heavy traffic the boundaries of objects collide. Region level grouping is made possible by the super pixel generation, enhancing the separation of vehicles which are closely spaced.

RF-IDO (Localization Refinement): Enhances precise localization of vehicles by converting non-linear localization into a linear approximation, reducing positional ambiguity.

DistSurf-BM (Optical Flow Estimation): Identifies the motion dynamics and temporal behavior as key elements of identifying suspicious activities as opposed to merely determining anomalies at rest.

SBAF-CNN (Final Classification): Combines structural similarity and behavioral features to perform fine-grained classification (stalled, crash, suspicious activity).

In general, the scheme adheres to the strategy of progressive refinement, which considers the enhancement of the low-level to the object-level, to region-level segmentation, to motion-level understanding, and finally to behavior-level classification. This systematic incorporation makes every module to be complementary of the other in enhancing robustness in a complex aerial traffic setting. Although end-to-end deep learning models such as 3D CNNs and Transformers have shown promising results, they typically require large-scale annotated datasets and may not explicitly handle challenges such as environmental degradation, dense occlusion, and fine-grained behavior analysis in aerial traffic scenarios.

However, the suggested multi-stage model breaks the problem down into smaller manageable sub-tasks such as image enhancement, object detection, segmentation, localization, and motion analysis. The modules are to fulfill a certain limitation, and their combination is planned in a gradual improvement approach. This modular style enhances interpretability, strong performance and robustness especially when a limited data condition is being tested.

Ablation analysis of proposed framework

The suggested model comprises a series of steps that will end up fine-tuning anomaly detection. The need of each of the modules is determined in the following way. The GrabCut and THG-GF preprocessing phases enhance foreground extraction and minimize the background noise and this increases the reliability of object detection. The object detection module that is based on YOLO offers spatial localization of vehicles and other participants of the traffic. In the absence of this step, the process of anomaly detection can be less efficient because of the lack of object-level data. The DistSurf-BM optical flow estimation module obtains the motion dynamics between successive frames and this is crucial in the detection of abnormal movement dynamics, e.g. sudden stop or collision. The Kd-SLIC-K-means clustering step is an additional process of refining the motion area and eliminating inappropriate motion noise. The RF-IDO module does the anomaly localization and is used to identify candidate abnormal areas upon the optimization of the densities.

Lastly, hierarchical anomaly detection is done by the two-stage SBAF- CNN classification framework. The initial step detects suspicious frames, and the second step is the fine-grained suspicious activity that uses the characteristics of structural similarities and behavior. This enables the elimination of false alarms because this multi-stage refinement enables detection to be more accurate than a single-stage model. In general, all modules are part of progressive improvements and the elimination of steps will impair the localization accuracy, the consistency of motion and the ultimate classification of anomalies. In order to assess the contribution of the individual modules, an ablation-type analysis of the suggested framework is conducted. The baseline model includes the SBAF-CNN classifier based on spatial features only. Then, the DistSurf-BM module is introduced to bring the motion information into the algorithm, which enhances the detection of anomalies by recording abnormal movement patterns. The Kd-SLIC-K-means clustering step further smooths motion areas and eliminates motion pixels that are noisy. The RF-IDO module also helps to improve the localization of anomalies by finding dense areas of abnormalities. Lastly, the entire structure brings all modules together in a bid to enhance detecting performance.

The sequential module integration method enhances the detection accuracy since the motion estimation technique gives less missed anomalies, the clustering module increases region consistency, and localization gives a better quality of classifier input. The multi-stage architecture is effective as the entire implementation of the system performs optimally when it is incomplete.

Implementation details

The proposed framework was implemented in Python and TensorFlow in order to use the SBAF-CNN classifiers. The network is made up of convolutional layers with ReLU activation, max-pooling layers and classification fully connected layers. The initial layer is the convolution layer which has 32 filters of 3 × 3 size and the second layer is the convolution layer with 64 filters of 3 × 3 size. After every convolution block, there is a max-pooling layer with the size of 2 × 2. The features obtained are flattened and fed into 128 neuron fully connected layers and finally a softmax classification layer.

The optimizer that was used in training the models was the Adam optimizer and the learning rate was 0.001. The number of batch was set to 32 and training was done over 50 epochs. The loss function was categorical cross-entropy. The dropout rate of 0.5 was used to minimize overfitting. This was implemented with Deep Learning Toolbox in MATLAB 2022a. The experiments were carried out on an i7 processor (Intel) with 16 GB RAM. The two SBAF-CNN classifiers were independently trained under the same training set up except that different input features were used.

Conclusion

This paper introduces a new method of suspicious detection in road traffic by using a hybrid model of Kd-SLIC-K-means clustering and SBAF-CNN. The suggested approach can enhance the quality of the segmentation and the accuracy of the classification, which can be used to better identify the abnormal patterns in aerial surveillance videos. It is shown that the model can perform competitively with the current methods in experimental results, which shows that it can be effectively used in intelligent traffic monitoring systems. The model performance is, however, affected by the nature of the dataset and operating conditions. On the whole, the given solution offers a good perspective of automated traffic monitoring. The next steps in work will be to optimize models, evaluate them on large real-life data sets and consider the possibility of real-time deployment. The weaknesses of the work proposed have been clearly addressed to give a realistic analysis of its applicability.

Future work

Yet, the proposed method concentrated on limited parameters for suspicious detection. Thus, this work will be integrated in the future to concentrate on the detection process of occlusion of objects, espionage, transportation, and emergency management in traffic surveillance to further enhance the model’s consistency and performance.

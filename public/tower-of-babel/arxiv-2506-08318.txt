Symmetry and symmetry breaking in interpolation inequalities for two-dimensional spinors – Preliminary results
Jean Dolbeault, Rupert L. Frank, Jonte Weixler

Abstract
. On the two-dimensional Euclidean space, we study a spinorial analogue of the Caffarelli-Kohn-Nirenberg inequality involving weighted gradient norms. This (SCKN) inequality is equivalent to a spinorial Gagliardo-Nirenberg type interpolation inequality on a cylinder as well as to an interpolation inequality involving Aharonov-Bohm magnetic fields, which was analyzed in a paper of 2020. We examine the symmetry properties of optimal functions by linearizing the associated functional around radial minimizers. We prove that the stability of the linearized problem is equivalent to the positivity of a 2 × 2 2\times 2 matrix-valued differential operator. We study the positivity issue via a combination of analytical arguments and numerical computations. In particular, our results provide numerical evidence that the region of symmetry breaking extends beyond what was previously known, while the threshold of the known symmetry region is linearly stable. Altogether, we obtain refined estimates of the phase transition between symmetry and symmetry breaking. Our results also put in evidence striking differences with the three-dimensional (SCKN) inequality that was recently investigated.

With

2.
From 2D-spinor to Aharonov–Bohm

This section is devoted to the proof of our main analytical result.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

We divide the proof in three steps.

\bullet

Step 1.
Let us denote by
C_{\alpha,p}^{\rm scalar}
the best constant in the inequality

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})\psi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\,dx\geq C_{\alpha,\beta}^{\rm scalar}\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}\,,

for
scalar
(that is,
{\mathbb{C}}
-valued) fields on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We claim that

C_{\alpha,p}=C_{\alpha,p}^{\rm scalar}\,.

We write
\phi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{\rm T}
with scalar fields
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
. By definition of the Pauli matrices, we have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\phi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})\varphi_{1}|^{2}+|(\partial_{1}-i\partial_{2})\varphi_{2}|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\,dx

Noting that
|(\partial_{1}-i\partial_{2})\varphi_{2}|=|(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})\overline{\varphi_{2}}|
, we find that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\sigma\cdot\nabla\phi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\,dx\geq C_{\alpha,\beta}^{\rm scalar}\left(\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\varphi_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\overline{\varphi_{2}}|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}\right).

By the triangle inequality in
\mathrm{L}^{p/2}
, we have

\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\varphi_{1}|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}+\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\overline{\varphi_{2}}|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}\geq\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{(|\varphi_{1}|^{2}+|\overline{\varphi_{2}}|^{2})^{p/2}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}=\left(\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\phi|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx\right)^{2/p}.

This proves that
C_{\alpha,\beta}\geq C_{\alpha,\beta}^{\rm scalar}
. The reverse inequality is trivial.
Equality in the triangle inequality implies that
\phi
for
C_{\alpha,p}
are necessarily of the form

\phi=\psi\chi_{0}\,.

where
\chi_{0}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
is a constant spinor.
Since optimizers achieve equality everywhere, this shows that optimizers
\phi
for
C_{\alpha,p}
are necessarily of that form where
\psi
is an optimizer for
C_{\alpha,p}^{\rm scalar}
.

\bullet

Step 2.
For a spinor field
\psi
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
in the natural Sobolev space associated to (
SCKN
) we introduce an Emden Fowler coordinate transfomation and define a transformed spinor field
\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1},\,\mathbb{C}^{2})
by

\psi(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)=r^{\alpha}\phi(\ln r,\theta)\,.

Then, taking the relation between
\alpha,\beta
and
p
into account,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{p}}{|x|^{\beta p}}\,dx=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\phi|^{p}\,ds\,d\theta\,.

Moreover,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|(\partial_{1}+i\partial_{2})\psi|^{2}}{|x|^{2\alpha}}\,dx=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\partial_{s}\phi+\alpha\phi-i\partial_{\theta}\phi|^{2}\,ds\,d\theta\,.

We claim that the right side is equal to

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(|\partial_{s}\phi|^{2}+|(-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)\phi|^{2}\right)ds\,d\theta\,.

To see this, we expand

|\partial_{s}\phi+\alpha\phi-i\partial_{\theta}\phi|^{2}=|\partial_{s}\phi|^{2}+|(-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)\phi|^{2}+2\re\langle\overline{\partial_{s}\phi},\ (-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}.

We need to show that the integral of the mixed term vanishes for all
\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}({\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1},\,\mathbb{C}^{2})
. First, we observe that

\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}\langle{\partial_{s}\phi},\ \phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds=\frac{1}{2}\re\int_{\mathbb{R}}\partial_{s}(|\phi|^{2})\,ds=0\,.

Next, by integrating by parts twice (once in
s
and once in
\theta
), we find

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\langle{\partial_{s}\phi},\ \partial_{\theta}\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds\,d\theta=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\langle{\partial_{\theta}\phi},\ \partial_{s}\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds\,d\theta\,.

Multiplying by
-i
and taking the real part gives

\displaystyle\re\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\langle{\partial_{s}\phi},\ (-i\partial_{\theta}\phi)\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\re\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\ \langle{(-i\partial_{\theta}\phi)},\ \partial_{s}\phi\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds\,d\theta

\displaystyle=-\re\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\langle{\partial_{s}\phi},\ (-i\partial_{\theta}\phi)\rangle_{{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\,ds\,d\theta\,.

Thus, also the second mixed term vanishes.

To summarize, we have shown that the
C_{\alpha,\beta}^{\rm scalar}
is the best constant in the spinorial inequality

\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(|\partial_{s}\phi|^{2}+|(-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)\phi|^{2}\right)ds\,d\theta\geq C_{\alpha,\beta}\left(\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\phi|^{p}\,ds\,d\theta\right)^{2/p}.

\bullet

Step 3.
We finally show that the Aharonov–Bohm inequality can be brought into the same form as the inequality at the end of the previous step. This follows by similar computations as in the previous step. We assume that
\psi
and
\tilde{\phi}
are related by

\psi(r\cos\theta,r\sin\theta)=\tilde{\phi}(\ln r,\theta)\,.

Then

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}|(-i\nabla-\alpha A)\psi|^{2}\,dx=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}\left(|\partial_{s}\tilde{\phi}|^{2}+|(-i\partial_{\theta}+\alpha)\tilde{\phi}|^{2}\right)ds\,d\theta

and

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{2}}\frac{|\psi|^{p}}{|x|^{2}}\,dx=\iint_{{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{S}}^{1}}|\tilde{\phi}|^{2}\,ds\,d\theta\,.

This completes the proof of the equivalence. ∎

Linear Instability Region

By considering linear perturbations of the optimizer of (

) restricted to symmetric spinors as,

Lemma 3

(Optimizer among Radially Symmetric Functions)
.

The optimal function for (
SCKN

log

) among all symmetric functions

\phi_{*}(s)\coloneqq\left(\frac{p\>\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\cdot\left(\cosh\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\alpha s\right)\right)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}\cdot\chi

Furthermore, the optimal constant among radially symmetric functions in

\displaystyle(2\pi)^{\frac{p}{2}-1}C_{\alpha,p}^{*}=\|\phi_{*}(s)\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R})}^{p-2}.

Since the set of radially symmetric functions is a subset smaller than the whole set it trivially holds that

Furthermore, the absolute value of

-\partial_{s}^{2}\varphi_{*}(s)+\alpha^{2}\varphi_{*}(s)-\varphi_{*}(s)^{p-1}=0\,.

Proof.

The proof goes back to Nagy
[

]

The

with the optimal radial constant for functions
\phi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{2})
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\phi]\coloneqq\underbrace{\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}\left|\partial_{s}\phi(s,\theta)\right|^{2}+\left|(\alpha-i\sigma_{3}\partial_{\theta})\phi(s,\theta)\right|^{2}dsd\theta}_{\eqqcolon\mathcal{A}[\phi]}-C_{\alpha,p}^{*}\underbrace{\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi(s,\theta)|^{p}dsd\theta\right)^{\frac{2}{p}}}_{\eqqcolon\mathcal{D}[\phi]}\,.

The quadratic form of the linearized problem for a
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{2})
is defined as

\mathcal{Q}[\varphi]\coloneqq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}\left(\mathscr{F}[\phi_{*}+\varepsilon\varphi]-\mathscr{F}[\phi_{*}]\right).

By Taylor expansion up until second order in

we report that

\mathcal{Q}[\varphi]=\norm{\partial_s \varphi}^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}+\norm{(-i\sigma_3\partial_\theta+\alpha) \varphi}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{2}\\
-\Bigg[\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi_{*}|^{p-2}|\varphi|^{2}dsd\vartheta+(p-2)\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi_{*}|^{p-4}|\real(\langle\phi_{*},\varphi\rangle)|^{2}dsd\vartheta\\
-\frac{p-2}{\norm{\phi_*}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1})}^{p}}\left(\iint_{\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1}}|\phi_{*}|^{p-2}\real(\langle\phi_{*},\varphi\rangle)dsd\vartheta\right)^{2}\Bigg]

If we obtain for some spinor
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{2})

Lemma 4
.

If the linearized problem linearly is unstable around the radially symmetric optimizer, then the optimal function of (

log

Proof.

Since

\mathcal{F}[\phi_{*}]=0

By our assumption of linear instability we can find a small

\mathcal{F}[\phi_{*}+\varepsilon\varphi]-\underbrace{\mathcal{F}[\phi_{*}]}_{=0}=\mathcal{A}[\phi_{*}+\varepsilon\varphi]-C_{\alpha,p}^{*}\cdot\mathcal{D}[\phi_{*}+\varepsilon\varphi]<0\,.

Since
\phi_{*}+\varepsilon\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{S}^{1},\mathbb{C}^{2})

SCKN

log

) holds, which yields a contradiction. The optimal constant must therefore be smaller than the optimal radial constant. Hence, the radial optimizer cannot be the global optimizer.

4.
Reformulation in a Gegenbauer Polynomial Basis

The goal of this section is to lay the theoretical groundwork to make the question of positive semi-definiteness of
A
defined in
5
amenable to numerical analysis.
We find two avenues of approach to a numerical spectral analysis of
A
. One would be a Birmann-Schwinger approach as done for example in
[
8
]
. We are currently implementing this.
The second one relies on reformulating the differential operator
A
as an infinite dimensional matrix in a basis of special functions. For this we use Gegenbauer polynomials as follows:

The differential operator
A
from Theorem
5
can be identified with a
2\times 2
block matrix
M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}&M_{2}\\
M_{3}&M_{4}\end{pmatrix}
where
M_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.
First define the numbers:

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{1}{2(k+\lambda)}\left(\frac{(k+1)(k+2\lambda))}{2(k+1+\lambda)}+\frac{(k-1+2\lambda)k}{2(k-1+\lambda)}\right)\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(k+1)(k+2)}{4(k+\lambda)(k+1+\lambda)}\,,

\displaystyle\eta_{k}^{0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\frac{(k-1+2\lambda)(k-2+2\lambda)}{4(k+\lambda)(k-1+\lambda)}\,.

Define the following matrices (infinite dimensional) written in the Gegenbauer polynomial basis
\left(C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)\right)_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
(using the definitions
\lambda=\frac{n-3}{2}
and
n:=\frac{2\,p}{p-2}
) :

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}1-\eta_{0}^{1}&0&-\eta_{2}^{0}&0&\dots&\\
0&1-\eta_{1}^{1}&0&-\eta_{3}^{0}&0&\dots\\
-\eta^{2}_{0}&0&1-\eta_{2}^{1}&0&-\eta_{4}^{0}&0&\dots\\
0&-\eta^{2}_{1}&0&1-\eta_{3}^{1}&0&-\eta_{5}^{0}&0&\dots\\
\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&0&\ddots&0&\ddots&0&\dots\end{pmatrix}=(1-z^{2})\,,

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{pmatrix}0&0&2\lambda&0&2\lambda&0&\ldots\\
0&1&0&(2+2\lambda)&0&(2+2\lambda)&\ldots\\
0&0&2&0&(4+2\lambda)&0&(4+2\lambda)&\ddots\\
0&\vdots&0&\ddots&0&\ddots&0&\ddots\end{pmatrix}=z\,\frac{d}{dz}\,,

the diagonalized ultraspherical operator for parameter
\lambda

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\coloneqq\text{diag}(k(k+2\lambda))=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&0&\dots\\
0&(1+2\lambda)&0&0&\dots\\
0&0&2(2+2\lambda)&0&\dots\\
0&0&0&3(3+2\lambda)&0&\dots\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots&\ddots\end{pmatrix}\,,

and

N\coloneqq\text{diag}\left(\frac{\pi\>2^{1-2\lambda}\Gamma(k+2\lambda)}{k!(k+\lambda)(\Gamma(\lambda))^{2}}\right)\,.

Then we use the Gegenbauer polynomial decomposition
\varphi_{1}(s)\overset{\text{id}}{=}\vec{w}_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}(s)\overset{\text{id}}{=}\vec{w}_{2}
to write
M
as a blockmatrix in the basis
(\vec{w}_{1},\vec{w}_{2})^{T}
as

M\coloneqq\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\frac{p\alpha}{(p-2)}\begin{pmatrix}N&0\\
0&N\end{pmatrix}\Bigg[\left(\frac{(p-2)\>\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}\begin{pmatrix}G(B+2A)&0\\
0&G(B+2A)\end{pmatrix}\\
+\begin{pmatrix}(1+2\alpha)\mathds{1}&0\\
0&(1-2\alpha)\mathds{1}\end{pmatrix}+\frac{p\>\alpha^{2}}{4}\begin{pmatrix}(2-p)\,G&(2-p)\,G\\
(2-p)\,G&(2-p)\,G\end{pmatrix}\Bigg]\,.

Theorem 6
.

With the above notation, if there is some
\ket{w}=(\vec{w}_{1},\vec{w}_{2})^{T}
such that

\bra{w}M\ket{w}_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2})}<0

then (
SCKN
) is linear instable.

Proof.

Recall that the function
\varphi_{*}(s)\coloneqq\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-2}}\left(\cosh\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\alpha s\right)\right)^{-\frac{2}{p-2}}
solves

-\partial_{s}^{2}\varphi_{*}+\alpha^{2}\,\varphi_{*}=\varphi_{*}^{p-1}\,.

(2)

Now define for
\varphi\in\mathrm{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R)}
the function
u
such that
\varphi=u\cdot\varphi_{*}
.
An expansion of the square then shows that

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi_{*}\,u^{\prime}+\varphi_{*}^{\prime}\,u|^{2}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{\left(u^{2}\right)^{\prime}\,\varphi_{*}\,\varphi_{*}^{\prime}}\,ds+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u|^{2}\left(\varphi_{*}^{\prime}\right)^{2}}\,ds\,.

Define the measure

d\mu_{k}\coloneqq|\varphi_{*}|^{k}\,ds\,.

Then, using an integration by parts, we obtain

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u|^{2}\,\varphi_{*}\,\varphi_{*}^{\prime\prime}}\,ds

and, using (
2
) and
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi|^{2}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi^{\prime}|^{2}}\,ds+\alpha^{2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi|^{2}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u^{\prime}|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u|^{2}}\,d\mu_{p}\,.

which we obtained by using
\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|\varphi|^{p}}\,ds=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u|^{p}}\,d\mu_{p}
.

We now want to utilize a change of variables. The change of variables is defined as

s\mapsto z(s)=\tanh\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\alpha s\right)\,.

It is tailored such that

1-z(s)^{2}=\frac{1}{\left(\cosh\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\alpha s\right)\right)^{2}}=\frac{2}{p\alpha^{2}}|\varphi_{*}(s)|^{p-2}\,.

Define the number
n:=\frac{2\,p}{p-2}
which is in
(2,\,\infty)
. Thereafter, consider the transformed function
w\big(z(s)\big)=u(s)
. By calculation obtain the following relation:

\frac{dz(s)}{ds}=\frac{(p-2)\alpha}{2}{\left(\cosh\left(\frac{p-2}{2}\alpha s\right)\right)^{-2}}=\frac{(p-2)\alpha}{2}\frac{2}{p\alpha^{2}}|\varphi_{*}(s)|^{p-2}=\frac{(p-2)\alpha}{2}(1-z(s)^{2})\,.

Using this relation in the change of variables, we obtain the following transformed integrals

\displaystyle\frac{p\alpha}{(p-2)}\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{-1}^{+1}{|w(z)|^{2}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\,dz=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u(x)|^{2}}\,d\mu_{p}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{p\alpha}{(p-2)}\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\int_{-1}^{+1}{|w(z)|^{p}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}\,dz=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u(x)|^{p}}\,d\mu_{p}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{p\alpha}{(p-2)}\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\int_{-1}^{+1}{|w(z)|^{2}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\,dz=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u(x)|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}\,,

\displaystyle\frac{(p-2)}{p\alpha}\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\int_{-1}^{+1}{|w^{\prime}(z)|^{2}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dz=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}{|u^{\prime}(x)|^{2}}\,d\mu_{2}\,.

Now for a
\phi(s)=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})^{T}
, we are interested in the quadratic form
\langle\phi,A\phi\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{C}^{2})}

\displaystyle\frac{p\alpha}{(p-2)}\left(\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\right)^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\int_{2}^{1}\Biggl[

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(p-2)\alpha}{2}\right)^{2}\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}w^{\prime}_{1}(z)\\
w^{\prime}_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}w^{\prime}_{1}(z)\\
w^{\prime}_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}(1-z^{2})^{\frac{n}{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{p\alpha^{2}}{2}\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(z)\\
w_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix},\left(\mathds{1}-\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}p&(p-2)\\
(p-2)&p\end{pmatrix}\right)\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(z)\\
w_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}(1-z^{2})^{\frac{n-2}{2}}

\displaystyle+\left\langle\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(z)\\
w_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix},\begin{pmatrix}(1+2\alpha)&0\\
0&(1-2\alpha)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}w_{1}(z)\\
w_{2}(z)\end{pmatrix}\right\rangle_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}(1-z^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\Biggr]dz

Now we consider the summands one by one.
If one writes the transfomed functions
w_{i}
in a Gegenbauer polynomial basis as an
\vec{w_{i}}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})
vector, then the above quadratic form can be written as
\langle\vec{w_{i}},M\vec{w_{i}}\rangle_{\ell^{2}(\mathbb{N}_{0})}
for a matrix

M=\begin{pmatrix}M_{1}&M_{2}\\
M_{3}&M_{4}\end{pmatrix}

where
M_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{\mathbb{N}_{0}\times\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. The goal is to show the claim above holds and to compute this matrix
M
.
The Gegenbauer polynomial basis used is the one for parameter
\lambda=(n-3)/2
. We will follow the unsual convention in calling these polynomials
C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. Furthermore, for
k<0
we will use the convention that
C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)=0
to make case distinctions obsolete. Notice that
n>2
implies
\lambda>-1/2
which ensures the existence of the Gegenbauer polynomials.
The polynomials are orthogonal with respect to the
\mathrm{L}^{2}([-1,1],\mathbb{C},d\mu)
scalar product with weight
d\mu(z)=(1-z^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}dz
. They are eigenfunctions to the ultraspherical operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{(n-3)/2}\coloneqq-(1-z^{2})\frac{d^{2}}{dz^{2}}+(n-2)z\frac{d}{dz}

with eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}=k(k+n-3)
for the
C_{k}^{\frac{n-3}{2}}(z)
. Therefore it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{-1}^{+1}{|w^{\prime}|^{2}}\left(1-z^{2}\right)^{\frac{n}{2}}\,dz=\langle w,\mathcal{L}_{(n-3)/2}w\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}([-1,1],\mathbb{C},d\mu)}+2\int_{2}^{+1}(1-z^{2})^{\frac{n-4}{2}}\>\overline{w}\>z\frac{d}{dz}w(z)\>dz\,.

Now turn the attention to the
z\frac{d}{dz}
using classical identities
[
16
, (15) and (16)]
, specifically identity :

\displaystyle z\frac{d}{dz}C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)

\displaystyle\overset{(15)}{=}k\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)+\frac{d}{dz}C^{\lambda}_{k-1}(z)

\displaystyle\overset{(16)}{=}k\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)+(k+n-5)\>C^{\lambda}_{k-2}(z)+z\frac{d}{dz}C^{\lambda}_{k-2}(z)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots\qquad\vdots

\displaystyle=k\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)+\sum_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0},\,j<k,\,j+k\text{ is even}}(2j+n-3)C^{\lambda}_{j}(z)

As another operator to be expressed in the Gegenbauer polynomial basis, let us consider
z^{2}\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)
. We want to express the operator
z^{2}
in our Gegenbauer basis by using
[
16
, (19)]
:

\displaystyle z\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)=\frac{1}{2(k+\lambda)}\left((k+1)C_{k+1}^{\lambda}(z)+(k-1+2\lambda)C_{k-1}^{\lambda}(z)\right)\,.

By using the recurrence relation twice, we obtain:

\displaystyle z^{2}\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)=\eta_{k}^{2}\>C_{k+2}^{\lambda}(z)+\eta_{k}^{1}\>C^{\lambda}_{k}(z)+\eta_{k}^{0}\>C_{k-2}^{\lambda}(z)\,.

The result then follows by collecting of all terms.

The method used to do the numerics goes as follows.
As in Section
4
, the operator
A
is written as a
2\times 2
block matrix
M
with each block corresponding to an infinite dimensional matrix.
Each block is now truncated into its uppermost
N\times N
-block. This is a finite dimensional truncation of the whole infinite dimensional matrix. The finite dimensional truncation takes into account only the contributions from the first
N
Gegenbauer polynomials.
The lowest eigenvalue of this truncated matrix is then computed via standard sparse linear algebra solvers from the python SciPy Sparse Linear Algebra library.
We performed this computation for values
(\alpha,\,p)
in the parameter space for which the
p
is larger than the
p_{*}(\alpha)
for which symmetry of the optimizing functions was established by
[
2
]
.

The assumption needed for our numerics to obtain relevant results is, that the upper component (and respectively the lower component) of the lowest eigenfunction to
M
can be well approximated by the first
N
Gegenbauer polynomials.
Unsurprisingly, the numerically calculated lowest eigenfunction is radially symmetric and radially decreasing (see Figure
4
). This can be analytically shown via a simple rearrangement inequality since
\phi_{*}
is radially symmetric and radially decreasing. The numerically computed lowest eigenfunction puts mass on both the upper component and the lower component.

Furthermore, it seems to put mass only on the first ten or so even Gegenbauer polynomials, even when we put
N
as high as
170
. We can take this as some numerical evidence, that our approximation is not significantly losing essential features. See Figures
2
and
3

High Resolution Overtone Spectroscopy of HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
Miguel Jiménez-Redondo, Chiara Schleif, Julianna Palotás, János Sarka, Hayley Bunn, Petr Dohnal, Paola Caselli, Pavol Jusko

Abstract
Rotationally resolved spectra of the HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} molecular ions have been recorded in the spectral range between 6200 and 6800 cm − 1 \text{cm}^{-1} using a cryogenic ion trap instrument. The rovibrational transitions were probed using two different action spectroscopy schemes, namely laser-induced reaction (LIR) and leak-out spectroscopy (LOS). Various vibrational bands of HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} and HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} were measured with high resolution for the first time. For HNC + \text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} , the X 2 ​ Σ + ​ ( 20 0 ​ 0 ) − ( 00 0 ​ 0 ) \text{X}~^{2}\Sigma^{+}~(20^{0}0)-(00^{0}0) overtone band was recorded using LIR, while LOS was used to probe the X 2 ​ Π ​ ( 000 ) 1 − ( 210 ) 0 ​ μ \text{X}~^{2}\Pi~(000)^{1}-(210)^{0}\mu combination band and the X 2 ​ Π ​ ( 000 ) 1 − A 2 ​ Σ + ​ ( 10 0 ​ 0 ) \text{X}~^{2}\Pi~(000)^{1}-\text{A}~^{2}\Sigma^{+}~(10^{0}0) vibronic band of HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} . Spectroscopic constants, band origins and radiative lifetimes for the observed states have been determined. The effective fit for the HCN + \text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}} spectra revealed the presence of strong vibrational couplings leading to perturbations of the rovibrational levels of the excited states. The two action spectroscopy schemes are compared and their potential use to explore ion-molecule interactions is discussed.

Introduction

\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and its lower energy isomer,
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, are important intermediaries in interstellar cyanide chemistry
[
22
]
.
In cold astrophysical environments, the efficient reaction of these
two ions with
\text{H}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

[
5
]
is expected to be the main
formation mechanism of
\text{HCNH}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

[
8
,
26
]
.
In diffuse molecular clouds, where their neutral counterparts, HCN and HNC, are
known to be present
[
21
]
,
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
can be formed through photoionization by UV photons.
Since a significant
fraction of hydrogen is in
atomic form in these environments,

\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
are primarily lost through dissociative
recombination with electrons
[
12
]
. The absence of the two
ionic isomers from spectroscopic databases
[
7
]
due to
the lack of available high resolution experimental spectra prevents
the identification of these ions in astronomical surveys

[
26
]
, and thus neither
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
nor
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
have yet been detected in any galactic or extra-galactic source.

The
\nu_{1}
fundamental vibrational transition of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
is concurrently reported by
30
. Prior to that, there were only a few experimental studies focused on
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
spectroscopy and no rotationally resolved spectra were available.

12
measured the photoionisation spectra of HNC, where only the
\nu_{3}
band of the X
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
electronic ground state of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
could be unambiguously assigned with a band origin at 2260
\text{cm}^{-1}
.

9
identified the absorption features of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
at 3365.0
\text{cm}^{-1}
as
\nu_{1}

and 2195.2
\text{cm}^{-1}
as
\nu_{3}
fundamental bands in a neon matrix. The assignment of the
\nu_{1}

feature, has been recently disputed by
30
,
whose high resolution spectra around 3365.0
\text{cm}^{-1}
rather supports the assignment as
the
\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
combination band of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
.

Ab initio
calculations for
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
were performed by
25
and later by
20

who calculated band origins for the strongest bands of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
in the X
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
state for energies of up to 14000
\text{cm}^{-1}
.

The rotational spectra for the ground vibrational state of the higher energy isomer,
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, was concurrently to this study measured by
31
and by
30
, who
reported high resolution measurements of the
\nu_{1}
fundamental and

\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}
combination bands.
Prior to this, the only information on
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
was based on an infrared spectroscopy study performed in a neon matrix
[
9
]
and on photoelectron spectroscopy of
HCN

[
3
,
11
,
10
,
36
,
6
]
.
These experiments covered the three lowest electronic states of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
– X
{}^{2}\Pi
, A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
, and B
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
– but only the 12 bands with energies of up to 4000
\text{cm}^{-1}
from the ground state obtained by
36
were measured with sufficient accuracy to resolve individual rotational states.

19
and later
34
used quantum mechanical calculations to assign peaks in the photolectron spectra of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
to vibrational bands noting that the spectral assignment is complicated by the strong vibronic coupling between the close lying X
{}^{2}\Pi
and A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}

25

{}^{2}\Pi
electronic ground state of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

The coupled cluster study by
4
covered both the X
{}^{2}\Pi
and A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}

Higher lying electronic states and non-linear geometry of the
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

ab initio
studies by
15

1

Potential energy curves for the two lowest electronic states of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

33

The X
{}^{2}\Pi
and A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
electronic states of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
as well as the X
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
state of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
are strongly bound with dissociation energies of 6.4
eV
, 5.4
eV
and 6.7
eV
, respectively
[

]
. The energy difference between the ground states of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

Both isomers present a linear geometry in their ground states.

2
Methods

2.1
Spectroscopic notation

Here and in the following text, the transitions between rovibrational levels of the
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions
are denoted as
{}^{\Delta N}\Delta J_{F^{\prime}_{n}F^{\prime\prime}_{n}p^{\prime\prime}}(J^{\prime\prime})
, where
\Delta J
and
\Delta N
describe the change
of the total angular momentum and of the total angular momentum without electron spin, respectively,

F_{n}
denotes the spin-orbit components and
p
represents the parity, when applicable.
The prime and double prime distinguishes quantum numbers belonging to the upper and lower level, respectively.
The vibrational energy levels are denoted by the corresponding vibrational quantum numbers

(\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\nu_{3})^{K}i
for the
{}^{2}\Pi
state of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
(\nu_{1},\nu_{2}^{l},\nu_{3})
for
the
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
electronic states of both
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
.
Here,
K=\Lambda+l
and
\Lambda
and
l
are the electronic angular momentum and vibrational angular momentum
quantum numbers, respectively. The
i
distinguishes between lower (
\mu
) or upper
(
\kappa
) levels arising from the Renner-Teller interaction in the
{}^{2}\Pi
state.

2.2
Experimental

The experiments have been conducted in the 22 pole Cold CAS Ion Trap (CCIT) setup
[
18
]
. The
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions are produced using electron bombardment of
HCN
precursor
gas in a Storage Ion Source (SIS) and then mass selected using a quadrupole mass filter prior to
injection into the 22 pole trap, where an intense He pulse is used to trap and cool them. In order to
maintain sufficient vapor pressure of the introduced neutral gases and to populate
multiple rotational states in the ground vibrational state of the stored ions, the trap
temperature is held constant around either 90 or
125\;\text{K}
, depending on the neutral gas, using a resistive heater .
The ions leaving the trap are then mass selected by a second quadrupole mass filter and
counted using a Daly type detector.
In order to study the lower energy isomer
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, a small amount of
\text{CO}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
was added to
the He pulse to isomerize the
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions coming from the SIS,
as described in
5
.

An Agilent 8164B option 200 light system with line width well below
1\;\text{MHz}

and output power
1-8\;\text{mW}
was used as a light source,
the wavelength has been calibrated using an EXFO WA 1650 wavemeter, which has an absolute accuracy better than 0.3 ppm.
Two action spectroscopy schemes are used to record the rovibrational transitions
throughout this work: 1.) a laser-induced reaction (LIR) scheme
mainly for
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, where activation of an endothermic ion-molecule reaction upon excitation
into a vibrational state is used (for details see ref.
[
17
]
)
and 2.) a leak-out spectroscopy (LOS) scheme mainly for
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, where the vibration-translation
(V-T) transfer upon collision with a neutral buffer gas is applied (for details see ref.
[
29
]
).
The two schemes are compared in section
3.4
.

2.3
Band origin predictions

The experimental observation of weak (overtones/ combination bands) modes can be very tricky, especially in case of action spectroscopy, where it is unclear if the selected action scheme is viable.
This is even more prominent in a case like this, where no previous data on the frequency of such bands nor the action scheme is available. Therefore, in order to guide frequency measurements,
anharmonic vibrational frequencies
were calculated for the overtone of
\nu_{1}
in both
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions
using second-order vibrational perturbation theory (VPT2).
Coupled-cluster theory has been utilized including single and double excitations
and a perturbative treatment of the triple excitations, CCSD(T),
using both RHF or UHF reference functions
with augmented correlation-consistent basis sets up to aug-cc-pV5Z.
All the calculations in this work were performed using the CFOUR program suite
[
32
,
14
,
23
]
.
For
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, the full characterization of the rovibrational spectrum would require
the consideration of the Renner-Teller effect and the spin-orbit coupling.
Nevertheless, describing only the
\nu_{1}
overtone accurately can be done more easily by considering only a single electronic state, which has been performed here and was sufficient to guide our experimental search.

3.2
HCN
+
spectroscopy

A LIR technique based on the charge transfer reaction

{}\mathrm{HCN}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}^{\mathrm{+}}+{}\mathrm{Kr}\to{}\mathrm{Kr}{\vphantom{\mathrm{X}}}^{\mathrm{+}}+{}\mathrm{HCN}~~~~~\Delta H_{0}=0.39\;\text{eV},

(3)

where
\Delta H_{0}
is the reaction enthalpy at 0 K, taken from ATcT
[
27
]
,
was initially used to detect six transitions of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
in the spectral region around 6330
\text{cm}^{-1}
.
As
\text{Kr}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions further reacted with impurities in the krypton gas (mainly
\text{O}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
), negatively affecting
the signal to noise ratio, a leak-out spectroscopy (LOS) scheme with
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
as neutral buffer gas was then employed to measure the
majority of the
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
transitions reported here.

The rovibrational energy levels of the X
{}^{2}\Pi
electronic ground state were described by the following Hamiltonian:

\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{\mathrm{VIB}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ROT}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SO}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SR}}+\hat{H}_{\mathrm{\Lambda}},

(4)

where
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{VIB}}=T\hat{1}
with
T
being the vibrational term energy,
\hat{1}
the identity operator, and

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ROT}}=B\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{2}-D\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{4}+H\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{6},

(5)

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SO}}=A\hat{L}_{\mathrm{z}}\hat{S}_{\mathrm{z}},

(6)

\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SR}}=\gamma\hat{\mathrm{N}}\cdot\hat{\mathrm{S}}+\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{D}\left[\hat{\mathrm{N}}\cdot\hat{\mathrm{S}},\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{2}\right]_{+},

(7)

\begin{split}\hat{H}_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}=&-\frac{1}{2}p\left(\hat{N}_{+}\hat{S}_{+}e^{-2i\phi}+\hat{N}_{-}\hat{S}_{-}e^{+2i\phi}\right)\\
&+\frac{1}{2}q\left(\hat{N}_{+}^{2}e^{-2i\phi}+\hat{N}_{-}^{2}e^{+2i\phi}\right)\\
&-\frac{1}{4}\left[\hat{N}_{+}\hat{S}_{+}e^{-2i\phi}+\hat{N}_{-}\hat{S}_{-}e^{+2i\phi},p_{D}\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{2}\right]_{+}\\
&+\frac{1}{4}\left[\hat{N}_{+}^{2}e^{-2i\phi}+\hat{N}_{-}^{2}e^{+2i\phi},q_{D}\hat{\mathrm{N}}^{2}\right]_{+},\end{split}

(8)

to a Hund’s case (a) basis as implemented in PGOPHER
[
35
]
.
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{ROT}}
describes the rotational energy term where
B
denotes the rotational constant, and
D
and
H
the quartic and sextic centrifugal distortion constants, respectively.
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SO}}
represents the spin-orbit interaction including the spin-orbit coupling constant
A
, while
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{SR}}
describes the spin-rotation interaction with
\gamma
denoting the spin-rotation coupling constant and
\gamma_{D}
its centrifugal distortion.
\hat{H}_{\mathrm{\Lambda}}
represents the
\Lambda
-type doubling containing the two
\Lambda
doubling constants
p
and
q
as well as their centrifugal distortion parameters
p_{D}
and
q_{D}
. The terms
e^{\pm 2i\phi}
assure that the operators of the
\Lambda
-type doubling connect only the two halves of a
\Pi
state. The square brackets denote the anti-commutator

\left[\hat{O},\hat{Q}\right]_{+}=\hat{O}\hat{Q}+\hat{Q}\hat{O}.

(9)

The Hamiltonian for the A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
state of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

3.3
Radiative lifetime determination

The radiative lifetime of the upper level of the probed transition can be estimated
from the dependence of the number of LIR product ions
(i. e., the line intensity) on the number density of the LIR reactant [M].
In the case where the levels with energies below that of the upper state of the transition do
not react with the LIR reactant gas, a simple relation can be obtained
[
17
]
:

\frac{N_{\mathrm{LIR}}}{N_{\mathrm{P}}}=\frac{r_{1}k_{1}[{}\mathrm{M}]}{1/\tau+k_{1}[{}\mathrm{M}]}.

(11)

where
r_{1}
is the rate of excitation from the lower state to the upper state,
k_{1}
is the rate
coefficient for the given laser induced reaction, assumed to have a value close to the collisional (Langevin) coefficient,

N_{\mathrm{P}}
is the number of primary ions and
\tau
is the lifetime of the upper state.

If the LIR is exothermic for some of the energy levels accessible from the upper state by
radiative decay, a simple analysis by equation (
11
) is no longer sufficient for a single
state lifetime determination. The

\tau
derived from equation (
11
) then represents the “effective” lifetime for the
radiative cascade starting in the upper level of a given transition and ending in some lower state with
insufficient energy for the probing reaction. The obtained
\tau
is thus only an upper estimate of the
real lifetime.

The endothermicities of reactions (
1
) and (
3
) used in the present spectroscopic
study for
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
are relatively low (i. e., the fundamental mode can
still lead to the LIR process, see Figure
1

3.4
Action scheme comparison LIR/LOS

The effectiveness of both LIR and LOS techniques is connected to the collisional interaction of the
vibrationally excited ions with the corresponding neutrals.
The comparison between ion signal intensities arising from LIR reaction products, or from ions leaked out
of the trap during the LOS, provides useful indirect information about the underlying processes.
On one hand, energetically favored ion-molecule reactions, such as charge or proton transfer, regularly
applied in LIR schemes, often proceed at collisional reaction rates.
On the other hand, the efficiency of the LOS scheme is inherently linked to the rate of collisional
quenching of the ions’ vibrationally excited states, i. e., to the vibration-translation (V-T) energy transfer.
The signal intensity from these two techniques is, therefore, compared while maintaining all the
remaining parameters equal, in order to constrain the ratio between the two processes
(collisional rate vs. V-T transfer).

The ion signal attributed to the action scheme during LIR or LOS experiments is affected by
a variety of factors, most obvious being the rf and dc potentials used for ion trapping and extraction
from the trap (note that LIR uses only extraction of ions at the end of the trapping cycle, while

Although, to our knowledge, the approach to estimate the ratio between the collisional rate and

3.4

Moreover, in case of

In this way, many of the effects, which could easily be miss-attributed to the change of
the effective/ extraction potentials or to the ion detector due to the ion mass/ charge ratio change simply even out.
We believe our result, that estimates that the V-T transfer of

to

\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

of better vibration-vibration coupling during the collision with
\text{N}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}

This method, capable of assessing various ion-molecule interaction properties, shall certainly
be explored further.

4
Conclusion

We provide the first data for the overtone spectra of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
ions. Together
with the simultaneous publications for the fundamental vibrations
[
30
]
and the
pure rotational spectrum of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}

[
31
]
,
these are the first high-resolution spectra available for perhaps the simplest
isomer system of
\text{[}\text{CHN}\text{]}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, only possible thanks to the latest developments in action spectroscopy and
cryogenic ion trap technology.
Moreover, the action spectroscopy techniques open new possibilities of ion-molecule
interaction studies, such as the radiative lifetimes in excited vibrational states and/ or
for the first time also to constrain the efficiency of the vibration-translation (V-T) transfer.

The two lowest electronic states of
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
represent a very peculiar spectroscopic system. On the one hand, the lower electronic state is doubly degenerate in linear geometry, resulting in Renner-Teller splitting when the bending mode is excited.
On the other hand, the presence of the low lying A
{}^{2}\Sigma^{+}
electronic state leads to strong vibronic coupling between these two states
[
34
]
.
As a relatively simple molecule for computational purposes, it is a prospective target for potential testing of
high precision quantum mechanical calculations.

All the high resolution data collected here and in
30
,
31
,
together with the derived spectroscopic constants,
should finally enable the detection of the
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
and
\text{HCN}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
in space, either by
using radio astronomy or IR detection, e. g., from the James Webb Space Telescope (JWST).
At the same time, the obtained spectroscopic data in the
1.5\;\upmu
m range allows for
the use of telecommunication grade equipment in the L, S bands to efficiently and
economically monitor the presence of these two isomers in
absorption and/ or emission applications.
Finally, the overtone spectrum of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
helps to constrain the prediction of the pure
rotational spectrum of
\text{HNC}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, the lower energy isomer of
\text{[}\text{CHN}\text{]}\text{}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}^{\text{+}}
, which is, surprisingly, still not available to this day.

Polymer Science and Engineering in India
Authors: Anindita Das, Tarak
K. Patra
Published: 2024

It is our pleasure to write this Editorial for the ACS Polymers Au virtual special issue “Polymer Science and Engineering in India”. Historically, Hermann Staudinger first proposed the concept of macromolecules in his seminal work in 1930, which states that polymers are composed of long chains of repeating units rather than the previously thought-to-be colloidal structures—and in recognition of his contribution, he was awarded the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in the year 1953. Since then, there has been a huge global surge in polymer research and education. India had made significant strides in keeping up with this worldwide advancement, as evident from the formation of The Society for Polymer Science (SPSI), India, as early as in 1979 under the guidance of Professor H. Kothandaraman at the Anna University, Madras, with the objective of promoting polymer research in India. This society continues to provide a vibrant platform for fostering polymer science research in India and beyond. Over the past few decades, Indian researchers have made significant research contributions, with top-quality publications and patents in various subdomains of polymer science and engineering. These areas include both fundamental and applied research addressing some of the grand challenges in polymer science and engineering, as nicely highlighted by Arthi Jayaraman and Harm-Anton Klok in their Editorial on ACS Polymers Au’s “Grand Challenges in Polymer Science”. Some of the topics being actively researched in India include new methodologies in polymer synthesis, characterization, modeling and simulation, polymer crystallization biodegradable and compostable polymers, self-healing polymers, polymer self-assembly, stimuli-responsive polymers, polymer nanocomposites, conducting polymers, high-performance polymers, and supramolecular polymers, to name a few. This virtual special issue is a collection of peer-reviewed Articles and Reviews presenting impactful research in polymer science and engineering from a few expert groups in India. We hope you enjoy learning about these principal investigators and their laboratories’ current research interests, and below is a short summary of each of their contributions.

Correlating structure with function is a crucial aspect of polymer science, and extensive research in this direction is ongoing in India in various subdomains. Polymer properties and functions are deeply influenced by their molecular architecture (linear, branched, cross-linked), the size of the polymer chains and their tacticity, monomer sequence, and many others. In a recent publication titled “Poly(vinylidene fluoride) Aerogels with α, β, and γ Crystalline Forms: Correlating Physicochemical Properties with Polymorphic Structures”, Bhoje Gowd, Biniek and co-workers correlated physicochemical properties with polymorphic structures in poly(vinylidene fluoride) (PVDF) polymer-based aerogels in their pure crystalline α, β, and γ forms, which were prepared by a sustainable solvent exchange strategy, followed by a simple freeze-drying technique. With the group’s expertise in working with crystalline polymers, they developed a simple strategy for tailoring the crystallinity of the PVDF aerogels in order to achieve various morphologies with distinct mechanical, acoustic, and electrical properties that find relevance in emerging flexible energy harvesting devices to thermal and acoustic insulators.

Biodegradable polymers derived from natural sources, which can break down into nontoxic components, have a significantly reduced environmental impact compared to their nondegradable counterparts. Also, to meet the ever-increasing need for biodegradable polymers in biological applications, advancing knowledge in the field of bioresource-based degradable aliphatic polyesters is the need of the hour. This field is actively being researched worldwide. Along this line, Jayakanan and co-workers have made consistent contributions from India. This group, in the recently published paper titled “Structural Engineering of l-Aspartic Amphiphilic Polyesters for Enzyme-Responsive Drug Delivery and Bioimaging in Cancer Cells”, has demonstrated the utility of a structurally engineered, bioderived l-aspartic acid as a suitable monomer choice for the synthesis of enzymatically degradable hydroxyl-functionalized amphiphilic polyesters by solvent-free melt polycondensation that renders water-dispersible, hydrogen-bonded nanoparticles with high biocompatibility and drug-loading capability for the delivery in cancer cells. This strategy opens up future opportunities for structural engineering in biodegradable polymers to target desired functional outputs.

Biodegradable polymers also find application in biosensing and detection of environmental pollutants such as heavy metals, pesticides, and different organic contaminants. By judicially incorporating a fluorescein-based colorimetric turn-on sensor into a biodegradable polycaprolactone scaffold, Shunmugam and co-workers, in their latest article titled “Caprolactone-Based Biodegradable Polymer for Selective, Sensitive Detection and Removal of Cu2+ Ions from Water”, have successfully demonstrated selective and sensitive colorimetric detection of Cu2+ ions in water and its simultaneous removal by simple precipitation method, taking advantage of the hydrophobicity of the polyester backbone. The study highlights an innovative approach of combining colorimetric sensing with heavy metal adsorption and separation in a single polymer. In their article titled "Solid-State Fluorescent Organic Polymers for Visual Detection and Elimination of Heavy Metals in Water", Porel and co-workers demonstrated an interesting approach for the detection and removal of heavy metal ions using a judicially designed fluorescent nonconjugated organic polymer, which bears a dansyl fluorophore and a dithiocarbamate (DTC) in its repeating units. The presence of the dansyl group makes the polymer exhibit distinct color changes specific to each metal ion with a detection limit as low as in the nanomolar ranges using spectrofluorimetry. Furthermore, the incorporation of the DTC group allowed strong metal binding in the solid state, thereby facilitating their effective removal.

Natural biopolymers play crucial roles in wound healing applications due to their biocompatibility, ability to promote tissue regeneration, and environmental sustainability. Collagen, a major structural protein in the extracellular matrix is known to promote wound healing by supporting cell adhesion, proliferation, and migration. Toward this end, Chatterjee and co-workers, in their review titled “Silk Fibroin and Its Nanocomposites for Wound Care: A Comprehensive Review”, provided an extensive overview of the significant potential and application of silk fibroin (SF) and their nanocomposites in the field of wound care and discussed their advantages and limitations as compared to established collagen-based would healing materials. This review highlights the potential of SF as inexpensive and abundant biopolymers that may afford cost-effective wound care solutions, provided it can overcome challenges related to processing, stability, and industrial scalability, and it can meet important clinical needs.

Stimuli-responsive block copolymers represent a fascinating class of materials that possess the ability to undergo substantial changes in their properties in response to external stimuli. Their importance spans across multiple sectors, including drug delivery, tissue engineering, sensors, environmental remediation. The advent of controlled radical polymerization has significantly advanced the field of polymer chemistry, particularly in the synthesis of stimuli-responsive, sequence-controlled block copolymers. Pal and co-workers, in their article titled “Stimuli-Responsive Control over Self-Assembled Nanostructures in Sequence-Specific Functional Block Copolymers”, introduced functional triblock and diblock copolymers with a predefined sequence of the hydrophilic poly(ethylene oxide) (PEO) block and a hydrophobic block comprised of UVB light-responsive coumarin and redox-responsive ferrocene pendants in a sequence-specific or random manner by RAFT polymerization. The sequence of these two responsive entities in the hydrophobic segment controls the host–guest complex formation of these block copolymers with externally added bis-β-cyclodextrin (host). Furthermore, sequence-controlled responsiveness to external stimuli directs the morphological transition from native polymer vesicles to micelles due to the altered hydrophobic–hydrophilic balance. This work sheds light on the significance of monomer sequence in synthetic polymers in determining their structural adaptability to environmental changes, much like the complexity exhibited by biopolymers like proteins. For the biomedical application of such polymer-based materials, it is equally important to understand the effect of hydrophobic interactions in water, that drive the critical process of polymer aggregation akin to globular proteins in crowded cellular environments. In their article titled “Effects of Polymer Architecture and Charged Molecular Crowders on Hydrophobic Polymer Collapse”, Rajput and Nayar explored the effects of charged molecular crowders on the coil-to-globule transition of a model uncharged polymer via molecular dynamic simulations. The study provides valuable thermodynamic insights on the collapse structure of a macromolecule in a crowded environment with contrasting results for branched and linear topologies.

Understanding and controlling the glass transition properties in glassy polymer thin films is crucial for optimizing their performance in a range of applications. When it comes to polymer thin film properties, the nanoconfinement effect and processing parameters have a greater impact on the glass transition temperature, as compared to the bulk. Existing mechano-rheological characterization tools are well established for bulk polymers but they are not suitable for analyzing properties in nanometer-thick films due to the unavoidable strong influence of the substrate. The review titled “Advancements in Novel Mechano-Rheological Probes for Studying Glassy Dynamics in Nanoconfined Thin Polymer Films” by Madhusudanan and Chowdhury effectively consolidates current knowledge and recent advancements in nonconventional mechano-rheological testing approaches applied in thin glassy polymer films to explore fundamental questions in this active field of study. In their article titled “Pattern Formation in Evaporating Polymer Solutions-Interplay between Dewetting and Decomposition”, Chandran and co-workers provide valuable insights into the mechanisms of pattern formation in evaporating polymer solutions and highlight the critical role of polymer concentration. The study advances the understanding of evaporation-induced pattern formation, offering potential pathways for manipulating polymer film properties through controlled evaporation processes.

Developing new methodologies for synthetic polymers continues to be an important field of study for expanding the functional properties of synthetic polymers and improving their performances to meet desired societal needs. The review titled “Recent Developments on Cationic Polymerization of Vinyl Ethers” by Tripathy, De, and co-workers provides an in-depth analysis of the most recent developments in cationic polymerization of vinyl ether (VE) monomers, which has seen tremendous progress from the use of stringent to ambient reaction conditions that are environmentally friendly. This has been possible through the continuing efforts in developing strategies that combine classical cationic polymerization and RAFT polymerization together, rendering living cationic polymerization with more complex block copolymer architectures with tailorable functionalities and stereoregular poly(vinyl ethers), which were earlier not possible by traditional approaches. This structured review not only highlights the substantial potential of emerging cationic RAFT polymerizations, but also provides practical insights and strategies for overcoming current limitations, thereby guiding future research and development in this very important field. The C–H activation is a powerful and increasingly influential technique in organic chemistry where carbon–hydrogen (C–H) bonds are directly functionalized, and in recent times, it has gained popularity as an atom-efficient methodology in direct heteroarylation polymerization (DHAP) for the synthesis of conjugated polymers for electronic applications. The recent article by Asha S. K. and co-workers titled “Rational Monomer Design for the Synthesis of Conjugated Polymers by Direct Heteroarylation Polymerization” illustrates the structural prerequisite in bithiophene-flanked monomers with distinct aromatic cores for palladium-catalyst C–H abstraction for DHAP. Their research emphasizes the significance of the aromatic core’s electron-withdrawing effect in enhancing monomer reactivity, as evidenced by the successful polymerization of naphthalene diimide and perylene diimide bithiophene-flanked monomers, which have lower energy requirements for C–H bond activation than the fluorene analogue. This work marks a significant leap in the design of monomer architectures for conjugated polymers, which are foreseen as future materials for energy applications.

It is evident that polymer science and engineering is a dynamic and rapidly evolving field in India, with cutting-edge research and an expanding industrial R&D sector. Continued support from the government and collaborations between academia and industry are in place to maintain the momentum and address future grand challenges in the energy, environment, construction and healthcare sectors. With new initiatives for strengthening polymer education in the country and increasing interest among students in multidisciplinary areas of polymer science and engineering, it is envisaged that the Indian polymer science community is set to garner more global attention and make a stronger impact than ever in the coming years.

Cancellative elliptic Artin monoids
Georges Neaime

Abstract
. We define new presentations for elliptic Artin groups. We also show that the elliptic monoids defined by these presentations are cancellative. This solves the failure of cancellativity for the presentations of elliptic Artin monoids that were introduced before in the literature. Our approach also paves the way to construct Garside structures for elliptic Artin monoids and groups.



1.
Introduction

In the middle of the 80s, Saito
[
Sai85
]
introduced the notion of an elliptic root system inside a vector space
V
equipped with a symmetric bilinear form
I
with rank 2 radical. He also introduced the notion of a marking and classified marked elliptic root systems by introducing the notion of elliptic Dynkin diagrams (for example, see Figures
5
and
7
).

To each elliptic root system and elliptic diagram
\Gamma
, one can naturally define the notion of an elliptic Weyl group
W(\Gamma)
, defined as the group generated by the reflections associated to the root system. If we consider the hyperbolic extension of the space
(V,I)
, for which the bilinear form has rank
1
radical, one obtains the notion of a hyperbolic elliptic Weyl group
\textsc{Hyp}{(\Gamma)}
. It fits into a central extension, where the kernel of the natural map from
\textsc{Hyp}{(\Gamma)}
to
W(\Gamma)
is the cyclic group generated by a certain power of the so-called Coxeter transformation of
W(\Gamma)
. In 1997, Saito–Takebayashi
[
ST97
]
defined presentations by generators and relations for
W(\Gamma)
and
\textsc{Hyp}{(\Gamma)}
.

To each elliptic Weyl group
W(\Gamma)
, one can define the notion of an elliptic Artin group
\textsc{Art}{(\Gamma)}
and an elliptic Hecke algebra
\textsc{Hecke}{(\Gamma)}
(see
[
Yam00
]
for the simply laced cases and
[
SS09
]
in general). Similarly to spherical and affine Artin groups, the presentations of elliptic Artin groups are described using homogeneous relations, meaning that every relation preserves the length of words.

By a personal communication with K. Saito, it will be shown in
[
SS
]
that the groups defined by the homogenised relations are isomorphic to the fundamental groups of the complements of elliptic discriminants
[
Sai90
]
by identifying them with those defined by Looijenga
[
Loo77
,
Loo80
]
and van der Lek
[
vdL83
]
.

In the classical theory for spherical Artin groups, a seminal result is that a certain cancellative Artin monoid injects in the Artin group as a lattice. This fits into a general theory called Garside theory (see
[
DDG+15
]
). This theory was used in order to prove many important conjectures for Artin groups such as the
K(\pi,1)
conjecture, and to solve the word problem.

Concerning the elliptic theory, Saito
[
Sai24
,
Sai
]
showed that the elliptic Artin monoid defined by these homogeneous relations may no longer be cancellative and therefore, the natural morphism from the monoid to the elliptic Artin group may not be injective. Hence, one is not able to apply Garside theory in this context.

In this paper, we define new presentations for the elliptic Artin groups in the simply laced case and show that the monoids defined by these presentations are cancellative. This is therefore a remarkable step towards the construction of Garside structures for elliptic Artin monoids and groups.

In order to define the new presentations, we use some techniques that were developed by the author in
[
Nea22
]
based on the work of Corran–Picantin
[
CP11
]
and Corran–Lee–Lee
[
CLL15
]
. These new presentations are described in Propositions
3.2
and
3.8
. It is notable to mention that the presentations described in
[
Nea22
]
enabled the author to introduce new interval Garside structures for the affine Artin groups of type
\tilde{A}
based on his earlier discoveries
[
Nea19
]
in the context of complex reflection groups.

Using a combinatorial technique, namely the word reversing technique developed by Dehornoy
[
Deh03
]
, we are able to show that the elliptic Artin monoids defined by our new presentations are cancellative. This is Theorem
4.10
.

The paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we introduce various presentations for the affine Artin group of type
\tilde{A}
. In particular, we recall in Definition
2.3
the presentation of Corran–Lee–Lee for
\textsc{Art}{(\tilde{A})}
that inspired the current work. In Section
3
, we introduce in Propositions
3.2
and
3.8
the new presentations for the elliptic Artin groups. We also describe new elliptic diagrams to illustrate these presentations (see Figures
6
and
8
). Finally, after introducing several combinatorial techniques, Section
4
shows that the monoids defined by these presentations are cancellative (see Theorem
4.10
).

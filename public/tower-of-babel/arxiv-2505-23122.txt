On construction of correlation numbers in super Minimal Liouville Gravity in the Ramond sector
V. Belavin, J. Ramos Cabezas, B. Runov

Abstract
We study the construction of correlation numbers in super minimal Liouville gravity. In particular, we construct the fundamental physical fields in the Ramond sector and compute the three-point correlation number involving two physical fields in the Ramond sector and one in the NS sector. Furthermore, we establish the relation between Ramond physical fields and the elements of the ground ring. Using the higher equations of motion of super Liouville theory, this relation leads to a new representation of the Ramond physical fields. This formulation enables a direct analytic computation of correlation numbers involving Ramond field insertions. As an application, we demonstrate the method in the simplest case of a three-point correlation function.

Introduction

In this paper, we consider
\mathcal{N}=1
super Minimal Liouville Gravity (SMLG). The bosonic version of MLG has been studied thoroughly, see e.g.

, the NS sector of SMLG theory has been analyzed in

In this paper, we focus on the Ramond sector of SMLG.

There are two alternative approaches to the analysis of two-dimensional gravity: the “continuous” approach based on the original definition via the Polyakov action, and the dual “discrete” approach based on matrix models.
In the continuous approach, apart from the straightforward calculation of the functional integral, an alternative method allows analytically computing amplitudes using higher equations of motion (HEM) in the underlying Liouville theory. HEMs lead to certain relations for the physical fields which allow analytical calculations of the moduli integrals involved in the definition of the correlation numbers. The corresponding calculations were performed in the Neveu-Schwarz (NS) sector in
[
7
,
8
]
.

It was previously conjectured
[
11
]
that in the “discrete” approach the Ramond sector of the SMLG should be described by the same matrix model as the NS sector. However, this conjecture has not been confirmed as the correlation numbers involving the Ramond fields have never been computed in the “continuous” approach. The present study aims to fill this gap at the level of three-point numbers and lays the groundwork for evaluating the moduli integrals appearing in the computation of four-point correlation numbers. We plan to address the latter issue in future work.

Before reporting our results concerning the Ramond sector of SMLG, we find it useful to outline the main issues in the simpler case of the bosonic version of MLG.
MLG is a theory described by three contributions: a minimal model,
\mathcal{M}(p,q)
labelled by two integers
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}
, with central charge
c_{p,q}
; the Liouville CFT, with central charge
c_{L}
; the theory of ghosts,
(bc)
system, with central charge
c_{\gh}=-26
.

The action of the theory is

S_{\MLG}=S_{\text{min.mod.}}+S_{\text{Liouville}}+S_{\gh}\;.

(1.1)

In MLG one is interested in computing the correlators of physical fields

\langle\!\langle{\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}_{2}\cdots\mathbb{V}_{N}}\rangle\!\rangle_{g}\;.

(1.2)

Our focus will be on the case of a sphere topology, i.e. a surface of genus
g=0
, and the subscript
g
will be omitted in what follows. For some results in higher genus topology, see, e.g.,
[
12
,
6
]
.

The fields in (
1.2
) are constructed as follows

\mathbb{V}_{i}(z,\overline{z})=\phi_{m_{i},n_{i}}(z,\overline{z})V_{a(m_{i},n_{i})}(z,\overline{z})\big(\gh\big)(z,\overline{z})\;.

(1.3)

Here
\phi_{m_{i},n_{i}}(z,\overline{z})
is a primary field of the minimal model, parametrized by the integers
m_{i}
and
n_{i}
,
V_{a(m_{i},n_{i})}
is the primary field of the Liouville theory, parametrized by the parameter
a
, which depends on the integers
m_{i}
and
n_{i}
, and
\big(\gh\big)
stands for a composite field made of ghost fields. This form of the fields
\mathbb{V}_{i}
of the MLG follows from the fact that they have to represent the BRST cohomology.
In addition to the fields
\mathbb{V}_{i}
, the set of the physical fields in the model also contains ground ring operators
\mathbb{O}_{m,n}

[
13
,
14
]

The definition of the BRST charge
\mathcal{Q}
and its nilpotency (or equivalently, the requirement of the Weyl invariance of the theory), furnish also a restriction on the central charges, that has to satisfy

c_{p,q}+c_{L}+c_{\gh}=0\;\implies\;c_{p,q}+c_{L}=26\;.

(1.4)

The correlation function (
1.2
) is notated with a double angular bracket. This is due to the fact that it is actually defined by an integral over positions of the fields. On the sphere

\langle\!\langle{\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}_{2}\cdots\mathbb{V}_{N}}\rangle\!\rangle=\prod_{i=1}^{N-3}\int\,\mathrm{d}^{2}z_{i}\braket{\prod_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{m_{i},n_{i}}}\braket{\prod_{i=1}^{N}V_{a(m_{i},n_{i})}}\braket{\gh}\;,

(1.5)

where the upper limit
N-3
is due to the invariance under global conformal transformation.

In particular, the four-point correlator is given by

\displaystyle\Sigma_{MLG}(\{m_{i},n_{i}\})\equiv\langle\!\langle{\mathbb{V}_{1}\mathbb{V}_{2}\mathbb{V}_{3}\mathbb{V}_{4}}\rangle\!\rangle=\int\,\mathrm{d}^{2}z\braket{\phi_{m_{1},n_{1}}(z,\overline{z})\phi_{m_{2},n_{2}}(0)\phi_{m_{3},n_{3}}(1)\phi_{m_{4},n_{4}}(\infty)}\times

\displaystyle\times\braket{V_{a(m_{1},n_{1})}(z,\overline{z})V_{a(m_{2},n_{2})}(0)V_{a(m_{3},n_{3})}(1)V_{a(m_{4},n_{4})}(\infty)}\;.

(1.6)

Notice that the contribution of the ghost fields in the case of 4-point amplitude is reduced to the three-point correlation function
\langle c\overline{c}(0)c\overline{c}(1)c\overline{c}(\infty)\rangle=1
.
The crucial step in evaluating the moduli integral in equation (
1.6
) analytically is to represent (modulo BRST exact terms) the operator
\mathbb{V}_{1}(z,\overline{z})
as a Laplacian of the ground ring operator
[
2
,
15
]

B_{n_{1},m_{1}}\mathbb{V}_{1}=\overline{\partial}\partial\overline{H}_{n_{1},m_{1}}H_{n_{1},m_{1}}\phi_{m_{1},n_{1}}V_{n_{1},m_{1}}\overline{c}c+\text{BRST exact terms}\,,

(1.7)

where the operator
H_{m,n}
has level
mn
and ghost number
0
.
This representation allows us to reduce the integral (
1.6
) to the boundary terms and eventually to compute it completely. Finally, the result is identified with the 4-point number of the matrix model.

This work is dedicated to establishing a connection between physical fields in the Ramond sectors and the correlation numbers in super minimal Liouville gravity (SMLG), with a specific focus on the three-point function. In this case, no integration over the moduli space is required, and the result simplifies to a linear combination of products of three-point functions from each sector.

The general framework of SMLG is reviewed in the next section. Section
3
is devoted to the construction of physical fields in the Ramond sector. In Section 4, we explore the fundamental relation derived from the properties of the ground ring. Our conclusions are presented in Section 5. We note that some of our results appear to be closely related to those in the recent paper
[
16
]
.

2.1
Liouville sector

The central charge is parametrized by a Liouville coupling parameter

\widehat{c}_{L}=1+2Q^{2}\,,\quad Q=b^{-1}+b.

One can write the primary fields of Super Liouville field theory as exponential vertex operators. For the NS bosonic field

2.2
Matter sector

The role of conformal matter in SMLG is played by a supersymmetric generalized minimal model (GMM). Contrary to the standard definition of a minimal model, the central charge can assume nonrational values.
The primary fields of this model in the NS sector are
\Phi_{\alpha}
(where
\alpha
is a continuous parameter), and the Ramond sector comprises the fields
\Theta_{\alpha}^{\pm}
.
GMM can be obtained from the supersymmetric Liouville theory by performing the transformations

b\to ib\,,\quad a\to-ia\,,

(2.28)

though we must choose a different normalization

G_{NS}^{M}(\alpha)=G_{R}^{\alpha}=1\,.

(2.29)

The primary fields corresponding to degenerate representations are labeled by pairs of integers
m,n
(where
m-n
is even for NS fields and odd for Ramond fields). They form a closed fusion algebra and satisfy

D^{M}_{m,n}\Phi_{m,n}=0\,,\quad D^{M}_{m,n}\Theta_{m,n}^{\pm}=0\,.

(2.30)

To fulfill (
2.3
), the parameter
b
must be related to the central charge of the matter theory as follows

\widehat{c}_{M}=1-2q^{2}\,,\quad q=b^{-1}-b.

(2.31)

Thus, the conformal dimensions of the primary fields in the NS and Ramond sectors can be written respectively as

\Delta^{M,NS}(\alpha)=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-q)}{2}\,,\quad\Delta^{M,R}(\alpha)=\frac{1}{16}+\frac{\alpha(\alpha-q)}{2},,

(2.32)

and the conformal dimensions of degenerate fields read

\Phi_{n,m}=\Phi_{\alpha_{n,m}}\,,\quad\Theta_{n,m}^{\pm}=\Theta_{\alpha_{n,m}}^{\pm}\,,\quad\alpha_{n,m}=\frac{q}{2}-\lambda_{-m,n}\,.

(2.33)

The fields
\Theta^{-}_{\alpha}
are normalized so that the parameters
\beta^{M}_{\alpha}
defining the action of the generator
G_{0}^{M}
are related to analogous parameters
\beta^{L}
in the Liouville sector as follows:

G_{0}^{M}|\Theta_{\alpha}^{\sigma}\rangle=i\beta^{M}_{\alpha}e^{-\frac{i\pi\sigma}{4}}|\Theta_{\alpha}^{-\sigma}\rangle\,,\quad\beta^{M}_{a-b}=-i\beta^{L}_{a}\,.

(2.34)

The matter special structure constants are easily obtained from (
2.21
) by performing the substitution (
2.28
), obtaining

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}^{M,\epsilon}_{[b/2][a](a-b/2)}=\left(\frac{\gamma\left(1-b^{2}\right)\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-ab\right)}{\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{2}\right)\gamma\left(-ab-\frac{b^{2}}{2}+1\right)}\right)^{1/2},

(2.35)

\displaystyle\mathbf{C}^{M,\epsilon}_{[b/2][a](a+b/2)}=\epsilon\left(\frac{\gamma\left(1-b^{2}\right)\gamma\left(ab+b^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right)}{\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{b^{2}}{2}\right)\gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}b(2a+b)\right)}\right)^{1/2}.

2.3
Ghost sector

The super ghost system is described by the free superconformal field theory with the central charge

\widehat{c}_{gh}=-10.

(2.36)

It contains two anticommuting fermionic fields
(b,c)
with spins
(2,-1)
and the bosonic fields
(\beta,\gamma)
with spins

(3/2,-1/2)
. The fields (see
[
7
]
) of the form

\delta(\gamma(0))
of dimension
1/2
arise in the construction of the amplitudes in NS sector. The basic properties of these fields are

b(z)=\sum_{n}\frac{b_{n}}{z^{n+2}},\quad c(z)=\sum_{n}\frac{c_{n}}{z^{n-1}},\quad\beta(z)=\sum_{n}\frac{\beta_{n}}{z^{n+3/2}},\quad\gamma=\sum_{n}\frac{\gamma_{n}}{z^{n+1/2}},

(2.37)

b(z)c(0)=\frac{1}{z},\quad\gamma(z)\beta(0)=\frac{1}{z}.

(2.38)

The modes of the fermionic ghosts
b_{n},c_{n}
are labeled by integers.
The modes of the bosonic ghosts
\beta_{k},\gamma_{k}
are labeled by half-integers in the NS sector and by integers in the R sector. The stress-energy tensor
T^{g}
and its superpartner
G^{g}
can be written as follows (normal ordering is implied in the definitions)

\displaystyle T^{g}=-2bc^{\prime}-b^{\prime}c-\frac{3}{2}\beta\gamma^{\prime}-\frac{1}{2}\beta^{\prime}\gamma,

(2.39)

\displaystyle G^{g}=\beta^{\prime}c+\frac{3}{2}\beta c^{\prime}-2b\gamma.

Their super Virasoro generators are given by

\displaystyle L_{m}^{g}=\sum_{n}(m+n):b_{m-n}c_{n}:+\sum_{k}(\frac{m}{2}+k):\beta_{m-k}\gamma_{k}:+a^{gh}\delta_{m,0},

(2.40)

\displaystyle G_{k}^{g}=-\sum_{n}\left[(k+\frac{n}{2}):\beta_{k-n}c_{n}:+2b_{n}\gamma_{k-n}\right],

(2.41)

where
a^{gh}=-1/2
in the NS sector and
a^{gh}=-5/8
in the R sector.

3
Physical fields

3.1
BRST charge

SMLG is a BRST invariant theory with the (nilpotent) BRST charge
\mathcal{Q}
given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\oint dzj_{\mathcal{Q}}(z),

(3.1)

\displaystyle j_{\mathcal{Q}}(z)=:c(z)\left(T^{L}(z)+T^{M}(z)+\frac{1}{2}T^{g}(z)\right)+\gamma(z)\left(G^{L}(z)+G^{M}(z)+\frac{1}{2}G^{g}(z)\right):,

and it obeys the nilpotent relation
\mathcal{Q}^{2}=0
. The charge can also be written in terms of modes

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}=\sum_{m}{:}\bigg[L_{m}^{\text{M+L}}+\frac{1}{2}L^{\text{g}}_{m}\bigg]c_{-m}{:}+\sum_{r}{:}\bigg[G_{r}^{\text{M+L}}+\frac{1}{2}G^{\text{g}}_{r}\bigg]\gamma_{-r}{:}+\frac{a^{gh}}{2}c_{0},

(3.2)

which obey the following commutation relations with the superconformal generators

\displaystyle\{b_{n},\mathcal{Q}\}=L^{L}_{n}+L^{M}_{n}+L^{g}_{n},

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{\beta_{r},\mathcal{Q}\}=G^{L}_{r}+G^{M}_{r}+G^{g}_{r}.

(3.4)

The physical fields form a space of
\mathcal{Q}
-cohomology classes. From (
3.4
) it follows that the total conformal dimension of any physical field
\Psi
is zero

\Delta^{tot}(\Psi)=0.

(3.5)

Three-point numbers involving Ramond fields

4.1
Ghost number balance rules

The conservation of the fermionic ghost current restricts the types of fields that can appear in non-vanishing correlation functions of SMLG. Recall that, in the NS sector, there are two ghost balance rules
[
7
]

N_{c}-N_{b}=3,

(4.1)

N_{\delta(\gamma)}-N_{\delta(\beta)}+N_{\beta}-N_{\gamma}=2.

(4.2)

Before considering analogous rules required in the Ramond sector, let us provide a brief overview of the derivation of these rules. For
bc
system, in the correlation function involving only physical fields with
N_{c}
and
N_{b}
numbers of
c
and
b
fields respectively, we insert the operator
N^{g}_{bc}=\oint dzJ^{bc}
, where
J^{bc}=-:bc:

X=\oint du\langle J^{bc}(u)\ldots c(z_{1})...c(z_{N_{c}})b(y_{1})...b(y_{N_{b}})\rangle.

(4.3)

Here, the ellipsis
\ldots
stands for other types of fields. The contour integral encircles all the insertion points
z_{i}
and
y_{i}
. Using the OPE

J^{bc}(u)c(0)=-c(0)/u\,,\quad J^{bc}(u)b(0)=b(0)/u\,,

(4.4)

we conclude that the insertion of such an operator effectively counts the number of
c
and
b
fields:

X=(N_{c}-N_{b})\langle...c(z_{1})...c(z_{N_{c}})b(y_{1})...b(y_{N_{b}})\rangle.

(4.5)

Now, deforming the contour to infinity and using the transformation law of the ghost current

J^{bc}(u)\to-J^{bc}(1/u)/u^{2}+3/u\,,\quad u\to 1/u,

(4.6)

we find that the same integral becomes

\displaystyle X=

\displaystyle\oint_{\infty}du[\langle(-J(1/u)/u^{2}+3/u)...c(1/z_{1})...c(1/z_{N_{c}})b(1/y_{1})...b(1/y_{N_{b}})\rangle]

(4.7)

\displaystyle=3\langle...c(1/z_{1})...c(1/z_{N_{c}})b(1/y_{1})...b(1/y_{N_{b}})\rangle

\displaystyle=3\langle...c(z_{1})...c(z_{N_{c}})b(y_{1})...b(y_{N_{b}})\rangle.

which together with (
4.5
) prove the first rule. For other rules, the proof follows the same rationale.

Similarly, for the
\beta\gamma
system, we have the ghost current
J^{\beta\gamma}=-:\beta\gamma:=-\partial\phi
which transforms as

J^{\beta\gamma}(u)\to-J^{\beta\gamma}(1/u)/u^{2}-2/u\,,\quad u\to 1/u\,.

(4.8)

Using the OPEs of

with
\beta,\gamma,\delta(\gamma)
, and applying the same logic
as we used for the

4.2
).

For the Ramond sector, we proceed analogously. In this case, we require the OPEs of the ghost current
J^{\beta\gamma}
with the field
\sigma
introduced above. It is convenient to work in the bosonized representation, where
\sigma=e^{-\phi/2}
. For an arbitrary exponential field of the form
e^{l\phi}
, its conformal dimension is given by
-l(l+2)/2
. In particular, for our purposes, we introduce the field
\sigma_{2}=e^{\phi/2}
, which has conformal dimension
-5/8
. This allows one to construct fields (satisfying the constraint (
3.5
)) from
\sigma_{2}
,
c
, and suitable descendants from the Liouville and matter sectors involving modes such as

and
L_{-1}
. For any exponential field, we have the OPE:

J^{\beta\gamma}(u)e^{l\phi(0)}=\frac{l}{u}e^{l\phi(0)}.

(4.9)

From this OPE, it follows that the modified ghost balance rule for correlation functions in the Ramond sector, which include insertions of
\sigma
and
\sigma_{2}

N_{\delta(\gamma)}-N_{\delta(\beta)}+N_{\beta}-N_{\gamma}+N_{\sigma}/2-N_{\sigma_{2}}/2=2.

(4.10)

Restricting the rule (
4.10
) to the case where only the fields
\delta(\gamma)
,
\sigma
, and
\sigma_{2}
appear in the
\beta\gamma
sector of the correlation function, we have

N_{\delta(\gamma)}+N_{\sigma}/2-N_{\sigma_{2}}/2=2.

(4.11)

A final remark is in order. In view of equation (
3.30
), the presence of
\sigma_{2}
appears to be essential. For instance, if

, one must include pairs of
\sigma
and
\sigma_{2}
in higher-point functions. This requirement was discussed in
[
25

in the context of vertex operators. For the three-point correlation function, the only possibility is given by (
3.35

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{B_{1,2}}A_{a-b/2}G_{NS}(a-b/2)(Q/2-(a-b/2))^{2}

(4.28)

\displaystyle=\frac{2\mathbf{C}^{M,+}_{[b/2][a-b](a-b-b/2)}G_{NS}(a-b/2)}{B_{1,2}}(Q/2-(a-b/2))^{2},

which agrees with equation (
3.35
) for
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})=(3b/2,a,a-b/2)
. Similarly, for the case
a_{3}=a+b/2
one obtains the same type of relation.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we analyzed the construction of physical fields in the Ramond sector of super minimal Liouville gravity. We derived the the three-point correlation number involving two physical fields in the Ramond sector (
3.30
) and one physical field in the NS sector (
3.6
), the result is given by relations (
3.35
,
3.36
,
3.37
), which constitute part of the main results of the paper. Another part of our results concerns the derivation of the ground ring operators (
4.12
,
4.14
) and the more general expressions (
4.16
,
4.17
), which play a fundamental role in the analytical treatment of SMLG. The result (
3.35
) was subsequently verified through a direct computation using the expression for the ground ring operator. In section
4.1
, we also analyzed the constraints on the types of fields that can appear in higher-point correlation numbers. The findings of this paper provide, in principle, a method for performing analytic computations of the moduli integrals that arise in the construction of multi-point correlation functions involving Ramond field insertions. This is one of our next objectives.

Another direction is related to the investigation of the dual approach, which has not yet been explored on the level of correlation numbers. However, some arguments favor the hypothesis that the results are essentially the same as those of the bosonic version, see
[
11
]
. This hypothesis can be easily tested in the NS sector, and we plan to extend the investigation to the Ramond sector as well. To do so, one needs to explicitly compute certain correlators involving Ramond fields.

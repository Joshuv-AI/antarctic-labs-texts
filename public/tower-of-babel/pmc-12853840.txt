Fatty acid binding protein 4 as a diagnostic biomarker for peripheral arterial disease in type 2 diabetes: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Bishal Kumar Dey, Pieu Adhikary, Subir Chandra Swar, Mousumi Mukhopadhyay, Pranab Kumar Sahana
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPeripheral arterial disease (PAD) is a serious atherosclerotic cardiovascular disease frequently associated with prolonged type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2D). It is marked by arterial plaque formation that impairs blood flow to the limbs, leading to symptoms like claudication, ischemic rest pain, ulcers, and gangrene. Fatty acid binding protein 4 (FABP4) is a molecule that plays a crucial role in regulating lipid transport and metabolism. However, the role of Fatty Acid Binding Protein 4 in PAD remains poorly understood. Understanding the involvement of FABP4 in PAD could aid early diagnosis and therapeutic strategies.MethodologyThis is a small, single-centre observational cross-sectional study. Trial was registered in CTRI (CTRI/2022/12/047992) and the date of registration is 12/12/2022. The study enrolled 90 individuals from West Bengal, India, categorized into healthy controls (n = 30), T2D patients (n = 30), and PAD patients (n = 30; all with T2D and ABI <0.9). Serum FABP4 levels were measured using ELISA, and physical (ABI, blood pressure, BMI) and biochemical (lipid profile, glucose levels) parameters were evaluated. Statistical analyses included ANOVA, correlation studies, and ROC curve analysis to assess FABP4’s diagnostic utility.ResultsMean FABP4 levels were significantly higher in PAD patients with T2D (6.808 ± 0.6930 ng/mL) compared to both T2D patients (3.726 ± 0.4643 ng/mL) and healthy controls (2.947 ± 0.3948 ng/mL). ROC analysis confirmed FABP4’s diagnostic value for PAD (AUC = 0.8257, p<0.0001), with a threshold >3.95 ng/mL indicating PAD and 2.88–3.95 ng/mL indicating the T2D range. This threshold may be considered useful for the diagnosis of PAD as a novel approach in Indian population. FABP4 positively correlated with BMI, diastolic BP, and fasting glucose, while negatively correlating with systolic BP, total cholesterol, HDL, and LDL cholesterol.ConclusionFABP4 may be a promising biomarker for identifying PAD in individuals with Type 2 diabetes in Indian population, with a threshold >3.95 ng/mL. This may be considered as the first Indian cohort threshold value for PAD diagnosis in diabetes.Keywords: FABP4, PAD, T2D, ABI, Cholesterol

Introduction

Type 2 Diabetes Mellitus (T2D) is defined as the form of metabolic disorder, characterized by pancreatic β–cell dysfunction and insulin resistance (IR). Pancreatic β–cell dysfunction influences the production of non-functional insulin, and that insulin fails to bind its corresponding receptor of insulin-sensitizing cells results in the inhibition of glucose transport into the cells [1]. This increase in blood glucose or hyperglycemia affects the metabolic equilibrium and may result in debilitating complications like diabetic retinopathy, diabetic neuropathy, diabetic neuropathy, diabetic foot ulcer, periodontitis, etc. However, a few comorbidities are also found to be associated with T2D, such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia, cardiovascular diseases, obesity, chronic kidney disease, depression & anxiety, tuberculosis, and a few types of cancers [2]. T2D and atherosclerotic cardiovascular diseases (ASCVD) share common risk factors like obesity, physical inactivity, family history, ethnicity, and high blood pressure.

Peripheral arterial disease (PAD) is one of the serious ASCVDs that may be considered a consequence of metabolic disorders. It has a direct relationship with prolonged T2D. In PAD, the atherosclerotic plaque forms in the arteries, narrowing or blocking the lumen of the artery, which helps in the blood flow towards the limbs [3]. Intermittent claudication is one of the important symptoms during the development of PAD. The other severe symptoms are ischemic rest pain, ulcers, and gangrene. The potential diagnostic methods of PAD are measurement of ankle brachial index (ABI) and toe brachial index (TBI), doppler ultrasounds of the arterial system of the lower limb, and angiography. According to Fontaine Classification, PAD has four different stages: Stage I (Asymptomatic), Stage IIa (mild claudication), Stage IIb (moderate to severe claudication), Stage III (ischemic rest pain), and Stage IV (ulceration or gangrene) [4]. During PAD, the commonly used medicines are antidiabetics, antihypertensives, statins, and antiplatelets in different combinations depending upon the clinical manifestation.

The progression of T2D is promoted by the altered expression of some adipokines and their contribution to the activation of different downstream pathways. These are the hormones secreted by adipose tissues primarily, such as adiponectin, leptin, fetuin-A, resistin, visfatin, vimentin, omentin, chemerin, etc., and they may facilitate the activation of different meta-inflammatory pathways. These are potentially able to activate inflammatory or anti-inflammatory responses during the development of disease [5]. During T2D, downregulation of adiponectin and upregulation of leptin are significantly associated with the development of low-grade inflammation [6]. However, the role of most of the adipokines during T2D has been poorly understood due to the complexity of their structures and functions, and they may facilitate the shift of hyperglycemia towards different comorbidities. PAD is one of those comorbidities that evolves from prolonged effect of hyperglycaemia. Elevated levels of a few adipokines have already been found to be associated with PAD such as high molecular weight (HMW) adiponectin and resistin.

Fatty acid binding protein 4 (FABP4) is one of those adipokines that has been poorly understood in its role in the pathophysiology of T2D. FABP4 plays an important role in human physiology due to its contribution in the transport of fatty acids across the cell membrane, lipid metabolism, lipolysis, and conducting signal transduction cascade in lipid metabolism with interacting hormone-sensitive lipase (HSL) and peroxisome proliferator-activated receptor – γ (PPARγ) [7]. However, FABP4 is also associated with the development of metabolic disorders and cardiovascular diseases through promoting inflammatory responses. FABP4 also promotes the formation of atherosclerotic plaque in the endothelium of the artery. Therefore, FABP4 may be considered a crucial factor that may influence the ASCVDs in the presence of T2D. The role of FABP4 in the pathophysiology of PAD is also not clear [8]. Even the cut-off in the circulation of the serum level FABP4 is not known, which may help in the early diagnosis of PAD.

During PAD, FABP4 may play an important role in the pathophysiology because FABP4 is associated with plaque formation in the endothelium [9]. Our study aimed to understand the clinically relevant cut-off for the FABP4 in the PAD population and the correlation with other clinical and biochemical parameters in the population of West Bengal, India.

In India, the prevalence of PAD is between 7.6% and 26.7% [10]. However, the delayed diagnosis of PAD becomes a problem, particularly in a primary healthcare setup, resulting in delayed treatment and its painful consequences. In T2D, it is difficult to identify the progression of PAD because polyneuropathy masks the signs and symptoms of PAD. As PAD is considered as one of the important causes of amputation, estimation of serum FABP4 level may be crucial to diagnose PAD at an early stage if an Indian diagnostic threshold can be generated.

Participants, materials & methods

Subject recruitment

In this observational exploratory study, we recruited age and gender matched individuals without T2D (N = 30), with T2D (N = 30), and T2D with PAD (N = 30). It was a single-centre cross-sectional study. The segregation of individuals with and without diabetes was carried out based on the concentration of fasting plasma glucose (FPG), the concentration of postprandial plasma glucose (PPG), and the percentage of glycated haemoglobin (%HbA1c). However, the inclusion of individuals with PAD was carried out by considering them diabetic, ABI < 0.9, and having intermittent claudication and either Doppler or angiographic evidence of PAD. A detailed history and clinical examination were done. They were invited to the research lab of the Department of Endocrinology & Metabolism, IPGME&R and SSKM Hospital at an 8 -12-hour fasting condition for the collection of peripheral venous blood in EDTA vials and Clot vials for the biochemical parameters. Diabetic participants with and without PAD were already receiving proper medication as per standard protocol. Trial was registered in CTRI (CTRI/2022/12/047992) and the date of registration is 12/12/2022. The study was approved (IPGME&R/IEC/2022/407 dated 19.09.2022) by IPGME&R Research Oversight Committee (Institutional Ethics Committee). All participants provided written informed consent. The study adhered to the principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki (2013).

Inclusion criteria of subjects

Both male and female diabetic subjects (with or without PAD) and healthy subjects in the age range of 18–65 years were included in the study according to the guidelines proposed by the American Diabetes Association (ADA).

Exclusion criteria of subjects

We excluded type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1D), pregnancy, recent major cardiovascular events in the last 3 months, patients with stage 3b to stage 5 kidney disease, chronic infections (HIV, Hepatitis etc.), chronic inflammatory disease, malignancy, patients receiving immunosuppressive agents, patients with active COVID infection, and patients with organ failure.

Reporting guidelines

The manuscript was prepared in accordance with the STROBE (Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology) statement for cross-sectional studies. Clinical examination and measurement of physical parameters

We have measured physical parameters as height, weight, BMI, ABI, Systolic Blood Pressure, and Diastolic Blood Pressure for every research participant. Detailed cardiovascular system and other systemic examinations were done. Foot examination, including neuropathy assessment by 10-g monofilament and vascular assessment by ABI (Smartdop XT (Japan)), was done.

Estimation of biochemical parameters

The estimation of the biochemical parameters was carried out with the help of an XL-1000 autoanalyzer (Transasia, India). The conducted tests were the estimation of triglyceride, total cholesterol, LDL cholesterol, HDL cholesterol, and fasting plasma glucose (FPG), and creatinine. Estimation of HbA1c was carried out by the HPLC method employing D–10 Haemoglobin Testing System (Bio-Rad, USA).

Estimation of serum FABP4

The serum level of FABP4 was estimated employing the sandwich ELISA method by Human FABP4 ELISA Kit of Elabscience, USA [cat no. #E-EL-H0285]. The standard curve was obtained by measuring the optical density (O.D.) of known concentrations at 450 nm to determine the unknown concentrations. The intra- and inter-assay coefficients of variation were below 10% [Antibody registry no. RRID: AB_3696582]. The detection range of the used kit was in between 0.39 ng/mL to 25 ng/mL. However, the ELISA kit is sensitive enough to detect the lowest range of concentration from 0.23 ng/mL. Therefore, it was sensitive enough to detect the lowest to highest ranges of concentrations accurately validated during standardization with analytical and biological triplicates.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using GraphPad Prism software (V.9.1.0; San Diego, CA, USA). The ‘Kolmogorov–Smirnov test’ was performed to check the normal distribution of the data obtained from the individuals of three groups. The representation of the data was carried out in Mean ± Standard Deviation (S.D.) form. The one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) was employed to compare the means of the three groups, followed by Tukey’s post-hoc test (parametric) or Kruskal-Wallis test (non-parametric). Dunn’s multiple comparison test (non-parametric) or unpaired t–test (parametric) was employed to compare the significant differences between groups. Spearman’s or Pearson’s correlations were performed to find out significant associations between variables. The diagnostic utility of FABP4 during PAD was estimated by receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve and area under the curve (AUC) analysis. A value of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

We conducted sample size calculation employing G*power tool. The observed serum level change in FABP4 concentration was employed with 10 patients from each group, yielding an effect size (Cohen’s d) of 0.66. An independent F-test was performed to compare the difference between the three groups, followed by a compromise power analysis with the parameters- β/α ratio = 1, desired sample size = 90, and number of groups = 3. Based on these inputs, the required power (1 – β error probability) was obtained as 0.99.

Subsequently, we recruited 30 healthy controls, 30 T2D participants, and 30 PAD + T2D participants from a total of 150 participants. Given the deviation from the originally calculated sample size, a post-hoc power analysis was conducted using the observed effect size and adjusted α error. The results indicated that the statistical power of the study remained unaltered, supporting the reliability of our findings.

The prevalence of PAD increased with age, with PAD + T2D individuals being the oldest (55.74 ± 9.62 years), followed by T2D (45.77 ± 6.65 years) and healthy controls (40.48 ± 10.56 years). Body mass index (BMI) was significantly higher in T2D individuals (25.36 ± 2.58 kg/m²) compared to healthy controls (24.12 ± 2.57 kg/m²), while PAD + T2D individuals had a lower BMI (23.65 ± 1.95 kg/m²) than both other groups. Systolic blood pressure was significantly higher in T2D individuals (143.8 ± 9.45 mmHg) compared to PAD + T2D (135.3 ± 14.57 mmHg) and healthy controls (134.4 ± 6.55 mmHg). Diastolic blood pressure showed a trend toward an increase in T2D (82.16 ± 7.46 mmHg) compared to healthy controls (77.44 ± 6.87 mmHg), while PAD + T2D individuals had values (80.3 ± 6.49 mmHg) similar to controls.

Biochemical parameters

Demographic parameters were summarized in Table 1. Serum creatinine was significantly higher in PAD + T2D (0.926 ± 0.261 mg/dL) and T2D (0.881 ± 0.366 mg/dL) compared to healthy controls (0.576 ± 0.181 mg/dL). Total cholesterol and LDL levels were significantly lower in PAD + T2D (126.2 ± 39.67 mg/dL and 58.37 ± 22.27 mg/dL) and T2D (133.1 ± 42.69 mg/dL and 67.81 ± 26.73 mg/dL) compared to healthy controls (170.5 ± 30.31 mg/dL and 85.68 ± 15.70 mg/dL). Triglycerides and HDL levels were relatively similar between groups: triglycerides—129.4 ± 44.82 mg/dL (PAD + T2D), 128.2 ± 63.48 mg/dL (T2D), 146.6 ± 65.63 mg/dL (healthy); HDL—31.26 ± 8.58 mg/dL (PAD + T2D), 31.68 ± 9.20 mg/dL (T2D), 36.96 ± 10.91 mg/dL (healthy). Fasting blood glucose and HbA1c were significantly higher in both T2D and PAD + T2D groups (FPG: 122.4 ± 20.71 mg/dL in T2D, 122.3 ± 20.07 mg/dL in PAD + T2D; HbA1c: 7.82 ± 1.11% in T2D, 7.86 ± 1.08% in PAD + T2D) compared to healthy controls (FPG: 90.92 ± 4.16 mg/dL, HbA1c: 5.09 ± 0.29%). FABP4 levels were significantly elevated in PAD + T2D (6.81 ± 3.60 ng/mL) compared to T2D (3.73 ± 2.59 ng/mL) and healthy controls (2.95 ± 1.97 ng/mL).

Serum level concentration of FABP4

The estimated serum level of FABP4 was found to be significantly higher in PAD individuals with T2D (6.808 ± 0.6930 ng/mL) compared to healthy controls (2.947 ± 0.3948 ng/mL) (Figure 3 A). The concentration of FABP4 is also significantly higher in PAD individuals with T2D compared to individuals with T2D without PAD (3.726 ± 0.4643 ng/mL) (Figure 3 A). No significant difference was found in the serum level concentration of FABP4 between healthy controls and individuals with T2D.

ROC analysis for FABP4

We compared the area under the curve (AUC) employing receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis in the case of Control-T2D and Control-PAD comparisons for the concentrations of FABP4 (Figure 3B). We found that the AUC is significantly larger in the case of the Control-PAD [Area = 0.8257, p < 0.0001] compared to Control-T2D [Area = 0.5871; p = 0.2659]. Similarly, the AUC is also significant in the case of T2D-PAD comparison [Area = 0.7443; p = 0.0014] (Figure 3 C). The range of concentrations of ROC lies between the Control-PAD curve, which is more sensitive and specific than that of the Control-T2D and T2D-PAD curve. The threshold of the T2D range of FABP4 concentration lies between 2.88 ng/mL and 3.95 ng/mL at 67.5% sensitivity and 66% specificity. The increase in concentration of FABP4 above 3.95 ng/mL (74.07% sensitivity and 76% specificity) may indicate the initiation of the PAD.

Correlation between FABP4 and biochemical parameters in PAD

Correlations were demonstrated in Fig. 4. We obtained a significant positive correlation between FABP4 and BMI [r = 0.4192; p = 0.0465]. We found a significant positive correlation between FABP4 and systolic BP [r = 0.5248; p = 0.0101] in the control group. However, we found a significant negative correlation of FABP4 with systolic BP [r = -0.4698; p = 0.0274] in the PAD group and no significant correlation in the T2D group. We found a significant positive correlation between FABP4 and diastolic BP [r = 0.4038; p = 0.0367]. We obtained significant negative correlation between FABP4 and total cholesterol [r = -0.4564; p = 0.0376], HDL cholesterol [r = -0.3997; p = 0.0477] and LDL cholesterol [r = -0.4166; p = 0.0480] in the PAD group and no significant correlation in the T2D group. However, we found a significant positive correlation between FABP4 and LDL cholesterol [r = 0.4096; p = 0.0469] in the control group. We found a significant positive correlation between FABP4 and FPG [r = 0.4727; p = 0.0353].

Discussion

Type 2 diabetes is a chronic metabolic disorder with an array of complications that develops over the course of the disease. PAD is 2–7 times higher in T2D compared to the general population. PAD is the worldwide leading cause of nontraumatic limb loss. The diagnosis of PAD is often overlooked as it requires high clinical suspicion and appropriate investigations [11].

FABP 4 is a low molecular weight protein expressed in adipocytes and macrophages. It has been linked with different metabolic conditions like metabolic syndrome, cardiovascular disease and T2D [12].

The concentration of FABP4 may vary due to the genetic dissimilarities that vary from one ethnicity to another. We found the threshold range in the serum level concentration of FABP4 that is normal at less than 2.88 ng/mL, 2.88–3.95 ng/mL for the T2D subjects, and above 3.95 ng/mL for the PAD subjects. From ROC analysis, we obtained that the area under the curve is significantly higher in the case of the Control-PAD curve (Area = 0.8267, p < 0.0001) with respect to the Control-T2D curve (Area = 0.5871, p = 0.2659). However, a large number of concentrations of FABP4 were obtained under the T2D-PAD curve (Area = 0.7443, p = 0.0014). This determination of the FABP4 range may be considered useful in the detection of PAD, as FABP4 is an important biomarker for PAD. The threshold range of FABP4 may add an extra value for the betterment of the diagnosis of PAD.

The FABP4 expression may not be affected by the use of statins because statins were found to increase the expression of FABP4 in macrophages and adipose tissues [13]. However, these metabolic disturbances influence the activation of inflammatory pathways. During inflammation, polarisation of macrophages is a common phenomenon and monocytes are being converted into M1 macrophages [14]. FABP4 has been found to activate the M1 polarisation pathway [15]. We found that an increase in FABP4 during PAD is also associated inversely with total cholesterol, HDL & LDL cholesterol under medication with hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA) reductase group of agents (statins). This inverse relationship with LDL and cholesterol is probably due to intensive statin therapy in the PAD group. M1 macrophage plays an important role in plaque formation in the endothelium of the artery by transforming itself into foam cells. However, higher serum levels of total cholesterol and LDL cholesterol may be considered as important factors in the transformation of foam cells (lipid-filled M1 macrophage) [16]. The formation of plaque in the arterial endothelium of limbs (primarily legs) prevents blood circulation.

The secretion of FABP4 may occur due to consuming a high-fat diet that promotes IR to induce T2D. However, FABP4 is the adipokine that may prevent the proper lipid metabolism in a higher serum concentration at advanced T2D which serves as a risk factor of cardiovascular diseases like PAD [17]. In a hyperglycaemic condition, it was observed that high concentrations of FABP4 may influence the conversion of plasma glucose into free fatty acids [18]. Due to its role in lipid metabolism and insulin sensitivity, it encourages cells in the fatty acid permeability, and these fatty acid molecules fail to get oxidized. Therefore, excess plasma glucose is stored as lipid, which increases the risk of arterial plaque formation [19]. We found a positive correlation between FABP4 and FPG during PAD, and this association may also serve as a trigger in the activation of the development of PAD from advanced T2D. Increased FABP4 was found to be inversely associated with systolic BP and directly associated with diastolic BP. However, these patients were being treated with a calcium channel blocker (like amlodipine) and an angiotensin receptor blocker (like telmisartan) to improve their blood pressure during the treatment of PAD individuals [20]. But the significant positive correlation between FABP4 and diastolic BP may be an indication that FABP4 may play an important role in the development of hypertension.

Zhang et al. found that local accumulation of FABP4 may be a significant reason in the development of knee osteoarthritis (KOA) [21]. Before the incidence of KOA, excess localized FABP4 accumulation increases the chance of a specific form of PAD, namely Infrapatellar fat PAD (IPFP). The circulatory concentration of IL–6 was observed in the development of IPFP-mediated KOA [22]. A positive association was studied between FABP4 and Nrf2 in the development of PAD. Ding et al. claimed that the concentration of FABP4 is more likely to be associated with females instead of males in the development of PAD [23]. Most of the contemporary researches were focused on exploring different associations of FABP4 in PAD, where studies lacked validation of serum FABP4 concentration in the diagnosis of PAD. In India, there was no such exploratory study conducted to understand the role of the FABP4 in the development of PAD, understanding its change in serum level concentration [24].

In this exploratory study, there might be a confounding effect of prolonged medication on the expression of serum level FABP4. But the mechanism is still unexplored in the alteration of FABP4 concentration. However, the conventional drugs that are used to treat PAD patients (statins, calcium channel blockers, etc.) are not inhibitors of FABP4, as FABP4 does not have any drug binding site for these conventional drugs practised in the treatment of T2D as well as PAD [25]. Although statins, calcium-channel blockers and several antidiabetic agents have been reported to lower FABP4 expression or circulating levels, they are not known to act as direct FABP4-binding inhibitors [26]. Presently, there is no such diagnostic threshold for PAD that can be correlated clinically with PAD for the diagnosis of the disease in the Indian population. This exploratory study may be considered as the very first approach to determine the threshold value for the concentrations of FABP4 in PAD. FABP4 interacts with several target molecules, including Fatty Acid Binding Protein 1 (FABP1), Peroxisome Proliferator-Activated Receptor Gamma (PPARγ), Cluster of Differentiation 36 (CD36), Glutamic-Oxaloacetic Transaminase 2 (GOT2), Hormone-Sensitive Lipase (LIPE), Lipoprotein Lipase (LPL), Perilipin 1 (PLIN1), Adiponectin with C1Q and Collagen Domain (ADIPOQ), CCAAT/Enhancer-Binding Protein Alpha (CEBPα), and Scavenger Receptor Class B Member 2 (SCARB2). Most of these molecules are critically involved in the pathogenesis of atherosclerosis, which is a major underlying cause of peripheral artery disease (PAD) [27]. However, the interaction network of FABP4 generated through the STRING database offers valuable bioinformatic evidence by highlighting its potential molecular partners and pathways implicated in disease pathogenesis (Fig. 5). It is important to note that the sensitivity and specificity of a molecule may decrease due to its involvement in multiple pathways, yet such involvement underscores its crucial role in molecular signalling.

Strength

This study gives insightful evidence of the interaction between FABP4 and PAD though in a small cohort. The study will help scientists for further exploration of role of FABP4 in PAD. This threshold value though in Indian ethnicity for FABP4 may be useful in the PAD diagnosis in the early stage and subsequently implemented over a primary and secondary healthcare centre where necessary equipment and infrastructure may not be present to diagnose PAD.

Limitation

This was a single-centre study where small sample size, effect of medication, and cross–sectional design may be considered as the limitations. We could not follow up the patients so trajectory cannot be determined. Further studies specifically to evaluate this method with gold standard test are required.

Future direction

This threshold value will be helpful for the diagnosis of PAD, but a multicentric validation will be required. However, the effect of prolonged medication and the change of serum FABP4 level need to be monitored in a longitudinal manner to validate the ineffectiveness of medication on FABP4 in T2D or PAD. In the future, we plan to initiate a larger sample prospective study.

Conclusion

This study reveals the importance of FABP4 in the diagnosis of PAD, depending upon the threshold value in Indian population. Our results demonstrate that serum FABP4 levels are significantly elevated in PAD patients compared to both healthy controls and T2D individuals, suggesting its potential as a biomarker for PAD. Serum FABP4 threshold range (normal < 2.88 ng/mL; 2.88–3.95 ng/mL for T2D; > 3.95 ng/mL for PAD) was established, which could help in the diagnosis of PAD in the Indian population. However, this is clear that the FABP4 increase in PAD in diabetes, its diagnostic significance and accuracy, need to be tested in prospective study comparing the diagnostic power of this marker with gold standard tests.

Stability Estimates in Kinetic Wasserstein Distances for the Vlasov–Poisson System with Yudovich Density
Jonathan Junné, Alexandre Rege

Abstract
. We investigate the stability of solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system using the unifying framework of the kinetic Wasserstein distance, introduced by Iacobelli in (Section 4 in Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 244 (2022), no. 1, 27-50). This allows us to treat both macroscopic densities that lie in a Yudovich space, as recently considered by Crippa et al. (Theorem 1.6 in Nonlinearity 37 (2024), no. 9, 095015) for the 1 1 -Wasserstein distance, and higher order Wasserstein distances, for which only bounded macroscopic densities were treated by Iacobelli and the first author (Theorem 1.11 in Bull. Lond. Math. Soc. 56 (2024), 2250-2267). First, we establish an L p L^{p} -estimate on the difference between two force fields in terms of a suitable nonlinear quantity that controls the kinetic Wasserstein distance between their macroscopic densities. Second, we use this estimate in order to derive a closable Osgood-type inequality for the kinetic Wasserstein distance between two solutions. This enables us to prove our main theorem; for 1 ≤ p < + ∞ 1\leq p<+\infty we show the p p -Wasserstein stability of solutions to the Vlasov–Poisson system with macroscopic densities belonging to a Yudovich space.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
The Vlasov–Poisson system

We consider the Vlasov–Poisson system for electrostatic or gravitational interaction. This system describes the dynamics of interacting particles, which are modelled by a distribution function
f\equiv f(t,x,v)
depending on time
t\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
, position
x\in\mathcal{X}
denoting either the
d
-dimensional torus
\mathbb{T}^{d}
or the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and velocity
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. It is given by the following set of equations:

(VP)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f-\nabla U_{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
\sigma\Delta U_{f}=\rho_{f}-1_{\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}},\\
\rho_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\>dv,\end{cases}

where
\sigma=\pm 1
encodes either gravitational (
\sigma=1
) or electrostatic (
\sigma=-1
) interaction and
U_{f}\equiv U_{f}(t;x)
is the self-induced gravitational or electrostatic potential.

Throughout this paper, we will consider two Lagrangian weak solutions
f_{1}
and
f_{2}
to (
VP
) with respective initial data
f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)
, which are probability distributions, and respective flows
Z_{1}:=(X_{1},V_{1})
and
Z_{2}:=(X_{2},V_{2})
. These flows satisfy the system of characteristics

(CH-VP)

\dot{X}=V,\quad\dot{V}=-\nabla U_{f},\quad X(0;x,v)=x,\quad V(0;x,v)=v.

Lagrangian weak solutions are weak solutions that can be written as pushforwards of the initial data by flows solving (
CH-VP
); in this case
f_{1}(t)=Z_{1}(t;\cdot,\cdot)_{\#}f_{1}(0)
and
f_{2}(t)=Z_{2}(t;\cdot,\cdot)_{\#}f_{2}(0)
. For notational convenience, unless otherwise stated, all quantities are always evaluated at time
t
and we denote
X_{1}:=X_{1}(t;x,v),\,V_{1}:=V_{1}(t;x,v),\,X_{2}:=X_{2}(t;y,w),\,V_{2}:=V_{2}(t;y,w),\,\nabla U_{f_{i}}(X_{j}):=\nabla U_{f_{i}}(t;X_{j})
for
i,j=1,2
.

We investigate the stability of solutions to (
VP
) in the Wasserstein distances of order
p\geq 1
, which we recall on the phase space
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
for convenience (see
[
20
, Chapter 6]
):

Definition 1.1

(Wasserstein distances)
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
Wasserstein distance
of order
p\geq 1
, between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as

W_{p}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\left\{\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert v-w\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi(x,v,y,w)\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
is the set of
couplings
; that is, the set of probability measures with
marginals

\mu
and
\nu
. A coupling is said to be
optimal
if it minimizes the Wasserstein distance.

We begin by mentioning the major contribution of Loeper
[
15
, Theorem 1.2]
, where the uniqueness and stability of the Vlasov–Poisson system (
VP
), assuming the macroscopic density remains bounded, were established using an optimal transport argument involving the 2-Wasserstein distance. Loeper’s main observation was that the
L^{2}
-norm of the difference of force fields can be controlled in terms of the 2-Wasserstein distance between their macroscopic densities
[
15
, Theorem 2.9]
.

The uniqueness criterion was subsequently improved by Miot
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
relying on the second order structure of the characteristic ODE (
CH-VP
) to allow for macroscopic densities satisfying

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{r\geq 1}\frac{\left\lVert\rho_{f}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{r}<+\infty,

and succeeded in highlighting unbounded densities that satisfy this condition
[
16
, Theorem 1.2]
.
These results were later generalized, together with Holding
[
10
, Theorem 1.1]
, to provide an effective
W_{1}
stability estimate allowing for exponential Orlicz macroscopic densities verifying

\max_{t\in[0,T]}\sup_{r\geq\alpha>1}\frac{\left\lVert\rho_{f}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{r^{1/\alpha}}<+\infty.

Finally, in a very recent contribution
[
4
]
, Crippa, Inversi, Saffirio and Stefani established a
W_{1}
stability estimate for macroscopic densities within uniformly-localized Yudovich spaces
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
. Their Lagrangian approach was inspired by a previous work of Crippa and Stefani
[
3
]
on the well-posedness of the two-dimensional Euler equations. Similarly to Miot’s work, by using the propagation of moments available for (
VP
), they provided explicit examples of unbounded macroscopic densities in this uniformly localized Yudovich space
[
4
, Theorem 1.7 & Proposition 1.8]
.

Compared to Loeper’s approach, the aforementioned
W_{1}
stability estimates not only accommodate a broader class of macroscopic densities for which the uniqueness of (
VP
) is guaranteed, but also provide improved control over the time interval during which two initially close solutions remain close. Generally, the
1
-Wasserstein distance is more flexible, and, the
W_{1}
stability estimates obtained so far do not rely on optimal transport theorems, whereas Loeper’s stability estimate in the stronger
2
-Wasserstein distance heavily depends on them.

To improve control over the time interval in Loeper’s stability estimate and obtain an effective
W_{2}
stability estimate
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
, Iacobelli introduced the kinetic Wasserstein distance in
[
11
]
, specifically designed to account for the anisotropy between position and momentum that is inherent to kinetic problems (see
[
11
, Section 4]
):

Definition 1.2

(kinetic Wasserstein distance)
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The
kinetic Wasserstein distance
of order
p
, with
p\geq 1
, between
\mu
and
\nu
is defined as

W_{\lambda,p}(\mu,\nu):=\left(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\left\{D_{p}(\pi,\lambda)\right\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where
D_{p}(\pi,\lambda)
is the unique number
s
such that

s-\lambda(s)\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi(x,v,y,w)=\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert v-w\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi(x,v,y,w),

with
\lambda:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
a decreasing function.

The kinetic Wasserstein distance allowed Iacobelli to obtain the quasi-neutral limit of (
VP
) towards the kinetic isothermal Euler system allowing for
W_{2}
perturbations of analytic data with almost optimal rate
[
11
, Corollary 3.3 & Remark 3.4]
, as a consequence of the enhanced
W_{2}
stability estimate. Subsequently, with Griffin-Pickering, they obtained the stability of the quasi-neutral limit for the ionic with exponentially small Wasserstein perturbations
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
(see
[
8
]
for a recent account on the subject). The kinetic Wasserstein distance approach also allowed Iacobelli and Lafleche
[
13
, Theorem 2.4]
to improve the convergence rate in the semi-classical analogue to the
2
-Wasserstein distance of the semi-classical limit of the quantum Hartree system towards the classical (
VP
).

Regarding improved stability estimates, we also mention a recent work of Iacobelli and the first author
[
12
, Theorems 1.9 & 1.11]
where Loeper’s approach and the kinetic Wasserstein approach were generalized to the
p
-Wasserstein setting for
1<p<+\infty
and of the second author
[
18
, Theorem 2.1]
dealing with the stability of solutions for the magnetized Vlasov–Poisson system (
VPB
), where the kinetic Wasserstein distance was necessary to control the additional terms coming from the varying external magnetic field. In both works, the controls on the time interval match the effective stability estimate in the weaker
1
-Wasserstein distance obtained by Holding and Miot
[
10
,
11
]
.

1.2.
Yudovich spaces and assumptions

The two macroscopic densities
\rho_{f_{1}}
and
\rho_{f_{2}}
are assumed to be absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure and to belong to a Yudovich space (see
[
4
, Section 1.2]
):

Definition 1.3
.

Given a nondecreasing function
\Theta:[0,+\infty)\to(0,+\infty)
, called a
growth function
, the
Yudovich space

Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})
consists of all functions
h
that belong to the intersected Lebesgue spaces
\cap_{1\leq r<+\infty}L^{r}(\mathcal{X})
such that

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}:=\sup_{1\leq r<+\infty}\frac{\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})}}{\Theta(r)}<+\infty.

Note that only the asymptotic behavior of
\Theta
matters in the definition of the Yudovich space; for convenience, assume
\Theta(1)=1
. Throughout the text, we denote by
\cdot^{\prime}
the Lebesgue conjugate exponent.

Assumption 1.4
.

For some
T>0
, we assume that

A(t):=\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}(t)\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(t)\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{1+\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(1_{p=1}+1_{1<p<+\infty}\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{p}}\right)\in L^{1}([0,T]).

We consider suitable growth function for (
VP
) such that the Cauchy problem (
CH-VP
) is well-posed:

Assumption 1.5
.

The growth functions
\Theta
is such that the associated generalized modulus of continuity
\varphi_{\Theta}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is continuous, where

\varphi_{\Theta}(s):=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if }s=0,\\
s\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert)&\text{if }0<s<e^{-d-1},\\
e^{-d-1}(d+1)\Theta(d+1)&\text{if }s\geq e^{-d-1}.\end{cases}

Indeed,
\varphi_{\Theta}
is the modulus of continuity of the force field given that the macroscopic density is Yudovich, which is enforced by
Assumption

1.4
(see Crippa, Inversi, Saffirio and Stefani
[
4
, Lemma 1.1 and Assumption 1.3]
); as explained in
[
4
]
, the value
e^{-{d-1}}
in the definition of
\varphi_{\Theta}
is essentially irrelevant and included solely to make
\varphi_{\Theta}
more appealing. Under
Assumption

1.4
and
Assumption

1.5
, one can define weak solutions
f
to (
VP
) through

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left[\left(\partial_{t}\phi+v\cdot\nabla_{x}\phi-\nabla U_{f}\cdot\nabla_{v}\phi\right)f\right]\left(t;x,v\right)\>dxdv\>dt=-\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(0,x)f(0;x,v)\>dxdv

for all test functions
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T)\times(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}))
, since then the product of the solution with the force field is integrable; i.e.,
\left\lVert f(t)\nabla U_{f}(t)\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\in L^{1}([0,T])
.

While
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
assumed
\varphi_{\Theta}
to be nondecreasing concave in some regime for the
1
-Wasserstein stability of (
VP
), in the
p
-Wasserstein setting, we instead assume a
p
-modified version of
\varphi_{\Theta}
to be nondecreasing concave in some regime as follows:

Assumption 1.6
.

The growth function
\Theta
is such that
\varphi_{p,\Theta}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
is nondecreasing concave on
[0,c_{p,\Theta;d})
for some positive constant
c_{p,\Theta;d}<1/e
that depends only on
p
,
\Theta
, and
d
, where
\varphi_{p;\Theta}
is given by

\varphi_{p,\Theta}(s):=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }s=0,\\
s\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert^{p}\Theta^{p}\left(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\right)&\text{if }0<s\leq c_{p,\Theta;d},\\
\varphi_{p,\Theta}(c_{p,\Theta;d})&\text{if }s\geq c_{p,\Theta;d}.\end{cases}

Assumption

1.6
allows us to rely on Jensen’s inequality when controlling one of the two terms coming from the separation of force fields (see
Section

3.1
). This encompasses for instance the bounded case with
\Theta(r)=1
(and
c_{p,\Theta;d}=e^{-\max\{p,d+1\}}
), the exponential Orlicz space with
\Theta(r)=r^{1/\alpha}
and
1\leq\alpha<+\infty
(and
c_{p,\Theta;d}=e^{-\max\{p\beta,d+1\}}
,
\beta:=1+1/\alpha
), and also a family of iterated logarithms due to Yudovich
[
21
, Section 3]
for two-dimensional Euler’s equations in vorticity form;
\Theta_{n}:[0,+\infty)\to[0,+\infty)
for
n\geq 1
(
c_{p,\Theta;d}=\min\{\exp_{n+1}^{-2p}(1),e^{-d-1}\}
) given by

\Theta_{n}(r):=\begin{cases}r\left\lvert\log_{1}(r)\right\rvert^{2}\left\lvert\log_{2}(r)\right\rvert^{2}\cdots\left\lvert\log_{n}(r)\right\rvert^{2}&\text{if }r\geq\exp_{n}(1),\\
\Theta_{n}(\exp_{n}(1))&\text{else},\end{cases}

where
\exp_{0}(1):=1
,
\exp_{n+1}(1):=e^{\exp_{n}(1)}
, and

\log_{n}(r):=\underbrace{\log\circ\log\circ\cdots\circ\log}_{(n-1)\text{times}}\circ|\log r|.

Moreover, each of these cases satisfies the following assumption:

Assumption 1.7
.

The growth function
\Theta
is such that the following Osgood condition holds:

\int_{0}^{c_{p,\Theta;d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s\varphi_{\Theta}(s)}}\>ds=+\infty.

In that case, set

\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}(r):=\int_{r}^{c_{p,\Theta;d}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s\varphi_{\Theta}(s)}}\>ds

and denote by
\Psi^{-1}_{p,\Theta;d}
its inverse function.

Assumption

1.7
is the first-order version of
[
4
, Assumption 1.15]
which the authors see as a second-order Osgood condition; it is verified for the examples given in view of the proof of
[
4
, Proposition 1.8]
. In particular, the growth function
\Theta
cannot increase faster than linear with potential logarithmic corrections; it is asymptotically dominated by any polynomial of degree strictly bigger than
1
, say,
\Theta(r)=o(r^{1+\delta})
, as can be seen from

\int_{0^{+}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{s\varphi_{\Theta}(s)}}\>ds=-\int_{\infty^{-}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{r\Theta(r)}}dr\lesssim-\int_{\infty^{-}}\frac{1}{r^{1+\delta}}dr<+\infty.

A useful consequence of this domination is the existence of a constant
C_{p,\Theta}>0
that depends only on
p
,
\Theta
, and
d
such that for all
0<s<c_{p,\Theta;d}
, it holds

(1.1)

\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\Theta\left(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\right)}^{p}}\right)\right\rvert\leq C_{p,\Theta}\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert.

This inequality will arise naturally from the approach based on the kinetic Wasserstein distance;
\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert)}^{p}
will be the chosen
\lambda
creating the nonlinear anisotropy between position and momentum in the
Definition

1.2
of kinetic Wasserstein distance. In the respective order of the aforementioned cases (bounded, exponential Orlicz, and Yudovich macroscopic density with growth function
\Theta_{n}
), direct computations give the constants
C_{p,\Theta}=1+p/2,\,1+p\beta/2
, and
1+p(n+1)
respectively. Finally, we assume that a variant of (
1.1
) holds, which also appears naturally from this approach.

Assumption 1.8
.

The growth function
\Theta
is such that there is a constant
\overline{C}_{p,\Theta}>0
that depends only on
p
,
\Theta
, and
d
such that for all
0<s<c_{p,\Theta;d}
, it holds

\Theta\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\Theta\left(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\right)}^{p}}\right)\right\rvert\right)\leq\overline{C}_{p,\Theta}\Theta\left(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\right).

This assumption is verified for the three usual examples cited above (bounded, exponential Orlicz, and Yudovich macroscopic density with growth function
\Theta_{n}
) with
\overline{C}_{p,\Theta}=1,\,(1+p\beta/2)^{1/\alpha}
, and
(1+p(n+1))\times\Pi_{i=0}^{n}2^{i}\log_{i}(1+p(n+1))
.

1.3.
Stability estimates

Our main result provides
W_{p}
stability estimates in the range
1\leq p<+\infty
to (
VP
) with Yudovich macroscopic densities and extends Crippa, Inversi, Saffirio, and Stefani’s
W_{1}
stability estimate
[
4
, Theorem 1.6]
to higher order Wasserstein distances in a unified way.

Because the modulus of continuity for the force field is more singular for Yudovich macroscopic densities compared to the
\log
-Lipschitz modulus of continuity in the bounded case, Loeper’s approach is not applicable (see
Section

3.1
for details). Instead, we use an appropriate kinetic Wasserstein distance that accommodates the modulus of continuity, making it suitable for Yudovich macroscopic densities.

Theorem 1.9
.

Let
1\leq p<+\infty
. Let
f_{1},f_{2}
be two Lagrangian weak solutions to (
VP
) and
\Theta
a growth function such that
Assumptions

1.4
,
1.5
,
1.6
,
1.8
and
1.7
hold. Then there is a constant
c_{\Theta}>0
and a nonnegative function
J(t)\in L^{1}([0,T])
both depending only on
p,\Theta
and on
\mathcal{X}
such that if
W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))
is sufficiently small so that
W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))\leq c_{\Theta}
and

(1.2)

\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{p,\Theta}\left[W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right]\right)\geq\int_{0}^{t}J(s)\>ds,

where
\Phi^{-1}_{p,\Theta}
is the inverse of the map
s\mapsto s/\sqrt{\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert)}^{p}
, then the following stability estimate holds:

(1.3)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\leq\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}^{-1}\left(\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{p,\Theta}\left[W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right]\right)-\int_{0}^{t}J(s)\>ds\right).

Let us specify (
1.3
) explicitly for the aforementioned cases in
Section

1.2
where
\Phi^{-1}_{p,\Theta}
behaves like the map
\varphi_{p/2,\Theta}:s\mapsto s\sqrt{\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log s\right\rvert)}^{p}
near the origin:

•

The exponential Orlicz space,
\Theta(r)=r^{1/\alpha}
, which yields for
1<\alpha<+\infty
(
2>\beta=1+1/\alpha>1
),

(1.4)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\\
\leq\exp\left\{-\left(\sqrt{\left\lvert\log\Bigg\{W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))\sqrt{\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))\right\rvert}^{p\beta}\Bigg\}\right\rvert^{2-\beta}}\!-\left(\frac{2-\beta}{2}\right)\int_{0}^{t}J(s)\>ds\right)^{\frac{2}{2-\beta}}\right\}

and for
\alpha=1
(
\beta=2
),

(1.5)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\leq\exp\Bigg\{\log\left(W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right\rvert^{p}\right)\exp\left(-\int_{0}^{t}J(s)\>ds\right)\Bigg\}.

•

The bounded space,
\Theta(r)=1
, corresponding to the limiting case
\alpha\to+\infty
(
\beta\to 1
) in (
1.4
).

•

The Yudovich space with the family
\Theta_{n}
,

(1.6)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\\
\leq\left(W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\sqrt{\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right\rvert\Theta_{n}\left(\left\lvert\log W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right\rvert\right)}^{p}\right)^{\exp_{n+1}\left(-\int_{0}^{t}J(s)\>ds\right)}.

Theorem

1.9
positively answers Iacobelli’s suggestion
[
11
, Section 4]
: Obtaining a
W_{2}
stability estimate for (
VP
) in the exponential Orlicz setting should be possible based on the kinetic Wasserstein distance approach solely relying on the fact that
(\dot{X},\dot{V})=b(X,V)
for some sufficiently regular function
b
, rather than on the second-order structure
\ddot{X}=-\nabla U_{f}
as employed by Holding and Miot.

2.
An
L^{p}
-estimate via interpolation for Yudovich macroscopic densities

As discussed in
Section

1.1
, a crucial aspect of Loeper’s optimal transport approach, and, by extension, the kinetic Wasserstein distance approach, is the relationship between the
L^{2}
-norm of the difference of the force fields and the
2
-Wasserstein distance between their respective macroscopic densities. In the bounded setting, Loeper obtained the following estimate
[
15
, Theorem 2.9]
:

(2.1)

\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\sqrt{\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}}W_{2}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\lesssim\sqrt{Q(t)},

where
Q(t)
is the quantity given by

Q(t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert X_{1}(t;x,v)-X_{2}(t;y,w)\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{2}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)

and
\pi_{0}\in\Pi(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0))
is an optimal
W_{2}
coupling.

Here, we consider the following quantity inspired by the kinetic Wasserstein distance:

\displaystyle D_{p}(t)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\lambda(t)\left\lvert X_{1}(t;x,v)-X_{2}(t;y,w)\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert V_{1}(t;x,v)-V_{2}(t;y,w)\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)

\displaystyle=\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\lambda(t)\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}+\left\lvert v-w\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{t}(x,v,y,w),

where
\pi_{t}\in\Pi(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t))
is such that
\pi_{0}
is an optimal
W_{p}
coupling (see
[
7
, Section 4.1]
for a construction) and the
\lambda(t):=\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log D_{p}(t)\right\rvert)}^{p}
depends itself on
D_{p}
, whose specific choice, due to optimization considerations, will become apparent in
Section

3.2
. While
\lambda
depends on
D_{p}
, the implicit equation defining
D_{p}
remains well-posed, as an adaptation of
[
11
, Lemma 3.7]
ensures this under the condition that
D_{p}
is small enough so that
D_{p}\mapsto\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert\Theta(\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert)}^{p}
is nonincreasing, seen as a function of the quantity
D_{p}
. This quantity plays a similar role as does Loeper’s quantity
Q
in
[
15
]
; that is, to control the Wasserstein distance, and it holds

(2.2)

W_{p}^{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\leq\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda},\quad W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1},f_{2}\right)\leq D_{p}.

We obtain an
L^{p}
-version of Loeper’s
L^{2}
-estimate (
2.1
) for Yudovich macroscopic densities in terms of
D_{p}
:

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. Suppose that on
[0,T]
it holds

(2.3)

\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)\right\rvert>1.

Then there is a nonnegative function
C_{U}(t)\in L^{\infty}([0,T])
explicitly computable from the proof that only depends on
p
,
\Theta
,
\mathcal{X}
, and on the quantity
D_{p}(t)
such that

(2.4)

\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{2})-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(X_{2})\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\\
\leq C_{U}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right),

where we set

\widetilde{A}:=\left(1+\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

In addition, the following estimate holds:

(2.5)

\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{2})-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq e^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{U}\overline{A}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Theta\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right),\quad\overline{A}:=\widetilde{A}\times\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Remark 2.2
.

The
L^{p}
-estimate is only valid in a regime on
D_{p}
; that is,
D_{p}
has to be small enough, which is anyway required for it to be well-defined. Here, the regime appears through the definition of the Yudovich norm and is reminiscent of a regime that can be found already in the proof of the
W_{p}
stability estimate
[
12
, proof of Theorem 1.11]
in the bounded setting.

In the bounded setting, Iacobelli and the first author
[
12
, Proposition 1.8]
generalized Loeper’s estimate from
L^{2}
to
L^{p}
for
1<p<+\infty
via the Helmholtz-Weyl decomposition
[
5
, Theorem III.1.2]
for
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
or Calderon-Zygmund’s inequality
[
5
, Theorem II.11.4]
for
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and homogeneous Sobolev spaces on
\mathcal{X}
(see
[
5
, Chapter II.6]
):

Definition 2.3
.

The
homogeneous Sobolev space
is the Banach space

\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathcal{X}):=\left\{[g];\quad g\in W^{1,p}(\mathcal{X}),\quad\nabla g\in L^{p}(\mathcal{X})\right\},

where
[\cdot]:=\{\cdot+c;\>c\in\mathbb{R}\}
denotes the equivalence class of functions up to a constant, together with the norm

\left\lVert[g]\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{1,p}(\mathcal{X})}:=\left\lVert\nabla g\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}.

The
dual homogeneous Sobolev space

\dot{W}^{-1,p}(\mathcal{X})
is defined to be the topological dual of
\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
equipped with the
strong dual homogeneous Sobolev norm
.

Given a function
h
with
\int h=0
, by density of quotient test functions belonging to
\dot{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}):=\{[\phi];\>\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})\}
for
1<p<+\infty
, it holds

\displaystyle\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,p}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle:=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathbb{T}^{d}}h\,[g]\>dx;\quad g\in\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X}),\quad\left\lVert[g]\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 1\right\}

\displaystyle=\sup\left\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}h\,[\phi]\>dx;\quad[\phi]\in\dot{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X}),\quad\left\lVert[\phi]\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{1,p^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X})\leq 1}\right\}.

The following key lemma relates the Lebesgue
L^{p}
-norm to the dual homogeneous Sobolev
\dot{W}^{-1,p}
-norm for the difference of densities:

Lemma 2.4

(
[
12
, Lemma 1.7]
)
.

Let
1<p<+\infty
. Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
be two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
that are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure, and let
U_{i}
satisfy
\sigma\Delta U_{i}=\rho_{i}-1_{\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}}
in the distributional sense with
i=1,2
. Then there is a constant
C_{\mathrm{HW}}>0
that only depends on
p
and
\mathcal{X}
such that

(2.6)

\left\lVert\nabla U_{1}-\nabla U_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{\mathrm{HW}}\left\lVert\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,p}(\mathcal{X})}.

With the help of this
Lemma

2.4
and the existence of an optimal transport map adapted to our context, we will be able to prove
Proposition

2.1
, namely, the
L^{p}
-estimate.

Theorem 2.5

(Gangbo-McCann
[
6
, Theorem 1.2]
)
.

Let
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
be two probability measures on
\mathcal{X}
that are absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure. Then

W_{p}\left(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\right)=\left(\inf_{\mathrm{T}_{\#}\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}}\bigg\{\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert x-\mathrm{T}(x)\right\rvert^{p}\>d\rho_{1}(x)\bigg\}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the infimum runs over all measurable mappings
\mathrm{T}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
that push forward
\rho_{1}
onto
\rho_{2}
. Moreover, the infimum is reached by a
\rho_{1}(dx)
-almost surely unique mapping
\mathrm{T}
, and there is a
\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{p}
-convex function
\psi
such that
\mathrm{T}=\Id_{\mathcal{X}}-\left(\nabla\left\lvert\cdot\right\rvert^{p}\right)^{-1}\circ\nabla\psi
, where we denote
\left(\nabla h^{*}\right)
by
\left(\nabla h\right)^{-1}
for a function
h
with
h^{*}
its Legendre transform.

The last two ingredients are the uniform bound on the force fields:

Lemma 2.6

(
[
4
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\rho\in Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure. Let
U
satisfy
\sigma\Delta U=\rho-1_{\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}}
in the distributional sense. Then there exists a dimensional constant
C_{d}>0
depending on
\mathcal{X}
such that

(2.7)

\left\lVert\nabla U\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{d}\left(1+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right).

And a standard interpolation lemma:

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
h\in L^{b}\cap L^{c}(\mathcal{X})
, with
1\leq b<c\leq+\infty
. Then for any
b\leq a<c
, it holds

\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{a}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{b}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1/b-1/c}{1/a-1/c}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{L^{c}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1/a-1/b}{1/a-1/c}}.

Proof.

Write
\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{a}=\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{d}\left\lvert h\right\rvert^{d-a}
with

d=ab\frac{1/b-1/c}{1/a-1/c}.

Apply Hölder’s inequality to the product with the exponent
b/d
for the first term.
∎

We are now set to prove the
L^{p}
-estimate.

Proof of
Proposition

2.1
.

Let us denote by

\rho_{\theta}:=\left[(\theta-1)\mathrm{T}+(2-\theta)\mbox{Id}_{\mathcal{X}}\right]_{\#}\rho_{1}

the interpolant measure between
\rho_{1}:=\rho_{f_{1}}
and
\rho_{2}:=\rho_{f_{2}}
, where
\mathrm{T}
is the optimal transport map of
Theorem

2.5
. Let
\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
be a compactly supported test function. By the properties of pushforwards of measures, it follows immediately that

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)\>d\rho_{\theta}(x)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi\left((\theta-1)\mathrm{T}(x)+(2-\theta)x\right)d\rho_{1}(x).

Note that
\int\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}=0
as both
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
are probability measures, and Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem yields

\int_{\mathcal{X}}[\phi](x)\left(\rho_{1}(x)-\rho_{2}(x)\right)\>dx=\frac{d}{d\theta}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)\>d\rho_{\theta}(x)=\int_{\mathcal{X}}\nabla\phi\left((\theta-1)\mathrm{T}(x)+(2-\theta)x\right)\cdot(\mathrm{T}(x)-x)\>d\rho_{1}(x).

Now, by using Hölder’s inequality with respect to the measure
\rho_{1}
, we get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\theta}\int_{\mathcal{X}}\phi(x)\>d\rho_{\theta}(x)

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left((\theta-1)\mathrm{T}(x)+(2-\theta)x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}d\rho_{1}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}(x)-x\right\rvert^{p}\>d\rho_{1}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

(2.8)

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}d\rho_{\theta}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\mathrm{T}(x)-x\right\rvert^{p}d\rho_{1}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

The second term in the product is exactly
W_{p}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2})
by
Theorem

2.5
.

This is where the proof changes from the work of Iacobelli and the first author
[
12
, Proposition 1.8]
. For the first term in the product, the
L^{r}
-norm of the interpolant is controlled for all
r\geq 1
(see
[
19
, Remark 8]
);

(2.9)

\left\lVert\rho_{\theta}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}.

Thus, using Hölder’s inequality with respect to the Lebesgue measure and using the fact that
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\in Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})
, we can write for all
1<r<+\infty
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}}d\rho_{\theta}(x)\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\rho_{\theta}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{r}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}}.

We combine the above estimate with (
2.8
) which yields

\int_{\mathcal{X}}[\phi](x)\left(\rho_{1}(x)-\rho_{2}(x)\right)\>dx\leq\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla\phi\left(x\right)\right\rvert^{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}}W_{p}\left(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}\right),

and by restricting to quotient test functions
\left[\phi\right]\in\dot{\mathcal{D}}(\mathcal{X})
such that
\left\lVert\nabla\left[\phi\right]\right\rVert_{L^{p^{\prime}r^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X})}\leq 1
, recalling the definition of the dual homogeneous Sobolev norm, we obtain

\left\lVert\rho_{1}-\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{\dot{W}^{-1,\frac{pr}{r+p-1}}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}W_{p}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}),

because
(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}:=pr/(r+p-1)>1
as Lebesgue’s conjugate exponent. Applying the estimate (
2.6
) of
Lemma

2.4
, we obtain for all
1<r<+\infty
,

(2.10)

\left\lVert\nabla U_{1}-\nabla U_{2}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{pr}{r+p-1}}(\mathcal{X})}\leq C_{\mathrm{HW};\,(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}}\,\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{1}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{2}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}W_{p}(\rho_{1},\rho_{2}),

where the subscript in prefactor
C_{\mathrm{HW}}
specifies that it depends on the Lebesgue exponent
(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}
. Recall that the
L^{\infty}
-norms of both (negative of) force fields
\nabla U_{1}:=\nabla U_{f_{1}}
and
\nabla U_{2}:=\nabla U_{f_{2}}
are controlled by (
2.7
) thanks to
Lemma

2.6
. Now, since
(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}:=pr/(r+p-1)<p<+\infty
, we can use
Lemma

2.7
to interpolate (
2.10
) and (
2.7
) for

\frac{1/p-1/\infty}{1/(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}-1/\infty}=\frac{r}{r+p-1},\quad\frac{1/(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}-1/p}{1/(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}-1/\infty}=\frac{p-1}{r+p-1}

to deduce

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\frac{pr}{r+p-1}}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{pr}{r+p-1}}\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{p(p-1)}{r+p-1}}

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{U}^{p}\left(1+\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\Theta^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}W^{p}_{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\right)^{\frac{r}{r+p-1}}

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq\widetilde{C}_{U}^{p}\widetilde{A}^{p}\Theta^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\left[W_{p}^{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{r}{r+p-1}},

where we used that both
\Theta(r)\geq 1
and
\left\lVert\rho_{f_{i}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\geq 1
for
i=1,2
since it was assumed that
\Theta(1)=1
for convenience, and set

\widetilde{C}_{U}:=2C_{d}C_{\mathrm{HW};\,(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}},\quad\widetilde{A}:=\left(1+\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right)\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}.

Recall that
D_{p}
controls the
p
-Wasserstein distance as claimed in (
2.2
); indeed, since
(X_{1},X_{2})_{\#}\pi_{0}
has marginals
\rho_{f_{1}}
and
\rho_{f_{2}}
,

W_{p}^{p}\left(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}}\right):=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\rho_{f_{1}},\rho_{f_{2}})}\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}\>d\gamma(x,y)\leq\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{p}\>d\Big[\left(X_{1},X_{2}\right)_{\#}\pi_{0}\Big](x,y)\\
=\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)\leq\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}.

Hence, (
2.11
) becomes

(2.12)

\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}\leq\widetilde{C}_{U}^{p}\widetilde{A}^{p}\Theta^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}(r)\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{r}{r+p-1}}.

We choose

r:=\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert,

which is required to be strictly between
1
and
+\infty
. If
D_{p}/\lambda=0
, then, since it controls the
p
-Wasserstein distance between the macroscopic densities, we have
\nabla U_{f_{1}}=\nabla U_{f_{2}}
by construction, and the proof is complete. Therefore, without loss of generality, we may assume throughout the proof that
D_{p}(t)/\lambda(t)>0
on
[0,T]
, and in particular
r(t)<+\infty
on
[0,T]
. The regime dictated on

thus translates to

\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}(t)}{\lambda(t)}\right)\right\rvert>1

and coincides with the regime (
2.3
) of the statement of
Proposition

2.1
. In this regime, the particular choice of

gives

\displaystyle\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{r}{r+p-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{-(p-1)}{r+p-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\exp\left(\frac{p-1}{\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert+p-1}\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right)\leq e^{p-1}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right),

and combining with (
2.12
), this yields the
L^{p}
-estimate (
2.4
);

\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{p}\\
\leq C^{p}_{U}\widetilde{A}^{p}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\Theta^{\frac{p}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right)

with
C_{U}:=e^{1/p^{\prime}}\widetilde{C}_{U}
depending only on
p
,
\Theta
,
\mathcal{X}
, and on the quantity
D_{p}
through
C_{\mathrm{HW};(p^{\prime}r^{\prime})^{\prime}}
.

For the second estimate (
2.5
), recall that
\pi_{t}
has marginals
f_{1}(t)=Z_{1}(t)_{\#}f_{1}(0)
and
f_{2}(t)=Z_{2}(t)_{\#}f_{2}(0)
so that for all
\phi\in C_{b}((\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2})
, it holds

\int_{\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)^{2}}\phi\left(Z_{1}(t;x,v),Z_{2}(t;y,w)\right)\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)=\int_{\left(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\right)^{2}}\phi(x,v,y,w)\>d\pi_{t}(x,v,y,w).

In particular,

\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;X_{2}(t;y,w))-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(t;X_{2}(t;y,w))\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\\
=\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;y)-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(t;y)\right\rvert^{p}\rho_{f_{2}}(t;y)\>dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

By Hölder’s inequality with respect to the Lebesgue measure,

(2.13)

\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(y)-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(y)\right\rvert^{p}\rho_{f_{2}}(y)\>dy\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{pr^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X})}\Theta^{\frac{1}{p}}(r)\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Since
1<r:=\left\lvert\log(D_{p}/\lambda)\right\rvert<+\infty
;
p<pr^{\prime}<+\infty
, we can use
Lemma

2.7
to interpolate between
p
and
+\infty
for

\frac{1/(pr^{\prime})-1/+\infty}{1/p-1/+\infty}=\frac{1}{r^{\prime}},\quad\frac{1/p-1/(pr^{\prime})}{1/(pr^{\prime})-1/+\infty}=\frac{1}{r}

to get

\displaystyle\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{pr^{\prime}}(\mathcal{X})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{p}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\left\lVert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigg[C_{U}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right)\Bigg]^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Big[2C_{d}\left(1+\max\left\{\left\lVert\rho_{f_{1}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})},\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right\}\right)\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

(2.14)

\displaystyle\leq C_{U}\widetilde{A}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}\left(1-\frac{1}{r}\right)}\Theta^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right),

courtesy of the
L^{p}
and
L^{\infty}
estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.7
) respectively, in addition to
\Theta(r)\geq 1
and
\left\lVert\rho_{f_{i}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\geq 1
for
i=1,2
. Recalling the definition of
\overline{A}:=\widetilde{A}\times\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}^{1/p}
and combining (
2.13
) with (
2.14
) and
(D_{p}/\lambda)^{1-1/r}=e(D_{p}/\lambda)
yields the
L^{p}
-estimate (
2.5
).

To conclude, note that since
1<p<+\infty
with

in the regime (

), we have that
1<(p^{\prime}r^{\prime}(t))^{\prime}\leq p<+\infty
on

. For the torus case

, this implies
C_{\mathrm{HW};\,(p^{\prime}r^{\prime}(t))^{\prime}}\in L^{\infty}([0,T])

3.
Stability estimates in
p
-kinetic Wasserstein distances

3.1.
Insufficiency of Loeper’s approach in the Yudovich setting

The classical approach established by Loeper falls short when addressing macroscopic densities whose Lebesgue norms increase at most according to a prescribed growth function, which is the Yudovich setting.

One studies the evolution of the following quantity, inspired by Loeper, which controls the
2
-Wasserstein distance between two solutions:

Q(t)=\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{2}+\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{2}\>d\pi_{0}.

After taking the time derivative and applying Cauchy-Schwartz inequality, one is left with

\frac{1}{2}\dot{Q}\leq Q+\sqrt{Q}\sqrt{\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{1})-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(X_{2})\right\rvert^{2}\>d\pi_{0}}.

Now, the classical step involves separating the force fields into two parts;

\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;X_{1})-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(t;X_{2})\right\rvert\leq\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;X_{1})-\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;X_{2})\right\rvert+\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(t;X_{2})-\nabla U_{f_{2}}(t;X_{2})\right\rvert.

The first term exhibits the regularity of a force field evaluated along two possibly distinct characteristics, which depends on the regularity of the associated macroscopic density. The second term compares the two force fields along the same characteristic.

In the bounded setting, the
\log
-Lipschitz regularity
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
, combined with an adapted nondecreasing concave function and Jensen’s inequality, leads to the following after some manipulations:

(3.1)

\sqrt{\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{1})-\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{2})\right\rvert^{2}d\pi_{0}}\lesssim\sqrt{Q}\left\lvert\log Q\right\rvert.

Recalling Loeper’s
L^{2}
-estimate (
2.1
) for the second term of the splitting and combining the estimates gives

\dot{Q}\lesssim Q\left\lvert\log Q\right\rvert,

which is a closable differential inequality.

In the Yudovich setting, the force field has a modulus of continuity
\varphi_{\Theta}
depending on the growth function
\Theta
so that applying Jensen’s inequality leads this time to the following estimate:

(3.2)

\sqrt{\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{1})-\nabla U_{f_{1}}(X_{2})\right\rvert^{2}d\pi_{0}}\lesssim\sqrt{Q}\left\lvert\log Q\right\rvert\Theta\Big(\left\lvert\log Q\right\rvert\Big).

Compared to (
3.1
), (
3.2
) introduces a factor
\Theta(\left\lvert\log Q\right\rvert)
. If the modulus of continuity is too strong, as in the case of
\Theta(r):=r^{1/\alpha}
, corresponding to the exponential Orlicz space studied by Holding and Miot in
[
10
]
, it may prevent the closability of the differential inequality.

3.2.
Proof of the main result

We first state a refined version of the modulus of continuity of the force fields with Yudovich macroscopic densities due to Crippa, Inversi, Saffirio and Stefani (see Miot
[
16
, Proposition 2.1]
and Holding and Miot
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
in the exponential Orlicz setting, and Loeper
[
15
, Lemma 3.1]
in the bounded setting):

Lemma 3.1

(
[
4
, Lemma 1.1 & Proposition 3.1]
)
.

Let
\rho\in Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})
be a probability measure. Let

satisfy
\sigma\Delta U=\rho-1_{\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}}
in the distributional sense. Then
\nabla U
has modulus of continuity
\varphi_{\Theta}
. More precisely, there is a dimensional constant
C_{d}>0
depending on
\mathcal{X}
such that for all

\int_{\mathcal{X}}\left\lvert\nabla G(x-q)-\nabla G(y-q)\right\rvert\rho(q)\>dq\leq C_{d}\left(1+\left\lVert\rho\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right)\varphi_{\Theta}(\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert),

where
G
denotes the Green function on
\mathcal{X}
associated to the Laplace equation.

We also recall Osgood’s lemma in this context, which enables us to close the differential inequality involving
D_{p}
:

Lemma 3.2

(
[
1
, Lemma 3.4 & Corollary 3.5]
)
.

Let
\Phi(s):=\sqrt{s\varphi_{\Theta}(s)}
satisfies the Osgood condition, namely,
Assumption

1.7
holds. Let
G,H
be two measurable functions such that

G(t)\leq G(0)+\int_{0}^{t}H(s)\Phi(G(s))\>ds.

If
G(0)
is such that

\int_{0}^{T}H(s)\>ds\leq\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}\left(G(0)\right),

then on
[0,T]
it holds

G(t)\leq\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}^{-1}\left(\Psi_{p,\Theta;d}\left(G(0)\right)-\int_{0}^{T}H(s)\>ds\right).

Remark 3.3
.

The

allows for the redistribution of the excessive contribution of the force fields’ modulus of continuity, balancing the roles of the position and momentum variables during the evolution of the quantity

The choice

Proof of
Theorem

.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p}\dot{D}_{p}=

\displaystyle\>\frac{1}{p}\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\dot{\lambda}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\lambda\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(X_{1}-X_{2})\cdot(V_{1}-V_{2})\>d\pi_{0}

\displaystyle+\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\left(\nabla U_{f_{2}}-\nabla U_{f_{1}}\right)\>d\pi_{0}.

Using Hölder’s inequality with respect to the measure

We proceed to the usual splitting of the difference of electric fields;

(3.4)

\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert\nabla U_{f_{1}}-\nabla U_{f_{2}}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq T_{1}+T_{2},

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}f+v\cdot\nabla_{x}f+\left(E_{f}+v\wedge B\right)\cdot\nabla_{v}f=0,\\
E_{f}=-\nabla_{x}U_{f},\quad-\Delta_{x}U_{f}:=\rho_{f}-1_{\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{T}^{d}},\\
\rho_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f\>dv,\end{cases}

where the external varying magnetic field
B
is bounded in both time and space, and with
\log
-Lipschitz regularity in position. Similarly to (
VP
), we denote
B(X_{i}):=B(t;X_{i})
for
i=1,2
, and the system of characteristics is then given by

(CH-VPB)

\dot{X}=V,\quad\dot{V}=E_{f}+V\wedge B,\quad X(0;x,v)=x,\quad V(0;x,v)=v.

The presence of an external magnetic field induces anisotropy between the two solutions, manifesting as the following additional term when analyzing the evolution of the quantity
D_{p}
:

(4.1)

\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\left[V_{2}\wedge\left(B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right)\right]\>d\pi_{0},

where, by orthogonality, we have rewritten

\left(V_{1}-V_{2}\right)\cdot\left(V_{1}\wedge B(X_{1})-V_{2}\wedge B(X_{2})\right)=(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\left[V_{2}\wedge\left(B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right)\right].

This isolated flow
V_{2}
can be controlled in terms of the electric and the external magnetic fields:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
18
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
f
be a weak solution to (
VPB
). Then for all
t\in[0,T],v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, it holds

\left\lVert V(t;\cdot,v)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}\leq\left\lvert v\right\rvert e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}+\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert E(s)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}e^{(t-s)\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}\>ds.

Applying Hölder’s inequality with respect to the measure
\pi_{0}
to (
4.1
) and
Lemma

4.1
, we obtain

(4.2)

\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{1}-V_{2}\right\rvert^{p-2}(V_{1}-V_{2})\cdot\left[V_{2}\wedge\left(B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right)\right]\>d\pi_{0}\\
\leq D_{p}^{\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert V_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\leq D_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(T_{3}+T_{4}\right),

where

T_{3}(t):=2e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathcal{X})}}\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{p}\left\lvert B(t;X_{1}(t;x,v)-B(t;X_{2}(t;y,w))\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

and

T_{4}(t):=2\int_{0}^{t}\left\lVert E_{f_{2}}(s)\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})}e^{(t-s)\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathcal{X})}}\>ds\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

We start by estimating
T_{3}
; we apply Hölder’s inequality for some
r\geq 1
,

T_{3}(t)\leq e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{pr}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)\right)^{\frac{1}{pr}}\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{pr^{\prime}}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{pr^{\prime}}}.

Given that the external magnetic field has a
\log
-Lipschitz regularity, we can control its Hölder norm. Indeed, there is a constant
C_{B}>0
such that for all
0<\gamma<1
and
\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert<1/e
, it holds that

\left\lvert B(t;x)-B(t;y)\right\rvert\leq C_{B}\frac{e^{-1}}{1-\gamma}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{\gamma},\quad\sup_{\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert<1/e}\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert^{1-\gamma}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert x-y\right\rvert\right\rvert=\frac{e^{-1}}{1-\gamma}.

Then, as long as
1/r^{\prime}\geq\gamma
,

\displaystyle\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert B(X_{1})-B(X_{2})\right\rvert^{pr^{\prime}}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{pr^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{B}\frac{e^{-1}}{1-\gamma}\left(\int_{\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert<1/e}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{\gamma}{p}}

\displaystyle\quad+\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}e^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}\left(\int_{\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{1}\right\rvert\geq 1/e}\left\lvert X_{1}-X_{2}\right\rvert^{p}\>d\pi_{0}\right)^{\frac{1}{pr^{\prime}}}.

We choose
r=\left\lvert\log(D_{p}/\lambda)\right\rvert
so that
\gamma:=1/r^{\prime}=1-1/\left\lvert\log(D_{p}/\lambda)\right\rvert
, and hence

\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\gamma}=\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}}=\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{\log(D_{p}/\lambda)}}\leq e\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right),\quad\frac{1}{1-\gamma}=\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert.

Note that

\left(\int_{(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{pr}\>d\pi_{0}(x,v,y,w)\right)^{\frac{1}{pr}}=\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{pr}\>df_{2}(0;y,w)\right)^{\frac{1}{pr}}\leq\overline{\Theta}(pr)\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\overline{\Theta}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};df_{2})},

for some growth function
\overline{\Theta}
, which naturally leads us to impose a bound on the moments of one of the two solutions.

Assumption 4.2
.

The moment of one of the two Lagrangian weak solutions belongs initially to a weighted Yudovich space;

\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\overline{\Theta}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\,df_{2}(0))}:=\sup_{1\leq r<+\infty}\frac{\left(\int_{\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\lvert w\right\rvert^{r}f_{2}(0;y,w)\>dydw\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}}{\overline{\Theta}(r)}<+\infty.

By controlling the extra magnetized terms as above, the second author
[
17
, Theorem 2.5]
obtained a stability criterion for (
VPB
). The main contribution was showing that the stability of solutions holds under an extra assumption, of type
Assumption

4.2
, on the growth of the velocity moments of one of the initial distributions, and crucially this assumption is verified by Maxwellians. Combining these ideas with the analysis from
Section

3.2
, we proceed to find a stability estimate for (
VPB
). We set
\widetilde{\Theta}(\cdot)\coloneqq\overline{\Theta}(p\times\cdot)
and assume that the growth function dominates that of an exponential Orlicz space; specifically,
\widetilde{\Theta}(l)\geq l^{1/\alpha}
for some
1\leq\alpha<+\infty
, which is a natural growth function ensuring that the magnetized terms are not controlled by the electric ones in the bounded setting (see
[
18
, Remark 2.4]
). In the Yudovich setting, to prevent this from happening, we may without loss of generality assume that
\widetilde{\Theta}\geq\Theta
. Now, we set
\lambda:=\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert\widetilde{\Theta}(\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert)}^{p}
, and thanks to both (
1.1
) under
Assumption

1.7
and
Assumption

1.8
for the growth function
\widetilde{\Theta}
in the new regime
D_{p}(t)\leq\min\{c_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d},c_{p,\Theta,d}\}
, we can estimate
T_{3}
as follows:

\displaystyle T_{3}(t)

\displaystyle\leq e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};df_{2})}\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}\widetilde{\Theta}\left(\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(e^{-\frac{1}{p^{\prime}}}C_{B}\left\lvert\log\left(\frac{D_{p}}{\lambda}\right)\right\rvert+e\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}\right)

(4.3)

\displaystyle\leq C_{B,\widetilde{\Theta},p;d}e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};df_{2})}\times D_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert\widetilde{\Theta}\left(\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert\right)}

with
C_{B,\widetilde{\Theta},p;d}:=\overline{C}_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d}(e^{-1/p^{\prime}}C_{B}C_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d}+e\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})})
.

We proceed in a similar fashion to estimate
T_{4}
, but without having to rely on
Assumption

4.2
, and we obtain

(4.4)

T_{4}(t)\leq C_{B,\widetilde{\Theta},p;d}\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(s)\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right)e^{(t-s)\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathcal{X})}}\>ds\times D_{p}^{\frac{1}{p}}\sqrt{\left\lvert\log D_{p}\right\rvert}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\widetilde{\Theta}\geq\Theta
. Under the
Assumptions

1.4
,
1.5
and
1.6
for the growth function
\Theta
,
Assumptions

1.8
and
1.7
for the growth function
\widetilde{\Theta}\geq\Theta
, and
Assumption

4.2
on the moments of one of the two solutions, we thus obtain, combining (
4.2
), (
4.3
) and (
4.4
) to the analysis of
Section

3.2
, the following
W_{p}
stability estimate for (
VPB
):

(4.5)

W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(t),f_{2}(t)\right)\leq\Psi_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d}^{-1}\left(\Psi_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d}\left(\Phi^{-1}_{p,\widetilde{\Theta}}\left[W_{p}^{p}\left(f_{1}(0),f_{2}(0)\right)\right]\right)-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{J}(s)\>ds\right)

with

\widetilde{J}(t):=p\times\left[1+\widetilde{C}_{p,\widetilde{\Theta};d}A(t)\times\left(1_{p=1}+1_{1<p<+\infty}e^{1/p}\max_{s\in[0,T]}C_{U}(s)\right)\right.\\
\left.+\,C_{B,\widetilde{\Theta},p;d}\left(e^{t\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{X})}}\left\lVert\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right\rVert_{Y^{\widetilde{\Theta}}(\mathcal{X}\times\mathbb{R}^{d};\,df_{2}(0))}+\int_{0}^{t}\left(1+\left\lVert\rho_{f_{2}}(s)\right\rVert_{Y^{\Theta}(\mathcal{X})}\right)e^{(t-s)\left\lVert B\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}([0,T)\times\mathcal{X})}}\>ds\right)\right].

While the stability estimate (
4.5
) provides a uniqueness criterion for (
VPB
) as a corollary, it remains necessary to construct weak solutions for which the Lebesgue norms of the macroscopic density grow as fast as
\Theta
. Crippa, Inversi, Saffirio, and Stefani
[
4
, Theorem 1.7 & Proposition 1.8]
constructed such Lagrangian weak solutions for (
VP
) with Yudovich macroscopic density building upon the construction of Miot
[
16
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.3]
, which was generalized to (
VPB
) by the second author
[
17
, Theorem 2.1]
, and, incidentally,
the same construction can be directly adapted to (
VPB
), yielding a solution to (
VPB
) for
\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{3}
starting from the initial datum

f_{0}(x,v)=\frac{1_{(-\infty,0]}\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert^{2}-\theta(x)^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)}{\tfrac{4}{3}\pi\left\lVert\theta\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}},

where the nonnegative function
\theta\in Y^{\Theta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
is such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\max\left\{1,\left\lvert x\right\rvert^{p}\right\}\theta(x)\>dx<+\infty.

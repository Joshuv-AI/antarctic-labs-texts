On quasi-Albanese morphisms for log canonical Calabi-Yau pairs
Yiming Zhu

Abstract
. We study the quasi-Albanese morphisms for log canonical Calabi-Yau pairs.

1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a complex projective variety with canonical singularities and
K_{X}
torsion. Kawamata proved that its Albanese morphism
\alpha:X\to A
is an étale fiber bundle
[
Kaw85
, Theorem 8.3]
. Inspired by the works
[
Iit76
,
Kaw81
,
Zha05
,
Fuj24
,
BFPT24
]
, we study quasi-Albanese morphisms for (the open part of) log canonical Calabi-Yau pairs.

Before stating the main theorem, we recall the definition of logarithmic Kodaira dimension and a theorem by Kawamata and Fujino. Let
X^{0}
be a smooth quasi-projective variety. Let
X
be a compactification of
X^{0}
such that
D_{X}:=X\setminus X^{0}
is a simple normal crossing divisor. The logarithmic Kodaira dimension
\overline{\kappa}(X^{0})
of
X^{0}
is defined as
\kappa(K_{X}+D_{X})
. By Iitaka
[
Iit77
]
,
\overline{\kappa}(X^{0})
is independent of the choice of such
X
.

Theorem 1
.

[
Kaw81
, Thoerem 28]
, see also
[
Fuj24
, Theorem 1.2]

If
\overline{\kappa}(X^{0})=0
, then the quasi-Albanese morphism of
X^{0}
is dominant and has irreducible general fibers.

The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
be a log smooth pair with
D_{X}
reduced and
K_{X}+D_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. Let
X^{0}=X\setminus D_{X}
and let
\alpha:X^{0}\to G
be its quasi-Albanese morphism. Then

(1)

\alpha
is surjective in codimension one: for any prime divisor
P\subset G
, there is a prime divisor
Q\subset X^{0}
dominating
P
,

(2)

\alpha
is flat in codimension one: for any prime divisor
Q\subset X^{0}
, if
Q
do not dominate
G
, then
\alpha(Q)
is of codimension one,

(3)

\alpha
is semistable in codimension one: for any prime divisor
P\subset G
, the pair
(X,\alpha^{*}P)
is log canonical near the generic point of
P
.

Theorem
2
is a consequence of the following theorem, whose proof depends on Theorem
1
and the techniques from the minimal model program.

2.
Preparations

We work over the field of complex numbers. For the singularities of pairs and the minimal model program, we refer to the book
[
KM98
]
.

A projective surjective morphism between normal varieties with connected fibers is called a fibration. Let
f:X\to Y
be a fibration between normal varieties. Let
D
be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on
X
. We can write
D=D^{h}+D^{v}
, where
D^{v}
does not dominate
Y
, and each component of
D^{h}
dominates
Y
.
D^{v}
(resp.
D^{h}
) is called the vertical part (resp. horizontal) part of
D
.
D
is called vertical (resp. horizontal) if
D=D^{v}
(resp.
D=D^{h}
). Assume that
D
is vertical, then
D
is called exceptional over
Y
if
\operatorname{codim}f(D)\geq 2
, and very exceptional over
Y
if for any prime divisor
P\subset Y
, there is a prime divisor
Q\subset X
such that
Q
dominates
P
but
Q\not\subset D
.

For a birational map
\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y
between normal varieties, we set
\operatorname{Ex}(\pi):=\sum P
, where the sum is taken over all prime divisors on
X
which are exceptional over
Y
. A birational map
\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y
is called a birational contraction if
\operatorname{Ex}(\pi^{-1})=0
.

We will use the following fact implicitly: Let
A
,
B
be two effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisors on a projective variety
X
. If
\operatorname{Supp}A=\operatorname{Supp}B
, then
\kappa(A)=\kappa(B)
. If
\operatorname{Supp}A\subset\operatorname{Supp}B
, then
\kappa(A)\leq\kappa(B)
. Let
f:Y\to X
be a projective surjective morphism from another projective variety, then
f^{-1}\operatorname{Supp}A=\operatorname{Supp}f^{*}A
.

For a projective variety
X
, we set
q(X):=\dim H^{1}(O_{X})
, and for a projective lc pair
(X,D_{X})
, we set
q(X,D_{X}):=\dim H^{0}(\Omega_{X^{0}}^{1}(\log\lfloor D_{X}|_{X^{0}}\rfloor))
, where
X^{0}
is the log smooth locus of
(X,D_{X})
.

Differential forms on lc pairs

Theorem 4

(
[
GKKP11
]
)
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
be an lc pair. Let
X^{0}
be its log smooth locus and let
\mathrm{nonklt}(X,D_{X})
be its non-klt locus. Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,D_{X})
and let
D_{Y}
be the codimension one part of
\pi^{-1}\mathrm{nonklt}(X,D_{X})
. Then
\pi_{*}\Omega_{Y}^{k}(\log D_{Y})
is reflexive and
H^{0}(Y,\Omega_{Y}^{k}(\log D_{Y}))\cong H^{0}(X^{0},\Omega_{X^{0}}^{k}(\log\lfloor D_{X}|_{X^{0}}\rfloor))
for all
k
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pair with
D_{X}
reduced. Let
\pi:Y\to X
be a log resolution of
(X,D_{X})
and let
D_{Y}=\pi^{-1}_{*}D_{X}+\operatorname{Ex}(\pi)
. Then

(1)

q(X)=q(Y)
,

(2)

q(X,D_{X})=q(Y,D_{Y})
,

(3)

\kappa(K_{X}+D)=\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y})
and
\nu(K_{X}+D)=\nu(K_{Y}+D_{Y})
,

where
\kappa
and
\nu
are Kodaira dimension and numerical Kodaira dimension respectively.

Proof.

Since klt varieties have rational singularities, we have (1). Since
(X,D_{X})
is lc, there is an effective exceptional divisor
E
on
Y
such that
K_{Y}+D_{Y}=\pi^{*}(K_{X}+D_{X})+E
, so we have (3).

Now we prove (2). Note that
D_{Y}\geq\pi^{-1}D_{X}
and
D_{X}=\mathrm{nonklt}(X,D_{X})
. By Theorem
4
, to show (2), it suffices to show that the injective homomorphism

\iota:H^{0}(\Omega_{Y}^{1}(\log\pi^{-1}D_{X}))\to H^{0}(\Omega_{Y}^{1}(\log D_{Y}))

is an isomorphism. Write
\pi^{-1}D_{X}=\sum_{i}P_{i}
and
D_{Y}-\pi^{-1}D_{X}=\sum_{j}E_{j}
as sum of prime divisors. By the exact sequence

0\to\Omega_{Y}^{1}\to\Omega_{Y}^{1}(\log(\sum_{i}P_{i}+\sum_{j}E_{j}))\to\oplus_{i}O_{P_{j}}\oplus\oplus_{j}O_{E_{j}}\to 0,

one checks that
\iota
is an isomorphism iff
\sum_{i}a_{i}c_{1}(O_{Y}(P_{i}))+\sum_{j}b_{j}c_{1}(O_{Y}(E_{j}))=0
implies that all
b_{j}
’s are zero. Which is indeed the case since all
E_{j}
’s are exceptional and
X
is
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial.∎

Lemma 6
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
and
(Y,D_{Y})
be two projective
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pairs with reduced
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
. Assume that there is a birational contraction
\pi:X\dashrightarrow Y
such that
D_{X}=\pi^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\operatorname{Ex}(\pi)
then
q(X,D_{X})=q(Y,D_{Y})
.

Proof.

Let
X\xleftarrow{p}W\xrightarrow{q}Y
be a common log resolution. Then
q^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\operatorname{Ex}(q)=p^{-1}_{*}D_{X}+\operatorname{Ex}(p)
. Hence, the Lemma follows from the previous proposition.
∎

2.2.
Quasi-Albanese morphism

Theorem 7

(Quasi-Albanese morphism
[
Iit76
]
, see also
[
Fuj24
]
)
.

Let
X
be a smooth quasi-projective variety. Then there is a morphism
\alpha:X\to G
to a quasi-abelian variety such that

(1) for any morphism
\alpha^{\prime}:X\to G^{\prime}
to a quasi-abelian variety, there is a morphism
h:G\to G^{\prime}
such that
\alpha^{\prime}=h\circ\alpha
, and

(2)
\alpha
is uniquely determined.

Construction.

We recall the constructions of quasi-Albanese morphisms in
[
Iit76
,
Fuj24
]
. Let
(X,D_{X}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}D_{i})
be a projective log smooth pair with
D_{X}
reduced. Where
D_{i}
’s are prime divisors. Let
X^{0}:=X\setminus D_{X}
and let
\iota:X^{0}\to X
be the open embedding. Let
p:X\to A
be the Albanese morphism. Set
q=q(X)
,
\overline{q}=q(X,D_{X})
, and
d=\overline{q}-q

Let
\eta_{i}

\eta_{i}

Let
\omega_{i}

,

, be such that these

Set

The quasi-Albanese variety of

We have the following Chevalley decomposition of

Fix a point

is then given by

X^{0}\to G,x\mapsto\left(\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\omega_{1},\cdots,\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\omega_{q},\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\phi_{1},\cdots,\int_{x_{0}}^{x}\phi_{d}\right).

Keep the notations introduced in the previous construction.

\overline{P}
be the closure of
P
in
\mathbb{P}_{A}
. Since any lc center of
(\mathbb{P}_{A},H)
is horizontal over
A
,
\overline{P}
contains no lc centers of
(\mathbb{P}_{A},H)
. Then
\overline{\kappa}(G\setminus P)=\kappa(K_{\mathbb{P}_{A}}+H+\overline{P})=\kappa(\overline{P})=\kappa(Q)>0

.

Case 2.
P
is horizontal over

. Then there is a subgroup

such that

\overline{\kappa}(G\setminus P)\geq\overline{\kappa}(F)+\overline{\kappa}(G^{\prime})=\overline{\kappa}(F)>0

Lemma 17
.

Let
(Y,D_{Y})
be a projective log smooth pair with
D_{Y}
reduced. Assume that
\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y})\geq 0
. Let
\Sigma\subset Y
be a reduced divisor which contains no lc centers of
(Y,D_{Y})
, then
\overline{\kappa}(Y\setminus D_{Y}+\Sigma)=\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y}+\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Let
\mu:Z\to Y
be a log resolution of
(Y,D_{Y}+\Sigma)
. We may assume that
\mu
is an isomorphism over
Y\setminus D_{Y}+\Sigma
. Let
\epsilon>0
be a sufficiently small rational number. Then

\displaystyle\overline{\kappa}(Y\setminus D_{Y}+\Sigma)

\displaystyle=\overline{\kappa}(Z\setminus\mu^{-1}(D_{Y}+\Sigma))

\displaystyle=\kappa(K_{Z}+\mu^{-1}(D_{Y}+\Sigma))

\displaystyle=\kappa(K_{Z}+\mu^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\mu^{-1}_{*}\Sigma+\operatorname{Ex}(\mu))

\displaystyle=\kappa(K_{Z}+\mu^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\epsilon\mu^{-1}_{*}\Sigma+\operatorname{Ex}(\mu))\text{ (by $\kappa(K_{Z}+\mu^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\operatorname{Ex}(\mu))\geq 0$)}

\displaystyle=\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y}+\epsilon\Sigma)\text{ (by $(Y,D_{Y}+\epsilon\Sigma)$ is lc)}.

\displaystyle=\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y}+\Sigma)\text{ (by $\kappa(K_{Y}+D_{Y})\geq 0$)}.

∎

Lemma 18
.

Let
\alpha:Z\to V
and
\beta:W\to V
be fibrations between projective normal varieties, and let

be a birational contraction over

. Let

be an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

, and let

\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor on

Let

be a birational morphism resolving the indeterminacies of

\kappa(\alpha^{*}L+E)=\kappa(u^{*}\alpha^{*}L+u^{*}E)=\kappa(v^{*}\beta^{*}L+u^{*}E)=\kappa(\beta^{*}L)=\kappa(L).

∎

4.
A remark on orbifold fundamental groups

This section is inspired by the work
[
GLM23
]
. Let
(X,D_{X}=\sum_{i}(1-\frac{1}{m_{i}})D_{i})
be an lc pair with standard coefficients, where each
D_{i}
is a prime divisor and
m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}\cup\infty
. For each
i
, let
\gamma_{i}
be a small loop around
D_{i}
. The orbifold fundamental group
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X,D_{X})
is defined as the quotient of
\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X})
by the normal subgroup generated by
\gamma_{i}^{m_{i}}
’s. Note that if
D_{X}
is reduced, then
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X,D_{X})=\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X})
.

Conjecture 20
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
be an lc pair with standard coefficients. If
K_{X}+D_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
, then
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X,D)
is virtually nilpotent.

Using the index-one cover and the Galois correspondence for orbifold fundamental groups (cf.
[
GLM23
, Proposition A.11]
), Conjecture
20
is reduced to the case
D_{X}
is reduced. By the following Lemma, Conjecture
20
is further reduced to the
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt case.

Lemma 21
.

Let
(X,D_{X})
and
(Y,D_{Y})
be two projective lc pairs with
D_{X}
and
D_{Y}
reduced. If there is a birational contraction
p:X\dashrightarrow Y
such that
D_{X}=p^{-1}_{*}D_{Y}+\operatorname{Ex}(p)
, then there is an isomorphism
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X,D_{X})\cong\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(Y,D_{Y})
.

Proof.

Let
Z\subset X
and
Z^{\prime}\subset Y
be closed subsets such that
X\setminus Z\cong Y\setminus Z^{\prime}
. Hence
X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus Z\cong Y^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus Z^{\prime}
and
X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X}\cup Z\cong Y^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{Y}\cup Z^{\prime}
. Since
p
is a birational contraction, we have
\operatorname{codim}Z^{\prime}\geq 2
and
D_{X}\cup Z=D_{X}\cup Z^{\prime\prime}
for some
\operatorname{codim}\geq 2
closed subset
Z^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X})\cong\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X}\cup Z^{\prime\prime})\cong\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X}\cup Z)\cong\pi_{1}(Y^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{Y}\cup Z^{\prime})\cong\pi_{1}(Y^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{Y})
.
∎

Let
(X,D_{X})
be a
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pair with
D_{X}
reduced and
K_{X}+D_{X}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
.

By a theorem due to Arapura and Nori
[
AN99
, Corollary 4.10]
, to show that
\pi_{1}^{\text{orb}}(X,D_{X})=\pi_{1}(X^{\operatorname{reg}}\setminus D_{X})
is virtually nilpotent, it suffices to show that it is polycyclic. Recall that a group
G
is called polycyclic if there is a series of subgroups
1=G_{n}\triangleleft G_{n-1}\triangleleft\cdots\triangleleft G_{0}=G
such that each
G_{i}/G_{i+1}
is cyclic. The advantage of the class of polycyclic groups is that they are closed under passing to subgroups, quotients, and extensions (cf.
[
KM79
, 4.4.3. Exercise]
), which is crucial to inductive arguments.

Assume that
q(X,D_{X})>0
, and let
g:(W,D_{W})\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
be the fibration given by Theorem
3
. By Lemma
21
,
(X,D_{X})
and
(W,D_{W})
have the same orbifold fundamental group. Let
W^{0}=g^{-1}G
and let
F
be a general fiber of
g
. Then
(F,{D_{W}}|_{F})
is lc and
K_{F}+D_{W}|_{F}\sim_{\mathbb{Q}}0
. By the following exact sequence (cf.
[
GLM23
, Lemma 2.18]
)

\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(F,D_{W}|_{F})\to\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(W^{0},{D_{W}}|_{W^{0}})\to\pi_{1}(G)\to 1,

we have
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(X,D_{X})\cong\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(W,D_{W})\cong\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(W^{0},{D_{W}}|_{W^{0}})
is polycyclic if
\pi_{1}^{\operatorname{orb}}(F,D_{W}|_{F})
is polycyclic. Hence, we have shown the following by induction on dimension.

Proposition 22
.

To prove Conjecture
20
, it suffices to prove
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial dlt pairs
(X,D_{X})
with
D_{X}
reduced and
q(X,D_{X})=0
have polycyclic orbifold fundamental groups.

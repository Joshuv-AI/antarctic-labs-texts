Forbidden non-unique β − \beta^{-} transitions and g A g_{A} -sensitive β \beta spectral shapes
Archana Saxena, P. C. Srivastava

Abstract
In the present work, we have done a systematic study of beta decay properties such as electron spectral-shapes, shape factors, and log f ​ t ft values for the higher forbidden non-unique β − \beta^{-} transitions in the mass region A=85-123. We have performed the nuclear shell model (SM) calculations to explore the sensitivity of the electron spectral-shapes for different axial coupling constants g A = 0.8 − 1.27 g_{A}=0.8-1.27 . The effective interactions GWBXG, G-matrix, SNET and SN100PN are used for different model spaces. In the present work, we have computed the electron spectral-shapes of 93 Zr, 135 Cs, 85 Br, 85 Br, 97 Zr, 101 Mo, 115 Cd, 117 Cd, 119 In and 123 Sn by constraining the small relativistic nuclear matrix element from conserved vector-current hypothesis (CVC). In the present study, we have found that the electron spectral-shapes are strongly dependent on g A g_{A} except second forbidden non-unique β − \beta^{-} transition 93 Zr.

II.1
Formalism

In the present study, we have computed the beta spectrum for forbidden non-unique
\beta^{-}
decay transitions, analyzing the energy distribution of beta electrons emitted in
\beta^{-}
decays. Based on the impulse approximation in nuclear
\beta
-decay, the generalized theoretical formalism is can be found in Refs
mika2017
;
mst2006
;
behrens1982
;
hfs1966
.
The partial half-life of
\beta
-decay is given as
mika2017
;
behrens1982

t_{1/2}=\frac{\kappa}{\tilde{C}}.

(1)

Here,
\tilde{C}
represents the dimensionless integrated shape function, while
\kappa
, which includes natural constants, has a value of 6289 s
Patrignani
. Furthermore,
\tilde{C}
can be calculated as follows

\displaystyle\tilde{C}=\int_{1}^{w_{0}}C(w_{e})pw_{e}(w_{0}-w_{e})^{2}F_{0}(Z,w_{e})dw_{e}.

(2)

In Eq.
2
, we use the usual dimensionless kinematics quantities which are divided by the electron rest mass as
w_{0}=W_{0}/m_{e}c^{2}
,
w_{e}=W_{e}/m_{e}c^{2}
, and
p=p_{e}c/m_{e}c^{2}=\sqrt{(w_{e}^{2}-1)}
.
The
w_{e}
and
p_{e}
are the total energy and momentum of emitted electrons and the
w_{0}
is the endpoint energy, respectively. The
F_{0}(Z,w_{e})
is the Fermi function where
Z
is the atomic number of the daughter nucleus.

The shape factor
C(w_{e})
in Eq.
2
is given as

\displaystyle C(w_{e})=\sum_{k_{e},k_{\nu},K}\lambda_{k_{e}}\Big[M_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})^{2}+m_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})^{2}

\displaystyle-\frac{2\gamma_{k_{e}}}{k_{e}w_{e}}M_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})m_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})\Big].

(3)

Here,
K
is the order of forbiddenness of the
\beta
decay,
k_{e}
and
k_{\nu}
(taking values 1, 2, 3,…) are positive integers related to the partial-wave expansion of the leptonic wave functions.
The expressions for
M_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})
and
m_{K}(k_{e},k_{\nu})
involve different nuclear matrix elements (NMEs) and kinematic factors
mika2017
;
behrens1982
.
Here,
\gamma_{k_{e}}=\sqrt{k_{e}^{2}-(\alpha Z)^{2}}
and
y=(\alpha Zw_{e}/p_{e}c)
are auxiliary quantities, where
\alpha=1/137
is the fine-structure constant. The term
\lambda_{k_{e}}={F_{k_{e}-1}(Z,w_{e})}/{F_{0}(Z,w_{e})}
represents the Coulomb function, where
F_{k_{e}-1}(Z,w_{e})
is the generalized Fermi function
mika2017
;
mst2006
;
Anil2020PRC
, which is given as below

\displaystyle F_{k_{e}-1}(Z,w_{e})

\displaystyle=4^{k_{e}-1}(2k_{e})(k_{e}+\gamma_{k_{e}})[(2k_{e}-1)!!]^{2}e^{\pi{y}}

(4)

\displaystyle\times\left(\frac{2p_{e}R}{\hbar}\right)^{2(\gamma_{k_{e}}-k_{e})}\left(\frac{|\Gamma(\gamma_{k_{e}}+iy)|}{\Gamma(1+2\gamma_{k_{e}})}\right)^{2}.

The NMEs, which contain nuclear structure information for a transition between an initial (i) and final (f) state, can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}{}^{\rm V/A}\mathcal{M}_{KLS}^{(N)}(pn)(k_{e},m,n,\rho)&\\
=\frac{\sqrt{4\pi}}{\widehat{J}_{i}}\sum_{pn}\,^{\rm V/A}m_{KLS}^{(N)}(pn)(&k_{e},m,n,\rho)(\Psi_{f}||[c_{p}^{\dagger}\tilde{c}_{n}]_{K}||\Psi_{i}).\end{split}

(5)

The term
(\Psi_{f}||[c_{p}^{\dagger}\tilde{c}_{n}]K||\Psi_{i})
represent the one-body transition densities (OBTDs), which are model-dependent. In contrast, the functions
{{}^{V/A}m{KLS}^{(N)}}(pn)(k_{e},m,n,\rho)
represent the single-particle matrix elements (SPMEs), which are independent of nuclear models for transitions between the initial
(\Psi_{i})
and final
(\Psi_{f})
nuclear states. In this work, the SPMEs are calculated using harmonic oscillator wave functions
mika2017
;
mst2006
. In Eq.
5
, the summation is taken over the single-particle states of protons (p) and neutrons (n).
In the present calculations, next-to-leading-order (NLO) corrections are also included in the shape factor, leading to an increased number of NMEs (see more about the NLO corrections in Refs.
mika2016
;
mika2017
).

The shape factor
C(w_{e})
in Eq. (
2
) can be broken down into vector, axial-vector, and mixed vector-axial-vector components as a function of electron energy
mika2017
;
mika2016
;
Joel_2017
;
Joel_2017_2
;
Suhonen1

\displaystyle C(w_{e})=g_{V}^{2}C_{V}(w_{e})+g_{A}^{2}C_{A}(w_{e})+g_{V}g_{A}C_{VA}(w_{e}).

(6)

By integrating Eq. (
6
) over the electron kinetic energy, we get an expression for the integrated shape function
\tilde{C}
, which corresponds to Eq. (
2
)

\displaystyle\tilde{C}=g_{V}^{2}\tilde{C}_{V}+g_{A}^{2}\tilde{C}_{A}+g_{V}g_{A}\tilde{C}_{VA}.

(7)

In Eq. (
6
) the shape factors
C_{i}
are functions of the electron kinetic energy, while the integrated shape factors
\tilde{C_{i}}
in Eq. (
7
) do not depend on the electron energy or simply we can say that the
\tilde{C_{i}}
are just constant numbers.

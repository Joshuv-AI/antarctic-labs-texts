Research progress and frontier trends in liver cancer immunotherapy in the post-COVID-19 era (2020–2024): a visualization analysis based on bibliometric methods
Authors: Shicai Liang, Xusheng Zhang, Xuebo Wang, Yannan Xie, Jialong Wang, Jiawei Wang, Bendong Chen
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe COVID-19 pandemic has transformed liver cancer research, making immunotherapy a key breakthrough for advanced cases. This study uses bibliometrics to reveal research hotspots and paradigm shifts of this field from 2020 to 2024.MethodsThe data were retrieved from the Web of Science Core Collection (2020 to 2024). Data quality was ensured through two rounds of independent data cleaning, achieving a Kappa coefficient of 0.89. A comprehensive bibliometric analysis was conducted on the literature related to liver cancer immunotherapy, utilizing tools such as Biblioshiny, VOSviewer, Scimago Graphica, CiteSpace, and Microsoft Office Excel (2022 version).ResultsFrom 2020 to 2024, China and the US led global liver cancer immunotherapy research, forming a collaboration network with Canada. Sun Yat-sen University is a key hub with over 600 publications and an annual growth rate of 18.5%, closely collaborating with Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Zhejiang University (correlation index 0.92). The journal impact is dominated by Frontiers in Immunology and Frontiers in Oncology, which form a dual-core citation network, with the top 1% journals contributing 35.7% of publications. Frontiers in Immunology has the highest h-index (35) and fastest growth rate. The co-citation network includes three major clusters: immune mechanisms (led by Nature), clinical trials (Clinical Cancer Research), and liver cancer pathology (Hepatology). Research themes evolve in four directions: prognostic models, tumor microenvironment, hepatocellular carcinoma mechanisms, and cancer immunotherapy. Hotspots include “cancer”, “immunotherapy”, and “hepatocellular carcinoma”, with rising trends in “tumor microenvironment” and “combination therapy” after 2021.ConclusionThe COVID-19 pandemic has spurred multidisciplinary integration and the use of real-world evidence in research. Emerging themes like Sino-American collaboration, knowledge diffusion in core journals, and tumor microenvironment are shaping liver cancer immunotherapy research in the post-pandemic era. This study offers strategic insights for optimizing resources and advancing clinical-basic translational research.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04144-0.Keywords: Liver cancer, Immunotherapy, COVID-19, Bibliometrics

Introduction

Liver cancer is a highly lethal tumor of the digestive system, characterized primarily by the malignant proliferation of hepatocytes. Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 75%–85% of primary liver cancer cases [1]. Its occurrence and development involve the abnormal activation of key signaling pathways such as Wnt/β-catenin (mutation rate 30%–40%) and PI3K/AKT/mTOR (activation rate 25%) [2]. According to molecular typing, HCC can be classified into proliferative type (high TP53 mutation), immune-activated type (high expression of PD-L1), and lipid metabolism reprogramming type, among which patients with the immune-activated type have a 2.7-fold higher response rate to PD-1 inhibitors [3, 4]. Epidemiological data show that in 2020, there were 906,000 new cases globally, with China contributing 45.3% of the cases. There are significant regional differences in distribution: East Asia is primarily driven by HBV infection (54.3% in China), whereas in Europe and the United States, HCV (38.7%) and alcoholic liver disease (22.1%) are the main drivers [5]. Notably, the incidence of HCC related to non-alcoholic fatty liver disease (NAFLD) is increasing at a rate of 8.4% per year, with obesity (BMI ≥ 30) and diabetes (HbA1c ≥ 7%) increasing the risk by 2.6 times and 1.8 times, respectively [6].

In clinical diagnosis and treatment, the Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) staging system remains the core stratification tool; however, liquid biopsy technology is gradually rewriting the paradigm of early diagnosis. For example, methylation detection of circulating tumor DNA (ctDNA) can increase the early detection rate of HCC to 74.5% (AUC = 0.89) [7]. For different stages, surgical resection is the first choice for very early-stage patients, with a 5-year survival rate of 70%–80%, but the 5-year recurrence rate remains as high as 60%–70% (Lancet Gastroenterol Hepatol, 2022). This high 5-year recurrence rate has driven research into adjuvant immunotherapy; for instance, the IMbrave050 study confirmed that adjuvant treatment with atezolizumab plus bevacizumab reduced the risk of recurrence by 28% [8]. Mid-stage treatment is primarily based on transarterial chemoembolization (TACE), and its combination with lenvatinib can increase the objective response rate to 54.1% [9]. In advanced systemic treatment, the strategy has shifted from single-target drugs to combination therapies, with the atezolizumab plus bevacizumab regimen achieving a median survival of 19.2 months [10, 11], while preliminary data on new bispecific antibodies (such as PD-L1/TGF-β inhibitors) show a median progression-free survival (PFS) exceeding 9.3 months [12].

Immunotherapy has reshaped the treatment landscape for advanced HCC, and its application is now expanding to earlier stages. In first-line treatment, the PD-L1 inhibitor atezolizumab combined with the anti-angiogenic drug bevacizumab has increased the objective response rate (ORR) to 27.3%; subgroup analysis in China shows that the median overall survival (OS) has been extended to 24.0 months [13]. In second-line treatment, the PD-1 inhibitor nivolumab has achieved a median survival of 15.6 months [14]. Furthermore, innovative combination treatment strategies have further driven efficacy breakthroughs: the ORR of PD-1 inhibitors combined with multi-target tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), such as camrelizumab and apatinib, has reached 34.3% [15]; meanwhile, the sequential application of immunotherapy and local ablation has increased the resection rate of unresectable HCC to 31.7% [16]. However, despite these advances, immunotherapy still faces multiple challenges—such as resistance mechanisms, patient selection, and adverse effects—that need to be addressed to further improve outcomes.

Bibliometrics is an interdisciplinary field that applies mathematical and statistical methods to systematically analyze academic literature [17]. By utilizing co-occurrence networks, citation tracking, and emerging trend detection, bibliometrics quantitatively reveals the evolution of knowledge structures and collaboration patterns within disciplines; this facilitates the identification of popular themes and emerging trends across research areas [18]. Consequently, many scholars, both domestically and internationally, have conducted bibliometric analyses on various diseases, employing tools such as CiteSpace and VOSviewer for analysis and visualization [19]. In this article, bibliometrics offers an indispensable systematic analysis framework to address the complexity, dynamic development, and global challenges of liver cancer immunotherapy research in the post-pandemic era through comprehensive sampling and multi-indicator analysis.

Materials and methods

This study is based on the Web of Science Core Collection database (WoSCC) [20] and employs a systematic search strategy to retrieve publications related to the theme through title search terms: TS=((“liver cancer” OR"hepatocellular carcinoma” OR “HCC”) AND (“immunotherapy”)) and PY=(2020–2024) and DT=(“article” or “review”). Original articles and literature reviews were included; letters and editorial materials were excluded; a language restriction to English was applied. After checking and removing duplicate entries, we found a total of 6,291 articles related to our topic.Two researchers confirmed that these publications matched the study’s focus. The research team conducted a multidimensional analysis using CiteSpace 6.2.R4 and VOSviewer 1.6.19 tools [21], combined with statistical validation in Excel and R language. In VOSviewer, the full counting method was applied. To identify the most prominent entities, we set stringent thresholds: for co-authorship analysis, only organizations with a minimum of 20 documents and authors with a minimum of 10 documents were included. For keyword co-occurrence analysis, a keyword was required to appear at least 100 times to be considered. In CiteSpace, the time slicing was set from 2020 to 2024 with 1 year per slice, and the selection criteria were set to g-index (k = 25), a standard threshold that effectively balances the comprehensiveness and clarity of the network. The pruning algorithms employed were Pathfinder and Pruning the merged network. All other parameters were kept at the software defaults.The analysis included: (1) Collaboration network analysis, covering co-occurrence frequency, node centrality, and modular clustering of authors, institutions, and countries, to identify core researchers, regional hubs, and transnational collaboration patterns [22]; (2) Keyword co-occurrence and theme evolution, tracking research hotspots before and after the pandemic (such as tumor microenvironment remodeling and biomarker discovery) and emerging directions (such as telemedicine and vaccine hesitancy) through clustering analysis, timeline views, and emergence detection [23]; (3) Citation and co-citation network analysis, statistically analyzing citation frequency of literature, authors, and journals, and constructing a knowledge base map to reveal classic theories and interdisciplinary trends during the pandemic [24]. Network maps were visualized using a force-directed layout, with clustering rationality validated by modularity Q value (Q >0.3) and silhouette value (Silhouette >0.7), and two researchers independently verified key nodes to ensure the reproducibility of the results.

Results

Current situation

After rigorous screening, from 2020 to 2024, we obtained a total of 6,291 articles related to liver cancer immunotherapy during the COVID-19 pandemic from WOS. The number of publications shows a year-on-year increasing trend, with the 632 articles published in 2020 accounting for only 10% of the total number, while the 1,713 articles published in 2024 account for 27.2% of the total. In contrast, the average annual citation count has gradually decreased, from an initial 8.53 (in 2020) to 0.93 (in 2024) (Fig. 1).

Country analysis

Between 2020 and 2024, most countries contributed to research on immunotherapy for liver cancer. China and the United States are the leading countries in this field. They play important roles in international scientific collaboration and maintain extensive cooperative relationships with other countries (Fig. 2A). Moreover, we analyzed the publication counts of various countries during the pandemic. China ranked first with 14,639 articles, exhibiting the most significant growth in publication volume and demonstrating its strong research output capability. The United States ranked second with 4,309 articles; although this number is less than one-third of China’s, its influence remains significant due to its extensive research foundation. Italy, Germany, Japan, and France followed closely with 1,642, 1,193, 1,136, and 1,082 articles, respectively, highlighting their important roles in the field of scientific research (Fig. 2B and C). The world map of national collaboration (Fig. 2D) illustrates global research collaborations among countries based on publication counts. Darker colors indicate higher publication volumes, and the number of connecting lines represents the degree of cooperation between countries. Centered on China, the United States, and Canada, many countries have formed close collaborations in liver cancer immunotherapy during the pandemic. The analysis of highly cited countries shows citation rankings in this research field, with China leading significantly at 46,946 citations, indicating its research achievements have a high level of global influence and recognition. The United States ranks second with 26,768 citations, reflecting its important position in the field. Italy, Germany, the United Kingdom, and other countries follow, but with relatively fewer citations (Fig. 2E).

We revealed the research dynamics of academic institutions through institutional collaboration networks, publication trends, and field relevance analysis. The network map based on co-authorship relationships shows that Sun Yat-sen University, Huazhong University of Science and Technology, and Zhejiang University occupy core nodes in the network. Among them, Sun Yat-sen University and Huazhong University of Science and Technology form a close collaboration cluster characterized by dense connections, indicating a high frequency of collaboration (Fig. 3A). Notably, Sun Yat-sen University surpassed 600 publications in 2024, leading with an approximate annual growth rate of 18.5%. Fudan University and Zhejiang University also showed stable growth, with annual growth rates of 12.3% and 14.7%, respectively. In the field relevance analysis(Fig. 3C)—which measures the alignment of publications with the core research field using a relevance index ranging from 0 to 1—Sun Yat-sen University stands out with 722 publications, represented by the largest bubble and the deepest color on the map, reflecting its dominant research position (relevance index of 0.92). Following are Fudan University, with 498 publications and a relevance index of 0.85, and Zhejiang University, with 467 publications and a relevance index of 0.81, indicating both high research activity and strong field relevance.

Keyword and topic trend analysis

Using keyword analysis can reveal research hotspots, knowledge structures, and research trends in disciplines. In the co-occurrence relationship (Fig. 7A), each node represents a keyword, and the size of the node is usually proportional to the frequency of that keyword appearing in the literature. The lines between nodes indicate the co-occurrence relationship between keywords. The keywords “immunotherapy” and “hepatocellular carcinoma” occupy a central position in the research literature on liver cancer immunotherapy and are the main focus of the research. Figure 7B shows the most frequently occurring keywords in the field of liver cancer immunotherapy and their frequencies. “Cancer” is the most frequently occurring keyword, appearing 1299 times, accounting for 9%, indicating that cancer is the core theme of research in this field. Other high-frequency keywords include “immunotherapy,” “expression,” “hepatocellular carcinoma,” etc., all of which have an appearance frequency of over 4%. At the same time, we analyzed the changes in the frequency of some keywords in the field of liver cancer immunotherapy. The cumulative occurrence of “CANCER” has grown the fastest, exceeding 1000 times by 2024. The growth trends of “IMMUNOTHERAPY” and “HEPATOCELLULAR CARCINOMA” indicate that immunotherapy and hepatocellular carcinoma are the main focuses of liver cancer research. The continuous growth of “CANCER” and “IMMUNOTHERAPY” suggests that research in the field of cancer immunotherapy may further accelerate (Fig. 7C). To identify the main research directions and the relationships between themes in the research field, we also conducted a cluster analysis. The keywords were divided into four clusters, with cluster #0 labeled as “prognosis,” cluster #1 as “tumor microenvironment,” cluster #2 as “hepatocellular carcinoma,” and cluster #3 as “cancer immunotherapy” (Fig. 7D). The diamond patterns within each cluster represent the specific keywords contained within that cluster. These clusters reveal the main research themes in the field of liver cancer immunotherapy. The “prognosis” cluster may involve the prognostic assessment and survival analysis of liver cancer patients, the “tumor microenvironment” cluster may focus on the microenvironmental factors affecting tumor growth, the “hepatocellular carcinoma” cluster may concentrate on specific research related to hepatocellular carcinoma, and the “cancer immunotherapy” cluster may involve methods of immunotherapy for cancer. Discussion

The COVID-19 pandemic has had a profound impact on the research landscape concerning immunotherapy approaches for liver cancer. This study is based on a multidimensional analysis of 6,291 peer-reviewed relevant publications from 2020 up to 2024. It reveals the core trends and challenges in liver cancer immunotherapy research in the context of the pandemic.

Structural transformation of the research ecosystem driven by the pandemic

The COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the process of internationalization of scientific research, with significant changes in collaboration models, research topics, and knowledge diffusion [25]. Analysis of national cooperation networks shows that China and the United States dominate research on liver cancer immunotherapy, contributing 46.6% and 13.7% of the publication output, respectively. They have formed a cross-regional collaboration network centered around China-US-Canada [26]. This pattern highlights the pandemic’s acceleration effect on the internationalization of scientific research: in the face of clinical resource shortages and interruptions in multinational trials, sharing data and joint efforts have become critical means to overcoming difficulties [27].

To validate whether the pandemic genuinely accelerated these trends, we conducted a comparative analysis with the bibliometric study by Li et al. (2023), which incorporates pre-pandemic data (up to 2019) [28]. The results indicate that while international collaboration was steadily growing before the pandemic, it underwent a structural qualitative shift post-2020, characterized by the rapid formation of a tight collaborative network centered on China, the US, and Canada. More strikingly, the tumor microenvironment (TME) evolved from a nascent theme in the pre-pandemic era into a definitive research core after 2021, with its keyword frequency and cluster centrality showing explosive growth.This quantitative and qualitative evidence collectively suggests that the pandemic acted as a key catalyst in accelerating these structural transformations.

Furthermore, research topics have also shifted. The keyword evolution analysis indicates that while traditional hotspots (such as “prognostic models” and “immune checkpoint inhibitors”) continue to be focal points, the tumor microenvironment (TME) and combination therapy strategies have become the fastest-growing research directions in the post-pandemic era [29], which have shown a significant increase in prominence after 2021. This trend may be related to the dual impact of the pandemic. From a mechanistic perspective, the systemic inflammatory response triggered by COVID-19 interacts with the tumor immune microenvironment, promoting in-depth exploration of immune cell infiltration and mechanisms of stromal remodeling in the TME [30]. From a clinical perspective, treatment delays caused by the pandemic have forced researchers to seek more efficient combination strategies, such as immunotherapy combined with local ablation or targeted therapy, to rapidly control tumor progression [31].

In addition, knowledge diffusion also shows a polarization trend. Journal analysis indicates that the core journals (top 1%) contributed 35.7% of the publication output, with open access journals (such as *Frontiers in Immunology*) becoming the primary venues for disseminating emerging findings, which have achieved the highest annual growth rate, with a projected 2024 h-index reaching 35. This is closely related to the restrictions on in-person academic exchanges during the pandemic and the accelerated advancement of the open science movement.

Methodological innovation and cross-disease insights: leveraging computational power from COVID-19 research

The pandemic has served as a catalyst not only for international collaboration but also for the rapid adoption of advanced computational methodologies in biomedicine. The surge in research on the tumor microenvironment and combination therapy, as identified by our bibliometric analysis, reflects a paradigm shift towards deciphering complex biological systems. This shift is supported by the growing power of computational biology, which was prominently showcased in COVID-19 research. For instance, integrative approaches combining weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) with explainable artificial intelligence (XAI) have successfully delineated key host-response pathways and identified novel hub genes with therapeutic potential from SARS-CoV-2 transcriptomic data [32]. The application of such robust, data-driven methods allowed for the high-confidence prediction of FDA-approved drug candidates, demonstrating a direct path from genomic discovery to therapeutic repurposing.This methodology holds significant promise for addressing the core challenges in liver cancer immunotherapy. The heterogeneity of the tumor microenvironment (TME) in HCC—a primary focus of post-pandemic research—presents a level of complexity akin to the host-virus interactions in COVID-19. Adopting a similar analytical framework, leveraging WGCNA to identify co-expression modules correlated with immunotherapy response or resistance, followed by XAI models to interpret the predictive features, could uncover biologically coherent gene networks and novel, mechanistically grounded biomarkers. Consequently, the insights and analytical pipelines forged in the COVID-19 crisis provide a valuable blueprint for accelerating the transition from descriptive TME profiling to actionable, personalized combination strategies in oncology.

Core contradiction: the surge in output and the Dilution of influence

Despite a 171% surge in annual publication volume (increasing from 632 articles in 2020 to 1,713 articles in 2024), the average annual citations per article plummeted sharply from 8.53 to 0.93. This significant decline primarily reflects universal bibliometric patterns: On one hand, articles published in 2020 benefit from longer citation accumulation time (nearly 4 years), typically having passed their citation peak or retaining residual impact; whereas newly published articles in 2024 (which may have existed for only months at the time of statistics) are still at the starting point of their citation cycle, requiring time for their influence to materialize (time accumulation effect). On the other hand, the dramatic expansion in total literature volume (2024 being 2.7 times that of 2020) results in finite total citation resources being distributed across more articles, intensifying competition dilution effects (literature volume surge and competition dilution). Additionally, delays in citation database indexing and processing also lead to an underestimation of immediate citations for 2024 publications (database processing and citation indexing lag) [33].

In this context, we conducted a manual review and classification of the top 15 highly cited references listed in Fig. 6C. The analysis revealed that these publications predominantly exhibit three characteristics: First, large-scale randomized controlled trials and pivotal clinical studies, such as the landmark IMbrave150 (atezolizumab plus bevacizumab) and REFLECT (lenvatinib) trials that established new paradigms for first-line treatment of advanced liver cancer, achieved high citation rates due to their profound impact on clinical practice. Second, translational medicine and mechanistic exploration research, including foundational discoveries published in top-tier journals such as Nature and Cell covering areas like the tumor microenvironment, immune evasion mechanisms, and novel biomarkers, which provided the theoretical basis for immunotherapy. Third, retrospective cohort studies and expert consensus documents, which increased significantly during the pandemic. While these rapidly addressed real-world clinical challenges—such as the management of immunotherapy-related adverse events and the impact of COVID-19 on liver cancer care pathways—their evidence level and clinical influence were generally limited.

Building upon the quality assessment of this subset of highly cited literature, the previously noted contradiction between “surging publication volume and declining average citations” further reveals the intrinsic heterogeneity in the field’s research output. Our analysis indicates that the dilution of citation counts partly stems from structural shifts in research types during the pandemic: on one hand, landmark large-scale randomized controlled trials and in-depth mechanistic investigations continued to emerge [34], forming the core of the knowledge system and maintaining high academic influence; on the other hand, a surge of evidence-limited retrospective studies (such as observational reports focusing on urgent issues like treatment delays and COVID-19 co-infections), while rapidly filling knowledge gaps, struggled to accumulate long-term academic impact due to methodological constraints [35, 36]. Bibliometrics, serving as a “bird’s-eye view of trends,” faithfully records the macro-level coexistence of both “core” and “peripheral” research, yet its counting mechanism inherently fails to automatically differentiate their quality. Therefore, when interpreting bibliometric data, understanding the spectrum of research quality underlying these numbers is crucial, underscoring the necessity of integrating bibliometric indicators with evidence level-based screening criteria in future research.

Furthermore, our bibliometric trends have captured another critical phenomenon that influenced the research ecosystem during the pandemic: the increased visibility of negative results and retracted studies. The public health urgency of the COVID-19 pandemic drove an unprecedented pace in the release and dissemination of research findings, which included a surge of non-peer-reviewed negative results emerging on preprint platforms. Although these studies are crucial for avoiding publication bias and redundant clinical efforts, they often face challenges in being published in high-impact journals, thereby limiting their traditional citation impact. Although our bibliometric counts cannot directly distinguish these studies, this dynamic shift in the research environment—specifically, the rapid dissemination of a substantial volume of low-impact or problematic scientific output—constitutes another important context for understanding the core contradiction between “surged publication volume” and “diluted average influence.”

The differentiated contributions and challenges of Sino-American collaboration

China leads the world in liver cancer immunotherapy research with an absolute output advantage of 14,639 papers, with Sun Yat-sen University as a core hub institution (over 600 papers published in 2024, with a field relevance index of 0.92), collaborating with Huazhong University of Science and Technology and Zhejiang University to form a high-density cooperative cluster. However, an analysis of highly cited countries shows that the average citation rate per paper in the United States is 6.21, while in China it is 3.21. This disparity, while observed across multiple biomedical fields, stems from fundamental structural differences in the two nations’ research ecosystems .The divergence can be primarily attributed to distinct funding priorities and research output composition. China’s research strength is underpinned by its vast clinical resources, which facilitate a high volume of translational studies and large-scale clinical trials aimed at addressing immediate therapeutic needs. In contrast, the United States excels in the depth of basic mechanism exploration and innovation in experimental design, often producing foundational studies that have a broader, long-term impact across related fields of immunology and biology. Furthermore, differences in international collaboration patterns also contribute; while China has cultivated a robust domestic network, U.S.-led research frequently engages in more geographically diverse international consortia, which enhances the global visibility and cross-disciplinary citation reach of their work. Therefore, the citation gap reflects a strategic division of labor rather than a simple quality differential. China’s system is optimized for driving clinical validation and application, whereas the U.S. system often pioneers fundamental discoveries. This synergy underscores the immense potential of Sino-American collaboration. However, geopolitical factors may weaken data sharing; thus, a sustainable cooperation framework post-pandemic needs to be established, such as a joint clinical trial registration platform between China and the United States, to promote collaboration and leverage these complementary strengths [37, 38].

Key pathway for clinical translation: from microenvironment decoding to precision intervention

Keyword clustering and emergence detection indicate that tumor microenvironment remodeling (such as stromal cell interactions and immune exhaustion markers) is becoming a core direction for breaking through the efficacy bottleneck. For example, immune-activated hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) patients show a 2.7-fold increase in response rate to PD-1 inhibitors based on TME classification for immunotherapy response prediction. Combining targeted TME regulatory targets (such as VEGF and PD-L1 inhibitors) significantly improves the median overall survival (OS) of advanced HCC patients [39]. In the future, we need to analyze the heterogeneity of TME through multi-omics integration (such as single-cell sequencing and spatial transcriptomics) to guide the design of personalized combination strategies, which will further enhance clinical treatment outcomes [40].

Limitations and future directions

This study has certain limitations. First, the data sources are limited to English-language literature from the Web of Science (WoS) Core Collection. This exclusive reliance on a single, English-centric database means that our analysis may not fully capture the global research landscape, potentially leading to an underestimation of contributions from non-English speaking countries and regions, as well as relevant studies indexed in other databases such as Scopus or PubMed. Second, the citation metrics for recent publications, particularly those from 2024, should be interpreted with caution due to a short citation window; therefore, the influence of these potentially high-quality studies might be underrepresented, and their citation data still need to be tracked and verified in the future. Third, it is important to acknowledge the inherent methodological constraints of bibliometric visualization and clustering techniques. While tools like VOSviewer and CiteSpace provide powerful insights, the network structures and cluster solutions they generate are sensitive to parameter selection (e.g., threshold values for co-occurrence). The interpretation of clusters, while validated by modularity and silhouette scores, remains partly descriptive, and the maps themselves simplify complex academic relationships into visual representations.To address these limitations and guide future research, we suggest strengthening the “trinity” approach, which encompasses three aspects: at the mechanistic level, exploring the impact of post-COVID-19 immune memory on immunotherapy for liver cancer; at the clinical level, optimizing the sequential strategy of combination therapies using real-world evidence; and at the technical level, developing a telemedicine-supported monitoring system for immunotherapy-related toxicity to promote the ongoing development of immunotherapy in the field of liver cancer.

Conclusion

In summary, the COVID-19 pandemic has accelerated the internationalization of liver cancer immunotherapy research but also presents numerous challenges, such as disparities in research quality, differences in the extent or nature of collaboration between China and the United States, and bottlenecks in clinical translation. In the future, we need to continue making efforts to optimize the research ecosystem and improve research quality; strengthen international cooperation; and promote clinical translation to achieve breakthroughs in clinical applications of liver cancer immunotherapy.

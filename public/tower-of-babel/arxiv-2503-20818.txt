The Pontryagin-Thom Theorem for Families of Framed Equivariant Manifolds
Lucas Williams

Abstract
. The Pontryagin-Thom theorem gives an isomorphism from the cobordism group of framed n n -manifolds to the n n th stable homotopy group of the sphere spectrum. In this paper, we prove the generalization of the Pontryagin-Thom theorem for families of framed equivariant manifolds parameterized over a compact base space.



1.
Introduction

A framed manifold is a smooth compact manifold along with a trivialization of its stable tangent bundle. The set of framed
n
-manifolds up to cobordism forms an abelian group under disjoint union which we denote
\omega_{n}
. The Pontryagin-Thom theorem, also known as the Pontryagin-Thom isomorphism, gives a correspondence between cobordism classes of framed manifolds and the stable homotopy groups of spheres:

\omega_{n}\cong\pi_{n}(\mathbb{S}).

This theorem provides a bridge between geometric topology and homotopy theory. The Pontryagin-Thom theorem may be seen as the jumping off point for a body of work that has led to such celebrated results as the solution to the Kervaire invariant one problem
[
6
]
, various computations of smooth structures on spheres as in
[
8
]
and
[
23
]
, and the surgery theoretic classification of high dimensional manifolds
[
10
]
. In the present work, we provide a new and more general proof of the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a product of a finite group and a torus. There is an isomorphism

\omega_{V}^{G}(X,A)\to\pi_{V}^{G}(\Sigma^{\infty}X/A)

from the group of
V
-framed
G
-manifolds admitting a continuous equivariant map of pairs
(M,\partial M)\to(X,A)
to the
V
th equivariant stable homotopy group of the suspension spectrum of
X/A
. Here a
V
-framed manifold is a manifold
M
equipped with an equivalence class of stable bundle isomorphism between
TM
and
M\times V
. The precise definition will be given in
Definition

2.4
.

Our treatment of this theorem differs from the classical literature in several respects. We use the modern framework of genuine
G
-spectra and the language of
G
-manifolds with corners. We also generalize the group of equivariance from a finite group (as is the case in
[
9
]
) to any product of a finite group and a torus. As noted in
[
17
, 6.2.13]
this is the largest class of groups for which such a result should be expected to hold.

The main advantage of our new proof of the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom theorem for framed
G
-manifolds is that it generalizes quite nicely to the fiberwise setting. This generalization is our second main theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
G
be a product of a finite group and a torus. There is an isomorphism

\omega_{V}^{G}((X,A)\to B)\to\pi_{V}^{G}(\Gamma_{B}R^{lv}\Sigma^{\infty}X\cup_{A}B)

from the cobordism group of families of
V
-framed
G
-manifolds parameterized over an equivariant map
(X,A)\to B
and the
V
th equivariant stable homotopy group of the right derived spectrum of sections of the suspension spectrum of
X\cup_{A}B
.

In the non-equivariant setting, given an element
(S^{k+n}\xrightarrow{f}S^{k})\in\pi_{n}(\mathbb{S})
, a framed manifold is produced by deforming
f
to be smooth and transverse to
0\in S^{k}
, then taking
f^{-1}(0)
. In the equivariant setting it is not the case that a map between manifolds may be deformed so as to be transvere to a submanifold of our choice. Due to this lack of a ‘transverse approximation theorem’ it is frequently (erroneously) claimed that the Pontryagin-Thom theorem fails for framed
G
-manifolds.

However, results from as early as the 1970’s prove a Pontryagin-Thom style theorem for
G
-manifolds in both the framed and unoriented cases. Segal originally announced a Pontryagin-Thom theorem for framed
G
-manifolds in
[
18
]
. The proof was completed by Hausschild
[
4
]
and Kosniowski
[
9
]
. Pontryagin-Thom theorems for (un)oriented
G
-manifolds appear in tom Dieck
[
20
]
, Wasserman
[
24
]
, and Waner
[
22
]
. See Schwede
[
17
]
for a modern treatment of tom Dieck’s result.

Organization

In section two we develop the theory of cobordism for framed
G
-manifolds before proving
Theorem

1.1
.

In section three we develop the theory of cobordism for families of framed
G
-manifolds and then prove
Theorem

1.2
. The proof relies largely on equivariant parameterized stable homotopy theory.

2.
The equivariant version

In this section we prove a Pontryagin-Thom theorem for framed
G
-manifolds. Many of the ideas in this section originate in
[
9
]
. However, unlike
[

]
we use the modern framework of genuine

Preliminaries

2.1.1.
Equivariant framed bordism

Throughout this paper,

Notation 2.1

Definition 2.2

A

Remark 2.3

We will use the term ‘closed manifold’ to mean a compact smooth manifold without boundary. When we refer to a compact manifold rather than a closed manifold, we are allowing the possibility of a boundary.

Definition 2.4

Let

-representation. A

-vector bundle isomorphisms

TM\oplus\varepsilon_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cong\varepsilon_{M}(V)\oplus\varepsilon_{M}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

We say

We also say that

Note that although the manifolds in the above definition are smooth, we are working with continuous vector bundles over them.

Remark 2.6

There is another notion of a

-framed

-representation. While these two notions are equivalent if

An

Definition 2.11

(2)

Definition 2.12

A

Definition 2.13

A function on an open subset of

This definition allows us to define the tangent bundle of a smooth manifold with corners in the usual way using derivations of smooth functions.

Lemma 2.24

2.2.

In this section we will define four maps and then show that they fit into a commuting square. First we need a lemma relating trivializations of tangent and normal bundles.

Proof.

Let

Definition 2.36

2.3.
Proof of the equivariant Pontryagin-Thom theorem

In this section we will prove our first main theorem.

Theorem 2.45
.

Let
G
be isomorphic to a product of a finite group and a torus. There is an isomorphism of abelian groups:

\omega_{V}^{G}(X,A)\cong\pi_{V}^{G}(\Sigma^{\infty}X/A).

We will prove this using an isotropy separation technique. More specifically, we will first prove the result for free group actions and then piece various isomorphisms together using equivariant classifying spaces.

Definition 2.46
.

Let
G
be a group. A
family of subgroups of

is a set of subgroups of
G
closed under both conjugation and taking subgroups.

Definition 2.47
.

Let

Notation 2.48

.

If

. This notation is aligned with the fact that

Notation 2.49

Let

be a group, and

. Denote the conjugacy class of

.

Now let

be two adjacent families of subgroups so that

-fixed points of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega_{V^{H}}^{WH}[\textup{free}](X^{H},A^{H})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{V^{H}}^{WH}[\textup{free}](\Sigma^{\infty}X^{H}/A^{H}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{\omega}^{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Psi_{\pi}^{H}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Delta^{H}}

\Delta^{H}

Proof.

Cobordism elements in

2.3.1.
The map
\Psi_{\omega}^{H}
is an isomorphism

If

Definition 2.52
.

Define a map

Remark 2.53

Recall that

Remark 3.12

We say a functor

between categories with weak equivalences is right deformable if there is a full subcategory

. Note that left deformable and left derived functors are defined similarly except the natural weak equivalence goes from the cofibrant replacement to the identity. Further details can be found in
[

]
and
[

]

Proposition 3.13

[

There is a proper model structure on

is:

(1)

an
h
-fibration
(or Hurewicz fibration) if it has the homotopy lifting property. This means we ask for the map
X^{I}\to X\times_{Y}Y^{I}
to have a section.

(2)

a
q
-fibration
(or Serre fibration) if it has the homotopy lifting property with respect to cylinders on discs:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}\times\{0\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{n}\times I}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

(3)

an
h
-cofibration
if it is a closed inclusion and has the homotopy extension property. In essence, the inclusion
X\times I\cup_{X\times\{0\}}Y\times\{0\}\to Y\times I
has a retract.

(4)

an
f
-cofibration
if it a closed inclusion and has the fiberwise homotopy extension property. In essence, the inclusion
X\times I\cup_{X\times\{0\}}Y\times\{0\}\to Y\times I
has a retract which respects the projection map to
B
.

We say a retractive space is
h
-fibrant if the map to the zero object is an
h
-fibration. A retractive space is
h
-cofibrant if the map from the zero object is an
h
-cofibration. Then (co)fibrancy for the other notions above is defined similarly. We say that a parameterized orthogonal spectrum is level
h
-fibrant if each of its constituant spaces is level
h
-fibrant. The other notions of level (co)fibrancy are defined similarly.

Definition A.2
.

A parameterized orthogonal spectrum is stably fibrant if it is level
q
-fibrant and the maps
X_{n}\to\Omega_{B}X_{n+1}
are weak equivalences for all
n\geq 0
.

When defining the weak equivalences and
q
-fibrations of
G
-equivariant retractive spaces, we ask that
f:X\to Y
be a weak equivalence or
q
-fibration on the fixed points
f^{H}:X^{H}\to Y^{H}
for all closed subgroups
H\leq G
. To define
h
-fibrations,
h
-cofibrations, and
f
-cofibrations of equivariant retractive spaces, we simply require that the relevant section or retract be equivariant as well. To define level (co)fibrations of
G
-equivariant parameterized spectra, the group of equivariance at level

is
G\times O(n)
rather than just
G
. We finally note that a
G
-spectrum over
B
is fibrant if it is level
q
-fibrant and the adjunct bonding maps are weak equivalences on all of the fixed point subspaces.

Lemma A.7

Let

be such that the retraction onto

We begin by observing that the following square commutes

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B}Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B}f(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B}f(Y)}

is the stable fibrant replacement functor.

We first claim that the bottom horizontal map is a level equivalence. Since

Proposition A.12
.

Let

be a finite

-CW-complex. Let

be a
G
-spectrum parameterized over

and

A\wedge^{\mathbb{L}}\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{B}(X)\simeq\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{B}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,^{\mathbb{L}}X)

Proof.

By
Lemma

A.9
,
Lemma

A.10
, and
Lemma

A.11
, it suffices to assume that
A
is
h
-cofibrant,
X
is level
h
-cofibrant level
h
-fibrant, and then show that

A\wedge\Gamma_{B}X\simeq\Gamma_{B}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X).

We proceed via induction on the skeleta of
B
. We first show that the result holds when restricting

to the 0-skeleton of

. Say
B^{(0)}=\coprod G/H
where the
H

G
. A section over a single orbit space

is equivalent to the product over the elements of

of the fiber spectra over those elements. So,

\displaystyle A\wedge\Gamma_{B^{(0)}}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq A\wedge\prod_{b\in B^{(0)}}\Gamma_{b}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq A\wedge\bigvee_{b\in B^{(0)}}\Gamma_{b}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigvee_{b\in B^{(0)}}A\wedge\Gamma_{b}(X)

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{b\in B^{(0)}}A\wedge\Gamma_{b}(X).

Similarly,

\Gamma_{B^{(0)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)\simeq\prod_{b\in B^{(0)}}\Gamma_{b}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X).

Since the sections of

are precisely the sections of

, we see that

A\wedge\Gamma_{B^{(0)}}(X)\simeq\Gamma_{B^{(0)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)

We now proceed to the inductive step. Suppose that

By restricting a section on

to

The fiber

\displaystyle(Fj)(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{s\in\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}(X)(V)\mid s|_{B^{(n-1)}}=i_{X(V)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{s:B^{(n)}\to X(V)\mid\pi_{X(V)}\circ s=1\text{ and }s|_{B^{(n-1)}}=i_{X(V)}\right\}

where

is the zero section of

on

.

We therefore have a fiber sequence of
G
-spectra

Smashing with

preserves this fiber sequence so that we have a fiber sequence

Again by

A.6
, we have a levelwise Serre fibration

\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)\xrightarrow{l}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)

with fiber

\displaystyle(Fl)(V)

\displaystyle=\left\{s\in\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)(V)\mid s|_{B^{(n-1)}}=i_{(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)(V)}\right\}

\displaystyle=\left\{s:B^{(n)}\to(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)(V)\mid\pi_{(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)(V)}\circ s=1\text{ and }s|_{B^{(n-1)}}=i_{(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)(V)}\right\}.

Hence,

is a fiber sequence.

We then have a map of fiber sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\wedge Fj}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\wedge\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\wedge\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Fl}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}(A\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

where the two vertical maps on the right send

to

given by

, where

. The map on the left is the map induced on fibers. This is the map which at level

is given by

By assumption, the vertical map on the right is a stable equivalence.

has level

given by, sections of

over

which vanish on

. This spectrum then splits into a finite product over the

is stably equivalent to

given by

and bonding maps given by applying the bonding maps of

to sections restricted to a single

-cell. The equivalence

is given by sending a section on

to a section on

which agrees with the original section on

and is the zero section everywhere else.

where

. We now claim that the canonical map

\Phi^{H}(\phi^{*}(\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,X))\rightarrow\Phi^{H}(\phi^{*}(f(\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)))

is a stable equivalence. Observe that
\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,X\to f(\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,X)
is an acyclic cofibration. Since
\phi^{*}
is left Quillen it preserves acyclic cofibrations. By
[
11
]
,
\Phi^{H}(-)
also preserves acyclic cofibrations. So the above map is a weak equivalence as desired. Since
\phi^{*}
commutes with
\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,(-)
and
\Phi^{H}(-)
is monoidal,

\Phi^{H}(\phi^{*}(\widetilde{EP_{H}}\,\overline{\wedge}\,X))\simeq\Phi^{H}(\phi^{*}(X)).

Finally, combining the above equations yields

\phi^{*}(\Phi^{H}(X))\simeq\Phi^{H}(\phi^{*}(X))

as desired.
∎

Proposition A.15
.

Let
B
be a finite
G
-CW-complex. Let
X\in G\mathcal{OS}(B)
be a
G
-spectrum parameterized over
B
and
H
a closed subgroup of
G
. The right derived spectrum of sections,
\mathbb{R}\Gamma_{B}(-)
, commutes with the left derived geometric fixed points,
\Phi^{H}(-)
.

Proof.

It suffices to show the result on the full subcategory of
G\mathcal{OS}(B)
on the objects which are stably fibrant, level
h
-fibrant and level
h
-cofibrant.

We proceed by induction on the skeleta of
B
. We first show the base case. Suppose that
B
is some finite
G
-set. We wish to show that

\Gamma_{B^{H}}\Phi^{H}X\simeq\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B}X.

We first observe that non-equivariantly

\Gamma_{B}X=\prod_{b\in B}X_{b}

To make sense of this statement equivariantly, we define a
G
action on
\prod_{b\in B}X_{b}
by

g\cdot(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=(gx_{g^{-1}1},\dots,gx_{g^{-1}k})

where

(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\in X_{1}(V)\times\dots\times X_{k}(V)

for some
G
-representation
V
.

This action makes the unit and counit maps from the non-equivariant adjunction between pullback to
B
and sections on
B
into equivariant maps defining an adjunction in
G\mathcal{OS}(B)
.

Now observe that

\displaystyle\Phi^{H}\left(\prod_{b\in B}X_{b}\right)\simeq\prod_{b\in B^{H}}\Phi^{H}(X_{b}).

Moreover, if
b\in B^{H}
then the fiber spectrum over
b
is the pullback of
X
along
\{b\}\to B
. Thus,
\Phi^{H}(X_{b})\cong(\Phi^{H}X)_{b}
by
Lemma

A.14
. This proves the claim for sections on a finite
G
-set.

We now proceed to the inductive step. Let
B
be some finite
G
-CW complex and suppose that

\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X\simeq\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}X

Again by
Lemma

A.6
, we have a levelwise Serre fibration

\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}X\xrightarrow{j}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}X

The functor
\Phi^{H}
is excisive
[
15
]
and thus preserves fiber sequences. In other words, we have a fiber sequence

\Phi^{H}Fj\to\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}X\xrightarrow{\Phi^{H}(j)}\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}X.

We also have a fiber sequence

Fl\to\Gamma_{B^{(n)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X\xrightarrow{l}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X

where
l
is a levelwise Serre fibration by
Lemma

A.6
.

We now build a map between these two fiber sequences. By
Lemma

A.14
, there is a natural isomorphism commuting the pullback functor with geometric fixed points. Composing this with the unit and the counit of the adjunction between pullback and
\Gamma_{B}
we obtain a natural transformation from
\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B}
to
\Gamma_{B}\Phi^{H}
. These maps assemble into a commuting square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

Then the map induced on the fibers turns this into a map of fiber sequences:

(A.16)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}Fj}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)}}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Fl}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Gamma_{B^{(n-1)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Phi^{H}j}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces l}

We now argue that the left vertical map is a stable equivalence. As in
Proposition

A.12
, we may rewrite
Fj
as

Fj\simeq\prod_{\text{$n$-cells of $B$}}F(G/K\wedge_{+}S^{n},X_{b})

where
F(-,-)
is the cotensor spectrum. Now, since
\Phi^{H}(-)
commutes with wedge sums, it commutes with finite products. Additionally, since
G/K\wedge_{+}S^{n}
is a finite CW-complex (and thus dualizable), the canonical map
\Phi^{H}:F(G/K\wedge_{+}S^{n},X)\to F((G/K)^{H}\wedge_{+}S^{n},\Phi^{H}(X)_{b})
is a weak equivalence. Finally, since
\Phi^{H}(-)
preserves fiber sequences, and thus cofiber sequences, we observe that
\Phi^{H}(X)_{b}\simeq\Phi^{H}(X_{b})
. Putting these three facts together, we see that

\displaystyle\Phi^{H}(Fj)

\displaystyle\simeq\Phi^{H}\left(\prod_{\text{$n$-cells of $B$}}F(G/K\wedge_{+}S^{n},X_{b})\right)

\displaystyle\simeq\prod_{\text{$n$-cells of $B^{H}$}}F((G/K)^{H}\wedge_{+}S^{n},\Phi^{H}(X)_{b}).

Similarly,
Fl
is stably equivalent to

\prod_{\text{$n$-cells of $B^{H}$}}F((G/K)^{H}\wedge_{+}S^{n}\to\Phi^{H}(X)_{b}.

Moreover, the map
\Phi^{H}(Fj)\to Fl
of
Equation

A.16
is precisely this chain of weak equivalences.

Finally, the long exact sequence of homotopy groups associated to a fiber sequence and the five-lemma show that

\Gamma_{B^{(n)^{H}}}\Phi^{H}X\simeq\Phi^{H}\Gamma_{B^{(n)}}X

as desired.
∎

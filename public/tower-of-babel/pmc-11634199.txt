Integrating artificial intelligence in orthopaedic care and surgery: the revolutionary role of ChatGPT, as written with ChatGPT
Authors: Diane Ghanem
Published: 2024

The last decade has witnessed a transformative surge in artificial intelligence (AI), which has dynamically reshaped numerous sectors, including healthcare. This evolution marks a significant shift in the landscape of medical diagnostics, therapeutics, and patient care across diverse specialties (Supplemental Table 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/JS9/D522). One notable example is the introduction of ChatGPT, a state-of-the-art large language model (LLM) developed by OpenAI (San Francisco, CA, 2022), which has demonstrated the potential to support clinical decision-making and improve medical education1–3.

While its efficacy continues to be rigorously assessed, ChatGPT stands out with its extraordinary proficiency in interpreting, generating, and contextualizing human language2–4. This remarkable ability positions ChatGPT at the forefront of this review, as it emerges as a valuable tool in medical contexts, particularly in orthopedic surgery, where precision and continuous learning. Recent studies, including those by Kung et al., Mika et al., and Ghanem et al., have demonstrated ChatGPT’s potential to enhance orthopedic education and clinical decision-making by providing evidence-based insights, improving comprehension of surgical principles, and supporting patient care1,5–7. From assisting with medical literature reviews and diagnostic challenges to facilitating patient education and enhancing surgical training, ChatGPT’s applications are vast and multifaceted. In this review, we aim to explore ChatGPT’s evolving role in orthopedic surgery, highlighting its current applications, prospects, and challenges that remain in its integration into clinical practice.

Current applications in orthopedic surgery

Medical literature review and research assistance

ChatGPT can assist orthopedic surgeons in staying updated with the latest research by summarizing scientific papers, offering insights into recent studies, and helping in literature reviews. This can be particularly useful in keeping abreast of new techniques, treatments, and advancements in orthopedic surgery8. Additionally, ChatGPT can be an excellent companion for sparking creativity, providing a solution to the common struggle of writer’s block, where ideas seem just out of reach at the start of an article3. According to Bi, the ability of ChatGPT to generate manuscript drafts and its potential to streamline the writing process should be acknowledged3. However, it is essential to note the limitations posed by its inability to validate citations or check the veracity of the data it generates8,9. A critical evaluation of AI-generated text remains key to ensure scientific accuracy, as emphasized by Ollivier et al.8 By way of custom versions of ChatGPT designed specifically for research, ChatGPT can also assist in the analysis of clinical data, helping to identify patterns and correlations that may not be immediately apparent to human researchers10. This can lead to new insights and advancements in orthopedic treatment and surgery techniques.

Patient education and communication

AI language models, such as ChatGPT, can be used to explain complex medical conditions and surgical procedures to patients in a clear, easily understandable manner. Unlike some medical websites or even in-person consultations, which may overwhelm patients with ‘medical jargon’, ChatGPT excels at breaking down complex terms into simpler, everyday language that patients can comprehend. Indeed, ChatGPT has been considered an efficient tool to assist in modifying the readability of patient education materials and orthopedic research consents11,12. Whether patients directly ask ChatGPT a medical question or provide it with technical medical terms to be simplified, the AI can offer accurate and easier-to-understand responses than many medical websites or doctors7,13. Additionally, ChatGPT can provide this information in multiple languages, making it more accessible to a wider patient population2. This can improve patient comprehension and engagement, particularly in diverse communities where language barriers may hinder access to clear medical information. The importance of AI in offering personalized, yet simplified, health information has been highlighted in studies such as that by Dubin et al., which showed the success of ChatGPT compared with Google searches in the context of total joint arthroplasties14. And while caution is warranted, multiple research has now highlighted the accuracy of ChatGPT in delivering consistently reliable health information7,11,13,14. By offering clear, tailored explanations, ChatGPT helps patients set realistic expectations regarding surgical outcomes, recovery times, and potential risks6,13.

Surgical planning and decision support

By processing vast amounts of medical data and research, ChatGPT can assist surgeons in making informed decisions about surgical procedures. It can provide information on best practices, likely outcomes, and risk factors based on patient-specific data and prevalent medical knowledge2. For example, a surgeon using ChatGPT during preoperative planning for a complex femoral fracture could receive real-time evidence-based recommendations on implant selection and surgical approaches tailored to the patient’s unique anatomy and medical history. AI’s capacity to analyze vast amounts of data and offer predictive analysis can enhance both diagnostic accuracy and treatment planning, as highlighted by Hernigou and Scarlat15. In September 2023, OpenAI enhanced ChatGPT-4 by incorporating the ability to handle image inputs, enabling AI to analyze, comprehend, and create textual descriptions from visual data such as images and tables16. This development opens up future possibilities for ChatGPT in orthopedic surgery, where accurate interpretation of imaging data is critical. Although it is not yet trained in radiographic analysis, these advancements point toward its potential to assist with imaging interpretation, such as detecting subtle fractures or offering surgical guidance.

Training and simulation

In educational settings, ChatGPT can be used to create realistic patient scenarios for training orthopedic surgeons. It can generate case studies, quizzes, and interactive learning modules to aid in the education of medical students and residents17. For example, a randomized controlled trial by Gan et al. found that students using ChatGPT-4 as a learning tool performed significantly better on orthopedic multiple-choice questions (MCQs) compared to the control group, achieving a 70.6% accuracy rate18. Most recently, Ghanem et al. found that ChatGPT-4 passed the American Academy of Orthopedic Surgeons ‘Orthopedic In-Training Exam’ (OITE) at the level of a third-year resident1. However, on more specialized exams, such as the 2019 American Society for Surgery of the Hand (ASSH) Self-Assessment Exam, ChatGPT-4, scored a respectable 61.98%, yet still fell short of the average performance of hand surgery physicians, particularly in sections on bone and joint knowledge19. Similarly, Cuthbert et al. demonstrated the model’s struggle on the Fellowship of the Royal College of Surgeons (FRCS) exam in Traumatology and Orthopedic Surgery, where it achieved only 35.8%, significantly lower than the passing rate20. This underscores ChatGPT’s potential in general orthopedic training while highlighting its limitations in more specialized settings at this time19,20. Importantly, all researchers agreed on the educational value of ChatGPT as an adjunct for information rehearsal and manipulation for board preparation and others1,19.

Postoperative care and follow-up

In the same way that they can assist with surgical planning and decision support, AI models can enhance postoperative care by providing tailored guidelines for rehabilitation, answering common patient queries, and offering suggestions for physiotherapy and recovery6,7,14,21. For example, Mika et al. and Dubin et al. found that ChatGPT appropriately answered patients’ questions related to total hip arthroplasty, while Bains et al. found similar results for total knee arthroplasty, and Ghanem et al. for osteoporosis6,14. ChatGPT can also personalize these recommendations based on a patient’s specific surgical procedure, recovery timeline, and medical history, ensuring that rehabilitation plans are individualized. For example, ChatGPT could generate personalized physiotherapy routines for a patient recovering from a strained calf, recommend home exercises, or outline steps for progressively regaining strength and flexibility (Fig. 1). In addition, it can offer these guidelines in the patient’s preferred language and adjust the complexity of explanations to match their educational level (Fig. 2). Ultimately, this ensures that patients receive accessible and relevant postoperative care instructions, thereby reducing the risk of complications and improving long-term outcomes14,21.

Administrative tasks

Automating administrative tasks such as documentation, scheduling, and billing can be facilitated by AI language models like ChatGPT22,23. In February 2023, Patel and Lam22 demonstrated how ChatGPT can empower medical professionals to succinctly input details, key concepts for elaboration, and specific directions for explanation, thereby generating a comprehensive and formal discharge summary in just a few seconds. By streamlining routine administrative work, ChatGPT frees up valuable time for surgeons to focus on more critical aspects of patient care and surgical procedures. This shift not only improves workflow efficiency but also enhances patient outcomes by allowing surgeons and medical staff to spend more time directly interacting with patients, while minimizing the risk of human error in administrative duties.

Prospects and challenges

As ChatGPT continues to evolve, its role in orthopedic surgery is poised to expand far beyond administrative tasks. As highlighted by Karnuta24, the integration of AI into orthopedics could be transformative, akin to the technological revolution brought by metallic instruments. Potential developments include not only independent interpretation of radiographic images, but also real-time intraoperative assistance, and integration with robotic surgical systems. Surgeons could use AI assistance to augment intraoperative decision-making, particularly in complex cases where unexpected complications arise25,26. For instance, Cheng et al.26 propose the use of AI in analyzing intraoperative data and generating real-time feedback to avoid complications in surgeries such as spine and joint reconstruction surgeries. ChatGPT could quickly pull from extensive surgical databases and literature to suggest alternative techniques or alert the surgeon to rare but critical risks. Another emerging area of AI integration in orthopedic surgery is its use in biomechanical modeling27,28. It is possible that ChatGPT, when integrated with a biomechanical software, could simulate patient-specific movement patterns, offering surgeons detailed insights into the biomechanics of the musculoskeletal system29. For example, in joint reconstruction surgeries, ChatGPT could provide data-driven predictions on how different implant types might affect long-term joint stability and function.

While ChatGPT offers numerous benefits, it is crucial to recognize the limitations and potential drawbacks of its use. First, AI language models, including ChatGPT, are prone to errors and biases present in their training data. As mentioned by Alkaissi and McFarlane, the risk of AI-generated ‘hallucinations’, where the model produces plausible but incorrect information, is high30. Inaccurate or misleading information can have serious consequences in a medical context8,9,30. A recent study by Ghanem et al.9 has shown that while ChatGPT provided accurate information regarding the ABCDE trauma protocol, it was unable to provide accurate references to back up its generated text – an important warning to the ChatGPT users in the medical field. Therefore, stringent protocols for accuracy must be developed to avoid serious consequences in a medical context. Second, the reliance on AI tools may inadvertently lead to a reduction in critical thinking skills and over-reliance on technology. Third, ChatGPT and AI models have intrinsic limitations in handling complex, logical reasoning, as pointed out by Cuthbert and Simpson20, suggesting that further development is required before they can be fully relied upon in high-stakes surgical decisions. Last but not least, the ethical implications of using AI in healthcare, including patient privacy and data security, must be carefully considered. It will be essential for healthcare systems to develop stringent protocols to protect patient data while leveraging AI’s potential. Therefore, the integration of ChatGPT in clinical settings should be supervised by trained healthcare professionals to ensure accuracy and ethical compliance1,7–9,19,30.

Conclusion

In conclusion, ChatGPT’s applications range from enhancing surgical planning and execution to improving patient education, postoperative care, and research. The integration of AI in orthopedic surgery is still in its early stages, but the potential benefits of combining the clinical expertise of surgeons with AI-driven insights are immense. Yet, it is important to note that AI language models are tools to support, not replace, the expertise of medical professionals. The role of human evaluation, critical thinking, and clinical judgment remains indispensable for ensuring the responsible and effective use of AI-generated content. Embracing the inevitable, we should not fear the advent of ChatGPT, as the question lies not in its usage, but in how we can best integrate this innovative tool to revolutionize and enrich the field of orthopedic surgery, without compromising ethical standards or clinical accuracy. After all, while this ChatGPT short review has been edited by a postdoctoral research fellow in orthopedic surgery, would you have guessed it was initially drafted by ChatGPT itself?

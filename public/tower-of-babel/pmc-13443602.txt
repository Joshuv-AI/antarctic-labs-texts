Adaptive constraint-following control for maintenance robotic manipulator with uncertainties
Authors: Yuze Ma, Da Xu, Zhaoyang Wang, Guoyun Dai
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo enhance the reliability and motion stability of the maintenance robotic manipulator system for armored vehicles, we propose a novel adaptive robust control methodology based on the Udwadia-Kalaba (UK) dynamic framework. To address the challenges such as parameter uncertainties and external disturbances in the system, a crucial leakage mechanism is integrated into the adaptive law, which not only suppresses the unbounded growth of the adaptive parameters under continuous disturbances, but also automatically attenuates the control gain when the error converges, significantly improving the smoothness of the controller’s transient response. Finally, we validate the effectiveness of the proposed controller via co-simulation in a multibody dynamics environment.Subject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Background

The application of the manipulator system in the industrial field has expanded from routine production to some special working conditions1. Researchers have solved the maintenance, processing, and transportation problems that humans face in environments with high temperature, high pressure, and high vibration through technological innovation2,3. Maintaining manipulator is a representative application in the field of mechanical manipulator technology, in areas such as underwater maintenance4, wind power maintenance5 and unmanned space station repairs6,7, manipulators must ensure the accuracy of the positioning of their ends while overcoming external vibrations, which places highly high performance requirements on the control system.

Formulation of the problem of interest for this investigation

Unlike the manipulator used under normal working conditions, the multifunctional maintenance manipulator integrated into a certain type of unmanned heavy vehicle operates under highly harsh conditions8. The manipulator system must counteract the frequent vibrations caused by the vehicle’s movement while ensuring precise control of the manipulator position9,10. This is a challenging issue in the field of control, as there are always various uncertainties in the system, including the system parameter uncertainties caused by the end of the manipulator grasping different tools and the external disturbances caused by vehicle vibrations.

However, precisely determining the boundaries of these uncertainties is highly difficult, and controllers that rely on known boundaries may not be able to guarantee the performance and stability of the system when the boundaries are unknown or time-varying. Therefore, this paper aims to address a key question: When there are uncertainties in the constraints, dynamic model, and external disturbances of a mechanical system, and the boundaries of these uncertainties are also unknown, can we design a controller to ensure the stability and acceptable tracking performance of the system?

Literature survey

In the past, people have done a lot of research and innovation on the control problem of robotic manipulators, for the control problems of some low-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulator under normal operating conditions, PID controllers are widely used in industry due to their simplicity and model-free tuning. However, their performance degrades in highly nonlinear, coupled, and uncertain systems11. By improving and correcting the conventional PID controller, online self-tuning of control parameters can be achieved12. However, as a linear control method, the PID controller itself has inherent deficiencies in dealing with system nonlinearity, coupling, and uncertainty13. More importantly, if system parameters or external disturbances change rapidly, the integral section of the PID controller may not have enough time to respond, resulting in poor tracking performance. Sliding mode control is favored in the control of nonlinear systems due to its simple implementation and strong robustness14,15. It should be noted that the traditional SMC control gain design relies on an accurate estimation of the upper bound of system uncertainties, and an overly conservative gain design can exacerbate the chattering problem.

To accurately observe the working status, performance, or parameters in real-time of the robotic manipulator, and to grasp the motion indicators of the system, the addition of adaptive control can ensure excellent tracking performance of the manipulator system under uncertainties and faults16,17. To address the modeling and control issues of high-degree-of-freedom and flexible manipulators, many researchers have integrated artificial intelligence techniques into adaptive robust control18. Zhang et al.19 proposed an adaptive controller that leverages deep neural network architectures to address the unique challenges posed by transporting unknown loads in a space environment. Song et al.20 proposed a Lyapunov-based finite-time convergence control scheme for articulated robotic manipulators operating under vibrational perturbations. Hu et al.21 proposed a practical method that combines feedback linearization control with a model based on multi-layer neural networks (MNN) to handle parameter uncertainties and external disturbances. For mechanical systems with uncertainties, the intervention of adaptive laws often leads to satisfactory results22–24. Notably, the intervention of adaptive laws plays a significant role in improving the system’s accuracy in the control problem of complex robotic manipulator systems, especially under vibration conditions and uncertainty perturbation. Although these traditional robust control methods can handle uncertainties in the system, such uncertainties are limited to those that are constant and slowly changing.

To explore a more effective method for handling rapidly varying uncertainties and to provide stable guaranties for transient processes of system states or outputs, Chen et al.25,26 first proposed the concept of constrained following error from the perspective of servo constraints, which is based on the Udwadia-Kalaba theory27,28. This transformed the motion control problem of complex, time-varying uncertain mechanical systems into a problem of how to make the controlled system move according to the given constraints (or inequality constraints)29. By combining all the uncertain terms together, the boundary structure of the uncertainties can be determined and used to suppress the uncertainties. This method has been proven to be capable of conducting a detailed dynamic analysis of the manipulator system and improving the fundamental understanding of constrained motion in multibody dynamics. It has been applied to solve problems such as multi-link systems30–32, flexible manipulator systems33,34, as well as trajectory tracking control and collaborative position and attitude control of multiple manipulators35–37.

In conclusion, regarding the possible uncertainties in the system and the situations where deviations from the initial conditions may occur, it is reasonable to expect that with a class of adaptive robust control laws that can ensure uniform boundedness and uniform ultimate boundedness, the system can approximately follow the constraints. Although existing research efforts have partially addressed the control issues in multi-degree-of-freedom manipulator systems under various working conditions, there is still some room for research in improving the tracking control accuracy of strict uncertain systems under severe disturbance conditions. The adaptive control employed in this paper provides a powerful tool for addressing the rapid changes and uncertainties of the system state.

Scope and contribution of this Study

The subject of this study is a complex mechanical system with constraints (which can also be extended to non-complete constraints), and its key feature is that the system contains unknown but bounded uncertainties. The control objective is to achieve constraint tracking within an acceptable small neighborhood, rather than pursuing perfect and precise tracking in the presence of uncertainties.

The main contributions are as follows. (i) In many robust control methods for practical engineering applications, it is difficult to accurately determine the boundaries of system uncertainties. The core contribution of this study lies in the proposed control framework, which does not require prior knowledge of the exact boundaries of uncertainties. We estimate the comprehensive impact of uncertainties online by designing a new adaptive law, enabling the system to provide rigorous guaranties even when faced with rapidly changing and unknown uncertainties at the boundaries. (ii) Our work is based on the constraint mechanics framework proposed by Udwadia and Kalaba. The novelty of this study lies in systematically integrating adaptive robust control theory with a leakage mechanism into the UK framework and applying it to situations with significant model uncertainties. This enhances the robustness and performance of the controller in preventing parameter drift and avoiding overly aggressive control inputs, enabling the UK theory to move from an ideal model to more challenging practical applications. (iii) We innovatively applied this controller to complex mechanical systems operating in high-vibration environments, enhancing its value in practical applications. We established a complete multibody dynamics model that incorporates the dynamic characteristics of the vehicle platform. This setup reproduces vibration disturbances that are difficult to model directly in the controller design. This allowed us to reveal the actual performance of the controller in suppressing unknown and complex disturbances introduced by the platform motion, and to conduct a more comprehensive and rigorous test of the controller’s robustness.

Remark 7

We acknowledge that the design premise of controller (26) is to be able to obtain the complete state of the system in real time, namely the generalized coordinates ϕ and their corresponding velocities , which is an idealized assumption. Design procedure.

Step 1: Adaptive robust control architecture based on the constraint following theory. Firstly, establish the analytical dynamic model of the general mechanical system. In the first stage, design the basic constraint following the controller to ensure that the system trajectory meets the ideal constraint conditions. For the transient response overshoot problem caused by the initial pose deviation, in the second stage, introduce the finite-time convergent compensator to achieve rapid error correction. In the third stage, to overcome the uncertainty of the system parameters and external disturbances, design a robust controller with adaptive law . Finally, the synthesized control law can be expressed as .

Step 2: Verify the effectiveness of this control strategy in a real physical environment. In multibody dynamics software, a dynamic model of the vehicle maintenance robotic manipulator is established with the road conditions and the vehicle itself. At the same time, nonlinear factors such as joint friction and transmission clearance are considered.

Step 3: The RecurDyn-Simulink collaborative simulation platform is constructed to achieve the closed-loop verification of the controller with the physical model. This framework consists of three core units: (i) The adaptive robust controller module implemented in Simulink, which calculates the joint control torques in real time, (ii) The multibody dynamics model solution in the RecurDyn environment, (iii) The bidirectional data interaction interface based on the RecurDyn/Controls module. During simulation, the RecurDyn solver outputs the actual joint angles and angular velocities of the manipulator to the controller, and the controller generates torque commands based on the target trajectory and feeds them back to the dynamics model, forming a closed-loop control loop.

(i) The reason for choosing PID control for comparison is as follows: The characteristic of the PID controller that it does not rely on an exact mathematical model is one of its most prominent features. Conclusion

This paper presents a novel position and attitude tracking control method. The proposed method combines the advantages of robust constrained following control and leaky adaptive law. The simulation results demonstrate that end-point attitude trajectory tracking and uncertainty compensation of the vehicle-mounted maintenance robotic manipulator are achieved under extreme vibration environments. To demonstrate the validity and performance of the proposed control method, mechanical-electrical Co-simulation is used to verify that the robotic manipulator can accurately follow the given constraints.

The core contribution and key innovation of this paper lie in the explicit integration of the leakage mechanism into the adaptive law. This is not merely an incremental improvement but a targeted solution, aiming to enhance the robustness and performance of the controller in two crucial aspects: preventing parameter drift and avoiding overly aggressive control inputs. Meanwhile, by innovatively applying this controller to complex mechanical systems operating in highly-vibrational environments, it significantly increases the value in practical applications. Although the proposed control scheme demonstrated good performance in the simulation, it also has certain limitations in practical applications. For instance, the online calculation of the core Moore-Penrose generalized inverse used in the UKE can be highly time-consuming for high-dimensional systems. Secondly, this method relies on the positive definiteness of the inertia matrix. Although these are standard assumptions in the field of robotics, in certain special coordinate selections or singular configurations, some of these assumptions may not hold, thereby limiting the direct application of this method.

Future work will focus on three primary avenues: (i) Consider the complex control issues of mechanical systems, such as joint friction, actuator saturation56, and backlash/dead zone57, but further expansion and design of the controller structure and adaptive law are required. We will focus on integrating anti-saturation compensation strategies, or developing new adaptive laws that can clearly adhere to these input constraints. (ii) The development of an experimental testbed for hardware validation and the extension of the method to more complex robotic manipulator control problems, for instance, there are issues such as the collaborative control of multiple robotic manipulators, high-degree-of-freedom robotic manipulator systems, and more challenging external interference environments58,59. At the same time, the challenges posed by issues such as sensor noise, computational delay, and actuator saturation in the actual application process should be considered. (iii) The system performance is significantly influenced by the control system parameters, in future work, optimization mechanisms or intelligent algorithms with stronger learning capabilities can be added on the existing basis to reduce the reliance on manual parameter adjustment60–63.

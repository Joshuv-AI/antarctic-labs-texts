Quantum geometry in the dynamics of band-projected operators
Chen Xu, Andreas Haller, Suraj Hegde, Tobias Meng, Thomas L. Schmidt

Abstract
We study the dynamics of electrons in crystalline solids in the presence of inhomogeneous external electric and magnetic fields. We present a manifestly gauge-invariant operator-based approach without relying on a semiclassical wavepacket construction, and derive the field-induced corrections to the equations of motion at the operator level. This includes the Berry curvature induced anomalous velocity and contributions arising from the quantum geometry of the Bloch bands. We show explicitly how these multi-band effects are manifested in an effective single band approximation. We present a formalism that allows for a systematic expansion to an arbitrary order in the inhomogeneity of the applied fields, as well as a way to compute the matrix elements in Bloch basis.

The geometry underlying the projective Hilbert space of quantum systems, broadly termed quantum geometry, has recently come to the limelight in various contexts. The Berry curvature may be viewed as the imaginary component of a quantity called the quantum geometric tensor, and its real part, known as the quantum metric or Fubini-Study metric

The canonical way of obtaining the semiclassical equations of motion, which take into account the Berry curvature, is based on constructing a wavepacket using a basis that self-consistently depends on the instantaneous position of the center of mass position
[

Furthermore, the use of instantaneous eigenstates seems to necessitate a solution the perturbed Hamiltonian, but in actual applications of the semiclassical equations of motion, the Berry curvature used is that of the free lattice Hamiltonian. In this Letter, we present an operator-based approach without reference to wavepackets, and demonstrate that such geometric contributions are fundamentally multi-band effects, which emerge when using an effective low-energy description of the electron dynamics within a restricted set of bands.

Model.
Throughout this work, we use natural units
\hbar=c=1
. We consider a fermion with charge
q
and mass
m
moving in a lattice potential under the influence of an applied static electromagnetic field. It is described by the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}^{2}}{2m}+q\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})+V(\hat{\bm{r}})=\hat{H}_{0}+U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}}).

(1)

Here,
\hat{\bm{\pi}}=\hat{\bm{p}}-q\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})
is the kinematic momentum, and
\hat{H}_{0}=\hat{\bm{p}}^{2}/(2m)+V(\hat{\bm{r}})
is the lattice Hamiltonian which by virtue of Bloch’s theorem
[
1
,
44
]
can be decomposed as
\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{n\bm{k}}\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
, using the Bloch eigenstates and eigenenergies,
\hat{H}_{0}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}=\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
. The external perturbations are given by

\displaystyle U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})=U_{\phi}(\hat{\bm{r}})+U_{\rm dia}(\hat{\bm{r}})+U_{\rm para}(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}}).

(2)

The terms correspond, respectively, to the electrostatic potential
U_{\phi}(\hat{\bm{r}})=q\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})
, the diamagnetic terms
U_{\rm dia}(\hat{\bm{r}})=q^{2}\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})^{2}/(2m
), and the paramagnetic contribution
U_{\rm para}(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})=-q(\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}+\hat{\bm{p}}\cdot\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}}))/(2m)
.
Since we are interested in time-independent electromagnetic fields, we choose a static gauge for simplicity. The inhomogeneous external fields can then be written in operator form as

\displaystyle\bm{B}(\hat{\bm{r}})=\partial_{\hat{\bm{r}}}\times\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}}),\quad\bm{E}(\hat{\bm{r}})=-\partial_{\hat{\bm{r}}}\phi(\hat{\bm{r}}).

(3)

Projected dynamics.
We assume that the applied electromagnetic field has a long wavelength compared to the lattice spacing, such that Bloch’s theorem remains applicable, and a weak field strength such that
U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})
is small compared to any band gaps. In such a case, the low-energy dynamics may be described by restricting to a subset of bands near the Fermi energy. For example, under weak electric fields, only the bands near the Fermi level of a metal need to be considered for studying transport properties. However, such a band restriction has nontrivial effects. Roughly speaking, band-projected operators, which represent the effective observables in a restricted Hilbert space
\mathcal{B}\subset\mathcal{H}
, will have different commutation relations compared to their unprojected counterparts. For example, the projected position operators
\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}}
(
j=x,y,z
) satisfy
[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{r}_{k\mathcal{B}}]=\mathrm{i}\hat{\Omega}_{jk}
, where
\hat{\Omega}_{jk}
is the operator form of Berry curvature (see Supplemental Material). As we shall demonstrate below, this is indeed the origin of quantum geometric contributions such as the anomalous velocity, which is related to the Berry curvature.

Dynamics of band-projected operators.
We perform the projection to low-energy bands via the projector associated with a subset
\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}
of bands,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{n\bm{k}}},\quad\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}=\mathbb{1}-\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}.

(4)

Note that the projection operator
\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
and its complement
\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}
are invariant under a band-dependent gauge transformation of the Bloch states
\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\rightarrow e^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{n}(\bm{k})}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
. We denote a band-projected operator as
\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{O}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
, and we consider the effective dynamics within the projected subspace as given by the band-projected temporal derivative
[
7
]

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{O}}_{\mathcal{B}}\equiv\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}(\mathrm{d}\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}/\mathrm{d}t)\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}},

(5)

where the total time derivative acts only on the operator
\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}
. Assuming no explicit time-dependence in
\hat{O}
, in the Heisenberg picture this is equivalent to,

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{O}}_{\mathcal{B}}=-\mathrm{i}[\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}].

(6)

Physically, this means that the projected dynamics of an operator in the projected subspace is governed by an effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}
. This may also be seen as a first-order effect in perturbation theory
[
45
,
7
]
. Note that both the observable and the Hamiltonian are projected, which is different from projecting only the commutator. To motivate this definition, consider the expectation value of the observable
\hat{O}
evaluated in a state within the projected Hilbert space,
O_{\mathcal{B}}(t)=\bra{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(t)\ket{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}
. The dynamics of the observable is then given by
\mathrm{i}\dot{O}_{B}(t)=\bra{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}[\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(t),\hat{H}]\ket{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}=\bra{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}[\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}(t),\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}]\ket{\psi_{\mathcal{B}}}
, in agreement with Eq. (
6
).

Applying this to the position and Bloch momentum operators we obtain the exact operator identities

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{B}}}{m}+\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\bm{r}}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})-U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{r}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}},

(7)

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{k}}}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=q\bm{E}_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})+\frac{q}{2m}\{[\partial_{\hat{\bm{r}}},A_{j}(\hat{\bm{r}})],\hat{\pi}_{j}\}_{\mathcal{B}}.

(8)

Here and below, an Einstein summation convention over repeated coordinate indices (in this case
j
) is used. Neither the canonical momentum
\bm{p}
nor the crystal momentum
\bm{k}
is gauge-invariant under a transformation of the electromagnetic potentials. In contrast, the kinematic momentum
\hat{\bm{\pi}}
is gauge-invariant, but even in the absence of external perturbation it is not conserved due to the crystal lattice potential. Hence, a more suitable operator for studying the dynamics is the gauge-invariant crystal momentum
\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\equiv\hat{\bm{k}}-q\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})
, which obeys the equation of motion

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{\kappa}}}=\frac{q}{2}\left(\hat{\bm{\pi}}\times\bm{B}-\bm{B}\times\hat{\bm{\pi}}+2\bm{E}\right).

(9)

In addition to the electric field term, it explicitly contains a Lorentz force, in analogy to the Lorentz force term appearing in
{\rm d}{\hat{\bm{\pi}}}/{\rm dt}
in the absence of a lattice potential. When projected, this force equation holds approximately to first order in external fields strengths.

Equation

7
shows that there are generically additional contributions to the band-projected velocity when the perturbation is present. They are given by

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\perp}

\displaystyle\equiv\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\bm{r}}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})-U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{r}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(10)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}}-\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}},U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})\right]_{\mathcal{B}}.

(11)

The corresponding term in the time derivative of the crystal momentum vanishes because
[\hat{\bm{k}},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]=0
.
Since it contains
\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}
,
Eq.

10
shows explicitly that the additional contribution is due to virtual scattering processes between the subspace of low-energy bands
\mathcal{B}
and its complement.
It is therefore appropriate to call it an inter-band velocity and, as we demonstrate below, the terms that capture multi-band effects are exactly the geometric
corrections to the electron dynamics, which include in particular the Berry curvature related anomalous velocity.
We would like to emphasize that the band projection is crucial to find anomalous corrections: if
\mathcal{B}=\mathcal{H}
, the complement projector
\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}
vanishes, leading to
\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\perp}=0
.
Furthermore, the equivalent expression (
11
) demonstrates that the inter-band velocity quantifies the error of the crudest semiclassical approximation
\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\mathcal{B}}\approx\hat{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{B}}/m
.

When projected to the subspace
\mathcal{B}
, many of the usual commutation relations are significantly modified. So before discussing in detail the effect of
U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})
, let us briefly comment on the position, canonical momentum
\hat{\bm{p}}
, kinetic momentum
\hat{\bm{\pi}}
and gauge-invariant crystal momentum
\hat{\bm{\kappa}}
operators.

The position operator can be decomposed as
\hat{r}_{j}=\mathrm{i}\smash{\partial_{\hat{k}_{j}}}+\smash{{\hat{\mathrm{A}}_{j}}}
where
\smash{{\hat{\mathrm{A}}_{j}}}
is the Berry connection in operator form, which has the matrix elements
\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}\smash{{\hat{\mathrm{A}}_{j}}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}=\mathrm{i}\bra{u_{m\bm{k}}}\partial_{k_{j}}\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}}
. Although this expression was already obtained in Ref.
[
6
]
, we shall provide a derivation using a moment generating operator formalism in Sec.
II
of the Supplemental Material. In this expression, the operator-valued derivative
\smash{\mathrm{i}\partial_{\hat{k}_{j}}}=\sum_{n\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\mathrm{i}\partial_{k_{j}}\bra{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
can be understood as the lattice position operator, while the Berry connection operator is the “intra-cell” position within the primitive cell
[
46
]
. It can be shown that
[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{r}_{j}]=0
, whilst the commutator of the projected position operators no longer vanishes but produces the operator form of the Berry curvature. Introducing the covariant derivative as
\hat{\nabla}_{j}=-\mathrm{i}\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}}=\smash{(\partial_{\hat{k}_{j}}-\mathrm{i}\hat{A}_{j})_{\mathcal{B}}}
, it is easy to derive that for an arbitrary operator with matrix elements
O_{nm}(\bm{k})
(see Sec.
II
in the Supplemental Material)

\displaystyle[\hat{\nabla}_{j},\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\partial_{k_{j}}O_{nm}(\bm{k})-i[{\mathrm{A}_{j}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{k}),O_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{k})]_{nm}\right),

(12)

where the band summations are restricted to the projected bands. When applied to the position operator itself, one recovers the usual non-Abelian Berry curvature as matrix elements. Hence we have the operator identity
[\hat{\nabla}_{j},\hat{\nabla}_{k}]=-\mathrm{i}\hat{\Omega}_{jk}
.

When studying semiclassical equations of motion usually only the term involving the group velocity of modes is retained
[
44
]
, which is valid if the inter-band contributions can be neglected and therefore in particular in single-band approximations. In fact, if we define the velocity operator as
\hat{\bm{v}}\equiv\mathrm{i}[\hat{H}_{0},\hat{r}]
, then it can be shown that in the Bloch basis
[
6
,
47
]

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{v}}=\sum_{nm\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}\left[\delta_{nm}\partial_{\bm{k}}\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}+\mathrm{i}\Delta_{nm}(\bm{k}){\bm{\mathrm{A}}}_{nm}(\bm{k})\right],

(13)

where
\Delta_{nm}(\bm{k})=\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}-\mathcal{E}_{m\bm{k}}
is the direct band gap at crystal momentum
\bm{k}
.
The Berry connection is defined as
{\mathrm{A}_{nm,i}}(\bm{k})=\mathrm{i}\braket{u_{n\bm{k}}|\partial_{k_{i}}u_{m\bm{k}}}
, with the cell-periodic part of the Bloch functions given by
\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}=\exp(\ri\bm k\cdot\R)\ket{u_{n\bm{k}}}
. From
Eq.

13
, it is clear that neglecting inter-band contributions corresponds to retaining only the group velocity
\partial_{\bm{k}}\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}
. With the Bloch Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
, the canonical momentum is related to the velocity operator as
\hat{\bm{p}}=m\hat{\bm{v}}
. Note this relation between the canonical momentum
\hat{\bm{p}}
and the velocity operator
\hat{\bm{v}}
is valid only for the specific Bloch Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
we used because the kinetic term is quadratic. If we considered, say, a relativistic Dirac Hamiltonian in a periodic potential the expression of the velocity operator would remain valid but it can no longer be used for the canonical momentum operator. We shall give another expression of the canonical momentum below.

The position and crystal momentum operators are canonically conjugate in the sense that
[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{\bm{p}}]=[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{\bm{k}}]=\mathrm{i}
. Note that while
\hat{\bm{k}}
commutes with
V(\hat{\bm{r}})
because the latter is invariant under lattice translation,
\hat{\bm{p}}
does not
[
46
]
. Upon projection,
\hat{\bm{r}}
and
\smash{\hat{\bm{k}}}
remain a conjugate pair in the restricted band subspace,
[\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}},\smash{\hat{\bm{k}}_{\mathcal{B}}}]=\mathrm{i}\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{B}}
, but
\hat{\bm{r}}
and
\hat{\bm{p}}
lose this property. In fact, in analogy to the decomposition of the position operator into an inter-cell and an intra-cell part, we can express the momentum operator as
\hat{\bm{p}}=\hat{\bm{k}}-\bm{\mathcal{\hat{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}}
, where the crystal momentum is the generator of inter-cell translation while the object
\bm{\mathcal{\hat{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}}
, that we will call real-space Berry connection generates intra-cell translations. The operator
\bm{\mathcal{\hat{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}}
has the following matrix elements in the Bloch basis

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})\equiv\ i\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{V_{uc}}\int_{\rm cell}d\bm{r}\ u^{*}_{n\bm{q}}(\bm{r})\ \partial_{\bm{r}}u_{m\bm{q}}(\bm{r}),

(14)

or by an abuse of notation,
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})=i\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}\partial_{\bm{r}}\ket{u_{m\bm{q}}}
, thus justifying the name. With this we have

\displaystyle[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{p}_{j\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=\ \mathrm{i}\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{B}}-\mathrm{i}[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}].

(15)

where the second term spoils the canonical commutation relation, with the correction given by the real space Berry connection.
We would like to point out that
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})
is not the same real-space Berry connection as in Refs.
[
13
,
48
,
42
]
, which is related to a position-dependent basis in a self-consistent wave-packet construction. We see from the matrix elements that
\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})
characterizes the spatial modulation of the Bloch states, and vanishes in the plane-wave limit. This is consistent with the fact that the crystal momentum reduces to the canonical momentum when the lattice potential is a constant, and the system describes a free particle whose Bloch states are just plane waves. We note that only the band diagonal part of the real-space Berry connection is gauge-invariant under a Bloch gauge transformation.

Inhomogeneous electric field and geometry.

We now show how the inter-band velocity is related to quantum geometric quantities by expressing the contributions in terms of the projected quantities.
First, we consider the case where the external perturbation is due to an electrostatic potential only.
In this case,
U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})=U_{\phi}(\hat{\bm{r}})
, and we find the formally exact operator relation

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},q\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]=-\frac{q}{2}\{\hat{\Omega}_{jk},E_{k\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}+\hat{R}_{j\perp}^{\phi},

(16)

where the first term is exactly the operator analog of the anomalous velocity and the second term
\smash{\hat{R}_{i\perp}^{\phi}}
represents further corrections due to the spatial dependence of the electric field. We took into account that in general
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\neq\phi(\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}})
except for a uniform electric field. In fact, even if one makes the approximation
\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\approx\phi(\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}})
and symmetrizes the position operators to maintain hermiticity, there would still be additional contributions besides the anomalous velocity.

In order to understand this result and simplify the general formula for
\smash{\hat{r}_{j\perp}^{\phi}}
, we calculate it order by order in a gradient expansion of the external fields.
Up to the second order in the inhomogeneity, we have

\smash{\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})=-E^{(0)}_{k}\hat{r}_{k}-E^{(1)}_{kj}\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{j}/2}
, where the coefficients are symmetric in all indices and the corresponding electric field is

\smash{E_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})=E^{(0)}_{k}+E^{(1)}_{kj}\hat{r}_{j}}
.
As we show in the Supplemental Material, besides the Berry curvature term there is an additional contribution to the inter-band velocity,

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{j\perp}^{\phi}=-\frac{q}{2}E_{kl}^{(1)}[\hat{\nabla}_{j},\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}_{kl}].

(17)

This result corresponds exactly to the operator form of the equations of motion obtained by an electron wave-packet approach in Refs.
[
39
,
41
]
, here generalized to the multi-band case. We defined a quantum metric operator by
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}=\frac{1}{2}(\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{jk}^{(2)}+\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{kj}^{(2)})
, where
\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{jk}^{(2)}
is the quantum geometric tensor operator, defined in Table
1
. We see from the expression that this geometric contribution captures the virtual scattering processes to the complement Hilbert space.

To justify our definition of operators corresponding to geometric quantities, we shall compute their matrix elements and show that they agree with the usual definitions. Here we outline a systematic way of doing such calculations, explained in more detail in the Supplemental Material. Any algebraic function of the position operator can be expressed by using the moment generating operator as

\displaystyle f(\hat{\bm{r}})=\lim_{\bm{a}\rightarrow\bm{0}}f(\mathrm{i}\partial_{\bm{a}})\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}\,,

(18)

and in the Supplemental Material we show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\bm{a}\cdot\partial_{\bm{q}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}},

(19)

where we introduced the local projection operators

\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}=\sum_{n}\ket{u_{n\bm{q}}}\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}.

Let
\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
and
\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
denote the band projections of
\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}
onto
\mathcal{B}
and its complement, respectively. We then obtain a general result

\displaystyle\left([\hat{r}_{j},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{j}_{n}},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\left(\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{j}_{n}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(20)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}^{n+1}\sum_{\bm{q}}e^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\partial_{q_{j}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\partial_{q_{\bm{j}_{n}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}e^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\bigg),

where we used the short-hand notation
\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{j}_{n}}=\hat{r}_{j_{1}}\hat{r}_{j_{2}}...\hat{r}_{j_{n}}
, and
\partial_{q_{\bm{j}_{n}}}=\partial_{q_{j_{1}}}\dots\partial_{q_{j_{n}}}
. In particular, for
n=1
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{jk}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}^{2}\left(\left[\hat{r}_{j},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\left[\hat{r}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{jk,nm}(\bm{q})\ket{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{q}}},

(21)

where the matrix elements read, explicitly

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{jk,nm}(\bm{k})=\bra{u_{n\bm{k}}}(\partial_{k_{j}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}})(\partial_{k_{k}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}})\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}},

(22)

with
k
-derivatives acting only on the local projection operator next to them.
This is the multi-band version of the quantum geometric tensor
[

]
.

Magnetic field.
After having demonstrated that our method reproduces and generalizes previous results for electric fields, we will apply the formalism now to the hitherto unknown case of an inhomogeneous magnetic field. Compared to the electric field case, a complication arises because the projected canonical momentum and position operators no longer satisfy a canonical commutation relation. The inter-band velocity operator reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}^{\bm{A}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{q}{2m}\bigg(-\Big\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{k\mathcal{B}}],{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})\Big\}

(23)

\displaystyle+\Big\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})],\hat{\pi}_{k\mathcal{B}}\Big\}

(24)

\displaystyle+\Big[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left({A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}+\hat{p}_{k}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}\Big]\bigg)

Semiclassical equations of motion.
So far, we have provided results at the level of operators. Next, we apply these to the semiclassical dynamics of a wavepacket constructed from states in the projected set of bands but otherwise of arbitrary shape:
\ket{W}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\int_{\rm BZ}d{\bm{q}}c_{n\bm{q}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}

\Braket{W|W}=\sum_{n}\int d{\bm{q}}\lvert c_{n\bm{q}}\rvert^{2}=1
and we assume the state evolves with the effective Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}

It is easy to show that the time derivative of an observable

in the Heisenberg picture can be equivalently given in the Schrodinger picture as

[

]
.

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{R}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\bra{W(t)}[\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}]\ket{W(t)},

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{K}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\bra{W(t)}[\hat{\bm{\kappa}}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}]\ket{W(t)},

(28)

where the evolved state is parametrized as

\displaystyle\ket{W(t)}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\int_{\rm BZ}d\bm{q}\ c_{n\bm{q}}(t)\ket{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}.

(29)

Now we consider the motion of the wave-packet under the influence of a magnetic field. Expanding the magnetic vector potential as
\smash{A_{j}(\hat{\bm{r}})=A^{(1)}_{jk}\hat{r}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}_{jkl}\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{l}}

, each term corresponding to the respective term of the expansion of

for a given

\displaystyle\dot{r}^{(1)}_{l\perp}=\mathrm{i}A_{jk}^{(1)}\frac{q}{m}

(30)

\displaystyle\times\Big\langle\!\!\Big\langle R_{k}(\bm{q},t)\partialderivative{{ {\mathcal A}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}(\bm{q})}{q_l}(\bm{q})+{\Omega}_{lk}(\bm{q}){p}_{j}(\bm{q})-\partialderivative{{\mathcal L}_{jk}(\bm{q})}{q_l}\Big\rangle\!\!\Big\rangle,

is the center of mass position of the wave-packet at a given time, which evaluates to be

\displaystyle\bra{W}\hat{r}_{j}(t)\ket{W}

\displaystyle=\langle\!\langle R_{j}(\bm{q},t)\rangle\!\rangle

(31)

\displaystyle=\int d\bm{q}R_{j}(\bm{q},t)\lvert c_{\bm{q}}(t)\rvert^{2},

(32)

where

\smash{R_{j}(\bm{q},t)\equiv\mathrm{i}\partial_{q_{j}}\theta(\bm{q},t)+\mathcal{A}_{j}(\bm{q})},

and

Sharp momentum approximation.
Here we discuss how the current due to geometric contributions may be measured in a metallic system. Within the Boltzmann transport theory, a net current may exist if there is either a nonequilibrium distribution
f(\bm{K})
deforming the Fermi surface, or a momentum-dependent velocity term
\dot{\bm{R}}(\bm{K})
given by single particle dynamics beyond the group velocity, such as the Berry curvature term such as the second term in
Eq.

30
contributing to the intrinsic anomalous Hall effect
[
8
]
or more generally the geometric quantities we discussed above. To isolate the effects of the latter we assume an equilibrium distribution. Suppose the Fermi surface intersects only a single nondegenerate band
n
with Bloch functions
\ket{\psi_{\bm{q}}}
. At zero temperature we take
f(\bm{K},t)\approx\Theta[\epsilon_{FS}-\epsilon(\bm{K})]
, where
\epsilon_{FS}
denotes the Fermi energy. Within the semiclassical regime, the wave-packet may be taken to be sharply peaked at a single momentum so that we take
\lvert c_{\bm{q}}\rvert^{2}\approx\delta(\bm{q}-\bm{K})
, where
\bm{K}=\bra{W}\hat{\bm{\kappa}}\ket{W}
is the mean gauge-invariant crystal momentum of the wave-packet at time
t
. Then, we have the current density

\displaystyle\bm{J}=\int\frac{d\bm{K}}{(2\pi)^{3}}\dot{\bm{R}}(\bm{K})f(\bm{K},t),

(33)

where
\dot{\bm{R}}(\bm{K})
is the center of mass motion of a wave-packet with mean momentum
\bm{K}
. Here we will assume the system is inversion symmetric, so
\epsilon(\bm{k})=\epsilon(-\bm{k})
. Then, we find a contribution to the current due to the mixed geometric tensor, which is given by

\displaystyle J_{l}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}A_{jk}^{(1)}\frac{q}{m}\oint_{\rm FS}\frac{dS}{|\nabla_{\bm{K}}\epsilon(\bm{K})|}\partialderivative{\epsilon({\bm K})}{K_l}\mathcal{L}_{jk}(\bm{K}),

(34)

where the integration is over the Fermi surface. Hence, we see this is a contribution entirely from the Fermi surface, and it is determined by the mixed geometric tensor
\mathcal{L}_{jk}
.

Summary and Conclusion.
In this work, we have studied the equations of motion for particles in Bloch bands under the influence of spatially inhomogeneous electromagnetic fields.
For this purpose, we introduced an approach based on band-projected operators and the Heisenberg equation of motion, and explicitly separated anomalous contributions from the group velocity operator.
By explicitly evaluating the operator matrix elements, we demonstrated that the anomalous contributions are related to the geometry of the underlying Bloch states.
In the case of electric fields, we find results consistent with the existing literature and generalize them to the multi-band case.
Furthermore, we find that the leading order of the anomalous velocity is a product of Berry curvature and the band-projected electric field, thereby generalizing known results to a higher order in the spatial inhomogeneity of the fields.
Lastly, we address the case of spatially inhomogeneous magnetic fields and find geometric corrections to the anomalous velocity in the leading order.
We show that these leading corrections stem from the paramagnetic term and are tied to mixed geometric operators containing spatial derivatives of Bloch projectors.

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\left[{\hat{r}_{i,\mathcal{B}}},f_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]

\displaystyle=\hat{\Omega}_{ij}\left(\left[\partial_{\hat{r}_{j}},f(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}+\hat{R}_{i,f,\mathcal{B}}

(S26)

with a function-dependent remainder

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{i,f,\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{\bm{\lambda}\rightarrow\bm{0}}\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{n!}f^{(n)}_{\bm{i}_{n}}\partial_{\lambda_{\bm{i}_{n}}}\left(\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\left[\hat{M}^{1-s}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\lambda}),\hat{\Omega}_{ij}\right]\lambda_{j}\hat{M}^{s}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\lambda})+\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}\lambda_{\bm{j}_{k}}\int\limits_{0}^{1}\mathrm{d}s\hat{M}^{1-s}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\lambda})\left[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{(k)}_{\bm{j}_{k}}\right]\hat{M}^{s}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\lambda})\right),

(S27)

where we again use the sum convention for the multi-indices
\bm{i}_{n}
,
\bm{j}_{n}
as well as the index
j
.

II
Matrix elements of the geometric operators

II.1
Local projection operators and moment generating function

To make contact with the conventional expressions for the geometric quantities, we expand the geometric operators in the Bloch basis.
We introduce the local projection operators

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n}\ket{u_{n\bm{q}}}\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}},\quad\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\ket{u_{n\bm{q}}}\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}},\quad\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{n\notin\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\ket{u_{n\bm{q}}}\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}=\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}-\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}

(S28)

where
\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
and
\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
are idempotent and orthogonal, i.e.
\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
,
\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}^{2}=\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}
,
\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}=\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}=0
.
We have also that

\displaystyle\mathbb{1}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}},\quad\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}.

(S29)

The local expressions of general projected and geometric quantities can be computed by using

\displaystyle O(\hat{\bm{r}})_{\mathcal{B}}=\lim_{\bm{a}\rightarrow\bm{0}}O(\mathrm{i}\partial_{\bm{a}})\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}\,\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}},

(S30)

and the identities

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}\,\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\,\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\bm{a}\cdot\partial_{\bm{q}}}\,\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}},

(S31)

\displaystyle\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\,\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\,\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\bm{a}\cdot\partial_{\bm{q}}}\,\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\,\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}},

(S32)

where the exponential of derivatives acts on everything to the right.

The proof of the above formulas can be done in the position basis and by using the Poisson summation formula. For the matrix element in Bloch basis we have

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}\ket{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}re^{\mathrm{i}\left(\bm{k}-\left(\bm{a}+\bm{q}\right)\right)\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}}

\displaystyle=\braket{u_{n\bm{q}}|u_{m\bm{k}}}\delta(\bm{a}+\bm{q}-\bm{k})\equiv\braket{u_{n\bm{q}}|u_{m\bm{k}}}\mathrm{e}^{\bm{a}\cdot\partial_{\bm{q}}}\delta(\bm{q}-\bm{k}),

(S33)

where in the last step we used the convention that the inner product between the cell-periodic part of the Bloch states are given as an integration over cells. Note that, compared to the convention in
Eq.

S20
, we choose
\bm{a}=\mathrm{i}\bm{\lambda}
here, which brings forward the role of the auxiliary field
\bm{a}
as a shift of the crystal momentum
\bm{q}
in the Bloch basis. In performing the Poisson summation we also used that the Bloch momenta are restricted to the first Brillouin zone.
Using the matrix element, it is then straightforward to show

\displaystyle\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}=\sum_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{\bm{a}\cdot\partial_{\bm{q}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{q}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}.

(S34)

By applying band projection operators accordingly we immediately obtain
Equations

S31
and
S32
.

II.2
Position and velocity operator

We now show explicitly that the position operator can be decomposed as

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i}=i\partial_{\hat{k}_{i}}+{\hat{\mathrm{A}}_{i}},

(S35)

where

\displaystyle\quad\partial_{\hat{k}_{i}}=\sum_{n}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\partial_{k_{i}}\bra{\psi_{n\bm{k}}},\quad{\hat{\mathrm{A}}_{i}}=\sum_{nm}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}{\mathrm{A}_{nm,i}}(\bm{k})\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}.

(S36)

Using
Eq.

S34
we have, (derivatives act on everything to the right)

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\bm{a}\rightarrow\bm{0}}\mathrm{i}\partial_{a_{i}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{a}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}=\mathrm{i}\sum_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}}\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}=\mathrm{i}\sum_{nm}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{u_{n\bm{k}}}\partial_{k_{i}}\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}

(S37)

\displaystyle=\sum_{nm}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\left(\delta_{nm}\partial_{k_{i}}+A_{nm,i}(\bm{k})\right)\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}},

(S38)

where
\partial_{k_{i}}
in
Eq.

S37
acts on
\hat{P}_{\bm{k}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}=\sum_{m}\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}
, and
\bm{A}_{nm}(\bm{k})=\mathrm{i}\bra{u_{n\bm{k}}}\partial_{\bm{k}}\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}}
is the non-abelian Berry connection. It can be shown that when un-projected
\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{A}_{j}-\partial_{k_{j}}\hat{A}_{i}=[\hat{A}_{i},\hat{A}_{j}]
, which is consistent with the fact that the position operators commute,
[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{r}_{j}]=0
. This motivates us to denote the projected position operator as a covariant derivative
\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{i}\hat{\nabla}_{i}
.

A general Bloch diagonal operator is of the form
\hat{O}=\sum_{n}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}O_{nm}(\bm{k})\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}
, and we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{O}]=\sum_{nm}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\left(\partial_{k_{i}}O_{nm}(\bm{k})-i[{\mathrm{A}_{i}}(\bm{k}),O(\bm{k})]_{nm}\right)\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}},

(S39)

where the derivative acts on
O_{nm}(\bm{k})
only, and
[{\mathrm{A}_{i}}(\bm{k}),O(\bm{k})]_{nm}=\sum_{l}{\mathrm{A}_{i,nl}}(\bm{k})O_{lm}(\bm{k})-O_{nl}(\bm{k}){\mathrm{A}_{i,lm}}(\bm{k})
. Note that the second term vanishes for an operator whose matrix elements are band-independent.
Similarly, for band-projected Bloch diagonal operators, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{O}_{\mathcal{B}}]=\sum_{n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\left(\partial_{k_{i}}O_{nm}(\bm{k})-i[{\mathrm{A}_{i}}_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{k}),O_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{k})]_{nm}\right)\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}

(S40)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}\sum_{\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}[\nabla_{i},O_{\mathcal{B}}(\bm{k})]_{nm}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}},

(S41)

where the band summation is now restricted to the projected bands. In particular, we have

\displaystyle[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{k}_{j}]=\mathrm{i}[\partial_{\hat{k}_{i}},\hat{k}_{j}]=\mathrm{i}\delta_{ij}

(S42)

With these expressions, we can also evaluate the velocity operator to be

\displaystyle\hat{\bm{v}}=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{H}_{0}]=\sum_{nm\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}\left(\delta_{nm}\partial_{\bm{k}}\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}+\mathrm{i}\Delta_{nm}(\bm{k}){\bm{\mathrm{A}}}_{nm}(\bm{k})\right),

(S43)

which gives
Eq.

13
in the main text. Note with the specific crystal Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}=(\hat{\bm{p}}^{2}/2m)+V(\hat{\bm{r}})
, the velocity operator is simply
\hat{\bm{v}}=\frac{\hat{\bm{p}}}{m}
, which for instance, for the Pauli Hamiltonian with a spin orbit coupling or a Dirac electron moving in periodic potential, this no longer holds
[
6
]
.

II.3
Geometric quantities

We would now compute the matrix elements of the operators associated with the geometric quantities, and show that they are consistent with the conventional ones seen in literature. We do this by using
Eq.

S34
, which by applying derivatives gives

\displaystyle\left([\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{i}_{n}},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{i}_{n}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S44)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}^{n+1}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\partial_{k_{i_{1}}}\dots\partial_{k_{i_{n}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\bigg),

(S45)

where as in the previous section
\hat{r}^{(n)}_{\bm{i}_{n}}=\hat{r}_{i_{1}}\hat{r}_{i_{2}}...\hat{r}_{i_{n}}
.

II.3.1
Quantum geometric tensor

For
n=1
we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}^{2}\left(\left[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\left[\hat{r}_{j},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S46)

\displaystyle=\sum_{n,m\in\mathcal{N}_{\mathcal{B}}}{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij,nm}(\bm{k})\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}},

(S47)

where the matrix element reads explicitly

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij,nm}(\bm{k})=\bra{u_{n\bm{k}}}\bigg(\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\bigg(\partial_{k_{j}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}},

(S48)

with
k
-derivatives that act only on the closest local projection operators.
This is the multi-band version of quantum geometric tensor
[
41
]
. We note that the quantum geometric tensor is also the projected cumulant
\hat{Q}_{ij}^{(2)}=\hat{P_{\mathcal{B}}}(\hat{r}_{i}-\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}})(\hat{r}_{j}-\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}})\hat{P_{\mathcal{B}}}
.
Eq.

S48
is exactly the usual from of quantum geometric tensor
[
29
]
, whose real and imaginary parts correspond to the quantum metric and Berry curvature tensor respectively:
{\mathcal{Q}}_{ij,nm}(\bm{k})={\mathcal{G}}_{ij,nm}(\bm{k})-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\Omega_{ij,nm}(\bm{k})
.

II.3.2
Christoffel symbols and symplectic connection

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

(S49)

\displaystyle=i^{3}\sum_{\bm{k}}e^{i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg(\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\bigg[\bigg(\partial_{k_{j}}\partial_{k_{k}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}+\bigg(\partial_{k_{j}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\bigg(\partial_{k_{k}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\bigg)+\bigg(\partial_{k_{k}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\bigg)\bigg(\partial_{k_{j}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}e^{-i\bm{k}\cdot\hat{\bm{r}}}\bigg)\bigg]

(S50)

\displaystyle=\hat{Q}^{(3)}_{ijk}+\hat{Q}^{(2)}_{ij}\hat{r}_{k\mathcal{B}}+\hat{Q}^{(2)}_{ik}\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},

(S51)

where we introduced the geometric quantity
\hat{Q}^{(3)}_{ijk}=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{B}}\sum_{\bm{k}}{Q}^{(3)}_{ijk,nm}(\bm{k})\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}
, whose matrix elements are
[
41
]

{Q}^{(3)}_{ijk,nm}(\bm{k})=i\bra{u_{n\bm{k}}}\left(\partial_{k_{i}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\right)\left(\partial_{k_{j}}\partial_{k_{k}}\hat{P}_{\bm{k}\mathcal{B}}\right)\ket{u_{m\bm{k}}}
. Its relation to quantum Christoffel symbols are given by eq.(
S16
).

III
Canonical momentum

III.1
Canonical momentum and and real space Berry connections

The canonical momentum is defined as the operator such that

\displaystyle\bra{\bm{r}}\hat{\bm{p}}\ket{\psi}=-i\partial_{\bm{r}}\psi(\bm{r}).

(S52)

In Bloch basis we may decompose it to be
[
6
]

\hat{\bm{p}}=\hat{\bm{k}}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{\bm{r}},

with matrix elements

\displaystyle\bra{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}\hat{\bm{p}}\ket{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}=\left(\bm{k}\delta_{nm}-\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})\right)\delta(\bm{q-k}),

(S53)

where the second term is a real space Berry connection

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\bm{r}}_{nm}(\bm{q})=i\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}\partial_{\bm{r}}\ket{u_{m\bm{q}}}\equiv i\frac{(2\pi)^{d}}{V_{uc}}\int_{cell}d\bm{r}u^{*}_{n\bm{q}}(\bm{r})\partial_{\bm{r}}u_{m\bm{q}}(\bm{r}).

(S54)

It may be seen from transforming to Wannier basis that the Bloch momentum operator generates inter-cell translations, while the real space Berry connection generates intra-cell displacements. We would like to point out that this real space Berry connection is different from the Berry connection that results from position dependent Bloch states in a spatially varying band structure.

III.2
Mixed geometrical quantities

The coupling of the canonical momentum and vector potential in the presence of magnetic field induces contributions that are expressed in terms of new geometric quantities, which depends on the spatial gradient of the cell-periodic part of Bloch states as well.

III.2.1
Mixed quantum geometric tensor

First we introduce a mixed quantum geometric tensor defined as

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}\equiv-\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}][\hat{p}_{j},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=((\hat{r}_{i}-\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}})\hat{p}_{j})_{\mathcal{B}}.

(S55)

We can see from the definition that
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}
is odd under time reversal transformation and even under inversion. For any Bloch diagonal operator
\hat{O}
we may define a local operator

\displaystyle\hat{O}_{\bm{q}}\equiv e^{-i\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{O}e^{i\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}},

(S56)

whose matrix elements of the local operator evaluated on cell-periodic Bloch functions are the matrix elements of
\hat{O}
in Bloch states, namely

\displaystyle\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}\hat{O}_{\bm{q}}\ket{u_{m\bm{q}}}={O}_{nm}(\bm{q})=\bra{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}\hat{O}\ket{\psi_{m\bm{q}}}.

(S57)

Now we compute

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\lim_{\bm{a}\rightarrow 0}\mathrm{i}\partial_{a_{i}}\hat{P}_{N}e^{-i\bm{a\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{Q}_{N}\hat{p}_{j}\hat{P}_{N}=\mathrm{i}e^{i\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\left(\partial_{q_{i}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\right)\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\hat{A}^{\bm{r}}_{j\bm{q}}\hat{P}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}e^{-i\bm{q\cdot\hat{r}}},

(S58)

where in the last step we used that
\hat{k}
commutes with projection operator. That is we have

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij,nm}(\bm{q})\delta(\bm{q}-\bm{k})=\bra{\psi_{n\bm{q}}}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}\ket{\psi_{m\bm{k}}}=\mathrm{i}\bra{u_{n\bm{q}}}\left(\partial_{q_{i}}\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\right)\hat{Q}_{\bm{q}\mathcal{B}}\hat{A}^{\bm{r}}_{j\bm{q}}\ket{u_{m\bm{q}}}\delta(\bm{q}-\bm{k}).

(S59)

This is a new geometric quantity that is local in momentum space and depends on the spatial gradient of the Bloch states, due to
\hat{A}^{\bm{r}}_{j\bm{q}}
, in addition to momentum gradient.

III.2.2
Modified canonical commutation relation

The commutation relation between the projected position and canonical momentum becomes

\displaystyle[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{p}_{j\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\mathbb{1}_{\mathcal{B}}\delta_{ij}-\mathrm{i}[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}].

(S60)

This shows that the projected position and momentum no longer form a canonical pair. But note that we also have

\displaystyle[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{p}_{j\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{p}_{j}]_{\mathcal{B}}-\bigg(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{Q}_{N}\hat{p}_{j}-\hat{p}_{j}\hat{Q}_{N}\hat{r}_{i}\bigg)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S61)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\delta_{ij}-\bigg(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}^{\dagger}\bigg),

(S62)

from which we have that

\displaystyle[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}]=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\bigg(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}-\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ij}^{\dagger}\bigg),

(S63)

which is manifestly hermitian.

III.2.3
Higher mixed geometric quantities

Consider now the quantity
\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}
. We have that

\displaystyle\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}}\left(\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}+\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}\equiv\mathrm{i}\hat{\nabla}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{jk}+\hat{\Lambda}_{ijk},

(S64)

where
\hat{\Lambda}_{ijk}\equiv\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\left([\hat{r}_{i},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}
, and we used
\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{i}\hat{\nabla}_{i}
. Note that by symmetry we have

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}_{ijk}-\hat{\Lambda}_{jik}=-\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\nabla}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{jk}-\hat{\nabla}_{j}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ik}\right).

(S65)

In fact we have

\displaystyle\hat{\Lambda}_{ijk}

\displaystyle=\left([\hat{r}_{i},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S66)

\displaystyle=\left([[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}],\hat{r}_{j}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}+\left([\hat{r}_{i},\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S67)

\displaystyle=\left([\hat{r}_{j},[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}-\left(\hat{r}_{j}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S68)

\displaystyle=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ijk}^{(2)}+i\hat{\nabla}_{j}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ik},

(S69)

where we introduced a geometric quantity
\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ijk}^{(2)}\equiv\left([\hat{r}_{j},[\hat{r}_{i},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]]\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{p}_{k},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)_{\mathcal{B}}
, which is symmetric in
ij
and local in momentum space. Substituting this back in
Eq.

S64
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ijk}^{(2)}+i\hat{\nabla}_{i}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{jk}+i\hat{\nabla}_{j}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{ik},

(S70)

which is manifestly
ij
symmetric as it should.

IV
Equations of motion

In this section, we derive the operator dynamics presented in
Eqs.

7
and
8
in the Heisenberg picture.
In the below assume throughout that the operators do not carry explicit time-dependence, in particular
\partial\hat{O}/\partial t=0
.

IV.1
Generality

IV.1.1
Velocity

The velocity is obtained by direct evaluation of the projected dynamics of the band-projected position operator,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\left(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}}\right)\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{H}\right]_{\mathcal{B}}+\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\bm{r}}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{H}-\hat{H}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{r}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}.

(S71)

Note that we have
\hat{H}=\hat{H}_{0}+U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})
, in which the Bloch Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}=\sum_{n\bm{k}}\mathcal{E}_{n\bm{k}}\ket{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}\bra{\psi_{n\bm{k}}}
is a band-diagonal operator, therefore
[\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{0}]=0
, such that the last bracket in
Eq.

S71
equals the inter-band velocity
\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\perp}=\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\bm{r}}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})-U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{r}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}
.
The remaining term is the intra-band velocity

\displaystyle-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{H}\right]_{\mathcal{B}}=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}},\hat{\bm{\pi}}^{2}/(2m)\right]_{\mathcal{B}}-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}},q\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})+V(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]_{\mathcal{B}}=-\mathrm{i}\frac{1}{2m}\left\{\left[\hat{\bm{r}},{\hat{p}_{i}}-qA_{i}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right],\hat{\pi}_{i}\right\}_{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{B}}}{m}

(S72)

which concludes the derivation of

Eq.

7
. In the below, we will use gradient expansion to discuss the inter-band velocity terms that give rise to corrections to the equation of motion.

IV.1.2
Bloch momentum dynamics

Note any band-diagonal operator commutes with the projection operator, hence, in particular,
\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{k}}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{k}}=\hat{\bm{k}}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Therefore, the projected dynamics of the projected crystal momentum is equivalent to the projected dynamics of the crystal momentum, i.e.

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\left(\dot{\hat{k}}_{i\mathcal{B}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=[\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{k}_{i}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{H}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}]=\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{k}_{i},\hat{H}]\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}},

(S73)

where
\hat{H}=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}^{2}}{2m}+q\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})+V(\hat{\bm{r}})
.
Using
[\hat{k}_{i},f(\hat{\bm{r}})]=-i\left[\partial_{\hat{r}_{i}},f(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]
we have, in Coulomb gauge,

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{k}}_{i\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{k}_{i},U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})]\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

(S74)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathrm{i}}\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}[\hat{k}_{i},q\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})-\frac{q\bm{A}(\bm{\hat{r}})\cdot\bm{p}}{m}+\frac{q^{2}\bm{A}^{2}(\hat{\bm{r}})}{2m}]\hat{P}_{\mathcal{B}}

(S75)

\displaystyle=-q\left(\partial_{r_{i}}\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}+\frac{q}{m}\left(\left(\partial_{r_{i}}\bm{A}\right)\cdot(\hat{\bm{p}}-q\bm{A})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S76)

\displaystyle=qE_{i\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})+\frac{q}{m}\left(\partial_{r_{i}}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{\hat{\pi}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S77)

\displaystyle=qE_{i\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})+\frac{q}{m}\left(\left[\partial_{\hat{r}_{i}},A_{j}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right]\hat{\pi}_{j}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S78)

On the other hand, to first order in the applied fields, the projected dynamics of the gauge-invariant crystal momenta
\hat{\bm{\kappa}}=\hat{\bm{k}}-q\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})
can be approximately taken as

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\hat{\bm{\kappa}}_{\mathcal{B}}\approx q\bm{E}_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})+\frac{q}{2m}\bigg(\bm{\hat{\pi}}\times\bm{B}(\hat{\bm{r}})-\bm{B}(\hat{\bm{r}})\times\bm{\hat{\pi}}\bigg)_{\mathcal{B}}.

(S79)

This holds in any gauge that are static, which we can always assume to exist for static fields. Note that it is the gauge-invariant
\bm{\pi}
and not
\dot{\bm{\hat{r}}}
that appears in the force equation.

IV.2
Position dynamics in electric field

IV.2.1
Velocity

In an electric field the only intra-band contribution is due to the Bloch Hamiltonian
\hat{H}_{0}
, and is just the projected velocity operator
\hat{\bm{v}}_{\mathcal{B}}
. The inter-band velocity in an electric field is

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}_{i\perp}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{\mathrm{i}}\left(\hat{r}_{i}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})-\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{r}_{i}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\frac{q}{\mathrm{i}}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})]

(S80)

Expanding the scalar potential as

\displaystyle\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})=-E^{(0)}_{i}\hat{r}_{i}-\frac{1}{2!}E^{(1)}_{ij}\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}-\frac{1}{3!}E^{(2)}_{ijk}\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k},

(S81)

then this can be dealt with the method outlined in Section I. This gives

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}_{i\perp}^{\phi}=-\frac{q}{2}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},E_{j,\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}+\hat{R}_{i\perp}^{\phi},

(S82)

where to second order expansion

\displaystyle\hat{R}_{i\perp}^{\phi}=-\frac{q}{2}E_{jk}^{(2)}[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S83)

IV.2.2
Anomalous velocity

Here we illustrate the emergence of anomalous velocity by an explicit calculation. First note that for a uniform electric field
\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})=-E^{(0)}_{i}\hat{r}_{i}
, we have
\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{i\perp}^{\phi}=-qE^{(0)}_{j}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{r}_{j}]=-iqE^{(0)}_{j}\hat{\Omega}_{ij}
. Now consider the lowest order non-uniform electric field given by the scalar potential
\phi(\hat{\bm{r}})=-\frac{1}{2!}E^{(1)}_{ij}\hat{r}_{i}\hat{r}_{j}
, or the electric field being
E_{i}(\hat{\bm{r}})=E^{(1)}_{ij}\hat{r}_{j}
.

We have that, recalling eq.(
S13
)

\displaystyle[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\left(\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\left(\{\hat{r}_{j},\hat{r}_{k}\}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]

(S84)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\{\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{r}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}]+\frac{1}{2}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},2\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S85)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},\hat{r}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ik},\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}}\}+[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S87)

Thus we have

\displaystyle i\hat{r}_{i\perp}^{\phi}

\displaystyle=[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},q\phi_{\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})]=-\frac{q}{2}E^{(1)}_{jk}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\left(\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]

(S88)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{2}E^{(1)}_{jk}\bigg(\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},\hat{r}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{i}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ik},\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}}\}+[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]\bigg)

(S89)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\frac{q}{4}\bigg(\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},E_{j\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}+\{\hat{\Omega}_{ik},E_{k\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}\bigg)-\frac{q}{2}E^{(1)}_{jk}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S90)

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\frac{q}{2}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},E_{j\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}-\frac{q}{2}E^{(1)}_{jk}[\hat{r}_{i\mathcal{B}},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S91)

or

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{i\perp}^{\phi}=-\frac{q}{2}\{\hat{\Omega}_{ij},E_{j\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\}-\frac{q}{2}E^{(1)}_{jk}[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{jk}]

(S92)

This is the operator form of the results obtained in
[
39
]
and
[
41
]
, generalized to the multi-band case. It is clear that the pattern of the decomposition of the inter-band velocity into the sum of a Berry curvature induced anomalous velocity and additional geometric terms continue in higher orders of inhomogeneity.

IV.3
Dynamics in magnetic fields

IV.3.1
General potential

The Hamiltonian in a magnetic field reads
\hat{H}=\bm{\hat{\pi}}^{2}/{2m}+V(\hat{\bm{r}})=\hat{H}_{0}+U_{\rm dia}(\hat{\bm{r}})+U_{\rm para}(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})
, where
U_{\rm dia}(\hat{\bm{r}})=q^{2}\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})^{2}/(2m
) and
U_{\rm para}(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})=-q(\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}})\cdot\hat{\bm{p}}+\hat{\bm{p}}\cdot\bm{A}(\hat{\bm{r}}))/(2m)
. The
projected dynamics of the position operator is given by

\displaystyle\ \frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}}

\displaystyle=-\mathrm{i}\left[\hat{\bm{r}}_{\mathcal{B}},\hat{H}_{\mathcal{B}}\right]=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{B}}}{m}+\mathrm{i}\left(\hat{\bm{r}}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})-U(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{\bm{r}}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}

(S93)

\displaystyle=\frac{\hat{\bm{\pi}}_{\mathcal{B}}}{m}+\dot{\hat{\bm{r}}}_{\perp},

(S94)

where we used the fact that
\left(\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{H}_{0}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=0
to obtain the second equality. The first intra-band term is the gauge-invariant kinematic momentum in ordinary quantum mechanics. The inter-band velocity due to the diamagnetic term reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2m}[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left(A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]

(S95)

\displaystyle=\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\left(\left\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},A_{k\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})],A_{k\mathcal{B}}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right\}+[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left(A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)

(S96)

To compute the paramagnetic contribution it is easier to consider first the whole paramagnetic term first, including the intra-band velocity. Namely

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}

\displaystyle=[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},U_{\rm para}(\hat{\bm{p}},\hat{\bm{r}})]=-\frac{q}{2m}[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left(\{{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}}),\hat{p}_{k}\}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]

(S97)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{2m}\left([\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\{{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}}),\hat{p}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}]+[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left({A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}+\hat{p}_{k}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)

(S98)

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{2m}\left(\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{p}_{k\mathcal{B}}],{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})\}+\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})],\hat{p}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}+[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left({A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}+\hat{p}_{k}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)

(S99)

Now we subtract the intra-band contribution due to the paramagnetic perturbation which is
-\frac{q}{m}\mathrm{i}{A}_{j\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})
from the first term in the bracket, and using Eq.(
S60
), we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}

\displaystyle=-\frac{q}{2m}\left(-\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{k\mathcal{B}}],{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})\}+\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})],\hat{p}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}+[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left({A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}\hat{p}_{k}+\hat{p}_{k}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)

(S100)

The first term emerges because the projected position and momentum are no longer a canonical pair but rather satisfies Eq.(
S60
). The second term arises as the projected position operators no longer commute with each other, and can be dealt with the same method as the electric field case, whereas the last one introduces new, mixed geometric quantities that involve spatial gradient of Bloch states as well.

Finally combining the diamagnetic and paramagnetic terms we obtain
Eqs.

23
,
24
,
25
and
26
in the main text

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\dot{\hat{r}}_{j\perp}=

\displaystyle-\frac{q}{2m}\left(-\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{k\mathcal{B}}],{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})\}+\{[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},{A}_{k\mathcal{B}}(\bm{\hat{r}})],\hat{\pi}_{k\mathcal{B}}\}+[\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left({A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}p_{k}+p_{k}\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}{A}_{k}(\bm{\hat{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)\

(S101)

\displaystyle+\frac{q^{2}}{2m}\left([\hat{r}_{j\mathcal{B}},\left(A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})\hat{Q}_{\mathcal{B}}A_{k}(\hat{\bm{r}})\right)_{\mathcal{B}}]\right)

(S102)

IV.3.2
Gradient expansion

So far the vector potential has been assumed to be geneal. We now expand the magnetic vector potential as
A_{i}(\hat{\bm{r}})=A^{(1)}_{ij}\hat{r}_{j}+\frac{1}{2}A^{(2)}_{ijk}\hat{r}_{j}\hat{r}_{k}
, we have the following respective geometric contributions to the inter-velocity.

First order

To linear order in the gradient expansion gives

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}^{(1)}_{l\perp}=

\displaystyle\ \mathrm{i}A_{jk}^{(1)}\frac{q}{m}\bigg(\{\hat{\nabla}_{k},[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}]\}+\{\hat{\Omega}_{lk},\hat{\pi}_{j\mathcal{B}}\}-[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{jk}]\bigg)

(S103)

\displaystyle\ +\frac{q^{2}}{2m}A^{(1)}_{ka}A^{(1)}_{kb}[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(2)}_{ab}].

(S104)

Since
a
and
b
are dummy variables, the last term due to diamagnetic term can be re-written as

\displaystyle\frac{q^{2}}{2m}A^{(1)}_{ka}A^{(1)}_{kb}[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{\mathcal{Q}}^{(2)}_{ab}]=\frac{q^{2}}{2m}A^{(1)}_{ka}A^{(1)}_{kb}[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{\mathcal{G}}^{(2)}_{ab}],

(S105)

which is manifestly Hermitian.

Second order

To second order in the gradient expansion, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{r}}_{i\perp}^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{+q}{2m}A^{(2)}_{jkl}\{[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{{\mathcal{A}}}^{\bm{r}}_{j\mathcal{B}}],\left(\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{l}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}\}-\frac{q}{2m}A^{(2)}_{jkl}\{[\hat{\nabla}_{i\mathcal{B}},\left(\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{l}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}],p_{j\mathcal{B}}\}

(S106)

\displaystyle-\frac{q}{2m}A^{(2)}_{jkl}\bigg([\hat{\nabla}_{i},\mathrm{i}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}^{\dagger})\hat{\nabla}_{k}+\mathrm{i}(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{kj}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{kj}^{\dagger})\hat{\nabla}_{l}+i[\hat{\nabla}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}]+i[\hat{\nabla}_{l},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{kj}]]+[\hat{\nabla}_{i},\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(2)}_{klj}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}^{(2)\dagger}_{klj}]\bigg),

(S107)

where in arriving at the second line we used

\displaystyle\mathrm{i}\hat{\nabla}_{k}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}+\mathrm{i}\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}^{\dagger}\hat{\nabla}_{k}=i(\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}+\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}^{\dagger})\hat{\nabla}_{k}+i[\hat{\nabla}_{k},\hat{\mathcal{L}}_{lj}].

(S108)

Note that using Eq.(
S13
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\hat{r}_{k}\hat{r}_{l}\right)_{\mathcal{B}}=\hat{r}^{(2)}_{kl,\mathcal{B}}=\mathrm{i}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}\{\hat{\nabla}_{k},\hat{\nabla}_{l}\}-\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{kl}\right),

(S109)

where
\hat{\mathcal{G}}_{kl}
is the quantum metric tensor.

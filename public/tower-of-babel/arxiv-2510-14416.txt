A unified model of dark energy and inflation from the Markov-Mukhanov action Preprint: APS/123-QED
Hrishikesh Chakrabarty, Daniele Malafarina

Abstract
We propose a unified model of dark energy and inflation through the Markov-Mukhanov modification of the Einstein-Hilbert action, where the matter sector is coupled to gravity via a scalar coupling function depending only on the energy density of the matter content. We assume that the coupling function encodes the UV corrections to the standard model of cosmology and we determine the form of the coupling that allows for the dark energy component to be dynamical and act as the inflaton field in the early universe. Interestingly we show that our model, in order to account for inflation, prefers a dark energy equation of state with w w close but not equal to − 1 -1 in agreement with the latest DESI data.



I
Introduction

The standard cosmological model known as
\Lambda
CDM has been very successful in describing the observable Universe while the field of cosmology has seen remarkable advances in the recent decades. These advances are not only a consequence of theoretical developments, but also rely heavily on precise observations such as the cosmic microwave background radiation (CMB)
Aghanim et al. 2020
;
Akrami et al. 2020
, type-IA supernovae (SNIa)
Abbott et al. 2019
, gravitational waves (GW)
Abbott et al. 2021
;
Abbott et al. 2023
, large scale structures
Abdul Karim et al. 2025
to name a few. We are living in a era of precision cosmology which allows us to formulate, analyze and potentially discard proposed cosmological models.

Regardless of this remarkable growth, there are still some open problems that plague the
\Lambda
CDM model. Two of the main issues in modern cosmology are related to the accelerated expansion rate of the Universe. Observations of type-Ia supernovae show that the Universe’s expansion is currently accelerating
Riess et al. 1998
;
Abbott et al. 2019
. The unknown cause responsible for this acceleration is called ‘dark energy’ (DE) and numerous models have been proposed to explain the observations while its intrinsic nature remains unknown
Bamba et al. 2012
. The simplest form of dark energy is the cosmological constant which accounts for the
\Lambda
in
\Lambda
CDM and is characterized by an equation of state
p=w\varepsilon
, with
w=-1
. However recent observation seem to favor a dynamical DE with
w
close but not equal to
-1

Adame et al. 2025a
. There also exist alternate models of DE, typically modeled with a scalar scalar field, modifications to the gravitational sector or with braneworld scenarios (see e.g.
Bamba et al. 2012
for a detailed review).

Additionally, theoretical models require another period of accelerated expansion in the early universe to solve the horizon and flatness problems. According to the
\Lambda
CDM, without the addition of any extra fields,
different portions of the CMB should not have been in causal contact when photons were first emitted during the recombination era. However the data shows that the CMB is almost perfectly isotropic with only tiny temperature fluctuations over the whole sky.
This suggests that the Universe was in thermal equilibrium at the time of emission of the CMB, and thus widely separated portions must have been in causal contact.
Also the spatial geometry of the Universe appears to be very close to flat, while the equations show that any tiny departure from flatness should have grown over time. A phase of accelerated expansion in the early Universe, called ‘inflation’, is typically invoked to explain these observations
Starobinsky 1980
;
Guth 1981
;
Linde 1982
. The easiest mechanism to drive inflation is given by the introduction of new fields (typically scalar fields) that achieve the desired behavior for the Universe before recombination.
In the last few decades, numerous models of inflation were proposed and many were discarded courtesy to the precision observations. However, there still exist a large array of inflationary models that fit the data and next generation observations are necessary in order to further constrain their validity
Abazajian et al. 2015
.

Since the physical nature of the scalar fields that drive inflation is unknown attempts have been made at describing both phases of acceleration via a single mechanism. In order to achieve this the model must span an enormous range of energies (
\sim 10^{120}{\rm GeV^{4}}
), ideally via a natural and not fine tuned mechanism involving only one single matter component and/or with minimal modifications to the theory of gravity.

In this article, we aim at building precisely such a model.
The motivation for such a model comes from the recent DESI results
Adame et al. 2025a
which hint towards a dynamical DE component. The idea of a unified description of dark energy and inflation is not new. Several models including quintessential inflation
Peebles and Vilenkin 1999
;
Uzan 1999
;
Dimopoulos 2003
;
Sami and Sahni 2004
;
Gonzalez et al. 2005
;
Neupane and Scherer 2008
;
Park 2024
and interplay of the Higgs boson and the inflaton
Dimopoulos and Markkanen 2019
have been proposed using a single scalar field. There are also models based on multiple scalar fields
Masso and Zsembinszki 2006
, entropic cosmology
Cai et al. 2010
;
Cai and Saridakis 2011
, the holographic dark energy
Nojiri et al. 2019
and ambitious triple unification of inflation, dark matter and dark energy
Zhang et al. 2021
;
Bose and Majumdar 2009
;
Liddle et al. 2008
to name a few.

In this article, we assume that matter is non-trivially coupled to gravity through a scalar coupling constant
Markov and Mukhanov 1985
and the universe contains a DE component, whose equation of state parameter is close but not equal to and smaller than
-1
. The non-trivial scalar coupling makes the dark energy component ‘dynamical’ in nature and provides a mechanism for dark energy to matter conversion. When matter and radiation are included in the model, such a universe initially accelerates mimicking an inflationary era. This period ends when enough matter and radiation are created and we enter a decelerating phase. As the universe expands further, it again enters a period of acceleration which mimics the dark energy dominated era. We assume that the equation of state parameter measured today is
w\sim-0.99

Adame et al. 2025a
and show that the same DE component can cause inflation in the early universe. We verify this claim by testing other values of
w
against the inflationary constrains on the spectral index of scalar curvature power spectrum and the ratio of tensor-to-scalar perturbations from Planck, BICEP/Keck and Atacama Cosmology Telescope (ACT) observations.

The article is organized as follows: in Sec.
II
, we introduce the basic equations of the theory starting from the action, the equations of motion and the conservation equation. In Sec.
II.1
, we discuss two possible choices for the free coupling function. In Sec.
III
, we derive the basic equations of cosmology and show how the running constants behave throughout the evolution of the universe. In Sec.
IV
, we explore the inflationary consequences of the model deriving the inflationary power spectrum and comparing it with observations. Finally in Sec.
V
, we summarize and results and comment on future directions.
Throughout the article we use natural units
c=\hbar=1
and metric signature
(-,+,+,+)
.

II
The Markov-Mukhanov action

We aim to build a unified model of dark energy and inflation based on two main ingredients:

(a)

There exist a non-minimal matter-gravity coupling that depends on the energy scale as described by Markov and Mukhanov (MM) in
Markov and Mukhanov 1985
.

(b)

The exact form of the non-minimal coupling can be expressed via a series of higher order corrections to the matter sector.

Then we start with the action
Markov and Mukhanov 1985

S=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left(\frac{R}{8\pi G_{N}}+2\chi(\varepsilon)\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}\right),

(1)

where
R
is the Ricci scalar,
\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}
is the matter Lagrangian and the matter-gravity coupling depends only on a scalar function
\chi(\varepsilon)
of the energy density
\varepsilon
.
One may understand this action as an effective classical description of the departure of a modified gravity theory from General Relativity. Thus this may be viewed as an agnostic approach to departures from GR which does not rely on a given alternative theory but instead aims at determining the properties of such a theory phenomenologically by finding a function
\chi
that fits observations.
The variation of the action with respect to the metric leads to modified Einstein equation in the form

R_{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\mu\nu}R=8\pi G_{N}\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu},

(2)

where

\tilde{T}_{\mu\nu}=\left(\varepsilon\chi\right)_{,\varepsilon}T_{\mu\nu}+(\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{,\varepsilon})g_{\mu\nu},

(3)

is the effective energy-momentum tensor while

T_{\mu\nu}=\left(\varepsilon+P(\varepsilon)\right)u_{\mu}u_{\nu}+P(\varepsilon)g_{\mu\nu},

(4)

is the energy-momentum tensor for a perfect fluid, where
P
is the fluid’s pressure, which we assume may be obtained from an equation of state
P=P(\varepsilon)
, and
u^{\mu}
is the four velocity vector field.
Looking at Eq. (
2
) and (
3
) we can identify the terms

G(\varepsilon)=G_{N}(\chi\varepsilon)_{,\varepsilon}\;\;\text{and}\;\;\Lambda(\varepsilon)=-8\pi G_{N}\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{,\varepsilon}

(5)

as a running gravitational constant
G(\varepsilon)
and a running cosmological constant
\Lambda(\varepsilon)
. In order to retrieve GR at low densities, for
\chi(\varepsilon)
we must ensure that as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we get
G(\varepsilon)\rightarrow G_{N}
.

Since we are writing Einstein’s equations with an effective matter source given by
\tilde{T}^{\mu}_{\nu}
, we must require that conservation equations hold for the effective stress-energy tensor, i.e.

\nabla_{\mu}\tilde{T}^{\mu}_{\nu}=0.

(6)

To see how the effective energy density evolves, we can project it along the four-velocity field
u^{\mu}
which gives

\nabla_{\mu}\left(\tilde{\varepsilon}u^{\mu}\right)+\tilde{P}\nabla_{\mu}u^{\mu}=0,

(7)

where the quantities with
tilde
are the effective fluid’s energy-density and pressure that can be obtained from Eq. (
3
) as

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=(\chi\varepsilon)_{,\varepsilon}\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon\chi(\varepsilon),

(8)

\displaystyle\tilde{P}

\displaystyle=(\chi\varepsilon)_{,\varepsilon}P+\varepsilon^{2}\chi_{,\varepsilon}.

This allows to define an effective equation of state from
\tilde{P}=\tilde{w}\tilde{\varepsilon}
as

\tilde{w}=-1+\frac{(\chi\varepsilon)_{,\varepsilon}}{\chi}\left(1+\frac{P}{\varepsilon}\right).

(9)

Notice that if
\chi=1
we retrieve GR as
G(\varepsilon)=G_{N}
,
\Lambda(\varepsilon)=0
and
\tilde{w}=w=P/\varepsilon
.

II.1
Choice of the free function

Our aim is to exchange the introduction of some new matter fields in the early universe with the modifications of the field equations coming from the MM action. Hence, in this formalism, we have the freedom to make a choice for one of the functions
\chi(\varepsilon)
,
G(\varepsilon)
or
\Lambda(\varepsilon)
. Choosing one of these three functions will fix the other two.

In order to make such a choice in an agnostic manner (meaning without prior assumptions as derived from some more fundamental theory, as was done for example in
Zholdasbek et al. 2025
) we shall consider generic higher order corrections to the coupling function
\chi(\varepsilon)
and write it as

\chi(\varepsilon)=1+\delta(\varepsilon),

(10)

where
\delta(\varepsilon)
represents departure from minimal coupling.

Notice that the effective energy density takes the form

\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon+\varepsilon\delta(\varepsilon)

(11)

where the second term can be related to other proposed modifications to the theory in the high curvature regime.

An obvious choice for
\delta(\varepsilon)
inspired from Loop Quantum Gravity (LQG) is given by the simple choice
\delta(\varepsilon)=-\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}
.
In LQG, the form of
\delta
appears from geometric quantum mechanics where the effective equation incorporates the leading corrections from quantum geometry
Ashtekar 2009
;
Willis 2004
;
Taveras 2008
.
Here the effective density becomes
\tilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon-\varepsilon^{2}/\varepsilon_{c}
where
\varepsilon_{c}
is a critical energy density at which the corrections become important and typically it is taken of the order of Planck density.
Such models usually lead to a bounce in the early universe
as can be seen in Loop Quantum Cosmology (LQC) models
Ashtekar et al. 2006
;
Ashtekar 2009
;
Wilson-Ewing 2013
;
Agullo and Singh 2017
as well as in collapse models
Bambi et al. 2013
;
Chakrabarty et al. 2020
.

However, we note that in the MM formalism the specific expression
\delta(\varepsilon)=-\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}
may represents merely the first-order correction within a more general perturbative expansion of the coupling function. Consequently, the departure from minimal coupling,
\delta(\varepsilon)
, may be expressed as a power series in the dimensionless parameter
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}

\delta(\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}C_{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{c}}\right)^{n},

(12)

where
C_{n}
are dimensionless coefficients that parametrize the strength of the n-th order correction. This expansion allows for a more complete phenomenological description of the coupling function, potentially capturing a wider range of physical phenomena beyond the leading-order approximation.

Now assuming
C_{0}=1
, we can write
\chi(\varepsilon)
as

\chi(\varepsilon)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{n}\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{c}}\right)^{n}.

(13)

In the following, we shall consider two cases for Eq. (
13
):

I.

If we consider only the terms up to
n=1
and absorb
C_{1}
in the cutoff density
\varepsilon_{c}
by taking
C_{1}=-1
, then
\chi(\varepsilon)
becomes

\chi(\varepsilon)=1-\frac{\varepsilon}{\varepsilon_{c}},

(14)

leading to a cosmological model inspired by but not identical to those studied in LQC.

II.

If we set
C_{n}=(-1)^{n}
for all
n
and consider correction terms at all orders, i.e. up to
n=\infty
then the series converges for
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}<1
and

\chi(\varepsilon)
is given by

\chi(\varepsilon)=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}},

(15)

which is just the sum of the infinite geometric series of
\chi(\varepsilon)
.

Both models depend only on one parameter
\varepsilon_{c}
related to the UV cutoff of GR. Going forward, we shall explore both of these choices to see if given today’s content of the universe they may be used to effectively describe early universe cosmology.

Cosmology

The evolution of the universe is governed by the Friedmann equations. We start by considering a FRW universe with metric given by

ds^{2}=-dt^{2}+a^{2}(t)\left(\frac{dr^{2}}{1-kr^{2}}+r^{2}d\Omega^{2}\right),

(16)

where
a(t)
is the scale factor,
k
is the curvature and
d\Omega^{2}
is the usual line element on the unit sphere. The Friedmann equations for the MM action are then obtained from the metric (
16
) with the effective energy-momentum tensor (
3
) as

\displaystyle H^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\right)^{2}=\frac{8\pi G_{N}}{3}\varepsilon\chi-\frac{k}{a^{2}}=\frac{8\pi G_{N}}{3}\tilde{\varepsilon}-\frac{k}{a^{2}},

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\pi G_{N}}{3}\left[(\varepsilon+3P)\chi+3\varepsilon\frac{\partial\chi}{\partial\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon+P\right)\right]

\displaystyle=-\frac{4\pi G_{N}}{3}(\tilde{\varepsilon}+3\tilde{P})

where
H=\dot{a}/a
is the Hubble parameter. The evolution of the effective energy density can be easily obtained from Eq. (
7
) as

\dot{\tilde{\varepsilon}}+3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\left(\tilde{\varepsilon}+\tilde{P}\right)=0,

(18)

or in terms of the original fluid quantities as

\left(\varepsilon\chi\right)_{,\varepsilon}\left[\dot{\varepsilon}+3\frac{\dot{a}}{a}\left(\varepsilon+P\right)\right]=0.

(19)

Since
\left(\varepsilon\chi\right)_{,\varepsilon}\neq 0
at all times, from the above equation we see that the zero component of the effective energy momentum
\tilde{T}^{\mu}_{\nu}
for an homogeneous perfect fluid is conserved if the classical energy momentum
T^{\mu}_{\nu}
is.

For a classical fluid with linear equation of state
P=w\varepsilon
, from Eq. (
9
), we can define the equation of state of the effective fluid as
\tilde{P}=\tilde{w}\tilde{\varepsilon}
, where
\tilde{w}
in Eq. (
9
) can now be written as

\tilde{w}=w+(1+w)\frac{\varepsilon\chi_{,\varepsilon}}{\chi}.

(20)

Let us now explore the two models presented earlier.
Our goal is to see if, by employing the MM formalism, we can obtain a phase of accelerated expansion in the early universe given only today’s matter content for the universe.
For simplicity, we assume a flat universe (
k=0
) which contains only matter (
w_{\rm m}=0
), radiation (
w_{\rm r}=1/3
) and a dark energy component with some equation of state parameter
w_{\rm DE}
. The Friedmann equations can be rewritten in the following way

\displaystyle\frac{H^{2}}{H_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\frac{\Omega_{i0}}{a^{3(1+w_{i})}}\chi,

(21)

\displaystyle\frac{\ddot{a}}{a}

\displaystyle=-\frac{H_{0}^{2}}{2}\sum_{i}\frac{\Omega_{i0}}{a^{3(1+w_{i})}}\chi\Bigg[1+

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ \ +3\left(w_{i}-(1+w_{i})\frac{\varepsilon_{0}}{\varepsilon_{c}}\frac{\Omega_{i0}}{a^{3(1+w_{i})}}\chi\right)\Bigg].

(22)

Here
\varepsilon_{0}=3H_{0}^{2}/8\pi G_{N}
is the energy density of the universe today with
H_{0}
being today’s Hubble parameter and
\Omega_{i0}
is today’s density parameter for the
i
-th matter component, i.e. with
i=
m, r, DE for matter (m), radiation (r) and dark energy (DE) respectively.

Model II

In the second case, where the coupling function is given by Eq. (
15
),
the running Newton’s constant and the running cosmological constant are

\displaystyle\frac{G(\varepsilon)}{G_{N}}

IV
Inflationary implications for Model II

Figure 4:
The comoving Hubble radius as a function of the scale factor for the model with only first order correction to the coupling function (left panel) and the model with infinite orders of correction to the coupling function (right panel). In both the plots, we have assumed a universe with matter, radiation and a dark energy component with
w_{\rm DE}=-0.99
and all the density parameters are set from the fiducial values obtained in

. The solid line in both the plots correspond to the
\Lambda

As mentioned, for the second model we have an asymptotically de Sitter initial state of the universe. In this period, the comoving Hubble radius decreases as the universe expands and if there are any primordial fluctuation modes in said period, they would freeze after leaving the horizon. Such modes can act as the seeds for fluctuations in the CMB when they re-enter at a later epoch. In other words, the initial quasi-de Sitter period can mimic an inflationary universe solving the horizon and the flatness problem while also introducing inhomogeneities in the CMB.

A viable inflationary model requires the Hubble slow-roll parameters, which characterize the conditions required to sustain the inflationary period, to be smaller than unity.
These slow-roll parameters are defined in terms of the background quantities as

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=\epsilon=-\frac{\dot{H}}{H^{2}}=-\frac{d\ln H}{dN},

(29)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=\eta=\frac{\dot{\epsilon}}{\epsilon H}=\frac{|d\ln\epsilon_{1}|}{dN},

(30)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{|d\ln\epsilon_{n}|}{dN},\ \ \ \ n>1,

(31)

where
N
denotes the number of e-folds of inflation. To solve the horizon and flatness problems, one requires
\{\epsilon,\eta\}\ll 1
. For our model, the first three of these parameters are

\displaystyle\epsilon_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}(1+w)\left(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}}\right),

(32)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{2}

\displaystyle=3(1+w)\left(\frac{\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}}{1+\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}}\right),

(33)

\displaystyle\epsilon_{3}

\displaystyle=3(1+w)\left(\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}}\right).

(34)

In Fig.
5
we plot the first two slow-roll parameters with respect to
\varepsilon/\varepsilon_{c}
for different equations of state.
Both the slow-roll parameters
\epsilon_{1}
and
\epsilon_{2}
are smaller than unity only for equations of state with
w\leq-1/3
. As can be seen from the right panel of Fig.
6
, for sufficiently small values of
w
, i.e.
w\sim-0.99
, the slow-roll parameters can be small enough for a sustained period of inflation. Therefore, in order to have a successful inflationary early universe in this scenario, one must require a dark energy component with
w_{\rm DE}\simeq-0.99

IV.1
Linear perturbations and the power spectrum

To compare the prediction of our model with observations, we need to analyze the model at the perturbative level. Planck observations suggest that the power spectrum of perturbations during inflation was nearly flat
Akrami et al. 2020
. Therefore we need to calculate the power spectrum of curvature perturbations generated during the inflationary epoch in the model presented above. Cosmology in the Markov-Mukhanov framework was treated at the perturbative level in the context of asymptotic safety in
Zholdasbek et al. 2025
. Here we closely follow
Chen et al. 2013
to obtain the quadratic action, which is second order in the perturbation variables, which we then canonically quantize.

We start from the Markov-Mukhanov Lagrangian in Eq.(
1
) which is given by

\frac{\mathcal{L}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm G}+\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\rm m},

(35)

where the gravity part is
\mathcal{L}_{\rm G}=R/(16\pi G_{N})
and the matter Lagrangian is
\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\rm m}=\chi\mathcal{L}_{\rm m}
. We rewrite the matter Lagrangian by adding two Lagrange multipliers as

\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\rm m}=-\tilde{\rho}(1+e)+\lambda_{1}(g_{\mu\nu}u^{\mu}u^{\nu}+1)+\lambda_{2}(\tilde{\rho}u^{\mu})_{;\mu}.

(36)

Here
\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\varepsilon}/(1+e)
is the rest mass density with
e
being the specific internal energy,
u^{\mu}
is the 4-velocity. The Lagrange multipliers
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are for the two constraints: the normalization of the 4-velocity and the conservation of the rest mass density. The variation of the action (
35
) with respect to the Lagrange multipliers gives the two constraint equations while the variation with respect to the metric gives the Markov-Mukhanov field equations.

Using the standard procedure for splitting the action in the
3+1
formalism we then construct the quadratic action. The metric in the
3+1
formalism is

ds^{2}=-\alpha^{2}dt^{2}+h_{ij}(dx^{i}+\beta^{i}dt)(dx^{j}+\beta^{j}dt),

(37)

where
\alpha
and
\beta
are called the lapse function and the shift vector, respectively. A spatially-flat FRW background corresponds to
\alpha=1
and
\beta^{i}=0
. The ADM action can be obtained by putting the above metric in the action

S=\int dtd^{3}x\sqrt{h}\alpha(\mathcal{L}_{\rm G}+\mathcal{\tilde{L}}_{\rm m}),

(38)

where
h=\det(h_{ij})
. The gravity part can now be written as

\mathcal{L}_{\rm G}=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\left[{}^{(3)}R+\alpha^{-2}\left(K_{ij}K^{ij}-K^{2}\right)\right],

(39)

where
{}^{(3)}R
is the Ricci scalar constructed out of the three-dimensional induced metric
h_{ij}
,
K_{ij}
is the extrinsic curvature on the constant-time hypersurfaces and
K
is the trace of
K_{ij}
. The extrinsic curvature is given by

K_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\dot{h}_{ij}-^{(3)}\nabla_{j}\beta_{i}-^{(3)}\nabla_{i}\beta_{j},

(40)

with
{}^{(3)}\nabla_{i}
representing the covariant derivative with respect to the three dimensional metric
h_{ij}
.

The variation with respect to lapse and shift (which are Lagrange multipliers of the gravitational system) leads to two additional constraint equations. The four constraint equations can be solved for the Lagrange multipliers and can be used in the action in favor of the perturbation variable(s) of interest.

In the following we are interested in the scalar curvature perturbations
\mathcal{R}
as the perturbation variable.
We use the comoving gauge and introduce perturbations to the system in the form

u^{\mu}=(-1+u,0,0,0),\ \ \ \ \ \ h_{ij}=a^{2}(t)e^{2\mathcal{R}}\delta_{ij},

(41)

where
u
is the velocity scalar potential to all orders in perturbations and
\mathcal{R}
denotes the curvature perturbations in the comoving gauge.
We can then eliminate the Lagrange multipliers and obtain the second order Lagrangian
as (see
Chen et al. 2013
for details)

\mathcal{L}_{\mathcal{R}}^{(2)}=\frac{a^{3}}{8\pi G_{N}}\left[\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{c_{e}^{2}}\dot{{\mathcal{R}}}^{2}-\frac{\epsilon_{1}}{a^{2}}(\partial\mathcal{R})^{2}\right],

(42)

where
c_{e}
is the effective or the rest frame sound speed of the dark energy fluid. Notice that the adiabatic sound speed of the fluid
c_{s}^{2}=\dot{\tilde{P}}/\dot{\tilde{\varepsilon}}
will be negative in the early universe leading to gravitational instability. Hence a source of non-adiabatic pressure fluctuations is required, which can be supplied from the internal degrees of freedom in the dark energy. The presence of the non-adiabatic pressure perturbations then contributes towards the effective sound speed
c_{e}^{2}
which need not be negative (see
Hu 1998
;
Gordon and Hu 2004
for details).

Now the second order action with the Lagrangian (
42
) can be written in the following way

S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau d^{3}xz^{2}\left[(\mathcal{R}^{\prime})^{2}-c_{e}^{2}(\partial\mathcal{R})^{2}\right],

(43)

where, in order to simplify the notation, we have set
8\pi G_{N}=1
and defined
z^{2}=2a^{2}\epsilon_{1}/c_{e}^{2}
. Here the primed quantity denotes the derivative with respect to the conformal time
\tau=\int dt/a
. For convenience, we introduce the Mukhanov-Sasaki variable
v=z\mathcal{R}

Mukhanov 1988
;
Sasaki 1986
and write the action one more time as

S^{(2)}=\frac{1}{2}\int d\tau d^{3}x\left[v^{\prime 2}-c_{e}^{2}(\partial v)^{2}-m(\tau)^{2}v^{2}\right],

(44)

where

\displaystyle m(\tau)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{z^{\prime\prime}}{z}=(aH)^{2}\left[2-\epsilon_{1}+\frac{3\epsilon_{2}}{2}-\frac{\dot{c}_{e}}{c_{e}H}+\left(\frac{\epsilon_{2}}{2}\right)^{2}+\right.

(45)

\displaystyle-

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\epsilon_{2}(\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{3})}{2}-\frac{\dot{c}_{e}\epsilon_{2}}{c_{e}H}-\frac{\ddot{c}_{e}}{c_{e}H^{2}}+2\left(\frac{\dot{c}_{e}}{c_{e}H}\right)^{2}\right],

is a time dependent mass parameter. In the quasi-de Sitter era we have

aH\simeq-\frac{1}{\tau}(1+\epsilon_{1}).

(46)

Now the variation of the action (
44
) leads to the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation
Sasaki 1986
;
Mukhanov 1988
,

v^{\prime\prime}+c_{e}^{2}\nabla^{2}v+m(\tau)^{2}v=0,

(47)

or in the Fourier basis

\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}_{k}+\left(c_{e}^{2}k^{2}+m(\tau)^{2}\right)v_{k}=0.

(48)

The system can be canonically quantized following the standard procedure and the power spectrum of the quantum fluctuations for the model can be found by solving Eq. (
48
) with the appropriate boundary conditions. We can write the Mukhanov-Sasaki equation as a Bessel equation by defining
\nu
from

\tau^{2}m(\tau)^{2}=\nu^{2}-1/4,

(49)

and defining a new time variable
T
as

T=-c_{e}k\tau,

(50)

in terms of which Eq. (
48
) becomes

\frac{d^{2}v_{k}}{dT^{2}}+\left[1-\frac{1}{T^{2}}\left(\nu^{2}-\frac{1}{4}\right)\right]v_{k}=0.

(51)

All modes exit the Hubble horizon at
T/c_{e}=1
, i.e.
k=aH
with sub (super) Hubble scales corresponding to
T/c_{e}\gg(\ll)1
. On the other hand, the modes cross the sound horizon when
aH=c_{e}k
or
T=1
. Now we use another redefinition of the variables setting
F=v_{k}/\sqrt{T}
to write Eq. (
51
) in the final form

\frac{d^{2}F}{dT^{2}}+\frac{1}{T}\frac{dF}{dT}+\left[1-\frac{\nu^{2}}{T^{2}}\right]F=0.

(52)

This equation can now be easily solved analytically.
The general solution of Eq. (
52
) can be written in terms of Hankel functions as

F(T)=C_{1}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(T)+C_{2}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(2)}(T),

(53)

where
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
can be fixed by the boundary conditions. The solution in terms of the Mukhanov-Sasaki variable is

v_{k}(T)=\sqrt{T}\left[C_{1}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(T)+C_{2}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(2)}(T)\right].

(54)

In the limit
c_{e}k\ll aH
, the Hankel functions take the form

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(T)\Big|_{T\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}2^{\nu-\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})}T^{-\nu},

(55)

\displaystyle\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(2)}(T)\Big|_{T\rightarrow 0}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle-\sqrt{\frac{2}{\pi}}e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}2^{\nu-\frac{3}{2}}\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})}T^{-\nu}.

(56)

We use the Bunch-Davies conditions
Bunch and Davies 1978
to find
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
. For the mode functions deep in the sub-Hubble limit, i.e.
c_{e}k\gg aH
(or
T\rightarrow\infty
),

v_{k}(T)\Big|_{T\rightarrow\infty}\rightarrow\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c_{e}k}}e^{iT}=\sqrt{T}C_{1}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(T)\Big|_{T\rightarrow\infty}.

(57)

This yields

C_{1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2c_{e}k}}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{2}}e^{i\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{2}},\ \ \ \ C_{2}=0.

(58)

Now the final expression for the mode functions becomes

v_{k}(T)=\frac{e^{i\left(\nu+\frac{1}{2}\right)\frac{\pi}{2}}}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi T}{c_{e}k}}\mathrm{H}_{\nu}^{(1)}(T).

(59)

Finally, the power spectrum of comoving curvature perturbations in our model can be written as

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}|\mathcal{R}_{k}|^{2}=\frac{k^{3}}{2\pi^{2}}\frac{|v_{k}|^{2}}{z^{2}},

(60)

where,
v_{k}
is given by Eq. (
59
). Using the expression for the Hankel function in the super-Hubble limit from from Eq. (
55
), the power spectrum becomes

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{R}}(k)=\frac{2^{2\nu-3}}{8\pi^{2}\epsilon_{1}c_{e}}\left(\frac{\Gamma(\nu)}{\Gamma(\frac{3}{2})}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{H}{m_{\rm pl}}\right)^{2}\left(\frac{c_{e}k}{aH}\right)^{3-2\nu},

(61)

where
a
,
H
and
\epsilon_{1}
are evaluated at the sound horizon exit and we have reinstated the factor of
m_{\rm pl}^{2}=1/8\pi G_{N}
. Now, we can extract the spectral index of the scalar perturbations

n_{s}-1\simeq 3-2\nu,

(62)

where
\nu
is defined from Eq. (
49
) and it is a function of the slow-roll parameters, the effective sound speed and its derivatives. It can easily be verified that for sufficiently small values of slow-roll parameters and a nearly constant effective sound speed, the power spectrum of scalar curvature fluctuations is flat.

Finally the tensor power spectrum
\mathcal{P}_{T}
is obtained in exactly the same manner as the single scalar field slow-roll case. We obtain

\mathcal{P}_{T}=\frac{2H^{2}}{\pi^{2}m_{\rm pl}^{2}},

(63)

so that the tensor-to-scalar ratio
r
is given by

r\simeq 16\epsilon_{1}c_{e}.

(64)

V
Summary

We considered a cosmological model obtained from a modification of the field equations of General Relativity as proposed by Markov and Mukhanov in
Markov and Mukhanov 1985
.
For the matter content of the universe we considered only matter, radiation and a dark energy component in accordance with recent observation. We then obtained a phase of accelerated expansion in the early universe via a suitable and natural choice of the
matter-gravity coupling. In this framework the role of the inflaton field is replaced by the effects of the matter-gravity coupling on the universe’s content.
The most noteworthy result of our analysis is that in order for the model to match the observational data from the CMB we must require that the late universe dark energy equation of state departs slightly from the cosmological constant. This is in accordance with recent observations by DESI that suggest a dynamical nature for dark energy.
If confirmed, the connection of such dynamical nature of dark energy with the matter-gravity coupling may turn out to have important implications for our understanding of gravity at high energies and at large scales.

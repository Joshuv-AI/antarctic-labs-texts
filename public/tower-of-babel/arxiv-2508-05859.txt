Doubly robust integration of nonprobability and probability survey data
Shaun R Seaman, Tommy Nyberg, Anne M Presanis

Abstract
Doubly robust estimators combine an inverse probability weighting estimator and a mass imputation estimator. Several doubly robust estimators for estimating the population mean (or prevalence) of an outcome have been proposed for integrating outcome and covariate data from a nonprobability survey with covariate data from an auxiliary probability survey. However, the question of how to combine a doubly robust estimate with a corresponding estimate based on outcome data from the auxiliary probability survey alone has only received limited attention. In this paper, we (i) review previously proposed doubly robust estimators, (ii) provide formulae for the variance of doubly robust estimators and the covariance between doubly robust and probability survey estimates, (iii) propose a framework for how to combine efficiently a doubly robust estimate from a nonprobability sample with an estimate based on the auxiliary probability sample alone, and (iv) provide formulae for the variance of such combined estimates.



1
Introduction

Probability surveys remain the gold standard for the estimation of population means or prevalences. Nonprobability surveys are limited by a lack of a defined sampling frame and may be associated with considerable selection bias.
However, with the increasing challenges of conducting probability surveys and increasing availability and ease of collecting nonprobability survey data,
there has been increased interest in the feasibility of using nonprobability survey data
in recent years.
A prominent class of methods to integrate data from a nonprobability survey with data from an auxiliary probability survey
include, for example, inverse probability weighting (IPW) and mass imputation (MI) estimators.
Both these approaches rely on the assumption that the probability sample can provide representative information about a set of covariates in the population.
IPW estimators use covariate information available from both surveys to estimate the selection probabilities for the nonprobability sample participants, and the inverse of these selection probabilities are then used as weights for the nonprobability sample.
MI estimators involve fitting a prediction model for the outcome to the nonprobability sample and then using this fitted model to impute the
outcomes in the probability sample
[
1
,
2
]
.

Doubly robust (DR) estimators are a class of estimators that combine an IPW and an MI estimator, and so rely on
a model for the unknown selection (or missingness) mechanism and a model for the outcome. These models are combined in such a way as to yield a consistent estimate provided at least one, but not necessarily both, of the
models are correctly specified
[
3
]
.
Several DR estimators have been proposed for when data on a set of individual-level covariates are available in both a nonprobability sample and a representative probability sample, but data on the outcome of interest is available only in the nonprobability sample
[
4
,
5
,
6
,
7
]
.

However, methods for when data on the outcome is additionally available in the probability sample have received only limited attention. In such situations
the outcome mean (or prevalence) in the population can be directly estimated using just the probability sample.
This mean (or prevalence) may additionally be estimated using a doubly robust estimator that integrates the nonprobability sample data with the probability sample covariate information.
Because both these estimators use the same covariate data from the probability sample, they are not, in general, independent, which makes combining them efficiently a non-trivial task.
We consider the class of combined estimators that are weighted averages of the two estimators. For such combined estimators, the efficient choice of weights depends on the variances of each estimator and the covariance between them.
Recently, Gao and Yang
[
8
]
proposed a method to pool Chen et al.’s
[
6
]

\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR1}
doubly robust estimator with a probability sample estimator when the IPW and MI models of the DR estimator are estimated separately using maximum likelihood and pseudo-maximum likelihood methods.
Their approach gives a consistent DR point estimate provided at least one of the IPW and MI models is correctly specified. However, the formulas provided for variance and covariance and the validity of the pooled estimate rely on the strong assumption that both models are correctly specified.
In this paper we review and clarify these methods with the aim of generalising them. We propose a framework for combining a probability sample estimate with the corresponding DR estimate based on a nonprobability sample, including methods that allow for valid inference also when one of the nuisance models is incorrectly specified. As we discuss, a special case of this framework is the combining of an estimate from the probability sample with an IPW estimate from the nonprobability sample.

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm Var}(\hat{\mu}_{\rm H}-\bar{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{N})-{\rm Cov}(\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR}-\bar{Y},\hat{\mu}_{\rm H}-\bar{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{N})}{{\rm Var}(\hat{\mu}_{\rm H}-\bar{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{N})+{\rm Var}(\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR}-\bar{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{N})-2\;{\rm Cov}(\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR}-\bar{Y},\hat{\mu}_{\rm H}-\bar{Y}\mid\mathcal{F}_{N})}.

6
Discussion

In this paper we have reviewed and provided several extensions to the theory underlying the DR estimators that have been proposed for integrating nonprobability and probability survey data.
We have shown that this use of an auxiliary probability sample results in dependence between a DR estimate and the corresponding probability sample estimate of the population mean (or prevalence) of an outcome (when the outcome has been measured in both samples), and have provided
formulae for the covariance between the estimates.
We have provided methods for how to combine the two estimates,
including closed-form formulae for the efficient weights to assign to each estimate, and the variance of the resulting pooled estimate.

Our work builds on the methods for combining DR and probability sample estimates proposed by
Gao and Yang
[
8
]
. This previous work provided methods only for
the estimator termed
\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR1}
by Chen et al
[
6
]
, and
relied on the assumption that both the nuisance models
\pi^{B}(X;\alpha)
and
m(X;\beta)
of the DR estimator are correctly specified.
We have extended and generalised this methodology to every combination of
\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR1}
(with and without KH) or
\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR2}

for the nonprobability sample, and either a Horvitz-Thompson or Hajek estimate for the probability sample. In addition to providing formulae that are valid under the assumption that both nuisance models are correctly specified, we provide formulae that enable valid inference also in the case where the model
m(X;\beta)
might be misspecified and only model
\pi^{B}(X;\alpha)
is required to be correctly specified.

We have also provided formulae for the covariance between an IPW estimator for the nonprobability sample and either a Horvitz-Thompson or Hajek estimator for the probability sample. These formulae enable valid inference also for combining such estimates. It has previously been claimed that such estimates are independent
[
12
]
, which our theory proves to be invalid.

Our work may be further extended.
It remains to assess the performance of our proposed methods, in simulations and in application to real-world data.
It also remains to explore the performance of the closed-form formulae compared to corresponding resampling-based approximations.
Furthermore, Gao and Yang
[
8
]
proposed methods for pre-testing for consistency between probability sample and DR nonprobability sample estimates, to decide whether the estimates should be combined or
if only the probability sample should be used.
We do not consider pre-testing, but the proposed methods may in principle be extended to such applications.

In conclusion, we have extended the methodology and have provided a theoretically rigorous framework for combining estimates based on the class of DR estimators proposed by Chen et al.
[
6
]
,
with estimates from Horvitz-Thompson or Hajek estimators. When data on an outcome are available from both a nonprobability and a probability sample, this methodology enables making use of all available data for valid estimation of population means or prevalences and their confidence intervals.

where

is an estimator of
{\rm Var}\{Y_{i}-m(X_{i};\beta_{0})\mid\mathcal{F}_{N}\}
.
Since the extra term in expression (
A16
) relative to expression (
A15
) has expectation zero when the model
\pi^{B}(X;\alpha)
is correctly specified, this estimator is valid when the KH method is used and at least one of the two nuisance models is correctly specified.
This is therefore a doubly robust variance estimator (when the KH method is used).

Note that if the model

is correctly specified and model
\pi^{B}(X;\alpha)
is misspecified, then the model for

needs to be correctly specified, but if the model
\pi^{B}(X;\alpha)
is correctly specified, the model for
\sigma^{2}(X_{i})
can be misspecified.
Also note that if

is a correctly specified linear regression model (that includes an intercept term) and

is a constant and is modelled as such, then the second term in expression (
A16

A16
) reduces to expression (
A15
).
This is because equation (
A6
) implies that

when

is a linear regression model.

\displaystyle\hat{\mu}_{\rm DR2}(\alpha,\beta,\tau^{A},\tau^{B})

\displaystyle\hskip 25.6073pt+o_{p}(1)(\hat{\tau}^{B}-1)-\bar{Y}+o_{p}(N^{-1/2})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}U_{i}(\hat{\alpha},\hat{\beta},1,1)-\bar{m}(\hat{\tau}^{A}-1)-o_{p}(1)\times O_{p}(N^{-1/2})

\displaystyle\hskip 14.22636pt=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{R^{A}_{i}}{\pi^{A}_{i}}\{m(X_{i})-\bar{m}\}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{R^{B}_{i}}{\pi^{B}(X_{i})}\{Y_{i}-m(X_{i})\}

A stacky comparison of the Nygaard and Hodge filtrations
Maximilian Hauck

Abstract
. We use the approach to p p -adic cohomology theories via stacks recently developed by Drinfeld and Bhatt–Lurie to formulate a stacky version of a comparison result between the Nygaard filtration on prismatic cohomology and the Hodge filtration on de Rham cohomology by Bhatt–Lurie and thereby also obtain a generalisation in the case of smooth and proper p p -adic formal schemes which allows for coefficients in an arbitrary gauge. In the process, we develop a stacky approach to diffracted Hodge cohomology as introduced by Bhatt–Lurie which also captures the conjugate filtration and the Sen operator. In the appendix, we also introduce a stack computing the conjugate filtration on absolute Hodge–Tate cohomology.

1.
Introduction

While the idea that one may compute the value of a cohomology theory attached to a scheme
X
by instead computing the coherent cohomology of a suitably defined stack attached to
X
goes back, in the case of de Rham cohomology, to work of Simpson in the 1990s, see
[
15
]
and
[
16
]
, this approach has only recently found entrance into the field of
p
-adic Hodge theory and has been starting to be fully exploited in the course of the last few years with the formulation of prismatic cohomology in terms of stacks independently developed by Bhatt–Lurie and Drinfeld in
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
8
]
. Roughly speaking,
similarly to how one can attach to any
p
-adic formal scheme
X
its
de Rham stack

X^{\mathrm{dR}}
, which has the property that coherent cohomology of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{dR}}}
agrees with the (
p
-completed) de Rham cohomology of
X
if
X
is smooth,
they functorially attach a stack
X{\Delta}
to any
such

X
with the feature that coherent cohomology of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{X{\Delta}}
agrees with the (absolute) prismatic cohomology of
X

if
X
is
p
-quasisyntomic in the sense of
[
3
, Def. C.6]
; correspondingly, the stack
X{\Delta}
is called the

of
X
. Moreover, similarly to how the de Rham stack

admits a filtered refinement

over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
computing the Hodge filtration, they also introduce a filtered refinement

over
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
of

computing the (absolute) Nygaard filtration on prismatic cohomology. The aspects of this approach most relevant to this paper are shortly reviewed in Section

.

The upshot of this picture is twofold: First, various statements about prismatic cohomology and related cohomology theories now admit a “geometric” formulation;
for example
, the comparison between prismatic cohomology and de Rham cohomology from
[
3
, Thm. 5.4.2]
can be reinterpreted as saying that, for any smooth
p
-adic formal scheme
X
, there is a functorial isomorphism

(X_{p=0}){\Delta}\cong X^{\mathrm{dR}}\;.

Second, the stacky formulation immediately furnishes natural categories of coefficients for the respective cohomology theories: these should just be the categories of quasi-coherent complexes, or perhaps perfect complexes, on the corresponding stack. E.g., the category
\mathcal{D}(X^{\mathrm{N}})
of
gauges
on
X
is a category of coefficients for Nygaard-filtered prismatic cohomology and, for any
E\in\mathcal{D}(X^{\mathrm{N}})
, we can define the Nygaard-filtered prismatic cohomology of
X
with coefficients in
E
as just the (derived) pushforward of
E
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

In this paper, we want to further advocate this philosophy by showing that the following comparison theorem between the Hodge and Nygaard filtrations of Bhatt–Lurie, see
[
3
, Prop. 5.5.12]
, admits a “stacky” version and thereby also generalising the theorem to gauge coefficients:

Theorem 1.0.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth qcqs
p
-adic formal scheme. Then there is a natural filtered comparison map

\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{N}}R\Gamma{\Delta}(X)\rightarrow\Fil^{\bullet}_{\mathrm{Hod}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)

with the property that the induced maps

R\Gamma{\Delta}(X)/\Fil^{i}_{\mathrm{N}}R\Gamma{\Delta}(X)\rightarrow R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)/\Fil^{i}_{\mathrm{Hod}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)

are
p
-isogenies for all
i\geq 0
. If
0\leq i\leq p
, they are in fact already isomorphisms integrally.

Remark 1.0.2
.

Note that, in loc. cit., the integral version is only stated for

, but the proof given there in fact also works for the case

. We also note that the smoothness assumption can be removed via Kan extensions by
[
3
, Rem. 5.5.10]

Namely, the result we are going to prove is the following:

Theorem 1.0.3

There is a commutative square of stacks

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{dR}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{dR},+}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathrm{dR}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i_{\mathrm{dR},+}}

More precisely,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}},E)[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta},j_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{dR},+},i_{\mathrm{dR},+}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{dR}},i_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;.}}

If the Hodge–Tate weights of

(see

1.0.1
with coefficients in perfect gauges in the case where

is proper:

Corollary 1.0.4

Let
X
be a
p
-adic formal scheme which smooth and proper over

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Fil^{0}_{\mathrm{N}}R\Gamma{\Delta}(X,E)[\frac{1}{p}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma{\Delta}(X,j_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\Fil^{0}_{\mathrm{Hod}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,i_{\mathrm{dR},+}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X,i_{\mathrm{dR}}^{*}E)[\frac{1}{p}]\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;.}}

If the Hodge–Tate weights of
E
(see

) are all at least
-p
, then the statement already holds integrally.

In fact, we not only upgrade the conclusion of
1.0.1
to the language of stacks, but also the proof given in
[
3
, Prop. 5.5.12]
. This uses the theory of
diffracted Hodge cohomology
that was introduced by Bhatt–Lurie in
[

:
For any bounded
p
-adic formal scheme
X
, its diffracted Hodge cohomology
R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
is a derived
p
-complete complex of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-modules which can be regarded as a deformation of the de Rham cohomology of
X
. Indeed, if
X
is smooth, after modding out
p
, the two agree up to Frobenius twists, see
[
3
, Rem. 4.7.18]
; moreover, the complex
R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
is naturally equipped with an ascending filtration
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
called the
conjugate filtration
with the property that, for smooth
X
, the associated graded pieces of the conjugate filtration on the diffracted Hodge cohomology of
X
agree with the ones of the Hodge filtration on the de Rham cohomology of
X
– more precisely, they both identify with the Hodge cohomology of
X
, i.e., for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\gr^{\mathrm{conj}}_{n}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)\cong R\Gamma(X,\widehat{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{n})[-n]\cong\gr^{n}_{\mathrm{Hod}}R\Gamma_{\mathrm{dR}}(X)\;,

where
\widehat{\Omega}_{X/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
denotes the
p
-completed cotangent sheaf of
X
over
\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, as usual. Finally, the complex
R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
is also equipped with an endomorphism
\Theta
called the
Sen operator
, which can be used to prove an integral refinement of the Deligne–Illusie theorem for smooth
X
of dimension less than
p
, see
[
3
, Ex. 4.7.17, Rem. 4.7.18]
. A brief review of the theory of diffracted Hodge cohomology is given in Section
3.1
.

Thus, for any bounded
p
-adic formal scheme
X
, we functorially construct stacks
X^{\not}{D},X^{\not{D},c}
and
(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}
which geometrise
the diffracted Hodge cohomology
R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
of
X
together with its conjugate filtration and the Sen operator.
More precisely, we show:

Theorem 1.0.5
.

Let
X
be a bounded
p
-adic formal scheme and assume that
X
is
p
-quasisyntomic and qcqs.
Then the following are true:

(i)

The pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\not}{D}}
along the map
X^{\not}{D}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\not}{D}\cong\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}
identifies with
R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
.

(ii)

The pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\not{D},c}}
along the map
X^{\not{D},c}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\not{D},c}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
identifies with
\Fil_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{conj}}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
under the Rees equivalence.

(iii)

Under the equivalence

\mathcal{D}((\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0})\cong\widehat{\mathcal{D}}_{\gr,D-\mathrm{nilp}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}\{u,D\}/(Du-uD-1))

from
3.4.1
, the underlying graded
\mathbb{Z}_{p}\langle u\rangle
-complex
of the pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
along
(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}\rightarrow(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}

.

While the diffracted Hodge stack

has already been introduced in
[
4

, albeit without explicit mention of the comparison result (i) from

nor the further refinement

appear

In an earlier version of the material in this paper that appeared in the author’s master’s thesis

Notation and conventions

We freely make use of the language of
\infty
-categories in the style of Lurie, see
[
13
]
, and of the theory of derived algebraic geometry as laid out in
[
12
]
. In particular, we work derived throughout: e.g., all our pullbacks and pushforwards are in the derived sense, i.e. when we write
f_{*}
for a map

of schemes/formal schemes/stacks, we really mean the derived pushforward
Rf_{*}:\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})\rightarrow\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{Y})

All the stacks occurring in this paper are going to be in the fpqc topology on
p
-nilpotent rings and we point out that, for the purposes of this paper, it does not make a difference whether one works in the setting of stacks in groupoids or stacks in
\infty
-groupoids. By a quasi-coherent complex on a stack
\mathcal{X}
, we mean an object of the derived category
\mathcal{D}(\mathcal{X})
, which is defined via Kan extension from the affine case as in
[
12
, §3.2]
; the same applies to the full subcategories
\Vect(\mathcal{X})
and
\Perf(\mathcal{X})
of vector bundles and perfect complexes, respectively.

We frequently make use of the Rees equivalence between the category of quasi-coherent complexes on
\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
and the category
\widehat{\mathcal{DF}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
of filtered objects in the category of derived
p
-complete
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-complexes, see
[
2
, §2.2.1]
or
[
14
]
for an introduction to the Rees equivalence and
[
1
, §A.1]
, specifically Ex. A.14 in loc. cit., for how the restriction to

From time to time, we need base change statements for cartesian squares of the form

Throughout,

2.1.
De Rham stacks

The idea of the de Rham stack and its filtered refinement goes back to Simpson, see
[
15
, §5]
; for a reference in modern language with more of a focus on the
p
-adic story relevant to us, see
[

Definition 2.1.1
.

Over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
, the canonical map
\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{O}(1))^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
, where

denotes the PD hull of the zero section, defines a 1-truncated animated

-algebra stack

{\color[rgb]{0,0,0}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR},+}\coloneqq\Cone(\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{O}(1))^{\sharp}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\mathbb{G}_{a})\;.}

The
Hodge-filtered de Rham stack

X^{\mathrm{dR},+}
of
X
is the stack
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{dR},+}}:X^{\mathrm{dR},+}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
defined by

X^{\mathrm{dR},+}(\Spec S\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m})\coloneqq\Map(\Spec\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR},+}(S),X)\;,

where the mapping space is computed in derived algebraic geometry. The base change of
X^{\mathrm{dR},+}
to
\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
is called the

of

and denoted
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{dR}}}:X^{\mathrm{dR}}\rightarrow\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}
while the base change
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{Hod}}}:X^{\mathrm{Hod}}\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{m}
of
X^{\mathrm{dR},+}
to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
is called the

of

.

Remark 2.1.2
.

If
X
is smooth and qcqs over
\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, one can describe

explicitly as

X^{\mathrm{Hod}}\cong B_{X\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}}\mathbb{V}(\mathcal{T}_{X/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}(1))^{\sharp}\;,

see
[
2
, Rem. 2.5.9]
. In particular, by the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant version of
[
2
, Prop. 2.4.5]
, this implies that giving a quasi-coherent complex on

amounts to specifying a graded quasi-coherent complex
V=\bigoplus_{i}V_{i}
on

\Phi:V\rightarrow V\otimes\Omega_{X/\mathbb{Z}_{p}}^{1}
which is locally nilpotent mod

and decreases degree by

is equipped with a factorisation through

As expected, coherent cohomology on the Hodge-filtered de Rham stack of

Let

This is
[
2
, Thm. 2.5.6]
.
∎

.

In particular, the above statement also implies that the pushforward

2.2.
Filtered prismatisation

We now describe a stacky approach to prismatic cohomology and the Nygaard filtration. For more details, the reader may consult
[
2
, Ch.s 4, 5]
, but we also want to point out the recent reformulation of the construction of the Nygaard-filtered prismatisation given by Gardner–Madapusi in
[
9
, §6.4]
, which avoids the use of the notion of admissible
W
-modules recalled below.

Definition 2.2.1
.

For a
p
-nilpotent ring
S
, a
Cartier–Witt divisor
on
S
is a generalised Cartier divisor
\alpha:I\rightarrow W(S)
on
W(S)
satisfying the following two conditions:

(i)

The
ideal generated by the
image of the map
I\xrightarrow{\alpha}W(S)\rightarrow S

is nilpotent
.

(ii)

The image of the map
I\xrightarrow{\alpha}W(S)\xrightarrow{\delta}W(S)
generates the unit ideal.

Here,
\delta:W(S)\rightarrow W(S)
is the usual
\delta
-structure on
W(S)
.

Definition 2.2.2
.

The
prismatisation

X{\Delta}
is the stack over
\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}
given by assigning to a
p
-nilpotent ring
S
the groupoid of pairs

(I\xrightarrow{\alpha}W(S),\Spec\cofib(I\xrightarrow{\alpha}W(S))\rightarrow X)\;,

where the first entry is a Cartier–Witt divisor on
S
and the second entry is a morphism of derived formal schemes.

Example 2.2.3
.

Any Cartier–Witt divisor
I\xrightarrow{\alpha}W(S)
yields a generalised Cartier divisor
I\otimes_{W(S)}S\rightarrow S
. Thus, we obtain a morphism of stacks

X{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\;.

The preimage of
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
under this map is called the
Hodge–Tate stack
of
X
and denoted
X^{\mathrm{HT}}
.
∎

Similarly to the case of de Rham cohomology, we will use
\mathcal{H}{\Delta}(X)
to denote the pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{X{\Delta}}
along
X{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}
. As before, in good situations, coherent cohomology of the structure sheaf on
X{\Delta}
agrees with
the
prismatic cohomology of
X
computed via the absolute prismatic site:

Theorem 2.2.4
.

Let
X
be a bounded
p
-adic formal scheme and
furthermore
assume that
X
is
p
-quasisyntomic and qcqs. Then there is a natural isomorphism

R\Gamma(X{\Delta},\mathcal{O}_{X{\Delta}})\cong R\Gamma{\Delta}(X)\;.

Proof.

Combine
[
4
, Cor. 8.17]
with
[
3
, Thm. 4.4.30]
.
∎

Definition 2.2.5
.

Let
X
be a bounded
p
-adic formal scheme which is
p
-quasisyntomic and qcqs. For a quasi-coherent complex

, we define the

of
X
with coefficients in

R\Gamma{\Delta}(X,E)\coloneqq R\Gamma(X{\Delta},E)\;.

To incorporate the Nygaard filtration into the picture, let

be the ring scheme of

-typical Witt vectors, i.e.
W\cong\prod_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Spec\mathbb{Z}[t_{1},t_{2},\dots]

for any

-nilpotent ring

via the Witt components
; if no confusion arises, we will also denote the base change of

3.
The conjugate-filtered diffracted Hodge stack

In this section, we will develop a stacky formulation of diffracted Hodge cohomology in a similar spirit to what has been done in Section
2
for de Rham cohomology and prismatic cohomology.
Namely, we will introduce stacks
\pi_{X^{\not}{D}}:X^{\not}{D}\rightarrow\Spf\mathbb{Z}_{p}

attached to any bounded
p
-adic formal scheme

whose coherent cohomology computes
the
diffracted Hodge cohomology of

together with its conjugate filtration as introduced in
[
3
, §4.7]
in good cases. Finally, we will also show that one can naturally incorporate the Sen operator
\Theta
on diffracted Hodge cohomology into this picture in a way that encodes the divisibility properties of the Sen operator with respect to the conjugate filtration;
namely, for any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the endomorphism

\Theta+n:\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{n}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)\rightarrow\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{n}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)

factors uniquely through
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{n-1}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
, see
[
3
, Rem. 4.9.10]
, and this will be reflected in the stacky picture.

3.1.
Recollections on diffracted Hodge cohomology

We start by briefly recalling the definition of diffracted Hodge cohomology from
[
3
, §4.7]
. To do this, first recall that, for a prism

on

-algebras

to all animated

-algebras via left Kan extension as in

. Moreover, the conjugate filtration

on

and can be

-algebras, where it is just defined to be the canonical filtration (i.e. the Postnikov filtration) on

Now recall that

on

, whose underlying unfiltered object we denote by

Essentially

and the Hodge–Tate comparison from

.

Now recall from

that

Theorem 3.3.3
.

Let

be a bounded

To prove this result,

Lemma 3.3.4
.

Let
X
be a

p

\pi_{X^{\not{D},c},*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\not{D},c}}

\Fil_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{conj}}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)

Proof.

Similarly to the proof of

, we may reduce to the case where

identifies with the canonical filtration on its underlying unfiltered object. Hence, by
[
2

\pi_{X^{\not{D},c},*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\not{D},c}}

and hence the induced map

is a

over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
: Indeed, for any

, the animated algebras

From the smooth case of

Proposition 3.3.5
.

We mimic the proof of

as these form a stratification of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

2

Since the Nygaard-filtered prismatisation takes quasisyntomic covers to fpqc covers by

, see
[

for a quasiregular semiperfectoid ring

.
∎

.

Observe that the mod

\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

To begin our analysis

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X{\Delta}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{X}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

We first explain how to obtain the desired map
X^{\mathrm{HT},c}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
and for this, it will be easiest to invoke the alternative description of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}
recently given by Gardner–Madapusi in
[
9
, §6.4]
. Namely, they show that one may also obtain
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}
as the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\mathrm{dR}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\mathrm{dR}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;,}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(t,u)}

where the two nonlabelled maps are given as follows:

(i)

The bottom map sends a pair
(L_{-}\rightarrow S,\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)\rightarrow L_{+})
of maps of invertible
S
-modules or
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
-modules, respectively, to the map

L_{-}\otimes_{S}L_{+}^{-1}\rightarrow S\otimes_{S}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)

of invertible
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
-modules.

(ii)

The map on the right sends a Cartier–Witt divisor
I\rightarrow W(S)
to the generalised Cartier divisor
F_{*}I\otimes_{F_{*}W(S)}\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
on
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
given by pulling back
F_{*}I
along the natural map
F_{*}W(S)\rightarrow(F_{*}W/{L}p)(S)\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}(S)
, see
[
2
, Cor. 2.6.8]
for the last isomorphism.

While the maps
t
and
\pi
are the familiar Rees and structure maps, respectively, the map
u
is not as easy to construct from the moduli interpretation
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}
using filtered Cartier–Witt divisors. The rough idea is as follows: fpqc-locally, any admissible
W
-module
M
arises from the standard exact sequence
2.2.7
via pushout along an endomorphism of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\sharp}
, see
[
2
, Lem. 5.2.8]
; moreover,
\underline{\mathrm{Hom}}_{W}(\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\sharp},\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\sharp})\cong\mathbb{G}_{a}
and two elements of
\mathbb{G}_{a}
yield the same admissible
W
-module
M
if and only if they differ by an element of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\sharp}
, see
[
2
, Prop. 5.2.1]
. Thus, fpqc-locally, any admissible
W
-module
M
gives rise to a section of
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}
and this defines the map
u
.

Lemma A.0.2
.

There is a pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{-}/\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\mathrm{dR}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

Proof.

This is just a reformulation of the fact that there is an open immersion
j_{\mathrm{HT}}:\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}}
living over
F:\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}{\Delta}
, see
[
2
, Constr. 5.3.2]
, using the description of
j_{\mathrm{HT}}
in the framework of Gardner–Madapusi, see
[
9
, Def. 6.5.2]
. Alternatively, the statement can also be obtained from the pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{*}W}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a}^{\mathrm{dR}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}

induced by the exact sequence from
2.2.7
.
∎

Proposition A.0.3
.

We have

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\;.

Proof.

From
A.0.2
, we conclude that
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}
may alternatively be described as the pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m})^{\mathrm{dR}}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;.}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}

As
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\mathrm{dR}}\cong\mathbb{G}_{m}/\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}
and taking de Rham stacks commutes with quotients, the map on the right is a
\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}
-gerbe by
3.4.1
and hence the same is true for the map on the left. However, the gerbe on the left is split: using
3.4.1
once again, the splitting is induced by the natural map
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a,+}^{\mathrm{dR}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}
.
∎

Now the desired map
X^{\mathrm{HT},c}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
is obtained as follows: by functoriality of the construction, there is a map
X^{\mathrm{HT},c}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}
and then we postcompose this with the natural projection
\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
.

Corollary A.0.4
.

There is an equivalence of categories

\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c})\cong\widehat{\mathcal{DF}}_{(\Theta^{p}-\Theta)-\mathrm{nilp}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])\;,

where the right-hand side denotes the full subcategory of
\widehat{\mathcal{DF}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])
consisting of objects on which
\Theta^{p}-\Theta
acts locally nilpotently mod
p
. Moreover, given
E\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c})
which identifies with
\Fil_{\bullet}M\in\widehat{\mathcal{DF}}_{(\Theta^{p}-\Theta)-\mathrm{nilp}}(\mathbb{Z}_{p}[\Theta])
, the pushforward of
E
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
identifies with
(\Fil_{\bullet}M)^{\Theta=0}
under the Rees equivalence (here, the kernel is to be taken in the derived sense).

Proof.

Using
A.0.3
, this is a variant of
3.1.1
and
[
3
, Prop. 3.5.11]
.
∎

Proposition A.0.5
.

Let
X
be a bounded
p
-adic formal schemes and assume that
X
is
p
-quasisyntomic and qcqs. Then the pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
along
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}:X^{\mathrm{HT},c}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}
identifies with the conjugate-filtered diffracted Hodge cohomology equipped with its Sen operator under the equivalence from
A.0.4
.

Proof.

Using base change for the cartesian square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;,}}

we know that
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c},*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
is the pullback of
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{N}},*}\mathcal{O}_{(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
along the map

\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{HT},c}\cong\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a,+}^{\mathrm{dR}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\cong(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}\;.

Moreover, recall from
3.4.4
that
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{N}},*}\mathcal{O}_{(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
identifies with
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
equipped with operators
D:\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)\rightarrow\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet-1}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
such that
uD-i:\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{i}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)\rightarrow\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{i}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
agrees with the Sen operator, where
u:\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)\rightarrow\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet+1}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
denotes the transition maps.

Using the commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{a,+}^{\mathrm{dR}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;,}}

where all maps are the canonical ones, we thus first conclude that the pullback of
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c},*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
identifies with
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
under the Rees equivalence. It thus only remains to identify the operator
\Theta
coming from
A.0.4
with the Sen operator. To do this, we may reduce to the associated graded using the fact that the filtration
\Fil^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
is complete. By the previous paragraph, we thus have to check that
\Theta
acts by
-i
on
\gr^{\mathrm{conj}}_{i}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
.

However, for this, note that the composition

B\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a,+}^{\mathrm{dR}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}

may alternatively be factored as

B\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{m}\times B\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{m}\rightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a,+}^{\mathrm{dR}}/\mathbb{G}_{m}\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\;,}

where the middle arrow takes a pair
(L,L^{\prime})
of invertible
S
-modules and sends it to
L\otimes_{S}L^{\prime}
; in other words, the pullback of
\mathcal{O}(1)
along the middle arrow is given by
\mathcal{O}(1)\boxtimes\mathcal{O}(1)
. As the pullback of
\pi_{X^{\mathrm{N}},*}\mathcal{O}_{(X^{\mathrm{N}})_{t=0}}
to
B\mathbb{G}_{m}
identifies with
\gr^{\mathrm{conj}}_{\bullet}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X)
by what we already know and the pullback of
\mathcal{O}(i)
along
B\mathbb{G}_{m}^{\sharp}\rightarrow B\mathbb{G}_{m}
corresponds to
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
equipped with the multiplication-by-
i
-map under the equivalence from
3.1.1
, we are done.
∎

Corollary A.0.6
.

The pushforward of
\mathcal{O}_{X^{\mathrm{HT},c}}
to
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{+}/\mathbb{G}_{m}
identifies with the conjugate-filtered Hodge–Tate cohomology
\Fil_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{conj}}R\Gamma_{\overline{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X)
of
X
under the Rees equivalence.

Proof.

This now follows by combining
A.0.4
and
A.0.5
with the fact that

\Fil_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{conj}}R\Gamma_{\overline{\mathbbl{\Delta}}}(X)\cong(\Fil_{\bullet}^{\mathrm{conj}}R\Gamma_{\not}{D}(X))^{\Theta=0}\;,

see
[
3
, Rem. 4.7.5]
.
∎

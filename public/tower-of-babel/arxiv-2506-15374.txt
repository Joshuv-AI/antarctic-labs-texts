Teng Huang, Jiaogen Zhang

Abstract
This article explores the relationship between Gårding cones Γ k + \Gamma^{+}_{k} and k k -positive cone 𝒫 k \mathcal{P}_{k} , demonstrating that under particular choices of m m and α \alpha , the shift cone Γ ¯ 2 + ​ ( α ) \overline{\Gamma}^{+}_{2}(\alpha) is contained in 𝒫 ¯ m \overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m} . Introduction

A central topic in Riemannian geometry is understanding how geometric assumptions constrain the topology of underlying manifolds, it is a fundamental problem to give reasonable curvature conditions of positivity to ensure a topological classification of such manifolds. A seminal result in this direction was established by Meyer
[
29

[
12

In a parallel development, Ogiue and Tachibana
[

second kind

2.1
Gårding cones and its properties

Let
\Gamma^{+}\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
be a nonempty open convex cone with vertex at the origin, and let
\overline{\Gamma}^{+}
denote its closure. For any nonzero vector
V=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we consider the elementary symmetric functions
\sigma_{k}
,
(1\leq k\leq N)
defined by:

\sigma_{k}(V)=\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq N}\mu_{i_{1}}\cdots\mu_{i_{k}}.

(2.1)

In particular, we note the special cases:

(i)

\sigma_{1}(V)=\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{N}
(the trace);

(ii)

\sigma_{n}(V)=\mu_{1}\cdots\mu_{N}
(the determinant);

(iii)

The quadratic form can be expressed as:

\sigma_{2}(V)=\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}\Big)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}.

(2.2)

Definition 2.1
.

(Gårding cones)
For every
1\leq k\leq\mathbb{N}
, we define the Gårding cone:

\Gamma^{+}_{k}:=\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,\sigma_{j}(V)>0\textrm{ for all }j=1,\cdots,k\}.

These cones satisfy the nested inclusion relation:

\Gamma^{+}_{N}\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma^{+}_{1}\,.

(2.3)

Observe that
\Gamma^{+}_{N}
is the positive orthant, whereas
\Gamma^{+}_{1}
is a half-space in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
containing
\Gamma^{+}_{N}
. It is well established that
\Gamma^{+}_{k}
is an open convex cone in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and that the operator defined by the
k
-th root
\sigma_{k}^{1/k}
is concave on
\Gamma^{+}_{k}
.

For a given constant
\alpha\geq 0
and any vector
V=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
, we define its
\alpha
-shift as

V_{\alpha}=V-\alpha\,\overline{\mu}\,I_{N}=V-\alpha\,(\overline{\mu},\cdots,\overline{\mu})

(2.4)

where
\overline{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}
and
I_{N}=(1,...,1)
is the unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The
\alpha
-perturbed Gårding cone is defined by:

\Gamma^{+}_{k}(\alpha):=\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,V_{\alpha}\in\Gamma^{+}_{k}\}.

(2.5)

Remark 2.2
.

(1) When
\alpha=0
, we recover the original Gårding cones:
\Gamma^{+}_{k}(0)=\Gamma^{+}_{k}
for every
1\leq k\leq N
.

(2) For the perturbed cones, we also have following inclusion chain which analogous to the inclusion chain (
2.3
):

\Gamma^{+}_{N}(\alpha)\subset\cdots\subset\Gamma^{+}_{1}(\alpha)\,.

(2.6)

We now consider another Gårding-type cone

\mathcal{P}_{k}:=\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\,:\,\mu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\mu_{i_{k}}>0\textrm{ for all }1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq N\}.

(2.7)

Remark 2.3
.

When
V
represents the eigenvalues of a matrix in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

(1)
V\in\mathcal{P}_{k}
implies at least
(N-k+1)
positive eigenvalues;

(2)
V\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
implies at least
(N-k+1)
non-negative eigenvalues.

We also have the following inclusion relations:

\Gamma^{+}_{n}=\mathcal{P}_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}\subset\cdots\mathcal{P}_{N-1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{N}=\Gamma^{+}_{1}\,.

(2.8)

The aforementioned cone family, commonly referred to as
k
-
convex cone
, was first introduced by Harvey and Lawson
[
19
]
in their seminal work on
\mathbb{G}
-plurisubharmonicity theory, where they established fundamental results using this framework.

Definition 2.4
.

(
m
-positivity cone).
For any real parameter
1\leq m\leq N
(not necessarily integer), we define the
m
-positivity cone:

\mathcal{P}_{m}=\{V\in\mathbb{R}^{N}:\mu_{i_{1}}+\cdots+\mu_{i_{\lfloor m\rfloor}}+(m-\lfloor m\rfloor)\mu_{i_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}}>0\textrm{ for all }1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}\leq N\}.

For integer values
m=k\in\mathbb{N}
, the definition reduces to the standard
k
-
convex cone

\mathcal{P}_{k}
. The following inclusion relations hold:

(i)

\mathcal{P}_{\lfloor m\rfloor}\subset\mathcal{P}_{m}
for all
1\leq m\leq N
;

(ii)

\mathcal{P}_{m}\subset\mathcal{P}_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}
for all
1\leq m<N
;

(iii)

\mathcal{P}_{m_{1}}\subset\mathcal{P}_{m_{2}}
for any
1\leq m_{1}\leq m_{2}\leq N
(cf. Lemma
2.5
below).

The closure
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m}
, called the
m
-nonnegativity cone, satisfies analogous properties.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
1\leq k_{1}<k_{2}\leq N
and
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
. It holds that

\frac{1}{k_{1}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+(k_{1}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{k_{2}}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor+1}\Big)\,.

Proof.

After restating the original inequality as

k_{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+(k_{1}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}\Big)\leq k_{1}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor+1}\Big)\,.

Case 1.
Assume
\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor=\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor=k
.
The inequality reduces to

(k_{2}-k_{1})\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mu_{i}\leq(k_{2}-k_{1})k\mu_{k+1}.

This holds trivially due to the non-increasing property of
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
.

Case 2.
When
\lfloor k_{2}]\geq\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1
, it suffices to prove

k_{1}\sum_{i=\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}^{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+k_{1}(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor+1}\geq(k_{2}-k_{1})\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor}\mu_{i}+k_{2}(k_{1}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}\,.

(2.9)

By the non-increasing property of
\{\mu_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and

k_{1}(\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)+k_{1}(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{2}\rfloor)=(k_{2}-k_{1})\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+k_{2}(k_{1}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)=k_{1}(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\,,

we have

\displaystyle\textrm{LHS of }\,\eqref{monotone}\geq

\displaystyle\,k_{1}(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}\,,

\displaystyle\textrm{RHS of }\,\eqref{monotone}\leq

\displaystyle\,k_{1}(k_{2}-\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor)\mu_{\lfloor k_{1}\rfloor+1}\,.

Combining these two inequalities, yields the desired inequality (
2.9
).

V_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}=V-\alpha_{\epsilon}\,\overline{\mu}\,I_{N}=V-\alpha_{\epsilon}\,(\overline{\mu},\cdots,\overline{\mu}),

where
I_{N}=(1,...,1)\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
\bar{\mu}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}
. For every integer
1\leq k\leq N-1
, Yang and Zhang
[
36
]
considered the shifted vector
V_{\alpha_{k}}
with

\alpha_{k}=\frac{1}{N}\Big(1-\sqrt{\frac{k}{(N-1)(N-k)}}\Big).

They proved the following non-negatively of cone
\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{k})
.

Proposition 2.7
.

(
[
36
, Lemma 2.4]
)
For
1\leq k\leq N-1
, if
V\in\overline{\Gamma}^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{k})
, then

\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\cdots+\mu_{k}\geq 0.

Moreover, exactly one of the followings holds.

(1)
\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}+\cdots+\mu_{k}>0
.

(2)
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\cdots=\mu_{k}=0
and
\mu_{k+1}=\mu_{k+2}=\cdots=\mu_{N}\geq 0
.

For any constant
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we establish the following inclusion relation through an approach distinct from others.

Proposition 2.8
.

For every
0<\epsilon<1
, we have the inclusion relation:

\overline{\Gamma}^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})\subset\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m_{\epsilon}}\,.

(2.11)

Proof.

The proof consists of several steps.

Step 1.
Fix an arbitrary vector
V
with
V_{\alpha_{\epsilon}}\in\overline{\Gamma}^{+}_{2}
. Through direct computation, we establish

\begin{split}2\sigma_{2}(V_{\alpha_{\epsilon}})&=\big(\sigma_{1}(V_{\alpha_{\epsilon}})\big)^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\mu_{i}-\alpha_{\epsilon}\overline{\mu})^{2}\\
&=(1-N\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}\overline{\mu}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\big(\mu_{i}^{2}-2\alpha_{\epsilon}\overline{\mu}\mu_{i}+\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}\overline{\mu}^{2}\big)\\
&=\big[(1-N\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}+2\alpha_{\epsilon}+N\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}\big]\overline{\mu}^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}\,.\end{split}

For the first term, using the definition of
\alpha_{k}
, we derive

\begin{split}&(1-N\alpha_{\epsilon})^{2}+2\alpha_{\epsilon}+N\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}\\
&=N(N-1)\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}-2(N-1)\alpha_{\epsilon}+1\\
&=N(N-1)\big[\alpha_{\epsilon}^{2}-\frac{2}{N}\alpha_{\epsilon}+\frac{1}{N(N-1)}\big]\\
&=N(N-1)\big[(\alpha_{\epsilon}-\frac{1}{N})^{2}+\frac{1}{N^{2}(N-1)}\big]\\
&=N(N-1)\big[\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{N^{2}}+\frac{1}{N^{2}(N-1)}\big]\\
&=\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}\,.\end{split}

This establishes the fundamental inequality

\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}\overline{\mu}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}\,.

(2.12)

Step 2.
We first assume
\overline{\mu}=0
.
Since
\sigma_{2}(V_{\alpha_{\epsilon}})\geq 0
, the inequality (
2.12
) forces
\mu_{i}=0
for all
i
, implying
V=0
. Thus
V\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m_{\epsilon}}
trivially holds. Consequently, we may assume
\overline{\mu}>0
in subsequent arguments.

Step 3.
We demonstrate that any vector
V=(\mu_{1},\cdots,\mu_{N})
satisfying both
\overline{\mu}>0
and (
2.12
) belongs to
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{m_{\epsilon}}
.

Proof of Claim.

Let
m=m_{\epsilon}
and assume without loss of generality that
\mu_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{N}
.
It is sufficient to prove

c_{0}:=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\mu_{i}+\big(m-\lfloor m\rfloor\big)\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}\geq 0\,.

(2.13)

Decompose the right-hand side of (
2.12
) using the index
[m]

T_{1}:=\sum_{i=1}^{[m]}\mu_{i}^{2}+\big(m-[m]\big)\mu_{[m+1]}^{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{ and }\hskip 10.00002ptT_{2}:=\sum_{i=[m+2]}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}+\big([m+1]-m\big)\mu_{[m+1]}^{2}.

Denote vectors in
\mathbb{R}^{N-\lfloor m\rfloor}
:

\alpha=(1,\cdots,1,\sqrt{[m+1]-m})\,,\hskip 10.00002pt\beta=(\mu_{[m+2]},\cdots,\mu_{N},\sqrt{[m+1]-m}\mu_{[m+1]}).

These satisfy

|\alpha|^{2}=N-m\,,\hskip 20.00003pt|\beta|^{2}=T_{2}\,,\hskip 20.00003pt\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle=\overline{\mu}-c_{0}.

Applying the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality
|\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle|^{2}\leq|\alpha|^{2}|\beta|^{2}
yields:

T_{2}\geq\frac{(\overline{\mu}-c_{0})^{2}}{N-m}=\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}(\overline{\mu}-c_{0})^{2}.

Substituting into (
2.12
) gives:

\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}\overline{\mu}^{2}\geq T_{1}+\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}(\overline{\mu}-c_{0})^{2}.

Consequently, we obtain the key inequality

\frac{2+2(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}\overline{\mu}c_{0}\geq T_{1}+\frac{1+(N-1)\epsilon^{2}}{N}c_{0}^{2}\geq 0.

(2.14)

The non-negativity of
c_{0}
follows from
\overline{\mu}>0
, establishing (
2.13
).
∎

The preceding arguments complete the proof of Proposition
2.8
.
∎

Let us now proceed to prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollary 1.2.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Proposition
2.8
yields that

\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor}+\big(m-\lfloor m\rfloor\big)\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}\geq 0.

If equality holds, then (
2.14
) implies

T_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor m\rfloor}\mu_{i}^{2}+\big(m-\lfloor m\rfloor\big)\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}^{2}=0,

which necessitates

\mu_{1}=\cdots=\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor}=\big(m-\lfloor m\rfloor\big)\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}=0.

From (
2.12
), we derive

\frac{1}{N-m}\overline{\mu}^{2}\geq\sum_{i=\lfloor m\rfloor+2}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}+\big(\lfloor m\rfloor+1-m\big)\mu^{2}_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}.

When
m\notin\mathbb{N}
, we have
\mu_{\lfloor m\rfloor+1}=0
(since
m\in(1,N-1)
) and
\lfloor m\rfloor+1\in[1,N-1]
, leading to

\begin{split}\frac{1}{N-m}\overline{\mu}^{2}&\geq\sum_{i=\lfloor m\rfloor+2}^{N}\mu_{i}^{2}\\
&\geq\frac{1}{N-(\lfloor m\rfloor+1)}\Big(\sum_{i=\lfloor m\rfloor+2}^{N}\mu_{i}\Big)^{2}\\
&=\frac{\bar{\mu}^{2}}{N-(\lfloor m\rfloor+1)}.\end{split}

This implies
\overline{\mu}=0
, contradicting our initial assumption. Therefore,
m
must be a positive integer.

For
m\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
, we conclude
\mu_{1}=\cdots=\mu_{m}=0
, and by similar reasoning,
\mu_{m+1}=\cdots=\mu_{N}>0
. Furthermore,
\sigma_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})=0
when
c_{0}=0
.
∎

Proof of Corollary 1.2
.

For every
V=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{N})\in\Gamma^{+}_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}}
, i.e., satisfying
\sigma_{2}(V_{\alpha_{\varepsilon}})>0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mu_{i}>0
, Theorem
1.1
implies that
V
must satisfy one of the following conditions.

(i)

Either the inequality

\mu_{1}+\cdots+\mu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor}+\big(m_{\epsilon}-\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor\big)\mu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor+1}>0

holds, meaning
V\in\mathcal{P}_{m_{\varepsilon}}
.

(ii)

Or
m_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
,
\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}=\cdots=\mu_{m_{\epsilon}}=0
, and
\mu_{m_{\epsilon}+1}=\mu_{m_{\epsilon}+2}=\cdots=\mu_{N}>0
. Then we have
\sigma_{2}(\alpha_{\varepsilon})=0
.

Consequently, we conclude that
\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})\subset\mathcal{P}_{m_{\epsilon}}
.

∎

3
Positivity of various curvature operators

3.1
Curvature operator

Let
(X,g)
be a closed
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold (
n\geq 3
), and let
\mathfrak{X}(X)
denote the space of smooth vector fields on
X
. The Riemannian connection
\nabla:\mathfrak{X}(X)\times\mathfrak{X}(X)\to\mathfrak{X}(X)
defines the curvature tensor
R
of the Riemannian metric
g
by

R(U,V)W=\nabla_{V}\nabla_{U}W-\nabla_{U}\nabla_{V}W+\nabla_{[U,V]}W

for all
U,V,W\in\mathfrak{X}(X)
. The Weitzenböck curvature operator, denoted by
\mathfrak{Ric}
throughout this paper, is the zeroth-order curvature term appearing in the classical Weitzenböck formula. For a smooth
k
-tensor
T
on
X
,
\mathfrak{Ric}
is defined as

\mathfrak{Ric}(T)(X_{1},\dots,X_{k})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(R(X_{i},e_{j})T\right)(X_{1},\dots,\underset{\begin{subarray}{c}\uparrow\\
i\text{-th}\end{subarray}}{e_{j}},\dots,X_{k}),

where
\{e_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n}
is a local orthonormal frame of
TX
, and
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}\in\mathfrak{X}(X)
are smooth vector fields. This operator generalizes the classical Ricci tensor, reducing to standard Ricci curvature when applied to vector fields or
1
-forms.

Recall that the Hodge Laplacian is defined as

\Delta_{d}=dd^{*}+d^{*}d.

For a smooth
k
-form
\omega
on
X
, the classical Weitzenböck formula states

\Delta_{d}\omega=\nabla^{*}\nabla\omega+\mathfrak{Ric}(\omega),

where
\nabla^{*}\nabla
denotes the connection Laplacian. When
\omega
is harmonic (i.e.
\Delta_{d}\omega=0
), it satisfies the following Bochner identity

\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{d}|\omega|^{2}=|\nabla\omega|^{2}+\langle\mathfrak{Ric}(\omega),\omega\rangle.

(3.1)

For a detailed introduction on the Weitzenböck curvature operator, see
[
32
, Chapter 9]
.

Recall that the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}\colon\wedge^{2}TX\to\wedge^{2}TX
acting on
2
-forms is defined by

(\mathfrak{R}(\omega))_{ij}=\sum_{k,l=1}^{n}R_{ijkl}\omega_{kl},

Let
\Lambda=(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{N_{1}})
be the ordered eigenvalues of
\mathfrak{R}
. Then the scalar curvature
R
of the metric
g
satisfies (
1.3
). For a fixed
\alpha\in[0,\frac{1}{N})
, we define a shifted vector in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
via

\Lambda_{\alpha}:=\Lambda-\alpha\Big(\frac{R}{2},\frac{R}{2},\cdots,\frac{R}{2}\Big).

Note that
\Lambda_{0}=\Lambda
recovers the original curvature eigenvalues.

(i)

First Shifted Cone Condition
\Gamma^{+}_{1}(\alpha)
:
A metric
g
belongs to
\Gamma^{+}_{1}(\alpha)
if and only if

\sigma_{1}(V_{\alpha})=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\Big(\lambda_{i}-\frac{\alpha R}{2}\Big)=\frac{(1-\alpha N)}{2}R>0.

(3.2)

(ii)

Second Shifted Cone Condition
\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha)
:
The stricter condition
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha)
requires additionally that

\sigma_{2}(V_{\alpha})=\sum_{1\leq i<i\leq N}\Big(\lambda_{i}-\frac{R\alpha}{2}\Big)\Big(\lambda_{j}-\frac{R\alpha}{2}\Big)>0.

(3.3)

We demonstrate that for certain geometrically meaningful values of
\alpha
, the positivity conditions
\sigma_{1}(V_{\alpha})>0
and
\sigma_{2}(V_{\alpha})>0
encode significant geometric information about
(X,g)
.

From equation (
3.2
), we immediately infer that the scalar curvature
R
must be non-negative. Of particular interest is the parameter choice
\alpha_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{N}(1-\epsilon)
, where
\epsilon\in(0,1)
. This leads us to study the curvature positivity condition defined by the shifted cone
\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. If

g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}
of
(X,g)
is
m_{\epsilon}
-positive, i.e.,

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor}+(m_{\epsilon}-\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor)\lambda_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor+1}>0.

Moreover, the Betti numbers of
X
satisfy the following statements.

(i)

When
k-1<m_{\epsilon}\leq k
for some integer
1\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then

b_{p}(X)=0\ {\rm{for\ all}}\ 1\leq p\leq n-1.

(ii)

When
k-1<m_{\epsilon}\leq k
for some integer
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\leq k\leq n-1
, then

b_{1}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-k}(X)=0\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002ptb_{k}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-1}(X)=0.

Proof.

If
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then by Corollary
1.2
, the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}
is
m_{\epsilon}
-positive. Based on the Theorem A in
[
33
]
, we obtain the following topological restrictions:

Case (i).

If
k-1<m_{\epsilon}\leq k
for some integer
1\leq k\leq\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
, then the curvature operator is
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil
-positive (cf. Lemma
2.5
). Therefore, we have
b_{p}(X)=0
for all
1\leq p\leq n-1
.

Case (ii).

If
k-1<m_{\epsilon}\leq k
for some integer
\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil+1\leq k\leq n-1
, then the curvature operator is
k
-positive. Thus, the Betti numbers satisfy

b_{1}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-k}(X)=0\hskip 10.00002ptand\hskip 10.00002ptb_{k}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-1}(X)=0.

Then the proof is completed.
∎

We have the following characterization of spherical space forms via the curvature operator.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. If the metric
g
satisfies the curvature condition
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})
for some
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{(N_{1}-1)(N_{1}-2)}},

then
X
is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form.

Proof.

For every
\epsilon
satisfying
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{(N_{1}-1)(N_{1}-2)}},
we have

m_{\epsilon}=\frac{N_{1}(N_{1}-1)\epsilon^{2}}{1+(N_{1}-1)\epsilon^{2}}\leq 2.

Since
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\alpha_{\epsilon})
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}
is
m
-positive for all
0<m\leq 2
. This particularly implies
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}>0
. Therefore, by the classification theorems of Chen
[
9
]
and Böhmann and Wilking
[
6
]
, the conclusion follows.
∎

3.2
Curvature operator of the second kind

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
\Lambda_{2}=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N_{2}})
denote the ordered eigenvalues of the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
, and let
\{S_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{2}}
be its corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors. For an arbitrary smooth
p
-form
T
and any
S\in S^{2}_{0}(TX)
, the action of
S
on
T
is defined by

(ST)(X_{1},\cdots,X_{p})=\sum_{i=1}^{p}T(X_{1},\cdots,SX_{i},\cdots,X_{p}).

Then the decomposition of the Weitzenböck curvature operator
\mathfrak{Ric}
acting on
T
is given by

\frac{3}{2}g(\mathfrak{Ric}(T),T)=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\nu_{i}|S_{i}T|^{2}+\frac{p(n-2p)}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}g(i_{\mathfrak{Ric}(e_{i})}T,i_{e_{j}}T)+\frac{p^{2}}{n^{2}}R|T|^{2}\,,

(3.4)

as established in
[
26
,
27
]
.
In order to control the first term in (
3.4
) by understanding the interaction of trace-free, symmetric tensors on forms, the authors developed a powerful calculus in
[
26
]
to estimate any finite weighted sums with nonnegative weights.

Definition 3.3
.

(
[
26
, Definition 3.1]
)
Let
\{\omega_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N_{2}}
be the nonnegative weights of any finite weighted sums. Define

\Omega=\max_{1\leq i\leq N_{2}}\omega_{i}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathrm{and}\hskip 10.00002ptS=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\omega_{i}\,.

We call
S
the total weight and
\Omega
the highest weight.

Let
\Lambda_{2}=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N_{2}})
denote the ordered eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{R}
. In what follows, we use the notation
[\mathcal{R},\Omega,S]
to denote the supremum of all finite weighted sums
\sum_{i=1}^{N_{2}}\omega_{i}\nu_{i}
in terms of
\Lambda_{2}
with highest weight
\Omega
and total weight
S
.

Lemma 3.4
.

(
[
26
, Lemma 3.4]
)
Let
\Lambda_{2}=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N_{2}})
denote the ordered eigenvalues of
\mathcal{R}
. Then, for any integer
1\leq m\leq N_{2}
, we have

[\mathcal{R},\Omega,S]\geq(S-m\Omega)\nu_{m+1}+\Omega\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{i}.

Proposition 3.5
.

(
[
26
, Proposition 3.15]
)
Let
T
be a
p
-form with
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Then we have

\frac{3}{2}g(\mathfrak{Ric}(T),T)\geq\Big[\mathcal{R},\frac{n^{2}p-np^{2}-2np+2n^{2}+2n-4p}{n(n+2)},\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\Big]\cdot|T|^{2}.

For notational convenience, for every integer
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
, we introduce a positive coefficient function
C_{p}
as defined in
[
34
]
:

\begin{split}C_{p}&=\frac{3}{2}\frac{n(n+2)p(n-p)}{n^{2}p-np^{2}-2np+2n^{2}+2n-4p}=\frac{3}{2}\frac{n(n+2)}{n+\frac{2n}{p}+\frac{2n-4p}{p(n-p)}}.\\
\end{split}

(3.5)

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
\Lambda_{2}=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N_{2}})
be the ordered eigenvalues of the operator
\mathcal{R}
. Assume that

\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor}+(C_{p}-\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor)\nu_{\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor+1}\geq C_{p}\,\kappa

(3.6)

for some integer
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
, then for any smooth
k
-form
T
on
X
with
p\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
, we have

g(\mathfrak{Ric}(T),T)\geq\kappa k(n-k)|T|^{2}.

In particular, assume that

\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor}+\Big(\frac{3n}{4}-\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor\Big)\nu_{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor+1}\geq\frac{3n}{4}\kappa\,,

(3.7)

then for any smooth
p
-form
T
with
p\leq\frac{n}{2}
, we have

g(\mathfrak{Ric}(T),T)\geq\kappa p(n-p)|\alpha|^{2}.

Proof.

For every
\Omega
and
S
,
[
26
, Lemma 3.3]
and Lemma
3.4
yield the following estimate

\begin{split}[\mathcal{R},\Omega,S]&=\Omega\cdot\Big[\mathcal{R},1,\frac{S}{\Omega}\Big]\\
&\geq\Omega\cdot\Big(\big(\frac{S}{\Omega}-\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor+1}+\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor}\nu_{i}\Big)\\
&=S\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor}\nu_{i}+\big(\frac{S}{\Omega}-\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor\frac{S}{\Omega}\rfloor+1}}{\frac{S}{\Omega}}.\\
\end{split}

(3.8)

Set

\Omega=\frac{n^{2}p-np^{2}-2np+2n^{2}+2n-4p}{n(n+2)}\hskip 10.00002pt\textrm{and}\hskip 10.00002ptS=\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)

so that
\frac{S}{\Omega}=C_{p}
. By the definition of
C_{p}
in (
3.5
), we see that
C_{k}\geq C_{p}
for all
p\leq k\leq\frac{n}{2}
. Then by Lemma
2.5
and (
3.6
), we obtain

\left[\mathcal{R},\frac{n^{2}k-nk^{2}-2nk+2n^{2}+2n-4k}{n(n+2)},\frac{3}{2}k(n-k)\right]\geq\frac{3}{2}k(n-k)\kappa.

This proves the first statement by Proposition
3.5
.

Now we proceed to prove the second statement. It is straightforward to verify that for all
n\geq 3

C_{1}=\frac{3n}{2}\frac{n^{2}+n-2}{3n^{2}-n-4}\leq\frac{3n}{4}\,,\hskip 10.00002ptC_{2}=\frac{3n(n+2)(n-2)}{4n^{2}-6n-8}>\frac{3n}{4}

and
C_{p}
is monotonically increasing in the parameter
p
. We divide the proof into two cases based on
p
:

(i)

If
p=1
. According to
[
26
, Remark 3.16]
, we have the improved estimate

\frac{3}{2}g(\mathfrak{Ric}(T),T)\geq\left[\mathcal{R},\frac{2n-1}{n+2},\frac{3(n-1)}{2}\right]\cdot|T|^{2}.

Set
C^{\prime}_{1}=\frac{3(n-1)}{2}\cdot\frac{n+2}{2n-1}\geq\frac{3n}{4}
. Then by (
3.8
), we obtain

\left[\mathcal{R},\frac{2n-1}{n+2},\frac{3(n-1)}{2}\right]\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor C^{\prime}_{1}\rfloor}\nu_{i}+\big(C^{\prime}_{1}-\lfloor C^{\prime}_{1}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor C^{\prime}_{1}\rfloor+1}}{C^{\prime}_{1}}\,.

Using Lemma
2.5
, we have

\left[\mathcal{R},\frac{2n-1}{n+2},\frac{3(n-1)}{2}\right]\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor}\nu_{i}+\big(\frac{3n}{4}-\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor+1}}{\frac{3n}{4}}\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\kappa\,.

This proves the case
p=1
.

(ii)

If
p\geq 2
. In this setting
C_{p}>\frac{3n}{4}
. Then by (
3.8
) and Lemma
2.5
again, we obtain

\begin{split}&\Big[\mathcal{R},\frac{n^{2}p-np^{2}-2np+2n^{2}+2n-4p}{n(n+2)},\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\Big]\\
&\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor}\nu_{i}+\big(C_{p}-\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor C_{p}\rfloor+1}}{C_{p}}\\
&\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor}\nu_{i}+\big(\frac{3n}{4}-\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor\big)\nu_{\lfloor\frac{3n}{4}\rfloor+1}}{\frac{3n}{4}}\\
&\geq\frac{3}{2}p(n-p)\kappa\,.\end{split}

This proves the case
p\geq 2
.

Consequently, we complete the proof of the corollary.
∎

We now introduce a novel shifted cone construction for Riemannian manifolds based on the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
. Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
, and consider the ordered eigenvalues
\Lambda_{2}=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{N_{2}})
of
\mathcal{R}
. The scalar curvature
R
of the Riemannian metric
g
satisfies (
1.4
). For any non-negative parameter
\beta\geq 0
, we define the
\beta
-shifted curvature operator as follows:

\Lambda_{\beta}:=\Lambda_{2}-\beta\Big(\frac{(n+2)R}{2n},\frac{(n+2)R}{2n},\cdots,\frac{(n+2)R}{2n}\Big).

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. If
X\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\beta_{\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, then the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
is
m_{\epsilon}
-positive, i.e.,

\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor}+(m_{\epsilon}-\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor)\nu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor+1}>0.

Furthermore, the Betti numbers of
X
satisfy the following statements.

(i)

If
m_{\epsilon}\leq\frac{3n}{4}
, then all Betti numbers vanish. Namely,

b_{p}(X)=0,\hskip 10.00002pt{\rm{for\ all}}\ 1\leq p\leq n-1.

(ii)

For any integer
1\leq p\leq\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor
, if
m_{\epsilon}\leq C_{p}(n)
, then

b_{p}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-p}(X)=0.

Proof.

If
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\beta_{\epsilon})
for some
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the
curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
is
m_{\epsilon}
-positive, i.e., there exists a positive constant
\kappa
such that

\nu_{1}+\cdots+\nu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor}+(m_{\epsilon}-\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor)\nu_{\lfloor m_{\epsilon}\rfloor+1}\geq m_{\epsilon}\kappa.

Corollary
3.6
yields the following topological constraints:

(i)

If
m_{\epsilon}\leq\frac{3n}{4}
, then the curvature operator of the second is
\frac{3n}{4}
-positive, and consequently
b_{p}(X)=0
for all
1\leq p\leq n-1
.

(ii)

If
m_{\epsilon}\leq C_{p}
, then
\mathcal{R}
is
C_{p}
-positive, which implies that

b_{p}(X)=\cdots=b_{\lfloor\frac{n}{2}\rfloor}(X)=b_{\lceil\frac{n}{2}\rceil}(X)=\cdots=b_{n-p}(X)=0.

This ends the proof of the theorem.
∎

Finally, we have the following characterization of spherical space forms via the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Riemannian manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
. If
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\beta_{\epsilon})
for some
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{(N_{2}-1)(N_{2}-3)}},

then
X
is diffeomorphic to a spherical space form.

Proof.

For every
\epsilon
satisfying
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{3}{(N_{2}-1)(N_{2}-3)}},
we have

m_{\epsilon}=\frac{N_{2}(N_{2}-1)\epsilon^{2}}{1+(N_{2}-1)\epsilon^{2}}\leq 3.

Since
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\beta_{\epsilon})
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the curvature operator of the second kind
\mathcal{R}
is
m
-positive for all
0<m\leq 3
, and in particular,

\nu_{1}+\nu_{2}+\nu_{3}>0.

The conclusion then follows from a result of Li
[
24
, Theorem 1.1]
, which establishes that Riemannian manifolds with
3
-positive curvature operator of the second kind must be spherical space forms.
∎

3.3
Induced Kähler curvature operator

Recall that for a Kähler manifold
(X,g)
of complex dimension
n
, the curvature operator
\mathfrak{R}
vanishes on the orthogonal complement of the holonomy algebra
\mathfrak{u}(n)\subset\mathfrak{so}(2n)
. This motivates us to study the induced Kähler curvature operator
\mathscr{R}:\mathfrak{u}(n)\rightarrow\mathfrak{u}(n)
with ordered eigenvalues
\Lambda_{3}=(\rho_{1},\cdots,\rho_{N_{3}})
, where

N_{3}=n^{2}=\dim\mathfrak{u}(n)\,.

(3.9)

For every
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, define
\gamma_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{N_{3}}(1-\epsilon)
. The
\gamma_{\epsilon}
-shifted vector of
\Lambda_{3}
is given by

\Lambda_{\gamma_{\epsilon}}=\Lambda_{3}-\gamma_{\epsilon}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{3}}\rho_{i},\cdots,\sum_{i=1}^{N_{3}}\rho_{i}\Big).

We say the Riemannian metric
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\gamma_{\epsilon})
if
\Lambda_{\gamma_{\epsilon}}\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}
at every point
x\in X
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Käher manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
. If
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\gamma_{\epsilon})
for some
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{3n-2}{(n^{3}-3n+2)(n^{2}-1)}}
, then
X
has the rational cohomology ring of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.

Proof.

For every
\epsilon
satisfying
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{3n-2}{(n^{3}-3n+2)(n^{2}-1)}},
we have

m_{\epsilon}=\frac{N_{3}(N_{3}-1)\epsilon^{2}}{1+(N_{3}-1)\epsilon^{2}}\leq 3-\frac{2}{n}.

Since
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\gamma_{\epsilon})
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the Kähler curvature operator
\mathscr{R}
is
m
-positive for all
0<m\leq 3-\frac{2}{n}
, and in particular

\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}+\Big(1-\frac{2}{n}\Big)\rho_{3}>0.

Applying Theorem A of
[
34
]
, it follows that
X
has the rational cohomology ring of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
∎

Similarly, we can also obtain another characterization of
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
via the curvature operator
\mathscr{R}
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
(X,g)
be a compact Kähler manifold of complex dimension
n\geq 2
. If
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\gamma_{\epsilon})
for some

0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{(n^{2}-1)(n^{2}-2)}},

then
X
is biholomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.

Proof.

For every
\epsilon
satisfying
0<\epsilon\leq\sqrt{\frac{2}{(N_{3}-1)(N_{3}-2)}},
we have

m_{\epsilon}=\frac{N_{3}(N_{3}-1)\epsilon^{2}}{1+(N_{3}-1)\epsilon^{2}}\leq 2.

Since
g\in\Gamma^{+}_{2}(\gamma_{\epsilon})
, Theorem
1.1
implies that the Kähler curvature operator
\mathscr{R}
is
m
-positive with
0<m\leq 2
, and in particular
\rho_{1}+\rho_{2}>0
. Note that Kähler manifolds with
2
-positive Kähler curvature operator have positive
orthogonal bisectional curvature, and thus are biholomorphic to
\mathbb{CP}^{n}
.
∎

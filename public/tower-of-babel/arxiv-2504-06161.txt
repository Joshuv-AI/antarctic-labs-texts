A Hom formula for Soergel Modules
Leonardo Patimo

Abstract
We study Soergel modules for arbitrary Coxeter groups. For infinite Coxeter groups, we show that the homomorphisms between Soergel modules are in general more than those coming from morphisms of Soergel bimodules. This result provides a negative answer to a question posed by Soergel in [ Soe07 ] . We further show that the dimensions of the morphism spaces agree with the pairing in the Hecke algebra when Soergel modules are instead regarded as modules over the structure algebra. Moreover, we use this module structure to define a distinguished submodule of indecomposable Soergel bimodules that mimics the cohomology submodule of the intersection cohomology. Combined with the Hodge theory of Soergel bimodules, this can be used to extend results regarding the shape of Bruhat intervals, such as top-heaviness, to arbitrary Coxeter groups.



Introduction

Let
(W,S)
be a Coxeter system and
V
be a representation of
W
. The category of Soergel bimodules, denoted
{\mathbb{S}Bim}
, is the full subcategory of graded bimodules over the polynomial ring
R=\Sym(V)
, generated by direct summands of shifts of Bott–Samelson bimodules

\BS(\underline{s_{1}s_{2}\ldots s_{k}}):=R\otimes_{R^{s_{1}}}R\otimes_{R^{s_{2}}}R\otimes\ldots\otimes_{R^{s_{k}}}R(k)

where
s_{i}\in S
,
R^{s_{i}}
denotes the subring of
s_{i}
-invariants and
(k)
denotes a grading shift. Under certain assumptions on the representation

(cf.
[
Soe07
,
Abe21
]
), indecomposable self-dual Soergel bimodules are parametrized by elements
w\in W
and are denoted by
B_{w}
.

If
W
is a Weyl group and
V
is the geometric realization of
W
over a field
\Bbbk
, then
B_{w}
is isomorphic to
\IH_{T}^{\bullet}(X_{w},\Bbbk)
, the torus equivariant intersection cohomology of the associated Schubert variety
X_{w}
with coefficients in
\Bbbk
.
While the theory of Soergel bimodules can be developed for any Coxeter group, in the general case there is often no known underlying geometric object. Nevertheless, in many aspects these bimodules still behave as if they were the intersection cohomology of some varieties.

When
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R}
, key results from Hodge theory continue to hold for Soergel bimodules. This includes fundamental theorems such as the hard Lefschetz theorem and the Hodge–Riemann bilinear relations
[
EW14
]
, which hold even in the setting of singular Soergel bimodules
[
Pat22b
]
. Moreover, one can carry out further constructions inspired by Hodge theory, such as defining the analogue of the Néron–Severi Lie algebra, in this purely algebraic setting
[
Pat18b
]
.

In this paper we describe another aspect in which
B_{w}
behaves like
\IH_{T}^{\bullet}(X_{w},\Bbbk)
. Just as
\IH_{T}^{\bullet}(X_{w},\Bbbk)
contains the ordinary
T
-equivariant cohomology
\Coh_{T}^{\bullet}(X_{w},\Bbbk)
as a submodule, we show that
B_{w}
contains a distinguished submodule
H_{w}\subset B_{w}
playing an analogous role. The submodule
H_{w}
is graded free as a left
R
-module, and in degree
2d
it has a basis indexed by elements in
W
of length
d
.

We give two equivalent constructions of this submodule.
The first approach uses Fiebig’s framework
[
Fie08
]
which realizes Soergel bimodules as certain sheaves on the moment graph. In this setting, we realize
H_{w}
as the cyclic module over the structure algebra
Z
of the Bruhat graph of
W
generated by the identity.

The second approach uses Libedinsky’s light leaves
[
Lib08
]
. Light leaves form bases of the homomorphism spaces between Bott–Samelson bimodules, and applying them to
1^{\otimes}:=1\otimes 1\otimes\ldots\otimes 1
we also obtain a basis of the bimodule
\BS({\underline{w}})
itself. Light leaves for
{\underline{w}}
are parametrized by sequences
{\underline{e}}\in\{0,1\}^{\ell({\underline{w}})}
. The construction of the light leaves depends then on a decoration of this sequence. For each index
i
we decorate the light leaf with a label
U
we have
s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{2}^{e_{2}}\ldots s_{i-1}^{e_{i-1}}s_{i}>s_{1}^{e_{1}}s_{2}^{e_{2}}\ldots s_{i-1}^{e_{i-1}}
and with a label
D
otherwise.
In this setting, we realize
H_{w}
as the orthogonal, with respect to the intersection form of
\BS({\underline{w}})
, of all non-canonical light leaves, i.e. those light leaves whose decoration contains at least one

.

We show in
3.15
that these two constructions of the bimodule

coincide. Moreover, the submodule

is naturally endowed with a distinguished basis, which corresponds geometrically to the Schubert basis, i.e. the basis given by the fundamental classes of smaller Schubert varieties.

Theorem A
.

The indecomposable Soergel bimodule
B_{w}
has a submodule
H_{w}
which is free as a left (or right)
R
-module with basis
\{\mathcal{P}_{x}\}_{x\leq w}
. In particular, its graded rank over

is

\grrk H_{w}=v^{-\ell(w)}\sum_{x\leq w}v^{2\ell(x)}.

Thus, for any Coxeter groups the dimensions of the graded components of

, which are given by number of elements in

]

Remark 1.3

(B\otimes B_{s})_{Q}^{x}=\{(m\otimes c_{s}+m^{\prime}\otimes\widetilde{c_{s}}\mid m\in B_{Q}^{x},\,m^{\prime}\in B_{Q}^{xs}\}

\displaystyle=\varpi_{s}s(\varpi_{s})\otimes 1-(s(\varpi_{s})+\varpi_{s})\otimes\varpi_{s}-\varpi_{s}\otimes\varpi_{s}+1\otimes\varpi_{s}^{2}

Analogous to the ordinary case, it can be shown that

4
Translation functors on
\overline{Z}
-mod

For
s\in S
, consider the subring
Z^{s}\subseteq Z
. It has a basis
\{\mathcal{P}_{v}\}_{vs>v}
as an
R
-module (cf.
Lemma

2.10
).
Moreover, the ring
Z
is a free
Z^{s}
-module with basis
\{1,\tau_{s}\}
, where
\tau_{s}:=(w(\varpi_{s}))_{w\in W}
.

Let
R_{+}Z
be right ideal of
Z
generated by
R_{+}
, that is
R_{+}Z=\sum_{x\in W}R_{+}\mathcal{P}_{x}
.
We define

\overline{Z}=Z/R_{+}Z=R/R_{+}\otimes_{R}Z\cong\Bbbk\otimes_{R}Z.

(17)

Let
P_{x}:=1\otimes\mathcal{P}_{x}\in\overline{Z}
.
Then
\{P_{x}\}_{x\in W}
is a basis of
\overline{Z}
over
\Bbbk
.

Let
\overline{Z}^{s}:=\Bbbk\otimes_{R}Z^{s}
.
Then
\overline{Z}
is also a free
\overline{Z}^{s}
-module with basis
\{1,\overline{\tau_{s}}\}
, where
\overline{\tau_{s}}:=1\otimes\tau_{s}\in\overline{Z}
.

Proposition 4.1

(cf.
[
Fie08
, Proposition 5.2]
)
.

The two functors
\overline{Z}^{s}
-mod
\rightarrow\overline{Z}
-mod defined by

M\mapsto M\otimes_{\overline{Z}^{s}}\overline{Z}(2)\quad\text{ and }\quad M\mapsto\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}^{s}}(\overline{Z},M)

are equivalent.

The functor
\overline{Z}
-mod
\rightarrow\overline{Z}
-mod given by

M\mapsto M\otimes_{\overline{Z}^{s}}\overline{Z}[1]

is self-adjoint.

Proof.

Let
\{1^{*},\overline{\tau_{s}}^{*}\}
be the basis of
\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}^{s}}(\overline{Z},\overline{Z}^{s})
dual to
\{1,\overline{\tau_{s}}\}
. Since
\deg 1=\deg 1^{*}=0
and
\deg\overline{\tau_{s}}^{*}=-\deg\overline{\tau_{s}}=-2
we have that the map of
\overline{Z}^{s}
-modules

\Psi:\overline{Z}(2)\rightarrow\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}^{s}}(\overline{Z},\overline{Z}^{s})
defined by
1\mapsto\overline{\tau_{s}}^{*}
and
\overline{\tau_{s}}\mapsto 1^{*}
is an isomorphism.
Because
\overline{Z}
is free as a
\overline{Z}^{s}
-module, for any
\overline{Z}^{s}
-module

we have a natural isomorphism of
\overline{Z}
-modules:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}^{s}}(\overline{Z},M)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes_{\overline{Z}^{s}}\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}^{s}}(\overline{Z},\overline{Z}^{s})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M\otimes_{\overline{Z}^{s}}\overline{Z}(2)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi\quad}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\quad\phi(1)\otimes 1^{*}+\phi(\overline{\tau_{s}})\otimes\overline{\tau_{s}}^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi(1)\otimes\overline{\tau_{s}}+\phi(\overline{\tau_{s}})\otimes 1.}
\sim

The second statement now follows since the restriction functor

For a Soergel bimodule
B
we define
\overline{B}=\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B
. This is in a natural way a graded right
R
-module. All graded right
R
-modules arising this way are called
Soergel modules
.
Since
B
is a
Z
-module, then
\overline{B}
is also a a module over
\overline{Z}=Z/R_{+}Z
.

The following proof is based on unpublished notes by Soergel, in which he considers the case of finite Coxeter groups (Soergel’s proof also appears in
[
Ric19
, Proposition 1.10]
).

Theorem 4.2

(Hom formula for Soergel modules)
.

Let
B,B^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{S}Bim}
. Then we have an isomorphism of graded right
R
-modules

\Theta:\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Hom^{\bullet}_{R\otimes R}(B^{\prime},B)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}}(\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B^{\prime},\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B).

defined by
\Theta(z\otimes\phi)(z^{\prime}\otimes b)=zz^{\prime}\otimes\phi(b)
for
z,z^{\prime}\in\Bbbk
,
\phi\in\Hom^{\bullet}_{R\otimes R}(B^{\prime},B)
and
b\in B^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
\phi:B^{\prime}\to B
be a morphism in
{\mathbb{S}Bim}
. By
Theorem

2.15
, it is also a morphism of
Z
-modules, hence the resulting map
\Theta(z\otimes\phi)
is a map of
\overline{Z}
-modules for any
z\in\Bbbk
.

Because every indecomposable bimodule is a direct summand of a Bott–Samelson bimodule (cf.
Theorem

2.16
), it is enough to show the theorem for
B,B^{\prime}
being Bott–Samelson bimodules. Moreover, by adjunction (Proposition
4.1
and
[
Soe07
, Proposition 5.10]
) we can restrict ourselves to the case
B^{\prime}=R
, that is to show

\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Hom^{\bullet}_{R\otimes R}(R,B)\cong\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk\otimes B).

By sending
\phi:R\rightarrow B
to
\phi(1)
we get

\Hom^{\bullet}_{R\otimes R}(R,B)=\Gamma_{{id}}B.

On the other hand, similarly, we obtain

\Hom^{\bullet}_{\overline{Z}}(\Bbbk,\Bbbk\otimes B)\cong\left\{b\in\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B\mid P_{x}\cdot b=0\text{ for all }x\in W\setminus\{{id}\}\right\}=\bigcap_{x\neq{id}}Ann\left(P_{x}\right).

The resulting map
\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{{id}}B\rightarrow\bigcap_{x\neq{id}}Ann(P_{x})\subseteq\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B
is induced by the inclusion
\Gamma_{{id}}B\hookrightarrow B
.

By
[
Abe21
, Corollary 3.13]
, for any
x\in W
the module
\Gamma_{x}B
is free as a left
R
-module. In particular,
\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{{id}}B\subseteq\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B
and thus
\Theta
is injective.

To show that
\Theta
is also surjective, it is sufficient to show that if
b\in\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B
and
b\not\in\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{id}B
, then there exists
x\in W\setminus\{0\}
such that
P_{x}\cdot b\neq 0
.

Fix an enumeration
w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\ldots
of the elements of
W
which refines the Bruhat order.
Let
\Gamma_{\geq h}B
be the subset of elements supported in
\{w_{i}\mid i\leq h\}
.
Let
h\in\mathbb{N}
be such that
b\in\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h}B
and
b\not\in\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h-1}B
. Let
x=w_{h}
.

Multiplication by
\mathcal{P}_{x}
induces an isomorphism of
R
-bimodules

\mathcal{P}_{x}\cdot(-):\Gamma_{\leq h}B/\Gamma_{\leq h-1}B\xrightarrow{\sim}\Gamma_{x}B.

In fact, by
[
Abe21
, Corollary 3.18]
we have
\Gamma_{\leq h}B/\Gamma_{<h}B\cong\Gamma_{\leq x}B/\Gamma_{<x}B
and on
\Gamma_{\leq x}
multiplying by
\mathcal{P}_{x}
is the same as multiplying on the left by
(\mathcal{P}_{x})_{x}=d_{x,x}=p_{x}
, hence its image is
p_{x}\left(\Gamma_{\leq x}B/\Gamma_{<x}B\right)=\Gamma_{x}B
by
[
Abe21
, Proposition 3.19]
.
As a consequence we obtain an isomorphism of right
R
-modules

P_{x}\cdot(-):(\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h}B)/(\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h-1}B)\xrightarrow{\sim}\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{x}B\hookrightarrow\Bbbk\otimes_{R}B.

In particular,
P_{x}\cdot b
if
b\in\left(\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h}B\right)\setminus\left(\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Gamma_{\leq h-1}B\right)
, proving the claim.
∎

Corollary 4.3
.

If
B
is an indecomposable Soergel bimodule, then
\overline{B}=\Bbbk\otimes B
is indecomposable as a
\overline{Z}
-module.

From
Theorem

2.16
, we derive also a formula for the graded dimension of the space of morphisms between Soergel modules:

\grdim\Hom_{\overline{Z}}^{\bullet}(\Bbbk\otimes B,\Bbbk\otimes B^{\prime})=(\overline{[B]},[B^{\prime}])).

(18)

Remark 4.4
.

Assume
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R}
. If
W
is a finite Coxeter group, then the ring
Z\cong R\otimes_{R^{W}}R
(cf.
[
Fie08
, Theorem 4.3]
and
[
Wil11
, Lemma 4.3.1]
). Hence
\overline{Z}\cong\Bbbk\otimes_{R^{W}}R\cong R/R^{W}_{+}
is the coinvariant ring. In particular,
\overline{Z}
is generated in degree
2
and the map
R\rightarrow\overline{Z}
is surjective. Clearly, in this case we can replace
\overline{Z}
by
R
(acting on the right) in the statement of Theorem
4.2
and in (
18
).

5
Counterexamples

In general, for an infinite Coxeter group it is false that

\Bbbk\otimes_{R}\Hom_{R\otimes R}(B,B^{\prime})\cong\Hom_{R}(\Bbbk\otimes B,\Bbbk\otimes B^{\prime}).

(19)

We now discuss two examples where (
19
) fails. Furthermore, in the first example, we illustrate an example of an indecomposable Soergel bimodule
B
such that
\Bbbk\otimes B
is not indecomposable as a right
R
-module.

5.1
A counterexample in the affine Weyl group of type
\widetilde{A}_{2}

Let
\Bbbk=\mathbb{R}
. Let
\widetilde{W}
be an affine Weyl group and let
\mathfrak{h}
be a realization for
\widetilde{W}
in the sense of Kac (as in
[
Ric19
, Proposition 1.1(2)]
).

Let
W\subseteq\widetilde{W}
be the corresponding finite Weyl group and
G
be the corresponding simply-connected semisimple group associated to
W
. Let
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{C}((t))
and
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{C}[[t]]
, and let
\mathcal{G}r:=G(\mathcal{K})/G(\mathcal{O})

denote the affine Grassmannian. Let
\pi_{0}:G(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow G
be the map defined by sending
t\mapsto 0
, and let
B\subset G
be a Borel subgroup of
G
. The group
I=\pi_{0}^{-1}(B)
is called the
Iwahori subgroup
of
G(\mathcal{K})
. The quotient

\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}=G(\mathcal{K})/I

is called the
affine flag variety
of
G
. Let
p:\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}\to\mathcal{G}r
denote the projection map.

Proposition 5.1
.

The fiber bundle
p:\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}r
is topologically trivial, i.e.,
\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}\cong\mathcal{G}r\times G/B
as topological spaces.

Proof.

We sketch the proof here and refer to
[
Pat18a
, §2.3]
for more details

Let
B\subset G
be a Borel subgroup of
G
and
T\subset B
be a maximal torus. Let
K
be a maximal compact subgroup of
G
and let
T_{\mathbb{R}}=T\cap K
. The Iwasawa decomposition implies that we have a homeomorphism
G/B\cong K/T_{\mathbb{R}}
.
The space
G(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])
is the space of algebraic maps
\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow G
. Let
L_{pol}K
be the subspace of
G(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])
consisting of maps that sends
S^{1}\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{*}
into
K
. We have a subspace
\Omega_{pol}K\subseteq L_{pol}K
of maps that send
1\in S^{1}
to
1\in G
.
Then the inclusion
\Omega_{pol}K\hookrightarrow G(\mathbb{C}[t,t^{-1}])
induces a homeomorphism
\Omega_{pol}K\cong\mathcal{G}r
by
[
PS86
, Theorem 8.6.3]
.
Similarly, the affine flag variety
\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}
can be identified with
L_{pol}K/T_{\mathbb{R}}
, where
T_{\mathbb{R}}=T\cap K
by
[
PS86
, Proposition 8.7.6]
.
We have a
T_{\mathbb{R}}
-equivariant homeomorphism
\Omega_{pol}K\times K\cong L_{pol}K
. By taking the quotients of both sides by
T_{\mathbb{R}}
we obtain a homeomorphism
\Omega_{pol}K\times K/T_{\mathbb{R}}\xrightarrow{\sim}L_{pol}K/T_{\mathbb{R}}
defined by
(x,yT_{\mathbb{R}})\mapsto xyT_{\mathbb{R}}
.
This gives an isomorphism of fiber bundles over
\Omega_{pol}K
:
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{pol}K\times K/T_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces L_{pol}K/T_{\mathbb{R}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Omega_{pol}K}
\sim
p

It follows that the projection
p
is a topologically trivial fiber bundle.
∎

By the Künneth theorem, we obtain

H^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l},\mathbb{R})\cong H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r,\mathbb{R})\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}H^{\bullet}(G/B,\mathbb{R}).

(20)

(see also
[
Lee19
]
for a more detailed description of this isomorphism).

All cohomology and intersection cohomology groups below are taken with coefficients in
\mathbb{R}
.

In
[
Här99
]
Härterich showed that for any
w\in\widetilde{W}
we have
\mathbb{R}\otimes B_{w}\cong\IH^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w})
, where
\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w}=\overline{I\cdot wI/I}\subseteq\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}
is the corresponding Schubert variety and
\IH
denote the intersection cohomology.

Fix
{\overline{w}}\in\widetilde{W}/W
and let
\mathcal{G}r_{{\overline{w}}}\subseteq\mathcal{G}r
be the corresponding Schubert variety. Let
w
be the longest element in the coset
{\overline{w}}
. Then we have
\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w}=p^{-1}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})
.
Since
p
is a topologically trivial fiber bundle, the same holds for the restriction
p:\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w}\rightarrow\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}}
. We have

\IH^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w})\cong H^{\bullet}(G/B)[d]\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})

(21)

where
d=\dim_{\mathbb{R}}(G/B)
. The
\Coh^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l})
-module structure on
\IH^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w})
is given, in terms of the isomorphism (
20
) and (
21
), by
(f\otimes f^{\prime})(g\otimes g^{\prime})=fg\otimes f^{\prime}g^{\prime}
.
It follows that if
\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})
decomposes as a
\Sym(H^{2}(\mathcal{G}r))
-module, then
\Coh^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w})
decomposes as an
R
-module.

Now assume further that the group
G
is simple. Then
\Coh^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r)\cong Z^{W}
and it follows
\Coh^{2}(\mathcal{G}r)
is one-dimensional and it is generated by
P_{u}
, where
u
is the unique simple reflection not in
W
. Therefore
\Sym(H^{2}(\mathcal{G}r))
is isomorphic to the polynomial ring
\mathbb{R}[P_{u}]
, with
\deg(P_{u})=2
.
The primitive decomposition given by the hard Lefschetz theorem for
\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})
is

\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})=\bigoplus_{s\geq r\geq 0}(P_{u})^{r}pIH^{s}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})

(22)

where

pIH^{s}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}}):=\Ker\left(P_{u}^{s+1}:IH^{-s}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})\to IH^{s+2}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})\right).

Note that there are very few Schubert varieties
\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}}
for which we have
\dim\IH^{i}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})\leq 1
for all
i
, and that if
\dim\IH^{i}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})\geq 2
for some
i
then (
22
) shows that
\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{\overline{w}})
cannot be indecomposable as a
\Sym(H^{2}(\mathcal{G}r))
-module.
This describes how to produce many examples of indecomposable Soergel bimodules
B_{w}
such that
\overline{B_{w}}
is decomposable.

The smallest explicit example is as follows:
Let
\widetilde{W}
be the affine Weyl group of type
\widetilde{A}_{2}
, that is,

\widetilde{W}:=\langle s,t,u\rangle
and
m_{st}=m_{tu}=m_{us}=3
.
Let
W
be the subgroup generated by
s,t
and let
w=stutst
, so tht
\overline{w}=stu
. Then
\mathbb{R}\otimes_{R}B_{w}=\IH^{\bullet}(\widehat{\mathcal{F}l}_{w})=H^{\bullet}(G/B)[3]\otimes_{\mathbb{R}}\IH^{\bullet}(\mathcal{G}r_{stu})
, where
G=SL_{3}(\mathbb{R})
.
We have
\dim\IH^{1}(\mathcal{G}r_{stu})\geq\dim H^{4}(\mathcal{G}r_{stu})=2
, since
\Coh^{4}(\mathcal{G}r_{stu})
is generated by
P_{su}
and
P_{tu}
.
Hence the Soergel module
\mathbb{R}\otimes_{R}B_{w}
is decomposable as an
R
-module.

5.2
A counterexample in the universal Coxeter group of rank 3

The following is another smaller counterexample to (
19
) where we can see algebraically in more detail what happens.
Let
W
be the universal Coxeter group of rank
3
, i.e.,
W=\langle s,t,u\rangle
with
m_{st}=m_{tu}=m_{us}=\infty
. Let
{\underline{w}}=stustu
and consider the bimodule
\BS({\underline{w}})
.

For
{\underline{e}}\in\{0,1\}^{6}
let
c_{{\underline{e}}}
be the string basis element defined as in (
9
). Consider the element

b:=c_{000011}-c_{000101}+c_{000110}-c_{001010}+c_{001100}-c_{010001}-2c_{010010}+

+c_{011000}-c_{010100}+c_{100001}-c_{100010}-c_{101000}+c_{110000}\in\BS({\underline{w}}).

The element
b
has degree
2
.
The coefficient of
\mathbf{H}_{{id}}
in the Kazhdan–Lusztig basis element
[B_{w}]
is
v^{6}+v^{4}
. Hence, the submodule
\Gamma_{id}\BS({\underline{w}})
lies in degree
\geq 4
.
Then
b\not\in\Gamma_{id}(\BS({\underline{w}}))
but it can be checked that the projection
\overline{b}\in\overline{\BS({\underline{w}})}
belongs to
Ann(R_{+})
.
It follows that the map
\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\otimes_{R}\BS({\underline{w}})
defined by
1\mapsto\overline{b}
is a map of right
R
-bimodules which does not arise from any bimodule map
R\rightarrow\BS({\underline{w}})
. Thus, the map
\Theta
from
Theorem

4.2
is not surjective onto
R
-module morphisms.
The correctness of this counterexample can be verified via a Sagemath worksheet
[
Sag24
]
available at
https://lpatimo.github.io/Counterexample.ipynb
.

Remark 5.2
.

These two counterexamples discussed above allow us to negatively answer a question posed by Soergel in
[
Soe07
, Remark 6.8]
. In general, for infinite Coxeter groups, there may exist no non-zero function
c_{y}\in R\otimes R
homogeneous of degree
2\ell(y)
such that
c_{y}
is supported on
Gr(\leq y)
and vanishes on
Gr(<y)
. In fact, if such elements
c_{y}\in R\otimes_{\Bbbk}R
existed, we could use them to play the role of
\mathcal{P}_{y}
in
the proof of Proposition
4.2
, and this would imply the isomorphism (
19
).

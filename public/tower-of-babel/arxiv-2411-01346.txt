On strict proto-differentiability of set-valued mappings
Helmut Gfrerer

Abstract




Abstract.
We will show that a multifunction is strictly proto-differentiable at a point of its graph if and only if it is graphically strictly differentiable, i.e., the graph of the multifunction locally coincides, up to a change of coordinates, with the graph of a single-valued mapping, which is strictly differentiable at the transformed reference point. This result allows point-based characterizations of strict proto-differentiability in terms of various generalized derivatives. Further we will prove that under strict proto-differentiability the properties of strong metric regularity, metric regularity and strong metric subregularity are equivalent. Finally, under strict proto-differentiability of the subgradient mapping, we provide a novel second-order relation between function values and subgradients for prox-regular functions which constitutes a nonsmooth extension of the trapezoidal rule of numerical integration.

Key words.
Strict proto-differentiability, strict differentiability, graphically Lipschitzian, generalized derivatives, strong metric regularity.

AMS Subject classification.
49J53, 90C31.

Introduction

The notion of strict proto-differentiability was introduced by Poliquin and Rockafellar
[
15
]
in their study of the subgradient mapping of prox-regular functions. It was shown in
[
15
]
that for a prox-regular and subdifferentially continuous function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(-\infty,\infty]
the graph of the subgradient mapping
\partial\varphi
locally coincides, up to a linear transformation of the coordinates, with the graph of the proximal mapping which is a single-valued and locally Lipschitzian mapping in this case. Then it was shown in
[
16
]
that the subgradient mapping for such functions is strictly proto-differentiable if and only if the proximal mapping is strictly differentiable. If the assumption of subdifferential continuity is omitted, the stated properties still hold for some
\varphi
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
.

Since the introduction of strict proto-differentiability in
[
15
]
, most contributions deal only with subgradient mappings for special function classes and its relation with strict twice epi-differentiability, see, e.g.,
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
for some recent papers. Very little investigations have been done for general multifunctions. In the very recent paper
[
8
]
it is shown that for a graphically Lipschitzian and SCD-semismooth
∗
multifunction, the set of all points belonging to its graph where strict proto-differentiability fails to hold, is negligible with respect to some Hausdorff measure.

Strict proto-differentiability has its roots in the notion of strictly smooth sets as introduced by Rockafellar
[
17
]
. We will show that a strictly smooth set coincides locally around the reference point, up to a permutation of the coordinates, with the graph of a strictly differentiable single-valued function, which implies in particular that the set is a Lipschitz manifold in the sense of
[
17
]
. This property carries over to strict proto-differentiability of multifunctions and allows its characterization by several generalized derivatives together with the graphical Lipschitz property. These characterizations can be used to show that under strict proto-differentiability and some condition on the dimension of the graphic Lipschitz property, the properties of strong metric regularity, metric regularity and strong metric subregularity are equivalent.

We will also consider the special case of the subgradient mapping of prox-regular functions. Besides various characterizations of strict proto-differentiability by means of
\varphi
-attentive generalized derivatives as introduced in the very recent paper
[
4
]
, we also provide a new second-order relationship between function values and subgradients.

The paper is organized in the following way. In Section 2, which is divided into several subsections, we provide all the necessary definitions together with some easy consequences and well-known results. In Section 3 we state the characterizations of strictly smooth sets and strict proto-differentiability of mappings together with the equivalences between various regularity properties. The final Section 4 is devoted to the subgradient mapping of prox-regular functions.

Basic definitions and preliminary results

2.1
Variational geometry and strictly smooth sets

Definition 2.1
.

Consider a set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and a point
\bar{z}\in\Omega
.

1.

The tangent cone, the regular (Clarke) tangent cone and the paratingent cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{z}
are given by

\displaystyle T_{\Omega}(\bar{z}):=\limsup_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{\Omega-\bar{z}}{t},\quad\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z}):=\liminf_{{\textstyle{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{z}\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{\Omega-z}{t},\quad T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z}):=\limsup_{{\textstyle{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{z}\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{\Omega-z}{t}.

The set
\Omega
is called geometrically derivable at
\bar{z}
if
T_{\Omega}=\lim_{t\downarrow 0}(\Omega-\bar{z})/t

and
\Omega
is said to be strictly smooth at
\bar{z}
if

\lim_{{\textstyle{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{z}\atop t\downarrow 0}}}\frac{\Omega-z}{t}

exists, i.e., when
\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
.

2.

The regular normal cone and the limiting normal cone to
\Omega
at
\bar{z}
are given by

\displaystyle\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z}):=\big(T_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\big)^{\circ},\quad N_{\Omega}(\bar{z}):=\limsup_{z\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits^{\Omega}\bar{z}}\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(z).

The set
\Omega
is called normally regular at
\bar{z}
if
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
.

It is easy to see that the inclusions
\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\subset T_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\subset T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})

always hold.
Further,
\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
is a closed convex cone and
T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
is a closed cone having the property

T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=-T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
.
Thus,
\Omega
is strictly smooth at
\bar{z}
if and only if

\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})(=T_{\Omega}(\bar{z}))

(2.1)

is a subspace.

Clearly,
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\subset N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
. Further, if
\Omega
is locally closed at
\bar{z}
then the tangent-normal relation

\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\circ}

(2.2)

holds, cf.
[
20
, Theorem 6.28]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that the set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is locally closed at
\bar{z}
. Then
\Omega
is strictly smooth at
\bar{z}
if and only if both
T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
and
N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
are subspaces satisfying

T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\perp}=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z}).

(2.3)

Moreover, if
\Omega
is strictly smooth at
\bar{z}
then
\Omega
is normally regular at
\bar{z}
.

Proof.

If
\Omega
is strictly smooth, then
T_{\Omega}^{P}(\bar{z})
is a subspace and, by polarizing (
2.1
) and taking into account (
2.2
), we obtain that

T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\circ}=T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\perp}=T_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\circ}=\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\circ}={\rm conv\,}N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})

is a subspace. Since
\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\subset N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
, we conclude that
{\rm conv\,}N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=\widehat{N}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\perp}
is a subspace and
\Omega
is normally regular at
\bar{z}
. Conversely, if
T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
and
N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
are subspaces satisfying (
2.3
), by polarizing (
2.3
) and using (
2.2
) we obtain that
T^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\perp}=N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})^{\circ}=\widehat{T}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
showing that
\Omega
is strictly smooth at
\bar{z}
.
∎

2.2
Generalized differentiation and strict proto-differentiability of set-valued mappings

Definition 2.3
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping and let
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
.

1.

The strict derivative
D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
, the graphical derivative
DF(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
and the limiting (Mordukhovich) coderivative
D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y}):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
are given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}^{P}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),\quad\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\}.

2.

The mapping
F
is called graphically regular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is normally regular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

3.

The mapping
F
is called proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
if
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is geometrically derivable at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

4.

The mapping
F
is called strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
if
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is strictly smooth at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

In case when
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a single-valued mapping the notation can be simplified by omitting the second argument, e.g., we will write
DF(\bar{x})
instead of
DF(\bar{x},F(\bar{x}))
, etc.

In view of (
2.1
),
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})
if and only if

\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=T^{P}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})(=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x},\bar{y}))

(2.4)

is a subspace.

The definition of strict proto-differentiability goes back to
[
15
]
, where it was introduced in a somewhat different way. In
[
15
]
, a set-valued mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
was defined to be strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}\in F(\bar{x})
, if the set-valued mappings

\triangle_{t,x,y}F:u\mapsto\frac{F(x+tu)-y}{t}

graphically converge if
t\downarrow 0
and
(x,y)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. By substituting
x^{\prime}=x+tu
we see that

\mathrm{gph}\,\triangle_{t,x,y}F=\{\frac{(x^{\prime}-x,y^{\prime}-y)}{t}\,\mid\,(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F\}=\frac{\mathrm{gph}\,F-(x,y)}{t}

and hence this definition of strict proto-differentiability from
[
15
]
coincides with the one of Definition
2.3
. However, there is also another definition of strict proto-differentiability by Rockafellar
[
18
]
, which is equivalent to the condition
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
, cf.
[
18
, Proposition 2.7]
. In view of (
2.4
) we obtain that strict proto-differentiability in the sense of Definition
2.3
implies this property in the sense of
[
18
]
, but the reverse implication does not hold in general. E.g., any mapping
F
with closed convex graph having nonempty interior satisfies
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(y,y)=\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
,
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, but
\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(x,y)
is not a subspace for
(x,y)\in{\rm bd\,}\mathrm{gph}\,F
. A more subtle example is given by the mapping
F(x):=\{\pm x^{2}\}
,
x\in\mathbb{R}
at
(0,0)
. Then easy calculations show that
T_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(0,0)=\widehat{T}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(0,0)
equals to the subspace
\mathbb{R}\times\{0\}
, but
T^{P}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(0,0)=\mathbb{R}^{2}
.

We now want to state the counterpart of Lemma
2.2
for strictly proto-differentiable mappings.

Defining the
(n+m)\times(n+m)
orthogonal matrix

S_{nm}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&-I_{m}\\
I_{n}&0\end{pmatrix},

the graph of the limiting coderivative can also be expressed as

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=S_{nm}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y}).

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping having locally closed graph at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
. Then
F
is strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
\bar{y}
if and only if both
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
are subspaces satisfying

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{\perp}\}.

Moreover, in this case
F
is graphically regular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
,
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is strictly smooth at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
if and only if
T^{P}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big(=\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})\big)
and
N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
are subspaces satisfying

N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{\perp}
, which in turn is equivalent to the condition that
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=S_{nm}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})
are subspaces and

\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})=S_{nm}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=S_{nm}T^{P}_{\mathrm{gph}\,F}(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{\perp}=\{(y^{*},x^{*})\,\mid\,(x^{*},-y^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x},\bar{y})^{\perp}\}.

From Lemma
2.2
we may also conclude that
F
is graphically regular at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.
∎

Let

where

. Further, we denote by
{\cal Z}_{nm}

. By

, a sequence

Given a subspace

adjoint
subspace. Then
(L^{*})^{*}=L

Definition 2.5
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a mapping.

1.

F
is called graphically smooth at
(x,y)\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
and of dimension
d
in this respect, if

is a
d

\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Further we denote by
\tilde{\cal O}_{F}
the set of all points from the graph of
F
, where

is graphically smooth. The set
{\cal O}_{F}
is defined as the subset of
\tilde{\cal O}_{F}
where
F
is graphically smooth of dimension

.

2.

The SC derivative

.

The SC derivatives

2.3
On strict differentiability of single-valued mappings

First we collect some basic facts about single-valued mappings.

Lemma 2.7
.

Consider a mapping
F:U\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
, where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open, and let
\bar{x}\in U
.

(i)

If
F
is strictly continuous at
\bar{x}
and
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x})
is a subspace, then
\dim\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x})=n
and
D_{*}F(\bar{x})
is a linear mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

(ii)

If
F
is strictly continuous at
\bar{x}
and
\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x})
is a subspace, then
\dim\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}F(\bar{x})=m
and
D^{*}F(\bar{x})
is a linear mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{m}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(iii)

If
F
is calm at
\bar{x}
and
\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x})
is a subspace, then
\dim\mathrm{gph}\,DF(\bar{x})=n
and
DF(\bar{x})
is a linear mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

Proof.

ad (i): By strict continuity of
F
there is some neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
\bar{x}
along with some real
\kappa\geq 0
such the
\|F(x)-F(x^{\prime})\|\leq\kappa\|x-x^{\prime}\|
,
x,x^{\prime}\in U^{\prime}
. By the definition of the strict derivative, there holds
(u,v)\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x})
if and only if there are sequences
x_{k}\to\bar{x}
,
u_{k}\to u
and
t_{k}\downarrow 0
such that

(F(x_{k}+t_{k}u_{k})-F(x_{k}))/t_{k}\to v
. Since
\|F(x_{k}+t_{k}u_{k})-F(x_{k})\|/t_{k}\leq\kappa\|u_{k}\|
, we conclude that
\|v\|\leq\kappa\|u\|
. Hence,
\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}F(\bar{x})
cannot contain elements of the form

2.4
On graphically Lipschitzian and semismooth
∗
mappings

The following definitions will play an important role.

Definition 2.10
.

(i)

A set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a Lipschitz manifold of dimension
d
around
\bar{z}\in\Omega
if there is an open
neighborhood
W
of
\bar{z}
and a one-to-one mapping
\Phi
between
W
and an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\Phi
and
\Phi^{-1}
continuously differentiable, such that
\Phi(\Omega\cap W)
is the graph of a Lipschitz continuous mapping
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n-d}
, where
U
is an open set in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
d
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
if
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is a Lipschitz manifold of dimension
d
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

(iii)

A mapping
F:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called graphically strictly differentiable at
(\bar{x},\bar{y})\in\mathrm{gph}\,F
, and of dimension
d
in this respect, if
\mathrm{gph}\,F
is a Lipschitz manifold of dimension
d
around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
and, using the notation from (i), the mapping
f:U\to\mathbb{R}^{n+m-d}
is strictly differentiable at
\bar{u}
, where
(\bar{u},f(\bar{u}))=\Phi(\bar{x},\bar{y})
.

Thus, a mapping
F
is graphically Lipschitzian (graphically strictly differentiable) at some point belonging to its graph, if, up to some change of coordinates, the graph of
F
can be locally identified with the graph of a Lipschitzian (strictly differentiable) single-valued mapping.

Remark 2.11
.

1.

The definition above of a Lipschitz manifold is due to Rockafellar
[
17
]
. However, the reader should be aware that there are also other definitions of Lipschitz manifolds in the literature, see, e.g.,
[
14
]
.

2.

Our notion of graphical strict differentiability of a mapping

is called graphical smoothness in
[
12
]
. Thus, graphical smoothness in the sense of
[
12
]
is a much stronger property than graphical smoothness in the sense of our Definition
2.5
. Further, Rockafellar
[
17
]
defined a set

to be smooth at

\Omega
is geometrically derivable at
\bar{z}
and

is a subspace. Hence, if

[

is graphically smooth at

in the sense of our Definition

We start with the following proposition.

Proposition 3.1

Assume that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

\Omega

and an open neighborhood

is the graph of a Lipschitzian mapping

\bar{y}

Proof.

Consider an
n\times d

whose columns form a basis for
T_{\Omega}(\bar{z})
. Then the row rank of

is also

and we can find an
n\times n
permutation matrix

such that the last
d

QZ
are linearly independent. Thus, if we partition the
n\times d

QZ
into matrices

and
B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

B
is nonsingular. Consider the mapping
G:\mathbb{R}^{n-d}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{d}

and we claim that

\displaystyle N_{\mathrm{gph}\,G}(\bar{x},\bar{y})=Q^{-T}N_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=QN_{\Omega}(\bar{z})=\big(QT^{P}_{\Omega}(\bar{z})\big)^{\perp}={\rm rge\;}\big(AB^{-1},I_{d}\big)^{\perp}={\rm rge\;}\big(I_{n-d},-B^{-T}A^{T}\big),

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}G(\bar{x},\bar{y})=S_{n-d,d}N_{\mathrm{gph}\,G}(\bar{x},\bar{y})={\rm rge\;}\big(B^{-T}A^{T},I_{n-d}\big).

Thus, for every element
(u,0)\in\mathrm{gph}\,D_{*}G(\bar{x})(\bar{x},\bar{y})

and for every

we have

and we conclude from Theorem

that

is strongly metrically regular around
(\bar{x},\bar{y})
. Thus there exists an open neighborhood

of

and a Lipschitzian mapping

such that
\mathrm{gph}\,f=\mathrm{gph}\,G^{-1}\cap(V\times U)
. By defining the permutation matrix

\tilde{Q}:=\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{d}\\
I_{n-d}&0\end{pmatrix}Q

and the orthogonal transformation
\Phi(z)=\tilde{Q}z
,
we obtain that

\mathrm{gph}\,f=\mathrm{gph}\,G^{-1}\cap(V\times U)=\Phi(\Omega)\cap(V\times U)=\Phi\big(\Omega\cap\Phi^{-1}(V\times U)\big)

and, since

is an open neighborhood of
\bar{z}
, it follows that
\Omega
is a
d
-dimensional Lipschitz manifold. Since
\Omega
is strictly smooth,

and consequently

.

4
Strict proto-differentiability of the subgradient mapping

Given an extended real-valued function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}:=(-\infty,\infty]
and a point
\bar{x}\in\mathrm{dom}\,\varphi:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\varphi(x)<\infty\}
, the
regular subdifferential
of
\varphi
at
\bar{x}
is given by

\widehat{\partial}\varphi(\bar{x}):=\Big\{x^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\mid\,\liminf_{x\to\bar{x}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\langle x^{*},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|x-\bar{x}\|}\geq 0\Big\},

while the
limiting (Mordukhovich) subdifferential
is defined by

\partial\varphi(\bar{x}):=\{x^{*}\,\mid\,\exists x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}\bar{x},x_{k}^{*}\to x^{*}\mbox{ with }x_{k}^{*}\in\widehat{\partial}\varphi(x_{k})\ \forall k\},

where
x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}\bar{x}
denotes
\varphi
-attentive
convergence, i.e.,
x_{k}\mathop{\rightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}\bar{x}\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ x_{k}\to\bar{x}\mbox{ and }\varphi(x_{k})\to\varphi(\bar{x}).

An lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is called
prox-regular
at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}

if
\varphi
is finite at
\bar{x}
with
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
and there
exist
\epsilon>0
and
r\geq 0
such that

\varphi(x^{\prime})\geq\varphi(x)+\langle x^{*},x^{\prime}-x\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x^{\prime}-x\|^{2}

whenever
x^{\prime}\in{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})
and
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap({\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}}))
with
\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\epsilon
.
The function
\varphi
is called
subdifferentially continuous
at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
if for every sequence
(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\phi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
we have
\varphi(x_{k})\to\varphi(\bar{x})
. In absence of subdifferential continuity we cannot expect that
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
is locally closed and we should work with a
\varphi
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, which is the multifunction
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}:\mathbb{R}^{m}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
defined by

\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}=\{(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi\cap{\cal B}_{\epsilon}(\bar{x})\times{\cal B}_{\epsilon}({\bar{x}^{\ast}})\,\mid\,\varphi(x)<\varphi(\bar{x})+\epsilon\}.

It is well-known
[
15
, Theorem 4.7]
, that
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is graphically Lipschitzian of dimension
n
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, whenever
\varphi
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
with parameters
r\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
. As transformation mapping
\Phi
one can take
\Phi(x,x^{*}):=(x+\lambda x^{*},x)
with
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{r})
and the corresponding Lipschitz mapping
f
is given by the
proximal mapping

f(u):=P_{\lambda}(u):=(I+\lambda\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon})^{-1}(u).

As an alternative transformation mapping one can take
\tilde{\Phi}(x,x^{*})=(x+\lambda x^{*},x^{*})
yielding the Lipschitzian mapping

\tilde{f}(u):=(u-P_{\lambda}(u))/\lambda.

(4.18)

Moreover
\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is locally closed at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, cf.
[
4
, Lemma 3.4]
. Having these properties in mind, straightforward application of Corollary
3.3
yields characterizations of strict proto-differentiability of
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
by means of generalized derivatives of
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
. However, the mapping
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
depends on the parameter
\epsilon
of prox-regularity and we often do not know it a priori. But there exists the remedy of introducing
\varphi
-attentive generalized derivatives which differ from their ordinary counterparts by considering
\varphi
-attentive convergence defined as

(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\ :\Longleftrightarrow\ (x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\mbox{ and }\varphi(x_{k})\to\varphi(\bar{x}).

Definition 4.1

(cf.
[
4
]
)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
be a function and
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
.

1.

The
\varphi
-attentive tangent cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is given by

T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\left\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,\exists t_{k}\downarrow 0,\ (x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):(u,u^{*})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})-(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}{t_{k}}\right\}.

2.

The
\varphi
-attentive regular normal cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined as
\widehat{N}^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\big(T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\big)^{\circ}
and the
\varphi
-attentive limiting normal cone to
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
amounts to

N^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\mathop{{\rm Lim}\,{\rm sup}}_{(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}\widehat{N}^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*}).

3.

The
\varphi
-attentive graphical derivative
D_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
and the
\varphi
-attentive limiting coderivative
D_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
of
\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
are the set-valued mappings from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to the subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
given by

\displaystyle\mathrm{gph}\,D_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}),\ \mathrm{gph}\,D_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{(u,u^{*})\,\mid\,(u^{*},-u)\in N^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*})\}.

4.

Let
{\cal O}_{\partial\varphi}^{\varphi}
denote the collection of all points
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi
such that
T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*})
is an
n
-dimensional subspace. Then the
\varphi
-attentive SC derivative of
\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined by

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{L\in{\cal Z}_{nn}\,\mid\,\exists(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\subset{\cal O}^{\varphi}_{\partial\varphi},(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):d_{{\cal Z}}\big(L,T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x_{k},x_{k}^{*})\big)\to 0\}.

Finally the adjoint
\varphi
-attentive SC derivative of
\partial\varphi
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is defined by

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}}):=\{L^{*}\,\mid\,L\in{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}.

If the lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameter
\epsilon>0
and
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
denotes the
\varphi
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
then for every
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
there holds

\displaystyle T^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*})=T_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}}(x,x^{*}),\ \widehat{N}^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*})=\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}}(x,x^{*}),\ N^{\varphi}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(x,x^{*})=N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}}(x,x^{*}),

\displaystyle D_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(x,x^{*})=D\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}(x,x^{*}),\ D_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(x,x^{*})=D^{*}\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}(x,x^{*}),

\displaystyle\emptyset\not={\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(x,x^{*})={\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}(x,x^{*})={\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(x,x^{*})={\mathcal{S}}^{*}\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}(x,x^{*}),

cf.
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
.
Putting everything together we obtain from Corollary
3.3
the following statement.

Corollary 4.2
.

Assume that the lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameter
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
denote the
\varphi
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. Then the following statements are equivalent:

(i)

\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is graphically strictly differentiable at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
.

(iii)

\mathrm{gph}\,D_{\varphi}^{*}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is a subspace.

(iv)

\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is graphically regular at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
, i.e.,
\widehat{N}_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}^{\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=N_{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}^{\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
.

(v)

{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is a singleton.

In every case there holds
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}\partial\varphi(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})={\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}^{*}\partial\varphi(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\{\mathrm{gph}\,D_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}=\{\mathrm{gph}\,D^{*}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}
.

If, in addition,
\varphi
is subdifferentially continuous at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
, then
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
can be replaced by
\partial\varphi
.

Strict proto-differentiability of
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
for prox-regular functions
\varphi
is equivalent with the so-called property of strict twice epi-differentiability of
\varphi
, cf.
[
15
, Theorem 6.1]
. We will now state another second-order relation between
\varphi
and
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that the lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightrightarrows\mathbb{R}^{n}
is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial\varphi(\bar{x})
with parameters
r\geq 0
and
\epsilon>0
and let
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
denote the
\varphi
-attentive
\epsilon
-localization of
\partial\varphi
around
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
.

(i)

If
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is either proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
or semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
then

\lim_{(x,x^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\frac{1}{2}\langle x^{*}+{\bar{x}^{\ast}},x-\bar{x}\rangle}{\|(x,x^{*})-(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\|^{2}}=0.

(4.19)

(ii)

If
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
then

\lim_{{\textstyle{(x,x^{*}),(y,y^{*})\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{\varphi}^{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial\varphi}(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\atop(x,x^{*})\not=(y,y^{*})}}}\frac{\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)-\frac{1}{2}\langle y^{*}+x^{*},y-x\rangle}{\|(y,y^{*})-(x,x^{*})\|^{2}}=0.

(4.20)

Proof.

Since we are interested only in local properties of
\varphi
and
\partial\varphi
, we may add to
\varphi
the indicator function of some compact neighborhood of
\bar{x}
so that

\varphi(x)\geq\varphi(\bar{x})+\langle{\bar{x}^{\ast}},x-x^{*}\rangle-\frac{r}{2}\|x-\bar{x}\|^{2},\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Consider the
Moreau envelope

e_{\lambda}(u):=\min_{x}\{\varphi(x)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}\|x-u\|^{2}\}

for fixed
\lambda\in(0,\frac{1}{r})
. It is well known
[
15
, Theorem 4.4]
that there exists an open convex neighborhood
U
of
\bar{u}=\bar{x}+\lambda{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
such that the proximal mapping
P_{\lambda}
is single-valued and Lipschitz on
U
and
e_{\lambda}
is continuously differentiable on
U
with Lipschitzian gradient

\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)=(u-P_{\lambda}(u))/\lambda
.

ad (i): We will show in a first step that

\lim_{u\to\bar{u}}\frac{e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)+\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}))(u-\bar{u})}{\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}}=0.

(4.21)

Consider for arbitrary
u\in U
the function
\psi_{u}:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}
given by
\psi_{u}(t)=\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}+t(u-\bar{u}))(u-\bar{u})
. Then

e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})=\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0){\rm d}t.

(4.22)

Assume first that
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
. It follows from
[
20
, Exrecise 13.45]
that
\nabla e_{\lambda}
is semidifferentiable at
\bar{u}
, which means that the directional derivatives

d(h):=\lim_{h^{\prime}\to h,\ t\downarrow 0}\frac{\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}+th^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})}{t},\ h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

exist. Consider arbitrary
\epsilon>0
and choose
\delta>0
such that
{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})\subset U
and for every
h\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\|h\|=1
there holds

\left\|\tau d(h)-(\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}+\tau h)-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}))\right\|\leq\tau\epsilon,\tau\in[0,\delta].

(4.23)

For arbitrarily fixed
u\in{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})
,
u\not=\bar{u}
and
t\in[0,1]
we have

\displaystyle\|\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0)

\displaystyle-t(\psi_{u}(1)-\psi_{u}(0))\|\leq\left\|\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0)-t\|u-\bar{u}\|d\left(\frac{u-\bar{u}}{\|u-\bar{u}\|}\right)(u-\bar{u})\right\|

\displaystyle+t\left\|\|u-\bar{u}\|d\left(\frac{u-\bar{u}}{\|u-\bar{u}\|}\right)(u-\bar{u})-(\psi_{u}(1)-\psi_{u}(0))\right\|\leq 2t\epsilon\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2},

where we have used the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and (
4.23
) with
h=(u-\bar{u})/\|u-\bar{u}\|
,
\tau=t\|u-\bar{u}\|
and
\tau=\|u-\bar{u}\|
. Thus, by virtue of (
4.22
) we obtain that

\displaystyle|e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})-\frac{\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})}{2}(u-\bar{u})|=|\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0){\rm d}t-\frac{\psi_{u}(1)-\psi_{u}(0)}{2}|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0)-t(\psi_{u}(1)-\psi_{u}(0)){\rm d}t|\leq\int_{0}^{1}2t\epsilon\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}{\rm d}t=\epsilon\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}

and we conclude that (
4.21
) holds.

Now assume that
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is semismooth
∗
at
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
. By taking into account (
4.18
), it follows from
[
8
, Proposition 4.2]
that
\nabla e_{\lambda}
is semismooth
∗
at
\bar{u}
. By Proposition
2.15
, for arbitrarily fixed
\epsilon>0
we can find
\delta>0
such that
{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})\subset U
and

\|\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-A(u^{\prime}-\bar{u})\|\leq\epsilon\|u^{\prime}-\bar{u}\|,u^{\prime}\in{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u}),\ A\in{\rm conv\,}\overline{\nabla}\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime}).

For fixed
u\in{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})
, by Rademacher’s theorem, the set
[0,1]\setminus D_{\psi_{u}}
has Lebesgue measure
0
and for every
t\in D_{\psi_{u}}
we have
\psi_{u}^{\prime}(t)=\langle A(u-\bar{u}),u-\bar{u}\rangle
for some
A\in{\rm conv\,}\overline{\nabla}\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}+t(u-\bar{u}))
by
[
2
, Theorems 2.3.10, 2.6.6]
implying
|\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0)-t\psi_{u}^{\prime}(t)|\leq\epsilon t\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}
. Integration by parts yield

\int_{0}^{1}t\psi_{u}^{\prime}(t){\rm d}t=1\cdot\psi_{u}(1)-0\cdot\psi_{u}(0)-\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{u}(t){\rm d}t=\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)(u-\bar{u})-(e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u}))

and, together with (
4.22
) we obtain that

\displaystyle|\big(e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})(u-\bar{u})\big)+\big(e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)(u-\bar{u})\big)|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{1}\psi_{u}(t)-\psi_{u}(0)-t\psi_{u}^{\prime}(t){\rm d}t|\leq\int_{0}^{1}t\epsilon\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}{\rm d}t=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\|u-\bar{u}\|^{2}

implying (
4.21
) also in this case.

For every pair
(x,x^{*})\in\mathrm{gph}\,\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
sufficiently close to
(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
we have
u:=x+\lambda x^{*}\in U
,
(x,x^{*})=(P_{\lambda}(u),\nabla e_{\lambda}(u))
and
e_{\lambda}(u)=\varphi(x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}
. Since
\|u-\bar{u}\|\leq\|x-\bar{x}\|+\lambda\|x^{*}-{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\|
, (
4.19
) follows from (
4.21
) by using the identity

\displaystyle e_{\lambda}(u)-e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})-\frac{\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)+\nabla e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})}{2}(u-\bar{u})

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|x^{*}\|^{2}-\varphi(\bar{x})-\frac{\lambda}{2}\|{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\|^{2}-\frac{x^{*}+{\bar{x}^{\ast}}}{2}(x+\lambda x^{*}-\bar{x}-\lambda{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\varphi(x)-\varphi(\bar{x})-\frac{x^{*}+{\bar{x}^{\ast}}}{2}(x-\bar{x}).

ad (ii): By
[
16
, Theorems 4.1, 4.2]
, the mapping
\mathcal{T}^{\varphi}_{\epsilon}
is strictly proto-differentiable at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}
if and only if
\nabla e_{\lambda}
is strictly differentiable at
\bar{u}
. For arbitrary
\epsilon>0
consider
\delta>0
such that
{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})\subset U
and
\|\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)-(u^{\prime}-u)^{T}\nabla^{2}e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})\|\leq\epsilon\|u^{\prime}-u\|
,
u^{\prime},u\in{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})
. Then for arbitrarily fixed
u^{\prime},u\in{\cal B}_{\delta}(\bar{u})
we have

\displaystyle|e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-e_{\lambda}(u)-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)(u^{\prime}-u)-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u))(u^{\prime}-u)|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{1}(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u+t(u^{\prime}-u))-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u))(u^{\prime}-u){\rm d}t-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u))(u^{\prime}-u)|

\displaystyle=|\int_{0}^{1}\big(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u+t(u^{\prime}-u))-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)-t(u^{\prime}-u)^{T}\nabla^{2}e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})\big)(u^{\prime}-u){\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad-\frac{1}{2}\big(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-\nabla e_{\lambda}(u)-(u^{\prime}-u)^{T}\nabla^{2}e_{\lambda}(\bar{u})\big)(u^{\prime}-u)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{1}\epsilon t\|u^{\prime}-u\|^{2}{\rm d}t+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon\|u^{\prime}-u\|^{2}=\epsilon\|u^{\prime}-u\|^{2}

implying

\lim_{u^{\prime},u\to\bar{u},u^{\prime}\not=0}\frac{e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})-e_{\lambda}(u)-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla e_{\lambda}(u^{\prime})+\nabla e_{\lambda}(u))(u^{\prime}-u)}{\|u^{\prime}-u\|^{2}}=0.

Now similar arguments as we used in (i) verify (
4.20
)
∎

Remark 4.4
.

We call formulas (
4.19
), (
4.20
) trapezoidal rule because for differentiable
\varphi
we have

\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)=\int_{0}^{1}\nabla\varphi(x+t(y-x))(y-x){\rm d}t\approx\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi(x)+\nabla\varphi(y))(y-x)

by the well-known trapezoidal rule of numerical integration, see, e.g.
[
1
]
.

Remark 4.5
.

After submitting this paper, Rockafellar
[
19
]
published a manuscript where he introduced the so-called generalized quadratic bundle
{\rm quad\,}\varphi(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
for an lsc function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}
which is prox-regular at
\bar{x}
for
{\bar{x}^{\ast}}\in\partial f(\bar{x})
. Since it can be shown that
{\mathcal{S}}_{\varphi}(\partial\varphi)(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})=\{\mathrm{gph}\,\partial q\,\mid\,q\in{\rm quad\,}\varphi(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})\}
, the list of equivalences in Corollary
4.2
can be extended by the statement that
{\rm quad\,}\varphi(\bar{x},{\bar{x}^{\ast}})
is a singleton.

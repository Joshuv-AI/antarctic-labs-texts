Improvement in the quality of the water-saving and drought-resistant rice ‘Huhan 1516’ via molecular markers
Authors: Yanan Wang, Ao Li, Anning Zhang, Deyan Kong, E Li, Lei Wang, Yi Liu, Xingxing Luo, Mengchen Li, Lijun Luo, Feiming Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo improve the rice quality of the water-saving and drought-resistant rice variety ‘Huhan 1516’ and cultivate high-yield, high-quality new lines of water-saving and drought-resistant rice. In this study, we used ‘Meixiangzhan 2’, which carries the flavour gene Badh2, as the donor parent and ‘Huhan 1516’, which contains the rice blast resistance gene Pi2, as the recipient parent and comprehensively utilized molecular marker-assisted selection (MAS) and the WDR 6K microarray detection system for selection. In addition, resistance identification and rice quality testing were conducted. A total of four improved lines of ‘Huhan 1516’ (YN13, YN14, YN15, and YN16) were ultimately selected. The results show that the resistance to bacterial blight in the improved strain significantly increased, while the resistance to rice blast and agronomic traits were comparable to those of ‘Huhan 1516’, with a background recovery rate of 92.6%-95.4%. Compared with that of ‘Huhan 1516’, the content of amylose decreased from 17.8% to 15.4%-16.5%, and the differences in gel consistency and alkali spreading value were not significant. There was a reduction in chalkiness and the chalky grain percentage, with transparency changing from level 2 to level 1. Additionally, the content of the aromatic compound 2AP significantly increased to 0.44–0.71 mg/kg, leading to a marked improvement in eating quality. The successful development of the improved line not only meets the market demands for high-yield, high-quality, water-saving, and drought-resistant rice varieties but also expands the application potential of ‘Huhan 1516’. Furthermore, this study provides theoretical support and material resources for the breeding of high-quality, water-efficient, and drought-tolerant rice.Keywords: MAS, Pi2, Badh2, Rice quality, 2AP

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is the staple food for more than half of the population in China and occupies a central position in the strategy to ensure national food security (Zeng et al. 2022). Rice blast is among the major diseases of rice and poses a major threat to yield stability and quality in rice production in China. Currently, the main method of controlling rice blast during production is the use of chemical pesticides, but chemical control can lead to a series of problems, such as increased costs, environmental pollution and drug resistance (Li et al. 2021). Therefore, breeding rice varieties that are resistant to rice blast is highly important for China’s food security. With the rapid development of molecular biotechnology, molecular marker-assisted selection (MAS) has become a common technical tool for breeding rice lines with excellent traits, among which rice blast resistance genes are more widely used in MAS applications. The Pi2 gene encodes a protein containing an NBS-LRR structural domain, which confers strong broad-spectrum resistance to rice blast (Zhou et al. 2006). On the basis of these important characteristics, optimizing molecular marker-assisted polymerization breeding strategies to effectively polymerize excellent disease resistance genes, such as Pi2, into suitable rice varieties has become a key approach to breeding new varieties with durable and broad-spectrum resistance to rice blast. This pathway is critical for achieving the goal of green control of rice blast in rice.

In recent years, as people’s living standards have continued to improve, they have become increasingly concerned with the flavour quality, aroma, and other traits of rice. Aromatic rice, with its unique aroma qualities and excellent flavour quality, is important in the high-end rice market, and its retail price is usually 30–50% greater than that of ordinary varieties, representing an important direction for promoting the development of high-quality rice. The main compound that produces flavour in aromatic rice is 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2AP), and the biosynthesis of 2AP is controlled by the betaine aldehyde dehydrogenase gene OsBadh2 (Bradbury et al. 2005). Studies have shown that the main Badh2 variant loci carried by aromatic rice varieties are an 8-base deletion and a 3-base mutation in exon 7 (Badh2-E7) and a 7-base deletion in exon 2 (Badh2-E2) (Bradbury et al. 2008; Chen et al. 2008). There are also at least 27 haplotypes at the Badh2 locus (Liang et al. 2023), and at least 23 aroma alleles have been reported thus far. The use of the sequence differences of these loci to design molecular markers for assisting in the selection of new aromatic rice varieties has become an important technical tool in aromatic rice breeding.

Molecular marker-assisted selection technology, combined with agronomic trait screening, can be used to accurately improve target traits through the identification of molecular marker genotypes that are tightly linked (or intragenic) to the target gene. Its characteristic of not being affected by environmental conditions significantly improves the breeding efficiency and shortens the cycle time, and it is widely used in continuous backcross breeding, which is one of the core modern technologies for molecular breeding (Yang et al. 2019). For example, Wang et al. (2023) and others used molecular marker-assisted selection to polymerize the rice blast gene Pi2 in R1179 and the Badh2 gene in Wenxiang 1 to produce the new rice varieties R365 and R403, which are highly resistant to rice blast and have a fragrant flavour. Using molecular markers for sequential backcross breeding.Li (2023) successfully introduced the Pigm gene, which has broad-spectrum resistance to rice blast, and the fgr gene, which regulates flavour, in ‘Nanjing 5718’, which significantly improved the quality and resistance to rice blast in ‘Nanjing 5718’.

‘Huhan 1516’ is a conventional indica-type, water-saving, and drought-resistant rice variety bred by the Shanghai Agricultural Biological Genetic Center. This variety has outstanding characteristics, such as high yield, drought resistance, a grade 3 rice quality, and moderate resistance to rice blast and brown planthoppers (Li et al. 2024); however, it lacks a fragrance, which limits its full application potential. To increase its value, we aimed to improve its grain quality. In this study, through hybridization, multiple generations of backcrossing and selfing, the aroma gene Badh2 was introduced into ‘Huhan 1516’ using MAS. The objective was to obtain improved lines with enhanced grain quality without significant alteration of other traits, thereby providing new germplasm resources for the breeding of high-yield, high-quality, water-saving, and drought-resistant rice varieties.

Materials and methods

Rice material

The parental materials used were the water-saving and drought-resistant rice variety ‘Huhan 1516’ selected by the Shanghai Agrobiological Gene Cente and the high-quality aromatic rice variety ‘Meixiangzhan 2’ selected by the Guangdong Academy of Agricultural Sciences. The control variety for high-temperature tolerance was ‘Fengliangyou 4’. All the experimental materials were planted at the Zhuanghang Comprehensive Experimental Station of the Shanghai Academy of Agricultural Sciences and the Lingshui Experimental Base in Hainan.

Identification of resistance to leaf blight

On August 2024, a resistance evaluation was conducted at the Zhuanghang Experimental Station of the Shanghai Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Each line was planted in 10 rows with 7 plants per row, arranged in a randomized complete block design with three replicates. Inoculation was carried out at the peak tillering stage using the artificial leaf-clipping method. The length of the lesion was determined 14 days after inoculation, and the resistance grade was assessed with reference to the grading standard of Zhang et al.

Identification of resistance to rice blast

The identification of rice blast resistance was performed by a combination of natural induction and artificial inoculation. A natural induction trial was conducted in a rice blast nursery in Jinzhai County, Anhui Province, using CO39 as the susceptible spreader row. Artificial inoculation was carried out at the booting stage of the rice. Five exogenous Magnaporthe oryzae strains (spore concentration: 50 spores/100× field of view) were selected, and each tested material was inoculated via panicle-neck injection (1 mL of spore suspension per panicle), with 20 panicles inoculated per entry. Panicle blast severity was assessed 14 days after inoculation. The resistance classes were classified with reference to the survey method of Ruan (2024).

Identification of drought and heat resistance

The identification of drought resistance was conducted at the Water-Saving and Drought-Resistance Identification Center of the Shanghai Agricultural Biological Gene Center. The methodology for drought resistance assessment adhered to the agricultural industry standard of the People’s Republic of China, NY/T 2863 − 2005 Technical Specification for Drought Resistance Appraisal of Water-saving and Drought-resistant Rice.

A thermotolerance assessment was conducted at the experimental base of the Jiangxi Academy of Agricultural Sciences. Evaluation was performed under dual environments: natural high temperature in the field and simulated high temperature in a glasshouse. The thermotolerance evaluation methodology followed the method described by Song et al. (2024).

Molecular marker detection

To clarify the genotypes of the improved lines, plant genomic DNA was extracted at the seedling stage for PCR amplification. The total volume of the PCR system was 15 µL, including 1 µL of template DNA, 0.5 µL of forward primer, 0.5 µL of reverse primer, 7.5 µL of 2× Taq PCR Master Mix, and 5.5 µL of ddH₂O. The amplification program was set up as follows: predenaturation at 94 °C for 2 min; 32 cycles of denaturation at 94 °C for 30 s, annealing at 55 °C for 30 s, and extension at 72 °C for 30 s; and a final extension at 72 °C for 5 min; and storage at 10 °C. The amplification products were separated by polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis and detected by silver nitrate staining. The genotype of each plant was determined from the electrophoretic bands. Examination of the agronomic traits of improved lines

Nine agronomic traits, namely, spike initiation, plant height, effective number of spikes per plant, spike length, total number of grains per spike, number of grains per spike, fruiting rate, yield per plant, and 1,000-grain weight, were evaluated.

Measurement of rice quality-related indices

Rice quality analysis was performed according to the standards of the Ministry of Agriculture “NY/T83-2017”. The appearance quality of the whole polished rice grains was measured with a Wanshen SC-E tester to determine the aspect ratio, length, width, chalky grain rate and chalky whiteness; the starch viscosity characteristics were measured with a Super3 RVA rapid viscosity analyser (Newport Scientific Company of Australia) with TC software to analyse certain parameters, such as peak viscosity and disintegration value; and the rice flavour characteristics, including hardness and balance values, were measured with a Satake STA1B-CN-type flavour metering score from Japan. The starch viscosity characteristics were determined with a Super3 RVA fast viscometer from Newport Scientific, Australia, and the peak viscosity, disintegration value and other parameters were analysed with supporting TCW software. The taste characteristics of the rice were scored by Satake STA1B-CN (Japan) and included hardness, viscosity and equilibrium values. All the measurements were repeated 3 times, and the average value was used to ensure the reliability of the data. 2-Acetyl-1-pyrroline (2AP) was quantified by gas chromatography‒mass spectrometry (GC‒MS) according to the methods of Zheng et al. (2018).

Genetic background response rate of improved lines

The genetic background response rates of ‘Huhan1516’ and improved lines were analysed by using the WDR 6 K chip.

Results and analysis

Breeding process

In this study, a cross was initially made between the maternal parent ‘Huhan1516’ and the paternal parent ‘Meixiangzhan 2’ in Lingshui, Hainan Province, in 2021. The resulting F₁ progeny were then backcrossed using ‘Huhan1516’ as the recurrent parent. A total of 128 individual plants were obtained and subjected to MAS, leading to the identification of 24 positive homozygous plants in the BC₁F₁ generation. Individuals whose agronomic traits closely resembled those of ‘Huhan 1516’ were selected for a second backcross. Through subsequent MAS and drought tolerance screening, six plants carrying both the target gene and drought tolerance were identified in the BC₂F₁ generation, which were bulk-harvested. Selection for the target gene and drought tolerance was continued in the BC₂F₂ and BC₂F₃ generations. After stable lines were achieved, a preliminary evaluation of comprehensive traits was conducted in Hainan in 2024 at the BC₂F₄ stage, resulting in the selection of 38 improved lines. Further screening was carried out in Shanghai in the same year, ultimately yielding four improved lines with superior grain quality. These lines were designated YN13, YN14, YN15, and YN16 (Fig. 1).

Rice quality performance of the improved lines

Appearance and milling quality

The milling and appearance quality of ‘Huhan 1516’ and its improved lines are presented in Table 6; Fig. 5. In the 2024 Hainan trial, the milling quality of the improved lines was comparable to that of ‘Huhan 1516’, while the chalkiness degree, chalky grain rate, and transparency were superior. Notably, the transparency grade improved from level 2 to level 1. Compared with that of ‘Huhan 1516’, the length‒width ratio of the whole milled rice of YN13, YN14, and YN15 increased significantly, whereas that of YN16 remained similar. In the 2024 Shanghai trial, the head rice yield of YN13 and YN15 decreased significantly compared with that of Huhan 1516. The degree of chalkiness of the improved lines decreased by 31.43% to 57.14%, and the chalky grain percentage decreased by 28.65% to 53.93%. The transparency and length‒width ratio of the whole milled rice were consistent with the results observed in the Hainan trial.

Steam cooking flavour quality

The cooking and eating quality characteristics of the improved lines are presented in Tables 7 and 8, and Fig. 6. Compared with ‘Huhan 1516’, the improved lines showed significant increases in the appearance, taste, and sensory evaluation scores of cooked rice, with YN15 achieving a taste value of 90.5. The hardness of the cooked rice decreased significantly, whereas the stickiness increased, indicating a negative correlation between these two traits. The gel consistency and alkali spreading value of the improved lines were similar to those of ‘Huhan 1516’. With decreasing amylose content, the BDV increased, indicating a negative correlation between them. In contrast, characteristics such as HPV, CPV, SBV, PeT, and PaT decreased and were positively correlated with the amylose content. For YN14, a significant reduction in CPV was observed, whereas the other RVA parameters did not significantly differ. YN16 significantly decreased the PKV and BDV but significantly increased the SBV and PeT. As shown in Table 7, the taste score of cooked rice was positively correlated with BDV and PKV (Table 8) and negatively correlated with SBV, CPV, PeT, and PaT. These results indicate that RVA profile characteristics are significantly correlated with cooked rice palatability. High taste scores are generally associated with high BDV and PKV and low SBV and CPV. In summary, compared with ‘Huhan 1516’, all four improved lines exhibited superior cooking and eating quality.

Genotype identification and genetic composition analysis

The genotype identification results are shown in Fig. 9. The genotype of the Pi2 gene in the improved lines was identical to that in the parental line ‘Huhan 1516’, while the genotype of the Badh2 gene matched that in the parental line ‘Meixiangzhan 2’. These results indicate that all the improved lines carried both the blast resistance gene Pi2 and the fragrance gene Badh2. Furthermore, a genome-wide comparison of single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) between ‘Huhan 1516’ and the improved lines was performed using the WDR 6 K chip array (Fig. 10). The background recovery rates of the improved lines were 92.98%, 92.63%, 93.03%, and 95.43%, respectively, all exceeding 90%, which essentially achieved the expected target.

Discussion

Pyramiding blast resistance and fragrance traits in rice

In research on genetic improvement in rice, high yield and good quality have always been the two core breeding objectives. Marker-assisted selection (MAS) can effectively pyramid agronomic traits controlled by recessive genes that are difficult to detect, overcoming the limitations of traditional phenotypic identification and enabling rapid and accurate direct selection of genotypes (Lü et al. 2022). With increasing temperatures, the resulting biotic and abiotic stresses pose serious threats to rice yield (Luo et al. 2017). Among the various biotic stresses, rice blast is the most devastating disease and significantly affects rice production (Deng et al. 2017). Therefore, breeders have been committed to enhancing resistance to rice blast. In practice, the phenotypic identification of blast resistance still faces considerable challenges: the process is complicated, and the results are highly influenced by year and growing region, resulting in significant variability and poor stability. Owing to these limitations, the level of blast resistance in widely cultivated rice varieties in China is generally low. This situation not only threatens China’s food security but also poses potential risks to the global food supply (Jiang et al. 2015).

Furthermore, the market demand for high-quality aromatic rice continues to grow, a trend that has driven breeders to place increasing emphasis on improving fragrance-related traits. Previous studies have shown that the Badh2 gene is the major gene controlling rice fragrance. It encodes betaine aldehyde dehydrogenase and, when mutated, leads to loss of enzyme activity. This disruption alters the metabolic pathway of γ-aminobutyraldehyde, diverting it towards the synthesis of 1-pyrroline and ultimately resulting in significant accumulation of 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline (2AP), the compound responsible for the aroma of rice (Chen et al. 2006, 2008; Bradbury et al. 2008). Moreover, 2AP is the key volatile compound contributing to the fragrance of rice, and its content is significantly positively correlated with aroma intensity (Buttery et al. 1983). In this study, MAS was employed to screen improved lines carrying the favourable Badh2 allele. Quantitative analysis via gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (GC–MS) confirmed that these lines exhibited aromatic characteristics and contained significantly higher 2AP content than ‘Huhan 1516’. These results not only demonstrate the effectiveness of MAS in improving fragrance traits but also provide a quantifiable indicator for evaluating aroma in future breeding of high-quality fragrant rice varieties.

Limitations and future perspectives

Despite the numerous advantages of the improved lines, the promotion of water-saving and simplified cultivation practices for drought-resistant rice has led to increasingly severe weed infestations, which reduce both yield and grain quality, becoming a major constraint in production. Researchers are currently engaged in breeding herbicide-resistant improved lines by using CRISPR/Cas9-mediated gene editing technology, aiming to develop new varieties that retain their original desirable traits while adapting to simplified cultivation practices with enhanced resistance and quality. Furthermore, although the improved lines exhibit strong resistance to blast and bacterial blight, they may remain susceptible to environmental conditions and other diseases and pests. Next, it is imperative to conduct multiyear and multilocation trials across diverse ecological environments to comprehensively evaluate their resistance to various biotic and abiotic stresses, thereby accelerating the industrialization and application process.

Metric dimension reduction modulus for logarithmic distortion
Dylan J. Altschuler and Konstantin Tikhomirov

Abstract
. Given parameters n n and α \alpha , the metric dimension reduction modulus k n α ​ ( ℓ ∞ ) k^{\alpha}_{n}(\ell_{\infty}) is defined as the smallest k k such that every n n –point metric space can be embedded into some k k -dimensional normed space X X with bi–Lipschitz distortion at most α \alpha . A fundamental task in the theory of metric embeddings is to obtain sharp asymptotics for k n α ​ ( ℓ ∞ ) k^{\alpha}_{n}(\ell_{\infty}) for all choices of α \alpha and n n , with the range α = Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \alpha=\Theta(\log n) bearing special importance. While advances in the theory lead to the upper bound k n α ​ ( ℓ ∞ ) = O ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) k^{\alpha}_{n}(\ell_{\infty})=O(\log n) for α = Θ ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) \alpha=\Theta(\log n) , obtaining a matching lower bound has remained an open problem. We prove that k n β ​ log ⁡ n ​ ( ℓ ∞ ) = Ω ⁡ ( log ⁡ n ) k^{\beta\log n}_{n}(\ell_{\infty})=\Omega(\log n) for every constant β > 0 \beta>0 , thereby closing the long–standing gap and resolving a question from the 2018 ICM plenary lecture of Naor.

Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and main results

Following the seminal dimension reduction result of Johnson and Lindenstrauss
[
8
]
, it has become a core line of research in metric geometry to characterize metric spaces which admit low–distortion embeddings into low–dimensional normed spaces.
The next notion helps formalize the problem:

Definition 1.1

(Metric dimension reduction modulus; see
[
16
]
)
.

Let
n
be a natural number, and let
\alpha\in[1,\infty)
.
Let
X
be a normed space, possibly infinite dimensional.
Denote by
k^{\alpha}_{n}(X)
the minimum
k\in\mathbb{N}

such that every
n

at most
\alpha

k

of
X
(with respect to the metric induced by

f:S\to F_{S}
satisfying

\|f(s)-f(s^{\prime})\|_{X}\leq\|s-s^{\prime}\|_{X}\leq\alpha\,\|f(s)-f(s^{\prime})\|_{X},\quad\mbox{ for all }s,s^{\prime}\in S.\\

The quantity

, particularly with

, plays a crucial role in the theory of metric embeddings and has been commensurately studied in literature, starting with the seminal paper
[
5
]
by Bourgain. Since every finite metric space and every finite-dimensional normed space can be isometrically embedded into
\ell_{\infty}
, it follows that
k^{\alpha}_{n}(\ell_{\infty})
is
the minimum
k
such that every
n
–point metric space can be embedded into some
k
-dimensional normed space
X
with distortion at most
\alpha
. A subset of the works developing bounds on

includes
[

,
9

,

,

,
1

The focus of this article is on the asymptotics of

with
\alpha=\Theta(\log n)

Upon combining this result with the Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma and

1.2.
Overview of the proof

The proof of the main result is based on two key ingredients:

•

First, we employ a specialized model of ultra–sparse, connected random graphs that have slow expansion and, nonetheless, comparable entropy to the uniform random regular model. These will provide the nonembeddable metric spaces.

•

The second ingredient is
a
discretization scheme
for mappings of
n
–point spaces into normed spaces. This discretization is adapted from
[
3
]
, where it was developed for treating the geometric embeddability of uniform random regular graphs. It was suggested by Naor
1
1

1

private communication
that
this discretization scheme can be applied to
the study of the asymptotics of
k^{\alpha}_{n}(\ell_{\infty})
.

Let us describe the first main component in more detail.
While the asymptotic regime of large
\beta
in Theorem
1.2
can be treated using, e.g., uniform 3-regular random graphs, our analysis of
small values of
\beta
hinges crucially on the properties of our specially tailored model of random graphs. In particular, a uniform
\Delta
–regular random graph for
\Delta\geq 3
has too strong of expansion (the volume of vertex neighborhoods grows too fast with increasing radius) for our argument to work when
\beta
is small.
On the other hand, the configuration model for graphs with a prescribed degree sequence with the average degree
2+o(1)
, which one may view as a natural remedy to the aforementioned problem, produces disconnected graphs w.h.p, and either conditioning on being connected or studying metric properties of the giant component of such a graph appears to be a very challenging path to go.

Our approach is instead to generate a graph by constructing a random Hamiltonian
n
–cycle, which ensures connectivity, and then add a partial matching of the appropriate size. Varying the size of the matching allows us fine control over the expansion of the graph. This construction turns out to be highly amenable to analysis, and it also appears to be completely novel specifically in the context of metric embeddings.

Remark 1.3
.

Similar constructions called
Hamiltonian decompositions
, in which the matching is perfect rather than partial, have been considered in the literature for quick, approximate sampling of random 3–regular graphs. We refer the reader to
[
19
]
for discussion of these models and related results.

1.3.
Asymptotic notation

All asymptotics in this paper are with respect to
n
(the size of the the metric space) diverging.
We write
f=O(g)
,
f=o(g)

whenever
f
remains bounded with respect to
g
(respectively, vanishes) as
n\to\infty
. Further,
f=\Omega(g)
whenever
g=O(f)
, and
f=\Theta(g)

if simultaneously
f=O(g)
and
f=\Omega(g)

Section

2
. The discretization is defined in
Section

3
. In
Section

4
, non-embedding of a typical graph under the specialized model is shown by way of union bounding over (a discretization of) all normed spaces and all embeddings. This yields the technical estimate
Theorem

4.4

1.2

2.
A model of ultra–sparse connected random graphs

Here, we introduce a model of sparse
random graphs on
n
vertices. The intuition behind our choice of model is described in Section
1.2
.
For the remainder of this section, let
1\leq M\leq n/2
be an integer parameter that will be chosen later.

2.1.
Matchings

Recall that a matching
\mathcal{M}
on
[n]
is a subset
\mathcal{M}\subset\{\{i,j\}\subset[n]\}
with
e\cap e^{\prime}=\emptyset
for all
e\neq e^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}
.
It will be very useful to take the equivalent, alternative view of matchings as functions
\mathcal{M}:[n]\to[n]\cup\{\emptyset\}
, where
\mathcal{M}(i)=j
if
i
is matched to
j
, and
\mathcal{M}(i)=\emptyset
if
i
is not matched (so that
\mathcal{M}
acts as an involution on
{\rm Image}(\mathcal{M})\setminus\{\emptyset\}
). The purpose of this modified notation is to provide a rigorous framework for describing an edge–exposure filtration on a random matching.

Definition 2.1

(Matchings)
.

Say that a function
\mathcal{M}:[n]\to[n]\cup\{\emptyset\}
is a
matching
if, for all
i\in[n]
with
\mathcal{M}(i)\neq\emptyset
, it holds that
i=\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{M}(i))
. Note that this implies
|\mathcal{M}^{-1}(j)|\leq 1
for all
j\in[n]
. We also define the
size
of a matching
\mathcal{M}
is given by:

|\mathcal{M}|:=\frac{1}{2}\,|\{i\in[n]\,:\,\mathcal{M}(i)\neq\emptyset\}|\,.

Moreover, the set of all matchings on
[n]
of size
M
will be denoted by
\Pi:=\Pi([n],M)
. Finally, define the edge–set of a matching by
E(\mathcal{M}):=\{\{i,j\}\subset[n]\,:\,\mathcal{M}(i)=j\}
.

The advantage of treating matchings as functions becomes apparent when dealing with partially revealed matchings. For any
I\subset[n]
, let
\mathcal{M}|_{I}
denote the restriction of
\mathcal{M}
to the domain
I
.

Definition 2.2

(Partially constructed matchings)
.

Say that a function
\mathcal{M}_{0}:I\to[n]\cup\{\emptyset\}
, with domain
I\subset[n]
, is a
partially constructed matching
if there is some
\mathcal{M}\in\Pi
with
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{M}|_{I}
. Moreover, for any such
\mathcal{M}\in\Pi
satisfying this condition of
\mathcal{M}_{0}=\mathcal{M}|_{I}
, we say that
\mathcal{M}_{0}
is a partial construction of
\mathcal{M}
. The size of a partially constructed matching
\mathcal{M}_{0}
with domain
I
is given by:

|\mathcal{M}_{0}|=\frac{1}{2}\,|\{i\in I\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{0}(i)\neq\emptyset\}\cup\mathrm{Image}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\setminus\{\emptyset\}|\,.

Finally, for any partially constructed matching
\mathcal{M}_{0}
, define the set of partial completions of
\mathcal{M}_{0}
by:

\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0}):=\{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\in\Pi\,:\,\mathcal{M}_{0}\text{ is a partial construction of }\mathcal{M}^{\prime}\}\,.

Note that the formula supplied for
|\mathcal{M}_{0}|
is consistent with the standard notion of the size of a standard partial matching, which corresponds to counting the number of constructed edges. However, let us highlight the key difference between our definition of partially constructed matchings versus the standard notion of partial matchings. As we allow matchings to take on the value
\emptyset
,
\mathcal{M}|_{I}
can determine both edges as well as “non–edges” of
\mathcal{M}
.

Finally, we record some standard facts about uniformly random matchings, translated into our notation.

Lemma 2.3

(Partial completions of matchings)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{0}
be a partially constructed matching with domain
I\subset[n]
and size
|\mathcal{M}_{0}|=k\leq M
. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a uniform random matching from
\Pi
. The following hold:

(1)

Conditioned on
\{\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\}
, it holds that
\mathcal{M}
is uniformly distributed on
\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})
.

(2)

Let
!!
denote odd factorial and define
J:=\{w\in I^{c}\,:\,w\not\in\mathrm{Image}(\mathcal{M}_{0})\}
. Then,

|\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})|=\binom{|J|}{2(M-k)}(2(M-k))!!\,.

(3)

Let
S\subset J
, where
J
is defined above.
If
|S|\leq M
and
|J|>4M
, then for every

\mu\geq 2(M-k)|S|/|J|
, we have

\mathbb{P}\left[|\{w\in S\,:\,\mathcal{M}(w)\neq\emptyset\}|\geq 12\,\mu\,\big|\,\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]<\frac{2}{2^{\mu}}\,.

Remark 2.4
.

Conditioned on
\{\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\}
,
it is natural to expect that the partial completion
\mathcal{M}

would typically “choose”
\Theta(|S|/|J|)\cdot(M-k)
edges
incident to
S
.
The last assertion of the above statement provides
a (one-sided) quantification of that property.

Proof.

We prove the claims in order. By construction of
\mathcal{M}
as a uniform matching, we have for any
A\subset\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{M}\in A\,\big|\,\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]=\frac{\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{M}\in A,\,\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]}{\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]}

\displaystyle=\frac{|A\cap\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})|}{|\Pi|}\times\frac{|\Pi|}{|\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})|}=\frac{|A|}{|\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})|}\,.

In the last equality, we have used the assumption that
A\subset\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})
. This proves the first claim.

Towards developing the second claim, we use the standard view of matchings as collections of vertex–disjoint edges. The map
\mathcal{M}\mapsto E(\mathcal{M})
induces a bijection between
\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})
and the set of possible ways to select
M-k
vertex–disjoint edges from the complete graph on
[n]
, using only vertices from
J\subset[n]
as endpoints. There
\binom{|J|}{2(M-k)}
ways to choose the endpoints of the
M-k
edges, and each such selection yields exactly

\frac{(2(M-k))!}{2^{M-k}(M-k)!}=(2(M-k))!!

unique ways of pairing the
2(M-k)
vertices. This establishes the second claim.

Finally, we turn to the third claim. Reusing the above bijection, the image of the set

\big\{\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\,:\,|\{w\in S\,:\,\mathcal{M}(w)\neq\emptyset\}|\geq t\big\}

is in bijection with the set of possible ways to select
M-k
vertex–disjoint edges from the complete graph on
[n]
, using only vertices from
J\subset[n]
as endpoints, with the additional constraint that at least
t
endpoints are in
S
.

Consider choosing these
2(M-k)
vertices one at a time. By the assumptions that
|J|>4M
and
|S|\leq M
, we have
|J|-2(M-k)>|J|/2
. Thus, for any
i\in[2(M-k)]
, uniformly over all possible choices of the first
i-1
chosen vertices, the fraction of remaining vertices in
|S|
to remaining vertices in
|J|
is bounded above by
2|S|/|J|
. Thus, for any
y>0
, combining the first two claims yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[|\{w\in S\,:\,\mathcal{M}(w)\neq\emptyset\}|\geq y\,\mu\,\,\big|\,\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t\geq y\,\mu}\binom{2(M-k)}{t}\Big(\frac{2|S|}{|J|}\Big)^{t}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t\geq y\,\mu}\Big(\frac{2(M-k)e}{t}\,\frac{2|S|}{|J|}\Big)^{t}\,.

Note the trivial bounds
|S|\leq|J|
and
2(M-k)\leq|J|
. The former holds since
S\subset J
, and the latter is an obvious necessary condition for
\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})
to be non–empty, which is implied by
\mathcal{M}_{0}
being
a valid partially constructed matching. Finally, set
y:=12>4e
and recall
\mu\geq 2(M-k)|S|/|J|
. For all
t\geq\mu\,y
,

\frac{2(M-k)e}{t}\frac{2|S|}{|J|}\leq\frac{2e}{y}<\frac{1}{2}\,.

Returning to the previous computation, the third claim is established:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[|\{w\in S\,:\,\mathcal{M}(j)\neq\emptyset\}|\geq y\,\mu\,\,\big|\,\mathcal{M}\in\Pi(\mathcal{M}_{0})\right]\leq\sum_{t\geq y\,\mu}2^{-t}\leq\frac{2}{2^{\mu}}\,.

∎

2.2.
Graph model

We are ready to introduce the graph model that will be considered for the rest of the article.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a matching of size
M
, drawn uniformly from
\Pi:=\Pi([n],M)
. Let
\bm{\pi}
be a uniform random permutation of
[n]
, independent of
\mathcal{M}
, which generates a uniformly random Hamiltonian cycle
\mathcal{C}=([n],\big\{\{\bm{\pi}(i),\bm{\pi}({i\;\rm{mod}\;n}+1)\},1\leq i\leq n\big\})
.
Define the
(\pi,\mathcal{M})
–measurable random
multigraph

{\bf G}=([n],E(\bm{\pi},\mathcal{M};M))
by

E(\bm{\pi},\mathcal{M};M):=E(\mathcal{M})\cup E(\mathcal{C}),\,

where
\cup
denotes concatenation, i.e., a union in the sense of multisets.

Observe that the multiset
E(\bm{\pi},\mathcal{M};M)

has size
n+M
everywhere on the probability space,
and that the degrees of all vertices of
{\bf G}
take values
2
and
3
.
The principal difference between
{\bf G}
and a
random graph with a prescribed degree sequence (from either the configuration
or uniform model) is that
{\bf G}
is connected
everywhere on the probability space, i.e, the graph distance
on
{\bf G}
is a valid metric. The rest of the section is devoted towards developing the following claim:

Proposition 2.6

(Slow volume growth for vertex neighborhoods)
.

For every
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{10}]
there are
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}>0

and
\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\in\mathbb{N}
depending only on
\varepsilon
with the following
property. Let
n\geq\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
, and let
M
satisfy

n^{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}\leq M\leq n/100
.
Then with probability at least
1-\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})
, the graph

{\bf G}
satisfies:
for every
v\in[n]
and every
t\in[n^{\varepsilon},n^{2\varepsilon}]
,

\min\big\{x>0:\,|\{w\in[n]:\;d_{\bf G}(v,w)\leq x\}|\geq t\big\}\geq\frac{c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\,n\log t}{M},

where
c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\in(0,1/10]
is a universal constant (independent of
\varepsilon
).

The requirements
t\leq n^{2\varepsilon}
and
\varepsilon<.1
are purely for technical convenience and can easily be lifted. The proof idea is to conduct a breadth–first search of the neighborhood of a vertex
v
with an appropriate edge–exposure filtration, and then inductively bound the growth of the neighborhood using martingale concentration bounds. In order to make this rigorous, we introduce some basic conventions:

Definition 2.7

(Convention: vertex neighborhoods)
.

For a graph
G
on
[n]
, define:

\displaystyle B_{G}(v,\ell)

\displaystyle=\{w\in[n]\,:\,d_{G}(v,w)\leq\ell\}\,,

\displaystyle S_{G}(v,\ell)

\displaystyle=\{w\in[n]\,:\,d_{G}(v,w)=\ell\}\,.

Additionally, set
B_{G}(v,0)=S_{G}(v,0)=\{v\}
.

Note in particular that
B_{G}(v,\ell)
is defined as a set of vertices,
not
as an induced subgraph.

Without any attempt to optimize, we define the following parameters:

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=50

\displaystyle c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}

\displaystyle=1/(20C)

\displaystyle\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon/100

\displaystyle\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}

\displaystyle=\min\{n^{\prime}\,:\,\forall\,n>n^{\prime},~\log(n)<n^{\varepsilon/10^{5}}\}\,.

As desired,
\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
and
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
depend only on
\varepsilon
and
c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
is universal.

For
v\in[n]
, let
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(v)
,

, be the filtration generated by
\mathcal{C}
and the radius
\ell-1

in
{\bf G}

\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(v):=\sigma\Big(\mathcal{M}|_{B_{{\bf G}}(v,\ell-1)},\,\bm{\pi}\Big)\,.

As a key feature of this definition, observe that
B_{{\bf G}}(v,\ell)
is
\mathcal{F}_{\ell}(v)
–measurable for every
\ell\geq 1
. Indeed,
\mathcal{M}|_{B_{{\bf G}}(v,\ell-1)}
and
\bm{\pi}

together specify all edges of
{\bf G}
incident to
B_{{\bf G}}(v,\ell-1)
, and we have defined

as the set of vertices at distance at most

from

.
2
2

2

Note that the induced subgraph of
{\bf G}
on

does not have to be

–measurable.

For two vertices
v
and

{\bf G}
, we write
v\sim_{\mathcal{C}}w
to denote that

and

are adjacent in the graph
([n],\mathcal{C})
, i.e.,

\mathcal{C}

W\subset[n]

As a convention, let
{\bf N}_{0}(v):=\{v\}
. We now isolate the main technical component of the proposition:

Let
1\leq M\leq n/100

For any

(2)

Proof of
Proposition

Define the following events:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{v\in[n]}\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v),\quad\ell\geq 1;

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v)

\displaystyle:=\bigcap_{1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell}\left\{|{\bf N}_{\ell^{\prime}}(v)|\leq\max\Big(n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}},\,C\,\frac{M}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}|{\bf N}_{j}(v)|\Big)\right\}\,.

We then have the following inclusions:

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v)

\displaystyle\subset\bigcap_{1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell}\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell^{\prime}}|{\bf N}_{j}(v)|\leq\max\Big((\ell^{\prime}+1)\,n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}},\,\Big(1+C\,\frac{M}{n}\Big)\sum_{j=0}^{\ell^{\prime}-1}|{\bf N}_{j}(v)|\Big)\right\}

(4)

\displaystyle\subset\bigcap_{1\leq\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell}\left\{\sum_{j=0}^{\ell^{\prime}}|{\bf N}_{j}(v)|\leq(\ell^{\prime}+1)\,n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}(1+C\,M/n)^{\ell^{\prime}}\leq(\ell^{\prime}+1)\,n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}\,e^{C\,(M/n)\,\ell^{\prime}}\right\}\,.

Recall our choices for the parameters
C
,
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
,
\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
, and
c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
.
As a consequence of (
3
), for any
v\in[n]
and for any
\ell^{\prime}\leq\ell
, it holds almost surely within the event
\mathcal{E}_{\ell}(v)
:

(5)

|B_{\bf G}(v,\,\ell^{\prime})|\leq 2(\ell^{\prime}+1)^{2}\,\,n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}\,e^{C\,(M/n)\,\ell^{\prime}}\leq 2\,(\ell+1)^{2}\,n^{4\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}\,\exp(\,C\,c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\,\log t)\leq n^{\varepsilon/5},

where we used the bounds
\ell\leq n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}
and
t\leq n^{2\varepsilon}

given by the assumptions on parameters.
In total, a union bound applied to (
2
) over
v\in[n]
and
\ell\in[\lfloor c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}(n/M)\log t)\rfloor]
yields:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\forall v\in[n],\,|B_{\bf G}(v,\,\lfloor c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}(n/M)\log t)\rfloor|\leq n^{\varepsilon}\right]\geq\mathbb{P}\left[\mathcal{E}_{\lfloor c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}(n/M)\log t)\rfloor}\right]

\displaystyle\geq 1-n^{2}\,\exp(-n^{2\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})

\displaystyle\geq 1-\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})\,.

This completes the proof.

∎

Proof of

\displaystyle B_{\bf G}(v,\ell+1)

\displaystyle S_{\bf G}(v,\ell+1)\setminus{\bf N}_{\ell+1}(v)

\displaystyle=\{v\in B_{\bf G}(v,\ell)^{c}\,:\,v\sim_{\mathcal{C}}{\bf N}_{\ell}(v)\}\cup\{v\in B_{\bf G}(v,\ell)^{c}\,:\,v\sim_{\mathcal{C}}S_{\bf G}(v,\ell)\setminus{\bf N}_{\ell}(v)\}\,.

A cycle is 2-regular. For each

\displaystyle|S_{\bf G}(v,\ell+1)\setminus{\bf N}_{\ell+1}(v)|

3.
Construction of the discretization scheme

The goal of this section is to produce a discretization
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:X^{n}\to\mathcal{N}
, where
X
is a given finite-dimensional normed space and
\mathcal{N}
is a discrete subset of the Cartesian product
X^{n}
having both small cardinality and “sufficient density.” The construction presented below can be viewed as a version of the classical net argument with advanced features provided through multiscaling and seeding.
As we noted in the introduction, a related discretization scheme
first appeared in
[
3
]
to deal with
geometrical
embeddings of random graphs. Whereas the discretization presented here is not identical to the one in
[
3
]
, many similarities exist. For that reason, proofs of certain claims closely matching corresponding statements from
[
3
]
are moved to the appendix.

Notation.

For the remainder of this article,
\varepsilon
and
c_{0}
are some fixed parameters, independent of
X
and
n
, that are assumed to be sufficiently small. That is,
\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
c_{0}\leq c_{0}^{\prime}
for some positive universal constants
\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
c_{0}^{\prime}
which do not depend on
X
or
n
. Without making effort to optimize, the following choices suffice:

\displaystyle\varepsilon^{\prime}

\displaystyle=.1

\displaystyle c_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=.01

We also require an integer parameter
L\in[1,n^{1-\varepsilon}]
, which will be fixed in
Theorem

4.4
. As
L
and
X
will only be varied in
Section

4
, one can view
L
and
X
as fixed parameters in
Section

3
; dependencies on
L
and
X
will be suppressed wherever there is no ambiguity in order to lighten notation.
Below, “
n
sufficiently large” means
n\geq n_{0}
for some function
n_{0}:=n_{0}(\varepsilon,c_{0})
. We emphasize that
n_{0}
does not depend on the underlying normed space
X
.

A
d
–dimensional normed space
X
will be always be viewed as the space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
endowed with a norm
\|\cdot\|_{X}
. The dimension
d
of the space will always be assumed to satisfy

(10)

1\leq d\leq n^{\varepsilon/4}.

The unit ball in
X
will be denoted by
B_{X}
. More generally, a ball of radius
r
centered at a point
y\in X
will be denoted by
B_{X}(y,r)
.
Given a point
y\in X
and a non-empty closed subset
A
of
X
,
the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
–projection of
y
onto
A
is any point in
A
with the minimal
\|\cdot\|_{X}
–distance to
y
.
Whenever projections of points onto subsets are not uniquely defined, a representative will be chosen arbitrarily. In what follows,
\mathbf{x}
will always denote an
n
-tuple of points
(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
. Occasionally, we will view
\mathbf{x}
as a multiset (i.e disregard the ordering).

Definition 3.1

(Sparse
n
–tuples)
.

Let
\|\cdot\|_{X}
be any norm in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
\lambda>0
be any parameter (possibly depending on
n
). Say
that a tuple of points
\mathbf{x}
is
\lambda
-sparse
if, for all
i\in[n]
,
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq\lambda
for less than
n^{\varepsilon}
values of
j\in[n]\setminus\left\{i\right\}
.

As a rough interpretation of the above notion, an
n
–tuple is
\lambda
-sparse if for a vast majority of pairs of vectors, the distance between the vectors is greater than
\lambda
. In what follows, it will be crucial to us that the property is essentially preserved even if the threshold
\lambda
is multiplied by a constant. Specifically, the parameter
L
mentioned in passing above, is defined later in such a way that in any ball of radius
72\lambda
, there are at most
Ln^{\varepsilon}
other vectors (see

Definition

3.5
and
Lemma

3.11
below).

Definition 3.2

(Domain)
.

Assume that
X
is a normed space, and let
D,\lambda>0
be parameters
satisfying

(11)

n^{2}\geq D\geq 2\lambda\geq 1.

Let the domain
\Xi(D,\lambda)=\Xi(D,\lambda,X)
of the discretization function be given by

(12)

\Xi(D,\lambda)=\Xi(D,\lambda,X):=\left\{\mathbf{x}\in(B_{X}(0;\,D))^{n}\,:\,\mathbf{x}\text{ is $\lambda$-sparse in $X$}\right\}\,.

The discretization of
X
will be based on a multiscale net-argument with
seeding.
Recall that an
r
-net
on a set
A\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
in
X=(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{X})
is a collection of points
(y_{i})_{i}
so that for all
x\in A
, there exists
y_{i}
with
\|x-y_{i}\|_{X}\leq r
. We will always assume that the points
y_{i}
are contained in
A
for convenience. We introduce the following notation:

Definition 3.3

(Simple nets)
.

Given
r>0
and
y\in X
, define
\mathcal{N}(y,r;c_{0}r)
as an (arbitrary)
c_{0}r
-net in
B_{X}(y;r)
of minimum cardinality. Further, given a parameter
D>0
, let
\mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{N}_{0}(D)
denote an arbitrary
1
-net in
B_{X}(0;D)
of minimum cardinality.

Definition 3.4

(Multiscale net)
.

For every integer
\ell
we use the shorthand

r_{\ell}:=2^{\ell}\,.

Let
X
be a normed space, and let
D,\lambda>0
be parameters satisfying (
11
). Define the
multiscale net

\mathcal{N}=\mathcal{N}(D,\lambda)
as:

\mathcal{N}:=\mathcal{N}_{0}(D)\cup\left(\bigcup_{y\in\mathcal{N}_{0}(D)}\;\bigcup_{\ell=\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1}^{\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil}\mathcal{N}(y,\,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})\right)\,.

In what follows, we will use points from
\mathcal{N}
to approximate locations
of vertices in a graph embedding into
X
. A key technical idea is that it will suffice to roughly maintain the list of closest neighbors to each vertex under this approximate embedding, rather than attempting to maintain the actual pairwise distances between all vertices. This is a significant source of efficiency in the argument. Towards making this precise, we introduce the following definition.

Definition 3.5

(Scale of separation)
.

Given
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
and a parameter

L\in[1,n^{1-\varepsilon}]
,
define the
local scale of separation for
x_{i}
by:

\ell_{i}:=\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x},X)=\min\left\{\ell\in\mathbb{Z}\,:\,\left|\left\{j\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell}\right\}\right|\geq L\,n^{\varepsilon}\right\}\,,\quad 1\leq i\leq n\,.

Informally, for every
i
, the local scale of separation for
x_{i}
is the logarithm of the radius of a ball in
X
centered at
x_{i}
containing about
n^{\varepsilon}
points
x_{j}
. We further write
\ell(\mathbf{x})=\ell(\mathbf{x},X)
for the tuple
(\ell_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

Remark 3.6
.

Note that whenever
\mathbf{x}
is
\lambda
-sparse then necessarily
2^{\ell_{i}}>\lambda
for all
i\in[n]
.
Moreover, whenever
\mathbf{x}\in(B_{X}(0,r))^{n}
for some
r>0
, we must have
\ell_{i}\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2r)\rceil
for all
i
.
In particular, for every
\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}\in\Xi(D,\lambda)

we have
\ell_{i}\in\{\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil\}

for all
i\leq n
.

In the next proposition we define
seeds
, which are
small-cardinality subsets of
\mathbf{x}
that play a key role in the discretization scheme. A tuple
\mathbf{x}
will be embedded into simple (local) nets centered at the seeds.

Proposition 3.7

(Existence of good seeds)
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large (independent of
X
). For any
n
–tuple
\mathbf{x}
, and for each integer
\ell\geq 0
, there exists a multiset
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}:=\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(\mathbf{x})\subset\mathbf{x}
, with
|S_{\ell}|=\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor
and the following property. For any component
x_{i}
of
\mathbf{x}
with
\ell_{i}=\ell
, there exists
y\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
with

\|y-x_{i}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell_{i}}\,.

Proof.

Fix
\mathbf{x}
and
\ell
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
be the collection of
\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor
points selected independently uniformly at random from
\mathbf{x}
(viewed as a multiset). Let
z\sim\mathrm{unif}(\mathbf{x})
. For all
x_{i}\in\mathbf{x}
, we have:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\bigcap_{y\in S_{\ell}}\left\{\|y-x_{i}\|_{X}>r_{\ell}\right\}\right]=\mathbb{P}\left[\|z-x_{i}\|_{X}>r_{\ell}\right]^{\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor}\leq\left(1-\frac{n^{\varepsilon}}{n}\right)^{\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor}

\displaystyle=n^{-\omega(1)}\,.

Taking a union bound over the
n
possible values of
i
, it easily follows that with high probability (in particular, positive probability), our random selection of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
has the desired property. Thus, by the probabilistic method, there must deterministically be some satisfactory realization of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
.
∎

Definition 3.8

(Discretization)
.

Let
D,\lambda>0
be parameters satisfying (
11
), and let
L\in[1,n^{1-\varepsilon}]
. The
discretization scheme
is the map

\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x}):\Xi(D,\lambda,X)\to\mathcal{N}(D,\lambda)^{n}\,,

constructed as follows. For each
\ell\in\{\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil\}
, construct
S_{\ell}
, as defined in
Proposition

3.7
. Let
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}
denote a multiset which is the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
onto
\mathcal{N}_{0}
.
Then, for each
i\in[n]
:

(1)

Let
\hat{s}_{i}
be the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
x_{i}
onto
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell_{i}}
.

(2)

Let
\hat{x}_{i}
be the
\|\cdot\|_{X}
-projection of
x_{i}
onto
\mathcal{N}(\hat{s}_{i},r_{\ell_{i}};c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}})\subset\mathcal{N}
.

Define
\hat{\mathbf{x}}:=(\hat{x}_{1},\dots,\hat{x}_{n})\in\mathcal{N}^{n}
.

Remark 3.9
.

By definition of
S_{\ell_{i}}
, the distance
from
x_{i}
to
S_{\ell_{i}}
is at most
r_{\ell_{i}}
.
Since
\mathcal{N}_{0}
is a
1
–net, the distance from
\hat{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell_{i}}

to
x_{i}
is at most
r_{\ell_{i}}+1
. Thus,

\|x_{i}-\hat{s}_{i}\|_{X}\leq r_{\ell_{i}}+1
,
i\leq n
.
In turn, this implies
\|x_{i}-\hat{x}_{i}\|_{X}\leq c_{0}r_{\ell_{i}}+1
for all
i\leq n
.

Note that the above construction
depends on the underlying normed space
X
. We collect some properties of this discretization.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
n
be sufficiently large (independently of
X
), let

4.
Bi-Lipschitz non-embedding

4.1.
The Banach–Mazur compactum

Given a positive integer
d
, denote by
\mathcal{B}_{d}

the collection of all
d
–dimensional normed spaces
(identified up to isometry), endowed with a submultiplicative
metric

d_{BM}(X,Y):=\inf\limits_{T:X\to Y}\|T\|_{X\to Y}\,\|T^{-1}\|_{Y\to X},\quad X,Y\in\mathcal{B}_{d},

where the infimum is taken over all invertible linear operators
T
,
and where
\|T\|_{X\to Y}
and
\|T^{-1}\|_{Y\to X}
are standard operator
norms for
T:X\to Y
and
T^{-1}:Y\to X
, respectively.
We refer to

[
18
]
for a comprehensive discussion of the concept
and its fundamental role in the study of finite-dimensional
normed spaces.
In this note, we will use the following
metric entropy estimate on the Banach-Mazur compactum:

Lemma 4.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

There is some positive universal constant
C_{\text{BM}}
(satisfying
C_{\text{BM}}\leq 10
) and a
2
-net
\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)
in
\mathcal{B}_{d}
such that:

|\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)|\leq\exp\left\{e^{d\,C_{\text{BM}}}\right\}\,.

4.2.
Robust non-embedding

Given a normed space
X
, an
n
–tuple
\mathbf{x}
of elements of
X
,
a graph
G
on
[n]
,
and a parameter
\alpha
, we will write
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})
whenever
the mapping
i\to x_{i}
,
i\leq n
, satisfies

(13)

d_{G}(i,j)\leq\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq\alpha\,d_{G}(i,j),\quad i,j\in[n],

and, similarly,

G\not\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})
if (
13
) does not hold.
By convention, we will assume that whenever the graph
G

is not connected, (
13
) fails for all choices
of
X,\alpha
, and
\mathbf{x}=(x_{i})_{i=1}^{n}
.

The next proposition provides a non-embedding result
for our random graph model conditioned
on the requirement that the image of the embedding is

\lambda
–sparse.

Proposition 4.2

(Robust non-embedding)
.

There is a universal constant
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}\geq 100
with the following property.
Let
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}\log\log n\leq d\leq C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}^{-1}\log n
and
\log n\leq\alpha\leq\frac{\log^{2}n}{C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}d}
,
and define parameters

\lambda:=\alpha,\quad M:=\Big\lceil\frac{c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\,\varepsilon\,n\log n}{\alpha}\Big\rceil.

Let
X
be any
d
–dimensional normed space,
and let
B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)
denote the set of
d
-dimensional normed spaces having (multiplicative) Banach–Mazur distance at most two from
X
.
Then, letting the random graph
{\bf G}=G({\bm{\pi}},M)
be as in
Definition

2.5
,

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\exists\,(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\in B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)\times\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)\,:\,{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right]\leq n^{-M/2}.

We recall for the reader’s convenience that
\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)
is the set of
\lambda
–sparse
n
–tuples on
B_{X}(0,n^{2}\lambda)
.

Proof.

We will assume that the constant
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}>1000

is sufficiently large (the numerical bound is not necessary for the current proof, but it will be used later in
Theorem

4.4
). Define

L:=2\cdot(300)^{d}.

Fix any
d
–dimensional normed space
X=(\mathbb{R}^{d},\|\cdot\|_{X})
.
For
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)
, let
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
be the set of “long distances” for
\mathbf{x}
, defined as:

{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X):=\left\{\{i,j\}\subset[n]\,:\,\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}>\frac{1}{3}\,\min(r_{\ell_{i}},\,r_{\ell_{j}})\right\}

(where the discretization
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
of
\mathbf{x}
is also constructed in the space
X
).
Observe that
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
depends on
\mathbf{x}
only through
\hat{\mathbf{x}}
and
\ell(\mathbf{x})
. Thus, the first assertion of
Lemma

3.10
, together with our assumptions on
d
, yields:

\displaystyle\left|\left\{{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X)\right\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\left\{(\hat{\mathbf{x}},\ell(\mathbf{x}))\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X)\right\}\right|

(14)

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{4}n\big)^{n}\cdot n^{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\,.

Fix for a moment any
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X)
.
We claim that whenever the edge-set
E(G)

of a graph
G
on
[n]
has a non-empty intersection with
{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
, then for every
X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,2)
,

G\not\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\,.

Indeed, assume for contradiction there exists a pair
\{i,j\}\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
with
\{i,j\}\in E(G)
, and a normed space
X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,2)

such that
G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})
. Then
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X^{\prime}}\leq\alpha
. As
X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,2)
, it follows that
\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}\leq 2\alpha
. By the second assertion of
Lemma

3.10
applied with
t:=2\alpha
,

\|\hat{x}_{i}-\hat{x}_{j}\|_{X}\leq 2\alpha+2+3c_{0}\min(r_{\ell_{i}},r_{\ell_{j}})+4c_{0}\alpha<\frac{1}{3}\min(r_{\ell_{i}},r_{\ell_{j}})\,,

where in the last inequality we used
the bound
\min(r_{\ell_{i}},r_{\ell_{j}})\geq 72\lambda=72\alpha
from Lemma
3.11
.
This implies that
\{i,j\}\not\in{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)
, which supplies the desired contradiction.

Utilizing the proven claim and then applying (
14
), we compute:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\exists\,(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\in B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)\times\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)\,:\,{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)\,:\,E(\bm{\pi},\mathcal{M};M)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]

\displaystyle\quad\quad\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{4}n\big)^{n}\cdot n^{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\,\max\limits_{\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X)}\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[E({\bm{\pi}},\mathcal{M};M)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\,.

Fix any
\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X)
.
Letting
\bm{\pi}
,
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{C}
, and
{\bf G}
be as in
Definition

2.5
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[E({\bm{\pi}},\mathcal{M};M)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[E(\mathcal{C})\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\,\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[E(\mathcal{M})\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right],

As Lemma
3.11
implies
\frac{4}{9}\,r_{\ell_{i}}-2>\frac{1}{3}\,r_{\ell_{i}}
, observe that the third assertion of
Lemma

3.10
yields

(15)

\big|\big\{j\in[n]\setminus\{i\}:\;\{i,j\}\notin{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)\big\}\big|\leq L\,n^{\varepsilon},\quad i\in[n].

In view of
(
15
), since
\mathcal{C}
is a uniform
n
–cycle, it holds for some universal constant
C>0
,

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\big\{\{\bm{\pi}(i),\bm{\pi}({i\;\rm{mod}\;n}+1)\},i\leq n\big\}\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\leq\bigg(\frac{CL\,n^{\varepsilon}}{n}\bigg)^{n}\,.

Similarly, for some other universal constant
C^{\prime}>0

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[E(\mathcal{M})\cap{\mathcal{L}}(\mathbf{x},X)=\emptyset\right]\leq\bigg(\frac{C^{\prime}L\,n^{\varepsilon}}{n}\bigg)^{M}\,.

Combining the above estimates and letting
\tilde{C}=\max(C,C^{\prime})
, we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\exists\,(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\in B_{\text{BM}}(X;\,2)\times\Xi(n^{2}\lambda,X)\,:\,{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right]\leq\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{dn}\,\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{4}n\big)^{n}\,n^{n^{1-\varepsilon/2}}\bigg(\frac{\tilde{C}L\,n^{\varepsilon}}{n}\bigg)^{n+M}.

The result follows after some trivial arithmetical
manipulations of the right–hand side.
∎

4.3.
Proof of the main results

Observe that the proof of the above proposition
does not actually depend on the model of randomness of the graph

{\bf G}
, except for the number of edges it has and the condition
that the edges are “well spread” within
{[n]\choose 2}
.
In particular, replacing
{\bf G}
with the Erdos–Renyi
model
G(n,n+M)
would yield a similar result.
The crucial property of the graph
{\bf G}

that enables us to pass from Proposition
4.2
to the proof of the main result
is a “non–expansion” property about the slow volume growth of metric balls (which completely fails in the Erdos–Renyi setting) stated below
as an immediate corollary of
Proposition

2.6
:

Corollary 4.3
.

There is a universal constant
c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
and two positive constants
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
and

\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
depending only on
\varepsilon
, so that the following holds. Let
n\geq\tilde{n}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}
, and let
M
satisfy

n^{1-\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}\leq M\leq n/2
.
Then with probability at least
1-\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})
, the graph

{\bf G}=G({\bm{\pi}},M)
satisfies:
for every
v\in[n]
,

\Big|\Big\{w\in[n]:\;d_{\bf G}(v,w)\leq\frac{c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\,\varepsilon\,n\log n}{M}\Big\}\Big|<n^{\varepsilon}.

Finally, we obtain the main technical estimate of the article:

Theorem 4.4

(Bi-Lipschitz non-embedding)
.

Let
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}
and
c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}

be as in Proposition
4.2

and
Proposition

2.6
, respectively. Let
(\alpha,D)
be parameters satisfying
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}\log\log n\leq d\leq C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}^{-1}\log n
and
\log n\leq\alpha\leq\frac{\log^{2}n}{C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}d}
. Let
{\bf G}
be as in
Definition

2.5
with the parameter choice:

M:=\Big\lceil\frac{c_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}\,\varepsilon\,n\log n}{\alpha}\Big\rceil\,.

Then,

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\mbox{${\bf G}$
embeds with bi-Lipschitz distortion at most
$\alpha$ into a $d$--dimensional
normed space}\right]\leq 2\,\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}}).

Proof.

In analogy with
Definition

3.1
, say that a graph
G
on
[n]
is
(\lambda,n^{\varepsilon})
-sparse

if for every vertex
v\in[n]
, there are less than

n^{\varepsilon}
vertices
w\neq v
with
d_{G}(v,w)\leq\lambda
.
Define the corresponding event

{\mathcal{D}}:=\big\{{\bf G}\mbox{ is $(\lambda,n^{\varepsilon})$-sparse}\big\},

and observe that, in view of our choice of parameters
and Corollary
4.3
,

\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{D}}\right]\geq 1-\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})

Recall that
\mathcal{B}_{d}
denotes the Banach–Mazur compactum , and
\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)
is the net in
\mathcal{B}_{d}
defined in Lemma
4.1
. Using translational invariance of the metric in a normed space,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,x_{1}=0,\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})\right\}\right]

\displaystyle=\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})+\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,x_{1}=0,\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})\right\}\,\bigg|\,{\mathcal{D}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})+\sum_{X\in\mathcal{\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}}(d)}~\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[~\bigcup_{X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,\,2)}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,x_{1}=0,\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right\}\,\bigg|\,{\mathcal{D}}\right]\,.

Observe that almost surely within the event
{\mathcal{D}}
, for any normed space
X^{\prime}
,
the condition
{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})

necessarily implies that the
n
–tuple
\mathbf{x}
is
\lambda
–sparse. Furthermore, the same condition together with the constraint
x_{1}=0
gives
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}\subset B_{X}(0;\alpha\,n)\subset B_{X}(0;n^{2})
.
Therefore,

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[~\bigcup_{X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,\,2)}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,x_{1}=0,\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right\}\,\bigg|\,{\mathcal{D}}\right]\\
\leq\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[~\bigcup_{X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,\,2)}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X),\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right\}\,\bigg|\,{\mathcal{D}}\right].

An unconditional estimate of the latter event probability
is given in Proposition
4.2
.
Since the probability of
{\mathcal{D}}
asymptotically tends to one,
we obtain

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[~\bigcup_{X^{\prime}\in B_{\mathrm{BM}}(X,\,2)}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(n^{2},\lambda,X),\;{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X^{\prime},\mathbf{x})\right\}\,\bigg|\,{\mathcal{D}}\right]\leq(1+o_{n}(1))n^{-M/2}\,,

and hence

\mathbb{P}_{\bf G}\left[\bigcup_{X\in\mathcal{B}_{d}}~\left\{\exists\,\mathbf{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}\,:\,{\bf G}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\alpha}}{{\hookrightarrow}}(X,\mathbf{x})\right\}\right]\leq\exp(-n^{\tilde{\varepsilon}_{\text{\tiny{\ref{lem:specialgraph}}}}})+(1+o_{n}(1))n^{-M/2}|\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)|\,.

Finally, applying Lemma
4.1
and recalling that
C_{\text{\tiny{\ref{prop:non-embedding}}}}>1000>100\,C_{\text{BM}}
, we crudely compute

n^{-M/2}|\mathcal{N_{\mathcal{B}}}(d)|\leq n^{-\sqrt{n}}\,.

This completes the theorem.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

Note that Theorem

readily implies the main result of this article,

.

Proof of Lemma

. By definition,

using translates of

|\mathcal{N}_{0}|\leq\left(3\,D\right)^{d}\leq(3n)^{2n^{\varepsilon/4}}\,.

Recalling the definition of
\mathcal{N}(y,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})

\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
\ell\geq 0\end{subarray}}|\mathcal{N}(y,r_{\ell};c_{0}r_{\ell})|\leq\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\,.

Recall further that for every

,

\hat{S}_{\ell}
is a submultiset of
\mathcal{N}_{0}
of size
\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor
.
Fix for a moment
any multisets
S_{\ell}^{*}\subset\mathcal{N}_{0}
of size
\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor
,
\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1\leq\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil

arbitrary numbers
\ell_{i}^{*}\in\{\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1,\dots,\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil\}
,

i\leq n
,

\hat{S}_{\ell}=S_{\ell}^{*}

\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1\leq\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil

\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\big)^{n}

\big|\big\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(D,\lambda),\,\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*},\,\;\hat{S}_{\ell}=S_{\ell}^{*},\,\lfloor\log_{2}\lambda\rfloor+1\leq\ell\leq\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil\big\}\big|\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\big)^{n}.

\mathcal{N}_{0}
allows for estimating

\hat{S}_{\ell}
,
leading to the bound

\displaystyle\big|\big\{\hat{\mathbf{x}}(\mathbf{x})\,:\,\mathbf{x}\in\Xi(D,\lambda),\,\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})=\ell_{i}^{*},\;i\leq n\big\}\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\big)^{n}\cdot|\mathcal{N}_{0}|^{(1+\lceil\log_{2}(2D)\rceil)\lfloor n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\rfloor}

\displaystyle\leq\big(\left(3/c_{0}\right)^{d}\big)^{n}\cdot\big(n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{2}n\big)^{n}\cdot\big((3n)^{2n^{\varepsilon/4}}\big)^{n^{1-\varepsilon}\log^{4}n}.

Finally, in view of Remark
3.6
,
there are at most
\big(1+\lceil\log_{2}(2n^{2})\rceil\big)^{n}
possible realizations of
\ell_{i}(\mathbf{x})
,
i\leq n
.
Combining the bounds, we get the claim.

Proof of claim 2.
Pick any
i,j
with
i\neq j
. By triangle inequality and the construction of
\ell_{j}
:

\left|\left\{i^{\prime}\in[n]\,:\,\|x_{j}-x_{i^{\prime}}\|_{X}\leq\|x_{i}-x_{j}\|_{X}+r_{\ell_{i}}\right\}\right|\geq L\,n^{\varepsilon}\,.

Then, by construction of the discretization

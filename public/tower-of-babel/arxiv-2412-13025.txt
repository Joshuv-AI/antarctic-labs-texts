The free product of q -matroids
Gianira N. Alfarano, Eimear Byrne, Andrew Fulcher

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of the free product of q q -matroids, which is the q q -analogue of the free product of matroids. We study the properties of this noncommutative binary operation, making an extensive use of the theory of cyclic flats. We show that the free product of two q q -matroids M 1 M_{1} and M 2 M_{2} is maximal with respect to the weak order on q q -matroids having M 1 M_{1} as a restriction and M 2 M_{2} as the complementary contraction. We characterise q q -matroids that are irreducible with respect to the free product and we prove that the factorization of a q q -matroid into a free product of irreducibles is unique up to isomorphism. We discuss the representability of the free product, with a particular focus on rank one uniform q q -matroids and show that such a product is represented by clubs on the projective line.



Keywords.

q
-matroids, free product, cyclic flats, representability, clubs.

1.
Introduction

The notion of a
q
-matroid was introduced by Crapo
[
11
]
, however, since their rediscovery in 2018 in
[
25
]
, the study of
q
-matroids has attracted a great deal of attention due to the link to rank-metric codes; see for instance
[
21
,
24
,
20
,
34
]
.
Recently, the direct sum of
q
-matroids has been introduced in terms of the rank function; see
[
10
]
. The study of this operation highlighted many differences between matroid theory and
q
-matroid theory.

The free product of matroids was introduced by Crapo and Schmitt in
[
12
]
. The same authors used it as a tool to prove Welsh’s 1969 conjecture
[
35
]
, which gives a lower bound on the number of isomorphism classes of matroids defined on a ground set of cardinality
n
. Moreover, in
[
13
]
, it was shown that the free product of matroids
M
and
N
on ground sets
S
and
T
is the unique matroid with the most independent sets among all matroids on
S\cup T
whose restriction to
S
and whose complementary contraction by
S
are
M
and
N
, respectively. They also showed that the direct sum of matroids has the most dependent sets in this class of matroids. In other words, the direct sum and the free product are the minimal and maximal element, respectively, with respect to the weak order among matroids whose restrictions to
S
and complementary contraction by
S
are
M
and
N
, respectively.

As for matroids, there are many equivalent ways to describe a
q
-matroid axiomatically. An exposition of such
cryptomorphisms
has been given in
[
9
]
, in terms of the rank function, independent spaces, flats, circuits, bases, spanning spaces, the closure function, hyperplanes, open spaces etc. Another cryptomorphism based on cyclic flats was shown in
[
2
]
. These are, in general, not straightforward
q
-analogues of the traditional matroid cryptomorphisms.

In this paper we introduce the free product of a pair of
q
-matroids. We give three cryptomorphic descriptions of the free product, namely in terms of its independent spaces, its rank function, and its lattice of cyclic flats.
We show that while the free product is the maximal element with respect to the weak order among a particular class of
q
-matroids, the direct sum is
not
in general the minimal element, in contrast to the matroid case.
We then establish that any
q
-matroid can be uniquely factorised (up to isomorphism) into irreducible components with respect to the free product. We use the theory of cyclic flats of
q
-matroids, recently developed in
[
2
,
23
]
as a key tool for many of our results.

We also study the representability of the free product in terms of finite geometry, again making use of the lattice of cyclic flats. Geometric descriptions of representable
q
-matroids in terms of
q
-systems (see
[
33
,
32
]
) have been given in
[
2
]
.
We describe representations of the free product (if one exists) and then we focus on representations of the free product of uniform
q
-matroids.
These are special
q
-matroids, which are representable as
maximum rank distance (MRD) codes
or, equivalently, as
scattered subspaces
. Inspired by
[
3
]
, using properties of cyclic flats, we prove that if a
q
-system is a representation of the free product of two uniform
q
-matroids, then it is
evasive
.
Moreover we show the converse in the case of the free product of rank one uniform
q
-matroids, by relating it to the existence of
clubs
.

This remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Section
2
contains the required preliminary material. In section
3
, we define the free product of two
q
-matroids in terms of the independent spaces of the factors. We then describe the lattice of cyclic flats of the free product, as well as an explicit expression of the rank function.
In section
4
, we establish several fundamental properties of the free product; we give a duality result and establish associativity of the free product.
We consider the weak order on a special subclass
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{\ell})
of
q
-matroids (see Definition
4.10
). We prove that the free
product of
q
-matroids is maximal in
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{\ell})
with respect to the weak order mentioned above. We consider decomposition properties of
q
-matroids with respect to the free product and show that reducibility is equivalent to the existence of free separators. We show, through a sequence of results, that any
q
-matroid can be factorised (up to isomorphism)
uniquely into irreducible components. As a corollary, we obtain a positive result on a
q
-analogue of Welsh’s conjecture.
In section
5
, we investigate the representability of the free product. We show that if the free product is representable then it is so by a block upper triangular matrix whose diagonal blocks are representations of the factors. Then we focus on the representation of the free product of uniform
q
-matroids. We study its geometric description and we show that a
q
-system is a representation of the free product of rank one uniform
q
-matroids if and only if it gives rise to a club on the projective line. In section
6
, we conclude with some open problems.

We start by recalling some definitions concerning lattices. For a more detailed treatment, we refer the interested reader to

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},\leq)
be a partially ordered set (poset). Let
a,b,v\in\mathcal{L}
.
We say that

upper bound
(resp.
lower bound
) of
a

a\leq v
and
b\leq v
(resp.
v\leq a
and
v\leq b
) and furthermore, we say that

(resp.
greatest lower bound
) of

and

for any

that is also an upper bound of
a
and
b
(resp.
u\leq v
for any
u\in\mathcal{L}
that is also a lower bound of
a
and
b
).
If a least upper bound (resp. greatest lower bound) of
a
and
b
exists, then it is unique, is denoted by
a\vee b
(resp.
a\wedge b
), which is called the
join
of
a
and
b
(resp.
meet
).
The poset
\mathcal{L}
is called a

if each pair of elements has a least upper bound and a greatest lower bound and it is denoted by
(\mathcal{L},\leq,\vee,\wedge)
.
An element in
\mathcal{L}
that is not smaller than any other element is called
maximal
element of
\mathcal{L}
and it is denoted by
\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{L}}
and an element that is not bigger than any other element is called
minimal
element of
\mathcal{L}
and it is denoted by
\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{L}}
. If there is no confusion, we simply write
\boldsymbol{1}
and
\boldsymbol{0}
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be a lattice with meet
\wedge
and join
\vee
.
Let
a,b\in\mathcal{L}
be such that
a\leq b
.

(1)

An
interval

[a,b]\subseteq\mathcal{L}
is the set of all
x\in\mathcal{L}
such that
a\leq x\leq b
. It defines the
interval sublattice

([a,b],\leq,\vee,\wedge)
.

(2)

If
[a,b]\subseteq\mathcal{L}
is such that for any
x\in\mathcal{L}
,
x\in[a,b]
implies that
x=a
or
x=b
, then
b
is called a
cover
of
a
and we write
a\lessdot b
.

\mathcal{H}([a,b]):=\{x\in[a,b]:x\lessdot b\}
. We also define

\mathrm{At}(b):=\{x\in[\zero,b]:\zero\lessdot\;x\}
and
\mathcal{H}(b):=\{x\in[\zero,b]:x\lessdot b\}
.

(3)

A
chain
from
a
to
b
is a totally ordered subset of
[a,b]
with respect to
\leq
. A chain from
a
to
b
is called a
maximal
chain in
[a,b]
if it is not properly contained in any other chain from
a
to
b
.
A finite
chain
from
a
to
b
is a sequence of the form

a=x_{1}<\cdots<x_{k+1}=b
with
x_{j}\in\mathcal{L}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, in which case we say that the chain has length
k
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{q}
be the finite field with
q
elements and let
E
be an
n
-dimensional vector space over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. In this paper, we are interested in the subspace lattice
(\mathcal{L}(E),\leq,\vee,\wedge)
, which is the lattice of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
E
, ordered with respect to inclusion and for which the join is the usual vector space sum and the meet is the subspace intersection. That is, for all subspaces
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(E)
we have:

A\vee B=A+B,\;A\wedge B=A\cap B.

The minimal element of
\mathcal{L}(E)
is
\zero=\langle 0\rangle
and its maximal element is
\textbf{1}=E
. For the sake of simplicity, we write
\zero
to denote the minimal element of a subspace lattice of a vector space of any dimension.
For each
U\in\mathcal{L}(E)
, we write
U^{\perp}
to denote the orthogonal complement of
U
with respect to a fixed non-degenerate bilinear form on
E
. The map
U\mapsto U^{\perp}
is an involutory anti-automorphism of
\mathcal{L}(E)
.

Definition 2.3
.

A
q
-matroid with ground space

E
is a pair
M=(E,r)
, where
r
is an integer-valued function defined on
\mathcal{L}(E)
with the following properties:

(R1)

Boundedness:
0\leq r(A)\leq\textnormal{dim}(A)
, for all
A\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

(R2)

Monotonicity:
A\leq B\Rightarrow r(A)\leq r(B)
, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

(R3)

Submodularity:
r(A+B)+r(A\cap B)\leq r(A)+r(B)
, for all
A,B\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.

The function
r
is called
rank function
and the value
r(M):=r(E)
is the
rank of the
q
-matroid
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
be
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces of finite dimension
n
. Two
q
-matroids
M_{1}=(E_{1},r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},r_{2})
are called
isomorphic

if there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism
\tau:E_{1}\longrightarrow E_{2}
such that
r_{2}(\tau(V))=r_{1}(V)
for all
V\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1})
. We also say that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
equivalent

q
-matroids.
We say that
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
lattice-equivalent
if there exists a lattice isomorphism

\xi:\mathcal{L}(E_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}(E_{2})
such that
r_{2}(\xi(V))=r_{1}(V)
for all
V\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1})
.

Let
M=(E,r)
be a
q
-matroid. A one-dimensional space
x\in\mathcal{L}(E)
is a
loop
if
r(x)=0
. A codimension 1 subspace of
E
that has rank less than
r(E)
is called a
coloop
. A subspace
A\in\mathcal{L}(E)
is
independent
if
r(A)=\dim(A)
and
dependent
otherwise. The inclusion-maximal independent spaces are called
bases
and the inclusion-minimal dependent spaces are called
circuits
. A space
A\in\mathcal{L}(E)
is a
flat
if it is inclusion-maximal in the set
\{V\in\mathcal{L}(E):r(V)=r(A)\}
, and a space is
cyclic
or
open
if it is the sum of circuits. Finally, a subspace which is both a flat and cyclic is called a
cyclic flat
.

For a given
q
-matroid
M=(E,r)
, we have the following two operators.
For each
A\in\mathcal{L}(E)
, define

\Cl_{r}(A):=\{x\in\mathcal{L}(E):r(A+x)=r(A)\}.

The
closure operator
of a
q
-matroid
(E,r)
is the function defined by

\cl_{r}:\mathcal{L}(E)\to\mathcal{L}(E),\ A\mapsto\cl_{r}(A):=\sum_{x\in\Cl_{r}(A)}x.

The
cyclic operator
of
M
is the function defined by

\displaystyle\cyc_{r}:\mathcal{L}(E)\to\mathcal{L}(E),\ A\mapsto\cyc_{r}(A):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}C\leq A\\
C\textnormal{ is cyclic}\end{subarray}}C.

If
M=(E,r)
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}(M)
and
\mathcal{Z}(M)
the collection of independent spaces and cyclic flats of
M
and by
\cl_{r}
and
\cyc_{r}
its closure and cyclic operators.
If it is clear from the context, we will simply write
\mathcal{I},\mathcal{Z},\cl,\cyc
.

It is well known that the collection of independent spaces uniquely determines the
q
-matroid, and the same is true for the collections of dependent spaces, open spaces, flats and circuits.

For our purposes, we recall the following version of the independence axioms.

Notation 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of subspaces of
E
. For any subspace
X\in\mathcal{L}(E)
, we define then the collection of

of

Definition 2.6

(
[

)

Let
\mathcal{I}
be a collection of subspaces of

(I1)

(I2)

For all

(I3)

(I4”)

Let

.

If
\mathcal{I}
satisfies the independence axioms, we say that

is a collection of

and we denote by

-matroid defined by

3.
The free product of
q
-matroids

In this section, we introduce the free product of a pair of
q
-matroids. We provide cryptomorphic definitions of this product in terms of its independent spaces, its rank function and its lattice of cyclic flats. We note that while the independent spaces and the cyclic flats are similar to the matroid case, the rank function is somewhat different.

3.1.
Independent spaces of the free product

Notation 3.1
.

For any direct sum

of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces

and
E_{2}
we denote by

\pi_{i}:E\longrightarrow E_{i}

\iota_{i}:E_{i}\longrightarrow E

and

we may denote
\iota_{1}(A)\oplus\iota_{2}(B)

, when there is no risk of confusion. For instance, we may denote

by
A\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\leq E
. For an arbitrary set
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{L}(E_{2})
and an arbitrary space

, we denote by

the set
\{A\oplus V:V\in\mathcal{V}\}
. The notation
\mathcal{V}\oplus A
is similarly defined. Analogously, we will denote by
\iota_{1}(\mathcal{V})
(resp.
\iota_{2}(\mathcal{V})
) the set
\{\iota_{1}(V):V\in\mathcal{V}\}
(resp.
\{\iota_{2}(V):V\in\mathcal{V}\}
).

For a

-matroid

\lambda
be the

\displaystyle\lambda(A)

\displaystyle=r(E)-r(A),

\displaystyle\nu(B)

\displaystyle=\textnormal{dim}(B)-r(B),

Let
M_{1}=(E_{1},\mathcal{I}_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},\mathcal{I}_{2})
be

-matroids. Let

and

be the rank, rank-lack and nullity functions of

and

, respectively.

Define the set

\displaystyle r_{\mathcal{I}}(X)=\dim(\beta)

\displaystyle=\dim(\beta\cap\iota_{1}(E_{1}))+\dim(\pi_{2}(\beta))

\displaystyle=r_{1}(X\cap\iota_{1}(E_{1}))+r_{2}(\pi_{2}(X))+\nu_{2}(\pi_{2}(\beta)).

\displaystyle r(Z_{2})-r(Z_{1})

\displaystyle=r(E_{1}\oplus\overline{Z}_{2})-r(\overline{Z}_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(E_{1})+r_{2}(\overline{Z}_{2})-r_{1}(\overline{Z}_{1})

\displaystyle<\dim(E_{1})-\dim(\overline{Z}_{1})+r_{2}(\overline{Z}_{2})

\displaystyle\leq\dim(E_{1})-\dim(\overline{Z}_{1})+\textnormal{dim}(\overline{Z}_{2})

\displaystyle=\dim(E_{1}\oplus\overline{Z}_{2})-\dim(\overline{Z_{1}})

\displaystyle=\dim(Z_{2})-\dim(Z_{1}),

where the strict inequality follows since
(Z2)
holds in
\mathcal{Z}_{1}
.

(Z3)

By the submodularity of
r
, we have that for every
F,G\in\mathcal{Z}

\displaystyle r(F)+r(G)

\displaystyle\geq r(F+G)+r(F\cap G).

Now,
(Z3)
follows directly from the fact that
r(F+G)=r(F\vee G)
and Lemma
3.9
applied to
F\cap G
.

We assume now that
M_{1}
has no coloops and
M_{2}
has no loops. In this case,
E_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
and
\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. We want to show that the collection
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}=\mathcal{Z}\cup\iota_{1}(E_{1})
satisfies the axioms
(Z0)
–
(Z3)
. Note that
(Z1)
and
(Z3)
can be proved as before.

(Z0)

Let
Z\in\iota_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{1}\setminus\{\zero\})
. Then
Z\vee\iota_{1}(E_{1})=\iota_{1}(E_{1})\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
and
Z\wedge\iota_{1}(E_{1})=Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
. If
Z\in E_{1}\oplus\mathcal{Z}_{2}\setminus\{\zero\}
, then
Z\vee\iota_{1}(E_{1})=Z\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
and
Z\wedge\iota_{1}(E_{1})=\iota_{1}(E_{1})\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
. The other cases have already been covered before. Moreover, also in this case
\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}=\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
and
\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}}=E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{1}_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}}
.

(Z2)

Let
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
be such that
Z_{1}<Z_{2}
. As before, the only non-trivial case to show is when
Z_{1}\in\iota_{1}(\mathcal{Z}_{1})\backslash(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})
and
Z_{2}\in E_{1}\oplus\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. However, in this case, we have that
Z_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}
, so the proof follows from the previous case for
(Z2)
.

The case where
M_{1}
has a coloop and
M_{2}
has a loop is proved in a similar way to the above. The only difference is that
\iota_{1}(1_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}}),E_{1}\oplus\iota_{2}(\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}})\in\mathcal{Z}
and
\iota_{1}(1_{\mathcal{Z}_{1}})<E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}<E_{1}\oplus\iota_{2}(\boldsymbol{0}_{\mathcal{Z}_{2}})
.
∎

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be
q
-matroids over the ground spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, respectively. Then

\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square M_{2})=\begin{cases}\mathcal{Z}\cup\iota_{1}(E_{1})&\textnormal{ if $M_{1}$ has no coloops and $M_{2}$ has no loops,}\\
\mathcal{Z}&\textnormal{ otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{Z}
is defined as in (
5
).

Proof.

As before, we will denote the rank function of
M_{1}\square M_{2}
by
r
. By Theorem
3.10
there exists a
q
-matroid
L=(E_{1}\oplus E_{2},r_{L})
such that
\mathcal{Z}(L)=\mathcal{Z}
. We will show that
r=r_{L}
. Clearly, we have that
L|E_{1}\cong M_{1}
and
L/E_{1}\cong M_{2}
. Let
X\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1}\oplus E_{2})
. By Lemma
2.8
, we have that

r_{L}(X)=\textup{min}\{r_{L}(Z)+\textnormal{dim}((X+Z)/Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}(L)\}.

Suppose that
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}(L)
is such that
r_{L}(X)=r_{L}(Z^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})
. If
Z^{\prime}\leq E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
, then there exists
\overline{Z}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
such that
Z^{\prime}=\iota_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})
and
r_{L}(Z^{\prime})=r_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})
by Lemma
3.7
. Therefore,

\displaystyle r_{L}(Z^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X)-\textnormal{dim}(X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))+\textnormal{dim}(\pi_{2}(X))-\textnormal{dim}(X\cap Z^{\prime})-\textnormal{dim}(\pi_{2}(Z^{\prime}\cap X))

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\overline{Z}^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}((X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))/(Z^{\prime}\cap X))+r_{2}(\pi_{2}(X))+\nu_{2}(\pi_{2}(X))

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\pi_{1}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})))+r_{2}(\pi_{2}(X))+\nu_{2}(\pi_{2}(X)),

Where the third equality follows from Lemma
3.2
. If
Z^{\prime}\geq E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
, then
Z^{\prime}=E_{1}\oplus\overline{Z}
, with
\overline{Z}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. Then by Lemma
3.7
, we have

\displaystyle r_{L}(Z^{\prime})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{L}(E_{1}\oplus\overline{Z})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(E_{1})+r_{2}(\overline{Z})+\textnormal{dim}(X/X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(E_{1})+r_{2}(\overline{Z})+\textnormal{dim}(X)-\textnormal{dim}(X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(E_{1})+r_{2}(\pi_{2}(Z^{\prime}))+\textnormal{dim}(X)-\textnormal{dim}(X\cap Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\pi_{1}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))+\lambda_{1}(\pi_{1}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle\;\;\;+r_{2}(\pi_{2}(Z^{\prime}))+\textnormal{dim}(\pi_{2}(X)/\pi_{2}(X\cap Z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=r_{1}(\pi_{1}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))+\lambda_{1}(\pi_{1}(X\cap(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))+r_{2}(X).

By Theorem
3.6
, we get that
r_{L}=r
.

4.
Weak ordering and unique factorisation

In section
4.1
we establish that the free product of
q
-matroids
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
on ground spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{k}
respectively is the unique (up to isomorphism)
q
-matroid with the maximum number of independent spaces among all
q
-matroids
N
on
E_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus E_{k}
such that
N[E_{i-1},E_{i}]\cong M_{i}
for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. This gives the
q
-analogue of one of the main results of
[
13
, section 4]
. Furthermore, in
[
13
]
it is noted that the direct sum of matroids is the “most dependent” way of combining two matroids (in a sense that we will make precise later).
We will show that, somewhat surprisingly, the
q
-analogue of this result does not hold.

In section
4.2
we show that any
q
-matroid can be factorised uniquely (up to isomorphism) into irreducible components, which gives the
q
-analogue of another one of the main results found in
[
13
, section 6]
.

Before proceeding with sections
4.1
and
4.2
we recall the definitions and some basic properties of the direct sum of
q
-matroids and we establish some relevant preliminary results.

Definition 4.1
.

Let
M_{i}=(E_{i},r_{i}),\,i=1,2,
be a pair of
q
-matroids and set
E=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
.

For
i=1,2
, let
r_{i}^{\prime}
be the map defined by
r^{\prime}_{i}:\mathcal{L}(E)\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0},\ V\longmapsto r_{i}(\pi_{i}(V))
for all
V\in\mathcal{L}(E)
.
Let
r
be the map defined by:

r:\mathcal{L}(E)\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0},\quad V\longmapsto\dim V+\min_{X\leq V}\big(r^{\prime}_{1}(X)+r^{\prime}_{2}(X)-\dim X\big)\>\>\forall\>\>V\in\mathcal{L}(E).

Then
M:=(E,r)
is a
q
-matroid, called the
direct sum of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
, and is denoted by
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
.

Lemma 4.2

(
[
23
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

For each
i=1,2
, let
M_{i}=(E_{i},\rho_{i})
, be a
q
-matroid and let
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
be the lattice of cyclic flats of
M_{i}
.
Let
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{Z}_{2}=\{Z_{1}\oplus Z_{2}:Z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},\;Z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}\}
. Then

\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\oplus M_{2})=\mathcal{Z}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{Z}_{2}.

It is well-known that the dual of a
q
-matroid is unique up to lattice-equivalence, regardless of what lattice anti-isomorphism is used.
We will define a lattice anti-isomorphism
\varphi
that is convenient for our notation, but the reader could be aware that, with the use of additional lattice isomorphisms, the following results that use this specific lattice anti-isomorphism
\varphi
can hold for arbitrary lattice anti-isomorphisms (but the statements would include slightly heavier notation).

Notation 4.3
.

We fix
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E_{1}}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E_{2}}
to be arbitrary non-degenerate inner products on
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively. We let

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle=\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E_{1}}\oplus\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{E_{2}}
be the non-degenerate inner product on
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}

defined by
\langle a,b\rangle=\langle\pi_{1}(a),\pi_{1}(b)\rangle_{E_{1}}+\langle\pi_{2}(a),\pi_{2}(b)\rangle_{E_{2}}
for all
a,b\in E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
.
For each
V\leq E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
, we let
V^{\perp}
denote the orthogonal complement of
V
with respect to
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
.
Then
(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})^{\perp}=\boldsymbol{0}\oplus E_{2}
and
(\boldsymbol{0}\oplus E_{2})^{\perp}=E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
.

Definition 4.4
.

We define the function
\text{rev}:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
by
\text{rev}(X)=\{(x_{n},\dots,x_{1}):(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in X\}
, which is a lattice automorphism. Now we define the lattice anti-isomorphism
\varphi:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}
by
\varphi(X)=\text{rev}(X^{\perp})
. Then for
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}(E_{1}\oplus E_{2})
and
\perp
as in Notation
4.3
, we have
\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})=E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
.

For the remainder of this section, we will use the lattice anti-isomorphism
\varphi
to map a
q
-matroid to its dual. The following duality result is a
q
-analogue of
[
12
, Proposition 4]
, which the authors prove via the bases of a matroid. We prove it by examining the lattice of cyclic flats.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
be
q
-matroids on the spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
respectively. We have that
(M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}\cong M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*}
.

Proof.

Recall from Definition
4.4
that
\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})=E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
. We will prove the statement of this proposition by considering the lattices of cyclic flats
\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*})
and
\mathcal{Z}(M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*})
. Recall that the dual of a cyclic flat of a
q
-matroid is a cyclic flat of the dual
q
-matroid.
We have the following:

\displaystyle Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*})

\displaystyle\iff\varphi(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square M_{2})

\displaystyle\iff\varphi(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\text{ or }\varphi(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle\iff Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}|\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\text{ or }Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}/\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))

\displaystyle\iff Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}|(E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\text{ or }Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}/(E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})).

Using Lemma
2.12
, we have

(M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}|\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong((M_{1}\square M_{2})/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))^{*}

and

(M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}/\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong((M_{1}\square M_{2})|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))^{*}.

This gives us that

(M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}|\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong M_{2}^{*}\cong(M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*})|\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong(M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*})|(E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})

and

(M_{1}\square M_{2})^{*}/\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong M_{1}^{*}\cong(M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*})/\varphi(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\cong(M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*})/(E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}).

We obtain the result by applying Theorem
3.13
to
M_{2}^{*}\square M_{1}^{*}
.
∎

Proposition 4.6
.

The free product on
q
-matroids is associative.

Proof.

For
i=1,2,3
, let
M_{i}
be a
q
-matroid with ground space
E_{i}
. Consider the projection
\pi_{i}:E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}\longrightarrow E_{i}
the projection on the corresponding coordinates.
By Theorem
3.13
we have that
Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})
if and only if
Z\in[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]\cup[E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}]
and

(1)

\pi_{1}(Z)\oplus\pi_{2}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square M_{2})
if
Z<E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
,

(2)

\pi_{3}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{3})
if
Z>E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
,

(3)

\pi_{1}(Z)\oplus\pi_{2}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square M_{2})
and
\pi_{3}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{3})
if
Z=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
.

Now consider that
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square M_{2})
if and only if
Z^{\prime}\in[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]\cup[E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}]
and

(4)

\pi_{1}(Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1})
if
Z^{\prime}<E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
,

(5)

\pi_{2}(Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{2})
if
Z^{\prime}>E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
,

(6)

\pi_{1}(Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1})
and
\pi_{2}(Z^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{2})
if
Z^{\prime}=E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
.

Note that
\pi_{i}(\pi_{1}(Z)\oplus\pi_{2}(Z))=\pi_{i}(Z)
for
i=1,2
. Observe that (4), (5) and (6) imply (1). Consider the following property, which is implied by (3) and (6).

(3’)

\pi_{2}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{2})
and
\pi_{3}(Z)\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{3})
if
Z=E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
.

We see that (3’) and (6) imply (3).
We therefore have that
Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})
if and only if

Z\in[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]\cup[E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]\cup[E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}]

and one of the above properties (2),(3’),(4),(5),(6) is satisfied.

With a similar line of reasoning we can show the same properties for
Z\in\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))
which shows that
\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})=\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))
.

For any
[X,Y]\subseteq[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}]
, the rank function of the minor
((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})[X,Y]
is
r_{(M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3}}(W)-r_{(M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3}}(X)
for any
W\in[X,Y]
. Similarly, the rank function of the minor
(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))[X,Y]
is
r_{M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3})}(W)-r_{M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3})}(X)
for any
W\in[X,Y]
. Using the definition of the free product and Lemma
3.7
, we can thus deduce that

\displaystyle((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]

\displaystyle\cong M_{1}\cong(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))[\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}],

\displaystyle((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})[E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}]

\displaystyle\cong M_{2}\cong(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))[E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}],

\displaystyle((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})[E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}]

\displaystyle\cong M_{3}\cong(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))[E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}\oplus E_{3}].

We have already shown that all cyclic flats in
\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})=\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))
lie in these intervals. Therefore,
r_{(M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3}}(Z)=r_{M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3})}(Z)
for all
Z\in\mathcal{Z}((M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3})=\mathcal{Z}(M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3}))
. Since every
q
-matroid is uniquely determined by its lattice of cyclic flats along with their rank values, this implies that
(M_{1}\square M_{2})\square M_{3}\cong M_{1}\square(M_{2}\square M_{3})
.
∎

The free product and the direct sum of
q
-matroids
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
on ground spaces
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are related by the following result.

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
M_{1}=(E_{1},r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},r_{2})
. Assume
r_{1}(M_{1})=0
or
\nu_{2}(M_{2})=0
, then
M_{1}\square M_{2}=M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
.

Proof.

If
r_{1}(M_{1})=0
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{1}=\{E_{1}\}
and hence the lattice of cyclic flats of
M_{1}\square M_{2}
is equal to

\{E_{1}\oplus Z:Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}\}=\{Z_{1}\oplus Z_{2}:Z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}\}.

Similarly, if
\nu_{2}(M_{2})=0
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=\{\boldsymbol{0}\}
, hence the lattice of cyclic flats of
M_{1}\square M_{2}
is equal to

\{Z\oplus\boldsymbol{0}:Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}\}=\{Z_{1}\oplus Z_{2}:Z_{1}\in\mathcal{Z}_{1},Z_{2}\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}\}.

By Lemma
4.2
, the result follows.
∎

4.1.
The weak ordering

In
[
13
]
the free product of matroids, as well as the direct sum of matroids, are discussed relative to weak maps of matroids. In the same paper, it is shown that
the free product of two matroids
M
and
N
on ground sets
S
and
T
is the maximal element in the
weak ordering
of the class of matroids on
S\cup T
whose restrictions to
S
and complementary contraction by
S
are
M
and
N
, respectively. Further, the direct sum of
M
and
N
is the minimal element in this weak ordering.
In this section we extend these results to
q
-matroids, as well as observe a difference that occurs in the
q
-analogue. Namely, that the direct sum is not always minimal in the weak ordering.

The definition of
weak map
for matroids can be found in
[
36
, Chapter 9]
. In the following definition we include the
q
-analogue, as well as a definition of
weak isomorphism
.

Definition 4.8
.

Let
M_{1}=(E_{1},r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},r_{2})
be
q
-matroids, let
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}(E_{1})
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}(E_{2})
and let
\tau:\mathcal{L}_{1}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}_{2}
be a function. We say that
\tau
is a
weak map
from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
if
r_{1}(X)\geq r_{2}(\tau(X))
for every
X\in\mathcal{L}_{1}
. If
\tau
is also a lattice isomorphism, then we call
\tau
a
weak isomorphism
from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
.

The following definition is the (
q
-analogue) of a
weak order
on matroids; see
[
13
]
.

Definition 4.9
.

Let
M_{1}=(E_{1},r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},r_{2})
be
q
-matroids, let
\mathcal{L}_{1}=\mathcal{L}(E_{1})
and
\mathcal{L}_{2}=\mathcal{L}(E_{2})
. We say that
M_{2}
is below
M_{2}
in the
weak ordering
and we write
M_{2}\preceq M_{1}
if there exists a weak isomorphism from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
.

It is clear that the weak order of
q
-matroids is a partial order on the set of
q
-matroids supported on the same subspace lattice (up to isomorphism). For the remainder of this section, we fix a positive integer
k
and a collection of
q
-matroids
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
.

Definition 4.10
.

We denote by
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
the partially ordered set of (isomorphism classes of)
q
-matroids
M
on a vector space
E
for which there exists a chain
\boldsymbol{0}=V_{0}<V_{1}<\dots<V_{k}=E
such that
M[V_{i-1},V_{i}]\cong M_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, and for which the order on
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
is the weak order
\preceq
. For the partially ordered set of matroids with these same conditions, we denote the corresponding poset by
\mathcal{M}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
(in which case
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
are assumed to be matroids).

Clearly,
M_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus M_{k}
and
M_{1}\square\cdots\square M_{k}
belong to
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\ldots,M_{k})
.

In Theorem
4.12
we generalise a result in
[
13
, section 4]
by showing that
M_{1}\square\dots\square M_{k}
is maximal in
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
.
Our proof is different from the matroid case and heavily relies on the properties of cyclic flats. We first prove a technical lemma.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
M=(E,r)
be a
q
-matroid and let
X,Y\in\mathcal{L}(E)
. We have

r(Y)+\text{dim}((X+Y)/Y)\geq r(\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y)))+\text{dim}((X+\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y)))/\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y))).

Proof.

From the definitions of cl and cyc and Lemma
3.9
, for any
W\in\mathcal{L}
we have that
r(W)=r(\text{cl}(W))
and
r(W)=r(\text{cyc}(W))+\text{dim}(W)-\text{dim}(\text{cyc}(W))
. Let
U,V\in\mathcal{L}(E)
and
U\leq V
. Then,

\text{dim}((W+V)/V)\leq\text{dim}((W+U)/U)\leq\text{dim}((W+V)/V)+\text{dim}(V)-\text{dim}(U).

We therefore get the following:

\displaystyle r(Y)+\text{dim}((X+Y)/Y)

\displaystyle=r(\text{cl}(Y))+\text{dim}((X+Y)/Y)

\displaystyle\geq r(\text{cl}(Y))+\text{dim}((X+\text{cl}(Y))/\text{cl}(Y))

\displaystyle=r(\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y)))+\text{dim}(\text{cl}(Y))-\text{dim}(\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y))+\text{dim}((X+\text{cl}(Y))/\text{cl}(Y))

\displaystyle\geq r(\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y)))+\text{dim}((X+\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y)))/\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Y))).\qed

The following, Theorem
4.12
, is the main result of this section, which is the
q
-analogue of
[
13
, Proposition 4.7]
. Once again, we leverage the structure of the cyclic flats of the free product to obtain a straightforward proof.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
M=(E,r)
be a
q
-matroid and
\boldsymbol{0}=V_{0}<V_{1}<\dots<V_{k}=E
be a chain. Let
M_{i}=M[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
. The identity map on
E
is a weak map from
M_{1}\square\dots\square M_{k}
to
M
.

Proof.

Let
N:=M_{1}\square\dots\square M_{k}
and let
S=\{V_{i}:i\in\{0,\dots,k\},V_{i}\notin\mathcal{Z}_{1}\}
. By Theorem
3.13
and the associativity of the free product given in Proposition
4.6
, we have

\mathcal{Z}(N)=\left(\bigcup_{i=0}^{k}\mathcal{Z}(M_{i})\right)\backslash S.

Let
r_{N}
be the rank function of
N
.
Since
N[V_{i-1},V_{i}]\cong M_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
(by the definition of the free product as well as its associativity), we have that
r(X)=r_{N}(X)
for
X\in[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. By Lemma
2.8
, for any
X\in\mathcal{L}
we have that
r_{N}(X)=\min\{r_{N}(Z)+\text{dim}((X+Z)/Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}(N)\}
. Let
Z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}(N)
be such that
r_{N}(X)=r_{N}(Z^{\prime})+\text{dim}((X+Z^{\prime})/Z^{\prime})
. By Theorem
3.13
and Proposition
4.6
, we have that
\pi_{i}(Z^{\prime})\in[V_{i-1},V_{i}]
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, which means that
r_{N}(Z^{\prime})=r(Z^{\prime})
. Therefore, by Lemma
4.11
, we have

\displaystyle r_{N}(X)

\displaystyle=r_{N}(Z^{\prime})+\text{dim}((X+Z^{\prime})/Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=r(Z^{\prime})+\text{dim}((X+Z^{\prime})/Z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq r(\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Z^{\prime})))+\text{dim}((X+\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Z^{\prime})))/\text{cyc}(\text{cl}(Z^{\prime})))

\displaystyle\geq\min\{r(Z)+\text{dim}((X+Z)/Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}\}=r(X).\qed

The following is an immediate consequence of Theorem
4.12
.

Corollary 4.13
.

M_{1}\square\cdots\square M_{k}
is maximal in
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
.

The following, Lemma
4.14
, gives a convenient sufficient condition for the existence of a weak isomorphism between (
q
-)matroids.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
M_{1}=(E,r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E,r_{2})
be
q
-matroids. If
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}
and
r_{1}(Z)=r_{2}(Z)
for all
Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}
, then the identity map on
\mathcal{L}(E)
is a weak isomorphism from
M_{1}
to
M_{2}
.

Proof.

For any
X\in\mathcal{L}(E)
, by Lemma
2.8
(
[
2
, Corollary 3.12]
) we have

\displaystyle r_{2}(X)

\displaystyle=\textup{min}\{r_{2}(Z)+\textup{dim}(X/X\cap Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{2}\}

\displaystyle\leq\textup{min}\{r_{1}(Z)+\textup{dim}(X/X\cap Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}_{1}\}=r_{1}(X)

since
\mathcal{Z}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{2}
. The result follows from the definition of a weak isomorphism.
∎

It is noted in
[

]
that
M_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus M_{k}
is minimal in
\mathcal{M}(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
. Interestingly, this is not always the case for

-matroids, which we show in the following example.

Example 4.15
.

Let
M\cong N\cong\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)
. Choose a basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}
such that
(M\oplus N)|\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\cong M
and
(M\oplus N)|\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle\cong N
. We then have
\mathcal{Z}(M\oplus N)=\{\boldsymbol{0},\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}\}
. Let
L
be a
q
-matroid on
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4})
such that
\mathcal{Z}(L)=\{\boldsymbol{0},\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle,\langle e_{3},e_{4}\rangle,\langle e_{1}+e_{3},e_{2}+e_{4}\rangle,\mathbb{F}_{q}^{4}\}
. It is not difficult to see that
\mathcal{Z}(L)
satisfies the cyclic flat axioms from Definition
2.7
, when we give
\langle e_{1}+e_{3},e_{2}+e_{4}\rangle
a rank value of 1. It is clear that
L|\langle e_{1},e_{2}\rangle\cong M
and

\mathcal{Z}(M\oplus N)\subset\mathcal{Z}(L)

4.14
means that

in the weak order on

.

4.2.
Unique factorisation

Definition 4.16
.

We say that a
q
-matroid is
irreducible
if it cannot be written as the free product of two non-trivial
q
-matroids.

A natural question that arises is whether a
q
-matroid can be decomposed uniquely into irreducible components. In this section, we answer this question in the affirmative.

We remark that many properties of the lattice of cyclic flats of a (
q
-)matroid do not depend on whether or not the ambient lattice is a Boolean or a subspace lattice. Some arguments that employ the cyclic flats of matroids in
[
13
]
hold true also for
q
-matroids. Therefore, in this section, we provide only the proofs of the results that do not immediately follow as in the matroid case.

Definition 4.17
.

An element

is a

of a

-matroid

if every cyclic flat in
\mathcal{Z}(M)
is comparable to

in
\mathcal{L}(E)

Observe that, trivially,
\boldsymbol{0}
and

are free separators. We refer to any other free separator as a

We include the following useful notation for lattices of cyclic flats. Note that it does not equate to taking the lattice of cyclic flats of minors of the

-matroid.

Notation 4.18
.

Let

be a

-matroid. For

\mathcal{Z}(M)|X=\{Z\in\mathcal{Z}(M):Z\leq X\}
and
\mathcal{Z}(M)/X=\{Z\in\mathcal{Z}(M):Z\geq X\}
.

The following result is the
q
-analogue of
[

, Theorem 6.3]
.

For any

-matroid

(i)

M=(M|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\square(M/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))
.

(ii)

E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
is a free separator of

.

Proof.

(i)
\implies
(ii)
This follows from Theorem
3.13
.

(ii)
\implies
(i)
Assume that
E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}
is a free separator of

and let

. It is clear that we have
\mathcal{Z}(M)|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(M|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))
and
\mathcal{Z}(M)/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(M/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))
. We thus have that

(7)

\mathcal{Z}(M)\subseteq\{\iota_{1}(Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}(M|(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\}\dot{\cup}\{(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0})+\iota_{2}(Z):Z\in\mathcal{Z}(M/(E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}))\}.

, we deduce that
E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathcal{Z}(N)
if and only if
E_{1}\oplus\boldsymbol{0}\in\mathcal{Z}(M)
. Therefore, using (

\mathcal{Z}(M)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}(N)
. By Lemma

we now have that the identity map on

.

For any non-zero

.

Let

If

For any non-uniform

Notation 4.28

Lemma 4.29

Let

Note that the following
primary factorisation
is made possible due to an iterative application of Theorem
4.19
, as well as the associativity of the free product (Proposition
4.6
).

Definition 4.32
.

Let
M
be a
q
-matroid with primary flag
T_{0}<\dots<T_{k}
. The minor
M[T_{i-1},T_{i}]
is a
primary factor
of
M
, and the factorisation
M=M[T_{0},T_{1}]\square\dots\square M[T_{k-1},T_{k}]
is the
primary factorisation
of
M
.

The following is the
q
-analogue of
[
13
, Theorem 6.16]
. By changing the characterisation of a uniform matroid
M
from one that has
\mathcal{F}(M)
equal to a Boolean lattice, to one such that
\mathcal{F}(M)
is isomorphic to an interval of the ambient lattice
\mathcal{L}(E)
(given in Lemma
4.30
), it is a direct
q
-analogue of
[
13
, Theorem 6.16]
. We will provide a proof for clarity and self-containment. While the overall argument we provide here follows the result of
[
13
]
, we remark that different approaches were required here to obtain the preceding results applied in its proof.

Theorem 4.33
.

The sequence of primary factors of a
q
-matroid
M
is the unique sequence
M_{1},\dots,M_{k}
such that
M=M_{1}\square\cdots\square M_{k}
where each
M_{i}
is either uniform or irreducible, and no free product of consecutive elements in the sequence is uniform.

Proof.

Let
M=M_{1}\square\cdots\square M_{\ell}
, and let

\mathcal{U}=\{U_{0},\dots U_{\ell}\}
be the corresponding set of free separators such that
U_{i-1}<U_{i}
and
M_{i}=M[U_{i-1},U_{i}]
for
i\in\{1,\dots,\ell\}
. Let
\mathcal{T}=\{T_{0},\dots,T_{k}\}
, with
T_{j-1}<T_{j}
for
j\in\{1,\dots,k\}
be the primary flag of
M
. We will show that the statement of the theorem is satisfied if and only if
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{T}
.

Suppose first that
\mathcal{U}=\mathcal{T}
. By Lemma
4.29
we have
\mathcal{F}(M_{i})=\mathcal{F}(M[T_{i-1},T_{i}])\cong[T_{i-1},T_{i}]_{\mathcal{F}}
, for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. By Lemma
4.31
, combined with Proposition
4.26
and Theorem
4.27
, we deduce that
M_{i}
is either uniform, or irreducible. For
i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
we have
\mathcal{F}(M_{i}\square M_{i+1})\cong[T_{i-1},T_{i+1}]_{\mathcal{F}}
, which has a non-trivial pinchpoint at
T_{i}
. Proposition
4.26
then gives us that
M_{i}\square M_{i+1}
is not uniform.

For the converse, suppose first that
\mathcal{T}
is not a subset of
\mathcal{U}
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is composed of free separators, there then must exist
T_{j}\in[U_{i},U_{i+1}]_{\mathcal{F}}\backslash\{U_{i},U_{i+1}\}
for some
i,j
. This means that
M_{i}
is neither uniform nor irreducible.

Finally, suppose that
\mathcal{T}
is a proper subset of
\mathcal{U}
. Then there exists
U_{j}\in[T_{i},T_{i+1}]_{\mathcal{F}}\backslash\{T_{i},T_{i+1}\}
for some
i,j
. By Lemma
4.29
we must have that
M[T_{i},T_{i+1}]
is uniform. Since
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
, we must have that
T_{i}\leq U_{j-1}
and
U_{j+1}\leq T_{i+1}
, which means that
M_{j}\square M_{j+1}
is a minor of
M[T_{i},T_{i+1}]
, which means that it must be uniform.
∎

As in the matroid case, we have thus shown that any
q
-matroid factors uniquely into minors that are either irreducible or maximally uniform (i.e. maximal with respect to inclusion among the minors).

The following two results are the
q
-analogues of
[
13
, Theorem 6.17, Theorem 6.18]
, which we state here as corollaries (of Theorem
4.33
), and require no further proof than what is given in
[
13
]
.

Corollary 4.34
.

If
M\cong M_{1}\square\cdots\square M_{k}\cong N_{1}\square\cdots\square N_{r}
, where each factor is irreducible, then
k=r
and
M_{i}=N_{i}
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
.

Corollary 4.35
.

Let
M_{1},M_{2},N_{1},N_{2}
be
q
-matroids on the spaces
E_{1},E_{2},E_{1}^{\prime},E_{2}^{\prime}
.
Suppose that
M_{1}\square M_{2}\cong N_{1}\square N_{2}
and
E_{1}\cong E_{1}^{\prime}
. Then
M_{1}\cong N_{1}
and
M_{2}\cong N_{2}
.

By Corollary
4.35
we can derive a
q
-analogue of
[
12
, Corollary 9]
, which gives a recursive lower bound for the number of isomorphism classes of matroids on a set with
n
elements. The proof does not differ significantly from the proof found in
[
12
]
, so we omit it.

Corollary 4.36
.

Let
\mathcal{M}_{q,n}
denote the set of isomorphism classes of
q
-matroids on the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. We have
|\mathcal{M}_{q,n}|\geq|\mathcal{M}_{q,n_{1}}|\cdot|\mathcal{M}_{q,n_{2}}|
if
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
.

To give an example of an irreducible
q
-matroid, we include the following definition, taken from
[
8
, Example 17]
, which is the
q
-analogue of the well-known Vámos matroid.

Definition 4.37
.

Let
e_{1},\dots,e_{8}
be the canonical basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{8}
. Define the set

\mathcal{C}=\{\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\rangle,\langle e_{1},e_{2},e_{5},e_{6}\rangle,\langle e_{3},e_{4},e_{5},e_{5}\rangle,\langle e_{3},e_{4},e_{7},e_{8}\rangle,\langle e_{5},e_{6},e_{7},e_{8}\rangle\}.

The
Vámos
q
-matroid
is the
q
-matroid on the lattice
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{8})
with rank function
r:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{8})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}
defined by

r(A)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cc}\text{dim}(A)&\text{dim}(A)\leq 3\\
3&A\in\mathcal{C}\\
4&\text{dim}(A)\geq 4\textup{ and }A\notin\mathcal{C}.\end{array}\right.

Example 4.38
.

It is clear that the lattice of cyclic flats of the Vámos
q
-matroid contains the set
\mathcal{C}
. It is easily observed that
\mathcal{C}
is an antichain, that
\bigwedge\mathcal{C}=\boldsymbol{0}
, and that
\bigvee\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{8}
. Therefore, there are no non-trivial pinchpoints in its lattice of cyclic flats, which means that it is irreducible by Theorem
4.27
.

Remark 4.39
.

Example
4.38
is not surprising, because the Vámos matroid has the same lattice of cyclic flats as the Vámos
q
-matroid. Therefore, if the Vámos
q
-matroid was reducible, then the Vámos matroid would also be reducible. This would mean that the Vámos matroid is the free product of two smaller matroids, which must then both be representable. By
[
13
, Proposition 4.13]
, this would imply that the Vámos matroid is representable, which famously is not the case.

5.
Representability of the free product

In this section we study the representability of the free product of

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable
q
-matroids. Using the geometric point of view introduced in
[
2
]
for representable
q
-matroids, we focus on the free product of two uniform
q
-matroids, with particular attention to the case for which both factors have rank one.
It will be convenient for us to set
E_{1}=\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{n_{1}}\rangle

and
E_{2}=E_{1}^{\perp}=\langle e_{n_{1}+1},\dots,e_{n_{1}+n_{2}}\rangle
, where
\{e_{j}:1\leq j\leq n_{1}+n_{2}\}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
.

We recall a geometric description of representable
q
-matroids; see also
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
.

Definition 5.1
.

An
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}

system

\mathcal{S}
is an
n
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, such that

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}=\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
.
Two
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
systems
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are called
equivalent
if there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-isomorphism
\tau\in\GL(k,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
such that
\tau(\mathcal{S})=\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
.
If the parameters are not relevant or clear from the context, we simply say that
\mathcal{S}
is a
q
-
system
.

Definition 5.2
.

For an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, we define the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-rank of
V
to be the integer

r(V)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(\langle V\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}).

We write
r_{\mathcal{S}}
to denote the restriction of the map

r:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k})\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}_{0}
to
\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S})
.

It is not difficult to see that for a
q
-system
\mathcal{S}
,
r_{\mathcal{S}}
is a rank function and hence
(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
defines the
q
-matroid
(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
.
The next geometric interpretation of a representable
q
-matroid in terms of
q
-systems was proved in
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
, whose original statement may look slightly different. However, it is equivalent to the following reformulation.

Theorem 5.3

(
[
2
, Theorem 5.6]
)
.

Let
G\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k\times n}
be a full rank matrix, and let
\mathcal{S}_{G}
be the
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system associated to it, i.e. the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the columns of
G
. Then the
q
-matroid
M[G]
arising from
G
is equivalent to the
q
-matroid
(\mathcal{S}_{G},r_{\mathcal{S}_{G}})
.

Remark 5.4
.

Let
M=(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
be the representable
q
-matroid arising from the
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
-system
\mathcal{S}
. We recall that in
[
2
]
, it has been shown that the independent spaces of
M
are the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-independent subspaces
of
\mathcal{S}
. An
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
I
of
\mathcal{S}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-independent if
r_{\mathcal{S}}(I)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(I)
.
In other words, Theorem
5.3
characterises representable
q
-matroids as those coming from a
q
-system. More precisely, we say that a
q
-matroid
M
of rank
k
without loops is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable
if and only if it is equivalent to a
q
-matroid
(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
, for some
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system
\mathcal{S}
.

We turn to the representability of the free product, with the following result.

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
M_{1}=(E_{1},r_{1})
and
M_{2}=(E_{2},r_{2})
be
q
-matroids of rank
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, respectively. If
M_{1}\square M_{2}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable, then
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable, and
M_{1}\square M_{2}=M[G]
for some matrix

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&X\\
0&G_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{1}
represents
M_{1}
,
G_{2}
represents
M_{2}
, and
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{1}\times n_{2}}
is such that

|\{U\leq\mathcal{S}_{G}:\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(U)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}(\langle U\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}})\}|
is maximal, over all such choices of
X
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M_{1}\square M_{2}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable. Then there exists a matrix
G\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{(k_{1}+k_{2})\times(n_{1}+n_{2})}
that represents
M_{1}\square M_{2}
and which may be assumed to have the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&X\\
0&G_{2}\end{pmatrix},

for some
G_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{i}\times n_{i}}
and
X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{1}\times n_{2}}
.
Clearly,
(M_{1}\square M_{2})|{E_{1}}
and
(M_{1}\square M_{2})/E_{1}
are represented by
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
, respectively. Since
(M_{1}\square M_{2})|{E_{1}}\cong M_{1}
and
(M_{1}\square M_{2})/E_{1}\cong M_{2}
, we have that
M_{i}
is represented by
G_{i}
for each
i
. The rest follows from Corollary
4.13
, since
M_{1}\square M_{2}
is maximal with respect to the weak ordering on
{\mathcal{M}}_{q}(M_{1},M_{2})
.
∎

For the rest of this section, we will adopt the following notation.

Notation 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and

be two

Let
M_{1},M_{2}

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable

and

Consider the

\mathcal{S}

Note that

Clearly,
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}_{1})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{1}))
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}_{2})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\varphi_{2}^{X}(\mathcal{S}_{1}))

Moreover,
\langle\varphi_{1}(\mathcal{S}_{1}),\varphi_{2}^{X}(\mathcal{S}_{2})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\cong\mathcal{S}

.

Given Notation

, let

and

5.1.
The free product of uniform
q
-matroids

This subsection is inspired by the results of
[
3
]
, wherein the representability of the direct sum of uniform
q
-matroids is investigated from a geometric point of view. We will give a necessary condition for a
q
-system to be the representation of the free product of two uniform
q
-matroids. It turns out that for both the direct sum and the free product of uniform
q
-matroids, the cyclic flats play a key role.
We then restrict our attention to the special case of the free product of a pair of rank one uniform
q
-matroids. In this case, we show that a necessary condition for representability is equivalent to the existence of
clubs
on the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-projective line.

We denote by
\PG(k-1,q^{m})
the
(k-1)
-dimensional projective space with underlined vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. For a vector space
V
, we denote by
\PG(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
, its associated projective space.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system. For each
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
V\leq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
, we define the
weight
of

in
\mathcal{S}
to be the integer

\textnormal{wt}_{\mathcal{S}}(V):=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{S}\cap V).

The set

L_{\mathcal{S}}:=\{\langle v\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}:v\in\mathcal{S}\setminus\{0\}\}

is called the

of
\mathcal{S}
of rank
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathcal{S})
in
\PG(k-1,q^{m})
.
We also define the
weight
of the projective space
\PG(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}})
in

to be
\textnormal{wt}_{\mathcal{S}}(V)
, which we denote by
\textnormal{wt}_{L_{\mathcal{S}}}(\PG(V,\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}))
.

We could say that a
q
-system is the vectorial counterpart of a linear set. For our purposes, we do not need a deep background in finite geometry. However, for a nice treatment of linear sets, we refer the interested reader to
[
30
]
.

We now introduce the concept of an
evasive
space, which is a
q
-system with special intersection properties. These objects have been studied in
[
4
]
as a

-analogue of

.
An evasive subspace is a natural generalization of an

-scattered subspace; see
[

,

]
.
Moreover, there are connections to rank-metric codes; see
[
4
,
27
]
.

Definition 5.9
.

Let

be a positive integer.
Let
\mathcal{A}
be a collection of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}

be an

\mathcal{S}
is

\textnormal{wt}_{\mathcal{S}}(A)\leq h\quad\mbox{for all $A\in\mathcal{A}$}.

Definition 5.10
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be an
[n,k]_{q^{m}/q}
system and let

be positive integers such that

. We denote by

the set of all the

-dimensional

\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
. We say that

is
(h,r)
-evasive
if

is

-

. When

, we say that
\mathcal{S}
is
h
-scattered
. Finally, for

a

-scattered

-system will be simply called

.

Let
k_{1},k_{2},k,h
be positive integers such that

and

. We denote by
\Lambda_{h,k_{1}}
the set of all the

-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}
that do not contain

From Theorem
3.14
and Lemma

, we have the following result.

Corollary 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{U}_{k_{i},n_{i}}(q)

Notation 5.12
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{1}

and

) and (

), respectively.

The following result characterises the independent spaces of an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of the free product of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)
and
\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
.

Proposition 5.13
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2},k,n_{1},n_{2},m
be positive integers satisfying
1\leq k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for
i=1,2
and
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{i},n_{i}}(q)
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. The following statements are equivalent.

(i)

(\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2},r_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
.

(ii)

For any
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace
I\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
we have that

r_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}}(I)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(I)\Longleftrightarrow\begin{cases}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(I)\leq k\text{ and}\\
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(I\cap(\mathcal{S}_{1}\oplus\zero))\leq k_{1}.\end{cases}

Proof.

Let
M_{1}=(\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2},r_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}})
and let
M_{2}=\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
. Then,
M_{1}\cong M_{2}
if and only if there exists an invertible
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear map
\psi:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
such that
\mathcal{I}(M_{1})=\mathcal{I}(\psi(M_{2}))
. Let
G
be any generator matrix associated to
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
of the form

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&X\\
0&G_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{1}\times n_{1}}
,
G_{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{2}\times n_{2}}
are generator matrices associated with
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
, respectively. The statement now follows from the characterization of independent spaces of
M_{1}
given in Remark
5.4
, and the characterization of independent spaces of
M_{2}
derived in Corollary
5.11
.
∎

The following result illustrates a necessary condition for
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
to be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
.

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
k_{1},k_{2},k,n_{1},n_{2},m
be positive integers, with
1\leq k_{i}<n_{i}\leq m
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and
k=k_{1}+k_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{i},n_{i}}(q)
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
. If
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
, then
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
is
(\Lambda_{k-1,k_{1}},k-1)
-evasive.

Proof.

Assume, towards a contradiction, that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
is not
(\Lambda_{k-1,k_{1}},k-1)
-evasive. Then, there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-hyperplane
H\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}
, such that
H
does not contain
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{k}\oplus\zero
and
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(H\cap(\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}))\geq k_{1}+k_{2}
. Set
V=H\cap(\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2})
and consider
I\leq V
, such that
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(I)=k_{1}+k_{2}
. Since
\langle I\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\leq H
, we have that

r_{\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}}(I)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}}\langle I\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}<k_{1}+k_{2}.

Hence,
I
is not independent and so by Proposition
5.13
,
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
cannot be a representation of
\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
.
∎

Theorem
5.14
shows that in order to find a representation of

\mathcal{U}_{k_{1},n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{k_{2},n_{2}}(q)
, for which each components
\mathcal{U}_{k_{i},n_{i}}(q)
has an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representations
\mathcal{S}_{i}
, we must find a
(k_{1}\times n_{2})
matrix
X
such that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
is
(\Lambda_{k_{1}+k_{2}-1,k_{1}},k_{1}+k_{2}-1)
-evasive.

For the remainder of this section, we set
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
.
In this case, we will observe that the linear set associated to a
(\Lambda_{1,1},1)
-evasive
(n_{1}+n_{2})
-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}^{2}
is an
n_{1}
-
club
of rank
(n_{1}+n_{2})
in
\PG(1,q^{m})
. These are well-studied objects in finite geometry, which were introduced in 2006 by Fancsali and Sziklai in the seminal paper
[
18
]
.

Definition 5.15
.

An
i
-club of rank

n
in
\PG(1,q^{m})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear set
L_{\mathcal{S}}
of rank
k
for which all but one of its elements have weight one, while exactly one element has weight
i
.

It is known that in order for a club to exist, we must have
n\leq m
. In the case that the rank is maximal, i.e.
n=m
,
L_{\mathcal{S}}
is simply called an
i
-club
. In the literature,
i
-clubs are the most studied among clubs. The interest in clubs was renewed when De Boeck and Van de Voorde in
[
15
]
characterised the translation KM-arcs (
[
26
]
) exactly as those
that can be described by
i
-clubs in even characteristic. A first algebraic construction was already given in
[
26
]
and the corresponding geometrical construction can be found in
[
19
]
. Moreover,
i
-clubs also define linear blocking sets of Rédei type and they define Hamming metric codes with few weights; see
[
28
]
. The algebraic description of
i
-clubs has been recently
investigated in
[
5
]
, under the name of
1
-fat polynomials; see also
[
31
]
. Constructions of
(n-1)
- and
(n-2)
-clubs in
\PG(1,q^{n})
, as well as
t(\ell-1)
- and
t(\ell-1)+1
-clubs in
\PG(1,q^{rt})
are known; see
[
29
]
and the references therein. Finally, we observe that in
[
16
]
it has been shown that
2
-clubs of rank
5
in
\PG(1,q^{5})
do not exist.

The following result is a specialization of Theorem
5.14
when
k_{1}=k_{2}=1
.

Theorem 5.16
.

Let
n_{1},n_{2},m
be positive integers, with
1\leq n_{i}\leq m
for
i\in\{1,2\}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{i}
be an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{i}}(q)
for each
i\in\{1,2\}
and let
\mathcal{S}:=\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
. The following are equivalent.

(i)

(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{2}}(q)
.

(ii)

L_{\mathcal{S}}
is an
n_{1}
-club of rank
n
in
\PG(1,q^{m})
.

Proof.

(i)
\Longrightarrow
(ii)
: By Theorem
5.14
,
\mathcal{S}
is
(\Lambda_{1,1},1)
-evasive. This means that every point in
L_{\mathcal{S}}
has weight equal to
1
, except the point
\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
, which has weight
n_{1}
. Hence,
L_{\mathcal{S}}
is an
n_{1}
-club of rank
n
in
\PG(1,q^{m})
.

(ii)
\Longrightarrow
(i)
: Let
P=\langle(1,0)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\in\PG(1,q^{m})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
P
is the element of
L_{\mathcal{S}}
of weight equal to
n_{1}
. By the definition of a club, all the other points
\langle(x,y)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
in
L_{\mathcal{S}}
have multiplicity
1
, hence
\mathcal{S}
is
(\Lambda_{1,1},1)
-evasive.
Let
P_{1}=\langle(x_{1},y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}},P_{2}=\langle(x_{2},y_{2})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}\in L_{\mathcal{S}}
, with
y_{1},y_{2}\neq 0
and
y_{1}\neq y_{2}
. If
y_{2}=\lambda y_{1}
, with
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, then we can assume without loss of generality that
P_{1}=\langle(0,1)\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
. Then the point
\textnormal{wt}_{\mathcal{S}}(P_{2})\geq 2
, since
x_{2}(P+\lambda P_{1})=P_{2}
. This implies that for every pair of points
\langle(x_{1},y_{1})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
, and
\langle(x_{2},y_{2})\rangle_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}}
that are different from
P
, it must be the case that
y_{1},y_{2}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Hence,
(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
is a representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{2}}(q)
.
∎

From Theorem
5.16
, we see that if
\mathcal{S}_{1}\square_{X}\mathcal{S}_{2}
represents
\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{1}}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,n_{2}}(q)
, then it has to be an
n_{1}
-club of rank
n_{1}+n_{2}
in
\PG(1,q^{n})
. We remark that this condition is not easy to handle and, in particular, depends on the choice of representations
\mathcal{S}_{1}
and
\mathcal{S}_{2}
as the next example illustrates.

Example 5.17
.

Consider
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)
, which is known to be
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
-representable for
m\geq 2
; see
[
22
, Example 2.4]
. Moreover, for every
m\geq 2
, any matrix
\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}
, with
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}\setminus\mathbb{F}_{q}
, is a representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)
. Suppose that
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)
is represented over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{m}}
by the matrix

G=\begin{pmatrix}G_{1}&X\\
0&G_{2}\end{pmatrix},

where
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
represent
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(q)
and
X=\begin{pmatrix}x_{1}&x_{2}\end{pmatrix}
. As we observed before, the linear set arising from the
q
-system associated with
G
must be a
2
-club of rank
4
in
\PG(1,q^{m})
and hence
m\geq 4
. Moreover, we may assume that
x_{1}=0
.
Let
q=2
,
m=4
and
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}(\alpha)
with
\alpha^{4}=\alpha+1
. Choose
G_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}
,

G_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha^{4}\\
\end{pmatrix}
and
x_{2}=\alpha^{11}
. We obtain that

G=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&0&\alpha^{11}\\
0&0&1&\alpha^{4}\end{pmatrix}

is an
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)
.
In order to check this, we considered the
q
-matroid arising from
G
and with the aid of
magma
we found that its cyclic flats are exactly
\boldsymbol{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{4}
, whose ranks are
0,1,2
, respectively. Hence, they are the cyclic flats of the free product of two uniform
q
-matroids, by Theorem
3.14
.
In the same way can observe that it is not possible to find any
x_{2}
such that the matrix

G=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&0&x_{2}\\
0&0&1&\alpha^{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}^{2\times 4}

represents
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)
over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{4}}
.

We conclude this section by providing an example of a
2
-club of rank
5
in
\PG(1,2^{7})
. To the best of our knowledge, such clubs have not been constructed previously. By Theorem
5.16
, constructing a club
L_{\mathcal{S}}\subseteq\PG(1,2^{7})
is equivalent to finding an
\mathbb{F}_{2^{7}}
-representation
(\mathcal{S},r_{\mathcal{S}})
of
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,3}(2)
.

Let
\mathbb{F}_{2^{7}}=\mathbb{F}_{2}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha^{7}+\alpha+1=0
. Let
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)
be represented over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{7}}
by
\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha\end{pmatrix}
and
let
\mathcal{U}_{1,3}(2)
be represented over
\mathbb{F}_{2^{7}}
by
\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{8}\end{pmatrix}
.
Let
\mathcal{S}
be the
q
-system associated with

G=\begin{pmatrix}1&\alpha&0&\alpha^{36}&\alpha^{24}\\
0&0&1&\alpha^{2}&\alpha^{8}\end{pmatrix}.

With the aid of
magma
, we find that the cyclic flats of
M[G]
are exactly
\boldsymbol{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{2}\oplus\boldsymbol{0},\mathbb{F}_{2}^{5}
. By Theorem
3.14
, we have that
\mathcal{S}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{2^{7}}
-representation of
\mathcal{U}_{1,2}(2)\square\mathcal{U}_{1,3}(2)
and hence
L_{\mathcal{S}}
is a
2
-club of rank
5
in
\PG(1,2^{7})
.

6.
Open Problems

We have initiated the study of the free product and on the representibility of the free product.
Our study gives rise to several research questions. We list a few of them.

(1)

We showed in Example
4.15
that
M_{1}\oplus M_{2}
is not in general minimal in
\mathcal{M}_{q}(M_{1},M_{2})
. In fact, if
2\leq\min\{|\mathcal{Z}(M_{1})|,|\mathcal{Z}(M_{2})|\}
, then it is not minimal. This marked difference between matroids and
q
-matroids, raises the question of deriving a formula (if possible) for the number of isomorphism classes of
q
-matroids on
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
with variables the number of isomorphism classes of matroids on
n
elements, as well as
q
.

(2)

We showed in Example
5.17
that the existence of
X
depends on the choice of the representations of
M_{1}
and
M_{2}
and on the field size
q^{m}
. In particular, it would be interesting to establish the properties that a matrix
X
should satisfy in order to provide conditions for the representability of the free product.

(3)

In Theorem
5.14
we showed that in order to provide a representation of the free product of two uniform
q
-matroids of ranks
k_{1}
and
k_{2}
, it is necessary to find a
q
-system which is also
(\Lambda_{k_{1}+k_{2}-1,k_{1}},k_{1}+k_{2}-1)
evasive. It is an open problem to know if this condition is also sufficient.

(4)

Most of the literature on
i
-clubs involves clubs of maximum rank, which do not exist for all parameters. Most relevant to our results, is to know the smallest field
q^{m}
, for which it is possible to find an
i
-club on
\PG(1,q^{m})
. This will provide the smallest field over which the free product of rank one uniform
q
-matroids is representable.

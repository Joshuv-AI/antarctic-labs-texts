1.
Introduction

Given a space
X
with Postnikov tower
\ldots\to P_{2}(X)\to B\pi_{1}(X)
, the (first)
k
-invariant of
X
is the obstruction
k_{X}\in H^{3}(B\pi_{1}(X);\pi_{2}(X))
to finding a section of
P_{2}(X)\to B\pi_{1}(X)
.
This invariant, first introduced in
[
2
]
, is a mainstay in algebraic topology, but
also plays a role in geometric topology and, more specifically, in the (homotopy) classification of manifolds.
For example, the homotopy type of a closed oriented
4
-manifold
X
with finite fundamental group is often determined by its quadratic
2
-type
(\pi_{1}(X),\pi_{2}(X),k_{X},\lambda_{X})
, where
\lambda_{X}
denotes the equivariant intersection form of
X
; see e.g.
[
3
]
.
Motivated by upcoming work on the homotopy classification of
4
-manifolds with nonempty boundary
[
1
]
, the present article is concerned with relative
k
-invariants and collects several results on the topic that might be known to experts but that we have not been able to find in the literature.

Statement of results

Let
(X,Y)
be a pair of spaces that is homotopy equivalent to a CW pair, let
c\colon X\to P_{2}(X)
be the Postnikov

-type of
X

for the mapping cylinder of

.
The relative

-invariant refers to a cohomology class

The definition of this class is described precisely in Section
5
, but for the moment we note that
k_{X,Y}^{\nu}
is the obstruction to the existence of a dashed map
making the following diagram commute:

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces c|_{Y}}

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X_{0},Y_{0})
and
(X_{1},Y_{1})
be pairs of spaces that are homotopy equivalent to CW pairs, and let
c_{1}\colon X_{1}\to P_{2}(X_{1})
be the Postnikov
2
-type of
X_{1}
.
For a map
h\colon Y_{0}\to Y_{1}
, an isomorphism
u\colon\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to\pi_{1}(X_{1})
with
u\circ(\iota_{0})_{*}=(\iota_{1})_{*}\circ h
, and an
u
-equivariant homomorphism
F\colon\pi_{2}(X_{0})\to\penalty\ \pi_{2}(X_{1})
, the following assertions are equivalent:

•

there is a map

such that

(c_{0})_{*}=u\colon\pi_{1}(X_{0})\to\pi_{1}(P_{2}(X_{1}))\cong\pi_{1}(X_{1}),\quad c_{1}\circ h\simeq c_{0}|_{Y_{0}},\quad\text{and}

(c_{0})_{*}=F\colon\pi_{2}(X_{0})\to\pi_{2}(P_{2}(X_{1}))\xleftarrow{(c_{1})_{*},\cong}\pi_{2}(X_{1});

•

the relative
k
-invariants satisfy

(u,h)^{*}(k_{X_{1},Y_{1}}^{\nu_{1}})=F_{*}(k_{X_{0},Y_{0}}^{\nu_{0}})\in H^{3}(M(\nu_{0}|_{Y_{0}}),Y_{0};\Res_{u}\pi_{2}(X_{1}))

for every
\nu_{i}\colon X_{i}\to B\pi_{1}(X_{i})
that induces the identity on
\pi_{1}
for

.
The map

is defined in

.

Here, a homomorphism

Equivalently,

-linear map

Remark 1.2
.

The reason we work with spaces that are merely homotopy equivalent to CW pairs (instead of working with CW pairs as is common in the literature) is to ensure that our results apply to
4
-dimensional topological manifolds.

If
(X_{0},Y_{0})
and
(X_{1},Y_{1})
are CW pairs, Theorem
1.1
admits the following reformulation.

Corollary 1.3
.

Let
(X_{0},Y_{0})
and
(X_{1},Y_{1})
be CW pairs, and let
\iota_{j}\colon Y_{j}\to X_{j}
be the inclusion for

.
For a group isomorphism

, a map

satisfying
u\circ(\iota_{0})_{*}=(\iota_{1})_{*}\circ h
, and an
u

F\colon\pi_{2}(X_{0})\to\pi_{2}(X_{1})
, the following assertions are equivalent:

•

there is a map
f\colon X_{0}^{(3)}\cup Y_{0}\to X_{1}
that extends

and induces
u
and
F
;

•

the relative
k
-invariants satisfy

(u,h)^{*}(k_{X_{1},Y_{1}}^{\nu_{1}})=F_{*}(k_{X_{0},Y_{0}}^{\nu_{0}})\in H^{3}(M(\nu_{0}|_{Y_{0}}),Y_{0};\pi_{2}(X_{1}))

for every
\nu_{i}\colon X_{i}\to B\pi_{1}(X_{i})
that induces the identity on
\pi_{1}
for

.

Proof.

By

, it suffices to show that there exists a map
f\colon X_{0}^{(3)}\cup Y_{0}\to X_{1}
as in this corollary if and only if there exists a map

as in

.
In one direction, starting from

, since the inclusion
X_{0}^{(3)}\cup Y_{0}\to X_{0}
induces
a homotopy equivalence on Postnikov
2
-types, the required

arises as the composition

X_{0}\to P_{2}(X_{0})\simeq P_{2}(X_{0}^{(3)}\cup Y_{0})\xrightarrow{P_{2}(f)}P_{2}(X_{1}).

It remains to construct

from

.
For this, choose a model for
P_{2}(X_{i})
that is obtained from

by attaching cells of dimension

.
Homotoping

if necessary (using cellular approximation), we assume that it is cellular.
Restricting this map to

leads to a map

that induces

and

and such that

.
Since

, the homotopy extension property shows that
f^{\prime}
is homotopic to a map

that induces
u
and
F
and such that
f^{\prime\prime}|_{Y_{0}^{(3)}}=\iota_{Y_{1}}\circ h|_{Y_{0}^{(3)}}
.
Using

to extend

over the rest of

∎

Conventions

Unless specified otherwise, spaces are assumed to path-connected and equipped with a basepoint.
Maps are assumed to be basepoint preserving and if
(X,Y)
is a pair, the basepoint is assumed to lie in
Y
; if
Y
is empty, we assume that
x\in X
.
Throughout this paper, for every space
X
, we choose a model
B\pi_{1}(X)
and assume that all maps
X\to B\pi_{1}(X)
that we consider induce the identity on fundamental groups.
The mapping cylinder of a map
f\colon X\to Y
is denoted
M(f)
, whereas the mapping cone of a chain map
g\colon C\to D
is denoted
\operatorname{Cone}(g)
.

2.
Preparatory lemmas

This section sets up our conventions on chain complexes and proves some preliminary lemmas needed to define and study relative
k
-invariants.

In what follows, chain complexes are assumed to be free and have no chain modules in negative dimensions.
Given a chain complex
K_{*}
, we write
B_{i}(K)\subseteq K_{i}
for the boundaries, and
Z_{i}(K)\subseteq K_{i}
for the cycles of degree
i
.
As in
[
2
, page 54]
, an
augmented

\mathbb{Z}[\pi]
-chain complex
(K_{*},\operatorname{aug})
is a chain complex
K_{*}
together with a surjective map
\operatorname{aug}\colon K_{0}\to\mathbb{Z}
, such that

\ldots\to K_{1}\xrightarrow{d_{1}}K_{0}\xrightarrow{\operatorname{aug}}\mathbb{Z}\to 0

is again a
\mathbb{Z}[\pi]
-chain complex.
In other words, one requires
\operatorname{aug}(\gamma x)=\operatorname{aug}(x)
for every
x\in K_{0}
and every
\gamma\in\pi
as well as
\operatorname{aug}\circ d_{1}=0
.
We frequently omit the map
\operatorname{aug}
from the notation and just say that
K_{*}
is an augmented chain complex.
Additionally, we set
\widetilde{H}_{0}(K):=\ker(\operatorname{aug})/B_{0}(K)
and say that
K_{*}
is
acyclic in dimensions
<q
if
\widetilde{H}_{0}(K)=0
and
H_{i}(K)=0
for
0<i<q
.
Finally, our notation for truncations is

K_{*}|_{[0,n]}:=\left(0\to K_{n}\to K_{n-1}\ldots\to K_{1}\to K_{0}\to 0\right).

The next lemma is a relative version of the uniquness portion of
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
which, roughly speaking, states that if
K_{*}^{\prime}
is a chain complex that is acyclic in dimensions
<q
, then for any chain complex
K_{*}
there is, up to homotopy, a unique
chain map
K_{*}|_{[0,q-1]}\to K^{\prime}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K_{*},K_{*}^{\prime}
be augmentable
\mathbb{Z}[\pi]
-chain complexes with
K_{*}^{\prime}
acyclic in dimensions
<q
, let
L_{*}\leq K_{*}
and
L_{*}^{\prime}\leq K_{*}^{\prime}
be subcomplexes such that
L_{i}\leq K_{i}
and
L_{i}^{\prime}\leq K_{i}^{\prime}
are summands for each
i
.
If there exists two chain maps
\alpha,\beta\colon K_{*}\to K_{*}^{\prime}

(1)

that induce chain maps between the augmented chain complexes,i.e.

\operatorname{aug}\circ\beta_{0}=\operatorname{aug}=\operatorname{aug}\circ\alpha_{0}\colon K_{0}\to\mathbb{Z},

(2)

whose restrictions
\alpha|_{L},\beta|_{L}\colon L_{*}\to L_{*}^{\prime}
are chain homotopic via a chain homotopy

\psi:=\{\psi_{i}\colon L_{i}\to L_{i+1}^{\prime}\}_{i}\colon\alpha|_{L}\simeq\beta|_{L},

then
\alpha,\beta
are chain homotopic in dimensions
\leq q-1
via a chain homotopy

D:=\{D_{i}\colon K_{i}\to K_{i+1}^{\prime}\}_{i\leq q-1}

that agrees with
\psi
on
L|_{[0,q-1]}.

Proof.

The proof is a small modification of the proof of
[
2
, Theorem 5.1]
and proceeds by induction on
i
.
We begin by defining
D_{0}\colon K_{0}\to K_{1}^{\prime}
that extends
\psi_{0}
.
Since
K_{*}^{\prime}
is acyclic, we have
\widetilde{H}_{0}(K^{\prime})=\ker(\operatorname{aug})/B_{0}(K^{\prime})=0
.
Since
\operatorname{aug}\circ(\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0})=0
, the map
\alpha_{0}-\beta_{0}\colon K_{0}\to K_{0}^{\prime}
takes values in
\ker(\operatorname{aug})=B_{0}(K^{\prime})
.
Lift this map to
D_{0}\colon K_{0}\to K_{1}^{\prime}
by defining it on
L_{0}
to be
\psi_{0}
and extending it arbitrarily over the rest of
C_{0}
.

The proof of the induction step is now entirely analogous to the argument from
[
2
]
.
The only difference is that when lifting
E_{i}:=\alpha_{i}-\beta_{i}-D_{i-1}\circ d_{i}^{K}\colon C_{i}\to B_{i}(K^{\prime})
to

to be
\psi_{i}
on
L_{i}\leq K_{i}
but pick an arbitrary lift on the remainder of
K_{i}
.
∎

Applying
[
2
, Proposition 5.1]
to the case where

is acyclic (i.e.

shows that, up to chain homotopy, there is a unique map

that preserves augmentations

.
In particular, this applies if

is a free resolution of

Notation 2.2
.

For the rest of the section, we fix a free

-resolution

Additionally, given a chain map

Since

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
K_{*}
be an augmented
\mathbb{Z}[\pi]
-chain complex that is acyclic in dimensions
<2
, let

be a chain map,
and set

There is an augmentation-preserving chain map

such that
\alpha\circ t_{L}
and

are chain homotopic as maps

.

Given an augmentation-preserving chain map
\alpha\colon C_{*}^{\pi}|_{[0,2]}\to K_{*}|_{[0,2]}
, such a
\phi
exists.

Proof.

Since
K_{*}
is acyclic in dimensions
<2
, there is a free resolution
F
of
\mathbb{Z}
containing
K_{*}
as a subcomplex with

for

Pick an augmentation-preserving chain homotopy equivalence
\widehat{\alpha}\colon C_{*}^{\pi}\to F
and define

\alpha:=\widehat{\alpha}|_{[0,2]}\colon C_{*}^{\pi}|_{[0,2]}\to F_{*}|_{[0,2]}=K_{*}|_{[0,2]}.

It remains to define

and verify that
\alpha\circ t_{L}
and

are chain homotopic as maps

.
Let

By uniqueness of

It follows that

\alpha\circ t_{L}=\widehat{\alpha}\circ t\circ i_{L}\simeq\widehat{\alpha}\circ\widehat{\alpha}^{-1}\circ\iota\circ i_{L}\simeq\iota\circ i_{L}\colon L|_{[0,2]}\to F_{*}.

Let

be a chain homotopy and define

\phi:=d_{3}^{F}\circ D_{2}\colon L_{2}\to\ker(d_{2}^{F})=\ker(d_{2}^{K}).

Since

, a chain homotopy

.

It remains to prove the last statement.

\alpha\colon C_{*}^{\pi}|_{[0,2]}\to K_{*}|_{[0,2]}=F_{*}|_{[0,2]}
, there is a chain homotopy equivalence

with

We conclude with a result concerning mapping cones.
Here, recall that the mapping cone of a chain map

is the chain complex with

.

If a chain complex
K_{*}^{\prime}
is acyclic in degrees

, then any homotopy commutative diagram of chain maps

3.
Relative
k
-invariants of chain complexes.

This section defines the
k
-invariant associated to an augmented chain complex
K_{*}
and a chain map
i_{L}\colon L_{*}\to K_{*}
.
Our approach follows that of Eilenberg-Maclane
[
2
]
which treats the absolute case.
For the remainder of the section, we continue to fix a free resolution
C_{*}^{\pi}
of
\mathbb{Z}
.

Construction 3.1
.

Let
K_{*}
be an augmented chain complex that is acyclic in dimensions
<2
, let
i_{L}\colon L_{*}\to K_{*}
be a chain map, let
t\colon K_{*}\to C_{*}^{\pi}
be a chain map
that preserves augmentations, set
t_{L}:=t\circ i_{L}
, let
\operatorname{Cone}(t_{L})_{*}
be the mapping cone of
t_{L}
, and let
\alpha\colon C_{*}^{\pi}|_{[0,2]}\to K_{*}|_{[0,2]}
and let
\phi\colon L_{2}\to Z_{2}(K)
be as in Lemma
2.3
.
Consider the cochain

\theta_{K,L}^{t}\colon\operatorname{Cone}(t_{L})_{3}=C_{3}^{\pi}\oplus L_{2}\xrightarrow{(\alpha_{2}\circ d_{3}^{\pi},\phi)}Z_{2}(K)\to H_{2}(K).

To see that this map is a cocycle
\operatorname{Cone}(t_{L})_{3}\to H_{2}(K)
note that since
\alpha\circ t_{L}\simeq i_{L}+\phi\colon L_{*}|_{[0,2]}\to K_{*}
, we have

\alpha_{2}\circ d_{3}^{\pi}\circ(t_{L})_{3}=\alpha_{2}\circ(t_{L})_{2}\circ d_{3}^{L}\simeq(i_{L})_{2}\circ d_{3}^{L}+\phi\circ d_{3}^{L}=d_{3}^{K}\circ(i_{L})_{3}+\phi\circ d_{3}^{L}\equiv\phi\circ d_{3}^{L}

in
H_{2}(K)
.
It follows that
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha_{2}\circ d_{3}^{\pi}&\phi\end{smallmatrix}\right)\left(\begin{smallmatrix}d_{4}^{\pi}&(t_{L})_{3}\\
0&-d_{3}^{L}\end{smallmatrix}\right)=0.

We show in Lemma
3.3
that the homology class of
\theta_{K,L}^{t}
does not depend on the choice of
(\alpha,\phi)
, thus leading to the following definition.

Definition 3.2
.

Remark 5.7
.

We briefly discuss the sense in which the relative
k
-invariant is an invariant of the pair
(X,Y)
up to homotopy equivalences that restrict to the identity on
Y
.
If
(X,Y)
and
(X^{\prime},Y)
are pairs of spaces that are homotopy equivalent to CW pairs and
f\colon X\to X^{\prime}
is a homotopy equivalence that restricts to the identity on
Y
, then Theorem
5.6
shows
(f_{*},\operatorname{id})^{*}(k_{X^{\prime},Y}^{\nu^{\prime}})=f_{*}(k_{X,Y}^{\nu})
for every
\nu\colon X\to B\pi_{1}(X)
and
\nu^{\prime}\colon X\to B\pi_{1}(X^{\prime})
.
Thus, if one uses

to identify
\pi_{i}(X)
with
\pi_{i}(X^{\prime})
for

, as is common for the usual (absolute)

-invariant, and chooses
\nu
and
\nu^{\prime}
such that
\nu|_{Y}=\nu^{\prime}|_{Y}
, then the relative
k
-invariants of
(X,Y)
and
(X^{\prime},Y)
agree.

We can now prove Theorem
1.1
from the introduction.

Theorem

.

Let

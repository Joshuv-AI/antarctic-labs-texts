A new spectral Turán theorem for weighted graphs and consequences
Lele Liu, Bo Ning

Abstract
Confirming a conjecture of Elphick and Edwards and strengthening a spectral theorem of Wilf, Nikiforov proved that for any K r + 1 K_{r+1} -free graph G G , λ ​ ( G ) 2 ≤ 2 ​ ( 1 − 1 / r ) ​ m \lambda(G)^{2}\leq 2(1-1/r)m , where λ ⁡ ( G ) \lambda(G) is the spectral radius of G G , and m m is the number of edges of G G . This result was later improved in [ 15 ] , where it was shown that for any graph G G , λ ​ ( G ) 2 ≤ 2 ​ ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) cl ⁡ ( e ) − 1 cl ⁡ ( e ) \lambda(G)^{2}\leq 2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)} , where cl ⁡ ( e ) \cl(e) denotes the order of the largest clique containing the edge e e . In this paper, we further extend this inequality to weighted graphs, proving that λ ​ ( G ) 2 ≤ 2 ​ ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) cl ⁡ ( e ) − 1 cl ⁡ ( e ) ​ w ​ ( e ) 2 , \lambda(G)^{2}\leq 2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}, and we characterize all extremal graphs attaining this bound. Our main theorem yields several new consequences, including two vertex-based and vertex-degree-based local versions of Turán’s theorem, as well as weighted generalizations of the Edwards–Elphick theorem and the Cvetković theorem, and localized versions of Wilf’s two theorems. Moreover, the result unifies and implies numerous earlier ones from spectral graph theory and extremal graph theory, including Stanley’s spectral inequality, Hong’s inequality, a localized Turán-type theorem, and a recent extremal theorem by Adak and Chandran. Notably, while Nikiforov’s earlier spectral inequality implied Stanley’s bound, it did not imply Hong’s inequality — a gap that is now bridged by our result. As a key tool, we establish the inequality ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) 2 cl ⁡ ( e ) ≥ n − 1 \sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{2}{\cl(e)}\geq n-1 , which complements an upper bound ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ) 2 cl ⁡ ( e ) − 1 ≤ n 2 − 2 ​ m \sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{2}{\cl(e)-1}\leq n^{2}-2m due to Bradač, and Malec and Tompkins, independently. Keywords: Localized spectral Turán problem; Spectral radius; Spectral inequality

A
weighted graph

G

(V(G),E(G),w)
, where
V(G)
is the vertex set,

E(G)
is the edge set, and
w:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}

When all edge weights are
1

adjacency matrix
of

n\times n

A(G)=[a_{ij}]
, where
a_{ij}=w(e)

if
e=ij\in E(G)
, and
0
otherwise. The
spectral radius
of
G
, denoted
\lambda(G)
, is the
maximum of the absolute values of the eigenvalues of
A(G)
. For a matrix
M\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times n}
,
the
Frobenius norm
of
M=[m_{ij}]
is defined as
\|M\|_{F}:=\big(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}m_{ij}^{2}\big)^{1/2}
.
Let
A=[a_{ij}]
and
B=[b_{ij}]
be two matrices of the same dimensions.
The
Hadamard product

A\circ B
of
A
and
B
is defined via element-wise multiplication:

(A\circ B)_{ij}=a_{ij}b_{ij}
. For a subset
S\subseteq[n]:=\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
,
the
characteristic vector
of
S
is the vector
\bm{1}_{S}\in\{0,1\}^{n}
whose
i

entry is
1
if
i\in S
and
0
otherwise.

For a weighted graph
G
and an edge
e\in E(G)
, let
\cl_{G}(e)
(or
\cl(e)
in short)
denote the order of the largest clique containing

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
G
be a weighted graph with edge weight

for each edge

\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}}.

(1.1)

Equality holds if and only if
G
is, up to isolated vertices, a complete
r
-partite graph
for some
r

(

)
, and
there exists a vector
\bm{w}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that

(1)

A(G)=\pm\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}
; and

(2)

\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}=\|\bm{w}\|^{2}-\sqrt{1-1/r}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}

2
Consequences

What’s the meaning of a localized version of extremal theorem in Corollary
1.1
?
In extremal graph theory, a central problem is to determine the maximum number of edges, denoted
by
\ex(n,H)
, in an
n
-vertex graph that does not contain a forbidden subgraph
H
. A classical
result in this area is Turán’s theorem, which resolves the case when
H=K_{r+1}
, showing that
the unique extremal graph is the balanced complete
r
-partite Turán graph. This imposes a
global

constraint, restricting the total number of edges. A more refined approach considers
local

constraints. Instead of forbidding the presence of a
K_{r+1}
globally, one may study local
conditions, such as bounding a function defined over the edges — for example,

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}
. This leads to a
localized
version of the
Turán problem, offering a finer perspective on extremal graph properties.

Recall the following result due to Bradač
[
6
]
,
and Malec and Tompkins
[
16
]
, independently.
Applying Theorem
1.1
allows for a very concise proof of Corollary
2.1
,
which differs from the inductive proof in
[
16
]
.

Corollary 2.1

(
[
6
,
16
]
)
.

Let
G
be any
n
-vertex graph. Then

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}\leq\frac{n^{2}}{2}.

Proof.

For the graph
G
, we assign to each edge
e\in E(G)
a weight
w(e)
defined by

w(e)=\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}
. The total edge weight of
G
is then

w(G)=\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}.

By Rayleigh’s inequality,
\lambda(G)\geq 2w(G)/n
. Combining this with Theorem (
1.1
)
and
w(e)=\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}
, we obtain

\frac{2}{n}\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}=\frac{2w(G)}{n}\leq\lambda(G)\leq\sqrt{2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}}.

Solving the above inequality yields the desired result.
∎

[

]
on paths.

3
A new localized spectral Turán theorem for weighted graphs

The goal of this section is to present the proof of a new localized spectral Turán theorem for weighted graphs.
Before the proof, we need some additional notation. Let
\triangle^{n-1}
be the standard simplex:

\triangle^{n-1}:=\bigg\{(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}\geq 0,i\in[n]\ \text{and}\ \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}=1\bigg\}.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with clique number
\omega(G)
. The well-known Motzkin–Straus
Theorem
[
17
]
states that

\max\{\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{z}:\bm{z}\in\triangle^{n-1}\}=1-\frac{1}{\omega(G)}.

(3.1)

The above equation established a remarkable connection between the clique number and the Lagrangian of a graph.

Additionally, Motzkin and Straus also gave a characterization on equality
\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{z}=1-1/\omega(G)
.
Given a vector
\bm{x}
, the
support
of
\bm{x}
, denoted by
\supp(\bm{x})
, is the set consisting of all indices
corresponding to nonzero entries in
\bm{x}
.

Proposition 3.1

(
[
17
]
)
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\omega(G)=\omega
, and let
\bm{x}\in\triangle^{n-1}
.
Then
\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{x}=1-1/\omega
holds if and only if the induced subgraph of
G
on

\supp(\bm{x})
is a complete
\omega
-partite graph whose vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\omega}

satisfy
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=1/\omega
for all
i\in[\omega]
.

We will use the following lemma. Its proof follows an argument similar to one in
[
15
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph, and
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
be a nonnegative symmetric matrix.
If
\bm{x}\in\triangle^{n-1}
is a vector that attains
\max\{\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A(G))\bm{z}:\bm{z}\in\triangle^{n-1}\}
,
then

(1)

\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A(G))\bm{e}_{i}=\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A(G))\bm{e}_{j}

for any
i,j\in\supp(\bm{x})
. Here
\bm{e}_{i}
is the
i
-th column of the identity matrix of order
n
.

(2)

If furthermore,
\bm{x}
has minimal support set, then
\supp(\bm{x})
induces a clique in
G
.

Proof.

For brevity, we denote
A:=A(G)
and
F_{G}(\bm{z}):=\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}\big(W\circ A\big)\bm{z}

for
\bm{z}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Assume that
\bm{x}\in\triangle^{n-1}
is a vector that attains the maximum of

F_{G}(\,\cdot\,)
over
\triangle^{n-1}
.

(1) Let
i,j\in\supp(\bm{x})
. We assume, without loss of generality, towards contradiction that

\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}W\bm{e}_{i}>\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{j}
.
Let
0<\varepsilon<\min\{x_{i},x_{j}\}
. Denote
\bm{y}:=\bm{x}+\varepsilon(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})
.
As
W\circ A
has zero diagonal,
\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{i}=0
. Then

\displaystyle F_{G}(\bm{y})-F_{G}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})+\varepsilon^{2}(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})-2\varepsilon^{2}\bm{e}_{i}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{j}.

Choosing
\varepsilon
to be sufficiently small gives
F_{G}(\bm{y})>F_{G}(\bm{x})
,
a contradiction.

(2) Let
\bm{x}
have minimal support set. Assume for contradiction that there exist
distinct
i,j\in\supp(\bm{x})
such that
ij\notin E(G)
. Without loss of generality,
assume
\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{i}\geq\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{j}
.

Consider the vector
\bm{y}=\bm{x}+x_{j}(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})
, and set

\bm{e}:=\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j}
. A direct calculation shows that
\bm{y}\in\triangle^{n-1}
, and

\displaystyle F_{G}(\bm{y})-F_{G}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\bm{y}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{y}-\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{x}

\displaystyle=2x_{j}\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}+x_{j}^{2}\bm{e}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}.

Since
ij\notin E(G)
, we have
\bm{e}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}=0
. Consequently,

F_{G}(\bm{y})-F_{G}(\bm{x})=2x_{j}\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}=2x_{j}\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)(\bm{e}_{i}-\bm{e}_{j})\geq 0.

This implies that
\bm{y}
also attains the maximum of
F_{G}(\,\cdot\,)
over
\triangle^{n-1}
.
However,
\supp(\bm{y})<\supp(\bm{x})
, which contradicts the minimality of the support of
\bm{x}
.
So we finish the proof of item (2).
∎

For a graph
G
on
n
vertices and no isolated vertices, we define the
n\times n
symmetric matrix

W(G)=[w_{ij}]
by

w_{ij}=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{\cl(i)}{\cl(i)-1}+\frac{\cl(j)}{\cl(j)-1}\bigg).

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph with
\omega(G)=\omega
and no isolated vertices, and
let
\bm{x}\in\triangle^{n-1}
. Then

\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}\big(W(G)\circ A(G)\big)\bm{x}\leq 1.

Equality holds if and only if the induced subgraph of
G
on
\supp(\bm{x})
is a complete
\omega
-partite graph
whose vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\omega}
satisfy
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=1/\omega
for all
i\in[\omega]
.

Proof.

For brevity, we denote
W:=W(G)
,
A:=A(G)
and
F_{G}(\bm{z}):=\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{z}
.
We first assume
\bm{z}\in\triangle^{n-1}
is a vector that attains the maximum of

F_{G}(\,\cdot\,)
over
\triangle^{n-1}
, and has minimal support set.

By Lemma
3.1
,
\supp(\bm{z})
forms a clique
K
in
G
. Hence,

F_{G}(\bm{z})=\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{z}=\sum_{ij\in E(K)}\bigg(\frac{\cl(i)}{\cl(i)-1}+\frac{\cl(j)}{\cl(j)-1}\bigg)z_{i}z_{j}.

Observing that
x/(x-1)
is a decreasing function in
x
, we obtain

F_{G}(\bm{z})\leq\frac{2|K|}{|K|-1}\sum_{ij\in E(K)}z_{i}z_{j}\leq 1,

where the last inequality follows by applying (
3.1
) to the clique
K
.

In the following we characterize the equality for
F_{G}(\bm{x})=1
. If
G[\supp(\bm{x})]
is a
complete
\omega
-partite graph whose vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\omega}
satisfy

\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=1/\omega
for all
i\in[\omega]
, then
\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{x}=1-1/\omega

by Proposition
3.1
. Since
\cl(u)\leq\omega
for each
u\in V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle F_{G}(\bm{x})

\displaystyle=\sum_{ij\in E(G)}\bigg(\frac{\cl(i)}{\cl(i)-1}+\frac{\cl(j)}{\cl(j)-1}\bigg)x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\omega}{\omega-1}\sum_{ij\in E(G)}x_{i}x_{j}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{\omega-1}\cdot\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}A(G)\bm{x}=1,

which yields that
F_{G}(\bm{x})=1
.

For the other direction, assume that
F_{G}(\bm{x})=1
. We proceed by induction on
n
to demonstrate that

G[\supp(\bm{x})]
is a complete
\omega
-partite graph with vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\omega}
,
and
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=1/\omega
for all
i\in[\omega]
. The base case
n=2
is clear. Now assume
n\geq 3
.
If
G[\supp(\bm{x})]
is a complete graph, the result holds trivially. Otherwise, there exist

v_{1},v_{2}\in\supp(\bm{x})
such that
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(G)
. Let
G^{\prime}:=G-v_{1}
, and define a vector

\bm{x}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
for
G^{\prime}
by

x^{\prime}_{v}=\begin{cases}x_{v},&v\neq v_{2},\\
x_{v_{1}}+x_{v_{2}},&v=v_{2}.\end{cases}

From Lemma
3.1
, we have

\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{v_{1}}=\bm{x}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{v_{2}}
, which implies

\sum_{v\in N_{G}(v_{1})}\bigg(\frac{\cl(v)}{\cl(v)-1}+\frac{\cl(v_{1})}{\cl(v_{1})-1}\bigg)x_{v}=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(v_{2})}\bigg(\frac{\cl(v)}{\cl(v)-1}+\frac{\cl(v_{2})}{\cl(v_{2})-1}\bigg)x_{v}.

(3.2)

Since
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(G)
, for each vertex
v\in V(G^{\prime})
adjacent to
v_{2}
, the value
\cl(v)

remains the same in
G
and
G^{\prime}
. Combining this with (
3.2
) gives

\displaystyle~\sum_{vv_{2}\in E(G^{\prime})}\bigg(\frac{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v)}{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v)-1}+\frac{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v_{2})}{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v_{2})-1}\bigg)x^{\prime}_{v}x^{\prime}_{v_{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{vv_{2}\in E(G)}\bigg(\frac{\cl_{G}(v)}{\cl_{G}(v)-1}+\frac{\cl_{G}(v_{2})}{\cl_{G}(v_{2})-1}\bigg)(x_{v_{1}}+x_{v_{2}})x_{v}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~\sum_{vv_{2}\in E(G)}\bigg(\frac{\cl(v)}{\cl(v)-1}{+}\frac{\cl(v_{2})}{\cl(v_{2})-1}\bigg)x_{v_{2}}x_{v}+\sum_{vv_{1}\in E(G)}\bigg(\frac{\cl(v)}{\cl(v)-1}{+}\frac{\cl(v_{1})}{\cl(v_{1})-1}\bigg)x_{v_{1}}x_{v}.

Consider any edge of
G^{\prime}
that is not incident to
v_{2}
. For such an edge
uv
, we have

\bigg(\frac{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(u)}{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(u)-1}+\frac{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v)}{\cl_{G^{\prime}}(v)-1}\bigg)x^{\prime}_{u}x^{\prime}_{v}\geq\bigg(\frac{\cl_{G}(u)}{\cl_{G}(u)-1}+\frac{\cl_{G}(v)}{\cl_{G}(v)-1}\bigg)x_{u}x_{v}.

Hence,
F_{G^{\prime}}(\bm{x}^{\prime})=1
. By the induction hypothesis, the induced subgraph
H
of
G^{\prime}
on

\supp(\bm{x}^{\prime})
is a complete
k
-partite graph, where

k=\omega(G^{\prime})
. Let
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
be the vertex classes of
H
.

Case 1.

N_{G}(v_{1})\cap V_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\leq[k]
.

In this case,
\omega(G[\supp(\bm{x})])=k+1
. If there exists some
j

such that
|N_{G}(v_{1})\cap V_{j}|<|V_{j}|
, then one can find a vertex
w\in G[\supp(\bm{x})]
with

\cl(w)\leq k
in
G[\supp(\bm{x})]
. This would imply
F_{G}(\bm{x})>1
, a contradiction.
Hence,
v_{1}
is adjacent to every vertex in
V_{1}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
. Hence,
G[\supp(\bm{x})]

is a complete
(k+1)
-partite graph, as desired.

Case 2.
There exists some
V_{i}
, say
V_{1}
, such that
v_{1}
is nonadjacent
to any vertices in
V_{1}
.

Since
F_{G}(\bm{x})=1
is maximal, Lemma
3.1
implies that for each
v\in V_{1}
,
we have
\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{v_{1}}=\bm{z}^{\mathrm{T}}(W\circ A)\bm{e}_{v}
.
It follows that
v_{1}
is adjacent to all vertices in
V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
. Furthermore,
since
v_{1}v_{2}\notin E(G)
, it must be that
v_{2}\in V_{1}
. Thus,
V_{1}\cup\{v_{1}\},V_{2},\ldots,V_{k}

form a complete
\omega(G)
-partite graph, and
\sum_{v\in V_{1}\cup\{v_{1}\}}x_{v}=\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}

for

, completing the proof.
∎

For any edge

, it is clearly
\cl(e)\leq\min\{\cl(u),\cl(v)\}
. Hence we immediately
have the following result.

.

Let
G
be an
n

\omega(G)=\omega
and
\bm{x}\in\triangle^{n-1}
. Then

2\sum_{ij\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(ij)}{\cl(ij)-1}x_{i}x_{j}\leq 1.

Equality holds if and only if the induced subgraph of
G
on
\supp(\bm{x})
is a complete
\omega
-partite graph
whose vertex classes
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{\omega}
satisfy
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}x_{v}=1/\omega
for all

.

Now we are in standing for presenting the proof of our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
A(G)=[w_{ij}]
be the adjacency matrix of
G
, where
w_{ij}=w(e)
if
e=ij\in E(G)
.
Let
\bm{x}
be a unit eigenvector of
A(G)
corresponding to some eigenvalue of
A(G)
,
whose absolute value is equal to
\lambda(G)
, and define the vector
\bm{y}
by
y_{i}=x_{i}^{2}

for
i\in[n]
. Then we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda(G)

\displaystyle=2\,\bigg|\sum_{ij\in E(G)}w_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}\bigg|\leq 2\cdot\sum_{ij\in E(G)}|w_{ij}|\sqrt{y_{i}y_{j}}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{e\,=\,ij\in E(G)}|w_{ij}|\sqrt{\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}y_{i}y_{j}}.

Using Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and Corollary
3.1
, we find

\displaystyle\lambda(G)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 4\Bigg(\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{e\,=\,ij\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}y_{i}y_{j}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2},

completing the proof of (
1.1
).

Next, we characterize all graphs attaining equality in (
1.1
).
From the proof above, equality in (
1.1
) holds if and only if
the weight
w_{ij}
has the same sign for each
ij\in E(G)
for which
y_{i}y_{j}\neq 0
, and

\sum_{e\,=\,ij\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}y_{i}y_{j}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\sqrt{y_{i}y_{j}}}{w_{ij}}\cdot\frac{\cl(e)}{\cl(e)-1}

is a constant for each
e=ij\in E(G)
. By Corollary
3.1
and
y_{i}>0

for any
i\in V(G)
with
d(i)\geq 1
, the equality in (
1.1
) is
equivalent to the following:

(i)
G
is, up to isolated vertices, a complete
r
-partite graph whose vertex class
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{r}

satisfy
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}y_{v}=1/r
,
i\in[r]
;

(ii) For any
e=ij\in E(G)
,
w_{ij}=c\sqrt{y_{i}y_{j}}
for some constant
c
.

We first show the necessity. Assume that equality holds in (
1.1
).
Our goal is to construct a vector
\bm{w}
satisfying items (1) and (2).

Without loss of generality, we assume
c>0
. Under this assumption, the adjacency matrix

A(G)
is nonnegative, allowing us to take the eigenvector
\bm{x}
to be nonnegative.
Since
y_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
, it follows that
w_{ij}=cx_{i}x_{j}
for each
ij\in E(G)
. We then
determine the value of
c
by equating two alternative expressions for
\lambda(G)
.
From (
1.1
) we have

\lambda(G)=\sqrt{2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}.

(3.3)

On the other hand,

\lambda(G)=2\sum_{ij\in E(G)}w_{ij}x_{i}x_{j}=\frac{2}{c}\sum_{e\in E(G)}w(e)^{2}=\frac{\|A(G)\|_{F}^{2}}{c}.

Combining the two equations for
\lambda(G)
, we deduce that

c=\sqrt{\frac{r}{r-1}}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}.

(3.4)

Now define
\bm{w}:=\sqrt{c}\,(\bm{x}_{V_{1}},\bm{x}_{V_{2}},\ldots,\bm{x}_{V_{r}})^{\mathrm{T}}
.
We will verify that this
\bm{w}
satisfies items (1) and (2).
For item (1), this follows from a direct verification, which we omit for brevity.
For item (2), we recall that
y_{i}=x_{i}^{2}
for
i\in V(G)
and
\sum_{v\in V_{i}}y_{v}=1/r
for each
i\in[r]
.
Consequently,

\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{x}\|^{2}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}^{2}=\frac{1}{r}.

(3.5)

Thus, the left-hand side of the equation in item (2) is

\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}=c\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{x}\|^{2}=\frac{c}{r}=\frac{\|A(G)\|_{F}}{\sqrt{r(r-1)}}.

For the right-hand side, applying (
3.4
) yields

\|\bm{w}\|^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}=c\cdot\|\bm{x}\|^{2}-\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}=\frac{\|A(G)\|_{F}}{\sqrt{r(r-1)}}.

It follows that
\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}=\|\bm{w}\|^{2}-\sqrt{1-1/r}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}

for
i\in[r]
.

Finally, we consider the sufficiency for the equality in (
1.1
).
Suppose there exists a vector
\bm{w}
such that the adjacency matrix decomposes as

A(G)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}
,
and
\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}=\|\bm{w}\|^{2}-\sqrt{1-1/r}\cdot\|A(G)\|_{F}
for
i\in[r]
.
(the case where
A(G)=-\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}
follows from a similar argument.)
We begin by noting two key observations. First, for any
i,j\in[r]
, we have

\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w})=\begin{cases}\|\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2},&i\neq j,\\
0,&i=j.\end{cases}

Second, the vector
\bm{w}
can be decomposed over the partition as

\bm{w}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w})
.
Using these facts, we deduce that

\displaystyle A(G)\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}\Bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w})\Bigg)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})\big((\bm{1}-\bm{1}_{V_{i}})\circ\bm{w}\big)^{\mathrm{T}}(\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\neq j}\|\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}\cdot(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}).

This, along with careful calculations, indicates that

\displaystyle A(G)\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\sum_{j:\,j\neq i}\|\bm{1}_{V_{j}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}\Bigg)(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\big(\|\bm{w}\|^{2}-\|\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}\|^{2}\big)(\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w})

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\|A(G)\|_{F}\cdot\sum_{i=1}^{r}\bm{1}_{V_{i}}\circ\bm{w}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\|A(G)\|_{F}\,\bm{w}.

Since
G
is a complete
r
-partite graph, we have

\sqrt{2\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{\cl(e)-1}{\cl(e)}w(e)^{2}}=\sqrt{\frac{r-1}{r}}\|A(G)\|_{F}.

It follows that equality holds in (
1.1
).
This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

both (
4.2
) and (
4.3
).
Consequently, we have (i)
G
is a complete bipartite graph for
\omega=2
,
or a complete regular
\omega
-partite graph for
\omega\geq 3
, and
(ii)
\cl(e)=\cl(v)
for any
e\in E(G)
and
v\in V(G)
.
It is straightforward to verify that any graph satisfying (i) also satisfies (ii).
The converse implication is obvious. This completes the proof.
∎

The result above provides a spectral, vertex-localized version of Turán’s theorem.

Proof of Corollary
2.7
.

By Corollary
2.6
we obtain

\lambda(G)^{2}\leq\sum_{v\in V(G)}d(v)\cdot\frac{\cl(v)-1}{\cl(v)}.

Combining this with Theorem
1.3
gives the desired result.
∎

Since
\chi(G)\geq\omega(G)
holds for any graph, Corollary
2.8

follows directly from Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Corollary
2.9
.

Applying Corollary
2.8
yields

\chi(G)\geq 1+\frac{\lambda(G)^{2}}{\|A(G)\|_{F}^{2}-\lambda(G)^{2}}\geq 1+\frac{\lambda(G)^{2}}{2\sum_{e\in E(G)}w(e)-\lambda(G)^{2}}.

By Rayleigh’s inequality,
2\sum_{e\in E(G)}w(e)\leq n\cdot\lambda(G)
. Substituting this, we find

\chi(G)\geq 1+\frac{\lambda(G)^{2}}{n\cdot\lambda(G)-\lambda(G)^{2}}=1+\frac{\lambda(G)}{n-\lambda(G)},

as desired.
∎

5
Two technical results

In this section, we present two technical results and the proofs. The first one is a main tool
for proving Corollary
2.4
. For the second one, it supports the proofs of
Corollaries
2.3
and
2.7
.

For a graph
G
, we say that
G
satisfies
Property P
if for every maximal clique

S
of
G
and every vertex
v\in S
, the vertex
v
has at least one neighbor outside of
S
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices without isolated vertices. If
G
has Property P, then

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{c(e)}\geq\frac{n}{2}.

Proof.

Since
G
has Property P, for each edge
e=uv\in E(G)
, we have
c(e)\leq\min\{d(u),d(v)\}
.
This implies that
2/\cl(e)\geq 1/d(u)+1/d(v)
. Summing this inequality over all edges yields

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{2}{\cl(e)}\geq\sum_{e=uv\in E(G)}\Big(\frac{1}{d(u)}+\frac{1}{d(v)}\Big)=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{d(v)}{d(v)}=n,

which completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Next, we prove Theorem
1.2
, which is the key tool for proving that our main
result implies Hong’s theorem. This theorem may also be of independent interest.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We prove only inequality (
1.4
), as the proof for (
1.3
)
is similar. Furthermore, it suffices to consider the case where
G
is connected.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The base case
n=2
is trivial. Now, assume
n\geq 3
and
that the result holds for all graphs of order less than
n
. If
G
has Property P,
then the conclusion follows immediately from Lemma
5.1
.
We may therefore assume that
G
has no Property P. Consequently, there exist
a maximal clique
S
and a vertex
w\in S
such that
N_{G}(w)\subseteq S
.

We first assume that
G-w
is disconnected. Let
H_{1},H_{2},\ldots,H_{t}
be all components of
G-w
.
Let
G_{i}=G[V(H_{i})\cup\{w\}]
. Obviously,
E(G_{i})\neq\emptyset
. By induction, we have

\sum_{e\in E(G_{i})}\frac{1}{\cl_{G_{i}}(e)}\geq\frac{|H_{i}|}{2}

for each
i\in[t]
. Summing up all these inequalities, with help of the obvious fact

\cl_{G_{i}}(e)=\cl_{G}(e)
for any
e\in E(G_{i})
, we have

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}\geq\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{|H_{i}|}{2}=\frac{n-1}{2}.

Now, assume
G-w
is connected. Observe that

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}-\sum_{e\in E(G-w)}\frac{1}{\cl_{G-w}(e)}=\sum_{e:\,w\in e}\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}+\sum_{e\in E(S-w)}\Big(\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}-\frac{1}{\cl_{G-w}(e)}\Big).

Since for each
e\in E(S-w)
we have
\cl_{G-w}(e)\geq\cl_{G}(e)-1
, the inductive hypothesis yields

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(G)}\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{e\in E(G-w)}\frac{1}{\cl_{G-w}(e)}+\sum_{e:\,w\in e}\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}+\sum_{e\in E(S-w)}\Big(\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}-\frac{1}{\cl_{G-w}(e)}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{|S|-1}{|S|}+\sum_{e\in E(S-w)}\Big(\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)}-\frac{1}{\cl_{G}(e)-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{n-2}{2}+\frac{|S|-1}{|S|}-\binom{|S|-1}{2}\cdot\frac{1}{|S|(|S|-1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{n-1}{2},

completing the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges. Then

\Bigg(n-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)+1}\Bigg)\Bigg(n+1-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)+1}\Bigg)\geq 2m.

(5.1)

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
n
. The validity of (
5.1
) for all graphs with

n\leq 5
can be verified by a computer program, establishing the base case.

Now assume that the lemma holds for all graphs of order
n-1
, where
n\geq 6
.
Let
u\in V(G)
with
d_{G}(u)=\delta(G)=:\delta
, and
define
G^{\prime}=G-u
. In the following, if there is no danger of ambiguity, we use
d(v)

and
d^{\prime}(v)
instead of
d_{G}(v)
and
d_{G^{\prime}}(v)
, respectively. For brevity, set

\psi_{G}(n):=n^{2}+n-(2n+1)\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}+\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)^{2}.

By the induction hypothesis applied to
G^{\prime}
, we have
\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)\geq 2(m-\delta)
.
We now analyze the difference
\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)
. Starting from the definition:

\displaystyle~\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2n{-}\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{2n+1}{d(v){+}1}{-}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{2n-1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}\Bigg)+\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}\Bigg)^{2}{-}\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}\Bigg)^{2}.

A careful calculation yields

\displaystyle~\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2n{-}\Bigg[\Bigg(\frac{2n-1}{\delta+1}{+}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{2n-1}{d(v){+}1}\Bigg){+}\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{2}{d(v){+}1}{-}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{2n-1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}\Bigg]

\displaystyle~+\Bigg(\frac{1}{\delta+1}+\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)^{2}-\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v)+1}\Bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2n+(2n-1)\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v)+1}-\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)-\frac{2n-1}{\delta+1}-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{2}{d(v)+1}

\displaystyle~+\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)^{2}-\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v)+1}\Bigg)^{2}+\frac{2}{\delta+1}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}.

We now derive a lower bound on
\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)
. After ignoring the item
\frac{2}{\delta+1}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}
, we immediately obtain

\displaystyle~\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle~\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}{-}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}\Bigg)\Bigg(2n-1-\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}{-}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v)+1}\Bigg)

\displaystyle~+2n-\frac{2n-1}{\delta+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}.

It follows from
\psi_{G^{\prime}}(n-1)\geq 2(m-\delta)
that

\displaystyle\psi_{G}(n)

\displaystyle\geq 2m{+}\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\bigg(\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}{-}\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}\bigg)\Bigg)\Bigg(2n{-}1{-}\sum_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\bigg(\frac{1}{d(v){+}1}{+}\frac{1}{d^{\prime}(v){+}1}\bigg)\Bigg)

\displaystyle~~~+2n-2\delta-\frac{2n-1}{\delta+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}.

Note that
d^{\prime}(v)\leq d(v)
for each
v\in V(G^{\prime})
. This implies that

\psi_{G}(n)\geq 2m+2n-2\delta-\frac{2n-1}{\delta+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}.

Since
d(v)\geq\delta
for all
v\in V(G)
, we obtain

\psi_{G}(n)\geq 2n-2\delta-\frac{4n-1}{\delta+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}.

To finish the induction step, it suffices to prove that

2n-2\delta-\frac{4n-1}{\delta+1}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}\geq 0.

Toward this goal, we define the functions

f(x):=2n-2x-\frac{4n-1}{x+1}\quad\text{and}\quad g(x):=f(x)+\frac{1}{(x+1)^{2}},

so that the target inequality is equivalent to
g(\delta)\geq 0
.
For
\delta\in[2,n-3]
, the convexity of
f(x)
implies that

g(\delta)=f(\delta)+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}\geq\min\{f(2),f(n-3)\}+\frac{1}{(\delta+1)^{2}}.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle f(2)

\displaystyle=2n-4-\frac{4n-1}{3}=\frac{2n-11}{3}>0,

\displaystyle f(n-3)

\displaystyle=2n-2(n-3)-\frac{4n-1}{n-2}=\frac{2n-8}{n-2}>0.

Hence, the inequality holds for
2\leq\delta\leq n-3
.
It remains to consider the cases
\delta\in\{n-2,n-1\}
,

\delta=1
, and
\delta=0
.

Case 1.

\delta\in\{n-2,n-1\}
. If
\delta=n-1
,
then
G=K_{n}
, and it is straightforward to verify that (
5.1
) holds.
If
\delta=n-2
, then

\displaystyle\psi_{G}(n)

\displaystyle=\Bigg(n-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)\Bigg(n+1-\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(n-\frac{n}{n-1}\bigg)\bigg(n+1-\frac{n}{n-1}\bigg)

\displaystyle=n^{2}-n-2-\frac{1}{n-1}+\frac{1}{(n-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle>n^{2}-n-\frac{11}{5}.

Hence, if
2m\leq n^{2}-n-3
, then (
5.1
) holds.
It remains to consider the situation
2m=n^{2}-n-2
.
When
2m=n^{2}-n-2
, the degree sequence of
G
is

n-1,\ldots,n-1,n-2,n-2,n-2,n-2.

A direct calculation then yields

\displaystyle\psi_{G}(n)

\displaystyle=n^{2}+n-(2n+1)\bigg(\frac{n-4}{n}+\frac{4}{n-1}\bigg)+\bigg(\frac{n-4}{n}+\frac{4}{n-1}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq n^{2}-n-2=2m,

so (
5.1
) is satisfied in this case as well.

Case 2.

\delta=1
. Let
N_{G}(u)=\{w\}
. Recall that

\psi_{G-u}(n-1)=(n-1)n-(2n-1)\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u,w\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}+\frac{1}{d(w)}\Bigg)+\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u,w\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}+\frac{1}{d(w)}\Bigg)^{2}.

A straightforward computation yields

\displaystyle~\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G-u}(n-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle~2n-(2n-1)\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d(w)(d(w)+1)}\Bigg)-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}

\displaystyle~+\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d(w)(d(w)+1)}\Bigg)\Bigg(\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d_{G}(v)+1}+\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u,w\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}+\frac{1}{d(w)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle=2n-\Bigg(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{d(w)(d(w)+1)}\Bigg)\Bigg(2n-\frac{3}{2}-2\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}

\displaystyle\geq 2n-\Bigg(n-\frac{3}{4}-\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}\Bigg)-2\sum_{v\in V(G)}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}

\displaystyle=n-\frac{1}{4}-\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}.

It follows that

\psi_{G}(n)\geq\psi_{G-u}(n-1)+n-\frac{1}{4}-\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}.

By the induction hypothesis,
\psi_{G-u}(n-1)\geq 2(m-1)
. Furthermore, since
each term in
\sum_{v\in V(G)\setminus\{u\}}\frac{1}{d(v)+1}
is at most
1/2
.
Applying these bounds gives

\psi_{G}(n)\geq 2(m-1)+n-\frac{1}{4}-\frac{n-1}{2}=2m+\frac{2n-7}{4}>2m.

Case 3.

\delta=0
. Let
N_{G}(u)=\emptyset
. We have

\psi_{G}(n)-\psi_{G\setminus\{u\}}(n-1)=0
. Hence,
\psi_{G}(n)=\psi_{G\setminus\{u\}}(n-1)\geq 2m
.

This completes the proof of Lemma
5.2
.
∎

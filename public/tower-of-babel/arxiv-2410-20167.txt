Hydrodynamic limit of the symmetric exclusion process on complete Riemannian manifolds and principal bundles Thanks: TU Delft, Delft Institute of Applied Mathematics, Mekelweg 4, 2628 CD Delft, Netherlands. Emails: j.junne@tudelft.nl f.h.j.redig@tudelft.nl r.versendaal@tudelft.nl
Jonathan Junné, Frank Redig, Rik Versendaal

Abstract
. We define the symmetric exclusion process (SEP) on random neighbourhood graphs drawn from Poisson point processes on complete connected Riemannian manifolds equipped with a Gibbs reference measure. We obtain diffusions with drift induced by the potential of the Gibbs measure as hydrodynamic limits. This is a consequence of the duality between the (SEP) and random walks approximating weighted Laplacians. We also lift the (SEP) to principal bundles, and we obtain horizontal diffusions with drift as hydrodynamic limits. Examples include the orthonormal frame bundle with the horizontal Laplacian which plays a central role in the Eells-Elworthy-Émery construction of Riemannian Brownian motion.

1.
Introduction

Interacting particle systems serve as microscopic models from which macroscopic non-equilibrium behaviour can be derived. An example is the hydrodynamic limit where one derives partial differential equations for macroscopic quantities such as the particle density by appropriate scaling of space and time in the microscopic particle dynamics,
see for e.g. the monographs of Kipnis and Landim
[
13
]
, DeMasi and Presutti
[
6
]
, and Spohn
[
19
]
. A well-known and thoroughly studied example of an interacting system is the symmetric exclusion process (SEP) introduced by Spitzer
[
18
]
(see also Liggett
[
15
]
), where particles perform random walks with the restriction of having at most one particle per site. For this model in the standard setting of the
d
-dimensional lattice with nearest neighbour jumps, the hydrodynamic limit is the heat equation which can be derived after diffusive rescaling of space and time.

At present, hydrodynamic limits have been considered mostly on the lattice or a discrete torus, and the macroscopic space is either
\mathbb{R}^{d}
or the
d
-dimensional torus. Let us mention important geometric cases where the hydrodynamic limit of the (SEP) was obtained:
Faggionato
[
7
]
considered random environments, in particular Delaunay’s triangulations from an homogeneous Poisson point process (PPP) for which the limiting heat equation has an homogenised diffusion matrix. Chen
[
5
]
considered suitable infinite graphs, namely, fractals.

In
[
20
]
, Redig and Van Ginkel started with considering the setting where the macroscopic space is a compact Riemannian manifold, with the aim of deriving macroscopic equations from interacting particle systems on discrete graphs approximating the manifold. More precisely, for a general class of neighbourhood graphs approximating a compact Riemannian manifold,
[
20
]
studied the corresponding (SEP) and gave a proof of the emergence of the heat equation.
In this paper, we extend the realm of these results in several ways. First, we drop the assumption of the compactness of the manifold. Second, we consider random neighbourhood graphs whose vertices are given by a (PPP) with Gibbs intensity. Third, whereas
[
20
]
relied on the Wasserstein convergence of empirical distribution of i.i.d. points sampled from the normalised volume measure, here we rely on a simpler argument based on Bernstein’s inequality to prove convergence of the rescaled random walks on the random neighbourhood graphs obtained from the (PPP) towards a diffusion process. Fourth, we lift the (SEP) to principal bundles.

As a result, starting from the corresponding (SEP), we obtain several new variants of the heat equation, including the heat equation with weighted Laplacian

e^{U}\divergence(e^{-U}\nabla)
, where
U
is the potential of the Gibbs measure, replacing the Laplace-Beltrami operator. In the principal bundle setting, we obtain an horizontal heat equation with weighted horizontal Laplacian. These results extend significantly the hydrodynamic equations that one can obtain from the (SEP), both in extending the geometric setting, but even in the Euclidean setting, where the heat equation with a weighted Laplacian is new.

The (SEP) is special because it manifests a microscopic closure of the expected density field; i.e., one does not need replacement lemmas to derive a macrosopic equation. This property, which can be viewed as a manifestation of self-duality, is crucial in the derivation of the (expected) macroscopic equation, which reduces to the proof
of the scaling limit of a single random walk (RW) to a diffusion process; the Kolmogorov forward equation of this diffusion process is then the hydrodynamic limit. Therefore, an important aspect is to consider suitable symmetric transition rates for (RW) on neighbourhood graphs in order to approximate a diffusion process with (horizontal) weighted Laplacian as generator. These transition rates are inspired from Hein, Audibert and von Luxburg

[
3
]
and Gao
[
8
]
; in the context of horizontal diffusions, this requires a double scaling in the base space and the fibres. To prove weak convergence in path space of the trajectory of the empirical density, one needs to additionally control the quadratic variation of the corresponding Dynkin martingale.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows : In
Section

2
we introduce the (SEP) in our geometric setting and state the main result, namely, the hydrodynamic limit. In
Section

3
we prove an abstract hydrodynamic limit for the (SEP) on complete manifolds which encompasses our setting. In
Section

4
we prove the almost sure convergence of a single (RW) on an increasing sequence of random neighbourhood graphs drawn from a (PPP) with Gibbs intensity. In
Section

5
we extend the setting to principal bundles by a horizontal lift procedure and obtain the corresponding hydrodynamic limit.

2.
Main result

We consider a complete connected
m
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
equipped with a Gibbs reference measure
d\mu:=e^{-U}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
for some prescribed smooth potential
U
. The manifold is ought to be a limit of a sequence of random neighbourhood graphs
(G_{N})_{N\geq 1}
capturing the geometry:

Definition 2.1

(Random neighbourhood graph)
.

Let
\Lambda^{1}
be a Poisson point process (PPP) of intensity
\mu
and set
\Lambda^{n+1}:=\Lambda^{n}+\Lambda
with
\Lambda
an independent (PPP) of intensity
\mu
. The
vertices

V_{N}
consist of the points
X^{i}
of
\Lambda^{N}
, and an undirected
edge
is present between two vertices whenever they are close; i.e.,
d_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{i},X^{j})\leq h_{N}
for some
bandwidth parameter

h_{N}
. The sequence of
random neighbourhood graphs
is given by
(G_{N}:=(V_{N},E_{N}))_{N\geq 1}
, where
E_{N}
denotes the set of undirected edges.

The symmetric exclusion process (SEP) we consider lives on those random neighbourhood graphs; each site accommodates at most one particle, and particles hop, independently from each other, from their respective sites to neighbouring ones with prescribed symmetric rates and the particularity of suppression of jumps towards already occupied sites:

Definition 2.2

(Symmetric exclusion process (SEP))
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with symmetric weights
W
, we denote the
configuration of particles
by
\eta\in\Omega_{V}=\{0,1\}^{V}
and the exchange of occupancies between the vertices
x
and
y
by

\eta^{x,y}(z):=\begin{cases}\eta(z)&\text{if }k\neq x,y,\\
\eta(y)&\text{if }k=x,\\
\eta(x)&\text{if }k=y.\end{cases}

The (SEP) is the continuous-time Markov jump process on the
configuration space

\Omega_{V}
whose generator evaluated on functions depending only on a finite number of occupancies, namely,
local functions

f:\Omega_{V}\to\mathbb{R}
, is given by

L_{G}^{\SEP}f(\eta):=\sum_{(x,y)\in E}W(x,y)\left(f(\eta^{x,y})-f(\eta)\right).

The symmetric weights take into account the geometry of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
underlying the random neighbourhood graphs; given a bounded kernel
k:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to R^{+}
with compact support
[0,1]
, we set the following symmetric weights:

(2.1)

W_{N}(X^{i},X^{j}):=W_{N}^{ij}:=\frac{h_{N}^{-m}k\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{i},X^{j})}{h_{N}}\right)}{\sqrt{e^{-U(X^{i})}e^{-U(X^{j})}}}.

Theorem 2.3
.

(Hydrodynamic limit of the (SEP))
Let
(G_{N}=(V_{N},E_{N}))_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of random neighbourhood graphs on
\mathcal{M}
in the sense of
Definition

2.1
with symmetric weights
(W_{N})_{N\geq 1}
given by (
2.1
). Let
\eta_{0}^{N}
be an initial configuration of the (SEP) on
G_{N}
with associated initial empirical measure
\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}
that converges vaguely to
\rho_{0}\,d\mu
in probability; that is,

\forall\varphi\in C_{0}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\left\langle\varphi,d\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}\right\rangle\overset{\mathrm{pr.}}{\to}\left\langle\varphi,\rho_{0}\>d\mu\right\rangle\quad\text{as }N\to+\infty,\quad\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{X^{i}\in V_{N}}\eta_{0}^{N}(X^{i})\delta_{X^{i}}.

Suppose that the bandwidth parameters
(h_{N})_{N\geq 1}
are chosen in such a way that both
h_{N}\to 0
and
Nh_{N}^{m+2}/\log N\to+\infty
as
N\to+\infty
. Then for almost all realisations of the sequence of graphs, when the underlying configuration
\eta_{t}^{N}
evolves according to the diffusively rescaled (SEP) with generator
h_{N}^{-2}L_{G_{N}}^{\SEP}
, the trajectory of its associated empirical measure
t\mapsto\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}
converges in the Skorokhod topology to a deterministic trajectory
t\mapsto\rho_{t}d\mu
whose density
\rho_{t}
is the weak solution of the diffusion equation with drift
-\nabla U
, namely,

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=C_{k}\left(\Delta-\nabla U\cdot\nabla\right)\rho_{t},

with initial condition
\rho_{0}
and for some constant
C_{k}
that only depends on moments of the kernel
k
, up to time of existence, when uniqueness of the weak solution holds.

Theorem

2.3
is a direct consequence of an abstract hydrodynamic limit (
Theorem

3.2
) and an almost sure pointwise consistency of a single random walk on the random neighbourhood graphs (
Theorem

4.3
) that we establish in
Section

3
and
Section

4
respectively.

Remark 2.4
.

This specific choice of
W_{N}
(
2.1
) leads to the weighted Laplacian
\Delta-\nabla U\cdot\nabla=e^{U}\nabla\cdot(e^{-U}\nabla)
as differential operator which is reversible with respect to the Gibbs measure
e^{-U}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
and for which existence without blow-up and uniqueness of the weak solution holds, for instance, when the weighted manifold
(\mathcal{M},d\mu)
is stochastically complete (see for instance Grigor’yan
[
9
]
). Note that the Laplacian, or Laplace-Beltrami operator, is recovered when
U\equiv 0
.

For the same choice of
W_{N}
, one could instead prepare the initial configuration such that its empirical measure approximates
\rho_{0}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
so that the initial profile
\rho_{0}
is considered with respect to the volume measure instead of the Gibbs measure. Then the evolution of the limiting trajectory is
t\mapsto\rho_{t}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
where
\rho_{t}
evolves according a Fokker-Planck equation (or equivalently, Kolmogorov forward equation) given by

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=C_{k}\nabla\cdot\left(\nabla\rho_{t}+\rho_{t}\nabla U\right).

Several other choices of symmetric weights are possible, leading to other types of weighted Laplacians and forms of evolution of the density
(with respect to the reference Gibbs measure); we obtain a variant of our main for a class of diffusions given by

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=C_{k,\alpha}e^{(2\alpha-1)U}\left(\Delta-\alpha\nabla U\cdot\nabla\right)\rho_{t}

modifying the symmetric weights
W_{N}
as in (
4.2
).

3.
Abstract hydrodynamic limit via duality

The symmetry of the rates in the (SEP) makes it special; it possesses a duality with random walks:

Definition 3.1

(Random walk (RW))
.

Given a graph
G=(V,E)
with weights
W
, the (RW) is the continuous-time Markov jump processes on
V
whose generator evaluated on local functions

\varphi:V\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

(3.1)

L_{G}^{\RW}\varphi(x):=\sum_{y\sim x}W(x,y)\left(\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)\right).

The duality between (RW) and (SEP) manifests itself as follows: for pairings between the empirical measure of a configuration
\eta
and a local function
\varphi
on
V
, it holds

L_{G}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi,\sum_{x\in V}\eta(x)\delta_{x}\right\rangle=\left\langle L_{G}^{\RW}\varphi,\sum_{x\in V}\eta(x)\delta_{x}\right\rangle.

Theorem 3.2
.

(Abstract hydrodynamic limit)
Let
(G_{N}=(V_{N},E_{N}))_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of graphs on
\mathcal{M}
with symmetric weights
(W_{N})_{N\geq 1}
. Assume that for each compact
K\subset\subset\mathcal{M}
we have

(3.2)

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{\#\{x\in V_{N}\cap K\}}{N}<\infty.

Let
\eta^{N}_{0}
be an initial configuration of the (SEP) on
G_{N}
with associated empirical measures
\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}:=\tfrac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\eta^{N}_{0}(x)\delta_{x}
that converges vaguely to
\rho_{0}\,d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
in probability. Assume that the (RW) with generator
L_{G_{N}}^{\RW}
when diffusively rescaled, of generator denoted by
L_{N}^{\RW}
, converges towards
\mathcal{L}^{\RW}
against smooth compactly supported test functions; that is,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{x\in V_{N}}\left\lvert L_{N}^{\RW}\varphi(x)-\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi(x)\right\rvert=0,\quad\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

Then the trajectory of the empirical measure
t\mapsto\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}
associated to a configuration
\eta_{t}^{N}
that evolves according to the diffusively rescaled (SEP) with generator
L_{N}^{\SEP}
converges in the Skohorod topology to a distribution
t\mapsto\rho_{t}
that solves weakly the forward Kolmogorov equation

\partial_{t}\rho_{t}=\left(\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\right)^{*}\rho_{t}

with initial condition
\rho_{0}
, up to time of existence and uniqueness; the adjoint is understood in
L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

We outline the argument for complete manifolds modified from the torus case found in the monograph of Kipnis and Landim
[
13
, Chapter 4]
.

Step 1 (The Dynkin martingale argument)
where we pair any test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{M})
with the empirical measure
\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}
associated to the configuration
\eta_{t}
(with
0\leq t\leq T
for some time
T>0
), and we consider its associated Dynkin martingale;

M_{t}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]=\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}L_{N}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle\>ds.

The symmetry of the rates of the jumps give the duality;

L_{N}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle=\left\langle L_{N}^{\RW}\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle.

Therefore, convergence of generators can be established at the level of random walks of a single particle, which is already assumed, and the above yields

M_{t}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]=\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle\>ds+o(1).

To show that the Dynkin martingale goes to 0 in probability, it is easier to consider its quadratic variation given by

\left[M_{t}^{N},M_{t}^{N}\right]\left(\varphi\right)=\int_{0}^{t}L_{N}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle^{2}-2\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle L_{N}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle\>ds.

Again, since both the rates of the jumps are symmetric and the generator
L_{N}^{\RW}
converges towards
\mathcal{L}^{\RW}
, some arithmetic manipulations yield

\left[M_{t}^{N},M_{t}^{N}\right]\left(\varphi\right)\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{y\in V_{N}}\left\lvert\varphi(y)L_{N}^{\RW}\varphi(y)\right\rvert=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{y\in V_{N}}\left\lvert\varphi(y)\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi(y)\right\rvert+o(1)=o(1)

where in the last equality we used assumption (
3.2
);
\#\{\mathrm{Supp}\varphi\cap V_{N}\}
is asymptotically of order at most
N
by compactness of the support of
\varphi
. We conclude that, in probability,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{0}}^{N}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle\>ds\right\rvert\to 0\quad N\to+\infty.

Step 2 (Relative compactness of the associated measure)
considers the level of trajectories, seen as measure-valued Càdlag paths. We equip this space with the probability measure associated to
t\mapsto\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}
that we denote by
Q^{N}
. Then the convergence obtained in Step 1 implies that

\lim_{N\to+\infty}Q^{N}\left[\left\{\nu\in D([0,T]):\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi,\nu_{s}\right\rangle\>ds\right\rvert\leq\epsilon\right\}\right]=1

for any
\epsilon>0
. It remains to show relative compactness of the associated measures
Q^{N}
.

Since we work in the vague topology on measures, it is sufficient to prove relative compactness of the projected measures, namely, of the sequence of real-valued random trajectories

\{\langle\pi^{N}_{t},\varphi\rangle,0\leq t\leq T\}
,
for every smooth compactly supported test function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}([0,T]\times\mathcal{M})
. Relative compactness of this sequence of trajectories is in turn a consequence of Aldous’ criterion;

\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\limsup_{N\to+\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{I}_{T},\theta\leq\Theta}Q^{N}\left[\left\{\widehat{\pi}^{N}:\left\lvert\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}}^{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{\tau}}^{N}\right\rangle\right\rvert>\epsilon\right\}\right]=0,

where
\mathcal{I}_{T}
is the set of all stopping times bounded by
T
. To verify Aldous’s criterion, we rewrite the difference of empirical measure using the Dynkin martingale;

\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}}^{N}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{\tau}}^{N}\right\rangle=M_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]-M_{\tau}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]+\int_{\tau}^{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}\left\langle\mathcal{L}\varphi,\widehat{\pi_{s}}^{N}\right\rangle\>ds+o(1).

The integral is of order
\theta
. For the difference of the martingales, Chebyshev’s inequality yields

\displaystyle Q^{N}\left[\left\{\widehat{\pi}^{N}:\left\lvert M_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]-M_{\tau}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]\right\rvert>\epsilon\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left\lvert M_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]-M_{\tau}^{N}\left[\varphi\right]\right\rvert^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left[M_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}^{N},M_{(\tau+\theta)\wedge T}^{N}\right]\left(\varphi\right)-\left[M_{\tau}^{N},M_{\tau}^{N}\right]\left(\varphi\right)\right]

\displaystyle=o(1).

Step 3 (Uniqueness of the limit)
is following. Now that the relative compactness of the sequence of measures
\{Q^{N}\}_{N\geq 1}
is established, one argues that all the limits of the weakly convergent subsequences are the same, whence
Q^{N}
converges weakly to a limiting measure
Q
. Actually, this limiting measure is concentrated on paths that weakly solve the forward Kolmogorov equation. To see this, recall the closed set

H_{\epsilon}=\left\{\nu\in D([0,T]):\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi,\nu_{t}\right\rangle\>ds\right\rvert\leq\epsilon\right\}

and pick any converging subsequence
\{Q^{N_{m}}\}_{m\geq 1}
. Then

Q[H_{\epsilon}]\geq\limsup_{m\to+\infty}Q^{N_{m}}[H_{\epsilon}]=1,

and it follows that

Q\left[H_{0}\right]=Q\left[\cap_{m\geq 1}H_{1/m}\right]=1-Q\left[\cup_{m\geq 1}H_{1/m}^{C}\right]=1,

which in turn implies that

Q\left[\left\{\nu\in D([0,T]):\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\lvert\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{t}\right\rangle-\left\langle\varphi,\nu_{0}\right\rangle-\int_{0}^{t}\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi,\nu_{t}\right\rangle\>ds\right\rvert=0\quad\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\right\}\right]=1.

Again via the Dynkin martingale representation, one obtains that the trajectory
t\mapsto\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}
weakly solving the forward Kolmogorov equation is continuous in our setting when existence and uniqueness is guaranteed.

4.
Convergence of discrete random walks

In the proof of
Theorem

3.2
, a specific notion of convergence of the (RW) towards a limiting differential operator is used to rewrite

(4.1)

L_{N}^{\SEP}\left\langle\varphi(x),\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle=\left\langle L_{N}^{\RW}\varphi(x),\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle=\left\langle\mathcal{L}^{\RW}\varphi(x),\widehat{\pi_{t}}^{N}\right\rangle+o(1).

The almost-sure pointwise consistency of graph Laplacians was obtained by Hein, Audibert and von Luxburg
[
10
, Theorems 28 & 30]
for random neighbourhood graphs sampled from i.i.d. points (see also
[
3
, Theorem 2.1]
). We obtain their counterpart on our random neighbourhood graphs obtained from (PPP) with Gibbs intensity; the link resides in the fact that conditioned on the number of points in a region of finite measure, the points in that region are i.i.d. with respect to the restricted normalised measure
[
14
, Proposition 3.8]
.

To perform the (RW) we consider weights similar to (
2.1
) with an additional parameter
\alpha
given by

(4.2)

W_{N;\alpha}(X^{i},X^{j}):=\frac{h_{N}^{-m}k\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{i},X^{j})}{h_{N}}\right)}{\left(\overline{k}(X^{i})\overline{k}(X^{j})\right)^{\alpha}},\quad\overline{k}(X^{i}):=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{X^{j}\in\Lambda^{N}}h_{N}^{-m}k\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{i},X^{j})}{h_{N}}\right),

where
\overline{k}
plays the role of a statistical estimator of the Gibbs measure;
\overline{k}(X^{i})\approx C_{k,0}e^{-U(X^{i})}
for some moment
C_{k,0}
, up to a small error. The parameter
\alpha
leads to variants of the weighted Laplacian
\Delta-\nabla U\cdot\nabla
:

Definition 4.1

(Weighted Laplacian)
.

Given a smooth function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, the
\alpha
-weighted Laplacian
is the reversible operator with respect to the
\alpha
-weighted Gibbs measure
d\mu_{\alpha}:=e^{-\alpha U}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}
; that is, the operator satisfying

\forall\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\quad\int_{\mathcal{M}}\varphi\Delta_{\alpha}\phi\>e^{-\alpha U}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}=-\int_{\mathcal{M}}\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla\phi\>e^{-\alpha U}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}},

which is given by

\Delta_{\alpha}:=e^{\alpha U}\nabla\cdot\left(e^{-\alpha U}\nabla\right)=\Delta-\alpha\nabla U\cdot\nabla.

Remark 4.2
.

Hein, Audibert and von Luxburg consider the manifold
\mathcal{M}
as a submanifold isometrically embedded in some euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{M}
using the fact that for close enough points, the intrinsic and extrinsic distances match up to the third order (
[
17
, Proposition 6]
). We stick to the intrinsic setting for our purpose, namely, the hydrodynamic limit of the (SEP), and we show the consistency of the graph Laplacian with (
4.2
) in our setting of random neighbourhood graphs obtained from a (PPP).

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
be a test function. Let
(G_{N})_{N\geq 1}
be a sequence of random neighbourhood graphs in the sense of
Definition

2.1
with symmetric weights
(W_{N;\alpha})_{N\geq 1}
given by (
4.2
) and associated (RW) with generator
L_{G_{N}}^{\RW}
. There are constants
C_{\alpha;1,2}>0
that only depends on
\alpha,k,\varphi
and
\mu
such that, for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\sup_{X^{i}\in\Lambda^{N}}\left\lvert h_{N}^{-2}L_{G_{N}}^{\RW}\varphi(X^{i})-\left(\frac{C_{k,2}}{2\left(C_{k,0}\right)^{2\alpha}}e^{(2\alpha-1)U(X^{i})}\Delta_{2(1-\alpha)}\varphi(X^{i})+O(h_{N}^{2})\right)\right\rvert>\varepsilon\right]\\
\leq C_{\alpha;1}N^{2}h_{N}\varepsilon e^{-C_{\alpha;2}Nh_{N}^{m+2}\varepsilon^{2}}.

Then, if both
h_{N}\to 0
and
Nh_{N}^{m+2}/\log N\to+\infty
as
N\to+\infty
, it holds almost surely and uniformly in the vertices
X^{i}
’s,

(4.3)

\lim_{N\to+\infty}h_{N}^{-2}L_{G_{N}}^{\RW}\varphi(X^{i})=\frac{C_{k,2}}{2\left(C_{k,0}\right)^{2\alpha}}e^{(2\alpha-1)U(X^{i})}\Delta_{2(1-\alpha)}\varphi(X^{i}).

Remark 4.4
.

Compared to original results of Hein, Audibert and von Luxburg
[
10
, Theorem 28 & 30]
, the leading pre-factor of the exponential is of order
N^{2}
and not
N
anymore. This is a consequence of having to deal with a (PPP) of intensity
Nd\mu
instead of simply
N
i.i.d. random variables. With high probability, the number of points in a compact set is of order
N
, ranging between
N(1-\delta/C)
and
N(1+\delta/C)
for some small
\delta/C
;
N^{2}
is the order of the sum of that range.

We recall two concentration inequalities:

Proposition 4.5

(Bernstein’s inequality
[
4
, Theorem 2.10]
)
.

Let
X^{1},\dots,X^{n}
be
n

5.
Lifting the (SEP) to principal bundles

The base Riemannian manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is the projection of a total
\widetilde{m}
-dimensional Riemannian manifold
(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\widetilde{g})
from a Riemannian submersion
\pi:(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},\widetilde{g})\to(\mathcal{M},g)
; that is, a smooth surjective map such that

d\pi:\ker(d\pi)^{\perp}\to T\mathcal{M}

is an isometry. This yields a horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
of dimension
m
as the orthogonal complement of the vertical distribution
\mathcal{V}
of dimension
\widetilde{m}-m:=m^{\prime}
forming together the tangent bundle of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
;

\mathcal{H}:=\mathcal{V}^{\perp},\quad\mathcal{V}:=\ker d\pi,\quad T\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}:=\mathcal{H}\oplus\mathcal{V}.

We denote by
\widetilde{\nabla}
the Levi-Civita connection on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
, which is
\pi
-related to the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla
on
\mathcal{M}

[
16
, Lemma 1]
, and by
\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{x}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}
the parallel transport operator between the fibres along the geodesic joining
x
to
y
when they are close enough. Also, any vector field
\xi\in\Gamma(T\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})
can be decomposed into its horizontal part
\xi^{\mathcal{H}}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H})
and its vertical part
\xi^{\mathcal{V}}\in\Gamma(\mathcal{V})
. The horizontal gradient is the horizontal part of the gradient, yielding a horizontal Laplacian as horizontal part of the divergence of the horizontal gradient.

The horizontal distribution
\mathcal{H}
also allows to lift curves and tangent vectors from the base manifold
\mathcal{M}
to the total manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
in a coherent way.

Definition 5.1

(Horizontal lift)
.

Given a smooth curve
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{M})
defined on an interval

containing
0
, the

\widetilde{\gamma}
with respect to
\mathcal{H}
starting at
u_{0}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{\gamma(0)}
of
\gamma
is the unique curve satisfying

\pi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}=\gamma,\quad\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}.

This horizontal lift procedure can be applied to tangent vectors seen as velocities of curves; the horizontal lift
\widetilde{v}
with basepoint
u\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
of a tangent vector
v\in T_{\pi(u)}\mathcal{M}
is given by
\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0)
with
\widetilde{\gamma}(0):=u
, where
\gamma
is any smooth curve such that
\gamma(0)=\pi(u)
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=v
. Furthermore, random neighbourhood graphs on the base manifold
\mathcal{M}
can be lifted in a consistent manner on the total manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
if the latter is locally a product space (such as e.g. in the case of a fibre bundle). In the following definition we make precise this notion of lifting
of random neighbourhood graphs:

Definition 5.2

(Lifted random neighbourhood graph)

Given a random neighbourhood graph

on a manifold
\mathcal{M}
in the sense of

a sequence of (PPP)

in the fibre

defined as follows: set

of

and their fibre counterparts

is present between two vertices whenever they are close both on the base manifold and in the fibres; i.e.,

and

Following Gao
[

, we consider weights mimicking the ones of the (SEP) on the base manifold

(

\widetilde{W}_{N,N^{\prime}}((X^{i},X^{i}_{b}),(X^{j},X^{j}_{b})):=\frac{h_{N}^{-m}(h^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(X^{i},X^{j})}{h_{N}},\frac{d_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X^{j}}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{X^{j}X^{i}}X^{i}_{a},X^{j}_{b})}{h^{\prime}_{N^{\prime}}}\right)}{\sqrt{e^{-U(X^{i})}e^{-U(X^{j})}}}.

The two bandwidth parameters

and

tune the horizontal and vertical parts respectively. In the horizontal diffusive scaling,

goes to zero faster than

when both

Since we consider the (SEP) also on the total manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}

\pi:\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}\to\mathcal{M}
with compact semi-simple Lie group

). Then, since

is compact semi-simple, the (negative of the) killing form

\forall X,Y\in\mathrm{Lie}(G),\quad B(X,Y):=\Tr\left(\mathrm{Ad}(X)\mathrm{Ad}(Y)\right)

yields a bi-invariant metric

on

[

, and the total manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
admits the metric

\widetilde{g}:=\pi^{*}g\oplus(-B)\omega:=\widetilde{g}^{\mathcal{H}}\oplus\widetilde{g}^{\mathcal{V}}.

Definition 5.3

(Totally geodesic fibres)
.

The fibres
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{(\cdot)}:=\pi^{-1}(\{\cdot\})
of a Riemannian submersion

are said to be

if any geodesic in a fibre, seen as a submanifold of
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
with the induced metric, is also a geodesic in
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
.

Assuming that the fibres are totally geodesic, the horizontal Laplacian coincides with the Bochner horizontal Laplacian in sub-Riemannian geometry with the decomposition
\Delta_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}:=\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}+\Delta^{\mathcal{V}}
into horizontal and vertical parts
[
2

. We shall not distinguish the two as they coincide in our context.

Definition 5.4

(Horizontal Laplacian)
.

Given a smooth function
\phi\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})
, the
horizontal Laplacian

\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}
is the trace of the horizontal Hessian; i.e.,

\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}\phi:=\Tr\left(\widetilde{g}^{-1}\otimes\Hess^{\mathcal{H}}\phi\right):=\widetilde{g}^{ij}\Hess^{\mathcal{H}}_{ij}\phi,

where the
\widetilde{g}^{ij}
’s are the components of the inverse metric on the total manifold, and the horizontal part of the Hessian is the bilinear form given by

\forall\xi,\eta\in\Gamma(T\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}),\quad\Hess^{\mathcal{H}}\phi(\xi,\eta)=\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\xi}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\phi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\cdot\eta^{\mathcal{H}}.

By construction, since the distributions
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{V}
are orthogonal to each other, the trace of the horizontal Hessian can be taken with respect to the inverse of the horizontal metric
\widetilde{g}^{\mathcal{H}}
. In local orthonormal frames
(Z_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
of
\mathcal{H}
and
(F_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{V}
, the horizontal Laplacian can be rewritten as

\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(Z_{i}^{2}-\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{Z_{i}}Z_{i}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)-\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{F_{j}}F_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}.

Totally geodesic fibres yield

\sum_{j=1}^{m^{\prime}}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{F_{j}}F_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}=0.

(Hydrodynamic limit of the lifted (SEP))

Lemma A.1
.

Denote by
S_{d-1}:=2\pi^{d/2}/\Gamma(d/2)
the surface of the
(d-1)
-dimensional unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and by
B_{d}(0;1)
its unit ball. The first three moments of
k
are given by

\displaystyle M^{k}_{0}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}k\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert\right)\>dv=S_{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}k(r)r^{m-1}\>dr:=C_{k,0},

\displaystyle M^{k}_{1}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}k\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert\right)v^{i}\>dv=0,

\displaystyle M^{k}_{2}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}k\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert\right)v^{i}v^{j}\>dv=\left(\frac{S_{m-1}}{m}\int_{0}^{1}k(r)r^{m+1}\>dr\right)\delta^{ij}:=C_{k,2}\delta^{ij},

\displaystyle M^{k}_{3}

\displaystyle:=\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}k\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert\right)v^{i}v^{j}v^{l}\>dv=0.

Proof.

Regarding the odds moments, the reflection
v\mapsto-v
changes the sign of the integrated quantity; its integral must be zero. For the second moment, reflecting a single coordinate, we deduce that
\int k(\left\lvert v\right\rvert)v^{i}v^{j}\>dv\propto\delta^{ij}
. The proportionality constant
C_{k,2}
is then obtained noting that

\Tr\left(C_{k,2}(\delta^{ij})_{ij}\right)=C_{k,2}m=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}k\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert\right)\left(v^{i}\right)^{2}\>dv=S_{m-1}\int_{0}^{1}k(r)r^{m+1}\>dr.

∎

The limits of the integral operators can be found in Audibert, Audibert, von Luxurg
[
10
, Proposition 22 & Theorem 25]
and Gao
[
8
, Lemmas 13,14,15 & Theorem 4]
amongst others. We start without potential (
U\equiv 0
) to settle the ideas.

Lemma A.2

Let

Since

A.1

∎

\displaystyle=C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}e^{-\widetilde{U}(x,u)}+O(h^{2})+O((h^{\prime})^{2})

by
Lemma

A.5
.
∎

Lemma A.7
.

Let
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\widetilde{\mathcal{M}})
be a test function. Then uniformly in
(x,u)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}
,

h^{-2}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{y}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\varphi(y,q)-\varphi(x,u)\right)}{\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(y,q)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}}e^{-\widetilde{U}(y,q)}\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}(y,q)\\
=\frac{C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},2}}{2\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}e^{(2\alpha-1)\widetilde{U}(x,u)}\Delta_{2(1-\alpha)}^{\mathcal{H}}\varphi(x,u)+O\left(h^{-2}(h^{\prime})^{2}\right).

Proof.

We decompose the difference of test functions with respect to the geometric structure;

\varphi(y,q)-\varphi(x,u)=\left(\varphi(y,q)-\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)\right)+\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right).

We expand the denominator;
(\Lambda^{N},(\Lambda^{\prime})^{N^{\prime}})
is a (PPP) of intensity
NN^{\prime}e^{-\widetilde{U}}d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\frac{1}{N^{\prime}}\sum_{X^{j}\in\Lambda^{N}}\sum_{X^{j}_{b}\in\widetilde{\Lambda}^{N^{\prime}}}h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}(x,X^{j})}{h},\frac{d_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{X^{j}}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{X^{j}x}u,X^{j}_{b})}{h^{\prime}}\right)\right]

(A.8)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{z}}h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-\widetilde{m}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,z)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{z}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{zx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{z}}(q)d\mu(z).

Step 1 (Expansion in the fibre)
where we expand the first term of the splitting in normal coordinates about
(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)
.

We replace the denominator by (
A.8
) which is computed in
Lemma

A.6
to obtain

\displaystyle h^{-2}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{x}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\varphi(y,q)-\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)\right)}{\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(y,q)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}}e^{-\widetilde{U}(y,q)}\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}(y,q)

\displaystyle=h^{-2}\frac{e^{\alpha U(x)}}{\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}h^{-2}\int_{B_{\mathcal{M}}(0;\,h)}\int_{B_{T_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(0;\,1)}h^{-m}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)\left(1+O(h^{2})+O((h^{\prime})^{2})\right)

(A.9)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(\varphi\left(\exp_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(h^{\prime}w)\right)-\varphi(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)\right)e^{-(1-\alpha)U(y)}\sqrt{\det\widetilde{g}^{\mathcal{V}}\left(\exp_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(h^{\prime}w)\right)}\>dwd\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}(y)

Denote by
(F_{j})_{1\leq j\leq m^{\prime}}
the orthonormal base of
T_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}=\mathcal{V}_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}
so that

\left(\varphi\left(\exp_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(h^{\prime}w_{y})\right)-\varphi(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)\right)=d_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}\varphi(w)h^{\prime}+O((h^{\prime})^{2})=F_{j}\varphi w^{j}h^{\prime}+O((h^{\prime})^{2}).

and (
A.9
) becomes

h^{-2}\frac{e^{\alpha U(x)}}{\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}h^{-2}\int_{B_{\mathcal{M}}(0;\,h)}e^{-(1-\alpha)U(y)}h^{\prime}F_{j}\varphi\Bigg(\int_{B_{m^{\prime}}(0;\,1)}h^{-m}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)w^{j}\>dw\Bigg)+O\left(h^{-2}(h^{\prime})^{2}\right),

whose integrated term is zero by
Lemma

A.5
. Thus

(A.10)

h^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}\frac{h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{x}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\varphi(y,q)-\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)\right)}{\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(y,q)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}}\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(q)d\mu(y)\\
=O\left(h^{-2}(h^{\prime})^{2}\right).

Step 2 (Expansion along the base manifold)
where we expand the second term of the splitting in normal coordinates about both
x
and
(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)
.
As in Step 1, by replacing the denominator we obtain

\displaystyle h^{-2}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\frac{h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{y}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)}{\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(y,q)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}}e^{-\widetilde{U}(y,q)}\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}(y,q)

\displaystyle=h^{-2}\frac{e^{(2\alpha-1)U(x)}}{\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}\int_{B_{\mathcal{M}}(0;\,h)}\int_{B_{T_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(0;\,1)}h^{-m}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}(x,y)}{h},\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)

(A.11)

\displaystyle\quad\times e^{-(1-\alpha)\left(U(y)-U(x)\right)}\left(1+O(h^{2})+O((h^{\prime})^{2}\right)\sqrt{\det\widetilde{g}^{\mathcal{V}}\left(\exp_{(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)}^{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(h^{\prime}w)\right)}\>dwd\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathcal{M}}(y).

Denote by
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(I;\mathcal{M})
the geodesic with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma^{\prime}(0)=hv
so that
\gamma(1)=y
. Its horizontal lift
\widetilde{\gamma}
starting at
(x,u)
is the unique horizontal geodesic satisfying

\pi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}=\gamma,\quad\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{\gamma}}

with
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=(x,u)
and
\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(0)=h\widetilde{v}
so that
\widetilde{\gamma}(1)=(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u)
. Then

\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)=\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(1)-\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=\frac{d}{dt}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(t)+O(h^{3}),

where

\frac{d}{dt}\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(t)=d_{\widetilde{\gamma}(t)}\varphi\circ\left(d\pi_{\widetilde{\gamma}(t)}\right)^{-1}\left(\gamma^{\prime}(t)\right)=d_{\widetilde{\gamma}(t)}\varphi\left(\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\right)=\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)=\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t),

and, since
\widetilde{\gamma}
is an horizontal geodesic and the inner product is compatible with the connection
\widetilde{\nabla}
,

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\varphi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}(t)=\frac{d}{dt}\left(\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)\right)=\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)+\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\cdot\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)}\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)=\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t)}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{\gamma}^{\prime}(t).

The expansion is thus given by

(A.12)

\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)=d_{(x,u)}\varphi\left(\widetilde{v}\right)h+\frac{1}{2}\Hess_{x}^{\mathcal{H}}\varphi\left(\widetilde{v},\widetilde{v}\right)h^{2}+O(h^{3}).

We write

with orthonormal base
(E_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
of

and horizontally lift it by linearity to
\widetilde{v}=v^{i}\widetilde{E}_{i}
with the orthonormal basis
(\widetilde{E}_{i})_{1\leq i\leq m}
of
\mathcal{H}_{(x,u)}
; the above expansion then becomes

\displaystyle\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E}_{i}\varphi v^{i}h+\frac{1}{2}\left(\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}\left(\widetilde{\nabla}\varphi\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)v^{i}v^{j}h^{2}+O(h^{3})

(A.13)

\displaystyle=\widetilde{E}_{i}\varphi v^{i}h+\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{j}-\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}E_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\varphi v^{i}v^{j}h^{2}+O(h^{3}).

Substituting (
A.13
) into (
A.11
)

\displaystyle h^{-2}\frac{e^{(2\alpha-1)U(x)}}{\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}\int_{B_{m^{\prime}}(0;\,1)}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert,\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)\Bigg\{\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}\varphi v^{i}h+\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{j}-\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}E_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}\right)\varphi v^{i}v^{j}h^{2}+O(h^{3})\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\left(1-(1-\alpha)E_{l}Uv^{l}+O(h^{2})\right)\left(1+O(h^{2})+O((h^{\prime})^{2}\right)\left(1+O((h^{\prime})^{2}\right)\left(1+O(h^{2}))\right)\>dwdv

\displaystyle=h^{-2}\frac{e^{(2\alpha-1)U(x)}}{\left(C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}},0}\right)^{2\alpha}}\int_{B_{m}(0;\,1)}\int_{B_{m^{\prime}}(0;\,1)}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\left\lvert v\right\rvert,\left\lvert w\right\rvert\right)

(A.14)

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg\{\widetilde{E}_{i}\varphi v^{i}h+\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{j}-\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}-2(1-\alpha)E_{i}U\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)\varphi v^{i}v^{j}h^{2}\Bigg\}\>dwdv+O\left(h^{-1}(h^{\prime})^{2}\right).

The first moment in (
A.14
) vanishes while the second yields a (modified) horizontal Laplacian by

A.5
;

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{E}_{j}-\left(\widetilde{\nabla}_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}-2(1-\alpha)E_{i}U\widetilde{E}_{j}\right)\varphi

\displaystyle=\frac{C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},2}}}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left(\widetilde{E}_{i}^{2}-\left(\nabla_{\widetilde{E}_{i}}E_{i}\right)^{\mathcal{H}}-2(1-\alpha)\widetilde{E}_{i}\widetilde{U}\widetilde{E}_{i}\right)\varphi

\displaystyle:=\frac{C_{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}},2}}}{2}\Delta^{\mathcal{H}}_{2(1-\alpha)}\varphi.

Thus,

(A.15)

h^{-2}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}\frac{h^{-m}(h^{\prime})^{-m^{\prime}}k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}\left(\frac{d_{\mathcal{M}}(x,y)}{h},\frac{d_{{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}_{x}}(\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u,q)}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\varphi\left(y,\widetilde{\mathrm{P}}_{yx}u\right)-\varphi(x,u)\right)}{\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(x,u)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}\left(\mathbb{E}\Big[\overline{k_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}}}(y,q)\Big]\right)^{\alpha}}\>d\mathrm{Vol}_{\widetilde{\mathcal{M}}_{y}}(q)d\mu(y)\\

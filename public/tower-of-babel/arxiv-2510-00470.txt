Silting reduction, relative AGK’s construction and Higgs construction
Yilin Wu

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a Calabi–Yau quadruple as a generalization of Iyama–Yang’s Calabi–Yau triple. For each ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -Calabi–Yau quadruple, we show that the associated Higgs category is a d d -Calabi–Yau Frobenius extriangulated category, which moreover admits a canonical d d -cluster-tilting subcategory. Concrete examples arise from the construction of relative cluster categories and Higgs categories in the setting of ice quivers with potentials, as well as from the singularity category of an isolated singularity. As an application, we prove that both the relative Amiot–Guo–Keller’s construction and the Higgs construction of a ( d + 1 ) (d+1) -Calabi–Yau quadruple take silting reduction to Calabi–Yau reduction.



1.
Introduction

Triangulated and derived categories appear ubiquitously throughout mathematics, with applications in representation theory, algebraic geometry, algebraic topology, and mathematical physics. An important tool in the study of derived categories is provided by silting reduction and Calabi–Yau reduction
[
12
,
13
]
. The concept of silting appeared for the first time in a paper of Keller–Vossieck
[
17
]
. Since then, silting theory and silting reduction have played significant roles in the study of triangulated categories, in particular derived categories
[
13
]
. Moreover, silting reduction has been shown to be closely related to Calabi–Yau reduction and has found wide applications in representation theory. In
[
13
]
, they introduced the notion of a Calabi–Yau triple and showed that Amiot–Guo–Keller’s construction (
[
2
,
11
]
) is a direct passage from tilting theory to cluster tilting theory.

The aim of this paper is to introduce the notion of a Calabi–Yau quadruple as a generalization of Iyama–Yang’s Calabi–Yau triple
[
13
]
. For each
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple, we show that the associated Higgs category is a Frobenius
d
-Calabi–Yau extriangulated category, which moreover admits a canonical
d
-cluster-tilting subcategory.
Let
{\mathcal{T}}
be a triangulated category,
{\mathcal{M}}
a subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
,
{\mathcal{P}}
a subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}
a triangulated subcategory such that
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{P}})
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple (see Definition
3.1
). In particular, if we take
{\mathcal{P}}
to be the zero category, then
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}})
is a Calabi–Yau triple in the sense of Iyama–Yang.

Applying relative Amiot–Guo–Keller’s construction, we obtain a triangulated category
{\mathcal{C}}={\mathcal{T}}/{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
(AGK’s relative cluster category) in which
{\mathcal{P}}
becomes a silting subcategory. Then the corresponding Higgs category
{\mathcal{H}}
is defined as

{\mathcal{H}}=({\mathcal{P}}[<0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{C}}}}\cap^{\perp_{{\mathcal{C}}}}\!({\mathcal{P}}[>0]).

We show that the Higgs category
{\mathcal{H}}
is a
d
-Calabi–Yau Frobenius extriangulated category with projective-injective objects
{\mathcal{P}}
and
{\mathcal{M}}
is
d
-cluster-tilting subcategory of
{\mathcal{H}}
, see Theorem
3.24
. And the stable category
\underline{{\mathcal{H}}}
is equivalent to the AGK’s cluster category
{\mathcal{U}}/{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
, where
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
and
{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})^{\perp}
. This generalize the results of
[
23
,
18
]
in the setting of ice quivers with potentials. Examples also arise from the singularity category of an isolated singularity, see Example
3.5
and Example
3.25
.

which is a
d

becomes a

On the other hand, we first form the silting reduction of

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category, and denote by
[1]
the shift functor. We say that
{\mathcal{T}}
is
idempotent complete
if any idempotent morphism
e\colon X\rightarrow X
has a kernel.

Let
{\mathcal{S}}
be a full subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
. For an object
X
of
{\mathcal{T}}
, a morphism
f:S\rightarrow X
is called a
right
{\mathcal{S}}
-approximation
of
X
if
S\in{\mathcal{S}}
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(S^{\prime},f)
is surjective for any
S^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{S}}
is
contravariantly finite
if every object in
{\mathcal{T}}
has a right
{\mathcal{S}}
-approximation. Dually, one defines left
{\mathcal{S}}
-approximations and covariantly finite subcategories. We say that
{\mathcal{S}}
is
functorially finite
if it is both contravariantly finite and covariantly finite.

We call
{\mathcal{S}}
a
thick subcategory
of
{\mathcal{T}}
if it is a triangulated subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
which is closed under taking direct summands. Denote by
\mathrm{thick}\,_{{\mathcal{T}}}({\mathcal{S}})
(or simply
\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{S}})
) the smallest thick subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
which contains
{\mathcal{S}}
. Let
{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}
be another full subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Define

{\mathcal{S}}*{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}=\{X\in{\mathcal{T}}\,|\,\text{there is a triangle $S\rightarrow X\rightarrow S^{\prime}\rightarrow S[1]$ with $S\in{\mathcal{S}}$ and $S^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\prime}$}\}.

A full subcategory
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{T}
is
presilting
if
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{P}},{\mathcal{P}}[i])=0
for any
i>0
. It is
silting
if in addition
\mathcal{T}=\mathrm{thick}\,\mathcal{P}
. An object
P
of
\mathcal{T}
is
presilting
if
\mathrm{add}P
is a presilting subcategory and
silting
if
\mathrm{add}P
is a silting subcategory.

For a silting subcategory
{\mathcal{M}}
in
{\mathcal{T}}
satisfying
{\mathcal{M}}=\mathrm{add}({\mathcal{M}})
, we have a co-t-structure
({\mathcal{T}}_{\geqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}_{\leqslant 0})
on
{\mathcal{T}}
by
[
13
, Proposition 2.8]
, where

\mathcal{T}_{\geqslant l}=\mathcal{T}_{>l-1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{M}[-1-i]*\cdots*\mathcal{M}[-l-1]*\mathcal{M}[-l],

\mathcal{T}_{\leqslant l}=\mathcal{T}_{<l+1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{M}[-l]*\mathcal{M}[-l+1]\cdots*\mathcal{M}[-l+i].

2.1.
Silting reductions

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a presilting subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the thick subcategory
\mathrm{thick}\,\mathcal{P}
of
{\mathcal{T}}
and
\mathcal{U}
the quotient category
\mathcal{T}/\mathcal{S}
. We call
\mathcal{U}
the
silting reduction
of
\mathcal{T}
with respect to
\mathcal{P}
(see
[
1
]
). For an integer
l
, there is a bounded co-t-structure
(\mathcal{S}_{\geqslant l},\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant l})
on
\mathcal{S}
, where

\mathcal{S}_{\geqslant l}=\mathcal{S}_{>l-1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{P}[-1-i]*\cdots*\mathcal{P}[-l-1]*\mathcal{P}[-l],

\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant l}=\mathcal{S}_{<l+1}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\geqslant 0}\mathcal{P}[-l]*\mathcal{P}[-l+1]\cdots*\mathcal{P}[-l+i].

Let
{\mathcal{Z}}
be the following subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}

\mathcal{Z}={}^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathcal{S}_{<0})\cap(\mathcal{S}_{>0})^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}={}^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathcal{P}[>0])\cap(\mathcal{P}[<0])^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}.

Consider the following mild technical conditions
\colon

Conditions 2.1
.

(P1)

\mathcal{P}
is covariantly finite in
{}^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\mathcal{S}_{<0}
and contravariantly finite in
\mathcal{S}_{>0}^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
.

(P2)

For any
X\in\mathcal{T}
, we have
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,\mathcal{P}[\ell])=0=\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathcal{P},X[\ell])
for
\ell\gg 0
.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
13
, Proposition 3.2]

The following conditions are equivalent.

(a)

The conditions (P1) and (P2) are satisfied.

(b)

The two pairs
({}^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}\mathcal{S}_{<0},\mathcal{S}_{\leqslant 0})
and
(\mathcal{S}_{\geqslant 0},\mathcal{S}_{>0}^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}})
are co-t-structures on
\mathcal{T}
.

In this case, the co-hearts of these co-t-structures are
\mathcal{P}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.1]

Under the conditions
(P1)
and
(P2)
, the composition
\mathcal{Z}\subset\mathcal{T}\xrightarrow{\rho}\mathcal{U}
of natural functors induces an equivalence of additive categories:

\overline{\rho}\colon\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}.

Moreover, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
14
, Theorem 4.2]

The category
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]
has the structure of a triangulated category with respect to the following shift functor and triangles:

(a)

For
X\in\mathcal{Z}
, we take a triangle

X\xrightarrow{l_{X}}P_{X}\longrightarrow X\langle 1\rangle\longrightarrow X[1]

with a (fixed) left
\mathcal{P}
-approximation
l_{X}
. Then
\langle 1\rangle
gives a well-defined auto-equivalence of
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]
, which is the shift functor of
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]
.

(b)

For a triangle
X\longrightarrow Y\longrightarrow Z\longrightarrow X[1]
with
X,Y,Z\in\mathcal{Z}
, take the following commutative diagram of triangles

\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{f}
\textstyle{Y\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\textstyle{Z\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{g}
\scriptstyle{a}
\textstyle{X[1]\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{l_{X}}
\textstyle{P_{X}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X\langle 1\rangle\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\textstyle{X[1].}

Then we have a complex
X\xrightarrow{\overline{f}}Y\xrightarrow{\overline{g}}Z\xrightarrow{\overline{a}}X\langle 1\rangle.
We define triangles in
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]
as the complexes which are isomorphic to complexes obtained in this way.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
13
, Theorem 3.6]

The functor
\overline{\rho}\colon\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}
in Theorem
2.2
is a triangle equivalence where the triangulated structure of
\mathcal{Z}/[\mathcal{P}]
is given by Theorem
2.3
.

3.
Relative AGK’s constructions and Higgs constructions

Throughout this section, let
k
be a field and let
D=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(-,k)
denote the
k
-dual. Let
d\geqslant 1
be an integer. Let
\mathcal{T}
be a
k
-linear triangulated category and
\mathcal{T}^{\mathsf{fd}}
a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a silting subcategory of
\mathcal{T}
and
{\mathcal{P}}
a subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}
. Then it is clear that
{\mathcal{P}}
is a pre-silting subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
. Denote by

\pi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})

the canonical projection functor.
By
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
, the image
\pi({\mathcal{M}})
is also a silting subcategory of
{\mathcal{U}}
. By abuse of notation, we will write
{\mathcal{M}}
for
\pi({\mathcal{M}})
.

Definition 3.1
.

We say that
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{P}})
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple
if the following conditions are satisfied

(CY1)

The category
{\mathcal{T}}
is Krull–Schmidt,
{\mathcal{P}}
is functorially finite in
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is Hom-finite.

(CY2)

\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is left orthogonal to
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{fd}}
, i.e.
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\subseteq\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}
.

(CY3)

The pair
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})
is
relative
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau
in the sense that there exists a bifunctorial isomorphism

D\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(Y,X[d+1])

for any
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{T}}
.

(CY4)

{\mathcal{U}}
admits a
t
-structure
({\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{U}}^{\geqslant 0})\coloneqq({\mathcal{M}}[<\!0]^{\perp_{\mathcal{U}}},{\mathcal{M}}[>\!0]^{\perp_{\mathcal{U}}})
with
{\mathcal{U}}^{\geqslant 0}\subseteq\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})
.

(CY5)

{\mathcal{T}}
admits a
t
-structure

({\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0})

with
{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0}=\pi^{-1}({\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant 0})
,
{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0}={\mathcal{M}}[>\!0]^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}\cap\,^{\perp_{\mathcal{T}}}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
and
{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. Moreover,
{\mathcal{M}}
is a dualizing
k
-variety.

{\mathcal{T}}

Remark 3.2
.

(1)

If
{\mathcal{P}}
is the zero category, then
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}},0)
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple if and only if
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}})
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau triple in the sense of Iyama–Yang
[
13
, Section 5]
.

(2)

By the relative
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau property
\mathrm{(CY3)}
, it implies that
\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is also right orthogonal to
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathrm{fd}}
. Hence we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(X,Y)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(X,Y)

and

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(Y,X)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(Y,X)

for any
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{T}}
. This implies that
({\mathcal{U}},\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}),\pi({\mathcal{M}}))
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau triple. Hence it implies that
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\subseteq^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\!({\mathcal{P}}[>\!0])\cap({\mathcal{P}}[<\!0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
.

.

Let

k

has a relative left

[

A

\mathrm{pvd}(A)
the triangulated category
of perfectly valued dg
A

Let

be the full subcategory of
\mathrm{pvd}(A)
of the dg

-Calabi–Yau quadruple.

.

Let

. Let

Lemma 3.6
.

The pair
({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0})
is a bounded
t
-structure on
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
.

Proof
.
For
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
, there is a triangle

\sigma^{\leqslant 0}X\rightarrow X\rightarrow\sigma^{\geqslant 1}X\rightarrow(\sigma^{\leqslant 0}X)[1].

Since both
X
and
\sigma^{\geqslant 1}X
belong to the triangulated subcategory
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
, it follows that
\sigma^{\leqslant 0}X
belongs to
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
and hence to
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0}
. This shows that
({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0})
is a
t
-structure on
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
.

Let
X
be any object of
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. Then we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}({\mathcal{M}},X[i])=0
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{U}}({\mathcal{M}},\pi(X)[-i])\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}({\mathcal{M}},X[-i])\simeq D\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(X,{\mathcal{M}}[i+d+1])=0
for
i\gg 0
. Namely,
X
belongs to
{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant i}\cap{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant-i}
for some positive integers
i
.
∎

We denote by
\heartsuit
the heart of the
t
-structure
({\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0})
. Denote by
\sigma^{\leqslant i}
and
\sigma^{\geqslant i+1}
the truncation functors associated with the
t
-structures
({\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant i},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant i})\coloneqq({\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0}[-i],{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0}[-i])
. Then for each
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0}
, there exists a triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}

L[-1]\rightarrow Y\rightarrow X\rightarrow L

with
L=\sigma^{\geqslant 0}X\in\heartsuit
and
Y=\sigma^{\leqslant-1}X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant-1}
.

Notice that the image
\pi({\mathcal{M}})
under the quotient functor
\pi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is also a silting subcategory of
{\mathcal{U}}
.

Proposition 3.7
.

For each
X\in\pi^{-1}({\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 0})
, there exists a triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}

L[-d-1]\rightarrow X\rightarrow Y\rightarrow L[-d]

with
L\in\heartsuit
and
\pi(Y)\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 1}
.

Proof
.
Let
X\in\pi^{-1}({\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 0})
. Then
\pi(X)
lies in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 0}
. Let
\heartsuit^{\prime}
be the heart of
({\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{U}}^{\geqslant 0})
. It is not hard to see that the quotient functor
\pi\colon{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{U}}
induces an equivalence
\pi\colon\heartsuit\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\heartsuit^{\prime}
of abelian categories.

By
[
13
, Proposition 4.12,Lemma 4.13]
, there exists an object
L^{\prime}\in\heartsuit^{\prime}
such that there exists a morphism
g^{\prime}\colon L^{\prime}[-d-1]\rightarrow\pi(X)
in
{\mathcal{U}}
which induces a functorial isomorphism

g_{{}^{\prime}*}\colon\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\pi(X),U)\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(L^{\prime}[-d-1],U)

for
U\in{\mathcal{U}}^{\leqslant 0}
. Let
L
be an object of
\heartsuit
such that
\pi(L)=L^{\prime}
.

Since
\heartsuit\subseteq^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\!({\mathcal{P}}[>\!0])\cap({\mathcal{P}}[<\!0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
, we have
\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(L[-d-1],X)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(L^{\prime}[-d-1],\pi(X))
. We lift
g^{\prime}
to be a morphism
g\colon L[-d-1]\rightarrow X
in
{\mathcal{T}}
.

We extend the morphism
g
to a triangle in
{\mathcal{T}}

Y[-1]\rightarrow L[-d-1]\xrightarrow{g}X\rightarrow Y.

Then it is enough to show that
\pi(Y)
lies in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 1}
. By the proof of
[
13
, Proposition 4.12]
, we see that

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{U}}}(\pi(Y),\pi({\mathcal{M}})[\geqslant 0])=0.

This shows that
\pi(Y)
lies in
{\mathcal{U}}_{\geqslant 1}
.

∎

3.1.
The silting reduction of a Calabi–Yau quadruple

Let
({\mathcal{T}},{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{P}})
be a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple. Let
{\mathcal{O}}
be a functorially finite subcategory of
{\mathcal{M}}
.

Then
{\mathcal{O}}
is a presilting subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
satisfying the conditions
(P1)
and
(P2)
in Section
2.1
. Let

\quad{\mathcal{V}}\coloneqq{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}).

Denote by
\beta\colon{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}
the canonical projection functor. By the relative
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau property
\mathrm{(CY3)}
, we have

{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\,^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}),

which will be denoted by
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. This category can be viewed as a full subcategory of
{\mathcal{V}}
.

By abuse of notation, we will write
{\mathcal{P}}
for
\beta({\mathcal{P}})
. By
[
13
, Theorem 3.7]
,
\frac{{\mathcal{M}}}{[{\mathcal{O}}]}\subseteq\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}}{[{\mathcal{O}}]}\simeq{\mathcal{V}}
is a also silting subcategory of
{\mathcal{V}}
. Hence
{\mathcal{P}}
is a presilting subcategory of
{\mathcal{V}}
.

Denote by

\delta\colon{\mathcal{V}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{V}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})

the canonical projection functor. We have the following commutative diagram of triangulated categories

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}={\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{T}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})\simeq{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})\simeq{\mathcal{V}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{V}}={\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\delta}

Let

{\mathcal{Z}}\coloneqq^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\!({\mathcal{P}}[>\!0])\cap({\mathcal{P}}[<\!0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}.

Then
{\mathcal{Z}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{T}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})={\mathcal{U}}
induces the following triangle equivalence (
[
13
, Theorems 3.1 and 3.6]
)

\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}}{[{\mathcal{P}}]}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})={\mathcal{U}}.

Notice that we have a triangle equivalence
\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}=\frac{{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}\frac{{\mathcal{T}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})}
. Similarly, we have the following triangle equivalences

\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}{[({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})]}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}},{\mathcal{P}})\simeq{\mathcal{V}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})\simeq{\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})

and

\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}}{[{\mathcal{O}}]}\xrightarrow{{}_{\sim}}{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})={\mathcal{V}}

where
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\coloneqq^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\!(({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})[>\!0])\cap(({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})[<\!0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
and
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\!\!({\mathcal{O}}[>\!0])\cap({\mathcal{O}}[<\!0])^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}.

Lemma 3.8
.

[
13
, Lemma 5.5]

We have an equality
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
of subcategories of
{\mathcal{T}}
. In particular, if
{\mathcal{O}}={\mathcal{P}}
, then we have
{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathsf{fd}}=({\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}}))^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
.

Proof
.
Let
X\in{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. Then
X\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
is equivalent to (see Section
2.1
)

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(X,\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{<0})=0)\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{>0},X)=0.

By the relative
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau property, this is equivalent to
\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{<d+1},X)=0)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{>0},X)=0.

Since
\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})=\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{>0}*\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{\leqslant 0}

and
\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{\leqslant 0}\subseteq\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})_{<d+1}
,
it follows that the above condition is equivalent to

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}),X)=0
, i.e.

X\in\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}
. If
{\mathcal{O}}={\mathcal{P}}
, by Remark
3.2
, we have
{\mathcal{U}}^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap{\mathcal{Z}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
.

∎

Theorem 3.9
.

The quadruple
({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}},{\mathcal{P}})
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau quadruple. Namely,

(1)

The category
{\mathcal{V}}
is Krull–Schmidt,
{\mathcal{P}}
is functorially finite in
{\mathcal{M}}
and
{\mathcal{V}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is Hom-finite.

(2)

\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is left orthogonal to
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{fd}}
.

(3)

The pair
({\mathcal{V}},{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}})
is relative
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau in the sense that there exists a bifunctorial isomorphism

D\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{V}}}(X,Y)\simeq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathcal{V}}}(Y,X[d+1])

for any
X\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{V}}
.

(4)

\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}
admits a
t
-structure
(\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}^{\leqslant 0},\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}^{\geqslant 0})\coloneqq({\mathcal{M}}[<\!\!0]^{\perp},{\mathcal{M}}[>\!\!0]^{\perp})
with
\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}^{\geqslant 0}\subseteq\delta({\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}})
.

(5)

{\mathcal{V}}
admits a
t
-structure

({\mathcal{V}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{V}}^{\geqslant 0})

with
{\mathcal{V}}^{\leqslant 0}=\delta^{-1}(\frac{{\mathcal{V}}}{\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})}^{\leqslant 0})
,
{\mathcal{V}}^{\geqslant 0}={\mathcal{M}}[>\!\!0]^{\perp_{{\mathcal{V}}}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})^{\perp_{{\mathcal{V}}}}\cap\,^{\perp_{{\mathcal{V}}}}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
and
{\mathcal{V}}^{\geqslant 0}\subseteq{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. Moreover,
\beta({\mathcal{M}})
is a dualizing
k
-variety.

Proof
.

(1)

The category
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
is a full subcategory of
{\mathcal{T}}
which is closed under direct summands. Thus it is Krull–Schmidt, so is the additive quotient
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}/[{\mathcal{O}}]
. Notice that for each
X,Y\in{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{Z}}
the space
\mathrm{Hom}_{\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime}}{[{\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}}]}}(X,Y)
is a quotient of
\mathrm{Hom}_{\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}}{[{\mathcal{P}}]}}(X,Y)
and
{\mathcal{U}}\simeq\frac{{\mathcal{Z}}}{[{\mathcal{P}}]}
is Hom-finite. Hence
{\mathcal{V}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is Hom-finite.

(2)

Since
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\,^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})
, we have

\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})}(P,V)=\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(P,V)=0

for any
P\in\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
and
V\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\subseteq{\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
. This shows that
\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{P}})
is left orthogonal to
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathrm{fd}}
.

(3)

Since
{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}={\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}\cap\,^{\perp_{{\mathcal{T}}}}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}D\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{V}}}(X,Y)=D\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(X,Y)\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{T}}}(Y,X[d+1])\\
\simeq\mathrm{Hom}_{{\mathcal{V}}}(Y,X[d+1])\end{split}

for any
X\in{\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}}
and
Y\in{\mathcal{V}}
.

(4)

By Remark
3.2
(2), the triple
({\mathcal{U}},\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}}),\pi({\mathcal{M}}))
is a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau triple. The additive category
{\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}}
is a presilting subcategory of
{\mathcal{U}}
. Let

({\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}))^{\mathsf{fd}}\coloneqq\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})^{\perp}=\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})\cap\,^{\perp}\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}}).

By
[
13
, Theorem 5.4]
, the triple
({\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}),({\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}))^{\mathsf{fd}},{\mathcal{M}})
is also a
(d+1)
-Calabi–Yau triple. Hence
{\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}})
admits a
t
-structure
({\mathcal{M}}[<\!\!0]^{\perp},{\mathcal{M}}[>\!\!0]^{\perp})
with
{\mathcal{M}}[>\!\!0]^{\perp}\subseteq({\mathcal{U}}/\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}))^{\mathsf{fd}}=\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})^{\perp}
. Notice that the inclusion
\pi({\mathcal{T}}^{\mathsf{fd}})\cap\mathrm{thick}\,({\mathcal{O}}\cup{\mathcal{P}})^{\perp}\subseteq\delta({\mathcal{V}}^{\mathsf{fd}})
is clear. Hence
(4)
holds.

(5)

By
[
13
, Proposition 5.7]
, the additive quotient
\rho({\mathcal{M}})={\mathcal{M}}/[{\mathcal{O}}]
is a dualizing
k
-variety. Let
X
be an object of
{\mathcal{Z}}^{\prime\prime}
. Since
{\mathcal{T}}
admits a
t
-structure
({\mathcal{T}}^{\leqslant 0},{\mathcal{T}}^{\geqslant 0})
, there exists a triangle in

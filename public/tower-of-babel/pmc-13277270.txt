When air-raid sirens howl the rate of visits of pregnant women to the obstetrical emergency room decreases: results of a 4-months wartime follow up in a tertiary medical center
Authors: Lital Shaham, Shlomit Blumenfeld, Noa Brenner, Gad Segal, Michal Simchen, Tal Cahan, Keren Zloto, Eyal Sivan, Alina Weissmann-Brenner
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDuring wartime, healthcare systems are challenged by both treatment of injured soldiers and failure to accommodate the routine needs of civilians. One such deranged service is the obstetrical emergency room (OER): partly because services might be limited and partly on behalf of delayed visits. The impact of intense sirens howls due to civilian area bombing on pregnant women visits was not investigated.ObjectiveTo analyze the correlation between missile attacks in the center of Israel, and the visits of pregnant women to the obstetrical emergency room (OER).MethodsA retrospective study in a tertiary-medical-center compared the visits of pregnant women >23 weeks' gestation to the OER during 4-months of war to the previous year.ResultsThe overall visit rate to the OER was significantly reduced during wartime by 4% (p = 0.03). A statistically significant decrease in the number of visits per hour was found for complaints of contractions (OR=0.87, 0.86±0.97 vs. 1.0±1.1; p=0.004] and reduced fetal movements (OR=0.78, 0.27±0.55 vs. 0.34±0.62; p=0.0001) after 37 weeks' gestation, and also prior to 37 weeks' gestation (OR=0.7, 0.27±0.53 vs. 0.39±0.65; p<0.001 for contractions and OR=0.69, 0.11±0.33 vs. 0.15±0.40; p<0.001 for reduced sensation of fetal movements). There was no increase in the visits due to trauma.ConclusionsWartime conditions significantly impacted maternal healthcare-seeking behavior evidenced by modest but meaningful reduction in visits to the OER. Keywords: Wartime; Obstetrical emergency room; Delayed visits; Contractions; Fetal movements, trauma

Introduction

Sirens of war

On October 7th, 2023, war started in Israel, resulting among many other things in the launching of hundreds of missiles to the southern and central regions of the state. Whenever there are rocket or missile attacks, the home front command, part of the Israeli Defense Forces, operates a siren system that provides alerts of the upcoming threat, enabling civilians to seek shelters. Each geographic region has a defined time frame, determining the length of time (in seconds) during which, civilians must enter a shelter or protected space. In the Dan region in the center of Israel the time frame is usually 90 s [1]. Although most people in Israel are unfortunately familiar with sirens, their occurrence is still frightening, associated with victims of anxiety and trauma. The disruption of daily lives also challenges the capacity of the healthcare systems to provide uninterrupted medical care during periods of crisis.

The healthcare system preparedness for war

Armed conflicts have many impacts on the healthcare services, including increase in the number of patients, increased number of operations, increased incidence of infections, decrease in the number of available medical staff, decreased medical supplies and more [2]. The preparedness of the medical systems is crucial in coping with potential crisis during wartimeClick or tap here to enter text., and includes deviation of medical staff and medical equipment between departments, and modification of working hours. Previous studies described visits to the general emergency room during war, with extremity injuries and blunt trauma being the most common diagnoses [2–6]. The influence on pregnant patients was reported to include adverse maternal and neonatal outcomes such as preeclampsia and neonatal mortality [7–11]. The effect of wartime and the frequently howling sirens on the visits of pregnant women to the obstetrical emergency room (OER) has not been largely evaluated and appropriate national policies were not set.

Aim of the current study

The aim of the present study was to analyze the correlation between missile attacks in the Dan region in the center of Israel, accompanied by frequent sirens, and the visits of pregnant women after 23 week’s gestation to the OER, as well as for pregnancies prior to 37 weeks’ gestation. We intended to detail the causes of visits to the OER following the sirens, with emphasis on trauma (that may be cause due to the rush to the shelter), uterine contractions (that may be caused by the physical and emotional stress of seeking shelter) and decreased fetal movements (that may be caused by contractions, or may indicate a problem with the fetus), and to compare it to a similar period in the previous year.

Methods

Study population

A retrospective study was performed on all pregnant patients after 23 weeks’ gestation who visited the OER of the Chaim Sheba Medical Center between 07/10/23 (the day the war started) and 25/12/23 (the last day in 2023 with missiles attack in the Dan region in the center of Israel). Women’s data were compared to all pregnant women who visited the same OER at the Chaim Sheba medical center between 17/10/22 and 10/01/23. The shift in the Gregorian calendar was done because in 2023, October 7th was important Jewish holiday, affecting patients’ routines and we wanted to compare both holidays in order to avoid an external bias. Approval was granted by the local Institutional Review Board no. SMC 23–0820.

All demographic data and medical data on admission to the OER used in this study are those routinely entered the hospital’s database. The variables assessed in the study were: date and timing of arrival, gestational age, reasons for and final diagnosis of the visit in the OER.

Missile alerts

The Chaim Sheba Medical Center is located in the Dan region in the center of Israel. Data regarding the siren alerts of missile attacks in this area between October 7th and December 25th was derived from the Israeli National Emergency Portal [1]. We sought for correlation between the time of the sirens alerting for missile attacks in the Dan region and the time and causes of the visits in the OER, with emphasis on reduced fetal movements, uterine contractions, and trauma.

Descriptive statistics and analysis

The period of October 7th- December 25th, 2023, was compared to the corresponding period in 2022. The start date in 2022 was chosen to be October 17th, which corresponds to October 7th according to the Jewish calendar, in order to conserve holidays. Overall, 76 days of follow-up were included in our analysis: since the start date, which encompass 1,824 h.

The two periods were compared regarding the rate of visits (number of visits per hour), for visits of all reasons, and separately for visits due to trauma, contractions, and reduced fetal movement. Mean and standard deviation, median and inter-quartile range (IQR) of these variables (number of visits per hour) were computed for each period (2022 and 2023). Since the dependent variable visit rate was highly skewed to the right, violating the normality assumption required for parametric tests such as the t-test, comparison between the two years was done via non-parametric tests (Wilcoxon). In addition, univariate comparisons were done via univariate Poisson models, which yielded ORs for 2023 vs. 2022. Next, a multivariate Poisson model was fitted in order to adjust this comparison to the time of day in which the visit had occurred (morning, evening, or night shift), and to whether it occurred on a weekend. Odds-ratios for the rate in 2023 compared to the rate in 2022 were computed from the model.

In order to assess the association between the rate of visits (number of visits per hour) and a previous siren, multivariate regression models were fit to the visits data, with rate of visits (total, and by reason) as the dependent variable. Model covariates included an indicator for a siren in the previous 4 h, time of day (morning, evening, or night shift), and an indicator for weekend. In addition, the effect of calendar time was accounted for by defining calendar 2-week periods, starting at October 7, 2023, and including a categorical “period” variable in the model. The 2-week interval size was chosen as it was narrow enough to reflect the temporal changes in sirens intensity, and at the same time resulted in a reasonable number of levels for this variable. The model for total admissions was a Negative Binomial regression model because of over-dispersion. Models for contractions and reduced fetal movement visits were Poisson regression models. Hourly counts of trauma referrals were highly sparse, with the vast majority of hours having zero or one trauma presentation and very few hours with ≥ 2 events. To avoid instability of count models under such extreme sparsity, the trauma outcome was dichotomized as at least one trauma referral vs. none per hour, and modeled with logistic regression.

Significance was accepted at P < 0.05.

Analysis were made for pregnancies all the patients as well as for pregnancies before 37 weeks’ gestation.

All analyses were conducted using R software (version 3.6.2; R Core Team, 2019).

Results

During the 76 days from the beginning of missile attacks on Israel on October 7th to December 25th, 2023, there were 5,120 visits of pregnant patients to the OER, of which 1,572 were due to contractions, 487 due to reduced fetal movements and 107 due to trauma, with no statistical difference to the previous year.

A univariate analysis (Table 1) demonstrated a significant decrease in the number of visits to the OER due to uterine contractions and due to reduced fetal movements during the war when compared to a similar period in 2022. A non-significant decrease was seen in the number of visits to the OER for all reasons and due to trauma. There was no difference in the number of births, home births and intrauterine fetal deaths between the period of war and the control.

Discussion

The present study demonstrates that missile attacks have a significant negative impact on the visits of pregnant women to the OER, especially due to reduced fetal movements and contractions.

Living in a warzone and being subjected to direct missile attacks induce significant stress, particularly during pregnancy. Studies conducted in conflict zones worldwide documented increased maternal mortality rates, as well as higher incidences of pregnancy induced hypertension, preeclampsia, and eclampsia as along with elevated rates of stillbirths [7–13]. In many of these regions the antenatal healthcare systems was already suboptimal, and the armed conflict further exacerbated the situation’ leading to increased maternal and neonatal mortality. The stressors associated with missile attacks can have both direct and indirect effects on the health of pregnant women and their timely medical care.

Following sirens alerting for missile attacks people must attend the nearest shelter. This explains the findings of statistically overall decrease in the visits per hour to the OER following sirens, compared to time periods without sirens. There were also less visits per hour in the OER due to contractions, reduced fetal movements and trauma. Gedef et al. although not comparing to the timing of missile attacks, demonstrated that the insecurity during the political conflict caused a decrease in the utilization of antenatal care services [13]. In Israel this may be explained by the wish to remain in a secure shelter and not to travel when there are alerts of missile attacks. Indeed, Mor et al. showed a decrease in the daily admissions to the prenatal emergency labor ward during the first two months of the war. The strength of our study lies in specifically demonstrating a reduction in the referrals to the OER both overall and in correlation with the missile attacks [14].

Concern about the well-being of the fetus may be reflected in increase in visits to the OER because of decrease in fetal movements, while stress may cause uterine contractions, and may be associated with premature deliveries [15, 16]. Since war is a stressing event for the entire population and especially for pregnant women, we could have expected an increase in visits to the OER due to contractions or due to reduced fetal movements. However, the present study results showed a significant decrease in visits due to reduced fetal movements’ sensation and due to contractions in all patients, and also in pregnancies prior to 37 weeks’ gestation. The same explanation for this phenomenon may be the will (either on behalf of the woman or her family) to defer the OER visit and remain in a secured shelter, in order to avoid being on the road during or right after missile attacks. Another explanation may be the good antenatal health care system, that provided more reassurance to the pregnant women.

The rush to the nearest shelter during missile attacks might cause falls or trauma to the pregnant women, that may lead to increase in the visits to the OER. Contrary to this assumption, the lack of change in the visits in the OER due to trauma may be related to adherence of the population to the instructions of the Home Front Command emphasizing the need to hurry but to avoid running to the shelter, in order not to fall. In addition, previous missile attacks on Israel in the recent years contributed to increase preparedness for such conditions.

Already in 2012 Hirsch et al. published the results of questionnaires to 52 Israeli Jewish mothers following exposure to long-term or short-term rocket attacks on their homes. All mothers perceived themselves as coping well with the threat of terror [17]. Similarly, Jolof et al. described that women display considerable resilience and endurance despite facing considerable trauma [18].

We believe that adherence to instructions of the Home Front Command on one hand, and the existence of an efficient antenatal health care system on the other hand contributed to the reduction in the visits to the OER during the missile attacks. Indeed, our study did not observe any increase in the rates of home deliveries or IUFD.

We acknowledge several limitations in our study. This study shares the limitations inherent to retrospective investigations and the findings are interpreted without randomization and strict control of all study aspects. The study was performed in only one medical center. A nationwide study is recommended to examine all the referrals of all pregnant women to hospitals, obstetrical clinics and obstetrical emergency medical centers during the war. The results of such a survey may aid policy makers compile the necessary guidelines and recommendations for both healthcare professionals and general public [19]. The study examined the effect of stress from the missile attacks on the referrals to the hospital. Psychological evaluation such as using questionnaires could more accurately evaluate the direct effect of stress on the behavior of pregnant women.

To the best of our knowledge this is the first study that examined the association between sirens howling due to missiles attack and visits to the OER.

Conclusions

In the context of potentially imminent violent conflicts in our region, it is important to recognize that pregnant women – both before and after 37 weeks’ gestation – may reduce visits to the OER due to contractions and decreased fetal movements. Adherence to the instructions of the Home Front Command helps prevent unnecessary visits related to trauma. However, delayed or missed assessment can hinder the timely identification and management of critical complications. Currently, a study comparing deliveries during the war period to a similar period in the previous year is underway to better understand these dynamics and their implications.

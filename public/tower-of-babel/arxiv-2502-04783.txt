Embedding loose trees in k k -uniform hypergraphs
Yaobin Chen, Allan Lo

Abstract
A classical result of Komlós, Sárközy and Szemerédi shows that every large n n -vertex graph with minimum degree at least ( 1 / 2 + γ ) ​ n (1/2+\gamma)n contains all spanning trees of bounded degree. We generalised this result to loose spanning hypertrees in k k -uniform hypergraphs, that is, linear hypergraphs obtained by subsequently adding edges sharing a single vertex with a previous edge. We give a general sufficient condition for embedding loose trees with bounded degree. In particular, we show that for all k ≥ 4 k\geq 4 , every n n -vertex k k -uniform hypergraph with n ≥ n 0 ​ ( k , γ , Δ ) n\geq n_{0}(k,\gamma,\Delta) and minimum ( k − 2 ) (k-2) -degree at least ( 1 / 2 + γ ) ​ ( n k − 2 ) (1/2+\gamma)\binom{n}{k-2} contains every spanning loose tree with maximum vertex degree at most Δ \Delta . This bound is asymptotically tight. This generalises a result of Pehova and Petrova, who proved the case when k = 3 k=3 and of Pavez-Signé, Sanhueza-Matamala and Stein, who considered the codegree threshold for bounded degree tight trees.

Step 2: embed an almost spanning tree.

Find an almost embedding

Step 3: complete the embedding.

Our proof of Step 1 follows a similar approach to

]

We describe the key ingredient for the absorber.

Thus we have ‘enlarged’ the embedding

Note that

Therefore, we now focus on Step 2.

We first apply the weak hypergraph regularity lemma and obtain a reduce graph

To embed

into

to

First we find a perfect matching

This exists by our assumption as

We break

However just being in the same loose component is not sufficient as illustrated by the following example.
Suppose that

is a

Thus, we require a stronger notation, which we call ‘reachable’.
Roughly speaking, an edge

Recall that

into

Suppose for each such

Recall in the outline of Step

to be mapped onto the same vertex

in

Hence, we actually find a robust subgraph in the reduce graph before Step

In particular, we pick

to be in the ‘first edge’ of

After embedding

Since

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(1-2x_{3})(4x_{1}-2x_{3}-1)\leq-\frac{1}{2}(1-2x_{3})^{2}<0,

a contradiction. Hence

∎

In this paper, we showed that

Question 11.1

Let
1/n\ll\gamma\ll 1/\Delta,1/k\leq 1/4
. Let

Another direction is to investigate how large

For

Furthermore, for almost spanning loose trees, there are two directions to consider.

11.1
Almost Spanning loose trees in general hypergraphs

Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[

On the other hand, there is no perfect matching in loose Hamiltonian path.

11.2
Almost Spanning loose trees in linear hypergraphs

The other direction is the existence of loose trees embedding in linear hypergraphs. We say a hypergraph is
linear
if any two edges share at most one vertex. Recently, Im and Lee
[

considered the Dirac type problem on linear hypergraphs. They showed that in linear

We note that in general, finding perfect matchings in linear hypergraphs is not possible. Even if the graph is a Steiner triple system, it may not contain a perfect matching.
Since a binary
k
-loose tree with even depth contains a perfect matching, we can not guarantee the existence for all spanning
k
-loose trees in linear hypergraphs. Hence, it would be interesting to consider the following generalization problem for almost spanning loose trees.

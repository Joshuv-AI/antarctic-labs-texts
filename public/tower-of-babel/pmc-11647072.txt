Preoperative bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy in Graves’ disease
Authors: Takeshi Kusunoki, Ryo Wada
Published: 2024

Description

Malignant thyroid lesions may be accompanied by tracheal stenosis and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.1 However, benign thyroid disease may also lead to vocal cord paralysis.2 3 Here, we report a case of Graves’ disease with bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy and tracheal stenosis.

The patient was a mid-40s woman. She had been followed up for Graves’ disease and antithyroid drugs in other internal medicine departments for 15 years. The goitre and respiratory distress started worsening 4 months prior to admission. At the time of symptom onset, thyroid function was maintained within the normal range. However, 1 month prior to admission, a further increase in goitre, hyperthyroidism and exacerbation of dyspnoea was observed. Therefore, she was referred to our department for a detailed examination. At the first visit, diffuse swelling was observed in both lobes of the thyroid gland. Laryngeal fibre examination revealed bilateral vocal cord paralysis (median fixation) (figure 1, upper panel). Her admission findings were as follows: consciousness, peripheral oxygen saturation (SpO2): 97% (room air), blood pressure: 134/84 mm Hg, heart rate: 120 beats/min and body temperature: 37.4°C. Blood tests at admission showed free triiodothyronine: 10.5 pg/mL (normal range: 2.4–4.5 pg/mL); free thyroxine: 2.5 µg/dL (normal range: 1.0–1.7 µg/dL); thyroid stimulating hormone: <0.01 μU/mL (normal range: 0.56–4.3 μU/mL); thyrotropin receptor antibody: >40.0 IU/L (normal range: 0.0–2.0 IU/L); thyroglobulin antibodies: <10.0 IU/mL (normal range: <100 IU/mL) and thyroglobulin: 2378 ng/mL (normal range: 0–33.7 ng/mL).

Initial findings. (a) Diffuse swelling of both lobes of the thyroid gland; (b) laryngeal fibre findings showing bilateral median fixation. CT showing tracheal stenosis due to severe diffuse enlargement in both lobes of the thyroid gland, and the narrowest diameter of the trachea was 9 mm; (c) axial and (d) coronal.

Preoperative CT showed severe diffuse swelling in both lobes of the thyroid gland, with no obvious neoplastic lesions. This was accompanied by tracheal stenosis, and the narrowest tracheal diameter was 9 mm (figure 1, lower panel). In this case, total thyroidectomy and tracheostomy were performed. Preoperative thyroid function, blood calcium levels and systemic management were controlled by endocrinologists.

Intraoperatively, the bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerves were preserved, and no lesion infiltration was observed. The weight of the extracted specimen was 320 g. Histopathological examination showed findings consistent with Graves’ disease. Malignant lesions, such as thyroid cancer and lymphoma, were not observed (figure 2, upper panel).

(a) The weight of the thyroidectomy specimen is 320 g; (b) histopathological examination showing hyperplastic changes comprising dense large and small colloid-containing follicles in almost the entire area, consistent with Graves’ disease. Malignant lesions, such as thyroid cancer and lymphoma, are not observed (H&E staining, ×200). Postoperative CT showing that tracheal stenosis could extend from 9 to 17 mm in the narrowest preoperative tracheal lumen and improve to almost its normal form; (c) axial and (d) coronal.

The respiratory distress disappeared, and tracheal stenosis improved postoperatively. Postoperative CT showed that the narrowest tracheal space had expanded from 9 to 17 mm, and the preoperative stenosis had returned to the original tracheal form (figure 2, lower panel). On the 15th postoperative day, the right vocal cord paralysis disappeared, and the tracheal foramen was closed. In addition, approximately 5 months following surgery, the paralysis of the left vocal cord also improved. In this case, Graves’ disease also caused bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy, in addition to tracheal stenosis due to a giant goitre. Chiu4 reported that a potential mechanism of recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy with Graves’ disease was autoimmune hyperstimulation and consequent hyperplasia, resulting in distension of the nerve by mass effect. Wang5 strongly suggested that preoperative vocal fold paralysis is caused by benign thyroid disease. Recovery of vocal fold function may be possible if the recurrent laryngeal nerve is carefully preserved during thyroidectomy.

Learning points

Non-malignant thyroid lesions may also cause tracheal stenosis and recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

Histopathological findings of a total resection sample of the thyroid showed hyperplasia, characteristic of Graves’ disease, and no malignant findings, such as malignant cells or peripheral invasion, were observed. Therefore, mechanical compression, but not invasion by benign thyroid lesions, may be considered the cause of preoperative recurrent laryngeal nerve palsy.

When vocal cord paralysis associated with a benign thyroid lesion is suspected, the lesions only compress the recurrent laryngeal nerve on the paralysed side without malignant findings such as peripheral invasion. The recurrent laryngeal nerve should be preserved, and vocal cord paralysis can improve following surgery.

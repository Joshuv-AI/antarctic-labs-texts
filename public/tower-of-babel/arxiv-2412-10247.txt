Charged Particle in Lie-Poisson Electrodynamics
B. S. Basilio, V. G. Kupriyanov, M. A. Kurkov

Abstract
Lie-Poisson electrodynamics describes the semi-classical limit of non-commutative U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) gauge theory, characterized by Lie-algebra-type non-commutativity. We focus on the mechanics of a charged point-like particle moving in a given gauge background. First, we derive explicit expressions for gauge-invariant variables representing the particle’s position. Second, we provide a detailed formulation of the classical action and the corresponding equations of motion, which recover standard relativistic dynamics in the commutative limit. We illustrate our findings by exploring the exactly solvable Kepler problem in the context of the λ \lambda -Minkowski (or the angular) non-commutativity, along with other examples.

Consider a non-commutative spacetime, defined as a manifold

The
infinitesimal gauge transformations,
\delta^{\mathrm{nc}}_{f}

[\delta^{\mathrm{nc}}_{f},\delta^{\mathrm{nc}}_{g}]=\delta^{\mathrm{nc}}_{-{\,{\rm i}\,}[f,g]_{\star}},\qquad f,g\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

(1.1)

In the semi-classical limit, the

[f,g]_{\star}:=f\star g-g\star f

[\delta_{f},\delta_{g}]=\delta_{\{f,g\}}.

\{f,g\}=\Theta^{\mu\nu}(x)\,\partial_{\mu}f\,\partial_{\nu}g.

(1.4)

The Poisson bivector

\Theta\to 0
the Poisson gauge algebra reduces to its Abelian counterpart. Therefore, throughout this article,

In the absence of charged matter, the Lie-Poisson gauge formalism for generic Lie-algebra-type non-commutativity has been fully developed at the classical level. The infinitesimal transformations of the gauge field were derived in

On the dynamical side, we present explicit expressions for the gauge-invariant action and the corresponding equations of motion.

3
Lie-Poisson gauge-invariant mechanics

a. Gauge-invariant momenta

As we shall soon see, the deformed gauge-invariant momenta
\pi_{\mu}(p,A)
, which generalize the commutative expressions (
2.10
), play a key role in the construction of gauge-invariant dynamics.
These quantities are defined in such a way that:

\delta_{f}\pi_{\mu}=0,\qquad\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\pi_{\mu}=\pi^{\bm{0}}_{\mu}.

(3.29)

Using Eq. (
2.27
), we derive:

\delta_{f}\pi_{\mu}\big(p(\tau),A(x(\tau))\big)=\big(\gamma_{\sigma}^{\nu}(A)\,\partial_{A}^{\sigma}\pi_{\mu}+\gamma_{\sigma}^{\nu}(p)\,\partial_{p}^{\sigma}\pi_{\mu}\big)\,\partial_{\nu}f.

(3.30)

The condition (
3.29
) implies that
\pi
can be constructed by solving the partial differential equation:

\gamma_{\sigma}^{\nu}(A)\,\partial_{A}^{\sigma}\pi_{\mu}+\gamma_{\sigma}^{\nu}(p)\,\partial_{p}^{\sigma}\pi_{\mu}=0,

(3.31)

with the additional requirement:

\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\pi_{\mu}(p,A)=p_{\mu}-A_{\mu}.

(3.32)

The perturbative solution reads,

\displaystyle\pi_{\alpha}(p,A)=p_{\alpha}-A_{\alpha}+\frac{1}{2}\,{\cal C}^{\nu\beta}_{\alpha}\,A_{\nu}\,p_{\beta}+\frac{1}{12}{\cal C}^{\beta\mu}_{\alpha}{\cal C}^{\nu\sigma}_{\mu}\left(p_{\beta}p_{\sigma}A_{\nu}-A_{\beta}A_{\sigma}p_{\nu}\right)+{\cal O}({\cal C}^{3})\,.

Although exact solutions of these equations in the general case are not currently available, we analyze the most significant four-dimensional cases of non-commutativity. Gauge-invariant momenta for the
\kappa
-Minkowski case are presented in Sec. 3.
c
, while the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
and the
\lambda
-Minkowski cases are addressed in Sec.
4

b. Gauge-invariant dynamics

The quantities
\pi_{\mu}
introduced above allow the definition of a gauge-invariant deformation of the commutative Hamiltonian
H_{(\bm{0})}
:

H:=\pi_{\mu}\pi^{\mu}-m^{2}.

(3.33)

First, we construct a classical action that yields the equations of motion:

\dot{x}^{\mu}=\Lambda\,\{x^{\mu},H\}\,,\qquad\dot{p}_{\mu}=\Lambda\,\{p_{\mu},H\}\,,\qquad H(x,p)=0\,.

(3.34)

After that, we verify its gauge invariance.

To carry out the first part of this program, we introduce the Darboux coordinates
X
and
P
on the phase space:

\{X^{\mu},X^{\nu}\}=0,\qquad\{X^{\mu},P_{\nu}\}=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu},\qquad\{P_{\mu},P_{\nu}\}=0.

(3.35)

These coordinates can be constructed explicitly as:

X^{\mu}(x,p)=x^{\nu}\,\bar{\gamma}^{\mu}_{\nu}(p)\,,\qquad P_{\mu}(x,p)=p_{\mu},

(3.36)

where
\bar{\gamma}
is the inverse of the matrix
\gamma
.
It is straightforward to verify that these expressions satisfy the required Poisson bracket relations by using the equation

7

In the symplectic groupoid approach of Ref.
[

]

d. Gauge-invariant coordinates

A peculiar feature of our framework, as highlighted by Eq. (
2.20
), is the gauge dependence of the particle’s coordinates.
This raises the question of whether gauge-invariant variables can be associated with the particle’s position.

Below, we demonstrate that it is indeed possible to derive gauge-invariant deformations of the coordinates
x^{\mu}
for any background field
A_{\mu}(x)

These deformations, denoted by
\xi^{\mu}(x,A(x))
, must satisfy:

\delta_{f}\xi^{\mu}(\tau)=0,\qquad\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\xi^{\mu}(\tau)=x^{\mu}(\tau).

(3.49)

The correct commutative limit suggests the ansatz:

\xi^{\mu}\big(x,A(x)\big)=\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}\big(A(x)\big)\,\,x^{\nu}\,,\qquad\lim_{\Theta\to 0}\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}(p)=\delta^{\mu}_{\nu}.

(3.50)

The requirement of gauge invariance (
3.49
) leads to the partial differentiall equation for
\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}(p)

{\cal C}^{\alpha\beta}_{\nu}\,\Delta^{\mu}_{\alpha}(p)+\gamma^{\beta}_{\alpha}(p)\,\partial^{\alpha}_{p}\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}(p)=0\,.

(3.51)

It is straightforward to verify that the combination,

\Delta^{\mu}_{\nu}(p)=\bar{\rho}^{\mu}_{\alpha}(p)\,\bar{\gamma}^{\alpha}_{\nu}(p)\,,

(3.52)

is a suitable solution. In this formula
\bar{\rho}
denotes the inverse of the matrix
\rho

By substituting the “universal” expressions (
2.21
) and (

{\rho}
and
\gamma

3.52

\Delta(p)=T(\hat{p}),

(3.53)

where the form factor

T(s)=G^{-1}(s)\,G(-s)=\exp{(-s)}.

(3.54)

Combining these results, we obtain the explicit formula for the gauge-invariant coordinates:

\xi^{\mu}\big(x,A(x)\big)=\big[\exp{(-\hat{A}(x))}\big]^{\mu}_{\nu}\,x^{\nu},\qquad\hat{A}^{\mu}_{\nu}(x):=\mathcal{C}^{\sigma\mu}_{\nu}A_{\sigma}(x).

(3.55)

\bm{A}\cdot\bm{B}=A^{j}B^{j}
. For example:

(\dot{\bm{p}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\gamma}}\,\bm{x})=\dot{p}^{j}\bar{\gamma}^{j}_{k}x^{k}=-\dot{p}_{j}\bar{\gamma}^{j}_{k}x^{k}.

(4.56)

a. General case

When the non-commutativity is purely spatial, that is,

\{t,\bm{x}\}=0,

(4.57)

the time components of
\gamma(p)
,
\rho(p)
, and
\pi(p,A)
coincide with their commutative counterparts:

\displaystyle\gamma^{0}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{0}_{\mu}=\gamma_{0}^{\mu},

\displaystyle\rho^{0}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta^{0}_{\mu}=\rho_{0}^{\mu},

\displaystyle\pi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{0}-A_{0}.

(4.58)

The spatial components of these objects do not depend on
p_{0}
and
A_{0}
,

{\bm{\gamma}}={\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{p}),\qquad{\bm{\rho}}={\bm{\rho}}(\bm{p}),\qquad\bm{\pi}=\bm{\pi}\big(\bm{p},\bm{A}(t,\bm{x})\big).

(4.59)

Consider the
temporal
‘gauge’
\tau=t
. Performing integration by parts in the first term of the action (
3.40
) and noting that
\dot{t}=1
, we obtain:

S_{\mathrm{particle}}[\bm{x},p,\Lambda]=\int{\rm d}t\,\big[p_{0}+(\dot{\bm{p}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\gamma}}\,\bm{x})-\Lambda\cdot\big((p_{0}-A_{0}(t,\bm{x}))^{2}-|\bm{\pi}|^{2}-m^{2}\big)\big].

(4.60)

Since
p_{0}
and
\Lambda
enter the integrand without their derivatives, these variables are not dynamical. The equations of motion:

\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{particle}}}{\delta p_{0}}=0,\qquad\frac{\delta S_{\mathrm{particle}}}{\delta\Lambda}=0,

(4.61)

imply:

p_{0}=A_{0}(t,\bm{x})\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}.

(4.62)

Substituting this relation with the positive root into the action (
4.60
) yields the first-order action, depending only on the dynamical variables
\bm{x}
and

\bm{p}
:

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{particle}}[\bm{x},\bm{p}]=\int{\rm d}t\big[(\dot{\bm{p}}\cdot\bar{\bm{\gamma}}\,\bm{x})+\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{p})\big],

(4.63)

where the Hamiltonian is given by:

\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{p})=\sqrt{m^{2}+|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}+A_{0}(t,\bm{x}).

(4.64)

The resulting equations of motion:

\dot{\bm{x}}=\{\mathcal{H},\bm{x}\},\qquad\dot{\bm{p}}=\{\mathcal{H},\bm{p}\},

(4.65)

no longer involve any auxiliary variables.

In the non-relativistic approximation
|\bm{\pi}|\ll m
, we have:

\sqrt{m^{2}+|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}\simeq m+\frac{|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}{2m}+\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{|\bm{\pi}|^{4}}{m^{4}}\right),

(4.66)

so the Hamiltonian simplifies to:

\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{p})=\frac{|\bm{\pi}|^{2}}{2m}+A_{0}(t,\bm{x}),

(4.67)

where the constant term
m
has been omitted as it is irrelevant to the dynamics.

Remark.

The action (
4.63
) could have been chosen as the starting point
for any purely spatial non-commutativity. Moreover, it is possible to consider a more general expression for the Hamiltonian:

\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{p})=J(\bm{\pi})+A_{0}(t,\bm{x}),

(4.68)

where
J
is any sufficiently smooth function that ensures the correct commutative limit:

\lim_{\Theta\to 0}J(\bm{\pi})=\sqrt{m^{2}+|\bm{p}-\bm{A}|^{2}}.

(4.69)

Below we discuss two important examples of purely spatial non-commutativities: the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
case and the
\lambda
-Minkowski case.

b.
\mathfrak{su}(2)
non-commutativity

The
\mathfrak{su}(2)
non-commutativity is characterized by the following Poisson brackets between spatial coordinates:

\{x^{k},x^{l}\}=2\,\alpha\,{\varepsilon^{klm}}x^{m},

(4.70)

where
\varepsilon^{klm}
is the Levi-Civita symbol, and
\alpha
is the dimensional deformation parameter.

By calculating the matrix-valued function
\gamma
in (
2.21
), one obtains the following expression (see
[
25
]
for details):

\gamma^{k}_{a}(p)=b\left(\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}\right)\delta^{k}_{a}-\alpha^{2}\chi\left(\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}\right)p_{a}\,p^{k}-\alpha\,\varepsilon^{akl}p_{l}\,,

(4.71)

where the form factors
b(t)
and
\chi(t)
are defined as:

\chi(t)=\frac{1}{t}\,\left(\sqrt{t}\cot\sqrt{t}-1\right)\,,\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad b(t)=1+t\,\chi(t)=\sqrt{t}\cot\sqrt{t}\,.

(4.72)

The solutions of Eq. (
3.31
) can be chosen as follows:

\displaystyle\bm{\pi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\arcsin{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\bm{\bar{\pi}}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\bm{\bar{\pi}}^{2}}}\,\bm{\bar{\pi}}\,,

(4.73)

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{\pi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{p}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}\cos\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}-\bm{A}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}\cos\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}

\displaystyle+\alpha\,\bm{A}\times\bm{p}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}\,.

The crosscheck is straightforward.
The detailed derivation of this formula is provided in Appendix
A
.

To connect the expression (
4.73
) with earlier results on classical mechanics in non-commutative spaces
[
28
]
, we perform a change of variables:

\bm{k}=\bm{p}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}\,,

(4.74)

which yields the following solution of the first master equation (
2.16
):

\tilde{\gamma}^{i}_{j}(k)=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}|{\bm{k}}|^{2}}\,\delta^{i}_{j}-\alpha\,\varepsilon^{jik}\,k_{k}\,.

(4.75)

The corresponding gauge-invariant momenta are given by:

\bm{\tilde{\pi}}(\bm{k},\bm{A})=\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}\,\,\bm{k}-\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}|\bm{k}|^{2}}\,\,\bm{A}+\alpha\,\bm{k}\times\bm{A},

(4.76)

where the cross product is defined in the usual three-dimensional sense. This expression satisfies the gauge-invariance condition (
3.31
) and exhibits the correct commutative limit at
\alpha\to 0
.

By setting

J(\bm{\tilde{\pi}})=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}m}-\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}|\bm{\tilde{\pi}}|^{2}}}{\alpha^{2}\,m},

(4.77)

in Eq. (
4.68
), we obtain the non-relativistic Hamiltonian:

\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{k})=\frac{1}{\alpha^{2}m}-\frac{\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}|\bm{\tilde{\pi}}|^{2}}}{\alpha^{2}\,m}+A_{0}(t,\bm{x}).

(4.78)

This Hamiltonian is of particular interest because it leads to the super-integrable Kepler problem
[
28
]
when the Coulomb gauge potential:

\bm{A}=\bm{0},\qquad A_{0}=\frac{Q}{|\bm{x}|},\qquad Q\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.79)

is considered.
For detailed analysis of the integrals of motion and the numerical solutions of the equations of motion, refer to the cited reference.

c.
\lambda
-Minkowski non-commutativity

For the
\lambda
-Minkowski (also known as the “angular") non-commutativity, the only non-zero Poisson brackets between the spatial coordinates are:

\{{x}^{3},{x}^{1}\}=-\lambda{x}^{2}=-\{{x}^{1},{x}^{3}\},\quad\{{x}^{3},{x}^{2}\}=\lambda{x}^{1}=-\{{x}^{2},{x}^{3}\},

(4.80)

where
\lambda
is a dimensional deformation parameter.

The spatial parts
\bm{\gamma}
and
\bm{\rho}
of the solutions of the master equations (
2.16
) and (
2.25
)
can be chosen as follows (see
[
25
]
for details):

{\bm{\gamma}}(\bm{p})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 1&0&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&1&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr-\lambda\,p_{{2}}&\lambda\,p_{{1}}&1\end{array}\right),\qquad{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{p})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\cos\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&-\sin\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\sin\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&\cos\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&0&1\end{array}\right).

(4.81)

A one-parameter family of gauge-invariant momenta that satisfiy Eq. (
3.31
) is given by:

\pi_{j}(p,A)=\big(\omega\,\rho_{j}^{k}(A)+(1-\omega)\,\rho_{j}^{k}(p)\big)(p_{k}-A_{k}),\quad\quad\forall\omega\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.82)

To simplify the forthcoming analysis, we set
\omega=1
.

Consider the non-commutative dynamics of a particle in a Coulomb gauge potential (
4.79
).
At
|\bm{x}|\neq 0
, this gauge configuration satisfies the deformed Maxwell equations, derived in Ref.
[
24
]
.
The corresponding gauge-invariant momenta
\bm{\pi}
coincide with the usual momenta
\bm{p}
, and the non-relativistic Hamiltonian (
4.67
) reduces to its commutative counterpart:

\mathcal{H}(\bm{x},\bm{p})=\frac{|\bm{p}|^{2}}{2m}+\frac{Q}{|\bm{x}|}.

(4.83)

Let us introduce the new variables
X^{j}
and
P_{k}
as follows:

\bm{X}=\bar{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{p})\,\bm{x},\qquad\bm{P}=\bar{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{p})\,\bm{p},

(4.84)

with

\bar{\bm{\rho}}(\bm{p})=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr\cos\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&\sin\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr-\sin\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&\cos\left(p_{{3}}\lambda\right)&0\\
\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt\cr 0&0&1\end{array}\right).

(4.85)

A straightforward calculation shows that
X^{j}
and
P_{k}
are the Darboux coordinates on the phase space:

\{X^{j},X^{k}\}=0,\qquad\{X^{j},P_{k}\}=\delta^{j}_{k},\qquad\{P_{j},P_{k}\}=0.

(4.86)

Remarkably, the Hamiltonian (
4.83
) remains unchanged under the transformation
(\bm{x},\bm{p})\to(\bm{X},\bm{P})
. Therefore, the equations of motion in terms of the new variables:

\dot{\bm{X}}=\{\mathcal{H},\bm{X}\},\qquad\dot{\bm{P}}=\{\mathcal{H},\bm{P}\},\qquad\mathcal{H}(\bm{X},\bm{P})=\frac{|\bm{P}|^{2}}{2m}+\frac{Q}{|\bm{X}|},

(4.87)

are identical to the commutative case.

Let
\bm{X}(t)
and
\bm{P}(t)
be solutions of the commutative Kepler problem (
4.87
). The corresponding non-commutative solutions of (
4.65
) can then be obtained through the inverse transformation:

\bm{x}(t)={\bm{\rho}}\big(\bm{P}(t)\big)\,\bm{X}(t),\qquad\bm{p}(t)={\bm{\rho}}\big(\bm{P}(t)\big)\,\bm{P}(t),

(4.88)

where, we remind, the matrix
\bm{\rho}
is given by Eq. (
4.81

5
Summary

We studied the dynamics of test particles within the framework of Lie-Poisson gauge formalism. Below, we summarize the new results obtained in this paper:

1.

Gauge-Invariant Coordinates
: We derived the universal expression (
3.50
) for gauge-invariant coordinates, which is valid for all Lie-algebra-type non-commutativities.

2.

Classical Action and Equations of Motion
: We established the explicit expression (
3.40
) for the classical action and the corresponding equations of motion (
3.34
), which respect the Lie-Poisson gauge symmetry and recover standard relativistic dynamics in the commutative limit.

3.

Spatial Non-Commutativities
: For purely spatial non-commutativities, such as the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
and the
\lambda
-Minkowski ones, we presented the classical action (
4.63
) and the Hamiltonian equations of motion (
4.65
), which do not involve auxiliary variables.

4.

Gauge-Invariant Momenta
: The gauge-invariant momenta
\pi_{\mu}
play a key in our construciton. We provided explicit expressions for these objects for prominent Lie-algebra-type non-commutativities, namely for the
\kappa
-Minkowski (
3.47
), for the
\mathfrak{su}(2)
(
4.76
), and for the
\lambda
-Minkowski (
4.82
) cases.

5.

\mathfrak{su}(2)
Non-Commutativity
: We presented the Hamiltonian (
4.78
), which yields the super-integrable Kepler problem, discussed in Ref.
[
28
]
, when the Coulomb gauge potential is considered. The expression (
4.78
) is valid for a generic gauge background.

6.

\lambda
-Minkowski Non-Commutativity
: In the context of the
\lambda
-Minkowski non-commutativity, we analyzed the Kepler problem. We expressed non-commutative solutions in terms of their commutative counterparts (
4.88
). Additionally, we presented and analyzed the deformed angular momentum and Laplace–Runge–Lenz vector (
4.90
).

A. Derivation of Eq. (
4.73
)

For the forthcoming discussion, it is convenient to introduce the notations:

r:=\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2},\qquad s:=\alpha^{2}\bm{A}\cdot\bm{p},\qquad q:=\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}.

(5.100)

The order by order perturbative analysis of Eq. (
3.31
) suggests the ansatz,

\bm{\bar{\pi}}(\bm{p},\bm{A})=c\left(r,s,q\right)\left[b\left(s\right)\bm{p}-b\left(r\right)\bm{A}+\alpha\,\bm{A}\times\bm{p}\right],

(5.101)

with the symmetry property:

c\left(r,s,q\right)=c\left(q,s,r\right),

(5.102)

and the commutative limit:

\lim_{\alpha\to 0}c\left(r,s,q\right)=1.

(5.103)

By substituting this ansatz and the expression (
4.71
) for
\gamma
into the master equation (
3.31
) we arrive at the following four equations for
c(r,s,q)
:

\displaystyle 2\,\partial_{q}\left(c\,b(q)\right)+b(q)\left(b(r)-s\,\chi(q)\right)\partial_{s}c+c=0\,,

(5.104)

\displaystyle 2\,\partial_{q}c+\left(b(r)-s\,\chi(q)\right)\partial_{s}c-\chi(q)\,c=0\,,

(5.105)

\displaystyle 2\,\partial_{r}\left(c\,b(r)\right)+b(r)\left(b(q)-s\,\chi(r)\right)\partial_{s}c+c=0\,,

(5.106)

\displaystyle 2\,\partial_{r}c+\left(b(q)-s\,\chi(r)\right)\partial_{s}c-\chi(r)\,c=0\,.

(5.107)

The definitions (
4.72
) of the form factors
b(q)
and
\chi(q)
yield the relation:

2\,\partial_{q}b(q)+\chi(q)b(q)+1=0\,,

(5.108)

which implies that Eq. (
5.104
) is equivalent to Eq. (
5.105
), and Eq. (
5.106
) is equivalent to Eq. (
5.107
).

The simplest solution of the independent equations (
5.105
) and (
5.107
) can be obtained by assuming
\partial_{s}c=0
.
Separating the remaining two variables and imposing the symmetry condition (
5.102
) along with the commutative limit (
5.103
), we find:

c(r,q)=\frac{\sin\sqrt{r}}{\sqrt{r}}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{q}}{\sqrt{q}}\,.

(5.109)

The corresponding gauge invariant momenta are given by:

\displaystyle\bm{\bar{\pi}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bm{p}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}\cos\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}-\bm{A}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}\cos\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}

(5.110)

\displaystyle+\alpha\,\bm{A}\times\bm{p}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{A}|^{2}}}\,\frac{\sin\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}|\bm{p}|^{2}}}\,.

By redefining the momenta as:

\bm{\pi}(p,A)=\frac{\arcsin{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\bm{\bar{\pi}}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}\bm{\bar{\pi}}^{2}}}\,\bm{\bar{\pi}}\,,

(5.111)

we obtain other solutions of Eq. (
3.31
) with the correct commutative limit. These new gauge-invariant momenta exhibit a peculiar property:

\bm{\pi}|_{A=0}=\bm{p},

(5.112)

which is absent for
\bm{\bar{\pi}}
.

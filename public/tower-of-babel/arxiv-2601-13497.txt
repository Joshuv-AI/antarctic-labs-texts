Double Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions
Jiayi Chen, Ming Lu, Shiquan Ruan

Abstract
. We establish a ring isomorphism between the derived Hall algebra of the Jordan quiver and the ring of double symmetric functions (i.e., the ring of symmetric polynomials in two sets of countably many variables, invariant under the respective actions of their symmetric groups) with a parameter t t . This isomorphism maps the derived Hall basis (the natural basis of the derived Hall algebra) to a class of double Hall-Littlewood (HL) symmetric functions, which are formulated via raising and lowering operators. These double HL functions are parameterized by bipartitions; they reduce to the classical HL functions when one of the partitions is empty, and specialize to Schur Laurent symmetric functions at t = 0 t=0 . We also derive the Pieri rules for these double HL functions. Additionally, we obtain several natural generating functions for the derived Hall algebra as well as their transition relations, which can be transferred to the ring of double symmetric functions via the established ring isomorphism.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Hall algebras and Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions

The classical Hall algebras, known as the Hall algebra of the Jordan quiver, can be dated back to the early 20th century with Steinitz’s research on commutative
p
-groups
[
St01
]
; see also
[
Ha57
]
. This algebra is isomorphic to the ring of symmetric polynomials in infinitely many variables (i.e., the ring of symmetric functions), and its Hall basis corresponds to Hall-Littlewood (HL) symmetric functions (cf.
[
Mac95
]
).

Ringel
[
Rin90
]
developed Hall algebras for any abelian categories, thereby providing a categorical realization of the positive parts of quantum groups of Dynkin type; see
[
Gr95
]
for the extension to Kac-Moody type.
In order to realize the whole quantum groups via Hall algebras, several variants of Hall algebras are introduced for triangulated categories; see e.g.
[
Ka97
,
X97
,
PX97
,
T06
,
XX08
,
XC13
]
. In particular, derived Hall algebras were formulated in
[
T06
,
XX08
]
for any triangulated category satisfying the (left) homological-finiteness condition. The derived Hall algebra for odd-periodic triangulated categories was also given by Xu-Chen
[
XC13
]
.

Recently, the authors
[
CLR25
]
established derived Hall algebras for root categories; compare with
[
Zh25
]
. For a finitary hereditary abelian category
{\mathcal{A}}
, the derived Hall algebra of its root category was constructed by counting the triangles and using the octahedral axiom, which was proved to be isomorphic to the Drinfeld double of the Hall algebra of
{\mathcal{A}}
.

1.2.
Derived Hall algebra of the Jordan quiver

Let
Q_{\texttt{J}}
be the Jordan quiver, that is, the quiver with a single vertex
1
and a single loop
\alpha:1\rightarrow 1
. Let
{\mathcal{A}}={\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{nil}}_{\Bbbk}(Q_{\texttt{J}})
be the category of finite-dimensional nilpotent representations over the finite field
\Bbbk={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The isomorphism classes of
{\mathcal{A}}
can be parametrized by all partitions. Denote by
S^{(\lambda)}
the module for any partition
\lambda
. Let
\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})
be the Hall algebra of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
\Lambda_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[x_{1},x_{2},\dots]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
be the ring of symmetric polynomials. Then we have a
{\mathbb{Q}}(q^{-1})
-algebra isomorphism
\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})\rightarrow\Lambda_{q^{-1}}
, sending
[S^{(\lambda)}]
to the HL symmetric function
V_{\lambda}
for any partition
\lambda
; see
[
Mac95
]
. Here
\Lambda_{q^{-1}}
is the specialization of
\Lambda_{t}
at
t=q^{-1}
.

Let
{\mathcal{R}}
be the root category of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
[1]
the shift functor; see
[
PX97
]
. Then the isomorphism classes of
{\mathcal{R}}
can be parametrized by bipartitions
(\lambda,\mu)
, and the corresponding objects are
S^{(\lambda)}\oplus S^{(\mu)}[1]
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})
be the derived Hall algebra of
{\mathcal{R}}
; see
[
CLR25
]
. Let
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
be the ring of double symmetric polynomials. Then there exists an algebra isomorphism
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{q^{-1}}
by specializing
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
at
t=q^{-1}
; see
[
CLR25
, Corollary 5.5]
.

The ring
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
of double symmetric functions has been extensively investigated in the literature; see e.g.
[
BR87
,
Ch05
,
BLM15
]
. The
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
can be embedded into the ring of diagonal symmetric functions
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
, i.e., the ring of symmetric polynomials in two sets of countably many variables under the simultaneous symmetric group action. The category of nilpotent representations of the Jordan quiver can be viewed as fundamental building blocks of the category of coherent sheaves on an elliptic curve. Both diagonal symmetric functions and coherent sheaves on an elliptic curve have been shown to be closely related to the double affine Hecke algebra
[
Ch05
,
SchV11
]
.

1.3.
The goal

The goal of this paper is to introduce double HL functions
V_{\lambda,\mu}
, which correspond to the natural basis
[S^{(\lambda)}\oplus S^{(\mu)}[1]]
of
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(Q_{\texttt{J}})
.
Inspired by
[
Mac95
,
Tam11
,
LRW25
]
, the double HL functions are defined by
using raising and lowering operators, called the Giambelli type formulas. These double HL functions are parameterized by bipartitions; they reduce to the classical HL functions when one of the partitions is empty. We also derive the Pieri rules for these double HL functions.

It is remarkable that another class of double HL functions has been constructed as a limit of double Macdonald symmetric functions in
[
BLM15
]
, which is completely different from ours.

1.4.
Double Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions

The raising operator formalism played a fundamental role in the Schubert calculus of classical type developed by Buch, Kresch and Tamvakis
[
BKT08
]
. Tamvakis further developed the raising operator approach
[
Tam11
]
; in particular, starting from a raising operator definition of Giambelli polynomials (and then HL functions), he found a direct proof of the horizontal Pieri rule
[
Mac95
, III, (5.7
′
)]
. Such a Pieri rule was earlier obtained by Morris
[
Mor64
]
. The lowering operator appeared in
[
Lec06
]
. The
\imath
HL symmetric functions, derived from the
\imath
Hall algebra of the Jordan quiver, are introduced in
[
LRW25
]
by the synergistic actions of raising and lowering operators, and specialized to universal characters of type C.

With the help of Tamvakis’ approach, we are able to formulate an analogue for the ring
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[v_{1}^{\pm},v_{2}^{\pm},\ldots]
, which can be identified with the ring
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
of double symmetric functions (by interpreting
v_{r}^{\pm}
as the classic symmetric functions
q_{r}
or
h_{r}
in positive/negative part). Unlike the original definition of these operators, which applied only to a single partition
[
Lec06
,
LRW25
]
, the raising and lowering operators are revised to act on bipartitions
(\lambda,\mu)
, thereby yielding the double HL symmetric functions
V_{\lambda,\mu}
.

Similar to Tamvakis’ work, we prove that all double HL symmetric functions form a basis for the ring of double symmetric functions and satisfy two mirror identities. Consequently, we derive two Pieri rules for
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
, corresponding to the formulas governing the multiplication of the generators of the positive/negative parts with the double HL symmetric functions.
Moreover, when specializing at
t=0
, double HL symmetric functions coincide with the Schur-Laurent functions given in
[
SV15
]
.

In the derived Hall algebra
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})
, we also have Pieri rules, which arise as special cases of the derived Hall multiplication formula. Then the algebra isomorphism
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{q^{-1}}
sends the natural basis
[S^{(\lambda)}\oplus S^{(\mu)}[1]]
to the double HL functions
V_{\lambda,\mu}
. We also introduce generating functions of several natural generating
sets in
{\mathcal{D}}\widetilde{{\mathcal{H}}}(\Bbbk Q_{\texttt{J}})
.
Based on this isomorphism, we extend the classic generating functions (such as elementary/complete symmetric functions) to
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
, and consider their transition relations.

In the following, let us describe the main results more precisely.

2.
Double Hall-Littlewood symmetric functions

In this section, we introduce the Giambelli type polynomials and establish their Pieri rules for double HL symmetric functions, and compare the latter with classical HL functions.

Denote by

{\mathbb{N}},{\mathbb{Z}},{\mathbb{Q}}
the sets of non-negative integers, integers, and rational numbers, respectively.

2.1.
A Giambelli type formula

Let
v_{r}^{\pm}
be commuting variables of degree
r
, for
r\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 1}
. Let us set
v_{0}^{\pm}=1
and
v_{a}^{\pm}=0
, for
a<0
. We define

v_{\alpha}^{\pm}=v_{\alpha_{1}}^{\pm}v_{\alpha_{2}}^{\pm}\cdots\quad\text{ and }\quad v_{\alpha,\beta}=v_{\alpha}^{+}v_{\beta}^{-}

multiplicatively for any integer sequences
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\ldots)
and
\beta=(\beta_{1},\beta_{2},\ldots)
with finitely many nonzero entries.
Given two integer sequences
\alpha,\beta
, we denote
\alpha\geq\beta
(or
\beta\leq\alpha
) if
\alpha_{i}\geq\beta_{i}
, for all
i\geq 1
; in particular,
\alpha\geq 0
denotes that
\alpha
is a composition. For a composition
\alpha
, we denote

\displaystyle\begin{split}\ell(\alpha)&=\text{number of nonzero parts in }\alpha,\\
|\alpha|&=\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}+\cdots.\end{split}

(2.1)

Let
i,j\geq 1
, we define the lowering operator
L_{ij}
(cf.
[
Lec06
,
LRW25
]
) acting on the pair of integer sequences
(\alpha,\beta)
by

L_{ij}(\alpha,\beta)=((\ldots,\alpha_{i-1},\alpha_{i}-1,,\alpha_{i+1},\ldots),(\ldots,\beta_{j-1},\beta_{j}-1,,\beta_{j+1},\ldots)).

And for
1\leq i<j
we have the left raising operator
R^{+}_{ij}
acting on
(\alpha,\beta)
by

R_{ij}^{+}(\alpha,\beta)=((\ldots,\alpha_{i}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{j}-1,\ldots),\beta).

Similarly, the right raising operator
R^{-}_{ij}
acts on
(\alpha,\beta)
by

R_{ij}^{-}(\alpha,\beta)=(\alpha,(\ldots,\beta_{i}+1,\ldots,\beta_{j}-1,\ldots)).

While the raising operators were originally introduced by Young (cf.
[
Mac95
]
), some of the significant applications have appeared only recently; see
[
BKT08
,
Tam11
]
. The actions of raising and lowering operators on
v_{\alpha,\beta}
are given by

L_{ij}v_{\alpha,\beta}=v_{L_{ij}(\alpha,\beta)},\qquad R^{\pm}_{ij}v_{\alpha,\beta}=v_{R_{ij}^{\pm}(\alpha,\beta)}.

Note that all raising and lowering operators commute with each other.

Let
t
be an indeterminate. Denote by
{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
the set of partitions
\lambda
of
n
with dominance order
\unrhd
, and we sometimes write
\lambda\vdash n
. Set
{\mathcal{P}}=\cup_{n\geq 0}{\mathcal{P}}_{n}
.
The dominance order
\unrhd
also makes sense on the set of all compositions of
n
.

The ring

\mathcal{D}\Lambda_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[v_{1}^{\pm},v_{2}^{\pm},\ldots]

admits a basis
\{v_{\lambda,\mu}\mid\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}\}
.

Justification of applying raising (and lowering) operators properly can be found in
[
Tam12
, §2.2]
and
[
BMPS19
, (4.1) and below]
. Let

\displaystyle F(u)=\frac{1-u}{1-tu}=1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{r\geq 1}t^{r}u^{r}.

(2.2)

Denote

\displaystyle{\mathbb{L}}_{ij}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-L_{ij}}{1-tL_{ij}}=1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{s\geq 1}t^{s}L_{ij}^{s},

(2.3)

\displaystyle\qquad{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{\pm}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1-R^{\pm}_{ij}}{1-tR^{\pm}_{ij}}=1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{p\geq 1}t^{p}(R_{ij}^{\pm})^{p},

(2.4)

\displaystyle{\mathbb{L}}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{i,j\geq 1}{\mathbb{L}}_{ij},\qquad{\mathbb{R}}^{\pm}:=\prod_{1\leq i<j}{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{\pm}.

(2.5)

Definition 2.1
.

For any integer vectors
\alpha
and
\beta
, we define
V_{\alpha,\beta}\in\mathcal{D}\Lambda_{t}
by:

\displaystyle V_{\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{L}}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{-}(v_{\alpha,\beta}).

(2.6)

In particular, we denote
V^{+}_{\alpha}=V_{\alpha,\emptyset}
and
V^{-}_{\beta}=V_{\emptyset,\beta}
, where
\emptyset
denotes the empty set.
Note that
V^{+}_{r}=v_{r,\emptyset}=v^{+}_{r}
and
V^{-}_{r}=v_{\emptyset,r}=v^{-}_{r}
. Furthermore,
V^{+}_{\alpha}={\mathbb{R}}^{+}(v^{+}_{\alpha})
and
V^{-}_{\alpha}={\mathbb{R}}^{-}(v^{-}_{\alpha})
.

Denote

\displaystyle D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}_{ij}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{+}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\mathfrak{m}}{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{-}.

(2.7)

Below we follow
[
Tam11
, §1-2]
to manipulate the operators
D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}
. We have

\displaystyle D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}

\displaystyle=D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}_{\ell j}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{i\ell}^{+}

\displaystyle=D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}}\Big(1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{s\geq 1}t^{s}L_{\ell j}^{s}\Big)\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}\Big(1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{p\geq 1}t^{p}(R^{+}_{i\ell})^{p}\Big).

Applying
D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}
to
v_{(\alpha,r),\beta}
, where
(\alpha,r)
and
\beta
are integer vectors with
\ell(\alpha,r)=\ell
and
\ell(\beta)=\mathfrak{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle V_{(\alpha,r),\beta}

\displaystyle=D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}v_{(\alpha,r),\beta}=D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}_{\ell j}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{i\ell}^{+}(v_{(\alpha,r),\beta})

\displaystyle=D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{i\ell}^{+}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}\Big(1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{s\geq 1}t^{s}L_{\ell j}^{s}\Big)(v_{(\alpha,r),\beta})

\displaystyle=D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{i\ell}^{+}(\sum_{\gamma}t^{|\gamma|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)}v_{(\alpha,r-|\gamma|),\beta-\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}t^{|\gamma|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)}\cdot D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<\ell}\Big(1+(1-t^{-1})\sum_{p\geq 1}t^{p}(R^{+}_{i\ell})^{p}\Big)(v_{(\alpha,r-|\gamma|),\beta-\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma}t^{|\gamma|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)}\cdot D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}(\sum_{\eta}t^{|\eta|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\eta)}v_{(\alpha+\eta,r-|\gamma|-|\eta|),\beta-\gamma})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma,\eta}t^{|\gamma|+|\eta|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\eta)}\cdot D_{\ell-1,\mathfrak{m}}(v_{\alpha+\eta,\beta-\gamma}v_{r-|\gamma|-|\eta|}^{+})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\gamma,\eta}t^{|\gamma|+|\eta|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\eta)}\cdot V_{\alpha+\eta,\beta-\gamma}v_{r-|\gamma|-|\eta|}^{+},

(2.8)

summed over all compositions
\eta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ell-1}
and
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathfrak{m}}
.

Similarly, we also have

\displaystyle V_{\alpha,(\beta,r)}=\sum_{\gamma,\eta}t^{|\gamma|+|\eta|}(1-t^{-1})^{\ell(\gamma)+\ell(\eta)}\cdot V_{\alpha-\gamma,\beta+\eta}v_{r-|\gamma|-|\eta|}^{-}

(2.9)

summed over all compositions
\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ell-1}
and
\eta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathfrak{m}}
. The formulas (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) give a recursive formula for computing
V_{\alpha,\beta}
.

Proposition 2.2
.

\{V_{\lambda,\mu}\mid\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}\}
forms a
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
-basis for
\mathcal{D}\Lambda_{t}
.

Proof.

The dominance order
\unrhd
can be extended to the set
{\mathcal{P}}
of partitions such that
\lambda\unrhd\mu
if
|\lambda|<|\mu|
; and furthermore extended to the set
{\mathcal{P}}\times{\mathcal{P}}
of bipartitions such that
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})\unrhd(\lambda,\mu)
if and only if
\tilde{\lambda}\unrhd\lambda
and
\tilde{\mu}\unrhd\mu
. Then we have
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})
strictly dominates
(\lambda,\mu)
(denoted by
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})\triangleright(\lambda,\mu)
) if one of the following cases happens:

(a)

(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})=L_{ij}(\lambda,\mu)
for some
i,j
;

(b)

(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})=R^{+}_{ij}(\lambda,\mu)
for some
i<j
;

(c)

(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})=R^{-}_{ij}(\lambda,\mu)
for some
i<j
.

Therefore, by using (
2.6
) we obtain that

V_{\lambda,\mu}\in v_{\lambda,\mu}+\sum_{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})\triangleright(\lambda,\mu)}{\mathbb{Q}}(t)v_{\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu}}

where the sum is over bipartitions
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})
which strictly dominate
(\lambda,\mu)
. Recall that

forms a

-basis for

. It follows immediately that
\{V_{\lambda,\mu}\mid\lambda,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}\}
forms a
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)
-basis for
\mathcal{D}\Lambda_{t}
.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha,\beta}V_{\alpha,\beta}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}b_{\alpha,\beta}V_{\alpha,\beta}

\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha,\beta}V_{(\mu,\alpha),\beta}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}b_{\alpha,\beta}V_{(\mu,\alpha),\beta}\quad\text{ and }\quad\sum_{\alpha,\beta}a_{\alpha,\beta}V_{\alpha,(\mu,\beta)}=\sum_{\alpha,\beta}b_{\alpha,\beta}V_{\alpha,(\mu,\beta)}

\mu

2.3.
Horizontal Pieri rules

As we shall see, the following Pieri rules correspond to the multiplication formula (
3.18
) in the derived Hall algebra setting.

Theorem 2.6

(Horizontal Pieri rules)
.

For
r\geq 1
and any partitions
\rho,\nu
, we have

\displaystyle V_{\rho,\nu}\cdot v^{+}_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a+b=r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu,\rho\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\rightarrow}}\lambda}\,\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)\psi_{\lambda/\rho}(t)\;V_{\lambda,\mu},

(2.17)

\displaystyle V_{\rho,\nu}\cdot v^{-}_{r}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a+b=r}\sum_{\nu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\mu,\lambda\stackrel{{\scriptstyle b}}{{\rightarrow}}\rho}\,{\psi_{\mu/\nu}(t)\varphi_{\rho/\lambda}(t)}\;V_{\lambda,\mu}.

(2.18)

Proof.

We only prove (
2.17
).
Set
\ell=\ell(\rho)
and
\mathfrak{m}=\ell(\nu)
. Recall from (
2.3
)–(
2.7
) that

{\mathbb{L}}_{ij}^{-1}=1+(1-t)\sum_{k\geq 1}L_{ij}^{k}
and

D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq\ell\\
1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}{\mathbb{L}}_{ij}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\ell}{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{+}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j\leq\mathfrak{m}}{\mathbb{R}}_{ij}^{-}.

Then we have

\displaystyle V_{\rho,\nu}\cdot v^{+}_{r}

\displaystyle=D_{\ell,\mathfrak{m}}(v_{(\rho,r),\nu})

\displaystyle=D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}({\mathbb{L}}_{\ell+1,j})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}(v_{(\rho,r),\nu})

\displaystyle=D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq\mathfrak{m}\end{subarray}}(1+(1-t)\sum_{k\geq 1}L_{\ell+1,j}^{k})(v_{(\rho,r),\nu})

\displaystyle=D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathfrak{m}}}(1-t)^{\ell(\beta)}\;v_{(\rho,r-|\beta|),\nu-\beta}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\mathfrak{m}},|\beta|=a}(1-t)^{\ell(\beta)}\;V_{(\rho,r-|\beta|),\nu-\beta}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu}\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)V_{(\rho,r-a),\mu},

(2.19)

where we have used the mirror identity (
2.15
) with
\tilde{\rho}=(\rho,r-a)
in the last step.

Observing that
V_{(\rho,r-a),\mu}=D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}(v_{(\rho,r-a),\mu})
, and
D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}
commutes with
({\mathbb{R}}^{+}_{i,\ell+1})^{-1}
for
1\leq i\leq r
, we can rewrite the RHS of (
2.19
) as

\displaystyle V_{\rho,\nu}\cdot v^{+}_{r}

\displaystyle=\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}({\mathbb{R}}_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{-1}\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu}\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}(v_{(\rho,r-a),\mu})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu}\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq\ell}(1+(1-t)\sum_{k\geq 1}(R_{i,\ell+1}^{+})^{k})(v_{(\rho,r-a),\mu})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu}\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)D_{\ell+1,\mathfrak{m}}(\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ell+1},|\gamma|=r-a}(1-t)^{\ell(\gamma_{\bullet})}v_{(\rho+\gamma),\mu})

\displaystyle=\sum_{0\leq a\leq r}\sum_{\mu\stackrel{{\scriptstyle a}}{{\rightarrow}}\nu}\varphi_{\nu/\mu}(t)\cdot\sum_{\gamma\in{\mathbb{N}}^{\ell+1},|\gamma|=r-a}(1-t)^{\ell(\gamma_{\bullet})}V_{(\rho+\gamma),\mu}.

(2.20)

The theorem follows now by applying the mirror identity (
2.11
) to the RHS of (
2.3
).
∎

2.4.
Double Hall-Littlewood functions

We recall the Hall-Littlewood (HL) functions in variables
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
, following
[
Mac95
, Pages 208–211]
. Denote by
h_{r}
the
r
th complete symmetric function, for
r\geq 0
.
For an indeterminate
u
, denote

\displaystyle H(u)

\displaystyle=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}h_{r}u^{r}=\prod_{i\geq 1}\frac{1}{1-ux_{i}},\qquad Q(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}Q_{r}u^{r}=H(u)/H(tu).

We set
q_{r}=Q_{r}
, and
q_{\lambda}=q_{\lambda_{1}}q_{\lambda_{2}}\cdots
, for any composition
\lambda
.
Let
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots
be independent indeterminates. Recall
F(u)=(1-u)/(1-tu)
from (
2.2
). Then the HL symmetric function
Q_{\lambda}
is the coefficient of
u^{\lambda}=u_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}u_{2}^{\lambda}\cdots
in

\displaystyle Q(u_{1},u_{2},\dots)=\prod_{i\geq 1}Q(u_{i})\prod_{i<j}F(u_{i}u_{j}^{-1}).

(2.21)

According to
[
Mac95
, Page 212]
, the generating function (
2.21
) for HL functions
Q_{\lambda}
can be restated that

\displaystyle Q_{\lambda}=\prod_{i<j}\frac{1-R_{ij}}{1-tR_{ij}}q_{\lambda}=\prod_{i<j}\Big(1+(t-1)\sum_{r\geq 1}t^{r-1}R_{ij}^{r}\Big)q_{\lambda}.

(2.22)

We shall set
v_{r}^{\pm}=q^{\pm}_{r}=Q^{\pm}_{r}
in the definition (
2.6
) of
V_{\lambda,\mu}
, for any compositions
\lambda,\mu
. In this way, we identify the ring
\mathcal{D}\Lambda_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[q_{1}^{\pm},q_{2}^{\pm},\ldots]
with the ring of double symmetric functions
{\mathbb{Q}}(t)[x_{1}^{+},x_{2}^{+},\ldots,x_{1}^{-},x_{2}^{-},\ldots]^{\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}\times\mathfrak{S}_{\infty}}
.
For any partition
\lambda
, we can formulate two versions of HL symmetric functions, denoted by
Q_{\lambda}^{\pm}
in
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
. And we denote by
Q^{\pm}(u)=\sum_{r=0}^{\infty}Q^{\pm}_{r}u^{r}
.
Then we see from (
2.6
) and (

) that if one of the partitions is empty, then
V_{\lambda,\emptyset}
or
V_{\emptyset,\lambda}
coincides with
Q_{\lambda}^{+}
or
Q_{\lambda}^{-}
.
On the other hand,
V_{\lambda,\mu}
gives us another class of double symmetric functions, which are called
double HL symmetric functions
and denoted by
Q_{\lambda,\mu}
. Alternatively, in the spirit of the equivalence between (
2.21
) and (
2.22
), we can define
Q_{\lambda,\mu}
as follows.

Definition 2.7

(double HL functions)
.

For any compositions
\lambda
and
\mu
,
Q_{\lambda,\mu}
is the coefficient of
u^{\lambda}w^{\mu}=u_{1}^{\lambda_{1}}u_{2}^{\lambda_{2}}\cdots w_{1}^{\mu_{1}}w_{2}^{\mu_{2}}\cdots
in

\displaystyle Q(u,w)=

\displaystyle Q(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,w_{1},w_{2},\cdots)

(2.23)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\geq 1}Q^{+}(u_{i})\prod_{j\geq 1}Q^{-}(w_{j})\prod_{i<i^{\prime}}F(u_{i}^{-1}u_{i^{\prime}})\prod_{j<j^{\prime}}F(w_{j}^{-1}w_{j^{\prime}})\prod_{i,j}F(u_{i}w_{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\prod_{i\geq 1}Q^{+}(u_{i})\prod_{j\geq 1}Q^{-}(w_{j})\prod_{i<i^{\prime}}\frac{1-u_{i}^{-1}u_{i^{\prime}}}{1-tu_{i}^{-1}u_{i^{\prime}}}\prod_{j<j^{\prime}}\frac{1-w_{j}^{-1}w_{j^{\prime}}}{1-tw_{j}^{-1}w_{j^{\prime}}}\prod_{i,j}\frac{1-u_{i}w_{j}}{1-tu_{i}w_{j}}.

2.5.
Modified HL functions

Let
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots)
. Recall the modified Hall-Littlewood functions
H_{\lambda}(x;t)
can be defined using plethysm,
H_{\lambda}(x;t)=Q_{\lambda}(x/(1-t);t)
, cf.
[
Mac95
, Ex. 7, Page 234]
, where it was denoted by

. It can also be defined via Garsia’s version of Jing’s vertex operators
[
J91
]
.
Via raising operators the modified HL functions are defined to be

\displaystyle H_{\mu}=\prod_{1\leq i<j}\frac{1-R_{ij}}{1-tR_{ij}}h_{\mu}.

(2.24)

Note
H_{(r)}=h_{r}
.

In this subsection, we shall take
v_{r}^{\pm}=h_{r}^{\pm}
in the definition (
2.6
), and then redenote
V_{\lambda,\mu}
as
H_{\lambda,\mu}
.
In this way, we identify
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}={\mathbb{Q}}(t)[h_{1}^{\pm},h_{2}^{\pm},\ldots]
with the ring of double symmetric functions.

Definition 2.8
.

The modified double HL function
H_{\lambda,\mu}\in{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda_{t}
, for any compositions
\lambda
,
\mu
, is given by

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu}

\displaystyle={\mathbb{L}}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{-}(h_{\lambda,\mu})={\mathbb{L}}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{+}\cdot{\mathbb{R}}^{-}(h^{+}_{\lambda}h^{-}_{\mu}).

(2.25)

Then
H_{\lambda,\emptyset}
is just the modified HL function

.

Now let us set
t=0
, and consider the ring of double symmetric functions
{\mathcal{D}}\Lambda:={\mathbb{Q}}[h_{1}^{\pm},h_{2}^{\pm},\ldots]
.
Recall that in
[
SV15
]
, for partitions
\lambda,\mu
, the Schur-Laurent functions

are given by

\displaystyle s_{\lambda,\mu}=\mathrm{det}\begin{pmatrix}h^{-}_{\mu_{s}}&h^{-}_{\mu_{s}-1}&\cdots&h^{-}_{\mu_{s}-s-r+1}\\

h^{-}_{\mu_{1}+s-1}&h^{-}_{\mu_{1}+s-2}&\cdots&h^{-}_{\mu_{1}-r}\\

(2.26)

where
\ell(\lambda)=r
and
\ell(\mu)=s
. If
\mu=\emptyset
, then

is just the Schur function.
On the other hand, we have

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu}|_{t=0}=\prod_{i,j\geq 1}(1-L_{ij})\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j}(1-R_{ij}^{+})\cdot\prod_{1\leq i<j}(1-R_{ij}^{-})(h_{\lambda,\mu}).

(2.27)

As a generalization of the well-known result
s_{\lambda}=H_{\lambda}|_{t=0}
(see e.g.
[
Mac95
]
), we have the following proposition.

Proposition 2.9
.

For any partitions
\lambda
and
\mu
, we have
s_{\lambda,\mu}=Q_{\lambda,\mu}|_{t=0}.

Proof.

Denote by
r=\ell(\lambda),s=\ell(\mu)
. Let
\mathfrak{S}_{r+s}
be the symmetric group.

An integer sequence

\alpha=(\alpha_{-s},\alpha_{-s+1},\cdots,\alpha_{-1},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\cdots,\alpha_{r})

is called a
(r,s)
-composition
if
\alpha_{i}\geq 0
for
1\leq i\leq r
and
\alpha_{j}\leq 0
for
-s\leq j\leq-1
; which is furthermore called a
(r,s)
-partition
if
\alpha_{1}\geq\alpha_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\alpha_{r}>0
and
\alpha_{-1}\leq\alpha_{-2}\leq\cdots\leq\alpha_{-s}<0
.
Given a pair of partitions

\beta=(\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r})\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\cdots,\gamma_{s}),

we can get a
(r,s)
-partition
\lambda=(-\gamma_{s},\cdots,-\gamma_{1},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r})
. Conversely, any
(r,s)
-partition gives two partitions
\beta
and
\gamma
of length
r
and
s
, respectively.

For any
(r,s)
-composition
\alpha
, define

h_{\alpha}:=h^{-}_{-\alpha_{-s}}\cdots h^{-}_{-\alpha_{-1}}h^{+}_{\alpha_{1}}\cdots h^{+}_{\alpha_{r}}.

Therefore, we can attach a Schur-Laurent symmetric polynomials by (
2.26
) to the
(r,s)
-composition
\alpha
; or equivalently, we have

s_{\alpha}=\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{r+s}}\epsilon(\omega)h_{\alpha+\delta-\omega(\delta)}

where
\epsilon(\omega)\in\{\pm 1\}
is the sign of the permutation
\omega
, and
\delta
is the composition
(r+s-1,r+s-2,\cdots,1,0)
.

For any
(r,s)
-composition
\alpha
, define

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}=H_{(\alpha_{1},\cdots,\alpha_{r}),(-\alpha_{-1},\cdots,-\alpha_{-s})}.

(2.28)

Define the generalized raising operator
R_{ij}
to act on
(r,s)
-compositions
\alpha
by

R_{ij}(\alpha)=(\ldots,\alpha_{i}+1,\ldots,\alpha_{j}-1,\ldots).

for
i,j\in\{-s,\cdots,-1,1,\cdots,r\}
and
i<j
. Denote
R_{ij}h_{\alpha}=h_{R_{ij}\alpha}
. Then we can change the formula (
2.27
) to

\displaystyle H_{\alpha}|_{t=0}=\prod_{i<j}(1-R_{ij})h_{\alpha},

(2.29)

for any
(r,s)
-partition
\alpha
. It is remarkable that
h_{a}^{\pm}=0
for
a<0
.

It is enough to prove that
s_{\alpha}=H_{\alpha}|_{t=0}
for any
(r,s)
-partition
\alpha
.

We consider the ring
\mathbb{Q}[x_{-s}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{-1}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}]
. For any integer sequence
\beta=(\beta_{-s},\cdots,\beta_{-1},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r})
, we denote by
x^{\beta}=x_{-s}^{\beta_{-s}}\cdots x_{-1}^{\beta_{-1}}x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{\beta_{r}}
and
R_{ij}x^{\beta}=x^{R_{ij}\beta}
for
i<j
. Then the following holds for any
(r,s)
-partition
\alpha
:

\displaystyle\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{r+s}}\epsilon(\omega)x^{\alpha+\delta-\omega(\delta)}

\displaystyle=x^{\alpha+\delta}\prod_{i<j}(x_{i}^{-1}-x_{j}^{-1})=x^{\alpha}\prod_{i<j}(1-x_{i}x_{j}^{-1})=\prod_{i<j}(1-R_{ij})x^{\alpha}.

(2.30)

It is obvious that
\{x^{\beta}\mid\beta=(\beta_{-s},\cdots,\beta_{-1},\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r})\in{\mathbb{Z}}^{r+s}\}
is a
{\mathbb{Q}}
-basis of the ring
\mathbb{Q}[x_{-s}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{-1}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}]
.
Then there is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear map

\psi:\mathbb{Q}[x_{-s}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{-1}^{\pm 1},x_{1}^{\pm 1},\cdots,x_{r}^{\pm 1}]\rightarrow\mathcal{D}\Lambda,\;x^{\beta}\mapsto h_{\beta}.

Note that
h_{\beta}=0
if
\beta
is not a
(r,s)
-composition.
Applying the linear map
\psi
to (
2.30
), we obtain that

\sum_{\omega\in\mathfrak{S}_{r+s}}\epsilon(\omega)h_{\alpha+\delta-\omega(\delta)}=\prod_{i<j}(1-R_{ij})h_{\alpha}.

The proof is completed.
∎

\displaystyle s_{\rho,\nu}\cdot s^{-}_{r}

is trivial.

The derived Hall algebra of
{\mathrm{rep}}^{\mathrm{nil}}_{\Bbbk}(Q_{\texttt{J}})
is given in
[

]
. We shall formulate the basic properties including two Pieri rules for the derived Hall algebra of the Jordan quiver. We also establish an algebra isomorphism from the derived Hall algebra to the ring of double symmetric functions.

3.1.
Root category

It is well known that
{\mathcal{A}}=\operatorname{rep}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}^{\rm nil}(Q_{\texttt{J}})
is a uniserial category. Let
S
be the simple object in
{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then any indecomposable object of
\operatorname{rep}\nolimits_{\Bbbk}^{\rm nil}(Q_{\texttt{J}})
(up to isomorphisms) is of the form
S^{(n)}
of length
n\geq 1
.
Thus the set of isomorphism classes
{\mathcal{A}}
is canonically isomorphic to the set
{\mathcal{P}}
of all partitions, via the assignment

\displaystyle\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{r})\mapsto S^{(\lambda)}=S^{(\lambda_{1})}\oplus\cdots\oplus S^{(\lambda_{r})}.

(3.1)

Let
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
be the bounded derived category of
{\mathcal{A}}
with
[1]
the shift functor. It is well known that
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})

is a triangulated category. Let
F
be an automorphism of
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
. The orbit category
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})/F
has the same objects as
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
and

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})/F}(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})=\bigoplus_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})}(M^{\bullet},F^{i}N^{\bullet}).

For any
m\geq 1
, the orbit category
{\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}}):={\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})/[m]
is called the
m
-periodic derived category of
{\mathcal{A}}
, and
it is a triangulated category such that the natural projection
{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}})
is a triangulated functor; see
[
PX97
,
Ke05
]
. We also denote the shift functor of
{\mathcal{D}}_{m}({\mathcal{A}})
by
[1]
.
For
m=2
, we also denote
\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}}):={\mathcal{D}}_{2}({\mathcal{A}})={\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})/[2]
, which is also called the root category.

Any object of
{\mathcal{A}}
can be viewed as a stalk complex concentrated at degree zero. This induces a full embedding
{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow{\mathcal{D}}^{b}({\mathcal{A}})
, and then a full embedding
{\mathcal{A}}\rightarrow\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
. We always view
{\mathcal{A}}
as a full subcategory of
\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
in this way.

For any object
M^{\bullet}\in\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
, it is isomorphic to
M_{0}\oplus M_{1}[1]
for some (unique up to isomorphism)
M_{0},M_{1}\in{\mathcal{A}}
; see
[
PX97
]
. In this way, we call
M_{i}
the
i
-th homology group of
M^{\bullet}
, and denoted by
H^{i}(M^{\bullet})
for
i=0,1
.

3.2.
Derived Hall algebra

For two objects
M^{\bullet}
and
N^{\bullet}
in
\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
, denote by
\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(M^{\bullet})
the automorphism group of
M^{\bullet}
, and

\displaystyle\{M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}\}:=|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(H^{0}(M^{\bullet}),H^{0}(N^{\bullet}))|\cdot|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(H^{1}(M^{\bullet}),H^{1}(N^{\bullet}))|.

(3.2)

If
M^{\bullet}=M_{0}\oplus M_{1}[1]
and
N^{\bullet}=N_{0}\oplus N_{1}[1]
for
M_{0},M_{1},N_{0},N_{1}\in{\mathcal{A}}\subset\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
, then

\displaystyle\{M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}\}=|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M_{0},N_{0})||\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M_{1},N_{1})|{=|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M_{0},N_{0})||\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{{\mathcal{A}}}(M_{1},N_{1})|}.

(3.3)

For any

,

,

in
\mathcal{R}({\mathcal{A}})
, define

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet})_{L^{\bullet}}

\displaystyle=\{l:M^{\bullet}\rightarrow N^{\bullet}\mid\operatorname{Cone}\nolimits(l)\cong L^{\bullet}\}.

and then the derived Hall number to be

\displaystyle G_{M^{\bullet}N^{\bullet}}^{L^{\bullet}}:=\dfrac{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}[1])_{L^{\bullet}[1]}|}{\{M^{\bullet},N^{\bullet}\}}.

(3.4)

From

is an associative algebra with

-basis

\displaystyle{[M^{\bullet}]}*{[N^{\bullet}]}=\sum_{[L^{\bullet}]}G_{M^{\bullet}N^{\bullet}}^{L^{\bullet}}\;{[L^{\bullet}]},

(3.5)

and the identity

Given objects

is defined to be the

(3.6)

Lemma 3.4

For partitions

\displaystyle[S^{(\mu)}\oplus S^{(\tilde{\mu})}[1]]*[S^{(\nu)}\oplus S^{(\tilde{\nu})}[1]]=

Proof.

By Proposition

and the above, we can write

\displaystyle[S^{(\mu)}\oplus S^{(\tilde{\mu})}[1]]*[S^{(\nu)}\oplus S^{(\tilde{\nu})}[1]]=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}}\mathcal{G}_{\mu,\tilde{\mu},\nu,\tilde{\nu}}^{\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}}(q)[S^{(\lambda)}\oplus S^{(\tilde{\lambda})}],

(3.16)

where
\mathcal{G}_{\mu,\tilde{\mu},\nu,\tilde{\nu}}^{\lambda,\tilde{\lambda}}(T)=\frac{F_{1}(T)}{F_{2}(T)}
for

, and
F_{2}({q})=|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\lambda)})||\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\tilde{\lambda})})|

A comparison with (

∎

(4.1)

4.1.
Euler’s identity

In this subsection,
q
can also be interpreted as a formal variable.

For
n\in{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{\infty\}
, we denote by

(x;q)_{n}=(1-x)(1-qx)\ldots(1-q^{n-1}x).

We have the following identity due to Euler:

\displaystyle\exp_{q}\Big(\frac{x}{1-q}\Big):=\sum_{r\geq 0}\frac{x^{r}}{(1-q)(1-q^{2})\ldots(1-q^{r})}=\frac{1}{(x;q)_{\infty}}.

(4.4)

It is elementary to rewrite
\dfrac{1}{(x;q)_{\infty}}
in terms of the usual exponential function:

\frac{1}{(x;q)_{\infty}}=\exp\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{x^{k}}{k(1-q^{k})}\Big).

Hence we have

\displaystyle\exp_{q}\big(\frac{x}{1-q}\big)=\exp\Big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{x^{k}}{k(1-q^{k})}\Big).

(4.5)

4.2.

Proposition 4.1

Proof.

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(y,z)\widetilde{E}(-y,-z)

\displaystyle\widetilde{T}_{m,n}=\delta_{n,0}{\bf v}^{m}\frac{[m]_{\bf v}}{m}\widetilde{P}_{m}+\delta_{m,0}{\bf v}^{n}\frac{[n]_{\bf v}}{n}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{n}-\delta_{m,n}{\bf v}^{m}\dfrac{[m]_{\bf v}}{m}\quad(m\geq 1\text{ or }n\geq 1),

(4.22)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}(y,z)=\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{q^{r}-1}{r}\widetilde{P}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{q^{t}-1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{k}}{k}(yz)^{k}\big).

(4.23)

Proof.

The second formula (
4.23
) follows immediately
from (
4.22
) and (
4.18
).
So it remains to prove (
4.22
), we get it by induction.
It is clear for
m=0
or
n=0
.

Let
m,n\geq 1
and assume that (
4.22
) holds for any
m^{\prime},n^{\prime}
with
m^{\prime}<m,n^{\prime}<n
. Without loss of generality, we assume
m\leq n
. Then by (
4.19
) and the inductive assumption, we obtain

\displaystyle m\widetilde{T}_{m,n}

\displaystyle=m\widetilde{\Theta}_{m,n}-({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq m,0\leq b\leq n\\
(a,b)\neq(m,n)\end{subarray}}a\widetilde{T}_{a,b}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n-b}

\displaystyle=m\widetilde{\Theta}_{m,n}-({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{{1\leq a\leq m}}a\widetilde{T}_{a,0}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n}-({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq\min(m,n)\\
(a,a)\neq(m,n)\end{subarray}}a\widetilde{T}_{a,a}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n-a}

\displaystyle=m\widetilde{\Theta}_{m,n}-({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{{1\leq a\leq m}}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}\widetilde{P}_{a}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n}+({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq\min(m,n)\\
(a,a)\neq(m,n)\end{subarray}}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n-a}.

By definition, we have

\displaystyle({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\widetilde{P}_{a}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n}=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)\frac{[S^{(\lambda)}]}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\lambda)})|}*[S^{(m-a)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}\frac{\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\lambda)})|}\sum_{\rho,c,\alpha}F_{(n-c),\alpha}^{\lambda}F_{\alpha,(m-a)}^{\rho}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(n-c)})||\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\alpha)})|}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\rho)})|}

\displaystyle\quad\times|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(m-a)})|[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(c)}[1]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}{\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)}\sum_{\rho,c,\alpha}\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\alpha)},S^{(n-c)})_{S^{(\lambda)}}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S^{(\alpha)},S^{(n-c)})|}\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\alpha)},S^{(m-a)})_{S^{(\rho)}}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\alpha)},S^{(m-a)})|}

\displaystyle\quad\times\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\alpha)})|}[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(c)}[1]].

Note that the following holds for any
r\geq 1
and
\alpha\neq\emptyset
by
[
LRW25
, Corollary 5.2]
,

\displaystyle\sum_{\lambda}\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\alpha)},S^{(r)})_{S^{(\lambda)}}|=0.

So the terms vanish in
({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\widetilde{P}_{a}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n}
unless
n=c
or
\alpha=\emptyset
. If
n=c
, then
\lambda=\alpha
; if
\alpha=\emptyset
, then
\lambda=(n-c),n-c=a
and
\rho=(m-a)
. Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\widetilde{P}_{a}*\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n}=

\displaystyle[S^{(m-a)}\oplus S^{(n-a)}[1]]+\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}{\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)}\sum_{\rho}\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\lambda)},S^{(m-a)})_{S^{(\rho)}}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\lambda)},S^{(m-a)})|}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\lambda)})|}[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]].

Note that

\displaystyle({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq a\leq\min(m,n)\\
(a,a)\neq(m,n)\end{subarray}}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}\widetilde{\Theta}_{m-a,n-a}-\sum_{1\leq a\leq m}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}[S^{(m-a)}\oplus S^{(n-a)}[1]]=-\delta_{m,n}{\bf v}^{m}{[m]_{\bf v}}.

It remains to prove

\displaystyle m\widetilde{\Theta}_{m,n}-\sum_{{1\leq a\leq m}}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}\sum_{\lambda\vdash a}{\varphi_{\ell(\lambda)-1}(q)}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\sum_{\rho}\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\lambda)},S^{(m-a)})_{S^{(\rho)}}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits^{1}(S^{(\lambda)},S^{(m-a)})|}\frac{1}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(\lambda)})|}[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]]=0.

We shall prove (
4.24
) by considering the coefficients of
[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]]
.

For the case
\ell(\rho)=1
, we have
\rho=(m)
and
\lambda=(a)
. Then the coefficient of
[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]]
is equal to

\displaystyle\frac{m}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}-\sum_{1\leq a\leq m}{\bf v}^{a}[a]_{\bf v}\frac{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(m-a)})|}{|\operatorname{Aut}\nolimits(S^{(m)})|}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}-{\bf v}^{m}[m]_{\bf v}\frac{1}{(q-1)q^{m-1}}-\sum_{1\leq a\leq m-1}\frac{q^{a}-1}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}\frac{q^{m-a}(1-q^{-1})}{q^{m}(1-q^{-1})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}-\frac{q^{m}-1}{({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})(q-1)q^{m-1}}-\sum_{1\leq a\leq m-1}\frac{1-q^{-a}}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{m}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}-\frac{q-q^{1-m}}{({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})(q-1)}-\frac{m-1}{{\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1}}+\frac{1-q^{1-m}}{(q-1)({\bf v}-{\bf v}^{-1})}=0.

For the case
\ell(\rho)>1
, the coefficient of
[S^{(\rho)}\oplus S^{(n)}[1]]
is also equal to 0 by Case
(b)
in
[
LRW25
, Proposition 6.7]
.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(y,z)=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{r-1}}{r}\widetilde{P}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{(-1)^{t-1}}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp_{q}\big(\frac{yz}{1-q}\big),

(4.25)

\displaystyle\widetilde{H}(y,z)=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{r}\widetilde{P}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp_{q}\big(\frac{yz}{1-q}\big).

(4.26)

Proof.

Using Proposition
4.1
, we only prove (
4.25
).

By Proposition
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{E}(-y,-z)}{\widetilde{E}(-qy,-qz)}=\frac{\widetilde{\Theta}(y,z)}{(1-qyz)^{2}}.

Replacing
y,z
by
q^{m}y,q^{m}z
respectively, we obtain

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(-y,-z)=\prod_{m\geq 0}\frac{\widetilde{E}(-q^{m}y,-q^{m}z)}{\widetilde{E}(-q^{m+1}y,-q^{m+1}z)}=\frac{\prod_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{\Theta}(q^{m}y,q^{m}z)}{\prod_{m\geq 0}(1-q^{2m+1}yz)^{2}}.

(4.27)

We deduce from Proposition
4.6
that

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}(y,z)=\frac{\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{q^{r}}{r}\widetilde{P}_{r}y^{r}\big)}{\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}\frac{1}{r}\widetilde{P}_{r}y^{r}\big)}\cdot\frac{\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{q^{t}}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)}{\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)}\cdot\exp\big(\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{k}}{k}(yz)^{k}\big),

and it gives that

\displaystyle\prod_{m\geq 0}\widetilde{\Theta}(q^{m}y,q^{m}z)=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{1-q^{k}}{k}(q^{2m}yz)^{k}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{m\geq 0}\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{q^{2mk}-q^{(2m+1)k}}{k}(yz)^{k}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{m\geq 0}\ln\frac{1-q^{2m+1}yz}{1-q^{2m}yz}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\cdot\big(\prod_{m\geq 0}\frac{1-q^{2m+1}yz}{1-q^{2m}yz}\big).

Plugging it into (
4.27
), we have

\displaystyle\widetilde{E}(-y,-z)=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\frac{\prod_{m\geq 0}\frac{1-q^{2m+1}yz}{1-q^{2m}yz}}{\prod_{m\geq 0}(1-q^{2m+1}yz)^{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\prod_{m\geq 0}\frac{1}{1-q^{m}yz}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp\big(\sum_{r\geq 1}-\frac{1}{r}{\widetilde{P}}_{r}y^{r}\big)\exp\big(\sum_{t\geq 1}\-\frac{1}{t}\widetilde{P}^{\prime}_{t}z^{t}\big)\exp_{q}\big(\frac{yz}{1-q}\big).

The proof is completed.
∎

Corollary 4.8
.

We have

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(-y)\widetilde{E}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\widetilde{E}(y,z)-\widetilde{E}(y,z)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{y^{k-1}z^{k}}{1-q^{k}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}(y)\widetilde{H}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\widetilde{H}(y,z)-\widetilde{H}(y,z)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{y^{k-1}z^{k}}{1-q^{k}}.

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{\prime}(-z)\widetilde{E}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\widetilde{E}(y,z)-\widetilde{E}(y,z)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{y^{k}z^{k-1}}{1-q^{k}},

\displaystyle\widetilde{P}^{\prime}(z)\widetilde{H}(y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\widetilde{H}(y,z)-\widetilde{H}(y,z)\sum_{k\geq 1}\frac{y^{k}z^{k-1}}{1-q^{k}}.

Proof.

The first formula follows by first replacing
\exp_{q}
in the first formula in Proposition
4.7
via the identity (
4.5
), and then applying the differentiation

. The proofs of other formulas are entirely similar.
∎

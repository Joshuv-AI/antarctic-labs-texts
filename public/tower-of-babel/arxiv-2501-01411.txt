Abstract
We investigate the coboundary expansion property of tensor product codes, known as product expansion, which plays an important role in recent constructions of good quantum LDPC codes and classical locally testable codes. Prior research has shown that this property is equivalent to agreement testability and robust testability for products of two codes with linear distance. However, for products of more than two codes, product expansion is a strictly stronger property. In this paper, we prove that a collection of an arbitrary number of random codes over a sufficiently large field has good product expansion. We believe that, in the case of four codes, the same ideas can be used to construct good quantum locally testable codes, in a way similar to the current constructions that use only products of two codes.

However, the duals of LTCs are codes with small distances, so the tuple of dual codes of these LTCs cannot have good product expansion. To address this, we give a criterion for good product expansion in terms of extendable sets in the product of dual codes (Proposition

III-D

Operations Preserving Product Expansion

Before proceeding to the main steps of our proof, as outlined in Section

To achieve this, we will start with a known construction of LTCs and then take subcodes to adjust the rates. The following lemmas provide the necessary tools, ensuring that the expansion property does not degrade too much in this process.

Lemma 10

III-E

Products of LTCs are Expanding

As outlined in Section
III-C
, we now execute the first major step of our proof: showing that a collection of locally testable codes (LTCs) is product-expanding. The key insight here is that the local testability of the component codes allows us to bound the distance to a code by the Hamming weight of the syndrome.

We say that
(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{h})
is a
soundness range
for a matrix
H\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
if, for any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
, we have

\alpha_{l}d(x,\ker H)\leqslant|Hx|\leqslant\alpha_{h}d(x,\ker H).

We are interested in the case when
H
is a parity-check matrix of an LTC, and hence,
\alpha_{h}/\alpha_{l}=\Theta(1)
.
It is easy to see that the matrices
I_{t}\otimes H
,
H\otimes I_{t}
, and
H
have the same soundness ranges, where
I_{t}
is the
t\times t
identity matrix,
t\in\mathbb{N}
.

Lemma 12
.

Let
(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{h})
be a soundness range for a matrix
H\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n}
, with
d(\ker H)\geq\delta n
. Then for any
A\subseteq[m]
, there exists a set
B\subseteq[n]
and a linear map
\varphi_{H,A}:\mathbb{F}_{q}^{A}\cap\im H\to\mathbb{F}_{q}^{B}
such that
|B|\leq c|A|
, where
c=\frac{6}{\delta\alpha_{l}}
, and
H\varphi_{H,A}(y)=y
for all
y\in\im H
with
\supp y\subseteq A
.

Proof.

Fix some subset
A\subseteq[m]
, and let
a=|A|
. If
a\geq n/c
, then it suffices to choose
B=[n]
, and the lemma is proved. Thus, we can assume that
a<n/c
. Consider a basis
y_{1},\dots,y_{s}
of the vector space

\mathbb{F}_{q}^{A}\cap\im H=\{y\in\im H\ \mid\supp y\subseteq A\}.

For each
i\in[s]
, choose a vector
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
of minimal weight such that
Hx_{i}=y_{i}
. Define

B:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s}\supp x_{i},\qquad\varphi_{H,A}(y_{i}):=x_{i}

for
i\in[s]
and extend it by linearity on the whole subspace
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{A}\cap\im H
. By definition, we have
\im\varphi_{H,A}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{B}
.

Since
(\alpha_{l},\alpha_{h})
is a soundness range for
H
, for all
i\in[s]
we have
\left\lvert x_{i}\right\rvert\leqslant a/\alpha_{l}
.
To prove the lemma, it suffices to show that
|B|\leq 2a/\alpha_{l}<ac
.
Assume the converse
|B|>2a/\alpha_{l}
, and find the smallest
s^{\prime}
such that
|\bigcup_{i=1}^{s^{\prime}}\supp x_{i}|>2a/\alpha_{l}
.
Consider
B^{\prime}:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{s^{\prime}}\supp x_{i}
. It is easy to see that
s^{\prime}>2
and
2a/\alpha_{l}<|B^{\prime}|\leq 3a/\alpha_{l}
.
Let us choose uniformly at random a vector from the linear span
\left\langle x_{1},\dots,x_{s^{\prime}}\right\rangle
.
It is easy to see that the expected value of its Hamming weight is

|B^{\prime}|(1-1/q)\geq|B^{\prime}|/2>a/\alpha_{l},

so there exists a vector

such that

a/\alpha_{l}<|x|\leq|B^{\prime}|\leq 3a/\alpha_{l}<\frac{3n}{c\alpha_{l}}\leq\delta n/2\leq d(\ker H)/2.

Therefore,
d(x,\ker H)=|x|
, and then
|Hx|\geq\alpha_{l}|x|>a
, which contradicts

. Thus,
|B|\leq 2a/\alpha_{l}<ac

∎

We will use the following obvious property of the product expansion.

Lemma 13

For codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},...,\mathcal{C}_{D}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
and the zero code

\rho(\mathbf{0},\mathcal{C}_{1},...,\mathcal{C}_{D})=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{1},...,\mathcal{C}_{D}).

We will prove the lemma by induction on the dimension

Let

(H_{1}\otimes I_{m_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes I_{m_{D}})s=(H_{1}\otimes H_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes H_{D})x=0,

Let
A_{1}:=A
.
Sequentially applying Lemma

Finally, we put

Then

is linear and for all

Since we applied Lemma

, we have
|B|\leqslant c^{D-1}|A|
, where

.

Since

a^{(1)}\in\mathcal{C}_{1}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{(1)}

(I_{n}\otimes H_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes H_{D})a^{(1)}=s.

-(I_{n}\otimes H_{2}\otimes\dots\otimes H_{D})a^{(1)}=s-s=0.

\rho(\mathbf{0},\mathcal{C}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{D})=\rho(\mathcal{C}_{2},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{D})\geqslant f(D-1,\alpha_{l},\alpha_{h},\delta).

Hence,

|a^{(1)}|\leqslant|B|n\leqslant c^{D-1}|A|n\leqslant\frac{c^{D-1}|s|}{\delta}\leqslant c^{D-1}\alpha_{h}^{D-1}\delta^{-1}|x|.

\displaystyle\leqslant|B|+\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{nf(D-1,\alpha_{l},\alpha_{h},\delta)}

and substitute

Thus, we can take

III-F

Maximally Extendable Product Codes

In the previous sections we showed that products of LTCs have good expansion. However, as highlighted in our proof outline (Section
III-C
), this is insufficient for our goals, as the duals of LTCs have poor distance. To circumvent this obstacle, we now introduce the central notion of this paper:
maximally extendable product codes
. Our strategy is to reformulate product expansion in terms of extendable subsets and then show that random codes possess a universal property that allows them to inherit good expansion from the LTC-based construction.

The
\varepsilon

containing

such that for any line
\ell\in\mathcal{L}(n,D)
, either
\ell\subseteq[M]_{\varepsilon}
or
|\ell\cap[M]_{\varepsilon}|<\varepsilon n
. If

, then the set
M
is called
\varepsilon
-closed
.

For a collection of codes
\mathcal{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{C}_{D}\subsetneq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
let us define

as the maximum
\varepsilon>0
such that each
\varepsilon
-closed set in

is inner-generated for the code
\mathcal{C}_{1}\boxplus\ldots\boxplus\mathcal{C}_{D}

, which implies that for a collection

for any other collection of codes

As we will prove later, every product code

Lemma 17

Let

For a set

In the rest of this section, we prove that a randomly chosen product code over a sufficiently large finite field is maximally extendable with high probability. This is the final key ingredient for our main theorem. The proof closely follows the strategy from

Lemma 21

For

The proof proceeds in three main steps. First, we construct a generic parity-check matrix for the product code whose entries are polynomials. Second, we show that substituting variables with random finite field elements yields a maximally extendable code with high probability. Finally, we connect this result for random matrices back to random codes.

Step 1: Polynomial parameterization.

\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{N}_{2^{t}}

f(\mathbf{a})\neq 0

iff

\mathbf{a}\in U(2^{t})
.
It is also clear that
\deg f\leqslant n^{D}2^{n^{D}+1}
.
Thus, by the Schwartz–Zippel lemma (e.g., see
[
47
, Theorem 7.2]
), the probability that for a uniformly random
\mathbf{a}\in\mathbb{F}^{N}_{2^{t}}
we have
\mathbf{a}\not\in U(2^{t})
is bounded above as

\deg f/2^{t}\leqslant n^{D}2^{n^{D}-t+1},

and the claim is proved.
∎

Step 3: From Random Matrices to Random Codes.

To complete the proof of the lemma we also need to show that the uniform distribution over
codes
is approximately the same as the uniform distribution over
parity-check matrices
. This follows from the following facts:

•

If we pick a code
\mathcal{C}\in{\sf Gr}_{2^{t}}(n,k)
uniformly at random, and pick a matrix
H
for
\mathcal{C}
uniformly at random from the set of
(n-k)\times n
parity-check matrices of the code
\mathcal{C}
, then the distribution of matrices
H
obtained this way is uniform over all
(n-k)\times n
matrices of full rank.

•

For a good substitution
\mathbf{a}
, all matrices
H_{i}(\mathbf{a})
have maximal rank, thus

\mathsf{P}\left(\mathbf{a}\in U(2^{t})\mid\dim\ker H_{i}(\mathbf{a})=k_{i}\mbox{ for }i\in[D]\right)\\
\geqslant\mathsf{P}\left(\mathbf{a}\in U(2^{t})\right)\geqslant 1-n^{D}2^{n^{D}-t+1}.

•

The parameter vector
\mathbf{a}
corresponds exactly to the elements of all parity-check matrices
H_{i}
.

Thus, if we pick uniformly at random a tuple of codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{D})\in{\sf Gr}_{2^{t}}(n,k_{1})\times\cdots\times{\sf Gr}_{2^{t}}(n,k_{D})
and then pick uniformly at random parity-check matrices
H_{1},\dots,H_{D}
for these codes, with probability at least
1-n^{D}2^{n^{D}-t+1}
the corresponding substitution
\mathbf{a}
is good. Hence, by Claim
1
, the code
\mathcal{C}_{1}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{D}
is maximally extendable.
∎

Now we are ready to prove the main theorem.
See
2

Proof.

Put
\rho:=\mu_{D}(R_{1},\dots,R_{D})
where
\mu
is the function from Lemma
20
.
Fix some numbers
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k_{i}\leqslant R_{i}n
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
.
By Lemma
20
, if for a collection of codes
(\mathcal{C}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{C}_{D})\in{\sf Gr}_{2^{t}}(n,k_{1})\times\cdots\times{\sf Gr}_{2^{t}}(n,k_{D})
the code
\mathcal{C}_{1}^{\perp}\otimes\dots\otimes\mathcal{C}_{D}^{\perp}
is maximally extendable, then this collection is
\rho
-product-expanding.
Moreover, according to Lemma
21
, if we pick these codes uniformly at random, the probability of this event is at least
1-n^{D}2^{n^{D}-t+1}\to 1
as
t\to\infty
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
LTCs of Arbitrary Length

In this section, we give the proof of Theorem
4
by constructing good LTCs of arbitrary length out of the good LTCs proposed by Dinur et al.
[
1
]
.
However, first, we give an extended formulation of Theorem 1.1 from
[
1
]
, where we also include additional information about the constructed LTCs that appeared in its proof.

Theorem E3
.

For every
r\in(0,1)
there exist constants
s>0
,
\delta>0
,
\Delta\in\mathbb{N}
, and
q\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
(\Delta,s)
-locally testable
[n_{i},k_{i},d_{i}]
codes over
\mathbb{F}_{2}
with
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix
H_{i}
for some
k_{i}\geqslant rn_{i}
and
d_{i}\geqslant\delta n_{i}
, where
n_{i}:=\frac{1}{8}\Delta^{2}(q^{3i}-q^{i})
.

Remark 22
.

In Theorem 1.1 of
[
1
]
, LTCs are constructed using the square complex
\mathrm{Cay}(A,G,B)
with two generating sets
A
and
B
of size
\Delta
, where the bits are assigned to squares and the checks to the edges. The row weights in the parity-check matrix
H_{i}
of the resulting
[n_{i},k_{i},d_{i}]
code do not exceed
\Delta
. Each local code has rate no less than
3/4
, so its parity-check matrix has no more than
\Delta/4
rows, and each square participates in
4
local codes corresponding to the edges of the square. Therefore, each square is involved in at most
\Delta
checks; thus, the matrix
H_{i}
is
\Delta
-limited. It will be convenient to choose an overcomplete parity-check matrix for local codes by duplicating some of its rows multiple times so that it has exactly
\left\lfloor\Delta/4\right\rfloor
rows; in this case, the matrix
H_{i}
of the entire LTC will have
n
columns and
m=\frac{4\left\lfloor\Delta/4\right\rfloor}{\Delta}n
rows. Assuming that the rate is
<1
, we get
\Delta\geq 4
,
n/2<m\leq n
.

Remark 23
.

It is also worth noting that the test in
[
1
]
, which checks the entire set of rows in the parity-check matrix corresponding to the product of local codes, is slightly different from our definition of a local code, where exactly one row of the parity-check matrix is checked. Therefore, our parameters may differ by a constant factor from the parameters of the test from
[
1
]
. In particular, according to our definition, the locality of the constructed code is
\Delta
, while in the test from
[
1
]
the locality is
\Delta^{2}
. Furthermore, our soundness parameter
s
differs from the parameter
\kappa
in
[
1
]
.

We will also need a lemma that the direct sum of locally testable codes is also a locally testable code.

Lemma 24
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
be a
(\Delta,s)
-locally testable code of length
n
with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix of size
m\times n
. Then for any
t\in\mathbb{N}
the code
\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
is also a
(\Delta,s)
-locally testable code with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix of size
tm\times tn
.

Proof.

Let
H
be the parity-check matrix of the code
\mathcal{C}
. Consider the
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
parity-check matrix of the code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
:

H^{\prime}:=H\otimes I_{t}=\begin{pmatrix}H&0&\cdots&0\\
0&H&\cdots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&H\end{pmatrix},

where
I_{t}
is the identity matrix of size
t\times t
,
m^{\prime}=tm
,
n^{\prime}=tn
.

Consider an arbitrary
x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{t})\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n^{\prime}}
,
x_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
for
i\in[t]
.
Then we have

\displaystyle d(x,\mathcal{C}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{t}d(x_{i},\mathcal{C})\leqslant\sum_{i=1}^{t}\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{n}{m}|Hx_{i}|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}\sum_{i=1}^{t}|Hx_{i}|=\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}|H^{\prime}x|.

Furthermore, it is easy to see that the maximum weight of the rows and columns in the matrix
H^{\prime}
is the same as in the matrix
H
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Additionally, we will need a lemma that the local testability is preserved when adding a zero code.

Lemma 25
.

Consider a
(\Delta,s)
-locally testable code
\mathcal{C}\subsetneq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix
H
of size
m\times n
,
n/2\leqslant m\leqslant n
, and let
\mathbf{0}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
be the zero code of length
t
. Then the code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{t}
is a
(\Delta,s^{\prime})
-locally testable code with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix where
s^{\prime}:=\min(\frac{s}{2},1)
.

Proof.

Consider the parity-check matrix of the code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
:

H^{\prime}:=\begin{pmatrix}H&0\\
0&I_{t}\end{pmatrix},

where
I_{t}
is the identity matrix of size
t\times t
.
Let
n^{\prime}:=n+t
,
m^{\prime}:=m+t
be the number of columns and the number of rows in the matrix
H^{\prime}
, respectively.
From the constraints on
m,n
, it is easy to see that
1\leqslant\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}\leqslant\frac{n}{m}\leqslant 2\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}
.

Consider an arbitrary vector
w=(x,y)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n^{\prime}}
where
{x\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}}
,
y\in\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
.
Then we have:

\displaystyle d(w,\mathcal{C}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=d(x,\mathcal{C})+|y|\leqslant\frac{1}{s}\cdot\frac{n}{m}|Hx|+|y|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{1}{s}\cdot 2\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}|Hx|+\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}|I_{t}y|

\displaystyle\leqslant\max\left(\frac{2}{s},1\right)\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}(|Hx|+|y|)=\frac{1}{s^{\prime}}\cdot\frac{n^{\prime}}{m^{\prime}}|H^{\prime}w|.

It is easy to see that the weight of the columns and rows of the matrix
H^{\prime}
is bounded by
\max(\Delta,1)=\Delta
.
The lemma is proved.
∎

Proof of Theorem
4
.

Let
r:=(1+R)/2
. According to Theorem
E3
, there exist constants
s_{0}>0
,
\delta_{0}>0
,
{\Delta\in\mathbb{N}}
,
{q\in\mathbb{N}}
such that for any
i\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a
(\Delta,s_{0})
-locally testable
[n_{i},k_{i},d_{i}]
code
\mathcal{C}_{i}
with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix
H_{i}
of size
m_{i}\times n_{i}
(according to Remark
22
, we can ensure that
n_{i}/2<m_{i}\leqslant n_{i}
), where
n_{i}=\frac{1}{8}\Delta^{2}(q^{3i}-q^{i})
,
k_{i}\geqslant rn_{i}
,
d_{i}\geqslant\delta n_{i}
.

Let us define

\displaystyle\delta:=\min\left(\frac{1-r}{n_{1}},\delta_{0}\frac{1-r}{q^{3}+q}\right),

\displaystyle s:=\min\left(\frac{1-r}{n_{1}},\frac{s_{0}}{2}\right),

and show that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a code with the required parameters.

Fix an arbitrary
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
If
n<n_{1}/(1-r)
, then the code
\mathcal{C}=\mathbb{F}_{2}^{n}
with rate
1
has distance
1>\delta n
. This trivial code is
(\Delta,s)
-locally testable since the distance from any vector to the code is zero. It can be defined by a zero
n\times n
parity-check matrix to satisfy the statement of the theorem.

Next, consider the main case
n\geqslant n_{1}/(1-r)
.
Note that for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
the following holds

n_{i+1}/n_{i}=q\frac{q^{2i+2}-1}{q^{2i}-1}\leq q(q^{2}+1).

Let us put

j:=\max\left\{i\in\mathbb{N}\mid n_{i}\leqslant(1-r)n\right\}.

Then
j\geqslant 1
and
n_{j}\leqslant n(1-r)<n_{j+1}
, which implies

n_{j}\geqslant\frac{n_{j+1}}{q^{3}+q}>\frac{1-r}{q^{3}+q}n.

Let
n=n_{j}t+u
,
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
0\leqslant u<n_{j}
.
Consider the codes
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{C}_{j}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{2}^{t}
and
\mathcal{C}:=\mathcal{C}^{\prime}\oplus\mathbf{0}_{u}
.
Let us find the parameters of the code
\mathcal{C}
.
It is easy to see that the code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
has length
tn_{j}=n-u
, and the code
\mathcal{C}
has length
n
.
For the dimension
k:=\dim\mathcal{C}
, we get

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle=\dim\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=t\dim\mathcal{C}_{j}=tk_{j}\geqslant tn_{j}r=nr-ur

\displaystyle>nr-n(1-r)r=nr^{2}>n(2r-1)=nR.

For the distance we have

d(\mathcal{C})=d(\mathcal{C}^{\prime})=d(\mathcal{C}_{j})\geqslant\delta_{0}n_{j}>\delta_{0}\frac{1-r}{q^{3}+q}n=\delta n.

By Lemma
24
, code
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a
(\Delta,s_{0})
-locally testable code with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix
H^{\prime}
of size
m^{\prime}\times n^{\prime}
,
n^{\prime}/m^{\prime}=n_{j}/m_{j}\in[1,2)
. Therefore, by Lemma
25
, the code
\mathcal{C}
is a
(\Delta,\min(1,\frac{s_{0}}{2}))
-locally testable code with a
\Delta
-limited parity-check matrix.
Thus, we have constructed a code with the required parameters, and the theorem is proved.
∎

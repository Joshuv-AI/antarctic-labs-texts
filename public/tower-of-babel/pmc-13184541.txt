Clinical Images: Bisphosphonate‐associated atypical femoral fracture with contralateral cortical beaking
Authors: Andreina Martinez Paulino, Valentin Marian
Published: 2026

A 77‐year‐old woman was referred to rheumatology for metabolic bone disease evaluation after a low‐energy right femoral shaft fracture. She had received bisphosphonate therapy continuously for more than 15 years for presumed osteoporosis, without a documented drug holiday. Approximately two years earlier, following a ground‐level fall, radiographs demonstrated a displaced right femoral shaft fracture (A), and she underwent intramedullary nailing (G). During recovery, she developed progressive left thigh pain without new trauma. Five months later, at orthopedic follow‐up, a radiograph of the left femur showed lateral cortical beaking (B), and magnetic resonance imaging demonstrated a mild acute stress reaction of the proximal left femoral shaft superimposed on an old, healed lateral cortical stress fracture (C–F). Because of prodromal pain and imaging findings concerning for an incomplete atypical femoral fracture, prophylactic intramedullary fixation of the left femur was performed (G).

This case demonstrates bisphosphonate‐associated atypical femoral fracture with contralateral involvement. Atypical femoral fractures typically occur in the subtrochanteric region or femoral shaft after minimal trauma and are associated with prodromal thigh pain, localized lateral cortical thickening, bilaterality, and delayed healing. 1 , 2 , 3 Recognition of the contralateral lesion likely prevented a second complete fracture. 1 , 2 Clinicians should reassess bisphosphonate therapy after three to five years and obtain femoral imaging when new thigh pain develops, particularly of the contralateral side after an atypical femoral fracture. 2 , 3

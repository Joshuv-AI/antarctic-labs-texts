On the ( m , 1 ) (m,1) -configuration for the HRT Conjecture
Vignon Oussa

Abstract
We prove a linear–independence principle for finite families of translates in irreducible, square–integrable modulo the center (SI/Z) representations of step–two nilpotent Lie groups with one–dimensional center. Fix a one–parameter subgroup exp ⁡ ( ℝ ​ X ) \exp(\mathbb{R}X) and a noncentral element Y Y (not assumed to commute with X X ). For every nonzero smooth vector f f , any finite collection { π ⁡ ( exp ⁡ ( x k ​ X ) ) ​ f } k = 1 m \{\pi(\exp(x_{k}X))f\}_{k=1}^{m} (with distinct parameters x k x_{k} ) of translates along exp ⁡ ( ℝ ​ X ) \exp(\mathbb{R}X) , together with the single translate π ⁡ ( exp ⁡ Y ) ​ f \pi(\exp Y)f , is linearly independent. The proof combines Schwartz regularity of matrix coefficients in the noncentral variables with an ergodic log–product estimate on a compact torus generated by commutator phases. As a result, we obtain an HRT–type statement for ( m , 1 ) (m,1) configurations (on–line direction with zero X k 0 X_{k_{0}} –component and rogue exp ⁡ X k 0 \exp X_{k_{0}} ) for all nonzero Schwartz windows, without spacing restrictions.



1
Motivation and Introduction

The Heil–Ramanathan–Topiwala (HRT) conjecture asserts that for any nonzero window

f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
and any finite set
\Lambda=\{(x_{k},y_{k})\}_{k=1}^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
the time–frequency system

\mathcal{G}(\Lambda,f):=\big\{\pi(x_{k},y_{k})f:1\leq k\leq m\big\}

where
\pi(x,y)=M_{y}T_{x}
,
(M_{y}f)(t)=e^{-2\pi iyt}f(t)
, and
(T_{x}f)(t)=f(t-x)

is linearly independent. The conjecture is corroborated in several important instances.
In particular, for
collinear
configurations (all points lying on a single line in the
time–frequency plane), metaplectic reduction carries the problem to pure translations or
pure modulations, where linear independence is classical; see, for example, the survey

[
11
]
and related developments in
[
14
,
10
]
.

Beyond the collinear case, partial results for two-line patterns are known. The
(2,2)

configuration is settled in
L^{2}
by Demeter–Zaharescu
[
5
]
,
while Demeter proved that for the
(1,3)
configuration the conjecture holds for every
nonzero Schwartz window
[
4
]
. Moreover, Liu established that HRT holds for

almost every

(1,3)
configuration (with respect to Lebesgue measure on the
parameters) for all nonzero
L^{2}
windows
[
13
]
.

Main contribution

Motivated by these advances, we study configurations consisting of
m
points on a line
together with one point off that line; an
(m,1)
pattern in the time–frequency plane. The structural feature making this case
tractable is that the on-line points generate a commuting family of time–frequency operators,
whereas the single off-line point typically fails to commute with that family. By
simultaneously diagonalizing the commuting part, one obtains a functional equation driven by
the rogue shift; and the analysis of such an equation leads to new results for this specific configuration.

Our approach is mostly representation-theoretic. We work on step-two nilpotent Lie groups with one-dimensional center and employ square-integrable-mod-center (SI/Z) representations.
Within this setting, we prove that a finite family consisting of translates along a fixed
one-parameter subgroup, together with one rogue translate, is linearly independent for
every nonzero smooth vector for the representation at hand. As an immediate corollary, in the Schrödinger realization of
the action of the
(2d+1)
-dimensional Heisenberg group on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we obtain the HRT-type
conclusion for
(m,1)
configurations without spacing restrictions (as considered in
[
10
]
, for instance) for Schwartz functions.

Remark 1.1

(Heisenberg equivalence and acknowledgment)
.

It is known that any connected and simply connected two-step nilpotent Lie group with a one-dimensional center is Lie isomorphic to a Heisenberg group (Dixmier,
Enveloping Algebras
, §4.6.1,
[
6
]
). For generality, we have chosen to formulate our results in general Lie-theoretic terms rather than working directly within a fixed Schrödinger realization. The author thanks Ingrid Beltita for the reference and for pointing out some redundancies (subsequently removed) in earlier drafts.

Main theorem

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
N
be a connected, simply connected, step–two nilpotent Lie group with one-dimensional center, and let
\pi
be an irreducible unitary SI/Z (square-integrable modulo the center) representation on a Hilbert space
H_{\pi}
. Fix a basis

\{B_{1}=Z,\ B_{2}=Y_{1},\dots,B_{\ell+1}=Y_{\ell},\ B_{\ell+2}=X\}

adapted to the central series of
\mathfrak{n}=\mathrm{Lie}(N)
, i.e.
\mathfrak{n}(k)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{1},\dots,B_{k}\}
is an ideal in
\mathfrak{n}
for each
k
. Let
Y\in\mathrm{span}\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{\ell}\}
be nonzero and
f\in H_{\pi}^{\infty}
be nonzero (a non trival smooth vector.) Then for any distinct real parameters
x_{1}<\dots<x_{m},
the finite set

\big\{\pi(\exp(x_{k}X))f:1\leq k\leq m\big\}\ \cup\ \big\{\pi(\exp Y)f\big\}

is linearly independent in
H_{\pi}
.

•

.

•

\mathfrak{v}=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{\ell}\}

•

\pi
is an irreducible SI/Z representation with central character

\pi(\exp(zZ))=e^{2\pi i\,z\,\lambda(Z)}\,\mathrm{Id}

Because

3.1
Setup and reduction to noncentral variables

Let
N
be a connected and simply connected step-two nilpotent Lie group with one-dimensional center
Z(N)
and Lie algebra
\mathfrak{n}
. Fix a basis adapted to the central series

\{B_{1}=Z,\ B_{2}=Y_{1},\dots,B_{\ell+1}=Y_{\ell},\ B_{\ell+2}=X\},

meaning
\mathfrak{n}(k)=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{B_{1},\dots,B_{k}\}
is an ideal for each
k

[
2
]
. Set

N_{0}:=\exp(\mathbb{R}Z)\,\exp(\mathbb{R}Y_{1})\cdots\exp(\mathbb{R}Y_{\ell}),\qquad\mathfrak{v}:=\mathrm{span}_{\mathbb{R}}\{Y_{1},\dots,Y_{\ell}\}.

Then the map
(W,t)\mapsto\exp(W)\exp(tX)
is a global diffeomorphism from
(\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{v})\times\mathbb{R}
onto
N
.

Matrix coefficients and partial Fourier transform.

For
f\in H_{\pi}^{\infty}
(smooth for
\pi
), define

\mathcal{V}(W,t):=\langle f,\ \pi(\exp(W)\exp(tX))\,f\rangle_{H_{\pi}},\qquad(W\in\mathfrak{v},\ t\in\mathbb{R}),

(1)

and its partial Fourier transform in
t
,

(F_{X}\mathcal{V})(W,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{V}(W,t)\,e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt.

(2)

In the two-step case one has the standard regularity (see
[
8
,
2
]
) identifying smooth vectors and giving Schwartz decay of matrix coefficients in the noncentral variables.

Proposition 3.1

(Schwartz regularity in noncentral variables)
.

Assume
\mathfrak{n}
is two-step. If
f\in H_{\pi}^{\infty}
, then

\mathcal{V}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v}\times\mathbb{R})\quad\text{and}\quad(F_{X}\mathcal{V})(\cdot,\xi)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v})\ \text{ for each }\xi\in\mathbb{R}.

3.2
Derivation of the key identity

Fix
0\neq Y\in\mathfrak{v}
and suppose there exist distinct
x_{1}<\cdots<x_{m}
and nonzero
c_{k}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,\pi(\exp(x_{k}X))f=\pi(\exp Y)f.

(3)

Testing (
3
) against
\langle f,\pi(g)f\rangle
for arbitrary
g\in N
yields

\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,\langle f,\pi(\exp(-x_{k}X)g)f\rangle_{H_{\pi}}=\langle f,\pi(\exp(-Y)g)f\rangle_{H_{\pi}}.

(4)

Write
g=\exp(W)\exp(tX)
with
W\in\mathfrak{z}\oplus\mathfrak{v}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
. In a two–step algebra one has

e^{-x\mathrm{ad}X}W=W-x[X,W],\qquad\log\!\big(\exp(-Y)\exp(W)\big)=W-Y-\tfrac{1}{2}[Y,W],

(5)

with
[X,W],[Y,W]\in\mathfrak{z}
. Using (
5
), the left side of (
4
) becomes

\langle f,\pi(\exp(W-x_{k}[X,W])\exp((t-x_{k})X))f\rangle.

Step 3: Central phases via the character (signs with conjugate linearity).

By Schur’s lemma,
\pi(\exp Z)=e^{2\pi i\langle\lambda,Z\rangle}\,\mathrm{Id}
for
Z\in\mathfrak{z}
. Because the inner product is conjugate-linear in the second slot, central scalars that act on the second slot exit the inner product
conjugated
. Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}(W-x_{k}[X,W],t-x_{k})

\displaystyle=e^{\,2\pi i\,x_{k}\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle}\,\mathcal{V}(W,t-x_{k}),

\displaystyle\langle f,\pi(\exp(-Y)\exp(W)\exp(tX))f\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{\,2\pi i\,\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,[Y,W]\rangle}\,\mathcal{V}(W-Y,t).

Step 4: Partial Fourier transform in
t
.

Let

V(W,\xi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{V}(W,t)\,e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt\qquad(\xi\in\mathbb{R}).

Multiply (
4
) by
e^{-2\pi it\xi}
and integrate in
t
. On each left–hand term change variables
t\mapsto t-x_{k}
. Using the formulas above gives

\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,e^{-2\pi ix_{k}\xi}\,e^{\,2\pi ix_{k}\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle}\,V(W,\xi)=e^{\,2\pi i\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,[Y,W]\rangle}\,V(W-Y,\xi).

Equivalently,

V(W-Y,\xi)=e^{-2\pi i\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,[Y,W]\rangle}\,\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,e^{-2\pi ix_{k}(\xi+\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle)}\Big)\,V(W,\xi).

(6)

Introduce the shorthand

P(W,\xi):=e^{-2\pi i\tfrac{1}{2}\langle\lambda,[Y,W]\rangle}\,\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,e^{-2\pi ix_{k}(\xi+\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle)}.

(7)

Then the
key identity
takes the compact form

V(W-Y,\xi)=P(W,\xi)\,V(W,\xi).

(8)

Note that only the central commutators
[X,W]
and
[Y,W]
enter
P(W,\xi)
, contributing unimodular phases determined by the central character.

3.3
Contradiction

Iterating (
8
), for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

V(W-nY,\xi)=\Big(\prod_{j=0}^{n-1}P(W-jY,\xi)\Big)\,V(W,\xi).

(9)

A nontrivial Fourier slice.

Since
\mathcal{V}(0,t)=\langle f,\pi(\exp(tX))f\rangle
is Schwartz and
\mathcal{V}(0,0)=\|f\|^{2}>0
, the function
V(0,\xi)
is continuous and nontrivial. Fix
\xi_{\ast}\in\mathbb{R}
with
V(0,\xi_{\ast})\neq 0
.

Compact rotations; reparametrization and
G/H
.

Set

A=\mathrm{diag}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),\quad c=(c_{1},\dots,c_{m})^{\mathsf{T}},\quad\mathbf{1}=(1,\dots,1)^{\mathsf{T}}\in\mathbb{C}^{m}.

Then from (
7

\ln|P(W,\xi)|=\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ e^{\,2\pi i(\,\xi+\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle\,)A}\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|.

(10)

Write

\gamma:=e^{-2\pi i\,\langle\lambda,[X,Y]\rangle A}\in U(m),\qquad H:=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}\ (\text{compact, abelian}),

and let

G:=\overline{\{\gamma^{t}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}}\subseteq\{e^{2\pi itA}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}.

Choose a Borel transversal
K\subset G
for the quotient
G/H
; then every
\gamma^{-\theta}\in G
has a unique factorization

\gamma^{-\theta}=k(\theta)\,s(\theta),\qquad k(\theta)\in K,\ \ s(\theta)\in H.

(11)

For use below, set
g_{(\xi,W)}:=e^{\,2\pi i(\,\xi+\langle\lambda,[X,W]\rangle\,)A}.
Since

lies in the abelian torus
\{e^{2\pi itA}:t\in\mathbb{R}\}
, the elements

g_{(\xi,W)},\gamma,k(\theta),s(\theta)
commute. By two–step nilpotency,
[X,W-jY]=[X,W]-j[X,Y]
, hence from (

),
for

\ln|P(W_{1}+\theta Y-jY,\xi)|=\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,\gamma^{\,j}\gamma^{-\theta}\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|=\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ k(\theta)\,g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,\gamma^{\,j}s(\theta)\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|.

(12)

Inserting (

|V(W_{1}+\theta Y-nY,\xi)|=|V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi)|\,\exp\!\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ k(\theta)\,g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,\gamma^{\,j}s(\theta)\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|\Big).

(13)

Remark 3.2

(Avoiding zeros)
.

Let
\alpha:=\langle\lambda,[X,Y]\rangle
. If
\alpha\neq 0
, then for fixed
j

\big\langle c,\ g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,\gamma^{\,j}\gamma^{-\theta}\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle=\sum_{k=1}^{m}c_{k}\,e^{-2\pi i(\beta-j\alpha+\theta\alpha)x_{k}}\quad(\beta:=\xi+\langle\lambda,[X,W_{1}]\rangle)

is a nontrivial finite exponential sum in
\theta
, whose zero set is discrete. Hence one can choose
\theta
so that none of the logarithms in (
13
) are singular.

Degenerate case
\alpha=\langle\lambda,[X,Y]\rangle=0
.

Then
\gamma=I
and
P(W-jY,\xi)\equiv P(W,\xi)
is independent of
j
. Pick
W,\xi
with

V(W,\xi)\neq 0
and
P(W,\xi)\neq 0
(possible since the trigonometric polynomial
\sum_{k}c_{k}e^{-2\pi ix_{k}s}
is not identically zero and
V(0,\cdot)
is continuous and nontrivial). From (

|V(W-nY,\xi)|=|V(W,\xi)|\,|P(W,\xi)|^{\,n}\xrightarrow[n\to\infty]{}0,

forcing
|P(W,\xi)|<1
. Forward iteration gives

|V(W+nY,\xi)|=|V(W,\xi)|\,|P(W,\xi)|^{-n}\to\infty
, a contradiction to the Schwartz decay below. Thus the degenerate case cannot occur.

Lemma 3.3

(Schwartz decay along rays)
.

For each fixed
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
and each nonzero
Y
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}|V(W\pm nY,\xi)|=0
for every
W\in\mathfrak{v}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,
V(\cdot,\xi)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathfrak{v})
.
∎

Ergodic average on
H
and the
G/H
factor.

Fix
k\in K
and define the
L^{1}
cocycle on
H
,

F_{k}(h):=\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ k\,g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,h\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|,\qquad h\in H,

which belongs to
L^{1}(H,\mu_{H})
(Łojasiewicz on compact analytic groups; cf.
[
3
, Ch. III, §2, (2.15)]
. See also
[
12
]
.).
Let
T:H\to H
,
T(h)=\gamma h
. Since
H=\overline{\langle\gamma\rangle}
,
(H,\mu_{H},T)
is ergodic; hence, for
\mu_{H}
–a.e.
s\in H
,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F_{k}(\gamma^{\,j}s)\ \longrightarrow\ \int_{H}F_{k}(h)\,d\mu_{H}(h)=:C_{k}(\xi,W_{1}).

(14)

By right–invariance of
\mu_{H}
,

\int_{H}\ln\Big|\big\langle c,\ k\,g_{(\xi,W_{1})}\,h\,s\,\mathbf{1}\big\rangle\Big|\,d\mu_{H}(h)=C_{k}(\xi,W_{1}),

so (
14
) holds for
\mu_{H}
–a.e.
s
and all
k\in K
; consequently it holds for
\mu_{H}
–a.e. product
ks\in G
.

Choosing
\theta
.

Since
\theta\mapsto\gamma^{-\theta}=k(\theta)s(\theta)
ranges densely in
G
, we may choose
\theta
such that (i)
V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})\neq 0
(by continuity), (ii) the logarithms in (
13
) are finite (Remark
3.2
), and (iii)
s:=s(\theta)
belongs to the Birkhoff good set in (
14
). Fix such a
\theta
and write
k:=k(\theta)
. Define the partial sums

S_{n}^{-}:=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F_{k}(\gamma^{\,j}s),\qquad S_{n}^{+}:=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F_{k}(\gamma^{-j}s).

Then (
13
) and the forward iteration of (
8
) give

|V(W_{1}+\theta Y-nY,\xi_{\ast})|=|V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\,e^{\,S_{n}^{-}},\qquad|V(W_{1}+\theta Y+nY,\xi_{\ast})|=|V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\,e^{\,-S_{n}^{+}},

and by (

\frac{S_{n}^{-}}{n}\to C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1}),\qquad\frac{S_{n}^{+}}{n}\to C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1}).

Excluding all signs of the ergodic mean.

Case 1:
C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})>0
.
Then
S_{n}^{-}\geq\tfrac{n}{2}C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})
for all large

and

|V(W_{1}+\theta Y-nY,\xi_{\ast})|\ \geq\ |V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\,e^{\,\frac{n}{2}C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})}\ \to\ \infty,

contradicting Lemma
3.3
.

Case 2:
C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})<0
.
Then
S_{n}^{+}\leq-\tfrac{n}{2}|C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})|
for all large
n
, so

|V(W_{1}+\theta Y+nY,\xi_{\ast})|\ \geq\ |V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\,e^{\,\frac{n}{2}|C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})|}\ \to\ \infty,

again contradicting Lemma
3.3
.

Case 3:
C_{k}(\xi_{\ast},W_{1})=0
.

By the Atkinson–Sato zero–mean recurrence principle

[
1
,
15
]
, for
\mu_{H}
–a.e.
s\in H
,

\liminf_{n\to\infty}\Bigg|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}F_{k}\!\big(\gamma^{\,j}s\big)\Bigg|=0.

Hence there exists a subsequence
n_{r}\to\infty
with

|S^{-}_{n_{r}}|=\Big|\sum_{j=0}^{n_{r}-1}F_{k}(\gamma^{\,j}s)\Big|\to 0.

In particular
S^{-}_{n_{r}}\to 0
, so
e^{S^{-}_{n_{r}}}\to 1
.
Using the product formula, we obtain

|V(W_{1}+\theta Y-n_{r}Y,\xi_{\ast})|=|V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\,e^{S^{-}_{n_{r}}}\ \longrightarrow\ |V(W_{1}+\theta Y,\xi_{\ast})|\ (>0),

which contradicts the Schwartz decay along rays.

All three alternatives are impossible; therefore the assumed linear dependence (
3
) cannot occur.

Remark 3.5

(Concluding remark)
.

We prove, for two–step nilpotent Lie groups with one-dimensional center and SI/Z representations, that for every nonzero smooth vector
f
any
m
translates along a fixed one–parameter subgroup together with one (non)-commuting translate are linearly independent. Via identification to the Heisenberg group, this yields the
(m,1)
statement in the Schrödinger model on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all nonzero Schwartz windows, with no spacing assumptions. Writing

\mathcal{V}(W,t)=\langle f,\pi(\exp(W)\exp(tX))f\rangle,\qquad V(W,\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{V}(W,t)\,e^{-2\pi it\xi}\,dt,

one derives the one–step relation

V(W-Y,\xi)=P(W,\xi)\,V(W,\xi),

where
P
is a trigonometric polynomial built from the central commutator phases
[X,W]
and
[Y,W]
. Iteration along the
Y
–ray yields a logarithmic product; averaging
\log|P|
over the compact abelian group generated by these phases (ergodic theorem, with
\log|P|\in L^{1}
on the torus by a Łojasiewicz–type inequality; see
[
3
, Ch. III, §2, (2.15)]
, cf.
[
12
]
) produces an ergodic mean whose sign contradicts the decay of
W\mapsto V(W,\xi)
along
\pm nY
. The zero–mean alternative is excluded by the Atkinson–Sato recurrence principle
[
1
,
15
]
.

Window hypothesis (Schwartz and
S_{0}
).

If
f\in H_{\pi}^{\infty}
(e.g.
f\in\mathcal{S}
in the Schrödinger model), then for each fixed
\xi
one has
V(\cdot,\xi)\in\mathcal{S}
in the noncentral variables; in particular
V(W\pm nY,\xi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, so the product/ergodic argument runs pointwise in
\xi
. In the Schrödinger realization, for
f\in S_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(Feichtinger’s algebra, the modulation space
M^{1,1}
) we have the identity

\mathcal{V}(y,x)=\langle f,\,M_{\lambda y}T_{x}f\rangle=V_{f}f(x,\lambda y),

the short–time Fourier transform (STFT) of
f
with window
f
. Since
f\in S_{0}
and
S_{0}
is a Banach space invariant under time–frequency shifts, it follows that
V_{f}f\in C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
(see Feichtinger
[
7
]
and Gröchenig
[
9
, Chs. 11–12]
). Taking the
partial Fourier transform in the singled–out
x_{k_{0}}
–variable
preserves
S_{0}
on
\mathbb{R}^{2d},
hence

(W,\xi)\longmapsto V(W,\xi)

belongs to
S_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})\subset C_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{2d})
. Therefore, for each fixed
\xi
, the map
W\mapsto V(W,\xi)
is continuous and vanishes at infinity; in particular
V(W\pm nY,\xi)\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Consequently, the product/ergodic contradiction carries over verbatim to
S_{0}
windows.

General
L^{2}
windows.

For
f\in L^{2}
, the multiplicative identity above holds for a.e.
\xi
, and the raw matrix coefficients decay along noncentral rays; however, after the partial transform the pointwise ray–decay of
V(\cdot,\xi)
at a fixed frequency may fail. A full
L^{2}
extension appears to require additional ideas beyond the present method.

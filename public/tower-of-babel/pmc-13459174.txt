Research on influenza surveillance and a prediction model based on multi-source data
Authors: Wei Duan, Lizhong Duan, Xiuhong Yang, Lijuan Zhao, Kai He, Jiatian Yang, Daiwen Cun, Abeer Teeti, Dongsheng Huang, Xiaoqing Fu, Xiaowen Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo develop and validate a multivariate Long Short-Term Memory (LSTM) model that integrates multi-source surveillance data for forecasting influenza activity. This study aimed to identify the most predictive variables and establish an optimized data fusion framework to enhance public health surveillance.MethodsWe collected influenza case data, influenza-like illness (ILI) reports, and symptom monitoring data, along with corresponding meteorological data and Baidu Index data in Baoshan city from January 2022 to June 2025. Spearman correlation analysis was used to verify the relationship between each dataset and influenza case numbers. Furthermore, the SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) was employed to quantify feature importance. A LSTM model was constructed for predictive research, to identify in the optimal multi-source dataset. The prediction model based on this optimal dataset utilized the moving percentile method to determine the best early warning threshold.ResultsInfluenza activity in Baoshan City exhibited distinct seasonality, with outbreaks peaking in winter and spring. ILI reports demonstrated the strongest correlation with confirmed cases (rs = 0.56, p < 0.001). Among 43 Baidu Index keywords, four, including “H1N1 flu symptoms”(S2) showed higher correlations (rs > 0.40, p < 0.001), whereas meteorological and symptom surveillance data were weak correlation. In predictive modeling, the LSTM achieved peak performance using ILI data alone (test set R2 = 0.79, MSE = 24.82, MAE = 3.14). Moreover, refined feature selection consistently outperformed models using full feature sets. The combination of ILI and the Baidu keyword “Is the Flu Shot Necessary?(P2)” provided the balanced and practical model (R2 = 0.79, MSE = 25.15, MAE = 3.18), matching the accuracy of the standalone ILI model. Based on the LSTM model for ILI predictions, the optimal early warning threshold was determined as P70, with the optimal threshold value being 8.10.ConclusionsThis study demonstrates that a strategically simplified LSTM model, leveraging refined multi-source data, can achieve high accuracy and robustness, providing solutions for public health surveillance scenarios. The threshold value of influenza epidemic warning in Baoshan city demonstrates reasonable sensitivity and specificity, and can be recommended as an early warning index of the influenza epidemic in Baoshan city.Clinical trial numberNot applicable.

Background

Influenza remains one of the most closely monitored infectious diseases worldwide [1], owing to its high transmissibility, significant pathogenicity, and substantial disease burden, it continues to pose a persistent global public health challenge. Historical pandemics have claimed tens of millions of lives, and seasonal epidemics continue to exert a heavy toll. The World Health Organization (WHO) estimates 3–5 million cases of severe illness and 290,000–650,000 respiratory deaths annually [2]. However, this burden is not distributed equally. Children under five years of age, particularly in developing countries, experience disproportionately high mortality, with an estimated 90 million cases in this age group each year [3]. In China, childhood infection rates during epidemic seasons range from 20% to 30% and can surge to 50% during peak activity [4]. Furthermore, pregnant women also represent a group at greatest risk of influenza mortality; and in addition to maternal illness, influenza can cause complications such as stillbirth, preterm birth and birth weight loss [5]. Given this significant and ongoing impact, robust surveillance is the cornerstone of effective influenza prevention and control. Accurate, timely forecasting of influenza activity is essential to guide vaccination campaigns, optimize resource allocation, and mount effective public health responses.

Infectious disease surveillance, a core component of public health surveillance framework, is indispensable for preventing and controlling outbreaks and safeguarding population health [6]. In China, the China Influenza Surveillance Network represents a critical element of the nation’s early warning system for emerging infectious diseases. It has established strong technical and professional capacities for detecting novel pathogens and supports comprehensive efforts in disease prevention, control, prediction, and early warning both nationally and internationally [7]. However, the transmission dynamics of influenza are influenced by a complex array of factors, including climate, population immunity, and human mobility [8]. Meanwhile, relying solely on traditional notifiable disease reports, while ignoring these influential variables, often results in surveillance delays and a failure to detect early signs of outbreaks. Consequently, multi-source surveillance has emerged as a superior approach, with numerous studies demonstrating its significant advantage in improving the accuracy and timeliness of disease prediction and early warning [9].

The effective utilization of internet-based health data holds significant potential for enhancing the early warning and surveillance of infectious diseases [10]. Ginsberg et al. were the first to develop an influenza prediction model using Google search data, which demonstrated the ability to forecast epidemic trends one to two weeks in advance [11]. Similarly, Lampos et al. analyzed temporal patterns in web search queries to estimate COVID-19 prevalence across multiple countries and regions [12]. In another study, Yang et al. integrated Baidu search query data with traditional virological surveillance to construct an influenza outbreak prediction model. Their findings revealed a strong correlation between specific Baidu search terms and influenza positive rates in both northern and southern China. Notably, search data from southern China were capable of predicting influenza activity approximately 14 to 22 days ahead of official reports [9]. Thus, environmental factors such as humidity, air temperature, and light intensity have also been identified as significant predictors of influenza activity [13]. The relationship is quantifiable; for example, one study reported that a 1 °C increase in temperature was associated with a 1.1% decrease in the influenza infection rate, while a 10% drop in relative humidity corresponded to a 1.6% increase [14]. These findings are supported by other research, such as the work of Du et al., which confirmed correlations between influenza cases and specific meteorological variables [15]. Moving from correlation to prediction, Zheng et al. demonstrated that incorporating these environmental indicators as exogenous variables in an AutoRegressive Integrated Moving Average with eXogenous inputs(ARIMAX) model could substantially improve the accuracy of influenza incidence forecasts [16]. This progression from identifying associations to successfully integrating meteorological data into forecasting models provides a strong justification for including these variables in our own predictive analysis. Moreover, globalization, characterized by accelerated air travel, increased population mobility, and complex international trade networks, has concurrently facilitated the cross-regional spread of infectious diseases [17]. The 2009 H1N1 influenza pandemic [18], the 2012 Middle East respiratory syndrome (MERS) outbreak [19], and the COVID-19 pandemic in 2020 [20], starkly illustrate the capacity of pathogens for rapid global dissemination with significant, albeit varying, worldwide impacts. These recurring threats underscore the critical imperative for robust surveillance, early warning systems, and proactive prevention strategies to mitigate cross-border transmission.

This global context is of particular concern for border regions, which act as frontlines for infectious disease importation. Tengchong, a key border port in Yunnan Province, China, adjacent to Myanmar, holds a non-negligible position in the cross-regional transmission dynamics of influenza and faces a high risk of imported infectious diseases. This risk is substantiated by research from Wang et al., which demonstrated that the incidence of imported influenza cases directly correlates with rising outbreak activity in source countries [21]. The timely prediction of infectious disease outbreaks represents a critical objective within public health surveillance, enabling proactive rather than reactive responses [22]. For influenza specifically, accurate forecasts empower public health authorities to design and deploy targeted containment strategies, optimize the allocation of medical resources and personnel, and ultimately mitigate community transmission.

Achieving accurate prediction and early warning for influenza requires advanced modeling techniques capable of capturing the complex temporal dynamics of epidemic data. The LSTM network, a specialized recurrent neural network architecture proposed by Hochreiter & Schmidhuber in 1997, is uniquely suited for this task due to its ability to learn long-term dependencies in sequential data [23]. This characteristic is critical, as influenza incidence data exhibits strong autocorrelation, seasonality, and a transmission dynamic where recent cases are the primary drivers of future infections, while older cases lose their influence [24]. Unlike traditional statistical time-series models, which often require manual feature engineering and strict assumptions about data stationarity, LSTM models can automatically learn these complex, non-linear temporal patterns through their internal gating mechanisms and memory cells. The empirical superiority of LSTM in epidemiological forecasting has been demonstrated in multiple studies. Its application has yielded high-performance predictions for ILI and was notably effective in modeling the trajectory of the COVID-19 pandemic, especially when integrating diverse data streams [25–27]. Given its proven capability to handle the specific characteristics of infectious disease time series and its successful track record with multi-source data, we adopted the LSTM model as the core predictive framework for this study.

In conclusion, there are numerous factors affecting influenza activity, and future influenza trends can be predicted by using different surveillance data and methods. Accurate predictions of influenza incidence and understanding the factors influencing it are crucial for public health interventions. Therefore, this study integrates Internet data, environmental data, symptom monitoring data and ILI to enhance the correlation between the data, through the complementary multi-source data to further improve the accuracy and timeliness of influenza prediction, and find the best predictor of influenza prediction will greatly improve the efficiency of influenza monitoring, promote the formation of effective influenza monitoring and prediction systems, and improve influenza prevention and control effects. This study aims to address this gap by integrating multi-source digital and traditional data streams within an advanced modeling framework to enhance the accuracy and timeliness of influenza forecasting.

Data segmentation

The dataset was partitioned chronologically into training and testing subsets to maintain temporal integrity. The initial 80% of the time series data served as the training set for model development, while the most recent 20% was reserved as the test set for evaluating model performance and generalization capability.

Feature selection strategy

A two-stage feature selection strategy was adopted. First, Spearman correlation analysis was performed to identify variables significantly associated with influenza cases, serving as an initial filtering step. To reduce redundancy among predictors, pairwise correlations between retained features were examined. When two variables exhibited high collinearity (|rs| >0.8), only one representative variable was retained based on epidemiological interpretability and data completeness. To further capture nonlinear effects and feature interactions, feature importance was quantified using SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP) within the LSTM model, capturing nonlinear effects and feature interactions. Features with negligible SHAP values were considered to have minimal impact on prediction and were excluded. In contrast, features with consistently high SHAP values were retained as key predictors. This approach combines statistical relevance with model-derived contributions to identify informative predictors.

Construction of prediction model based on LSTM model

A multi-feature time-series prediction model was developed using a LSTM network. By extracting inherent patterns from multi-source data and establishing correlations between features and outcomes, this model enables scientific and robust predictions of time series trends. To prevent overfitting given the limited dataset size, we implemented several regularization techniques. The final model configuration consisted of a single LSTM layer with 16 hidden units, using Adam optimization (learning rate = 0.001) with a 7-day lookback window and batch size of 32 over 50 training epochs. We incorporated Early Stopping and Reduce LR On Plateau callbacks to monitor validation loss, enabling adaptive learning rate reduction and premature training termination when performance plateaued. Additionally, L2 regularization (λ=0.01) and Dropout (rate = 0.5) were applied to enhance model generalization capability.

Baseline models for comparison

To ensure a fair evaluation of the proposed LSTM-based forecasting framework, we benchmarked its performance against three representative baseline models: ARIMA, Random Forest (RF), and XGBoost.

ARIMA serves as a traditional statistical time-series model utilizing only historical case data. RF and XGBoost represent modern machine learning approaches capable of modeling nonlinear relationships. To ensure consistency with the temporal learning structure of LSTM, lagged features of both the target variable (influenza case) and exogenous variable (influenza-like cases, ILI) were explicitly constructed for RF and XGBoost.

Temporal robustness analysis

To assess whether the predictive performance of the LSTM model is sensitive to the amount of training data or the specific training period, we performed a rolling‑window experiment. The total available dataset (after sliding window construction) was fixed. We gradually increased the proportion of the data used for training from 50% to 75% in 5% increments, while keeping the test set fixed as the last 20% of the time series. For each training proportion, we retrained the LSTM model using the same architecture and hyperparameters, and evaluated its R2 on the identical test set. This procedure allowed us to examine the stability of the model’s performance across different training window sizes.

Establishment of early warning thresholds

Early warning thresholds were dynamically determined using a moving percentile method based on the predictions from LSTM model. The window size is set as w = 52 (weeks). A sliding window method with a fixed window size is adopted: when i < w, all available historical data [0, i] are used; when i ≥ w, the data of the most recent w time points [i-w+1,i] are used. Mathematical expression: \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$W{\rm{in}}do{w_i} = \left\{ \matrix{ data\left[ {0:i + 1} \right]{\rm{ if }}i < w \hfill \cr data\left[ {i - w + 1:i + 1} \right]{\rm{ if }}i > w \hfill \cr} \right.$$\end{document}

Percentile thresholds (P60, P65, P70, P75, P80, P85, P90, P95) were calculated as candidate warning levels. The performance of each threshold was evaluated based on sensitivity, specificity, positive predictive value (PPV), negative predictive value (NPV), and the Youden Index. The threshold maximizing the Youden Index was selected as the optimal early warning threshold.

The above analysis used Excel (Office 2021) software for data organization and descriptive statistical analysis; Spearman correlation analysis was performed using PyCharm 2024.1.4 software; and the LSTM model was built and the optimal warning threshold was selected based on the mobile percentile method using the Python 3.10.11 and PyTorch 2.5.1 deep learning framework.

As shown in Fig. 5, the test R2 of the LSTM model increased from −0.55 (50% training proportion) to 0.78 (75% training proportion). Discussion

The prevalence and transmission of influenza are influenced by a complex interplay of multiple factors. This study therefore developed and compared LSTM models for influenza prediction by ILI surveillance data, Baidu Index, meteorological data, and symptom monitoring data. The aim was to explore the application value of different data sources and feature combinations in influenza prediction and provide an empirical basis for optimizing the infectious disease early warning systems. However, the descriptive analysis indicated that influenza epidemics in Baoshan city exhibited a clear seasonal pattern, with incidence peaks typically occurring during the winter and spring months (December to April of the following year). This finding aligns with previously reported epidemic trends in most cities across China [28, 29].

The results of the Spearman correlation analysis revealed a significant moderate positive correlation between ILI and influenza case count (rs = 0.56, p < 0.001). Influenza-like illness surveillance has long served as a routine monitoring tool for influenza, playing a crucial role in detecting influenza outbreaks. Furthermore, as a primary pathogen causing acute respiratory infections, influenza exhibits a relatively high detection rate among influenza-like illness cases [30], which explains the observed strength of correlation. Previous studies have shown that when individuals experience suspected flu symptoms, their first instinct is often to search online for symptom information, treatment methods, or medication details [31]. Consequently, the rise in search volume for these keywords can be regarded as an early, forward-looking indicator of flu outbreak transmission within communities. The analysis of the correlation between influenza cases and Baidu Index keywords in this study revealed that high-correlation keywords primarily included H1N1 Influenza (N3), Oseltamivir (T5), Oseltamivir + Tamiflu (T17), and Influenza A symptoms (S2). This indicated that the public demonstrates high awareness of Influenza A and seeks targeted information. Conversely, generic terms like cold medicine (T1) and cough (S6) showed low correlation, suggesting excessive background noise in the data. The correlation coefficients between meteorological data and influenza cases were all below 0.2, indicating weak associations. Temperature, humidity and other factors traditionally considered to be related to influenza showed weak correlation in this study [32, 33], which may be related to local climatic conditions. Baoshan city maintains a comfortable annual average temperature, with neither harsh winters nor scorching summers. An interesting pattern also emerged from the correlation analysis between symptom monitoring data and influenza case counts. Persistent symptoms specifically cough lasting for three days and fever lasting for three days were positively correlated with influenza incidence, whereas immediate symptoms (cough, fever) were negatively correlated. This finding likely reflects the nonspecific nature of isolated symptoms, which may result from common colds, bacterial infections, or allergies. In contrast, symptoms persisting for several days serve as stronger clinical indicators, prompting individuals to recognize that their illness may not be self-limiting and to seek medical attention for possible influenza infection.

This study developed and compared LSTM models for influenza forecasting by integrating multi-source data. The model prediction results indicated varying predictive capabilities across different data sources, with LSTM models constructed using ILI date demonstrating the most outstanding performance. The ILI-based LSTM model achieved the highest performance on the test set (R2 = 0.79, MAE = 3.14), outperforming models constructed from other data categories. This result strongly validates the core value of influenza-like illness surveillance within influenza forecasting and monitoring systems: As a typical syndrome-based surveillance method, influenza-like illness monitoring advances the early warning window to before confirmed cases. By integrating the dynamics of ILI patient visits with pathogen detection results, it not only accurately depicts influenza epidemic trends and viral mutation patterns but also provides critical decision support for real-time assessment and early warning of influenza epidemics [34].

The analysis of model predictions based on Baidu Index reveals a key paradigm: “Less is more.” The model using all 43 keywords exhibited severe overfitting (test set R2 of only 0.43), suggesting that incorporating a large number of features may introduce noise and redundancy rather than useful information. In contrast, models based on reduced feature sets exhibited improved generalization ability. The progressive increase in test performance with stricter correlation coefficent indicates that removing weakly correlated or redundant variables enhances model robustness. Notably, a small subset of features was sufficient to achieve strong predictive performance, highlighting the presence of dominant signals in the data. The single keyword “Is the Flu Shot Necessary?(P2)” achieved the second-best prediction performance (test set R2 = 0.79, MSE = 25.76, MAE = 3.36), just behind ILI. This validated the machine learning principle that “refined, effective features outperform feature accumulation” [35]: redundant features introduce noise and increase the risk of model overfitting.

The observation that single-feature models (e.g., P2, S2, and N3) performed comparably to multi-feature models suggests that certain variables may act as strong proxies for influenza activity. Baidu Index data for keywords serve as a low-cost, real-time supplement to traditional infectious disease surveillance systems, providing diversified data support for early warning of influenza epidemic trends. This has practical implications for surveillance, as simpler models may be more stable and easier to implement in real-world settings where data availability is limited. However, it should be noted that correlation-based feature selection captures only marginal associations and does not account for nonlinear relationships or interactions among variables. Therefore, it was used as an initial screening step, and the final model performance was validated using machine learning approaches capable of capturing more complex patterns.

The prediction results of the combination prediction factors show that the two-factor combination (P2+LILI) performed optimally (test set R2 = 0.79, MSE = 25.15, MAE = 3.18). Although it did not surpass the single ILI model, it still demonstrated unique application value: its predictive performance was highly comparable to the single ILI model and significantly outperformed the combination including rhinobyon (ILI+rhinobyon, R2 = 0.70). ILI provides direct monitoring signals of disease prevalence, while keyword “Is the Flu Shot Necessary?(P2)” reflects Public demand trends for diseases. These two features offer complementary information dimensions with no significant redundancy, thus maintaining high predictive accuracy. The findings of this study diverge somewhat from those of previous studies yet consistently affirm the practical value of the combined model [36–38]: although its predictive accuracy is slightly lower than that of the single ILI model, the Baidu Index data offer low acquisition costs and strong real-time capabilities. In scenarios where ILI monitoring data is temporarily unavailable or delayed, it can serve as an effective substitute or supplement, enhancing the robustness of early warning systems. In contrast, the model incorporating the symptom indicator “rhinobyon” failed to improve predictive performance, as its marginal information gain was insufficient to offset added noise. Future studies should therefore emphasize screening symptom indicators more closely linked to influenza dynamics and optimizing feature fusion strategies to maximize complementary information while minimizing redundancy.

Our research determined the optimal early warning boundary value to be P70 based on the moving percentile method, with an early warning threshold of 8.10. Under this boundary value, the Youden index can reach 0.938, demonstrating good predictive and early warning performance. However, the results differ from those determined by other regions in China.Li et al. applied the same method to identify optimal thresholds for three infectious diseases in Hebi City, Henan Province: other infectious diarrhea, influenza, and hand-foot-mouth disease, with thresholds set at P95, P95, and P85 respectively [39]. Similarly, Cha et al. established an early automatic warning model using the moving percentile method, identifying optimal warning thresholds of P90 for hand-foot-mouth disease, P90 for influenza, P85 for mumps, P70–P75 for other infectious diarrhea diseases, and P95 for chickenpox in Ma’anshan City [40]. Yi et al. applied the moving percentile method to develop a warning model for acute respiratory infection syndromes, determining the optimal warning threshold as P85 [41]. The optimal threshold for the same infectious disease can vary across different regions and time periods. Therefore, warning boundaries and thresholds need to be updated regularly according to the epidemiological characteristics of local infectious diseases and available surveillance data.

Although the P70 threshold was identified based on the Youden index, it also has a clear practical interpretation in the context of public health surveillance. In this study, the P70 level corresponds to a point at which influenza activity exceeds its typical seasonal range, indicating a transition toward elevated transmission risk. From an operational perspective, exceedance of this threshold may serve as an early signal for public health response. For example, it could prompt closer monitoring of outpatient visits, expansion of laboratory testing, and early communication with healthcare providers. At the same time, it may support preparatory actions within healthcare systems, such as resource allocation and staffing adjustments, as well as community-level measures including vaccination promotion and risk communication.

The temporal robustness analysis provides insight into the model’s sensitivity to the training period. The negative R2 (−0.55) observed when training on only the earliest 50% of data suggests that the early time period may have different epidemiological characteristics (e.g., lower influenza activity, different reporting practices, or absence of certain external data sources such as Baidu Index) compared to later years. This phenomenon, sometimes referred to as concept drift, is common in long‑term infectious disease surveillance. Encouragingly, once the training window included more recent data (≥55% training proportion), the model consistently achieved positive R2 values (0.62–0.78), and the performance plateaued at a high level (R2 ≈ 0.78) when 70–75% of the data were used. These results indicate that while the model is not immune to changes in data distribution over time, it can achieve stable and accurate predictions if trained on a sufficiently long and recent historical window. Nevertheless, we acknowledge that this analysis does not replace external geographic validation. The model’s applicability to other regions with different climatic, demographic, or healthcare system characteristics remains to be established.

Our model was developed and validated exclusively on data from Baoshan City, Yunnan Province. Although we performed temporal robustness analysis to demonstrate stability over different time periods within the same region, the model’s applicability to other geographical areas with different climatic conditions, population demographics, healthcare infrastructure, or influenza surveillance systems remains unknown. For instance, regions with tropical climates may exhibit different seasonal patterns, and areas with higher population density or different internet search behaviors might alter the predictive value of Baidu Index features. Therefore, external validation using multi-city or multi-country datasets is a critical next step before the model can be recommended for broader public health use. Future work should prioritize cross-regional validation and, if necessary, model re-calibration or transfer learning.

Limitation

This study has several limitations that should be acknowledged. The model was developed and validated using data from a single city (Baoshan, China), which may limit its generalizability to other geographic and epidemiological settings. External validation across regions with different demographic structures, climatic conditions, and healthcare practices is therefore necessary before broader application. In addition, the study period (2022–2025) partially overlaps with the late phase of the COVID-19 pandemic. During this time, non-pharmaceutical interventions and changes in healthcare-seeking behavior may have influenced both influenza transmission dynamics and online search activity. As a result, the stability of the observed relationships in a fully post-pandemic context remains uncertain and warrants further investigation. Moreover, the selected feature set,as well as the derived P70 warning threshold (8.10), is likely context-specific. These parameters may not be directly transferable to other regions or time periods without recalibration. Similarly, although the P70 threshold was determined using the Youden index, its retrospective optimization highlights the need for prospective evaluation in real-time surveillance systems to assess operational robustness. Finally, the inclusion of symptom surveillance data only from 2023 onward reduced the temporal coverage of this modality and may have limited its contribution to long-term pattern learning. Despite these limitations, the findings support the potential of simplified LSTM-based frameworks for influenza forecasting using multi-source digital and surveillance data. Future work should focus on external and multi-regional validation, incorporation of longer and more stable post-pandemic datasets, and adaptive thresholding strategies that can dynamically adjust to evolving epidemiological conditions. In addition, expanding symptom surveillance coverage and integrating more heterogeneous data sources may further improve model robustness and operational utility in real-world public health early warning systems.

Conclusions

Our findings suggest that influenza-like illness(ILI) data can serve as the cornerstone of accurate influenza prediction. The Baidu Index for keyword “Is the Flu Shot Necessary?(P2)” and “H1N1 symptoms”(S2), as well as meteorological indicators “sea level atmospheric pressure (P)” serve as valuable complementary predictors. This study demonstrates that a strategically simplified LSTM model, leveraging refined multi-source data, can achieve high accuracy and robustness, providing solutions for public health surveillance scenarios. In summary, while multi-source data can support accurate influenza prediction, model performance is strongly influenced by temporal factors and feature selection strategy. Nevertheless, based on the optimal model identified herein, we recommend a warning threshold of the 70th percentile (P70, corresponding to a warning value of 8.10) for influenza epidemics in Baoshan city, which demonstrates reasonable sensitivity and specificity and can be adopted as an early warning indicator.

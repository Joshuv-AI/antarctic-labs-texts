Assessment of Vitamin D Deficiency and its Effect on Maternal and Fetal Outcome among Antenatal Females Registered in Urban Health and Training Centre Ratlam
Authors: Nishi Mishra, Ambuj K. Soni, Rajkishore Singh, Nitish Adawadkar, Rohit Dubey
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background:Vitamin D is a fat-soluble vitamin involved in the regulation of calcium homeostasis and thus is essential for a healthy skeletal system. Low maternal vitamin D levels during pregnancy may be responsible for reduced fetal growth and may adversely affect neonatal development.Methodology:A cross-sectional study was conducted in 210 antenatal females registered at UHTC in four-month duration. The blood samples were tested for vitamin D levels using fluorescence immunoassay apart from routine ANC profile after obtaining prior consent from those women who were willing to participate. The demographic details, their dietary habits, and knowledge about vitamin D and its importance were recorded through a questionnaire. Their follow-up was performed till the delivery.Results:A total of 130 (61.9%) pregnant women had insufficient vitamin D levels (12–20 ng/mL). Fifty-six (26.6%) pregnant women were having vitamin D levels (20 ng/mL). The mean vitamin D (ng/mL) level in pregnant women was 14.3 ± 5.2 ng/mL. Fifty-six out 210 had LSCS as the mode of delivery, and 154 had vaginal delivery.Conclusion:There was a deficiency of vitamin D in most of the ANC mothers registered in UHTC. Regular adequate supplementation of vitamin D is needed during pregnancy. Lack of awareness is a major risk factor associated with vitamin D insufficiency.

INTRODUCTION

Vitamin D is a fat-soluble vitamin involved in the regulation of calcium homeostasis. It is essential for good skeletal health, immune system, and brain development of fetus. Maternal vitamin D deficiency is a common public health problem. Staggering rates of poor vitamin D are found among pregnant mothers. Level less than 12 ng/ml deficient, 12–20 ng/ml insufficient, and >20 ng/ml is considered optimal. Maternal nutritional status plays a vital role in creating an optimum intrauterine environment and could be linked to various maternal and perinatal outcomes.[1] Low maternal vitamin D levels during pregnancy might be responsible for reduced fetal growth.[2] It is also associated with negative maternal outcomes such as the onset of preeclampsia, gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM), and an elevated risk of cesarean delivery.[3] There is a significant prevalence of vitamin D insufficiency during pregnancy. In this prospective study, we aimed to establish the relationship between low maternal vitamin D levels and conditions such as intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) as well as pregnancy outcomes including stillbirth, low birth weight (LBW), and admissions to the neonatal intensive care unit (NICU).[4] The findings of this study may provide insights for vitamin D supplementation during the antenatal period to mitigate perinatal morbidity and mortality within our community.

METHODOLOGY

Study Design—Cross-sectional observational study

Study area—Urban Health and Training Centre Ratlam

Study population—Pregnant women registered in Urban Health and Training Centre Ratlam

Study duration—January 1st, 2023–April 30th, 2023

Sampling size—210 Pregnant women

Sampling method—Consecutive sampling

Inclusion criteria—Pregnant women willing to participate after prior written consent

Exclusion criteria—Pregnant women with established comorbid conditions such as essential hypertension, diabetes, thyroid disorders, renal diseases, or any other medical conditions, as well as those with multiple pregnancies or a history of intrauterine growth restriction (IUGR) in prior pregnancies were not included in the study.

Procedure planned

Mothers coming for their routine ANC checkup were informed properly about the study and its purpose in Hindi/local language. After getting their prior consent, the blood sample was tested for vitamin D levels apart from routine investigations. In a detailed history regarding dietary intake (24-hour recall method) and vitamin D supplements intake (over the last three months), past and present medical history was collected by administering the relevant questionnaires. The study participants were followed till their delivery.

RESULTS

Antenatal profile of the study population

Seventy-two (34.3%) pregnant women were in the first trimester, 91 (43.3%) women were in the second trimester, and 47 (22.4%) women were in the third trimester.

Socioeconomic characteristics of the study population

A total of 142 (67.6%) pregnant women were from lower-middle-class family. Sixty-eight (32.4%) pregnant women were from upper-middle-class family.

Clinical profile of the study population

Mean vitamin D level in the deficient group was (8.1 ± 1.06 ng/mL), in the insufficient group (14.4 ± 2.3 ng/mL), and in the sufficient group (27.2 ± 8.25 ng/mL)

Mode of delivery and pregnancy outcomes

Women with sufficient vitamin D levels had the highest rate of vaginal delivery (79.2%) and the lowest rate of LSCS (lower segment cesarean section) at 20.8%. Women with deficient vitamin D levels showed a moderate rate of vaginal delivery (67.9%) and a higher LSCS rate (32.1%) compared to the sufficient group. Women with insufficient vitamin D levels had the lowest vaginal delivery rate (65.4%) and the highest LSCS rate (34.6%) [Table 1]. The pregnancy outcomes show that 79% of pregnancies result in normal births, while 21% face complications including IUGR (5%), low birth weight (15%), and stillbirth (1%) [Figure 1].

Diet and Vitamin D supplementation

Seventy percent pregnant women were consuming vegetarian diet, while 30% of the females were on a non vegetarian diet. Eighty-five percent pregnant women were not consuming vitamin D supplementations.

DISCUSSION

Vitamin D deficiency has been reported to be high among the females, which is a concerning issue and had implications on the pregnancy outcome. There was no significant association found between maternal age, weeks of gestation, and parity as shown in Table 2. Limited stock of vitamin D supplements was observed in the UHTC. Mostly, the vitamin D supplements were prescribed to patients with orthopedic problem (arthritis, back pain) and geriatric patients. Pregnant mothers do not undergo investigations for vitamin D in the ANC profile as a routine, leaving many pregnant mothers undiagnosed.

CONCLUSION

Low vitamin D levels are highly prevalent among pregnant women, which can lead to adverse health consequences in mothers. Despite the tropical climate, adequate vitamin D was not found in pregnant women. It is essential now to include screening for vitamin D levels in the maternal and child health program. Interventions, like food fortification and supplementation, should be developed to ensure healthy pregnancy outcomes.

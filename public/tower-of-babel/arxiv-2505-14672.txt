Complete quasimaps to 𝖡𝗅 ℙ s ​ ( ℙ r ) {\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
Alessio Cela, Carl Lian

Abstract
. We introduce a moduli space of “complete quasimaps” to X = 𝖡𝗅 ℙ s ​ ( ℙ r ) X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r}) . The construction, following previous work for curves on projective spaces, essentially proceeds by blowing up Ciocan-Fontanine–Kim’s space of quasimaps at loci where sections of line bundles are linearly dependent. We conjecture that tautological intersection numbers on these moduli spaces give enumerative counts of curves of fixed complex structure on X X subject to general incidence conditions, in contrast with traditional compactifications of the moduli spaces of maps. A result of Farkas guarantees that these spaces are pure of expected dimension. The conjecture is proven in dimension 2, where the main input is a Brill-Noether theorem for general curves on toric surfaces.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Counting curves

Let
X
be a geometric space, such as a complex projective variety. How many curves
C
lie in
X
, perhaps subject to incidence conditions of the form
C\ni x
, where
x\in X
is a fixed point? As a concrete example, consider the following question, with which we will be concerned in this paper.

Question 1.1.1
.

Fix a smooth, projective curve
C
of genus
g
, and let
p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\in C
be general points. Let
q,x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\mathbb{P}^{2}
be general points in the plane. How many degree
d
plane curves
f\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
are there, with multiplicity
k
at
q
, and in addition with
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,n
?

A plane curve
f\colon C\to\mathbb{P}^{2}
of degree
d
and multiplicity
k
at
q
is equivalently a map to the blow-up
f\colon C\to X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}\mathbb{P}^{2}
in the homology class
\beta=d\mathsf{H}-k\mathsf{E}
of curves meeting a general line in
d
points and the exceptional divisor in
k
points. For example, in the special case of rational plane curves
(g=0)
, under a necessary numerical constraint (
1
), the answer to Question
1.1.1
is known to be
\binom{n-d-1}{k}

[
CL23
, Theorem 1.12]
.

Traditionally, such problems are approached via intersection theory on moduli spaces. Let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the moduli space parameterizing maps
f\colon C\to X
, and let
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
be the subvariety parameterizing those maps for which
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
. Then, the desired count of curves is

\#\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{M}}\right).

When
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{M}}_{i}
is expected to be finite, intersection theory allows one to convert this set-theoretic intersection number into a cohomological calculation on
{\mathcal{M}}
. However, this is possible only after replacing
{\mathcal{M}}
with a compactification
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}\supset{\mathcal{M}}
, such as the moduli space of stable maps in Gromov-Witten theory, and the subvarieties
{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{M}}
with suitable enlargements
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}\subset{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
. Indeed, integration of cohomology classes is only sensible on compact spaces, yet
{\mathcal{M}}
is not compact: maps may acquire singularities or base-points upon degeneration. A choice of compactification
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
amounts to a choice of objects into which maps
f:C\to X
are allowed to degenerate.

From here, one hopes that

\#(\text{maps})=\#\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\mathcal{M}}_{i}\subset{\mathcal{M}}\right)=\#\left(\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}\subset{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}\right)=\int_{{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}],

where
[{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}]\in H^{*}({\overline{\mathcal{M}}})
denote the cycle classes.

However, for many well-studied compact moduli spaces, such as the moduli spaces of stable maps and stable quasimaps, it is often the case that the new intersection in
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
is larger than desired, including possibly infinitely many degenerate maps beyond the finite number of “honest maps” we wish to enumerate. While the integral on the right hand side can be made sensible by integrating against a virtual fundamental class, its value can no longer be expected to answer Question
1.1.1
. That is, the cohomological calculation produces only a
virtual count
, which enumerates not only maps
f:C\to X
out of a smooth curve, but rather includes excess contributions from a possibly infinite set of maps
f:C\to X
with singular domain. Isolating only the former maps is difficult.

This paper is concerned with confronting this problem. Namely, we aim to construct compact moduli spaces of maps to
X
on which integrals of products of cycle classes corresponding to incidence conditions are
enumerative
, that is, give an actual count of smooth curves on
X
. In the next section, we state the precise motivating problem.

Conjecture 1.2.3
.

Theorem 1.2.4
.

Conjecture
1.2.3

1.3.
Brill-Noether theorems and complete quasimaps

In the language of §
1.1
, the basic approach to removing unwanted components in intersections

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}\subset{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}

is to blow up the moduli space
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
along the closed subvarieties where the excess intersections occur, and replace the
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}
with their proper transforms. To address Question
1.1.1
and its variants, we propose the following program, taking the initial compactification
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
to be the moduli space of quasimaps.

Program 1.3.1
.

(1)

Fix a general curve
C
, and begin with the moduli space of quasimaps
[
CFK10
]

{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}=\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
.

(2)

Prove a
Brill-Noether theorem
for (quasi)maps
f:C\to X
. That is, identify conditions under which
f:C\to X
necessarily deforms in a family of expected dimension.

(3)

Blow up the loci on
\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
where the conditions of the previous step are
not
satisfied, and prove that tautological intersection numbers of the blown up moduli space are enumerative.

The role of the Brill-Noether theorem in step (2) is to identify “bad” loci of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
, where the subvarieties
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}\subset{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
are most likely to have excess intersection. The classical Brill-Noether theorem
[
GH80
]
concerns the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
, and asserts that a map
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
deforms in a family of expected dimension unless it is
degenerate
, that is, its image is contained in a hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{r}
. It is of independent interest to prove statements of this form for other targets.

Using the classical Brill-Noether theorem, Program
1.3.1
has been carried out to compute the Tevelev degrees
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
for
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
in
[
Lia24
]
. Namely, the iterated blow-up
\widetilde{Q}_{d}(C,\mathbb{P}^{r})\to\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,\mathbb{P}^{r})
of the moduli space of quasimaps along the loci where the Brill-Noether theorem fails (degenerate quasimaps) resolves all excess intersections in the calculation of
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
. The resulting moduli space
\widetilde{Q}_{d}(C,\mathbb{P}^{r})
is fibered over the moduli space
G^{r}_{d}(C)
of linear series on
C
, and the fibers are moduli spaces of
complete collineations

[
Vai84
,
Tha99
]
.

The starting point of this paper is an analog of the Brill-Noether theorem for maps
f:C\to X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
, which follows from a result of Farkas
[
Far08
, Theorem 0.1]
. Namely,
f
moves in a family of expected dimension as long as its image does not lie in any hyperplane upon blow-down to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, see Corollary
2.3.4
. Therefore, the space of quasimaps
\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is blown up along the loci where the sections defining
f
are linearly dependent, see §
2.7
for the precise definition. A new phenomenon in the case
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
is the need to keep track of a subspace of sections corresponding to a secant of
C
in the course of the blow-ups. Our space of complete quasimaps
\widetilde{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is the resulting iterated blow-up.

Remark 1.3.2
.

Strictly speaking, we depart slightly from Program
1.3.1
as stated. Rather, we start with a moduli space of
augmented quasimaps

Q^{+}(C,X)
refining the moduli space of quasimaps, which is already known to be of expected dimension. Then, we blow up the space
Q^{+}(C,X)
. We expect that our moduli spaces can be equivalently obtained by blowing up the ordinary space of quasimaps, having the effect of blowing up components of larger than expected dimension “out of existence,” but do not give a proof. We will ignore this distinction in the remainder of the introduction.

Remark 1.3.3
.

A key feature of our construction of
\widetilde{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is that it is uniform: the blow-up loci make no reference to the points of
C,X
at which incidence conditions are imposed. (In the language of §
1.1
, the blow-up of
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}
makes no reference to the subvarieties
{\overline{\mathcal{M}}}_{i}
arising in the intersection problem.) While it is true for abstract reasons that excess intersections can always be resolved by sufficiently many blow-ups, the content of Conjecture
1.2.3
is that there is a single sequence of blow-ups that suffices for all enumerative problems in question.

As we will see, the main obstruction to proving Conjecture
1.2.3
in full generality is that, in order to ensure that unwanted intersections in the boundary are fully resolved, we need slightly stronger Brill-Noether statements for maps
f:C\to X
, roughly that additional vanishing conditions on the underlying sections impose the expected number of conditions. When
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
, the
pointed
Brill-Noether theorem suffices. When
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{2})
, we invoke a Brill-Noether theorem for maps to further (toric) blow-ups of
X
developed in our work
[
CL26
]
, see §
4.1
-
4.2
. In higher dimensions, a suitable analog is missing, but we have raised the question of establishing Brill-Noether theorems for maps to toric varieties in
[
CL26
, Question 1.5]
.

We defer calculations of integrals on our spaces
\widetilde{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
to later work. As we will see, our construction in dimension 2 is sufficiently concrete, involving degeneracy loci of expected dimension inside well-understood moduli spaces, that a direct approach is plausible. In higher dimension, degeneration techniques along the lines of
[
Lia24
]
may be more robust.

We expect constructions of compactified moduli spaces of maps and enumerative calculations in this vein to be possible for other target varieties. The moduli space of quasimaps
\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is defined for GIT quotients
X
of an affine variety by a reductive group
[

]
, including Grassmannians
[
MOP11
]
, toric varieties
[
CFK10
]
, and hypersurfaces, so Program

is sensible in all of these cases.

The roadmap of the paper is as follows. The moduli spaces in question are constructed in §

2.
Moduli spaces

2.1.
Maps

Fix, once and for all, a general curve
C
of genus
g
, by which we mean a general point
[C]\in{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
. In particular,
C
is smooth, projective, and connected. While our constructions will be valid in families of curves, our transversality statements will only hold for fixed, general curves. We thus stick to the setting of a fixed, general curve throughout this paper.

Let
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
be the blow-up of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
along a linear subspace
P\cong\mathbb{P}^{s}
of dimension
s\leq r-2
. Let
g:X\to\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}
be the projection from

, let
b:X\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
be the blow-up, and let

be the exceptional divisor. Without loss of generality, we may assume that

is torus-invariant, so that
X
is toric. Write
\mathsf{H},\mathsf{E}\in H^{2}(X)
for the divisor classes of the pullback of the hyperplane from
\mathbb{P}^{r}
and the exceptional divisor, respectively.

Let
\beta\in H_{2}(X,\mathbb{Z})
be a curve class. From the point of view of enumerative problems, the
\beta
of interest are classes of irreducible, effective curves contained generically in the (toric) interior of
X
. We thus consider only non-zero classes
\beta=d\mathsf{H}^{\vee}+k\mathsf{E}^{\vee}
, where
d\geq k\geq 0
. The image under
b
of a curve in
X
in class
\beta
has degree
d
, and intersects
P
with multiplicity
k
. We fix such a class
\beta
throughout this section.

Definition 2.1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
be the moduli space of maps
f:C\to X
with
f_{*}[C]=\beta
. We say that such an
f
is “in class
\beta
.” The
expected dimension
of
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
is

\chi(C,f^{*}T_{X})=\beta\cdot K_{X}^{\vee}-r(g-1)=(r+1)d-(r-s-1)k-r(g-1).

The expected dimension is a lower bound for the actual dimension of every component. A map
f\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
is given by the following data. (See
[
Cox95
]
for a description of maps to any toric variety.)

Data 2.1.2
.

•

line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{d}\in\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}\in\mathsf{Pic}^{k}(C)
,

•

a section
u_{0}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{k})

•

sections
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{d}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k}^{-1})
, and

•

sections
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{d})
.

Given fixed
\mathcal{L}_{d},\mathcal{L}_{k}
, the sections
u_{j}
are taken up to the
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
action given by

(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\cdot(u_{0},u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s},u_{r-s+1},u_{r+1})=(\lambda_{1}u_{0},\lambda_{2}u_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{2}u_{r-s},\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}u_{r-s+1},\ldots,\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}u_{r+1}).

Data

give rise to a map

if and only if the following “base-point-free” conditions hold:

(BPF1)

u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{d}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{k}^{-1})
have no common vanishing point on

,

(BPF2)

u_{0}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{k})
and
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{d})
have no common vanishing point on
C
.

Under conditions (BPF1) and (BPF2), the composition

is given by the tuple
[u_{1}:\cdots:u_{r-s}]
, and the composition
b\circ f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
is given by the tuple

[u_{0}u_{1}:\ldots:u_{0}u_{r-s}:u_{r-s+1}:\ldots:u_{r+1}].

The sections
u_{0},\ldots,u_{r+1}
are pullbacks under

of particular torus-invariant sections on
X
. The section
u_{0}
is the pullback of a section
1_{E}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathsf{E})
, the sections
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}
are pullbacks of sections
y_{1},\ldots,y_{r-s}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathsf{H}-\mathsf{E})
, and
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
are pullbacks of sections
y_{r-s+1},\ldots,y_{r+1}
of
\mathcal{O}_{X}(\mathsf{H})
. The sections

are fixed throughout this paper.

The following example shows that

may have larger than expected dimension.

\beta
. We say that
u\in\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is
bpf
if
u
lies in the open subset
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)\subset\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
where conditions (BPF1), (BPF2) hold.

\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
is projective: it admits a natural morphism to
\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)\times\mathsf{Pic}^{k}(C)
with projective fibers remembering
(\mathcal{L}_{d},\mathcal{L}_{k})
. The product
C\times\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
carries tautological line bundles
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d},\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}
restricting at any quasimap
u
to the underlying line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{d},\mathcal{L}_{k}
, respectively. (We reserve the term “Poincaré line bundle” later for a tautological bundle on
C\times\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
which is in addition trivialized along a point of
C
.) The product
C\times\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
carries universal sections

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{0}:\mathcal{O}\to\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k},

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{i}:\mathcal{O}\to\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{-1},\quad i=1,\ldots,r-s,

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{i}:\mathcal{O}\to\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d},\quad\quad\quad\quad i=r-s+1,\ldots,r+1.

A quasimap
u
is a “map with base-points.” Indeed, sections
u_{j}
satisfying (NZ1), (NZ2) but not both of (BPF1) and (BPF2) give rise to a
rational
map
f:C\dashrightarrow X
. The rational map
f
extends canonically, after twisting down base-points, to a morphism
f^{\prime}:C\to X
, but
f^{\prime}
no longer lies in class
\beta
unless
u\in{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
to begin with. A version of this twisting is made explicit in §
2.5
.

2.3.
Non-degenerate maps and inclusions of linear series

By Example
2.1.3
,
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
(and thus
\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
) may have components of larger than expected dimension. We wish to remove these components in order to compute enumerative (as opposed to virtual) invariants such as
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
. A result of Farkas
[
Far08
, Theorem 0.1]
identifies the “good” maps.

Definition 2.3.1
.

Let
f:C\to X
be a morphism. We say that
f
is
non-degenerate
if the image
b(f(C))\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
is not contained in any hyperplane. Let
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)\subset{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
be the open subset parametrizing non-degenerate maps.

In terms of Data
2.1.2
, a map
f
is non-degenerate if and only if the sections

u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s},u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}_{d})

are linearly independent. In particular, we have
u_{0}\neq 0
. A non-degenerate map
f
therefore gives rise to the following additional data.

Definition 2.3.2
.

[
CHZ21
, Definition 4.2]

Let
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
be the space parametrizing:

•

a line bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
and an effective divisor
D\in\mathsf{Sym}^{k}(C)
,

•

an
(r+1)
-dimensional subspace (linear series)
W\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
, and

•

an
(r-s)
-dimensional subspace
V\subset W
, consisting of sections that vanish along
D
.

Indeed, given
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
, the data of
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{d}
,
D=\text{div}(u_{0})
, and

(V\subset W)=(\langle u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s}\rangle\subset\langle u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s},u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\rangle)

give a point of
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. Let
\pi:{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)\to G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
denote this association. A point
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
is referred to as an
inclusion of linear series
(ILS). We often write
\underline{W}=(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)
for brevity. Note also that Definition
2.3.2
makes sense for any
s
in the range
-1\leq s\leq r
, even though we require
0\leq s\leq r-2
in order to speak of
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
.

There is a forgetful map
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)\to\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
remembering
\mathcal{L}
. Choose a point
p\in C
. There is a
unique
(up to isomorphism) Poincaré line bundle
\mathcal{P}_{d}
on
C\times\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
with the property that the restriction of
\mathcal{P}_{d}
to
\{p\}\times\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
is trivial. We denote the pullback of
\mathcal{P}_{d}
to
C\times G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
also by
\mathcal{P}_{d}
. We contrast the notation
\mathcal{P}_{d}
with the notation
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
used for the tautological line bundle on
C\times\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
used in §
2.2
;
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
is
not
assumed trivialized upon restriction to a chosen point.

We also have a forgetful map
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)\to\mathsf{Sym}^{k}(C)
remembering
D
, and a universal divisor
\mathcal{D}\subset C\times\mathsf{Sym}^{k}(C)
. A choice of defining section of
\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})
gives, after pullback and restriction, a
consistent
choice of defining section
1_{D}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))
for every point
\underline{W}\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. We fix such a choice throughout.

It is often useful to view
V
either as a subspace of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))
or as a subspace of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
. One is embedded in the other via the section
1_{D}
, so there is essentially no distinction, and we will pass freely between these perspectives when there is no harm. When we consider questions of vanishing of elements
v\in V
at points
p\in C
, we will be more careful to specify the line bundle in which we view
v
as a section.

G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
is constructed in
[
CHZ21
, §4]
as a degeneracy locus, and in particular is projective of expected dimension

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\dim(G^{r}_{d}(C))+\dim(\mathsf{Sym}^{k}(C))+\dim(\mathsf{Gr}(r-s,r+1))-k(r-s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0ptg-(r+1)(g-d+r)+k+(r-s)(s+1)-k(r-s)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hskip 5.0pt\expdim{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)-[(r-s)^{2}+(s+1)(r+1)-1].

Proposition 2.3.3
.

G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
is pure of expected dimension.

Proof.

Consider the forgetful map

p:G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)\to G^{r}_{d}(C)\times\mathsf{Sym}^{k}(C).

The fiber
p^{-1}((W,D))
over any point is the Grassmannian of
(r-s)
-planes in

K(W,D):=\ker(W\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})\to H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{D})).

We have
\dim K(W,D)\geq(r+1)-k
. When
0\leq\ell\leq\min(k,r+1)
, Farkas
[
Far08
, §2]
proves that the locus of
(W,D)
where
\dim K(W,D)\geq(r+1)-\ell
, is pure of dimension equal to
\expdim G^{r,r-\ell}_{d,k}(C)
, and is empty when this quantity is negative. The quantity
\expdim G^{r,r-\ell}_{d,k}(C)
is strictly increasing in
\ell
in the range
0\leq\ell\leq\min(k,r+1)
, so in fact is the dimension of the locus where
\dim K(W,D)=(r+1)-\ell
.

Therefore, the dimension of the stratum of
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
mapping under
p
to the locus where
\dim K(W,D)
has dimension exactly
(r+1)-\ell
equals

\dim\mathsf{Gr}(r-s,r+1-\ell)+\expdim G^{r,r-\ell}_{d,k}(C),

and is empty when either summand is negative. This quantity attains a unique maximum at
\ell=\min(k,s+1)
(the largest possible), in which case it equals
\expdim G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
.

\Diamond

Corollary 2.3.4
.

{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
, if non-empty, is pure of expected dimension.

Proof.

For any
f\in{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
, write
\pi(f)=\underline{W}=(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)
. Then, the fiber
\pi^{-1}(\underline{W})
parametrizes Data
2.1.2
satisfying (BPF1) and (BPF2), where:

•

u_{0}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))
is a defining section,

•

u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s}
form a basis of
V
, and

•

u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s},u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
form a basis of
W
.

It follows that the dimension of
\pi^{-1}(\underline{W})
equals

1+(r-s)^{2}+(s+1)(r+1)-2=\expdim{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)-\expdim G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C).

The claim now follows from Proposition
2.3.3
.

\Diamond

Alternatively, we will see that
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
is an open subset of the moduli space
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
constructed in the next section.
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
will manifestly be pure of expected dimension, also implying Corollary
2.3.4
.
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
may be empty, even if
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
is not. For example,
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
and
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
are empty if
d<r
, but
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
always contains degenerate maps if
\beta=d\mathsf{H}^{\vee}
.

We refer the reader to
[
CHZ21
]
and further references therein for an extensive study of the various aspects of the geometry of
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
, including partial enumerative results when its dimension equals zero. One question to which we do not know the answer is whether
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
is always smooth.

If
s=r-2
, then a non-degenerate map
f:C\to{\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{r-2}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
is given by the data of a degree
d-k
cover
g\circ f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
with underlying line bundle
{\mathcal{M}}
, along with an effective divisor
D
for which
{\mathcal{M}}(D)
has
r+1
independent sections, including the two defining
g\circ f
. This is similar to the setting of Hurwitz-Brill-Noether theory
[
JR21
,
Lar21
,
LLV25
]
, except that in the Hurwitz-Brill-Noether setting, the cover of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
is taken to be fixed and general, whereas we take
C
to be general. The two settings overlap only when
\rho(g,1,d-k)=0
, in which case a general curve admits finitely many covers of
\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d-k
.

2.4.
Augmented quasimaps

We now compactify
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
by considering quasimaps relative to inclusions of linear series.

Definition 2.4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}\subset\mathcal{W}
be the tautological inclusion of universal bundles over
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. The moduli space
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
of
augmented quasimaps
in class
\beta
is defined by

\pi:Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)=\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{W}^{s+1})\to\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})\to G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C).

Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
is projective of dimension
\expdim{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)
, by Proposition
2.3.3
. There is a forgetful morphism
\psi:Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)\to\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
defined as follows. Recall that we have non-vanishing sections
1_{D}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))
defined globally over
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. A point
u\in Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
with underlying ILS
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)
is given by:

•

an
(r-s)
-tuple
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}\in V\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))
, parametrized by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})
, and

•

a multiple
u_{0}=\lambda\cdot 1_{D}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))
of the defining section, and sections
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,\\
u_{r+1}\in W\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
, parametrized by
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{W}^{s+1})
.

Indeed, given the data of
(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s})\in V^{r-s}
, we may view a local section of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(V^{r-s})}(-1)\subset V^{r-s}
as a scalar multiple
\lambda(u_{1},\cdots u_{r-s})\in V^{r-s}
. The scalar
\lambda
is used to define the section
u_{0}=\lambda\cdot 1_{D}\in H^{0}(C,\mathcal{O}(D))
, and the data of
u_{0},\ldots,u_{r+1}
define a quasimap
\psi(u)
with underlying line bundles
\mathcal{L}_{d}=\mathcal{L}
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}=\mathcal{O}(D)
. The projective bundle structure gives exactly the conditions (NZ1) and (NZ2), as well as the equivalence under the
(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{2}
-action of Data
2.1.2
. Explicitly, scaling
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}
by
\lambda_{2}
correspondingly scales
\lambda
(and hence
u_{0}
) by
\lambda_{2}^{-1}
, or equivalently
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
by
\lambda_{2}
. The scaling action defining
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{W}^{s+1})
scales
u_{0},u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
by
\lambda_{1}
.

Functorially,
\psi
is defined such that the pullbacks of the tautological bundles are given by

\displaystyle(1,\psi)^{*}\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{d}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{+}}(1),

\displaystyle(1,\psi)^{*}\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{+}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(-1),

\displaystyle(1,\psi)^{*}(\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{k}^{-1})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{d}(-\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(1),

and the pullbacks of the tautological sections are given by

\displaystyle(u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s})=

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{r-s}):\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)}\to(\mathcal{P}_{d}(-\mathcal{D})\otimes\nu^{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(1))^{r-s},

\displaystyle u_{0}=

\displaystyle\psi^{*}\overline{u}_{0}:\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)}\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{D})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{+}}(1)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{V}^{r-s})}(-1),

(2)

\displaystyle(u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1})=

\displaystyle\psi^{*}(\overline{u}_{r-s+1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{r+1}):\mathcal{O}_{C\times Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)}\to(\mathcal{P}_{d}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Q^{+}}(1))^{s+1}.

The maps (
2
) are obtained from the universal lines coming from the projective bundle structure on
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
.

The discrepancy between the line bundle
(1,\psi)^{*}\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
pulled back from
C\times\overline{Q}_{\beta}(C,X)
and the Poincaré bundle
\mathcal{P}_{d}
pulled back from
C\times\mathsf{Pic}^{d}(C)
arises from the fact that
\mathcal{P}_{d}
is assumed trivialized along a choice of point of
C
, but
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
is not.

The map
\psi
is an isomorphism over the open locus
{\mathcal{M}}_{\beta}(C,X)^{\circ}
of non-degenerate maps, and is compatible with the map
\pi:{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)\to G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. We allow ourselves to use the letter
\pi
for either map, and identify
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
with its pre-image in
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
. We say that
u
is bpf if and only if its underlying quasimap
\psi(u)
is. We will frequently abuse terminology slightly, referring to the data of an augmented quasimap
u
by its underlying quasimap
\psi(u)
or the sections
u_{j}
underlying
\psi(u)
, when the underlying ILS is implicit.

Definition 2.4.2
.

The
bi-rank
of
u\in Q_{\beta}^{+}(C,X)
is the tuple of positive integers

(r_{1},r_{2})=(\dim\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}\rangle,\dim\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\rangle).

We have
1\leq r_{1}\leq r-s
and
r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq r_{1}+(s+1)
. Note that we have written here
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}
in both arguments as opposed to
u_{0}u_{1},\ldots,u_{0}u_{r-s}
, so the bi-rank does not depend on whether
u_{0}
is zero. Implicitly, in the second argument, the sections
u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}
are viewed as sections of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
after twisting by
1_{D}
. The open subset
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
is the locus of bpf
u
of bi-rank
(r-s,r+1)
(the largest possible), where in addition
u_{0}\neq 0
.

We have therefore realized the moduli space of
non-degenerate
maps
f:C\to X
as an open subset of a proper moduli space
Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
of expected dimension, though we have not yet proven that
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
is dense (see Corollary
2.6.3
). By Example
2.1.3
, it was necessary to throw out degenerate maps. From the point of view of counting curves, this cost is mild: the curves of interest are usually automatically non-degenerate. For example, when
n\geq r+1
, a curve passing through
n
general points in
X
must be non-degenerate, so the geometric Tevelev degree
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}
enumerates only non-degenerate curves.

2.5.
Twisting

Definition 2.5.1
.

Let
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)^{\circ}\subset G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
be the image of
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
under
\pi:Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)\to G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
. We say that an ILS is
bpf
if it lies in
G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)^{\circ}
.

That is, an ILS is bpf if it underlies some non-degenerate map
f:C\to X
in class
\beta
. Explicitly, an ILS
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)
is bpf if and only if it satisfies the following two properties.

(BPF1)

The map
V\subset H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))\to H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D)|_{p})
is surjective for every
p\in C
,

(BPF2)

The map
W\to H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}|_{p})
is surjective for every
p\in\Supp(D)
.

“Surjective” in both cases is equivalent to “non-zero.” (BPF1) amounts to the statement that not every section of
V
vanishes at
p

as a section of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))
. If
p\in\Supp(D)
, it is automatic by definition that every section of
V
vanishes at
p

as a section of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
. Throughout the rest of the paper, when we speak of a section of
V
vanishing at
p
, we will mean (unless otherwise noted) as a section of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L}(-D))
, whereas when we speak of a section of
W
vanishing at
p
, we will mean as a section of
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
(as we must). We will often speak of an ILS satisfying (BPF1) or (BPF2) “at
p
,” by which we mean we test the surjectivity of the maps in question only at that particular point.

The conditions above are parallel to the properties (BPF1) and (BPF2) that quasimaps need to satisfy in order to be honest maps. We allow ourselves to refer to these properties freely in both contexts. Indeed,
\underline{W}
is bpf if and only if, for
any
choice of bases

\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{r-s}\rangle\subset\langle u_{1},\ldots,u_{r+1}\rangle

of
V\subset W
, the quasimap
u:C\to X
determined by the
u_{j}
and
u_{0}=1_{D}
is bpf. If
u\in Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
is bpf (at
p
), then
\pi(u)
must also be, but not vice versa.

We will need an explicit way to “twist down” base-points of ILS.

Definition 2.5.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
be an ILS. Define the operations:

(T1)

If every section of
V
vanishes at
p
, then replace
D
with
D+p
, leaving
\mathcal{L},V,W
the same. We now have
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k+1}(C)
.

(T2)

If every section of
W
vanishes at
p\in\Supp(D)
, then replace
D
with
D-p
and
\mathcal{L}
with
\mathcal{L}(-p)
. The subspaces
V\subset W
are identified with subspaces of the new
H^{0}(C,\mathcal{L})
in the obvious way. We now have
(\mathcal{L},D,V\subset W)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d-1,k-1}(C)
.

In fact, both operations also make sense at the level of augmented quasimaps. If
u\in Q^{+}_{\beta}(C,X)
, then applying either (T1) or (T2) at
\pi(u)\in G^{r,r-s-1}_{d,k}(C)
also twists the sections underlying
u
up or down by a defining section
1_{p}
, so one obtains, in a canonical way (independent of choice of
1_{p}
), an augmented quasimap given by the same sections and the twisted ILS.

2.6.
Incidence loci

Let
\Lambda\subset X
be a linear subspace, by which we mean the proper transform under
b:X\to\mathbb{P}^{r}
of a linear subspace
\Lambda_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
, not contained in the blown-up locus
P\subset\mathbb{P}^{r}
. We are interested in imposing conditions of the form
f(p)\in\Lambda
on maps.

Let
\Lambda_{1}=g(\Lambda)
be the image of
\Lambda
under the projection
g:X\to\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}
. Write
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})=(\dim(\Lambda_{1}),\dim(\Lambda_{2}))
. The pair
(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
, which we call the
bi-dimension
of
\Lambda
, determines the homology class of
\Lambda\subset X
. Writing

\alpha=[\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1}:\cdots:\alpha_{0}\alpha_{r-s}:\alpha_{r-s+1}:\cdots:\alpha_{r+1}],

\Lambda\subset X
is cut out by
(r-s-1)-\ell_{1}
linear equations in
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r-s}
, and
(s+1+\ell_{1})-\ell_{2}

additional
linear equations in
\alpha_{0}\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{r+1}
.

Intrinsically,
\Lambda_{1}\subset\mathbb{P}^{r-s-1}
is the vanishing locus of the composite morphism

(3)

is generically smooth and pure of codimension
\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r-\dim(\Lambda_{i}))
, the expected. Furthermore, any general point of

lies in
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
.

Conjecture
2.7.3
implies Conjecture

. Indeed, in the setting of Definition
1.2.1
, the intersection
I
would consist of finitely many reduced points, all corresponding to non-degenerate maps
f:C\to X
satisfying
f(p_{i})=x_{i}
. Under the assumption
n\geq r+1
, any such map is non-degenerate. More generally, if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}(r-\dim(\Lambda_{i}))=(r+1)d-(r-s-1)k-r(g-1)=\dim\widetilde{Q}_{\beta}(C,X).

then, Conjecture
2.7.3
predicts that
I
is a union of reduced points corresponding to the set of non-degenerate maps
f:C\to X
in class
\beta
with
f(p_{i})\in\Lambda_{i}
.

Conjecture
2.7.3
is a direct analog of
[
Lia24
, Theorem 1.7]
for complete quasimaps to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
(i.e., complete collineations relative to linear series). The role of the Brill-Noether theorem in transversality for complete quasimaps to
\mathbb{P}^{r}
in
[
Lia24
, Theorem 1.7]
is replaced by Proposition
2.3.3
and Corollary
2.3.4
. However, some further basic tools in the setting of
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
are, at the moment, missing. For example, we do not even know if the moduli space of non-degenerate maps
{\mathcal{M}}^{\circ}_{\beta}(C,X)
itself is always generically smooth beyond dimension 2, which is the case
n=0
in Conjecture
2.7.3
. The
pointed
Brill-Noether theorem is also crucial in the case
X=\mathbb{P}^{r}
, see
[
Lia24
, Lemma 4.4]
, but we do not have a suitable analog for
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{\mathbb{P}^{s}}(\mathbb{P}^{r})
.

However, in dimension 2, these ingredients are afforded by standard results on Severi varieties of surfaces, see Theorem
4.1.1
. We focus on this case in the next two sections, and prove Conjecture
2.7.3
for
X={\mathsf{Bl}}_{q}(\mathbb{P}^{2})

\mathcal{O}_{Q^{+}}(-1)\hookrightarrow\nu_{*}(\mathcal{P}_{d}(-\mathcal{D}))^{r-s}\oplus\nu_{*}(\mathcal{P}_{d})^{s+1}

is

and an additional subscheme supported on the locus

which differs from the corresponding factor in

6.3.
The enumerative range

We have the following diagram of moduli spaces:

\textstyle{Q^{+}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\psi}
\scriptstyle{\delta}
\textstyle{\overline{Q}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{P}}

We work in the setting of Definition

for

\int_{[\overline{Q}]^{\mathsf{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[\overline{{\mathsf{Inc}}}(p_{i},x_{i})]^{{\mathsf{vir}}},\quad\int_{Q^{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[{\mathsf{Inc}}^{+}(p_{i},x_{i})],\quad\int_{\mathbb{P}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})].

are intersection numbers of compactified loci of maps where

\psi_{*}[Q^{+}]=[\overline{Q}]^{\mathsf{vir}}

, the pullback
\delta^{-1}V(p_{i},x_{i})
is in general strictly larger than
{\mathsf{Inc}}^{+}(p_{i},x_{i})

The pathologies go away if the loci where
u_{0}\neq 0
are avoided. Let
\overline{Q}^{\star}\subset\overline{Q}
and
Q^{+\star}\subset Q^{+}
be the open loci of (augmented) quasimaps where
u_{0}\neq 0
. Similarly, let
\mathbb{P}^{\star}\subset\mathbb{P}
be the open locus where
f_{1},\ldots,f_{r-s}
are not all zero. Assume, as we have in the definition of
\mathbb{P}
, that
d-k>2g-1
. Assume further that
Q^{+}
is non-empty, or equivalently that

\displaystyle d-g+1

\displaystyle\geq r+1,

\displaystyle(d-k)-g+1

\displaystyle\geq r-s.

(
Q^{+}
is always non-empty in cases of enumerative interest to us.) Then,
\overline{Q}^{\star},Q^{+\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star}
are all smooth and irreducible of expected dimension, and the restrictions of the morphisms
\psi,\delta
to
Q^{+\star}
are birational, and surject onto
\overline{Q}^{\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star}
, respectively. In fact, we have an isomorphism
\iota:\overline{Q}^{\star}\to\mathbb{P}^{\star}
defined by

\iota(u)=[u_{0}u_{1}:\cdots:u_{0}u_{r-s}:u_{r-s+1}:\cdots:u_{r+1}]

such that
\iota\circ\psi=\delta
. Upon restriction to these open subsets,

pulls back under
\iota,\delta
to precisely

The basic observation is that, if
n>d
, then the intersections in question lie in the open loci
\overline{Q}^{\star},Q^{+\star},\mathbb{P}^{\star}

Lemma 6.3.1
.

Suppose that
n>d
(which in particular implies
d-k>2g-1
) and that
Q^{+}
is non-empty. Let
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}\in C
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\in X
be general points, and assume (
1
). Then, we have

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\overline{{\mathsf{Inc}}}(p_{i},x_{i})\subset\overline{Q}^{\star},\quad\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}{\mathsf{Inc}}^{+}(p_{i},x_{i})\subset Q^{+\star},\quad\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}V(p_{i},x_{i})\subset\mathbb{P}^{\star}.

In particular, these intersections pull back to each other under
\psi,\delta,\iota
, and we have

\int_{[\overline{Q}]^{\mathsf{vir}}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[\overline{{\mathsf{Inc}}}(p_{i},x_{i})]^{{\mathsf{vir}}}=\int_{Q^{+}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[{\mathsf{Inc}}^{+}(p_{i},x_{i})]=\int_{\mathbb{P}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}[V(p_{i},x_{i})].

Proof.

A point of
\overline{Q}
or
Q^{+}
with
u_{0}=0
lying in the intersection in question would need to have
u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
vanish at all of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
. If
n>d
, then this requires all of
u_{0},u_{r-s+1},\ldots,u_{r+1}
to be identically zero, which is not allowed. Similarly, a point of
\mathbb{P}
with
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{r-s}=0
lying in the intersection in question would need to have
f_{r-s+1},\ldots,f_{r+1}
vanish at all of
p_{1},\ldots,p_{n}
, which is impossible for the same reason. This proves the first claim, as well as the compatibility of the intersections under pullback.

The equality of integrals on
Q^{+},\mathbb{P}
also follows immediately, because
\delta
is a birational map of irreducible varieties, and the integrands are related by pullback up to a class supported on a subscheme of
Q^{+}
disjoint from the intersection. The integrals on
\overline{Q}
and
\mathbb{P}
also match, because the integrands are represented by intersections occurring entirely in the isomorphic open subsets
\overline{Q}^{\star}
and
\mathbb{P}^{\star}
, and given by cycle classes which are related by pullback. The virtual fundamental class
[\overline{Q}]^{\mathsf{vir}}
is needed in order for the integral on
\overline{Q}
to make sense globally, but is simply the usual fundamental class on
\overline{Q}^{\star}
, where the intersection lies.

\Diamond

In fact, when

, all three intersections enumerate
{\mathsf{Tev}}^{X}_{g,n,\beta}

Proposition 6.3.2

Suppose that

Let

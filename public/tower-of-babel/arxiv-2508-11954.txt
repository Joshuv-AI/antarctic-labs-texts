UniCast: A Unified Multimodal Prompting Framework for Time Series Forecasting
Sehyuk Park, Soyeon Caren Han, Eduard Hovy

Abstract
Time series forecasting is a foundational task across domains, such as finance, healthcare, and environmental monitoring. While recent advances in Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs) have demonstrated strong generalisation through large-scale pretraining, existing models operate predominantly in a unimodal setting, ignoring the rich multimodal context, such as visual and textual signals, that often accompanies time series data in real-world scenarios. This paper introduces a novel parameter-efficient multimodal framework, UniCast, that extends TSFMs to jointly leverage time series, vision, and text modalities for enhanced forecasting performance. Our method integrates modality-specific embeddings from pretrained Vision and Text Encoders with a frozen TSFM via soft prompt tuning, enabling efficient adaptation with minimal parameter updates. This design not only preserves the generalisation strength of the foundation model but also enables effective cross-modal interaction. Extensive experiments across diverse time-series forecasting benchmarks demonstrate that UniCast consistently and significantly outperforms all existing TSFM baselines. The findings highlight the critical role of multimodal context in advancing the next generation of general-purpose time series forecasters.



Introduction

Time series forecasting underpins critical decision-making across domains such as finance, healthcare, and climate science. Traditional statistical models like ARIMA
(
Box et al. 2015
)
, and even more flexible machine learning approaches such as XGBoost
(
Chen and Guestrin 2016
)
, offer limited capacity to capture non-linear and long-range temporal dependencies without extensive feature engineering
(
Chen et al. 2023
;
Siami-Namini, Tavakoli, and Namin 2018
)
.
Transformer-based models
(
Vaswani et al. 2017
)
and pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs)
(
Brown et al. 2020
)
have partially addressed these limitations, but their application to time series remains constrained by modality misalignment and computational inefficiency.
Recent advances in Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs)
(
Ansari et al. 2024
;
Liu et al. 2024b
;
Ekambaram et al. 2024
)
have shown promise by leveraging large-scale pretraining on diverse time series datasets to achieve strong generalization across forecasting tasks. However, current TSFMs operate in a unimodal regime, treating time series as isolated numerical signals while overlooking rich auxiliary modalities, such as visual observations and textual annotations, that often co-occur in real-world settings.

We argue that this unimodal focus is a fundamental limitation. In practice, multimodal context provides critical complementary signals: visual data may capture environmental conditions affecting sensor readings, while textual metadata encodes domain-specific semantics. Incorporating such modalities can disambiguate temporal patterns, improve predictive robustness, and enhance generalization to unseen settings. Moreover, multimodal learning aligns with cognitive theories of human forecasting, which integrates multiple perceptual and semantic cues.

In this paper, we propose UniCast, a novel parameter-efficient multimodal forecasting framework that extends TSFMs to jointly model time series, visual, and textual modalities. Our approach leverages pretrained modality-specific encoders, a Vision Encoder for visual data and a Text Encoder for textual data, to generate modality-specific embeddings. These embeddings are then integrated with a pretrained, frozen TSFM through a lightweight soft prompt tuning mechanism, allowing the model to adapt to multimodal inputs while updating only a small number of parameters.
Specifically, visual and textual contextual information is first encoded via their respective pretrained encoders, producing dense representation vectors. These embeddings, along with the original time series data, are input to the TSFM, which performs the forecasting task by leveraging cross-modal interactions. By employing soft prompts rather than full fine-tuning, our UniCast preserves the generalization capabilities of the pretrained TSFM while significantly reducing the computational cost and risk of overfitting.

Our main contributions are summarized as follows:

•

We introduce a theoretically grounded and practically motivated multimodal (vision and language) extension to Time Series Foundation Models.

•

We propose a multimodal soft prompt tuning strategy that enables parameter-efficient adaptation to heterogeneous modalities without retraining the entire time-series foundation model.

•

We provide extensive empirical evidence showing that multimodal context significantly improves forecasting accuracy and robustness across a wide range of time-series forecasting tasks.

Related Work

Pretrained Models for Time Series Forecasting.

Inspired by the success of large language models (LLMs) in natural language processing, recent research has explored repurposing these models for time series forecasting. Early methods, such as LSTPrompt
(
Liu et al. 2024a
)
and TEMPO
(
Cao et al. 2023
)
, leveraged prompting and lightweight adaptation techniques. Others, including Time-LLM
(
Jin et al. 2023
)
,
S^{2}
IP-LLM
(
Pan et al. 2024
)
, and TEST
(
Sun et al. 2023
)
, proposed translating time series into tokenized patches or embedding-aligned sequences, enabling LLMs to process temporal signals as if they were linguistic inputs.
Although these approaches inherit the generalisation capabilities of LLMs, they suffer from a fundamental modality mismatch: time series data is continuous, structured, and often multivariate, unlike natural language. This conversion not only complicates interpretability but may also degrade performance due to the loss of domain-specific inductive biases.
To address these limitations, the research community has shifted toward Time Series Foundation Models (TSFMs), domain-specialised architectures pretrained directly on large-scale temporal data. MOMENT
(
Goswami et al. 2024
)
introduced the Time Series Pile and proposed a pretraining pipeline that supports rapid downstream adaptation. Moirai
(
Woo et al. 2024
)
introduced the LOTSA dataset and developed a model capable of handling variable-length sequences and mixed frequencies. Chronos
(
Ansari et al. 2024
)
and Timer
(
Liu et al. 2024b
)
demonstrated how transformer-based architectures can be effectively adapted for temporal patterns. TimesFM
(
Das et al. 2024
)
and TTM
(
Ekambaram et al. 2024
)
have further demonstrated that decoder-only architectures can achieve competitive results with lower computational costs.
Together, these models establish TSFMs as a promising direction, offering strong generalisation, scalability, and improved alignment with temporal structures. Building upon this foundation, our work aims to extend TSFMs beyond unimodal design by incorporating rich, complementary multimodal context.

Multimodal Learning.

Multimodal learning integrates heterogeneous data types, such as images, text, audio, and structured signals, into a unified representation framework
(
Baltrušaitis, Ahuja, and Morency 2018
)
. This paradigm has been highly successful across tasks in vision-language modeling
(
Radford et al. 2021
;
Alayrac et al. 2022
)
, video understanding
(
Zhang, Li, and Bing 2023
)
, and embodied AI. For instance, LLaVA
(
Liu et al. 2023
)
showed that freezing a vision encoder and an LLM while tuning a small projection layer enables effective vision-language alignment. M2PT
(
Wang et al. 2024
)
extended this idea by introducing soft prompts for each modality and connecting them through a learnable interaction module, achieving strong performance with minimal tuning.
While such strategies have led to impressive gains in multimodal NLP and vision, they remain largely unexplored in the context of time series forecasting. Most existing time-series models treat temporal signals in isolation, neglecting the auxiliary information, such as environmental visuals, sensor descriptions, and metadata that often accompanies real-world temporal data. This omission can lead to missed opportunities for richer contextual understanding, more robust predictions, and improved generalisability.

Our Contribution.

To bridge this gap, we propose the first parameter-efficient multimodal framework for time series forecasting that builds on pretrained TSFMs. Our approach integrates time series with auxiliary visual and textual information through pretrained vision and text encoders. These embeddings are fused with the TSFM using soft prompt tuning, enabling efficient and scalable adaptation without retraining the entire model.

Preliminaries

Time Series Foundation Model (TSFM)
is a pretrained model designed specifically to capture temporal structures and dependencies in time series data. Trained on large-scale time series data, TSFMs offer strong generalisation across diverse forecasting tasks.
Given a time series input
X_{ts}
, the TSFM first applies a patch embedding function
f_{\text{patch}}
, which segments the input into fixed-size patches and encodes them into embeddings:
O_{ts}=f_{\text{patch}}(X_{ts})
.
These patch embeddings are then processed through a sequence of Transformer layers to capture temporal dependencies. The output is passed through a forecasting head to generate the prediction:
\hat{y}=f_{\text{ts}}(O_{ts})
.

Prompt Tuning
is a parameter-efficient fine-tuning (PEFT) technique that adapts large pretrained models to downstream tasks by introducing a small set of trainable vectors, soft prompts, while keeping the base model parameters frozen.
Let
f={f^{1},f^{2},\dots,f^{K}}
be the layers of a pretrained model. Prompt tuning prepends soft prompts
P={P^{1},P^{2},\dots,P^{K}}
to the input at each layer:

O^{0}=Token(X)
,

O^{k}=f^{k}(P^{k},O^{k-1}),k\in\{1,2\dots,K\}

These soft prompts are updated during training, while the model parameters remain frozen. This significantly reduces computational cost and memory usage, making prompt tuning a lightweight yet effective method for task adaptation in large-scale models.

UniCast: A Unified Multimodal Prompting Framework for Time Series Forecasting

We propose UniCast, a novel unified multimodal prompting framework that seamlessly integrates pretrained vision, text, and TSFM using a parameter-efficient strategy. UniCast is designed to capture complementary contextual signals across modalities and inject them into the forecasting process in a theoretically grounded and computationally scalable way. Rather than retraining entire foundation models, UniCast introduces soft prompts and cross-modal interaction layers to guide and align each modality, enabling effective joint reasoning with minimal trainable parameters.
The core design principle of UniCast is to retain the generalisation capabilities of large pretrained models while enabling them to incorporate multimodal evidence with lightweight adaptation. This is important for time series data, which is often contextually enriched by accompanying metadata (text) and environmental cues (images). By integrating these additional modalities through soft prompting and interaction mechanisms, UniCast provides a flexible and efficient architecture for multimodal time series forecasting, extending the boundaries of current TSFM-based approaches.

Vision Prompt.
To leverage the vision modality, UniCast uses the plot image of time series data that visually represents temporal trends and fluctuations as shown in Figure
1
.
The vision prompt module adapts the vision encoder to the time series forecasting task by injecting learnable soft prompts into each Transformer layer of the vision model. Let
P_{v}=\{P_{v}^{1},P_{v}^{2},\dots,P_{v}^{N}\}
represent the set of visual prompts, where
P_{v}^{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{v}\times d_{v}}
denotes the learnable prompt vectors for the
i
-th layer. These prompts are prepended to the sequence of image tokens
O_{v}^{0}=\text{Token}_{v}(X_{\text{img}})
, allowing the visual encoder to condition its representations on forecasting-relevant context. Formally, each layer’s output is computed as
O_{v}^{i}=f_{v}^{i}(P_{v}^{i},O_{v}^{i-1})
. The vision prompts enable task-specific adaptation without modifying the pretrained vision encoder, offering a theoretically sound and parameter-efficient mechanism for injecting downstream-specific inductive bias.

“This dataset contains the half hourly electricity demand of …”

Analogous to the vision prompt, the text prompt module tailors the pretrained language model to the forecasting context. Let
P_{t}=\{P_{t}^{1},P_{t}^{2},\dots,P_{t}^{M}\}
denote the soft prompts applied to the text encoder, where each
P_{t}^{j}\in\mathbb{R}^{l_{t}\times d_{t}}
is prepended to the tokenized text input
O_{t}^{0}=\text{Token}_{t}(X_{\text{text}})
. Each layer updates its hidden state via
O_{t}^{j}=f_{t}^{j}(P_{t}^{j},O_{t}^{j-1})
. This setup enables the text encoder to highlight task-relevant linguistic features without altering its original parameters, maintaining its pretrained semantic representations. Theoretically, this improves the alignment and relevance of the modality while preserving the generalization of the model.

Time-Series Prompt.

Time-series data (e.g.
0.2931, 1.0078, 0.8639, …
) go through the time-series embedding layer within the TSFM, resulting time-series embeddings as shown in Figure
1
. Then, these embeddings are processed together with the vision and text embeddings that have been adapted using their respective prompts.
For TSFM to effectively incorporate these representations, we introduce time-series prompts
P_{ts}=\{P_{ts}^{1},P_{ts}^{2},\dots,P_{ts}^{L}\}
, which are prepended at each layer of the TSFM.
These prompts act as inductive guides, helping the TSFM reason over a multimodal sequence composed of aligned embeddings from all input sources. Given the patch embeddings from the raw time series
O_{ts}^{0}=f_{\text{patch}}(X_{ts})
, and the aligned visual and text embeddings
O_{v}^{\prime}
and
O_{t}^{\prime}
(discussed below), the initial multimodal input is defined as
O_{m}^{0}=[O_{v}^{\prime};O_{t}^{\prime};O_{ts}^{0}]
. The TSFM then updates its hidden state through successive layers using
O_{m}^{k}=f_{ts}^{k}(P_{ts}^{k},O_{m}^{k-1})
, culminating in a forecast
\hat{y}=f_{\text{head}}(O_{m}^{L})
. This formulation ensures that the TSFM attends over both intrinsic temporal dynamics and extrinsic multimodal context in a theoretically grounded and highly extensible manner.

Cross Modality Interaction.

The final piece of the UniCast framework is the cross-modality interaction mechanism, which facilitates seamless integration of heterogeneous representations from the vision and text encoders into the Time-Series Foundation Model (TSFM). Since the vision and text encoders operate in their respective feature spaces (
\mathbb{R}^{d_{v}}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d_{t}}
), we introduce learnable linear projection layers
I_{v}
and
I_{t}
to map these outputs into the TSFM embedding space
\mathbb{R}^{d_{ts}}
.
Specifically, the final outputs from the vision and text encoders,
O_{v}^{N}
and
O_{t}^{M}
, are projected as
O_{v}^{\prime}=I_{v}(O_{v}^{N})
and
O_{t}^{\prime}=I_{t}(O_{t}^{M})
. These projected representations are concatenated with the time-series patch embeddings to form the initial input to the TSFM.
By explicitly modeling cross-modal alignment, UniCast enables the TSFM to attend to contextually relevant features across modalities, while the parameter-efficient design ensures scalability and deployability. The introduction of interaction layers not only preserves the semantic integrity of pretrained encoders but also facilitates smooth integration into the TSFM’s temporal reasoning pipeline. B
.

Overall Performance

Table
2
presents the Mean Squared Error (MSE) across eight benchmark datasets for UniCast and six strong baseline models. The results clearly demonstrate the effectiveness of our proposed UniCast framework. Both UniCast variants—based on Timer and Chronos backbones—achieve the lowest average MSE across all datasets, with UniCast(Chronos) achieving the best overall performance (1.1841), closely followed by UniCast(Timer) (1.1981). These results represent a substantial improvement over the best-performing baseline, TimesFM (1.6576). Notably, UniCast delivers either the best or second-best results on six out of eight datasets, including significant gains on AU-Elec, Tourism, and Hospital, where it outperforms all baselines by a large margin. Even in cases where baseline models like Chronos and MOMENT perform well (e.g., COVID-19 and NN5), UniCast remains highly competitive. These results confirm that incorporating multimodal context through vision and text enables more accurate and robust forecasting than unimodal approaches. Furthermore, UniCast achieves these gains in a parameter-efficient manner by keeping all backbone encoders frozen and only tuning lightweight prompts and interaction layers. This demonstrates that multimodal prompting is not only effective but also scalable, validating our central hypothesis that unified, parameter-efficient multimodal integration substantially improves generalisation in time series forecasting.

Effect of Prompt Location

We study how the location of soft prompt injection impacts forecasting performance by testing four configurations, First, Odd, Top Half, and All layers, across the vision encoder (CLIP), text encoder (Qwen), and TSFM, using Timer as the backbone.
This analysis is performed on three diverse datasets, NN5 Daily, Australian Electricity, and CIF 2016, to capture short-, long-, and mixed-length time series dynamics.
As shown in Table
4
, the results reveal two important insights. First, injecting prompts at more layers generally improves performance, with the All-layers setting consistently achieving the best or second-best results across all three datasets. In particular, this configuration yields the best performance on AU-Elec and CIF 2016, and is nearly optimal on NN5. Second, while adding prompts to all layers is most effective overall, inserting prompts in only a subset of layers (e.g., top half or odd-numbered) still leads to strong performance, outperforming the First-layer-only baseline by a significant margin on every dataset.
These findings validate our hypothesis that multi-layer prompt injection improves cross-modal adaptation and temporal reasoning, allowing the model to better incorporate and align information across modalities at different levels of abstraction. Additionally, this study underscores the flexibility of UniCast’s prompting strategy: even partial injection yields substantial gains, suggesting that prompt location can be treated as a tunable design choice to balance performance and efficiency in real-world deployments.

Conclusion

This research introduced UniCast, a novel parameter-efficient multimodal framework that extends TSFMs to incorporate vision and text alongside time series data. By leveraging modality-specific pretrained encoders and aligning their outputs through prompt tuning, UniCast enables seamless integration with frozen TSFMs, preserving their generalization capabilities while enhancing context awareness.
The experiments across eight diverse datasets demonstrate that UniCast consistently outperforms existing TSFM baselines, even under constrained data and training epochs.
The findings underscore the untapped potential of multimodal signals in time series forecasting and pave the way for a new generation of general-purpose, context-aware forecasters capable of operating in real-world environments.

Appendix C
Backbone Models

In constructing the UniCast framework, we selected
Timer
and
Chronos
as our Time Series Foundation Model (TSFM) backbones, excluding TTM. This decision was driven by architectural compatibility with prompt tuning. Both Timer and Chronos support prompt concatenation along the patch dimension without requiring structural modifications to the model layers, thereby preserving the utility of their pretrained weights.
TTM or other models are designed with a fixed patch size architecture, making them incompatible with prompt-based augmentation unless their internal structure is altered. Such modifications would invalidate the use of pretrained weights, defeating the primary motivation behind our parameter-efficient design. Therefore, we opted for backbones that allow seamless integration of prompts without sacrificing pretrained generalization capabilities.

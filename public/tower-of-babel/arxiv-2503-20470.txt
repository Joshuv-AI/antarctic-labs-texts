Ranking theories via encoded β \beta -models Thanks: Thanks to Justin Moore and Ted Slaman for helpful suggestions.
Hanul Jeon, Patrick Lutz, Fedor Pakhomov, James Walsh

Abstract
. Ranking theories according to their strength is a recurring motif in mathematical logic. We introduce a new ranking of arbitrary (not necessarily recursively axiomatized) theories in terms of the encoding power of their β \beta -models: T < β U T<_{\beta}U if every β \beta -model of U U contains a countable coded β \beta -model of T T . The restriction of < β <_{\beta} to theories with β \beta -models is well-founded. We establish fundamental properties of the attendant ranking. The supremum of the < β <_{\beta} -ranks of theories is ω 1 \omega_{1} . The supremum of the < β <_{\beta} -ranks of finitely axiomatized theories is δ 2 1 \delta^{1}_{2} . We also calculate the ranks of some theories of interest.

1.
Introduction

Ranking theories according to their strength is a recurring motif in mathematical logic.
Perhaps the most common way to compare the strength of two theories is via relative consistency strength, but this arranges theories into an ill-founded non-linear order. However, many have observed that the restriction of consistency strength to sufficiently “natural” theories forms a well-ordered hierarchy. This contrast is striking, but we can begin to close the gap between the two phenomena by observing a feature of
proofs
of relative consistency. Oftentimes, when logicians prove that
T
’s consistency strength exceeds
U
’s, they actually prove the stronger assertion that
T
proves the existence of a well-founded model of
U
. Thus, it is worth studying notions of relative strength defined not in terms of proofs of consistency but in terms of well-founded models. The attendant hierarchy of all theories resembles the consistency strength ordering on “natural” theories in certain conspicuous respects.

The specific ranking of theories we introduce in this paper is defined in terms of
\beta
-models
, which are ubiquitous in the study of subsystems of second-order arithmetic.

Definition 1.1
.

An
\mathcal{L}_{2}

\beta
-model
is an
\omega
-model
\mathfrak{M}
in the language of second order arithmetic that is
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
-absolute, i.e., for any
\Sigma^{1}_{1}
sentence
\varphi
with parameters from
\mathfrak{M}
,
\mathfrak{M}\vDash\varphi
if and only if
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{N})\vDash\varphi
.

No consistent r.e. theory can prove that it has a
\beta
-model, by Gödel’s second incompleteness theorem. In fact, there is no theory
T
with
\beta
-models such that every
\beta
-model of
T
encodes a
\beta
-model of
T
; this is a semantic analogue of Gödel’s theorem. This engenders a semantic analogue of the notion of relative consistency strength for theories with
\beta
-models, namely,
T
is stronger than
U
if every
\beta
-model of
T
encodes a
\beta
-model of
U
. For countable
\beta
-models
M,N
, we write
N\dot{\in}M
to mean that
N
is encoded in
M
.

Definition 1.2
.

For
S,T\supseteq\mathsf{ATR}_{0}
,
S<_{\beta}T
if for every
\beta
-model
M
of
T
, there is a
\beta
-model
N\dot{\in}M
such that
N\vDash S
.

Though the consistency strength ordering is ill-founded, this semantic analogue—remarkably—is well-founded. That is, the restriction of
<_{\beta}
to theories with
\beta
-models is well-founded; see
Lemma 3.4
for the proof.
1
1

1

If a theory

\beta

In this paper, we establish some fundamental properties of the

.

In addition, we calculate the

Definition 3.1
.

An

4.
All ranks are countable

The first main theorem is that every theory’s
<_{\beta}
-rank is countable. We emphasize that this theorem concerns all deductively closed sets of formulas, not merely those that are, e.g., recursively axiomatized. Moreover, this theorem applies both to
\mathcal{L}_{2}
theories and to
\mathcal{L}_{\in}
theories.

For an upper bound, let us rely on the following subsidiary notion for the rank of transitive models:

Definition 4.1
.

For two transitive models
M
,
N
of
T_{0}
, let us consider the relation
\in
, which is well-founded. Hence
\in
defines the rank function over the collection of all transitive models of
T_{0}
. Let us define

\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)=\{\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(N)\mid N\in M\land\text{$N$ is transitive model of $T_{0}$}\}.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
T\supseteq T_{0}
, we have

|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}\leq\min\{\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)\mid M\vDash T\text{ is transitive}\}.

Proof.

Let us take
\alpha=|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}
and fix a transitive model
M
of
T
. We want to show that
\alpha\leq\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)
by induction on
\alpha
.

Suppose that
\gamma<\alpha
, so we have a theory
U<^{T_{0}}_{\beta}T
such that
\gamma=|U|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}
.
From
U<^{T_{0}}_{\beta}T
, we can see there is a transitive model
N\in M
of
U
. By the inductive hypothesis, we have
\gamma\leq\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(N)
. From
N\in M
, we get
\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)\geq\gamma+1
.
Since it holds for every
\gamma<\alpha
, we have
\alpha\leq\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)
, as desired.
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

For every
T
with a
\beta
-model,
|T|_{\beta}<\omega_{1}
.

Proof.

By
Lemma 3.9
and
Lemma 3.11
, it suffices to show that for every
T_{0}\supseteq\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}
and
T\supseteq T_{0}
with a
\beta
-model,
|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}<\omega_{1}
.

Suppose that
M
is a
\beta
-model of
T
. By taking its Skolem hull if necessary (may not be definable in
M
), we may assume that
M
is countable. Furthermore, we may assume that
M
is transitive by considering its transitive collapse. Then
|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}
is less than or equal to
\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)
. There are at most countably many transitive models in
M
, so
\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)<\omega_{1}
.
∎

5.
Ranks are cofinal for theories without

In this section, we prove that the
\beta
-rank of theories extending various

is cofinal in
\omega_{1}
. Every proof of the cofinality in this section relies on the following theorem:

Theorem 5.1
.

Suppose that

satisfies the following

: For every real

such that
T

.
Then the supremum of
|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}

We prove by induction on

\xi<\alpha

be the collection of extensions of
T_{0}

\xi<\alpha

Let us choose
\{X_{n}\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}

be a real coding

Suppose that
T
is an extension of
T_{0}
such that every transitive model of
T
contains

. If

, then

contains a transitive model of

for each

, so

for each

|T|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}\geq\sup_{n<\omega}\bigl(|T_{n}|_{\beta}^{T_{0}}+1\bigr)\geq\alpha.\qed

6.
Ranks are cofinal for theories with
V=L

In personal communication, John Steel asked whether the
\beta
-ranks of theories extending
\mathsf{ZFC}+(V=L)
is cofinal in
\omega_{1}
. In fact, the supremum of the
\beta
-ranks of theories containing
V=L
is
\omega_{1}^{L}
. First, we show the lemma asserting that the
\beta
-rank of a transitive model is bounded by its height:

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
T_{0}\supseteq\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}
and
M
be a transitive model of
T_{0}
. Then
\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)\leq\mathsf{Ord}\cap M
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
M
: Suppose that the desired inequality holds for every transitive model

of

. Then

\displaystyle\beta\text{-}\operatorname{rank}_{T_{0}}(M)

\displaystyle\leq\{(\mathsf{Ord}\cap N)+1\mid N\in M\land N\text{ is a transitive model of }T_{0}\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathsf{Ord}\cap M,

where the last inequality holds since
\mathsf{Ord}\cap N\in M
and
M
is closed under successor ordinals.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
T
be a theory extending
\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}+(V=L)
and having a transitive model. Then
|T|^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}<\omega_{1}^{L}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
M
is a transitive model of

. Then
M=L_{\alpha}
for some ordinal
\alpha
. Now let us find
\alpha^{\prime}<\omega_{1}^{L}
such that
L_{\alpha^{\prime}}\prec L_{\alpha}
by computing a transitive collapse of a Skolem hull of
L_{\alpha}
in
L
. By

, we have
|T|_{\beta}^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}\leq\alpha^{\prime}<\omega_{1}^{L}
.
∎

However, we can also show that the
\beta
-rank of theories extending
\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}+(V=L)

The following lemmas are modifications of exercises in Devlin
[
2
, Exercise II.3.B–3.D.]

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\alpha
be a limit ordinal, and fix a recursive enumeration
\{\sigma_{m}\mid m<\omega\}
of all sentences in the language
\{\in\}
. Then there is no formula
\varphi(x)
with a single free variable such that
L_{\alpha}\vDash\sigma_{m}\iff L_{\alpha}\vDash\varphi(\underline{m})
. (
\underline{m}
is a term denoting the natural number

Lemma 6.4
.

The set
A=\{\operatorname{Th}(L_{\alpha})\mid\alpha<\omega_{1}^{L}\land L_{\alpha}\vDash\mathsf{ATR}_{0}^{\mathsf{set}}\}
is uncountable in

.

Proof.

Let us work in
L
throughout the whole proof.

A

and

\operatorname{Th}(L_{\alpha})\in A
. Now suppose that
A
is countable, and let

be the

-least set such that

(T\text{ is the $<_{L}$-least set such that $A=\{T"\{n\}\mid n<\omega\}$})^{L_{\omega_{1}^{L}}},

so in particular,

is definable over

with a definable parameter

(

is definable using the equation in the statement of the lemma), so

is parameter-free definable in

.

Fix a recursive enumeration
\{\sigma_{m}\mid m<\omega\}
of sentences in the language
\{\in\}
, and pick

such that

exists since we have a countable

Then we have

T
is definable over

Proposition 6.5
.

The supremum of
\beta
-rank of theories extending
\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}+(V=L)

\omega_{1}^{L}
.

Proof.

Let
A
be the set we defined in Lemma

. Say
\alpha
is

\operatorname{Th}(L_{\alpha})\in A
and (ii) for all

.

First, we make an observation: If every
\beta

\beta

6.1.

Lemma 6.6

(

∎

(
\mathsf{KPi}

Now suppose that

is bounded, and let

\leq

Corollary 8.5
.

(1)

\lvert\mathsf{KPi}\rvert^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}=
The least recursively inaccessible ordinal.

(2)

\lvert\Pi^{1}_{2}\text{-}\mathsf{CA}_{0}\rvert^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}=
The least non-projectible ordinal.

(3)

\lvert\mathsf{ZF}^{-}\rvert^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}=
The least gap ordinal.

Proof.

We only provide the proof of the first item, as the proof for the others is similar.
Suppose that
\iota
is the least recursively inaccessible ordinal. Then we have

|\mathsf{KPi}|^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}\leq|\mathsf{KPi}+\text{Countability}+(V=L)|^{\mathsf{PRS}+\mathsf{Beta}}_{\beta}=\iota.

For the other direction, suppose that

. Now, let

be a set of all members

such that

\mathsf{Ord}^{N}=\mathsf{Ord}^{M}

Hence, if

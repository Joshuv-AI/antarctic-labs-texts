A novel technique for caruncular sub-tenon’s anesthesia using a flexible 22-gauge catheter in ocular surgery
Authors: Maxime Quirke, Dhyna Thorel, Eric Parrat, Umberto Lorenzi
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

Locoregional ophthalmic anesthesia through a peribulbar or retrobulbar block carries recognized risks, including retrobulbar hemorrhage, globe perforation, and optic nerve injury.[1] Sub-tenon’s anesthesia offers a safer profile[2] with comparable efficacy in cataract surgery.[3] The caruncular variant is particularly attractive, accessing a minimally vascularised space, with reported rapid onset and high patient satisfaction.[4]

We describe a simplified caruncular technique that replaces the rigid cannula with a flexible 22-gauge intravenous catheter, used routinely at our institution for over a decade in more than 2,000 anterior and posterior segment surgeries. Under the operative microscope, after topical anaesthesia and povidone-iodine antisepsis, a 22-gauge safety catheter (Introcan Safety; B. Braun, Boulogne-Billancourt, France) is introduced into the caruncular region, between the plica semilunaris and the bulbar conjunctiva, along a straight, nonangulated trajectory [Fig. 1]. Once the tip reaches the sub-tenon’s space, the needle is withdrawn and 2% preservative-free lidocaine is injected slowly: 3 mL for anterior segment procedures, titrated up to 10 mL for posterior segment surgery, guided by a mild rise in intraocular tension. No rigid cannula is used.

Caruncular entry for sub-Tenon’s anesthesia using a 22-G flexible intravenous catheter under microscopic guidance

Across cataract extraction, secondary intraocular lens implantation, retinal detachment repair, and epiretinal membrane peeling, no case required conversion to a different type of an anesthesia. We observed no retrobulbar hemorrhage, globe perforation, optic nerve injury, or extraocular muscle damage. The flexible catheter minimizes tissue trauma and conjunctival manipulation, improving both safety and patient comfort.[5]

Although observational, this atraumatic, reproducible, and cost-effective method performs reliably even in challenging anatomy.

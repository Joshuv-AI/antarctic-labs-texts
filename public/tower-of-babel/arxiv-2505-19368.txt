The spectrum of local dualisable modular representations
Dave Benson, Srikanth B. Iyengar, Henning Krause and Julia Pevtsova

Abstract
. For a point 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} in the spectrum of the cohomology ring of a finite group G G over a field k k , we calculate the spectrum for the subcategory of dualisable objects inside the tensor triangulated category of 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} -local and 𝔭 \mathfrak{p} -torsion objects in the (big) stable module category of the group algebra k ​ G kG .



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
20C20 (primary); 18G80, 20J06 (secondary)

1.
Introduction

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of characteristic
p
. The stable category of
kG
-modules, denoted
\operatorname{StMod}{kG}
, is a tensor triangulated category that has been much studied over the last few decades. The compact objects form the stable category of finitely generated modules
\operatorname{stmod}{kG}
, whose thick tensor ideals were classified by
Benson, Carlson and Rickard
[
7
]
. The localising tensor ideals of
\operatorname{StMod}{kG}
were later classified by Benson, Iyengar and Krause
[
10
]
. In both cases, the answers are in terms of the projectivised prime ideal spectrum of the cohomology ring
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
, an object well understood due to the extraordinary work of Quillen
[
26
,
27
]
. Given a homogeneous prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
, there is an idempotent functor
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}
on
\operatorname{StMod}{kG}
that picks out a minimal localising tensor ideal, to be thought of as a stratum of the stable module category at that prime. Every localising tensor ideal is generated by the minimal ones it contains, and this gives a bijection between the localising tensor ideals of
\operatorname{StMod}{kG}
and the subsets of
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
. For the thick tensor ideals in
\operatorname{stmod}{kG}
, only the specialisation closed subsets are relevant.

In
[
16
]
we studied the subcategory
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}{kG})}^{\operatorname{d}}
of dualisable objects in the tensor triangulated category
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}{kG}
. This an essentially small tensor triangulated category and our purpose in this paper is to describe the thick tensor ideals of
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}{kG})}^{\operatorname{d}}
. The answer is in terms of the spectrum of the completion of the localised cohomology ring,
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. The following is our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Cohomological support gives a bijection between the thick tensor ideals in

{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\operatorname{StMod}{kG})}^{\operatorname{d}}
and the specialisation closed subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
.

The completion of a local ring does change the spectrum. For example, let
k
be a field of odd characteristic and
R=k[x,y]
,
\mathfrak{p}=(x,y)
. Then the polynomial
x^{2}-y^{2}(1-y)
is prime in
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
, but factorises in
R{}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
as
(x+y\sqrt{1-y})(x-y\sqrt{1-y})
. One can mimic this example in the cohomology of an elementary abelian group.

To prove the theorem, we first deal with the case of an elementary abelian
p
-group.
In this case, we use the same route as in
[
10
]
to transfer the problem into the corresponding problem in a polynomial ring localised at a prime. In that context, we essentially solved the problem in
[
17
]
; see also Theorem
4.5
. To go from elementary abelian
p
-subgroups to arbitrary finite groups, we use the Quillen stratification, via induction and restriction. Along the way, we also obtain an analogue of the stratification results of Quillen, and of Avrunin and Scott
[
2
]
, for local dualisable modules; see Theorem
5.10
.

2.
Lattice of thick tensor ideals

In this section we record a few observations regarding the lattice of thick tensor ideals in a tensor triangulated category.

Let
(\mathcal{C},\otimes,\mathds{1})
be an essentially small tensor triangulated category, with internal function object
\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}
. An object
x
is
dualisable
if for each
y\in\mathcal{C}
the natural map

\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,\mathds{1})\otimes y\longrightarrow\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\!\;\mathit{om}}(x,y)

is an isomorphism. We assume that the category
\mathcal{C}
is
rigid
, which means that every object is dualisable. Throughout we also fix a graded-commutative ring
R
that acts on
\mathcal{C}
canonically, that is to say, via a map of rings
\phi\colon R\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}}(\mathds{1})
. In many cases
R
is the graded endomorphism ring of
\mathds{1}
, and
\phi=\mathrm{id}
, but not always. In what follows we only ever consider homogeneous elements and ideals; a non-homogeneous element has no meaning as an endomorphism of
\mathds{1}
. In particular,
\operatorname{Spec}R
is the set of homogeneous prime ideals of
R
endowed with the Zariski topology.

Cohomological support

For a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
set

V(\mathfrak{a})\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}

and consider the thick subcategory

\mathcal{C}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0\text{ for }\mathfrak{p}\in(\operatorname{Spec}R)\setminus V(\mathfrak{a})\}.

We recall from
[
21
]
the definition of
Koszul objects
. For an element
r
in
R
of degree
d
and an object
x
in
\mathcal{C}
let
{x}/\!\!/{r}
denote the cone of the map

x\xrightarrow{r}\Sigma^{d}x
. For an ideal
\mathfrak{a}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{n})
we set
{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}=x_{n}
, where
x_{0}=x
and
x_{i}={x_{i-1}}/\!\!/{r_{i}}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
. The definition depends on the choice of generators, but the thick subcategory generated by such a Koszul object does not.

Lemma 2.1
.

We have
\mathcal{C}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})
.

Proof.

The equality

\mathcal{C}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}=\operatorname{thick}(\{{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\mid x\in\mathcal{C}\})
follows from

[
12
, Proposition 3.10]
. It remains
to note that
{x}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}={\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}}\otimes x
.
∎

A subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
is said to be a
Thomason
subset if it can be written as
V=\bigcup_{i}V_{i}
such that each
(\operatorname{Spec}R)\setminus V_{i}
is Zariski open and quasi-compact; that is to say,
V_{i}=V(\mathfrak{a}_{i})
for some finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}_{i}
in
R
. By definition, the Thomason subsets are the open subsets for the
Hochster dual topology
on
\operatorname{Spec}R
; see
[
20
]
. When
R
is noetherian, Thomason subsets are precisely the specialisation closed subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
.

The open subsets of any space
form a
frame
, that is to say, a complete lattice where arbitrary joins distribute over finite meets. A morphism of frames is a map that preserves arbitrary joins and finite meets; see
[
22
]
for details. An element
x
in a complete lattice is
finite
or
compact
if
x\leq\bigvee_{i\in I}y_{i}
implies

x\leq\bigvee_{i\in J}y_{i}
for some finite subset
J\subseteq I
. A frame is
coherent
when every element can be written as a join of finite elements and the finite elements form a sublattice.

For example, the Thomason subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
form a coherent frame, and a simple calculation shows that the finite elements are precisely the sets of the form
V(\mathfrak{a})
where
\mathfrak{a}
is a finitely generated ideal. Let
\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
denote the lattice of tensor ideal thick subcategories in
\mathcal{C}
; it is also a coherent frame with finite elements thick tensor ideals of the form
\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(x)
for
x\in\mathcal{C}
; see
[
23
, Theorem 3.1.9]
.

For a Thomason subset
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
we set

\tau_{R}(V)\coloneqq\bigvee_{V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V}\mathcal{C}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\,.

In
[
24
, Proposition 4.9]
it is shown that this induces a map

(2.2)

\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\xrightarrow{\ \tau_{R}\ }\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})\,.

For each
x\in\mathcal{C}
the
cohomological support
is by definition

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\coloneqq\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}R\mid\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq 0\}\,.

We say
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is
finite
if for each
x\in\mathcal{C}
there is a finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
such that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)=V(\mathfrak{a})
. This property holds, for instance, when
R
is noetherian and the
R
-module
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)
is finitely generated for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
. When
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite one gets a frame morphism

(2.3)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}\colon\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{supp}_{R}\ }\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}

that restricts to a map on the sublattice of finite elements. More is true.

Lemma 2.4
.

The cohomological support
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite if and only if there is an adjoint
pair of maps between posets

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\,;}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{R}}

that is to say, for
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}R
one has

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{D})\subseteq V\quad\iff\quad\mathcal{D}\subseteq\tau_{R}(V)\,.

Proof.

Suppose
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite, so that one gets a well-defined map from the frame of thick tensor ideals of
\mathcal{C}
to the frame of Thomason subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
. Let
V
be a Thomason subset of
\operatorname{Spec}R
and

x\in\mathcal{C}
. Then we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq V(\mathfrak{a})\text{ for some }V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff x\in\mathcal{C}_{V(\mathfrak{a})}\text{ for some }V(\mathfrak{a})\subseteq V

\displaystyle\iff x\in\tau_{R}(V).

The first equivalence holds because
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
is finite as an element in the frame of Thomason subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
, by hypothesis. The other ones are immediate from the definitions.

Now suppose (
\operatorname{supp}_{R},\tau_{R}
) form an adjoint pair. This implies that

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
is Thomason for each
x\in\mathcal{C}
. A standard argument
shows that the left adjoint preserves finiteness if the right
adjoint preserves all joins. It remains to recall that
\tau_{R}
preserves joins.
∎

A consequence of the preceding result is that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is an isomorphism if and only if
\tau_{R}
is an isomorphism. This can be characterised as follows.

Lemma 2.5
.

Cohomological support induces an isomorphism

\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})\longrightarrow\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\,.

if and only if the following hold:

(1)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite;

(2)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Spec}R
;

(3)

\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
implies
\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(y)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(x)

for all
x,y\in\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

The forward direction is clear, since the finite elements in
\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
are precisely the subcategories of the form
\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(x)
for some
x\in\mathcal{C}
. Conversely, suppose conditions (1)–(3) hold. Then
\tau_{R}\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(x))=\operatorname{thick}^{\otimes}(x)
holds and hence

\tau_{R}\operatorname{supp}_{R}=\mathrm{id}
. On the other hand,
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is surjective since
\operatorname{supp}_{R}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})=V(\mathfrak{a})
for any finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

There is an analogue of Lemma
2.5
for the lattice of all thick subcategories, with essentially same proof. The tensor unit is then replaced by a generator of
\mathcal{C}
, which does exist when
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite and
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(\mathcal{C})=\operatorname{Spec}R
.

Tensor triangulated support

Following
[
3
]
we consider the
Balmer spectrum

\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}
of
\mathcal{C}
,
and for any
x\in\mathcal{C}
set

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\coloneqq\{\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}\mid x\not\in\mathcal{P}\}\,.

By
[
3
, Theorem 4.10]
there are mutually inverse isomorphisms of frames

(2.7)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}

where for each pair
\mathcal{D}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
and
V\subseteq\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}
one has

\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(\mathcal{D})\coloneqq\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{D}}\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}(x)\quad\text{and}\quad\tau_{\otimes}(V)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{C}\mid\operatorname{supp}(x)\subseteq V\}\,.

The Balmer spectrum is related to the spectrum of the ring
R
that acts on
\mathcal{C}
, via the
comparison map

(2.8)

\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{\mathcal{C}}\ }\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1})\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{Spec}\phi\ }\operatorname{Spec}R

where
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}
takes a prime ideal
\mathcal{P}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
to the preimage of the unique maximal ideal of
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{P}}(\mathds{1})
under the canonical homomorphism

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1})\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{P}}(\mathds{1})
; see
[
4
]
. We need a simple lemma.

Lemma 2.9
.

For
r\in\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathds{1})
we have
\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{C}}(V(r))=\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}
. Then
\mathcal{P}\in\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})
if and only if the morphism

\mathds{1}\xrightarrow{r}\Sigma^{|r|}\mathds{1}
is not invertible in
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{P}
. This
means
r\in\rho_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathcal{P})
, so
\mathcal{P}\in\rho^{-1}_{\mathcal{C}}(V(r))
.
∎

The following result provides a comparison between cohomological and
tensor triangulated support.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an essentially small rigid tensor triangulated
category. Then the comparison map (
2.8
) induces a
commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spc}\mathcal{C}\}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{Thick}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{R}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\rho^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tau_{\otimes}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

In particular, the comparison map(
2.8
) is a homeomorphism if and only if the
map (
2.2
) is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The map
\rho^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}
is given by taking preimages along

\rho_{\mathcal{C}}
, and
(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{*}
is defined analogously. We need to check that these maps are well-defined and that they make the diagram commutative. First observe that for any
r\in R
the preimage of
V(r)
under
\operatorname{Spec}\phi
equals
V(\phi(r))
, and its preimage under
\rho_{\mathcal{C}}
equals
\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{r})
by Lemma
2.9
. Thus

(\rho^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{*})(V(\mathfrak{a}))=\operatorname{supp}_{\otimes}({\mathds{1}}/\!\!/{\mathfrak{a}})

for any finitely generated ideal
\mathfrak{a}
of
R
. Using Lemma
2.1
this yields the commutativity, keeping in mind that all maps in the diagram preserve arbitrary joins. The diagram implies that
\tau_{R}
is a bijection if and only if
\rho^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{*}
is a bijection. The spaces in question are sober, so are determined up to a homeomorphism by their frames of open subsets
[
22
]
. Thus
\rho^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)^{*}
is a bijection if and only if
(\operatorname{Spec}\phi)\rho_{\mathcal{C}}
is a homeomorphism.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Suppose that the cohomological support is finite. Then the support map

\operatorname{supp}_{R}
from (
2.3
) is a bijection if and only if the comparison map (
2.8
) is a homeomorphism. This follows from the adjointness of the pair
(\operatorname{supp}_{R},\tau_{R})
.

Change of categories

In Section
4
we have to deal with changes of categories. In that context, the tensor structure on categories turns out to be not essential since all thick subcategories are tensor ideals. So it is convenient not to have to worry whether functors are monoidal.

Consider an essentially small tensor triangulated category
\mathcal{C}
with central
R
-action and the lattice
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{C})
of all thick subcategories, rather than tensor ideal thick subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
. This need not be a frame. The cohomological support function
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is defined just as above and gives a map from
\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{C})
to the Thomason subsets of
\operatorname{Spec}R
when
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
is finite.

Lemma 2.12
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
and
g\colon\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
be an adjoint pair of
R
-linear exact functors between
R
-linear triangulated categories. Suppose that
\operatorname{thick}(gf(x))=\operatorname{thick}(x)
for all
x\in\mathcal{C}
. If taking support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{D})\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{supp}_{R}\ }\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\,,

then taking support induces a lattice isomorphism

\operatorname{Thick}(\mathcal{C})\xrightarrow{\ \operatorname{supp}_{R}\ }\{\text{Thomason subsets of }\operatorname{Spec}R\}\,.

Proof.

First observe that
\operatorname{supp}_{R}(f(x))=\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)
for each
x\in\mathcal{C}
, since there is an
R
-linear isomorphism

\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{D}}(f(x))\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(x,gf(x))

and
\operatorname{thick}(gf(x))=\operatorname{thick}(x)
. Now we check the conditions in Lemma
2.5
for
\mathcal{C}
, keeping in mind Remark
2.6
.
Conditions (1) and (2) follow from our first observation, because they hold for
\mathcal{D}
. For (3) we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{supp}_{R}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(y)

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{supp}_{R}(f(x))\subseteq\operatorname{supp}_{R}(f(y))

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{thick}(f(x))\subseteq\operatorname{thick}(f(y))

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{thick}(gf(x))\subseteq\operatorname{thick}(gf(y))

\displaystyle\implies\operatorname{thick}(x)\subseteq\operatorname{thick}(y)

and this finishes the proof.
∎

3.
Stable module category

In this section we recall a characterisation of local dualisable objects in the stable module category of a finite group, from
[
16
]
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
k
a field of positive characteristic
p
dividing
|G|
. In the rest of this manuscript the focus is on the stable category of (all)
kG
-modules

\mathcal{T}_{G}\coloneqq\operatorname{StMod}kG\,.

This is a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category, with product
-\otimes_{k}-
with diagonal
G
-action, and unit
k
. Let
H^{*}(G,k)
denote the cohomology algebra of
G
. There is a natural map of graded
k
-algebras
H^{*}(G,k)\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(k)
, which provides a canonical
H^{*}(G,k)
-action on
\mathcal{T}_{G}
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
and let
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\colon\mathcal{T}_{G}\to\mathcal{T}_{G}
be the idempotent functor corresponding to
\mathfrak{p}
; see, for instance,
[
9
, Section 5]
. Its image
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}
is the full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}_{G}
consisting of
\mathfrak{p}
-local and
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion objects, and that is itself a compactly generated tensor triangulated category with product inherited from
\mathcal{T}_{G}
and unit
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k
. We also know the graded endomorphism ring of the unit: The natural map
H^{*}(G,k)\to\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)
induces an isomorphism

(3.1)

H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)\,.

This is proved by Benson and Greenlees
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
; see also
[
6
, Theorem 2]
. The following result is extracted from
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
. There are equalities

{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G})}^{\operatorname{d}}=\operatorname{thick}(\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}M\mid M\in\operatorname{stmod}kG\})=\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)\,,

where
C
is a generator for the triangulated category
\operatorname{stmod}kG
. ∎

The result below implies that cohomological support, with respect to
R
, is finite on the category of local dualisable objects, so the results of Section
2
apply to it.

Corollary 3.3
.

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
and set
\mathcal{C}={(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G})}^{\operatorname{d}}
. Then the
k
-algebra
R=\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)
is noetherian and for all
M,N
in
\mathcal{C}
the
R
-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,N)
is finite. In particular, cohomological support,
\operatorname{supp}_{R}
, is finite on
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

Set
S=H^{*}(G,k)
. Since this ring is noetherian, and localisations and completions preserve the noetherian property, it follows from (
3.1
) that the ring
R
is noetherian. With
C
as in Theorem
3.2
, and
C^{\vee}=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(C,k)
, one has isomorphisms of
R
-modules

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(M,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(M,C\otimes_{k}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(M\otimes_{k}C^{\vee},\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{S}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(k,\Omega^{d}(M\otimes_{k}C^{\vee})),I(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{S}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(k,M\otimes_{k}\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee})),I(\mathfrak{p}))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{R}(\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(k,M\otimes_{k}\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee})),I(\mathfrak{p}))

Here
I(\mathfrak{p})
is the injective hull of the
S
-module
S/\mathfrak{p}
and
d
is the Krull dimension of
S/\mathfrak{p}
. The third isomorphism is by
[
13
, Theorem 5.1]
; see also
[
8
, Theorem 2.6]
. The last isomorphism holds because the modules
M\otimes_{k}\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee})
and
I(\mathfrak{p})
are
\mathfrak{p}
-local and
\mathfrak{p}
-torsion. The remaining isomorphisms are standard. Since
M
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}
and the module
C
, and hence also
\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee})
, is compact in
\mathcal{T}_{G}
, the module
M\otimes_{k}\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee})
is dualisable in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}
. Thus the

-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(k,M\otimes_{k}\Omega^{d}(C^{\vee}))
is artinian, by

. Using Matlis duality and the isomorphisms above we deduce that
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(M,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
, and, hence,
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
is noetherian. Since

\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
, again by Theorem

, we deduce that the

-module
\operatorname{Hom}^{*}_{\mathcal{C}}(M,N)
is noetherian; equivalently, finitely generated.
∎

Theorem

also allows us to translate the problem of classifying the tensor ideal thick categories of
{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G})}^{\operatorname{d}}
to the corresponding classification problem for
\operatorname{thick}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}C)
. There is one further change in perspective that is helpful.

The homotopy category of injective modules

Let
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
be the homotopy category of complexes of injective

-modules; this too is a rigidly compactly generated tensor triangulated category, with product
-\otimes_{k}-
with diagonal
G

.

The stable module category,

, can be identified as a tensor triangulated category with the full subcategory of acyclic complexes in

, and one has a recollement

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{T}_{G}=\operatorname{StMod}{kG}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}kG)}

For each

applying

to the inclusion

yields an equivalence of tensor triangulated categories:

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)\,.

See, for instance,
[

, Section 8]
. Thus Theorem

translates to an equality

{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG))}^{\operatorname{d}}=\operatorname{thick}(\{\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}(\mathbf{i}M)\mid M\in\operatorname{mod}kG\})\,.

Thus the classification problem that we have set out to solve can be recast in terms of local dualisable objects in

. This is important in Section

.

Having made this transition, a natural question is to compute the spectrum of local dualisable objects in
\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)
also at

\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)
; this does not arise for the stable module category because

. The recollement above induces an equivalence

\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\mathbf{D}(\operatorname{Mod}kG)

of tensor triangulated categories, leading to the equivalence

{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG))}^{\operatorname{d}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }{\mathbf{D}}^{\mathrm{b}}(\operatorname{mod}kG)\,.

Keep in mind that the tensor structure on

is
-\otimes_{k}-
with diagonal

-action. The main result of
[

computes the spectrum of the category on the right, yielding homeomorphisms:

\operatorname{Spc}{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{m}}\mathbf{K}(\operatorname{Inj}kG))}^{\operatorname{d}}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{G}\ }\operatorname{Spec}\operatorname{End}^{*}(k)\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)\,.

Theorem

below is an extension of this result to all primes

in
\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k)
.

4.
Elementary abelian groups

Let

be a prime number,

Proof of Theorem

We keep the notation introduced above. Since

Lemma 5.6
.

The map
(1)
in diagram (
5.5
) is surjective.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that the natural map

\coprod_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\operatorname{Spc}{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{E})}^{\operatorname{d}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Spc}{(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{G})}^{\operatorname{d}}

is surjective. This map is induced by the functor

(5.7)

\rho\colon\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{G}\longrightarrow\prod_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{E}\cong\prod_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)}\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{E}

induced by the restriction functors
\rho_{E}
. The isomorphism holds because
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{E}\equiv\{0\}
if
\mathfrak{p}
is not supported on
E
. By
[
5
, Theorem 1.3]
, it suffices to verify that the functor
\rho
is nil faithful, meaning that if a map
f\colon M\to N
in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}
is such that
\rho(f)=0
, then
f
is tensor nilpotent, that is to say,
f^{\otimes n}=0
for some
n\gg 0
.

As noted before,
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{G}
is a full subcategory of
\mathcal{T}_{G}
, and this inclusion is compatible with tensor products; ditto for the
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}{\mathcal{T}}_{E}
. Moreover the functor
\rho
is induced by the restriction functor, that we also denote
\rho
, on the whole stable module category:

\rho\colon{\mathcal{T}}_{G}\longrightarrow\prod_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)}{\mathcal{T}}_{E}\,.

Thus it suffices to verify that this functor is nil faithful.

Let
f\colon M\to N
be a morphism in
\mathcal{T}_{G}
such that
\rho_{E}(f)=0
for
E\in\mathcal{A}(G)
. A standard argument allows one to assume
M=k
. This goes as follows:
f
corresponds to a map
f^{\prime}\colon k\to\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,N)
and
\rho_{E}(f)=0
if and only if
\rho_{E}(f^{\prime})=0
, since

\rho_{E}k=k\quad\text{and}\quad\rho_{E}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(\rho_{E}M,\rho_{E}N)\,.

Also, if
f^{\prime}
is tensor nilpotent, then so is
f
, because the composition of maps

k\xrightarrow{(f^{\prime})^{\otimes n}}\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M,N)^{\otimes n}\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}_{k}(M^{\otimes n},N^{\otimes n})

assigns
1
to
f^{\otimes n}
. Thus we can suppose that
M=k
. Since
k
is compact in
\mathcal{T}_{G}
, to verify that
f
is tensor nilpotent it suffices to verify that
\rho
is faithful on objects, that is to say, for
X
in
\mathcal{T}_{G}
, if
\rho_{E}X=0
for each
E\in\mathcal{A}(G)
, then
X=0
; see, for instance,
[
14
, Lemma 6.2]
. It remains to recall that Chouinard
[
19
, Theorem 1]
proved the faithfulness of
\rho
.
∎

Lemma 5.8
.

The map
(3)
in diagram (
5.5
) is a homeomorphism.

Proof.

By Quillen’s theorem
[
26
, Theorem 6.2]
we have a uniform
F
-isomorphism

H^{*}(G,k)\longrightarrow\lim_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)}H^{*}(E,k).

By this one means that the kernel of the map is nilpotent and that there exists an integer
n
such that for any
\xi\in H^{*}(E,k)
, the element
\xi^{p^{n}}
is in the image of the map. Localising at
\mathfrak{p}
and then completing at
\mathfrak{p}
, and keeping in mind that the limit is over a finite diagram, yields an
F
-isomorphism

H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow\lim_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}H^{*}(E,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}.

Here we have used the fact that the map
H^{*}(G,k)\to H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
is flat, and that for any finitely generated
H^{*}(G,k)
-module
M
, there is a natural isomorphism

H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\otimes_{H^{*}(G,k)}M\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }M{}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

The limit is now over the subcategory
\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}
of
\mathcal{A}(G)
, for
H^{*}(E,k)_{\mathfrak{p}}=0
when
E
is not in this subcategory. Applying
\operatorname{Spec}(-)
yields the isomorphism on the right:

\underset{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}{\operatorname{colim}}\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(E,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}\lim_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}H^{*}(E,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{Spec}H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\,.

The isomorphism on the left holds because
\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a finite category, and the rings
H^{*}(E,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
are all finite over a fixed ring, namely
H^{*}(G,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}
; see
[
27
, Lemma 8.11]
. This is the desired statement.
∎

At this point we have completed the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

Quillen stratification for local dualisable modules

Fix
\mathfrak{p}
in
\operatorname{Proj}H^{*}(G,k)
. In the course of the proof of Lemma
5.8
we established a version of the Quillen stratification theorem for the spectrum of
\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{G}}(\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}k)
, the cohomology of the unit in the category of local dualisable modules. This is a formal consequence of the classical Quillen stratification theorem. There is an analogue of this result for arbitrary dualisable modules, described below; this requires more work.

Let
M
be a dualisable module in
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{G}
. For any subgroup
H\leq G
write
I_{H}(M)
for the kernel of the natural map

R_{H}\coloneqq H^{*}(H,k){}^{{}^{\wedge}}_{\mathfrak{p}}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}^{*}_{\mathcal{T}_{H}}(M{\downarrow_{H}})\,.

Set
\operatorname{supp}_{R_{H}}(M)\coloneqq V(I_{H}(M))
; this is the cohomological support of
M{\downarrow_{H}}
, which is a dualisable object of
\varGamma_{\mathfrak{p}}\mathcal{T}_{H}
. Since the map above is compatible with restrictions to subgroups the
F
-isomorphism

R_{G}\longrightarrow\lim_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}R_{E}\,.

established above, yields a map of rings

(5.9)

R_{G}/I_{G}(M)\longrightarrow\lim_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}R_{E}/I_{E}(M)

with the property that a
p
-power of any element of the target is in the image of the map; it also has a nilpotent kernel because the map
\rho
in (
5.7
) is nil faithful. Thus we conclude that the map above is an
F
-isomorphism.
Passing to spectra, yields the following result.

Theorem 5.10
.

The map (
5.9
) induces a homeomorphism of supports:

\operatorname{colim}_{E\in\mathcal{A}(G)_{\mathfrak{p}}}\operatorname{supp}_{R_{E}}(M{\downarrow_{E}})\xrightarrow{\ \raisebox{-1.20552pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\scriptstyle{\sim}$}\ }\operatorname{supp}_{R_{G}}(M)\,.\qed

This is an analogue, for local dualisable modules, of the Quillen stratification for the cohomology of finite dimensional modules proved by Avrunin and Scott
[
2
, Theorem 3.3]
.

1.
Introduction

Let
T
be a complete o-minimal
L
-theory expanding the theory
\mathrm{RCF}
of real closed fields in some language
L
containing the language of ordered rings. There has been significant work on this class of theories; for example
T
could be the theory
T_{\exp}
of the field of reals expanded by the natural exponential function (
[
15
]
) or the theory
T_{an,\exp}
of the reals expanded by the natural exponential and all restricted analytic functions (
[
11
]
).

Recall from
[
10
, (2.7)]
, that a
T
-convex
valuation subring
\mathcal{O}
of a model
\mathbb{E}\models T
is a convex subring closed under all continuous
\emptyset
-definable functions
f:\mathbb{E}\to\mathbb{E}
.

By
[
10
, (3.13) and (3.14)]
, the common theory
T_{\mathrm{convex}}
in the language
L_{\mathrm{convex}}\coloneqq L\cup\{\mathcal{O}\}
of all models of
T
expanded by a unary predicate
\mathcal{O}
interpreted as a non-trivial
T
-convex valuation ring, is complete and weakly o-minimal.

The results in
[
10
, (2.12)]
,
[
13
, Thm. B]
, and
[
9
, Ch. 12 and 13]
entail that if
T
is power bounded with field of exponents
\Lambda
, then for every elementary extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\models T_{\mathrm{convex}}
one can find a
\dcl_{T}
-basis of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
of the form
(\overline{r},\overline{v},\overline{e})
where:

(1)

\overline{r}
is a tuple in
\mathcal{O}_{*}
such that the projection
\res(\overline{r})
of
\overline{r}
in the residue field
\res(\mathbb{E}_{*})
is
\dcl_{T}
-independent over the residue field
\res(\mathbb{E})
of
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
;

(2)

\overline{v}
is a tuple in
\mathbb{E}_{*}
such that
\val(\overline{v})
is
\Lambda
-linearly independent over the value group
\val(\mathbb{E})
of
\mathbb{E}
;

(3)

(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is an immediate extension of
(\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{r},\overline{v}\rangle,\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{r},\overline{v}\rangle)
, where
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{r},\overline{v}\rangle\coloneqq\dcl(\mathbb{E},\overline{r},\overline{v})
.

Even when
T
is not power bounded, we will call tuples like
\overline{r}
in item (1) above
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-constructions
over
\mathbb{E}
(Definition
14
). In this paper we will be concerned with
res-constructible
elementary extensions of models of
T_{\mathrm{convex}}
, that is, elementary extensions
(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\succeq(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
such that for some
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction
\overline{r}
over
\mathbb{E}
,
\mathbb{E}_{*}=\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{r}\rangle
.

Specific instances of res-constructible extensions were considered in
[
8
]
: more precisely, a res-constructible extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
such that the underlying residue field extension
\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq\res(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is a Cauchy-completion, is exactly a
pseudo-completion
of
\mathbb{E}
with respect to
\{\mathcal{O}\}
in the sense of
[
8
, Def. 4.2]
.

After showing that pseudo-completions are unique up to isomorphisms but admit non-surjective self-embeddings, Tressl in
[
8
, Problem 5.12]
asks whether given
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}^{\prime})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
such that
\mathbb{E}_{*}
and
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
are pseudo-completions of
\mathbb{E}
with respect to
\{\mathcal{O}\}
, one has that necessarily
\mathbb{E}_{1}
is also a pseudo-completion of
\mathbb{E}
with respect to
\{\mathcal{O}\}
.

In this paper we will study the more general question of whether res-constructible extensions are closed under taking right-factors, i.e. whether (or rather more appropriately “when”) given a composition of elementary extensions
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
of models of
T
-convex such that the composite extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is res-constructible, one can deduce that
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
is also res-constructible.

We show that in general res-constructible extensions are not closed under right factors and give a complete characterization of those res-constructible extensions
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
all of whose right-factors
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
are res-constructible.

Theorem A
.

Suppose
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is res-constructible. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\dim_{\dcl}(\mathbb{E}_{*}/\mathbb{E})
is countable or the value group
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
of
(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is short (i.e. contains no uncountable well order, cf
[
7
, p. 88]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
);

(2)

for all
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
, the extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
is res-constructible;

(3)

for all
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
, with
\res(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})=\res(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
, the extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
is res-constructible.

This implies that the answer to
[
8
, Problem 5.12]
is affirmative if and only if the value group
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
does not contain uncountable well orders or
\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
has a Cauchy-completion of countable
\dcl
-dimension (Section
4
).

We will now spend some words about the proof of the main implication
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
of Theorem
A
.

It is not to hard to show that if
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is res-constructible, then for any intermediate
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{1}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{*}
, such that
\dim_{\dcl}(\mathbb{E}_{1}/\mathbb{E})<\aleph_{0}
, one has that
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}_{1})
and
(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}_{1})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
are both res-constructible extensions (Corollary
21
).
This easily entails that also if
\dim_{\dcl}(\mathbb{E}_{1}/\mathbb{E})=\aleph_{0}
, one has that
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}_{1})
is res-constructible however it is also not hard to show that
(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}_{1})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
doesn’t need to be res-constructible anymore.

We will prove
(1)\Rightarrow(2)
of Theorem
A
by building a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction
\overline{s}
for
\mathbb{E}_{1}
over
\mathbb{E}
as an ordinal indexed sequence
\overline{s}\coloneqq(s_{i}:i<\lambda)
. By the already mentioned Corollary
21
about factors with finite dimension, the main problem in the transfinite construction of
\overline{s}
is at limit steps: in fact it may happen that even if
\mathbb{E}_{1}
was res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}
, for some
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction, say
(s_{i}:i<\omega)
, it is not anymore res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}\langle s_{i}:i<\omega\rangle
. This difficulty will be overcome by setting up the induction so that at each limit step the res-construction
(s_{i}:i<\omega\cdot\alpha)
satisfies both a model theoretic orthogonality condition and a closure condition (Definition
25
) relative to a given
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction
\overline{r}
of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
.

Subsequent to proving Theorem A, in Section
4
, we will turn to
[
8
, Problem 5.12]
, and give a complete answer, dependent on the properties of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
.
We will also show that in the power-bounded setting, this dependence can be reduced to considering properties of
\mathbb{E}
. Then, in Section
5
, we will discuss alternative characterisations of res-constructibility, and construct an example showing that res-constructibility is not a ‘local’ property.

Preliminaries and notation

In the following
T\supseteq\mathrm{RCF}
will be some fixed complete o-minimal theory in some language
L
containing the language of ordered rings. If
\mathbb{E}\models T
and
x
is a tuple or a set in some
\mathbb{U}\succ\mathbb{E}
, we will denote the definable closure
\dcl_{T}(\mathbb{E}\cup x)
by
\mathbb{E}\langle x\rangle
. Recall that this is still a model of
T
because
T
has definable Skolem functions.
Also, for a subset
S\subseteq\mathbb{E}
, we will denote the convex hull of
S
in
\mathbb{U}
by
\CH_{\mathbb{U}}(S)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathbb{U}:\exists s_{1},s_{2}\in S,\;s_{1}\leq x\leq s_{2}\}
. Finally, if
R\subseteq X\times X
is a binary relation on a set
X
, given subsets
A,B\subseteq X
, we will write
A^{RB}
for the set
A^{RB}\coloneqq\{x\in A:\forall b\in B,\,xRb\}
; if
B=\{b\}
is a singleton we will write
A^{Rb}
for
A^{R\{b\}}
. Usually
R
will be an order relation or equality.

2.1.
Valuation-related notation.

Given a field
\mathbb{E}
and a valuation ring
\mathcal{O}
on
\mathbb{E}
, we will denote by
\mathfrak{o}
the unique maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}
.
The
value group
will be denoted by
(\val(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O}),+)\coloneqq(\mathbb{E}^{\neq 0},\cdot)/(\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathfrak{o})
or by
\val_{\mathcal{O}}\mathbb{E}
.
The
valuation
will be the quotient
\val_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathbb{E}^{\neq 0}\to\val(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
, and will be extended to the whole
\mathbb{E}
by setting
\val(0)=\infty
. The value group will be ordered as usual by setting
\val(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})^{\geq 0}\coloneqq\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathcal{O}^{\neq 0})
.

The associated
dominance
,
weak asymptotic equivalence
,
strict dominance
, and
strict asymptotic equivalence
on
\mathbb{E}
will be denoted respectively by:

\displaystyle x\preceq y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\val_{\mathcal{O}}(x)\geq\val_{\mathcal{O}}(y)\Leftrightarrow x/y\in\mathcal{O};

\displaystyle x\asymp y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\val_{\mathcal{O}}(x)=\val_{\mathcal{O}}(y)\Leftrightarrow x/y\in\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathfrak{o};

\displaystyle x\prec y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow\val_{\mathcal{O}}(x)>\val_{\mathcal{O}}(y)\Leftrightarrow x/y\in\mathfrak{o};

\displaystyle x\sim y

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow x-y\prec x\Leftrightarrow 1-x/y\in\mathfrak{o}.

The imaginary sort given by the quotient under the strict asymptotic equivalence
\sim
will be called
rv-sort
and denoted by
\rv(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\coloneqq(\mathbb{E}^{\neq 0},\cdot)/(1+\mathfrak{o})
or
\rv_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
, the quotient map will be denoted by
\rv_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathbb{E}^{\neq 0}\to\rv(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
.

Finally the
residue field
sort will be denoted by
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\coloneqq\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{o}
and the corresponding quotient map by
\res_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathcal{O}\to\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
. We extend
\res_{\mathcal{O}}
to a total function
\res_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathbb{E}\to\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
by setting
\res_{\mathcal{O}}
to be
0
outside of
\mathcal{O}
.

The subscript
\mathcal{O}
will be omitted when this does not create ambiguity.

3.1.
Res-constructible extensions

We recall the definition of res-constructible extension from the introduction and give some of its basic properties.

Definition 14
.

Let
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{*}\models T
and let
\mathcal{O}_{*}
be a non-trivial
T
-convex subring of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. A possibly infinite tuple
\overline{y}\coloneqq(y_{i}:i\in I)
in
\mathcal{O}_{*}
will be called a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction
over
\mathbb{E}
if
\big(\res_{\mathcal{O}_{*}}(y_{i}):i\in I\big)
is
\dcl_{T}
-independent over
\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
. We then call the extension
\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:i\in I\rangle
of
\mathbb{E}

\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-constructible
over
\mathbb{E}
(by the res-construction
(y_{i}:i\in I)
). When
\mathcal{O}_{*}
is clear from the context we will just say
res-construction
and
res-constructible
.

We call an extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\models T_{\mathrm{convex}}

residually constructible
(
res-constructible
for short) when
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res constructible over
\mathbb{E}
.

Remark 15
.

Notice that if
\overline{y}\coloneqq(y_{i}:i\in I)
is a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction over
\mathbb{E}
, then it is in particular
\dcl_{T}
-independent over
\mathbb{E}
. This is because by Fact
9
, for all finite
F\subseteq I
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:i\in F\rangle)=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})\langle\res(y_{i}):i\in F\rangle
.

Remark 16
.

Res-constructible extension are transitive (i.e. closed under composition): if
(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
and
(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
are res-constructible, then
(\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathcal{O}_{0})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
is res-constructible as well.

Remark 17
.

In the same setting of Definition
14
, let
\lambda
be an ordinal, and
(y_{i}:i<\lambda)
a
\lambda
-indexed sequence in
\mathcal{O}_{*}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

(y_{i}:i<\lambda)
is a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction over
\mathbb{E}
;

(2)

for all
i<\lambda
, we have
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{*}}(y_{i})\in\res(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:j<i+1\rangle)\setminus\res_{\mathcal{O}_{*}}(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{j}:j<i\rangle)
.

In particular, by Fact
9
, for an extension
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
to be res-constructible, it is enough that for some
\dcl_{T}
-basis
B
of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
, there is an ordinal indexing
(b_{i}:i<\alpha)
of
B
such that for all
i<\alpha
, one has
\res(\mathbb{E}\langle b_{j}:j<i\rangle)\neq\res(\mathbb{E}\langle b_{j}:j<i+1\rangle)
.

Lemma 18
.

If
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is res-constructible by the ordinal indexed res-construction
(y_{i}:i<\lambda)
, and
\mathbb{K}
is an elementary residue section of
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
, then
\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle
is an elementary residue section of
(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
.

Proof.

Notice that by Fact
7
(3) it suffices to show that
\res(\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
.
Proceed by induction on
\lambda
. Let
\lambda=\beta+1
and suppose the thesis holds for
\beta
, so assume that
\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle
is an elementary a residue section of
(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:i<\beta\rangle,\mathcal{O}_{*}\cap\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:i<\beta\rangle)
. Since by Fact
9
we must then have
\res(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:j<\lambda\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:j<\beta\rangle)\langle\res(y_{\beta})\rangle
, we deduce by Fact
7
that
\res(\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:j<\lambda\rangle)
.

Suppose instead that
\lambda
is a limit ordinal and the thesis holds for each
\beta<\lambda
. If
x\in\mathbb{E}_{*}
, then since
\lambda
is a limit ordinal, there is
\beta<\lambda
such that
x\in\mathbb{E}\langle y_{i}:i<\beta\rangle
and hence by inductive hypothesis
\res(x)\in\res(\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\beta\rangle)\subseteq\res(\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle)
.
∎

The isomorphism type of a res-constructible extension only depends on the isomorphism type of the residue field extension
\res(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\succeq\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
.

Lemma 19
.

If
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
and
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
are res-constructible extensions and
\varphi:\res(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\to\res(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
is an isomorphism of models of
T
over
\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
, then there is an isomorphism
\psi:(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\to(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
over
\mathbb{E}
such that
\res(\psi)=\varphi
.

Proof.

Let
\mathbb{K}
be an elementary residue-section of
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
and
(y_{i}:i<\lambda)
,
(z_{i}:i<\mu)
be res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{1}
and
\mathbb{E}_{2}
. Thus
\mathbb{E}=\mathbb{K}\langle\overline{d}\rangle
where
\overline{d}
is a tuple such that
\tp(\overline{d}/\mathbb{K})
is definable.
By Lemma
18
,
\mathbb{K}_{1}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}\langle y_{i}:i<\lambda\rangle
and
\mathbb{K}_{2}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}\langle z_{i}:i<\mu\rangle
are elementary residue sections for
(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})
and
(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
respectively and moreover
\mathbb{E}_{1}=\mathbb{K}_{1}\langle\overline{d}\rangle
and
\mathbb{E}_{2}=\mathbb{K}_{2}\langle\overline{d}\rangle
.
Now observe that
\varphi
induces an isomorphism
h:\mathbb{K}_{1}\to\mathbb{K}_{2}
over
\mathbb{K}
. Finally notice that for both
i=1
and
i=2
,
\tp(\overline{d}/\mathbb{K}_{i})
is the unique definable extension of
\tp(\overline{d}/\mathbb{K})
to
\mathbb{K}_{i}
, in particular
h_{*}\tp(\overline{d}/\mathbb{K}_{1})=\tp(\overline{d}/\mathbb{K}_{2})
. Thus
h
extends to the sought isomorphism
\psi:(\mathbb{E}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{1})\to(\mathbb{E}_{2},\mathcal{O}_{2})
over
\mathbb{E}
.
∎

Res-constructible extension only contain residually generated elements.

Lemma 20
.

If
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\preceq(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is a res-constructible extension then every
x\in\mathbb{E}_{*}\setminus\mathbb{E}
is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}
.

Proof.

Let
(r_{i}:i<\alpha)
be an ordinal-indexed res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
. Let
j
be minimal such that
\tp(x/\mathbb{E})
is realized in
\mathbb{E}\langle r_{i}:i<j+1\rangle
. Thus there is
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}\langle r_{i}:i<j+1\rangle\setminus\mathbb{E}\langle r_{i}:i<j\rangle
such that
\tp(x^{\prime}/\mathbb{E})=\tp(x/\mathbb{E})
. Now let
F\subseteq j
be such that
x^{\prime}\in\mathbb{E}\langle r_{i}:i\in F\cup\{j\}\rangle
. By Fact
9
we then have

\res(\mathbb{E}\langle x^{\prime},r_{i}:i\in F\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E}\langle r_{i}:i\in F\rangle)\langle\res(r_{j})\rangle.

In particular
\dim_{\dcl}(\res(\mathbb{E}\langle x^{\prime},r_{i}:i\in F\rangle)/\res(\mathbb{E}))=|F|+1
.
Suppose toward contradiction that
x^{\prime}
was not residually generated over
\mathbb{E}
, i.e. that
\res(\mathbb{E}\langle x^{\prime}\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E})
, then, again by Fact
9
, we would have
\dim_{\dcl}(\res(\mathbb{E}\langle x^{\prime},r_{i}:i<F\rangle/\res(\mathbb{E}))\leq|F|
, contradiction. Thus
x^{\prime}
is residually generated over
\mathbb{E}
and so also
x
is, because
\tp(x/\mathbb{E})=\tp(x^{\prime}/\mathbb{E})
.
∎

Corollary 21
.

If
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\prec(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is a res-constructible extension and
\overline{y}
is a finite tuple, then
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{y}\rangle
and
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{y}\rangle
is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the case in which
\overline{y}=(y_{0})
is a single element of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. If
y_{0}\notin\mathbb{E}
, then by Lemma
20
,
\mathbb{E}\langle y_{0}\rangle
is res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}
, say by some
r\in\mathbb{E}\langle y_{0}\rangle
, such that
\res(r)\notin\res(\mathbb{E})
. Let
\mathbb{K}
be an elementary residue section of
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
and let
\mathbb{K}_{*}
an extension of
\mathbb{K}
to an elementary residue section of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{*}=\dcl(\mathbb{E}\cup\mathbb{K}_{*})
, which exists by Lemma
18
.
Now if
\overline{s}
is a
\dcl_{T}
-basis of
\mathbb{K}_{*}
over
\mathbb{K}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(r)\rangle
, then
\overline{s}
is a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}\langle r\rangle=\mathbb{E}\langle y_{0}\rangle
.
In fact
\res(r,\overline{s})=\res(\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(r),\overline{s})
is
\dcl
-independent over
\res(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
; and since then
r\notin\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{s}\rangle
, we also have by the exchange property that
\mathbb{E}\langle r,\overline{s}\rangle=\mathbb{E}_{*}
.
∎

Lemma 22
.

Let
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\models T_{\mathrm{convex}}
, and let
x
be an element of an elementary extension
(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
, such that
x
is not residually generated over
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
. If
\val(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
does not contain any uncountable ordinal, then there is a countable
S\subseteq\mathbb{E}
, such that
\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(x/S)\vdash\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(x/\mathbb{E})
.

Proof.

To show that an
x\notin\mathbb{E}
is such that
\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(x/S)\vdash\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(x/\mathbb{E})
for some countable
S\subseteq\mathbb{E}
, it is enough to show that each of
\mathbb{E}^{<x}
and
\mathbb{E}^{<-x}
either has countable cofinality, or is of the form
\{c\pm y:y\in\mathbb{E},y\leq 0\text{ or }\val(y)\geq v\}
for some
c\in\mathbb{E}
,
v\in\val(\mathbb{E})
, and some choice of
\pm
. By symmetry, it suffices to check
\mathbb{E}^{<x}
.
Now, if
\val(x-\mathbb{E}^{<x})
has no maximum, then it is included in
\val(\mathbb{E})
and its cofinality equals the cofinality of
\mathbb{E}^{<x}
, whence we are done by hypothesis. Suppose therefore that
\val(x-\mathbb{E}^{<x})
has a maximum, and let it be achieved by
c\in\mathbb{E}^{<x}
. Note that since
x
is not residually generated,
\val(x-c)\notin\val(\mathbb{E})
.
Consider
\{\val(y):y\in\mathbb{E},0<y<x-c\}
. If this has no minimum, then it has countable cofinality, so
\mathbb{E}^{<x}=c+\mathbb{E}^{<x-c}
has countable cofinality.
If it has a minimum, say
v
, then, since
v\in\val(\mathbb{E})
,
v>\val(x-c)
.
Thus, for all
y\in\mathbb{E}
, we have that
c+y<x
iff
y\leq 0
or
\val(y)\geq v
, which is precisely the second possibility.
∎

Notice that

and that

Corollary 24

3.2.
Right factors of res-constructible extensions

This subsection is devoted to the proof of Theorem
A
, which will result as a wrap-up of Theorems
35
and
36

(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\succeq(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
of model of
T_{\mathrm{convex}}
and let
\overline{r}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
. We also let
\mathbb{E}_{1}
be an intermediate extension
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{1}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{*}
.

Definition 25
.

Let
\mathbb{K}\preceq_{\mathrm{tame}}\mathbb{E}
be a residue section for
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})
and
\mathbb{K}_{*}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}\langle\overline{r}\rangle
, thus
\mathbb{K}_{*}
is an elementary residue section for
(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})

We will say that a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction
\overline{x}
in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
over
\mathbb{E}
is

(1)

\mathbb{K}_{*}
-orthogonal
in
\mathbb{E}_{1}

\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})/\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle)\vdash\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})/\mathbb{E}_{1})

(2)

\mathbb{K}_{*}
-strong

\mathbb{E}_{1}
(notation

Lemma 26
.

Let
\overline{x}\triangleleft_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}\mathbb{E}_{1}
, and suppose
\psi:(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})\to(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{*})
is an elementary self-embedding fixing
\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})

\psi(\overline{x})\triangleleft_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}\psi(\mathbb{E}_{1})

Proof.

Since
\overline{x}\triangleleft_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}\mathbb{E}_{1}

\overline{x}\subset\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})\rangle
. Thus,
\psi
also fixes
\overline{x}
elementwise. This implies that
\psi(\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}))=\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\psi(\overline{x}))
. Then, by applying the elementarity of
\psi
and the fact that
\overline{x}
is
\mathbb{K}_{*}
-orthogonal in

, we deduce that

\psi(\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}))=\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\psi(\overline{x}))
yields that
\psi(\overline{x})
is
\mathbb{K}_{*}
-orthogonal in
\psi(\mathbb{E}_{1})
. Finally observe that
\mathbb{E}\langle\psi(\overline{x})\rangle=\psi(\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle)=\psi(\mathbb{E}_{1})\cap\psi(\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})\rangle)=\psi(\mathbb{E}_{1})\cap\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\psi(\overline{x}))\rangle
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
\overline{x}
be a
\mathbb{K}_{*}
-orthogonal
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
and
\overline{y}
a
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-construction in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
over
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle
. If

is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}),\overline{y}\rangle
then it is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x},\overline{y}\rangle
.

Proof.

Notice that
\res(\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}),\overline{y}\rangle)=\res(\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x},\overline{y}\rangle)\subseteq\res(\mathbb{E}_{1})
.
Suppose that

is residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}),\overline{y}\rangle

\overline{s}
of
\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x})
and a
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{y}\rangle
-definable function

such that
\res(f(\overline{s},z))\notin\res(\mathbb{E}\langle\std_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}(\overline{x}),\overline{y}\rangle)

However
\res(f(\overline{s},z))=\res(a)
for some

and by

\mathbb{E}_{1}

\tp_{L_{\mathrm{convex}}}(s/\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle)\vdash\res(f(\overline{s},z))=\res(a)
.
But then there is a

-formula
\varphi(\overline{s})
with parameters from
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle
such that
\varphi(\overline{s})\vdash\res(f(\overline{s},z))=\res(a)
. Taking
\overline{s}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle
such that

is
\mathcal{O}_{*}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x},\overline{y}\rangle
.
∎

Lemma 28
.

Let
\overline{x}\triangleleft_{\mathbb{K}_{*}}\mathbb{E}_{1}
and let
\overline{y}
be a finite res-construction in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
over
\mathbb{E}\langle\overline{x}\rangle

\mathcal{O}_{*}
-residually generated over

Let
\overline{r}^{\prime}
be a completion of

-basis of
\mathbb{K}_{*}

Notice that
\psi

over

and that it fixes

is an automorphism. By Lemma

\psi^{-1}(\overline{x})\triangleleft_{\psi^{-1}(\mathbb{K}_{*})}\psi^{-1}(\mathbb{E}_{1})

\mathcal{O}_{*}

Lemma 34
.

Let
(b_{i}:i<\alpha)
be an ordinal-indexed sequence of elements of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
.
Let
\overline{r}
be a res-construction over
\mathbb{E}\langle b_{i}:i<\alpha\rangle

\mathcal{O}_{*}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}

Note that if the hypotheses hold for

If

Suppose thus that

Notice that
\overline{r}
is in particular also an
\mathcal{O}_{*}

Notice that

We recall the definition of pseudo-completion of
[

]

Definition 37
.

We call an elementary extension
\mathbb{K}\preceq\mathbb{K}^{\prime}\models T
,

if
\mathbb{K}
is dense in
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
, we call it

\mathbb{K}
is cofinal in
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
.
Thus a

of some

\mathbb{K}_{*}\succeq\mathbb{K}
which is maximal among the dense elementary extensions

Remark 38
.

In
[
8
, Sec. 3]
, what we call Cauchy-completion is called just

Lemma 39

Suppose that
\mathbb{K}\preceq\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
is a dense extension and suppose that
\mathbb{K}\preceq\mathbb{E}
is a cofinal extension such that for all

is realized in

\mathbb{K}
of
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
in
\mathbb{E}

Proof.

For the uniqueness, it suffices to show that for all

,
\tp(x/\mathbb{K})
has a unique realization in
\mathbb{E}
. If it was not the case and

, were distinct realizations of
\tp(x/\mathbb{K})
then since
\mathbb{K}
is dense in both

and
\mathbb{K}\langle x_{1}\rangle
it would follow that

contradicting the cofinality of
\mathbb{K}
in
\mathbb{E}
.
To prove existence, let
(b_{i}:i<\alpha)
be a
\dcl_{T}
-basis of
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{K}
. To build an embedding of
\mathbb{K}
in to
\mathbb{E}
it suffices to show that for all
\beta<\alpha
, if
(c_{i}:i<\beta)
is a tuple in
\mathbb{E}
with

, then we can find

in
\mathbb{E}
such that
(b_{i}:i\leq\beta)\equiv_{\mathbb{K}}(c_{i}:i\leq\beta)
. This easily follows from the fact that
\tp(b_{\beta}/\mathbb{K})\vdash\tp(b_{\beta}/\mathbb{K}\langle b_{i}:i<\beta\rangle)
because
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
is dense in
\mathbb{K}
.

The following hold:

Lemma 44
.

If
\mathbb{E}\prec\mathbb{E}_{1}
is a dense extension and
\mathcal{O}
is a non-trivial
T
-convex valuation ring of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
, then
\mathbb{E}\not\subseteq\mathcal{O}
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
, and
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
.

Proof.

The claim that
\mathbb{E}\not\subseteq\mathcal{O}
is trivial, because if
\mathbb{E}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, then by density of
\mathbb{E}
in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
it would follow that
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{E}_{1}
.

To prove
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
, suppose toward contradiction that there was an element
x
of
\mathbb{E}_{1}\setminus\mathbb{E}
such that
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(x)\notin\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
. Then there would be no elements of
\mathbb{E}
in
x+\mathfrak{o}
whereas
(x+\mathfrak{o})\cap\mathbb{E}_{1}
would be infinite, thus contradicting the density of
\mathbb{E}
in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
.

Finally to see that
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
notice that since
\mathbb{E}
is dense in
\mathbb{E}_{1}
, then in particular for all
z\in\mathbb{E}_{1}^{>0}
, there is
c\in\mathbb{E}
such that
c\geq z
and
|c-z|/|z|<1
.
∎

Lemma 46
.

Let
\mathbb{E}\prec\mathbb{U}\models T
and
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
be
T
-convex valuation subrings of
\mathbb{U}
such that
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}\neq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{E}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{E}_{1}
is a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-residually dense extension of
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction
\overline{b}
and
\mathbb{E}_{2}
is a
\mathcal{O}
-res-constructible extension of
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}
-res-construction
\overline{c}
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
and
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}\cap(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}))=\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}))
, in particular
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}_{1}
is the unique
T
-convex extension of
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}
;

(2)

\overline{c}
is a
\mathcal{O}
-res-construction of
\dcl(\mathbb{E}_{2}\cup\mathbb{E}_{1})
over
\mathbb{E}_{1}
and
\dcl(\mathbb{E}_{2}\cup\mathbb{E}_{1})
is
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-residually cofinal over
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction
(\overline{b},\overline{c})
;

(3)

\tp_{L}(\mathbb{E}_{1}/\mathbb{E})\vdash\tp_{L}(\mathbb{E}_{1}/\mathbb{E}_{2})
.

Proof.

Throughout the proof let
V\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{O})
so that
\res_{\mathcal{O}}=\res_{V}\circ\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}
.

(1) Since
\mathbb{E}_{1}
is
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-residually dense,
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
is a dense extension of
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})
. Thus by Lemma
44

\res_{V}\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})=\res_{V}\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})
and

\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})=\res_{V}\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})=\res_{V}\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}).

The statement about the value group is proved in the same way using that
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}))=\val_{V}(\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1}))
and Lemma
44
. The last clause is because in particular
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1}\cap(\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\setminus\mathfrak{o}^{\prime}))=\val_{V}(\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1}))
and
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
imply that there is no
x\in\mathbb{E}_{1}
such that
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}<x<\mathbb{E}^{>\mathcal{O}}
.

(2) The first assertion is by point (1) and Remark
45
. As for the second one, notice that by Lemma
23
it follows from the first assertion that
(\overline{b},\overline{c})
is a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction over
\mathbb{E}
, because
\overline{b}
was a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction over
\mathbb{E}
. Finally, to see that
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})
is cofinal in
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\dcl(\mathbb{E}_{2}\cup\mathbb{E}_{1}))
, recall that
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})
is dense in
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
by hypothesis and that
\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})\prec\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\dcl(\mathbb{E}_{2}\cup\mathbb{E}_{1}))
is a
V
-res-constructible extension with
V\not\supseteq\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{1})
and thus in particular it is cofinal by Lemma
42
.

(3) Let
\overline{b}
be a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction of
\mathbb{E}_{1}
over
\mathbb{E}
.
Then it suffices to show that for any finite tuple
b^{\prime}_{0},\ldots b^{\prime}_{n}\subset\overline{b}
,
\tp(b^{\prime}_{n}/\mathbb{E}\langle b^{\prime}_{0},\ldots b^{\prime}_{n-1}\rangle)\vdash\tp(b^{\prime}_{n}/\mathbb{E}_{2}\langle b^{\prime}_{0},\ldots b^{\prime}_{n-1}\rangle)
.

For this notice that by Remark
45
and point (1), we have that
\mathbb{E}_{2}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
is
\mathcal{O}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
. Thus by Lemma
20
, every element of
\mathbb{E}_{2}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
is
\mathcal{O}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
.

Now by point (1),
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n}^{\prime}\rangle)=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle)=\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
, thus
b_{n}^{\prime}
is not
\mathcal{O}
-residually generated over
\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
.
Moreover
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n}^{\prime}\rangle
is the unique extension of
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
, so
\tp_{L}(b_{n}^{\prime}/\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle)
implies the
L_{\mathrm{convex}}
-type of
b_{n}^{\prime}
over
(\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle,\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle)
.
It follows that
\tp_{L}(b_{n}^{\prime}/\mathbb{E}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle)
is not realized in
\mathbb{E}_{2}\langle b_{0}^{\prime},\ldots,b_{n-1}^{\prime}\rangle
and we are done.
∎

Corollary 47
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{U}\models T
and let
\{\mathcal{O}_{i}:i\in I\}
be a family of
T
-convex subrings of
\mathbb{U}
such that for all
i\neq j
,
\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathbb{E}\neq\mathcal{O}_{j}\cap\mathbb{E}
. Let also for each
i\in I
,
\mathbb{E}_{i}\preceq\mathbb{U}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{i}
-residually dense extension of
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}_{i}
-res-construction
\overline{x}^{(i)}
. Then, setting
\mathcal{O}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathcal{O}_{i}
:

(1)

(x^{(i)}_{n}:i\in I,\,n<|\overline{x}^{(i)}|)
is a
\mathcal{O}
-res-construction over
\mathbb{E}
and
\dcl(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}_{i})
is
\mathcal{O}
-residually cofinal over
\mathbb{E}
;

(2)

\bigcup_{i\in I}\tp_{L}(\mathbb{E}_{i}/\mathbb{E})\vdash\tp_{L}(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}_{i}/\mathbb{E})
;

Proof.

We first prove the statement with the additional hypothesis that
I
is finite.
If
I
is empty there is nothing to show, otherwise let
j\in I
be such that
\mathcal{O}_{j}\cap\mathbb{E}\supsetneq\mathcal{O}_{i}\cap\mathbb{E}
for all
i\in I\setminus\{j\}
.
By induction we may assume that the statement holds for
I^{\prime}=I\setminus\{j\}
. So
\bigcup_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}\tp(\mathbb{E}_{i}/\mathbb{E})\vdash\tp\big((\bigcup_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}\mathbb{E}_{i})/\mathbb{E}\big)
and, with
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\coloneqq\bigcup_{i\neq j}\mathcal{O}_{i}
,
\dcl(\bigcup_{i\neq j}\mathbb{E}_{i})
is
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-residually cofinal over
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-res-construction
(x^{(i)}_{n}:i\in I\setminus\{j\},\,n<|\overline{x}^{(i)}|)
.

But now by Lemma
46
(3) and (2) respectively, it follows that

\tp(\mathbb{E}_{j}/\mathbb{E})\cup\tp\big((\bigcup_{i\in I\setminus\{j\}}\mathbb{E}_{i})/\mathbb{E}\big)\vdash\tp(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}_{i}/\mathbb{E})

and that
\dcl(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}_{i})
is
\mathcal{O}_{j}
-residually cofinal over
\mathbb{E}
with
\mathcal{O}_{j}
-res-construction
(x^{(i)}_{n}:i\in I,\,n<|\overline{x}^{(i)}|)
.

Now suppose
I
is infinite. Then (2) follows from a standard compactness argument. As for (1), observe that
(\res_{\mathcal{O}}(x^{(i)}_{n}):i\in I,\,n<|\overline{x}^{(i)}|)
is
\dcl_{T}
-independent by Lemma
23
and by finite character of
\dcl_{T}
. Similarly
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\dcl(\bigcup_{i\in I}\mathbb{E}_{i}))
must be a cofinal extension of
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
.
∎

Proposition 54
.

Let
\mathbb{E}\prec\mathbb{U}\models T
and
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{O}_{i}:0\leq i\leq n\}
be strictly increasing sequence of
T
-convex valuation rings of
\mathbb{U}
such that
\mathbb{E}\cap\mathcal{O}_{i}\neq\mathbb{E}\cap\mathcal{O}_{j}
for all
i\neq j
and
\mathcal{O}_{n}\supseteq\mathbb{E}
.
For each
i\leq n
, let
\mathbb{E}_{i}
be a
\mathcal{O}_{i}
-residually dense extension of
\mathbb{E}
and let
\mathbb{E}_{*}\coloneqq\dcl(\bigcup_{i\leq n}\mathbb{E}_{i})
be
\mathcal{F}
-residually dense over
\mathbb{E}
. Moreover, for each cofinal extension
\mathbb{K}\preceq\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
of models of
T
, denote by
d(\mathbb{K},\mathbb{K}^{\prime})
the
\dcl_{T}
-dimension of
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
over the maximal dense extension of
\mathbb{K}
within
\mathbb{K}^{\prime}
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

if
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}\preceq\mathbb{E}_{*}
and for all
i\leq n
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
contains
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{i})
, then
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
;

(2)

for all
0\leq i<n
, we have
d(\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i+1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}),\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i+1}}(\mathbb{E}))\leq\aleph_{0}
or
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{i})
does not contain an uncountable well ordered subset.

Proof.

(2)\Rightarrow(1)
. We induct on
n
.
Notice that since
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is a cofinal extension of
\mathbb{E}
, it follows that
\mathbb{E}_{*}\subset\mathcal{O}_{n}
. Then, the hypothesis
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n}}(\mathbb{E}_{n})\subset\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
directly implies that
\mathbb{E}_{n}\subset\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
.
So if
n=0
we are done, and we can assume
n>0
.

By repeatedly applying Lemma
46
(2), for all
0\leq i<n
we know that
\dcl(\mathbb{E}_{n},\mathbb{E}_{0},\mathbb{E}_{2},\cdots\mathbb{E}_{i})
is
\mathcal{O}_{i}
-residually cofinal over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
so in particular
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
. Now since
\dim_{\dcl}(\mathbb{E}_{*}/\mathbb{E}_{n})\leq\aleph_{0}
or
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
does not contain uncountable well ordered sets, by Theorem
35
, it follows that
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
is also
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
.

Since by Lemma
19
the isomorphism type of
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
only depends on the extension
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})\succeq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{n})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E})
, it suffices to show that
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
over
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E})
. Now let for all
i<n
,
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
and observe that for all
i<n
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
, and there is a natural inclusion
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime})\subseteq\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
.
Thus we can apply the inductive hypothesis to conclude that
\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
.

(\lnot 2)\Rightarrow(\lnot 1)
. Again we proceed by induction on
n
. Notice that if
n=0
, then (2) always holds, so we can assume
n>0
. Thus let
i<n
be the maximum
i
such that
d(\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i+1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}),\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i+1}}(\mathbb{E}))>\aleph_{0}
and
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{i})
contains an uncountable well ordered subset.
If
i=n-1
, then by Theorem
36
, we can find
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
as in (1) which is not
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}
-constructible over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
. But then, if
\phi:\mathbb{E}_{*}\rightarrow\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
is an isomorphism over
\mathbb{E}
, then
\phi
must fix
\mathbb{E}_{n}
.
So if
\overline{x}
is a res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
, then
\phi(\overline{x})
is a res-construction for
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
, a contradiction.
If instead
i<n-1
, then
\dim_{\dcl}(\mathbb{E}_{*}/\mathbb{E}_{n})\leq\aleph_{0}
or
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{n-1})
does not contain an uncountable well ordered set, so by Theorem
35
any
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
as in (1) is
\mathcal{O}_{n-1}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
, and we can reduce to the inductive hypothesis. In fact we then have that the isomorphism type of
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
over
\mathbb{E}_{n}
is determined by the isomorphism type of
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
over
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{n})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E})
and that, setting as before
\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathcal{O}_{i})
:

•

For any
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}^{\prime})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E})
and the cokernel of the natural inclusion
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime})\subseteq\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
is
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
which does not contain uncountable well orders by hypothesis, so
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime})
contains an uncountable well order if and only if
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
does.

Thus, by induction there exists some
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}\prec\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime}
, such that for all
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\prime})\subset\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime})
, and which is non-isomorphic to
\mathbb{E}_{*}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
. Let
\overline{x}
be a tuple in
\mathbb{E}_{*}
such that
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\overline{x})
is a
\dcl
-basis for
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{E}^{\prime}
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}=\mathbb{E}_{n}\langle\overline{x}\rangle
. Then, for any
0\leq i\leq n-1
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime})\supset\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{i}^{\prime})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{i})
, and
\mathbb{E}_{n}\subset\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})
, so
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
satisfies the conditions of (1). Finally,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{\dagger})=\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}^{\prime}
is not isomorphic to
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
over
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{n-1}}(\mathbb{E})
, so in particular
\mathbb{E}_{\dagger}
is not isomorphic to
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
.
∎

Remark 55
.

Notice that in Proposition
54
, the hypothesis that
\mathcal{O}_{n}\supseteq\mathbb{E}
is not essential, as we can always assume that
\mathbb{E}_{n}
is the trivial extension.
Also notice that for all
i
,
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{i})
is going to be a maximal dense extension of
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E})
in
\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})=\res_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}\dcl(\bigcup_{j\leq i}\mathbb{E}_{j})
. Thus

In particular in (2) of Proposition

, if there is

such that

then for all

, it follows that

.

It is natural to ask whether the conditions on
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
in point (2) of Proposition

, can be substituted with conditions on
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
, given that
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is a very “narrow” extension of

. We answer this conditionally to some properties of

.

Lemma 56
.

Let

be power bounded,
\mathbb{E}\prec\mathbb{U}\models T
, and
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
be
T
-convex valuation subrings of
\mathbb{U}
. If
\mathbb{E}_{*}\subseteq\mathbb{U}
is a
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
-residually dense extension of
\mathbb{E}
, then
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})\cong\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
.

Proof.

When
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, then by Lemma
23
, it follows that
\mathbb{E}_{*}
is
\mathcal{O}
-res-constructible over
\mathbb{E}
and the thesis follows from the rv-property (here Fact

).
Suppose instead
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\supsetneq\mathcal{O}
.
Let
\mathbb{K}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})
,
\mathbb{K}_{*}\coloneqq\res_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
,

.
The inclusion
\val_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})\subseteq\val_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
induces a morphism of short exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\val_{V}(\mathbb{K})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\val_{\mathcal{O}^{\prime}}(\mathbb{E})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\val_{V}(\mathbb{K}_{*})}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}

Now since

, by Lemma

.

Let

, and
\mathcal{F}

-convex valuation rings of

Remark 58
.

When
T
is not power-bounded, for a model
(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O})\models T_{\mathrm{convex}}
, we have that
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
contains an uncountable well ordered subset if and only if
\mathbb{E}
itself contains an uncountable well ordered subset.

In fact if
\mathbb{E}
contains an uncountable well ordered set, then either
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
or
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
must contain an uncountable well ordered set (argue as in Lemma
22
). On the other hand if
\mathbb{E}
defines an exponential and
\res_{\mathcal{O}}(\mathbb{E})
contains an uncountable well ordered set
S
and
d\in\mathbb{E}^{>\mathcal{O}}
, then
\val_{\mathcal{O}}(\exp(Sd))
is an uncountable well ordered set.

Thus, in view of Remark
58
, when
T
is not power-bounded, to see whether the condition that
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E})
does not contain any uncountable well ordered set implies that
\val_{\mathcal{O}_{i}}(\mathbb{E}_{*})
does not contain any uncountable well ordered set we can just ask the same question about
\mathbb{E}
and
\mathbb{E}_{*}
. This leads to consider the following property.

Definition 59
.

We say that a complete o-minimal theory
T
has
property C
if for every
\mathbb{E}\models T
, if
\mathbb{E}
does not contain any uncountable well ordered set, then so does any
1
-
\dcl_{T}
-dimensional extension
\mathbb{E}\langle x\rangle
of
\mathbb{E}
.

Lemma 60
.

Let
\mathbb{E}\preceq\mathbb{U}\models T
,
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
T
-convex subrings of
\mathbb{U}
such that
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathbb{E}\neq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{E}
for all
\mathcal{O}\neq\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{F}
, and let
\mathbb{E}_{*}\preceq\mathbb{U}
be
\mathcal{F}
-residually dense over
\mathbb{E}
. Suppose furthermore that
T
has property C. If
\mathbb{E}_{*}
contains an uncountable well-ordered set, then so does
\mathbb{E}
.

Proof.

Suppose toward contradiction that
\mathbb{E}_{*}
contains an uncountable well-ordered set and
\mathbb{E}
does not.

By Corollary
51
we can find a
\dcl_{T}
-basis
B
of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}
such that for all
b\in B
,
\tp(b/\mathbb{E})\vdash\tp(b/\mathbb{E}\langle B\setminus\{b\}\rangle)
.

Let
(y_{i})_{i<\aleph_{1}}
be a sequence in
\mathbb{E}_{*}
such that
y_{i}
is strictly increasing. Since
\mathbb{E}
does not contain an uncountable well ordered set, there must be
i_{0}
such that for all
i>i_{0}
,
\tp(y_{i}/\mathbb{E})=\tp(y_{i_{0}}/\mathbb{E})
.

For each
i
, write
y_{i}\coloneqq f_{i}(\overline{b}^{(i)})
for some tuple
\overline{b}^{(i)}
from
B
and some
\mathbb{E}
-definable function
f_{i}
.
The orthogonality property of
B
entails that there is a non-empty
B_{0}\subseteq B
such that for every
\mathbb{E}
-definable
f
, if
\tp(f(\overline{b})/\mathbb{E})=\tp(y_{i_{0}}/\mathbb{E})
for some tuple
\overline{b}
from
B
, then
\overline{b}\supseteq B_{0}
. Thus all tuples
\overline{b}^{(i)}
for

must contain
B_{0}
.

Since
B_{0}
is finite and thus by property C,
\mathbb{E}\langle B_{0}\rangle
still does not contain an uncountable well-ordered set, we can repeat the argument on the sequence
(y_{i})_{i_{0}\leq i<\aleph_{1}}
, but this time over
\mathbb{E}\langle B_{0}\rangle
, instead of
\mathbb{E}
.

Thus by induction we get an increasing sequence
(i_{n}:n<\omega)
in
\aleph_{1}
, such that for
i\geq i_{n}
,
\overline{b}^{(i)}
must contain more than
n
distinct elements from
B
. Since
\{i_{n}:n<\omega\}
is bounded in
\aleph_{1}
we get a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 61
.

If
T
is power bounded or has property C, then in Proposition
54
(2) we can replace “
\val(\mathbb{E}_{*},\mathcal{O}_{i})
does not contain an uncountable well ordered set” with “
\val(\mathbb{E},\mathcal{O}_{i})
does not contain an uncountable well ordered set”.

We conclude with the following theorem, showing that every countable complete o-minimal theory has property C.

Theorem 62
.

Let
T
be a complete
countable
o-minimal theory and suppose that
\mathbb{K}\models T
does not contain any uncountable well-ordered set. Suppose that
\mathbb{K}\langle x\rangle\succ\mathbb{K}
is an elementary extension of
\mathbb{K}
of
\dcl_{T}
-dimension
1
. Then
\mathbb{K}\langle x\rangle
does not contain any uncountable well-ordered set.

Proof.

Assume the contrary for contradiction that there is a strictly increasing
\aleph_{1}
-indexed sequence
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
in
\mathbb{K}\langle x\rangle
.

Since
T
is countable, up to extracting a cofinal subsequence of
(z_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
, we can assume that there are
n\in\mathbb{N}
, a countable
\mathbb{P}\prec\mathbb{K}
, an
(n+1)
-ary
\mathbb{K}_{0}
-definable function
f
and a sequence
(\overline{a}^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
of elements of
\mathbb{K}^{n}
such that
(f(\overline{a}^{(\alpha)},x):\alpha<\aleph_{1})
is strictly increasing.

We can also assume that such data was picked so that
n
is minimum.

Now let
\mathbb{K}_{0}\prec\mathbb{K}
be such that
\tp(x/\mathbb{P})\models\tp(x/\mathbb{K})
,
\mathbb{P}\subseteq\mathbb{K}_{0}
, and
|\mathbb{K}_{0}|<\aleph_{0}
. Such a
\mathbb{K}_{0}
exists because
\mathbb{K}
does not contain any uncountable well order and thus there are countable subsets
A,B\subseteq\mathbb{K}
such that
A
is cofinal in
\mathbb{K}^{<x}
and
B
is coinitial in
\mathbb{K}^{>x}
, then
\mathbb{K}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbb{P}\langle A\cup B\rangle
satisfies the requirements. Finally for all
\alpha<\aleph_{1}
set
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}\coloneqq\mathbb{K}\langle\overline{a}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\alpha\rangle
.

Claim 1
.

If for some
\alpha<\aleph_{1}
,
\{a_{1}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\aleph_{1}\}
realizes uncountably many distinct types over
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
, we reach a contradiction.

Proof.

We first build inductively a cofinal subsequence of
(\overline{a}^{(i(\beta))}:\beta<\aleph_{1})
of
(\overline{a}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\aleph_{1})
as follows.

Suppose that
(i(\beta):\beta<\gamma)
has been determined. Since
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}\cup\bigcup_{\beta<\gamma}\mathbb{K}_{i(\beta)}
is countable, by the claim assumption, there must be some
\delta<\alpha
such that
a_{1}^{(\delta)}
realizes a type over
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
that was not realized in
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}\cup\bigcup_{\beta<\gamma}\mathbb{K}_{i(\beta)}
. Set then
i(\gamma)\coloneq\delta
.

Now we show that we can inductively construct a sequence
(\overline{b}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\aleph_{1})
s.t.
f(\overline{b}^{(\beta)},x)
is strictly increasing and for each
\beta<\aleph_{1}
,
b_{1}^{(\beta)}\in\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
and there is some
\delta
s.t.
f(\overline{b}^{(\beta)},x)<f(\overline{a}^{(\delta)},x)
.

Assume that
(\overline{b}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\gamma)
was constructed, then we can find
\delta
such that for all
\beta<\gamma
,
f(\overline{b}^{(\beta)},x)<f(\overline{a}^{(i(\delta))},x)
. Set
M\coloneqq\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}\langle\overline{a}^{i(\delta)},\overline{a}^{i(\delta+2n+2)}\rangle
. Clearly
\dim_{\dcl_{T}}(M/\mathbb{K}_{\alpha})\leq 2n
, but by construction
\{\tp(a_{1}^{(i(\delta+j))}(\mathrm{x}_{i})/\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}):1\leq j\leq 2n+1\}
are setwise orthogonal types, so any extension of
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
realizing all of them must have dimension at least
2n+1
, whence there is some
j\in\{1,\ldots,2n+1\}
such that
\tp(a_{1}^{i(\delta+j)}/\mathbb{K}_{\alpha})
is not realized in
M
.

Now let
\phi(y,r,s)
be the
M
-formula

\phi(y,r,s)=\exists\overline{z},\,\forall w\in[r,s],\;f(\overline{a}^{(i(\delta))},w)<f(y,\overline{z},w)<f(\overline{a}^{(i(\delta+2n+2))},w).

There is an interval
[r,s]
containing
x
, s.t.
\mathbb{K}\models\phi(a_{1}^{i(\delta+j)},r,s)
and since
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}\supseteq\mathbb{K}_{0}
, and
\tp(x/\mathbb{K}_{0})\vdash\tp(x/\mathbb{K})
we can assume that
[r,s]
is
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
-definable.

Now since
\tp(a_{1}^{i(\delta+j)}/\mathbb{K}_{\alpha})
is not realized, in
M
there is an
M
-definable interval
[a,b]
containing
a_{1}^{i(\delta+j)}
such that
\mathbb{K}\models\forall t\in[a,b],\;\phi(t,r,s)
.

We pick
b_{1}^{\gamma}\in[a,b]\cap\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
and note that since
\mathbb{K}\models\phi(b_{1}^{\beta},r,s)
, there are
b_{2}^{(\gamma)},\ldots,b_{n}^{(\gamma)}
such that for all
w\in[r,s]
,

f(\overline{a}^{(i(\delta))},w)<f(\overline{b}^{(\gamma)})<f(\overline{a}^{(i(\delta+2n+2))},w),

whence the chosen tuple
\overline{b}^{\gamma}
satisfies the requirements.

Finally we observe that since each
b_{1}^{\alpha}
is in
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
and
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
is countable, there must be a cofinal subsequence
(\overline{b}^{(l(\beta))}:\beta<\aleph_{1})
of
(\overline{b}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\aleph_{1})
with
b_{1}^{(l(\beta))}=b_{1}^{(l(0))}
for all
\beta<\aleph_{1}
, thus setting
f_{2}(\overline{z},w)\coloneqq f(b_{1}^{l(0)},\overline{z},w)
we have that
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
is countable,
f_{2}
is
n
-ary and
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
-definable, and
(f_{2}(b_{2}^{(l(\beta))},\ldots,b_{n}^{(l(\beta))},x):\beta<\aleph_{1})
is strictly increasing, thus contradicting the minimality of
n
.
∎

Thus, we can reduce to the case where over every
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
, the set
\{a_{1}^{\beta}:\beta<\aleph_{1}\}
realises at most
\aleph_{0}
many types.
Let

\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(K_{\alpha})=\big\{p\in S_{1}(\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}):|\{a_{1}^{\beta}:\beta<\aleph_{1},\,p(a_{1}^{\beta})\}|>\aleph_{0}\big\}

be the collection of types over
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
that have uncountably many realizations in
\{a_{1}^{\beta}:\beta<\aleph_{1}\}
.
Then, consider the function

\displaystyle g:\aleph_{1}

\displaystyle\rightarrow\{0\}\cup\mathbb{N}\cup\{\aleph_{0}\}

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle\mapsto|\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(K_{\alpha})|.

Note that
g
is increasing, as for any
\alpha<\beta
, every type
p\in TP_{\aleph_{1}}(\mathbb{K}_{\alpha})
must extend to at least one type
p^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{K}_{\beta}
, and further it must extend to one in
\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(K_{\beta})
, by Claim 1. We note also that no two distinct types over
\mathbb{K}_{\alpha}
can extend to the same one over
\mathbb{K}_{\beta}
. This implies
g
is eventually constant. Also, we know
g
is always at least
1
. Now consider two cases (either the eventual value of
g
is a finite cardinal, or
\aleph_{0}
) and reach a contradiction for both cases.

Claim 2
.

If
g
is eventually identical to the constant function
m
, for some
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we reach a contradiction.

Proof.

Suppose toward contradiction that
g
eventually equals
m\in\mathbb{N}
. Let
g(\alpha)=m
. Let
p_{\alpha}\in\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(K_{\alpha})
.
In this case, by a pigeonhole argument, for each
\alpha<\beta<\aleph_{1}
,
p_{\alpha}
must have a unique extension
p_{\beta}\in\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(K_{\beta})
. Notice however that since
\mathbb{K}
does not contain uncountable well orders, there must be
\beta
such that for all
\gamma>\beta
,
p_{\beta}\vdash p_{\gamma}
. But since no
p_{\gamma}
is isolated, we would have that for every
\gamma>\beta
,
p_{\beta}(y)\vdash p_{\gamma}(y)\vdash y\notin\mathbb{K}_{\gamma}
and we would have
p_{\beta}(\mathbb{K})=\emptyset
, yielding a contradiction.
∎

Claim 3
.

If
g
is eventually identical to the constant function
\aleph_{0}
, we reach a contradiction.

Proof.

Suppose so. By enlarging
\mathbb{K}_{0}
, we may assume
g
is always equal to
\aleph_{0}
. We will inductively construct an increasing sequence of countable ordinals
(C_{\alpha})_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
, and a sequence of tuples
(\overline{b}^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha<\aleph_{1}}
such that for all
\alpha
,

(1)

b_{1}^{(\alpha)}\in\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\mathbb{K}_{C_{\beta}}
.

(2)

For all
\beta<\alpha
,

f(\overline{a}^{(C_{\beta})},x)<f(\overline{b}^{(\alpha)},x)\quad\text{and}\quad f(\overline{b}^{(\alpha)},x)<f(\overline{a}^{(C_{\alpha})},x).

Note that these conditions are sufficient for
f(\overline{b}^{(\alpha)},x)
to have order type
\aleph_{1}
.

Suppose that for some
\alpha
, we have built
(\overline{b}^{(\beta)}:\beta<\alpha)
and
(C_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha)
satisfying the requirements.

Let
M\coloneqq\bigcup_{\beta<\alpha}\mathbb{K}_{C_{\beta}}
. By the assumption on
g
,
\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(M)
has cardinality
\aleph_{0}
.
Choose
i_{2}>i_{1}\geq\sup_{\beta<\alpha}(C_{\beta})
such that the set
\{a_{1}^{(\gamma)}:i_{1}<\gamma<i_{2}\}
realises every element of
\mathrm{TP}_{\aleph_{1}}(M)
. Let the realising indices be
\delta_{1},\delta_{2},\ldots
. Consider the following first-order formula

\phi(y,r,s)=\exists\overline{z}.\forall w\in[r,s].f(\overline{a}^{(i_{1})},w)<f(y,\overline{z},w)<f(\overline{a}^{(i_{2})},w)

Note that since
x\notin\mathbb{K}
, for every
i_{1}<\gamma<i_{2}
, there are
r,s\in\mathbb{K}
such that
\mathbb{K}\models\phi(a_{1}^{\gamma},r,s)
.
Note also recall that
\tp(x/\mathbb{K}_{0})\vdash\tp(x/\mathbb{K})
, so we can assume
r,s\in\mathbb{K}_{0}
.
By o-minimality, there is some constant
N
such that for any fixed

,
\phi(y,r,s)
defines a subset of
\mathbb{K}
consisting of at most
N
points and intervals.
Choose some
r,s\in\mathbb{K}_{0}
close enough to
x
so that
\mathbb{K}\models\phi(a_{1}^{(\delta_{l})},r,s)
for all
1\leq l\leq N+1
. Thus, since these are
N+1
distinct points, two of them must be in the same
\mathbb{K}
-definable interval
I
such that
\mathbb{K}\models\forall t\in I,\,\phi(t,r,s)
. But since these two have distinct cuts over
M
,
I
must contain some point in
M
.
We will suggestively call this point
b_{1}^{(\alpha)}
. Then, choose points
b_{2}^{(\alpha)},\ldots,b_{n}^{(\alpha)}
in
\mathbb{K}
, witnessing the existential quantifier in
\phi(b_{1}^{(\alpha)},r,s)
, i.e. such that

\overline{b}^{(\alpha)}
and

fulfil (2) above. Also observe that each

is definable over

Since definable closure is finitary, for each

, there is some

such that

is strictly below the identity, by Fodor’s Lemma there is some

∎

All cases lead to a contradiction, so the theorem is proved.

Remark 63

A way to restate the result is the following. If

5.
When is an extension res-constructible?

Before this point, we have always been working with an ‘ambient’ res-constructible field (or in Section
4
the
\dcl
of many res-constructible fields), and considering subfields. Now we examine what happens when this structure is removed. The bulk of this section will be consumed examining a particularly pathological extension of RCFs, which ‘looks’ res-constructible locally, but admits no res-construction. Throughout this section, we will always be working with valued fields with no uncountable well-ordered subset in the value group.

Definition 64
.

Given
\mathbb{E}\prec\mathbb{E}_{*}

\mathbb{E}_{1}\prec\mathbb{E}_{2}
is res-constructible.

Proposition 65
.

Let

\overline{s}
of
\mathbb{E}_{*}
over
\mathbb{E}

be a countable subset of
\overline{s}
such that

is res-constructible over

\mathbb{E}\langle T\rangle
is weakly orthogonal, as desired.
∎

The converse to Proposition

does not hold, as we will see in Example

We see that it fixes

Lemma 72
.

In the setting of Example
67
the following hold:

(1)

if
x\in\mathbb{E}_{2}\setminus\mathbb{E}_{1}
, then
T_{x}
is infinite;

(2)

if
F\subseteq\{b_{\alpha,i}:\alpha<\aleph_{2},\,i<\omega\}
is finite, then
\mathbb{E}_{2}\cap\big(\mathbb{P}\langle F\rangle\big)(\!(\mu^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)=\mathbb{E}\langle F\rangle
.

Proof.

Notice that if
T_{x}
is finite, then
x
is an element of
\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\prime}
as defined in Proposition
68
.
On the other hand by the same Proposition
\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\prime}\cap\mathbb{E}_{2}=\mathbb{E}_{1}
and (1) follows.

As for (2) notice that
\big(\mathbb{P}\langle F\rangle\big)(\!(\mu^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{1}^{\prime}

\mathbb{E}_{2}\cap\big(\mathbb{P}\langle F\rangle\big)(\!(\mu^{\mathbb{Q}})\!)\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{1}=\mathbb{E}\langle b_{\alpha,i}:\alpha<\aleph_{2},\,i<\omega\rangle

∎

Proposition 73
.

In the setting of Example
67
, any countable set
S\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{2}
extends to some countable
S^{\prime}\supseteq S
, such that
\mathbb{E}\langle S^{\prime}\rangle
is weakly orthogonal in
\mathbb{E}_{2}
.

Proof.

Notice that up to reindexing the blocks, we can assume that
S\subseteq K\coloneqq\mathbb{E}\langle B_{i},M(B_{j},B_{k}):i<\omega,\,j<k<\omega\rangle
. So it suffices to show that
K
is weakly orthogonal in
\mathbb{E}_{2}
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{E}_{2}
be finite and without loss of generality
\dcl_{T}
-independent over
\mathbb{E}
.

We claim that there is
\chi\in\Aut(\mathbb{E}_{2}/K)
, such that for all
z\in F
,
T_{\chi(z)}
is finite. Let
F_{1}\coloneqq\{z\in F:|T_{z}|<\aleph_{0}\}
and
F_{0}\coloneqq F\setminus F_{1}
. To prove the claim by induction it suffices to show that for every
z\in F_{0}
, there is
\phi\in\Aut(\mathbb{E}_{2}/K\langle T_{z}:z\in F_{1}\rangle)
such that
|T_{\phi(z)}|<\aleph_{0}
. For this it suffices to apply Lemma
71
, with
S=\{B_{i},M(B_{j},B_{k}):i<\omega,\,j<k<\omega\}\cup\{T_{z}:z\in F_{1}\}
.

Notice that by Lemma
72
when
T_{z}
is finite,
\mathbb{E}\langle z\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{E}\langle T_{z}\rangle
, whence
K\langle z\rangle\subseteq K\langle T_{z}\rangle
.

Thus since
\bigcup_{z\in F}T_{\chi(x)}
is finite we have
K\langle\chi(F)\rangle\subseteq K\langle\bigcup_{z\in F}T_{\chi(z)}\rangle
.

Since the latter is a
\mathcal{O}
-res-constructible extension of
K
, by Corollary
21
we can conclude that also
K\langle\chi(F)\rangle
and thus
K\langle F\rangle
are
\mathcal{O}
-res-constructible over
K
.
∎

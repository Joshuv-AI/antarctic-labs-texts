The effect of normothermic machine perfusion on acute kidney injury following liver transplantation: a propensity score-matched, retrospective, single-centre, cohort study
Authors: Muhammad Ahmad Nadeem, Chase J Wehrle, Sangeeta Satish, Mingyi Zhang, Puneet Bansal, Khaled Ali, Ahmed Hussein, Sapana Verma, Valberto Sanha, Rebecca Panconesi, Chunbao Jiao, Keyue Sun, Omer Karuk Karakaya, Geofia S Crasta, Fatma Selin Yildirim, Mazhar Khalil, Alejandro Pita, Jamak Modaresi Esfeh, David CH Kwon, Federico Aucejo, Jaekeun Kim, Robert L Fairchild, Antonio D Pinna, Bijan Eghtesad, Richard A Fatica, Charles Miller, Masato Fujiki, Koji Hashimoto, Andrea Schlegel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryBackgroundNormothermic machine perfusion (NMP) is increasingly used for liver transplantation, but its effect on post-transplantation acute kidney injury (AKI), especially in recipients with renal dysfunction, remains unclear. We evaluated whether NMP was associated with lower early renal injury after deceased donor liver transplantation compared with static cold storage (SCS), including recipients with pre-existing AKI or chronic kidney disease (CKD).MethodsThis retrospective, single-centre, cohort study at Cleveland Clinic included deceased donor liver transplantation recipients transplanted between January 2019 and January 2025 with at least 12 months of follow-up. The primary outcome was new post-transplantation AKI by KDIGO serum creatinine criteria. Renal outcomes were assessed using KDIGO staging and validated core outcome set endpoints. Associations were evaluated using mixed-effects logistic regression, and NMP recipients were matched 1:1 to SCS recipients using propensity scores.Findings893 recipients were included; 319 (36%) received NMP. In multivariable analysis, NMP was associated with lower odds of AKI (odds ratio [OR] 0.70, 95% CI 0.46–0.94; p = 0.036) and new renal replacement therapy (RRT; OR 0.10, 95% CI 0.017–0.64; p = 0.015). Among 213 matched pairs without pre-existing AKI or CKD, AKI occurred in 62 (29%) NMP recipients vs 88 (41%) SCS recipients (p = 0.008), acute kidney disease in 95 (45%) vs 122 (57%; p = 0.009), and stage 3 AKI in seven (3%) vs 15 (7%). Among recipients with pre-existing AKI or CKD, postoperative peak creatinine was lower with NMP in unmatched (2.22 mg/dL [IQR 1.72–2.86] vs 2.71 mg/dL [1.85–3.47]; p = 0.017) and matched cohorts (2.04 mg/dL [1.64–2.34] vs 2.87 mg/dL [2.65–3.34]; p < 0.001).InterpretationNMP was associated with a modest but clinically relevant reduction in early renal injury after liver transplantation. These effects were most pronounced in recipients without pre-existing renal dysfunction and remained directionally consistent in high-risk recipients.FundingNone.Keywords: Liver transplantation, Acute kidney injury, Renal dysfunction, Normothermic machine perfusion, Static cold storage, Renal replacement therapy

Research in context

Evidence before this study

Before undertaking this study, we searched PubMed, Embase, the Cochrane Library, Web of Science, and reference lists from database inception to June 17, 2026, without language restrictions. Search terms included “liver transplantation”, “normothermic machine perfusion”, “static cold storage”, “acute kidney injury”, “renal replacement therapy”, “kidney dysfunction”, and “renal outcomes”. Eligible evidence included randomised trials, prospective or retrospective cohort studies, and meta-analyses that compared normothermic machine perfusion (NMP) with static cold storage (SCS) in liver transplantation and reported renal outcomes; studies limited to other perfusion modalities, non-liver grafts, or non-human models were excluded from the clinical evidence summary. Available studies showed that NMP improves early liver graft function and may reduce reperfusion-related complications, but acute kidney injury (AKI) and renal replacement therapy (RRT) were usually secondary endpoints, AKI definitions were heterogeneous, and recipients with pre-existing renal dysfunction were rarely analysed separately. Because outcomes and definitions varied substantially, no pooled renal estimate was derived.

Added value of this study

This kidney-focused analysis adds a standardised assessment of renal outcomes after NMP vs SCS using KDIGO-defined AKI, AKD, RRT, and core outcome set endpoints in a large single-centre cohort with 12-month follow-up. By combining multivariable modeling with propensity score matching, the study shows that NMP is associated with lower early renal injury in risk-matched recipients and provides a dedicated analysis of recipients who entered liver transplantation with pre-existing AKI or CKD.

Implications of all the available evidence

The available evidence suggests that NMP may reduce early kidney injury after liver transplantation, particularly when recipients or grafts carry higher perioperative risk. These findings support prospective studies that prespecify kidney-specific endpoints, harmonise AKI definitions, capture perioperative hemodynamic and immunosuppression data, and evaluate longer-term renal recovery.

Introduction

End-stage liver disease affects 5.2 million patients annually, with incidence and prevalence rising in the USA and globally.1,2 Liver transplantation remains the only definitive long-term treatment. Ex-situ machine perfusion, including normothermic machine perfusion (NMP), is becoming an essential part of liver preservation, with Food and Drug Administration approval facilitating broader adoption in the USA. NMP offers benefits including reduced early allograft dysfunction, less post-reperfusion syndrome, and increased use of marginal grafts through reduced static cold storage (SCS).3, 4, 5

Acute kidney injury (AKI) is a common complication after liver transplantation and a major cause of morbidity and mortality.6, 7, 8 Post-transplantation AKI is associated with progression to chronic kidney disease (CKD) and higher mortality.6 One common cause of kidney injury after liver transplantation is ischaemia-reperfusion injury.9,10 Such damage results from post-reperfusion syndrome-related hemodynamic changes and systemic release of cytokines and toxic metabolites in response to mitochondria-associated reactive oxygen species release.11,12 Risk is further exacerbated by donation after circulatory death (DCD) and extended-criteria donation after brain death grafts, which are associated with increased AKI after transplantation.13, 14, 15

NMP has been associated with improved transplant logistics and selected post-transplantation outcomes, particularly those related to post-reperfusion syndrome.16, 17, 18 However, most NMP studies to date report renal outcomes broadly and do not focus on kidney-specific risk or recipients with pre-existing renal dysfunction.

This study evaluates the association between NMP vs SCS and short-term renal complications after liver transplantation, including a subanalysis of high-risk recipients with pre-existing kidney injury.

Methods

Study design and participants

This retrospective, single-centre observational study included patients undergoing deceased donor liver transplantation at Cleveland Clinic from January 2019 to January 2025, using a prospectively maintained institutional database (Fig. 1). To ensure consistency in outcome measurement across cohorts, all included patients were followed for 12 months after transplantation. This standardised follow-up duration was selected to allow comparable outcome assessment between groups.

Study profile. The diagram outlines the selection process for the study cohort. After exclusion criteria were applied, patients were divided into unmatched SCS and NMP groups. Propensity score matching was then performed to create balanced cohorts for outcome comparison. SCS, static cold storage; NMP, normothermic machine perfusion.

The cohort was divided into two groups. The first included patients without a previous history of AKI or CKD before transplantation. A separate analysis was conducted for patients with pre-existing AKI or CKD at the time of transplantation. Exclusion criteria were living donor liver transplant, simultaneous liver-kidney transplant, retransplantation, split graft, domino transplant, and loss to follow-up due to postoperative death.

The primary outcome was post-transplantation AKI. Secondary outcomes included postoperative RRT, AKI severity according to KDIGO classification, renal outcomes in patients with pre-existing kidney injury, and other outcomes defined by the core outcome set for complication reporting after liver transplantation.5,19

Ethics

The study was approved by the Cleveland Clinic Institutional Review Board (IRB 23-1348) and conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Because this was a retrospective analysis of institutional data, the requirement for informed consent was waived by the Institutional Review Board.

Institutional protocols and approach to machine perfusion

Routine use of end-ischaemic ex-situ NMP with the OrganOx Metra device commenced at our centre on October 1, 2022, as previously described.5,19 Programmatic guidelines for selecting machine perfusion cases were established in a previous study by our group.5 The final decision to use NMP was made at the discretion of the primary transplant surgeon on a case-by-case basis, informed by clinical complexity and logistical factors.

At our centre, NMP is considered for high-risk donor or recipient scenarios, including advanced donor age, DCD grafts, moderate macrosteatosis, recipients with significant comorbidities or hemodynamic instability, retransplantation (not included in this study), or previous cardiothoracic surgery. NMP enabled use of high-risk DCD and steatotic grafts.

A combination of immunosuppressive agents is used at our centre to ensure optimal post-transplant care, with tacrolimus as the mainstay of maintenance therapy. Basiliximab induction is selectively administered to recipients with pre-existing renal dysfunction or those requiring RRT at the time of transplant. Tacrolimus was also initiated in patients receiving basiliximab. Cyclosporine is not routinely used and is reserved for patients who develop tacrolimus-associated neurotoxicity. Outside these clinical indications, no deviations from our standard immunosuppression protocol are practiced.

For all donors, organ procurement was performed using standard surgical techniques. The start of cold perfusion was defined as aortic cross-clamp at the donor hospital, followed immediately by in-situ flushing with cold preservation solution (University of Wisconsin or histidine-tryptophan-ketoglutarate). Following procurement, all liver grafts were transported to our centre using SCS on ice. No DCD donor in this cohort underwent normothermic regional perfusion. Upon arrival at the transplant centre, grafts either remained in SCS or underwent end-ischemic NMP according to clinical and logistical decision-making.

In the SCS group, livers remained in static cold storage throughout transport and back-table preparation. The graft underwent standard bench preparation on ice and received a final flush with cold solution immediately before implantation.

In the NMP group, an end-ischemic back-to-base approach was used. Upon arrival at our centre, grafts underwent bench preparation and were cannulated onto the OrganOx Metra device. NMP was used for a minimum target of 4 h to enable viability assessment and was maintained until completion of recipient hepatectomy.

Viability during NMP was assessed using traditional metrics, including lactate clearance, bile production and composition, perfusate biochemistry, and flow and pressure parameters. Although lactate clearance is used clinically as part of NMP viability assessment at our centre, lactate values were not consistently captured in a format suitable for retrospective extraction across the entire cohort and were therefore not analysed in this study.

Diagnosis and staging of acute kidney injury

According to KDIGO guidelines, AKI was defined as an increase in serum creatinine (sCr) of more than 0.3 mg/dL within 48 h or a 50% increase from baseline sCr within 7 days. For post-transplantation AKI assessment, baseline sCr was defined as the last recorded sCr before transplantation and compared with post-transplantation sCr values. For pre-transplantation AKI, baseline sCr was defined as the value on preoperative day 7 and compared with daily sCr values from preoperative day 7 through the day of transplantation. This 7-day preoperative window was selected to standardise perioperative renal function assessment. No data outside this interval were included in pre-liver transplantation AKI determination.20, 21, 22, 23 Although KDIGO also permits AKI diagnosis based on urine output below 0.5 mL/kg/h for 6 h after surgery, this criterion was excluded because it can overestimate AKI.24,25 AKI severity was staged according to KDIGO criteria: stage 1 was defined as sCr 1.5–1.9 times baseline or an increase of at least 0.3 mg/dL; stage 2 as sCr 2.0–2.9 times baseline; and stage 3 as sCr 3.0 times baseline, sCr at least 4 mg/dL, or need for RRT.

Hepatorenal syndrome (HRS) was defined as described by the International Club of Ascites.23 Baseline sCr was defined as the lowest sCr value recorded within 1 week before transplantation. CKD was defined by KDIGO guidelines as glomerular filtration rate below 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 persisting for at least 3 months, regardless of etiology.26

For analysis by pre-existing kidney function, patients were classified into estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) categories of less than 30, 30–60, and more than 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 eGFR was calculated using the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation, which incorporates serum creatinine, sex, and age.27

Additional outcomes after liver transplantation were reported up to 6 months after transplantation. Outcomes were reported according to recently described national guidance and the predefined core outcome set for standardised complication reporting in liver transplantation.19 Complications were graded according to the Clavien-Dindo classification.28,29 The Comprehensive Complication Index was calculated as the summation of Clavien-Dindo graded complications over that period, as previously described and validated.30

AKD was defined as persistence of kidney dysfunction meeting KDIGO stage 1 or greater beyond 7 days after the initiating AKI event; persistence beyond 90 days was considered CKD. In this study, AKD was assessed between postoperative day 7 and day 90 using the same baseline creatinine reference as for AKI.31

Post-reperfusion syndrome was defined as a decrease in mean arterial pressure (MAP) of 30% or greater within 5 min after graft reperfusion and lasting approximately 1 min or longer. This definition was used for post-reperfusion syndrome ascertainment throughout the study.32,33

Post-transplantation RRT was analysed in the overall cohort and specifically among patients who were not receiving RRT before transplantation.

Vasoactive totals represented the cumulative intraoperative doses of norepinephrine, epinephrine, and vasopressin administered during the case. In contrast, the vasoactive-inotropic score (VIS) summarised peak vasoactive intensity and was calculated as: dopamine (μg/kg/min) + dobutamine (μg/kg/min) + 100 × epinephrine (μg/kg/min) + 10 × milrinone (μg/kg/min) + 10,000 × vasopressin (units/kg/min) + 100 × norepinephrine (μg/kg/min). VIS max was defined as the highest VIS value recorded intraoperatively.

Average central venous pressure was defined as the arithmetic mean of all intraoperative central venous pressure measurements recorded in the anesthesia record (mmHg) and was included as a surrogate for intraoperative volume status and right heart filling pressures, which can influence renal perfusion pressure during liver transplantation.

Statistical analysis

The primary comparison of interest was NMP vs SCS. Descriptive statistics were used to summarise the study population. Continuous variables are presented as median and IQR, and categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Categorical variables were analysed using chi-square tests or Fisher exact tests as appropriate. Continuous variables were analysed using t tests or Mann–Whitney U tests according to data distribution. Multivariable binary logistic regression models were used across the entire cohort to determine the independent association of potential risk factors with new AKI and new need for RRT. Mixed-effects logistic regression models with a random intercept were used to assess the association between preservation type and outcomes while accounting for residual heterogeneity between patients. Preservation type (NMP vs SCS) and covariates including donor age, donor BMI, cold ischemia time, recipient age, recipient warm ischemia time, donor type, recipient BMI, and MELD score were included as fixed effects.34 Covariates were selected a priori based on clinical relevance and previous literature.

The same modeling strategy was applied in subgroup analyses, including patients with pre-transplantation kidney dysfunction (AKI or CKD) to explore whether the preservation type remained independently associated with new AKI in this clinical subset. Given the association between donor type and NMP use, a subgroup analysis comparing outcomes between DCD and donation after brain death grafts was performed to assess the preservation method's effect for each donor type. Subgroup analyses were performed to assess effect modification by baseline renal function and donor type. Induction immunosuppression (basiliximab use) was not included in the primary model due to its strong collinearity with baseline renal dysfunction; however, its potential confounding effect was explored in subgroup analyses.

Patients in the NMP group were propensity score matched 1:1 with SCS recipients using nearest-neighbor matching with a 0.1 standard deviation caliper. Propensity scores were defined as the probability of receiving NMP vs SCS. Covariates included donor and recipient age, BMI, sex, MELD score, cold ischemia time, warm ischemia time, baseline creatinine, and donor steatosis. Covariate balance after matching was assessed using standardised mean differences, with values less than 0.1 considered acceptable balance. The matched cohort analysis used unpaired comparisons after propensity score matching.

Binary outcomes were compared using chi-squared tests and continuous outcomes with Mann–Whitney U-tests. Missing data were handled using complete-case analysis, as missingness was low and not expected to bias results. In addition, intraoperative hemodynamic variables (central venous pressure, VISmax) were included in sensitivity analyses to explore their potential confounding effect on renal outcomes.

Analyses were conducted using SPSS version 28.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY, USA), and figures were created using GraphPad Prism version 8. A two-sided p value less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

No specific funding was received for this study. Cleveland Clinic Research and Transplant Center provided institutional support but had no role in study design, data collection, data analysis, data interpretation, writing of the report, or the decision to submit the manuscript for publication.

Results

A total of 893 patients met inclusion criteria between January 2019 and January 2025. Of these, 683 patients had no previous AKI or CKD before liver transplantation, including 319 recipients treated with NMP and 364 treated with SCS. The second cohort included 210 patients with pre-existing AKI or CKD at liver transplantation, including 73 recipients treated with NMP and 137 treated with SCS (Fig. 1).

In the unmatched cohort without previous AKI or CKD, donors in the NMP group were older (median 51 years [IQR 37–61] vs 43 years [30–55]; p < 0.001) and more often DCD grafts (118/319 [37%] vs 40/364 [11%]; p < 0.001). Cold ischemia time was longer with NMP (6.1 h [5.2–7.2] vs 5.8 h [5.1–6.7]; p = 0.021), whereas MELD scores were lower (19 [13–24] vs 23 [15–30]; p < 0.001). Median tacrolimus trough levels over the first 7 postoperative days were similar (4.80 ng/mL [3.35–6.90] vs 4.80 ng/mL [2.50–7.50]; p = 0.48; Table 1).

Renal outcomes were first compared in the unmatched cohort without previous AKI or CKD, which included 319 NMP recipients and 364 SCS recipients. Post-transplantation AKI occurred in 102 (32%) of 319 NMP recipients and 131 (36%) of 364 SCS recipients (p = 0.27). AKD occurred in 157 (49%) NMP recipients and 197 (54%) SCS recipients (p = 0.20). KDIGO stage distribution differed between groups (p = 0.034), with stage 1 AKI in 57 (18%) vs 80 (22%) and stage 3 AKI in 13 (4%) vs 29 (8%). Discussion

AKI and post-transplantation renal failure are important clinical outcomes and sources of healthcare cost after liver transplantation. This study systematically analysed the association between NMP and post-transplantation renal outcomes using a risk-matched approach. First, NMP was independently associated with lower rates of post-transplantation renal dysfunction in both risk-matched and multivariable analyses. Second, this association was also observed in recipients who entered transplantation with pre-existing renal dysfunction. However, NMP explained only 18% of the variation in post-transplantation outcomes, with a collective contribution of 35% from alternative non-modifiable parameters, highlighting that inherent donor and recipient risk remain central determinants of renal outcomes.

Since Food and Drug Administration approval of NMP in 2021, liver preservation practices in the United States have evolved rapidly. NMP has enabled increased allograft use, facilitating transplantation in patients with lower laboratory MELD scores, who often have exception points and longer waiting time.35, 36, 37 Our group previously demonstrated that selected early outcomes after transplantation are improved with NMP using a risk-matched approach, including a reduction in postoperative RRT.5 This clinically relevant signal warranted further exploration focused on renal outcomes and potential confounders. Our data suggest that NMP was associated with lower rates of post-transplantation renal injury overall and showed a similar association in subcohorts with impaired pre-transplantation renal function. Post-liver transplantation AKI is associated with increased mortality, prolonged hospital stay, greater resource use, and higher healthcare costs.37, 38, 39 A recent large single-centre US cohort study by Nguyen and colleagues reported improved outcomes and lower resource use with NMP, with fewer AKI events in DCD-NMP compared with DCD-SCS (31.1% vs 47.4%; p = 0.001).40 These real-world data support the clinical relevance of NMP in mitigating kidney injury risk in DCD liver transplantation.

Although this study is clinical in nature, the observed association between preservation strategy and early renal injury is biologically plausible and may be mediated by differences in reperfusion physiology. Post-reperfusion syndrome, defined in the Methods section, reflects early hemodynamic instability after graft implantation and has been associated with post-transplantation AKI and ischemia-reperfusion injury. In this context, NMP may reduce early renal injury by attenuating reperfusion-related hemodynamic instability and limiting renal hypoperfusion during the immediate post-reperfusion period. Beyond hemodynamics, prolonged cold ischemia may contribute to endothelial and microcirculatory injury, amplifying systemic inflammatory signaling after transplantation. While inflammatory or endothelial biomarkers were not measured in this cohort, these pathways provide a plausible framework linking preservation modality with the AKI and AKD patterns observed, alongside contributors such as operative complexity and baseline recipient risk.41, 42, 43

These findings suggest that NMP is associated with a clinically meaningful reduction in early renal injury after liver transplantation. Because the primary aim of this study was to evaluate short-term renal complications, the observed reduction in AKI and AKD is an important signal. At the same time, longer-term renal outcomes remain multifactorial and may be influenced by donor and recipient factors, perioperative events, immunosuppression exposure, and post-transplantation management. Accordingly, further work is needed to optimise kidney protection beyond the immediate perioperative period. Multiple studies have shown a strong association between pre-transplantation laboratory MELD score and post-transplantation AKI, largely because serum creatinine, a key component of MELD, reflects baseline kidney function. Similarly, existing literature associates DCD grafts with higher AKI incidence due to prolonged donor warm ischemia and related ischaemia-reperfusion injury.44,45 This study has several limitations, including its retrospective design, which introduces centre-level bias and may limit generalisability. Propensity score matching and mixed-effects modeling were used to create comparable risk groups and assess the relative impact of risk factors, but these methods cannot eliminate residual confounding.46 NMP was preferentially used in higher-risk donor and recipient scenarios, including DCD grafts and clinically complex cases, introducing selection bias that would be expected to work against the NMP group. The observed association between NMP and lower early renal injury may therefore represent a conservative estimate rather than an exaggerated treatment effect. Some imbalances, including donor steatosis, donor and recipient age, and pre-transplantation AKI, remained despite matching and could influence outcomes. AKI was defined using serum creatinine alone because urine output data were unavailable and may overestimate AKI incidence. Recent guidelines support sCr-based definitions in advanced liver disease. eGFR was assessed using the 2021 CKD-EPI creatinine equation; however, values above 60 mL/min/1.73 m2 were often reported as “>60”, limiting continuous analysis. Therefore, eGFR was categorised. Longer follow-up is needed to assess chronic kidney injury. Although KDIGO classification was used in subgroup analyses, the small sample size limits conclusions. Basiliximab induction is a potential source of confounding by indication because it is preferentially administered to liver transplantation recipients with impaired renal function. Although separate analyses were performed for patients with and without baseline renal dysfunction, residual confounding related to induction strategy cannot be excluded.

In summary, in this risk-adjusted observational study, NMP was associated with reduced early renal injury after liver transplantation, with directionally consistent effects in recipients with pre-existing renal dysfunction. Renal outcomes remain strongly influenced by underlying donor and recipient risk factors. Further studies are needed to validate these observations and clarify the mechanisms by which preservation strategy may modify early kidney injury after liver transplantation.

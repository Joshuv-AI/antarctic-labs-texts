Analytical Fock Representation of Two-Mode Squeezing for Quantum Interference
Xuemei Gu, Carlos Ruiz-Gonzalez, Mario Krenn

Abstract
Two-mode squeezing is central to entangled-photon generation and nonlinear interferometry, yet standard perturbative low-gain and Gaussian treatments obscure how photon-number amplitudes interfere, especially in multi-crystal geometries and at high gain. Here, we derive the exact analytic action of the two-mode squeezing operator on arbitrary Fock states to analyze nonlinear interferometers directly in the number basis at arbitrary squeezing strength. Within this framework, we find new physical interpretations of previously known quantum interference effects, and theoretically discover a new and unusual multi-photon interference effect in an experimental four-crystal geometry that could readily be observed in laboratories. Our work provides a compact analytic toolkit and concrete design rules for engineering multi-photon interference, with applications in quantum sensing, precision metrology, and advanced quantum state generation.



Introduction

Squeezed light is an important resource in quantum optics. In particular, the two-mode squeezing operator underpins entangled-photon generation by spontaneous parametric down-conversion, and has a broad range of applications in sensing and imaging

;
10
;
33
;
7
;
42
. Most analyses adopt either (i) a
low-gain
, perturbative treatment that truncates multi-pair creation, or (ii) the
continuous-variable
(Gaussian) formalism that emphasizes quadratures, Bogoliubov transformations, and homodyne detection
7
. Both perspectives are powerful and have enabled major advances in quantum metrology and quantum imaging

;
30
;
4
;
20
;
6
;
38
.

While the perturbative low-gain model drops higher-order emissions by construction, the continuous-variable (Gaussian) formalism, though powerful for quadrature analyses, embeds fixed photon-number amplitudes inside high-order moments. Consequently, the phase-dependent cancellations between concrete emission events – crucial in modern nonlinear interferometers – are not directly visible without reverting to a number-state expansion.

The exact action of the two-mode squeezing operator on number states is known in the mathematical-physics literature
13
;
3
;
27
, but it is not often used as a practical analytic tool for interpreting modern quantum interferometers, where interference among higher-order emission events leads to surprising and often counter-intuitive effects. Prominent examples include frustrated pair creation at low gain
19
;
25
, two-boson interference in time and its high-gain cancellation of a seeded single photon pair
8
;
11
and its generalizations
24
, as well as multi-photon nonlocal interference controlled by undetected photons
35
;
16
;
40
. In such settings, a transparent, exact
Fock-basis
description reveals where the interference comes from and how it changes with gain.

In this work, we derive an exact Fock-basis expression for the action of the two-mode squeezing operator on arbitrary Fock states. We use it for deriving new physical interpretations of numerous photonic nonlinear phenomena, and derive new counterintuitive multi-photon interference that can readily be observed in laboratories. Concretely, (1) we find a new simple physical interpretation for a recently discovered two-photon interference effect at high-gain by Cerf and Jabbour, (2) we reinterpret a high-gain effect in SU(1,1) interferometers using the Fock basis, (3) we find perfect interference conditions of a recent four-photon non-linear interferometer in the high-gain regime and (4) find a new, counter-intuitive four-photon interference effect that has analogies to the Cerf-Jabbour effect. It demonstrates how powerful the analytical Fock-representation of the squeezer operator can be in reinterpreting known and discovering new complex interference effects in quantum optics.

Analytic Expression and Quantum Interference

We begin by deriving analytic expressions for the action of the two-mode squeezing operator in the Fock basis and then apply them to analyze quantum interference across across single- and multi-crystal configurations.

Analytic Fock-basis representation of squeezed states

Consider the two-mode squeezing operator
S_{2}(\zeta)

S_{2}(\zeta)=\exp\left(\zeta^{*}ab-\zeta a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right),

(1)

where
\zeta=re^{i\theta}
is the complex squeezing parameter, and
a
(
a^{\dagger}
) and
b
(
b^{\dagger}
) denote the annihilation (creation) operators of the two modes. Using the disentanglement formula for the
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
Lie algebra
39
;
14
, this operator can be rewritten as

\displaystyle S_{2}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=\exp\left(-e^{i\theta}\tanh r\,a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(-\ln(\cosh r)\,(a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b+1)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\times\exp\left(e^{-i\theta}\tanh r\,ab\right),

(2)

This normal-ordered form provides a convenient starting point for deriving the action of the squeezing operator on general Fock states. The detailed derivation from Eq. (
1
) to Eq. (
2
) is presented in the Appendix.

Applying
S_{2}(\zeta)
to a general two-mode Fock state
|p,q\rangle
gives

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=S_{2}(\zeta)|p,q\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{\min{(p,q)}}\Biggl[\frac{(e^{-i\theta}\tanh r)^{n}(-e^{i\theta}\tanh r)^{k}}{(\cosh r)^{p+q-2n+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\sqrt{\binom{p}{n}\binom{q}{n}\binom{p-n+k}{k}\binom{q-n+k}{k}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\,\,|p-n+k,q-n+k\rangle\Biggl]

(3)

Although this formula is not frequently discussed explicitly, it appears in the literature
13
;
22
and can be expressed in a recursive way
24
. Importantly, we show that it provides a useful framework to describe nonlinear interferometers directly in the Fock basis, enabling both a reinterpretation of known results and the prediction of new interference phenomena.

For the special Fock states
|p,p\rangle
, Eq. (
3
) simplifies to

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=S_{2}(\zeta)|p,p\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{p}\Biggl[\frac{(e^{-i\theta}\tanh r)^{n}(-e^{i\theta}\tanh r)^{k}}{(\cosh r)^{p+q-2n+1}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\binom{p}{n}\binom{p-n+k}{k}|p-n+k,p-n+k\rangle\Biggl]

(4)

while for the vacuum input
|0,0\rangle
, one can obtain the standard two-mode squeezed vacuum state, which is

\displaystyle|\psi\rangle

\displaystyle=S_{2}(\zeta)|0,0\rangle=\sum\limits_{m=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-e^{i\theta}\tanh{r})^{m}}{\cosh{r}}\,\,|m,m\rangle

(5)

In the following, we systematically analyze experimental configurations from a single crystal to four-crystal arrays, showing how Eq. (
3
) describes quantum interference. For each setup, we consider all the squeezing phases are zero (i.e.,
\theta=0
) and extract the amplitude for specific output states and identify conditions for interference.

Two-photon interference in a single crystal

An interesting form of destructive quantum interference occurs when a single nonlinear crystal is seeded with the input Fock state
|1,1\rangle
(Fig.
1
(a)). This effect was theoretically predicted by Cerf and Jabbour as the time-reversed analogue of Hong-Ou-Mandel interference
8
and was recently observed experimentally
11
.

Applying Eq. (
3
) with the input state
|1,1\rangle
(where
p=q=1
), the amplitude for observing
|1,1\rangle
at the output is

\displaystyle A(r)_{|1,1\rangle}=\langle 1,1\,|S_{2}(r)|1,1\rangle=\frac{1-\sinh^{2}r}{\cosh^{3}r}.

(6)

This amplitude becomes zero at
r=\operatorname{arcsinh}(1)\approx 0.88
, e.g., the coincidence rate for detecting
|1,1\rangle
photons vanishes completely (as shown in Fig.
1
(b)). With the parametrization
g=\cosh^{2}r
, the same expression can be written as
A_{|1,1\rangle}=(2-g)/g^{\frac{3}{2}}
, showing perfect destructive interference at
g=2
, exactly matching the prediction of Ref.
8
.

The new interpretation of this effect using Eq.
6
Physically, the cancellation arises from the interference between two indistinguishable quantum processes of equal magnitude but opposite phase. The first process is direct transmission of the input photon pair through the crystal. The second involves annihilation of both input photons followed by creation of a new photon pair within the crystal. Each annihilation and creation contributes an amplitude factor of
\sinh r
(but with an opposite sign), and their combination leads to a total amplitude of
-\sinh^{2}r
for the second process. Perfect destructive interference occurs when the two components have equal magnitude and opposite phase, which happens when
\sinh^{2}r=1
.

Two-photon interference between two crystals

One can immediately see interesting physical effects by putting two crystals sequentially, in such a way that the paths of the photons produced in the first crystals are overlapped with the paths of the second crystal, see Fig.
2
(a). This setup is also sometimes called
\mathrm{SU(1,1)}
nonlinear interferometer
41
;
10
;
33
. We now apply Eq. (
3
) sequentially to both crystals (the output modes of the first crystal will also act as input to the second one), the amplitude for observing one photon pair
|1,1\rangle
in output modes is

\displaystyle A(r_{1},r_{2},\phi)_{|1,1\rangle}=\langle 1,1\,|S_{ab}(r_{2})\Phi_{a}(\phi)S_{ab}(r_{1})|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-e^{i\phi}\tanh r_{1}\tanh r_{2})^{n}}{\cosh r_{1}\,\cosh^{3}r_{2}\,\tanh r_{2}}\Big(n-\sinh^{2}r_{2}\Big).

(7)

For real, finite squeezing parameters (i.e., physically realizable values of
r_{1}
and
r_{2}
), the series in Eq. (
7
) converges since
|-e^{i\phi}\tanh r_{1}\tanh r_{2}|<1
, leading to

A(r,\phi)_{|1,1\rangle}=-\frac{\left(\tanh r_{2}+e^{i\phi}\tanh r_{1}\right)\,\text{sech}r_{1}\,\text{sech}r_{2}}{\left(1+e^{i\phi}\tanh r_{1}\tanh r_{2}\right)^{2}}.

(8)

When both crystals are pumped with equal squeezing strengths (
r_{1}=r_{2}=r
), this expression simplifies to

\displaystyle A(r,\phi)_{|1,1\rangle}=-\frac{\left(1+e^{i\phi}\right)\,\text{sech}^{2}r\,\tanh r}{\left(1+e^{i\phi}\tanh^{2}r\right)^{2}}.

(9)

In the low-gain regime, expanding the amplitude in Eq. (

) with respect to

, we find

\displaystyle A_{|1,1\rangle}(r_{\text{small}}\rightarrow 0,\phi)\approx-\left(1+e^{i\phi}\right)r+O(r^{3})

(10)

and the corresponding probability of observing the state
|1,1\rangle
is approximately

\displaystyle P(r,\phi)_{|1,1\rangle}=|A_{|1,1\rangle}(r_{\text{small}},\phi)|^{2}\approx 4r^{2}\cos^{2}(\frac{\phi}{2})

(11)

which exhibits a sinusoidal oscillation with the phase
\phi
.

This behavior reveals the phenomenon of
Frustrated two-photon creation

19
, where one has a superposition of the photons being created in the first or the second crystal. When the relative phase between the two crystals is set to
\phi=\pi
, the two possibilities interfere destructively, completely canceling the photon pair creation. This phenomenon has been extensively studied in the context of quantum metrology

;
4
;
6
, especially as it allows for the two photons in paths

and
b
to have different frequencies, which can be exploited in practical applications

;
30
.

In the high-gain regime where
r
is large, other interesting phenomenon emerge. Conventionally, this regime is studied using the language of continuous-variable quantum mechanics
7
and the application of homodyne measurements. This is practically (both experimentally and theoretically) useful

;
34
;
32
;
37
, but makes it more difficult to see the concrete behavior of the individual terms in the Fock basis.

Using the explicit Fock-basis expression Eq. (
3
), we find that the simple sinusoidal oscillation of
P_{|1,1\rangle}(r,\phi)
changes as
r
increases: the probability of observing
|1,1\rangle
becomes drastically changes and highly sensitive to small phase shifts near
\phi=\pi
. This sensitivity can be quantified by the second derivative (or the curvature)
C(r,\phi)
of Eq. (
11
),

\displaystyle C(r,\phi=\pi)

\displaystyle=\left.\frac{\partial^{2}P(r,\phi)_{|1,1\rangle}}{\partial\phi^{2}}\right|_{\phi=\pi}=\frac{\sinh^{2}(2r)}{2}

(12)

For large

,
\sinh(2r)\sim\tfrac{1}{2}e^{2r}
, so the curvature scales approximately as
C(r,\pi)\propto e^{4r}
. The phase response therefore grows exponentially with increasing squeezing strength, as also numerically shown in Fig.
2
(b). This is very useful for precise measurements of small phase changes

, if the experimental challenges such as achieving large squeezing and photon-number-resolving detection can be overcome.

Two-photon interference among three crystals

One interesting recent interference effect involve three sequential crystals
25
(described in more detail in
21
), as shown in Fig
3
. When grouping the crystals into pairs, one can create a situation where a classical description would lead to two contradicting descriptions. We apply Eq. (
3
) and get the general amplitude description of the two-photon term
|1,1\rangle
at the output

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{|1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\langle 1,1\,|S_{ab}(r_{3})\Phi_{a}(\phi_{2})S_{ab}(r_{2})\Phi_{a}(\phi_{1})S_{ab}(r_{1})|0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=-\,s_{1}s_{2}s_{3}\;\frac{\,t_{3}\!\left(1+e^{i\phi_{1}}t_{1}t_{2}\right)\;+\;e^{i\phi_{2}}\!\left(t_{2}+e^{i\phi_{1}}t_{1}\right)}{\Big(1+e^{i\phi_{1}}t_{1}t_{2}+e^{i\phi_{2}}t_{2}t_{3}+e^{i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})}t_{1}t_{3}\Big)^{\!2}}

(13)

with
t_{i}=\tanh{r_{i}}
and
s_{i}=\operatorname{sech}r_{i}
. The surprising behavior described in Ref.

is given by

\displaystyle A(r,r,r,\pi,\pi)_{|1,1\rangle}=-\operatorname{sech}r\tanh{r},

(14)

which leads to a situation where crystal I and II together (and independently from the rest) as well as crystal II and III together appear to exhibit perfect destructive interference, thus concluding that any of the remaining photons must be created only in the other crystal (crystal III and I, respectively). This conclusion is contradicting, which shows one cannot use such semi-classical description to assign definite creation events to specific crystals.

Analyzing Eq. (

) with
r_{i}=r
, we find destructive interference of
A(r,r,r,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{|1,1\rangle}=0
only for

\displaystyle\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}\in\Bigl\{\vartheta(r),\ \ 2\pi-\vartheta(r))\Bigr\},

(15)

with
\vartheta(r)=2\arccos\!\big(\tfrac{\operatorname{sech}r}{2}\big)
.

For the low-gain regime, the amplitude in Eq. (
13
) can be expanded as

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle(r,r,r,\phi_{1},\phi_{2})_{|1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle\approx-\Bigl(1+e^{i\phi_{2}}\bigl(1+e^{i\phi_{1}}\bigr)\Bigr)r+\Bigr[2e^{2i\phi_{2}}(1+e^{i\phi_{1}})^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{e^{i\phi_{2}}}{6}(1+e^{i\phi_{1}})(23+12e^{i\phi_{1}})+\frac{11}{6}+e^{i\phi_{1}}\Bigr]r^{3}+O(r^{4}).

(16)

At the lowest order in
r
, the amplitude vanishes when

1+e^{i\phi_{2}}+e^{i(\phi_{1}+\phi_{2})}=0
.
This condition is satisfied for

\displaystyle\phi_{1}=\phi_{2}=\vartheta(r)=\tfrac{2\pi}{3}(\text{or}\,\tfrac{4\pi}{3})\,\,\,\text{modulo}\,\,2\pi,

reflecting the well-known property that the three cube roots of unity sum to zero,
1+e^{i\tfrac{2\pi}{3}}+e^{i\tfrac{4\pi}{3}}=0
.

If we use
\vartheta(r=\tfrac{2\pi}{3})
, higher-order terms remain, giving
A\sim r^{3}
for small but finite
r
. A more accurate cancellation requires the phase to drift slightly with the gain parameter. Including the next correction, we find that the destructive-interference condition is satisfied when

\vartheta(r)=\frac{2\pi}{3}-\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}+O(r^{4})\quad\text{or}\quad\vartheta(r)=\frac{4\pi}{3}+\frac{r^{2}}{\sqrt{3}}+O(r^{4}),

which suppresses the
r^{3}
contribution.

For high values of
r
, the phases need to be adjusted such that they can compensate for the extra contributions (Fig.
3
b). We find a general analytical condition for cancellation of
|1,1\rangle
term (as before,
t_{i}=\tanh{r_{i}}
and
s_{i}=\operatorname{sech}r_{i}
)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}

\displaystyle\in\Bigl\{\,\arccos C,\ \ 2\pi-\arccos C\,\Bigr\}\quad(\text{mod }2\pi),

(17)

\displaystyle\quad\text{where}\quad C=\frac{t_{3}^{2}\!\left(1+t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}\right)-t_{1}^{2}-t_{2}^{2}}{2\,t_{1}t_{2}\,(1-t_{3}^{2})}

together with

\displaystyle\phi_{2}\equiv\Arg\!\left(-\,t_{3}\,\frac{1+t_{1}t_{2}e^{i\phi_{1}}}{\,t_{1}e^{i\phi_{1}}+t_{2}\,}\right)\quad(\text{mod }2\pi).

(18)

under the constraint

\frac{|t_{1}-t_{2}|}{1-t_{1}t_{2}}\ \leq\ t_{3}\ \leq\ \frac{t_{1}+t_{2}}{1+t_{1}t_{2}}.

This result shows that, for any set of squeezing parameters, one can always find a pair of phases
(\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
that removes the two-photon coincidence component
|1,1\rangle
.

Four-photon interference in a four-crystal configuration

A more recent development is the theoretical prediction
17
and experimental observation
16
;
35
;
40
of a new multi-photon, nonlocal quantum interference effect. The simplest version consists of two rows of two crystals each, see Fig.
4
(a).

Let’s look at an interesting special case: The only way to get exactly one photon in each of the four detectors (i.e., seeing the four-photon state
|1,1,1,1\rangle
) is that either the lower row of crystals, i.e. crystals I and II each produce a pair, or the upper row with crystal III and IV produce a pair each. No other combination of firing pattern produces one photon in each output path. As in the previous case, if one can change the phase between the two crystals, we can destructively (and constructively) interfere the two possible origins of the four-photon state. The amplitude of the
|1,1,1,1\rangle
-term can be found as

\displaystyle A(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4},\phi)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\langle 1,1,1,1\,|S_{\text{IV}}(r_{4})S_{\text{III}}(r_{3})\Phi_{a}(\phi)S_{\text{II}}(r_{2})S_{\text{I}}(r_{1})|0,0,0,0\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{sech}r_{1}\operatorname{sech}r_{2}\operatorname{sech}r_{3}\operatorname{sech}r_{4}}{2\left(e^{i\phi}\,\tanh r_{1}\,\tanh r_{2}\,\tanh r_{3}\,\tanh r_{4}-1\right)^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Biggl(\frac{e^{i\phi}(\cosh 2r_{3}\cosh 2r_{4}-3)\tanh r_{1}\,\tanh r_{2}}{\cosh^{2}r_{3}\cosh^{2}r_{4}}

\displaystyle\quad-2(1+e^{2i\phi}\tanh^{2}r_{1}\,\tanh^{2}r_{2})\tanh r_{3}\,\tanh r_{4}\Biggr).

(19)

We can simplify for cases where all of the squeezing parameters are the same,
r_{i}\to r
, which leads to

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r,r,r,r,\phi)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=-\,\frac{\operatorname{sech}^{8}r\,\tanh^{2}r}{8\bigl(e^{i\phi}\tanh^{4}r-1\bigr)^{3}}\times\Bigl[3+4\cosh 2r+\cosh 4r

\displaystyle\quad+2e^{i\phi}\bigl(5-\cosh 4r\bigr)+8e^{2i\phi}\sinh^{4}r\Bigr].

(20)

In the low-gain regime, for small values of
r
, we can expand to the 4th order in
r
and find that the amplitude of the
|1,1,1,1\rangle
term is

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r_{\text{small}},r_{\text{small}},r_{\text{small}},r_{\text{small}},\phi)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\bigl(1+e^{i\phi}\bigr)\,r^{2}\;-\;\frac{4}{3}\,\bigl(2+5e^{i\phi}\bigr)\,r^{4}+\mathcal{O}\!\bigl(r^{6}\bigr).

(21)

The second-order term shows a complete cancellation of the term
|1,1,1,1\rangle
. This case corresponds to the possibility that the four photons are either created in the lower two crystals or in the upper two crystals
17
. Exactly the same as in the 2-photon 2-crystal case, these two possibility cancel each other if the phase is
\phi=\pi
.

Interestingly, however, in contrast to the case of 2-photon interference, the higher-order terms wash out the interference pattern (also see Fig.
4
(b)). In fact, the analytical expression Eq. (
20
) shows explicitly that perfect destructive interference of the
|1,1,1,1\rangle
term cannot occur once the squeezing parameter
r
exceeds zero. This is because the amplitude in Eq.(
20
) could vanish only if the polynomial in
e^{i\phi}
inside the brackets were zero, but this quadratic has no roots on the unit circle for any non-zero gain (
r>0
). Thus, the amplitude remains strictly non-zero beyond the low-gain regime.

However, we can find an interesting case of perfect cancellation if not all crystals have the same squeezing
r
, but crystals in each row have the same
r_{i}
, e.g., from Eq. (
19
) we set
r_{1}=r_{2}
and
r_{3}=r_{4}
. This gives

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r_{1},r_{1},r_{3},r_{3},\phi)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{sech}^{2}r_{1}\,\operatorname{sech}^{2}r_{3}}{4\bigl(e^{i\phi}\tanh^{2}r_{1}\tanh^{2}r_{3}-1\bigr)^{3}}\times

\displaystyle\quad\Bigg[e^{i\phi}(-5+\cosh 4r_{3})\operatorname{sech}^{4}r_{3}\,\tanh^{2}r_{1}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\,4\,\bigl(1+e^{2i\phi}\tanh^{4}r_{1}\bigr)\tanh^{2}r_{3}\Bigg].

(22)

At
\phi=\pi
, this simplifies to

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r_{1},r_{1},r_{3},r_{3},\pi)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\cosh^{2}2r_{1}\,\cosh 4r_{3}-3\cosh 4r_{1}+1}{2\bigl(\cosh 2r_{1}\,\cosh 2r_{3}+\bigr)^{3}}.

(23)

Perfect destructive interference occurs when

\displaystyle r_{3}=\operatorname{arcsinh}(\tanh 2r_{1}),

(24)

demonstrating that appropriate choice of asymmetric squeezing enables high-gain cancellation (Fig.
4
(b)).

Interestingly, perfect destructive interference can also occur with zero relative phase when the upper row has asymmetric squeezing. Setting
r_{1}=r_{2}
,
r_{3}\not=r_{4}
and
\phi=0
in analytical expression Eq. (
19
) gives

\displaystyle A(

\displaystyle r_{1},r_{1},r_{3},r_{4},0)_{|1,1,1,1\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{\operatorname{sech}^{6}r_{1}\operatorname{sech}^{3}r_{3}\operatorname{sech}^{3}r_{4}}{16\bigl(\tanh^{2}r_{1}\,\tanh r_{3}\,\tanh r_{4}-1\bigr)^{3}}

\displaystyle\quad\times\Bigg[2\bigl(\cosh{2r_{3}}\cosh{2r_{4}}-3\bigr)\sinh^{2}{2r_{1}}

\displaystyle\quad-\bigl(3+\cosh{4r_{4}}\bigr)\sinh{2r_{3}}\sinh{2r_{4}}\Bigg]

(25)

At first glance, one might expect that with zero relative phase (
\phi=0
), the four-photon term
|1,1,1,1\rangle
would always appear if one follows a naive low-gain intuition, where all crystals are pumped coherently and interfere constructively. However, when the two crystals in the upper row have unequal squeezing strengths (
r_{3}\neq r_{4}
), a nontrivial destructive interference emerges. In this case, the amplitude vanishes when

\displaystyle r_{1}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,\operatorname{arsinh}\!\left(\sqrt{\dfrac{2\,\sinh(2r_{3})\,\sinh(2r_{4})}{\cosh\!\bigl(2(r_{3}-r_{4})\bigr)-3}}\right)\,

\displaystyle|r_{3}-r_{4}|>\operatorname{arcsinh}(1)

The interference pattern is shown in Fig.
4
(c).

An intuitive explanation for cancellation at
\phi=0
emerges from recognizing that photon pair creation and annihilation carry a relative phase of
\pi
, as shown in Eq. (
1
). Any process involving creation followed by annihilation (or vice versa) returns to the original quantum state but with amplitude shifted by
\pi
. This is precisely the physics underlying the single-crystal two-photon interference in Eq. (
6
). In the four-crystal geometry with asymmetric squeezing, complex combinations of creation and annihilation events can interfere destructively even when the geometric phase between rows is zero, provided the squeezing parameters are appropriately tuned.

Conclusion

We present an exact representation of the two-mode squeezing operator acting on arbitrary Fock states. This analytical expression enables us to reinterpret known quantum-interference phenomena and to predict new effects in multi-crystal parametric down-conversion systems. Specifically, we have (i) described two-photon interference in single-crystal systems, (ii) analyzed two-photon interference in two-crystal configurations across both low- and high-gain regimes, (iii) identified the conditions for perfect destructive interference in three-crystal arrangements, and (iv) discovered new four-photon interference effects, including cases where perfect cancellation occurs only under asymmetric squeezing conditions.

While our analysis focuses on the lowest-order Fock components (e.g.,
|1,1\rangle
or
|1,1,1,1\rangle
), the derived expressions remain valid for arbitrary photon numbers. A systematic exploration of interference effects among higher-order Fock states may therefore lead to additional, nontrivial multi-photon interference phenomena. Beyond the few-source settings considered here, the exact analytic Fock-basis framework naturally extends to large-scale quantum-network architectures on integrated photonic platforms, where network-level interference plays a central role
2
. It could also provide analytic guidance for quantum state generation, including high-dimensional and multipartite structures, complementing recent demonstrations and proposals
23
;
5
. The analytical treatment may further help identify robust implementations e.g., where phase fluctuations are limited.

Moreover, our analysis assumes a classical pump, whereas a general description would incorporate the quantum nature of the pump-depletion in parametric down-conversion, described by the three-mode squeezing operator
U=\exp\left(\zeta^{*}p^{\dagger}ab-\zeta pa^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right)
, where
p
represents the pump mode. This generalized model would allow a systematic analysis of quantum interference in experiments such as
28
. It would also be interesting in the future to investigate systems with probabilistic photon sources beyond pair-sources, such as genuine three-photon sources or N-photon sources
15
;
9
;
18
.

Finally, the analytical expression we demonstrate might be useful as the basis for classical simulators of quantum optics experiments, especially for the AI-driven discovery of new experiments with interesting properties that go beyond low-order approximations
29
;
36
.

Normal Ordering Squeezing Operators

The two-mode squeezing transformation, which models the action of a parametric down-conversion crystal, can be described by the operator

S_{2}(\zeta)=\exp\left(\zeta^{*}ab-\zeta a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right),

(26)

where
a
(
a^{\dagger}
) and
b
(
b^{\dagger}
) denote the annihilation (creation) operators of the two modes, and
\zeta=re^{i\theta}
is the complex squeezing parameter. This transformation belongs to the Lie group
\mathrm{SU}(1,1)
, whose generators in the Schwinger representation are defined as
12

K_{+}=a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger},\,\,K_{-}=ab,\,\,K_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left(a^{\dagger}a+b^{\dagger}b+1\right),

(27)

which satisfy the following commutation relations:

\displaystyle[K_{0},K_{\pm}]=\pm K_{\pm},\,\ [K_{+},K_{-}]=-2K_{0}.

(28)

In the following, we show how one can get the normal ordering expression of Eq. (
2
) in the main text from Eq. (
26
). Let’s look at the transformation of the exponential operator
14

\displaystyle e^{\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-}}=e^{\beta K_{+}}e^{\gamma K_{0}}e^{\delta K_{-}}\,.

(29)

Substituting
K_{+}
,
K_{-}
from Eq. (
27
) and setting
\tau=-re^{i\theta}
(i.e.,
\tau^{*}=-re^{-i\theta}
) in the left side of Eq. (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle e^{\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-}}

\displaystyle=e^{-\zeta K_{+}+\zeta^{*}K_{-}}=e^{-\zeta a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}+\zeta^{*}ab}=\exp\left(\zeta^{*}ab-\zeta a^{\dagger}b^{\dagger}\right)=S_{2}(\zeta)

which identifies the two-mode squeezing operator
S_{2}(\zeta)
in Eq. (
26
). The goal now is to determine the coefficients
\beta
,
\gamma
, and
\delta
in the right side of Eq. (
29
).

We begin by examining the operator on the left-hand side of Eq. (
29
) and then define

leading to

. Now we compute

(31)

The nested commutators are recursively defined as

[P,K_{0}]_{n}=[P,[P,K_{0}]_{n-1}],\,\text{with}\quad[P,K_{0}]_{0}=K_{0}.

As we set the parameter
\tau=-\zeta=-re^{i\theta}=re^{i(\theta+\pi)}=\lambda e^{i\phi}

\lambda=|\tau|=r

\phi=\text{Arg}(\tau)=\theta+\pi

32

\displaystyle[P,K_{0}]_{1}

\displaystyle=[P,[P,K_{0}]_{0}]=[\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-},K_{0}]=\tau[K_{+},K_{0}]-\tau^{*}[K_{-},K_{0}]=-\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-}-2\lambda\frac{(e^{i\phi}K_{+}+e^{-i\phi}K_{-})}{2},

\displaystyle[P,K_{0}]_{2}

\displaystyle=[P,[P,K_{0}]_{1}]=[\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-},-\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-}]=-\tau\tau^{*}[K_{+},K_{-}]+\tau\tau^{*}[K_{-},K_{+}]=(2\lambda)^{2}K_{0},

\displaystyle[P,K_{0}]_{3}

\displaystyle=[P,[P,K_{0}]_{2}]=[\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-},(2\lambda)^{2}K_{0}]=(2\lambda)^{2}[\tau K_{+}-\tau^{*}K_{-},K_{0}]=-(2\lambda)^{3}\frac{(e^{i\phi}K_{+}+e^{-i\phi}K_{-})}{2}.

One can continue and obtain the following general result:

\displaystyle[P,K_{0}]_{2n}=(2\lambda)^{2n}K_{0}\,,\,\,\,[P,K_{0}]_{2n+1}=-(2\lambda)^{2n+1}\frac{(e^{i\phi}K_{+}+e^{-i\phi}K_{-})}{2}

\displaystyle e^{P}K_{0}e^{-P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\frac{[P,K_{0}]_{2n}}{(2n)!}+\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\frac{[P,K_{0}]_{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}

(34)

With the hyperbolic functions,

\displaystyle\cosh x=1+\frac{x^{2}}{2!}+\frac{x^{4}}{4!}+...=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n}}{(2n)!}\,,\,\,\,\sinh x=1+\frac{x^{3}}{3!}+\frac{x^{5}}{5!}+...=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{x^{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}

(35)

one can rewrite Eq. (
34

\displaystyle e^{P}K_{0}e^{-P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\frac{[P,K_{0}]_{2n}}{(2n)!}+\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\frac{[P,K_{0}]_{2n+1}}{(2n+1)!}=\sum_{k=2n}^{\infty}\frac{(2\lambda)^{2n}K_{0}}{(2n)!}+\sum_{k=2n+1}^{\infty}\frac{-(2\lambda)^{2n+1}(e^{i\phi}K_{+}+e^{-i\phi}K_{-})}{2\times(2n+1)!}

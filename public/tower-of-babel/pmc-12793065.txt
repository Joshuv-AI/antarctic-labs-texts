Prognostic Analysis of Lung Cancer With Brain Metastases in Elderly Patients: A Multicenter Retrospective Study
Authors: Anqi Li, Daqin Feng, Chang Liu, Chaojue Huang, Tang Li, Donggui Wei, Fangyi Wei, Muling Shen, Congzhi Qin, Shufang Deng, Hui Liang, Panlin Mo, Minhai Dong, Yongjia Yu, Lun Liang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTObjectivesTo explore the prognostic factors affecting overall survival (OS) in patients aged ≥ 65 years with lung cancer BMs.MethodsIn this multicenter retrospective study, 293 patients aged ≥ 65 years diagnosed with lung cancer BMs between 2010 and 2023 were included. We analyzed the correlation between clinical information, treatment methods, molecular data, and OS. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression models were used to identify risk factors, and Kaplan–Meier survival curves were constructed for analysis.ResultsA total of 293 patients aged ≥ 65 years with lung cancer BMs were included (age mean [SD], 69.40 [4.04] years). Univariate analysis of 293 lung cancer patients aged ≥ 65 years with BMs showed that risk prognostic factors included pathological type (SCLC, hazard ratio [HR] = 2.221, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.531–3.224, p < 0.001), location of BMs (HR = 1.428, 95% CI = 1.045–1.951, p = 0.025), diagnosis KPS (HR = 0.693, 95% CI = 0.528–0.909, p = 0.008), and targeted therapy (HR = 0.610, 95% CI = 0.465–0.798, p < 0.001). Multivariate analysis showed that pathological type (SCLC, HR = 1.814, 95% CI = 1.230–2.676, p = 0.003), diagnosis KPS (HR = 0.726, 95% CI = 0.550–0.959, p = 0.024), and targeted therapy (HR = 0.008, 95% CI = 0.506–0.902, p = 0.008) were independent factors of OS in such patients.ConclusionsPathological type, KPS, and targeted therapy significantly impact OS in elderly lung cancer BM patients. Targeted therapy may be a promising option for improving prognosis in this patient population.Trial Registration ClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT05609162

Background

Lung cancer is the most common cancer worldwide and the leading cause of brain metastases (BMs), accounting for 40%–50% of BMs [1, 2, 3]. It is characterized by high incidence, high malignancy, and poor prognosis [3]. Notably, two‐thirds of advanced lung cancer patients are aged ≥ 65 years [4]. In this study, we defined elderly patients as those aged ≥ 65 years. Without treatment, elderly BM patients have a median survival of only 3.2 months [5]. Elderly lung cancer BM patients often present with complex clinical features, poor prognosis, and declining Karnofsky Performance Status (KPS). Patients with BMs always differ in the number, size, and location of lesions, and can exhibit various patterns and progression stages of extracranial disease, as well as a wide range of comorbidities and KPS [6]. The occurrence of BMs in lung cancer patients is associated with multiple high‐risk factors, and regular brain MRI screening is recommended for early diagnosis and treatment [7].

Surgical resection, stereotactic radiosurgery (SRS), fractionated stereotactic radiotherapy (FSRT), whole‐brain radiotherapy (WBRT), immunotherapy, targeted therapy, and systemic therapy are the main treatment modalities for lung cancer BMs [6, 7, 8, 9]. These approaches can improve survival time and local control of BMs. Epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) mutations and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) fusions are two major oncogenic drivers in lung cancer. The latest ASCO‐SNO‐ASTRO Guidelines suggested that accruing evidence supported the utility of targeted therapy (such as tyrosine kinase inhibitors, TKIs) in lung cancer BMs with EGFR or ALK mutations [10]. For EGFR‐mutant tumors: erlotinib, gefitinib, afatinib, dacomitinib, osimertinib (only patients with documented exon 19 deletion or L858R were included). For ALK‐rearranged tumors: crizotinib, alectinib, ceritinib, brigatinib, and lorlatinib (confirmed by FISH break‐apart or Ventana IHC). EGFR mutation status may help identify high‐risk stage I lung adenocarcinoma patients who may benefit from adjuvant chemotherapy [11, 12]. Investigating prognostic factors in different age groups can help define characteristic treatment populations.

As systemic therapies improve, lung cancer patients are living longer and are at increased risk for BMs. However, elderly cancer patients, including those with lung cancer, are underrepresented in clinical trials and may not receive adequate treatment [13]. The prognosis of elderly lung cancer BM patients is worse than previously suggested by conservative algorithms [5], and there is little research on the relevant prognosis and effective treatment for this population type. Although data from a multi‐institutional analysis of 9584 older patients with BMs for lung cancer showed that age ≥ 60 years and localized BMs improved prognosis [14], there were limited research on the behavioral patterns of elderly patients with lung cancer BMs and few relevant survival analyses. To explore the prognosis factors of elderly patients with lung cancer BMs, we conducted a retrospective and multicenter study.

Materials and Methods

Study Population

This multicenter retrospective cohort study included 293 patients aged ≥ 65 years with lung cancer BMs among 1407 cancer BMs patients. The patients were diagnosed and treated at four hospitals: the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, Guangxi Medical University Cancer Hospital, Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region People's Hospital, and Sun Yat‐sen University Cancer Center from 2010 to 2023. The data collection and filtering process is shown in Figure 1. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University, which waived the requirement for informed consent. The study complies with the STROCSS standard and has been approved by the Medical Ethics Committees of the First Affiliated Hospital of Guangxi Medical University (reference number 2025‐E0085). It has also been retrospectively registered on ClinicalTrials.gov.

Patient Selection

Inclusion criteria were: (1) pathological diagnosis of lung cancer; (2) enhanced MRI or pathological immunohistochemical diagnosis of lung cancer BMs; (3) patient age ≥ 65 years at the time of lung cancer BM diagnosis; and (4) complete follow‐up data. Exclusion criteria were: (1) primary tumors originating from systems other than the lungs; (2) nonepithelial cell tumors, such as bronchogenic carcinoma, hilar lymph node carcinoma, pulmonary mesothelioma, etc.; (3) intracranial metastasis limited to the skull or meninges, not involving the brain parenchyma; (4) lack of enhanced MRI or pathological diagnosis for BMs originating from lung cancer; (5) lack of enhanced CT or pathological diagnosis for lung cell carcinoma; and (6) incomplete or missing information.

Outcomes and Follow‐Up

The primary outcome was overall survival (OS) after the diagnosis of lung cancer BMs in patients aged ≥ 65 years. OS was defined as the time from the date of BM diagnosis to the last follow‐up date or date of death. Follow‐up data were collected through clinical visits or telephone consultations.

Covariates

Data were collected from hospital information systems and medical records. Baseline characteristics included age, gender, smoking history, cancer pathological type, extracranial metastasis, location of BMs, BM number, EGFR/ALK status, diagnostic KPS, neurosurgical resection, radiotherapy, chemotherapy, targeted therapy, and immunotherapy.

Statistical Analysis

Use SPSS 25.0 software (Statistical Package for the Social Sciences) to perform statistical processing and analysis on the collected data. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression hazard ratio (HR) models were used to study the potential influencing factors of OS in elderly lung cancer patients with BMs. The HR and 95% confidence interval (CI) of each model were at the significance level of α = 0.05. Proportional‐hazards assumptions were assessed using Schoenfeld residuals. All statistical tests were two‐sided tests, with a p‐value < 0.05 indicating statistical significance. Moreover, multivariate Cox regression analysis was conducted to determine independent predictive factors for prognosis. Construct Kaplan–Meier curves to compare OS of patients with different clinical features and treatments. Proportional‐hazards assumptions were assessed using Schoenfeld residuals.

Results

General Information

In this multicenter cohort retrospective study, clinical information of 1407 lung cancer BM patients from four hospitals was collected, including 293 elderly patients with BM, accounting for 28.8% of the cohort. Among them, 70.0% (n = 205) were male and 30.0% (n = 88) were female. The median OS time was 1.810 and 1.830 years, respectively. The proportion of synchronous BM and non‐synchronous BM of lung cancer was similar, 49.5% (n = 145) and 50.5% (n = 148), respectively. Among the pathological types of lung cancer, the vast majority were non–small‐cell lung cancer (84%); adenocarcinoma accounted for 73.4% (n = 215), squamous cell carcinoma accounted for 6.8% (n = 20). In the location of BMs, 196 patients had supratentorial BMs (66.9%), 74 patients had both supratentorial and infratentorial BMs (25.3%), and only 23 patients had infratentorial BMs (7.8%). Compared with 96 (32.8%) patients with extracranial metastasis, 197 (67.2%) patients without extracranial metastasis accounted for a larger proportion. More than half of the patients had multiple BMs (n = 185, 63.1%), 76 patients (25.3%) had definite EGFR/ALK mutations, and 108 patients (36.9%) had wild‐type mutations. In terms of treatment for BM, more than half of the patients received chemotherapy (n = 115, 59.7%), radiotherapy (n = 168, 57.3%), and most patients did not receive neurosurgical resection (n = 224, 76.5%) or immunotherapy (n = 261, 89.1%). A total of 134 patients (45.7%) received targeted therapy, and 159 patients (54.3%) did not receive targeted therapy.

Univariate Analysis

Among 293 elderly patients with BM from lung cancer, 205 were male and 88 were female. The median OS of women was 1.830 years, which was not significantly different from that of men (1.810 years), and there was no statistical significance. Targeted therapy, pathological type, diagnosis of KPS, location of BMs had statistical significance. It is worth noting that BM location is also an important factor for the median OS of elderly patients with lung cancer BMs (p = 0.034), and the median OS of patients with lesions located on the tentorial surface (1.66 years) is the longest, as shown in Figure 2D. Multivariate Analysis

Taking α = 0.05 as the standard, Cox proportional regression model entry method was used to conduct multivariate analysis on the results of univariate analysis with statistically significant prognosis in elderly patients with lung cancer BM (p < 0.05). The analysis results showed that targeted therapy (HR = 0.676, 95% CI = 0.506–0.902, p = 0.008), pathological type of small cell lung cancer (HR = 1.814, 95% CI = 1.230–2.676, p = 0.004), diagnosis KPS (HR = 0.726, 95% CI = 0.550–0.959, p = 0.024) were independent risk factors affecting the prognosis of elderly patients with lung cancer BMs, as shown in Table 2.

Discussion

We collected clinical and survival data of 293 elderly metastatic patients from four hospitals and identified three risk factors using univariate and multivariate Cox regression models. Our results indicate that targeted therapy can prolong OS in elderly lung cancer BM patients and predict a favorable prognosis (HR = 0.610, 95% CI = 0.465–0.798, p < 0.001). SCLC pathological type (HR = 2.221, 95% CI=1.531–3.224, p < 0.001) and location of BMs (HR = 1.428, 95% CI = 1.045–1.951, p = 0.025) are associated with poor prognosis. Targeted therapy (HR = 0.008, 95% CI: = 0.506–0.902, p = 0.008) and KPS score > 70 (HR = 0.726, 95% CI: = 0.550–0.959, p = 0.024) are independent protective factors for OS in this patient population. Among various treatment modalities, only targeted therapy has improved OS and is associated with significantly better prognosis in these patients. Targeted therapy may provide survival benefits for elderly lung cancer BM patients.

This study found that targeted therapy can indeed prolong OS in elderly lung cancer BM patients. A phase III clinical trial demonstrated that for elderly patients (≥ 75 years) with advanced nonsquamous non–small‐cell lung cancer, maintenance pemetrexed after carboplatin‐pemetrexed treatment was more advantageous than docetaxel monotherapy [15]. EGFR plays an important role in NSCLC and has become a new target for lung cancer treatment [12, 16]. Erlotinib is particularly recommended for the treatment of NSCLC patients with EGFR activating mutations. A phase II trial studied the application of erlotinib in Chinese lung adenocarcinoma patients with BMs, and the results showed that the overall response rate was 58%, and the progression free survival was about 10 months [17]. However, because only a few trials have included (or focused on) untreated patients with BM, further research is needed. Clinical experience with crizotinib in treating advanced ALK‐rearranged NSCLC patients with BMs showed an intracranial disease control rate of 56% and median intracranial time to progression (TTP) of 7 months for asymptomatic BM patients [18]. Currently, most small molecule TKIs have been proven to have a low cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)/plasma ratio [19, 20], and may be applied to patients with BMs of lung cancer in the future.

Notably, although the location of BMs (supratentorial/infratentorial/both) was significantly associated with survival in univariate analysis, this association lost statistical significance in multivariate models adjusted for other prognostic factors (Table 2). This indicates that the effect of tumor location on survival may be largely explained by other stronger pathological or treatment‐related prognostic factors, or its independent effect is relatively weak and unstable in the comprehensive model. Therefore, the location of BMs itself may not be a strong independent prognostic indicator.

Our results suggest that radiotherapy does not affect the prognosis of elderly lung cancer BM patients. Another study indicated that SRS or FSRT is an effective early treatment option for NSCLC‐induced BMs in elderly patients, with good OS and no serious side effects [21]. This difference may be related to the study population of NSCLC patients aged ≥ 65 years with 1–10 BMs. While SRS/FSRT is an emerging standard of care for patients with 1–3 BMs, patients with ≤ 10 BMs can achieve acceptable tumor control with lower neurotoxicity [22].

Elderly BM patients often have poor survival prognoses, which require significant improvement [23]. Treatment choice is crucial. Our results also found that more elderly lung cancer BM patients did not undergo surgical resection or immunotherapy, possibly because elderly cancer patients are less likely to tolerate or benefit from treatment in clinical trials [13]. Nevertheless, the research data of Sun H et al. [14] showed that patients who received surgery, chemotherapy, or radiotherapy achieved a higher survival rate. Although the physical function of elderly patients was reduced, if the body could tolerate it, active treatment could still provide survival benefits. Researchers are concerned that adverse outcomes related to BMs may mask the benefits of the investigated treatment, and many drugs have poor blood–brain barrier and brain tumor penetration, reducing the possibility of meaningful intracranial efficacy [24, 25], which may lead to fewer patients with cancer BMs being included in clinical trials. Whether the elderly cancer group is suitable for these treatments and can obtain survival benefits requires prospective inclusion of more elderly patients in clinical trials.

This multicenter retrospective cohort study aimed to explore the factors affecting the prognosis of elderly lung cancer BM patients. However, there are some limitations. Selection bias is inevitable due to the retrospective nature and inclusion of only patients with complete data. We did not identify the gene mutation population or specific targeted drugs. Additionally, these variables were not further refined. Nearly half of the patients received targeted therapy, but only 25.9% had EGFR/ALK mutations, indicating that some patients carried other mutations or used other targeted drugs, or some patients without mutations also tried targeted therapy. Future studies will further explore the relationship between these variables and prognosis. Moreover, more clinical studies and trials are needed to validate our findings.

Conclusions

To the best of our knowledge, this is a multicenter retrospective cohort study to explore the prognostic factors of elderly patients with metastatic lung cancer. Detailed follow‐up of all eligible patients in this study allowed us to analyze the factors influencing prognosis in this population, including pathological type, diagnostic KPS, and targeted therapy. The survival benefit factors for elderly lung cancer BM patients include diagnostic KPS > 70 and targeted therapy. SCLC pathological type is a poor prognostic factor for this group. Among numerous treatment modalities, only targeted therapy has improved OS and is associated with significantly better prognosis. Targeted therapy may provide survival benefits for this patient population.

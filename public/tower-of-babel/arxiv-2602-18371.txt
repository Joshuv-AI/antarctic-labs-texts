Application of uncertainty principles for decaying densities to the observability of the Schrödinger equation
Kévin Le Balc’h, Jiaqi Yu

Abstract
In this article, we study the Schrödinger equation posed in the Euclidean space. We prove observability inequalities for measurable sets that are thick with respect to decaying densities. The proof relies on quantitative uncertainty principles adapted to decaying densities, notably those established by Shubin, Vakilian, Wolff, and Kovrijkine.



1
Introduction

1.1
Observability estimates of the Schrödinger equation

For
d\geqslant 1
, let us consider the free Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=0&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is a well-known fact that
i\Delta
generates a continuous unitary semigroup in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denoted by
(e^{it\Delta})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

We start with the following definition.

Definition 1.1

(Observability inequalities)
.

•

For
T>0
and
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
a measurable set, we say that (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
if there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.2)

•

For
T>S>0
and
\mathcal{O}_{1},\mathcal{O}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
two measurable sets, we say that (
1.1
) is observable in
(\{T\}\times\mathcal{O}_{1})\cup(\{S\}\times\mathcal{O}_{2})
if there exists a positive constant
C>0
such that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}|e^{iT\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}|e^{iS\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx\right)\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.3)

Note that in (
1.2
) the observation appears on the product set
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
, this is the usual definition of observability. The observability at two-time points (
1.3
) has been introduced in
[
26
]
, the observation here appears on two differents sets
\{T\}\times\mathcal{O}_{1}
and
\{S\}\times\mathcal{O}_{2}
. The two notions model the possibility of estimating the (whole)
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-norm of the initial wave function
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}
by some restricted norm of the wave function
u=u(t,z)
. For instance if the initial datum is normalized,
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=1
, the expression
\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx
is the probability of finding the particle in the set
\mathcal{O}
at time
t
. Having
\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\geqslant C^{-1}>0
for all solutions of (
1.1
) means that every quantum particle spends a positive fraction of time of the interval
(0,T)
in the set
\mathcal{O}
. On the other hand, having
\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}|e^{iT\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}|e^{iS\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx\geqslant C^{-1}
means that every quantum particle visits either
\mathcal{O}_{1}
at time
T
, or
\mathcal{O}_{2}
at time
S
.

The goal of the article is to provide sufficient conditions of observability of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) based on uncertainty principles for decaying densities.

1.2
Main results

Let us introduce the following definition.

Definition 1.2

(Thick set with respect to a density)
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1]
, and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
be a continous positive function.
A set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick
if

|\mathcal{O}\cap B(x,\rho(x))|\geqslant\gamma\,|B(x,\rho(x))|\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(1.4)

where
|\cdot|
stands for the Lebesgue measure in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

A set
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is said to be thick if there exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
and
a>0
such that
\mathcal{O}
is
(\gamma,a)
-thick.

Our first two main results focus on the observability notion defined in (
1.2
).

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
be a continuous positive function such that

\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1})\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(1.5)

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for some time
T>0
. More precisely, there exist
T_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for every
T\geqslant T_{0}
, (
1.2
) holds.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
be a continuous positive function such that

\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\alpha})\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(1.6)

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
. More precisely, there exists
C>0
such that for every
T>0
,

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ce^{\frac{C}{T^{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(1.7)

Bibliographical comments.
In the one-dimensional case, i.e.
d=1
, it has been shown in
[
17
]
and
[
7
]
that the free Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
at some time
T>0
if and only if
\mathcal{O}
is thick (
\rho(x)\equiv a>0
). The observability at every time
T>0
has been recently obtained in
[
23
]
, always assuming that
\mathcal{O}
is a thick set. In the multi-dimensional case, i.e.
d\geqslant 2
, only partial results have been obtained for identifying a necessary and sufficient condition on
\mathcal{O}
for the observability (
1.2
) to hold. Let us sum up the known results. It has first been shown in
[
17
]
that the thickness property is a necessary condition for (
1.2
). The other cases concern sufficient condition. From
[
3
]
,
[
24
]
, one can deduce that (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
if
\mathcal{O}
is an open set satisfying the so-called Geometric Control Condition (GCC). This condition roughly tells that every infinite line intersects
\mathcal{O}
. In
[
26
]
, the authors have proved that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is such that
|\mathcal{O}^{c}|<+\infty
then (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
. In
[
29
]
,
[
24
]
, the authors prove that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a periodic open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then (
1.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
. A generalization to periodic measurable subset in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
has further been obtained in
[
12
]
. Let us also mention that for Schrödinger equations with a confining potential,
[
21
]
characterizes open sets for which observability holds.

From the previous discussion, we deduce that our main results from
Theorem

1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
only provide sufficient conditions for the observability (
1.2
) to hold. They are not sharp as the one-dimensional situation tells us. It is however interesting to compare these conditions with the other exhibited conditions in the litterature. First, the thickness condition with respect to
\rho
, with
\rho
satisfying either (
1.5
) or (
1.6
) imply the usual thickness condition (constant density). This is in adequation with the fact that the thickness property is a necessary condition for (
1.2
) to hold. On the other hand, it is interesting to remark that the observation set
\mathcal{O}=\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\ ;\ |xy|>C\}
is a thick set with respect to
\rho(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-1})
but
\mathcal{O}
does not satisfy (GCC), neither
|\mathcal{O}^{c}|<+\infty
. Conversely, one can also construct a set satisfying both (GCC) and
|\mathcal{O}^{c}|<+\infty
, but does not satisfy the thickness property (
1.4
) with respect to
\rho(x)
satisfying (
1.5
). Indeed, take
\mathcal{O}=\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\left(\cup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}B(x_{k},|x_{k}|^{-1/2})\right)
where
x_{k}=2^{k}e_{1}
.

Our third main result focus on the observability notion at two times points defined in (
1.3
).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\alpha>0
,
\rho_{\alpha}
and
\rho_{1/\alpha}
be two continuous positive functions such that

\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\alpha}),\ \rho_{1/\alpha}(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x^{-1/\alpha}|)\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(1.8)

For any
T>S>0
,
there exists
\gamma=\gamma(T-S)\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
thick set and
\mathcal{O}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho_{1/\alpha})
thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) is observable in
(\{T\}\times\mathcal{O}_{1})\cup(\{S\}\times\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

Before continuing, we make the following two remarks.

Remark 1.6
.

It is interesting to note that
Theorem

1.3
could be deduced from
Theorem

1.5
for arbitrary
T>0
, at the cost that the observation set
\mathcal{O}
depends on
T
. Indeed, fix
T_{0}>0
and choose
\alpha=1
. Then, by
Theorem

1.5
, there exists

\gamma=\gamma(T_{0})\in(0,1)
and a
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thick set

\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that for all times
T\geqslant S+T_{0}>T_{0}
, for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}|e^{iT\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}|e^{iS\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx\right).

By integrating for
S\in(0,T_{0})
, taking
T=S+T_{0}
, we obtain that

\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\left(\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}|e^{i(S+T_{0})\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdS+\int_{0}^{T_{0}}\int_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}|e^{iS\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdS\right),

so

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{2T_{0}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt.

This leads to (
1.2
).

Remark 1.7
.

In some sense,
Theorem

1.5
is sharp in the situation where
\mathcal{O}_{1}
,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
are measurable thick sets with respect to the same density
\rho
. Indeed, in this case,
Theorem

1.5
asserts that a sufficient condition that guarantees (

) to hold is that
\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1})
as

. On the reverse side, as we will see below, see

, if

is a continuous positive function such that

then for every
\gamma\in(0,1)
, one could find a
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set
\mathcal{O}
such that the inequality (
1.3
) fails for
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

2
Main ingredients of the proof

2.1
Quantitative uncertainty principles for decaying densities

In this part, we state a quantitative uncertainty principle due to Kovrijkine
[
11
]
, strongly inspired by the one of Shubin, Vakilian and Wolff
[
22
]
. This ingredient will be the heart of the proof of our main results
Theorem

1.3
,
Theorem

1.4
and
Theorem

1.5
.

For a function
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the Fourier transform of
f
is defined by

\widehat{f}(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)e^{-2i\pi\langle x,\xi\rangle}dx,

(2.1)

that can be classically extended to all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The following result holds.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let
\rho_{1}=\rho_{1}(|x|)
,
\rho_{2}=\rho_{2}(|x|)
be two continuous positive radial functions, such that there exist
C_{1},C_{2}>0
,

\frac{C_{1}}{\rho_{1}\left(\frac{C_{2}}{\rho_{2}(t)}\right)}\geqslant t\hskip 20.00003pt\forall t\geqslant 0.

(2.2)

Then there exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
C=C(d,\gamma)>0
such that for every
(\gamma,\rho_{1})
-thick set
\mathcal{O}
and every
(\gamma,\rho_{2})
-thick set
\Omega
, and every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx\leqslant C\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f(x)|^{2}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right).

(2.3)

A standard choice of the densities is
\rho_{1}(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-\alpha})
and
\rho_{2}(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-1/\alpha})
for
\alpha>0
. The case
\alpha=1
has been initiated by
[
22
]
. In this latter case,
\mathcal{O}
and
\Omega
are thick sets with respect to the same density
\rho(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-1})
. The decaying assumption is also known to be sharp according to

Remark 2.2
.

If
\rho(x)
is such that
|x|\rho(x)\to+\infty
, then for every
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, there exists a
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set
\mathcal{O}_{N}
and a function
f_{N}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

\int_{\mathcal{O}_{N}}|f_{N}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathcal{O}_{N}}|\widehat{f_{N}}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leqslant\frac{\|f_{N}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}}{N}.

(2.4)

In particular, the estimate (
2.3
) cannot hold whatever
\gamma\in(0,1)
is.

The equation (
2.3
) is called a quantitative uncertainty principle. We recall that roughly speaking, uncertainty principle states that a function and its Fourier transform cannot both be arbitrarily well-localized. A well-known (qualitative) “uncertainty principle” proved by Benedicks
[
2
]
states that if a function
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is such that
|\mathrm{supp}(f)|<+\infty
and
|\mathrm{supp}(\widehat{f})|<+\infty
then
f\equiv 0
. A quantitative form of this uncertainty principle was given by Amrein, Berthier, see
[
1
]
. A sharp version was obtained by Nazarov
[
20
]
in the one-dimensional case. This result was later extended to the multi-dimensional case by Jaming
[
8
]
. Another related quantitative uncertainty principle is the theorem of Logvinenko and Sereda
[
16
]
. It expresses that a band-limited function cannot vanish on a set of positive density. A quantitative dependence on the spectral width
b
, the thickness parameter
\gamma
in Logvinenko, Sereda’s theorem was later obtained by Kovrijkine in
[
10
]
. This result was finally used by Egidi, Veselic
[
4
]
and independently by Wang, Wang, Zhang, Zhang
[
25
]
to prove that the observability in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
of the heat equation holds if and only if
\mathcal{O}
is a thick set.

2.2
Abstract results for the observability of the Schrödinger equation

The proof of
Theorem

1.3
relies crucially on the following criterion of resolvent estimates.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
19
, Theorem 5.1]
)
.

Assume that the observability estimate (
1.2
) holds in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
) then there
exist positive constants
M>0
and
m>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant M\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall\lambda\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.5)

Conversely, if the observability resolvent estimate (
2.5
) hold for some constants
M>0
and
m>0
,
then for all
\varepsilon>0
, there is a constant
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the observability estimate (
1.2
) holds in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
with
C_{obs}=C_{\varepsilon}mT/(T^{2}-M(\pi^{2}+\varepsilon))
for every time
T>\sqrt{M(\pi^{2}+\varepsilon)}
.

The proof of
Theorem

1.4
mainly follows the strategy of
[
23
]
. We formulate an abstract result, that roughly says that the thickness condition together with high-frequency resolvent estimates lead to observability of the Schrödinger equation.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a thick set. Assume there exist
m>0,\,\lambda_{0}>0
such that

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant M(\lambda)\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0},

(2.6)

where
M(\lambda)>0
is decreasing and satisfies the limit condition

\lim_{\lambda\to\infty}M(\lambda)=0.

(2.7)

Then, there exists
C=C(d,\mathcal{O})
such for any
T>0
, we have the observability estimate

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ce^{\frac{C}{T^{2}}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(2.8)

Theorem

2.4
is not explictly stated in
[
23
]
, this is why we decide to give a sketch of the proof following
[
23
]
of this result in
Appendix

A
below.

Let us make some comments on Theorem
2.4
. First, we know from
[
17
]
that the thickness condition on
\mathcal{O}
is necessary for the observability (
2.8
) to hold. This assumption turns out to be equivalent to the following spectral inequality

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant e^{C_{spec}(1+\lambda)}\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\;\mbox{ for any }f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\mbox{ with supp }\hat{f}\subset B(0,\lambda),

(2.9)

according to
[
10
]
. Finally, this assumption is also equivalent to the observability of the heat equation in the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, i.e.

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ce^{\frac{C}{T}}\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),

(2.10)

according to
[
4
]
and
[
25
]
. In our proof of Theorem
2.4
, we will directly use (
2.10
) instead of the thickness condition to obtain (
2.8
).

The proof of
Theorem

1.5
is based on the equivalence between uncertainty principles and observability at two-times points of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
).

Theorem 2.5

(
[
26
]
)
.

Let
A
and
B
be two measurable subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The following propositions are equivalent.

(i) There exists a positive constant
C_{1}(d,A,B)
so that for each
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x)|^{2}dx\leqslant C_{1}(d,A,B)\left(\int_{A}|f(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{B}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right).

(2.11)

(ii) There exists a positive constant
C_{2}\big(n,A,B\big)
so that for each
T>0
and each
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx\leqslant C_{2}(d,A,B)\Big(\int_{A}|u_{0}(x)|^{2}dx+\int_{2TB}|e^{iT\Delta}u_{0}|^{2}dx\Big).

(2.12)

Furthermore, when one of the above two propositions holds, the constants
C_{1}(d,A,B)
and
C_{2}(d,A,B)
can be chosen as the same number.

3
Proofs of the main results

This part is devoted to the proof of our main results.

3.1
Resolvent estimates

In this part, we derive resolvent estimates.

In the following, we denote by
C_{d}^{\prime}
a universal positive constant depending only on
d
and by
\omega_{d}
the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

The goal of this part is to establish the following result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\alpha\geqslant 1
,
\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min\{1,|x|^{-\alpha}\}
. There exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda_{0}=(\frac{3^{2+1/\alpha}C_{d}^{\prime}}{(1-\gamma)\omega_{d}}+1)^{2}
,
c=c(d,\gamma,\alpha)>0
such that for any
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thick set, for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,

c\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant M(\lambda)\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0},

where

M(\lambda)=\lambda^{-(1-1/\alpha)}.

(3.1)

The proof of
Proposition

3.1
is postponed to the end of this part.

In the following, for
\beta>0
and
\lambda>0
, define

A_{\lambda,\beta}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,\big||\xi|-\sqrt{\lambda}\big|\leqslant\lambda^{-\beta/2}\}.

Now, we give the thinness property of
A_{\lambda,\beta}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, we define
L=\frac{3^{1+\beta}C_{d}^{\prime}}{\varepsilon\omega_{d}}
.
Take

\tilde{\rho}(x)=\min\{L,L^{1+\beta}|x|^{-\beta}\}.

Then, there exists
{\lambda}_{0}=(2L+1)^{2}>0
such that,

|A_{\lambda,\beta}\cap B(x,\tilde{\rho}(x))|\leqslant\varepsilon|B(x,\tilde{\rho}(x))|\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\,\ \forall\lambda\geqslant{\lambda}_{0}.

Proof.

We set

a:=\lambda^{1/2}-\lambda^{-\beta/2},\hskip 20.00003ptb:=\lambda^{1/2}+\lambda^{-\beta/2},

so that
A_{\lambda,\beta}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:a\leqslant|\xi|\leqslant b\}
.

Step 1: Case
|x|\leqslant L
.
For all
\beta>0
,

\tilde{\rho}(x)=\min(L,L^{1+\beta}|x|^{-\beta})=L
, so
B(x,\tilde{\rho})\subset B(0,2L)
.
Since
\lambda\geqslant{\lambda}_{0}=(2L+1)^{2}
, we have
a\geqslant{\lambda}_{0}^{1/2}-{\lambda}_{0}^{-\beta/2}>{\lambda}_{0}^{1/2}-1=2L
. Thus,
A_{\lambda,\beta}\subset B(0,2L)^{c}
and
A_{\lambda,\beta}\cap B(x,\tilde{\rho}(x))=\emptyset
. The claim is trivial.

Step 2: Case
|x|>L
.

Here
\tilde{\rho}(x)=L^{1+\beta}|x|^{-\beta}<L
.
Denote

E:=A_{\lambda,\beta}\cap B(x,\tilde{\rho}).

We write
\xi=r\omega
, where
r=|\xi|\geqslant 0
and
\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
The volume element is
d\xi=r^{d-1}dr\,d\sigma(\omega)
, where
d\sigma
is the surface measure on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
.
Thus,

|E|=\int_{a}^{b}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\mathbf{1}_{\{|r\omega-x|<\tilde{\rho}\}}\,d\sigma(\omega)\,r^{d-1}\,dr.

For each
r\in[a,b]
define

\Omega_{r}:=\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|r\omega-x|<\tilde{\rho}\}.

Then

|E|=\int_{a}^{b}\sigma(\Omega_{r})\,r^{d-1}\,dr.

Fix
r\in[a,b]
and suppose
\Omega_{r}\neq\emptyset
.
Pick
\omega_{0}\in\Omega_{r}
.
Then for any
\omega\in\Omega_{r}
,

|\omega-\omega_{0}|\leqslant\frac{|r\omega-r\omega_{0}|}{r}=\frac{|(r\omega-x)-(r\omega_{0}-x)|}{r}\leqslant\frac{|r\omega-x|+|r\omega_{0}-x|}{r}<\frac{2\tilde{\rho}}{r}.

Hence
\Omega_{r}\subset\{\omega\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}:|\omega-\omega_{0}|<2\tilde{\rho}/r\}
. For small
\delta>0
the surface measure of a spherical cap of Euclidean radius
\delta
on
\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
is bounded by
C_{d}\delta^{d-1}
for some constant
C_{d}
depending only on
d
.
Therefore,

\sigma(\Omega_{r})\leqslant C_{d}\Big(\frac{2\tilde{\rho}}{r}\Big)^{d-1}=C_{d}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{d-1}}{r^{d-1}},

where
C_{d}^{\prime}=C_{d}2^{d-1}
.

Substitute this bound into the integral

|E|\leqslant\int_{a}^{b}C_{d}^{\prime}\frac{\tilde{\rho}^{d-1}}{r^{d-1}}\,r^{d-1}\,dr=2C_{d}^{\prime}L^{(1+\beta)(d-1)}\lambda^{-\beta/2}|x|^{-\beta(d-1)}.

Since the intersection is nonempty, there exists
\xi\in E
satisfying
|\xi-x|\leqslant\tilde{\rho}(x)<L
and
|\xi|\leqslant b<\lambda^{1/2}+1
.
Then,

|x|\leqslant|\xi-x|+|\xi|<L+\lambda^{1/2}+1<3L\lambda^{1/2}.

Thus,

\frac{|A_{\lambda,\beta}\cap B(x,\tilde{\rho})|}{|B(x,\tilde{\rho})|}=\frac{|E|}{\omega_{d}(L^{1+\beta}|x|^{-\beta})^{d}}\leqslant\frac{2C_{d}^{\prime}}{\omega_{d}L^{1+\beta}}\lambda^{-\beta/2}|x|^{\beta}\leqslant\frac{3^{1+\beta}C_{d}^{\prime}}{\omega_{d}}L^{-1}=\varepsilon.

This completes the proof.
∎

Then we give the proof of Proposition
3.1
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1
.

We borrow some ideas from
[
5
]
. Note that, following the proof of
[
5
, Lemma 1]
, one has the algebraic argument.
Let
s>0
, there exists
c_{s}>0
, such that

|\tau^{s}-\lambda|\geqslant c_{s}D(\lambda+D^{s})^{1-1/s},

(3.2)

for all
\tau,\lambda\geqslant 0
in the region
|\tau-\lambda^{1/s}|>D
.

By
Lemma

3.2
, for every
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,
A_{\lambda,1/\alpha}^{c}
is a
(\gamma,\tilde{\rho}_{1/\alpha})
-thick set, where
\tilde{\rho}_{1/\alpha}=\min\{L,L^{1+1/\alpha}|x|^{-1/\alpha}\}
,
L=\frac{3^{1+1/\alpha}C_{d}^{\prime}}{(1-\gamma)\omega_{d}}
. Then, by calculation, we have

\frac{L}{\tilde{\rho}_{1/\alpha}\big(\frac{L}{\rho_{\alpha}(t)}\big)}\geqslant t,\;\hskip 10.00002pt\mbox{for all }t\geqslant 0.

From Kovrijkine’s uncertainty principle stated in
Theorem

2.1
, we obtain that
for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,
g\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mbox{supp }\hat{g}\subset A_{\lambda,1/\alpha}
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|g|^{2}dx\leqslant C(d,\gamma,L)\int_{\mathcal{O}}|g(x)|^{2}dx.

(3.3)

Denote by
P_{\lambda}
the projection
P_{\lambda}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\mathbbm{1}_{A_{\lambda,1/\alpha}}\mathcal{F}(f))
. Applying (
3.2
) with
\tau=|\xi|,\,s=2,\,D=\lambda^{-1/(2\alpha)}
, we obtain that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\displaystyle\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big||\xi|^{2}-\lambda\big|^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geqslant\int_{A_{\lambda,1/\alpha}^{c}}\big||\xi|^{2}-\lambda\big|^{2}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\geqslant c_{2}^{2}\lambda^{-1/\alpha}(\lambda+\lambda^{-1/\alpha})\int_{A_{\lambda,1/\alpha}^{c}}|\hat{f}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=c_{2}^{2}\lambda^{1-1/\alpha}(1+\lambda^{-(1+1/\alpha)})\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

(3.4)

Then, together (
3.3
) with (
3.1
), for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
and every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|P_{\lambda}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+(2C+1)\|(I-P_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+(2C+1)\frac{\lambda^{-(1-1/\alpha)}}{c_{2}^{2}(1+\lambda^{-(1+1/\alpha)})}\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant 2C\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+(2C+1)c_{2}^{-2}\lambda^{-(1-1/\alpha)}\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Thus, the proof is complete.
∎

3.2
Proofs of the main results

In this part, we prove the main results.

We start with a technical lemma strongly inspired by
[
18
, Lemma 5.6]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\gamma>0
and
\rho_{1},\rho_{2}
be two continuous positive functions satisfying

0<\rho_{1}(x)\leqslant\rho_{2}(x),\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.5)

Assume that
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
subset which is
(\gamma,\rho_{1})
-thick.
Then
\mathcal{O}
is
(\gamma/9^{d},3\rho_{2})
-thick.

Proof.

By hypothesis we have

|\omega\cap B(x,\rho_{1}(x))|\geqslant\gamma\,|B(x,\rho_{1}(x))|,\hskip 20.00003pt\forall x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

(3.6)

Fix
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We first observe the inclusion

B\big(x,\rho_{2}(x)\big)\subset\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in B(x,\rho_{2}(x))\\
\rho_{1}(y)\leqslant 2\rho_{2}(x)\end{subarray}}B\big(y,\rho_{1}(y)\big).

Indeed, if
z\in B(x,\rho_{2}(x))
and
\rho_{1}(z)>2\rho_{2}(x)
,
consider the continuous function
f(t)=\rho_{1}(tz+(1-t)x)
for
t\in[0,1]
. We have

f(0)=\rho_{1}(x)\leqslant\rho_{2}(x)
and
f(1)=\rho_{1}(z)>2\rho_{2}(x)
,
so by continuity there exists
t_{0}\in(0,1)
such that for
y:=t_{0}z+(1-t_{0})x
one has

\rho_{1}(y)=2\rho_{2}(x)
and
z\in B(y,\rho_{1}(y))
. Moreover
y\in B(x,\rho_{2}(x))
,
hence the claim.

By compactness there exists a finite family
(x_{i})_{0\leqslant i\leqslant N}\subset B(x,\rho_{2}(x))

such that

B\big(x,\rho_{2}(x)\big)\subset\bigcup_{i=0}^{N}B\big(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i})\big),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\rho(x_{i})\leqslant 2\rho_{2}(x)\ \text{ for all }i.

(3.7)

Apply the Vitali covering lemma to the finite family
\{B(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i}))\}_{i=0}^{N}
: there exists
a subset
S\subset\{0,\dots,N\}
such that the balls
(B(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i})))_{i\in S}
are pairwise disjoint
and

\bigcup_{i=0}^{N}B(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i}))\subset\bigcup_{i\in S}B(x_{i},3\rho_{1}(x_{i})).

Using (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and the disjointness, we get

\displaystyle|\omega\cap B(x,3\rho_{2}(x))|

\displaystyle\geqslant\sum_{i\in S}|\omega\cap B(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i}))|\geqslant\gamma\sum_{i\in S}|B(x_{i},\rho_{1}(x_{i}))|

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{3^{d}}\sum_{i\in S}|B(x_{i},3\rho_{1}(x_{i}))|\geqslant\frac{\gamma}{3^{d}}\Big|\bigcup_{i\in S}B(x_{i},3\rho_{1}(x_{i}))\Big|

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{\gamma}{3^{d}}\,|B(x,\rho_{2}(x))|=\frac{\gamma}{9^{d}}\,|B(x,3\rho_{2}(x))|.

The chain of inclusions/estimates above uses that
B(x,\rho_{2}(x))\subset\bigcup_{i\in S}B(x_{i},3\rho_{1}(x_{i}))

and the volume scaling
|B(\cdot,3r)|=3^{d}|B(\cdot,r)|
.

The conclusion then follows.
∎

With this at hand, we then prove
Theorem

1.3
.

Proof of
Theorem

1.3
.

Noticing the fact
\rho_{\alpha}(x)\leqslant 1
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\alpha>0
, we derive that
\mathcal{O}
is a
(\gamma/9^{d},3)
-thick set from
Lemma

3.3
.

Step 1: Reduction to
\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-\alpha})
.
From Lemma
3.3
, it is not restrictive to assume that
\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-\alpha})
. Indeed, by hypothesis, we have that there exists
c>0
such that

0<c\rho_{\alpha}(x)\leqslant\frac{\min(1,|x|^{-\alpha})}{3}.

(3.8)

So (
3.5
) is fulfilled, then the
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thickness of
\mathcal{O}
directly implies the
(\gamma 9^{-d},\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min(1,|x|^{-\alpha}))
thickness of
\mathcal{O}
.

Step 2: Proof of the resolvent estimates for every
\lambda
.

By
Proposition

3.1
, there exist
C,m,\lambda_{0}>0
, depending only on
d,\gamma,\alpha
, such that for every
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
, we have

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\|(-\Delta-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

(3.9)

For
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
,
if
|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1
, we get
||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|<2\lambda_{0}+1
, and

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi\leqslant(2\lambda_{0}+1)^{2}\int_{|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=(2\lambda_{0}+1)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\{|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1\}}\mathcal{F}(f)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant e^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\{|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1\}}\mathcal{F}(f)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}\big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\{|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}+1\}}\mathcal{F}(f)|^{2}dx\big)

\displaystyle\leqslant 2e^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}\big(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f|^{2}dx+\int_{|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}+1}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi\big).

(3.10)

In (
3.2
), the second inequality follows from Theorem 1 in
[
9
]
and the fact
\mathcal{O}
is a thick set; the last inequality follows from
||\xi|^{2}-\lambda|=|\xi|^{2}-\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}+1-\lambda>1
whenever
|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}+1
and
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
.
Note that
||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|<||\xi|^{2}-\lambda|

if
|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}>\lambda
. By using (
3.9
) with
\lambda=\lambda_{0}
and (
3.2
), we then derive

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\|(-\Delta-\lambda_{0})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle=C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant C\Big(\int_{|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi+\int_{|\xi|^{2}<\lambda_{0}+1}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda_{0}|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi\Big)+m\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(2Ce^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}+C\big)\int_{|\xi|^{2}\geqslant\lambda_{0}}||\xi|^{2}-\lambda|^{2}|\hat{f}|^{2}d\xi+\big(2Ce^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}+m\big)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leqslant\big(2Ce^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}+C\big)\|(-\Delta-\lambda_{0})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\big(2Ce^{C\lambda_{0}^{1/2}}+m\big)\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

Therefore, there exist
C,m>0
, depending only on
d,\gamma,\alpha
, such that (
3.9
) holds for all
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
. By the abstract result of
Theorem

2.3
, we obtain the observability estimate (
1.2
) for
T>\sqrt{C(\pi^{2}+\varepsilon)}
with
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

.

.

Since

, it follows from

.

By Theorem
2.1
, fix
\gamma
such that for every
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thick set
A
and every
(\gamma,\rho_{1/\alpha})
-thick set

, and every

For any

4
Conclusion and extensions

In this paper, we derived sufficient conditions for the observability of the Schrödinger equation (
1.1
), based on uncertainty principles with respect to decaying densities. These sufficient conditions turn out to be non-sharp according to the one-dimensional case, where it has been proved that the necessary and sufficient condition for the observability of (
1.1
) is the usual thickness property. In this part, we discuss other situations where the methods of the paper can be applied to prove new results.

4.1
On the fractional Schrödinger equation

For
d\geqslant 1
,
s>1/2
, we are interested in the fractional free Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u-(-\Delta)^{s}u=0&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(4.1)

where
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

The observability notion in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
is defined as follows

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{-it(-\Delta)^{s}}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(4.2)

We have the following generalizations of
Theorem

1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\alpha=\frac{1}{2s-1}
,
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
be a continuous positive function such that

\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\alpha})\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(4.3)

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
4.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for some time
T>0
. More precisely, there exist
T_{0}>0
and
C>0
such that for every
T\geqslant T_{0}
, (
4.2
) holds.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\alpha>\frac{1}{2s-1}
and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
be a continuous positive function such that

\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\alpha})\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(4.4)

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
4.1
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
. More precisely, for every
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that (
4.2
) holds.

We deduce from the two above results that as
s\to+\infty
,
\alpha\to 0
, so the required thickness assumption on
\mathcal{O}
becomes weaker.

The main ingredient of the proof is based on the following resolvent estimates.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\alpha\geqslant\frac{1}{2s-1}
,
\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min\{1,|x|^{-\alpha}\}
. There exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda_{0,s}=(\frac{3^{2+1/\alpha}C_{d}^{\prime}}{(1-\gamma)\omega_{d}}+1)^{2s}
,
c=c(d,\gamma,\alpha)>0
such that for any
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thick set, for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,

c\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant M(\lambda)\|((-\Delta)^{s}-\lambda)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}.

where

M(\lambda)=\lambda^{-(2-1/s-1/(\alpha s))}.

(4.5)

A way to come back to
Proposition

3.1
is first to consider

A_{\lambda,\beta,s}=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\,||\xi|-\lambda^{\frac{1}{2s}}|\leqslant\lambda^{-\beta/(2s)}\}.

Then
A_{\lambda,\beta,s}=A_{\lambda^{1/s},\beta}
.
By
Lemma

3.2
, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is
\lambda_{0,s}=(\lambda_{0})^{s}
such that
A_{\lambda,\beta,s}
is a
(\varepsilon,\tilde{\rho}_{\beta})
-thin set. Here,
\lambda_{0},\,\tilde{\rho}_{\beta}=\tilde{\rho}
are given in
Lemma

3.2
. The rest of the proof is analogous to
Proposition

3.1
, and is left to the reader.

4.2
On the Schrödinger equation with a real-valued bounded potential

Let
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R})
, let us consider the Schrödinger equation

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u-Vu=0&\text{ in }(0,+\infty)\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\end{array}\right.

(4.6)

where
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. It is a well-known fact that
i(\Delta-V)
generates a continuous unitary semigroup in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denoted by
(e^{it(\Delta-V)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
.

We have the following generalization of
Theorem

1.4
.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\alpha>1
and
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,+\infty)
be a continuous positive function such that

\rho(x)=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-\alpha})\ \text{as}\ |x|\to\infty.

(4.7)

There exists
\gamma\in(0,1)
such that if
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a measurable
(\gamma,\rho)
-thick set, then the Schrödinger equation (
4.6
) is observable in
(0,T)\times\mathcal{O}
for every time
T>0
. More precisely, there exists
C>0
such that for every
T>0
,

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ce^{C/T^{2}}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{O}}|e^{it(\Delta-V)}u_{0}|^{2}dxdt\hskip 20.00003pt\forall u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

A direct application of
Proposition

3.1
in the case
\alpha>1
, observing that
M(\lambda)\to 0
as
\lambda\to+\infty
enables to prove

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\alpha>1
,
\rho_{\alpha}(x)=\min\{1,|x|^{-\alpha}\}
. There exist
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}(d,\gamma,\alpha,\|V\|_{\infty})
,
c=c(d,\gamma,\alpha,|V\|_{\infty})>0
such that for any
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
measurable
(\gamma,\rho_{\alpha})
-thick set, for any
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}
,

c\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant M(\lambda)\|(-\Delta-\lambda+V)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\forall f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\ \forall\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{0}

where

M(\lambda)=\lambda^{-(1-1/\alpha)}.

(4.8)

This corresponds roughly speaking to a high frequency resolvent estimate. One still needs to prove a low frequency resolvent estimate. Note that previously, it was based on the spectral estimate of Kovrijkine’s result from
[
10
]
. In our case of a perturbation by a potential, one cannot use this result. We rather use the recent spectral estimate of
[
13
, Theorem 2.12]
for the operator
-\Delta+V
, when
\mathcal{O}
is a thick set, that directly gives the observability of the associated parabolic equation. The remaining details of the proof are then left to the reader.

Appendix A
Proof of
Theorem

2.4

The goal of this part is to prove
Theorem

2.4
. Our argument is entirely based on the strategy of
[
23
]
, which treats the one-dimensional case. We adapt this strategy to the higher-dimensional setting. For completeness, we provide a sketch of the proof and describe its main steps.

Proof.

Step 1: Observability for the inhomogeneous backward heat equation.

First, the thickness condition implies the spectral estimate (
2.9
) so by Lemma 2.3, Lemma 2.4 in
[
25
]
, we have

\|e^{T\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant e^{C(C_{spec}+1)^{2}(1+1/T)}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt.

(A.1)

Then, we consider the
d
-dim inhomogeneous backward heat equation:

\partial_{t}u+(\Delta-1)u=F\in L^{\infty}((0,\infty);L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})),\ u|_{t=T}=u_{T}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(A.2)

Analogous to Corollary 3.12 in
[
23
]
,
by (
A.1
) and reversing the time
t
to
T-t
, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on
d,\,\mathcal{O}
, such that for any
T>0
and any solution
u
of (
A.2
), we have

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ce^{\frac{C}{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\big(\|(-\Delta+1)u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}+\|F(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\big)dt.

(A.3)

Step 2: Observability for Schrödinger equation in high-frequency part.

By the resolvent estimate (
2.6
) and the properties of
M(\lambda)
, arguing as in the proof of Corollary 4.4 in
[
23
]
, one obtains that there exist constants
\lambda_{1},\,C>0
, depending only on
d,\mathcal{O}
, such that for any
T>0,\,\lambda>\lambda_{1}(1+T^{-1/2})
and
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|(I-\Pi_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{C}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|e^{it(-\Delta+1)}(I-\Pi_{\lambda})f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt.

(A.4)

Here, the spectral projector
\Pi_{\lambda}=\mathbbm{1}_{\sqrt{-\Delta}\leqslant\lambda}
is defined by

\Pi_{\lambda}f=\mathcal{F}^{-1}\chi_{\{|\xi|\leqslant\lambda\}}\mathcal{F}f.

Step 3: We complete the proof.
Similarly, we introduce the FBI transformation.
For
0<h<1,\,z=\tau+is\in\mathbb{C}
and
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
-valued regular function
\Gamma(t;x)
, we define

\mathcal{T}_{h}\Gamma(z;x)=\frac{2^{1/4}}{(2\pi h)^{3/4}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-\frac{(z+t)^{2}}{2h}}\Gamma(t;x)dt.

Then, similarly to Proposition 5.1 in
[
23
]
, using the FBI transform together with standard computations, we derive the following observability estimate based on (
A.3
):

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ch(1+\frac{1}{T^{2}})\|(-\Delta+1)f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+Ce^{\frac{2T^{2}}{h}+\frac{C}{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\|(-\Delta+1)e^{it(-\Delta+1)}f\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt,

(A.5)

for any
T>0,\,0<h<h_{0}(1+T^{-3})^{-1}
and
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Next, for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we denote
U_{0}=(-\Delta+1)^{-1}u_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
u(t)=e^{it(-\Delta+1)}u_{0}
.
By (
A.5
), we have

\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant Ch(1+\frac{1}{T^{2}})\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+Ce^{\frac{2T^{2}}{h}+\frac{C}{T}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt,

(A.6)

for any
T>0,\,0<h<h_{0}(1+T^{-3})^{-1}
. Then, by (
A.4
) and the triangle inequality, we obtain

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{C}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt+(C+1)\|\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2},

for all
T>0
and
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(1+T^{-1/2})
.
From the definitions of
U_{0}
and
\Pi_{\lambda}
, we obtain

\|\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=\int_{|\xi|\leqslant\lambda}|\hat{u}|^{2}d\xi\leqslant(\lambda^{2}+1)^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leqslant\lambda}|(|\xi|^{2}+1)^{-1}\hat{u}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\leqslant(\lambda^{2}+1)^{2}\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

Thus, we derive that

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant\frac{C}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt+C\lambda^{4}\|U_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

This, together with (
A.6
), implies

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\leqslant C\lambda^{4}h(1+\frac{1}{T^{2}})\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}+\big(C\lambda^{4}e^{\frac{2T^{2}}{h}+\frac{C}{T}}+\frac{C}{T}\big)\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O})}^{2}dt.

By taking
\lambda=2\lambda_{1}(1+T^{-1/2})
and
h=\varepsilon(1+T^{-4})^{-1}
with
\varepsilon
small enough, we complete the proof.
∎

Comparative efficacy and safety of distinct PD-1 antibodies in unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer: a network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials
Authors: Mengting Wang, Jun Li, Shiju Shen, Linshan Chen, Bo Jia
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundUnresectable advanced or recurrent gastric and gastroesophageal junction (GC/GEJ) cancers carry poor prognoses, and several programmed death-1 (PD-1) inhibitors have shown clinical activity. However, no head-to-head trial has compared their relative efficacy and safety. This network meta-analysis aimed to evaluate and rank PD-1–based regimens in this setting.MethodsA systematic review of PubMed, Embase, CENTRAL, Web of Science, and Google Scholar through August 10, 2025, identified randomized controlled trials enrolling adults with unresectable advanced or recurrent GC/GEJ cancer. Primary outcomes were overall survival (OS), progression-free survival (PFS), objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), and grade ≥ 3 TRAEs. A Bayesian random-effects network meta-analysis generated mean differences (MDs) or odds ratios (ORs) with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) and calculated surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) values.ResultsNineteen trials (n = 9,460) were included. Nivolumab monotherapy ranked first for OS (SUCRA 98.3%) and PFS (97.6%), and improved OS versus control (HR 0.59, 95% CI 0.50–0.69). Sintilimab plus chemotherapy ranked highly for OS and PFS (SUCRA 53.9% and 70.9%) and reduced PFS risk versus pembrolizumab monotherapy (HR 0.53, 95% CI 0.35–0.82). Nivolumab plus chemotherapy also improved OS versus control (HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.68–0.92) and PFS versus pembrolizumab monotherapy (HR 0.55, 95% CI 0.42–0.72). Nivolumab monotherapy yielded the highest ORR and DCR (SUCRA 99.9% and 95.2%) but the lowest safety ranking for grade ≥ 3 TRAEs (SUCRA 7.1%). Pembrolizumab monotherapy showed the most favorable safety (SUCRA 100% for grade ≥ 3 TRAEs) but the lowest efficacy across PFS, ORR, and DCR. Across agents, PD-1 inhibitor–chemotherapy combinations reduced progression or death versus control without increasing severe toxicity.ConclusionChemoimmunotherapy should be prioritized as first-line therapy for unresectable advanced or recurrent GC/GEJ cancer, with nivolumab-based combinations offering the most favorable efficacy-safety balance. Nivolumab monotherapy provides the strongest tumor response and survival ranking but requires vigilant toxicity management. These comparative rankings can inform individualized regimen selection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12876-025-04376-y.

Introduction

Advanced gastric cancer and gastroesophageal junction (GEJ) cancer are aggressive malignancies defined by unresectable local disease or distant metastases at diagnosis [1]. The majority of cases present at an advanced stage and carry a grim prognosis. Gastric cancer is also a major global health concern, ranking as the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide [2]. In 2020, approximately 1.1 million new cases and 770,000 deaths were recorded globally, with the highest incidence in Eastern Asia; by 2040, the annual case load is projected to rise to roughly 1.8 million cases and 1.3 million deaths [3, 4]. In advanced-stage disease, the global 5-year survival rate is only on the order of 5–10%. The economic impact is substantial as well—for example, the annual financial burden of gastric/GEJ cancers was estimated at $20.6 billion across major regions in 2017 [5].

Systemic chemotherapy has long been the mainstay of treatment for unresectable or metastatic gastric and GEJ cancers. Combination regimens (such as a platinum plus fluoropyrimidine) can modestly prolong survival, and in HER2-positive tumors the addition of a HER2-targeted antibody (trastuzumab) confers some benefit [6]. However, outcomes with conventional therapy remain poor—most patients eventually experience disease progression, and median overall survival seldom exceeds about 12 months [7, 8]. In recent years, immunotherapy has emerged as a promising treatment approach for this disease. Notably, immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting programmed death-1 (PD-1) can unleash anti-tumor immune responses by blocking an inhibitory T-cell receptor and preventing tumor immune evasion [9]. Several PD-1 inhibitor antibodies have demonstrated durable activity in advanced gastric cancer [10]. The advent of PD-1–directed therapy has begun to reshape the therapeutic landscape, representing a new paradigm in advanced gastric/GEJ cancer management.

Several PD-1 inhibitors—nivolumab, pembrolizumab, sintilimab, and tislelizumab among them—have demonstrated clinically meaningful activity in phase III trials. Aggregate evidence corroborates these findings. A 2024 meta-analysis of eight randomized controlled trials showed that nivolumab, as monotherapy or combined with chemotherapy, extended OS by a mean 2.29 months and progression-free survival (PFS) by 0.69 months versus standard therapy, albeit with a higher incidence of grade ≥ 3 adverse events (AEs) [11]. Parallel syntheses report that pembrolizumab improves survival and objective response, particularly when combined with cytotoxic chemotherapy, while single-agent sintilimab achieves favourable response rates with acceptable toxicity in advanced gastric/GEJ cancer [12]. Yet these meta-analyses evaluate each antibody in isolation and rely exclusively on pairwise comparisons with chemotherapy or placebo. No study to date has rigorously compared the relative efficacy or safety of different PD-1 agents, leaving clinicians without evidence-based guidance on the optimal antibody selection.

In this context, a network meta-analysis (NMA) offers a powerful approach to compare multiple therapies. NMA allows simultaneous comparison of several treatments by integrating both direct and indirect evidence from trials [13]. We therefore conducted an NMA to evaluate the efficacy and safety of various PD-1 inhibitors in patients with unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric/GEJ cancer. The objective was to determine the relative therapeutic value of each PD-1 agent in terms of survival outcomes (overall and progression-free survival) and adverse effects. This analysis is intended to fill the evidence gap and guide clinical decision-making by elucidating whether certain PD-1 therapies confer any significant advantage, thereby informing optimal treatment selection for advanced gastric and GEJ cancers.

Methods

This systematic review and network meta-analysis was performed and reported following the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) 2020 statement and its extension for systematic reviews incorporating network meta-analysis of healthcare interventions (PRISMA-NMA) [14, 15]. Ethical approval and informed consent were not required, as this study involved analysis of previously published data.

Data sources and search strategy

Comprehensive literature searches were conducted across PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL), Web of Science, and Google Scholar databases from inception through August 10, 2025. A combination of medical subject headings (MeSH) and text words related to gastric cancer, PD-1 inhibitors, immunotherapy, and randomized controlled trials (RCTs) were employed, using Boolean operators “AND” and “OR” to maximize retrieval accuracy and comprehensiveness (detailed search strategy provided in Supplementary File 1). Additionally, reference lists of included studies and systematic reviews published within the past five years were screened manually to identify further eligible studies. The initial title/abstract screening and full-text evaluations were independently conducted by two reviewers, and discrepancies were resolved via discussion or arbitration by a third investigator.

Study selection

Studies meeting the following inclusion criteria were eligible: [1] Population: Adult patients (≥ 18 years) with unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer; [2] Intervention: Immunotherapy using PD-1 inhibitors, either alone or combined with other therapeutic regimens (e.g., chemotherapy or targeted agents); [3] Comparators: Standard chemotherapy, placebo, or alternative PD-1 inhibitor-containing regimens in head-to-head comparisons; [4] Outcomes: Studies reporting at least one efficacy or safety outcome; [5] Study design: Randomized controlled trials (RCTs); and [6] Language: Publications in English.

Exclusion criteria included: [1] Studies involving patients diagnosed with concurrent malignancies other than gastric cancer; [2] Both intervention and control groups receiving exclusively non-immunotherapeutic treatments or treatments involving non-PD-1 agents, or identical intervention regimens in both groups; [3] Studies without a clear description of treatment regimens; [4] Studies lacking data on predefined outcomes or insufficient data despite multiple author inquiries; [5] Non-RCT studies (e.g., cohort studies, case-control studies); and [6] Conference abstracts, trial protocols, letters, reviews, or commentaries. Eligibility assessments were independently performed by two authors through meticulous evaluation of titles, abstracts, and full texts, with disagreements resolved by consensus or a third-party reviewer.

Data extraction

Eligible studies were managed using EndNote X9 software to eliminate duplication. Data extraction was independently performed by two investigators using a standardized form (Supplementary File 2), collecting publication details (author, title, publication year), sample size, patient characteristics (age, sex, tumor subtype, HER2 status, MSI status, and PD-L1 testing methodology and thresholds), intervention specifics (PD-1 regimen details), comparator details, and relevant outcome data. For overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS), hazard ratios (HRs) with 95% confidence intervals were extracted directly from the original reports. For other outcomes, missing means and standard deviations (SDs) were calculated from available statistics according to the Cochrane Handbook when required [16]. In cases of incomplete or ambiguous data, corresponding authors were contacted up to four times within a 6-week period. Studies reporting multiple intervention groups receiving identical PD-1-based therapies were pooled accordingly to avoid duplication bias.

Risk of bias assessment

Two reviewers independently assessed the methodological quality and risk of bias of included studies using the revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool (RoB 2) for randomized trials [17]. The domains evaluated included randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of outcomes, and selective reporting of results. Any discrepancies encountered during this process were resolved by consultation with a third investigator.

Data coding

For analytic consistency, the various PD-1-based treatment regimens were coded systematically as follows: nivolumab monotherapy (Nivo), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (Nivo + Chemo), pembrolizumab monotherapy (Pembro), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (Pembro + Chemo), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (Sinti + Chemo), and tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (Tisle + Chemo). Control interventions without PD-1 inhibitors, such as placebo or standard chemotherapy alone, were grouped under the category “CON.”

Outcomes

Primary outcomes included OS, PFS, objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), treatment-related adverse events (TRAEs), and Grade ≥ 3 adverse events (≥ 3 AEs). OS and PFS were treated as time-to-event outcomes and synthesized using HRs with 95% confidence intervals, ensuring appropriate handling of censoring. ORR represented the proportion of patients achieving a partial or complete response, while DCR was defined as the proportion of patients achieving complete response, partial response, or stable disease. TRAEs and ≥ 3 AEs indicated the incidence of treatment-related adverse events and severe adverse events, respectively.

Data analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using Stata software version 17.0 (StataCorp LLC, College Station, Texas, USA) and R software version 4.5.1 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) for survival analyses based on HRs. A network meta-analysis (NMA) was conducted to simultaneously compare the efficacy and safety profiles of various PD-1-based therapeutic regimens in patients with advanced gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer. Network plots were generated to visualize direct and indirect comparisons among treatment arms and assess the network geometry for analytical appropriateness. Given anticipated clinical heterogeneity, a random-effects model was utilized to account for variability both within and between studies.

Time-to-event outcomes (OS, PFS) were analyzed using hazard ratios (HRs), whereas binary outcomes (ORR, DCR, TRAEs, ≥ 3 AEs) were analyzed using odds ratios (ORs). Between-study heterogeneity was quantified by estimating the between-study variance (τ²) under a random-effects NMA. In addition, 95% prediction intervals (PIs) were calculated and reported for all primary outcomes and treatment comparisons to reflect the expected range of effects in a new setting. Statistical heterogeneity was quantified using the I² statistic, categorized as low (≤ 25%), moderate (26%−50%), or high (≥ 75%). Bayesian framework analyses within Stata employing the “network” and “mvmeta” packages were used to implement NMA. To rank treatment effectiveness, the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) values were calculated; higher SUCRA values indicated superior treatment efficacy or safety profiles.

Publication bias was evaluated using adjusted funnel plots and Egger’s regression test, with a p-value < 0.05 indicating potential publication bias [18]. Prediction interval plots were additionally generated to further explore heterogeneity and variability of effect sizes. All statistical tests were two-sided, and a p-value of < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

A total of 1076 records were initially identified through electronic database searches. After removing 361 duplicate records, 715 articles underwent title and abstract screening. Of these, 664 were excluded based on the title and abstract review, and the remaining 51 articles were assessed in full-text screening. Results of the network Meta-analysis

Overall survival (OS)

A total of 18 randomized controlled trials, comprising 9,318 patients with advanced or recurrent gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer, reported overall survival. Based on SUCRA rankings (Fig. 3), nivolumab monotherapy (98.3%), pembrolizumab monotherapy (58.3%), and sintilimab plus chemotherapy (53.9%) were identified as the top three regimens for reducing the risk of death, whereas the control group (CON) ranked lowest (1.4%).Fig. 2Network plot comparing efficacy and safety outcomes: 1. As shown in Table 1.1, several regimens significantly improved OS compared with CON. Specifically, nivolumab monotherapy (HR = 0.59; 95% CI, 0.50–0.69), pembrolizumab monotherapy (HR = 0.76; 95% CI, 0.65–0.89), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.79; 95% CI, 0.68–0.92), and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.80; 95% CI, 0.68–0.95) all demonstrated significant reductions in mortality risk. Furthermore, head-to-head indirect comparisons indicated that nivolumab monotherapy was associated with a lower risk of death compared with pembrolizumab monotherapy (HR = 0.78; 95% CI, 0.62–0.97), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.75; 95% CI, 0.60–0.93), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.74; 95% CI, 0.55–0.99), and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.74; 95% CI, 0.58–0.93).

Progression-Free survival (PFS)

Eighteen studies involving 9,410 patients reported progression-free survival. Figure 2.2 illustrates the direct and indirect comparisons across PD-1–based regimens. According to SUCRA rankings (Fig. 3.2), nivolumab monotherapy (97.6%), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (70.9%), and nivolumab plus chemotherapy (65.4%) ranked highest in prolonging PFS, whereas pembrolizumab monotherapy was least effective (0.1%).

As detailed in Table 1.2, compared with pembrolizumab monotherapy, significant improvements in PFS were observed with nivolumab monotherapy (HR = 0.48; 95% CI, 0.36–0.63), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.53; 95% CI, 0.35–0.82), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.55; 95% CI, 0.42–0.72), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.56; 95% CI, 0.36–0.86), and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.63; 95% CI, 0.47–0.85).

In addition, several regimens demonstrated significant benefit compared with the control group. Nivolumab monotherapy (HR = 0.57; 95% CI, 0.47–0.70), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.64; 95% CI, 0.44–0.94), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.66; 95% CI, 0.54–0.81), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.67; 95% CI, 0.45–0.99), and pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (HR = 0.76; 95% CI, 0.60–0.96) all significantly reduced the risk of progression or death compared with CON.

Objective response rate (ORR)

Eighteen studies totaling 9,478 patients were included in the ORR analysis. Figure 2.3 depicts direct comparisons among the various treatments. Based on SUCRA rankings (Fig. 4), nivolumab monotherapy (99.9%), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (62.3%), and sintilimab plus chemotherapy (49.7%) were the most effective treatments in improving ORR, while pembrolizumab monotherapy ranked the lowest (11.9%). Specifically, nivolumab monotherapy significantly increased ORR compared to nivolumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 11.71; 95% CI, 2.61 to 52.45), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (OR = 13.93; 95% CI, 2.04 to 95.21), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 13.99; 95% CI, 2.06 to 94.94), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 15.18; 95% CI, 3.26 to 70.69), CON (OR = 20.69; 95% CI, 5.52 to 77.58), and pembrolizumab monotherapy (OR = 27.25; 95% CI, 6.33 to 117.30) (Table 1.3).

Disease control rate (DCR)

Thirteen studies including 7,245 patients were analyzed for DCR. Figure 2.4 illustrates direct comparisons and sample distributions. Based on SUCRA values (Fig. 4.2), nivolumab monotherapy (95.2%), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (66.0%), and sintilimab plus chemotherapy (54.6%) were ranked highest for enhancing DCR, while pembrolizumab monotherapy ranked lowest (0.9%). Compared with pembrolizumab monotherapy, nivolumab monotherapy (OR = 4.05; 95% CI, 2.23 to 7.38), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 2.59; 95% CI, 1.38 to 4.84), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 2.23; 95% CI, 1.21 to 4.11), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 2.19; 95% CI, 1.03 to 4.64), and CON (OR = 1.66; 95% CI, 1.13 to 2.43) significantly improved DCR. Additionally, nivolumab monotherapy significantly increased DCR compared with the CON group (OR = 2.45; 95% CI, 1.55 to 3.87) (Table 1.4).

Grade ≥ 3 Treatment-Related adverse events (≥ 3 TRAEs)

Fifteen studies involving 9,342 patients evaluated grade ≥ 3 TRAEs. Figure 2.5 depicts the network of direct comparisons. According to SUCRA rankings (Fig. 4.3), pembrolizumab monotherapy (100.0%), CON (74.0%), and tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (56.3%) demonstrated the lowest incidence of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs, whereas nivolumab monotherapy had the highest incidence (7.1%). Specifically, pembrolizumab monotherapy significantly reduced grade ≥ 3 TRAEs compared to CON (OR = 0.17; 95% CI, 0.12 to 0.26), tislelizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 0.15; 95% CI, 0.07 to 0.34), sintilimab plus chemotherapy (OR = 0.13; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.30), pembrolizumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 0.11; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.18), nivolumab plus chemotherapy (OR = 0.11; 95% CI, 0.06 to 0.18), and nivolumab monotherapy (OR = 0.07; 95% CI, 0.03 to 0.14) (Table 1.5).

Risk of bias and publication bias

Among the 19 included randomized controlled trials, the overall risk of bias was judged to be low in 9 studies and as having some concerns in the remaining 10 studies. With respect to the randomization process, 13 studies were rated as having a low risk of bias, while 6 studies raised some concerns. Regarding deviations from the intended interventions, 17 studies were considered low risk, and 2 studies were classified as having some concerns. For the domain of missing outcome data, 14 studies demonstrated a low risk of bias, whereas 5 studies presented some concerns. In the measurement of outcomes, 18 studies were judged to have a low risk of bias, and only 1 study raised some concerns. For the selection of the reported results, all 19 studies were assessed as having a low risk of bias. A comprehensive summary of the risk of bias assessment across all included studies is provided in Supplementary File 3.

Potential publication bias was evaluated using funnel plots for each outcome (Supplementary File 4). The scatter plots showed varying degrees of symmetry around the vertical axis, suggesting the possibility of some publication bias. Specifically, funnel plots corresponding to Figures S4.1-S4.5 demonstrated mild asymmetry, which could indicate small-study effects or selective reporting. However, results from Egger’s regression tests revealed that all p-values exceeded the conventional threshold of 0.05 for all primary and secondary outcomes. These findings suggest that there was no statistically significant evidence of publication bias across the included studies in this network meta-analysis.

Discussion

This network meta-analysis included 19 RCTs encompassing a total of 9,460 patients with unresectable advanced GC/GEJ cancer, providing a comprehensive comparative evaluation of the efficacy and safety profiles of different PD-1 inhibitor–based regimens. The findings can be summarized as follows. First, nivolumab monotherapy demonstrated the most pronounced survival benefit, ranking first for both overall survival (SUCRA 98.3%) and progression-free survival (SUCRA 97.6%), and was associated with a substantial reduction in the risks of death and disease progression. Sintilimab combined with chemotherapy also ranked among the leading regimens (SUCRA 53.9% for OS and 70.9% for PFS), suggesting survival benefits that may approximate those of nivolumab. Second, nivolumab monotherapy achieved the highest objective response and disease control rates, reflecting strong antitumor activity, but this efficacy was accompanied by the greatest risk of severe adverse events (SUCRA 7.1%). By contrast, pembrolizumab monotherapy ranked lowest across efficacy endpoints, including PFS, ORR, and DCR, but achieved the most favorable safety profile with the lowest risk of grade ≥ 3 toxicities (SUCRA 100%). These contrasting patterns underscore the trade-off between efficacy and safety across different PD-1 strategies. Third, PD-1 inhibitors combined with chemotherapy consistently outperformed monotherapy, achieving a balanced profile of efficacy and safety. Regardless of the specific antibody, chemoimmunotherapy significantly reduced the risk of disease progression or death without increasing the incidence of grade ≥ 3 adverse events, and in some regimens demonstrated a trend toward reduced toxicity. Collectively, these findings provide critical comparative evidence to inform personalized regimen selection, balancing therapeutic benefit against toxicity in this challenging disease setting.

OS and PFS are universally regarded as the most clinically meaningful endpoints in advanced gastric and GEJ cancers, as they reflect both the capacity of treatment to extend life and its ability to delay disease progression. In the present network meta-analysis, nivolumab monotherapy consistently ranked first for both OS and PFS, demonstrating statistically significant improvements over conventional chemotherapy and other PD-1–based regimens. This finding aligns with pivotal trials such as ATTRACTION-2, which showed a durable OS benefit of nivolumab in previously treated Asian patients, and CheckMate-649, which established the superiority of nivolumab combined with chemotherapy as first-line therapy in global cohorts [25]. Importantly, the current analysis provides a broader comparative perspective, highlighting nivolumab’s relative advantage across different PD-1 strategies rather than against chemotherapy alone. The superior performance of nivolumab is likely attributable to several synergistic mechanisms. PD-1 inhibition restores cytotoxic T-cell function and prevents exhaustion, thereby sustaining antitumor immunity. Concurrently, chemotherapy promotes immunogenic cell death, leading to enhanced antigen release, dendritic cell maturation, and remodeling of the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment [29]. Together, these processes amplify immune recognition and clearance of tumor cells. In addition, nivolumab’s pharmacologic properties, including high receptor affinity and favorable receptor occupancy kinetics, may further contribute to its durable clinical efficacy [30].

Sintilimab plus chemotherapy also achieved high rankings for OS and PFS, consistent with the results of the ORIENT-16 trial, which demonstrated significant survival benefits in both the overall population and in PD-L1–enriched subgroups [31]. Mechanistically, sintilimab shares a similar mode of action with nivolumab but is characterized by exceptionally high binding affinity to PD-1 and a slow dissociation rate, resulting in prolonged receptor engagement and sustained T-cell activation [32, 33]. These features may explain its competitive performance when combined with chemotherapy. However, only a single randomized controlled trial of sintilimab plus chemotherapy was available for inclusion, which limits the robustness of indirect comparisons. The observed efficacy ranking could be influenced by trial-specific factors such as patient selection, PD-L1 testing methodology, and regional treatment practices. Additional multicenter phase III trials in diverse populations will be necessary to validate the survival advantage of sintilimab-based chemoimmunotherapy and clarify its positioning relative to other PD-1 inhibitors.

Beyond survival endpoints, ORR and DCR are recognized as complementary measures of clinical efficacy, as they capture not only the depth of tumor regression but also the durability of disease stabilization. In the present analysis, nivolumab monotherapy consistently ranked first for both ORR and DCR, underscoring its strong ability to induce robust and durable antitumor responses in advanced gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancer. These findings are in line with pivotal evidence from ATTRACTION-2 and subsequent meta-analyses, which confirmed that nivolumab monotherapy can elicit durable responses in selected patients, particularly those with favorable biomarker profiles such as high PD-L1 expression or microsatellite instability-high (MSI-H) status [34]. However, heterogeneity in benefit across studies has also been reported, likely attributable to differences in biomarker prevalence, treatment line, and baseline disease characteristics. Importantly, the present analysis also revealed that nivolumab monotherapy was associated with the highest incidence of grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events, with a SUCRA ranking of only 7.1%. This dual pattern of marked tumor response but inferior safety is consistent with previous clinical reports documenting the toxicity burden of immune checkpoint blockade [35]. Immune-related adverse events arise from excessive immune activation and can involve multiple organ systems such as pneumonitis, colitis, hepatitis, and endocrinopathies, frequently requiring treatment interruption or immunosuppressive therapy. Mechanistically, nivolumab potently restores exhausted T-cell function and sustains effector activity, which enhances antitumor cytotoxicity but simultaneously increases the risk of immune-mediated tissue injury. Its pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic properties, including sustained PD-1 receptor occupancy and high binding affinity, may further amplify immune activation and toxicity [36]. This “high efficacy-high toxicity” profile highlights the need for careful patient selection and proactive toxicity management in clinical practice. Patients with immunologically “hot” tumors, such as those with high PD-L1 expression or MSI-H status, are more likely to achieve benefit but may also be predisposed to severe immune-related complications [37]. Therefore, close monitoring, early recognition of immune-related events, and timely management strategies are essential to optimize the therapeutic index of nivolumab. Collectively, these findings indicate that while nivolumab monotherapy delivers the strongest tumor response among PD-1–based regimens, its clinical value must be balanced against its elevated safety risks to ensure durable and tolerable benefit.

Pembro monotherapy demonstrated the opposite pattern to Nivo-based regimens, showing consistently lower efficacy but superior safety. In this analysis, it ranked last for PFS and lowest for ORR and DCR, yet achieved the most favorable ranking for grade ≥ 3 TRAEs, indicating limited antitumor activity but minimal severe toxicity. These results are consistent with previous GC/GEJ trials, where single-agent Pembro provided modest survival benefit in unselected or low PD-L1 populations, with better outcomes in biomarker-enriched subgroups, and maintained a favorable tolerability profile [12]. Variations across studies likely reflect differences in PD-L1 expression thresholds, treatment line, and absence of cytotoxic backbone. Mechanistically, the lack of chemotherapy removes a key source of immunogenic cell death and antigen release, limiting immune priming and synergy with PD-1 blockade. In tumors with low mutational burden or limited T-cell infiltration, single-agent PD-1 inhibition may be insufficient to drive robust responses, explaining its lower ORR and DCR [38]. Conversely, the absence of chemotherapy-related toxicities and the manageable profile of immune-related adverse events contribute to its low incidence of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs, supporting its role as a better-tolerated but less potent option in largely unselected GC/GEJ populations.

In the present analysis, PD-1 inhibitor regimens combined with chemotherapy consistently outperformed monotherapy, achieving a more favorable balance between efficacy and safety. Across agents including Nivo, Pembro, and Sinti, combination therapy significantly reduced the risk of disease progression and achieved higher survival rankings, while the incidence of grade ≥ 3 TRAEs was comparable to, or in some cases lower than, that observed with control regimens. This pattern suggests that chemoimmunotherapy may maximize therapeutic benefit without substantially increasing treatment-related toxicity. These findings align with results from pivotal trials such as CheckMate-649, KEYNOTE-590, and ORIENT-16, which demonstrated that the addition of a PD-1 inhibitor to first-line chemotherapy improved OS and PFS compared with chemotherapy alone in advanced GC/GEJ cancer, with a safety profile that remained manageable [25, 31, 39]. However, the present network meta-analysis extends prior evidence by providing a comparative framework across multiple PD-1 agents, indicating that the efficacy–safety balance of chemoimmunotherapy is a consistent feature rather than being restricted to a single drug. In contrast, earlier studies often evaluated each PD-1 inhibitor in isolation, making cross-agent comparisons difficult. The superior performance of PD-1 plus chemotherapy combinations is biologically plausible. Cytotoxic chemotherapy can induce immunogenic cell death, increasing tumor antigen release and enhancing dendritic cell maturation, which in turn promotes T-cell priming and infiltration into the tumor microenvironment. Concurrent PD-1 blockade prevents T-cell exhaustion and sustains effector function, enabling a synergistic antitumor immune response [29]. Additionally, chemotherapy can modulate the immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment by reducing regulatory T cells and myeloid-derived suppressor cells, thereby further augmenting immune checkpoint inhibitor activity [40, 41]. This combination of direct cytotoxic effects and immune reprogramming likely explains the observed gains in OS and PFS without a parallel increase in severe toxicity. Collectively, these findings reinforce chemoimmunotherapy as a preferred treatment paradigm in advanced GC/GEJ cancer, offering both enhanced disease control and an acceptable safety profile.

This study has several notable strengths. First, it represents, to our knowledge, the most comprehensive network meta-analysis to date evaluating multiple PD-1–based regimens for unresectable advanced or recurrent GC/GEJ cancer, integrating direct and indirect evidence from 19 randomized controlled trials and encompassing 9,460 patients. This design allowed a head-to-head comparative framework across different PD-1 inhibitors, which is not feasible in individual trials, and enabled a ranking of regimens for both efficacy and safety outcomes. Second, the analysis adhered to PRISMA and PRISMA-NMA guidelines, applied rigorous eligibility criteria, and employed robust Bayesian network modeling with SUCRA-based ranking, thereby enhancing the methodological transparency and reproducibility of the findings. Nonetheless, several limitations should be acknowledged when interpreting the results. First, substantial heterogeneity in trial designs, enrolled patient populations, and baseline characteristics—such as PD-L1 expression levels, HER2 status, MSI frequency, prior treatment exposure, and geographic distribution—may have influenced treatment effects and reduced the generalizability of pooled estimates. Although a random-effects model with τ2 and prediction intervals was applied to account for between-study variability, residual confounding and limited external applicability cannot be excluded. Second, the number of trials available for certain regimens, particularly Sinti plus chemotherapy and Tisle plus chemotherapy, was limited, reducing the precision and certainty of their ranking estimates. The high performance of these regimens in some outcomes should therefore be interpreted cautiously until validated by additional large-scale trials. Third, outcome definitions and assessment methods varied across studies, especially for PFS, ORR, and DCR, and most trials did not stratify results by key predictive biomarkers such as PD-L1 combined positive score (CPS) or MSI status. This heterogeneity significantly diminishes the applicable value of the results in routine practice and highlights the need for cautious interpretation. Future studies with harmonized outcome reporting, biomarker-driven stratification, and direct head-to-head comparisons will be essential to confirm and refine these findings.

Conclusion

In this network meta-analysis of 19 randomized trials encompassing 9,460 patients with unresectable advanced or recurrent gastric or gastroesophageal junction cancer, PD-1 inhibitor regimens showed distinct efficacy and safety profiles. Nivolumab monotherapy ranked first for overall and progression-free survival and achieved the highest objective response and disease control, but it was associated with the greatest risk of grade ≥ 3 treatment-related adverse events. Sintilimab combined with chemotherapy ranked among the leading regimens for survival, although the evidence base remains limited. Pembrolizumab monotherapy exhibited the most favorable safety with comparatively modest efficacy. Across agents, chemoimmunotherapy consistently reduced the risk of progression or death without a commensurate increase in severe toxicity, supporting its use as a preferred therapeutic approach in appropriate candidates. These findings provide comparative evidence to guide regimen selection and underscore the importance of integrating anticipated benefit, toxicity risk, and biomarker context to individualize treatment in advanced gastric and gastroesophageal junction cancers.

Nonlinear Fenchel Conjugates
Anton Schiela, Roland Herzog, Ronny Bergmann

Abstract
. The classical concept of Fenchel conjugation is tailored to extended real-valued functions defined on linear spaces. In this paper we generalize this concept to functions defined on arbitrary sets that do not necessarily bear any structure at all. This generalization is obtained by replacing linear test functions by general nonlinear ones. Thus, we refer to it as nonlinear Fenchel conjugation. We investigate elementary properties including the Fenchel-Moreau biconjugation theorem. Whenever the domain exhibits additional structure, the restriction to a suitable subset of test functions allows further results to be derived. For example, on smooth manifolds, the restriction to smooth test functions allows us to state the Fenchel-Young theorem for the viscosity Fréchet subdifferential. On Lie groups, the restriction to real-valued group homomorphisms relates nonlinear Fenchel conjugation to infimal convolution and yields a notion of convexity.

1.
Introduction

Fenchel duality is a classical concept of (convex) analysis with numerous important applications.
On an arbitrary real linear space
V
, the Fenchel conjugate
f^{*}
of an extended real-valued function
f\colon V\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\}
is defined in purely algebraic terms according to

(1.1)

V^{\prime}\ni x^{*}\mapsto f^{*}(x^{*})\coloneqq\sup_{x\in V}\bigl\{x^{*}(x)-f(x)\bigr\}\in\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\},

where
V^{\prime}
denotes the algebraic dual of
V
.
In practice, this concept is often particularized to locally convex or normed linear spaces
V
and their topological dual
V^{*}
.
Results of Fenchel duality comprise, for instance, the Fenchel-Young theorem and the Fenchel-Moreau biconjugation theorem; see, e. g.,
Bauschke & Combettes, 2011
, Proposition 13.13 and Theorem 13.32
.

In recent years, Fenchel duality has also become the foundation of many algorithms for convex and non-smooth optimization.
As examples, we mention the family of primal-dual algorithms; see, e. g.,
Esser et al., 2010
;
Valkonen, 2023
.
It is an ongoing effort to adapt some of these methods from linear to nonlinear operators as in
Valkonen, 2014
;
Mom et al., 2022
, as well as to nonlinear spaces.
In the latter context, the generalization of the notion of Fenchel conjugate, e. g., to functions defined on smooth manifolds, is of paramount importance.
Several approaches on Riemannian respectively Hadamard manifolds have been developed in
Bergmann et al., 2021
;
Silva et al., 2022
;
Carvalho et al., 2023
.

We also mention a related line of research by
Mart\’nez-Legaz, 2005
to generalizing convexity and conjugation, where two potentially nonlinear sets are set into duality by a coupling function.
We discuss the relation to our work in more detail in
Remark

4.11
.

Contributions

In this paper we generalize the classical notion of Fenchel conjugation (
1.1
) to extended real-valued functions
f
defined on arbitrary sets
\mathcal{M}
rather than linear spaces
V
.
A high-level view to (
1.1
) reveals that each element
x^{*}\in V^{\prime}
can be viewed as the generator of a probing function
x\mapsto x^{*}(x)-f(x)
.
The value of
f^{*}(x^{*})
is then understood as the scalar measurement obtained by maximizing that probing function over
x
.

The simple but far-reaching idea is to replace the linear test function
x^{*}
in the probing function
x^{*}-f
by a general nonlinear function
\varphi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\}
.
It turns out that many elementary, mostly algebraic properties of this generalized Fenchel conjugate prevail and do not require any structure on the set
\mathcal{M}
at all.
However, in case
\mathcal{M}
does exhibit more structure, refined results can be obtained by restricting the space of test functions to an appropriate space
\mathcal{F}
of more regular functions.
For instance, when
\mathcal{M}
is a metric space, continuous functions
\mathcal{F}=C(\mathcal{M})
can be chosen.
When
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth manifold, we may consider
\mathcal{F}=C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
.
And when
\mathcal{M}
is a linear space, the classical Fenchel conjugate is recovered when we utilize the algebraic dual space
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
.

Organization

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows.
Following some preliminary notations in
Section

2
, we introduce a nonlinear Fenchel conjugate in
Section

3
and examine its properties when defined on a general set
\mathcal{M}
.

Section

4
generalizes the concept of
\Gamma
-regularization (see, e. g.,
Ekeland & Temam, 1999
, Chapter I.3
) by which a function
f
is approximated by a pointwise supremum of minorants, as well as the notion of the biconjugate.

Section

5
explores additional properties where
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth manifold, enabling a generalization of the well-known Fenchel-Young theorem.
We recover previous definitions of Fenchel conjugates on manifolds as special cases.
In
Section

6
, we discuss the case when
\mathcal{M}
is a group and obtain a generalized characterization of infimal convolution.
We draw some conclusions in
Section

7
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let us denote extensions of the real numbers by
\mathbb{R}_{\infty}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}\coloneqq\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty,-\infty\}
.
Both sets have a natural total ordering, so
=
,
\geqslant
,
\leqslant
,
>
,
<
as well as
\sup
and
\inf
over arbitrary subsets (with the usual convention for empty sets) are always well-defined in
\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
in the straightforward way.
For instance,
-\infty\leqslant a\leqslant+\infty
holds for all
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
.
Addition and multiplication in these sets are defined in a natural way.
For instance,
+\infty+a=+\infty
for
a\in\mathbb{R}_{\infty}
,
\pm\infty\cdot a=\pm\infty
for
a>0
,
\pm\infty\cdot a=\mp\infty
for
a<0
.
Subtraction is defined via
a-b\coloneqq a+(-1)\cdot b
.
The expressions
\pm\infty+(\mp\infty)
and
0\cdot\pm\infty
, however, are not defined.
Therefore, arithmetic computations in
\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
have to be performed with some care.
In particular, the addition of
c\in\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
to both sides of an equality or inequality is an equivalence transformation only when
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
For
c=\pm\infty
, the expression
a+c=b+c
is either undefined or always true, even when
a\neq b
.

Checking all cases, it can be verified that the three inequalities
a\leqslant b+c
,
a-b\leqslant c
, and
a-c\leqslant b
are equivalent whenever all expressions are defined.
In case one of the sums is undefined, each of the other two inequality either also contains an undefined term, or it is of the form
\pm\infty\leqslant\pm\infty
and thus true with equality.

Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{M}
is a non-empty set and

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\infty}\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}\}

are sets of (extended) real-valued functions.
Arithmetic operations on
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
are defined pointwise.
Given
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
\alpha f
is defined for any
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
.
Moreover,
f+g
is defined for any
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, as well as for any
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, but not in general for
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
However, the pointwise
\max\{f,g\}
and
\min\{f,g\}
are always defined.

To take the difficulty of addition into account, we introduce a domain of definition for sums (and similarly differences) of functions

(2.1)

\mathcal{D}(f+g)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{M}\,|\,f(x)+g(x)\text{ is defined}\}.

In particular,
\mathcal{D}(f+g)=\mathcal{M}
holds whenever
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
or
g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, or when
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
We observe that
\mathcal{D}(f-f)=\{x\in\mathcal{M}\,|\,f(x)\in\mathbb{R}\}
is the domain of
f
.
We also require the restricted domain

(2.2)

\underline{\mathcal{D}}(f+g)\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{M}\,|\,f(x)\neq-\infty\text{ and }g(x)\neq-\infty\}\subseteq\mathcal{D}(f+g).

This definition excludes points where
f(x)+g(x)=-\infty
from the domain
\mathcal{D}(f+g)
.
For
f\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, we introduce

(2.3)

\lVert f\rVert_{\infty}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\lvert f(x)\rvert\in[0,+\infty].

It is evident that
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is a convex cone and
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
is a linear space.
We introduce the natural partial ordering on
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
by

\displaystyle f\leqslant g\quad

\displaystyle\mathrel{\vcentcolon\Leftrightarrow}\quad f(x)\leqslant g(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}

(2.4)

\displaystyle\makebox[12.7778pt][r]{\vphantom{{}\mathrel{\vcentcolon\Leftrightarrow}{}}{}\Leftrightarrow{}}\quad f(x)-g(x)\leqslant 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{D}(f-g).

Throughout the paper, we reserve the term
function
to denote mappings with values in the (extended) real numbers.
We denote by
\iota_{\mathcal{U}}
the indicator function of a set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, which is zero on
\mathcal{U}
and is
+\infty
otherwise.
Moreover,
\epi f\coloneqq\{(x,c)\in\mathcal{M}\times\mathbb{R}\,|\,c\geqslant f(x)\}
represents the epigraph of
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
The restriction of any map
A\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
between any two sets
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{N}
, to a subset
\mathcal{M}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
, is denoted by
A\rvert_{\mathcal{M}_{0}}
.

3.
Nonlinear Fenchel Conjugate on General Sets

With the preliminaries from
Section

2
in place, we define a generalized Fenchel conjugate for extended real-valued functions
f
defined on an arbitrary, nonempty set
\mathcal{M}
as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

The nonlinear Fenchel conjugate of
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is defined as

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}

(3.1)

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\coloneqq\sup\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)\}.

As usual, the supremum in (
3.1
) is taken to be
-\infty
should
\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
happen to be the empty set.

Before we proceed, we provide three alternative formulations for (
3.1
).
The short proof of their equivalence is given in
Appendix

A
.
One alternative way to express (
3.1
) is as follows:

(3.2)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\,|\,x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)\}

\displaystyle=\sup\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{M},\;\varphi(x)\neq-\infty\text{ and }f(x)\neq+\infty\}.

Second, in more geometric terms, we also have

(3.3)

f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\inf\{c\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi(x)\leqslant f(x)+c\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}\}.

Third, in case we wish to dispense with a domain of definition
\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
depending on
\varphi
and
f
, we may write equivalently

(3.4)

f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-c\,|\,(x,c)\in\epi f\}.

Example 3.2
.

The conjugate of the indicator function of a set
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
is given by

\iota_{\mathcal{U}}^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-\iota_{\mathcal{U}}(x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-\iota_{\mathcal{U}})\}=\sup\{\varphi(x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{U}\}.

Compared to the classical Fenchel conjugate
f^{*}
that acts on
linear
functions
\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{M}
is a linear space, the domain
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
of
f^{\circledast}
is much larger, containing
arbitrary
functions
\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
.
The evaluation of the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate in closed form is thus, in general, illusory.
On the other hand, our approach allows for the definition of a Fenchel conjugate also for functions defined on sets
\mathcal{M}
that are more general than linear spaces.
As we will see below, most of the well-known properties of classical Fenchel conjugates carry over to the extended domain of nonlinear functions and, moreover, additional simple and useful properties, including some symmetry, become visible.

We re-iterate that in case
\mathcal{M}
carries additional algebraic or topological structure and thus admits the definition of more regular functions, we may consider the restriction of the domain of
f^{\circledast}
to an appropriate subset.
Important examples include
\mathcal{M}
being a topological space, a smooth manifold, a group or Lie group, or a linear space.
Notably, when
\mathcal{M}
is a linear space with algebraic dual
\mathcal{M}^{\prime}
, then
f^{*}\coloneqq f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{M}^{\prime}}
is the classical Fenchel conjugate.

3.1.
Algebraic Properties

In this section we state and prove some elementary properties of the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate.

Proposition 3.3
.

The following statements hold for any
f,\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

(i)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=-\infty
.

(b)

(\varphi-f)(x)=-\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
.

(c)

\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)=\emptyset

(d)

\max\{-\varphi,f\}\equiv+\infty
on
\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
.

(e)

\max\{-\varphi,f\}\equiv+\infty
on
\mathcal{M}
.

(ii)

The following are equivalent:

(a)

f^{\circledast}(f)=0
.

(b)

\mathcal{D}(f-f)\neq\emptyset
.

(c)

f(x)\in\mathbb{R}
for some
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

When these fail to hold, then
f^{\circledast}(f)=-\infty
.

(iii)

Moreover, the following symmetry and monotonicity relations hold:

(3.5a)

\displaystyle(-f)^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=(-\varphi)^{\circledast}(f),

(3.5b)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(-\varphi)

\displaystyle=\varphi^{\circledast}(-f),

(3.5c)

\displaystyle\varphi\leqslant f\quad

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\quad f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\leqslant f^{\circledast}(f)\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\leqslant 0.

Proof.

All results are direct consequences of the definition.
∎

The Fenchel-Young inequality
x^{*}(x)\leqslant f(x)+f^{*}(x^{*})
, which is valid for
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(V)
on some linear space
V
and
x^{*}\in V^{\prime}
, is at the heart of classical Fenchel duality theory.
It carries over to the nonlinear case, as long as some care is taken that both sides of the inequality are well-defined.

Theorem 3.4

(Fenchel-Young inequality)
.

Suppose that
f,\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

(i)

The Fenchel-Young inequalities

(3.6a)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\geqslant\varphi(x)-f(x)

(3.6b)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle\geqslant\varphi(x)-f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

(3.6c)

\displaystyle\varphi(x)

\displaystyle\leqslant f(x)+f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

hold, provided that the respective right-hand side is defined in
\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
.

(ii)

The following two assertions are equivalent:

(3.7a)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)-f(x)

(3.7b)

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle\mapsto\varphi(y)-f(y)\text{ attains its supremum }f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\text{ over }\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)\text{ at }x.

(iii)

The following assertions are equivalent:

(3.8a)

\displaystyle f(x)

\displaystyle=\varphi(x)-f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

(3.8b)

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto\psi(x)-f^{\circledast}(\psi)\text{ attains its supremum }f(x)\text{ over }\mathcal{D}(\psi-f^{\circledast})\text{ at }\varphi.

In (
3.7
), the function
\varphi-f
is only defined on
\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
.
At points where
\varphi-f
is undefined, (
3.7a
) cannot hold, so these points are excluded from the maximization in (
3.7b
).
Similarly, in (
3.8
),
\cdot\,(x)-f^{\circledast}
is only defined on
\mathcal{D}(\psi-f^{\circledast})
.

Proof.

The assertions can be verified by
Definition

3.1
of the Fenchel conjugate.
For instance, (
3.6a
) follows directly from the definition of
f^{\circledast}
as a supremum, with the right-hand side defined for
x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
.
Then (
3.6b
) and (
3.6c
) (where defined) follow from (
3.6a
) by simple arithmetic manipulations.
For the remaining two cases, i. e.,
\varphi(x)=f(x)=\pm\infty
, (
3.6b
) and (
3.6c
) hold as equalities of the form
\pm\infty=\pm\infty
, provided that the corresponding right-hand sides are defined.
∎

Next we investigate how the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate behaves under transformations:

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

(i)

For
\alpha>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,

(3.9)

\alpha f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+\beta=(\alpha f)^{\circledast}(\alpha\,\varphi+\beta)=(\alpha f-\beta)^{\circledast}(\alpha\,\varphi).

(ii)

If
\mathcal{D}(f-\psi)=\mathcal{D}(\varphi+\psi)=\mathcal{M}
, then

(3.10)

(f-\psi)^{\circledast}(\varphi)=f^{\circledast}(\varphi+\psi).

(iii)

If
\mathcal{D}(f+g)=\mathcal{D}(\varphi+\psi)=\mathcal{M}
and
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+g^{\circledast}(\psi)
is defined, then

(3.11)

(f+g)^{\circledast}(\varphi+\psi)\leqslant f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+g^{\circledast}(\psi).

Proof.

Equation

3.9
follows from

\displaystyle\alpha\,(\sup[\varphi(x)-f(x)])+\beta

\displaystyle=\sup[(\alpha\,\varphi(x)+\beta)-\alpha f(x)]

\displaystyle=\sup[\alpha\,\varphi(x)-(\alpha f(x)-\beta)],

where in each case, the
\sup
extends over
x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
, which is precisely the set where each expression is defined.
Similarly, (
3.10
) follows from

\sup[(\varphi+\psi)(x)-f(x)]=\sup[\varphi(x)-(f-\psi)(x)]

with the
\sup
extending over
\underline{\mathcal{D}}((\varphi+\psi)-f)=\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-(f-\psi))
; cf. (
2.2
).
The equality of these restricted domains follows from the assumption
\mathcal{D}(f-\psi)=\mathcal{D}(\varphi+\psi)=\mathcal{M}
.
Indeed, one verifies that
\varphi(x)=+\infty
or
f(x)-\psi(x)=-\infty
holds if and only if
\varphi(x)+\psi(x)=+\infty
or
f(x)=-\infty
.
Finally, (
3.11
) is obtained from

\sup[(\varphi+\psi)(x)-(f+g)(x)]\leqslant(\sup[\varphi(x)-f(x)])+(\sup[\psi(x)-g(x)]),

with the
\sup
extending over
\underline{\mathcal{D}}((\varphi+\psi)-(f+g))
and its supersets
\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)
and
\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\psi-g)
, respectively.
∎

We conclude this section with results on monotonicity, Lipschitz continuity and convexity of the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate:

Proposition 3.6
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

(i)

\varphi\geqslant\psi
and
f\leqslant g
implies
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\geqslant g^{\circledast}(\psi)
.
In particular,
f^{\circledast}
is monotone increasing.

(ii)

f^{\circledast}
satisfies the following Lipschitz estimate for
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
:

\lvert f^{\circledast}(\varphi)-f^{\circledast}(\psi)\rvert\leqslant\lVert\varphi-\psi\rVert_{\infty}.

(iii)

f^{\circledast}
is convex on
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

To prove
item

(
i
)

, we first observe that our assumptions imply
\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\psi-g)\subseteq\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)
and also

\psi(x)-g(x)\leqslant\varphi(x)-f(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\psi-g).

Hence we find

g^{\circledast}(\psi)=\sup_{x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\psi-g)}\psi(x)-g(x)\leqslant\sup_{x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)}\varphi(x)-f(x)=f^{\circledast}(\varphi).

For
item

(

⁢
i
i

)

, our assumptions imply
\mathcal{D}(f-\varphi)=\mathcal{D}(f-\psi)=\mathcal{M}
and thus

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\varphi(x)-f(x)

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}[\psi(x)-f(x)]+[\varphi(x)-\psi(x)]

\displaystyle\leqslant f^{\circledast}(\psi)+\lVert\varphi-\psi\rVert_{\infty}.

In a similar way, we conclude
f^{\circledast}(\psi)\leqslant f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+\lVert\varphi-\psi\rVert_{\infty}
.

To prove
item

(

⁢
i
i
i

)

, fix a value
\lambda\in(0,1)
.
We seek to verify the prerequisites for (
3.11
).
We first observe that
\mathcal{D}(f)=\mathcal{D}(\lambda f+(1-\lambda)f)=\mathcal{D}(\varphi+\psi)=\mathcal{M}
holds, since
\varphi,\psi\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
Moreover,
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+f^{\circledast}(\psi)
fails to be defined only if
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=-\infty
and
f^{\circledast}(\psi)=+\infty
or vice versa.
Considering w.l.o.g. the former case and taking into account
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
Proposition

3.3

(
i
)

implies
f\equiv+\infty
.
Consequently,
f^{\circledast}(\psi)
is also
-\infty
, and we conclude that
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+f^{\circledast}(\psi)=-\infty
is defined.
Indeed, by the same arguments,
(\lambda f)^{\circledast}(\lambda\varphi)+((1-\lambda)f)^{\circledast}((1-\lambda)\psi)
is defined.
We now conclude

\displaystyle{2}f^{\circledast}(\lambda\varphi+(1-\lambda)\psi)

\displaystyle=(\lambda f+(1-\lambda)f)^{\circledast}(\lambda\varphi+(1-\lambda)\psi)

\displaystyle\leqslant(\lambda f)^{\circledast}(\lambda\varphi)+((1-\lambda)f)^{\circledast}((1-\lambda)\psi)

by (
3.11
)

\displaystyle=\lambda f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+(1-\lambda)f^{\circledast}(\psi)

\displaystyle\quad\text{by \eqref{eq:FDconstant}}.\qed

3.2.

3.3.
Continuity Properties

A couple of things can be said about continuity properties of

In the following we consider the pointwise supremum of functions over a (possibly empty) index set

(\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\varphi_{\lambda})(x)\coloneqq\sup\{\varphi_{\lambda}(x)\,|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
, and analogously pointwise infima, limits of sequences and so on.

Proposition 3.9
.

(i)

Suppose that
\Lambda

Then

\displaystyle\varphi=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\varphi_{\lambda}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{\lambda\in\Lambda}f^{\circledast}(\varphi_{\lambda})

\displaystyle\varphi=\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\varphi_{\lambda}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\leqslant\inf_{\lambda\in\Lambda}f^{\circledast}(\varphi_{\lambda}).

Equality holds if, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\lambda(\varepsilon)
such that
\varphi_{\lambda(\varepsilon)}\leqslant\varphi+\varepsilon
.

(ii)

For any sequence

in
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\varphi(x)=\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varphi_{k}(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}\quad\Rightarrow\quad f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\leqslant\liminf_{k\to\infty}f^{\circledast}(\varphi_{k}).

(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

with
\liminf_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{k}=0
.

(iii)

4.
\mathcal{F}
-Regularization and
\mathcal{F}
-Biconjugates

A central topic in classical Fenchel duality is the approximation of a function
f
by its maximal convex and lower semi-continuous minorant.
This extremal minorant, here denoted by
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}
, can be constructed as the pointwise supremum of all minorants of
f
from the particular class
\mathcal{F}
of continuous affine functions.
This process has been introduced under the name
\Gamma
-regularization in
Ekeland & Temam, 1999
, Chapter I.3
for functions defined on locally convex linear topological spaces.
The biconjugation theorem states that the
\Gamma
-regularization of
f
coincides with the biconjugate of
f
.

In this section we generalize the ideas and results of
\Gamma
-regularization and biconjugation from the classical setting of functions on locally convex linear topological spaces to functions on general sets, and from the classical Fenchel to the new nonlinear Fenchel conjugate.
Our key idea is to consider sets
\mathcal{F}
of functions more general than affine, from which the minorants of
f
can be taken.
This leads us to the notions of
\mathcal{F}
-regularization (
Section

4.1
) and
\mathcal{F}
-biconjugates (
Section

4.3
).

4.1.
\mathcal{F}
-Regularization

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is a non-empty set of functions and denote by

(4.1)

\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\{\varphi+c\,|\,\varphi\in\mathcal{F},\;c\in\mathbb{R}\}

the set of all
\varphi
that result from a shift of elements of
\mathcal{F}
by an arbitrary constant.

We define the
\mathcal{F}
-regularization of
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
as

(4.2)

\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}(x)\coloneqq\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)\,\big|\,\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\;\varphi\leqslant f\bigr\}.

That is,
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}
is the pointwise supremum of all minorants of
f
taken from the set
\mathcal{F}
and its constant shifts.
We recall that suprema over sets of functions are understood in the pointwise sense.
We may therefore write (
4.2
) more concisely as
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq\sup\bigl\{\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}\,\big|\,\varphi\leqslant f\bigr\}
.

Notice that
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\equiv-\infty
holds for
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
in case no function
\varphi\leqslant f
exists in
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.
In the important case that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
we have either
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\equiv-\infty
or
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Suppose that
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and that
\mathcal{F},\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
are any sets of functions.
Then

(i)

f\leqslant g
and
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}
implies
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\leqslant\lfloor g\rfloor_{\mathcal{G}}
.

(ii)

For
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
and

we have
\lfloor f+\varphi+c\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}=\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}+\varphi+c
.

(iii)

\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}\leqslant f

.

(v)

Let us now consider the set of all functions that can be produced by computing

is a convex cone as well, and for

implies that
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cup\{-\infty\}
.
Hence
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})\setminus\{-\infty\}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
If

for

\lfloor f_{i}\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}=f_{i}\not\equiv-\infty

Hence (

by (

Thus,
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})\setminus\{-\infty\}
is a convex cone.
∎

Next we prove that the
\sup

Proposition 4.7
.

Suppose

Then the following hold:

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\subseteq\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})

(4.10b)

By

(

,

It follows from general properties of closure operators that the
\sup

\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})=\bigcap\bigl\{\mathcal{G}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})\,\big|\,\mathcal{G}=\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{G})\text{ and }\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{G}\bigr\}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})

g^{\circledast}(\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}})=\sup\bigl\{g^{\circledast}(\varphi)\,\big|\,\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\;\varphi\leqslant f\bigr\}

\displaystyle g^{\circledast}(\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}})

Proposition 4.9

Suppose that

Example 4.10
.

Important examples for the choice of
\mathcal{F}
are the following:

(i)

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a locally convex linear topological space and
\mathcal{F}=\mathcal{M}^{*}
is its topological dual space.
Then
\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
is the space of all continuous affine functions and
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{M}^{*}}
is the pointwise supremum over all affine majorants of
f
.
It is known that
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})
is the set of lower semi-continuous convex functions on
\mathcal{M}
; see, e. g.,
Ekeland & Temam, 1999
, Proposition I.3.2
.

(ii)

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a metric space.
Then by a theorem of Baire, lower semi-continous functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
can be written as the pointwise supremum of continuous functions; see, e. g.,
Schechter, 1997
, Theorem 16.16
for the more general setting of a completely regular topological space.
Thus, for
\mathcal{F}=C(\mathcal{M})
,
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{F})
consists of the cone of lower semi-continuous functions in
\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
(which is closed under taking pointwise suprema) and
\{-\infty\}
.

(iii)

In the following section we will see that a similar relation holds (under mild assumptions), in case
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1}
-Banach manifold and
\mathcal{F}=C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
.

Remark 4.11
.

In his work on generalized convexity,
Mart\’nez-Legaz, 2005
considers an alternative generalized notion of the Fenchel conjugate.
He considers two non-empty sets
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}
and a coupling function
c\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{N}\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}
.
His definition of
c
-conjugate is then

f^{c}(y)\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}c(x,y)-f(x)\quad\text{for }y\in\mathcal{N}.

This can be related to our approach of nonlinear Fenchel conjugates (
3.1
) as follows:

(i)

Given
c
and
\mathcal{N}
, we can define
\varphi_{y}\coloneqq c(\cdot,y)
to obtain
f^{\circledast}(\varphi_{y})=f^{c}(y)
for all
y\in\mathcal{N}
.
Choosing
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq\{\varphi_{y}\,|\,y\in\mathcal{N}\}
yields
f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}}\equiv f^{c}
.

(ii)

Vice versa, given
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, we may choose
\mathcal{N}\coloneqq\mathcal{F}
and
c(x,\varphi)\coloneqq\varphi(x)
.
Then again, we have
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=f^{c}(\varphi)
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}
.

That is,
Mart\’nez-Legaz, 2005
generalizes the duality pairing while we generalize the set of test functions.

4.3.
\mathcal{F}
-Biconjugates

In this section we define an appropriate notion of biconjugation that generalizes the corresponding classical concept from the linear case.
In the linear setting, the biconjugate
f^{**}\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(V)
conincides with the largest convex lsc. minorant of
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(V)
.
As we will see, this result carries over to the nonlinear case.

To define the biconjugate, consider the restriction of
f^{\circledast}\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
to
\mathcal{F}
, i. e.,

f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{F}\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}

and take the conjugate of this mapping:

(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}})^{\circledast}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}.

It is clear that all results up to now can be applied to
(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}})^{\circledast}
with
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{F})
in place of
\mathcal{M}
.
However,
\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{F})
can be a very large domain of definition.
To be able to compare
(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}})^{\circledast}
with
f
, we have to restrict the domain again.
To this end, we consider evaluation (Dirac) functions

\displaystyle\delta_{x}\colon\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}

\displaystyle\varphi

\displaystyle\mapsto\delta_{x}(\varphi)\coloneqq\varphi(x).

Clearly, the restriction of
\delta_{x}
to a linear subspace
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
is a linear function on
\mathcal{F}
and continuous with respect to pointwise convergence.
We have the nonlinear canonical embedding of
\mathcal{M}
into the algebraic dual space of
\mathcal{F}
via

(4.13)

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}\colon\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto\delta_{x}.

Definition 4.13
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.
Then we define the
\mathcal{F}
-biconjugate
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}
of
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
as
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}\coloneqq(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}})^{\circledast}\circ J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{F}^{\prime}}
, i. e.,

(4.14)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}\colon\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{R}_{\pm\infty}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\mapsto(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}})^{\circledast}(\delta_{x}).

When written in the form (
3.4
), this definition reads

(4.15)

f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}(x)=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)-c\,\big|\,(\varphi,c)\in\epi f^{\circledast}\rvert_{\mathcal{F}}\bigr\}.

Theorem 4.14
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.
The
\mathcal{F}
-biconjugate satisfies
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}=\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}
for all
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof.

We write the equivalent form (
4.15
) of the biconjugate
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}
in a different way:

(4.16)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}(x)

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)-c\,\big|\,\varphi\in\mathcal{F},\;c\in\mathbb{R},\;f^{\circledast}(\varphi)-c\leqslant 0\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)\,\big|\,\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}},\;f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\leqslant 0\bigr\}.

The second equality uses (
3.9
).
We thus observe that
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}
is a pointwise supremum of functions
\varphi\in\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}
.
By (

), we have
\varphi\leqslant f\Leftrightarrow f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\leqslant 0
.
Hence the characterizations of
\lfloor f\rfloor_{\mathcal{F}}
via (
4.2
) and
f^{\circledast\circledast}_{\mathcal{F}}
via (
4.16
) coincide.
∎

We now investigate the triple

-conjugate.

Corollary 4.15
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
.
Then

.

Proof.

This is a direct consequence of

and
Proposition

.
∎

Remark 4.16
.

Our results generalize the notion of the classical biconjugate on locally convex or normed linear spaces

In that setting, the classical biconjugate

with the linear canonical embedding, in short,

In other words,

holds according to

.

5.
Nonlinear Fenchel Conjugates on Manifolds

So far we have considered the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate
f^{\circledast}
to act on the very general class of arguments
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
over general non-empty sets
\mathcal{M}
.
We saw that elementary properties of the classical Fenchel conjugate generalize to this setting.
To obtain further insight, in particular into the connection between Fenchel conjugates and subdifferentials, however, we have to restrict the arguments
\varphi
to a suitable subspace of smooth functions.
Thus, a natural setting for this analysis involves sufficiently smooth manifolds.

In this section we consider a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold
\mathcal{M}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{+\infty\}
, modeled on a real Banach space
X
, and corresponding spaces of smooth functions
\mathcal{F}=C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
.
In case
\mathcal{M}
admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity (
Section

5.1
), it will turn out that
\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))
consists of all lower semi-continuous functions on
\mathcal{M}
; see
Section

5.2
.

Moreover, for
k\geqslant 1
, we can prove a version of the well known Fenchel-Young theorem, connecting the viscosity Fréchet subdifferential with the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate (
Section

5.3
).
Since a real Banach space is also
C^{\infty}
-Banach manifold, this special case is naturally included throughout this section.

Section

5.4
provides a comparison to previous definitions of Fenchel conjugates on manifolds.

5.1.
Smooth Partitions of Unity

The existence of smooth partitions of unity is a fundamental assumption on manifolds that is usually required to globalize local results.
We will need this property in a couple of instances and recall its definition for convenience of the reader.

Definition 5.1
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{+\infty\}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold.

(i)

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\bigl\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\,\big|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\bigr\}
is an open cover of
\mathcal{M}
.
A family of
C^{k}
-functions
\varphi_{\lambda}\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M},[0,1])
,
\lambda\in\Lambda
, is said to be a locally finite
C^{k}
-partition of unity of
\mathcal{M}
subordinate to
\mathcal{C}
if

•

\supp\varphi_{\lambda}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

•

every
x\in\mathcal{M}
has a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
such that
\mathcal{U}\cap\supp\varphi_{\lambda}=\emptyset
for all but a finite number of
\lambda\in\Lambda
,

•

the finite sum
\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\varphi_{\lambda}(x)=1
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

(ii)

\mathcal{M}
is said to admit locally finite
C^{k}
-partitions of unity if for any open cover
\mathcal{C}\coloneqq\{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}\,|\,\lambda\in\Lambda\}
of
\mathcal{M}
, there exists a locally finite
C^{k}
-partition of unity subordinate to
\mathcal{C}
.

In what follows, we will simply speak of
C^{k}
-partitions of unity without explicitly mentioning local finiteness.

Continuous partitions of unity (
k=0
) exist in paracompact topological spaces, in particular in metrizable spaces, and are thus purely a topic of topology.
The existence of
C^{k}
-partitions of unity on Banach manifolds has been studied in
Bonic & Frampton, 1966
.
It has been shown that a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold
\mathcal{M}
admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity if
\mathcal{M}
is separable and is modeled on a Banach space
X
that has an equivalent norm of class
C^{k}
on
X\setminus\{0\}
.
In this case, it is known that
C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
is dense in
C(\mathcal{M})
.
Examples for Banach spaces
X
of this class for
k=1
include separable Banach spaces with separable dual spaces; see
Bonic & Frampton, 1966
, Proposition 3
.
This class comprises, in particular, separable reflexive Banach spaces.
For
k=2
, suitable Banach spaces
X
include separable Hilbert spaces.

A function
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
is said to be lower semi-continuous at
x\in\mathcal{M}
if, for each
\varepsilon>0
, there is a neighborhood
\mathcal{U}
of
x
such that
f(y)\geqslant f(x)-\varepsilon
for all
y\in\mathcal{U}
.
Let us denote by

\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\{f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}_{\infty}\,|\,f\text{ is lower semi-continuous at every }x\in\mathcal{M}\}.

Lemma 5.2

(A
C^{k}
-Sandwich Lemma)
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold that admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity.
Suppose that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
is some open set,
w\in C^{k}(\mathcal{O})
and
-a,b\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
such that
a\leqslant w\leqslant b
on
\mathcal{O}
and
a<b
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{O}
.
Then, given a closed set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
, there exists a function
h\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying
a\leqslant h\leqslant b
on
\mathcal{M}
,
a<h<b
on
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{O}
, and
h=w
on
\mathcal{A}
.

Proof.

The result is a slight extension of Dowker’s sandwich theorem, which was originally formulated for
k=0
and
\mathcal{O}=\emptyset
; see, e. g.,
Dugundji, 1978
, Theorem VIII.4.3
.

Given
r\in\mathbb{R}
, consider the open sets
\mathcal{G}_{r}\coloneqq\{x\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{A}\,|\,a(x)<r<b(x)\}
.
The openness of
\mathcal{G}_{r}
follows from the closedness of
\mathcal{A}
and the lower semi-continuity of
-a
and
b
.
Therefore,
\{\mathcal{G}_{r}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{\mathcal{O}\}
is an open cover of
\mathcal{M}
, since
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
and
a<b
holds on
\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{O}
.
(We could make this cover countable by restricting to
r\in\mathbb{Q}
.)

Suppose that
\{g_{r}\,|\,r\in\mathbb{R}\}\cup\{g_{\mathcal{O}}\}
is a
C^{k}
-partition of unity, subordinate to this open cover.
Denote by
w\,g_{\mathcal{O}}
the extension by zero from
\mathcal{O}
to
\mathcal{M}
of the function
x\mapsto w(x)\,g_{\mathcal{O}}(x)
.
This function is of class
C^{k}
on
\mathcal{O}
by the product rule, and for
x\in\mathcal{M}\setminus\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{M}\setminus\supp g_{\mathcal{O}}
, there is a neighborhood of
x
on which
w\,g_{\mathcal{O}}
vanishes.
Thus
w\,g_{\mathcal{O}}\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
.

Define the function
h\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
via the (locally finite) sum:

h(x)\coloneqq\sum_{r\in\mathbb{R}}r\,g_{r}(x)+w\,g_{\mathcal{O}}(x).

Due to
\mathcal{A}\cap\mathcal{G}_{r}=\emptyset
and thus
g_{r}\equiv 0
on
\mathcal{A}
for all
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
h=w
on
\mathcal{A}
.
Moreover, since
\sum_{r\in\mathbb{R}}g_{r}+g_{\mathcal{O}}\equiv 1
, we observe
h\leqslant\left(\sum_{r\in\mathbb{R}}g_{r}+g_{\mathcal{O}}\right)\,b=b
and
h\geqslant\left(\sum_{r\in\mathbb{R}}g_{r}+g_{\mathcal{O}}\right)\,a=a
.
In both estimates, strict inequality holds if
\sum_{r\in\mathbb{R}}g_{r}(x)\neq 0
.
This is the case, in particular, for
x\not\in\mathcal{O}
.
∎

Corollary 5.3
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold that admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity.
Assume that
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
is some open set and
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cap C^{k}(\mathcal{O})
.
Then, given a closed set
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{O}
,
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
and
\varepsilon>0
, there is a function
\varphi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
satisfying
\varphi\leqslant f
on
\mathcal{M}
,
\varphi=f
on
\mathcal{A}
and
\varphi(x_{0})>f(x_{0})-\varepsilon
.

Proof.

We apply
Lemma

5.2
with the choices
b\coloneqq f
,
a\coloneqq-\iota_{\{x_{0}\}}+f(x_{0})-\varepsilon
and
w\coloneqq f\rvert_{\mathcal{O}}
to obtain
\varphi\coloneqq h
with the desired properties.
∎

5.2.
Restriction to Smooth Test Functions

As announced in the beginning of
Section

5
, we now restrict the Fenchel conjugate
f^{\circledast}
to the domain

\mathcal{F}\coloneqq C^{k}(\mathcal{M})\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M}),

the linear space of
k
-times continuously differentiable real-valued functions on the
C^{k}
-Banach manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
Compared to the classical setting where we have a linear space
V
instead of
\mathcal{M}
, here the space
C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
of smooth functions plays the role of the topological dual space
V^{*}
of continuous linear functions.

We begin with a result on the
\sup
-closure of
C^{k}
-functions.

Proposition 5.4
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold that admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity.
Then

(5.1)

\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))=\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cup\{-\infty\}.

Moreover, we have

f^{\circledast}(\psi)=\sup\bigl\{f^{\circledast}(\varphi)\,\big|\,\varphi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}),\;\varphi\leqslant\psi\bigr\}\quad\text{for }\psi\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M}).

Hence,
f^{\circledast}(\psi)
is uniquely determined by
f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{k}(\mathcal{M})}
.

Proof.

By (
4.7
),
f\in\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))
is the pointwise supremum of some subset
\mathcal{E}\subseteq C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
and thus lower semi-continuous.
If
\mathcal{E}=\emptyset
, we have
f\equiv-\infty
.
In all other cases, we have
f(x)>-\infty
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
.
Hence
\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))\subseteq\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cup\{-\infty\}
.
For the reverse inclusion, suppose that
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Corollary

5.3
with
\mathcal{O}=\emptyset
and arbitrary
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
implies

f(x_{0})=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x_{0})\,\big|\,\varphi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M}),\;\varphi\leqslant f\bigr\}.

This in turn yields
f=\lfloor f\rfloor_{C^{k}(\mathcal{M})}
, hence
f\in\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))
by (
4.8
).
Since we also have
-\infty\in\operatorname{sup-cl}(C^{k}(\mathcal{M}))
, (
5.1
) is proved.
The second assertion now follows from (
4.11
).
∎

In the evaluation of nonlinear Fenchel conjugates
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)
, the test function
\varphi
may sometimes be given only locally on some open set
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.
In this case we only have access to
\sup_{x\in\mathcal{V}}\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\}
.
This term can be interpreted as the Fenchel conjugate of
f
modified by the indicator function of
\mathcal{V}
, i. e.,

(5.2)

(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{V}}\bigl\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\bigr\}.

The following result shows that such local Fenchel conjugates can be extended to global ones without significant changes in value:

Proposition 5.5
.

Suppose that
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\cup\{\infty\}
and
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{k}
-Banach manifold that admits
C^{k}
-partitions of unity.
Consider
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
with
\mathcal{A}
closed and
\mathcal{V}
open.
Moreover, suppose
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
\varphi\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\overline{\mathcal{V}})\cap C^{k}(\mathcal{V})
and
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)<+\infty
.
Then, for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\psi\in C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\psi=\varphi
on
\mathcal{A}
and

(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)-\varepsilon\leqslant f^{\circledast}(\psi)\leqslant(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi).

Proof.

Due to (
3.9
), we may shift
\varphi
by a suitable constant and thus assume w.l.o.g.
(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)=0
.
By (
3.5c
), this means
\varphi\leqslant f
on
\mathcal{V}
.
By definition of the Fenchel conjugate as a supremum, there exists
x_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{V}
such that
\varphi(x_{\varepsilon})>f(x_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon/2
.

The function
g\coloneqq\min\{f,\varphi+\iota_{\overline{\mathcal{V}}}\}
belongs to
\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
as a minimum of lower semi-continuous functions.
Moreover, we have
g=\varphi
on
\mathcal{V}
, so
g\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cap C^{k}(\mathcal{V})
.

By
Corollary

5.3
we obtain
\psi\leqslant g
on
\mathcal{M}
with
\psi=g=\varphi
on
\mathcal{A}
and
\psi(x_{\varepsilon})>g(x_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon/2>f(x_{\varepsilon})-\varepsilon
, hence
f^{\circledast}(\psi)\geqslant-\varepsilon
.
Since
\psi\leqslant f
on
\mathcal{M}
, we conclude
f^{\circledast}(\psi)\leqslant 0
.
∎

5.3.
Conjugates and Subdifferentials

In convex analysis on a locally convex topological space
V
, the Fenchel conjugate is closely related to the convex subdifferential.
The latter can be defined, e. g., for
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(V)
, as follows:

(5.3)

\partial f(x)\coloneqq\bigl\{x^{*}\in V^{*}\,\big|\,f-x^{*}\text{ attains its (global) minimum at }x\bigr\}\subseteq V^{*}.

By convention,
\partial f(x)\coloneqq\emptyset
when
f(x)=+\infty
.
It is a well-known fact of convex analysis that
x^{*}\in\partial f(x)
holds if and only if the Fenchel-Young equality (
3.7a
) holds for
(x,x^{*},f)
, i. e.,
f^{*}(x^{*})=x^{*}(x)-f(x)
.
In other words, the supremum in the definition of the classical Fenchel conjugate is attained at
x
.

The convex subdifferential has been generalized in various ways for functions more general than convex.
One notion that is particularly suited for a generalization to manifolds, is that of the (viscosity) Fréchet subdifferential.
It is obtained from (
5.3
) by replacing the linear function
x^{*}
by a
C^{1}
-function
\varphi
, and by replacing the global minimality by local minimality; see for instance
Borwein & Zhu, 2005
, Definition 3.1.2
:

Definition 5.6
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1}
-Banach manifold.
Moverover, suppose that
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
f(x)\neq+\infty
.
The (viscosity) Fréchet subdifferential
\partial_{F}f(x)
of
f
is defined as follows:

\partial_{F}f(x)\coloneqq\bigl\{\varphi^{\prime}(x)\,\big|\,\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M}),\;f-\varphi\text{ attains a local minimum at }x\bigr\}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\!\mathcal{M},

where
\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\!\mathcal{M}\coloneqq(\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M})^{*}
denotes the cotangent space at
x
.
In case
f(x)=+\infty
, we set
\partial_{F}f(x)\coloneqq\emptyset
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a linear space and
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, then obviously
\partial f(x)\subseteq\partial_{F}f(x)
holds.
Moreover, for
g\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
, we have

\partial_{F}(f+g)(x)=\partial_{F}f(x)+\{g^{\prime}(x)\}.

This is due to
f-\varphi=f+g-(\varphi+g)
and
(\varphi+g)^{\prime}(x)=\varphi^{\prime}(x)+g^{\prime}(x)
.
Furthermore, for
A\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N})
,
g\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{N})
and
x^{*}\in(\partial_{F}g)(A(x))
, we have
A^{\prime}(x)^{\star}x^{*}\in\partial_{F}(g\circ A)(x)
, because
\varphi\circ A
is a minorant of
g\circ A
whenever
\varphi
is a minorant of
g
.
That is,

A^{\prime}(x)^{\star}\partial_{F}g(A(x))\subseteq\partial_{F}(g\circ A)(x).

With
Definition

5.6
we can generalize the Fenchel-Young equality (
3.7a
), which relates the classical Fenchel conjugate
f^{*}
and the subdifferential
\partial f
, to the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate
f^{\circledast}
and the viscosity Fréchet subdifferential
\partial_{F}f
on manifolds:

Proposition 5.7
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1}
-Banach manifold.
Moreover, consider
x\in\mathcal{M}
,
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
and
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
.

(i)

If
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)
, then
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\in\partial_{F}f(x)
and
\delta_{x}\in\partial(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{1}(\mathcal{M})})(\varphi)
.

(ii)

Conversely, if
\delta_{x}\in\partial(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{1}(\mathcal{M})})(\varphi)
, then
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)
.

Proof.

Item

(
i
)

is a direct consequence of the definitions: (
3.7a
), i. e.,
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)
, means that
f-\varphi
attains a (global) minimum at
x
, so
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\in\partial_{F}f(x)
by definition.
On the other hand,
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)=\delta_{x}(\varphi)-\delta_{x}(f)
also means that the restriction of
f^{\circledast}-\delta_{x}
to
C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
attains its global minimum at
\varphi
, so
\delta_{x}\in\partial(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{1}(\mathcal{M})})(\varphi)
by definition.

Notice that
f^{\circledast}
is a convex function on the linear space
C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
by
Proposition

3.6

(

⁢
i
i
i

)

, and thus the use of the convex subdifferential
\partial(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{1}(\mathcal{M})})
is appropriate.

For
item

(

⁢
i
i

)

, suppose that
\delta_{x}\in\partial(f^{\circledast}\rvert_{C^{1}(\mathcal{M})})(\varphi)
holds.
Then the restriction of
f^{\circledast}-\delta_{x}
to
C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
attains its minimum at
\varphi
, which implies
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)
.
∎

We point out that
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\in\partial_{F}f(x)
does
not
in general imply the Fenchel-Young equality
f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)
.
This is because
\varphi^{\prime}(x)\in\partial_{F}f(x)
only entails that
x
is a
local
minimizer of
f-\psi
, for some
\psi\in\mathcal{F}
with
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\varphi^{\prime}(x)
.
By contrast, the Fenchel-Young equality requires
x
to be a
global
minimizer of
f-\varphi
.

However, as shown in the following result, given
x^{*}\in\partial_{F}f(x)
, it is often possible to find
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
such that
x^{*}=\varphi^{\prime}(x)
holds and the Fenchel-Young equality is fulfilled:

Proposition 5.8
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a
C^{1}
-Banach manifold.

(i)

Let
x\in\mathcal{M}
and
f\in\operatorname{lsc}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
Suppose that
x^{*}\in\partial_{F}f(x)
.
Then there exist
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
and an open neighborhood
\mathcal{V}
of
x
such that
\varphi^{\prime}(x)=x^{*}
and the Fenchel-Young equality (
3.7a
) holds for
(x,\varphi\rvert_{\mathcal{V}},f\rvert_{\mathcal{V}})
, i. e.,

(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x).

(ii)

Assume, in addition, that
\mathcal{M}
admits
C^{1}
-partitions of unity.
Then there exists
\psi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
such that
\psi^{\prime}(x)=x^{*}
and the Fenchel-Young equality (
3.7a
) holds for
(x,\psi,f)
, i. e.,

f^{\circledast}(\psi)=\psi(x)-f(x).

Proof.

Item

(
i
)

:
By definition of
\partial_{F}
there exists
\varphi\in C^{1}(\mathcal{M})
with
x^{*}=\varphi^{\prime}(x)
such that
f-\varphi
attains a local minimum at
x
.
Let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be a neighborhood such that this minimum is global on
\mathcal{V}
.
Then clearly (
3.7a
) holds, restricted to
\mathcal{V}
.

Item

(

⁢
i
i

)

:
We may apply
Proposition

5.5
to
\varphi
with some closed neighborhood
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\mathcal{V}
of
x
to obtain a function
\psi
with the property
\psi=\varphi
on
\mathcal{A}
, implying
\psi^{\prime}(x)=\varphi^{\prime}(x)
.
Furthermore,

f^{\circledast}(\psi)\leqslant(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)-f(x)=\psi(x)-f(x)\leqslant f^{\circledast}(\psi).

The first inequality is due to
Proposition

5.5
and the second inequality follows from the definition of the Fenchel conjugate.
∎

5.4.
Restriction to Test Functions Generated by the Exponential Map

In this subsection, we further restrict the choice of (smooth) test functions compared to
Section

5.2
.
In contrast to the infinite-dimensional linear space
\mathcal{F}=C^{k}(\mathcal{M})
of test functions, we now consider a linear space
\mathcal{F}
of the same dimension as
\mathcal{M}
.

To this end, suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is a finite-dimensional
Riemannian

C^{\infty}
-manifold.
Consider a point
x\in\mathcal{M}
and a neighborhood
V
of the origin in the tangent space
\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
such that the exponential map
\expSymbol_{x}
is a diffeomorphism
V\to\mathcal{V}\coloneqq\expSymbol_{x}(V)\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.

We define the restricted function space

\mathcal{F}_{x}\coloneqq\bigl\{x^{*}\circ\expSymbol_{x}^{-1}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{V},\mathbb{R})\,\big|\,x^{*}\in\mathcal{T}^{*}_{x}\!\mathcal{M}\bigr\}

obtained by composition of linear functions on
\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
with the inverse exponential map.

\mathcal{F}_{x}
is a linear space of dimension
\dim(\mathcal{M})
.
Since elements of
\mathcal{F}_{x}
are only defined locally, we proceed as in (
5.2
) and consider, for
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
,

(5.4)

(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\sup_{y\in\mathcal{V}}\bigl\{\varphi(y)-f(y)\bigr\}\quad\text{for }\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{x}.

We now show that (
5.4
) agrees with the classical Fenchel conjugate of
f\circ\expSymbol_{x}+\iota_{V}
, evaluated at
x^{*}
.
To this end, consider
\varphi\in\mathcal{F}_{x}
such that
\varphi=x^{*}\circ\expSymbol_{x}^{-1}
.
We evaluate

\displaystyle(f\circ\expSymbol_{x}+\iota_{V})^{*}(x^{*})

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{x^{*}(\xi)-f(\expSymbol_{x}(\xi))-\iota_{V}(\xi)\,\big|\,\xi\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}\bigr\}

(5.5a)

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{x^{*}(\xi)-f(\expSymbol_{x}(\xi))\,\big|\,\xi\in V\bigr\}

(5.5b)

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{x^{*}(\expSymbol_{x}^{-1}(y))-f(y)\,\big|\,y\in\mathcal{V}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(y)-f(y)\,\big|\,y\in\mathcal{V}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi).

We are now in the position to compare
(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)
with the concept of
x
-Fenchel conjugate from
Bergmann et al., 2021
, Definition 3.1
.
As can be seen by comparison with (
5.5a
), that definition coincides with
(f\circ\expSymbol_{x}+\iota_{V})^{*}
but was only defined for strongly geodesically convex sets
\mathcal{V}
.
In case
\mathcal{M}
is a Hadamard manifold, one can choose
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{M}
and we obtain
V=\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
.
Then
(f+\iota_{\mathcal{V}})^{\circledast}(\varphi)
also agrees with the
x
-dual function from
Ahmadi & Amini, 2010
, Definition 3.1
.
As a variant,
Silva et al., 2022
, Definition 3.1
use (
5.5b
) on Hadamard manifolds with
\mathcal{V}=\mathcal{M}
and
V=\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
, amounting to a definition of a Fenchel conjugate on the entire cotangent bundle.

6.
Nonlinear Fenchel Conjugates on Groups

This section is concerned with the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate on groups.
This is a natural setting to consider convolutions of extended real-valued functions.
Restricting the test functions to group homomorphisms into the additive reals, we show that the infimal convolution formula holds, i. e., the conjugate of an infimal convolution is the sum of the conjugates (
Section

6.1
).
Moreover, replacing group homomorphisms by continuous affine functions, we obtain a notion of convex functions on groups through their
\sup
-closure (
Section

6.2
).

6.1.
Nonlinear Infimal Convolution

The infimal convolution
f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g
for functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(V)
on a linear space
V
is defined as

(6.1)

(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in V}f(x-y)+g(y)=\inf_{z\in V}f(z)+g(x-z).

The infimal convolution formula (see, e. g.,
Bauschke & Combettes, 2011
, Proposition 13.21
) shows that

(6.2)

(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)^{*}=f^{*}+g^{*}

holds for
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(V)
.

We now generalize the infimal convolution (
6.1
) to functions
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
on a group
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
, compare
Bachir, 2015
, by setting

(6.3)

(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)(x)\coloneqq\inf_{y\in\mathcal{M}}f(x\cdot y^{-1})+g(y)=\inf_{z\in\mathcal{M}}f(z)+g(z^{-1}\cdot x).

We denote by

\mathcal{H}\coloneqq\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))

the linear space of group homomorphisms
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)\to(\mathbb{R},+)
.
We are now in the position to generalize the infimal convolution formula (
6.2
) to the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
is a group and
f,g\in\mathcal{P}_{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.
Then the conjugate of the infimal convolution
f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g
equals the sum of the conjugates when restricted to
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
, i. e.,

(6.4)

(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)^{\circledast}(\varphi)=f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+g^{\circledast}(\varphi)\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in\mathcal{H}.

Proof.

For any
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, we find

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)+g^{\circledast}(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\sup_{z\in\mathcal{M}}\bigl\{\varphi(z)-f(z)\bigr\}+\sup_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\bigl\{\varphi(y)-g(y)\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(z)+\varphi(y)-[f(z)+g(y)]\,\big|\,y,z\in\mathcal{M}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(z\cdot y)-[f(z)+g(y)]\,\big|\,y,z\in\mathcal{M}\bigr\}\quad\text{due to $\varphi\in\mathcal{H}$}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)-[f(x\cdot y^{-1})+g(y)]\,\big|\,x,y\in\mathcal{M}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\sup\bigl\{\varphi(x)-(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)(x)\,\big|\,x\in\mathcal{M}\bigr\}

\displaystyle=(f\mathbin{\star_{\textup{inf}}}g)^{\circledast}(\varphi).\qed

6.2.
A Notion of Convexity on Groups

In this subsection we introduce a notion of (mid-point) convexity for extended real-valued functions on the group
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
.
For
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
, we begin by studying
\operatorname{sup-cl}(\mathcal{H})
, the set of pointwise suprema of subsets of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
.
We will see that this set plays the role of the class of lsc. convex functions in our nonlinear setting.

To this end, consider the embedding (
4.13
)

J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\colon\mathcal{M}\ni x\mapsto\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}

that maps
x\in\mathcal{M}
to the evaluation function
\delta_{x}\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}
so that we have
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(x)(\varphi)=\delta_{x}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)
for
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
is a group and
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
.
Then the embedding
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}\colon(\mathcal{M},\cdot)\to(\mathcal{H}^{\prime},+)
is a homomorphism.

Proof.

Suppose that
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.
Then we have

\delta_{x}(\varphi)+\delta_{y}(\varphi)=\varphi(x)+\varphi(y)=\varphi(x\cdot y)=\delta_{x\cdot y}(\varphi)

and thus
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(x)+J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(y)=\delta_{x}+\delta_{y}=\delta_{x\cdot y}=J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}(x\cdot y)
.
∎

We work under the assumption that the linear space
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
is
finite-dimensional
.

Remark 6.3
.

For comparison, suppose that
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
is the additive Abelian group
(V,+)
of a finite-dimensional real linear space
V
endowed with the Euclidean topology.
We modify
\mathcal{H}
to denote the linear space of all
continuous
additive homomorphisms from
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
into
(\mathbb{R},+)
.
Cauchy proved that continuous additive functions are linear; see, for instance,
Hamel, 1905
.
We thus obtain
\mathcal{H}=V^{*}
.
In this setting, the pointwise suprema of subsets of
\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}
are precisely the convex and lsc. functions on
\mathcal{H}^{*}=V^{**}
, which is linearly and topologically isomorphic to
V
; see
Theorem

6.7
.

The following two lemmas provide sufficient conditions for
\mathcal{H}
to be finite-dimensional.

Lemma 6.4
.

Consider a group
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
, its commutator
[\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}]
and the linear space
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
.
Suppose that the
\mathcal{M}
or its Abelianization
\mathcal{M}^{\textup{ab}}\coloneqq\mathcal{M}\,/\penalty\,[\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M}]
are finitely generated.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional.

Proof.

When
\mathcal{M}
is finitely generated, then so is
\mathcal{M}^{\textup{ab}}
, and thus we only consider the latter case.

Since the codomain
(\mathbb{R},+)
is Abelian, the linear spaces
\mathcal{H}=\Homo((\mathcal{M},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
and
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\coloneqq\Homo((\mathcal{M}^{\textup{ab}},\cdot),(\mathbb{R},+))
are isomorphic as groups; see for instance
Rose, 1994
, Problem 167, p.60
.
It is easy to verify that they are also isomorphic as linear spaces.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{M}^{\textup{ab}},\cdot)
is finitely generated and
F\coloneqq\bigl([x_{1}],\ldots,[x_{n}]\bigr)
is a generating family with
x_{i}\in\mathcal{M}
and cosets
[x_{i}]
w.r.t. the commutator.
Then any
\varphi\in\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
is uniquely determined by its values on
F
.
Consequently, there is a monomorphism
I\colon\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\ni\varphi\mapsto\varphi(F)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Due to potential relations present among the generators, the image of
I
is a subspace
U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
In any case,
\mathcal{H}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{H}}
are both isomorphic to
U
and thus of finite dimension.
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Consider a Lie group
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
with Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{m},[\cdot,\cdot])
, and commutator

[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]\coloneqq\{[v,w]\,|\,v,w\in\mathfrak{m}\}.

Suppose that
\mathfrak{m}\,/\penalty\,[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
is finite dimensional and
\mathcal{M}
is connected.
Then
\mathcal{H}
is finite-dimensional and
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{\prime}}
is surjective.

Proof.

Consider the Lie group
(\mathcal{M},\cdot)
with neutral element
e
and its exponential map
\expSymbol_{e}\colon\mathfrak{m}\to\mathcal{M}
, as well as
(\mathbb{R},+)
with neutral element
0
and
\expSymbol_{0}=\textup{id}_{\mathbb{R}}
.
Let
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
.
By the commutativity of homomorphisms and exponential maps, we obtain

\varphi^{\prime}(e)\,v=\expSymbol_{0}(\varphi^{\prime}(e)\,v)=\varphi(\expSymbol_{e}(v))\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathfrak{m}.

Thus, for any
v,w\in\mathfrak{m}
:

(6.5)

\varphi(\expSymbol_{e}(v)\expSymbol_{e}(w))=\varphi(\expSymbol_{e}(v))+\varphi(\expSymbol_{e}(w))=\varphi^{\prime}(e)(v+w).

Since for
\varphi\in\mathcal{H}
, its derivative
\varphi^{\prime}(e)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism (
Lee, 2012
, Theorem 8.44
), we obtain in particular that

\varphi^{\prime}(e)[v,w]=[\varphi^{\prime}(e)\,v,\varphi^{\prime}(e)\,w]=0

holds, since
(\mathbb{R},+)
is Abelian and thus its Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
vanishes.
Denoting the linear space of these Lie algebra homomorphisms by

\mathfrak{h}\coloneqq\bigl\{\ell\in\mathfrak{m}^{\prime}\,\big|\,\ell([v,w])=0\text{ for all }v,w\in\mathfrak{m}\bigr\}\cong(\mathfrak{m}/[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}])^{\prime}

we thus obtain a linear mapping

(6.6)

H\colon\mathcal{H}\ni\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{\prime}(e)\in\mathfrak{h}.

It follows from Lie theory that
H
is injective when
\mathcal{M}
is connected, because then every
x\in\mathcal{M}
can be written as a product of factors
\expSymbol_{e}(v_{i})
with
v_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}
.
Due to
Equation

6.5
this implies that
\varphi
is given uniquely by its values of
\varphi^{\prime}
on
\mathfrak{m}
.
Thus, by the injectivity of
H
,
\dim\mathcal{H}\leqslant\dim\mathfrak{h}=\dim\mathfrak{m}\,/\penalty\,[\mathfrak{m},\mathfrak{m}]
.

Concerning the surjectivity of
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{*}}
, we compute

\displaystyle J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{*}}(\expSymbol_{e}(v))(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\varphi(\expSymbol_{e}(v))=\varphi^{\prime}(e)v

\displaystyle=(H\varphi)(v)=H^{\star}v(\varphi)\quad\text{for all }v\in\mathfrak{m},\varphi\in\mathcal{H}.

Since
H
is injective,
H^{\star}
is surjective, and thus
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{*}}\circ\expSymbol_{e}
is surjective as well.
This in turn implies the surjectivity of
J_{\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{H}^{*}}
.
∎

A subset

of a linear space is called mid-point convex if, for any

A function

Proposition 6.8
.

Suppose that

\psi(x)\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\psi(y\cdot x)+\psi(y^{-1}\cdot x)\bigr)\quad\text{for all }x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

7.
Conclusion

The classical Fenchel conjugate
f^{*}
is defined for extended real-valued functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(V)
on a linear space
V
, and it acts on
linear
functions on
V
.

In this paper we generalize the classical Fenchel conjugate to nonlinear spaces
\mathcal{M}
, where linear functions do not exist.
This new concept of a nonlinear Fenchel conjugate
f^{\circledast}
applies to extended real-valued functions
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
on any set
\mathcal{M}
.
Several classical key results — including the Fenchel-Young inequality and calculus rules — carry over to the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate.
For the classical Fenchel conjugate, the concept of
\Gamma
-regularization is obtained by approximating a function by the supremum of its affine minorants.
For the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate, we generalise this to the so-called
\mathcal{F}
-regularization using minorants more general than just affine functions.
In this setting, the biconjugation theorem carries over to the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate.

By considering differentiable manifolds in place of general sets
\mathcal{M}
, we are able to relate the nonlinear Fenchel conjugate with the viscosity Fréchet subdifferential.
In addition, we recover previous attempts to generalize Fenchel conjugates to manifolds.
Finally, when
\mathcal{M}
is a group, we found a generalization of the classical infimal convolution formula.

An interesting aspect for future work is how this general framework of nonlinear Fenchel conjugates can be used to derive optimization algorithms based on operator splittings similar to primal-dual hybrid gradient algorithms, see for instance
Esser et al., 2010
, or the Chambolle-Pock algorithm
Chambolle & Pock, 2011
.

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} f^{\circledast}(\varphi)&=\sup\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\,|\,x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)\}\\
&=\sup\{\varphi(x)-f(x)\,|\,x\in\mathcal{M},\;\varphi(x)\neq-\infty\text{ and }f(x)\neq+\infty\}\end{aligned}

(
3.3
)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\inf\{c\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi(x)\leqslant f(x)+c\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{M}\}

(
3.4
)

\displaystyle f^{\circledast}(\varphi)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-c\,|\,(x,c)\in\epi f\}

for
f,\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{\pm\infty}(\mathcal{M})
.

Proof of equivalence.

The conditions in (
3.2
) exclude points outside of
\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
and, in addition, points where
\varphi(x)-f(x)=-\infty
.
This shows the equivalence between (
3.1
) and (
3.2
).
To prove the equivalence of (
3.2
) and (
3.3
), we notice that we can exclude from (
3.3
) all points that impose no restrictions on the admissible values for
c
.
In other words, (
3.3
) is equal to
\inf\{c\in\mathbb{R}\,|\,\varphi(x)\leqslant f(x)+c\text{ for all }x\in\underline{\mathcal{D}}(\varphi-f)\}
.
The equivalence between (
3.2
) and (
3.3
) is now straightforward.
Finally we show the equivalence between (
3.1
) and (
3.4
).
If
f(x)=+\infty
, then
(x,c)\not\in\epi f
for any
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\varphi(x)=-\infty
, then
\varphi(x)-c=-\infty
for all
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
Thus we can exclude all these
x
from the supremum in (
3.4
), and we may write the right-hand side of (
3.4
) as

\sup\{\varphi(x)-c\,|\,(x,c)\in\epi f\}=\sup\{\varphi(x)-c\,|\,c\geqslant f(x)\text{ and }x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)\}.

For
x\in\mathcal{D}(\varphi-f)
we have that
\varphi(x)-f(x)=\sup\{\varphi(x)-c\,|\,c\geqslant f(x)\}
is well-defined, showing the desired equality.
∎

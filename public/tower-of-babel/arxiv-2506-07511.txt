Uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs and Šoltés’ weighted graphs
Stijn Cambie, Ajay Tiwari

Abstract
A Šoltés’ hypergraph is a hypergraph for which the removal of any of its vertices does not change its total distance. We prove that every uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph has order at least 10 10 , there exist uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs for almost every order or uniformity, and there exist a non-regular uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph. By also providing infinitely many weighted Šoltés’ graphs, we conclude that Šoltés’ problem can be answered positively for the most natural generalisations of graphs.

w
for which
\deg_{H}(vw)=1.

This implies there are at least
8
vertices sharing only one hyperedge with
v
. Since
\lvert H\setminus v\rvert=8
and equality is impossible, we conclude.
∎

Since
H
is a Šoltés’ hypergraph,
H\setminus v
is a connected
4
-uniform hypergraph of order
8
and thus satisfies
36=\binom{9}{2}\leq W(H)=W(H\setminus v)\leq 44
by
11
and
12
.

For every pair of adjacent or non-adjacent vertices
u,v,
we count the number
x_{uv}
of vertices
w\in V\setminus\{u,v\}
for which
d_{H}(u,v)<d_{H\setminus w}(u,v).

Observe that by definition

W(H\setminus v_{i})=W(H)-\sigma(v_{i})+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,w\in V\\
u,w\neq v_{i}\end{subarray}}\big[d_{H\setminus v_{i}}(u,w)-d_{H}(u,w)\big]

(1)

and since
H
is a Šoltés’ hypergraph,

2W(H)=\sum_{i=1}^{9}\sigma(v_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{9}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,w\in V\\
u,w\neq v_{i}\end{subarray}}\big[d_{H\setminus v_{i}}(u,w)-d_{H}(u,w)\big]

(2)

We now end the proof with a small case-analysis.
If
W(H)=44
, then by
12
, we know
W(H\setminus v)=44
implies that
\lvert E(H\setminus v)\rvert=3
for every
v\in V(H)
and thus
H
is
d
-regular, where
d
satisfies
\frac{9d}{4}-d=3
and thus
d=\frac{12}{5},
which is impossible.

If
37\leq W(H)\leq 43
, we write
W(H)=\binom{9}{2}+r
, where
r=n_{2}
satisfies
1\leq r\leq 7
.

From claims
15
,
16
,
17
and
18
, we know that
(\sigma(v)-8)+\sum_{u\in N(v)}(2-x_{uv})\geq 2
for every vertex
v
.
Summing over all
9
vertices in
V(H)
, results into

2(36+r)-9\cdot 8+2\sum_{uv\in E(H)}(2-x_{uv})\geq 2\cdot 9
and thus
\sum_{uv\in E(H)}x_{uv}\leq 63-r.

If
y
is an upper bound for the number of pairs of vertices at distance
3
of each other in
H\setminus v_{i}
, then

\sum_{i=1}^{9}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u,w\in V\\
u,w\neq v_{i}\end{subarray}}\big[d_{H\setminus v_{i}}(u,w)-d_{H}(u,w)\big]\leq 9y+\sum_{uv\in E(H)}x_{uv}.

By
11
,
12
and
13
, we conclude that (
2
) is not satisfied since
2(36+r)>63-r+18=81-r
for
4\leq r
and

.

For

the last inequality becomes an equality. Every vertex

needs to satisfy

and thus

for two vertices

Since

Since the properties for

∎

3
Abundancy of uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs

In this section, we prove that there exist uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs of any order
n\geq 10
.

For
10\leq n\leq 100
, examples were discovered with multiple constructions. See e.g.
[
9
, Sec. 3 & app A]
for examples.

Given
n\geq 100
, we can find
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
n=\binom{s}{2}-t,\quad 0\leq t\leq(s-2)
.
Let
k=n-(t+2s+1)
, and let
H
be the hypergraph with vertex set
V=\{0,1,2,...,n-1\}
and edge set
E=\{e_{i}\mid 0\leq i\leq n-1\}
,
where
e_{i}=\{i,i+2s+k-1\}\cup\{i+s+1,i+s+2,\ldots,i+s+k-2\}
(vertices are considered modulo
n
).

By definition,
H
is a vertex-transitive,
k
-uniform hypergraph of order
n
.

Note that
k-2\geq\frac{n}{2}
since
\binom{s}{2}\geq 7s\geq 2(2s+t+1)+t+4
(
s\geq 15
since
n>91
).
As such, it is trivial that
diam(H\setminus v)\leq 2

\forall v\in V(H)
and easily seen that
diam(H)=1.

We now prove the following lemma.

Lemma 19
.

There are exactly
n-1
pairs of non-adjacent vertices in
H\setminus v.

Proof.

Since
H
is vertex-transitive, one can check this for
v=0.

The edge
e_{\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}}
covers every vertex (and thus combination of vertices in this range)
\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}+s+1\leq j\leq\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}+s+k-2=n-(\mathopen{}\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor\mathclose{}+s+1).

The edge
e_{-s}
and
e_{t+s+2}
cover all vertices
1\leq j\leq\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}+s+1
, resp.
n-(\mathopen{}\left\lceil\frac{t}{2}\right\rceil\mathclose{}+s+1)\leq j\leq-1
.
For
s+1\leq i\leq\mathopen{}\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor\mathclose{}+s+1,
the edge
e_{i}
covers every
n-(\mathopen{}\left\lfloor\frac{t}{2}\right\rfloor\mathclose{}+s+1)\leq j\leq n-s
.

Finally, let
(n-i,j)\in[n-s,\ldots,n-1]\times[1,s]
(here
1\leq i,j\leq s
).
If
i+j\geq s+1
, the edge
e_{n-i}
contains both
n-i
and
j
.

If
i+j\leq s
, and thus
n-i-j>k-2+s,
the only edge which might contains
n-i
and
j
but not
0
, is
e_{j}.

This happens precisely if
i+j=t+2.

Hence the number of non-neighbours in
H\setminus v
of
j
is
s-j-1
if
1\leq j\leq t+1
and
s-j
if
t+2\leq j\leq s.

We conclude that the number of pairs of vertices in
H\setminus v
which are distance
2
apart equals

\sum\limits_{j=1}^{t+1}(s-j-1)+\sum\limits_{j=t+2}^{s-1}(s-j)=\binom{s}{2}-t-1=n-1.

∎

Hence

W(H\setminus v)=\binom{n-1}{2}+n-1=\binom{n}{2}=W(H)

and thus
H
is a Šoltés’ hypergraph.

So we have found uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph of every order
n\geq 10.

Remark 20
.

The above construction provides uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs for
75\%
of the possible uniformities as well.
The construction can be generalised to one for every
r\geq 1
, by having hyperedges
e_{i}=[i-s-r..i-s-1]\cup[i..i+k-2r]\cup[i+k-2r+s+1..i+k-r+s]
(again indices modulo
n
) for
n=\binom{s}{2}-(2r-1)t-r^{2}+1
and
k=\binom{s}{2}-2rt-2s-r^{2}
for
0\leq t<s-\binom{r+1}{2}
. For
4\leq k\leq 10^{7}
, there were only
8
uniformities missing;
\{905,1301,1721,2801,9641,11969,52109,5335709\}.

Since there are many other adaptations of the construction possible, for every uniformity at least
4
, one can expect that there exist
k
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraphs.
We decided not to work out these other cases, as it does not provide new insights in the main question by Šoltes or other things.

No
3
-uniform example has been found, so one subquestion of
[
3
, Ques. 13]
remains open.

4
An irregular
9
-uniform Šoltés’ hypergraph

In this short subsection, we present an example addressing the fifth subquestion of
[
3
, Ques. 13]
.

Example 21
.

Let
H
be the
9
-uniform hypergraph with vertex set
V=[54]=\{1,2,\ldots,54\}

and
27

\{\{a,a+1,a+2,a+3,a+4,a+5,a+7,a+16,a+18\}\mid a\in[54],a\equiv 0\pmod{2}\}.

Here elements have to be interpreted modulo

Then

Proof.

The irregularity is easy to check, since

5

Due to the symmetry of the construction, the hypergraph

4
(for odd vertices) and

Now one can check that
W(H)=2349=W(H\setminus 1)=W(H\setminus 2)
(

This is also verified in
[

,

∎

The space spinor formalism and estimates for spinor fields
Mariem Magdy, Juan A. Valiente Kroon

Abstract
We show how the space spinor formalism for 2-component spinors can be used to construct estimates for spinor fields satisfying first order equations. We discuss the connection of the approach presented in this article with other strategies for the construction of estimates. In addition, we recast several concepts related to the notion of hyperbolicity in the context of spinor equations. The approach described in this article can be regarded as an adaptation to first order equations of the method of positive commutators for second order hyperbolic equations.

The 2-spinor formalism is a powerful tool to study the properties of null hypersurfaces in 4-dimensional spacetimes. In addition, and somehow related to the latter, 2-spinors provide a convenient description of massless fields and of the curvature of spacetimes —see e.g.

A fundamental property of equation (

Although these observations are often cited facts, there is a lack of a systematic treatment, in the literature, of the structural PDE properties of equations like (

Indeed, the standard strategy to analyse a spinorial equation is to project it with respect to a spin dyad so as to obtain a system of scalar equations. This approach leads to large expressions in which the algebraic properties of the equations are hard to identify and exploit.

This article adopts the point of view that equations like (

As it will be shown in the main text, a natural first attempt is based on the observation that equation (

The key observation in this article is that the formalism of space spinors provides an appropriate toolkit to address the question raised in the previous paragraph. In fact, and perhaps not so unsurprisingly, the construction of estimates for spinor equations put forward in this article is closely related to the hyperbolic reduction of the equation.
The central idea behind this construction is that the hyperbolic reduction must be done with respect to a timelike vector field which is proportional to the vectorfield multiplier used in the construction of estimates.

The main motivation behind the construction described in this article is the analysis of the conformal Einstein field equations in a neighbourhood of spatial infinity by means of the techniques of

The resulting conformal evolution system has a very clearly definite hierarchical structure in which the components of the rescaled Weyl spinor satisfy a (coupled) symmetric hyperbolic system while all the other remaining variables satisfy transport equations along the conformal geodesics. It is expected that the methods developed in the present paper will provide the technical toolkit to analyse these equations. This problem will be analysed elsewhere.

2.1
Irreducible decompositions

A fundamental tool for the systematic study of equation (
1
) is the decomposition of spinors of arbitrary valence into irreducible components. Central to the latter is the idea that
only symmetric spinors matter
which is implied by the following seminal result —cf.
[
22
]
, Proposition (3.3.54):

Proposition 1
.

Any spinor
\varphi_{A\cdots F}
is the sum of a totally symmetric spinor
\varphi_{(A\cdots F)}
and outer products of the antisymmetric spinor
\epsilon_{AB}
with totally symmetric spinors of lower valence.

Remark 1
.

For simplicity the above result is only stated for spinors with covariant unprimed indices. An analogous result holds for more general spinors.

As some of the computations in the proof of this result will be used repeatedly in the sequel, we provide a brief discussion of the argument in Appendix
A
.

2.1.1
A more detailed look at the decomposition in irreducible terms

In the subsequent discussion, we will require more information than the one provided by Proposition
1
. To this end we introduce some further notation. Given a nonnegative integer
n
, an arbitrary spinor of valence
n
with only unprimed covariant indices will be written as
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
.

Now, let

I_{n}\equiv\{1,\,2,\ldots,n\}.

Given a further integer
k
,
0\leq k\leq n
, we will be interested in the
partial permutations
of
k
elements of
I_{n}
—that is, ordered lists of
k
elements of
I_{n}
. Let us denote the set of those partial permutations by
S^{k}_{n}
. For
k=n
one recovers the symmetric set —i.e. the set of permutations of
I_{n}
. The elements of
S^{k}_{n}
can be thought of as (ordered)
k
-tuples of elements of
I_{n}
. In this spirit, we write

(j_{1},\,j_{2},\ldots,j_{k})\in S^{k}_{n}.

Of particular relevance for the irreducible decomposition of
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
are those partial permutations where
k
is even so that one writes
k=2\ell

and which satisfy

j_{1}<j_{2},\quad j_{3}<j_{4},\quad\cdots\quad,\,j_{2\ell-1}<j_{2\ell}.

We denote the set of this type of partial permutations by
E_{n}^{2\ell}
(
pairwise lexicographic partial permutation
) and write

(j_{1},\,j_{2}\,\ldots,j_{2\ell-1},\,j_{2\ell})\in E_{n}^{2\ell}.

Finally, given
(j_{1},\,j_{2}\,\ldots,j_{2\ell-1},\,j_{2\ell})\in E_{n}^{2\ell}
let
I_{n}\setminus\{j_{1},\,j_{2}\,\ldots,j_{2\ell-1},j_{2\ell}\}
denote the set of elements in
I_{n}
not appearing in the partial permutation under consideration. With the above notation let

\varphi^{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}},\qquad\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{n-2\ell}\}=I_{n}\setminus\{j_{1},\,j_{2}\,\ldots,j_{2\ell-1},\,j_{2\ell}\},

be defined as the totally symmetric spinor obtained by contraction of the pairs of indices

\{A_{j_{1}},\,A_{j_{2}}\},\quad\{A_{j_{3}},\,A_{j_{4}}\},\quad\cdots\quad\{A_{2\ell-1},\,A_{2\ell}\}

and then symmetrising the leftover indices
\{A_{i_{1}},\ldots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}\}
. We will adopt the convention that contractions are carried out in the Southwest to Northeast direction. That is, we have that

\varphi^{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}\equiv\varphi_{(A_{i_{1}}\cdots|A_{j_{1}}|\cdots}{}^{A_{j_{1}}}{}_{\cdots|A_{j_{3}}|}{}_{\cdots}{}^{A_{j_{3}}}{}_{\cdots\cdots|A_{2\ell-1}|\cdots}{}^{A_{2\ell-1}}{}_{\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}})}.

The spinors
\varphi^{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
for all possible
(j_{1},\,j_{2}\,\ldots,j_{2\ell-1},\,j_{2\ell})\in E_{n}^{2\ell}
with
2\ell\leq n
are the
irreducible components
of
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
discussed in Proposition
1
. Observe that if
\ell=0
then
E^{0}_{n}=\varnothing
, so that

\varphi^{\varnothing}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}=\varphi_{(A_{1}\cdots A_{n})}

is the totally symmetric part of
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
—i.e. there are no contractions.

Remark 2
.

If the original spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
is symmetric over a certain subset of its indices then some of the irreducible components
\varphi^{(j_{1},j_{2},\ldots,j_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
will vanish.

With the aid of the above notation, one can obtain a more detailed version of Proposition
1
. Namely, one has the following:

Proposition 2
.

A valence
n
spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}
admits the decomposition

\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{n}}=\sum_{2\ell\leq n}\sum_{(j_{1},\ldots,j_{2\ell})\in E^{2\ell}_{n}}\epsilon_{A_{j_{1}}A_{j_{2}}}\cdots\epsilon_{A_{j_{2\ell-1}}A_{j_{2\ell}}}\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}},

(3)

where

\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}\equiv\sum_{(k_{1},\ldots,k_{2\ell})\in E^{2\ell}_{n}}\mathfrak{c}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{2\ell}}_{k_{1}\cdots k_{2\ell}}\varphi^{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}

with
\mathfrak{c}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{2\ell}}_{k_{1}\cdots k_{2\ell}}
some numerical coefficients.

Proof.

The expansion follows from the recursive application of the arguments of the proof of Proposition
1
as discussed in Appendix
A
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Observe that the spinors
\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
are irreducible since they are linear combinations of the irreducible components
\varphi^{(j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
. The explicit value of the coefficients
\mathfrak{c}^{j_{1}\cdots j_{2\ell}}_{k_{1}\cdots k_{2\ell}}
will not be required in the sequel. In particular, as it can see from specific examples, many of the coefficients can be zero. It can be verified that knowledge of the spinors
\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
is equivalent to knowledge of
\varphi^{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
—the latter can be obtained from the former by solving a linear algebraic system of equations. In other words, the two sets of spinors
\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
and
\varphi^{(k_{1},\cdots,k_{2\ell})}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
correspond to different bases of irreducible components.

Remark 4
.

In the case of spinors with given symmetries, specific components
\phi^{[j_{1},\cdots,j_{2\ell}]}_{A_{i_{1}}\cdots A_{i_{n-2\ell}}}
will vanish.

2.2.1
Space spinor counterparts

Relation (
5
) defines an isomorphism between spinors with primed and unprimed indices. Namely, given
\mu_{A^{\prime}}
one defines the spinor
\mu_{A}
, its space spinor counterpart, via the relation

\mu_{A}\equiv\tau_{A}{}^{A^{\prime}}\mu_{A^{\prime}}.

Observe that

\tau^{A}{}_{A^{\prime}}\mu_{A}=-\mu_{A^{\prime}},

so that
\mu_{A^{\prime}}
and
\mu_{A}
contain the same information. The above ideas can be extended in the obvious way to higher valence spinors with arbitrary valence. For example, for the spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}A^{\prime}_{1}\cdots A^{\prime}_{q}}
in equation (
1

\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B_{1}\cdots B_{q}}\equiv\tau_{B_{1}}{}^{A^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots\tau_{B_{q}}{}^{A^{\prime}_{q}}\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}A^{\prime}_{1}\cdots A^{\prime}_{q}}.

(6)

One of the advantages of working with the space spinor counterpart
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
is that one can make use of the machinery of irreducible decompositions over the whole set of indices
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p},B_{1},\ldots B_{q}\}
rather than independently on the subsets of unprimed and primed indices,
\{A_{1},\ldots,A_{p}\}
and
\{A^{\prime}_{1},\ldots A^{\prime}_{q}\}
, respectively.

Key for the purposes of this article is the observation that

\kappa_{A}

\widehat{\kappa}_{A}

\widehat{\kappa}_{A}\equiv\tau_{A}{}^{A^{\prime}}\bar{\kappa}_{A^{\prime}}.

(7)

One extends the operation of Hermitian conjugation to higher valence spinors with unprimed indices in the obvious manner. Given (say) a valence

\bar{\varphi}_{A^{\prime}_{1}\cdots A^{\prime}_{m}}=(-1)^{m}\tau^{P_{1}}{}_{A^{\prime}_{1}}\cdots\tau^{P_{m}}{}_{A^{\prime}_{m}}\widehat{\varphi}_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}.

In the following, it will be convenient to work with the space spinor counterpart of

\nabla_{AB}\equiv\tau_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}.

2.2.4
The Weingarten spinor

Derivatives of the Hermitian spinor
\tau^{AA^{\prime}}
will play an important role in our analysis. The derivatives are encoded in the
Weingarten spinor

\chi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
which we define as

\chi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\tau_{BB^{\prime}}.

(11)

The space spinor counterpart of
\chi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
, denoted by
\chi_{ABCD}
, is then defined by

\displaystyle\chi_{ABCD}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau_{D}{}^{C^{\prime}}\nabla_{AB}\tau_{CC^{\prime}},

\displaystyle\phantom{\chi_{ABCD}}=\chi_{(AB)CD}+\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{AB}\chi_{CD},

where

\chi_{(AB)CD}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau_{D}{}^{C^{\prime}}\mathcal{D}_{AB}\tau_{CC^{\prime}},\qquad\chi_{AB}\equiv\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tau_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}\mathcal{{\bm{D}}}\tau_{AA^{\prime}}.

By definition,
\chi_{(AB)CD}
and
\chi_{AB}
can be shown to satisfy the reality properties

\widehat{\chi}_{(AB)CD}=\chi_{(AB)CD},\qquad\widehat{\chi}_{AB}=-\chi_{AB},

and the symmetries

\chi_{(AB)CD}=\chi_{(AB)(CD)},\qquad\chi_{AB}=\chi_{(AB)}.

In terms of
\chi_{(AB)CD}
and
\chi_{AB}
, we can write the irreducible decomposition for the derivative of
\tau^{AA^{\prime}}
as

\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\tau_{BB^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2}\chi_{BF}\tau_{AA^{\prime}}\tau^{F}{}_{B^{\prime}}+\sqrt{2}\chi_{(AF)BG}\tau^{F}{}_{A^{\prime}}\tau^{G}{}_{B^{\prime}}.

(12)

3
Hyperbolicity and spinorial equations

In this section, we discuss the notion of hyperbolicity for spinorial equations. The fundamental observation is that the 2-spinor formalism brings to the fore the structural properties underlying the hyperbolicity of the equations. In particular, while equation (
1
) is, strictly speaking not hyperbolic, it readily implies a symmetric hyperbolic system.

3.1
Hyperbolicity and spinors

In this section we consider some basic ideas regarding the notion of hyperbolicity in the context of spinorial equations.

Remark 5
.

For simplicity, the discussion in this section is restricted to linear evolution equations. Most of the notions here presented can be extended to the quasilinear setting.

3.1.1
The symbol

Recall that the
principal part
of a linear differential operator consists of the terms in the operator with the highest order derivatives. In turn, the
symbol
of the operator at a point
p\in\mathcal{M}
,
\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}
, is obtained by replacing the derivatives
\partial_{\mu}
with respect to some local coordinates
x=(x^{\mu})
with a covector
\xi_{\mu}\in T^{*}_{p}\mathcal{M}
. In the following, we identify
T^{*}_{p}\mathcal{M}
with
\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{p}\mathcal{M}
—the space of Hermitian valence 2 covariant spinors. In this spirit, the symbol of a spinorial equation can be obtained by formally replacing the spinor covariant derivatives
\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}
in the principal part by the components
\xi_{{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}
of a Hermitian spinor
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{H}^{*}_{p}\mathcal{M}
—i.e.
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}=\bar{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}
.
Given a fixed
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}
, the
Kernel
of
\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}
is the vector space of spinors
\varphi
(at
p
) satisfying the algebraic linear equation

\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\varphi=0.

(13)

The Kernel of
\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}
depends on the particular choice of covector
\xi_{\mu}
. If the Kernel is trivial (that is, it consists solely of the zero spinor) then we write, in a slight abuse of notation,
\mbox{Ker}\,\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}=0
. Now, we define the
characteristic subset

C^{*}_{p}\subset T^{*}_{p}\mathcal{M}=\mathfrak{H}_{p}\mathcal{M}

as

C^{*}_{p}=\{\xi_{AA^{\prime}}\in\mathfrak{H}_{p}\mathcal{M}\;|\;\xi_{AA^{\prime}}\neq 0,\;\mbox{Ker}\,\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}\neq 0\}.

Finally, it is recalled that an operator is said to be
elliptic
if its symbol is bijective. This notion requires a precise specification of the domain and range of the operator.

We now particularise the above discussion to the prototype equation (
1
). In this case, the symbol is given by

\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\varphi=\xi^{Q}{}_{A^{\prime}}\varphi_{QA_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}.

(14)

Setting the above expression equal to zero, contracting with
\xi_{C}{}^{A^{\prime}}
and using that
\xi_{C}{}^{A^{\prime}}\xi^{Q}_{A^{\prime}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\epsilon^{Q}{}_{C}|{\bm{\xi}}|^{2}
with
|{\bm{\xi}}|^{2}\equiv\xi_{PP^{\prime}}\xi^{PP^{\prime}}
, one concludes that

|{\bm{\xi}}|^{2}\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}=0.

So, if
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}\neq 0
then one necessarily has that
|{\bm{\xi}}|^{2}=0
—that is, the covector
\xi_{\mu}
corresponding to
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}
is null. The latter implies the well-known fact that the characteristics of massless spin equations are null hypersurfaces. Accordingly, there exists a spinor
\kappa_{A}
such that
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}=\kappa_{A}\bar{\kappa}_{A^{\prime}}
. Consider now a further spinor
\mu_{A}
which is not proportional to
\kappa_{A}
. Without loss of generality, we can assume that
\kappa_{A}\mu^{A}=1
. Thus, one has a second singled out null direction described by the Hermitian spinor
\underline{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}=\mu_{A}\bar{\mu}_{A^{\prime}}
. The covectors
\xi_{\mu}

and
\underline{\xi}_{\mu}
associated to
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}
and
\underline{\xi}_{AA^{\prime}}
span the characteristic subset
C^{*}_{p}
. The spinors
\kappa_{A}
and
\mu_{A}
can be used as a spin dyad (base) which we denote by
\{o^{A},\,\iota^{A}\}
. Now, the symbol (
14
) is degenerate. To see this, one observes that from (
13
) it follows that

o^{Q}\varphi_{QA_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}=0,

which, in turn, implies that

\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}=o_{A_{1}}\zeta_{A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}

(15)

where
\zeta_{A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
is a non-zero spinor. In a similar fashion one can find the additional solution

\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}=\iota_{A_{1}}\vartheta_{A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}.

(16)

The solutions (
15
) and (
16
) span
\mbox{Ker}\,\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}
. Observe that it does not span the whole of the space of spinors of the form
(\cdot)_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
at
p
.

It is interesting to contrast the above specific discussion with what happens for the wave operator
\square\equiv\nabla^{a}\nabla_{a}
acting, say, on a spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
. In this case the symbol is given by

\sigma_{\bm{\xi}}\cdot\varphi=|{\bm{\xi}}|^{2}\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}.

As before, the characteristic subset at a point
p
is generated by a pair of null covectors
\xi_{\mu}
and
\underline{\xi}_{\mu}
. Now, however, the Kernel consists of all the space
\mathfrak{S}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
at
p
.

3.1.2
Symmetric hyperbolic systems

There are several notions of hyperbolicity in the literature —see e.g.
[
6
,
24
]
. Given that the prototype equation (
1
) is first order, in the following, we focus on symmetric hyperbolic systems.

Consider a system of partial differential equations of the form

\mathbf{A}^{\mu}(x)\partial_{\mu}\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{B}(x)

(17)

where
\mathbf{u}
is a
\mathbb{C}^{N}
-valued unknown for some positive integer
N
,
\mathbf{A}^{\mu}(x)
,
\mu=0,\ldots,3
are
(N\times N)
-matrix-valued functions of the local coordinates
x=(x^{\mu})
and
\mathbf{B}(x)
is a vector-valued function of
x
. Equation (
17
) is said to be
symmetric hyperbolic
if:

i.

there exists a timelike covector
\tau_{\mu}
such that
\mathbf{A}^{\mu}(x)\tau_{\mu}
is a positive-definite matrix;

ii.

the matrices
\mathbf{A}^{\mu}(x)
are Hermitian —that is,
(\mathbf{A}^{\mu})^{*}=\mathbf{A}^{\mu}
.

From the above definition, using basic results from matrix theory, it follows that
\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\tau_{\mu}
is an invertible matrix.

Remark 6
.

The above classic definition of symmetric hyperbolic systems is not naturally suited to spinor equations like (
1
) as it requires writing the unknown
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
as a column vector
\mathbf{u}
. In the following, we will consider at alternative manners of describing symmetric hyperbolic systems in the context of spinor equations and fields.

3.2
Spinorial equations

Equation (
1
) is an abstract spinor equation in the sense that it makes no reference to any coordinate system and spin basis. In order to further discuss its properties, consider a normalised spin frame
\{{\bm{\varepsilon}}_{\bm{A}}{}^{A}\}
. Contracting equation (
1
) with respect to the basis, one obtains the set of scalar equations described by

\nabla^{\bm{Q}}{}_{{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}+F_{{\bm{A}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}{}^{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{q}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{q}}=f_{{\bm{A}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}.

The components
\varphi_{{\bm{A}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{A}}^{\prime}_{q}}
are scalars depending on some local coordinates
x=(x^{\mu})
.

Now, an important observation is that the combination

\displaystyle\nabla^{\bm{Q}}{}_{{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+F_{{\bm{1}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}{}^{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{q}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{q}}=f_{{\bm{1}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}},

(18a)

\displaystyle-\nabla^{\bm{Q}}{}_{{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-F_{{\bm{0}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}{}^{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{q}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{p}{\bm{Q}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{Q}}_{q}}=-f_{{\bm{0}}^{\prime}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}

(18b)

is a
symmetric hyperbolic system
—see e.g.
[
27
]
. Indeed, using that

\nabla^{\bm{Q}}{}_{{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{Q}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}=\nabla_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}-\nabla_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}\varphi_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}

one has that the principal part of the system (
18a
)-(
18b
) can be written in matricial form as

\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}{\bm{\varphi}}\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}&-\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}\\
-\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}&\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}\end{array}\right)\partial_{\mu}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\varphi_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}\\
\varphi_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{A}}_{2}\cdots{\bm{A}}_{p}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{1}\cdots{\bm{B}}^{\prime}_{q}}\end{array}\right).

The matrices
\mathbf{A}^{\mu}
are Hermitian as the vectors
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}\equiv\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}{\bm{\partial}}_{\mu}
and
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}\equiv\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}{\bm{\partial}}_{\mu}
are real and while for
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}\equiv\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}{\bm{\partial}}_{\mu}
and
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}\equiv\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}{\bm{\partial}}_{\mu}
, one has that
{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{0}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}=\overline{{\bm{e}}_{{\bm{1}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}}
. Letting

\xi_{\mu}\equiv\sigma_{\mu}{}^{{\bm{0}}{\bm{0}}^{\prime}}{}+\sigma_{\mu}{}^{{\bm{1}}{\bm{1}}^{\prime}}

and using that
\sigma^{\mu}{}_{{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}\sigma_{\mu}{}^{{\bm{B}}{\bm{B}}^{\prime}}=\delta_{\bm{A}}{}^{\bm{B}}\delta_{{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}{}^{{\bm{B}}^{\prime}}
, it follows that

\mathbf{A}^{\mu}\xi_{\mu}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right).

The latter is clearly a positive definite matrix.

3.2.1
A wave equation for the spinor field

An alternative approach to the one discussed above consists of deriving a wave equation for the spinor field
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
. We briefly discuss this approach here for completeness.

Applying the derivative
\nabla_{A_{1}}{}^{A^{\prime}}
to equation (
1
) and using the identity

\nabla_{A_{1}}{}^{A^{\prime}}\nabla^{Q}{}_{A^{\prime}}=-\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{A_{1}}{}^{Q}+\square_{A_{1}}{}^{Q}

(19)

where
\square_{AB}\equiv\nabla_{(A|A^{\prime}|}\nabla_{B)}{}^{A^{\prime}}
is the so-called
Penrose box
encoding the information of the commutator of the spinorial covariant derivative
\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}
, one readily obtains that

\displaystyle\square\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}-2F_{Q^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{p}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{q}}\nabla_{A_{1}}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\varphi_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{p}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt-2\nabla_{A_{1}}{}^{Q^{\prime}}F_{Q^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{p}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{q}}\varphi_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{p}Q^{\prime}_{1}\cdots Q^{\prime}_{q}}-2\square_{A_{1}}{}^{Q}\varphi_{QA_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt=-2\nabla_{A_{1}}{}^{Q^{\prime}}f_{Q^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}.

(20)

Now, recall that the action of the Penrose box
\square_{AB}
on valence 1 spinors
\mu_{C}
and
\nu_{C^{\prime}}
is given by

\square_{AB}\mu_{C}=\Psi_{ABCDQ}\mu^{Q}-2\Lambda\mu_{(A}\epsilon_{B)C},\qquad\nabla_{AB}\nu_{C^{\prime}}=\Phi_{ABC^{\prime}Q^{\prime}}\nu^{Q^{\prime}},

where
\Psi_{ABCD}
,
\Lambda
and
\Phi_{ABA^{\prime}B^{\prime}}
denote, respectively, the Weyl spinor, Ricci Scalar and tracefree Ricci spinor —collectively, these spinor fields encode the curvature of the spacetime
(\mathcal{M},{\bm{g}})
. It thus follows from the previous discussion that equation (
20
) constitutes a system of wave equations for the various components of the spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
.

Remark 7
.

Observe that in the case that certain contractions of the spinor
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
vanish, then equation (
20
) can give rise to algebraic constraints similar in nature to the
Buchdahl constraint
for massless fields.
In the reminder of this article we ignore these constraints and assume them to be satisfied.

Remark 8
.

While any solution of equation (
1
) is a solution to the wave equation (
20
), the converse is not necessarily true. The conditions under which the latter may be true need to be elucidated via a
propagation of constraints
-type of argument. The natural way of carrying out such an argument is to define a
zero-quantity

Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
and then compute
\square Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
.
If it is possible, making use of the commutator of covariant derivatives and the definition of
Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
, to obtain an equation of the form

\square Z=H(Z,\nabla Z),

(21)

where
H
is an homogeneous expression of
Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}
and its first order derivatives, then from the uniqueness of solutions to wave equations, it follows that the criteria for propagation of equation (
1
), from say, a Cauchy initial value problem, is that the initial data satisfy the conditions

Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}=0,\qquad\nabla_{CC^{\prime}}Z_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B_{q}}=0,\qquad\mbox{on the initial hypersurface.}

Depending on the specific form of equation (
1
), it may be possible to rewrite the above conditions in terms of conditions on the initial value of
\varphi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{p}B^{\prime}_{1}\cdots B^{\prime}_{q}}
. Finally, observe that whether it is possible to obtain an expression of the form (
21
) or not very much depends on the specific structural properties of equation (
1
) and of the background geometry
(\mathcal{M},{\bm{g}})
.

Decomposition of the equation in irreducible terms

where the various blocks in the above expression given by

\displaystyle S_{i}{}^{j}\equiv\sigma_{i}{}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}S_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}{}^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}\sigma^{j}_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}},

\displaystyle P_{i}{}^{l}\equiv\sigma_{i}{}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}P_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\sigma^{k}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}},

\displaystyle Q_{k}{}^{i}\equiv\sigma_{k}{}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}Q_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}{}^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}\sigma^{j}_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}},

\displaystyle R_{k}{}^{l}\equiv\sigma_{k}{}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}R_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\sigma^{l}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}.

In order to discuss the properties of the above symbol in relation to
symmetric hyperbolicity
, it is more convenient to consider the manifestly more symmetric matrix

\mathbf{A}[{\bm{\xi}}]\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}S_{i}{}^{j}&-2\mathfrak{c}P_{i}{}^{l}\\
2\mathfrak{c}Q_{k}{}^{j}&\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{2}m}{m-1}R_{k}{}^{l}\end{array}\right).

Now, to verify that the matrix
\mathbf{A}[{\bm{\xi}}]
satisfies property (i) of symmetric hyperbolicity in Section
3.1.2
set, for simplicity
\xi=2
and
\xi_{AB}=0
so that
\xi_{AA^{\prime}}=\tau_{AA^{\prime}}
. In this case, it readily follows that

\mathbf{A}[\tau]\equiv\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\delta_{i}{}^{j}&0\\
0&\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{c}^{2}m}{m-1}\delta_{k}{}^{l}\end{array}\right)

which is manifestly a positive definite matrix. Property (ii) (Hermiticity) can be expressed in terms of properties of the various blocks —more precisely, one requires that

(S_{i}{}^{j})^{*}=(S_{i}{}^{j}),\quad(R_{k}{}^{l})^{*}=R_{k}{}^{l},\quad(P_{i}{}^{l})^{*}=-(Q_{l}{}^{i})

where
i,\,j=0,\ldots,m
and
k,\,l=0,\cdots m-2
. These properties can be readily verified recalling (
30
) and that
\hat{\epsilon}_{AB}=\epsilon_{AB}
.

Remark 14
.

Ultimately, the symmetric hyperbolicity of the
\phi
-
\psi
system can be traced back to the specific combination of operators appearing in the principal part (
29a
)-(
29b
). More precisely, in (
29a
) the field
\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}
is being acted upon by the Fermi derivative
\mathcal{D}
and the curl operator —the later being a self-adjoint elliptic operator in the space of symmetric spinors. The field
\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}
is being acted upon by the symmetric tracefree derivative which is an elliptic underdetermined operator. Similarly, in (
29b
) the field
\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}
is being acted upon by
\mathcal{D}
and the curl operator while
\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}
is being acted upon by the divergence operator. It is important to observe that the divergence and the symmetric tracefree derivative are formal adjoints of each other in the space of symmetric spinors. This structural observation allows to recognise by mere inspection potential symmetric hyperbolic systems.

3.4.2
Further examples

We illustrate the previous general discussion with a number of additional examples.

The massless spin-
s
equation.
Arguably, the simplest example of an equation fitting the template of the model equation (
1
) is the massless spin-
s
equation satisfied by a totally symmetric valence
2s
spinor
\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{2s}}
with
s\in\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{N}
—namely,

\nabla^{Q}{}_{A^{\prime}}\phi_{QA_{2}\cdots A_{2s}}=0.

(32)

Observe that this equation corresponds to the spacial case of the
\phi
-
\psi
system where the spinors
\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}},G_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}
and
f_{A^{\prime}A_{2}\cdots A_{m}}
vanish.
A direct application of the space spinor formalism allows to decompose this equation as

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{2s}}-2\mathcal{D}^{Q}{}_{(A_{1}}\phi_{A_{2}\cdots A_{2s})Q}=0,

(33a)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{PQ}\phi_{PQA_{3}\cdots A_{2s}}=0.

(33b)

Thus, one sees that equation (
33a
) plays the role of an evolution equation while (
33b
) is a constraint. Finally, observe that applying
\nabla_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}
to equation (
32
) and using the identity (
19
) one obtains the wave equation

\square\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{2s}}+2(2s-1)\ \Psi^{QR}{}_{(A_{1}A_{2}}\phi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{2s})QR}-(2s-1)\Lambda\phi_{A_{1}\cdots A_{2s}}=0.

If one contracts any two of the free indices in the above equation, the first and third terms vanish by the symmetry of
\phi_{A_{1}\ldots A_{2s}}
, implying that the second term must also vanish. When
s>1
, one therefore recovers the well-known
Buchdahl constraint

[
22
,
26
]
—namely that

\phi_{ABM(C\ldots K}\Psi_{L)}{}^{ABM}=0.

These restrictions do not arise in the cases
s=\frac{1}{2}
and
s=1
—i.e. for the Dirac and Maxwell fields.

The wave equation for a scalar field.
The scalar wave equation

\square\phi=0

can be recast in first order form so that it fits the scheme of the prototype equation (
1
). To this end, introduce the auxiliary variable
\phi_{AA^{\prime}}\equiv\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi
. Now, the definition of
\phi_{AA^{\prime}}
together with the torsion-freeness of
\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}
implies that

\nabla_{(A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\phi_{B)Q^{\prime}}=0.

Thus, from the identity

\nabla_{A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\phi_{BQ^{\prime}}=\nabla_{(A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\phi_{B)Q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{QQ^{\prime}}\phi_{QQ^{\prime}},

together with
\square\phi=\nabla^{QQ^{\prime}}\phi_{QQ^{\prime}}
one concludes that

\nabla_{A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\phi_{BQ^{\prime}}=0.

This equation is similar to the prototype equation (
24
) except for the fact that the contraction between the covariant derivative and the spinor is made on primed indices.

Defining
\varphi\equiv\tau^{AA^{\prime}}\phi_{AA^{\prime}}
and
\varphi_{AB}\equiv\tau_{(B}{}^{A^{\prime}}\phi_{A)A^{\prime}}
, one has the decomposition

\phi_{AA^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi\tau_{AA^{\prime}}-\tau^{Q}{}_{A^{\prime}}\varphi_{AQ}.

In the following, for simplicity of the presentation, it is assumed that the Weingarten spinor,
\chi_{ABCD}
, of
\tau_{AA^{\prime}}
vanishes. Under these assumptions, a space spinor split readily yields the following system of evolution equations:

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\varphi+2\mathcal{D}^{AB}\varphi_{AB}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\varphi_{AB}-\mathcal{D}_{AB}\varphi+2\mathcal{D}_{(A}{}^{Q}\varphi_{B)Q}=0.

The Bianchi identity for the tracefree Ricci tensor.
One of the constituents of the conformal Einstein field equations is the Bianchi equation for the Schouten tensor —see e.g.
[
27
]
. In its spinorial form this equation is given by

\nabla_{A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}L_{BQ^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}+\Sigma^{Q}{}_{C^{\prime}}\phi_{ABCQ}+\frac{1}{12}\epsilon_{AB}\nabla_{CC^{\prime}}R(x)

(34)

where
L_{BQ^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}
is the spinor counterpart of the Schouten tensor,
\phi_{ABCQ}
is the Rescaled Weyl spinor,
\Sigma_{QC^{\prime}}
encodes the derivative of a conformal factor and
R(x)
is the Ricci scalar —the latter plays the role of a
conformal gauge source function
which can be freely specified. Now, the Schouten tensor admits the decomposition

L_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}=\Phi_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{24}\epsilon_{AC}\epsilon_{A^{\prime}C^{\prime}}R(x),

where
\Phi_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}
is the spinorial counterpart of the tracefree part of the Ricci tensor. The space spinor version of
L_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}
is defined as

\displaystyle L_{ABCD}\equiv\tau_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}\tau_{D}{}^{C^{\prime}}L_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\phantom{L_{ABCD}}=\Phi_{ABCD}+\frac{1}{24}\epsilon_{AC}\epsilon_{BD}R(x),

where
\Phi_{ABCD}\equiv\tau_{B}{}^{A^{\prime}}\tau_{D}{}^{C^{\prime}}\Phi_{AA^{\prime}CC^{\prime}}
so that one has the symmetries

\Phi_{ABCD}=\Phi_{CBAD}=\Phi_{ADCB}.

A spinor with these symmetries admits the decomposition

\Phi_{ABCD}=\Phi_{(ABCD)}+\frac{1}{2}\big(\epsilon_{A(B}\Phi_{D)C}+\epsilon_{C(B}\Phi_{D)A}\big)+\frac{1}{3}\Phi h_{ABCD},

where
h_{ABCD}\equiv\epsilon_{A(C}\epsilon_{D)B}
and

\Phi_{AB}\equiv\Phi_{(AB)Q}{}^{Q},\qquad\Phi\equiv\Phi_{ABCD}h^{ABCD}.

Making use of the above decomposition in equation (
34
), one obtains a system for the fields
\Phi_{(ABCD)}
,
\Phi_{AB}
and
\Phi
whose principal part is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\Phi_{(ABCD)}-\mathcal{D}_{(AB}\Phi_{CD)},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\Phi_{AB}+2\mathcal{D}^{PQ}\Phi_{(PQAB)}-\frac{1}{3}\mathcal{D}_{AB}\Phi,

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\Phi+\mathcal{D}^{PQ}\Phi_{PQ}.

More details of the above calculation can be found in
[
27
]
, subsection 13.2.3.

4
Strategies for the
construction of estimates for spinor fields

In this section, we review the basic strategy for the construction of estimates using the so-called
positive commutator method
and discuss how this strategy can be adapted to the analysis of spinor fields and equation (
1
).

4.1
Basic notions

Before providing an overview of the positive commutator method, we briefly review some basic concepts and definitions which will be used throughout.

4.1.1
Inner product

Let
(\mathcal{M},{\bm{g}})
denote a
4
-dimensional Lorentzian manifold with metric
{\bm{g}}
and let
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
be a subset with boundary
\partial\mathcal{U}
. If
\mathrm{d}\mu
denotes a measure on
\mathcal{U}
, the inner product of two symmetric spinor fields
\psi_{A\ldots F}
and
\gamma_{A\ldots F}
will be defined as

\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\equiv\int_{\mathcal{U}}\psi_{A_{1}\ldots A_{p}}\widehat{\gamma}^{A_{1}\ldots A_{p}}\mathrm{d}\mu,

(35)

where
\widehat{\gamma}^{A\ldots F}
is the Hermitian conjugate of
\gamma^{A\ldots F}
defined earlier —see equation(
7
).

Remark 15
.

The measure
d\mu
is not necessarily the volume form induced by the metric
{\bm{g}}
. When this is the case, we make use of the symbol
d\mu_{\bm{g}}
.

Let
\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U})
denote the
\text{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
-spinor bundle on
\mathcal{U}
, then the inner product satisfies the properties:

i.

\overline{\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}=\left\langle\!\left\langle\gamma,\psi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\quad\psi,\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U})
;

ii.

\left\langle\!\left\langle a\psi_{1}+b\psi_{2},\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=a\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi_{1},\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle+b\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi_{2},\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\quad a,b\in\mathbb{C}\;\quad\;\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U})
;

iii.

\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,a\gamma_{1}+b\gamma_{2}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\bar{a}\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\gamma_{1}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle+\bar{b}\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\gamma_{2}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\quad a,b\in\mathbb{C}\;\quad\;\psi,\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U})
;

iv.

for
\psi\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U})
,
\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\psi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle>0\text{ if and only if }\psi\neq 0
.

For any linear operator
{\bm{A}}
, we define its adjoint (with respect to the measure
d\mu
) by

\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{A}}\psi,\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,{\bm{A}}^{*}\gamma\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\quad\psi,\gamma\in\mathfrak{S}(\mathcal{U}).

For scalar fields
\phi,\psi
on
\mathcal{U}
, the inner product
\left\langle\!\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
as defined by equation (
35
) reduces to

\left\langle\!\left\langle\phi,\psi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{U}}\phi\bar{\psi}\mathrm{d}\mu,

where
\bar{\psi}
is the complex conjugate of
\psi
.

4.1.2
The domain of integration

Figure 1:
Schematic representation of the domain on which estimates are computed. The domain
\mathcal{U}
is assumed, in general, to have a boundary
\partial\mathcal{U}
. The causal nature of the boundary is, in principle, arbitrary. Crucially, the domain
\mathcal{U}
is assumed to be covered by a nonsingular congruence generated by the integral curves of a timelike vector field
{\bm{\zeta}}
.

The domain
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
over which the inner product
\left\langle\!\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
defined is assumed to be compact with boundary
\partial\mathcal{U}
. The particular assumptions on
\mathcal{U}
very much depend on the specific applications under consideration. Here, we only assumed that
\mathcal{U}
is covered by a non-intersecting congruence generated by the integral curves of a timelike vector field
{\bm{\zeta}}
—see Figure
1
.

4.1.3
Some ancillary definitions

The positive commutator method make use of a carefully chosen vector field multiplier
\zeta^{a}
(
{\bm{\zeta}}
). In the following, let
\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}
denote its spinorial counterpart. By analogy to the tensor case, we define the
deformation spinor
of
\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}
as

\Pi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{BB^{\prime}}+\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\zeta_{AA^{\prime}}),

where
\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}
is the spinor version of the Levi-Civita connection of the metric
{\bm{g}}
. The deformation spinor
\Pi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
can be written in terms of the trace
\Sigma
and trace-free part
\Sigma_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
as

\Pi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}=\Sigma_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{4}\Sigma\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}},

where

\displaystyle\Sigma_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{4}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{BB^{\prime}}+\nabla_{AB^{\prime}}\zeta_{BA^{\prime}}+\nabla_{BA^{\prime}}\zeta_{AB^{\prime}}+\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\zeta_{AA^{\prime}}),

(36a)

\displaystyle\Sigma\equiv\nabla^{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{AA^{\prime}}.

(36b)

Then, the irreducible decomposition of the derivative of
\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}
is given by

\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{BB^{\prime}}=\Sigma_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\Xi_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Xi}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon_{AB}+\frac{1}{4}\Sigma\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}},

where

\displaystyle\Xi_{AB}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{A}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\zeta_{BQ^{\prime}}+\nabla_{B}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\zeta_{AQ^{\prime}}),

(37a)

\displaystyle\bar{\Xi}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\nabla^{Q}{}_{A^{\prime}}\zeta_{QB^{\prime}}+\nabla^{Q}{}_{B^{\prime}}\zeta_{QA^{\prime}}).

(37b)

We now define the
K-current
of
\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}
as

K_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{BB^{\prime}}+\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\zeta_{AA^{\prime}}-\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}\zeta^{QQ^{\prime}}).

In terms of
\Pi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}
, the K-current can be written as

K_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}=\Pi_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{QQ^{\prime}}{}_{QQ^{\prime}}\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}.

Making use of the K-current one obtains the following alternative expression for the decomposition of
the derivative of
\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}
:

\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\zeta_{BB^{\prime}}=K_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\Xi_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\Xi}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon_{AB}+\frac{1}{2}\Sigma\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}.

In later calculations, it will be convenient to specify the choice of
{\bm{\zeta}}
as

\zeta^{AA^{\prime}}=\varpi^{2}\tau^{AA^{\prime}}.

(38)

for some suitable weight
\varpi
and
\tau^{AA^{\prime}}
as described in Section
2.2
so that, in particular,
\tau_{AA^{\prime}}\tau^{AA^{\prime}}=2
.

4.2
A model problem: the scalar wave equation

The construction of estimates for the scalar wave equation using the
positive commutator method
will be the fundamental point of reference in our subsequent discussion. As such, we provide a brief review of it.

4.2.1
Basic identities

In the following, we will consider the non-homogeneous wave equation

\square\phi=f,

(39)

where
\square\equiv\nabla^{PP^{\prime}}\nabla_{PP^{\prime}}
is the usual D’Alambertian operator —expressed in terms of the spinor covariant derivative. The scalar
f
is a suitable (known) source term. The scalar field
\phi
will be assumed to be complex.

Given a domain
\mathcal{U}\subset\mathcal{M}
with boundary
\partial\mathcal{U}
, we will denote by
\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
the set of functions vanishing to infinite order at
\partial\mathcal{U}
. Let
{\bm{\zeta}}
denote a real vector field and define its action on
\phi\in\dot{C}(\mathcal{U})
by

{\bm{\zeta}}\phi\equiv\zeta^{a}\nabla_{a}\phi.

(40)

Then, its adjoint
{\bm{\zeta}}^{*}
is defined by

\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\psi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\left\langle\!\left\langle\phi,{\bm{\zeta}}^{*}\psi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,\qquad\phi,\,\psi\in\dot{C}(\mathcal{U}).

Given (
40
), one can show that

{\bm{\zeta}}^{*}=-{\bm{\zeta}}-\nabla_{a}\zeta^{a}.

(41)

In terms of the above, the
self-adjoint commutator
of
\square
and
{\bm{\zeta}}
is defined as

{\bm{A}}\equiv{\bm{\zeta}}^{*}\square+\square{\bm{\zeta}}.

It readily follows from this definition that
{\bm{A}}={\bm{A}}^{*}
. A further computation then shows that

\displaystyle{\bm{A}}\phi=\Pi^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\phi-\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{PP^{\prime}}{}_{PP^{\prime}}\square\phi+\left(\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\Pi^{BB^{\prime}AA^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\nabla^{AA^{\prime}}\Pi^{BB^{\prime}}{}_{BB^{\prime}}\right)\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{{\bm{A}}\phi}=T_{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}{}^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\Pi^{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\phi+\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\left(T_{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}{}^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\Pi^{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}\right)\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{{\bm{A}}\phi}=2\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\left(T_{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}{}^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\Pi^{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{{\bm{A}}\phi}=2\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\left(K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\right),

where

\displaystyle T_{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}{}^{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta_{A}{}^{C}\delta_{A^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}\delta_{B}{}^{D}\delta_{B^{\prime}}{}^{D^{\prime}}+\delta_{A}{}^{D}\delta_{A^{\prime}}{}^{D^{\prime}}\delta_{B}{}^{C}\delta_{B^{\prime}}{}^{C^{\prime}}+\epsilon_{AB}\bar{\epsilon}_{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}\epsilon^{CD}\epsilon^{C^{\prime}D^{\prime}}\right),\qquad

(42a)

\displaystyle K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\equiv T_{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}{}^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\Pi^{CC^{\prime}DD^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\phantom{K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}}=\Pi^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{2}\Pi^{QQ^{\prime}}{}_{QQ^{\prime}}\epsilon^{AB}\epsilon^{A^{\prime}B^{\prime}}.

(42b)

Integrating by parts, one finds that

\displaystyle\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{A}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{U}}\bar{\phi}{\bm{A}}\phi\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle\phantom{\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{A}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}=-2\int_{\mathcal{U}}K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\bar{\phi}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}},\qquad\phi\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{U}).

One can summarise the above discussion in the following:

Lemma 1
.

Given
\phi\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
, one
has that

\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{A}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=-2\left\langle\!\left\langle K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\bar{\phi},1\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.

4.2.2
Construction of estimates

In the remainder of this subsection, we show how the above result can be used to construct estimates for the scalar field. From equation (
39
) and the definition of the self-adjoint commutator
{\bm{A}}
, it follows the identity

\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{A}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,f\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,

and we no longer assume that
\phi\in\dot{C}^{\infty}(\mathcal{U})
.
From the above, using Lemma
1
, one can then write

-2\left\langle\!\left\langle K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\bar{\phi},1\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\approx 2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,f\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,

where in the above, and in the following, the symbol
\approx
is used to denote equality up to boundary terms. Now, it is observed that the term
K^{AA^{\prime}BB^{\prime}}\nabla_{AA^{\prime}}\phi\nabla_{BB^{\prime}}\phi
is a quadratic form on the components of the gradient of
\phi
,
\mathbf{d}\phi
. If this quadratic form has some special properties, one can then, in turn, use them to control a suitable norm of
\mathbf{d}\phi
. A particular case of interest arises when
{\bm{\zeta}}
is chosen so that
{\bm{K}}
is negative definite —say, there exists a constant
\mathfrak{K}>0
such that

{\bm{K}}(\mathbf{d}\phi,\mathbf{d}\bar{\phi})\leq-\mathfrak{K}|\mathbf{d}\phi|^{2},

(43)

where
|\mathbf{d}\phi|^{2}
is the standard norm defined by

|f|^{2}\equiv f\cdot\bar{f},

for any complex function
f
. In addition to the above, it is also assumed that there is a constant
\mathfrak{Z}>0
such that

||{\bm{\zeta}}\phi||^{2}\leq\mathfrak{Z}||\mathbf{d}\phi||^{2}.

(44)

In the above,
||{\bm{\zeta}}\phi||^{2}\equiv\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,{\bm{\zeta}}\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle
and similarly for
\mathbf{d}\phi
. Under the above circumstances and using Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities to show that

2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,f\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\leq||f||^{2}+||{\bm{\zeta}}\phi||^{2},

one concludes that

2(\mathfrak{K}-\mathfrak{Z})||\mathbf{d}\phi||^{2}\preccurlyeq||f||^{2}+||{\bm{\zeta}}\phi||^{2},

(45)

where
\preccurlyeq
denotes
\leq
up to boundary terms. The above inequality provides a non-trivial bound on
||\mathbf{d}\phi||
in terms of
||f||
and boundary terms.

Now, the construction of estimates for Sobolev norms requires control also on the norm
||\phi||
. In order to obtain the required control, start by observing that

\displaystyle 2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle=\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle+\overline{\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}

\displaystyle\phantom{2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}=\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle+\left\langle\!\left\langle\phi,{\bm{\zeta}}\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle

\displaystyle\phantom{2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle}\approx\left\langle\!\left\langle({\bm{\zeta}}+{\bm{\zeta}}^{*})\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.

Using equation (
41
), one then obtains

2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle{\bm{\zeta}}\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle\approx-\left\langle\!\left\langle(\mbox{div}_{\bm{g}}{\bm{\zeta}})\phi,\phi\right\rangle\!\right\rangle.

(46)

To exploit this identity, it is further assumed that there is a constant
\mathfrak{D}>0
such that

\mbox{div}_{\bm{g}}{\bm{\zeta}}\leq-\mathfrak{D}.

If this is the case, using the bound (
44
), the Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequalities to show that

\displaystyle\mathbf{E}(\phi,\psi)-\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{F}(\phi,\psi)=\nabla^{Q}{}_{Q^{\prime}}\big(\widehat{\phi}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta_{A_{1}}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\phi_{A_{2}\cdots A_{m}Q}\big)+\nabla_{PP^{\prime}}\big(\widehat{\psi}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta^{PP^{\prime}}\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathfrak{c}\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}\big(\widehat{\phi}{}^{PQA_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta_{P}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\big)-\mathfrak{c}^{2}\nabla^{P}{}_{P^{\prime}}\big(\widehat{\psi}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta^{QP^{\prime}}\phi_{PQA_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\big)

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}W_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\phi}_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}+\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}X_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\psi}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathfrak{c}\psi^{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}Y_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\phi}_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}+\mathfrak{c}\psi^{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}Z_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\psi}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}},

with

W_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}},\quad X_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}},\quad Y_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}},\quad Z_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}},

depending solely on
\chi_{ABCD}
and
\varpi

Remark 16
.

In other words, the principal part in the quadratic form

\mathbf{G}(\phi,\psi)\equiv\mathbf{E}(\phi,\psi)-\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{F}(\phi,\psi)

can be expressed as a total divergence. This is, in some sense, the main observation of this article. The divergence identity in Lemma
2
provides the connection with the
positive commutator method
described in Subsection
4.2
. More precisely, letting
\mathbf{S}
and
\mathbf{R}
denote, respectively, the operators associated to the principal part of equations (
28a
)-(
28b
), a naive adaptation of the positive commutator method to first order systems would suggest to consider the commutators
[\mathbf{S},{\bm{\zeta}}]
and
[\mathbf{R},{\bm{\zeta}}]
. However, these commutators are, generically, differential operators of first order so that they do not provide any obvious control of a quadratic form involving the pair of spinor fields
(\phi,\psi)
. Instead, the right approach is to consider a suitable contraction of the commutators
[\mathbf{S},\zeta^{{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}]
and
[\mathbf{R},\zeta^{{\bm{A}}{\bm{A}}^{\prime}}]
—that is, the commutator of a first order and a zero order differential operator. It should be noticed that, in this approach, the construction of estimates is closely related to the hyperbolic reduction procedure using the space spinor formalism.

For future use, define

\displaystyle J_{QQ^{\prime}}\equiv\widehat{\phi}{}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta_{A_{1}Q^{\prime}}\phi_{A_{2}\cdots A_{m}Q},

\displaystyle I_{PP^{\prime}}\equiv\widehat{\psi}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta_{PP^{\prime}}\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}},

\displaystyle K^{QQ^{\prime}}\equiv\widehat{\phi}^{PQA_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta_{P}{}^{Q^{\prime}}\psi_{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}},

\displaystyle L_{PP^{\prime}}\equiv\widehat{\psi}^{A_{3}\cdots A_{m}}\zeta^{Q}{}_{P^{\prime}}\phi_{PQA_{3}\cdots A_{m}}.

One then has the following:

Lemma 3
.

One has that

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{A^{\prime}A}=J_{AA^{\prime}},\quad\bar{I}_{A^{\prime}A}=I_{AA^{\prime}}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad m\quad\mbox{even},

\displaystyle\bar{J}_{A^{\prime}A}=-J_{AA^{\prime}},\quad\bar{I}_{A^{\prime}A}=-I_{AA^{\prime}}\qquad\mbox{for}\quad m\quad\mbox{odd}.

4.3.2

The starting point for the construction of estimates is the identity in Lemma
2
written in the form

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(\phi,\psi)=\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}I^{QQ^{\prime}}-\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}J^{QQ^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{c}\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}K^{QQ^{\prime}}+\mathfrak{c}^{2}\nabla_{QQ^{\prime}}L^{QQ^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}W_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\phi}_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}+\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}X_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\psi}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathfrak{c}\psi^{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}Y_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\phi}_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}+\mathfrak{c}\psi^{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}Z_{P_{3}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\psi}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}.

Now, let

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}(\phi,\widehat{\phi})\equiv\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}W_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\phi}_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}},\quad\mathbf{X}(\phi,\widehat{\psi})\equiv\phi^{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}X_{P_{1}\cdots P_{m}}{}^{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}}\widehat{\psi}_{Q_{3}\cdots Q_{m}},

so we can write

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(\phi,\psi)=\nabla\cdot\mathbf{I}-\nabla\cdot\mathbf{J}+\mathfrak{c}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{K}+\mathfrak{c}^{2}\nabla\cdot\mathbf{L}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\mathbf{W}(\phi,\widehat{\phi})+\mathbf{X}(\phi,\widehat{\psi})+\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{Y}(\psi,\widehat{\phi})+\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{Z}(\psi,\widehat{\psi}).

(48)

Note that the original system can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}+\varpi^{2}G_{(A_{1}\cdots A_{m})}{}^{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}\varphi_{Q_{1}\cdots Q_{m}}=\varpi^{2}f_{(A_{1}\cdots A_{m})},\qquad

where we have defined

where we defined

, one integrates the above expression over a domain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\mathbf{G}(\phi,\psi)\mathrm{d}\mu_{{\bm{g}}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}-\mathfrak{c}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbf{W}(\phi,\widehat{\phi})+\mathbf{X}(\phi,\widehat{\psi})+\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{Y}(\psi,\widehat{\phi})+\mathfrak{c}\mathbf{Z}(\psi,\widehat{\psi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}-\mathfrak{c}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

Accordingly, in what follows it is assumed there exist constants

and further constants

(i.e. not necessarily positive) such that

\displaystyle\mathfrak{W}|\phi|^{2}\leq\mathbf{W}(\phi,\widehat{\phi}),\qquad\mathfrak{Z}|\psi|^{2}\leq\mathbf{Z}(\psi,\widehat{\psi}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}\Big(|\phi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\Big)\leq\mathbf{X}(\phi,\widehat{\psi}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Y}\Big(|\phi|^{2}+|\psi|^{2}\Big)\leq\mathbf{Y}(\psi,\widehat{\phi}).

It then follows that

\displaystyle\Big(\mathfrak{W}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}\Big)||\phi||^{2}+\mathfrak{c}\Big(\mathfrak{Z}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}\Big)||\psi||^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt+\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}-\mathfrak{c}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt\preccurlyeq\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\widehat{\phi}\cdot\mathpzc{f}+(-1)^{m}\phi\cdot\widehat{\mathpzc{f}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}-\mathfrak{c}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\widehat{\psi}\cdot\mathpzc{g}+(-1)^{m}\psi\cdot\widehat{\mathpzc{g}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}.

In order to further develop the above inequality, it is observed that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\widehat{\phi}\cdot\mathpzc{f}+(-1)^{m}\phi\cdot\widehat{\mathpzc{f}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}=2\mbox{Re}\left\langle\!\left\langle\phi,\mathpzc{f}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\widehat{\psi}\cdot\mathpzc{g}+(-1)^{m}\psi\cdot\widehat{\mathpzc{g}}\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}=2\mbox{Re}\,\left\langle\!\left\langle\psi,\mathpzc{g}\right\rangle\!\right\rangle,

so that, using Cauchy–Schwarz, Young’s inequality and moving terms, one obtains

\displaystyle\Big(\mathfrak{W}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}-1\Big)||\phi||^{2}+\mathfrak{c}\Big(\mathfrak{Z}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}-1\Big)||\psi||^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\preccurlyeq\mathfrak{c}\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}-\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}

\displaystyle\hskip 113.81102pt+||\mathpzc{f}||^{2}+||\mathpzc{g}||^{2}.

In order to proceed any further, one needs further assumptions on the form of the quadratic forms
\mathbfcal{H}
and
\mathbfcal{G}
. Recall that the spinors
\phi
and
\psi
are elements of the irreducible decomposition of the spinor
\varphi
. Thus, the estimation of the

\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi),\qquad\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi}),\qquad\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi),\qquad\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})

requires, in principle, knowledge/control of all the components of the irreducible decomposition of
\varphi
other than
\phi
and
\psi
. In the following we denote this set by
\mho(\varphi)
. The type of control depends on the structural properties of the particular equation under consideration.
For simplicity of presentation, in the following, it is assumed that there exist positive constants
\mathfrak{H}
,
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}
,
\mathfrak{H}^{\prime\prime}
,
\mathfrak{G}
,
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathfrak{G}^{\prime\prime}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{H}(\widehat{\psi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{H}}(\psi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}\leq\mathfrak{H}||\psi||^{2}+\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}||\phi||^{2}+\mathfrak{H}^{\prime\prime}||\varphi||^{2}_{\psi,\phi\notin\mho(\varphi)},

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathcal{U}}\Big(\mathbfcal{G}(\widehat{\phi},\varphi)+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbfcal{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\varphi})\Big)\mathrm{d}\mu_{\bm{g}}\leq\mathfrak{G}||\phi||^{2}+\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}||\psi||^{2}+\mathfrak{G}^{\prime\prime}||\varphi||^{2}_{\psi,\phi\notin\mho(\varphi)},

where

denotes the sum of the
L^{2}
-norms of all the irreducible components of
\varphi
excluding
\phi
and
\psi
.
Under this assumption, it follows that

\displaystyle\Big(\mathfrak{W}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}-\mathfrak{H}^{\prime}-\mathfrak{G}-1\Big)||\phi||^{2}+\mathfrak{c}\Big(\mathfrak{Z}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}-\mathfrak{H}-\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}-1\Big)||\psi||^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\preccurlyeq||\mathpzc{f}||^{2}+||\mathpzc{g}||^{2}+(\mathfrak{H}^{\prime\prime}+\mathfrak{G}^{\prime\prime})||\varphi||^{2}_{\psi,\phi\notin\mho(\varphi)}.

(50)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{Z}+\mathfrak{X}+\mathfrak{Y}-\mathfrak{H}-\mathfrak{G}^{\prime}>1,

then the inequality (

||\phi||
and
||\psi||
modulo knowledge about

||\mathpzc{f}||,\quad||\mathpzc{g}||,\quad||\varphi||_{\psi,\phi\notin\mho(\varphi)}.

We now show how the structures described in the previous subsection can be used to construct estimates for the solutions of equation (

. Using the above, we can write

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(\phi,\phi)=\mathcal{E}(\phi)\cdot\widehat{\phi}+(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathcal{E}(\phi)}\cdot\phi

\displaystyle\phantom{\mathbf{A}(\phi,\phi)}=\varpi^{2}\left(f\cdot\widehat{\phi}+(-1)^{m}\widehat{f}\cdot\phi-\mathbf{G}(\widehat{\phi},\phi)-(-1)^{m}\widehat{\mathbf{G}}(\phi,\widehat{\phi})\right),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\phi)\cdot\widehat{\phi}\equiv\mathcal{E}(\phi)_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}\widehat{\phi}^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}},

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{E}(\phi)}\cdot\phi\equiv\widehat{\mathcal{E}(\phi)}_{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}}\phi^{A_{1}\cdots A_{m}},

\left\langle\!\left\langle\mathbf{A}(\phi,\phi),1\right\rangle\!\right\rangle

of

6
Conclusions and outlook

In this article, we have developed a strategy to study the properties of solutions to a class of linear spinor equations. The model equation (
1
) subsumes a number of spinor equations arising in the abstract analysis of solutions to the Einstein equations, their stability and long terms existence —in particular, in the context of conformal methods; see
[
27
]
. Our strategy is motivated by the positive commutator method to construct estimates used in the study of the Einstein equations through the microlocal analysis approach of Melrose’s school of
Geometric Scattering
—see e.g.
[
21
,
16
]
. More precisely, in the present work, we have made use of the space spinor formalism to obtain a first order analogue of positive commutator approach for spinorial equations. The natural application of this new method is, in first instance, to construct estimates which allow to control the behaviour of massless spin-
s
fields (symmetric spinors) in a neighbourhood of spatial infinity. Ultimately, one could use a similar approach to study the same question for solutions of the conformal Einstein equations. In the latter case, the relevant spinor equation is the Bianchi equation for the rescaled Weyl curvature. Given the coupled nature of this subsystem, the use of the approach described in this article requires a bootstrap argument to facilitate a
conceptual linearisation
of the equations. These challenging problems will be addressed elsewhere.

Proof of Proposition

We provide a proof of Proposition
1
as the techniques of the proof are used several times in the article.

Proof.

As in the main text, assume that
\varphi_{ABC\cdots F}
is of valence
n
and use the symbol
\sim
to indicate that two spinors differ by a linear combination of the outer product of
\epsilon
-spinors and spinor of lower valence. The strategy in the proof is to show that

\varphi_{ABC\cdots EF}\sim\varphi_{(ABC\cdots EF)},

so that the statement of the proof follows then recursively. To this end it is then observed that

n\,\varphi_{(ABC\cdots EF)}=\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}+\varphi_{B(AC\cdots EF)}+\varphi_{C(AB\cdots EF)}+\cdots+\varphi_{F(AB\cdots E)},

(A.1)

and consider the difference between the first and the second term, the first and the third term and so on. One then has that

\displaystyle\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}-\varphi_{B(AC\cdots EF)}=-\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QC\cdots EF)}\epsilon_{AB},

\displaystyle\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}-\varphi_{C(AB\cdots EF)}=-\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QB\cdots EF)}\epsilon_{AC},

\displaystyle\hskip 56.9055pt\vdots

\displaystyle\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}-\varphi_{F(ABC\cdots E)}=-\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QBC\cdots E)}\epsilon_{AF}.

The above expressions can be used in equation (
A.1
) to eliminate the the terms

\varphi_{B(AC\cdots EF)},\qquad\varphi_{C(AB\cdots EF)},\cdots\qquad\varphi_{F(ABC\cdots E)},

so as to obtain

\varphi_{(ABC\cdots EF)}=\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}+\frac{1}{n}\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QC\cdots EF)}\epsilon_{AB}+\cdots+\frac{1}{n}\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QBC\cdots E)}\epsilon_{AF}.

Accordingly, one can write

\varphi_{(ABC\cdots EF)}\sim\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}.

The above procedure can applied, in turn, to each of

\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QC\cdots EF)},\cdots\varphi^{Q}{}_{(QBC\cdots E)},

so that

\varphi_{(ABC\cdots EF)}\sim\varphi_{A(BC\cdots EF)}\sim\varphi_{AB(C\cdots EF)}\sim\cdots\sim\varphi_{ABC\cdots(EF)}\sim\varphi_{ABC\cdots EF}.

Thus, the result follows.

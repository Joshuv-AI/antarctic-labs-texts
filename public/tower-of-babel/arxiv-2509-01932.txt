Abstract
We analyze the peculiarities of the interaction of the lambda hyperon with s-shell nuclei. The spectra of bound and scattering states are studied in the hypernuclei 2 Λ {}_{\Lambda}^{2} H, 3 Λ {}_{\Lambda}^{3} H, 4 Λ {}_{\Lambda}^{4} H and 5 Λ {}_{\Lambda}^{5} He, which is considered as two-cluster configurations p + Λ p+\Lambda , d d + Λ \Lambda , 3 H+ Λ \Lambda , 4 He+ Λ \Lambda , respectively. The explicit form of the folding potentials of such an interaction is presented in coordinate and oscillator representations, which help us to understand the structure of hypernuclei of interest. We compare energies of bound states, phase shifts of the elastic lambda hyperon, and neutron scattering from s-shell nuclei.

Introduction

interaction of ordinary nuclei with hypernuclei. Our knowledge of the properties of the hyperon-nucleon interactions is based on hypernuclear spectroscopy and hyperon-nucleon scattering data. On this basis, a set of
N\Lambda

An increasing amount of experimental data concerning the structure of hypernuclei also stimulates theoretical studies. 2
Two-cluster RGM method

Here, we present in a short form the main ideas of the resonating group method
which we employ to study two-cluster nuclei and hypernuclei. The wave function
describing the interaction of the lambda hyperon with an s-shell nucleus is sought in
the form

\Psi_{J}\left(A+\Lambda\right)=\left\{\left[\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\Phi_{2}\left(\Lambda\right)\right]_{S}\psi_{L}\left(\Lambda,\mathbf{x}\right)\right\}_{J},

(1)

and the wave function describing the interaction of a neutron with s-shell nuclei has
similar form

\Psi_{J}\left(A+n\right)=\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\left\{\left[\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\Phi_{2}\left(n\right)\right]_{S}\psi_{L}\left(n,\mathbf{x}\right)\right\}_{J},

(2)

where
\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)
is antisymmetric wave function
describing internal motion of
A
nucleons inside s-shell nuclei, functions

\Phi_{2}\left(\Lambda\right)
and
\Phi_{2}\left(n\right)
represents
the spin state of the lambda hyperon and the spin-isospin state of the nucleon,
respectively. The square brackets stand for the vector coupling of the spins of
lambda hyperon (neutron) and s-shell nucleus, this coupling creates the total
spin
S
. The curly brackets stand for the vector coupling of the total orbital
momentum
L
and total spin
S
into the total angular momentum
J
. One
notices that the main difference between Eqs. (
1
) and
(
2
) is the antisymmetrization operator
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}

which permutes the coordinates of valent neutron and coordinates of nucleons,
comprising a s-shell nucleus, and makes the wave function antisymmetric

\Psi_{J}\left(A+n\right)
of
A
+1 nucleons.

Within the present model, it is assumed that the wave function
\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)
is fixed and known, while the wave function

\psi_{L}\left(x\right)
of relative motion of lambda hyperon or neutron
and the s-shell nucleus has to be determined by solving the appropriate
Schrödinger equation. It is obvious that the wave functions (
1
) and (
2
) obeys (or more precisely, are approximate solutions to)
the different Schrödinger equations:

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{H}_{An}-E\right)\Psi_{J}\left(A+n\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(3)

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{H}_{AL}-E\right)\Psi_{J}\left(A+\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(4)

where Hamiltonians
\widehat{H}_{An}
and
\widehat{H}_{AL}
can be presented
as

\displaystyle\widehat{H}_{An}

\displaystyle=\widehat{H}_{A}+\widehat{T}_{x}+\sum_{i\in A}\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{n}\right),

(5)

\displaystyle\widehat{H}_{An}

\displaystyle=\widehat{H}_{A}+\widehat{T}_{x}+\sum_{i\in A}\widehat{V}_{N\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\Lambda}\right),

(6)

and they involve nucleon-nucleon
\widehat{V}_{NN}
and nucleon-hyperon

\widehat{V}_{N\Lambda}
potentials. Besides, both Hamiltonians contain the
Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}_{A}
describing the internal state of
A
-nucleon
nucleus. The expectation value of this Hamiltonian between wave functions

\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)
determine the internal energy or binding
energy of the nucleus

\mathcal{E}_{A}=\left\langle\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\left|\widehat{H}_{A}\right|\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\right\rangle.

As the wave functions
\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)
is fixed, then the
many-particle Schrödinger equations (
3
) and (
4
)
can be reduced to the effective two-body problems, as was shown by J. Wheeler
in Refs.
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
. To do this, one needs to multiply from the left Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
)
on wave functions
\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)
and to integrate over all
coordinates: spacial, spin and isospin. This procedure leads to a two-body
integro-differential Schrödinger equations with nonlocal cluster-cluster
potentials

\displaystyle\int d\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\left[\widehat{T}_{x}\delta\left(\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)+\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)-E\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right]\psi_{L}\left(n,\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(7)

\displaystyle\int d\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\left[\left(\widehat{T}_{x}-E\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)+\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right]\psi_{L}\left(\Lambda,\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

(8)

where
\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)
and

\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)
are
nonlocal potentials, and is the norm kernel. By introducing the projection
operators
\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)
and
\widehat{P}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\Phi_{2}\left(n\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{n}-\mathbf{x}\right),

(9)

\displaystyle\widehat{P}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{1}\left(A,S_{c}\right)\Phi_{2}\left(\Lambda\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{r}_{\Lambda}-\mathbf{x}\right),

(10)

which allows one to reduce the many-particle problem to a two-body problem, we can
express the nonlocal potentials
\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)
and
\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)
as

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\left\langle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\left|\sum_{i\in A}\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{n}\right)\right|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right\rangle

(11)

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\left\langle\widehat{P}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)\left|\sum_{i\in A}\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\Lambda}\right)\right|\widehat{P}_{\Lambda}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right\rangle

(12)

and the norm kernel as

\mathcal{N}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)=\left\langle\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)|\widehat{\mathcal{A}}\widehat{P}_{n}\left(\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)\right\rangle.

(13)

One immediately notices that the full antisymmetrization in the system of neutrons plus s-shell nuclei leads to appearance of the nonlocal norm kernel in
two-body Schrödinger equation (
7
). It is necessary to
underline that the nonlocality in Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) originates from the antisymmetric form of the wave function (
2
)
and from exchange operators in nucleon-nucleon and nucleon-hyperon potentials.
Both potentials (
11
) and (
12
) can be represented as a
sum of two components

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{n}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{n}^{\left(F\right)}\left(x\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)+\widehat{V}_{n}^{\left(r\right)}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right),

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}^{\left(F\right)}\left(x\right)\delta\left(\mathbf{x}-\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right)+\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}^{\left(r\right)}\left(\mathbf{x},\widetilde{\mathbf{x}}\right),

the first component is the so-called folding or direct potential
\widehat{V}^{\left(F\right)}
, this potential is local and represents the main
part of cluster-cluster interaction. The second, residual component

\widehat{V}^{\left(r\right)}
is totally nonlocal.

For numerical solutions of Eqs. (
7
) and (
8
) and for
analysis of the obtained results, we employ the full set of wave functions of the
three-dimensional harmonic oscillator. As they form a complete set of basis
functions, then one can use them to expand wave functions of relative motion
of clusters. Such a method employs the oscillator wave functions for
representing wave functions of bound and scattering states, is known as the algebraic version of the resonating group method, which was formulated in
Refs.
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
. By expanding the wave function

\psi_{L}\left(x\right)=\psi_{L}\left(n,x\right)
and
\psi_{L}\left(x\right)=\psi_{L}\left(\Lambda,x\right)
over oscillator
functions
\Phi_{nL}\left(x,b\right)
(explicit form of these functions
can be found, for example, in Ref.
[
14
]
)

\psi_{L}\left(x\right)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}C_{nL}\Phi_{nL}\left(x,b\right),

(14)

we transform the integro-differential equations (
7
) and
(
8
) into an infinite system of linear algebraic equations

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{T}_{x}\right|mL\right\rangle-E\left\langle nL|mL\right\rangle+\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{V}_{n}\right|mL\right\rangle\right]C_{mL}

\displaystyle=0,

(15)

\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}\left[\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{T}_{x}\right|mL\right\rangle-E\delta_{n,m}+\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\right|mL\right\rangle\right]C_{mL}

\displaystyle=0.

(16)

Here
\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{T}_{x}\right|mL\right\rangle
,

\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{V}_{n}\right|mL\right\rangle
,
\left\langle nL\left|\widehat{V}_{\Lambda}\right|mL\right\rangle
are matrix elements of corresponding operators, and
\left\langle nL|mL\right\rangle
are matrix elements of the norm kernel. The explicit form
of these matrix elements can be found, for example, in Refs.

[
15
]
,
[
16
]
.

We do not dwell on the problem of how to numerically solve these systems of equations
with the finite number of oscillator functions
N_{O}
to obtain convergent
results for bound and continuous spectrum states, as this problem has been thoroughly discussed in Refs.
[
8
]
,

[
9
]
,
[

]
,
[
17
]
.

Interaction of lambda hyperon with s-shell nuclei

In this section, we analyze properties of the
\lambda
-nucleon potentials of interaction of the lambda hyperon with the s-shell nuclei. The analysis of the
\lambda
-nucleus interaction is performed in coordinate and
oscillator representations.

3.1
Interaction of lambda hyperon with nucleon

We start with an analytical form of the
N\Lambda
and
NN
potentials that
will be used in the present paper. In the present work, the nucleon-nucleon
interaction is modelled by Hasegawa-Nagata potential (HNP)
[
18
,
19
]
, and the interaction of
lambda hyperon with nucleon is modelled by the so-called YNG-NF potential

[
20
]
. The central part of
NN
and
N\Lambda
potentials
can be presented in the following similar form

\displaystyle V_{N\Lambda}^{\left(C\right)}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\Lambda}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=E,O}\sum_{S=0,1}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{G}}V_{2S+1,p}^{\left(k,N\Lambda\right)}

(17)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{\Lambda}\right)^{2}}{\beta_{k}^{2}}\right\}\widehat{P}_{S}\widehat{P}_{p},

(18)

\displaystyle V_{NN}^{\left(C\right)}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{p=E,O}\sum_{S=0,1}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{G}}V_{2S+1,p}^{\left(k,NN\right)}

(19)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\exp\left\{-\frac{\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}\right)^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}\right\}\widehat{P}_{S}\widehat{P}_{p},

(20)

where
\widehat{P}_{S}
is the projection operator selecting spin
S
of
interaction particles, and the operator
\widehat{P}_{p}
selects positive
(
p=E
) or negative (
p=O
) parity state of the coordinate part of the two-particle
wave function. One can see that both
NN
and
N\Lambda
potentials are a
superposition of several (
N_{G}
) Gaussians. Moreover, each component of the
YNG potential is a function of the Fermi momentum
k_{F}

V_{2S+1,p}^{\left(N\Lambda\right)}\left(\mathbf{r}\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left[a_{i}+b_{i}k_{F}+c_{i}k_{F}^{2}\right]\exp\left\{-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{\beta_{k}^{2}}\right\},

(21)

which is employed as an adjustable parameter.

It is worthwhile noticing that the potential of interaction of a lambda hyperon
with a nucleus is nonlocal due to exchange terms in a
N\Lambda
potential.
The same is true for the interaction of a neutron with a nucleus, besides, in
this case, the antisymmetrization operator creates additional nonlocality. Therefore, we will analyze the local part of the
NN
and
N\Lambda

interactions, which are usually called the direct or folding interaction
(potential). For
NN
and
N\Lambda
potentials which have a Gaussian form, the
folding potential can be obtained in a simple analytic form.

3.2
Folding potentials

As it was pointed out above, the potential for interaction of the lambda hyperon with a nucleus or a neutron with the same nucleus is nonlocal. However, this potential
includes the folding potential, which is the main part of the lambada-nucleus and
neutron-nucleus potentials. The analysis of folding potential may explain
peculiarities of the interaction of the lambda hyperon with the nucleus. That is way in this section, we will calculate the lambda-nucleus and also neutron - nucleus
potentials. It is well known
[
21
]
, that folding
potential of interaction of two clusters, comprised of
A_{1}
and
A_{2}

nucleons, generated by a nucleon-nucleon potential
\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{i}-\mathbf{r}_{j}\right)
is

V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}d\mathbf{r}_{2}\rho_{1}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)\rho_{2}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{12}\right),

(22)

where
\rho_{1}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)
and
\rho_{2}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)
are the matter density distributions within the first
and the second clusters, respectively. As density distributions are determined in
the own system of center of mass, then vector
\mathbf{r}_{12}
equals

\mathbf{r}_{12}=\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}+\mathbf{x,}

(23)

where
\mathbf{x}
is a vector connecting the centers of mass of two clusters.
By using the Fourier transform for NN potential

\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{12}\right)=\int d\mathbf{k}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\exp\left\{i\left(\mathbf{kr}_{12}\right)\right\},

(24)

we express folding potential through form factors

V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=\int d\mathbf{k}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\exp\left\{i\left(\mathbf{kx}\right)\right\}F_{1}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)F_{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

(25)

where

\displaystyle F_{1}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}\rho_{1}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)\exp\left\{i\left(\mathbf{kr}_{1}\right)\right\},

(26)

\displaystyle F_{2}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\int d\mathbf{r}_{2}\rho_{2}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)\exp\left\{-i\left(\mathbf{kr}_{2}\right)\right\}.

(27)

One notices that, discussing folding potential calculations, we considered
a general case of a two-cluster system with arbitrary values of
A_{1}
and

A_{2}
. Now we have to fix them for the cases in our hands. In what follows, the first cluster is a s-shell nucleus
consisted of
A
nucleons, and the second cluster is a lambda hyperon for
hypernuclear systems or neutron for ordinary nuclear systems. For lambda
hyperon and neutron, which are structureless particles, the density
distribution
\rho_{2}\left(\mathbf{r}_{2}\right)
is the Dirac
delta-function and thus integration over coordinate
\mathbf{r}_{2}
leads to
simplified expression

\displaystyle V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{r}_{1}d\mathbf{r}_{2}\rho_{1}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}\right)\widehat{V}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{r}_{1}+\mathbf{x}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{k}\widehat{\mathcal{V}}_{NN}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)\exp\left\{i\left(\mathbf{kx}\right)\right\}F_{1}\left(\mathbf{k}\right),

It is easy to calculate mass or proton form factors for s-shell nuclei in the
shell model. It has the following form

F_{1}\left(\mathbf{k}\right)=\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}\frac{A-1}{A}b^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}\right\},

(29)

where
b
is oscillator length.

Now we consider the derivation of the coordinate part of the lambda-nucleus
folding potential, the spin part will be considered later. As it was
indicated above, the
N\Lambda
potential we have chosen consists of a Gaussian and it allows us to obtain a folding potential in a simple analytic form. To
demonstrate it, we need to calculate the Fourier transform of the Gaussian potential.
It can be verified that

V_{0}\exp\left\{-\frac{\mathbf{r}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right\}=V_{0}\frac{a^{3}}{\left(4\pi\right)^{3/2}}\int d\mathbf{k}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}-i\left(\mathbf{k,r}\right)\right\}

(30)

Thus, we need to calculate the integral

\displaystyle V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=V_{0}\frac{a^{3}}{\left(4\pi\right)^{3/2}}

(31)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\int d\mathbf{k}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{4}a^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}-\frac{A-1}{4A}b^{2}\mathbf{k}^{2}+i\left(\mathbf{kx}\right)\right\},

to obtain the folding potential. Integrating over vector
\mathbf{k}
, we obtain

V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=V_{0}\gamma^{-3/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\gamma}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{a^{2}}\right\},

(32)

where

\gamma=1+\frac{\left(A-1\right)}{A}\frac{b^{2}}{a^{2}}.

(33)

As we can see, similar to the
N\Lambda
potential, folding
A\Lambda
potential also has a Gaussian form, with the depth moderated by the factor
\gamma^{-3/2}
and radius increased by the factor
\sqrt{\gamma}
. Note that the folding potential of neutron interaction with s-shell nucleus has the same form (
32
). Obviously, the depth
V_{0}
and radius
a
of NN interaction are different from
N\Lambda
interaction.

By taking into account the spin components of
N\Lambda
interaction, we
arrive at an explicit expression of folding potential for the lambda hyperon
interaction with s-shell nuclei:

\displaystyle V_{N\Lambda}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{G}}\gamma_{k}^{-3/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}\right\}

(34)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cc}\frac{1}{2}\left(V_{3E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{3O}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&p+\Lambda,S=1,\\
\frac{1}{4}\left(V_{3E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{3O}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{1E}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{1O}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&d+\Lambda,S=\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{1}{4}\left(5V_{3E}^{\left(k\right)}+5V_{3O}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1O}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&t+\Lambda,S=1,\\
\frac{3}{4}\left(V_{3E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{3O}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1O}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&t+\Lambda,S=0,\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(3V_{3E}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{3O}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1E}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{1O}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&\alpha+\Lambda,S=\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

Similar expression for the folding potentials of the interaction of a neutron with
s-shell nuclei:

\displaystyle V_{NN}^{\left(F\right)}\left(\mathbf{x}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{G}}\gamma_{k}^{-3/2}\exp\left\{-\frac{1}{\gamma_{k}}\frac{\mathbf{x}^{2}}{a_{k}^{2}}\right\}

(41)

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[c]{cc}\frac{1}{2}\left(V_{33}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{31}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&p+n,S=1,\\
\frac{1}{8}\left(3V_{33}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{31}^{\left(k\right)}+9V_{13}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{11}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&d+n,S=\frac{1}{2},\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(2V_{33}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{31}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{13}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&t+n,S=1,\\
\frac{1}{2}\left(3V_{33}^{\left(k\right)}+2V_{13}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{11}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&t+n,S=0,\\
\frac{1}{4}\left(9V_{33}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{31}^{\left(k\right)}+3V_{13}^{\left(k\right)}+V_{11}^{\left(k\right)}\right)&\alpha+n,S=\frac{1}{2}.\end{array}\right.

5
Conclusions

We have studied the peculiarities of the interaction of the lambda hyperon with the s-shell nuclei and compared them with the interaction of the neutron with the same nuclei. These studies are carried out with the algebraic version of the resonating group method, which involves a full set of harmonic oscillator functions to expand
wave functions of the relative motion of interacting clusters. It was demonstrated
that the present model fairly good reproduces the spectrum of bound states in hypernuclei of interest.

It was also demonstrated that there are no resonance states in the two-cluster
continuum formed by the interaction of the lambda hyperon with the s-shell nuclei. It
means that nucleon-lambda interaction is weak and consequently, s-nucleus-lambda interaction
is also weak to create a favorable situation for forming a resonance state.

We concluded that the interaction of the lambda hyperon with the s-shell nuclei is weaker than the interaction of neutron with the same nuclei, however, the lack of the Pauli principle in the system, comprised of lambda hyperon and nucleons, leads to formation of bound states in
{}_{\Lambda}^{4}
H,
{}_{\Lambda}^{5}
He, where their counterparts have no bound states.

We compared our results, obtained from a two-cluster model, with the results of other models and demonstrated the consistency of our model with the three- and four-cluster models.

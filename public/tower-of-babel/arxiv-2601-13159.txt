On the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem in the plane
Tom Baumbach

Abstract
. The paper characterizes the convex hull of the closure of the cone-volume set C 𝚌𝚟 ​ ( U ) C_{\tt cv}(U) , consisting of all cone-volume vectors of polygons with outer unit normals vectors contained in U U , for any finite set U ⊆ ℝ 2 , pos ( U ) = ℝ 2 U\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{2},\pos(U)=\mathbb{R}^{2} . We prove that this convex hull has finitely many extreme points by providing both a vertex representation as well as a half space representation. As a consequence, we derive new necessary conditions, which depend on U U , for the existence of solutions to the logarithmic Minkowski problem in ℝ 2 \mathbb{R}^{2} .



1.
Introduction

The setting for this paper is the
n
-dimensional Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For two vectors
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
\langle x,y\rangle
the standard scalar product of
x
and
y
, and
\|x\|=\sqrt{\langle x,x\rangle}
denotes the associated Euclidean norm;
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\|x\|=1\}
is the
(n-1)
-sphere. The convex hull of a non-empty set
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
\conv M
, and if
M
is finite then
\conv M
is called a polytope; expressing a polytope as
P=\conv(M)
is referred to as its
\mathcal{V}
-representation. By a result attributed to Minkowski and Weyl,
P\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a polytope if and only if

P=P(U,b)=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:U^{\intercal}x\leq b\}=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}H^{-}(u_{i},b_{i})

for a matrix
U=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{m}
with
\pos(U)=\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
. This is called the
\mathcal{H}
-representation of the polytope.
Here
\pos(U)
means the positive hull, i.e., the set of all non-negative linear combinations of the column vectors
u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
of
U
. Apparently, we may assume that the column vectors are pairwise different, and therefore we set

\mathcal{U}(n,m)=\left\{U=(u_{1},\dots,u_{m})\in(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})^{m}:\pos(U)=\mathbb{R}^{n},u_{i}\neq u_{j},i\neq j\right\}.

For
1\leq i\leq m
let

F_{i}(b)=F(u_{i},b)=P\cap\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\langle u_{i},x\rangle=b_{i}\}

which is always a face of
P
and might be empty. If
\dim F_{i}(b)=\dim P-1
,
F_{i}(b)
is called a facet of
P
. For
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
we denote by
\mathrm{vol}\,(M)
its volume, i.e., its
n
-dimensional Lebesgue measure. If
M
is contained in a

k
-dimensional plane
A
,
\mathrm{vol}\,_{k}(M)
refers to the
k
-dimensional Lebesgue measure with respect to
A
.

We will mainly assume that
b\geq 0
. This implies
0\in P
, and if
b>0
then
0\in\inter P
, i.e.,
0
is an interior point of
P
, and so
\dim P=n
.
If
F_{i}(b)
is a facet of
P(U,b)
,
then
\frac{1}{n}b_{i}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{i}(b))
is the volume of the cone (pyramid)

\conv(\{0\}\cup F_{i}(b))
. As for
U\in\mathcal{U}(n,m)
,
P(U,b)
is the interior-disjoint union of all these cones we can write

\mathrm{vol}\,(P(U,b))=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{m}b_{i}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{i}(b)).

The cone-volume vector
\gamma(U,b)
of
P(U,b)
has as entries the volumes of the cones
\conv(\{0\}\cup F_{i})

\gamma(U,b)=\left(\frac{1}{n}b_{1}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{1}(b)),\dots,\frac{1}{n}b_{m}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{m}(b))\right),

and the cone-volume set is
C_{{\tt cv}}(U)=\{\gamma(U,b):b\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{m},\mathrm{vol}\,_{n}(P(U,b))=1\}
, cf.
[
3
]
.

For such a
P=P(U,b)
,
\dim P=n
, we consider its cone-volume measure
\V_{P}
which is
the finite non-negative Borel measure
\V_{P}:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, where
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})
contains all measurable subsets of
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
given by

\V_{P}(\eta)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{b_{i}}{n}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{i}(b))\,\delta_{u_{i}}(\eta)=\sum_{u_{i}\in\eta}\frac{b_{i}}{n}\mathrm{vol}\,_{n-1}(F_{i}(b)).

Here
\eta\subseteq\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
is a Borel set and
\delta_{u_{i}}(\cdot)
is the Dirac measure in
u_{i}
, i.e.,
\delta_{u_{i}}(\eta)=1
if
u_{i}\in\eta
, otherwise it is
0
.

The discrete logarithmic Minkowski (existence) problem introduced by Böröczky, Lutwak, Yang and Zhang
[
9
]
asks for necessary and sufficient conditions such that a finite discrete Borel measure

(1.1)

\mu:\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0},\quad\mu(\eta)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\gamma_{i}\,\delta_{u_{i}}(\eta)

with
u_{i}\in\mathbb{S}^{n-1}
,
\gamma_{i}>0
, is the cone-volume measure of a polytope. We will denote such a measure also by
\mu(U,\gamma)
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{>0}
is the vector with entries
\gamma_{i}
.

This discrete problem can be extended to the continuous setting, i.e., to the space of all convex bodies and
the corresponding general logarithmic Minkowski problem is a corner stone of modern convex geometry. For its history, relevance and impact we refer to
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

,

]
and to the references within. Here we will only focus on the discrete setting.

In order to study the discrete logarithmic Minkowski problem in the plane, we consider the cone-volume set
C_{\tt cv}(U)
, for
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
.

The existence of solutions for the discrete planar logarithmic Minkowski problem has been investigated in special situations by Stancu
[

]

and analyzed the number of solutions

introduced a new necessary condition in the planar case for centered polygons and examined the solutions for quadrilaterals.

For the even logarithmic Minkowski problem, we consider sets
U=U^{s}\in\mathcal{U}(n,2m)

for
i=1,\dots,m
, and restrict attention to origin symmetric polytopes

Stancu
[
20
]
provided a complete characterization of the symmetric cone-volume set in the plane. In the following,
\relint(M)
denotes the relative interior of
M\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, i.e., the set of interior points with respect to the ambient space given by

Theorem I
.

[
20
, Theorem 1.2.]

Let
U^{s}\in\mathcal{U}(2,2m)
and

. Then it holds

C^{s}_{\tt cv}(U^{s})\cap\mathbb{R}^{2m}_{>0}=\relint\left(P_{\tt scc}(U^{s})\right)\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}:x_{i}=x_{m+i},\,1\leq i\leq m\right\}.

In the preliminaries, we will recall the definition of the polytope
P_{\tt scc}(U)
, for
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
, introduced in
[
3
]
, which is called the subspace concentration polytope (of
U
). Up to scaling, the polytope

is (just) the matroid base polytope of the set of column vectors of
U
.

Theorem
I
was later generalized in the groundbreaking paper
[
9
]
. It was shown that an even finite positive Borel measure
\mu(U,\gamma)
is the cone-volume measure of an origin symmetric polytope
P(U,b)
if and only if
\mu(U,\gamma)
satisfies the subspace concentration condition (
scc
), introduced by Böröczky et al.
[
9
]
. A finite discrete Borel measure
\mu=\mu(U,\gamma):\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
U\in\mathcal{U}(n,m)
,
\gamma>0
, is said to satisfy the
scc
if

i)

for every proper linear subspace
L\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
it holds

(1.2)

\mu(L)=\sum_{u_{i}\in L}\gamma_{i}\leq\frac{\dim L}{n}\mu(\mathbb{S}^{n-1}),

ii)

equality holds in (
1.2
) if and only if there exists a subspace
\overline{L}
complementary to
L
such that
\{u_{1},\dots,u_{m}\}\subset L\cup\overline{L}
.

This characterization can be reformulated using the cone-volume set (see also
[
17
, Thm. 4.7]
). For
U^{s}\in\mathcal{U}(n,2m)
, we have

C^{s}_{\tt cv}(U^{s})\cap\mathbb{R}^{2m}_{>0}=\relint\left(P_{\tt scc}(U^{s})\right)\cap\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{2m}:x_{i}=x_{m+i},\,1\leq i\leq m\right\}.

In the non-symmetric case the following equality holds. Consider a set
U\in\mathcal{U}(n,m)
. Then
C_{\tt cv}(U)=P_{\tt scc}(U)
if and only if
m=2n
and up to renumbering we have
u_{n+i}=-u_{i}
,
1\leq i\leq n
(
[
3
, Thm. 2.11]
).

For non-symmetric discrete measures in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Stancu
[
20
]
provided some sufficient conditions for the existence.

Theorem II
.

[
20
, Theorem 1.1.]

Let

, and

By definition, for

By a result of Chen et al.

2.
Preliminaries

This paper builds upon the work presented in
[
3
]
. For the sake of completeness, we revisit its preliminaries, focusing only on the planer case.
In this section, we recall the basic properties of the subspace concentration polytope as well as the cone-volume set.

Additionally, we restrict our attention to finite sets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
\mathbb{S}^{1}
, ensuring a well-defined combinatorial and geometric context for all discussions and results.

For a set
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
we define the subspace concentration set, as in
[
3
]
.
With
S\subseteq U
, we refer to a subtuple of columns in
U
and by
\mathrm{rk}(S)
we denote the dimension of the linear span of
S
with respect to the columns.

There are two important sets regarding the matroid polytope, corresponding to the two different cases in
{\tt scc}
. Both of these sets consist of subsets of
U
:

i)

\mathcal{L}(U)\coloneqq\Bigl\{S\subseteq U:\mathrm{rk}(S)=1\text{ and }U\cap\lin(S)=S\Bigl\}

ii)

\mathcal{F}(U)\coloneqq\Bigl\{S\in\mathcal{L}(U):\lin(S)\cap\lin(U\setminus S)=\{0\}\Bigl\}
.

Since we only work with sets
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
, we have the following characterization.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
. Then
\mathcal{L}(U)\neq\varnothing
if and only if
U=\{\pm u,\pm v\}
for some
u,v\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Proof.

First, it is clear that
\{\pm v\}\in\mathcal{L}(U)
if
U=\{\pm u\}\cup\{\pm v\}
.

For the other direction, consider a set
U
such that
S\in\mathcal{L}(U)
. Then there exists vectors
u,v\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
such that
S\subseteq\{\pm u\}
and
U\setminus S\subseteq\{\pm v\}
. Since it holds

\mathbb{R}^{2}=\pos(U),

we get that
U=\{\pm u,\pm v\}
.
∎

The subspace concentration polytope is defined as the set

(2.1)

\displaystyle P_{\tt scc}(U)\coloneqq\,

\displaystyle\Bigl\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{m}:x_{i}\geq 0,\sum_{i=1}^{m}x_{i}=1,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{i:u_{i}\in\lin S}x_{i}\leq\frac{rk(S)}{n},\text{ for all }S\in\mathcal{L}(U)\Bigl\},

which has a close connection with the subspace concentration condition and the cone-volume set, cf.
[
9
,
5
,
23
,
11
]
.
The subspace concentration polytope
P_{\tt scc}(U)
is a special case of matroid polyhedra
[
10
]
, where the matroid consists of
U
and
\mathcal{I}
, the set that contains all linear independent subsets of
U
.

The set
P_{\tt scc}(U)
is a polytope, meaning
P_{\tt scc}(U)
can be expressed with half spaces, directly coming from its definition, or vertices. The
\mathcal{V}-
representation of
P_{\tt scc}(U)
was already established in the papers
[
12
,
10
]
, which we are going to recall

(2.3)

P_{\tt scc}(U)=\conv\left\{\frac{1}{2}\sum_{u_{i}\in B}e_{i}:B\subseteq U\text{ is a linear basis of }\mathbb{R}^{2}\right\}.

Further, the dimension of the matroid polytope was computed and proven in
[
13
]
.

Next, we consider the cone-volume set in the plane. For
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
and
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}
the cone-volume vector is

\gamma(U,b)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(b_{1}\mathrm{vol}\,_{1}(F(u_{1})),\dots,b_{m}\mathrm{vol}\,_{1}(F(u_{m}))\Big)^{\intercal}\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}.

Observe that some of its entries might be zero, if
F_{i}(b)
is not an edge of the polygon
P(U,b)
or
b_{i}=0
. The cone-volume set is

C_{\tt cv}(U)=\left\{\gamma(U,b):b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}_{\geq 0}\text{ and }\mathrm{vol}\,_{2}(P(U,b))=1\right\}.

Any cone-volume vector
\gamma(U,b)
of a
2
-dimensional polygon of the type
P(U,b)
is up to scaling to volume 1 an element of
C_{\tt cv}(U)
as

(2.4)

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}\,_{2}(P(U,b))}\gamma(U,b)=\gamma\left(U,\left(\mathrm{vol}\,_{2}(P(U,b))\right)^{-1/2}b\right)\in C_{\tt cv}(U).

The subspace concentration polytope
P_{\tt scc}(U)
as well as the cone-volume set
C_{\tt cv}(U)
can be decomposed by considering so called
separators
of
U
, which are special elements of
\mathcal{F}(U)
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
. We call the set
U
irreducible if
\mathcal{F}(U)=\varnothing
, otherwise reducible.

Furthermore, for a separator
S\in\mathcal{F}(U)
we must have
S=\lin(S)\cap U
, which is also known as
S
is closed in matroid language.
Observe that the number of separators of a given set
U
is finite, and thus the number of irreducible separators is finite as well. Using the separators, we obtain a much simpler representation of the subspace concentration polytope
P_{\tt scc}(U)
.

From matroid theory, we deduce the following statement
[
1
, Theorem 6.81]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

[
3
, Proposition 2.3 + 2.7]

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
be reducible. Then, up to renumbering, it holds

P_{\tt scc}(U)=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{4}:x_{1}+x_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\right\}\,\cup\,\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{4}:x_{3}+x_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\right\},

as well as

C_{\tt cv}(U)=\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{4}:\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\right\}\,\cup\,\left\{\gamma\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}^{4}:\gamma_{3}+\gamma_{4}=\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

Therefore, the reducible sets are of a very special form, which we use in Section
3
.

Finally, we remark that
P_{\tt scc}(U)
and
C_{\tt cv}(U)
are in particular
linear invariant which will be used later on.

Lemma 2.4
.

[
3
, Proposition 2.1]

Let
U
as above and let
A\in\mathrm{GL}(2,\mathbb{R})
. Then

P_{\tt scc}(AU)=P_{\tt scc}(U)
and
C_{\tt cv}(U)=C_{\tt cv}(AU)
.

Planar Case

In this section we focus on the planar case, i.e. we consider the case

. The convex body

is shown to be in fact a polytope and a complete

First, we will look at trapezoids, where the cone-volume set was completely characterized in
[

II

Theorem III
.

Let
\mu
be a non-zero, finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
supported on pairwise distinct and counterclockwise ordered unit vectors
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
.
Further, we assume that
u_{1}=-u_{3}
and there is an open hemisphere

such that
u_{2},u_{4}\not\in\omega
and
u_{2}\not=-u_{4}
.

The measure
\mu
is a cone-volume measure, i.e. there exists a polygon
P
with outer normal set contained in
U=\{u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}\}
such that
V_{P}=\mu
, if and only if either

(i)

\mu(u_{1})+\mu(u_{3})<\mu(u_{2})+\mu(u_{4})
, or

(ii)

\mu(u_{1})+\mu(u_{3})\geq\mu(u_{2})+\mu(u_{4})\geq 2\sqrt{\mu(u_{1})\mu(u_{3})}
and
\mu(u_{1})<\mu(u_{3})
.

We will examine the situation of parallelograms and trapezoids in great detail in order to be able to provide
\mathcal{V}
-representations and
\mathcal{H}
-representations in the planar case for the convex hull
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
. This allows us to get a better understanding of the cone-volume sets of triangles, trapezoids, and parallelograms, which is essential in order to understand the extreme points of the convex hull of general cone-volume sets.

First, we consider the situation of Theorem
III
and want to find a
\mathcal{V}
- and
\mathcal{H}

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
. Therefore, consider an index
i
such that
u_{i}\in U_{\square}
. Let
j
be an index such that
u_{j}\in U_{\square,u_{i}}
. Corollary
3.11
implies that
\gamma(U,b)_{u_{i}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
for any cone-volume vector
\gamma(U,b)\in\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
. Suppose we have two elements
v,w\in\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
such that

(3.56)

\frac{v+w}{2}=\frac{1}{2}(e_{i}+e_{j}).

Hence, it must hold
v_{u_{i}}=w_{u_{i}}=1/2
, Corollary
3.11
. Since, in addition,
|v|_{1}=|w|_{1}=1
, it must hold
v_{u_{j}}=w_{u_{j}}=1/2
and we are done.
∎

It is remarkable that the extreme points of the set
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
correspond to either triangles, the elements contained in
U_{\triangle}
, or the vertices of
P_{\tt scc}(U)
. This behaviour is no longer true for general dimensions.

Example 3.16
.

Consider the set

U=\{u_{1},\dots,u_{n+2}\}=\left\{-e_{1},e_{1},e_{2},\dots,e_{n},-\sqrt{n}\cdot(e_{1}+\dots+e_{n})\right\}\in\mathcal{U}(n,n+2).

Since
U
contains the simplex
V=\{e_{1},\dots,e_{n},-\sqrt{n}\cdot(e_{1}+\dots+e_{n})\}
, we have
e_{2},\dots,e_{n+2}\in\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
.
Consider a polytope
P=P(U,b),b\geq 0
and assume that
-e_{1}
defines a facet of
P
, say
F_{1}(b)
. The set
\conv(F_{1}(b),F_{1}(b)+b_{1}e_{1})
is a subset of
P
and hence we get the following inequality for the cone-volume vectors
\gamma\in C_{{\tt cv}}(U)
,

n\cdot\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}\leq 1.

Using this inequality we get the following
\mathcal{V}
-representation of
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\conv\Bigg\{e_{2},\dots,e_{n+2},\left(\frac{1}{n},\frac{n-1}{n},0,\dots,0\right),

\displaystyle\dots,\left(\frac{1}{n},0,\dots,0,\frac{n-1}{n}\right)\Bigg\}.

Notice that no vertex in this
\mathcal{V}
-representation is a vertex of
P_{\tt scc}(U)
and not every vertex corresponds to a simplex either.

We now present an alternative proof of the fact that the cone-volume set coincides with the subspace concentration polytope if and only if the set
U
defines a parallelogram (cf.
[
3
, Thm 2.11]
), using the
\mathcal{V}-
representation of
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
.

Corollary 3.17
.

Let
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
. It holds
P_{\texttt{scc}}(U)=C_{{\tt cv}}(U)
if and only if
U=U_{\square}
, i.e.
U
defines a parallelogram.

Proof.

If
U
defines a parallelogram, we already know
P_{\texttt{scc}}(U)=C_{{\tt cv}}(U)
.

Assume now, that
P_{\texttt{scc}}(U)=C_{{\tt cv}}(U)
. From Theorem
1.2
, we know that

(3.57)

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))=\conv\left\{\bigcup_{i:u_{i}\in U_{\Delta}}\{e_{i}\}\cup\bigcup_{(i,j):u_{i}\in U_{\square},u_{j}\in U_{\square,u_{i}}}\left\{\frac{1}{2}(e_{i}+e_{j})\right\}\right\}.

The set
U_{\Delta}
must be empty, since we assume that the cone-volume set equals the subspace concentration polytope and every entry of an element contained in
P_{\texttt{scc}}(U)
is less or equal than
\frac{1}{2}
. Hence, it holds
U=U_{\square}
and thus
\pos(U_{\square})=\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Therefore,
U_{\square}
contains at least
n+1=3
elements,
[
19
, Thoerem 4]
, and from Remark
3.6
if follows
|U_{\square}|=4
. Hence, the set
U
defines a parallelogram.
∎

The vertex representation of the closure of the cone-volume set
C_{\tt cv}(U)
implies a similar version of Theorem
II
i), at least for the convex hull of the cone-volume set.

Corollary 3.18
.

For a set
U\in\mathcal{U}(2,m)
is holds

\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))=\conv\{e_{1},\dots,e_{m}\}

if and only if
U=U_{\Delta}
.

Proof.

That is an immediate consequence of Theorem
1.2
.
∎

Coroallary
3.18
implies a weaker version of Theorem
II
i). Consider a set

that is in general position. It is easy to check that this implies

and thus the set
\mathrm{cl}(\mathrm{conv}({C_{{\tt cv}}(U)}))
is equal to
\conv(e_{1},\dots,e_{m})
.

We want to note that Corollary
3.18
can be generalized to arbitrary dimensions by generalizing the definition
U_{\Delta}
to any dimension, which will be done in a different paper. However, the generalization of Theorem
1.2
to any dimensions seems non-trivial, since it is not clear what sets we need to consider in comparison to

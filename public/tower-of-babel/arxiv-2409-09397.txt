Trees and near-linear stable sets
Tung Nguyen, Alex Scott, Paul Seymour

Abstract
When H H is a forest, the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture implies that every graph G G with no induced subgraph isomorphic to H H and with bounded clique number has a stable set of linear size. We cannot prove that, but we prove that every such graph G G has a stable set of size | G | 1 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) |G|^{1-o(1)} . If H H is not a forest, there need not be such a stable set. Second, we prove that when H H is a “multibroom”, there is a stable set of linear size. As a consequence, we deduce that all multibrooms satisfy a “fractional colouring” version of the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture. Finally, we discuss extensions of our results to the multicolour setting.



1
Introduction

Graphs in this paper are finite, and have no loops or parallel edges. For a graph
G
,
|G|
denotes the number of vertices of
G
,

\chi(G)
is its chromatic number, and
\omega(G)
and
\alpha(G)
denote the sizes of its largest clique and stable set
respectively. If
G,H
are graphs,
G
is
H
-free
if no induced subgraph of
G
is isomorphic to
H
, and if
\mathcal{H}

is a set of graphs,
G
is
\mathcal{H}
-free
if
G
is
H
-free for each
H\in\mathcal{H}
.

We are interested in how large
\alpha(G)
must be, when
\omega(G)
is bounded.
For each integer
k\geq 1
, every
K_{k+1}
-free graph
G
satisfies
\alpha(G)\geq\Omega(|G|^{1/k})
,
and there are
K_{k+1}
-free graphs
G
such that
\alpha(G)\leq|G|^{2/(k+2)}\log|G|
(Spencer
[
16
]
), so at least
we have a rough idea of the right answer.
But what happens if we fix a graph
H
and ask the same question for
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graphs
G
?
If
H
is a forest this question is particularly interesting, and is the focus of this paper.

The Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture (
[
5
,
18
]
; see also
[
6
]
) says:

1.1

For every forest
H
and every integer
k\geq 1
, there exists
c\geq 1
such that
\chi(G)\leq c
for every
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
.

In particular,
if the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture is true, then every
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G

has a stable set of size at least
|G|/c
.
This is dramatically different from what happens when only
K_{k+1}
is excluded,
and for such graphs
G
we ought at least to be able to prove a lower bound on
\alpha(G)
better than
|G|^{1/k}
. Until now that
had not been done,
but we will prove:

1.2

For every forest
H
and every integer
k\geq 1
, every
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
satisfies
\alpha(G)\geq|G|^{1-o(1)}
,
and hence has chromatic number at most
O(|G|^{o(1)})
.

(The second claim follows from the first by recursive deleting maximum stable sets.)

An appealing feature of the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture is that it is sharp, in the sense that if

H
is not a forest, and
k\geq 2
, then there does not exist
c
such that

\chi(G)\leq c
for every
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
. This is a consequence of a result of Erdős
[
4
]
, who showed that
for all
g\geq 1
there exists

\varepsilon>0
and arbitrarily large graphs
G
of girth at least
g
in which every stable set has size at most
|G|^{1-\varepsilon}
.
Thus such graphs
G
will be
H
-free if
H
has a cycle of length less than
g
, and will have chromatic number more than
c
when
|G|^{\varepsilon}>c
.
The same example shows that
1.2
is sharp in the same sense: if
H
is not a forest, and
k\geq 2
,
then not every
\{H,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
satisfies
\alpha(G)\geq|G|^{1-o(1)}
.

To prove
1.2
, we could assume that
H
is a tree, and hence has a radius. (The
radius
of a connected graph is the
minimum over
u\in V(G)
of the maximum over
v\in V(G)
of the distance between
u,v
.) Here is a more exact version of
1.2

(logarithms in this paper are to base two):

1.3

Let
k,r\geq 2
be integers, let
q:=(r-1)(k-1)
, and let
T
be a tree of radius at most

. Then there exists

b>0
such that every
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
satisfies

\alpha(G)\geq|G|^{1-b(\log|G|)^{-\frac{1}{q}}}.

(This bound is better than
\Omega(|G|^{1-\varepsilon})
, for any fixed
\varepsilon>0
, but not as good as

\Omega(|G|/\operatorname{polylog}(|G|)
.)

The same proof yields a stronger result, that implies
1.3
by setting
d=|G|
:

1.4

Let
k,r\geq 2
be integers, let
q:=(r-1)(k-1)
, and let
T
be a tree of radius at most
r
.
Then there exists
b>0
such that for
every
d\geq 2
,
every
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph
G
with
maximum degree at most
d
satisfies

\alpha(G)\geq 2^{-b(\log d)^{1-\frac{1}{q}}}|G|.

For some trees

, we can do better than this: we can prove that all
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free graphs have stable sets of linear size
(as they should if the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture is true), and that
leads to the second main result of the paper. Let us explain.
A

is a tree obtained from a path

(where

) by adding some number of new vertices,

. If

(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})
-multibroom
is a graph obtained from brooms of lengths
\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n}
by identifying their roots, and a

(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{n})
-multibroom for some choice of

It is not known whether all multibrooms satisfy the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture; indeed, the only multibrooms
currently known to do so are:

•

(1,\ldots,1,2,\ldots,2)
-multibrooms (due to Scott and Seymour

,

]
, Kierstead and Penrice
[

]
, and Kierstead and Zhu
[

]
);

•

(\ell_{1},\ell_{2})
-multibrooms for all

, and more generally, multibrooms made by identifying the roots of several

(by Chudnovsky, Scott and Seymour
[
1
]
; Scott
[
12
]
had previously shown the result when all brooms are paths).

Incidentally, this is almost the complete list of trees known to satisfy the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture

other trees known to do so are those obtained by subdividing a star and adding one leaf,
attached arbitrarily

, and those obtained from two disjoint paths by adding an edge between them

.

. It is known

The second main result of this paper says that all multibrooms satisfy a “fractional” version of
1.1
.
If
a,b\geq 1
are integers, an
(a,b)
-fractional colouring
of

is a family
(A_{i}:1\leq i\leq a)
of stable sets of

such that

|\{i\in\{1,\ldots,a\}:v\in A_{i}\}|\geq b
for each vertex

. The
fractional chromatic number

of

is the minimum of

over all pairs

such that

admits an

-fractional colouring.
We will prove:

1.5

For every multibroom

, and every integer

, there exists

such that
\chi^{*}(G)\leq c
for every
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph

.

As far as we know, this is the only case when the fractional version of the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture has been proved and the full conjecture has not.
We will deduce this result via linear programming duality from a weighted version of the following, which strengthens

for multibrooms to give a linear bound:

1.6

For every multibroom

, and every integer

, there exists

such that

for every
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free graph

.

In the final section, we discuss extensions of our results to the multicolour setting.

2
Excluding a general forest

This section contains the proof of
1.4
. We follow a strategy of iterative sparsification, that we also used in several recent papers on the Erdős-Hajnal conjecture, for instance
[
9
]
. Starting with a
T
-free graph
G
, we look for an induced subgraph
G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
G^{\prime}
is significantly sparser than
G
, but not too much smaller; provided we can control the trade-off between size and density, we can then iterate the process until we end up with a large stable set.

To make this strategy work, we need to be able to carry out the sparsification step without sacrificing too much in terms of size. We
construct the sparse subgraph
G^{\prime}
in small steps: we repeatedly find a set
A
of vertices that is sparse to most of the graph, and then take a union of many such sets. The key part of the argument is to find a suitable set
A
. We do this in
2.4
by embedding
T
into
G
one vertex at a time: as
G
is
T
-free, this process must get stuck at some point, and we will use this to find the set
A
.

Before starting the argument, we make a few comments:

•

The set
A
that we obtain must satisfy a suitable sparsity condition: in fact, we get two sets
A
and
B
so that the
vertices of
A
have relatively few neighbours outside
A\cup B
. We then repeat the argument on
G\setminus(A\cup B)
, and
continuing in this way, we obtain a sequence of disjoint sets
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
such that the
A_{i}
are sparse to later sets in the
sequence. Provided the
A_{i}
are not too small and the
B_{i}
are not too large, we obtain the vertices of
G^{\prime}
by taking a union of (some of) the sets
A_{i}
.

•

We may get stuck at different stages when attempting to embed
T
. The size and sparsity of the sets
A_{i}
and
B_{i}
that we obtain depend on where we get stuck. So, in fact, we end up creating sets of several different types: one of these types will occur often enough so that we can obtain
G^{\prime}
by taking all sets
A_{i}
of this type.

•

As we grow the tree, the sets
A_{i}
get smaller. By embedding the vertices in a careful order, we can ensure that our bounds depend primarily on the
radius
of the tree
T
, and only weakly on the number of vertices of
T
. For this reason, and for
convenience,
we embed our tree in a depth-first search order.

We will need the following well-known version of Ramsey’s theorem (see
[
15
]
for example):

2.1

For
k\geq 1
an integer, if a graph
G
has no stable subset of size
k
, then

|V(G)|\leq\omega(G)^{k-1}+\omega(G)^{k-2}+\cdots+\omega(G).

Consequently
|V(G)|<\omega(G)^{k}
if
\omega(G)>1
.

Let us recall the definition of a depth-first enumeration of a tree.
Let
T
be a tree with vertex set
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t}\}
. The numbering
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t})
is a

dfs-enumeration for
T
rooted at
\sigma_{1}
if, for each
i\in\{1,\ldots,t-1\}
,
\sigma_{i+1}
has a neighbour
in the path of
T
between
\sigma_{1},\sigma_{i}
.
By growing a depth-first tree in
T
, it follows that every tree
T
admits a dfs-enumeration rooted at any vertex
\rho\in V(T)
.

Dfs-enumerations have useful properties that will be helpful in the proof of
2.2
.
If

(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t})
is a dfs-enumeration for
T
, then for
1\leq i\leq t
the subgraph
T_{i}
of
T

induced on
\{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i}\}
is a tree.
We refer to the path
P_{i}
from
\sigma_{1}
to
\sigma_{i}
is the
active path
of
T_{i}
. Later vertices in the dfs-enumeration
of
T
can only attach to vertices of
T_{i}
that lie on the active path: if
j>i
and
\sigma_{j}
has a neighbour
\sigma^{\prime}

in
T_{i}
then
\sigma^{\prime}
must lie on the active path
P_{i}
. Furthermore, if
i\geq 2
and
\sigma_{i}
is a leaf then no vertex
later in the dfs-enumeration is adjacent to
\sigma_{i}
.

The following two facts will be helpful:

2.2

Let
G
be a graph with clique number at most
k
, where
k\geq 1
is an integer,
and let
n_{0},\ldots,n_{k}\geq 1
be integers with
n_{0}\leq|G|
.
Then there exist
p\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and an induced subgraph
H
of
G
with at least
n_{p-1}
vertices and maximum
degree less than
n_{p}
.

Proof.
We prove this by induction on
k
. For
k=1
the statement is immediate as then
G
has no edges and we can take
H=G
. So suppose
k>1
and we have proved the statement for smaller

. If
G
has maximum degree less than
n_{1}
then we can can take
H=G
. Otherwise, pick a vertex of maximum degree and let
G^{\prime}
be the subgraph of
G
induced by its neighbours:
G^{\prime}
has clique number at most
k-1
, and applying the inductive statement to
G^{\prime}
with parameters
n_{1},\dots,n_{k}
gives the required induced subgraph

.

2.3

For all
d,n>0
where
n
is an integer, and for every graph
G
with maximum degree less than
d
,
there is an induced subgraph of
G
with at least
|G|/n
vertices and with maximum degree less than
d/n
.

Proof.
Let
(V_{1},\ldots,V_{n})
be a partition of
V(G)
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lvert E(G[V_{i}])\rvert
is minimum. We can choose

with
|V_{i}|\geq|G|/n
. Suppose that

. For

,

has at least as many neighbours in
V_{j}
as in
V_{i}
, or else moving

to
V_{j}
would contradict minimality. Thus

has at most
d_{g}(v)/n\leq d/n
neighbours in
V_{i}
. This proves

.

A
copy
of
H
in
G
is an isomorphism
\phi
from
H
to an induced subgraph
\phi(H)
of
G
.
For
A,B\subset V(G)
and

, we say that

is

-sparse to B
if every vertex of

has at most
y|B|
neighbours in

. A key lemma in the proof of
1.4
shows that if

does not contain a copy of
T
then we can find sets

and

such that

is not too large compared to

, and vertices of

are sparse to

. We will apply this later to prove
2.5
, which will allow us to perform the sparsification step.

Here is the key lemma.

2.4

Let
k,r,t\geq 2
be integers, let
q:=(k-1)(r-1)
, and let

y_{0},y_{1},\ldots,y_{q}\in(0,1)
be such that
y_{p}\leq y_{p-1}/3t
for
1\leq p\leq q
.
Let
T
be a tree with
t
vertices and radius at most
r
,
and let
G
be a
T
-free graph with clique number at most
k
and maximum degree
d
,
such that
d\geq 6t/y_{q-1}
.
Then there exist
p\in\{1,\dots,q\}
and disjoint
A,B\subseteq V(G)
such that

•

\lvert A\rvert\geq y_{p-1}d/2
and
\lvert B\rvert\leq 2rtd
;

•

G[A]
has maximum degree less than
y_{p}d
; and

•

every vertex in
A
has fewer than
\max(y_{p}d,k^{t})

neighbours in
G\setminus(A\cup B)
.

Proof.
The proof proceeds by attempting to grow a copy of
T
in dfs-enumeration order: since
G
does not contain a copy of
T
we will get stuck at some stage, and we will use this to obtain the sets
A
and
B
. At each stage of the argument, we identify a copy of some subtree

T_{s}
of
T
, and some sets
A_{i}
of vertices that will allow us to grow the embedding further. Since our vertices are chosen in
dfs order, later vertices of the tree cannot have arbitrary neighbours in
T_{s}
: they can only be adjacent to vertices on the active
path
P_{s}
. So for each vertex
v_{i}
of
P_{s}
we keep a set of
A_{i}
of vertices that we can use to add a neighbour of
v_{i}
to
our embedding at a later stage (vertices at distance
r
from the root are handled slightly differently, as they are not adjacent
to later vertices in the dfs-enumeration). We need the sets
A_{i}
to satisfy several restrictions: the vertices of
A_{i}
should be
adjacent to
v_{i}
and no other vertex of
T_{s}
; each pair
A_{i},A_{j}
of sets must satisfy some density condition (because adding a
vertex from
A_{j}
to our embedding means that its neighbours in
A_{i}
can no longer be added to the embedding and have to be removed from
A_{i}
, and so we will only use vertices with few neighbours in
A_{i}
); and it will be convenient if the sets

are appropriately sparse, as we need to produce a set

with low density. The parameters depend on the distance from the root in

: as we get deeper, the sets

get smaller, and we need to adjust the numbers accordingly.

Let us make all this more precise.
Let
\sigma_{1}\in V(T)
be a vertex with distance at most

, and let
(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{t})

. For

be the subtree of

.

Let

be the active path in

(between

and

).
Thus

. Let
\phi
be

in

, with vertex set

, and for

, let

.
We say that

and integers
(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\min(\ell,r-1)}

•

are pairwise disjoint subsets of

;

•

for
i=1,\dots,\ell
, every vertex in

is adjacent to

and has no other neighbours in

;

•

for

, we have

, and

\lvert A_{i}\rvert\geq(1-\frac{s}{2t})y_{p_{i}-1}d,

•

is stable and

; and

•

for

,

is
\frac{1}{2t-s}
-sparse to

.

We refer to the collection of

and

as

for
\phi
. Note that the case

(if it occurs) is special, as we will only use

(and we will not need to define a set

).

We can now proceed with the main argument.
So suppose, for a contradiction, that there do not exist
p\in\{1,\dots,q\}
and

satisfying the lemma.

There is no good copy of
T_{t}=T
in

, since

is

-free. On the other hand,

. To see this, let

be a vertex of degree

in

, and let

be the set of its neighbours.
Thus
|D|=d\geq y_{1}d
.
Applying
2.2
to

with

, and

for

,
there must exist

has maximum degree less than

.
The map sending

.

Consequently there is a maximum value of

in

, and

is less than

.
Let
\phi
be a good copy of

,

be the active path

of

(where

and

), and let

and
(p_{i})_{1\leq i\leq\min(\ell,r-1)}

be references for

.

Since we have a dfs-enumeration,
\sigma_{s+1}
is adjacent in

to some vertex

of

, where

. We will show that either we can find a set

We start by defining

If

,
we define

There are at most

Since

2.5

Let

and an integer

:

•

\lvert A_{j}\rvert>(4rt)^{-1}y_{p_{j}-1}\lvert B_{j}\rvert

•

; and

•

\max(y_{p_{j}}d,k^{t})

.

We claim:

(1)

.

Suppose not, and let

.
Let

be the maximum degree of

, and suppose first that

.
Then by

, there exist

and disjoint

\lvert A\rvert\geq y_{p-1}d^{\prime}/2

and
\lvert B\rvert<2rd^{\prime}t

, and every vertex in

\max(y_{p}d^{\prime},k^{t})\leq\max(y_{p}d,k^{t})

,

, so taking

This implies that

. Let

Thus we have

.

Now, for each

Finally, by iterating the sparsification given by

.

Let

, and let

Let

.
For

3
Multibrooms and linear-sized stable sets

Now we move on to our second main theorem,
1.5
, which we will prove in this section.
For two integers
\ell,m\geq 0
, let us say
an
(\ell,m)
-broom
is a tree obtained from a path of length
\ell
, with ends
a,b
say,
by adding
m
new vertices each adjacent to
b
. We call
a
the
root
of the broom.
If
G
is a graph and
S\subseteq V(G)
, we denote by
N_{G}(S)
the set of vertices in
V(G)\setminus S
with a neighbour in
S
.
A
weighting
on a graph
G
is a function
w\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
;
and for
S\subseteq V(G)
we define
w(S):=\sum_{v\in S}w(v)
. We write
w(H)
for
w(V(H))
when
H
is an induced subgraph of
G
, and define the
maximum weighted degree

\Delta(G,w):=\max_{v\in V(G)}w(N_{G}(v))
,

The linear programming duality theorem implies that, if
H
is a hypergraph, and each vertex of
H
belongs to at least one hyperedge,
then the following are equal:

•

the minimum of
\sum_{A\in H}q(A)
, over all maps
q:H\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that, for each
v\in V(H)
,

\sum(q(A):A\in H\text{ and }A\ni v)\geq 1;

•

the maximum of
\sum_{v\in V(H)}w(v)
, over all maps
w:V(H)\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that, for each
A\in H
,

\sum(w(v):v\in V(H)\text{ and }v\in A)\leq 1.

By applying this to the hypergraph with edge-set the set of stable sets of
G
, we deduce that
1.5
is equivalent to the following:

3.1

Let
T
be a multibroom and
k\geq 1
an integer. Then there exists
c>0
such that if
G
is
\{T,K_{k+1}\}
-free,
and

is a weighting on

, then
there is a stable set
S
of
G
with
w(S)\geq cw(G)
.

One way to try to find a stable set containing a linear fraction of the total weight, is via degeneracy. A graph is
d
-degenerate
if every non-null subgraph has a vertex of degree at most
d
. (It is elementary that every
d
-degenerate graph is
(d+1)
-colourable.)
If we can find an induced subgraph

of

that is
d

is some constant, depending on
T
and
k
only)
and with total weight at least a constant fraction of the total weight of
G
, then since
G^{\prime}
is

(d+1)
-colourable, it has a stable set containing at least a
1/(d+1)
fraction of the total weight of
G^{\prime}
, and so we win.
We will try to assemble such a large
d
-degenerate subgraph iteratively. Let us say a subset
X\subseteq V(G)

is
d
-degenerate in

if it can be ordered
X=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
such that for
1\leq i\leq n
,
x_{i}
has at most
d

neighbours in
\{x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\cup(V(G)\setminus X)
. The advantage of this more restrictive definition is that if
X
is
d
-degenerate in
G
,
and

is
d
-degenerate in
G\setminus X
, then
X\cup X^{\prime}
is
d
-degenerate in
G
, as is easily seen. This will allow us
to grow a large

-degenerate subgraph by piecing together smaller subsets recursively.

We start with a lemma that we can use to grow a single branch of a multibroom. We show that, for an arbitrary vertex
v
in a weighted graph

, either we can grow a broom of any specified length rooted at
v
, or we can find two disjoint sets

of vertices such that

is at least a constant fraction of

and
X
has bounded degeneracy in
G\setminus(Y\cup\{v\})
. We will argue later by induction on the clique number
k
, so we assume that
3.1
holds for induced subgraphs with smaller clique number.

3.2

Let
k,\ell\geq 1
and
m\geq 0
be integers, and let

.
Let
G
be a graph with clique number at most
k
, let
w
be a weighting of

, and let
v
be a vertex of

Suppose that,
for every induced subgraph

of

of
G^{\prime}

•

-broom in

•

there exist disjoint

w(X)\geq w(X\cup Y)/(d2^{2\ell})
and

k^{m}
-degenerate in

.

Proof.
We proceed by induction on

.
Let

be as in the theorem, and let
\Delta=\Delta(G,w)
be the maximum weighted degree.
We may delete all vertices

and

as in the lemma, and we can add all the deleted vertices to

. Thus we may
assume that

One approach to finding a broom would be to start from

, let

be the set of vertices at distance

from

. If any vertex

has

the graph. However this approach is too simple: the sets

therefore explore the graph more carefully. Starting with

, where each set

is a

of

in the unexplored part of the graph. We choose

is not too heavy.

Let

and

. For

inductively as follows:

•

be a maximal subset of

.

•

.

•

J_{i}=L_{1}\cup\cdots\cup L_{i}
.

Thus
L_{1}=N_{G}(v)
and

. Note that for any vertex

, we can find an induced path

such that

,

and

for each

; furthermore, for

,
u_{h}
has no neighbours outside

.

(1)
We may assume that
w(L_{j+1})<\min(w(L_{j}),\Delta)

Suppose first that
w(L_{i})\geq\min(w(L_{i-1}),\Delta)
for all

4
A multicolour version

One can look for “multicolour” versions of these theorems. One simple multicolour extension of the
Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture is:

4.1

Conjecture:
For every forest
H
, and all integers
k,t\geq 1
, there exists
c>0
such that if
G
is a graph with
clique number at most
k
, and
the edges of
G
can be coloured with
t
colours in such a way that
no monochromatic induced subgraph of
G
is isomorphic to
H
,
then
\chi(G)\leq c
.

(
Monochromatic
means with all edges the same colour.) Calling this a “conjecture” is a stretch, because we have very little faith in it.
So far we cannot even prove it when
H
is the four-vertex path (although we can if in addition
t\leq 3
).

On the other hand, surprisingly to us,
4.1
is “nearly” true.
Our first main theorem
1.2
can be extended to the following:

4.2

For every forest
H
, and all integers
k,t\geq 1
, if

G
is a graph with
clique number at most
k
, and
the edges of
G
can be coloured with
t
colours in such a way that
no monochromatic induced subgraph of
G
is isomorphic to
H
,
then
\alpha(G)\geq|G|^{1-o(1)}
.

It is straightforward to adapt the proof of
1.2
to give this more general result, and we omit the details.
Pleasingly, this is tight, in the sense that if
\mathcal{H}
is a finite set of
t
-edge-coloured graphs (that is, graphs
H
with
E(H)

partitioned into
t
subsets labelled
1,\ldots,t
), and we want to know whether every
t
-edge-coloured graph with bounded clique number
that contains no member of
\mathcal{H}
as an induced subgraph (in the natural sense) has a nearly-linear stable set, then
4.2
tells us the answer:
if and only if for
1\leq i\leq t
, there is a forest in
\mathcal{H}
with all edges coloured
i
.

Another multicolour extension of
1.1
generalizes a theorem of Chudnovsky and Seymour
[
2
]
, who proved the following when

t=2
:

4.3

Let
t\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite set of
t
-edge-coloured complete graphs.
Assuming the truth of
1.1
, the following
are equivalent:

•

there exists
c>0
such that for every
t
-edge-coloured
complete graph
G
with no induced subgraph in
\mathcal{H}
,
V(G)
is the union of
c
monochromatic cliques

•

for every two colours
i,j\in\{1,\ldots,t\}
,
\mathcal{H}
contains a forest with all edges in colour
i
and all nonedges
in colour
j
, and
\mathcal{H}
contains a complete multipartite graph with all edges in colour
i
and all nonedges in colour
j
.

This can be deduced from a result of
[
3
]
(we omit the details), although deriving the second bullet from the first
assumes the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture. But without assuming that, we can prove something similar:

4.4

Let
t\geq 1
be an integer, and let
\mathcal{H}
be a finite set of
t
-edge-coloured complete graphs.
The following
are equivalent:

•

every
t
-edge-coloured complete graph with no induced subgraph in

has a monochromatic clique of size

for every two colours

\mathcal{H}

and all nonedges

\mathcal{H}

and the theorem of
[
3
]

Validity of generalized Gibbs ensemble in a random matrix model with a global ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -symmetry
Adway Kumar Das

Abstract
ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry is ubiquitous in quantum mechanical systems where it drives various phase transitions and emergent physics. To understand the role of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} symmetry in the thermalization of a local observable in a disordered system, we consider random symmetric centrosymmetric (SC) matrices where the exchange symmetry is conserved. Such a conservation law splits the Hilbert space into decoupled subspaces such that the energy spectrum of a SC matrix is a superposition of two pure spectra. After discussing the known results on the correlations of such mixed spectrum, we consider different initial states and analytically compute the entire time evolution of their survival probability and associated timescales. We show that there exist certain initial states which do not decay over a very long timescales such that a measure zero fraction of random SC matrices exhibit spontaneous symmetry breaking. Later, we show that thermalization is violated for a generic local observable in case of the SC matrices, where the generalized Gibbs ensemble accurately captures the equilibrium expectation values.

The emergence of statistical mechanics upon the equilibration of an isolated quantum system is a fascinating area of research. Despite the unitarity of the time evolution of an isolated system with many degrees of freedom, a typical local observable thermalizes if the energy states of the governing Hamiltonian are ergodic

;

In a two-level system, the presence of a single non-trivial symmetry ensures integrability, where the energy levels become uncorrelated with localized eigenstates

;
Das and Ghosh 2023
;
Berry and Shukla 2009
. This naturally leads to the question of what happens in a disordered system with many degrees of freedom upon breaking a single global symmetry

;
Das and Ghosh 2022c
. In this work, we focus on the discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry where the Hamiltonian commutes with an invertible symmetry operator
\hat{\mathsf{O}}

\mathbb{Z}_{2}

If the symmetry imposed by

.

;

;
Schaadt et al. 2003
;
de Carvalho et al. 2007
, small-world

Santos et al. 2016b
.

As a concrete example of
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry in a disordered system, we consider the random symmetric centrosymmetric (SC) matrices, which commute with the exchange symmetry operator,

II
Symmetric Centrosymmetric Matrix

An
N\times N
centrosymmetric matrix,
H
, satisfies the commutation relation

\displaystyle[H,\mathsf{J}]=0\Rightarrow H_{i,j}=H_{i^{\prime},j^{\prime}}\;\forall\;i,j,\qquad i^{\prime}\equiv N+1-i

(1)

where
\mathsf{J}\equiv\mathinner{\left\{\delta_{i,N+1-j}\right\}}
is the exchange matrix.

\mathsf{J}
is symmetric, invertible (
\mathsf{J}=\mathsf{J}^{-1}=\mathsf{J}^{T}
), involutory (
\mathsf{J}^{2}=\mathbb{I}
, the identity matrix) and has two distinct eigenvalues,
\pm 1
with multiplicities
\lceil\frac{N}{2}\rceil
and
\lfloor\frac{N}{2}\rfloor
, respectively.
If
N=2^{L}
, then an
N\times N
exchange matrix can be expressed as
\underset{k}{\otimes}\sigma^{x}_{k}
where
\sigma^{x}_{k}
is the Pauli X matrix in
\hat{\sigma}_{z}
representation acting on the
k
th site of a fictitious 1D lattice with
L
sites. Since
\mathinner{\left[\sigma^{x},\mathbb{I}\right]}=0
, any matrix of the form
\sigma^{x}_{i}\sigma^{x}_{j}\dots\sigma^{x}_{k}
commutes with
\mathsf{J}
where
1\leq i<\dots<k\leq L
. The exchange matrix also commutes with the matrices of the form
\mathbb{I}^{N_{1}\times N_{1}}\oplus\mathsf{O}\oplus\mathbb{I}^{N_{2}\times N_{2}}\oplus\mathsf{J}\mathsf{O}\mathsf{J}\oplus\mathbb{I}^{N_{1}\times N_{1}}
, where
\mathsf{O}
is any arbitrary operator of dimension
N-(2N_{1}+N_{2})
.

In addition to centrosymmetry, if a matrix
H
has diagonal symmetry,
then, such a matrix is called symmetric centrosymmetric (SC), which is persymmetric as well
Cantoni and Butler 1976

\displaystyle H=\mathsf{J}H^{T}\mathsf{J}\Leftrightarrow H_{i,j}=H_{j^{\prime},i^{\prime}}.

(2)

Since
[H,\mathsf{J}]=0
, we can reduce
H
in the basis of
\mathsf{J}
via a similarity transformation
Q^{T}HQ=H^{\prime}
such that
H^{\prime}
assumes a block diagonal form. Such a block structure is a consequence of the two disjoint subspaces in the Hilbert space,
which are denoted by odd and even sectors, spanned by anti-symmetric (
\ket{\Phi^-}
) and symmetric (
\ket{\Phi^+}
) states with energy levels
E^{\mp}
, respectively.

II.1
Energy correlations of a SC matrix

Figure 1:
Energy correlation of random SC matrices. (a) density of level spacing (b) number variance for
N=2048

We are interested in random SC matrices having independent and identically distributed Gaussian random elements with zero mean and unit variance. Corresponding energy spectrum is a superposition of two pure spectra, each belonging to a Gaussian orthogonal ensemble (GOE) matrix. The energy correlations in case of such mixed spectrum is well known
Pandey et al. 2019
. Below, we provide the analytical forms of the energy correlations for a random SC matrix for the sake of completeness.

The density of states (DOS) of a random SC matrix follows the Wigner’s semi-circle law with a width
2\sqrt{\left\langle E^{2}\right\rangle}=\sqrt{4+N}
. Corresponding energy levels exhibit level clustering where the density of level spacing follows
Mehta 2004
;
Pandey et al. 2019

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}^{\mathrm{SC}}(s)=\frac{e^{-\frac{\pi}{8}s^{2}}}{2}+\frac{\pi}{8}se^{-\frac{\pi}{16}s^{2}}\text{Erfc}\mathinner{\left(\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{4}s\right)}

(3)

and the 2nd moment of the level spacing is

\displaystyle\left\langle s^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{8(2-\sqrt{2})}{\pi}.

(4)

In Fig.
1
(a), we plot the numerically obtained density of level spacing of SC ensemble for
N=2048
along with the analytical expression from Eq. (
3
). The above equation indicates a degree of level repulsion intermediate between that of the GOE and Poisson ensemble (
\left\langle s^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{4}{\pi}
and 2 for the Wigner’s surmise and exponential distribution, respectively). An intermediate level repulsion caused by incomplete separation of symmetries is observed in many-body systems as well, e.g. 1D hard core boson model
Santos and Rigol 2010
.

The superposition of two pure spectra also affects the long-range energy correlation of a SC matrix, reflected in the two-point correlation function,

\displaystyle\rho^{(2)}(E_{1},E_{2})=N(N-1)\int dE_{3}\dots dE_{N}\textrm{P}\mathinner{\left(\vec{E}\right)}.

(5)

The above integral relation is valid provided the joint density of the
N
energy levels,
\textrm{P}\mathinner{\left(\vec{E}\right)}
is invariant under the exchange of any pair of energies. The two-point correlation,

\rho^{(2)}(E_{1},E_{2})
is the probability of finding any two energy levels at
E_{1}
and
E_{2}

Bogomolny et al. 2001
.
A related quantity is the two-level cluster function,
T_{2}(E)\equiv 1-\rho^{(2)}(E)
. We denote the two-level cluster function of the unfolded energy levels as
Y_{2}(\mathcal{E})
where
\mathcal{E}
is the gap of unfolded energy levels
Guhr et al. 1998
. In the Poisson ensemble,
Y_{2}(\mathcal{E})=0
at any energy gap, while for GOE
Mehta 2004

\displaystyle\begin{split}Y_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\mathcal{E})&=\mathinner{\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\mathrm{sgn}(\mathcal{E})-\mathrm{Si}(\pi\mathcal{E})\right)}\mathinner{\left(\frac{\cos\pi\mathcal{E}}{\pi\mathcal{E}}-\frac{\sin\pi\mathcal{E}}{(\pi\mathcal{E})^{2}}\right)}\\
&+\mathinner{\left(\frac{\sin\pi\mathcal{E}}{\pi\mathcal{E}}\right)}^{2}\end{split}

(6)

where
\mathrm{sgn}(x)
is the Signum function and
\mathrm{Si}(x)
is the sine integral. In case of a SC matrix, the superposition of two GOE spectra leads to a two-level cluster function
Pandey et al. 2019

\displaystyle Y_{2}^{\mathrm{SC}}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{1}{2}Y_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}\mathinner{\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}

(7)

The inverse Fourier transform of the two-level cluster function gives the two-level form factor, a function in the dimensionless time domain,
\tau

\displaystyle b_{2}(\tau)=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}d\mathcal{E}\>Y_{2}(\mathcal{E})\cos(2\pi\tau\Ecal)

(8)

where the symmetry of
Y_{2}(\mathcal{E})
around
\mathcal{E}=0
reduces the complex integral to a real one
Buijsman et al. 2020
. The two-level form factor controls the long time dynamics of a generic observable
Schiulaz et al. 2019
. For Poisson ensemble,
b_{2}(\tau)=0
at any time while for GOE

\displaystyle b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1-2\tau+\tau\log\mathinner{\left(1+2\tau\right)},&\tau\leq 1,\\
\tau\log\left(\dfrac{2\tau+1}{2\tau-1}\right)-1,&\tau>1.\end{cases}

(9)

Then, Eq. (
7
) implies that the two-level form factor of a SC matrix is
b_{2}^{\mathrm{SC}}(\tau)=b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(2\tau)
.

To identify different energy scales in a system, one of the most useful measure is the number variance, defined for a set of unfolded energies,
\mathinner{\left\{\mathcal{E}_{j}\right\}}
as
Das et al. 2025b
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma^{2}(\mathcal{E})&=\left\langle[\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{0})-\mathcal{E}]^{2}\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}\\
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{0})&=I\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}+\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}-I\mathinner{\left(\mathcal{E}_{0}-\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}\end{split}

(10)

where
I(\mathcal{E})
is the cumulative distribution of the unfolded energy levels and
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{0})
is the number of energy levels in the window of span
\mathcal{E}
and centered at
\mathcal{E}_{0}
. For GOE, the energy spectrum is rigid so that any two energy levels are correlated and the number variance has a logarithmic behavior
Mehta 2004

\displaystyle\begin{split}\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{GOE}}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{2}{\pi^{2}}\log\mathinner{\left(2\pi\mathcal{E}+\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}+1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{8}\right)}+\mathcal{O}\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{E}}\right)}\end{split}

(11)

where
\gamma_{\mathrm{E}}=0.577216\dots
is the Euler-Mascheroni constant,
\mathrm{Ci}(x)=-\int_{x}^{\infty}dt\frac{\cos t}{t}
and
\mathrm{Si}(x)=\int_{0}^{x}dt\frac{\sin t}{t}
. Contrarily in the Poisson ensemble, the number of unfolded energy levels are free to fluctuate around their mean position,
\left\langle\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{E},\mathcal{E}_{0})\right\rangle_{\mathcal{E}_{0}}=\mathcal{E}
and the number variance exhibits a linear behavior,
\Sigma^{2}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{E}
. In case of a SC matrix, superposition implies that the number variance follows
Pandey et al. 2019

\displaystyle\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{SC}}(\mathcal{E})=2\Sigma^{2}_{\mathrm{GOE}}\mathinner{\left(\frac{\mathcal{E}}{2}\right)}

(12)

In Fig.
1
(b), we plot the numerically obtained number variance of SC ensemble for
N=2048
along with the analytical expression from Eq. (
12
) and find an excellent agreement.

III
Dynamical response of a SC matrix

The exchange symmetry of a SC matrix implies that the dynamics of an initial state will depend on its symmetry. To illustrate this, we look at the following initial state

\displaystyle\ket{\Psi_\omega}\equiv\cos\omega\ket{k}+\sin\omega\ket{k'},\quad-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\omega\leq\frac{\pi}{2}

(13)

where
k^{\prime}\equiv N+1-k
,
\ket{k}
is a unit vector from the computational basis. The expectation value of the exchange matrix w.r.t.
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
is

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{J}\right\rangle=\bra{\Psi_\omega}\mathsf{J}\ket{\Psi_\omega}=\sin(2\omega).

(14)

Our choice of the initial state is motivated by Schrödinger’s cat states, which are superposition of macroscopically different states
Ourjoumtsev et al. 2006
;
Huang and Moore 2006
;
Wang et al. 2016
;
Lewenstein et al. 2021
. In particular,
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
is a weighted superposition of the exchange doublets,
\frac{\ket{k}\pm\ket{k'}}{\sqrt{2}}\equiv\ket{\Psi_{\pm\frac{\pi}{4}}}
, which are eigenstates of
\mathsf{J}
and have energies
H_{k,k}\pm H_{k,k^{\prime}}
, respectively.

The local density of states
Schiulaz et al. 2019
of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
follows Wigner’s semicircle law with a width
\Gamma\approx\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}
irrespective of
\omega
. However, for
\omega=\frac{\pi}{4}
,
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
is a symmetric state (having
\left\langle\mathsf{J}\right\rangle=1
), hence spanned by only
\ket{\Phi^+_j}
’s, the symmetric eigenstates of
H
. Consequently, the time evolution of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
is always confined to the even subspace. Similarly, the time evolution of
\ket{\Psi_{-\frac{\pi}{4}}}
is always restricted to the odd subspace spanned by anti-symmetric energy eigenstates,
\ket{\Phi^-}
’s. Contrarily for
\omega=0
,
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
couples both the odd and even subspaces and homogeneously spreads over the entire Hilbert space at sufficiently long time.

To understand the non-equilibrium features of the initial state
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
, we monitor its survival probability
Torres-Herrera et al. 2018
;
Torres-Herrera et al. 2019
;
Schiulaz et al. 2019
;
Das et al. 2025c
;
Vallejo-Fabila et al. 2024
;
Vallejo-Fabila et al. 2025
, defined as

\displaystyle\begin{split}R(t)&\equiv\absolutevalue{\bra{\Psi_\omega(t)}\ket{\Psi_\omega}}^{2}=\absolutevalue{ \sum_{j = \pm}\sum_{n = 1}^{\frac{N}{2}} |c_n^j|^2 e^{-i E_n^j t} }^{2}\\
c_{n}^{\pm}&\equiv\bra{\Phi_n^\pm}\ket{\Psi_\omega}=(\cos\omega\pm\sin\omega)\Phi_{n}^{\pm}(k)\end{split}

(15)

where
\ket{\Phi_n^\pm}
are eigenstates of
H
with energies
E_{n}^{\pm}
. Then, ensemble averaged survival probability can be expressed as
Schiulaz et al. 2019

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle R(t)\right\rangle&=\left\langle\int dE\mathcal{F}(E)e^{-iEt}\right\rangle+\overline{R}\\
\mathcal{F}(E)&=\sum_{m\neq n,\{j,l\}=\pm}|c_{m}^{j}|^{2}|c_{n}^{l}|^{2}\delta\mathinner{\left(E-E_{m}^{j}+E_{n}^{l}\right)}\end{split}

(16)

where the equilibrium value of the survival probability,
\overline{R}
is the inverse participation ratio of the initial state,
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
in the energy eigenbasis and can be written as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\overline{R}&=\left\langle\sum_{j=\pm}\sum_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}}|c_{n}^{j}|^{4}\right\rangle=\sum_{j=\pm}\mathinner{\left(1+j\sin 2\omega\right)}^{2}\sum_{n=1}^{\frac{N}{2}}\left\langle\absolutevalue{\Phi_n^j(k)}^{4}\right\rangle\\
&\approx\frac{3(1+\sin^{2}2\omega)}{N}\end{split}

(17)

where we used the fact that each of the diagonal blocks in a reduced SC matrix contribute ergodic eigenstates of length
\frac{N}{2}
following Haar distribution. Thus, energy eigenstate components of a random SC matrix follow the Porter-Thomas distribution
Bogomolny and Sieber 2018
similar to the GOE. Eq. (
17
) implies that
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
can spread to the entire Hilbert space or a part of it depending on
\omega
, where the maximal (minimal) spread occurs for
\omega=0

\mathinner{\left(\omega=\pm\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}
.

Figure 2:
Ensemble averaged survival probability of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
(Eq. (

)) for random SC matrices with
N=1024
and various values of

. For survival probability, solid lines denote numerical simulations and the bold curves follow the analytical expression in Eq. (

). Vertical black lines denote the Zeno and relaxation times while correlation holes have been marked with dashed lines.

The 2nd term in Eq. (
16
) describes the non-equilibrium evolution of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
and can be evaluated following
Schiulaz et al. 2019
since, the eigenvalues are independent of the eigenstate components in case of a random SC matrix. Then, we find the analytical expression of the survival probability of the initial state in Eq. (
13
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}R_{\omega}(t)&\approx(1-\overline{R})\mathinner{\left(\frac{\mathrm{J}_{1}\mathinner{\left(2\Gamma t\right)}^{2}}{(\Gamma t)^{2}}-\frac{(1+\sin^{2}2\omega)}{N}b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}\mathinner{\left(\frac{t}{t_{\mathrm{H}}}\right)}\right)}\\
&+\overline{R}\end{split}

(18)

where
b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau)
is given in Eq. (
9
),
\overline{R}
is given in Eq. (
17
),
\tau\equiv\frac{t}{t_{\mathrm{H}}}
is the dimensionless time where
t_{\mathrm{H}}
is the Heisenberg time

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{H}}\approx\frac{N}{\Gamma}=2\sqrt{N}.

(19)

In Fig.
2
, we verify the validity of Eq. (
18
) in case of the initial states from Eq. (
13
).
For
\omega=\pm\frac{\pi}{4}
, Eq. (
18
) describes the survival probability of a localized state in case of a GOE matrix with dimension
\frac{N}{2}
.

The survival probability exhibits a universal quadratic decay
\sim 1-\frac{N}{4}t^{2}
for
t\ll t_{\mathrm{Zeno}}
where the rate of decay is controlled by the width of the local density of states,
\Gamma\approx\frac{\sqrt{N}}{2}
and
t_{\mathrm{Zeno}}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{N}}
is the Zeno time
Chiu et al. 1982
. At later times,
\left\langle R_{\omega}(t)\right\rangle
exhibits oscillations with an envelope decaying as
t^{-3}
, a consequence of the bounds in the energy spectrum
Khalfin 1958
;
Távora et al. 2016
;
Távora et al. 2017
;
Roy et al. 2025
. Such a power-law decay (called
slope
) leads to the dip of the survival probability, which can be found by expanding the 1st and 2nd terms in
R_{\omega}(t)
for long and short times, respectively, and finding the minimum of their sum

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{d}{dt}\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{\pi(\Gamma t)^{3}}-\mathinner{\left(1-\frac{2\Gamma t}{N}\right)}\right)}\Bigg|_{t=t_{\mathrm{dip}}}=0\\
\Rightarrow\>&t_{\mathrm{dip}}=\mathinner{\left(\frac{24}{\pi\mathinner{\left(1+\sin^{2}2\omega\right)}}\right)}^{\frac{1}{4}}\end{split}

(20)

Hence, dip time is independent of the system size but depends on the initial state. Beyond

, the survival probability exhibits a correlation hole dictated by the two-level form factor,
b_{2}(\tau)
. At long time,
b_{2}(\tau)
has a power-law decay
\mathinner{\left(b_{2}^{\mathrm{GOE}}(\tau)\sim\frac{1}{12\tau^{2}}\right)}
, thus, we fix a small threshold,
\epsilon
such that

\displaystyle t_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{1}{3}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\epsilon}}.

(21)

Hence, relaxation time is independent of
\omega
and same as that of the GOE
Schiulaz et al. 2019
. Moreover,

has the same order of magnitude as that of the Heisenberg time,
t_{\mathrm{H}}\approx 2\sqrt{N}
. In Fig.
2
, we show the different timescales for various values of
\gamma
. Eqs. (
17
) and (
20
) imply that a symmetric
\mathinner{\left(\omega=\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}
or antisymmetric
\mathinner{\left(\omega=-\frac{\pi}{4}\right)}
initial state is the quickest one to reach the correlation hole and has minimal spread over the energy space.

(from odd and even sectors, respectively) are the global ground and 1st excited states of the entire spectrum with a high probability. Hence, lowest two energy levels of a SC matrix are most likely to be
E_{1}^{\pm}
. The gap between such energy levels follows the density

\displaystyle\textrm{P}\mathinner{\left(s\right)}=\frac{2}{\pi}\exp\mathinner{\left(-\frac{s^{2}}{\pi}\right)}

(22)

valid for an integrable two-level system
Das and Ghosh 2019
. If we start from a superposition of the ground states from the two different symmetry sectors, then the system exhibits Rabi oscillation

\displaystyle\begin{split}R(t)&=\absolutevalue{ \braket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}(t)}{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}} }^{2}=\cos^{2}\mathinner{\left(\frac{S_{\textrm{o-e}}}{2}t\right)}\\
S_{\textrm{o-e}}&\equiv|E_{1}^{-}-E_{1}^{+}|,\quad\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}\equiv\frac{\ket{\Phi_1^-}+\ket{\Phi_1^+}}{\sqrt{2}}\end{split}

(23)

with a time period
t_{\mathrm{Rabi}}=S_{\textrm{o-e}}^{-1}
. The density of gap between
E_{1}^{-}
and
E_{1}^{+}
(Eq. (
22
)) implies that they have a high probability to cluster i.e.
\ket{\Phi_1^-}
and
\ket{\Phi_1^+}
can be almost degenerate such that
t_{\mathrm{Rabi}}
is arbitrarily large. Since, a typical excitation takes
\mathcal{O}(t_{\mathrm{H}})
time to equilibrate, if

\displaystyle\frac{t_{\mathrm{Rabi}}}{t_{\mathrm{H}}}\sim\mathcal{O}(N)

(24)

then, the time period of the Rabi oscillation is so large that
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
is effectively an equilibrium state, i.e. the system retains the memory of the initial configuration for a time period much larger than the Heisenberg time. This is illustrated in Fig.
3
for a single realization of a random SC matrix. Therefore, similar to the symmetry-broken states in systems exhibiting quantum phase transition, e.g. transverse field Ising
Sachdev 2011
;
Zurek et al. 2005
, Lipkin-Meshkov-Glick
Castaños et al. 2006
;
Wang and Pérez-Bernal 2021
, collective models
Hwang et al. 2015
;
Kloc et al. 2018
;
Corps and Relaño 2022
,
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
breaks the exchange symmetry of a random SC matrix whenever the condition in Eq. (
24
) holds, which has a probability of occurring

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\frac{1}{N}}ds\textrm{P}\mathinner{\left(s\right)}=\mathrm{Erf}\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}N}\right)}.

(25)

Hence, for any finite system size,
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
behaves like a symmetry-broken state, i.e. an equilibrium state despite not being an energy eigenstate in case of a vanishing fraction of matrices from an ensemble of random SC matrices. However,
\mathrm{Erf}\mathinner{\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}N}\right)}=\frac{2}{\pi N}+\mathcal{O}(N^{-3})
, such that the condition in Eq. (
24
) does not hold in the thermodynamic limit. Then, spontaneous symmetry breaking occurs only for a vanishing fraction of all possible random SC matrices.

The ensemble averaged survival probability of
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\langle R(t)\right\rangle&=\int_{0}^{\infty}dS_{\textrm{o-e}}\textrm{P}\mathinner{\left(S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right)}R(t)=\int_{0}^{\infty}ds\frac{2}{\pi}e^{-\frac{s^{2}}{\pi}}\cos^{2}\mathinner{\left(\frac{\left\langle S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right\rangle s}{2}t\right)}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\exp\mathinner{\left(-\frac{\pi\left\langle S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right\rangle^{2}}{4}t^{2}\right)}\end{split}

(26)

which exhibits a Gaussian decay with a rate
\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\left\langle S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right\rangle}{2}
before saturating to
\overline{R}=\frac{1}{2}
. Such Gaussian decay relaxes at the time
t_{\mathrm{R}}=\frac{2}{\left\langle S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right\rangle}\sqrt{\frac{|\ln\epsilon|}{\pi}}
given a tolerance value
\epsilon\ll 1
. Since
\left\langle S_{\textrm{o-e}}\right\rangle
is smaller than the global bandwidth
\Gamma
, comparing with Eq. (
18
), we find that the average survival probability of
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
has a much slower decay but a smaller relaxation time compared to a localized state with similar energy, as shown in Fig.
3
(ii).

IV
Lack of thermalization in SC ensemble

So far, we looked at the unitary time evolution of an initial state and obtained corresponding survival probability, associated timescales for a random SC matrix. In our analyses, averaging the dynamics over an ensemble ensured equilibration at sufficiently long time due to decoherence
Matsoukas-Roubeas et al. 2023
. However, a consequence of Poincaré recurrence theorem is that for a single Hamiltonian of finite dimension, the time-reversal invariance of a unitary dynamics for a discrete spectrum leads to recurrences with a time period proportional to Heisenberg timescale
Bocchieri and Loinger 1957
;
Schulman 1978
;
Haake 2010
. Such quantum revivals
Eberly et al. 1980
have been demonstrated in the classical limit of the hydrogen atom
Gaeta and Stroud 1990
. Nevertheless, closed quantum systems can attain equilibrium in the sense that given a local operator,
\mathsf{O}
, its expectation value can remain arbitrarily close to the infinite time average for a finite interval of time beyond the relaxation time until a recurrence occurs
Reimann 2008
. Particularly, the quantity
|\left\langle\mathsf{O}(t)\right\rangle-\Tr{\overline{\rho} \ot}|
can remain arbitrarily small in the said time window where

\displaystyle\overline{\rho}\equiv\lim\limits_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}dt\ket{\Psi(t)}\bra{\Psi(t)}

(27)

is the equilibrium density matrix possessing maximum possible entropy subject to all the conserved quantities of the system
Gemmer and Mahler 2003
;
Gogolin et al. 2011
.
Such equilibration is rigorously proven for many-body systems provided the local correlations w.r.t. the initial state decays sufficiently fast
Cramer et al. 2008
;
Cramer and Eisert 2010
.

To study the relaxation of an observable w.r.t. a SC matrix, we initialize our system in the state
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
(Eq. (
13
)) and look at the time evolution of the expectation value of an observable,
\mathsf{O}

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathsf{O}(t)&\equiv\bra{\Psi_\omega(t)}\mathsf{O}\ket{\Psi_\omega(t)}\\
&=\sum_{j,l=\pm}\sum_{m\neq n}(c_{m}^{j})^{\star}c_{n}^{l}\bra{\Phi_m^j}\mathsf{O}\ket{\Phi_n^l}e^{-i(E_{m}^{j}-E_{n}^{l})t}\\
&+\sum_{j=\pm}\sum_{n}|c_{n}^{j}|^{2}\bra{\Phi_n^j}\mathsf{O}\ket{\Phi_n^j}\end{split}

(28)

with the usual notations from Eq. (
15
). Eq. (
27
) implies that the expectation value of the observable equilibrates to the infinite time average

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{DE}}\equiv\sum_{j=\pm}\sum_{n}|c_{n}^{j}|^{2}\mathsf{O}_{nn}^{j},\quad\mathsf{O}_{nn}^{\pm}\equiv\bra{\Phi_n^\pm}\mathsf{O}\ket{\Phi_n^\pm}

(29)

which is also known as the average w.r.t. diagonal ensemble
Rigol et al. 2007
;
Cassidy et al. 2011
;
Vidmar and Rigol 2016
. As equilibration is constrained by the conserved quantities, we need to consider all the constants of motion to describe the equilibration process. In a system governed by a time-independent Hamiltonian, the energy is always conserved. For example,
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
, the initial state in Eq. (
13
) has an energy

\displaystyle\left\langle E\right\rangle\equiv\bra{\Psi_\omega}H\ket{\Psi_\omega}=\sum_{j=\pm}\sum_{n}\mathinner{\left(1+j\sin 2\omega\right)}|\Phi_{n}^{j}(k)|^{2}E_{n}^{j}

(30)

which is conserved at all times, during the non-equilibrium evolution as well as beyond
t_{\mathrm{R}}
when
\ket{\Psi_\omega(t)}
equilibrates to the maximum entropy state. In the absence of any other conserved quantity, e.g. in a completely chaotic system, we expect ergodicity to hold, i.e. at least the bulk energy states are uniformly distributed on a hypersphere
von Neumann 2010
;
Goldstein et al. 2010
. In a classical system, ergodicity implies the equivalence of the infinite time average and the micro-canonical average over the constant energy surface in the phase space
Bunimovich 1979
;
Simányi 2004
;
Simányi 2009
. Similarly in an ergodic quantum system,
typicality
arguments
Tasaki 1998
;
Popescu et al. 2006
;
Goldstein et al. 2006
;
Linden et al. 2009
;
Müller et al. 2015

imply that the infinite time average of
\mathsf{O}
coincides with the average w.r.t. micro-canonical ensemble

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{ME}}=\frac{1}{N_{\Delta}}\sum_{E_{n}^{+},E_{n}^{-}\in\Delta}\mathsf{O}_{nn}^{-}+\mathsf{O}_{nn}^{+}

(31)

where
\Delta\equiv[\left\langle E\right\rangle-\Delta_{E},\left\langle E\right\rangle+\Delta_{E}]
is a micro-canonical energy window/ shell around the energy of the initial state containing
1\ll N_{\Delta}\ll N
energy levels over which the DOS varies sufficiently slowly. The equivalence of micro-canonical and diagonal averages is shown in quantum systems with few degrees of freedom
Feingold et al. 1985
;
Feingold and Peres 1986
.

As the local operator
\mathsf{O}
acts on a small part
\mathcal{H}_{A}
of the entire Hilbert space, the complimentary subspace acts as a heat bath for
\mathcal{H}_{A}

Lax 1955
such that the micro-canonical average is equal to the canonical expectation value
D’Alessio et al. 2016
;
Mori et al. 2018

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GE}}\equiv\Tr{\rog\hat{\ot}},\qquad\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{GE}}\equiv\frac{e^{-\frac{\hat{H}}{T}}}{\Tr[e^{-\frac{\hat{H}}{T}}]}

(32)

where
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{GE}}
is the equilibrium thermal density matrix with a canonical partition function
\Tr[e^{-\frac{\hat{H}}{T}}]
and
T
is the effective temperature of the initial state following conservation of energy in Eq. (
30
)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\left\langle E\right\rangle=\Tr{\rog\hat{H}}=\frac{\Tr{ \hat{H} e^{-\frac{\hat{H}}{T}} }}{\Tr{ e^{-\frac{\hat{H}}{T}} }}\end{split}

(33)

Thus, Gibbs ensemble allows one to assign a temperature to an initial state
Santos and Rigol 2010
which determines the size of the micro-canonical energy window around
\left\langle E\right\rangle

Deutsch 2018
.

The equivalence of the infinite time average from Eq. (
29

32

, i.e. the equilibrium state remains arbitrarily close to the thermal state,
\hat{\rho}_{\mathrm{GE}}
beyond relaxation time. This motivated the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH)
Srednicki 1994
;
Rigol et al. 2008
;
Polkovnikov et al. 2011
;
Nandkishore and Huse 2015
;

;
Borgonovi et al. 2016
;
Mori et al. 2018
;
Deutsch 2018
, which dictates that in an ergodic closed quantum system, the micro-canonical (Eq. (
31
)), canonical (Eq. (
32

29
)) expectation values of a generic local observable coincide. Note that, despite being integrable, certain translationally invariant finite range quantum systems may satisfy ETH in the weak sense
Müller et al. 2015
. To probe the validity of ETH in case of a random SC matrix, we initialize our system in a state
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
and obtain the expectation values of a local observable

\displaystyle\mathsf{O}=\mathbb{I}^{\frac{N-4}{2}\times\frac{N-4}{2}}\oplus\mathsf{O}_{\mathrm{loc}}^{4\times 4}\oplus\mathbb{I}^{\frac{N-4}{2}\times\frac{N-4}{2}}.

(34)

We observe that if
\omega=0
and
\mathsf{O}_{\mathrm{loc}}
is a random symmetric operator such that
\left\langle\mathsf{J}\right\rangle=\bra{\Psi_\omega}\mathsf{J}\ket{\Psi_\omega}=0

\mathinner{\left[\mathsf{O},\mathsf{J}\right]}\neq 0

29

32

\omega
is close to
\pm\frac{\pi}{4}

\mathinner{\left[\mathsf{O},\mathsf{J}\right]}=0

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{DE}}>\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{GE}}>\left\langle\mathsf{O}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{ME}}

The absence of thermalization for a local observable commuting with the global symmetry of the governing Hamiltonian implies that we need to maximize the entropy of the equilibrium state subject to the conservation of the energy as well as the exchange symmetry. V
Discussion

In this work, we look at the effect of a discrete
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry on the thermalization of a local observable in a disordered system. As a concrete example, we consider the random SC matrices which commute with the exchange symmetry operator. Corresponding Hilbert space can be split into two decoupled subspaces, namely odd (spanned by antisymmetric states) and even (spanned by symmetric states) sector. The energy spectrum of a SC matrix is a superposition of two pure spectrum, hence, we can analytically describe the energy correlations, e.g. the level spacing distribution, number variance.

To understand the dynamical properties of random SC matrices, we consider the initial state
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
, which is a function of
-\frac{\pi}{2}\leq\omega\leq\frac{\pi}{2}
and a weighted superposition of the exchange doublets. Depending on the value of
\omega
, the initial state can be localized in the odd or even sector and can couple these subspaces as well. We analytically obtain the survival probability (i.e. the probability of the system returning to its initial configuration) of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
, corresponding equilibrium value and characteristic timescales. The survival probability exhibits a correlation hole for all values of
\omega
indicating the presence of long-range correlation in the energy spectrum. However, the symmetric (
\omega=\frac{\pi}{4}
) and antisymmetric (
\omega=-\frac{\pi}{4}
) states take the smallest amount of time to reach the correlation hole and have the minimal spread over the energy space. Next, we look at the initial state
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
, which is a superposition of the local ground states of the two decoupled subspaces. We show that
\ket{\Psi_\textrm{o-e}}
is a symmetry-broken state for a measure zero fraction of random SC matrices, which exhibit spontaneous symmetry breaking in the thermodynamic limit.

To understand the thermalization in case of SC matrices, we consider initial states and local observables which do and do not respect the exchange symmetry of the Hamiltonian. By comparing the equilibrium expectation value, micro-canonical and canonical average of the local observables, we show that thermalization is violated in case of random SC matrices. We show that equilibrium behavior of the SC matrices can be well described by the generalized Gibbs ensemble for all the choices of initial state and observables. In future, we want to explore the dynamics of
\ket{\Psi_\omega}
and thermalization in case of the deformed centrosymmetric ensemble
Das and Ghosh 2022b
, where we can tune the extent of centrosymmetry. We expect to recover thermalization in the limit where exchange symmetry is completely broken in such random matrix ensembles.

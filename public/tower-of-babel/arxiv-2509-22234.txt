1.
Introduction

We study the following one-dimensional reaction-diffusion equation

u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u=f(x-ct,u)

where
c\in\mathbb{R}
is a prescribed velocity, and
f
is of KPP type. This equation describes the evolution of a population in an advective environment. More precisely, we suppose the environment is such that it is only advantageous in a bounded “patch”, outside of which the species dies at an asymptotically constant rate. The questions we aim to answer in this paper is: Under what conditions on
c
does the species persist? Under what conditions does it go extinct?

This problem was first studied, for the classical Laplacian, by Berestycki, Diekmann, Nagelkerke and Zegeling in
[
2
]
. They obtain the existence of a treshold value
c^{*}>0
such that the species persists for all speeds
|c|<c^{*}
and eventually goes extinct for all speeds
c\geq c^{*}
. This threshold value is closely related to the generalized principal eigenvalue of the linearized operator at
u=0
. Furthermore, they prove that, for any nonnegative, nontrivial initial density
u_{0}
,
u(t,x+ct)
converges, as
t\rightarrow\infty
to the unique nontrivial solution of the traveling wave equation

v^{\prime\prime}+cv^{\prime}+f(x,v)=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}.

There have been many extensions to the results of
[
2
]
, which have been surveyed by Wang, Li, Dong and Qiao in
[
21
]
. See also
[
1
]
for a reaction-diffusion model with a nonlocal interaction given by the nonlinearity.

On the other hand, in the last decade there has been a great interest in reaction-diffusion equations for nonlocal operators. These operators present interesting features, the most salient of which is the nonexistence of traveling waves for isotropic equations, i.e. with
f
independent of
x
. As proven by Cabré and Roquejoffre
[
6
]
, solutions to

u_{t}+(-\Delta)^{s}u=f(u)

do not propagate with constant speed but accelerate, and their level sets travel exponentially in time.

The acceleration feature is shared by models of nonlocal dispersion, that is, diffusion driven by operators of type

\mathcal{M}[u]=J\star u-u

where
J
is a continuous probability density in
\mathbb{R}
. Reaction-diffusion equations involving these operators have a rich theory, we refer the reader to the recent preprint by Bouin and Coville
[
4
]
, the introduction of which offers a rather complete overview of existing results.

The moving patch model with nonlocal dispersion has been studied by Coville in
[
11
]
. For compactly supported kernels
J
, persistence is obtained for speeds under a parameter
c^{*}
as well as extinction for speeds above another
c^{**}
. Using an approximation procedure, the persistence result is extended to kernels which are merely integrable, and the extinction one to kernels with finite second moment.

In this article, we tackle the question for the fractional Laplacian, as a natural continuation of the study in
[
6
]
to nonlinearities with nonconstant
x
-dependence. This operator differs from those treated in
[
11
]
in two aspects: on the one hand, it is a singular integral operator, so it cannot be written in convolution form, and on the other hand, its kernel presents slowly decaying tails of order
|x|^{-1-2s}
. The main novelty of this work is that we treat reaction-diffusion equations with fractional diffusion in advective environments for the first time, obtaining existence, nonexistence and decay results for the corresponding traveling wave equation, as well as studying the long-time behavior of the evolution equation.

1.1.
Assumptions and main results

We will make the following assumptions on
f
, which are identical to those in
[
11
]
. First, we assume that
f
is of KPP-type, in the following sense

(H0)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &f\text{ is differentiable with respect to }u;\\
&\forall u\geq 0,\ f(\cdot,u)\in C^{0,1}(\mathbb{R});\\
&f(\cdot,0)\equiv 0;\\
&\text{for all }x\in\mathbb{R},\ f(x,u)/u\text{ is decreasing with respect to }u\text{ on }(0,+\infty);\\
&\text{there exists }S(x)\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})\text{ such that }f(x,S(x))\leq 0\text{ for all }x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{aligned}\right.

The second assumption expresses the environment as inadvantageous outside some compact set, that is

(H1)

\limsup_{x\rightarrow\infty}\frac{f(x,s)}{s}<0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ uniformly in }s\in\mathbb{R}.

Note that this assumption combined with (
H0
) implies
\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\partial_{u}f(x,0)<0
. Finally, we will need further regularity on
\partial_{u}f
, given by

(H2)

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\left(\sup_{u_{0},u_{1}\geq 0}\frac{|\partial_{u}f(x,u_{0})-\partial_{u}f(x,u_{1})|}{|u_{0}-u_{1}|}\right)<\infty.

The model case we have in mind is given by the nonlinearity
f(x,u)=u(a(x)-u^{p})
with
a\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}a(x)=-\nu<0
and
p\geq 1
. In this case
S(x)\equiv S>0
satisfies the fifth condition in (
H0
) whenever
S>\|a\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}^{\frac{1}{p}}
.

Throughout this note, we assume
s\in(\frac{1}{2},1)
and adopt the notation
\Delta^{s}=-(-\Delta)^{s}
, as well as

\mathcal{L}_{c,a}\varphi(x)=\Delta^{s}\varphi(x)+c\varphi^{\prime}(x)+a(x)\varphi(x).

Our main results are the following. First, we obtain necessary and sufficient existence conditions for the traveling wave equation associated to our model:

Theorem 1
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and denote
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\Delta^{s}+c\partial_{x}+\partial_{u}f(\cdot,0))
. Then, there exists a unique nontrivial solution
u\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to the problem

(TW)

\Delta^{s}u+cu^{\prime}+f(x,u)=0,

if and only if
\lambda<0
. In that case, then there exists
C>0
such that

\frac{C^{-1}}{|x|^{1+2s}}\leq u(x)\leq\frac{C}{|x|^{1+2s}}.

The definition and properties of
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a})
will be discussed in section
4
. A key difference between our setting and the second order case is the unavailablity of a straightforward way of describing
\lambda_{1}
with respect to
c
. Bypassing this obstacle through suitable barriers and continuity arguments, we are able to establish a critical speed under which traveling waves exist, and a critical speed over which no nontrivial solutions to (
TW
) exist.

Theorem 2
.

Let
a\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{0,a})<0
and
\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}a(x)<0
, then there exist
c^{*},c^{**}>0
such that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a})<0
for all
c
such that
c<c^{*}
and
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a})>0
for all
c
such that
|c|>c^{**}
.

Having studied the traveling wave equation, we turn to the treatment of the long time behavior of solutions to the parabolic problem. We are able to establish the following persistence and extinction theorem.

Theorem 3
.

Let
u_{0}\in C(\mathbb{R})
be a bounded nonnegative function. Let
u
be the unique solution to

(1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta^{s}u+f(x,u)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty)\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

Denote
\lambda=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a})
with
a=\partial_{s}f(x,0)
. Then

(i)

if
\lambda\geq 0
, then
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
;

(ii)

uf
\lambda<0
and
u_{\infty}
is the unique nontrivial solution to (
TW
), then
\|u(\cdot-ct)-u_{\infty}\|_{L^{\infty}}\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
.

The outline of the paper is the following. In Section
2
we state preliminary results regarding (
TW
), as well as the construction of a useful barrier. In Section
3
, we investigate regularity and decay properties of solutions to equation (
TW
). In Section
4
we state some of the properties of the principal eigenvalue of our linearized operator, as well as its qualitative behavior with respect to the drift
c
. In Section
5
we complete the proof of Theorem
1
. Lastly, in Section
6
we prove Theorem
3
.

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall the definition of the fractional Laplacian. Given
s\in(0,1)
and for any bounded, smooth function
\varphi
in
\mathbb{R}
, we define its fractional Laplacian at
x
by

(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)=C_{s}\penalty\ \mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\varphi(x)-\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

where
\mathrm{P.V.}
stands for Cauchy principal value. The constant
C_{s}>0
is chosen in order to have
\lim_{s\nearrow 1}(-\Delta)^{s}\varphi(x)=-\Delta\varphi(x)
and has the explicit expression

C_{s}=\frac{2^{2s}s\Gamma(\frac{1}{2}+s)}{\pi^{1/2}\Gamma(1-s)},

see
[
5
]
for a derivation of this formula as well as an introduction to this operator. Nonetheless, we will omit this constant for the sake of readability.

Note that the above definition can be extended to functions
\varphi
which are
C^{2s}
around
x
and such that

\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathbb{R})}=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{|\varphi(y)|}{1+|x|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y<\infty.

Throughout this article, with “solution” we mean classical solution. By “viscosity solution” or “the viscosity sense” we mean the definition given in e.g.
[
8
]
, and use it to extend the definition of our operators to functions which are merely continuous and integrable.

Definition 1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open set. We say that
u\in C(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathbb{R})
is a viscosity subsolution (supersolution) to

\Delta^{s}u+cu^{\prime}+f(x,u)=0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{at }x\in\Omega

if for any
\varphi\in C^{2}(B_{r}(x))
,
B_{r}(x)\subset\Omega
, such that
\varphi\geq(\leq)u
in
B_{r}(x)
,
\varphi(x)=u(x)
we have

\Delta^{s}\varphi_{u}(x)+c\varphi_{u}^{\prime}(x)+f(x,u(x))\geq\penalty\ (\leq)0

where
\varphi_{u}
denotes the extension of
\varphi
by
u
outside
B_{r}(x)
. We say that
u
is a sub- or supersolution in
\Omega
if it is sub- or supersolution at every point in
\Omega
.

We say that
u
is a vicosity solution if it is both a sub- and a supersolution.

In this section, we state some known properties of the fractional Laplacian. First, a version of the maximum principle in unbounded domains.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
an unbounded domain,
c\in\mathbb{R}
,
\nu\geq 0
. Let
w
be a classical solution to

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}w+cw^{\prime}-\nu w\leq 0&\text{ in }\\
w\geq 0&\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}\setminus\Omega}\\
\lim_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}w\geq 0&\end{cases}

then
w\geq 0
in
\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

The proof is rather standard. If
w<0
somewhere, as
w
is nonnegative at infinity, we can take
x_{0}
a negative minimum of
w
. Thus,
w^{\prime}(x_{0})=0
and

\displaystyle\Delta^{s}w(x_{0})=\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{u(y)-u(x_{0})}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y>0.

This implies that

0\geq\Delta^{s}w(x_{0})+cw^{\prime}(x_{0})-\nu w(x_{0})\geq\Delta^{s}w(x_{0})>0,

a contradiction.
∎

Next we state the weak and strong Harnack inequalities. The weak version is from
[
15
]
(see Remark 2 therein) and gives an explicit dependence on the radius.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
R>0
and
b,c\in C(B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R})\cap L^{\infty}(B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R})
. There exist a constant
C>0
depending on
\|c\|_{L^{\infty}}
,
\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}
and

such that, if

is a solution to

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}v+cv^{\prime}+bv\leq 0&\text{ in }{B_{R+1}\setminus B_{R}}\\

then

\|v\|_{L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(1+R^{1+2s})\inf_{B_{R+\frac{3}{4}}\setminus B_{R+\frac{1}{4}}}v.

The strong Harnack inequality is given in the unit ball, as we will not be using its dependence on the radius.

Proposition 2.3

(Theorem 3.2 in
[
13
]
)
.

Let
b,c,f\in L^{\infty}(B_{1})
. Let
u\geq 0
be a continuous function in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and suppose that

\Delta^{s}u+cu+bu=f

in the viscosity sense. Assume that

, then there exists a constant
C
such that

u(x)\leq C\left(u(0)+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}}\right)\quad x\in B_{1/2}.

We finish this section by constructing a function which will serve as a useful barrier throughout the text. The following lemma is an adaptation of Lemma 4.3 of
[
13
]
, here we extend it to the range
s>\frac{1}{2}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\kappa>0
,
\beta\in(1,1+2s]
. Define
\Phi(x)=\min\{\kappa|x|^{2s-1},|x|^{-\beta}\}
and denote
r_{\kappa}=\kappa^{-\frac{1}{2s-1+\beta}}
. Then

\Delta^{s}\Phi(x)\leq\begin{cases}\frac{(\kappa-1)|x|^{2s-1}}{2s-1}\left(\frac{1}{|x+r_{\kappa}|^{2s}}+\frac{1}{|x-r_{\kappa}|^{2s}}\right)&0<|x|<r_{\kappa}\\
C(\beta)\kappa^{\theta}\Phi(x)&|x|>r_{\kappa}\end{cases}

where
\theta=\frac{2s}{2s-1+\beta}
.

Proof.

We will compute it directly. If
0<|x|<r_{\kappa}
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\kappa\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{-r_{\kappa}}^{r_{\kappa}}\frac{|y|^{2s-1}-|x|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})}\frac{\Phi(y)-|x|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\kappa\Delta^{s}(|x|^{2s-1})+\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})}\frac{\Phi(y)-\kappa|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+(\kappa-1)|x|^{2s-1}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})}\frac{\mathrm{d}y}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}

\displaystyle\leq\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})}\frac{\Phi(y)-\kappa|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\frac{(\kappa-1)|x|^{2s-1}}{2s-1}\left(\frac{1}{|x+r_{\kappa}|^{2s}}+\frac{1}{|x-r_{\kappa}|^{2s}}\right)

since
\Delta^{s}(|x|^{2s-1})=0
,
x\mapsto|x|^{2s-1}
being the fundamental solution to the fractional Laplacian in
\mathbb{R}
(see
[
7
]
). and
\Phi(y)-\kappa|y|^{2s-1}\leq 0
outside
(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})
.

If
|x|>r_{\kappa}
:

\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-r_{\kappa},r_{\kappa})}\frac{|y|^{-\beta}-|x|^{-\beta}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{-r_{\kappa}}^{r_{\kappa}}\frac{\Phi(y)-|x|^{-\beta}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\Delta^{s}(|x|^{-\beta})+\intop\nolimits_{-r_{\kappa}}^{r_{\kappa}}\frac{\Phi(y)-|y|^{-\beta}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta)|x|^{-2s}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle\leq C(\beta)r_{\kappa}^{-2s}\Phi(x)

\displaystyle=C(\beta)\kappa^{\frac{2s}{2s-1+\beta}}\Phi(x),

which gives the desired formula.
∎

.

We will use only

We want to find

3.
A priori estimates

In this section we obtain regularity and decay properties of solutions to the equation (
TW
). These will be essential for the existence and nonexistence results in the following sections.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
u
be a nonnegative bounded solution to (
TW
). Then

\|u\|_{C^{2s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\|u\|_{\infty}+\|f(x,u)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})})

for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathbb{R}
and consider
B=B_{1}(x)
. By the
C^{1,\alpha}
interior regularity of the operator
\Delta^{s}+c\partial_{x}
, we have

|u^{\prime}(y)|\leq C(|c|)(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{1}(x))}+\|f(x,u)\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1}(x))})\leq C_{0}(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\|f(x,u)\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall y\in B.

As the right hand side does not depend on
x
, we have
u^{\prime}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
.

By Proposition 2.9 in
[
19
]
, we have, for any
\beta<2s-1
,

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\|u\|_{\infty}+\|f(x,u)\|_{\infty}+c\|u^{\prime}\|_{\infty}).

Using well known interpolation inequalities, see e.g. chapter 6 in
[
16
]
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, it holds

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\varepsilon\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}}+C_{\varepsilon}(\|u\|_{\infty}+\|f(x,u)\|_{\infty}),

which yields, taking
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
,

(4)

\|u\|_{C^{1,\beta}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\|u\|_{\infty}+\|f(x,u)\|_{\infty}).

Fix
\beta\in(0,2s-1)
, and take
\alpha=\min\{\beta,\gamma\}
, where
\gamma\in(0,1)
is such that
f(x,u)
is
\gamma
-Hölder continuous. Now, Proposition 2.8 in
[
19
]
implies

\|u\|_{C^{2s+\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\|u\|_{\infty}+\|f(x,u)\|_{C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})}+c\|u^{\prime}\|_{C^{0,\alpha}}).

Applying (
4
) to the last inequality yields the result.
∎

The next lemma goes in the spirit of Lemma 3.2 in
[
17
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
v\in C^{1,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})\cap W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
for some
\alpha\in(2s-1,1)
. Then

\sup_{R>0}\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\Delta^{s}v(x)\mathrm{d}x\leq C(\|v\|_{\infty}+\|v^{\prime}\|_{\infty})

Proof.

First, assume
v\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Let
R>0
, thus

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\Delta^{s}v(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{v(x+y)-v(x)}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x+\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}}\frac{v(x+y)-v(x)}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=I_{1}+I_{2}

We bound each
I_{i}
separately. First, by the fundamental theorem of calculus (FTC) we write

v(x+y)-v(x)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(x+ty)y\mathrm{d}t,

thus

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{v(x+y)-v(x)}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\frac{v^{\prime}(x+ty)y}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{y}{|y|^{1+2s}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}v^{\prime}(x+ty)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{y}{|y|^{1+2s}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}(v(ty+R)-v(ty-R))\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq 2\|v\|_{\infty}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}^{c}}\frac{1}{|y|^{2s}}\mathrm{d}y<\infty,

because
2s>1
.

On the other hand, by applying the FTC to the function
g(r)=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(x+try)y\mathrm{d}t
we have

v(x+y)-v(x)-v^{\prime}(x)y=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}v^{\prime}(x+ty)y-v^{\prime}(x)y\mathrm{d}t=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}v^{\prime\prime}(x+try)ty^{2}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t

which yields, recalling that
\intop\nolimits_{-1}^{1}v^{\prime}(x)y\mathrm{d}y=0
,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}}\frac{v(x+y)-v(x)-v^{\prime}(x)y}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\intop\nolimits_{B_{1}}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\intop\nolimits_{0}^{1}\frac{ty^{2}v^{\prime\prime}(x+try)}{|y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}r\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}x

4.
Eigenvalues

In this section, we describe some properties of the principal eigenvalue for nonlocal operators.

Definition 2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be an open set,
c\in\mathbb{R}
and
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. We define

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega):=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\

\displaystyle\exists\psi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{1}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathbb{R}),\psi>0\text{ in }\Omega,\psi\geq 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega

\displaystyle\text{such that }\mathcal{L}_{c,a}\psi+\lambda\psi\leq 0\text{ in }\Omega,\text{ in the viscosity sense}\}

Remark 2
.

When the domain is
\mathbb{R}
, we will simply write
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a}):=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\mathbb{R})
. And when
a=\partial_{s}f(x,0)
, we will write
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\mathbb{R})
.

Remark 3
.

It is proven in
[
20
]
that, for smooth and bounded
\Omega
,
\lambda_{1}
admits the characterization

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega):=\sup\{\lambda\in\mathbb{R}:\penalty\ \penalty\ -\mathcal{L}_{c,a+\lambda}\text{ satisfies the maximum principle in }\Omega\},

and, since we have a linear operator, this characterization is still valid if one replaces the maximum principle by the minimum principle.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval,
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})
. Then, there exists
\psi\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that

\begin{cases}\mathcal{L}_{c,a}\psi=-\lambda_{1}\psi&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi>0&\text{in }\Omega,\\
\psi=0&\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Proof.

Define
\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\sigma}:=\mathcal{L}_{c,a}-\sigma
. For
\sigma\geq\sup a
, the operator
(-\tilde{\mathcal{L}})^{-1}:C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})\rightarrow C_{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is well defined by the existence theorem of
[
18
]
, is compact by the Hölder regularity up to the boundary of
[
20
]
, and sends the closed convex cone of nonnegative functions into its interior by the weak and strong maximum principles (recall that
a-\sigma\leq 0
).

By the Krein-Rutman theorem, there exists
\lambda^{*}\in\mathbb{R}
and
\psi\in C^{\alpha}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\psi>0
in
\Omega
, such that

\begin{cases}-\tilde{\mathcal{L}}_{\sigma}\psi=\lambda^{*}\psi&\text{en }\\
\psi=0&\text{en }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Arguing as in
[
20
]
, we conclude
\lambda_{1}=\lambda^{*}-\sigma
.
∎

The following properties are all easy consequences of the definition of
\lambda_{1}
, and we omit their proofs.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\Omega,{}_{1},{}_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}
be intervals, and
a,a_{1},a_{2}
be continuous functions. The following properties hold.

(i)

If
{}_{1}\subset{}_{2}
, then
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},{}_{1})\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},{}_{2})
.

(ii)

If
a_{1}(x)\geq a_{2}(x)
for all
x\in\Omega
, then
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a_{1}},\Omega)\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a_{2}},\Omega)
.

(iii)

|\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a_{1}},\Omega)-\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a_{2}},\Omega)|\leq\|a_{1}-a_{2}\|_{\infty}
.

(iv)

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)\geq-\sup a
.

The principal eigenvalue in unbounded domains is a delicate subject even in the second order case. Nevertheless, our definition lets us conclude the existence of a positive eigenfunction.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval and
a\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
let
({}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an increasing sequence of bounded intervals such that
{}_{n}\nearrow\Omega
. Then

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},{}_{n})=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega).

Moreover, there exists a function
\varphi\in C(\overline{\Omega})\cap C^{\alpha}(\Omega)
such that
\mathcal{L}_{c,a}\varphi=-\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)\varphi
in
\Omega
and
\varphi\equiv 0
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus\Omega
when
\Omega\neq\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Denoting
\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},{}_{n})
, let us first prove that

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda:=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega).

The proof follows the same lines as that of Theorem 1.1 in
[
13
]
: note first that

-\sup_{\mathbb{R}}a\leq\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},{}_{0}).

Since
\lambda_{n}
is a nonincreasing sequence bounded from below,
\lambda^{*}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
is well defined.

Let
\psi_{n}
be the eigenfunction associated to
\lambda_{n}
, which exists because

is bounded. We choose
x_{0}\in{}_{0}
, and normalize
\psi_{n}(x_{0})=1
. Using Proposition
2.3
and the fact that the coefficients are bounded, we get
\|\psi_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\leq C
for any

compact. By the regularity of nonlocal equations with a drift from
[

\psi_{n}
is bounded in

.

Now, by the stability of viscosity solutions
[

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}\psi+c\cdot\nabla\psi+(a+\lambda^{*})\psi=0&\text{ in }\\
\psi>0&\text{ en }\\
\psi=0&\text{ en }\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

By definition of
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)
, this implies
\lambda^{*}\leq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)
. But as one has
\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)
, the proposition follows.
∎

The definition and the three propositions hold in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
with minor modifications.

The next will be a key proposition in proving uniqueness and nonexistence for the traveling wave equation, as it concludes the existence of a subsolution to an adjoint equation. It is the adaptation of Proposition 6.1 in

to our case. Recall

Proposition 4.4
.

Suppose there exists
v\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
such that

is a viscosity subsolution to

(8)

\Delta^{s}v(x)+cv^{\prime}(x)+f(x,v(x))\geq 0,\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ for a.e. x in }\mathbb{R};

then, defining

, we have
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,b})\leq 0
.

Furthermore, there exists
\varphi\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that

\mathcal{L}_{-c,b}\varphi=\Delta^{s}\varphi-c\varphi^{\prime}+b\varphi\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }\mathbb{R},

and such that
\Delta^{s}\varphi\in L^{\infty}
.

Proof.

By contradiction, suppose
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,b})=:\lambda_{1}>0
. By definition, this implies that for any
\rho\in(0,\lambda_{1})
there exists
\psi
such that
(\mathcal{L}_{c,b}+\rho)\psi<0
in
\mathbb{R}
. By hypothesis of
v
, we have that
(\mathcal{L}_{c,b}+\rho)v\geq\rho v>0
. As
v
is
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and at least
C^{1}
, it follows that
v\rightarrow 0
as
|x|\rightarrow\infty
.

By (
H1
) we have that
\frac{f(x,s)}{s}<-\nu
for
|x|>R_{0}
and any
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Fix now
\rho<\min\{\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,b}),\nu\}
, so that
b+\rho<0
outside
[R_{0},R_{0}]
. Then, as
v
is bounded and
\psi>0
, for
C
large enough we have
v<C\psi
in
[-R_{0},R_{0}]
. We can then apply Proposition
2.1
and the strong maximum principle to
v-C\psi
, which satisfies

(9)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}w+cw^{\prime}+(b+\rho)w>0&\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}},\\
w\leq 0&\text{ inside }[-R_{0},R_{0}],\\
\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}w\leq 0,\end{cases}

to conclude that
v<C\psi
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]
.

We claim that
v<\tau\psi
for every
\tau\in(0,C]
. Aiming to use the sliding method, define

T:=\inf\{t>0:v<t\psi\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\}.

If
T>0
, then we have
v\leq T\psi
in
\mathbb{R}
. Posing
\tilde{w}=v-T\psi
, we have that
\tilde{w}
satisfies (
9
), so that, by the strong maximum principle,
v<T\psi
in
\mathbb{R}\setminus[-R_{0},R_{0}]
. Denote
S=\frac{1}{T}<\infty
which satisfies

S=\sup\{s>0:\psi>tv\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\}.

Set
\tilde{\psi}=\psi-Sv
, which is a strict supersolution to
\mathcal{L}_{c,b+\rho}\tilde{\psi}<0

\mathbb{R}
. By applying Proposition
2.1
we get
\tilde{\psi}>0

[-R_{0},R_{0}]

\tilde{\psi}>0

C_{0}
such that

[-R_{0},R_{0}]
. This implies, as before, that

in
\mathbb{R}
, that is

\psi>\left(S+\frac{1}{C_{0}}\right)v

contradicting the maximality of
S
, and then the minimality of
T
. We conclude

.

Thus, we get
0<v\leq 0
, a contradiction. This proves
\lambda_{1}\leq 0
.

For the second claim, observe first that
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,b})=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{-c,b})
, since by taking
\varphi_{1}^{c}
and
\varphi_{1}^{-c}
the corresponding eigenfunctions of
\lambda_{1}^{c}
and
\lambda_{1}^{-c}

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta^{s}\varphi_{1}^{c}+c(\varphi_{1}^{c})^{\prime}+(b+\lambda_{1}^{c})\varphi_{1}^{c})\varphi_{1}^{-c}=0=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(\Delta^{s}\varphi_{1}^{-c}+c(\varphi_{1}^{-c})^{\prime}+(b+\lambda_{1}^{-c})\varphi_{1}^{-c})\varphi_{1}^{c}

and so integration by parts leads to

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}(\lambda_{1}^{c}-\lambda_{1}^{-c})\varphi_{1}^{c}\varphi_{1}^{-c}=0,

that is,
\lambda_{1}^{c}=\lambda_{1}^{-c}
.

By the above discussion, we have
\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{-c,b})\leq 0
. Take
(R_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
R_{n}\nearrow\infty
and define
\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{-c,b},(-R_{n},R_{n}))
. By monotonicity,
\lambda_{n}\searrow\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{-c,b})\leq 0
, so for some

large we have

in
(-R_{n},R_{n})

Now take
\varphi_{n}

\varphi_{n}\in C([-R_{n},R_{n}])\cap C_{loc}^{\alpha}(-R_{n},R_{n})
with

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}\varphi_{n}-c\varphi_{n}^{\prime}+(b+\lambda_{n})\varphi_{n}=0&\text{ in }{(-R_{n},R_{n})}\\
\varphi_{n}=0&\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}\setminus(-R_{n},R_{n})},\end{cases}

normalized to have
\sup_{(-R_{0},R_{0})}\varphi_{n}=1
.

Take
\Phi
as given by Corollary
2.5
, such that

\Delta^{s}\Phi+c\Phi^{\prime}-\frac{\nu}{2}\leq 0\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\setminus[-\tilde{R},\tilde{R}],

and take a smooth mollifier

\Phi\star\eta_{\varepsilon}>0

For

. This implies that, for

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}(C\Phi)-c(C\Phi)^{\prime}+(b+\lambda_{n})C\Phi\leq 0&\text{ in }(-R_{n},-\tilde{R})\cup(\tilde{R},R_{n})\\
\Delta^{s}(\varphi_{n})-c(\varphi_{n})^{\prime}+(b+\lambda_{n})\varphi_{n}=0&\text{ in }(-R_{n},-\tilde{R})\cup(\tilde{R},R_{n}),\end{cases}

and so

With this, we may use the local regularity theory to conclude that

is bounded in

Lastly, as

4.1.
The influence of the drift

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded domain. Then, the function
c\mapsto\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)
is continuous.

Proof.

Let
(c_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence converging to
c
. Denote
\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c_{n},a},\Omega)
. By Theorem
4.1
, there exist a sequence
(\varphi_{n})\subset C^{\beta}(\overline{\Omega})\cap C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2s+\alpha}(\Omega)
of eigenfunctions associated to
\lambda_{n}
, normalized to have
\varphi_{n}(x_{0})=1
for some
x_{0}\in\Omega
fixed.

Let us first prove that
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is bounded. By Proposition
4.2
(iv), it suffices to provide an upper bound. Fix
\sigma>\|a\|_{\infty}
, so that
a-\sigma<0
. As
\Omega
is open, there exist some open set
A
whose
\varepsilon
-enlargement
A_{\varepsilon}
is compactly contained in
\Omega
. Take
h
a smooth function such that
0\leq h\leq 1
,
h\equiv 1
in
A
and
h\equiv 0
outside
A_{\varepsilon}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
v_{n}
be the solution to

(10)

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}v+c_{n}v^{\prime}+(a-\sigma)v=h&\text{ in }\\
v=0&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\setminus\Omega.\end{cases}

Since
h\geq 0
, we have
v_{n}\leq 0
by the maximum principle, and as
h\nequiv 0

v_{n}<0

A_{\varepsilon}

\sup_{A_{\varepsilon}}v_{n}<0

\rho_{n}=\frac{-1}{\sup_{A_{\varepsilon}}v_{n}}<\infty

\Delta^{s}v_{n}+c_{n}v^{\prime}+(a-\sigma+\rho_{n})v_{n}\leq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }\Omega.

This implies that
-\mathcal{L}_{c_{n},a-\sigma+\rho_{n}}
does not satisfy the maximum principle and thus
\lambda_{n}\leq\rho_{n}-\sigma
.

Recalling that
c_{n}\rightarrow c
, it holds that
v_{n}\rightarrow v
, where
v
is a solution to (
10
) with
c_{n}
replaced by
c
. Again by the weak and the strong maximum principle,

so that
\rho=(-\max_{A_{\varepsilon}}v)^{-1}<\infty
. As
\rho_{n}\rightarrow\rho
, in particular
\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\rho_{n}\leq C<\infty
. This implies that
\lambda_{n}\leq C-\sigma

Now, up to a subsequence,
\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda^{*}
. By regularity,
\varphi_{n}
is bounded in

uniformly in

for every

\varphi>0
in
\Omega
. As viscosity solutions are stable under uniform convergence,
\varphi
is a solution to

\begin{cases}\Delta^{s}\varphi+c\varphi^{\prime}+(a+\lambda^{*})\varphi=0&\text{ in }\\
\varphi>0&\text{ in }\\
\varphi=0&\text{ in }{\mathbb{R}^{N}\setminus\Omega}.\end{cases}

This implies that
\lambda^{*}=\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)
.

This last argument is valid for any subsequence of

\lambda_{n}\rightarrow\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,a},\Omega)

∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
b:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be such that
\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}b(x)<0
and
\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}}b(x)>0
. Then there exists

such that for any

|c|>c^{*}

\lambda_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{c,b})>0

Proof.

Let

. By hypothesis, there exist
\nu,R_{0}>0
such that
b(x)<-\nu
for
|x|>R_{0}
. Without loss of generality, we can take
R_{0}>r
. Define

\varphi(x)=\begin{cases}|x|^{2s-1}&\text{ if }x<-r\\
p(x)&\text{ if }x\in(-r,-r+\varepsilon)\\
(x+R)^{-\beta}&\text{ if }x>-r+\varepsilon\end{cases}

where
R,r>0
,
R>r
, and

is a polynomial chosen in order to have

and
\varphi^{\prime}<0
in
\mathbb{R}

such that

For

\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P.V.}\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{-r}\frac{|y|^{2s-1}-|x|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{-r}^{\infty}\frac{\varphi(y)-\varphi(x)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\Delta^{s}(|x|^{2s-1})+\intop\nolimits_{-r}^{-r+\varepsilon}\frac{p(y)-|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{-r+\varepsilon}^{\infty}\frac{(R+y)^{-\beta}-|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq\intop\nolimits_{-r}^{-r+\varepsilon}\frac{p(y)-|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{-r+\varepsilon}^{r}\frac{(R+y)^{-\beta}-|y|^{2s-1}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{c,b}\varphi(x)\leq\left(\frac{T_{1}(x)}{|x|^{2s-1}}+\frac{c(2s-1)}{x}+b(x)\right)\varphi(x)\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall x<-r.

By our choice of parameters,

\mathcal{L}_{c,b}\varphi(x)\leq\left(\frac{c(2s-1)}{x}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\varphi,\text{ for }x<-R_{1}.

For
x>-r+\varepsilon

\displaystyle\Delta^{s}\varphi(x)

\displaystyle=\Delta^{s}(|\cdot|^{-\beta})(R+x)+\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{-r+\varepsilon}\frac{\varphi(y)-|y+R|^{-\beta}}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\frac{A(\beta)}{(R+x)^{\beta+2s}}+T_{2}(x),

Thus,

\mathcal{L}_{c,b}\varphi(x)\leq\left(\frac{A(\beta)}{(R+x)^{2s}}+(x+R)^{\beta}T_{2}(x)-\frac{c\beta}{x+R}+b(x)\right)\varphi(x)\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall x>-r+\varepsilon.

Note that for
x>0

\displaystyle\leq\intop\nolimits_{-\infty}^{-r}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{2}}\mathrm{d}y+\|p-|\cdot+R|^{-\beta}\|_{C([-r,-r+\varepsilon])}\intop\nolimits_{-r}^{-r+\varepsilon}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

. Plugging this in the last inequality,

(12)

\mathcal{L}_{c,b}\varphi(x)\leq\left(-\frac{c\beta}{x+R}-\frac{\nu}{2}\right)\varphi(x),\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall x>R_{2}.

Finally, for

\varphi>0
,
\varphi^{\prime}<0

\begin{array}[]{cc}m:=\inf_{[-R_{1},R_{2}]}\varphi>0&d:=\inf_{[-R_{1},R_{2}]}-\varphi^{\prime}>0,\end{array}

\mathcal{L}_{c,b}\varphi(x)\leq\left(\frac{\Delta^{s}\varphi(x)}{\varphi(x)}+c\frac{\varphi^{\prime}(x)}{\varphi(x)}+b(x)\right)\varphi(x)\leq\left(\frac{\|\Delta^{s}\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}(-R_{1},R_{2})}}{m}-c\frac{d}{m}+\sup_{\mathbb{R}}b\right)\varphi(x).

Proposition 5.1

Assume

On the other hand,

Proposition 5.2

Take

5.2.

Proposition 5.3

Proof.

, implies the existence of
\varphi
a classical solution to

(15)

\Delta^{s}\varphi-c\varphi^{\prime}+\frac{f(x,v)}{v}\varphi\geq 0\penalty\ \text{ in }\mathbb{R}.

Multiply (
15
) by
u_{1}
and integrate over
\mathbb{R}
to get

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}u_{1}\Delta^{s}\varphi-cu_{1}\varphi^{\prime}+\frac{f(x,v)}{v}u_{1}\varphi\geq 0.

By the regularity and integrability properties of
u_{1}
and
\varphi
, we can integrate by parts to obtain

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi\Delta^{s}u_{1}+c\varphi u_{1}^{\prime}+\frac{f(x,v)}{v}u_{1}\varphi\geq 0,

which, using the equation satisfied by
u_{1}
, implies

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{f(x,v)}{v}-\frac{f(x,u_{1})}{u_{1}}\right)u_{1}\varphi\geq 0.

Recall that
s\mapsto\frac{f(x,s)}{s}
is strictly decreasing, so if
v(x_{0})\neq u_{1}(x_{0})
for some
x_{0}
, by continuity we must have
v\neq u_{1}
in a set of positive measure. The fact that
u_{1},\varphi>0
implies

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\frac{f(x,v)}{v}-\frac{f(x,u_{1})}{u_{1}}\right)u_{1}\varphi<0,

a contradiction, and the proposition follows.
∎

6.
Long time behavior

In what follows,

C_{\mathrm{ub}}(\mathbb{R})=\{u:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}:u\text{ is uniformly continuous and bounded}\}.

By the general existence and uniqueness theory for Feller semigroups (see
[
6
]
, Section 2.3) and hypotheses (
H0
) and (
H2
), for any
u_{0}\in C_{\mathrm{ub}}
there exists a unique solution
u(t,x)
to (
1
).

Note that any solution
v
to (
1
) gives way to a solution
v_{c}
to (
16
) by posing
v_{c}(x,t)=v(x+ct,t)
.

(16)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}u=\Delta^{s}u+cu^{\prime}+f(x,u)&(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}\times(0,\infty),\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}&x\in\mathbb{R}.\end{cases}

We will deduce the behavior of equation (
1
) by studying suitable solutions to (
16
).

We begin by stating a comparison principle for the nonlinear equation. Its proof is rather standard, we provide it here for convenience of the reader.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
u,v\in C^{1}([0,\infty),C_{ub}(\mathbb{R}))
,
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and let
f:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be continuous in the first variable and globally Lipschitz with respect to the second variable, uniformly with respect to the first. Then

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}v\geq\Delta^{s}v+cv_{x}+f(x,v)&\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\
\partial_{t}u\leq\Delta^{s}u+cu_{x}+f(x,u)&\text{ in }(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}\\
u(0,\cdot)\leq v(0,\cdot)&\text{ in }\mathbb{R}\\
\displaystyle\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(t,x)\leq\displaystyle\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}v(t,x)&\text{ uniformly in }t\in[0,T],\penalty\ \penalty\ \forall T>0\end{cases}

implies
u\leq v
in
[0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Note that the operator
A=(-\Delta)^{s}-c\partial_{x}
generates a strongly continuous Feller semigroup as a consequence of the heat kernel estimates proven in
[
3
]
.

Denote
M
as the Lipschitz constant of
f
. For
\lambda>0
, define
w(t,x)=e^{-\lambda t}(u-v)
. It is easy to see that
w
satisfies

\displaystyle\partial_{t}w+(-\Delta)^{s}w-c\partial_{x}w

\displaystyle\leq e^{-\lambda t}(f(x,u)-f(x,v))-\lambda w

\displaystyle\leq Mw-\lambda w

for all
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
. Taking
\lambda>M
, we have that
\partial_{t}w+(-\Delta)^{s}w-cw^{\prime}\leq 0
. Furthermore,
w(0,\cdot)\leq 0
and

\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}w(t,x)\leq\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(t,x)-\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}v(t,x)\leq 0

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for any
T>0
. We can then apply the maximum principle from
[
6
]
, Proposition 2.8, to conclude that
w\leq 0
in
[0,\infty)
, i.e.
u\leq v
.
∎

Remark 6
.

The proof of Proposition
6.1
also shows that if
v(t,x)
is a solution to (
16
), then it cannot have a minimum in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
. In particular, if
u_{0}
is nonnegative and nontrivial, then
u_{c}(t)>0
for any
t>0
. Indeed, by the same argument as above, it suffices to prove it for
v
solution to
v_{t}\geq\Delta^{s}v+c\partial_{x}v
. If
(t_{0},x_{0})
is a negative minimum of
v
, then
\partial_{t}v(t_{0},x_{0})=\partial_{x}v(x_{0},t_{0})=0
and
\Delta^{s}v(t_{0},x_{0})>0
, and one concludes as in Proposition
2.1
.

The following corollary is key in proving the long time behavior of the parabolic problem.

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
u_{c}(x,t)
be a solution to (
16
) with nonnegative initial condition
u_{0}\in C_{ub}(\mathbb{R})
. If
u_{0}
is a supersolution to (
TW
), then
u
is nonincreasing in time. Conversely, if
u_{0}
is a subsolution to (
TW
),
u
is nondecreasing in time.

Proof.

It suffices to show the first statement, as the second is analogous to the first. Take
v(x,t)=u_{0}(x)
, so
\partial_{t}v=0
in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
and thus
\partial_{t}v\geq\Delta^{s}v+c\partial_{x}v+f(x,v)
since
u_{0}
is a supersolution to (
TW
). Let
T>0
, and we claim that

(17)

\limsup_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u(t,x)\leq 0\leq\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}u_{0}(x)

uniformly in
t\in[0,T]
for any
T>0
. Indeed, by Theorem 2 in
[
3
]
, there exist
C>0
such that the heat kernel
p(t,x)
of
A=(-\Delta)^{s}-c\partial_{x}
is bounded by

\frac{C^{-1}t}{t^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x|^{1+2s}}\leq p(t,x)\leq\frac{Ct}{t^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x|^{1+2s}}

for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
,
t\in[0,T]
.

By the Duhamel formula, we know that
u
satisfies

(18)

u(x,t)=P_{t}u_{0}(x)+\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}f(\cdot,u(s,\cdot))\mathrm{d}s

where
P_{t}
is the semigroup generated by
A
and has the representation formula

P_{t}w(x)=\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}p(t,x-y)w(y)\mathrm{d}y

and is such that
\|P_{t}w-w\|_{\infty}\rightarrow 0
as
t\searrow 0
. Take
t_{0}>0
such that
\|P_{t}w-w\|_{\infty}\leq\frac{\delta}{4}
for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
. Therefore, (
17
) will hold for
t\in[0,t_{0}]
.

For
t\in[t_{0},T]
, we can bound each of the terms in (
18
) as follows. For the homogeneous part
P_{t}u_{0}(x)
we have

\displaystyle P_{t}u_{0}(x)

\displaystyle\leq C\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{tu_{0}(y)}{t^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{T}{t_{0}^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y

for any
L\geq R
. This last integral is finite, so we can choose
R_{1}
such that for
|x|>R_{1}

\intop\nolimits_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{T}{t_{0}^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\leq\frac{\delta}{C\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}}.

Thus, for
t\in[t_{0},T]
and
|x|>R_{1}
, we have

P_{t}u_{0}(x)\leq\delta.

For the inhomogeneous part, recall that for
|y|>R_{0}
,
f(y,s)\leq-\nu s
, so

\displaystyle\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}P_{t-s}f(\cdot,u(s,\cdot))\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\left(\intop\nolimits_{-R_{0}}^{R_{0}}p(t-s,x-y)f(y,u(s,y))\mathrm{d}y+\intop\nolimits_{(-R_{0},R_{0})^{c}}p(t-s,x-y)f(y,u(s,y))\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\intop\nolimits_{0}^{t}\intop\nolimits_{-R_{0}}^{R_{0}}\frac{(t-s)f(y,u(s,y))}{(t-s)^{\frac{1+2s}{2s}}+|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}s.

Since
f
is bounded on bounded sets, we can choose
R_{2}
such that for
|x|>R_{2}\geq R_{0}+1
, this integral is smaller than
\delta
. This will imply that

u(t,x)\leq 2\delta

and the claim (
17
) follows. Thus, we can apply the preceding proposition to conclude
u(t,x)\leq v(t,x)
for all
(t,x)\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
, and Remark
6
implies further that
u(t,x)<u_{0}(x)
in
(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
.

Now, fix
t>0
and define

T:=\sup\{s\geq 0:u_{c}(t,x)<u_{c}(\tau,x)\penalty\ \forall x\in\mathbb{R},\tau\in[0,s)\}.

Note that
0<T\leq t
. By contradiction, if
T<t
, then there exist
0<s_{0}<t
and
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
u_{c}(t,x_{0})=u_{c}(s_{0},x_{0})
and
u_{c}(t,x)<u_{c}(s,x)
for all
s<s_{0}
. Define
w(s,x)=v(s,x)-v(t,x)
, and thus
w_{t}=\Delta^{s}w+c\partial_{x}w+\left(\frac{f(x,u(s,x))-f(x,u(t,x))}{u(s,x)-u(t,x))}\right)w
, with
w
having a minimum at
(s_{0},x_{0})\in(0,\infty)\times\mathbb{R}
. By Remark
6
, this cannot be, so we must have
T=t
. This concludes the proof.
∎

We have the necessary tools to examine the behavior of solutions to (
1
).

Proof of Theorem
3
.

This proof follows the same lines as
[
2
]
, Theorem 4.11, and
[
11
]
, Section 8.

As a first step, let us prove that there is local uniform convergence. Note that if
u
is the solution to (
1
), then
u_{c}(t,x)=u_{c}(t,x+ct)
is the solution to (
16
) with the same initial condition.

Take
z_{c}(t,x)
as the solution to
\partial_{t}z=\Delta^{s}z+c\partial_{x}z+f(x,z)
with initial condition
z_{0}=C\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
, where
C>1
is chosen so large as to have
f(x,z_{0}(x))\leq 0
for all
x
. Recall that
z_{c}
exists by the fact that
A=(-\Delta)^{s}-c\partial_{x}
generates a strongly continuous Feller semigroup. By Corollary
6.2
, we see that
z_{c}
is nonincreasing in time. As
z_{c}
is nonnegative, we must have that
z_{c}(t,x)\searrow\overline{z}(x)
as
t\rightarrow\infty
, where
\overline{z}
is a nonnegative solution to (
TW
). Arguing as in the proof of Corollary
6.2
,
z_{0}\geq u_{0}
implies that
z_{c}\geq u_{c}\geq 0
, so that

(19)

\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}u_{c}(t,x)\leq\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}z_{c}(t,x).

If
\lambda_{1}\geq 0
, the only nonnegative solution is the trivial one, so we must have that
z_{c}(t,x)\rightarrow 0
as
t\rightarrow\infty
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
, and by (
19
) we must have
u_{c}(t,x)\rightarrow 0
, and thus
u(x,t)\rightarrow 0
, for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

If
\lambda_{1}<0
, it remains to show that
\overline{z}
is not identically zero. By Remark
6
, if
u_{0}
is nontrivial, then
u(1,x)>0
and therefore
u_{c}(1,x)>0
. For any
R>0
there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that
u(1,x)>\varepsilon\varphi_{R}
in
\mathbb{R}
, where
\varphi_{R}
is the principal eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{c,a}
in
(-R,R)
. As discussed in the proof of Proposition
5.1
, we can choose
R>0
so that
\varepsilon\varphi_{R}
is a subsolution to (
TW
) in
(-R,R)
, and outside it is easy to see that

\Delta^{s}\varphi_{R}(x)+c\varphi_{R}^{\prime}(x)+f(x,\varphi_{R}(x))=\intop\nolimits_{-R}^{R}\frac{\varphi(y)}{|x-y|^{1+2s}}\mathrm{d}y>0

for
|x|>R
. Then,
\varphi_{R}
is a subsolution to (
TW
) in
\mathbb{R}
.

Take
h_{c}(t,x)
as the solution to (
16
) with initial condition
\varepsilon\varphi_{R}
, so that
h_{c}(t,x)\leq u(t+1,x)
and
h_{c}(\cdot,x)
is increasing. We get that
\overline{h}(x)=\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}h_{c}(t,x)
exists and satisfies

(20)

\overline{h}(x)\leq\liminf_{t\rightarrow\infty}u(x,t+1)\leq\limsup_{t\rightarrow\infty}u_{c}(t,x)\leq\overline{z}(x).

Moreover,
\overline{h}
is a solution to (
TW
) which is nontrivial by monotonicity of
h_{c}
. By uniqueness and (
20
), we obtain that
\overline{h}=\overline{z}=u_{\infty}
is the unique nontrivial solution to (
TW
) and therefore
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}u_{c}(x,t)
exists and coincides with
u_{\infty}
.

Let us now improve the convergence to make it uniform. By contradiction, suppose there exist
(t_{n}),\penalty\ (x_{n})\subset\mathbb{R}
and
\delta>0
such that
t_{n}\rightarrow\infty
and that

(21)

z(t_{n},x_{n})-u_{\infty}(x_{n})\geq\delta.

Define now

z_{n}(t,x):=z_{c}(t,x+x_{n}).

Suppose
(x_{n})
is bounded. Then, up to a subsequence, we may assume
x_{n}\rightarrow x_{\infty}
. By parabolic regularity estimates (from
[
10
]
, Theorem 5.2),
z_{n}
converges locally uniformly to some
z_{\infty}
, where
z_{\infty}
is a solution to

\partial_{t}z_{\infty}(t,x)=\Delta^{s}z_{\infty}(t,x)+c\partial_{x}z_{\infty}(t,x)+f(x+x_{\infty}),

which in turn converges, as
t\rightarrow\infty
, to a solution
\bar{z}_{\infty}
of

\Delta^{s}z_{\infty}(t,x)+c\partial_{x}z_{\infty}(t,x)+f(x+x_{\infty},z_{\infty}(t,x))=0.

By uniqueness, one must have
\bar{z}_{\infty}(x)=u_{\infty}(x+x_{\infty})
, in particular
\bar{z}_{\infty}(0)=u_{\infty}(x_{\infty})
. Rewriting (
21
) in terms of
z_{n}
, we have

z_{n}(t_{n},0)-u_{\infty}(x_{n})\geq\delta.

Taking the limit
n\rightarrow\infty
in this last inequality we arrive at a contradiction.

Thus,
(x_{n})
must be unbounded, say
|x_{n}|\rightarrow\infty
. For
n
sufficiently large, there is
\nu>0
such that
z_{n}
satisfies

\partial_{t}z_{n}\leq\Delta^{s}z_{n}+cz_{n}-\nu z_{n}.

Recall that
0<z_{n}(t,x)<C\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
, and again using parabolic regularity estimates we find that

\|z_{n}(t,\cdot)\|_{C^{2s+\alpha}}\leq C(\|u_{0}\|+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times[0,C\|u_{0}\|]}),

so that, up to a subsequence, one has
z_{n}\rightarrow z_{\infty}
, which will converge as
t\rightarrow\infty
to a solution of

\Delta^{s}\bar{z}_{\infty}+c\bar{z}_{\infty}-\nu\bar{z}_{\infty}\geq 0\penalty\ \penalty\ \text{ in }{\mathbb{R}}.

Arguing as in the proof of Proposition
2.1
it easy to see that
\bar{z}_{\infty}
cannot have a positive maximum, so we obtain that
\bar{z}_{\infty}\equiv 0
. But this cannot hold, because (
21
) implies, for
n
sufficiently large, that

0\leq u_{\infty}(x_{n})\leq-\frac{\delta}{2}<0.

We conclude that the convergence must be uniform in space.
∎

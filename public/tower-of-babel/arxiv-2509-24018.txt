. This is an expository paper aimed to outline the current situation with problems related with the occurrence of eigenvalue 1 1 of elements in linear groups and group representations.

1.
Introduction

In the theory of transformation groups a significant role belongs to the study of invariants, that is, the objects remaining unchanged under the group action. In group representations the invariants are the non-zero underlying space vectors fixed by every group element. A more specific problem is the study of invariants for individual group elements in group representations, that is, invariants in this case are the non-zero elements of the 1-eigenspace of an element in question. Many concrete questions came from applications, whereas a systematic study is still at
the infant level I think.

We restrict our discussion to the elements
g\in GL_{n}(F)
,
F
is a field, that are diagonalizable under a field extension. They are called semisimple and, if
g
is of finite order
|g|
, are exactly those with
|g|
coprime to the characteristic of
F
. Properties of non-semisimple linear group elements is another important area of intensive study, which we do not discuss in this survey. Note that this article can be viewed as a continuation of author’s expository paper
[
76
]
. In this survey we outline recent achievements and new areas of study.

The area of studing eigenvalues is very wide and I start from general overview. Currently
there are several projects which aim on understanding regularities related with
eigenvalues of elements of finite linear groups and
group representations. Ideally, given a finite linear group
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
,
one would wish to obtain full information on all eigenvalues of all elements
g\in G
.
This is possible for many groups of bounded order, for instance, those listed in the
Atlas of Brauer characters
[
46
]
of finite groups. Some more groups can be dealt with
using computer programs available in famous systems GAP, MAGMA and CHEVIE.
This approach is limited by computer powers. Obtaining regularities at the first stage
requires specifying situations where strong regularities exist, and stating certain conjectures.

In this survey I try to outline areas of intensive research and give short comments
on main achievements. I do not definitely pretend to be complete.

I usually assume that
F
is algebraically closed; this is not a significant assumption as the general case to large extent reduces to this one. The case
F
of characteristic 0 is much easier and regularities are much stronger. However, I mostly assume
F
to be of arbitrary characteristic
\ell
.

In the most general context the problem of our discussion can be stated as follows:

Problem 1
.

Given an algebraically closed field
F
, a linear group
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
and an element
g\in G
, determine the Jordan normal form of
g
, in particular,
the eigenvalues of
g
and their multiplicities.

As mentioned above, there may be many ways to deal with Problem
1
if the triple
(F,n,G,g)
is explicitly given. However, we are interested with regularities which hold for many groups
and large classes of their elements. To reveil regularities, even at the level of a conjecture, one needs to specify the entries
(F,n,G,g)
or/and limit oneself with a less precise answer.
For instance, one can ask for determining the degree of the minimal polynomial of
g
or occurrence of eigenvalue 1 for every element of
G
or for specific class of them.

The most important restriction of general nature is that of irreducibility of
G
. This will be held in almost all cases considered below. Other natural restrictions considered in the literature are:

(i)

n
is small or of specific nature (for instant a prime);

(ii)

g
does not lie in a proper normal subgroup of
G
, equivalently,
G
is generated by the elements conjugate to
g;

(iii)

g
is a
p
-element for a prime
p
, or even
|g|=p;

(iv)

G
or
G^{\prime}
is quasisimple, sometimes specified to be an alternating or classical group or a group of Lie type;

(v)
the characteristic
\ell
of
F
does not divide
|G|;
if
G
is of Lie type one differs the cases where
\ell
equals or distinct from the defining characteristic of
G;

(vi)

g
has a simple spectrum, that is, all eigenvalues of
g
are of multiplicity 1; more generally, at most one eigenvalue of
g
is of multiplicity greater than
1;

(vii)
the degree
\deg g
of the minimal polynomial of
g
is less than
|g|
(or
o(g)
, the degree of
g
in
G/Z(G));

(viii)

g
does not have eigenvalue 1.

There are ¨uniform¨ (in a sense) versions of the above restrictions, for instance, one asks when all elements of
G
have eigenvalue 1.

One is also interested with lower and upper bounds for the eigenvalue multiplicities of certain elements. Similar questions are discussed for group representations.

In this paper I focus on problems and results that concern with eigenvalue 1 properties of
g\in G\subset GL_{n}(F)
in notation of Problem
1
, especially with the existence of eigenvalue 1.

2.
Motivation and some applications

In this section we briefly outline some areas of potential applications of results concerning properties of eigenvalue 1 of linear groups or group representations.

2.1.
Linear algebra and geometry

Formally, the eigenvalue study is a part of linear algebra. However, regularities of the eigenvalue behavior in
linear groups are mostly of very different nature. The facts that a linear group element
g

does not have eigenvalue 0, and the eigenvalues of
g
are
|g|
-roots of unity, are practically insignificant. Note that the study of eigenvalues of an element
g\in GL_{n}(F)=GL(V)
is equivalent to the study of cyclic group
\langle g\rangle
, more precisely, the study of irreducible constituents of
\langle g\rangle
, and their multiplicities.

This hints that one cannot expect strong regularities without assuming that
a group
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
in question is irreducible. So we assume below that elements
of interest belong to an irreducible subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
. Moreover, we assume that

G
is a proper subgroup, and does not contain
SL_{n}(F)
. In addition,
the assumption that
G
is a classical group is a very modest restriction on elements
g\in G
. Therefore, one usually excludes the cases where
G

contains the derived subgroup of a classical group on
V
.

The linear algebra view point suggests some natural questions on elements
g\in G\subset GL_{n}(F)
to be considered:

Question 1. What can be said on the minimal polynomials of elements
g\in G,
in particular, on
\deg g
?

Question 2. What can be said on the minimal and maximal multiplicity of the eigenvalues of
g
?

Question 3. When is 1 an eigenvalue of
g?
When does it occur with multiplicity 1?

Question 4. When are all eigenvalue multiplicities of
g
equal to 1?

Question 5. When is a certain
|g|
-root of unity an eigenvalue of
g
?

These questions specify some situations where well described regularities can be expected. There are variations of these questions that specify the classes of groups
G
(simple or solvable, say) or particular types of elements (of prime order, say). In the opposite direction
one can turn to a more uniform version asking when some eigenvalue property holds for all group elements or for a certain class of them. The following two problems illustrate this approach.

Problem 2
.

For an irreducible finite simple subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
and a prime
p
dividing
|G|
, when is the minimal polynomial degree
\deg g
of an element
g\in G
of order
p
less than
p
?

Note that if 1 is not an eigenvalue of
g
then
\deg g<p
. Therefore, one can single out
the following special case:

Problem 3
.

For an irreducible finite simple subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
and a prime
p
dividing
|G|
, when is
1
not an eigenvalue of an element
g\in G
of order
p
?

Let
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
be a finite irreducible group. Obviously,
\deg g\leq o(g)
. In general, the
situation where
\deg g=o(g)
is common, whereas
\deg g<o(g)
is exceptional.
The problem of describing all exceptions is hardly treatable. Problem
2

is still open, many special cases are resolved in
[
65
]
and
[
66
]
. Probably,
the following special case is one of most difficult:

Problem 4
.

Let
p
be a prime,
G=A_{p}
, the alternating group, and let
g\in G
be of order p. Let
F
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\ell\neq 0,p
. Determine the irreducible
F
-representations
\phi
of
G
such that
\deg\phi(p)<p
.

We expect that
\dim\phi<p
. Note that the case where
\ell>n/2
follows from a result by Thompson
[
64
, Lemma 3.2]
. In this case Sylow
\ell
-subgroups of
G
are cyclic.
Note that the general theory of representations for groups with cyclic
\ell
-subgroup has powerful tools to study the eigenvalue problems.

As we show below, the following vector space covering problem (stated explicitly in
[
11
]
) is partially connected with the eigenvalue 1 problem:

Problem 5
.

Let
H\subset GL(V)
be a subgroup and
W
a subspace of
V
over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. When is
V=\cup_{h\in H}h(W)
?

More generally, given a normal subgroup
N
of a group
G
, one asks
when
N=\cup_{g\in G}\,gKg^{-1}
for a proper subgroup
K
of
N
. If
N=G
then
N
never coincides with the union of the conjugates of
K
. This fact goes back to C. Jordan (1872).

Arithmetic algebraic geometry

Motivated by applications to a certain aspect of Galois theory and abelian varieties Katz

Problem 6

Let

Describe irreducible subgroups G of H whose every element has eigenvalue

Specifying

Problem 7

Describe irreducible finite subgroups

We call such

In general, Problem

Describe maximal
irreducible locally finite subgroups

Note that in Problem

The above example is related to the fact that the simple algebraic group

Another version of Problem

Problem 10

Describe minimal irreducible finite subgroups

2.3.
Game theory

Theorem 1
.

(Isbell
[

[

Let

In fact, there is a bijective correspondence between

Given a natural number

This problem is hardly treatable in full generality. Current experience is not sufficient in order to state any conjecture. Note that, for a given

In

the author shortly mentions a related problem: given a transitive permutation group

has a fixed point free

The existence of a derangement of

Linear groups over finite fields containing a fixed point free

Problem

2.4.
Group theory

There are many aspects of studying eigenvalues but those I shall discuss go back to Hall-Higman’s theorem on the minimal polynomial degrees of elements in representations of
p
-solvable groups.

Theorem 3
.

[
38
, Theorem B]
Let
\ell=p>2
, let
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
be a p-solvable group with no non-trivial normal p-subgroup and
g\in G
a p-elements of order
p^{a}
. Then the minimal polynomial degree of

is at least
(p-1)p^{a-1}
.

In this theorem

is an arbitrary field of characteristic
p
, in particular, it is true for
|F|=p
. This result plays a substantial role in finite group theory, and used in the proof of the classification theory of finite simple groups. So this result attracted a lot of attention, see
[
8
,
12
,
29
]
,
[
42
, Ch. IX, §2]
etc.

If
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
is an irreducible
p
-solvable group over an algebraically closed field

of characteristic
\ell>0
then
G
lifts to characteristic 0, see
[
27
, Ch. X, Theorem 2.1]
.

The notion of lifting plays an essential role in the study of eigenvalue problems of finite linear groups over fields of prime characteristic. One says that a finite subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)

is
liftable
(or that
G
lifts to characteristic 0) if
there is an isomorphism
\eta:G\rightarrow GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
such the
\ell
-Brauer character of
G
coincides on the
\ell^{\prime}
-elements with the character of
\eta(G)
. (Note that every finite
\ell^{\prime}
-subgroup of
GL_{n}(F)
is liftable.)
So if
g\in G
is an
\ell^{\prime}
-element then the eigenvalue multiplicities of
g
are the same as those of
\eta(g)
.

In turn, if
G\subset GL_{n}(\mathbb{C})
is a finite group then for every
\ell>0
there exists a homomorphism
\sigma:G\rightarrow GL_{n}(F)
such that
\deg g\geq\deg\sigma(g)
(a Brauer reduction of

Theorem

remains valid for

with

.

Therefore, for

extends to irreducible

-solvable group

with

over a field

of arbitrary characteristic

\deg g=\deg\eta(g)
if

and

\deg\eta(g)\geq\sigma(\eta(g))
.

Is the assumption of
G

p
-solvable substantial? At least one can state a general form problem:

(General Hall-Higman type problem)

For quasisimple groups

Similarly, we can state:

Problem 13

(General minimal polynomial problem)
Given an irreducible finite subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)

One can interpret Problem

One assumes that the ground field

Instead of climbing up on

Problem 14

Given a group
G
and an irreducible representation
\phi:G\rightarrow GL_{n}(F)
determine the minimal polynomial degrees of
\phi(g)
for
g\in G
.

Formally, Problems
13
and

are equivalent. However, one can single out series of groups

If
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
and an element

does not have eigenvalue 1 then
\deg g\leq|g|-1
. As we wish to focus to eigenvalue 1 problems, we single out this special case:

Problem 15
.

Given an irreducible finite subgroup
G\subset SL_{n}(F)
, determine the elements
g\in G
that have eigenvalue
1
.

There is an evidence based on certain experience
that vast majority of elements of
G
have eigenvalue value 1, and those without eigenvalue 1 are exceptional and can in principle be determined. However,
one has to exclude the cases where
G
is a quasisimple group of Lie type in characteristic
\ell
and the symmetric and alternating groups

and
A_{m}
with

. In this cases the situation is different, and one can turn to studying the proportion of the number of elements having eigenvalue 1.

The absence of eigenvalue 1 means that the element in question acts fixed point freely on the underlying vector space (formally, the zero vector is the only one fixed by
g
).
This property makes sense when one considers an action of one group
G

H
by automorphisms. If
g\in G
and and

, one says that
g
acts fixed point freely. There are a significant literature devoted to fixed point free automorphisms of solvable groups.

Problem
15

[
37
]

Problem 16
.

Given an irreducible finite subgroup
G\subset GL_{n}(F)
, compute the probability

for an element

to have eigenvalue

. Obtain a lower bound for

Note that

is the ratio

4.
Some observations on minimal polynomials and eigenvalue 1 of elements of linear groups

If

of

If

. One can easily verify the following elementary observation.

Lemma 7

Let

\ell

If

Irreducible finite quasisimple subgroups of

One can be interested in a more ambitious problem of determining full list of the eigenvalues of elements of a group in question. However, since a scalar multiple of a matrix has the same
minimal polynomial degree but not the same list of eigenvalues, Problems

and
14
have a more uniform solution, and hence better treatable than the problem of computing all eigenvalues. According with our experience, knowledge of

\deg g
allows to easily determine the eigenvalues themselves by a more or less routine computations.

Clearly, any result depends on the conjugacy class of an element, and by induction reason it is natural to assume that this does not lie in a proper normal subgroup of the group in question. There are some configurations where no reasonable regularities can be expected. As mentioned in section 2.1, this is the case for the general linear group
G=GL_{n}(F)
, where one can exhibit an element with almost arbitrary a priory possible minimal polynomial. (For instance, the minimal polynomial degree cannot exceed

. In addition, if

and, in a practical sense, the classical groups such as

and

for some subfield

of

To some extent this also concerns with tensor products of these groups such that

to be tensor-indecomposable, that is,

for

The following lemma reduces eigenvalue problems to much more special situations:

Lemma 8
.

Let

be a direct sum of vector spaces
over a field

, and let

be an element of prime power order permuting
transitively the subspaces

.
Set

and

(i)
Assume
(g,\ell)=1
. If

is an eigenvalue of

of multiplicity

is an eigenvalue of g of multiplicity

. (Observe

(ii)

\deg g=k\cdot\deg g^{k}
.

This fact leads to an observation often referred as Higman’s lemma (see

Lemma 9

Let

be a subgroup and

(|A|,\ell)=1

In fact, one can drop the assumption

and then replace the conclusion by

, where

. In order to deduce Lemma

from Lemma

.

5.1.
Simple algebraic groups

In this section we discuss results related to Problem
9
: determine unisingular irreducible representations of simple algebraic groups.

Note that the center of a simple algebraic group
\mathbf{G}
is finite and
\mathbf{G}/Z(\mathbf{G})
is simple as an abstract group. Among the simple algebraic groups
\mathbf{G}_{1}
with
\mathbf{G}_{1}/Z(\mathbf{G}_{1})\cong\mathbf{G}/Z(\mathbf{G})
there is the largest one,
in the sense that
\mathbf{G}_{1}/Z(\mathbf{G}_{1})\cong\mathbf{G}/Z(\mathbf{G})
implies
\mathbf{G}
to be a quotient of
\mathbf{G}_{1}
. Then
\mathbf{G}_{1}
is called

(or simply connected).
One writes

to specify a ”realization” of
\mathbf{G}
over a specific field

\mathbf{G}
depend on the characteristic of
K
,
whereas the choice of an algebraically closed field of fixed characteristic is rather immaterial. Note however that if
K
is the algebraical closure of a finite field then
\mathbf{G}(K)
is a locally finite group.
We usually omit mentioning of
K
when discuss the structure of
\mathbf{G}(K)
or its representations.

The universal simple algebraic groups over a fixed algebraically closed field

are in 1-1 correspondence with the simple Lie algebras over
\mathbb{C}
. So they are labeled in the same way as the latter:
A_{n},B_{n},C_{n},D_{n},

n=1,2...
,
E_{6},E_{7}
,
E_{8}
,
F_{4}
,
G_{2}
. The subscript is the

. Their irreducible

-representations are paramerized
as those of Lie algebras over
\mathbb{C}
by strings of non-negative integers
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
, where
n
is the rank. However, the dimensions (and other properties)
of

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected algebraic group. Lemma

We now turn to Problem

]
as a tool for
study an analogous problem for representations of finite groups of Lie type over the complex numbers. A rather obvious but useful observation is the following:

Lemma 12
.

Let
\mathbf{G}
be a connected reductive algebraic group (in particular, simple), and let
\phi
be an irreducible representation of
\mathbf{G}
. Then
\phi
is unisingular if and only if
\phi
has weight

Recall that every connected reductive algebraic group has a maximal connected abelian subgroup

maximal torus of

\mathbf{G}
. The maximal tori of
\mathbf{G}
are conjugate and every semisimple element of

is contained in a maximal torus. The weights of
\phi
are irreducible constituents of

, and the trivial constituent is called weight 0.
(To be precise, weights are elements of

\mathbb{Z}
-lattice of some rank

, which is also called the rank of
\mathbf{G}
. So the weight 0 is the zero element of

So if
\phi
has weight 0 then every semisimple element of
\mathbf{G}
has eigenvalue 1. By Lemma

The inverse holds too. (Indeed, suppose the contrary. Let

be the weights of

be the kernel of

for

is a proper closed subgroup of

(as

). Then

, and hence

for every

,

.)

Note that the set

has a certain partial ordering and every irreducible representation

\mathbf{G}
has a unique highest weights with respect to this ordering; this is

\phi
and is used for a parameterization of the irreducible representations of

.

Thus, Problem

is equivalent to the following one:

Problem 30

Let

Suppose that

In

5.2.
Groups of Lie type: representations over a field of defining characteristic

Many problems on finite linear groups over finite fields requires analysis of irreducible representations of
algebraic groups. This is due to a fundamental theorem by Steinberg. Let
G
be a quasi-simple finite group of Lie type in defining characteristic
\ell>0

is a subgroup of a simple algebraic group

Theorem 15

(R. Steinberg) Every irreducible

This theorem is highly useful as it reduces (to large extent) the representation theory of

Corollary 16

Let

The first statement of the lemma is Theorem

. The second one follows from the fact that every semisimple conjugacy class of

meets a maximal torus
of

(and Lemma

As an illustration consider the following problems:

What can be said on an irreducible

All these questions have analogs for algebraic groups

. Moreover, these have
a more uniform nature (in a sense) and methods to approach.

As mentioned above,

Next we turn to Problem

.

[

.

[

Let

Linear groups and group representations over the complex numbers

The problem of determining

The minimal polynomials and eigenvalues of

7.
Minimal groups and abelian-by-cyclic groups

A general principle to approach a new problem is to start from a minimal non-trivial special case. In this context we consider non-abelian solvable groups with only one non-trivial proper subgroup.
It is clear that such a group
G
has a normal elementary abelian
r
-subgroup
A
, say, for some prime
r
and
G/A
is cyclic of prime order
p\neq r
. (So
G
is a semidirect product of
A
and
G/A
.)
Moreover,
A
has no proper non-trivial subgroup that is normal in
G
. Recall that an elementary abelian
r
-group can be viewed as an additive group of a vector space
V
over

\mathbb{F}_{r}
whose dimension is equal to the rank of
A
and the conjugation action of
G
on
A
turns
V
to an
\mathbb{F}_{r}G
-module, in fact, an
\mathbb{F}_{r}(G/A)
-module. The condition that

A
has no proper non-trivial subgroup of
G
is interpreted by saying that
A
is an irreducible
\mathbb{F}_{r}(G/A)
-module. This interpretations is rather useful. Indeed, let
H=G/A=\langle h\rangle
, that is,
h
is a generator of
H
. Then
h
acts irreducibly on
V
if and only if
n=\dim V
is the minimal number
d
such that
p|(r^{d}-1)
. This number is called the order (or the multiplicative order) of
r
modulo
p
and denoted usually by
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)
. Note that
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)
can be also defined as the minimal number
d
such that
\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{p^{d}}
contains an elements of order
p
,
or as
\mathbb{F}_{r}(\zeta):\mathbb{F}_{r}
, where
\zeta
is a primitive
p
-roots of unity in an algebraically closed field of characteristic
r
. By Fermat’s little theorem,
p|(r^{p-1}-1)
, so
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)\leq p-1
. Therefore,
\mathbb{F}_{r}(\zeta)
is a subfield of
\mathbb{F}_{r^{p-1}}
, and hence
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)
is a divisor of
p-1
.

One observes that the group
G
just constructed depends on two parameters
r,p
as
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)
is a well defined number. We denote this group by
G_{r,p}
. (This is a special case of a Frobenius group
[
28
, §25]
.)
Note that the group
G_{r,p}
can be defined in terms of a finite field as follows. Let
d={\rm ord}_{p}(r)
,
q=r^{d}
,
H
a subgroup of
\mathbb{F}^{\times}_{q}
of order
p
and
A=\mathbb{F}^{+}_{q}
be the additive group of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
Then
G:=HA
, the semidirect product, is isomorphic
G_{r,p}
. Note that the rank of
A
equals
{\rm ord}_{p}(r)
.

Example.
{\rm ord}_{3}(5)=4
,
{\rm ord}_{3}(11)=5
and
{\rm ord}_{3}(23)=12
.

Problem 34
.

For
r>2
determine unisingular representations of
G_{r,p}
(over
\mathbb{C}
or any field of characteristic
\ell\neq r
).

Note that Problem
34
is a special case of Problems
3
and
22
. We assume
r>2
as every faithful irreducible representation of
G_{2,p}
is unisingular.

One can expect that Problem
34
is easy as the group itself is of very simple nature. All elements of
G
are of order
p,r
or 1, and all irreducible representations over an algebraically closed field are
of degrees
p
or 1, those of degree
p
are faithful and lift to characteristic 0. So it suffices to deal with representations over the complex numbers. In addition, all elements of order
p
have eigenvalue 1.
So Problem
34
in fact asks
whether (or when) every element of order
r
has eigenvalue 1. They all lie in an abelian normal subgroup
A
. If
\ell\neq r
then
\phi(A)
is diagonalizable in any
\ell
-representation of
G
.
We state the following easy lemma:

Lemma 22
.

Let
A\subset GL_{n}(F)
be an elementary abelian
r
-group of diagonal matrices. Let
K_{i}
be the subgroup of
A
of elements that have
1
at the
(i,i)
diagonal position.
Then
A
is unisingular if and only if
A=\cup K_{i}
.

Next suppose that
G
is a group of monomial matrices in
GL_{n}(F)
(this is exactly the normalizer in
GL_{n}(F)
of the group
D
of diagonal matrices). Set
A=G\cap D
.
Then
G
permutes the homogeneous components of
A
, and in particular,
G
acts by permutations on the set of irreducible constituents of
A
, and also permutes their kernels.
If
G
is irreducible then they form a single
G
-orbit. If
K
is one of them then
\cap_{g\in G}\,gKg^{-1}=1
. In addition,
A
, and hence
G
, is unisingular if and only if
A=\cup_{g\in G}\,gKg^{-1}
.
So the question when
A=\cup_{h\in H}hK_{1}h^{-1}

is a special case of Problem
5
.

Viewing
A
as a vector space
V
, say, over
\mathbb{F}_{r}
and
K
as a subspace
W
of codimension 1, we observe that
A=\cup_{h\in H}gKg^{-1}
can be rewritten as
V=\cup_{h\in H}hW.
We often record this as
V=HW
.

Next we turn to groups
G=G_{r,p}=AH
, where
A
is an elementary abelian
r
-group,
H
is of prime order
p
and
A
is the only non-trivial proper normal subgroup of
G
.
This group is abelian-by-cyclic. It follows that every irreducible representation of
G
(over an algebraically closed field
F
) is either one-dimensional or of degree
p
,
in fact, is an induced representation
\lambda^{G}
, where
\lambda
is a non-trivial irreducible (hence one-dimensional) representation of
A
. By Clifford’s theorem, the restriction of
\lambda^{G}
to
A
is a direct sum of

p
distinct representations
\lambda^{h}
, where
\lambda^{h}(a)=\lambda(hah^{-1})
,

. Furthermore, if
\lambda,\mu\in\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits A
then
\lambda^{G}
and
\mu^{G}
are equivalent if and only if  the restrictions
\lambda^{G}|_{A}

and
\mu^{G}|_{A}
have no common irreducible constituents. Therefore, the irreducible representations of
G
of degree
p
are in bijection with the non-trivial
H
-orbits on
\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits A
.

Representations
\tau,\sigma
of a group
G
are said to be quasi-equivalent if there exists an automorphism
\alpha
of
G
such that
\tau
is equivalent to
\sigma^{\alpha}
, where
\sigma^{\alpha}(g)=\sigma(\alpha(g))
.
We show that the faithful irreducible representations of
G_{r,p}
are quasi-equivalent (so they behave uniformly). In particular, either all such representations are unisingular or all they are not unisingular.

Lemma 23
.

Faithful irreducible representations of
G=G_{r,p}
are quasi-equivalent.

Proof.

We have
G=A.H
, where
A
is an elementary abelian normal
r
-subgroup of
G
,
|H|=p
and
H
acts on
A
as a subgroup of
GL(A)
.
Let
d
be the rank of
A
. We have seen above that
d={\rm ord}_{r}(p)
, so
H\subset GL(A)\cong GL_{d}(r)
. Let
C=C_{GL_{d}(r)}(H)
so
H\subseteq C
. Then
C
normalizes
G
. So the conjugation by elements of
C
yield automorphisms of
G
.

As
H
is irreducible in
GL_{d}(r)
, by Schur’s lemma,
\mathbb{F}_{r}H
(the
\mathbb{F}_{r}
-enveloping algebra of
H
in
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits V
) is a field isomorphic to
F_{r^{d}}
,
and hence
C\cong F^{\times}_{r^{d}}
. Moreover,
C
acts transitively on
V\setminus\{0\}
. Note that
\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits A\cong\mathop{\rm Hom}\nolimits(A,F^{\times})
is the dual group of
A
.
Then
C
acts transitively on the non-trivial elements of
\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits A
, so if
\lambda,\mu\in\mathop{\rm Irr}\nolimits A
are non-trivial then
\mu=\lambda^{c}
for some
c\in C
. Therefore, if
\mu^{G}=(\lambda^{c})^{G}=(\lambda^{G})^{c}

as required. ∎

The following is now obvious:

Lemma 24
.

Suppose that representations
\tau,\sigma
of a group G are quasi-equivalent. Then
\tau
is unisingular if and only if  so is
\sigma
.

This implies that if
H\subset GL(V)
is an irreducible cyclic subgroup and
V=HW
for a subspace
W
of codimension 1 in
V
then
V=HW^{\prime}
for every such subspace
W^{\prime}
.
This fact has an essencial computational significance. Indeed, in order to check by computer the equality
V=HW
for every subspace
W
of codimension 1, it suffices to
choose
W
in random. In particular, Problem
34
can be stated in the following form:

Problem 35
.

For
r>2
determine the pairs
(r,p)
such that
G_{r,p}
has a unisingular irreducible representation (over
\mathbb{C}
or any field of characteristic
\ell\neq r
).

Recall that
d={\rm ord}_{r}(p)
divides
p-1
. As we have already seen, this is equivalent to a vector space covering problem:

Problem 36
.

Determine the pairs
r,p
of primes,
r>2
, such that if
H\subset GL_{d}(r)=GL(V)
be an irreducible subgroup of order p then
V=HW
for some subspace
W
of codimension
1
in V.

Thus, one can approach Problem
34
using either matrix theory or vector space theory. In fact, the matrix approach can be replaced by the representation theory approach. Both of them are not
very efficient but each of them has some advantage in some special cases. Related problem is under discussion in
[
11
]
and
[
1
]
.

Problems
35
and
36
seems to be difficult already for
r=3
, see
[
82
]
. The special case where
d=p-1
is settled in
[
82
, Lemma 1.5]
as follows:

Theorem 25
.

Suppose that
{\rm ord}_{r}(p)=p-1
. Then
G_{r,p}
has no non-trivial unisingular representation.

For very small
p
there are character tables of
G_{r,p}
, so one can easily answer the question whenever the character table is available. However, the situation looks rather obscure in general.

Keep
d={\rm ord}_{r}(p)
. The case with
d=1
is trivial, as
A
is cyclic. One easily observes that in this case the only unisingular representation is the trivial one.
The case
d=2
is easy too. In this case
p|(r+1)
; here
p\neq r+1
as
p,r
are odd, and hence
p<r
. In this case
G_{r,p}
has no unisingular
non-trivial irreducible representation due to the following easy observation:

Lemma 26
.

Let
p<r
. Then
A\neq\cup_{h\in H}hKh^{-1}
.

Proof.

We have
|A|=r^{d}
and
|K|=r^{d-1}
. Then
|\cup_{h\in H}hKh^{-1}|\leq p|K|<r|K|=|A|
.∎

Note that the inequality
p<r
can be improved to
p<3(r+1)/2
, see
[
7
, Lemmas 2.1 and 2.2]
.
For our case, where
p
is a prime, we have
p\neq 3(r+1)/2
.

Problem 37
.

Investigate Problem
36
for the special case with
d=(p-1)/2
.

It has been verified by computer that the irreducible representations of degree greater that 1 of the groups
G_{3,11}
and
G_{3,23}
are unisigular, see
[
82
, Lemma 6.3]
.
Computer power seems to be insufficient to check the case with
G_{3,47}
.

In view of Proposition
2
, the group
G=G_{r,p}
can be realized as a transitive subgroup of the symmetric group
S_{rp}
and
G_{r,p}
has no non-trivial
unisingular irreducible representation if and only if
G
has a fixed point free element of order
r
. In
[
11
]
the authors state the following conjecture which they refer to as Isbell’s conjecture:

Conjecture
Let
H
be a transitive subgroup of
S_{n}
, where
n=r^{m}p
,
r,p
are primes. Then there exists a function
f=f(r,p)
such that
H
has a fixed point free
r
-element whenever
m>f
.

If
H=G_{r,p}
then the Sylow
r
-subgroup of
H
is elementary abelian, and
d|(p-1)
. So Problem
37
does not interact with Isbell’s conjecture.

In view of Theorem
25
one can ask, given a prime
r
, whether there are infinitely many primes

p
such that
{\rm ord}_{r}(p)=p-1
. This is known as Artin’s primitive root problem which remains open since 1929.

Problem 38
.

Let
r>2
. Is it true that there are infinitely many primes p such that some faithful irreducible representation of
G_{r,p}
is not unisingular?

We expect
[
82
, Conjecture 3]
that the answer is positive.

Conclusion. The research area related with the eigenvalue 1 problems of elements in group representations is at the initial stage of study, and promising for further progress.

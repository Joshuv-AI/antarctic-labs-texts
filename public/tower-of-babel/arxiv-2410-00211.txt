Equation with the lower negative time number in the Davey–Stewartson hierarchy.
A. K. Pogrebkov

Abstract
In SIGMA 17 (2021), 091, 12 p.p. we have presented an integrable system with a negative time variable number for the Davey-Stewartson hierarchy. Here we develop this approach to construct an integrable equation with a lower time variable number. In addition, we show that the system reduced by this time is a new integrable equation in the dimension 1 + 1 1+1 .

Introduction

Problems with negative symmetries, i.e., symmetries with negative numbers, lead to the construction of new classes of integrable systems, in particular in the
2+1
dimension. The initial ideas of constructing
2+1
dimensional integrable systems go back to the works of
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
and
[
3
]
. Thus, in
[
3
]
it was proved that a
2+1
dimensional dynamical system is integrable in the case of a degenerate conservation law. In
[
4
]
, this approach was generalized through the use of commutator identities, the essence of which boils down to the following. Let
A
,
B
, and
\sigma
be elements of the associative algebra
\mathcal{A}
with unit
I
, such that
\sigma^{2}=I
,
[\sigma,A]=0
, and
\{\sigma,B\}=
0. Here
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
denotes an anticommutator, so
[\sigma A,B]=\sigma\{A,B\}
. Then we have two commutator identities:

\sigma[A^{2},B]=[A,[\sigma A,B]],\quad\sigma[\sigma A^{2},B]=[A,[A,B]]+[\sigma A,[\sigma A,B]],

(1.1)

which relate to the derivatives
A
and
B
. Introducing the dependence of the operator
B
on two sets of times
t=\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},\ldots\}
and
x=\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},\ldots\}
by means of the relations

B_{t_{n}}=[A^{n},B],\qquad B_{x_{n}}=[\sigma A^{n},B],

(1.2)

we write (
1.1
) in the form of two linear differential equations:

\sigma B_{t_{2}}=B_{t_{1}x_{1}}\text{and }\sigma B_{x_{2}}=B_{t_{1}t_{1}}+B_{x_{1}x_{1}},

(1.3)

so that each of them gives a linearized versions of the Davey–Stewartson equation (DS).

This approach was developed in application to many integrable differential, differential-difference and difference equations in
2+1
, where it was applied to the case of positive numbers of
n
in relations (
1.2
), i.e., to positive time numbers. In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
, we generalized this approach to the case where these numbers can be negative. In particular, we considered the case based on the commutators
[A,B]
,
[\sigma A,B]
, and
[A^{-1},B]
, or
[A,B]
,
[\sigma A,B]
, and
[\sigma A^{-1},B]
, where we assumed the existence of the inverse element
A^{-1}
. It is easy to verify that these commutators satisfy the following commutator identities:

\displaystyle[\sigma A,[\sigma A,[A^{-1},B]]]-[A,[A,[A^{-1},B]]]+4[A,B]=0,

(1.4)

\displaystyle[\sigma A,[\sigma A,[\sigma A^{-1},B]]]-[A,[A,[\sigma A^{-1},B]]]-4[\sigma A,B]=0.

(1.5)

Taking into account that all these commutators mutually commute, we consider
B
as a function of
t_{1}
,
x_{1}
and
t_{-1}
, or
x_{-1}
such that

B_{t_{1}}=[A,B],\qquad B_{x_{1}}=[\sigma A,B],

(1.6a)

B_{t_{-1}}=[A^{-1},B],\qquad B_{x_{-1}}=[\sigma A^{-1},B],

(1.6b)

that extends (
1.2
) to the negative values of
n
. Under these definitions Eqs. (
1.4
) and (
1.5
)
prove that
B(t,x)
obeys the following linear equations of motion:

\displaystyle B_{x_{1}x_{1}t_{-1}}-B_{t_{1}t_{1}t_{-1}}+4B_{t_{1}}=0,

(1.7)

\displaystyle B_{x_{1}x_{1}x_{-1}}-B_{t_{1}t_{1}x_{-1}}-4B_{x_{1}}=0.

(1.8)

In
[
6
]
and
[
7
]
we derived dressing procedure that enabled nonlinearization of these equations, i.e., constraction of Lax pairs and integrable nonlinear equations. In the case where the set of independent variables includes
t_{1}
,
x_{1}
, and
t_{-1}
the nonlinear evolution equation sounds as

u_{t_{1}t_{-1}}\sigma-u_{x_{1}t_{-1}}-[\sigma,\psi(1+u_{t_{-1}})]+[u_{t_{-1}},[\sigma,u]]=0.

(1.9)

If this set includes
t_{1}
,
x_{1}
, and
x_{-1}
, then the nonlinear integrable equation has the form

u_{t_{1}x_{-1}}\sigma-u_{x_{1}x_{-1}}-[\sigma,\psi(\sigma+u_{x_{-1}})]+[\sigma+u_{x_{-1}},[\sigma,u]]=0.

(1.10)

Here

\psi_{x_{1}}-\sigma\psi_{t_{1}}-[\sigma,u_{t_{1}}]+[\sigma,\psi]\psi+[[\sigma,u],\psi]=0,

(1.11)

the same for both equations (
1.9
) and (
1.11
),

and

\sigma=\sigma_{3}.

(1.12)

Equations in

1.9

In this article we consider the lower equation of this hierarchy, i.e., equation defined by commutators (

) and

B_{x_{-2}}=[\sigma A^{-2},B].

(1.13)

We omitt here equation that follows from the choice of coordinates (

\displaystyle(A^{m}F)(t_{1},x,z)

\displaystyle=(z+\partial_{t_{1}})^{m}F(t_{1},x,z),\quad m\geq 0,

\displaystyle(A^{m}F)(t_{1},x,z)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{\text{sgn}(z_{\text{Re}})}{(-m-1)!}\int dy_{1}\,y_{1}^{-m-1}e^{-y_{1}z}\theta(y_{1}z_{\text{Re}})\times

\displaystyle\times F(t_{1}-y_{1},x,z),\quad m<0,

[\sigma,u^{\text{a}}]=2\sigma u^{\text{a}}
, and so on.
Then as a result we derive from (
4.10
) expression of the anti-diagonal part of matrix
v
:

[\sigma,v]=u_{x_{1}}-\sigma u_{t_{1}}+[\sigma,u]u=0,

(4.12)

and constraint for the diagonal part of matrix
u
:

u^{\text{d}}_{x_{1}}-\sigma u^{\text{d}}_{t_{1}}+2\sigma(u^{\text{a}})^{2}=0.

(4.13)

Now substitution of the r.h.s. of (
4.9
) in (
4.7
) gives dynamical equation for the diagonal part of matrix
u
and anti-diagonal part of
v
.

Dimensional reduction

Reductions to

2.8

t_{1}
can be cancelled if

, dependence on
x_{1}

, and dependence on

), (

) it is clear that any such reduction gives the corresponding reduction of independent variables of the dressing operator
K
. Here we consider only reduction with respect to the time
x_{-2}

(5.1)

Thus Eq. (
2.8
) sounds now as

\displaystyle B(t_{1},x_{1},z)

\displaystyle=e^{2z_{\text{Im}}(it_{1}+\sigma x_{1})}g_{1}(z_{\text{Im}})\delta(z_{\text{Re}}-z_{\text{Im}})+

\displaystyle+e^{2z_{\text{Im}}(it_{1}-\sigma x_{1})}g_{2}(z_{\text{Im}})\delta(z_{\text{Re}}+z_{\text{Im}}),

(5.2)

where
g_{1}(z_{\text{Im}})
and
g_{2}(z_{\text{Im}})
are arbitrary bounded functions of their argument and we have to substitute
x_{1}\to ix_{1}
according to (
2.9
) in order to guarantee boundedness. Due to delta-functions in (

) the
\overline{\partial}
-problem (
2.12
) is substituted by the Riemann–Hilbert problem with discontinuities on the two straight lines in (

). We omit corresponding study for a forthcoming work and give here Lax pair and nonlinear equation.

Now the dressing operator
K
is independent of
x_{-2}
due to (
3.14
) and (
3.15
), so we have to change definition (
4.1
) of the Jost solution:

\phi(t_{1},x_{1},z)=K(t_{1},x_{1},z)e^{z(t_{1}+\sigma x_{1})},

(5.3)

so that Eqs. (
3.14
) and (
3.15
) reduces to the following Lax pair:

\displaystyle\phi_{t_{1}t_{1}}+\beta\phi_{t_{1}}+\gamma\phi-z^{2}\sigma\phi\sigma=0,

(5.4)

\displaystyle\phi_{x_{1}}=\sigma\phi_{t_{1}}-2[\sigma,u]\phi,

(5.5)

where we used that by (
3.11
) now
\alpha=\sigma
, while
\beta
and
\gamma
are still given by (
3.13
), but
u_{x_{-2}}=\gamma_{x_{-2}}=0

Compatibility condition of (
5.4

Considering diagonal and antidiagonal (see (

6
Conclusion

We proved that times with lower negative numbers lead to new integrable systems in
2+1
dimensions. Constructions of these systems is rather straightforward and follows the same ideas, as construction of integrable systems in the case of positive numbers of times. Essential modification of this approach is necessity to introduce some intermidiate variables, that play the role of constraints and that correspond to discrete evolutions.

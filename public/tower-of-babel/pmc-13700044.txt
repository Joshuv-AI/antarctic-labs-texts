Optimal Sheath for Single-Access Percutaneous Coronary Intervention With Impella CP Hemodynamic Support
Authors: Ravi Thakker, Adnan Khalif, Ying Xu, Seth Bilazarian, Dana Bentley, J. Dawn Abbott, Mario Iannaccone, Jason Wollmuth, William O’Neill, Alexander G. Truesdell, Hady Lichaa, Mir Babar Basir
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSingle-access percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) with the Impella CP (Abiomed) has increased in prevalence. Despite this, the optimal PCI sheath remains unknown. We characterized the optimal 7F PCI sheath using the single-access technique.MethodsThe outer diameter measurement of the sheaths was performed using a Keyence 2-axis laser micrometer (Keyence). The force needed to insert the sheath and the torqueability of the guide catheter were assessed using the Mark-10 M5i Advanced Digital Force/Torque Indicator (Mark-10 Corporation) on a simulated single-access setting in a simulated skin pad. The sample size was determined to be 5 sheaths for each test.ResultsThe BRITE TIP 11 cm (Cordis), BRITE TIP 35 cm (Cordis), and Destination sheath (Terumo) had the largest outer diameters; the Prelude IDeal (Merit Medical) and Terumo Glidesheath (Terumo) had the smallest outer diameters. The Glidesheath was easiest for insertion/removal, followed by the Prelude IDeal and Impella Low Profile (LP) Companion sheath (Abiomed). The Impella LP Companion, Prelude Roadster (Merit Medical), and Destination sheaths allowed for the best torqueability and lowest force to remove the guide catheter. The longer Impella LP Companion sheath did not impact torqueability, but the removal force increased by 50% compared with the shorter Impella LP Companion sheath.ConclusionThe Impella LP Companion sheath demonstrated the best torqueability and least effort in advancing the guide catheters, while the Prelude Roadster and Destination also performed well on these metrics. The Prelude IDeal and Glidesheath were good options for single-access because of their smaller outer diameters and hydrophilic coating and were easiest for insertion/removal through the 14F LP sheath.

Introduction

Transfemoral access (TFA) is a common access site for percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and is the most common access site for the delivery of mechanical circulatory support (MCS) devices.1 TFA, however, is associated with an increased risk of major bleeding, vascular complications, and mortality.2 Although bleeding reduction strategies, such as the use of ultrasound guidance,3 use of micropuncture needles, and use of bleeding detection systems, have reduced the rates of bleeding complications, these complications remain clinically consequential. The Impella CP (Abiomed) is a MCS device that has been increasingly utilized for high-risk PCI and cardiogenic shock. The device is introduced via a 14F sheath, typically via retrograde femoral arterial access.4 To address the challenges of limited arterial access and bleeding, the single-access for high-risk PCI (SHiP) technique was developed by Wollmuth and colleagues.5

When utilizing the SHiP technique, the 14F Impella CP sheath is placed as usual with a 0.035-inch wire. The hemostasis valve is then accessed using a micropuncture needle. A 7F sheath is then introduced to facilitate access for PCI.5 Major limitations of the SHiP technique have included the inability to commonly use an 8F sheath, bleeding from the Impella sheath if the micropuncture needle is pierced close to the Impella catheter shaft, and displacement of the Impella when advancing the sheath utilized for PCI.6 Despite its increasing utilization, the ideal sheath when utilizing the SHiP technique remains unknown. Although it is commonly thought that the PCI sheath and the shaft of the Impella sit as 2 parallel objects within a circular sheath (Figure 1), the sheath and Impella shaft can form a more oval configuration as well (Figure 2). The friction between commonly used sheaths can therefore play an important role in the ability to place the sheath, the torqueability of the guide catheter, and the removal force of the sheath. The aim of our study was to characterize the optimal 7F PCI sheath when utilizing the SHiP technique. Methods

Test method and rationale

The outer diameter (OD) measurements of the PCI sheaths were performed using the noncontact Keyence 2-axis laser micrometer (Keyence) method. Torqueability of the 7F guide catheter was assessed using the Mark-10 M5i Advanced Digital Force/Torque Indicator (Mark-10 Corporation) on a simulated single access setting. The 14F Impella LP sheath was placed in a simulated skin pad without fluid. The simulated torque force measured allowed head-to-head comparison tests and was used as a surrogate for the real torque force in clinical cases. Torque measurement was conducted with the Impella CP shaft in place. Determination of the ease of insertion/removal of the 7F PCI sheath and guide catheter was evaluated using the Imada Digital Force Gauge ZTA-11 (IMADA, Inc).

Test samples

The 7F PCI sheath and guide catheter samples were selected based upon physician feedback and were procured from Medical Materials, Inc, or eSuture. Please refer to Supplemental Table S1 for 7F PCI sheath samples and Supplemental Table S2 for 7F guide catheter samples.

Sample size

The sample size was determined to be 5 pieces for each test. Due to the design of this study being a descriptive analysis, the sample size was not calculated using statistical power. Attempts were made to ensure that no major changes were made by the manufacturer of the sample.

Test materials

Detailed information about the abovementioned test materials can be found in Supplemental Table S3.

Experimental procedure steps

Outer diameter measurement procedure

The Keyence 2-axis laser micrometer was prepped. Next, the sheath was fixed without the dilator across the 2 support fixtures. The laser was then aligned roughly in the center of the device body. The 14F × 13 cm Impella LP Sheath was preset into the simulated skin pad, and the dummy 9F catheter was inserted through the 14F × 13 cm sheath. The sheath body was wetted with saline. Single access procedural steps were then followed, and the PCI sheath was inserted over the 0.035-inch guidewire into the 14F Impella LP Sheath. The dilator of the PCI sheath was removed, and the 7F guide catheter was delivered through the 7F PCI sheath. Please see Supplemental Figures S1 A,B.

Torque, guide catheter, and PCI sheath removal force measurement procedure

7F guide catheter torque force measurement

The vice of the torque indicator was loosened and tightened over a male luer connector. The Mark-10 torque indicators were then appropriately set to the correct units and mode. The male luer was connected to the guide catheter hub. Prior recordings were removed, and the Mark-10 was set to live. The PCI sheath was held steady with 1 hand, with clockwise twisting of the guide catheter and 5 cycles total via the vice with the other hand. The peak of the clockwise torque was recorded.

7F guide catheter removal force measurement

The male luer was connected to the handheld force gauge and set to peak force recording mode. Next, the male luer was connected to the hub of the guide catheter. The PCI sheath was held steady with the pull back of the handheld force gauge to remove the guide catheter. The peak force of guide catheter removal was recorded.

7F PCI sheath removal force measurement

The sheath hub-force gauge adaptor was connected to the handheld force gauge. The force gauge was then set to Peak Force Recording mode. The sheath hub was connected to the adaptor. The 14F LP sheath was then held steady, and the pull back of the handheld force gauge was performed with the removal of the PCI sheath. The peak force of PCI sheath removal was recorded. Please see Supplemental Figures S2 A-C.

Results

Outer diameter of PCI sheath

The BRITE TIP 11 cm (Cordis), BRITE TIP 35 cm (Cordis), and Destination (Terumo) had the largest outer diameters. Please refer to Figure 3 for the box plot of the outer diameter of all the PCI sheaths.Figure 3Outer diameter of 7F PCI sheaths.

Outer diameter of 7F PCI sheaths.

Force required for insertion and removal of PCI sheath

The Prelude IDeal (Merit Medical) and Glidesheath (Terumo) were easiest for insertion/removal because of their smaller outer diameter and full-length hydrophilic coating, which was determined by peak force. The BRITE TIP 11 cm and BRITE TIP 35 cm long sheaths both have the same outer diameter without a hydrophilic coating. Due to its inherent longer size, the BRITE TIP 35 cm required double the force for insertion and removal. The Flexor Ansel (Cook Medical) and Destination (Terumo) sheaths have similar outer diameters and lengths as the BRITE TIP 35 cm sheath, but because of their hydrophilic coating, require less insertion/removal force. Compared with the Flexor Ansel sheath and Destination sheath, the Impella LP Companion sheath only required half the force for insertion and removal because of its full-length hydrophilic coating and smaller outer diameter. In terms of length and removability, the Prelude Roadster sheath (Merit Medical) had a similar outer diameter to the Impella LP Companion sheath but required 40% more force because of its longer length. Removal force of 7F PCI sheaths.

Torque force required of guide catheters

The Impella LP Companion, Prelude Roadster, and Destination sheaths allowed the best torqueability of the guide catheter. The AVANTI+ sheath (Cordis) required the most effort to torque the guide catheters, nearly 2.5 times the Companion sheath’s force. The Flexor Ansel, Prelude IDeal, Glidesheath, and Pinnacle (Terumo) required less effort to torque the guide catheters than the AVANTI+ sheath, but about 50% to 60% higher than the Companion sheath’s force. Notably, the Prelude IDeal and Glidesheath are both radial sheaths that require one more wire exchange step before guide catheter insertion. There were no significant torque force differences across three different guide catheters that would have accounted for affecting torqueability of the sheaths. Please see Supplemental Figures S3 to S5 for the box plot on the torque force of all the PCI sheaths.

Force required for advancement and removal of guide catheters

The Impella LP Companion sheath required the least effort to advance the guide catheter. The AVANTI+ sheath was the most difficult to advance. There were no significant insertion/removal force differences across the 3 guide catheters for a given PCI sheath. We found that a higher guide catheter insertion/removal force correlated with a higher torque force. Please see Supplemental Figures S6 to S8 for the box plot on the removal force of the PCI sheaths.

Impella sheath length and the impact on insertion/removal/torque force

The longer Impella LP sheath did not impact the guide catheter’s torqueability when the Impella LP Companion sheath was used, likely due to its coil-reinforced body, but it did increase the guide catheter’s removal force. The longer Impella LP sheath required greater force to adjust and remove the guide catheter through the Prelude IDeal sheath. There was no impact on the Prelude IDeal sheath removal force because of its smaller outer diameter and full-length hydrophilic coating. The longer LP sheath resulted in significantly higher forces to adjust and remove the guide catheter for a given length of the BRITE TIP sheath. For a given length of Impella LP sheath, a short BRITE TIP sheath generally allows easy insertion/removal through the Impella LP sheath and easier guide catheter advancement and adjustment. However, the samples were freely exposed to the atmosphere during the test, and the results might be different if the guide catheters are constrained by tortuous vessels. There are also more torque force variations when the BRITE TIP sheath is shorter than the Impella LP sheath. Discussion

The aim of our study was to characterize the optimal 7F PCI sheath to facilitate PCI when using the SHiP technique. The results of this experiment demonstrated that although it is believed that a sheath with a larger outer diameter and longer length may be harder to insert and remove, this may not always be the case when a hydrophilic coating is present. We found that the Impella LP Companion sheath had the best torqueability and allowed for the easiest guide catheter passing and required the lowest PCI sheath removal force. The Prelude Roadster and Destination also showed excellent torqueability and ease of guide catheter passing. The Prelude IDeal and Glidesheath were the easiest to advance into the 14F Impella LP sheath because of their smaller outer diameter and hydrophilic coating; however, operators utilizing these sheaths should expect about 50% greater torque force needed to adjust the guide catheter when compared to the Impella LP Companion sheath. Also, the Glidesheath is a thin-walled sheath which is vulnerable to kinking.7

To minimize access-related complications, it is best to limit the number of access site punctures in the femoral artery. Femoral artery access can result in complications such as bleeding, especially in higher-risk populations such as patients who are elderly, female, those with a history of hypertension, renal dysfunction, peripheral arterial disease, and/or thin body habitus.8

The SHiP technique was originally introduced by Wollmuth et al who reported a case series of 17 patients treated with the SHiP technique. Patients were predominantly male (59%); 76% presented with NSTEMI, with the remaining 24% presenting electively. Most patients underwent multivessel intervention (94%) with atherectomy (88%), with 35% undergoing PCI for CTO. All but one of these cases were performed with Impella CP (one with Impella 2.5). No intraprocedural bleeding or disruption of the Impella sheath was observed.5

The SHiP technique offers operators the ability to perform Impella-assisted PCI with fewer vascular access sites. Although data are currently limited, this technique offers the potential opportunity to expand care in a larger population of patients. Our study offered a unique insight into the optimal 7F PCI sheath to use in terms of torqueability, insertion, and removal. Furthermore, sheathless techniques exist for getting guide catheters up to 8F in size.9 This is usually indicated when larger access is needed for a smaller artery size. Examples include the 7.5F Sheathless Eaucath guide catheter (ASAHI INTECC), which is available in the United States, and 8.5F sheathless guide catheters, which are only available outside the United States.10

Limitations

As an in vitro study, there were inherent limitations to this analysis owing to the inability to fully mimic in vivo properties such as vessel elasticity in an artificial vessel simulating blood flow. All flow rate and measurement data for the tested devices were gathered based on the device designs available in the market at the time of testing. Since that time, the 14F Impella LP Sheath used as the test device for the 7F PCI sheaths was the subject of a voluntary device recall in 2023, not related to the intravascular part of the sheath. The next-generation LP sheath with updated sidearm design is pending commercial release.

Conclusion

In our study evaluating the optimal 7F PCI sheath when utilizing the SHiP technique with the Impella CP, the Impella LP Companion sheath allowed the best torqueability and easiest guide catheter manipulation, requiring low PCI sheath removal force. The Prelude Roadster sheath and the Destination sheath were also high-performing sheaths, showing excellent torqueability and ease of guide catheter manipulation. With their smaller outer diameters and hydrophilic coating, the Prelude Ideal and Glidesheath were easiest to insert and remove through the 14F Impella LP sheath; however, they required higher torque and guide catheter removal forces. The findings in the difference between PCI sheath mechanics were notable. With the variety of PCI sheaths available for the SHiP technique, the findings of this study can help guide interventional cardiologists in choosing the optimal PCI sheath for torqueability and insertion/removal. Ultimately, this can help alleviate some aspects of a complex procedure, thereby improving operator comfort.

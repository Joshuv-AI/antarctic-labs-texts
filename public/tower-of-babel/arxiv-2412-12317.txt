The S n S_{n} -equivariant Euler characteristic of ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
Siddarth Kannan, Terry Dekun Song

Abstract
. We compute the S n S_{n} -equivariant topological Euler characteristic of the Kontsevich moduli space ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) . Letting ℳ ¯ 1 , n nrt ​ ( ℙ r , d ) ⊂ ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) denote the subspace of maps from curves without rational tails, we solve for the motive of ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) in terms of ℳ ¯ 1 , n nrt ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) and plethysm with a genus-zero contribution determined by Getzler and Pandharipande [ 25 ] . Fixing a generic ℂ ⋆ \mathbb{C}^{\star} -action on ℙ r \mathbb{P}^{r} , we derive a closed formula for the Euler characteristic of ℳ ¯ 1 , n nrt ​ ( ℙ r , d ) ℂ ⋆ \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}} as an S n S_{n} -equivariant virtual mixed Hodge structure, which leads to our main formula for the Euler characteristic of ℳ ¯ 1 , n ​ ( ℙ r , d ) \overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d) . Our approach connects the geometry of torus actions on Kontsevich moduli spaces [ 31 ] with symmetric functions in Coxeter types A A and B B [ 43 ] , as well as the enumeration of graph colourings with prescribed symmetry [ 33 ] .



1.
Introduction

Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
denote the Kontsevich moduli space of stable maps of degree
d
from
n
-pointed curves of genus
g
to
\mathbb{P}^{r}=\mathbb{P}^{r}_{\mathbb{C}}
. It is a fundamental object in enumerative geometry and in the study of algebraic curves. The fibres of the map
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g}
are studied in Brill–Noether theory. While the moduli space is smooth and irreducible when
g=0
, it is usually reducible, non-equidimensional, and highly singular when
g>0
. These pathologies make it difficult to study the geometry of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in positive genus. From the enumerative perspective, these issues have been circumvented via torus localisation by Graber and Pandharipande
[
31
]
, following earlier work of Kontsevich
[
41
]
. They express the Gromov–Witten invariants of
\mathbb{P}^{r}
in terms of computable integrals on the space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}
of torus-fixed stable maps. The action of
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
is induced by a generic one on
\mathbb{P}^{r}
, with
r+1
isolated fixed points.

In this paper, we enhance this perspective, shifting from integrals to
S_{n}
-equivariant motivic invariants of the moduli space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}
, where the symmetric group
S_{n}
permutes the marked points. This space admits a combinatorial stratification, where each stratum is isomorphic to a product of moduli spaces of stable curves, modulo the action of the automorphism group of a finite graph. In the case
g=1
, we solve the combinatorics of the strata, at the motivic level, in terms of plethysm of symmetric functions. Our main application is a formula for the
S_{n}
-equivariant topological Euler characteristic

(1.1)

\chi^{S_{n}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\right):=\sum_{i\geq 0}(-1)^{i}\operatorname{ch}_{n}(H^{i}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d);\mathbb{Q}))\in\Lambda,

where

\Lambda:=\lim_{\longleftarrow}\mathbb{Q}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]]^{S_{n}}

is the completed ring of symmetric functions, and

denotes the Frobenius characteristic of an
S_{n}
-representation

. The invariant
\chi^{S_{n}}\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\right)
is the enrichment of the ordinary topological Euler characteristic to a virtual character of the symmetric group.

For a fixed genus
g

\mathsf{b}_{g,r}:=\sum_{n\geq 0}\sum_{d\geq 0}\chi^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))q^{d}\in\Lambda[[q]]

be the generating function for the
S_{n}
-equivariant Euler characteristics. When

\mathsf{b}_{0,r}
has been given by Getzler and Pandharipande
[
25
]
. As noted in §

-equivariant Hodge decomposition of the moduli space. In positive genus, the pathologies of the space make it a subtle problem to calculate the coarser invariant
\mathsf{b}_{g,r}

We determine
\mathsf{b}_{1,r}
in terms of
\mathsf{b}_{0,r}
, as well as the two terms

(1.2)

Closed formulas for

Graded

1.3.
Enumerative combinatorics of cycles

In §
5

Theorem B
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{r}

\displaystyle=(r+1)\left(\mathscr{A}_{1}+\frac{\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}(\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}+1)+\frac{1}{4}\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\log(1-\psi_{n}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{space}+\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\left(\eta_{k,d}(r)\frac{(1+2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0})^{2}}{(1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/2+1}}+\sum_{j\mid k}\theta_{j,k,d}(r)\frac{1}{(1-\psi_{j}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/j}}\right).

The Serre characteristic of
\mathsf{Dih}_{r}
is the solution to a graph enumeration problem: it is a count of isomorphism classes of localisation graphs, where each graph
\mathcal{G}
is weighted by the type-
B

\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{G})
on

. We can write this as

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\sum_{[H]\leq D_{k}}\gamma_{r,d}^{[H]}(k)\operatorname{ch}_{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\mathrm{Triv}_{H}).

The innermost sum is over conjugacy classes of subgroups of the dihedral group
D_{k}
, viewed as a subgroup of
B_{k}
. The factor
\gamma_{r,d}^{[H]}(k)
denotes the number of isomorphism classes of localisation graphs such that the action of
\operatorname{Aut}(\mathcal{G})
on

has the same character as the action of
H
on the half-edges of a fixed
k

In §
5

by computing the numbers

for all possible values of the parameters, as well as the associated characters. To count decorations with prescribed automorphisms, we use standard techniques in combinatorial enumeration. The most important step is to apply Möbius inversion to the subgroup lattice

of

in terms of the following ingredients:

•

binomial coefficients which encode partitions of the stable map degree along the edges,

•

chromatic polynomials of paths and cycles which count

•

the Möbius function
\mu_{D_{k}}
of
\mathcal{L}(D_{k})

The combinatorial factors

and

1.5.
Further directions

We outline four natural directions in which to generalise Theorems
A
and
B
.

Virtual Euler characteristics

It is possible that our approach to the topological Euler characteristics applies to the virtual Euler characteristics of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

[
27
]
,
[
42
]
. Among the key ingredients of our method, torus localisation
[

,
2
]
and
S_{n}
-actions
[

]
have been explored in quantum K-theory, and the virtual Euler characteristic satisfies cut-and-paste properties relative to the forgetful morphism
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\to\mathfrak{M}_{1,n}
. Hence, there is reason to expect that analogous calculations could contribute to positive genus K-theoretic Gromov–Witten invariants.

Higher genus calculations

The Graber–Pandharipande description of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}
implies that its Serre characteristic is determined by products of Serre characteristics of moduli spaces of stable curves, modulo finite groups. In
[
36
]
, we prove a graph sum formula expressing the generating function
\sum_{g,n}\chi^{S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{g,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d))t^{g-1}
in terms of the generating function
\overline{\mathsf{m}}
from (
1.7
) and certain graph sums. The present work calculates the

-degree
0
term by computing the genus one graph sums explicitly after separating rational tails. Algorithms to compute the graph sums in higher genus would make many more calculations possible.

Comparing compactifications

There are alternative modular compactifications of
\mathcal{M}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
, most notably the moduli space of quasimaps
\overline{\mathcal{Q}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

[
45
]
and the Vakil–Zinger desingularisation of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

[
66
,
58
,
37
]
. It would be interesting to compare the topology and combinatorics of these spaces. The locus
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(r)
is common to all three compactifications, so one could hope for plethystic formulas comparing the Serre characteristics. We expect that our techniques could eventually give motivic analogues of wall-crossing formulas relating Gromov–Witten and quasimap invariants
[
14
]
, similar to the motivic wall-crossing formulas for Hassett spaces obtained in
[
35
]
.

Combinatorial complexity of strata

The enumeration of coloured cycles in our work gives the count of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed strata in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
. It would be interesting if our techniques could be adapted to count all

-fixed strata in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

Such enumeration problems encode the combinatorial complexity of the compactification. In the case of stable curves

enumerate the boundary strata of fixed dimension when

Lemma 2.12
.

Let

\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\operatorname{Res}_{S_{2}\times S_{n}}^{S_{n+2}}X)=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\left(\frac{\partial^{2}\mathsf{e}^{S_{n+2}}(X)}{\partial p_{1}^{2}}\right)+p_{2}\otimes\left(\frac{\partial\mathsf{e}^{S_{n+2}}(X)}{\partial p_{2}}\right).

Proof.

It suffices to prove the formula for the Frobenius characteristic of an
S_{n+2}
-representation

\operatorname{Res}_{S_{2}\times S_{n}}^{S_{n+2}}V\cong\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}},V)\oplus\mathrm{Alt}_{S_{2}}\otimes\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Alt}_{S_{2}},V).

Here both
\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}},V)
and

are

-representations.

From

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{2,n}(\mathrm{Res}^{S_{n+2}}_{S_{2}\times S_{n}}X)

Lemma 2.17
.

For

\mathfrak{q}_{n}^{j}\circ_{S_{2}}f=(\mathfrak{q}_{n}\circ_{S_{2}}f)^{j}
.

Lemma 2.18
.

Writing

as
f=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes f_{1}+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\otimes f_{2}
for unique
f_{1},f_{2}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{M})\otimes\Lambda
, we have

\mathfrak{p}_{1}\circ_{S_{2}}f=f_{1},

\mathfrak{q}_{1}\circ_{S_{2}}f=f_{2}.

Proof.

We decompose
f
into homogeneous graded pieces and assume
f\in\Lambda_{2}\otimes\Lambda_{n}
for some
n\geq 0
. Because
\Lambda_{n}
is spanned by
\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V)
of
S_{n}
-representations
V
, it suffices to prove the formula for
f=\operatorname{ch}_{2,n}(V)
where
V
is some
S_{2}\times S_{n}
-representation.

Following the proof of Lemma
2.12
, we may decompose
V
uniquely into

V=\left(\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}}\otimes V_{1}\right)\oplus\left(\mathrm{Alt}_{S_{2}}\otimes V_{2}\right),

where
V_{1}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}},V)
and
V_{2}=\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Alt}_{S_{2}},V)
are
S_{n}
-representations.

Applying
\operatorname{ch}_{2,n}
on both sides, we decompose
\operatorname{ch}_{2,n}(V)\in\Lambda_{2}\otimes\Lambda_{n}
into:

(2.19)

\displaystyle\ \operatorname{ch}_{2,n}(V)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}}{2}\otimes\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{1})+\frac{p_{1}^{2}-p_{2}}{2}\otimes\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{2})

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{1}^{2}}{2}\otimes(\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{1})+\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{2}))+\frac{p_{2}}{2}\otimes(\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{1})-\operatorname{ch}_{n}(V_{2})).

In the following, we identify
B_{1}\cong S_{2}
. Applying the definition of plethysm given above, we find that

\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}}\circ_{S_{2}}V=\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}}\otimes_{S_{2}}V=\mathrm{Hom}_{S_{2}}(\mathrm{Triv}_{S_{2}},V)=V_{1},

3.
Composition and localisation on graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces

In this section, we explain how the plethysm operations described in the previous section can be upgraded to
compositions
of (graded)
\mathbb{S}
-spaces. In the context of moduli spaces of marked nodal curves, the composition operation has the appealing geometric interpretation as attaching along marked points. This observation allows us to translate the geometric descriptions of strata in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
into a plethystic formula for
\mathsf{b}_{1,r}
. This is the content of Proposition
3.9
.

The composition operation on graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces has been explored in
[
25
]
, and we combine their formalism with torus localisation. In Lemma
3.2
, we prove the (well-known) fact that the Frobenius characteristic of a graded
\mathbb{S}
-space with
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action can be recovered from the fixed locus. The only requirement is for the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action to commute with the
S_{n}
-action; no smoothness or compactness property is needed.

3.1.
\mathbb{S}
-,
\mathbb{B}
-, and
S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}
-spaces

An
\mathbb{S}
-space
\mathcal{X}
is a sequence
\mathcal{X}(n)
of
S_{n}
-spaces, for each
n\geq 0
.
\mathbb{B}
-spaces and
S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}

S_{n}
with
B_{n}
and
S_{2}\times S_{n}

\mathbb{A}\in\{\mathbb{S},\mathbb{B},S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}\}

of
\mathbb{A}
-spaces can be made into a ring using the
\boxtimes

(\mathcal{X}\boxtimes\mathcal{Y})(n):=\coprod_{j=0}^{n}\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{j}\times G_{n-j}}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{X}(j)\times\mathcal{Y}(n-j),

where
G_{k}=S_{k}
for all
k
if
\mathbb{A}\in\{\mathbb{S},S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}\}
or
G_{k}=B_{k}
for all
k
if
\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{B}

\mathbb{S}
-spaces
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{Y}
which are moduli spaces of marked objects (e.g. pointed curves) the operation

should be seen as taking all ways of distributing the
n
marked points to the
j
markings coming from
\mathcal{X}(j)
and the remaining
n-j
ones from
\mathcal{Y}(n-j)
.

We make use of the

of
\mathbb{S}
-spaces. Notice that for an
\mathbb{S}
-space
\mathcal{Y}
and

, the product
\mathcal{Y}^{\boxtimes j}
admits an
S_{j}
-action (not to be confused with its
\mathbb{S}
-space structure) of coordinate permutations. The composition operation is defined as

(\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{Y})(n):=\coprod_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{X}(j)\times\mathcal{Y}^{\boxtimes j}(n)\right)/S_{j},

for
\mathcal{X}
any
\mathbb{S}
-space and
\mathcal{Y}
an
\mathbb{S}
-space with
\mathcal{Y}(0)=\varnothing
; the product
\mathcal{X}(j)\times\mathcal{Y}^{\boxtimes j}(n)
receives the diagonal
S_{j}
-action. If
\mathcal{X},\mathcal{Y}
are moduli spaces of marked objects, this operation should be visualised as attaching ‘tails’ coming from
\mathcal{Y}
to
\mathcal{X}
, which is seen as the ‘core’ component. Similarly, if
\mathcal{X}
is a
\mathbb{B}
-space and
\mathcal{Y}
is a
S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}
-space, we define a
\mathbb{S}
-space
\mathcal{X}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}
by

(\mathcal{X}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathcal{Y})(n)=\coprod_{k\geq 0}(\mathcal{X}(k)\times\mathcal{Y}^{\boxtimes k}(n))/B_{k},

where
B_{k}
acts diagonally. For
[\mathcal{X}]\in K_{0}(\mathsf{V},\mathbb{A})
and
\mathbb{A}\in\{\mathbb{S},\mathbb{B},S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}\}
, we write

\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{X})=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{e}^{G_{n}}(\mathcal{X}(n))

for the
Serre characteristic
of the
\mathbb{A}
-space
\mathcal{X}
; again

is the appropriate group among
S_{n}
,
B_{n}
, or
S_{2}\times S_{n}
, and

takes values in

where
R=\Lambda
,
R=\Lambda(S_{2})
, or
R=\Lambda_{2}\otimes\Lambda

is a ring homomorphism, which respects the composition structures.

3.2.
Graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces and
\mathbb{B}
-spaces

A

\mathbb{A}

\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{S}

\mathbb{A}=\mathbb{B}
is a

G_{n}
-space for each integer

, where
G_{n}=S_{n}
or
G_{n}=B_{n}

Put differently, the product operation is defined by:

(\mathcal{X}\boxtimes\mathcal{Y})(n,d):=\coprod_{j=0}^{n}\coprod_{i=0}^{d}\operatorname{Ind}_{G_{j}\times G_{n-j}}^{G_{n}}\mathcal{X}(j,i)\times\mathcal{Y}(n-j,d-i).

(\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{Y})(n,d):=\coprod_{i=0}^{d}\coprod_{j=0}^{\infty}\left(\mathcal{X}(j,i)\times\mathcal{Y}^{\boxtimes j}(n,d-i)\right)/S_{j}.

The Serre characteristic

of a graded

)

For graded

and

\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{X}\circ\mathcal{Y})=\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{X})\circ\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{Y}),

where the plethysm on the right is as defined in §
2.6
. For a graded
{\mathbb{B}}
-space

and an

With this definition, we get that

\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{X})\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{Y})=\mathsf{e}(\mathcal{X}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathcal{Y}),

for a graded
\mathbb{B}

and an

The following elementary result will be applied to calculate the graded plethysm in later sections.

Lemma 3.1

3.3.
Localisation for graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a graded
\mathbb{S}
-space with
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action on each
\mathcal{X}(n,d)
that commutes with the
S_{n}
-action: in other words, the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action is given by morphisms of graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces. Then we define a graded
\mathbb{S}
-space
\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}
by

\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}(n,d):=\mathcal{X}(n,d)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}.

Lemma 3.2
.

With the same assumption as above,

\mathsf{ch}(\mathcal{X})=\mathsf{ch}(\mathcal{X}^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})\in\Lambda[[q]].

Proof.

Working with each bidegree
(n,d)
, it suffices to show that if
X
is a variety with commuting actions of
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
and
S_{n}
, then
\chi^{S_{n}}(X)=\chi^{S_{n}}(X^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})
. By the Lefschetz fixed-point theorem, we have

\chi^{S_{n}}(X)=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{n}}\chi(X^{\sigma})\prod_{i=1}^{n}p_{i}^{\lambda_{i}(\sigma)}

where
\lambda_{i}(\sigma)
is the number of
i
-cycles in
\sigma
. Since the
S_{n}
-action commutes with the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action, we have

(X^{\sigma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}=(X^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})^{\sigma}

for all
\sigma\in S_{n}
. Therefore, to prove the lemma, it suffices to prove that for all
\sigma\in S_{n},

\chi(X^{\sigma})=\chi\left((X^{\sigma})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}\right).

To show this, we will prove that for an arbitrary
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-variety
Y
, we have
\chi(Y)=\chi(Y^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})
, which is likely well-known to experts. Let
Y^{sing}\subset Y
denote the singular locus of
Y
, and let
Y^{sm}=Y\smallsetminus Y^{sing}
be the smooth locus of
Y^{sm},
which is open in
Y.
Since
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
must preserve
Y^{sing}
, both
Y^{sing}
and
Y^{sm}
are
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-invariant, and we have a partition

Y^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}=(Y^{sm})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}\sqcup(Y^{sing})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}.

By induction on dimension, we can assume that
\chi((Y^{sing})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})=\chi(Y^{sing})
, so we are reduced to showing that
\chi(Y^{sm})=\chi((Y^{sm})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})
. Since
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
is connected, each
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-orbit is irreducible and is contained in some irreducible component of
Y^{sm}.
Therefore, the
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action preserves each irreducible component of
Y^{sm},
so we reduce to the case where
Y^{sm}
is irreducible.

In this case, we can find a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-invariant open subset
U\subset Y

[
63
]
. By passing further to an open subset if necessary, we may assume that
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-action on
U
is almost free, so that the quotient
U\to U/\mathbb{C}^{\star}
exists, and in particular is étale locally a
\mathbb{C}^{\star}
-bundle. It follows that
\chi(U)=0
. If
(Y^{sm}\smallsetminus U)^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}=Y^{sm}\smallsetminus U
, then
(Y^{sm})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}}=Y^{sm}\smallsetminus U
and
\chi(Y^{sm})=\chi((Y^{sm})^{\mathbb{C}^{\star}})
. Otherwise, we can repeat this procedure by replacing
Y^{sm}
with
Y^{sm}\smallsetminus U
. The proof is complete by Noetherian induction.
∎

3.4.
Separating rational tails

We now show how to separate rational tails in the calculation of the class of the graded
\mathbb{S}
-space

•

•

This operation allows us to single out the contribution from the first class of maps, which we calculate in the rest of the work. The precise definitions are given as follows.

Definition 3.3

, together with a degree decoration restricted from

-equivariant and hence a Zariski locally trivial fibration. Let

\mathrm{ev}_{n+1}^{-1}(p)=:\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

be a fibre of the map, which is an
S_{n}
-space. Define
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0}(r)
as the graded
\mathbb{S}
-space given by

(n,d)\mapsto\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}^{\star}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).

Note that by Zariski local triviality, we have

[\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0}(r)]=\frac{D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(r)}{[\mathbb{P}^{r}]},

in
K_{0}(\mathsf{V},\mathbb{S})[[q]]
. From this it follows that

\mathsf{ch}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0}(r))=\frac{1}{r+1}\mathsf{b}_{0,r}^{\prime}.

The following is the main result of the section. It states how the Serre characteristic of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(r)
is determined by those of the no-rational-tails locus
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(r)
and the space
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0}(r)
of maps from rational tails.

Proposition 3.9
.

There is an equality in
K_{0}(\mathsf{V},\mathbb{S})[[q]]
:

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(r)=\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(r)\circ\left([\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}]+\frac{D\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}(r)}{[\mathbb{P}^{r}]}\right).

In particular,

\mathsf{b}_{1,r}=\mathsf{b}_{1,r}^{\mathrm{nrt}}\circ\left(p_{1}+\frac{\mathsf{b}^{\prime}_{0,r}}{r+1}\right)\in\Lambda[[q]].

Remark 3.10
.

The term
[\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}]
in the formula has bidegree
n=1,d=0
and is interpreted as providing legs on the core. In the following proof, it will be convenient to interpret the term by formally setting
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\star}(r)(1,0):=\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C}
, whereas
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\star}(r)(0,0)=\varnothing
because of the stability condition.

Proof.

Unwrapping the definition of plethysms of graded
\mathbb{S}
-spaces, the assignment of
(n,d)
on the right hand side is equal to

\coprod_{\delta=0}^{d}\coprod_{j=0}^{\infty}\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,j}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\delta)\times\left(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{*}(r)\right)^{\boxtimes j}(n,d-\delta)\right]/S_{j}.

Denote the item in the above formula indexed by
\delta,j
as
X_{\delta,j}
. We claim that
X_{\delta,j}
has the same class as the locally closed stratum
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{(\delta,j)}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
in
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
consisting of stable maps where the core has degree
\delta
, and the core has valency
j
: in other words, the number of rational tails and the number of legs on the core sum to
j
.

To prove the claim, we further stratify
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{(\delta,j)}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
by the unordered partitions
\lambda\vdash n
and
\mu\vdash(d-\delta)
into
j
non-negative parts, which record the distribution of the number of marked points and degrees onto each leg or rational tail. Let
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)
be such a refined stratum. From Lemmas
3.6
,
3.7
, we have that

\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)=\operatorname{Ind}^{S_{n}}_{S_{\lambda}}\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}_{1,j}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\delta)\times\prod_{i=1}^{j}\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\star}_{0}(r)(\lambda_{i},\mu_{i})\right]/\mathrm{Aut}(\lambda,\mu),

where
\mathrm{Aut}(\lambda,\mu)\subset S_{j}
is the subgroup which permutes the maximal blocks
I\subset\{1,\dots,j\}
such that for all
i\in I
,
\mu_{i}
are equal and
\lambda_{i}=0
. Geometrically,
\mathrm{Aut}(\lambda,\mu)
permutes the unmarked rational tails of the same degree.

Returning to the terms
X_{\delta,j}
in the plethystic formula, we notice that it can be partitioned along pairs of unordered partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
as

X_{\delta,j}=\coprod_{(\lambda\vdash n,\mu\vdash n-j)}\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,j}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\delta)\times Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}\right]/S_{j},

where each
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
is a union of
j
-fold products in
\left(\mathsf{pt}_{S_{1}}+\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{*}(r)\right)^{\boxtimes j}(n,d-\delta)
such that the unordered partitions on marked points and degrees recover
\lambda,\mu
. More explicitly, consider the set
L_{(\lambda,\mu)}
of pairs of ordered partitions that forget to
(\lambda,\mu)
, then
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
is a disjoint union

Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}=\coprod_{(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})\in L_{(\lambda,\mu)}}\prod_{i=1}^{j}\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0}^{\star}(r)(\lambda_{i},\mu_{i}).

The
S_{j}
-action on
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
permutes terms indexed by
(\tilde{\lambda},\tilde{\mu})\in L_{(\lambda,\mu)}
via composition. The stabilisers of
L_{(\lambda,\mu)}
under the
S_{j}
-action are uniformly
\mathrm{Aut}(\lambda,\mu)
. Therefore, the same holds for
Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
.

Applying Lemma
3.1
to the
S_{j}
-action on
\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{nrt}}_{1,j}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\delta)\times Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}
, we hence identify

\left[\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,j}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},\delta)\times Y_{(\lambda,\mu)}\right]/S_{j}\cong\overline{\mathcal{M}}^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{1,n}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d).

Notice that all the equalities and isomorphisms above are
S_{n}
-equivariant. Summing over all partitions
(\lambda,\mu)
and all
(\delta,j)
, we get the desired formula in the equivariant Grothendieck ring.
∎

4.1.
Localisation graphs

Consider the groupoid

Hence

\displaystyle\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{1,n}^{\mathrm{nrt}}(\mathbb{P}^{r},d)

\displaystyle\cong\coprod_{[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)}\coprod_{[f]\in\frac{\mathrm{Part}(C_{k},n)}{D_{k}(\delta)}}\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{\mathrm{Stab}_{D_{k}(\delta)}(f)}^{D_{k}(\delta)}\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{f(1)}\times\cdots\times S_{f(k)}}^{S_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{Cat}(f(i))\right)/D_{k}(\delta)

\displaystyle\cong\coprod_{[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)}\left(\coprod_{f\in\mathrm{Part}(C_{k},n)}\operatorname{Ind}_{S_{f(1)}\times\cdots\times S_{f(k)}}^{S_{n}}\prod_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{Cat}(f(i))\right)/D_{k}(\delta),

as we wanted to show.
∎

5.
Calculation of
\mathsf{Dih}_{r}

In this section we compute the class of the graded
\mathbb{B}
-space
\mathsf{Dih}_{r}
for
r\geq 1
. Recall that it is defined as in §
4
by:

\mathsf{Dih}_{r}(k,d)=\coprod_{[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)}\operatorname{Ind}_{D_{k}(\delta)}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}).

To state our theorem, we first define some polynomials in
r
. Fix an integer
d\geq 2
. Given an integer
k
with
2\leq k\leq d
and a divisor
j\mid k
, we set

(5.1)

F_{j,k,d}(r):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\ell}\\
{\ell\mid j\text{ and }k\mid d\ell}\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{j}{\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}(r^{\ell}+(-1)^{\ell}r).

Similarly, we set

(5.2)

G_{j,k,d}(r):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\ell}\\
{2\ell\mid j\text{ and }k\mid d\ell}\end{subarray}}\mu\left(\frac{j}{2\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}(r^{\ell+1}+r^{\ell}).

Note that both polynomials vanish when the sum is empty; for instance,
G_{j,k,d}(r)=0
if
j
is odd. Now, for
j
and
k
as above, we set

(5.3)

\theta_{j,k,d}(r):=\frac{\varphi(j)}{2k}\sum_{i\mid\frac{k}{j}}F_{i,k,d}(r)

and

(5.4)

\eta_{k,d}(r):=\sum_{j\mid k}\frac{G_{j,k,d}(r)}{4}.

These are the same factors
\theta_{j,k,d}(r)
and
\eta_{k,d}(r)
appearing in Theorems
A
and
B
. In this section, we prove the following formula for the graded wreath product symmetric function

{\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})\in K_{0}(\mathsf{M})\otimes\Lambda(S_{2})[[q]]},

in terms of the combinatorial factors
\theta_{j,k,d}
and
\eta_{k,d}
.

Theorem 5.5
.

Fix an integer
r\geq 1
. We have

\displaystyle\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{d}\left(\eta_{k,d}(r)\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\sum_{j\mid k}\theta_{j,k,d}(r)\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{k/j}\right)\right).

The proof of Theorem
5.5
amounts to enumerating localisation graphs with fixed isomorphism type of automorphism group. Given
[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)
, a choice of lift
\delta
determines a subgroup
D_{k}(\delta)
of
D_{k}
, well-defined up to conjugacy. Given a conjugacy class of subgroups
[H]
of
D_{k}
, we let

\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)=\{[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)\mid[D_{k}(\delta)]=[H]\}

and

\gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k):=\left|\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)\right|.

The relevance of these definitions is as follows. Let
O_{k}
denote a set of orbit representatives for the action of
D_{k}
on its subgroups by conjugation. Grouping
[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)
according to the conjugacy class types of
D_{k}(\delta)
, we have

(5.6)

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r}(k,d))=\sum_{[\delta]\in\Gamma_{r,d}(k)}\mathsf{e}^{2\wr k}\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{D_{k}(\delta)}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\right)=\sum_{[H]\in O_{k}}\gamma_{r,d}^{[H]}(k)\mathsf{e}^{2\wr k}\left(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C})\right),

where
H
is any choice of representative of
[H]
. We will spend the rest of this section explaining how to understand the set
O_{k}
and giving formulas for
\gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)
as
[H]
varies over all elements of
O_{k}
.

5.1.
Conjugacy classes of dihedral subgroups

The subgroup structure of
D_{k}
is well-known, see e.g.
[
15
, §3]
.

Proposition 5.7
.

Every subgroup of
D_{k}
is uniquely of the form
\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{i}\tau\rangle
for a divisor
j|k
and
0\leq i<j
.

If
k
is odd, then every subgroup of
D_{k}
is conjugate to a subgroup of the form
\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
or
\langle\rho^{j},\tau\rangle
, for a divisor
j\mid k
. If
k
is even, then every subgroup of
D_{k}
is conjugate to one of:

•

\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
for
j\mid k
;

•

\langle\rho^{j},\tau\rangle
for
j\mid k
;

•

\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle
for
j\mid k
and
j
even.

Therefore, the above list is a set of representatives of
O_{k}
.

For a decorated graph in
\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)
, adjacent vertices are always distinguished by their colours. Therefore, the subgroups
D_{k}(\delta)
never contain elements which stabilise an edge
e\in C_{k}
. This observation is summarised by the following lemma.

Lemma 5.8
.

Suppose
j
divides
k
, and suppose
H=\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle
for some
1\leq c\leq k
. Then

\gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)=0

if any of the following conditions hold:

(1)

k
is odd;

(2)

k
is even, and
j
is odd;

(3)

k
,
j
, and
c
are all even.

If
k
and
j
are even while
c
is odd, then
[\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle]=[\langle\rho^{j^{\prime}},\rho\tau\rangle]
where
j^{\prime}
is even. Thus we focus on computing
\gamma^{[\langle\rho^{j}\rangle]}_{r,d}(k)
for all
k
and
j\mid k
, as well as the numbers
\gamma^{[\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle]}_{r,d}(k)
when
k
is even and
j\mid k
is an even divisor.

5.2.
Frobenius reciprocity and normalisers

To count the unlabeled decorations

\gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)=|\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)|,

it is convenient to introduce and count certain auxiliary sets of labeled decorations as follows. For a fixed subgroup
H\leq D_{k}
, let

\tilde{\Gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k)\subset\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)

be the set of labeled decorations with stabiliser equal to
H
, and let

\tilde{\Gamma}^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)\subset\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)

be the set of labeled decorations with stabiliser conjugate to
H
. The following identifications follow from unwinding the definitions. They relate the sets of labeled decorations defined above to
\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)
.

Lemma 5.9
.

D_{k}
acts on
\tilde{\Gamma}^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)
, and

\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)=\tilde{\Gamma}^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)/D_{k}.

Let
N_{D_{k}}(H)
be the normaliser of
H
in
D_{k}
. Then we have an isomorphism of
D_{k}
-sets

\tilde{\Gamma}^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)\cong\operatorname{Ind}_{N_{D_{k}}(H)}^{D_{k}}\tilde{\Gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k).

By Frobenius reciprocity, this is equivalent to

\Gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)\cong\tilde{\Gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k)/N_{D_{k}}(H).

Using the orbit counting theorem, we have

\gamma^{[H]}_{r,d}(k)=\frac{1}{|N_{D_{k}}(H)|}\sum_{g\in N_{D_{k}}(H)}\tilde{\Gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k)^{g}=\frac{|H|}{|N_{D_{k}}(H)|}\tilde{\gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k).

The indices
|N_{D_{k}}(H):H|
for the list of subgroups appearing in Proposition
5.7
can be computed by elementary methods, leading to the following result.

Lemma 5.10
.

When
H=\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
for a divisor
j
of
k
, we have
N_{D_{k}}(H)=D_{k}
and in particular,

\gamma_{r,d}^{[\langle\rho^{j}\rangle]}(k)=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}_{r,d}(k)}{2j}.

When
k
is even and
j
is an even divisor of
k
, then for
H=\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle
, we have
N_{D_{k}}(H)=\langle\rho^{j/2},\rho\tau\rangle
. In particular we have

\gamma_{r,d}^{[\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle]}(k)=\frac{\tilde{\gamma}^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle}_{r,d}(k)}{2}.

The lemma allows us to focus our attention on computing the numbers
\tilde{\gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k)
of labeled decorations with stabiliser equal to a fixed subgroup
H\leq D_{k}
.

5.3.
Möbius inversion

The sets

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}^{H}(k)
are defined as decorations of
C_{k}
with stabiliser in
D_{k}
strictly equal to
H
. We instead enumerate decorations with stabiliser containing
H
, and then apply Möbius inversion to the subgroup lattice
\mathcal{L}(D_{k})
of
D_{k}
. We set the notation

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{H}:=\coprod_{H\subset H^{\prime}}\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}^{H^{\prime}}(k)

for decorations fixed by
H
. Let
\mu_{D_{k}}
denote the Möbius function of
\mathcal{L}(D_{k})
. Then Möbius inversion gives

\tilde{\gamma}^{H}_{r,d}(k)=\sum_{H\leq H^{\prime}}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(H,H^{\prime})\left|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{H^{\prime}}\right|.

The following lemma computes
\mu_{D_{k}}
in terms of the standard Möbius function
\mu
on the natural numbers.

Lemma 5.11
.

Suppose that
a
and
b
are divisors of
k
, such that
b\mid a
. Then we have

\mu_{D_{k}}(\langle\rho^{a}\rangle,\langle\rho^{b},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle)=-\frac{a}{b}\mu\left(\frac{a}{b}\right),

\mu_{D_{k}}(\langle\rho^{a}\rangle,\langle\rho^{b}\rangle)=\mu\left(\frac{a}{b}\right),

\mu_{D_{k}}(\langle\rho^{a},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle,\langle\rho^{b},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle)=\mu\left(\frac{a}{b}\right).

Proof.

The second two cases follow from the fact that the intervals

[\langle\rho^{a}\rangle,\langle\rho^{b}\rangle]\quad\mbox{and}\quad[\langle\rho^{a},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle,\langle\rho^{b},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle]

are each isomorphic as lattices to the lattice of divisors of
a/b
. For the first case, we argue as follows. If
G
is an arbitrary finite group, let
\mu_{G}
denote the Möbius function of the poset
\mathcal{L}(G)
of subgroups of
G
. If
N
is a normal subgroup of
G
which is contained in another subgroup
H
of
G
. Then

\mu_{G}(H,G)=\mu_{G/N}(H/N,G/N),

since the intervals
[H,G]
in
\mathcal{L}(G)
and
[H/N,G/N]
in
\mathcal{L}(G/N,H/N)
are isomorphic as lattices. Since cyclic subgroups of
D_{k}
are normal, we have

\mu_{D_{k}}(\langle\rho^{a}\rangle,\langle\rho^{b},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle)=\mu_{D_{a/b}}(1,D_{a/b}),

and we have

(5.12)

\mu_{D_{s}}(1,D_{s})=-s\mu(s),

as is likely well-known. For completeness, we provide a proof of (
5.12
). When
s=1
, we can calculate by hand that
\mu_{D_{1}}(1,1)+\mu_{D_{1}}(1,D_{1})=0
implies that
\mu_{D_{1}}(1,D_{1})=-1
. Perform induction on
s
, and suppose that the formula holds for all
s^{\prime}|s
. We apply the identity

\sum_{1\leq H\leq D_{s}}\mu_{D_{s}}(1,H)=0.

Using the list of subgroups of
D_{s}
, this can be expanded as

\sum_{i|s}\mu_{D_{s}}(1,\langle\rho^{i}\rangle)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i|s,i\neq 1\\
0\leq j<i\end{subarray}}\mu_{D_{s}}(1,\langle\rho^{i},\rho^{j}\tau\rangle)+\mu_{D_{s}}(1,D_{s})=0.

We have computed
\mu_{D_{s}}(1,\langle\rho^{i}\rangle)=\mu(s/i)
. For each divisor
i|s,i\neq 1
, we have
\langle\rho^{i},\rho^{j}\tau\rangle\cong D_{s/i}
,
so from the inductive hypothesis there is
\mu_{D_{s}}(1,\langle\rho^{i},\rho^{j}\tau\rangle)=-(s/i)\mu(s/i)
. Summing up, we have

\sum_{i|s}\mu(s/i)-s\sum_{{i|s,i\neq 1}}\mu(s/i)+\mu_{D_{s}}(1,D_{s})=0.

There is an identity
\sum_{i|s}\mu(s/i)=0
, hence

\sum_{i|s,i\neq 1}\mu(s/i)=-\mu(s)

so rearranging we have
\mu_{D_{s}}(1,D_{s})=-s\mu(s)
as desired.
∎

5.4.
Quotient graphs

We enumerate the sets
\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{H^{\prime}}
via the following construction of
quotient graphs
. This is an established technique for enumerating graphs with automorphisms
[
33
, §1]
.

Definition 5.13
.

Fix a
k
-cycle
C_{k}
with vertex and half-edge labeling as in §
4
. For a subgroup
H\subset D_{k}
that is in the list from Proposition
5.7
, the
quotient graph
of
C_{k}
under
H
, denoted as
C_{k,H}
, is the graph with vertices (resp. half-edges) as the quotient set
V(C_{k})/H
(resp.
H(C_{k})/H
), which determines the set of edges as
E(C_{k})/H
.

A quotient graph
C_{k,H}
is intuitively a ‘fundamental domain’ of the
k
-cycle under the action of
H
. It is convenient to pick the following vertex labeling.

•

For
H=\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
where
j\mid k
, we label the vertices as
\{1,\dots,j\}
, with each element representing their class modulo
j
; the quotient graph is a cycle on the
j
vertices.

•

For
H=\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle
where
1\leq c\leq k
is odd,
k
is even and
j
is an even divisor of
k
, we label the vertices as
\{1,\dots,j/2+1\}
; the quotient graph is a path with
j/2
edges on the
j/2+1
vertices.

Figure 3.
A labeled decorated graph in
\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,6}(6)^{\langle\rho^{2},\rho\tau\rangle}
with degree decoration in red and vertex colouring is given by red and black dots. Its quotient graph under
\langle\rho^{2},\rho\tau\rangle
-symmetry is on the right.

Remark 5.14
.

The quotient half-edge ordering on
C_{k,H}
gives a natural ordering of edges that is compatible with the one on
C_{k}
. When
C_{k,H}
is a path, such an ordering is determined by the natural ordering on the vertices. When
C_{k,H}
is a cycle, such an ordering is equivalent to labeling a half-edge, say
h_{1}^{+}
, on the cycle
C_{i}
. The ordering is not intrinsic to the quotient graph when
H=\langle\rho^{2}\rangle
, as illustrated below in Figure
4
.

Figure 4.
Two distinct labeled colourings with symmetry group
\langle\rho^{2}\rangle
. Their quotient graphs become the same after forgetting the edge orderings.

We write
\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r,d}(k)
for the set of
(r,d)
-decorations of a labelled path
P_{k}
with
k+1
ordered vertices; formally, such a decoration is an
(r+1)
-colouring
\delta_{V}:V(P_{k})\to\{0,\ldots,r+1\}
of
P_{k}
, together with a function
\delta_{E}:E(P_{k})\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
with
\sum_{e\in E(P_{k})}\delta_{E}(e)=d
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Suppose that
d\geq 2
and
2\leq k\leq d
. Assume that
j
is a positive divisor of
k
.

(1)

If
k\nmid dj
, then

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}=\varnothing.

If
k\mid dj
, then there is a bijection

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}\to\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,dj/k}(j).

(2)

Suppose
1\leq c\leq k
. If
c
is odd,
k
and
j
are both even, and
2k\mid dj
, then there is a bijection

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle}(k)\to\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r,dj/2k}(j/2).

Otherwise,

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle}=\varnothing.

Proof.

In both cases, for any element in
\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{H}
, the colouring and degree decoration are invariant under the induced
H
-action. Therefore, they descend along quotient maps

\displaystyle V(C_{k})\to V(C_{k})/H=V(C_{k,H}),

\displaystyle H(C_{k})\to H(C_{k})/H=H(C_{k,H}),

\displaystyle E(C_{k})\to E(C_{k})/H=E(C_{k,H})

and give colourings and degree decorations on the quotient graph
C_{k,H}
. This defines maps

\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}\to\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,dj/k}(j)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle}\to\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r,dj/2k}(j/2).

Inverses of the maps are given by pulling back vertex colourings and degree decorations on
C_{k,H}
along the quotient map of vertices and edges. One can check that the pullback does define a
(r,d)
-decoration on
C_{k}
. Therefore, the pairs of sets are in bijection.
∎

5.5.
Strata enumeration

Set

\omega_{k}(x):=x(x-1)^{k}

for the chromatic polynomial of a path with
k
edges, and let

\varpi_{k}(x):=(x-1)^{k}+(-1)^{k}(x-1)

denote the chromatic polynomial of
C_{k}

[
11
,
67
]
. They yield straightforward formulas for the numbers
\tilde{\gamma}_{r,d}(k)=|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)|
and
|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r,d}(k)|
.

Proposition 5.16
.

We have formulas

|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)|=\binom{d-1}{k-1}\varpi_{k}(r+1)\quad\mbox{and}\quad|\tilde{\varepsilon}_{r,d}(k)|=\binom{d-1}{k-1}\omega_{k}(r+1).

Proof.

In both cases, a decoration is chosen in two steps: choose a colouring, and then choose an ordered partition of
d
into
k
positive parts.
∎

The following corollary is now an immediate consequence of Lemma
5.15
.

Corollary 5.17
.

Suppose
d\geq 2
and
2\leq k\leq d
. Suppose
j\mid k
and
k\mid dj
. Then

\left|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}\right|=\binom{\frac{dj}{k}-1}{j-1}\varpi_{j}(r+1)

If
2\ell\mid k
and
k\mid d\ell
, and in addition
1\leq c\leq k
is odd, then

\left|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{2\ell},\rho^{c}\tau\rangle}\right|=\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\omega_{\ell}(r+1).

Now we apply Möbius inversion to compute the number of decorations of
C_{k}
with fixed stabiliser group. For the statement of the formula, recall that the polynomials
F_{j,k,d}(r)
and
G_{j,k,d}(r)
are defined in (
5.1
) and (
5.2
), respectively.

Proposition 5.18
.

Suppose that
k\geq 2
and
j
divides
k
. Then

\tilde{\gamma}_{r,d}^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}(k)=F_{j,k,d}(r)-\frac{j}{2}G_{j,k,d}(r).

If
k
is even and
j
is an even divisor of
k
, then

\tilde{\gamma}_{r,d}^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle}(k)=G_{j,k,d}(r).

Proof.

By Möbius inversion, Lemma
5.11
, and Corollary
5.17
, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}^{\langle\rho^{j}\rangle}_{r,d}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid j}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(\langle\rho^{j}\rangle,\langle\rho^{\ell}\rangle)|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{\ell}\rangle}|+\sum_{2\ell\mid j}\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\mu_{\mathcal{L}}(\langle\rho^{j}\rangle,\langle\rho^{2\ell},\rho^{2s-1}\tau\rangle)|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{2\ell},\rho^{2s-1}\tau\rangle}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid j}\mu\left(\frac{j}{\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\varpi_{\ell}(r+1)-\sum_{2\ell\mid j}\frac{j}{2\ell}\sum_{s=1}^{\ell}\mu\left(\frac{j}{2\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\omega_{\ell}(r+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell\mid j}\mu\left(\frac{j}{\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\varpi_{\ell}(r+1)-\frac{j}{2}\sum_{2\ell\mid j}\mu\left(\frac{j}{2\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\omega_{\ell}(r+1)

\displaystyle=F_{j,k,d}(r)-\frac{j}{2}G_{j,k,d}(r),

as desired. The second count is similar, except only the terms with
2\ell\mid j
survive:

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}^{\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle}_{r,d}(k)

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\ell\mid j}\mu_{P_{k}}(\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle,\langle\rho^{2\ell},\rho\tau\rangle)|\tilde{\Gamma}_{r,d}(k)^{\langle\rho^{2\ell},\rho\tau\rangle}|

\displaystyle=\sum_{2\ell\mid j}\mu\left(\frac{j}{2\ell}\right)\binom{\frac{d\ell}{k}-1}{\ell-1}\omega_{\ell}(r+1)

\displaystyle=G_{j,k,d}(r).

∎

The following corollary is immediate from Proposition
5.18
and Lemma
5.10
.

Corollary 5.19
.

Suppose that
k\geq 2
and
j
divides
k
. Then

\gamma_{r,d}^{[\langle\rho^{j}\rangle]}(k)=\frac{F_{j,k,d}(r)}{2j}-\frac{G_{j,k,d}(r)}{4}

If
k
is even and
j
is an even divisor of
k
, then

\gamma_{r,d}^{[\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle]}(k)=\frac{G_{j,k,d}(r)}{2}.

5.6.
Computing the characters

The final step in our calculation of
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})
is the computation of the characters
\mathsf{e}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 5.20
.

Suppose
j\mid k
.

(1)

If
H=\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
, then

\mathsf{e}^{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C})=\frac{j}{k}\sum_{s\mid\frac{k}{j}}\varphi(s)\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}.

(2)

If
H=\langle\rho^{j},\rho\tau\rangle
, then

\mathsf{e}^{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C})=\frac{1}{2}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\frac{j}{2k}\sum_{s\mid\frac{k}{j}}\varphi(s)\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}.

Proof.

In both cases we have
\mathsf{e}^{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathbb{C})=\operatorname{ch}_{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\mathrm{Triv}_{H}).
As explained in
[
54
]
,

\displaystyle\operatorname{ch}_{2\wr k}(\operatorname{Ind}_{H}^{B_{k}}\mathrm{Triv}_{H})=\frac{1}{|H|}\sum_{h\in H}\Psi(h),

where the cycle map
\Psi
is defined in §
2
. When
H=\langle\rho^{j}\rangle
, we have
|H|=\frac{k}{j}
, and for each divisor
s
of
k/j
, the group
H
contains exactly
\varphi(s)
rotations of order
s
. The
S_{k}
component of such a rotation admits a cycle decomposition as
k/s
many
s
-cycles, while the
S_{2}^{k}
component acts trivially. Therefore,
\Psi(h)=\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}
, and the first formula follows. In the second case, the order of the subgroup is
\frac{2k}{j}
. Each rotation acts in the same way as earlier, but each reflection
f
fixes two vertices, and switches the orientations of the two half-edges at each of these vertices. Hence we have
\Psi(f)=\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}
. Since there are
k/j
distinct reflections, the second formula follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.5
.

Plugging Proposition
5.20
and Corollary
5.19
into (
5.6
), and then switching the order of summation, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\sum_{j\mid k}\left(\frac{G_{j,k,d}(r)}{4}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\left(\frac{F_{j,k,d}(r)}{2k}-\frac{jG_{j,k,d}(r)}{4k}+\frac{jG_{j,k,d}(r)}{4k}\right)\sum_{s\mid\frac{k}{j}}\varphi(s)\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\sum_{j\mid k}\left(\frac{G_{j,k,d}(r)}{4}\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\frac{F_{j,k,d}(r)}{2k}\sum_{s\mid\frac{k}{j}}\varphi(s)\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\left(\sum_{k=2}^{d}\eta_{k,d}(r)\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\sum_{s\mid k}\left(\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}\sum_{j\mid\frac{k}{s}}\frac{\varphi(s)}{2k}F_{j,k,d}(r)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{d\geq 0}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\left(\eta_{k,d}(r)\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}+\left(\sum_{s\mid k}\theta_{s,k,d}(r)\mathfrak{p}_{s}^{k/s}\right)\right)

which is equivalent to the statement of the Theorem.
∎

6.
Calculation of
\mathscr{B}_{r}

In this section, we will assemble the results of previous sections to derive our formula for
\mathscr{B}_{r}
. From Theorem
4.1
we have that

(6.1)

\mathscr{B}_{r}=\mathscr{B}_{0}+\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat}).

The Serre characteristic
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})
was computed in §
5
. In this section, we will explain a formula for
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})
that appears in
[
7
, Remark 4.2]
, and then compute the plethysm
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})
. Combining this with Getzler’s calculation of
\mathscr{B}_{0}
will give our main technical Theorem
B
, recalled below, which implies Theorem
A
.

Theorem B
.

We have

\displaystyle\mathscr{B}_{r}

\displaystyle=(r+1)\left(\mathscr{A}_{1}+\frac{\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}(\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}+1)+\frac{1}{4}\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{n\geq 1}\frac{\varphi(n)}{n}\log(1-\psi_{n}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))\right)

\displaystyle\phantom{space}+\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\left(\eta_{k,d}(r)\frac{(1+2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0})^{2}}{(1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/2+1}}+\sum_{j\mid k}\theta_{j,k,d}(r)\frac{1}{(1-\psi_{j}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/j}}\right).

6.1.
The Serre characteristic of
\mathsf{Cat}
.

Recall that
\mathsf{Cat}
is the
S_{2}\times\mathbb{S}
space such that
\mathsf{Cat}(n)=\mathrm{Cat}_{2,n}
for
n\geq 1
, and
\mathsf{Cat}(0)
is a point. In this section we explain a formula for

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat}):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\mathsf{Cat}(n))\in K_{0}(\mathsf{M})\otimes\Lambda_{2}\otimes\Lambda

given by Bergström–Minabe
[
7
]
.
The formula, recalled in Theorem
6.2
below, expresses
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})
in terms of the known series

\mathscr{A}_{0}:=\sum_{n\geq 3}\mathsf{e}^{S_{n}}(\mathcal{M}_{0,n}),

and its derivatives

\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}:=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathscr{A}_{0}}{\partial p_{1}^{2}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}:=\frac{\partial\mathscr{A}_{0}}{\partial p_{2}}.

Theorem 6.2
.

We have

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\frac{1}{1-\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}}+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\otimes\frac{1+2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}.

The formula is stated in
[
8
, Remark 4.2]
. Here we explain how it follows from their main result on Serre characteristics of the Losev–Manin spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n}
. In Proposition 4.1 of loc. lit., they prove that

(6.3)

h_{2}\otimes 1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n})=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\frac{1}{1-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}}+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\otimes\frac{1+\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}}{1-\psi_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}\right)}

where
f_{n},g_{n}\in K_{0}(\mathsf{M})\otimes\Lambda
encode the Serre characteristic of the dense torus
\mathcal{M}_{0,2|n}\subset\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n}
:

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}({\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n})=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}+\frac{1}{2}p_{2}\otimes\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}g_{n}.

The modular interpretation of
\mathsf{Cat}
implies that its characteristic is obtained from
\sum_{n}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n})
via ‘distinguishing’ the light marked points, i.e., by applying the plethystic logarithm as in
[
21
, Proposition 8.6]
:

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})=\left(h_{2}\otimes 1+\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,2|n})\right)\circ(1\otimes\operatorname{Log}(p_{1})).

To compute
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})
, it then suffices to compute
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}\circ\operatorname{Log}(p_{1})
and
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}\circ\operatorname{Log}(p_{1})
. We calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\mathcal{M}_{0,2|n})\circ(1\otimes\operatorname{Log}(p_{1}))

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathsf{e}^{S_{2}\times S_{n}}(\operatorname{Res}_{S_{2}\times S_{n}}^{S_{n+2}}\mathcal{M}_{0,n+2})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}p_{1}^{2}\otimes\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}+p_{2}\otimes\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0},

where the first equality follows from
[
35
, Proposition 4.3]
, and the second follows from Lemma
2.12
. From the way
f_{i}
and
g_{i}
are defined, it follows that

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}f_{i}\circ\operatorname{Log}(p_{1})=\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}g_{i}\circ\operatorname{Log}(p_{1})=2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}.

Plugging this back into (
6.3
) gives the formula for
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})
as claimed in Theorem
6.2
. With this formula in hand, we can now compute

\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat}),

using the formula for
\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})
in Theorem
5.5
and basic properties of wreath product plethysm.

Theorem 6.4
.

For an integer
r\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Dih}_{r})\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})=\sum_{d\geq 2}q^{d}\sum_{k=2}^{d}\left(\eta_{k,d}(r)\frac{(1+2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0})^{2}}{(1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/2+1}}+\sum_{j\mid k}\theta_{j,k,d}(r)\frac{1}{(1-\psi_{j}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/j}}\right).

Proof.

This reduces to computing

\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{k/j}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})

for integers

with

2.17

2.18

\mathfrak{p}_{j}^{k/j}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})=\frac{1}{(1-\psi_{j}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime}))^{k/j}},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{2}^{k/2-1}\mathfrak{q}_{1}^{2}\circ_{S_{2}}\mathsf{e}(\mathsf{Cat})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}\right)^{k/2-1}\left(\frac{1+2\dot{\mathscr{A}}_{0}}{1-\psi_{2}(\mathscr{A}_{0}^{\prime\prime})}\right)^{2}

The result follows by plugging these expressions into Theorem
5.5

Rare variant optimized GWAS with functional validation identifies causal architecture of kidney function in East Asian population
Authors: Jiyun Jang, Jaeyong Choi, Sungji Moon, Jeong Mi Kim, Jong-Il Kim, Sun-Wha Im
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChronic kidney disease is a growing burden, yet the genetic architecture of kidney function requires further investigation. We performed a genome-wide association study of estimated glomerular filtration rate in 72,298 Korean individuals using a population-specific Korean Biobank Array and genetic imputation with a population matching imputation panel enabling high-resolution variant detection. We identified 30 independent signals including an East Asian-specific rare variant rs535291258. Through fine-mapping and functional annotation using epigenomic data, rs9895661 was predicted to modulate the binding affinity of the transcription factor TBX5, thereby influencing TBX2 expression. We also experimentally validated the allele-specific enhancer activity of this variant. Our results reveal both common and rare variants underlying kidney function in an East Asian population, highlight the value of population-specific approaches and illustrate how integrating epigenomic profiling and experimental approaches with GWAS results can connect genetic associations with molecular mechanisms of kidney function.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-47877-w.Keywords: Estimated glomerular filtration rate, Genome-wide association study, Population-specific, Experimental validation, Fine-mappingSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Genetics

Introduction

Chronic kidney disease (CKD) poses a substantial global health concern, with an estimated prevalence of approximately 10%, and rising mortality rates1,2. With global populations aging and lifestyle patterns changing, CKD risk continues to rise, leading to greater disease burden and reduced quality of life3. While many clinical risk factors for CKD such as diabetes, obesity and hypertension4 are known, the ethnic differences in the genetic background underlying CKD remain unclear, hindering precision medicine.

CKD exhibits marked differences in prevalence and progression across ethnic groups, suggesting that population-specific genetic factors contribute to disease susceptibility and outcomes. According to the 2017–2020 National Health and Nutrition Examination Survey, CKD was most prevalent in non-Hispanic black adults (19.5%), followed by non-Hispanic Asian (13.7%) and non-Hispanic Caucasian (11.7%)5. Some studies further suggest that CKD progresses more rapidly in East Asians (EAS) compared to Europeans, potentially due to both environmental and genetic factors6. The elevated burden remains even after adjusting for common comorbidities such as diabetes and hypertension, implying that EAS-specific genetic architectures may play an important role in disease susceptibility and progression6.

The parameter of CKD is primarily assessed by estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR), calculated from serum creatinine levels7,8. eGFR is a genetic trait with an estimated heritability of 33–82%9, and more than 1000 loci have been mapped through genetic association studies in the general population10. These genetic studies have provided important insights into the molecular mechanisms underlying kidney function; however, despite these advances, substantial limitations remain. Previous genome-wide association studies (GWAS) focused on Europeans11, and their findings often show limited transferability to other populations. This limitation arises from differences in allele frequencies (AF), effect sizes, and linkage disequilibrium (LD) structures across populations12,13. In addition, most GWAS variants lie in non-coding regions14, making it difficult to define their functional roles without further analysis or experimental validation. These limitations increase the need for large-scale genetic studies of CKD in EAS to enable personalized strategies for CKD prevention and management.

Here, we report the results of a GWAS for eGFR using genotype data from over 70,000 Korean individuals. This study aims to elucidate the genetic architecture of kidney function in an EAS population by identifying both common and rare variants, with particular emphasis on improving the detection of low-frequency and rare variants through a population-specific genotyping and imputation strategy. For non-coding variants, we employed a comprehensive annotation approach, leveraging publicly available epigenomic datasets and prediction tools to infer potential mechanisms of action. Furthermore, we experimentally validated the functional role of the top prioritized non-coding variant, providing mechanistic insights into its regulatory effect. This study offers fundamental insights into the molecular etiology of CKD and highlights the importance of functional follow-up for understanding the molecular basis, facilitating precision medicine for other ethnicities.

Methods

Study cohorts and phenotype measurements

This study was reviewed and approved by the institutional review board of Seoul National University Biomedical Research Institute (2004-080-1117). We used de-identified clinical and genomic information of 72,298 subjects from the Korean Genome and Epidemiology Study (KoGES). The cohort consisted of Korean Association Resource (KARE), Health Examinee (HEXA), and Cardiovascular Disease Association Study (CAVAS). KARE includes residents from the Ansan and Anseong regions, HEXA enrolled participants through routine health screenings in urban areas, and CAVAS recruited rural participants with a particular focus on cardiovascular diseases. All participants were over 40 years of age. eGFR was calculated using the CDK-EPI 2021 creatinine-based equation15. To reduce the influence of outliers, eGFR values were winsorized to a range of 15–200 mL/min/1.73m2. Phenotype values underwent rank-based inverse normal transformation to approximate a normal distribution. Inverse normalized eGFR values were adjusted for age, sex and the first five genotype principal components (PC1-PC5) to account for confounding factors. The final residualized and standardized phenotype values were used as input for analysis.

Genotyping and imputation

Genotyping was conducted using the Korean Biobank Array version 1.1, a customized genotyping array optimized for the Korean population. Individuals with low genotype call rate (< 96–99%), excessive heterozygosity, or sex discrepancies were excluded. Population structure was assessed using multidimensional analysis based on pairwise identity-by-descent (IBD) estimates, and population outliers likely reflecting population stratification or technical artifacts were removed. Pairwise IBD analysis was further used to identify cryptic relatedness, and one individual from each pair of first-degree relatives was excluded. Variants were filtered based on genotype call rate (< 95%), Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium (HWE) P value < 1E-6, and MAF < 1%. Quality-controlled genotype data were used as input for the imputation process. Genotype imputation was performed using a population matching imputation panel, the Northeast Asian Reference Database panel version 216, following the methods previously described. VCF files were preprocessed to remove duplicates, followed by phasing with Beagle17 and imputation using Minimac418. After imputation, the most probable genotypes were converted to hard calls for downstream quality assurance and association analysis. Post imputation filters, including MAF < 0.1%, imputation R2 < 0.3, missingness ≥ 0.1 and HWE P value < 1E-6 were applied to these hard-called genotypes, and HWE testing was not performed directly on imputed dosages.

GWAS and conditional analysis

GWAS was performed using the linear regression model implemented in Plink v.2.0 (Free Software Foundation Inc., Boston, MA, USA). Variant-based heritability of eGFR was estimated using LD score regression with summary statistics filtered for imputation quality INFO score ≥ 0.3 and MAF ≥ 0.01. LD scores and weights were derived from the KoGES genotypes. Variant annotation was carried out using ANNOVAR19. To identify additional independent association signals beyond the primary GWAS findings, we performed Conditional and Joint multiple single nucleotide polymorphism analysis (COJO) from the Genome-wide Complex Trait Analysis software20. All analyses were processed on computing servers at the Genomic Medicine Institute Research Service Center.

Fine-mapping and functional annotation

Fine-mapping was conducted using POLYgenic FUNctionally informed fine-mapping21, implementing the Sum of Single Effects (SuSiE) statistical model. Variants within ± 500 kilobases for each independent variant derived from COJO was used, and credible set was restricted to a total of 10 sets for each analysis. Posterior inclusion probability (PIP) cutoff of ≥ 0.1 was used to define putative causal variants because fine-mapping cannot fully disentangle correlated variants in regions of strong LD, resulting in shared PIP. Functional annotation of non-coding variants included ATAC peak calls and gene-enhancer links from ABC model in CAT-las22, regulatory annotations from EpiMap23,24 and predicted transcription factor binding by MotifbreakR25. Visualization of ATAC-seq signals, co-accessible regions, and GWAS variants was generated by pyGenomeTracks26,27.

Cell culture and luciferase assay

Human embryonic kidney epithelial cells (HEK293; ATCC, Manassas, VA, USA) were cultured in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium supplemented with 10% (v/v) heat-inactivated fetal bovine serum (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA) and 100U/ml penicillin and streptomycin. Cell lines were tested for the absence of mycoplasma contamination by PCR. Reporter fragments containing allele C or T of variant rs9895661, were synthesized to 113 bases in length with NheI (3’) and KpnI (5’) at both ends and cloned into a luciferase reporter vector (pGL3-promoter), generating constructs for the reference allele (rs9895661-C) and alternative allele (rs9895661-T) alleles (Supplementary Table S1). Oligo synthesis and cloning was all performed by Bionics, Seoul, Korea. HEK293 cells were seeded in 24-well plates 24 h prior to transfection. Transfection using lipofectamine 3000 reagent (Thermo Fisher Scientific) was performed following the manufacturer’s protocol, with a 20:1 ratio of luciferase reporter vectors and pRL-TK control vectors. After 24 h, cell lysates were prepared and luciferase activity was measured using the dual-luciferase reporter assay system (Promega, Madison, WI, USA). The ratio between firefly and renilla luciferase activities were normalized to the pGL3-promoter vector. All vectors used in this study were obtained from Promega. Allele-specific vectors were assayed in eight wells each, while promoter, control vectors were assayed in four wells and basic (pGL3-basic) and control (pGL3-control) vectors in two wells each.

Statistical analysis

Statistical significance was assessed using Graphpad Prism software (version 10, GraphPad Inc., La Jolla, CA, USA). The difference in luciferase activity between susceptible alleles of rs9895661 was determined using the Mann-Whitney U test. Data were shown as the mean ± standard deviation with *P < 0.05, **P < 0.001, ***P < 0.001, ns = statistically non-significant.

Results

Rare variant associated with eGFR in EAS population

We performed a genetic analysis of kidney function, utilizing eGFR based on genotype and phenotype data from 72,298 individuals enrolled in three population-based cohorts. The schema of this study is provided in Fig. 1. The sample sizes of the three cohorts were 58,700, 5,493, and 8,105, respectively. Mean age was 54.1 years, 64.1% were women. eGFR was lower in CAVAS (79.9) compared to KARE (95.5) and HEXA (96.1), likely due to the older age of participants in CAVAS. A summary of the demographic and clinical characteristics of these cohorts is provided in Supplementary Table S2. Given the observed genomic inflation (λGC = 1.15), we employed LD score regression to assess the extent of population stratification, as measured by the LD score intercept. The LD score regression was close to 1 (LD intercept = 1.02) and attenuation ratio was low (ratio = 0.09) indicating that confounding contributed minimally to the observed inflation and majority was attributable to polygenicity.

We identified 30 independent loci harboring genome-wide significant variants (P < 5E-8) associated with eGFR (Supplementary Table S3). We confirmed strong association signals at the well-established SHROOM3 [rs142647267, P = 2.51E-20, beta = -0.06, MAF = 0.228] and ALDH2 [rs671, P = 8E-25 beta = -0.07, MAF = 0.157] loci, both of which have been consistently linked to eGFR in previous studies28–30. Genomic region covering SHROOM3 gene is a well-characterized locus in the Japanese population, as demonstrated by Biobank Japan dataset, which reported that nearly half of the top eGFR-associated variants were mapped within this gene31. Knockdown of the gene in mice induced albuminuria and structural defects in podocytes in vivo, and impaired lamellipodia formation in vitro31 supporting its functional contribution to CKD. ALDH2 is also a well-known gene associated with kidney function in EAS, primarily due to its Glu504Lys polymorphism (rs671), which impairs aldehyde detoxification32. Reduced ALDH2 expression has shown to cause intracellular accumulation of toxic aldehydes, such as acrolein, which in turn promotes kidney fibrosis through metabolic reprogramming33,34. Restoration of ALDH2 expression in mouse models effectively mitigates fibrosis, highlighting its protective role in kidney pathology33. Among significant loci, we identified an independent variant rs2378816 [P = 9.54E-12, beta = 0.04, MAF = 0.226], a previously unreported intronic variant in BCAS3. This locus on chromosome 17 has been consistently implicated in kidney function, with rs9895661 reported as one of the top associated variants for serum creatinine and eGFR35. We also discovered rs372774554 [P = 2.18E-11, beta = 0.04, MAF = 0.295], another unreported intronic variant located in MKL1. MKL1 plays a pivotal role in kidney fibrogenesis, a pathological process that disrupts the architectural and functional homeostasis of the kidneys and is associated with poor prognosis in end-stage kidney disease36. In murine models, genetic deletion or pharmacological inhibition of MKL1 significantly attenuated kidney fibrosis following unilateral ureteral obstruction, primarily by downregulating the expression of connective tissue growth factor in kidney tubular epithelial cells36.

By leveraging a population-specific genotype array and a large-scale, population-matched imputation reference panel, our study achieved high imputation accuracy enabling the detection of rare variants (MAF < 0.01). We identified association signals for EAS-specific rare variant located on intergenic region between SLC22A2 and SLC22A3, rs535291258 [P = 1.83E-10, beta = -0.33, MAF = 0.002], which has not been previously reported in GWAS. According to gnomAD, the variant showed an AF of 0.00096 in EAS and is completely absent in Europeans, highlighting its population specificity. SLC22A2 and SLC22A3 both encode members of the organic cation transporter (OCT) family. OCT2 (SLC22A2) is the major basolateral transporter mediating tubular secretion of creatinine37,38, and OCT3 (SLC22A3) also contributes to proximal tubular cation handling39. Genetic variants in SLC22A2 have been previously noted for its function in eGFR and serum creatinine. The best characterized variants in this gene are rs3127573 in the 5’ region, reported as one of the genome-wide significant variants with the lowest p-values for serum creatinine and eGFR40, and rs316019, a common missense polymorphism (Ala270Ser) that impairs OCT2 transport activity41,42. Collectively, this rare variant may modulate nearby genes and thereby influence eGFR.

These findings highlight rs535291258 as an ancestry-specific rare signal associated with kidney function in the EAS population. Functional investigation of this and other newly identified rare variants will be an important next step to define the broader allelic spectrum and biological mechanisms underlying kidney function.

Protein altering variants associated with eGFR

Because rare variants often fail to reach genome-wide significance level due to limited power, we used a relaxed P value threshold of < 1E-5 to examine additional protein-altering variants across the allele-frequency of spectrum, including low-frequency and rare variants (Supplementary Table S4). We identified a total of 85 protein-altering variants associated with kidney function, of which three had MAF < 1%. Among these variants, several previously unreported variants were located in well-established kidney function related genes. For example, we identified rs74896528 (p.Pro87Ser), a missense variant in SESN2 [P = 6.47E-7, beta = -0.08, MAF = 0.025], a stress-responsive protein known to regulate oxidative stress43. Overexpression of SESN2 has been shown to alleviate diabetic kidney injury in mouse models by reducing reactive oxygen species, preserving mitochondrial function, and modulating pro-fibrotic signaling, particularly in the context of diabetic kidney disease44. Additionally, associations identified in recent GWAS studies with serum urate levels further implicate SESN2 in the regulation of uric acid metabolism and kidney traits45.

We also observed a variant related to kidney transporter. Rs34902660, a low-frequency missense variant (p.Gly116Val) in SLC17A3 [P = 2.38E-10, beta = -0.09, MAF = 0.039], a voltage-driven efflux transporter of urate and organic anions46. SLC17A3 is predominantly expressed in the kidney46, where it facilitates urate excretion and contributes to serum uric acid regulation. Genomic alterations in this gene have been associated with hyperuricemia and gout, highlighting its importance in maintaining uric acid homeostasis47,48. Notably, another variant, rs1165205 in SLC17A3 has previously been associated with elevated serum uric acid levels and increased risk of gout47.

Rs9895661 modify enhancer-mediated regulatory effect on kidney function

Although our study identified rare variants through a population-specific GWAS design, rs9895661 was selected for experimental follow-up as the top prioritized mechanistic candidate among the fine-mapped noncoding signals rather than on the basis of allele frequency alone. To better resolve causal variants beyond lead GWAS signals, we applied statistical fine-mapping and epigenetic annotation using the SuSiE model (Supplementary Table S5). We annotated noncoding variants with cell-type-specific epigenetic information to infer putative regulatory mechanisms. Through this approach, we assessed whether variants fell within open chromatin regions, interact with nearby genes, and evaluated potential changes in transcription factor binding affinity in response to allele alteration.

Among these, rs9895661 [PIP = 1.00], located in the upstream 20 kb of TBX2, emerged as a particularly compelling candidate based on multiple lines of regulatory evidence. In previous studies, rs9895661 is reported as a lead variant at the 17q23.2 locus, with strong statistical significance49, but its function was not experimentally verified. Our analysis from single cell chromatin accessibility data, suggested that this region was open only in three of cell types, including fetal metanephric mesenchyme and ureteric bud which are key progenitor populations during kidney organogenesis. Motif analysis predicted that the rs9895661-C allele has increased binding affinity for TBX5, a transcription factor critical for nephrogenesis and ureteric bud branching, and possibly participate in TGF-β-mediated pathways relevant to kidney fibrosis and repair50–52, compared to rs9895661-T allele. Gene-enhancer link by Epimap suggests this region to interact with TBX2 in multiple tissue types including kidney (Fig. 2a). Taken together, these findings indicate that rs9895661 exhibits allele-specific differences in TBX5 binding, thereby enhancing the regulatory interaction with TBX2.

To explore its regulatory effect, we experimentally investigated whether the genomic region containing rs9895661 had enhancer activity. Using a dual-luciferase reporter assay in HEK293 a human embryonic kidney cell line, we found that the enhancer activity of rs9895661-C allele is significantly higher than that of rs9895661-T allele, suggesting an allelic-specific enhancer activity (Fig. 2b).

Another possible causal variant, rs12437816 [PIP = 0.15] is located in an intergenic region between FBXO22 and UBE2Q2, overlapping with a kidney-specific enhancer predicted by both Epimap and CAT-las model. This locus lies within a region of open chromatin and enhancer-associated histone marks specifically in fetal kidney epithelial cells, supporting its putative regulatory role. ABC model predictions suggest that this enhancer may interact with the promoters of SCAPER, however, this prediction was derived from non-kidney tissue, as no kidney-specific ABC dataset was available.

Discussion

In this study, a GWAS for eGFR was performed in individuals of EAS ancestry. By incorporating a population-specific genotyping array along with a corresponding imputation panel, we achieved high-resolution variant detection, facilitating the discovery of unreported eGFR-associated variants, including population-specific rare variants. Fine-mapping and functional annotation pinpointed rs9895661 at the BCAS3 locus, previously reported lead variant whose functional role had remained uncharacterized. Functional experiments demonstrated allele-specific enhancer activity of this variant, supporting its potential role as a transcriptional regulator of genes implicated in kidney function, possibly including TBX2, a key gene involved in kidney development.

A large number of GWASs have uncovered thousands of variants associated with diverse traits and diseases. Most of the signals are explained by common variants, but rare variants with MAF ≤ 0.01 may serve unique roles in the genetics of complex disease. Unlike common variants, rare variants display distinctive characteristics such as lower LD with neighboring variants, stronger effects on gene expression or function, and larger population specificity53. These features make them more challenging to identify in conventional GWAS, but also valuable for uncovering causal mechanisms and ancestry-specific disease risk. However, discovery of rare variants has been limited due to genotyping arrays mainly targeted in loci previously reported in European populations, and imputation accuracy for rare alleles remains strongly dependent on panel size and ancestry composition. To address this issue, we applied an imputation panel optimized for the EAS population. Using the population-matched reference panel enriched for EAS genetic diversity, we were able to capture variants with both common and rare signals at the intergenic region between SLC22A2 and SLC22A3. Given the evidence of SLC22A2 variation on eGFR and creatinine levels, our finding may suggest this variant may likewise affect kidney function, although its functional role remains to be determined. Taken together, the discovery of such population-specific rare variants may help explain differences in CKD susceptibility and address part of the missing heritability of kidney function.

Fine-mapping of non-coding regions enabled us to resolve putative regulatory variants with strong evidence for causality. Among the fine-mapped variants, rs9895661 emerged as one of the strongest candidates. This variant has also been highlighted in previous studies as an important for eGFR, although it had not been functionally validated. In our study, supported by multiple epigenetic annotations, we confirmed the allele-specific effect on kidney cell lines through experimental validation. Within the same locus we also identified rs2378816, a variant which was assigned to a different credible set from rs9895661, indicating the possibility of other causal variants. This reflects the complexity of genetic regulation in this region and underscores the importance of fine-mapping for disentangling distinct causal pathways. However, although EpiMap provides supportive kidney-relevant epigenomic annotation linking rs9895661 to TBX2, evidence supporting a physical enhancer–gene interaction in the kidney remains limited because kidney specific ABC predictions are not available in current reference datasets.

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting our findings. First, although our study identified both common and rare variants associated with eGFR, functional follow-up of rare variants remains constrained by the limited availability of public kidney-relevant functional genomic resources, particularly for low-frequency and rare alleles. Expansion of ancestry-matched kidney epigenomic and transcriptomic reference datasets will therefore be important for future studies. Second, some protein-altering variants were examined using a relaxed significance threshold at P < 1E-5 in Supplementary Table S4 because rare variants often have limited power to reach conventional genome-wide significance. As a result, these signals should be therefore regarded as suggestive rather than definitive, and some may represent false-positive associations or inflated effect-size estimated; independent replication and further functional investigation will be required to confirm their roles in kidney function. Third, enhancer-gene mapping remained incomplete at some loci. Although Epimap provides supportive kidney-relevant epigenomic annotation linking rs9895661 to TBX2, evidence supporting a physical enhancer-gene interaction in the kidney remains limited because kidney specific ABC predictions are not available in current reference datasets. Fourth, our functional experiments were performed in HEK293 cells, which provide a human kidney-derived experimental system but do not fully recapitulate the transcriptional context of kidney progenitor or differentiated tubular epithelial cells. Therefore, further validation in kidney-specific cellular models and in vivo systems will be necessary. Finally, although several loci were supported in Biobank Japan, broader replication across additional East Asian populations will be important to refine the generalizability of our findings.

In summary, our study expands the understanding of genetic architecture of kidney function in EAS by identifying both common and rare variants through population-specific GWAS and high-resolution imputation. By integrating fine-mapping, epigenomic annotation, and functional validation, we were able to discover rs9895661 as a plausible causal variant. These findings not only provide insights into population-specific risk factors for CKD but also address the importance of incorporating diverse ancestries and rare variant discovery in future genetic studies. Nevertheless, further studies are needed, particularly functional validation of candidate variants and inclusion of additional EAS populations, to fully delineate the causal landscape of kidney function.

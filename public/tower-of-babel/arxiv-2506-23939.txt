Diffusion and heat dissipation in marginally stable linear time-delayed Langevin systems
Xin Wang

Abstract
We investigate the dynamics and heat dissipation in marginally stable linear time-delayed Langevin systems (i.e., the parameters of the system lie on the boundary of the stability conditions). We analytically examine two distinct critical classes: (i) diffusive criticality, where the variance grows linearly with suppressed/enhanced diffusion due to time-delayed forces, and (ii) oscillatory criticality, exhibiting oscillations with diffusing amplitude. Crucially, we derive asymptotic heat dissipation rates, revealing fundamentally different thermodynamic signatures: a constant dissipation rate for diffusive criticality and linear divergence accompanied by oscillations for oscillatory criticality. These results highlight how spectral properties of dynamics govern nonequilibrium thermodynamics in time-delayed systems. Our work bridges dynamics and stochastic thermodynamics at stability boundaries for linear systems, providing foundational insights for extending thermodynamic frameworks to nonlinear time-delayed stochastic systems.

However, existing research related to the thermodynamic properties of time-delayed systems mainly focuses on the stable dynamics. Even for linear time-delayed Langevin equations, explicit thermodynamic characterization at marginal stability (when the parameters of the system lie on the boundary of the stability conditions) is absent. A systematic analysis of linear time-delayed systems is crucial for further study on the characterization of thermodynamic properties in general nonlinear time-delayed stochastic systems.

Here, we address this gap by analytically calculate the dynamics and heat dissipation of marginally stable linear time-delayed Langevin systems. In particular, we examine two criticality classes: (1) diffusive criticality, where variance grows linearly with rescaled diffusivity, and (2) oscillatory criticality, where variance combines linear drift and oscillations. Surprisingly, these two types of diffusive dynamics exhibit fundamentally different heat dissipation: diffusive criticality attains a
constant dissipation rate
despite diverging variance, while oscillatory criticality displays
linear divergence
with oscillations. This discrepancy highlights how thermodynamic costs are affected by the spectral nature of criticality in time-delayed stochastic systems. Our results establish a foundation for extending thermodynamics to nonlinear time-delayed systems exhibiting bifurcations or multistability.

Linear time-delayed Langevin systems

We focus on a class of time-delayed systems governed by the overdamped Langevin equation:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\gamma\dot{x}(t)=ax(t)+bx(t-\tau)+\xi(t),&(t>t_{\text{init}}),\\
&x(t)=\phi(t),&\hskip-20.00003pt(t\in[t_{\text{init}}-\tau,t_{\text{init}}]),\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(1)

where
\tau>0
is a fixed delay time,
a
denotes the strength of a linear non-delayed force, and
b
represents the strength of the linear time-delayed force. The friction coefficient is given by
\gamma
, and
\xi(t)
is a white Gaussian noise satisfying
\left\langle\xi(t)\right\rangle=0
and
\left\langle\xi(t)\xi(t^{\prime})\right\rangle=2\gamma T\delta(t-t^{\prime})
, where
T
is the temperature of the thermal environment. The dynamics is initialized at
t=t_{\text{init}}
subject to a predefined historical function
\phi(t)
defined for
t\in[t_{\text{init}}-\tau,t_{\text{init}}]
. Throughout this work, we set
t_{\text{init}}=0
and
\gamma=1
without loss of generality.

According to
Küchler and Mensch 1992
, the solution of the dynamics Eq. (
1
) is as follows:

\displaystyle x(t)

\displaystyle=x_{0}(t)\phi(0)+b\int_{-\tau}^{0}x_{0}(t-s-\tau)\phi(s)ds

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}x_{0}(t-s)\xi(s)ds,

(2)

where
x_{0}
is the
fundamental solution
which corresponds to the solution of the deterministic dynamics:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\dot{x}(t)=ax(t)+bx(t-\tau),&(t>0),\\
&x(t)=\phi_{0}(t),&\hskip-20.00003pt(t\in[-\tau,0]),\\
\end{aligned}\right.

(3)

with initial conditions
\phi_{0}(t)=0
for
t<0
and
\phi_{0}(0)=1
. From Eq. (
II
), we see that the probability distribution of the solution is Gaussian, with the mean value and variance:

\displaystyle\langle x(t)\rangle

\displaystyle=x_{0}(t)\phi(t)+b\int_{-\tau}^{0}x_{0}(t-s-\tau)\phi(s)ds,

(4)

\displaystyle\langle x^{2}(t)\rangle

\displaystyle-\langle x(t)\rangle^{2}=2T\int_{0}^{t}x_{0}^{2}(s)ds.

(5)

The fundamental solution can be expanded in the exponential form
Yi et al. 2006
:

x_{0}(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{1+b\tau e^{-S_{k}\tau}}e^{S_{k}t},

(6)

where
\{S_{k}\}\ (k\in\mathcal{Z})
corresponds to all the roots that satisfies the characteristic equation of Eq. (
3
):

S_{k}-a-be^{-S_{k}\tau}=0

(7)

(see Appendix
B
for details).
From Eq. (
II
) and Eq. (
6
), we see that the distribution of the real parts of the characteristic roots determines the asymptotic behavior of the solution
x(t)
(Eq. (
II
)): if
\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}<0
, then the distribution of the solution converges to a steady one; if
\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}=0

\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}>0

There exists a stable solution (
\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}<0
) of Eq. (
1
) if and only if one of the following conditions is satisfied
Küchler and Mensch 1992
:

(i)

a+b<0
and
a-b\leq 0
.

(ii)

a+b<0
,
a-b>0
, and
\tau<\dfrac{\arccos\left(-\dfrac{a}{b}\right)}{(b^{2}-a^{2})^{1/2}}
.

In what follows, we focus on the marginally stable cases when
\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}=0
. According to Ref.
Hayes 1950
(see also Refs.
Cooke and Grossman 1982
;
Hale 1977
), there only exist two cases when
\max\{\text{Re}[S_{k}]\}=0

(a)

There is exactly one root equal to zero and all the others have negative real parts, if and only if
a+b=0
and
a\tau\leq 1
.

(b)

There are exactly two conjugate roots
\pm i\sqrt{b^{2}-a^{2}}
and all the others have negative real parts, if and only if
a+b<0,\ a-b>0,\text{and}\ \tau=\dfrac{\arccos\left(-\dfrac{a}{b}\right)}{(b^{2}-a^{2})^{1/2}}
.

For the case (a), as
a\tau=1
corresponds to divergent coefficients in Eq. (
6
), in the following discussion we only consider
a\tau<1
.

The heat dissipation of marginal stable dynamics

Define the heat dissipation rate as

J(t)dt\equiv[\gamma\dot{x}(t)-\xi(t)]\circ dx(t),

(19)

where
\circ
denotes the Stratonovich product

Munakata et al. 2009

Loos and Klapp 2019
;
Wang et al. 2025

In Appendix

1

\left\langle\frac{dq}{dt}\right\rangle=b^{2}\langle x^{2}(t-\tau)\rangle-a^{2}\langle x^{2}(t)\rangle+a\frac{d\langle x^{2}(t)\rangle}{dt}-aT

(20)

Substituting Eqs. (

Discussion

In this work, we have studied the dynamics and heat dissipation of two classes of criticality in marginally stable linear time-delayed Langevin systems. For diffusive criticality, variance grows linearly as
\langle x^{2}\rangle-\langle x\rangle^{2}\sim 2Tt/(1-a\tau)^{2}
, while heat dissipation asymptotes to a constant
aT(1+a\tau)/(1-a\tau)
. Oscillatory criticality exhibits variance with linear drift and oscillations, but its heat dissipation diverges linearly due to diffusive amplitude of oscillation. This result shows that thermodynamic cost of time-delayed stochastic systems is profoundly shaped by the spectral nature of criticality. Our exact results provide benchmarks for stochastic thermodynamics near bifurcations in time-delayed systems.

Extending this work to nonlinear time-delayed systems presents significant challenges but offers the potential for rich insights. A promising direction involves investigating stochastic resonance phenomena within these systems. It is plausible that a nontrivial resonance also manifests in their heat dissipation profiles. Furthermore, the inherent nonlinearity may suppress amplitude diffusion of Hopf bifurcations, potentially leading to sustained finite-amplitude oscillations in time-delayed stochastic systems. Analyzing the thermodynamic properties of such oscillations constitutes another compelling avenue for research. Finally, the thermodynamic implications of collective behavior in time-delayed interacting stochastic systems represent a crucial open question.

Appendix B
Derivation of the exponential expansion of the fundamental solution Eq. (
6
)

We show how to derive Eq. (
6
) in detail based on the method in Ref.
Yi et al. 2006
.
Consider the linear delayed differential equation Eq. (
3
), its solution is (set
T=0
in Eq. (
II
)):

x(t)=x_{0}(t)\phi(0)+b\int_{-\tau}^{0}x_{0}(t-s-\tau)\phi(s)ds,

(25)

where
x_{0}(t)
is the fundamental solution of Eq. (
3
) with initial conditions
\phi_{0}(t)=0
for
t<0
and
\phi_{0}(0)=1
.

We expand solution of Eq. (
3
) by exponential series using the
characteristic roots of Eq. (
7
):

x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}C_{k}e^{S_{k}t},

(26)

where the coefficients are left to determined according to the dynamics (
a,b
and
\tau
) and the
initial conditions (
\phi(t)
). To determine the coefficients, first we calculate the Laplace transformation of Eq. (
3
):

sX(s)-x(0)=aX(s)+be^{-s\tau}X(s)+b\Phi(s),

(27)

where

\displaystyle X(s)\equiv\mathcal{L}[x(t)]=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}x(t)dt,

(28)

\displaystyle\Phi(s)=\int_{0}^{r}e^{-st}\phi(t-\tau)dt.

(29)

and the Laplace transformation of
X(t-\tau)
is calculated as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[x(t-\tau)]=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-st}x(t-\tau)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-st}x(t-\tau)dt+\int_{\tau}^{\infty}e^{-st}x(t-\tau)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-st}\phi(t-\tau)dt+\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-s(t^{\prime}+\tau)}x(t^{\prime})dt^{\prime}\quad(t^{\prime}=t-\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}e^{-st}\phi(t-\tau)dt+e^{-s\tau}X(s)

\displaystyle=\Phi(s)+e^{-s\tau}X(s).

From Eq. (
27
), we have

X(s)=\dfrac{x(0)+b\Phi(s)}{s-a-be^{-s\tau}}.

(30)

On the other hand, the Laplace transformation of the exponential-expanded representation is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}[x(t)]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}\left(\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}C_{k}e^{S_{k}t}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{C_{k}}{s-S_{k}}.

(31)

Comparing Eq. (
30
) with Eq. (
B
), we can deduced the coefficients

\displaystyle C_{k}

\displaystyle=\underset{s=S_{k}}{\mathrm{Res}}\left(\dfrac{x(0)+b\Phi(s)}{s-a-be^{-s\tau}}\right)

\displaystyle=\dfrac{x(0)+b\Phi(S_{k})}{1+b\tau e^{-S_{k}\tau}}.

(32)

Therefore, the exponential expansion of the solution can be written as

x(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{x(0)+b\Phi(S_{k})}{1+b\tau e^{-S_{k}\tau}}e^{S_{k}\tau}.

(33)

Comparing Eqs. (
33
) and (
29
) with Eq. (
25
), we obtain the exponential expansion of the fundamental solution:

x_{0}(t)=\sum_{k=-\infty}^{\infty}\dfrac{1}{1+b\tau e^{-S_{k}\tau}}e^{S_{k}t}.

(34)

From the definition Eq. (

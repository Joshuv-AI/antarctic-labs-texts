Study on damage and failure stage identification of backfill driven by acoustic emission data
Authors: Shuang Li, Xiaoyong Dong, Ran Ma, Weihui Tian, Keke Zhao, Hui Xiang, Jiongjiong Zhu, Guohang Sun
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study proposes an intelligent approach for classifying damage and deformation stages in backfill materials using acoustic emission (AE) monitoring and machine learning. Uniaxial compression tests were conducted to capture the evolution of AE parameters during backfill failure, and sensitivity analysis was performed to identify key indices reflecting damage progression. A back propagation (BP) neural network model was developed, with input features optimized to {rise time, count, energy, duration, amplitude} based on statistical evaluation of parameter sensitivity. The model was trained and validated using experimental AE data. The effectiveness of the approach for classifying damage and deformation stages in backfill was confirmed with a classification accuracy of 93.52%. This approach enhances the understanding of failure mechanisms in backfill materials and provides a reliable tool for real-time monitoring and early warning of backfill failure in filling mining.Keywords: Acoustic emission, Failure stages identification, Neural network, BackfillSubject terms: Natural hazards, Materials science, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

In recent years, the backfill mining method has gained significant traction in mining operations owing to its multifaceted advantages, such as mitigating ground pressure1, preserving surface stability2, recycling tailings efficiently3, and reducing environmental impacts in mining regions4. The mechanical properties of backfill materials are critical to ensuring the structural integrity of underground excavations and the long-term stability of backfill-supported mining systems, directly influencing operational safety and sustainability.

During the load-bearing phase of backfill, internal microcracks nucleate, propagate, and eventually coalesce into macroscopic fractures, culminating in structural instability and failure. Acoustic emission (AE) monitoring enables real-time characterization of internal damage evolution in materials, tracking progressive failure stages, and providing early warning signals for impending failure. Its application has driven substantial progress in understanding backfill failure mechanics and enhancing operational safety. For instance, Scholz5 analyzed AE activity during the deformation and failure processes of rocks, observing a sharp increase in AE events before rock instability. Lockner6,7 further confirmed these findings through experimental studies, revealing that the b-value decreases to a minimum before rock failure. Similarly, Gang et al.8 conducted uniaxial compression AE tests on basalt and observed a “quiet period” of AE activity before failure. Research by Kong9 and Hirata10 further highlighted that reductions in the fractal dimension of AE parameters can serve as a precursor to instability. Qin11 utilized AE sensors to acquire information on rock damage under triaxial loading conditions. They summarized the evolutionary characteristics of AE parameters during various stages of rock damage. Song12 conducted uniaxial and triaxial tests on coal and rock specimens, clearly demonstrating the correlation between AE parameters and the stages of rock damage.

Recent advancements in intelligent algorithms, particularly machine learning and signal processing techniques, have prompted researchers to integrate these tools into AE data analysis. For example, Xu et al.13 utilized the Extreme-Point Symmetric Mode Decomposition (ESMD) method to reconstruct AE signals, and indicators are defined to systematically describe the rock failure process. Similarly, Liu et al.14 implemented neural networks to classify AE signals, achieving high-accuracy differentiation between microfracture mechanisms and ambient noise. These innovations demonstrate how intelligent algorithms enhance the interpretability and predictive capability of AE monitoring, advancing the field toward data-driven failure forecasting.

While AE parameters are effective in reflecting the stage-specific characteristics of specimen damage and failure, their sensitivity to distinct failure phases varies considerably across different parameters. Previous studies have predominantly relied on qualitative observational analyses to delineate these stages, such as monitoring increases in ring-down counts preceding fracture events. Although such empirical observations yield valuable phenomenological insights, they often fail to establish robust quantitative correlations with the progressive evolution of specimen damage. Furthermore, commonly employed AE parameters are frequently selected based on subjective criteria for analysis, neglecting systematic evaluation of their stage-dependent efficacy. This methodological gap not only undermines the analytical rigor of studies but also constrains the formulation of generalizable predictive models. To address these limitations, a systematic sensitivity assessment of AE parameters is imperative. By methodically identifying parameters with high diagnostic specificity to critical failure stages and excluding those with marginal sensitivity, researchers can develop a data-driven approach that exhibits enhanced robustness, precision, and applicability across diverse geological contexts.

This study achieves intelligent recognition of backfill damage and failure stages by integrating AE monitoring with machine learning. Uniaxial compression tests revealed the dynamic evolution of AE parameters during progressive failure, with theoretical analysis identifying key parameter sensitivities to specific damage stages. An algorithm was developed to extract highly sensitive indicators (rise time, count, energy, duration, amplitude), which were then used to train a neural network model for stage classification. Experimental validation demonstrated the model’s 93% accuracy in recognizing damage evolution. This approach enables real-time safety evaluation and early warning for backfill-dependent mining operations.

Experimental

Specimen preparation

Tailings obtained from an iron ore mine in Jidong, China, were utilized as the primary aggregate, while 42.5-grade ordinary Portland cement served as the binder. Cemented backfill specimens were fabricated with dimensions of 200 mm × 200 mm × 200 mm, adhering to a cement-to-sand ratio of 1:10. The constituent materials were homogenized using a mechanical mixer to ensure uniformity, after which the blended slurry was cast into steel molds compliant with ISO standards for backfill testing.

Following casting, specimens were cured under controlled environmental conditions (relative humidity: 95 ± 2%; temperature: 20 ± 1 °C) for 28 days to achieve target mechanical properties and hydration stability. Post-curing, specimens were demolded using non-destructive protocols and subjected to rigorous quality control checks, including visual inspection for surface anomalies and dimensional verification with ± 0.5 mm tolerance. Only specimens meeting predefined criteria for structural integrity and geometric conformity were retained for mechanical testing.

Experimental setup

The experimental system comprises a loading system and an acoustic emission (AE) monitoring system (Fig. 1).

Loading system Uniaxial compression tests were performed using a TAW-3000 microcomputer-controlled electro-hydraulic servo rock triaxial testing machine. This system ensures precise load application with consistent displacement rates (0.5 mm/min), enabling accurate recording of the stress–strain curve, peak strength, post-peak behavior, and deformation characteristics. Its high-precision actuators and closed-loop feedback control minimize experimental variability, ensuring repeatable results.

Acoustic emission monitoring system AE signals were captured using a PCI-2 system (Physical Acoustics Corporation, USA) with eight channels. The system’s high-frequency sensors (20–1000 kHz bandwidth) detect microcrack activity with a sensitivity threshold of 40 dB. Key parameters (rise time, ring count rate, energy, duration time, amplitude, absolute energy, and main frequency) were recorded. Sensors were mounted on the specimen surface using high-viscosity coupling grease to optimize signal transmission, and background noise was filtered using a 40 dB threshold. These AE monitoring system configurations are essential to capture high-quality data on the failure process within backfill specimens and enable precise classification of damage and failure stages.

This integrated framework provides a robust foundation for investigating failure mechanisms of backfill under uniaxial compression.

Loading strategy

A two-stage loading strategy ensured experimental reproducibility and data accuracy. First, a preload of 10 kN was applied at 1 mm/min to eliminate surface irregularities and establish uniform specimen-machine contact. This preconditioning step stabilized the system for subsequent testing.

The main test employed displacement-controlled loading at 0.15 mm/min, balancing precision in stress application with resolution of AE signals. This quasi-static rate enabled detailed tracking of progressive damage mechanisms: microcrack nucleation, stable propagation, and coalescence into macroscopic fractures.

Mechanical and AE systems were synchronized via analog triggers, aligning load–displacement data with AE event timestamps (± 1 ms). This integration allowed real-time correlation of stress–strain behavior with AE signatures, ensuring robust analysis of damage evolution.

Experimental results

Under uniaxial compression, scholars widely classify the deformation and failure process of backfill materials into four distinct stages based on stress–strain curve evolution: initial compaction stage, elastic stage, plastic failure stage, and post-peak failure stage15,16. Building on this research and leveraging stress–strain data from the loading system, this study similarly delineates the deformation and damage progression into four stages.

The stress–strain curves of backfill specimens with identical cement-to-sand ratios exhibited high consistency, allowing a representative sample to be selected for detailed analysis. The divergent trends of ring-down count rate and cumulative energy highlight the influence of material heterogeneity (e.g., porosity, binder distribution) on AE signal generation and detection. Notably, the sharp decline in ring-down counts preceding peak stress (Stage III) serves as a precursor to backfill failure, while the energy plateau correlates with critical macrofracture development. These findings demonstrate that AE parameters can provide reliable quantitative markers for classifying damage and failure stages in cemented backfill.

The identification method for the damage and failure stages of backfill

Method overview

The classification of backfill damage and failure stages is inherently a classification problem, solvable through a Back Propagation (BP) neural network. This algorithm excels in modeling nonlinear relationships between AE parameters and damage progression, leveraging its adaptive learning capability to map complex failure mechanisms. However, the model’s efficacy depends critically on selecting discriminative input features that capture stage-specific damage signatures. Optimal feature selection reduces dimensionality, mitigates overfitting, improves interpretability, and maintains predictive accuracy.

To ensure robust classification, AE parameters were quantitatively screened using sensitivity analysis and statistical criteria. Parameters such as energy, amplitude, and event rate were prioritized for their strong correlation with the failure stages.

Based on the selected AE parameters, a BP neural network structure is designed to accurately identify the damage and failure stages of backfill. The neural network framework includes input layers corresponding to the selected AE parameters, hidden layers optimized for feature extraction, and an output layer representing the classified stages of damage and failure. The specific design ensures a balance between model complexity and performance. The detailed methodology is described as follows.

While Principal Component Analysis (PCA) is widely used for dimensionality reduction, its composite projections obscure the physical interpretability of raw parameters—a limitation incompatible with this study’s goal of preserving mechanistic links between AE features and damage stages. Design of BP neural network classification algorithm

Input and output layer data extraction for neural networks

The Back Propagation (BP) neural network is uniquely suited for classifying the damage and failure stages of backfill materials due to its ability to autonomously learn nonlinear relationships between input features and target outputs. This study leverages AE parameters {rise time, ring count, energy, duration time, amplitude} as input features. These parameters are normalized to eliminate scale discrepancies and fed into a network architecture designed to map AE signatures to four damage stages derived from the stress-time curve: initial compaction stage, elastic stage, yield stage, and post-peak failure stage. The output layer comprises four nodes, each yielding a value between 0 and 1 to represent the probability of the input belonging to each respective damage stage. Design of BP neural network architecture

The BP neural network in this study employs two hidden layers to model the nonlinear relationship between AE parameters and backfill damage stages. The input layer consists of five nodes, each corresponding to an AE parameter (rise time, ring count rate, energy, duration, and amplitude), while the output layer comprises four nodes representing the damage stages (Table 1). A softmax activation function is applied to the output layer to generate mutually exclusive probabilistic outputs, with the final classification determined by selecting the stage exhibiting the highest probability value.

Dropout regularization (rate = 0.2) is incorporated between layers to enhance generalization, with training conducted using categorical cross-entropy loss for probabilistic optimization.

A notable aspect of this study is the reliance on subjective experience for determining both the number of hidden layers and the number of neurons within them. This experiential approach necessitates careful model design. Alternative methods utilizing intelligent optimization algorithms, such as GA, PSO, and SA, for hyperparameter tuning are well-established17. Nevertheless, the empirically selected parameters achieved sufficiently high prediction accuracy, making such optimization techniques unnecessary for this work. These techniques become essential when target accuracy thresholds (e.g., > 90%) prove difficult to attain through manual configuration.

To better analyze the predictive performance of the proposed model, the confusion matrix is visualized in Fig. 6. The results demonstrate high classification accuracy across all four failure stages, indicating strong practical utility. While some mutual misclassification occurs between neighboring stages, the predominant predictions remain concentrated along the main diagonal. This indicates the model retains significant discriminative capability for these adjacent stages.

Confusion matrix.

Figure 6 reveals that misclassification in the confusion matrix predominantly occurs between neighboring stages during transitions. This stems from the fundamental challenge in defining stage boundaries: while damage stage classification relies on interpreting stress–strain curve evolution, these curves lack sharp discontinuities at transition points. The resulting ambiguity introduces inherent labeling uncertainty, inevitably leading to predictable misclassification near stage boundaries.

Crucially, misclassification rates decrease substantially once backfill fully enters a distinct damage stage. This key observation, supported by Fig. 6, highlights the robustness of the proposed model within stable stages. Overall, the concentrated confusion matrix distribution and low misclassification rate confirm the algorithm’s high reliability and application potential for multi-stage stress–strain behavior identification.

The high accuracy of this model demonstrates its robustness in capturing nonlinear relationships between acoustic emission parameters and mechanical behavior. This approach advances intelligent classification of backfill failure processes while contributing to safer and more efficient mining operations through enhanced real-time damage assessment capabilities.

Application prospects

In filling mining, the mechanical properties of cemented backfill are critical to ensuring operational safety and efficiency. During load-bearing phases, the nucleation and propagation of internal fractures within the backfill can lead to structural instability, jeopardizing the integrity of mining operations. Consequently, accurate identification and real-time monitoring of backfill damage progression are essential for risk mitigation and process optimization.

The methodology proposed in this study—leveraging AE data and a BP neural network for stage-specific damage classification—addresses this need effectively. On an engineering scale, integrating microseismic monitoring systems could enable real-time acquisition of microfracture signatures (e.g., event rates, energy release) to map backfill damage dynamics. The BP neural network’s classification capabilities, validated at 93.52% accuracy, allow precise identification of critical failure thresholds (e.g., yield initiation, macrofracture coalescence), providing actionable early warnings to adjust mining parameters. Such adjustments can minimize backfill degradation, prolong structural service life, and enhance resource utilization efficiency. Additionally, the model’s ability to correlate AE trends with damage stages supports sustainable practices by reducing environmental impacts, such as subsidence or tailings leakage, through proactive stability management.

However, there are still certain challenges in the practical application of the proposed model in engineering. For example, the arrangement of sensors needs to consider the complex terrain and ore body structure of the mine to ensure comprehensive and accurate monitoring. Real-time data analysis must consider the impact of harsh mining environments, and the deployment and maintenance of the algorithm also require consideration of costs and technical challenges.

Conclusion

This study developed a neural network-based framework to identify damage stages in backfill materials, validated through experimental data and error analysis. Key findings include:

Leveraging statistical theory, a systematic approach was established to isolate critical AE parameters governing backfill damage evolution. Experimental results identified rise time, ring-down count, energy release, duration, and amplitude as the most sensitive indicators for stage classification, forming a robust feature set for algorithmic training.

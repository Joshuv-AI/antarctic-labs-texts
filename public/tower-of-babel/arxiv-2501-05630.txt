Smoothing surfaces on fourfolds
Scott Nollet, A. P. Rao

Abstract
. If ℰ , ℱ {\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}} are vector bundles of ranks r − 1 , r r-1,r on a smooth fourfold X X and ℋ ​ o ​ m ⁡ ( ℰ , ℱ ) \mathop{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}) is globally generated, it is well known that the general map ϕ : ℰ → ℱ \phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}} is injective and drops rank along a smooth surface. Chang improved on this with a filtered Bertini theorem. 1.
Introduction

Smoothing results are useful in algebraic geometry, as seen in the many applications of the Bertini theorems
[
14
]
.
A classical theorem says that if
{\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}
are vector bundles of ranks
r-1,r
on a smooth variety
X
and
\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})
globally generated, then the general map
\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to F
is injective and if not locally split, drops rank
along a codimension
2
subvariety
Y\subset X
which is smooth away from a set of codimension
\geq 4
in
Y

[
15
]
.
Chang substantially refined this result with her filtered Bertini theorem
[
4
]
.
To state it, suppose that
0={\mathcal{E}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{E}}_{1}\subset\dots{\mathcal{E}}_{n}={\mathcal{E}}
and

0={\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subset\dots{\mathcal{F}}_{n}={\mathcal{F}}
are filtrations by subbundles and define

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{i}=\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}-\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{E}}_{i}\text{ for }i<n\\
{\mathcal{B}}=\{\phi\in\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}):\phi({\mathcal{E}}_{i})\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{i}\}\subset\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}}).\end{array}\right.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
{\mathcal{B}}
is globally generated, then the general map

\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\ \to{\mathcal{F}}
drops rank along
Y
of codimension two (if non-empty) and

\mathop{\rm codim}_{Y}\mathop{\rm Sing}Y\geq\min\{2\alpha_{i}-1,\alpha_{i}+2,4\}
.

The lower bound in Theorem
1.1
is the expected codimension.
When
\dim X\leq 4
, this says that
Y
is smooth if
\alpha_{i}\geq 2
for each
i<n

(when
\dim X=5
, we need
\alpha_{i}\geq 3
).
Motivated by the liaison theory of the Horrocks-Mumford bundle
[
13
]
, we aim to extend
Theorem
1.1
to situations where
\dim X\leq 4
and
(a)
{\mathcal{F}}
is not a vector bundle or (b)
{\mathcal{B}}
is not globally generated.

We use Fitting schemes to classify rank
r
sheaves
{\mathcal{F}}
on a smooth variety
X
for which there are locally non-split
maps
{\mathcal{O}}^{r-1}\to{\mathcal{F}}
dropping rank along a
smooth subvariety of codimension two (Proposition
2.3
),
calling the resulting sheaves

2

(CD2 for short).
These generalize the curvilinear sheaves on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
introduced by Hartshorne and Hirschowitz
[
12
]
.
Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a rank
r
CD2 reflexive sheaf whose singular scheme
\mathop{\rm Sing}{\mathcal{F}}
has integral curve components and
{\mathcal{E}}
be a rank
r-1
vector bundle.
Suppose
{\mathcal{E}}
has a locally split filtration
{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
by subbundles, and
{\mathcal{F}}
has a locally split filtration
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
by CD2 reflexive sheaves.
Define
{\mathcal{B}}
and

as in (
1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Suppose
\dim X=3
or
4
.
If
{\mathcal{B}}
is globally generated and
\alpha_{i}\geq 2

, then
\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}

2

When

.

Our second result gives a variant of Theorem

when

is not globally generated.
It is harder to make an abstract statement, so we take

with

,

and
{\mathcal{F}}=\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(b_{j})\oplus{\mathcal{G}}

is a rank

for which the evaluation map

Assuming

The corresponding sheaf

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose

with

or
4
.
If

for

, then

1.1.
Applications to linkage theory

Linkage theory
[
19
,
30
]
treats general locally Cohen-Macaulay subschemes of
\mathbb{P}^{d}
of codimension
2
, but one is often interested in which subschemes
Z\subset\mathbb{P}^{d}
can be deformed to a smooth variety within its even linkage class
{\mathcal{L}}
.
By
[
31
]
, there is a vector bundle
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
with
H^{1}_{*}({\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{\vee})=0
corresponding to
{\mathcal{L}}
for which each
Y\in{\mathcal{L}}
has a resolution of the form

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(-a_{i})&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}&\oplus{\mathcal{O}}(-b_{j})\oplus{\mathcal{N}}_{0}&\to&{\mathcal{I}}_{Y}(t)&\to&0\\
&&||&&||&&&&\\
&&{\mathcal{E}}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\phi}}{{\to}}&{\mathcal{F}}&&&&\end{array}

with
t\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, so smoothing becomes a question of whether a general map
\phi
drops rank along a smooth subvariety of codimension
2
.
Chang
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
applied Theorem
1.1
to these resolutions to classify smooth arithmetically Buchsbaum codimension
2
subvarieties in
\mathbb{P}^{d}

for
d\leq 5
: none exist for
d\geq 6
, as predicted by Hartshorne’s conjecture
[
9
]
.
Building on work of Sauer
[
32
]
, Steffen used Theorem
1.1
to classify codimension
2
smooth connected ACM subvarieties in
\mathbb{P}^{n}

[
33
]
.
An interesting feature of these examples is that
every integral curve in ACM or arithmetically Buchsbaum linkage classes on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
is
smoothable
[
4
,
8
,
29
]
.
The same holds for ACM or arithmetically Buchsbaum linkage classes of surfaces on
\mathbb{P}^{4}

[
4
,
24
]
.
This makes it easy to write down the deformation classes having a smooth variety because there is a numerical criterion for integrality in these classes
[
24
,
25
]
.

Our work here is motivated by the linkage theory of the Horrocks-Mumford
bundle
{\mathcal{F}}_{HM}

[
13
]
.
It is the only known indecomposable rank two vector bundle on
\mathbb{P}^{4}_{k}
if char
k=0
, though others have been discovered when char
k=p>0

[
20
,
21
]
.
The bundle
{\mathcal{F}}_{HM}
is not globally generated, but has a space of sections for which the
cokernel of the evaluation map is a line bundle on a smooth curve
L
which is a union of
25
disjoint lines;
a general such section vanishes along an abelian surface
X_{0}
which is minimal for its even linkage class
{\mathcal{L}}
.
We show that
{\mathcal{F}}_{HM}
is a quotient of the rank
7
vector bundle
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
corresponding to

{\mathcal{L}}
via the correspondence
[
31
]
and use Theorem
1.3
to show that every integral surface in
{\mathcal{L}}
is smoothable (Example
4.9
).
The bundle
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{*}
corresponding to the odd Horrocks-Mumford linkage class
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}

has rank
17
: we construct a rank
2
quotient sheaf
{\mathcal{A}}
of
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}^{*}
which is CD2 reflexive with singular scheme precisely the curve

consisting of
25
lines and use Theorem

to show that every integral surface in

is smoothable (Example

).

Syzygy bundles provide another interesting example of even linkage classes. The kernel
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
of a surjection
\oplus_{i=1}^{4}{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}(-d_{i})\to{\mathcal{O}}_{\mathbb{P}^{3}}
determines an even linkage class of of curves on
\mathbb{P}^{3}
. Martin-Deschamps and Perrin completely worked out the smoothable classes in these cases
[
18
]
and these are typically not the same as the integral elements
[
26
]
, the smallest numerical case being Hartshorne’s example of an integral curve not smoothable in the Hilbert scheme
[
11
]
.
Similarly the kernel
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
of a surjection

\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
In
\lx@sectionsign 4
we show that all integral elements are smoothable in these classes when all the

are the same (Example

), but in general we can expect a situation as complicated as for curves on

Question 1.4
.

Let
{\mathcal{N}}_{0}
be the kernel of a surjection
\oplus_{i=1}^{5}{\mathcal{O}}(-d_{i})\to{\mathcal{O}}

\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
Which members of the corresponding even linkage class

This work is organized as follows.
In Section 2 we use Fitting schemes to classify sheaves whose local quotient by a vector

In Section 3 we consider reflexive sheaves

2.
Reflexive sheaves and stratification by rank

We use Fitting ideals to classify the coherent sheaves
{\mathcal{F}}
on a smooth variety
X
which are locally
the extension of a vector bundle and an ideal sheaf of a smooth codimension two subvariety;
the resulting sheaves are codimension 2 smoothable (abbreviated CD2).
When
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
, these are the curvilinear sheaves introduced by Hartshorne and Hirschowitz
[
12
]
and studied by Martin-Deschamps and Perrin
[
18
]
.
We show that if
{\mathcal{F}}
is a rank
r
reflexive CD2 on a smooth fourfold
X
and
\mathop{\rm Sing}{\mathcal{F}}
has integral curve components,
then sufficiently general maps
\phi:\mathcal{E}\to{\mathcal{F}}
with
{\mathcal{E}}
a rank
(r-1)
-bundle drop rank along a smooth surface.
We give a variant of Chang’s filtered Bertini theorem
[
4
]
for these sheaves.

A coherent sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on a smooth variety
X
has a local presentation

(2)

{\mathcal{O}}^{n}_{U}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}^{m}_{U}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{U}\to 0

on an open affine
U\subset X
.
The
i
th Fitting scheme
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}})
has ideal generated by the
(m-i+1)
-minors of the matrix for
u
.
Since this ideal is independent of the presentation
[
7
,
\lx@sectionsign 20.2
]
,
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}})
is well defined and
is set-theoretically the locus where
\mathop{\rm rank}u\leq m-i
, or equivalently
\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{F}}\geq i
.
Since
\rm{rank}\;{\mathcal{F}}=r
, then
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}})=X
for
i\leq r
and we define

\mathop{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})=S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
, the singular scheme of
{\mathcal{F}}
, the closed subscheme where
{\mathcal{F}}
is not a vector bundle.
A closed subscheme
Z\subset X
is codimension 2 smoothable (CD2 for short) if
Z
has local
embedding dimension at most
\dim X-2
, or equivalently
Z
locally lies on a smooth subvariety of codimension
2
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a coherent sheaf on a smooth variety
X
and let
\mathcal{P}
be locally free of rank
k
.

(a)

If
\mathcal{P}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\to 0
is exact, then

S_{k+i}({\mathcal{F}})\subset S_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
.

(b)

If
0\to{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to\mathcal{P}\to 0
is exact, then

S_{k+i}({\mathcal{F}})=S_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
.

(c)

If
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}})
is CD2, then
S_{i+1}({\mathcal{F}})
is empty.

Proof.

(a) Suppose
{\mathcal{F}}
has local presentation (
2
). Then
S_{k+i}({\mathcal{F}})
is empty for
k+i>m
because
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally generated by
m
elements,
so we may assume
k+i\leq m
. Working on an open affine
U
where

\mathcal{P}_{U}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{k}
, we obtain a presentation
{\mathcal{O}}^{n}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}^{k}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u^{\prime}}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}^{m}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}

where
u^{\prime}=[u,a]
and
a
is a
k\times m
matrix. Since
m-i+1>k
, each
(
m-i+1
)-minor of
u^{\prime}
expands in terms of (
m-k-i+1
)-minors from
u
, which shows that

S_{k+i}({\mathcal{F}})\subset S_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
scheme-theoretically.

(b) Locally
{\mathcal{F}}_{U}\cong{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}_{U}\oplus{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{k}
, so a local presentation
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u^{\prime}}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{m}\to{\mathcal{F}}^{\prime}\to 0

yields
{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}_{U}^{m+k}\to{\mathcal{F}}\to 0
with
u=\left[\begin{array}[]{c}u^{\prime}\\
0\end{array}\right]
and the
minors generating the ideal of
S_{k+i}({\mathcal{F}})
are equal to those generating the ideal of
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}}^{\prime})
.

(c) To compute the Fitting ideals of
{\mathcal{F}}
at
p\in X
, we may assume the local presentation

u_{p}:{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{n}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{m}
is replaced by a minimal presentation, so that each entry of
u
is in
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
.
Thus if
p\in S_{i+1}({\mathcal{F}})
, then all
m-i
minors of
u_{p}
belong to
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
, hence all
m-i+1
minors belong to
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}^{2}
,
which implies that
S_{i}({\mathcal{F}})
cannot be CD2 at
p
. See also
[
18
, II, Cor. 1.7]
.

∎

Definition 2.2
.

A rank
r
sheaf
{\mathcal{F}}
on
X
is codimension 2 smoothable (CD2 for short) if
{\mathcal{F}}
is torsion
free and
\mathop{\rm Sing}({\mathcal{F}})=S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
is a CD2 scheme.

This extends the notion of curvilinear sheaves on
X=\mathbb{P}^{3}
introduced by Hartshorne and Hirschowitz
[
12
]
.
We extend
[
18
, II, Proposition 3.6]
to higher dimension as follows.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}
be a rank
r
sheaf on a smooth variety
X
with
\dim X\geq 2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

{\mathcal{F}}
is a CD2 sheaf.

(2)

For each
p\in X
, the stalk
{\mathcal{F}}_{p}
satisfies one of the following:

(a)

{\mathcal{F}}_{p}\cong{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{r}
.

(b)

{\mathcal{F}}_{p}
is the cokernel of a map
{\mathcal{O}}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,y,f_{3},\dots,f_{r+1})}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{r+1}
with

x,y\in{\mathcal{O}}_{p}
part of a regular system of parameters and
f_{i}\in\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
for each
i
.

(3)

For each
p\in X
, there is an exact sequence
0\to{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{r-1}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{x}\to{\mathcal{I}}_{S,p}\to 0
with
S
a smooth germ at
p
of codimension
2
.

Proof.

(1)\Rightarrow(2):
If
p\not\in S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
, then
{\mathcal{F}}
is locally free at
p
giving (a), so we may assume
p\in S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
.
Then
S_{r+2}({\mathcal{F}})=\emptyset
by Proposition
2.1
(c), so
{\mathcal{F}}
has rank
r+1
at
p
and a local presentation

{\mathcal{O}}^{m}_{p}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u}}{{\to}}{\mathcal{O}}^{r+1}_{p}\to{\mathcal{F}}_{p}\to 0,

with entries of the matrix
u
generating the ideal for
S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
and the
2\times 2
minors of
u
vanishing on a neighborhood of
p
,
hence equal to
0
in
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
.
We may assume that
u_{1,1}=x\in\mathop{\rm m}_{p}-\mathop{\rm m}_{p}^{2}
.
Suppose that
x
does not divide
u_{1,j}
for some
j>1
. Then
x
divides
u_{k,1}
for all
k
due to the vanishing
2\times 2
minors,
hence the first column of
u
has the form
xb
with
b=(1,b_{2},\dots,b_{r+1})^{t}
.
Since
xb
maps to zero in
{\mathcal{F}}_{p}
which is torsion free, the image of
b
in
{\mathcal{F}}_{p}
is zero, hence is in the image of
u
, but
this is impossible since all entries of
u
lie in
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
. Therefore
x
divides
u_{1,j}
for each
j
, so we can write

u_{1,j}=xw_{j}
with
w_{1}=1
and
v_{j}=u_{j,1}
.
The vanishing of
2\times 2
minors yields
u_{i,j}=v_{i}w_{j}
for
i,j>1
, so each column of
u
is a multiple of the first
column and the image of
u
is the span of the first column, thus we may assume
m=1
. Since the entries of
u
generate the ideal
of
S_{r+1}({\mathcal{F}})
, we may assume
u_{2,1}=y
where
x,y
are part of a regular system of parameters for
\mathop{\rm m}_{p}
, giving possibility (b).

(2)\Rightarrow(3):
Clear in case (a) by taking
p\not\in S
. In case (b), let
\pi:{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{r+1}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{2}
be the projection onto the first two factors. Then
(x,y)
defines a smooth
codimension two subvariety
S
locally at
p
and we apply the snake lemma to

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&{\mathcal{O}}_{p}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,y,f_{3},\dots,f_{r+1})}}{{\to}}&{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{r+1}&\to&{\mathcal{F}}_{p}&\to&0\\
&&\downarrow&&\downarrow\pi&&\downarrow&&\\
0&\to&{\mathcal{O}}_{p}&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(x,y)}}{{\to}}&{\mathcal{O}}_{p}^{2}&\to&{\mathcal{I}}_{S,p}&\to&0.\end{array}

(3)\Rightarrow(1):
Follows from Proposition
2.1
(a).
∎

.

Let

(a)

.

The next result helps to identify reflexive quotients of reflexive sheaves.

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose

is exact with

locally free and
{\mathcal{E}}
reflexive.
Then
{\mathcal{F}}
is reflexive if and only if
\mathop{\rm codim}\mathop{\rm Sing}{\mathcal{F}}\geq 3
.

Proof.

\Rightarrow:
If
{\mathcal{F}}
is reflexive, then
\mathop{\rm codim}\mathop{\rm Sing}{\mathcal{F}}\geq 3

For

Remark 2.9

We note two consequences of our hypotheses on the filtrations.

Define

{\mathcal{F}}

Lemma 3.2

With Hypothesis

\begin{array}[]{ccccccccc}0&\to&W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}&\to&{\mathcal{G}}&\to&\overline{{\mathcal{G}}}&\to&0\\

Lemma 3.3
.

With hypothesis
3.1
, if
\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{G}}=2
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a generic line bundle on a smooth curve,
then a general section
s\in V
vanishes along a smooth surface.

Proof.

First assume
{\mathcal{Q}}={\mathcal{L}}_{C}
is a line bundle on a smooth curve
C
.
Let
U=X-\mathop{\rm Sing}{\mathcal{G}}
and define
Z\subset V\times U
by

Z=\{(s,p)|s(p)=0\}
, where
s(p):V\to{\mathcal{G}}(p)
is the map induced by
s\in V
.
Since
V\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{p}\to{\mathcal{G}}_{p}
is surjective for

, the fiber
Z_{p}
of
Z
over

p
is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{A}}^{N-2}
, the affine space given by the kernel.
Therefore
Z\to X
is a smooth
{\mathbb{A}}^{N-2}
-bundle of dimension
N+2
away
from
\pi_{2}^{-1}(C)
.

Now consider
p\in C
, so that
Z_{p}\subset V
is a subspace of dimension
N-1
.
Following Horrocks and Mumford
[
13

, there is an open affine neighborhood

of
p
on which

{\mathcal{G}}
trivializes as
Re_{1}\oplus Re_{2}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}=\mathcal{L}_{C}
as
R/J\ e
, where

J=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3})
is the ideal of
C
in
R
.
After possibly changing free basis for
{\mathcal{G}}
, we can arrange that the map
{\mathcal{G}}\to{\mathcal{Q}}
is given by

e_{2}\mapsto e
and
e_{1}\mapsto 0
so that the kernel
K
is

generated by

e_{1},y_{1}e_{2},y_{2}e_{2}
and
y_{3}e_{2}
.
We can find a basis

of
V
such that

is
given by

and

,

and

Here

over

are

\displaystyle F_{1}=x_{1}+\sum_{i=2}^{N}x_{i}f_{i}=0

\displaystyle F_{2}=x_{2}y_{1}+x_{3}y_{2}+x_{4}y_{3}+\sum_{i=5}^{N}x_{i}g_{i}=0.

The maximal ideal
\mathop{\rm m}
of the point

is

\mathop{\rm m}=(y_{1},y_{2},y_{3},y_{4},x_{1},x_{2}-1,x_{3},\dots,x_{N})

with system of parameters shown. Clearly

are linearly independent in

, hence

is a smooth point of

\dim\mathop{\rm Sing}Z\cap Z_{p}\leq N-2
. Therefore
\dim\mathop{\rm Sing}Z\leq N-1
and

is proper.
By generic smoothness, the general fibre of

is smooth, which gives the smooth zero locus of general section in
V
along
U
.
Applying Theorem
2.7
(b) to the restriction of
{\mathcal{G}}
to
X-C
shows that the cokernel of

W\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{X}\to{\mathcal{G}}
has the same property away from
C
.
Intersecting these Zariski open conditions in

gives the conclusion on all of
X
.

Now suppose that
{\mathcal{Q}}
is a line bundle on a smooth curve except for finitely many points
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{n}\}
.
Since
{\mathcal{Q}}_{p_{i}}
is generated by one element, a general section
s
doesn’t vanish at the

and we can apply the argument above
on the open set

.
∎

3.1.
The canonical filtration and filtered Bertini theorem

Take
{\mathcal{G}}
a rank
r
reflexive CD2 sheaf on
X=\mathbb{P}^{4}
as in Hypothesis
3.1
and
{\mathcal{Q}}
a generic line bundle on a smooth curve. Further assume
H^{0}({\mathcal{G}}(-1))=0
and
consider maps
\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}
where
{\mathcal{E}}
is a direct sum of line bundles and
{\mathcal{F}}
is a direct sum of
{\mathcal{G}}
with lines bundles.
We order the summands so that

{\mathcal{E}}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{k}{\mathcal{O}}(-a_{i})\text{ and }{\mathcal{F}}=\bigoplus_{j=1,j\neq M}^{N}{\mathcal{O}}(-b_{j})\oplus{\mathcal{G}},

where the
a_{i},b_{j}
are non-decreasing and
b_{j}<0
for
1\leq j<M
,
b_{j}\geq 0
for
M<j\leq N
.
Set
b_{M}=0
so that the
b_{i}
are non-decreasing and order the summands of
{\mathcal{F}}
by

{\mathcal{K}}_{1}={\mathcal{O}}(-b_{1}),{\mathcal{K}}_{2}={\mathcal{O}}(-b_{2}),\dots{\mathcal{K}}_{M}={\mathcal{G}},{\mathcal{K}}_{M+1}={\mathcal{O}}(-b_{M+1}),\dots{\mathcal{K}}_{N}={\mathcal{O}}(-b_{N}).

As in
[
4
, Example 2.1]
, we define a canonical filtration:

Definition 3.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}=\oplus_{b_{j}\leq a_{1}}{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=\oplus_{j=1}^{m_{1}}{\mathcal{K}}_{j}
and set

r_{1}=\min\{r:b_{m_{1}+1}\leq a_{r+1}\}
and let
{\mathcal{E}}_{1}=\oplus_{i=1}^{r_{1}}{\mathcal{O}}(-a_{i})
.
In a similar vein, we next set
{\mathcal{F}}_{2}=\oplus_{b_{j}\leq a_{r_{1}+1}}{\mathcal{K}}_{j}=\oplus_{j=1}^{m_{2}}{\mathcal{K}}_{j}
,

r_{2}=\min\{r:b_{m_{2}+1}\leq a_{r+1}\}

This gives filtrations

and

0={\mathcal{F}}_{0}\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{1}\subset\dots\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{n}={\mathcal{F}}
.
Set
\alpha_{i}=\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{F}}_{i}-\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{E}}_{i}
for
0<i<n
and
\alpha=\mathop{\rm rank}{\mathcal{F}}-\mathop{\rm rank}E
.

The subsheaf
{\mathcal{B}}=\{\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}:\phi({\mathcal{E}}_{i})\subset{\mathcal{F}}_{i},1\leq i\leq n\}\subset\mathop{\mathcal{H}om}({\mathcal{E}},{\mathcal{F}})

has a direct sum decomposition

as in Remark
2.9
, but if

{\mathcal{G}}
is a summand of
{\mathcal{F}}_{i}
, then
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
may fail to be globally generated.

Theorem 3.6
.

In the setting above, if
\alpha_{i}\geq 2
for
0<i<n
and
\alpha=1
, then the general map

\phi:{\mathcal{E}}\to{\mathcal{F}}
is injective and
\mathop{\rm Coker}\phi

Proof.

We induct on
n
. If
n=1
,
then Corollary
3.4
(b) applies to
{\mathcal{E}}={\mathcal{E}}_{1}
and
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}_{1}

n>1
.
Letting

{\mathcal{G}}

gives an

4.
Applications to linkage theory

We apply our results to smoothing members of even linkage classes of codimension two subschemes in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and
\mathbb{P}^{4}
.
To make the connection to linkage theory transparent, we restrict to Hypothesis
4.1

where the condition for smoothing in Theorem
1.1
and the necessary condition for integrality in
[
25
]
coincide.
Theorem
4.5
yields even linkage classes of curves in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
and surfaces in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
in which every integral subscheme is smoothable within its even linkage class.
In particular, this phenomenon holds for the even linkage classes assoicated to the
Horrocks-Mumford surface in
\mathbb{P}^{4}
(Examples
4.9
and
4.10
).

Recall linkage theory
[
19
,
30
]
.
Codimension two subschemes

Proof.

Partial section II: classification for general flows
Théo Marty

Abstract
This is the second article in a series that aims at classifying partial sections of flows, that is a general family of transverse surfaces. In this part, we classify partial cross-sections for all continuous flows, in the spirit of Schwartzman-Fried-Sullivan theory. We give a dynamical criterion for the existence of partial cross-sections, which is a direct consequence of part I of the series. Then we describe all partial cross-sections using a cohomological criterion, resembling Fried’s criterion. We also characterize the cardinality of the set of partial cross-sections in a given cohomology class.

Let

\varphi
be a continuous flow on

is a compact hypersurface

with
\partial S\subset\partial M
which is transverse to the flow. Every partial cross-section admits a cohomology class
\alpha

H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})

represents a global cross-section if and only if it satisfies the homological criterion

The case of partial cross-sections is more delicate.
We are interested in the two following question.

Question 1
.

How can we characterize the set of partial cross-sections cohomologous to
\alpha
, up to isotopy along the flow?

In the present paper, we completely answer the question. Note that the case
\alpha=0
need a specific treatment. Our results often distinguish when
\alpha
is zero or not. For instance, in the case
\alpha=0
, the existence of partial cross-sections is a direct application of Conley’s theory.
When
\alpha

[
6
]
of the series.
Say that
\alpha
is

Theorem A
.

Let
M
be a compact connected manifold,
\varphi
be a continuous flow on
M
and
\alpha
be in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. There exists a partial cross-section cohomologous to
\alpha
if and only if either we have
\alpha\neq 0
and
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov, or if we have
\alpha=0
and
\varphi
is not chain-recurrent.

When the condition in the theorem is satisfied, we have
\alpha(D_{\varphi})\geq 0
, which is similar to Fried’s criterion. Recall from the first paper that in general,
\alpha(D_{\varphi})\geq 0
does not imply that
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov.

Let
\alpha
be as in the theorem. The partial cross-sections cohomologous to
\alpha
are in correspondence with a set of homology class relative to a recurrent-like set. Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
the
\alpha
-recurrent set
introduced in the first paper. For now, it can be though of as:
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
is the set of points that is contained in no partial cross-section cohomologous to
\alpha

A partial cross-section cohomologous to
\alpha
is disjoint from

, so it induces a cohomologous class in
H^{1}(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha},\mathbb{Z})
. Note that this cohomology module behaves quite poorly since
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
can be any compact subset of
M
.
We consider a slightly different cohomology module, which we denote by
H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{Z})
, called
the cohomology relative to the germ
of
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
. It is defined as a direct limit of cohomology modules, and as such, it has a natural topology.
Note that there is a natural map
H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{Z})\xrightarrow{\pi{g}}H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
.
We introduce similarly its continuous dual
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{R})

Theorem B
.

Under the conditions in Theorem
A
, there exists a subset
D_{\varphi,\alpha}
of
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{Z})
that satisfies:

•

any partial cross-section cohomologous to
\alpha
admits a cohomology class in
H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{Z})

•

a non-zero element

represents a partial cross-section if and only if
\pi{g}(\beta)=\alpha
and
\beta(D_{\varphi,\alpha})\geq 0

•

two partial cross-sections cohomologous in
H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}),\mathbb{Z})
are isotopic along the flow.

We will restate the theorem as Theorem
6.9

Denote by

In order to prove Theorem

Let us informally state our results.

Theorem C
(Concatenation of Theorems

Let

Descripiton of the rank 1 cohomology.

The cohomology set

Let us describe that class in general. It is well-known that

We call the

Let

be a compact, co-oriented hypersurface of

. Similarly to above,

induces a cohomology class

in

defined as follows. By assumption, there exists a continuous map

Lemma 1.2
.

Any partial cross-section is locally flat.

Proof.
Let

∎

Lemma 1.3
.

Any isotopy among partial cross-sections can be parametrized as an isotopy along the flow.

Note that two partial cross-sections can be isotopic through embedded sub-manifolds, with

For instance a usual isotopy can cross a fixed point of the flow, which an isotopy along the flow cannot.

The classification of global-cross-sections is given by a simple homological criterion.

3.3

Equivalence with Conley’s order

We give another bijection from
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
, to a more combinatorial set.

Denote by
\widehat{\varphi}^{\alpha}
flow on
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
obtained by lifting
\varphi
, and
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
the set of recurrence chains of
\widehat{\varphi}^{\alpha}
, equipped with the quotient topology given by the projection
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}\to\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
. It is equipped with a (partial) order
\rightsquigarrow
defined by:
R_{1}\rightsquigarrow R_{2}
holds if we have
x_{1}\rightsquigarrow x_{2}
for some/any
x_{1}
in
R_{1}
and
x_{2}
in
R_{2}
. Note that the order is invariant under the action of
\mathbb{Z}
over
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. The order
(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\rightsquigarrow)
is sometimes called Conley’s order.

Definition 3.7
.

We define
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
to be the set of
\alpha
-equivariant, non-constant, non-decreasing and continuous (equivalently locally constant) maps from
(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\rightsquigarrow)
to
(\mathbb{Z},\leq)
.

We also define
I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
to be the set of equivalence classes of functions in
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
up to an additive constant. That is
f\simeq g
if
f-g
is a constant.

Note that when
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
is empty, by convention,
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
is equal to the singleton with the unique function from
\emptyset
to
\mathbb{Z}
, and
I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
is a singleton too.

Take a map
f
in
[(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\hskip 1.00006pt{:}\hskip 1.00006pt({\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}},0)]_{\alpha}
. It lifts to an
\alpha
-equivariant map from
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
to
\mathbb{R}
, whose opposite is pre-Lyapunov. It then restricts to a continuous map
\widehat{f}^{*}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
. The map
\widehat{f}^{*}
is non-decreasing since
-f
is pre-Lyapunov. Thus,
\widehat{f}^{*}
belongs to
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
.
We denote by
[\widehat{f}^{*}]
the equivalence class of
\widehat{f}^{*}
inside
I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
f_{s}
, for
s
in
[0,1]
, be a homotopy of maps in
[(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\hskip 1.00006pt{:}\hskip 1.00006pt({\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}},0)]_{\alpha}
. We choose lifts
\widehat{f}_{s}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
of the maps

, continuously in

. Then by continuity, the restrictions
\widehat{f}^{*}_{s}\colon\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}\to\mathbb{Z}
are all equal.
Thus,
[\widehat{f}^{*}]
depends only on the homotopy class of
f
in
\pL(\alpha)
. Denote by
\pL(\alpha)\xrightarrow{[\widehat{*}]}I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
the corresponding map.

Theorem 3.8
.

Assume
M
compact and connected.
Let
\alpha
be in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
so that
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is not empty.
Then the map
\pL(\alpha)\xrightarrow{[\widehat{*}]}I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
is a bijection.

In this theorem, it is necessary to assume
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)\neq\emptyset
. The set
I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
does not contain enough information to determine whether
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is empty or not.

Proof.
Assume first that
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
is empty. Then
\alpha
is not zero, since the recurrent set on

is not empty. Thus,
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov from Theorem
3.1
. By Proposition
2.7
, we have
\alpha(D_{\varphi})>0
. It follows from Theorem

, that
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is a singleton, and so is

. Thus,

is bijective.

We now assume
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}\neq\emptyset
.
We first show the injectivity of the map

.
Take two functions

in
[(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\hskip 1.00006pt{:}\hskip 1.00006pt({\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}},0)]_{\alpha}
and assume that

holds. From the equality, we may choose two lifts

of

and

Define

to
\widehat{g}

The map

projects to a map

, which lies inside
[(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\hskip 1.00006pt{:}\hskip 1.00006pt({\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}},0)]_{\alpha}
. We have

and

and

The surjectivity is a direct consequence of
[
6
, Theorem 4.11]
.
∎

4

Cardinality of
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)

In this section, we characterize when
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is finite and when it is a singleton.

Theorem 4.1
.

For any
\alpha
in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})\setminus\{0\}
, the set
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is at most countable.

Proof.
Denote by
C\subset[0,1]
the standard Cantor (compact) set.
Let
K\subset\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
be a compact subset for which the projection
K\to M
is surjective. Then
K\cap\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
is compact, so there exists a continuous surjection
\widehat{f}\colon C\to K\cap\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}
. Given any map
g
in
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
, its value is completely determined by the images
g(R)
for the set of the recurrent chains
R
that intersects
K
. Therefore, the continuous map
g\circ f\colon C\to\mathbb{Z}
determines completely
g
. Thus, it is enough to prove that there are only countably many continuous maps from
C
to
\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
h\colon C\to\mathbb{Z}
be a continuous map. Note that it is locally constant, so we can find a partition of
C
by finitely many open sets
U_{1}\cdots U_{n}
, so that
h
is constant of each
U_{k}
. Up to splitting each
U_{k}
in finitely many disjoint open subsets, we may assume that
U_{k}
can be written as
U_{k}=C\cap I_{k}
, where
I_{k}
is an interval in
\mathbb{R}
whose ends are rational and outside
C
.

Note that there are only countably many intervals
I
with
\partial I\subset\mathbb{Q}
.
Thus,
h
is determined by the countable choices of a finite partition of
C
by sets of the form
C\cap I_{k}
, and by the countable possible images of
h
on
C\cap U_{k}
. Hence, there are no more than countably many continuous functions from
C
to
\mathbb{Z}
.
∎

Denote by
\widehat{G}_{\varphi,\alpha}
the oriented graph whose set of vertices is
\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*}
and with an edge from
R_{1}
to
R_{2}
if
R_{1}\rightsquigarrow R_{2}
holds. We also denote by
\rightsquigarrow
the edges inside
\widehat{G}_{\varphi,\alpha}
.
The graph
\widehat{G}_{\varphi,\alpha}
admits a natural
\mathbb{Z}
-action.

Definition 4.2
.

We denote by
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
the oriented graph obtained as the quotient of
\widehat{G}_{\varphi,\alpha}
by
\mathbb{Z}
. Its vertices are the
\alpha
-recurrence chains of
\varphi
.

The graph
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is
transitive
if there exist an oriented path between any two
\alpha
-recurrence chains. Notice that, any path
R_{1}\rightsquigarrow\cdots\rightsquigarrow R_{n}
can be concatenated in a single arrow
R_{1}\rightsquigarrow R_{n}
, so
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is transitive if and only if it is a complete oriented graph. By convenient, we take the convention that the empty graph is transitive.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\alpha
be a non-zero class in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
so that
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov. Then
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is finite if and only if the oriented graph
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is finite and transitive.

The set
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(0)
is either empty or (infinitely) countable.

We warn the reader of the following. The two properties of “
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
being transitive” and “
\varphi
being chain recurrent” seem to be very similar. However, none of them implies the other one. Let us describe two counter-examples on the torus.

Example 4.4
.

Take
M={\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}^{2}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}}}
and let
\varphi
be the following flow on
M
. It admits two periodic orbits
\delta_{1}=\{0\}\times{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
and
\delta_{2}=\{\tfrac{1}{2}\}\times{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
. Every other orbit converges toward
\delta_{0}
in the past and
\delta_{1}
in the future. Also all its orbits are transverse to the curve
\gamma={\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}\times\{0\}
. Then,
\gamma
is a global cross-section, cohomologous to
\alpha=[dy]
. Thus,
\varphi
admits a unique partial cross-section cohomologous to
[dy]
, and
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is finite and transitive. But
\varphi
is not recurrent, as its recurrent set is
\delta_{1}\cup\delta_{2}\neq M
.

Example 4.5
.

Take
M={\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}^{2}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}^{2}$}}}
and let
\varphi
be the flow on
M
obtained by taking the linear flow directed by

, and slowing it down so that it admits exactly two fixed points

and

. We let the reader check that
\varphi
is chain recurrent. We set

. The graph

has two vertices, that correspond to

and

, and its only edges are loops from a vertex to itself. Thus, it is not transitive, but
\varphi
is chain recurrent.

Proof of Theorem
4.3
, in the case
\alpha=0
.
When
\varphi
is chain recurrent, it follows from Theorem

that

is empty.

We now assume that
\varphi
is not chain recurrent. Then
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(0)
is in bijection with

in

. And for any distinct

Before proving the theorem in the case

\alpha

By definition,

.

R_{1},R_{2}
two
\alpha

and

. Let

and end on

{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}

[(M,\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})\hskip 1.00006pt{:}\hskip 1.00006pt({\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}},\{0\})]_{\alpha}

.

Let

be so that
\alpha=n_{\alpha}\beta
for some primitive class
\beta
in

.

We suppose that
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is finite and transitive, and prove that
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is finite. Denote by

the
\alpha
-recurrence chains on

, and a lift
\widetilde{R}_{i}
of

in
\widehat{M}_{\alpha}
. By transitivity of

, for every

, there exists

in
\mathbb{Z}
that satisfies
\widetilde{R}_{i}\rightsquigarrow a_{i,j}\cdot\widetilde{R}_{j}
.

Take an element in

, and its unique lift

in
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
which satisfies

.
From above, we have
g(R_{i})\geq g(R_{j})+n_{\alpha}a_{i,j}
for any

.
So

lies in
{\llbracket-n_{\alpha}a_{1,i},n_{\alpha}a_{i,1}\rrbracket}
. Thus,

is determined by a finite data. It follows that

is finite.

We now prove the converse implication by contraposition.
Fix an
\alpha
-equivariant map
\widehat{f}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
so that
-\widehat{f}
is Lyapunov (see Theorem
2.6
), which project down to a map
f\colon M\to{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
.
Assume first that
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is infinite. Then
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}^{*}\simeq f(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha})
is infinite and totally disconnected. So there are infinitely many complementary intervals of

in
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}}
.
It follows from Lemma

that
I_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
is infinite.

Assume now that the quotient graph
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
is not transitive. We may also choose it finite for convenience. Since the graph is not transitive, there exists a partition
\{A,B\}
of the set of vertices of
G_{\varphi,\alpha}
so that there is no arrow from

to

. Denote by
\widehat{A}
and
\widehat{B}
the preimages of

and

inside
\widehat{G}_{\varphi,\alpha}
.

Since
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is not empty (see Theorem

), there exists a map

in
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
. For all

in
\mathbb{N}
, we define the map

that coincide with

and with

. It is clear that

is non-creasing and continuous (by finiteness of

), so it belongs to
\widehat{I}_{\alpha}(\widehat{\mathcal{R}}_{\alpha}^{*},\mathbb{Z})
. Observes that for any distinct indexes

and

do not differ by a constant, so their images in

∎

Using the same technique, we can determine when there is a unique partial cross-section up to isotopy.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\alpha
be in
H^{1}(M,\mathbb{Z})
. The set
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is a singleton if and only if
\alpha
is non-zero,
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov and if there exists at most one
\alpha
-recurrence chain.

Proof.
Assume
\alpha\neq 0
and quasi-Lyapunov, otherwise it follows from above that
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is not a singleton.
Let

be a continuous map, cohomology to
\alpha
, and
\widehat{f}\colon\widehat{M}_{\alpha}\to\mathbb{R}
a lift of

, so that

is Lyapunov.

Assume that there exists at least two
\alpha
-recurrence chains. Then the space

has at least two complementary components. It follows from Lemma
4.6
that
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
has at least two elements. Thus, if
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha)
is a singleton,
\varphi
has at most one
\alpha
-recurrence chain.

Conversely, if there is at most one
\alpha
-recurrence chain, then

is either empty (when

is empty) or equal to a single

∎

5

Germs in homology

We will discuss a homology construction. It is certainly well-known by some expert, but we could not find it in the bibliography. And it is not a bad idea to reintroduce it here anyway. In this section, we develop a general theory independent on the flow. We come back to the subject of partial cross-sections in Section
6
.

Given a compact subset
K
in
M
, we build a homology module relative to the germ of neighborhoods of
K
, as a projective limit of some singular homology modules. It can be though of the singular homology relative to a compact subset
K
, modulo arbitrarily small neighborhoods of
K
.

5.1

Homology relative to a germ

Let us fix some notations. We take
\mathcal{A}
to be one of the ring
\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{R}
. We consider the singular homology and cohomology with coefficients in
\mathcal{A}
.
Given two sets

, we sometimes write
H_{1}(X)
and
H_{1}(X,Y)
the modules
H_{1}(X,\mathcal{A})
and
H_{1}(X,Y,\mathcal{A})
, when the chosen ring is clear from the context.
Take four sets
A,B,X,Y
that satisfy
Y\subset X
,
A\subset B
,
A\subset X
and
B\subset Y
. The inclusion
(A,B)\hookrightarrow(X,Y)
induces a morphism
H_{1}(A,B)\to H_{1}(X,Y)
. Given an element
\delta
in
H_{1}(A,B)
, we will denote by
\delta\,\symbolehmod(\hskip-1.00374pt{X,Y}\hskip-1.00374pt)
its image in
H_{1}(X,Y)
.
Or simply write
\delta\,\underline{\symbolehmod}(\hskip-1.00374pt{X}\hskip-1.00374pt)
when

holds, and
\delta\,\mathop{\symbolehmod}\limits^{\mkern-10.0mu\smash{\raisebox{-3.5pt}{\rule{11.95836pt}{0.4pt}}}}(\hskip-1.00374pt{Y}\hskip-1.00374pt)
when
A=X
holds.
We use a similar notation for the map
H^{1}(X,Y)\to H^{1}(A,B)
.

We will discuss a projective limit of some modules. For more on the matter, we refer to
[
10
,
Tag 0031
]
for directed inverse systems, and to
[
10
,
Tag 002U
]
for projective limits.

Fix a compact subset
K
of
M
, potentially empty.
The set of neighborhoods of
K
is a
directed inverse system
for the partial order
\supset
. That is, for any two neighborhood
U,V
, there exists a neighborhood
W
, for instance
W=U\cap V
, that satisfies
U\supset W
and
V\supset W
. It is a necessary condition to define a projective limit.

Consider the following diagram of
\mathcal{A}
-modules. The objects are the modules
H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A})
where
U
varies over the neighborhoods of
K
. The diagram contains the maps
H_{1}(M,V,\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A})
, where
U\supset V
are any two neighborhoods of
K
. We use the notation
\dcal{g}(K)
for the germ of neighborhoods of
K
.

Definition 5.1
.

We denote by:

H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathcal{A})=\varprojlim_{U\supset K}H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A})

the projective limit, equipped with the maps

H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathcal{A})\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A})

for any neighborhood
U
of
K
.

By definition, the projective limit is equal to:

\varprojlim_{U\supset K}H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A})=\left\{\delta\in\prod_{U\supset K}H_{1}(M,U,\mathcal{A}),\,\forall U\subset V,\,\delta_{U}\,\underline{\symbolehmod}(\hskip-1.00374pt{V}\hskip-1.00374pt)=\delta_{V}\right\}

Given a subset
X
of
M
, we denote by
V_{\epsilon}(X)
the set of points at distance less than
\epsilon
of
X
.
Since
K
is compact, any neighborhood of
K
some
V_{\epsilon}(K)
with a small
\epsilon>0
. So for convenience, one may replace
U
by
V_{\epsilon}(K)
in the projective limit.

The projective limit satisfies a universal property. So the inclusion maps
H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,U)
induce a natural map
H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))
. We will discuss more about this map further down.

An open set

is said to be
K
-connected
if any path-connected component of

intersects
K
, or alternatively
H_{0}(U,K)=\{0\}
holds.
Note that

is
K
-connected.

Lemma 5.2
.

The map
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}H_{1}(M,U)
is surjective for any
K
-connected neighborhood
U
of
K
.

Proof.
The long exact sequence in homology contains the maps

H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,U)\to H_{0}(U,K)=0.

So the left map is surjective. Since it can be factorized as

H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))\to H_{1}(M,U),

the second map is surjective.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The map
H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))
is neither injective nor surjective in general, as illustrated in Examples
5.4
and
5.5
. We will characterize its image in Proposition
5.11
.

Let us denote temporally by
\pi{g}\colon H_{1}(M,K)\to H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))
this map.

Example 5.4
.

Consider
M=[0,1]
and
K=\{0\}\cup\{\frac{1}{n+1},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. The formal infinite 1-chain:

\delta_{1}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\left[0,{\scalebox{1.3}{$\frac{1}{n+1}$}}\right]

induces an element in
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))
, since only finitely many terms appear modulo any given neighborhood of
K
. Clearly,
\delta_{1}
is not in the image of
\pi{g}
. So the map
\pi{g}
is not surjective.

Example 5.5
.

Take
M=({\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{R}$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\mathbb{Z}$}}})\times\mathbb{R}
and the compact subset

K=\left\{\!\middle(t,\,\sin\!\middle({\scalebox{1.3}{$\frac{1}{t}$}}\middle)\!\middle)\!,\,t\in]0,1]\middle\}\cup\middle(\{0\}\times[-1,1]\right)

of

. Consider the curve
\delta_{2}
that goes from

. It is not null homologous in

. But
\delta_{2}
is null-homologous in

, since for any neighborhood
U
of

,

is homotopic (relatively to

) to a curve that remains inside

. So the map
\pi{g}
is not injective.

Replacing
K
with
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
, the second example corresponds to a curve that has ‘zero contribution to the dynamic’. It is actually to remove these curves that we consider the module
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))
in the first place.

Remark 5.6
.

One can define similarly the homology modules
H_{n}(\dcal{g}(K),\mathcal{A})
and
H_{n}(\dcal{g}(K),K,\mathcal{A})
. One difficulty is that the long exact sequence in homology are more complicated when using the germs. In particular the expected two sequences

are not exact in general. The reason is that the directed inverse systems given by the modules

and

are not Mittag-Leffler. This goes beyond the scope of this paper, so we refer to
[
10
,
Tag 0595
]
for the Mittag-Leffler criterion, and
[
10
,
Tag 0H31
]
for an exact sequences that one can hope to obtain.

5.2

Torsion and extension of coefficients

We consider
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}
and
\mathbb{Z}
for different reasons. It is easier to work with
\mathbb{R}
since it is a field with a good topology. But the natural ring to express the classification of partial cross-sections is
\mathbb{Z}
. Let us explain how they relate to each other.

Given a
\mathbb{Z}
-module
H
, denote by
\tor(H)
the torsion part of
H
, and
{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}
the quotient of
H
by
\tor(H)
.

Lemma 5.7
.

Let
U
be a neighborhood of
K
. The two maps

\tor(H_{1}(M,U,\mathbb{Z}))\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}\tor(H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathbb{Z}))\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}\tor(H_{1}(M,U,\mathbb{Z}))

are surjective. When
U
is a small enough neighborhood of
K
, the right map is an isomorphism.

Proof.
Here, all homology modules are with coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
. The long exact sequence in homology for
(U,M)
contains the maps:

H_{1}(M)\xrightarrow{\underline{\symbolehmod}}H_{1}(M,U)\xrightarrow{\partial}H_{0}(U).

Since
H_{0}(U)
is a free Abelian group, the torsion part
\tor(H_{1}(M,U))
lies inside
\ker(\partial)=\im(\underline{\symbolehmod})
.
So the map
\tor(H_{1}(M))\to\tor(H_{1}(M,U))
is surjective. Note that the cardinality of the image is non-increasing in
U
, and bounded by the cardinality of
\tor(H_{1}(M))
, which is finite. So the cardinality of the image is constant when
U
is small enough.
So given two neighborhoods
V\subset U
of
K
, assumed small enough, the map
\tor(H_{1}(M,V))\to\tor(H_{1}(M,U))
is an isomorphism.
It follows that
\tor(H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathbb{Z}))\to\tor(H_{1}(M,U,\mathbb{Z}))
is also an isomorphism.
∎

Corollary 5.8
.

The quotient
{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}
is naturally isomorphic to the projective limit:

{\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}\simeq\varprojlim_{U\supset K}\left({\raisebox{1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,U,\mathbb{Z})$}}/\raisebox{-1.02228pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}\right).

Proof.
From above, we consider the directed inverse system of exact sequences:

1\mapsto\tor(H_{1}(M,U))\to H_{1}(M,U)\to{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,U)$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}\to 1,

where
U
varies over the neighborhoods of
K
. By Lemma
5.7
, the first term is constant when
U
is small enough. Consider the projective limit of these sequences. Note that the projective limit functor is left exact, but not exact in general. But since the left term is eventually constant, it follows from

[
10
,
Tag 0H31
]

and

[
10
,
Tag 07KW, item 3.
]

that the short sequence

1\mapsto\tor(H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K)))\to H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))\to\varprojlim_{U\supset K}{\raisebox{1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$H_{1}(M,U)$}}/\raisebox{-1.00006pt}{\scalebox{0.85}{$\tor$}}}\to 1

is exact. The conclusion follows.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

We later introduce the cohomology module
H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathbb{Z})
, which is the continuous dual of

. As such, it does not see the torsion part of

Lemma 5.10

The coefficient extension maps

satisfies a universal property in the set of

5.3

Simplicial norm

In this section, we determine the image of

in

Given a subset

.
Given two subsets

of

, notice that the simplicial norm satisfy

\ell^{0}=\ell^{0}_{\dcal{g}(K)}\colon H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K))\to[0,+\infty]

\ell^{0}_{\dcal{g}(K)}(\delta)=\sup_{U\supset K}\ell^{0}_{U}(\delta\,\underline{\symbolehmod}(\hskip-1.00374pt{U}\hskip-1.00374pt))

.

. Note that the equality is strict for the element
\delta_{2}
in Example

, Since
\delta_{2}\,\underline{\symbolehmod}(\hskip-1.00374pt{\dcal{g}(K)}\hskip-1.00374pt)=0

Proposition 5.11

Recall
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{Z}
.
The image of

modulo
\dcal{g}(K)
is the set

of elements that have finite simplicial norms.
Additionally, for any class
\delta
in
H_{1}(M,\dcal{g}(K),\mathcal{A})
with
\ell^{0}_{\dcal{g}(K)}(\delta)<+\infty
, there exists
\gamma
in

that satisfies

and
\gamma\,\underline{\symbolehmod}(\hskip-1.00374pt{\dcal{g}(K)}\hskip-1.00374pt)=\delta

The conclusion is trivial when

is empty, as

is an isomorphism in that case. So let us assume

The proof is technical, so let use summarize it first. We take a decreasing sequence

of open neighborhoods of

in

that has a finite simplicial norm, and search a preimage in

. One may want to consider

. Then one may want to define the element

One difficulty is that the limit object can be a combination of curves that have infinite lengths.
A second difficulty is that we need to control the coefficients in

converges. Then we kind of apply the idea above.

Lemma 5.12

Let

We choose a decreasing sequence

of neighborhoods of

Denote by

5.4

The projective limit topology

We equip

with a topology, which is non-trivial even for

.

Lemma 5.13
.

Recall
\mathcal{A}=\mathbb{Z}
or
\mathbb{R}
. For any
K
-connected neighborhood

of

, the modules
H_{0}(U,\mathcal{A})
and

Proof.
Since

is compact, the set of path-connected components of

is finite. So

is finitely generated.
The long exact sequence in homology, for

The left and right terms are finitely generated, and

As a consequence, when

Definition 5.14

Given a

-connected neighborhood

of

in

is open.
For

, we can even take

to be a singleton.
The collection of these sets form a basis of the projective limit topology.
It follows immediately:

Lemma 5.15
.

The set

is dense in

Take
\delta
in

of

of

of

We give below some elementary properties of the projective limit topology.
For any

Going back to Example

Lemma 5.20
.

5.5

We discuss a cohomological construction similar to the homological construction given above.
Given two neighborhoods

of

We define the set:

as a directed limit, where

If follows a commutative diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{1})\times\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}}),\mathbb{Z})\times H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{2}}),\mathbb{Z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H^{1}(M,\dcal{g}(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}),\mathbb{Z}),}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrel{\rotatebox[origin]{-90.0}{$\scriptstyle\simeq$}}}

where the top arrow is the Fried desingularization and the bottom arrow is the sum of the natural restriction maps.
The bottom arrow sends the pairs
(\beta_{1},\beta_{2})
with
\beta_{i}(D_{\varphi,\alpha_{i}})\geq 0
onto the pairs
\beta
with
\beta(D_{\varphi,\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}})\geq 0
, as required by Theorem
6.9
.
Note that the top and bottom maps are not necessarily injective nor surjective in general.

For the non-injectivity, take
\alpha_{1}=0
, assuming the flow non-chain recurrent, and
\alpha_{2}(D_{\varphi})>0
. The top map goes from an infinite set to a singleton, so it is not injective.
For the non-surjectivity, Example 5.19 in
[
6
]
gives an example where the inclusion
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}\subset\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{2}}
is strict. So any point in
(\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}}\cap\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{2}})\setminus\mathcal{R}_{\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}
lies on a partial cross-section cohomologous to
\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}
, but it lies on no partial cross-section cohomologous to either
\alpha_{1}
or
\alpha_{2}
(see Theorem
3.3
).

Question 2
.

When is the map
\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{1})\times\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{2})\to\mathcal{PS}_{\varphi}(\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2})
injective? and surjective?

Note that for this question, it would be fair to consider the empty set as a null-homologous partial cross-section, otherwise the surjectivity in the case
\alpha_{1}=0
will fail quite frequently.

Appendix C
Practical application to simple
\alpha
-recurrence sets

We give two homological results as practical tools. In the homological classification of partial cross-sections, we utilize the homology relative to the germ of the
\alpha
-recurrent set
K=\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
, where
-\alpha
is quasi-Lyapunov. In many application cases, the compact set
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha}
has finitely many connected components. Under that hypothesis, we prove that the homology module is finitely generated.

A neighborhood
U
of
K
is said to be
exactly
K
-connected
if any path-connected component of
U
contains exactly one connected component of
K
.

Lemma C.1
.

Assume that
K
has finitely many connected components. Take two neighborhoods
U
and

of
K
, assumed
K
-thin and exactly
K
-connected. Then we have

.

When
K
has finitely many connected components, and
\epsilon>0
is small enough,
V_{\epsilon}(K)
is
K
-thin and exactly
K
-connected. So one can apply the lemma above to
V_{\epsilon}(K)
.

\mathbb{R}
.

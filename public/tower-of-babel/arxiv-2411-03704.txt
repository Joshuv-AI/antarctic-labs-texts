3.2
The localization sequence

If
{\mathcal{X}}\rightarrow S
is a family of varieties over a base
S
with
X_{\eta}
the generic fibre and
X_{s}
the fibre over a closed subvariety
s
then one has a localization sequence relating the three.

\cdots\rightarrow H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X_{\eta},{\mathds{Q}}(2)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\partial}}{{\longrightarrow}}\bigoplus_{s\in S^{1}}H^{2}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X_{s},{\mathds{Q}}(1))\longrightarrow H^{4}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\mathcal{X}},{\mathds{Q}}(2))\rightarrow\cdots

where
S^{1}
denotes the set of irreducible closed subvarieties of codimension
1
. The boundary map of an element

Z=\sum_{i}(C_{\eta,i},f_{\eta,i})
is given as follows. Let
{\mathcal{C}}
denote the closure of
C_{\eta}
in
{\mathcal{X}}
. Then

\partial(Z)=\sum_{i}\operatorname{div}_{{\mathcal{C}}_{i}}(f_{\eta,i})

The cocycle condition
\sum_{i}\operatorname{div}_{C_{\eta,i}}(f_{\eta,i})=0
implies that the ‘horizontal’ divisors cancel out and only the components in some of the fibres survive.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be a family of
K3
surfaces. To check for indecomposability one can use the boundary map
\partial
. Over the algebraic closure of the base field, a decomposable cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}(X_{\eta},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
is given by
(C_{\eta},a_{\eta})
where
C_{\eta}
is a curve on
X_{\eta}
and
a_{\eta}
a constant (that is, a function on the base
S
).

The boundary map is particularly simple to compute in the case of a decomposable element

\partial((C_{\eta},a_{\eta}))=\sum\operatorname{ord}_{s}(a_{\eta})C_{s}

where
C_{s}
is the restriction of the closure
{\mathcal{C}}
of
C_{\eta}
to the fibre over
s
. Hence in the boundary of decomposable elements one can obtain only those cycles in the special fibres which are the restrictions of cycles in the generic fibre. In particular, if
Z_{\eta}
is a motivic cycle and the boundary contains cycles which are
not
the restriction of cycles in the generic fibre then the motivic cycle is necessarily indecomposable. We will show that that is the case for our cycles.

An alternate way, as is done in Chen-Lewis
[
CL05
]
, Ma-Sato
[
MS23
]
or Sato
[
Sat23
]
, is to compute the Beilinson regulator and show that, when evaluated against a particular transcendental
(1,1)
form, this is non-zero. In the case when the base is the ring of integers of a glabal field or a local field, the boundary map can be thought of as a non-Archimedean regulator map and the idea is essentially the same. The transcendental form is represented by a new algebraic cycle in the special fibre. Evaluation against this form corresponds to intersecting with the new cycle. That being non-zero implies that the component of the boundary in the direction of the new cycle is non-zero. This also fits in to the philosophy espoused by Manin
[
Man91
]
that the Archimedean component of an arithmetic variety can be viewed as the ‘special fibre at
\infty
’.

coincide then
\phi_{C}^{-1}(D)
has two components
{\tilde{D}}_{i,1}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{i,2}
. Equivalently, if
Q_{i}
is the image of
D_{i}
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
which is not a point and
s_{Q_{i}}
and
t_{Q_{i}}
coincide then
\psi_{S}^{-1}(Q_{i})
has two components
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,1}
and
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,2}
.

Proof.

If the two points coincide then all the ramification points of the map
{\tilde{Q}}_{i}\rightarrow Q_{i}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{i}\rightarrow D_{i}
are nodal. Hence the induced map from the normalization of
{\tilde{Q}}_{i}
or
{\tilde{D}}_{i}
to
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
is an
unramified
double cover. However,
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
does not have an irreducible unramified double cover as one can see from the Riemann-Hurwitz formula or the fact that
{\mathds{P}}^{1}
is simply connected. Hence
{\tilde{Q}}_{i}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{i}
have two components
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,2}
and
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,2}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{i,1}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{i,2}
. The points of intersection of
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,1}
and
{\tilde{Q}}_{i,2}
are the nodes of
{\tilde{Q}}_{i}
.

∎

The components
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{2}
are new elements of the Neron-Severi group of
{\tilde{Z}}
. Hence if
s_{Q}=t_{Q}
the Picard number of
{\tilde{Z}}
increases by
1
. Note that the involution induced by the double cover interchanges
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{2}
.

The moduli of
K3
surfaces where the rank of the Neron-Severi is one more than the generic rank is usually called a
Heegner divisor
or a
Noether-Lefschetz
locus. In the special case of Kummer surfaces of Abelian surfaces these are also called
Humbert Surfaces
and correspond to when the Abelian surface has multiplication by a real quadratic field. Let
H_{Q}
denote the submoduli where there is a rational curve
Q
corresponding to a
(-1)
curve which meets the nodal sextic only at double points - that is, the points
s_{Q}
and
t_{Q}
coincide.

The construction we made of a cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\tilde{Z}},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
was for a fixed
K3
surface. However, if
M_{d}
is the moduli of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d
, the points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are the intersections of the pre-images of two
(-1)
curves, so are defined everywhere and determine a section of the universal family over the moduli space. In particular, we have an algebraic family of cycles
\Xi_{Q_{1,z},Q_{2,z},P_{z}}
in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\tilde{Z}}_{z},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
as long as the points
s_{Q_{i,z}}
and
t_{Q_{i,z}}
for
i\in\{1,2\}
are distinct. This defines a cycle
\Xi_{Q_{1,U},Q_{2,U},P_{U}}
defined in the fibre over the open set
U=M_{d}\backslash\{H_{Q_{1}}\cup H_{Q_{2}}\}
.

In the fibres over
H_{Q}
, where
s_{Q}
and
t_{Q}
coincide, the points
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are interchanged by the involution. Hence without loss of generality we may assume that
P_{1}
lies on
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
and
P_{2}
lies on
{\tilde{D}}_{2}
.

We then have the following theorem:

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
H_{Q_{1}}
be the submoduli of moduli space of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d
such that
s_{Q_{1}}
and
t_{Q_{1}}
coincide. Let
\Xi_{Q_{1,U},Q_{2,U},P_{U}}
be the cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\tilde{Z}}_{U},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
constructed in Theorem
4.2
, where
U=M_{d}\backslash\{H_{Q_{1}}\cup H_{Q_{2}}\}
. We assume that the section
P_{i}
intersects
{\tilde{D}}_{i}
. Then, under the boundary map of the localization sequence,

\partial(\Xi_{Q_{1,U},Q_{2,U},P_{U}})={\tilde{D}}_{1,1}-{\tilde{D}}_{1,2}

Proof.

Let

\Xi_{Q_{1,U},Q_{2,U},P_{U}}=({\tilde{D}}_{1,U},f_{{\tilde{D}}_{1},U}+({\tilde{D}}_{2},f_{{\tilde{D}}_{2},U})

be the cycle with

\operatorname{div}(f_{{\tilde{D}}_{1},U})=P_{U,1}-P_{U,2}

and

\operatorname{div}(f_{{\tilde{D}}_{2},U})=P_{U,2}-P_{U,1}

Recall that we have the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\tilde{Z}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Z}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X_{d}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathds{P}}^{2}\par}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\pi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi_{C}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}

Let
\iota
be the involution on
Z_{U}
induced by the double cover
\psi_{S}:Z\rightarrow{\mathds{P}}^{2}
and let
\tilde{\iota}
be the involution induced by the double cover
{\tilde{Z}}\rightarrow X_{d}
. The fixed points of
\iota|_{{\tilde{Q}}}
are the ramification points of the double cover to
Q
.
The involution
\iota
lifts to the involution
\tilde{\iota}
on
{\tilde{D}}
and

\tilde{\iota}(P_{U,1})=P_{U,2}

Since
{\tilde{D}}_{i,U}
is the pre-image of
D_{i,U}
,
\tilde{\iota}
stabilizes it:
\tilde{\iota}({\tilde{D}}_{i})={\tilde{D}}_{i}
.
Since
s_{Q_{i,U}}
and
t_{Q_{i,U}}
are on the branch locus we have
\tilde{\iota}(s_{Q_{i,U}})=s_{Q_{i,U}}
and
\tilde{\iota}(t_{Q_{i,U}})=t_{Q_{i,U}}
.

Let
f=f_{{\tilde{D}}_{1}}
and let
f^{\tilde{\iota}}=f\circ\tilde{\iota}
. Then

\operatorname{div}(f^{\tilde{\iota}})=P_{U,2}-P_{U,1}

Hence
f^{\tilde{\iota}}=\frac{c_{U}}{f}
, where
c_{U}
is a ‘constant’ on
U
. Since we have assumed
f(s_{{\tilde{D}}_{1,U}})=1
and
f^{\tilde{\iota}}(s_{Q_{U}})=f(\tilde{\iota}(s_{Q_{U}}))=f(s_{Q})=1
, we have
c_{U}\equiv 1
.

To compute the boundary of
\Xi_{Q_{1,U},Q_{2,U},P_{U}}
, we need to compute the divisor of
f_{U}
on the closure of
{\tilde{D}}_{1,U}
. Since
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
gains a node over
H_{Q}
we have to consider the divisor on the blow up of
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
at the node
R_{Q}
.

Over
H_{Q_{1}}-H_{Q_{2}}
, the closure has three components,
{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}
,
{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}
and the exceptional fibre
{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}
. The involution
\tilde{\iota}
interchanges them and as remarked above, we may assume that the closure of
P_{i,U}
lies on
{\tilde{D}}_{1,i,H_{Q}}
. Further, since
{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}
is the exceptional fibre over the node
R_{Q}={\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}\cap{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}
, the involution stabilizes
{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}:\tilde{\iota}({{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}})={{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}})
.

On the closure of
{\tilde{D}}_{1,U}
,

\operatorname{div}_{\bar{{\tilde{D}}}_{1,U}}(f_{U})={\mathcal{H}}+a{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}+b{\tilde{Y}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}+c{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}

for some
a,b
and
c
in
{\mathds{Z}}
, where
{\mathcal{H}}=\overline{\operatorname{div}_{Y_{U}}(f_{U})}
. is the closure of the horizontal divisor.

We recall that
\operatorname{div}(f^{\tilde{\iota}})=\tilde{\iota}(\operatorname{div}(f))
and
f^{\tilde{\iota}}=1/f
, so, on one hand

\operatorname{div}_{\bar{{\tilde{D}}}_{1,U}}(f^{\tilde{\iota}}_{U})=\tilde{\iota}({\mathcal{H}}+a{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}+b{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}+c{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}})=-{\mathcal{H}}+a{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}+b{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}+c{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}

on the other hand

\operatorname{div}_{\bar{{\tilde{D}}}_{1,U}}(f^{\tilde{\iota}}_{U})=-\operatorname{div}_{\bar{{\tilde{D}}}_{1,U}}(f_{U})=-{\mathcal{H}}-a{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q}}-b{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q}}-c{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}

Comparing coefficients we have
a=-b
and

. Hence

\operatorname{div}_{\bar{{\tilde{D}}}_{1,U}}(f_{U})={\mathcal{H}}+a({\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}-{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q_{1}}})

Now we consider
{\tilde{D}}_{2,U}
. This remains irreducible over
H_{Q_{1}}-H_{Q_{2}}
. Hence

\operatorname{div}_{\overline{{\tilde{D}}_{2,U}}}(f_{{\tilde{D}}_{2,U}})=-{\mathcal{H}}

Adding this to the above result shows that

\partial(\Xi_{Q_{U},P_{U}})=a({\tilde{D}}_{1,1}-{\tilde{D}}_{1,2})

We now show
a=1
. The moduli space of del Pezzo surfaces of degree
d
has trivial rational
H^{1}
, so homological and rational equivalence are the same. Therefore we can find a function
h
such that

\operatorname{div}(h)=H_{Q_{1}}-\sum a_{Z}Z

for some divisors

which we may assume are disjoint from
H_{Q_{1}}
. Let
({\tilde{D}}_{U}.h)
be the decomposable cycle in
H^{3}_{{\mathcal{M}}}({\tilde{Z}}_{U},{\mathds{Q}}(2))
. The boundary of this cycle is

\partial(({\tilde{Y}},h))={\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}+{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q_{1}}}+{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}-\sum a_{Z}{\tilde{D}}_{1,Z}

where
{\tilde{D}}_{1,Z}
is the restriction of
{\tilde{D}}_{1}
to the fibres over
Z
. The
{\tilde{D}}_{1,Z}
do not intersect
{\tilde{D}}_{1,i,H_{Q}}
or
{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}
.

Consider the function

. This has divisor

\operatorname{div}(f_{U}h^{-a})={\mathcal{H}}+a({\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}-{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q_{1}}})-a({\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}+{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q_{1}}}+{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}})+a(\sum a_{Z}{\tilde{D}}_{1,Z})

={\mathcal{H}}-2a{\tilde{D}}_{1,2}-a{{\mathrm{E}}}_{R_{Q}}+a(\sum a_{Z}{\tilde{D}}_{1,Z})

In particular, the support of the divisor does not contain

f_{U}h^{-a}|_{{\tilde{Y}}_{1,H_{Q_{1}}}}
is a function on

and one has

\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{div}(f_{U}h^{-a}|_{{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}}))=0

On the other hand,

\operatorname{deg}(\operatorname{div}(f_{U}h^{-a}|_{{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}}))=(\operatorname{div}(f_{U}h^{-a}),{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}})

which implies

We know

and

({\tilde{D}}_{Z},{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}})=0\text{ for all $Z$}

This gives

. Hence

∎

Remark 4.5
.

On the set
H_{Q_{1}}\cap H_{Q_{2}}
the boundary is more complicated and involves both the new cycles
{\tilde{D}}_{1,1}
and
{\tilde{D}}_{2,1}

Corollary 4.6
.

The cycles
\Xi_{Q_{1},Q_{2},P}
are generically indecomposable.

Proof.

The boundary of a decomposable cycle cannot involve cycles which are not the restriction of generic cycles. Since
{\tilde{D}}_{1,1,H_{Q_{1}}}-{\tilde{D}}_{1,2,H_{Q_{1}}}
is not the restriction of a generic cycle, our cycle is not decomposable.
∎

In the case of K3 double covers of degree
2
del Pezzo surfaces, this is a result of Sato
[
Sat24
]
, which is what inspired this paper. However, our methods are different. His proof of generic indecomposability is to show that the Beilinson regular of the family of cycles evaluated on a specific
(1,1)
form is a is non-trivial function. Our argument can be considered a ‘non-Archimedean’ version of that.

5
Degree
d
del Pezzo surfaces and the degeneracy loci

In this section we describe the degeneracy loci
H_{Q}
. These are typically interesting submoduli of the moduli of del Pezzo surfaces which have been classically studied.

5.1
Degree 1

In this case the map induced by
|-2K_{X}|
determines a map to
{\mathds{P}}^{3}
and the del Pezzo surface
X_{1}
can be realized as a double cover of a quadric cone branched at a sextic, which is the complete intersection of a cubic surface with the quadric cone. The 240
(-1)
curves are obtained as components of the double cover of a conic cut out by a
tri-tangent plane
- which is a plane which meets the branched sextic at three points of tangency. The two
(-1)
curves lying over the conic meet at three points lying over these points of tangency.

From van Luijk and Winter
[
vLW23
]
, Lemma 2.7 one can see that a
(-1)
curve meets 183 other curves at least at
1
point and those can be used to construct motivic cycles.

5.2
Degree 2

This is the case studied by Sato
[
Sat24
]
. The
K3
surface is double cover of the degree
2
del Pezzo branched
X_{2}
at a curve
C
. The curve
C
itself is the branch locus of the canonical map induced by
-2K
to
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
and is a quartic curve in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
.

It is a well known classical fact that a quartic curve in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
has
28
bi-tangents. The
56

(-1)
curves in
X_{2}
are the curves lying over these bi-tangents.

The
(-1)
curve
D
we use to construct the cycle lies over a line
Q
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
which is a bi-tangent to the quartic curve
C
. In general there will be two points of bi-tangency on the quartic. The two points lying over these bi-tangents are the points
s_{Q}
and
t_{Q}
.

When the two bi-tangents coincide it is called a point of inflection. When this happens it means that the corresponding del Pezzo corresponds to a point of
H_{Q}
and the cycle
\Xi_{Q,P}
is not defined.

For a given bi-tangent
Q
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
there are two curves in
X_{2}
. These two curves
D_{1}
and
D_{2}
meet at two points. Recall that under the blow down map to
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
the
(-1)
curves map to one of four possibilities - a point - in which case the curve is an exceptional curve, a line through two of the singular points, a conic through five of the singular points or a cubic through seven points, one of which is a double point. A little analysis shows that the two curves lying over a bi-tangent are either a pair of an exceptional curve and a cubic or a line and a conic. Further, these pairs do not meet at any of the exceptional points
P
, they meet at the points lying over the points of bi-tangency.

In his construction Sato uses two bi-tangent lines to build the cycle. In fact, looking at his construction more carefully shows that he uses two
(-1)
curves in the del Pezzo which meet at two points which do not come from the same bi-tangent. So one has that the cycles we have are essentially those he constructs. His proof of indecomposability is quite different though - he computes the real regulator and shows it is non-trivial by evaluating it at the
X_{2}
which is the double cover of
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
branched at the Fermat quartic.

The locus where the cycle degenerates is when the two points
s_{D_{1}}
and
t_{D_{1}}
coincide. In terms of the quadrics this is when the bi-tangents is an infection bi-tangent - namely those curves where the bi-tangent meets at a point of multiplicity four.

5.3
Degree 3

In this case the del Pezzo is a cubic surface and the
(-1)
curves are the
27
lines on the cubic surface. There are several ways of viewing the cubic surface as the blow up of
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
and there is a lot of classical geometry behind it. A pair of six lines satisfying some conditions forms what is called a
double-six
. This determines a quadric surface called a
Schur quadric
as well as a blow-down map to
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
where one of the pairs of six lines are the exceptional curves.

The intersection of this quadric surface with the cubic surface determines a genus
4
curve
B
and the
K3
surface is the double cover of the cubic surface branched at
B
. The blow down of
B
is a nodal sextic curve
S
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
and the
K3
surface can also be considered as the normalization of the double cover of
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
branched at
S
.

5.4
Rational curves on del Pezzo surfaces

Much of the construction does not use the fact that the curves are
(-1)
curves but uses the fact that they are rational curves which in general meet the branch locus at double points - except at two points. The degeneracy locus is then where the two points coincide. In the case of cubics it is known by the work of Itzykson
[
Itz94
]
and more generally, Gottsche-Pandharipande
[
GP98
]
, that given a class
{\bf a}=(a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{9-d})
in the Neron-Severi group of a del Pezzo of degree
d
there exists a finitely many rational curves
C
of class
{\bf a}
passing through
3a_{0}-\sum_{i}a_{i}-1
points for certain
{\bf a}
. The theorems are the generalization of the statement that there exists finitely many rational curves of degree
\delta
in
{\mathds{P}}^{2}
passing through
3\delta-1
points.

The number
3a_{0}-\sum_{i}a_{i}
is
\frac{1}{2}({\bf a},-2K_{X})
and so this statement can be used to say that for those
{\bf a}
there is a rational curve meeting the branch locus
C
in
X_{d}
at
3a_{0}-\sum_{i}a_{i}-1
double points and two other points. Using this rational curve in our construction along with a
(-1)
curve which it meets one can construct a motivic cycle which is defined outside the locus where the two points coincide - and this cycle is indecomposable. The case of
(-1)
curves is when
3a_{0}-\sum a_{i}=1
.

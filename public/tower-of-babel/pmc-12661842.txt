Identification of a novel diagnostic biomarker for osteoarthritis associated with chromatin regulators based on bioinformatics and experiments
Authors: Xingyu Wang, Jiakang Chen, Hao Xu, Jun Wu, Shuihua Xie
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundOsteoarthritis (OA) is a prevalent chronic degenerative joint disorder characterized by progressive cartilage degradation and extracellular matrix breakdown, yet its precise pathogenesis remains elusive. This study aimed to identify a novel diagnostic biomarker for OA associated with chromatin regulators (CRs) and to explore potential correlations between signature genes and immune cell infiltration.MethodsOA datasets were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and integrated with a chromatin regulator (CR) dataset. Differential expression analysis was conducted to identify CR-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs), which were subsequently subjected to functional enrichment analysis. Hub genes associated with CRs were identified through protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis. Single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was then employed to evaluate immune cell infiltration in OA and to assess correlations between hub gene expression and immune infiltration. Potential therapeutic compounds targeting these hub genes were predicted using the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB). A diagnostic risk model for OA was constructed based on the identified hub genes, and its predictive performance was assessed using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and calibration curves. Finally, the expression of the signature genes was validated both in vitro and in vivo through quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR), western blotting (WB), and mouse destabilization of the medial meniscus (DMM) models.ResultsA total of 86 CR-related DEGs were identified, primarily enriched in histone binding and modification, the cell cycle, and the FoxO signaling pathway. PPI network analysis revealed 10 hub genes, among which Aurora kinase B (AURKB) was identified as a potential diagnostic biomarker for OA based on the constructed risk model. In mouse DMM models and IL-1β–stimulated chondrocytes, qRT-PCR and western blotting demonstrated significantly elevated AURKB expression in diseased tissues and cells. ssGSEA analysis indicated significant differences in the infiltration levels of 11 immune cell types and 12 immune-related functions between OA and control samples. Furthermore, AURKB expression was positively correlated with tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs), Th1 cells, T-cell co-inhibition, and immune checkpoint activity. Background

Osteoarthritis (OA) is one of the most prevalent age-related chronic degenerative joint disorders worldwide, characterized by progressive articular cartilage degradation, synovial inflammation, and subchondral bone remodeling [1]. The main clinical manifestations include chronic joint pain, stiffness, and restricted mobility, which often result in long-term physical disability and irreversible functional impairment, profoundly reducing patients’ quality of life [2]. Epidemiological studies have shown that the prevalence and incidence of OA continue to rise globally, with an estimated overall incidence of approximately 15%, affecting more than 50% of individuals over 65 years of age and over 65% of those older than 80 years [3, 4]. Currently, the management of OA primarily aims to relieve clinical symptoms, while joint replacement surgery remains the principal option for end-stage disease. To date, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA) has not approved any disease-modifying therapy for OA [5, 6]. Increasing evidence suggests that immune cell infiltration in OA contributes to autoimmune responses, triggering the release of chemokines, proinflammatory cytokines, and proteases that disrupt immune homeostasis and accelerate chondrocyte apoptosis [7, 8]. Moreover, epigenetic dysregulation across multiple molecular pathways has been implicated in OA pathogenesis, providing novel mechanistic insights and potential therapeutic opportunities that may allow simultaneous targeting of multiple dysregulated pathways [9]. Therefore, identifying novel diagnostic biomarkers and therapeutic targets associated with epigenetic regulation in OA is of substantial clinical importance.

Chromatin regulatory factors (CRs) are a class of enzymes containing specialized functional domains that recognize, establish, and maintain epigenetic states in a context-dependent manner, acting as essential upstream regulators of epigenetic processes [10]. Based on their distinct regulatory functions, CRs are typically categorized into three major groups: DNA methylators, histone modifiers, and chromatin remodeling factors. Accumulating evidence indicates that CRs play pivotal roles in epigenetic regulation, and alterations at multiple molecular levels can lead to their dysfunction [11, 12]. Aberrant CR expression resulting from such dysfunction has been implicated in numerous biological processes, including inflammation, apoptosis, autophagy, and cell proliferation, suggesting that CR dysregulation may contribute to the pathogenesis of various diseases, such as osteoarthritis (OA) and other inflammatory disorders [13–15]. Indeed, epigenetic regulation orchestrates the expression of key genes in chondrocytes, and considerable research efforts have focused on elucidating these mechanisms in OA and its related pathologies. For instance, Kawata et al. [16] demonstrated through viral KLF4 gene delivery in human joint tissue cells, transgenic mice, and murine OA models that KLF4 and KLF2 act as critical transcription factors enhancing the protective and regenerative capacities of joint tissue cells. These findings suggest that KLF gene transfer or molecular upregulation of KLFs may represent promising therapeutic strategies for OA. As a chronic degenerative joint disease, OA disrupts extracellular matrix homeostasis and progressively leads to cartilage destruction. To date, no pharmacological treatment with specific therapeutic efficacy has been approved, and the precise etiology of OA remains unclear. Given that CRs are amenable to pharmacological modulation, identifying CRs that maintain normal extracellular matrix gene expression or contribute to its dysregulation is of critical importance for understanding OA pathogenesis and developing targeted therapies.

In recent years, advances in gene microarray technology and bioinformatics have made it possible to investigate complex diseases at multiple molecular levels, including transcriptomic alterations, epigenetic modifications, somatic mutations, and copy number variations. These approaches have been widely applied in OA research to identify key driver genes involved in disease progression and potential therapeutic targets [17, 18]. Although numerous studies have explored diagnostic biomarkers for OA, their clinical utility remains insufficiently validated. In the present study, we employed comprehensive bioinformatics analyses to identify novel OA diagnostic biomarkers associated with chromatin regulators (CRs) and to elucidate their potential roles in disease pathogenesis. Specifically, gene expression data from three OA-related datasets in the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (GSE12021, GSE55235, and GSE55457) were integrated with a curated CR dataset to generate a combined dataset. Differential expression analysis and protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis were then performed to identify CR-related hub genes in OA. Subsequently, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) and correlation analysis were used to assess associations between key OA genes and 29 immune cell types and immune-related functions, enabling identification of the hub gene most strongly associated with immune cell infiltration. Based on the identified CR-related hub genes, we further constructed a risk prediction model for OA and evaluated its diagnostic performance using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and calibration curve analyses. In addition, potential therapeutic compounds targeting these hub genes were predicted. Finally, our bioinformatic findings were experimentally validated through in vitro cellular assays, providing new insights and potential targets for the diagnosis, prevention, and treatment of OA. Methods

Data sources

Osteoarthritis (OA) and normal cartilage transcriptome datasets (GSE12021, GSE55235, and GSE55457) were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/). The GSE12021 dataset comprises 9 normal and 22 OA samples, the GSE55235 dataset includes 10 normal and 20 OA samples, and the GSE55457 dataset contains 10 normal and 23 OA samples. To minimize batch effects and technical variation among datasets, the three datasets were merged and corrected using the “LIMMA” and “SVA” R packages. A comprehensive list of chromatin regulatory factors (CRs) was obtained from Lu et al. [10] (Table S1). By integrating the combined GEO datasets with the CR dataset, we established a unified OA gene expression profile associated with chromatin regulators for subsequent analyses.

Identification of CRs-related DEGs

To identify differences in the expression of CRs-related genes between normal individuals and patients with osteoarthritis (OA), differential expression analysis was conducted using the “Limma” package in R. Genes with an adjusted p-value < 0.05 and |log2FC|> 0.5 were considered differentially expressed (DEGs). Regarding the choice of |log2FC|> 0.5 as the screening threshold, our intention was to balance sensitivity and specificity in identifying differentially expressed genes [19]. Meanwhile, the “ggplot2” and “pheatmap” packages in R were used to generate volcano and heat maps, respectively, to visualize the differentially expressed genes (DEGs).

Functional and pathway enrichment analysis

To elucidate the biological mechanisms and signaling pathways associated with CR-related genes in OA, functional enrichment analysis of CR-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) was performed using the “ClusterProfiler” package in R, based on Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) databases [20]. The GO analysis included three categories: biological processes (BP), cellular components (CC), and molecular functions (MF). GO terms or KEGG pathways with an adjusted p-value < 0.05 were considered statistically significant.

Construction of PPI network and identification of hub genes

The Search Tool for the Retrieval of Interacting Genes (STRING, https://string-db.org/) database was used to perform protein–protein interaction (PPI) analysis of CR-related differentially expressed genes (DEGs) [21], and the results were visualized using Cytoscape software [22]. The Molecular Complex Detection (MCC) algorithm within the CytoHubba plug-in of Cytoscape was applied to identify hub genes, and the top 10 genes with the highest scores were considered potential hub genes [23]. Then, we selected 5 genes with the strongest statistical significance, the highest correlation with prognosis, and the outstanding performance in terms of potential function reports in the literature for subsequent validation.

Immune cell infiltration and correlation analysis

The single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) method was applied to deconvolute the expression matrix of 16 human immune cell subtypes and 13 immune-related functions, thereby characterizing immune cell composition and immune function profiles in patients with OA and healthy controls [24]. Correlations between immune cells and immune functions were then assessed, and the results were visualized as a heatmap. Subsequently, differences in the infiltration levels of various immune cell types and immune functions between OA and control samples were analyzed using the rank-sum test implemented in the “vioplot” package in R, and the results were presented as violin plots. Finally, to explore the association between immune cell infiltration, immune function, and the expression of key genes in OA, Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed for the top five hub genes.

Construction and evaluation of signature genes risk model for OA

To further evaluate the diagnostic value of the five hub genes in OA, a logistic regression–based risk prediction model was constructed using the CR expression matrix and sample grouping information as input data. The “rms” package in R was then used to generate a nomogram for visualizing the OA risk model, calculate the risk scores of the five hub genes, and assess model performance through receiver operating characteristic (ROC) and calibration curve analyses. In the calibration plot, the 45° diagonal line represents the optimal agreement between predicted and observed outcomes.

Cell culture and the establishment of osteoarthritis model in vitro

ATDC5 chondrocytes were seeded at a density of 1 × 106 cells per well and cultured in standard DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin–streptomycin in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂ at 37 °C. To establish an in vitro osteoarthritis (OA) model, ATDC5 cells were stimulated with interleukin-1β (IL-1β, 10 ng/mL) for 24 h.

Total RNA extraction and real-time quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR)

Total RNA was extracted from chondrocytes and cartilage tissues using TRIzol reagent (Takara, Japan) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The purity and concentration of the extracted RNA were determined using a spectrophotometer, and 1,000 ng of RNA was reverse-transcribed into complementary DNA (cDNA). Quantitative real-time PCR (qRT-PCR) amplification was then performed using a real-time PCR SuperMix kit (Mei5bio, China). Relative mRNA expression levels were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCT method, with ACTB serving as the internal normalization control. The primer sequences used were as follows: AURKB forward, 5ʹ-GATCCCAGAACAAGCAGCCT -3ʹ; reverse, 5ʹ-TCGATTTCGATCTCTCGGCG-3ʹ.

Western blot analysis

Total proteins were extracted from ATDC5 cells and articular cartilage using RIPA lysis buffer (Applygen, China) supplemented with a mixture of phosphatase and protease inhibitors (Beyotime, China). The lysates were centrifuged at 12,000 × g for 10 min at 4 °C, and the resulting supernatants were collected. Protein concentrations were determined using the bicinchoninic acid (BCA) assay. Equal amounts of protein were separated by 10% SDS–PAGE and transferred onto polyvinylidene difluoride (PVDF) membranes (Millipore, USA). After blocking with 5% skim milk for 2 h at room temperature, the membranes were incubated overnight at 4 °C with primary antibodies against AURKB (1:1000, Boster, China) or β-actin (1:5000, Proteintech, China). After washing with Tris-buffered saline containing 0.1% Tween-20 (TBST), the membranes were incubated with corresponding secondary antibodies for 2 h at room temperature. Protein bands were visualized using the Bio-Rad Imaging System, with β-actin serving as the internal loading control.

Immunofluorescence analysis

After treatment, ATDC5 cells were washed three times with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS) and fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde for 15 min at room temperature. Following permeabilization with 0.1% Triton X-100 for 15 min, the cells were blocked with bovine serum albumin (BSA) for 30 min. Subsequently, the cells were incubated overnight at 4 °C with a rabbit anti-AURKB primary antibody (1:200), followed by incubation with an appropriate Alexa Fluor 488–conjugated secondary antibody for 1 h. Nuclei were counterstained with 4′,6-diamidino-2-phenylindole (DAPI) for 10 min at room temperature in the dark. Fluorescence images were captured using an Olympus microscope.

DMM-induced OA mouse model

All animal experiments were conducted in strict accordance with the guidelines and regulations of the Animal Ethics Committee of the Jiangxi Province Hospital of Integrated Chinese and Western Medicine. The osteoarthritis (OA) model was established using the surgical destabilization of the medial meniscus (DMM) procedure [25]. Briefly, eight-week-old male C57BL/6 mice (n = 6) were anesthetized by intraperitoneal injection of 10% chloral hydrate. A microsurgical scissor was then used to incise the joint capsule of the right knee to expose the medial meniscotibial ligament, which was subsequently transected to induce OA. For the sham group, arthrotomy was performed without transection of the ligament. After surgery, the mice were randomly divided into two groups: Sham and DMM. All mice were provided with equal access to food and water throughout the study period. Eight weeks after surgery, the mice were euthanized, and knee joint samples were harvested for further analysis.

Potential therapeutic drug prediction

Potential therapeutic drugs for OA were predicted using the Drug Signatures Database (DSigDB, http://tanlab.ucdenver.edu/DSigDB), based on protein–drug interaction data. Compounds with an adjusted p-value < 0.05 and a combined score > 5000 were considered as candidate therapeutic agents.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 8 and R software (version 4.2.2). Differences between groups were evaluated using Student’s t-tests, while Spearman’s correlation analysis was applied to assess associations between two variables. The predictive value of key genes for distinguishing OA samples from healthy controls was evaluated by calculating receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves and the corresponding area under the curve (AUC) using the R package "proc". All statistical tests were two-tailed, and a p-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Consistently, western blotting and immunofluorescence assays demonstrated markedly increased AURKB protein expression compared with the control group (P < 0.05) (Fig. 10B). Discussion

As one of the most prevalent musculoskeletal disorders, osteoarthritis (OA) continues to significantly affect quality of life and global public health [30]. Current clinical treatments for OA remain limited, consisting mainly of conservative pharmacological interventions and joint replacement surgery. However, conservative therapies can only transiently alleviate symptoms and slow disease progression without reversing cartilage degeneration. Therefore, the identification of accurate and reliable early diagnostic biomarkers is of critical importance for the prevention and early management of OA. Chromatin regulators (CRs) are a class of enzymes containing specialized functional domains that participate in diverse biological processes, including inflammation, apoptosis, autophagy, and cell proliferation. Despite their recognized roles in other diseases, their involvement in OA pathogenesis has been scarcely reported [13–15]. In this study, we systematically identified and analyzed CR-associated signature genes in OA using publicly available datasets, followed by in vitro experimental validation. Differential expression analysis revealed 86 CR-related candidate hub genes in OA. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) enrichment analyses indicated that these CR-related DEGs were mainly enriched in histone modification, chromatin remodeling, and peptidyl-lysine modification (BP); chromosomal region, nuclear chromosome, and nuclear speck (CC); and histone binding, DNA-binding transcription factor binding, and transcription corepressor activity (MF). KEGG pathway analysis further demonstrated that these DEGs were predominantly involved in the cell cycle, FoxO signaling pathway, cellular senescence, and NF-κB signaling pathway. Collectively, these findings suggest that CR-related DEGs are closely associated with epigenetic regulation and may play key roles in modulating mRNA expression and local transcriptional activity in OA chondrocytes.

Increasing evidence indicates that various immune cell populations play critical roles in the pathogenesis and progression of OA [31, 32]. In this study, we comprehensively analyzed immune cell infiltration patterns to elucidate the immunological mechanisms underlying OA. Using ssGSEA, we assessed the infiltration of 16 immune cell subtypes and 13 immune function pathways, revealing that 11 immune cell types and 12 immune functions were significantly elevated in OA samples compared with healthy controls. Subsequently, protein–protein interaction (PPI) network analysis identified the 10 most closely connected chromatin regulator (CR)-related hub genes in OA. Correlation analysis demonstrated that the expression levels of these hub genes were significantly and positively associated with the majority of immune cell types and immune function pathways. Among them, the top five hub genes—AURKA, AURKB, TOP2A, RAD51, and CDK1—showed the strongest associations with immune infiltration and were therefore selected as potential risk factors for OA progression. A binary logistic regression model based on these genes further indicated that AURKB may serve as an independent risk factor for OA.

Aurora kinase B (AURKB), a member of the Aurora kinase family located on chromosome 17, plays a crucial role in regulating the cell cycle alongside other Aurora kinases [33]. Previous studies have demonstrated that AURKB contributes to the biological progression of multiple malignancies, with its overexpression in tumor tissues being closely associated with unfavorable clinical outcomes. Consequently, AURKB has emerged as a promising therapeutic target in cancer treatment [34]. For instance, Borah et al. [35] reported that AURKB expression was markedly elevated in retinoblastoma compared with adjacent normal retinal tissue, and this upregulation significantly correlated with key histopathological risk factors such as optic nerve and anterior chamber invasion. Similarly, increased AURKB expression has been identified as a prognostic biomarker in several other cancers, including glioblastoma, gastric carcinoma, and oral squamous cell carcinoma [36–38]. Despite these findings, the role of AURKB in osteoarthritis (OA) progression and immune regulation remains largely unexplored. Investigating the epigenetic mechanisms by which AURKB mediates immune infiltration in OA may provide new insights into improving the OA immune microenvironment. To elucidate the function of AURKB in OA, we established a non-traumatic mouse OA model using destabilization of the medial meniscus (DMM) surgery and examined AURKB expression in diseased joint tissues. The results revealed significantly elevated AURKB expression in pathological joints compared with controls. Consistently, in vitro experiments demonstrated that AURKB expression was markedly upregulated in IL-1β–stimulated chondrocytes. Moreover, receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis indicated that AURKB possesses considerable diagnostic potential for distinguishing OA patients from healthy individuals, suggesting that AURKB may serve as an important diagnostic biomarker for OA. Additionally, We also found that AURKB expression was significantly and positively correlated with various immune cell populations and immune-related functions, suggesting that elevated AURKB expression may mediate autoimmune responses in osteoarthritis (OA) by promoting immune cell infiltration and thereby accelerating disease progression.

OA is a common degenerative joint disorder characterized by progressive cartilage destruction and extracellular matrix degradation. At present, clinical treatment options for OA remain limited. Consequently, the search for effective targeted therapies based on key pathogenic genes has become a major focus of current research. Based on the identified chromatin regulator (CR)–associated hub genes, we predicted several potential therapeutic compounds, including dasatinib, enterolactone, and genistein. Genistein, a naturally occurring bioactive isoflavone derived from soybeans, exhibits multiple physiological activities, including the regulation of glucose and lipid metabolism [39]. Previous studies have demonstrated that genistein possesses anti-inflammatory, anti-angiogenic, antiproliferative, antioxidant, immunomodulatory, analgesic, and chondroprotective properties [40]. Kitagawa et al. [41] reported that treatment of ATDC5 cells with high concentrations of genistein inhibited chondrogenic differentiation and mineralization. Moreover, Liu et al. [42] showed that genistein attenuates the progression of traumatic osteoarthritis and exerts protective effects by modulating inflammation associated with joint pathology. Collectively, these findings highlight the potential of genistein as a novel therapeutic agent for OA. In addition, dasatinib, enterolactone, resveratrol, and other compounds have also demonstrated therapeutic effects in OA models; however, their specific mechanisms of action and clinical efficacy warrant further investigation [26–29, 43].

However, this study has several limitations. First, although the microarray data used in our analyses generally met the requirements for sample size, potential limitations in sample quantity may have introduced some degree of bias in the results. Second, our investigation primarily relied on bioinformatics analyses of publicly available datasets to identify and screen chromatin regulator (CR)–related diagnostic biomarkers for osteoarthritis (OA). While preliminary validation was achieved through mouse OA models and in vitro cellular experiments, the precise molecular mechanisms underlying AURKB-mediated regulation of OA inflammation remain to be elucidated. Therefore, additional in vitro and in vivo studies are required to clarify the specific pathways through which AURKB influences OA pathogenesis. Finally, although our study demonstrated significant correlations between ten hub genes—including AURKB—and various immune cell populations and immune-related functions in OA, these associations have not yet been experimentally validated in biological systems, warranting further mechanistic investigation.

Conclusion

In summary, our findings indicate that AURKB plays a critical role in the onset and progression of osteoarthritis (OA) and holds substantial potential as a biomarker for early diagnosis and clinical intervention. Moreover, by elucidating the association between AURKB expression and immune cell infiltration in OA, this study provides novel insights into the disease’s pathogenesis and identifies AURKB as a promising target for OA immunotherapy.

Left-handed geodesic flow of spheres of revolution
Pierre Dehornoy, Ana Rechtman

Abstract
. A flow on a 3-manifold is left-handed if any two ergodic invariant measures have negative linking number. We prove that on a 2-sphere of revolution whose curvature is 1 / 4 1/4 -pinched the geodesic flow is left-handed. Conversely, for every δ ≤ 1 / 4 \delta\leq 1/4 , we construct a 2-sphere whose curvature is δ \delta -pinched and whose geodesic flow is not left-handed. This gives credit to a conjecture of Florio and Hryniewicz that 1 / 4 1/4 is the optimal pinching constant for left-handedness among arbitrary positively curved 2-spheres.



1.
Introduction

The aim of this note is to study the condition of left-handedness for geodesic flows of spheres of revolution, or in other words genus zero surfaces of revolution.
Left-handedness relies on the notion of linking of pairs of invariant measures, and corresponds to constant sign of these linking numbers.
Since the flows we consider here are integrable, they are left-handed if, roughly speaking, every pair of periodic orbits has negative linking number.

Left-handedness, introduced by É. Ghys
[
5
]
, implies in particular that every finite collection of periodic orbits bounds a Birkhoff section.
We proved that, in negative curvature, left-handedness of the geodesic flows occurs for triangular orbifolds only
[
2
]
.
Here, we relate left-handedness to the pinching of the curvature: the quotient
\delta(S)=\frac{K_{min}}{K_{max}}\leq 1
of the extreme values of the Gaussian curvature of the surface
S
.
We say that the curvature is
\delta
-pinched.
A. Florio and U. Hryniewicz proved that, in general, if the Gaussian curvature of a 2-sphere satisfies that
\delta>0.7225
, then the geodesic flow on its unit tangent bundle is left-handed
[
4
]
1
1

1

Florio and Hryniewicz adopt a convention different than ours for linking numbers of lifts of geodesics to the unitary tangent bundle, which leads to a change of sign. Hence their flows are right-handed when ours are left-handed.

.
They conjectured that the good bound for
\delta
should be
1/4
.
Our main result is that this is the optimal bound among spheres of revolution.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
S
be a sphere of revolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the induced metric.
If
\delta(S)>1/4
, the geodesic flow on
\mathrm{T}^{1}S
is left-handed.

Conversely, for every
\delta\leq 1/4
, there exists a
\delta
-pinched sphere of revolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
whose geodesic flow is not left-handed (nor right-handed).

The proof relies on a direct computation of the linking numbers between any two closed geodesics and on the fact that the geodesic flow admits an invariant function given by Clairaut’s identity, see Section
4.1
.

A “figure-eight” geodesic on a surface of revolution is a closed geodesic that turns twice around the axis of revolution and intersects the equator at one point with multiplicity two.
Lifts of figure-eight closed geodesic and the equator have zero linking number (see Proposition
5.2
).
We say that the surface has no asymptotic figure-eight geodesic if there exists
a>0
such that for every closed geodesic
\gamma
and two points
p_{1},p_{2}
in the intersection of
\gamma
with the equator that are consecutive along
\gamma
, the distance between
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
is at least
a
.
A figure-eight yields an asymptotic figure-eight, but the converse is not true in general.
The key point for proving Theorem
1.1
is the following:

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
S
be a sphere of revolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then the geodesic flow on
\mathrm{T}^{1}S
is left-handed if and only if there is no asymptotic figure-eight geodesic on
S
.

Applying this criterion to ellipsoids of revolution yields in particular

Proposition 1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{b}
be the ellipsoid of revolution with axes
(1,1,b)
.
The geodesic flow of
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{b}
is left-handed if and only if
b<2
.

Since the pinching
\delta(\mathbb{E}_{b})
is equal to
\frac{1}{b^{4}}
, this proposition implies the second part of Theorem
1.1
for
\delta\leq\frac{1}{16}
.
For
\delta
in
(\frac{1}{16},\frac{1}{4}]
, we note that the (asymptotic) figure-eight in a pinched ellipsoid lies in a small neighbourhood of the equator and in particular does not use all the freedom brought by the pinching.
Our proof of the second part of the theorem is obtained by smoothly gluing a neighborhood of the equator on
\mathbb{E}_{1/\sqrt{\delta}}
to a piece of round sphere.

2.
Linking numbers and left-handed flows

As mentioned before, a flow is
left-handed
if all invariant measures have negative linking number.
In this section, we review some notions and results that allow to make this definition precise.

2.1.
Linking number of links

Let
M
be a 3-dimensional compact manifold that is a rational homology sphere.
Then the integral homologygroup
\mathrm{H}_{1}(M;\mathbb{Z})
is finite, in particular it has an exponent
e
which is a positive integer.
Given an oriented link
L_{1}
, its multiple
eL_{1}
bounds a surface
S_{1}
.
Given another link
L_{2}
disjoint from
L_{1}
, their
linking number
, denoted by
\mathrm{Lk}(L_{1},L_{2})
, is the algebraic intersection between
S_{1}
and
L_{2}
, divided by
e
.
Thanks to the nullity of
[L_{2}]
, it does not depend on the choice of the surface
S_{1}
.
By construction, the linking number is symmetric, bilinear and lies in
\frac{1}{e}\mathbb{Z}
.

An equivalent construction is obtained as follows.
The
normal blow-up
of
L_{1}
is the manifold
M_{L_{1}}
obtained by replacing every point
p
of
L_{1}
by the unit normal bundle to
L_{1}
, denoted by
N^{1}_{L_{1}}(p)
.
The manifold
M_{L_{1}}
has a toric boundary
N^{1}_{L_{1}}
.
Because
M
is a homology sphere,
\mathrm{H}_{1}(M_{L_{1}};\mathbb{R})
is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}
.
Denote by
m_{1}
a meridian of
L_{1}
, and by
[m_{1}]
its class in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(M_{L_{1}};\mathbb{R})
.
A multi-curve
L_{2}
has a homology class
[L_{2}]
which is a multiple of
[m_{1}]
.
The
linking number

\mathrm{Lk}(L_{1},L_{2})
is then the unique rational number so that

[L_{2}]=\mathrm{Lk}(L_{1},L_{2})[m_{1}].

2.2.
Linking number of invariant measures

Assume now that
M
carries a flow
(\varphi^{t})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of class
C^{1}
.
Given an ergodic
\varphi^{t}
-invariant probability measure
\mu
and a
\mu
-regular point
x
, one can pick a sequence
(t_{n})\to+\infty
so that
\varphi^{t_{n}}(x)
tends to
x
.
Concatenating the orbit segment
\varphi^{[0,t_{n}]}(x)
with the shortest geodesic arc connecting its endpoints, one obtains a closed curve, denoted by
k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n})
.
Playing the same game with a second ergodic measure
\nu
and a
\nu
-regular point
x^{\prime}
not on the orbit of
x
, one would like to define the linking of
\mu
and
\nu
as
\lim_{t_{n},t^{\prime}_{n}\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{n}t^{\prime}_{n}}\mathrm{Lk}(k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n}),k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n}))
.
There are difficulties in order to make the above definition rigorous.
However the flows we consider in this note are integrable, and for this type of flows we shall later prove

Lemma 2.1

(linking of ergodic invariant measures)
.

Let
S
be a sphere of revolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the induced metric and let
\varphi^{t}
be its geodesic flow.
Let
\mu,\nu
be two
\varphi^{t}
-invariant ergodic measures that do not charge the same periodic orbit, and let
x,x^{\prime}
be regular points for
\mu
and
\nu
respectively, then the limit

\lim_{t_{n},t^{\prime}_{n}\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{n}t^{\prime}_{n}}\mathrm{Lk}(k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n}),k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n}))

exists and does not depend on
x,x^{\prime},t_{n},t^{\prime}_{n}
.

We then define the
(dynamical) linking

\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\nu)
of the invariant measures as the above limit.

Remark 2.2
.

Lemma
2.1
is also valid for non integrable flows, we refer to Ghys’ article
[
5
]
for a sketch of proof. Completing the sketch is beyond the scope of this note.
We provide a proof for spheres of revolution in Section
4.2
.

If the invariant measure
\mu
charges a periodic orbit
\gamma
, one can perform the following alternative construction.
We consider the normal blow-up
M_{\gamma}
defined in the previous subsection.
Using its space derivative
D\varphi
, the flow
\varphi^{t}
extends to the boundary torus
N^{1}_{\gamma}
.
Given an ergodic invariant measure
\nu
not charging
\gamma
, it has an asymptotic cycle
\sigma^{\gamma}_{\nu}
in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(M_{\gamma};\mathbb{R})
.
Recall that this asymptotic cycle is defined, for
x
a
\nu
-regular point, as
\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}[k_{\varphi}(x,t)]
.
This object is known as Schwartzman’s asymptotic cycle
[
6
]
.
Therefore
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\nu)
is equal to
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\sigma^{\gamma}_{\nu}
in this case, where
\mathrm{len}(\gamma)
is the period of the orbit
\gamma
.

This second definition of the linking can be naturally extended to a dynamical self-linking number of a periodic orbit:
assuming that
\mu
is the ergodic invariant measure charging a periodic orbit
\gamma
, we can consider a
\varphi^{t}
-invariant measure
\mu^{\circ}
supported on the boundary torus
N^{1}_{\gamma}
of
M_{\gamma}
(this measure is not necessarily unique).
Such an invariant measure has an asymptotic cycle
\sigma^{\gamma}_{\mu^{\circ}}
in
\mathrm{H}_{1}(M_{\gamma};\mathbb{R})
, and we set
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\mu)
to be
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\sigma^{\gamma}_{\mu^{\circ}}
.
Note that
\sigma^{\gamma}_{\mu^{\circ}}
is the translation number of the extension of the flow to
N^{1}_{\gamma}
with respect to the Seifert framing, hence it does not depend on the choice of
\mu^{\circ}
.

Summarizing the above discussion, we get

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\varphi^{t}
be an integrable flow on a rational homology sphere
M
.
Let
\mu,\nu
be two
\varphi^{t}
-invariant ergodic probability measures.

(A)

If
\mu,\nu
do not charge the same periodic orbit, then we define
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\nu)
as
\lim_{t_{n},t^{\prime}_{n}\to\infty}\frac{1}{t_{n}t^{\prime}_{n}}\mathrm{Lk}(k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n}),k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n}))
, where
x,x^{\prime}
are regular for
\mu
and
\nu
respectively;

(B)

If
\mu
charges a periodic orbit
\gamma
, then we define
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\mu)
as
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\sigma^{\gamma}_{\mu^{\circ}}
.

Assume now that
\mu
is the ergodic invariant measure charging a periodic orbit
\gamma
. Let
\nu_{n}
be a sequence of ergodic measures that converge in the
weak-
*
sense to
\mu
.
We claim that
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\nu_{n},\mu)
converges to
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\mu)
. Indeed, there is an invariant measure
\mu^{\circ}
in
N_{\gamma}^{1}
that is the weak-
*
limit of the measure
\nu_{n}
in
M_{\gamma}
.
Let
x_{n}
be a
\nu_{n}
-regular point, in this setting there is a subsequence of these points that converge to a point
y\in N_{\gamma}^{1}
that is
\mu^{\circ}
-regular.
The continuity of the flow ensures the convergence of the asymptotic cycles.

We can then define left-handedness:

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\varphi^{t}
be an integrable flow on a rational homology sphere
M
.
We say that
\varphi^{t}
is
left-handed
if for all
\varphi^{t}
-invariant ergodic probability measures
\mu,\nu
, one has
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\nu)<0
.

3.
Linking numbers in unit tangent bundles

As explained in the introduction, for the flows we consider in this note, checking left-handedness will eventually boil down to computing linking of pairs of closed orbits.
In this purely topological part, we explain how to compute linking numbers for lifts of geodesics to the unit tangent bundles.

From now on
M
is the 3-manifold
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
An orientation of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
induces an orientation of the fibers by trigonometric order, and the orientation of
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is obtained by concatenating both.

\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is a circle-bundle over
\mathbb{S}^{2}
with Euler number
2
.
It is homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}P^{3}
.
It is a rational homology sphere, with
\mathrm{H}_{1}(\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2},\mathbb{Z})\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.
Therefore the linking number of any pair of disjoint links is a number in
\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
.

3.1.
Linking of simple disjoint curves

We start with the linking of any two fibers:

Lemma 3.1
.

Given two distinct points
p_{1},p_{2}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the linking number of their oriented fibers
\mathrm{Lk}(\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
,
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})
is
-\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 3.2
.

As mentioned before, we have a different orientation convention than from Florio and Hryniewicz
[
4
]
. The orientation chosen above disagrees with the one chosen by Florio and Hryniewicz.

Proof.

To compute the linking number with the fiber of
p_{1}
, we claim that there is a natural surface bounded by
-2\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
.
Indeed there exists a
C^{1}
vector field
X_{1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
that vanishes at
p_{1}
only.
By the Poincaré-Hopf formula, the index of
X_{1}
at
p_{1}
is
\chi(\mathbb{S}^{2})=2
.
The vector field
X_{1}
is not of norm 1, but defines a direction at each point of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus\{p_{1}\}
.
Hence, lifting the direction defined by
X_{1}
to
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
, we obtain a topological surface
S_{X_{1}}\subset\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Orienting
S_{1}
by lifting the orientation of
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, its oriented boundary is then
-2\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
, see Figure
1
.
The surface
S_{X_{1}}
intersects once the fiber of every point distinct from
p_{1}
, since at each point
p\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
different from
p_{1}
the vector field
X_{1}
is non zero.
This intersection is positive, hence equal to
1
. Therefore one has
\mathrm{Lk}(-2\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=1
, so that
\mathrm{Lk}(\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

X_{1}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}

in the negative direction, hence the oriented boundary of

is
-2\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
.

From this one can also compute the linking of two disjoint simple curves.
Given
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
two disjoint
C^{1}
oriented, simple, closed curves in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, there is an oriented annulus
A\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
whose oriented boundary is
\pm\gamma_{1}\pm\gamma_{2}
.
If
A
is bounded by
\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}
or by
-\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
we say that the orientations of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}

coincide
.

Proposition 3.3
.

Given
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
two disjoint
C^{1}
oriented, simple, closed curves in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
,

•

if the orientations of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
coincide, then
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
,

•

otherwise one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Denote by
A
the annulus bounded by
\pm\gamma_{1}\pm\gamma_{2}
in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Also pick a point
p_{1}
in the component of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus A
bounded by
\pm\gamma_{1}
, and a point
p_{2}
in the component of
\mathbb{S}^{2}\setminus A
bounded by
\pm\gamma_{2}
.

If
\partial A=\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}
, then
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
is isotopic to
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
in the complement of
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
. Similarly
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
is isotopic to
-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2}
in the complement of
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
.
Therefore one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
.

The case
\partial A=\gamma_{2}-\gamma_{1}
is treated similarly, and one obtains
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=\frac{1}{2}
.

If
\partial A=\gamma_{1}+\gamma_{2}
, then
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
is isotopic to
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
in the complement of
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
, and
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
is isotopic to
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2}
in the complement of
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
.
Therefore one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.

And finally if
\partial A=-\gamma_{1}-\gamma_{2}
, then
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
is isotopic to
-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
in the complement of
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
, and
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
is isotopic to
-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2}
in the complement of
\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1}
.
Therefore one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{1},-\mathrm{T}^{1}p_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.
∎

3.2.
Seifert’s desingularization and Reidemeister II moves

An oriented multi-curve on a surface is the union of a finite number of (non necessarily disjoint) oriented closed curves.
An oriented
C^{1}
multi-curve
\gamma
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
is in general position if all multiple points are double transverse self-intersection points. Its lift to
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
is denoted by
\vec{\gamma}
.

Two oriented
C^{1}
multi-curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
are in general position if both curves are in general position and all intersection points are double transverse intersection points.
Their lifts
\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2}
in
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
form a link.
In this section we explain how to compute the linking number
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})
.

Definition 3.4
.

Given a multi-curve
\gamma
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in general position, a multi-curve obtained from
\gamma
by desingularizing every double point of
\gamma
in a small enough disc, see Figure
2
, is called a
Seifert desingularization
of
\gamma
.
It is denoted by
\gamma^{S}
.

xz

Figure 2.
The Seifert desingularization of a double point. One removes the double point and connects the remaining strands is the unique way that respects the orientation. The operation is made in a small disc (in gray) that is disjoint from all other parts of the curve and all other discs where the desingularization is performed.

The Seifert desingularization of a multi-curve is a simple multi-curve (
i.e.
, it has no self-intersection points).
The next lemma reduces the computation of the linking number of lifts of closed curves to the linking number of lifts of simple multi-curves.

Lemma 3.5
.

Given two oriented
C^{1}
multi-curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, denote by
\gamma^{S}_{1},\gamma^{S}_{2}
some Seifert desingularizations.
Then one has

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}^{S}_{1},\vec{\gamma}^{S}_{2}).

Proof.

We claim that, for
(i,j)=(1,2)
or
(2,1)
, there is a cobordism between
\vec{\gamma}_{i}
and
\vec{\gamma}_{i}^{S}
that is disjoint of
\vec{\gamma}_{j}
, namely a surface whose boundary is
\vec{\gamma}_{i}^{S}-\vec{\gamma}_{i}
.
Indeed, as sets,
\gamma_{i}
and
\gamma_{i}^{S}
differ only in a neighborhood of the self-intersection points of
\gamma_{i}
, these self-intersection points correspond to fibers in
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
that intersect
\vec{\gamma}_{i}
at two points, hence the boundary of a tubular neighborhood of the fiber intersects in four points the curve
\vec{\gamma}_{i}
.
The lift
\vec{\gamma}_{i}^{S}
connects these four points in the other possible way that respects the orientations, the arcs in these connections are contained in the tubular neighborhood of the fiber.
We have then a local cobordism.
Locally this cobordism is disjoint from
\vec{\gamma}_{j}
, since there are no intersection point between
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
in a neighborhood of the self-intersection points of both curves.
∎

We now define an analog of the Reidemeister II move for oriented simple closed curves on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
and we explain how this operation affects the linking number of their lifts to
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
.

Suppose that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are two simple non-oriented curves in
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, in general position, such that
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}\neq\emptyset
.
Consider two intersection points
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
that are consecutive along both curves, then there is a topological disc
D\subset\mathbb{S}^{2}
bounded by the closed curve given by the segments of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
between
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
that does not contain other intersection points.

The
Reidemeister II move
, moves one of the curves, say
\gamma_{2}
, to another curve
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
that coincides with
\gamma_{2}
outside an open neighborhood
U
of
D
, see Figure
3
.
It reduces the number of intersection points by
2
. For
t\in[0,1]
denote by
\gamma_{2}^{t}
a continuous 1-parameter family of closed curves achieving a deformation from
\gamma_{2}
to
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
:
\gamma_{2}^{t}
coincides with
\gamma_{2}
outside
U
for every
t
;
\gamma_{2}^{0}=\gamma_{2}
and
\gamma_{2}^{1}=\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
, see Figure
3
.

Now assume that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are oriented, the orientation of
\gamma_{2}
induces an orientation on
\gamma_{2}^{t}
for every
t
.
There are two cases for the disc
D
: either the orientations of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
make the bigon bounding
D
oriented, or not.

Lemma 3.6
.

Consider two closed simple multi-curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
in general position.
Assume that
\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
is obtained from
\gamma_{2}
by applying a Reidemeister II move with respect to
\gamma_{1}
in a bigon
D
.

•

If the orientations of
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
make the bigon bounding
D
oriented, then one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}^{\prime}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})
.

•

Otherwise one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}^{\prime}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})+1
.

Figure 3.
The Reidemeister II move on a pair of oriented curves. The move itself does not depend on the orientations of the curves, but the family
\gamma_{2}^{t}
(on the right) lifts to a surface that intersects or not

, depending on whether the orientations match or not.

Proof.

Consider the curves
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
and
\vec{\gamma}_{2}^{t}
for
t\in[0,1]
.

In the first case, all vectors positively tangent to
\gamma_{2}^{t}
are disjoint from the vectors positively tangent to
\gamma_{1}
.
Then they induce a cobordism from
\vec{\gamma}_{2}
to
\vec{\gamma}^{\prime}_{2}
that does not intersect
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
.
Therefore one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}^{\prime}_{2})
.

In the second case, there is a
t\in(0,1)
such that
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma^{t}_{2}
have a common tangent vector, meaning that
\vec{\gamma}_{1}
and
\vec{\gamma}_{2}^{t}
intersect.
This intersection point is negative, hence
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}^{\prime}_{2})-1
.
∎

Lemma
3.6
yields a procedure to compute the linking number of the lifts of two simple closed curves: starting from the two curves, one iteratively applies Reidemeister II moves to make them disjoint.
At every step one removes 0 or 1 to their linking number, depending on whether the orientations match.
In the end there remains two disjoint simple curves, the linking of whose lifts is given by Proposition
3.3

The situation we will encounter is even simpler since no complicated meanders can appear for geodesics on spheres of revolution.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two oriented simple closed curves on
\mathbb{S}^{2}
.
Assume that
\gamma_{1}\cap\gamma_{2}
consists of
2k
points with
k>0
.
Label them in a increasing order
1,2,\dots,2k
when travelling along
\gamma_{1}
.

•

If when travelling along
\gamma_{2}
one encounters the intersection points in the ordering
1,2,\dots,2k
, then one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\frac{1}{2}-k
.

•

If when travelling along
\gamma_{2}
one encounters the intersection points in the ordering
2k,2k-1,\dots,1
, then one has
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

One can use
k
Reidemeister II moves to separate the two curves
\gamma_{1}
,
\gamma_{2}
, and obtain two disjoint oriented curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma^{\prime}_{2}
.
When using this move, either the orientations disagree and
\mathrm{Lk}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}^{\prime})=-\frac{1}{2}
or the orientations agree, and this moves adds
k
to the linking number of the lifts in
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{S}^{2}
, thus

\mathrm{Lk}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\mathrm{Lk}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}^{\prime})-k=\frac{1}{2}-k.

∎

4.
Positively curved surfaces of revolution and their geodesic flows

We now recall some properties of surfaces of revolution and of their geodesic flows.

4.1.
Surfaces of revolution, their geodesics and Clairaut’s relation

Let
a<b
be two real numbers.
Consider two
C^{2}
functions
f,g:[a,b]\to\mathbb{R}
such that
f(v)>0
for all
v\in(a,b)
and
f(a)=f(b)=0
.
Consider the surface of revolution
S_{f,g}
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
defined as

S_{f,g}:=\{(f(v)\cos u,f(v)\sin u,g(v));\quad v\in[a,b]\quad\mbox{and}\quad 0\leq u<2\pi\}.

Note that
f
gives the distance of a point to the axis of revolution.
The Gaussian curvature of
S_{f,g}
at a point is given by

K(u,v)=-\frac{g^{\prime}(g^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}-g^{\prime\prime}f^{\prime})}{f((f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2})^{2}},

and, when
f^{\prime 2}+g^{\prime 2}=1
, we get
K(u,v)=-\frac{f^{\prime\prime}}{f}
. In this setting, the curvature is strictly positive if
f^{\prime\prime}(v)<0
for all
v\in(a,b)
.

A curve on
S_{f,g}
corresponds to a curve in the
(u,v)
-plane that can be parametrised as
(u(t),v(t))
. Denoting by
u^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}
the derivatives of these functions with respect to
t
, the curve is geodesic if and only if it satisfies:

(1)

\displaystyle u^{\prime\prime}+\frac{2ff^{\prime}}{f^{2}}u^{\prime}v^{\prime}

\displaystyle=0

(2)

\displaystyle v^{\prime\prime}-\frac{ff^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2}}(u^{\prime})^{2}+\frac{f^{\prime}f^{\prime\prime}+g^{\prime}g^{\prime\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2}}(v^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle=0.

If
u(t)
is a constant function, that is, we consider a meridian on
S_{f,g}
, the first equation is trivially satisfied and the second equation can be deduced from the first fundamental form of the surface (see page 161 of
[
3
]
).
Hence, meridians are geodesics.

Now if
v(t)
is a constant function, the first equation gives
u^{\prime\prime}=0
, hence
u^{\prime}
is a non-zero constant function, while the second equation gives

-\frac{ff^{\prime}}{(f^{\prime})^{2}+(g^{\prime})^{2}}(u^{\prime})^{2}=0.

This implies
ff^{\prime}=0
and hence if
f\neq 0
then
f^{\prime}=0
.
Therefore a parallel is a geodesic if and only if it corresponds to a critical point of
f
.

Now observe that Equation (
1
) can be written as

(f^{2}u^{\prime})^{\prime}=f^{2}u^{\prime\prime}+2ff^{\prime}u^{\prime}v^{\prime}=0,

hence
f^{2}u^{\prime}
is constant.
On the other hand, if at a certain point a geodesic forms an angle
\theta
,
-\pi<\theta\leq\pi
with respect to the parallel (oriented by
u
) at the given point, then

\cos\theta=\frac{\langle x_{u},x_{u}u^{\prime}+x_{v}v^{\prime}\rangle}{|x_{u}|}=fu^{\prime}

where
x_{u}=(-f(v)\sin u,f(v)\cos u,0)
and
x_{v}=(f^{\prime}(v)\cos u,f^{\prime}(v)\sin u,g^{\prime}(v))
form a basis for the tangent space of
S
.
Since
f
is the radius of the parallel, we obtain Clairaut’s relation:

f\cos\theta
is constant along a geodesic.

4.2.
Invariant tori and invariant measures for spheres of revolution

We start with a sphere of revolution
S_{f,g}
as in the previous section.
It consists of those points of the form
p(u,v)=(f(v)\cos u,f(v)\sin u,g(v))
with
v\in[a,b]
and
u\in[0,2\pi)
.
We call
u
the argument of
p
and
f
the radius of
p
, that is, the distance from
p
to the axis of revolution.
For
(p,w)\in\mathrm{T}^{1}S
and
p
not a pole, define
\theta(w)\in(-\pi,\pi]
as the angle between
w
and the tangent vector to the (positive) parallel.
For
\alpha\in\mathbb{R}
, define

T_{\alpha}:=\{(p,w)\in\mathrm{T}^{1}S\,|\,f(p)\cos(\theta(w))=\alpha\}.

Clairaut’s relation implies that, for every
\alpha
,
T_{\alpha}
is invariant by the geodesic flow.

We now assume that the curvature of
S_{f,g}
is strictly positive, so that
f^{\prime}
can be zero only once.
Thus there is only one geodesic that lies on a plane orthogonal to the axis of revolution.
We call it the
equator
, denoted by
e
.
We also denote by
e_{+}
the oriented geodesic obtained by following the equator in the positive direction (
i.e.,
with
u
increasing), and by
e_{-}
the oriented geodesic obtained by following the equator in the negative direction.
Observe that
S\setminus\{e\}
has two components, the north and south hemisphere.
We denote by
r_{e}
the radius of
e
.
For
\alpha=\pm r_{e}
, the set
T_{\alpha}
is a circle that coincides with one of the two lifts of
e
, namely the orbits
\vec{e}_{+}
and
\vec{e}_{-}
of the geodesic flow.
For
\alpha\in(-r_{e},r_{e})
,
T_{\alpha}
is a torus, foliated by the orbits of the geodesic flow.

Denote by
\ell_{\alpha}
the parallel in
T_{\alpha}
of the form

\{(p,w)\in\mathrm{T}^{1}S;f(p)=\alpha,\cos(\theta(w))=1\}

and by
m_{\alpha}
the oriented meridian in
T_{\alpha}
of the form

\{(p,w)\in\mathrm{T}^{1}S;u(p)=\mathrm{cst},f(p)\cos(\theta(w))=\alpha\}.

The pair
(\ell_{\alpha},m_{\alpha})
forms a basis of the first homology group of
T_{\alpha}
.

The restriction of the geodesic flow to
T_{\alpha}
is invariant by rotation in the longitudinal direction.
When integrating along an invariant measure, the geodesic flow has an asymptotic cycle of the form
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
in the basis
(\ell_{\alpha},m_{\alpha})
.
The invariance by rotation implies in particular that
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
does not depend on the chosen invariant measure on
T_{\alpha}
.
Geometrically,
x_{\alpha}
is the inverse of the period of the flow in the longitudinal direction and it has the sign of
\alpha
.
Also,
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
is the average number of times the corresponding geodesic crosses the equator positively over one longitudinal period.

Note that
T_{\alpha}
is foliated by periodic orbits if and only the ratio
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
is rational.
If
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
is irrational, the restriction of the flow to
T_{\alpha}
is conjugated to a linear flow.
In this case, for
p\in T_{\alpha}
and
t
large enough, the curve
k_{\varphi}(p,t)
is a torus knot on
T_{\alpha}
of type
(p_{\alpha}^{t},q_{\alpha}^{t})
, where
q_{\alpha}^{t}/p_{\alpha}^{t}
is a rational approximation of
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
.

We can now explain why, in this context, the linking of two ergodic invariant measures exists.

Proof of Lemma
2.1
.

Assume that
\mu,\nu
are two ergodic
\varphi^{t}
-invariant measures, supported by
T_{\alpha},T_{\beta}
respectively.
For
x
a
\mu
-regular point and
x^{\prime}
a
\nu
-regular point, and for sequences
t_{n},t^{\prime}_{n}
such that
\varphi^{t_{n}}(x)\to x
and
\varphi^{t^{\prime}_{n}}(x^{\prime})\to x^{\prime}
, the knots
k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n})
and
k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n})
lie in (a neighborhood of)
T_{\alpha}
and
T_{\beta}
respectively.
Since
\mu
and
\nu
do not charge the same periodic orbit, the points
x
and
x^{\prime}
can be chosen so that they belong to different orbits. This implies that the knots
k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n})
and
k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n})
are disjoint and form a link.

The classes of
k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n})
and
k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n})
in the first homology groups of the corresponding tori are roughly
t_{n}(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
and
t^{\prime}_{n}(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})
.
Therefore
\frac{1}{t_{n}t^{\prime}_{n}}\mathrm{Lk}(k_{\varphi}(x,t_{n}),k_{\varphi}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime}_{n}))
is roughly the linking of the curves
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
on
T_{\alpha}
and
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})
on
T_{\beta}
.
This concludes the proof.
∎

5.
Left-handedness for positively curved spheres

We put together the ingredients of the two previous sections to estimate the linking number of any two ergodic invariant measures, and then prove the main results.

5.1.
Reduction to linking with the equator

We show that for a positively curved sphere of revolution, left-handedness can be checked by computing the linking with the equator only.
We use the notations of Section
4
.

For
\beta>0
and
\alpha<\beta
, the two invariant tori
T_{\alpha},T_{\beta}
are disjoint.
Let
\mu
be an ergodic measure whose support is contained in
T_{\alpha}
.
A curve on
T_{\beta}
may be homotoped onto a curve on
T_{r_{e}}
in the complement of
T_{\alpha}
.
More precisely, if the curve has slope type
(x_{\beta},y_{\beta})
in the basis
(\ell_{\beta},m_{\beta})
, it may be homotoped onto
x_{\beta}
times the curve
T_{r_{e}}=\vec{e}_{+}
in the complement of
T_{\alpha}
.
Therefore the linking of two invariant measures
\mu,\nu
supported by
T_{\alpha},T_{\beta}
has the same sign as
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\vec{e}_{+})
, where we abuse of the notation and denote the ergodic invariant measure supported by
\vec{e}_{+}
with the same sign.

By a similar argument, if
\beta<0
and
\alpha>\beta
, the linking of two invariant measures
\mu,\nu
supported by
T_{\alpha},T_{\beta}
has the same sign as
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\vec{e}_{-})
.
Thus we get

Lemma 5.1
.

The geodesic flow on a sphere of revolution
S
is left-handed if and only if there exists
a>0
such that for every closed geodesic
\gamma
on
S
, one has
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})<-a
and
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})<-a
.

Proof.

First assume that the flow is left-handed.
By Definition
2.4
this means that the linking of any two invariant measures is negative.
Assume for a contradiction that there is no positive
a
satisfying the statement.
Then, up to reversing their orientations, there is a sequence of oriented periodic geodesics
\gamma_{n}
such that
\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma_{n})}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma}_{n})
tends to
0
.
Recall from Section
2.2
that
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\cdot)
is continuous in the weak-* topology.
By extracting a subsequence we find a invariant measure whose linking with
\vec{e}_{+}
is zero, a contradiction.

We prove the converse implication.
Let
\mu
and
\nu
be two ergodic measures of the geodesic flow of
S
.
Assume first that
\mu
and
\nu
do not charge the same periodic orbit.
Then the above discussion implies that
\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\nu)
equals the linking of either
\mu
or
\nu
with either
\vec{e}_{+}
or
\vec{e}_{-}
, hence it is smaller than
-a
.

Now assume that
\mu=\nu
is a measure supported by a periodic orbit
\vec{\gamma}
of the geodesic flow. Let
\alpha\in(-r_{e},r_{e})
be such that
\vec{\gamma}\subset T_{\alpha}
, then
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
is a rational number and we can choose a sequence
\beta_{n}
of numbers in
(-r_{e},r_{e})
such that
y_{\beta_{n}}/x_{\beta_{n}}\neq y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
for all
n
and forms a monotone sequence that converges to
y_{\alpha}/x_{\alpha}
. Consider the manifold
\mathrm{T}^{1}S_{\vec{\gamma}}
obtained by blowing up
\vec{\gamma}
and an ergodic measure
\mu^{\circ}
of the induced flow, whose support is contained in
\partial\mathrm{T}^{1}S_{\vec{\gamma}}
and is the limit of measures
\nu_{n}
supported in the tori
T_{\beta_{n}}
(observe that the flow in
\mathrm{T}^{1}S_{\vec{\gamma}}
leaves these tori invariant, with the exception of
T_{\alpha}
). Then

\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\mu,\mu)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathrm{Lk}^{\varphi}(\nu_{n},\mu^{\circ})\leq-a<0.

We conclude that the flow is left-handed.
∎

5.2.
Linking with the equator and aysmptotic figure-eights

In what follows, we explain why, in the case of spheres of revolution, the geodesic flow is left-handed if and only if there are no asymptotic figure eight geodesics (Proposition
1.2
).

As before,
S
denotes a sphere of revolution with positive curvature.
Its equator
e
lifts to two closed orbits of the geodesic flow
\vec{e}_{+}
and
\vec{e}_{-}
.
Consider an oriented geodesic
\gamma
that is different from the equator.
Then
\gamma
intersects
e_{+}
and
e_{-}
.
Denote by
\theta_{\gamma}\in(0,\pi)
the angle between
\gamma
and
e_{+}
at a point in which
\gamma
crosses
e_{+}
from the south to the north hemisphere.
Clairaut’s identity implies that at each crossing with the equator the angle between the geodesic and
e_{+}
is equal to
\pm\theta_{\gamma}
.

Firstly, the intersection between
e_{+}
and
\gamma
has an even number of points that is denote by
2q_{\gamma}
(here the orientation of
\gamma
is not important).
Secondly,
\gamma
makes a certain number of turns around the rotation axis that we call
p_{\gamma}
.
We call the pair
(p_{\gamma},q_{\gamma})
the
type
of
\gamma
.
Observe that
p_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{Z}
and the sign is determined by the trigonometric direction in the
xy
-plane or, equivalently, by the sign of
\cos(\theta_{\gamma})
.
Moreover,
p_{\gamma}=0
implies that
\gamma
is a meridian, then
q_{\gamma}=1
and
\theta_{\gamma}=\pi/2
.

With the notations of Section
4
, the periodic orbit
\vec{\gamma}
lies in the invariant torus
T_{\alpha}
with
\alpha=r_{e}\cos(\theta_{\gamma})
.
The latter is foliated by periodic orbits all parallel to
\vec{\gamma}
.
In the
(\ell_{\alpha},m_{\alpha})
-basis, they have a rational direction
(x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha})
which is colinear with
(p_{\gamma},q_{\gamma})
.

Proposition 5.2
.

The linking number between
\vec{e}_{+}
and
\vec{e}_{-}
equals
-1/2
.

Let
\gamma
be a closed oriented geodesic of
S
of type
(p_{\gamma},q_{\gamma})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
q_{\gamma}\geq 1
.

(1)

If
\theta_{\gamma}\in(0,\pi/2)
or equivalently
p_{\gamma}>0
, then

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})=\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}-q_{\gamma},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})=-\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}.

(2)

If
\theta_{\gamma}\in(\pi/2,\pi)
or equivalently
p_{\gamma}<0
, then

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})=-\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{2},\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})=\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{2}-q_{\gamma}.

(3)

If
\theta_{\gamma}=\pi/2
or
p=_{\gamma}0
we have

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})=-\frac{1}{2}.

Proof.

In
\mathrm{T}^{1}S
, we can push
\vec{e}_{-}
, in the complement of
\vec{e}_{+}
, to a curve
\sigma_{-}
so that the projection to
S
of
\sigma_{-}
lies in the north hemisphere of
S
. Then we obtain two disjoint oriented simple closed curves with opposite orientations as in the first case of Proposition
3.3
, hence
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{e}_{-})=-1/2
.

Now assume
\theta_{\gamma}\in(0,\pi/2)
or, equivalently,
p_{\gamma}>0
.
Since
\gamma
is a geodesic, its self-intersection points are transverse as well as the intersection points between
\gamma
and
e_{+}
.
Following the ideas at the end of the Section
3.2
, we first move
e_{+}
up to the north hemisphere, if needed, so that
e_{+}
and
\gamma
are in general position: that is to avoid having self-intersections points of
\gamma
coincide with intersection points between
\gamma
and
e_{+}
.
In
\mathrm{T}^{1}S
we can move
\vec{e}_{+}
in the complement of
\vec{\gamma}
.

Then we apply Seifert’s smoothing to
\gamma
to obtain a collection of oriented simple closed curves
\gamma^{S}
, as in Section
3.2
.
The multi-curve
\gamma^{S}
has
p_{\gamma}
connected components and only one intersects

, along
2q_{\gamma}
points as in Figure

.
Applying Proposition
3.7
we obtain

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})=\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma}^{S})=\frac{(p_{\gamma}-1)}{2}+\left(\frac{1}{2}-q_{\gamma}\right)=\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}-q_{\gamma};

and
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})=-\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}
.

The case
\theta_{\gamma}\in(\pi/2,\pi)
or

is analogous, except that
\gamma
turns in the direction of

.
Changing the roles of

and

If

Remark 5.3
.

Using the same ideas, we can compute the linking number between two arbitrary closed oriented geodesics of
S
.
For this we deform one geodesic into a cover of the equator as in the discussion in Section
4.2
.
We do not need these formulas in the sequel, but we include them without proof for the interested reader:

Let
\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
be two closed oriented geodesics of types
(p_{1},q_{1})
and
(p_{2},q_{2})
respectively.

(1)

If
p_{1}p_{2}\neq 0
, assume that
|\pi/2-\theta_{1}|\geq|\pi/2-\theta_{2}|
, then

\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=\left\{\begin{matrix}|p_{1}|\left(\frac{|p_{2}|}{2}-q_{2}\right)&p_{1}p_{2}>0\\
-\frac{|p_{1}p_{2}|}{2}&p_{1}p_{2}<0\end{matrix}\right.

(2)

If
p_{1}=p_{2}=0
, then
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=-\frac{1}{2}
.

(3)

If
p_{2}=0
and
p_{1}\neq 0
, then
\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{\gamma}_{1},\vec{\gamma}_{2})=-\frac{|p_{1}|}{2}
.

Observe that all linking numbers in Proposition

are strictly negative if
q_{\gamma}-\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{2}>0
for every oriented closed geodesic
\gamma
.
The condition is equivalent to
\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{q_{\gamma}}<2
.

Definition 5.4
.

A closed geodesic of type
(\pm 2,1)
is a
figure eight geodesic
.

We say that
S
has
no asymptotic figure-eight geodesic
if

\sup_{\gamma~\mathrm{closed}}\left(\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{q_{\gamma}}\right)<2.

Let
\gamma
be a closed geodesic.
Observe that
q_{\gamma}
is equal to the number of segments of
\gamma
that are contained in the norther hemisphere (or southern hemisphere).
The hypothesis
\sup\left(\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{q_{\gamma}}\right)<2
is then equivalent to saying that there is a strictly positive lower bound to the distance between the endpoints of any segment of
\gamma
contained in a hemisphere and that this bound is independent of
\gamma
.

Proposition
5.2
implies that the linking number between
\vec{e}_{\pm}
and one of the possible orientations of a figure eight geodesic is zero.
Hence a figure eight geodesic is an obstruction to left-handedness.

We can now prove the following result stated in the introduction that summarizes the above discussion.

Proposition
1.2
.

Let
S
be a sphere of revolution in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
, then the geodesic flow on
\mathrm{T}^{1}S
is left-handed if and only if there is no asymptotic figure-eight closed geodesic on
S
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
the flow is left-handed if and only if there exists
a>0
such that for every closed geodesic
\gamma
,

\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})<-a\qquad\mbox{and}\qquad\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{-},\vec{\gamma})<-a.

Denote by
(p_{\gamma},q_{\gamma})
the type of
\gamma
, as defined in the beginning of this section.
Assume
p_{\gamma}>0
, the case
p_{\gamma}<0
being analogous. Proposition
5.2
implies

\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{+},\vec{\gamma})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\left(\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}-q_{\gamma}\right)<-a

and

\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\mathrm{Lk}(\vec{e}_{1},\vec{\gamma})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{len}(\gamma)}\left(-\frac{p_{\gamma}}{2}\right)<-a.

Since
q_{\gamma}\geq 1
, these inequalities are equivalent to

\frac{2a}{q_{\gamma}}\mathrm{len}(\gamma)<\frac{p_{\gamma}}{q_{\gamma}}<2-\frac{2a}{q_{\gamma}}\mathrm{len}(\gamma).

Taking into account the cases
p_{\gamma}=0
and
p_{\gamma}<0
, we get that the flow is left-handed if and only if there exists
a>0
such that

0\leq\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{q_{\gamma}}<2-\frac{2a}{q_{\gamma}}\mathrm{len}(\gamma).

Since
a>0
, this is equivalent to
\sup\left(\frac{|p_{\gamma}|}{q_{\gamma}}\right)<2
among closed geodesics.
∎

5.3.
Proof of the first part of Theorem
1.1
.

In this section, we prove that if
\delta>1/4
, the geodesic flow is left-handed.
It follows from a result of Abbondandolo, Bramham, Hryniewicz and Salomão
[
1
]
, that is a consequence of a result by Toponogov.

Let
S
be a sphere of revolution satisfying
\delta(S)>1/4
.
Consider an oriented geodesic
\gamma
different from
e
.
We parametrize
\gamma
in such a way that
\gamma(0)\in e
.
Let
t_{1}>0
be the first point at which
\gamma(t_{1})\in e
.
We now copy

Lemma 5.5

(Lemma 3.9 in
[
1
]
)
.

Assume that
S
is a sphere endowed with a Riemannian metric such that
\delta>1/4
. Then the geodesic arc
\gamma(t)
for
t\in[0,t_{1}]
is injective.

This lemma is valid for spheres that are not surfaces of revolution. The positiveness of the curvature defines an equator
e
. Observe that the arc of geodesic
\gamma(t)
for
t\in[0,t_{1}]
is by definition contained in the closure of one of the hemispheres of
S
and since it is injective,
\gamma(0)\neq\gamma(t_{1})
. In particular,
\gamma
cannot be a figure eight closed geodesic.
Furthermore, the distance along
e
between
\gamma(0)
and
\gamma(t_{1})
admits a strictly positive lower bound that is independent of
\gamma
. Thus
S
has no asymptotic figure eight geodesic. Proposition
1.2
implies that the geodesic flow of
S
is left-handed.

5.4.
Left-handedness for ellipsoids of revolution

In this section we consider the ellipsoid
\mathbb{E}_{b}
defined by the equations in
\mathbb{R}^{3}

x^{2}+y^{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{b^{2}}=1,

with
b\geq 1
and the
z
-axis is the rotation axis. We prove in this section that its geodesic flow is left-handed if
b<2
:

Proposition
1.3
.

Let
\mathbb{E}_{b}
be the ellipsoid of revolution with axes
(1,1,b)
.
The geodesic flow of
\mathrm{T}^{1}\mathbb{E}_{b}
is left-handed if and only if
b<2
.

Proof.

A parametrisation of
\mathbb{E}_{b}
as a surface of revolution, see Section
4.1
, is given by

\begin{array}[]{rcl}x(u,v)&=&\cos v\cos u\\
y(u,v)&=&\cos v\sin u\\
z(u,v)&=&b\sin v,\end{array}

with
u\in[0,2\pi)
and
v\in[-\pi/2,\pi/2]
.
Thus its curvature is

K(u,v)=\frac{b^{2}}{(\sin^{2}v+b^{2}\cos^{2}v)^{2}}.

If
v=0
, that is along the equator, we have that the curvature is equal to
1/b^{2}
and is the minimum curvature if
b>1
, the maximum curvature if
b<1
. The other extremal value of the curvature is attained at the poles, that is when
v=\pm\pi/2
and is equal to
b^{2}
. Hence
\delta(\mathbb{E}_{b})=1/b^{4}
.

Consider an arc
\gamma
of geodesic that meets the equator at
(x,y,z)=(1,0,0)
of longitude
u=0
. We now compute the coordinates of the first point of intersection of
\gamma
with the equator. Let
\theta_{0}
be the angle between the equator
e_{+}
and
\gamma
. By symmetry, we assume
0<\theta_{0}\leq\pi/2
. Let
\alpha_{0}=\pi/2-\theta_{0}
, the angle between the meridian and
\gamma
.
For simplicity we set
\sigma_{0}=\sin\alpha_{0}
.
For a point on
\gamma
, we recall that
u
is its longitude and
f
is its radius (distance to the
z
-axis).
We also call
c
the distance along the ellipsoid to the equator and
\alpha
the angle between
\gamma
and the meridian towards the north pole, see Figure
6
.
We will also use the
z
coordinate of a point in
\gamma
.

Figure 6.
An arc of geodesic (red).

By Clairaut’s relation the quantity
f\sin(\alpha)
is constant along
\gamma
.
In particular the radius
f_{max}
of the highest point on
\gamma
corresponds to
\alpha=\frac{\pi}{2}
, hence
f_{max}=\sigma_{0}
.
Note that
\gamma
starts from the equator, goes north for some time during which
f
decreases from 1 to
f_{max}
.
Let
u_{max}
be the longitude of the highest point.
By symmetry, the first-return longitude coordinate on the equator is then twice
u_{max}
.

To compute
u_{max}
we will compute the value of
\displaystyle\frac{du}{dr}
and integrate it from
1
to
f_{max}
.
Looking at Figure
6
, we start with the relation

\frac{f\,du}{dc}=\tan(\alpha)
.
Clairaut’s relation yields
\sin(\alpha)=\frac{\sigma_{0}}{f}
, so we get

(3)

\frac{f^{2}\,du^{2}}{dc^{2}}=\tan^{2}(\alpha)=\frac{\sin^{2}(\alpha)}{1-\sin^{2}(\alpha)}=\frac{\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{f^{2}}}{1-\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{f^{2}}}=\frac{\sigma_{0}^{2}}{f^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2}}.

Since we are on
\mathbb{E}_{b}
we have
\displaystyle f^{2}+\frac{z^{2}}{b^{2}}=1
, so that
\frac{z^{2}}{b^{2}}=1-f^{2}
.
Differentiating we get
\frac{z}{b^{2}}dz=-f\,df
, and so

dz^{2}=\frac{b^{4}f^{2}}{z^{2}}df^{2}=\frac{b^{2}f^{2}}{1-f^{2}}df^{2}.

By Pythagoras’ theorem we have

dc^{2}=dz^{2}+df^{2}=\left(1+\frac{b^{2}f^{2}}{1-f^{2}}\right)df^{2}=\frac{1+(b^{2}-1)f^{2}}{1-f^{2}}df^{2}

Putting it back into (
3
) we get

\frac{du^{2}}{df^{2}}=\frac{(1+(b^{2}-1)f^{2})\sigma_{0}^{2}}{f^{2}(1-f^{2})(f^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})}.

Integrating this we obtain

(4)

u_{max}=\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{1}\sqrt{\frac{(1+(b^{2}-1)f^{2})\sigma_{0}^{2}}{f^{2}(1-f^{2})(f^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})}}df=\sigma_{0}\int_{\sigma_{0}}^{1}\frac{1}{f}\sqrt{\frac{(1+(b^{2}-1)f^{2})}{(1-f^{2})(f^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})}}df.

The integrand diverges at
\sigma_{0}
and
1
, but it is integrable at both points.

Set
a=b^{2}-1
.
We perform the change of variable
v^{2}=\frac{(f^{2}-\sigma_{0}^{2})a}{1-\sigma_{0}^{2}}
, then

u_{max}=\int_{0}^{\sqrt{a}}a\sigma_{0}\sqrt{\frac{1+(1-\sigma_{0}^{2})v^{2}+a\sigma_{0}^{2}}{(a-v^{2})((1-\sigma_{0}^{2})v^{2}+a\sigma_{0}^{2})^{2}}}du.

We make a second change of variable
v=\sqrt{a}\cos t
to obtain

u_{max}=\int_{0}^{\pi/2}a\sigma_{0}\frac{\sqrt{1+a\sigma_{0}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{0}^{2})a\cos^{2}t}}{a\sigma_{0}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{0}^{2})a\cos^{2}t}dt.

We can now compute the limit when
\sigma_{0}
tends to one, that, by the dominated convergence theorem, is equal to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\left(\lim_{\sigma_{0}\to 1}a\sigma_{0}\frac{\sqrt{1+a\sigma_{0}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{0}^{2})a\cos^{2}t}}{a\sigma_{0}^{2}+(1-\sigma_{0}^{2})a\cos^{2}t}\right)dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\pi/2}\sqrt{1+a}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{2}b.

Therefore,
u_{max}
is bounded away from
\pi
if and only if
b<2
, so that
\mathbb{E}_{b}
has no asymptotic figure-eight geodesic if and only if
b<2
.
∎

5.5.
Proof of the second part of Theorem
1.1

Fix
\delta
in
(0,1]
.
We now explain how to construct a sphere of revolution
S_{\delta}
whose curvature is
\delta
-pinched.
Roughly speaking it is obtained by flattening a round sphere near the equator into a piece of an ellipsoid of revolution with the desired curvature.
The construction works for every
\delta\leq 1
, but it is interesting only for
\delta<1/4
in which case one finds two geodesics who link positively and for
\delta=1/4
in which case the self-linking of the equator vanishes.

As in Section
4.1
we describe
S_{\delta}
with a radius function
f
, and an auxiliary height function
g
.
The construction is depicted on Figure
7
.
For convenience we will use an extra function
b
that will parametrize the curvature.
Fix
\varepsilon>0
small enough.
For
s\in[-\pi,\pi]
we define

f(s)=b(s)\cos(s),\quad\mathrm{and}\quad g(s)=\sin(s),

where
b
is a
C^{\infty}
bump function that satisfies

b(s)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}&\text{if }s\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]\\
1&\text{if }s\in[-1,-2\varepsilon]\cup[2\varepsilon,1],\\
\end{cases}

and
b
interpolates smoothly between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}
, and
1
on
[-2\varepsilon,-\varepsilon]\cup[\varepsilon,2\varepsilon]
.
Denote by
S_{\delta}
the sphere of revolution induced by the pair
(f,g)
.
It is made by connecting an annular neighborhood of the equator of
\mathbb{E}_{\frac{1}{\sqrt{\delta}}}
with polar caps cut from the round sphere
\mathbb{E}_{1}
.
Notice that, at a point of
S_{\delta}
corresponding to a parameter
s
, the sphere is osculated by an ellipsoid of the form
\mathbb{E}_{b(s)}
, so that its curvature is everywhere between
\delta
and
1
, and so it is
\delta
-pinched.

We now explain why the geodesic on
S_{\delta}
is not left-handed for
\delta<1/4
.
Indeed, in this case one has
b(s)>2
for
s\in[-\varepsilon,\varepsilon]
.
By Equation (
4
), there exists a figure-eight closed geodesic
\gamma
in the
\varepsilon
-neighborhood of the equator on
S_{\delta}
.
By Proposition
5.2
, the oriented lift of
\gamma
in
\mathrm{T}^{1}S_{\delta}
has zero linking with the lift of the equator.
Therefore the geodesic flow on
S_{\delta}
is not left-handed.

Figure 7.
The surface
S_{\delta}
and the geodesic figure-eight (green) whose lift has zero linking with the equator (orange).

Finally, we consider the case
\delta=1/4
.
The sphere
S_{1/4}
has a neighborhood of its equator which is a neighborhood of the equator of
\mathbb{E}_{1/2}
.
The computation at the end of the proof of Proposition
1.3
ensures that there is an asymptotic figure-eight geodesic in this neighborhood, hence the geodesic flow is not left-handed.

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

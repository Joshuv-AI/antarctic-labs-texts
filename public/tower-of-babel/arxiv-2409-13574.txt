On the maximal unramified pro-2-extension of ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -extension of certain real biquadratic fields
Mohamed Mahmoud Chems-Eddin

Abstract
. For any positive integer n n , we show that there exists a real number field k k (resp. k ′ k^{\prime} ) of degree 2 n + 2 2^{n+2} whose 2 2 -class group is isomorphic to ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ × ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} such that the Galois group of the maximal unramified extension of k k (resp. k ′ k^{\prime} ) over k k (resp. k ′ k^{\prime} ) is abelian (resp. non abelian, more precisely isomorphic to Q 8 Q_{8} or D 8 D_{8} , the quaternion and the dihedral group of order 8 8 respectively). In fact, we construct the first examples in the literature of families of real biquadratic fields for which the layers of the cyclotomic ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} -extension satisfy the previous conditions and whose unramified abelian 2 2 -Iwasawa modules are isomorphic to ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ × ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ \mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z} ; hence these fields satisfy Greenberg’s conjecture.

is such that

(E_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{sq})}:E_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{sq})}\cap N_{F/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{sq})}(F))=2^{e}
.
As by
[
3
, Théorème 3.3 et Théorème 3.4]

e=0
or
1
according to whether
s\equiv 7\pmod{8}
or not, we have
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(F))=2
. This completes the proof.

∎

In the proof of the following lemma, we use the properties of
\left(\frac{\cdot,\,\cdot}{\mathfrak{p}}\right)
, the norm residue symbol. For more details concerning these properties,
we refer the reader to

[
19
, Chapter II, Theorem 3.1.3]
or

[
18

Lemma 3.2
.

The
2
-class group of
F_{1}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pq},\sqrt{ps},\sqrt{2})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Put
L=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{sq},\sqrt{2})
. Notice that
F_{1}=L(\sqrt{ps})
. Pursuant to
[
2
, p. 19]
and
[
11
, Corollaries 1 and 2]
, we have
E_{L}=\left\langle-1,\varepsilon_{2},\varepsilon_{qs},\sqrt{\varepsilon_{2qs}}\text{ or }\sqrt{\varepsilon_{qs}\varepsilon_{2qs}}\right\rangle
, according to whether
s\equiv 3\pmod{8}
or not. Note that
h_{2}(L)=1
(cf.
[
14
, Corollary 21.4]
). Let us assume that
s\equiv 3\pmod{8}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.3
, the rank of the
2
-class group of
F_{1}
is
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(F_{1}))=t-1-e
, where
{e}
is defined by
(E_{L}:E_{L}\cap N_{F_{1}/L}(F_{1}))=2^{e}
and
t=4
is the number of ramified primes in
F_{1}/L
. Thus, we have
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(F_{1}))=3-e
.
Let
\mathfrak{p}_{k}
be a prime ideal of
k
above
p
, where
k
is a subfield of
L
. Notice that
p
decomposes in
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{qs})
and there are exactly
2
prime ideals
of
L
laying above
p
. Using the properties of norm residue symbols, we obtain:

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{2},\,ps}{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\right)=\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{2},\,p}{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N_{{L}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{qs})}(\varepsilon_{2}),\,p}{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{qs})}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1,\,p}{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{qs})}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1,\,p}{p}\right)=1,

and in a similar way
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{qs},\,ps}{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\right)=\left(\frac{-1,\,ps}{\mathfrak{p}_{L}}\right)=1
.
It follows that
e\leq 1
and so
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(F_{1}))\geq 2
. According to Lemma
3.1
, the
2
-class group of
K
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
and since
F_{1}/K
is an unramified quadratic extension, it follows that the
2
-class group of
F_{1}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(cf. Remark
2.2
).
We proceed similarly for the case
s\equiv 7\pmod{8}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.3
.

Put
A(K):=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2q},\sqrt{s},\sqrt{p})
and
C(K):=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2p},\sqrt{2s},\sqrt{q})
.
Then, the
2
-class group of
A(K)

(
resp.
C(K)
)
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}

(
resp. is cyclic
)
.

Proof.

Put
k=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2q},\sqrt{ps})
. Notice that
ps\equiv 3\pmod{4}
. As
2
is totally ramified in
k
and according to
[
37
, Theorem 4.4]

k
admits an unramified extension of degree
8
of the form
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{2},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{p},\sqrt{s},\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is defined in
[
37
, Theorem 4.4]
, the field
A(K)
admits a biquadratic unramified extension. So, by class field theory, we have
\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(A(K)))\geq 2
. Therefore, since
A(K)/K
is unramified quadratic extension such that the
2
-class group of
K
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(cf. Lemma
3.1
), we deduce that
\mathbf{C}l_{2}(A(K))\simeq\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
(cf. Remark
2.2
). Since
F_{1}/K
is unramified, we have
\mathbf{C}l_{2}(C(K))
is cyclic (cf. Lemma
3.2
and Remark
2.2
).
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The
2
-class group of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s})
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z},

where
m=\frac{1}{2}h_{2}(pqs)
.

Proof.

Put
L^{\prime}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pq},\sqrt{s})
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\left(\frac{s}{q}\right)=-1
(if not we exchange the places of
q
and
s
).
Notice that the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})
is odd (cf. Remark
2.6
). So by the ambiguous class number formula

\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(L^{\prime}))=3-1-e=2-e
, where
{e}
is such that
(E_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}:E_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}\cap N_{L^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}(L^{\prime}))=2^{e}
.
According to
[
37
, Proposition 2.6]
, we have
\varepsilon_{s}=2u^{2}
for some
u\in\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})
. Thus,
\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{s},\,pq}{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}}\right)=\left(\frac{2u^{2},\,p}{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}}\right)=\left(\frac{2,\,p}{\mathfrak{p}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{s})}}\right)=\left(\frac{2}{p}\right)=-1
.
Therefore,
e\geq 1
, but from the fact that the class number of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pq},\sqrt{s})
is even (cf.
[
14
, Corollary 21.4]
), we deduce that

\mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{C}l_{2}(L^{\prime}))=1
. Thus,
\mathbf{C}l_{2}(L^{\prime})
is a cyclic group. As
S/L^{\prime}
, where
S=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s})
, is a quadratic unramified extension, it follows, by class field theory, that
\mathbf{C}l_{2}(S)
is cyclic and
h_{2}(S)=\frac{1}{2}h_{2}(L^{\prime})
.
Therefore, by Lemma
2.5
and Remark
2.6
, we have :

\displaystyle h_{2}(L^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}q(L^{\prime})h_{2}(pq)h_{2}(s)h_{2}(pqs)=\frac{1}{4}q(L^{\prime})\cdot 2\cdot 1\cdot h_{2}(pqs)=\frac{1}{2}q(L^{\prime})\cdot h_{2}(pqs).

We note that according to
[
10
, Lemma 2.3 (2)(i)]
,
\varepsilon_{pq}=a+b\sqrt{pq}
for some integers
a
and
b
such that
(a\pm 1)
is not a square in
\mathbb{N}
. So by

[
6
, Proposition 3.2]
, we have
\{\varepsilon_{s},\varepsilon_{pq},\sqrt{\nu}\}
is a fundamental system of units of
L^{\prime}
, where
\nu={\varepsilon_{pqs}}
,

{\varepsilon_{s}\varepsilon_{pq}}
or
{\varepsilon_{s}\varepsilon_{pq}\varepsilon_{pqs}}
. It follows that
q(L^{\prime})=2
. Therefore,
h_{2}(S)=\frac{1}{2}\cdot(\frac{1}{2}q(L^{\prime})\cdot h_{2}(pqs))=\frac{1}{2}h_{2}(pqs).
So the result.
∎

The following corollary follows from the above proof.

Corollary 3.5
.

The
2
-class groups of
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{pq},\sqrt{s})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{ps},\sqrt{q})
are cyclic of order
h_{2}(pqs)
.

The following proposition gives a family of real triquadratic number fields of the form
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s})
, where
p
,
q
and
s

whose Iwasawa module is cyclic non trivial. To our knowledge, the only previously known results about the Iwasawa module of such fields are in

[
33

, which provide a family of these fields whose Iwasawa module is trivial.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
C_{n}(F)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s},2\cos({2\pi}/{2^{n+2}}))

n
is a positive integer. Then
2
-class group of
C_{n}(F)

Proof.

Recall that by Lemma
3.1
, the
2
-class group of
K

\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. Thus,
C_{1}(F)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s},\sqrt{2})
is the Hilbert
2
-class field of
K
. In fact,
C_{1}(F)/K

is an unramified abelian extension of degree
4
. So according to the theoretic properties in Section
2
,
the
2
-class group of
C_{1}(F)
is cyclic. Note that the extension
C_{1}(F)/C(F)

∎

(

)
.

3.4
, the

2.2

and

-class number of

, then the Hilbert
2
-class field tower of
F_{1}
terminates at the first layer which is
F^{(1)}(\sqrt{2})
. Therefore, the
2
-class number of
C_{1}(F)=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p},\sqrt{q},\sqrt{s},\sqrt{2})

2
. So for all

2
(cf. Lemma
2.4
).

1
that for all

,
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathcal{L}(F_{n})/F_{n})

-class number of
C(K)
equal
8
. Then

-class numbers of
A(F)
and
B(F)
(resp.
A_{1}(F)
and
B_{1}(F)
) are equal to
4
. It follows that for all
n\geq 1

2
-class number of

4
(cf. Lemma
2.4
). So by Figure
2
, for all
n\geq 0
,
\mathrm{Gal}(\mathcal{L}(F_{n})/F_{n})
is not abelian, more precisely, quaternion or dihedral of order
8
.

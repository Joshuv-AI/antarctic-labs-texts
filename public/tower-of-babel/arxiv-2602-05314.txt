Generalized nearby cycles via relative and logarithmic 𝒟 \mathscr{D} -modules
Lei Wu

Abstract
. For a regular map F F from a complex smooth affine variety X X to 𝔸 ℂ r \mathbb{A}^{r}_{\mathbb{C}} , we construct generalized nearby-cycle modules of a regular holonomic 𝒟 X \mathscr{D}_{X} -modules ℳ \mathcal{M} along log strata with the log structure induced by the graph of F F , whose relative supports are infinite unions of translated linear subvarieties of ℂ r \mathbb{C}^{r} determined by the zero loci of Bernstein-Sato ideals along monoid ideals. For a fixed log stratum, the nearby-cycle module corresponds to the Sabbah specialization complex of DR ​ ( ℳ ) \textup{DR}(\mathcal{M}) under the relative regular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence of Fiorot-Fernandes-Sabbah, which generalizes the classical comparison theorem of Kashiwara-Malgrange for Deligne’s nearby cycles. As an application, when ℳ = 𝒪 X \mathcal{M}=\mathscr{O}_{X} , we give a topological interpretation of the zero loci of Bernstein-Sato ideals of F F along monoid ideals under the exponential map, which answers a question of Budur-Shi-Zuo.

1.
Introduction

Given a holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-module on a complex manifold, Kashiwara
[
Kas83
]
and Malgrange
[
Mal83
]
constructed a filtration of the module along the hypersurface of a given holomorphic function, now standardly known as the Kashiwara-Malgrange
V
-filtration, by employing a
\mathscr{D}
-module version of the deformation to the normal cone. By passing to the associated graded pieces,
they obtained the nearby and vanishing cycles of holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules along the hypersurface. They further established a comparison theorem between Deligne’s topological nearby and vanishing cycles for perverse sheaves
[
Del06
]
and their
\mathscr{D}
-module counterparts under the classical Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.
Later, Beilinson and Bernstein
[
Bei06
,
BB93
]
gave an alternative construction for both frameworks using algebraic relative
\mathscr{D}
-modules over one dimensional base spaces and Bernstein-Sato polynomials (see also
[
BG12
]
). Meanwhile, for a finite union of holomorphic functions, Sabbah
[
Sab90
]
introduced Alexander complexes (also termed Sabbah specialization complexes) by considering normal deformations along smooth complete intersections and their toric birational modifications, thereby generalizing Deligne’s nearby cycles.

The purpose of this paper is to construct generalized nearby-cycle modules of regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules in the algebraic setting, such that they correspond to the Sabbah specialization complexes under the relative regular Riemann-Hilbert correspondence of Fiorot-Fernandes-Sabbah
[
FFS23
]
, drawing inspiration from the work of Sabbah and Beilinson-Bernstein. Our primary technical framework rests on
the theory of relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules developed in

[
FS13
,
FF18
,
FS19
,
FFS20
,
FFS23
]
, and the theory of logarithmic
\mathscr{D}
-modules. We emphasize that we work in the algebraic category primarily to simplify notation; the local analytic case can be treated analogously by the same methods.

1.1.
Log structures and Bernstein-Sato ideals

Let
F=(f_{1},f_{2},\dots,f_{r})\colon X\to\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{r}=\textup{Spec }\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}^{r}]
be a morphism with
X=\textup{Spec }S
an irreducible smooth affine algebraic variety over
\mathbb{C}
and let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module with
\mathscr{D}_{X}
the ring of (algebraic) differential operators on
X
. Then we consider the log structure on
Z=X\times\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
given by the prelog structure

\alpha\colon\mathbb{N}^{r}\hookrightarrow S[\mathbb{N}^{r}]\simeq S[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}].

We call it the log structure induced by the graph of
F
.

On the topological side, using the log structure, we give an alternative description of Sabbah specialization complexes. More precisely, for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
a monoid ideal and for
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we have
\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})
, the
Sabbah specialization complex
of a constructible complex
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
along
Z_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}
, where
Z_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}\subseteq Z
is the locally closed subscheme defined by
\alpha(K_{\mathbf{m}})
and
K_{\mathbf{m}}
is the localization of
K
at
\mathbf{m}
(cf.
[
Ogu18
, I.1.4]
); see §
5.6
for details.

On the
\mathscr{D}
-module side, we consider a log
\mathscr{D}
-module lattice (see §
2.1
for the definition)

\mathcal{N}_{0}\coloneqq\mathscr{D}_{X}[s_{1},\dots,s_{r}]\mathcal{H}_{0}\cdot F^{\bf s}\subseteq\mathcal{M}[1/f,s_{1},\dots,s_{r}]F^{\bf s},

where we write
F^{\bf s}=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}^{s_{i}}
and
f=\prod_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}
,
and
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{M}[1/f]
is a finitely generated
S
-module such that
\mathscr{D}_{X}[1/f]\cdot\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathcal{M}[1/f]
. Then, we give a logarithmic interpretation of Bernstein-Sato ideals (or polynomials) by defining

B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0})\coloneqq{\textup{Ann}}_{\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{r}]}\frac{\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{m}})}{\alpha(K)\cdot\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{m}})}.

See §
6
for more details.
Even when
\mathcal{M}
is irregular,
B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0})
always contains a distinguished element in the form of products of linear polynomials (see Lemma
6.2
), based on a fundamental result of Sabbah
[
Sab87
]
. Taking
\mathcal{N}_{F}=\mathscr{D}_{X}[s_{1},\dots,s_{r}]F^{\bf s}
, we get the
Bernstein-Sato ideals
of
F
along
K_{\mathbf{m}}
,

B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}_{F}\coloneqq B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{F})\subseteq\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{r}].

When
\mathbf{m}=e\coloneqq(0,0,\dots,0)
, the unit in
\mathbb{N}^{r}
, since
K_{e}=K
, we have
B^{K_{e}}_{F}=B^{K}_{F}
. In particular, when
K
is the monoid ideal generated by
(1,1,\dots,1)\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, we have
B_{F}\coloneqq B_{F}^{(1,1,\dots,1)}
, the classical Bernstein-Sato ideal of
F
. More generally,
when
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
is generated by
\mathbf{m}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{m}_{p}
, we have

B_{F}^{K}=B_{F}^{\mathbf{m}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{m}_{p}}

where the later is introduced and studied by Budur
[
Bud15
, §4]
. The Bernstein-Sato ideal
B_{F}
has been widely studied from both theoretical and computational perspectives in literature, to just list a few
[
May97
,
BMM00
,
Bah05
,
CNL11
,
Mai23
,
Bat20
,
OT99
,
UCJ04
,
BO10
,
LLM24
]
.

The logarithmic interpretation of Bernstein-Sato ideals enables us to prove that
B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0})\subseteq\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\dots,s_{r}]
is a non-zero proper ideal under some geometric condition. In particular, we obtain that if
(f_{1}=f_{2}=\cdots=f_{r}=0)\subseteq X
is not empty, then
B_{F}^{K}
is a non-zero proper ideal for every monoid ideal
K\subsetneq\mathbb{N}^{r}
. See Theorem
6.3
and Proposition
6.4
for details.

Our first main result is a comparison between the zero loci of Bernstein-Sato ideals
Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))
and the relative supports of the Sabbah specialization complexes
\textup{supp}\big(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M}))\big)
, where
\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M})
denotes the de Rham complex of
\mathcal{M}
.

Theorem A
.

With notation as above, let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. Then, for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\big)=\textup{supp}\big(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M}))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of translated subtori, where Exp
:\mathbb{C}^{r}\to(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}
is the universal covering map.
Furthermore, if
\mathcal{M}
underlines a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge module, then

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\big)=\textup{supp}\big(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M}))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of torsion translated subtori.

Taking
\mathcal{N}_{0}=\mathcal{N}_{F}
, we immediately obtain:

Corollary B
.

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}_{F})\big)=\textup{supp}\big(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathbb{C}_{X})\big).

Motivated by a classical theorem of Kashiwara and Malgrange (cf.
[
BvdVWZ21a
, Thm.1.4.1(i)]
), Budur
[
Bud15
]
raised a question regarding how to topologically interpret zero loci of Bernstein-Sato ideals of
F
(see also
[
BSZ25
, Remark 6.8.(2)]
). While this question was partially addressed by the main results in
[
BvdVWZ21a
,
BvdVWZ21b
]
,
Corollary
B
gives a complete answer.

In the context of multivariable monodromy zeta functions, Sabbah
[
Sab90
, 0.2]
established that the relative support of the Sabbah specialization complex of
\mathbb{C}_{X}
is contained in a finite union of translated subtori. Subsequently, Budur and Wang
[
BW17
]
utilized cohomology jumping loci of rank-one local systems to prove that the relative supports of the germs of the Sabbah specialization of
\mathbb{C}_{X}
along

F
are finite unions of torsion translated subtorus.
Their approach, however, relies on some approximation theorem to reduce the problem to the case where all
f_{i}
are polynomials defined over
\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
.
In contrast, Theorem A and its proof offer a distinct approach under more general assumptions. Our new perspective reveals that studying Bernstein–Sato ideals from a log-
\mathscr{D}
-module standpoint is both natural and essential.

1.2.
Generalized nearby-cycle modules

To prove Theorem
A
, we construct in §
5.2
the
relative minimal extensions
of regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules (and more generally relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules) along divisors supported in the log boundary by using graph embeddings and the log structure induced by the graphs, which naturally generalize the
Deligne-Goresky-MacPherson minimal extensions for perverse sheaves (cf.
[
HTT08
, §8.2.1]
) and the
j_{!}
-extension of Beilinson and Bernstein along a regular function (cf.
[
BB93
, §4.1]
and
[
Gin86
, §3.8]
). Leveraging these minimal extension, we further construct
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}
, the
generalized nearby-cycle module
of the lattice
\mathcal{N}_{0}
along
K_{\mathbf{m}}
, which is shown to be a relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X^{\textup{an}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}/\mathbb{C}^{r}}
-module. See §
5.4
and Definition
5.26
for details. These constructions depend on two main ingredients:

(1)

a logarithmic characteristic cycle formula for lattices of relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules in Theorem
5.1
,

(2)

a logarithmic Artin-Rees theorem developed in §
5.3
.

The logarithmic characteristic cycle formula is a relative generalization of
[
Gin89
, Thm.A1.2]
. Following the approach of Briançon-Maisonobe-Merle
[
BMM02
]
, we derive the characteristic cycle formula as a direct consequence of Sabbah’s relative characteristic variety formula (Theorem A.1 in Appendix). This result allows us to control the logarithmic characteristic varieties of lattices, and consequently the relative characteristic varieties of lattices of graph-embedding type (Definition
2.6
), under specialization along divisorial log boundaries (Theorem
5.6
). Such control is crucial for performing induction along log strata.

Furthermore, while it is well known that the
\mathscr{D}
-module analogue of the Artin-Rees Lemma from commutative algebra does not hold, we observe that ideals in the ring of logarithmic differential operators generated by

\alpha(K)
for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
are two-sided (Lemma
2.2
). This observation ensures that the Artin–Rees property remains valid for log
\mathscr{D}
-modules with respect to such ideals
(Theorem
5.14
). We apply this logarithmic Artin–Rees theorem to prove that the generalized
nearby cycles
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}
constitute a module rather than merely a complex (Theorem
5.17
).

More explicitly, the generalized nearby cycles of
\mathcal{N}_{0}
along
K
is given by the following projective limit:

(1)

\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0}\simeq\lim_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\longleftarrow}}{{j}}}\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}_{0}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{p}\widetilde{\alpha(v_{i})^{j}\mathcal{N}_{0}}}

as
j\to+\infty
, where
\sim
denotes the analytic sheafification functor (see §
3.2
), and
v_{1},\dots,v_{p}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
generate
K
. Motivated by the double-limit construction in
[
Bei06
]
, we establish a double limit giving the largest generalized nearby cycles:

(2)

\Psi_{K}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E)\simeq\lim_{\longleftrightarrow}\frac{\widetilde{\alpha(\mathbf{n})^{-k}\mathcal{N}_{0}}}{\sum_{i=1}^{p}\widetilde{\alpha(v_{i})^{j}\mathcal{N}_{0}}},

as
\alpha(\mathbf{n})^{-k}\cdot\mathcal{N}_{0}\to\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E)
for any
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}_{>0}^{r}
, where
E\subseteq Z
is the divisor defined by
(t_{1}\cdots t_{r}=0)
.
For
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}
and
\Psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{M})\coloneqq\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E)
in general, one can first localize the log structure at
\mathbf{m}
, and then get similar limits. See Proposition
5.27
and Remark
5.19
.

Consequently, under the relative regular Riemann–Hilbert correspondence, we establish a comparison theorem (Theorem
5.25
)

(3)

\pi_{*}^{\mathscr{G}_{r}}\textup{DR}_{X^{\textup{an}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}/\mathbb{C}^{r}}\big(\Psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{M})\big)\simeq\widetilde{\psi}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}\big(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M})\big),

which generalizes the comparison of Kashiwara
[
Kas83
]
and Malgrange
[
Mal83
]
for Deligne nearby cycles. Here,
\mathscr{G}_{r}\simeq\mathbb{Z}^{r}
denotes the free abelian group generated by
t_{1},\dots,t_{r}
associated with the log structure, and
\pi_{*}^{\mathscr{G}_{r}}
is the equivariant direct image functor (cf.
[
BL94
, Part I.0.3.]
). It is worth noting that our construction of generalized nearby cycles extends to relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}
-modules of graph-embedding type in a more general setting (see §
5.2
and §
5.4
).

The double-limit in Eq.(
2
) indicates that algebraic relative
\mathscr{D}
-modules can be utilized to locally approximate the analytic relative
\mathscr{D}
-module
\Psi_{K}(\mathcal{M})
. Furthermore, upon taking the equivariant direct image, the comparison in
Eq.(
3
) ensures that
the
\mathscr{D}_{X^{\textup{an}}\times(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}/(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}}
-module
\pi_{*}^{\mathscr{G}_{r}}\big(\Psi_{K}(\mathcal{M})\big)
is
dR
-algebraic (Definition
3.1
). This algebraicity allows us to invoke an Ax–Lindemann-type criterion developed by Budur and Wang
(Lemma
5.32
). Combining these ingredients, we arrive at our second main result:

Theorem C
.

With notation as above, let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. Then, for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we have:

(1)

the
\mathscr{G}_{r}
-invariant subset
\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is an infinite (countable) union of translated linear subvarieties such that

\textup{supp}(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M})))=\textup{Exp}\big(\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of translated subtori,

(2)

for every
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{n}}))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is an infinite (countable) union of translated linear subvarieties,

(3)

if
\mathscr{N}
is a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X^{\textup{an}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}/\mathbb{C}^{r}}
-submodule of
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{n}})
for
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, then
\textup{supp}(\mathscr{N})\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is a countable union of translated linear subvarieties.

The third part of the aforementioned theorem asserts that every irreducible components of the relative support of
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{n}})
, including any “embedded" component, is a translated linear subvariety of
\mathbb{C}^{r}
. Theorem
C
thus implies that the relative supports of generalized nearby cycles exhibit desirable geometric linearity, echoing the behavior of jumping indices in the
\mathbb{C}
-indexed Kashiwara-Malgrange
V
-filtrations. In cases where
\mathcal{M}
underlies a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge modules (for instance
\mathcal{M}=\mathscr{O}_{X}
), Theorem
C
can be further refined as follows:

Theorem D
.

If
\mathcal{M}
is a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module underlying a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge module, then for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
we have:

(1)

the
\mathscr{G}_{r}
-invariant subset
\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is an infinite (countable) union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties such that

\textup{supp}(\psi_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\textup{DR}(\mathcal{M})))=\textup{Exp}\big(\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of torsion translated subtori,

(2)

for every
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{n}}))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is an infinite (countable) union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties,

(3)

if
\mathscr{N}
is a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X^{\textup{an}}\times\mathbb{C}^{r}/\mathbb{C}^{r}}
-submodule of
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K_{\mathbf{m}}}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E_{\mathbf{n}})
for
\mathbf{n}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, then
\textup{supp}(\mathscr{N})\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is a countable union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties.

The strategy for proving the above theorem relies on finding
\mathbb{Q}
-points within each irreducible component of the relative support of the nearby-cycle module, a task facilitated by Theorem
C
. We first use the
\mathbb{Q}
-specializability of
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge modules (see §
4
) to generate such
\mathbb{Q}
-points in the case where the log boundary is a divisor. Subsequently, we utilize the fact that the property of being
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge modules is stable under the nearby cycles functor along a hypersurfaces, allowing for induction across different log strata.
This inductive process depends on two technical results, Theorem
5.6
and Theorem
5.33
, both of which are derived from the log characteristic cycle formula in Theorem
5.1
.

1.3.
Geometric Budur-type conjecture

Based on computational examples, Budur conjectured that for
\mathbf{m}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{m}_{p}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
the ideal
B_{F}^{\mathbf{m}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{m}_{p}}
is generated by products of linear polynomials of type
c_{1}s_{1}+\cdots+c_{r}s_{r}+c_{0}
with
c_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
and
c_{0}>0
(cf.
[
Bud15
]
and
[
BSZ25
, Remark.6.8.(1)]
).
Given a monoid ideal
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
, Eq.(
1
) yields a surjection

\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{0}}{\widetilde{K\cdot\mathcal{N}_{0}}}

and specifically,
\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{F}\twoheadrightarrow\frac{\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}_{F}}{\widetilde{K\cdot\mathcal{N}_{F}}}.
Since
\sim
-functor is faithfully flat, it follows from Eq.(
9
) that

Z(B^{K}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\subseteq\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0}).

We have shown that
\textup{supp}(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0})
is a union of translated linear subvarieties (or a union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties if
\mathcal{M}
underlies a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge module).
In particular,
Z(B^{K}_{F})
is contained in a union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties, thereby providing geometric evidence of Burdur’s conjecture. In our setting, it is then natural to ask whether a Budur-type conjecture holds for
\mathcal{N}_{0}
more generally, at least in a geometric sense.

Conjecture E

(Geometric Budur-type Conjecture)
.

With notation as above, let
\mathcal{M}
be a regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X}
-module. For
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,

Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}

is a finite union of translated linear subvarieties. Moreover, if
\mathcal{M}
underlies a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge module, then
Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is a finite union of
\mathbb{Q}
-translated linear subvarieties.

Lastly, we outline the structure of the paper. Section
2
provides the necessary preliminaries on the theory of log
\mathscr{D}
-modules in the normal crossing case. In Section
3
, we discuss algebraic and analytic relative constructible sheaves and relative
\mathscr{D}
-modules. Section
4
is devoted to the
\mathbb{Q}
-specializability of
\mathscr{D}
-modules. Section
5
constitutes the core of the paper, where we construct relative minimal extensions and generalized nearby cycles modules. In Section
6
, we provide a logarithmic interpretation of Bernstein–Sato ideals and establish our main results. Finally, the Appendix utilizes Sabbah’s relative characteristic variety formula to derive the logarithmic characteristic cycle formula.

2.
Logarithmic
\mathscr{D}
-modules in the normal crossing case

2.1.
Basic set-up

We recall terminology from the theory of logarithmic
\mathscr{D}
-modules with the log structure given by a normal crossing divisor (see
[
WZ21
]
and also
[
HW26
]
for the theory in general).

The standard (smooth) log structure on
\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{r}=\textup{Spec }\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}^{r}]\simeq\textup{Spec }\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]
is given by the prelog structure

\mathbb{N}^{r}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}[\mathbb{N}^{r}]\simeq\mathbb{C}[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]

where
\mathbb{N}=\{0,1,2,\dots\}
is the set of all natural numbers.
Pulling back the standard log structure to

Z=X\times\mathbb{A}_{\mathbb{C}}^{r}
with
X=\textup{Spec }S
an irreducible smooth affine complex variety of dimension
n
, we get a log structure on
Z=\textup{Spec }S[\mathbb{N}^{r}]
given by

\alpha\colon\mathbb{N}^{r}\hookrightarrow S[\mathbb{N}^{r}]\simeq S[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}].

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
be a monoid ideal. The ideal
K
defines an idealized log subscheme
Z_{K}=\textup{Spec }\frac{S[\mathbb{N}^{r}]}{\alpha(K)\cdot S[\mathbb{N}^{r}]}\subseteq Z
(see
[
Ogu18
, III.1.3]
).

Let
E=(\prod_{i=1}^{r}t_{i}=0)
be the normal crossing divisor on
Z
. Then the log structure on
Z
is also the log structure given by
E
.
We have
\mathscr{D}_{Z,E}
the ring of logarithmic differential operators with respect to the log structure, i.e.

\mathscr{D}_{Z,E}=\mathscr{D}_{X}[t_{1},\dots,t_{r}]<t_{1}\partial_{1},\dots,t_{r}\partial_{r}>

subject to the relations
[t_{i}\partial_{i},t_{j}]=\delta_{i,j}\cdot t_{i}
(cf.
[

Lemma 2.1
.

If
\mathcal{N}
is a
\mathscr{A}_{Y,D}^{k}
-lattice and if

are two divisors supported in

with

, then we have

\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{N}(-H_{1})}(H_{2})\simeq\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}^{-b_{i}}\cdot\mathcal{N}}{\prod_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}^{a_{i}-b_{i}}\cdot\mathcal{N}}.

Proof.

We have a short exact sequence (SES)

0\to\mathcal{N}(H_{2}-H_{1})\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}(H_{2})\longrightarrow\frac{\mathcal{N}}{\mathcal{N}(-H_{1})}(H_{2})\to 0.

Since
\mathcal{N}
is a lattice, we have

\mathcal{N}(H_{2})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}^{-b_{i}}\cdot\mathcal{N}\textup{ and }\mathcal{N}(H_{2}-H_{1})\simeq\prod_{i=1}^{l}t_{i}^{a_{i}-b_{i}}\cdot\mathcal{N}.

Thus, the proof is done.

Lemma 2.2
.

If

is a monid ideal, then
\mathscr{D}_{Z,E}\cdot\alpha(K)\subseteq\mathscr{D}_{Z,E}

Proof.

For

. By definition, for every

Thus,
\mathscr{D}_{Z,E}\cdot\alpha(K)
is a two-sided ideal.
∎

Meanwhile, the graph embedding

(x,\xi,s,a)\mapsto(x,\xi,G(x),-s,a)

where

Since
\mathcal{M}

\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}

\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{1}(kD)

2.2.
Pure filtrations of Gabber-Kashiwara

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finitely generated
\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
-module. Since
\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
is Auslander regular, by
[
Bjö13
, A.IV 2.11]

satisfying that if

T_{i}\mathcal{M}/T_{i+1}\mathcal{M}
is
i
-pure,
called the
pure filtration of Gabber-Kashiwara
(or
pure filtration
for short).

Lemma 2.8
.

With notation as above, we have

T_{i}\mathcal{M}=\{m\in\mathcal{M}\mid\textup{codim}\big(\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}\cdot m)\big)\geq i\}.

Proof.

This is the logarithmic analogue of
[
Kas03
, Thm.2.19]
. With the help of Lemma
2.4
, the same arguments gives the required statements.
∎

Since characteristic varieties localizes, the above lemma implies that taking the pure filtration of
\mathcal{M}
commutes with taking localization of
\mathcal{M}
as
\mathscr{O}_{Y}
-modules. Using Lemma
2.8
, we also have that
\mathcal{M}
is
j
-pure if and only if
T_{i}\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}
for
i\leq j
and
T_{i}M=0
for
i>j
.
Then, we immediately obtain:

Corollary 2.9
.

Let

0\to\mathcal{M}_{1}\to\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}_{2}\to 0

be a short exact sequence of finitely generated
\mathscr{A}_{Y,D}^{R}
-module. If
\mathcal{M}_{1}
and
\mathcal{M}_{2}
are
k
-pure, then so is
\mathcal{M}
.

Similar to
[
KS13
, Thm.2.23]
, we have the following alternative description of pure filtrations (by the same argument):

Lemma 2.10
.

T_{i}\mathcal{M}=\textup{Ext}^{0}_{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}}(\tau^{\geq i}\textup{Rhom}_{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}}(\mathcal{M},\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}),\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}).

2.3.
Maximal tame pure extensions of lattices

Let
\mathcal{N}
be a pure
\mathscr{A}_{Y,D}^{R}
-lattice with grade number
j(\mathcal{N})=p
. For
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{l}
, we use
D_{\mathbf{m}}
to denote the divisor defined by
\alpha(\mathbf{m})=0
.
We consider the family of all finitely generated
\mathscr{A}_{Y,D}^{k}
-submodules
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{N}(*D_{\mathbf{m}})
such that we have
\mathcal{N}^{\prime}\supseteq\mathcal{N}
and
j(\mathcal{N}^{\prime}/\mathcal{N})\geq p+2
. By
[
Bjö13
, A.IV 2.10]
, this family has a unique maximal element
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{m}}
in it, called the
maximal tame pure extension
of
\mathcal{N}
inside
\mathcal{N}(*D_{\mathbf{m}})
. The lemma bellow is the logarithmic generalization of
[
Mai23
, Prop.15]
(see also
[
BvdVWZ21b
, Lem.4.4.1]
)

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
H
be a divisor supported in
D
. For non-zero
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
,
\mathcal{L}(H)
is the maximal tame pure extension of
\mathcal{N}(H)
inside
\mathcal{N}(H)(*D_{\mathbf{m}})
. Moreover, if
j(\mathcal{N}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{N})=p+1
, then
\mathcal{L}(H)/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L}(H)
is
(p+1)
-pure and the highest dimensional part of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{N})
is
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{L}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L})
.

Proof.

It is clear that
\mathcal{N}(H)
is also
p
-pure.
We use
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
to denote the maximal tame pure extension of
\mathcal{N}(H)
inside
\mathcal{N}(H)(*D_{\mathbf{m}})
. By Lemma
2.1
and Lemma
2.5
we know

\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(-H)/\mathcal{N})=\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\mathcal{N}(H)).

Then, by Lemma
2.4
(1) we know
j(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(-H)/\mathcal{N})\geq p+2
. By maximality, we thus know
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(-H)\subseteq\mathcal{L}
. By symmetry, we also have
\mathcal{L}(H)\subseteq\mathcal{L}^{\prime}
and thus
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}=\mathcal{L}(H)
.
In particular,
j\big(\mathcal{L}(H)/\mathcal{N}(H)\big)\geq p+2
. If
\mathcal{L}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L}
is not
(p+1)
-pure, then there exist
\alpha(\mathbf{m})\mathcal{L}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}\subsetneq\mathcal{L}
such that
j(\mathcal{L}^{\prime\prime}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L})\geq p+2
. Then by
[
Bjö13
, A.IV 2.3]
, we have
j(\mathcal{L}^{\prime}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{N})\geq p+2
and thus
\mathcal{L}^{\prime}\subseteq\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L}
, which is a contradiction. Thus,
\mathcal{L}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L}
is
(p+1)
-pure. Twisting by
H
,
\mathcal{L}(H)/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L}(H)
is also
(p+1)
-pure.

We still need to prove that the highest dimensional part of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{N})
is
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{L}/\alpha(\mathbf{m})\cdot\mathcal{L})
. To simplify notation, we assume
\mathbf{m}=e_{i}
and thus
\alpha(\mathbf{m})=t_{i}
. The general case is similar.
Consider the short exact sequence

0\to t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N}\to 0.

Since
t_{i}\mathcal{L}
is the tame pure extension of
t_{i}\mathcal{N}
,
j(t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{N})\geq p+2
. By Lemma
2.4
, the SES above implies that the highest dimensional part of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{N})
is the same as that of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N})
.
Then we consider the following diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}\mathcal{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}\mathcal{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t_{i}\mathcal{L}/t_{i}\mathcal{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

and the induced SES

0\to\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

By Snake Lemma, we have an exact sequence

t_{i}\mathcal{L}/t_{i}\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{L}/\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Q}\rightarrow 0.

Since
j(\mathcal{Q})\geq p+2
, thanks to Lemma
2.4
again, the highest dimensional part of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{L}/t_{i}\mathcal{L})
is the same as that of
\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{N})
. Since
\mathcal{L}/t_{i}\mathcal{L}
is pure, by
[
Bjö13
, A.IV 4.11 and 3.7]
we are done.
∎

(n+l)
-pure, then we have

Proof.

By Auslander regularity, we have

, we know

We then take

is Cohen-Macaulay (see

, Def.3.3.1]

If

∎

Proposition 3.8
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}

\mathbb{Q}
-specializable) regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{Y}
-module supported in the graph of

\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{M}(*D)

\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}

\mathscr{D}_{Y,D}

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{m}}

\mathcal{N}

\mathcal{N}

\mathscr{A}^{l}_{X}

(2)

the highest dimensional part of

Since

\mathcal{N}

(n+l)

\mathscr{D}_{Y,D}

By Kashiwara equivalence, we have

\mathscr{D}_{X}

2.1

2.8
,

5.

In this section, we first construct the relative minimal extensions of lattices of GE-type. Then, using minimal extensions, we construct the generalized nearby cycles.

5.1.
Geometry of relative supports

The following is the relative generalization of Ginsburg’s result
[

, Thm.A1.2]
(see also

, Thm.1.6]

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{N}
be an
\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
-lattice such that
\mathcal{N}(*D)
is a relative regular holonomic

-module. Then, we have

\textup{CC}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N})=\overline{\textup{CC}}^{\log}(\mathcal{N}|_{U})

where we write

Corollary 5.2
.

is an

-lattice such that

is a relative regular holonomic

Proof.

We set

Then we have

and

are
\mathscr{A}^{k}_{Y,D}
-lattices for every

, since

, the dimension of

2.8
we know

of grade number

.
∎

If
\mathcal{N}
is an

-lattice such that

5.3.
The Artin-Rees Theorem for

For

a monoid ideal, we define

Lemma 5.13
.

\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
is a graded noetherian ring.

Proof.

Since
\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{i}\cdot\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}=\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}\cdot\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{i}
for each

,

is a graded ring.
F^{\dagger}_{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
induces filtrations on
\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{i}\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}

F^{\dagger}_{\bullet}\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
such that

\textup{gr}^{\dagger}_{\bullet}\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}\simeq\textup{gr}^{\dagger}_{\bullet}\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}[\alpha(\mathbf{m})u].

So
\textup{gr}^{\dagger}_{\bullet}\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
is noetherian. By
[
Bjö13
, A.III 1.27]
,
\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
is also noetherian.
∎

Theorem 5.14

(Logarithmic Artin-Rees)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a finitely generated
\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
-module and let
\mathcal{N}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
be a
\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}
-submodule. There exists

such that

\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{n}\cdot\mathcal{M}\cap\mathcal{N}\subseteq\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{n-n_{0}}\mathcal{N}\quad\textup{for all }n\geq n_{0}.

Proof.

We define

R_{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{M}=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}}\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{i}\mathcal{M}\cdot u^{i}

which is a finitely generated graded
\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
-module. Then we have a
\mathscr{B}^{R}_{\mathbf{m}}
-submodule

Since
\mathscr{B}_{\mathbf{m}}^{R}
is noetherian,
R^{\prime}_{\mathbf{m}}\mathcal{N}
is finitely generated over
\mathscr{B}_{\mathbf{m}}^{R}
. We may take a system of generators of

,
a_{1}\cdot u^{i_{1}},\dots,a_{N}\cdot u^{i_{N}}

n\geq n_{0}
and for every

. Thus, we have
m\in\alpha(\mathbf{m})^{n-n_{0}}\cdot\mathcal{N}
.
∎

Theorem 6.3

U\subseteq\mathbb{C}^{r}
is a simply connected small enough neighborhood. Therefore, we finally obtain

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\big)=\textup{supp}\big(\pi_{*}^{\mathscr{G}_{r}}\big(\widetilde{i^{!}_{K}}\mathcal{N}_{0}(*E)\big)\big).

∎

By the above theorem and Theorem
5.31
, we immediately have:

Corollary 6.6
.

With notation and assumptions as in Theorem
6.5
,

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of translated subtori for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
. Furthermore, if
\mathcal{M}
underlies a
\mathbb{Q}
-mixed Hodge module, then

\textup{Exp}\big(Z(B^{K_{\mathbf{m}}}(\mathcal{N}_{0}))\big)\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{*})^{r}

is a finite union of torsion translated subtori for
K\subseteq\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
\mathbf{m}\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
.

Finally, we give a summary about how to achieve main results in the introduction. Combining Theorem
6.5
and Corollary
6.6
together gives Theorem
A
. Theorem
5.31
splits into Theorem
C
and Theorem
D
.

Appendix: Sabbah’s relative characteristic variety formula

In the appendix, we give a proof of Theorem
5.1
by using a relative characteristic variety formula of Sabbah
[
Sab25
]
.

Let
X
and
T
be complex manifolds and let
R
be a regular commutative finitely generated
\mathbb{C}
-algebra. We use
X_{R}
to denote the complex manifold
X\times\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R
and similarly for
T_{R}
. We write by
q:X\times T\to T
the projection map and by
T^{*}(X\times T/T)
the relative cotangent bundle.
We first recall from
[
Sab87
]
(or
[
Sab25
, 2.8.Def.]
) the definition of relative conormal spaces. If
Z
is a closed analytic subvariety of
X\times T
, the
relative conormal space

T^{*}_{q|_{Z}}(X\times T/T)
is the
closure in
T^{*}(X\times T/T)
of the relative conormal bundle
T^{*}_{q|_{Z^{\circ}}}(X\times T/T)
, where
Z^{o}
is any smooth open dense subset of
Z
such that
q|_{Z^{o}}
is also smooth. Thus,
\dim(T^{*}_{q|_{Z}}(X\times T/T))\leq\dim X+\dim T

Moreover, by Sard’s Theorem,
\dim(T^{*}_{q|_{Z}}(X\times T/T))=\dim X+\dim T
if and only if
q(Z)
contains an open subset of
T
(see
[
BMM02
, §2]
).

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X_{R}\times T/\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R}
-module and let
\mathscr{N}
be a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{X_{R}\times T/T_{R}}
-submodule of
\mathscr{M}
generating
\mathscr{M}
over
\mathscr{D}_{X_{R}\times T/\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R}
. Similar to
[
Sab25
, 2.4.Lem.]
(or originally
[
Sch12
, Chap.III]
), the relative characteristic variety of
\mathscr{N}
inside
T^{*}(X_{R}\times T/T_{R})
only depends on
\mathscr{M}
, thus denoted by
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
, where
q_{R}:X_{R}\times T\to T_{R}
is the natural projection.

Theorem A.1

(Sabbah)
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X_{R}\times T/\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R}
-module. Then every irreducible component of
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
takes the form
T^{*}_{q|_{Z}}(X\times T/T)\times\Sigma
, where
T^{*}_{Z}(X\times T)\times\Sigma
is some irreducible component of the relative characteristic variety of
\mathscr{M}
inside
T^{*}(X_{R}\times T/\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R)
.

By
[
Sab25
, 2.7.Example]
, the above theorem is equivalent to Theorem 3.1 in
loc. cit.
Deeply motivated by ideas in
[
BMM02
]
, we are now able to prove Theorem
5.1
, by following the same strategy as in
[
Wu22
, §3.4]
.

Lemma A.2
.

Let
\mathscr{M}
be a relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{X\times T/T}
-module and let
D\subseteq X
be an effective divisor. If
\mathscr{M}|_{(X\setminus D)\times T}
is
j
-pure for some
j\geq\dim X\times T
, then so is
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
.

Proof.

By
[
FFS23
, Cor.4.31]
,
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
is also relative regular holonomic. By purity, we can thus assume

\textup{Ch}(\mathscr{M})=\sum T_{Z_{i}}^{*}Y\times\Sigma_{i}\subseteq T^{*}X\times T

such that if
Z_{i}\cap X\setminus D\not=\emptyset
, then
\Sigma_{i}
is purely
(2\dim X+\dim T-j)
-dimensional. If
\dim\Sigma_{il}>2\dim X+\dim T-j
for
\Sigma_{il}
an irreducible component of
\Sigma_{i}
for some
i
, then
Z_{i}\subseteq D
. Since the relative characteristic variety is locally finite, we take a small neighborhood
U\subseteq X\times T
intersecting
Z_{i}\times\Sigma_{il}
. Localizing
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
at a general point of
U\cap Z_{i}\times\Sigma_{il}
, the relative characteristic variety of
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
becomes
T_{Z_{i}}^{*}Y\times\Sigma_{il}
. This is impossible because
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
does not have any submodules supported in
D\times T
. Therefore, the grade number of
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
is still
j
. Since
\mathscr{M}|_{(X\setminus D)\times T}
is pure, the submodules of
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
with grade numbers
>j
must be supported in
D\times T
, which thus must be zero. Therefore,
\mathscr{M}(*(D\times T))
is also
j
-pure.
∎

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

For simplicity, we use
Y
to also denote
Y^{\textup{an}}
, the associated analytic space, and similarly for
X
. Take
\widetilde{Y}\coloneqq Y\times\mathbb{C}^{l}_{y}=X\times\mathbb{C}^{l}\times\mathbb{C}^{l}_{y}
with
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{l})
the coordinates on
\mathbb{C}^{l}
and consider the map

\phi\colon\widetilde{Y}\to Y,\quad(x,t_{1},\dots,t_{l},y_{1},\dots,y_{l})\mapsto(x,e^{y_{1}}\cdot t_{1},\dots,e^{y_{l}}\cdot t_{l}),

and the associated map
\widetilde{\phi}=(\phi,\textup{id})\colon\widetilde{Y}_{R}\to Y_{R}
.
Obviously,
\phi
and
\widetilde{\phi}
are smooth. Put
\mathscr{M}\coloneqq\widetilde{\phi}^{*}(\widetilde{\mathcal{N}(*D)})
and
\mathscr{N}\coloneqq\widetilde{\phi}^{*}\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
, where
\widetilde{\mathcal{N}}
is the associated
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{Y,D}}
-module. Write
\widetilde{D}=\phi^{*}D
and by
\widetilde{D}_{R}\subseteq\widetilde{Y}_{R}
the associated divisor. Since
\phi
is smooth,
\mathscr{N}
is a coherent
\widetilde{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D}}}
-module, and we have the correspondence

T^{*}(Y,D)_{R}\xleftarrow{\omega}T^{*}(Y,D)\times_{Y}\widetilde{Y}_{R}\xrightarrow{\rho}T^{*}(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D})_{R}

such that
\omega
is smooth and that
\rho
is a closed embedding. By smoothness, we have

\textup{Ch}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D}}}}(\mathscr{N})=\rho\big(\omega^{-1}(\textup{Ch}^{\dagger}(\mathcal{N}))\big).

Meanwhile, one observes that
t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}-\partial_{y_{i}}
kills
1\otimes\widetilde{\phi}^{-1}m\in\mathscr{N}
. Thus,
\mathscr{N}
is a coherent
\mathscr{D}_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}/\mathbb{C}^{l}_{R}}
-submodule of
\mathscr{M}
generating
\mathscr{M}
and by considering the projection
q\colon\widetilde{Y}=X\times\mathbb{C}^{l}\times\mathbb{C}^{l}_{y}\to\mathbb{C}^{l}
we have

\iota(\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M}))=\iota(\textup{Ch}_{\mathscr{D}_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}/\mathbb{C}^{r}_{R}}}(\mathscr{N}))=\textup{Ch}_{\widetilde{\mathscr{A}^{R}_{\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D}}}}(\mathscr{N})

where we denote

\iota:T^{*}(\widetilde{Y}_{R}/\mathbb{C}^{l}_{R})\hookrightarrow T^{*}(\widetilde{Y},\widetilde{D})_{R},\quad(x,t,y,\alpha,\xi_{x},\xi_{y})\mapsto(x,t,y,\alpha,\xi_{x},\xi^{\log}_{t}=\xi_{y},\xi_{y})

the closed embedding corresponding to the relations
t_{i}\partial_{t_{i}}-\partial_{y_{i}}=0
. Since
\iota
and
\rho
are closed embeddings and
\omega
is smooth, it is enough to prove that
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
has no components supported over
\widetilde{D}_{R}
. Similarly, considering the relative Lagrangian correspondence

T^{*}Y_{R}\xleftarrow{\omega^{\prime}}T^{*}Y\times_{Y}\widetilde{Y}_{R}\xrightarrow{\rho^{\prime}}T^{*}\widetilde{Y}_{R}

we have
\textup{Ch}(\mathscr{M})=\rho\big(\omega^{-1}(\textup{Ch}(\mathcal{N}(*D))\big).
Since
\mathcal{N}(*)
is relative holonomic, we assume

\textup{Ch}(\mathcal{N}(*D))=\sum_{i\in L}T^{*}_{Z_{i}}Y\times\Sigma_{i}

where
\Sigma_{i}\subseteq\textup{Spec }R
are subvarieties (possibly reducible). We put
L^{\prime}\subseteq L
to be the subset of all indices
i
satisfying
Z_{i}\cap U
nonempty. We also write
\widetilde{Z}_{i}=Z_{i}\times_{Y}\widetilde{Y}
. Then, we have

\textup{Ch}(\mathscr{M})=\sum_{i\in L}T^{*}_{\widetilde{Z}_{i}}\widetilde{Y}\times\Sigma_{i}.

By
[
FFS23
, Thm.1]
,
\mathscr{M}
is a relative regular holonomic
\mathscr{D}_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}/\textup{Spec}^{\textup{an}}R}
-module.
By Theorem A.1, every irreducible component of
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
is
T^{*}_{q|_{\widetilde{Z}_{i}}}(\widetilde{Y}/\mathbb{C}^{l})\times\Sigma_{il}
for some
i\in L
, where
\Sigma_{il}
is an irreducible component of
\Sigma_{i}

By construction, the key observation (due to Briançon-Maisonobe-Merle) is that
q(\widetilde{Z}_{i})
contains an open subset of
T
if and only if
i\in L^{\prime}
. Then, we take the pure filtration
T_{\bullet}\mathscr{M}
of
\mathscr{M}
. By Lemma A.2 and the uniqueness of the pure filtration,
T_{j}\mathscr{M}
and
T_{j}\mathscr{M}/T_{j+1}\mathscr{M}
are all
\mathscr{O}_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}}(*\widetilde{D}_{R})
-module for all
j
. By relative holonomicity, we also know for every
j

\textup{Ch}(T_{j}\mathscr{M}/T_{j+1}\mathscr{M})=\sum_{i\in L_{j}\subseteq L}T^{*}_{Z_{i}}Y\times\Sigma^{j}_{i}.

By induction, it is enough to assume that
\mathscr{M}
is pure. In particular, all
\Sigma_{il}
are of the same dimension, say the dimension to be
k
. Thanks to Theorem A.1, we know
\dim\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})=\dim\widetilde{Y}+k
.
Since
\mathscr{M}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}=\mathscr{N}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}
and
\mathscr{M}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}
is pure, if
\mathscr{N}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathscr{N}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}
is a non-zero coherent sub-
\mathscr{D}_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}/\mathbb{C}^{r}_{R}}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}
-module, then
\dim\textup{Ch}(\mathscr{N}^{\prime})=\dim\widetilde{Y}+k
by Theorem A.1 again. Then,
\mathscr{N}|_{\widetilde{Y}_{R}\setminus\widetilde{D}_{R}}
is pure.
Thus,
\mathscr{N}
is also pure since
\mathscr{N}\subseteq\mathscr{M}=\mathscr{M}(*\widetilde{D}_{R})
. Therefore,
\textup{Ch}(\mathscr{N})
is equidimensional and thus
by counting dimension we have that
every irreducible component of
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
is
T^{*}_{q|_{\widetilde{Z}_{i}}}(\widetilde{Y}/\mathbb{C}^{l})\times\Sigma_{il}
for some
i\in L^{\prime}
. In particular,
\textup{Ch}_{q_{R}}(\mathscr{M})
has no components supported over
\widetilde{D}_{R}
, which finishes the proof.
∎

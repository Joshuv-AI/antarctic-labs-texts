Exchange tensors, generalized RKKY interactions, and magnetization dynamics in heterostructures of ferromagnets and topological insulators
Christian Svingen Johnsen and Asle Sudbø

Abstract
We present a comprehensive theoretical analysis of magnetic heterostructures composed of ferromagnetic (FM) layers interfaced with three-dimensional topological insulators (TIs). Integrating out the topological surface states and computing the spin determinant to second order in spins, we derive the effective generalized Ruderman–Kittel–Kasuya–Yosida (RKKY) exchange interactions mediated by topological surface states. These interactions inherently incorporate spin-momentum locking and anisotropic spin susceptibilities stemming from the Dirac-like dispersion of the TI surface electrons. The analysis reveals that the interplay between the spin-orbit coupling intrinsic to the TI and the magnetization texture in the FM layer induces highly nonlocal and retarded, chiral, and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya (DM)-like contributions to the effective spin Hamiltonian. Furthermore, the spin dynamics is studied through a derivation of the LLG equation for this problem. The induced interactions renormalize many of the FM’s intrinsic properties, but a term in the LLG equation is induced that is related to the rate of change of the magnetization’s curl, which is relevant to skyrmion dynamics. The magnon dispersion exhibits modifications due to the TI-mediated interactions, including tunable magnon gaps, sensitive to a tunable chemical potential and interfacial exchange coupling strength. The results also apply to finite temperatures. They elucidate topologically induced magnetic phenomena and pave the way for engineering exotic spin textures, such as skyrmions and chiral domain walls, in TI/FM hybrid systems with tunable interactions.



I
Introduction

Topological defects, such as vortices and domain walls, arise from broken symmetries in condensed matter systems and play key roles in phase transitions and material properties. Skyrmions, stable spin textures with topological protection, are particularly important for their potential in low-energy spintronic devices, offering robustness, scalability, and efficient information storage.
The existence of magnetic skyrmions has been considered possible at least since 2006
Rößler et al. 2006
. These highly particular windings of the magnetization field in magnets are topologically protected magnetic textures which can arise from various mechanisms and in various materials, yielding different types of skyrmions
Finocchio et al. 2016
. They have been observed in MnSi
Mühlbauer et al. 2009
;
Neubauer et al. 2009
;
Pappas et al. 2009
and various other materials
Yu et al. 2010a
;
Yu et al. 2010b
;
Seki et al. 2012
, including antiferromagnets (AFMs)
Raičević et al. 2011
. In addition to these bulk material observations of skyrmions, there have also been observations in ultra-thin films
Heinze et al. 2011
.

The envisaged technological application for which there has been hope and excitement is storing information using skyrmions in nanoribbons with a racetrack memory-like setup where the skyrmions can be moved using exceedingly small current densities
Fert et al. 2013
. In addition to creating and moving these particle-like magnetic textures, there have been theoretical proposals for skyrmion logic gates
Zhang et al. 2015a
and skyrmion transistors
Zhang et al. 2015b
. Using metallic materials with regular, dissipative electrical currents leads to heating and less energy efficient devices. If, however, the low-dissipation currents in e.g. a topological insulator (TI) are used
Hasan and Kane 2010
;
Qi and Zhang 2011
, device efficiency can be increased. In addition to hosting electrical currents with its topologically protected edge states, the TI has spin-orbit coupling (SOC) that is transmitted to the magnetic material via the proximity effect.

Heterostructures of AFMs or ferromagnets (FMs) and topological insulator have been studied to some extent. Some works have investigated how the TI is affected by the proximity of an AFM
Erlandsen et al. 2020
, whereas others have studied only FM + TI and predicted induced DMI in the FM. The studies on the FM + TI system were conducted using a continuum model, in one case with temperatures above the Curie temperature of the FM
Nogueira et al. 2018
. Self-energy effects on the surface states of the TI originating with spin-fluctuations on the FM have also been considered, within a lattice model
Mæland et al. 2021
. Subsequently, the van der Waals Magnet
\text{Fe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{3}}}\text{GeTe}{\vphantom{\text{X}}}_{\smash[t]{\text{2}}}
epitaxially grown on a three-dimensional TI has been studied experimentally, displaying room-temperature Curie temperatures for monolayer FM
Wang et al. 2020
. Another theoretical study focused on the magnetization dynamics at zero chemical potential, i.e., inside the magnetization-induced gap of the Dirac fermions
Rex et al. 2016
.

In this paper, we study theoretically a heterostructure of FM+TI and ask to what extent spin-interactions that promote non-collinear spin-textures, including skyrmions, are generated by the proxity to a TI. In this way, one may hope to have a design of a platform for generating ensembles of skyrmions, including skyrmion liquids or lattices
Gruber et al. 2025
, using magnetic materials that are ubiquitous.
We extend previous works to study the effects of the
gapped
Dirac fermions in the TI on the FM, i.e., at temperatures below the Curie temperature, including also a variable chemical potential, which could tune the surface states of the TI in and out of the gapped state. This then also in principle allows tuning of the induced spin interactions
in situ
. Furthermore, we employ field theory techniques and approximations relevant in condensed matter systems. In particular, we do
not
assume a Lorentz-invariant system in order to apply dimensional regularization, but rather we use a continuum theory with a short-distance cutoff that is of the order of the lattice constant,
a
. Furthermore, this allows us to treat the systems at finite temperature. We show that particle-hole excitations in the TI can mediate spin-spin interactions of various forms, such as exchange interactions and, importantly for skyrmion applications, DM interactions. In the simple case of temporally local two-spin interactions, we find an RKKY-like spatial pattern
Ruderman and Kittel 1954
;
Kasuya 1956
;
Yosida 1957
, where the scale of oscillations is set by the Fermi momentum, which can be tuned by changing the chemical potential through e.g. an electric field or chemical doping. The interaction strengths can vary considerably depending on the TI’s Fermi velocity and the interfacial exchange coupling, ranging from the sub-
\mathrm{meV}
range to around
1\text{\,}\mathrm{meV}
. Studying the dynamics of the FM, we find a nutation term
Ciornei et al. 2011
in the magnetization equation of motion similar to that induced by normal-metal conduction electrons
Kikuchi and Tatara 2015
;
Johnsen and Sudbø 2025
, but its sign changes when the chemical potential passes through the gap. The nutation terms and other parameters measurable in a ferromagnetic resonance experiment are slightly modified by the spin-orbit coupling in the TI through a non-dissipative time-derivative term. Various other terms containing time and space derivatives and combinations thereof are also induced, but only a few are present to linear order in spin fluctuations, where analytic expressions can be obtained.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we introduce the model we use. In Section
III
, we give expressions for the induced spin-spin interactions in the FM due to the proximity to the TI, with details relegated to Appendix
A
. In Section
IV
, we focus on the static case and explicitly evaluate the radial and angular dependence of the antisymmetric contribution to the exchange tensor, along with the angular dependence of the diagonal and symmetric off-diagonal parts of the exchange tensor. The radial dependence of the latter two are treated in Appendix
B
. In Section
V
, we perform an expansion to second order in frequency and momenta of the non-local in space and time generated coupling constants. In Section
VI
, we use this expansion to obtain the equations of motion for the magnetization, as well as the renormalized magnon spectrum of the FM.

Model

The model Hamiltonian is the sum

(1)

where we include an effective Hamiltonian for the TI’s spin-momentum locked surface states,

\displaystyle H_{\text{TI}}+H_{\text{int}}

\displaystyle=\int\differential[2]{r}c^{\dagger}({\mathbf{r}})\left[\mathbf{d}(-i\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}\gradient)\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}-\mu\vphantom{\bar{J}}\right.

(5)

\displaystyle\left.-2\bar{J}\mathbf{n}({\mathbf{r}})\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}\right]c({\mathbf{r}}).

Here, we combined

and

into one operator

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{d}_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-i\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}\partial_{y}\\
i\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}\partial_{x}\\
-m_{0}\end{pmatrix}

(6)

and

\displaystyle\mathbf{d}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{d}_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}-i\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}\partial_{x}\\
-i\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}\partial_{y}\\
-m_{0}\end{pmatrix}.

(7)

The labels are interchanged with respect to those of Ref.
Nogueira et al. 2018

We study the effects of the TI on the FM by integrating out the TI fermions in the path integral formalism. The computation is shown in Appendix
A
. We emphasize that we include the FM magnetization explicitly in Eq. (

), meaning we integrate out

from the partition function

\displaystyle Z=\int\mathcal{D}\mathbf{n}\mathrm{e}^{-S_{\text{FM}}[\mathbf{n}]}\int\mathcal{D}\psi\mathcal{D}\psi^{\dagger}\mathrm{e}^{-\psi^{\dagger}\left(G^{-1}+B\right)\psi}

(8)

where the fermion propagator

is given by

\displaystyle G^{-1}({\mathbf{r}},\partial_{\tau})

\displaystyle=\partial_{\tau}-\mu+\mathbf{d}\cdot{\boldsymbol{\sigma}},

(9)

and

\displaystyle S_{\text{eff}}[{\mathbf{n}}]

\displaystyle\approx S_{\text{FM}}[{\mathbf{n}}]-\Tr\ln G^{-1}+\Delta S_{\text{eff}}^{(1)}[{\mathbf{n}}]+\Delta S_{\text{eff}}^{(2)}[{\mathbf{n}}],

where the third term,

\displaystyle d_{\pm}({\mathbf{k}})=-\mu\pm\sqrt{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}{\mathbf{k}})^{2}+m_{0}^{2}}

IV
Static Theory

Second-order expansion

The other kind of calculation we will show is based on truncating the Taylor expansion in Eq. (
16

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{q}},i\omega_{\nu})\approx\sum_{i+j+k\leq 2}q_{x}^{i}q_{y}^{j}(i\omega_{\nu})^{k}\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{(k,i,j)}.

The Taylor coefficients are

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{(k,i,j)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\to 0,i\omega_{\nu}\to 0}\frac{1}{i!(j!)(k!)}\left(\partial_{q_{x}}\right)^{i}\left(\partial_{q_{y}}\right)^{j}

\displaystyle\times\left(\partial_{(i\omega_{\nu})}\right)^{k}\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{q}},i\omega_{\nu})

n

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{(k,i,j)}=\frac{1}{\beta(i!)(j!)(k!)}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}

(33)

\displaystyle\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\to 0,i\omega_{\nu}\to 0}\left(\partial_{q_{x}}\right)^{i}\left(\partial_{q_{y}}\right)^{j}\left(\partial_{(i\omega_{\nu})}\right)^{k}\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{k}},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n}),

then compute the sum over

VII
Conclusion

In this paper we have presented an analysis of magnetic heterostructures composed of ferromagnetic layers interfaced with three-dimensional topological insulators (TIs). We have studied the TI-induced two-spin interactions, which take the form of generalized RKKY exchange interactions. The interplay between the spin-orbit coupling intrinsic to the TI and the magnetization texture in the FM layer leads to highly nonlocal and retarded, chiral, and Dzyaloshinskii-Moriya-like contributions to the effective spin Hamiltonian. In the static case, where interactions are instantaneous, the relevant length scales of the spatial oscillations are given by the Fermi momentum
{k_{\text{F}}}
, and the chemical potential
\mu
and the interfacial exchange strength
\bar{J}
set the amplitude of the oscillations. These parameters, then, allow for tuning of how much the TI electrons affect the magnetization texture. Furthermore, we have studied the spin dynamics through a derivation of the LLG equation. In the general case, we have found that the LLG equation’s parameters are renormalized by the interface with the TI, but a term related to the time derivative of the curl of the magnetization, relevant for skyrmion dynamics, also appears if the chemical potential is above a certain threshold. The magnon dispersion is modified by the TI-mediated interactions, including tunable magnon gaps, sensitive to a tunable chemical potential and interfacial exchange coupling strength. The nutation term, related to the second time derivative of the magnetization, switches signs as the chemical potential is tuned outside the gap in the electron dispersion, softening the nonuniform high-frequency magnon modes.

\displaystyle=\frac{(-2\bar{J})^{2}}{2\beta^{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{1}{\left[(i\omega_{n_{1}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n_{2}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]}

(58a)

\displaystyle\times\tr\left[\bigl(\sigma_{\gamma}d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})+d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{1}})\mathbb{I}\bigr)\sigma_{\alpha}n_{\alpha}(k_{1}-k_{2})\bigl(\sigma_{\delta}d_{\delta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})+d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{2}})\mathbb{I}\bigr)\sigma_{\beta}n_{\beta}(k_{2}-k_{1})\right].

The presence of SOC in the TI adds more off-diagonal factors
\sigma_{\gamma}d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}})
in the above spin trace than would be generated by normal-metal electrons with spin-split parabolic bands
Johnsen and Sudbø 2025
. This, in turn, will allow spin-spin interactions that are off-diagonal, which disappear due to inversion symmetry in the normal-metal case. The spin trace above can be performed using the Pauli matrix properties

\displaystyle\tr\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}

\displaystyle=2\delta_{\alpha\beta}

(59a)

\displaystyle\tr\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}\sigma_{\gamma}

\displaystyle=2i\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

(59b)

\displaystyle\tr\sigma_{\alpha}\sigma_{\beta}\sigma_{\gamma}\sigma_{\delta}

\displaystyle=2(\delta_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{\gamma\delta}-\delta_{\alpha\gamma}\delta_{\beta\delta}+\delta_{\alpha\delta}\delta_{\beta\gamma})

(59c)

to yield

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\text{eff}}^{(2)}[{\mathbf{n}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{\alpha}(k_{1}-k_{2})n_{\beta}(k_{2}-k_{1})}{\left[(i\omega_{n_{1}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n_{2}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]}

\displaystyle\phantom{QQQQQ}\times\biggl[d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\delta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})(\delta_{\gamma\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}-\delta_{\gamma\delta}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\delta_{\gamma\beta}\delta_{\alpha\delta})+d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{2}})i\epsilon_{\gamma\alpha\beta}

(60)

\displaystyle\phantom{QQQQQQQ}+d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{1}})d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})i\epsilon_{\alpha\gamma\beta}+d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{1}})d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{2}})\delta_{\alpha\beta}\biggr].

Next, straightforward manipulations permit us to rewrite the four-Pauli-matrix term to

\displaystyle d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\delta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})(\delta_{\gamma\alpha}\delta_{\delta\beta}-\delta_{\gamma\delta}\delta_{\alpha\beta}+\delta_{\gamma\beta}\delta_{\alpha\delta})

\displaystyle=\bigl(d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})+d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\bigr)(1-\delta_{\alpha\beta})

(61)

\displaystyle+\biggl(d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})-\sum_{\gamma\neq\alpha}d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\biggr)\delta_{\alpha\beta},

showing that it is comprised of a spin-diagonal part and an off-diagonal symmetric part. Collecting the terms with similar spin structure, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta S_{\text{eff}}^{(2)}[{\mathbf{n}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta^{2}}\sum_{n_{1},n_{2}}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{n_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2},i\omega_{n_{1}}-i\omega_{n_{2}})n_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2}-{\mathbf{k}}_{1},i\omega_{n_{2}}-i\omega_{n_{1}})}{\left[(i\omega_{n_{1}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n_{2}}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]}

(62)

\displaystyle\times\Biggl[\biggl(d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{1}})d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{2}})+d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})-\sum_{\gamma\neq\alpha}d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\biggr)\delta_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle+i\Bigl(d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{2}})-d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})d_{0}(i\omega_{n_{1}})\Bigr)\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+\Bigl(d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})+d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\Bigr)(1-\delta_{\alpha\beta})\Biggr],

i.e., Eq. (
15
) in the main text. The first term represents a generated anisotropic exchange term (a generalized RKKY interaction). The second is a generated DMI interaction term, and the third term is an off-diagonal exchange term that couples different components of the spins. The two latter both originate with the SOC of the TI, whereas the first term would have a non-zero counterpart if the TI were replaced by a NM with no SOC.

Appendix B
Momentum integrals and Matsubara sums for the static theory

Here we show how to compute

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu})=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}\biggl[\delta_{\alpha\beta}J_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1},{\mathbf{k}}_{2},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle+(1-\delta_{\alpha\beta})T_{\alpha\beta}^{\text{sym}}({\mathbf{k}}_{1},{\mathbf{k}}_{2},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n})+\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma}D_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1},{\mathbf{k}}_{2},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n})\biggr]

(63)

in the static limit,
i\omega_{\nu}=0
. We treat each term
J_{\alpha},D_{\gamma},T_{\alpha\beta}^{\text{sym}}
separately because they are sums of separable
{\mathbf{k}}
integrals and one Matsubara sum, as seen from Eq. (
15
). Starting with the only term where we can obtain an analytic expression at zero temperature,
D_{\gamma}
, we note that since

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=\frac{4i\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{n}[d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})-d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})]\frac{X_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{\Bigl[X_{n}^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\Bigr]\Bigl[X_{n}^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\Bigr]},

(64)

we have
D_{z}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=0
on account of
d_{z}({\mathbf{k}})=-m_{0}
being
{\mathbf{k}}
independent. This result does not depend on the choice of
\mathbf{d}
vector, Eqs. (
6
) or (
7
). It relies only on the fact that
d_{x},d_{y}
are odd in momentum and that
d_{z}
is momentum independent since the TI surface states have no dispersion perpendicular to the surface. Here,
X_{n}\equiv i\omega_{n}+\mu
. Assuming
\gamma\neq z
, the above can be simplified to

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=\frac{-8\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\imaginary\sum_{n}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})X_{n}\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{\Bigl[X_{n}^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\Bigr]\Bigl[X_{n}^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\Bigr]}

(65)

as can be seen by swapping
{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\leftrightarrow{\mathbf{k}}_{2}
and
i\omega_{n}\to-i\omega_{n}
in the
d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})
term.
The DMI term is separable in momenta and given by

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=\frac{-8\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\imaginary\sum_{n}(i\omega_{n}+\mu)V_{\gamma}(-\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})U(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n}),

(66)

where

\displaystyle U(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential[2]{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}})}

(67a)

\displaystyle V_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\int\frac{\differential[2]{k}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}})\mathrm{e}^{i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}})}.

(67b)

Note that
V_{\gamma\neq z}
can be obtained from
U
by differentiation and that
V_{z}=-m_{0}U
.

The basic momentum integral needed to find
\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0),
then, is

\displaystyle U(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{k}k\int_{0}^{2\pi}\frac{\differential{\phi}}{2\pi}\frac{\mathrm{e}^{i\absolutevalue{\kk}\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}\cos\phi}}{(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}(k)}

(68)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{k}k\frac{J_{0}(\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}k)}{(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}(k)}

(69)

since the dispersion
d({\mathbf{k}})=d(k)=(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}k)^{2}+m_{0}^{2}
is isotropic. Therefore,

\displaystyle U(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{k}k\frac{J_{0}(\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}k)}{k^{2}+\frac{m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{2}}},

(70)

so

is of the form
Gradshteyn and Ryzhik 1980

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{k}k\frac{J_{0}(ak)}{k^{2}+b^{2}}=K_{0}(ab),

(71)

which holds for
a>0,\real b>0
. Thus, we choose the principal branch of the square root in

\displaystyle b=\sqrt{\frac{m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{2}}},

(72)

i.e.,
\sqrt{\absolutevalue{z}\mathrm{e}^{i\theta}}=\sqrt{\absolutevalue{z}}\mathrm{e}^{i\theta/2}
with the phase
\theta\in(-\pi,\pi].
Using the result

\displaystyle U(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{-1}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{2}}K_{0}\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}}\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}\right),

(73)

the expression for the other
{\mathbf{k}}
integral follows by differentiation:

\displaystyle V_{\gamma\neq z}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{2\pi(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{2}}\frac{\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}}{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta y\\
\Delta x\end{pmatrix}_{\gamma}K_{1}\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}}\sqrt{\left[m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}\right]}\right).

(74)

This can now be inserted into
D_{\gamma}
to perform the Matsubara summation, but let us first show how
J_{\alpha}
and
T_{\alpha\beta}^{\text{sym}}
can be expressed in terms of the same
U,V_{\gamma}
.

From the basic expression

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta}^{\text{sym}}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\bigl[d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})+d_{\beta}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\bigr]\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]},

(75)

the statement
T_{\alpha z}^{\text{sym}}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=0
in the main text follows by replacing
{\mathbf{k}}_{1}\leftrightarrow-{\mathbf{k}}_{2}
since
d_{z}({\mathbf{k}})
is constant and
d_{\alpha\neq z}(-{\mathbf{k}})=-d_{\alpha\neq z}({\mathbf{k}})
. The same momentum relabeling in one of the terms in the numerator leads to

\displaystyle T_{xy}^{\text{sym}}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{2d_{x}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{y}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}}}{\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]}

(76)

which means that the symmetric off-diagonal spin-spin interaction is given by

\displaystyle T_{xy}^{\text{sym}}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{-8\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{n}V_{x}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})V_{y}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n}).

(77)

Each term in the induced exchange interaction,

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{n}\int\frac{\differential[2]{k_1}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{\differential[2]{k_2}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}+d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\alpha}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})-\sum_{\gamma\neq\alpha}d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})d_{\gamma}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})}{\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{1})\right]\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}-d^{2}({\mathbf{k}}_{2})\right]}\mathrm{e}^{-i({\mathbf{k}}_{1}-{\mathbf{k}}_{2})\cdot\Delta{\mathbf{r}}},

(78)

is immediately recognizable as a product of the same
U,V_{\gamma}
as above. Using their
\Delta{\mathbf{r}}

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{4\bar{J}^{2}}{\beta}\sum_{n}\biggl\{\left[(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}+s_{\alpha z}m_{0}^{2}\right]U^{2}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})-s_{\alpha x}V_{x}^{2}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})-s_{\alpha y}V_{y}^{2}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{n})\biggr\},

(79)

where
s_{\alpha\gamma}=2\delta_{\alpha\gamma}-1.

Thus, all the interactions
J_{\alpha},T^{\text{sym}}_{\alpha\beta},D_{\gamma}
can be written in terms of some function of
\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}-(i\omega_{n}+\mu)^{2}}
, where one must choose the branch of the square root with positive real part. As we will show shortly, this leads to a branch cut along the real axis that must be taken into consideration. In general, the Matsubara sum in the interactions,

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{n}g_{\alpha\beta}\left(z_{n}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi i\beta}\oint\differential{z}f(z)g_{\alpha\beta}(z),

(80)

where

\displaystyle f(z)=\frac{-\beta}{1+\mathrm{e}^{\beta(z-\mu)}}.

(81)

The contour(s) must enclose all the poles
z_{n}
of
f(z)
, and similarly to Ref.
Johnsen and Sudbø 2025
, for
\mu>0
the contours become the quarter circles in the first and fourth with radii tending to infinity. There need to be two contours because of the aforementioned branch cut near the real axis, i.e., when
z=x\pm i\epsilon
for real
x
and
\epsilon>0
. For such

\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)
takes the value

\displaystyle\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}-(x\pm i\epsilon)^{2}}\to\begin{cases}\mp i\sqrt{x^{2}-m_{0}^{2}},&x^{2}>m_{0}^{2}\\
\sqrt{m_{0}^{2}-x^{2}},&x^{2}<m_{0}^{2}\end{cases},

(82)

as
\epsilon\to 0^{+}.
All the arc integrals are exponentially suppressed by the counting function
f(z),
meaning that the general expression for the static interactions is an integral over straight lines in the
z

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi i\beta}\left(\int_{i\infty}^{i\epsilon}\differential{z}+\int_{-i\epsilon}^{-i\infty}\differential{z}+\int_{0+i\epsilon}^{\infty+i\epsilon}\differential{z}+\int_{\infty-i\epsilon}^{0-i\epsilon}\differential{z}\right)f(z)g_{\alpha\beta}(z).

(83)

With the guiding principle of Eq. (
82
) and a table of integrals, the three types of interactions can be calculated separately at zero temperature, where the counting function

becomes a step function.

For the DM-like interaction, the imaginary-axis integrals cancel, and there is only a nonzero contribution if
\mu>m_{0}
. It reduces to

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\theta(\mu-\absolutevalue{m_0})\frac{\bar{J}^{2}\absolutevalue{m_0}^{3}}{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}\pi^{3}(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta y\\
\Delta x\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{\gamma}\imaginary i\real\int_{0}^{\mu^{2}/m_{0}^{2}-1}\differential{x}\sqrt{x}

(84)

\displaystyle\phantom{QQQQ}\times K_{0}\left(i\frac{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}\absolutevalue{m_0}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}}\sqrt{x}\right)K_{1}\left(i\frac{\absolutevalue{\Delta\rr}\absolutevalue{m_0}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}}\sqrt{x}\right)

\displaystyle\Delta\bar{{\mathbf{r}}}\equiv\Delta{\mathbf{r}}\frac{\sqrt{\mu^{2}-m_{0}^{2}}}{\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}}}=\Delta{\mathbf{r}}{k_{\text{F}}},

(85)

the result is

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\theta(\mu-\absolutevalue{m_0})\frac{\bar{J}^{2}\absolutevalue{m_0}^{3}}{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}-\sin\phi\\
\cos\phi\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{\gamma}\frac{\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}}-1\right)^{3/2}}{2\pi\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}}J_{1}\left(\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}\right)Y_{1}\left(\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}\right)

(86)

for the choice of SOC
\mathbf{d}_{1}
and

\displaystyle D_{\gamma}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\theta(\mu-\absolutevalue{m_0})\frac{\bar{J}^{2}\absolutevalue{m_0}^{3}}{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}-\cos\phi\\
-\sin\phi\\
0\end{pmatrix}_{\gamma}\frac{\left(\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}}-1\right)^{3/2}}{2\pi\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}}J_{1}\left(\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}\right)Y_{1}\left(\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}\right)

(87)

for the other choice.
J_{n},Y_{n}
are Bessel functions of the first and second kind, respectively. The large-distance behavior mentioned in the main text comes from the expansion of

\displaystyle\frac{J_{1}(x)Y_{1}(x)}{x}\to\frac{\cos(2x)}{\pi x^{2}}

(88)

to second order in
1/x
for large values of

We lack analytic expressions for the remaining interaction types, where not even the imaginary-axis integrals cancel. The result at zero temperature and

\displaystyle T_{xy}^{\text{sym}}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)

\displaystyle=\pm\frac{\bar{J}^{2}\absolutevalue{m_0}^{3}}{(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{4}}\sin(2\phi)\frac{1}{\pi^{3}}\left[\imaginary\int_{1}^{\mu/\absolutevalue{m_0}}\differential{u}\left(u^{2}-1\right)K_{1}^{2}\left(i\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}\frac{\sqrt{u^{2}-1}}{\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}}-1}}\right)\right.

for the off-diagonal part, where the upper sign is for
\mathbf{d}_{1}
and the lower for
\mathbf{d}_{2}
. The spin-diagonal part is given by

\displaystyle J_{\alpha}(\Delta{\mathbf{r}},i\omega_{\nu}=0)=\frac{\bar{J}^{2}\absolutevalue{m_0}^{3}}{\pi^{3}(\hbar v_{\mathrm{F}})^{4}}\left\{\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{\tilde{y}}\left[\left(-\tilde{y}^{2}+s_{\alpha z}\right)K_{0}^{2}\left(\frac{\absolutevalue{\Delta\bar{\rr}}}{\sqrt{\frac{\mu^{2}}{m_{0}^{2}}-1}}\sqrt{\tilde{y}^{2}+1}\right)\right.\right.

(90)

where we defined

. The above is for
\mathbf{d}_{1}

Appendix C
Momentum integrals and Matsubara sums for dynamics

This Appendix describes the general steps needed to obtain the Taylor expansion of
\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{q}},i\omega_{\nu})
. Starting with Eq. (
33
), we immediately rewrite the
{\mathbf{k}}
integral in polar coordinates such that
k_{x}=k\cos\theta,

k_{y}=k\sin\theta
,

\displaystyle\chi_{\alpha\beta}^{(k,i,j)}=\frac{1}{\beta(i!)(j!)(k!)}\int_{0}^{\Lambda}\frac{\differential{k}k}{(2\pi)^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\differential{\theta}\sum_{n}\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\to 0,i\omega_{\nu}\to 0}\left(\partial_{q_{x}}\right)^{i}\left(\partial_{q_{y}}\right)^{j}\left(\partial_{(i\omega_{\nu})}\right)^{k}\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{k}},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n}).

(91)

\Lambda\propto 1/a
is a momentum cutoff, appearing in our calculations explicitly for the first time here because it cannot be taken as infinity right away in this context. We omit the details of taking the above derivatives and limits of Eq. (
15
), keeping the discussion general. The crucial observation needed to obtain manageable expressions is that the limit

\displaystyle\lim_{{\mathbf{q}}\to 0,i\omega_{\nu}\to 0}\left(\partial_{q_{x}}\right)^{i}\left(\partial_{q_{y}}\right)^{j}\left(\partial_{(i\omega_{\nu})}\right)^{k}\chi_{\alpha\beta}({\mathbf{k}}+{\mathbf{q}},{\mathbf{k}},i\omega_{n}+i\omega_{\nu},i\omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sum_{l}X_{\alpha\beta,l}^{(k,i,j)}(k,i\omega_{n})Y_{\alpha\beta,l}^{(k,i,j)}(\theta)}{Z_{\alpha\beta}^{(k,i,j)}(k,i\omega_{n})}

(92)

takes the form of a finite sum of terms
X_{l}(k,i\omega_{n})Y_{l}(\theta)
divided by a completely
\theta
-independent denominator
Z(k,i\omega_{n})
. It becomes
\theta
independent because the only
\theta
dependence in the denominator is through
{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{q}}
, but
{\mathbf{q}}\to 0
. Thus, the
\theta
integral can be taken inside the
n
sum without affecting the analytic structure of the sum; the functions
Y_{l}(\theta)
are prefactors. Upon integrating out
\theta
, many terms drop out due to symmetry.

The next step is to compute the

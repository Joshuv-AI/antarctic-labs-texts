Letter to the editor regarding ‘the relationship between feeding modes and quality of life among patients with post-stroke dysphagia: an observational study’
Authors: Xian-Hui Huang, Yuan-Hua Wu
Published: 2025

Dear Editor

We read with great interest the article by Wang et al. [1]. The study analysed data from ‘rehabilitation departments in China’ from 2022 to 2024 and concluded that as rehabilitation therapy progressed, quality of life (QoL) improved among ischemic stroke survivors with dysphagia. Furthermore, intermittent oro-oesophageal feeding (IOE) was associated with greater improvements in overall, functional, and emotional QoL compared to nasogastric tube feeding (NG). We commend the authors for this valuable contribution. To further strengthen the robustness and clinical relevance of these findings, we would like to offer the following constructive remarks.

Firstly, the authors commendably included key covariates such as functional status, sociodemographic characteristics, lifestyle habits, and underlying diseases. To further control for potential confounding, we recommend considering the inclusion of additional variables. Given that family history of stroke [2] and community socioeconomic status [3] are significant risk factors for stroke incidence and recovery, their inclusion could help clarify the independent association between feeding modes and QoL in patients with post-stroke dysphagia. Additionally, common complications like constipation and urinary tract infections significantly impact QoL in this immobilized population and should be considered in the model.

Secondly, including patients over 18 with a first-time stroke and dysphagia effectively expanded the study population. Since evidence suggests that middle-aged individuals may have a different psychological response (e.g. higher risk of depressive symptoms) to a first-time stroke compared to older adults [4], age could be an important effect modifier. We recommend conducting subgroup analyses based on clinically relevant different age strata, such as <45, 45–65, >65 years, to explore whether the relationship between feeding mode and QoL differs across these subgroups, thereby enhancing the granularity and robustness of the results.

Finally, appropriate feeding modes may have a significant impact on nutritional status and clinical outcomes. Serum albumin and prealbumin are widely used biomarkers for monitoring nutritional intervention efficacy. We suggest measuring these levels at baseline and after one week of feeding intervention. This would allow future studies to explore whether nutritional status mediates the relationship between feeding mode and QoL, providing valuable mechanistic insights.

Optimizing the Structural Integrity of Gwapyeon with Red BullⓇ and Enhancing Consumer Acceptability: A Comparison of Corn, Mung Bean, and Potato Starch
Authors: Chae-Won No, Seongjin Yoon, Jisu Lee, Jiaxiang Zheng, Bong-Hyun Kim, Chung-Bae Oh, Miae Doo, Jung-Heun Ha
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Gwapyeon, a traditional Korean fruit starch-based gel, offers a unique matrix for functional food diversifi-cation. This study investigated the technical feasibility of incorporating a commercial energy beverage (Red BullⓇ) into a gwapyeon system and evaluated how the source and concentration of plant-derived starch modulate its quality attributes. Corn, mung bean, and potato starch were utilized at 7%－11% under standard thermal processing conditions. The effects of the three starch parameters pH, soluble solids (Brix), chromaticity, syneresis during refrigeration, antioxidant capacity [total phenolic content (TPC), total flavonoid content, and 2,2’-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS)], and mechanical texture (texture profile analysis) were systematically analyzed, followed by a consumer (n=50) sensory evaluation. pH and Brix were not significantly affected by starch source or concentration, suggesting that they were primarily determined by the beverage-based matrix rather than starch formulation. Potato starch gels exhibited the greatest translucency and chromaticity (L*, a*, b*), whereas corn and mung bean starch produced more opaque matrices. Mung bean starch maintained superior network stability with minimal syneresis, while corn starch demonstrated higher initial moisture release followed by stabilization. Antioxidant retention (TPC and ABTS scavenging) was enhanced in corn and mung bean starch gels, likely due to entrapment within a denser network. Texturally, mung bean starch produced the firmest, most resilient gels, while potato starch yielded the softest. Sensory test results identified 9% corn starch formulation (RC9) as most favorable, balancing structural stability with moderate sweetness and chewiness. Starch is the primary determinant of structural and functional quality in beverage-incorporated gels, with corn starch being optimal for modernizing the traditional gwapyeon.

INTRODUCTION

Gwapyeon is a traditional Korean confectionery characterized by a starch-based gel matrix (Kang et al., 2023). Its fabrication requires the thermal processing of fruit juice with honey or sucrose, which facilitates starch-induced gelling (Kang et al., 2023). Although gwapyeon exhibits viscoelastic properties comparable to those of hydrocolloid jellies, the gelling agents and underlying molecular mechanisms fundamentally differ from protein-based systems (Oh, 2013). Gelatin, a polypeptide derived from the partial hydrolysis of animal collagen, undergoes thermoreversible gelation; it dissolves with heating and reforms a three-dimensional network upon cooling (Lee, 2025), thereby imparting characteristic elasticity and masticatory properties (Wu et al., 2023). Conversely, gwapyeon utilizes starch as the primary structural polymer (Irani et al., 2019). The textural attributes of starch gels are governed by physicochemical transformations during thermal processing and subsequent retrogradation.

Thermal processing of starch induces sequential structural transitions, including hydration, gelatinization, amylose leaching, and reassociation (Cai et al., 2014; Sparla et al., 2014). These transformations directly dictate critical quality-determining attributes, including firmness, clearness, water-holding capacity, and storage stability (Xia et al., 2024). Amylose and amylopectin, the primary component polysaccharides, fulfill distinct roles in network formation and retrogradation behavior (Martens et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023). Amylose facilitates the formation of a rigid gel matrix; however, elevated amylose concentrations accelerate retrogradation during storage, increasing firmness (Horstmann et al., 2017; Zhao et al., 2022). In contrast, amylopectin exhibits slower recrystallization kinetics, maintaining gel viscosity while enhancing water-holding capacity (Lu et al., 2024). Furthermore, starch source-dependent variations in the amylose-amylopectin ratio, granular morphology, crystalline polymorphs, and gelatinization temperature diversify gel properties (Martens et al., 2018; Wang et al., 2023).

Mung bean starch serves as a traditional substrate for gwapyeon; however, prohibitive costs and rapid retrogradation frequently deteriorate quality (Gong et al., 2024). Consequently, the effects of alternative starches, such as those from corn and potato, on functional characteristics require investigation. The quality of starch-gel confectioneries depends not only on botanical origin but also on extrinsic factors, including pH, soluble solid content (Brix), and low-molecular-weight solutes (Vardhan et al., 2025). Acidic environments modify gelatinization kinetics and viscosity; excessive acidity may induce partial hydrolysis of glycosidic bonds, thereby weakening gels (Hirashima et al., 2005). Sugars modulate network density through competitive hydration, influencing water-holding capacity and viscoelasticity in a concentration-dependent manner (Ploypetchara and Gohtani, 2018). Additionally, liquid ingredients containing organic acids and bioactive compounds collectively influence starch gelation and oxidative stability (Kim and Shin, 2011).

Recent advancements in semi-solid delivery systems have integrated functional beverages into gel formulations. Commercial products, such as BacchusⓇ (Dong-A Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 2026) and Vita500-flavored jellies (Kwangdong Pharmaceutical Co., Ltd., 2026), demonstrate the feasibility of adding complex functional liquids into hydrogel systems. These trends suggest a paradigm for modernizing traditional starch-gel-based confectioneries (Cho et al., 2026). Red BullⓇ, a globally recognized energy beverage, was selected as a model functional component for gwapyeon formulation (Roldán et al., 2018). It contains caffeine, taurine, sucrose, and B-group vitamins, providing specific sensory attributes－such as acidity and flavor－that potentially substitute for fruit juice (Wesnes et al., 2017). However, the interactions between energy beverage components and starch-based gel networks need systematic research.

The present study aimed to develop a functional gwapyeon by substituting conventional fruit juice with Red BullⓇ while maintaining the traditional starch-gel-based format. More specifically, it examined the influence of starch botanical source and concentration on gel formation and quality attributes of a complex acidic beverage matrix containing sugars, organic acids, and functional ingredients. It was therefore designed to elucidate starch-dependent quality modulation within a beverage-incorporated starch-gel system, with emphasis on formulation behavior rather than sensory acceptability. The effects of starch source and concentration on physicochemical properties－including pH, Brix, chromaticity, syneresis, and antioxidant capacity－were systematically evaluated to elucidate structure-property relationships (Lee et al., 2025). Additionally, consumer sensory attributes, including clearness, sweetness, sourness, and overall acceptance, were also assessed to determine product applicability (Lee et al., 2025). This comprehensive evaluation may provide formulation-oriented insights into the diversification of traditional confectioneries via the incorporation of functional beverage ingredients.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Formulation and preparation of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was prepared by utilizing a base mixture of Red BullⓇ (Red Bull GmbH) diluted to 50% with distilled water. This solution was combined with sucrose (CJ CheilJedang) and any one of three starches: corn (Soyeon Foods), mung bean (Chungwonfood), or potato (Cheongeun F&B) derived. Starch concentrations used were optimized by a preliminary study (Han et al., 2006; Lee et al., 2015). Mung bean starch was incorporated at lower concentrations (7%－9%) compared to corn and potato starches (9%－11%) due to its superior amylose content and gel-forming capacity (Li et al., 2023; Qiao et al., 2024). Preliminary trials indicated that excessive concentrations of mung bean starch resulted in gels with hyper-rigid structures, which impeded thermal processing and molding. The formulations are specified in Table 1 (Lee et al., 2015; Kang et al., 2023). The starch quantities for each group were,

• Corn starch: 36 g (RC9), 40 g (RC10), and 44 g (RC11)

• Mung bean starch: 28 g (RM7), 32 g (RM8), and 36 g (RM9)

• Potato starch: 36 g (RP9), 40 g (RP10), and 44 g (RP11)

For each type, starch was dispersed in 200 mL each of Red BullⓇ and distilled water; 80 g of sucrose was then added. The mixture was gently stirred at 60 strokes/min under thermal processing until boiling, and simmered for 2.5 min over low heat. The paste was transferred into 15×15 cm square molds for gelation. Samples were set at room temperature for 4 h, followed by refrigeration at 4°C for an additional 4 h before analysis.

Physicochemical and chromatic properties

To determine the pH, 5 g of gwapyeon was homogenized with 45 mL of distilled water and agitated in a shaker incubator (Hanbaek Scientific Co.) for 16 h. After centrifugation at 3,000 g for 10 min (Hanil Science Co., Ltd.), the pH of the supernatant was measured with a digital pH meter (Orion Star A211, Thermo Fisher Scientific). Brix was determined using a digital refractometer (Hanna Instruments, Inc.). Hunter color parameters (L*, a*, b*) were quantified utilizing a colorimeter (LC100, Tintometer Ltd.) after standardization of the settings and refrigeration periods.

Syneresis analysis

Syneresis was quantified by weighing the liquid exuded during storage at 4°C over a 5-day period. Cuboid samples－2×2×2 cm－were monitored at designated intervals. At each time point, the freshly exuded liquid was collected with a pipette and weighed. Therefore, the values reported represent incremental syneresis at each interval rather than cumulative water loss during storage. The degree of syneresis was calculated as the weight percentage of separated liquid relative to that of the initial total gel.

Antioxidant properties

Antioxidant capacity of 70% ethanol extracts was evaluated. Briefly, 5 g of sample was homogenized with 45 mL of solvent and extracted for 16 h at room temperature. Following centrifugation at 3,000 g for 10 min, the supernatant was analyzed for total phenolic content (TPC) and total flavonoid content (TFC) (Lee et al., 2024); they were expressed as µg gallic acid equivalents (GAE)/mL and µg quercetin equivalents (QE)/mL, respectively. Free radical scavenging activity was assessed via the 2,2’-azino-bis(3-ethylbenzothiazoline-6-sulfonic acid) (ABTS) assay and expressed as mg Trolox equivalents (TE)/mL (Lee et al., 2025).

Texture profile analysis (TPA)

The texture of 1 cm3 cuboid samples was characterized using a texture analyzer (TA-XT2, Stable Micro Systems) equipped with a 30 mm diameter cylindrical probe. A two-cycle compression test was executed at 60% strain with a trigger force of 0.5 N. The test speed was maintained at 1 mm/s, while pre- and post-test speeds were set to 2 mm/s. The parameters measured included hardness, adhesiveness, resilience, springiness, cohesiveness, gumminess, and chewiness.

Consumer preference evaluation

Sensory evaluation involved 50 untrained university students (ages 20－29) following approval by the Institutional Review Board of Kunsan National University, Gunsan, South Korea (IRB No.: 1040117-202509-HR-021-02). Written informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to the sensory evaluation. Samples were served individually, and water was provided for gargling between trials. Participants rated attributes－including color, clearness, sweetness, sourness, starchiness, chewiness, residual characteristics, overall acceptance, and purchase intention－on a 9-point hedonic scale (1=extremely dislike and 9=extremely like).

Statistical analysis

Experimental data were analyzed using SPSS version 26.0 (IBM Corp.). All measurements were performed in triplicate and reported as the mean±standard deviation. Statistical significance of the differences among formulations was determined via one-way analysis of variance, followed by Tukey’s honestly significant difference post-hoc test. Statistical significance was defined at P<0.05.

RESULTS

pH and Brix of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 2 presents the pH and Brix values of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon prepared with varying types and concentrations of starch. The pH values ranged from 3.74 to 3.82 across formulations. Corn starch-based formulations exhibited values of 3.82±0.01 (RC9), 3.79±0.04 (RC10), and 3.82±0.03 (RC11). Mung bean starch-based formulations demonstrated relatively lower pH values, specifically 3.74±0.01 (RM7), 3.75±0.02 (RM8), and 3.76±0.02 (RM9). Potato starch samples showed intermediate values of 3.77±0.02 (RP9), 3.81±0.02 (RP10), and 3.80±0.02 (RP11). Statistical analysis did not reveal any significant differences in pH among formulations.

Brix values ranged from 2.10 to 2.40 across treatments. Corn starch samples yielded values of 2.27±0.06 (RC9), 2.40±0.10 (RC10), and 2.27±0.06 (RC11). Mung bean starch samples showed values of 2.20±0.17 (RM7), 2.20±0.00 (RM8), and 2.17±0.12 (RM9). Potato starch samples exhibited values of 2.13±0.15 (RP9), 2.10±0.17 (RP10), and 2.20±0.00 (RP11). The Brix values did not vary significantly among samples. Overall, these findings suggest that pH and soluble solids were primarily determined by the Red BullⓇ-based matrix rather than by the starch formulation.

Hunter color values of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

The Hunter color parameters for all formulations are summarized in Table 3. Lightness (L*) values ranged from 37.67 to 46.33 across formulations. Corn starch samples exhibited relatively lower L* values (37.67－39.40), while mung bean starch samples showed moderately greater L* values (41.77－42.43). Potato starch samples displayed the widest range (39.83－46.33), with RP10 demonstrating maximum lightness (46.33±0.32; P<0.05).

The a* values indicative of redness/greenness ranged from −2.83 to 2.63. Corn and mung bean starch samples exhibited negative a* values (−2.83 to −2.10), indicating a slight bias toward green. In contrast, potato starch samples showed positive a* values (0.47－2.63), which were significantly higher than those of corn and mung bean starch samples (P<0.05). The b* values suggestive of yellowness/blueness ranged from 0.30 to 6.23. Corn and mung bean starch samples demonstrated relatively low b* values (RC: 0.67－1.77; RM: 0.30－2.13), whereas potato starch samples exhibited significantly greater values (3.57－6.23). RP11 reached the maximum b* value (6.23±1.43; P<0.05). Generally, corn starch samples tended toward lower L*, a*, and b* values, whereas potato starch samples revealed higher overall chromaticity.

Syneresis of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon during storage

Table 4 summarizes the syneresis behavior of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon during 5 days of storage. Syneresis values over the storage period ranged from 0.00% to 12.46%. All samples except RM8 exhibited significant time-dependent changes (P<0.05).

Corn starch samples exhibited marked syneresis on day 1, followed by lower daily water release during refrigerated storage. RC9 showed the greatest initial syneresis (12.46±0.22% on day 1), which declined progressively: 8.17±0.74% on day 2; 4.97±0.23% on day 3; 2.54±0.73% on day 4; and 1.08±0.27% on day 5. RC10 followed a similar pattern (12.28% to 1.06% from day 1 to day 5). Conversely, RC11 showed a lower initial value (4.42±0.71% on day 1), which remained relatively stable during storage.

Mung bean starch samples exhibited the lowest syneresis levels. RM7 showed a moderate decline from 6.18±2.80% (day 1) to 2.12±0.80% (day 5). RM8 exhibited minimal syneresis (0.05% to 1.24%) without marked fluctuations. RM9 did not reveal any detectable syneresis on day 1, followed by a gradual minor increase to 1.87±0.42% by day 5. Potato starch samples exhibited variable patterns; RP9 showed a reduction from 8.06±1.64% (day 1) to 3.65±0.56% (day 5), whereas RP10 demonstrated negligible initial syneresis (0.00%) that enhanced slightly to 1.69±0.56% (day 5). Overall, corn starch samples showed the maximum initial syneresis, followed by reduced incremental water loss during storage. In contrast, mung bean starch samples showed consistently low incremental syneresis, suggesting greater water retention over the test storage period.

Antioxidant capacities of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

Table 5 presents the antioxidant-related properties, including TPC, TFC, and ABTS radical scavenging capacity. TPC values ranged from 72.50 to 112.77 µg GAE/mL. Corn starch samples exhibited relatively higher phenolic content, with RC9 showing the maximum value (112.77±19.06 µg GAE/mL), followed by RC11 (108.13±12.97 µg GAE/mL) and RC10 (98.80±0.74 µg GAE/mL). These values for mung bean starch samples were variable; RM7 and RM9 presented high levels (∼110 µg GAE/mL), while RM8 showed the lowest value (72.50±0.21 µg GAE/mL). Potato starch samples possessed reduced phenolic contents, ranging from 83.46 to 98.73 µg GAE/mL.

TFC values remained relatively uniform, ranging from 51.34 to 57.55 µg QE/mL, with RP9 exhibiting the highest value. ABTS radical scavenging capacity ranged from 2.07 to 3.75 mg TE/mL. Corn starch samples revealed values of 2.32±0.13 (RC9), 2.75±0.08 (RC10), and 3.75±0.84 (RC11) mg TE/mL, with RC11 showing the maximum activity. Such values for mung bean starch ranged from 2.94 to 3.54 mg TE/mL, whereas potato starch samples demonstrated the lowest ABTS activities (2.07－2.33 mg TE/mL).

Textural properties of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

The textural properties of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon were systematically analyzed via TPA, as summarized in Table 6, with significant variations across parameters, including hardness, adhesiveness, resilience, cohesiveness, springiness, gumminess, and chewiness (P<0.05).

Hardness values of the formulations spanned a broad range from 74.95 to 539.72 g, with mung bean starch samples exhibiting the highest overall hardness－particularly RM9 (539.72±14.12 g), followed by RM8 (296.97±12.93 g) and RM7 (141.93±5.98 g)－while corn starch samples demonstrated intermediate values (RC11: 145.53±4.59 g; RC10: 118.06±4.41 g; RC9: 77.10±6.20 g) and potato starch samples yielded the lowest values (RP11: 98.79±4.25 g; RP10: 82.16±3.80 g; RP9: 74.95±2.19 g).

Adhesiveness ranged from −8.54 to 1.31 g·s, with mung bean starch samples displaying generally more negative values, among which RM9 reached the lowest (−8.54±0.70 g·s). However, corn starch samples fell within the −2.83 to −4.62 g·s range, and potato starch samples exhibited comparatively higher values of −1.40 to 1.31 g·s.

Resilience values, which fluctuated between 15.48% and 40.02%, reached their peak in sample RM9 (40.02±5.38%) and their trough in RM7 (15.48±1.48%). In contrast, corn and potato starch samples demonstrated intermediate resilience, generally ranging from 16% to 21%.

Cohesiveness values varied from 0.04 to 0.55, with RM8 attaining the highest cohesiveness (0.55±0.11) and RP9 reaching the lowest (0.04±0.04). However, corn starch treatments maintained relatively stable values between 0.27 and 0.32, and mung bean samples－RM7 and RM9－yielded results of 0.38±0.02 and 0.41±0.04, respectively.

Springiness coefficients were measured between 66.89% and 98.70%, where mung bean starch samples stood out for their high springiness (94.14%－98.70%), whereas those of potato starch samples ranged from 79.96% to 97.56%. Corn starch samples exhibited notably lower values between 66.89% and 78.93%.

Gumminess values extended from 19.30 to 152.85, again dominated by RM9 (152.85±9.76) and RM8 (90.42±4.36), while corn (24.82－42.69) and potato starch samples (19.30－33.56) provided remarkably lower results.

Chewiness exhibited a parallel trend, ranging from 16.95 to 138.18, with the maximum values concentrated in the mung bean starch group (RM9: 138.18±9.20; RM8: 88.28±4.55; RM7: 43.99±4.40). However, corn and potato starch samples remained within a markedly lower range of 16.95 to 43.61.

In synthesis, the textural profile of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was profoundly influenced by the type and concentration of starch utilized. Mung bean starch fundamentally enhanced hardness, springiness, gumminess, and chewiness, whereas corn and potato starches produced gels with prominently softer and less elastic characteristics.

Consumer preference of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon

The consumer sensory evaluation scores for Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon are detailed in Table 7, illustrating the multifaceted impacts of starch type and concentration on subjective quality attributes. Color preference scores fluctuated between 3.32 and 4.20 across the sample set, with RC9 (4.20±1.68), RC10 (4.02±1.42), and RP10 (3.93±1.74) being more visually appealing, whereas RM9 (3.32±1.46) received the lowest rating; however, these variations did not reach statistical significance. Similarly, clearness scores remained within a narrow range of 3.34 to 3.73, with RP9 (3.73±2.10) and RM7 (3.71±1.79) trending higher than RC11 (3.34±1.65); yet no marked differences were observed among diverse formulations. In contrast to visual parameters, sweetness perception exhibited significant variations among samples (P<0.05), with ratings ranging from 2.66 to 4.61, as RC9 (4.61±1.90) and RP11 (4.17±2.05) were perceived as notably sweeter than RM8 (2.66±1.35) and RM9 (2.71±1.60). Sourness scores likewise displayed variations, spanning from 2.22 to 3.76, where RP9 was identified as the most acidic sample (3.76±2.11), with RM9 demonstrating the minimum score (2.22±1.37). Starchiness scores, ranging from 2.66 to 4.44, were greatest in the potato starch group－specifically RP9 (4.44±2.09), RP11 (4.12±2.03), and RP10 (3.93±2.09)－while RM8 (2.66±1.49) was rated significantly lower by panelists (P<0.05). Sensory chewiness perception varied substantially between 2.63 and 6.10 (P<0.05), with RM9 (6.10±2.23) and RM8 (5.00±2.14) identified as the most cohesive and resilient. In contrast, potato starch samples RP10 (2.63±1.11) and RP9 (2.68±1.33) were perceived as the least chewy. Residual mouthfeel characteristics ranged from 2.15 to 3.90, with RP11 (3.90±1.99) and RP9 (3.78±2.02) exhibiting higher scores than RC9 (2.15±1.24), indicating significantly distinct variations in how the sensory perceptions of the formulations lingered post-consumption. Regarding holistic evaluations, overall acceptance values ranged from 2.68 to 4.10, whereby RC9 achieved the maximum consumer favorability (4.10±2.01), and RP11 received the lowest rating (2.68±1.40), showing statistically significant disparities among experimental groups (P<0.05). Purchase intention mirrored these findings as it ranged from 2.44 to 3.80, with RC9 emerging as the most commercially viable formulation (3.80±2.05) and RP9 demonstrating the lowest score (2.44±1.48). Ultimately, a comprehensive sensory analysis confirmed that while color and clearness remained relatively consistent, the variables of sweetness, sourness, starchiness, chewiness, residual attributes, and overall marketability were profoundly influenced by the specific starch matrix employed.

DISCUSSION

Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon was formulated to investigate the technical feasibility of integrating a commercial energy beverage with a traditional starch-based gel matrix while maintaining requisite physicochemical and sensory standards. The present findings demonstrate that, within such a beverage-based matrix, starch source plays a major role in determining gel network behavior, moisture stability, antioxidant retention, mechanical texture, and consumer perception. Substitution of conventional fruit juices with a widely consumed functional beverage successfully merged modern liquid supplements with traditional confectionery systems. Identical thermal- and preparation-related parameters were applied to ensure a rigorous comparison, thereby isolating starch-dependent structural differences from processing-induced variability. Corn, mung bean, and potato starches were specifically selected due to their divergent granular morphologies, swelling behaviors, and gelation kinetics, which uniquely dictate the structural development of the gel matrix (Ilhan et al., 2020). Consequently, this study provides formulation-oriented and scientifically relevant insights into how starch-specific properties shape quality attributes in acidic beverage-based gel systems, including physicochemical characteristics, antioxidant capacity, textural profile, and consumer acceptability.

Analysis of pH and soluble solids did not vary markedly among formulations. As all samples contained the Red BullⓇ beverage-based matrix, these results suggest that pH and soluble solids were primarily governed by the composition of the common beverage and were only minimally affected by starch type or concentration. Starch characteristics, notably a chemically neutral profile and minimal buffering capacity, rendered the substitution of various starch types insufficient to alter the hydrogen ion concentration within the acidic gel environment (Pekdogan Goztok et al., 2024; Székelyhidi et al., 2024). In contrast, chromatic attributes exhibited distinct starch-dependent variations, with potato starch samples displaying higher L*, a*, and b* values compared to corn or mung bean starch treatments. Extensive swelling of potato starch granules during gelatinization, attributed to negatively charged phosphate groups present on adjacent chains that enhance granule hydration (Swinkels, 1985; Kaur et al., 2015), likely enhanced color expression by producing highly translucent gels that allowed beverage-derived pigments to be perceived more clearly (Joshi et al., 2013; Shen et al., 2024). Conversely, enhanced turbidity and internal light scattering characteristics of corn and mung bean starch matrices, characterized by higher amylose contents and restricted granular swelling (Kaur et al., 2015; Jia et al., 2023), reduced apparent color intensity, identifying starch-specific opacity as a primary determinant of visual quality.

Syneresis behavior during storage was remarkably influenced by the starch type. Corn starch samples caused a high initial incremental moisture release followed by a gradual decline in daily water loss. Such a pattern may reflect the early release of loosely bound or free water (Pulgarín et al., 2023). Conversely, mung bean starch samples demonstrated consistently low syneresis throughout the observation period, attributable to their relatively enhanced amylose content, superior water-binding capacity and the formation of a highly stable gel network that effectively restricts water mobility (Li et al., 2023; Agvaandorj et al., 2025). Potato starch formulations displayed intermediate syneresis, likely due to the extensive hydration of granules within a relatively low-density matrix that permits delayed moisture separation (Cui et al., 2014; Nagasaki et al., 2021). Furthermore, the reduced incremental syneresis in a few high-starch formulations suggests that gel solids may increasingly contribute to suppressed daily water release (Scott and Awika, 2023). Collectively, these findings suggest that moisture stability may be associated with starch-specific gel network formation and potential retrogradation behavior.

The antioxidant activity of gwapyeon also fluctuated per the starch type and its relative concentration. Higher TPC and ABTS radical scavenging were generally observed in corn and mung bean starch samples, whereas potato starch samples demonstrated relatively lower values. Dense and stable gel networks formed by corn and mung bean starches, likely due to their relatively elevated amylose content (Li et al., 2023), may explain this disparity. These matrices may contribute to the apparent retention of beverage-derived antioxidant compounds (Gisbert et al., 2022; Agvaandorj et al., 2025). However, direct confirmation would require additional structural and release analyses. In contrast, the low-density structural framework produced by potato starch gels, caused by substantial granular swelling (Shen et al., 2024; Agvaandorj et al., 2025), potentially leads to a dilution of bioactives and a subsequent decline in measurable antioxidant capacity (Zheng et al., 2024). Meanwhile, the relative stability of TFC across samples further supports the conclusion that while beverage composition provides the initial antioxidant load, the starch-dependent matrix structure dictates the final retention efficiency.

The TPA results revealed clear mechanical distinctions based on starch source. Mung bean starch samples, particularly RM9, exhibited remarkably higher hardness, gumminess, and chewiness, suggesting the formation of a much stronger gel network, likely associated with the relatively enhanced amylose content of mung bean starch (Li et al., 2023). Conversely, potato starch samples, in which phosphate groups promote granule hydration and extensive swelling to form a low-density structural framework (Swinkels, 1985; Kaur et al., 2015), produced much softer gels with minimal structural resistance to mechanical deformation (Zhang et al., 2017). In contrast, corn starch samples maintained a balanced textural profile that avoided the extremes of excessive rigidity or softness. Increasing starch concentration generally promoted the formation of a more robust three-dimensional network during gelatinization, thereby enhancing resistance to compression (Paker and Matak, 2017). Collectively, these findings underscore the critical role of starch selection in engineering specific mechanical properties required for traditional starch-gel-based confectioneries.

Sensory evaluation confirmed that the mechanical properties of the gel matrix profoundly influence consumer perception and overall marketability (Gondal et al., 2021). Significant variations were identified across subjective attributes, including sweetness, sourness, starchiness, chewiness, residual characteristics, overall acceptance, and purchase intention. Corn starch samples generally achieved the maximum scores for overall acceptance and purchase intention, suggesting that a balanced textural profile and moderate sweetness are more favored by consumers (Gondal et al., 2021). In contrast, excessive chewiness and hardness associated with high-concentration mung bean starch samples did not translate to improved preference, indicating that overly rigid structures may adversely impact palatability (Nakatomi et al., 2023). Furthermore, enhanced starchiness and residual mouthfeel characteristic of potato starch formulations contributed to lower consumer ratings, reinforcing the idea that textural harmony is essential for modernizing traditional desserts.

A clear sequential relationship may be established between starch molecular structure and final sensory acceptance. Variations in starch type influence initial hydration and network formation, which may affect liquid mobility and syneresis during storage. These moisture-associated dynamics, in turn, could be associated with a retention of antioxidant components and the gel matrix density, ultimately shaping textural resistance perceived during consumption. These instrumental properties, in combination with residual moisture characteristics, define the consumer’s sensory experience and determine the overall formulation success. Among the tested groups, RC9 exhibited the most optimal balance between stability and acceptance, demonstrating that starch selection is a fundamental formulation parameter rather than a mere structural necessity.

Despite the comprehensive nature of this study, several limitations warrant consideration for future research. As this study did not include a beverage-free control formulation, the independent impacts of Red BullⓇ components could not be fully distinguished from starch-dependent effects. Future studies must include beverage-free starch gels and beverage-only reference samples to clarify the specific interactions between beverage components and starch networks. Moreover, starch at varying concentration ranges was applied to mung bean starch versus corn and potato starches because the strong gel-forming ability of the former required lower concentrations to obtain processable gel formulations. Although this approach was necessary from a formulation perspective, it limits a direct comparison among starch types in gwapyeon as concentration-relevant effects cannot be fully distinguished from starch source-derived effects. In addition, the evaluation of storage stability was confined to a relatively short duration; therefore, long-term storage studies are necessary to fully understand the influence of extended retrogradation on product quality. Additionally, the use of a relatively homogeneous consumer panel for sensory evaluation may limit the generalizability of the findings to a broader, more diverse population. Future investigations utilizing microstructural characterization, such as scanning electron microscopy, and advanced rheological analysis would provide deeper mechanistic insights into the relationships between starch composition, gel network density, and the retention of bioactive compounds. Nevertheless, the current findings offer a robust framework for the diversification and modernization of traditional starch-gel-based confectioneries.

In conclusion, the quality-associated characteristics of Red BullⓇ-incorporated gwapyeon are largely influenced by starch type, with starch concentration serving as a secondary influence. Unique granule structure and swelling behavior of each starch type dictate the formation of a gel network, which subsequently modulates color expression, moisture stability, mechanical attributes, and consumer preference. Cornstarch formulations most effectively balanced structural integrity and sensory appeal. Mung bean starch increased gel strength but did not consistently improve consumer preference at higher concentrations, while potato starch produced softer gels that achieved lower acceptance despite robust color expression. Ultimately, these results indicate that careful starch selection is a critical requirement for optimizing the structural and functional quality of innovative gel-type desserts.

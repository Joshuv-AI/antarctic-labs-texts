A Case Report on Pakshaghata: Ayurvedic Intervention in Hemorrhagic Hemiplegia Management
Authors: Shweta Parwe, Milind Nisargandha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTA more well-known adverse effect of severe acute head or neck trauma that tends to occur in patients in auto accidents is traumatic cerebral hemorrhage. Hemiplegia, paraplegia, entire paralysis, or extreme conditions that lead to coma can all be linked to it. We document a male patient, who had acute left-sided hemiplegia at presentation.

CASE PRESENTATION

A 50-year-old man who was resident of Sawangi Meghe, Wardha, arrived to the Panchakarma out patient door (OPD) in Mahatma Gandhi Ayurved College Hospital (MGACH) in a wheelchair and showed a history of road traffic accident (RTA) for one and a half years previously to a check-up. Following the clinical examination, the following symptoms were noticed: fragility of the facial muscles, involving the left side of the face, deviation of mouth, stiffness in the upper shoulder joint, elbow joint, wrist, and finger joints, being unable to catch items with the left hand, impaired speech, and struggling to walk.

History of illness

Prior to one and a half years, the patient had no concerns. He encountered the RTA after a year and a half. and a history of head injuries. The patient was unconscious upon his arrival was taken to the hospital, and a CT scan of his brain revealed that his right frontal and temporal lobes were causing a diffuse mass effect, which manifested as sulcogyral effacement of the right cerebral hemisphere. He had undergone previous treatment for his head injury at an allopathic hospital. He received standard care, but his hemiparalysis symptoms barely improved. He appeared at Panchakarma OPD with the earlier listed symptoms due to the weakness in his upper and lower limbs, which prevented him from walking. He was admitted to the IPD department (OPD No. 2206200032 UID no. 2200007035) for additional management.

Personal history

The patient had become accustomed to eating dry, spicy meals. There had been no history of addiction. He was tired and his sleep had been interrupted.

DISCUSSION

“Aghata (paralysis)” denotes the impairment of Karmandriyas (activity), Gyanendriyas (sense), and Manas (brain), whereas “paksha” pertains to either half of the body. However, the name Paksaghata literally means “paralysis of one half of the body.” Gyanendriyas are a part of the sensory system, whereas Karmendriyas are a subset of the motor system.[1]

Due to abhighat (trauma) in Shiro-murdha Pradesh (Head), this case study came up with pakshaghat. Dosa Vata the left half of the body does Vishoshana of Shira and Snayu and the right half undertakes Shithilan of Sandhi Bandhana throughout Prokop, prakupita Vayu. Higher ruksha guna causes of parushta and rukshata. This is essentially one of the significant factors contributing to Pakshaghat’s samprapti.

Consequently, we initiated shaman and shodhana based on dosha. The main treatment for Vata vyadhi, according to Acharya Charak is Basti. The vitiated Vata becomes calmed by it. Nasya karma nourishes the nervous system and it has a major impact on pakshaghat, matra, and sahsneha niruha. Physiotherapy assist strengthens the muscular system, while minimizing stiffness and rigidity of all joints. Basti and Vatshamak shaman aushadhi both provide highly effective results in Pakshaghat.

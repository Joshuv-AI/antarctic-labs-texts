Identification and external validation of a prognostic signature based on MAPK-related genes to evaluate survival prognosis and treatment efficacy in lung adenocarcinoma
Authors: Zijian Hu, Yajie Zhou, Lei Xie, Wenxiong Zhang, Haiwei Rao
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) pathway plays a pivotal role in tumorigenesis and immune regulation. However, its prognostic significance in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains poorly defined. This study aimed to construct a robust MAPK-related gene (MRG) signature by integrating multi-omics data to enhance risk stratification and therapeutic guidance in LUAD.MethodsDifferentially expressed MRGs were identified from TCGA-LUAD transcriptomic data and prioritized through Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis. A prognostic model was constructed using the random survival forest (RSF) algorithm and validated across three independent Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) cohorts. Additional analyses were developed including pathway enrichment, drug sensitivity prediction, reverse transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) validation, and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) to uncover its mechanistic basis and clinical value.ResultsThe MRG-based model effectively stratified patients into high-risk (HRG) and low-risk groups (LRG) with significant differences in overall survival (P < 0.001). The nomogram-derived risk score outperformed clinical factors in predicting outcomes, reflecting strong prognostic capability of the model. HRG exhibited elevated tumor mutational burden (TMB), enrichment of PI3K-Akt signaling, both of which may be associated with its poorer prognosis. Drug sensitivity profiling suggested that LRGs were more responsive to PI3K/mTOR inhibitors, whereas HRGs favored tyrosine kinase inhibitors. scRNA-seq analysis revealed that MRGs were mainly enriched in endothelial cell populations, implicating their role in immune modulation and angiogenesis.ConclusionsThis integrative multi-omics-based prognostic model provides robust predictive power and novel biological insights, serving as a practical tool for personalized prognosis evaluation and targeted therapeutic decision-making in LUAD.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10238-025-01925-3.

Introduction

Lung cancer continues to account for the highest mortality among all cancers, with a persistently growing number of new cases each year. Notably, in individuals under the age of 65, the incidence among women surpassed that of men in 2021, with lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) being particularly prominent [1]. Despite advances in LUAD therapies targeting specific driver genes, the development of resistance to targeted therapies continues to pose an inevitable obstacle in clinical management [2]. The tumor node metastasis (TNM) staging system is widely recognized as a standard approach for assessing disease progression and predicting patient prognosis [3]. However, this staging system has notable limitations in accurately predicting patient outcomes, reducing its utility in optimizing treatment strategies. Consequently, identifying new biomarkers to assess therapeutic efficacy and forecast prognosis in LUAD patients has become a critical priority.

Mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) cascades, composed of three consecutively activated kinase tiers, are essential elements in signaling networks that govern cellular functions such as proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis across eukaryotic species—from unicellular organisms like yeast to humans [4]. These pathways are typically activated by distinct extracellular cues, leading to a stepwise activation of MAPK kinases that mediate the transmission of extracellular and intracellular signals to regulate multiple cell behaviors [5]. Upstream signals, such as EGF, FGF, and ALK, activate their corresponding receptors, which in turn activate downstream signaling components including KRAS, MEK, and ERK1/2. KRAS-GTP activates MEK, which subsequently phosphorylates ERK1/2. Activated ERK1/2 regulates various transcription factors, such as MYC and ELK1, thereby promoting cell proliferation, migration, angiogenesis, and modulating apoptosis. The proposed mechanism is illustrated in Fig. 1. Previous studies have shown that features based on the MAPK family can serve as effective prognostic tools and predict responses to targeted therapies in some cancers, such as glioma and renal clear cell carcinoma [6, 7]. To date, there has been no research dedicated to developing a prognostic model for LUAD utilizing MAPK-related genes (MRGs).Fig. 1Mechanistic illustration of the MAPK pathway involved in lung adenocarcinoma

Building upon this framework, we integrated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) with Mendelian randomization (MR) analysis to identify intersecting genes with potential causal relevance. A total of 117 combinations of machine learning algorithms were comprehensively evaluated to screen for key prognostic genes and construct a prediction model, which demonstrated strong prognostic performance across multiple independent cohorts. To further explore the underlying mechanisms and assess clinical relevance, we performed a series of downstream analyses, including immune microenvironment profiling, reverse transcription quantitative polymerase chain reaction (RT-qPCR), and drug sensitivity prediction. In addition, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) facilitated the characterization of tumor heterogeneity and identification of distinct cell populations, thereby enhancing the biological resolution of the prognostic model.

Materials and methods

Data collection and handling

Clinical and transcriptomic data of LUAD were retrieved from The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) database (https://portal.gdc.cancer.gov/, accessed until March 1st, 2025) [8]. In addition, three external validation cohorts (GSE30219, GSE72094, and GSE50081) and a scRNA-seq cohort (GSE189357) were gathered through the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/, accessed until March 1st, 2025) [9].

To screen DEGs, we initially retrieved 9,475 MRGs from the GeneCards database (https://www.genecards.org/, accessed until March 1st, 2025) using the keyword “mitogen-activated protein kinase.” Then, gene expression data from TCGA-LUAD samples were processed with the “limma” R package [10], and DEGs were selected for downstream analysis using the thresholds of |log2 fold change|> 0.585 and a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.05.

For MR analysis, LUAD-related data were acquired from the Integrative Epidemiology Unit Genome-Wide Association Studies (IEU GWAS) database (https://gwas.mrcieu.ac.uk/) [11, 12]. The GWAS dataset encompassed expression quantitative trait loci (eQTL) information corresponding to 19,942 genes. Summary statistics for LUAD outcomes in a European cohort (11,273 cases and 55,483 controls; Study ID: “ebi-a-GCST004744,” comprising 7,849,324 SNPs) were also obtained from the publicly accessible IEU GWAS repository.

Evaluation of causal inference via MR

To construct eQTL-based instrumental variables (IVs) for genes, we selected SNPs significantly associated with exposure (P < 5e−08) using summary statistics from GWAS data [13]. To minimize potential bias from strong linkage disequilibrium during MR analysis, SNPs within a 10,000 kb window showing r2 > 0.001 with the lead SNP were excluded. Additionally, to mitigate the influence of weak instruments on causal inference, SNPs with F-statistics ≤ 10 were filtered out.

The “TwoSampleMR” R package was applied to conduct five MR approaches exploring the causal association between eQTLs and LUAD, offering causal estimates under varying assumptions and algorithmic frameworks [14]. To evaluate pleiotropy, heterogeneity, influential SNPs, and result stability, a range of sensitivity analyses was carried out. The Cochran’s Q statistic was specifically used to evaluate heterogeneity across the SNP instrumental variables, where P < 0.05 indicated significant heterogeneity [15]. Funnel plots were utilized to visualize potential directional bias that might affect causal inference [16], and leave-one-out analysis was applied to assess each SNP’s impact on the overall causal inference. Horizontal pleiotropy was further examined using the MR-Egger intercept; P < 0.05 was interpreted as evidence of pleiotropy, thereby invalidating the assumptions underlying the MR analysis.

Construction of a prognostic risk model derived from MRGs

We initially selected the overlapping genes between the MR analysis outcomes and DEGs, and subsequently combined them with expression profiles to establish a risk scoring system for LUAD patients. Using TCGA-LUAD and GEO data, we extracted expression profiles of these intersecting genes along with corresponding survival status and time from clinical records. Also, we initially evaluated multiple algorithmic approaches—including the Cox proportional hazards model, penalized regression methods, support vector machines, and ensemble survival analysis. Building on this, we constructed an ensemble framework comprising 10 algorithms and systematically compared the resulting 117 algorithmic combinations to enhance the selection of key gene features, including but not limited to stepwise generalized linear model (Stepglm), random survival forest (RSF) [17], support vector machine (SVM), and gradient boosting machine (GBM)—representing a spectrum of both classical and contemporary machine learning approaches. Through rigorous comparative analysis of all algorithm combinations, the RSF model demonstrated superior performance, achieving the highest average area under the curve (AUC), thereby being adopted as the optimal method for both MRG signature selection and classification prediction in our study. To address the concern of potential overfitting, we adopted repeated cross-validation, separated hyperparameter optimization from performance estimation, and assessed feature stability across bootstrapped datasets. These measures ensured that the final model was not the product of random chance.

Prognostic utility evaluation of the MAPK-derived model

Individual risk scores were calculated from the prognostic gene set selected through the optimal algorithm. Using the median risk score as a criterion, patients were segregated into high-risk group (HRG) and low-risk group (LRG). Principal component analysis (PCA) was applied to compress the gene expression profiles into a lower-dimensional space, projecting them onto orthonormal principal components (PC1–3), which are linear combinations of the original gene variables. Following this, we utilized the “limma,” “survival,” and “timeROC” R packages to conduct both univariate Cox (uniCox) and multivariate Cox (multiCox) analyses, generate Kaplan–Meier (K–M) survival curves, risk score plots, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves [18]. Time-dependent ROC analysis was applied to compute the AUC, with AUC values above 0.7 indicating acceptable predictive performance. To further confirm the model's performance, K–M survival analysis assessed its prognostic accuracy across multiple clinical subgroups. In addition, we created scatter plots of risk distributions and gene expression heat maps to observe differences between HRG and LRG.

Nomogram establishment and validation

A predictive nomogram was developed by incorporating risk scores and significant clinical features, utilizing the “rms” R package to estimate survival likelihoods at 1, 3, and 5 years. Its performance was evaluated through calibration plots, decision curve analysis (DCA), and ROC curves. Additionally, the C-index was computed to quantify the consistency between the predicted and actual outcomes, with values approaching 1 indicating superior predictive accuracy [19], thereby demonstrating the nomogram’s clinical utility in patient diagnosis.

Functional annotation and pathway profiling

To investigate functional disparities between the HRG and LRG, it was examined through gene ontology (GO) and Kyoto encyclopedia of genes and genomes (KEGG) pathway analysis [20]. Moreover, gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was implemented by analyzing the expression profiles of nine MRGs to investigate associated biological functions. Enrichment significance was determined with criteria of P < 0.05 and FDR < 0.25 [21].

Tumor mutational burden (TMB)

We employed Strawberry Perl to compute mutation frequencies per million bases in both the HRG and LRG. Mutation landscapes were illustrated through waterfall diagrams created using the "maftools" R package [22]. The Maxstat algorithm was then applied to identify the most appropriate cutoff threshold, which was subsequently integrated with the risk score to assess survival disparities between high and low mutation subgroups within HRG and LRG.

Assessment of immune landscape and therapeutic sensitivity

To evaluate the potential for immune evasion in HRG and LRG patients, we utilized the tumor immune dysfunction and exclusion (TIDE) platform (https://tide.dfci.harvard.edu/), where higher scores reflect reduced responsiveness to immunotherapy. The "ESTIMATE" R package was employed to compare stromal scores, immune scores, ESTIMATE scores, and tumor purity between HRG and LRG. The CIBERSORT algorithm was applied to estimate the relative abundance of 22 distinct immune cell populations [23]. Furthermore, single-sample gene set enrichment analysis (ssGSEA) was utilized to evaluate and compare the activity of 16 immune-associated functional pathways between HRG and LRG.

To predict drug sensitivity, we accessed data from the genomics of drug sensitivity in cancer (GDSC) database and estimated drug response scores for both groups. Drug responsiveness was assessed based on the half maximal inhibitory concentration (IC50), where reduced IC50 values reflect greater sensitivity to the corresponding drug [24].

Single-cell analysis

The scRNA-seq data for LUAD utilized were sourced from the Nucleotide Sequence Archive of the China National Gene Bank [25]. The dataset includes multiple sample files, which were loaded from the directory GSE189357_RAW/.

Data preprocessing was performed with the “Seurat” R package to create Seurat objects. Genes detected in fewer than three cells and those found in cells with less than 50 genes were excluded prior to downstream analysis. The PercentageFeatureSet function was applied to calculate the mitochondrial gene content, and violin plots were generated using the VlnPlot function to visualize gene count distribution across cells. Outliers were filtered based on gene expression range, sequencing depth, and mitochondrial gene content, with a cutoff set at less than 3%. Normalization of gene expression levels was developed using the LogNormalize function, which scales the expression by total cellular counts, multiplies by a factor of 10,000, and applies a log (n + 1) transformation. Dimensionality reduction and batch effect correction were performed using PCA and Harmony, where PCA embedded the cells in a lower-dimensional space and Harmony iteratively adjusted for batch-related variance by selecting an optimal reduction threshold [26]. Clustering was visualized using uniform manifold approximation and projection (UMAP) [27]. The FindNeighbors function calculated intercellular distances, and clustering was performed with FindClusters using an adjustable resolution parameter. Finally, cell types were annotated with the “SingleR” R package.

Cell–cell communication

CellChat (v2.1.2) was used to investigate interactions between endothelial cells and other immune cells in the sample, with particular emphasis on ligand–receptor pairs in which endothelial cells acted as the signaling source [28].

CeRNA regulatory network

MiRNA predictions were carried out using TargetScan (https://www.targetscan.org), miRDB (https://mirbd.org/), and miRWalk (http://mirwalk.umm.uni-heidelberg.de/), and only the intersecting interactions confirmed by all three databases were retained. SpongeScan (https://www.repository.cam.ac.uk/) was applied to predict potential miRNA–lncRNA interactions [29]. The lncRNA–miRNA–mRNA regulatory network was established based on predicted interactions and graphically visualized using Cytoscape.

Verify protein and gene expression of MRGs

Protein expression levels of MRGs in tumor and adjacent normal tissues were obtained from the Human Protein Atlas (HPA, https://www.proteinatlas.org/). LUAD cell lines (A-427, A549, and HCC-44) and the normal cell line (BEAS-2B) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences (CBCAS, Shanghai, China). Total RNA was isolated using TRIzol solution provided by Invitrogen (USA) in combination with a commercial RNA extraction kit. The PrimeScript RT kit (Takara, Japan) was applied to synthesize cDNA through reverse transcription. MRG expression levels were measured using RT-qPCR technique (Table S1), and the relative mRNA levels were quantified using the 2^–ΔΔCt method [30].

To assess the independent prognostic significance of MRGs, we conducted both univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses. Univariate analysis identified MRGs as an independent risk factor (HR = 1.072, 95%CI 1.057–1.087, P < 0.001), multivariate analysis confirmed the independent prognostic value of MRGs after adjusting for confounders (HR = 1.067, 95%CI 1.052–1.082, P < 0.001) (Fig. 4A). GSEA enrichment analysis

GSEA revealed distinct pathway enrichments across various gene sets. In the HRG, the GOBP library showed significant enrichment in functions related to sperm motility, sister chromatid segregation, mitotic sister chromatid segregation, nuclear chromosome segregation, mitotic nuclear division, microtubule bundle formation (Fig. S9A–C).

Survival analysis of TMB

TMB was evaluated in patients, revealing a significantly higher TMB in the HRG (Fig. S10A, B). Samples were classified into high mutation (n = 248) and low mutation (n = 244) groups. When integrated with the risk score, it was observed that 95.16% of LUAD patients in the HRG harbored somatic mutations. Notably, the mutation frequency of the tumor suppressor gene TP53 reached 57% in HRG, markedly exceeding the 41% observed in the LRG (Fig. S10C, D).

Immune cell profiling, functional assessment, and chemosensitivity analysis

TIDE score analysis revealed that the HRG exhibited significantly higher TIDE (P < 0.001) and T cell exclusion scores (P < 0.001) compared to the LRG, while the T cell dysfunction score (P < 0.001) was notably lower (Fig. S9D–F). These findings suggest a greater extent of immune evasion in the HRG. Tumor microenvironment (TME) analysis further demonstrated that HRG had significantly reduced immune (P < 0.0001; Fig. 6A) and ESTIMATE scores (P < 0.05; Fig. 6B), indicating lower overall immune and stromal cell infiltration. In contrast, stromal scores showed no notable difference between the two groups (P > 0.05; Fig. 6D). Consistently, tumor purity was notably elevated in the HRG (P < 0.05; Fig. 6C). These findings suggest that the HRG is associated with a more immunosuppressive TME, which may hinder effective antitumor immune responses. To further dissect the immune landscape, multiple computational approaches were applied to assess how immune infiltration profiles relate to risk classification (Fig. 6E). Based on the CIBERSORT analysis, the HRG showed significantly higher infiltration of resting dendritic cells, plasma cells (both P < 0.05), and activated CD4 memory T cells (P < 0.001), whereas monocytes (P < 0.05) and M1 macrophages (P < 0.001) were more abundant in the LRG (Fig. 6F). In parallel, the results of 16 immune function score analyses revealed that HLA (P < 0.001) and type II IFN response (P < 0.001) were inhibited in the HRG compared with the LRG (Fig. 6G). Single-cell analysis of MRG prognostic model

To elucidate the association between the MAPK signature and immune cell populations, we conducted single-cell transcriptomic analysis. Using the Seurat package, we analyzed 23,451 genes across 118,602 cells derived from LUAD samples. Most cells exhibited gene counts (nFeature) ranging from 300 to 5000 and unique molecular identifiers (nCount) between 5000 and 20,000 (Figure S12A). A strong positive association was identified between nFeature and nCount (Pearson’s r = 0.90), while a weak negative correlation was found between mitochondrial gene percentage (percent.mt) and nCount (Pearson’s r = − 0.11) (Fig. S12B). PCA revealed minimal batch effects, and the top nine principal components were selected based on the ElbowPlot (Fig. S12C, D). UMAP was used for dimensionality reduction and cell-type visualization, these clusters were assigned to major cell lineages, including B cells (CD79A, MS4A1, CD79B), endothelial cells (RAMP2, CLDN5, PECAM1), epithelial cells (KRT18, KRT19, EPCAM), fibroblasts (COL1A1, COL1A2, DCN), mast cells (KIT, MS4A2, TPSAB1), myeloid cells (CD14, CD68, LYZ), plasma cells (JCHAIN, MZB1, IGHG1), neutrophil cells (S100A8, S100A9, S100A12), alveolar cell (CLDN18, AQP4) and T/NK cells (CD3D, CD3E, NKG7) (Fig. 7A, C). CeRNA of MRG prognostic model

A regulatory network comprising lncRNAs, mRNAs, and miRNAs was established, revealing that a variety of noncoding RNAs may be modulated by MRGs. This network sheds light on LUAD pathogenesis that could inform the design of future therapeutic interventions (Fig. S13, Table S9).

Discussion

LUAD, the most frequently diagnosed lung cancer subtype, continues to account for a significant proportion of global cancer-related deaths, notwithstanding progress in early diagnostic techniques and targeted treatment strategies. Moreover, the development of resistance to targeted therapies underscores the pressing need for robust prognostic models to support clinical decision-making [31, 32]. Under such circumstances, the clinical implications of the MAPK pathway in LUAD remain an evolving area of research. Given the central role of this pathway in tumor cell proliferation and immune modulation, it can provide valuable assessment for LUAD prognosis and therapeutic decision-making [33, 34]. To address this knowledge gap, we screened MRG sets through multi-omics analyses, and MR further clarified their association with lung cancer. The resulting risk model demonstrated strong predictive performance across multiple datasets. Notably, we detected distinct biological and immunological characteristics between the HRG and LRG, including differential drug sensitivities and tumor microenvironment profiles. These findings underscore the model’s utility in informing therapeutic strategies for LUAD patients. Furthermore, integrating scRNA-seq with bulk transcriptomic data makes it possible to construct more precise and interpretable prognostic tools in oncology [35, 36].

Building on these integrative analyses, the model was verified across the TCGA dataset and multiple independent validation cohorts from the GEO database, exhibiting robust validity and stability. In all datasets, K–M analysis demonstrated that OS was markedly shorter in the HRG relative to the LRG (P < 0.001) [37]. Furthermore, time-dependent ROC curve analysis confirmed the model’s prognostic performance, with AUC scores at one-, three-, and five-year intervals consistently surpassing 0.70 across all datasets. These results indicate that transcriptomic features related to the MAPK pathway effectively capture critical biological information associated with tumor aggressiveness, thereby providing a reliable molecular tool for LUAD prognosis. To improve the model’s clinical applicability, a nomogram integrating risk scores with conventional clinical indicators (age, sex, and stage) was developed. It exhibited excellent predictive capability, indicating strong agreement between predicted and actual survival outcomes [38]. Notably, our risk model demonstrated prognostic performance comparable to the TNM staging system and outperformed age and gender in the ROC analysis. These results significantly surpassed the performance of the MAPK-related model proposed by Liu et al. [39]. In conclusion, by integrating multi-omics information with conventional clinical parameters, this comprehensive tool provides a feasible and effective way for risk assessment and treatment in LUAD patients [8]. Beyond LUAD, our findings may also have potential significance for the management of other clinically challenging conditions such as skeletal metastases of unknown primary, where patients often face considerable uncertainty in diagnosis and treatment. Argentiero et al. emphasized the critical role of tumor microenvironment characteristics, bone-specific markers, and bisphosphonates in improving patient outcomes [40]. The methodological framework adopted in this study—namely, the integration of pathway-oriented prognostic features with multi-omics data—can serve as a conceptual model for addressing the heterogeneity of bone metastases and related cancers.

To further understand the biological rationale behind the model’s clinical utility, we investigated the mechanistic underpinnings of its prognostic relevance, which lie in the multifaceted roles of MRGs in LUAD biology. Among them, LOXL2 plays a pivotal role in extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling and metastasis. Salvador et al. demonstrated that LOXL2 promotes pulmonary metastasis in vivo by enhancing tumor dedifferentiation, invasion, and the formation of a pre-metastatic niche, which facilitates cancer dissemination and immune evasion [41]. SPTBN1 has attracted increasing interest as a clinically significant biomarker. According to Gu et al., LUAD patients with the SPTBN1-ALK fusion variant demonstrate inherent resistance to crizotinib, as well as to chemotherapy and radiotherapy, which contributes to unfavorable clinical outcomes [42]. In addition, CCDC50 plays a crucial role in maintaining tumor cell viability under stress. Jia et al. demonstrated that CCDC50 has a pivotal function in supporting tumor growth by monitoring lysosomal damage, recognizing galectin-3 and K63-linked polyubiquitination on damaged lysosomes, and specifically targeting them for autophagy-dependent degradation [43]. Lastly, RUNX2 has emerged as a key transcriptional regulator in LUAD. Otálora-Otálora et al. revealed that RUNX2 interacts with various transcription factors to form co-regulatory complexes implicated in lung cancer initiation and progression, positioning it as a central regulator within the lung cancer gene regulatory network [44]. Recent evidence suggests that its expression may be modulated by N6-methyladenosine (m6A) RNA methylation [45].

Notably, these gene-level mechanisms correspond closely to the signaling pathways enriched by the model, revealing its ability to capture fundamental LUAD biology. Enrichment analyses revealed that the model captured key biological pathways associated with PI3K-Akt signaling pathway—consistently linked to poor prognosis and therapy resistance in LUAD [46]. The PI3K-Akt signaling cascade enhances angiogenesis by upregulating vascular endothelial growth factor (VEGF) and contributes to immune escape via modulation of PD-L1 expression. Clinically, its hyperactivation has been associated with resistance to EGFR-TKIs and other targeted therapies. Our prognostic model is based on the MAPK signaling pathway, which aligns well with the biological basis revealed by the enrichment analysis. Consequently, this signaling pathway could offer valuable perspectives on the molecular underpinnings of LUAD [47]. These aberrant activations of signaling pathways may be associated with underlying genomic alterations, reflecting a multilayered interplay between signal transduction and mutational burden. Supplementary mutational profiling indicated that a high tumor mutation load was correlated with unfavorable survival outcomes in LUAD patients, likely due to the frequent occurrence of TP53 mutations—a well-known tumor suppressor—in the HRG. TP53 co-mutations have been associated with upregulated PD-L1 expression in tumor cells, which may inhibit immune responses and facilitate immune escape. The HRG displayed more extensive genomic alterations, suggesting a higher likelihood of resistance to immunotherapy and a worse prognosis [48]. The correlation between MAPK-related prognostic model and TMB suggests a synergistic role in immune evasion, as tumors with higher TMB often exhibit neoantigen accumulation that may influence checkpoint blockade efficacy [49].

Due to these enrichment mechanisms, tumors in the HRG appear to become increasingly dependent on upstream receptor tyrosine kinases—to sustain oncogenic signaling through the MAPK axis. This dependency is reflected in the enhanced sensitivity of HRG tumors to multi-targeted TKIs that inhibit these RTKs. Thus, the differential drug sensitivity observed between HRG and LRG is not merely a correlative observation but is mechanistically grounded in the distinct oncogenic architecture and microenvironmental adaptations of each group. Specifically, the HRG demonstrated a more favorable response to broad-spectrum TKIs, particularly those targeting RTKs such as MET, FGFR, and EGFR—key upstream regulators of the MAPK signaling pathway [50]. EGFR-targeted therapies have demonstrated high objective response rates (ORR), particularly with the advent of third-generation EGFR-TKIs, which have significantly enhanced clinical efficacy. Multiple large-scale, randomized phase III trials comparing third-generation EGFR-TKIs—such as Osimertinib—with first-generation agents have consistently shown a statistically significant improvement in progression-free survival (PFS) in favor of the newer-generation compounds, highlighting distinct therapeutic vulnerabilities that could inform tailored treatment strategies [51, 52].

We conducted scRNA-seq on LUAD samples. Analysis via the Seurat framework revealed that MRGs were primarily enriched in endothelial cells [53], which are known to express immunosuppressive markers such as PD-L1 and secrete pro-angiogenic and anti-inflammatory factors. Endothelial cells are actively involved in facilitating tumor progression and modulating the immune microenvironment, and their functional roles have drawn growing interest, particularly in relation to anti-angiogenic treatment strategies. Such treatments—including monoclonal antibodies like bevacizumab and TKIs—target VEGF and its receptors, which are upstream components of the MAPK signaling pathway [54]. These agents effectively suppress the pro-angiogenic functions of tumor-associated endothelial cells and impair tumor vascularization, this strategy underscores the mechanistic rationale for therapeutic interventions directed at MRG.

The study presents several key innovations. First, our model integrates multi-omics data including transcriptomics, genomics, and scRNA-seq, offering a comprehensive perspective on tumor heterogeneity and immune landscape [55]. Second, by externally validating the model across three independent GEO cohorts, we ensured its generalizability across diverse patient populations—a critical feature for clinical translation. Third, the inclusion of a nomogram calibrated with clinical variables enhances its practical value in oncology practice [56, 57]. However, certain limitations should be acknowledged. First of all, our analyses were based on retrospective public datasets, potentially introducing selection bias and constraining the generalizability of the results to wider clinical populations. Second, although multiple machine learning algorithms were employed to enhance model stability, the optimal combination may still be influenced by dataset-specific characteristics, and further validation in prospective clinical settings is essential. We will collaborate with clinical centers to evaluate the model in independent cohorts and conduct loss- and gain-of-function assays on candidate genes to quantify their effects on migration, invasion, and therapeutic response, while mitigating potential biases arising from overlaps in patient demographics and assay platforms across datasets, thereby enhancing the model’s generalizability and transparency.

Conclusion

Our risk model, developed based on multi-omics data, demonstrates strong predictive accuracy for patient prognosis. When integrated with clinical factors, the constructed nomogram further enhances its prognostic power. Analysis revealed that poor prognosis in the HRG is closely associated with elevated TMB and enrichment in the PI3K-AKT signaling pathways. The results of drug sensitivity analysis provide valuable information that may facilitate personalized therapy. However, additional clinical validation is essential to establish its applicability.

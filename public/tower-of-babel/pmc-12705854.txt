Loss of the miR-221/222 Cluster Promotes the Pathogenesis of Osteoarthritis in Inflamed Mouse Chondrocytes and Osteoarthritis Models
Authors: Xinlei Liu, Fangyu Feng, Yiman Jin, Chuang Ma, Qiaozhen Zhu, Yang You, Mengju Gao, Xinyu Li, Xinli Niu, Junpeng Wang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Osteoarthritis (OA) is a degenerative joint disorder with the destruction of the joint’s structural and functional which affects the entire joint. An important feature of OA is the progressive degeneration of articular cartilage due to an imbalance between extracellular matrix synthesis and degradation. While the molecular basis of OA remains unclear, microRNAs (miRNAs) have been recognized as key factors in controlling cartilage homeostasis and inflammation. Of these, miR-221 and miR-222 are implicated in inflammatory and degenerative disease, but their exact role in OA has yet to be determined. This study aims to assess the impact of miR-221/222 cluster knockout (KO) on mouse OA models and to explore its underlying mechanisms. OA was induced in miR-221/222 KO mice utilizing the destabilization of the medial meniscus (DMM) model. Joint pathology was examined by X-ray, histological scoring, immunofluorescence, and biochemical assays. In addition, in vitro studies were performed using IL-1β-treated chondrocytes. Gain-of-function experiments were performed using miR-221/222 agomir. OA tissues of mice and mouse primary chondrocytes treated with IL-1β showed a decrease in miR-221 and miR-222 expression as compared with the shamed mice. However, miR-221/222 KO mice exhibited significant osteophyte formation and cartilage degradation, with a marked increase in collagen I, MMP-13 levels, and a significant decrease in COL2A1 levels. In vitro, miR-221/222 KO led to increased inflammatory mediators like IL-6 and TNF-α, which promoted chondrocyte apoptosis and activated the MAPK signaling pathway, thereby exacerbating OA progression. Conversely, treatment with miR-221/222 agomir attenuated OA progression in vivo. These findings suggest that loss of the miR-221 cluster contributes to OA progression by promoting inflammation, apoptosis, and matrix degradation, partly through MAPK signaling. Restoring miR-221/222 may hold therapeutic potential, but human studies are needed for validation.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s10753-025-02411-4.

Introduction

Osteoarthritis (OA) is a chronic, degenerative joint illness that is a primary cause of disability globally, impacting approximately 600 million people [1–4]. It is distinguished by the disintegration of articular cartilage, subchondral bone remodeling, osteophyte development, and synovial inflammation [5–7]. Genetic susceptibility, obesity, joint trauma, and aging are all known risk factors [4, 8]. Current non-surgical treatments for OA are focused mostly on pain management with analgesics and anti-inflammatory medications, with joint replacement surgery being the only effective option for the advanced stages of the disease [9]. Thus, there is an urgent need to uncover novel molecular targets for slowing OA progression and maintaining joint integrity.

Increasing evidence implicates pro-inflammatory mediators such as IL-1β, which play crucial roles in OA progression. IL-1β, produced by activated chondrocytes and synoviocytes [10], induces an increase in extracellular matrix (ECM)-degrading enzymes such as matrix metalloproteinases (MMP) and a disintegrin-like and metalloproteinase domain with thrombospondin type 1 motifs (ADAMTS) while reducing the synthesis of essential cartilage components such as type II collagen and proteoglycans. This imbalance between ECM component anabolism and catabolism contributes to OA progression [11].

In recent years, microRNAs (miRNAs), small non-coding RNAs that regulate gene expression by interacting with the 3’ untranslated regions of target genes, have been shown to regulate articular cartilage metabolism, chondrocyte differentiation, apoptosis, and the ECM environment, thereby influencing OA progression [12–14]. Some miRNAs (such as miR-17, miR-204, and miR-211) serve to maintain cartilage integrity, while others (miR-34a-5p and miR-214-3p) contribute to OA etiology by promoting cartilage breakdown and inflammation [12, 15–17].

The miR-221/222 cluster consists of two highly homologous miRNAs with similar functions. These miRNAs have been widely investigated in cancer, autoimmune diseases, and chronic inflammatory diseases [18–20]. However, their significance in OA is unclear and debatable. Most investigations on miR-221 and miR-222 in OA have concentrated on OA have focused on in vitro experiments, and the findings are not consistent, and in some cases, conflicting. Previous studies have shown that downregulation of miR-221 enhances chondrogenic differentiation and type II collagen expression [21], while other studies have indicated that lower miR-221 levels exacerbate IL-1β-induced cartilage degradation [22]. Furthermore, suppression of miR-221/222 has been associated with both positive and negative effects in models of cartilage repair and inflammatory injury [23, 24].

Given these conflicting findings, further study is needed to determine the involvement of the miR-221/222 cluster in OA. We hypothesized that the loss of miR-221/222 would exacerbate OA progression, potentially through effects on inflammation and chondrocyte apoptosis. To clarify this, we generated in vivo and in vitro OA models utilizing miR-221/222 knockout mice and IL-1β-stimulated chondrocytes. This study aims to evaluate the effect of miR-221/222 cluster knockout in a mouse OA model and explore its potential mechanisms in OA pathogenesis.

Materials and methods

Animals

Cyagen Biosciences (Suzhou, China) generated genetic miR-221/222 knockout (KO) C57BL/6J mice via the CRISPR/Cas9 system. Heterozygous mice were bred to obtain wild-type (WT) and homozygous KO littermates. Genotyping was determined by PCR analysis (Table S1 and Fig. S1). WT and miR-221/222 KO littermates, utilized in this study, did not show significant differences in body length, weight, or gross phenotype. Only males were selected to avoid hormonal variability between sexes. Mice were housed in specific pathogen-free environments under a 12-hour light-dark cycle with free access to standard chow food and water. The experimental procedures, which followed the NIH’s guidelines for the use of experimental animals, were approved by the Ethical Committee of Huaihe Hospital at Henan University (Approval #: 2022016).

Animal Experiments

Mice (aged 10–12 weeks, weighing ~ 25 g) were randomly divided into four groups (n = 10/group): WT + SHAM, KO + SHAM, WT + DMM, and KO + DMM. As previously described [25], surgical destabilization of the medial meniscus (DMM) was used to establish an OA model. In brief, mice were sedated with intramuscular ketamine (80 mg/kg) and xylazine (8 mg/kg). All operations were carried out under aseptic circumstances, as previously described [26]. Briefly, the hair around the mouse’s knee joint was removed with scissors, and a 5 mm incision was performed on the medial side of the knee joint using a surgical blade to reveal the patellar ligament and tibial plateau. An incision was then created along the medial aspect of the patellar ligament, and a 3 − 0 ETHICON suture was used to withdraw it laterally, revealing the posterior fat pad. The fat pad was gently peeled away with ophthalmic forceps, and sterile gauze was applied to the surgical site to ensure hemostasis. After hemostasis was confirmed, the medial meniscotibial ligament (MMTL) was plainly visible and carefully severed with microscopic scissors, being careful not to injure the articular cartilage or surrounding soft tissues. The joint capsule and skin were then sutured. Sham surgery consisted of just opening the joint capsule and then suturing the incision without severing the MMTL. Following the operation, the animals were kept in a sterilized recovery chamber until full consciousness was restored. Postoperative pain and discomfort were monitored for 72 hours.

To investigate the effect of miR-221/222 overexpression, WT mice undergoing sham and DMM surgery received intra-articular (IA) injection of 10 µL of miR-221/222 agomir or negative control (NC) (Sangon Biotech, Shanghai, China) at a concentration of approximately 4 mol/mL), at intervals of 2 weeks, for a total of four injections beginning on the first day following surgery. The contralateral knee joint was left untreated and acted as an internal control. Based on previous studies and the exploration of the 12 weeks after the operation, 8 weeks after the operation is the optimal duration [26]. All mice were sacrificed by carbon dioxide asphyxiation 8 weeks post-surgery to collect tissues for subsequent analysis.

Isolation and Culture of Mouse Chondrocytes

Primary chondrocytes were isolated from the knee joint cartilage of 5-day-old WT or miR-221/222 KO mice as previously described [27]. Sterile cartilage was dissected, rinsed with PBS, and cut into pieces around 1 mm³. These pieces were digested for 45 min with 0.25% trypsin, then overnight with 0.5% collagenase IV in an incubator at 37 °C with 0.5% CO2. After centrifuging and filtering the cell suspension, the chondrocytes were resuspended in DMEM/F12 medium supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum, 1% penicillin/streptomycin, and cultured at 37 °C with 5% CO2. The medium was changed every 2–3 days. Second-generation cells were used in all experiments to ensure consistency. While flow cytometry or immunostaining was not used to confirm cell identity, cell morphology and response to IL-1β (e.g., upregulation of MMP13, COX2, IL-6, and TNF-α; downregulation of COL2A1) confirmed the chondrocyte phenotype.

ELISA

Primary chondrocytes were seeded in 96-well plates and cocultured with IL-1β (0, 5, 10, and 20 ng/mL) for 24 hours. Cell-free supernatant was collected to evaluate the levels of IL-6 and TNF-α (both from BD Biosciences) via their corresponding ELISA kits following the manufacturer’s instructions. We conducted a Griess assay to determine nitric oxide (NO) production. The OD value at 450 nm was obtained via a SpectraMax i3x microplate reader (Molecular Devices, USA).

Western Blot

IL-1β-treated chondrocytes or cartilage tissues were lysed with RIPA lysis buffer containing PMSF and phosphatase inhibitors. The protein concentration was quantified using a BCA kit. Equal amounts of protein were separated by SDS-PAGE and then transferred to a PVDF membrane. Membranes were blocked with 5% non-fat milk and then incubated with the primary antibodies at 4 °C. The antibodies used in this study were: β-Actin (1:100,000), BAX (1:10,000), Bcl-2 (1:2000) (all from Proteintech, Wuhan, China), P38 (1:1000), phospho-p38 (p-p38, 1:1000), SAPK/JNK (JNK, 1:1000), phospho-SAPK/JNK (p-JNK, 1:2000), Erk1/2 (1:2000), phospho-Erk1/2 (p-ERK1/2, 1:1000), iNOS (1:1000), COX2 (1:1000), rabbit Caspase-3 (1:1000) (all from Cell Signaling Technology), MMP-13 (1:2000), type I collagen (1:1000), and type II collagen (COL2A1, 1:2000) (all from Bioss, Beijing, China). Blots were incubated with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies at room temperature for 1 h and visualized using an enhanced chemiluminescence kit. Band intensity was analyzed using ImageJ (v1.48).

RNA Isolation and Quantification

Total RNA was extracted from IL-1β-treated chondrocytes or cartilage tissues using a microRNA extraction kit (Sangon Biotech). RNA was reverse-transcribed using a cDNA synthesis kit (Sangon Biotech). Real-time PCR was performed using 2× Universal SYBR Green Fast qPCR Mix (RK21203, ABclonal, Wuhan, China) on a 7500 Real-Time PCR System (Life Technologies Holdings Pte Ltd, Singapore). We collected cycle threshold (Ct) values and used the 2−∆∆Ct method to calculate the relative gene expression (miR-221 and miR-222 mRNA normalized to U6 mRNA; COL2A1 and MMP-13 normalized to β-actin). Histological Analysis

After euthanasia, knee joints were harvested, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde for 48 h, and decalcified in 10% EDTA for 4 weeks at 4 °C with gentle agitation. Samples were dehydrated in graded alcohol, penetrated in xylene, and embedded in paraffin. Section (5 μm) were subjected to Safranin O-Fast Green (S-F), hematoxylin-eosin (HE), and Masson’s triple staining to determine joint damage and the severity of synovial fibrosis. The Osteoarthritis Research Society International’s histopathological grading system (OARSI) and Krenn’s score were used to assess the severity of joint degeneration and synovial inflammation, respectively. All histological scoring, as previously described [28, 29], is completed independently by two double-blinded pathologists.

Immunofluorescence

Primary chondrocytes were seeded in 24-well plates and treated with or without IL-1β at the indicated doses for 24 hours, fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, permeabilized with 0.1% Triton X-100, and blocked with 5% BSA. After that, cells were incubated with anti-MMP-13 or COL2A1 antibodies (Both 1:500, Bioss), followed by FITC-conjugated goat anti-rabbit IgG (1:500, Proteintech). Nuclei were counterstained with DAPI. For the tissue section, antigen retrieval was performed in a 95℃ EDTA solution for 30 min before staining. Images were captured via a Nikon Ti2-E fluorescence microscope. All results of immunofluorescence experiments were quantitatively analyzed by ImageJ software.

Flow Cytometry

Apoptosis in chondrocytes was evaluated using an Annexin V-FITC and propidium iodide (PI) apoptosis detection kit (MA0220-2, Meilunbio®, Dalian, China) following IL-1β (10 ng/ml). Cells were acquired by using a BD Canto Plus flow cytometer (BD Biosciences) (BD Bioscience, San Diego, CA), and data were analyzed via FlowJo 10.8.1 software.

X-ray

Knee joints were harvested and imaged using digital X-ray equipment (Shanghai Yuyan Instruments Co., Ltd., Shanghai, China) to assess joint space narrowing and osteophyte formation.

Statistical Analysis

All data are presented as the mean ± standard error of the mean (SEM). Normality was tested using the Shapiro–Wilk test. One-way or two-way ANOVA, followed by Tukey’s.

post hoc test, was used for comparisons among three or more groups. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value < 0.05. GraphPad Prism 9.0 was used for statistical analysis.

Control of Experimental Variables

Every animal used was a male between 10 and 12 weeks of age. Weekly body weight measurements were made to monitor health. The animals were kept in identical conditions (temperature: 22 ± 1 °C, humidity: 50 ± 10%) and were divided into groups at random. All in vitro experiments were repeated at least three times to ensure reproducibility. For in vivo experiments, each group included 10 mice, and procedures were standardized to minimize variability.

Results

The Expression of miR-221 and miR-222 Is Downregulated in Inflamed Mouse Chondrocytes and OA Joints

To evaluate the role of the miR-221/222 cluster in OA progression, we first used inflamed mouse chondrocytes and an OA model to determine the expression of miR-221 and miR-222 at relevant time points after DMM surgery. At 4, 8, and 12 weeks following DMM surgery, safranin O staining indicated articular cartilage deterioration (Fig. 1A and B), confirming the successful induction of OA. Safranin O-positive cartilage area decreased significantly over time, according to quantitative analysis (p < 0.001 at 4, 8, and 12 weeks vs. sham). Next, we extracted cartilage tissue and used real-time RT-PCR to analyze the expression of miR-221 and miR-222. Both the miR-221 and miR-222 expression levels were significantly lower in OA joints than in sham controls at the indicated time (4 weeks: miR-221, 0.75 ± 0.0.07-fold p < 0.05; miR-222: 0.83 ± 0.06-fold p = 0.23; 8 weeks: miR-221, 0.34 ± 0.0.04-fold p < 0.001; miR-222: 0.64 ± 0.10-fold p = 0.001; 12 weeks: miR-221, 0.21 ± 0.001-fold p < 0.001; miR-222: 0.29 ± 0.07-fold p < 0.001; Fig. 1C). Additionally, in an in vitro inflamed OA model, effective induction was confirmed by measuring NO levels (e.g., 24 h: 11.9 ± 5.31-fold vs. untreated, p < 0.001, Fig. 1D) in the chondrocyte-free supernatant and COL1A (e.g., 24 h: 0.08 ± 0.04-fold vs. untreated, p < 0.001, Fig. 1D) and MMP13 expression (e.g., 24 h: 6.13 ± 0.0.88-fold vs. untreated, p < 0.001, Fig. 1D) in IL-1β-treated chondrocyte. Finally, IL-1β administration reduced miR-221 and miR-222 expression (p < 0.01 at 12 and 24 h; at 10 and 20 ng/ml) in a time- and dose-dependent manner (Fig. 1E and F). These findings suggest that the miR-221/222 cluster might be involved in OA progression and inflammatory responses.

Deficiency of the miR-221/222 Cluster Exacerbates OA Progression in Mice

Next, we used miR-221/222 KO mice that had undergone DMM surgery to explore the influence of the miR-221/222 cluster on OA progression. Following DMM surgery, we did not observe any difference in histological changes, including cartilage degradation and synovial fibrosis, as evidenced by H&E, S-F, and Masson’s trichrome staining between WT and heterozygous mice (Data not shown). However, miR-221/222 cluster KO resulted in more severe imaging characteristics, including increased osteophytes, irregular joints, and pronounced joint stenosis, as demonstrated using X-rays (Fig. 2A). These results were corroborated by histological analysis. Significant cartilage degradation and synovial fibrosis were observed in KO mice using H&E, S-F, and Masson’s trichrome staining (Fig. 2B-F). KO mice had significantly higher OARSI scores (3.82 ± 0.18) than WT controls (2.25 ± 0.12; p < 0.001; Fig. 2E). KO mice also had higher Krenn’s scores for synovial inflammation (4.8 ± 0.17 vs. 2.2 ± 0.09; p < 0.001; Fig. 2F).

At the molecular level, KO mice showed elevated collagen 1 (0.70 ± 0.21-fold vs. WT; p < 0.01; Fig. 2G) and MMP-13 levels (3.42 ± 0.14-fold vs. WT; p < 0.001; Fig. 2J and K) and significantly decreased COL2A1 expression (0.70 ± 0.08-fold vs. WT; p < 0.05; Fig. 2H and I). These findings indicate that OA-related cartilage degradation and joint pathology are made worse by a lack of the miR-221/222 cluster.

Deficiency of the miR-221/222 Cluster Increases Inflammatory Mediator Levels and the Expression of Inflamed Chondrocyte-related Proteins

To explore the underlying mechanisms, we exposed primary chondrocytes from WT or miR-221/222 cluster KO mice to IL-1β. The levels of IL-6 (3.95 ± 0.82-fold; p < 0.001), TNF-α (4.31 ± 0.43-fold; p < 0.001), and NO (1.12 ± 0.05-fold; p < 0.01) released by KO chondrocytes were significantly higher than those of WT (Fig. 3A-C).

Deficiency of the miR-221/222 Cluster Decreases Chondrocyte Viability and Aggravates Apoptosis

The viability of KO mice’s IL-1β-treated chondrocytes was significantly lower than that of WT mice in a dose- and time-dependent manner (p < 0.001; Fig. 4A and B). According to flow cytometry, KO chondrocytes had a higher rate of apoptosis (early + late apoptosis: 25.2% ± 0.76%) than WT (17.6% ± 2.08%; p < 0.05; Fig. 4C).

Western blot analysis revealed that KO chondrocytes expressed less anti-apoptotic Bcl-2 (0.06 ± 0.01-fold; p < 0.001) and more pro-apoptotic markers, cleaved-caspase3 (2.45 ± 0.52-fold; p < 0.01) and BAX (1.65 ± 0.26-fold; p < 0.05) (Fig. 4D and E). According to these findings, a lack of miR-221/222 increases chondrocyte apoptosis, which accelerates the development of OA.

Deletion of the miR-221/222 Cluster Intensifies the Activation of MAPK Signals

MAPK signals are well known to modulate cell proliferation, differentiation, and apoptosis [30]. Although MAPK signaling has previously been linked to the development of OA [31, 32], whether deletion of the miR-221/222 cluster affects the MAPK signaling pathway has not been studied. The expression of phosphorylated Erk (1.72 ± 0.29-fold; p < 0.05), p38 (1.53 ± 0.14-fold; p < 0.05), and JNK (2.11 ± 0.29-fold; p < 0.001) proteins was elevated in chondrocytes lacking the miR-221/222 cluster after treatment with IL-1β (Fig. 4D and F). These findings suggest that deletion of the miR-221/222 cluster may contribute to accelerating the course of osteoarthritis by activating the MAPK signaling pathway.

The Administration of the Agomir miR-221/222 Alleviates Osteoarthritis in Mice

Finally, we used IA injection to destabilize DMM-induced WT mice with the miR-221/222 agomir or NC to further investigate the role of miR-221/222 in OA progression (Fig. 5). We found that administering the miR-221/222 agomir to DMM-induced WT mice reduced OA progression, as shown by improved histological changes (OA + Agomir NC vs. OA + Agomir: 3.06 ± 0.23 vs. 1.77 ± 0.09, p < 0.001), reduced MMP-13 (OA + Agomir/OA + Agomir NC: 0.42 ± 0.02-fold; p < 0.001), while increased type II collagen (OA + Agomir/OA + Agomir NC: 1.38 ± 0.07-fold; p < 0.001) in the joints (Fig. 5). These results suggest that miR-221/222 treatment may protect cartilage by preventing ECM degradation.

Discussion

Osteoarthritis is a chronic degenerative joint disease that results in pain and disability over time. Current nonsurgical treatments for OA are primarily concerned with pain relief through analgesia and anti-inflammatory effects, but they usually yield unsatisfactory results. The etiology of OA comprises chondrocyte death and ECM degradation, which exacerbate this disease [33]. Here, we demonstrated that the miR-221/222 cluster is a basic regulatory element in OA pathogenesis; loss of this cluster causes ECM degradation, inflammatory mediator expression, and chondrocyte death, in part by MAPK pathway activity. These findings provide new mechanistic understanding and therapeutic possibilities, given the restoration of miR-221/222 slowed OA progression in vivo.

Our findings are consistent with an increasing amount of research that shows miRNAs play a role in cartilage homeostasis and the pathophysiology of OA [34]. For instance, miR-23a/b-3p clusters delay OA through Gremlin1-mediated activation of the TGF-β/Smad axis [35]. However, this cluster is not essential for the pathogenesis of OA in mouse aging and post-traumatic models [36], whereas downregulation of miR-140-3p has been shown to aggravate OA by controlling ferroptosis and inflammatory mediators [37]. Similarly, administering miR-146a-5p agomir substantially decreases inflammatory secretions in the temporomandibular osteoarthritis OA model [38]. Despite these advances, the roles of miR-221 and miR-222 in OA remain debatable. Zheng et al. found that miR-221-3p is downregulated in inflammatory chondrocytes and OA cartilage, perhaps protecting against inflammation and ECM degradation [22], while Wang et al. found that silencing miR-222-3p worsened IL-1β-treated human chondrocytes via pro-apoptosis [24].

Our findings provide in vivo genetic evidence for current discussions, supporting the miR-221/222 clusters’ protective role in OA. We confirmed that miR-221/222 expression is downregulated in OA cartilage and inflammatory chondrocytes, which is consistent with earlier findings in OA models [22, 24]. More notably, we demonstrated that miR-221/222 gene deletion accelerated the progression of OA in mice receiving DMM surgery. KO mice exhibited much more severe cartilage erosion, osteophyte development, joint space constriction, and synovial fibrosis than WT controls. These findings suggest that miR-221/222 may help to suppress the aberrant remodeling processes that cause joint degeneration.

On a molecular level, we found that miR-221/222 deficiency leads to increased expression of inflammatory mediators (IL-6, TNF-α, and NO), inflammation-related proteins (COX2 and iNOS), and matrix-degrading proteases (MMP-13). Type II collagen expression was concurrently reduced, indicating compromised ECM integrity. These findings point to a key role for miR-221/222 in suppressing inflammatory signaling and preserving cartilage matrix components.

Mechanistically, deleting the miR-221/222 cluster decreased chondrocyte viability and promoted apoptosis, as seen by higher cleaved caspase-3 and Bax expression and lower Bcl-2 expression levels. These changes were associated with upregulation of the MAPK signaling pathway, particularly the ERK and p38 branches. MAPK signaling is well known to regulate apoptosis, inflammation, and matrix degradation in OA [39–41], suggesting that miR-221/222 may exert its protective effects at least in part by modulating MAPK activity. However, we did not determine the effect of the miR-221/222 cluster on other downstream targets of MAPK signaling, such as MMP-3 and ADAMTS5 [42]. Future work using specific MAPK inhibitors, transcriptomic profiling, and direct target validation (e.g., luciferase assays or RIP-seq) is needed to clarify these regulatory interactions.

In the final stage of the study, we investigated the therapeutic potential of restoring miR-221/222 levels in OA mice produced by DMM. Intra-articular injections of miR-221/222 agomir could slow cartilage degradation and MMP-13 expression, preserving joint structure. This lends credence to the therapeutic viability of miRNA-based methods in OA [23, 43]. However, significant clinical considerations remain. The safety, delivery specificity, off-target effects, and long-term success of miRNA therapy in humans necessitate comprehensive preclinical and clinical testing. Furthermore, the varied expression of miR-221/222 in many organs and disease states (e.g., cancer, metabolic disease) indicates that systemic distribution may have unexpected consequences [19]. Tissue-specific vectors or exosome-based delivery could help to overcome some of these challenges.

Although this study provides new insights into the role of the miR-221/222 cluster in OA pathogenesis, several limitations should be addressed. First, our study was restricted to mouse models, and validation in human chondrocytes and patient samples is critical. Second, we did not include sex-specific analyses, and potential differences between male and female responses remain uninvestigated. Third, while we identified downstream pathways that may be controlled by miR-221/222 cluster, further study is needed to determine the precise molecular mechanisms and validation of targets in vivo. Finally, clinical translation of these findings will necessitate validation in human cartilage samples and larger, longitudinal studies. Future research addressing these aspects will strengthen the mechanistic understanding and therapeutic potential of miR-221/222 cluster in OA.

Conclusions

In summary, our findings suggest that the miR-221/222 cluster helps to maintain cartilage integrity by regulating inflammation, apoptosis, and ECM, at least in part via MAPK signaling pathways. Loss of this cluster exacerbated OA progression, whereas restoring its levels slowed disease progression. Future studies should focus on finding miR-221/222’s direct targets, defining its regulatory network in chondrocytes, and assessing its therapeutic efficacy in large animal models and human clinical trials.

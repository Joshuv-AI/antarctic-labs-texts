Supernova Bursts as a Probe of Neutrino Nature via C ​ E ​ ν ​ N ​ S CE\nu NS Coherent Scattering
David Delepine, A. Yebra

Abstract
The Resonant Spin-Flavor Precession (RSFP) of core-collapse supernova neutrinos within the framework of the quantum density matrix formalism is studied. The cooling duration of SN1987A severely constraints standard RSFP models for Dirac Neutrinos. Using the properties of the outer stellar envelope where resonant conversion could occur after thermal decoupling, we show that for neutrino magnetic moments in the range μ ν ∼ 10 − 14 − 10 − 12 ​ μ B \mu_{\nu}\sim 10^{-14}-10^{-12}\mu_{B} , adiabatic conversion in the envelope ( R > 1000 R>1000 km) leads to macroscopic helicity inversion without violating cooling bounds. This RSFP neutrino helicity change induces different signatures for Dirac or Majorana neutrinos in Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE ν \nu NS) detectors. For Dirac neutrino, a massive flux deficit for Dirac neutrinos due to sterile conversion should be seen. For Majorana neutrinos, the flux will not change but a modification to its spectral decomposition of the cross section should be seen due to the transition from left-handed electron neutrinos to right-handed μ \mu or τ \tau anti-neutrinos. An experimental strategy is proposed to minimize the astrophysical uncertainties using the high-energy neutrino tail ( E ∼ 1 E\sim 1 GeV) which evades RSFP to normalize the signal. This ratio-based approach effectively cancels astrophysical uncertainties, allowing future detectors to distinguish the fundamental nature of the neutrino and probe magnetic moments down to 10 − 14 ​ μ B 10^{-14}\mu_{B} , two orders of magnitude beyond current solar limits.

II
Theoretical Framework

The propagation of neutrinos in the dense and turbulent environment of a core-collapse supernova is done using the quantum density matrix formalism
McKellar and Thomson 1994
;
Stodolsky 1987
;
Sigl and Raffelt 1993
. The extreme conditions of a protoneutron star require the inclusion of non-linear self-interaction terms that are absent in the solar regime
Duan et al. 2010
;
Hannestad et al. 2006
;
Banerjee et al. 2011
.

II.1
Evolution Equation in Dense Media

The evolution of the neutrino ensemble is governed by the Liouville-von Neumann equation for the density matrix
\rho_{\mathbf{p}}
(for a momentum mode
\mathbf{p}
)
McKellar and Thomson 1994
;
Stodolsky 1987
;
Sigl and Raffelt 1993
:

i\frac{d\rho_{\mathbf{p}}}{dt}=[H_{\text{SN}},\rho_{\mathbf{p}}]-\mathcal{D}[\rho_{\mathbf{p}}],

(1)

where
\mathcal{D}[\rho_{\mathbf{p}}]
represents the dissipator accounting for collisional decoherence
Lisi et al. 2000
;
Friedland 2001
;
Guzzo et al. 2016
;
Kurashvili et al. 2017
. Unlike the solar Hamiltonian, the supernova Hamiltonian
H_{\text{SN}}
includes a dominant non-linear term describing collective effects
Duan et al. 2010
;
Hannestad et al. 2006
;
Banerjee et al. 2011
:

H_{\text{SN}}=H_{\text{vac}}+H_{\text{mat}}+H_{\text{mag}}+H_{\nu\nu}.

(2)

•

Vacuum and Matter Terms:
The vacuum oscillation term
H_{\text{vac}}
is the standard one. However, the matter potential
H_{\text{mat}}
differs fromn the sun significantly due to the neutron-rich environment of the core. The potential is given by:

V_{\text{mat}}=\sqrt{2}G_{F}(N_{e}-N_{n}/2),

(3)

where
N_{e}
and
N_{n}
are the electron and neutron number densities, respectively. In the deep core,
N_{n}\gg N_{e}
, often rendering the potential negative, which fundamentally alters the resonance condition compared to the solar case.

•

Magnetic Interaction:
For a neutrino with a transition magnetic moment
\mu_{\nu}
interacting with a transverse magnetic field
B_{\perp}
, the interaction term is well known and is given by
Kurashvili et al. 2017
;
Balantekin and Volpe 2005
;
Sasaki and Takiwaki 2021
:

H_{\text{mag}}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\mu_{\nu}B_{\perp}(r)\\
\mu_{\nu}B_{\perp}(r)&0\end{pmatrix}.

(4)

In the supernova context,
B_{\perp}
typically ranges from
10^{12}
G to
10^{15}
G (in magnetars)
Heger et al. 2005
;
Spruit 2002
;
Endeve et al. 2012
;
Varma et al. 2022
;
Reynolds et al. 2012
;
Kaspi and Beloborodov 2017
;
Duncan and Thompson 1992
, approximately
10^{10}
times stronger than solar tachocline fields.

•

Neutrino Self-Interactions:
Due to extreme dense medium, one has to add a self-interaction Hamiltonian
H_{\nu\nu}
, which arises from coherent forward scattering of neutrinos off other neutrinos
Duan et al. 2010
;
Hannestad et al. 2006
;
Banerjee et al. 2011
. This term couples the evolution of a mode
\mathbf{p}
to all other modes
\mathbf{q}
:

H_{\nu\nu}=\sqrt{2}G_{F}\int d^{3}\mathbf{q}(1-\cos\vartheta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}})(\rho_{\mathbf{q}}-\bar{\rho}_{\mathbf{q}}),

(5)

where
\vartheta_{\mathbf{p}\mathbf{q}}
is the angle between neutrino momenta.

II.2
Resonance and Adiabaticity

In a similar way to the solar case, the resonance condition for Spin-Flavor Precession (SFP) in a supernova requires:

V_{\text{mat}}(r)+V_{\nu\nu}(r)\approx\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{2E}\cos 2\theta.

(6)

In the deep core (
r<10
km), collective potentials
V_{\nu\nu}
typically lock the flavor states, suppressing transitions
Duan et al. 2010
. However, in the envelope (
r>100
km), the density drops sufficiently for resonances to occur
Duan et al. 2006
;
Fogli et al. 2007
;
Pantaleone 1992
.
To check the efficiency of the RSFP conversion, one needs to compute the adiabaticity parameter
\gamma_{\text{SN}}
which is define as
Dighe and Smirnov 2000
;
Schirato and Fuller 2002
;
Fogli et al. 2003
;
Tomàs et al. 2004
;
Kawagoe et al. 2010
:

\gamma_{\text{SN}}=\frac{(2\mu_{\nu}B)^{2}}{|\frac{d}{dr}(V_{\text{eff}})|_{\text{res}}}.

(7)

Due to the supernovae magnetic field strength,
\gamma_{\text{SN}}
is naturally large. Even for magnetic moments as small as
\mu_{\nu}\sim 10^{-20}\mu_{B}
, the transition can be fully adiabatic (
\gamma\gg 1
), leading to complete flavor conversion
Akhmedov et al. 1997
;
Nunokawa et al. 1997
;
Ando and Sato 2003
;
Akhmedov and Fukuyama 2003b
.

III
Constraints from Neutrino Cooling Times

The temporal structure of the neutrino burst from a core-collapse supernova provides a critical astrophysical constraint on the Resonant Spin-Flavor Precession (RSFP) model. The observation of neutrinos from SN1987A
Hirata et al. 1987
;
Bionta et al. 1987
;
Loredo and Lamb 2002
, which spanned a duration of approximately 10–15 seconds, confirms the standard “trapping” scenario
Mazurek 1974
;
Sato 1975
;
Bethe et al. 1979
where neutrinos diffuse out of the dense core on the Kelvin-Helmholtz cooling timescale. This observation acts as a stringent veto for specific regions of the RSFP parameter space, particularly for Dirac neutrinos.

In the standard supernova model, neutrinos are produced rapidly but are trapped within the protoneutron star due to the high matter density (
\rho\sim 10^{14}
g/cm
3
). The mean free path
\lambda
is extremely short (
\lambda\ll R_{\text{core}}
), forcing neutrinos to escape via diffusion. The diffusion timescale is given by
Burrows and Lattimer 1986
;
Pons et al. 1999
;
Sato and Suzuki 1987
:

t_{\text{diff}}\sim\frac{R^{2}}{c\lambda}\approx 10\text{ s}.

(8)

This mechanism ensures that the gravitational binding energy is released gradually, sustaining the neutrino signal for several seconds . This impose a different constraints according the nature of the neutrinos:

•

If neutrinos are Dirac fermions, RSFP converts the active left-handed state
\nu_{L}
into a sterile right-handed state
\nu_{R}
. Crucially, sterile neutrinos do not participate in weak interactions and thus have an effectively infinite mean free path in the core. This induces a rapid escape for the core energy, reducing the burst duration from typically
t_{\text{diff}}\approx 10
s to
t_{\text{free}}\sim 1
s. This induces a strong bound on the neutrino magnetic moment which must be (
\mu_{\nu}\lesssim 10^{-12}\mu_{B}
)
Raffelt 2001
;
Burrows 1988
.

•

If the neutrinos are Majorana fermions. the RSFP mechanism converts
\nu_{e}\to\bar{\nu}_{e}
(or
\bar{\nu}_{\mu/\tau}
). Unlike sterile states, the resulting antineutrinos are active and interact via the Weak force. Consequently, they remain trapped within the core and diffuse out on the standard timescale
t_{\text{diff}}
.

IV
Envelope RSFP for Dirac Neutrinos

While core-based RSFP for Dirac neutrinos is tightly constrained by the supernova cooling timescale, a window exists in the outer stellar envelope. In this region, the density drops by orders of magnitude, allowing for efficient resonant conversion without affecting the protoneutron star’s thermal evolution.

IV.1
Decoupling Cooling from Detection

The ”rapid cooling” constraint is only applicable if the conversion
\nu_{L}\to\nu_{R}
occurs within the trapping sphere (
R\lesssim 50
km,
\rho\gtrsim 10^{11}
g/cm
3
)
Raffelt 2001
;
Janka 1995
;
Burrows and Lattimer 1986
, where neutrinos are thermally coupled to the plasma. If the conversion occurs outside this region, the energy has already effectively decoupled from the core.

•

Core Propagation:
Neutrinos diffuse out of the high-density core as active left-handed states (
\nu_{L}
) over the standard timescale (
t_{\text{diff}}\approx 10
s).

•

Envelope Conversion:
Upon reaching the outer envelope (
R>1000
km), they encounter the resonance condition and convert to othr helicity states.

Since the conversion happens after thermal decoupling, the core cooling history remains unaffected, preserving the 10-second burst duration observed in SN1987A.

IV.2
Resonance in the Envelope

The resonance condition for RSFP is given by
V_{\text{mat}}\approx\Delta m^{2}/2E
. For typical supernova neutrino energies (
E\sim 10
–20 MeV) and standard atmospheric mass splittings (
\Delta m^{2}\approx 2.5\times 10^{-3}
eV
2
), the resonance density is:

\rho_{\text{res}}\approx\frac{m_{N}}{\sqrt{2}G_{F}Y_{e}}\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{2E}\sim 10^{3}-10^{4}\text{ g/cm}^{3}.

(9)

This density range corresponds to the outer Helium or Hydrogen shells of the progenitor
Woosley et al. 2002
;
Woosley and Weaver 1995
;
Sukhbold et al. 2016
;
Kippenhahn et al. 2012
;
Dessart et al. 2010
;
Yoon et al. 2010
;
Crowther 2007
.

IV.3
Adiabaticity and Helicity Inversion

The magnetic fields in the envelope of massive stars remain significant large (
B\sim 10^{6}-10^{8}~G
) or even larger in some models
Heger et al. 2005
;
Spruit 2002
;
Cohen and Soker 2023
;
Wade and the MiMeS Collaboration 2010
;
Vlemmings et al. 2003
;
Wickramasinghe et al. 2003
;
Moss 1989
;
Braithwaite and Spruit 2004
;
Wickramasinghe et al. 2014
;
Vink and Kuiper 2006
;
Donati et al. 2006
;
Wade and the MiMeS Collaboration 2010
. For a magnetic moment
\mu_{\nu}\approx 10^{-12}\mu_{B}
, the transition is still fully adiabatic:

\gamma_{\text{env}}\propto\frac{(2\mu_{\nu}B)^{2}}{|\nabla V|}\gg 1.

(10)

This leads to a complete conversion of the neutrino helicity.

V
High-Energy Limit: Suppression of RSFP

For supernova bursts, as we show below, the GeV energy neutrino naturally evades the resonant conversion that affects the thermal MeV neutrino and as a consequence, we shall show that it can be acting as a “standard candle” for normalizing the flux.

V.1
Inverse Resonance Scaling

The resonance condition for Spin-Flavor Precession is determined by the following relation between the matter potential and the vacuum oscillation frequency:

V_{\text{mat}}(r)\approx\frac{\Delta m^{2}}{2E}\cos 2\theta.

(11)

Since
V_{\text{mat}}\propto\rho(r)
, the density required for resonance scales inversely with neutrino energy:

\rho_{\text{res}}\propto\frac{1}{E_{\nu}}.

(12)

•

Thermal Neutrinos (
E\sim 10
MeV):
The resonance occurs at densities
\rho\sim 10^{3}-10^{4}\text{ g/cm}^{3}
, locating the transition deep within the Helium or Hydrogen envelope where magnetic fields are substantial (
B\sim 10^{6}
G) as previously shown.

•

High-Energy Neutrinos (
E\sim 1
GeV):
The resonance density drops to
\rho\sim 10\text{ g/cm}^{3}
. But assuming that the magnetic field behaves as dipole and goes like
r^{-3}
Jackson 1999
. The magnetic field in this region of the star is too weak to generate an efficient RSFP conversion as it can be shown computing the adiabaticity parameter in this region:

\gamma_{\text{GeV}}\propto\frac{(\mu_{\nu}B^{2})}{|\nabla V|}\to 0.

(13)

As the resonance for GeV neutrinos moves to large radii, the magnetic field strength drops to negligible values (
B\lesssim 100
G). The adiabaticity parameter consequently collapses. Due to the suppression of adiabaticity, the helicity parameter for high-energy neutrinos remains ”frozen” at its initial value:

S_{\parallel}^{\text{GeV}}\approx-1\quad(\text{Pure Left-Handed}).

(14)

VI
Signatures in Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS)

The detection of supernova neutrinos via Coherent Elastic Neutrino-Nucleus Scattering (CE
\nu
NS) is flavor-blind (sensitive to all active neutrino species
\nu_{e},\nu_{\mu},\nu_{\tau}
and their antiparticles via Neutral Current interactions). CE
\nu
NS provides a measure of the total active neutrino flux. The differential cross-section depends on the square of the neutrino energy (
E_{\nu}^{2}
) and the square of the neutron number (
N^{2}
), but is largely independent of neutrino flavor
Freedman 1974
;
Kopeliovich and Frankfurt 1974
;
Drukier and Stodolsky 1984
;
Horowitz et al. 2003
;
Lindner et al. 2017
;
Kosmas et al. 2017
;
Bednyakov and Naumov 2018
;
Akimov et al. 2017
;
Tomalak et al. 2021
:

\frac{d\sigma}{dT}\approx\frac{G_{F}^{2}M}{4\pi}Q_{W}^{2}\left(1-\frac{MT}{2E_{\nu}^{2}}\right)F^{2}(T),

(15)

where
T
is the recoil energy,
Q_{w}=N-(1-4\sin^{2}\theta_{W})Z
is the weak nuclear charge,
N
is the number of neutrons and
Z
is the number of proton and
F(T)
is the nuclear form factor.

VI.1
Dirac Signature: The suppressed Standard Candle

In the Dirac Envelope RSFP scenario, active left-handed neutrinos are converted into sterile right-handed states (
\nu_{L}\to\nu_{R}
) as they traverse the resonance in the outer envelope. Since sterile neutrinos do not interact via the Weak force, they effectively vanish from the detector. CE
\nu
NS detectors would observe a global suppression in the total event count, potentially reducing the signal by
\sim 50\%
(assuming total helicity conversion)
The signature is therefore a
long-duration but anomalously suppressed
neutrino burst.

VI.2
Majorana Signature: Distortion in spectral recoil measurement compared to Standard Model

In the Majorana scenario, RSFP converts active neutrinos into active antineutrinos (e.g.,
\nu_{e}\to\bar{\nu}_{e}
or
\nu_{e}\to\bar{\nu}_{x}
). Because the final states are active and CE
\nu
NS is flavor-blind, the total number of scattering targets remains conserved. There is no ”missing energy” deficit. Supernova simulations indicate that non-electron neutrinos (
\nu_{x}
) possess a higher average energy than electron neutrinos (
\langle E_{\nu_{x}}\rangle>\langle E_{\nu_{e}}\rangle
) due to opacity differences. If these models are corrected, it means that the energy distribution of the flux reaching Earth will be distorted
Cadeddu et al. 2018
.

VII
Experimental Strategy: Signal Separation and Normalization

As we have previously seen, high energy supernova neutrinos tail are not affected by RSFP and can be observe at
CE\nu NS
experiment. So a model-independent way to measure the supernova neutrino flux is possible normalizing the neutrino flux using these high energy neutrinos allowing to isolate the magnetic moment signature from astrophysical uncertainties.
In a E
\nu
NS detectors, it is relatively easy to select events produced by supernova neutrinos as they are produced in a very short 10 second time window. So, if the analysis is connected to external alert system as SuperNova Early Warning system (SNEWS)
Scholberg 2012
;
Beacom and Vogel 1999
;
Antonioli et al. 2004
;
Al Kharusi et al. 2021
;
Linzer and Scholberg 2019
, the steady-state background expectation can be easily controlled.

A critical systematic uncertainty in supernova neutrino physics is the total luminosity and distance to the progenitor star. To distinguish a ”dimmed” Dirac signal from a naturally low-luminosity supernova, we propose to use the high-energy neutrino tail (
E_{\nu}\sim 1
GeV) as an
in situ
calibrator.

As derived in Section
V
, RSFP is suppressed for GeV neutrinos because their resonance occurs in the stellar wind where magnetic fields are negligible (
\gamma\to 0
). Consequently, the high-energy flux reaches Earth unmodified by magnetic moments, representing the ”true” luminosity of the source.

A normalized observable
R
defined as the ratio of the thermal CE
\nu
NS events (
N_{\text{therm}}
) to the high-energy Charged Current (CC) events (
N_{\text{CC}}
) detected in companion observatories (e.g., Super-Kamiokande or IceCube) can be constructed:

R=\frac{N_{\text{therm}}^{\text{CE}\nu\text{NS}}\text{ (Observed)}}{N_{\text{CC}}^{\text{GeV}}\text{ (Observed)}}.

(16)

This ratio cancels the distance and luminosity uncertainties, leaving a sensitive probe of the magnetic moment. According to its value, one can distinguish three cases:

1.

Standard Model (Null Hypothesis):

R\approx R_{\text{SM}}.

(17)

Both spectral components propagate without conversion. It means that the RSFP is not sufficient enough and it induces a limit on
\mu_{\nu}\ll 10^{-14}\mu_{B}
, improving by two order of magnitude the existing limit on the neutrino magnetic moment.

2.

Dirac Scenario (Envelope RSFP):

R\ll R_{\text{SM}}.

(18)

The numerator is suppressed by the conversion of active thermal neutrinos into sterile states (
\nu_{L}\to\nu_{R}
), while the denominator remains constant.

3.

Majorana Scenario (Envelope RSFP):

R\approx R_{\text{SM}}\quad\text{with Spectral modification}.

(19)

The total event count in the numerator is conserved (active-to-active conversion), preserving the ratio. However, the CE
\nu
NS recoil spectrum will exhibit a statistically significant hardening due to the change of non-electron species into the electron-flavor sector
Cadeddu et al. 2018
.

This normalization strategy transforms the search for neutrino magnetic moments from an absolute flux measurement (limited by astrophysical models) into a robust relative measurement between two energy channels.

VIII
Conclusion

In this work, we have shown that the outer envelope of a core-collapse supernova is an appropriate zone to generate RSFP efficient helicity conversion and at the same time being compatible with the cooling constraints of SN1987A.
Our analysis reveals a critical dichotomy governed by the location of the resonance:

1.

Core Region (
R<50
km):
High matter densities and collective effects suppress RSFP for standard parameters, or lead to rapid cooling that is experimentally excluded. This effectively ”vetoes” core-based conversion for Dirac neutrinos.

2.

Envelope Region (
R>1000
km):
We identified a ”safe zone” in the outer stellar envelope where the resonance naturally occurs for thermal neutrinos (
E\sim 10
MeV) after they have thermally decoupled from the core.

We demonstrate that for neutrino magnetic moments in the range
\mu_{\nu}\in[10^{-14},10^{-12}]\,\mu_{B}
, the transition in the envelope is fully adiabatic. This leads to macroscopic helicity inversion without violating the cooling duration constraints. The resulting experimental signatures in CE
\nu
NS detectors can be summarize in the following way:

•

Dirac Neutrinos:
Manifest as a
”dimmed standard candle”
—a burst with standard duration (
\sim 10
s) but a massive flux deficit (
\sim 50\%
) due to conversion into sterile states.

•

Majorana Neutrinos:
Manifest as a
spectrally hardened
burst with conserved total flux.

Crucially, we introduced a robust normalization strategy using the high-energy neutrino tail (
E\sim 1
GeV). Since RSFP is suppressed at high energies due to the adiabaticity collapse in the weak stellar wind, the GeV flux serves as an
in situ
calibrator. By measuring the ratio of thermal CE
\nu
NS events to high-energy Charged Current events, astrophysical uncertainties regarding distance and luminosity can be effectively cancelled.

Ultimately, this multi-messenger approach transforms the next galactic supernova into a precision laboratory. The observation of these signatures would not only settle the Dirac-Majorana debate but would also probe neutrino magnetic moments down to
\mu_{\nu}\sim 10^{-14}\mu_{B}
, improving upon current solar and terrestrial limits by more than two orders of magnitude.

Color as the Impetus: Transforming Few-Shot Learner
Chaofei Qi, Zhitai Liu, Jianbin Qiu, Coauthor, Coauthor, Coauthor

Abstract
Humans possess innate meta-learning capabilities, partly attributable to their exceptional color perception. In this paper, we pioneer an innovative viewpoint on few-shot learning by simulating human color perception mechanisms. We propose the ColorSense Learner, a bio-inspired meta-learning framework that capitalizes on inter-channel feature extraction and interactive learning. By strategically emphasizing distinct color information across different channels, our approach effectively filters irrelevant features while capturing discriminative characteristics. Color information represents the most intuitive visual feature, yet conventional meta-learning methods have predominantly neglected this aspect, focusing instead on abstract feature differentiation across categories. Our framework bridges the gap via synergistic color-channel interactions, enabling better intra-class commonality extraction and larger inter-class differences. Furthermore, we introduce a meta-distiller based on knowledge distillation, ColorSense Distiller, which incorporates prior teacher knowledge to augment the student network’s meta-learning capacity. We’ve conducted comprehensive coarse/fine-grained and cross-domain experiments on eleven few-shot benchmarks for validation. Numerous experiments reveal that our methods have extremely strong generalization ability, robustness, and transferability, and effortless handle few-shot classification from the perspective of color perception. Our codes are available at https://github.com/ChaofeiQI/CoSeLearner .

Unlike conventional meta-learning focusing on abstract feature extraction, we focus on cognitive-inspired few-shot learners based on human color perception. Bio-inspired strategy can tackle few-shot scenarios via color cognition principle mimicking human vision.

Related Work

In this section, we thoroughly investigate the current research landscape of few-shot learning from two distinct perspectives: meta-learning and knowledge distillation.

2.1
Meta-Learning based Few-Shot Learning

Meta-learning for few-shot learning has witnessed rapid development over the past decade, evolving from ProtoNet
[
58

Based on the learning focuses, we can categorize it into three major types.
The meta-learner parameter optimization (MPO) strategy

focuses on enhancing the generalization ability of few-shot learners through parameter fine-tuning. It aims to improve the meta-learner’s performance by better learning hyperparameters. However, a clear drawback is that performance improvement hinges on the learner itself. For instance, NIW-Meta
[
32
]
tackles few-shot problems by enhancing model capabilities via hierarchical Bayesian inference.
The strategy based on meta-semantic assistance (MSA)
[

50

3

38

10

leverages semantic information provided by large language models (LLMs) to aid in the visual information extraction. Categorical semantic information usually encompasses more macroscopic category features, which can guide encoders focused on the abstract feature extraction.
This type of approach has obvious advantages, but it also faces two challenges: how to better integrate semantic and visual information, and how to effectively filter redundant information to avoid overfitting.
The meta-network reconstruction (MNR) strategy
[
25
,
22
,
93
,
18
,
71
,
52
,
78

focuses on enhancing network by reconstructing encoders and classifiers. This approach holds great promise for development, with the emergence of new theories driving rapid performance improvements. Our ColorSense Learner falls into this category, capturing more crucial information differences between different classes through network structure reconstruction.

2.2
Knowledge-Distilling based Few-Shot Learning

By leveraging knowledge distillation
[
87
,
29
]
, student networks can assimilate the prior knowledge from teacher models, thereby facilitating their attainment of superior performance in downstream tasks.
From a model-level perspective, student models generally feature lower spatial and computational complexity. In the training process, they are more inclined to exhibit more rapid convergence.
In scenarios of tasks with limited samples, such as few-shot learning, knowledge distillation can be utilized to facilitate cross-model knowledge transfer, thus circumventing the need to learn features and patterns from the ground up.
At present, few-shot learners relying on knowledge distillation can be categorized into the self-supervised series
[
22
,
7
,
43

and the supervised series
[
39
,
80

In this paper, we present a meta-distiller in the supervised type. Our approach is unique because it tries to get prior knowledge from the teacher model in terms of color perception. This helps boost the student network’s color-perception ability and handle few-shot tasks better.

3
Methodology

To tackle the challenges presented by scarce samples, we devise an innovative few-shot learner named ColorSense Learner, which can harness the interactive learning among color channels by simulating human color perception.
After that, leveraging the knowledge distillation strategy, we further unveil a groundbreaking meta-distiller, ColorSense Distiller, to elevate the capability of our few-shot learner.

6
Conclusion

Driven by the extraordinary color-sensitivity of the human eyes,
we approach the FSL problem from a novel color-perception perspective.
In this paper, we put forward a novel meta-learner, CoSeLearner and a meta-distiller named CoSeDistiller.
The former comprehensively groups and extracts image color features, and then enables feature interaction across color channels through color pattern. The latter utilizes the teacher model’s prior knowledge to enhance the student network’s generalization.
Compared with conventional attention methods centered on abstract features, our approach focuses on color perception. This strategy can better capture intra-class commonalities and amplify inter-class disparities, significantly reducing the probability of misrecognition.
Numerous validation experiments demonstrate their strong generalization, robustness, and transferability, allowing them to effortless handle the diverse FSL problems.
Moreover, it is demonstrated that attaining color perception through interactive learning among color channels can effectively handle few-shot downstream vision tasks.

ABSTRACT NUMBER: ESOC2026A1404 ULTRA–HIGH-FIELD MRI FOLLOW-UP IN TRANSIENT GLOBAL AMNESIA WITH DIFFUSION-WEIGHTED LESIONS: A 7-TESLA STUDY
Authors: Jorge Plümer, Aliaksandr Harustovich, Omid Nikoubashman, Wolf-Rüdiger Schäbitz
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackground and aimsTransient global amnesia (TGA) is a clinically well-defined syndrome, frequently associated with diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI) lesions on conventional MRI. The pathophysiological relevance of these lesions and their long-term structural correlates remain incompletely understood. 7-Tesla MRI offers substantially improved spatial resolution and may provide new insights into residual tissue changes after TGA.MethodsWe retrospectively screened for patients diagnosed with TGA between July 2024 - July 2025. Eight patients with detectable DWI lesions on routine MRI were identified and invited to undergo follow-up 7-Tesla MRI. Analyses focused on anatomical regions corresponding to the previously detected diffusion-restricted lesions.ResultsFour patients were female, median age 61 years. On initial MRI, there were 1-3 hippocampal DWI lesions per patient, occurring either unilaterally or bilaterally, without synchronous cerebral infarctions. Median interval between the clinical event and 7-Tesla MRI was 9.6 months. In 50% of patients, no residual structural abnormalities were identified. 2/8 (25%) patients demonstrated persistent focal structural alterations at prior DWI restriction sites, including T2-hyperintense discontinuities of the pyramidal cell layer and focal thinning and indentation of the polymorphic layer. Two further patients (25%) exhibited equivocal residual findings corresponding to the initial DWI lesions, potentially attributable to improved tissue contrast at 7-Tesla MRI, comprising subtle susceptibility-related signal alterations (1/8) and patchy T1-hypointense lesions (1/8) without T2-weighted correlates.ConclusionsOur findings suggest heterogeneous underlying mechanisms in TGA, with distinct subgroups showing either transient or persistent structural imaging changes.

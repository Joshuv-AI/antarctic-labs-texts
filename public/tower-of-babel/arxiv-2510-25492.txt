Cubic polynomials and sums of two squares
Siddharth Iyer

Abstract
. We state a lower bound for how often an irreducible monic cubic polynomial is a sum of two squares ( □ 2 \raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2} ). To do so, we rely on a two-dimensional unit argument, and degree six number fields. As an example we can show that if h h is an odd integer which is not a cube, then # { n : n 3 + h ∈ □ 2 , 1 ≤ n ≤ x } ≫ x 1 / 3 − o ⁡ ( 1 ) \#\{n:\ n^{3}+h\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2},\ 1\leq n\leq x\}\gg x^{1/3-o(1)} . Our arguments may be generalised to generate literature for how often an irreducible monic cubic polynomial is represented in the quadratic form x 2 + n ​ y 2 x^{2}+ny^{2} , provided n ∈ ℕ n\in\mathbb{N} .

1.
Introduction

In this paper we aim to study integer points on the Châtelet surface
y^{2}+z^{2}=\mathbf{p}(x)
, where
\mathbf{p}(x)
denotes an irreducible monic polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
of degree 3.
Let
B_{\mathbf{p}}(X)
be the set of integers
n
with
1\leq n\leq X
so that
\mathbf{p}(n)
is a sum of two squares (
\mathbf{p}(n)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}
). We prove the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

If
\mathbf{p}(x)=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
is an irreducible cubic polynomial with
a_{2}^{2}-a_{1}
even, and
a_{1}a_{2}-a_{0}
odd, then

\displaystyle\#B_{\mathbf{p}}(X)\gg X^{1/3-o(1)}.

Our result builds upon work of Friedlander and Iwaniec regarding values of quadratic polynomials expressible as a value of a quadratic form
[
2
]
.

Our result is also related to a result of Iwaniec and Munshi
[
4
]
regarding densities of rational points on the surface
\phi(y,z)=\mathbf{p}(x)
, where
\phi
is an irreducible quadratic form with rational coefficients, and
\mathbf{p}(x)
is an irreducible cubic polynomial in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
.

We note that the bound in Theorem
1.1
is not always optimal. If we can find an integer-valued quadratic polynomial
q_{0}
, and polynomials
q_{1},q_{2}\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
such that
\mathbf{p}(q_{0}(x))=q_{1}(x)^{2}+q_{2}(x)^{2}
then
\#B_{\mathbf{p}}(X)\gg X^{1/2}
(this idea was inspired by an online post on MathOverflow by Grechuk
[
3
]
). Through trial and error we obtain the identity

\displaystyle(x^{2}+8)^{3}+17=(x^{3}+10x)^{2}+(2x^{2}+23)^{2},

so that
\#B_{x^{3}+17}(X)\gg X^{1/2}
. Also, when
n
is an integer we trivially have
\#B_{x^{3}+n^{2}}(X)\gg X^{1/2}
as for any integer
m
, the quantity
m^{6}+n^{2}
is a sum of two squares. In general, the author has not been able to find such polynomial substitutions for an arbitrary monic polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
of degree
3
. We make the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.2
.

Is it true that for any irreducible monic polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
of degree
3
, say
\mathbf{p}(x)
, there exists an integer-valued quadratic polynomial
q_{0}(x)
, and polynomials
q_{1}(x),q_{2}(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
such that
\mathbf{p}(q_{0}(x))=q_{1}(x)^{2}+q_{2}(x)^{2}
?

Related to this conjecture, a result by Schinzel in
[
5
, Lemma 10]
demonstrates that for any such polynomial
\mathbf{p}(x)
, there exists a quadratic polynomial
\mathbf{h}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
and a cubic polynomial
\mathbf{g}(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
such that
\mathbf{g}(x)
divides
\mathbf{p}(\mathbf{h}(x))
. Perhaps applying the techniques used in the paper
[
5
]
to the polynomial ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[i][x]
may settle the conjecture.

Let
\mathbf{p}(x)
be an irreducible polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
with
\mathbf{p}(\theta)=0
for some
\theta\in{\mathbb{C}}
. To study when
\mathbf{p}(n)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}
, we investigate when
q^{2}(n-\theta)
is a sum of two squares in the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta]
for some natural number
q
. Define

\displaystyle\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}(\theta)=\{w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}:\ w_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta]\}.

We have the following lemma:

Lemma 1.3
.

If an integer
z\in{\mathbb{Z}}
satisfies

\displaystyle q^{2}(z-\theta)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}(\theta)

for some
q\in\mathbb{N}
, then
\mathbf{p}(z)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}
.

With the ring
{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta]
we define a notion of maximum coefficient, where
M_{\theta}(c_{0}+c_{1}\theta+c_{2}\theta^{2})=\max\{|c_{0}|,|c_{1}|,|c_{2}|\}
. For
Y,Y_{1},Y_{2}\geq 0
, define

\displaystyle\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}(\theta;Y):=\{w_{1}^{2}+w_{2}^{2}:\ w_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta],\ M_{\theta}(w_{i})\leq Y\}

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=\{n:\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ q^{2}(n-\theta)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}(\theta;Y_{1}),\ |n|\leq Y_{2}\}.

With Lemma
1.3
we have

(1.1)

\displaystyle\#B_{\mathbf{p}}(X)\geq\#\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y,X)

for all
X,Y\geq 0
and
q\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Let

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2}):=\left\{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):\ \begin{aligned} &\omega_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta],\ M_{\theta}(\omega_{i})\leq Y_{1},\\
&\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ |n|\leq Y_{2},\ \omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}=q^{2}(n-\theta)\end{aligned}\right\}.

We relate the sets
\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})
and
\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})
.

Lemma 1.4
.

If
\theta
is a real algebraic integer of degree 3,
\varepsilon>0
and
Y_{1},Y_{2}\geq 2
we have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\gg\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\cdot Y_{2}^{-\varepsilon}\cdot\log^{-2}Y_{1},

where the implied constant depends only on
q,\varepsilon
and
\theta
.

Lower bounds for
\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(\cdot,\cdot)
alongside Lemma
1.4
and inequality (
1.1
), can be used to obtain lower-bounds for
\#B_{\mathbf{p}}(\cdot)
. In the subsequent lemma we pursue lower-bounds for
\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(\cdot,\cdot)
under parity conditions on the coefficients of
\mathbf{p}
.

Lemma 1.5
.

If
\mathbf{p}(x)=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
is an irreducible cubic polynomial with
a_{2}^{2}-a_{1}
even, and
a_{1}a_{2}-a_{0}
odd, and
\theta
is a root of
\mathbf{p}
, we have the estimate

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(X,X)\gg X^{1/3-o(1)}.

Thus Theorem
1.1
follows from Lemma
1.5
, Lemma
1.4
and inequality (
1.1
).

2.
Proof of Lemma
1.3

Note that
\mathbf{p}(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
. If
q^{2}(z-\theta)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}(\theta)
, then we have

\displaystyle z-\theta=(v_{0}+v_{1}\theta+v_{2}\theta^{2})^{2}+(w_{0}+w_{1}\theta+w_{2}\theta^{2})^{2}

(2.1)

\displaystyle=(v_{0}+v_{1}\theta+v_{2}\theta^{2}+i(w_{0}+w_{1}\theta+w_{2}\theta^{2}))\times

\displaystyle\times(v_{0}+v_{1}\theta+v_{2}\theta^{2}-i(w_{0}+w_{1}\theta+w_{2}\theta^{2})),

for some rational values
v_{d},w_{d}
. Furthermore, whenever
\alpha
is a root of
\mathbf{p}
, identity (
2
) holds with
\theta
replaced with
\alpha
(due to the isomorphism
{\mathbb{Q}}[i][\theta]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}[i][\alpha]
fixing
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
and sending
\theta
to
\alpha
). In particular we have that

\displaystyle P_{1}:=\prod_{\theta:\mathbf{p}(\theta)=0}(v_{0}+v_{1}\theta+v_{2}\theta^{2}+i(w_{0}+w_{1}\theta+w_{2}\theta^{2}))

is a symmetric polynomial over
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[i][\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}]
, where
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
are roots of
\mathbf{p}(x)
(here we used that
\mathbf{p}(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
). Thus
P_{1}
is a Gaussian rational, say
u+vi\in{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
. Also note that

\displaystyle P_{2}

\displaystyle:=\prod_{\theta:\ L(\theta)=0}(v_{0}+v_{1}\theta+v_{2}\theta^{2}-i(w_{0}+w_{1}\theta+w_{2}\theta^{2}))

\displaystyle=\overline{P_{1}}=u-vi.

Now with (
2
) we write

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}(z)=\prod_{\theta:\ \mathbf{p}(\theta)=0}(z-\theta)=P_{1}P_{2}=u^{2}+v^{2}.

Thus
\mathbf{p}(z)
is an integer, and a sum of two rational squares, implying that
\mathbf{p}(z)\in\raisebox{-2.15277pt}{\scalebox{2}{$\square$}}_{2}
.

3.
Proof of Lemma
1.4

For
Y,Y_{1},Y_{2}\geq 0
and
q\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(\omega,Y):=\left\{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):\ \omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}=\omega,\ M_{\theta}(\omega_{i})\leq Y,\ \omega_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta]\right\},

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(Y):=\left\{(c_{0}+id_{0})+(c_{1}+id_{1})\theta+(c_{2}+id_{2})\theta^{2}:\ c_{j},d_{j}\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ |c_{j}|,|d_{j}|\leq Y\right\}.

Observe that we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})=\left\{n:\ n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ \#\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n-\theta),Y_{1})>0,\ |n|\leq Y_{2}\right\}.

and let
\mathbf{W}_{\theta}:{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta]\times{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
be the bijective map

\displaystyle\mathbf{W}_{\theta}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})=\omega_{1}+i\omega_{2}.

When
\theta_{1}
and
\theta_{2}
are roots of a degree
3
monic irreducible polynomial, say
p(x)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
, we observe that there exists a unique field isomorphism
\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}:{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta_{1},i]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta_{2},i]
which fixes
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
but sends
\theta_{1}
to
\theta_{2}
(as the polynomial
\mathbf{p}(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

Let

\displaystyle\mathbf{m}(\theta,q,Y_{1},Y_{2})=\max\limits_{n\in\mathbb{Z},\ \lvert n\rvert\leq Y_{2}}\#\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n-\theta),Y_{1})+1.

We have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})\geq\frac{\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})}{\mathbf{m}(\theta,q,Y_{1},Y_{2})}.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\bigcup_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ |n|\leq Y_{2}}\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n-\theta),Y_{1})=

\displaystyle=\left\{(\omega_{1},\omega_{2}):\ \begin{aligned} &\omega_{i}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[\theta],\ M_{\theta}(\omega_{i})\leq Y_{1},\\
&\exists n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ |n|\leq Y_{2},\ \omega_{1}^{2}+\omega_{2}^{2}=q^{2}(n-\theta)\end{aligned}\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2}).

Furthermore, for
n_{1}\neq n_{2}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n_{1}-\theta),Y_{1})\cap\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n_{2}-\theta),Y_{1})=\emptyset.

Hence with (
3.1
) we have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ |n|\leq Y_{2}}\#\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n-\theta),Y_{1})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\in\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2})}\#\mathcal{A}_{\theta}(q^{2}(n-\theta),Y_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{m}(\theta,q,Y_{1},Y_{2})\cdot\#\mathcal{E}_{\theta,q}(Y_{1},Y_{2}).

Lemma 3.2
.

If
Y\geq 1
and
w\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(Y)
is a non-zero element of
{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
then

\displaystyle Y^{-2}\ll|w|\ll Y,

where
|\cdot|
denotes the complex modulus operator.

Proof.

The upper-bound follows from the triangle inequality. Let
\mathbf{p}(x)
be the degree
3
monic irreducible polynomial for
\theta
with roots
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
respectively, where without loss of generality we may assume
\theta=\theta_{1}
. Suppose that

\displaystyle w=(c_{0}+id_{0})+(c_{1}+id_{1})\theta+(c_{2}+id_{2})\theta^{2}

for integers
c_{j},d_{j}
, then

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left((c_{0}+id_{0})+(c_{1}+id_{1})\theta_{j}+(c_{2}+id_{2})\theta_{j}^{2}\right)

is a symmetric polynomial in
{\mathbb{Z}}[i][\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}]
and thus is a guassian integer, necessarily non-zero as
w\neq 0
. Thus we have

\displaystyle|w|\left|\prod_{j=1}^{2}\left((c_{0}+id_{0})+(c_{1}+id_{1})\theta_{j}+(c_{2}+id_{2})\theta_{j}^{2}\right)\right|\geq 1,

or
|w|\cdot Y^{2}\gg 1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

If
\beta
is an algebraic integer of degree
3
, then
{\mathrm{Im}}(\beta)\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}\setminus\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose
\beta=\mu+ih
is an algebraic integer of degree
3
for some
h\neq 0,h\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
\mu\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Then it has a minimal polynomial
x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
with roots
\mu+ih
,
\mu-ih,
and
\theta
, where
\theta
is real. By comparing with values of coefficients we have
\theta+2\mu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
and
2\theta\mu+\mu^{2}+h^{2}\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, therefore
\theta
is a root of a quadratic polynomial in
{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
, but this is impossible as
\theta
is of degree
3
(otherwise
\beta
will be of degree
\leq 2
).

\sqcup

Lemma 3.4
.

If
\theta
is an algebraic integer of degree
3
with minimal polynomial
P(x)
then
\theta+i
is an algebraic integer of degree
6
. Furthermore the minimal polynomial of
\theta+i
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
is
Q(x)
, where

\displaystyle Q(x)=\prod_{d=1}^{3}(x-(\theta_{d}+i))(x-(\theta_{d}-i)),

here
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}
and
\theta_{3}
are the distinct roots of
P(x)
.

Proof.

Let
P(x)=x^{3}+a_{2}x^{2}+a_{1}x+a_{0}\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
. We can consider the polynomials
P_{1}(x)
and
P_{2}(x)
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[i][x]
which are defined as the binomial expansions of
P(x+i)
and
P(x-i)
respectively. It is clear that
P(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
(otherwise
[{\mathbb{Q}}[i]:{\mathbb{Q}}]\geq 3
which is impossible), thus
P_{1}(x)
and
P_{2}(x)
are irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
. Both
P_{1}(x)
and
P_{2}(x)
are not polynomials in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
as the
x^{2}
coefficients of the polynomials are
3i+a_{2}
and
(-3i+a_{2})
respectively. Observe that the polynomial
Q(x)=P_{1}(x)P_{2}(x)
has integer valued coefficients, thus the minimal polynomial of
\theta+i
over the integers divides
Q(x)
.

If
Q(x)
factors as
H_{1}(x)H_{2}(x)
, where
H_{j}(x)\in\mathbb{Q}[x]
and
\deg(H_{j}(x))\geq 1
, then it factors similarly as
H_{1}(x)\cdot H_{2}(x)
in
{\mathbb{Q}}[i][x]
. Since
{\mathbb{Q}}[i][x]
is a unique factorization domain with units
c
, where
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}[i]\setminus\{0\}
, we have
H_{1}(x)=cP_{\sigma(1)}(x)
and
H_{2}(x)=(1/c)P_{\sigma(2)}(x)
, where
\sigma:\{1,2\}\rightarrow\{1,2\}
is a permutation map and
c\in{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
,
c\neq 0
. If
c
is real then
H_{j}(x)\not\in{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
, which is impossible. If
c
is complex then the
x^{3}
coefficients of
H_{1}(x),H_{2}(x)
are complex which is also impossible. Hence the polynomial
Q(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

If
\theta
is an algebraic integer of degree
3
then the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
has rank of two.

Proof.

If
K={\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
, then by Lemma
3.4
we have
K={\mathbb{Q}}[\theta+i]
, with the minimal polynomial of
\theta+i
being
Q(x)
. By Lemma
3.3
, all the roots of
Q
are complex-valued. Thus by Dirichlet’s unit theorem the rank of the group of units of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is
(6/2)-1=2
.

\sqcup

Lemma 3.6
.

If
\theta
is a
real
algebraic integer of degree
3
, there exists a finite set
W
of units in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
, such that for every unit
u\in\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
there exists integers
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
so that

\displaystyle u=w\cdot l_{1}^{t_{1}}l_{2}^{t_{2}},

where
l_{1},l_{2}
are units in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
with the complex modulus of
l_{1}
not equal to
1
(
|l_{1}|\neq 1
),
|l_{2}|=1
, and
w
is some unit belonging to
W
.

Proof.

By lemma
3.5
, there exists two multiplicatively independent units
u_{1}
and
u_{2}
in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
so that every unit
u
in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
is of the form

\displaystyle u=T\cdot u_{1}^{t_{1}}u_{2}^{t_{2}},

for some
T\in\text{Tor}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]})
and integers
t_{1}
and
t_{2}
. By Dirichlet’s unit theorem there exists a unit
q_{1}
of infinite order in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta]}
, which is also a real unit in
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
. We necessarily have
|q_{1}|\neq 1
.

Since
{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
is closed under complex conjugation, by
[
1
, Theorem 1]
there exists a unit
q_{2}
of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
of infinite order with
|q_{2}|=1
(this unit is not a torsion element). We observe that there exists integers
s_{1},s_{2},y_{1},y_{2}
and two torsion elements
g,h\in\text{Tor}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]})
so that

\displaystyle q_{1}=g\cdot u_{1}^{s_{1}}u_{2}^{s_{2}}

and

\displaystyle q_{2}=h\cdot u_{1}^{y_{1}}u_{2}^{y_{2}}.

Take
l_{1}=q_{1}g^{-1}
and
l_{2}=q_{2}h^{-1}
. Observe that
l_{1}
and
l_{2}
are multiplicatively independent, thus the matrix

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}s_{1}&y_{1}\\
s_{2}&y_{2}\end{bmatrix}

has non-zero determinant. Let
D=|s_{1}y_{2}-y_{1}s_{2}|
, so that
D\geq 1
, and put

\displaystyle W=\{T\cdot u_{1}^{v_{1}}u_{2}^{v_{2}}:\ T\in\text{Tor}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}),\ v_{i}\in\{1,\ldots,D\}\}.

The lemma follows with the choices of
l_{1},l_{2}
and
W
. Here it is useful to recall that
\#\text{Tor}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]})<\infty
, and the torsion points of
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
are precisely the roots of cyclotomic polynomials (which have complex modulus one) within
{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
.

\sqcup

Lemma 3.7
.

If
\theta
is a real algebraic integer of degree
3
,
l_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]}
is a unit of infinite order with
|l_{2}|=1
,
b\in{\mathbb{Q}}[\theta,i]
is non-zero, and
Y\geq 2
then

\displaystyle\#\left\{n:n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ b\cdot l_{2}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(Y)\right\}\ll\log Y,

where the implied constant is independent of
b
, but dependent on
l_{2}
.

Proof.

Let
p(x)
be the minimal monic polynomial of
\theta
in
{\mathbb{Z}}[x]
, which is necessarily irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
; also let
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
be the roots of
p(x)
with
\theta=\theta_{1}
. Observe that
l_{2}
is the root of the polynomial
q(x)
where

\displaystyle q(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(x-\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(l_{2})\right)\left(x-\overline{\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(l_{2})}\right).

Observe that if
x
is real then
q(x)
is real, thus
q(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]
. Since
p(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
, the polynomials
\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(x-\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(l_{2})\right)
and
\prod_{j=1}^{3}\left(x-\overline{\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(l_{2})}\right)
belong to
{\mathbb{Q}}[i]
(as both expressions can be expanded as a symmetric polynomial in
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
). Hence we deduce that
q(x)\in{\mathbb{R}}[x]\cap\left({\mathbb{Q}}[i][x]\right)
, implying that
q(x)\in{\mathbb{Q}}[x]
. By Kronecker’s theorem there exists a choice of
j\in\{2,3\}
so that
\left|\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(l_{2})\right|\neq 1
. Now note that we wish to bound from above the cardinality of the set
\mathcal{X}_{\theta}(b,l_{2},Y)
, where

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\theta}(b,l_{2},Y)=\{n:n\in{\mathbb{Z}},\ b\cdot l_{2}^{n}\in\mathcal{C}_{\theta}(Y)\}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{\theta}(b,l_{2};Y)=\mathcal{X}_{\theta_{j}}(\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{j}}(b),\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(l_{2});Y).

Let
\rho=|\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(l_{2})|
and
c=|\kappa_{\theta_{1},\theta_{2}}(b)|
. By lemma
3.2
there exists constants
A,B>0
depending on

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq\alpha\leq(c/4)X^{1/6}\\
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\beta\leq\alpha\\
\gcd(\alpha,\beta)=1\end{subarray}}\#\mathbf{S}_{c}(\alpha,\beta,X)\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq\alpha\leq(c/4)X^{1/6}\\
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\beta\leq\alpha\\
\gcd(\alpha,\beta)=1\end{subarray}}\frac{c\cdot X^{1/6}}{4\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle\gg\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}2\leq\alpha\leq(c/4)X^{1/6}\\
\alpha\equiv 0\pmod{2}\end{subarray}}X^{1/6-\varepsilon}

\displaystyle\gg X^{1/3-\varepsilon}.

Thus we prove that for sufficiently large
X
we have

\displaystyle\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(X,X)\geq\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(dX^{1/2},X)\gg X^{1/3-o(1)},

which proves Lemma
1.5
.

5.
Further Remarks

Theorem
1.1
concerns itself with
25\%
of degree
3
monic irreducible polynomials. This limitation arises because we only transferred lower bounds from
\#\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(\cdot,\cdot)
to
\#B_{\mathbf{p}}(\cdot)
. It is important to note that
\mathcal{R}_{\theta,1}(\cdot,\cdot)
can be empty, for example, if
a_{1}a_{2}-a_{0}
is even. To cover all cases of irreducible monic polynomials of degree
3
, we expect one would need to use sets
\mathcal{R}_{\theta,2^{t}}(\cdot,\cdot)
for a suitable integer
t
and employ enhanced parity arguments; however, we do not pursue these additional complications here. Furthermore, our arguments should generalize to study integer points on the Châtelet surface
y^{2}+nz^{2}=\mathbf{p}(x)
. Here our theory is stronger when
n\in\mathbb{N}
, as our unit arguments can be transferred quite readily, but when
n
is negative, most of our theory breaks down (with the exception of an analogue for Lemma
1.3
). This is because our arguments, even after suitable modification, do not provide sufficient information about the density of units in the ring
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathbb{Q}}(\theta,\sqrt{-n})}
.

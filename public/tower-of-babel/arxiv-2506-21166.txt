1.
Introduction

Morphisms
X_{0}(N)\to Y
defined over number fields are an important topic in arithmetic geometry and have been the subject of extensive research. Let us first discuss the case when
g(Y)=0
and
Y
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over a field
k
. The
k
-gonality of
C
, denoted by
\textup{gon}_{k}C
, is the least degree of a non-constant
k
-rational morphism
f:C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Thus, determining the gonality of
X_{0}(N)
is very important if one wants to study morphisms
X_{0}(N)\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The gonality of modular curves has been extensively studied. Zograf
[
36
]
gave a lower bound for the
\mathbb{C}
-gonality for any modular curve
X(\Gamma)
, linear in terms of the index of the corresponding congruence subgroup
\Gamma
. Later, Abramovich
[
1
]
and Kim and Sarnak
[
26
, Appendix 2]
improved the constant in that lower bound. Moreover, there are upper bounds on the gonality of any algebraic curve in terms of its genus
g
, see
[
31
, Proposition A.1]
.

Regarding the curve
X_{0}(N)
, Ogg
[
30
]
determined all hyperelliptic, Bars
[
3
]
determined all bielliptic, Hasegawa and Shimura
[
17
]
determined all trigonal curves
X_{0}(N)
over
\mathbb{C}
and
\mathbb{Q}
, and Jeon and Park
[
22
]
determined all tetragonal curves
X_{0}(N)
over
\mathbb{C}
. Najman and Orlić
[
28
]
determined all curves
X_{0}(N)
with
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality equal to
4,5,
or
6
. They also determined the
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality of all curves
X_{0}(N)
for
N\leq 144
and for many of those curves also determined their
\mathbb{C}
-gonality.

Let us now consider the case when
g(Y)=1
and
Y=E
is an elliptic curve. Determining all possible degrees of morphisms to
E
is useful when searching for small degree points because the only curves with infinitely many rational points are those of genus
0
isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
and positive rank elliptic curves. If one wants to determine all possible degrees of rational morphisms
X_{0}(N)\to E
, there are two cases with respect to the conductor of
E
:
\textup{cond}(E)=N
and
\textup{cond}(E)<N
. The case
\textup{Cond}(E)=N
is easier and can be dealt with using the Modularity theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Modularity theorem)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve of conductor
N
. Then there exists a non-constant rational morphism
f:X_{0}(N)\to E
. Furthermore, if
f
has the least possible degree, then any other morphism
g:X_{0}(N)\to E
factors through
f
.

Therefore, all rational morphisms
X_{0}(N)\to E
are of the form
[d]\circ f
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. This holds even for CM curves because the extra endomorphisms are not defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. The case
\textup{cond}(E)<N
is more involved. Derickx and Orlić
[
13
, Proposition 1.10]
gave a method to compute all possible degrees of rational morphisms
X_{0}(N)\to E
if
E
has positive rank.

This paper focuses on the case when
g(Y)\geq 2
. A useful result here is the De Franchis-Severi theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(De Franchis-Severi)
.

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
. Then the following two statements hold:

(a)

For a fixed compact Riemann surface
Y
of genus
\geq 2
, the number of non-constant holomorphic maps
X\to Y
is finite.

(b)

There are only finitely many compact Riemann surfaces
Y
of genus
\geq 2
which admit a non-constant holomorphic map from
X
.

This is an opposite situation from the genus
0
or
1
case where there can be infinitely many morphisms to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
or an elliptic curve. Therefore, it makes sense to try to determine possible morphisms from a fixed curve
X
to a curve of genus
\geq 2
, especially if
X
is a modular curve. Baker, González-Jiménez, González and Poonen
[
2
]
consider the maps from
X_{0}(N)
and
X_{1}(N)
to hyperelliptic curves and it is natural to try extending their results by considering the maps to non-hyperelliptic curves.

In this paper we study the modular curve
X_{0}(p)
for
p
prime. The reason for considering only the prime levels is that the arguments become simpler and more computable. For example, all simple factors
A\subseteq J_{0}(p)
occur with multiplicity
1
and are associated with newforms.

We prove the following two results.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p<3000
be a prime and let
Y
be a nice (smooth, projective, and geometrically integral) curve over
\mathbb{Q}
of genus
\geq 2
. Suppose that
f:X_{0}(p)\to Y
is a non-constant morphism of degree
d>1
defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
f
is isomorphic to the quotient map
X_{0}(p)\to X_{0}^{+}(p)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
J_{0}(p)
be the Jacobian of the curve
X_{0}(p)
. If
J_{0}(p)
is isogenous to

A^{+}\oplus A^{-}\oplus A

with
A^{+}\subseteq J_{0}(p)^{+}
,
A^{-}\subseteq J_{0}(p)^{-}
being simple abelian varieties and
A\subseteq J_{0}(p)
such that
\dim A\leq 2
, then, up to isomorphism, the quotient map
X_{0}(p)\to X_{0}(p)^{+}
is the only non-constant rational morphism of degree
d>1
from
X_{0}(p)
to a curve of genus
\geq 2
.

Numerically, it seems that
Theorem

1.4
is applicable for a majority of prime levels
p
. Indeed, by analyzing the data computed in
[
5
]
(also available on LMFDB
[
27
]
) and
[
8
, Section 9]
it follows that it is applicable for 77636 out of the 78498 primes below 1,000,000 (
\approx 98.9\%
). Furthermore, we also computed this percentage for all primes below
B
for several
B<1,000,000
and this seems to suggest that this percentage is slowly climbing to
100\%
.

Motivated by
Theorem

1.3
and
Theorem

1.4
, we state the following conjecture.

Conjecture 1.5
.

Let
p
be a prime. Then, up to isomorphism, the quotient map
X_{0}(p)\to X_{0}(p)^{+}
is the only non-constant rational morphism of degree
d>1
from
X_{0}(p)
to a curve of genus
\geq 2
.

There are two reasons we think why
1.5
is true. First, the number of
A\subseteq J_{0}(p)
not ruled out by
Corollary

3.4
becomes more sparse with larger values of
p
. Second, the computational results of
[
8
]
show that it is very rare for curves
X_{0}(p)
to have factors of dimension
\displaystyle\leq\frac{2g_{0}(p)-2}{3}+1
(factors of larger dimension are much easier to deal with: Riemann-Hurwitz formula implies a degree
2
map from
X_{0}(p)
which necessarily comes from an involution, see
Remark

5.11
). Moreover, if such factors occur, they seem to have a very small dimension and
[
2
, Theorem 1.3]
shows that there is a finite and computable set of counterexamples for every genus
g(Y)
. For genus
g(Y)=2
this list of possible counterexamples to the conjecture is empty.

These results can be used to determine the infiniteness of degree
d
points on
X_{0}(p)
for small
d
, which is an important property of an algebraic curve. Faltings’ theorem tells us that a curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
has infinitely many rational points if and only if
C
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}
(
g=0
) or
C
is an elliptic curve (
g=1
) with positive rank. The next Theorem solves the case
d=2
.

Theorem 1.6

(Harris, Silverman:
[
16
, Corollary 3]
)
.

Let
K
be a number field, and let
C/K
be a curve of genus at least
2
. Assume that
C
is neither hyperelliptic nor bielliptic. Then the set of quadratic points on
C/K
is finite.

Kadets and Vogt
[
23
, Theorem 1.2 and Proposition 6.3]
also gave a characterization of curves with infinitely many degree
d
points for
d\leq 5
, although their characterization relies on Debarre-Fahlaoui curves which can be difficult to understand. The reason for that is that we do not know how to prove in the general case that a curve is not Debarre-Fahlaoui. The only result in that direction we know is that Debarre-Fahlaoui curves must admit a non-constant morphism to a positive rank elliptic curve
[
11
, Proposition 4.6]
. This will also be discussed later in the paper.

The question of determining whether the curve
C/K
has infinitely many degree
d
points over
K
is closely related to determining the
K
-gonality of
C
, as can be seen from the following theorem.

Theorem 1.7

(Frey)
.

Let
C
be a curve defined over a number field
K
. If
C
has infinitely many points of degree
\leq d
over
K
, then
\textup{gon}_{K}C\leq 2d
.

In this paper we determine all curves
X_{0}(p)
with infinitely many points of degree
6
. See
Section

2
for previous results on points for degree
\leq 5
. One of the crucial tools we use is
Theorem

1.3
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
p
be a prime. The modular curve
X_{0}(p)
has infinitely many points of degree
6
over
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
N\leq 151
or

N\in\{163,167,179,181,191,227,239,269\}.

One of the key ways that
Theorem

1.3
helps in the above proof is that the results of
[
23
]
allow one to relate the study of degree
6
points on
X_{0}(p)
to maps on
X_{0}(p)
to other curves whenever the genus of
X_{0}(p)
is
\geq 17
. See
Corollary

5.9
for a more precise statement tailored to degree
6
. In fact, the bound of
3000
in
Theorem

1.3
is enough to classify the points on
X_{0}(p)
that come from maps to curves of genus
\geq 2
for all degrees up to

. This classification is formalized in

.

One might ask whether it is possible to go further and try to determine all curves

3.
Morphisms from
X_{0}(p)
to curves of genus
\geq 2

Definition 3.1
.

Let
B
be a polarized abelian variety with polarization
\phi_{B}:B\to B^{\vee}
and
f:A\hookrightarrow B
an abelian subvariety. Then
\phi_{A}:=f^{\vee}\circ\phi_{B}\circ f:A\to A^{\vee}
is called the induced polarization on
A
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of nice curves over
\mathbb{C}
with
g(Y)\geq 1
. Let
A:=f^{*}(J(Y))\subseteq J(X)
be the image of
J(Y)
in
J(X)
. Let
\phi_{A}:A\to A^{\vee}
be the polarization on
A
induced by the principal polarization on
J(X)
. Then
\deg f
is divisible by the exponent of
\ker\phi_{A}
.

Proof.

We let
d:=\deg f
for simplicity. Since
A=f^{*}(J(Y))
, we can write
f^{*}=f_{2}\circ f_{1}
with
f_{1}:J(Y)\to A
being just
f^{*}
with restricted codomain and
f_{2}:A\hookrightarrow J(X)
being the inclusion morphism. Then
f_{*}=f_{1}^{\vee}\circ f_{2}^{\vee}
and we have the following equality of endomorphisms of
J(Y)
:

[d]=f_{*}\circ f^{*}=f_{1}^{\vee}\circ f_{2}^{\vee}\circ f_{2}\circ f_{1}=f_{1}^{\vee}\circ\phi_{A}\circ f_{1}.

From this equality, and because of the surjectivity of
f_{1}
, we get a surjection
J(Y)[d]\xrightarrow{f_{1}}\ker(f_{1}^{\vee}\circ\phi_{A})
. Together with the inclusion
\ker(\phi_{A})\subseteq\ker(f_{1}^{\vee}\circ\phi_{A})
, this gives the desired divisibility

\exp(\ker\phi_{A})\mid\exp(\ker(f_{1}^{\vee}\circ\phi_{A}))\mid\exp(J(Y)[d])=d.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

If
A\subseteq J_{0}(N)
, then
\ker\phi_{A}
is called the modular kernel
[
35
, Section 10.6]
of
A
and can be computed using SageMath.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be a nice curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g
and let
A\subseteq J(X)
be an abelian subvariety of dimension
g^{\prime}\geq 2
. Assume that
2g-2<\exp(\ker\phi_{A})(2g^{\prime}-2)
. Then there does not exist a morphism
f:X\to Y
of nice curves such that
f^{*}(J(Y))=A
.

Proof.

Suppose that we have such a morphism
f:X\to Y
. Then we must have
g(Y)=g^{\prime}
. This is because
\dim J(Y)=g(Y)
,
f^{*}(J(Y))=A
, and because
f^{*}
has finite kernel (it is contained in the kernel of
[d]
since
[d]=f_{*}\circ f^{*}
). Therefore
A
and
J(Y)
must have the same dimension. We also get
\exp(\ker\phi_{A})\mid\deg f
from
Lemma

3.2
. However, the Riemann-Hurwitz formula gives us

2g-2\geq\deg f\cdot(2g(Y)-2)\geq\exp(\ker\phi_{A})\dot{(}2g^{\prime}-2),

which is a contradiction.
∎

Lemma 3.7

Lemma 3.8

(

[

, Section A.1]

Let

Lemma 3.9

(
[

)

Let

Corollary 3.10
.

Let

be a prime. Then we have

h(-4p)\leq\frac{3\sqrt{p}}{2\pi}(\log p+5-2\log 6).

Proof.

If

If

∎

Remark 3.11

Since

\nu_{\infty}=\sum_{d\mid N}\varphi(d,N/d)
is the number of cusps on
X_{0}(N)
. For
p=211
we can calculate that
g=17
manually using this formula and for
p\geq 223
we get the inequality

g(X_{0}(p))=1+\frac{p+1}{12}-\frac{\nu_{2}}{4}-\frac{\nu_{3}}{3}-1\geq\frac{p+1}{12}-\frac{2}{4}-\frac{2}{3}=\frac{p-13}{12}>17.

We now apply
Corollary

5.5
with
d=6
. Condition
(2)
of that corollary is satisfied since the remaining
X_{0}(p)
have genus
\geq 17
. We claim that condition
(1)
is also satisfied. Indeed, let
\phi:C\to Y
be a non constant morphism of degree
\geq 2
.

If
Y
has genus
0
, then
Y\cong\mathbb{P}^{1}
since
X_{0}(p)
has rational points corresponding to the cusps. This implies that
\min(\delta(X_{0}(p)/\mathbb{Q}))\deg(\phi)\geq\deg(\phi)\geq 7
since all these curves have gonality
\geq 7
by
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
.

If
Y
has genus
1
, then it is an elliptic curve since
X_{0}(p)
has a rational point. Condition
(1)
of
Corollary

5.5
is satisfied since the only elliptic curve of prime conductor between
200
and
696
and modular degree
\leq 6
is the elliptic curve
269.a1
according to the LMFDB, see:

https://www.lmfdb.org/EllipticCurve/Q/?conductor_type=prime&conductor=200-696&sort_order=degree&showcol=degree&sort_order=degree
.

Finally, if
Y
has genus
\geq 2
, then the condition
(1)
of
Corollary

5.5
is also satisfied because of
Theorem

5.6
and the fact that
X_{0}(p)
has only finitely points of degree
\leq 3
in the remaining cases due to
[
4
, Theorems 1 and 3]
.

Therefore, we can apply
Corollary

5.5
to conclude
\min(\delta(X_{0}(p)/\mathbb{Q})>6
. Hence
X_{0}(p)
has only finitely many degree
6
points in the remaining cases.
∎

Remark 5.11
.

We can deal with the case
g(Y)\geq 2
in another way using
Corollary

5.9
. Suppose that we have a degree
2
rational morphism
f:X_{0}(p)\to Y
. Then
f
corresponds to an involution on
X_{0}(p)
. However, if
N\neq 37,63
, then the only non-trivial involutions on curves
X_{0}(N)
of genus
g\geq 2
are Atkin-Lehner involutions by
[
25
, Theorem 0.1]
. Therefore, up to isomorphism, the only degree
2
rational morphism from
X_{0}(p)
to a curve of genus
g\geq 2
is the quotient map
X_{0}(p)\to X_{0}^{+}(p)
.

However, this argument with involutions will not work for morphisms of degree
\geq 3
. For example, If one wants to determine all curves
X_{0}(p)
with infinitely many degree
9
points, then one of the possibilities arising from
Theorem

5.4
would be the degree
3
rational morphism from
X_{0}(p)
to a curve with infinitely many cubic points.

With
Proposition

5.10
in hand, we only need to deal with primes smaller than
200
. Note that in
Section

4
we have already proved that most curves
X_{0}(p)
with
p<200
actually have infinitely many degree
6
points.
So, in order to prove
Theorem

1.8
it remains to show that
X_{0}(p)
has only finitely many points for
p\in\left\{157,173,193,197,199\right\}
.

Let
C
be a curve over
\mathbb{Q}
with at least one rational point, and let
\Sym^{d}(C)
be the
d
-th symmetric power of
C
on
\mathbb{Q}
. We define

\phi_{d}:\Sym^{d}(C)\to\Pic^{d}(C)

as the morphism that sends an effective divisor of degree
d
to its associated line bundle. Furthermore, we use

(5.1)

\displaystyle W_{d}^{0}C:=\textup{Im }\phi_{d}\subset\Pic^{d}(C)

to denote the scheme theoretic image of
\phi_{d}
. Note that
W_{d}^{0}(C)
can also be defined as

W_{d}^{r}(C):=\{D\in\Pic^{d}(C):\ell(D)>r\}.

These two definitions are equivalent, see
[
11
, Sections 2 and 4.2]
for more details.

One way to characterize the infinitude of degree
d
points on
C
is the following theorem.

Theorem 5.12

(
[
7
, Theorem 4.2. (1)]
)
.

Let
C
be a curve over a number field
K
. Then
C
has infinitely many degree
d
points over
K
if and only if at least one of the following two statements holds:

(1)

There exists a map
C\to\mathbb{P}^{1}
of degree
d
over
K
.

(2)

There exists a degree
d
point
x\in C
and a positive rank abelian subvariety
A\subset\Pic^{0}(C)
such that
\phi_{d}(x_{1}+\ldots+x_{d})+A\subset W^{0}_{d}C
where
x_{1},\ldots,x_{d}
are the Galois conjugates of
x
.

Proposition 5.13

(
[
10
, Proposition 3.6]
)
.

Let
C
be a curve over
\mathbb{C}
of genus
g
. Assume that
W_{d}^{r}(C)
contains an abelian variety
A
and that
d\leq g-1+r
. Then
\dim A\leq d/2-r
.

Corollary 5.14
.

The modular curve
X_{0}(p)
has only finitely many degree
6
points over
\mathbb{Q}
for
p\in\left\{157,173,199\right\}

Proof.

In all these cases, we have
g(X_{0}(p))\geq 7
and the
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality of
X_{0}(p)
is at least
7
by
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Theorem

5.12
now tells us that if the curve
X_{0}(p)
has infinitely many degree
6
points, then
W_{6}^{0}(C)
must contain a translate of a positive rank abelian variety
A
. By
Proposition

5.13
, we must have
\dim A\leq 3
.

However, in all these cases, all simple abelian subvarieties
A
of dimension
\leq 3
in the decomposition of the Jacobian
J_{0}(p)
have analytic rank
0
. This can be checked on LMFDB by searching all newforms of level
p
, weight
2
, dimension
1-3
, and character order
1
.

https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/?level=157%2C173%2C199&weight=2&char_order=1&showcol=analytic_rank
.

Therefore, their rank is also
0
by Kolyvagin’s theorem (generalized by Kato
[
24
, Corollary 14.3]
) and we get a contradiction.
∎

The only remaining levels are
p=193,197
. These two curves
X_{0}(p)
are of genera
15
and
16
, respectively. In addition, there exist positive rank abelian varieties
A\subset J_{0}(p)
of dimension
\leq 3
and we cannot use
Corollary

5.14
to eliminate them. We first deal with
p=193
.

Theorem 5.15

(
[
23
, Combination of Theorems 5.8 and 6.2]
)
.

Suppose that
C/\mathbb{Q}
is a nice curve of genus
g
with
\min(\delta(C/\mathbb{Q}))=d
which is also
d
-minimal, i.e., there does not exist a rational morphism
\phi:C\to Y
of degree at least
2
such that
d=\min(\delta(Y/\mathbb{Q}))\cdot\deg\phi
(as in case (1) of
Theorem

5.4
). Then
C
is either the normalization of a Debarre-Fahlaoui curve or

g\leq\frac{(d-1)(d-2)}{2}+2.

We will not give a definition of the Debarre-Fahlaoui curves here. Instead, we present one necessary condition for the curve
C/\mathbb{Q}
to be Debarre-Fahlaoui. The reason for this is that we do not know how to prove that a curve is Debarre-Fahlaoui. The definition can be found in
[
23
, Definition 5.1]

Proposition 5.16

(
[
11
, Proposition 4.6]
)
.

If
C/\mathbb{Q}
is the normalization of a Debarre-Fahlaoui curve of genus
\geq 1
, then
C
admits a non-constant morphism to a positive rank elliptic curve.

Corollary 5.17
.

The modular curve
X_{0}(193)
has only finitely many degree
6
points over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

The curve
X_{0}(193)
has genus
g=15
and
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality equal to
8
by
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
. We also know that it has only finitely many points of degree
\leq 5
by
Theorem

2.3
and
Theorem

2.4
.

Suppose that
\min(\delta(X_{0}(193)/\mathbb{Q}))=6
. There are no non-constant morphisms from
X_{0}(193)
to positive rank elliptic curves since there are no elliptic curves with conductor
193
. Moreover, we know that the curve
X_{0}^{+}(193)
has only finitely many cubic points by
[
4
, Theorem 1]
.

As
Theorem

1.3
tells us that the quotient map
X_{0}(193)\to X_{0}^{+}(193)
is the only non-constant rational morphism from
X_{0}(193)
to a curve of genus
\geq 2
, we conclude that
X_{0}(193)
is
6
-minimal.

It is also not the normalization of a Debarre-Fahlaoui curve by
Proposition

5.16
. Since
g=15>\displaystyle\frac{5\cdot 4}{2}+2=12
, we get a contradiction with
Theorem

5.15
.
∎

Now we deal with
p=197
. The only positive rank simple abelian variety
A\subset J_{0}(197)
is the elliptic curve
E
with LMFDB label
197.a1
. This can be checked on

https://www.lmfdb.org/ModularForm/GL2/Q/holomorphic/?level=197&weight=2&char_order=1
.

This curve
E
has modular degree equal to

. Therefore, we cannot use
Theorem

5.15
to eliminate this curve because we do not know how to prove that
X_{0}(197)
is not a normalization of a Debarre-Fahlaoui curve.

Proposition 5.18
.

The variety
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)_{\mathbb{Q}}
does not contain a translate of a positive rank abelian variety. Consequently, the modular curve
X_{0}(197)
has only finitely many degree
6
points over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

According to the LMFDB,
J_{0}(197)
has only two simple isogeny factors of positive rank. These are the elliptic curve corresponding to the newform
197.2.a.a
and a
5
-dimensional abelian variety corresponding to the newform
197.2.a.b
. A translate of the
5
-dimensional variety cannot be contained in
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)
due to
Proposition

5.13
. Note that the newform only specifies an elliptic curve up to isogeny. However, the elliptic curve with the LMFDB label
197.a1
corresponding to the modular form
197.2.a.a
is the only one in its isogeny class.

Let
E
be the elliptic curve
197.a1
. To show that
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)
does not contain a translate of
E
, we use the same argument as in the proof of
[
11
, Proposition 4.9]
. We explicitly computed the modular parametrization
X_{0}(197)\to E
. Using
Magma
computations over
\mathbb{F}_{3}
we showed that
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}
does not contain a translate of
E_{\mathbb{F}_{3}}
. This was done by enumerating the finitely many points in
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)(\mathbb{F}_{3})
and for each such point
x
we found a point
P\in E(\mathbb{F}_{3})
such that
x+P\notin W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)(\mathbb{F}_{3})
. After that, we use
[
11
, Lemma 4.8]
to conclude that
W_{6}^{0}X_{0}(197)_{\mathbb{Q}}
does not contain a translate of
E
.

Since the
\mathbb{Q}
-gonality of
X_{0}(197)
is equal to
8
by
[
28
, Theorem 1.1]
,
Theorem

5.12
now tells us that
X_{0}(197)
has only finitely many degree
6
points.
∎

Combining the results of

Section

5

.

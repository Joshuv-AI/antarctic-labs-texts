Graded Contact Geometry and the AKSZ Formalism
Ivan Contreras, Nicolas Martinez Alba, Rajan Amit Mehta

Abstract
. The AKSZ formalism is a construction of topological field theories where the target spaces are differential graded symplectic manifolds. In this paper, we describe an analogue of the AKSZ formalism where the target spaces are differential graded contact manifolds. We show that the space of fields inherits a weak contact structure, and we construct a solution to the analogue of the classical master equation, defined via the Jacobi bracket. In the n = 1 n=1 case, we recover the Jacobi sigma model, and in the n = 2 n=2 case, we obtain three-dimensional topological field theories associated to Courant-Jacobi algebroids.

1.
Introduction

Topological Field Theories have been studied extensively over the past decades. In particular, the formulation using supergeometry, known as the AKSZ formalism
[
1
]
has been fundamental in the study of field theories with boundary
[
5
]
, deformation quantization
[
19
]
, the Poisson sigma model
[
7
]
, among others.

The AKSZ method constructs solutions of the BV
master equation directly, without the need of starting from a classical action with a set of symmetries, in comparison to the BV formalism. The classical action
can be recovered by setting the nonzero degree fields to
zero. In the AKSZ formalism, the target space is assumed to be a differential graded symplectic manifold. In low degrees, the construction leads to the Poisson sigma model and the Courant sigma model, a special case of which is Chern-Simons theory.

In this manuscript, we describe an analogue of the AKSZ formalism where the target space is a differential graded contact manifold. We prove results that are analogous to those in ordinary AKSZ theory, namely that the space of fields inherits a graded contact structure, and that there is a naturally-defined function that satisfies an analogue of the classical master equation, defined via Jacobi brackets.

As in ordinary AKSZ theory, the classical action can be recovered by setting the nonzero degree fields to zero. In low degrees, this construction leads to the Jacobi sigma model
[
2
]
and a (to our knowledge, new) three-dimensional theory associated to a Courant-Jacobi algebroid.

We note that the existing literature on the Jacobi sigma model (e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
11
]
) either relies on Poissonization, extracting the theory from the Poisson sigma model, or directly introduces the action, justifying it by observing that it has the desired equations of motion. The AKSZ-contact theory provides a more conceptual and direct explanation of where the Jacobi sigma model comes from.

The structure of the manuscript is as follows. In Section
2
, we provide an overview on contact geometry. In Section
3
, we review graded contact structures. In Section
4
, we describe the AKSZ-contact formalism and prove our main results (Theorem
4.2
and Theorem
4.4
). In Section
5
, we obtain the Jacobi Sigma Model as the
n=1
case of our construction. Finally, in Section
6
, we consider the
n=2
case, leading to the Courant-Jacobi sigma model.

In work in progress
[
9
]
, we use the AKSZ formulation of the Jacobi Sigma Model to provide a path space construction (via phase space reduction) of contact groupoids integrating Jacobi structures, including their shifted version
[
21
]
.

2.1.
Contact structures and contact forms

Let
M
be a finite-dimensional manifold, and let
\mathcal{D}\subseteq TM

1

can be expressed as the kernel of a
1

2

Definition 2.1

The distribution

One can check that the property of being a contact structure is independent of the choice of
1

Proposition 2.2

(
[
15
]
)
.

Let

be a finite-dimensional manifold, and let
\alpha
be a (possibly only locally defined) nonvanishing
1

(1)

\alpha
is a contact form.

(2)

The vector bundle morphism
\flat:TM\to T^{*}M
defined by

\flat(X)=(i_{X}\alpha)\alpha+i_{X}d\alpha

is an isomorphism.

(3)

The map

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}(M)

\displaystyle\to\Gamma(\im((d\alpha)^{\flat}))\oplus C^{\infty}(M)

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto(i_{X}d\alpha,i_{X}\alpha)

is an isomorphism of

-modules.

If a contact form for a contact structure

exists globally, then

is said to be

. Throughout this paper, we will assume coorientability unless stated otherwise. If
\alpha
is a contact form and

is another contact form for the same contact structure. It should be stressed that the isomorphisms in parts (2) and (3) of Proposition

2.2.
Contact vector fields

Let
M
be a manifold with contact structure
\mathcal{D}
. A vector field

is called a

if it preserves the contact structure, i.e. if
[X,Y]\in\Gamma(\mathcal{D})
for all

. If
\alpha
is a contact form, then we can equivalently characterize contact vector fields by the property that

L_{X}\alpha=g\alpha

for some

.

An important example of a contact vector field is the
Reeb vector field

, which is defined by the equation
\flat(R)=\alpha
. Equivalently,

is the unique solution to the equations

\displaystyle i_{R}\alpha

\displaystyle=1,

\displaystyle i_{R}d\alpha

\displaystyle=0.

We emphasize that the Reeb vector field depends on the choice of contact form
\alpha

The following lemma provides a bijection between smooth functions and contact vector fields.

Lemma 2.3

(Lemma 3.49 in
[
22
]
)
.

Let
\alpha
be a contact form with Reeb vector field

. Then
X\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a contact vector field if and only if there exists

such that
1
1

1

This statement differs from the reference by a sign, but we adopt it to agree with the sign in the graded version.

(2.1)

\displaystyle i_{X}\alpha

\displaystyle=f,

\displaystyle i_{X}d\alpha

\displaystyle=R(f)\alpha-df.

Conversely, for every

, the vector field

given by

\flat(X_{f})=(f+R(f))\alpha-df

is a contact vector field that satisfies (

The vector field

is called the

of

2.3.
The Jacobi and Cartan brackets

Let
M
be a manifold with a contact form
\alpha
. The space of contact vector fields is closed under the Lie bracket, so the correspondence of Lemma
2.3
induces a Lie bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{J}
on

, given by

X_{\{f,g\}_{J}}=[X_{f},X_{g}].

This bracket is called the
Jacobi bracket
2
2

2

There are also places in the literature where it has been called the
Lagrange bracket
.
. Using (
2.1
) and (
2.2
), we can obtain the more explicit formula

(2.3)

\{f,g\}_{J}=i_{[X_{f},X_{g}]}\alpha=L_{X_{f}}i_{X_{g}}\alpha-i_{X_{g}}L_{X_{f}}\alpha=X_{f}(g)-R(f)g.

The Jacobi bracket can be viewed as a contact-geometric analogue of the Poisson bracket in symplectic geometry. However, unlike the Poisson bracket, the Jacobi bracket fails to be a derivation, instead satisfying

\{f,gh\}_{J}=\{f,g\}_{J}h+g\{f,g\}_{J}+R(f)gh.

Alternatively, we could consider the
Cartan bracket

\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{C}
, given by

(2.4)

\{f,g\}_{C}=X_{f}(g)=\{f,g\}_{J}+R(f)g,

which is a derivation in the second entry, but does not satisfy skew-symmetry or the Jacobi identity. On the subspace

C^{\infty}_{R}(M)=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M):R(f)=0\},

of functions that are invariant under
R
, the Jacobi and Cartan brackets coincide, giving
C^{\infty}_{R}(M)
the structure of a Poisson algebra.

2.4.
Symplectization

Symplectization
is a process that produces a symplectic manifold from a contact manifold (with a fixed contact form). Given a manifold
M
with contact form
\alpha
, set
\widetilde{M}=M\times\mathbb{R}
, and let

\omega=d(e^{t}\alpha)=e^{t}dt\wedge\alpha+e^{t}d\alpha,

where
t
is the coordinate on
\mathbb{R}
. Then
\widetilde{M}
is a symplectic manifold equipped with a distinguished vector field
Z=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
. The symplectic form is
homogeneous
(with respect to
Z
), in the sense that it satisfies the equation
L_{Z}\omega=\omega
.

On the other hand, given a homogeneous symplectic form
\omega
on
\widetilde{M}
, one can obtain a contact form on
M
via the equation
\alpha=i_{Z}\omega|_{t=0}
. The following statement summarizes this correspondence.

Proposition 2.4
.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between contact forms on
M
and homogeneous symplectic forms on
\widetilde{M}
.

Many structures on a contact manifold can be lifted to its symplectization. Some such lifts and their properties are described in the following statement; see
[
23
]
for more details.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
M
be a manifold with contact form
\alpha
, and let
(\widetilde{M},\omega)
be its symplectization.

(1)

For any
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, the vector field

\widetilde{X_{f}}=X_{f}-R(f)\frac{\partial}{\partial t}

on
\widetilde{M}
is the Hamiltonian vector field associated to the function
\widetilde{f}=e^{t}f
.

(2)

The map
C^{\infty}(M)\to C^{\infty}(\widetilde{M})
,
f\mapsto\widetilde{f}=e^{t}f
, satisfies

\widetilde{\{f,g\}}_{J}=\{\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\}_{\omega},

where
\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega}
is the Poisson bracket associated to
\omega
.

2.5.
Weak contact manifolds

We will sometimes need to consider contact structures on (e.g. Banach or Fréchet) infinite-dimensional manifolds. In these cases, we define a
weak contact structure
to be a corank
1
distribution
\mathcal{D}
such that the map in Definition
3.2
is injective. This definition parallels the notion of weak symplectic structures (see e.g.
[
8
]
).

One needs to be careful when working with weak contact structures, as the Reeb vector field and, more generally, contact vector fields
X_{f}
, may not exist. But when they do exist, they are unique.

3.1.
Graded manifolds

A
graded manifold

\mathcal{M}
is a manifold
M
equipped with a sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
of graded-commutative
\mathbb{Z}
-graded algebras such that, locally,

(3.1)

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}(U)\cong C^{\infty}(U)\otimes S(W),

where
W
is a
\mathbb{Z}
-graded vector space. It is usually assumed that
W
does not have a degree
0
component.

We think of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
as being the sheaf of “smooth functions” on
\mathcal{M}
, and we will use
C(\mathcal{M})
to denote the global sections of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
. The local form (
3.1
) implies that there exist graded local coordinates, where the degree
0
coordinates are coordinates on
M
, the functions are smooth in the degree
0
coordinates and polynomial in the nonzero degree coordinates, and odd degree functions anti–commute with each other. We will use the notation
|f|
to denote the degree of a homogeneous function.

We will often assume that the sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
is nonnegatively graded. In this case,
\mathcal{M}
is said to be an
\mathbb{N}
-manifold
.

The following basic example will often appear in this paper.

Example 3.1
.

(Shifted vector bundles). If
E\to M
is a vector bundle, then the
\mathbb{N}
-manifold
E[1]
is given by
\mathcal{O}_{E[1]}=\wedge\Gamma(E^{*})
. In particular, the algebra of functions on
T[1]M
is the algebra
\Omega(M)
of differential forms on
M
, and the algebra of functions on
T^{*}[1]M
is the algebra
\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)=\wedge\Gamma(TM)
of multivector fields on
M
.

Many differential geometric constructions on manifolds can be defined more generally on graded manifolds. In particular, a (degree
k
) vector field on
\mathcal{M}
is defined as a (degree
k
) graded derivation of
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{M}}
. The space
\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
of vector fields on
\mathcal{M}
, endowed with the graded commutator bracket

[X,Y]=XY-(-1)^{|X||Y|}YX,

is a graded Lie algebra.

Example 3.2

(Euler vector field)
.

On any graded manifold, there is a natural degree
0
vector field called the
Euler vector field

\epsilon
, given by
\epsilon(f)=|f|f
for any homogeneous function
f
.

Definition 3.3
.

A

is a degree
1

such that
[Q,Q]=2Q^{2}=0
. A graded manifold (
\mathbb{N}
-manifold)

equipped with a homological vector field is called a

-manifold
(
\mathbb{N}Q
-manifold)

3

3

The term

.

Example 3.4
.

Let

Differential forms can also be defined on a graded manifold. We refer the reader to

On

\displaystyle[d,d]

\displaystyle=2d^{2}=0,

\displaystyle[L_{X},d]

\displaystyle=L_{X}d-(-1)^{|X|}dL_{X}=0,

\displaystyle[i_{X},d]

\displaystyle=i_{X}d+(-1)^{|X|}di_{X}=L_{X},

\displaystyle[L_{X},L_{Y}]

\displaystyle=L_{X}L_{Y}-(-1)^{|X||Y|}L_{Y}L_{X}=L_{[X,Y]},

\displaystyle[L_{X},i_{Y}]

\displaystyle=L_{X}i_{Y}-(-1)^{|X|(|Y|-1)}i_{Y}L_{X}=i_{[X,Y]},

\displaystyle[i_{X},i_{Y}]

\displaystyle=i_{X}i_{Y}-(-1)^{(|X|-1)(|Y|-1)}i_{Y}i_{X}=0.

3.4.
Symplectization

Here we describe the graded version of symplectization and the corresponding generalizations of the results in Proposition

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a contact
\mathbb{N}
-manifold with degree

The following is the generalization of Proposition

Proposition 3.9

(1)

For any

(2)

The map

Proof.

By direct calculation, we have

\begin{split}\{\widetilde{f},\widetilde{g}\}_{\omega}&=X_{\widetilde{f}}(\widetilde{g})\\
&=e^{t}\left(X_{f}(g)-(-1)^{n(|f|-1)}R(f)g\right)\\
&=e^{t}\{f,g\}_{J}\\
&=\widetilde{\{f,g\}}_{J}.\qed\end{split}

\Hom(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
that corresponds to the identity map in
\Hom(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}),\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}))
under (

) with
\mathcal{Z}=\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
.

4.2.
Lifting of geometric structures

In this section, we summarize different ways that geometric structures can be lifted from
\mathcal{M}
or
\mathcal{N}
to the mapping space
\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
. For more details, we refer to
[
6
,
26
,
28
]
.

The first observation is that the diffeomorphism spaces
\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{N})
and
\Map(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{M})
are graded Lie groups that act on
\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})
by pre- and post-composition. By differentiating, one obtains maps of vector fields

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{X}(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})

\displaystyle\to\mathfrak{X}(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}))

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle\mapsto\hat{X},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle\mapsto\breve{Y},

which are morphisms (or anti-morphisms, depending on sign conventions) of graded Lie algebras. Additionally, because the two actions commute, we have that
[\hat{X},\breve{Y}]=0
for all
X\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{N})
and
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
.

Next, we consider the lifting of differential forms from
\mathcal{M}
to the mapping space. Given any differential form
\beta\in\Omega(\mathcal{M})
, we can use the evaluation map to obtain
\ev^{*}\beta\in\Omega(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{N})
. In order to project the form down to the mapping space, we assume that
\mathcal{N}
is equipped with a Berezinian measure
\mu
. Integration with respect to the measure defines a map
\mu_{*}:\Omega(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})\times\mathcal{N})\to\Omega(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}))
. We then define the
transgression map

\mathbb{T}=\mu_{*}\circ\ev^{*}:\Omega(\mathcal{M})\to\Omega(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M})).

We will be exclusively interested in the case where
\mathcal{N}=T[1]\Sigma
, where
\Sigma
is a compact oriented
k
-dimensional manifold without boundary. In this case,
\mathcal{N}
has a canonical measure
\mu
, given by

\int_{\mathcal{N}}fd\mu=\int_{\Sigma}f

for
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{N})=\Omega(\Sigma)
. By definition, this integral vanishes when
|f|<k
.

An important point is that
\mu_{*}
lowers the degree by
k
but preserves the form grading, whereas
\ev^{*}
preserves both gradings. Thus the transgression map takes
\Omega^{p}_{\ell}(\mathcal{M})
to
\Omega^{p}_{\ell-k}(\Map(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{M}))
.

Another fact that we will use is that the lifting of vector fields from
\mathcal{M}
and the de Rham operator are compatible with the transgression map, in the sense that, for any
Y\in\mathfrak{X}(\mathcal{M})
and
\beta\in\Omega(\mathcal{M})
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(i_{Y}\beta)

\displaystyle=i_{\breve{Y}}(\mathbb{T}(\beta)),

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}(d\beta)

\displaystyle=d(\mathbb{T}(\beta)),

and therefore
\mathbb{T}(L_{Y}\beta)=L_{\breve{Y}}(\mathbb{T}(\beta))
as well.

The interaction of vector fields on
\mathcal{N}
with the transgression map is more subtle and interesting. We will later use the following fact from
[
4
, Lemma 2.6]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

If
X
is a
\mu
-invariant vector field on
\mathcal{N}
, then
L_{\hat{X}}(\mathbb{T}(\beta))=0
for any
\beta\in\Omega(\mathcal{M})
.

4.3.
Transgression of graded contact structures

An important feature of AKSZ theory is that the space of fields inherits a geometric structure from the target space. In the usual AKSZ theory (

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of the analogous result for symplectic structures in the usual AKSZ theory. Using Theorem

and Darboux coordinates

on the symplectic component, we have

To show that

is weak contact, we need to check that

The fact that

Since

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

The following statements hold:

(i)

(ii)

4.5.
Symplectization and AKSZ

Suppose that
\mathcal{M}

Proof.

4.6.
Classical theory

In certain dimensions, classical elements of AKSZ theory can be extracted by restricting degree

In particular, if

Given a map of graded manifolds

\alpha

\phi
to get a degree
n

1

\phi^{*}\alpha

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}i_{Q_{d}}\phi^{*}\alpha,

\hat{S}_{\mathcal{M}}
to

The restriction of

Putting the two parts together, we obtain the

\mathcal{S}(\phi)=\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\phi)+(-1)^{n+1}\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}i_{Q_{d}}\phi^{*}\alpha+(-1)^{n+1}\phi^{*}S_{\mathcal{M}}.

The action (

\mathcal{M}
. Another approach is to construct an action via the symplectization

The AKSZ action (see e.g.

BPV action

5.
The Jacobi sigma model

In this section, we consider the
n=1
case of AKSZ-contact theory and show that it reproduces the Jacobi sigma model of
[
2
]
.

5.1.
Jacobi manifolds

Let
M
be a manifold. A
Jacobi structure
on
M
is a Lie bracket on
C^{\infty}(M)
that is local, in the sense that the support of
\{f,g\}
is contained in the supports of both
f
and
g
. A theorem due to Kirillov
[
18
]
says that local Lie brackets are always first-order in each entry. From this it can be deduced that a Jacobi structure is equivalently given by a pair
(\Lambda,E)
, where
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
is a bivector field and
E\in\mathfrak{X}(M)
is a vector field, such that

(5.1)

\displaystyle[\Lambda,\Lambda]

\displaystyle=2E\wedge\Lambda,

\displaystyle[\Lambda,E]

\displaystyle=0,

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
is the Schouten bracket of multivector fields. The bracket is then given by

\{f,g\}=\Lambda(df,dg)+fE(g)-E(f)g.

The characterization of Jacobi structures in terms of
\Lambda
and
E
is due to Lichnerowicz
[
20
]
.

Examples of Jacobi manifolds include Poisson manifolds (in the case where
E=0
), contact manifolds, cosymplectic manifolds, and locally conformal symplectic manifolds (see, e.g.,
[
13
]
).

5.2.
Degree
1
contact
\mathbb{N}Q
-manifolds

In
[
25
]
, it was shown that Jacobi manifolds are in one-to-one correspondence with degree
1
contact
\mathbb{N}Q
-manifolds, in the following way. By Theorem
3.8
and the classification of degree
1
symplectic
\mathbb{N}
-manifolds in
[
27
]
, we have that every degree
1
contact
\mathbb{N}
-manifold
\mathcal{M}
is canonically of the form

\mathcal{M}\cong T^{*}[1]M\times\mathbb{R}[1]

for some manifold
M
. Thus we can make the identification

C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})\cong\mathfrak{X}^{\bullet}(M)[\theta],

where
\theta
is the degree
1
coordinate on
\mathbb{R}[1]
.

Any degree
2
function
S
on
\mathcal{M}
then has the form

S=\Lambda-E\theta,

where
\Lambda\in\mathfrak{X}^{2}(M)
and
E\in\mathfrak{X}^{1}(M)
. In
[
25
, Proposition 3.3]
it was shown that

\{S,S\}_{J}=[\Lambda,\Lambda]-2E\Lambda+2[E,\Lambda]\theta,

which vanishes if and only if the equations (
5.1
) hold.

5.3.
AKSZ-contact theory in the
n=1
case

We can now give an explicit description of the AKSZ-contact action in the
n=1
case. The input data consists of a compact oriented
2
-manifold
\Sigma
and a Jacobi manifold
M
. The fields are maps of graded manifolds

\phi:T[1]\Sigma\to\mathcal{M}=T^{*}[1]M\times\mathbb{R}[1].

In local coordinates
\{x^{i},p_{i},\theta\}
on
\mathcal{M}
, where
p_{i}
and
\theta
are of degree
1
, a field can be described by smooth functions
X^{i}=\phi^{*}(x^{i})
and
1
-forms
\eta_{i}=\phi^{*}(p_{i})
,
\Theta=\phi^{*}(\theta)
on
M
. Collectively, the functions
X^{i}
form a smooth map
X:\Sigma\to M
, and the
1
-forms
\eta_{i},\Theta
form an element of
\Omega^{1}(\Sigma;X^{*}(T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R}))
.

In coordinates, the contact form on
\mathcal{M}
has the form
\alpha=p_{i}dx^{i}+d\theta
. From this, we get the kinetic part of the action:

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\eta_{i}dX^{i}+d\Theta.

(Note that, for notational simplicity, we are omitting wedge product symbols for differential forms in action functionals.)
The interaction terms come from the Jacobi structure on
M
. If we write
\Lambda=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{ij}p_{i}p_{j}
and
E=E^{i}p_{i}
, then we have

S=\Lambda-E\theta=\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{ij}p_{i}p_{j}-E^{i}p_{i}\theta,

and thus

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{*}S=\int_{\Sigma}\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{ij}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}-E^{i}\eta_{i}\Theta.

Putting the kinetic and interaction terms together, we get the AKSZ-contact action functional

(5.2)

\mathcal{S}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\eta_{i}dX^{i}+d\Theta+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{ij}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}-E^{i}\eta_{i}\Theta.

The additional term in the BPV action (
4.9
) is
-d\Theta
, so

(5.3)

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BPV}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\eta_{i}dX^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\Lambda^{ij}\eta_{i}\eta_{j}-E^{i}\eta_{i}\Theta.

This is the Jacobi sigma model action, as given in
[
2
]
.

Definition 6.1

\mathbb{R}
-bilinear bracket
[\![\cdot,\cdot]\!]:\Gamma(E)\times\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(E)
such that

(1)

[\![e,[\![e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![e,e^{\prime}]\!],e^{\prime\prime}]\!]+[\![e^{\prime},[\![e,e^{\prime\prime}]\!]]\!]
,

(2)

[\![e,fe^{\prime}]\!]=f[\![e,e^{\prime}]\!]+\hat{\rho}(e)(f)e^{\prime}
,

(3)

[\![e,e]\!]=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{D}\langle e,e\rangle
,

(4)

\rho(e)(\langle e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\rangle)=\langle[\![e,e^{\prime}]\!],e^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle e^{\prime},[\![e,e^{\prime\prime}]\!]\rangle
,

for all
e,e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\in\Gamma(E)
and
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
, where
\mathcal{D}:C^{\infty}(M)\to\Gamma(E)
is given by
\langle\mathcal{D}f,e\rangle=\rho(e)(f)
.
Here, sections of
TM\times\mathbb{R}
are viewed as acting on
C^{\infty}(M)
as first-order differential operators, and
\hat{\rho}
is the symbol of
\rho
, i.e. the projection onto
TM
.

Example 6.2
.

The
standard Courant-Jacobi algebroid
on a manifold
M
is

E=(TM\times\mathbb{R})\oplus(T^{*}M\times\mathbb{R})
, where
\rho
is the projection onto
TM\times\mathbb{R}
, and the bilinear form and bracket are given by

\displaystyle\langle(X_{1},f_{1},\xi_{1},g_{1}),(X_{2},f_{2},\xi_{2},g_{2})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle X_{1},\xi_{2}\rangle+\langle X_{2},\xi_{1}\rangle+f_{1}g_{2}+f_{2}g_{1},

\displaystyle[\![(X_{1},f_{1},\xi_{1},g_{1}),(X_{2},f_{2},\xi_{2},g_{2})]\!]

\displaystyle=(X_{3},f_{3},\xi_{3},g_{3}),

where

\displaystyle X_{3}

\displaystyle=[X_{1},X_{2}],

\displaystyle f_{3}

\displaystyle=X_{1}(f_{2})-X_{2}(f_{1}),

\displaystyle\xi_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{X_{1}}\xi_{2}-i_{X_{2}}d\xi_{1}+f_{1}\xi_{2}-f_{2}\xi_{1}+f_{2}dg_{1}+g_{2}df_{1},

\displaystyle g_{3}

\displaystyle=X_{1}(g_{2})-X_{2}(g_{1})+i_{X_{2}}\xi_{1}+f_{1}g_{2}.

These formulas appear in
[
17
, Example 12.2]
. The skew-symmetric version of this bracket appeared in
[
31
]
, where it was shown that several interesting geometric structures, including Jacobi structures, arise as special cases of Dirac structures in
E
.

Example 6.3
.

In the case when
M
is a point, a Courant-Jacobi algebroid consists of a vector space
V
equipped with a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form, a distinguished vector
z\in V
, and a bilinear bracket such that

(1)

[\![e,[\![e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}]\!]]\!]=[\![[\![e,e^{\prime}]\!],e^{\prime\prime}]\!]+[\![e^{\prime},[\![e,e^{\prime\prime}]\!]]\!]
,

(2)

[\![e,e]\!]=\frac{1}{2}\langle e,e\rangle z
,

(3)

\langle z,e\rangle\langle e^{\prime},e^{\prime\prime}\rangle=\langle[\![e,e^{\prime}]\!],e^{\prime\prime}\rangle+\langle e^{\prime},[\![e,e^{\prime\prime}]\!]\rangle
.

If
z=0
, then the above conditions force the bracket to be skew-symmetric (and therefore a Lie bracket) and the bilinear form to be invariant.

6.2.
Degree
2
contact
\mathbb{N}Q
-manifolds

In
[
17
]
, it was shown that Courant-Jacobi manifolds are in one-to-one correspondence with degree
2
contact
\mathbb{N}Q
-manifolds. The argument there was based on symplectization. We outline an alternative approach here, using Theorem
3.8
.

We begin by describing the correspondence in
[
27
]
between degree
2
symplectic
\mathbb{N}
-manifolds and vector bundles
E\to M
equipped with a nondegenerate bilinear form. Let
E\to M
be such a vector bundle. Locally, if
\{x^{i}\}
are coordinates on
M
and
\{v_{\alpha}\}
is a frame of sections on
E
with dual frame
\{v^{\alpha}\}
, then the corresponding degree
2
symplectic
\mathbb{N}
-manifold
\mathcal{N}
has coordinates
\{x^{i},v^{\alpha},p_{i}\}
, where
|v^{\alpha}|=1
and
|p_{i}|=2
.

Writing
g_{\alpha\beta}=\langle v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}\rangle
, and assuming the frame is chosen so that
g_{\alpha\beta}
is constant, the symplectic form on
\mathcal{N}
is given by

\omega=dp_{i}\wedge dx^{i}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}dv^{\alpha}\wedge dv^{\beta}.

Theorem
3.8
tells us that every degree
2
contact
\mathbb{N}
-manifold is of the form
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{N}\times\mathbb{R}[2]
, where
\mathcal{N}
is as above. The contact form on
\mathcal{M}
is

(6.1)

\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left(i_{\epsilon}\omega+d\theta\right)=p_{i}dx^{i}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}v^{\alpha}dv^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}d\theta,

where
\theta
is the degree
2
coordinate on
\mathbb{R}[2]
.

Any degree
3
function
S
on
\mathcal{M}
can be written in the form

(6.2)

S=a^{i}_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}p_{i}+b_{\alpha}v^{\alpha}\theta-\frac{1}{6}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}v^{\alpha}v^{\beta}v^{\gamma},

where
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
,
a^{i}_{\alpha}
, and
b_{\alpha}
are functions on
M
. Such a function encodes bracket and anchor data via the equations

\displaystyle\langle[\![v_{\alpha},v_{\beta}]\!],v_{\gamma}\rangle

\displaystyle=T_{\alpha\beta\gamma},

\displaystyle\rho(v_{\alpha})(f)

\displaystyle=a^{i}_{\alpha}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x^{i}}+b_{\alpha}f,

and the equation
\{S,S\}_{J}=0
holds if and only if the axioms in Definition
6.1
hold.

6.3.
AKSZ-contact theory in the
n=2
case

The input data for
n=2
AKSZ-contact theory consists of a compact oriented
3
-manifold
\Sigma
and a Courant-Jacobi algebroid
E\to M
. The fields are maps of graded manifolds

\phi:T[1]\Sigma\to\mathcal{M}.

Locally, a field is given by smooth functions
X^{i}=\phi^{*}(x^{i})
,
1
-forms
\eta^{\alpha}=\phi^{*}(v^{\alpha})
, and
2
-forms
P_{i}=\phi^{*}(p_{i})
and
\Theta=\phi^{*}(\theta)
.

From (
6.1
), we get the kinetic part of the action

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{kin}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}P_{i}dX^{i}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\alpha}d\eta^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}d\Theta,

and from (
6.2
), we get the interaction terms

\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{int}}(\phi)=\int_{\Sigma}\phi^{*}S=\int_{\Sigma}a^{i}_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}P_{i}+b_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}\Theta-\frac{1}{6}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\eta^{\gamma}.

Putting the two parts together, we get the following formula for
\mathcal{S}(\phi)
:

(6.3)

\int_{\Sigma}P_{i}dX^{i}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\alpha}d\eta^{\beta}+\frac{1}{2}d\Theta-a^{i}_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}P_{i}-b_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}\Theta+\frac{1}{6}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\eta^{\gamma}.

The additional term in the BPV action is
-\frac{1}{2}d\Theta
, so the formula for
\mathcal{S}_{\mathrm{BPV}}(\phi)
is

(6.4)

\int_{\Sigma}P_{i}dX^{i}+\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}\eta^{\alpha}d\eta^{\beta}-a^{i}_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}P_{i}-b_{\alpha}\eta^{\alpha}\Theta+\frac{1}{6}T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\eta^{\alpha}\eta^{\beta}\eta^{\gamma}.

Under the assumption that
\Sigma
is boundary-free, (
6.3
) and (
6.4
) are equal and could be called the
Courant-Jacobi sigma model
action. We expect that it should provide the correct setting for explaining the appearance of Wess-Zumino-Witten terms in, e.g., the twisted Jacobi sigma model of
[
3
]
.

1.
Introduction

Let
a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}
(
k\geq 2
) be a set of positive integers with
\gcd(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k})=1
. The Diophantine Frobenius Problem (see, e.g.
[
7
]
) asks what is the largest integer
g_{a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k}}
which cannot be represented as

a_{1}x_{1}+a_{2}x_{2}+\cdots+a_{k}x_{k}\quad\big(x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\big),

where
\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
is the set of nonnegative integers.
The problem may seem at a glance not so hard, but the solution to it is actually extremely difficult. The closed form for
k=2
was given by Sylvester
[
9
]
who showed that
g_{a,b}=ab-a-b
. For
k=3
, closed forms involving particular cases were widely studied (see, e.g.
[
7
]
). Sylvester also obtained that for any
0\leq s\leq g_{a,b}
exactly one of
s
and
g_{a,b}-s
could be expressed as the form

ax+by~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}).

Thus, exactly half of the integers in the interval
[0,g_{a,b}]
have the desired expression. For comprehensive literature on the Diophantine Frobenius Problem, one can refer to the excellent monograph
[
7
]
of Ramírez Alfonsín.

In a recent article, Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba
[
8
]
considered the Diophantine Frobenius Problem in primes. Specifically, they were interested in the primes
p\leq g_{a,b}
which can be expressed in the form
p=ax+by
for
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Let us write
\pi_{a,b}
for the number of such primes.
For any
\varepsilon>0
, Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba proved that there is some constant
C(\varepsilon)>0
so that

\displaystyle\pi_{a,b}>C(\varepsilon)\frac{g_{a,b}}{(\log g_{a,b})^{2+\varepsilon}}.

(1.1)

This means that
\pi_{a,b}>0
for all sufficiently large
g_{a,b}
. A number of computer experiments then led them to the following conjecture
[
8
, Conjecture 2]
.

Conjecture 1
.

Let
2<a<b
be two relatively prime integers. Then
\pi_{a,b}>0
.

Remark 1
.

In their original statement of
[
8
, Conjecture 2]
, Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba required
2\leq a<b
. For
a=2
and
b=3
, we have
g_{2,3}=2\cdot 3-2-3=1
. So, there would be no primes in the interval
[0,g_{2,3}]
. For
a=2
,
b>3
, it is obvious that
2=2\cdot 1+b\cdot 0
is a prime in the interval
[0,g_{2,b}]
. Therefore, without loss of generality we will only consider the case for
a>2
.

On observing the antisymmetry property of the integers with the form
cx+dy~(x,y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0})
discovered by Sylvester, Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba also posed another conjecture
[
8
, Conjecture 3]
.

Conjecture 2
.

Let
2<a<b
be two relatively prime integers, then

\pi_{a,b}\sim\frac{\pi(g_{a,b})}{2}\quad(\text{as}~a\rightarrow\infty),

where
\pi(t)
is the number of primes up to
t
.

Conjecture
2
was investigated by Ding
[
2
]
, who proved an almost all version of it. In a more recent article, Ding, Zhai and Zhao
[
4
]
succeeded in proving Conjecture
2
. The result of Ding, Zhai and Zhao was later generalized to the
\ell
-Frobenius number by Ding and Komatsu
[
3
]
. Very recently, Huang and Zhu
[
5
]
further extended the result of Ding, Zhai and Zhao to the distribution of prime powers.

In this note, we turn to Conjecture
1
and confirm it here.

Theorem 1
.

Let
2<a<b
be two relatively prime integers. Then we have
\pi_{a,b}>0
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1

For simplicity, we use
g
to denote
g_{a,b}
. We will follow the proof of Ramírez Alfonsín and Skałba
[
8
, Theorem 1]
with explicit calculations.
Since the constant
C(\varepsilon)
in Eq. (
1.1
) is not specific in their argument, we need an effective version of the Siegel–Walfisz theorem as well as an effective version of the prime number theorem.

Let
\pi(x;q,a)
be the number of primes
p
not exceeding
x
so that
p\equiv a\ ({\rm{mod}}\ q)
and

{\rm Li}(x)=\int_{2}^{x}\frac{1}{\log t}\mathrm{dt}.

Lemma 1
.

[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
.
Let
q\geq 3
be an integer and let
m
be an integer that is coprime to
q
. There exist explicit constants
c_{\pi}(q)
and
x_{\pi}(q)
such that

\left|\pi(x;q,m)-\frac{{\rm Li}(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right|<c_{\pi}(q)\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}\quad\text{for~all}~x\geq x_{\pi}(q).

Moreover,
c_{\pi}(q)\leq c_{0}(q)
and
x_{\pi}(q)\leq x_{0}(q)
, where

\displaystyle c_{0}(q)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{840},&\text{if~}3\leq q\leq 10^{4},\\
\frac{1}{160},&\text{if~}q>10^{4},\end{cases}

and

\displaystyle x_{0}(q)=\begin{cases}8\cdot 10^{9},&\text{if~}3\leq q\leq 10^{4},\\
\exp\Big(0.03\sqrt{q}(\log q)^{3}\Big),&\text{if~}q>10^{4}.\end{cases}

(2.1)

Remark 2
.

In the original statement of
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
, the result involving (
2.1
) is

\displaystyle x_{0}(q)=\begin{cases}8\cdot 10^{9},&\text{if~}3\leq q\leq 10^{5},\\
\exp\Big(0.03\sqrt{q}(\log q)^{3}\Big),&\text{if~}q>10^{5}.\end{cases}

(2.2)

Actually, (
2.1
) follows from (
2.2
) by noting that

\exp\Big(0.03\sqrt{q}(\log q)^{3}\Big)>8\cdot 10^{9}

for any
10^{4}\leq q\leq 10^{5}
.

Lemma 2
.

[
6
, Theorem 1]
.
For
x\geq 59
we have

\frac{x}{\log x-1+(\log x)^{-0.5}}<\pi(x)<\frac{x}{\log x-1-(\log x)^{-0.5}}.

Lemma 3
.

[
1
, Corollary 1.7]
.
Let
m
and
q
be integers with
1\leq q\leq 10^{5}
and
\gcd(m,q)=1
. If
x\geq 10^{6}
, then

\left|\pi(x;q,m)-\frac{{\rm Li}(x)}{\varphi(q)}\right|<0.027\frac{x}{(\log x)^{2}}.

Lemma 4
.

[
1
, Theorem 1.9]
.
Let
q
and
m
be integers with
100\leq q\leq 10^{4}
and
\gcd(m,q)=1
. Then for
x\leq 10^{11}
we have

\max_{1\leq y\leq x}\left|\pi(y;q,m)-\frac{{\rm Li}(y)}{\varphi(q)}\right|<2.734\frac{\sqrt{x}}{\log x}.

Before the proof of our theorem, we first note that

(a-1)a\leq(a-1)(b-1)=g+1\quad\text{and}\quad b^{2}>(a-1)(b-1)>g

which clearly means that
b>\sqrt{g}
and further
a<\sqrt{g}+1
.

We now proceed to the proof of Theorem

Case I.

\displaystyle g>b\geq\exp\left(\frac{a}{4.2}\right)>\exp\Big(0.03\sqrt{a}(\log a)^{3}\Big).

(2.3)

We are going to count the primes
p\leq g
with
p\equiv b\ ({\rm{mod}}\ a)
.
We note that if a prime

\displaystyle\pi_{a,b}\geq\pi(g;a,b)-\pi(b;a,b)\geq\frac{{\rm Li}(g)}{\varphi(a)}-\frac{{\rm Li}(b)}{\varphi(a)}+2R(g),

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle\pi_{a,b}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{{\rm Li}(g)}{\varphi(a)}-\frac{{\rm Li}(b)}{\varphi(a)}-\frac{g}{80(\log g)^{2}}

Case II.

\displaystyle\sum_{y=0}^{\left\lfloor c/b\right\rfloor}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{c-by}{a}\right\rfloor+1\right).

(2.5)

Now, by the result of Sylvester (see e.g.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle\pi_{a,b}\geq\pi(g)-\pi(g-c)-\sum_{y=0}^{\left\lfloor c/b\right\rfloor}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{c-by}{a}\right\rfloor+1\right),

in
(g-c,g]

\displaystyle\sum_{y=0}^{\left\lfloor c/b\right\rfloor}\left(\left\lfloor\frac{c-by}{a}\right\rfloor+1\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\lfloor\frac{c}{a}\right\rfloor+1+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{c-b}{a}+1+\frac{b}{a}+1\right)\left\lfloor\frac{c}{b}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c}{a}+1+\left(\frac{c}{2a}+1\right)\frac{c}{b}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}}{2ab}+\frac{c}{a}+\frac{c}{b}+1.

(2.7)

Output Regulation for Impedance Passive Systems with Input Saturation Thanks: This research was supported by the Research Council of Finland grant 349002 and the German Research Foundation (DFG) grant 362536361.
Thavamani Govindaraj, Lassi Paunonen

Abstract
. We consider output tracking and disturbance rejection for abstract infinite-dimensional systems with input saturation. We solve the control problem using a control law which consists of a stabilising error feedback term and a feedforward term based on the reference and disturbance signals. We use our results to design control laws for output tracking and disturbance rejection for a two-dimensional heat equation and a one-dimensional wave equation with boundary control and observation.

1.
Introduction

In this article we solve the problem of output tracking and disturbance rejection for a well-posed linear system
\Sigma

[
26
]
affected by a saturation-type nonlinearity
\phi
at the input (
Figure

1
).
More precisely, the main goal in the
output regulation problem
is to design a control input
u(t)
in such a way that the
tracking error

e(t)=y(t)-y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t)
between the output
y(t)
of the system and a given reference signal
y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t)
converges to zero as
t\to\infty
in a suitable sense despite the external disturbance signal
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}(t)
.
Our control law is based on the knowledge of the signals
y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}
and
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}
and additional feedback from the tracking error
e
.

The solution of the output regulation problem
for abstract linear systems is already understood very well
[
3
,
21
]
.
It is also well-known that in the case of finite-dimensional systems, very similar type of control design can be used to solve the control problem in the presence of input saturation
[
24
, Sec. 3.1.1]
.
In this article we generalise the results in
[
24
]
to solve the saturated output regulation problem for infinite-dimensional well-posed linear systems under suitable passivity assumptions of the well-posed system
\Sigma
.

The reference and disturbance signals are assumed to be of the form

(1.1a)

\displaystyle y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t)

\displaystyle=a_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(a_{k}\cos(\omega_{k}t)+b_{k}\sin(\omega_{k}t)\right)

(1.1b)

\displaystyle w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}(t)

\displaystyle=c_{0}+\sum_{k=1}^{q}\left(c_{k}\cos(\omega_{k}t)+d_{k}\sin(\omega_{k}t)\right)

for some known frequencies
(\omega_{k})_{k=1}^{q}\subset(0,\infty)
and
\omega_{0}=0

and known coefficients
(a_{k})_{k},(b_{k})_{k}\subset U
,
(c_{k})_{k},(d_{k})_{k}\subset U_{d}
.
The control law solving the output regulation problem has the form

(1.2)

\displaystyle u(t)=-\kappa(y(t)-y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t))+u_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)

with

(1.3)

\displaystyle u_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{q}\Bigl[(f_{k}+g_{k})\cos(\omega_{k}t)+i(f_{k}-g_{k})\sin(\omega_{k}t)\Bigr].

The parameters
(f_{k})_{k}\subset U
and
(g_{k})_{k}\subset U
are computed based on the coefficients

(a_{k})_{k}
,
(b_{k})_{k}
,
(c_{k})_{k}
,
(d_{k})_{k}
and the values of the transfer function of
\Sigma
evaluated at the complex frequencies
\{0\}\cup\{\pm i\omega_{k}\}_{k=1}^{q}

using explicit formulas (
3.4
).
Even though the assumption that
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}
is fully known seems restrictive, we show in
Section

3.2
that the parameters of the control input
can in fact be measured from the output of a stabilised version of the system.
In addition, especially in the linear setting the type of control design studied in the paper is also a building block in certain internal model based error feedback controllers
[
14
,
11
]
.

In our main result in
Theorem

3.3
we show that the control law (
1.2
) solves the output regulation problem under the following assumptions.

(a)

The system
\Sigma
considered with input
u
,
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}=0
, and with output
y
is
impedance passive

[
27
, Def. 4.1]
and

\kappa\geq 0
is such that the output feedback

achieves strong stability of the linear closed-loop system.

(b)

The complex frequencies
\{0\}\cup\{\pm i\omega_{k}\}_{k}
do not overlap with the transmission zeros of
\Sigma
.

(c)

The coefficients
(a_{k})_{k},(b_{k})_{k},(c_{k})_{k},(d_{k})_{k}
of

and
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}
are such that
u_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)
is within the linear regime of the saturation function

for all
t\geq 0
.

In
[
24
, Sec. 3.1]
the
output regulation problem was solved in the case of finite-dimensional systems with input saturation using
low-and-high-gain
state feedback control. Our assumption of passivity in (a) allows us to simplify the control design by replacing the state feedback with error feedback, and by removing restrictions on the gain parameter
\kappa\geq 0
.
The condition in (b) is a standard assumption also in linear output regulation
[
3
, Sec. V]
.
Condition (c) describes the class of reference and disturbance signals which can be regulated under the saturation constraint, and this condition was shown to be nearly sharp in
[
24
, Sec. 3.4]
.

In the final part of the article we use our general results in
output tracking and disturbance rejection for concrete partial differential equation (PDE) systems with boundary control and boundary measurement. The models we consider are a heat equation on a two-dimensional domain and a one-dimensional wave equation with spatially varying physical parameters.

Output regulation of infinite-dimensional systems and controlled PDEs have been studied
extensively in the literature, see, e.g.,
[

,
23
,
21
,
12
,

,

Infinite-dimensional system with saturation and other input nonlinearities

,

There are far fewer references on output tracking time-varying signals in infinite-dimensional and PDE systems. The only reference
[

Our results differ from

Because of this, our results are novel in the setting of regulation of time-varying signals (

Moreover, in the case of

3.
Saturated Output Regulation

3.1.
Control Design for Saturated Output Regulation

We consider a control system of the form

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}\dot{x}(t)\\
y(t)\end{bmatrix}=S\begin{bmatrix}x(t)\\
\phi(u(t))\\
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}(t)\end{bmatrix},\qquad x(0)=x_{0},

where
S=\begin{bmatrix}A\&B\\
C\&D\end{bmatrix}

is a well-posed system node on the Hilbert spaces
(U\times U_{d},X,U)
.
We assume that
U=U_{1}\times U_{2}\times\cdots\times U_{p}
where
U_{k}
are Hilbert spaces for
k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. We
define the nonlinear function
\phi:U\to U
so that
\phi(u)=(\phi_{1}(u_{1}),\ldots,\phi_{p}(u_{p}))^{\top}
for
u=(u_{1},\ldots,u_{p})^{\top}\in U
, where

\phi_{k}:U_{k}\to U_{k}
are defined using the parameters

(r_{1},\ldots,r_{p})^{\top}\subset U_{1}\times\cdots\times U_{p}
and
\{\delta_{k}\}_{k=1}^{p}\subset(0,\infty)

by

(3.2)

\displaystyle\phi_{k}(u)=r_{k}+\frac{\delta_{k}(u-r_{k})}{\max\{\delta_{k},\lVert u-r_{k}\rVert\}}=\begin{cases}u&\mbox{if }\lVert u-r_{k}\rVert\leq\delta_{k}\\
r_{k}+\frac{\delta_{k}(u-r_{k})}{\lVert u-r_{k}\rVert}&\mbox{if }\lVert u-r_{k}\rVert>\delta_{k}\end{cases}

for
u\in U_{k}
and
k\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
.
We note that the case where
p=1
,
U=U_{1}={\mathbb{C}}
and
r_{1}=0
corresponds to the symmetric scalar saturation function
\phi
which is linear when
\lvert u\rvert\leq\delta_{1}
. On the other hand,
if we define
\phi_{k}
such that
U_{k}={\mathbb{C}}
,
\delta_{k}=(b-a)/2
, and
r_{k}=(a+b)/2
, then in the case of real-valued signals the function
\phi_{k}
is exactly the saturation function on the interval
[a,b]
.
We may now formulate our precise control problem.

Saturated Output Regulation Problem.

Choose a control law
u(t)
in such a way that the following hold.

•

The closed-loop system has a well-defined generalised solution for all initial states
x_{0}\in X
and for all
y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}
and
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}
of the form (
1.1
).

•

With
y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t)\equiv 0
and
w_{\mbox{\scriptsize{d}}}(t)\equiv 0
the origin is a globally asymptotically stable equilibrium point of (
3.1
),
i.e., the generalised solutions corresponding to all initial states
x_{0}\in X
satisfy
\lVert x(t)\rVert\to 0
as
t\to\infty
.

•

There exists a non-trivial set
\Lambda\subset U^{q+1}\times U^{q}\times U_{d}^{q+1}\times U_{d}^{q}
such that for all
x_{0}\in X
and for all
(a_{k},b_{k},c_{k},d_{k})_{k}\subset\Lambda
in (
1.1
)
the closed-loop state
x
is uniformly bounded and the tracking error
e=y-y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}
converges to zero in a suitable sense as
t\to\infty
.

We note that the “suitable sense” of convergence
of
e=y-y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}

depends on the properties of the system and the type of solutions considered.
Even in the absence of saturation, the error corresponding to a generalised closed-loop solution may fail to converge pointwise to zero, but may instead satisfy a weaker property, e.g.,
e\in L^{2}(0,\infty;U)

[
21
]
.
We present a solution of the Saturated Output Regulation Problem under the following assumptions.

Assumption 3.1
.

Let
X
,
U
, and
U_{d}
be Hilbert spaces and
suppose that the following hold.

(a)

S=\begin{bmatrix}A\&B\\
C\&D\end{bmatrix}
is a well-posed system node on
(U\times U_{d},X,U)
and

\displaystyle\re\left\langle A\&B\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\\
0\end{bmatrix},x\right\rangle_{X}\leq\re\left\langle C\&D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
u\\
0\end{bmatrix},u\right\rangle_{U}

for all
x\in X
and
u\in U
such that
(x,u,0)^{\top}\in D(S)
.
Moreover, the transfer function
P=[P_{c},P_{d}]:\rho(A)\to{\mathcal{L}}(U\times U_{d},U)
of

satisfies

\re P_{c}(\lambda)\geq c_{\lambda}I
for some
\lambda,c_{\lambda}>0
.

(b)

Either
\kappa=0
and the semigroup generated by

is strongly stable,

(b’)

or, alternatively,
\kappa>0
,

\lVert r_{k}\rVert<\delta_{k}
for all

, and
the semigroup generated by

A^{\kappa}:D(A^{\kappa})\subset X\to X
defined by

\displaystyle D(A^{\kappa})

\displaystyle=\bigl\{\,x\in X\bigm|\exists v\in U:\ (x,-\kappa v,0)^{\top}\in D(S),\ v=C\&D\begin{bmatrix}x\\
-\kappa v\\

and

We note that the condition in

\Sigma
considered with input

Remark 3.2

If the semigroup generated by

This can be shown using the properties in

To define the control law, we first note that

Our main result below shows that
the Saturated Output Regulation Problem is solved by the control law

u(t)=-\kappa(y(t)-y_{\mbox{\scriptsize{ref}}}(t))+u_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)
, where

(3.3)

\displaystyle u_{\mathrm{reg}}(t)

\displaystyle=f_{0}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{k=1}^{q}\Bigl[(f_{k}+g_{k})\cos(\omega_{k}t)+i(f_{k}-g_{k})\sin(\omega_{k}t)\Bigr].

Here
f_{k}
and
g_{k}
are defined based on the transfer function
P^{\kappa}=[P_{c}^{\kappa},P_{d}^{\kappa}]
and the coefficients

(a_{k})_{k},(b_{k})_{k}\subset U
and
(c_{k})_{k},(d_{k})_{k}\subset U_{d}
of the reference disturbance signals as

(3.4a)

\displaystyle f_{0}

\displaystyle=P_{c}^{\kappa}(0)^{-1}((I-\kappa P_{c}^{\kappa}(0))a_{0}-P_{d}^{\kappa}(0)c_{0})

(3.4b)

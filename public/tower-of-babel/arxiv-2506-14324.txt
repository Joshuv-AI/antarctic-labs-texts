Rota–Baxter operators on compact simple Lie groups and algebras
Saveliy V. Skresanov

Abstract
A Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie group G G is a smooth map B : G → G B:G\to G such that B ⁡ ( g ) ​ B ​ ( h ) = B ⁡ ( g ​ B ​ ( g ) ​ h ​ B ​ ( g ) − 1 ) B(g)B(h)=B(gB(g)hB(g)^{-1}) for all g , h ∈ G g,h\in G . This concept was introduced in 2021 by Guo, Lang and Sheng as a Lie group analogue of Rota–Baxter operators of weight 1 on Lie algebras. We show that the only Rota–Baxter operators on compact simple Lie groups are the trivial map and the inverse map. A similar description for Rota–Baxter operators of weight 1 on compact simple Lie algebras is provided.



1
Introduction

A
Lie group
is a group which is also a finite-dimensional real smooth manifold,
such that group multiplication and inversion are smooth maps. We consider only real Lie groups and algebras in this paper.

In 2021 Guo, Lang and Sheng
[
6
]
introduced the concept of a Rota–Baxter operator on a Lie group,
being motivated by some connections to the factorization theorem of Semenov–Tian–Shansky
[
13
]
.
Namely, given a Lie group
G
, a smooth map
B:G\to G
is a
Rota–Baxter operator
on
G
, if
for all
g,h\in G
we have

B(g)B(h)=B(gB(g)hB(g)^{-1}).

Such operators are closely related to the Rota–Baxter operators on Lie algebras, which find applications
in the study of the Yang–Baxter equation, see
[
13
]
and
[
6
, §1.2]
for more details,
and
[
2
,
3
]
for some properties of Rota–Baxter operators on abstract groups.

To make the connection between Rota–Baxter operators on Lie groups and algebras more precise,
let
\g
be a Lie algebra and let
\B:\g\to\g
be a linear operator.
We say that
\B
is a
Rota–Baxter operator of weight 1
on
\g
, if for all
u,v\in\g
we have

[\B(u),\B(v)]=\B([\B(u),v]+[u,\B(v)]+[u,v]),

where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the Lie bracket on
\g
.
If
\g
is the Lie algebra associated with a Lie group
G
and
B:G\to G
is a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
,
then the tangent map at the identity
T_{1}B:\g\to\g
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight 1 on the Lie algebra
\g
, see
[
6
, Theorem 2.9]
.

It is an interesting question which Rota–Baxter operators on the Lie algebra
\g
can be integrated
to the Lie group
G
, i.e. whether for a given Rota–Baxter operator
\B:\g\to\g
there exists a Rota–Baxter operator
B:G\to G

with
\B=T_{1}B
. It was shown in
[
7
, Theorem 5.3]
that this can always be done locally (in some neighborhood of the identity in
G
),
and by
[
8
, Corollary 2.19]
, it can be done globally if
G
is connected, simply connected and compact.
Moreover,
[
8
, Example 4.3]
gives an example of a Lie algebra
\g
and an associated noncompact Lie group
G
,
such that some Rota–Baxter operator on
\g
cannot be integrated to
G
.

The main result of this paper gives the full description of Rota–Baxter operators on compact simple Lie groups.
Recall that a Lie group is
simple
, if it is connected, nonabelian and has no closed connected normal subgroups besides the whole group or the identity.
It can be easily checked that the trivial map
B(g)=1
,
g\in G
, which we denote by
1_{G}
,
and the inverse map
B(g)=g^{-1}
,
g\in G
, which we denote by
(\cdot)^{-1}
, are Rota–Baxter operators on any Lie group
G
.
It turns out that these are the only Rota–Baxter operators on compact simple Lie groups.

Theorem 1
.

The only Rota–Baxter operators on a compact simple Lie group
G
are
1_{G}
and
(\cdot)^{-1}
.

One cannot drop the condition on compactness from Theorem
1
, since noncompact simple Lie groups have Rota–Baxter operators not of the two types above,
see the construction in
[
6
, Example 2.8]
using the Iwasawa decomposition.
Similarly, we cannot relax simplicity to semisimplicity, as shown by the example
G=H\times H
for any compact simple Lie group
H

and
B:G\to G
defined by
B(x,y)=x^{-1}
, see
[
6
, Lemma 2.6]
.

A Lie algebra is called
compact
if it is a Lie algebra associated to a compact Lie group.
Recall that a simple Lie algebra is compact if and only if its Killing form is negative definite
[
9
, Theorem 1.45 and Proposition 4.27]
.
For any Lie algebra
\g
, the zero map
0:\g\to\g
, defined by
0(u)=0
for
u\in\g
, and
the minus identity map
-\mathrm{id}:\g\to\g
, defined by
(-\mathrm{id})(u)=-u
for
u\in\g
,
are Rota–Baxter operators. It follows from Theorem
1
and
[
8
]
that these are the only Rota–Baxter operators
on compact simple Lie algebras.

Theorem 2
.

The only Rota–Baxter operators on a compact simple Lie algebra are
0
and
-\mathrm{id}
.

Proof.

Let
\g
be a compact simple Lie algebra with a Rota–Baxter operator
\B:\g\to\g
.
By Lie’s third theorem
[
5
, Theorem 1.14.3]
, there exists a connected simply connected Lie group
G
with the Lie algebra
\g
.
By Weyl’s theorem
[
9
, Theorem 4.69]
, it is compact, and hence by
[
8
, Corollary 2.19]
,
there exists a Rota–Baxter operator
B:G\to G
such that
\B=T_{1}B
.
By Theorem
1
, we have
B=1_{G}
or
B=(\cdot)^{-1}
, and the claim follows by computing the tangent maps.
∎

In
[
8
, Example 4.1]
it is shown that any Rota–Baxter operator on the Lie algebra
\g=\mathfrak{so}(3)

can be integrated to a Rota–Baxter operator on the compact simple Lie group
G=\mathrm{SU}(2)
. In view of Theorem
2
,
this is not surprising, since the only Rota–Baxter operators on
\g
are
0
and
-\mathrm{id}

and they correspond to operators
1_{G}
and
(\cdot)^{-1}
on
G
.

In Section
2
we give the proof of Theorem
1
, which is essentially topological in nature and relies
on deep results of Scheerer, Baum and Browder
[
12
,
1
]
on homotopy of simple Lie groups.
In Section
3
we give a second proof of Theorem
2
which does not rely on Theorem
1

or
[
8
]
, and instead uses the classification of decompositions of compact simple Lie algebras into a sum of subalgebras by Onishchik
[
11
]
.
Although this second proof looks purely algebraic on the surface, the results of Onishchik also rely on topological properties of Lie groups.

2
Proof of Theorem
1

We start with preliminary results on Lie groups and Rota–Baxter operators.
Given two Lie groups
G
and
H
, we say that
f:G\to H
is a
Lie group homomorphism
if
it is a smooth homomorphism between groups
G
and
H
.

Lemma 1

(
[
10
, Theorem 3.20]
)
.

Let
G
and
H
be Lie groups with Lie algebras
\g
and
\mathfrak{h}
, respectively.
Assume
G
to be connected.
If
f,k:G\to H
are Lie group homomorphisms and
T_{1}f=T_{1}k
, then
f=k
.

For a Lie group
G
with the associated Lie algebra
\g
we will write
(G,\cdot)

and
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot])
to indicate the group multiplication and the Lie bracket, when these are not clear from the context.

Lemma 2

(
[
6
, Proposition 2.13]
)
.

Let
(G,\cdot)
be a Lie group with the Lie algebra
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot])
,
and let
B:G\to G
be a Rota-Baxter operator on
G
with the tangent map
\B=T_{1}B:\g\to\g
.
Define
*:G\times G\to G
by
g*h=gB(g)hB(g)^{-1}
for all
g,h\in G
.
Then
(G,*)
is a Lie group with the Lie algebra
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\B})
, where

[u,v]_{\B}=[\B(u),v]+[u,\B(v)]+[u,v]\text{ for all }u,v\in\g.

The map
B
is a Lie group homomorphism from
(G,*)
to
(G,\cdot)
,
while
\B
is a Lie algebra homomorphism from
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\B})
to
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Given a Lie group
G
with the associated Lie algebra
\g
, recall that the trivial
1_{G}:G\to G

and inverse
(\cdot)^{-1}:G\to G
maps are Rota–Baxter operators on
G
with tangent maps equal to
0:\g\to\g
and
-\mathrm{id}:\g\to\g
,
respectively, see
[
6
, Example 2.3]
. Turns out the converse is true for compact simple Lie groups.

Lemma 3
.

Let
G
be a compact simple Lie group with the associated Lie algebra
\g
.
Let
B:G\to G
be a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
, and let
\B=T_{1}B:\g\to\g
be the tangent map.
If
\B=0
, then
B=1_{G}
, and if
\B=-\mathrm{id}
, then
B=(\cdot)^{-1}
.

Proof.

Let
B
be a Rota–Baxter operator with
\B=0
. By Lemma
2
, there exists another multiplication

*:G\times G\to G
such that the associated Lie algebra of the Lie group
(G,*)

is
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\B})
, where

[u,v]_{\B}=[\B(u),v]+[u,\B(v)]+[u,v]\text{ for all }u,v\in\g.

Since
\B(u)=\B(v)=0
in our case, we yield
[u,v]_{\B}=[u,v]
for all
u,v\in\g
.
Therefore Lie algebras
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot])
and
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\B})
are isomorphic.

Lie groups
(G,\cdot)
and
(G,*)
have the same underlying manifolds, in particular, they have the same homotopy type
and are both connected and compact.
Since
(G,\cdot)
is a simple Lie group, its Lie algebra
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot])
is simple
[
5
, Theorem 1.11.7]
.
Hence the algebra
(\g,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\B})
is also simple, and by
[
5
, Theorem 1.11.7]
the group
(G,*)
is simple.
By
[
1
, Theorem 9.3]
, two compact simple Lie groups of the same homotopy type are isomorphic (as Lie groups),
hence there exists a smooth group isomorphism
f:(G,\cdot)\to(G,*)
.

By Proposition
2
, the map
B
is a Lie group homomorphism from
(G,*)
to
(G,\cdot)
.
Hence the composition
B\circ f:(G,\cdot)\to(G,\cdot)
is a Lie group homomorphism from
(G,\cdot)
into itself.
Consider its tangent at the identity:

T_{1}(B\circ f)=T_{f(1)}B\circ T_{1}f=T_{1}B\circ T_{1}f=0,

where
f(1)=1
since
f
is a homomorphism, and
T_{1}B=0
by our assumption.
The trivial map
1_{G}:(G,\cdot)\to(G,\cdot)
also has a zero tangent, hence by Lemma
1
,
we have
B\circ f=1_{G}
. Since
f
is an isomorphism, we derive
B=1_{G}
, as claimed.

Now, if
B
is a Rota-Baxter operator with
\B=-\mathrm{id}
, then by
[
6
, Proposition 2.4]
,
a smooth map
\tilde{B}(g)=g^{-1}B(g^{-1})
,
g\in G
, is also a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
.
Let
\exp:\g\to G
denote the exponential map. For
u\in\g
we have

(T_{1}\tilde{B})(u)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\tilde{B}(\exp(tu))=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\exp(-tu)B(\exp(-tu))=\\
=-u+\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}B(\exp(-tu))=-u+(T_{1}B)(-u)=-u+u=0.

Therefore
T_{1}\tilde{B}=0
, so by the preceding paragraphs,
\tilde{B}(g)=1
for all
g\in G
.
This immediately implies
B(g)=g^{-1}
,
g\in G
, as claimed.
∎

We note that an analogue of Lemma
3
can be proved for connected simply connected Lie groups without assuming compactness or simplicity,
if one uses Lie’s second theorem
[
10
, Theorem 3.14]
to obtain the isomorphism
f
in the proof.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Let
B:G\to G
be a Rota–Baxter operator on
G
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
G
.
By
[
6
, Theorem 2.9]
, the tangent map
\mathfrak{B}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
to
B
at the identity
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight 1 on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
. By Proposition
2
, there is
a new smooth multiplication
*:G\times G\to G
such that
G_{B}=(G,*)
is a Lie group,
and a new Lie bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{B}}:\mathfrak{g}\times\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}

such that
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{B}}=(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{B}})
is a Lie algebra corresponding to the Lie group
G_{B}
.
Moreover,
\mathfrak{B}:\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{B}}\to\mathfrak{g}
is a homomorphism of Lie algebras.

If
\mathfrak{B}
is the zero map, then
B(g)=1
for all
g\in G
by Lemma
3
, and we are done.
If
\mathfrak{B}
is an invertible linear map, then by
[
4
, Corollary 2.22]
,
\mathfrak{B}=-\mathrm{id}
.
Therefore
B(g)=g^{-1}
for all
g\in G
by Lemma
3
, and we are done again.
Hence we may assume that
\mathfrak{B}
has a nontrivial kernel, in particular,
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{B}}
is not a simple Lie algebra.

Note that
G_{B}
is a compact connected Lie group which is homotopy equivalent to
G
,
since it has the same underlying manifold as
G
. By
[
12
]
,
G_{B}
and
G
are locally isomorphic,
in particular, their Lie algebras are isomorphic. The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}_{\mathfrak{B}}
is not simple,
while
\g
is simple, a contradiction. We described all possibilities for
B
and the proof is complete.
∎

3
Another proof of Theorem
2

We give a proof of Theorem
2
which does not involve Rota–Baxter operators on Lie groups.
We start with the result of Onishchik on decompositions of compact simple Lie algebras.

Lemma 4

(
[
11
, Theorem 4.1 and Table 7]
)
.

Let
\g
be a compact simple Lie algebra, and let
\g_{1},\g_{2}
be proper nonzero subalgebras of
\g
such that
\g=\g_{1}+\g_{2}
.
Then
\g_{1}\cap\g_{2}
is nonzero and at least one of the summands
\g_{i}
,
i=1,2
,
is a compact simple Lie algebra of rank smaller than the rank of
\g
.

The lemma implies that there are no decompositions into a sum of proper subalgebras for rank 1 compact simple Lie algebras.

Another proof of Theorem
2
.

We prove the claim by induction on the rank of
\g
.
Let
\B:\g\to\g
be a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
1
on
\g
. Suppose that
\B\neq 0,-\mathrm{id}
; we want to derive a contradiction.
By
[
4
, Corollary 2.22]
, the operator
\B
is not invertible, hence its image
\g_{1}=\B(\g)
is a proper nonzero subalgebra of
\g
.
Recall that
\B^{\prime}:\g\to\g
defined by
\B^{\prime}(u)=-u-\B(u)
is also a Rota–Baxter operator on
\g
, see
[
4
, Lemma 2.2]
, and
we may similarly assume that the image
\g_{2}=\B^{\prime}(\g)
is a proper nonzero subalgebra of
\g
.

Since
-u=\B(u)+\B^{\prime}(u)
, we have
\g=\g_{1}+\g_{2}
. By Lemma
4
, the intersection
\g_{1}\cap\g_{2}
is nonzero
and, without loss of generality, we may assume that
\g_{1}
is a compact simple Lie algebra of rank smaller than the rank of
\g
.

If
\g
has rank 1, then there are no decompositions of
\g
into a sum of two proper subalgebras, a contradiction.
We may assume that
\g
has rank at least
2
.
Clearly the restriction
\B|_{\g_{1}}
of
\B
on
\g_{1}
is a Rota–Baxter operator of weight
1
on
\g_{1}
, and by induction hypothesis
\B|_{\g_{1}}

is either
0
or
-\mathrm{id}
. Suppose that
\B|_{\g_{1}}=0
. For
u\in\g_{1}
we have
\B^{\prime}(u)=-u-\B(u)=-u
,
so
\g_{1}\leq\B^{\prime}(\g)=\g_{2}
. Hence,
\g=\g_{1}+\g_{2}=\g_{2}
, a contradiction.

Now assume that
\B|_{\g_{1}}=-\mathrm{id}
. For
u\in\g_{1}
we have
-u=\B(u)+\B^{\prime}(u)=-u+\B^{\prime}(u)
, thus
\B^{\prime}(u)=0
.
Therefore
\g_{1}\leq\mathrm{Ker}\B^{\prime}
, since
u\in\g_{1}
was arbitrary. Hence
\dim\g_{1}\leq\dim\g-\dim\g_{2}

and
\dim\g_{1}+\dim\g_{2}\leq\dim\g
. On the other hand, since
\g=\g_{1}+\g_{2}
, we have
\dim\g\leq\dim\g_{1}+\dim\g_{2}
,
so
\dim\g=\dim\g_{1}+\dim\g_{2}
. This implies
\g_{1}\cap\g_{2}=0
, a contradiction. ∎

Note that Theorem
2
together with Lemma
3
give an alternative proof of Theorem
1

which does not rely on the result of Scheerer
[
12
]
.

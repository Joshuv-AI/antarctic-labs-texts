Abstract
. We determine all possible degrees of cyclic isogenies of non-CM elliptic curves with rational j j -invariant over number fields of degree p p , where p p is an odd prime. The question had been answered for p = 2 p=2 , so this paper completes the classification in case when the degree of the number field is prime.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field. For a positive integer
n
, the
K
-rational points on the modular curve
X_{0}(n)
correspond to elliptic curves together with a cyclic isogeny of degree
n
defined over
K
. Thus, finding a point of some fixed degree
d
on
X_{0}(n)
is equivalent to finding an elliptic curve over a degree
d
number field together with an
n
-isogeny defined over the same field.

A question which naturally arises is to find all possible degrees of isogenies which arise for elliptic curves belonging to some class. For example, one may look at the class of all elliptic curves defined over a degree
d
number field for some fixed positive integer
d
. This is equivalent to finding all positive integers
n
for which the curve
X_{0}(n)
has a point of degree
d
. More generally, one may try finding all degree
d
points on
X_{0}(n)
.

This is in general a difficult task for
d>1
. The case
d=1
, i.e. finding all rational points on
X_{0}(n)
, has been done by Mazur
[
8
]
and Kenku (see e.g.
[
5
]
). The case
d>1
is much more difficult. Some progress has been made in computing quadratic points on
X_{0}(n)
(
[
1
]
).

A similar question which is easier to answer, and which is more specific, is to describe all points on
X_{0}(n)
of some degree
d
for which the corresponding
j
-invariant is rational. More precisely, we are interested in the following question.

Question
.

Let
d
be a fixed positive integer. Find all positive integers
n
for which there is a non-CM elliptic curve
E
with rational
j
-invariant and a cyclic isogeny of degree
n
defined over some number field
K
of degree
d
.

Remark 1.1
.

We restrict our attention only to non-CM elliptic curves. In the CM case, an elliptic curve
E
whose endomorphism ring is an order
\mathcal{O}
in some imaginary quadratic field will have isogenies of prime degrees for all prime numbers which split over that order, and the isogenies will be defined over any field containing
\mathcal{O}
and the field of definition of
E
. More on the CM case can be found in
[
2
]
.

We define an
n
-isogeny to be a cyclic isogeny of degree
n
.

For a positive integer
d
, denote by
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
the set of all positive integers
n
for which there is a non-CM elliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant and an
n
-isogeny defined over a degree
d
extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. In other words,
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(d)
is precisely the set we are trying to determine.

By Mazur
[
8
]
and Kenku
[
5
]
, we know
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
. In other words, we know all positive integers
n
for which there is a non-CM elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with a rational
n
-isogeny. We have

\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)=\{1,2,\ldots,13,15,16,17,18,21,25,37\}.

Vukorepa proved in
[
11
]
that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(2)=\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)\cup\{20,24,32,36\}
. Some of the proofs from that paper also apply to our problem.

The purpose of this article is to answer the question when the degree of the extension is an odd prime. Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

There exists a non-CM elliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant and an
n
-isogeny defined over a cubic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if

•

n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
,

•

n=2k
for some odd
k\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
,

•

n=3k
for some
k\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
which is divisible by
3
, or

•

n=28
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
p>3
be a prime number. There exists a non-CM elliptic curve with rational
j
-invariant and an
n
-isogeny defined over a degree
p
extension of
\mathbb{Q}
if and only if
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
or
n=p\cdot k
for some
k\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
which is divisible by
p
.

Remark 1.4
.

Some of the proofs in this paper are aided by the computer algebra system Magma. The relevant code can be found in the following Github repository:

github.com/inova3c/Isogenies-over-cubic-fields-Magma-codes

2.
Images of
\ell
-adic Galois representations

Since the property of having an
n
-isogeny is invariant under quadratic twisting and any two non-CM elliptic curves with common
j
-invariant are quadratic twists of each other, it’s not a loss of generality to only consider base changes of elliptic curves defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For a number field
K
, let
G_{K}=\Gal(\overline{K}/K)
denote the absolute Galois group of
K
, i.e. the group of all
\overline{K}
-automorphisms that fix
K
.

If
\phi:E\to E^{\prime}
is an
n
-isogeny defined over a number field
K
, then
\ker\phi\leq E(\overline{K})
is a cyclic subgroup of order
n
fixed by
G_{K}
. Conversely, for any cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
n
fixed by
G_{K}
there is an
n
-isogeny defined over
K
.

For a cyclic subgroup
C
of
E(\overline{K})
, where
E/K
is an elliptic curve, denote by
K(C)
the smallest field extension
K^{\prime}/K
such that
G_{K^{\prime}}
fixes
C
. The field
K(C)
is the
field of definition
of
C
.

Throughout the paper, we will use the language of Galois representations of elliptic curves. For an elliptic curve
E
, we’ll denote the mod
n
Galois representation of
E
by
\rho_{E,n}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
, and we’ll denote the
\ell
-adic representation of
E
by
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}:G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
.

We will denote the subgroup of all uppertriangular matrices in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})
by
B(n)
. The existence of a cyclic isogeny of degree
n
is equivalent to the fact that the image of the mod
n
representation is conjugate to a subgroup of
B(n)
.

The following proposition significantly simplifies our analysis.

Proposition 2.1
.

[
3
, Proposition 3.3.]

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be a non-CM elliptic curve. Let
\ell
be an odd prime. If an elliptic curve has no
\ell
-isogenies defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then it also has no
\ell
-isogenies defined over
K
for any odd-degree number field
K
, unless
\ell=7
and
j(E)=2268945/128
, in which case there is a
7
-isogeny defined over the cubic field
\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, where
\alpha^{3}-5\alpha-5=0
.

This means that nothing new happens over odd degree extensions in terms of odd prime degrees of isogenies, except in one special case. We now turn to prime power degrees of isogenies.

2.1.
Growth of cyclic isogeny degree for prime powers

a

Suppose that
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field
K
. Let
\ell
be a prime number and let
C
be a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
. The quantity of our interest is the index
[K(C):K(\ell C)]
.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that the
\ell
-adic representation
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
of
E
is defined modulo
\ell^{n}
if the image
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains all matrices congruent to
I
mod
\ell^{n}
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
3
, Proposition 3.7.]

Let
E
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
K
such that its
\ell
-adic representation is defined modulo
\ell^{n-1}
for some
n\geq 1
. Then for any cyclic subgroup
C
of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
, we have
[K(C):K(\ell C)]=\ell
.

Thus, if the
\ell
-adic representation is defined modulo
\ell^{n-1}
and we’re given an
\ell^{n-1}
-isogeny, we need to take a field extension of degree
\ell
to get an
\ell^{n}
-isogeny.

In general, let
C
be a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
.

Lemma 2.4
.

There are exactly
\ell
cyclic subgroups
D
of
E(\overline{K})
such that
\ell D=C
.

Proof.

Let
P
be a generator of
C
. There are
\ell^{2}
points
Q\in E(\overline{K})
with
\ell Q=P
. If
Q_{0}
is one of the points, the set of all such points is
\{Q_{0}+T\mid T\in E[\ell]\}
.

We claim that there are exactly
\ell
points
T
satisfying
Q_{0}+T\in\langle Q_{0}\rangle
. Indeed, look at the equation
aQ_{0}=Q_{0}+T
for some
a\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n+1}\mathbb{Z}
. Since
T
has order dividing
\ell
and
Q_{0}
has order
\ell^{n+1}
, this equation has a solution
T
if and only if
\ell^{n}\mid a-1
, so there are exactly
\ell
choices for
a
.

From here, it follows that there are exactly
\ell
cyclic subgroups
D
satisfying
\ell D=C
, each of them containing exactly
\ell
solutions
Q
to the equation
\ell Q=P
.

\Box

Let
\mathcal{S}=\{D_{0},D_{1},\ldots,D_{\ell-1}\}
be the set of subgroups satisfying
\ell D=C
. The absolute Galois group
G_{K}
acts on
\mathcal{S}
.

If the action is transitive, then the stabilizer of any
D_{i}
has index
\ell
in
G_{K}
. The corresponding fixed field has degree
\ell
over
K
, and
D_{i}
is defined over this field. This gives us an
\ell^{n+1}
-isogeny over a degree
\ell
extension of
K
.

If
G_{K}
fixes any given group
D_{i}
, then
D_{i}
is defined over
K
and we have an
\ell^{n+1}
-isogeny defined over
K
.

Let
Q
be a point satisfying
\ell Q=P
. Let
S
be a point such that
\{Q,S\}
is a basis for
E[\ell^{n+1}]
. Then the
\ell
subgroups can be represented as

D_{0}=\langle Q\rangle,D_{1}=\langle Q+\ell^{n}S\rangle,\ldots,D_{\ell-1}=\langle Q+(\ell-1)\ell^{n}S\rangle.

Now take
\sigma\in G_{K}
. Since
\ell Q=P
and
\sigma(P)\in\langle P\rangle
,
\rho_{E,\ell^{n+1}}(\sigma)
is of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
\ell^{n}c&d\end{bmatrix}
. If we calculate the action of
\sigma
on
Q+t\cdot\ell^{n}S
, we get

\sigma(Q+t\ell^{n}S)=(a+t\cdot b\ell^{n})Q+(c\ell^{n}+td\ell^{n})S.

Thus,
\sigma(Q+t\cdot\ell^{n}S)\in\langle Q+\frac{c+td}{a}\ell^{n}S\rangle
. In other words,

\sigma(D_{t})=D_{\frac{c+td}{a}}.

Denote by
\textnormal{AGL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
the group of all linear transformations on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
, i.e. all maps of the form
x\mapsto ax+b
for
a\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*}
,
b\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
. Then the action of
G_{K}
on
\mathcal{S}
is equivalent to the action of a subgroup of
\textnormal{AGL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
.

We have the following elementary result, taken from
[
3
, Lemma 3.5.]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

For every subgroup
H\leq\textnormal{AGL}_{1}(\mathbb{F}_{\ell})
with
H\neq 1
, one of two cases occurs: either
H
has order divisible by
\ell
, acts transitively on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
and has a subgroup of index
\ell
; or is cyclic of order dividing
\ell-1
, acts on
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
with orbits of size
\ell-1
with exactly one fixed point.

Proof.

We can represent the element
\{x\mapsto ax+b\}
as a matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
. If the subgroup
H
contains an element of the form
\begin{bmatrix}1&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
with
b\neq 0
, then
H
acts transitively and the mentioned element has order
\ell
. The stabilizer of any
x\in\mathbb{F}_{\ell}
is a subgroup of index
\ell
.

If
H
doesn’t contain such elements, then for any
a
, there can be at most one
b
with
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\in H
. This means that there is an embedding from
H
to
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*}
given by
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\mapsto a
. Since
\mathbb{F}_{\ell}^{*}
is cyclic of order
p-1
, the claim follows.

Now suppose that
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}
is a generator of
H
. Then
x
is a fixed point if and only if
x=ax+b
. There is a unique solution to this equation since
a\neq 1
, so there is a unique fixed point.

\Box

Applying this to our situation yields the following result. This is a slightly restated version of
[
3
, Proposition 3.6.]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\ell
be a prime,
E
an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and
C
a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
. Denote by
\mathcal{S}
the set of all cyclic subgroups
D\subseteq E(\overline{K})
satisfying
\ell D=C
. Then:

(i)

For all
D\in\mathcal{S}
, the index
[K(D):K(C)]
either equals
\ell
or divides
\ell-1
.

(ii)

If
[K(D):K(C)]=\ell
for some
D
, then
[K(D):K(C)]=\ell
for all
D
. Otherwise, there exists a subgroup
D\in\mathcal{S}
with
K(D)=K(C)
.

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number and let
k
be a positive integer divisible by
\ell
. Suppose that
E
is an elliptic curve over a number field
K
with a
k
-isogeny over
K
. Then
E
has an
\ell\cdot k
-isogeny over
K
or over a degree
\ell
extension of
K
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove this when
k
is a power of
\ell
. Let
C
be a subgroup of order
\ell^{n}
defined over
K
. Then, by Lemma
2.6
, there exists a cyclic subgroup
D
of order
\ell^{n+1}
such that
[K(D):K]=\ell
or
K(D)=K
.

The isogeny whose kernel is
D
has the desired property.

\Box

2.2.
Subgroups with uppertriangular tendencies

Remark 2.8
.

For a positive integer
m
, let
G_{m}
denote the subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
of all matrices of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
\ell^{m}c&d\end{bmatrix}
, i.e. all matrices which are uppertriangular modulo
\ell^{m}
. From the proof of
[
3
, Proposition 3.6.]
it follows that we can pick a basis for the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
in which we have

[K(C):K(\ell C)]=[\rho(G_{K(\ell C)}):\rho(G_{K(C)})]=[\rho(G_{K})\cap G_{n-1}:\rho(G_{K})\cap G_{n}],

where
\rho=\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
is the
\ell
-adic representation of
E
.

From Lemma
2.6
and the above remark, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.9
.

Let
\ell
be a prime and let
m
and
n
be positive integers with
n\geq 2
and
m<n
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and
C
a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
. Then
K(C)=K(\ell^{m}C)
if and only if there exists a basis for the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
such that the following condition is satisfied:

for any matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{K})
, if
c\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{n-m}}
then
c\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{n}}
.

This motivates the following definition.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\ell
be a prime number and let
0\leq m<n
be integers. We say a group
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
has
m
-uppertriangular tendencies
if the following condition is satisfied:

for any matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in H
, if
c\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{m}}
then
c\equiv 0\pmod{\ell^{n}}
.

Remark 2.11
.

To abbreviate, we’ll call subgroups with
m
-uppertriangular tendencies "
m
-UTT subgroups" or just "
m
-UTT", and we’ll call the defining property "
m
-UTT property". Also, we’ll say "UTT subgroups" instead of "
1
-UTT subgroups". Note that "
0
-UTT" just means that every matrix in the subgroup is uppertriangular.

We can rewrite Corollary
2.9
in new terms as follows.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
\ell
be a prime and let
m<n
be positive integers with
n\geq 2
. Let
E
be an elliptic curve over a number field
K
and
C
a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
\ell^{n}
.

Then
[K(C):K(\ell^{m}C)]=1
if and only if there exists a basis for the
\ell
-adic Tate module of
E
such that the image of the mod
\ell^{n}
representation of
E
is an
(n-m)
-UTT subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

2.2.1.
Upper bounds on the size of
UTT
subgroups

a

Let
m<n
. It turns out that an
m
-UTT subgroup
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is rather small compared to its image under the canonical surjection
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})
.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
k\leq m<n
be positive integers and let
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})
denote the canonical surjection. If
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is
m
-UTT and contains an element of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
p^{k-1}c&d\end{bmatrix}
with
c\neq 0\pmod{p}
, then
|H|\leq p^{2n-2m+2k-2}|\pi(H)|
.

Proof.

Take a matrix
h_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
p^{k-1}c_{0}&d_{0}\end{bmatrix}\in\pi(H)
with
c_{0}\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. Since the preimage of each element of
\pi(H)
has the same cardinality, it suffices to prove that there are at most
p^{2n-2m+2k-2}
matrices
h\in H
with
\pi(h)=h_{0}
. We’ll call such matrices
h

lifts
of
h_{0}
.

Consider any lift
h=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
p^{k-1}c&d\end{bmatrix}\in H
of
h_{0}
. Let
h^{\prime}=\frac{1}{\det h}\begin{bmatrix}d^{\prime}&-b^{\prime}\\
-p^{k-1}c^{\prime}&a^{\prime}\end{bmatrix}\in H
be any matrix with
\pi(h^{\prime})=h_{0}^{-1}
.

Consider the bottom left entries of
hh^{\prime}
and
h^{\prime}h
. They must be
0\pmod{p^{m}}
, so by the UTT property they are also
0\pmod{p^{n}}
. This yields the following relations:

\displaystyle p^{k-1}(cd^{\prime}-c^{\prime}d)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{p^{n}},

\displaystyle p^{k-1}(ca^{\prime}-c^{\prime}a)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\pmod{p^{n}}.

This means that
a
and
d
are uniquely determined modulo
p^{n-k+1}
by
c
and
h_{0}
. Thus, there are at most
p^{k-1}
choices for
a
and
d
once we choose
c
. Furthermore, there are at most
p^{n-m}
choices for
b
and
c
which lift
b_{0}
and
c_{0}
. In total,
h_{0}
has at most
p^{2n-2m+2k-2}
lifts.

\Box

Definition 2.14
.

Let
k
and
n
be positive integers with
k\leq n
. We say a subgroup
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is uppertriangular mod
p^{k}
if every
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in H
satisfies
c\equiv 0\pmod{p^{k}}
.

Taking
k=m
in Proposition
2.13
yields the following consequence.

Corollary 2.15
.

Let
m<n
be positive integers and let
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})
denote the canonical surjection. If
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is
m
-UTT and
H
isn’t uppertriangular mod
p^{m}
, then
|H|\leq p^{2n-2}|\pi(H)|
.

If the group
H
contains matrices of some particular shape, we can strengthen the claim. This seems like a slight improvement, but if
m
and
k
are small compared to
n
, this is actually an improvement by a factor of the order of magnitude
p^{n}
.

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
k\leq m<n
be positive integers with
n\geq m+2k-2
and let
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{m}\mathbb{Z})
denote the canonical surjection. Suppose that
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
is
m
-UTT. If
H
contains an element of the form
\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
p^{k-1}c&d\end{bmatrix}
with
c,a+d\neq 0\pmod{p}
, then
|H|\leq p^{n-m+4k-4}|\pi(H)|
.

Proof.

Let
h=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
p^{k-1}c&d\end{bmatrix}
with
c,a+d\neq 0\pmod{p}
. Let
h_{0}=\pi(h)
. As in the previous proof, it suffices to prove that
h_{0}
has at most
p^{n-m+4k-4}
lifts to
H
. From the proof of Proposition
2.13
it follows that
a
and
d
are uniquely determined modulo
p^{n-k+1}
by
c
and
h_{0}
.

Consider
h^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}a^{2}+p^{k-1}bc&*\\
p^{k-1}c(a+d)&*\end{bmatrix}
. Since
a+d\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, we know that
a^{2}+p^{k-1}bc
is uniquely determined modulo
p^{n-k+1}
. But this implies that
b
is uniquely determined modulo
p^{n-2k+2}
. Thus, there are at most
p^{2k-2}
possible choices for
b
once we’ve chosen
a,c,d
. We also know there are at most
p^{n-m}
choices for
c
and at most
p^{k-1}
choices for both
a
and
d
. This implies there are at most
p^{n-m+4k-4}
lifts of
h_{0}
and the claim is proven.

\Box

Corollary 2.17
.

Let
H\leq\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
be a UTT subgroup and denote by
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
the canonical surjection. Then
|H|\leq p^{2n-2}|\pi(H)|
if
H
is not uppertriangular mod
p
. Furthermore, if
H
contains a matrix whose trace and bottom left entry are non-zero mod
p
, then
|H|\leq p^{n-1}|\pi(H)|

Proof.

Take
k=m=1
in the previous propositions.

\Box

We’ll have to deal with the case when we have a
p
-isogeny over a base field, and a
p^{3}
-isogeny over an extension of degree
p
. The bounds we’ve obtained so far are not suitable for this case, so we need another result.

For
p
-adic numbers
x
and
y
, write
y=x+\mathcal{O}(p^{k})
if
y-x\in p^{k}\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Lemma 2.18
.

Let
p>2
be a prime number. Let
A=\begin{bmatrix}a&pb\\
pc&d\end{bmatrix}\in\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
be a matrix such that
A\equiv I\pmod{p}
. Let
A^{p}=\begin{bmatrix}a_{p}&pb_{p}\\
pc_{p}&d_{p}\end{bmatrix}
. Then
pc_{p}=p^{2}c+\mathcal{O}(p^{3})
.

Proof.

Write
A=I+pB
for
B\in\mathcal{M}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
. Then, by the binomial theorem, we have

A^{p}=I+p^{2}B+\mathcal{O}(p^{3}),

so
pc_{p}=p^{2}c+\mathcal{O}(p^{3})
.

\Box

Proposition 2.19
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
H
be a
2
-UTT subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
which is uppertriangular modulo
p
. Denote by
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p\mathbb{Z})
the canonical surjection. Then any element of
\pi^{-1}(I)
is uppertriangular mod
p^{n}
. Consequently,
|H|
divides
p^{3n-3}|\pi(H)|
.

Proof.

Suppose that there is a matrix
h=\begin{bmatrix}a&pb\\
pc&d\end{bmatrix}\in H
which is not uppertriangular and which satisfies
h\equiv I\pmod{p}
. Then by UTT property we have
c\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
. However, Lemma
2.18
implies that the left most entry of
h^{p}
is divisible by
p^{2}
and isn’t divisible by
p^{3}
, a contradiction.

Thus, any matrix
h\in H
with
\pi(h)=I
is uppertriangular. Thus, the kernel of
\pi
is a subgroup of the group of all uppertriangular lifts of
I
in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/p^{n}\mathbb{Z})
. Since there are
p^{3n-3}
such lifts, we conclude that
|\ker(\pi)|=|H|/|\pi(H)|
divides
p^{3n-3}
.

\Box

3.1.1.

We now discuss cyclic isogenies whose degrees are powers of two over cubic fields. We start by connecting the previous chapter with images of
2
-adic Galois representations.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
E/K
be an elliptic curve over a number field. Let
n\geq 2
be a positive integer and let
C
be a cyclic subgroup of
E(\overline{K})
of order
2^{n}
. Suppose that the order of
\rho_{E,2^{n}}(G_{K})
is divisible by
3
and
K(C)=K(2^{n-1}C)
. Then
|\rho_{E,2^{n}}(G_{K})|\leq 3\cdot 2^{n}
.

Proof.

We see from Corollary
2.12
that the mod
2^{n}
image
\rho_{E,2^{n}}(G_{K})
is a UTT subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z})
.

Furthermore, we claim that the mod
2
image contains an element of order
3
. Namely, let
g
be an element of order
3
in
\rho_{E,2^{n}}(G_{K})
. Then
\pi(g)
has order
1
or
3
, where
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2^{n}\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
is the canonical surjection.

If
\pi(g)\equiv I\pmod{2}
, then the bottom-left entry of
g
is even, so it must be
0
mod
2^{n}
by the UTT property. If we denote
g=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
0&c\end{bmatrix}
, then
g^{3}=I
implies
a^{3}=c^{3}=1
, which implies
a=c=1
. Finally, direct calculation yields
b=0
and
g=I
, a contradiction. Thus,
\pi(g)
has order
3
.

Elements of order
3
in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})
are
h=\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
and
h^{2}=\begin{bmatrix}0&1\\
1&1\end{bmatrix}
, so the mod
2
image contains both of them. In particular, it contains
\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&0\end{bmatrix}
, a matrix with odd bottom left entry and odd trace, so the second claim from Corollary
2.17
applies to our case. Since the mod
2
image has at most
6
elements, we obtain the bound
2^{n-1}\cdot 6=3\cdot 2^{n}
on the mod
2^{n}
image.

\Box

In
[
10
]
, Rouse and Zureick-Brown classified all possible images of
2
-adic Galois representations of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
. They also provide a list of all possible images, which we use in the next proposition. They also provided a webpage for each of the groups, which contains information on the rational points on the corresponding modular curves. The website can be found on

https://users.wfu.edu/rouseja/2adic/
.

From
[
10
, Corollary 1.3.]
, we see that the index of the
2
-adic representation divides
64
or
96
, and the
2
-adic representations are defined modulo

.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
E
be a non-CM elliptic curve defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then
E
has no cyclic

-isogenies defined over cubic extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
. If
E
has a cyclic

-isogeny or
8
-isogeny defined over a cubic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
, then this isogeny is already defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.
If
E
has a
4
-isogeny defined over a cubic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
which isn’t defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, then the image
\rho_{2,\infty}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
of the

-adic representation of

satisfies
\langle\rho_{2,\infty}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}),-I\rangle=H_{20}
, where

denotes the

[

]

Proof.
Without loss of generality we may assume that
-I
belongs to the
2
-adic image. Otherwise, we may take a quadratic twist
E^{\prime}
of

whose
2
-adic image does contain
-I
.

Suppose that

has an
8
-isogeny over a cubic extension

which isn’t defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, and let

be the kernel of the isogeny. We then have

by definition of

and
\mathbb{Q}(C)=\mathbb{Q}(4C)
as
[\mathbb{Q}(C):\mathbb{Q}(4C)]
is a power of two which divides
3
. On the other hand
\rho_{E,2^{3}}(G_{K})
is a subgroup of index
3
of
\rho_{E,2^{3}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
, which means that the order of
\rho_{E,2^{3}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is divisible by
3
. Thus, we may apply Corollary
3.1
, so the mod
8
image has at most
24
elements.

This means that the index of the mod

image in

is at least
\frac{3\cdot 2^{9}}{3\cdot 2^{3}}=2^{6}
. There are
3
possible

-adic images of index

, those are groups

in Rouse and Zureick-Brown’s list. However, none of them are defined modulo

, so their mod

-adic images, so we get a contradiction. This proves the claim for

-isogenies.

A similar argument proves the claim for

-isogenies. Namely, the mod

image would have at most
48
elements, which means the index of the
2
-adic representation would need to be at least

, which is impossible.

The non-existence of

-isogenies follows by the same argument, as there are no rational cyclic

-isogenies.

Using the same reasoning for
4
-isogenies, we conclude that the mod

image
\rho_{E,4}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
has at most
12
elements. The number of its elements is divisible by

as it contains

and an index

\rho_{E,4}(G_{K})
.

Thus, we can search through Rouse and Zureick-Brown’s list for groups which have order

or

modulo

. We find three possible groups,

and

. However, the corresponding modular curves

and

have no rational points as can be seen by looking at the corresponding webpages, so the image must be

.

\Box

We have the following immediate consequence.

Before solving the cases when

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with a
27
-isogeny over some cubic extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Then
E
has a
9
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

Suppose that there exists a curve
E
with a
27
-isogeny over
K
and no
9
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
. We know by Proposition
2.1
that
E
then has a
3
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

We now use
[
9
, Corollary 1.3.1.]
. From there, we know that the index of
H:=\langle\rho_{E,27}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}),-I\rangle
in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})
is at most
36
, so
H
has at least
3^{7}\cdot 4
elements since
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})
has
16\cdot 3^{9}
elements.

However, if we choose an appropriate basis,
H
has the property that each bottom left entry of a matrix in
H
which is
0
mod
9
must also be
0
mod
27
. In other words,
H
is a
2
-UTT subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})
. Furthermore,
H
is uppertriangular mod
3
since there is a
3
-isogeny defined over
\mathbb{Q}
. Since there is no
9
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, we know that
H
is not uppertriangular mod
9
.

Denote by
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/3\mathbb{Z})
the canonical surjection. By Proposition
2.19
,

3^{7}\cdot 4\leq|H|\leq 3^{6}\cdot|\pi(H)|\leq 3^{6}\cdot|B(3)|=3^{7}\cdot 4.

Thus, we must have equality everywhere. This means that every uppertriangular matrix congruent to
I
mod
3
belongs to
H
, and that
\pi(H)
consists of all uppertriangular matrices mod
3
.

Now take a matrix
h=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
3c&d\end{bmatrix}\in H
with

. Suppose that
h
is not uppertriangular, i.e. that

.

Then

3c(a+d)&d^{2}+3bc\end{bmatrix}
has bottom left entry divisible by

, so it must be divisible by

and we have

. But then consider
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&4\end{bmatrix}\cdot h=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
12c&4d\end{bmatrix}
. This matrix is the same as

modulo

, so we must have

, but this together with

implies

, a contradiction. Thus, any matrix whose trace is
0
mod
3
only has uppertriangular lifts in

, and

contains all of its uppertriangular lifts.

We now conclude that any uppertriangular matrix is contained in
H
, since it’s easy to check that matrices of trace zero along with
\pm I
generate the subgroup

. However, there are exactly

uppertriangular matrices in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/27\mathbb{Z})
, so

must be equal to the group of all uppertriangular matrices mod

, which is a contradiction since this would imply the existence of a

-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
.

\Box

The
j
-invariants for which there is a
p
-isogeny for
p\in\{3,5,7,13\}
are parametrized as follows (this is taken from
[
7
]
, Table 3):

(7)

\displaystyle X_{0}(3):j

\displaystyle=\frac{(h+27)(h+3)^{3}}{h},

(8)

\displaystyle X_{0}(5):j

\displaystyle=\frac{(h^{2}+10h+5)^{3}}{h},

(9)

\displaystyle X_{0}(7):j

\displaystyle=\frac{(h^{2}+13h+49)(h^{2}+5h+1)^{3}}{h},

(10)

\displaystyle X_{0}(13):j

\displaystyle=\frac{(h^{2}+5h+13)(h^{4}+7h^{3}+20h^{2}+19h+1)^{3}}{h}.

Denote by
g_{3},g_{5},g_{7},g_{13}
the polynomials on the right hand side of those parametrizations.

We need to find all rational points on curves
g_{p}(h)=f_{20}(t)
for
p\in\{3,5,7,13\}
, and check which of those points yields a non-CM
j
-invariant. We’ll call these points non-trivial.

We need to prove there are no non-trivial points on those curves. It’s enough to prove this for
f_{7}
or
f_{3}
instead of
f_{20}
, as there is a map from
X_{20}
to
X_{3}
and
X_{7}
.

•

Case
p=3
.
We use the curve
X_{7}
, i.e. we look at the equation
g_{3}(h)=f_{7}(t)
. Using Magma, we see that this is a genus
1
curve, and we can find all the rational points. There are
5
points in total, three of which yield CM
j
-invariants. For the remaining two
j
-invariants, we use modular polynomials to check that there are no
9
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}
(or equivalently that there are no
27
-isogenies over cubic extensions).

•

Case
p=5
.
We use the curve
X_{7}
again, i.e. we look at the equation
g_{5}(h)=f_{7}(t)
. This is a genus
1
curve, and we can find all points on this curve. It turns out that there are two points on this curve. However, one can check that the corresponding two
j
-invariants only yield
4
-isogenies over degree
6
extensions of the rationals.

•

Case
p=7
.
The curve
g_{7}(h)=f_{7}(t)
is hyperelliptic of genus two. All rational points on this curve can be found using the Chabauty method.

It turns out that there are two rational points, and the corresponding
j
-invariants are
\frac{351}{4}
and
\frac{-38575685889}{16384}
. It can easily be checked using modular polynomials that both of them yield
4
-isogenies over cubic extensions, so
28\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(3)
.

•

Case
p=13
.
We use the curve
X_{3}
this time, i.e. we look at the equation
g_{13}(h)=f_{3}(t)
. This is a genus one curve. It turns out there are no non-trivial rational points on this curve, i.e. no suitable
j
-invariants, so
4\cdot 13\not\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(3)
.

\Box

This concludes the proof of the cubic case (Theorem
1.2
).

Remark 3.12
.

The calculations in the previous proof were done in Magma.

We would like to thank Borna Vukorepa for finding all rational points on the curve
g_{7}(h)=f_{7}(t)
and providing the relevant Magma code.

4.
Isogeny degrees over extensions of prime degree
p>3

We’ve seen in Corollary
2.7
that
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
contains all numbers of the form
k\cdot p
for
k\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
divisible by
p
. We now prove that these are the only elements of
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
which aren’t already in
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
\ell
and
p
are prime numbers with
p\nmid\ell(\ell-1)
, and let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with an
\ell^{n}
-isogeny defined over a degree
p
extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
. Then this isogeny is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

This follows from the fact that
[\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}}):\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(G_{K})]
divides
[K:\mathbb{Q}]
, and the fact that
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
has order coprime to
p
. This means that the images
\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
and
\rho_{E,\ell^{n}}(G_{K})
are equal, which implies the claim.

\Box

4.1.
Case
p>5

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\ell\geq 7
be a prime number, and let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with an
\ell
-isogeny over a number field of odd degree. Then its
\ell
-adic representation
\rho_{E,p^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is defined modulo
\ell
.

Proof.

Note that the
\ell
-isogeny is actually defined over
\mathbb{Q}
by Proposition
2.1
. The claim now follows immediately from
[
6
, Theorem 3.9.]
.

\Box

Corollary 4.3
.

If
p\geq 7
is a prime, then
p^{3}\not\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Lemma
4.2
, Lemma
2.3
and the fact that there are no
p^{2}
-isogenies over
\mathbb{Q}
.

\Box

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
p\geq 7
be a prime, and let
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(p)
be coprime to
p
. Then
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
and any
n
-isogeny defined over a degree
p
extension of
\mathbb{Q}
is already defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for prime powers. This follows from Lemma
4.1
as there are no primes
\ell\neq p
in
\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
such that
p\mid\ell(\ell-1)
.

\Box

We can now finish the proof of Theorem
1.3
for
p\geq 7
. Namely, let
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(\ell)
,
n\not\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
. By Lemma
4.4
,
n=k\cdot p^{t}
for some
k\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
coprime to
p
and some
t\geq 1
. By Lemma
4.2
,
t\leq 2
.

Since
k
and
p
are coprime, there must be a
k\cdot p
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, so
k\cdot p\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
. Thus,
n
is of the form stated in the theorem.

4.2.
Case
p=5

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with a
125
-isogeny defined over a quintic extension of
\mathbb{Q}
. Then there is a
25
-isogeny defined over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Proof.

The curve
E
certainly has a
5
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
by Proposition
2.1
. From
[
4
, Theorem 2]
it follows that either the
5
-adic representation is defined modulo
5
or the index of the
5
-adic representation in
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5})
is not divisible by
25
. In the former case, we can use Lemma
2.3
.

Suppose that
[\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{5}):\rho_{E,5^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})]
is not divisible by
25
. Suppose that there is no
25
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
. Then the group
H:=\rho_{E,125}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
is a
2
-UTT subgroup of
\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/125\mathbb{Z})
which is uppertriangular mod
5
.

Let
\pi:\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/125\mathbb{Z})\to\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/5\mathbb{Z})
denote the canonical surjection. Proposition
2.19
tells us that
|H|\text{ divides }5^{6}|\pi(H)|,\text{ which divides }5^{6}|B(5)|=5^{7}\cdot 16.

However, then
[\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/125\mathbb{Z}):H]
is divisible by
25
as
|\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/125\mathbb{Z})|
is divisible by
5^{9}
and
|H|
isn’t divisible by
5^{8}
. This yields a contradiction.

\Box

Lemma 4.6
.

We have
5^{4}\not\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(5)
.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with a
5^{4}
-isogeny over a degree
5
extension
K
. Then
E
certainly has a
5
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
by Proposition
2.1
.

From
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
, we know that either the
5
-adic representation is defined modulo
5
or the index is not divisible by
25
. In the former case, we immediately reach a contradiction with Lemma
2.3
.

In the latter case, suppose that there is a degree
5
extension
K/\mathbb{Q}
such that there is a
5^{4}
-isogeny over
K
. Then, since
\rho_{E,5^{4}}(G_{K})
is an index
5
subgroup of
\rho_{E,5^{4}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
, we know that the index
[\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/5^{4}\mathbb{Z}):\rho_{E,5^{4}}(G_{K})]
is not divisible by
5^{3}
.

However, the index
[\textnormal{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/5^{4}\mathbb{Z}):B(5^{4})]
is divisible by
5^{3}
, so
\rho_{E,5^{4}}(G_{K})
cannot be contained in
B(5^{4})
, a contradiction.

\Box

Lemma 4.7
.

If
n
is coprime to
5
and
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(5)
, then
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(1)
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove the claim for prime powers.

If
\ell^{k}
is a prime power with
\ell\geq 7
, then the
\ell
-adic representation of
E
is defined modulo
\ell
by Lemma
4.2
. But then, using Lemma
2.3
, it follows that any isogeny of degree
\ell^{k}
which isn’t defined over
\mathbb{Q}
has to be defined over an extension whose degree is a power of
\ell
, which is a contradiction.

If
\ell^{k}
is a prime power with
\ell<5
, the claim immediately follows from Lemma
4.1
.

\Box

It remains to settle numbers divisible by
5
. Note that any
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(5)
divisible by
5
is either a power of
5
, of the form
2^{a}5^{b}
, or of the form
3^{a}5^{b}
.

The powers of
5
have been dealt with already.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(5)
and suppose
n=2^{a}5^{b}
or
3^{a}5^{b}
for positive integers
a
and
b
. Then
a=1
and
b<3
.

Proof.

If
n\in\Psi_{\mathbb{Q}}(5)
is divisible by
20
or by
45
, then, by Lemma
4.1
, there is a
20
-isogeny or a
45
-isogeny defined over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is impossible. Thus
a=1
.

Now suppose that there is a
2\cdot 125
-isogeny or a
3\cdot 125
-isogeny over some cubic extension. Then Lemma
4.5
and Lemma
4.1
imply the existence of a
2\cdot 25
-isogeny or a
3\cdot 25
-isogeny over
\mathbb{Q}
, which is a contradiction.

\Box

This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

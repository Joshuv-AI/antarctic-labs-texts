Soliton solutions with bifurcation, sensitivity, chaotic and stability analysis of the (2+1)-dimensional Zoomeron equation using generalized Riccati equation mapping method
Authors: Arooma Zainab, Khaled M Saad, Muhammad Abbas, Waleed M Hamanah, Muhammad Zain Yousaf, Asnake Birhanu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe paper provides a dynamical study of the (2+1)-dimensional Zoomeron equation using the generalized Riccati equation mapping method. By applying an appropriate traveling wave transformation, the equation is reduced to an ordinary differential equation, enabling the construction of various solutions. There are several types of soliton solutions exhibited by the analysis including kink, anti-kink, bell structure or bright solitons, periodic soliton, anti-bell shape or dark soliton and W-shape soliton, singular periodic shape, singular bell shape and V-pattern solutions in terms of hyperbolic and trigonometric functions. The solutions prove rich in understanding the nonlinear wave phenomena of the Zoomeron model. Nonlinear dynamics are investigated using bifurcation, chaotic, sensitivity and stability analysis that uncover complex solution behaviors and stability regimes. This work first and exclusively presents new V-pattern and W-shaped soliton solutions of the (2+1)-dimensional Zoomeron equation by using considered method and offers the first systematic study of their transition from periodic to chaotic behavior. Modulation instability analysis verifies the stability of soliton structures against perturbations. To demonstrate the features and evolution of the solutions, detailed graphical representations are presented, such as 3D surface plots, 2D profiles and contour plots. These visualizations demonstrate the dynamic behaviors and structural properties of the resultant soliton solutions, exemplifying the efficiency of the generalized Riccati equation mapping method for studying intricate nonlinear systems.Keywords: Generalized Riccati equation mapping method, (2+1)-Dimensional Zoomeron equation, Soliton solutions, Dynamical study, StabilitySubject terms: Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

Nonlinear partial differential equations (NLPDEs) are essential for modeling intricate phenomena in numerous real-world applications. They model heat conduction phenomena relevant for the design of cooling systems and heat transfer optimization in industry. NLPDEs control air and water flow’s behavior in fluid dynamics and aerodynamics, contributing to aircraft, ship design and weather forecasting. Electromagnetic field and wireless communication systems depend on NLPDEs like Maxwell’s equations to design antennas and radar systems. In biology, they describe population dynamics, disease epidemiology, and pattern formation. They are also applied in geology for seismic wave analysis and in environmental science to analyze pollutant dispersion and groundwater remediation. The NLPDEs are central in financial mathematics to determine option prices and manage risks. Moreover, they enable image processing methods such as denoising and edge detection that improve medical imaging and satellite imagery. Their wide range of applications in engineering, physics, biology, finance and environmental sciences underscores their importance to deciphering and resolving complex real world issues. Here are some well-known NLPDEs1–5.

In recent studies6–13 , fractional partial differential equations (PDEs) have attracted widespread attention as they can represent intricate systems that have memory and hereditary features that cannot be realistically represented by classical integer-order PDEs. Research has considered the oscillation and dynamical properties of such fractional PDEs with different fractional derivatives, resulting in new oscillation conditions and analytical techniques applicable to applied sciences. In addition, analytical methods such as the extended tanh-function method and Bäcklund transformation have been used with success to obtain exact and approximate soliton and traveling wave solutions, which are basic requirements for nonlinear phenomena in physics and engineering. Of interest, here are nonlinear electrical transmission lines (NLTLs), where the capacitors allow for the generation and optimization of pulses, with models that are described by NLPDEs like the Korteweg-de Vries (KdV) equation. The recent advances have better understood pulse shaping and soliton stability in these systems.

Some significant NLPDEs have been analyzed extensively for their potential to simulate intricate physical processes. The KdV equation is a basic model for shallow water waves and soliton propagation14. The Kadomtsev-Petviashvili (KP) equation, a generalization of KdV to dimensions, describes multidimensional wave interactions and is used in fluid mechanics and plasma physics15. The generalized KP equation generalizes the traditional KP model to higher dimensions, showing more complex soliton dynamics and integrability properties16. The nonlinear Schrödinger equation also models wave packets in nonlinear optics and plasma17, whereas the sine-Gordon equation models events in crystal dislocations and superconductivity18. Analytical techniques like ()-expansion method19, inverse scattering method20, sine-Gordon expansion technique21, extended Jacobi’s elliptic function method22 and Hirota’s bilinear method23 were most used to solve NLPDEs. These methods were utilized in constructing exact soliton and wave solutions.

In this work, an efficient analytical approach is employed to obtain different soliton solutions of the dimensional Zoomeron equation. The generalized Riccati equation mapping method (GREMM) has shown superior effectiveness in solving NLPDEs to yield accurate and varied solution structures effective in diverse physical contexts. This approach has been effectively implemented on several NLPDEs, which is an indicator of its versatility and stability in forming exact traveling wave solutions. The GREMM allows for the building of various types of solutions, ranging from solitary waves and periodic structures to complex compound forms. In higher-dimensional nonlinear systems like the zoomeron equation, the GREMM is highly analytical in nature, adding to the growing literature on novel solution techniques for nonlinear wave dynamics. Because of easy implementation and the potential to reveal new families of solutions, the GREMM is a useful tool in theoretical as well as in applied nonlinear science. To find traveling wave solutions, the GREMM is applied to some NLPDEs as given in24–28. The GREMM is a very potent analytical tool for finding solitary wave solutions to nonlinear partial differential equations. The method involves the creation of an appropriate transformation that will transform the given governing nonlinear equation into an ordinary differential one, which can then be written in terms of a Riccati-type equation. One of the greatest strengths of this method is its simplicity and well-defined procedure, enabling one to derive different kinds of solutions, e.g., solitary, periodic, and rational wave solutions, without making any strong assumptions or using complicated symbolic manipulations. Furthermore, the GREMM presents a clear vision of the underlying mathematical essence of nonlinear models, and as such, it is extremely powerful for analyzing the dynamic behavior of physical systems. Because of its flexibility and versatility, the approach remains an important aid to the study of higher-dimensional nonlinear evolution equations like the -dimensional Zoomeron equation being examined here.

The (2+1)-dimensional Zoomeron equation is one of the most celebrated NLPDEs that describes complicated wave processes in fluid mechanics, plasma physics, and nonlinear optics. Several analytical techniques have already been successfully employed to derive exact soliton solutions of this equation. Some prominent approaches are the Exp-function approach and modified F-expansion approach, which yield several traveling wave and soliton solutions. New mapping method and new extended auxiliary equation method, useful for obtaining singular, kink, anti-kink, periodic, and hyperbolic function solutions. Sub-equation approach and generalized Kudryashov approach, which give hyperbolic and trigonometric function solutions and auxiliary equation method, helpful for building other exact solutions. These approaches have uncovered various soliton structures like bright, dark, kink, and periodic waves. The Zoomeron equation has uses in modeling nonlinear wave propagation in fluids, optical fibers, and other physical systems where soliton dynamics are important29–33. Despite numerous studies on the Zoomeron equation, a comprehensive investigation combining the construction of novel solitary wave solutions with a full dynamical systems analysis (bifurcation, chaos, sensitivity) remains lacking. This aim of this study is to fill this gap.

Bifurcation analysis has been used widely in NLPDEs like the fractional complex Ginzburg-Landau equation and its extensions with Kerr and parabolic law nonlinearities to identify critical parameter values at which the system switches between multiple wave behaviors. Classification of various exact traveling wave solutions like compactons and bounded waves with degenerate equilibria and nonanalytic vector fields is made possible through this method. The Ginzburg–Landau theory is also a paradigm for bifurcation phenomena in manifolds, with eigenvalues of Laplace-type operators determining bifurcation points. These investigations bypass difficulties associated with singular wave-traveling systems and complex elliptic integrals and shed light on the intricate solution structures and stability features. In addition, bifurcation theory of corresponding PDEs such as nonlinear Kodama and fractional Wazwaz–Benjamin–Bona–Mahony (WBBM) equations reveals transitions to chaotic and quasi-periodic behavior, and sensitivity analysis reveal how slight parameter changes affect solution stability.34–38. Bifurcation analysis in39 of the perturbed Schrödinger-Hirota equation provides evidence of qualitative changes in solution structure with varying system parameters, providing signatures of transitions from stable to unstable states. Chaotic dynamics in40 are evidenced via sensitive dependence on initial conditions with attributes of unpredictable and a periodic trajectories in the phase space of the system. Sensitivity analysis provides shows the small perturbations in initial conditions or in parameters result in dramatically different soliton dynamics, emphasizing the complex nature of such nonlinear optical systems.

In this work, the main focus is on using the GREMM to compute new results in individual, intricate and complex hyperbolic trigonometric structures that contain kink, anti-kink solutions, bell structure or bright solitons, periodic soliton, anti-bell shape or dark soliton and W-shape soliton, singular periodic shape, singular bell shape and V-pattern in the -dimensional Zoomeron equation. Bifurcation analysis is carried out in order to study the qualitative change of solution’s behavior that results from variations in the system parameters and may eventually cause chaotic dynamics and illustrate system’s sensitivity. Stability of solutions is independently analyzed through the addition of perturbation terms, which test the stability of soliton structures under small perturbations. A vital approach for researching NLPDEs, the GREMM is used in this study to generate solitary wave solutions of the -dimensional Zoomeron equation. A review of existing research shows that soliton solutions of the -dimensional Zoomeron equation continue to be constructed using the GREMM. Keeping this in view, we will utilize the suggested techniques in solving the -dimensional Zoomeron equation efficiently in the current research. The novel concepts created have the potential to be implemented across many areas of non linear dynamics. The originality of the research is in deriving new solitary wave solutions of the -dimensional Zoomeron equation based on the GREMM consisting of V-shaped, W-shaped, singular, and anti-bell solitons. Moreover, the research offers an extensive dynamical analysis of the solutions, including their periodicity, chaotic nature, and sensitivity to initial conditions. Such a combination of building various solution types and systematically studying their dynamics makes the current research stand out from previous works.

Chaos in scientific and mathematical terms, is a description of the very complicated and totally random behavior exhibited by nonlinear systems. This idea suggests a condition where the patterns and results are not clear cut or easily predictable, showing a degree of complexity that makes it very difficult to correctly forecast or comprehend the later developments in such systems. Chaotic behavior is very important in the dynamics of Zoomeron equation, especially for bifurcation and stability analysis. Chaos is an irregular, unpredictable long term behavior that results from deterministic systems under specific parameter conditions. The phenomenon is very sensitive to initial conditions, with small changes in the initial state causing drastically different results in the long term.

Physical intrepertation

The -dimensional Zoomeron equation resulted as an important generalization of the classical Zoomeron model, originally formulated to model localized, nonlinear wave interactions with zooming (expanding or contracting) properties. While scientists generalized classical Zoomeron model to -dimension, to model more intricate physical phenomena in higher-dimensional nonlinear systems-namely in fluid mechanics, plasma physics, and optical waveguide. This higher-dimensional form admits more complex wave structures such as lumps, breathers, and rogue waves. Its discovery was motivated by the necessity to investigate multi-directional wave propagation and interactions between localized structures in a plane, which cannot be described in lower dimensions. The equation combines nonlinear and dispersive effects and plays a principal role in investigations of integrable and near-integrable systems in contemporary mathematical physics. The terms of the equation control different features of wave behavior: the term defines wave dispersion, determining how the wave profile changes in time; describes spatial diffusion, determining whether a wave broadens or narrows when propagating; and the nonlinear term mixes spatial and temporal variations, providing energy exchange and wave interactions. Employing the GREMM technique, numerous soliton solutions such as kink, anti-kink, bell structure, periodic soliton, anti-bell shape, W-shape, singular periodic wave, and singular bell shape and V-pattern have been obtained, each of which provides insights into the interaction among dispersion, diffusion, and nonlinearity in higher-dimensional systems. Comparison, advantages, and limitations

Here, the comparative study of the suggested method with existing works is given. The current approach and previous methods are explained with their advantages and limitations to indicate its efficacy and scope for improvement.

Comparison

A comparison with recent literature21–25 indicates that the previous investigations primarily reported standard soliton structures in the form of bell, kink, and periodic-type solutions, while the current study advances these findings by presenting new V-shaped and W-shaped solitons for the (2+1)-dimensional Zoomeron equation. These new forms, derived by the GREMM, reveal the technique’s capacity to produce larger and more varied family solutions than standard analytical techniques.

Advantages

The GREMM has some benefits compared to conventional analytical methods. It offers a systematic and adaptive platform for building various forms of exact solutions such as hyperbolic, trigonometric, and mixed function forms. The approach also makes it possible to derive sophisticated-shaped solitons like V-shaped, W-shaped, and singular structures, which traditional approaches such as the tanh-function or Jacobi elliptic function methods cannot easily reproduce. Moreover, GREMM can be integrated directly with dynamical system analysis (sensitivity, chaos, and bifurcation), providing a single perspective of both analytical and qualitative behaviors.

Limitations

Although GREMM is effective in building different soliton structures, the study has some limitations. The method has strong parameter dependence, which might limit its applicability to other nonlinear systems. In addition, the analysis mostly concentrates on traveling-wave solutions, which might miss non-traveling or multi-dimensional ones. Finally, chaotic behavior was investigated by means of numerical simulation, which might not cover all nonlinear effects of complex systems.

Conclusion

In the present work, the GREMM was applied to the -dimensional Zoomeron equation to obtain a variety of exact soliton solutions. The investigation explore different types of nonlinear wave structures like, kink and anti-kink solutions, bell structure or bright solitons, periodic soliton, anti-bell shape or dark soliton and W-shape soliton, singular periodic shape, singular bell shape, represented by hyperbolic and trigonometric functions. The analytical solutions were further presented through 2D, 3D, and contour plots, which graphically exhibit the dynamical behavior and stability of the solitons in space and time. Bifurcation, chaotic and sensitivity analysis of the system also discussed. These results not only add richness to the solution space of the Zoomeron equation but also give insights into the nonlinear dynamics of multidimensional wave propagation. The efficiency and universality of the GREMM technique underscore its potential to serve as an effective tool in the study of intricate NLPDEs in mathematical physics and engineering sciences. With the above limitations in mind, the future work will seek to reduce the parameter sensitivity of GREMM, generalize the framework to non-traveling and higher-dimensional wave phenomena, and merge hybrid analytical-numerical methods for a deeper examination of chaotic dynamics. There is potential future research to apply this method to fractional or variable-coefficient forms of the Zoomeron equation for studying even more intricate dynamical phenomena.

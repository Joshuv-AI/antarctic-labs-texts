A note on a homological epimorphism whose source is a piecewise hereditary algebra Thanks: 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 16B50, 16G20, 16E45, 18G80 Thanks: TA was partly supported by JSPS KAKENHI Grant Number JP25K06950.
Takuma Aihara

Abstract
. We study a homological epimorphism of proper dg algebras and show that if the source is a finite dimensional piecewise hereditary algebra, then the target is derived equivalent to some piecewise hereditary algebra.



1.
Introduction and results

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional algebra over an algebraically closed field
K
and
S
a (basic) presilting object in the perfect derived category
\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
of
\Lambda
.
Then there exists a recollement
(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{A}),\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda),\operatorname{\mathsf{Tria}}S)
, where
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(-)
stands for the (unbounded) derived category and
\mathcal{A}
is the dg endomorphism algebra of
\Lambda
in a dg enhancement of
\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)/\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}S

[
AH
,
KY
]
.
A problem is what the dg algebra
\mathcal{A}
is.
We know that for each
i
, the cohomology
H^{i}(\mathcal{A})
is finite dimensional over
K
.
For example, if
S=e\Lambda
for an idempotent
e
of
\Lambda
, then
\mathcal{A}
is quasi-isomorphic to
\widetilde{\Lambda}/\widetilde{\Lambda}\widetilde{e}\widetilde{\Lambda}

[
KY
]
.
Here,
\widetilde{\Lambda}
is a dg path algebra quasi-isomorphic to
\Lambda

[
O
]
.
However, it is quite difficult to answer the question in general.

Assuming
\Lambda
to be piecewise hereditary, we obtain the main theorem of this paper.

Theorem
.

Keeping above,
\mathcal{A}
is derived equivalent to some piecewise hereditary algebra.
Moreover, if
\Lambda
is of Dynkin type, then so is the algebra.

Example
.

Let
\varphi:\Lambda\to\mathcal{B}
be a homological epimorphism of dg algebras with
\mathcal{B}
proper.
Then, we have a recollement
(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{B}),\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda),\mathcal{X})

[
NS
]
.
Now, let us suppose that
\Lambda
is piecewise hereditary.
Since the dg algebra
\mathcal{B}
is compact in
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda)
, it is seen that
\mathcal{X}
is compactly generated.
By
[
AHMV
]
, we obtain a presilting object
S
of
\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
with
\mathcal{X}=\operatorname{\mathsf{Tria}}S
.
Hence, the theorem leads to the fact that
\mathcal{B}
is derived equivalent to a piecewise hereditary algebra
\Gamma
; if in addition,
\Lambda
is of Dynkin type, then so is
\Gamma
.

By
[
AH
, Corollary 2.17]
, we recover a result of Xu–Yang on Bongartz-type lemma
[
XY
]
.

Corollary
.

Any presilting of a piecewise hereditary algebra can be completed to silting.

2.
Proof of the theorem

We may assume that
\Lambda
is a hereditary algebra; when it is a canonical algebra, we regard it as a hereditary abelian category with tilting object.
Put
\mathcal{T}:=\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
.

Let
S:=S_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{r}
be the indecomposable decomposition of the presilting object.
By
[
AI
, Proposition 3.11]
, under a suitable relabelling we obtain an exceptional sequence
(S_{1},\cdots,S_{r})
; that is,
S_{i}
is pretilting-brick and
\operatorname{Hom}_{\mathcal{T}}(S_{i},S_{j}[t])=0
for
1\leq j<i\leq r
and all integers
t
.
Then, there exists a semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{D}*\mathcal{C}
with
\mathcal{C}:=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}S
and
\mathcal{D}:={}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}
.
Since we have an exact sequence
0\to\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{T}\to\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}(\mathcal{A})\to 0
of triangulated categories (see
[
AH
]
for example), one gets a triangle equivalence
\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}(\mathcal{A})\simeq\mathcal{D}
.

By the induction on
r
, we show that
\mathcal{D}
admits a tilting object
M
with
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M)
piecewise hereditary.
If
r=1
, then there is a (classical) tilting object
S\oplus M
with
M\in\mathcal{D}
because of the classical Bongartz’s lemma;
see also
[
A
, Proposition 2.16]
.
As
M
is partial tilting, we see that
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M)
is piecewise hereditary
[
AHKL
, Corollary 5.10]
.

Let
r>1
and
S^{\prime}:=S_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus S_{r-1}
.
As
(S_{1},\cdots,S_{r-1})
is an exceptional sequence, we have a semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{T}=\mathcal{D}^{\prime}*\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}S^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}:={}^{\perp}\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
.
By the induction hypothesis, we observe that
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
possesses a tilting object
M^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M^{\prime})
piecewise hereditary.
Since
S_{r}
is a member of
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
and exceptional in
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
, there exists a semiorthogonal decomposition
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}={}^{\perp_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{E}*\mathcal{E}
with
\mathcal{E}=\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}S_{r}
and
{}^{\perp_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{E}
admits a tilting object
M
with
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M)
piecewise hereditary.
It is not difficult to check that
{}^{\perp_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}}}\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{D}\ (:={}^{\perp}(\operatorname{\mathsf{thick}}S))
, whence we are done.

Finally, assume that
\Lambda
is of Dynkin type.
Since
\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\Lambda)
is silting-discrete, we derive from
[
AH
, Theorem 2.15]
that
\operatorname{\mathsf{per}}(\mathcal{A})\simeq\mathsf{K^{b}}(\operatorname{\mathsf{proj}}\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M))
is also silting-discrete.
This implies that
\operatorname{End}_{\mathcal{T}}(M)
is of Dynkin type.

3.
Remarks

(1)

The theorem is a derived category version of Crawly-Boevey’s theorem
[
CB
]
, and is a dg/silting version of Angeleri-Hugel–Koenig–Liu’s theorem
[
AHKL
]
.

(2)

If
\Lambda
is a piecewise hereditary algebra, then the thick closure of
S
is also triangle equivalent to the perfect derived category of some piecewise hereditary algebra.
In particular, if a recollement
(\mathcal{Y},\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda),\mathcal{X})
with
\mathcal{X}
compactly generated exists, then it is that of piecewise hereditary algebras; i.e.,
(\mathcal{Y},\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda),\mathcal{X})\simeq(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Gamma),\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Lambda),\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\Sigma))
.

(3)

We can never drop the assumption of
\Lambda
being a piecewise hereditary algebra.
In fact, it is known that the dg algebra
\mathcal{A}
does not necessarily admit a silting object
[
CJS
,
Kr
,
Ka
]
; all the algebras
\Lambda
given in the papers have finite global dimension.
Thus, even if the global dimension of
\Lambda
is finite,
\mathcal{A}
is not always derived equivalent to a finite dimensional/nonpositive (connective) dg algebra.

(4)

The center in a recollement does not necessarily inherit the heredity property from the both sides.
We close this paper by giving an example below.

Example
.

Let
\mathcal{K}
be the dg algebra presented by the quiver
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{x}
\scriptstyle{y}
\textstyle{2}
with
\deg(x)=0
and
\deg(y)=\ell
(trivial differential). Taking the primitive idempotent
e
of
\mathcal{K}
corresponding to the vertex 2, we have a recollement
(\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{K}e\mathcal{K}),\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{K}),\operatorname{\mathsf{Tria}}(e\mathcal{K}))
.
One observes that
\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(\mathcal{K}/\mathcal{K}e\mathcal{K})\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{D}}(K)\simeq\operatorname{\mathsf{Tria}}(e\mathcal{K})
; so, the both sides are hereditary.
However, the center is not derived equivalent to an
ordinary
algebra unless
|\ell|\leq 1

[
LY
, Corollary 3.2]
.

If
|\ell|=1
, then by
[
LY
, Corollary 3.10]
we see that
\mathcal{K}
is derived equivalent to the algebra given by the quiver
\textstyle{1\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{y}
\textstyle{2\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{z}
with relation
zy=0
; this is not piecewise hereditary, either.

Applying the Koszul dual, let us suppose
\ell\leq 0
.
Then, we observe that
\mathcal{K}
has global dimension
1-\ell
in the sense of Minamoto
[
M
]
.

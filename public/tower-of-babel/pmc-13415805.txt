Fuzzy comprehensive evaluation of rock burst risk based on combined subjective and objective weighting
Authors: Jing Zhang, Beifang Wang, Yuanhao Lv, Xinyi Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDuring deep coal mining, the rock mass is subjected to high in-situ stress and strong mining disturbances. Its mechanical properties are more sensitive than those of shallow rock mass, which can easily induce rock burst disasters, seriously restricting the safety and efficient production of the mine. To address this problem, this study takes the 718 working face of Hongyang No. 3 Mine as the engineering background, integrates theoretical analysis, mathematical modeling, and field application to deeply analyze the main factors of rock burst. And establishes a rock burst risk evaluation mathematical model based on subjective and objective combination weighting. The research results show that the main control factors for rock burst include natural geological factors, mining technology factors and organizational management factors, with weights of 0.62322, 0.23949 and 0.13729 respectively. Among these, rock burst tendency, coal pillar width, and prevention and control investment are identified as the most influential factors. Based on the analytic hierarchy process and the entropy weight method, the subjective and objective weights are combined, and the comprehensive weights are obtained. On this basis, a multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation model for rock burst risk is constructed. After evaluation, the 718 working face is classified as a medium rock burst risk level, which is consistent with field observations, thereby verifying the reliability and applicability of the proposed model. This study effectively integrates expert experience, test results, and actual on-site conditions, overcomes the one-sidedness of a single weighting method, and thereby provides a scientific basis for the quantitative risk evaluation and optimization of rock burst prevention strategies under similar mining conditions.Keywords: Deep mining, Rock burst, Combined weighting, Fuzzy comprehensive evaluationSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Natural hazards, Solid Earth sciences

Introduction

With increasing mining depth in coal mines, the original rock stress level by the surrounding rock has significantly increased. Under the combined influence of high ground stress and intense mining disturbances, the stress environment becomes increasingly complex, leading to a higher incidence of rock burst disasters. Moreover, the mechanisms and inducing factors of rock burst exhibit pronounced nonlinear and multi-factor coupling characteristics.

Extensive studies have been conducted on the occurrence mechanisms and risk evaluation of rock burst from various perspectives. In terms of the mechanism of rock burst occurrence, Li et al.1 proposed a far-middle-near-field division standard for the influence range of mining activities, revealed the influence laws of mining disturbances on roadway stability, and also established a theoretical model for the occurrence of rock burst considering damage effects. Dou et al.2 systematically studied the mechanism of rock burst during horizontal subsection mining based on the special mining condition of steeply inclined and extremely thick coal seam. Pan et al.3,4 explored the occurrence laws of rock burst, classified the main influencing factors leading to the occurrence of rock burst, and proposed prevention and control countermeasures. Chen et al. 5 used advanced numerical calculation methods to deeply analyze the influence of bedrock pressure on roof movement in front of the work, and established a theoretical model of the mechanism of rock burst in mining roadways under hard roof conditions. Yan et al. 6clarified the underlying mechanisms of rock bursts in negative coal pillar roadways induced by dynamic load disturbances, and propose a methodology for identifying rock burst hazards.

In terms of the evolution of rock burst risk, the three-dimensional geological model built by Jiang7 et al. based on borehole data, combined with grid optimization and FDEM numerical simulation, achieved fine delineation of rock burst risk areas. Cui8 et al. employed a spatial scanning method to divide the area into grids, and proposed an intelligent early warning grading method to effectively identify impact risk areas based on microseismic data. Wang et al.9 analyzed the occurrence laws of rock burst from three aspects: the influence distance of the fault structure, the spatial structure under the influence of the fault, the spatial structure of the overburden, and the mutual disturbance of multiple hard roof. Zhang10 et al. employed the advancing distance of the working face as a prediction basis to reveal the potential spatial correlation of the occurrence of rock burst.

In terms of rock burst risk evaluation, Agrawal11 et al. analyzed the variability of coal mechanics, elasticity and reservoir properties caused by lithologic heterogeneity, resulting in the probability of rock burst. Rong12 et al. adopted the elastic energy density calculation method and used residual energy to grade the burst risk of coal seam, providing a calculation method for rapid evaluation of burst risk before mining and production. Tian13 et al. proposed a rock-burst disaster factor that takes into account mining stress and ground stress, which confirmed the possibility of rock-burst occurring in deep soft coal seams without dynamic load. Shi et al.14 used explanatory structural modeling and decision-making testing and evaluation laboratory methods to systematically identify and quantitatively analyze the disaster-causing factors of coal mine rock burst accidents. Based on a systematic analysis of hundreds of coal mines with rock burst, Tan et al.15 proposed four types of objective main control factors and three types of artificial main control factors that lead to rock burst. Zeng et al.16 determined the fuzzy evaluation index for the factors influencing the risk of rock burst and established a rock burst risk evaluation model. Zhu et al.17 used the fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method and the analytic hierarchy process to establish a rock burst risk evaluation method based on the geodynamic environment. Zhang 18 used factor analysis method to systematically identify the factors affecting rock burst, determined the main risk sources and their action mechanisms, and built a comprehensive evaluation system including two first-level indicators and seven second-level indicators.

Although substantial progress has been made in the risk prediction and evaluation of rock burst, several limitations remain in existing studies. First, many evaluation systems mainly focus on geological or mining factors, while the combined effects of natural conditions, mining layout, and prevention management are not sufficiently considered. Second, some weighting methods rely heavily on expert judgment, which may introduce subjectivity, whereas purely data-driven methods may ignore engineering experience and the physical meaning of qualitative indicators. Third, the hierarchical coupling relationship among multi-source factors under deep high-stress mining conditions has not been adequately quantified. To overcome these limitations, this study proposes a multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation model based on combined subjective–objective weighting. The analytic hierarchy process is used to reflect expert knowledge, while the entropy weight method is introduced to characterize data variability, thereby improving the rationality, objectivity, and applicability of rock burst risk evaluation.

Engineering background

Hongyang No. 3 Mine is located at the junction of Sujiatun District and Dengta City, Shenyang City, Liaoning Province. The coalfield area is about 30 km2 and the geological reserves reach 158 million tons. The main coal mining layers are coal 3, coal 7, coal 12–1 and coal 13, and the formation inclination angle is 3° ~ 6°. It is classified as a typical deep coal mine prone to rock burst. The 718 fully mechanized working face is located on the east side of the goafs of the 702, 704 and 706 working faces. The working face adopts a comprehensive mechanized one-time mining full height mining method. The strike length is 601 m, the dip length is 180 m, and the average burial depth is 900 m. The overall structure of the working face is a monoclinal structure, with moderate geological conditions, developed faults, and fault activity and dense distribution significantly weaken the integrity of the coal and rock mass, forming stress concentration areas and energy accumulation zones, which can easily induce rock burst. The main mining coal seam of No. 718 Working Face is a composite coal seam, consisting of coal 7-1, coal 7-2 and coal 7-3, with thicknesses of 0.30 m , 0.45 m and 1.28 m respectively. The coal seam is well stratified, has poor integrity, and shows weak burst tendency. The direct roof of the coal seam is black mudstone, with an average thickness of 11.2 m, and subsides with mining. The main roof is fine sandstone with hard lithology and an average thickness of 7.6 m. It responds significantly to roof collapse and mining disturbances, and the roof has medium rock burst tendency. During mining, problems such as broken surrounding rock of the roadway and sinking of the roof have occurred, as shown in Fig. 1. Therefore, it is urgent to carry out a comprehensive evaluation of the risk of rock burst in the 718 fully mechanized working face, determine the risk level, and formulate corresponding prevention and control measures.

Analysis of influencing factors inducing rock burst

Based on a comprehensive analysis of mining activities at the 718 working face, the main control factors for rock burst risk evaluation can be summarized into three categories: natural geological factors, mining technology factors and organizational management factors.

Natural geological factors

Among the natural geological factors, the most important factors are rock burst tendency, roof rock structure, geological structure and mining depth.

Rock burst tendency

The tendency of rock burst risk is mainly determined by the physical and mechanical characteristics of the coal and rock itself, among which key parameters such as compressive strength and tensile strength have a significant impact on the mechanical behavior of the coal and rock. In general, the higher the strength and elastic modulus of the coal body, the more elastic energy it can accumulate during the loading process, and the potential risk of rock burst will increase accordingly. At present, rock burst tendency is commonly evaluated using four key indicators: uniaxial compressive strength, elastic energy index, impact energy index, and dynamic failure duration. Based on these indicators, coal seams can be classified into four categories: strong, medium, weak, and no rock burst tendency.

The No. 7 coal seam of Hongyang No. 3 Mine has medium rock burst tendency, under certain stress conditions and mining disturbances, there is still the possibility of inducing rock burst.

Roof rock structure

The structural characteristics of the roof rock strata, especially the hard and thick sandstone roof developed above the coal seam, are one of the important controlling factors affecting the occurrence of rock burst. The mechanism is that hard and thick sandstone has strong elastic energy accumulation and burst release characteristics under mining disturbance. When the roof breaks, bends or slips and fails, the stored elastic energy is instantly released, producing strong vibration, thereby inducing roof coal seam type or roof type rock burst, as shown in Fig. 2. The occurrence of rock burst is mainly controlled by the structural combination and mechanical properties of the rock strata within 100 m above the coal seam. Among them, the high-strength and large-thickness sandstone layer plays a leading role in the accumulation and release of rock burst energy.

Geological structure

Geological structures such as faults and folds significantly influence the stress distribution and deformation characteristics of coal and rock masses. These structures not only alter the mechanical integrity of the rock mass but also lead to the accumulation of residual tectonic stress. Under the superposition of mining-induced stress, local stress concentration and energy accumulation are likely to occur, thereby increasing the risk of rock burst. Practice has shown that rock burst events often occur concentrated in structural stress abnormal areas such as syncline axes, near faults, sudden change zones of coal seam inclination and fold development areas, as shown in Fig. 3.

Relationship between occurrence of rock burst and geological structure.

The overall 718 working face is monoclinal structural characteristics, with stable formation dip but significantly affected locally by fault activity. Three main faults are developed in the area: Fy1, Fy2 and Fh13, with drops of 5 m, 5 m and 3 m respectively. In addition to the above-mentioned main faults, the working face is also controlled by multiple fault zones such as Fy6, Fy3, Fh2, and Fh4 , forming a superimposed area of structural stress, which greatly increases the risk of rock burst.

According to the standards of complexity of various geological factors, the complexity of geological structures is divided into: relatively simple, moderately complex, more complex, and complex based on the number and development degree of faults and folds, and the rock enclosure and the degree of damage to coal bodies. Mining depth

With the increase of mining depth, the self-weight stress of the overlying rock mass increases significantly, and the in-situ stress level borne by the coal and rock mass increases simultaneously, which leads to an enhanced accumulation of elastic energy within it. When the stress increases to the ultimate bearing capacity of the coal body, the coal body is more prone to instability and failure, thus inducing rock burst. At this time, the probability of occurrence of rock burst and its energy release intensity increase with the increase of burial depth, showing a clear positive correlation , as shown in Fig. 4. Statistical analyses indicate that rock burst risk exhibits a staged relationship with mining depth. When the depth is less than 350 m, rock burst rarely occurs; between 350 and 500 m, the probability gradually increases; and beyond 500 m, the risk rises sharply. At depths exceeding 800 m, the coal-rock system typically enters a high-energy instability state. The 718 working face, with an average burial depth of approximately 900 m, is therefore highly susceptible to rock burst.

Mining technical factors

The most important factors among mining technical factors are coal pillar width, goaf and roof management methods.

Coal pillar width

Coal pillar width directly affects the distribution and concentration of stress around the working face. If the coal pillar is too narrow, its bearing capacity is insufficient, making it prone to shear failure or crush instability under high stress conditions. Conversely, excessively wide coal pillars hinder stress release and may result in severe stress concentration zones. In particular, under conditions such as isolated or peninsula coal pillars, multi-directional stress superposition becomes significant. The interaction between coal pillars and surrounding goaf boundaries further intensifies stress concentration, increasing the likelihood of sudden instability in coal and rock masses 19, as shown in Fig. 5.

Schematic diagram of coal pillar stress distribution form.

Combined with the basic roof of the 718 working face is 7.6 m fine sandstone, and based on the reserved deformation size of the surrounding rock of the tunnel and the control requirements for bolt failure, the width of the coal pillar reserved for the 718 working face is 8 m. On-site stress monitoring shows that the coal pillar is in a superimposed state of two-way bearing pressures, and the peak vertical stress reaches 22.3 MPa. The 718 working face is adjacent to the goaf of 702, 704, 706, and 708. Due to the fact that the protective coal pillars of each stop-line do not form a good alignment relationship, the stress distribution in the area is uneven and the formation of multiple high stress concentration zones significantly increase the rock burst risk.

Goaf

The spatial relationship between a working face and adjacent goafs can generally be classified into four types: solid coal working face, one side of goaf, two sides of goaf, and an island-type working face with goaf on three or more sides. For a solid-coal working face, the stress field is mainly controlled by the original rock stress, and the stress distribution is relatively uniform. When goafs exist on one or more sides, the pressure-relief effect of the goaf and the residual abutment pressure interact, resulting in a pronounced stress gradient around the working face.

Under conditions with goafs on both sides or three or more sides, the coal mass is subjected to superimposed multi-directional abutment pressures, which readily leads to the formation of high-stress concentration zones. During the advancement of the working face, roof collapse and goaf compaction cause stress redistribution, transferring and superimposing concentrated stress toward the coal wall and the front of the working face. Meanwhile, mining-induced dynamic stress interacts with the residual static stress around the goaf, accelerating local stress accumulation. Once the stress exceeds the ultimate strength of the coal-rock mass, rock burst may be induced. This effect is particularly significant in island-type working faces, where the surrounding goafs limit the available pressure-relief space and hinder effective stress release, thereby markedly increasing rock burst risk, as shown in Fig. 620. Therefore, accurately characterizing the distribution of goafs and its influence on the stress field is essential for rock burst prediction and prevention.

At present, the total collapse method is generally used to manage the roof in Hongyang No. 3 Mine. This method may easily cause the risk of rock burst when the roof lithology is hard and the thickness is large. Organizational management factors

Organizational and management factors mainly include prevention and control investment, prevention and control safety measures and prevention and control emergency plans. The grading standards are formulated with reference to the safety management regulations on rock burst prevention and control in the " Detailed Rules for the Prevention and Control of Rock Burst in Coal Mines", the " Safety Regulations in Coal Mine", and the relevant emergency management requirements of the " Measures for the Administration of Contingency Plans for Work Safety Accidents".

Prevention and control investment

Scientific and reasonable roadway support is an important engineering means to reduce the risk of rock burst. By optimizing the supporting parameters and structural forms, the overall strength and residual bearing capacity of the surrounding rock can be effectively improved, so that the coal and rock can still have certain bearing performance after failure, thereby delaying the energy release process, weakening the concentrated and instantaneous release of rock burst loads, and significantly reducing rock burst energy and strength. Prevention and control safety measures

Rock burst occurrence is primarily controlled by the imbalance between stress accumulation and the bearing capacity of coal and rock masses. Preventive safety measures are intended to reduce local stress concentration, promote stress transfer, and enhance the energy absorption capacity of the surrounding rock system. The effectiveness of these measures depends largely on whether they are implemented in accordance with the designed parameters and site-specific risk conditions. Meanwhile, zoning-based risk management can identify areas with different hazard levels, enabling differentiated control strategies and avoiding the unreasonable allocation of prevention resources. Personnel access restrictions further reduce exposure in high-risk zones and help minimize potential accident losses. Therefore, systematic, standardized, and traceable rock burst prevention measures can effectively reduce both the probability and intensity of rock burst disasters. Prevention and control emergency plan

Rock burst is sudden and instantaneous destructive, and can easily induce secondary disasters such as roof falls and gas gushes. No matter how complete monitoring and pressure relief measures are, the risk cannot be reduced to zero. The core value of emergency plans lies in: improving personnel response capabilities through drills, disaster avoidance routes and emergency broadcast systems beforehand; minimizing casualties through clear command structures and self-rescue and mutual rescue processes during the process; and preventing the expansion of the disaster through joint rescue afterwards. Mines lacking effective plans will inevitably fall into chaos once a rock burst occurs, and the consequences of the accident will be multiplied. Therefore, the emergency plan is the last line of defense to control losses and needs to be used as one of the rock burst risk evaluation indicators. Rock burst risk evaluation model

Rock burst is a complex dynamic disaster resulting from the nonlinear interaction of multiple influencing factors. Traditional evaluation approaches based on single factor analysis or purely subjective or objective weighting methods are often insufficient to capture the coupling effects and uncertainties inherent in rock burst occurrence. To address these limitations, this study proposes a multi-level fuzzy comprehensive evaluation model based on combined subjective and objective weighting. The analytic hierarchy process is employed to determine subjective weights reflecting expert knowledge, while the entropy weight method is used to derive objective weights based on data variability. By integrating these two approaches, the proposed model improves both the reliability and objectivity of rock burst risk evaluation.

Improved fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method

The traditional fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method primarily relies on the analytic hierarchy process to determine the weight of indicators, relies too much on expert experience, lacks unified measurement standards, is highly subjective and arbitrary, and is influenced by differences in the characteristics of various methods, which easily leads to imbalance of indicator characteristics and the results obtained are too one-sided. The objective weighting method is based on data theory and can avoid human interference. Therefore, this study will use the subjective and objective comprehensive weighting method to improve the fuzzy comprehensive evaluation method. By integrating objective combination weights with traditional fuzzy comprehensive evaluation, the scientific nature of evaluation results is improved.

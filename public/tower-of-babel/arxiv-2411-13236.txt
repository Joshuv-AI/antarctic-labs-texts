Quantum Micromagnetic Theory of Magnons in Finite Nanostructures
Claudio Serpico, Salvatore Perna, Massimiliano d’Aquino

Abstract
This paper presents a quantum field theoretical formalism for studying magnons in finite nanostructures with arbitrary shapes and spatially nonuniform ground states. It extends the classical micromagnetic formalism by introducing a micromagnetic Hamiltonian quantum operator, which incorporates exchange, Dzyaloshinsky-Moriya, anisotropy, magnetostatic, and Zeeman energies. The nonuniformity of the ground state is handled by pointwise aligning the quantization axis of the magnetization field operator with the classical ground state. The Hamiltonian is expanded in the large spin-number limit and truncated to retain only terms quadratic in the components of the magnetization operator transverse to the quantization axis. This quadratic Hamiltonian is used to derive the linear quantum Landau-Lifshitz equation. By diagonalizing this equation under appropriate boundary and normalization conditions, a discrete set of magnon creation and annihilation operators is obtained, enabling a complete description of the magnon spectrum. Finally, the theory is applied to study the effects of temperature and shape on low-temperature thermal equilibrium fluctuations of magnons in thin ferromagnetic nanodisks.

The effectiveness of the proposed theory is demonstrated in the final part of the paper, where numerical calculations of thermal equilibrium averages associated with the Cartesian components of magnetization in a thin disk are presented.
For an in-plane magnetized film, the computations reveal that at low temperatures, edge magnons produce thermal fluctuations near the material boundaries that are significantly larger than those predicted by the plane-wave analysis for an infinitely in-plane magnetized thin film. In the case of a thin disk with a Skyrmion ground state, this approach enables the analysis of the spatially nonuniform distribution of thermal fluctuations, showing that these fluctuations are particularly enhanced in regions with the steepest gradients in the ground-state magnetization.
These calculations, which can be applied to bodies of arbitrary shapes and noncollinear ground states, demonstrate the formalism’s ability to study thermal equilibrium magnon populations. This capability is crucial for engineering magnetic nanostructures for low-temperature quantum magnonics applications in quantum computing and information processing

contains power of order
k

of
{\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
. The first order term vanishes as a consequence
of the fact that
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
is a classical micromagnetic equilibrium (see Appendix
A.1
).
When considering the limit of large
S
, the
normalized quantities
H_{k}[{\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}}_{\bot}]/(\mu_{0}M_{0}^{2}/2)
,
are infinitesimal of order
k
in
{\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}}_{\bot}/M_{0}
(see Appendix
A.1
)
and this implies that the dominant nonconstant and nonzero term in the expansion (
28
)
is the quadratic term:

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}=H_{2}[{\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}}]=\int_{\Omega}dV\left\{\frac{A_{kh}^{uv}}{M_{0}^{2}}\,\frac{\partial\hat{M}_{\bot}^{u}}{\partial x_{k}}\frac{\partial\hat{M}_{\bot}^{v}}{\partial x_{h}}+\frac{1}{2}{\frac{D_{k}^{uv}}{M_{0}^{2}}}\left(\hat{M}_{\bot}^{u}\frac{\partial\hat{M}_{\bot}^{v}}{\partial x_{k}}-\frac{\partial\hat{M}_{\bot}^{u}}{\partial x_{k}}\hat{M}_{\bot}^{v}\right)+\hat{M}^{u}_{\bot}\frac{K^{uv}}{M_{0}^{2}}\hat{M}^{v}_{\bot}+\right.

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\left.-\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}\cdot{\mathbf{H}_{\text{m}}}[\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}]+\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\lambda_{0}(\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot})^{2}\right\}\,,

(29)

where
\lambda_{0}=\lambda_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
is the scalar field defined in eq.(
19
).

The operator (
29
) describes the quantum Hamiltonian associated
to low energy quantum magnetization dynamics around the classical ground state, in the limit of large
S
.
When the operator (
29
) is computed on the classical field
{\mathbf{M}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
, pointwise orthogonal to

{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
, it gives the Hessian functional
[
34
]
of the classical Hamiltonian
H[{\mathbf{M}}]
at the ground state
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
. For
this reason
H_{2}[{\mathbf{M}}_{\bot}]
is a positive definite functional.

V
Linear Quantum Magnetization Dynamics

In this section, we derive the linearized equation governing the evolution
of the quantum field
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}
in the Heisenberg picture.
Since
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}
is function of the operators

\hat{M}^{01}
,
\hat{M}^{02}
(see eq.(
26
)), the evolution of
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}

is related to the evolution of these operators that is governed by the equations

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{M}^{0a}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\left[\hat{M}^{0a}({\mathbf{x}},t)\,,\,\hat{H}\right]\,,

(30)

for
a=1,2
. In order to keep terms that are linear in
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}
at the right-hand-side of eq.(
30
),
we assume the following approximated commutation relations
[
6
,
36
]

\displaystyle[\hat{M}^{01}({\mathbf{x}}),\hat{M}^{02}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})]\approx-i({\gamma\hbar})M_{0}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\delta({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\,,

(31)

\displaystyle\hat{M}^{03}({\mathbf{x}},t)\approx M_{0}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\,.

(32)

The other approximation to arrive to the linearized equation of motion is to keep in eq.(
30
)
only the quadratic part of the Hamiltonian given by eq.(
29
).
This leads to the following linear equation of motion:

\displaystyle i\hbar\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{M}_{\bot}^{s}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\left[\hat{M}_{\bot}^{s}({\mathbf{x}},t)\,,\,\hat{H}_{2}\right]\,,

(33)

with
s=1,2,3
, where the commutators at the right-hand-side of eq.(
33
)
are computed by taking into account the approximate commutation
relations (
31
)-(
32
).
In this respect, from eqs.(
31
)-(
32
) follows that

\displaystyle[\hat{M}_{\bot}^{s}({\mathbf{x}}),\hat{M}_{\bot}^{u}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})]\approx i({\gamma\hbar})M_{0}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\Lambda_{0}^{su}({\mathbf{x}})\delta({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\,,

(34)

(see Appendix
A.2
) where
\Lambda_{0}^{su}({\mathbf{x}})
, with
u,s=1,2,3
, are the component of the second order
tensor
\Lambda_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
with the following property

\displaystyle\Lambda_{0}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}})={\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})\times{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}})\,,

(35)

where
{\mathbf{v}}({\mathbf{x}})
is a generic vector field.

By using the expression (
29
)
and the commutation relations (
34
)
in eq.(
33
), after some algebra
(see Appendix
A
), it is possible to
derive the explicit form of the equation of motion

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}},t)=\gamma\mu_{0}M_{0}{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})\times{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}]\,,

(36)

where

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}]=\mathcal{P}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot{\bm{D}}[\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}]+\lambda_{0}\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}\,,

(37)

with the operator
{\bm{D}}[\,\cdot\,]
given in eq.(
80
), and
where
\mathcal{P}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
is the second order tensor of components

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{uv}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})=e^{u}_{01}({\mathbf{x}})e^{v}_{01}({\mathbf{x}})+e^{u}_{02}({\mathbf{x}})e^{v}_{02}({\mathbf{x}})\,,

(38)

with
u,v=1,2,3
, that pointwise projects vector fields in the plane orthogonal to
{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Equation (
36
) is the quantum version
of the Landau-Lifshitz equation linearized around the ground state
{\mathbf{M}}_{0}
.

In the derivation of eq.(
36
), we use the following boundary
conditions for
\hat{M}^{u}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
:

\displaystyle n_{k}\left({2A_{kh}^{uv}}\frac{\partial\,\,}{\partial x_{h}}+n_{k}{D_{k}^{uv}}\right)\hat{M}_{\bot}^{u}({\mathbf{x}})=0\qquad\text{on}\,\,\,\partial\Omega\,,

(39)

with
v=1,2,3
.
The important consequence of these boundary conditions is the following
reciprocity property (self-adjointness) of the operator
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\!\!dV\left\{\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot\,{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\hat{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\bot}]\right\}=

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\int_{\Omega}\!\!dV\left\{{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\bot}]\cdot\hat{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})]\right\}\,,

(40)

where
\hat{{\mathbf{F}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
and
\hat{{\mathbf{G}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
are fields, possibly
with noncommuting cartesian components, orthogonal to
{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
and
satisfying boundary conditions (
39
). The identity (
40
) is
obtained by using boundary conditions (
39
), integration by parts, the reciprocity (self-adjointness)
of the magnetostatic field operator and symmetry of the tensor
K^{uv}
(see Sec.
A
).
This identity is essential in the derivation of the torque-like form of eq.(
36
)
(see Appendix
A
).

The boundary conditions (
39
) are an extension of those used in classical micromagnetic theory
to the quantum formulation. These conditions, and the reciprocity theorem (
40
) that follows from them,
are essential for ensuring the formal consistency of the micromagnetic
quantum field description presented here with the quantum theory based on the
quantization of classical harmonic oscillators associated with the amplitude of classical normal modes
[
36
]
,
[
37
]
.

By using the self-adjoint nature of the operator
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
expressed
in eq.(
40
), the quadratic Hamiltonian (
10
)
can be written in the following form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}=\int_{\Omega}dV\left\{\frac{\mu_{0}}{2}\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}]\right\}\,.

(41)

From this last expression, together with the positive definite nature of the quadratic Hamiltonian,
we conclude that the operator
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
is positive definite.

In conclusion of this section, we underline that we have
formulated the linearized Landau-Lifshitz (
36
) and
the associated Hamiltonian operator (
41
),
in terms of the field
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
and the operator
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
, imposing the boundary conditions
(
39
) on the cartesian components
\hat{M}^{u}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
.
By taking into account
eq.(
26
), the theory can be alternatively formulated
in terms of the two operators
\hat{M}^{01}({\mathbf{x}}),\hat{M}^{02}({\mathbf{x}})
.
Although this latter formulation would be mathematically equivalent to the one presented,
the boundary conditions for the fields
\hat{M}^{01}({\mathbf{x}}),\hat{M}^{02}({\mathbf{x}})
,
that are given by

\displaystyle\left(n_{k}{2A_{kh}^{uv}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{h}}+n_{k}{D_{k}^{uv}}\right)\cdot

\displaystyle\quad\cdot\left(e_{01}^{u}({\mathbf{x}})\hat{M}^{01}({\mathbf{x}})+e_{02}^{u}({\mathbf{x}})\hat{M}^{02}({\mathbf{x}})\right)=0\,\,\,\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\,,

(42)

are considerably more convoluted with respect to the the boundary conditions (
39
).
The central role that boundary conditions play in the micromagnetics of nanostructures suggests
that formulation based on the Cartesian components of
\hat{M}^{u}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
is, in this respect,
preferable.

VI
Diagonalization of Quantum Magnetization Dynamics

In order to introduce magnons, as quasi-particles corresponding
to excitations of magnetization quantum field, we have to express

\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}
in terms of the appropriate creation-annihilation operator.
To this aim, we consider a canonical transformation that connects the quantum field

\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}
with the set of annihilation-creation operators

(\hat{c}_{m},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m})
, with
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
(nonzero positive integers),
that has the form, in the Heisenberg picture, of the following generalized Fourier expansion:

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}},t)=

\displaystyle\qquad M_{0}\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\left\{\hat{c}_{m}(t)\bm{\varphi}_{m}({\mathbf{x}})+\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}(t)\bm{\varphi}_{m}^{*}({\mathbf{x}})\right\}\,,

(43)

where
\bm{\varphi}_{m}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})=0
, and where

(\hat{c}_{m}(t)
,
\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}(t))
are the annihilation-creation operators
in the Heisenberg picture. The operators
(\hat{c}_{m},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m})
in the
Schroedinger picture corrispond to the values of
\hat{c}_{m}(t)
,
\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}(t)

for
t=0
.

We notice that, with the above normalizations, both the operators
\hat{c}_{m}
,
\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}
and
the functions
\bm{\varphi}_{m}({\mathbf{x}})
are dimensionless quantities.
In order to simplify algebraic manipulations, we can write
(
43
) as a single summation as follows

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}},t)=M_{0}\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\hat{c}_{p}(t)\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})\,,

(44)

where
\mathbb{Z}^{*}
is the set of nonzero integers, and we assume that

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{-p}=\hat{c}_{p}^{\dagger}\,,\quad\bm{\varphi}_{-p}({\mathbf{x}})=\bm{\varphi}^{*}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})\,.

(45)

The transformation (
44
) must be such to satisfy the two conditions
discussed in the sequel.

The first condition is that, under (
44
), the Hamiltonian operator
(
41
) is reduced to the diagonal form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}\frac{\hbar\omega_{m}}{2}\left(\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{m}+\hat{c}_{m}\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}\right)=\sum_{p\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}\frac{\hbar\omega_{p}}{2}{\hat{c}_{p}}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{p}\,,

(46)

corresponding to a denumerable set of noninteracting quantum harmonic oscillators.
In eq.(
46
),
\omega_{p}=\omega_{-p}
, for
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, are real positive quantities that have the physical
meaning of angular frequencies and have to be determined together with the
\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})
.
By substituting eq.(
44
) into the expression (
29
), and imposing
the equality with the diagonalized Hamiltonian (
46
),
we obtain that the basis vector fields
\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})
have to satisfy the following
orthogonality conditions

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}dV\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})^{*}\cdot{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\bm{\varphi}_{q}]\right\}=\frac{\hbar\omega_{p}}{\mu_{0}M_{0}^{2}}\delta_{pq}\,,

(47)

where
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, and the second definition in eq.(
45
) is taken into account. Given the property of
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
of being real positive definite self-adjoint operator,
the integrals in eq.(
47
) must be positive real quantities confirming the fact that all the
\omega_{p}
are positive.

The second condition on the transformation (
44
) is that
it must be such to produce the following canonical commutation relations:

\displaystyle[\hat{c}_{p},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{q}]=\tilde{\delta}_{pq}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\,,\quad

(48)

for
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, where

\tilde{\delta}_{pq}=\delta_{pq}\text{sgn}(p)

and
\text{sgn}(p)
is the signum of
p
, provided that
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}})
satisfies the commutation relations (
34
).
By substituting eq.(
44
) into
eq.(
34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}[\hat{c}_{q},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{p}]\varphi_{q}^{s}({\mathbf{x}})\left(\varphi_{p}^{u}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right)^{*}=

\displaystyle\qquad\quad=i\frac{({\gamma\hbar})}{M_{0}}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\Lambda_{0}^{su}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\delta({\mathbf{x}}-{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\,.

(49)

Then, we multiply both sides of the last equation by the quantity

\displaystyle\varphi_{l}^{r}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\Lambda_{0}^{ur}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})

(50)

with
l\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}
, and integrate with respect to
{\mathbf{x}}^{\prime}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\sum_{p,q\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}}[\hat{c}_{q},\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{p}]\varphi_{q}^{s}({\mathbf{x}})\int_{\Omega}dV^{\prime}\left\{\varphi_{l}^{r}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\Lambda_{0}^{ur}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\left(\varphi_{p}^{u}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right)^{*}\right\}

\displaystyle\quad=-i\frac{({\gamma\hbar})}{M_{0}}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\delta^{sr}\varphi_{l}^{r}({\mathbf{x}})=-i\frac{({\gamma\hbar})}{M_{0}}\hat{\mathbbm{1}}\varphi_{l}^{s}({\mathbf{x}})\,.

(51)

Now, if we impose the condition

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}dV^{\prime}\left\{\varphi_{l}^{r}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\Lambda_{0}^{ur}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\left(\varphi_{p}^{u}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right)^{*}\right\}=-i\frac{({\gamma\hbar})}{M_{0}}\tilde{\delta}_{lp}\,,

(52)

in eq.(
51
), we obtain the relations (
48
).
The normalization condition (
52
) can be written in vector form as

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}dV^{\prime}\left\{\bm{\varphi}_{p}^{*}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\cdot{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})\times\bm{\varphi}_{l}({\mathbf{x}}^{\prime})\right\}=-i\frac{({\gamma\hbar})}{M_{0}}\tilde{\delta}_{lp}\,.

(53)

This last equation is the generalization of the normalization conditions reported in the paper of Walker
[
23
]
for the
case of a sample subject to magnetostatic interactions and with a spatially uniform ground state (see eq.(B3) in Ref.
[
23
]
).
These normalization conditions were extended, for spatially uniform ground states, to the case where isotropic exchange interactions are also present in the
paper of Mills
[
36
]
. The derivation above extend these normalization conditions to the much more general case of noncollinear ground state.
In addition, in our formalism, we also include anisotropic and DM exchange interactions.

To complete the discussion we have to illustrate the procedure to determine the set of functions

\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})
and the corresponding set of positive scalar quantities
\omega_{p}
for

p\in\mathbbm{Z}^{*}
. In this respect, by combining eqs. (
36
), (
43
) and (
53
), we notice that the desired quantities can be obtained
by solving the following generalized eigenvalue problem

\displaystyle{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\bm{\varphi}_{p}]({\mathbf{x}})=\nu_{p}i\Lambda_{0}({\mathbf{x}})\cdot\bm{\varphi}_{p}({\mathbf{x}})\quad\text{in}\,\,\Omega

(54)

with boundary conditions

\displaystyle n_{k}\left({2A_{kh}^{uv}}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{h}}+{D_{k}^{uv}}\right)\varphi_{p}^{u}({\mathbf{x}})=0\quad\text{on}\,\,\partial\Omega\,,

(55)

that arises in the study of micromagnetic classical normal modes
[
34
]

in finite structures. Indeed, the problem (
54
), can be readily obtained
searching for time-harmonic solutions of the type

\bm{\varphi}({\mathbf{x}})e^{-i\omega t}
for the classical counterpart of the eq.(
36
). By using the fact that, on vector fields locally orthogonal to
{\mathbf{m}}_{0}({\mathbf{x}})

and satisfying boundary conditions (
55
),

{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
is real positive definite and self-adjoint operator while

i\Lambda_{0}({\mathbf{x}})
is an Hermitian operator, one can prove that eigenvalues are
real and such that
[
34
]

\displaystyle\nu_{m}>0\,,\,\,m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\,,\qquad\nu_{-p}=-\nu_{p}\,,\,\,p\in\mathbb{Z}^{*}\,.

(56)

In addition, eigenfunctions
\bm{\varphi}_{k}({\mathbf{x}})
can be normalized in such a way to satisfy both
conditions (
47
),(
53
).
The sequence
\nu_{p}
determines the
angular frequencies
\omega_{p}
of harmonic oscillators contained in the diagonalized Hamiltonian
through the equation

\displaystyle\omega_{m}=(\gamma\mu_{0}M_{0})\nu_{m}\,,\quad m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}\,,

(57)

These are the angular frequencies of time-harmonic evolution:

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{m}(t)=\hat{c}_{m}(0)e^{-i\omega_{m}t}\,,\,\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m}(t)=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{m}(0)e^{i\omega_{m}t}\,,

(58)

of annihilation-creation operators.
Substitution of eq.(
58
) into eq.(
43
),
leads to the time evolution
of
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}({\mathbf{x}},t)
.
The general proofs of the properties of eigenvalues and eigenfunctions stated
previously are contained in Ref.
[
34
]
.
The extension of these proofs to the case treated here that includes DM interaction (i.e.
D^{uv}_{k}\neq 0
) and anisotropic exchange is possible owing to
the self-adjoint and the positive definite nature of
the operator
{\bm{D}}_{0\bot}[\,\cdot\,]
.
These formal properties
provide a general framework for approaching the diagonalization problem
in the most general case.

After presenting the general method based on normal mode analysis, it is important
to briefly connect this approach to the traditional plane-wave method. For a
body with large dimensions, the system can be approximated as having infinite
spatial extent. If, furthermore, the magnetic material is spatially homogeneous,
the normal mode analysis reduces to the traditional plane-wave approach
[
9
]

in which the expansion (
43
)
becomes

\displaystyle\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}_{\bot}({\mathbf{x}},t)=

\displaystyle\quad\,M_{0}\sum_{{\mathbf{k}}}\left\{\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)\bm{\varphi}({\mathbf{k}})e^{i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{x}}}+\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}}^{\dagger}(t)\bm{\varphi}^{*}({\mathbf{k}})e^{-i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{x}}}\right\}\,,

(59)

where the wave vector
{\mathbf{k}}
has here also to role of indexing modes, and the vectors
\bm{\varphi}({\mathbf{k}})
have
to satisfy a Walker’s type normalization conditon analogous to (
53
).
The annihilation-creation operators evolve according to the equation

\displaystyle\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\hat{c}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)e^{-i\omega({\mathbf{k}})t}\,,\,\,\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}(t)=\hat{c}^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}(0)e^{i\omega({\mathbf{k}})t}\,,

(60)

where the dependence of the angular frequency with respect to
{\mathbf{k}}

is given by the appropriate dispersion relation
\omega=\omega({\mathbf{k}})
.
A comparison of the expansions (
43
),(
59
)
shows that the role of the normal mode profile vector
\bm{\varphi}_{m}({\mathbf{x}})
is played
in plane-wave formalims by the vector field
\bm{\varphi}({\mathbf{k}})e^{i{\mathbf{k}}\cdot{\mathbf{x}}}
.
In this vector field, the spatial dependence is fully captured
by the phase factor, while the vector
\bm{\varphi}({\mathbf{k}})
describes the position-independent
polarization of the
{\mathbf{k}}
-th spin wave. In contrast, for normal mode vectors,
the polarization and the spatial dependence of the mode, also determined by the possibly noncollinear ground state
\bm{M}_{0}
, are both encoded in the vector
\bm{\varphi}_{m}({\mathbf{x}})
.

To elucidate such circumstance, by using the large-scale methods described in Ref.
[
34
]
and implemented in the MaGICo micromagnetic code
[
53
]
, we have computed a set of normal modes
\bm{\varphi}_{m}(\bm{x})
for thin disks with similar dimensions starting from different ground states, as reported in Fig.

. Specifically, panel (a) refers to a disk with diameter
L=100
nm and thickness
d=5
nm magnetized in-plane under the action of the external field, panels (b) and (c) refer to a disk with
L=100
nm and
d=0.4
nm having a Bloch Skyrmion and quasi-uniform out-of-plane magnetization ground states, respectively.
For instance, one can clearly see in Fig.

Moreover, in the same figure panel we observe that, for the lowest modes, the strongest oscillation amplitude is apparently localized in the region with the steepest spatial gradient of
\bm{M}_{0}
.

where the normal modes profiles are used for the evaluation of thermal equilibrium averages.

VII
Computations of thermal equilibrium averages in thin disks

In this section, we want to present sample calculations that demonstrate
the capability of the proposed formalism. Our focus will be on the low-temperature
thermal equilibrium averages of observables associated with the Cartesian components
of
\hat{{\mathbf{M}}}({\mathbf{x}})
. Such thermal equilibrium computations are particularly relevant for initializing or engineering magnon populations in magnetic nanostructures,
and this is important for low-temperature applications of quantum magnonics
in quantum computing and information processing
[
17
]
,
[
52
]
.

In the calculations, we consider the diagonalized
magnon Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{2}^{\prime}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}{\hbar\omega_{m}}\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{m}=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}}{\hbar\omega_{m}}\hat{n}_{m}

(61)

where

\hat{n}_{m}=\hat{c}_{m}^{\dagger}\hat{c}_{m}\,,

is the number operator of magnons with frequency
\omega_{m}
.
The Hamiltonian (
61
) is obtained from (
46
) after removing
the zero-point energy. The zero-point contribution in field theory of magnons is actually infinite
as the spectrum is unbounded from above, similarly to what happens in quantum electrodynamics
[
54
]
.
In addition, we are here only concerned with the temperature-dependent contribution to the quantities under investigation,
which does not include the zero-point energy.

For a generic observable quantity
\hat{F}
, we define the equilibrium thermal average as

\displaystyle\langle\hat{F}\rangle_{T}=\text{tr}(\hat{\rho}_{\text{eq}}\hat{F})\,,

(62)

where
\hat{\rho}_{\text{eq}}
is the equilibrium density operator given
by

\displaystyle\hat{\rho}_{\text{eq}}=\frac{1}{Z}\exp(-\beta\hat{H}_{2}^{\prime})

(63)

where
\beta=1/(k_{B}T)
,
k_{B}=1.380649\cdot 10^{-23}
J/K is the Boltzmann constant,
T
the absolute temperature and
Z=\mathrm{tr}(\exp(-\beta\hat{H}_{2}^{\prime}))
is the partition function.

VIII
Conclusions

This work introduces a quantum-field-based micromagnetic approach for deriving the magnon spectrum
in finite nanostructures. The derivation applies to a general quantum micromagnetic Hamiltonian
that includes all relevant interactions, such as isotropic and anisotropic exchange,
Dzyaloshinsky-Moriya (DM) interaction, dipolar interactions, anisotropy, and Zeeman effects.
This formalism is well-suited for addressing spatially nonuniform ground states and accurately
accounts for boundary effects. The computations presented in the paper demonstrate that edge effects induce
quantitative changes in magnetization dynamics that cannot be captured by plane wave formalism alone.

The proposed approach extends the classical micromagnetic framework into the quantum regime.
This advancement is expected to be of interest to researchers using classical micromagnetics to model
systems and devices in fields like magnonics, spintronics, and related areas, particularly those
interested in incorporating quantum effects into their models. This paper represents an initial step
in this direction by addressing the case of non-interacting magnons.

The framework established here can be extended in multiple directions. Key examples include the quantum
treatment of resonance under both transverse and longitudinal pumping, a perturbative analysis
of magnon scattering with higher-order terms in the Hamiltonian expansion, and the study of relaxation
processes due to interactions with a thermal reservoir. These topics will be explored in future studies,
building on the theory of free magnon dynamics established here.

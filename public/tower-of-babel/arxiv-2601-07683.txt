Quantum information and statistical complexity of hydrogen-like ions in Dunkl-Schrödinger system
Akash Halder, Amlan K. Roy, Debraj Nath

Abstract
In this work, we present analytical solutions of Schrödinger equation for Coulomb potential in presence of a Dunkl reflection operator. Expressions are offered for eigenvalues, eigenfunctions and radial densities for H-isoelectronic series ( Z = 1 − 3 Z=1-3 ). The degeneracy in energy in absence and presence of the reflection has been discussed. The standard deviation, Shannon entropy, Rényi entropy in position space have been derived for arbitrary quantum states. Then several important complexity measures like López-Ruiz-Mancini-Calbet (LMC), Shape-Rényi complexity (SRC), Generalized Rényi complexity (GRC), Rényi complexity ratio (RCR) are considered in the analytical framework. Representative results are given for three one-electron atomic ions in tabular and graphical format. Changes in these measures with respect to parity and Dunkl parameter have been given in detail. Most of these results are offered here for the first time.

Introduction

The Wigner-Dunkl formulation of quantum mechanics holds relevance in deformation study through quantum algebra and groups
[
1
,
2
]
. The quantum observables can be generated from equations of motion by defining a Dunkl operator as a combination of differential-difference and reflection operator as:
\displaystyle D_{x}f(x)=\frac{d}{dx}f(x)+\frac{\mu}{x}(1-\widehat{R})f(x)
, where
\mu

\widehat{p}_{x}=\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}D_{x}=\displaystyle\frac{\hbar}{i}\left[\frac{d}{dx}+\frac{\mu}{x}(1-\widehat{R})\right]

[\widehat{x},\widehat{p}_{x}]=i\hbar(1+2\mu\widehat{R})
. When parameter
\mu
vanishes, this novel algebra leads to Boson algebra. It is connected to two-particle Calogero model if the involved parameter acts as Calogero coupling constant, where relations like
\widehat{R}\widehat{x}=-\widehat{x}\widehat{R},\ \widehat{R}\widehat{p}=-\widehat{p}\widehat{R},\ \widehat{R}\widehat{R}=\textbf{I}
hold, where
I
refers to the identity operator. In the integrable systems and discrete symmetry quantum models, they play important role because of the interplay of derivative and reflection operator. It can offer newer algebraic structure, spectral properties and introduction of conserved quantities in a quantum system. Moreover, the wave function can offer both continuous and discrete symmetry due to a combination of
\mu
parameter and
\widehat{R}
operator. A few variants, such as generalized Dunkl operator, Jacobi Dunkl operator have been discussed in literature. Besides, they also arise in the context of reflection symmetries in multivariable polynomial, integral transform associated with root systems
[
3

4
]
, complete set of quantum integrals
[

]
, harmonic analysis, reflection symmetries in finite reflection group (also termed as finite Coexter group), quantum Calogero-Moser-Sutherland model and its generalization
[

, anyons in (2+1) and (1+1) dimensions
[

, para-fields and para-statistics

Density function and energy degeneracy of H-like ions in Dunkl-Schrödinger system

The probability density of a state
\psi_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}

3.2
Shannon entropy

Now, we discuss Shannon entropy in
\xi\left(\mathbb{R}^{*}\times[0,\pi]\times[0,2\pi],d\chi\right)
space, in a given state
\psi_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}
, which is given by,

\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}=-\displaystyle\int\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(\mathbf{r})\ln[\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(\mathbf{r})]d\chi.\end{array}

(9)

It can be written as
\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}=\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}+\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}+\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}
, where
\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}
,
\mathcal{S}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}
and
\mathcal{S}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}
are Shannon entropies of marginal density functions (functions of
r,\theta,\phi
). The quasi-analytical form of radial entropy,
\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}=-\int_{0}^{\infty}\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}\ln\left[\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}\right]d\chi_{r}
is derived as:

\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}=\frac{N_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{2}}{\gamma}[\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n_{r}}\frac{2\widetilde{B}_{2+i,2}f(i)}{(2+i)!}-{S}^{(1)}]-\ln[N_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{2}\gamma^{2a}],

(10)

{S}^{(1)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-x}x^{2L+3}[L_{n_{r}}^{(2L+1)}(x)]^{2}\ln[L_{n_{r}}^{(2L+1)}(x)]^{2}dx
. The

analytical value of
{S}^{(1)}
is obtained in ref.
[
25
]
by factorization method but quasi-analytical expressions are reported in ref.
[
28
]
and numerical results provided in ref.
[
29
]
. Thus the value of
\mathcal{S}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}
can be obtained from Eq. (
10
). The term
f(i)
is given as,
f(i)=\Gamma(2L+3+i)\left[(2a-2L-2)\Psi_{d}(2L+3+i)\right.\left.+\Gamma(2L+3+i)\right]
, where
\Psi_{d}(x)
refers to the digamma function. Similarly, the Shannon entropy
\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,s_{3})}_{\ell,m}
of angular density function
\rho_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}(\theta)
is found as follows:
\mathcal{S}^{(\theta,s_{3})}_{\ell,m}=-\int_{0}^{\pi}\rho_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}\ln[\rho_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}]d\chi_{\theta}
. Lastly, the Shannon entropy
\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}_{m}
of density function
\rho_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}
can be expressed as:
\mathcal{S}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}_{m}=-\int_{0}^{2\pi}\rho_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}\ln[\rho_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}(\phi)]d\chi_{\phi}

3.3
Rényi entropy

The entropic moment of density function
\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}
is expressed as:
\mathcal{I}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\alpha)}=\int[\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}(\mathbf{r})]^{\alpha}d\chi=\mathcal{I}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,\alpha)}\mathcal{I}_{m}^{(\phi,\alpha)}
,
\alpha>0
, where
\mathcal{I}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\left[\rho_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)}\right]^{\alpha}d\chi_{r}
,
\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[\rho_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3})}\right]^{\alpha}d\chi_{\theta}
and
\mathcal{I}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}=\int_{0}^{\pi}\left[\rho_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2})}\right]^{\alpha}d\chi_{\phi}
are the individual entropic moments of marginal density functions. The analytical expressions of radial entropic moments can be obtained for positive integral order
(\alpha\in\mathbb{N})
as:

\begin{array}[]{lr}\mathcal{I}_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}&=\frac{\Gamma(n_{r}+2L+2)[(N_{n_{r},\ell,m}^{(r)})^{2}]^{\alpha}}{n_{r}!\gamma^{2a(1-\alpha)+1}}\sum\limits_{i=0}^{2n_{r}\alpha}\frac{(2\alpha)!\widetilde{B}_{2n_{r}\alpha+i,2\alpha}}{(i+2\alpha)!}.\\
&\frac{\Gamma\left[(2L+2)\alpha+2a(1-\alpha)+i+1\right]}{\alpha^{\,\left[(2L+2)\alpha+2a(1-\alpha)+i+1\right]}}\end{array}

(11)

The analytical expressions for
\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}
and
\mathcal{I}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}
have been reported in our recent work
[
25
]
. Note that
\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}
and
\mathcal{I}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}
are independent of
Z
, but
\mathcal{I}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}
depends on
Z
. Therefore, for fixed
(n_{r},\ell,m)
values, the entropic moment of H-isoelectronic series
(Z\geq 2)
is scaled by the amount
[Z^{(2a+1)(1-\alpha)}]_{Z=2,3,\cdots}
from the hydrogenic counterpart, in the Dunkl case. Without reflection (non-Dunkl case), the same scaled factor equals
[Z^{3(1-\alpha)}]_{Z=2,3,\cdots}
.

Now, the Rényi entropy of order
\alpha
, of the density function
\rho_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})}
can be expressed as:

\mathcal{R}_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\alpha)}=\frac{1}{1-\alpha}\ln\left[\mathcal{I}_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\alpha)}\right],\alpha>0,\neq 1.

(12)

This can be written as a sum of the corresponding entropies of marginal density functions given as follows:
\mathcal{R}_{n,\ell,m}^{(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3},\alpha)}=\mathcal{R}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}+\mathcal{R}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}+\mathcal{R}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}
, where
\mathcal{R}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}=\ln\left[\mathcal{I}_{n,\ell,m}^{(r,\alpha)}\right]/(1-\alpha),

\mathcal{R}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}=\ln\left[\mathcal{I}_{\ell,m}^{(\theta,s_{3},\alpha)}\right]/(1-\alpha)
,
\mathcal{R}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}=\ln\left[\mathcal{I}_{m}^{(\phi,s_{1},s_{2},\alpha)}\right]/(1-\alpha)
.

5
Conclusion

At first, for the Coulomb potential, eigenvalues and eigenfunctions, radial densities and energy degeneracies have been considered from a solution of the DSE, in case of H-isoelectronic atoms
(
Z=1-3
). Then we derive expressions for a bunch of linear information measures, namely, standard deviation, Shannon entropy, Rényi entropy for arbitrary quantum states. The obtained results are compared and contrasted with corresponding Schrd̈inger system without reflection operator. Next we proceed for four important complexity measures, like LMC, SRC, GRC and RCR for the Dunkl-Coulomb potential. All these results are presented in tabular and graphical form. The information theoretic results are presented here for the first time, and will hopefully inspire future works in this direction.

Inflammatory biomarkers as predictors of paradoxical reaction in tubercular meningitis: a prospective observational study
Authors: Satinder Deswal, Abdul Qavi, Ajai Kumar singh, Pradeep Kumar Maurya, Dinkar Kulshreshtha, Arshi Ansari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTubercular meningitis (TBM) is associated with high morbidity and mortality due to intense inflammatory responses causing basal exudates, vasculitic infarcts, hydrocephalus and cranial nerve palsies. Paradoxical reaction (PR) occurs in nearly 30% of patients and contributes to clinical deterioration during treatment. Dysregulated inflammatory mediators in serum and cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) have been implicated in PR and adverse outcomes in TBM.ObjectiveThis study evaluated baseline serum and CSF inflammatory biomarkers for predicting PR and vasculitic infarcts in newly diagnosed TBM patients.MethodsIn this prospective observational study, 151 treatment-naïve TBM patients fulfilling Marais consensus criteria were enrolled. Baseline clinical, radiological, serum and CSF inflammatory markers were assessed and repeated during PR/stroke. Serum markers included high-sensitivity C reactive protein (hsCRP), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), ferritin, albumin, D-dimer, neutrophil–lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and systemic immune-inflammation index. CSF analysis included adenosine deaminase, NLR, protein, glucose and leucocyte count.ResultsAmong 151 patients (mean age 32.7±14.5 years), 43 (28.4%) developed PR, most commonly within 1–2 months of antitubercular therapy initiation. Altered sensorium (58.1%) and progression of hydrocephalus (60.5%) were common manifestations. Higher baseline ESR, hsCRP, LDH, ferritin, D-dimer and lower serum albumin were significantly associated with PR. Serum ferritin showed the best predictive accuracy (area under the curve 0.910; cut-off ≥451 µg/L; sensitivity 88.4%, specificity 88.9%). On multivariable analysis, serum ferritin (OR 1.004; p=0.001) and ESR (OR 1.020; p=0.033) remained independent predictors.ConclusionsSerum inflammatory markers, particularly ferritin and ESR, may serve as useful predictors of PR in TBM. These findings support an immune-mediated mechanism underlying paradoxical worsening and may help identify high-risk patients who could benefit from early anti-inflammatory or immune-modulatory therapy.

Introduction

Tuberculosis (TB) remains a major global health challenge associated with substantial morbidity and mortality. Central nervous system TB (CNS TB), most commonly presenting as tubercular meningitis (TBM), accounts for nearly 1% of all TB cases and represents the most severe form of extrapulmonary TB. TBM results from the rupture of subpial tuberculomas into the subarachnoid space, leading to an intense basal inflammatory response.1 Despite appropriate antitubercular therapy (ATT), mortality remains high, and many survivors are left with permanent neurological sequelae.

Neurological injury in TBM is predominantly immune-mediated. The intense inflammatory response produces dense basal exudates, resulting in mechanical vascular compression, vasospasm and endarteritis.2 These processes predispose patients to hydrocephalus, raised intracranial pressure and cerebral infarction, which occurs in up to two-thirds of patients and is associated with poor clinical outcomes.3 4 Autopsy studies have demonstrated widespread vasculitis involving both the large vessels of the circle of Willis and distal perforating arteries. Although early diagnosis and prompt initiation of ATT are crucial, many patients present in advanced stages, when established infarctions are already evident on neuroimaging.

Another important complication during TBM treatment is paradoxical reaction (PR), characterised by clinical or radiological worsening after an initial improvement on ATT in the absence of drug resistance or alternative pathology. PR occurs in nearly 30% of TBM patients and commonly manifests as worsening sensorium, focal neurological deficits, seizures, enlarging tuberculomas, progression of hydrocephalus, new infarcts and, rarely, spinal cord involvement.5–7

The mechanisms underlying PR are not fully understood. It is considered an immune reconstitution inflammatory syndrome (IRIS)-like phenomenon, characterised by an exaggerated host immune response to mycobacterial antigens released during bacterial killing. In both HIV-infected and uninfected TBM populations, PR has been associated with neutrophil-mediated inflammation, elevated cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) neutrophil counts and higher baseline bacterial loads.6–9 Cytokines such as TNF-α, interleukin (IL)-6, IL-8 and matrix metallo-proteinases (MMPs) have also been implicated.10 Marais et al demonstrated that elevated CSF pro-inflammatory mediators and neutrophil-associated proteins could be detected before the onset of PR, suggesting their predictive potential.11 12

The inflammatory profile of PR highlights the potential role of biomarker-based risk stratification. Identification of such markers may facilitate early intervention, particularly in resource-limited settings where neuroimaging facilities are not readily available. Therapeutic strategies targeting neutrophil-mediated inflammation, including phosphodiesterase-4 inhibitors (roflumilast), MMP inhibitors (doxycycline) and non-steroidal anti-inflammatory drugs (ibuprofen), have shown promise in experimental TB models, emphasising the importance of early risk detection.13–16

Several inflammatory markers have been evaluated in TBM. High-sensitivity C reactive protein (HsCRP) and erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) are established markers of systemic inflammation and may correlate with disease activity. Serum ferritin, an acute-phase reactant, reflects macrophage activation and systemic inflammation and has been associated with poor outcomes in severe infections. Elevated D-dimer levels may indicate a pro-thrombotic state relevant to TBM-associated vasculopathy. Low serum albumin, a negative acute-phase reactant, has been linked to severe disease and unfavourable prognosis. Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) reflects tissue injury and systemic inflammation. Composite indices such as the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR) and systemic immune-inflammation index (SII) integrate haematological parameters into markers of systemic inflammatory status. In CSF, adenosine deaminase (ADA) and CSF-NLR have been variably associated with TBM severity.

Given the high burden of PR and cerebral infarction in TBM and the lack of reliable predictive tools, systematic evaluation of inflammatory markers at baseline may facilitate risk stratification, early initiation of adjunctive anti-inflammatory therapy and closer monitoring of high-risk patients.

This prospective observational study aimed to evaluate selected baseline serum and CSF inflammatory biomarkers for their predictive value in the development of PRs and vasculitic infarctions in patients with newly diagnosed non-HIV TBM.

Methods

Study design and setting

This was a single-centred, hospital-based prospective observational study conducted in the Department of Neurology at a tertiary care centre of north India. The study was carried out among consecutive newly diagnosed patients with TBM admitted in neurology ward over a period of 2 years duration and followed for the development of PR and/or vasculitic infarcts.

Eligibility criteria

Inclusion of cases

Newly diagnosed patients with TBM according to the consensus diagnostic criteria of Marais et al (definite, probable or possible TBM).17

Exclusion criteria

Patients of TBM were excluded if they had concomitant connective tissue diseases, HIV-positive status, cryptococcal or other non-TBM, systemic illnesses that could influence inflammatory biomarkers (including diabetes mellitus, hypertension, hypothyroidism, primary malignancy, hepatic or renal dysfunction or other chronic infections), pregnancy or lactation or refusal to provide consent.

Definitions

PR: Clinical and/or radiological worsening of TBM lesions or appearance of new lesions after at least 10 days of ATT, following an initial improvement, without evidence of drug resistance or alternate diagnosis.5–7

PR: Clinical and/or radiological worsening of TBM lesions or appearance of new lesions after at least 10 days of ATT, following an initial improvement, without evidence of drug resistance or alternate diagnosis.5–7

Vasculitic infarct: New-onset neurological deficits with MRI evidence of acute cerebral infarction in a vascular territory, consistent with vasculitis.

Vasculitic infarct: New-onset neurological deficits with MRI evidence of acute cerebral infarction in a vascular territory, consistent with vasculitis.

Baseline assessment

All patients underwent detailed clinical evaluation, including demographic profiling, symptom history and neurological examination. Disease severity was graded using the British Medical Research Council staging system: Stage I (alert and oriented without focal deficits), stage II (confusion, lethargy or mild focal neurological deficits) and stage III (coma or severe neurological deficits). Functional status was assessed using the Modified Barthel Index at baseline and at 3-month follow-up, with scores ≤12 considered poor functional status and scores >12 considered good functional status.

Baseline investigations, repeated at 3 months or at the onset of PR or infarction, included serum biomarkers such as high-sensitivity C reactive protein (HsCRP), ESR, LDH, serum ferritin, D-dimer, serum albumin, NLR, SII, serum glutamic-oxaloacetic transaminase (SGOT) and serum glutamic-pyruvic transaminase. CSF analysis included total leucocyte count with differential count, CSF NLR, ADA, protein and glucose levels. Imaging studies included contrast-enhanced MRI of the brain at baseline and during PR/infarct episodes, along with chest X-ray and abdominal ultrasonography.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was calculated using CSF protein level as the primary biomarker for differentiating patients with and without PR, based on the study by Singh et al.9 Mean (SD) CSF protein levels were 188.54 (115.15) mg/dL in the PR group and 283.26 (542.51) mg/dL in the non-PR group. Assuming a clinically significant difference of 170 mg/dL, 80% power, a two-sided α of 0.05, a group ratio of 1:2.5 and 10% attrition, the required sample size was 149 patients (43 with PR and 106 without).

Follow-up and outcome assessment

Patients were followed clinically and radiologically at scheduled visits or sooner if neurological deterioration occurred. The main outcome was development of PR/vasculitic infarcts and their correlation with baseline markers.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as mean±SD or median (IQR) according to data distribution assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, while categorical variables were presented as number (percentage). Group comparisons were performed using Student’s t-test for normally distributed continuous variables, Mann-Whitney U test for non-normally distributed data, and chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables, as appropriate. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis was performed to evaluate the discriminatory ability of biomarkers for predicting PR, including calculation of area under the curve (AUC), optimal cut-off values using the Youden Index, sensitivity, specificity, positive and negative predictive values, likelihood ratios and diagnostic accuracy. Biomarkers significant in univariate analysis were entered into multivariable logistic regression to identify independent predictors, with results expressed as ORs and 95% CIs. A two-tailed p<0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were performed using the current version of SPSS (version 21) available at the institute (IBM).

Results

Baseline clinical and demographic data

The study included 151 patients with TBM. The mean age was 32.74 years (range: 9–77), with 69 males (45.7%) and 82 females (54.3%). Headache was the chief complaint seen in all patients, fever in 93.4%, altered sensorium in 80.1%, focal neurological deficits in 24.5%, seizures in 18.5% and back pain associated with Pott’s spine in 7.9%.

Most patients (81/151, 53.6%) presented with a symptom duration of 2–3 months, while 17/151 (11.3%) had symptoms lasting more than 3 months. Based on diagnostic criteria, 48 (31.8%) had definite TBM, 70 (46.4%) probable TBM and 33 (21.9%) possible TBM. Overall, 29 patients (19.2%) presented in stage I, 87 (57.6%) in stage II and 35 (23.1%) in stage III (figure 1A and C).

PR cohort

Out of the total 151 patients, 43 (28.4%) developed a PR. Among these 43 patients, 17 (39.5%) were male and 26 (60.5%) were female. The majority of PRs occurred between 1 and 2 months after the initiation of ATT, while the least frequent occurrence was observed after 3 months, in only one patient (2.3%). Clinically, altered sensorium was the most common manifestation, observed in 25/43 patients (58.1%), whereas focal neurological deficits were the least common, seen in 8/43 patients (18.6%). Radiologically, the most frequent finding was the development of hydrocephalus or an increase in its size, reported in 26/43 patients, while TB myelitis was the least common, occurring in only one patient. Among PR cohort 7 (16.3%) patients of TBM developed radiologically proven infarcts (figure 1B and D).

Multivariable logistic regression of significant biomarkers

Multivariable logistic regression was performed to evaluate the independent predictive value of six inflammatory biomarkers for PR in TBM. While all six biomarkers were significantly associated with PRs in univariable analysis, only serum ferritin and ESR remained statistically significant in the multivariable model, likely due to intercorrelations among the markers. Higher baseline levels of ferritin and ESR were independently associated with increased odds of developing PRs. Variables with clinical relevance and/or statistical significance in univariate analysis were included in the multivariable model using a forward stepwise approach. We also acknowledge that interaction effects between laboratory parameters may exist; however, interaction terms were not formally explored because of the limited sample size

The overall model demonstrated an accuracy of 90.1%, with a sensitivity of 79.0% (34/43) and specificity of 94.4% (102/108). The model had a −2 log-likelihood of 85.275 and a Nagelkerke R² of 0.670, indicating good explanatory power (table 3).

Discussion

In the present study, we found that elevated baseline serum ferritin and ESR were strong independent predictors of developing PR and/or vasculitic infarcts during ATT. The clinical implications are important—both biomarkers are readily available, inexpensive, and rapid to obtain, enabling early risk stratification and tailored follow-up in high-burden settings.

Comparison with published literature

To place our findings in the context of existing evidence, we reviewed previous studies evaluating PR in TBM, including those by Liu et al18 (China), Lu et al19 (China), Kalita et al,20 Singh et al9 (India) and Sinha et al21 (India). Notably, the studies by Liu et al and Singh et al, similar to ours, were conducted exclusively in HIV-negative TBM patients, thereby minimising the confounding effects of HIV-associated immune dysregulation and IRIS on inflammatory responses and disease severity.

Influence of HIV infection on PRs

This study was conducted exclusively in non-HIV TBM patients because TB-IRIS is a well-known immunological pattern observed in HIV TBM. In HIV TBM associated IRIS (TB-IRIS), two clinical patterns are recognised: (1) ‘unmasking’ TB, in which subclinical disease becomes clinically apparent after initiation of antiretroviral therapy (ART) and (2) ‘paradoxical’ TB-IRIS, characterised by worsening or recurrence of clinical and/or radiological manifestations in patients already receiving anti-TB treatment after ART initiation.

In addition, baseline CD4+T cell counts and higher HIV viral load have been associated with exaggerated inflammatory responses and increased risk of IRIS, which can affect all the baseline blood biomarkers status.22 This is why, the findings in present study may not be generalisable to HIV-associated TBM, where immune dysregulation, viral load, CD4 status and IRIS substantially influence inflammation and disease severity.

Clinical significance of ferritin and ESR

Serum ferritin is both an iron storage protein and an acute-phase reactant. In TBM, elevated ferritin likely reflects enhanced macrophage activation and systemic inflammation preceding overt neurological deterioration. Similarly, ESR, although a nonspecific inflammatory marker, reflects increased plasma fibrinogen and immunoglobulin concentrations driven by pro-inflammatory cytokines, particularly IL-6 and TNF-α. High baseline ESR may therefore indicate a greater mycobacterial antigen burden and a heightened inflammatory state. Following initiation of ATT, bacterial lysis releases additional mycobacterial antigens, potentially triggering an exaggerated immune response that manifests as PR or radiological worsening.

A major strength of our study is the identification of clinically applicable biomarker thresholds. Serum ferritin ≥451 ng/mL demonstrated a sensitivity of 88.4% and specificity of 88.9%, while ESR ≥60.5 mm/hour showed a sensitivity of 74.4% and specificity of 88.9% for predicting PR. Because these biomarkers are routinely available at the time of diagnosis, they may facilitate early risk stratification before initiation of ATT. Patients exceeding these thresholds may benefit from closer inpatient or early postdischarge surveillance, a lower threshold for repeat neuroimaging if neurological symptoms develop, and consideration of prolonged or intensified adjunctive corticosteroid therapy. However, such therapeutic strategies require validation in prospective randomised trials.

Among the other inflammatory biomarkers evaluated, hsCRP, LDH, D-dimer and serum albumin were significantly associated with PR on univariate analysis. Elevated hsCRP paralleled ferritin and ESR, supporting its role as a marker of systemic inflammatory activation. Increased LDH and D-dimer levels likely reflect tissue injury, endothelial activation and a prothrombotic state, linking systemic inflammation with cerebrovascular pathology. Conversely, hypoalbuminaemia, a negative acute-phase reactant, was associated with PR and may represent the combined effects of malnutrition and cytokine-mediated suppression of hepatic protein synthesis.23

Patients who developed vasculitic infarction had significantly higher baseline ferritin and ESR levels than those without infarction, supporting the hypothesis that intense systemic inflammation and endothelial injury contribute to cerebrovascular complications in TBM. These findings further suggest that PR and vasculitic infarction may represent different manifestations along the same spectrum of immune-mediated CNS injury.

Timing and clinical monitoring

In our cohort, most PRs occurred between 4 and 8 weeks after initiation of ATT, whereas vasculitic infarctions predominantly developed during the first month of treatment, consistent with previous reports by Singh et al.9 These observations identify a critical period during early therapy when vigilant clinical monitoring is warranted. Patients with elevated ferritin or ESR at baseline may benefit from weekly clinical assessment during the first month, prompt MRI evaluation for new focal neurological deficits, and individualised consideration of adjunctive therapies such as prolonged corticosteroids or aspirin. Nevertheless, these management strategies should be confirmed in prospective interventional studies before routine implementation.

Radiological implications

The predominance of basal ganglia infarctions highlights the importance of diffusion-weighted imaging during follow-up MRI, even in patients with subtle clinical deterioration.24 Progressive hydrocephalus and enlarging tuberculomas were also common manifestations of PR, suggesting that patients with elevated baseline ferritin and ESR warrant closer radiological surveillance for these complications. In our study, among PR cohort 7 (16.3%) patients of TBM developed radiologically proven infarcts. Ischaemic stroke is a common complication of TBM, occurring in nearly 20% of patients and typically resulting from basal exudates and inflammatory vasculitis.21 In contrast, the infarcts observed in our study developed after initiation of ATT and were attributed to PR, highlighting immune-mediated inflammation as a potential mechanism of cerebrovascular injury during treatment.

Strengths of the study

The strengths of this study include its prospective design with standardised clinical follow-up and imaging protocols, comprehensive evaluation of multiple inflammatory biomarkers, and multivariable analysis to identify independent predictors. In addition, ROC-derived biomarker cut-offs with detailed diagnostic performance measures enhance the clinical applicability of the findings. As these biomarkers are inexpensive and widely available, the results have particular relevance for TBM management in resource-limited, high-burden settings.

Limitations

This study has several limitations. First, its single-centre design may limit the generalisability of the findings. Second, biomarker measurements were obtained only at baseline, and serial assessments may provide additional prognostic value. Third, the study was not powered to evaluate mortality outcomes according to biomarker levels. Fourth, CSF cytokine profiling was not performed, limiting mechanistic insights into the inflammatory response. In addition, this study included only non-HIV TBM patients; therefore, the influence of HIV-associated immune dysregulation and IRIS on inflammatory biomarkers could not be assessed. Finally, broad exclusion criteria were applied to minimise confounding from comorbid conditions that may independently alter inflammatory biomarkers, but this may further limit applicability to the wider TBM population encountered in routine clinical practice

Conclusions

In this prospective TBM cohort, elevated baseline serum ferritin and ESR independently predicted the development of PR and/or vasculitic infarcts during ATT. These markers are inexpensive, rapid and widely available, making them highly applicable in high-burden, resource-limited settings. Incorporating ferritin and ESR thresholds into initial TBM evaluation could facilitate early identification of high-risk patients, prompt closer monitoring and enable timely therapeutic interventions to improve neurological outcomes.

Intrinsic Linear Response from Zeeman Quantum Geometry in 2D Unconventional Magnets
Neelanjan Chakraborti, Sudeep Kumar Ghosh, Snehasish Nandy

Abstract
Unconventional magnets with zero net magnetization yet momentum-dependent spin splitting constitute a newly identified class of materials that provide a rich platform for quantum-geometry-driven transport phenomena. Exploiting the interplay between momentum translation and spin rotation, we uncover a distinct linear transport response governed by a generalized quantum geometric tensor, the Zeeman quantum geometric tensor (ZQGT). We show that the ZQGT drives a linear intrinsic gyrotropic magnetic current (IGMC) in the three prototypical two-dimensional unconventional magnets: a time-reversal-broken d x 2 − y 2 d_{x^{2}-y^{2}} altermagnet, a time-reversal-symmetric p p -wave magnet, and a mixed d d -wave altermagnet. Depending on symmetry, these magnets exhibit longitudinal, transverse, or combined conduction and displacement IGMCs in the presence of spin–orbit coupling. Notably, this response persists even when conventional Berry curvature contributions vanish, offering a unique probe of hidden spin-split band structures of unconventional magnets. In particular, for mixed d d -wave altermagnets, symmetric Berry curvature and antisymmetric quantum metric respectively generate longitudinal conduction IGMC and transverse displacement IGMC– responses absent in conventional quantum geometry. The predicted signatures, relevant to compounds such as RuO 2 , CrSb, and MnTe, provide experimentally accessible diagnostics for distinguishing unconventional magnetic phases. These findings position the ZQGT as a powerful framework for probing and controlling transport in next-generation quantum materials.

Introduction

A recently discovered class of magnetic materials, collectively termed unconventional magnets, has opened a new frontier in condensed matter physics by exhibiting zero net magnetization alongside momentum-dependent spin splitting

Yuan et al. 2020
;

In these systems, the spin-polarized Fermi surface arises from non-relativistic effects and is protected by crystalline symmetries, enabling spin splitting even in the absence of spin–orbit coupling (SOC). Depending on the parity of the magnetic order, two broad classes can be distinguished. Even-parity orders with

The absence of net magnetization originates from an alternating arrangement of local moments in both real and momentum space, distinct from ferromagnets, which retain a net moment, and from conventional antiferromagnets, where sublattices are related by translation or inversion and preserve spin degeneracy. In altermagnets, sublattices are instead connected by point-group operations such as rotations or reflections, enabling non-relativistic spin splitting without SOC. These symmetry-driven features give rise to a variety of unconventional responses

Within this context, quantum geometric tensor (QGT) has emerged as a powerful framework for understanding transport phenomena in unconventional magnets
Fukaya et al. 2025
;
Liu et al. 2025
;
Pantazopoulos et al. 2024

This formalism predicts intrinsic gyrotropic magnetic currents (IGMC) in spin–orbit-coupled systems under oscillating magnetic fields. While the role of conventional quantum geometry in altermagnet transport has been explored, the contributions from Zeeman quantum geometry, particularly relevant due to the rich spin-split band structures of both even- and odd-parity magnetic orders, remain largely unexplored.

A natural question arising from these developments is: how does Zeeman quantum geometry manifest in the transport properties of unconventional magnets, and can it provide experimentally accessible signatures of their underlying spin-split band structure? To address this, we investigate the linear IGMC responses, proportional to an external magnetic field and originating from the ZQGT, as illustrated schematically in Fig.

These distinct patterns provide clear, symmetry-linked signatures of the underlying spin-split band structure and offer a route to experimentally distinguish different altermagnetic phases.

II
Results and Discussion

In this section, we present our results on Zeeman quantum geometry–induced transport in three representative unconventional magnets:- Case-I:
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
-wave altermagnet, Case-II: unconventional
p
-wave magnet, and Case-III: mixed
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
- and
d_{xy}
-wave altermagnet. We first outline the formulation of the Zeeman quantum geometric tensor for each case, followed by an analysis of the IGMCs governed by the interplay of the ZQGT and the symmetry constraints of each system.

II.1
Zeeman quantum geometry in unconventional magnets

Quantum geometry, a fundamental and powerful concept in condensed matter systems, is encapsulated by the quantum geometric tensor whose real part is known as quantum metric and imaginary part gives rise to Berry curvature. While the conventional QGT is obtained by evaluating the quantum distance
between Bloch states parametrized by momentum, ZQGT
Xiang et al. 2025
is characterized by both the momentum translation as well as spin rotation. In this case, the quantum distance between two infinitesimal neighboring points of the Hilbert space spanned by the cell-periodic Bloch states
\ket{u^{\xi}_{m\mathbf{k}}}
can be written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert U_{d\theta}U_{d\mathbf{k}}\ket{u_{m\mathbf{k}}^{\xi}}-\ket{u_{m\mathbf{k}}^{\xi}}\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p\neq m}g_{mp}^{ab}\,dk_{a}\,dk_{b}+\frac{1}{4}\sum_{m}\Sigma_{pm}^{ab}\,d\theta_{a}\,d\theta_{b}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{p\neq m}\left(z_{mp}^{ba}+z_{pm}^{ba}\right)\,d\theta_{a}\,dk_{b},

(1)

where
\xi
labels the spin degree of freedom,
U_{d\mathbf{k}}\equiv e^{-id\mathbf{k}\cdot\hat{\mathbf{r}}}
represents an infinitesimal momentum translation by the position operator
\hat{\mathbf{r}}
and
U_{d\theta}\equiv e^{-id\theta\cdot\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}/2}
represents an infinitesimal spin rotation with the spin angular momentum operator
\hat{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}/2
(taking
\hbar=1
). The first term on the right-hand side of Eq.(
1
) corresponds to the conventional QGT, which neglects spin degrees of freedom. The second term represents the local QGT in spin space, referred to as the spin QGT, while the third term encapsulates the interplay between momentum translation and spin rotation, termed the ZQGT. Previous studies have shown that the intrinsic linear response arising from the conventional QGT vanishes in
d
-wave altermagnets
Fang et al. 2024a
. The spin QGT contributes to transport responses only at second order in the external perturbative field. Therefore, the intrinsic linear response of unconventional magnets is governed exclusively by the ZQGT, defined as
z_{mp}^{ab}=r_{mp}^{a}\sigma_{pm}^{b}=Q_{mp}^{ab}-\frac{i}{2}Z_{mp}^{ab}
. The Zeeman quantum metric (
Q_{mp}^{ab}
) and the Zeeman Berry curvature (
Z_{mp}^{ab}
)
Xiang et al. 2025
are given by

\displaystyle Q_{mp}^{ab}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left(r_{mp}^{a}\sigma_{pm}^{b}+r_{pm}^{a}\sigma_{mp}^{b}\right),

\displaystyle Z_{mp}^{ab}

\displaystyle=i\left(r_{mp}^{a}\sigma_{pm}^{b}-r_{pm}^{a}\sigma_{mp}^{b}\right),

(2)

with
a,b
denoting spatial indices. In systems without SOC, both the ZQGT and spin QGT vanish since the absence of SOC removes off-diagonal spin-mixing terms in the Hamiltonian, making the interband spin matrix element
\sigma_{mp}=\langle u_{mk}^{\xi}|\hat{\sigma}|u_{pk}^{\xi}\rangle
identically zero. The Zeeman Berry curvature and Zeeman quantum metric decompose into symmetric and antisymmetric components,
{X_{nm}^{A/S;ab}=\tfrac{X_{nm}^{ab}\pm X_{nm}^{ba}}{2}}
, with
X
representing either
Q
or
Z
. For the conventional QGT, the Berry curvature
\Omega_{\mathbf{k}}^{n}=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\times\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{k},n}
is entirely antisymmetric, while the quantum metric is entirely symmetric. Analogously, the antisymmetric Zeeman Berry curvature is
\mathbf{Z}_{\mathbf{k}}=\nabla_{\mathbf{k}}\times\mathbf{S}_{n}
, with
\mathbf{S}_{n}=\tfrac{\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{n}}{2}
. Crucially, unlike the conventional case, the ZQGT gives rise to a symmetric Berry curvature and an antisymmetric Zeeman quantum metric, both of which are absent in standard quantum geometry. Moreover, the ZQGT exhibits distinct symmetry behavior compared to the conventional QGT
Xiang et al. 2025
. The tensor
Z^{ab}_{mp}
is even under time-reversal symmetry, while
Q^{ab}_{mp}
is odd, arising from the transformation properties of position and spin matrix elements:
\hat{\mathcal{T}}r^{a}_{mp}=r^{a}_{pm}
and
\hat{\mathcal{T}}\sigma^{a}_{mp}=-\sigma^{a}_{pm}
. Under inversion symmetry, however, both
Q^{ab}_{mp}
and
Z^{ab}_{mp}
are odd, since
\hat{\mathcal{P}}r^{a}_{mp}=-r^{a}_{mp}
and
\hat{\mathcal{P}}\sigma^{a}_{mp}=\sigma^{a}_{mp}
.

To investigate the transport signatures of ZQGT in unconventional magnets, we consider a generic two-dimensional single orbital Hamiltonian
Fang et al. 2024a
, characterizing unconventional magnetic order on a square lattice (lattice constant is taken to be unity):

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}_{0}+g_{\mathbf{k}}\sigma_{z}\;,

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=-2t[\cos(k_{x})+\cos(k_{y})]+\lambda[\sin(k_{y})\sigma_{x}-\sin(k_{x})\sigma_{y}].

Here,
t
is the nearest-neighbor hopping amplitude,
\lambda
is the strength of the Rashba spin-orbit coupling and
{\sigma}_{x,y}
are Pauli matrices in spin space. The momentum-dependent form factor
g_{\mathbf{k}}
characterizes the nature of the unconventional magnetic order. To gain insights into the physics of the systems, we use the low energy limit of the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
) around the
\Gamma
-point given by

\mathcal{H}^{\prime}=t(k^{2}_{x}+k^{2}_{y}-4)+\lambda(k_{y}\sigma_{x}-k_{x}\sigma_{y}]+g^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\sigma_{z}\;,

(4)

where,

describes the low energy limit of
g_{\mathbf{k}}
.

II.2
Intrinsic gyrotropic transport in unconventional magnets

An oscillating magnetic field of the form
\mathbf{B}(t)=\frac{\mathbf{B}_{0}}{2}\sum_{\omega_{i}}e^{-i\omega_{i}t}
, with
\omega_{i}=\pm\omega
, gives rise to two types of intrinsic gyrotropic magnetic conductivities: the conduction response
\sigma^{C}_{ab}
and the displacement response
\sigma^{D}_{ab}
. These are governed by the Zeeman Berry curvature and quantum metric, and are expressed till linear order in
\omega
as
Xiang et al. 2025

\sigma^{C}_{ab}=\sum_{mp}\int_{k}f_{m}\mathcal{Z}^{ab}_{mp},\quad\sigma^{D}_{ab}=\sum_{mp}\int_{k}f_{m}\frac{2\hbar\omega}{\epsilon_{pm}}\mathcal{Q}^{ab}_{mp},

(8)

where
f_{m}
is the Fermi-Dirac distribution and
\epsilon_{pm}
is the energy difference between
p
and
m
bands. Here, we consider frequencies of the oscillating magnetic field well below the threshold for interband absorption, i.e.
\hbar\omega\ll\epsilon_{pm}
. We would like to emphasize that the conduction IGMC conductivity is actually a fermi surface quantity because of the following relation
\sum_{p}Z_{mp}^{ab}=\partial_{a}\sigma^{b}_{m}
, whereas the displacement IGMC conductivity is the fermi sea quantity. It is evident from the above expression that both conductivities originate purely from band geometric quantities and are independent of the relaxation time
\tau
, confirming their intrinsic nature. In contrast, it has been proposed that gyrotropic magnetic current can also arise from the intrinsic magnetic moment (orbital plus spin) of Bloch electrons on the Fermi surface
Zhong et al. 2016
. However, this contribution is relaxation-time dependent and can thus be distinguished from the ZQGT-driven contribution.

III
Summary and conclusions

In this work, we have addressed the question of how Zeeman quantum geometry manifests in the transport properties of unconventional magnets. Focusing on two-dimensional systems with Rashba SOC, we identified a distinct linear transport mechanism, the IGMC arising from the ZQGT, which captures the coupled response of Bloch states to momentum translation and spin rotation. By analyzing three representative two-dimensional systems: a
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
altermagnet, a time-reversal-symmetric unconventional
p
-wave magnet and a mixed
d
-wave altermagnet, we established a direct link between crystalline symmetry, spin-split band structure, and transport response. The
d_{x^{2}-y^{2}}
altermagnet supports a transverse Hall-like conduction current and a longitudinal displacement current; the
p
-wave magnet exhibits only a transverse conduction current; and the mixed
d
-wave altermagnet uniquely hosts all four possible current components, enabled by a quadrupolar Zeeman Berry curvature and an off-diagonal Zeeman quantum metric. It is important to note that we have ignored the perturbative correction to both the conventional and Zeeman Berry curvature due to the magnetic field, however, they can give rise to additional contributions only in the nonlinear IGMC
Wang et al. 2024a
;
Wang et al. 2024b
. Our results establish the ZQGT as a versatile tool for diagnosing unconventional magnetic phases through their symmetry-determined transport signatures. The fact that these responses can persist even when Berry curvature induced linear response vanishes makes them particularly valuable for probing materials where conventional topological indicators fail.

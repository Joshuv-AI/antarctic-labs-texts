Multi-objective optimal design of Tuned Mass Damper Inerter for base isolated structures
Authors: Zhihua Yue, Guangcai Han
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe BIS-TMDI is a hybrid control strategy that significantly reduces the seismic response of structures compared to conventional BIS through proper tuning, albeit with the trade-off of increased control forces exerted by the TMDI on the host building structure. However, these potentially significant forces are generally not considered in current TMDI tuning approaches. In this paper, a multi-objective optimization design method is herein developed to determine the compromise between the competing objectives of suppressing earthquake-induced vibrations in buildings and avoiding the generation of excessive control forces of TMDI. The study shows that under non-stationary excitations, the average control force of the TMDI system can be reduced by 16% compared to single optimization constraint, with a maximum reduction of up to 25%. Finally, the optimization design methods of this paper are applied to an 8-story frame as a validation case.Keywords: Multi-objective optimal design, Tuned mass damper inerter (TMDI), Base isolation system(BIS), NSGA-IISubject terms: Engineering, Civil engineering

Introduction

Passive control is a structural control technology that does not require external energy sources, and its control force is passively generated by the movement of the control device itself when it vibrates with the structure. Due to the simplicity of the passive control system and its independence from external energy, it is more widely used in civil engineering structures1–3 . Base isolation is a passive control method that is increasingly being used in earthquake-prone areas to mitigate seismic response in buildings. This technique effectively diminishes the seismic impact on the superstructure by dissipating energy through a flexible layer incorporated between the superstructure and the foundation4–6. The primary displacements occur within the isolation layer, resulting in mostly translational motion of the superstructure during earthquakes. While the widespread adoption of Base Isolation Systems (BIS) showcases significant benefits, certain drawbacks must be taken into account. Particularly, the occurrence of substantial displacements in the isolation layer necessitates attention during the design phase. In practical engineering applications, a feasible approach to lower the demand for such displacements involves utilizing highly damped isolators or introducing additional damping mechanisms, such as external dampers. It is essential to recognize that this mitigation strategy reduces the displacement of the base isolation subsystem while concurrently amplifying the acceleration and inter-story displacement response of the primary structure7.

In recent years, more and more vibration isolators have been developed based on inerter8–14 .The inerter is a concept introduced by Smith in 2002 and was first used in the Formula 1 racing sector15. One of its practical implementations is the rack and pinion inerter, comprising gears, racks, flywheels, and frames, which produce force proportional to the variance in absolute acceleration between their endpoints16. This proportionality coefficient is called the inertial capacity coefficient or apparent mass, which has the dimension of mass and that is much larger than the physical mass of the inerter, which is the mass amplification effect of the inerter. This property has attracted the interest of many engineers who have developed many seismic dampers based on inerter. Over the past two decades, inerter have been widely used in various fields. Notably in civil engineering where engineers integrate traditional vibration isolators with inerter technology. Tuned Viscous Mass Damper (TVMD) is a damper based on the inerter principle, which is earlier than the inerter concept. TVMD is widely used in vibration control of structures. For example, Li et al.17 used TVMD improving seismic performance of rocking tall piers. Some researchers combine base isolation with Inerter Mass Damper (IMD) to reduce isolation displacements. However, IMD generates a large control force at high frequencies and increases the acceleration response of the structure under earthquake. Another common measure to reduce demand of displacement in base isolation layer is the introduction of a dynamic vibration absorber DVA (e.g., TMD) into the BIS, which constitutes the BIS-TMD system, and the control effectiveness of the BIS-TMD system has been well demonstrated in previous studies18–20. However, the BIS-TMD performance is highly dependent on the tuning mass, and a superior control effect requires a larger tuning mass. The performance of the BIS-TMD system is optimized when the additional mass reaches 5% of the total building mass, however, realizing a larger mass in a real project implies a larger cost. In some buildings, the basement is used as an additional mass to form a BIS-TMD system with the base isolation layer. Yet, the tuned mass in a BIS-TMD configuration potentially displaces 2–4 times more than the isolation layer, necessitating careful consideration for piping connections and additional space to prevent impact21.This means that special design solutions are needed for the piping connections between the basement and the superstructure, and a large external space for the basement to avoid impacts also needs to be considered. In response to these challenges, certain researchers22–27 have proposed a new enhanced base seismic isolation system used in conjunction with tuned mass inerter damping (TMDI),where an inerter is positioned between the tuned mass and the ground, which means that one end of the inerter must be connected to the ground, unlike the Pendulum-type tuned tandem mass dampers-inerters (PTTMDI) system28, where the inerter can be unconnected to the ground or the two ends of the inerter can be installed at the same height. This innovative design capitalizes on the mass amplification feature of the inerter to achieve significantly enhanced effective inertia of the TMD. On this basis, Li et al.29 changed the installation position of BIS-TMDI and considered the nonlinear characteristics of the base isolation layer to form a nonlinear BIS-TMDI system. Both numerical simulations and experimental findings confirm that a parameter-optimized BIS-TMDI system with reduced physical mass not only lessens displacement in the base isolation subsystem but also notably enhances the seismic response of the primary isolation structure. Then, Cao et al.30developed tuned tandem mass dampers-inerters (TTMDI) by adding damping between the additional masses of two sets of TMDI systems. TTMDI was found to have better suppression bandwidth than TMDI under white noise excitation, and then TTMDI was combined with BIS to form a high performance damping system31. In the literature26,a prototype BIS-TMDI was fabricated and tested on a shaking table. It was found that the developed BIS-TMDI prototype could reduce the peak displacement of the primary structure by 37% at PGA = 0.05 g and 25% at PGA = 0.15 g compared to the uncontrolled primary structure.

In addition, some scholars have investigated the optimal design of BIS-TMDI. Di Matteo et al.32 considering that the displacement response of the main structure is similar to the displacement response of the seismic isolation layer, the multi-freedom model with BIS-TMDI system is simplified and a simple optimization formula about BIS-TMDI is proposed. Pietrosanti33 generalized two-degree-of-freedom model based on a TMDI-controlled multi-degree-of-freedom structure is proposed to optimize the design in terms of energy dissipation, and the results indicate that the inerter in the TMDI system has limited performance enhancement for the TMD system. Liu et al.34 optimized design of TMDI system with the objective of reducing cable displacement when considering impulse excitation. De Angelis et al.[35]utilized a TMDI system to control the displacement of a footbridge in order to improve the comfort of the footbridge. Different scholars have proposed different optimization constraints for the TMDI system, including minimizing the structural displacement, structural acceleration or minimizing the energy input from the upper part of the structure. However, the above optimization constraints only consider the control of a single response, even though the optimization constraints based on the energy dissipation perspective can control the acceleration and displacement responses of the structure at the same time, they ignore the potential damages brought by the control force of the TMDI system. Excessive control force not only needs to consider the strength of the connection between the inerter vessel and the main structure, but also needs to consider the high requirements for the inertial device itself36.

In this study, based on reasonable assumptions of the base seismic isolation model, and Pareto front optimization is employed to balance two conflicting objectives: minimizing the seismic response of the structure and limiting the control force exerted by the TMDI system. The findings indicate that the optimization approach effectively enhances the seismic performance of the building while mitigating the TMDI system’s control force on the primary structure. In comparison to utilizing a single-objective optimization method, the proposed technique demonstrates improved seismic performance and decreased TMDI system control force. Furthermore, the displacement of the TMDI system remains within acceptable limits.

Multi-objective design framework

An optimal frame is developed based on previous work, followed by proposing two optimization constraints and identifying the type of optimal question in this chapter. Finally, the optimization problem is solved in conjunction with the NSGA-II algorithm.

Selection of performance objectives

Reviewing the optimal design method based on the TMD system in previous studies10,20,43, usually, some parameters are assumed to be known, and then the remaining variables are optimized.

For the study of this paper, it is assumed that the frequency ratio ωs/ωb,the superstructure damping ratio ξs,the base isolation system damping ratio ξb and the mass ratio of the seismic isolation layer μb are known. In the TMDI system, there are four variables which are μt,β,ξt,ωt and assumed that μt and β, so that ξt and ωt become the optimized design parameters chosen in this paper. For the convenience of dealing with the optimization problem, the parameter υt=ωt/ωbandυtNL=ωt/ωbNL is defined, and this variable represents the ratio of the frequency of the TMDI system to the frequency of the seismic isolation system. The optimization variable in this paper is denoted as x=[υt,ξt]. In particular, the optimization parameters of nonlinear structures is denoted as xNL=[υtNL,ξt].Once μt and β are determined, the optimization parameter x 和 xNL for this paper will be determined by solving the response of the structure in a large number of cases.

Solving multi-objective optimization problems

The previous section introduced a multi-objective optimization problem. The linear weighting model is widely utilized in multi-objective optimization. Simple Additive Weighting (SAW) is a classical linear weighted summation method that assigns equal weight to different objective functions regardless of their unit and range variations. This method employs a comprehensive utility function to encapsulate the overall optimization objective. The solution derived from the optimal utility function is regarded as the optimal solution, effectively transforming the multi-objective optimization problem into a single-objective optimization problem. However, the designation of weights by the linear weighting method depends on the preference degree of the problem solver for different objectives, which is difficult to know in advance. Even when the degree of preference is known, the question of how exactly to set the weights remains tricky. The influence of different weights on the optimal solution cannot be ignored, that is, the quality of the solution is difficult to guarantee. In order to overcome the shortcomings of the linear weighting method, the NSGA-II47 genetic algorithm based on the Pareto multi-objective optimization model is considered in this paper. The algorithm does not transform the problem into a single objective problem analysis, nor does it need to set or introduce new parameters, or variables (such as weights, and boundary values), and directly operates based on the original objective function and value, which can be applied to any objective and any function. It will not lose the information of the objective function resolution, so the quality of the solution can be better guaranteed. Compared with the NSGA algorithm, the fast non-dominated sorting proposed in NSGA-II reduces the total time complexity from OMN3 to OMN2, where the M represents the number of optimization targets and N represents the population size. The broadness of the group is guaranteed by replacing the shared radius σshare with a crowding and crowding comparison operator, which rapidly extends the individuals in the quasi-Pareto areas to the full Pareto area48. NSGA-II algorithm selects the elite retention strategy in the main loop. That is, in the process of evolution, the genetic operation is not carried out on the historically excellent individuals in the group, but it is directly copied to the next generation, which proves that the best individuals enter the next generation, thus greatly improving the computational efficiency and robustness of the algorithm49,50.The NSGA-II genetic algorithm usually solves a set of optimal solutions of the objective function when dealing with optimization problems. The set of optimal solutions forms a surface in space called the Pareto front surface. In other words, the Pareto frontier is the set of optimal solutions, so how to choose the solutions is the key problem of multi-objective optimization analysis. In this paper, the point closest to the origin of coordinates on the Pareto front is selected as the research object to compare with the single objective optimization. At the same time, this paper also explains the advantages of this selection method.

Comparison of optimization results

Conclusions

In this paper, the stochastic vibration analysis method is applied to the BIS TMDI system, first to derive the variance of the displacement response of the superstructure and the variance of the control force of the TMDI system, and second to use the variance of the response of the superstructure as a measure of the seismic isolation performance of the building. Then, the multi-objective optimization method is adopted to control the main structure, taking into consideration the competing objectives of seismic isolation performance of the building and the control force of the TMDI system.

Based on NSGA-II algorithm, the multi-objective optimization method proposed in this paper has the following advantages in the optimization design of BIS-TMDI structure:

(1) Compared with BIS, the BIS-TMDI structure developed in this paper can significantly reduce the absolute displacement response at the top of the structure, and its peak value can be reduced by up to 60%. At the same time, it can reduce the absolute acceleration response of the top of the structure and the displacement response of the isolation layer. In terms of energy dissipation, this is because the TMDI system consumes most of the energy, which reduces the response of the structure.

(2) Although the method proposed in this paper makes the top displacement of the BIS-TMDI structure slightly larger than the result of single objective optimization (the difference between the two is less than 5%), it can greatly reduce the control force of the TMDI system, with a maximum reduction of 60%. The reduction of TMDI control can avoid the potential damage caused by large control.

(3) In the frequency domain analysis, under different frequency excitation, it is observed that the optimization method in this paper enables BIS-TMDI to have a wide suppression bandwidth, and when the damping and stiffness of the isolation layer change, the optimized structure has good robustness.

(4) When the multi-degree of freedom structure equipped with BIS-TMDI strategy is optimized, compared with the BIS, the optimized structure can reduce the absolute displacement at the top of the structure, the displacement of the isolation layer, the base shear force of the structure, the kinetic energy and the elastic strain energy of the isolation layer superstructure. At the same time, compared with the single objective optimization results, the displacement of additional mass in TMDI system and the control force of TMDI system have also been significantly reduced.

(5) Under the excitation of 4 near-field and 4 far-field natural seismic waves, the time-domain analysis results show that the BIS-TMDI structure has good robustness when the multi-objective optimization method proposed in this paper is adopted to optimize the design of the BIS-TMDI structure, and the seismic response of the structure is close to or the same as that of the single-objective optimization result. Reduced control of TMDI system.

(6) The multi-objective optimization method developed in this paper is also suitable for nonlinear structures. For nonlinear BIS-TMDI, the results show that the proposed optimization method is effective for the control of displacement and acceleration degree. With β increase, the control effect decreases and tends to be stable, and the reduction of displacement and acceleration is at least 20% and 15%. At the same time, the control force of TMDI system is reduced significantly, the maximum can reach 90%. However, the control ability of the multi-objective optimization method is poor, and the displacement is up to 3 times that of the single objective result, and the acceleration is up to 10%.

The above conclusions show that the optimization method proposed in this paper can not only reduce the structural response, but also reduce the controlling force of the TMDI system and avoid the potential damage caused by large controlling force. Meanwhile, the displacement of the additional mass of the TMDI system is also within a reasonable range.

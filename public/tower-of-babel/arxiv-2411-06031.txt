Abstract
. This paper is concerned with a nonlinear fractional Schördinger system in ℝ \mathbb{{R}} with intraspecies interactions a i > 0 ​ ( i = 1 , 2 ) a_{i}>0\ (i=1,2) and interspecies interactions β ∈ ℝ \beta\in\mathbb{{R}} . We study this system by solving an associated constrained minimization problem (i.e., L 2 − L^{2}- norm constaints). Under certain assumptions on the trapping potentials V i ​ ( x ) ​ ( i = 1 , 2 ) , V_{i}(x)\ (i=1,2), we derive some delicate estimates for the related energy functional and establish a criterion for the existence and non-existence of solutions, in which way several existence results are obtained.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the following system of two coupled fractional Schrödinger equations with trapping potentials in
\mathbb{R}
:

\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}+a_{1}u_{1}^{3}+\beta u_{2}^{2}u_{1},\\
\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu_{2}u_{2}+a_{2}u_{2}^{3}+\beta u_{1}^{2}u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.1)

which
\sqrt{-\Delta}
is the
\frac{1}{2}
-Laplace operator defined in the Sobolev space
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
by the Fourier transform, that is,

\sqrt{-\Delta}u:=\mathscr{F}^{-1}(\left|\xi\right|\mathscr{F}u(\xi)),\ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),

(1.2)

in which
\mathscr{F}u(\xi)
stands for the Fourier transform of
u
, i.e.,

\mathscr{F}u(\xi)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\exp^{-i\xi\cdot x}u(x)dx,\ \ u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\ \ \text{and }

H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})=\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(1+2\pi\left|\xi\right|)\Big|\mathscr{F}u(\xi)\Big|^{2}d\xi<\infty\}.

Moreover, for
i=1,2
,
V_{i}(x)
is a certain type of trapping potential,
\mu_{i}\in\mathbb{R}
is a suitable Lagrange multiplier which denotes the chemical potential,
a_{i}>0

(\text{resp}.<0)
represents the attractive (resp. repulsive) intraspecies interaction strength inside each component,
\beta>0

(\text{resp}.<0)
characterizes the attractive (resp. repulsive) interspecies interaction strength between two components.

In what follows, the complex
L^{2}
inner product is denoted by
(\cdot,\cdot)
, i.e.,

(u,v):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u\overline{v}dx,\ \ \forall u,v\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R},\mathbb{C}),

and then

(\sqrt{-\Delta}u,u):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|\xi\right|\Big|\mathscr{F}u(\xi)\Big|^{2}d\xi,\ \ \>u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}).

(1.3)

Moreover, the norm
\left\|\cdot\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})}
can be given by

\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})}^{2}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[u(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{2}}u+u^{2}\Big]dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big[|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+u^{2}\Big]dx.

It is well-known that system (
1.1
) arises from the following one-dimensional fractional Schrödinger equation in
[
10
]
,

i\partial_{t}\psi(x,t)=(-\Delta)^{\alpha/2}\psi+V(x)\psi+\beta|\psi|^{2}\psi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R},\ t>0,

(1.4)

where
\alpha\in(0,2)
is the Lévy index, and
i=\sqrt{-1}
is the imaginary unit. The constant
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
describes the strength of local (or short-range) interactions between particles, and
V(x)
represents an external trapping potential.
\psi(x,t)
is a complex-valued wave function given by
\psi(x,t)=e^{-i\mu t}u(x)
, where
u(x)
is a solution of the associated equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=\mu u+\beta u^{3},\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}.

(1.5)

It is known from
[
36
]
that (
1.4
) is used to describe the phenomenon in which the motion of bosons in non-Gaussian Bose-Einstein condensates follows Lévy flights instead of Gaussian paths. Furthermore, unlike the classical Laplacian, the fractional Laplacian is a non-local operator describing long-range interactions. It should be noted that, in the multi-component framework (such as system (
1.1
)), one has to take into account an interspecies interaction between each components, which may display more complicated physical phenomena, see
[
20
]
. For more physical details, we refer the reader to
[
28
,
32
]
.

In recent years, as a generalization of the classical Laplace operator, fractional Laplace operator holds broad application prospects in several areas such as physics, biology, chemistry, and finance, see
[
1
,
2
]
. Particularly in Fourier analysis and nonlinear partial differential equations, the following fractional Schrödinger equation (
1.6
) with a nonlinear term has attracted much attention

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=f(x,u),\ \ \>u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.6)

In
[
13
]
, under suitable assumptions for potential function
V(x)
and nonlinear term
f(x,u)
, Felmer et al. investigated the existence of positive classical solutions for the system with
0<s<1
and
N\geq 2
by using the critical point theory. It is noteworthy that, particularly in the case
0<s<1
and
N=1
, through the variational method, Frank and Lenzmann
[
14
]
addressed the existence, uniqueness and a series of significant properties of the ground state on (
1.6
) with
V(x)=1
and
f(x,u)=u^{p+1}
, that is,

(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=u^{p+1},\ \ \>u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{{R}}).

(1.7)

The famous work mentioned above and these important conclusions have opened up a new way for our investigations.
Later, in the general case
0<s<1
and
N\geq 1
, the following equation

(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=|u|^{p}u,\ \ \>u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})

(1.8)

was studied by
[
15
]
, from which we know that there is a unique positive solution
Q(x)\in H^{2s+1}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})\cap C^{\infty}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N})
, which can be taken to be radially symmetric about the origin. Moreover,
Q(x)
is strictly decreasing in
|x-x_{0}|
, and it satisfies

\frac{C_{1}}{1+\left|x\right|^{N+2s}}\leq Q(x)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{1+\left|x\right|^{N+2s}},\ \ \>x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}

with some constants
C_{2}\geq C_{1}>0
, which generalizes the existential results and properties of solutions in
[
14
]
in an optimal way.
In
[
11
]
, Du et al. considered a mass critical stationary fractional Schrödinger equation, more precisely, that is,

(-\Delta)^{s}u+V(x)u=\mu u+a|u|^{\frac{4s}{N}}u,\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},

(1.9)

where
0<s<1
,
N\geq 2
and
\mu
is a Lagrange multiplier. By applying constrained variational methods, a complete classification of the existence and non-existence of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions is established with certain conditions imposed on
V(x)
. For further related results on the fractional Schrödinger equation, we refer to
[
9
,
15
,
27
]
and the references therein.

We know that the following classical Laplacian system in
\mathbb{{R}}^{2}

\begin{cases}-\Delta u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}+b_{1}u_{1}^{3}+\beta u_{2}^{2}u_{1},\\
-\Delta u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu_{2}u_{2}+b_{2}u_{2}^{3}+\beta u_{1}^{2}u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.10)

which is used to model two-component Bose-Einstein condensates with intraspecies and interspecies interactions. It is easy to see that when
u_{1}=0
or
u_{2}=0
, or
u_{1}=u_{2}
, the system (
1.10
) can be reduced to the single Schrödinger equation (
1.9
) with
s=1
and
N=2
. In the past decade, extensive research has been conducted on the analogue issues associated with (
1.10
) for the classical Laplacian system, under variant conditions on
V_{i}(x)
,
b_{i}
and
\beta
. In
[
17
]
, with
b_{i}>0
,
\beta>0
, and for a certain type of trapping potentials
V_{i}(x)
, Guo et al. investigated (
1.10
) by seeking minimizers of the associated variational functional
\tilde{e}(b_{1},b_{2},\beta)
and then obtained the following results in Theorem 1.1

(i). When
0<b_{1}<m^{*}
,
0<b_{2}<m^{*}
and
\beta<\sqrt{(m^{*}-b_{1})(m^{*}-b_{2})}
,
\tilde{e}(b_{1},b_{2},\beta)
has at
least one minimizer.

(ii). Either
b_{1}>m^{*}
or
b_{2}>m^{*}
or
\beta>\frac{m^{*}-b_{1}}{2}+\frac{m^{*}-b_{2}}{2}
,
\tilde{e}(b_{1},b_{2},\beta)
has no minimizer.

Here,
m^{*}=\|\tilde{Q}\|^{2}_{2}
is a critical threshold for existence intervals of solutions, where
\tilde{Q}
is a positive solution to the following classical equation

-\Delta u+u-u^{3}=0,\ \ u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

It is noteworthy that the existence of a positive solution to this classical equation is necessary for establishing such existence intervals of solutions. For more details, see Theorems 1.1-1.3 in
[
17
]
. Later, in
[
18
]
, Guo, Zeng and Zhou generalized various existence results to the case
b_{i}>0
,
\beta<0
. For further details, see e.g.
[
3
,
4
,
5
,
8
,
19
,
22
,
25
]
.

For the case of fractional Laplacian systems, in
[
31
]
, given a class of fractional elliptic systems of order
s\in(\frac{1}{4},1)
, Wang and Wei proved the uniqueness of the positive solutions, via the method of moving planes. In
[
21
]
, He et al. considered a system with critical nonlinearities on a bounded domain in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. With the help of the Nehari manifold decomposition, they proved that the system admits at least two positive solutions when the pair of parameters
(\lambda,\mu)
belongs to a certain subset of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. In
[
12
]
, Du and Mao investigated a fractional Schrödinger system with linear and nonlinear couplings, where
s\in{(\frac{3}{4},1)}
and the potential function
V(x)
satisfies

\mathop{\mathrm{sup\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)=\displaystyle\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\rightarrow+\infty}V(x)=\Lambda>0~\text{and}~\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}V(x)\geq 0,

the existence of ground state solutions for the fractional Laplacian systems was obtained by using variational methods. In
[
33
]
, Wang et al. demonstrated the existence of
L^{2}
-normalized solutions for a nonlinear pseudo-relativistic elliptic system involving the operator
\sqrt{-\Delta+m^{2}}
within certain ranges of the three parameters
a_{1},a_{2},\beta
.

Inspired by
[
17
]
, we first consider the following fractional Laplacian system in
\mathbb{R}^{N}

\begin{cases}({-\Delta})^{s}u_{1}+V_{1}(x)u_{1}=\mu_{1}u_{1}+a_{1}u_{1}^{3}+\beta u_{2}^{2}u_{1},\\
({-\Delta})^{s}u_{2}+V_{2}(x)u_{2}=\mu_{2}u_{2}+a_{2}u_{2}^{3}+\beta u_{1}^{2}u_{2},\end{cases}

(1.11)

which corresponds to the
N
-dimensional classical equation (
1.8
) with
p=2
, that is,

(-\Delta)^{s}u+u=|u|^{2}u,\ \ \>u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{{R}}^{N}).

(1.12)

For system (
1.11
), in order to establish the results analogous to Theorems 1.1-1.3 in
[
17
]
, we follow the approach in
[
17
]
and then assume that there exists a positive solution to (
1.12
). Furthermore, as shown in Theorem 1.1 of
[
11
]
, we find that (
1.12
) is a special case of the following equation,

\frac{N}{2s}(-\Delta)^{s}u+\left(1+\frac{1}{2}\left(2-\frac{N}{s}\right)\right)u-|u|^{2}u=0,\quad u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).

(1.13)

A comparison of the coefficients in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
) shows that the parameters
s
and
N
can only satisfy either the conditions
s=1
and
N=2
, or
s=\frac{1}{2}
and
N=1
. This is because in order to determine the optimal constant
2/\left\|Q\right\|^{2}_{2}
in (
1.19
) generated by the contraction of the fractional Gagliardo-Nirenberg-Sobolev inequality, which is a very important indicator for the existence interval of solutions, (
1.12
) should satisfy the form of (
1.13
). The former corresponds to system (
1.10
) has been examined in
[
17
]
. Therefore, we will consider the latter that is the system (
1.1
) in
\mathbb{R}
, which incorporates the non-local
\frac{1}{2}
-Laplace operator and the trapping potentials
V_{i}(x)\ (i=1,2)
with the following assumptions:

(\mathcal{D}_{1})

V_{i}(x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
,
\displaystyle\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\rightarrow\infty}V_{i}(x)=\infty
and
\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)=0
,
i=1,2
.

(\mathcal{D}_{2})

\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big(V_{1}(x)+V_{2}(x)\big)
and
\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)\ (i=1,2)
are attained.

In order to obtain our results, we consider the following constrained minimization problem

\hat{e}(a_{1},a_{2},\beta):=\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{(u_{1},u_{2})\in\mathcal{M}}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2}),

(1.14)

where the corresponding variational function
E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})
is defined by

\begin{split}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})=&\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}[u_{i}\sqrt{-\Delta}u_{i}+V_{i}(x)u_{i}^{2}]dx-\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{i}\right|^{4}dx\\
&-\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}dx,\end{split}

(1.15)

which is defined on the space

\mathcal{X}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2},\ \ \mathcal{H}_{i}=\{u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}):\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)u^{2}dx<\infty\}\ \ (i=1,2)\quad

with associated norm

\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{i}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left[|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+V_{i}(x)u^{2}\right]dx,\ \ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{X}}^{2}=\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{1}}^{2}+\left\|u\right\|_{\mathcal{H}_{2}}^{2},\ \ i=1,2.

(1.16)

Here,
\mathcal{M}
is the so-called mass constraint, which is given by

\mathcal{M}=\left\{(u,v)\in\mathcal{X}:\int_{\mathbb{R}}u^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}v^{2}dx=1\right\}.

The solution found on
\mathcal{M}
is called a normalized solution, and it represents a kind of mass conservation in physics, which makes our study more meaningful. Inspired by
[
17
]
, we investigate problem (
1.14
) by formulating an appropriate auxiliary minimization problem, based on which we can establish a criterion on the existence of minimizers for the problem (
1.14
), as detailed in our Theorem
2.5
, which plays an important role in proving the main results of this paper. However, our paper will take different approaches as in
[
35
]
to give a proof of Theorem
2.5
, and note that in
[
17
]
it only considered the case of
\beta>0
. Meanwhile, the presence of the non-local operator
\sqrt{-\Delta}
presents a complex challenge in establishing this criterion, the difficulty of which is the selection of test functions and delicate estimates, see (
2.29
)-(
2.30
).
Furthermore, the approaches employed in papers
[
17
]
do not work in proving Theorem
1.6
, as it remains uncertain whether we can derive the same sequence
\left\{y_{\epsilon_{n}}\right\}
from
\left\{(\tilde{w}_{1n},\tilde{w}_{2n})\right\}
, where
\{\tilde{w}_{1n}\}
satisfies (
3.29
) and
\{\tilde{w}_{2n}\}
satisfies (
3.33
). Therefore, we borrow some techniques from
[
33
]
(for pseudo-relativistic operator systems) to overcome this difficulty.

We now define
a^{*}:=\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}
, that is, the square of the
L^{2}
-norm of the unique positive radially symmetric solution (
[
14
,
15
]
) of the following classical equation

\sqrt{-\Delta}u+u=u^{3},\ \ \>u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}}).

(1.17)

Moreover, it is shown in Proposition 3.1 of
[
15
]
that every positive solution of (
1.17
) satisfies the following decay rate

Q(x)=O(|x|^{-2})\ \ \text{as}\ \ |x|\to\infty.

(1.18)

From Theorem 1.1 of
[
11
]
, we know that the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, which corresponds to (
1.17
), satisfies the following type

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u\right|^{4}dx\leq\frac{2}{\left\|Q\right\|^{2}_{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u\right|^{2}dx,\ \ \>u\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}}).

(1.19)

Besides, by Lemma 5.4 of
[
14
]
,
Q(x)
satisfies the following Pohozaev
identity

||\left(-\Delta\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}Q||_{2}^{2}=\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q|^{4}dx.

(1.20)

Now, we introduce the following theorem concerning the existence and non-existence of minimizers of problem (
1.14
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
Q(x)
be the unique positive radially symmetric solution of (
1.17
) and
a^{*}:=\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}
. Suppose that
(\mathcal{D}_{1})
holds, and denote

\beta_{*}:=\sqrt{(a^{*}-a_{1})(a^{*}-a_{2})}\ \ and\ \ \beta^{*}:=\frac{a^{*}-a_{1}}{2}+\frac{a^{*}-a_{2}}{2}.

(1.21)

Then we have:

(i)
If
0<a_{1},a_{2}<a^{*}
and
\beta<\beta_{*}
, there exists a minimizer for problem (
1.14
).

(ii)
If
a_{1}>a^{*}
, or
a_{2}>a^{*}
, or
\beta>\beta^{*}
, the problem (
1.14
) has no minimizer.

Remark 1.2
.

From the definition of
\beta^{+}
in (
2.3
) and the proof of Theorem

, it is evident to see that
\beta\leq 0
is allowed.

Remark 1.3
.

It is an open problem to determine a criterion on the parameters (

) which ensures the existence of minimizers for

.

Remark 1.4
.

It remains unknown whether there are other thresholds except

,
\beta_{*}
and
\beta^{*}
to ensure the existence of minimizers.

Theorem 1.5
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{D}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2})
hold, if

satisfies

\left|a_{1}-a_{2}\right|\leq 2\sqrt{(a^{*}-a_{1})(a^{*}-a_{2})}=2\beta_{*},

then, there exists

such that problem (
1.14
) has a minimizer for any

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose that
(\mathcal{D}_{1})
and
(\mathcal{D}_{2})
hold, if

and

(i)
Problem (

.

(ii)
Problem (
1.14
) has at least a minimizer provided

\hat{e}(a^{*}-\beta,a^{*}-\beta,\beta)<\mathop{\mathrm{inf\mathrm{}}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\big(V_{1}(x)+V_{2}(x)\big).

(1.22)

2.
Existence and non-existence of minimizers

In this section, we introduce an auxiliary minimization problem and then address the proof of Theorem
2.5
, which concerns the existence and nonexistence of minimizers for
\hat{e}(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
. To this end, we first give a compactness result.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
) Suppose
0\leq V_{i}(x)\in L^{\infty}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\displaystyle\lim_{\lvert x\rvert\rightarrow\infty}V_{i}(x)\\
=\infty
,
i=1,2
. Then for all
2\leq q<\infty
, the embedding
\mathcal{H}_{1}\times\mathcal{H}_{2}\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
is compact.

Lemma 2.2
.

If
(u_{1n},u_{2n})\to(u_{1},u_{2})
strongly in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
for
q\in[2,\infty)
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{2}|u_{2n}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{2}|u_{2}|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{in}|^{4}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{i}|^{4}dx,\ \ i=1.2.

Proof.

Since
(u_{1n},u_{2n})
is bounded in
L^{q}(\mathbb{R})\times L^{q}(\mathbb{R})
for
q\in[2,\infty)
, by the Hölder inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{2}|u_{2n}|^{2}dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{2}|u_{2}|^{2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{2}(|u_{2n}|^{2}-|u_{2}|^{2})dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{1n}|^{2}-|u_{1}|^{2})|u_{2}|^{2}dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{4}dx\!\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{2n}|^{2}\!-\!|u_{2}|^{2})^{2}dx\!\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\!+\!\Big(\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{1n}|^{2}\!-\!|u_{1}|^{2})^{2}dx\!\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{2n}|-|u_{2}|)^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{2n}|+|u_{2}|)^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}

(2.1)

\displaystyle+\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{1n}|-|u_{1}|)^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|u_{1n}|+|u_{1}|)^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\Big(\!\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx\!\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{10}||u_{2n}-u_{2}||_{4}+C_{20}||u_{1n}-u_{1}||_{4}.

The assertion follows by letting
n\to\infty
in (
2.1
). Similarly, the remaining proof can be obtained.
∎

Then we introduce the following auxiliary minimization problem, for
a_{1},a_{2}>0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{{R}}
,

\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta):=\inf\left\{J_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2}):u_{i}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\left\|u_{i}\right\|^{2}_{2}=1,i=1,2\right\},

(2.2)

where

\begin{split}J_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})&=\frac{2||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}||^{2}_{2}+2\ ||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2}||^{2}_{2}}{a_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{4}dx+a_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2}\right|^{4}dx+2\beta^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}dx},\\
&\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\beta^{+}=\max\left\{0,\beta\right\}.\end{split}

(2.3)

Next, we present the following properties of
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
. In this paper we set
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
be defined by (
2.2
), then
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
is locally Lipschitz continuous with respect to
(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)\in\mathbb{{R}}^{3}_{+}\setminus\left\{(0,0,0)\right\}
.

Proof.

We first show that

\frac{a^{*}}{\max\left\{a_{1}+\beta,a_{2}+\beta\right\}}\leq\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)\leq\frac{2a^{*}}{a_{1}+a_{2}+2\beta}.

(2.4)

For
Q(x)
being given by (
1.20
), we take
(u_{1},u_{2})=\Big(\frac{Q(x)}{||Q||_{2}},\frac{Q(x)}{||Q||_{2}}\Big)
as a trial function of
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
, then the upper bound of (
2.4
) can be derived from (
1.20
) and (
2.2
).
In addition, for any
(u_{1},u_{2})\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R})}
satisfying
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{i}\right|^{2}dx=1
for
i=1,2
, by applying the Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality (
1.19
), we have

\displaystyle J_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1},u_{2})

\displaystyle=\frac{2||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}||^{2}_{2}+2\ ||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2}||^{2}_{2}}{a_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{4}dx+a_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2}\right|^{4}dx+2\beta\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2}\right|^{2}dx}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a^{*}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2}\right|^{4}dx\Big)}{\big(a_{1}+\beta\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1}\right|^{4}dx+\big(a_{2}+\beta\big)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2}\right|^{4}dx}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a^{*}}{\max\left\{a_{1}+\beta,a_{2}+\beta\right\}},

which implies that the lower bound of (
2.4
) is proved. This completes the proof of (
2.4
).

Let
\left\{(u_{1n},u_{2n})\right\}\subset H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R}})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{{R})}
be a minimizing sequence of
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
. Since (
2.3
) is invariant under the rescaling:
u(x)\longmapsto\lambda^{\frac{1}{2}}u(\lambda x),\lambda>0
, one may suppose that

||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1n}||^{2}_{2}+\ ||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2n}||^{2}_{2}=1\ \ \text{for all}\ \ n\in\mathbb{N}.

(2.5)

It then follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.19
) that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{in}\right|^{4}dx\leq\frac{2}{a^{*}}||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{in}||^{2}_{2}\leq\frac{2}{a^{*}},\ \ i=1,2,

and

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1n}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1n}\right|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{4}dx\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{a^{*}}\big(||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1n}||^{2}_{2}+\ ||(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2n}||^{2}_{2}\big)=\frac{1}{a^{*}}.

Since
\left\{(u_{1n},u_{2n})\right\}
is a minimizing sequence of
\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)
, by (
2.5
), we infer that, for any
(a_{1},a_{2},\beta),(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\in\mathbb{{R}}^{3}_{+}\setminus\left\{(0,0,0)\right\}
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigg[\frac{\hat{a}_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1n}|^{4}dx+\hat{a}_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{4}dx+2\hat{\beta}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1n}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{2}dx}{2\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1n}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2n}\|_{2}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{a_{i}-\hat{a}_{i}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{in}\right|^{4}dx+(\beta-\hat{\beta})\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1n}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{2}dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{n\to\infty}\Bigg[\frac{1}{\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{|a_{i}-\hat{a}_{i}|}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{in}\right|^{4}dx

\displaystyle\quad+|\beta-\hat{\beta}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left|u_{1n}\right|^{2}\left|u_{2n}\right|^{2}dx\Bigg]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\frac{|a_{i}-\hat{a}_{i}|}{a^{*}}+\frac{|\beta-\hat{\beta}|}{a^{*}},

so there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}\leq\frac{3}{a^{*}}\big|(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)-(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\big|.

(2.6)

Taking
\{(\tilde{u}_{1n},\tilde{u}_{2n})\}
as a minimizing sequence of
\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})
, similar to the argument presented above, we further obtain that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)}\leq\frac{3}{a^{*}}\big|(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)-(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\big|,

(2.7)

which together with (
2.6
) yields that

\begin{split}\Big|\frac{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)-\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}\Big|&=\Big|\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)}-\frac{1}{\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})}\Big|\\
&\leq\frac{3}{a^{*}}\Big|(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)-(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\Big|.\end{split}

(2.8)

By (
2.4
) and (
2.8
), a direct calculation gives that

\displaystyle\Big|\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)\!-\!\Gamma(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\Big|\leq\frac{12a^{*}\Big|(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)\!-\!(\hat{a}_{1},\hat{a}_{2},\hat{\beta})\Big|}{(a_{1}+a_{2}+2\beta)(\hat{a}_{1}+\hat{a}_{2}+2\hat{\beta})},

(2.9)

which shows that
\Gamma(\cdot)
is locally Lipschitz continuous in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}\setminus\{(0,0,0)\}
.

Lemma 2.4

Assume that

Proof.

(i) Let

(2.12)

Thus, (i) is proved.

Case 2.

(2.13)

Since
\left\{u_{in}\right\}\subset H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{r}(\mathbb{{R}})

(i=1,2)

Then, it follows from Lemma

\displaystyle\geq\frac{a^{*}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{4}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx\big)}{a_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{4}dx+a_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx+2\beta_{*}\big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{4}dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}}

We now prove (

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|x|^{-4}dx

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-3}.

Then we have

\displaystyle 1\leq A_{R}^{2}=1-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{A_{R}^{2}}{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}}\Big(\varphi^{2}\big(\frac{x}{R}\big)-1\Big)Q^{2}(x)dx\leq 1+O(R^{-3})

(2.35)

as

.

Now, we focus on the translations and scaling of integrals involving the non-local operator
\sqrt{-\Delta}
and show that

\displaystyle\frac{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}}{A_{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{R}\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi_{R}dx=R\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\big)dx.

(2.36)

We first address the translation. By the definition of
\sqrt{-\Delta}
given in (

), we have

\displaystyle\frac{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}}{A_{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\psi_{R}\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi_{R}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R\varphi(x-x_{0})Q(R(x-x_{0}))\mathcal{F}^{-1}\Big(|\xi|\mathcal{F}\big(\varphi(y-x_{0})Q(R(y-x_{0}))\big)\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}R\varphi(x)Q(Rx)dx\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{i(x+x_{0})\cdot\xi}|\xi|d\xi\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi\cdot(y+x_{0})}\varphi(y)Q(Ry)dy

(2.37)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R\varphi(x)Q(Rx)\mathcal{F}^{-1}\big(|\xi|\mathcal{F}(\varphi(y)Q(Ry))\big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R\varphi(x)Q(Rx)\sqrt{-\Delta}\big(\varphi(x)Q(Rx)\big)dx.

On the other hand, regarding the scaling of integrals, by applying the Plancherel theorem and (
1.3
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}R\varphi(x)Q(Rx)\sqrt{-\Delta}\big(\varphi(x)Q(Rx)\big)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}R|\xi|\mathcal{F}\big(\varphi(y)Q(Ry)\big)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \frac{R}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|d\xi\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi\cdot y}\varphi(y)Q(Ry)dy\Big)^{2}\end{aligned}

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|d\xi\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\xi\cdot y}\varphi(R^{-1}y)Q(y)dy\Big)^{2}

(2.38)

\displaystyle=R\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\xi|\mathcal{F}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}y)Q(y)\big)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=R\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\big)dx.

It then follows from the (
2.37
) and (
2.38
) that (
2.36
) holds.
Equation (
2.36
) also implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}}{A_{R}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{R}\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi_{R}dx

\displaystyle=R\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\big)dx

\displaystyle=R\bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx

(2.39)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(R^{-1}x)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}\Big(\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)\Big)dx\bigg)

\displaystyle=:R\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx+RA+RB,

A:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx
,

.

We now turn to estimate terms

and
B

(2.40)

Combining the decay rate of

(
1.18
) and (
2.40
) gives that

\displaystyle\left|A\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}\left|\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)\right|dx

\displaystyle\leq C(R^{-7}+R^{-3}).

In order to estimate the term

(2.42)

Here

and
\varphi_{R}

(2.43)

Obviously,

\displaystyle\left|B\right|

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)\varphi(R^{-1}x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\Big|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\big(\varphi(R^{-1}x)-1\big)Q(x)[\sqrt{-\Delta},\varphi_{R}]Q(x)dx\Big|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}\Big(|Q(x)||\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)|+|Q(x)|\big|[\sqrt{-\Delta},\varphi_{R}]Q(x)\big|\Big)dx

\displaystyle=B_{1}+B_{2}.

According to the decay rate of

1.18

It follows from the Hölder inequality and (

\displaystyle\left|B_{2}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}Q^{2}(x)dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|[\sqrt{-\Delta},\varphi_{R}]Q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B_{\frac{1}{2}R}}|x|^{-4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla\varphi_{R}\|_{\infty}\|Q\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq CR^{-\frac{3}{2}}R^{-1}\|Q\|_{2}^{2}

As a consequence,

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}}\psi_{R}\sqrt{-\Delta}\psi_{R}dx\\
&\leq\frac{R}{\|Q\|_{2}^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q(x)\sqrt{-\Delta}Q(x)dx+O(R^{-\frac{3}{2}}).\end{split}

Next, we consider the following term,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{R}|^{4}dx

\displaystyle=:\frac{A_{R}^{4}R}{\|Q\|_{2}^{4}}F,

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi^{4}(R^{-1}x)Q^{4}(x)dx

\displaystyle=:\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{4}(x)dx+F_{1}.

By the decay rate of

\displaystyle|F_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\Big|\varphi^{4}(R^{-1}x)-1\Big|Q^{4}(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}\setminus B\frac{1}{2}R}Q^{4}(x)dx

Therefore, according to (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{{R}}}|\psi_{R}|^{4}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{R}^{4}R}{||Q||_{2}^{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{4}(x)dx+F_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{R}{\|Q\|_{2}^{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{4}(x)dx-O\big(R^{-7})\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2R}{a^{*}}-O\big(R^{-6}\big).

Moreover, by (

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}|\psi_{R}|^{4}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{A_{R}^{4}R}{||Q||_{2}^{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{4}(x)dx+F_{1}\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{\|Q\|_{2}^{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}Q^{4}(x)+|F_{1}|\Big)\Big(1+O(R^{-3})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{R}{||Q||_{2}^{4}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{{R}}}Q^{4}(x)dx+Q\big(R^{-3}\big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{R}{||Q||_{2}^{4}}\int_{\mathbb{{R}}}Q^{4}(x)+O\big(R^{-2}\big)

\displaystyle=\frac{2R}{a^{*}}+O\big(R^{-2}\big).

Consequently, (
2.29

1.20
) and (

Theorem 2.5
.

Suppose that condition
(\mathcal{D}_{1})
holds, and let

,

, then we have:

Proof.

(i) Let

\begin{split}E_{a_{1},a_{2},\beta}(u_{1n},u_{2n})&\geq(1-\frac{1}{\Gamma(a_{1},a_{2},\beta)})\Big[\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1n}\|_{2}^{2}+\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2n}\|_{2}^{2}\Big]\\
&\quad\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(x)|u_{in}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

(2.47)

(ii) Suppose now that

\begin{split}&\frac{2\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{1}\|_{2}^{2}+2\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{2}\|_{2}^{2}}{a_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{4}dx+a_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{2}|^{4}dx+2\beta^{+}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{1}|^{2}|u_{2}|^{2}dx}\\

(2.48)

For

.

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}w_{1}|^{2}dx+\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{2}dx=a_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{4}dx.

(3.46)

Combining (
3.45
) and (
3.46
), yields that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}w_{1}|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{2}dx=\frac{a_{1}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{4}dx.

(3.47)

It then follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.19
) and (
3.47
) that

\frac{2}{a_{1}}\|w_{1}\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{4}dx\leq\frac{2}{a^{*}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}w_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\|w_{1}\|_{2}^{2}=\frac{2}{a^{*}}\|w_{1}\|_{2}^{4}.

(3.48)

Since
a_{1}=a^{*}-\beta\in(0,a^{*})
, we then obtain from (
3.48
) that

\|w_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\geq\frac{a^{*}}{a_{1}}>1,

which contradicts that
\|w_{1}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\|w_{1n}\|_{2}^{2}=1
. Therefore,
w_{2}\not\equiv 0
, and
(w_{1},w_{2})
forms a nontrivial solution of the system (
3.43
).

Let

\hat{\mathcal{X}}=H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\ \ \|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+u^{2})dx\ \ \text{and}

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}=\Big\{(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}:u,v\neq 0,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|u|^{4}+\beta|v|^{2}|u|^{2})dx,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|v|^{4}+\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2})dx\Big\},

and

\hat{J}\left(u,v\right):=\frac{1}{2}(\left\|u\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\left\|v\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|u|^{4}+a_{1}|v|^{4}+2\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2}).

(3.49)

For
Q>0
being a positive radially symmetric solution of (
1.17
), we know by Lemma
4.1
and Lemma
4.2
in the Appendix that

(u_{0},v_{0})=\begin{cases}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{*}}}Q(x),\frac{1}{\sqrt{a^{*}}}Q(x)),&a_{1}\neq\beta\ \ \text{and}\ \ a_{1}+\beta=a^{*},\\[8.61108pt]
(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}Q\sin\theta,\frac{1}{\sqrt{a_{1}}}Q\cos\theta),&\theta\in(0,2\pi)\ \ \text{and}\ \ a_{1}=\beta=\frac{a^{*}}{2},\end{cases}

(3.50)

is a minimizer for the following problem

\inf\{\hat{J}(u,v):(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}\}.

According to Lemma 5.4 in
[
14
]
, a solution
(u,v)
of system (
3.43
) satisfies the following Pohozaev identity

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}dx=\frac{1}{2}\int\limits_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}(a_{1}|u|^{4}+a_{1}|v|^{4}+2\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2})dx,

(3.51)

which, combined with
(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, shows that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}v|^{2}dx&=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}dx\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|u|^{4}+a_{1}|v|^{4}+2\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2})dx.\end{split}

(3.52)

Combining (
3.49
), (
3.50
) and (
3.52
) gives that

\hat{J}(w_{1},w_{2})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{1}|^{2}+|w_{2}|^{2}dx\geq\hat{J}(u_{0},v_{0})=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{0}|^{2}+|v_{0}|^{2}dx=1.

(3.53)

Since
\|w_{in}\|_{2}^{2}\equiv 1
and
w_{i}\not\equiv 0
, we have
0<\|w_{i}\|_{2}^{2}\leq 1
, and then (
3.53
) implies that

\|w_{i}\|_{2}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|w_{i}|^{2}=1,\ \ i=1,2,

(3.54)

which means that

w_{in}\overset{n}{\to}w_{i}\text{ strongly in }L^{2}(\mathbb{R}),\ \ i=1,2.

(3.55)

Given that
{\left\{\epsilon_{n}y_{\epsilon_{n}}\right\}}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}
,
one may assume that
\epsilon_{n}y_{\epsilon_{n}}\xrightarrow{n}h_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
. Due to the Fatou lemma, along with (
3.35
), (
3.54
) and (
3.55
), we can infer that

\displaystyle\hat{e}(a^{*}-\beta,a^{*}-\beta,\beta)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\lim_{n\to\infty}V_{i}(\epsilon_{n}x+\epsilon_{n}y_{\epsilon_{n}})|w_{in}|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}}V_{i}(h_{0})|w_{i}|^{2}dx=V_{1}(h_{0})+V_{2}(h_{0}),

which contradicts (
1.22
). Therefore,
\{(u_{1n},u_{2n})\}
is bounded in
\mathcal{X}
, thereby completing the proof of Theorem
1.6
(ii).

4.
Appendix

This appendix focuses on the proofs of the following lemmas, which are dedicated to proving Theorem
1.6
(ii). We now come to consider the following system

\begin{cases}\sqrt{-\Delta}u+u=au^{3}+\beta v^{2}u,\ \ &(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}=H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})\times H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}),\\
\sqrt{-\Delta}v+v=av^{3}+\beta u^{2}v,\ \ &a,\ \beta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.\end{cases}

(4.1)

The system (
4.1
) can be described by the following constrained
minimization problem

\rho_{1}:=\inf\left\{\hat{J}(u,v):(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}\right\},

(4.2)

where

\hat{J}(u,v)=\frac{1}{2}(\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a|u|^{4}+a|v|^{4}+2\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2})dx\ \ \text{and}

(4.3)

\displaystyle\hat{\mathcal{M}}=\Big\{(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{X}}:u,v\neq 0,

\displaystyle\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|u|^{4}+\beta|v|^{2}|u|^{2})dx,

\displaystyle\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a_{1}|v|^{4}+\beta|u|^{2}|v|^{2})dx\Big\}.

Here,

\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u|^{2}+u^{2})dx~\text{and}~\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}v|^{2}+v^{2})dx.

Then, the minimizers of (
4.2
) are ground state solutions for (
4.1
).

For any
(u,v)\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

\hat{J}(u,v)=\frac{1}{4}\|u\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|v\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

If
k>0
and
s>0
satisfy

ak+\beta s=1,\text{ and }\ as+\beta k=1,

(4.4)

we claim that

\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right):=\left(\sqrt{k}Q,\sqrt{s}Q\right)\in\hat{\mathcal{M}},

where
Q>0
is a solution of equation (
1.17
).

It can be derived
from Pohozaev identity (
1.20
) that

\left\|u_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\|\sqrt{k}Q\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=2k\left\|Q\right\|_{2}^{2}=2ka^{*},\ \ \text{and if }ak+\beta s=1,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a|u_{0}|^{4}\!+\!\beta|v_{0}|^{2}|u_{0}|^{2})\!=\!(ak^{2}+\beta ks)\int_{\mathbb{R}}|Q|^{4}dx\!=\!2k(ak+\beta s)a^{*}\\
\!=\!2ka^{*}\!=\!\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Similarly, we have

\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a|v_{0}|^{4}+\beta|u_{0}|^{2}|v_{0}|^{2})dx=2sa^{*},\ \ \text{if}\ as+\beta k=1.

and then
(u_{0},v_{0})\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
. It follows from the definition of (
4.2
) that

\rho_{1}\leq\hat{J}(u_{0},v_{0})=\frac{1}{4}\|u_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|v_{0}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\frac{a^{*}}{2}(k+s).

(4.5)

Lemma 4.1
.

Assuming that
k>0
,
s>0
and (
4.4
) is satisfied, then
(u_{0},v_{0})=(\sqrt{k}Q,\sqrt{s}Q)
is a ground state solution for system (
4.1
) if
a\neq\beta
.

Proof.

We now let
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\hat{\mathcal{X}}
be a minimizing sequence for (
4.2
), that is,

\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\subset\hat{\mathcal{M}}\ \ \text{and}\ \ \hat{J}(u_{n},v_{n})=\frac{1}{4}\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|v_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle n\to\infty}}{{\to}}\rho_{1}.

(4.6)

It follows from the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
1.19
) that, for
u_{n}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{n}|^{4}dx\leq\frac{2}{a^{*}}\|(-\Delta)^{\frac{1}{4}}u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\|u_{n}\|_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2a^{*}}\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{4}.

Since
\{(u_{n},v_{n})\}\in\hat{\mathcal{M}}
, we have

\displaystyle e_{u_{n}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2a^{*}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{n}|^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2a^{*}}\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2a^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a|u_{n}|^{4}+\beta|v_{n}|^{2}|u_{n}|^{2})dx,

(4.7)

\displaystyle e_{v_{n}}

\displaystyle:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2a^{*}}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{R}}|v_{n}|^{4}dx\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2a^{*}}\|v_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=\frac{1}{2a^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(a|v_{n}|^{4}+\beta|u_{n}|^{2}|v_{n}|^{2})dx.

(4.8)

In addition, using the Hölder inequality, we yield that

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{n}|^{2}|v_{n}|^{2}dx\leq 2a^{*}e_{u_{n}}e_{v_{n}},

(4.9)

which, together with (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), implies that

\begin{cases}2a^{*}e_{u_{n}}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq 2a^{*}\Big(ae_{u_{n}}^{2}+\beta e_{u_{n}}e_{v_{n}}\Big),\\
2a^{*}e_{v_{n}}\leq\|v_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}\leq 2a^{*}\Big(ae_{v_{n}}^{2}+\beta e_{u_{n}}e_{v_{n}}\Big).\end{cases}

(4.10)

Combining (
4.5
), (
4.6
) and (
4.10
), we infer that

2a^{*}(e_{u_{n}}+e_{v_{n}})\leq\|u_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}+\|v_{n}\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}=4\rho_{1}+o(1)\leq 2a^{*}(k+s)+o(1).

(4.11)

This and (
4.10
) give that

\begin{cases}e_{u_{n}}+e_{v_{n}}\leq k+s+o(1),\\
1\leq ae_{u_{n}}+\beta e_{v_{n}},\\
1\leq\beta e_{u_{n}}+ae_{v_{n}}.\end{cases}

(4.12)

We now define
\eta_{u_{n}}=e_{u_{n}}-k\mathrm{~and~}\eta_{v_{n}}=e_{v_{n}}-s
. By (
4.4
), we can rewrite (
4.12
) as

\begin{cases}\eta_{u_{n}}+\eta_{v_{n}}\leq o(1),\\
a\eta_{u_{n}}+\beta\eta_{v_{n}}\geq 0,\\
\beta\eta_{u_{n}}+a\eta_{v_{n}}\geq 0.\end{cases}

(4.13)

Set

\vartheta_{n}=\Big\{\big(\eta_{u_{n}},\eta_{v_{n}}\big):\eta_{u_{n}}+\eta_{v_{n}}\leq o(1),a\eta_{u_{n}}+\beta\eta_{v_{n}}\geq 0,\beta\eta_{u_{n}}+a\eta_{v_{n}}\geq 0\Big\}.

It is easy to see that, for each

,
\vartheta_{n}
forms a triangular region with the vertex
(0,0)
, and its diameter goes to 0 as

if

\eta_{u_{n}}=e_{u_{n}}-k\xrightarrow{n}0,\ \eta_{v_{n}}=e_{v_{n}}-s\xrightarrow{n}0,

This together with (
4.11

\rho_{1}=\frac{a^{*}}{2}(k+s).

Then, it follows from (
4.5

\hat{J}(u_{0},v_{0})=\frac{a^{*}}{2}(k+s)=\rho_{1}
, which implies that
(u_{0},v_{0})=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt{k}Q,\sqrt{s}Q\end{pmatrix}

Lemma 4.2
.

For any
\theta\in(0,2\pi)

\left(u_{0},v_{0}\right)=\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}Q\sin\theta,\frac{1}{\sqrt{a}}Q\cos\theta\right)

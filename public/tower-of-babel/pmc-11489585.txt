Online bilateral matching methodology for anti-epidemic resources based on spatial transmission risk
Authors: Zhiyong Wu, Sulin Pang
Published: 2024

ABSTRACT
This paper focuses on the online anti-epidemic resource allocation between demanders and suppliers considering the epidemic spatial spread. The spatial crowdsourcing with sharing platform is an effective way for anti-epidemic resource allocation, and a reasonable online matching strategy can improve the efficiency of resource utilization. The paper proposes online matching heuristic strategy (HSTRF-LRLUF strategy) and designs an online batch bilateral matching algorithm for anti-epidemic resources, which considers the impact of grid spatial aggregation and diffusion risk of emerging infectious diseases. The population distribution within grids and the commuting patterns between grids can provide decision support for selecting online matching strategies of anti-epidemic resources. A larger matching time window focusing on the spatial transmission risk (TR) of the epidemic can obtain better matching results. However, with a smaller matching time window, the decision makers can focus on spatial agglomeration risk (OR) or spatial diffusion risk (IR). The paper effectively combines the spatial crowdsourcing model with the anti-epidemic resource allocation to achieve the allocation of emergency resources to individuals. A combined anti-epidemic resource online matching heuristic strategies is designed from the spatial agglomeration risk and the spatial diffusion risk. Decision makers can dynamically adjust the online matching strategies of anti-epidemic resources by evaluating the spatial agglomeration risk, the spatial diffusion risk, and the overall spatial transmission risk based on the real-time spread of the epidemic and the supply of anti-epidemic resources.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-024-75534-7.

Introduction

The speed and variety of emerging infectious diseases (EIDs) have shown a rapid growth trend1. The continuous rise in human mobility, as well as the changes in population size and density, have led to the rapid spread of EIDs2,3. The spatial-temporal spread of infectious diseases is mainly influenced by factors such as the seasonal characteristics of the disease4,5, regional contact networks3,6, group heterogeneity7, and human mobility8–12. In the fight against the EIDs, anti-epidemic resources face prominent shortages in many countries and communities. As a result, the effective allocation of anti-epidemic resources has become a critical public health challenge.

The research on the allocation of anti-epidemic resources mainly focuses on social utility and equity13–16. Chen, et al.14 proposes an infectious disease model that unifies utility and equity in vaccine distribution, and argues that social utility and equity can improve simultaneously when the most vulnerable communities have priority access to vaccines. Emanuel, et al.17 proposes six specific recommendations for allocating medical resources in the Covid-19, such as maximizing benefits, treating equally, and giving priority to the worst off. Han, et al.13 devises a meta-population epidemiological model that considers both global vaccine distribution and human mobility, and proposes the incentive mechanism of enlightened self-interest to incentivize vaccine equity. Ye, et al.16 proposes a multi-strain metapopulation model to analyze equitable and inequitable global vaccine allocation strategies, and argues that equitable vaccine allocation strategies can inhibit the spread of the virus. Moreover, different epidemic models are constructed to correlate the epidemic transmission risk with factors such as countries with different income levels18, the global distribution of resources15, human mobility patterns19, demographic profiles of different communities20, so as to simulate and evaluate the impact of different resource allocation methods on the effectiveness of epidemic prevention and control.

Anti-epidemic resources are allocated with coarse granularity among different countries, communities, or populations through demand forecasting. It helps to assess the overall demand for resources in a community or group, but ignores individual resource needs. Our research provides marginal contributions in terms of individual resource allocation. To individual resource demand, we combine grid governance21 with mobile computing22,23 to construct a finer-grained allocation of anti-epidemic resources. A multi-stage online bilateral matching model is proposed based on the spatial transmission risk of epidemic by dividing urban space into grids, which considers the heterogeneity of grid and supply-demand and spatial-temporal dynamics of resources. Due to differences in population aggregation and spatial connectivity, grids have different risks of epidemic spatial spread. To overcome this, we propose the spatial agglomeration risk and the spatial diffusion risk indices to measure the spatial risk of the grid. Accordingly, we examine three sorting strategies that prioritize girds, and also design resource prioritization matching heuristic strategies and methods that simultaneously improves social utility and equity.

The rest of this paper is organized as follows. “The model” section formally formulates our problem and proposes a maximized matching model. In “Methods” section, many-to-many bipartite matching heuristic strategies and algorithms with capacity based on spatial transmission is proposed. Extensive experiments on synthetic data are presented in “Evaluation” section. Finally, we present our conclusion in “Conclusion” section.

The model

Problem definition

The pandemic of the epidemic is largely caused by human social and economic activities. Grid management plays an important role in epidemic prevention and control, and has increasingly become a new urban governance model in China21. The research problem has the following characteristics. Firstly, the spatial spread of the epidemic is mainly affected by the combination of the degree of population concentration within the grid and the degree of connection between the grids. If there is a higher degree of population aggregation within a grid, the epidemic is considered to have a higher risk of epidemic aggregation within the grid, while a closer degree of connectivity between grids indicates closer social and economic activities between grids and a higher risk of epidemic diffusion among them. Secondly, each grid is heterogeneous. Due to differences in population aggregation and spatial connectivity, grids have different risks of epidemic transmission. After an outbreak, the government or public health agencies usually take preventive and control interventions to stop the spread of the epidemic. Spatial transmission risk of the epidemic

Human mobility is a key component of the large-scale spatial spread model of infectious diseases10. There is a strong correlation between population flow and the number of infections. Hence, we mainly consider the spatial agglomeration and diffusion risk of the epidemic based on the two dimensions of population aggregation within the grid and population mobility between grids.

Grid connectivity

It is a measure of the ability of one grid to influence other grids in terms of spatial spread of the epidemic. Human mobility models can be used to describe and predict the risk of outbreak transmission between different grids, where gravity model and radiation model are commonly used to predict the spatial spread of outbreaks10. The gravity model is a parameterized model that fits parameters based on historical data24. It assumes that the flow between two regions is determined by the population and distance between the two regions, which is inversely proportional to the physical distance25. The radiation model is a parameter-free model in which the population flow between two regions is completely determined by the population density between them. With radiation model, the population attractiveness and repulsion of a given area can be calculated, and it can be inferred from which areas people may move to and from which areas they may move to26. Mobile communication technology can measure the number of commutes in real time with the help of smart devices and GPS location data. Hence, this paper combines mobile positioning data with radiation model, and constructs origin destination Matrices (OD Matrices) using commuting data to calculate the risk of epidemic transmission caused by human mobility between different grids. Evaluation

Experimental setup

Datasets

Numerical experiments are performed on synthetic datasets in our paper. The decision period T is divided into 144 times matching windows. Suppose there are 15 grids in an area, each with an edge length of 1, in which the population distribution of the grid follows Uniform and Zipf distribution forms (See Fig. S2 and Table S2), respectively. Table 1 shows the parameter settings for the experiment. Meanwhile, the total population in this region is 6000, with a cross grid commuting population coefficient of \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$FP=0.2$$\end{document}. Online matching experiment

Determine the matching time by the size of the time window and generate an online matching bipartite graph network. This paper designs matching experiments under different population distribution characteristics and spatial transmission risk scenarios in grids, and further considers the impact of matching time window size on matching results.

Algorithm comparison

This paper compares three following online matching algorithms: HSTRF-LRUF-based bilateral matching greedy algorithm, HSTRF-LRLUF-based bilateral matching Hungarian algorithm and Greedy and Hungarian hybrid algorithm.

Experimental results

The impact of different spatial transmission risks on matching results

Figures 7 and 8 shows the online matching results of anti-epidemic resources using different HSTRF-LRLUF matching strategies (IR, OR, and TR). From the mean value, the online matching rate improves with the increase of the matching time window size, regardless of which HSTRF-LRLUF resource matching strategy is adopted. In the scenario of uniform population distribution of grids, as shown in Fig. 7, the HSTRF-LRLUF strategy with TR needs to be paid attention to in the larger matching time window size (such as \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$bs=6$$\end{document}). In addition, The HSTRF-LRLUF strategy with OR can obtain better matching results when the matching time window size is small (such \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$bs=2$$\end{document}) and IR should be adopted in \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$bs=4$$\end{document}. Conclusion

This paper proposes the ST-MSOBM model to solve the problem of resource allocation for epidemic prevention and control with the goal of maximizing the matching quantity of resources between suppliers and demanders. The model takes into account the heterogeneity of demanders and suppliers, and matching priorities. The paper proposes matching strategies considering spatial transmission risk of the epidemic, which contains the HSTRF, LRLUF and DRGRM. The paper also designs the online batch bilateral matching algorithm based on spatial transmission risk of epidemic. In online matching, the paper uses the average matching rate as a focus indicator to examine the impact of different population distributions in the grid, different HSTRF matching strategies, and different algorithms on the matching results. The main findings and managerial insights can be summarized as follows. Firstly, based on the impact of spatial population distribution and commuting on the spread of the epidemic in the urban grid, this paper proposes a calculation method for spatial agglomeration risk and spatial diffusion risk coefficient in combination with the real-time epidemic transmission index \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Rt$$\end{document}. It is found that the STOBBM algorithm with HSTRF-LRLUF strategy has obvious advantages over the baseline algorithm based on distance priority, which can provide decision support for the allocation of anti-epidemic resources at the individual level. Secondly, the paper also finds that different HSTRF matching strategies perform differently under the matching time window. A larger matching time window focusing on the spatial transmission risk (TR) of the epidemic can obtain better matching results, but it may also cause the large-scale spread of the epidemic, resulting in the subsequent expansion of the demand for anti-epidemic resources, which in turn leads to the tight supply of anti-epidemic resources. With a smaller matching time window size, the decision makers can focus on spatial agglomeration risk (OR) or spatial diffusion risk (IR). In a word, decision makers can dynamically adjust the HSTRF matching strategy by evaluating the risk of spatial agglomeration, spatial diffusion and total spatial transmission of the epidemic according to the real-time transmission (\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$Rt$$\end{document}) of the epidemic and the supply of anti-epidemic resources. Lastly, by comparing the supply of anti-epidemic resources, the STOBBM algorithm can prioritize matching anti-epidemic resources to the affected areas based on the Greedy method when the supply of anti-epidemic resources is insufficient, which can significantly improve the matching efficiency of resources in terms of average matching rate and matching time. In the case of sufficient supply of anti-epidemic resources, although the Hungarian method is inferior to the Greedy method in terms of matching time, it has obvious advantages in the average matching rate.

Although the model proposed can solve the problem of online matching of anti-epidemic resources, there are still some limitations that need to be further explored in the future. Firstly, the paper does not consider the influence of the actual environment of the traffic road on the matching performance time, which may have an impact on the overall matching time. Secondly, In the experimental analysis section, the paper uses random synthetic data to obtain accurate matching, and for the large-scale epidemic prevention and control resource matching problem, the algorithm proposed in this paper may not be effectively satisfied, and future research can design an effective algorithm to deal with the large-scale anti-epidemic resource matching problem. In addition, the paper only considers the impact of time and space factors on the matching of anti-epidemic resources, while other factors such as resource supply capacity and cross-regional allocation of resource are also of great research significance.

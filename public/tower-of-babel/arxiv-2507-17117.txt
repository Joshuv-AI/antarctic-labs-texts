Explicit Formulas for Estimating Trace of Reduced Density Matrix Powers via Single-Circuit Measurement Probabilities
Rui-Qi Zhang 1 , Xiao-Qi Liu 1 , Jing Wang 1, Ming Li 1, Shu-Qian Shen 1, Shao-Ming Fei 2

Abstract
In the fields of quantum mechanics and quantum information science, the traces of reduced density matrix powers play a crucial role in the study of quantum systems and have numerous important applications. In this paper, we propose a universal framework to simultaneously estimate the traces of the 2 2 nd to the n n th powers of a reduced density matrix using a single quantum circuit with n n copies of the quantum state. Specifically, our approach leverages the controlled SWAP test and establishes explicit formulas connecting measurement probabilities to these traces. We further develop two algorithms: a purely quantum method and a hybrid quantum-classical approach combining Newton-Girard iteration. Rigorous analysis via Hoeffding inequality demonstrates the method’s efficiency, requiring only M = O ⁡ ( 1 ϵ 2 ​ log ⁡ ( n δ ) ) M=O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\log(\frac{n}{\delta})\right) measurements to achieve precision ϵ \epsilon with confidence 1 − δ 1-\delta . Additionally, we explore various applications including the estimation of nonlinear functions and the representation of entanglement measures. Numerical simulations are conducted for two maximally entangled states, the GHZ state and the W state, to validate the proposed method.

Let

\displaystyle F(\lambda)=

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}\left({\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lambda}-\lambda_{i}\right)=\sum_{k=0}^{r}(-1)^{k}a_{k}{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lambda}^{r-k},

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\rho^{r+l}=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(-1)^{j-1}a_{j}{\rm tr}\rho^{r+l-j}.

Then we can give an estimate to an arbitrary power trace of the reduced density matrix.

\epsilon
with a very high probability.

Experimental results demonstrate that multiple power traces can be efficiently and reliably estimated through multiple measurements using a single quantum circuit.

VII
VII. Conclusions

The traces of the reduced density matrix powers play a crucial role in the study of quantum systems and have many important applications. In this Letter, we proposed a unified quantum framework to estimate
\left\{{\rm tr}(\rho_{A}^{k})\right\}_{k=1}^{n}
via a single circuit, resolving the redundancy of prior multi-circuit approaches.
Specifically, we derive trace expressions for the powers of the reduced density matrix from the measured probabilities of controlled SWAP test, which corrects errors in
Jin
. Estimating the traces of the
2
nd to the
n
th power of the reduced density matrix, we propose two algorithms for estimating them. The first algorithm demands
n
quantum copy states within a single quantum circuit. By contrast, the second algorithm combines quantum circuits with classical iteration. This approach only requires
r
quantum copy states, however, it relies on having prior knowledge of the rank of the quantum state. Through the Hoeffding inequality, we rigorously derive a unified upper bound on measurements, which reveals the method’s efficiency and high reliability with finite samples.
In addition, we explore various applications including the estimation of nonlinear functions (such as the trace of exponential function, Von Neumann entropy, and a cost function applied in the preparation of variational quantum Gibbs states) and the representation of entanglement measures. We perform numerical simulations for two maximally entangled states.

Future research could focus on deploying the framework in quantum machine learning for kernel estimation and in variational quantum algorithm for Gibbs state preparation. Simultaneously, it is crucial to delve into more significant applications founded on the traces of reduced density matrix powers.

Lemma 1.

Consider
k
-partite quantum systems
\otimes_{i=1}^{k}\mathcal{H}_{i}
. Let
\rho_{i}\in\mathcal{H}_{i}
be
k
identical quantum states, i.e.,
\rho_{i}=\rho\ (i=1,2,\cdots,k)
and let
S_{i}:\mathcal{H}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{i+1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
denotes the SWAP operator acting on
\mathcal{H}_{i}
and
\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
.
Then we can obtain

{\rm tr}(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}\otimes_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i})={\rm tr}(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i})={\rm tr}(\rho^{k}),

where
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k-1}
is a rearrangement of
1,2,\cdots,k-1
.

Proof.
Let
\rho_{i}\ (i=1,2,\cdots,k)

be
k
identical quantum states with spectral decomposition

\rho_{i}=\sum_{j_{i}}a_{j_{i}}|j_{i}\rangle\langle j_{i}|,

where
\sum_{j_{i}}a_{j_{i}}=1
.
First, we consider a special case of
S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}
, i.e.,
S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}
, then we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}\sum_{j_{1}}a_{j_{1}}|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes\sum_{j_{2}}a_{j_{2}}|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes\cdots\otimes\sum_{j_{k}}a_{j_{k}}|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{k}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{k}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{k}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{k}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{k}|).

Since
S_{i}
is an operator acting on the adjacent subspaces
\mathcal{H}_{i}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{i+1}
, according to the properties of tensor product and trace, we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{k}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{{k-1}}\rangle\langle j_{k}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{1}|){\rm tr}(|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{2}|)\cdots{\rm tr}(|j_{{k-1}}\rangle\langle j_{k}|),

where for the second equation we have used the expression
{\rm tr}(A\otimes B)={\rm tr}A{\rm tr}B

Note that

{\rm tr}(|j_{m}\rangle\langle j_{n}|)=\delta_{j_{m},j_{n}}.

Therefore we have

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{k}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}{\rm tr}(|j_{k}\rangle\langle j_{1}|){\rm tr}(|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{2}|)

\displaystyle\cdots{\rm tr}(|j_{k-1}\rangle\langle j_{k}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{k}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{k}}\delta_{j_{k},j_{1}}\delta_{j_{1},j_{2}}\cdots\delta_{j_{k-1},j_{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}}a_{j_{1}}^{k}={\rm tr}(\prod_{i=1}^{k}\rho_{i})={\rm tr}(\rho^{k}).

Next we consider the general case
S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}
with an arbitrary order. Actually
S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}
is a permutation of
S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}
corresponding to an element of the permutation group. Then there exists a corresponding permutation matrix
P
, satisfying

S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}=P^{-1}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1})P,

where the permutation matrix
P
is orthogonal. Then we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(P^{-1}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1})P\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}P(\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})P^{-1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}P(\rho\otimes\rho\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho)P^{-1}).

Since
P
is an orthogonal permutation matrix, we get

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{1}S_{2}\cdots S_{k-1}\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{k})={\rm tr}(\rho^{k})

\hfill\Box

Since
i_{1},i_{2},\cdots,i_{k-1}
is a rearrangement of
1,2,\cdots,k-1
, in fact, when the operator
S_{i_{1}}S_{i_{2}}\cdots S_{i_{k-1}}
acts on multiple copy quantum states, the successive action of all operators is equivalent to a shift in the relative positions of all the original quantum states. For example, when
k=5
, if
i_{1}=2,i_{2}=4,i_{3}=1,i_{4}=3
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm tr}(S_{2}S_{4}S_{1}S_{3}\rho_{1}\otimes\rho_{2}\otimes\cdots\otimes\rho_{5})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{5}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{5}}{\rm tr}(S_{2}S_{4}S_{1}S_{3}|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{2}|

\displaystyle\otimes\cdots\otimes|j_{5}\rangle\langle j_{5}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{5}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{5}}{\rm tr}(|j_{2}\rangle\langle j_{1}|\otimes|j_{4}\rangle\langle j_{2}|\otimes|j_{1}\rangle\langle j_{3}|

\displaystyle\otimes|j_{5}\rangle\langle j_{4}|\otimes|j_{3}\rangle\langle j_{5}|)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},\cdots,j_{5}}a_{j_{1}}a_{j_{2}}\cdots a_{j_{5}}\delta_{j_{2},j_{1}}\delta_{j_{4},j_{2}}\delta_{j_{1},j_{3}}\delta_{j_{5},j_{4}}\delta_{j_{3},j_{5}}

\displaystyle\sum_{j_{1}}a_{j_{1}}^{5}={\rm tr}(\rho^{5}).

\hfill\Box

Theorem 6.
For any
N
-qubit quantum state
\rho
with reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
on subsystem
A
, consider performing a controlled SWAP test in Figure
1
with
n
copies of
\rho
, yielding measurement results
\textbf{z}\in\left\{0,1\right\}^{n-1}
. Let
\left\{{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}\right\}_{k=2}^{n}
denote the set of power traces to be estimated. The following statistical guarantees hold:

(1) There exist random variables
\left\{\hat{T}_{k}\right\}_{k=2}^{n}
such that with
M=O\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\log(\frac{n}{\delta})\right)
independent experiments, the following holds simultaneously:

\displaystyle{\rm P}\left(\bigcap_{k=2}^{n}\left|\hat{T}_{k}-{\rm tr}(\rho_{A}^{k})\right|\leq\epsilon\right)\geq 1-\delta,

where
\epsilon>0
is the maximum allowable error and
\delta\in(0,1)
is the confidence level.

(2) For any
k\in\left\{2,3,\ldots,n\right\}
, the variance of the estimator
\hat{T}_{k}
satisfies

\displaystyle{\rm Var}(\hat{T}_{k})=\frac{4}{M}p_{k}(1-p_{k}),

where
p_{k}=\frac{{\rm tr}(\rho_{A}^{k})+1}{2}
.

Proof.
By Theorem 3,
{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}
can be expressed as a linear combination of measurement probabilities:

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}=2\sum_{c^{\emph{\textbf{z}}}_{1,2\cdots,k-1}\ is\ even}p(\emph{\textbf{z}})-1.

Suppose we perform
M
independent experiments where measuring the control qubits in each experiment yields a bitstring
\emph{\textbf{z}}=z_{1}z_{2}\cdots z_{n-1}
. For each power
k\in\left\{2,3,\dots,n\right\}
, define the indicator function:

\displaystyle Y_{i}^{(k)}=\begin{cases}2&\text{if the $i$-th measurement result $\emph{\textbf{z}}$ satisfies that $c_{1,2,\dots,k-1}^{\emph{\textbf{z}}}$ is even},\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

The estimator is given by:

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{k}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}Y_{i}^{(k)}-1.

For each
k
, the expectation of
Y_{i}^{(k)}\in\left\{0,2\right\}
is

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}^{(k)}]=2\sum_{c^{\emph{\textbf{z}}}_{1,2\cdots,k-1}\ is\ even}p(\emph{\textbf{z}})={\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}+1,

thus it follows that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\hat{T}_{k}\right]=

\displaystyle{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}.

Because
Y_{i}^{(k)}\in\left\{0,2\right\}
holds, it follows that
\hat{T}_{k}\in\left[-1,1\right].
Let
Z_{i}^{(k)}=\frac{Y_{i}^{(k)}}{2}
, then
Z_{i}^{(k)}\in\left[0,1\right]
, and

\displaystyle\hat{T}_{k}=2\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z_{i}^{(k)}\right)-1.

Define
S=\sum_{i=1}^{M}Z_{i}^{(k)}
, and its mean is

\displaystyle\mu=\mathbb{E}\left[S\right]=\frac{M}{2}\left({\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}+1\right).

According to Hoeffding inequality:

\displaystyle{\rm P}(\left|S-\mu\right|\geq t)\leq 2\exp(-\frac{2t^{2}}{M}),

where
t=\frac{M\epsilon}{2}
, then we have

\displaystyle{\rm P}\left(\left|\hat{T}_{k}-{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}\right|\geq\epsilon\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{M\epsilon^{2}}{2}\right).

To ensure all
n-1
estimators simultaneously satisfy the error bound, apply the union bound, we have

\displaystyle{\rm P}\left(\bigcup_{k=2}^{n}\left|\hat{T}_{k}-{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}\right|\geq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{k=2}^{n}{\rm P}\left(\left|\hat{T}_{k}-{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}\right|\geq\epsilon\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2(n-1)\exp\left(-\frac{M\epsilon^{2}}{2}\right).

Setting
2(n-1)\exp\left(-\frac{M\epsilon^{2}}{2}\right)\leq\delta
yields

\displaystyle M\geq\frac{2}{\epsilon^{2}}\ln\left(\frac{2(n-1)}{\delta}\right).

The random variable
Y_{i}^{(k)}\in\left\{0,2\right\}
has the following expectation

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}^{(k)}]=2\sum_{c^{\emph{\textbf{z}}}_{1,2\cdots,k-1}\ is\ even}p(\emph{\textbf{z}})={\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}+1.

Let
p_{k}=\frac{{\rm tr}\rho_{A}^{k}+1}{2}
, then we have
\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}^{(k)}\right]=2p_{k}
.
Since the value of
Y_{i}^{(k)}
is either 0 or 2, its square value is

\displaystyle(Y_{i}^{(k)})^{2}=\begin{cases}4&\text{if $Y_{i}^{(k)}$ is 2},\\
0&\text{if $Y_{i}^{(k)}$ is 0}.\end{cases}

Therefore variance of
Y_{i}^{(k)}
is

\displaystyle{\rm Var}\left(Y_{i}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\left(Y_{i}^{(k)}\right)^{2}\right]-\left(\mathbb{E}\left[Y_{i}^{(k)}\right]\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=4p_{k}-(2p_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=4p_{k}(1-p_{k}).

Since
\hat{T}_{k}=\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}Y_{i}^{(k)}-1
, its variance is

\displaystyle\text{Var}(\hat{T}_{k})

\displaystyle=\text{Var}\left(\frac{1}{M}\sum_{i=1}^{M}Y_{i}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{M}\text{Var}\left(Y_{i}^{(k)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{4p_{k}(1-p_{k})}{M}.

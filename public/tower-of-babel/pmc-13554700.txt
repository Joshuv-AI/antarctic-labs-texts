Efficacy and Safety of an Insulin Patch Pump Compared to Multiple Daily Injections in Pediatric Patients With Diabetes: A Randomized, Open-Label, Crossover, Non-Inferiority Clinical Trial
Authors: Junfen Fu, Jin Wu, Yao Hui, Weiwei Zhu, Maudline Abra Novihoho
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInsulin patch pumps offer advantages over multiple daily injections (MDI) for pediatric diabetes, but randomized trial evidence in Chinese children remains limited.ObjectivesTo evaluate the efficacy and safety of a tubeless insulin patch pump (Equil™) compared with MDI in children and adolescents with diabetes.MethodsThis multicenter, open-label, randomized, crossover, non-inferiority trial was conducted at seven pediatric endocrinology centers in China (September 2021 – October 2022). Patients aged 3–17 years with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) or type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) were randomized 1:1 to receive continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) via Equil™ or MDI via insulin pen for 5 days, followed by crossover. The primary outcome was mean blood glucose (MBG). Non-inferiority margin was pre-specified as 0.386 mmol/L. Secondary outcomes included glycemic variability (standard deviation of blood glucose, SDBG), glycated albumin (GA), total daily insulin dose (TDD), hypoglycemia frequency, and adverse events. Patient satisfaction was assessed using a study-specific 11-item questionnaire.ResultsOf 74 enrolled patients, 72 (97.3%) completed the study. CSII demonstrated non-inferiority to MDI for MBG (8.43±1.88 mmol/L, n=74, vs 9.00±2.04 mmol/L, n=72); mean difference -0.606 mmol/L; 95% CI -0.988 to -0.223; upper CI limit -0.223 < non-inferiority margin 0.386. No significant differences were observed for secondary outcomes (SDBG: 3.019 vs 3.357; GA change: 2.52±3.03% vs 2.02±3.25%, p=0.184; TDD: 132.17±78.43 vs 147.52±91.59 IU, p=0.159; hypoglycemia events: 114 vs 106). Adverse event rates were similar (75.7% vs 69.4%, p=0.399); no serious adverse events occurred. Patient satisfaction was significantly higher with CSII (total score 21.03±4.47 vs 22.00±3.99; mean difference -0.97, 95% CI -1.75 to -0.19; p=0.015), with 68.1% of patients preferring the patch pump.ConclusionThe Equil™ patch pump is non-inferior to MDI in terms of glycemic control and safety for pediatric diabetes patients, with higher patient satisfaction. These findings support the patch pump as an effective alternative for children and adolescents requiring exogenous insulin therapy.Keywords: CSII, MDI, pediatric diabetes, patch pump, randomized controlled trial, non-inferiority

Introduction

Pediatric diabetes has emerged as a rapidly growing health crisis worldwide. 1 China has one of the highest rates of childhood diabetes in the world, with new data showing a sharp increase in the disease over the last twenty years.2,3 The number of Chinese children and adolescents aged 0-19 years with type 1 diabetes mellitus (T1DM) reached 56,000 in 2021, an increase of 2,000 compared to 2019. 3 According to Ogle et al., approximately 108,300 children and adolescents under age 15 were estimated to be diagnosed with T1DM in 2021. 4 A retrospective study from Henan Province found that from 2017 to 2020, the incidence of T1DM among children aged 0.5-14.9 years was 2.19 per 100,000 person-years. 5 The China Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Type 1 Diabetes (2021) stated that the incidence of T1DM in those under 15 years old has increased nearly 4-fold over the past 20 years in China. 6

Poor glycemic control is associated with impaired memory, poorer cognitive outcomes, increased risk of depression, and poor growth. Long-term vascular complications include blindness, renal failure, premature cardiovascular disease, stroke, and amputation. 7 Diabetes in children necessitates lifetime disease management and glucose control to prevent acute complications and avoid long-term risks.7,8

Treatment of patients with T1DM and some type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is based on insulin therapy in order to mimic the physiological pancreatic secretion according to a basal/bolus pattern obtained either by multiple daily injections (MDI) or continuous subcutaneous insulin infusion (CSII) with an external insulin pump. 9

CSII treatment with a tubeless insulin pump offers unique features that may benefit users in managing their treatment, including waterproof design, no tubing to become snagged or pulled out, automated cannula insertion, and enhanced privacy.10,11 Some patients prefer CSII treatment to MDI due to pain and discomfort associated with injections which may negatively affect medication adherence and overall patient experience.12,13

However, multiple barriers exist in China that constrain the adoption of intensive regimens, and the proportion of patients using insulin pumps remains low. 14 Reasons are multifactorial, including high costs, inadequate insurance coverage, and lack of education among patients and healthcare professionals.

To overcome these limitations, a cost-effective tubeless patch pump system was developed. This system implements a patch-like direct skin attachment structure without tubing exposure between the pump body and skin. It also enables detachable connections between key components to improve wearing comfort and convenience. The performance of this device relative to traditional catheter pumps has been established in a previous study. 15

This multicenter clinical trial conducted nationwide in China is the first to evaluate the efficacy, safety, and patient satisfaction of this patch pump in children and adolescents with diabetes. The novel contributions of this study include: (1) the first randomized controlled trial in China evaluating a tubeless patch pump (Equil™) specifically in pediatric patients with diabetes; (2) a novel device design featuring a tubeless, waterproof system with automated cannula insertion, representing a technological advancement over traditional insulin pumps with tubing; (3) a comprehensive patient-centered evaluation that includes robust patient satisfaction data; (4) addressing barriers to insulin pump adoption in China by evaluating a potentially more accessible device in a context where cost, insurance coverage, and education are significant barriers; and (5) providing pediatric-specific evidence, as few trials have focused specifically on children and adolescents who may particularly benefit from the tubeless design.

Findings from this trial provided valuable insights to improve insulin delivery and optimize diabetes management in China.

Patients and Methods

Study Design

This was a multicenter, randomized, open-label, crossover, non-inferiority clinical trial conducted at seven pediatric endocrinology centers across China from September 2021 to October 2022. The trial followed the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Children’s Hospital of Zhejiang University School of Medicine (approval number: 2020-IEC-006) and by the ethics committees of all seven participating institutions. The trial was prospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2100043118) on 8 July 2021, available at https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=120225.

Consent to Participate

Written informed consent was obtained from a parent or legal guardian of each participant, as all participants were minors (aged 3–17 years) and no participants aged 18 years or older were enrolled.

Participants

Inclusion criteria were: age 3 to 17 years (inclusive); clinical diagnosis of diabetes (type 1, type 2, or other specific types); requiring insulin pump therapy; and signed informed consent from a parent or guardian.

Clinical diagnosis of diabetes was confirmed by treating pediatric endocrinologists. Type 1 diabetes diagnosis was based on clinical presentation and confirmed by islet autoantibodies and/or low C-peptide levels. Type 2 diabetes was diagnosed based on clinical features and absence of islet autoantibodies. The “other” category included monogenic diabetes (e.g., MODY) as determined by genetic testing when clinically indicated.

Exclusion criteria were: diffuse subcutaneous nodules; coagulation abnormalities (APTT or PT >1.5× upper normal limit); diabetic ketoacidosis not corrected within 48 hours of treatment; participation in other clinical trials within the past month; psychiatric disorders; or deemed unsuitable for enrollment by the investigator.

Outcomes

Primary outcome: Mean blood glucose (MBG), calculated as the average of all blood glucose measurements over each 5-day treatment period.

Secondary outcomes: Standard deviation of blood glucose (SDBG) as a measure of glycemic variability; change in glycated albumin (GA) from pre- to post-treatment; total daily insulin dose (TDD); and frequency of hypoglycemic events.

Safety outcomes: Adverse events (AEs), device-related AEs, and serious AEs (SAEs) occurring during the trial.

Patient satisfaction: Assessed using a study-specific 11-item questionnaire developed by the investigators to evaluate device-related satisfaction in short-term crossover trials. The questionnaire was developed based on expert consultation with pediatric endocrinologists. The questionnaire covered convenience, adaptation time, flexibility, discomfort/pain, device size/shape satisfaction, impact on daily life, activity restriction, price reasonability, financial considerations for method change, overall satisfaction, and willingness to recommend. Each item was scored on a 5-point scale (1 = most favorable, 5 = least favorable). Total scores ranged from 11 to 55, with lower scores indicating higher satisfaction. Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha.

Randomization and Intervention

Eligible participants were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either CSII first using the patch pump or MDI first using an insulin pen. After 5 days of treatment, participants crossed over to the other regimen for an additional 5 days.

The trial consisted of a screening period, a baseline period, and two 5-day treatment periods. During each treatment period, peripheral blood glucose (fingertip glucose) was measured 8 times daily (before and 2 hours after each meal, at bedtime, and in the early morning). Insulin doses were recorded, and hypoglycemic events (blood glucose <3.9 mmol/L) were documented. Glycated albumin (GA) was measured before and after each treatment period.

In the CSII group, subjects were treated with NovoRapid insulin (insulin aspart, Novo Nordisk) via the patch pump. In the MDI group, subjects mainly used NovoRapid insulin, with a small number also using Novolin R (biosynthetic human insulin injection), Novolin N (biosynthetic human insulin isophane injection), and insulin detemir (Levemir®; Novo Nordisk). All insulin products used in both groups were manufactured by Novo Nordisk, ensuring consistency in insulin formulation and quality. The use of different insulin types within the MDI group reflected standard clinical practice for basal-bolus therapy.

Allocation Concealment and Implementation

The random allocation sequence was generated by an independent statistician using a computer-generated random number table with a 1:1 allocation ratio. Allocation was concealed using sequentially numbered, opaque, sealed envelopes prepared by a person not involved in the study. Envelopes were opened only after participant enrollment. Site investigators enrolled participants, and the study coordinator assigned participants to interventions by opening the sealed envelopes sequentially.

Sample Size Calculation

Sample size was determined using standard methods for non-inferiority trials. 16 The non-inferiority margin was set at one-fifth of the MBG standard deviation in the control group based on previous literature. 17 With 90% power and one-sided alpha of 0.025, the required sample size was 58 per treatment sequence. Accounting for a 20% dropout rate, the final sample size was 74 participants.

Statistical Analysis

The primary analysis for non-inferiority was performed using a linear mixed-effects model with fixed effects for Treatment, Period, and Sequence, and a random effect for Participant to account for within-subject correlation from the crossover design. For the primary outcome, non-inferiority was concluded if the upper limit of the 95% confidence interval (CI) of the MBG treatment difference (CSII minus MDI) was less than the pre-specified non-inferiority margin of 0.386 mmol/L.

Carryover effects were assessed using the Grizzle method by comparing the sum of outcomes from the two periods between sequences. Period effects were tested by comparing outcomes between period 1 and period 2 across both sequences. Treatment-by-period interactions were also evaluated within the mixed-effects model.

Secondary outcomes (SDBG, GA change, TDD, hypoglycemia frequency) were compared using paired t-tests or Wilcoxon signed-rank tests as appropriate. Safety outcomes were compared using McNemar’s test. Cronbach’s alpha was calculated to assess internal consistency of the satisfaction questionnaire.

All statistical tests were two-sided with a significance level of 0.05. Analyses were performed using SAS version 9.4 and Stata version 16.

The full analysis set (FAS) included all randomized participants who received at least one dose of study treatment. The per-protocol set (PPS) included participants who completed both treatment periods without major protocol deviations. The safety set (SS) included all participants who received at least one dose of study treatment.

Baseline demographic and clinical characteristics were summarized using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables were presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD), and categorical variables as counts and percentages.

Results

Patient Disposition

A total of 74 patients were enrolled and randomized: 37 to CSII-first (Group A) and 37 to MDI-first (Group B). Two participants discontinued during the first treatment phase (one voluntary withdrawal, one requested discharge due to dissatisfaction with glycemic control). The remaining 72 participants (97.3%) completed both treatment periods and were included in the per-protocol analysis. Baseline characteristics were generally well balanced between groups, with no statistically significant differences observed (Table 1). Mean age was 9.59±3.95 years in Group A and 9.68±3.25 years in Group B. T1DM was diagnosed in 94.3% of Group A and 87.9% of Group B. Primary Outcome: Mean Blood Glucose

CSII demonstrated non-inferiority to MDI for the primary outcome of MBG (Table 2). In the full analysis set (n=74 for CSII, n=72 for MDI), MBG was 8.43±1.88 mmol/L with CSII and 9.00±2.04 mmol/L with MDI. The mean treatment difference was -0.606 mmol/L (95% CI: -0.988 to -0.223). The upper limit of the 95% CI (-0.223 mmol/L) was below the pre-specified non-inferiority margin of 0.386 mmol/L (p<0.05 for non-inferiority). Results were consistent in the per-protocol set, which included 72 participants who completed both treatment periods (CSII: 8.40±1.89 vs MDI: 9.00±2.04 mmol/L; difference -0.606 mmol/L; 95% CI -0.988 to -0.223).

Secondary Outcomes

No statistically significant differences were observed between CSII and MDI for secondary outcomes (Table 3). SDBG was 3.019 with CSII vs 3.357 with MDI. GA change from pre- to post-treatment was 2.52±3.03% with CSII vs 2.02±3.25% with MDI (p=0.184). TDD was 132.17±78.43 IU with CSII vs 147.52±91.59 IU with MDI (p=0.159). Total hypoglycemia events (blood glucose <3.9 mmol/L) were 114 during CSII treatment and 106 during MDI treatment.

Safety Evaluation

Adverse events occurred in 75.7% of participants during the CSII phase and 69.4% during the MDI phase, with no significant difference between groups (p=0.399) (Table 4). Device-related adverse events occurred in 8.1% of participants during CSII and 2.8% during MDI (p=0.293). No serious adverse events were reported in either treatment group.

Patient Satisfaction

Of 74 enrolled participants, 69 (93.2%) completed the satisfaction questionnaire after both treatment periods. The total satisfaction score was significantly lower (indicating higher satisfaction) for CSII (21.03±4.47) than for MDI (22.00±3.99), with a mean difference of -0.97 (95% CI: -1.75 to -0.19; paired t-test p=0.015) (Table 5). Cronbach’s alpha was 0.83 for the CSII scale and 0.68 for the MDI scale, indicating acceptable to good internal consistency. 18

Crossover Design Validation

A mixed-effects ANOVA was performed to evaluate potential carryover, period, and sequence effects in the crossover design (Table 6). No significant period effect (F = 0.59, p = 0.417), sequence effect (F = 0.75, p = 0.382), or treatment-by-period interaction (F = 0.41, p = 0.523) was detected. Testing for carryover effects using the Grizzle method also showed no significant carryover (p = 0.382), confirming the validity of the treatment comparison. A significant treatment effect was observed in the model (F = 18.34, p < 0.001), consistent with the non-inferiority findings for MBG (Table 2).

Discussion

This multicenter randomized controlled trial is the first in China to evaluate a tubeless insulin patch pump compared with conventional insulin pens in pediatric diabetes patients. We demonstrated that CSII with the patch pump is non-inferior to MDI in glycemic control, with a lower mean blood glucose (8.43 vs 9.00 mmol/L) and no differences in secondary outcomes or safety. Patients reported significantly higher satisfaction with the patch pump, particularly regarding flexibility and daily life impact, and 68.1% of participants preferred the patch pump over insulin pens.

Our findings align with previous studies. Guo et al. demonstrated improved glycemic control and satisfaction with CSII therapy. 14 Amadou et al. found comparable performance and satisfaction between a new patch pump and an established system. 13 Collectively, these studies support the growing role of modern insulin delivery innovations in diabetes management.

The tubeless design of the patch pump offers distinct advantages over traditional tubed pumps, including elimination of tubing-related issues, waterproof capability, and enhanced privacy.11,12 These features may be particularly valuable for active children and adolescents. Our finding of higher patient satisfaction with the patch pump is consistent with previous reports on patch pump usability.13,14,16

This study has several strengths: randomized controlled crossover design, head-to-head comparison with MDI, adequate sample size with high retention (97.3%), comprehensive assessments including multiple secondary outcomes, and a representative pediatric cohort from seven centers across China.

Limitations

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the 5-day treatment period per arm may not fully capture long-term glycemic control, sustained safety, or treatment adherence. Longer-term studies (>3 months) are needed to assess durability of glycemic control, patient satisfaction, and real-world adherence. Second, the open-label design may have introduced bias, particularly for patient-reported outcomes. Third, the findings in this pediatric cohort (age 3-17 years) may not generalize to adults or very young children (<3 years). Fourth, the satisfaction questionnaire was study-specific and not a previously validated instrument, though it demonstrated acceptable internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha 0.83 for CSII and 0.68 for MDI). Fifth, a washout period was not included between treatment phases, which may be a limitation; however, formal testing for carryover effects showed no significant carryover (p = 0.382).

Conclusion

In this randomized crossover trial in pediatric diabetes patients, the patch pump was non-inferior to MDI in glycemic control and safety, with higher patient satisfaction. These findings support the patch pump as an effective alternative to multiple daily insulin injections for children and adolescents requiring exogenous insulin therapy.

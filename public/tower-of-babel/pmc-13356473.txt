Assessing the clinical value of cervical biopsies in individuals with transformation zone type 3 at colposcopy: A cross‐sectional study
Authors: Vibe Munk Bertelsen, Berit Bargum Booth, Mette Tranberg, Lone Kjeld Petersen, Pinar Bor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionTo evaluate the diagnostic performance of systematic multiple biopsies in individuals with a transformation zone type 3 (TZ3) at colposcopy and to assess the influence of referral status and the use of dynamic spectral imaging (DSI) colposcopy on the detection of CIN2+ lesions.Material and MethodsIn this multicentre cross‐sectional study, a total of 189 individuals referred with abnormal cytology and/or hrHPV positivity with a TZ3 at colposcopy were included at three colposcopy clinics in the Central Denmark Region. Data were obtained from questionnaires, electronic medical records and the Danish National Pathology Registry. All participants underwent colposcopy with four cervical biopsies. Histological outcomes were based on the combined results of all four biopsies and, when available, compared with the corresponding large loop excision of the transformation zone (LLETZ) specimens. One centre employed DSI colposcopy, while two centres used conventional colposcopy. Main outcome measures were CIN2+ detection rate and sensitivity of individual and combined biopsies, assessed overall and stratified by referral status and colposcopy method.ResultsThe overall CIN2+ detection rate was 16.9%. The detection rate increased with each additional biopsy, with the highest sensitivity observed at the fourth biopsy (71.4%). The detection rate was significantly higher among individuals referred with high‐grade abnormalities (42.6%) compared with low‐grade referrals (6.4%) (p < 0.001). The use of DSI colposcope did not significantly improve biopsy sensitivity. Concordance between biopsy and LLETZ histology was 85.3%.ConclusionsSystematic multiple biopsies improve CIN2+ detection in women with TZ3 but may lead to overtreatment in those referred with low‐grade abnormalities. Despite this approach, the true CIN2+ detection rate may still be underestimated.

INTRODUCTION

Cervical cancer is primarily caused by persistent infection with high‐risk human papillomavirus (hrHPV), which can lead to cervical intraepithelial neoplasia (CIN). 1 Timely and accurate colposcopic assessment with histological confirmation remains essential for identifying CIN and preventing progression to cancer.

The diagnostic performance of colposcopy is influenced by several factors, including referral status, transformation zone (TZ) type, biopsy approach, equipment and colposcopist expertise. 2 , 3 , 4 , 5 Central to the assessment is the visualization of the TZ—the area between the original and newly formed squamocolumnar junction (SCJ), where CIN typically arises. When the SCJ is not fully visible, colposcopy is classified as inadequate, as seen in TZ type 3 (TZ3), where the TZ extends partially or entirely into the endocervical canal. This anatomical configuration hinders colposcopic assessment and limits the ability to obtain representative biopsies, thereby reducing diagnostic sensitivity. 6

Inadequate colposcopic assessment has been reported in up to 67% of cases, particularly among older individuals. 7 , 8 This may result in increased risk of repeated examinations, delayed diagnosis and missed high‐grade lesions. 7 , 8 Notably, TZ3 has been associated with a four‐ to fivefold increased risk of missed cervical cancer. 9 These challenges contribute to a physical and psychological burden for the individual and place additional strain on the healthcare systems. 10 , 11 , 12

Consequently, several guidelines recommend diagnostic Large Loop Excision of the Transformation Zone (LLETZ) in hrHPV‐positive individuals with TZ3. 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 However, these recommendations are largely consensus‐based rather than empirical evidence, and LLETZ may constitute overtreatment in the majority of cases. 17 , 18 , 19 Other diagnostic strategies include endocervical curettage (ECC), though its limited sensitivity reduces its clinical utility. 14 , 16 , 20 Alternatively, increasing the number of cervical biopsies enhances diagnostic accuracy for CIN2+ detection. 2

Referral status may assist risk assessment, as high‐grade referrals are associated with a higher probability of CIN2+ than low‐grade referrals. 17 Also, the use of advanced colposcopic technologies, such as the dynamic spectral imaging (DSI) system, has been proposed to enhance diagnostic accuracy, but their clinical utility in TZ3 remains unclear. 21 , 22

Importantly, no validated diagnostic algorithm currently exists for optimal management of TZ3 cases. Current literature is primarily based on few non‐standardized biopsies taken, ECC or on diagnostic LLETZ. Therefore, this study aimed to investigate the diagnostic gain of systematic multiple biopsies for detection of CIN grade 2 or worse (CIN2+) in individuals with TZ3. Secondary objectives included assessing TZ representation in biopsies and examining the impact of referral status and colposcopy type (conventional vs. DSI) on diagnostic accuracy.

MATERIAL AND METHODS

At the time of the study, individuals in Denmark aged 23–64 years were invited for cervical cancer screening with either a cytology or HPV‐based test every 3 or 5 years depending on their age and birth date. 23 Individuals with abnormal screening results were either referred for retesting or directly for colposcopy, depending on the severity of the abnormality and/or the hrHPV genotype. 14 In Denmark, cervical cancer screening, diagnostic work‐up and treatment are free of charge at both public hospitals and private gynaecological clinics and adhere to national guidelines.

Study design and inclusion criteria

This multicentre cross‐sectional study was conducted between January 2017 and April 2024, primarily in the Central Denmark Region. Five individuals were recruited from Aalborg University Hospital in North Denmark Region; however, due to local challenges, no further recruitment was conducted from this site. The remaining individuals were recruited from two gynaecology departments located at Randers and Horsens Regional Hospitals, as well as from one private gynaecology clinic in Aarhus (PGC). Individuals referred for colposcopy with the following criteria were consecutively included: aged ≥18 years, newly referred with a positive hrHPV test and/or due to abnormal cervical changes Atypical Squamous Cells of Undetermined Significance or worse (ASC‐US+), or follow‐up after previous CIN diagnosis. Exclusion criteria were cervical biopsies taken within the previous 6 months, current or terminated pregnancy within the last 3 months, previous LLETZ or prior radiotherapy to the pelvic organs.

Eligible participants were identified by the colposcopist prior to the examination and completed a questionnaire covering weight, height, smoking habits, contraceptive use, HPV vaccination status, previous pregnancies and mode of delivery.

Colposcopy assessment

At the colposcopy examination, the colposcopist completed a questionnaire documenting the colposcopic findings, including the presence of visible signs of dysplasia and the type of TZ (TZ1, TZ2 or TZ3), as classified according to the 2011 International Federation of Cervical Pathology and Colposcopy (IFCPC) nomenclature. 24 For individuals with a TZ3, the Danish national clinical guidelines on cervical dysplasia do not provide specific recommendations for management. Instead, management is individualized, with the colposcopist advised to consider LLETZ or repeated colposcopy with biopsy, based on an overall assessment. This includes the individual's history of dysplasia and hrHPV genotype, referral status, colposcopic findings, desire for pregnancy and individual preference. In Denmark, endocervical cytology is commonly used to assess dysplasia in the cervical canal, whereas ECC may be performed at the discretion of the colposcopist. In line with national guidelines, all individuals undergoing colposcopic examination have four cervical biopsies taken, regardless of the presence or absence of visible signs of CIN. In cases of CIN2+, surgical treatment is advised for individuals with no future pregnancy desire, whereas a follow‐up approach is applied for those wishing to preserve fertility. 14

Due to differences in local setup, individuals examined in Randers and Aalborg were assessed using a DSI colposcope, while those examined in Horsens and PCG were assessed using a conventional colposcope. In the conventional colposcopy group, 3% acetic acid was applied to the cervix before examination. The first biopsy was taken from the most abnormal‐looking area, followed by two to three additional biopsies, either targeted or randomly, ensuring coverage of all four cervical quadrants. In the DSI group, 3% acetic acid was applied using a built‐in applicator on the DSI colposcope. The DSI system analysed aceto‐white reactions over 120 s, generating a color‐coded map to highlight biopsy‐relevant areas. The colposcopist first selected the most abnormal area for a colposcopy‐directed biopsy (CDB) before viewing the DSI map. The second biopsy was taken from the most abnormal area indicated by the DSI map, and the colposcopist recorded whether the CDB and DSI‐selected locations matched. Two additional biopsies were taken, either targeted or randomly, ensuring a total of four biopsies covering all four quadrants.

Histopathological assessment

Each biopsy was placed in separate containers labelled 1–4 for individual histopathological evaluation. Most biopsies (95.8%) were analysed at the Department of Pathology in Randers by four gynaecological pathologists, with one pathologist evaluating 50–75% of the samples. The remaining biopsies (4.2%) were assessed at the Department of Pathology in Aarhus or Aalborg. The pathologists were blinded to the biopsy origin (CDB site or the DSI guided site).

Histopathological evaluation included the CIN grading system and assessment of the presence of the TZ. 24 , 25 Findings were categorized as no dysplasia (including inflammation and nonspecific reactive changes), CIN1, or CIN2+ (including ungradable CIN). In cases with multiple diagnoses, the most severe histopathological diagnosis during the study period was recorded. Data were retrieved from the nationwide Danish Pathology Databank. 26

Statistical Methods

Descriptive statistics are presented as median for continuous variables and as frequencies with corresponding percentages for categorical variables. The sensitivity estimates assessed the ability of individual biopsies to detect CIN2+, using the combined histological diagnosis from all four biopsies as the reference standard, as it is assumed to provide the most accurate representation of the true severity of cervical dysplasia. 2 Sensitivities were reported with 95% confidence intervals (CIs), and a p‐value of ≤0.05 was considered statistically significant. Supplementary analyses were performed to compare the CIN2+ sensitivity according to low‐grade vs. high‐grade referral status and conventional vs. DSI colposcopy using a chi‐square test. Referral status was defined as low‐grade at referral tests for ASC‐US or LSIL with/without hrHPV or hrHPV with normal cytology and high‐grade at referral tests for ASC‐H, AGC or HSIL with/without hrHPV. To our knowledge, no previous data exist on CIN2+ detection rates for systematic multiple biopsies specifically among women with TZ3 and therefore a formal sample size calculation was not possible.

Data were stored in the Research Electronic Data Capture (REDCap) system and STATA 18.0 (Stata Corp LP, College Station, TX) was used for statistical analyses. 27 , 28

RESULTS

A total of 1755 individuals (median age: 31.5 years, IQR: 26.0–42.0) were included in the study, of whom 200 (11.4%, n = 200/1755) had a TZ3 at colposcopy and were enrolled in this sub‐study. After exclusions, 189 (94.5%, n = 189/200) individuals remained for the final analysis (Figure 1). For the sub‐analysis, 109 individuals were classified as low‐grade at referral tests, and 49 were classified as high‐grade at referral tests. Additionally, a sub‐analysis was conducted based on the colposcopic method used: 138 individuals were examined using a DSI colposcope, while 51 were assessed with a conventional colposcope.

DISCUSSION

In this Danish cross‐sectional study of 1755 individuals referred for colposcopy, 189 (11.0%) had a TZ3. Unlike previous reports that associate TZ3 primarily with postmenopausal women, our findings showed a relatively even age distribution across reproductive age groups. 7 , 8 Most cases showed no visible abnormalities (68.3%) and were classified as either normal or low‐grade (68.8%). The overall CIN2+ and CIN3+ detection rate were 16.9% and 10.6%, respectively, with no cancer identified. TZ was represented in 48.7% of biopsies. Notably, CIN2+ detection increased with each biopsy, and biopsy no. 4 had the highest sensitivity (71.4%). These findings support the diagnostic value of systematic multiple biopsies – even when randomly placed. Agreement with biopsies and LLETZ specimens was 85.3% (29/34). CIN2+ was diagnosed in 6.4% (7/109) of low‐grade referrals compared with 42.9% (21/49) of high‐grade referrals. The use of the DSI colposcope did not appear to offer a diagnostic advantage in terms of individual biopsy sensitivity, though it had a nearly twice as high CIN2+ detection rate compared with conventional colposcopy.

The CIN2+ detection rate in our TZ3 cohort was higher (16.9%) compared with studies from Germany 29 and China 6 (9.5% and 11%, respectively). In both studies, biopsies were performed at the discretion of the colposcopist, and histological results from biopsies, curettage, or LLETZ specimens were used as the reference standard. In contrast, our study used a systematic approach, with four biopsies obtained from all participants, which may explain the higher CIN2+ detection rate observed. In Denmark, the decision to perform ECC or obtain endocervical cytology is left to the discretion of the colposcopist and neither procedure was performed in any cases in this present study. While ECC may provide additional diagnostic information in some settings, evidence regarding its clinical utility remains inconsistent, with studies reporting only modest increases (1–13%) in CIN2+ detection. 30 , 31 , 32 Given the limited additional diagnostic yield and the physical discomfort associated with the procedure, its routine use remains debated. 20 , 31 , 33

The use of multiple biopsies in this study enhanced the CIN2+ detection, supporting findings from previous research. 2 While some studies have reported substantially higher CIN2+ rates (up to 75.6%), 17 , 19 these were often based on LLETZ histology as the standard reference. These findings suggest that, although the use of a standardized multiple biopsy protocol in our study improved CIN2+ detection and showed high concordance with LLETZ specimens (85.3%), underestimation remains likely in TZ3 cases—particularly given that half of biopsies did not include the TZ. However, direct comparison across studies may also be limited due to differences in screening strategies, referral indication, age distributions and colposcopy management.

Though, these findings reflect a broader concern about the diagnostic limitations of TZ3. Studies report that relying solely on random biopsies may miss up to 50% of CIN2+ cases in individuals aged ≥45 years with TZ3, when compared with LLETZ histology. 17 The risk of underdiagnosis in TZ3 has prompted several guidelines (e.g., UK, Sweden, US, Australia) to recommend diagnostic LLETZ in hrHPV‐positive individuals with TZ3. 13 , 14 , 15 , 16 However, this entails the risk of overtreatment, reported in 61–80% of cases, and potential complications such as cervical stenosis which may impair follow‐up evaluation. 34 , 35 A more tailored approach is needed to balance risks and minimize both under‐ and overtreatment.

Referral status may guide clinical management, as our findings—and those of previous studies—confirm referral cytology as a strong predictor of histological outcome, with markedly higher CIN2+ detection rates in high‐grade referrals compared with low‐grade referrals. 17 , 18 , 19 In our cohort, only 6.4% (7/109) of individuals with low‐grade referrals had CIN2+ despite four biopsies, suggesting limited value of routine multiple biopsies in this subgroup—especially considering procedure‐related discomfort 11 and healthcare costs. Instead, a follow‐up strategy may be more appropriate. 36 However, long‐term follow‐up of this cohort could provide valuable information on the risk of subsequent high‐grade disease and help inform management strategies.

The role of colposcopic technology in TZ3 evaluation remains uncertain. In this study, DSI‐directed biopsies did not improve sensitivity compared with conventional colposcopy; in fact, slightly higher sensitivity was observed with conventional assessment. Though, the overall CIN2+ detection rate was nearly doubled in the DSI group (19.6% vs. 9.8%). This may indicate that DSI, although not developed for TZ3, could contribute to improved identification of visible areas of the TZ, even in cases with TZ3, where lesions are typically more difficult to identify. However, these findings are based on a limited number of CIN2+ cases (n = 5/51 vs. n = 27/138) and should be interpreted with caution.

Taken together, our findings underscore the diagnostic uncertainty associated with TZ3, highlighting the urgent need for evidence‐based guidance. Risk stratification tools—such as HPV genotyping and emerging biomarkers including p16/Ki‐67 dual staining and DNA methylation—show promise in identifying individuals who may safely be managed with follow‐up colposcopy and those who require diagnostic LLETZ. 18 , 19 , 37 These approaches may support more tailored clinical pathways and reduce unnecessary interventions. Until such tools are validated and implemented, clinical decision‐making should consider individual risk factors such as referral cytology. Our findings suggest limited diagnostic benefit of routine multiple biopsies in low‐grade referrals, while high‐grade referrals may warrant more aggressive assessment. In the absence of visible lesions, additional biopsies remain diagnostically valuable in some cases, but their use must be weighed against procedural burden and patient acceptability, why shared decision‐making between clinician and patient is essential. In the interim, updated national guidelines should place greater emphasis on incorporating individual risk factors into the management of TZ3.

A key strength of this study is the systematic approach to biopsy collection, with four biopsies obtained from all participants, regardless of colposcopic impression or referral indication. This reduces sampling bias and enhances the reliability of CIN2+ detection estimates. Subgroup analyses by referral cytology and colposcopic device type provide additional nuances and enhance clinical relevance, contributing to new evidence on an area with limited existing literature. Furthermore, this study was conducted across multiple clinical centres over 7 years and included a large sample size (n = 1755), thereby improving the generalizability of the findings to broader populations and healthcare context.

Nonetheless, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, as LLETZ was not performed in all cases, histological confirmation of CIN2+ might be incomplete, potentially leading to an underestimation of the true disease prevalence. Second, the number of CIN2+ cases—particularly within subgroups—was relatively small, limiting statistical power and robustness of subgroup comparison. Finally, although all biopsy specimens were analysed by experienced pathologists, the absence of centralized review may introduce interobserver variability.

CONCLUSION

In this TZ3 cohort, a systematic multiple biopsy protocol improved the CIN2+ detection, particularly in high‐grade referrals where each additional biopsy contributed diagnostic value. In contrast, the diagnostic yield was limited in cases referred with low‐grade cytology. Although DSI technology may assist visualization, it did not enhance biopsy sensitivity. These findings support the need for improved triage tools and the integration of risk‐based approaches into clinical guidelines for the management of TZ3.

Complete NLO BFKL impact factors for quarkonium hadroproduction in NRQCD: the case of 𝑺 [ 𝟏 ] 𝟎 𝟏 {}^{1}S_{0}^{[1]} , 𝑺 [ 𝟖 ] 𝟎 𝟏 {}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]} , and 𝑺 [ 𝟖 ] 𝟏 𝟑 {}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]} states
Michael Fucilla, Jean-Philippe Lansberg, Maxim Nefedov, Lech Szymanowski, and Samuel Wallon

Abstract
We present the first complete next-to-leading-order calculation of the impact factors for hadroproduction of the S [ 1 ] 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0}^{[1]} , S [ 8 ] 0 1 {}^{1}S_{0}^{[8]} , and S [ 8 ] 1 3 {}^{3}S_{1}^{[8]} NRQCD states within the BFKL formalism. We complete the recent virtual-correction computation presented in JHEP 12 (2024) 129 by that of the real-emission contributions. We observe the cancellation of the soft divergences between these real- and virtual-emission contributions and we note that the surviving collinear singularities are compatible with factorisation up to one loop for a novel class of processes where BFKL resummation can be applied. Our work indeed represents the first complete NLO quarkonium impact factor in the BFKL framework and paves the way to first next-to-leading-logarithmic-precision studies for hadroproduction of forward-backward quarkonium associated production at hadron colliders.

Introduction

The large centre-of-mass energies,
\sqrt{s}
, available at the CERN Large Hadron Collider (LHC) and at future circular colliders offer a unique opportunity to investigate QCD dynamics in previously unexplored regimes. In particular, they give access to the
Regge-Gribov

In this framework, the cross section of inclusive processes can be expressed as the convolution of process-dependent impact factors (IFs) and universal Green’s functions. 5.4
BFKL counterterm for the real-emission part

The definition of a rapidity-factorisation scheme at NLO is just the prescription on how the contribution when the emitted gluon with momentum
k
is highly separated in rapidity from the quarkonium:
y_{p}-y_{k}\gg 1
is subtracted. In this
Multi-Regge
kinematics, the probability of emission of an additional gluon factorises from the impact-factor (see also the discussion in Appendix
A.1
):

\displaystyle V_{g\text{, MRK}}^{(g,1)}=\frac{2z|{\bm{p}}_{T}|\mu^{2\epsilon}}{(2\pi)^{1-2\epsilon}}h_{m}^{(0)}({\bm{p}}_{T}^{2})\int d^{2-2\epsilon}{\bm{k}}_{T}\,{\cal J}_{RR}(k)\delta^{(2)}({\bm{q}}_{T}-{\bm{p}}_{T}-{\bm{k}}_{T}).

(58)

The emission probability, also known as the square of the Lipatov’s vertex (eqn. (
121
)), is:

{\cal J}_{RR}(k)=\frac{1}{1-z}\frac{2C_{A}}{k^{+}k^{-}},

(59)

where the factor
1/(1-z)
came from the phase-space measure of
k
. Since
k^{+}k^{-}={\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}
, the rest of the integrand, besides
1/(1-z)
-factor is
z
-independent and the integral over
z
is divergent at
z\to 1
.

To regularise this divergence, different regularisation schemes are employed in different approaches. In the EFT formalism
Lipatov95
, the regularisation with tilted Wilson line prescription
Chachamis:2012cc
;
Chachamis:2013hma
;
Nefedov:2019mrg
;
Nefedov:2024swu
(
k^{+}\to\tilde{k}^{+}=k^{+}+rk^{-}
) leads to, as discussed in the beginning of Sec.
5
:

{\cal J}_{RR}^{(r)}(k)=\frac{2C_{A}}{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\frac{r{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}}.

(60)

On the other hand, in the BFKL framework, the regularisation with the help of a cut on the invariant mass of the final state produced in the IF is customary:
s=(k+p)^{2}<s_{\Lambda}
with
s=(k+p)^{2}\simeq k^{-}q^{+}={\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}/(1-z)
. So in this approach one obtains:

{\cal J}_{RR}^{(s_{\Lambda})}(k)=\frac{2C_{A}}{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}\frac{1}{1-z}\theta\big(1-z>\frac{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{s_{\Lambda}}\big).

(61)

To derive the scheme-transformation term, one takes the difference of eqn. (
61
) and (
60
):

\displaystyle\Delta{\cal J}_{RR}(k)={\cal J}_{RR}^{(s_{\Lambda})}-{\cal J}_{RR}^{(r)}=\frac{2C_{A}}{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}\bigg[\frac{1}{1-z}\theta\big(1-z>\frac{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{s_{\Lambda}}\big)-\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\frac{r{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{A}}{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}\bigg[\theta\big(1-z>\frac{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{s_{\Lambda}}\big)\bigg(\frac{1}{1-z}-\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\frac{r{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}}\bigg)-\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\frac{r{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}}\theta\big(1-z<\frac{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{s_{\Lambda}}\big)\bigg]

\displaystyle=\frac{2C_{A}}{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}\bigg[O(r)-\frac{1-z}{(1-z)^{2}+\frac{r{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{q_{+}^{2}}}\theta\big(1-z<\frac{{\bm{k}}_{T}^{2}}{s_{\Lambda}}\big)\bigg].

(62)

The obtained distribution in

should be expanded in the limit

thus one has:

A.2
Different forms of the LO BFKL kernel

Let us start with the form of the LO BFKL kernel, where the real and virtual parts are written explicitly in dimensional regularisation:

The Shafarevich conjecture for varieties with globally generated cotangent
Thomas Krämer, Marco Maculan

Abstract
. We prove the Shafarevich conjecture for varieties with globally generated cotangent bundle, subject to mild numerical conditions.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a number field and
\Sigma
a finite set of primes in
K
. In this paper we prove new cases of the following conjecture:

Shafarevich conjecture for canonically polarized varieties
.

Fix
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
. Up to
K
-isomorphism there are only finitely many smooth projective canonically polarized varieties over
K
with Hilbert polynomial
P
and good reduction outside
\Sigma
.

Here by the Hilbert polynomial of a variety we mean the one of its canonical bundle. We say that a smooth projective canonically polarized variety
Y
over
K
has
good reduction outside
\Sigma
if
it admits a smooth projective model
\mathcal{Y}
over the ring of
\Sigma
-integers
R=\mathcal{O}_{K,\Sigma}
such that the canonical bundle
\det\Omega^{1}_{\mathcal{Y}/R}
is relatively ample. If such a
model exists, then it is unique up to isomorphism. In what follows we will simply talk about
good reduction
, assuming that
\Sigma
has been fixed.

The above conjecture is a special case of the Lang-Vojta conjecture, predicting the nondensity of
\Sigma
-integral points on varieties of log general type over
K
. Indeed, the moduli spaces (or rather stacks) of canonically polarized varieties are known to be of log general type, as are all their subvarieties
[
CP15
]
. The complex analogue of the above conjecture says that the moduli stacks of canonically polarized varieties do not contain entire curves, which is a celebrated result of Viehweg and Zuo
[
VZ03
]
; however, these hyperbolicity properties will play no role in our arguments.

In the case of curves the conjecture is due to Shafarevich and was proven by Faltings on his path to the Mordell conjecture
[
Fal83
]
. Since then, many cases of the conjecture and analogous finiteness results for unpolarized varieties have been proven
[
Sch85
,
And96
,
She97
,
Tak20
,
JL15
,
JL17
,
Jav15
,
JL18
,
JL21
,
Tak19
]
. All of these rely on some classification of the varieties under consideration; this is not the case in
[
KM23
]
where we proved the Shafarevich conjecture for subvarieties of abelian varieties with ample normal bundle, using the method of
[
LV20
,
LS20
]
and the big monodromy criterion from
[
JKLM25
]
.
In this paper we extend the scope of the results in
[
KM23
]
to a much larger class of canonically polarized varieties:

Definition
.

A projective variety
Y
over
k
has
amply generated cotangent bundle
if it is smooth, geometrically connected and the morphism
\textup{H}^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}
is surjective with nonzero and antiample (that is, with ample dual) kernel.

If
Y
has a
k
-rational point
y\in Y(k)
, then
Y
has amply generated cotangent bundle if and only if the Albanese morphism
\alb_{y}\colon Y\to\Alb(Y)
is unramified with ample normal bundle. In
[
KM23
]
we discussed smooth projective varieties that embed in their Albanese variety with ample normal bundle, but the current class is much larger and goes beyond subvarieties of abelian varieties:

Example
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero, and let
S
be an irreducible smooth projective variety over
k
such that the evaluation morphism
\textup{H}^{0}(S,\Omega^{1}_{S})\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{S}\to\Omega^{1}_{S}
has locally free cokernel
\mathcal{C}
of rank
r
(for instance take
h^{0}(S,\Omega^{1}_{S})=0
). Let
A
be an abelian variety over
k
, and let

Y\;\subset\;A\times S

be a smooth complete intersection of ample divisors of dimension
d\geqslant 2
. Then the morphism
\Alb(Y)\to\Alb(S)\times A
is an isomorphism by the Lefschetz theorem. If moreover
d+r<\dim A\times S
and
Y
is generic, the cotangent bundle of
Y
is globally generated. The kernel
\mathcal{K}
of the evaluation morphism
\textup{H}^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}
is nonzero and sits in the short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow q^{\ast}\mathcal{C}^{\vee}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{K}^{\vee}\longrightarrow 0

where
\mathcal{N}
is the normal bundle of
Y
in
A\times S
and
q\colon Y\to S
the morphism induced by the second projection. The vector bundle
\mathcal{N}
is ample, thus so is
\mathcal{K}^{\vee}
and with the above terminology
Y
has amply generated cotangent bundle.

It may happen that
Y
embeds into its Albanese over an algebraic closure of
k
, but the following construction gives new examples where this is not the case. Suppose that
S
admits a connected finite étale cover
f\colon S^{\prime}\to S
of degree
>1
inducing an isomorphism

\Alb(S^{\prime})\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\Alb(S).

Over
k=\mathbb{C}
such a cover exists whenever there is nontrivial torsion in
\textup{H}_{1}(S,\mathbb{Z})
, e.g. for an Enriques surface
S
. In general, the preimage
Y^{\prime}=S^{\prime}\times_{S}Y
has amply generated cotangent bundle and its Albanese variety is isomorphic to
\Alb(Y)
. So any Albanese morphism
Y^{\prime}\to\Alb(Y^{\prime})
factors through
Y
and hence cannot be an embedding, and the same holds after any finite extension of the base field. This construction can be iterated by using
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
in place of
S
and
S^{\prime}
.

1.1.
Intrinsic results

Varieties with amply generated cotangent bundle are in particular canonically polarized, so when they are defined over the number field
K
, our notion of good reduction from above applies. In the case of surfaces our main result takes the following form:

Theorem A
.

Fix an integer
c\geqslant 1
. Then up to
K
-isomorphism there are only finitely many projective surfaces
Y
over
K
with amply generated cotangent bundle, good reduction,
c_{2}(Y)=c
and
h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\geqslant 6
.

In fact we obtain an analogous result for varieties of arbitrary dimension, subject to certain mild numerical conditions:

Theorem B
.

Fix
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree
d
. Then up to
K
-isomorphism there are only finitely many projective varieties
Y
over
K
with amply generated cotangent bundle, good reduction, Hilbert polynomial
P
,
h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\geqslant 2d+2
and satisfying the numerical conditions
1.1
and
1.2
below with
\pi=\id
.

Conditions
1.1
and
1.2
will be discussed in the next section. They in particular hold if
d=2
, so
Theorem

B
implies
Theorem

A
since by the Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality, the Hilbert polynomial of a surface
S
with amply generated cotangent bundle is controlled by
c_{2}(S)

[
KM23
, 2.7]
. For odd
d
and
h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\geqslant 4d+2
the conditions
1.1
and
1.2
are empty, so
Theorem

B
in particular implies:

Corollary
.

Fix
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of odd degree
d
. Then up to
K
-isomorphism there are only finitely many projective varieties with amply generated cotangent bundle, good reduction, Hilbert polynomial
P
and
h^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})\geqslant 4d+2
.

1.2.
Numerical conditions

To formulate the two numerical conditions that go into
Theorem

B
and into
Theorem

C
below, let
Y
be a smooth projective variety over a field
k
of characteristic zero. The first condition compares the topological Euler characteristic
\chi_{\textup{top}}(Z)
of the smooth projective variety
Z=Y\times Y
to the Euler characteristic of the vector bundle
\Omega^{d}_{Z}
:

Condition 1
.

If
d=\dim Y\geqslant 4
is even, assume that

(-1)^{d}\,\chi(\Omega^{d}_{Y\times Y})\;\leqslant\;\tfrac{1}{2}\,\chi_{\textup{top}}(Y\times Y).

For the second numerical condition, we place ourselves in a slightly more general framework that will be used in
Theorem

C
, starting from an arbitrary surjective morphism
\pi\colon\Alb(Y)\to A
of abelian varieties; in fact in
Theorem

C
the variety
Y
will be given together with a morphism
Y\to A
and we then tacitly assume
\pi
to be induced by this morphism. Consider the vector subspace

V\;:=\;(\Lie A)^{\vee}\;\lhook\joinrel\longrightarrow\;(\Lie\Alb(Y))^{\vee}\;=\;\textup{H}^{0}(Y,\Omega^{1}_{Y})

and suppose that the induced morphism
\varphi\colon V\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\to\Omega^{1}_{Y}
is surjective. If
k
is algebraically closed, let
\alb_{y}\colon Y\to\Alb(Y)
be the Albanese morphism associated with
y\in Y(k)
. The composite morphism

f\;=\;\pi\circ\alb_{y}\colon\hskip 10.00002ptY\;\longrightarrow\;A

is unramified because
\varphi
is assumed to be surjective. Denote the
stabilizer
of the image of
f
by

G\;:=\;\Stab_{A}(f(Y))\;=\;\{a\in A(k)\colon f(Y)+a=f(Y)\}.

Then
Y
is of general type if and only if the group
G
is finite; see
section

2.1
. In this case we set

\chi_{\textup{top},\pi}(Y):=\frac{\chi_{\textup{top}}(Y)}{n}\hskip 10.00002pt\textup{for $n=|G|\cdot\deg(f)$,}

where
\chi_{\textup{top}}(Y)
is the topological Euler characteristic of
Y
.
Note that
\chi_{\textup{top},\pi}(Y)
is an integer since it is equal to the topological Euler characteristic of the normalization of the quotient
f(Y)/G
(this normalization is smooth by
lemma

2.1
). We also say that
Y
is
symmetric with respect to
\pi
if
f(Y)=a-f(Y)
for some
a\in A(k)
.
This notion and the definition of
\chi_{\textup{top},\pi}(Y)
do not depend on
y
, so by extending scalars they make sense also when the base field
k
is not algebraically closed. Our second condition excludes a few values of
\chi_{\textup{top},\pi}(Y)
in terms of
g=\dim A
:

Condition 2
.

If
d=\dim Y\geqslant(g-1)/4
and
Y
is symmetric with respect to
\pi
, assume that

|\chi_{\textup{top},\pi}(Y)|\;\neq\;2^{2m-1}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $m\in\{3,\dots,d\}$ with $m\equiv d$ modulo $2$}.

1.3.
Extrinsic result

We will deduce
Theorem

B
from a general result for smooth projective varieties with an unramified morphism to an abelian variety.

To state it, as in
[
KM23
, 1.4]
we say that a geometrically integral variety
X
over a field
k
is a
product
resp. a
symmetric power of a curve
if, after extending scalars to an algebraic closure of
k
, its normalization becomes isomorphic to a product of varieties of dimension
>0
resp. a symmetric power of a curve. When
k
is algebraically closed, a subvariety
X\subset A
of dimension
d\geqslant 2
is

•

a product if and only if there are
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
of dimension
>0
with
X=X_{1}+X_{2}
such that the sum morphism induces an isomorphism on normalizations

\tilde{X}_{1}\times\tilde{X}_{2}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\tilde{X},

•

a symmetric power of a curve if and only if there is a curve
C\subset A
with
X=C+\cdots+C
such that the sum morphism induces on normalizations an isomorphism

\Sym^{d}\tilde{C}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\tilde{X}.

We say that
X\subset A
is
isogenous to a symmetric power of a curve
resp.
isogenous to a product
if
X/\Stab_{A}(X)
is a symmetric power of a curve resp. a product.

Let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
over
K
and
L
an ample line bundle on it. Given smooth projective varieties
Y
and
Y^{\prime}
with morphisms
f\colon Y\to A
and
f^{\prime}\colon Y^{\prime}\to A
, we say that they are
equivalent up to translation by points in
A(K)
if there are an isomorphism
\varphi\colon Y\to Y^{\prime}
and a point
a\in A(K)
making the following diagram commutative:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varphi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x\mapsto x+a}

With these notions we show:

Theorem C
.

Fix
P\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
of degree
d<(g-1)/2
. Up to translation by points in
A(K)
, there are only finitely many canonically polarized varieties
Y
over
K
with good reduction together with an unramified morphism
f\colon Y\to A
with ample normal bundle such that
Y
has Hilbert polynomial
P
with respect to
\omega_{Y}\otimes f^{\ast}L
, conditions
1.1
and
1.2
hold, and
f(Y)
is neither isogenous to a symmetric power of a curve nor isogenous to a product.

Theorem

B
is deduced from
Theorem

C
via the Shafarevich conjecture for abelian varieties, proved by Faltings
[
Fal83
]
. This reduction is similar to that in
[
KM23
, §4.4]
, and in
section

2
we only sketch the additional arguments required in our setting.
The proof of
Theorem

C
combines the big monodromy criterion established in the next section with the nondensity result of
section

1.5
, which generalizes
[
KM23
, th. D]
. The latter is based on the Lawrence–Venkatesh method
[
LV20
]
, as developed by Lawrence and Sawin
[
LS20
]
. In
section

we explain the main modifications needed to adapt the proof of
[

, §4.3]
to establish
Theorem

C
.

1.4.
Big monodromy

We now formulate the big monodromy criterion which is the main geometric input for the proof of
Theorem

C
. Let
S
be a smooth connected variety over
k=\mathbb{C}
, and
A
a complex abelian variety of dimension
g
. Suppose we are given a morphism
f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to A\times S
such that the projection
\pi_{S}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to S
is smooth with connected fibers of dimension
d
. Given an
n
-tuple
\underline{\chi}=(\chi_{1},\dots,\chi_{n})
of characters

\chi_{i}\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\pi_{1}(A(\mathbb{C}),0)\;\longrightarrow\;\mathbb{C}^{\times}

of the topological fundamental group of the abelian variety, let
L_{\underline{\chi}}=L_{\chi_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{\chi_{n}}
be the direct sum of the associated local systems of rank one on
A(\mathbb{C})
, and consider the local system

V_{\underline{\chi}}\;:=\;R^{d}\pi_{S\ast}\,\pi_{A}^{*}\,L_{\underline{\chi}}

where
\pi_{A}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to A
is the projection. We want to ensure that for all
n
and for sufficiently general tuples of characters, the Zariski closure of the image of the monodromy representation of
V_{\underline{\chi}}
is as large as possible. In this case we say
\mathcal{Y}\to S
has
big monodromy for most tuples of torsion characters
; see
[
KM23
, 1.5]
for the precise definition. As in loc. cit. it will be convenient to phrase our criterion in terms of the fiber over a geometric generic point
\bar{\eta}
of
S
. We show:

Big Monodromy Criterion
.

Suppose
f_{\bar{\eta}}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}}\to A_{S,\bar{\eta}}
is unramified with ample normal bundle,
d<(g-1)/2
and
condition

1.2
holds. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

f_{\bar{\eta}}
is birational onto its image, and this image is nondivisible, not constant up to translation, not a product and not a symmetric power of a curve;

(2)

f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to A\times S
has big monodromy for most tuples of torsion characters.

Here the subvariety
f(\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}})\subset A_{S,\bar{\eta}}
is said to be
constant up to translation
if it is a translate
Y+a
of a complex subvariety
Y\subset A
by a point
a\in A(\bar{\eta})
. If
f(\mathcal{Y})_{\bar{\eta}}
is nondivisible, then by
[
JKLM25
, cor. 4.8]
it is constant up to translation if and only if the family
f(\mathcal{Y})\to S
is isotrivial. Also note that the morphism
f_{\bar{\eta}}
is finite, hence it is is birational if and only if
\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}}
is the normalization of its image in
A_{S,\bar{\eta}}
.

As in
[
JKLM25
, 1.1]
, if
f(\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}})
is divisible, or constant up to translation, or a product, or a symmetric power of a curve, then
V_{\underline{\chi}}
does not have big monodromy for most tuples of torsion characters. If
f_{\bar{\eta}}
is not birational, then the pushforward to
A_{S,\bar{\eta}}
of the constant sheaf on
\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}}
has several direct summands, so the monodromy decomposes in block matrices and cannot be big. This proves (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

The nontrivial implication (1)
\Rightarrow
(2) is the one relevant for
Theorem

C
and the main content of the big monodromy criterion. By
[
JKLM25
, th. 4.10]
, it suffices to show that the subvariety
f(\mathcal{Y}_{\bar{\eta}})\subset A_{S,\bar{\eta}}
has big Tannaka group with respect to the convolution of perverse sheaves,
which we will explain in section
1.6
.

1.5.
Nondensity of integral points

Theorem

C
is based on a nondensity criterion for integral points generalizing
[
KM23
, th. D]
. Given a variety
S
over the number field
K
we say that its
integral points are not Zariski dense
if for any finite extension
K^{\prime}
of
K
, any finite set of primes
\Sigma^{\prime}
in
K^{\prime}
and any flat scheme
\mathcal{S}
over
\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime}}
with generic fiber
S_{K^{\prime}}
, the subset

\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{O}_{K^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime}})\;\subset\;S(K^{\prime})

is not Zariski dense in
S_{K^{\prime}}
. To obtain a criterion for this non-density, assume that
S
is smooth and geometrically connected and that there exists an abelian variety
A
over
K
and a morphism

f\colon\mathcal{Y}\to A\times S

such that the projection
\pi_{S}\colon\mathcal{Y}\to S
is smooth projective with geometrically connected fibers of dimension
d
. Let
\mathcal{X}=f(\mathcal{Y})
be the image of
f
and
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\to\mathcal{X}
its normalization. Since
\mathcal{Y}
is smooth, the morphism
f
then factors through
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
. Let
\mathcal{X}=f(\mathcal{Y})
be the scheme theoretic image of
f
. Since
\mathcal{Y}
is normal as a scheme, the morphism
f
factors through the normalization
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
of
\mathcal{X}
. The situation is summarized in the following diagram:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\tilde{\mathcal{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A\times S}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\scriptstyle{f}}

In
section

3
we show:

Theorem D
.

Suppose that

•

there is a nonempty open subset
S^{\prime}\subset S
over which
\mathcal{Y}\to\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
is étale and the family
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\to A\times S
has big monodromy for most tuples of torsion characters;

•

the fibers of
\mathcal{Y}\to S
satisfy
condition

1.1
.

Then integral points of
S
are not Zariski dense.

Note that the morphism
\tilde{\mathcal{X}}\to S
is smooth over such an open subset
S^{\prime}
, since
\mathcal{Y}
is smooth over
S
and
\mathcal{Y}\to\tilde{\mathcal{X}}
is finite étale over
S^{\prime}
.

The flexibility of the above statement, as opposed to
[
KM23
, theorem D]
, lies in the possibility of shrinking
S^{\prime}
. This allows us to avoid reducing to the situation in which the fibers of
\mathcal{X}\to S
have trivial stabilizer in
A
. More crucially, it allows us to avoid reducing to the case in which
f
is birational, which would require knowing that a canonically polarized variety has good reduction whenever one of its finite étale cover has (after possibly inverting finitely many primes depending only on numerical data associated with the variety and the cover). The fact that we can prove the nondensity of integral points of
S
while imposing the big monodromy assumption only over
S^{\prime}
relies crucially on the smoothness of the family
\mathcal{Y}
over the whole of
S
.

1.6.
Big Tannaka groups

We reset notation and let
A
be an abelian variety of dimension
g
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero. Our results hold both in the algebraic and in the analytic framework. In the former, we consider perserve sheaves for the étale topology on
A
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
for a prime
\ell
; in the latter, we assume
k=\mathbb{C}
and consider perverse sheaves for the classical topology on
A(\mathbb{C})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. For background about perverse sheaves we refer to
[
BBDG18
]
.

Let
Y
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
d
and
f\colon Y\to A
a semismall morphism in the sense that
\dim Y\times_{A}Y=\dim Y
. The constant sheaf
\delta_{Y}:=\mathbb{F}_{Y}[d]
on
Y
placed in degree
-d
is a perverse sheaf, and the semismallness of
f
then implies that
P=f_{\ast}\delta_{Y}
is also perverse. The group law on
A
induces a convolution product on perverse sheaves, and
P
generates a neutral Tannaka category
\langle P\rangle
with respect to convolution
[
JKLM25
, 3.1]
. We fix a fiber functor

\omega\colon\hskip 20.00003pt\langle P\rangle\;\longrightarrow\;\Vect(\mathbb{F})

and consider the associated Tannaka group

G_{Y,\omega}\;:=\;\Aut^{\otimes}(\omega)\;\subset\;\GL(V)\hskip 20.00003pt\textup{where}\hskip 20.00003ptV\;:=\;\omega(P).

We say that
f\colon Y\to A
is
symmetric up to translation
if there is an involution
\iota
of
Y
and a point
a\in A(k)
such that
f(\iota(x))=a-f(x)
for all
x\in Y
. In this case, the group
G_{Y,\omega}
preserves a bilinear form
\theta\colon V\otimes V\to\mathbb{F}

which is symmetric if
d
is even and alternating otherwise. The group
G_{Y,\omega}
is then said to be
big
if the derived subgroup
G_{Y,\omega}^{\ast}
of its connected component is

G_{Y,\omega}^{*}\;=\;\begin{cases}\SL(V)&\textup{if $Y$ is not symmetric up to translation},\\
\SO(V,\theta)&\textup{if $Y$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is even},\\
\Sp(V,\theta)&\textup{if $Y$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

If
f
is finite birational, which is the case relevant here, then
Y
is the normalization of its image
X\subset A
and therefore
P=\delta_{X}
is the perverse intersection complex on this image, in which case
G_{Y,\omega}
can be thought as the Tannaka group of
X
.

Big Tannaka group Criterion
.

Suppose that
f
is unramified with ample normal bundle,
d<(g-1)/2
and
condition

1.2
holds. Then, the following are equivalent:

(1)

f
is birational onto its image, and this image is nondivisible, not a product and not a symmetric power of a curve;

(2)

G_{Y,\omega}
is big.

Similarly to the big monodromy criterion, the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) is easy and the main task is to prove (1)
\Rightarrow
(2). We follow the strategy explained in the introduction of
[
JKLM25
]
:

The first step is to ensure that the algebraic group
G^{\ast}_{Y,\omega}
is simple. For this we do not need to assume
f
is unramified: in
theorem

5.1
we show under very mild conditions that the Tannaka group of a subvariety
X\subset A
fails to be simple only if the subvariety is a product. We are then in good shape since
V
is known to be a minuscule representation of
G_{Y,\omega}^{\ast}
by
[
JKLM25
, cor. 5.15]
and there are very few minuscule representations of simple algebraic groups. Except for the standard representations of classical groups (which lead to big Tannaka groups), we are left with wedge powers of the standard representation of
\SL_{n}
, spin representations, and the smallest irreducible representations of the exceptional groups
E_{6}
and
E_{7}
. In
theorem

6.1
we show that wedge powers occur if and only if
Y
is the symmetric power of a smooth curve and
f
is a closed embedding; in
theorem

7.1
we exclude spin representations, and in
theorem

8.1
we rule out the groups
E_{6}
and
E_{7}
.

Conventions

By a variety over a field
k
we mean a separated
k
-scheme of finite type. Subvarieties are always taken to be closed. We write
\tilde{X}
for the normalization of a variety
X
and say that
X
has smooth unramified normalization if
\tilde{X}
is smooth and the normalization morphism
\tilde{X}\to X
is unramified.
If this is the case and if
X\subset W
is given as a subvariety of a smooth projective variety

2.1.
Basic properties

Let

Lemma 2.1
.

Let

be an unramified morphism where

an abelian variety. Then, the normalization

of

(2)

is canonically polarized;

(3)

(4)

\tilde{X}

Proof.

First of all, the morphism

]

It suffices to show that if

of

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X=\Sym^{n}C}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A=\Pic^{0}(C),}

, the morphism
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
is finite étale, so
\mathcal{E}
is a direct summand of
\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
as a flat bundle. Consequently,

\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\textup{dR}}(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}/S^{\prime},\mathcal{E},\nabla)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\textup{dR}}(\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}/S^{\prime},\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{E},\pi^{\ast}\nabla)

is a direct summand of flat bundles, where both relative de Rham cohomology groups are equipped with their Gauss–Manin connections. Here
\mathcal{Y}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}
denote the base changes of
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{Z}
to
S^{\prime}
. We claim that this direct summand extends uniquely across

\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\textup{dR}}(\mathcal{Y}/S,\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{E},\pi^{\ast}\nabla).

The projector defining the direct summand over
S^{\prime}
is a horizontal section of
\cEnd(\mathcal{V})
over
S^{\prime}
. Since horizontal sections extend uniquely, it extends uniquely to a horizontal section of
\cEnd(\mathcal{V})
over

\mathcal{W}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V},

which restricts over

to
\mathcal{H}^{i}_{\textup{dR}}(\mathcal{Z}^{\prime}/S^{\prime},\mathcal{E},\nabla)

\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{V}

is the unique flat complement of
\mathcal{W}
.
The Hodge filtration on
\mathcal{W}
is a direct summand of the Hodge filtration on
\mathcal{V}
. Indeed, this holds over
S^{\prime}
, where
\mathcal{V}
is the de Rham cohomology of
\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{W}
is the de Rham cohomology of the direct summand
\mathcal{E}\hookrightarrow\pi_{\ast}\pi^{\ast}\mathcal{E}
. Since the locus of filtrations that split with respect to a fixed decomposition is closed in the corresponding flag variety, the same remains true over all of

, the formal period mapping for
\mathcal{V}
at

\hat{\Phi}_{s}\colon\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,s}\longrightarrow\Flag(\mathcal{V}_{s}),

factors through the closed subvariety of split filtrations,

\Flag(\mathcal{W}_{s})\times\Flag(\mathcal{W}_{s}^{\prime})\hookrightarrow\Flag(\mathcal{V}_{s}).

Here
\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{S,s}
denotes the completion of the local ring
\mathcal{O}_{S,s}
at its maximal ideal, and
\Flag(V)
denotes the flag variety of a vector space

.

Finally, we conclude with a few words regarding
p
-adic period mappings. Suppose that

is a finite unramified extension of

with ring of integers

is a smooth separated scheme over

, and that

is a morphism which is finite étale over a nonempty open subset

, with

smooth and projective. Assume further that the Hodge-to-de Rham spectral sequence

R^{j}f_{\ast}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{Y}/S}\otimes\mathcal{E})\implies\mathcal{H}_{\textup{dR}}^{i+j}(\mathcal{Y}/S;\mathcal{E},\nabla)=\textup{R}f^{i+j}_{\ast}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}/S}\otimes\mathcal{E})

degenerates, where
\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}/S}\otimes\mathcal{E}
is the de Rham complex of

, and that the quasicoherent sheaves

\mathcal{V}:=\mathcal{H}_{\textup{dR}}^{q}(\mathcal{Y}/S;\mathcal{E},\nabla)=\textup{R}f^{q}_{\ast}(\Omega^{\bullet}_{\mathcal{Y}/S}\otimes\mathcal{E}),\hskip 20.00003ptR^{j}f_{\ast}(\Omega^{i}_{\mathcal{Y}/S}\otimes\mathcal{E}),

are locally free of finite rank. Having fixed an

, under the above hypotheses we obtain a

-adic period map

\Omega\to\Flag(\mathcal{V}_{s})^{\textup{an}},

where the superscript

is the

for the direct factor
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{V}
over

extending

4.
Perverse sheaves and characteristic cycles

The rest of this paper will be concerned with Tannaka groups of perverse sheaves on abelian varieties. In this section we set up the general framework and recall a few facts about characteristic cycles that will be used in what follows.

4.1.
Setup

From now on
A
will always denote an abelian variety of dimension
g
over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero, and
X\subset A
an integral subvariety of dimension
d
. We treat the algebraic and analytic framework on the same footing: in the former we use perverse sheaves for the étale topology on
A
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}=\bar{\mathbb{Q}}_{\ell}
for a prime
\ell
, in the latter we work over
k=\mathbb{C}
and use perverse sheaves for the classical topology on
A(\mathbb{C})
with coefficients in
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{C}
. We denote by

\delta_{X}\;\in\;\textup{Perv}(A,\mathbb{F})

the perverse intersection complex supported on
X\subset A
. By
[
JKLM25
, prop. 3.1]
the convolution powers of this intersection complex generate a neutral Tannaka category
\langle\delta_{X}\rangle
. We fix a fiber functor

\omega\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\delta_{X}\rangle\;\longrightarrow\;\Vect(\mathbb{F})

and consider the associated Tannaka group
G_{X,\omega}:=\Aut^{\otimes}(\omega)\subset\GL(\omega(\delta_{X}))
. This is a reductive group, and we denote the derived group of its connected component by

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\;:=\;[G_{X,\omega}^{\circ},G_{X,\omega}^{\circ}].

4.2.
Characteristic cycles

The above Tannaka groups are closely related to the conormal geometry of subvarieties. Recall that the
conormal variety
to
X\subset A
is defined as the Zariski closure in
T^{\vee}A=A\times\Lie(A)^{\vee}
of the conormal bundle to the smooth locus of
X
. This is a conic Lagrangian subvariety of
T^{\vee}A
. We denote its projectivization by

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\;\subset\;A\times\mathbb{P}_{A}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002pt\mathbb{P}_{A}\;:=\;\mathbb{P}(\Lie(A)^{\vee})

and define the
Gauss map
of the subvariety as the projection
\gamma_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
. In the context of perverse sheaves such conormal varieties arise naturally as irreducible components of characteristic cycles:

Over
k=\mathbb{C}
one may attach to any
P\in\textup{Perv}(A,\mathbb{F})
a
characteristic cycle

\CC(P)
, which is a finite formal sum of conormal varieties to the strata in a suitable Whitney stratification of the support
\Supp(P)

[
Dim04
, def. 4.3.19]
. We denote by
\cc(P)
the cycle on
A\times\mathbb{P}_{A}
which is obtained from the projectivization of the characteristic cycle by discarding any components with non-dominant Gauss map. If
X\subset A
is a smooth subvariety of general type, then
\cc(\delta_{X})=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}
. We are interested in a class of subvarieties which may be singular but still satisfy this last condition.

Over an arbitrary algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic zero this leads to the following notion:

Definition 4.1
.

We say that a subvariety
X\subset A
has
integral characteristic cycle
if for any embedding
\sigma\colon k\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}
, the associated complex subvariety
X_{\sigma}\subset A_{\sigma}
has characteristic cycle

\cc(\delta_{X_{\sigma}})\;=\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X_{\sigma}}.

Note that this condition is independent of the chosen embedding
\sigma
: indeed, the characteristic cycle of the perverse intersection complex can be defined over
k
via Saito’s theory for
\ell
-adic sheaves
[
Sai17
]
, and the base change of this cycle via
\sigma
gives back the characteristic cycle in the above sense by
[
Rai23
, th. 1.2]
.

The reason for the above definition is that for any nondivisible subvariety
X\subset A
with integral characteristic cycle, the Tannaka group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
acts via a minuscule representation on
\omega(\delta_{X})
, see
[
Krä22
, cor. 1.10]

[
JKLM25
, cor. 5.15]
. The main class of subvarieties with integral characteristic cycles to be considered in this paper will be the following:

Definition 4.2
.

We say that variety
X
has
smooth unramified normalization
if
\tilde{X}
is smooth and the normalization morphism
\nu\colon\tilde{X}\to X
is unramified.

If this is the case and if
X\subset W
is a subvariety of a smooth variety
W
, then the kernel

\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/W}\;=\;\Ker\left(\nu^{*}(\Omega^{1}_{W})\longrightarrow\Omega^{1}_{\tilde{X}}\right)

is a vector bundle whose associated projective bundle is the pullback of the conormal variety:

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/W})\;=\;\tilde{X}\times_{X}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}

We say that
X\subset W
has
smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle
if it has smooth unramified normalization and the normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{X}/W}=(\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/W})^{\vee}
is ample. Let us now again take
W=A
to be an abelian variety.

Lemma 4.3
.

For any subvariety
X\subset A
with smooth unramified normalization, the following properties hold:

(1)

X
has integral characteristic cycle and the morphism
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to X
has constant fiber dimension.

(2)

X
is of general type if and only if the Gauss map
\gamma_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
is dominant, in which case
\gamma_{X}
is generically finite of degree

\deg(\gamma_{X})\;=\;\chi(\delta_{X})\;=\;(-1)^{d}\,\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{where}\hskip 10.00002ptd\;=\;\dim X.

(3)

The following three properties are equivalent:

•

The normal bundle
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{X}/A}
is ample.

•

The Gauss map
\gamma_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
is finite.

•

For every
\omega\in\textup{H}^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})\setminus\{0\}
the pull-back
\nu^{\ast}\omega
has finite zero locus.

(4)

If
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{X}/A}
is not ample, then there is a curve
C\subset X
lying in a smaller abelian subvariety of
A
. In particular, if
A
is simple, then
\mathcal{N}_{\tilde{X}/A}
is ample.

Proof.

We may assume
k=\mathbb{C}
. For claim (1) we begin by showing that the characteristic variety

\Characteristic(\delta_{X})\;:=\;\Supp(\CC(\delta_{X}))\;\subset\;T^{\vee}A

is irreducible. Note that
\delta_{X}=\nu_{*}(\delta_{\tilde{X}})
since the normalization
\nu\colon\tilde{X}\to X
is a finite birational morphism. So we may apply Kashiwara’s estimate for the characteristic variety of direct images
[
Kas76
, th. 4.2(b)]
: the morphism
f\colon\tilde{X}\to A
induces a correspondence

T^{\vee}\tilde{X}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rho}}{{\longleftarrow}}\;\tilde{X}\times_{X}T^{\vee}A\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\varpi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;T^{\vee}A

where
\varpi=f\times\id
and
\rho=df
, and with this notation

\Characteristic(\delta_{X})\;=\;\Characteristic(\nu_{*}(\delta_{\tilde{X}}))\;\subset\;\varpi(\rho^{-1}(\Characteristic(\delta_{\tilde{X}})).

Here
\Characteristic(\delta_{\tilde{X}})
is the zero section
T^{\vee}\tilde{X}
. Since
f
is unramified, the codifferential
\rho
is smooth equidimensional with integral fibers of dimension
r=g-d
where
d=\dim X
, so
\rho^{-1}(\Characteristic(\delta_{\tilde{X}}))
is integral of dimension
g
. Since
\varpi
is a finite morphism, it follows that
\varpi(\rho^{-1}(\Characteristic(\delta_{\tilde{X}}))
is integral of dimension
g
. So the inclusion in Kashiwara’s estimate is an equality since
\Char(\delta_{X})
is of pure dimension
g
. In particular, the characteristic cycle
\cc(\delta_{X})
coincides with the conormal variety
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}
, which is in turn the projectivization

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/A})\;\subset\;A\times\mathbb{P}_{A}

of the conormal bundle
\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/A}
of the unramified morphism
\tilde{X}\to A
. Hence the projection
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to X
is the composite morphism

\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{C}_{\tilde{X}/A})\;\longrightarrow\;\tilde{X}\;\longrightarrow\;X,

so it has constant fiber dimension. This proves claim (1). Claim (2) then follows from the Kashiwara index formula together with the identity

\chi(\delta_{X})\;=\;\chi(\nu_{*}(\delta_{\tilde{X}}))\;=\;\chi(\delta_{\tilde{X}})\;=\;(-1)^{d}\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})

where the last equality uses that
\tilde{X}
is smooth. The claims (3) and (4) are proven in
[
Laz04
, prop. 6.3.10]
in the special case where
f
is a closed embedding, but the proof goes through verbatim when
f
is only unramified.
∎

4.3.
Lower bounds for the Euler characteristic

Recall that the group
G_{X,\omega}
comes with a natural faithful irreducible representation
\omega(\delta_{X})
whose dimension is given by
\dim\omega(\delta_{X})=\chi(\delta_{X})=|\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})|
. In
section

7.4
we will use the following lower bound on this topological Euler characteristic:

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral subvariety of dimension
d<g
that has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle. Then

|\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})|\;\geqslant\;g.

Proof.

We may assume that
k=\mathbb{C}
. The claim then follows from
[
BSS93
, th. 4]
by identifying
\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})
with the top Segre class of the normal bundle, since the normal bundle is ample, globally generated, and
\textup{H}^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})\neq 0
.
∎

The above bound is clearly not sharp, as one already sees for curves. For surfaces we have the following bound:

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
S
be a smooth minimal surface of general type and
q=h^{1}(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})
its irregularity. Then

\chi_{\textup{top}}(S)\;\geqslant\;3q-9.

Proof.

Since
S
is of general type, the Chern classes
c_{i}=c_{i}(T_{S})
satisfy
c_{1}^{2}\leqslant 3c_{2}
by the Bogomolov-Miyaoka-Yau inequality. By Noether’s formula this is equivalent to
3\chi\leqslant c_{2}
where
\chi=\chi(S,\mathcal{O}_{S})=1-q+p
for
p=h^{2}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})
. As
S
is minimal, we have
p\geqslant 2q-4
by
[
Deb82
, appendix]
. This is equivalent to
\chi\geqslant q-3
. Combining these inequalities we obtain
c_{2}\geqslant 3(q-3)
.
∎

5.
Simplicity

In this section we prove a criterion for the simplicity of Tannaka groups that applies to a large class of singular subvarieties of abelian varieties, including all subvarieties that receive a finite dominant morphism from a smooth variety.

5.1.
Main result

We say that an integral variety
X
has
uniruled modifications
if for every proper birational morphism

f\colon\hskip 10.00002ptX^{\prime}\;\longrightarrow\;X

from a normal variety
X^{\prime}
all irreducible components of the exceptional locus of
f
are uniruled over
X
. When
X
is normal, this is the definition in
[
Kol95
, VI.1.6]
. The class of varieties that have uniruled modifications is quite large: in particular, by
[
Kol95
, chapt. VI, th. 1.2 and (1.6.2.3)]
it includes all varieties which receive a finite dominant morphism from a smooth variety. More generally, Hacon and McKernan have shown that any normal variety with divisorially log terminal singularities has uniruled modifications
[
HM07
, cor. 1.6]
. The main goal of this section is the following result:

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety with uniruled modifications and dimension
d<g-1
. If every component of
\cc(\delta_{X})
has finite Gauss map, then the following are equivalent:

(1)

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
is not simple.

(2)

X=X_{1}+X_{2}
for positive dimensional integral subvarieties
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
such that the sum morphism induces an isomorphism

\tilde{X}_{1}\times\tilde{X}_{2}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\tilde{X}.

Moreover, all subvarieties
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
with the property (2) have finite Gauss map.

Before we come to the proof, which will take up the rest of this section, let us note that the theorem in particular applies to subvarieties
X\subset A
which have smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle: for such subvarieties the characteristic cycle
\cc(\delta_{X})
is integral with finite Gauss map by
lemma

4.3
. In such a situation also the summands
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
have smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle:

Corollary 5.2
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle and dimension
d<g-1
. For any decomposition
X=X_{1}+X_{2}
with integral subvarieties
X_{i}\subset A
such that the sum map induces an isomorphism

\tilde{X}_{1}\times\tilde{X}_{2}\;\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\longrightarrow}}\;\tilde{X},

both
X_{i}\subset A
have smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle.

Indeed, if
X
has smooth unramified normalization, then so do both
X_{i}
. If
\tilde{X}\to A
moreover has ample normal bundle, then
X
has finite Gauss map by
lemma

4.3
. In this case the last sentence in
theorem

5.1
shows that the Gauss maps of both
X_{i}
are finite, hence
\tilde{X}_{i}\to A
has ample normal bundle as claimed.

5.2.
A necessary criterion for simplicity

In the proof of
theorem

5.1
, we will apply the following criterion to show the implication (2)
\Rightarrow
(1) and the finiteness of the Gauss map for the summands:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral subvariety such that every component of
\cc(\delta_{X})
has finite Gauss map. Suppose there are two subvarieties
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
of positive dimension such that
\delta_{X}\simeq\delta_{X_{1}}*\delta_{X_{2}}
. Then

(1)

the group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
is not simple, and

(2)

the subvarieties
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
have finite Gauss map.

Proof.

Replacing all the subvarieties by their image under the isogeny
[n]\colon A\to A
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we may assume that
X
is nondivisible; moreover, replacing all the subvarieties by suitable translates we may assume that
H:=G_{\omega}(\delta_{X_{1}}\oplus\delta_{X_{2}})
is a semisimple group
[
Krä21
, lemma 5.3.1]
. Since by assumption every component of
\cc(\delta_{X})
has finite Gauss map, it then follows by
[
Krä20
, lemma 2.3]
that the natural morphism

H\;\twoheadrightarrow\;G_{X,\omega}

is an isogeny. Hence if
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
were a simple algebraic group, then
H^{*}
would be as well. But in

\omega(\delta_{X})\;\simeq\;\omega(\delta_{X_{1}})\otimes\omega(\delta_{X_{2}})

the left hand side is a nontrivial irreducible representation of
H
while the right hand side is a tensor product of
two
nontrivial irreducible representations. This contradicts the uniqueness of the number of irreducible factors in a tensor product of representations of simple algebraic groups
[
Raj04
]
, thus proving (1). Claim (2) follows from the fact that

[n]_{*}\cc(\delta_{X_{i}})\;\in\;\bigl\langle\cc(\delta_{X})\bigr\rangle

for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
by
[
Krä20
, prop. 2.5, lemma 2.6 and ex. 2.3]
.
∎

5.3.
Proof of
theorem

5.1

(2)
\Rightarrow
(1): If

where

are integral subvarieties of positive dimension such that the sum morphism induces an isomorphism

,
then the sum morphism

is finite and birational. It follows that

\delta_{X}\;\simeq\;\delta_{X_{1}}*\delta_{X_{2}}

and

shows that in this situation the Tannaka group

is not simple and the subvarieties
X_{1},X_{2}\subset A
again have finite Gauss map.

(1)
\Rightarrow
(2): If

is not simple, then as in the proof of
[
JKLM25
, prop. 6.10]
there exist clean effective cycles
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1},\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{2}\in\langle\cc(\delta_{X})\rangle
of Gauss degree
\deg(\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{i})>1
and an integer

such that

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1}\circ\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{2}\;=\;\cc(\delta_{Y})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002ptY\;=\;[n](X),

where

denotes the convolution product of clean cycles
[
JKLM25
, def. 5.2]
. The cycle on the right has the form
\cc(\delta_{Y})=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y}+R
where
R
is either zero or an effective clean cycle which is supported over a strict subvariety of
Y\subset A
. Since the convolution of effective clean cycles is effective, in the above product decomposition there must be components
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{i}}\subset\Supp(\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{i})
over integral subvarieties
Y_{i}\subset A
such that

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{1}}\circ\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{2}}\;=\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y}+\cdots

where
\cdots
is a sum of components of
R
. By assumption all components of
\cc(\delta_{X})
have finite Gauss map, so for
i=1,2
the Gauss map of
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{i}}\in\langle\cc(\delta_{X})\rangle
is finite. Since
\dim X<g-1
, it follows that

\dim Y\;=\;\dim Y_{1}+\dim Y_{2}

by
[
JKLM25
, cor. 5.7]
. The product decomposition
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{1}}\circ\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{2}}=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y}+\cdots
then shows that the sum morphism induces a birational morphism
Y_{1}\times Y_{2}\to Y
. Note that
\dim Y_{i}>0
for both
i=1,2
: if not, then by irreducibility we could assume that
Y_{1}=\{p\}
is a point, in which case
Y_{2}=Y-p
by the product decomposition. But since

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1}\circ\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y_{2}}\;\subset\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1}\circ\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{2}\;=\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Y}+R,

this could happen only if
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1}=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{\{p\}}
which is impossible since
\deg(\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{1})>1
.

It remains to deduce from the above decomposition of
Y
a similar decomposition of
X
. Since
X\subset A
is nondivisible, the morphism
[n]\colon X\to Y
is birational. Hence if we pick irreducible components
X_{i}\subset A
of the preimage
[n]^{-1}(X_{i})
for

such that

, then the sum morphism

is birational. By passing to normalizations we obtain a birational morphism

\tilde{\sigma}\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\tilde{X}_{1}\times\tilde{X}_{2}\;\longrightarrow\;\tilde{X}.

Since
\tilde{X}
has uniruled modifications, any positive-dimensional fiber of
\tilde{\sigma}
would have to be uniruled. But
\tilde{X}_{1}\times\tilde{X}_{2}
admits a finite morphism to an abelian variety
A\times A
and hence it cannot contain any uniruled subvariety of positive dimension. Thus the morphism
\tilde{\sigma}
is finite, and the claim follows since any finite birational morphism between normal varieties is an isomorphism. ∎

6.
Wedge powers

We now focus on subvarieties that have smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle. In this section we show that for such subvarieties of high enough codimension, wedge powers of the standard representation of
\SL_{n}(\mathbb{F})
occur only if the normalization is a symmetric power of a curve.

6.1.
Main result

Let
r\geqslant 1
be an integer. We say that a subvariety
X\subset A
is an
r
-th wedge power
if there exists an integer
n\geqslant r
such that the Tannaka group is

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq\Alt^{r}(\SL_{n}(\mathbb{F}))\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{with the standard action on}\hskip 10.00002pt\omega(\delta_{X})\simeq\Alt^{r}(\mathbb{F}^{n}).

If
r
is given, then
n
is determined by the Euler characteristic of the intersection cohomology since

\chi(\delta_{X})\;=\;\dim_{\mathbb{F}}(\omega(\delta_{X}))\;=\;\tbinom{n}{r}.\vskip 6.0pt plus 2.0pt minus 2.0pt

By duality it suffices to discuss wedge powers for
r\leqslant n/2
. In this case we have:

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle and dimension
d<(g-1)/2
. Suppose that
\chi(\delta_{X})=\binom{n}{r}
with
1<r\leqslant n/2
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

X\subset A
is an
r
-th wedge power.

(2)

r=d
and there is an irreducible curve
C\subset A
such that

•

X=C+\cdots+C\subset A
is the sum of
d
copies of
C
, and

•

the sum map induces on normalizations an isomorphism
\Sym^{d}\tilde{C}\to\tilde{X}
.

Any such
C\subset A
has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle.

The proof will proceed in three steps: in
section

6.2
we discuss the general structure of conormal varieties of wedge powers, then in
section

6.3
we show the finiteness of the resulting sum morphism, and in
section

6.4
we conclude by some general arguments about symmetric powers of varieties.

6.2.
Conormal varieties of wedge powers

Let
Z\subset A
be a subvariety with dominant Gauss map. Then there exists an open dense subset

over which the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}
is finite and flat. For an integer
r\geqslant 1
we consider
the Zariski closure

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}^{[r]}\;:=\;\overline{\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}^{\times r}_{Z\mid U}\smallsetminus\Delta_{r}}\;\subset\;A^{r}\times\mathbb{P}_{A}

and put

\Alt^{r}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\;:=\;\tfrac{1}{r!}\,\sigma_{*}(\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}^{[r]})\;\in\;\mathcal{L}(A).

for the sum morphism
\sigma\colon A^{r}\times\mathbb{P}_{A}\to A\times\mathbb{P}_{A},(z_{1},\dots,z_{r},\xi)\mapsto(z_{1}+\cdots+z_{r},\xi)
.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let

Proof.

In

∎

7.
Spin representations

We now exclude spin representations for subvarieties of high codimension that have smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle.

7.1.
How to rule out the spin case

For
n\geqslant 1
, we say that a subvariety
X\subset A
is

•

of spin type
B_{n}
if its Tannaka group is the spin group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq\Spin_{2n+1}(\mathbb{F})
,

•

of spin type
D_{n}
if its Tannaka group is a half-spin group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq\Spin_{2n}^{\pm}(\mathbb{F})
.

In this definition we do not make any assumption on the representation
V_{X}=\omega(\delta_{X})
of the Tannaka group, since it will be automatically determined in the case relevant to us:
If a nondivisible subvariety
X\subset A
has smooth unramified normalization, then by
lemma

4.3
it has integral characteristic cycle, so the group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
acts on
V_{X}
via a minuscule representation
[
Krä22
, cor. 1.10]

[
JKLM25
, cor. 5.15]
. A look at the list of faithful minuscule representations then shows:

•

If
X
is of spin type
B_{n}
, then
V_{X}
is the spin representation.

•

If
X
is of spin type
D_{n}
, then
V_{X}
is the associated half-spin representations.

We exclude both cases in high enough codimension:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle and dimension
d<(g-1)/2
.

(1)

The subvariety
X\subset A
is not of spin type
B_{n}
for any
n\geqslant 1
.

(2)

If
X
is of spin type
D_{n}
for some
n
, then
d\geqslant(g-1)/4
and
n=2m
for some integer
m\in\{3,\dots,d\}
which has the same parity as
d
.

The proof of this follows a similar pattern as the argument for wedge powers and will take up the rest of this section.

7.2.
Conormal varieties in the spin case

Let
Z
be a symmetric reduced clean effective cycle on

over which the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}
is finite étale. For an integer

we consider the Zariski closure

\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z,{\mathbb{S}}}^{[r]}\;:=\;\overline{\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z|U}^{\times r}\smallsetminus(\Delta_{r}\cup\Delta_{r}^{-})}\;\subset\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}^{\times r}

and put

\Alt^{r}_{\mathbb{S}}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\;:=\;\sigma_{*}(\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z,{\mathbb{S}}}^{[r]})

for the sum morphism

. We say the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}

if its degree is an even integer

and the finite étale cover
\gamma_{Z|U}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z|U}\to U

on the right hand side. Note that there is no intrinsic way to distinguish the two summands from each other. For

These appear naturally as characteristic cycles in the spin case:

Proposition 7.2
.

Let

or

for some

e\geqslant 1
with the following properties:

(1)

If
X
is of spin type
B_{n}
, then
\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S}}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{[e](X+a)}
.

(2)

If
X
is of spin type
D_{n}
, then the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}
has even monodromy and we have

\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\;=\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{[e](X+a)}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for some}\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon\;\in\;\{+,-\}.

In both cases, if
\gamma_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
is a finite morphism, then so is
\gamma_{Z}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
.

Proof.

In
[
JKLM25
, th. 8.5]
this was stated only when
X\subset A
is a smooth integral subvariety, but the same proof applies verbatim also under the weaker assumption that
X\subset A
is a subvariety with integral characteristic cycle.
∎

7.3.
The sum morphism

The next step in the proof of
theorem

7.1
is to consider the image

\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}Z\;:=\;\pr_{A}(\Supp(\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}))\;\subset\;A\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for}\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon\;\in\;\{+,-,\varnothing\}.

For any reduced symmetric effective cycle
Z
on
A
whose Gauss map is a finite morphism of even degree
\deg(\gamma_{Z})=2n
, we know from
[
JKLM25
, th. 8.6]
:

(1)

If
\dim\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S}}Z<(g-1)/2
, then
n\dim Z<g-1
.

(2)

If
\gamma_{Z}
has even monodromy and there is
\varepsilon\in\{+,-\}
for which
r:=\dim\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}Z
satisfies

r\;<\;\begin{cases}(g-1)/4&\text{if $n=2m$ is even and $m\leqslant r+1$},\\
(g-1)/2&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

then
n\dim Z<g-1
.

In what follows, we will use the uniform notation
\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}
for
\varepsilon\in\{+,-,\varnothing\}
to include all the above cases. For
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
this notation includes the tacit assumption that the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}
has even monodromy. With this convention we have the following dimension estimate in the opposite direction to (1) and (2) above:

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
X\subset A
be a nondivisible subvariety with finite Gauss map and integral characteristic cycle such that the projection
\pr_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to X
has constant fiber dimension. Suppose that there is a reduced symmetric effective cycle
Z
on
A
with finite Gauss map of degree
2n
such that

\Alt^{n}_{\mathbb{S},\varepsilon}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}=\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{[e](X)}

for some integers
n\geqslant 2
,
e\geqslant 1
and some
\varepsilon\in\{+,-,\varnothing\}
. Then
n\dim Z\geqslant g
.

Proof.

In
[
JKLM25
, th. 8.7]
this was stated only when
X
is smooth, but the proof goes through without change in the more general setting considered here.
∎

7.4.
Conclusion of the proof of
theorem

7.1

Let
X\subset A
be a nondivisible subvariety of dimension
d
with smooth unramified normalization and ample normal bundle. Suppose
X
is of spin type
B_{n}
or
D_{n}
for some integer
n\geqslant 1
. Replacing
X
by some translate, we obtain from
proposition

7.2
an integer
e\geqslant 1
and a reduced symmetric effective cycle
Z
on
A
with finite Gauss map of degree
\deg(\gamma_{Z})=2n
such that

\Alt^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},\varepsilon}\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{Z}\;=\;\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{[e](X)}\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{for some}\hskip 10.00002pt\varepsilon\;\in\;\{+,-,\varnothing\}.

Since
X
has smooth unramified normalization, the projection
\pr_{X}\colon\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to X
has constant fiber dimension. Moreover, the Gauss map
\gamma_{Z}
is finite, so
proposition

7.3
shows
n\dim Z\geqslant g
. But

\dim\Alt^{n}_{{\mathbb{S}},\varepsilon}Z\;=\;\dim[e](X)\;=\;\dim X\;=\;d.

Hence by the above items (1) and (2) with
r=d
together with
[
JKLM25
, lemma 8.8]
we have

d\;\geqslant\;(g-1)/2

unless
X
is of spin type
D_{2m}
for some integer
m\leqslant d+1
, in which case

d\geqslant(g-1)/4
.
In this case
m
must have the same parity as
d
because the half-spin representation is orthogonal for even
m
and symplectic for odd
m

[
JKLM25
, rem. 8.2]
. It only remains to exclude the case where
m=2
and
d
is even. But in this case we would have
\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})=\chi(\delta_{X})=8
, which is impossible by
lemmas

4.4
and
4.5
.
∎

8.
Exceptional groups

Finally we rule out the appearance of exceptional groups as Tannaka groups in the cases relevant to theorems
A
and
B
.

8.1.
How to rule out exceptional groups

The main goal of this section is the following result:

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle and dimension
d<(g-1)/2
. Then,

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\;\not\simeq\;E_{6},E_{7}.

This statement is sharp because for
(d,g)=(2,5)
the group
E_{6}
occurs as the Tannaka group of the Fano surface
X
of lines on a smooth cubic threefold, embedded in
A=\Alb(X)
. Actually, one can show as in
[
KLM24
, th. A]
that for
d<g/2
this is the only example of subvarieties with exceptional Tannaka group. Since we do not need this for theorems
A
and
B
, we here limit ourselves to the following positive statement:

Proposition 8.2
.

Suppose
X\subset A
is nondivisible of dimension
d<g/2
and has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle. If
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq E_{6}
, then
X
is surface with

\chi(\tilde{X},\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}})\;=\;6,\hskip 10.00002pt\;c_{2}(\tilde{X})\;=\;27\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptg\;=\;5.

The proof of this will be given in
section

8.2
.
To rule out the group
E_{7}
we will use a simpler argument, based on the decomposition of the convolution square. Recall that

\delta_{X}*\delta_{X}\;=\;\Sym^{2}(\delta_{X})\;\oplus\;\Alt^{2}(\delta_{X})

where the symmetry constraint of the convolution product acts trivially on
\Sym^{2}(\delta_{X})
and by
-\id
on
\Alt^{2}(\delta_{X})
. The representation theory of
E_{7}
then implies:

Proposition 8.3
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle. If
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq E_{7}
, then
d=\dim X
is odd, we have
X=a-X
for some
a\in A(k)
, and

\Alt^{2}(\delta_{X})\;\simeq\;P\oplus\delta_{a}\oplus N

for a simple perverse sheaf
P\in\textup{Perv}(A,\mathbb{F})
and a negligible complex
N\in{\mathrm{D}^{b}_{c}}(A,\mathbb{F})
.

The proof is the same as in
[
JKLM25
, cor. 8.2]
, since by
lemma

4.3
(1) the cycle
\cc(\delta_{X})
is integral. To exclude the situation in the above proposition for subvarieties of dimension
d<g/2
we then use as in loc. cit. a stronger geometric version of Larsen’s alternative. It will be more natural to state the latter in a more general setup. For a subvariety
X\subset A
of dimension
d
, we define

T_{+}(\delta_{X})\;:=\;\begin{cases}\Sym^{2}(\delta_{X})&\text{if $d$ is even},\\
\Alt^{2}(\delta_{X})&\text{if $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

If
X=a-X
for some
a\in A(k)
, then
\delta_{a}
is a direct summand in
T_{+}(\delta_{X})
. If
X
is nondivisible, then
a
is unique and we denote by
S_{+}(\delta_{X})
a complementary direct summand so that

T_{+}(\delta_{X})\;=\;S_{+}(\delta_{X})\oplus\delta_{a}.

The skyscraper summand gives rise to a bilinear form
\theta\colon V\otimes V\to\omega(\delta_{a})
which is symmetric if
d
is even and alternating if
d
is odd. Hence the derived subgroup
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
of the connected component of
G_{X,\omega}
is contained in
\SO(V,\theta)
is
d
is even and in
\Sp(V,\theta)
if
d
is odd. To have a uniform notation, in the case where
X
is not symmetric up to translation we set

S_{+}(\delta_{X})\;:=\;T_{+}(\delta_{X}).

We then have the following version of Larsen’s alternative:

Proposition 8.4
.

Let
X\subset A
be an integral nondivisible subvariety which has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle and dimension
d<g/2
. Then

S_{+}(\delta_{X})
is a perverse sheaf without negligible direct summands. If this perverse sheaf is simple, then

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\;=\;\begin{cases}\SL(V)&\text{if $X$ is not symmetric up to translation},\\
\SO(V,\theta)&\text{if $X$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is even},\\
\Sp(V,\theta)&\text{if $X$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

The proof of this will be given in
section

8.3
. Putting everything together, we can rule out exceptional Tannaka groups as follows:

Proof of
theorem

8.1
.

If
X\subset A
is the preimage of a subvariety
Y\subset B
under an isogeny
A\to B
, then we have
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\simeq G_{Y,\omega}^{\ast}

by
[
JKLM25
, cor. 3.5]
, and
Y
still has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle. Hence in what follows we will assume that
X\subset A
is nondivisible.

In this case,
proposition

8.2
rules out the Tannaka group
E_{6}
since
(d,g)=(2,5)
does not satisfy the condition
d<(g-1)/2
in
theorem

8.1
. Likewise, for
d<g/2
the Tannaka group
E_{7}
is ruled out by combining
propositions

8.3
and
8.4
.
∎

8.2.
Proof of
proposition

8.2

By
[
JKLM25
, cor. 4.4]
we may assume that
k=\mathbb{C}
and work analytically. We first prove that
X
is a surface with

\chi(\tilde{X},\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}})=6\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptc_{2}(\tilde{X})=27.

As noted above,
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
acts on
V:=\omega(\delta_{X})
via a minuscule representation.
The only minuscule representations of
E_{6}
are the irreducible representations of dimension
27
, hence

c_{2}(\tilde{X})\;=\;\chi_{\textup{top}}(\tilde{X})\;=\;27.

For the remaining claims we use Hodge theory. Recall that up to isomorphism the group
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}
does not depend on our choice of the fiber functor
\omega
. In what follows we fix a torsion character
\chi\colon\pi_{1}(A,0)\to\mathbb{C}^{\times}
with
\textup{H}^{i}(X,L_{\chi})=0
for all
i\neq d
and take

\omega\colon\hskip 10.00002pt\langle\delta_{X}\rangle\;\longrightarrow\;\Vect(\mathbb{C}),\hskip 20.00003ptP\;\longmapsto\;\textup{H}^{0}(A,P\otimes L_{\chi}).

Let
\mathcal{L}_{\chi}\in\Pic(A)
be the line bundle defined by the torsion character
\chi
, and let
r
be the order of this character. By
[
KM23
, prop. B.1]
the Hodge decomposition of the normalization of
[r]^{-1}(X)
gives a decomposition of the vector space
V=\textup{H}^{d}(\tilde{X},L_{\chi})
as a direct sum

V\;=\;\bigoplus_{p=0}^{d}\textup{H}^{p}(\tilde{X})\hskip 20.00003pt\textup{where}\hskip 20.00003pt\textup{H}^{p}(\tilde{X})\;=\;\textup{H}^{d-p}(\tilde{X},\Omega^{p}_{\tilde{X}}\otimes\mathcal{L}_{\chi}).

Moreover, we have

\dim\textup{H}^{p}(\tilde{X})\;=\;(-1)^{d-p}\chi(\tilde{X},\Omega^{p}_{\tilde{X}})

by
[
KM23
, prop. B.3]
. The normal bundle of the unramified morphism
\tilde{X}\to A
is ample, so
the zero locus on
\tilde{X}
of any nonzero differential form
\omega\in\textup{H}^{0}(A,\Omega^{1}_{A})
is finite by
lemma

4.3
(3). So by
[
KM23
, th. A.1]
we have

(-1)^{d-p}\chi(\tilde{X},\Omega^{p}_{\tilde{X}})\;\geqslant\;\begin{cases}g-d+1&\text{for\penalty\ $p\,\in\,\{0,d\}$},\\[1.00006pt]
2&\text{for\penalty\ $p\,\in\,\{1,d-1\}$},\\[1.00006pt]
1&\text{for\penalty\ $2\leqslant p\leqslant d-2$}.\end{cases}

Let
\lambda\colon\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\GL(V)
be the cocharacter such that
\lambda(z)
acts by
z^{2p-d}\id
on
\textup{H}^{p}(\tilde{X})
. By
[
KLM24
, th. C]
this cocharacter takes values in the subgroup
G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}\subset\GL(V)
, hence

d\;=\;2\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pth^{0}(\tilde{X})\;=\;\chi(\tilde{X},\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}})\;=\;6

due to
[
KLM24
, prop. 4.3]
. To apply this latter result, note that the cocharacter
\lambda
has the properties (H1), (H2), (H3) in loc. cit., see also
[
KM23
, prop. B.3]
.

It remains to show
g=5
, for which we will need to unveil the link with cubic threefolds. To begin with, Noether’s formula and the Hirzebruch-Riemann-Roch theorem yield
c_{1}(\tilde{X})^{2}=45
and
\chi(\tilde{X},\Omega^{1}_{\tilde{X}})=-15
. Consider the short exact sequence of vector bundles

(8.1)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow\Lie A\otimes_{k}\mathcal{O}_{\tilde{X}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{N}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{T}
is the tangent bundle of the smooth variety
\tilde{X}
and

\mathcal{N}
is the normal bundle of the unramified morphism
\tilde{X}\to A
. From this sequence we obtain

c_{1}(\mathcal{N})^{2}=45\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002ptc_{2}(\mathcal{N})=c_{1}(X)^{2}-c_{2}(X)=18.

The Gauss map
\gamma\colon\tilde{X}\to\Gr_{2}(\Lie A)
,
x\mapsto\textup{T}_{x}\tilde{X}
is a finite morphism because
X
is nondivisible
[
Deb95
, prop. 3.1]
. By interpreting
c_{2}(\mathcal{N})
as the top Segre class of
\mathcal{T}
, the positivity of
c_{2}(\mathcal{N})
implies that the morphism
\pi\colon\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{T})\to\mathbb{P}(\Lie A)
is generically finite onto its image. One easily checks that the generic degree of
\pi
is bounded by

\deg\pi\;\geqslant\;\deg D\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for the difference morphism}\hskip 10.00002ptD\colon X\times X\;\longrightarrow\;X-X,

see e.g.
[
KLM24
, lemma 7.3]
(the cited result is only for smooth subvarieties
Z\subset A
, but the same proof works for smooth varieties with a morphism
Z\to A
birational onto its image). A key point is now that

\deg D\;\geqslant\;6

by the same argument as in
[
KLM24
, prop. 4.6]
(which used smoothness only to ensure
\delta_{X}
has integral characteristic cycle). By construction the degree
\deg(\pi)
divides
c_{2}(\mathcal{N})=18
, thus

\deg\pi\in\{6,9,18\}.

Furthermore, the morphism
\tilde{X}\to\mathbb{P}(\Alt^{2}\Lie A)
given by
\gamma
followed by the Plücker embedding of
\Gr_{2}(\Lie A)
is the one defined by the canonical bundle
\mathcal{K}_{\tilde{X}}
of
\tilde{X}
. In particular
\deg\gamma
must divide
c_{1}(\mathcal{K}_{\tilde{X}})=c_{1}(\tilde{X})^{2}=45
, hence

(8.2)

\deg\gamma\in\{1,3,9\}.

It follows that image
Y\subset\mathbb{P}(\Lie A)
of
\pi
has dimension
3
and degree

\deg Y=c_{2}(\mathcal{N})/\deg\pi\in\{1,2,3\}.

The subvariety
X
generates the abelian variety
A
since it is nondegenerate, so
Y
is not contained in any hyperplane; hence
\deg Y\neq 1
. The classical lower bound (see e.g.
[
EH87
, prop. 0]
)

\deg Y\geqslant 1+\codim Y

implies that
Y
has codimension
1
or
2
in
\mathbb{P}(\Lie A)
. Up to replacing
X
by
\tilde{X}
, the proof of
[
KLM24
, prop. 7.4]
goes through without changes and shows that the codimension
2
case is not possible, hence
g=5
as required. ∎

8.3.
Proof of
proposition

8.4

With notation as in
proposition

8.4
, let
T_{-}(\delta_{X})
be the direct summand complementary to
T_{+}(\delta_{X})
in the convolution square
\delta_{X}\ast\delta_{X}
so that

\delta_{X}*\delta_{X}\;=\;T_{+}(\delta_{X})\oplus T_{-}(\delta_{X}).

We then have the following result:

Lemma 8.5
.

Suppose that
X\subset A
is nondivisible of dimension
d<g/2
and has smooth unramified normalization with ample normal bundle.

(1)

The convolution
\delta_{X}*\delta_{X}
is a perverse sheaf without negligible summands.

(2)

If
S_{+}(\delta_{X})
is simple, then

(a)

the sum morphism
f\colon\Sym^{2}X\to X+X
is birational, and

(b)

T_{-}(\delta_{X})
is a simple perverse sheaf with support
X+X
.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
KLM24
, cor. 8.7]
except for the following two changes: in (1) the Gauss map
\lx@scalerel@obj{{\rotatebox[origin={c}]{180.0}{$\mathbb{V}$}}}_{X}\to\mathbb{P}_{A}
is finite by
lemma

4.3
(3). In (2) one has to replace
\Sym^{2}X
by
\Sym^{2}\tilde{X}
. The latter is a rational homology manifold since
\tilde{X}
is smooth
[
Bri99
, prop. A.1(iii)]
; hence the intersection complex on
\Sym^{2}\tilde{X}
is the constant sheaf shifted in degree
-2d
by
[
HTT08
, prop. 8.2.21]
.
∎

Proof of
proposition

8.4
.

Part (1) of
lemma

8.5
shows in particular that
S_{+}(\delta_{X})
is a perverse sheaf. Hence it only remains to show that if this perverse sheaf is simple, then

G_{X,\omega}^{\ast}=\begin{cases}\SL(V)&\text{if $X$ is not symmetric up to translation},\\
\SO(V,\theta)&\text{if $X$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is even},\\
\Sp(V,\theta)&\text{if $X$ is symmetric up to translation and $d$ is odd}.\end{cases}

But this follows from part (2b) of
lemma

8.5
and
[
JKLM25
, lemma 3.7]
.
∎

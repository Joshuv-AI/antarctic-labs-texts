Characterization and phylogenetic analysis of the complete mitochondrial genome of Catathelasma laorentou Z.W. Ge et al. 2020
Authors: Xiaoqin Zheng, Xingting Huang, Maojie Zhao, Guohua Shen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe mitochondrial genome of C. laorentou was sequenced using Illumina in this study. The length of the circular mitochondrial genome is 56,088 bp, with a GC content of 30.26%. It encodes 44 genes, including 14 core protein coding genes (PCGs), 26 tRNA genes, 2 rRNA genes, 1 rps3 gene, and 1 free open reading frame (ORF). A phylogenetic tree was constructed based on a joint dataset of 14 mitochondrial core PCGs, which indicate that C. laorentou has the closest genetic relationship with Infundibulycybe hongyinpan and Leucopaxillus giganteus. This study provides the first mitogenome resource for the genus Catathelasma, offering crucial molecular data for its taxonomic placement and enriching fungal mitochondrial genome databases.Keywords: Mitochondrial genome, macrofungi, phylogenetic analysis, basidiomycota, comparative genomics, tricholomataceae

Introduction

Catathelasma laorentou Z.W. Ge et al. 2020, as a type of fungus in the Tricholomataceae family with important ecological and economic value, has unique fruiting body morphology and often forms mycorrhizal symbiosis with coniferous forests (Ge et al. 2020; Liu et al. 2012). It plays a key role in nutrient cycling and tree health maintenance in forest ecosystems. This species not only has potential edible and medicinal value (Liu et al. 2016; Liu et al. 2018), but its unique ecological adaptability also provides an ideal model for studying the interaction mechanism between fungi and the environment. However, due to the ambiguity in defining its morphological characteristics in traditional taxonomy and the lack of in-depth molecular systematics research, its taxonomic status has long been controversial, limiting further exploration of its ecological functions and resource value.

The fungal mitochondrial genome has become one of the core tools for analyzing the evolutionary history, taxonomic relationships, and population genetic structure of fungal taxa due to its compact structure, moderate evolutionary rate, and rich phylogenetic information (Li et al. 2013; Sandor et al. 2018; Zardoya 2020; Prevost et al. 2018). In recent years, research on the mitochondrial genome of large fungi has become popular, focusing on researching gene recombination, horizontal transfer, and codon preference (Wang et al. 2018; Nair and Shivaprasad 2023; Zhang et al., 2024). Especially in the Basidiomycota, mitochondrial genome data has been widely used to clarify the phylogenetic relationships of higher-order taxonomic groups, reveal the relationship between lineage differentiation, geographic distribution, and ecological adaptability, and provide key molecular data for the study of fungal diversity evolution (Kobayashi et al. 2023; Li et al. 2022; Ma et al. 2025; Zeb et al. 2024).

To reveal the mitochondrial genome characteristics of C. laorentou, this study sequenced, assembled, and annotated its complete mitochondrial genome for the first time. A multi gene phylogenetic tree was constructed to accurately locate the taxonomic position of this species in Basidiomycota. This study provides new data resources for the study of fungal mitochondrial evolution and promotes understanding of the Tricholomataceae phylogenetic system.

Materials and methods

Sample collection and identification

Fresh specimens of C. laorentou were collected from the Jiuzhaigou Valley Scenic and Historic Interest Area in Sichuan Province, China, during 2025 (Figure 1). The samples were immediately placed on ice packs for transport to the laboratory. Morphological identification was performed based on macroscopic characteristics. For molecular confirmation, genomic DNA was extracted, and the internal transcribed spacer (ITS) region of ribosomal DNA was amplified and sequenced. A voucher specimen was deposited at the Culture Collection Center of Chengdu University under the accession number cduZJ_01 (contact person: Xiaoqin Zheng; email: zhengxiaoqin@cdu.edu.cn).

Basidiocarps of Catathelasma laorentou in natural habitat, showing typical features: fleshy, centrally depressed pileus with a prominent umbo, decurrent lamellae, and a robust, tapering stipe with a distinct ring zone. Photo was taken by Xiaoqin Zheng.

Mitochondrial genome sequencing, assembly, and annotation

Total genomic DNA was extracted from the fruiting bodies of C. laorentou using a commercial fungal DNA extraction kit (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA, USA). A sequencing library was constructed with the NEBNext® Ultra™ II DNA Library Prep Kit (New England Biolabs, Beijing, China) following the manufacturer’s protocol. Paired-end (2 × 150 bp) sequencing was performed on an Illumina HiSeq 2500 platform (Illumina, San Diego, CA, USA). Raw reads were subjected to quality control: low-quality sequences and adapter contaminants were removed using ngsShoRT (Chen et al. 2014) and AdapterRemoval v2 (Schubert et al. 2016), respectively. De novo assembly of the mitochondrial genome was conducted with NOVOPlasty v4.3.3 (Dierckxsens et al. 2017), using a k-mer size of 33. The assembled mitochondrial genome was initially annotated with the MFannot (Valach et al. 2014) and MITOS (Bernt et al. 2013) webservers, following established pipelines (Li et al., 2020; Li et al. 2023). Protein-coding genes (PCGs) and open reading frames (ORFs) longer than 100 amino acids were predicted or verified using the NCBI ORF Finder (Coordinators 2017). Functional annotation of PCGs/ORFs was performed by BLASTP searches against the NCBI non-redundant protein database (Bleasby and Wootton 1990). Exon-intron boundaries were determined using exonerate v2.2 (Slater and Birney 2005). Transfer RNA (tRNA) genes were identified and verified with tRNAscan-SE v1.3.1 (Lowe and Chan 2016). A circular map of the mitochondrial genome was generated using OGDraw v1.2 (Lohse et al. 2013). The structure of genes containing introns was visualized with the PMGmap online tool (http://www.1kmpg.cn/pmgmap) (Zhang et al., 2024).

Phylogenetic analysis

Phylogenetic reconstruction was carried out according to previously described methods (Li et al., 2020; Li et al. 2021). Ganoderma leucocontextum (Polyporales) was selected as the outgroup (Li et al., 2019). Fourteen conserved mitochondrial protein-coding genes (atp6, atp8, atp9, cob, cox1, cox2, cox3, nad1, nad2, nad3, nad4, nad4L, nad5, and nad6) were extracted from the mitochondrial genome of C. laorentou and 14 other fungal species (see Figure 3 for list). Intron regions, if present, were excluded. Each gene was individually aligned using MAFFT v7.037 (Katoh et al. 2019). The aligned sequences were then concatenated into a single dataset using SequenceMatrix v1.7.8 (Vaidya et al. 2011). A partition homogeneity test was performed to assess potential conflicts among genes. The optimal partitioning scheme and corresponding nucleotide substitution models for the concatenated dataset were determined using PartitionFinder 2.1.1 (Lanfear et al. 2017). Phylogenetic trees were inferred using both Bayesian Inference (BI) and Maximum Likelihood (ML) methods. BI analysis was conducted with MrBayes v3.2.6 (Ronquist et al. 2012). ML analysis was performed with RAxML v8.0.0 (Stamatakis 2014), conducting 10,000 rapid bootstrap replicates to assess nodal support.

Results

Sequencing of the C. laorentou mitochondrial genome achieved an average coverage depth of 547.84× (Supplementary Figure S1). No cis-splicing gene was identified in the mitochondrial genome of Catathelasma laorentou. The complete mitochondrial genome was assembled into a circular molecule of 56,088 bp, exhibiting a GC content of 30.26%. The overall nucleotide composition was asymmetric, with A (33.99%) and T (35.75%) being more prevalent than G (15.57%) and C (14.69%).

Annotation of the genome identified a total of 44 genes (Figure 2). This complement includes 14 core protein-coding genes (PCGs) (atp6, atp8, atp9, cob, cox1, cox2, cox3, nad1, nad2, nad3, nad4, nad4L, nad5, and nad6), 26 transfer RNA (tRNA) genes, 2 ribosomal RNA (rRNA) genes (rns and rnl), one rps3 gene, and one free-standing open reading frame (ORF) of unknown function. Notably, no introns were detected within any of the PCGs.

The circular mitochondrial genome map of Catathelasma laorentou. Colored blocks outside each ring indicate that the genes are on the direct strand, while colored blocks within the ring indicate that the genes are located on the reverse strand. The inner grayscale bar graph shows the GC content of the mitochondrial sequences. The circle inside the GC content graph marks the 50% threshold.

Phylogenetic analyses based on the concatenated sequences of the 14 core mitochondrial PCGs were performed using both Bayesian Inference (BI) and Maximum Likelihood (ML) methods. The GTR+I + G model was selected for the first codon positions of cox1, cox2, cox3, and cob, while the GTR+G model was applied to the remaining partitions. The resulting trees from both approaches were topologically congruent and showed strong nodal support with comparable branch lengths (Figure 3). The analysis robustly placed C. laorentou within a clade alongside Infundibulicybe hongyinpan (NC_088561) and Leucopaxillus giganteus (NC_088560), indicating a close phylogenetic relationship among these species.

Discussion and conclusion

Mitochondrial genomes have emerged as a powerful molecular tool for resolving phylogenetic relationships and understanding evolutionary histories across fungal lineages (Li et al. 2024; Wang et al. 2025). Prior to this study, the absence of a mitochondrial reference genome for any species within the genus Catathelasma limited the application of mitogenomic data in clarifying its taxonomy and evolutionary placement among other edible macrofungi.

Our work fills this knowledge gap by presenting the first complete mitochondrial genome for a Catathelasma species. The mitogenome of C. laorentou displays a compact architecture, characterized by a complete set of core respiratory genes, a full suite of tRNA genes, and a striking lack of introns. This intron-less state contrasts with many other basidiomycete mitogenomes and may represent a derived genomic feature worthy of further comparative investigation (Wang et al. 2020; Ye et al. 2020).

The robust phylogenetic reconstruction, based on a conserved mitochondrial protein dataset, provides clear molecular evidence for the systematic position of C. laorentou. Its close affinity with Infundibulicybe hongyinpan and Leucopaxillus giganteus offers crucial insights for future taxonomic revisions within the broader Tricholomataceae family (Choi et al. 2022; Wu et al. 2018). This finding helps to anchor the genus Catathelasma within the molecular phylogenetic framework of basidiomycetes.

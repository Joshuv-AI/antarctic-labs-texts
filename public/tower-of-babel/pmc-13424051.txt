Development and user-centric evaluation of a clinical decision support website application for esthetic crown lengthening planning
Authors: Mohamed Omar Elboraey, Mostafa I. Fayad, Mohammed H. AbdElaziz, Rayan Mohammedfarooq Meer, Abdullah Alqhtani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
A gummy smile and altered passive tooth eruption pose a facial aesthetic challenge. Esthetic Crown Lengthening [ECL] requires the precise integration of aesthetic principles [Golden Proportion] and periodontal diagnosis [Coslet Classification] to achieve optimal outcomes. The complexity of manual planning necessitates a standardized, objective approach. This study describes the development of a web-based Clinical Decision Support System [CDSS] for ECL planning. It evaluates user satisfaction and the perceived clinical utility of its diagnostic logic among dental professionals. A client-side web application, the “Crown Lengthening Assistant,” was developed using JavaScript to automate ECL planning based on five clinical inputs and Coslet 4 classes of altered tooth passive eruption (1 A, 2 A, 1B, and 2B). A descriptive, cross-sectional survey was conducted among a convenience sample of dental professionals to evaluate user satisfaction and perceived accuracy using a 5-point Likert scale. A total of 63 dental professionals participated, with General Dentists [41.3%] and Periodontists [23.8%] forming the largest groups. The application received exceptionally high mean scores for design and presentation (4.78 ± 0.42) and high scores for clinical logical consistency and perceived analysis reliability (4.92 ± 0.27). Overall user satisfaction regarding the application’s accuracy was documented at 92.1%. The developed CDSS effectively automates complex aesthetic and periodontal algorithms into a user-friendly digital tool. The high user satisfaction and perceived clinical utility among dental professionals suggest its potential as an effective aid for standardizing and improving the consistency of the planning process for esthetic crown lengthening.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1007/s44445-026-00218-z.

Introduction

The importance of smile aesthetics and the golden proportion concept

The aesthetic appearance of the anterior region of the upper jaw and the gummy smile is not merely a physical trait; it has a direct impact on the social and functional life of the individual. Discrepancies in upper anterior tooth dimensions and an excessive gummy smile decrease self-confidence and directly affect individuals’ engagement in society. Therefore, therapeutic procedures and surgical interventions cannot be considered local therapeutic procedures alone; they are considerably more extensive, as they include gingival and psychological treatment, reflected in restoring the individual’s self-confidence and their integration within their personal and professional society (Foulger et al. 2010). Interventions aimed at refining these parameters, such as esthetic crown lengthening, should be conceptualized not merely as cosmetic enhancements. Rather, they function as therapeutic modalities, offering direct contributions to the patient’s Oral Health-Related Quality of Life by promoting favorable social perception and mitigating dentally induced social anxiety.

Modern macro-aesthetic dental planning is primarily anchored in the mathematical constant known as the Golden Proportion. Although this ratio was intended to elucidate harmonious sequential dimensions, its direct application to intra-tooth dimensions—specifically within the maxillary central incisors—has been the subject of significant evaluation in contemporary dental literature (Levin 1978; Snow 1999). Studies consistently demonstrate that the most aesthetically acceptable and applicable ratio, particularly for anterior teeth, is a width-to-length ratio approaching 0.78. This value serves as an objective standard for determining the ideal clinical crown length, which is a fundamental element for achieving overall smile symmetry and a guiding factor for precision procedures such as esthetic crown lengthening. The tool calculates the ideal length using the formula: (LIdeal = Width /0.78) (Zarbah et al. 2022).

While the universal applicability of the Golden Proportion remains open to challenge, its adoption as the computational basis for this tool was dictated by its established integration with prevailing periodontal classification systems, notably Coslet’s framework, thereby providing the most clinically relevant and standardized metric for preliminary assessment.

Diagnostic basis: altered passive eruption and coslet classification

The clinical appearance of excessively short dental crowns, often resulting in a gingival smile, is a common manifestation of Altered Passive Eruption (APE), a developmental disorder of gingival positioning. APE is defined as the incomplete migration of the gingival margin to the level of the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ) after the completion of the active eruption phase of the tooth. As a result of this disorder, a large portion of the anatomical crown remains covered by excessive gingival and osseous tissues (Gargiulo et al. 1961). The importance lies in making an accurate diagnosis to distinguish it from true macrodontia, a condition in which the tooth is actually larger than normal. The diagnosis of APE is usually confirmed by bone sounding to precisely determine the location of the cemento-enamel junction and the crest of the bone. The accuracy of this diagnosis is of great significance because the surgical management of APE differs fundamentally from that appropriate for cases of true macrodontia (Madaan et al. 2015).

The Coslet classification system is considered a fundamental diagnostic tool for systematically evaluating the causes of Altered Passive Eruption (APE) and for informing appropriate clinical decisions (Coslet et al. 1977). This classification is based on two key clinical measurements: probing depth and the position of the alveolar bone crest relative to the cemento-enamel junction (CEJ), which allow precise differentiation between the contributions of soft and hard tissues to the condition.

Justification

Manual pre-surgical planning for esthetic crown lengthening is inherently labor-intensive, as it requires the clinician to perform a series of precise, overlapping calculations for each tooth in the esthetic zone. This process must accurately integrate the ideal crown length based on the golden proportion with the more complex assessment derived from the Coslet classification scheme. In addition, the amount of osseous recontouring required must be precisely determined in accordance with biologic width standards.

The repetition of these multifactorial, highly calculation-based steps across the maxillary anterior teeth significantly increases the likelihood of error while simultaneously extending the duration of the pre-operative planning phase. The significance of this tool lies in its ability to automate all of these demanding procedures across the entire esthetic zone (#13 to #23), thereby substantially reducing chairside time devoted to complex planning and enhancing the efficiency of the clinical workflow.

The central hypothesis is that the shift towards digital profiling of this diagnostic protocol radically enhances the objectivity and reproducibility of results among surgeons in esthetic crown lengthening procedures. By automating multi-factor assessments, the tool provides the surgeon with accurate quantitative data derived from the Coslet classification and the principles of biologic width, enabling a conclusive determination of the extent of bone reshaping needed and the exact amount of gingival remodeling required. This digital decision-support system aims to neutralize the variance introduced by clinical self-assessment, ensuring that the surgical plan is anchored exclusively in established periodontal parameters (Nevins and Skurow 1984).

The aim of this study was, therefore, to overcome the difficulties in esthetic periodontal planning by developing a dedicated web-based Clinical Decision Support System (CDSS) for Esthetic Crown Lengthening (ECL) assessment. The main aim of this study is to describe the architectural development of this application and to test its efficacy by assessing users’ satisfaction and perceptions of diagnostic accuracy among dental professionals. By developing a standardized digital workflow, this study aims to bridge the gap between complex periodontal parameters and the perceived predictability of clinical outcome in the aesthetic zone.

Materials and methods

Software development

The diagnostic program interface was carefully designed as a standalone web platform that operates entirely on the client side to achieve the highest levels of stability, accessibility, and operational reliability across diverse clinical environments. The fundamental structure, which provides organized templates for data entry (such as the tooth-specific measurement table) and mechanisms for displaying the results, was built using HTML.

The visual aspects of the application and its dynamic responsiveness, which are essential to efficient performance across various clinical devices from desktop computers to tablets, were effectively implemented using the Tailwind CSS framework, which follows the “utility-first” philosophy.

As for the central computational engine responsible for executing the quantitative diagnostic algorithms—including calculating the golden proportion and applying the multi-factor Coslet classification logic—it was fully programmed using JavaScript. This client-side architecture provides the application with complete independence and high performance, without relying on external databases or server-side functionality. It enables rapid, efficient deployment that directly supports clinical decision-making at the point of care, grounded in evidence on the effectiveness of digital diagnostic systems in dentistry.

The version evaluated in this study was a fully client-side web application executed entirely within the user’s browser. The application did not collect, transmit, or store clinical information on external servers or cloud databases. All calculations were performed locally using JavaScript. Survey data were collected separately through Google Forms under the approved ethical protocol.

Furthermore, five essential clinical inputs were defined within the code—current length, width, gingival depth, bone sounding, and keratinized gingiva—as the pivotal variables for diagnostic assessment of the esthetic region extending from tooth #13 to tooth #23.

Cross-sectional survey

This survey was designed to assess the accuracy of performance analysis and application quality, and to gather open proposals for improvement. The survey was built and implemented on the Google Forms platform (using a system similar to Google Heart Survey), which enables reliable data collection. The questionnaire was divided into three parts: the first part was concerned with collecting data on the degree of professional classification of the participants and the frequency of performing esthetic crown lengthening procedures, the second part was concerned with the evaluation of the accuracy of the application, the quality of the evaluation and the design of the program interface, and the third part was concerned with the collection of suggestions for continuous development.

The survey was designed in accordance with the methodological practices used in the formulation of mHealth App assessment questionnaires, which focus on ease of use, technical quality, and professional benefit (Woulfe et al. 2022). Data were collected using Google Forms (which supports research purposes in the collection of electronic survey data) (Vasantha Raju and Harinarayana 2016). The study used a cross-sectional descriptive design to assess participants’ satisfaction levels (Alshali et al. 2024).

A 5-point Likert scale was used in the questionnaire as follows: 5 represents very satisfied, 4 is satisfied, 3 is neutral (the middle point of the scale), 2 is dissatisfied, and 1 is the least value and indicates not satisfied at all. This provides a balanced scope for measuring attitudes and opinions, indicating a quantitative evaluation methodology.

The survey was disseminated globally through specialized professional dental communities on social media, accessible to any practitioner regardless of geographic location. Descriptive details within this section outline the scope and structured nature of the survey items used. To encourage impartial feedback, no compensatory incentives were offered, and participation was entirely voluntary (Brasel et al. 2020).

Eligibility of participants was ensured by including survey questions that identified their specialty, such as whether they were general dentists, or periodontists, or other dental specialities. mHealth App assessment questionnaires settings were applied to prevent duplicate submissions, ensuring that no individual could respond more than once.

Statistical analysis

The collected data were analyzed using an advanced software package to provide accurate and reliable results. Software Used: Statistical calculations were performed in Python. Descriptive Analysis: Descriptive statistics were applied to analyze and summarize the sample data, including means, standard deviations (SD), and percentages to illustrate the distribution of responses. Specialized Python libraries, including Pandas, were used for data processing and descriptive statistical analysis. Ethical considerations The ethical approval required to conduct this study was obtained via ethic committee, college of dentistry, Taibah university with approval number (TUCDREC/031225/ MOElboraey). The survey did not include any sensitive data. It was limited to providing objective, non-sensitive feedback on professional performance and the quality of the application, while ensuring the confidentiality and anonymity of participants.

Results

Presentation of tool outputs

The precise outputs generated across the represented subtypes of Altered Passive Eruption (APE) demonstrated high procedural reliability and perceived clinical benefit of the developed software. By using carefully selected hypothetical case scenarios entered into the application’s digital interface, the operational functionality and perceived clinical utility of the application were demonstrated through simulated clinical scenarios. For example, a case with excessive soft tissue with a 5 mm probing depth and a bone sounding of 3 mm, where the classification represents 1 A of the Coslet classification, in addition to the clinically apparent crown length and width, the type of classification was properly analyzed using the application and matches the known classification of the case and also the output systematically displayed the calculated measures, including the ideal clinical crown length aesthetically required (Lideal), and the exact extension of the crown (Erequired). In addition, the multi-dental layout feature, by displaying simultaneous data for the entire aesthetic area (#13 to #23) in a unified tabular format, underscores the powerful tool’s ability to unify both aesthetic decision-making and decision-making in periodontal disease across the smile arch.

Survey results

The final sample size of 63 participants was sufficient for this descriptive evaluation. A post-hoc confidence interval analysis was conducted. For the primary evaluation metric (perceived analysis accuracy), which yielded a mean of 4.92 (SD = 0.27), the 95% confidence interval was [4.75, 4.93]. The small margin of error (0.09) confirms that the sample size provided high statistical precision for representing the users’ clinical feedback and satisfaction levels (Petrie and Sabin 2019).

The survey was conducted by 63 participants, mostly general dentists (41.27%), followed by periodontists (23.81%), with 19.05% other specialists, and 15.87% dental students as shown in Fig. 2. The frequency of procedures varied, with monthly (31.75%) being the most common and weekly (26.98%) the second most common as shown in Fig. 3. The mean design and presentation ratings were 4.78 ± 0.42, while the application’s accuracy was 4.92 ± 0.27, both on a 5-point scale. Satisfaction with the clinical scope, applicability, and quality/condition was overwhelmingly positive. Where 85.71% reported being very satisfied, with a mean of 4.86 ± 0.35. Cross-tables showed higher ideal scores for design among weekly users (100%) than among annual users (60%). Moderate positive associations were observed between assessments (r = 0.55–0.87), but chi-squared tests did not show any significant associations between demographics and assessments (p > 0.05). Six qualitative proposals focused on time-savings, ease of use, and regulatory benefits, with one recommending increased flexibility in single-tooth analytics.

Discussion

This study described the development of a novel web-based Clinical Decision Support System [CDSS] for esthetic crown lengthening [ECL] planning. It evaluated its user satisfaction and perceived accuracy among dental professionals. The principal finding is the exceptionally high level of user satisfaction and perceived accuracy, with mean scores of 4.78 ± 0.42 for design/presentation and 4.92 ± 0.27 for perceived accuracy of data analysis, and an overall satisfaction rate of 92.1% regarding the application’s accuracy. These results suggest a strong clinical acceptance and perceived utility for the “Crown Lengthening Assistant” as a tool for ECL planning.

The essential clinical advantage of the digital assistant lies in its ability to standardize outcomes across the entire maxillary esthetic zone, extending from canine to canine (teeth 13 to 23). Whereas conventional manual methodologies rely on treating each tooth individually—thereby increasing the likelihood of inter-tooth discrepancies in the final position of the gingival zenith—the digital system provides a highly standardized, consistent, and reproducible alternative, even with repeated data entries. In contrast to manual approaches, the digital system analyzes and processes esthetic and periodontal parameters—such as width and bone-sounding—for all six anterior teeth simultaneously, thereby offering a unified, objective solution for multi-tooth planning. By integrating esthetic metrics, including the Golden Proportion, with critical gingival dimensions such as the biologic width into unified computational outputs governing the entire smile arc, the system may promote greater consistency in surgical planning and esthetic assessment. This constitutes a fundamental pillar for achieving comprehensive harmonic integration and supports contemporary full-arch esthetic planning (Hershaw et al. 2024; Coachman et al. 2021; Sorrentino et al. 2024).

The digital decision-support systems for crown-lengthening procedures integrated into the application serve as a primary aid, providing precise, quantitative clinical guidance for alveolar bone removal. This may assist clinicians in reducing the risk of violating biologic width during treatment planning. Moreover, the system’s ability to compute the estimated osteotomy depth is aligned with the latest CAD-CAM protocols used in periodontal treatment planning, thereby supporting the standardization of treatment planning (Cong et al. 2024).

The high satisfaction scores observed in this study are consistent with the literature on successful CDSS adoption, which indicates that usability and perceived usefulness are key determinants. The web-based, client-side architecture, using modern frameworks such as Tailwind CSS, likely contributed to the high mean score for design and presentation [4.78 ± 0.42], ensuring accessibility and a positive user experience across various clinical devices.

Furthermore, the high perceived accuracy [4.92 ± 0.27] suggests that the dental professionals who participated in the survey found the application’s output to be clinically sound and trustworthy, which is paramount for a tool intended to guide surgical decisions. The strong endorsement from a diverse group of practitioners, including general dentists, prosthodontists, and specialists, underscores the broad clinical relevance of a standardized planning tool for ECL.

The high ratings, especially among frequent users, reflect the system’s usability and level of integration into the clinical environment. This aligns with observations from the Technology Readiness Index, which consistently show that frequent users exhibit higher levels of digital adoption and satisfaction (Schnitzler and Bohnet-Joschko 2025). The two pivotal components of the Technology Acceptance Model, namely perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use, are crucial in ensuring the acceptance of the system in the dental sector (Kassim and Alotaibi 2023). The results of the satisfaction variables indicate a well-integrated, global experience for users. Perceived quality stands out as an important element in shaping impression experiences, and this pattern aligns with several PTA models.

The observed ceiling of consistently high scores suggests a bias in the responses, as seen in online surveys in which satisfied participants participated. This is reflected in the need for wider sampling and greater adherence to questionnaire design guidelines, such as RISQ, to reduce test bias and improve the wider applicability of the results (Sharma et al. 2021; Shelley and Horner 2021).

Constructive feedback during the survey was that the application should allow single-tooth insertion and not force the insertion of the 6 upper anterior teeth, from canine to canine, for ease of use and even for larger clinical applications when planning a single tooth. Digital educational tools and CAD/CAM technologies have been reported to enhance engagement and learning experiences among dental students. for altered passive eruption. The addition of such a feature will lead to positive learning outcomes, especially among students, and greater application flexibility when used in conjunction with CAD/CAM educational integration (Aref et al. 2025; Ku et al. 2025).

The application has demonstrated significant potential and greatly enhanced and facilitated the planning process for esthetic crown lengthening, simplifying and solving complex problems. This process was long and intertwined, causing great confusion during planning. Still, the application addressed the complex, interrelated clinical parameters during planning and facilitates their repeated application in the simplest possible way. But future longitudinal studies with larger and more diverse samples are needed to confirm current findings, as well as a long-term evaluation of the outputs. This requires further development and credibility using controlled studies.

Limitations and challenges

Despite the promising findings, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, a ‘ceiling effect’ is observed, with a high concentration of positive responses across all metrics. While this reflects strong user acceptance and the application’s perceived efficacy, it may reduce the instrument’s sensitivity to distinguish minor differences among criteria. These results, however, accurately reflect the participants’ evaluations during the assessment phase.

In addition, the outcome is largely dependent on the precision of clinicians’ hand measurements, which could introduce bias. At present, the tool does not account for the dynamic nature of aesthetics, such as the angle of the teeth axis and lip movements, which are major components of comprehensive smile design. Moreover, convenience sampling, social desirability bias, involvement of dental students, and the use of an untested questionnaire could have influenced the validity of the findings. They are based on subjective estimates of accuracy and have not been clinically verified.

While the custom questionnaire provided valuable, specialized insights into the clinical workflow, we acknowledge that the lack of standardized assessment metrics is a study limitation, making direct comparability with other software challenging. Future studies can enhance methodological rigor by incorporating validated instruments, such as the System Usability Scale (SUS).

Conclusion and recommendations

In conclusion, the tool successfully integrates aesthetic principles (Golden Proportion) and fundamental periodontal principles (Coslet classification and Biologic Width) into an objective diagnostic system and shows potential for enhancing the consistency of clinical planning as perceived by dental professionals.

A development of the tool was suggested as a future recommendation to integrate patient photographs (Digital Smile Design) and to validate the tool’s accuracy through a clinical trial comparing its output with expert clinical decision-making.

Abstract
In this article we use Gaussian measure on ℝ N {\mathbb{R}}^{N} to define the coefficients of an elliptic diffusion on an open cone of ℝ 2 {\mathbb{R}}^{2} . We prove the existence and uniqueness of a stationary distribution for this diffusion. In a companion article, we show that the diffusion constructed in this work is the inviscid limit of the laws of the “enstrophy-energy” process of a stationary N N -dimensional Galerkin-Navier-Stokes type evolution with Brownian forcing and random stirring (the strength of which can be made to go to zero in the inviscid limit). In the present work, owing to the special properties of the coefficients constructed with the Gaussian measure, we bound the distance to 1 1 of the ratio of the expected energy to the expected enstrophy (this ratio is at most 1 1 with our normalization). Together with our companion article, this shows that for suitable Brownian forcings an inviscid condensation inducing an attrition of all but the lowest modes takes place.

Introduction

The incompressible two-dimensional Navier-Stokes equation with Brownian forcing on a square or a thin torus is a classical infinite dimensional Markovian evolution, see
[
14
]
. There is an extensive theory concerning the existence and the uniqueness of a stationary distribution for these Markov processes, see
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
14
]
. However, the nature of these stationary distributions remains, except for some simple and rather unrepresentative cases, ill understood. In particular, when an inviscid limit, with an adequate balancing of the forcing, is performed, little is known about their limit behavior, see
[
7
]
,
[
13
]
, and Chapter 5 of
[
14
]
. This feature persists when considering finite dimensional evolutions corresponding to Galerkin-Navier-Stokes. While chaotic behavior of the dynamics has been established, see
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
, straightforward questions remain unanswered: existing lower bounds on the ratio between the expectation of the energy and the expectation of the vorticity (the latter being fixed in this limit procedure) are so far blind to the limit procedure, the behavior of the variance of the enstrophy is poorly understood, and the same feature holds concerning the expected trend of the stationary distributions to condense on low Fourier modes under suitable forcings, see
[
1
]
,
[
5
]
, (4.2) in
[
17
]
, p. 127 of
[
19
]
.

In the present work, motivated by these questions, we introduce and study a stationary diffusion process in an open cone of
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
. We show in the companion article
[
21
]
that this two-dimensional stochastic process is the inviscid limit of the laws of the “enstrophy-energy” process of a stationary
N
-dimensional Galerkin-Navier-Stokes type evolution with Brownian forcing and random stirring (the strength of which can be made to go to zero in the inviscid limit). In the present article, we establish a condensation bound for the stationary diffusion in the cone. It bounds in a quantitative fashion the distance to
1
of the ratio of the expected energy to the expected enstrophy (which is at most
1
with our normalization). The coefficients of the two-dimensional diffusion that we construct here involve the conditional expectation for a Gaussian measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
of the square of the coordinate functions given the value of two quadratic forms on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
corresponding to the enstrophy and the energy.

We will now discuss the results in more detail.

We refer to Section 1 for precise assumptions. We consider on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
with
N=2n
, and
n\geq 4
, two quadratic forms
|x|^{2}=\Sigma_{\ell}\,x_{\ell}^{2}
and
|x|^{2}_{-1}=\Sigma_{\ell}\,x_{\ell}^{2}/\lambda_{\ell}
, where
\lambda_{2i}=\lambda_{2i-1}
(denoted by
\mu_{i}),1\leq i\leq n
, is increasing and
\mu_{1}=1
. The first quadratic form corresponds to (twice) the enstrophy and the second to (twice) the energy in the terminology of the paragraph above. We also have a Gaussian measure
\mu
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
for which the coordinate functions
x_{\ell},1\leq\ell\leq N
, are independent centered Gaussian variables with same variance equal to
a/2
, with
a>0
. Further, we consider the two-dimensional cone

(0.1)

C=\{w=(u,v)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{2};\,0\leq v\leq u\},

and its interior
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. We construct in Section 2, see also Appendix B, good versions
q_{\ell}(u,v)
of the conditional expectation of
x^{2}_{\ell}
given
|x|^{2}=u
, and
|x|^{2}_{-1}=v
, such that

(0.2)

q_{\ell}(|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1})=E^{\mu}\big[x_{\ell}^{2}\,\big|\,|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1}\big],\,\mbox{$\mu$-a.s. for $1\leq\ell\leq N$}.

The functions
q_{\ell}
are Lipschitz in
C
, positive in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, homogeneous of degree
1
, and on each subsector
\{(u,v)=C
;
0\leq\mu_{i-1}\,v\leq u\leq u_{i}v\}
, with
2\leq i\leq n
, they coincide with a rational function (dependent on
i
).

With the functions
q_{\ell}
, and a sequence
\delta_{\ell}\in(-1,0]
,
1\leq\ell\leq N
, such that
\delta_{2i}=\delta_{2i-1}
, for
1\leq i\leq n
(see also the observation below (
3.6
) about this last condition), we construct the differential operator on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
:

(0.3)

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}\psi=&\;2a\Big\{\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u}\psi+2\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u,v}\psi+\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\,\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial_{v}^{2}\psi\Big\}\\
&+\Big(a\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\lambda_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\Big)\,\partial_{u}\psi+\Big(a\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}q_{\ell}\Big)\,\partial_{v}\psi.\end{split}

We show in Section 3 that (
0.3
) gives rise to an elliptic second order differential operator with Lipschitz coefficients in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, and a well-posed martingale problem, see Theorem
3.2
. We prove in Section 4 that this diffusion process has a unique stationary distribution, see Theorem
4.1
, and we provide a martingale problem characterization for the law
\widetilde{P}
of the stationary process, see Proposition
4.2
(which is useful in the context of the convergence in law that we prove in the companion article
[
21
]
).

The stationary distribution mentioned above is typically unknown, but when all
\delta_{\ell}
coincide it is explicit. For instance, when all
\delta_{\ell}=0
, it coincides with the image measure of
\mu
under the map:
x\longrightarrow(|x|^{2},|x|^{2}_{-1})
, see Remark
4.5
.

In the companion article
[
21
]
we establish that
\widetilde{P}
mentioned above is the limit in law of the process
(|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2},|X^{\varepsilon}_{t}|^{2}_{-1})_{t\geq 0}
, as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, when
X^{\varepsilon}_{t},t\geq 0
, is the stationary solution of the Stratonovich differential equation on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
:

(0.4)

dX^{\varepsilon}_{t}=\big(-\Lambda X^{\varepsilon}_{t}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\varepsilon}$}\,b(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})\big)\,dt+\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\big(\lambda_{\ell}\,a(1+\delta_{\ell})\big)^{\frac{1}{2}}e_{\ell}\,\,d\beta_{\ell}(t)+\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\kappa}{\varepsilon}$}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}\displaystyle\sum^{M}_{1}Z_{m}(X^{\varepsilon}_{t})\,\circ d\widetilde{\beta}_{m}(t),

where
(\Lambda x)_{\ell}=\lambda_{\ell}\,x_{\ell}
, for
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
1\leq\ell\leq N
,
(e_{\ell})_{1\leq\ell\leq N}
is the canonical basis of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
,
\beta_{\ell}
,
1\leq\ell\leq N
,
\widetilde{\beta}_{m},1\leq m\leq M
, are independent Brownian motions, and
\kappa>0
measures the strength of the stirring and can be chosen arbitrarily small (and the limit law
\widetilde{P}
does not depend on
\kappa
). Moreover,
b(x)_{\ell}
is for each
\ell
a quadratic form in the coordinates of
x
, such that
{\rm div}\,b=0
,
\langle x,b(x)\rangle=0
,
\langle x,b(x)\rangle_{-1}=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
(with
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{-1}
the scalar products attached to
|\cdot|
and
|\cdot|_{-1}
). As for the stirring vector fields
Z_{m}
,
1\leq m\leq M
, they are constructed in
[
21
]
, and they satisfy
{\rm div}\,Z_{m}=0
,
\langle x,Z_{m}(x)\rangle=0
,
\langle x,Z_{m}(x)\rangle_{-1}=0
on
{\mathbb{R}}^{N}
.

As mentioned above, the convergence to the same limit law
\widetilde{P}
holds for all values of
\kappa>0
in (
0.4
), and hence the convergence in law to
\widetilde{P}
holds as well for some (non-explicit) function
\kappa_{\varepsilon}>0
tending to
0
with
\varepsilon
. When
\kappa=0
, the equation corresponding to (
0.4
) covers by suitable choices of
b(x)
some instances of a Galerkin-Navier-Stokes equation with Brownian forcing, see Remark 1.1 of
[
21
]
.

In the present article, we obtain a condensation bound for the law
\widetilde{P}
of the stationary diffusion in the open cone
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. It can be read in the perspective of the inviscid limit in law statement mentioned above (
0.4
). We introduce two positive numbers related to the Brownian forcing:

(0.5)

B_{0}=a\sum_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})<B_{1}=a\sum_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell}),

and we denote by
(U_{0},V_{0})
the location at time
0
of the stationary diffusion on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
governed by
\widetilde{P}
(so that
U_{0}>V_{0}>0,\widetilde{P}
-a.s.). We show in Theorem
5.1
a stronger form of the following inequality:

(0.6)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{for any $\ell_{0}$ in $\{3,\dots,N\}$, $2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\leq\displaystyle\frac{B_{1}-B_{0}}{\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1}+\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-1}\;\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{0}}{N-\ell_{0}}\;B_{0}$},\\[4.30554pt]
\mbox{with $B_{0}=2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}]$}\end{array}

(and
\widetilde{E}[\cdot]
the
\widetilde{P}
-expectation).

In particular, when
\ell_{0}
can be chosen so that
B_{1}/B_{0}
is much smaller than
\lambda_{\ell_{0}}
and
\ell_{0}
is much smaller than
N
, then
\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\,/\,\widetilde{E}[U_{0}]
is close to
0
, corresponding to “condensation”. The value
B_{1}/B_{0}
can be viewed as a kind of effective spectral value for the Brownian forcing, and the bound (
0.6
) is thus informative when this effective spectral value of the forcing is much smaller than
\lambda_{N}
, see also Remark 6.2 of
[
21
]
.

As it turns out, the proof of the condensation bound in Theorem
5.1
relies on the special
monotonicity property
of the functions
q_{\ell}
, as
\ell
varies in
\{3,\dots,N\}
, see Theorem
2.3
. In particular, it implies that the functions
q_{\ell}
with
\ell
small compared to
N
have size at most of order
1/N
. This feature links the Gaussian measure
\mu
to the condensation effect for the possibly highly non-Gaussian measure corresponding to the stationary distribution of the diffusion in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, when
B_{1}/B_{0}
is much smaller than
\lambda_{N}
.

As an aside, “condensation” is known to occur under the Gaussian measure, see Section 3.1 of
[
4
]
. In essence, fixing an infinite sequence
\lambda_{\ell}
tending to infinity, in a large
N
regime, under the measure
\mu
, when
0<v<u
are given, and one “forces
|x|^{2}/N
and
|x|^{2}_{-1}/N
to respectively take values close to
u
and
v
”, then
(x^{2}_{1}+x^{2}_{2})/N
is close to
v
(and the ratio of the sum of the remaining
x^{2}_{\ell}
with
N
is close to
u-v
). In the present article, we see that Gaussian measure also induces condensation for the stationary distribution of some of the diffusions that we consider; but this effect now happens through the properties of the functions
q_{\ell}
, notably Theorem
2.3
.

Let us finally describe the organization of the article.

Section 1 presents the set-up. Section 2 collects some properties of the functions

, imported in part from Appendix B, and proves the important monotonicity property in Theorem

, and establishes a Lyapunov-Foster condition in Proposition

. Section 4 is concerned with the stationary distribution of the diffusion process, and collects some of its properties. Section 5 is devoted to the “condensation bound”, see Theorem
5.1
. Appendix A contains some probability density and volume calculations. Appendix B establishes some of the properties of the functions

.

Combined with the continuity of the functions

(2.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}q_{\ell}(u,v)=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-2}$}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{u-v}{1-\lambda^{-1}_{\ell}}$}\;\;\mbox{for $3\leq\ell\leq N$ and}\\[8.61108pt]
q_{\ell}(u,v)=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{v}{2}$}-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-2}$}\;\displaystyle\sum_{m\geq 3}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{u-v}{\lambda_{m}-1}$}\;\mbox{for $\ell=1,2$, when $(u,v)\in\overline{\Delta}_{1}$},\end{array}

Remark 2.2
.

Note that
u\leq\lambda_{3}\,v
by assumption in (
2.10
), so that

q_{1}(u,v)=q_{2}(u,v)\geq\mbox{ \footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{v}{2}$}-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\displaystyle\frac{1}{N-2}$}\;\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\geq 3}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}-1}{\lambda_{\ell}-1}$}\;v\geq 0.

Also, the formulas (
2.10
) cannot be expected to extend to the entire cone

, for they do not satisfy the boundary conditions (
2.4
) - (
2.6
).
\square

The next proposition plays a crucial role in the proof of the condensation bound in Theorem
5.1
. In particular, it shows that the functions
q_{\ell}
of low index
\ell
are small when
N
is large. An important ingredient to this effect is the monotonicity property (
2.11
) in the theorem below. We recall the notation
\widehat{q}_{i}
in (
2.2
).

Theorem 2.3
.

(Monotonicity and upper bound)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\mbox{$(1-\mu_{i}^{-1})\;\widehat{q}_{i}(u,v)$ is non-decreasing in $i$ for $2\leq i\leq n$ and $(u,v)\in C$}.

(2.12)

\displaystyle\widehat{q}_{i}\,(u,v)\leq[(n-i+1)(1-\mu_{i}^{-1})]^{-1}(u-v),\;\mbox{for $2\leq i\leq n$ and $(u,v)\in C$}.

Proof.

Note that by (
2.3
) one has

(2.13)

\displaystyle\sum^{n}_{2}\;(1-\mu^{-1}_{i})\;\widehat{q}_{i}\,(u,v)=u-v,\;\mbox{for all $(u,v)\in C$}.

All the summands in the left member above are non-negative and (
2.12
) is an immediate consequence of the monotonicity property (
2.11
). To complete the proof of Theorem
2.3
, we thus only need to show (
2.11
). Due to the continuity of the
\widehat{q}_{i}
, it suffices to prove (
2.11
) when
(u,v)\in\;\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
.

To this end, we introduce
\widetilde{\Sigma}=\{2,\dots,n\}
(we recall that
\Sigma=\{3,\dots,n\}
, see (
A.4
) and that
\mu_{1}=1
, see (
1.4
)) and define the affine isomorphism
h
between
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Sigma}
and the affine hyperplane
\widetilde{H}=\{\widetilde{\sigma}=(\widetilde{s}_{i})_{2\leq i\leq n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
;
\Sigma_{2}^{n}\,\widetilde{s}_{i}=u-v\}
:

(2.14)

\begin{array}[]{l}h(\sigma)=\widetilde{\sigma}\;\mbox{with}\;\sigma=(s_{i})_{3\leq i\leq n},\;\widetilde{\sigma}=(\widetilde{s}_{i})_{2\leq i\leq n},\;\mbox{and}\\[4.30554pt]
\widetilde{s}_{i}=(1-\mu^{-1}_{i})\;s_{i},\;\mbox{for}\;3\leq i\leq n,\;\widetilde{s}_{2}=u-v-\sum^{n}_{3}(1-\mu_{i}^{-1})\,s_{i}.\end{array}

Then,
h
maps
{\mathbb{V}}_{u,v}=\{(s_{3},\dots,s_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\Sigma}_{+}
;
u-\mu_{1}v\geq\Sigma^{n}_{3}\,(1-\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{i}})\,s_{i}
and
u-\mu_{2}v\leq\Sigma^{n}_{3}\,(1-\frac{\mu_{2}}{\mu_{i}})\,s_{i}\}
, see (
A.5
), one-to-one onto

(2.15)

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}=\{\widetilde{\sigma}=(\widetilde{s}_{i})_{2\leq i\leq n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}_{+};\;\Sigma_{2}^{n}\,\widetilde{s}_{i}=u-v\;\,\mbox{and}\;\,\Sigma_{2}^{n}\,\gamma_{i}\,\widetilde{s}_{i}\geq u-\mu_{2}v\}\subseteq\widetilde{H},\\[4.30554pt]
\mbox{with}\;\gamma_{i}=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1-\mu_{2}/\mu_{i}}{1-\mu_{1}/\mu_{i}}$}=1-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{2}-\mu_{1}}{\mu_{i}-\mu_{1}}$},\;\mbox{for $2\leq i\leq n$ (and we recall that $\mu_{1}=1)$}.\end{array}

Note that the
\gamma_{i}
are non-decreasing in
i
and that
\gamma_{2}=0
. Further,
h
maps the Lebesgue measure on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\Sigma}
into a constant multiple of the surface measure on the hyperplane
\widetilde{H}
, so that
h
maps the barycenter of
{\mathbb{V}}_{u,v}
on the barycenter of
\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}
, which we denote by
\widetilde{q}(u,v)=(\widetilde{q}_{i}(u,v))_{2\leq i\leq n}
. We thus see by (
B.16
) that

(2.16)

h\big(\widehat{q}(u,v)\big)=\widetilde{q}(u,v),\;\mbox{with the notation $\widehat{q}(u,v)=\big(\widehat{q}_{i}(u,v)\big)_{3\leq i\leq n}$, i.e.}

(2.17)

\begin{split}\widetilde{q}_{i}(u,v)&=(1-\mu^{-1}_{i})\,\widehat{q}_{i}(u,v),\;\mbox{for $3\leq i\leq n$},\\
\widetilde{q}_{2}(u,v)&=u-v-\Sigma^{n}_{3}\,(1-\mu_{i}^{-1})\,\widehat{q}_{i}(u,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{B.17})}}{{=}}(1-\mu^{-1}_{2})\;\widehat{q}_{2}(u,v).\end{split}

The claim (
2.11
) thus amounts to showing that

(2.18)

\mbox{$\widetilde{q}_{i}(u,v),2\leq i\leq n$, is non-decreasing in $i$}.

For this purpose we consider
i_{0}\in\{2,\dots,n-1\}
and the map
S
swapping the
i_{0}
coordinate with the
i_{0}+1
coordinate on
{\mathbb{R}}^{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
, namely, such that for
\widetilde{\sigma}=(\widetilde{s}_{i})_{2\leq i\leq n}

(2.19)

\begin{split}S(\widetilde{\sigma})_{i}&=\widetilde{s}_{i},\;\mbox{when $i\in\widetilde{\Sigma}\;\backslash\,\{i_{0},i_{0}+1\}$},\\
&=\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1},\;\mbox{when $i=i_{0}$},\\
&=\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}},\;\mbox{when $i=i_{0}+1$}.\end{split}

We also introduce (see (
2.15
) for notation)

(2.20)

\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},+}=\{\widetilde{\sigma}\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v};\;\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}}<\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1}\},\;\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},-}=\{\widetilde{\sigma}\in\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v};\;\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1}<\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}}\}.

We then note that

(2.21)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},+}$, $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},-}$ are pairwise disjoint convex polytopes contained in $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}$},\\
\mbox{and their union differs from $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}$ by a negligible set}.\end{array}

The key observation is now that

(2.22)

\mbox{$S$ maps $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},-}$ into $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},+}$},

because, see (
2.15
) for notation,

(2.23)

\gamma_{i_{0}}\,\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}}+\gamma_{i_{0}+1}\,\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1}<\gamma_{i_{0}}\,\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1}+\gamma_{i_{0}+1}\,\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}}\;\mbox{when}\;\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1}<\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}},

since their difference equals
(\gamma_{i_{0}+1}-\gamma_{i_{0}})(\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}}-\widetilde{s}_{i_{0}+1})>0
, so that
S(\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},-})\subseteq\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}
, and (
2.22
) readily follows.

The map
S
preserves the surface measure on
\widetilde{H}
, and we thus see that

(2.24)

\mbox{the barycenter $\widetilde{q}(u,v)$ of $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{u,v}$ belongs to the closure of $\widetilde{{\mathbb{V}}}_{i_{0},+}$}.

This shows that
\widetilde{q}_{i_{0}}(u,v)\leq\widetilde{q}_{i_{0}+1}(u,v)
for all
i_{0}
in
\{2,\dots,n\}
and
(u,v)
in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. The claim (
2.18
) follows, and as already observed this completes the proof of Theorem
2.3
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

In the case
0\leq v\leq u\leq\mu_{2}v
, one has the explicit formulas, see (
2.10
),
\widehat{q}_{i}(u,v)=[(n-1)(1-\mu_{i}^{-1})]^{-1}(u-v)
, for
2\leq i\leq n
. So the upper bound (
2.12
) has a good quality when

remains small compared to

. Also, when

, we know by (

) that
\widehat{q}_{n}(u,v)=u=(1-\mu_{n}^{-1})^{-1}(u-v)
, so that the upper bound (
2.12
) is sharp in this case.
\square

3
The effective diffusion

In this section, we construct by means of a martingale problem, see Theorem
3.2
, the diffusion in the interior of the two-dimensional cone
C
defined in (
1.11
). Its generator is formally obtained by replacing the terms
x^{2}_{\ell}
in (
1.10
) by the functions
q_{\ell}
of Section 2. In the course of the proof we establish an important Lyapunov-Foster condition, see (
3.21
) of Proposition
3.3
.

With this in mind we introduce the diffusion matrix
\widetilde{a}
and the drift
\widetilde{b}
via

(3.1)

\widetilde{a}=4a\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}&\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\\[4.30554pt]
\Sigma_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}&\Sigma_{\ell}\;\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}\;(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\end{array}\right),\;\widetilde{b}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}a\,\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\\[4.30554pt]
a\,\Sigma_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\end{array}\right)\,.

The next lemma provides meaning to the above terminology.

Lemma 3.1
.

(3.2)

\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{b}
are Lipschitz continuous functions, homogeneous of degree
1
on
C
.

(3.3)

\widetilde{a}
is positive definite on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
.

Proof.

The claim (
3.2
) is a direct consequence of (
2.8
) and (
2.1
). To prove (
3.3
), we compute the trace and the determinant of
\widetilde{a}
. We have

(3.4)

\mbox{$tr\,\widetilde{a}=4a\,\Sigma_{\ell}\,\Big(\lambda_{\ell}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}\Big)(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}>0$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$, by (\ref{2.1}) and (\ref{1.7})}.

Moreover,

(3.5)

\begin{array}[]{lcl}&\hskip-21.52771pt{\rm det}\,\widetilde{a}=&\hskip-30.1388pt16a^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq\ell,m\leq N}\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{\lambda_{m}}$}-1\Big)(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{m})\,q_{m}\\[12.91663pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\small symmetrizing}}}{{=}}&8a^{2}\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq\ell,m\leq N}\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{\lambda_{m}}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{m}}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}-2\Big)(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{m})\,q_{m}\\[12.91663pt]
&\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mbox{\small(\ref{1.2}),(\ref{2.2})}}}{{\geq}}&\hskip-12.91663pt32a^{2}\,\inf\limits_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{i}}{\mu_{j}}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{j}}{\mu_{i}}$}-2\Big)\,\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i\not=j\leq n}\,(1+\delta_{2i})\,q_{2i}\,(1+\delta_{2j})\,q_{2j}\\[12.91663pt]
&>&\hskip-25.83325pt\mbox{$0$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$ by (\ref{2.1}) and (\ref{1.2})}.\end{array}

Hence, on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
the eigenvalues of
\widetilde{a}
are positive and (
3.3
) follows. This proves Lemma
3.1
.
∎

We can thus introduce the diffusion operator on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
attached to
\widetilde{a}
and
\widetilde{b}
; it acts on smooth functions
\psi
compactly supported in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
via

(3.6)

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}\psi=&\;2a\Big\{\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u}\,\psi+2\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\,\partial^{2}_{u,v}\psi+\Sigma_{\ell}\,\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}\;(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\ \partial^{2}_{v}\psi\Big\}\\[4.30554pt]
&+\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\big)\,\partial_{u}\psi+\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,q_{\ell}\big)\,\partial_{v}\psi\end{split}

(by Lemma
3.1
this is an elliptic second order operator with Lipschitz coefficients in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, which are homogeneous of degree
1
). As an aside, there is no gain of generality in allowing that
\delta_{2i}
differs from
\delta_{2i-1}
in (
1.7
). Indeed, due to the fact that
q_{2i}=q_{2i-1}
and
\lambda_{2i}=\lambda_{2i-1}
, see (
2.2
), (
1.2
),
\widetilde{A}
remains identical to the operator that one obtains with
\delta_{2i}
and
\delta_{2i-1}
replaced by the half of their sum.

We then consider the space
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
of continuous
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
-valued functions on
{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, endowed with its canonical
\sigma
-algebra
{\cal F}
, its canonical filtration
({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
, and its canonical process
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
. Our main object is the well-posedness of the martingale problem for
\widetilde{A}
in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
.

Theorem 3.2
.

Given
w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, there is a unique probability
\widetilde{P}_{w}
on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
) such that

(3.7)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$\widetilde{P}_{w}(W_{0}=w)=1$, and for any smooth compactly supported function $\psi$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$},\\[4.30554pt]
\mbox{$\psi(W_{t})-\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\widetilde{A}\,\psi(W_{s})\,ds,t\geq 0$, is an $({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}$-martingale}.\end{array}

Proof.

We approximate
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
from inside, and for

(3.8)

0<\eta<(\lambda_{N}-1)\,/\,2,

define the open subset of
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}

(3.9)

C_{\eta}=\{w=(u,v)\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}};1+\eta<u/v<\lambda_{N}-\eta\;\mbox{and}\;v>\eta\}

so that with Lemma
3.1

(3.10)

\mbox{$\widetilde{a}$ is uniformly elliptic on $C_{\eta}$}.

We then consider
w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
as in (
3.7
), and assume that
\eta>0
in (
3.8
) is small enough so that
w\in C_{\eta}
. By Theorem 5.2.2, p. 131 of
[
20
]
,
\widetilde{a}^{1/2}(\cdot)
is a Lipschitz function on
\overline{C}\!_{\eta}
. This is also the case for
\widetilde{b}(\cdot)
. This fact ensures that the law we are looking for, is uniquely determined up to the exit time of
C_{\eta}
. Specifically, we can consider arbitrary Lipschitz extensions to
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
of the restriction to
C_{\eta}
of
\widetilde{a}^{1/2}
and
\widetilde{b}
, denote by
A_{\eta}
the corresponding diffusion operator, so that
A_{\eta}\psi
coincides with
\widetilde{A}\psi
on
\overline{C}\!_{\eta}
, when
\psi
is supported in
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. The martingale problem attached to
A_{\eta}
is well-posed, see for instance
[
12
]
, p. 319, and letting
T_{\eta}
stand for the exit time of the canonical process (still denoted by
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0})
and
P^{\eta}_{w}
for the corresponding law with starting point

the law of
(W_{t\wedge T_{\eta}})_{t\geq 0}
under

does not depend on the choice of the extensions. By Corollary 10.1.2, p. 250 of
[
20
]
, Theorem
3.2
will be proved once we show that

(3.11)

\mbox{for any fixed $T>0$, $\lim\limits_{\eta\rightarrow 0}\,P^{\eta}_{w}[T_{\eta}\leq T]=0$}.

To establish (
3.11
), we will construct a smooth non-negative function
\Phi
on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, with sub-level sets
\{\Phi\leq t\}
, which are for every
t\geq 0
, (possibly empty) compact subsets of
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, a non-empty compact subset
\Gamma
of
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, and a positive real number

, so that

(3.12)

\widetilde{A}\Phi\leq-1
on

, and

(3.13)

\widetilde{A}\Phi\leq C_{\Phi}
on

(where

in (
3.6
) has been tacitly extended to smooth functions on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
).

This is a so-called Lyapunov-Foster condition. By Theorem 2.1 on p. 524 of
[
18
]
it is (much) more than what is needed to ensure that (

) holds and complete the proof of Theorem

. But this Lyapunov-Foster condition will be helpful in the next section to establish that the diffusion on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
constructed in this section has a unique stationary distribution.

\Phi=\Phi_{F}+\Phi_{G}+\Phi_{V},

(3.16)

\displaystyle 2(2\alpha_{0}+1)\,\max\limits_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})<\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell}),

(3.17)

\displaystyle 2(2\beta_{0}+1)\,\max\limits_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})<\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell}),

)
, and
(

)
),

(3.18)

\gamma_{0}=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{2\alpha}$}\,.

Then, one has

, so that

(3.19)

\mbox{$\widetilde{A}\Phi_{F}\leq c_{F},\widetilde{A}\Phi_{G}\leq c_{G}$, and $\widetilde{A}\Phi_{V}\leq c_{V}$ on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$},

and one can choose

positive such that setting

(3.20)

\displaystyle\Gamma=\{w=(u,v)\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}};u-v\geq f_{0},\lambda_{N}v-u\geq g_{0},v\leq v_{0}\},\;\mbox{and}\;\,c_{\Phi}=c_{F}+c_{G}+c_{V},

(3.21)

\Phi,\Gamma,c_{\Phi}
satisfy the Lyapunov-Foster condition corresponding to
(
3.12
), (
3.13
)

\displaystyle\mbox{and the assumptions above {\rm(\ref{3.12}), (\ref{3.13})}}.

Proof.

f(w)=u-v\,(>0)
for

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}f^{-\alpha}=&-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{f^{\alpha+1}}$}\,(a\Sigma_{\ell}\,(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)\,q_{\ell})\\[4.30554pt]

Noting that

and

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}\,f^{-\alpha}=&-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{f^{\alpha+1}}$}\,\Big\{a\Sigma_{\ell}\,(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})\\

\widetilde{A}f^{-\alpha_{0}}\leq-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\alpha_{0}}{f^{\alpha_{0}+1}}$}\;\Big\{\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{a\alpha_{0}}{2\alpha_{0}+1}$}\;\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\lambda_{N}f\Big\}.

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}g^{-\beta}=&-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{g^{\beta+1}}$}\;\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\big)\\[4.30554pt]
&+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\beta(\beta+1)}{g^{\beta+2}}$}\;2a\,\lambda_{N}\,\Sigma_{\ell}\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{\ell}}{\lambda_{N}}$}-2\Big)(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}.\end{split}

With a similar observation as above (

) we obtain

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}g^{-\beta}=&-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{g^{\beta+1}}$}\;\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})\\
&-2a(\beta+1)\,\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}-1\Big)\,q_{\ell}\,/\,\Sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\Big(\mbox{ \footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell^{\prime}}}$}-1\Big)\,q_{\ell^{\prime}}\\
&-2\Sigma_{\ell}\,(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}\Big\}.\end{split}

If we now choose
\beta=\beta_{0}
, with
\beta_{0}
as in (
3.17
), we find that

\begin{array}[]{l}2(\beta_{0}+1)\,\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}-1\Big)\,q_{\ell}\,/\,\Sigma_{\ell^{\prime}}\,\Big(\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell^{\prime}}}$}-1\Big)\,q_{\ell^{\prime}}\leq 2(\beta_{0}+1)\,\max\{(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})\}\\
\leq\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{0}+1}{2\beta_{0}+1}$}\;\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell}).\end{array}

Thus, coming back to (

\Sigma_{\ell}\,(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}=\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}\,(\frac{\lambda_{N}}{\lambda_{\ell}}-1)\,q_{\ell}\leq\lambda_{N}g
, we obtain

(3.27)

\widetilde{A}g^{-\beta_{0}}\leq-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\beta_{0}}{g^{\beta+1}}$}\;\Big\{\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{a\beta_{0}}{2\beta_{0}+1}$}\;\Sigma_{\ell}(\lambda_{N}-\lambda_{\ell})(1+\delta_{\ell})-2\lambda_{N}g\Big\}.

We also note that the right member of (
3.27
) is uniformly bounded from above, so that the second inequality of (
3.19
) holds (see (
3.15
) for notation).

Finally, we have for

and

with

,

(3.28)

\begin{split}\widetilde{A}h_{\gamma}(w)=&\Big\{2a\,\gamma^{2}\,\Sigma_{\ell}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}(1+\delta_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}+\gamma\,\big(a\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})-2u\big)\Big\}\,e^{\gamma v}\\
&\hskip-21.09712pt\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{1.7}),(\ref{2.3})}}{{\leq}}(2a\,\gamma v-2u+aN)\,\gamma e^{\gamma v}\leq(-u+aN)\,\gamma e^{\gamma v}(\leq aN\,\gamma e^{\gamma aN}).\end{split}

Thus, we choose

(3.29)

\displaystyle\gamma_{0}=(2a)^{-1}\;\mbox{and}\;c_{V}=\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{N}{2}$}\;e^{\frac{N}{2}},\mbox{so that}

(3.30)

\displaystyle\widetilde{A}h_{\gamma_{0}}\leq(-u+aN)\,/\,2a\;\,e^{v/2a}\leq c_{V},

and in particular the last inequality of (
3.19
) holds (see (
3.15
)).

Then, combining (
3.19
) with (
3.24
), (
3.27
), (
3.30
), we see that we can choose

,

(small enough) and

(large enough) so that on

;

,

, this completes the proof of (

.
∎

4
The stationary distribution

As seen in Theorem
3.2
of the previous section, the martingale problem attached to
\widetilde{A}
on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
is well-posed for all initial conditions
w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
. The corresponding family of solutions
\widetilde{P}_{w}
,
w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, thus provides a strong Markov process on the state space
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, see for instance Chapter 10 §1 of
[
20
]
. In this section we will show that this Markov process has a unique stationary distribution and we will collect some of its properties, see Theorem
4.1
and Propositions
4.2
to
4.4
.

The first main result of this section is

Theorem 4.1
.

(4.1)

\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{There is a unique stationary probability $\widetilde{\pi}$ for the diffusion on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$ given by}\\
\mbox{the collection $\widetilde{P}_{w}$, $w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$ in Theorem \ref{theo3.2}}.\end{array}

Moreover, with
\alpha_{0},\beta_{0},\gamma_{0}
as in (
3.16
), (
3.17
), (
3.18
), one has

(4.2)

\displaystyle\int_{\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}}\,(u-v)^{-\alpha_{0}}+(\lambda_{N}v-u)^{-\beta_{0}}+e^{\gamma_{0}v}\,d\,\widetilde{\pi}(w)<\infty\;\;\mbox{(with $w=(u,v)$)}.

Proof.

Specifically, we will show that the diffusion on
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
constructed in the last section is “positive Harris recurrent”, see Section 4 of
[
18
]
, see also
[
16
]
, p. 91-98, for further background.

With the Lyapunov-Foster condition (
3.21
) in Proposition
3.3
, an important part of the task has been completed. It will then follow from the application of Theorem 4.2 on p. 529 of
[
18
]
that (
4.1
) holds, and with the help of Corollary
3.4
that (
4.2
) holds, once we show that

(4.3)

for some
\eta>0
,
\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1}\in dx]\geq\eta\,1_{\Gamma}\,dx
, for all
w\in\Gamma

(we recall that
(W_{t})_{t\geq 0}
stands for the canonical process on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},{\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}})
, and
\Gamma
has been defined in (
3.20
)).

To this end we consider some smooth relatively compact domain
O
such that
\Gamma\subseteq O\subseteq\overline{O}\subseteq\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, and denote by
T_{O}=\inf\{s\geq 0;W_{s}\not=O\}
the exit time from
O
. As we now explain, for a suitable (small)
\gamma>0
, one has

(4.4)

\mbox{$\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1\wedge T_{O}}\in dx]\geq\gamma\,1_{\Gamma}\,dx$, for all $w\in\Gamma$},

which readily implies (
4.3
) (since the probability in the left member is the sum of
\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1}\in dx,T_{O}>1]
and the measure
\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{T_{O}}\in dx,T_{O}\leq 1]
which is carried by the frontier of
O
and hence orthogonal to
1_{\Gamma}\,dx
). To prove (
4.4
), we use an extension outside
\overline{O}
of the diffusion matrix
\widetilde{a}
and the drift
\widetilde{b}
, see (
3.1
), to uniformly Lipschitz functions, with uniform ellipticity for the extension of
\widetilde{a}
. We use Duhamel’s formula to derive a small time lower bound on the Dirichlet heat kernel in
O
for nearby points in a compact neighborhood of
\Gamma
contained in
O
, with the help of the full space heat kernel estimates (see Theorem 1 on p. 67 and (4.75) on p. 82 of
[
11
]
, and then a chaining argument to deduce (
4.4
)). This completes the proof of (
4.3
) and hence of Theorem
4.1
.
∎

We then introduce the stationary law of the effective diffusion:

(4.5)

\widetilde{P}=\displaystyle\int\widetilde{\pi}(dw)\,\widetilde{P}_{w}\quad\mbox{(a probability on $C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})$)}.

Then, one has the following convenient martingale characterization of
\widetilde{P}
via:

Proposition 4.2
.

\widetilde{P}
is the only probability
Q
on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
such that
Q
is stationary and for any smooth compactly supported function
\psi
on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
, under
Q

\psi(W_{t})-\displaystyle\int^{t}_{0}\,\widetilde{A}\psi(W_{s})\,ds,t\geq 0
, is an
({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
-martingale

(recall
({\cal F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is the canonical filtration on
C({\mathbb{R}}_{+},\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}})
, see above Theorem
3.2
).

Proof.

That
\widetilde{P}
satisfies these conditions is immediate. Conversely, given a stationary law
Q
as above, conditioning on
W_{0}=w\in\,\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
, the resulting law is a solution to the martingale problem (
3.7
), and hence coincides with
\widetilde{P}_{w}
, for a.e.

relative to the law of
W_{0}
under

. The stationarity of the law of

and (

) implies that the law of
W_{0}
under

is
\widetilde{\pi}
and hence

. This proves Proposition

.
∎

The above martingale characterization of
\widetilde{P}
will be handy in the companion article
[
21
]
to study the inviscid limit of a certain enstrophy-energy process.

Below are some further facts concerning
\widetilde{\pi}
.

Proposition 4.3
.

(4.6)

\mbox{$\widetilde{\pi}$ is absolutely continuous with respect to the Lebesgue measure on $\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}$}.

Proof.

One can let the open set
O
above (
4.4
) grow along a sequence

,

, of relatively open compact subsets of
\stackrel{{{}_{\circ}}}{{C}}
(with

, one finds that for every Lebesgue negligible subset

of

\begin{split}0&=\displaystyle\int_{\overline{O}_{n}}\!\widetilde{\pi}(dw)\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1\wedge T_{O_{m}}}\!\in\!N]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\uparrow}\displaystyle\int_{\overline{O}_{n}}\!\widetilde{\pi}(dw)\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1}\!\in\!N]\underset{n\rightarrow\infty}{\uparrow}\displaystyle\int\!\widetilde{\pi}(dw)\widetilde{P}_{w}[W_{1}\!\in\!N]\\[4.30554pt]

.

Denoting by

)

5
Condensation bound

The main object of this section is Theorem
5.1
. It states, in the context of the stationary diffusion of the previous section, a quantitative lower bound on the ratio of the expected energy with the expected enstrophy. This inequality takes full significance when the effective diffusion investigated in the present article appears as an inviscid limit in the companion article
[
21
]
. Theorem
2.3
, through its upper bound on the functions
\widehat{q}_{i},i\geq 2
, is an important ingredient in the proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We recall that
B_{0}=a\Sigma_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})
and
B_{1}=a\Sigma_{\ell}\,\lambda_{\ell}(1+\delta_{\ell})
, see (
4.8
). We have

Theorem 5.1
.

(Condensation bound)

For any
\ell_{0}
in
\{3,\dots,N\}
(recall
N=2n
), writing
\ell_{0}=2i_{0}
or
\ell_{0}=2i_{0}-1
, with
2\leq i_{0}\leq n
, depending on whether
\ell_{0}
is even or odd, one has

(5.1)

2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\leq\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{B_{1}-B_{0}}{\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-1}$}\;\sum^{i_{0}-2}_{k=1}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-k}$}\;B_{0}\leq\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{B_{1}-B_{0}}{\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1}$}+\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{3}}{\lambda_{3}-1}$}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{0}}{N-\ell_{0}}$}\;B_{0}

(where
\widetilde{E}
denotes the
\widetilde{P}
-expectation, see
(
4.5
)
, and the sum in the middle member of
(
5.1
)
is omitted when
i_{0}=2
).

Note that this inequality shows that the left member of (
5.1
) is small compared to
B_{0}=2\widetilde{E}[U_{0}]
, see (
4.9
), and there is some “condensation”, when
\ell_{0}
can be chosen so that
\frac{B_{1}}{B_{0}}\ll\lambda_{\ell_{0}}
and
\ell_{0}\ll N
. The ratio
B_{1}/B_{0}
can be viewed as an “effective spectral value” at which Brownian forcing occurs. We refer to Remark 6.2 of
[
21
]
for a more detailed discussion.

Proof.

We have by Proposition
4.4
and (
2.3
)

(5.2)

\begin{split}B_{1}-B_{0}=2\widetilde{E}\,\big[\Sigma_{\ell>2}(\lambda_{\ell}-1)\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})]&=2\widetilde{E}\,\big[\Sigma_{\ell>2}(\lambda_{\ell\vee\ell_{0}}-1)\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})\big]\\[4.30554pt]
&-2\widetilde{E}\,\Big[\sum_{2<\ell<\ell_{0}}(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-\lambda_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})\Big]\,.\end{split}

It then follows that

(5.3)

\begin{array}[]{l}2(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1)\,\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{2.3})}}{{=}}2(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1)\,\widetilde{E}\,\Big[\Sigma_{\ell>2}\;\Big(1-\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{\lambda_{\ell}}$}\Big)\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})\Big]\leq\\[4.30554pt]
2\widetilde{E}\big[\Sigma_{\ell>2}(\lambda_{\ell\vee\ell_{0}}-1)\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})\big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{5.2})}}{{=}}B_{1}-B_{0}+2\widetilde{E}\,\Big[\sum\limits_{2<\ell<\ell_{0}}(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-\lambda_{\ell})\,q_{\ell}(W_{0})\Big]\,.\end{array}

The last expectation of (
5.3
) will now be bounded with the help of the important inequality (
2.12
). It is equal to

(5.4)

\begin{array}[]{l}\widetilde{E}\,\Big[\sum\limits_{1<i<i_{0}}(\mu_{i_{0}}-\mu_{i})\,\widehat{q}_{i}(W_{0})\Big]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{2.12})}}{{\leq}}\sum\limits_{1<i<i_{0}}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\mu_{i_{0}}-\mu_{i}}{n-i+1}$}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]}{1-1/\mu_{i}}$}\leq\\[8.61108pt]
(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1)(1-\mu_{2}^{-1})^{-1}\,\Sigma^{i_{0}-2}_{k=1}\;\mbox{\footnotesize$\displaystyle\frac{1}{n-k}$}\;\widetilde{E}[U_{0}-V_{0}]\\[6.45831pt]
\mbox{(the sum is understood as $0$ when $i_{0}=2$)}.\end{array}

Inserting this upperbound for the last expectation in (
5.3

4.9

(\lambda_{\ell_{0}}-1)
yields the first inequality of (
5.1
). The second inequality is immediate since
2(i_{0}-2)\leq\ell_{0}
. This proves Theorem
5.1
.
∎

(B.15)

v^{(n-1)}\displaystyle\int^{\infty}_{0}r\,V^{i_{0}}(u/v-r,1-r/\mu_{i_{0}})\,dr.

When

, the values

, where

,

is a fixed homogeneous polynomial of degree

of

,
1-r/\mu_{i_{0}}
by Proposition

. In (

), performing the integration over the various intervals where

reaches the successive values

, then yields a non-zero polynomial function of

with degree at most

. When

, a similar argument can be applied. And when

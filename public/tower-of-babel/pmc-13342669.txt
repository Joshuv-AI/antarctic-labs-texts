Exploring multi-objective optimization scheduling strategies for power dispatch robots based on an improved particle swarm optimization algorithm framework
Authors: Han Yan, Min Zhang, Yuqi Zhu, Xi Mo, Zaihe Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
In the application of scheduling strategies for power dispatching robots, traditional particle swarm optimization algorithms often become trapped in local optima during the optimization process, preventing the attainment of globally optimal results. Therefore, this paper proposes multi-objective optimization scheduling strategies for power dispatch robots based on an improved particle swarm optimization algorithm framework. When constructing the multi-objective optimization model for power dispatch robots, the particle swarm optimization algorithm is applied, and an adaptive inertia weight adjustment mechanism is used to improve search efficiency, ultimately achieving the optimal solution for multi-objective scheduling. The experimental results show that using the improved particle swarm optimization algorithm to optimize the multi-objective scheduling task of the power dispatching robot significantly reduces the total operating cost of the power system by approximately 19,326.8 US dollars, while keeping monthly pollutant emissions below 100 tons and effectively reducing system network loss. This method not only improves power scheduling efficiency but also promotes the development of green energy, providing strong support for the sustainable development of the power system.

Introduction

With the increasing complexity and dynamism of power networks, traditional power dispatch methods usually focus only on a single optimization objective, such as cost minimization or reliability maximization1. However, during the regular operation phase of the power grid, power dispatch requires multi-objective balancing2, considering factors such as power generation cost, carbon emissions, and system stability. The multi-objective optimization scheduling strategy has attracted significant attention in the field of power systems and represents a hot topic in current scientific research3. Power dispatching robots utilize modern technologies such as artificial intelligence and big data analysis for power dispatch4. They have replaced the traditional manually dominated scheduling mode and established an intelligent scheduling mode5. This type of robot not only significantly improves scheduling efficiency and capabilities but also eliminates the potential impact of human factors, bringing significant practical value to the field of power scheduling6. The use of power dispatching robots for power system management can reduce the workload of staff, liberate human resources, and increase the accuracy of power dispatching7. Particle swarm optimization (PSO) has shown broad application prospects in many optimization fields owing to its simple implementation and strong robustness8. When handling multi-objective optimization tasks, the standard PSO algorithm may fall into local optima, making it difficult to obtain a comprehensive Pareto optimal solution set9. Therefore, improving the PSO algorithm is particularly important for power scheduling problems, as it can significantly enhance the exploration and exploitation capabilities of the algorithm through carefully designed particle velocity and position update strategies10. When combined with the capabilities of power dispatching robots, automatic power dispatching can be achieved. To effectively address multi-objective optimization problems, power dispatch systems can rely on advanced algorithms to obtain more comprehensive and scientific decision support11. As the global energy mix accelerates its transition toward renewable energy, the share of new energy sources in the power system continues to grow, presenting increasingly complex challenges for power dispatch. At the same time, increasingly stringent environmental regulations require the power industry to significantly reduce pollution emissions. Therefore, it is of great practical significance to study efficient multi-objective optimization strategies for power scheduling.

With the development of the power industry, the proportion of new energy in the power system is constantly increasing, which makes power dispatching face more complex challenges, as traditional dispatching methods are difficult to meet the demand. At the same time, the acceleration of intelligent construction of the power grid has put forward higher requirements for the intelligence and efficiency of power dispatching. Many researchers have studied power dispatching problems. Alizadeh et al. combined a deep learning algorithm and cooperative game method12 to achieve multi-stage optimal dispatching for microgrids. Deep learning algorithms provided precise multi-stage optimal scheduling solutions for microgrids by efficiently processing large amounts of data. Using cooperative game theory, the method comprehensively considered the interdependencies between microgrids and achieved global optimization through cooperative strategies. However, the training of deep learning artificial neural networks required a large amount of data and time, and the network structure needed to be continuously adjusted and optimized, which affected the real-time scheduling of microgrids and thus the scheduling results. Ramachandran et al. combined fuzzy adaptive technology with a hybrid crow search algorithm and applied it to the economic dispatch of cogeneration power grids13. This method combined various search strategies, including sorting, fuzzy logic, and hybrid crow search, and achieved satisfactory solutions in a relatively short time. However, the structure and implementation of the algorithm were relatively complex, requiring a large amount of computational resources, and had limited practical applicability. Roslan et al. optimally dispatched various energy sources in the microgrid through an optimization algorithm14, reducing microgrid emissions. This approach was particularly effective in microgrids utilizing clean energy, thereby further reducing greenhouse gas emissions. However, the optimization effect of this method was highly influenced by the actual situation of the microgrid, such as energy type, equipment performance, and network structure, and its scheduling was also easily affected by the fluctuation of the microgrid’s operating environment, leading to a high degree of uncertainty. Ghiasi et al. proposed an enhanced control strategy for renewable energy sources connected to the grid through voltage source converters in microgrids15. The scheme consisted of a voltage control loop with minimum inverter switching, a power sharing controller with minimum inverter switching, a negative sequence current controller, and a loop for recognizing the control system operation mode. All controllers were designed using a multipurpose finite control set model predictive control (FCS-MPC) strategy. Since these controllers used dynamic currents and VSC voltages, they could be applied to and transferred between grid-connected and islanded modes of operation. The method used voltage-frequency control instead of power control by VSC. One inverter controlled the voltage and the other controlled the current. The proposed control strategy was efficient, reliable, and compatible. However, the control strategy used a multipurpose finite control set model predictive control strategy design, which increased the technical complexity of the system. Cheng et al. argued that by integrating advanced computational techniques such as reinforcement learning and multi-agent systems, game-theoretic methods could be used to develop adaptive and intelligent mechanisms for resilient energy management16. The paper highlighted practical case studies in demand response, distributed energy management, and electric vehicle charging, demonstrating the transformative potential of game theory in optimizing behavior and enhancing the stability of sustainable urban grid systems. It also identified key gaps in existing research, particularly the need to incorporate socioeconomic factors and personalized behavioral models into game theory frameworks to better address the evolving needs of smart cities. By proposing forward-looking strategies that combined behavioral modeling with intelligent algorithms, the paper advanced the discourse on sustainable urban energy. It provided researchers, policymakers, and practitioners with a solid theoretical foundation and actionable insights, offering pathways for designing adaptive, resilient, and sustainable smart grids. By aligning with the key challenges of urban energy systems and exploring the frontiers of game theory innovation, this study laid out a blueprint for future research on sustainable cities and smart city energy management. Cheng et al. conducted an in-depth analysis of the applications of game theory and evolutionary game theory in modern power systems and electricity markets17. By exploring power generation planning, bidding strategies, demand response, and energy management, this study highlighted the broad applicability of game-theoretic models in optimization decision-making processes. It demonstrated the unique advantages of evolutionary game theory, particularly evolutionary stable strategies and replicator dynamics, for managing complex dynamics and uncertainties in distributed energy management and microgrids. Cheng et al. developed an integrated framework that combined prospect theory-driven decision modeling with evolutionary smart contracts and multi-stage negotiation protocols to enhance trading efficiency in cross-regional energy markets18. A mathematical foundation was established to incorporate loss aversion, probability distortion, and reference-dependent preferences into VPP decision-making, while adaptive contracts capable of autonomously evolving in response to market changes were developed. Through composite game-theoretic analysis, the nested interactions between contract evolution and negotiation dynamics were examined.

By fine-tuning and optimizing the configurations and strategies of the particle swarm optimization method, the proposed approach can avoid local optima, strengthen global search, and adaptively adjust the inertia factor, thus improving the efficiency of multi-objective optimal scheduling, showing strong robustness and good adaptability, and effectively solving complex power scheduling challenges. This method brings novel and efficient multi-objective optimization solutions to the field of power dispatch. It effectively improves the efficiency and stability of the power system, achieves a dual reduction in cost and carbon emissions, and promotes the development of green energy. A multi-objective optimal scheduling method for power dispatching robots

Overall architecture of the power dispatch robot

The core task of power dispatching robots is to monitor and perceive the operating status of the power grid in real time, while assisting power control personnel in efficiently executing scheduling tasks13. With the support of power big data, power dispatch robots have effectively applied artificial intelligence technology to power dispatch through data learning and other means. The overall architecture is shown in Fig. 1.

Fig. 1Overall architecture of the power dispatching robot.

Overall architecture of the power dispatching robot.

The power dispatch robot collects and analyzes power system data through learning and training. The data source covers multiple key links such as power generation, substations, and transmission and distribution14. These data have a strong correlation in the time and space dimensions, and the interaction between the data is stronger than that of other types of data15, which is crucial for the realization of the scheduling function of the power dispatch robot. Through data analysis and decision-making, the scheduling function and auxiliary decision-making of the power system can be realized. In accordance with the functional requirements of the power grid, a power dispatch robot system has been developed to ensure the timeliness and accuracy of tasks such as grid-wide data collection, system communication, anomaly alerts, intelligent monitoring, and dispatch operation management. This creates an integrated system comprising power grid dispatch robots and human staff, thereby enabling more effective execution of power grid dispatch operations19. The power dispatch robot uses a particle swarm optimization algorithm to accurately optimize the multi-objective scheduling model, aiming to ensure the stable operation of the power system.

Core architecture of the power system

To achieve multi-objective scheduling of the power system, the coupling relationship between the various energy sources within the system is clarified, as shown in Fig. 2.

Fig. 2Architecture or structural layout of the power system.

Architecture or structural layout of the power system.

The energy supply equipment in a power system includes photovoltaic (PV) systems, wind turbines (WTGs), micro gas turbines (MGTs), gas boilers (GB), heat pumps (HP), heat recovery steam boilers (HRB), electric refrigerators (ER), and absorption refrigeration machines (AR). Energy storage equipment includes energy storage systems (ESS), thermal energy storage (HSS), and cooling storage systems. The higher-level network covers the power grid and gas grid20, which mainly supply electrical, heating, and cooling loads.

A multi-energy microgrid overcomes the traditional separation between different energy sources by integrating PV, WTG, GB, and other energy supply equipment into a multi-energy integrated system to meet various load demands21. Photovoltaic equipment is the main power supply unit in the power system structure shown in Fig. 2. Wind turbines (WTGs) and micro gas turbines (MGTs) also play key roles. MGTs supply power to the load by consuming gas energy, achieving coupling between electricity and gas22. The thermal energy supply equipment mainly includes gas boilers (GB), heat pumps (HP), and heat recovery steam boilers (HRB). GB and HP enable the coupling of gas heating and electric heating within the system.

The HRB optimizes energy use efficiency by recovering the thermal energy generated by MGTs. The cold energy supply equipment mainly includes ER and AR, where ER consumes electricity to generate cold to realize electric-cold coupling, while AR consumes heat to generate cold to realize hot-cold coupling23. The energy storage system can effectively regulate the energy supply, stabilize the fluctuation of renewable energy sources, and ensure the smooth operation of the system.

Application of the optimized PSO algorithm to the multi-objective deployment mode

The core innovation of the adaptive inertia weight improved PSO algorithm based on optimal similarity proposed in this study lies in two aspects: first, the dynamic adaptive adjustment of inertia weights, which breaks through the limitations of traditional fuzzy adaptive PSO that relies on empirical rules to adjust weights. By calculating the cosine similarity between particles and optimal particles, the algorithm achieves quantitative dynamic adjustment of weights, maintaining high weights to enhance global search in the early stage of iteration, and reducing weights to enhance local convergence in the later stage of iteration, while avoiding falling into local optima. Second is the particle update mechanism that integrates crossover strategies. When the particle similarity exceeds 0.9, a two-point crossover strategy is introduced to locally optimize the particles, solving the problem of search stagnation that is prone to occur in existing MOPSO algorithms during particle aggregation. The power dispatching robot utilizes an improved PSO algorithm to optimize the multi-objective scheduling architecture for the power system. The power grid dispatching robot utilizes an advanced particle swarm optimization algorithm to improve the multi-objective scheduling efficiency of the power system. In particle swarm optimization, particles are represented by position and velocity vectors, with position vectors representing potential solutions and velocity vectors indicating their adjustment direction and magnitude. In multi-objective optimization scheduling for power systems, it is assumed that there are M feasible solutions (particles). To improve the efficiency of multi-objective optimization scheduling, it is necessary to finely adjust the configuration and strategy of the optimized particle swarm algorithm. To improve multi-objective optimization scheduling efficiency, the optimized particle swarm algorithm configuration and strategy are finely tuned. An adaptive inertia weight adjustment strategy based on optimal similarity is adopted to solve the problem of decreasing inertia weights and lack of guidance in traditional PSO29. The inertia coefficients are dynamically adjusted based on particle similarity during the iteration process. The particle position parameters are normalized using Eq. When the similarity is higher than 0.8, the inertia weights are reduced to make the particles closer to the global optimal solution; if the similarity is higher than 0.9, the two-point crossover method is used for the crossover operation to strengthen local optimization and avoid falling into the trap of local optima30. When the similarity is lower than 0.7, the inertia weight is increased to enhance the overall search capability31. By fitting the particle change curve and improving the PSO algorithm, the multidirectional optimal scheduling of the power network is obtained, and the power dispatch robot executes scheduling operations based on this, thereby enhancing the robustness and reliability of the power system operation.

The time complexity of the proposed improved PSO algorithm mainly consists of four parts: particle initialization, particle velocity/position update, similarity calculation, and constraint check. Let the particle swarm size be N, the dimension be D, and the number of iterations be T. The time complexity of particle initialization is O(N×D), the time complexity of particle update is O(N×D×T), the time complexity of cosine similarity calculation is O(N×D×T), and the time complexity of constraint check is O(N×T). Overall, the overall time complexity of the algorithm is O(N×D×T), which is consistent with that of the traditional PSO algorithm, indicating that no additional complexity is caused by the improved strategy. Meanwhile, as the number of convergence iterations of the improved algorithm is reduced by more than 40%, the actual calculation time is significantly lower than that of the traditional PSO algorithm, making it more suitable for the application of large-scale power dispatching systems.

The multi-objective optimization model of the proposed improved PSO algorithm for power dispatching robots can be applied in large-scale power grids through grid partition scheduling. The entire power grid is divided into multiple subregions, each of which is managed by a dispatching robot. The scheduling coordination between subregions is achieved through the population collaborative search mechanism of the improved PSO algorithm, taking into account the cost, emissions, and operational risks of the entire network. In microgrids, the model can be directly embedded into the microgrid energy management system and combined with the multi-energy coupling characteristics of microgrids, to optimize the output scheduling of distributed energy sources such as photovoltaics, wind power, and gas turbines, while matching the cooling, heating, and electricity load requirements of microgrids, thereby achieving economical and environmentally friendly operation of microgrids.

Discussion

This study assumes that the cost function is linear and that pollutant emissions are linearly correlated with unit output. This assumption is highly realistic under conventional grid operating conditions and aligns with China’s current cost accounting standards for thermal power units and pollutant emission monitoring regulations. However, in grids with high renewable energy penetration, the fluctuating output of wind and solar power leads to frequent adjustments in the output of thermal power units, causing the cost function to exhibit nonlinear characteristics. At the same time, the intermittency of renewable energy affects the grid’s overall emission levels, resulting in a deviation from the linear relationship between emissions and output. Therefore, while this model can be directly applied under conventional operating conditions, it requires appropriate extension under conditions of high renewable energy penetration. To address dynamic uncertainties under high renewable energy penetration, the model in this study can be extended in three ways: First, the linear cost function is modified into a piecewise nonlinear function. By incorporating the variable-load operating characteristics of thermal power units, cost coefficients are assigned to different output intervals to better align with actual unit cost accounting. Second, a robustness term for renewable energy output prediction errors is introduced. Penalty terms for wind and solar power output prediction errors are incorporated into the objective function to enhance the model’s adaptability to the volatility of renewable energy. Third, a multi-scenario stochastic optimization model is constructed. Based on historical data of renewable energy output, multiple output scenarios are generated, and the optimal dispatch strategy is determined through scenario probability weighting, enabling the model to make optimal decisions under uncertain conditions.

There is an inherent conflict of interest between the economic objective of power dispatch (minimizing operating costs) and the environmental objective (reducing pollutant emissions). Prioritizing the reduction of economic costs tends to rely on the output of low-cost, high-emission thermal power units, whereas prioritizing emission control requires increasing the output of renewable and clean energy units and boosting environmental protection investments, thereby driving up operating costs. The improved PSO algorithm proposed in this study achieves an optimal balance between the two objectives by solving for the Pareto optimal solution set. It reduces the monthly total operating cost of the power system by $19,326.80 while keeping monthly pollutant emissions below 100 tons. Under normal operating conditions with sufficient renewable energy output and during grid load troughs, the algorithm can significantly increase the renewable energy integration rate, achieving deep emission reductions while maintaining low costs, thereby achieving optimal dual-objective balance; however, in extreme scenarios such as prolonged rain or windless conditions where renewable energy output is severely insufficient, the algorithm must rely on thermal power units to maintain grid supply. In such cases, pollutant emissions approach the 100-ton control threshold, and the optimization effect for the environmental objective is somewhat weakened. Nevertheless, through optimized unit scheduling, operating costs can still be reduced by more than 15% compared to traditional methods, maintaining the optimization advantage for the economic objective. By setting the economic cost weight coefficient α (0–1) and the pollutant emission weight coefficient β = 1-α, the results show that when α > 0.7, the algorithm prioritizes the economic objective, with costs continuously decreasing as α increases, while emissions trend upward; when β > 0.7, the algorithm prioritizes environmental objectives, with emissions decreasing significantly as β increases and costs rising; when 0.3 ≤ α ≤ 0.7, the algorithm achieves an optimal balance between the two objectives, and this weight range provides a quantitative reference for scheduling decisions under varying grid environmental requirements and economic budgets.

The improved PSO algorithm proposed in this study demonstrates good real-time feasibility, and its integration with existing power grid systems enables seamless end-to-end connectivity. At the data acquisition level, it can directly interface with existing grid SCADA systems and PMUs (phasor measurement units) via standardized data interfaces to collect real-time operational data such as unit output, grid load, and node voltage. For legacy data acquisition devices lacking standardized interfaces, retrofitting with universal data acquisition terminals enables unified data collection, transmission, and formatting. In terms of decision-making timeliness, the improved PSO algorithm is embedded into the existing power grid’s energy management system (EMS) and dispatch automation system. The dispatch strategies obtained from the algorithm’s solution can be directly transmitted via the EMS to individual generating units and load-side execution units. Additionally, a dispatch strategy pre-calculation model is established to solve basic dispatch plans in advance based on short-term load forecast data. When grid conditions change dynamically, only local parameter adjustments are required, keeping the time for a single dispatch decision within 3 s and meeting the real-time requirements for second-level decision-making in power dispatch. In terms of hardware requirements, the algorithm places low demands on computing power and can be directly deployed on the industrial control computers of existing power dispatch robots and standard servers in the grid dispatch center, without the need for new high-end hardware. To address the partitioned scheduling needs of large-scale power grids, the master scheduling node is responsible for coordinating the entire grid, while the slave scheduling nodes handle local scheduling for each sub-region. The master and slave nodes communicate via industrial Ethernet to achieve low-latency communication, balancing the global nature and real-time requirements of scheduling. During the actual deployment of the algorithm, three core challenges must be addressed. First is the system compatibility challenge: by establishing a unified power dispatch communication protocol and data exchange format, the improved algorithm can be adapted and debugged to work with existing power grid dispatch systems and equipment, thereby eliminating software and hardware compatibility barriers. Second is the data security challenge: end-to-end encryption is applied to grid operation data during collection, transmission, and storage, and dedicated firewalls and intrusion detection modules are deployed within the dispatch system to prevent external cyberattacks and data breaches. Third is the fault redundancy challenge: when a dispatch robot or algorithm malfunctions, the system can automatically and seamlessly switch to the existing manual dispatch mode to ensure the continuity and stability of power grid dispatch operations. At the same time, a real-time fault monitoring mechanism for algorithms and equipment is established to enable rapid fault localization and early warning.

Conclusion

The multi-objective scheduling task of the power dispatching robot is optimized with an improved particle swarm optimization algorithm. The improved PSO algorithm effectively addresses the complex challenges of multi-objective optimization in power systems. The automation function of the power dispatch robot has enabled efficient power system dispatch. This study aims to significantly enhance the energy efficiency and reliability of power systems through optimized design. The introduction of renewable energy and energy storage technologies has significantly reduced the carbon emissions of the power systems. Experimental results have shown that this method performs outstandingly in multi-objective optimization, providing a comprehensive optimization approach for power dispatch robots. In addition, this method has strong robustness and good adaptability, and can effectively cope with complex power scheduling challenges. This method helps power dispatch robots achieve efficient and scientific power dispatch, improve system operation efficiency and stability, and reduce operating costs and carbon emissions. The development of green energy technologies supports the long-term sustainability of power systems.

The implementation of power dispatch robots in actual power grid systems still faces the following specific challenges:Cost: The high-precision sensing devices and edge computing hardware required for algorithm deployment involve significant initial investment costs. These costs can be reduced through phased deployment and retrofitting to ensure compatibility with existing power grid monitoring equipment.Reliability: The robots’ algorithmic decision-making and hardware operations are susceptible to electromagnetic interference from the power grid and network latency. Reliability can be enhanced by establishing redundant communication links and adopting edge computing to enable local real-time decision-making.Staff training: Power grid operations and maintenance personnel must acquire skills in debugging the robots’ algorithms and troubleshooting faults. Operational capabilities can be improved through specialized training programs developed in collaboration with universities, the creation of visual operation manuals, and the establishment of simulation training platforms.

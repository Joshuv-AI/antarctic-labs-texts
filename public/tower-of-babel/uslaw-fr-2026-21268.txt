SUMMARY: The U.S. Environmental Protection Agency (EPA) is finalizing 
an approval of a revision to the Arizona State Implementation Plan 
(SIP) submitted by the Arizona Department of Environmental Quality 
(ADEQ). This revision includes statutes and regulations amending the 
Cleaner Burning Gasoline (CBG) program, which is a control measure in 
the greater Phoenix metropolitan area to reduce emissions of ozone-
forming pollutants, carbon monoxide (CO), and particulate matter. 
Additionally, this revision addresses the Winter Oxygenated Fuel 
program to control CO emissions in the Tucson area. Finally, this 
revision repeals the Arizona Gasoline Set[hyphen]aside (GSA) Program, 
applicable to the 1971 carbon monoxide nonattainment area covering 
Maricopa County and a portion of Pima County. This SIP revision is 
administrative in nature. This final action updates the existing SIP-

[[Page 66004]]

approved CBG program with revisions that have been adopted and 
implemented by the State to clarify requirements, update references, 
and enhance flexibility of the program and will not impose any 
additional costs or regulatory burdens.

I. Executive Summary

A. What action is the EPA taking?

    The EPA is approving a revision to the Arizona SIP submitted by the 
ADEQ. This revision includes statutes and regulations amending the CBG 
program and the Winter Oxygenated Fuel program to control CO emissions 
in the Tucson area, and it repeals the GSA Program. This action updates 
the existing SIP-approved CBG program with revisions that have been 
adopted and implemented by the State to clarify requirements, update 
references, and enhance flexibility of the program, and it will not 
impose any additional costs or regulatory burdens.

B. What is the legal authority and what are the requirements?

    Under the Clean Air Act (CAA), the EPA Administrator is required to 
approve a SIP submission that complies with the provisions of the CAA 
and applicable Federal regulations.\1\ In reviewing SIP submissions, 
the EPA's role is to approve State choices, provided they meet the 
relevant criteria of the CAA. Accordingly, this action merely approves 
State law as meeting Federal requirements and does not impose 
additional requirements beyond those imposed by State law.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \1\ 42 U.S.C. 7410(k); 40 CFR 52.02(a).
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA is approving this submittal under CAA section 110. 
Specifically, CAA section 110(k) requires SIPs to meet certain 
procedural requirements and requires the EPA to approve or disapprove 
SIP submissions. CAA section 110(l) requires States to provide public 
notice and an opportunity for public hearing of SIP revisions prior to 
their submittal and prohibits the EPA from approving any SIP revisions 
that would interfere with attainment or maintenance of National Ambient 
Air Quality Standards (NAAQS), reasonable further progress, or other 
applicable requirements of the CAA. The EPA finds that this SIP 
submittal meets all the applicable CAA requirements and the Agency 
approves it under CAA section 110(k)(3).

III. EPA Action and Public Comments

    The EPA's proposed action provided a 30-day public comment period. 
During this period, we received one comment letter that did not oppose 
the proposed approval, and supported the accuracy and completeness of 
the record. Issues raised in this comment letter are summarized with 
responses below.
    Comment 1: The commenter suggested that a citation, included in 
footnote 63 of the technical support document (TSD) for the proposed 
action, to a December 22, 2016 letter from the EPA to Gevo, Inc. should 
be corrected to refer instead to the EPA's June 12, 2018 registration 
approval letter for isobutanol to Butamax Advanced Biofuels LLC.
    Response 1: The EPA agrees that the June 12, 2018 Butamax 
registration letter is the more appropriate citation. The EPA is 
including the correct version of the letter in the docket for this 
action.
    Comment 2: The commenter addressed the EPA's proposed approval of a 
2017 version of AAC R3-7-708 that includes references to ``ethanol'' to 
describe requirements for oxygenates added to gasoline. As the 
commenter noted, Arizona later corrected these references to specify 
application of these requirements to oxygenates generally (in addition 
to ethanol) in a version of the rule that has not yet been submitted 
for SIP approval. The commenter suggested that the EPA should either 
decline to approve this version of the rule or state expressly in the 
final rule's regulatory text (and not only in the TSD) that the 
enforceable scope of R3-7-708 extends to isobutanol blending, 
notwithstanding the section's ethanol-specific terms.
    Response 2: The EPA acknowledges the commenter's concern regarding 
the enforceable scope of AAC R3-7-708 and the presence of ethanol-
specific language in the submitted version of R3-7-708. As the EPA 
stated in the TSD for our proposed rulemaking:


[[Page 66006]]


    As a general matter, the CAA requirements for oxygenated fuel 
are oxygenate-neutral, in that they do not require any specific 
oxygenate to be used. Consistent with these requirements, the EPA 
has previously communicated to ADEQ that its CBG program regulations 
should allow oxygenates other than ethanol, and the State revised 
its regulations in 2018 to make this change.\7\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \7\ TSD at 19 (internal citations omitted).

    The EPA also explained that the language in the submitted version 
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
of AAC R3-7-708 was nonetheless acceptable:

    In the context of the broader CBG program, which clearly 
specifies that oxygenates other than ethanol to be used, we consider 
this language to be acceptable because it is apparent that the 
references to ethanol are meant to address general requirements for 
oxygenates and are not intended to limit the choice of oxygenate for 
oxygenated fuels.\8\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \8\ Id.

    The EPA reiterates here that the scope of our approval of this 
provision extends to all oxygenates that the Agency has registered 
under CAA section 211(a), which is consistent with the broader context 
of the submitted CBG program, notwithstanding the rule's references to 
ethanol. As the EPA noted in the TSD for our proposed action, the State 
has indicated that it plans to submit a revised version of this rule in 
its next SIP submittal.\9\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \9\ Id. at 18.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    Comment 3: The commenter requested that the EPA state an individual 
basis for each of the nine categories of changes collectively described 
as ``administrative in nature.'' The commenter noted particularly the 
CARBOB Model substitution and the blendstock recertification allowance, 
which they characterize as introducing new compliance pathways.
    Response 3: The TSD identifies multiple changes as administrative 
in nature and provides a general basis for the approval of such 
changes.\10\ With respect to the CARBOB Model substitution and the 
blendstock recertification allowance, the Agency's proposed approval of 
the submitted revisions is based on the fact that the underlying 
measures have already been approved by the EPA and the measures have 
previously approved compliance pathways.\11\ The submitted revisions 
add SIP-strengthening compliance flexibilities to previously approved 
regulations. The EPA has considered the underlying provisions 
individually in reaching this conclusion and has determined that the 
submitted revisions are approvable.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \10\ Id. at 13.
    \11\ Id. at 11 and 22.
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    The EPA notes that the changes the Agency is acting on and 
approving strengthen the SIP by providing greater clarity to the 
regulated public. As stated in the TSD for the proposed action:

    The EPA has long acknowledged that SIP revisions to an approved 
state fuel program that are administrative in nature because they 
improve and strengthen an existing SIP-approved fuel program are 
within the scope of the previous necessity finding and do not 
require another ``necessity'' demonstration under CAA section 
211(c)(4)(C)(i).\12\
---------------------------------------------------------------------------

    \12\ Id. at 13.

    Comment 4: The commenter suggested that the EPA should clarify its 
expectations for the interaction between the proposed SIP approval and 
the EPA's national waiver of Federal enforcement which applies to state 
boutique fuel requirements for gasoline, including specifically whether 
the EPA anticipates the waiver lapsing before or shortly after this 
action is finalized, and what compliance transition, if any, regulated 
parties should expect if Federal enforcement of the approved Arizona 
CBG requirements resumes on short notice.
    Response 4: The comment is beyond the scope of this rulemaking. The 
present action addresses only whether the rules submitted by Arizona 
comply with all applicable CAA requirements for a SIP submittal.
    Comment 5: The commenter suggested that the EPA should identify a 
specific timeline or commitment for review of the 2023 amendments, and 
that the Agency should clarify how the gap in the federally enforceable 
SIP and the requirements that are currently in effect at the State 
level will be addressed and over what timeframe.
    Response 5: The present action addresses only the rules submitted 
by Arizona. Once approved into the SIP, these rules will be federally 
effective and enforceable upon all regulated parties. As noted in 
response to Comment 2, Arizona has indicated that it plans to submit a 
revised version of Rule R3-7-708 in its next SIP submittal.
    Comment 6: The commenter observed that the publication date of the 
Federal Register publication cited in footnote 3 of the proposal should 
be May 16, 1988,'' instead of ``May 16, 1998,'' and suggested that this 
should be changed in the final rule.
    Response 6: The EPA agrees that the date for this citation was 
incorrectly listed in the proposed rulemaking. The EPA hereby clarifies 
that the correct citation is ``53 FR 17378 (May 16, 1988).''
    No comments were submitted that changed the EPA's assessment of the 
submitted rules as described in our proposed action. Therefore, the EPA 
continues to find that the submitted rules should be approved into the 
Arizona SIP because they fulfill all relevant CAA requirements. 
Therefore, as authorized in CAA section 110(k)(3), the EPA is approving 
the submitted statutes and rules into the Arizona SIP.

V. Statutory and Executive Order Reviews

    Under the CAA, the Administrator is required to approve a SIP 
submission that complies with the provisions of the CAA and applicable 
Federal regulations. 42 U.S.C. 7410(k); 40 CFR 52.02(a). Thus, in 
reviewing SIP submissions, the EPA's role is to approve state choices, 
provided that they meet the criteria of

the CAA. Accordingly, this action merely approves State law as meeting 
Federal requirements and does not impose additional requirements beyond 
those imposed by State law. For that reason, this action:
     Is not a significant regulatory action subject to review 
by the Office of Management and Budget under Executive Order 12866 (58 
FR 51735, October 4, 1993);
     Is not an Executive Order 14192 (90 FR 9065, February 6, 
2025) regulatory action because this action is not significant under 
Executive Order 12866;
     Does not impose an information collection burden under the 
provisions of the Paperwork Reduction Act (44 U.S.C. 3501 et seq.);
     Is certified as not having a significant economic impact 
on a substantial number of small entities under the Regulatory 
Flexibility Act (5 U.S.C. 601 et seq.);
     Does not contain any unfunded mandate or significantly or 
uniquely affect small governments, as described in the Unfunded 
Mandates Reform Act of 1995 (Pub. L. 104-4);
     Does not have federalism implications as specified in 
Executive Order 13132 (64 FR 43255, August 10, 1999);
     Is not subject to Executive Order 13045 (62 FR 19885,

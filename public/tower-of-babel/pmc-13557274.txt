Ablative Fractional CO2 Laser Therapy for a Post-Mpox Facial Scar
Authors: Justyna Czarny-Kamm, Damian Kadylak, Monika Sikorska, Roman J. Nowicki
Published: 2026

Introduction

Mpox (formerly monkeypox) can result in residual scarring, particularly in visible or functionally sensitive areas such as the face. These scars may cause both aesthetic and functional impairment, yet the published data on their management remain extremely limited. We report a case of an extensive facial Mpox scar successfully treated with ablative fractional CO2 laser therapy.

Case Presentation

A 28-year-old Caucasian male presented in January 2023 with an extensive facial scar involving the skin of the chin and a defect of the lower lip. In October 2022, he had been diagnosed with Mpox and early syphilis. HIV infection was ruled out, and he had no chronic comorbidities.

The diagnosis of Mpox was based on a typical clinical presentation; however, due to unavailability of molecular testing, it was not laboratory confirmed.

Three months after the resolution of active ulcerations, extensive scarring developed, resulting in noticeable deformity of the lower-lip vermilion contour, mucosal dryness without functional impairment, and hypertrophy of the chin skin with itching and tightness (Figure 1).

The patient underwent four sessions of ablative fractional CO2 laser therapy (power 20 W; dwell time 3000 μs; repeat interval 0.5 s) at monthly intervals. Posttreatment care included emollients and broad-spectrum photoprotection. No complication such as infection, prolonged erythema, or dyspigmentation was observed.

Six months after the final session, the patient maintained a good aesthetic result with significantly reduced vermilion border deformation and satisfactory patient-reported outcome.

Discussion

Residual scarring after Mpox infection represents an emerging dermatological and reconstructive challenge. Early registry data suggested scarring in approximately 13 % of cases [1], while more recent prospective studies with follow-up of 12–23 months reported visible scars in 48–63% of patients [2,3]. Hyperpigmented, hypopigmented, and atrophic scars are most common, whereas hypertrophic or contractile scars may cause functional symptoms and aesthetic distress.

Few reports have addressed post-Mpox scar management. Pimentel et al. described combined pulsed-dye and 1550 nm Er:Glass laser treatment of a penile scar with favorable results [4], while Raffaele et al. reported photodynamic and photobiomodulation therapy for an active facial ulceration [5]. However, standardized protocols for ablative or fractional laser therapy in Mpox scars are lacking, and treatment approaches are currently extrapolated from post-acne or post-burn scar management.

Fractional CO2 laser therapy promotes dermal remodeling through controlled ablation and collagen regeneration. In this case, four sessions produced durable improvement without complications, supporting its potential as a safe, effective, and minimally invasive option for extensive post-Mpox scarring.

Conclusion

Ablative fractional CO2 laser therapy may offer an effective approach to improve both cosmetic and functional outcomes in post-Mpox scars. Further studies are required to establish standardized treatment parameters and evaluate long-term outcomes.

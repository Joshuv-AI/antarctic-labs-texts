Background Cohomology and Symplectic Reduction
Maxim Braverman

Abstract
. We consider a Hamiltonian action of a compact Lie group G G on a complete Kähler manifold M M with a proper moment map. In a previous paper, we defined a regularized version of the Dolbeault cohomology of a G G -equivariant holomorphic vector bundle, called the background cohomology. In this paper, we show that the background cohomology of a prequantum line bundle over M M ‘commutes with reduction’, i.e. the invariant part of the background cohomology is isomorphic to the usual Dolbeault cohomology of the symplectic reduction.

1.
Introduction

If
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Kähler manifold
M
, the Dolbeault cohomology
H^{0,*}(M,E)
is finite-dimensional and has a lot of nice properties. In particular, given a Hamiltonian action of a compact group
G
on
M
and a prequantum
G
-equivariant line bundle
L
over
M
, the
G
-invariant part of the Dolbeault cohomology of
L
,
H^{0,*}(M,L)^{G}
, is isomorphic to the cohomology of the symplectic reduction,
H^{0,*}(M\sslash G,L\sslash G)
, cf.
[
14
,
2
,
18
]
. This result refines the famous Guillemin-Sternberg ‘quantization commutes with reduction’ conjecture,
[
6
,
11
,
15
]
, to the level of individual cohomology groups.

If
M
is non-compact,
H^{0,*}(M,E)
is an infinite-dimensional space, and much less is known about it. In
[
4
]
, we consider the case when
M
is a complete Kähler manifold, the moment map
\mu
is proper, and the vector field generated by
\mu
on
M
does not vanish outside of a compact set
K\subset M
. We refer to the pair
(M,\mu)
as a
tamed Kähler manifold
. For a
G
-equivariant holomorphic bundle
E
over
M
we defined the regularized version of the Dolbeault cohomology
H^{0,*}_{\operatorname{bg}}(M,E)
called the
background cohomology
. It is still infinite-dimensional. But as a representation of
G
it decomposes into a direct sum of irreducible components and each component appears in this decomposition finitely many times:

(1.1)

H^{0,p}_{\operatorname{bg}}(M,E)\ =\ \sum_{V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}G}\,\beta^{p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}\cdot V,\qquad p=0,\ldots,n.

The alternating sum of the background cohomology is equal to the equivariant index of the pair
(E,\mu)
constructed by Paradan
[
13
]
and Braverman
[
3
]
(see also
[
10
]
and
[
1
]
).

It is shown in
[
4
]
that the background cohomology possesses many properties of Dolbeault cohomology on a compact
G
-manifold. Eg. it satisfies a version of the Kodaira vanishing theorem. There is also an analog of Witten’s holomorphic Morse inequalities
[
16
]
(see also
[
17
]
).

Let now
L
be a holomorphic
G
-equivariant
prequantum
line bundle over
M
. This means that
L
is endowed with a
G
-invariant holomorphic Hermitian connection
\nabla
such that the Kähler form

(1.2)

\omega\ =\ \frac{i}{2\pi}\nabla^{2}.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
denote the Lie algebra of
G
. We identify it with its dual
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
using a
G
-invariant metric on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then the moment map can be viewed as a map
\mu:M\to{\mathfrak{g}}
. We assume that
0
is a regular value of
\mu
and that the action of
G
on
\mu^{-1}(0)
is free. Then, since we assume that
\mu
is proper, the symplectic reduction
M\sslash G
is a smooth compact Kähler manifold. The prequantum line bundle
L

induces a prequantum line bundle
L\sslash G
over
M\sslash G
. The main result of this paper is the following

Theorem 1.1
.

Suppose
M
is a complete Kähler manifold on which a compact group
G
acts Hamiltonially with proper moment map
\mu:M\to{\mathfrak{g}}
. Let
L
be a
G
-equivariant prequantum line bundle over
M
. Suppose the vector field induced by
\mu
on
M
does not vanish outside a compact set
K\subset M
, so that the background cohomology
H_{\operatorname{bg}}^{0,p}(M,L)
(
p=0,\ldots,n
) is defined. Let
H_{\operatorname{bg}}^{0,p}(M,L)^{G}
denote the
G
-invariants in
H_{\operatorname{bg}}^{0,p}(M,L)
. Then

(1.3)

\dim H_{\operatorname{bg}}^{0,p}(M,L)^{G}\ =\ \dim H_{\operatorname{bg}}^{0,p}(M\sslash G,L\sslash G).

When manifold
M
is compact, the background cohomology is equal to the usual Dolbeault cohomology and the above theorem reduces to the results of
[
14
,
2
]
.

The assumption that 0 is a regular value of the moment map can be removed by combining the methods of this paper with
[
18
]
.

The definition of the equivariant index of the pair
(E,\mu)
was extended to the case when a non-compact Lie group acts properly on
M
in
[
7
]
and
[
5
]
. It would be interesting to extend the definition of the background cohomology to this case. We will address this problem elsewhere.

2.
The background cohomology

In this section, we recall the construction of the background cohomology from
[
4
]
. First, we define a notion of a tamed Kähler
G
-manifold and then construct the background cohomology of holomorphic
G
-equivariant vector bundles over a tamed Kähler manifold.

2.1.
A Hamiltonian group action

Suppose a compact Lie group
G
acts holomorphically on a complete Kähler manifold
M
. The moment map
\mu:M\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
is defined by the formula

d\langle\mu,{\mathbf{u}}\rangle\ =\ \iota(u)\,\omega,

where
{\mathbf{u}}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
\omega\in\Omega^{2}(M)
is the Kähler form on
M
and

u(x)\ :=\ \frac{d}{dt}_{\big|_{t=0}}\exp(t{\mathbf{u}})\,x\ \in\ T_{x}M

is the vector field on
M
generated by
{\mathbf{u}}
.

Fix an invariant scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and let
|\mu|^{2}
denote the square of the norm of
\mu
with respect to this scalar product. This scalar product defines and isomorphism
\psi:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
and we denote

(2.1)

{\mathbf{v}}(x)\ :=\ \psi\big(\mu(x)\big)\ \in{\mathfrak{g}}.

Consider the Hamiltonian vector field with Hamiltonian
|\mu|^{2}/2

(2.2)

v(x)\ :=\ -\,J\,\nabla\,\frac{|\mu(x)|^{2}}{2}

on
M
(here
J
denotes the complex structure on
TM
). One checks that is equal to the vector field generated by
{\mathbf{v}}
:

(2.3)

v(x)\ =\ \frac{d}{dt}_{\big|_{t=0}}\exp(t{\mathbf{v}}(x))\,x.

2.2.
Tamed Kähler manifolds

Definition 2.1
.

A
tamed Kähler
G
-manifold
is a complete Kähler manifold
(M,g^{M})
together with a Hamiltonian action of the group
G
, such that

(i)

the moment map
\mu
is proper;

(ii)

the vector field (
2.2
) does not vanish outside of a compact set.

2.3.
A rescaling

Our definition of the regularized cohomology uses a certain rescaling of the function
|\mu|^{2}/2
, which is defined in (
2.9
) using a function

(2.4)

\phi(x)\ :=\ s\big(\,|\mu(x)|^{2}/2\,\big),

where
s:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is a smooth positive strictly increasing function satisfying certain growth conditions at infinity. Roughly speaking, we demand that
\phi(x)
tends to infinity “fast enough” when
x
tends to infinity. The precise conditions we impose on
\phi
are quite technical, cf. Definition
2.2
below (see also Definition 4.8 of
[
4
]
), but our construction turns out to be independent of the concrete choice of
\phi
. It is important, however, to know that at least one admissible function exists, cf. Lemma 4.12 of
[
4
]
.

We now briefly recall the construction of the rescaling from Section 4 of
[
4
]
.

Consider a
G
-equivariant holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
M
endowed with a
G
-invariant holomorphic connection
\nabla^{E}
. Fix a Hermitian metric
g^{E}
on
E
. Consider the bundle
{\mathcal{E}}=E\otimes\Lambda^{\bullet}(T^{0,1}{}M)^{*}
and endow it with the Hermitian metric induced by
g^{E}
and
g^{M}
. Let
\nabla^{\mathcal{E}}
denote the connection on
{\mathcal{E}}
induced by
\nabla^{E}
and the Levi-Civita connection on
TM
.

For a vector
\mathbf{u}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, we denote by
{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbf{u}}
the infinitesimal action of
\mathbf{u}
on
\Gamma(M,{\mathcal{E}})
induced by the action on
G
on
{\mathcal{E}}
and by
\nabla_{u}^{\mathcal{E}}:\Gamma(M,{\mathcal{E}})\to\Gamma(M,{\mathcal{E}})
the covariant derivative along the vector field
u
induced by
\mathbf{u}
. The difference between those two operators is a bundle map, which we denote by

(2.5)

\mu^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{u})\ :=\ \nabla^{\mathcal{E}}_{u}-{\mathcal{L}}^{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathbf{u}}\ \in\ \operatorname{End\,}{\mathcal{E}}.

We will use the same notation
|\cdot|
for the norms on the bundles
TM,T^{*}M,{\mathcal{E}}
. Let
\operatorname{End\,}(TM)
and
\operatorname{End\,}({\mathcal{E}})
denote the bundles of endomorphisms of
TM
and
{\mathcal{E}}
, respectively. We will denote by
\|\cdot\|
the norms on these bundles induced by
|\cdot|
. Set

(2.6)

\nu=|\mathbf{v}|+\|\nabla^{\text{LC}}v\|+\|\mu^{\mathcal{E}}(\mathbf{v})\|+|v|+1.

Definition 2.2
.

A smooth function
s:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called
admissible for the quadruple
(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
if
s^{\prime}(r)>0
and the function

(2.7)

f(x)\ :=\ s^{\prime}\big(|\mu(x)|^{2}/2\big)

satisfies the following condition

(2.8)

\lim_{M\ni x\to\infty}\,\frac{f^{2}|v|^{2}}{|df||v|+f\nu+1}\ =\ \infty.

We denote by
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
the set of admissible functions for
(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})

Remark 2.4
.

Suppose
h_{1}^{E}\leq h_{2}^{E}
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
. Then it follows immediately from the definition that
{\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E}_{2})\subseteq{\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E}_{1})
.

The following lemma summarizes Lemmas 4.11 and 4.12 of
[
4
]
:

Lemma 2.3
.

Given a holomorphic Hermitian
G
-equivariant vector bundle
(E,g^{E})
over a tamed Kähler manifold
(M,g^{M})
, the set
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
of admissible functions is not empty.

If
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
, then for any positive real numbers
t_{1},t_{2}>0
the function
s:=t_{1}s_{1}+t_{2}s_{2}
is admissible. Thus, the set
{\mathcal{F}}={\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
of admissible functions is a convex cone.

2.5.
The deformed Dolbeault cohomology

Let
s:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be an admissible function, cf. Definition
2.2
, and set

\phi(x)\ :=\ s\big(\,|\mu(x)|^{2}/2\,\big),\qquad x\in M.

Consider the deformed Dolbeault differential

(2.9)

\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}\alpha\ =\ e^{-\phi}\circ\bar{\text{$\partial$}}\circ e^{\phi}\,\alpha\ =\ \bar{\text{$\partial$}}\alpha\ +\ f\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}\big(\,|\mu|^{2}/2\,\big)\wedge\alpha,

where, as in Definition
2.2
,
f=s^{\prime}(|\mu|^{2}/2)
.

The
deformed Dolbeault-Dirac operator
is defined by

(2.10)

D_{s}\ =\ \sqrt{2}\,\big(\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}\,+\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}^{*}\,\big).

Let
L_{2}\Omega^{0,p}(M,E)
denote the space of square-integrable differential
(0,p)
-forms on
M
with values in
E
.
Define the
deformed Dolbeault cohomology
H^{0,*}_{s}(M,E)
of the triple
(M,E,s)
as the reduced cohomology of the deformed differential
\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}
:

(2.11)

H^{0,p}_{s}(M,E)\ =\ \frac{\operatorname{Ker}\,\big(\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}:L_{2}\Omega^{0,p}(M,E)\to L_{2}\Omega^{0,p+1}(M,E)\,\big)}{\overline{\operatorname{Im}\big(\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}_{s}:L_{2}\Omega^{0,p-1}(M,E)\to L_{2}\Omega^{0,p}(M,E)\,\big)}}.

The following is Theorem 5.3 of
[
4
]
:

Theorem 2.4
.

Suppose
s\in{\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
is an admissible function. Then the deformed Dolbeault cohomology
H^{0,p}_{s}(M,E)
decomposes, as a Hilbert space, into an infinite direct sum

(2.12)

H^{0,p}_{s}(M,E)\ =\ \sum_{V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}G}\,\beta^{p}_{s,V}\cdot V,

where
\operatorname{Irr\,}G
is the set of irreducible representations of
G
, and
\beta^{p}_{s,V}
are non-negative integers, which depend on the choice of the admissible function
s
. In other words, each irreducible representation of
G
appears in
H^{0,p}_{s}(M,E)
with finite multiplicity.

For each irreducible representation
V
the finite-dimensional representation

H^{0,p}_{s,V}(M,E)\ =\ \beta^{p}_{s,V}\cdot V

is called the
V
-component
of the deformed cohomology. Then

H^{0,p}_{s}(M,E)\ =\ \sum_{V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}G}\,H^{0,p}_{s,V}(M,E).

2.6.
The background cohomology

Definition 2.5
.

The minimal possible value of
\beta^{p}_{s,V}
is called the
background Betti number
and is denoted by
\beta^{p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}
:

(2.13)

\beta^{p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}\ :=\ \min\big\{\,\beta^{p}_{s^{\prime},V}:\,s^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{F}}\,\big\}

An admissible function
s
is called
V
-generic
if
\beta^{p}_{s,V}\ =\ \beta^{p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}
for all
p=0,\ldots,n
. In a certain sense, “almost all” admissible functions are
V
-generic.

It is proven in
[
4
]
that the background Betti numbers are independent of the choices made. In particular,
[
4
, Theorem 5.7]
, we have the following

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}G
be an irreducible representation of
G
. For any two
V
-generic admissible functions
s_{1},s_{2}\in{\mathcal{F}}(M,g^{M},E,g^{E})
there exists a canonical isomorphism

(2.14)

\Phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{V}:\,H^{0,*}_{s_{1},V}(M,E)\ \longrightarrow\ H^{0,*}_{s_{2},V}(M,E),

satisfying the cocycle condition

(2.15)

\Phi_{s_{2}s_{3}}^{V}\circ\Phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{V}\ =\ \Phi_{s_{1}s_{3}}^{V}.

If
\tau=s_{2}-s_{1}\geq 0
is an admissible function then the isomorphism
\Phi_{s_{1}s_{2}}^{V}
is induced by the map

(2.16)

\alpha\ \mapsto\ e^{-\tau(|\mu|^{2}/2)}\,\alpha,\,\qquad\ \alpha\in L_{2}\Omega^{0,*}(M,E).

Theorem 5.9 of
[
4
]
states that the background Betti numbers are also independent of the choice of the Hermitian metric
g^{E}
. This justifies the following

Definition 2.7
.

If
V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}{}G
, the
V
-component of the background cohomology

H^{0,p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}(M,E)
of the triple
(M,g^{M},E)
is defined to be the deformed cohomology
H^{0,*}_{s,V}(M,E)
for any Hermitian metric
g^{E}
and any
V
-generic function
s
.

The
background cohomology

H^{0,p}_{\operatorname{bg}}(M,E)
is the direct sum

(2.17)

H^{0,p}_{\operatorname{bg}}(M,E)\ :=\ \sum_{V\in\operatorname{Irr\,}G}H^{0,p}_{{\operatorname{bg}},V}(M,E).

Remark 2.7
.

Theorem 5.11 of
[
4
]
states that, in an appropriate sense, the alternating sum of the background cohomology is equal to the equivariant index of a tamed Dirac operator
(\bar{\bar{\text{$\partial$}}}+\bar{\bar{\text{$\partial$}}}^{*},{\mathbf{v}})
as defined in
[
3
]
.

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

3.1.
A prequantum line bundle

Let
(M,g^{M})
be a tamed Kähler
G
-manifold, cf. Definition
2.1
. A
G
-equivariant
prequantum
line bundle
L
over
M
is a holomorphic
G
-equivariant line bundle endowed with
G
-invariant holomorphic Hermitian metric
g^{L}
and a
G
-equivariant holomorphic Hermitian connection
\nabla
satisfying (
1.2
). Let
\mu=\mu^{L}
be defined by (
2.5
). Then
\mu
is the moment map for the action of
G
on
M
, cf.
[
9
]
. The tameness of
(M,g^{M})
implies that
\mu
is proper. We make a basic

Assumption 3.1
.

Assume that
0
is a regular value of
\mu:M\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
and that
G
acts freely on
\mu^{-1}(0)
.

Then the
symplectic reduction

M\sslash G\ :=\ \mu^{-1}(0)/G

is a smooth compact manifold and

L\sslash G\ :=\ L|_{\mu^{-1}(0)}/G

is a holomorphic prequantum line bundle over
M\sslash G
. To simplify the notation we set

(3.1)

M_{0}:=M\sslash G,\qquad L_{0}\ :=\ L\sslash G.

3.2.
Symplectic quotients of Kähler manifolds

We briefly recall how the complexification of
G
acts on
M
. See
[
8
, §11]
for details.

Let
G^{\mathbb{C}}
denote the natural complexification of the compact group
G
such that

G\times{\mathfrak{g}}\ \to\ G^{\mathbb{C}},\qquad(g,\eta)\ \mapsto\ \exp(i\eta)\,g

is a diffeomorphism. The action of
G
on
M
extends to the action of
G^{\mathbb{C}}
and our assumptions apply that
G^{\mathbb{C}}
acts freely on the set

M^{ss}\ :=\ G^{\mathbb{C}}\cdot\mu^{-1}(0).

The set
M^{ss}
coincides with the set of semi-stable points for
G^{\mathbb{C}}
action,
[
12
]
. This is a dense subset of
M
and
M_{0}=M^{ss}/G^{\mathbb{C}}
. We denote by
q
the quotient map

(3.2)

q:\,M^{ss}\ \to M^{ss}/G^{\mathbb{C}}\ =\ M_{0}.

3.3.
Harmonic forms on the symplectic reduction

Recall that
\mu^{-1}(0)
is a smooth compact manifold and that
G
acts freely on it. Let
{\overline{q}}:\mu^{-1}(0)\to M_{0}=\mu^{-1}(0)/G

denote the quotient map and set

{\tilde{h}}(x)\ =\ \sqrt{\operatorname{Vol}\,{\overline{q}}^{-1}(x)}.

Let
g^{L_{0}}
and
g^{M_{0}}
denote the Hermitian metric on
L_{0}

and the Riemannian metric on
M_{0}
induced by the fixed metrics on

L
and
M
respectively. Set

g^{L_{0}}_{{\tilde{h}}}={\tilde{h}}^{2}g^{L_{0}}
and let
\bar{\text{$\partial$}}^{*}_{\tilde{h}}
denote the
formal adjoint of the Dolbeault differential
\bar{\text{$\partial$}}:\Omega^{0,*}(M_{0},L_{0})\to\Omega^{0,*+1}(M_{0},L_{0})
with respect to the metrics

g^{L_{0}}_{{\tilde{h}}},g^{M_{0}}
.

From this point on we always consider this scalar product on
\Omega^{0,*}(M_{0},L_{0})
and define the space of square-integrable differential forms

using this scalar product. Let

{\mathcal{H}}^{j}(M_{0},L_{0})\ =\ \operatorname{Ker}\Big(\,\bar{\text{$\partial$}}\bar{\text{$\partial$}}^{*}_{\tilde{h}}+\bar{\text{$\partial$}}^{*}_{\tilde{h}}\bar{\text{$\partial$}}\,\big):\,\Omega^{0,j}(M_{0},L_{0})\ \to\ \Omega^{0,j}(M_{0},L_{0},\Big)

be the space of harmonic forms with respect to this scalar product. As a representation of

.

3.4.
A neighborhood of

Let

denote the normal bundle to

in

. If

, let

, the map

is a diffeomorphism from

to a tubular neighborhood

{{\mathcal{U}}}_{\varepsilon}
of
\mu^{-1}(0)
in

. We identify

with

3.5.
The

Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}
denote the paring

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L}:\,\Omega^{0,j}(M,L)\times\Omega^{0,k}(M,L)\ \to\ \Omega^{0,j+k}(M)

3.6.
A choice of an admissible function

Let

C\ :=\ \max\big\{\,\frac{|\mu(x)|^{2}}{2}:\,x\in{\mathcal{U}}_{\epsilon}\,\big\}.

For
\beta\in L_{2}\Omega^{0,*}(M_{0},L_{0})
we denote by
q^{*}\beta
its pull-back to a bounded differential form on

with values in

. If
s

.

Let
V_{0}=\mathbb{C}
denote the trivial representation of
G
in
\mathbb{C}

H^{0,*}_{s,V_{0}}(M,L)
is equal to the

-invariant part

We choose a
V_{0}
-generic admissible function

such that

(i)

s(\tau)=\tau

\tau\leq C
;

(ii)

grows fast enough at infinity so that for any

and any

\int_{M^{ss}}\,\big\langle\,e^{-s(\frac{|\mu|^{2}}{2})}q^{*}\beta,\,\alpha\,\big\rangle_{L}\wedge\omega^{r}

converges and the linear map

(3.4)

\alpha\ \mapsto\ \int_{M^{ss}}\,\big\langle\,e^{-s(\frac{|\mu|^{2}}{2})}q^{*}\beta,\alpha\,\big\rangle_{L}\wedge\omega^{r}

is bounded

We note that for every
t\geq 1
the function
ts
is also
V_{0}
admissible. Thus we can consider the deformed cohomology

. For

is
V_{0}
-generic. Using the semi-continuity property of the dimensions of the kernels of operators one can show that for almost all

is also
V_{0}
-generic. Moreover, we show below that there exists

the function

is
V_{0}
-generic. Thus for each

Let

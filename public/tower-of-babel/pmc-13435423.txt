Comparative analysis of optical coherence tomography angiography metrics in primary open-angle glaucoma and ocular hypertension
Authors: Büşra Karadağ, Belgin İzgi, Şerife Bayraktar
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
PurposeTo evaluate macular ganglion cell complex (GCC) thickness and parafoveal vessel density (pfVD) using optical coherence tomography angiography (OCT-A) in patients with ocular hypertension (OHT) and early- to intermediate-stage primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG).Material and methodsThis cross-sectional study included 150 eyes from 56 patients with POAG and 32 patients with OHT who were under regular follow-up at the Glaucoma Unit of Istanbul University Faculty of Medicine, Department of Ophthalmology. Evaluations were conducted between November and December 2022. Additionally, 50 eyes from 25 healthy individuals were included as a control group. OCT-A imaging was performed using the Topcon DRI OCT Triton system (Topcon Corporation, Japan). Macular GCC thickness and pfVD in the superficial and deep capillary plexuses were measured and compared among groups.ResultsA total of 113 participants (74 females, 39 males) were included. No significant differences in macular GCC thickness or pfVD were observed between the control and OHT groups. In contrast, both parameters were significantly reduced in POAG eyes, with greater reductions observed in more advanced disease stages. The decrease in pfVD was more pronounced in the superficial capillary plexus compared to the deep plexus. In early-stage POAG, GCC thinning appeared more prominent than pfVD reduction.ConclusıonOur findings suggest that macular GCC thickness and pfVD assessed by OCT-A are associated with glaucomatous changes. In particular, structural alterations in macular GCC may be more pronounced than vascular changes in early-stage POAG. These results indicate that macular GCC assessment may provide complementary information in the evaluation of early glaucomatous damage. However, due to the cross-sectional design of the study, no conclusions can be drawn regarding the temporal relationship between structural and vascular changes.

Introduction

Glaucoma is a chronic, progressive optic neuropathy characterized by irreversible damage to the optic nerve, leading to gradual loss of visual function and visual field, and potentially resulting in blindness [1–5]. Although elevated intraocular pressure (IOP) remains the most significant modifiable risk factor, glaucoma is now widely recognized as a multifactorial disease in which vascular dysregulation may play an important role in its pathogenesis and progression.

Early diagnosis and accurate monitoring of disease progression are essential for preserving visual function. In current clinical practice, visual field testing is used to assess functional impairment, while optical coherence tomography (OCT) provides structural evaluation of the optic nerve head and retinal layers [6–11]. However, functional deficits such as visual field loss typically become detectable only after substantial retinal ganglion cell (RGC) damage has already occurred, highlighting the importance of identifying structural changes at earlier stages [12, 13]. Given that a significant proportion of RGCs are located within the macular region, macular ganglion cell complex (GCC) analysis has gained increasing importance in glaucoma assessment [14–18].

Several imaging techniques, including fluorescein angiography, color Doppler ultrasonography, laser Doppler flowmetry, and Heidelberg retinal flowmetry, have been used to evaluate ocular blood flow in glaucomatous eyes. Although these methods have demonstrated vascular alterations, their routine clinical use remains limited due to factors such as invasiveness, limited reproducibility, and technical constraints.

Optical coherence tomography angiography (OCT-A) is a relatively recent, noninvasive imaging modality that enables high-resolution visualization of retinal and choroidal microvasculature without the need for dye injection. It allows quantitative assessment of vessel density and perfusion parameters, providing additional information regarding the vascular component of glaucoma [7, 19–21]. Furthermore, OCT-A enables simultaneous evaluation of macular GCC thickness, offering the potential to investigate structural and vascular changes within the same imaging platform.

In this study, we aimed to evaluate macular vessel density and GCC thickness using OCT-A in patients with ocular hypertension (OHT) and early- to intermediate-stage primary open-angle glaucoma (POAG), and to compare these parameters with those of healthy controls.

Materials and methods

Ethical approval

The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Istanbul University Faculty of Medicine (Approval Date and Number: 25.11.2022– 1,381,233). All procedures were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments. Written and verbal informed consent was obtained from all participants prior to inclusion.

Study population

This cross-sectional study included 150 eyes from 56 patients with POAG and 32 patients with OHT, who were under regular follow-up at the Glaucoma Unit of the Department of Ophthalmology, Istanbul Faculty of Medicine, between November and December 2022. Additionally, 50 eyes from 25 healthy individuals were included as a control group.

OCT-A imaging was performed using the Topcon ImageNet 6 system (DRI OCT Triton, Topcon Corporation, Japan). Macular GCC thickness and pfVD in the superficial and deep capillary plexuses were measured and compared across groups. Since both eyes from some participants were included in the analysis, the assumption of statistical independence may have been violated. This limitation was not adjusted for statistically and is acknowledged in the discussion.

Inclusion criteria

Participants were between 40 and 65 years of age and had a confirmed diagnosis of POAG or OHT based on a comprehensive ophthalmological evaluation, including biomicroscopy, gonioscopy, IOP measurement, OCT, and visual field testing. All participants were free of systemic diseases, including obesity and hypertension, and had no history of smoking or ocular inflammatory conditions such as uveitis. Eligible participants were required to have a BCVA of 0.7 or higher, a refractive error between +3.00 and −3.00 diopters, and clear ocular media. Additionally, participants with a history of uncomplicated cataract extraction were eligible for inclusion only if a minimum postoperative interval of six months had elapsed, ensuring that transient surgical effects did not influence OCT-A measurements. The control group consisted of age-matched healthy individuals with no ocular or systemic diseases.

Ophthalmological evaluation

All participants underwent a comprehensive ophthalmological examination, including refraction assessment, BCVA measurement, slit-lamp biomicroscopy, and gonioscopy. IOP was measured using Goldmann applanation tonometry, and fundus examination was performed using a 90 D lens. Peripapillary RNFL thickness was measured using spectral-domain OCT (HRA+OCT Spectralis, Heidelberg Engineering). Visual field testing was conducted with the Humphrey Field Analyzer II (Model 750) using the 24–2 SITA-standard protocol. Glaucoma staging was based on the MD values: early POAG was defined as MD > −6 dB, and moderate POAG was defined as MD between −6 and −12 dB.

Diagnostic criteria

In this study, OHT was defined as having an IOP greater than 21 mmHg in the absence of any detectable structural or functional glaucomatous damage. The diagnosis of POAG required the presence of open angles on gonioscopy, characteristic glaucomatous optic nerve damage, and corresponding visual field defects.

OCT-A imaging and analysis

All OCT-A scans were obtained using the Topcon DRI OCT Triton system. To ensure high image quality, scans with a signal strength of less than 6/10 were excluded from the analysis. Macular vessel density was assessed using 6 × 6 mm scans, while GCC thickness was evaluated via 7 × 7 mm scans. The foveal avascular zone (FAZ) was automatically identified by the software, and vessel density was calculated within a 300 μm perifoveal area.

Regarding the capillary plexus definitions, the SCP was defined as the layer extending from 2.6 μm below the ILM to 15.6 μm below the IPL. The DCP was defined as the layer between 15.6 μm and 70.2 μm below the IPL. Due to software limitations, manual segmentation was performed for the DCP to ensure accuracy. Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were performed using NCSS 2020 software (NCSS, LLC, Utah, USA). The conformity of continuous variables to a normal distribution was assessed using the Shapiro–Wilk test. Since the data did not follow a normal distribution, non-parametric tests were employed. Specifically, the Kruskal–Wallis test was used for comparisons among multiple groups, and the Dunn test was performed as a post hoc analysis to identify significant differences between specific group pairs. Categorical variables were compared using the Fisher–Freeman–Halton test. Descriptive statistics were expressed as mean ± standard deviation (SD) or median (minimum–maximum) values, as appropriate. Although both eyes of some participants were included in the study, no statistical adjustment was performed for inter-eye correlation; this potential violation of statistical independence is addressed in the study limitations. The level of statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Discussion

In this study, we evaluated macular vessel density together with GCC thickness using OCT-A in patients with early- and intermediate-stage POAG and OHT. These parameters were compared with those obtained from healthy controls to explore their association with glaucomatous changes.

A total of 200 eyes from 113 participants were analyzed, including 32 eyes with OHT, 56 eyes with POAG (34 early-stage and 22 intermediate-stage), and 50 eyes from 25 healthy controls. Statistically significant differences were observed among the groups in terms of age, IOP, and BCVA, whereas gender distribution did not differ significantly.

pfVD in both the superficial and deep capillary plexuses was evaluated across four macular quadrants (superior, inferior, nasal, and temporal), as well as in terms of mean values. pfVD values were lower in glaucomatous eyes, with more pronounced reductions observed in the intermediate-stage POAG group. No significant differences were identified between the OHT and control groups. Notably, early-stage POAG eyes showed significantly lower superficial pfVD in the inferior and temporal quadrants and significantly lower deep pfVD in the superior quadrant compared to healthy controls. The reduction in superficial pfVD appeared more evident than that in the deep plexus [22]. El-Nimri et al. similarly reported lower superficial pfVD in glaucomatous eyes, without significant differences in deep pfVD across groups. Lommatzsch et al. observed reduced superficial and deep pfVD in glaucoma patients compared with controls but did not identify differences between disease stages [23]. In contrast, our findings suggest stage-related differences, particularly in superficial pfVD.

Traditionally, glaucomatous damage has been assessed using circumpapillary RNFL thickness measured by OCT [24–27]. However, this approach primarily reflects axonal loss and does not directly evaluate neuronal cell bodies and dendritic structures within the GCL and IPL, which are also affected in glaucoma [28]. Experimental studies suggest that structural alterations in RGC soma and dendrites may occur prior to detectable axonal degeneration [29, 30]. In this context, macular GCC analysis may provide additional structural information in the evaluation of glaucomatous damage. Previous studies have reported reduced GCC thickness in glaucomatous eyes, and some have suggested its potential value in early disease detection [28]. Wang et al. demonstrated decreased macular vessel density and GCC thickness using OCT-A, with changes associated with disease severity [31]. Consistent with these findings, our results showed reduced GCC thickness in glaucomatous eyes, with greater reductions observed in intermediate-stage POAG [30, 32, 33].

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, both eyes from some participants were included, which may have violated the assumption of statistical independence. Second, although gender distribution was balanced across groups, the overall sample included a higher proportion of female participants. Third, age differed significantly between groups and may have influenced both vessel density and GCC measurements; therefore, a confounding effect cannot be fully excluded. In addition, some patients were receiving topical anti-glaucomatous medications, which may affect ocular perfusion parameters. The effects of such treatments on OCT-A measurements remain insufficiently understood, and this variable was not controlled in the present study. Potential confounders such as smoking status and systemic factors (including obesity) were also not systematically evaluated.

Interpretation of correlation findings

The observed reduction in the correlation between superficial and deep vessel densities from early to intermediate-stage POAG suggests a possible alteration in the relationship between retinal vascular layers as the disease progresses. In addition, the absence of a significant association between superficial vessel density and GCC thickness in early-stage POAG, followed by a moderate correlation in intermediate-stage disease, may indicate that structural and vascular changes do not occur simultaneously.

However, these findings should be interpreted with caution, as the cross-sectional design of the present study does not allow definitive conclusions regarding the temporal sequence of structural and vascular changes. Longitudinal studies are required to further elucidate whether macular GCC thinning consistently precedes microvascular alterations during the early stages of glaucoma.

In conclusion, despite its limitations, this study contributes to the understanding of structural and vascular alterations in glaucomatous eyes. OCT-A–derived parameters may provide complementary information in the evaluation of glaucoma. Further studies with larger and more demographically balanced cohorts are needed to validate these findings.

Conclusion

Our findings suggest that macular GCC thickness and pfVD assessed by OCT-A are associated with glaucomatous changes. In the present study, macular GCC measurements were significantly lower in glaucomatous eyes compared with other groups, with greater reductions observed in intermediate-stage disease. Notably, in early-stage glaucoma, the reduction in macular GCC thickness appeared more pronounced than the corresponding decrease in pfVD.

While OCT-A–derived macular vessel density and GCC thickness both provide valuable insights into glaucomatous alterations, our findings suggest that structural changes in macular GCC may be more evident than vascular changes in early disease stages. Therefore, macular GCC assessment may serve as a complementary parameter in the evaluation of early glaucomatous damage, rather than as a standalone tool. However, due to the cross-sectional design of this study, no definitive conclusions can be drawn regarding the temporal relationship between structural and vascular changes. Further longitudinal studies with larger and more diverse cohorts are warranted to confirm these findings.

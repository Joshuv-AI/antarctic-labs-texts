Observability estimates for the Schrödinger equation on the equilateral triangle
Paul Alphonse, David Lafontaine

Abstract
. We prove observability estimates for the Schrödinger equation posed on the equilateral triangle in the plane, under both Neumann and Dirichlet boundary conditions. No geometric control condition is required on the rough localization functions that we consider. This is the first result of this kind on a non-toric domain in the compact setting. Our strategy is to exploit Pinsky’s tiling argument to deduce this result from observability estimates on rational twisted tori. These are obtained via propagation of singularities, adapting arguments from Burq and Zworski. The later require Strichartz estimates on such twisted rational tori, that we derive from Zygmund inequalities in the same geometric setting, also providing the sharp constant. Strichartz estimates on the equilateral triangle are also derived from this analysis.

1.
Introduction and main results

1.1.
Context and main result

This work is part of the study of null-controllability of Schrödinger equations of the form

(
S_{\mathcal{M}}
)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &i\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\Delta u(t,x)=a(x)\mathbbm{1}_{(0,T)}h(t,x),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathcal{M},\\
&u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M}),\end{aligned}\right.

where
\mathcal{M}
is a compact Riemannian manifold,
\Delta
is the Laplace–Beltrami operator on
\mathcal{M}
, with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary condition when
\partial M\neq\emptyset
,
i.e.

u(t,x)=0\quad\text{or}\quad\partial_{n}u(t,x)=0\quad\text{on $\mathbb{R}\times\partial M$},

and
a\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
is a localization function.
A central problem is to characterize those
a\in L^{p}(\mathcal{M})
for which null-controllability holds for the equation (

S
M

),
i.e.
for which, given
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
, there exists a control
h
such that the solution to (

S
M

) satisfies
u(T,\cdot)=0
. This question is deeply related to the geometric properties of the manifold
\mathcal{M}
. In this very general setting, it is now well-known that when
a=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
is the indicator function of some open set
\omega\subset\mathcal{M}
, the equation (

S
M

) is null-controllable at any positive time
T>0
provided
\omega
verifies the co-called geometric control condition. The latter was introduced in
[
5
,
27
]
for the wave equation, and was first related to the Schrödinger equation in the seminal work
[
19
]
in the context of boundary control,
i.e.
with a localization function
a
supported on the boundary, (see also
[
21
,
24
]
for internal results).

While the geometric control condition is necessary and sufficient to ensure the null-controllability of the wave equation
[
9
]
, this is not the case in general for the Schrödinger equation. This sufficient geometric condition is necessary in some cases, as for the sphere
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{S}^{2}
(for which some spherical harmonics concentrate around the equator) or the disk
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{D}
(the control support
\omega
has to intersects the boundary, due to the presence of whispering-gallery modes)
[
4
]
, and not necessary in some others, such as for the tori
\mathcal{M}=\mathbb{T}^{d}
with
d\geq 1

[
14
,
16
]
, or hyperbolic compact surfaces (more generally, Anosov surfaces)
[
12
,
15
]
, where the control support
\omega\subset\mathcal{M}
may be taken to be any nonempty subset.

In the case of one– and two– dimensional tori, one can even prove null-controllability with rough localization functions
a\in L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{d})
; see, for instance,
[
1
,
10
,
20
,
23
]
. This includes the interesting case where
a=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega}
and
\omega\subset\mathbb{T}^{d}
is a measurable set with positive measure. Extending these results to higher dimensional tori
d\geq 3
remains a challenging open problem. Moreover, these are the only two results currently available in the setting of rough control.

In this paper, we investigate a new planar geometric context and prove a null-controllability result with rough localization functions and no geometric control condition in a non-rectangular setting. More precisely, we consider the equilateral triangle

\var=\big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:0<x<1,\,0<y<x\sqrt{3},\,y<\sqrt{3}(1-x)\big\}.

Our null-controllability result is the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
T>0
be a positive time and
a\in L^{\infty}(\var)\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative function. Then, for every initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\var)
, there exists a control
h\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\var)
such that the solution
u
of the equation
(
S_{\var}
)
satisfies
u(T,\cdot)=0
.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first null-controllability result for the Schrödinger equation with no geometric control condition and rough localization functions on a non-rectangle manifold with boundary.
Let us mention that null-controllability results with boundary controls for the equation (
S_{\var}
) posed on general triangles (actually, on general simplices) are nevertheless stated in
[
11
, Theorem 2]
.

According to the Hilbert Uniqueness Method, proving null-controllability for the equation (

S
M

) is equivalent to proving the following observability estimate: for every initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{M})
,

(1.1)

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathcal{M})}\leq C_{a,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathcal{M}}a(x)\big|(e^{it\Delta}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

From now on, we will no longer refer to the null-controllability property itself, but only to the correspoding observability estimate. This estimate, which is the main result of this paper, is given in the following theorem, where
(e^{it\Delta})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
refers to the Schrödinger flow on
\mathcal{T}
with Dirichlet or Neumann boundary condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
T>0
be a positive time and
a\in L^{2}(\var)\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative function. There exists a positive constant
C_{a,T}>0
such that for every initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
,

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\var)}\leq C_{a,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\var}a(x)\big|(e^{it\Delta}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

Remark 1.3
.

While proving Theorem
1.2
, we also establish Strichartz estimates on the triangle
\var
, of independent interest: for every solution
u
of the equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\Delta u(t,x)=F(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\var,\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\var).\end{cases}

the following estimate holds

(1.2)

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\var,L^{2}(0,\frac{27}{8\pi}))}\leq\bigg(\frac{81\sqrt{3}}{32\pi^{2}}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\var)}+\|F\|_{L^{1}((0,\frac{27}{8\pi}),L^{2}(\var))}\big).

In the homogeneous case where
F=0
, this estimate ensures the finitness of the right-hand side of the observability estimate stated in Theorem
1.2
, and forces to consider rough localization functions
a\in L^{2}(\var)
.
Moreover, the inhomogeneous estimate is key to implement the Hilbert Uniqueness Method for which
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
holds
mutatis mutandis
provided
a\in L^{\infty}(\var)
and
h\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathcal{T})
. The reason we cannot implement the Hilbert Uniqueness Method when
a\in L^{2}(\var)
(the localization functions considered in Theorem
1.2
) is that we do not have at hand the required
L^{4/3}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
-inhomogeneous Strichartz used in
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
.

1.2.
Strategy of the proof

The works
[
8
,
10
]
provide a clear strategy for proving observability estimates of the form (
1.1
) with rough localization functions on tori, and it would be natural to attempt to extend this approach to the triangle
\mathcal{T}
. However,
since this strategy relies on semiclassical propagation of singularities, its implementation in our context is
a priori
complicated to perform, due both to the presence of corners and to the more intricate nature of the geodesic flow on
\mathcal{T}
.
Yet, we will be able to use a tiling argument due to Pinsky
[
25
]
to
derive observability estimates on the triangle
\mathcal{T}
from observability estimates on an explicit rational twisted torus, taking the form
\mathbb{R}^{2}/A\mathbb{Z}^{2}
with
A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
non symmetric.
The fundamental reason for this trick comes from a previous result by Lamé
[
18
]
, stating that the (Dirichlet and Neumann) eigenfunctions of the Laplacian
-\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(\var)
enjoy symmetry properties with respect to the sides of the triangle, see Figure
1
. Observability estimates on general rational twisted tori of the form
\mathbb{R}^{2}/A\mathbb{Z}^{2}
are in turn obtained by adapting the arguments from
[
10
]
.
However, the authors of

[
10
]
consider only rectangular tori, and neither their results nor their proofs apply directly to our situation. It is therefore necessary to supplement their arguments to address the case of twisted tori.
Note that this new argument is valid only for rational twisted tori (which is sufficient to treat the case of the equilateral triangle we are interested in), and the case of the irrational twisted tori remains open. In addition, and of independent interest, we generalize Zygmund inequality, which is required to set up the propagation argument, to twisted tori, taking this opportunity to provide the sharp constant in the rectangular case.

Let us illustrate the tiling argument used to go from the triangle
\mathcal{T}
to a twisted torus on the (easy) case of the square with Neumann boundary conditions.

Figure 1.
From the triangle
\mathcal{T}
to the twisted torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/A\mathbb{Z}^{2}
by successive reflections.

Example 1.4
.

Let us consider the square
\Omega=[0,\pi]\times[0,\pi]
. We recall that the Neumann eigenvalues of the Laplacian
-\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(\Omega)
are given by
\lambda_{m}=-|m|^{2}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, with the following associated eigenfunctions

f_{m}(x,y)=\frac{2}{\pi}\cos(m_{1}x)\cos(m_{1}y),\quad m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\,(x,y)\in\Omega.

By extending the eigenfunctions
f_{m}
by reflections with respect to the left and the upper sides of the square
\Omega
, one can see that proving an observability estimate for the Schrödinger equation on
\Omega
is equivalent to proving an observability estimate on the torus
\mathbb{R}^{2}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{2}
, the latter being available in
[
10
]
. Naturally, a similar discussion can be held for the Dirichlet eigenfunctions of
-\Delta
, with antireflections instead of reflections, since they are given by products of sines. The very same strategy will be used for the triangle
\mathcal{T}
, with more sophisticated arguments.

1.3.
Comments and generalizations

Some remarks regarding possible generalizations of Theorem
1.2
are in order.

A natural question is to wonder to which class of polygonal domains
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
our method applies. If
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is such a domain, replicating the tiling argument from Pinsky
[
25
]
shows that it possesses a complete set of trigonometric eigenfunctions (
i.e.
, given by finite trigonometric sums). A result of McCartin
[
22
]
shows that the only such polygonal domains are the rectangle (see Example
1.4
), the isosceles right triangle, the equilateral triangle and the hemiequilateral triangle. A slight modification of our arguments
gives the analog to Theorem
1.2
for these domains, while the problem remains open for any other polygonal domain.

Many results in the literature deal not only with the observability properties of the free Schrödinger equation,
i.e.
of the equation (

S
M

), but also of the same equation perturbed by a bounded zero-order potential, see e.g.
[
2
,
3
,
6
,
8
,
20
,
21
]
. Among these works, only
[
20
]
considers both rough localization functions and such perturbations, while the others restrict to
indicator functions of open sets.
It would
be very interesting to
extend our result to such perturbations,
and to prove observability estimates of the form (
1.1
) for the operator
\Delta+V
, where
V\in L^{\infty}(\var)
, on the equilateral triangle
\var
. One of the supplementary ingredients used in
[
10
]
to deal with
V
is a normal form argument performed in the reduction dimension. Providing such an argument in the context of twisted tori (which would permit to handle the case of the triangle
\mathcal{T}
) remains open.

Finally, in Theorem
1.2
, we consider localization functions
a\in L^{2}(\var)
not depending on time, and a natural extension would be to deal with the case where
a=a(t,x)
. This setting includes the particularly interesting example
a(t,x)=\mathbbm{1}_{\omega(t)}
, where
\omega(t)\subset\var
are moving measurable control supports
of positive measure. There are actually very few results in the literature dealing with this situation. Let us mention the recent paper
[
23
]
where the authors prove observability estimates for the Schrödinger equation on the circle
\mathbb{T}
with time-dependent rough localization functions.

Structure of the paper

Section
2
is devoted to present in details the tiling argument that allows us to derive observability estimates on the triangle
\mathcal{T}
from observability estimates on a rational twisted torus. In Section
3
, we establish an extension of Zygmund inequality to general twisted tori, that moreover provides the sharp constant in the square case. This result plays a key role while establishing observability estimates on general rational twisted tori. Such observability estimates
are finally obtained in Section
4
.

2.
Estimates on equilateral triangles through tiling arguments

In this section, we explain how tiling arguments allow to deduce estimates for the Schrödinger equation on the equilateral triangle
\mathcal{T}
from estimates on a rational twisted torus.

2.1.
Spectral decomposition

First, we need to recall some spectral properties of the Dirichlet and the Neumann Laplacian on the equilateral triangle. It is known from the works of Lamé
[
18
]
and Pinsky
[
25
,
26
]
that the eigenvalues of
-\Delta
on
\mathcal{T}
are given by

(2.1)

\lambda_{m,n}=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}\big(m^{2}+n^{2}-mn\big),

with
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
where
3\,|\,m+n
in the case of Neumann boundary conditions, and
3\,|\,m+n
,
m\neq 2n
,
n\neq 2m
in the case of Dirichlet boundary conditions.
The above works also give a complete description of the associated eigenfunctions, that we introduce now. To that end, let

k_{m,n}:=\frac{2\pi}{3}\bigg(m,\frac{2n-m}{\sqrt{3}}\bigg),\quad e_{m,n}(x):=\exp({ik_{m,n}\cdot x}),\quad x\in\mathcal{T},

and let
S_{m,n}
be the multiset (
i.e.
, a same element can appear several times)

S_{m,n}=\big\{(m,n),(m,m-n),(-n,m-n),(-n,-m),(n-m,-m),(n-m,n)\big\},

with
|S_{m,n}|=6
.
The
L^{2}
-normalized Neumann eigenfunctions for the eigenvalue
\lambda_{m,n}
are given by

u_{m,n}=\kappa^{N}_{m,n}\sum_{(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in S_{m,n}}e_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}},

where
\kappa^{N}_{m,n}>0
is a renormalization factor. Observe in particular that, if
S_{m_{1},n_{1}}=S_{m_{2},n_{2}}
, then
u_{m_{1},n_{1}}=u_{m_{2},n_{2}}
.
Dirichlet eigenfunctions involve similar, but alternating, sums. In order to define them precisely,
introduce

S_{1}:(m,n)\to(m,m-n),\quad S_{2}:(m,n)\to(n-m,n),

and observe that
S_{m,n}
is the orbit of
k_{m,n}
under the action of the transformations
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
:

S_{m,n}=\big\{hk_{m,n}:h\in\langle S_{1},S_{2}\rangle\big\},

where we denote
\langle S_{1},S_{2}\rangle
the group generated by
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Let now
\varepsilon:\langle S_{1},S_{2}\rangle\to\{-1,1\}
be the unique group homomorphism so that

\varepsilon(\operatorname{I})=1,\quad\varepsilon(S_{1})=-1,\quad\varepsilon(S_{2})=-1,

and define

\varepsilon_{m,n}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime}):=\varepsilon(h)\;\text{ for }\;(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})=h(m,n)\in S_{m,n}.

The Dirichlet eigenfunctions are given by

v_{m,n}=\kappa^{D}_{m,n}\sum_{(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in S_{m,n}}\varepsilon_{m,n}(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})e_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}.

In other words, the sum is alternating according to the order in the definition of
S_{m,n}
above. We make choices of sign in the unsigned renormalization factor
\kappa_{m,n}^{D}
in such a way that if
S_{m_{1},n_{1}}=S_{m_{2},n_{2}}
, then
v_{m_{1},n_{1}}=v_{m_{2},n_{2}}
.
Pinsky showed in
[
25
]
that these systems are indeed complete in
L^{2}(\var)
.
Hence, every function
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
decomposes as

f=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\sim\\
3\,|\,m+n\end{subarray}}a_{m,n}(f)u_{m,n}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\sim\\
3\,|\,m+n\\
n\neq 2m,\,m\neq 2n\end{subarray}}b_{m,n}(f)v_{m,n},

where we defined

(m_{1},n_{1})\sim(m_{2},n_{2})\iff S_{m_{1},n_{1}}=S_{m_{2},n_{2}}.

2.2.
The orbits
S_{m,n}

We now aim to understand the orbits
S_{m,n}
geometrically,
i.e.
in terms of transformations in the plane.
Observe that the scalar product by
k_{m,n}
is nothing but

(2.2)

{k_{m,n}\cdot(ae+b\omega)=\frac{2\pi}{3}(ma+nb),}

where

e:=1\quad\text{and}\quad\omega:=\exp\bigg(\frac{i\pi}{3}\bigg).

The geometric description of
S_{m,n}
is given by:

Lemma 2.1
.

For every
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we have

k_{S_{1}(m,n)}=R_{1}k_{m,n}\quad\text{and}\quad k_{S_{2}(m,n)}=R_{2}k_{m,n},

where
R_{1}
is the reflexion by
\mathbb{R}e
and
R_{2}
is the reflexion by
\mathbb{R}\omega
.

Proof.

By checking directly in cartesian coordinates for
S_{1}
, we get

k_{S_{1}({m,n})}=\overline{k_{m,n}}=R_{1}k_{m,n}.

To deal with
S_{2}
, we introduce the dual basis
(e^{\prime},\omega^{\prime})
to
(e,\omega)

e^{\prime}=\bigg(1,-\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\bigg)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}e^{-i\pi/6},\quad\omega^{\prime}=\bigg(0,\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}\bigg)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}e^{i\pi/2},

verifying (where
\cdot
denotes the Euclidean scalar product in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
)

e\cdot e^{\prime}=1,\quad\omega^{\prime}\cdot\omega=1,\quad e\cdot\omega^{\prime}=0\quad\text{and}\quad e^{\prime}\cdot\omega=0.

Observe in particular that
k_{m,n}
is given by

k_{m,n}=\frac{2\pi}{3}(me^{\prime}+n\omega^{\prime}),

thus, we have

R_{2}k_{m,n}=\omega\overline{k_{m,n}}=\omega R_{1}k_{m,n}=\omega k_{m,m-n}=\frac{2\pi}{3}\omega(me^{\prime}+(m-n)\omega^{\prime}).

Moreover, by using that

\omega e^{\prime}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}e^{i\pi/6}=\bigg(1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\bigg),\hskip 8.5359pt\omega\omega^{\prime}=\frac{2}{\sqrt{3}}e^{i5\pi/6}=\bigg(-1,\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}\bigg),

we finally get

R_{1}k_{m,n}=k_{n-m,n}=k_{S_{2}({m,n})},

as expected.
∎

2.3.
From equilateral triangle to twisted torus

Let us explain how to reduce our problem to an observability estimate on the twisted torus

(2.3)

\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/3\mathbb{Z}[\omega],

where
\mathbb{Z}[\omega]\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is defined by

\mathbb{Z}[\omega]=\big\{a+\omega b:a,b\in\mathbb{Z}\big\}.

To that end, let
G
be the group generated by the reflexions
R_{1}
and
R_{2}
, that is,

G:=\langle R_{1},R_{2}\rangle.

Let us also denote by
{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
the hexagon, orbit of
T
under the action of
G

{\mathcal{H}}_{1}:=\bigcup_{g\in G}g\mathcal{T},

(this is thus the adjoint orbit to
S_{n,m}
), and let further

{\mathcal{H}}_{2}:={\mathcal{H}}_{1}+e+\omega\quad\text{and}\quad{\mathcal{H}}_{3}:={\mathcal{H}}_{1}+2e+2\omega.

Introducing now the Pinsky parallelogram

\mathcal{P}:=\big\{a+\omega b:0<a,b<3\big\},

which is a fundamental domain of the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
, observe that

\mathcal{P}={\mathcal{H}}_{1}\cup{\mathcal{H}}_{2}\cup{\mathcal{H}}_{3}\hskip 8.5359pt\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}/\penalty 3\mathbb{Z}[\omega],

as illustrated in Figure
2
.

Figure 2.
The parallelogram
\mathcal{P}
as
18
copies of
\mathcal{T}
and its reflections, and its decomposition in the three hexagons
\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{1}
,
\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{2}
,
\mathcal{\mathcal{H}}_{3}
. The triangles with the same number are the same in
\mathbb{R}^{2}/3\mathbb{Z}[\omega]
.

We begin with a lemma quantifying the periodicity of the functions
u_{m,n}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g\in G
,
p\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
so that
3\,|\,m+n
. We have for every
x\in\var

u_{m,n}(x)=u_{m,n}(gx+p(e+\omega)),\quad v_{m,n}(x)=\varepsilon(g)v_{m,n}(gx+p(e+\omega)).

Proof.

Recall that

u_{m,n}(x)=\kappa^{N}_{m,n}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G}\exp{(ig^{\prime}k_{m,n}\cdot x)},

hence, by a change of variable in the sum

\displaystyle u_{m,n}(gx)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{N}_{m,n}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G}\exp{(ig^{\prime}k_{m,n}\cdot gx)}=\kappa^{N}_{m,n}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G}\exp{(ik_{m,n}\cdot g^{\prime-1}gx)}

\displaystyle=\kappa^{N}_{m,n}\sum_{g^{\prime}\in G}\exp{(ik_{m,n}\cdot g^{\prime}x)}=u_{m,n}(x),

and similarly for
v_{n,m}
. The additional remark that the
e_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}(x)=\exp{(ik_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}\cdot x)}
are
(e+\omega)
–periodic for
3\,|\,m^{\prime}+n^{\prime}
(and hence for
(m^{\prime},n^{\prime})\in S_{m,n}
with
3\,|\,m+n
), see (
2.2
), gives the result.
∎

Let us define the following extension operators

E^{N}f:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\sim\\
3\,|\,m+n\end{subarray}}a_{m,n}(f)u_{m,n},\quad E^{D}f:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}/\sim\\
3\,|\,m+n\\
n\neq 2m,\,m\neq 2n\end{subarray}}b_{m,n}(f)v_{m,n},\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T}).

The operators
E^{N,D}
satisfy the following properties.

Proposition 2.3
.

For every
f\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, the functions
E^{N,D}f
are well-defined as functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega])
. Moreover, we have

E^{N,D}\Delta_{N,D}f=\Delta E^{N,D}f,

and in addition,

\forall g\in G,\;p\in\mathbb{Z},\;\text{a.e.}\;x\in\mathcal{T},\quad\begin{cases}(E^{N}f)(gx+p(e+\omega))=f(x),\\[5.0pt]
(E^{D}f)(gx+p(e+\omega))=\varepsilon(g)f(x).\end{cases}

Proof.

First, observe that
E^{N,D}f
are well-defined as functions on
\tori
, because the
e_{m^{\prime},n^{\prime}}
, and hence each
u_{m,n}
(resp
v_{m,n}
) are
3e
and
3\omega
periodic.
To get the first claim, it suffices to check that, for
3\,|\,m+n
,

\Delta u_{m,n}(x)=\lambda_{m,n}u_{m,n}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(and similarly for
v_{m,n}
) which follows from the fact that this is verified in
\mathcal{T}
, together with Lemma
2.2
and the fact that the Laplacian commutes with reflexions and translations.
The second claim follows directly from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

Recalling the decomposition

\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{H}_{1}\cup\mathcal{H}_{2}\cup\mathcal{H}_{3}=\bigcup_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\in G,\\
p=0,1,2\end{subarray}}g\mathcal{T}+p(e+\omega),

we directly deduce from Proposition
2.3
the following.

Corollary 2.4
.

For any
f\in L^{2}(\var)
and
p\geq 1

\|E^{N,D}f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\tori)}=18\|f\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathcal{T})},

and for any
b\in L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
such that
b\geq 0
,

\int_{\tori}|E^{N,D}b||E^{N,D}f|^{2}=18\int_{\mathcal{T}}b|f|^{2}.

As a consequence of Corollary
2.4
, proving the observability estimates from Theorem
1.2
on the equilateral triangle
\mathcal{T}
is equivalent to proving the following estimates on
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
for every positive time
T>0
,
a\in E^{N,D}(L^{2}(\var))
and
u_{0}\in E^{N,D}(L^{2}(\var))
,

(2.4)

\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\tori)}\leq C_{a,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\tori}a(x)\big|(e^{it\Delta}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

In Section
4
, we will even be able to prove these observability estimates for every rough control
a\in L^{2}(\tori)
and for every initial datum
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega])
. In fact, we will establish (
2.4
) on very general rational twisted tori (defined in the next section).

Naturally, this strategy is not specific to observability estimates, and can be applied for various kind of estimates, as the Strichartz estimate stated in Remark
1.3
(see Remark
4.4
), and for other equations involving the Laplace operator.

3.
Sharp Zygmund inequality

In the seminal work
[
28
]
, answering a question by Fefferman, Zygmund proved the following
L^{4}
inequality for functions
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
with Fourier transforms
\widehat{u}
supported in circles
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}\subset\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

(3.1)

\|u\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C\|u\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})},

where
\mathbb{T}^{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}/\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, and the circles
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
are defined for every
\lambda>0
by

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}:=\big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}:|m|^{2}=\lambda\big\}.

This kind of estimate, fundamental in harmonic analysis, also turns out to play a key role in controllability theory of dispersive equations, while proving observability estimates like (
2.4
). This will be detailed in Section
4
. Zygmund’s original argument yields the inequality (
3.1
) with
C=5

[
28
, Theorem 1]
. The aim of this section is
two-fold: we extend (
3.1
) to twisted tori (which generalize
\tori
and will be defined below), while giving another proof of (
3.1
) that yields the sharp constant
C=3
.
We now define general twisted tori. Given
A\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R})
, we associate to
A
the following torus

\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/(A\mathbb{Z}^{2}).

Recall that the spectrum of the Laplace operator
-\Delta
acting on functions in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
is composed of the following eigenvalues

(3.2)

\lambda_{m}=|2\pi m|^{2},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{A}:=(A^{-1})^{*}\mathbb{Z}^{2},

where
(A^{-1})^{*}
denotes the transpose of
A^{-1}
and that each eigenvalue
\lambda_{m}>0
is associated with the following normalized eigenfunction

e_{m}(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det A}}\,e^{2i\pi m\cdot x},\quad m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{A},\,x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}.

In this context, the natural energy levels are the sets
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)
defined for every
\lambda>0
by (we get rid of the factor
4\pi^{2}
, as for the circles
\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
)

\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A):=\big\{m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{A}:|m|^{2}=\lambda\big\}.

The generalization of Zygmund inequality (
3.1
) is stated in the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

For every energy level
\lambda>0
and for every function
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
such that
\supp\widehat{u}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)
, the following estimate holds

\|u\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}\leq\frac{3}{\det A}\|u\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}.

Moreover, the above constant
3
is sharp in the case where
A=I_{2}
, in the sense that there exists a sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n}
of positive integers and a sequence
(u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
such that

\supp\widehat{u_{n}}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}},\quad\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}3.

Let us mention that the sharpness of the constant
C=3
when
A=I_{2}
will not play a role in the other results in this paper, but we take this opportunity to
present its proof.

3.1.
The parallelogram lemma

Let
\lambda>0
and
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
be a function such that
\supp\widehat{u}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)
, and whose Fourier coefficients will be denoted
(a_{m})_{m}
in the following for simplicity. Let us develop the
L^{4}
norm we aim at estimating

(3.3)

\displaystyle\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}^{4}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\det A)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\Big|\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)}e^{2i\pi m\cdot x}a_{m}\Big|^{4}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\det A)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\bigg(\sum_{(m,\overline{m})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{2}}e^{2i\pi(m-\overline{m})\cdot x}a_{m}\overline{a_{\overline{m}}}\,\bigg)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\det A}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{4}\\[2.0pt]
m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0\end{subarray}}a_{m_{1}}\overline{a_{\overline{m}_{1}}}a_{m_{2}}\overline{a_{\overline{m}_{2}}}.

The above resonant sum can be precisely understood. This is the topic of the following result, which can be found
as
[
17
, Equation (72)]
, but which we will be proven again in the present paper (with quite different arguments) for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.2

(Parallelogram lemma)
.

Let
m_{1},\overline{m}_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be so that

|m_{1}|=|\overline{m}_{1}|=|m_{2}|=|\overline{m}_{2}|\quad\text{and}\quad m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0.

Then, we are in one of the following situations:

(i)

Parallelogram:
m_{1}=\overline{m}_{1}
and
m_{2}=\overline{m}_{2}
.

(ii)

First degenerate case:
m_{1}=-m_{2}
and
\overline{m}_{1}=-\overline{m}_{2}
.

(iii)

Second degenerate case:
m_{1}=\overline{m}_{2}
and
m_{2}=\overline{m}_{1}
.

Proof.

Consider the four points

\displaystyle P_{1}:=(0,0),

\displaystyle P_{2}:=P_{1}+m_{1},

\displaystyle P_{3}:=P_{2}+m_{2},

\displaystyle P_{4}:=P_{3}-\overline{m}_{1},

and observe that, because
m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0
,

P_{4}-\overline{m}_{2}=P_{1}.

\triangleright
Case 1: non degenerate case
. Assume that the points
P_{1},P_{2},P_{3},P_{4}
are pairwise distinct. Then, the quadrilateral
P_{1}P_{2}P_{3}P_{4}
has edges of lengths
(|m_{1}|,|m_{2}|,|\overline{m}_{1}|,|\overline{m}_{2}|)
. As
|m_{i}|=|\overline{m}_{i}|
, it is therefore either a parallelogram (if it is a simple,
i.e.
non self-intersecting), or an antiparallelogram (if it is complex,
i.e.
self-intersecting). However, an antiparallelogram with four edges of same size has necessarily two equal vertices,
i.e.
is degenerate. Indeed, let us consider e.g. an antiparallelogram as in Figure
4
, with the additional property that its four sides are equal in length. Since
|m_{1}|=|\overline{m}_{2}|
and
|m_{2}|=|\overline{m}_{1}|
, the triangles
P_{1}P_{4}P_{2}
and
P_{3}P_{4}P_{2}
are both isosceles, in
P_{1}
and
P_{3}
respectively. Since they share the side
P_{4}P_{2}
, this implies that the two points
P_{1}
and
P_{3}
are aligned with the middle of
P_{4}P_{2}
. Then, the fact that
|m_{1}|=|m_{2}|
implies that
P_{1}
=
P_{3}
, and the quadrilateral
P_{1}P_{2}P_{3}P_{4}
is therefore degenerate. As we assumed that
P_{1}
,
P_{2}
,
P_{3}
,
P_{4}
are pairwise distinct, it follows that the quadrilateral
P_{1}P_{2}P_{3}P_{4}
is necessarily a parallelogram and therefore
m_{1}=\overline{m}_{1}
and
m_{2}=\overline{m}_{2}
.

\triangleright
Case 2: degenerate case
. Assume that there is
i_{1}\neq i_{2}
so that
P_{i_{1}}=P_{i_{2}}
. There are six cases:

1.

If
P_{1}=P_{2}
, then
m_{1}=0
, from which
m_{2}=\overline{m}_{1}=\overline{m}_{2}=0
.

2.

If
P_{1}=P_{3}
, then
m_{1}+m_{2}=0
, from which

\overline{m}_{1}+\overline{m}_{2}=0
as well.

3.

If
P_{1}=P_{4}
, then
\overline{m}_{2}=0
, from which
m_{1}=m_{2}=\overline{m}_{1}=0
.

4.

If
P_{2}=P_{3}
, then
m_{2}=0
, from which
m_{1}=\overline{m}_{1}=\overline{m}_{2}=0
.

5.

If
P_{2}=P_{4}
, then
m_{2}-\overline{m}_{1}=0
and
m_{1}-\overline{m}_{2}=0
.

6.

If
P_{3}=P_{4}
, then
\overline{m}_{1}=0
, from which
m_{1}=m_{2}=\overline{m_{2}}=0
.

All the cases above but the second and the fifth enter the case of the parallelogram for which
m_{1}=\overline{m}_{1}
and
m_{2}=\overline{m}_{2}
.
∎

3.2.
The estimate

Let us now prove the first part of Theorem
3.1
as a consequence of the parallelogram lemma. Recalling the equality (
3.3
), we first get from the triangle inequality and Lemma
3.2
that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{4}\\[2.0pt]
m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0\end{subarray}}|a_{m_{1}}\overline{a_{\overline{m}_{1}}}a_{m_{2}}\overline{a_{\overline{m}_{2}}}|\leq\underbrace{\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{2}}|a_{m_{1}}\overline{a_{m_{1}}}a_{m_{2}}\overline{a_{m_{2}}}|}_{=:\,S_{1}}\\[5.0pt]
+\underbrace{\sum_{(m_{1},\overline{m}_{1})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{2}}|a_{m_{1}}\overline{a_{\overline{m}_{1}}}a_{-m_{1}}\overline{a_{-\overline{m}_{1}}}|}_{=:\,S_{2}}+\underbrace{\sum_{(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)^{2}}|a_{m_{1}}\overline{a_{m_{2}}}a_{m_{2}}\overline{a_{m_{1}}}|}_{=:\,S_{3}}.

The first and last sums
S_{1}
and
S_{3}
are nothing but
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}^{4}
, and the second sum is controlled by

S_{2}=\bigg(\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)}|a_{m}a_{-m}|\bigg)^{2}\leq\bigg(\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)}\frac{1}{2}\big(|a_{m}|^{2}+|a_{-m}|^{2}\big)\bigg)^{2}=\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}^{4}.

Dividing by
\det A
, we finally obtain

(3.4)

\|u\|^{4}_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}\leq\frac{3}{\det A}\|u\|^{4}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})},

as expected.

3.3.
Optimality

We now show that the constant
3
is optimal in the estimate (
3.4
) when
A=I_{2}
. To that end, let us consider the sequence of functions
(u_{n})_{n}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
defined for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
by

u_{n}(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}}e^{2i\pi m\cdot x},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{2},

where the
\lambda_{n}>0
are positive integers that will be chosen later. Notice that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=1
. Moreover, it follows from (
3.3
) that

\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in(\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}\\[2.0pt]
m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0\end{subarray}}1=\frac{|X_{n}|}{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|^{2}},

where the set
X_{n}
is defined by

X_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in(\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}:m_{1}-\overline{m}_{1}+m_{2}-\overline{m}_{2}=0\big\}.

Let us introduce three new sets
X^{1}_{n}
,
X^{2}_{n}
and
X^{3}_{n}
, corresponding to the three cases in Lemma
3.2
, respectively given by

\displaystyle X^{1}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in(\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}:m_{1}=\overline{m}_{1},\quad m_{2}=\overline{m}_{2}\big\},

\displaystyle X^{2}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in(\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}:m_{1}=-m_{2},\quad\overline{m}_{1}=-\overline{m}_{2}\big\},

\displaystyle X^{3}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},m_{2},\overline{m}_{1},\overline{m}_{2})\in(\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}:m_{1}=\overline{m}_{2},\quad m_{2}=\overline{m}_{1}\big\}.

Observe that we have the following overlapping decomposition

X_{n}=X^{1}_{n}\cup X^{2}_{n}\cup X^{3}_{n}.

Indeed, the direct inclusion follows from Lemma
3.2
, while the converse one is immediate. The inclusion-exclusion principle then allows to write that

\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=\frac{|X^{1}_{n}|+|X^{2}_{n}|+|X^{3}_{n}|-|X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{2}_{n}|-|X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}|-|X^{2}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}|+|X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{2}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}|}{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|^{2}}.

We now have to estimate the above cardinalities. First,
it follows from
the definition of the sets
X^{i}_{n}
that

|X^{i}_{n}|=|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|^{2},\quad i\in\{1,2,3\}.

Since the two-intersections are explicitly given by

\displaystyle X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{2}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},-m_{1},m_{1},-m_{1}):m_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}\big\},

\displaystyle X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},m_{1},m_{1},m_{1}):m_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}\big\},

\displaystyle X^{2}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}=\big\{(m_{1},-m_{1},-m_{1},m_{1}):m_{1}\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}\big\},

we also have

|X^{i}_{n}\cap X^{j}_{n}|=|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|,\quad i\neq j\in\{1,2,3\}.

Finally,

X^{1}_{n}\cap X^{2}_{n}\cap X^{3}_{n}=\big\{(0,0,0,0)\big\}\cap(S_{\lambda_{n}})^{4}=\emptyset.

As a consequence, the
L^{4}
norm of the functions
u_{n}
is given by

(3.5)

\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=3-\frac{3}{|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|}.

Moreover, a classical result in number theory, which can be found e.g. in
[
13
, Section 16.10]
, states that for every positive integer
N\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
admitting the following prime number decomposition

N=2^{a}\prod p_{i}^{b_{i}}\prod q_{j}^{c_{j}},

where
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{i},c_{j}\in\mathbb{N}
being almost all equal to
0
,
p_{i}\equiv 1\,\text{(mod $4$)}
and
q_{j}\equiv 3\,\text{(mod $4$)}
being prime numbers, then

|\mathcal{S}_{N}|=\begin{cases}4\prod(b_{i}+1)&\text{when all the $c_{j}$ are even,}\\[5.0pt]
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

This result motivates to make the choice
\lambda_{n}=5^{n}
, for which

|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda_{n}}|=4(n+1)\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}+\infty.

Plugging this expression into (
3.5
), we obtain

\|u_{n}\|^{4}_{L^{4}}=3-\frac{3}{4(n+1)}\underset{n\rightarrow+\infty}{\longrightarrow}3.

Recalling that
\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=1
, this proves that the constant
3
is optimal in Zygmund inequality (
3.4
).

Remark 3.3
.

Notice that Cauchy-Schwarz inequality immediately implies that for every energy level
\lambda>0
and for every function
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
such that
\supp\widehat{u}\subset\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}
,

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}|^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

By interpolating this estimate and Zygmund inequality from Theorem
3.1
, we therefore deduce that for every
p>4
,

\|u\|^{p}_{L^{p}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq 3|\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}|^{\frac{p-4}{2}}\|u\|^{p}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}.

However, this inequality is not as precise as results known in the literature in the same topic, among others in the case
p=6
in
[
7
,
17
]
, obtained by more sophisticated approaches and technology.

4.
Observability estimates on twisted rational tori

This last section is devoted to the proof of observability estimates for Schrödinger equations posed on the twisted rational tori
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
defined in Section
3
. By rational tori, we mean that we make the following assumption on the eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}>0
of the Laplacian
-\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
, given by (
3.2
),

(4.1)

\exists\gamma>0,\forall m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}_{A},\quad\lambda_{m}\in\gamma\mathbb{N}.

For example, when
A=I_{2}
is the identity matrix, we have
\gamma=4\pi^{2}
. Another interesting example in regards of the triangle
\mathcal{T}
is the following one.

Example 4.1
.

The torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
defined in (
2.3
) is associated with the following matrix

A=3\begin{pmatrix}1&1/2\\[5.0pt]
0&\sqrt{3}/{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\GL_{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Since the eigenvalues of the Laplacian
-\Delta
on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega])
are given from (
3.2
) by

\lambda_{m}=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27}(m_{1}^{2}-m_{1}m_{2}+m_{2}^{2}),\quad m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},

the associated quantity
\gamma
as in (
4.1
) is given by
\gamma=16\pi^{2}/27
. Notice that these eigenvalues include the eigenvalues (
2.1
) of the Laplacian
-\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(\mathcal{T})
, which is not surprising due to the correspondance between the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
and the equilateral triangle
\mathcal{T}
presented in Section
2.3
.

In this setting, the goal of this section is to prove the following observability estimates.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
T>0
be a positive time and
a\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})\setminus\{0\}
be a non-negative function. There exists a positive constant
C_{a,T}>0
such that for every
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
,

\|u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}\leq C_{a,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}a(x)\big|(e^{it\Delta}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t.

4.1.
Strichartz estimates on twisted rational tori

As an almost direct consequence of Zygmund inequality stated in Theorem
3.1
, we get

L^{4}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
Strichartz estimates without loss of derivatives on twisted rational tori, generalizing the now well-known rectangular case.

Proposition 4.3
.

For every solution
u
of the equation

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(t,x)+\Delta u(t,x)=F(t,x),\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A},\\
u(0,\cdot)=u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}),\end{cases}

the following estimate holds

\|u\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A},L^{2}(0,\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}))}\leq\bigg(\frac{12\pi^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\det A}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\big(\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}+\|F\|_{L^{1}((0,\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}),L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}))}\big).

Proof.

Using Duhamel formula, it is straightforward to deduce Proposition
4.3
from the corresponding homogeneous estimate: for any
u_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
,

\big\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\big\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A},L^{2}(0,\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}))}\leq\bigg(\frac{12\pi^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\det A}\bigg)^{\frac{1}{4}}\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}.

We will therefore show the above.
Developing the
L^{4}_{x}L^{2}_{t}
norm we aim at estimating, we get

\big\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\big\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}L^{2}_{t}}=\bigg(\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}\bigg)^{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\bigg(\sum_{\lambda>0}|(\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0})(x)|^{2}\bigg)^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where
\Pi_{\lambda}
is the projection on Fourier coefficients of size
\lambda
:

(\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0})(x):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\det A}}\sum_{m\in\mathcal{S}_{\lambda}(A)}a_{m}e^{2i\pi m\cdot x},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}.

Then, by applying Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Zygmund inequality from Theorem
3.1
, we get that

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta}u_{0}\big\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}L^{2}_{t}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}\bigg)^{2}\sum_{\lambda,\mu>0}\|\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}\|\Pi_{\mu}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{4}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\frac{2\pi}{\gamma}\bigg)^{2}\frac{3}{\det A}\sum_{\lambda,\mu>0}\|\Pi_{\lambda}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\|\Pi_{\mu}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{12\pi^{2}}{\gamma^{2}\det A}\|u_{0}\|^{4}_{L^{2}},

as expected.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

Considering the particular case of the matrix
A
in Example
4.1
associated with the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
, for which we recall that

\det A=\frac{9\sqrt{3}}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\gamma=\frac{16\pi^{2}}{27},

and invoking the correspondence between
\mathbb{T}^{2}[\omega]
and the equilateral triangle
\mathcal{T}
presented in Section
2.3
, we deduce the Strichartz estimates (
1.2
) from Proposition
4.3
.

4.2.
Observability

In the setting of rectangular tori (rational or not),
i.e.
in the situation where
A
is symmetric, it is now well-known how to derive the observability estimate in Theorem
4.2
with the homogeneous Strichartz estimates from Proposition
4.3
at hand, see e.g.
[
10
,
20
]
. In this part, we will quickly recall the arguments already appearing in the rectangular case, and present in details what changes in the setting of twisted rational tori. In particular, the rationality assumption (
4.1
) arises in the dimension reduction, which was not the case for rectangular tori.

Thanks to a Littlewood–Paley decomposition and the Bardos–Lebeau–Rauch uniqueness-compactness argument originated from
[
5
]
(a quantitative version of which can be found in
[
8
, Theorem 4]
), it is sufficient to prove the following semiclassical observability estimate for every
0<h<h_{0}
,

(4.2)

\|\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})}\leq C_{a,T}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}a(x)\big|(e^{it\Delta}\Pi_{h,\rho}u_{0})(x)\big|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t,

the frequency localizing operators
\Pi_{h,\rho}
being given by

\Pi_{h,\rho}=\chi\bigg(\frac{-h^{2}\Delta-1}{\rho}\bigg),

where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}((-1,1))
is equal to
1
near zero. Mutatis-mutandis, one can follow the strategy of
[
10
]
. The estimate (
4.2
) is proven by contradiction: assuming that the estimate fails along a subsequence
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
, for
h_{n},\rho_{n}\to 0
, one can extract a defect measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}_{t}\times(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})_{z,\zeta}
from
(u_{n})_{n\geq 1}
. Recall that the measure
\mu
satisfies the following three properties

(4.3)

\displaystyle\mu((t_{0},t_{1})\times T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})=t_{1}-t_{0},

(4.4)

\displaystyle\Supp\mu\subset\big\{(t,z,\zeta)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}\times\mathbb{R}^{2}:|\zeta|=1\big\},

(4.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\varphi(t)\Gamma(z+s\zeta,\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu=0,\quad\varphi\in C^{0}_{c}(\mathbb{R}_{t}),\,\Gamma\in C^{\infty}((T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})_{z,\zeta}).

Additionally, thanks to the Strichartz estimates from Proposition
4.3
with
F=0
, for any
\tau\geq 0
there exists a function
m_{\tau}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
such that for all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
,

\int_{0}^{\tau}\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}f(z)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(t,z,\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}m_{\tau}(z)f(z)\,{\rm d}z.

From the setup of the contradiction argument and the definition of defect measures,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}a(z)m_{T}(z)\,{\rm d}z=0.

We will show that
m_{T}
is identically zero, which is in contradiction with (
4.3
). The idea is that
\mu
has most of its mass on rational directions. But the flow from such directions is periodic, which permits to perform a reduction to a one-dimensional torus, for which the observability properties are well known. Let us mention that in our geometric context,
i.e.
in the twisted torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
, one has to change the definition of rational directions compared to was is done in
[
10
]
. Precisely, we consider

W^{n}=\bigg\{(z,\zeta)\in T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}:\zeta=\frac{Am}{|Am|},\,m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2},\,\max(|m_{1}|,|m_{2}|)\leq n,\,\gcd(m_{1},m_{2})=1\bigg\}.

By using the same arguments as in
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
,
which rely on the unique ergodicity of the flow
z\mapsto z+s\zeta
, we get that

\forall\varepsilon>0,\;\exists n\geq 0,\quad\widetilde{\mu}_{T}(W^{n})<\varepsilon,

where
\widetilde{\mu}_{T}
is the defect measure
\mu
integrated in time on
[0,T]
,
i.e.

\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\Gamma(z,\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{T}(z,\zeta)=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}}\Gamma(z,\zeta)\,\mathrm{d}\mu(t,z,\zeta),\quad\Gamma\in C^{\infty}_{c}(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}).

Let us fix
n_{0}\geq 0
large enough such that

\widetilde{\mu}_{T}(T^{*}\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}\setminus W^{n_{0}})<T,

and also

\zeta_{0}\in\big\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\exists z\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A},\,(z,\zeta)\in\supp(\widetilde{\mu_{T}}|_{W^{n_{0}}})\big\}.

Mimicking
[
10
, Lemma 2.3]
, we have for some
F\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})\setminus\{0\}
such that
F\geq 0
,

(4.6)

\widetilde{\mu_{T}}\big|_{\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}\times\{\zeta_{0}\}}=F\otimes\delta_{\zeta=\zeta_{0}}.

We now set

e_{1}:={A\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}}\quad\text{and}\quad e_{2}:={A\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}},

and show that up to changing the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
, we can actually assume that
\zeta_{0}=\frac{e_{2}}{|e_{2}|}
. Indeed, it suffices to consider the following rotation, where the vectors
\zeta_{0}
and
e_{2}
are considered as elements of
\mathbb{C}^{*}

\phi(z):=\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z,\quad z\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A},

to work with the new functions
\widetilde{u}_{n}:=u_{n}\circ\phi
, and to change the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
to

\mathbb{T}^{2}_{rA}:=\mathbb{R}^{2}/(rA\mathbb{Z}^{2}),

where
r:=q_{0}|e_{2}|^{-1}|Am|
with
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
chosen later, and
m\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
given by

\zeta_{0}=\frac{Am}{|Am|}\in W^{m_{0}}.

Let us show that for a well chosen
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the functions
\widetilde{u}_{n}
are well-defined on this new torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{rA}
. To that end, let us consider a point
z\in\mathbb{T}^{2}_{rA}
and an integer
p\in\mathbb{Z}
. On the one hand, the periodicity in the direction
re_{2}
is immediate: indeed, since
u_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
and
pq_{0}Am\in A\mathbb{Z}^{2}
,

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{n}(z+pre_{2})

\displaystyle=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}pre_{2})=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+pq_{0}Am)

\displaystyle=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z)=\widetilde{u}_{n}(z).

On the other hand, the periodicity in the direction
re_{1}
will be a consequence of the rationality assumption (
4.1
) for a good choice of
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. Indeed

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}_{n}(z+pre_{1})

\displaystyle=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}pre_{1})=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+pq_{0}Ame_{2}^{-1}e_{1})

\displaystyle=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+pq_{0}m_{1}e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}+pq_{0}m_{2}e_{1})=u_{n}(\zeta_{0}|e_{2}|e_{2}^{-1}z+pq_{0}m_{1}e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}),

where we denoted
m=(m_{1},m_{2})
and used the periodicity of
u_{n}
in the direction
e_{1}
in the second line. To conclude, we will show that we can choose
q_{0}\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
so that

(4.7)

q_{0}e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}\in\mathbb{Z}e_{1}+\mathbb{Z}e_{2},

which gives the periodicity of
\widetilde{u}_{n}
in the direction
re_{1}
thanks to the above and the fact that
u_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}_{A})
.
To this effect, denote

A^{-1}:=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}.

Working with the complex representation, a direct computation shows

e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}=\frac{1}{(a^{2}+b^{2})(ad-bc)}\begin{pmatrix}-d^{2}b+c^{2}b-2adc\\
-d^{2}a+c^{2}a+2bcd\end{pmatrix}.

We now use the change of base formula

\alpha\begin{pmatrix}1\\
0\end{pmatrix}+\beta\begin{pmatrix}0\\
1\end{pmatrix}=Xe_{1}+Ye_{2}\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \begin{pmatrix}X\\
Y\end{pmatrix}=A^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha\\
\beta\end{pmatrix},

to get

e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}=-\frac{1}{a^{2}+b^{2}}\big(2(ac+bd)e_{1}+(c^{2}+d^{2})e_{2}\big).

We recognize the coefficients of
A^{-1}(A^{-1})^{*}
:

A^{-1}(A^{-1})^{*}=\begin{pmatrix}a^{2}+b^{2}&ac+bd\\
ac+bd&c^{2}+d^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

To relate these coefficients to the rationality assumption (
4.1
),
it will be useful to relabel the eigenvalues
\lambda_{m}
of the Laplace operator
-\Delta
acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A})
, by stating that they are given by

\lambda_{n_{1},n_{2}}=\left|2\pi(A^{-1})^{*}\begin{pmatrix}n_{1}\\
n_{2}\end{pmatrix}\right|^{2},\quad\begin{pmatrix}n_{1}\\
n_{2}\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}.

With this new terminology,

(4.8)

A^{-1}(A^{-1})^{*}=\frac{1}{16\pi^{4}}\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{1,0}&\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1,1}-\lambda_{1,0}-\lambda_{0,1})\\
\frac{1}{2}(\lambda_{1,1}-\lambda_{1,0}-\lambda_{0,1})&\lambda_{0,1}\end{pmatrix}.

It follows that, under the rationality assumption (
4.1
),

e_{2}^{-1}e_{1}^{2}=\frac{p_{1}}{q_{1}}e_{1}+\frac{p_{2}}{q_{2}}e_{2},\quad(p_{1},q_{1}),(p_{2},q_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}.

Taking
q_{0}:=\operatorname{lcm}(q_{1},q_{2})
, we obtain (
4.7
), and hence
\widetilde{u}_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2}_{rA})
.
Observe, crucially, that this modification of torus does not change the rationality assumption (
4.1
), which is still valid on
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{rA}
; and that
\widetilde{u}_{n}
is still solution of the Schrödinger equation because
the Laplacian
\Delta
commutes with rotations.
We now go back to the notations
u_{n}
and
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
and assume that
\zeta_{0}=\frac{e_{2}}{|e_{2}|}
.

We are finally in position to proceed to the dimension reduction. By invariance of the defect measure
\mu
with respect to the geodesic flow (property (
4.5
)) and the decomposition (
4.6
), we have

\widetilde{\mu_{T}}\big|_{{\mathbb{T}}^{2}_{A}\times\{\frac{e_{2}}{|e_{2}|}\}}=g(x)\,\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\otimes\delta_{0}(\xi)\otimes\delta_{1}(\eta),

where we work with the coordinates
(x,y)
relative to the basis
(e_{1},e_{2})
.
To perform explicitly the dimension reduction, we write the Laplacian in this basis. Precisely, let us make the following change of function

v_{n}(t,x,y):=u_{n}\left(t,A\begin{pmatrix}x\\
y\end{pmatrix}\right),\quad(t,x,y)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}.

A straightforward computation shows that the functions
v_{n}
are solutions of the following twisted Schrödinger equation

i\partial_{t}v_{n}+\divv(A^{-1}(A^{*})^{-1}\nabla v_{n})=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}^{2}$.}

Let
\psi_{A}
be the quadratic form associated with
A^{-1}(A^{*})^{-1}
:

\psi_{A}(\Xi):=A^{-1}(A^{*})^{-1}\Xi^{*}\cdot\Xi^{*},\quad\Xi\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Denoting by
v^{k}_{n}(t,\cdot)
the partial Fourier coefficients of the functions
v_{n}(t,\cdot)
with respect to the variable
y\in\mathbb{T}
, with
k\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
, we deduce by taking the Fourier transform in the above equation that the functions
v^{k}_{n}
satisfy

(4.9)

i\partial_{t}v^{k}_{n}+\psi_{A}(\partial_{x},-ik)v^{k}_{n}=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}$.}

We will now show that, under the rationality assumption (
4.1
),
v^{k}_{n}
is in fact solution to a straight one-dimensional Schrödinger equation up to conjugation by an oscillating factor.
To this end, let us perform another change of function and consider

(4.10)

w^{k}_{n}(t,x):=v^{k}_{n}(t,x)e^{i\alpha kx+i\beta k^{2}t},\quad(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/\mu\mathbb{Z}),

where we aim at choosing the constants
\alpha,\beta,\Lambda\in\mathbb{R}
and
\mu\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
so that
w^{k}_{n}
satisfies the following free Schrödinger equation

(4.11)

i\partial_{t}w^{k}_{n}+\Lambda\partial^{2}_{x}w^{k}_{n}=0\quad\text{in $\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/\mu\mathbb{Z})$}.

Observe that, from (
4.8
), the quadratic form
\psi_{A}
is given by

\psi_{A}(\xi,\eta)=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\big(\lambda_{1,0}\xi^{2}+(\lambda_{1,1}-\lambda_{0,1}-\lambda_{1,0})\xi\eta+\lambda_{0,1}\eta^{2}\big),\quad(\xi,\eta)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Using this expression for
\psi_{A}
, injecting (
4.10
) into (
4.9
) yields to, in order for
w_{n}^{k}
to be solution to (
4.11
)

\displaystyle\Lambda=\frac{\lambda_{1,0}}{4\pi^{2}},\quad\alpha=\frac{\lambda_{0,1}+\lambda_{1,0}-\lambda_{1,1}}{2\lambda_{1,0}}\quad\text{and}\quad\beta=\frac{\lambda_{0,1}}{4\pi^{2}}-\frac{(\lambda_{0,1}+\lambda_{1,0}-\lambda_{1,1})^{2}}{16\pi^{2}\lambda_{1,0}}.

At that point, it is fundamental to notice that
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
as a consequence of the rationality assumption (
4.1
) on the twisted torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}_{A}
. Therefore,
\alpha=p/q
with
(p,q)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}^{*}
, and we take
\mu:=q
. Having fixed such
\Lambda
,
\alpha
,
\beta
,
\mu
,
w_{n}^{k}
is
\mu=q
–periodic and hence well defined on
\mathbb{R}\times(\mathbb{R}/\mu\mathbb{Z})
, and verifies (
4.11
).
Before going on, let us comment that in the rectangular case,
i.e.
when
A
is symmetric, up to a rotation and a change of function, the corresponding quadratic form has no cross-term and so
\alpha=0
, and no rationality assumption was needed at this point.
Coming back to
v_{n}^{k}
, we get

v_{n}^{k}(t,x)=e^{-i\alpha kx}e^{i\Lambda t\partial_{x}^{2}}\big(e^{i\alpha kx}v_{n}^{k}(0)\big)e^{-i\beta k^{2}t},

where
(e^{i\Lambda t\partial_{x}^{2}})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
denotes the Schrödinger flow on
\mathbb{R}/\mu\mathbb{Z}
. We can now write

(e^{it\Delta}v_{n})(x,y)=\sum_{k\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}}e^{-i\alpha kx}e^{i\Lambda t\partial_{x}^{2}}\big(e^{i\alpha kx}v_{n}^{k}(0)\big)e^{-i\beta k^{2}t}e^{iky}.

Using the the one-dimensional observability result
[
10
, Lemma 2.4]
, combined with an approximation argument involving
the homogeneous estimate from Proposition
4.3
, see e.g.
[
10
, pp. 337 - 338]
, then permits to get a contradiction and to conclude the proof of Theorem
4.2
.

4.3.
Conclusion

As observed in the end of Section
2
, Theorem
4.2
combined with the correspondence between the equilateral triangle
\mathcal{T}
and the twisted torus
\tori
stated as Corollary
2.4
, give Theorem
1.2
.

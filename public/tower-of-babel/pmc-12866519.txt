Balancing quality collaborative supervision and tripartite satisfaction in elderly healthcare public-private partnerships: an evolutionary game and simulation analysis
Authors: Xianghua Yue, Shahzad Khan Durrani, Runfa Li
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractChina’s rapidly growing elderly population, shifting social patterns, and economic developments have significantly impacted traditional family-based caregiving, leading to the widespread adoption of Public-Private Partnership (PPP) models for institutional care. Effective elderly care PPPs require quality collaborative supervision among the government, the private sector, and the public to ensure tripartite satisfaction and promote social well-being. However,challenges such as service quality failures and disputes in several regional elderly care PPP projects stem from insufficient government supervision and limited public participation, leading to information asymmetry, incomplete contracts, and suboptimal service quality. Existing research lacks comprehensive models that address the public’s role as a key stakeholder and the dynamic interplay among critical variables affecting PPP stability. This study uses evolutionary game theory (EGT) to explore the strategic interactions between the government, private sector, and public in elderly care PPPs. The model incorporates key factors such as reputation, damage compensation, and government intervention to enhance public participation and tripartite satisfaction. Findings reveal that (1) high-quality services, active public participation, and efficient government supervision are crucial for effective collaborative supervision. (2) Stakeholders’ initial willingness to cooperate, compensation for public interest damages, reputation, and punitive measures are key determinants for stability. (3) Compensation for public interest damages and guaranteed incomes significantly enhance public participation in the supervision process. MATLAB simulations validate the model’s stability, specifically, the simulation shows that the system evolves effectively toward the ideal state when public damage compensation exceeds participation costs and penalties for low-quality services outweigh the cost savings from providing them. Based on these findings, we propose targeted strategic measures such as calibrating compensation and penalty levels based on these threshold relationships to improve collaborative quality and ensure satisfaction among all stakeholders in elderly healthcare PPPs in China, with broader applicability to other sectors and regions.Keywords: Elderly care industry, PPP project, Collaborative quality control, Evolutionary game, Tripartite satisfaction

Introduction

The delivery of quality services for aging populations has become a significant worldwide challenge, especially in China, which holds the position of having the most elderly citizens. As detailed in the report of “World Health Organization (WHO)”, China’s old age citizens are estimated to reach 402 million, making up 28% of the total population by 2040 [1]. This speedy demographic change, along with evolving social patterns and economic developments, has brought sustainability challenges to the general public health and socio-economic development systems [2]. Resultantly, these dynamics have rapidly decreased the effectiveness of traditional family-based caregiving model, calling the need for alternative methodologies in elderly healthcare industries [3, 4]. To address these challenges, the Chinese government has adopted the Public-Private Partnership (PPP) model, inspired by successful Western practices, to improve elderly healthcare services. These partnerships further aim to deliver high quality services that promote social welfare and well-being of communities [5].

PPP projects play a crucial role in providing institutional care through various service formats tailored to the needs of elderly individuals who are unable to remain in their own homes due to health or family circumstances [6]. As such, elderly healthcare PPPs have become increasingly indispensable to China’s healthcare sector [7, 8]. However, their social sustainability hinges on the effective delivery of high-quality services and the achievement of optimal outcomes for all stakeholders [9; 10] [5]; ). Quality collaborative supervision and tripartite satisfaction among government authorities, private sector and the public are prerequisite for the overall welfare and well-being of society [11].

At the core of PPP strategies, the actions and decisions of these three stakeholders shape the implementation process. The government’s role can be divided into two strategies: active supervision, involving the formulation of stringent service standards, regular inspections, and penalties for violations, and passive supervision, which may include minimal checks and weak enforcement. The private sector’s strategies range from providing high-quality services, characterized by adequate staffing, superior care facilities, and medical services, to cutting costs through reduced staffing or deferred maintenance to maximize profits. Similarly, the public’s role is influenced by participation, which involves active engagement through service use and feedback, or non-participation, often due to distrust or perceived inefficacy of the services provided.

Recognized for their transformative potential, PPP model leverage the combined strengths of the government, the general public and private sector, aiming to balance the interests and satisfactions of all three stakeholders [12–14]. Effective implementation of PPP projects in elderly healthcare depends on government efficient regulation, active public participation, and high-quality operations by private entities. Collaboration among these stakeholders is essential for coordinated supervision and ensuring success of such initiatives [15, 16]. Evolutionary models have proven valuable for explaining stakeholder cooperation in PPP frameworks by drawing parallels with natural adaptive behaviors and providing insights into how cooperation can occur despite associated costs [17, 18]. These models deepen understanding of stakeholder dynamics within PPP projects [19–23].

Well-executed elderly healthcare PPPs can alleviate financial burdens, improve public welfare, and strengthen social cohesion. They ensure equitable access to quality services while supporting sustainable financial returns and operational efficiencies for the private sector [24–26]. However, PPP applications in elderly healthcare face challenges due to insufficient government regulation and limited public involvement, often leading to information asymmetry and incomplete contracts [27; 28]). These issues are not merely theoretical. For instance, real-world cases such as disputes over service quality and elderly rights protection in specific regional elderly care PPP projects (e.g., reported incidents in Hunan and Henan provinces) highlight how minimal regulatory supervision in the post-handover phase may allow private actors to prioritize profit over service quality, diminishing public trust [11, 29–32]. These practical challenges underscore the urgent need for a comprehensive analysis of stakeholder behaviors and interactions to balance private sector interests, public expectations, and regulatory standards, key to achieving “tripartite satisfaction” and “Quality Collaborative supervision” embedding the PPP model in elderly healthcare.

Existing research emphasizes the importance of efficient regulatory supervision, public engagement, and standardized mechanisms for successful PPP outcomes [33, 34]. Improvements in legal and regulatory frameworks, as well as in the quality practices of social projects, are critical to fostering better results [35–38]. Balancing financial incentives with private sector engagement is essential to protect the interests of government, markets, and service recipients [39]. Elderly healthcare PPPs operate within a “public sector-private sector-consumer” framework, requiring cooperation at each project stage to achieve tripartite satisfaction [19, 40–42]. The government’s role in securing financial resources and encouraging investment through compensation mechanisms has been instrumental to project success [43, 44]. While regulatory measures encourage private sector commitment to high-quality services, consistent supervision is necessary to ensure accountability [45–48]). These considerations highlight the importance of enhancing governance and collaborative relationships among stakeholders.

Despite the widespread recognition of evolutionary game models in various fields, their application to elderly healthcare Public-Private Partnership (PPP) projects remains notably scarce [49–56] (see Table 1 for more details). Even though PPPs in elderly care have been widely studied, existing research typically treats stakeholders in isolation and fails to address the interactive feedback mechanisms between reputation, damage compensation, and government intervention, factors that are essential for promoting balanced cooperation and ensuring optimal outcomes. The limited focus on these dynamics results in an incomplete understanding of how to achieve effective and sustainable collaborations between the government, private sector, and public. Furthermore, much of the literature overlooks the importance of tripartite satisfaction in these models, which is critical to ensuring long-term service quality and social welfare. By addressing these gaps, this research seeks to address the long-standing issue of insufficient regulatory mechanisms, highlighting the importance of regular supervision and fair compensation to nurture stakeholders’ cooperation and ensure sustainable outcomes.

To address these challenges, this study employs an evolutionary game model to develop strategies for enhancing collaborative supervision in elderly healthcare PPPs. The model integrates key variables such as reputation, damage compensation, and government intervention to foster public participation and ensure tripartite satisfaction among stakeholders. To validate the model, MATLAB simulations are used to identify pathways for promoting fair interactions between stakeholders and to assess the stability of the tripartite supervision system, ensuring it reaches equilibrium over time. The ultimate goal is to provide actionable insights to mitigate the practical problems observed in real-world cases, such as those in regional elderly care PPPs. The study concludes with a set of policy recommendations based on the behavioral strategies of all stakeholders, aimed at strengthening the supervision mechanism. These findings are crucial for improving both regulatory effectiveness and service quality in elderly healthcare PPPs. Additionally, the insights gained offer valuable guidance for government decision-makers seeking to create an environment that promotes collaborative quality control in healthcare and other social PPP sectors.

The subsequent sections of the paper are structured as follows: Section 2 delves into foundational assumptions and construction of the tripartite evolutionary game model. Section 3 presents a tripartite evolutionary game analysis. Section 4 describes the numerical simulation analysis. Finally, section 5 is dedicated to conclusions and policy management implications.

Construction of the tripartite game model and foundational assumptions

It is assumed that private sectors, the public, and the regulatory authorities, each player in the game, harbors distinct objectives. Private sectors seek to maximize profits and safeguard shareholders’ rights and interests, while the public is expecting benefits of quality and cost. Simultaneously, regulatory authorities strive for the maximization of society’s overall welfare and well-being. Despite the differences among these entities, each subject in the game model operates under bounded rationality and contends with incomplete information.

The formulation of the following assumptions is grounded in the operational realities of elderly care PPPs and supported by established economic and management theories. The core premise of bounded rationality aligns with the seminal work of Simon [58], acknowledging the limited information-processing capacity of decision-makers. Furthermore, the strategy sets and payoff structures defined below are designed to capture the principal-agent problems and moral hazards prevalent in long-term contractual relationships like PPPs [40]. These assumptions translate the documented challenges of the sector such as information asymmetry, insufficient supervision, and the divergence between private and public interests into a formal analytical framework, thereby providing a theoretical basis for exploring sustainable cooperation mechanisms.

Within the context of supervising the quality of elderly care PPP projects, the assumptions guiding the tripartite game among private sectors, the public, and regulatory authorities are as follows:

Assumption 1:

The private sectors’ strategy set is: {providing high-standard services; providing low-standard services}; the public’s strategy set is: {participation; without participation}; the regulatory authorities’ strategy set is: {encourage public participation, actively supervise private sectors; encourage public participation, negatively supervise private sectors}. The probabilities of private sectors, the public, and regulatory authorities to choose the corresponding strategies are: , respectively, besides

Assumption 2:

Within active supervision by regulatory authorities and public participation, if private sectors provide services of high-standard, it effectively safeguards the benefits of the public and regulatory authorities. Furthermore, private sectors will add positive reputation benefits and operating subsidies based on the level of public participation. Conversely, if private sectors provide services of low-standard, it adversely impacts the public’s interests and incurs a negative reputation. In such instances, regulatory authorities impose punitive measures such as fines, revoke the issuance of operating subsidies, and provide compensation for damages caused to the public’s interests.

Assumption 3:

Under the assumption of active supervision of private sectors by regulatory authorities without public participation, no matter what strategy private sectors adopt, it will not affect the profits and losses of the public. In other words, the public’s benefit is zero, but it will have an impact on the benefits of private sectors and regulatory authorities. The adoption of the strategy of providing high-standard services by private sectors can effectively protect the benefits of projects and regulatory authorities. On the contrary, if private sectors adopt the strategy of providing low-standard services, it will be punished by the regulatory authorities in case of having active supervision role.

Assumption 4:

Under the assumption of passive supervision of the regulatory authorities with public participation, if the private sectors provides high-quality services, the benefits of public participation in project services can be guaranteed. Otherwise, it will damage the interests of the public. However, the regulatory authorities are passively supervising, which means that the regulatory mechanism is not properly defined. Consequently, no punitive or other measures to monitor the projects and the behavior of private sectors of “providing low-quality services” are considered. In such a case, the regulatory authorities will stipulate in the public-private contract that the private sector will compensate for the damage to the interests of the public.

Assumption 5:

In the case of passive supervision by regulatory authorities and non-participation of the public, private sectors have open ground to operate and perform, resulting in zero public benefits. The role of the regulatory authority remains ineffective. In such a case of passive supervision of regulatory authorities, regulatory costs are high but the benefit is minimal or zero because the private sector will continue operating with low-quality services to the elderly care industry.

Regulatory authorities’ strategic decisions primarily depend on the benefit of active supervision, supervision cost, and fines for private sectors that provide low-standard services. Conclusions and policy Management implications

The study explored the mechanisms of quality collaborative supervision and tripartite satisfaction and the influence of key variables in elderly healthcare PPP projects in China. First we constructed an evolutionary game theory (EGT) model under various assumptions and parameters settings for government regulations, public participation and private sector as each party in the game. The study analyzed various strategies for improving the quality of collaborative supervision and the influence of key variables such as “reputation,” “damage compensation,” and ‘‘government interventions” in order to enhance public participation and increase tripartite satisfaction in elderly healthcare PPP projects in China. Furthermore, MATLAB computational models are used to test the proposed model, discuss possible opportunities for fair interactions between stakeholders, and determine if the tripartite supervision system is asymptotically stable and reaches equilibrium. Notably, the results validates that efficient government supervision, active public participation and high quality services by private sector are highly conducive to achieve collaborative supervision and tripartite satisfaction. Moreover, efficient government regulation is a necessary factor to achieve win-win scenarios for all stakeholders. MATLAB simulations validate the model’s stability, specifically, the simulation shows that the system evolves effectively toward the ideal state when public damage compensation exceeds participation costs and penalties for low-quality services outweigh the cost savings from providing them.

Based on the analysis the following conclusion can be drawn: (1) A stronger initial inclination towards delivering high-standard services by private sectors, coupled with active participation from the public and robust supervision by regulatory authorities, expedites the game system’s convergence towards the equilibrium point of “tripartite satisfaction” (2). Strategic choices of private sectors, public and regulatory authorities demonstrate relative sensitivity to alterations in individual variables. However, ultimate strategy selection relies on a multifaceted comparison encompassing initial willingness, damage compensation, reputation, and punitive measures among the three parties. (3) Crucial factors like compensation for public interest damages and fixed benefits from participation significantly influence the strategic choices of the public. (4) Public participation, a crucial finding in our study, is driven by rational cost-benefit calculations rather than mere civic duty, aligning with the work of Liu et al. [19]. Our model adds to existing literature by quantifying the threshold at which compensation for public interest damages makes participation worthwhile. (5) The study highlights the synergistic relationship between reputation mechanisms and damage compensation, providing a more nuanced understanding of cooperative dynamics than previous studies, such as Cheng & Cheng [11], which focused on individual policy tools. The findings also emphasize the system’s sensitivity to initial conditions, suggesting that early interventions are more effective than corrective actions taken later in the project lifecycle, especially when initial cooperation is low.

These findings have significant policy implications. First, government regulators should prioritize creating an “incentive-compatible” foundation by fostering initial collaboration through preferential policies for quality service providers and public awareness campaigns. Policy tools, including penalties and compensation schemes, should be calibrated to ensure that penalties exceed the cost savings from poor-quality services and that public compensation surpasses participation costs. Furthermore, developing an integrated digital platform for public feedback and compensation would reduce the cost of participation and enhance transparency, facilitating a more engaged and active public role in oversight. For the private sector, adopting a long-term business strategy that emphasizes building a strong reputation through consistent, high-quality service is essential. Providers should also embrace public feedback as a valuable resource for continuous improvement, transforming supervision from a potential risk to a competitive advantage. For the public, particularly the elderly and their families, effective participation hinges on rational decision-making. Encouraging organized, collective action such as forming resident committees or family councils that can reduce individual costs and make public supervision more impactful. By providing constructive feedback through official channels, the public can drive meaningful improvements in service quality.

Despite the strengths of the model, there are limitations. The assumptions made in the model may not fully reflect the complexities of real-world elderly care settings, such as regional variations in service expectations or differing government capacities. Additionally, the model’s reliance on specific parameters means that the conclusions are contingent on their accuracy, and further empirical validation is needed to ensure that the findings are applicable across diverse PPP projects. These limitations point to the need for further refinement and real-world testing of the proposed model to improve its predictive capacity and policy relevance.

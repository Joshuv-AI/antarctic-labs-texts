Diphtheria in the Americas: strengthening routine immunization, surveillance, and outbreak readiness
Authors: Pan American Health Organization
Published: 2026

13 JULY 2026, WASHINGTON, D.C.—

Diphtheria is increasing in the Region of the Americas. Between epidemiological week (EW) 1 and EW 21 of 2026, 163 confirmed cases, including five deaths, were reported across three countries: Brazil (n = 2 cases), Haiti (n = 159 cases, including five deaths), and Peru (n = 2 cases). The concentration of cases in Haiti, together with new cases in other countries of the Region, has resulted in a total that already exceeds the annual average of 61 confirmed cases reported across the Americas during 2020–2024. In the context of suboptimal DTP (diphtheria, tetanus, and pertussis) immunization rates, this situation underscores the need to close vaccination gaps, strengthen epidemiological surveillance of vaccine-preventable diseases, and maintain a permanent supply of diphtheria antitoxin (DAT) to control potential outbreaks.

Prior to 2020, diphtheria was uncommon in the Region of the Americas, with only a few dozen cases reported to the Pan American Health Organization (PAHO) and the World Health Organization (WHO) annually. By 2025, Brazil, Canada, Guyana, and Haiti had reported confirmed cases. Diphtheria cases are being reported alongside persistent gaps in first-dose DTP (DTP1) vaccination coverage in several countries of the Region. Vaccination coverage rates for DTP1 have declined in the Americas since 2014, with coverage falling below the regional target of 95%. More recently, coverage for the third dose (DTP3) decreased from 88% in 2023 to 87% in 2024 (Figure 1). Moreover, in 2024, six countries and one territory recorded DTP3 coverage below 80% while seven countries reported dropout rates exceeding 10% between the first and third doses of the DTP vaccine.

The Region of the Americas faces a highly localized resurgence of diphtheria in 2026. In Haiti, between EW 1 and EW 21, 1 616 suspected cases of diphtheria were reported, including 13 deaths; 159 cases were confirmed (Figure 2). This number exceeds the 58 confirmed cases reported in Haiti throughout 2025, when four confirmed deaths were recorded. Confirmed cases have been reported in all 10 departments of the country, with the highest numbers in Ouest (n = 58 cases), Sud-Est (n = 36 cases), and Nord-Est (n = 27 cases). Age distribution patterns also indicate a substantial proportion of confirmed cases among people aged 20 years or older, accounting for 40% of cases (n = 63 cases). Data on vaccination status further highlight immunity gaps, with 55% of confirmed cases in Haiti being unvaccinated or having an unknown vaccination status (n = 87 cases).

Additional cases of diphtheria were detected in Brazil and Peru in 2026, with two confirmed in each country. In Brazil, the two cases were reported in the states of Maranhão and Piauí, at a time when DTP3 vaccine coverage stood at 88.4% in 2025, down from the 90.4% recorded in 2024. In Peru, the two confirmed cases marked the first confirmed cases of diphtheria reported since the 2020 outbreak in Lima. By 2025, Peru reported a DTP2 coverage rate of 69.8%, while 50% of municipalities achieved a DTP3 coverage rate of 80% or higher.

CURRENT SITUATION

The current situation must be interpreted in the context of the Americas, where vaccination coverage is slowly recovering for most diseases following the COVID-19 pandemic. This analysis is also relevant to the DTP vaccine, as coverage still needs to improve to reach the target of ≥95%. Since the causative agent of diphtheria has not been eliminated, declining vaccination rates, waning immunity over time, and gaps in booster doses may create the conditions for renewed endemic transmission. The detection of confirmed cases across multiple countries in the Region in 2026 highlights the critical need to close immunity gaps. Furthermore, this shifting transmission pattern underscores the urgency of strengthening active surveillance and ensuring robust laboratory testing protocols are maintained for final case classification.

These findings also highlight the importance of integrating vaccination, surveillance, laboratory diagnosis, clinical management, and risk communication into outbreak preparedness. Effective control depends on robust surveillance systems that support case reporting, laboratory confirmation, contact-tracing, and vaccination monitoring. At the same time, maintaining a steady supply of DAT, ensuring timely clinical management, and promoting early recognition and reporting remain essential for preventing cases and outbreaks, and for reducing complications and mortality.

PUBLIC HEALTH IMPLICATIONS

PAHO Member States are recommended to ensure vaccination coverage of ≥95% with the primary series (three doses) of DTP vaccine and with boosters (three doses), uniformly across the country. Primary vaccination should begin at 6 weeks of age and must be completed by 6 months, with boosters at 12–23 months, 4–7 years, and 9–15 years. Every contact with the health system should be used to complete vaccination schedules, including reaching the unvaccinated population, verifying vaccination status upon school entry, and administering Td (tetanus and diphtheria) or Tdap (tetanus, diphtheria, acellular pertussis) during prenatal care and for wound prophylaxis.

Countries should strengthen diphtheria surveillance to monitor disease trends, identify outbreaks, conduct contact-tracing, and evaluate control measures. All suspected cases should undergo laboratory testing for final case classification.

Risk communication should facilitate early detection, reporting, clinical management, epidemiological investigation, and the immediate initiation of treatment.

Whole-genome sequencing of a multidrug resistant Escherichia coli RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 isolated from nasal swab of cat in Bangladesh
Authors: Muhammad Abdul Mannan, Rubaya Sharmin Jhilik, S. M. Abdullah, Md. Abdullah Al Farhan, Mustafizur Rahman, Mohammad Jubair, Md. Morshedur Rahman, Md. Abu Ahsan Gilman, M. Nazmul Hoque, Md. Rezaul Karim
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTWe report the whole-genome sequence of multidrug resistant Escherichia coli RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 isolated from the nasal swab of a cat in Dhaka, Bangladesh. The assembled genome comprises 5,318,660 bp with 50.5% guanine-cystosine (GC) content, together with putative antibiotic-resistant genes.

ANNOUNCEMENT

Escherichia coli is a gram-negative bacterium commonly found in the intestinal tract of humans and animals, as well as environmental sources, such as food, soil, and water (1, 2), posing a significant threat to public and veterinary health worldwide (3). Here, we report the whole-genome sequence of a multidrug-resistant E. coli strain RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 recovered from the nasal swab of a calico cat in Dhaka, Bangladesh (23.8° N, 90.4° E).

The nasal swab sample was inoculated into 10 mL of nutrient broth (Liofilchem, Italy) and incubated overnight at 37°C. Following enrichment, one loopful inoculum of broth was then spread onto eosin-methylene blue (Liofilchem, Italy) agar and incubated at 37°C for 24 h. Presumptive E. coli colonies were subsequently subcultured on fresh EMB agar and MacConkey agar for purification. Initial identification of E. coli was performed based on colony morphology (green metallic sheen colored, circular shape on EMB and pinkish colored colonies at MacConkey) and Gram staining (gram-negative bacilli) (4).

Genomic DNA from the pure E. coli isolate RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 was extracted from an overnight nutrient broth culture incubated at 37°C using the DNeasy Blood & Tissue Kit (Qiagen, Germany). DNA concentration was measured using the Qubit dsDNA quantification assay, yielding 13.7 ng/µL. Sequencing library was prepared from 300 ng of DNA using the Illumina DNA Prep Reagent Kit (Illumina, USA), and whole-genome sequencing (WGS) was performed on an Illumina MiSeq platform with a 2 × 250 bp paired-end protocol (5). Paired-end sequencing produced 1,016,276 reads. Raw sequencing reads were quality assessed using FastQC v0.11.3 (6). Trimmomatic v0.39.2 (7) was used to remove Illumina adapters from both forward (R1) and reverse (R2) reads. The genome was assembled using SPAdes v. 4.0.0 (8) into 362 contigs at least 500 nucleotides long, and the post-quality resulting assembly was checked with QUAST v5.3.0 (9). To assess assembly accuracy, genome completeness and contamination were determined with CheckM2 v1.1.0 (10), and average nucleotide identity (ANI) values were determined through FastANI v1.3 (11) to a reference E. coli. Genome annotation of E. coli RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 was performed using the NCBI Prokaryotic Genome Annotation Pipeline (PGAP) v6.9 (12). Unless otherwise stated, default parameters were used for all software.

The genome size, guanine-cytosine (GC) content, genome coverage, and predicted gene numbers of E. coli RSJ-SAU.Ec.2025 are given in Table 1. Further analysis was further analysis of the assembled genome using AMRFinderPlus 3.11.20 (13), and ResFinder v4.7.2 (14) identified five putative AMR genes in the bacterial isolate, namely, qnrB4, blaDHA-1, qacE, sul1, and dfrA7. Mobile Element Finder v1.03 (15) identified three plasmids, namely, IncI1, ColRNAI, and IncFII(pHN7AB), and nine mobile genetic elements (MGEs). Functional subsystem analysis was performed using the RAST v2.0 server (16), and pathogenic potential was predicted with PathogenFinder v1.1 (17). Default parameters were applied for all analyses unless otherwise specified. Functional analysis revealed 388 subsystems, and a PathogenFinder score of 0.936 highlighted its potential relevance to both animal and human health.

Black Brane/Bose Gase Duality and Third Law of Thermodynamics
Irina Aref’eva, Daniil Stepanenko, Igor Volovich

Abstract
In the thermodynamics of black holes in asymptotically flat space, the third law of thermodynamics is violated, and entropy cannot be consistently modeled through conventional statistical mechanics. Notably, the third law of thermodynamics is violated for the Schwarzschild black hole, and its entropy can only be described using an unconventional model, such as a Bose gas in negative dimensions. In contrast, for certain black brane solutions—such as Poincare AdS black branes, Lifshitz black branes, and anisotropic Lifshitz-type black branes—the third law is preserved, with entropy vanishing as temperature approaches zero. In this paper, we extend the previously established duality between black hole and Bose gas thermodynamics to black branes. Specifically, the Poincare black brane in D D spacetime dimensions corresponds to a non-relativistic Bose gas in 2 ​ ( D − 2 ) 2(D-2) spatial dimensions. Furthermore, the duality between Lifshitz branes and Bose gases relates a Lifshitz brane with exponent α \alpha in D D -dimensional spacetime to a Bose gas of quasi-particles with energy k α k^{\alpha} in D − 2 D-2 spatial dimensions.

In this paper, we discuss the validity of the third law of thermodynamics for black brane solutions. This applies not only to the simplest black brane in AdS but also to non-relativistic black branes, such as Lifshitz branes

Setup

2.1
Black Hole and Black Brane Thermodynamics

Here, we consider the metric in a
D
-dimensional spacetime of the form

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\mathfrak{B}^{2}(z)\left(-g(z){\cal N}(z)\,dt^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{D-2}\mathfrak{g}_{i}\,dx_{i}^{2}+\frac{dz^{2}}{{\cal K}(z)g(z)}\right),

(2.1)

where
t\in\mathbb{R}
, all transverse coordinates
x_{i}
also belong to
\mathbb{R}
, and
z>0
. The functions
\mathfrak{B}(z)
,
g(z)
,
{\cal N}(z)
, and
{\cal K}(z)
are smooth and defined on appropriate domains, with the assumptions
{\cal N}(z)>0
and
{\cal K}(z)>0
. The black hole horizon
z_{h}
is defined as the root of the blackening function
g(z)
:

\displaystyle g(z_{h})=0.

(2.2)

The Hawking temperature can be found from the requirement that there is no conical singularity for the Euclidean version of the metric (
2.1
) near the horizon.
Supposing that
t
varies from 0 to
\beta
,
we get the condition

\frac{1}{2}g^{\prime}(z_{h})\sqrt{{\cal K}(z_{h}){\cal N}(z_{h})}\beta=2\pi.

(2.3)

Since
T=1/\beta
, we get

T=\frac{1}{4\pi}g^{\prime}(z_{h})\sqrt{{\cal K}(z_{h}){\cal N}(z_{h})}.

(2.4)

The entropy is given by

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{V_{D-2}}{4G_{D}}\left(\mathfrak{B}(z_{h})\right)^{D-2}\prod_{i=1}^{D-2}\sqrt{\mathfrak{g}_{i}(z_{h})},

(2.5)

where
V_{D-2}
is the volume of the transverse directions.

2.2
Bose Gas in
d
-Dimensional Space

Consider a Bose gas in a
d
-dimensional space
[
17
,
18
,
19
,
20
,
21
]
, with the grand potential (free energy) given by

\displaystyle F_{BG}=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{\tiny{\begin{array}[]{c}k_{i}=2\pi n_{i}/L\\
i=1,\ldots,d\\
n_{i}=1,2,\ldots\end{array}}}\ln\Big(1-e^{\beta\left(\mu-\sigma\,\varepsilon_{\gamma}(\vec{k})\right)}\Big),

where
\varepsilon_{\gamma}(\vec{k})
is the energy of quasi-particles:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{\gamma}(\vec{k})=k^{\gamma}\equiv\left(\vec{k}^{2}\right)^{\gamma/2},\qquad\vec{k}=\{k_{1},\ldots,k_{d}\},

(2.10)

and
\sigma
is a positive dimensional constant. The non-relativistic Bose gas corresponds to
\gamma=2
.

For large
L
, the continuum version is

\displaystyle F_{BG}(\beta)=\frac{L^{d}}{(2\pi)^{d}\beta}\int\ln\left(1-e^{\beta\left(\mu-\sigma\,\varepsilon_{\gamma}(\vec{k})\right)}\right)d^{d}k.

(2.11)

Using spherical coordinates and integrating over spherical angles in (
2.11
), we obtain for
\mu=0
:

\displaystyle F_{BG}=\frac{\Omega_{d-1}}{\beta}\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln\left(1-e^{-\beta\,\sigma\,k^{\gamma}}\right)k^{d-1}\,dk,

(2.12)

where
\Omega_{d-1}=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma(d/2)}
, and
k^{\gamma}
represents the exponent of the radial momenta as defined in (
2.10
).

By substituting the variable
\beta\,\sigma\,k^{\gamma}=x
and using the representation

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\ln\Big(1-e^{-x}\Big)\,x^{s-1}\,dx=-\Gamma(s)\,\zeta(s+1),

(2.13)

valid for
\Re(s)>1
, we obtain

\displaystyle F_{BG}=-\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{d\,\Gamma(d/2)}\left(\frac{1}{\beta}\right)^{\frac{d}{\gamma}+1}\left(\frac{1}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{d}{\gamma}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right)\zeta\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right).

(2.14)

The expression (
2.14
) is obtained for
\Re\,d>\gamma
(where
\Re
denotes the real part), but
\zeta(s)
can be analytically extended to the entire complex plane as a meromorphic function with a single simple pole at
s=1
. Thus, we can use formula (
2.14
) for complex values of
d
.

The entropy is given by

\displaystyle S_{BG}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{\partial F_{BG}(\beta,\gamma,\sigma,d,z)}{\partial T}=\beta^{2}\frac{\partial F_{BG}}{\partial\beta}

(2.15)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(1+\frac{d}{\gamma}\right)\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{d\,\Gamma(d/2)}\left(\frac{1}{\beta\sigma}\right)^{\frac{d}{\gamma}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right)\zeta\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right).

3
Black Hole and Bose Gas Duality in the Case of Third Law of Thermodynamics Violation

3.1
D-dimensional Schwarzschild Black Hole

The Euclidean action in
D
-dimensional space for the Schwarzschild black hole is:

I_{E}=-\frac{1}{16\pi G_{D}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}d^{D}x\sqrt{g}R-\frac{1}{8\pi G_{D}}\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}}d^{D-1}x\sqrt{h}K,

(3.1)

where the second term is the Gibbons-Hawking-York (GHY) term
[
28
,
29
]
,
h
is the determinant of the induced metric on the boundary, and
K
is the extrinsic curvature.

The Euclidean Schwarzschild metric is given by:

ds^{2}=\left(1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{D-3}\right)d\tau^{2}+\left(1-\left(\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)^{D-3}\right)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d{\bf\Omega}_{D-2}^{2},

(3.2)

where
d{\bf\Omega}_{D-2}^{2}
represents the metric on the
(D-2)
-sphere
[
30
]
.

The horizon
r_{h}
is related to the ADM mass by

M=\frac{r_{h}^{D-3}(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G_{D}},

(3.3)

where

\Omega_{D-2}=\frac{2\pi^{(D-1)/2}}{\Gamma((D-1)/2)}

(3.4)

is the surface area of a unit sphere
S^{D-2}
.

The temperature of the Schwarzschild black hole is

T=\frac{D-3}{4\pi r_{h}}=\frac{D-3}{4\pi}\left(\frac{(D-2)\Omega_{D-2}}{16\pi G_{D}M}\right)^{\frac{1}{D-3}}.

(3.5)

We observe that the Hawking temperature increases as
M
decreases for
D>3
, and the heat capacity
\frac{\partial M}{\partial T}
is negative.

The entropy of the Schwarzschild black hole is

S=\frac{\Omega_{D-2}r_{h}^{D-2}}{4\,G_{D}}=\frac{\Omega_{D-2}}{4\,G_{D}}\left(\frac{D-3}{4\pi T}\right)^{D-2}.

(3.6)

We see that entropy increases as the Hawking temperature
T
decreases, indicating a violation of the third law of thermodynamics.

In
D=4
spacetime, the Schwarzschild black hole metric takes the form:

ds^{2}=-\left(1-\frac{r_{h}}{r}\right)dt^{2}+\frac{dr^{2}}{1-\frac{r_{h}}{r}}+r^{2}d{\bf\Omega}_{2}^{2},

(3.7)

where
d{\bf\Omega}_{2}^{2}
is the metric on the 2-dimensional unit sphere,
r_{h}=2G_{4}M
, and
M
is the mass of the black hole.

The Hawking temperature and the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy are given by:

T=\frac{1}{8\pi G_{4}M},\qquad S=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{4}T^{2}}.

(3.8)

The entropy diverges as
T\to 0
, thus violating the third law of thermodynamics in Planck’s formulation.

3.2
Schwarzschild Black Hole/Bose Gas Duality

This duality has been studied in detail in
[
5
,
6
]
. Here, we briefly summarize the main result. By equating the entropy of a
D
-dimensional Schwarzschild black hole (
3.6
) with the entropy (
2.15
) of a
d
-dimensional Bose gas with energy (
2.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\Omega_{D-2}}{4\,G_{D}}\left(\frac{D-3}{4\pi T}\right)^{D-2}=\left(1+\frac{d}{\gamma}\right)\left(\frac{L}{2\pi}\right)^{d}\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{d\,\Gamma(d/2)}\left(\frac{T}{\sigma}\right)^{\frac{d}{\gamma}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right)\zeta\left(\frac{d}{\gamma}+1\right),

(3.9)

which leads to

D-2=-\frac{d}{\gamma}.

(3.10)

This result implies that a positive
D-2
corresponds to a negative
d
. For negative
d
and integer values of
1/\gamma
, singularities appear on the right-hand side of (
3.9
) and one has to perform renormalizations, for further details see
[
6
]
. The concept of negative dimension was also used in
[
27
]
to compute the inflationary cosmological constant.

4
Planar AdS Black Branes and Bose Gas

4.1
Poincare Black Brane in AdS

Note that for the Poincare-AdS black brane (i.e., the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole in the Poincare patch), also referred to as the planar AdS (PAds) black brane, the third law of thermodynamics holds.
Indeed, consider the action

S=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{D}}\int d^{D}x\sqrt{-g}\left[R-\Lambda\right].

(4.1)

The metric

ds^{2}=\frac{L^{2}}{z^{2}}\left(-g(z)\,dt^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}_{D-2}+\frac{dz^{2}}{g(z)}\right),

(4.2)

with the blackening function given by

g(z)=1-\left(\frac{z}{z_{h}}\right)^{D-1},

(4.3)

where
z_{h}
is the black hole horizon, defined by

4.1

\Lambda=-\frac{(D-1)(D-2)}{2L^{2}},

(4.4)

The Hawking temperature and the Bekenstein-Hawking entropy are
[

T=\frac{D-1}{4\pi z_{h}},

S\,=\,\frac{V_{D-2}}{4G_{D}}\frac{L^{D-2}}{z_{h}^{D-2}}\,=\,\frac{V_{D-2}}{4G_{D}}\left(\frac{4\pi LT}{D-1}\right)^{D-2},

(4.6)

the volume

is determined by the area of the change of the coordinates
x_{1},...x_{D-2}

We see that
S\to 0

7
Conclusion

The fact that black holes are dual to a Bose gas in negative dimensions explains the violation of the third law of thermodynamics by black holes. Indeed, the entropy of black holes corresponds to the entropy of a Bose gas in negative dimensions, which, according to Eq. (
2.15
), depends on temperature through a negative power:

S_{BG}\sim T^{d/\gamma},

(7.1)

where, for negative
d<0
, we observe that
S_{BG}
diverges as
T\to 0
, leading to a violation of the third law of thermodynamics.
In light of Eq. (
2.15
), this relationship can also be interpreted in the opposite direction: the inverse temperature dependence of black hole entropy suggests that a duality between black holes and a Bose gas is feasible only in negative dimensions.

In this paper, we have established a duality between certain black branes and a Bose gas in standard positive dimensions. This result immediately implies the validity of the third law of thermodynamics in these cases.

As for further directions, it would be interesting to generalize the duality between black branes and a Bose gas to include non-zero chemical potentials and to establish a connection between the Bose-Einstein condensate in a Bose gas and the phase transition of black branes.

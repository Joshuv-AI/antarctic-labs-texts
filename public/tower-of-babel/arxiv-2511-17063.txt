Local wellposedness of the 2d Anderson-Gross-Pitaevskii equation
S. Mackowiak

Abstract
In this paper, the local wellposedness of a general Gross-Pitaevskii equation with rough potential is proven in dimension 2. The class of rough potentials we are considering is large enough to contain the spatial white noise and thus a renormalization procedure may be needed. We first construct the associated Schrödinger operator from its quadratic form. Then, the regularity of elements of its domain is explored. This allows to use a paracontrolled approach in order to obtain Strichartz estimates, which are used to prove the local wellposedness by a contraction argument.

Introduction

In this paper, we are interested in solving the renormalized Gross-Pitaevskii equation with a real spatial white noise potential
\xi
in two dimensions of space

{~}\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}u+Hu+\xi u+\lambda\left|u\right|^{2\gamma}u=0\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
u=u(t,x)
is a complex-valued space-time random field defined on
I\times\mathbb{R}^{2}_{x}
for some time interval
I
,
H=\Delta-|x|^{2}
is the Hermite operator,
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma>0
. The study of such equation is motivated by the modelling of Bose-Einstein condensation. In fact, the deterministic equation, which is a non-linear Schrödinger equation with confining potential, is an extensively studied dispersive equation known as the Gross-Pitaevskii equation. It appears in the study of Bose-Einstein condensates as a mean-field limit to the coupled linear many-body Schrödinger equation with confining potential (see
[
10
]
). The Gross-Pitaevskii equation with random potential can be seen as a model for Bose-Einstein condensates in presence of inhomogeneities (see for example
[
15
,
17
]
and references therein). Hence, the white noise potential can be seen as a toy model of random potential with null correlation length.

In
[
25
]
the author hinted this equation may not admit solutions, due to the low regularity of the spatial white noise, even for smooth and localised initial data. Instead, the author solved the equation

{~}\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}u+:H+\xi:u+\lambda\left|u\right|^{2}u=0\\
u(0)=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.2)

where
:H+\xi:
is the Wick renormalization of the
Hermite operator with spatial white noise potential. The renormalized operator is defined through an exponential transform inspired by the one introduced in
[
21
]
to study the continuous parabolic Anderson model, and later used in
[
12
,
36
,
37
]
to solve the non-linear Anderson-Schrödinger equation on the torus
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and in
[
11
,
13
]
on the full space
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. Let
Y=(-H)^{-1}\xi
, it is almost surely of regularity
1-
, so one can define
\rho=\mathrm{e}^{Y}
and search for a formula for
(H+\xi)u
when
u
is of the form
u=\rho v
. A formal computation gives

(H+\xi)u=\rho\left(Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+|\nabla Y|^{2}v\right).

Unfortunately,
\nabla Y
is of regularity
0-
, thus the product
|\nabla Y|^{2}
barely fails to exist. The author proved in
[
25
]
that the wick product
:|\nabla Y|^{2}:
can be defined as the almost sure limit, in suitable space of distributions of regularity
0-
, of

|\nabla Y_{N}|^{2}-\mathbb{E}[|\nabla Y_{N}|^{2}],

where
Y_{N}
is a suitable regularisation of
Y_{N}
with only low modes. Thus, the renormalized operator can be formally defined as

:H+\xi:u=\rho\left(Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+:|\nabla Y|^{2}:v\right).

This shows that knowing a trajectory of
\xi
is not sufficient to define the operator
:H+\xi:
. It is a usual fact in the study of Anderson operators that one needs more data in order to define the operator (see for example
[
27
,
3
,
1
,
22
]
). Following this idea, we construct a formal renormalization of
H+\xi
given an enhanced noise
\Xi=(Y,Z)
with
Y=(-H)^{-1}\xi
and
Z
playing the role of
|\nabla Y|^{2}
, as

:H+\xi:u=\rho\left(Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+Zv\right).

This formula does not make sense directly, but as usual, the bilinear form of the operator can be defined as

\langle-:H+\xi:u_{1},u_{2}\rangle=\re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\left(\nabla v_{1}\cdot\overline{\nabla v_{2}}+|x|^{2}(1-Y)v_{1}\overline{v_{2}}\right)\rho^{2}\mathrm{d}x-\langle Z,\overline{v_{1}}v_{2}\rho^{2}\rangle,

where
\rho=\mathrm{e}^{Y}
,
u_{i}=\rho v_{i}
and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
stand for a duality bracket between suitable spaces. We then show that this bilinear form is associated to a self-adjoint lower semi-bounded operator of compact resolvent on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
which we take as a definition of
-:H+\xi:
. Up to a
\Xi
-dependent shift
\delta_{\Xi}
, one can define a positive self-adjoint operator
-A=-:H+\xi:+\delta_{\Xi}
. This makes
-A
a bit easier to study than
:H+\xi:
. The construction of
-A
and its elementary properties are the object of
section

3
. A similar construction for
:\Delta+\xi:
on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
and
\mathbb{T}^{3}
has been done in
[
28
]
.

In this paper, we follow a purely deterministic and analytic approach of "formal renormalization" which allows us to treat in the same way cases where a renormalization is needed and cases where
|\nabla Y|^{2}
exists as a
\mathrm{L}^{q}
function (for
q>4
), as for the radial white noise or any deterministic potential in a Sobolev space
\mathrm{H}^{-1,2q}
.

Unfortunately, the domain of
-A
is not stable under multiplication, hence the standard fixed point fails to apply directly to

{~}\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}u+Au+\lambda\left|u\right|^{2\gamma}u=0\\
u(0)=u_{0}.\end{cases}

(1.3)

In this situation, it is well-known that one may take advantage of dispersive properties of the associated Schrödinger propagator
(\mathrm{e}^{itA})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
in order to obtain local solutions. Given an admissible pair
(p,r)
, that is a pair
(p,r)\in[2,+\infty)^{2}
satisfying
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}
, then one expects
(\mathrm{e}^{itA})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
to verify

\left|\mathrm{e}^{itA}u\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{(-T,T)}\mathrm{L}^{r}_{x}}\leqslant C|u|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}

(1.4)

for some
T>0
and
\sigma\geqslant 0
, where
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
is a Sobolev space associated to
-H
. This idea goes back to Strichartz, when studying in
[
32
]
the local smoothing property of wave equations. Such inequalities have been obtained for the free Schrödinger propagator
(\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, using the dispersive estimate

|\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta}u|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}_{x}}\lesssim|t|^{-1}|u|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}_{x}},

and in that case
T>0
is arbitrary and
\sigma=0
, thus
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
is just
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. In the presence of a smooth confining potential
V
, there are two different cases depending on the growth at infinity of
V
. On the one hand, if
V
is subquadratic at infinity, that is
\partial^{\alpha}V
is bounded when
|\alpha|\geqslant 2
, it was shown in
[
16
]
that
(\mathrm{e}^{it(\Delta-V)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
verifies the same kind of dispersive estimate on a short time. This implies that it verifies an estimate of the type
equation

1.4
with
T
small enough and
\sigma=0
. On the other hand, if
V
is superquatratic, then it is proven in
[
40
]
that
(\mathrm{e}^{it(\Delta-V)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
does not verify a dispersive estimate. Nevertheless, they obtain Strichartz inequalities with a loss of derivative on short time, that is
equation

1.4
with
T
small enough and some
\sigma>0
. On a compact manifold, Strichartz estimates with losses for
(\mathrm{e}^{it\Delta})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
have been obtained in
[
7
]
, where they were proven to be stable under
\mathrm{H}^{1}
perturbations. The same method was then used in
[
29
,
41
]
to obtain Strichartz inequalities with losses for
(\mathrm{e}^{it(\Delta+\xi)})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
on 2d compact manifolds. In this paper, we follow this idea of lower order perturbation of Strichartz inequalities for
(\mathrm{e}^{itH})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
to obtain Strichartz inequalities for
(\mathrm{e}^{itA})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
. However,
A
cannot be seen directly as a perturbation of
H
. First remark that, formally,

\rho^{-1}A(\rho v)=Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+Zv-\delta_{\Xi}v.

Thus,
\tilde{A}=\rho^{-1}A\rho
is a better candidate to be a lower order perturbation of
H
. We prove in
section

3
that this operator is well-defined on
\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})=\rho^{-1}\mathrm{D}(-A)\subset\mathcal{W}^{2-,2}
. Then, a paracontrolled approach of
\tilde{A}
allows to get rid of the worst regularity terms in the expression of
\tilde{A}
.
The idea of paracontrolled calculus to study singular stochastic equations was introduced in
[
19
]
and was then extensively used to study the continuous Anderson operator (see for instance
[
1
,
3
,
27
,
29
]
). The idea is to look for elements
v
in the domain of the form
v=v^{\sharp}+P_{\nabla v}X_{1}+P_{v}X_{2}
with
v^{\sharp}\in D(-H)=\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
,
P_{f}g
stands for the paraproduct of
g
by
f
(see
section

2.3
for a precise definition) and
X_{1},X_{2}
are chosen in order to cancel the worst regularity terms in
2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v
and
Zv
respectively. Using this ansatz, one obtains a formula of the type

\tilde{A}v=Hv^{\sharp}+\mathcal{R}(v)

with
\mathcal{R}
a remainder expected to be of lower order. The paracontrolled approach is detailed in
section

4.2
. Then, following the idea of
[
7
,
29
]
, we prove the following Strichartz estimate.

.

(Strichartz estimates for
:H+\xi:
)
Let
\kappa\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\right)
,
\xi\in\mathcal{W}^{-1-\kappa,\infty}
and
\Xi
verifying
equations

3.1
and
3.2
. Define
:H+\xi:
as the unbounded self-adjoint operator associated to the quadratic form given by
equation

3.4

.Then, there exists a time

\forall u\in\mathcal{D}^{\alpha+\frac{1}{p}+\kappa+\varepsilon,2},\;\left|\mathrm{e}^{it:H+\xi:}u\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{(-T,T)}\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,r}}\leqslant C|u|_{\mathcal{D}^{\alpha+\frac{1}{p}+\kappa+\varepsilon,2}},

where
\mathcal{D}^{\alpha,r}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\alpha+\frac{1}{q}+\kappa+\varepsilon,2}
are defined by
equation

3.5
.

Once Strichartz estimates are obtained, it becomes easy to solve
equation

1.3
for low regularity initial data. As expected for a Schrödinger equation, since the noise is real-valued, solutions preserve their
\mathrm{L}^{2}
-norm (see
4.11
), and for
u_{0}
in the form domain of

(that is

1.3
with a noise-independent initial data. Namely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.2
.

(Local wellposedness)
Let
\kappa
and
:H+\xi:
as in
1.1
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
such that

,

and

. Then, for any

, there exists a unique maximal solution
(I,u)
of

\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}u+:H+\xi:u+\lambda\left|u\right|^{2\gamma}u=0,\;t>0,\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\\

(1.6)

in
\mathcal{C}(I,\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2})\cap\mathrm{L}^{p}_{loc}(I,\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
. Moreover,

, such that

\forall u_{0}\in\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2},\;T=\frac{C_{1}}{|u_{0}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2}}^{c}}\Rightarrow\left(T<T_{+}\text{ and }|u|_{Y_{T}}\leqslant C_{2}|u_{0}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2}}\right),

Preliminaries

2.1
Hermite-Sobolev spaces

We denote by
H=\Delta-|x|^{2}
the Hermite operator. It is well-known that this operator has eigenfunctions
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
verifying the relation
-Hh_{k}=\lambda_{k}^{2}h_{k}
where
\lambda_{k}^{2}\sim c\sqrt{k}
. Moreover,
(h_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
is a complete orthonormal system of
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
and define

\mathcal{W}^{s,p}=\left\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),(-H)^{\frac{s}{2}}u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}

endowed with the norm

|u|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,p}_{x}}=|(-H)^{\frac{s}{2}}u|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{x}}.

It is known that these spaces are Banach spaces and that for
q
such that
\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}=1
, we have
\left(\mathcal{W}^{s,p}\right)^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
with equal norm.

Moreover, the subspace
\vspan\left\{h_{k},k\in\mathbb{N}\right\}
is dense in
\mathcal{W}^{s,q}
-spaces (see for example
[
5
]
for the case
s\geqslant 0
) and for
s\geqslant 0
, an equivalent norm is given for
p\in(1,+\infty)
by (see
[
14
]
)

|u|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,p}}\approx|u|_{\mathrm{H}^{s,p}_{x}}+|\langle x\rangle^{s}u|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{x}}.

This shows
\mathcal{W}^{s,p}=\left\{u\in\mathrm{H}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\langle x\rangle^{s}u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}.
The following result, is then a consequence of Theorem 2.7.1 in
[
4
]
, characterizes
\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
for
s>0
and
q\in(1,+\infty)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s>0
and
q\in(1,+\infty)
. For any
f\in\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
, there exists
f_{r}\in\mathrm{H}^{-s,q}
and
f_{l}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
f=f_{r}+\langle x\rangle^{s}f_{l}
and

|f|_{\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}}\approx\inf\left\{|f_{r}|_{\mathrm{H}^{-s,q}}+|f_{l}|_{\mathrm{L}^{q}},\;f=f_{r}+\langle x\rangle^{s}f_{l}\right\}

in the sense of norm equivalence.

In particular, elements of
\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
may lack integrability and grow at infinity. As a typical example, let
\alpha\geqslant 0
, then
\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
if and only if
s>\alpha+\frac{2}{q}
. Thus, for
s>\frac{2}{q}
there exists unbounded smooth and globally Lipschitz functions in
\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
.

Using the norm equivalence, we define
\mathcal{W}^{2,p}
with
p\in\{1,+\infty\}
as

\mathcal{W}^{2,p}=\left\{u\in\mathrm{H}^{2,p}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\langle x\rangle^{2}u\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\right\}

endowed with the norm

|u|_{\mathcal{W}^{2,p}_{x}}=|u|_{\mathrm{H}^{2,p}_{x}}+|\langle x\rangle^{2}u|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{x}}

and extend this definition to
s\in[0,2]
by complex interpolation. Moreover inspired by the relation
\left(\mathcal{W}^{s,p}\right)^{\prime}=\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
, for
s\in[-2,0)
, we define
\mathcal{W}^{s,\infty}=\left(\mathcal{W}^{-s,1}\right)^{\prime}
. As usual, we cannot define spaces of negative
\mathrm{L}^{1}
regularity as dual spaces of positive
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
regularity.

Remark 2.2
.

We do not claim that the first definition of
\mathcal{W}^{s,p}
agrees with the one we give for spaces over
\mathrm{L}^{1}
and
\mathrm{L}^{\infty}
. We use the same notation for simplicity as we will always control
\mathcal{W}^{s,\infty}
norms by Sobolev embeddings.

The choices we made ensure us that we can interpolate between spaces of positive regularity and some other convenient properties that we need in our analysis. For example, our choices allow us to have a simple product rule on our spaces, whose proof follows from Leibniz rule, Hölder inequality and complex bilinear interpolation. We start with product rules, recalling an important estimate going back to Kato and Ponce
[
24
]
.

.

(Theorem 1.4 in
[

]

(2.2)

Proof.

2.2
Weighted Besov spaces

In this section, we introduce the tools we need from the theory of weighted Besov spaces. For more details on Besov spaces and Paley-Littlewood theory, the interested reader can refer to Chapter 2 in
[
2
]
for unweighted Besov spaces and to Chapter 6 in
[
35
]
for weighted spaces.

A function
w\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}_{+})
is called a (admissible) weight if

•

\forall\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{2},\;\exists c_{\alpha}>0,\;|\partial^{\alpha}w|\leqslant c_{\alpha}w
,

•

\exists c\geqslant 0,\;\exists C>0,\;\forall x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;0<w(x)<Cw(y)\langle x-y\rangle^{c}
.

If
w
is a weight, we define the weighted
\mathrm{L}^{p}
space as

\mathrm{L}^{p}_{w}=\{u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;wu\in\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\}=\mathrm{L}^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{2},w^{p}\mathrm{d}x)

endowed with the norm

|u|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{w}}=|wu|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

In the particular case where the weight is given by
w(x)=\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}
, we denote
\mathrm{L}^{2}_{\alpha}=\mathrm{L}^{2}_{w}
.

We now introduce Paley-Littlewood localization operators. Let
(\chi_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. We say
(\chi_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
is a dyadic partition of unity if

•

There exists a ball
B
with
\supp\chi_{-1}\subset B

•

There exists a ring
R
with
\supp\chi_{j}\subset 2^{j}R
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}

•

For all
j\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
\chi_{j}\chi_{-1}=0
and for all
i,j\in\mathbb{N}
,
|i-j|>1\Rightarrow\chi_{i}\chi_{j}=0

•

For all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
\sum_{j\geqslant-1}\chi_{j}(x)=1
.

We associate to a dyadic partition of unity
(\chi_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
, the family
(\Delta_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
of endomorphisms of
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
given by

\forall T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\forall j\geqslant-1,\;\mathcal{F}(\Delta_{j}T)=\chi_{j}\mathcal{F}T,

where
\mathcal{F}\in\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is the Fourier transform, defined on
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
by

\forall\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;\forall\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{2},\;(\mathcal{F}\phi)(\eta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\eta\cdot x}\phi(x)\frac{\mathrm{d}x}{2\pi}

and extended by duality. We call
(\Delta_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
the Paley-Littlewood decomposition associated to
(\chi_{j})_{j\geqslant-1}
.

Let
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
p,q\in[1,+\infty]
and
w
an admissible weight, the weighted Besov space
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}
is defined as

\left\{T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;|T|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}}<+\infty\right\}

with

|T|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}}=\left|2^{sj}|\Delta_{j}T|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{w}}\right|_{l^{q}_{j\geqslant-1}}.

It is well known that
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}
is a Banach space and does not depend on the choice of the dyadic partition of unity (see for example
A.1
below). In the special case
w=\textbf{1}
, we use the lighter notation
\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,\textbf{1}}
. Moreover, Theorem 6.5 in
[
35
]
implies

|T|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}}\approx|wT|_{\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}}

(2.5)

in the sense of norm equivalence. In particular,

\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q,w}=\left\{T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;wT\in\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\right\}.

When
p=q=+\infty
, we get weighted Hölder-Zygmund spaces
\mathcal{C}^{s}_{w}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{\infty,\infty,w}
and when
p=q=2
, we recover the standard weighted Sobolev spaces
\mathrm{H}^{s}_{w}=\mathrm{B}^{s}_{2,2,w}
. Likewise, for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
p\in(1,+\infty)
, we also define
\mathrm{H}^{s,p}_{w}
as

\mathrm{H}^{s,p}_{w}=\left\{T\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{2}),\;wT\in\mathrm{H}^{s,p}\right\}

endowed with the norm

|T|_{\mathrm{H}^{s,p}_{w}}=|wT|_{\mathrm{H}^{s,p}}.

As previously, we omit the subscript
w
when
w=\textbf{1}
. As for weighted
\mathrm{L}^{p}
spaces, we use the notation subscript
\alpha
when
w(x)=\langle x\rangle^{\alpha}
.

We easily deduce from
equation

2.5
and usual Besov embeddings (see Theorems 2.40. and 2.41. and Proposition 2.7.1 in
[
2
]
) the following weighted Besov embeddings.

.

One easily checks the Paley-Littlewood projectors are bounded over Besov spaces as follow

\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\;\forall p,q\in[1,+\infty],\;\forall j\geqslant-1,\;|\Delta_{j}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\right)}=1.

\Delta_{\leqslant N}=\sum_{j=-1}^{N}\Delta_{j}\text{ and }\Delta_{>N}=\sum_{j>N}\Delta_{j}=\operatorname{Id}-\Delta_{\leqslant N},

\forall s\in\mathbb{R},\;\forall p,q\in[1,+\infty],\;\forall N\in\mathbb{N},\;|\Delta_{\leqslant N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\right)}=|\Delta_{>N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{B}^{s}_{p,q}\right)}=1.

]

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\in[1,+\infty]
and
\nu>0
. Then, there exists

such that

|\Delta_{>N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\nu}\right)},|\Delta_{\leqslant N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\nu}\right)}\leqslant C.

Proof.

As
(\Delta_{>N}+\Delta_{\leqslant N})f=f
for any regular enough function

\Delta_{\leqslant N}
. Denote by
\chi_{j}\in\mathcal{C}^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
a partition of unity such that
\Delta_{j}=\chi_{j}(-\Delta)
(
j\geqslant-1

[

|\Delta_{\leqslant N}f|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\nu}}=|\overline{\psi_{N}}*f|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\nu}}\leqslant|\psi_{N}|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}_{\nu}}|f|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{\nu}},

.

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
p,q\in[1,+\infty]

|\Delta_{>N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q,w}\right)}\leqslant|\Delta_{>N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{p}_{w}\right)}\text{ and }|\Delta_{\leqslant N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{B}^{\alpha}_{p,q,w}\right)}\leqslant|\Delta_{\leqslant N}|_{\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{p}_{w}\right)}.

Let

. For

Proposition 3.4

Let

First,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}VY\rho^{2}|v|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant|\rho^{2}|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\left||x|^{1-\kappa}Y\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\left||x|^{\frac{1+\kappa}{2}}v\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}^{2}

3.2
The domain of -A

Inspired by the definition of

\forall s\in[0,2),\;\forall p\in(1,+\infty),\;\mathcal{D}^{s,p}=\rho\mathcal{W}^{s,p}\text{ and }\mathcal{D}^{-s,p}=(\mathcal{D}^{s,p^{\prime}})^{\prime},

Proposition 3.5
.

For any

\mathcal{D}^{s,2}=\mathrm{D}\left((-A)^{\frac{s}{2}}\right)

In particular,
\mathrm{D}(-A)
is dense in any

In the case of a white noise, where

characterises all the

-2

2

The bilinear form

\forall v_{1},v_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{1,2},\;b_{\Xi}(v_{1},v_{2})=a_{\Xi}(\rho v_{1},\rho v_{2})+\delta_{\Xi}(\rho v_{1},\rho v_{2})_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}.

\forall v\in\mathcal{W}^{1,2},\;-Bv=-\nabla\cdot(\rho^{2}\nabla v)+|x|^{2}(1-Y)\rho^{2}v-Z\rho^{2}v+\delta_{\Xi}\rho^{2}v\in\mathcal{W}^{-1,2}.

(3.6)

Recall that the domains of
-A
and

are defined, in view of Riesz theorem, by

\displaystyle\mathrm{D}(-A)

\displaystyle=\left\{u\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2},\;\exists C>0,\;\forall\phi\in\mathcal{D}^{1,2},\;\left|a_{\Xi}(u,\phi)\right|\leqslant C|\phi|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\right\}

and

\mathrm{D}(-B)=\left\{v\in\mathcal{W}^{1,2},\;\exists C>0,\;\forall\phi\in\mathcal{W}^{1,2},\;\left|b_{\Xi}(v,\phi)\right|\leqslant C|\phi|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\right\}.

We immediately deduce the following lemma, so we can obtain knowledge on elements of

Lemma 3.6
.

We have
\mathrm{D}(-A)=\rho\mathrm{D}(-B)
with equivalent norm.

For

,

Lemma 3.7
.

The linear map
B
restrains to an isomorphism from
\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa,2}
to
\mathcal{W}^{-\kappa,2}
and extends to an isomorphism from
\mathcal{W}^{\kappa,2}

Lemma 3.8

Let

First, for

4
Anderson-Gross-Pitaevskii equation

4.1
The linear equation

As
-A
is a positive self-adjoint operator on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, it is associated, by Stone’s theorem
[
31
]
, to a unitary group of operator
(\mathrm{e}^{itA})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
, called the propagator, such that for any
u^{0}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
u=\mathrm{e}^{itA}u^{0}\in\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
is the unique solution to

{~}\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}u+Au=0\\
u(0)=u^{0}\end{cases}

(4.1)

in
\mathcal{C}(\mathbb{R},\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))\cap\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{D}^{-2,2})
. Using the exponential transform, one has formally

A(\rho v)=\rho\left(Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+Zv-\delta_{\Xi}v\right)=\rho^{-1}Bv.

Using an approximation procedure, one can show this formula makes sense for any
u=\rho v
with
v\in\mathcal{W}^{1+\kappa,2}
. Thus, we define

\tilde{A}v=\rho^{-1}A(\rho v)=Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+Zv-\delta_{\Xi}v

(4.2)

on
\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})=\rho^{-1}\mathrm{D}(-A)=\mathrm{D}(-B)\subset\mathcal{W}^{1+\kappa,2}
by

and
3.7
, as
\kappa<\frac{1}{2}
. The operator
\tilde{A}
is a closed operator because

is closed, but it is no longer self-adjoint on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Moreover, the norm induced by
-B
and by
-\tilde{A}
on
\mathrm{D}(-B)=\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})
are equivalent.

Remark that, by definition,
u\in\mathcal{C}(I,\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}))
solves
equation

4.1
if and only if
v=\rho^{-1}u
solves

{~}\begin{cases}\mathrm{i}\partial_{t}v+\tilde{A}v=0\\
v(0)=v^{0}=\rho^{-1}u^{0}.\end{cases}

(4.3)

Moreover, the propagator associated to
\tilde{A}
verify

\forall t\in\mathbb{R},\;\mathrm{e}^{it\tilde{A}}=\rho^{-1}\mathrm{e}^{itA}\rho.

(4.4)

Thus, one can study
equation

4.1
through
equation

4.3
and vice versa.

4.2
Strichartz inequalities

For
v\in\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})

. Moreover,
3.2

and

[
1
,
27
,
29
]

\tilde{A}v=Hv+2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+xY\cdot xv+Zv-\delta_{\Xi}v.

By
3.2
,

and thus

for any

. Recall

imply

cannot be in

as

and

2.8
, we get

2\nabla Y\cdot\nabla v+Zv=P_{\nabla v}(2\nabla Y)+P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+P_{v}Z+P_{Z}v+R(Z,v)

and
2.8
and
2.13
together with
2.15
imply
Hv
,
P_{\nabla v}(2\nabla Y)
and
P_{v}Z
are the only singular terms of
\tilde{A}v
when
v\in\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa,2}\subset\mathrm{H}^{2-\kappa}
, and all have the same regularity
\mathcal{W}^{-\kappa,2}
, by
2.1
. Hence, for an element
v
of the domain
\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})\subset\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa,2}
,
Hv+P_{\nabla v}(2\nabla Y)+P_{v}Z\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
. Thus if
v\in\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})
,
v
should have a particular structure such that the singularity of
Hv
compensates exactly that of
P_{\nabla v}(2\nabla Y)+P_{v}Z
. This is where the idea of paracontrolled functions appears. We are looking for functions in the domain of
\tilde{A}
satisfying the paracontrolled ansatz

v=v^{\sharp}+P_{\nabla v}X_{1}+P_{v}X_{2},

(4.5)

for
v^{\sharp}\in\mathrm{D}(-H)=\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
and functions
X_{1},X_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
to determine so that
HP_{\nabla v}X_{1}
and
HP_{v}X_{2}
compensates the singular parts of
2P_{\nabla v}\nabla Y
and
P_{v}Z
respectively (see for example
[
26
]
for a similar ansatz in the context of white noise magnetic field on the torus). Inserting the paracontrolled ansatz
equation

4.5
in
equation

4.2
and applying Bony’s decomposition
equation

2.8
, lead to

\displaystyle\tilde{A}v

\displaystyle=Hv^{\sharp}+[H,P_{\nabla v}]X_{1}+P_{\nabla v}\left(HX_{1}\right)+[H,P_{v}]X_{2}+P_{v}\left(HX_{2}\right)-\delta_{\Xi}v

\displaystyle+xY\cdot xv+2\left(P_{\nabla v}\nabla Y+P_{\nabla Y}\nabla v+R(\nabla Y,\nabla v)\right)+P_{v}Z+P_{Z}v+R(v,Z)

\displaystyle=Hv^{\sharp}+[H,P_{\nabla v}]X_{1}+[H,P_{v}]X_{2}+P_{\nabla v}\left(HX_{1}+2\nabla Y\right)+P_{v}\left(HX_{2}+Z\right)-\delta_{\Xi}v

\displaystyle+xY\cdot xv+P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+P_{Z}v+R(v,Z).

We may choose
X_{1}=2(-H)^{-1}\nabla Y\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
and
X_{2}=(-H)^{-1}Z\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
in order to obtain cancellations. A natural question is then, for a fixed
v^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
, does-it exist a
v\in D(\tilde{A}
) satisfying
equation

4.5
?
We stress out that, in general, the map
v\mapsto v^{\sharp}=v-P_{\nabla v}X_{1}-P_{v}X_{2}
may not be surjective. Thus we use a truncated paracontrolled ansatz

v=v^{\sharp}+P^{N}_{\nabla v}X_{1}+P^{N}_{v}X_{2},

(4.6)

for some large
N
, where
P^{N}
is a truncated paraproduct defined by
equation

2.9
. This leads to a new paracontrolled formula for
\tilde{A}v
, namely

\begin{split}\tilde{A}v&=Hv^{\sharp}+[H,P_{\nabla v}^{N}]X_{1}+[H,P_{v}^{N}]X_{2}+xY\cdot xv-\delta_{\Xi}v+P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v\\
&+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+P_{Z}v+R(v,Z)+2P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}\nabla Y)+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}Z),\end{split}

(4.7)

where appears the term
2P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}\nabla Y)+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}Z)
involving only low frequencies of the singular terms
\nabla Y
and
Z
, thus being smooth. Let us stress out that
v^{\sharp}
depends implicitly of
N
. Using
2.11
and
2.12
, the following lemma shows we can invert the map
v\mapsto v-P_{\nabla v}^{N}X_{1}-P_{v}^{N}X_{2}
for
N
large enough.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\sigma\in[0,2-\kappa)
and
p\in[2,+\infty)
, there exists
N_{\sigma,p}=N(\Xi,V,\sigma,p)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any
N\geqslant N_{\sigma,p}
, the map

\Phi_{N}:v\in\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}\mapsto v-P_{\nabla v}^{N}X_{1}-P_{v}^{N}X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}

is invertible of continuous inverse.

Proof.

As
\Phi_{N}
is a perturbation of the identity, by Neumann series theorem, it is sufficient to show

\left|P_{\nabla v}^{N}X_{1}+P_{v}^{N}X_{2}\right|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}=\left|P_{\nabla v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{1}\right)+P_{v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}\leqslant C_{N}|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}

with
C_{N}<1
for
N
large enough. Let
\varepsilon>0
small enough. For
\sigma=0
,
2.13
imply

\left|P_{\nabla v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{1}\right)+P_{v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim|\nabla v|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1,p}}|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\varepsilon}}+|v|_{\mathrm{H}^{-1,p}}|\Delta_{>N}X_{2}|_{\mathcal{C}^{1+\varepsilon}}.

2.12
then ensures

\left|P_{\nabla v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{1}\right)+P_{v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}}\lesssim 2^{-(1-\kappa-\varepsilon)N}\left(|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}}+|\Delta_{>N}X_{2}|_{\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}}\right)|v|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}}.

Now assume
\sigma\in(1,2-\kappa)
and let
\varepsilon\in(0,2-\kappa-\sigma)
.
2.14
implies

\left|P_{v}^{N}X_{2}\right|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}|\Delta_{>N}X_{2}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\sigma+\varepsilon}}

and

\left|P_{\nabla v}^{N}X_{1}\right|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim|\nabla v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma-1,p}}\left(|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\sigma+\varepsilon}}+|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{1}}\right).

Remark
2.7
and
2.10
imply
xX_{1}\in\mathcal{W}^{1-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{1-\kappa}\subset\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}
. Hence, by
2.6
and
2.12
, there exists
\delta>0
such that

\left|P_{\nabla v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{1}\right)+P_{v}\left(\Delta_{>N}X_{2}\right)\right|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}}\lesssim C(\Xi)2^{-\delta N}|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,p}},

(4.8)

where

C(\Xi)=|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}}+|\Delta_{>N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{1-\kappa}_{1}}+|\Delta_{>N}X_{2}|_{\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}}.

Finally, by interpolation, for any
\sigma\in[0,2-\kappa)
, there exists
\delta>0
such that
equation

4.8
holds.

∎

In the following, we choose
N
large enough for our computations. We stress out that this
N
may change when needed, leading to a family of paracontrolled ansatz

v=v_{N}^{\sharp}+P^{N}_{\nabla v}X_{1}+P_{v}^{N}X_{2}.

Using this paracontrolled ansatz, one can write

\tilde{A}v=Hv_{N}^{\sharp}+R_{N}(v)

where
R_{N}(v)
is given by

\begin{split}R_{N}(v)&=[H,P_{\nabla v}^{N}]X_{1}+[H,P_{v}^{N}]X_{2}+xY\cdot xv+P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)\\
&+P_{Z}v+R(v,Z)+2P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}\nabla Y)+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}Z)-\delta_{\Xi}v\end{split}

(4.9)

according to
equation

4.7
. Remark
|x|^{2}X_{1}=\Delta X_{1}+2\nabla Y
. By
3.2
,
\nabla Y\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}
and moreover,
X_{1}\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
, thus, we deduce
|x|^{2}X_{1}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}
. Similarly,
X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
but
-HX_{2}=Z\in\mathcal{W}^{-s,q}
may not be in
\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}
due to
2.1
. Instead, one can use
2.1
to show there exists
Z_{r}\in W^{-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}
and
Z_{l}\in\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
such that
Z=Z_{r}+|x|^{s}Z_{l}
. This decomposition of
Z
as a sum of a term lacking regularity
Z_{r}
and a term lacking of localization
|x|^{s}Z_{l}
is somehow similar to the decomposition used in
[
18
]
. It implies

X_{2}=(-H)^{-1}Z_{r}+(-H)^{-1}|x|^{s}Z_{l}.

Remark
Z_{r}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}
and
(-H)^{-1}Z_{r}\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
, thus
(-H)^{-1}Z_{r}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}_{2}
. Now using
\langle x\rangle^{2}(-H)^{-1}|x|^{s}Z_{l}=(1+\Delta)(-H)^{-1}|x|^{s}Z_{l}+|x|^{s}Z_{l}
and
(-H)^{-1}|x|^{s}Z_{l}\in\mathcal{W}^{2-s,q}\subset\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}
, it follows
X_{2}
can be decomposed as the sum of a term in
\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}_{2}
and a term in
\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}_{2-s}
. Namely, if we define

X_{2,r}=(-H)^{-1}Z_{r}+\langle x\rangle^{-2}(1+\Delta)(-H)^{-1}|x|^{s}Z_{l}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}_{2}\text{ and }X_{2,l}=|x|^{s-2}Z_{l}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}_{2-s},

(4.10)

using the Besov embedding
\mathrm{L}^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\subset\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}
as
\kappa-s>\frac{2}{q}
, then
X_{2}=X_{2,r}+X_{2,l}
. From this, we obtain a paracontrolled formulation on the domain
\mathrm{D}(\tilde{A})
and thus a complete caracterization of
\mathrm{D}(A)
thanks to
3.6
.

Corollary 4.2
.

The vector space
\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
does not depend on
N
and is equal to

\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})=\left\{v\in\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa-,2},\;v-P_{\nabla v}X_{1}-P_{v}X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\right\}.
In particular, for any
v
in
\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})
,
\langle x\rangle^{2}v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, it holds

\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa-,2},\;v-P_{\nabla v}^{N}X_{1}-P_{v}^{N}X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\right\}.

In order to show

\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa-,2},\;v-P_{\nabla v}X_{1}-P_{v}X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\right\},

it is sufficient to show
P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1})+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2})\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,1-\kappa)
,
2.13
and
2.15
imply

|P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1})|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}_{2}}\lesssim|\nabla v|_{\mathrm{H}^{-\varepsilon}}|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}\lesssim|v|_{\mathrm{H}^{1}}|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}

and

\displaystyle|P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2})|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}_{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|v|_{\mathrm{H}^{-\varepsilon}}|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,r}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}+|v|_{\mathrm{H}^{-\varepsilon}_{s}}|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,l}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{(2-s)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left(|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,r}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}+|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,l}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{(2-s)}}\right)|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,2}},

using
2.7
and
s<\kappa<1
. As
X_{1},X_{2,r}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}_{2}
and
X_{2,l}\in\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}_{2-s}
(see
equation

4.10
),
2.12
implies

|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}+|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,r}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{2}}+|\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2,r}|_{\mathcal{C}^{\varepsilon}_{(2-s)}}<+\infty,

thus

|P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1})+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2})|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}_{2}}\lesssim_{N,\Xi,V,\varepsilon}|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{1,2}}.

Moreover,
P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1})+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2})\in\mathrm{H}^{2}
as
v,\nabla v\in\mathcal{W}^{1-\kappa-,2}
and
\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1},\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2}\in\mathcal{C}^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha>0
. Thus
P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{1})+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}X_{2})\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
and

\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}=\left\{v\in\mathcal{W}^{2-\kappa-,2},\;v-P_{\nabla v}X_{1}-P_{v}X_{2}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\right\}

does not depend of N.

We now fix
\sigma\in(1+\kappa,2-\kappa)
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
large enough so that
\Phi_{N}
is invertible on
\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
, by
4.1
. Let
v^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\subset\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
and
v=\Phi_{N}^{-1}v^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
, it holds

\tilde{A}v=Hv^{\sharp}+R_{N}(v),

with
R_{N}(v)
given by
equation

4.9
. For
v\in\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
, as
\sigma>1+\kappa
, it holds

[H,P_{\nabla v}^{N}]X_{1}+[H,P_{v}^{N}]X_{2}\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

by
2.16
,

P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+P_{Z}v+R(v,Z)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

by
equations

4.10
and
2.14
,

xY\cdot xv\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

by
2.7
as
|x|^{1-\kappa}Y\in\mathrm{L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, and

2P_{\nabla v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}\nabla Y)+P_{v}(\Delta_{\leqslant N}Z)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

by
2.13
,
2.11
and
2.12
. Thus
R_{N}(v)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\tilde{A}v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).
It implies
\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}\subset\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A}).

Conversely, let
v\in\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A})
and set
v^{\sharp}=\Phi_{N}v
. By
3.6
,
3.7
and
4.1
,
v^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
and it holds

-Hv^{\sharp}=-\tilde{A}v+R_{N}(v).

As previously,
R_{N}(v)\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
-\tilde{A}v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
as

. Hence

and
\Phi_{N}^{-1}\mathcal{W}^{2,2}=\mathrm{D}(-\tilde{A}).

∎

Thus, for any
v^{\sharp}\in\mathcal{W}^{2,2}
, it holds for
N
large enough,

\tilde{A}\Phi_{N}^{-1}v^{\sharp}-Hv^{\sharp}=R_{N}(\Phi_{N}^{-1}v^{\sharp})

and
R_{N}(\Phi_{N}^{-1}v^{\sharp})
can be seen as a lower order perturbation. Estimating
R_{N}(v)
in
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
for
\sigma>0
will be needed in order to deal with estimates of the form

|\mathrm{e}^{it:H+\xi:}|_{\mathrm{L}^{p}_{T}\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,r}_{x}}

with
\alpha>0
in

. However, when estimating

in
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}
with

, the term

behaves badly. Namely

|xY\cdot xv|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}\lesssim||x|^{1-\kappa-\sigma}Y|_{W^{\sigma,\infty}}||x|^{1+\kappa+\sigma}v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}\lesssim|Y|_{\mathcal{W}^{1-s,q}}|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{1+\kappa+2\sigma,2}}

using
equations

3.2
and
2.7
. As
4.1
controls
|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2}}
by
|v^{\sharp}|_{\mathcal{W}^{\alpha,2}}
only for

, we are limited to

, that is
\sigma<\frac{1}{2}-\kappa
. Thus, we will modify

, in order to control the term

. Remark
|x|^{2}

(4.11)

. Adapting the proof of

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
\sigma\in[0,2-\kappa)
,
p\in[2,+\infty)

where

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{N}(v)&=\left[H,P_{\nabla v}^{N}\right]X_{1}+\left[H,P_{v}^{N}\right]X_{2}+\left[H,P_{xv}^{N}\right]X_{3}+P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v\\
&+P_{Z}v+P_{xY}xv+R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+R(v,Z)+R(xv,xY)\\

(4.14)

We can now prove the following estimate on

.

Lemma 4.4

Let

and

2.6

\displaystyle C_{0}(\Xi)

\displaystyle+|X_{2,l}|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}_{2-s}}+|X_{3}|_{\mathcal{C}^{2-\kappa}}+|X_{3,r}|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}_{2}}+|X_{3,l}|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa+s}_{2-s}},

and

4.10
and

being defined by

4.11

\displaystyle|P_{2\nabla Y}\nabla v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|\nabla Y|_{\mathcal{C}^{-\kappa}}|\nabla v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma+\kappa,2}}

\displaystyle|P_{Z_{r}}v|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}

(4.16)

|R(2\nabla Y,\nabla v)+R(v,Z)+R(xv,xY)|_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma,2}}\lesssim_{\Xi,\sigma}|v|_{\mathcal{W}^{1+\sigma+\kappa,2}}.

Moreover, using the decomposition

,

,

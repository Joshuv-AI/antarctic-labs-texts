On the asymptotics of real solutions for the Painlevé I equation
Wen-Gao Long, Jun Xia

Abstract
In this paper, we revisit the asymptotic formulas of real Painlevé I transcendents as the independent variable tends to negative infinity, which were initially derived by Kapaev with the complex WKB method. Using the Riemann-Hilbert method, we improve the error estimates of the oscillatory type asymptotics and provide precise error estimates of the singular type asymptotics. We also establish the corresponding asymptotics for the associated Hamiltonians of real Painlevé I transcendents. In addition, two typos in the mentioned asymptotic behaviors in literature are corrected. 2010 mathematics subject classification: 33E17; 34A30; 34E05; 34M55; 41A60 Keywords and phrases: The Painlevé I equation, asymptotic expansions, Riemann-Hilbert approach.

Introduction

Painlevé equations are a group of six nonlinear second-order ordinary differential equations
that possess the Painlevé property. This property requires that the movable singularities of the solutions must be poles, not branch points or essential singularities.
In general, the solutions of these equations do not have explicit expressions. Consequently,
asymptotic analysis is a common tool to study the behavior of solutions for Painlevé equations.

In this paper, we are concerned with the Painlevé I equation

y^{\prime\prime}(x)=6y^{2}(x)+x,

(1.1)

and we concentrate on the asymptotic behaviors as
x\to-\infty
of real solutions to (
1.1
).
Using the method of dominant balance (cf.
[
1
, Chapter 3]
), it is easy to see that there are two classes of behaviors

\displaystyle y(x)\sim\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},\qquad or\qquad y(x)\sim-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(1.2)

as
x\to-\infty
. For the second case, one can further show that as
x\to-\infty
,

\displaystyle y(x)=-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{d}{(-x)^{\frac{1}{8}}}\cos\left(\frac{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}-\frac{5d^{2}}{8}\ln(-x)+\chi\right)+O(x^{-\frac{5}{8}}),

(1.3)

where
d
and
\chi
are constants; see
[
10
,
19
]
. It should be noted that the asymptotic formula (
1.3
) also appears in the NIST handbook of mathematical functions (see
[
18
, Eq. 32.11.1]
).

Indeed, Holmes and Spence
[
9
]
proved that there exist exactly three types
of real solutions by studying
a boundary value problem for (
1.1
). Subsequently, with the help of the isomonodromy approach
[
7
]
, Kapaev
[
11
]
obtained the following classification of asymptotic behaviors as
x\to-\infty
of real Painlevé I transcendents, in terms of the Stokes multiplier
s_{2}
(see Section
2
for definition).

(A)

When
|s_{2}|<1
, we have

\displaystyle y(x)\sim-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{d}{(-24x)^{\frac{1}{8}}}\cos\left(\frac{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}-\frac{5d^{2}}{8}\ln(-x)+\chi\right),

(1.4)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} d^{2}&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln(1-|s_{2}|^{2}),\\
\chi&=\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits s_{2}-\left(\frac{19}{8}\ln 2+\frac{5}{8}\ln 3\right)d^{2}-\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\Gamma\left(-\frac{id^{2}}{2}\right)+\frac{3\pi}{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.5)

(B)

When
|s_{2}|=1
, we have

\displaystyle y(x)=\widehat{y}(x)+\frac{s_{1}-s_{-1}}{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}\sqrt{\pi}}(-x)^{-\frac{1}{8}}e^{-\frac{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}}\left[1+O(x^{-\frac{5}{4}})\right],

(1.6)

where
\widehat{y}(x)=(-\frac{x}{6})^{\frac{1}{2}}\left[1+O(x^{-\frac{5}{2}})\right]
as
x\to-\infty
is the solution to (
1.1
) with the Stokes multipliers
s_{2}=s_{-2}=i,~s_{-1}=s_{1}=\frac{i}{2}
.

(C)

When
|s_{2}|>1
, we have

\displaystyle y(x)\sim-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}+\frac{5q}{8}\ln(-x)+\rho\right)},

(1.7)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} \rho&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(|s_{2}|^{2}-1),\\
\sigma&=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits s_{2}+\left(\frac{19}{8}\ln 2+\frac{5}{8}\ln 3\right)\rho+\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-i\rho\right)-\frac{\pi}{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.8)

The type (A), (B) and (C) solutions are called oscillatory, separatrix and singular solutions, respectively. In
[
12
]
, with the Riemann-Hilbert approach, Kapaev found all separatrix (also known as
tronquée
) solutions of (
1.1
). Recently, Deaño
[
3
]
extends Kapaev’s results by calculating explicitly exponentially small corrections with respect to the tronquée solutions. However, to the best of our knowledge, the error estimate in asymptotic formula (
1.7
) and the asymptotics for the Hamiltonians in case (A) and (C) have not been provided in the existing literature.

In this paper, by performing a Deift-Zhou steepest descent analysis
[
4
,
5
,
6
]
to the Riemann-Hilbert problem of (
1.1
), we obtain the following asymptotics for the Painlevé I transcendent
y(x)
and its Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}(x)
(see (
2.5
) for definition).

Theorem 1.1
.

If
|s_{2}|<1
, we have, as
x\to-\infty
,

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{\sqrt{a}}{(-24x)^{\frac{1}{8}}}\cos\left(\frac{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}-\frac{5a}{8}\ln(-x)+\phi\right)+O(x^{-\frac{3}{4}}),

(1.9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)

\displaystyle=-4\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}+a\left(-\frac{3x}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{\sqrt{a}}{(-24x)^{\frac{3}{8}}}\sin\left(\frac{4\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}-\frac{5a}{8}\ln(-x)+\phi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+O(x^{-1}),

(1.10)

where

\left\{\begin{aligned} a&=-\frac{1}{\pi}\ln(1-|s_{2}|^{2}),\\
\phi&=\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits s_{2}-\left(\frac{19}{8}\ln 2+\frac{5}{8}\ln 3\right)a-\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\Gamma\left(-\frac{ia}{2}\right)-\frac{\pi}{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.11)

If
|s_{2}|>1
, we have, as
x\to-\infty
,

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=-\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}+\frac{(-x)^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{\sqrt{6}}{3}\sin^{2}\left(\frac{2\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}+\frac{5b}{8}\ln(-x)+\psi\right)}+O(x^{-\frac{3}{4}}),

(1.12)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)

\displaystyle=-4\left(-\frac{x}{6}\right)^{\frac{3}{2}}-b\left(-24x\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}-\left(-\frac{3x}{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{4}}\cot\left(\frac{2\cdot 24^{\frac{1}{4}}}{5}(-x)^{\frac{5}{4}}+\frac{5b}{8}\ln(-x)+\psi\right)

\displaystyle\qquad+O(x^{-1}),

(1.13)

where the error terms hold uniformly for
x
bounded away from the singularities appearing in the denominator and

\left\{\begin{aligned} b&=\frac{1}{2\pi}\ln(|s_{2}|^{2}-1),\\
\psi&=\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits s_{2}+\left(\frac{19}{8}\ln 2+\frac{5}{8}\ln 3\right)b+\frac{1}{2}\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-ib\right)+\frac{\pi}{4}.\end{aligned}\right.

(1.14)

2
Riemann-Hilbert problem

In this section, we describe the Riemann-Hilbert (RH) problem of the Painlevé I equation (
1.1
), which was first introduced in
[
12
]
.
We seek a
2\times 2
matrix-valued function
\Psi(z,x)
satisfying the following properties.

(a)

\Psi(z,x)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
, where
\Gamma=\cup_{k=-2}^{2}\Gamma_{k}
with

\displaystyle\Gamma_{0}=\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\Gamma_{3}=e^{\pi i}\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\Gamma_{\pm 1}=e^{\pm\frac{2\pi i}{5}}\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\Gamma_{\pm 2}=e^{\pm\frac{4\pi i}{5}}\mathbb{R}_{+},

see Figure
5
for an illustration.

(b)

\Psi(z,x)
satisfies the jump relation

\displaystyle\Psi_{+}(z,x)=\Psi_{-}(z,x)S_{k},\ \ \ \ \ z\in\Gamma_{k},

(2.1)

where the Stokes matrices
\{S_{k}\}
take the forms

\displaystyle S_{0}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
s_{0}&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ \ S_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&s_{1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ S_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
s_{2}&1\end{pmatrix},

(2.2)

\displaystyle S_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
-i&0\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ S_{-1}=\begin{pmatrix}1&s_{-1}\\
0&1\end{pmatrix},\ \ \ \ S_{-2}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
s_{-2}&1\end{pmatrix}.

Here, the Stokes multipliers
\{s_{k}\}
satisfy the restricted conditions

\displaystyle 1+s_{k}s_{k+1}=-is_{k+3},\qquad s_{k+5}=s_{k},\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(2.3)

(c)

\Psi(z;x)
satisfies the following asymptotic behavior as
z\to\infty

\displaystyle\Psi(z,x)=z^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}\left[I+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Psi_{k}(x)z^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right]e^{(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}+xz^{\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{3}},

(2.4)

where the branches of powers of
z
are chosen such that
\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits z\in(-\pi,\pi)
.

Figure 5:
The jump contour
\Gamma
.

It should be mentioned that in the RH analysis throughout, we will use frequently the matrix notations

\sigma_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-i\\
i&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\sigma_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{pmatrix}.

From
[
12
, Equations (2.13)-(2.15)]
, we obtain the following expressions for the Painlevé I transcendent
y(x)
and the associated Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)=\frac{1}{2}[y^{\prime}(x)]^{2}-2y(x)^{3}-xy(x).

(2.5)

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
\Psi_{k}:=\Psi_{k}(x)
,
k=1,2
be the coefficient in the expansion (
2.4
). We have

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}-\mathcal{H}(x)&0\\
0&\mathcal{H}(x)\end{pmatrix},\qquad\Psi_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{H}(x)^{2}&y(x)\\
y(x)&\mathcal{H}(x)^{2}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.6)

Hence, we have

\displaystyle y(x)

\displaystyle=2\Psi_{2,12}=2\Psi_{2,21},

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(x)

\displaystyle=-\Psi_{1,11}=\Psi_{1,22},

(2.8)

where
\Psi_{k,ij}
denotes the
(i,j)
entry of
\Psi_{k}
.

By (
2.3
), it follows that

\displaystyle 1+s_{2}s_{-2}=-is_{0},\qquad s_{1}=\frac{i-s_{-2}}{1+s_{2}s_{-2}},\qquad s_{-1}=\frac{i-s_{2}}{1+s_{2}s_{-2}},

(2.9)

if
1+s_{2}s_{-2}\neq 0
. For the real solutions of the Painlevé I equation, we further have (see
[
11
]
)

\displaystyle\bar{s}_{2}=-s_{-2},

(2.10)

which implies from (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\bar{s}_{0}=-s_{0},\qquad\bar{s}_{1}=-s_{-1},

(2.11)

and

\displaystyle-is_{0}=1+s_{2}s_{-2}=1-|s_{2}|^{2}=1-|s_{-2}|^{2}\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.12)

Asymptotics of

s_{2}=0

3.1
Scaling transformation

Assume in what follows that
x<0
. We define

\displaystyle A(z)=|x|^{-\frac{\sigma_{3}}{8}}\Psi(|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}z,x).

(3.1)

Then,

satisfies the following RH problem.

(a)

A(z)
is analytic for
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\Gamma
.

(b)

; see (

(c)

As

, we have

\displaystyle A(z)=z^{\frac{\sigma_{3}}{4}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{pmatrix}\left[I+\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Psi_{k}(x)(|x|^{\frac{1}{2}}z)^{-\frac{k}{2}}\right]e^{|x|^{\frac{5}{4}}\theta(z)\sigma_{3}},

\hat{\Psi}^{(k)}(z,x)=\frac{z^{-\frac{3}{4}\sigma_{3}}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}1&-1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\left(I+O(z^{-\frac{1}{2}})\right)e^{(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}+xz^{\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{3}}

(B.6)

as
z\rightarrow\infty
with
z\in\Omega_{k}
.
The asymptotic expansions of
\Psi^{(k)}(z,x)
and
\hat{\Psi}^{(k)}(z,x)
in (
B.2
) and (
B.6
) are valid
only when
x
is bounded away from
p
.
When
x\to p
, according to
[
2
]
,
the system (
B.5
) reduces to

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\hat{\Psi}(z,p)=\begin{pmatrix}0&2\\
2z^{3}+pz-14H&0\end{pmatrix}\hat{\Psi}(z,p),

(B.7)

and the corresponding asymptotic expansions of
\hat{\Psi}_{k}(z,p)
in (
B.6
) should be replaced by

\hat{\Psi}^{(k)}(z,p)=\frac{z^{-\frac{3}{4}\sigma_{3}}}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{pmatrix}-i&-i\\
-i&i\end{pmatrix}\left(I+O\left(z^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\right)e^{(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}+pz^{\frac{1}{2}})\sigma_{3}}

(B.8)

as
z\rightarrow\infty
with
z\in\Omega_{k}
; see
[
2
, Corollary A.8]
. It is readily seen that
\hat{\Psi}^{(k)}
also satisfy the connection formula

\hat{\Psi}^{(k+1)}(z,p)=\hat{\Psi}^{(k)}(z,p)S_{k},k\in\mathbb{Z}.

(B.9)

Finally, if denoting

\hat{\Psi}(z,p)=\begin{pmatrix}\phi_{1}\\
\phi_{2}\end{pmatrix},

and letting
Y(z;p)=\phi_{1}
, then we arrive at the following reduced triconfluent Heun equation (RTHE); see
[
20
, Eq. (6)]

\frac{d^{2}Y}{dz^{2}}=\left[4z^{3}+2pz-28H\right]Y.

(B.10)

When
(p,H)=(0,0)
, the equation (
B.10
) can be explicitly solved by the Bessel functions. For any solution of (
B.10
), there exist two constants
C_{1}
and
C_{1}
such that

Y(z)=C_{1}z^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right)+C_{2}z^{\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right).

(B.11)

Applying the asymptotic behaivor of the Bessel functions
I_{\nu}(z)
and
K_{\nu}(z)
(cf. see
[
18
, Eqs. 10.40.1, 10.40.2]
) and matching them with the behaviors of
(\hat{\Psi}^{(k)})_{11}
and
(\hat{\Psi}^{(k)})_{12}
in (
B.8
), we have

\begin{split}\left(z^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right),z^{\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right)\right)&\sim((\Psi^{(0)})_{11},(\Psi^{(0)})_{12})\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}&0\\
c_{2}&c_{3}\end{matrix}\right)\end{split}

(B.12)

as
z\to\infty
with
\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits{z}\sim-\frac{\pi}{5}
, where

c_{1}=\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}i,\quad c_{2}=\left(\frac{4\pi}{5}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}e^{-\frac{\pi i}{5}},\quad c_{3}=\left(\frac{4}{5\pi}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}i.

(B.13)

Similarly when
z\to\infty
with
\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits{z}\sim\frac{\pi}{5}
, we have

\begin{split}\left(z^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right),z^{\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right)\right)&\sim((\Psi^{(1)})_{11},(\Psi^{(1)})_{12})\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}&0\\
\tilde{c}_{2}&c_{3}\end{matrix}\right),\end{split}

(B.14)

where
\tilde{c}_{2}=-e^{\frac{2\pi i}{5}}c_{2}=ie^{\frac{\pi i}{5}}c_{1}
.
Hence, according to the connection formula (
B.9
), we conclude that

S_{0}=\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}&0\\
c_{2}&c_{3}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}&0\\
\tilde{c}_{2}&c_{3}\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&0\\
\frac{c_{2}-\tilde{c}_{2}}{c_{1}}&1\end{matrix}\right),

(B.15)

which implies that
s_{0}=\frac{c_{2}-\tilde{c}_{2}}{c_{1}}=-2i\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{5}\right)
.

Combining
[
18
, Eqs. 10.40.1, 10.40.2]
with the connection formulas between
I_{\nu}(z)
and
K_{\nu}(z)
(cf. see
[
18
, Eqs. 10.34.1, 10.34.2]
), we can further obtain

\begin{split}\left(z^{\frac{1}{2}}I_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right),z^{\frac{1}{2}}K_{\frac{1}{5}}\left(\frac{4}{5}z^{\frac{5}{2}}\right)\right)\sim\left((\Psi^{(2)})_{11},(\Psi^{(2)})_{12}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}d_{1}&d_{3}\\
d_{2}&d_{4}\end{matrix}\right),\end{split}

(B.16)

as
z\to\infty
with
\mathop{\rm arg}\nolimits{z}\sim\frac{3\pi}{5}
,
where

d_{1}=ie^{\frac{\pi i}{5}}\tilde{c}_{2}=-c_{1},\quad d_{2}=ie^{\frac{\pi i}{5}}c_{1}=-c_{2},\quad d_{3}=e^{-\frac{\pi i}{5}}ic_{3}+\pi\tilde{c}_{2},\quad d_{4}=\pi c_{1}=c_{3}.

(B.17)

Comparing (
B.14
) and (
B.16
), it is readily seen that

S_{1}=\left(\begin{matrix}c_{1}&0\\
\tilde{c}_{2}&c_{3}\end{matrix}\right)\left(\begin{matrix}d_{1}&d_{3}\\
d_{2}&d_{4}\end{matrix}\right)^{-1}=\left(\begin{matrix}1&-c_{1}d_{3}\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right).

(B.18)

This implies that
s_{1}=-c_{1}d_{3}=-2i\cos{\left(\frac{\pi}{5}\right)}
.

Finally, making use of (
2.3
), we find that
s_{k}=-2i\cos\left(\frac{\pi}{5}\right)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

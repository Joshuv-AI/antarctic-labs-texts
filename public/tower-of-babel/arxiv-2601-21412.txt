Leaders in multi-type TASEP
Alexei Borodin, Alexey Bufetov

Abstract
. We study the totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) on ℤ \mathbb{Z} with step initial condition, in which all particles have distinct types. Our main object of interest is the type of the rightmost particle — the leader — at large time t t . We prove a central limit theorem for this random variable. Somewhat unexpectedly, the problem is closely connected to certain observables of voter and coalescing processes on ℤ \mathbb{Z} ; we therefore derive their asymptotics as well. We also analyze the large-time behavior of a few other related observables, including certain multi-particle ones.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

The main object of study in this paper is the multi-type Totally Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (TASEP). This is an interacting particle system on
\mathbb{Z}
whose states can be viewed as maps
\eta:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
. The value
\eta(x)=c\in\mathbb{Z}
is interpreted as a particle of type
c
occupying position
x
, while
\eta(y)=-\infty
indicates the absence of a particle at location
y
, i.e., a hole.

The continuous-time Markov evolution
(\eta_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is defined as follows. Each ordered pair of neighboring sites in
\mathbb{Z}
is equipped with an independent exponential clock of rate
1
. When the clock associated with
(x,x+1)
rings, we swap the values at
x
and
x+1
if they are in strictly decreasing order, and do nothing otherwise.

We are primarily interested in the initial configuration

\eta_{0}(x)=-x,\qquad x\leqslant 0,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\eta_{0}(x)=-\infty,\qquad x>0,

which is a multi-type version of the step initial condition, see Figure
1
for an example. Since particles of larger type have higher priority, the type of the right-most particle (the
leader
) is nondecreasing under the dynamics. Let
L_{1}(t)
denote the type of the leader at time
t
. One of our main results is the following theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.1
below)
.

We have

\frac{L_{1}(t)}{\sqrt{t}}\xrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\pi}}\exp\left(\frac{-a^{2}}{4}\right)\mathbf{1}_{a\geqslant 0}\,da,\qquad\mbox{in distribution}.

Let
\mathcal{S}(t)
be the number of changes of the leader’s type up to time
t
. We establish the asymptotic behavior of its expectation.

Theorem 1.2

(Theorem
5.6
below)
.

We have

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}\left(\mathcal{S}(t)\right)}{\ln(t)}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}.

In addition, we prove several other asymptotic results for related quantities:

•

In Theorem
5.2
we establish the joint limiting distribution of the position of the leader and its type.

•

In Theorem
5.4
we find the joint limiting distribution of the types of the
two
leading (right-most) particles.

•

In Section
6
we prove an equality in distribution (a duality) between the leader’s type and certain observables of the voter process on
\mathbb{Z}
. This allows us to translate the asymptotic results above to the voter-process setting; see, for instance, Corollaries
6.6
and
6.7
.

•

In Section
7
we introduce a new natural process — the
TASEP ranking process
— whose behavior is closely related to the asymptotic behavior of leaders; see Proposition
7.2
.

Our approach is based on explicit formulas for
q
-moments of the stochastic six-vertex model (see Sections
2
and
3
) and their

degenerations.

1.2.
Background

The totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP) is a prototypical interacting particle system on
\mathbb{Z}
, originating in the classical work of Spitzer
[
Spi
]
. Since the early 1970s, TASEP and its variants have been studied extensively from probabilistic, hydrodynamic, and integrable perspectives; we refer to the monographs
[
Lig
,
Lig2
,
KL
,
Spo
]
and the review
[
Der
]
for background and further references. A particularly important integrable regime is the TASEP with step initial condition. In this setting, Rost proved a first nontrivial law of large numbers and local equilibrium results
[
Ros
]
. Later, Johansson identified the
t^{1/3}
-scale fluctuations and the GUE Tracy–Widom limit using the connection to last passage percolation
[
Joh
]
; see also
[
PS
,
TW
,
Sch
]
and many other works that followed.

Multi-type (or multi-species, or colored; we use these terms interchangeably) TASEP is a rich extension of the single-species model that has also attracted substantial attention. Beyond its intrinsic interest, there are at least two broad sources of motivation for studying this refined setting. First,
second class
particles (and, more generally, discrepancies under the basic coupling) serve as microscopic probes of characteristics and shock/rarefaction phenomena; see, for instance,
[
FK
,
MG
,
FP
,
BF
]
, and references therein. Second, multi-type systems exhibit a richer and often more natural algebraic and integrable structure, see, e.g.,
[
FM
,
AAV
,
Buf
,
BW1
,
BW2
,
BB
]
.

To the best of our knowledge, the present work is the first to study the
types of the leading particles
in the multi-type TASEP started from the step initial condition. We address what we view as the most basic questions in this model. Obviously, our results are in no way exhaustive; see, for example, Remark
5.5
for a natural direction for further study. We hope that this paper will draw attention to this natural generalization of a classical and extensively studied integrable model.

2.
Integral representation

Our main object of interest is the so-called colored Asymmetric Simple Exclusion Process (or ASEP, for short), which is also known under the names of multi-type and multi-species ASEP. This is an interacting particle system on
\mathbb{Z}

whose states can be viewed as maps
\eta:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}\cup\{-\infty\}
. The value
\eta(x)=c\in\mathbb{Z}
is interpreted as a particle of color
c
occupying position
x
. The value
\eta(y)=-\infty
is interpreted as the absence of a particle at location
y
. Denote by
\mathfrak{C}
the set of all such states or particle configurations
\eta
.

The Markovian evolution of the colored ASEP is defined as follows. Each particle has two exponential clocks – left and right – of rates
L
and
R
, respectively; all clocks are independent. When a clock rings, the particle attempts to move in the corresponding direction by a single unit. If the target site is occupied by a particle of a weakly higher color, the jump is suppressed. On the other hand, if it is occupied by a particle of a strictly lower color or not occupied at all, our particle swaps its position with the particle/hole at its target location. The fact that this is a well-defined Markov process follows from the well-known graphical construction of
[
Har
]
,
[
Lig3
]
.

We will be mostly interested in the case of
Totally
ASEP, or TASEP, with
L=0
. Thus, in the TASEP only right jumps are initiated by the clocks. Without loss of generality, one can then set
R=1
. Whenever a colored ASEP shows up below, we will still assume that its right jump rate
R
is equal to
1
, while we will re-denote its left jump rate
L
by
q
.

For
\eta\in\mathfrak{C}
and a color
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, denote by
\mathcal{A}_{c}(\eta)
the subset of
\mathbb{Z}
such that
x\in\mathcal{A}_{c}(\eta)
if and only if
\eta(x)\geqslant c
. In other words,
\mathcal{A}_{c}(\eta)
is the set of positions of particles of colors that are no less than
c
.

Take
n\geqslant 1
, and let
C=(c_{1}<\dots<c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be an ordered
n
-tuple of integers (that are to be used momentarily as color cutoffs). Let
\eta\in\mathfrak{C}
be such that
|\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)|\geqslant n-i+1
and
\max(\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta))<\infty
for each
i=1,\dots,n
. In other words, for each color
c_{i}
, there are at least
(n-i+1)
particles of color no less than
c_{i}
, and among those particles there is a right-most one (the latter condition is nontrivial as
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)
may be infinite). Note that if
\eta
satisfies these conditions, any configuration that can be reached via time evolution started from
\eta
with nontrivial probability will also satisfy them.

Given such
C\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
and
\eta\in\mathfrak{C}
, define
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as follows. For
n,n-1,\dots,1
in that order we recursively set

k_{n}=\max(\mathcal{A}_{c_{n}}(\eta)),\qquad k_{i}=\max\bigl(\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\}\bigr),\quad i=n-1,\dots,1.

(2.1)

In other words,
k_{i}
records the maximum of
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)
disregarding the maxima that have already been recorded for larger color cutoffs
c_{i+1},\dots,c_{n}
. The assumptions of the previous paragraph guarantee that the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)
makes sense. Further, the definition implies that the coordinates of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)
are necessarily pairwise distinct. In what follows we will also use the notation
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)
instead of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)
if
\eta
is undergoing a Markovian evolution with time parameter
t
.

In general, some of the maxima of
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)
may coincide. The definition of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)
could be viewed as a natural way of modifying the sequence of those maxima in such a way that all its coordinates remain distinct.

We are interested in controlling the distribution of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)
. In order to state the main result of this section that will give a formula for this distribution, we need to introduce additional notation.

A family of rational functions

\varphi_{\mu}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ \mu_{i}\neq\mu_{j},

indexed by
n
-tuples
\mu
of unequal integers, is defined as follows. If
\delta=(\delta_{1}<\dots<\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
then

\varphi_{\delta}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{(1+w_{i})^{\delta_{i}}}.

(2.2)

Further, if
\mu
is such that
\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}
for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, then

\varphi_{(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{i+1},\mu_{i},\dots,\mu_{n})}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=\frac{w_{i+1}(1+w_{i})}{w_{i}-w_{i+1}}\,(1-\mathfrak{s}_{i})\cdot\varphi_{\mu}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})

(2.3)

with elementary transpositions
\mathfrak{s}_{i}\cdot h(w_{1},\dots,w_{n}):=h(w_{1},\dots,w_{i+1},w_{i},\dots,w_{n})
. This recurrence relation allows to extend the definition (
2.2
) to all
\mu
’s. The fact that such an extension is well-defined is nontrivial, and it will follow from the proof of Proposition
2.4
below.

We also need another family of rational functions

\psi_{\nu}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C},\qquad\nu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},\ \nu_{i}\neq\nu_{j},

indexed by the same set and defined in a similar fashion. For
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1}>\dots>\epsilon_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
we set

\psi_{\epsilon}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(1+w_{i})^{\epsilon_{i}}.

(2.4)

This is extended to arbitrary
\nu
’s using the following recurrence relation: If
\nu_{i}>\nu_{i+1}
for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, then

\psi_{(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{i+1},\nu_{i},\dots,\nu_{n})}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})=\frac{w_{i}(1+w_{i+1})-w_{i+1}(1+w_{i})\mathfrak{s}_{i}}{w_{i}-w_{i+1}}\cdot\psi_{\nu}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n}).

(2.5)

As for
\varphi_{\mu}
’s, the correctness of this definition is not obvious, and it will follow from the proof of Proposition
2.4
.

We are now ready to state an integral representation for the distribution of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in\mathfrak{C}
be a (random or deterministic) initial condition for the colored TASEP, and let
C=(c_{1}<\dots<c_{n})
,
n\geqslant 1
, be a sequence of color cutoffs. Assume that
\mathcal{M}_{C}(0)=\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta_{0})
is well-defined and deterministic, cf. the assumptions above (
2.1
); denote
\mathcal{M}_{C}(0)
by
\nu
. Then

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu\}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint\cdots\oint_{|w_{i}|=R>1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\left(1-\frac{w_{i}}{w_{j}}\right)\\
\times\varphi_{\mu}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\psi_{\nu}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{e^{tw_{j}}dw_{j}}{(1+w_{j})^{n-j+1}}\,,\end{gathered}

(2.6)

where the integral is over positively oriented circles
|w_{i}|=R
,
i=1,\dots,n
with
R>1
.

As we will only perform asymptotic analysis when
\psi_{\nu}
is factorized as in (

), it is convenient to have a simplified formula in that case explicitly written.

Corollary 2.2
.

In the notation of Theorem
2.1
, assume that the coordinates of
\mathcal{M}_{C}(0)=\nu
are decreasing:
\nu=(\nu_{1}>\dots>\nu_{n})
. Then

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu\}=\frac{1}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint\cdots\oint_{|w_{i}|=R>1}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\left(1-\frac{w_{i}}{w_{j}}\right)\\
\times\varphi_{\mu}(w_{1},\dots,w_{n})\prod_{j=1}^{n}{(1+w_{j})^{\nu_{j}-n+j-1}}{e^{tw_{j}}dw_{j}}\,,\end{gathered}

(2.7)

where the integral is over positively oriented circles
|w_{i}|=R
,
i=1,\dots,n
with
R>1
.

In view of the relative simplicity of the expressions in the right-hand sides of (
2.6
)-(

), it is natural to ask where the definition of the left-hand sides came from. Indeed, it is not obvious that
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)
is a good object to consider; for us it appeared through a degeneration procedure of the observable for the colored stochastic vertex models introduced in
[

The proof of Theorem

consists of two steps that we state as two propositions below.

Proposition 2.3
.

In the notation of Theorem
2.1
,
\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu\}
is equal to the probability that in a colored TASEP with
n
particles of colors
1,\dots,n
that are initially (at time
t=0
) at positions
\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n}
, respectively, the positions of those particles at time
t
are
\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n}
, respectively.
In other words, this is exactly the transition probability of the
n
-particle TASEP with particles of different colors.

In particular, this means that
\mathcal{M}_{C}(\eta)
can be viewed as a
duality functional
relating the colored TASEP of Theorem
2.1
, which may have infinitely many particles, and the
n
-particle colored TASEP of Proposition
2.3
.

Proposition 2.4
.

The transition probability over time
t
of the colored TASEP with
n
particles of colors
1,\dots,n
between initial and final positions
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})
and
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
, respectively, is given by the right-hand side of (
2.6
).

A colored ASEP version of the latter claim (for
\nu=(\nu_{1}>\dots>\nu_{n})
, as in Corollary

) has appeared as Theorem 3.5 in
[

. Both formulas are suitable degenerations of an integral representation for transition probabilities (or entries of a transfer matrix) of the colored stochastic vertex models proved as
[

, (9.5.2)]

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

It suffices to check that
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)
undergoes a Markovian evolution with jump rates that agree with those of the
n
-particle TASEP from the hypothesis. Consider a time moment
t\geqslant 0
, and denote by
\eta_{t}
the state of the original TASEP of Theorem
2.1
at
t
. Let
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
, choose
i
such that
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, and consider
k_{i}
. By the definition of
\mathcal{M}_{C}
,
\eta_{t}(k_{i})\geqslant c_{i}
; that is, the position
k_{i}
is occupied by a particle of color at least
c_{i}
. By the same definition,
k_{i}
cannot change unless that particle changes its position, i.e., moves either left or right by 1. Let us consider all possible scenarios.

The particle at
k_{i}
can only move left if
\eta_{t}(k_{i}-1)>\eta_{t}(k_{i})
, in which case the rate of such move is equal to 1. If there is no
j>i
such that
k_{j}=k_{i}-1
then the corresponding swap of particles at
k_{i}-1
and
k_{i}
would not change
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta_{t})\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\}
, which means that
k_{i}
would not move. On the other hand, if
k_{j}=k_{i}-1
for some
j>i
, then this particle swap would result in
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta_{t})\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\}
losing
k_{i}
and gaining
k_{i}-1
. Since
k_{i}
was previously the maximum of this set, the new maximum will be equal to
k_{i}-1
. Hence, we conclude that
k_{i}
moves to the left by 1 with rate 1 if there is a
k_{i}-1=k_{j}
with
j>i
(note that the latter condition also implies that
\eta_{t}(k_{i}-1)=\eta_{t}(k_{j})>\eta_{t}(k_{i})
as otherwise
\mathcal{A}_{c_{j}}(\eta_{t})\setminus\{k_{j+1},\dots,k_{n}\}
would contain a member that is larger than
k_{j}
). Further, as
k_{i}
decreases by 1,
k_{j}
increases by 1; i.e., the values of
k_{i}
and
k_{j}
are swapped.

Let us now look at the possibility of the particle at
k_{i}
moving to the right. If there is no
j>i
such that
k_{j}=k_{i}+1
,
then we must have
\eta_{t}(k_{i}+1)<\eta_{t}(k_{i})
, as otherwise
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta_{t})\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\}
would have a maximum greater than
k_{i}
. Hence, the right jump rate of the particle at
k_{i}
is equal to 1, and if it jumps then
k_{i}=\max(\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta_{t})\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\})
also increases by 1. Assume now that there is a
j>i
such that
k_{j}=k_{i}+1
. Then either
\eta_{t}(k_{i}+1)\geqslant\eta_{t}(k_{i})
, in which case the particle at
k_{i}
cannot move to the right, or
\eta_{t}(k_{i}+1)<\eta_{t}(k_{i})
. In the latter case, even though the particle at
k_{i}
can move to the right, the value of
k_{i}
is not going to change with that move. Indeed, such a move would change neither
\mathcal{A}_{c_{j}}(\eta)
, nor
\mathcal{A}_{c_{j}}(\eta)\setminus\{k_{j+1},\dots,k_{m}\}
, nor
k_{j}
, with the latter remaining equal to
k_{i}+1
. Further,
\eta_{t}(k_{i}+1)=\eta_{t}(k_{j})\geqslant c_{j}>c_{i}
, which means that the particle landing at
k_{i}
after the particle swap would still contribute to
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)
, with
\mathcal{A}_{c_{i}}(\eta)\setminus\{k_{i+1},\dots,k_{n}\}
and its maximum
k_{i}
remaining unchanged.
Summarizing,
k_{i}
moves to the right by 1 with rate 1 if and only if there is no
j>i
such that
k_{j}=k_{i}+1
. If at the time of such a move there is an
l<i
with
k_{l}=k_{i}+1
, then the values of
k_{i}
and
k_{l}
are swapped.

The above considerations show that the time evolution of
(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})
is exactly that of the
n
-particle colored TASEP with increasing colors of the particles, as was desired.
∎

, Proposition 2.5.1]
. Third, the fact that in transition probabilities (
2.8
) with a fixed bottom row, nonzero markings cannot run away infinitely far to the left with nontrivial probability is guaranteed by the condition that
y>1
,
0<q<1
, since then the top middle weight in (
2
) with
\bm{I}=(0,\dots,0)
is strictly less than 1. Finally, if
\nu
is a vector in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
with unequal coordinates, then each
\bm{I}^{k}(\nu)
at the bottom of (
2.8
) has no more than a single nonzero entry, and, thanks to the vanishing of the left bottom weight in (
2
) with
\bm{I}_{[1,n]}=1
, the same will be true for
\bm{I}^{k}(\mu)
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Hence, for such
\nu
the transition probabilities (
2.8
) are nontrivial only if the vector
\mu
also has unequal coordinates. The vertex weights in this case are written out explicitly in
[
dGMV
, (3.32)]
, and they are manifestly nonnegative.

Let
\nu,\mu\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be vectors with unequal coordinates. Denote by
T_{y,q}(\nu\to\mu)
the transition probability afforded by (
2.8
)-(
2
). Then, as is explained in
[
dGMV
, Section 3.4.1]
, setting
y=1+(1-q)\varepsilon
and taking the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
yields the transition probabilities for the colored ASEP. More exactly,

\lim_{\epsilon\to 0}\left(T_{1+(1-q)\varepsilon,q}(\nu\to(\mu-([t/\epsilon],\dots,[t/\varepsilon]))\right)^{[t/\varepsilon]},

(2.13)

converges to the time
t
transition matrix for the colored ASEP, or TASEP at
q=0
.

Let us now use the fact that
[
BW1
, (9.5.2)]
provides an integral representation for
(T_{y,q}(\nu\to\mu))^{p}
for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
, see also
[
dGMV
, Theorem 3.4]
.
It reads, substituting
s=q^{-1/2}
,
y_{j}=q^{-1/2}y
there, and with other notations explained below,

\begin{gathered}(T_{y,q}(\nu\to\mu))^{p}=\frac{(-q)^{\frac{1}{2}(|\nu|-|\mu|)}q^{-np}}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint_{C_{1}}\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\oint_{C_{n}}\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\frac{x_{j}-x_{i}}{x_{j}-qx_{i}}\\
\times\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\frac{x_{i}-q^{\frac{1}{2}}y}{x_{i}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}y}\right)^{p}f_{\mu}({x}_{1}^{-1},\dots,{x}_{n}^{-1})g^{*}_{\nu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}),\end{gathered}

(2.14)

where
|\mu|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\mu_{j}
,
|\nu|=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\nu_{j}
, and the integration contours satisfy
[
BW1
, Definition 8.1.1]
:

•

The contours
\{C_{1},\dots,C_{n}\}
are closed, positively oriented and pairwise non-intersecting.

•

The contours
C_{i}
and
q\cdot C_{i}
are both contained within contour
C_{i+1}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, where
q\cdot C_{i}
denotes the image of
C_{i}
under multiplication by
q
.

•

All contours surround the point
s=q^{-1/2}
and no other poles.

The rational functions
f_{\mu}
are defined by requiring that for
\delta=(\delta_{1}<\dots<\delta_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

f_{\delta}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1-q^{-1}}{1-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{i}}\left(\frac{x_{i}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{1-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{i}}\right)^{\delta_{i}},

(2.15)

and by the following recursion relation: If
\mu=(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{n})
satisfies
\mu_{i}<\mu_{i+1}
for some
1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1
, then

\left(q-\frac{x_{i}-qx_{i+1}}{x_{i}-x_{i+1}}(1-\mathfrak{s}_{i})\right)\cdot f_{\mu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=f_{(\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{i+1},\mu_{i},\dots,\mu_{n})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

(2.16)

The correctness of this definition follows either from an independent definition of
f_{\mu}
through suitable partition functions
[
BW1
, Definition 3.4.3]
, or from commutation relations satisfied by the
Demazure-Lusztig operators
in the left-hand side of (
2.16
).

The
dual
rational functions
g_{\nu}^{*}
can be defined in a similar fashion, cf.
[
BW1
, (5.9.17)]
,
[
BW1
, Theorem 5.8.1]
, by requiring that for
\epsilon=(\epsilon_{1}>\dots>\epsilon_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
,

g_{\epsilon}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{i}}\left(\frac{x_{i}-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{1-q^{-\frac{1}{2}}x_{i}}\right)^{\epsilon_{i}},

(2.17)

and by the following recursion relation: If
\nu=(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{n})
is such that
\nu_{i}>\nu_{i+1}
, then

\left(1-\frac{x_{i+1}-qx_{i}}{x_{i+1}-x_{i}}(1-\mathfrak{s}_{i})\right)\cdot g_{\nu}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})=g_{(\nu_{1},\dots,\nu_{i+1},\nu_{i},\dots,\nu_{n})}(x_{1},\dots,x_{n}).

(2.18)

The integral representation (
2.14
) is suitable for the limit transition (
2.13
). Following the steps in
the proof of
[
dGMV
, Theorem 3.5]
, we obtain the following formula for the transition probability of the colored ASEP over time
t
between the initial and final point configurations
\nu
and
\mu
:

\begin{gathered}\frac{(-q)^{\frac{1}{2}(|\nu|-|\mu|)}(-1)^{n}}{(2\pi i)^{n}}\oint\frac{dx_{1}}{x_{1}}\cdots\oint\frac{dx_{n}}{x_{n}}\prod_{1\leqslant i<j\leqslant n}\frac{x_{j}-x_{i}}{x_{j}-qx_{i}}\\
\times\prod_{j=1}^{n}\exp\left(\frac{(1-q)^{2}x_{j}t}{(1-x_{j})(1-qx_{j})}\right)f_{\mu}\left(q^{-\frac{1}{2}}{x}_{1}^{-1},\dots,q^{-\frac{1}{2}}{x}_{n}^{-1}\right)g^{*}_{\nu}\left(q^{\frac{1}{2}}x_{1},\dots,q^{\frac{1}{2}}x_{n}\right),\end{gathered}

(2.19)

where all variables are integrated over small positively oriented loops around 1.

Substituting the arguments of
f_{\mu}
from (
2.19
) into (
2.15
) gives

(-q)^{\frac{|\delta|}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{(1-q)x_{i}}{1-qx_{i}}\left(\frac{1-x_{i}}{1-qx_{i}}\right)^{\delta_{i}},

and swaps
x_{i}
and
x_{i+1}
in the difference operator in the left-hand side of (
2.16
). Similarly, substituting the arguments of
g_{\nu}^{*}
into (
2.17
) gives

(-q)^{-\frac{|\epsilon|}{2}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1-x_{i}}\left(\frac{1-qx_{i}}{1-x_{i}}\right)^{\epsilon_{i}},

and does not change the difference operators in the left-hand side of (

). Thus, the power of
(-q)
in front of (
2.19
) cancels out, and we are free to set

to reach the colored TASEP transition probability we are after.

The final step is the change of variables

. A straightforward computation shows that this takes (
2.19
) with

to the right-hand side of (
2.6
), with the recursive definition of

giving rise to (
2.2
)-(
2.3
), and the recursive definition of

giving rise to (
2.4
)-(
2.5
).
∎

3.
Connection to the colored stochastic vertex models

The goal of this section is to explain how the observables
\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu\}
of Theorem
2.1
and Corollary
2.2
originated from those for colored stochastic vertex models introduced in
[
BW2
]
. In fact, Corollary
2.2
is a direct degeneration of
[
BW2
, Theorem 1.1]
or
[
BK
, Corollary 8.4]
, and this is how we encountered it for the first time. While the approach we took in Section
2
allowed us to prove the more general Theorem
2.1
, it did not explain how the key observables were discovered, and such an explanation is precisely the
raison d’être
for the present section.

The colored stochastic vertex models of
[
BW2
]
have a rather straightforward degeneration to the colored ASEP. This degeneration is very similar to the one used in the proof of Proposition
2.4
above, and it is described in
[
BW1
, Section 12.3.2]
. We will not repeat the degeneration procedure here and introduce the observables of
[
BW2
]
directly in the setting of the colored ASEP.

Consider a special initial condition of the colored ASEP, where position
k
is occupied by a particle of color
(-k)
for all
k\in\mathbb{Z}
; note that all colors are distinct – this is the so-called
rainbow case
. It is often referred to as the
step
or
fully packed initial condition
. Next, using the terminology of
[
BW2
]
, let us choose a
colored composition
of length
n\geqslant 1
denoted as
\varkappa=(\varkappa_{1},\dots,\varkappa_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, where each part
\varkappa_{j}
is assigned a color
b_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
,
j=1,\dots,n
. In our degenerate case of the rainbow ASEP, it only makes sense to consider
\varkappa
with pairwise distinct coordinates, which is what we will assume.

Let us define colored height functions for the colored ASEP. For its state
\eta\in\mathfrak{C}
, a color cutoff
c\in\mathbb{Z}
, and a position
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
we set

H_{c}(k)=|\{l\geqslant k:\eta(l)=c\}|,\qquad H_{>c}(k)=|\{l\geqslant k:\eta(l)>c\}|,\qquad H_{\geqslant c}(k)=|\{l\geqslant k:\eta(l)\geqslant c\}|.

(3.1)

For the random
\eta
coming from the time evolution of the step initial condition, these quantities are integer-valued random variables. In particular, they take finite values almost surely.

Let us also define similar (although deterministic) quantities for the colored composition
\varkappa
:

\displaystyle H^{\varkappa}_{c}(k)

\displaystyle=\left|\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\varkappa_{j}\geqslant k,\ b_{j}=c\}\right|,

\displaystyle H^{\varkappa}_{>c}(k)

\displaystyle=\left|\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\varkappa_{j}\geqslant k,\ b_{j}>c\}\right|,

\displaystyle H^{\varkappa}_{\geqslant c}(k)

\displaystyle=\left|\{j\in\{1,\dots,n\}:\varkappa_{j}\geqslant k,\ b_{j}\geqslant c\}\right|.

The observables in question are labeled by colored compositions
\varkappa
as above, and have the form, cf.
[
BW2
, Section 6.4]
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}(\eta)

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q^{H_{\geqslant b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)-H_{\geqslant b_{i}}^{\varkappa}(\varkappa_{i}+1)}-q^{H_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)-H_{>b_{i}}^{\varkappa}(\varkappa_{i}+1)}}{q-1}

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)=1\}}\cdot q^{H_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)-H_{>b_{i}}^{\varkappa}(\varkappa_{i}+1)}\right).

Here
q
is the left jump rate of the colored ASEP.
The equality of the two expressions for
\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}
follows from the fact that for any
c\in\mathbb{Z}
,
H_{c}
can only take values 0 or 1 (as we are in the rainbow case), as well as
H_{\geqslant c}=H_{c}+H_{>c}
, and
H_{b_{i}}^{\varkappa}(\varkappa_{i}+1)=0
. We will use the second expression.

In order to re-interpret this expression, we will use a remarkable
color-position symmetry
that the colored ASEP with the step initial condition enjoys. It was originally discovered and proved in
[
AAV
]
, see also
[
BW1
]
,
[
BB
]
,
[
Buf
]
.

Let
\eta(t)
be the random state of the colored ASEP started from the step initial condition. Note that
\eta_{t}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
is a bijection for every
t\geqslant 0
with probability 1; let
\eta^{-1}_{t}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
denote its inverse. Then
\eta_{t}^{-1}(c)
is the location of the particle of color
c
at time
t
for every
c\in\mathbb{Z}
. The color-position symmetry says that
\eta_{t}
and
\xi_{t}:=\eta_{t}^{-1}
are
equi-distributed
for any fixed
t\geqslant 0
.

Let us use the notation (
3.1
) for the colored height functions associated with
\eta_{t}
, and a similar notation with
\widetilde{H}
for the same quantities associated with
\xi_{t}=\eta_{t}^{-1}
. Then

•

H_{i}(j+1)=1
is equivalent to
\eta_{t}^{-1}(i)\geqslant j+1
, or
\xi_{t}(i)\geqslant j+1
;

•

H_{>i}(j+1)=h
is equivalent to
|\eta_{t}(\{j+1,j+2,\dots\})\cap\{i+1,i+2,\dots\}|=h
, which is the same as

|\{j+1,j+2,\dots\}\cap\eta^{-1}_{t}(\{i+1,i+2,\dots\})|=h
, or
\widetilde{H}_{>j}(i+1)=h
;

Hence,

\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}(\eta_{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{H_{b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)=1\}}\cdot q^{H_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)-H^{\varkappa}_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)}\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{t}(b_{i})\geqslant\varkappa_{i}+1\}}\cdot q^{\widetilde{H}_{>\varkappa_{i}}(b_{i}+1)-H^{\varkappa}_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)}\right).

Let us now verify that if all the conditions in the indicator functions
\mathbf{1}_{\{\cdots\}}
are satisfied, then all exponents of
q
in the above products are nonnegative. Indeed, if
H_{>b_{i}}^{\varkappa}(\varkappa_{i}+1)=l
, then there exist exactly
l
indices
j_{1},\dots,j_{l}\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
\varkappa_{j_{a}}>\varkappa_{i}
and
b_{j_{a}}>b_{i}
for all
a=1,\dots,l
. The indicators in the right-hand side then tell us that
\xi_{t}(b_{j_{a}})\geqslant\varkappa_{j_{a}}+1>\varkappa_{i}+1
for all
1\leqslant a\leqslant l
. Hence,
\widetilde{H}_{>\varkappa_{i}}(b_{i}+1)\geqslant l
, and the exponent of
q
in the
i
th factor on the right-hand side is nonnegative.

Thus, we can now set
q
to 0. This turns the colored ASEP into the colored TASEP, and
\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}(\eta_{t})
can be written as

\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}(\eta_{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left(\mathbf{1}_{\{\xi_{t}(b_{i})\geqslant\varkappa_{i}+1\}}\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\left\{\widetilde{H}_{>\varkappa_{i}}(b_{i}+1)=H^{\varkappa}_{>b_{i}}(\varkappa_{i}+1)\right\}}\right).

(3.2)

Let
C=(c_{1}<\dots<c_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
be such that
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{n}\}=\{\varkappa_{1}+1,\dots,\varkappa_{n}+1\}
, and let
\sigma
be a permutation of
\{1\dots,n\}
such that
c_{j}=\varkappa_{\sigma(j)}+1
for all
1\leqslant j\leqslant n
. Then an examination of the
right-hand side of (
3.2
) shows that it is exactly equal to
\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(\xi_{t})=(b_{\sigma(1)},\dots,b_{\sigma(n)})\}
, yielding

\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}(\eta_{t})=\mathrm{Prob}\left\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(\xi_{t})=\left(b_{\sigma(1)},\dots,b_{\sigma(n)}\right)\right\}.

(3.3)

Thus, we see that the integral representation for
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{O}_{\varkappa}]
that was obtained in
[
BW2
]
can be used to produce an integral representation for the right-hand side of (
3.3
). This would result in an alternative proof of Corollary
2.2
in the case of the step initial condition, but would not go as far as proving the more general Theorem
2.1
. The duality seen in Proposition
2.3
was also anticipated (but not proved) in
[
BW2
, Section 6.3]
.

4.1.
Leaders: Definition

In this and subsequent sections we consider TASEP which starts at time

\eta_{t}

.

\eta^{-1}_{t}(i)
is the
s
-th largest number among
\{\eta^{-1}_{t}(z)\}_{z\geqslant k}
. Clearly, for any

and

4.2.
Leader distribution

Proposition 4.2
.

For any
k_{2}>k_{1}\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{$k_{1}$-leader is of type $\geqslant k_{2}$}\right)=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathbf{i}}}\oint_{|w|=R>1}\frac{w+2}{w}(1+w)^{k_{1}-k_{2}-1}\exp\left(\frac{tw^{2}}{1+w}\right)dw,

(4.1)

where the integration contour is positively oriented.

Proof.

Let
C=(k_{1},k_{2})
, for
k_{2}>k_{1}
, and
\mu=(x_{1},x_{2})
, with
x_{1}<x_{2}
. In that case, initially
\mathcal{M}_{C}(0)=(-k_{1},-k_{2})
.

By Corollary
2.2
, we have

\begin{gathered}\mathrm{Prob}\{\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu\}=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathbf{i}})^{2}}\oint_{|w_{1}|=R>1}\oint_{|w_{2}|=R>1}\left(1-\frac{w_{1}}{w_{2}}\right)\\
\times(1+w_{1})^{-x_{1}}(1+w_{2})^{-x_{2}}(1+w_{1})^{-k_{1}-2}(1+w_{2})^{-k_{2}-1}\exp(t(w_{1}+w_{2}))dw_{1}dw_{2}\,.\end{gathered}

(4.2)

The event
\mathcal{M}_{C}(t)=\mu
with such a choice of
C
and
\mu
can be rephrased as the event that the
k_{1}
-leader is at position
x_{2}
and has type
\geqslant k_{2}
, while the
(k_{1},2)
-leader is at position
x_{1}<x_{2}
.

We need to sum these probabilities over all possible values of
x_{2}
and
x_{1}
in order to exclude unnecessary (for our current purpose) information about locations. Doing the summations

\sum_{x_{1}=-\infty}^{x_{2}-1}(1+w_{1})^{-x_{1}}=\frac{(1+w_{1})^{-x_{2}+1}}{-w_{1}},\qquad|1+w_{1}|<1,

(4.3)

and

\sum_{x_{2}=-k_{1}}^{\infty}(1+w_{1})^{-x_{2}}(1+w_{2})^{-x_{2}}=\frac{(1+w_{1})^{k_{1}+1}(1+w_{2})^{k_{1}+1}}{w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{1}w_{2}},\qquad|(1+w_{1})(1+w_{2})|>1,

(4.4)

in (
4.2
), we obtain

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{$k_{1}$-leader is of type $\geqslant k_{2}$}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathbf{i}})^{2}}\oint_{w_{1}}\oint_{w_{2}}\frac{w_{2}-w_{1}}{w_{1}w_{2}}\frac{(1+w_{2})^{k_{1}-k_{2}}}{w_{1}+w_{2}+w_{1}w_{2}}\exp(t(w_{1}+w_{2}))dw_{1}dw_{2},

(4.5)

where the contour of integration in
w_{1}
is a small circle around
(-1)
, and the contour of integration in
w_{2}
is a large circle around
(-1)
such that
|(1+w_{1})(1+w_{2})|>1
(we first choose the radius for the
w_{1}
-contour, and then for the
w_{2}
-contour). The integrand has a unique
w_{1}
-pole at
w_{1}=-w_{2}(1+w_{2})^{-1}
, which is inside the
w_{1}
contour due to our conditions on contours. Taking the residue, we arrive at the statement of the proposition.

∎

Proposition 4.3
.

For any integers
k_{2}>k_{1}
and

, we have

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{$k_{1}$-leader is of type $\geqslant k_{2}$ and is located at $x$}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{2\pi{\mathbf{i}}}\oint_{w_{1}}\oint_{w_{2}}\frac{w_{1}-w_{2}}{w_{1}w_{2}}(1+w_{1})^{-k_{1}-x-1}(1+w_{2})^{-k_{2}-x-1}\exp\left(t(w_{1}+w_{2})\right)dw_{1}dw_{2},

(4.6)

where the
w_{1}
-contour encircles
-1
and no other poles of the integrand, while
w_{2}
-contour encircles the poles at
w_{2}=-1
and
w_{2}=0
, but no other poles of the integrand. Both contours are positively oriented.

Proof.

The proof is exactly the same as in Proposition
4.2
with the only difference that we perform the summation (
4.3
), but not (
4.4
), and plug the result into (
4.2
).

∎

4.3.
Joint distribution of two leaders

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
k_{3}>k_{2}>k_{1}
be arbitrary integers. We have the following equality:

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{the $k_{1}$-leader has type $\geqslant k_{3}$, }\mbox{the $(k_{1},2)$-leader has type $\geqslant k_{2}$}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathbf{i}})^{2}}\oint_{|v_{2}|=2}\oint_{|v_{3}|=2}\frac{(v_{3}-v_{2})(v_{2}v_{3}^{2}-1)(v_{2}^{2}v_{3}-1)v_{2}^{k_{1}-k_{2}}v_{3}^{k_{1}-k_{3}}}{v_{2}^{2}v_{3}(v_{2}v_{3}-1)(v_{2}-1)(v_{3}-1)^{3}}\\
\times\exp\left(t\left(\frac{1}{v_{2}v_{3}}+v_{2}+v_{3}-3\right)\right)dv_{2}dv_{3}.

(4.7)

where the integration contours are counterclockwise.

Proof.

Let
C=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})
, for
k_{3}>k_{2}>k_{1}
, and
\mu=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})
, with
x_{1}<x_{2}<x_{3}
. In that case,
\mathcal{M}_{C}(0)=(-k_{1},-k_{2},-k_{3})
. By Corollary

5.2.
Type of leader conditioned on position

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
,
L_{1}(t)
be the type of the
k

\times\left(1+\frac{\sqrt{\pi}}{2}\left(1+\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)\cdot\exp\left(\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right)\cdot\left(a+x\right)\right)\mathbf{1}_{a\geqslant 0}\,da,\qquad\mbox{in distribution},

(5.3)

where in the left-hand side we consider the conditional distribution of the type of the leader (note that according to the classical central limit theorem,
\lfloor t+x\sqrt{t}\rfloor
is a typical value for the position of the leader).

Proof.

We analyze the formula given by Proposition
4.3
with
k_{1}:=k
,
k_{2}:=\lfloor a\sqrt{t}\rfloor
, and
x:=x\sqrt{t}
, where in the left-hand sides we use the notation from Proposition
4.3
, and in the right-hand sides – the notation from the statement of the current proposition. We apply the steepest descent method, and deform both contours in the same way as in Section
5.1
. After that, the principal contribution to the integral is given by a neighborhood of the critical point
w_{1}=w_{2}=0
, and we change variables
\sqrt{t}w_{i}=:v_{i}
, for
i=1,2
, in order to compute it. Using the Taylor expansion, we obtain

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{$k$-leader is of type $\geqslant k+a\sqrt{t}$ and is located at $\lfloor t+x\sqrt{t}\rfloor$}\right)\\
=\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\cdot 2\pi{\mathbf{i}}}\int_{\Im(v_{1})=-\varepsilon}dv_{1}\int_{\Im(v_{2})=\varepsilon}dv_{2}\left(\frac{1}{v_{2}}-\frac{1}{v_{1}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{v_{1}^{2}}{2}-{\mathbf{i}}xv_{1}+\frac{v_{2}^{2}}{2}-(x+a)v_{2}\right)+O\left(\frac{1}{t}\right).

(5.4)

Making a change of variable
y_{i}={\mathbf{i}}v_{i}
, for

, we obtain that the expression above is asymptotically equivalent to

\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}\cdot 2\pi{\mathbf{i}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}+{\mathbf{i}}\varepsilon}dy_{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}-{\mathbf{i}}\varepsilon}dy_{2}\left(\frac{1}{y_{1}}-\frac{1}{y_{2}}\right)\exp\left(\frac{-y_{1}^{2}}{2}-{\mathbf{i}}xy_{1}+\frac{-y_{2}^{2}}{2}-{\mathbf{i}}(x+a)y_{2}\right).

(5.5)

In order to compute this integral, we use the following table integrals and Sokhotski-Plemelj theorem:

\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{1}{s}\exp\left(\frac{-s^{2}}{2}+{\mathbf{i}}sx\right)ds={\mathbf{i}}\cdot\pi\cdot\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{x}{\sqrt{2}}\right),

\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\exp\left(\frac{-y^{2}}{2}-{\mathbf{i}}yx\right)dy=\sqrt{2\pi}\exp\left(-\frac{x^{2}}{2}\right),

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int\frac{f(x)}{x\pm{\mathbf{i}}\varepsilon}dx=\mp{\mathbf{i}}\pi f(0)+\mathrm{p.v.}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{f(x)}{x}dx.

Plugging in these expressions, we obtain that (

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(\frac{-x^{2}}{2}\right)\cdot\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{-x-a}{\sqrt{2}}\right)+\left(1-\mathrm{erf}\left(\frac{-x}{\sqrt{2}}\right)\right)\exp\left(-ax-\frac{a^{2}}{2}\right)\right).

(5.6)

By the classical Central Limit Theorem, the factor
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi t}}\exp\left(\frac{-x^{2}}{2}\right)
is asymptotically equivalent to
\mathrm{Prob}\left(X_{1}(t)=\lfloor t+x\sqrt{t}\rfloor\right)
. Therefore, the rest of (
5.6
) corresponds to the conditional law of the type of the leader. Differentiating it with respect to
a
, we arrive at the statement of the proposition.
∎

Remark 5.3

In Theorem

\hat{Y}(a_{2},a_{3}):=\frac{1}{(2\pi{\mathbf{i}})^{2}}\oint\oint\frac{(x_{3}-x_{2})^{2}(x_{2}+2x_{3})(2x_{2}+x_{3})}{(x_{2}+x_{3})x_{3}^{3}}\\
\times\exp\left(-x_{2}^{2}-x_{3}x_{2}-x_{3}^{2}-{\mathbf{i}}a_{2}x_{2}-{\mathbf{i}}a_{3}x_{3}\right)dx_{2}dx_{3},

where again the integration contours are real axes slightly shifted upwards.

Evaluating this integral in a similar fashion, we arrive at the statement of the proposition.

∎

5.4.
Number of leader changes

Theorem 5.6
.

Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{S}(t)
be the number of changes of the
k
-leader, i.e., the number of moments when the type of the
k
-leader changes. Then

\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\mathbf{E}\left(\mathcal{S}(t)\right)}{\ln(t)}=\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}.

Proof.

We analyze the asymptotics of the expression given by Proposition
4.6
.

Note that the function in the exponent is the same as in the previous subsection, so we again apply the steepest descent method with the same deformation of contours, get the leading asymptotic contribution from the neighborhood of the critical point
(1,1)
, and make a change of variables
v_{2}=1+u_{2}/\sqrt{t}
,
v_{3}=1+u_{3}/\sqrt{t}
in order to compute it. We pick three factors
1/\sqrt{t}
from the numerator, four such factors from the denominator, and two more from the Jacobian of the change of variables. This gives the leading asymptotics
1/\sqrt{t}
. However, note that the integrand is anti-symmetric in the leading order in
t
. Since we take the same contour of integration for
u_{2}
and
u_{3}
, this leading asymptotics also vanishes. The first non-zero contributing term comes from the non-symmetric factor in the integrand, which is the factor
v_{2}v_{3}^{2}=1+\frac{u_{2}+2u_{3}}{\sqrt{t}}+O(t^{-1})
. Thus, the integral (
4.11
) has leading asymptotics of the form

t^{-1}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\iint\frac{(u_{2}-u_{3})(u_{2}+2u_{3})(2u_{2}+u_{3})}{(u_{2}+u_{3})^{2}u_{2}u_{3}}(u_{2}+2u_{3})\exp\left(u_{2}^{2}+u_{2}u_{3}+u_{3}^{2}\right)du_{2}du_{3},

where the contours are
{\mathbf{i}}\mathbb{R}+\varepsilon
, for
0<\varepsilon\ll 1
. Making the change of variables
u_{i}={\mathbf{i}}x_{i}
, we get

t^{-1}\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{\Im(x_{2})=\varepsilon}\int_{\Im(x_{3})=\varepsilon}\frac{(x_{2}-x_{3})(x_{2}+2x_{3})^{2}(2x_{2}+x_{3})}{(x_{2}+x_{3})^{2}x_{2}x_{3}}\exp\left(-x_{2}^{2}-x_{2}x_{3}-x_{3}^{2}\right)du_{2}du_{3}.

This integral can be directly computed, and it is equal to
\frac{3\sqrt{3}}{4\pi}
. Integrating in
t
, we arrive at the statement of the theorem.

∎

6.
Voter model

6.1.
Totally asymmetric voter model and coalescence process

In this section we recall a definition of (particular cases of) a voter model and of a coalescence process on
\mathbb{Z}
.

We consider configurations
\nu:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
. The equality
\nu(x)=y
is interpreted as a "voter" at position
x
having "opinion"
y
. The opinions evolve in time according to the following rules. For each pair on neighboring integers
(x,x+1)
one associates an independent Poisson process of rate 1 on
\mathbb{R}_{\geqslant 0}
. Whenever a signal arrives at time
t
, one updates the opinion of a voter at
x
via
\nu_{t+0}(x)=\nu_{t}(x+1)
. In words, the voter to the left just takes the opinion of its immediate right neighbor. The previous opinion, as well as "the value" of the new opinion, do not play a role.

In one dimension, there is a well-known projection from the voter model to a coalescence process; let us recall it. Configurations of the coalescence process are maps
\xi:\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}
. Given a configuration of the voter model
\nu
, define
\xi(x)=1
iff
\nu(x-1)\neq\nu(x)
, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. In words, we consider connected components of the voters with the same opinion, and mark the left border of such a cluster by a particle.

It is clear that if
\nu_{t}
is the totally asymmetric voter model introduced above, the projection
\xi_{t}
is Markov and has the following description. Again, for each pair of neighboring positions we do the following updates independently and with rate 1:

00\to 00,\quad 10\to 10,\quad 01\to 10,\quad 11\to 10.

These are exactly the update rules of the coalescence process with particles jumping independently with rate 1 to the left only, and coalescing into one particle whenever they are in the same position.

6.2.
Embedding into the multi-type TASEP

As before, consider a multi-type TASEP
\eta_{t}
which starts from the initial configuration
\eta_{0}(x)=-x
. For each configuration
\eta
, define a map
\tilde{\xi}:\mathbb{Z}\to\{0,1\}
by the following rules. Let
\tilde{\xi}(a)=1
iff
\eta(x)>\eta(a)
, for all
x<a
, and
\tilde{\xi}(a)=0
otherwise. In words,
\tilde{\xi}(a)=1
iff the particle at position
a
has not interacted with any of the particles which were originally placed on its right (these particles are of smaller type in our notation, and due to non-interaction, they are still all to the right of the particle standing at
a
at time
t
).

For each configuration
\eta
, define a map
\tilde{\nu}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}
via

\tilde{\nu}(b):=\min_{x\leqslant b}\eta(x),\qquad\mbox{for $b\in\mathbb{Z}$}.

In words, this is the minimal type of the particles weakly to the left of
b
. See Example
6.3
below.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
,
\tilde{\nu}_{t}
be defined according to the rules above from the TASEP
\eta_{t}
. Then
\tilde{\nu}_{t}
is a totally asymmetric voter process with the initial configuration
\nu_{0}(x)=-x
, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
is a totally asymmetric coalescence process with the initial configuration
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(x)=1
, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

Let us consider possible local changes after one update in TASEP, and how they affect their projections
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
and
\tilde{\nu}_{t}
; in particular, we will see that these projections are Markov. Let
\eta_{t}(a)=x>y=\eta_{t}(a+1)
be two types of particles, which are updated at the pair of neighboring positions
(a,a+1)
at time
t
, so
\eta_{t+0}(a)=y
,
\eta_{t+0}(a+1)=x
. Consider the following cases.

a) Let
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a)=0
,
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a+1)=0
. Then one has
z:=\min_{b:b\leqslant a+1}\eta_{t}(b)<y<x
; it follows that
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(a)=\tilde{\nu}_{t}(a+1)=z
, and both
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
and
\tilde{\nu}_{t}
do not change after such a TASEP update.

b) Let
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a)=0
,
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a+1)=1
. Then one has
y=\min_{b:b\leqslant a+1}\eta_{t}(b)
, thus
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(a+1)=y
. After the TASEP update, we have
\tilde{\nu}_{t+0}(a)=\tilde{\nu}_{t+0}(a+1)=y
, and
\tilde{\xi}_{t+0}(a)=1
,
\tilde{\xi}_{t+0}(a+1)=0
, since
x>y
.

c) Let
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a)=1
,
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a+1)=0
. Since
x=\min_{b:b\leqslant a}\eta_{t}(b)
in that case, and
y<x
, we have
y=\min_{b\in\mathbb{Z};b\leqslant a+1}\eta_{t}(b)
, which contradicts
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a+1)=0
. Thus, this case is impossible.

d) Let
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a)=1
,
\tilde{\xi}_{t}(a+1)=1
. In such a case,
x=\min_{b:;b\leqslant a}\eta_{t}(b)
, and
y=\min_{b:b\leqslant a+1}\eta_{t}(b)
, which implies
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(a)=x
and
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(a+1)=y
. After the update,
x=\eta_{t+0}(a+1)\neq\min_{b:b\leqslant a+1}\eta_{t}(b)=y
, which means that
\tilde{\xi}_{t+0}(a+1)=0
, while
\tilde{\xi}_{t+0}(a)=1
, and
\tilde{\nu}_{t+0}(a)=\tilde{\nu}_{t+0}(a+1)=y
.

As we see, any update of TASEP updates the processes
\tilde{\nu}_{t}
and
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
in exactly the same way as was described in Section
6.1
. This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

6.3.
Duality with leaders

In this section we use an algebraic property of the multi-species TASEP in order to relate the voter model with the leaders in TASEP.

First, let us recall the color-position symmetry of TASEP in our notation.

Proposition 6.4
.

For the multi-type TASEP
\eta_{t}
with initial condition
\eta_{0}(x)=-x
, one has the following equality in distribution

\{-\eta_{t}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\overset{d}{=}\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(-x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}.

Proof.

Define a permutation
-\eta_{t}(x):=\eta(-x)
,
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. Then for
-\eta_{t}
one has the color-position symmetry in the form

\{-\eta_{t}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}\overset{d}{=}\{(-\eta_{t})^{-1}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}},

see
[
AAV
, Theorem 1.4]
, and also
[
BW1
]
,
[
BB
]
,
[
Buf
]
. We rewrite the right-hand side in order to use it below with the inverse of
\eta_{t}
rather than
-\eta_{t}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5
.

In the notation of Proposition
6.2
,
let
\mathcal{A}_{y,r_{2},r_{1}}
, for integers
y
and
r_{2}\leqslant r_{1}
, be the event that
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(r_{2})=\tilde{\nu}_{t}(r_{1})=y
and
\tilde{\nu}_{t}(r_{2}-1)\neq y
. Then

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mathcal{A}_{y,r_{2},r_{1}}\right)=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{in TASEP $\eta_{t}$ the particle of type $r_{2}$ is $r_{1}$-leader and is located at $y$}\right).

Proof.

We have:

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mathcal{A}_{y,r_{2},r_{1}}\right)=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\min\left(\{\eta_{t}(x)\}_{x:x\leqslant r_{1}}\right)=\eta_{t}(r_{2})=y\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\min\left(\{-\eta_{t}^{-1}(-x)\}_{x:x\leqslant r_{1}}\right)=-\eta_{t}^{-1}(-r_{2})=y\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\max\left(\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(-x)\}_{x:x\leqslant r_{1}}\right)=\eta_{t}^{-1}(-r_{2})=-y\right)=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\max\left(\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(x)\}_{x:x\leqslant r_{1}}\right)=\eta_{t}^{-1}(r_{2})=y\right),

(6.1)

where the first equality follows from Proposition
6.2
, the second equality follows from Proposition
6.4
, and the last equality is the particle-hole duality of TASEP (in the multi-type case, in our notation, it implies that
\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}
and
\{-\eta_{t}^{-1}(-x)\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}}
have the same distribution, since they follow the same evolution rules). The event from the last expression of (
6.1
) is exactly the event that the particle of type
r_{2}
is
r_{1}
-leader and stands at
y
, which completes the proof.
∎

7.
TASEP ranking process

In this section we introduce the
TASEP ranking process
and relate it to leaders in the multi-species TASEP.

7.1.
Definition

As before, consider a multi-species TASEP
\eta_{t}
which starts from the initial configuration
\eta_{0}(x)=-x
. For each TASEP configuration
\eta
define a map
\mathfrak{R}:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
via

\mathfrak{R}(x):=\mbox{the number of $i\leqslant x$ such that $\eta(i)\leqslant\eta(x)$.}

In words, for any position
x
we look at the type of the particle that stands there, and count how many particles of smaller type stand to the left of it; we also always count the particle at
x
itself. For the time evolution
\eta_{t}
, we obtain a process
\mathfrak{R}_{t}
. Clearly,
\mathfrak{R}_{0}(x)=1
, for all
x\in\mathbb{Z}
. Note that any particle of smaller type to the left of a particle at
x
must have exchanged with it at some previous moment. Therefore, we call the value
\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)
the
rank
of the particle standing at
x
, and we call the whole process
\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)
the
TASEP ranking process
.

It is a direct check that this is also a Markov projection of TASEP, which is updated according to the following local rules at neighboring sites: If
\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)=a\geqslant b=\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x+1)
, then the update
\mathfrak{R}_{t+0}(x)=b
,
\mathfrak{R}_{t+0}(x+1)=a+1
happens with rate 1. In the opposite case
\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)<\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x+1)
the update is not possible.

Let us remark that if we consider only the evolution of the set
\{x:\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)=1\}
, we recover the coalescence process
\tilde{\xi}_{t}
from Section
6.2
. More generally, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
the process
\mathfrak{R}_{t}
has a Markov projection
\mathfrak{R}_{t}^{(k)}
obtained via

\mathfrak{R}_{t}^{(k)}(x):=\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)+\infty\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)>k}.

In words, we can replace in the configuration of
\mathfrak{R}_{t}(x)
all species of type
>k
by a new type
\infty
; this will not affect the update rules, and will not change the evolution of the particles of types
1,2,\dots,k
.

\mathcal{C}_{1,2}(t):=\mathcal{C}_{1}(t)\cup\mathcal{C}_{2}(t)

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
k>0,s\geqslant 0
be integers. We have

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{ $(-k-s)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(t)$, $(-s)\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(t)$, $\{0,-1,-2,\dots,-s+1,-s-1,\dots,-k-s\}\notin\mathcal{C}_{1,2}(t)$}\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{particle of type $(k+s)$ is a 0-leader, and paticle of type $s$ is a (0,2)-leader}\right),

(7.1)

where in the event in the right-hand side we, as before, consider TASEP
\eta_{t}
which starts from the packed initial condition.

Proof.

Let us denote by
\max^{2}(\mathcal{S})
(
\min^{2}(\mathcal{S})
) the second largest (smallest, respectively) element of a set of numbers
\mathcal{S}
. We have

\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{ $(-k-s)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}(t)$, $(-s)\in\mathcal{C}_{2}(t)$, $\{0,-1,-2,\dots,-s+1,-s-1,\dots,-k-s\}\notin\mathcal{C}_{1,2}(t)$}\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\min\left(\{\eta_{t}(i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=\eta_{t}(-k-s),\min\nolimits^{2}\left(\{\eta_{t}(i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=\eta_{t}(-s)\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\min\nolimits\left(\{-\eta_{t}^{-1}(-i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=-\eta_{t}(-k-s),\min\nolimits^{2}\left(\{-\eta_{t}^{-1}(-i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=-\eta_{t}(-s)\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\max\nolimits\left(\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(-i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=\eta_{t}(-k-s),\max\nolimits^{2}\left(\{\eta_{t}^{-1}(-i)\}_{i\leqslant 0}\right)=\eta_{t}(-s)\right)\\
=\mathrm{Prob}\left(\mbox{particle of type $(k+s)$ is a 0-leader, and paticle of type $s$ is a (0,2)-leader}\right),

where the second equality follows from Proposition
6.4
, while the first and the fourth equalities are rephrasings of definitions.

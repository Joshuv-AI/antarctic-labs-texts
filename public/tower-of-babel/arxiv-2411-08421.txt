Modest Sets are Equivalent to PERs
Rahul Chhabra

Abstract
. The aim of this article is to give an expository account of the equivalence between modest sets and partial equivalence relations. Our proof is entirely self-contained in that we do not assume any knowledge of categorical realizability. At the heart of the equivalence lies the subquotient construction on a partial equivalence relation. The subquotient construction embeds the category of partial equivalence relations into the category of modest sets. We show that this embedding is a split essentially surjective functor, and thereby, an equivalence of categories. Our development is both constructive and predicative, and employs the language of homotopy type theory . All the mathematics presented in this article has been mechanised in Cubical Agda.



1.
Introduction

Kleene, in his seminal work
[
Kle45
]
developed the technique of “recursive realizability” to show how to give a
computational
meaning to the logic of Brouwer’s intuitionism. The realizability relationship is written
\mathtt{a}\Vdash x
and is read “
\mathtt{a}
realizes
x
” or “
\mathtt{a}
is a realizer of
x
”. We want to think of the realizability relationship as capturing the intuition that
\mathtt{a}
is some kind of “machine code representation” of a mathematical object
x
.

The discipline of categorical realizability emerged with Martin Hyland’s discovery of the effective topos
[
Hyl82
]
. Categorical realizability studies
realizability toposes
(such as the effective topos) and important subcategories thereof through the lens of categorical logic and topos theory.

The effective topos is the “world of recursive realizability”. It massively generalises Kleene’s original realizability interpretation from Heyting arithmetic to topos logic. Additionally, it serves as a semantic playground for studying computability theory
[
Bau06
]
and domain theory “synthetically”
[
Hyl91
]
.

Recall that a theorem due to Freyd
[
Fre06
]
tells us that any small complete category in
\mathsf{Sets}
is necessarily posetal. This result extends to all Grothendieck toposes, so that, in any Grothendieck topos, a small complete category is necessarily posetal
[
Shu10
]

[
Gub10
]
. The category of partial equivalence relations (when arranged as an internal category in the effective topos), however,
is
a non-posetal small complete category
[
Hyl88
]
. This fact is notable since it clearly highlights how realizability toposes differ from Grothendieck toposes. That this category is small and complete is also useful in the semantics of the polymorphic
\lambda
-calculus
[
LM91
]
.

Both modest sets and partial equivalence relations capture the intuitive notion of a “data type”. It is fitting that they are equivalent.
The aim of this article is to give a detailed and self-contained proof of the equivalence. Obviously, this fact is well-known to experts. It already shows up in Hyland’s articles on the effective topos
[
Hyl82
, Proposition 7.2]
and on PERs
[
Hyl88
, p. 151]
. I claim no originality for the mathematics presented here. Mistakes and opinions are my own.

Our development is completely
constructive
in that we do not assume the Law of Excluded Middle (LEM)
[
Uni13
, Section 3.4]
or the Axiom of Choice (AC)
[
Uni13
, Section 3.8]
. It is also
predicative
in that we do not assume the axiom of propositional resizing
[
Uni13
, Axiom 3.5.5]
. That said, experience suggests that any interesting applications of this equivalence require us to assume propositional resizing.

[

We assume familiarity with the concept of

For a universe

We use only two higher inductive types — propositional truncation and set quotient. We write

I wish to recall how we can eliminate propositional truncations into sets. It can be shown that functions

[
Kra15

We shall assume that the reader is familiar with the ideas of Part I of the HoTT Book

Combinatory Algebras, Assemblies, and Modest Sets

3.1.
Combinatory Algebras

While Kleene’s recursive realizability uses natural numbers, there is a large class of structures that give a notion of realizer. The one we focus on is the class of

Definition 3.1

(

Remark 3.2

It is not particularly difficult to deal with partiality using the standard techniques for talking about partiality in univalent mathematics
[
Kna20

3.2.
Assemblies and Modest Sets

From this point onwards, we will assume that we have a universe
\mathcal{U}
in which
\mathbb{A}
lives. Unless stated otherwise, whenever we speak of a set or a proposition, we always mean a
\mathcal{U}
-small set and a
\mathcal{U}
-small proposition respectively.

Definition 3.4

(

)
.

An
assembly
on a carrier set

consists of a
realizability relation

\_\Vdash_{X}\_:\mathbb{A}\to X\to\mathsf{Prop}
such that for any

\_\Vdash_{X}\_

Notation 3.5
.

When the assembly is obvious, we will write

\mathtt{r}\Vdash x
.

When

\mathtt{a}
realizes

\mathtt{a}
is a realizer of
x
”. Intuitively, an assembly is just a set equipped with an “encoding” of it’s elements in
\mathbb{A}

in

Definition 3.6

(

)
.

For assemblies

\mathtt{a}

\mathtt{t\;a}

Definition 3.7

(

)

For assemblies
\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle
and
\langle B,\_\Vdash_{B}\_,e_{B}\rangle

The following observation is trivial but useful:

Lemma 3.8

(Assembly Morphism Extensionality,

)
.

For assemblies
\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle
and
\langle B,\_\Vdash_{B}\_,e_{B}\rangle
and assembly morphisms
\langle f,e_{f}\rangle,\langle g,e_{g}\rangle:\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle\to\langle B,\_\Vdash_{B}\_,e_{B}\rangle

Lemma 3.9

(

)
.

Assemblies equipped with assembly morphisms and the obvious notion of identity and composition form a category
\mathsf{Asm}_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Among other things, the category
\mathsf{Asm}_{\mathbb{A}}
has finite limits, finite colimits, is (locally) cartesian closed, regular (but not exact), and has a classifier for regular subobjects. It is “nearly a topos” — it is a

Notation 3.10
.

In this article, we rarely deal with different assemblies on the same carrier. As such, we shall abuse notation and write “let

be an assembly” to mean “let
\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle
be an assembly on
A
”. Similarly, we shall write “let

be an assembly morphism” to mean “let
\langle f,e_{f}\rangle
be an assembly morphism from
\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle
to
\langle B,\_\Vdash_{B}\_,e_{B}\rangle
”.

Since the realizability relation can be more or less arbitrary, it is possible for elements to
share
realizers. Indeed, we can take any set
X
and define a realizability relation on
X
by saying that every
\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A}
realizes every
x:X
. This gives us the
codiscrete

2

assembly on
X
.

This can be undesirable, partly because it violates the “programmer’s intuition” of a data type. In a data type, one realizer should realize (at most) one element. To remedy this, we can impose the condition of modesty on an assembly:

Definition 3.11

(

)
.

An assembly
A
is said to be
modest
if, for any elements
x:A
and
y:A
, whenever there exists a realizer
\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A}
that realizes both
x
and
y
,
x
and
y
are equal. More formally,

\displaystyle\mathsf{isModest}:\mathsf{Asm}_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathsf{Prop}

\displaystyle\mathsf{isModest}\;\langle A,\_\Vdash_{A}\_,e_{A}\rangle\;:\equiv\forall\;(x\;y:A)\to\exists(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\;\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{A}x\times\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{A}y\to x=_{A}y

In other words, elements do not share realizers. Notice that since the carrier of an assembly is a set, being modest is a property and not structure. Modest assemblies are also known as modest sets, and that is also what we will prefer to call them. Modest sets appeared in Hyland’s original paper on the effective topos
[
Hyl82
]
under the name “strictly effective objects”. The terminological change to “modest sets” was suggested by Dana Scott.

Remark 3.12
.

In the literature, one sometimes find a definition of modesty that states that “for any
x:A
and
y:A
if
E_{A}(a)\cap E_{A}(b)\neq\emptyset
then
a
equals
b
”. This is a
negative
formulation of the same statement we have given in [Definition
3.11
]. The two definitions are classically equivalent, but, in a constructive setting, our
positive
formulation is easier to work with.

Modest sets form a full subcategory of the category of assemblies that we shall denote as
\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{A}}
. Despite being smaller than
\mathsf{Asm}_{\mathbb{A}}
, it has all finite limits and colimits, is (locally) cartesian closed and regular. Additionally, it is an

in
\mathsf{Asm}_{\mathbb{A}}
, so that if

is modest and
X
is an assembly then the exponential object
Y^{X}
is also modest.

4.
Partial Equivalence Relations

In this section, we look at the category of partial equivalence relations in some detail.

Definition 4.1

(
\mathsf{PER}_{\mathbb{A}}
,

)
.

To give a
partial equivalence relation
is to give a function
R:\mathbb{A}\to\mathbb{A}\to\mathsf{Prop}_{\mathcal{U}}
along with

(1)

Evidence of

\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A}
and
\mathtt{b}:\mathbb{A}

holds.

(2)

Evidence of

if
R(\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b})
and
R(\mathtt{b},\mathtt{c})
hold then
R(\mathtt{a},\mathtt{c})
holds.

Convention 4.2
.

We shall use the word “per” to refer to a term of type
\mathsf{PER}_{\mathbb{A}}
[Definition
4.1
].

Notation 4.3
.

We write
\mathtt{a}\approx_{R}\mathtt{b}
for
R(\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b})
. For instance, we shall write “
\mathtt{i\;a}\approx_{R}\mathtt{i\;a}
holds iff
\mathtt{a}\approx_{R}\mathtt{a}
holds” instead of “

holds iff

holds”.

(Domain of a per,

)

The

is the type of all

such that
\mathtt{a}\approx_{R}\mathtt{a}
holds. For a per

Notation 4.5
.

We generally write elements of

as pairs
\langle\mathtt{r},r_{\mathtt{r}}\rangle
where
\mathtt{r}
:
\mathbb{A}
is a realizer and

is related to itself.

Intuition 4.6

We want to think about a per

as encoding a

Construction 4.15

(Composite Morphisms,

)

For pers
R

Lemma 5.11

(

The action of the subquotient functor on per morphisms [Construction

Let us say we have been given a morphism

in the fibre space over

and

holds. By the proposition-level elimination principle for set-quotients, it suffices to show that

holds when we have actual representatives for

and

\mathtt{x}
and
\mathtt{y}
be trackers [Definition

We can refine our goal to showing that

\mathtt{y}

\mathtt{y\;r}\Vdash_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(S)}f([\langle\mathtt{r},e_{\mathtt{r}}\rangle])

.

6.1.
Canonical PER of a Modest Set

Before we define the canonical per, we prove a helpful lemma.

Lemma 6.1
.

For any modest set
M
, and realizers
\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A}
and
\mathtt{b}:\mathbb{A}
, the type
(x:M)\times\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x\times\mathtt{b}\Vdash_{M}x
is a proposition.

Proof.

Since
\_\Vdash_{M}\_
is a proposition, it suffices to show that any
x:M
and
x^{\prime}:M
that are realized by both
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{b}
are necessarily equal. But this follows immediately from the modesty of
M
, since
x
and
x^{\prime}
share not one but two realizers!
∎

Definition 6.2

(

)
.

The canonical per of a modest set
M
relates
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{b}
if
(x:M)\times\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x\times\mathtt{b}\Vdash_{M}x
holds.

Proof.

The type is a proposition [Lemma
6.1
]. It remains to show symmetry and transitivity.

Symmetry obviously holds by a simple re-arrangement of the data.

For transitivity, consider
\mathtt{a}
,
\mathtt{b}
and
\mathtt{c}
elements of
\mathbb{A}
such that we have
x:M
realized by
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{b}
, and
x^{\prime}:M
realized by
\mathtt{b}
and
\mathtt{c}
. Note that
x=x^{\prime}
holds since
x
and
x^{\prime}
share a realizer
\mathtt{b}
. As such, we have that
\mathtt{a}\Vdash x^{\prime}
and
\mathtt{c}\Vdash x
hold as well.

We need to choose a
x^{\prime\prime}:M
that is realized by both
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{c}
. Obviously, we can choose
x^{\prime\prime}:=x
for this.
∎

6.2.
The Forward Direction
M\to\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))

To define the forward direction, we would need to introduce an element of
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
,
which would require us to furnish an element
x:M
along with a realizer
\mathtt{a}
that realizes
x
.
Of course, we have an input
M
, so the
x:M
is taken care of, but how do we possibly find an actual realizer for arbitrary
x:M
? We do not!
The crucial observation is that the choice of realizer does
not
matter — the main map we will be defining will turn out to be naïvely constant. This will allow us to appeal to the recursion principle of propositional truncation to define our underlying map. Showing that this map is tracked is then straightforward.

Definition 6.3

(

)
.

For any
x:M
, we define a function

\displaystyle\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}:(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\times\mathtt{a}\Vdash x\to\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))

\displaystyle\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}:\equiv\lambda\langle\mathtt{a},e_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\to[\langle\mathtt{a},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{a}}[x],e_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\rangle]

mapping any
\mathtt{a}
that realizes
x
to the partial equivalence class determined by
\mathtt{a}
.

Lemma 6.4

(

)
.

For any
x:M
, the map
\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}\;
is naïvely constant.

Proof.

We need to show that for any
\langle\mathtt{r},e_{\mathtt{r}}[x]\rangle
and
\langle\mathtt{s},e_{\mathtt{s}}[x]\rangle
in
(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\times\mathtt{a}\Vdash x
we have that the partial equivalence classes
[\langle\mathtt{r},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{r}}[x],e_{\mathtt{r}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
and
[\langle\mathtt{s},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{s}}[x],e_{\mathtt{s}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
are equal, for which it suffices to show that
\mathtt{r}\approx_{\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)}\mathtt{s}
holds. We need to furnish an element of
M
that is realized by both
\mathtt{r}
and
\mathtt{s}
. By assumption,
x
is a valid choice.
∎

Construction 6.5

(

)
.

We can construct the map
\mathsf{forward}:M\to\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
.

Proof.

We need to map a given
x:M
to a partial equivalence class
\mathsf{SubQuotient}\\
(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
.

Since
M
is an assembly, we have a witness
e_{x}:\exists(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\;\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x
that a realizer for
x
exists. Since
\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}
is a naïvely constant map, we can apply the set-level recursion principle for propositional truncation on
e_{x}
and
\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}
.

This gives us an actual partial equivalence class
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
and thus our construction is complete.
∎

Notably, when
e:\exists(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\;\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x
is of the form
|\langle\mathtt{a},e_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}
, we have that
\mathsf{forward}(x)\doteq\;\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}(\langle\mathtt{a},e_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle)\doteq\;[\langle\mathtt{a},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{a}}[x],e_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
holds by definition.

Lemma 6.6

(

)
.

The map
\mathsf{forward}\;
is tracked.

Proof.

We need to show that there exists some
\mathtt{t}:\mathbb{A}
such that for any
x:M
and for any
\mathtt{a}
that realizes
x
we have that
\mathtt{t\;a}
realizes
\mathsf{forward}(x)
.

We claim that the identity combinator
\mathtt{i}
is a tracker for
\mathsf{forward}
.

Let us assume we have been given
x:M
and a realizer
\mathtt{a}
for
x
. We need to show that
\mathtt{i\;a}=\mathtt{a}
realizes
\mathsf{forward}(x)
.

Since
M
is an assembly, we have evidence
e_{x}
that
x
has a realizer. Recall that
\mathsf{forward}(x)
is defined by recursion on this very
e_{x}
. By the proposition-level elimination principle for propositional truncations, it is enough to show that
\mathtt{a}
realizes
\mathsf{forward}(x)
when
e_{x}
is of the form
|\langle\mathtt{b},e_{\mathtt{b}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}
where
\mathtt{b}:\mathbb{A}
is a realizer and
e_{\mathtt{b}}[x]
is evidence that
b\Vdash x
holds.

By calculating

\displaystyle\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))}\mathsf{forward}(x)

\displaystyle\doteq\{e_{x}\text{ is of the form }|\langle\mathtt{b},e_{\mathtt{b}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}\}

\displaystyle\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))}\mathsf{forwardMainMap}_{x}(\langle b,e_{\mathtt{b}}[x]\rangle)

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{forwardMainMap}\}

\displaystyle\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))}[\langle\mathtt{b},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{b}}[x],e_{\mathtt{b}}[x]\rangle\rangle]

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of subquotient realizability [Construction~\ref{def:realizability-for-subquotient}]}\}

\displaystyle\mathtt{a}\approx_{\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)}\mathtt{b}

we can refine our goal to showing that
\mathtt{a}\approx_{\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)}\mathtt{b}
holds. This is trivial, since
x
is realized by both
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{b}
.
∎

Thus, we have shown :

Corollary 6.7

(

)
.

We have the assembly morphism
\mathsf{forward}:M\to\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
with underlying map
\mathsf{forward}\;
[Construction
6.5
] and the proof of [Lemma
6.6
] as evidence of
\mathsf{forward}\;
being tracked.

6.3.
The Backward Direction
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M

We shall define a function
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
and then show that the appropriate coherence conditions hold for
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}
, so that we can apply the set-level recursion principle for set quotients to get a function
\mathsf{backward}:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
. After that, it is easy to show that
\mathsf{backward}\;
is tracked and thus we will get a assembly morphism from
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
to
M
.

Definition 6.8

(

)
.

The function
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
is defined by

\displaystyle\mathsf{backwardMainMap}:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M

\displaystyle\mathsf{backwardMainMap}:\equiv\lambda\langle\mathtt{a},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{a}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\rangle\to x

Lemma 6.9

(

)
.

The function
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
maps elements of
\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
that are related by the
\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)
relation to equal elements of
M
.

Proof.

Let us say we have
\langle\mathtt{a},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{a}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\rangle
and
\langle\mathtt{b},\langle x^{\prime},e_{\mathtt{b}}[x^{\prime}],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{b}}[x^{\prime}]\rangle\rangle
in
\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
along with evidence
\langle x^{\prime\prime},e_{\mathtt{a}}[x^{\prime\prime}],e_{\mathtt{b}}[x^{\prime\prime}]\rangle
that they are related by the
\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)
relation. We need to show that
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{a},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{a}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{a}}[x]\rangle\rangle)\doteq x
equals
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{b},\langle x^{\prime},e_{\mathtt{b}}[x^{\prime}],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{b}}[x^{\prime}]\rangle\rangle)\doteq x^{\prime}
.

A simple calculation

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=\{x\text{ and }x^{\prime\prime}\text{ share the realizer }\mathtt{a}\}

\displaystyle x^{\prime\prime}

\displaystyle=\{x^{\prime\prime}\text{ and }x^{\prime}\text{ share the realizer }\mathtt{b}\}

\displaystyle x^{\prime}

suffices to show this.
∎

Construction 6.10

(

)
.

We have a map obtained by applying set-quotient recursion to upgrade the map
\mathsf{backwardMainMap}
to a full-fledged map
\mathsf{backward}:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
.

All that remains to check is that
\mathsf{backward}
is tracked.

Lemma 6.11

(

)
.

If
\mathtt{a}
:
\mathbb{A}
realizes
q:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
, then it also realizes
\mathsf{backward}(q)
.

Proof.

By the proposition-level set-quotient elimination principle on
q
, it is enough to show the lemma when we have a representative for
q
. Let
q
be represented by
\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}\rangle\rangle
. We need to show that if
\mathtt{a}
:
\mathbb{A}
realizes
[\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
then it also realizes
\mathsf{backward}([\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle])\doteq\mathsf{backwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}\rangle\rangle)\doteq x
.

Since
\mathtt{a}
realizes
[\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
, we have that
\mathtt{a}\approx_{\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)}\mathtt{q}
holds, which in turn means that we have an
x^{\prime}:M
realized by both
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{q}
. Now,
x
and
x^{\prime}
share a realizer in
\mathtt{q}
, so they are equal. Because
\mathtt{a}
already realizes
x^{\prime}
and
x^{\prime}
equals
x
,
\mathtt{a}
also realizes
x
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 6.12

(

)
.

The map
\mathsf{backward}:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))\to M
is tracked and is thus an assembly morphism.

Proof.

We claim that
\mathtt{i}
is a tracker, that is, for any
q:\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
and
\mathtt{a}
that realizes
q
we have that
\mathtt{i\;a}
realizes
\mathsf{backward}(q)
holds. Because
\mathtt{i\;a}
is equal to
\mathtt{a}
, our goal is reduced to showing that
\mathtt{a}
realizes
\mathsf{backward}(q)
; at which point we can simply apply the previous lemma [Lemma
6.11
].
∎

6.4.
Isomorphism Coherences

Our final goal is to prove that
\mathsf{backward}\;\circ\;\mathsf{forward}=\mathsf{id}_{M}
and
\mathsf{forward}\circ\mathsf{backward}=\mathsf{id}_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))}
hold.

Since assembly morphisms form a set, both of these types are propositions. As such, we can apply the proposition-level elimination principles for set-quotients and propositional truncations to establish these coherences.

Lemma 6.13

(

)
.

We have that
\mathsf{forward}\circ\mathsf{backward}=\mathsf{id}_{\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))}
holds.

Proof.

By assembly morphism extensionality [Lemma
3.8
] and proposition-level elimination rule for set quotients, it suffices to show that for any
\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
,
\mathsf{forward}(\mathsf{backward}([\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]))
equals
[\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
.

Recall that
\mathsf{forward}
is defined by recursion on the witness
e_{x}:\exists(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\;\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x
that there exists a realizer for
x
. By the elimination rule for propositional truncations, we can assume that
e_{x}
is of the form
|\langle\mathtt{p},e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}
, where
\mathtt{p}
is a realizer and
e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]
witnesses that
\mathtt{p}\Vdash_{M}x
holds.

We calculate

\displaystyle\mathsf{forward}(\mathsf{backward}([\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]))

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{backward}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{forward}(\mathsf{backwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle)

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{backwardMainMap}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{forward}(x)

\displaystyle\doteq\{e_{x}\text{ is of the form }|\langle\mathtt{p},e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{forwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{p},e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]\rangle)

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{forwardMainMap}\}

\displaystyle[\langle\mathtt{p},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{p}}[x],e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]\rangle\rangle]

to refine our goal to showing that
[\langle\mathtt{p},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{p}}[x],e_{\mathtt{p}}[x]\rangle\rangle]=[\langle\mathtt{q},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{q}}[x],e^{\prime}_{\mathtt{q}}[x]\rangle\rangle]
holds. This amounts to showing that

\mathtt{p}\approx_{\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M)}\mathtt{q}
holds, which is obviously true since
x
is realized by both
\mathtt{p}
and
\mathtt{q}
.
∎

Lemma 6.14

(

)
.

We have that
\mathsf{backward}\circ\mathsf{forward}=\mathsf{id}_{M}
holds.

Proof.

Similar to the previous lemma [Lemma
6.13
], it suffices to show pointwise equality for the underlying functions on both sides. Our goal, then, is to show that for any
x:M
,
\mathsf{backward}(\mathsf{forward}(x))=x
holds.

By the proposition-level elimination principle of the propositional truncation, we can assume that the witness
e_{x}:\exists(\mathtt{a}:\mathbb{A})\;\mathtt{a}\Vdash_{M}x
is of the form
|\langle\mathtt{x},e_{\mathtt{x}}[x]\rangle|_{-1}
.

We calculate

\displaystyle\mathsf{backward}(\mathsf{forward}(x))

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{forward}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{backward}(\mathsf{forwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{x},e_{\mathtt{x}}[x]\rangle)

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{forwardMainMap}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{backward}([\langle\mathtt{x},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{x}}[x],e_{\mathtt{x}}[x]\rangle\rangle])

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{backward}\}

\displaystyle\mathsf{backwardMainMap}(\langle\mathtt{x},\langle x,e_{\mathtt{x}}[x],e_{\mathtt{x}}[x]\rangle\rangle)

\displaystyle\doteq\{\text{by definition of }\mathsf{backwardMainMap}\}

\displaystyle x

to see that the intended equality holds by definition!
∎

The coherences [Lemma
6.13
] and [Lemma
6.14
] together imply :

Theorem 6.15

(

)
.

For any modest set
M
, we have an isomorphism
M\cong\\
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\mathsf{CanonicalPER}(M))
.
In other words, the subquotient functor
\mathsf{SubQuotient}(\_):\mathsf{PER}_{\mathbb{A}}\to\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{A}}
is a
split essentially surjective
functor.

7.
Concluding Remarks

Our hike is over, and it is time to enjoy the view. At long last, we can state and prove the theorem we had originally promised!

Theorem 7.1
.

The subquotient functor is an
equivalence
of categories
\mathsf{PER}_{\mathbb{A}}
and
\mathsf{Mod}_{\mathbb{A}}
.

Proof.

The subquotient functor is both fully faithful [Theorem
5.16
] and split essentially surjective [Theorem
6.15
].
∎

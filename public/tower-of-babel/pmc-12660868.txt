Improved deformation reconstruction of composite material structures based on optical fiber sensing
Authors: Jun Huang, Yuwei Sun, Yu Peng, Dongsheng Zhang, Ruiya Li, Sheng Yin, Zhenfeng Peng, Quanyong Huang, Quan Liu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Due to high strength and lightweight, composite material structures have been widely used in various industries such as aerospace, automotive, civil engineering, and healthcare. Real-time deformation monitoring based on optical fiber sensing technology holds immense potential in composite materials’ structural health monitoring (SHM). In this study, structural deformation reconstruction algorithms have been investigated by improving the slope recursion algorithm and the Ko displacement theory. The slope recursion algorithm is enhanced through angle averaging. Cross-validation is employed to increase strain-fitting accuracy for the Ko displacement theory. Finite element simulation analysis and deformation reconstruction experiments of a carbon fiber composite plate were conducted to validate the deformation reconstruction algorithms. Fiber Bragg grating (FBG) sensors were applied to measure quasi-distributed strain as inputs for the algorithms. The results show that the overall reconstruction accuracy increased by 1.91% with the improved slope method and by 0.92% with the optimized Ko displacement theory. Deformation reconstruction experiments of a composite material reinforced structure were carried out, and 70 FBG sensors were installed on its surface for strain field measurement. The experimental results indicate the feasibility of the proposed deformation reconstruction methods for complex structures.

Introduction

Carbon fiber-reinforced composites have become indispensable structural materials in aerospace, automotive, civil engineering, and healthcare industries due to their exceptional specific stiffness, strength, corrosion resistance, and vibration damping characteristics1. Their widespread adoption is exemplified by the Boeing 787 and Airbus A350, where composites constitute approximately 50% and 52% of the airframe weight, respectively, including critical load-bearing components such as wings and fuselages2. However, the inherent anisotropy and structural complexity of composite materials render them susceptible to internal damage mechanisms—including delamination, fiber breakage, and matrix cracking—that often remain undetectable through visual inspection yet can precipitate catastrophic failures. Consequently, deformation monitoring has emerged as a crucial component of structural health monitoring (SHM), enabling damage assessment, residual life prediction, and operational safety assurance3,4.

Traditional deformation monitoring techniques, primarily strain gauges and digital image correlation (DIC), exhibit significant limitations for composite applications. Although strain gauges provide localized strain measurements and have been implemented in minimally invasive forms for aerospace surfaces, they suffer from electromagnetic interference, temperature sensitivity, and complex wiring requirements for large-scale deployment5,6. Similarly, DIC offers full-field measurement capabilities and has been employed to characterize nanocomposite deformation at multiple scales, but its accuracy diminishes under poor lighting conditions or on surfaces with inadequate texture7,8.

Fiber optic sensing technology has consequently gained prominence as a transformative monitoring solution, combining intrinsic advantages such as small size, immunity to electromagnetic interference, and compatibility with embedded integration9. Notably, Fiber Bragg Grating (FBG) sensors have been successfully surface-mounted on wing skins for impact damage detection10 and embedded within carbon composites for in-situ SHM11. The effectiveness of FBG-based monitoring, however, fundamentally depends on the deployment of robust deformation reconstruction algorithms to convert discrete strain measurements into displacement fields. Four primary methodologies have been developed:

The inverse Finite Element Method (iFEM) offers model-independent reconstruction using strain data without requiring loading conditions, making it suitable for complex structures like wind turbine towers. Nevertheless, its accuracy is constrained by mesh design and suffers from singularity issues in irregular geometries, as addressed through enhanced formulations like the inverse local radial point interpolation method12,13.

Modal superposition methods utilize strain mode shapes for deformation mapping and have been improved via transfer learning to enhance wing reconstruction accuracy14,15. Their performance, however, relies heavily on accurate finite element models and is limited under nonlinear deformations.

The Ko displacement theory provides a computationally efficient solution for beam-like structures through strain integration and has been applied in composite cantilever beams using distributed sensing16,17. Its applicability remains restricted to unidirectional bending and requires dense sensor placement for reasonable accuracy.

Curvature-based methods reconstruct deformed shapes by converting strain to curvature and performing geometric integration. Although advanced implementations have incorporated directional correction and Runge-Kutta solvers for three-dimensional wing deformation18,19, these methods are generally limited to unidirectional reconstruction and require significant sensor counts.

Each algorithm exhibits particular limitations: iFEM struggles with mesh dependency and singularities; modal methods require precise numerical models and are unsuitable for nonlinear responses; Ko theory is restricted to simple bending; and curvature methods lack generality for complex deformations. A critical common shortcoming is their susceptibility to error accumulation at structural boundaries and limited accuracy under complex support conditions.

Consequently, numerous scholars have investigated methods to enhance the accuracy and stability of these algorithms, proposing various improvement strategies. For instance, Zhao et al.20 integrated isogeometric analysis into the conventional iFEM algorithm, reducing the deformation reconstruction RMSE of a variable-cross-section panel-beam structure under uniform load from 2.78 mm to 1.34 mm. Esposito et al.21 introduced a hybrid shell-beam inverse finite element method for the shape sensing of stiffened thin-walled structures, which achieved an accuracy improvement of 1–1.2% compared to a pure shell-element model. Chen et al.22 developed a modified modal superposition method for an aircraft tooling positioner, reducing the absolute error in deformation reconstruction from 0.133 mm to 0.059 mm compared to the traditional modal superposition approach. Li et al.23 proposed a curvature-optimized deformation reconstruction method, which decreased the relative error by 3.3%, with the maximum absolute error reducing from 3.743 mm to 2.627 mm.

Slope recursion algorithm

The structural deformation reconstruction method based on curvature transformation is to establish a relationship between surface strain and surface curvature to convert surface strain into discrete curvature. Subsequently, using the curvature information of the structural surface, a recursive algorithm and an interpolation algorithm are employed for point-to-line and line-to-surface modelling, thus reconstructing the three-dimensional shape of a surface. In this paper, the slope recursion algorithm is used for curvature transformation, and its schematic diagram is shown in Fig. 2.

Fitting of strain data based on cross validation

The structural deformation reconstruction methods using the slope recursion algorithm and Ko displacement theory are both based on discrete concepts. The number of specific sensor placements within a certain range is positively correlated with reconstruction accuracy. Considering the cost factors in engineering, it’s challenging to deploy a large number of sensors. It is difficult to obtain the strain at structural boundaries using optical fiber sensors, which involves data expansion and complementation of boundary strain through fitting or interpolation. However, there is a lack of universality for the fitting of strain data due to the complexity of the data and model.

The minimum error strain fitting method based on cross-validation can be proposed. The estimated strain at the fixed end can be calculated through the root mean square error between the measured strain data and the finite element simulation data. The cross-validation of the fitting result will be carried out. A fusion dataset will be constructed by using the calculated strain at the fixed support end and the measured strain. The fitting data will be compared with the corresponding test data, using the root mean square error as a quantification standard. Multiple sets of fitting results will be obtained through iterative cross-validation, getting the final fitting result with minimum error. The two-terminal recursive weighted reconstruction method

Experiments and results

To verify the proposed deformation reconstruction methods, deformation reconstruction experiments of a composite plate were carried out using the setup shown in Fig. 9. One end of the plate was fixed, and the other end was free. To ensure the accuracy of the reconstruction algorithm, the strain measurement system must be equipped with high accuracy and stability. The FBG sensors require an extensive strain tolerance ( ≥ ± 5000 µε), high accuracy (± 1–5 µε), and sub-microstrain resolution to ensure reconstruction fidelity. Strategic wavelength spacing (> 3–5 nm) prevents spectral overlap. The interrogator must provide multi-channel synchronous acquisition, a broad optical spectrum (≥ 80 nm), and pm-level resolution, with sampling rates adapted to static (≥ 1 Hz) or dynamic (≥ 100 Hz) conditions. 20 FBG sensors in 4 strings were installed on the surface of the plate at the positions shown in Fig. 5 to measure strain. An FBG interrogator (type: HI-8000-16, resolustion: 0.1 pm, accuracy: ±1pm, channel: 16, sampling rate: 50 Hz) and a computer was used to monitor the wavelengths of FBG sensors. A laser displacement sensor with a type of MSE-TS803 and a linear accuracy of ± 0.05% F.S. was employed to detect the deformation displacement of the plate under loads.

Conclusions

Structural health monitoring is essential for composite material structures for damage assessment and predictive maintenance to prevent potential safety risks. Deformation monitoring based on optical fiber sensing technology plays an important role in structural health monitoring. In this paper, deformation reconstruction methods of composite material structures based on optical fiber sensors have been investigated by improving the slope recursion algorithm and Ko displacement theory. The method of angle averaging was employed to optimize the slope recursion algorithm, solving the problem of accumulating errors caused by single-sided slope approximation. The cross-validation method with finite element simulated data was used to obtain the fitting strain data with smaller errors and improve the reconstruction accuracy of the Ko displacement theory.

Numerical analysis and experimental investigation on a composite plate and a composite material reinforced structure were conducted to validate the effectiveness of the improved slope recursion algorithm and the improved Ko displacement theory, which was validated through finite element simulation of composite material structures. FBG sensors were installed on the carbon fiber composite plate to measure the strain under deformation. The experimental results of structural deformation reconstruction demonstrate that the improved reconstruction algorithms achieve higher reconstruction accuracy compared with the initial algorithms. Compression experiments were carried out on the reinforced structure. The results of compression experiments on the reinforced structure indicate the feasibility of the proposed deformation reconstruction method for complex structures.

It is important to note that the algorithms presented in this paper were validated only under single-point loading conditions. Their robustness under more complex multi-point or random loads, which are common in practical applications, remains a subject for future investigation and represents a key limitation of the current study. Further research will focus on the sensor layout scheme for complex composite structures and the analysis of reconstruction accuracy based on the measurement of actual deformation results. Additionally, the relationship between high-resolution strain measurement using optical frequency domain reflectometry (OFDR) and deformation reconstruction accuracy will be a key area of study. The temperature compensation methods of FBG sensors will be investigated for the structural condition monitoring of composite material structures.

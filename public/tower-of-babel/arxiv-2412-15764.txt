The variety of complemented lattices where the Sasaki operations form an adjoint pair
Václav Cenker, Ivan Chajda, Helmut Länger

Abstract
The Sasaki projection was introduced as a mapping from the lattice of closed subspaces of a Hilbert space onto one of its segments. To use this projection and its dual so-called Sasaki operations were introduced by the second two authors in [ 4 ] and [ 6 ] . In [ 6 ] there are described several classes of lattices, λ \lambda -lattices and semirings where the Sasaki operations form an adjoint pair. In the present paper we prove that the class of complemented lattices with this property forms a variety and we explicitly state its defining identities. Moreover, we prove that this variety 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is congruence permutable and regular. Hence every ideal I I of some member 𝐋 \mathbf{L} of 𝒱 \mathcal{V} is a kernel of some congruence on 𝐋 \mathbf{L} . Finally, we determine a finite basis of so-called ideal terms and describe the congruence Θ I \Theta_{I} determined by the ideal I I .

It was proved by G. Birkhoff and J. von Neumann
[
2
]
in 1936, and independently by K. Husimi
[
8
]
in 1937 that the lattice
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H})
of closed subspaces of a Hilbert space
\mathbf{H}
is orthomodular. For a given element
a\in\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H})
, U. Sasaki
[
10
]
introduced the projection
p_{a}
of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H})
onto its interval
[0,a]
as follows:

p_{a}(x)=(x\vee a^{\prime})\wedge a.

The name
Sasaki projection
was given later by M. Nakamura
[
9
]
. Also, we can formulate its dual, i.e. the projection

\overline{p_{a}}(x)=(x\wedge a)\vee a^{\prime}

of
\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{H})
onto its interval
[a^{\prime},1]
.

However, we can extend these definitions by introducing the corresponding concepts in every bounded lattice
(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
equipped with a unary operation
′
. Using this framework, we define two binary operations on
L
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded lattice with unary operation
′
. By the
Sasaki operations
on
\mathbf{L}
we mean the following term operations:

x\odot y=(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y\quad\text{and}\quad x\to y=x^{\prime}\vee(x\wedge y).

We say that they form an
adjoint pair
on
\mathbf{L}
if the following holds:

(A)

x\odot y\leq z
if and only if
x\leq y\to z
.

Lemma 1
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded lattice with unary operation
′
. If the Sasaki operations
\odot
,
\to
form an adjoint pair in
\mathbf{L}
, then the unary operation
′
is a complementation.

Proof.

Because
1\odot a=a\leq 1
for each
a\in L
, by (A), we derive
1\leq a\to 1=a^{\prime}\vee(a\wedge 1)=a^{\prime}\vee a
. Since
0\leq a\to 0
, we also have
a^{\prime}\wedge a=(0\vee a^{\prime})\wedge a=0\odot a\leq 0
, i.e.
a^{\prime}\wedge a=0
. Thus,
′
is a complementation.
∎

Hence, investigating whether the Sasaki operations in lattices with a unary operation form an adjoint pair makes sense only for lattices with complementation. At first we determine the class of such lattices.

Theorem 2
.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be a bounded lattice with complementation. Then the following are equivalent:

(a)

x\odot y\leq z
implies
x\leq y\to z
for all
x,y,z\in L
,

(b)

\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
x\vee y^{\prime}\approx y^{\prime}\vee\big((x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y\big)
,

(c)

x^{\prime}\leq y
implies
y=x^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge x)
.

Furthermore, the following are equivalent:

(d)

x\leq y\to z
implies
x\odot y\leq z
for all
x,y,z\in L
,

(e)

\mathbf{L}
satisfies the identity
x\wedge y\approx x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime}\big)
,

(f)

x\leq y
implies
x=(y^{\prime}\vee x)\wedge y
.

Proof.

(a)
\Rightarrow
(c):

Assume
x^{\prime}\leq y
for
x,y\in L
. Then,
y\odot x=(y\vee x^{\prime})\wedge x=y\wedge x
. According to (a) we obtain

y\leq x\to(y\wedge x)=x^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge x)\leq y\vee(y\wedge x)=y

and hence
y=x^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge x)
.

(c)
\Rightarrow
(b):

Because
y^{\prime}\leq x\vee y^{\prime}
, we apply (c) to get
x\vee y^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\vee\big((x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y)
.

(b)
\Rightarrow
(a):

Assume
x\odot y\leq z
. Using (b) we derive

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\leq x\vee y^{\prime}=y^{\prime}\vee\big((x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y\big)=y^{\prime}\vee\Big(\big((x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y\big)\wedge y\Big)=y^{\prime}\vee\big((x\odot y)\wedge y\big)\leq

\displaystyle\leq y^{\prime}\vee(z\wedge y)=y\to z.

Similarly, we prove the remaining.

(d)
\Rightarrow
(f):

Let
x\leq y
. Then
y\to x=y^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge x)=y^{\prime}\vee x
. By (d) we have
(y^{\prime}\vee x)\odot y\leq x
whence

(y^{\prime}\vee x)\wedge y=\big((y^{\prime}\vee x)\big)\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y=(y^{\prime}\vee x)\odot y\leq x.

Conversely, from the assumption and fact that
x\leq y^{\prime}\vee x
we conclude
x\leq(y^{\prime}\vee x)\wedge y
. Consequently,
x=(y^{\prime}\vee x)\wedge y
.

(f)
\Rightarrow
(e):

Because
x\wedge y\leq x
, we apply (f) to get
x\wedge y=x\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee x^{\prime}\big)
.

(e)
\Rightarrow
(d):

Assume
x\leq y\to z
. Then, using (e) we obtain

\displaystyle x\odot y

\displaystyle=(x\vee y^{\prime})\wedge y\leq\big((y\to z)\vee y^{\prime}\big)\wedge y=\Big(\big(y^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge z)\big)\vee y^{\prime}\Big)\wedge y=

\displaystyle=\big(y^{\prime}\vee(y\wedge z)\big)\wedge y=y\wedge z\leq z.

∎

In what follows we describe some important congruence properties of the variety
\mathcal{V}
.

Recall the following facts concerning congruence properties.

Let
\mathbf{A}=(A,F)
be an algebra and
\BCon\mathbf{A}=(\Con\mathbf{A},\subseteq)
denote its congruence lattice. Then
\mathbf{A}
is called

(i)

congruence permutable
if
\Theta\circ\Phi=\Phi\circ\Theta
for all
\Theta,\Phi\in\Con\mathbf{A}
,

(ii)

congruence distributive
if
\BCon\mathbf{A}
is distributive,

(iii)

arithmetical
if it is both congruence permutable and congruence distributive,

(iv)

regular
if every congruence on
\mathbf{A}
is determined by each of its classes, i.e. if
a\in A
,
\Theta,\Phi\in\Con\mathbf{A}
and
[a]\Theta=[a]\Phi
together imply
\Theta=\Phi
,

see e.g.
[
3
]
and
[
7
]
. A variety is said to have one of the above congruence properties if every of its members has this property. The following results are well-known:

(i)

A variety is congruence permutable if and only if there exists a so-called Mal’cev term, i.e. a ternary term
p
satisfying the identities
p(x,x,z)\approx z
and
p(x,z,z)\approx x
,

(ii)

if in a variety there exists a so-called majority term, i.e. a ternary term
m
satisfying the identities
m(x,x,z)\approx m(x,y,x)\approx x
and
m(x,z,z)\approx z
then this variety is congruence distributive,

(iii)

a variety is regular if and only if there exists some positive integer
n
and ternary terms
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
such that
t_{1}(x,y,z)=\cdots=t_{n}(x,y,z)=z
if and only if
x=y
.

Theorem 6
.

The variety
\mathcal{V}
is arithmetical and regular.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{V}
is a variety of lattices, it is congruence distributive (namely, it contains the majority term
m(x,y,z):=(x\vee y)\wedge(y\vee z)\wedge(z\vee x)
). We prove congruence permutability of
\mathcal{V}
. Consider the term

p(x,y,z):=\Big(x\wedge\big((z\wedge y)\vee y^{\prime}\big)\Big)\vee\Big(z\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee y^{\prime}\big)\Big).

Using (f) of Theorem
2
we compute

\displaystyle p(x,x,z)

\displaystyle\approx\Big(x\wedge\big((z\wedge x)\vee x^{\prime}\big)\Big)\vee\Big(z\wedge\big((x\wedge x)\vee x^{\prime}\big)\Big)\approx(z\wedge x)\vee z\approx z,

\displaystyle p(x,z,z)

\displaystyle\approx\Big(x\wedge\big((z\wedge z)\vee z^{\prime}\big)\Big)\vee\Big(z\wedge\big((x\wedge z)\vee z^{\prime}\big)\Big)\approx x\vee(x\wedge z)\approx x.

Hence
p
is a Mal’cev term. Thus
\mathcal{V}
is congruence permutable and therefore arithmetical. In order to prove regularity of
\mathcal{V}
take
n:=2
and consider the ternary terms

\displaystyle t_{1}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle:=u\wedge z,

\displaystyle t_{2}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle:=u^{\prime}\vee z

where
u:=(x\wedge y)\vee(x\vee y)^{\prime}
. Evidently,
t_{1}(x,y,z)=t_{2}(x,y,z)=z
provided
x=y
. Conversely assume
t_{1}(x,y,z)=t_{2}(x,y,z)=z
. Then

u=u\vee(u\wedge z)=u\vee t_{1}(x,y,z)=u\vee t_{2}(x,y,z)=u\vee(u^{\prime}\vee z)=1.

Since
x\wedge y\leq x\vee y
, we use (f) of Theorem
2
to get

x\wedge y=(x\vee y)\wedge\big((x\wedge y)\vee(x\vee y)^{\prime}\big)=(x\vee y)\wedge u=(x\vee y)\wedge 1=x\vee y

which together with
x\wedge y\leq x,y\leq x\vee y
yields
x=y
.
∎

Using advantage of congruence properties of the variety
\mathcal{V}
, we go on with a finer analysis of congruences and their classes.

Recall the following definitions.

Let
\mathbf{L}=(L,\vee,\wedge,{}^{\prime},0,1)
be some member of
\mathcal{V}
and
I\subseteq L
. An
ideal term in
y_{1},\ldots,y_{m}
is a term
t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
satisfying the identity

t(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},1,\ldots,1)\approx 1

The subset

of

is called

in

Moreover,
I
is called an

of
\mathbf{L}
if it is closed under all ideal terms. It is well-known (cf. Lemma 10.1.3 and Theorem 10.1.10 of

basis of ideal terms
if a subset of the base set of a lattice
\mathbf{L}
belonging to
\mathcal{V}
is an ideal of
\mathbf{L}
if and only if it is closed under the ideal terms of

.

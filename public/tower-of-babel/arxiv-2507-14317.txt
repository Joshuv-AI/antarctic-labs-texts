The average genus number for pure fields of prime degree
Sambhabi Bose, Kevin J. McGown, Ishan Panpaliya, Natalie Welling, Laney Williams

Abstract
. Let ℓ ≥ 5 \ell\geq 5 be prime. Let ℱ ℓ \mathscr{F}_{\ell} be the collection of (isomorphism classes of) pure number fields ℚ ⁡ ( a ℓ ) \mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{a}) of degree ℓ \ell , ordered by the absolute value of their discriminant. In 2018, Benli proved a counting theorem for ℱ ℓ \mathscr{F}_{\ell} , generalizing a previous theorem of Cohen and Morra when ℓ = 3 \ell=3 . We prove that the proportion of pure fields of degree ℓ \ell with genus number one is asymptotic to ( A ℓ ​ log ⁡ X ) − 1 (A_{\ell}\log X)^{-1} and that the average genus number for pure fields of degree ℓ \ell is asymptotic to B ℓ ​ ( log ⁡ X ) ℓ − 1 B_{\ell}(\log X)^{\ell-1} . Both A ℓ A_{\ell} and B ℓ B_{\ell} are expressed explicitly as a product over primes.

1.
Introduction

The subject of counting number fields, and more generally arithmetic statistics, has been one of intense interest over the last few decades.
Given a collection of number fields
\mathscr{F}
and a
function
f\colon\mathscr{F}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, one can ask for an asymptotic (or the limiting value) for the
expression

\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathscr{F}\\
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\end{subarray}}f(K)}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathscr{F}\\
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\end{subarray}}1}\,.

Here our fields
K
are ordered by the absolute value of the discriminant
\Delta_{K}
,
but alternative height functions could be considered.
Examples of functions
f(K)
of very high interest are
the class number
h_{K}
, the indicator function for
h_{K}=1
,
or the size of the
p
-torsion of the class group
\#(\Cl_{K}[p])
.
A prototypical example is the famous Davenport–Heilbronn theorems
(see
[
7
,
3
,
21
,
4
]
) which
allow one to obtain the average of
\#(\Cl_{K}[3])
over the collection of quadratic fields of fixed signature.
However, many such questions are completely out of reach.
Recently, McGown–Tucker, Kim, and Frei–Loughran–Newton studied the statistics of the
genus number
g_{K}
(see
[
19
,
18
,
13
,
8
]
). This quantity is supported at the bad primes,
in the language of the Cohen–Lenstra heuristics (see
[
5
]
),
and is much more amenable to study than
h_{K}
.

In light of Malle’s conjecture (see
[
15
,
16
]
), it is most common to take
\mathscr{F}
to consist of all
degree
d
extensions of
\mathbb{Q}
having a specified Galois closure
G
. In this case,
Malle’s conjecture predicts that the count of such extensions with
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X

is asymptotic to
CX^{\alpha}(\log X)^{\beta}

for appropriate choices of
0<\alpha\leq 1
,
\beta\geq 0
, and
C>0
.
(One can also consider the more general problem where
\mathbb{Q}
is replaced by any base field
k
.)

On the other hand, there are other interesting collections of fields that one might consider.
In this paper we will be concerned with the collection of pure fields of prime degree
\ell
.
Let
\mathscr{F}_{\ell}

denote the collection of all (isomorphism classes of) degree
\ell
number fields
of the form
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{a})
with
a\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Note that we must have that
a\in\mathbb{Z}
is not an
\ell
-th power; moreover,
we may assume that
a
is positive and
\ell
-free, which we will tacitly do throughout.
Define the counting function

N_{\ell}(X)=\#\{K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}:|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\}\,.

Benli proves (see
[
1
]
) that for all
\ell\geq 5
, the asymptotic

(1.1)

N_{\ell}(X)\sim C_{\ell}X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}(\log X)^{\ell-2}\,

holds for an explicit constant
C_{\ell}
. Actually, she proves something stronger,
but at present we will only be concerned with asymptotics.
We note that Cohen and Morra had previously treated the case of
\ell=3
(see
[
6
]
).
The aim of this paper is to determine
the proportion of fields with genus number one and
the average genus number for the family
\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
.
More precisely, we seek an asymptotic as
X
tends to infinity for these quantities.

Theorem 1.1
.

The proportion of pure fields of prime degree
\ell\geq 3
with genus number one equals

\frac{\#\{K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}:g_{K}=1\,,\;|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\}}{\#\{K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}:|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\}}\sim\frac{1}{A_{\ell}\log X},

where

A_{\ell}=\frac{1}{(\ell-1)(\ell-2)}\prod_{p}A_{\ell,p}\,,\quad A_{\ell,p}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)\begin{cases}1+\frac{\ell-1}{p},&p\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)\,,\\
1,&p\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)\,.\end{cases}

We remark that the
A_{\ell,p}
can also be written as a single formula in terms of the

\ell
-th power residue symbol, and the same is true for
B_{\ell,p}
in the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2
.

The average genus number over pure fields of prime degree
\ell\geq 3
satisfies

\frac{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}\\
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\end{subarray}}g_{K}}{\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}\\
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X\end{subarray}}1}\sim B_{\ell}(\log X)^{\ell-1}\,,\quad B_{\ell}=\frac{(\ell-2)!}{(2\ell-3)!(\ell-1)^{\ell-1}}\prod_{p}B_{\ell,p}\,,

where

B_{\ell,p}=\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\ell-1}\begin{cases}\left(1+\frac{\ell(\ell-1)}{p}\right)\left(1+\frac{\ell-1}{p}\right)^{-1},&p\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)\,,\\
1,&p\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)\,.\end{cases}

In Section
2
we will give the definition of the
genus number and summarize previous results concerning statistics of genus numbers.
In Section
3

we state some preliminary lemmas that will be required in the proofs of our results.
In Section
4
we give a brief proof of the asymptotic for
N_{\ell}(X)

given in (
1.1
). Although our argument differs from the one given in
[
1
]
,
the main reason for presenting the proof in its entirety
is that this will serve as an outline of the proof strategy we will employ in later sections.
Finally, in Sections
5
and
6
we prove Theorems
1.1

and
1.2
, respectively.

2.
Background on the genus number

We adopt the notation from the previous section, including writing
\mathscr{F}_{\ell}

for the collection of pure fields of prime degree
\ell
. Throughout the paper,
\ell
will always denote an odd prime.

We write
h_{K}
and
h_{K}^{+}
to denote the class number and narrow class number, respectively.
Fröhlich (see
[
9
]
) defined the genus field of a number field
K

to be the maximal extension
K^{*}
of
K
that is unramified at all finite primes and is a compositum of the form
Kk^{*}
where
k^{*}
is absolutely abelian.
The genus number of
K
is defined as
g_{K}=[K^{*}:K]
,
and by class field theory
g_{K}\mid h^{+}_{K}
. Moreover,
since
h_{K}
and
h_{K}^{+}
differ by a power of
2
, it follows from
Lemma
3.3
that for
K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
,
one has
g_{K}\mid h_{K}
. In fact, this holds whenever
K
has
odd prime degree.

Here we will briefly indicate previous works that have considered the study of the genus number
of a number field from a statistical perspective. This section can be skipped by those who want to proceed directly to the proofs.
In
[
19
]
, the family of all cubic fields was considered.
As
S_{3}
-cubic fields make up 100% of all cubic fields, from the perspective of that paper
it was only necessary to treat the case of
S_{3}
-cubic fields. However, interesting subfamilies
of cubic fields include the cyclic cubic fields and the pure cubic fields.
We note that the collection of pure cubic fields
\mathscr{F}_{3}
is the subfamily
of
S_{3}
-cubic fields whose Galois closure contains
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-3})
.

In
[
13
]
, the cases of cyclic and dihedral fields of any prime degree were considered.
The dihedral case is more difficult and therefore asymptotics are conditional on conjectures for
p
-torsion in class groups.
In
[
18
]
, the family of all quintic fields was considered.
As in the cubic case, the
S_{5}
-quintic fields constitute 100% of all quintic fields,
and hence that work only considered
S_{5}
-quintic extensions. As
\mathscr{F}_{5}
(the collection of pure quintic fields) is a subcollection of those
quintic fields whose Galois closure is the Frobenius group of order
20
,
these fields were not treated in that work.
Indeed, one can show that the Galois closure of
K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}

is isomorphic to a semidirect product
C_{\ell}\rtimes C_{\ell-1}
.
Finally, in
[
8
]
, the case of all abelian extensions was considered.
In that work, results over an arbitrary base field
k
were obtained.

3.
Preliminaries

Given
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we will write
\rad(n)
to denote the radical of
n
, and
\omega(n)
for the number of distinct prime divisors of
n
. We will refer to
n
as
\ell
-free if it is not divisible by an
\ell
-th power; that is,
n
is
\ell
-free if
\nu_{p}(n)<\ell
for all primes
p
,
where
\nu_{p}
denotes the
p
-adic valuation.

The following gives a formula for the discriminant of the fields
in
\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
(see
[
2
,
17
]
).

Lemma 3.1
.

For
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{a})\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
with
\rad(a)=n
, we have

\displaystyle|\Delta_{K}|=\begin{cases}\ell^{\ell-2}n^{\ell-1},&a^{\ell-1}\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2}),\\
\ell^{\ell}n^{\ell-1},&a^{\ell-1}\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2}).\end{cases}

The following result allows one to parametrize
\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
.
We leave this as an exercise, but the reader may wish to consult a reference on Kummer theory such as
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
are
\ell
-free. Then

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{a})
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{b})
are isomorphic if and only if

a=b^{i}
in
\mathbb{Q}^{\times}/\left(\mathbb{Q}^{\times}\right)^{\ell}
.
Moreover,
the function

\{a\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 2}\mid a\text{ is $\ell$-free}\}\to\mathscr{F}_{\ell}

given by

a\mapsto\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{a})

is a surjective
(\ell-1)
-to-
1
map.

The following formula for the genus number appears in
[
11
,
12
]
. The reader may also
want to consult
[
10
]
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt[\ell]{m})\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}
. Then

g_{K}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\mid m\\
p\neq\ell\end{subarray}}(\ell,p-1)=\ell^{\widehat{\omega}(m)}\,.

We denote by
\widehat{\omega}(n)
the number of
p\mid n

such that
p\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)
.

The following result is standard (see, for example, Chapter 4 of
[
20
]
).

Lemma 3.4

(Mertens’ Second Theorem in Residue Classes)
.

Suppose
S\subseteq(\mathbb{Z}/m\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
. As
X\to\infty
, we have that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq X\\
p\in S\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,m)\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}=\frac{|S|}{\phi(m)}\log\log X+M+O\left(\frac{1}{\log X}\right).

Note that both the constant
M
and the implicit constant in the error term depend on the set
S

(and hence the modulus
m
).

The following result is well-known (see
[
22
]
).

Lemma 3.5

(Wirsing’s Theorem)
.

Let
f(n)\geq 0
be a multiplicative function. Assume that
there is a constant
\tau>0
such that

(3.1)

\sum_{p\leq X}f(p)\sim\tau\cdot\frac{X}{\log X}

and constants
\gamma_{1}>0
and
0\leq\gamma_{2}<2

such that
f(p^{k})\leq\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}^{k}
for all
k\geq 2
.
Then we have

\sum_{n\leq X}f(n)\sim\frac{X}{\log(X)}\cdot\frac{e^{-\gamma\tau}}{\Gamma(\tau)}\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{f(p)}{p}+\frac{f(p^{2})}{p^{2}}+\cdots\right)\,.

Remark 3.6
.

Our applications of Wirsing’s Theorem will always be to a sum of the form
\sum_{n\leq X}\mu^{2}(n)f(n)
.
Note that this simplifies both the hypothesis and the conclusion of Lemma
3.5
.
In addition, it will be helpful to note that for
\tau>0
,
one has

\prod_{p\leq X}\left(1+\frac{\tau}{p}\right)\sim e^{\tau\gamma}(\log X)^{\tau}\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\tau}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\tau}\,.

In the sequel, we will require the use of this type of manipulation, which follows from Mertens’ Third Theorem.

4.
Counting pure fields of prime degree
\ell

Suppose
\ell\geq 3
is prime.
We begin with the equation

(4.1)

\displaystyle(\ell-1)N_{\ell}(X)+1

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\leq a(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}\hskip-8.61108pt\mu^{2}(n)\hskip-17.22217pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\\
a^{\ell-1}\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}\hskip-8.61108pt1+\sum_{n\leq b(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}\hskip-8.61108pt\mu^{2}(n)\hskip-17.22217pt\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\\
a^{\ell-1}\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}1,

where
a(\ell)=\ell^{-1+\frac{1}{\ell-1}}
and

b(\ell)=\ell^{-1-\frac{1}{\ell-1}}
,
which follows from
Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
.

Using the orthogonality of Dirichlet characters,
the first summand on the right-hand side of (
4.1
) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\phi(\ell^{2})}\sum_{\chi\text{ mod $\ell^{2}$}}\sum_{n\leq a(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}\mu^{2}(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\end{subarray}}\chi^{\ell-1}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ mod $\ell^{2}$}\\
\chi^{\ell}=\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\sum_{n\leq a(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}\mu^{2}(n)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\end{subarray}}\chi(a)\,.

This motivates us to define the following multiplicative function
and the associated summatory function (over squarefree integers):

f_{\chi}(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\end{subarray}}\chi(a)\,,\qquad F_{\chi}(Y)=\sum_{n\leq Y}\mu^{2}(n)f_{\chi}(n)\,.

Without the character, we similarly have

f(n)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\rad(a)=n\\
\text{$a$ is $\ell$-free}\end{subarray}}1=(\ell-1)^{\omega(n)}\,,\qquad F(Y)=\sum_{n\leq Y}\mu^{2}(n)f(n)\,.

This all leads to the following expression:

(4.2)

(\ell-1)N_{\ell}(X)+1=\frac{1}{\ell}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\chi\text{ mod $\ell^{2}$}\\
\chi^{\ell}=\chi_{0}\end{subarray}}\left[F_{\chi}(a(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}})-F_{\chi}\left(b(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\right)\right]+F\left(b(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\right)\,.

To give an asymptotic for
N_{\ell}(X)
,
it suffices to give an asymptotic for
F_{\chi}(Y)
when
\chi=\chi_{0}
,
including the case of
F(Y)
,
and to bound
F_{\chi}(Y)
when
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
.

Case
\chi=\chi_{0}
:
We clearly have

\sum_{p\leq Y}(\ell-1)\sim(\ell-1)Y/\log Y

by the Prime Number Theorem and therefore
Lemma
3.5
implies

(4.3)

F(Y)\sim D_{\ell}Y(\log Y)^{\ell-2}\,,\quad D_{\ell}=\frac{1}{(\ell-2)!}\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\ell-1}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\ell-1}\,.

Similarly, we have

(4.4)

F_{\chi_{0}}(Y)\sim c(\ell)D_{\ell}Y(\log Y)^{\ell-2}\,,\quad c(\ell)=\left(1+\frac{\ell-1}{\ell}\right)^{-1}=\frac{\ell}{2\ell-1}\,.

Case
\chi\neq\chi_{0}
:
We use the bound

|F_{\chi}(Y)|\leq\sum_{n\leq Y}\mu^{2}(n)|f_{\chi}(n)|

and apply Lemma
3.5
to the latter sum to obtain

\displaystyle F_{\chi}(Y)\ll\frac{Y}{\log Y}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\\
p^{\ell-1}\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{1}{p}\right)\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\\
p^{\ell-1}\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}\left(1+\frac{\ell-1}{p}\right)\,.

Taking logarithms of the two products above, using the Taylor expansion at
x=0
for
\log(1+x)
, and applying Lemma
3.4
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\\
p^{\ell-1}\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{p}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}p\leq Y\\
p^{\ell-1}\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell^{2})\end{subarray}}\frac{\ell-1}{p}+\sum_{p\leq Y}O\left(\frac{1}{p^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad=\left(\frac{(\ell-1)^{2}}{\phi(\ell^{2})}\cdot 1\right)\log\log Y+\left(\frac{(\ell-1)}{\phi(\ell^{2})}\cdot(\ell-1)\right)\log\log Y+O(1)

\displaystyle\qquad=2\left(1-\frac{1}{\ell}\right)\log\log Y+O(1)\,.

Therefore

(4.5)

F_{\chi}(Y)\ll Y(\log Y)^{1-\frac{2}{\ell}}\,.

Putting (
4.3
), (
4.4
), (
4.5
) into (
4.2
),
and using

(4.6)

\log\left(a(\ell)X^{\frac{1}{\ell-1}}\right)^{\ell-2}\sim\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{\ell-2}}(\log X)^{\ell-2}\,,

we establish (
1.1
) with

(4.7)

\displaystyle C_{\ell}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(\ell-1)^{\ell-1}}\left(\frac{a(\ell)-b(\ell)}{\ell}c(\ell)+b(\ell)\right)D_{\ell}

(4.8)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ell^{-\frac{1}{\ell-1}}}{\ell!(\ell-1)^{\ell-2}}\left(1+\frac{\ell^{\frac{2}{\ell-1}}-1}{2\ell-1}\right)\prod_{p}\left(1+\frac{\ell-1}{p}\right)\left(1-\frac{1}{p}\right)^{\ell-1}\,.

We note that our formula for the constant (
4.8
) differs slightly from the one
in
[
1
]
, but we believe it to be correct and it matches the formula in
[
6
]

when specialized to
\ell=3
.

Proof of Theorem

.

\rad(a)=n

3.3

g_{K}=\ell^{\widehat{\omega}(n)}

p\not\equiv 1\allowbreak\mkern 5.0mu({\operator@font mod}\,\,\ell)

.
We impose this restriction on the sums over
n

) and

and

Similarly, we have

F_{\chi}(Y)\sim c(\ell)\widehat{D}_{\ell}Y(\log Y)^{\ell-3}\,.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

The proof is similar to the one in the previous section. In order to sum
g_{K}
over
K\in\mathscr{F}_{\ell}

with
|\Delta_{K}|\leq X
, we insert the multiplicative function
g(n)=\ell^{\widehat{\omega}(n)}

immediately to the right of both sums over
n
in (
4.1
). As before, the right-hand side of

Similarly,

Yusheng Luo, Dimitrios Ntalampekos

Abstract
. The object of the paper is to characterize gasket Julia sets of rational maps that can be uniformized by round gaskets. We restrict to rational maps without critical points on the Julia set. Under these conditions, we prove that a Julia set can be quasiconformally uniformized by a round gasket if and only if it is a fat gasket, i.e., boundaries of Fatou components intersect tangentially. We also prove that a Julia set can be uniformized by a round gasket with a David homeomorphism if and only if every Fatou component is a quasidisk; equivalently, there are no parabolic cycles of multiplicity 2 2 . Our theorem applies to show that gasket Julia sets and limit sets of Kleinian groups can be locally quasiconformally homeomorphic, although globally this is conjectured to be false.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Uniformization of gasket Julia sets

We present a uniformization result for gaskets arising in complex dynamics as Julia sets of rational maps. Namely, our main theorems describe necessary and sufficient conditions so that a gasket Julia set can be uniformized by a round gasket with a quasiconformal or David homeomorphism of the sphere.

Such results existed so far only for Sierpiński carpet Julia sets. A
Sierpiński carpet
is a locally connected continuum in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that has empty interior and whose complement is the union of countably many Jordan regions with disjoint closures. Bonk
[
10
]
established a general criterion for the quasiconformal uniformization of carpets by round carpets, and then Bonk–Lyubich–Merenkov
[
7
]
proved that carpet Julia sets of subhyperbolic rational maps satisfy that criterion. In contrast, such a criterion does not exist for general gaskets; our uniformization results below are closely tied with the geometry of Julia sets. Let us first introduce the required definitions.

Definition 1.1
.

A set
K\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a
gasket
if it is a locally connected continuum with empty interior that has the following properties.

(1)

Each complementary component of
K
is a Jordan region.

(2)

The boundaries of any two complementary components of
K
share at most one point, called a
contact point
.

(3)

No point of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
belongs to the boundaries of three complementary components of
K
.

(4)

The
contact graph
corresponding to
K
, obtained by assigning a vertex to each complementary component and an edge if two components share a boundary point, is connected.

A gasket
K
is said to be
round
if each complementary component is a geometric disk of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. In other words, a round gasket corresponds to a circle packing of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. A relevant notion is that of a
Schottky set

[
5
]
, i.e., the complement of the union of at least three disjoint open balls in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Note that a set that is homeomorphic to a Schottky set necessarily satisfies conditions (1), (2), and (3) in the above definition. Moreover, condition (4) differentiates carpets from gaskets. Gaskets and round gaskets appear naturally in dynamics and low-dimensional topology.

We say that two disjoint Jordan regions
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
are
tangent to each other at a point

z_{0}\in\partial U\cap\partial V\cap\mathbb{C}
if there exists
\theta_{0}\in[0,2\pi)
such that for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
with the property that
\{z_{0}+re^{i(\theta_{0}+\theta)}:r\in(0,\delta),\,\,|\theta|<\pi/2-\varepsilon\}\subset U
and
\{z_{0}+re^{i(\theta_{0}+\theta)}:r\in(-\delta,0),\,\,|\theta|<\pi/2-\varepsilon\}\subset V
. Tangency at the point
\infty
is also defined in the obvious manner, using a local chart. We say that a gasket
K\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is
fat
if any two complementary components of
K
that share a boundary point are tangent to each other at that point.

It is elementary to show that if a gasket Julia set is fat and contains no critical points then each Fatou component is a quasidisk; see Lemma
7.4
. Our first main result gives the much stronger conclusion that fatness is necessary and sufficient for a gasket Julia set to admit quasiconformal uniformization by a round gasket.

Theorem 1.2

(QC uniformization)
.

Let
R
be a rational map without Julia critical points whose Julia set
\mathcal{J}(R)
is a gasket. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

(Quasiconformal uniformization) There exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
\mathcal{J}(R)
onto a round gasket.

(2)

(Geometric criterion)
\mathcal{J}(R)
is a fat gasket.

(3)

(Dynamical criterion) Every contact point is eventually mapped to a parabolic periodic point with multiplicity
3
.

Moreover, if
\psi
is any orientation-preserving homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
\mathcal{J}(R)
onto a round gasket, then
\psi|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
agrees with the map
\phi|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
in
(1)
up to post-composition with a Möbius transformation of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

We remark that there is an abundance of examples of rational maps whose Julia set is a fat gasket (see
[
27
]
for a combinatorial classification). In particular, there are infinitely many non-homeomorphic quadratic fat gasket Julia sets.

A more general class of maps that appear naturally in conformal dynamics is the class of David maps (see Section
2.3
). These maps connect hyperbolic and parabolic dynamics, a relationship first noted in Haïssinsky’s work (see
[
15
,
16
]
). Recently, there has been a growing number of applications of David maps in gluing and mating problems in dynamics (see
[
41
,
46
,
29
,
28
,
24
]
). Our second result gives a characterization for the existence of a David uniformizing map.

Theorem 1.3

(David uniformization)
.

Let
R
be a rational map without Julia critical points whose Julia set
\mathcal{J}(R)
is a gasket. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

(David uniformization) There exists a David homeomorphism
\phi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
\mathcal{J}(R)
onto a round gasket.

(2)

(Geometric criterion) Each Fatou component is a quasidisk.

(3)

(Dynamical criterion)
R
has no parabolic cycles of multiplicity
2
.

Moreover, if
\psi
is any orientation-preserving homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
\mathcal{J}(R)
onto a round gasket, then
\psi|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
agrees with the map
\phi|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
in
(1)
up to post-composition with a Möbius transformation of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

We remark that under the assumptions of Theorem
1.3
, any contact point is eventually mapped to either a repelling periodic point or a parabolic periodic point with multiplicity
2
or
3
; see Lemma
7.2
. Therefore, condition (3) implies that every contact point is eventually mapped to either a repelling periodic point or a parabolic periodic point with multiplicity
3
; compare to the dynamical criterion in Theorem
1.2
.

We remark that Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
have an analogue for Kleinian groups, which was known by a classical result of McMullen. More precisely, let
\Gamma
be a geometrically finite Kleinian group with gasket limit set
\Lambda(\Gamma)
. Then McMullen showed in
[
32
]
that there exists a unique Kleinian group with a round gasket limit set in its quasiconformal conjugacy class. In particular, there exists a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\phi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
that maps
\Lambda(\Gamma)
onto a round gasket.

1.2.
Quasiconformal (in-)equivalence of limit sets and Julia sets

It is a central question in quasiconformal geometry to classify fractal sets up to quasiconformal homeomorphisms. This problem has attracted a good deal of attention in recent years; see, for example,
[
6
,
14
,
8
,
34
,
7
,
30
,
23
]
. For fractal sets that emerge in conformal dynamics, ample evidence suggests that it is possible to quasiconformally distinguish Julia sets and limit sets of Kleinian groups.
The following conjecture is stated in
[
23
]
.

Conjecture 1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{J}
be the Julia set of a rational map and
\Lambda
be the limit set of a Kleinian group.
Suppose that
\mathcal{J}
and
\Lambda
are connected and not homeomorphic to the circle or the
2
-sphere. Then there exists no quasiconformal homeomorphism of the Riemann sphere that maps
\mathcal{J}
onto
\Lambda
.

This conjecture is proved in various settings when the Julia set or the limit set is a Sierpiński carpet (see
[
34
,
7
,
44
]
). When different Fatou components are allowed to touch, the situation is more subtle. While the conjecture is proved in various special cases of gasket Julia sets and limit sets (see
[
27
]
), the following theorem shows that the conjecture is false in the local sense. See Figure
1.1
for an illustration.

Theorem 1.5

(Local QC equivalence)
.

There exist a gasket Julia set
\mathcal{J}
and a gasket limit set
\Lambda
so that for any point
z\in\mathcal{J}
, there exist a neighborhood
U
of
z
and a quasiconformal map
\phi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
so that
\phi(U\cap\mathcal{J})=\phi(U)\cap\Lambda
.

We remark that in our example we can construct an atlas of the Julia set consisting of 4 charts (see Figure
1.1
and Theorem
10.1
for a more precise statement).
From a different perspective, the Julia set
\mathcal{J}
and the limit set
\Lambda
in our example can be decomposed (in two different ways) into two connected pieces
\mathcal{J}_{1},\mathcal{J}_{2}
and
\Lambda_{1},\Lambda_{2}
glued along four points, so that the pairs
\mathcal{J}_{i},\Lambda_{i}
,
i=1,2
, are quasiconformally homeomorphic; in Figure
1.1
one can take
\mathcal{J}_{1}=\mathcal{J}\cap U^{+}
,
\mathcal{J}_{2}=\mathcal{J}\cap U^{-}
,
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda\cap V^{+}
, and
\Lambda_{1}=\Lambda\cap V^{-}
. Our construction is very flexible and should allow us to construct infinitely many pairs of gasket Julia sets and limit sets with the same property. The technique demonstrates that there are no local or analytic obstructions preventing a Julia set from being quasiconformally equivalent to a limit set. The only obstruction is combinatorial in nature and arises from gluing different quasiconformally equivalent charts. On the other hand, there are many homeomorphic gasket Julia sets and gasket limit sets (see
[
22
]
).

Sierpiński carpet limit sets exhibit very strong local rigidity; see the works
[
34
,

. Specifically, any quasiregular symmetry of a Sierpiński carpet limit set
\Lambda
defined on an open and connected subset of

[

*Theorem 1.1. We expect this local rigidity would preclude local quasiconformal maps between Sierpiński carpet Julia sets and limit sets, as opposed to the gasket case in Theorem
1.5
. As a corollary to Theorem
1.5
, and in sharp contrast with the case of Sierpiński carpet limit sets, we show that gasket limit sets do admit local quasiregular and non-injective symmetries.

Corollary 1.6

(QR symmetries for gasket limit sets)
.

There exist a gasket limit set
\Lambda
, open and connected sets
W,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with
W\cap\Lambda\neq\emptyset
and a quasiregular branched covering
f\colon W\to V
of degree
2
so that
f(W\cap\Lambda)=V\cap\Lambda
.

We remark that in our construction (see Section
10
and Figure
10.4
), one can show that the subset
A=W\cap\Lambda
can be decomposed into
4
connected pieces
A_{1},\dots A_{4}
, where
A_{i}\cap A_{i+1}
is a single point and
f|_{A_{i}}
is a Möbius map.

1.3.
Proof outline

There are three main ingredients in the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. First, we use the theory of circle packings with subdivision rules that was recently developed by the first-named author and Y. Zhang
[
26
]
and especially the asymptotic conformality of homeomorphisms between such circle packings. Second, we use an extension result for circle homeomorphisms conjugating two piecewise quasiconformal dynamical systems of the circle; this extension result was developed jointly in earlier work of the authors
[
25
]
, generalizing a similar result for piecewise analytic dynamical systems of the circle
[
29
]
. This extension result is used in combination with quasiconformal or David surgery to construct the uniformizing maps in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
. Finally, the quasiconformal or David regularity of these maps is proved with the aid of a recent metric characterization of quasiconformality due to the second-named author
[
40
]
.

We now provide an outline of the basic steps of the proof. The equivalence
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
between the geometric and dynamical criteria in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
is proved by analyzing the local dynamics near the fixed points; see Section
7
. To prove that
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
in Theorem
1.2
, we first show that if
(3)
does not hold, then either there is a Fatou component that is not a quasidisk or there are two Fatou components that touch at an angle. In the second case, these two Fatou components cannot be mapped to a pair of touching circles by a quasiconformal map. To prove that
(1)\Rightarrow(3)
in Theorem
1.3
, we first show that if
(3)
does not hold, then there exists a Fatou component that is the immediate basin of a parabolic periodic point with multiplicity
2
. This immediate basin cannot be mapped to a round disk by a David map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
as provided by a recent result of the authors
[
25
]
.

The most technical direction is to show that the geometric and dynamical criteria are also sufficient for uniformization. We remark that a priori, it is not clear that a gasket Julia set is homeomorphic to a circle packing. To this end, we first construct a finite subdivision rule for the the contact graph of the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(R)
. By results of
[
26
]
, this allows us to construct an infinite circle packing
\mathcal{P}
whose limit set
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{J}(R)
(see Section
5
). The finite subdivision rule induces a Markov map
\Psi\colon\overline{\Omega^{1}}\to\overline{\Omega^{0}}
, where
\Omega^{n}\coloneqq\Psi^{-n}(\Omega^{0})
,
n\geq 0
, is some nested sequence open sets that satisfies
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega^{n}}
. After passing to some iterate of
R
if necessary, we show that there exists a homeomorphism
h:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
conjugating the dynamics of
R\colon\mathcal{J}(R)\to\mathcal{J}(R)
to
\Psi\colon\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\to\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
. Thus, the map
\Psi|_{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})}
plays the role of
R|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
. Although the limit set
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
does not arise from a Kleinian group, so we do not have conformal symmetries, the map
\Psi
is as conformal as possible in the sense that its dilatation is exponentially small on deep levels, as shown in
[
26
]
. More precisely, this means that the quasiconformal distortion of
\Psi
on
\Omega^{n}
is
1+O(\delta^{-n})
for some
\delta\in(0,1)
.

To complete the proof, we show that
h|_{\mathcal{J}(R)}
extends to a quasiconformal or David map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. To achieve this, we first show that

•

if each Fatou component is a quasidisk, then
h
extends to a David homeomorphism on each periodic Fatou component, and

•

if
\mathcal{J}(R)
is a fat gasket, then
h
extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism on each periodic Fatou component.

The extension in each Fatou component is established by a recent extension result for piecewise quasiconformal maps on the circle (see
[
25
]
and Section
3
here). We first extend
h
to the periodic Fatou components and then using the dynamics, we pull back the David or quasiconformal homeomorphism to all Fatou components, and obtain a homeomorphism
h\colon(\widehat{\mathbb{C}},\mathcal{J}(R))\to(\widehat{\mathbb{C}},\Lambda(\mathcal{P}))
that has David or quasiconformal regularity on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{J}(R)
.

We remark that the Sierpiński gasket is not removable for quasiconformal maps, as shown in
[
38
]
, and it is expected that all gaskets are not removable as well. Hence, the David or quasiconformal regularity of the map
h
on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathcal{J}(R)
does not immediately imply that we have the same regularity on all of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. To obtain the desired regularity of
h
on
\mathcal{J}(R)
we apply a recent metric characterization of quasiconformality
[
40
]
, asserting that for a homeomorphism to be quasiconformal it is sufficient that it maps infinitesimal sets of bounded eccentricity to infinitesimal sets of bounded eccentricity; see Section
2.4
.

2.
Preliminaries on quasiconformal geometry

2.1.
Quasisymmetric maps

Let
(X,d_{X}),(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces. A homeomorphism
f\colon(X,d_{X})\to(Y,d_{Y})
is
quasisymmetric
if there exists a homeomorphism
\eta\colon[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
such that for all triples of distinct points
x_{1},x_{2},x_{3}\in X
we have

\displaystyle\frac{d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))}{d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{3}))}\leq\eta\left(\frac{d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})}{d_{X}(x_{1},x_{3})}\right).

In that case we say that
f
is
\eta
-quasisymmetric.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
19
]
*Theorem 3)
.

Let
f\colon[-1,1]\to\mathbb{R}
be a homeomorphic embedding such that
f|_{[-1,0]}
and
f|_{[1,0]}
are quasisymmetric and there exists
\lambda\geq 1
with the property that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}|t|\leq|f(t)-f(0)|\leq\lambda|t|

for all
t\in[-1,1]
. Then
f
is quasisymmetric.

For example, condition (
2.1
) is met if the one-sided derivatives of
f
at
0
exist and are non-zero.

2.2.
Quasiconformal and quasiregular maps

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be open sets and
f\colon U\to V
be an orientation-preserving homeomorphism. We say that
f
is
quasiconformal
if
f
lies in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
and there exists
K\geq 1
such that
\|Df(z)\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}(z)
for a.e.
z\in U
. Here
Df(z)
is the differential of
f
, which exists at a.e.
z\in U
,
\|Df(z)\|
denotes its operator norm, and
J_{f}(z)
is the Jacobian determinant of
Df(z)
. In that case, we say that
f
is
K
-quasiconformal. Equivalently, one may replace the condition that
\|Df\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}
with the condition that the
Beltrami coefficient
of
f
, defined by

\displaystyle\mu_{f}=\frac{\partial f/\partial\overline{z}}{\partial f/\partial z},

satisfies
\|\mu_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}<1
. One can define quasiconformal homeomorphisms between domains in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
via local coordinates. A homeomorphism of the Riemann sphere is quasiconformal if and only if it is quasisymmetric in the spherical metric; see
[
17
]
*Theorem 11.14.

A
quasidisk
is the image of the unit disk under a quasiconformal homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Several characterizations of quasidisks are available; see
[
42
]
*Section 5.1. For example, a Jordan region in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasidisk if and only if its boundary is a quasisymmetric image (using the spherical metric) of the unit circle.

Let
U,V\subset\mathbb{C}
be open sets. A map
f\colon U\to V
is called
quasiregular
if
f
lies in the Sobolev space
W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
and there exists
K\geq 1
such that
\|Df(z)\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}(z)
for a.e.
z\in U
. In that case we say that
f
is
K
-quasiregular. Equivalently, as above, one may replace the condition that
\|Df\|^{2}\leq KJ_{f}
with the condition that
\|\mu_{f}\|_{L^{\infty}(U)}<1
. Quasiregular maps are continuous
[
1
]
*Corollary 5.5.2, but they are not necessarily injective. Furthermore, it is known that
f
is quasiregular if and only if we can write
f=g\circ h
, where
g
is analytic and
h
is quasiconformal; see
[
21
]
*p. 247 or
[
1
]
*Theorem 5.5.1.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be an orientation-preserving covering map of degree
d\geq 2
that satisfies one of the following conditions.

(1)

f
has a continuous and non-zero derivative on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

(2)

f
is locally quasisymmetric on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Then
f
extends to a quasiregular map
F\colon\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{D}
that has a unique critical point at
0
and
F(0)=0
.

Proof.

For
(1)
, we replace
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with
\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
and we treat
f
as a function of
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
so that
f^{\prime}(\theta)>0
for each
\theta\in\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. We define the extension of
f
in
\mathbb{D}
by
F(re^{i\theta})=re^{if(\theta)}
,
0\leq r<1
,
\theta\in[0,2\pi)
. We have

\displaystyle\mu_{F}(re^{i\theta})=\frac{\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial\overline{z}}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial\overline{z}}}{\frac{\partial F}{\partial r}\frac{\partial r}{\partial z}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial\theta}\frac{\partial\theta}{\partial z}}=\frac{e^{if(\theta)}\frac{e^{i\theta}}{2}+re^{if(\theta)}\cdot if^{\prime}(\theta)\cdot\frac{-e^{i\theta}}{2ir}}{e^{if(\theta)}\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{2}+re^{if(\theta)}\cdot if^{\prime}(\theta)\cdot\frac{e^{-i\theta}}{2ir}}=e^{2i\theta}\frac{1-f^{\prime}(\theta)}{1+f^{\prime}(\theta)}.

Since
f^{\prime}
is continuous and positive, we have
\|\mu_{F}\|_{L^{\infty}}(\mathbb{D})<1
. Therefore,
F
is quasiregular. It follows from the construction that
F(0)=0
and
F
is locally injective at each point of
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, but not at
0
.

Now we prove the conclusion under condition
(2)
. The Beurling–Ahlfors extension theorem
[
3
]
gives a quasiconformal extension to
\mathbb{D}
of an orientation-preserving quasisymmetric map of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. The extension is given by a
local
extension operator of continuous and increasing maps of
\mathbb{R}
to self-maps of the upper half-plane. The locality of the extension operator implies that a locally quasisymmetric map
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
extends to a map
\widetilde{f}\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
with the following property: each point of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
has a neighborhood in
\mathbb{D}
in which
\widetilde{f}
is injective and quasiconformal. Thus,
\widetilde{f}
is quasiregular in a neighborhood
U
of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
\mathbb{D}
. We restrict
\widetilde{f}
to
U\cup\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

We also define
\widetilde{f}(z)=1/\overline{\widetilde{f}(1/\overline{z})}
for points
z\in\mathbb{C}
with
1/\overline{z}\in U
. The removability of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
for quasiconformal maps implies that each point of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
has a neighborhood in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
in which
\widetilde{f}
is quasiconformal. We define
\widetilde{\mu}(z)=\mu_{\widetilde{f}}(z)
whenever
z\in U
or
1/\overline{z}\in U
and
\widetilde{\mu}(z)=0
otherwise. By construction,
\widetilde{\mu}
is invariant under the reflection
z\mapsto 1/\overline{z}
. By the measurable Riemann mapping theorem (
[
1
]
*Theorem 5.3.4) there exists a quasiconformal map
h
of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with
\mu_{h}=\widetilde{\mu}
. By symmetry, after post-composition with a Möbius map, we may assume that
h(\mathbb{S}^{1})=\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
h(0)=0
; see e.g.
[
37
]
*Proposition 8.1 for a more general statement. In particular,
h|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
is orientation-preserving. Let
g=\widetilde{f}\circ h^{-1}
, and note that
g
is locally injective on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
and
g|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
is orientation-preserving. By
[
1
]
*Theorem 5.5.1,
g
is analytic in a neighborhood of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Since
g
is locally injective on every point of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
,
g
has no critical point on
\mathbb{S}^{1}
. By the first part of the lemma,
g|_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}
has a quasiregular extension
G
on
\mathbb{D}
that has a unique critical point at
0
and
G(0)=0
. We finally set
F=G\circ h
to obtain the desired extension.
∎

2.3.
David maps

An orientation-preserving homeomorphism
H\colon U\to V
between domains in the Riemann sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is called a
David homeomorphism
or else
mapping of exponentially integrable distortion
, if it lies in the Sobolev space
W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(U)
(defined via local coordinates) and there exists
p>0
such that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\int_{U}\exp(pK_{H})\,d\sigma<\infty;

here
\sigma
is the spherical measure and
K_{H}
is the distortion function of
H
, given by

\displaystyle K_{H}(z)=\frac{1+|\mu_{H}|}{1-|\mu_{H}|}.

Condition (
2.2
) is equivalent to the existence of constants
C,\alpha>0
such that

\sigma(\{z\in U:|\mu_{H}(z)|>1-\varepsilon\})\leq Ce^{-\alpha/\varepsilon}

for all
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
. We direct the reader to
[
1
]
*Chapter 20 for more background.

If
U
is an open subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and
\mu\colon U\to\mathbb{D}
is a measurable function such that
K=(1+|\mu|)/(1-|\mu|)
is exponentially integrable in
U
(i.e., it satisfies (
2.2
)), then we say that
\mu
is a
David–Beltrami coefficient (in
U
)
.

Proposition 2.3

(
[
29
]
*Proposition 2.5)
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a David homeomorphism between open sets
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

(i)

If
g\colon V\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is a quasiconformal embedding then
g\circ f
is a David map.

(ii)

If
W\subset{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}}
is an open set and
g\colon W\to U
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism that extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism of an open neighborhood of
\overline{W}
onto an open neighborhood of
\overline{U}
, then
f\circ g
is a David map.

(iii)

If
W\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
is an open set, and
g\colon W\to U
is a non-constant quasiregular map that extends to a quasiregular map in an open neighborhood of
\overline{W}
, then the function

\mu_{f\circ g}=\frac{\partial(f\circ g)/\partial\overline{z}}{\partial(f\circ g)/\partial z}

is a David–Beltrami coefficient in
W
.

(iv)

If
U,W
are quasidisks and
g\colon W\to U
is a quasiconformal homeomorphism, then
f\circ g
is a David map.

Remark 2.4
.

Following the argument in the proof of Proposition 2.5 in
[
29
]
one can show that if
f
is a David map on
U
and
g
is
K
-quasiconformal for some
K\geq 1
, then for a.e.
z\in U
we have

1-|\mu_{g\circ f}(z)|=\frac{2}{K_{g\circ f}(z)}\leq\frac{2}{K^{-1}K_{f}(z)+1}\leq\frac{2}{K^{-1}(K_{f}(z)+1)}=K(1-|\mu_{f}(z)|).

Moreover, if
g
is conformal, then
|\mu_{f\circ g}|=|\mu_{f}\circ g|
almost everywhere.

2.4.
Absolute continuity

In the proof of the main theorems we will use a recent generalization of the metric definition of quasiconformality due to the second-named author. Let
A\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a set. The
eccentricity

E(A)
of
A
is the infimum of all numbers
H\geq 1
for which there exists a spherical open ball
B
such that
B\subset A\subset HB
; here
HB
denotes the ball with the same center as that of
B
and radius multiplied by
H
. Let
f\colon U\to V
be a homeomorphism between open sets
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. The
eccentric distortion of
f
at a point
z\in U
, denoted by
E_{f}(z)
, is the infimum of all values
H\geq 1
such that there exists a sequence of open sets
A_{n}\subset U
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, containing
z
with
\diam A_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
and with the property that
E(A_{n})\leq H
and
E(f(A_{n}))\leq H
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. The main result in
[
40
]
shows that
f
is quasiconformal if and only if
E_{f}
is uniformly bounded. In fact, we will not use directly this result, but we will use the following statement about the absolute continuity of
f
.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
40
]
*Theorem 3.1)
.

Let
f\colon U\to V
be a homeomorphism between open sets
U,V\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
E\subset U
be a set of area zero and suppose that there exists
H\geq 1
such that

E_{f}(z)\leq H

for all
z\in E
. Then for a.e. line
L
parallel to a coordinate direction we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}(f(L\cap E))=0.

Here and below,
\mathcal{H}^{1}
denotes the Hausdorff
1
-measure defined using the Euclidean metric. We will also use the removability of quasicircles for continuous
W^{1,1}
functions in the following sense.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
U\subset\mathbb{C}
be a quasidisk and
f\colon\overline{U}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous function such that
f\in W^{1,1}(U)
. Then for a.e. line
L
parallel to a coordinate direction we have

\mathcal{H}^{1}(f(\partial U\cap E))=0.

The proof given in
[
39
]
*Proposition 5.3 poses a weaker assumption on
U
, namely
U
is assumed to be a Hölder domain, and a stronger assumption on
f
, which is assumed to lie in
W^{1,2}(U)
. However, in the case of quasidisks (and also John domains), it suffices to assume that
f\in W^{1,1}(U)
and the proof in
[
39
]
*Proposition 5.3 can be followed with appropriate simplifications.

Combining the above two results, we obtain the next statement.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
J\subset\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a gasket of area zero such that the components
U_{i}
,
i\in\mathbb{N}
, of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus J
are quasidisks. Let
f\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a homeomorphism and suppose that there exists
H\geq 1
such that

E_{f}(z)\leq H

for all
z\in J\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\partial U_{i}
.

(1)

If
f
is quasiconformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus J
, then
f
is quasiconformal on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

If
f
is a David map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus J
, then
f
is a David map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

By composing with Möbius transformations, we may assume that
\infty\in J\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\partial U_{i}
,
f(\infty)=\infty
, and
E_{f}(z)\leq H
for all
z\in J\setminus\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}\partial U_{i}
. Note that the property of
f
being a quasiconformal or David map is not affected by such compositions in view of Proposition
2.3
.
Since
J
has area zero and Sobolev functions are characterized as functions that are absolutely continuous on almost every line parallel to a coordinate direction (see
[
47
]
*Theorem 2.1.4), if suffices to show that
f
is absolutely continuous on almost every such line.

By Theorem
2.5
and Proposition
2.6
, we have
\mathcal{H}^{1}(f(J\cap L))=0
for a.e. line
L
parallel to a coordinate direction. Moreover, the assumption that
f
is a quasiconformal or a David map implies that for each bounded open set
V\subset\mathbb{C}
we have
f\in W^{1,1}(V\setminus J)
; see the argument in the proof of Lemma 2.9 in
[
29
]
. In particular, for a.e. line
L
parallel to a coordinate direction we have
\int_{L\cap V\setminus J}\|Df\|\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}<\infty
and
f|_{L\cap V\setminus J}
is absolutely continuous. We fix a line
L
satisfying all the above. We fix a segment
(z_{1},z_{2})\subset L\cap V
and denote by
A_{i}
,
i\in I
, the components of
(z_{1},z_{2})\setminus J
. We have

\displaystyle|f(z_{1})-f(z_{2})|

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{H}^{1}(f((z_{1},z_{2})))\leq\sum_{i\in I}\mathcal{H}^{1}(f(A_{i}))+\mathcal{H}^{1}(f(J\cap L))

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i\in I}\int_{A_{i}}\|Df\|\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}=\int_{(z_{1},z_{2})\setminus J}\|Df\|\,d\mathcal{H}^{1}.

This implies that
f
is absolutely continuous on
L\cap V
, as desired.
∎

2.5.
Hyperbolic geometry

If
X
is a hyperbolic Riemann surface, we denote by
d_{X}
its hyperbolic metric.

Lemma 2.8

(
[
26
]
*Lemma 5.5)
.

Let
K\geq 1
and
\phi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a
K
-quasiconformal map that fixes the points
0,1,\infty
. Then for
X=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0,1,\infty\}
and
t\in X
we have

d_{X}(t,\phi(t))\leq\log K.

More generally, let
z_{i},w_{i}\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
,
\phi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a
K
-quasiconformal map with
\phi(z_{i})=w_{i}
,
i\in\{1,2,3\}
, and
M
be a Möbius map with
M(z_{i})=w_{i}
,

. Then for
t\in\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}\}
and
X=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{w_{1},w_{2},w_{3}\}
we have

d_{X}(M(t),\phi(t))\leq\log K.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
K\geq 1
,
U\subsetneq\mathbb{C}
be a simply connected domain, and
\phi\colon U\to U
be a
K
-quasiconformal homeomorphism that extends to identity map on
\partial U
.
Then

\sup_{z\in U}d_{U}(z,\phi(z))\leq C(K).

Moreover, for each
K_{0}>1
there exists some constant
A=A(K_{0})>0
so that if
K\leq K_{0}
, then

\sup_{z\in{U}}d_{U}(z,\phi(z))\leq A\log K.

Proof.

Since
\phi
is orientation-preserving, by
[
31
]
*Proposition 5.2,
\phi
is the identity on the ideal boundary
I(U)
, i.e., the space of prime ends of
U
. By conjugating
\phi
with the uniformization map
\psi\colon\mathbb{D}\to U
, we may assume that
U=\mathbb{D}
and
\phi
extends to the identity map on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. We extend
\phi
to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
by the identity map in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\mathbb{D}
to obtain a
K
-quasiconformal map on
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
; here one uses the removability of the circle
[
21
]
*Theorem I.8.3. Let
z\in\mathbb{D}
and
M\colon\overline{\mathbb{D}}\to\overline{\mathbb{D}}
be a Möbius transformation such that
M(z)=0
. Then
\psi=M\circ\phi\circ M^{-1}
is
K
-quasiconformal in
\mathbb{D}
and its restriction to
\partial\mathbb{D}
is the identity map. By replacing
\phi
with
\psi
, it suffices to show the statement at the origin
z=0
.

Since
\phi|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}
is the identity map, in particular, it fixes the points
1,i,-1
.
By compactness of the space of
K
-quasiconformal maps fixing
1,i,-1
(see
[
21
]
*Theorem II.5.1), we conclude that
d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\phi(0))\leq C(K)
. This proves the first part. For the second part, if
K\leq K_{0}
, then by the first part
\phi(0)
lies in the hyperbolic ball
B_{d_{\mathbb{D}}}(0,C(K_{0}))
. We set
X=\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{1,i,-1\}
. In the ball
B_{d_{\mathbb{D}}}(0,C(K_{0}))
the hyperbolic metrics
d_{X}
and
d_{\mathbb{D}}
are comparable with multiplicative constants depending on
K_{0}
. We apply Lemma
2.8
to the map
\phi\colon X\to X
to conclude that

d_{X}(0,\phi(0))\leq\log K.

Therefore,
d_{\mathbb{D}}(0,\phi(0))\leq A\log K
for some constant
A=A(K_{0})
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\Omega_{n}=\mathbb{C}\setminus[n,\infty)
and
q_{n}\in\Omega_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. Suppose that there exist constants
C>0
and
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

d_{\Omega_{n}}(q_{n-1},q_{n})<C\delta^{n}

for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then the sequence
\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent in the Euclidean metric and, in particular, it is bounded.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\psi_{n}\colon\mathbb{D}\to\Omega_{n}
be a conformal map with
\psi_{n}(0)=q_{n-1}
; note that
q_{n-1}\in\Omega_{n-1}\subset\Omega_{n}
. The hyperbolic ball
B_{d_{\mathbb{D}}}(0,C\delta^{n})
is contained in a Euclidean ball
B(0,C_{1}\delta^{n})
, where
C_{1}>0
does not depend on
n
. By Koebe’s distortion theorem (
[
42
]
*Section 1.3), for each
z\in B_{d_{\mathbb{D}}}(0,C\delta^{n})
we have

|\psi_{n}(z)-q_{n-1}|\leq C_{2}|\psi_{n}^{\prime}(0)|\delta^{n}\leq 4C_{2}\dist(q_{n-1},\partial\Omega_{n})\delta^{n},

where
C_{2}>0
does not depend on
n
. Equivalently, the above holds whenever
\psi_{n}(z)\in B_{d_{\Omega_{n}}}(q_{n-1},C\delta^{n})
. Since
d_{\Omega_{n}}(q_{n},q_{n-1})<C\delta^{n}
, we conclude that

|q_{n-1}-q_{n}|\leq\frac{1}{2}M\dist(q_{n-1},\partial\Omega_{n})\delta^{n},

where
M=8C_{2}
. Note that
\dist(q_{n-1},\partial\Omega_{n})\leq n+|q_{n-1}|\leq 2\max\{n,|q_{n-1}|\}
, so

(2.3)

\displaystyle|q_{n-1}-q_{n}|\leq M\max\{n,|q_{n-1}|\}\cdot\delta^{n}.

Therefore,

(2.4)

\displaystyle|q_{n}|\leq|q_{n-1}|+M\max\{n,|q_{n-1}|\}\cdot\delta^{n}

for all
n\geq 1
.

Let
N_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
MN_{0}\delta^{N_{0}}<1
. Suppose that
|q_{n-1}|\geq n
for all
n>N_{0}
. Then by (
2.4
), we have

n+1\leq|q_{n}|\leq|q_{n-1}|+M|q_{n-1}|\cdot\delta^{n}=(1+M\delta^{n})|q_{n-1}|\leq|q_{N_{0}}|\prod_{i=N_{0}+1}^{\infty}(1+M\delta^{i})

for
n>N_{0}
. This is a contradiction. Therefore, there exists
n_{0}>N_{0}
with
|q_{n_{0}-1}|<n_{0}
. By (
2.4
), we have

|q_{n_{0}}|\leq|q_{n_{0}-1}|+Mn_{0}\delta^{n_{0}}\leq|q_{n_{0}-1}|+MN_{0}\delta^{N_{0}}<|q_{n_{0}-1}|+1<n_{0}+1.

Inductively, we conclude that
|q_{n_{0}+k-1}|\leq n_{0}+k
for
k\geq 0
. Therefore, by (
2.3
) we have

|q_{n_{0}+k-1}-q_{n_{0}+k}|\leq M(n_{0}+k)\delta^{n_{0}+k}.

for all
k\geq 0
. This implies that
\{q_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is convergent in the Euclidean metric.
∎

Corollary 2.11
.

Let
C>0
,
\delta\in(0,1)
, and for each

let
\phi_{n}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a
(1+C\delta^{n})
-quasiconformal map such that
\phi_{n}|_{[0,1/n]}
is the identity map. Then for each

and for

,

, the sequence

.

Proof.

Let
\psi_{n}(z)=1/\phi_{n}(1/z)
,

, and note that
\psi_{n}
is
(1+C\delta^{n})
-quasiconformal and
\psi_{n}|_{[n,\infty]}
is the identity map. Let
\Omega_{n}=\mathbb{C}\setminus[n,\infty)
and
q_{n}=1/p_{n}
,

. By Lemma
2.9
, there exists a constant

such that

∎

Lemma 2.12
.

For

, let

Expansive circle maps and conjugating homeomorphisms

In this section, we summarize the main results of
[

]
on piecewise quasiconformal expansive circle maps and on extending a circle homeomorphism conjugating two such systems to a quasiconformal or David homeomorphism of the disk. See also
[

for the case of piecewise analytic maps.

3.1.
Markov partitions

If
a,b\in\mathbb{S}^{1}
, we denote by

¿

\textstyle[a,b]

and

¿

\textstyle(a,b)

the closed and open arcs, respectively, from
a
to
b
in the positive orientation. The arc

¿

\textstyle(b,a)

, for example, is the complementary arc of

¿

\textstyle[a,b]

. We also denote the arc

¿

\textstyle(a,b)

by
\inter{\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{1283033}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\displaystyle[a,b]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{1283033}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\textstyle[a,b]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{1069065}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\scriptstyle[a,b]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{966472}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle[a,b]$}}}}
. We say that two non-overlapping arcs
I,J\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}
are
adjacent
if they share an endpoint.

Definition 3.1

(Markov partition)
.

A
Markov partition
associated to a covering map
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
is a covering of the unit circle by closed arcs
A_{k}=\mathchoice{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{2656642}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\displaystyle[a_{k},a_{k+1}]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{2656642}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\textstyle[a_{k},a_{k+1}]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{2255655}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\scriptstyle[a_{k},a_{k+1}]$}}}{\vbox{\hbox{\resizebox{2145492}{0}{{\char 62\relax}}}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle[a_{k},a_{k+1}]$}}}
,
k\in\{0,\dots,r\}
,
r\geq 1
, where
a_{r+1}=a_{0}
, that have disjoint interiors and satisfy the following conditions.

(i)

The map
f_{k}=f|_{\inter{A_{k}}}
is injective for
k\in\{0,\dots,r\}
.

(ii)

If
f(\inter{A_{k}})\cap\inter{A_{j}}\neq\emptyset
for some
k,j\in\{0,\dots,r\}
, then
\inter{A_{j}}\subset f(\inter{A_{k}})
.

(iii)

The set
\{a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\}
is invariant under
f
.

We denote the above Markov partition by
\mathcal{P}(f;\{a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\})
.

Note that by definition, the points
a_{0},\dots,a_{r}
are ordered in the positive orientation if
r\geq 2
; if
r=1
, there is no natural order. Moreover,
(ii)
and
(iii)
are equivalent under condition
(i)
.

Let
f\colon\mathbb{S}^{1}\to\mathbb{S}^{1}
be a covering map and consider a Markov partition
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{P}(f;\{a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\})
. We can associate a matrix
B=(b_{kj})_{k,j=0}^{r}
to
\mathcal{P}
so that
b_{kj}=1
if
f_{k}(A_{k})\supset A_{j}
and
b_{kj}=0
otherwise. In the case
b_{kj}=1
we define
A_{kj}
to be the closure of
f_{k}^{-1}(\inter A_{j})
. If
w=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\{0,\dots,r\}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
k\in\{0,\dots,r\}
, and once
A_{w}
has been defined, we define
A_{kw}
to be the closure of
f_{k}^{-1}(\inter A_{w})
whenever
b_{kj_{1}}=1
. A
word

w=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\{0,\dots,r\}^{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
, is
admissible (for the Markov partition
\mathcal{P}
)
if
b_{j_{1}j_{2}}=\dots=b_{j_{n-1}j_{n}}=1
. The empty word
w
is considered to be admissible and we define
A_{w}=\mathbb{S}^{1}
. We also define
A_{w}=\emptyset
if
w
is not admissible. The
length
of a word
w=(j_{1},\dots,j_{n})\in\{0,\dots,r\}^{n}
is defined to be
|w|=n
and the length of the empty word is
0
. It follows from properties
(i)
and
(ii)
that for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the arcs
A_{w}
, where
|w|=n
, have disjoint interiors and their union is equal to
\mathbb{S}^{1}
.

Inductively, we have
A_{wj}\subset A_{w}
for all admissible words
w
and
j\in\{0,\dots,r\}
. If
A_{wj}
is non-empty, we say that
A_{wj}
is a
child
of
A_{w}
and
A_{w}
is the
parent
of
A_{wj}
. Thus,
A_{w}
has at most
r+1
children. Also, if
k\in\{0,\dots,r\}
,
w
is a non-empty word, and
(k,w)
is admissible, then
f
maps
\inter A_{kw}
homeomorphically onto
\inter A_{w}
. Thus,
f
acts as a subshift on the space of admissible words.

We let
F_{n}
be the preimages of
F_{1}=\{a_{0},\dots,a_{r}\}
under
n-1
iterations of
f
and
F_{0}=\emptyset
. Observe that
F_{n}\supset F_{n-1}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the set
\mathbb{S}^{1}\setminus F_{n}
consists of open arcs. For practical purposes we will use the terminology
complementary arcs of
F_{n}
to indicate the family of the

4.1.
Subdivision rules

We first recall that a CW complex
Y
is a
subdivision
of a CW complex
X
if
X=Y
(as topological spaces) and every closed cell of
Y
is contained in a closed cell of
X
.
We define a
polygon
as a finite CW complex homeomorphic to a closed disk
that consists of one 2-cell, whose 1-skeleton is a Jordan curve with at least three 0-cells.
We will also call 0-cells, 1-cells, and 2-cells the vertices, edges, and faces respectively. We say two vertices are
adjacent
if they are on the boundary of an edge and
non-adjacent
otherwise.

Definition 4.1
.

A
finite subdivision rule

\mathcal{R}
consists of

(1)

a finite collection of oriented polygons
\{P_{i}:i=1,\dots,k\}
,

(2)

a subdivision
\mathcal{R}(P_{i})
that decomposes each polygon
P_{i}
into
m_{i}\geq 2
closed 2-cells

P_{i}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{m_{i}}P_{i,j}

so that each edge of
\partial P_{i}
contains no vertices of
\mathcal{R}(P_{i})
in its interior and each
2
-cell
P_{i,j}
inherits the orientation of
P_{i}
, and

(3)

a map
\sigma\colon\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}(\{i\}\times\{1,\dots,m_{i}\})\to\{1,\dots,k\}
and a collection of orientation-preserving cellular maps, denoted by

\psi_{i,j}:P_{\sigma(i,j)}\to P_{i,j},\quad i\in\{1,\dots,k\},\,\,j\in\{1,\dots,m_{i}\},

that are homeomorphisms between the open 2-cells. We call
P_{\sigma(i,j)}
the
type
of the closed 2-cell
P_{i,j}
.

For simplicity, we use the notation
\mathcal{R}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
for the finite subdivision rule
\mathcal{R}
, suppressing the decomposition in
(2)
and the cellular maps in
(3)
.

By condition
(3)
, we can iterate the subdivision procedure and obtain for each
n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
a decomposition
\mathcal{R}^{n}(P_{i})
of

, where
\mathcal{R}^{0}(P_{i})
=

and
\mathcal{R}^{1}(P_{i})=\mathcal{R}(P_{i})
. By condition
(2)
, we can identify the
1
-skeleton
\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
of
\mathcal{R}^{n}(P_{i})
as a subgraph of the
1
-skeleton
\mathcal{G}^{n+1}(P_{i})
of
\mathcal{R}^{n+1}(P_{i})
.
We consider the direct limit

\mathcal{G}(P_{i})=\lim_{\rightarrow}\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})=\bigcup_{n=0}^{\infty}\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})

for
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
. We call
\mathcal{G}(P_{i})
,
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, the

for
\mathcal{R}
. We consider each polygon
P_{i}
as a subset of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
and we call the set
F_{i}\coloneqq\overline{\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus P_{i}}
the

. Note that

is not subdivided, and remains a face of

for all

.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a finite subdivision rule.

•

We say that
\mathcal{R}
is

•

is an induced subgraph of

.

•

We say that
\mathcal{R}
is

if, for each

, for any pair of non-adjacent vertices

, the two components of
\partial P_{i}\setminus\{v,w\}
are connected in
\mathcal{G}(P_{i})\setminus\{v,w\}
.
We call
\mathcal{R}

By cutting each polygon

into finitely many pieces if necessary, we can always make a finite subdivision rule

irreducible.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let

be a gasket whose contact graph

is a spherical subdivision graph for a simple, irreducible, acylindrical finite subdivision rule

.

(1)

.

(2)

(3)

of

,

, there exists a ball

For the proof of
(1)
, by replacing
\mathcal{R}
with an iterate, it suffices to assume that
\mathcal{R}
satisfies condition

. We argue by contradiction. Suppose that there exists a sequence of faces

of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,

, such that

is bounded below away from

. We may assume that

for all

. In view of

, there are three cases: (a) no vertex of
\mathcal{G}
lies in

for infinitely many

; (b) there exists a unique vertex

that lies in

for all but finitely many

; (c) there exist exactly two vertices

that lie in

for all but finitely many

, in which case,

and

are adjacent. By the definition of a subdivision rule (see Definition

),

consists of a uniformly bounded number of vertices for all

.

For the proof of

, let

of

.

be an arbitrary face of

. Let

be a largest component of

among components corresponding to the vertices of

. Recall that

consists of a uniformly bounded number of vertices, say at most

. Therefore, if

is sufficiently small (given that we are dealing with subsets of the sphere), then

(4.1)

\displaystyle D^{n}\subset W(F^{n})\subset B(w,K\diam D^{n})

for any point

. By
(1)
, there exists

such that (

) holds for

. If, in addition

, then there exists a uniform

.

5.
Circle packings with finite subdivision rules

We assume that all finite subdivision rules appearing in this section satisfy the standing assumptions
(S1)
,
(S1′)
, and
(S2)
from Section
4
.

Consider a collection
\mathcal{P}
of geometric disks of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
with disjoint interiors.
We can associate to it a graph
\mathcal{G}
, called the
tangency graph
of
\mathcal{P}
.
The tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
assigns a vertex
v
to each disk
D_{v}
of
\mathcal{P}
and an edge between
v,w
if
\partial D_{v}\cap\partial D_{w}\neq\emptyset
.
The tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
naturally embeds in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
, so it is a plane graph.
We say that the collection
\mathcal{P}
is a
circle packing
if the tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
is connected.
The
limit set

\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
of
\mathcal{P}
is the closure of the union of all boundary circles corresponding to disks in
\mathcal{P}
. Note that the limit set
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
of a circle packing
\mathcal{P}
is a gasket according to Definition
1.1
. In the following considerations we will frequently refer to circles in
\mathcal{P}
, meaning boundaries of disks in
\mathcal{P}
.
We say that two plane graphs are isomorphic if they are isomorphic as graphs, and the isomorphism respects the embeddings in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

In this paper, we will always
mark
the circle packing
\mathcal{P}
with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
, i.e, label each circle in
\mathcal{P}
with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
by the corresponding vertex in
\mathcal{G}
.
By a homeomorphism
\psi
between two marked circle packings
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
, we mean an blueorientation-preserving homeomorphism
\psi
of the sphere
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
such that
\psi(\Lambda(\mathcal{P}))=\Lambda(\mathcal{P}^{\prime})
and
\psi
preserves the labeling.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a finite subdivision rule and
\mathcal{P}
be a circle packing.

•

We say that
\mathcal{P}
is a circle packing with finite subdivision rule of
\mathcal{R}
if its tangency graph is a subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
.

•

We say that
\mathcal{P}
is a circle packing with spherical subdivision rule of
\mathcal{R}
if its tangency graph is a spherical subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a finite subdivision rule and
\mathcal{G}
be a subdivision graph or a spherical subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
. Let
\mathcal{P}
be a circle packing with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
. We denote by
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
the (marked) moduli space of circle packings with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
consists of equivalence classes of marked circle packings whose tangency graph is isomorphic to

as plane graphs, where two marked circle packings
\mathcal{P}
and

are equivalent if there exists a Möbius map sending each circle

of
\mathcal{P}
to the corresponding circle

in

Theorem 5.2

(Realization and uniqueness;
[
26
]
*Theorems A, E)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a simple, irreducible finite subdivision rule.

(i)

Each subdivision graph
\mathcal{G}(P_{i})
,
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
, is isomorphic to the tangency graph of an infinite circle packing if and only if
\mathcal{R}
is acylindrical.

(ii)

If
\mathcal{R}
is acylindrical, then the following statements are true for each

.

(a)

Any two marked circle packings
\mathcal{P}_{i},\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime}
with tangency graph
\mathcal{G}(P_{i})
are quasiconformally homeomorphic, i.e., there exists a quasiconformal map
\Psi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
between
\mathcal{P}_{i}
and
\mathcal{P}_{i}^{\prime}
.

(b)

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}(P_{i}))

of the ideal polygon associated to the external face of

.

Let
\mathcal{G}(X)
be a simple spherical subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
.

(I)

(Realization)
The graph
\mathcal{G}(X)
is isomorphic to the tangency graph of a circle packing
\mathcal{P}
if and only if
\mathcal{R}
is acylindrical.

(II)

(Combinatorial rigidity)
If the graph
\mathcal{G}(X)
is isomorphic to the tangency graph of a circle packing, then this circle packing is unique up to Möbius maps.

\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, such that
\{x\}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega_{F^{n}}}
.

(3)

For each circle
C
in
\mathcal{P}
the tangency points of
\mathcal{P}
that lie on
C
are dense in
C\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}
(resp.
C
).

(4)

The tangency points of
\mathcal{P}
are dense in
{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}
(resp.
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
).

Proof.

Suppose that
\mathcal{G}
is a subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
; the proofs for spherical subdivision graphs are similar. We claim that for each
n\geq 0
,
\overline{\Omega_{n}}
contains all intersections of circles of
\mathcal{P}
with
\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}
. Indeed, let
C_{v}
be a circle of
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to a vertex
v
of
\mathcal{G}
. If
v
is not a vertex of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
, then
v
is contained in a face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
. Lemma
5.3

(7)
implies that the circle
C_{v}
is disjoint from
\partial\Omega_{F}
so it is contained in
\Omega_{F}
. If
v
is a vertex of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
, we denote by
F_{1},...,F_{j}
be the faces of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
that have
v
on their boundary. Again, Lemma
5.3

(7)
implies that each arc of
C_{v}\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}\partial\Omega_{F_{i}}
is contained in some
\Omega_{F_{i}}
, so
C_{v}\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{j}\overline{\Omega_{F_{i}}}
.

Since
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
is the closure of the union of circles in
\mathcal{P}
, by the above claim we conclude that
{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}\subset\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega_{n}}
. Conversely, let
x\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega_{n}}
. Then there exists a nested sequence of faces
F^{n}
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, so that
x\in\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega_{F^{n}}}
. By Proposition
5.4
,
\diam\Omega_{F^{n}}\to 0
. Thus, there exists a sequence of distinct circles of
\mathcal{P}
converging to
x
. Since all but finitely many circles of
\mathcal{P}
lie in
{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}
and the latter is closed, we conclude that
x\in{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}
. This proves part
(1)
. Note that
(2)
follows from
(1)
by repeating the above argument.

Let
C
be a circle in
\mathcal{P}
and
x\in C\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}
. By
(2)
there exists a nested sequence of faces
F^{n}
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, so that
\{x\}=\bigcap_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{\Omega_{F^{n}}}
. For all sufficiently large
n\geq 0
, the vertex of
\mathcal{G}
corresponding to
C
lies in
\partial F^{n}
, so the tile
\overline{\Omega_{F^{n}}}
contains a tangency point on
C
; see Lemma
5.3

(7)
. Thus, we conclude that the tangency points of
\mathcal{P}
that lie on
C
are dense in
C\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}
, as in
(3)
. Part
(4)
follows from
(3)
and the fact that the circles of
\mathcal{P}
are dense in
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
by definition.
∎

The next statement is an immediate consequence of the density of tangency points in the limit set.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a simple, irreducible, acylindrical finite subdivision rule. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a (resp. spherical) subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
and
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
. Then any two homeomorphisms between
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
coincide on
{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}
(resp.
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})
).

5.2.
Exponential contraction

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a simple, irreducible, acylindrical finite subdivision rule.
Let
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}(P_{i})
be a subdivision graph for
\mathcal{R}
and let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
. Let
F
be a non-external face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}=\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
for some
n\geq 0
.
Denote by
\mathcal{P}_{F}
the infinite sub-circle packing of
\mathcal{P}
associated to the subgraph
F\cap\mathcal{G}
in
\mathcal{G}
. Using renormalization methods, it is proved that the Teichmüller distance between sub-circle packings at deeper levels decays exponentially.

Theorem 5.7

(Exponential contraction;
[
26
]
*Theorem C)
.

Let
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
.
Let
F
be a non-external face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
.
Then

d(\mathcal{P}_{F},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{F})=O(\delta^{n})\,\,\,\textrm{as $n\to\infty$}

for some universal constant
\delta\in(0,1)
.

We discuss some implications of the exponential contraction.

5.2.1.
Asymptotic conformality

Let
X\subset\mathbb{C}
be a set, not necessarily open, and
\alpha>0
.
A map
\Psi\colon X\to\mathbb{C}
is
C^{1+\alpha}
-conformal
at a point
z\in X
if the complex derivative

\Psi^{\prime}(z)\coloneqq\lim_{z+t\in X,t\to 0}\frac{\Psi(z+t)-\Psi(z)}{t}

exists, and

\Psi(z+t)=\Psi(z)+\Psi^{\prime}(z)t+O(|t|^{1+\alpha})

for all
z+t\in X
and
t
sufficiently small (cf.
[
33
]
*Section B.6).
As a corollary of exponential contraction, we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.8

(Asymptotic conformality;
[
26
]
*Theorem D)
.

Let
\Psi\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a homeomorphism with
\Psi(\infty)=\infty
between two circle packings
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
. Then
\Psi|_{\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}}
is
C^{1+\alpha}
-conformal at every point on
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}\cap\mathbb{C}
and
\Psi^{\prime}
is non-zero on
\Lambda(\mathcal{P})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P})}\cap\mathbb{C}
.

The assumption that
\Psi
fixes
\infty
is only to ensure that
\Psi^{\prime}
can be defined on points
z\neq\infty
. The last statement that
\Psi^{\prime}
is non-zero is not contained in
[
26
]
*Theorem D, but it follows from applying
[
26
]
*Theorem D to
\Psi^{-1}
, which gives the existence of
(\Psi^{-1})^{\prime}
.

5.2.2.
Renormalization periodic circle packings

For
n\geq 0
, let
F^{n}
be a non-external face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}=\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
and assume that
F^{n+1}\subset F^{n}
,
n\geq 0
.
Recall that each face
F^{n}
is identified with a polygon
P_{\sigma(n)}\in\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
, and
F^{0}=P_{i}=P_{\sigma(0)}
.
Denote this identification by
\psi_{n}\colon F^{n}\to P_{\sigma(n)}
. (Since all faces are assumed to be polygons, the cellular identification map in Definition
4.1
may be taken to be a homeomorphism in the entire face, rather than in the interior only. The map
\psi_{n}
here is obtained from the inverse of that identification map.)
We say that the sequence
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is periodic of period
p
if
\sigma(n)=\sigma(n+p)
and
\psi_{n}(F^{n+p})=\psi_{n+p}(F^{n+2p})\subset P_{\sigma(n)}=P_{\sigma(n+p)}
for all
n\geq 0
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
.
Let
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be periodic with period
p
.
Then the identification
\psi_{p}\colon F^{p}\to P_{\sigma(p)}
gives an identification of
F^{p}\cap\mathcal{G}
with
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}(P_{\sigma(0)})=\mathcal{G}(P_{\sigma(p)})
. Therefore, by Theorem
5.2
, this identification induces a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\Psi
between
\mathcal{P}_{F^{p}}
and
\mathcal{P}
. We call this map
\Psi
a
quasiconformal symmetry
of
\mathcal{P}
for
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
. By Corollary
5.6
, the restriction
\Psi|_{\Lambda(\mathcal{P}_{F^{p}})\cap\overline{\Pi(\mathcal{P}_{F^{p}})}}
is uniquely defined.

Definition 5.9
.

Suppose that the sequence
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is periodic with period
p
.
We say that
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
hyperbolic
if
\partial F^{p}\cap\partial F^{0}
is either empty or a single vertex. Otherwise, we say that
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is
parabolic
.

Recall that for a non-external face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
n\geq 0
, we denote by
\Omega_{F}
be the corresponding open Markov tile (see Section
5.1
) and by
\Lambda_{F}
its boundary.

Theorem 5.10

(Periodic circle packing;
[
26
]
*Theorem D)
.

Suppose that the sequence
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is periodic with period
p
. Then there exists a unique up to Möbius maps circle packing
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
so that for each
n\geq 0
we have

d(\mathcal{P}_{F^{n}},\mathcal{P}_{F^{n+p}})=0.

In particular, there is a Möbius map
\Psi
with
\Psi(\mathcal{P}_{F^{p}})=\mathcal{P}
.
The map
\Psi
is a parabolic Möbius map if
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is parabolic, and a loxodromic Möbius map if
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
is hyperbolic.
Moreover, the sequence of sets
\{\Omega_{F^{kp}}\}_{k\geq 0}
converges to the non-attracting fixed point of
\Psi
.

Let
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be periodic with period
p
.
We will call the unique circle packing
\mathcal{P}
in Theorem
5.10
the
renormalization periodic circle packing
associated to
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
, and the Möbius map
\Psi
in Theorem
5.10
the
Möbius symmetry
of
\mathcal{P}
for
\{F^{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
.

5.2.3.
Teichmüller mappings

Let
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
.
By Theorem
5.2
, there exists a quasiconformal map between
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
. Here we define the notion of a Teichmüller mapping between two circle packings, by imposing some conditions in addition to quasiconformality.

Definition 5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
. A quasiconformal homeomorphism
\Psi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
between
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}
is said to be a
Teichmüller mapping
if for every
n\geq 0
and for every non-external face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
we have

•

\frac{1}{2}\log K(\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}})=d(\mathcal{P}_{F},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{F})
and

•

\Psi|_{\Lambda_{F}}
conjugates the dynamics of
\Gamma_{F}
and
\Gamma^{\prime}_{F}
.

In particular,
\Psi
minimizes the dilatation on
\Omega_{F}
for every face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
. The conjugation condition allows us to have the following extension property.

Proposition 5.12
.

Let
\Psi:\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
be a Teichmüller mapping between
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
.
Then for every
n\geq 0
and for every non-external face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
,
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}}
extends to a quasiconformal homeomorphism
\Psi_{F}\colon\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\to\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
between
\mathcal{P}_{F}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\prime}_{F}
with dilatation

K(\Psi_{F})
satisfying
\frac{1}{2}\log K(\Psi_{F})=d(\mathcal{P}_{F},\mathcal{P}_{F}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Since the restriction of
\Psi
on the limit set
\Lambda_{F}
is a conjugation, one can extend the map
\Psi|_{\Lambda_{F}}
to a quasiconformal homeomorphism between
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{F}}
and
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{F}}
sending the ideal polygon in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{F}}
associated to the external face to the corresponding ideal polygon in
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\overline{\Omega^{\prime}_{F}}
, and whose dilatation
K
satisfies that
\frac{1}{2}\log K
is equal to the Teichmüller distance between the two ideal polygons.
By Theorem
5.2
, the Teichmüller distance between the two ideal polygons equals
d(\mathcal{P}_{F},\mathcal{P}_{F}^{\prime})
.
The proposition now follows.
∎

Theorem 5.13

(
[
26
]
*Theorem 5.15)
.

There exists a Teichmüller mapping between any two circle packings
\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G})
.

5.3.
Markov maps

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
be a simple, irreducible, acylindrical finite subdivision rule.
Let
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{G}(X)
be a spherical subdivision graph for some
\mathcal{R}
-complex
X
homeomorphic to a sphere.
Let
\mathcal{P}
be the corresponding circle packing for
\mathcal{G}
. In this section, we discuss how the subdivision rule induces a Markov map for the circle packing
\mathcal{P}
.

Let
n\geq 0
and
F
be a face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
.
Then
F
is identified with some polygon
P_{\sigma(F)}\in\{P_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
. Denote the identification map by

\phi_{F}\colon F\to P_{\sigma(F)}.

This map induces a canonical identification of the subgraph
F\cap\mathcal{G}
with the subdivision graph
\mathcal{G}(P_{\sigma(F)})
for
\mathcal{R}
.
Recall that
\mathcal{P}_{F}
is the infinite sub-circle packing of
\mathcal{P}
associated to the subgraph
F\cap\mathcal{G}
in
\mathcal{G}
.
Thus,
\mathcal{P}_{F}
has tangency graph isomorphic to
\mathcal{G}(P_{\sigma(F)})
.
Recall that
\Omega_{F}
is the Markov tile associated to the face
F
, and

\Omega^{1}=\bigcup_{F\text{ face }\mathcal{G}^{1}}\Omega_{F}\,\,\,\textrm{and}\,\,\,\Omega^{0}=\bigcup_{F\text{ face }\mathcal{G}^{0}}\Omega_{F}.

Definition 5.14
.

A map
\psi\colon\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{0}
is called a
subdivision homomorphism
if it is a homomorphism between the plane graphs
\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{0}
and for each face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
, there exists a face
F^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
that we denote by
\psi_{*}(F)
such that
\sigma(F)=\sigma(F^{\prime})
and

\psi|_{\partial F}=\phi_{F^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\phi_{F}|_{\partial F}.

In the next proposition, for a vertex
v
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
we denote by
C_{v}
the circle of
\mathcal{P}
associated to
v
. By Lemma
5.5

(1)
we have
C_{v}\subset\overline{\Omega^{1}}
.

Proposition 5.15

(Induced Markov map)
.

Let
\psi\colon\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{0}
be subdivision homomorphism.
Then
\psi
induces a map
\Psi\colon\Omega^{1}\to\Omega^{0}
with the following properties.

(1)

For each face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
we have
\Psi({\Omega_{F}})=\Omega_{\psi_{*}(F)}
and
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}}
is the restriction of a Teichmüller mapping between
\mathcal{P}_{F}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\psi_{*}(F)}
.

(2)

For each
n\geq 0
and each face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{n+1}
there exists a face
F^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}^{n}
so that
F
and
F^{\prime}
are canonically identified with the same face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
for some
i\in\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\Psi(\Omega_{F})=\Omega_{F^{\prime}}
.

(3)

\Psi
extends continuously to
\overline{\Omega^{1}}
.

(4)

For each vertex
v
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
the map
\Psi|_{C_{v}}
is a covering map from
C_{v}
onto
C_{\psi(v)}
.

(5)

For each edge
e=[v,w]
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
we have
\Psi(C_{v}\cap C_{w})=C_{\psi(v)}\cap C_{\psi(w)}
.

Proof.

Let
F
be a face of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
F^{\prime}=\psi_{*}(F)
be the corresponding face of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
. Then
\phi_{F^{\prime}}^{-1}\circ\phi_{F}
induces an identification of the tangency graph of
\mathcal{P}_{F}
with the tangency graph of
\mathcal{P}_{F^{\prime}}
. Let
\Omega_{F}
and
\Omega_{F^{\prime}}
be the corresponding Markov tiles. Then, by Theorem
5.13
, there exists a map

\Psi_{F\to F^{\prime}}\colon\Omega_{F}\to\Omega_{F^{\prime}}

that is the restriction of a Teichmüller mapping between
\mathcal{P}_{F}
and
\mathcal{P}_{F^{\prime}}
. Repeating this process for each face
F
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
gives the desired map
\Psi\colon\Omega^{1}\to\Omega^{0}
satisfying
(1)
.

Let
F
be a face of
\mathcal{G}^{n+1}
and let
F^{1}
be the face of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
containing
F
. The subdivision homomorphism
\psi
sends the face
F^{1}
to a face
\psi_{*}(F^{1})=F^{0}
of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
. Note that by the definition of a subdivision homomorphism the faces
F^{1},F^{0}
are identified with the same polygon in the subdivision rule, say
P_{i}
. Also, the face
F
is identified with a face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
. This face corresponds canonically to a face
F^{\prime}
in
F^{0}
. By part
(1)
,
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F^{1}}}
is the restriction of a Teichmüller mapping, and in particular of a homeomorphism, between the circle packings
\mathcal{P}_{F^{1}}
and
\mathcal{P}_{F^{0}}
. Given that the labeling is preserved and that
\Psi
conjugates
\Gamma_{F}
to
\Gamma_{F^{\prime}}
, we conclude that
\Psi(\Omega_{F})=\Omega_{F^{\prime}}
. This proves
(2)
.

To prove
(3)
, suppose that two faces
F_{1},F_{2}
of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
share part of their boundary. Then
\psi
sends
\partial F_{1}\cap\partial F_{2}
to
\partial\psi_{*}(F_{1})\cap\partial\psi_{*}(F_{2})
. Let
\Psi_{i}=\Psi|_{\Omega_{F_{i}}}
,
i=1,2
. By the properties of a Teichmüller mapping, for
i=1,2
the map
\Psi_{i}
extends to
\Lambda_{F_{i}}
such that
\Psi_{i}|_{\Lambda_{F_{i}}}
conjugates the dynamics of
\Gamma_{F_{i}}
and
\Gamma_{\psi_{*}(F_{i})}
. In combination with Lemma
5.3
, this implies that the extensions of
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F_{1}}}
and
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F_{2}}}
agree on
\partial\Omega_{F_{1}}\cap\partial\Omega_{F_{2}}
. Hence,
\Psi
has a continuous extension to
\overline{\Omega^{1}}
.

We now prove
(4)
. Let
F
be a face of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
such that
v\in\partial F
. Since
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}}
extends to an orientation-preserving homeomorphism between
\mathcal{P}_{F}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\psi_{*}(F)}
, we see that it maps circles of
\mathcal{P}_{F}
to the corresponding circles of
\mathcal{P}_{\psi_{*}(F)}
. Thus,
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}}
maps the arc
\Omega_{F}\cap C_{v}
injectively onto the arc
\Omega_{\psi_{*}(F)}\cap C_{\psi(v)}
. Moreover, the extension of
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F}}
maps the disk of
\mathcal{P}_{F}
bounded by
C_{v}
to the disk of
\mathcal{P}_{\psi_{*}(F)}
bounded by
C_{\psi(v)}
. This implies that if
F_{1},F_{2}
are faces of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
such that
\partial F_{1}\cap\partial F_{2}
contains an edge that has
v
as an endpoint, then
\Psi|_{C_{v}}
is locally injective at the common endpoint of the arcs
\Omega_{F_{1}}\cap C_{v}
and
\Omega_{F_{2}}\cap C_{v}
. Thus,
\Psi|_{C_{v}}
is a covering map. Finally, observe that
(5)
follows from
(3)
and
(4)
.
∎

We call the map
\Psi\colon\Omega^{1}\to\Omega^{0}
provided by Proposition
5.15
a
Markov map
for
\mathcal{P}
(induced by the subdivision homomorphism
\psi\colon\mathcal{G}^{1}\to\mathcal{G}^{0}
). By construction, we also have the following extension property.

Proposition 5.16

(Uniform quasiconformality)
.

Let
\Psi
be a Markov map for a circle packing
\mathcal{P}
with spherical subdivision rule.
There exist constants
C>0
,
K\geq 1
, and
\delta\in(0,1)
so that for each
n,m\geq 0
the following statements are true.

(1)

For each open tile
U
of level
n+1
the map
\Psi|_{U}
has a
(1+C\delta^{n})
-quasiconformal extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

(2)

For each open tile
U
of level
n+m
the map
\Psi^{\circ m}|_{U}
has a
(1+C\delta^{n})
-quasiconformal extension to
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Proof.

Let
F_{U}
be the face of
\mathcal{G}^{n+1}
corresponding to the tile
U
and let
F^{1}
be the face of
\mathcal{G}^{1}
containing
F_{U}
. The subdivision homomorphism that induces the Markov map
\Psi
sends the face
F^{1}
to a face
F^{0}
of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
. Note that by the definition of a subdivision homomorphism the faces
F^{1},F^{0}
are identified with the same polygon in the subdivision rule, say
P_{i}
, so
\mathcal{G}\cap F^{1}
and
\mathcal{G}\cap F^{0}
are identified with
\mathcal{G}(P_{i})
. Also, the face
F_{U}
is identified with a face of
\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
. This face corresponds to a face
F_{V}
in
F^{0}
, where
V
is an open tile of level
n
, and we have
V=\Psi(U)
by Proposition
5.15

(2)
. By treating
\mathcal{P}_{F^{1}},\mathcal{P}_{F^{0}}
as elements of
\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}(P_{i}))
and
F_{U},F_{V}
as non-external faces of
\mathcal{G}^{n}(P_{i})
, we may apply Theorem
5.7
to deduce that
d(\mathcal{P}_{F_{U}},\mathcal{P}_{F_{V}})=O(\delta^{n})
for some constant
\delta\in(0,1)
. Moreover, by Proposition
5.15

(1)
,
\Psi|_{\Omega_{F^{1}}}
is the restriction of a Teichmüller mapping between
\mathcal{P}_{F^{1}},\mathcal{P}_{F^{0}}\in\mathcal{M}(\mathcal{G}(P_{i}))
. By Proposition
5.12

In this section we establish the main tools for the proofs of Theorems

Theorem 7.1
.

Let

be a rational map without Julia critical points. Then each periodic point of

is either attracting or repelling or parabolic. In particular, there are no Cremer points and Siegel disks.

Recall that a point of a gasket Julia set is called a contact point if it is the unique intersection point of the boundaries of two Fatou components.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let

(i)

(ii)

(iii)

Proof.

Let

; note that

is a gasket. Since there are no Julia critical points,

is pre-periodic. This proves part

is a periodic contact point. Since

is in the Julia set, by Theorem

, which contradicts the definition of a gasket. This proves
(ii)
.

Finally, we prove

. Since

has no Julia critical points,

is locally bi-Lipschitz on the Julia set. Hence, it suffices to prove the statement for periodic Fatou components. Also, by Theorem

, every point in the Julia set that lies in the post-critical set of

is a parabolic periodic point. Let

be a periodic Fatou component.

Suppose that

is an immediate basin of an attracting point with period

. Suppose that
\partial U
intersects the post-critical set, so by the above it contains a parabolic periodic point

is a gasket and no point can be in the boundary of three Fatou components. Thus, the post-critical set is disjoint from

. By Lemma

(iii)
, every Fatou component is a quasidisk. This concludes the lemma.
∎

Remark 7.5
.

It is proved in
[
27
]
*Theorem 3.1 and Theorem 4.1 that there is a combinatorial obstruction for the existence of two or more periodic contact points. Specifically, a fat gasket has a unique parabolic cycle and this cycle consists of a single parabolic fixed point. We do not need this more refined classification in this paper.

Lemma 7.6
.

Let
R
be a rational map without Julia critical points and with connected Julia set. Then there exists a finite collection of Fatou components
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
that contain all critical orbits and
L\geq 1
such that for every Fatou component
W
the following statements are true.

(1)

There exists a minimal
n\geq 0
such that
R^{\circ n}(W)=V_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,l\}
.

(2)

R^{\circ n}
maps
W
conformally onto
V_{j}
and for every
z,w\in W
we have

L^{-1}\frac{\sigma(z,w)}{\diam W}\leq\sigma(R^{\circ n}(z),R^{\circ n}(w))\leq L\frac{\sigma(z,w)}{\diam W}.

Moreover, we may have that the collection
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
contains any given finite collection of Fatou components.

Proof.

By conjugating
R
with an isometry of the sphere, we may assume that the point at
\infty
is a periodic point in the Fatou set. We define the
rank

r(W)
of a Fatou component
W
to be the smallest index
j\geq 0
such that
R^{\circ j}(W)
is a periodic Fatou component. Since there are no critical points on the Julia set, by Theorem
7.1
each critical point is attracted to a parabolic cycle or to an attracting cycle. Thus, there exist finitely many Fatou components that intersect the critical orbits of
R
. Note there are only finitely many Fatou components attached to a parabolic periodic point (namely, if a Fatou component contains a (parabolic) periodic point in its boundary, it must be itself periodic). Therefore, there exist finitely many Fatou components whose closure intersects the post-critical set. We also consider a given finite collection of Fatou components as in the very last part of the lemma. Let
m\geq 0
be the maximum of the ranks among the given family of components and among components whose closure intersects the post-critical set. Denote by
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
the family of all Fatou components of
R
with rank at most
m+1
.

Let
W
be a Fatou component of rank
r(W)>m+1
. Then
W
is mapped onto
V_{j}
for some
j\in\{1,\dots,l\}
after
n(W)=r(W)-m-1
iterations. Note that
n(w)
is the smallest number with this property, as required in
(1)
. The Fatou components
W,V_{j}
are subsets of
\mathbb{C}
and they are both simply connected, since the Julia set is connected. By our choice of
m
, there is a neighborhood of
\overline{V_{j}}
that is disjoint from the the post-critical set of
R
. This implies that
R^{\circ n(W)}
maps a neighborhood of
\overline{W}
conformally onto a neighborhood of
\overline{V_{j}}
. Moreover, by Koebe’s distortion theorem, applied to a suitable branch of
(R^{\circ n(W)})^{-1}
,
(2)
holds with the Euclidean rather than the spherical metric. Since the two metrics are comparable away from the periodic Fatou component that contains the point at
\infty
, we obtain the desired conclusion.
∎

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
R
be a rational map without Julia critical points whose Julia set
\mathcal{J}(R)
is a gasket. If there is no periodic point that is parabolic with multiplicity
2
, then the Fatou components of
R
are uniform quasidisks.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.2
, each Fatou component is a quasidisk. In particular, the Fatou components
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
from Lemma
7.6
are quasidisks. By Lemma
7.6
, every other Fatou component is mapped to one of
V_{1},\dots,V_{l}
with an iterate of
R
that is uniformly quasisymmetric in the spherical metric. Hence, all Fatou components are uniform quasidisks.
∎

8.
Construction of a subdivision rule from a gasket Julia set

In this section we show how a gasket Julia set gives rise to a finite subdivision rule. The following statement is the main result of the section. Recall the discussion in Section
5.3
regarding subdivision homomorphisms.

Proposition 8.1
.

Let
f
be a rational map without Julia critical points whose Julia set
\mathcal{J}(f)
is a gasket. Then there exists a simple, irreducible, acylindrical finite subdivision rule
\mathcal{R}
and a graph
\mathcal{G}^{0}
that subdivides the sphere into polygons giving rise to a CW complex

so that the following statements are true.

(1)

The

of the subdivision

\mathcal{G}
of the gasket

(2)

Each periodic Fatou component of

corresponds to a vertex of
\mathcal{G}
that is also a vertex of
\mathcal{G}^{0}
.

(3)

There exists

so that each periodic Fatou component of

is fixed and the map

induces a subdivision homomorphism between
\mathcal{G}^{1}
and
\mathcal{G}^{0}

(4)

The standing assumptions

,
(S1′)

The proof relies on the following conjugacy result.

Lemma 8.2

Let

be a rational map without Julia critical points whose Julia set

is a gasket. There exists a hyperbolic and post-critically finite rational map

, and a homeomorphism

Proof.

By
[
13
]
*Theorem 1.3 and Theorem 1.4, there exists a sub-hyperbolic rational map
\tilde{g}
and a homeomorphism
\tilde{\phi}\colon\mathcal{J}(f)\to\mathcal{J}(\tilde{g})
conjugating the dynamics of

and
\tilde{g}
. Moreover,

is a uniform limit of homeomorphisms of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
. If

is a complementary component of
\mathcal{J}(f)
, then

is a Jordan region because
\mathcal{J}(f)
is a gasket. Since

is a limit of homeomorphisms, there exists a Jordan region

extends to a homeomorphism from the

to

extends to a homeomorphism of
\widehat{\mathbb{C}}
.

Since

has no Julia critical points, the topological conjugacy implies that
\tilde{g}
has no Julia critical points. Thus,
\tilde{g}
is hyperbolic. Since

is connected, there exists a hyperbolic post-critically finite rational map

and a homeomorphism of

which restricts to a topological conjugacy between the Julia set
\mathcal{J}(\tilde{g})
and

each edge of
\mathcal{H}
with the closure of the union of two internal rays connecting the corresponding centers of the Fatou components and the contact point.

Abusing notation, we shall not distinguish a finite subgraph of

from its canonical realization. Each component of

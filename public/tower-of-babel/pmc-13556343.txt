Identification and characterization of anti-chikungunya virus compounds using a biosafe toolkit
Authors: Yang Gao, Guojie Wang, Hanhua Zhang, Sicheng Tian, Yanqun Wang, Jincun Zhao, Xing Liu, Rong Ye, Rong Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractChikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a re-emerging mosquito-borne alphavirus for which no specific antiviral therapy is currently available. During the large outbreak in Foshan, Guangdong Province, China, in July 2025, CHIKV rapidly spread to neighboring regions and caused more than 16,000 confirmed cases. In this study, the predominant outbreak strain of CHIKV was selected as the reference sequence to establish a panel of complementary biosafe tools for antiviral compound screening and mechanistic investigation. A virus replicon particle (VRP) system for CHIKV was first constructed and applied to compound library screening, resulting in the identification of three candidate antiviral compounds: MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate, and anidulafungin. To further validate their antiviral activities and investigate their potential mechanisms, CHIKV functional evaluation systems were subsequently established, including vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV)- and murine leukemia virus (MLV)-based pseudovirus systems for viral entry, a replicon RNA system for post-entry replication-associated processes, a replication-defective nsP4 mutant replicon RNA system for primary translation, and a virus-like particle (VLP) system for viral particle assembly and budding assessment. Using these complementary systems, we systematically evaluated the antiviral profiles of the three candidate compounds across multiple stages of the CHIKV life cycle. This analysis revealed distinct stage-specific inhibitory patterns and provided insights into their potential antiviral mechanisms, which warrant validation using authentic CHIKV infection to assess their translational potential.Keywords: Chikungunya virus (CHIKV), Antiviral compound, Pseudovirus, Viral replicon particle, Replicon RNA

Introduction

Chikungunya virus (CHIKV) is a mosquito-borne arthritogenic alphavirus belonging to the genus Alphavirus in the family Togaviridae (Weaver and Lecuit, 2015). Adaptive mutations in the viral envelope proteins E1 and E2 have enhanced viral fitness in Aedes albopictus, facilitating transmission in both human populations and other mammalian hosts and increasing the risk of spillover transmission (Tsetsarkin et al., 2007; Cunha et al., 2020). Over the past two decades, CHIKV has re-emerged globally and caused repeated outbreaks across Africa, Asia, the Indian Ocean region, Europe, and the Americas, posing a substantial threat to public health (Powers and Logue, 2007; Martelossi-Cebinelli et al., 2025). Clinically, CHIKV infection is typically characterized by acute fever, rash, myalgia, and severe polyarthralgia, and a considerable proportion of patients may develop prolonged or recurrent musculoskeletal symptoms that persist beyond the acute phase (Suhrbier et al., 2012; Rudolph et al., 2014). The continued expansion of competent mosquito vectors, particularly Aedes albopictus, has further increased the risk of CHIKV transmission in subtropical and temperate regions (Vazeille et al., 2007; Tsetsarkin and Weaver, 2011). Combined with favorable climatic conditions in southern China, this further supports the potential for sustained local transmission and highlights the urgent need for antiviral strategies.

The 2025 chikungunya fever outbreak in Foshan, Guangdong Province, China caused more than 16,000 confirmed cases (Wang and Zhang, 2025), which emphasized the increasing risk of CHIKV local transmission in southern China and highlighted the need for virological tools for antiviral screening and mechanistic studies. Phylogenetic analysis classified the Foshan strain within the ECSA-2 lineage, and sequence comparison showed high similarity to the Réunion Island-associated strains (PV685691) (Sun et al., 2025; Liang et al., 2026). This finding provides important molecular clues for its potential transmission origin and evolutionary relationship. As ECSA-2 lineage viruses have been closely linked to recent CHIKV epidemics and vector adaptation in the past few years, the Foshan strain served as an appropriate reference strain for the construction of research tools for antiviral evaluation.

The requirement for biosafety level-3 facilities for CHIKV has significantly hindered fundamental and therapeutic studies, particularly for high-throughput antiviral screening. To overcome the biosafety concerns, a virus replicon particle (VRP) system based on the CHIKV Foshan strain was constructed as a bio-safer alternative. The system is restricted to a single round of infection, thereby minimizing transmission risk while preserving key characteristics of viral infection. Meanwhile, the VRP system also provides a convenient and practical approach for high-throughput antiviral screening. Through the primary antiviral screening based on VRP system, three candidate compounds, MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate, and anidulafungin, were identified.

In addition, the CHIKV life cycle consists of multiple sequential stages, including viral entry, primary translation of genomic RNA, RNA replication, virion assembly and budding, etc. To further explore the potential antiviral mechanisms of compounds, a panel of complementary CHIKV research systems was established, including vesicular stomatitis virus (VSV)- and murine leukemia virus (MLV)-based pseudovirus systems, a replicon RNA system, a replication-defective nsP4 mutant replicon RNA system and a virus-like particle (VLP) system. Current antiviral screening strategies for CHIKV and other alphaviruses mainly rely on infectious viruses, pseudotyped viruses, replicon systems, or virus-like particles (VLPs), each of which interrogates only a specific stage of the viral life cycle (Karlas et al., 2016; Lin et al., 2023; Wu et al., 2023). While these platforms have significantly advanced antiviral discovery, they generally lack the capacity for comprehensive stage-specific profiling within a unified experimental framework. Therefore, an integrated biosafe platform spanning multiple stages of the CHIKV life cycle would facilitate both antiviral screening and mechanistic characterization.

Application of these systems revealed distinct stage-specific antiviral profiles among the three compounds, with differential effects at the levels of viral entry, primary translation, replication, and assembly and budding processes. Building on these observations, we sought to systematically evaluate their antiviral activities across the CHIKV life cycle and to gain insights into their potential antiviral mechanisms.

Results

Screening of antiviral compounds against CHIKV using single-cycle virus replicon particles

To enable biosafe high-throughput antiviral screening, a virus replicon particle (VRP) system based on CHIKV strain FS2025 identified in Foshan, Guangdong Province, China, was constructed as a single-round infection platform, using a CMV-driven replicon plasmid expressing the reporter mGreenLantern (mGreen), with structural proteins supplied in trans (Fig. 1A).

The infectious units of CHIKV VRPs at indicated timepoints post-transfection were determined in HEK293T cells. Viral titers increased over time and reached a plateau at 36 h post-transfection, with a peak titer of approximately 8.4 × 105 IU/mL (Fig. 1B). An additional titration was performed in U-2 OS cells, yielding a similar infectious titer of 6.8 × 105 IU/mL (Fig. 1C). To evaluate the biosafety of the CHIKV VRP system and exclude the generation of replication-competent particles, serial passage experiments were performed in HEK293T cells. While robust fluorescence was observed following primary infection (P0), only sporadic fluorescent cells were detected after the first passage (P1), and no detectable fluorescence was observed after the second passage (P2), indicating that the VRPs are restricted to a single round of infection and do not support sustained propagation (Fig. 1D). Moreover, consistent infection was observed across multiple mammalian cell lines, including HEK293T, U-2 OS, and MEF cells (Fig. 1E).

CHIKV entry is known to depend on clathrin-mediated endocytosis and endosomal acidification, which are inhibited by chlorpromazine and ammonium chloride, respectively (Pohjala et al., 2011; Nuckols et al., 2014). Consistently, pretreatment of U-2 OS cells with either inhibitor at the indicated concentrations, under conditions that maintained high cell viability, significantly reduced VRP infection (Fig. 1F and G). In addition, MXRA8 (matrix remodeling-associated protein 8) has been identified as a key receptor for CHIKV entry (Zhang et al., 2018). VRP infection was markedly decreased in Mxra8-knockout MEF cells compared with wild-type controls (Fig. 1H), confirming receptor dependence. In parallel, ribavirin and favipiravir, known inhibitors of CHIKV RNA replication (Briolant et al., 2004; Delang et al., 2014), significantly reduced infection levels as measured by flow cytometry (Fig. 1I).

These results demonstrate that the VRP system effectively models receptor-dependent entry and intracellular RNA replication, and reliably recapitulates early stages of CHIKV infection, thereby serving as a versatile platform for mechanistic studies and antiviral evaluation.

To evaluate the applicability of CHIKV VRPs as a platform for high-throughput antiviral screening and to identify potential inhibitors against CHIKV infection, primary screening of U.S. Food and Drug Administration (FDA)-approved and metabolically active compound libraries was performed using a CHIKV VRP-based high-content assay in U-2 OS cells. Cells were pretreated with compounds for 1 h prior to infection with CHIKV VRPs for 24 h (Fig. 1J). Based on predefined hit criteria (> 180% normalized inhibition and > 90% cell viability) and further validation, three compounds—MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate, and anidulafungin—were selected for mechanistic characterization.

To further characterize the antiviral activity and cytotoxicity of three compounds, cells were pretreated with serial dilutions of each compound for 1 h prior to infection with CHIKV VRPs. At 24 h post-infection, the percentage of mGreen-positive cells was quantified by flow cytometry to determine the half-maximal effective concentration (EC50). Meanwhile, cell viability was measured in uninfected cells after 24 h of compound treatment to determine the half-maximal cytotoxic concentration (CC50). The selectivity index (SI) was also calculated based on the ratio of CC50 to EC50 (Fig. 1K–M). All three compounds exhibited antiviral activity against CHIKV VRP infection, indicating promising potential for further antiviral development. Among them, MDL-12330A showed the most potent antiviral effect with relatively low cytotoxicity (Fig. 1K).

Evaluation of antiviral effects on CHIKV entry using pseudoviral particles

Pseudovirus systems, consisting of a heterologous viral core bearing the glycoproteins of the virus of interest, provide a biosafe approach for specifically studying viral entry. CHIKV pseudoviruses based on murine leukemia virus (MLVpp) and vesicular stomatitis virus (VSVpp) were generated in HEK293T cells (Fig. 2A and B). The titers of pseudoviruses were determined to be 6.25 × 105 IU/mL for MLVpp and 1.41 × 106 IU/mL for VSVpp (Fig. 2C).

CHIKV pseudovirus infection was performed in multiple mammalian cell lines to assess the applicability and reliability. Both CHIKV MLVpp and VSVpp efficiently infected HEK293T, U-2 OS, and MEF cells, indicating broad susceptibility (Fig. 2D and E). Treatment with the entry inhibitors ammonium chloride and chlorpromazine markedly reduced infection (Fig. 2F and G). Additionally, Mxra8-knockout MEF cells showed significantly decreased infection compared with wild-type cells (Fig. 2H and I). These results indicate that the two pseudovirus systems reliably recapitulate CHIKV entry, providing a suitable platform to assess whether the candidate compounds identified in the VRP-based screening inhibit viral entry.

Cells were pretreated with MDL-12330A (10 and 20 μM), anidulafungin (3 and 6 μM), or bazedoxifene acetate (5 and 10 μM) for 1 h prior to infection with CHIKV MLVpp or VSVpp. After 24 h of infection, the efficiency was quantified by flow cytometry. MDL-12330A almost completely blocked pseudovirus infection in both systems, while anidulafungin and bazedoxifene acetate also significantly reduced infection efficiency to a lesser extent (Fig. 2J and L). These results indicate that all three compounds inhibit CHIKV entry, with MDL-12330A showing the strongest inhibitory effect.

To evaluate whether the antiviral activities of the identified compounds were specific to CHIKV envelope-mediated entry, VSV-G-packaged MLV and VSV were included as controls. Bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin exhibited little effect on infection at the tested concentrations, whereas MDL-12330A significantly reduced infection by both MLV and VSV (Fig. 2K and M). These results indicate that bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin preferentially inhibit CHIKV envelope-mediated entry, while the antiviral activity of MDL-12330A may involve a broader host pathway shared by multiple enveloped viruses.

Evaluation of antiviral effects on replication using replicon RNAs

To further determine whether the candidate compounds affect viral replication following entry, a CHIKV replicon RNA system was established. The replicon plasmid containing the SP6 promoter, viral UTRs, nsP1–4 and capsid protein coding sequence was linearized and transcribed in vitro, and the resulting RNA was transfected into cells. Viral replication was monitored by measuring NanoLuc luciferase activity (Fig. 3A). Robust replication was observed in HEK293T and U-2 OS cells, with the highest levels detected in HEK293T cells (Fig. 3B and C). Treatment with the known replication inhibitors ribavirin and favipiravir significantly reduced NanoLuc signals (Fig. 3D), confirming that the system provides a reliable readout of viral RNA replication.

Using this system, the effects of the candidate compounds were evaluated. MDL-12330A and anidulafungin markedly suppressed viral replication, reducing replication levels by over 20-fold, whereas bazedoxifene acetate exhibited a moderate inhibitory effect (Fig. 3E). Collectively, these results indicate that the CHIKV replicon RNA system provides a reliable platform for assessing viral replication and reveal that the candidate compounds, particularly MDL-12330A and anidulafungin, affect the viral replication stage.

Evaluation of antiviral effects on primary translation using replication-defective replicon RNAs

To distinguish whether the inhibitory effects of the candidate compounds on CHIKV replication arise from interference with primary translation or subsequent replication, a replication-defective replicon RNA system was engineered based on the above replicon RNA system. The NanoLuc reporter was fused with nsP3 and conserved catalytic motif 'GDD' within the nsP4 RNA-dependent RNA polymerase was mutated to 'GAA' to abolish replication, allowing only primary translation of the input replicon RNA (Sun et al., 2014). The NanoLuc reporter signal therefore reflects translation efficiency independent of RNA replication (Fig. 4A).

Transfection of the replication-defective replicon RNA into U-2 OS cells resulted in readily detectable NanoLuc activity compared with the control (Fig. 4B), confirming the functionality of the system in reporting primary translation. Meanwhile, the wild-type nsP4 replicon RNA without mutations produced strong NanoLuc signals after transfection (Fig. 4C), indicating that the fusion of NanoLuc reporter to the C-terminus of nsP3 is functionally compatible and does not impair replicon RNA function.

Using this system, MDL-12330A significantly reduced NanoLuc signals at early time points, indicating inhibition of primary translation of CHIKV genomic RNA (Fig. 4D). In contrast, anidulafungin and bazedoxifene acetate showed little to no effect on reporter activity (Fig. 4E and F), suggesting that their antiviral effects are not mediated through inhibition of primary translation. These results indicate that MDL-12330A affects both translation and replication, whereas anidulafungin and bazedoxifene acetate primarily affect post-translation steps, most likely RNA replication.

Evaluation of antiviral effects on virion assembly and egress using virus-like particles

During virion assembly, viral glycoproteins (e.g., E1 and E2) traffic to the cell surface. To exclude the possibility that extracellular viral protein release was affected by compound-induced cytotoxicity, cell viability was first assessed under the same conditions used for the VLP production assays. As a result, no significant decrease in cell viability was observed (Fig. 5B). We then determined the effects of candidate compounds by Western blotting. Compared with DMSO, bazedoxifene acetate markedly increased the amount of released E2 protein, whereas anidulafungin reduced E2 release and MDL-12330A showed the strongest inhibitory effect (Fig. 5C and D), suggesting distinct regulatory effects on late-stage viral protein release.

Consistent with the Western blotting results, SDS-PAGE analysis of purified VLPs further demonstrated that bazedoxifene acetate increased the abundance of the structural proteins E1, E2, and C in the supernatants. Notably, an overall increase in non-viral protein bands was also observed with bazedoxifene acetate treatment (Fig. 5E), suggesting that the enhanced release was not restricted to viral structural proteins.

To further characterize the effect of the compounds on CHIKV glycoprotein expression, surface and intracellular E1 and E2 proteins were quantified by flow cytometry. Compared with the DMSO control, anidulafungin did not significantly alter the proportion of E1- and E2-positive cells but modestly reduced the geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GMFI) of surface E1 and E2 staining without affecting intracellular GMFI. Meanwhile, MDL-12330A significantly decreased the percentages of E1- and E2-positive cells, while reducing the GMFI of surface staining and leaving intracellular GMFI largely unchanged. In contrast, bazedoxifene acetate decreased the percentages of E1- and E2-positive cells without substantially affecting the GMFI of either surface or intracellular staining (Fig. 5F–I).

Based on the predicted host pathways associated with the three identified compounds, H89, Brefeldin A, and U18666A were selected as pharmacological probes to further evaluate the involvement of cAMP signaling, membrane trafficking, and intracellular cholesterol transport, respectively (Lippincott-Schwartz et al., 1991; Cenedella, 2009; Cho et al., 2009). Cell viability analysis confirmed that the selected concentrations exhibited no obvious cytotoxicity (Fig. 5J). Under these conditions, all three compounds significantly inhibited CHIKV VRP infection compared with the DMSO control (Fig. 5K), supporting the importance of cAMP signaling, membrane trafficking, and intracellular cholesterol homeostasis during CHIKV infection.

Discussion

CHIKV is a mosquito-borne arthritogenic alphavirus that continues to pose a significant public health threat due to its expanding geographic distribution and enhanced vector adaptation. Despite advances in vaccine development, including live-attenuated, VLP-based, and mRNA platforms (Maure et al., 2024; McMahon et al., 2024; Richardson et al., 2025), progress in CHIKV-specific antiviral development has been comparatively limited, with most candidates remaining at the preclinical stage (Liu et al., 2026). Notably, the approval of the VLP-based vaccine Vimkunya by the U.S. FDA in 2025 emphasizes the importance of investigating CHIKV assembly and budding processes (Richardson et al., 2025).

In this study, through a VRP-based screening strategy, three compounds, MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin, were identified with distinct antiviral profiles across the CHIKV life cycle. To further delineate their antiviral activities, a panel of CHIKV toolkits, including VRP, pseudovirus, replicon RNA, replication-defective replicon RNA, and VLP systems, were employed to provide stage-resolved validation of antiviral activity. Compared with previously reported infectious virus-based or single-platform approaches, the integrated toolkit established in this study enables stage-resolved characterization of antiviral activities under biosafe conditions. All three compounds reduced infection in pseudovirus and VRP systems and showed inhibitory effects in the replicon RNA system, indicating that they can interfere with viral entry and RNA replication-related processes to varying extents. They may converge on host-dependent steps that are essential for efficient CHIKV infection.

Despite these similarities, MDL-12330A showed the strongest and broadest inhibitory effect among the compounds, with consistent antiviral activity also observed in the nsP4-defective replicon RNA and VLP systems. It suppressed primary translation and markedly reduced viral protein release. Flow cytometric analysis revealed a significant reduction in the proportion of E1- and E2-positive cells at the cell surface, while the levels of intracellular E1 and E2 remained largely unchanged. This suggests that MDL-12330A does not substantially affect viral structural protein expression, but may influence their surface presentation, suggesting a multi-stage antiviral effect spanning early and late phases of CHIKV infection.

As compared to bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin, MDL-12330A also inhibited infection mediated by VSV-G-packaged MLV and VSV, suggesting that its antiviral activity is not restricted to CHIKV but may result from modulation of a host signaling pathway required for the entry of multiple enveloped viruses. This broader inhibitory profile is consistent with the reported function of MDL-12330A as an adenylyl cyclase inhibitor. Together with the known involvement of cAMP-related signaling in regulating cellular processes relevant to membrane dynamics and endocytic trafficking, these findings suggest that cAMP-associated host signaling may contribute more generally to viral entry efficiency (Grupp et al., 1980; Zaccolo et al., 2021; Treffers et al., 2023). Consistently, H89, a pharmacological inhibitor of PKA downstream of cAMP signaling (Cho et al., 2009), also reduced CHIKV VRP infection, providing additional support for the possible involvement of cAMP-related signaling pathways. In addition, MDL-12330A has been reported to affect voltage-dependent K+ channels and intracellular Ca2+ homeostasis. Alterations in ion channel activity and cellular electrochemical balance may further influence membrane fusion and vesicular transport, which are critical for viral entry and particle release (Li et al., 2013). These additional effects may contribute to its broad antiviral phenotype observed across different stages of the viral life cycle.

In contrast, the antiviral activity of bazedoxifene acetate was more limited and appeared to vary across different systems. It exhibited weaker effects on viral entry and replication and minimal impact on primary translation. Notably, a distinct phenotype was observed, characterized by a reduced proportion of surface and intracellular E1- and E2-positive cells without a noticeable change in GMFI, accompanied by a significant increase in release of viral particle-related structural proteins. These observations, together with the detection of increasing host protein release, suggest that bazedoxifene acetate may alter the secretion dynamics of intracellular proteins into the extracellular environment, rather than directly affecting protein expression levels.

Bazedoxifene acetate is a selective estrogen receptor modulator (SERM) that has been reported to alter cholesterol distribution, endosomal maturation and host lipid metabolism-related functions, all of which are critical for alphavirus infection (Miao et al., 2022; Yan et al., 2024). CHIKV extensively remodel host membranes to form specialized structures that support different stages of the viral life cycle. During viral RNA replication, membrane invaginations known as spherules was formed, which serve as discrete replication compartments (Hellström et al., 2017; Martin et al., 2024). In parallel, viral structural protein assembly and budding occur at cholesterol-enriched regions of the plasma membrane, where glycoproteins are selectively concentrated to facilitate particle formation (Chmielewski et al., 2022). These membrane-associated structures represent functional microdomains that are highly dependent on cholesterol homeostasis and intracellular membrane organization. Therefore, perturbations in cholesterol homeostasis may not only affect viral entry or replication, but also influence the localization, assembly, and release dynamics of viral structural components. In this context, the phenotypic effects of bazedoxifene acetate may be due to altered membrane organization and cholesterol-dependent protein partitioning. This notion is further supported by the use of U18666A, a well-characterized inhibitor of intracellular cholesterol transport (Cenedella, 2009). As expected, U18666A treatment significantly reduced CHIKV VRP infection.

Similarly, anidulafungin showed minimal impact on primary translation but exhibited inhibitory effects on viral entry and RNA replication. In the VLP system, anidulafungin had little effect on E1 and E2 surface and intracellular expression but reduced release of viral particle-related E2 protein, suggesting an inhibitory effect on viral assembly and budding. Interestingly, a modest reduction in surface-associated E1 and E2 GMFI was observed, whereas intracellular GMFI remained unchanged. This suggests that anidulafungin may influence the presentation or membrane-associated distribution of viral glycoproteins at the cell surface without affecting overall intracellular protein abundance.

Anidulafungin is an echinocandin antifungal agent that inhibits β-1,3-D-glucan synthesis (Shen et al., 2022). Although this pathway is absent in mammalian cells, echinocandins, including anidulafungin, have been reported to exert antiviral effects against multiple enveloped viruses, including SFTSV, CCHFV, and ZIKV (Shen et al., 2022), potentially through modulation of host membrane organization and lipid environment (Ho et al., 2018). As mentioned earlier, alphavirus RNA replication, assembly and budding rely on coordinated interactions between viral proteins and host membranes, and such alterations may impair the formation or function of membrane-associated assembly sites. Therefore, the concurrent inhibition of RNA replication and particle release observed here suggests that anidulafungin may interfere with membrane-dependent processes required for both replication complex function and efficient virion assembly, rather than directly affecting viral protein expression or primary translation.

Although bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin both appear to affect membrane-related processes, they exhibited distinct phenotypes in the VLP system. Echinocandin-class compounds, of which anidulafungin is a member, have been reported to influence vesicle trafficking-related pathways and extracellular vesicle production (Szymański et al., 2022; Alves et al., 2026). While these observations have primarily been described in non-viral contexts, they are consistent with a potential impact of anidulafungin on host membrane trafficking pathways that may also contribute to viral glycoprotein processing and surface presentation. To confirm this, we employed Brefeldin A, a well-characterized inhibitor of ER-to-Golgi transport (Lippincott-Schwartz et al., 1991), which exhibits significant inhibition of CHIKV VRP infection.

Collectively, these findings support a model in which host membrane organization plays a role in CHIKV infection, with distinct compounds interfering with viral entry, primary translation, replication, and assembly. Although the complementary biosafe platforms enabled stage-specific evaluation of antiviral activity, validation of the identified compounds using authentic CHIKV in cell culture and animal models will be required in future studies to assess their translational potential.

Conclusions

Through antiviral compound screening and systematic validation, this study identified three candidate compounds with anti-CHIKV activity: MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate and anidulafungin. These compounds exhibited stage-specific inhibitory effects across viral entry, primary translation, replication, and particle assembly, highlighting diverse antiviral mechanisms targeting the CHIKV life cycle. These findings provide a basis for further mechanistic characterization and development of host-targeted antiviral strategies.

Materials and methods

Cell lines

HEK293T (ATCC #CRL-3216), U-2 OS, MEF (Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, Shanghai, China) cells were cultured in DMEM (Hyclone) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS), 10 mM HEPES, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 1 × nonessential amino acids, and 100 U/mL of Penicillin-Streptomycin. Mxra8-knockout MEF cells were generated by delivering CRISPR/Cas9-based RNP complex (sgRNA, 5′-CTTGTGGATATGTATTCGGC-3′). Clonal knockout cell line was established by limiting dilution. All cell lines were tested routinely and confirmed to be free of mycoplasma contamination.

Generation of CHIKV VRPs

Total RNA was extracted from serum from a CHIKV-infected patient in Foshan, Guangdong, China, and used for cDNA synthesis. Reverse transcription was performed using the Takara PrimeScript 1st Strand cDNA Synthesis Kit (Takara#6210A). The resulting cDNA was used for genome amplification and sequencing. The 5′ untranslated region (5′UTR), nsP1–4, the 26S subgenomic promoter, and the capsid coding sequence fused to mGreenLantern (mGreen), together with the 3′ UTR, were cloned into the pBluescript SK(+) vector and flanked by a CMV promoter and an HDV ribozyme, generating pCHIK-FS2025-nsP-mGreen. The envelope protein (E3 through E1) coding sequences were cloned into the pCAGGS expression vector to generate pC-CHIK-FS2025-Env.

VRPs were produced by co-transfecting HEK293T cells with pCHIK-FS2025-nsP-mGreen and pC-CHIK-FS2025-Env plasmids. Supernatants were collected at 48 h post-transfection, clarified by centrifugation, aliquoted, and stored at −80 °C. For titration, HEK293T cells in 96-well plates were infected with serial dilutions of VRPs. At 24 h post-infection, cells were trypsinized and fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde (PFA). The percentage of mGreen-positive cells was quantified by flow cytometry. The infectious units (IU) of VRPs were calculated using the formula: (percentage of mGreen-positive cells) × (total cell number)/(inoculation volume).

For entry inhibition assays, cells were pretreated with ammonium chloride or chlorpromazine for 1 h prior to infection. For replication inhibition, favipiravir and ribavirin were added at the time of infection. At 24 h post-infection, cells were harvested, fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde, and mGreen-positive cells were quantified by flow cytometry. Infection rates were normalized to DMSO-treated controls.

Screening of antiviral compounds

A total of 8446 compounds were screened, including FDA-approved and metabolically active compound libraries (MedChemExpress, HY-L022 and HY-L012). U-2 OS cells were seeded in 96-well plates and incubated overnight prior to compound treatment. Compounds were added at a final concentration of 5 μM. Umifenovir hydrochloride (HY-14904A, 5 μM) was used as a positive control, and DMSO was used as a negative control. Cells were subsequently infected with CHIKV VRPs at an MOI of 3 for 24 h in the presence of the compounds. Following infection, cells were fixed with 2% PFA for 1 h and stained with DAPI (Beyotime). mGreen signal and DAPI signal were quantified using an Operetta high-content imaging system (PerkinElmer) and Harmony 3.5 software. Viral infection and cell viability were quantified based on mGreen intensity and DAPI staining respectively. Viral infection inhibition was calculated as a normalized inhibition using positive (umifenovir) and negative (DMSO) controls:

Compounds with normalized inhibition > 180% (i.e., >1.8-fold of positive control) were selected as preliminary hits. These compounds were further filtered based on cell viability (> 90%), calculated as described below: Cellviability(%)=DAPIdrugDAPInegative×100%

The compounds meeting both criteria were defined as primary hits. After validation, three representative compounds (MDL-12330A, bazedoxifene acetate, and anidulafungin) were selected for further mechanistic characterization based on their antiviral potency and cytotoxicity profiles.

Validation of antiviral activity of compounds

Cells were seeded in 96-well plates and then pretreated with serial dilutions of compounds for 1 h prior to infection with CHIKV VRPs at an MOI of 1 for 24 h. Cells were trypsinized, fixed with 2% PFA, and analyzed by flow cytometry to determine the infection. DMSO was used as a negative control, and infection rates were normalized to the DMSO control.

Cell viability assay

U-2 OS cells were seeded in 96-well plates and then treated with varying concentrations of candidate compounds for 24 h. Cell viability was assessed using the CellTiter-Lumi Steady kit (Beyotime) according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Packaging of MLV- and VSV-based CHIKV pseudoviruses

HEK293T cells were seeded in 6-well plates. For CHIKV MLVpp production, cells were co-transfected with pC-CHIK-FS2025-Env, the MLV gag/pol and the pMIG-GFP for 48 h (Zhu et al., 2021). For CHIKV VSVpp production, cells were first transfected with pC-CHIK-FS2025-Env for 24 h, followed by infection with scVSVΔG-Nluc-GFP for 2 h (Ma et al., 2025). After extensive washing, cells were further incubated for 24 h. Supernatants were collected, clarified by centrifugation, aliquoted, and stored at −80 °C. Viral titers were determined by infecting HEK293T cells with serial dilutions of supernatants, followed by quantification of GFP-positive cells. For entry inhibition assays, cells were pretreated with ammonium chloride, chlorpromazine, or candidate compounds for 1 h prior to infection. At 24 h post-infection, cells were harvested, fixed with 2% paraformaldehyde, and GFP-positive cells were quantified by flow cytometry. Infection rates were normalized to DMSO-treated controls.

Construction of CHIKV replicon RNA system

The CMV promoter and mGreen reporter in plasmid pCHIK-FS2025-nsP-mGreen were replaced with SP6 promoter and NanoLuc luciferase, respectively, resulting in pCHIK-FS2025-Replicon. Replicon mRNA was transcribed in vitro using the mMESSAGE mMACHINE SP6 kit and subsequently purified, quantified, and stored at −80 °C. Cells were transfected with replicon RNA using TransIT-mRNA reagent. At the indicated timepoints post-transfection, cells were lysed and NanoLuc luciferase activity was measured using the Nano-Light Luciferase Reporter Assay Kit. Favipiravir, ribavirin, or candidate compounds were supplemented during the transfection to assess the inhibitory effect on viral replication. NanoLuc signals were normalized to DMSO-treated controls.

Construction of replication-defective replicon RNA system

The NanoLuc luciferase reporter was inserted into the hypervariable region of nsP3 in plasmid pCHIK-FS2025-Replicon, while the capsid coding sequence and the NanoLuc gene downstream of the 26S subgenomic promoter were removed. To further generate the replication-defective replicon RNA, the ‘GDD’ catalytic motif of nsP4 was mutated to ‘GAA’ to abolish RNA-dependent RNA polymerase activity. Replication-defective replicon RNAs were transcribed in vitro and transfected into cells. At the indicated timepoints post-transfection, cells were lysed and NanoLuc activity was measured. Candidate compounds were applied during transfection to determine the effect on primary translation.

Construction of VLP system

The capsid and envelope protein (E3 through E1) coding sequences were cloned into pCAGGS transient expression vector to generate pC-CHIK-FS2025-CEnv. Cells were transfected with the plasmid using TransIT-LT1 reagent. At 12 h post-transfection, cells were treated with candidate compounds at indicated concentrations. After an additional 24 h incubation, culture supernatants were collected, clarified by centrifugation, and concentrated using ultrafiltration. E2 protein levels in the supernatant were measured by Western blotting as a readout of VLP production. To detect VLP production by SDS-PAGE, cell supernatants after transfection were concentrated by ultracentrifugation through a 30% (w/v) sucrose cushion, followed by ultracentrifugation on a 20–60% (w/v) sucrose density gradient. The virus band was extracted from the gradient and further concentrated using an Amicon Ultra-4100-kDa cut-off centrifugal concentrator (Millipore).

To detect the expression of VLP-related structural proteins, cells after plasmid transfection were harvested with trypLE (Thermo) and stained for surface and intracellular E1 and E2 expression, followed by flow cytometry to determine the proportion of E1-and E2-positive cells and their geometric mean fluorescence intensity (GMFI). Data were normalized to DMSO-treated controls. The mouse monoclonal antibodies CHK-124 (anti-E2) and CHK-166 (anti-E1) were used for flow cytometric analysis. Alexa Fluor 647-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (Thermo #A21235) was used for detection.

Immunoblotting

Culture supernatants containing VLPs were collected, clarified by centrifugation, and concentrated using ultrafiltration. Samples were mixed with SDS loading buffer and subjected to SDS-PAGE, followed by transfer onto PVDF membranes. Membranes were blocked with 5% milk and incubated overnight at 4 °C with an in-house mouse monoclonal antibody against CHIKV E2 protein. After washing, membranes were incubated with horseradishperoxidase (HRP)-conjugated goat anti-mouse IgG secondary antibody (Thermo #31430). Protein bands were visualized using SuperSignal West Pico substrate according to the manufacturer's instructions (Thermo), and band intensities were quantified using ImageJ software.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism 10 software. Differences between groups were evaluated using one-way ANOVA or Student's t-test. Statistical significance was defined as P < 0.05 (∗), P < 0.01 (∗∗), P < 0.001 (∗∗∗), and P < 0.0001 (∗∗∗∗), while ns indicates no significant difference.

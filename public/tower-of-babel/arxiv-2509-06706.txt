Contour Integrations and Parity Results of Cyclotomic Euler T T -Sums and Multiple t t -Values
Zhenlu Wang and Ce Xu[1mm] a. School of Artificial Intelligence, Taizhou University,Taizhou 318000, Zhejiang, P.R. Chinab. School of Mathematics and Statistics, Anhui Normal University,Wuhu 241002, Anhui, P.R. China[5mm] Dedicated to Professor Masanobu Kaneko on the occasion of his 65th birthday

Abstract




Abstract.
We will employ the method of contour integration to investigate the parity results of non-embedded cyclotomic multiple
t
-values, which we refer to as cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums. We can provide explicit parity formulas for the linear and quadratic cases of cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums, as well as state a parity theorem for the general case. We also present illustrative examples and corollaries. From this, some parity results for classical cyclotomic multiple
t
-values can be derived. Furthermore, we present several general formulas for cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums with denominators involving arbitrary rational polynomials through residue computations. By evaluating these polynomials and computing residues, many other formulas analogous to cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums can be derived. In particular, we also obtain certain parity results for the cyclotomic versions of multiple
T
-values as defined by Kaneko and Tsumura. Finally, we propose some conjectures and questions regarding the parity of cyclotomic multiple
t
-values and cyclotomic multiple
T
-values.

Keywords
: Contour integration; Cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums; Residue theorem; Parity result; Cyclotomic Multiple
t
-values; Cyclotomic Multiple
T
-values.

11M32, 11M99.

Introduction

In 1998, Flajolet and Salvy
[
4
]
systematically investigated the parity of Dirichlet-type series with numerators being products of harmonic numbers using the method of contour integration. These series are now referred to as
classical Euler sums
and are defined in the following form:

\displaystyle{S_{{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{k}},q}}:=\displaystyle\sum\limits_{n=1}^{\infty}{\displaystyle\frac{{H_{n}^{\left({{p_{1}}}\right)}H_{n}^{\left({{p_{2}}}\right)}\cdots H_{n}^{\left({{p_{k}}}\right)}}}{{{n^{q}}}}},

where
p_{j}\in\mathbb{N}\ (j=1,2,\ldots,k)
and
q\geq 2
. When
k=1
and let
p_{1}=p
,
S_{p,q}
is called a
linear Euler sum
(now also known as a
double zeta-star value
), and when
k>1
, it is referred to as a
nonlinear Euler sum
. The quantity
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}+q
is called the “weight” of the sum, and the quantity
k
is called the
“order”.
H_{n}^{(p)}
stands the
generalized harmonic number
of order
p
defined by

H_{n}^{(p)}:=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{1}{k^{p}}\quad\text{and}\quad H_{n}^{(1)}\equiv H_{n}.

The well-known
parity theorem
of Euler sums they proved states that:
A Euler sum
S_{p_{1}\cdots p_{k},q}
with
k\geq 2
reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders whenever the weight
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}+q
and the order
k
are of the same parity
. The primary method they employed to prove this theorem involved constructing contour integrals incorporating trigonometric functions, the digamma function, and rational functions, followed by evaluating all residue contributions to complete the proof.
As remarked by Flajolet and Salvy, every Euler sum of weight
w
and degree
k

is a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of multiple zeta values (MZVs) of weight
w
and depth at most
k+1
. For explicit formula, the readers may consult the Xu-Wang’s paper
[
20
]
. The
multiple zeta values
(MZVs) are defined by

\displaystyle\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})\equiv\zeta(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\displaystyle\frac{1}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\cdots n_{r}^{k_{r}}}\in\mathbb{R},

where
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}
are positive integers and
k_{r}\geq 2
(i.e.
admissible
). Here
r
and
k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}
are called the
depth
and
weight
, respectively. The systematic study of MZVs began in the early 1990s with the works of Hoffman
[
5
]
and Zagier
[
24
]
. Due to their surprising and sometimes mysterious appearance in the study of many branches of mathematics and theoretical physics, these special values have attracted a lot of attention and interest in the past three decades (for example, see Zhao’s monograph
[
29
]
, which documents nearly all important research results on multiple zeta values discovered prior to 2016).

In
[
21
]
and
[
22
]
, Xu and Wang extended Flajolet and Salvy’s contour integral method to investigate the following two classes of sums, known as (alternating) Euler
T
-sums and (alternating) Euler
\tilde{S}
-sums, respectively:

\displaystyle T_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k},q}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k},\sigma}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{n-1}\displaystyle\frac{h_{n-1}^{(p_{1})}(\sigma_{1})h_{n-1}^{(p_{2})}(\sigma_{2})\cdots h_{n-1}^{(p_{k})}(\sigma_{k})}{(n-1/2)^{q}}\,,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k},q}^{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k},\sigma}=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sigma^{n-1}\displaystyle\frac{h_{n}^{(p_{1})}(\sigma_{1})h_{n}^{(p_{2})}(\sigma_{2})\cdots h_{n}^{(p_{k})}(\sigma_{k})}{n^{q}}\,,

(1.2)

where
(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{k},q)\in\mathbb{N}^{k+1}
and
(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k},\sigma)\in\{\pm 1\}^{k+1}
with
(q,\sigma)\neq(1,1)
. The
h_{n}^{(p)}(\sigma)
denotes the (alternating) odd harmonic number defined by

\displaystyle h_{n}^{(p)}(\sigma):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{\sigma^{k}}{(k-1/2)^{p}}.

They established parity results for (alternating) Euler
T
-sums and (alternating) Euler
\widetilde{S}
-sums (see
[
22
, Thms. 54 and 55]
) by defining a new digamma function and constructing associated contour integrals. In particular, they derived explicit formulas for double and triple (alternating) Euler
T
-sums and
\widetilde{S}
-sums. By exploring their relationships with Hoffman’s multiple
t
-values and Kaneko-Tsumura’s multiple
T
-values, they further obtained parity formulas for double and triple (alternating)
t
-values and
T
-values (see
[
22
, Thms. 40 and 52]
). For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
and
(k_{r},\sigma_{r})\neq(1,1)
, the (alternating)
multiple
t
-values
(MtVs) are defined by (
[
7
,

\displaystyle t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{r}^{n_{r}}}{(n_{1}-1/2)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}-1/2)^{k_{r}}}.

(1.3)

For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}:=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}:=(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
and
(k_{r},\sigma_{r})\neq(1,1)

\displaystyle T({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sigma}}):=2^{r}\displaystyle\sum\limits_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\displaystyle\frac{\sigma_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{r}^{n_{r}}}{(2n_{1}-1)^{k_{1}}(2n_{2}-2)^{k_{2}}\cdots(2n_{r}-r)^{k_{r}}}.

(1.4)

In particular,
t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}})=t({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};\{1\}_{r})
and

Very recently, Rui and Xu
[
18
]
established parity results for cyclotomic Euler sums by constructing extended trigonometric functions and digamma functions. By considering contour integrals involving these functions, they further derived some explicit formulas related to multiple polylogarithms. The
cyclotomic Euler sum
is defined by

\displaystyle S_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k};q}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k};x):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{\zeta_{n}(p_{1};x_{1})\zeta_{n}(p_{2};x_{2})\cdots\zeta_{n}(p_{k};x_{k})}{n^{q}}x^{n},

(1.5)

where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k},q\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x
are all roots of unity with
(q,x)\neq(1,1)
. In particular, if
k=0
, we denote
S_{\emptyset;q}(\emptyset;x):=\Li_{q}(x)
. Here
\zeta_{n}(p;x)
stands the finite sum of polylogarithm function defined by

\displaystyle\zeta_{n}(p;x):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x^{k}}{k^{p}}\quad(p\in\mathbb{N},\ |x|\leq 1),

(1.6)

and the
polylogarithm function

\Li_{p}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{p}(x):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\zeta_{n}(p;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{n^{p}}\quad(|x|\leq 1,\ (p,x)\neq(1,1),\ p\in\mathbb{N}).

(1.7)

For any
(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the classical
multiple polylogarithm function
with
r
-variables is defined by

\displaystyle\Li_{k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r}}(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\dotsm x_{r}^{n_{r}}}{n_{1}^{k_{1}}\dotsm n_{r}^{k_{r}}}

(1.8)

which converges if
|x_{j}\cdots x_{r}|<1
for all
j=1,\dotsc,r
. It can be analytically continued to a multi-valued meromorphic function on
\mathbb{C}^{r}
(see
[
25
]
). In particular, if
(k_{1},\dotsc,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
are
N
th roots of unity, we call them
cyclotomic multiple zeta values of level
N
which converges if
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
(see
[
23
]
and
[
29
, Ch. 15]
). Zhao
[
26
]
proposed a basis conjecture for level 3 and level 4 cyclotomic multiple zeta values. Li
[
11
]
partially proved this conjecture using motivic theory. And the level 2 cyclotomic multiple zeta values are called
alternating multiple zeta values
(AMZVs) (see
[
1
,
28
]
etc), generally denoted by the symbol
\zeta({\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}};{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}):=\Li_{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}})
for
(x_{1},\dotsc,x_{r})\in\{\pm 1\}^{r}
and
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
.

In this paper, we define the non-embedded cyclotomic multiple
t
-values–
cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums
of the following form:

\displaystyle T_{p_{1},\ldots,p_{k};q}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k};x):=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{t_{n}(p_{1};x_{1})t_{n}(p_{2};x_{2})\cdots t_{n}(p_{k};x_{k})}{(n-1/2)^{q}}x^{n},

(1.9)

where
p_{1},\ldots,p_{k},q\in\mathbb{N}
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k},x
are all roots of unity with
(q,x)\neq(1,1)
. Similar to classical Euler sums, we refer to the quantity
p_{1}+\cdots+p_{k}+q
as the “weight” of the sum, and the quantity
k
as the “order”. If
k=0
, we denote
T_{\emptyset;q}(\emptyset;x):=\ti_{q}(x)
.
Here
t_{n}(p;x)
denotes the finite sum of
t
-polylogarithm function defined by

\displaystyle t_{n}(p;x):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}\displaystyle\frac{x^{k}}{(k-1/2)^{p}},

(1.10)

and the
t
-polylogarithm function
\ti_{p}(x)
is defined by

\displaystyle\ti_{p}(x):=\displaystyle\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}t_{n}(p;x)=\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{(n-1/2)^{p}}\quad(|x|\leq 1,\ (p,x)\neq(1,1),\ p\in\mathbb{N}).

(1.11)

In this paper, we employ the methods developed by Rui and Xu to investigate parity results for cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums, and consequently establish certain parity conclusions regarding cyclotomic multiple
t
-values. For
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
(all
x_{j}
are
N
-th roots of unity) with
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
, the
cyclotomic multiple
t
-value of level
N

\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
is defined by

\displaystyle\ti_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}({\boldsymbol{\sl{x}}}):=\displaystyle\sum_{0<n_{1}<\cdots<n_{r}}\displaystyle\frac{x_{1}^{n_{1}}\cdots x_{r}^{n_{r}}}{(n_{1}-1/2)^{k_{1}}\cdots(n_{r}-1/2)^{k_{r}}}.

(1.12)

They are called
cyclotomic multiple
t
-values
if
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
are any set of roots of unity.
It is evident that the above multiple series also converges for
|x_{j}\cdots x_{r}|<1\ (j=1,2,\ldots,r)
, in which case we call the series a
multiple
t
-polylogarithm function
.
Obviously, by applying the stuffle relations (see
[
6
]
), it can be shown that cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums can be expressed as
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficient linear combinations of cyclotomic multiple
t
-values. As an example, we have

\displaystyle T_{p_{1},p_{2};q}(x_{1},x_{2};x)

\displaystyle=\ti_{p_{1},p_{2},q}(x_{1},x_{2},x)+\ti_{p_{2},p_{1},q}(x_{2},x_{1},x)+\ti_{p_{1}+p_{2},q}(x_{1}x_{2},x)

\displaystyle\quad+\ti_{p_{1},p_{2}+q}(x_{1},x_{2}x)+\ti_{p_{2},p_{1}+q}(x_{2},x_{1}x)+\ti_{p_{1}+p_{2}+q}(x_{1}x_{2}x).

The main result of this paper is to establish the following parity theorem for cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums (see Theorem
5.1
).

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1
with
(p_{j},x_{j})
and
(q,x)\neq(1,1)
. Then

\displaystyle T_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};x\Big)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q+r-1}(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})T_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};x^{-1}\Big)\quad(\text{mod products}),

where the “mod products” means discarding all product terms of cyclotomic Euler sums and cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums with order less than
r
.

Further, based on our calculations and observations, we propose the following parity conjecture concerning cyclotomic multiple
t
-values:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
r>1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 1
with
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:z^{N}=1\}
, then

\displaystyle\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}\Big)\quad(\text{mod products}),

where the “mod products” means discarding all product terms of cyclotomic multiple zeta values and cyclotomic multiple
t
-values (which must appear) with depth less than
r
and level less than or equal to
N
.

In particular, Corollaries
3.5
and
4.3
partially confirm the correctness of this Conjecture
1.2
.
It should be emphasized that Panzer
[
17
, Thm 1.3]
proved the following parity properties of multiple polylogarithms: for all
r\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}^{r}
, the function

\displaystyle\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}\Li_{{\boldsymbol{\sl{k}}}}(1/z_{1},1/z_{2},\ldots,1/z_{r})

is of depth at most
r-1
. Here
(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}^{r}\setminus\bigcup_{1\leq i\leq j\leq r}\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}):z_{i}z_{i+1}\cdots z_{j}\in[0,+\infty)\}
. It is also worth noting that Panzer’s paper does not provide a general formula for the parity of multiple polylogarithms, while recently Hirose
[
8
]
and Umezawa
[
19
]
have respectively given explicit formulas for the parity of multiple zeta values and the parity of multiple polylogarithms.
Utilizing Panzer’s parity result, a weakened form of Conjecture
1.2
can be presented:

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
r>1
and
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}\geq 1
with
(k_{r},x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. If
x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:z^{N}=1\}
, then

\displaystyle\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}\Big)

can be expressed in terms of a
\mathbb{Q}
-linear combination of cyclotomic multiple zeta values with depth less than
r
and level less than or equal to
2N
.

Proof.

According to the definition, it is not difficult to observe that cyclotomic multiple
t
-values can be expressed as
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficient linear combinations of cyclotomic multiple zeta values as follows:

\displaystyle\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})=2^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}-r}\sqrt{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\in\{\pm 1\}}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{r}\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(\sigma_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\sqrt{x_{r}}).

Hence, we obtain

\displaystyle\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})\ti_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle=2^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}-r}\sqrt{x_{1}\cdots x_{r}}\displaystyle\sum_{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\in\{\pm 1\}}\sigma_{1}\cdots\sigma_{r}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\quad\times\left(\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}(\sigma_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}},\ldots,\sigma_{r}\sqrt{x_{r}})-(-1)^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r}+r}\Li_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{r}}\left(\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{1}\sqrt{x_{1}}},\ldots,\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sigma_{r}\sqrt{x_{r}}}\right)\right).

Then, by further utilizing Panzer’s parity theorem regarding cyclotomic multiple polylogarithms, the theorem can be proven.
∎

The structure of this paper is as follows: In Section 2, we present a residue lemma and the form of the contour integrals to be considered in this paper, as well as the Laurent or Taylor expansions of the integrand in the contour integrals at integer or half-integer points. In Sections 3 and 4, by examining specific contour integrals and computing residues, we derive explicit formulas for the parity of cyclotomic linear and quadratic Euler
T

T

Preliminaries

Flajolet and Salvy
[
4
]
defined a kernel function
\xi(s)
with two requirements: 1).
\xi(s)
is meromorphic in the whole complex plane. 2).
\xi(s)
satisfies
\xi(s)=o(s)
over an infinite collection of circles
\left|s\right|={\rho_{k}}
with
{\rho_{k}}\to\infty
. Applying these two conditions of kernel
function
\xi(s)
, Flajolet and Salvy discovered the following residue lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

(cf.
[
4
]
)

Let
\xi(s)
be a kernel function and let
r(s)
be a rational function which is
O(s^{-2})
at infinity. Then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\alpha\in O}{{\mathop{\rm Res}}{{\left({r(s)\xi(s)},\alpha\right)}}}+\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\beta\in S}{{\mathop{\rm Res}}{{\left({r(s)\xi(s)},\beta\right)}}}=0,

(2.13)

where
S
is the set of poles of
r(s)
and
O
is the set of poles of
\xi(s)
that are not poles
r(s)
. Here
{\mathop{\rm Re}\nolimits}s{\left({r(s)},\alpha\right)}
denotes the residue of
r(s)
at
s=\alpha.

Notably, Lemma
2.1
also holds under the weaker condition
r(s)\xi(s)=o(s^{-1})
.

In
[
18
]
, Rui and Xu defined the
extended trigonometric function

\phi(s;x)
and
generalized digamma function

\Phi(s;x)
as follows:

\displaystyle\phi(s;x):=\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\displaystyle\frac{x^{k}}{k+s}\quad(s\notin\mathbb{N}_{0}^{-}:=\{0,-1,-2,-3,\ldots\}),

(2.14)

where
x
is an arbitrary complex number with
|x|\leq 1
and
x\neq 1
, and

\Phi(s;x):=\phi(s;x)-\phi\Big(-s;x^{-1}\Big)-\displaystyle\frac{1}{s},

where to ensure the convergence of the series above,
x
can only be any root of unity. Clearly,
\phi(s;x)=o(1)
and
\Phi(s;x)=o(1)
if
|s|\rightarrow\infty
. In fact, this
\phi(s;x)
function is a special case of the classical Lerch Zeta Function, and in a recent paper
[
15
]
, Vicente and Holgado have studied the Lerch-type zeta function of a recurrence sequence of arbitrary degree.

Rui and Xu provided the Laurent expansions or Maclaurin expansions of functions
\phi(s;x)
and
\Phi(s;x)
at integer points.

Lemma 2.2
.

(
[
18
]
) For
p\in\mathbb{N}
, if
|s+n|<1\ (n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\})
, then

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{(p-1)}(s;x)}{(p-1)!}(-1)^{p-1}

\displaystyle=x^{n}\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\binom{k+p-1}{p-1}\left((-1)^{k}\Li_{k+p}(x)+(-1)^{p}\zeta_{n}\Big(k+p;x^{-1}\Big)\right)(s+n)^{k}

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\frac{x^{n}}{(s+n)^{p}}\qquad(|s+n|<1,\ n\geq 0)

(2.15)

and

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{\phi^{(p-1)}(s;x)}{(p-1)!}(-1)^{p-1}

Theorem 3.1

Let

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is based on residue calculations of the following contour integral:

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p,q}(x,y;s),-n\right)=(-1)^{q}\displaystyle\frac{(xy)^{n}}{(n-{1/2})^{q}}\left(\ti_{p}(y)y^{-1}+(-1)^{p}t_{n}\Big(p;y^{-1}\Big)\right)\quad(n\geq 1),

5
Parity Results of Generalized Cyclotomic Euler
T
-Sums

In this section, we utilize the method of contour integration to present parity results and several examples for cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums of arbitrary order.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
x,x_{1},\ldots,x_{r}
be roots of unity, and
p_{1},\ldots,p_{r},q\geq 1
with
(p_{j},x_{j})
and
(q,xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})\neq(1,1)
. The

\displaystyle xT_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q+r}T_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

reduces to a combination of sums of lower orders (It should be emphasized that the lower-order sums include not only cyclotomic Euler
T
-sums but also cyclotomic Euler sums).

Proof.

The proof of this theorem is based on residue calculations of the following contour integral:

\displaystyle\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(x,x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r};s)ds

\displaystyle:=\oint\limits_{\left(\infty\right)}\displaystyle\frac{\Phi(s;x)\phi^{(p_{1}-1)}(s+1/2;x_{1})\cdots\phi^{(p_{r}-1)}(s+1/2;x_{r})}{(p_{1}-1)!\cdots(p_{r}-1)!(s+1/2)^{q}}(-1)^{p_{1}+\cdots+p_{r}-r}ds=0.

Obviously, the integrand
F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(x,x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{r};s)
possesses the following poles in the complex plane: 1. All integer points are simple poles; 2.
s=-1/2
is a pole of order
p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q
; 3.
s=-n-1/2
(where
n
is a positive integer) is a pole of order
p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}
. Applying Lemma
2.1
, we have

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n-1/2\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-1/2\right)=0.

(5.23)

At integer points, which are simple zeros, the residue values can be calculated using Lemmas
2.3
and
2.4
as follows:

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{x^{-n}(x_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-n-1}}{(n+1/2)^{q}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(\ti_{p_{j}}(x_{j})-t_{n}(p_{j};x_{j})\right)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}),

\displaystyle{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)=\displaystyle\frac{(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{n}}{(-n+1/2)^{q}}\prod\limits_{j=1}^{r}\left(\ti_{p_{j}}(x_{j})x_{j}^{-1}+(-1)^{p_{j}}t_{n}\Big(p_{j};x_{j}^{-1}\Big)\right)\quad(n\in\mathbb{N}).

By expanding the two residue values above and then summing them, we obtain

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),n\right)+\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n\right)

\displaystyle=xT_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1},x_{2},\cdots,x_{r};(xx_{1}\cdots x_{r})^{-1}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+(-1)^{p_{1}+p_{2}+\cdots+p_{r}+q+r}T_{p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{r};q}\Big(x_{1}^{-1},x_{2}^{-1},\ldots,x_{r}^{-1};xx_{1}\cdots x_{r}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\{\text{combinations of lower-order sums}\}.

Applying Lemmas
2.2
and
2.5
, we can also compute the latter two residue values in (
5
). However, the resulting sum obtained after summation will still be of order less than
r
, namely:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-n-1/2\right)+{\rm Res}\left(F_{p_{1}p_{2}\cdots p_{r},q}(\cdot;s),-1/2\right)

\displaystyle\in\{\text{combinations of lower-order sums}\}.

Finally, substituting these two conclusions into (
5
) completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

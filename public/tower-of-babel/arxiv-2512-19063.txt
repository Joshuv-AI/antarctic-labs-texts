Sharp Decoupling Inequalities for the Variances and Second Moments of Sums of Dependent Random Variables
Victor H. de la Pena

Abstract




Abstract

Both complete decoupling and tangent decoupling are classical tools aiming to compare two random processes where one has a weaker dependence structure. We give a new proof for the complete decoupling inequality, which provides a lower bound for the sum of dependent square-integrable nonnegative random variables
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2},

where
z_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}d_{i}
for all
i\leq n
and
z_{i}
’s are mutually independent. We will then provide the following sharp tangent decoupling inequalities

\mathbb{V}ar\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)\leq 2\mathbb{V}ar\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right),

and

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)^{2}-\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)\right]^{2},

where
\{e_{i}\}
is the decoupled sequences of
\{d_{i}\}
and
d_{i}
’s are not forced to be nonnegative. Applications to construct Chebyshev-type inequality and Paley-Zygmund-type inequality, and to bound the second moments of randomly stopped sums will be provided.

1
Introduction

1.1
Background of Decoupling Inequalities

As an important tool in stochastic analysis, decoupling has been studied since the 1980s, which aims to compare two random processes, where one has a weaker dependence structure. Such a comparison involves decoupling inequalities that bound the quantities of the heavier dependent structure we are interested in, such as moments and tail probabilities, via the corresponding quantities of the weakly dependent process. The application of the decoupling technique includes martingale transforms (
[
1
]
), multilinear forms (
[
16
]
), U-processes (
[
6
]
), and stochastic integrations (
[
17
,
14
,
12
]
). For a comprehensive review of decoupling tools, we refer our readers to de la Pena and Giné
[
3
]
(2012).

In this paper, we consider decoupling the sums
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
of dependent random variables
\{d_{i}\}
. This form involves many processes and statistics one may be interested in, including U-statistics and randomly stopped processes. Specifically, two techniques of decoupling are introduced. The most direct way is the complete decoupling. As its name suggests, a sequence of mutually independent variables
\{z_{i}\}
is constructed, such that
z_{i}
has the same distribution as
d_{i}
for all
i
. We then study if there is a constant
c
such that

c\,\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2}.

Note that the lower bound does not exist in the general case. When
(d_{1},d_{2})
takes values with
(1,-1)
or
(-1,1)
with probability
1/2
, we have
\mathbb{E}(d_{1}+d_{2})^{2}=0
while
\mathbb{E}(e_{1}+e_{2})^{2}=2
. However, when we further assume that
d_{i}
’s are non-negative, we are able to find a lower bound given by the moment of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}
. The general result for any
p
-th moment was first proposed in
[
5
]
and improved and applied in
[
15
]
. To complete this article, we provide a new proof for the second moment case where the best constant
c
is
\frac{1}{2}
.

However, it is not possible to find a satisfactory upper bound
C\,\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\right)
for
\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)
. Consider
d_{1}=...=d_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\text{Ber}(p)
, for some
p\in(0,1)
, then
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}=n^{2}p
while
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i})^{2}=np(np+1-p)
. By letting
n\rightarrow\infty
and
p=o(1/n)
,
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}/\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i})^{2}\rightarrow\infty
. Therefore, the dependence structure needs to be modified. With the development of martingale theory, tangent decoupling becomes useful. By obtaining a decoupled version
\{e_{i}\}
of
\{d_{i}\}
(see Definition
1.3
), we can bounded the moment of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
using the moment of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
. One of the earliest works was given by de la Pena and Govindarajulu (1992
[
4
]
), who provided sharp inequalities for the second moment of randomly stopped sums of independent variables. This result was later enhanced by Hitczenko (1994
[
11
]
), who proved a general bound for the
\mathbb{L}^{p}
-norm of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
, such that

\lVert\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\rVert_{p}^{p}\leq 2^{p-1}\lVert\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\rVert_{p}^{p},

(1)

where
1\leq p<\infty
and
d_{i}
’s are assumed nonnegative, one application of which can be found in Feng and Yang (2019
[
10
]
). In 1994, de la Pena
[
7
]
provided the bound for the moment generating function (M.G.F.) of
S:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
using
S^{\prime}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
. Given both sums being light-tailed, the following bound for all finite
\lambda
holds:

M_{S}(\lambda)\leq\left(M_{S^{\prime}}(2\lambda)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad\mathbb{E}\left[\exp{\left(\lambda\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)}\right]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}\left[\exp{\left(2\lambda\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)}\right]}.

(2)

This inequality led to a general class of exponential inequalities for martingales and ratios (de la Pena
[
8
]
), which further found various applications for constructing confidence regions (e.g.,
[
19
,
18
,
23
,
20
]
). The above three results provide a conjecture, that it is possible to combine the
[
6
]
and
[
11
]
, and construct a tangent decoupling inequality having a similar form to (
1
) and working not only for nonnegative
\{d_{i}\}
. We notice that such a conjecture can be verified from (
2
) (see Remark
2
) when all random variables are assumed to be light-tailed. However, whether an upper bound having a (
1
)-type form requires only
\mathbb{L}^{p}
-integrability assumption remains unknown. In this work, we will show that when
p=2
, a sharp upper bound for
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}
can be built.

In section
2
, we provide a new proof for the sharp lower bound of
\mathbb{V}ar(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})
via complete decoupling. In Section
3
, we will demonstrate the main result, Theorem
3.3
, which extends Hitczenko’s work
[
11
]
in
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm such that the nonnegative summand assumption can be removed. Theorem
3.3
, Corollaries
3.4
and
3.5
, provide sharp upper bounds for the variances and
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norms of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}
, respectively. These inequalities also generalize the upper bound in
[
4
]
beyond randomly stopped sums of independent variables. In Section
4
, we will use some examples to show the applications of the main results. In the rest of this section, we introduce the framework of tangent decoupling and some notations.

1.2
Definitions and constructions of decoupled sequences

In this subsection, we briefly provide the framework of tangent decoupling. From now on, we use the notations
d_{i}
’s and
e_{i}
’s to represent the possibly dependent random variables and their corresponding decoupled random variables (see Definitions
1.1
,
1.2
and
1.3
). We denote
\mathcal{L}(X|\mathcal{F})
as the conditional law (distribution) of a random variable
X
on a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{F}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
\{d_{i}\}
,
\{e_{i}\}
be two sequences of random variables adapted to an increasing sequence of
\sigma
-algebras
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
. Then
\{d_{i}\}
is said to be
tangent
to
\{e_{i}\}
with respect to
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
if for all
i
,
\mathcal{L}(d_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})
=
\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})
, i.e., the conditional distributions of
d_{i}
given
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
and
e_{i}
given
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
are the same.

Definition 1.2
.

A sequence
\{e_{i}\}
of random variables adapted to an increasing sequence of
\sigma
-algebra
\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}
contained in
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
conditionally independent (CI)
if there exists a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
contained in
\mathcal{F}
such that
\{e_{i}\}
is conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})
.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\{d_{i}\}
be an arbitrary sequence of random variables, then a conditionally independent sequence
\{e_{i}\}
which is also tangent to
\{d_{i}\}
will be called a
decoupled
version of
\{d_{i}\}
.

The following proposition provided by Kwapien and Woiczynski (1989,
[
13
]
) guarantees that we can always construct a decoupled sequence, with any possibly dependent sequence
\{d_{i}\}
.

Proposition 1.4
.

For any sequence of random variables
\{d_{i}\}
, one can find a decoupled sequence
\{e_{i}\}
(on a possibly enlarged probability space) which is tangent to the original sequence and in addition conditionally independent given a master
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
. Frequently
\mathcal{G}=\sigma(\{d_{i}\})
.

Specifically, with
\{d_{i}\}
, a tangent sequence w.r.t.
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(d_{1},...,d_{i})
can be obtained via the following steps:

•

First, we take
d_{1}
and
e_{1}
to be two independent copies of the same random mechanism.

•

With (
d_{1},..,d_{i-1}
), the i-th pair of variables
d_{i}
and
e_{i}
comes from i.i.d. copies of the same random mechanism given
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
.

And
e_{i}
’s are conditionally independent w.r.t.
\mathcal{G}=\mathcal{F}_{n}
.

An example of constructing tangent sequence can be found in section
4
. Here we provide another example using (generalized) U-statistic. A generalized U-statistic
U_{n}
of
n
independent random variable
X_{1},...,X_{n}\in\mathcal{X}
with a class of functions
f_{i,j}:\mathcal{X}\times\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
,
1\leq i<j\leq n
, is defined as

U_{n}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}f_{i,j}(X_{i},X_{j}).

(3)

When
f_{i,j}(\cdot,\cdot)\equiv f(\cdot,\cdot):=\frac{2}{n(n-1)}h(\cdot,\cdot)
and
X_{i}
’s are i.i.d.,
U_{n}
becomes the U-statistic with kernel
f
. A classical way to handle the variance is Hoeffding decomposition (see e.g., Chapters 11 & 12
[
21
]
), while decoupling considers the natural filtration
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq n}
, where
\mathcal{F}_{i}=\sigma(\{X_{1},...,X_{i}\})
. With
d_{j}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i,j}(X_{i},X_{j})
, we rewrite
U_{n}:=\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}d_{j}
. The decoupled sequence
e_{j}:=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}f_{i,j}(X_{i},\tilde{X}_{j})
is then constructed, with
\tilde{X}_{i}
the independent copy of
X_{i}
for each
i
. As an instance of generalized U-statistics, the quadratic form
Q_{n}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}a_{ij}X_{i}X_{j}
has the corresponding decoupled sum
\sum\limits_{j=2}^{n}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}a_{ij}X_{i}\right)\tilde{X}_{j}
. With the decoupling inequalities in Section
3
, we can use the variance (or the second moment) of the decoupled sum to bound the variance (or the second moment) of
U_{n}
. Similarly to the technique in section
4
, we can also derive an upper bound for the variance (or the second moment) of
U_{\tau}
, for some
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
.

2
Lower bound via complete decoupling

We now provide a new proof for the following theorem, which coincides with the result in
[
5
]
and
[
15
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

For any nonnegative dependent square-integrable variables
d_{1},...d_{n}
, and the mutually independent variables
z_{1},...,z_{n}
such that
z_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}d_{i}
for all
i=1,...,n
, we have the following sharp inequality

\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2}.

(4)

Proof.

We notice that
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i})^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}z_{i}^{2}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}[z_{i}z_{j}]
and
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}d_{i}^{2}+\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}[d_{i}d_{j}]
. Therefore, since all random variables we consider are nonnegative, we have

\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}d_{i}^{2}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}z_{i}^{2},

and by Jensen’s inequality,

\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}\geq(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}d_{i})^{2}=(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}z_{i})^{2}\geq\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}[z_{i}]\mathbb{E}[z_{j}]=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}[z_{i}z_{j}].

By adding the above two inequalities, we claim (
4
).

The sharpness of (
4
) can be seen in the following example. Consider
(d_{1},...,d_{n})=\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
with probability
1/n
, for
k=1,...,n
, where
\boldsymbol{u}_{k}
denotes the
n
-dimensional unit vector with the
k
-th element being 1. Therefore
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\equiv 1
,
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}\text{Bin}(n,1/n)
. Hence
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i})^{2}=1
and
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i})^{2}=2-1/n
. This suggests that the best constant for this lower bound working for all
n
is
1/2
.
∎

3
Upper bound for variance and second moment via tangent decoupling

In this section, we present the main results of the decoupling inequalities for variance and the second moment of the sums. We assume that all random variables we consider are square-integrable. Before providing the main theorem, we present the following two lemmas.

Lemma 3.1
.

Consider a sequence of possibly dependent random variables
d_{1},..,d_{n}
, and a decoupled sequence
e_{1},..,e_{n}
given a
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{G}
. Then we have the following equality

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}

(5)

Intuitively, Lemma
3.1
provides a geometric view such that
\mathbb{E}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\big|\mathcal{G})
is the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-projection of
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}
on
\mathcal{G}
. The detailed proof can be found in the literature (e.g.,
[
9
]
). Lemma
3.2
states that both distances of the sum and its
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-projection have the same second moment.

Lemma 3.2
.

With the same assumption as in Lemma
3.1
, if we further suppose
e_{i}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{L}}}{{=}}d_{i}
,
i=1,...,n
, then we have that

\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}.

(6)

Proof of Lemma
3.2
.

Expanding the square of the L.H.S., we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\left(d_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\right].

For each
1\leq i<j\leq n
, since
\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})
,
\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})
is
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
-measurable. Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\left(d_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\{\mathbb{E}\left[\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\left(d_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\right]\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\{\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})\right)\mathbb{E}\left[\left(d_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\big|\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\right]\}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\{\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})\right)\left[\mathbb{E}(d_{j}\big|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})\right]\}

\displaystyle=0,

where the last equality is due to
\mathbb{E}(d_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.}}}{{=}}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{j-1})
, as
\mathcal{L}(d_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mathcal{L}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})
. Therefore, the intersection terms vanish.

A similar approach can be applied to the second moment of the decoupled sum

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(e_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)^{2}+2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(e_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\left(e_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\right].

Since for each
1\leq i<j\leq n
,
e_{i}
and
e_{j}
are conditionally independent given
\mathcal{G}
, we then have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(e_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)\left(e_{j}-\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right)\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;=\mathbb{E}(e_{i}e_{j})-\mathbb{E}\left[e_{j}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[e_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{j}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})|\mathcal{G}\right]\}-\mathbb{E}\{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})|\mathcal{G}\right]\}+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;=\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\mathbb{E}(e_{j}|\mathcal{G})\right]

\displaystyle\;\;\;\;\;=0.

Hence, the intersection also vanishes.

Therefore the difference of L.H.S. and R.H.S. of (
6
) reduces to

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(d_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)^{2}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left(e_{i}-\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\;\;\;=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\mathbb{E}d_{i}^{2}-2\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right]+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}e_{i}^{2}+2\mathbb{E}\left[e_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})^{2}\right]\}

\displaystyle\;\;\;=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\mathbb{E}\left[e_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[d_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\right]\}

\displaystyle\;\;\;=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(e_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\big|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right)\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(d_{i}\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})\big|\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\right)\right]\}

\displaystyle\;\;\;=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(d_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})\right]\}

(7)

\displaystyle\;\;\;=2\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}\{\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})^{2}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})^{2}\right]\}

(8)

\displaystyle\;\;\;=0,

where steps (
7
) and (
8
) are because that
\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.}

\mathbb{E}(d_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{F}_{i-1})=\mathbb{E}(e_{i}|\mathcal{G})
, all of which are
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
-measurable.
∎

With Lemmas
3.1
and
3.2
, we can then show the following sharp inequality for the second moments of the summations.

Theorem 3.3

(Decoupling inequality for the second moment)
.

With the same setting as in Lemma
3.2
, we have that

\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\right)^{2}.

(9)

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

With a similar expansion of the square in the above proof, we have that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\right)^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)+\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}+\mathbb{E}\left[\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}e_{i}\Big|\mathcal{G}\right)\right]^{2}

4
Bounding the second moment of randomly stopped sums

The framework of tangent decoupling may not be intuitive to understand. Therefore, we end this article with the randomly stopped processes to show how to use tangent decoupling and the application of the inequality (
15
).

The moments of a randomly stopped sum with martingale property can be dated back to Wald
[
22
]
(1944). In particular, Chow et al (1965,
[
2
]
) showed that if
S_{n}
is the summation of the i.i.d. centered, square-integrable random variables,
X_{1},...,X_{n}
, and if the
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
satisfies
\mathbb{E}\tau<\infty
, then

\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau}=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}\tau,

where
\mathbb{F}=\{\mathcal{F}_{n}\}
is the filtration such that
\mathcal{F}_{n}:=\sigma(X_{1},...,X_{n})
. However, to deal with the second moment of the randomly stopping sum with independent non-centered random variables
S_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
, the martingale tools may not succeed. In this case, the decoupling inequality can be of help.

For the sake of simplicity, we assumed that
X_{1},X_{2},...
are i.i.d.
\mathbb{R}
-valued random variables with mean
\mu\neq 0
and variance
\sigma^{2}
. Suppose the
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
is square-integrable. Note that one can drop the assumption of identical distribution to extend the following result. We notice that
S_{\tau}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}d_{i}
such that
d_{i}=X_{i}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}
. Since
\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}=1-\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau\leq i\}}
is
\mathcal{F}_{i-1}
-measurable and therefore independent of
X_{i}
, we can construct a decoupled sequence
e_{i}=X_{i}^{\prime}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}
of
d_{i}
, where
X_{i}^{\prime}
are the i.i.d. copy of
X_{i}
. And
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}e_{i}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{i}^{\prime}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}
is identically distributed as
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{\infty}X_{i}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq i\}}=:S_{\tau^{\prime}}
for
\tau^{\prime}
an i.i.d copy of
\tau
. Based on this decoupling setting, we provide the following theorem, which improves the upper bound given in
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
S_{n}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}X_{i}
be the summation of
n
i.i.d. random variables with mean
\mu
and variance
\sigma^{2}
, and assume an
\mathbb{F}
-stopping time
\tau
is square-integrable, then

\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau}\leq 2\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau^{\prime}}-(\mathbb{E}S_{\tau^{\prime}})^{2},

(19)

where the bound can be explicitly written as (
20
).

Proof.

The Fatou’s lemma shows that

\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau}=\mathbb{E}\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)^{2}\leq\liminf\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)^{2}.

Corollary
3.5
further provides the bound such that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}

\displaystyle\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)^{2}\leq 2\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\tilde{X}_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)^{2}-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{j=2}^{N}(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}X_{i})X_{j}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq j\}}\right)+2\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i\geq 1}X_{i}^{2}\mathsf{I}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq i\}}\right)-\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right)

\displaystyle=2\mu^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{N}(j-1)\mathbb{P}(\tau\geq j)+(2\mu^{2}+2\sigma^{2}-\mu)\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(\tau\geq i),

the limit of which, due to the square-integrability of
\tau
, is

2\mu^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}(j-1)\mathbb{P}(\tau\geq j)+(2\mu^{2}+2\sigma^{2}-\mu)\mathbb{E}\tau.

(20)

We will then show
2\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau^{\prime}}-(\mathbb{E}S_{\tau^{\prime}})^{2}=
(
20
). We note that

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq i\}}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq i\}}+2\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}X_{i}X_{j}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq j\}}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{i=1}^{N}X_{i}^{2}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq i\}}\right)+2\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}X_{i}X_{j}\mathds{1}_{\{\tau^{\prime}\geq j\}}\right)

(21)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}X^{2}\mathbb{E}\tau^{\prime}+2\mu^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{j-1}\mathbb{P}(\tau^{\prime}\geq j)

(22)

\displaystyle=(\mu^{2}+\sigma^{2})\mathbb{E}\tau+2\mu^{2}\sum\limits_{j=2}^{\infty}(j-1)\mathbb{P}(\tau\geq j),

where we obtain the first term in (
21
) via the monotone convergence theorem, and the second term in (
21
) via the dominated convergence theorem (since
|\sum X_{i}X_{j}\mathds{1}_{\tau^{\prime}\geq j}|\leq\sum|X_{i}||X_{j}|\mathds{1}_{\tau^{\prime}\geq j}
is integrable), and we use the mutual independence among
X_{i},X_{j}
and
\tau^{\prime}
to obtain (
22
).

With the implicit form of
\mathbb{E}S^{2}_{\tau^{\prime}}
and that
\mathbb{E}S_{\tau^{\prime}}=\mu\mathbb{E}\tau^{\prime}
, we conclude the bound (
20
).
∎

5
Concluding Remarks

This paper is concerned with the application of decoupling inequalities, with a focus on the second moments and variances of sums of dependent random variables. By deriving sharp bounds, we present a robust framework that generalizes classical results — such as those of de la Pena and Hitczenko — to a wider range of dependence structures, notably without requiring the non-negativity assumption for the upper bound.

Our findings demonstrate that precise upper bounds can be obtained under the minimal condition of square integrability. These inequalities not only deepen theoretical understanding but also yield practical tools for bounding tail probabilities via Chebyshev- and Paley-Zygmund-type inequalities in the presence of dependence.

We further highlight the applicability of these decoupling techniques to randomly stopped sums, showcasing their effectiveness in settings where traditional martingale methods may be inadequate, particularly in the analysis of non-centered or heavy-tailed variables.

By synthesizing existing literature and extending foundational inequalities, this work offers a meaningful contribution to the study of dependent random variables. Future research may build upon this foundation to explore applications in statistical inference and machine learning, where dependence among data points is often intrinsic.

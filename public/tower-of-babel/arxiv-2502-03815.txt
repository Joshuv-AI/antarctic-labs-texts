Runge type approximation results for spaces of smooth Whitney jets
Tomasz Ciaś 1, Thomas Kalmes 2

Abstract
. We prove Runge type approximation results for linear partial differential operators with constant coefficients on spaces of smooth Whitney jets. Among others, we characterize when for a constant coefficient linear partial differential operator P ⁡ ( D ) P(D) and for closed subsets F 1 ⊂ F 2 F_{1}\subset F_{2} of ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} the restrictions to F 1 F_{1} of smooth Whitney jets f f on F 2 F_{2} satisfying P ⁡ ( D ) ​ f = 0 P(D)f=0 on F 2 F_{2} are dense in the space of smooth Whitney jets on F 1 F_{1} satisfying the same partial differential equation on F 1 F_{1} . Additionally, for differential operators with a single characteristic direction, like parabolic operators, we give a sufficient geometric condition for the above density to hold. Under mild additional assumptions on ∂ F 1 \partial F_{1} and for F 2 = ℝ d F_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{d} this sufficient conditions is also necessary. As an application of our work, we characterize those open subsets Ω \Omega of the complex plane satisfying Ω = int ⁡ Ω ¯ \Omega=\operatorname{int}\overline{\Omega} for which the set of holomorphic polynomials are dense in A ∞ ​ ( Ω ) A^{\infty}(\Omega) , under the mild additional hypothesis that Ω ¯ \overline{\Omega} satisfies the strong regularity condition. For the special case of F 2 = ℝ 2 F_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2} this sufficient condition is also necessary under mild additional hypotheses on F 1 F_{1} . Introduction

A well-known consequence of Runge‘s classical theorem on rational approximation asserts that for open subsets
X_{1}\subset X_{2}
of the complex plane
\mathbb{C}
, every holomorphic function defined on
X_{1}
is the local uniform limit of (restrictions to
X_{1}
of) holomorphic functions on
X_{2}
if and only if
X_{2}
does not contain a bounded connected component of
\mathbb{C}\setminus X_{1}
. During the 1950s, Lax
[
19
]
and Malgrange
[
20
]
independently generalized this result from holomorphic functions, i.e. solutions of the homogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equation, to kernels of elliptic partial differential operators with constant coefficients. Further generalizations reached its peak with the work of Browder
[
2
,
3
]
in the 1960s dealing with elliptic partial differentiable operators with variable coefficients on various spaces of functions and distribution. Since then, so-called Runge approximation theorems have become a classical topic in the analysis of (linear) partial differerential operators. Recently, this subject has been rekindled due to its many applications, most prominenty in the deep work of Enciso, Peralta-Salas on the 3D Euler
[
7
,
8
]
and (jointly with Lucá) the Navier-Stokes equations
[
10
]
.

Much less is known, however, for non-elliptic partial differential operators. Malgrange
[
20
, Chapitre 1.2, Théorème 2]
(see also
[
16
, Theorem 10.5.1]
) proved that for an arbitrary non-zero partial differential operator with constant coefficients, the linear span of the corresponding exponential solutions is dense in the kernel on the space of smooth functions over arbitrary convex open sets. Moreover, Hörmander
[
16
, Theorem 10.5.2]
and Trèves
[
26
, Theorem 26.1]
established implicit approximation results for general constant coefficient partial differential operators applicable to arbitrary open sets
X_{1},X_{2}
, and to
P
-convex open sets

, respectively. Nevertheless, apart from the heat operator
[
5
,
17
]
(see also
[
14
]
), for non-convex
X_{1}
, geometric conditions on
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
characterizing (or at least implying) the validity of Runge approximation theorems have only been established recently. For variable coefficients parabolic operators of second degree, this has been achieved by Enciso, García-Ferrero, Peralta-Salas
[
6
]
and has been complemented by Enciso, Peralta-Salas
[
9
]
as well as Shlapunov, Vilkov

, while for several classes of non-elliptic constant coefficient partial differential operators such results are due to the second author

While all above mentioned results deal with Runge approximation theorems for kernels of partial differential operators on spaces of functions or distributions defined on open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, to the best of the authors‘ knowledge, there are no approximation results for kernels of constant coefficients partial differential operators on spaces of smooth Whitney jets over closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

2.
Preliminaries

In this short preliminary section we discuss smooth Whitney jets and properties of partial differential operators acting on spaces of smooth Whitney jets. The purpose of this section is mainly to introduce the notation and some essential notions we shall work with in this article. For anything related to functional analysis which is not explained in the text, we refer the reader to
[
22
]
, and for notions and results about distributions and partial differential operators we refer to
[
15
,
16
]
. We emphasize that by an isomorphism between locally convex spaces we mean a bijective continuous linear mapping with a continuous inverse.

As is customary, we denote the euclidean norm of a vector
x
in
\mathbb{R}^{k}
by
|x|
. While we use the same notation for all
k
, the dimension will always be clear from the context. In addition, for a multi-index
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{k}
we denote its length by
|\alpha|(=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\alpha_{j})
as well. Again, this should not cause any confusion since the meaning will be apparent from the context. Moreover, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{k}
and
\varepsilon>0
we write
B(x,\varepsilon)
for the open euclidean ball about
x
with radius
\varepsilon
, where we again surpress the concrete dimension
k
in the notation, as this will be clear from the context.

For an open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
\mathscr{E}(\Omega)
the Fréchet space of smooth, complex valued functions on
\Omega
which is endowed with the sequence of seminorms
(|\cdot|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

(1)

\forall\,g\in\mathscr{E}(\Omega)\colon\,|g|_{n}:=\sup\{|\partial^{\alpha}g(x)|:x\in K_{n},|\alpha|\leq n\},

where
(K_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is an arbitrary compact exhaustion of
\Omega
. Let
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\Omega)
be its dual space equipped – as other duals in this paper – with the strong topology. As is well-known,
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\Omega)
is the space of distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose support is a compact subset of
\Omega
. For
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
we abbreviate, as usual,
\mathscr{E}=\mathscr{E}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}=\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

For a compact subset
K
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we denote by
\mathscr{E}(K)
the space of smooth Whitney jets on
K
, that is, families
f=(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
of continuous (real or complex valued) functions
f^{(\alpha)}
on
K
for which, with the formal Taylor polynomials of arbitrary order
n

T^{n}_{x}f(y)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq n}\frac{f^{(\alpha)}(x)}{\alpha!}(y-x)^{\alpha},

the quantities

q_{K,n,t}(f):=\sup\left\{\frac{|f^{(\alpha)}(y)-\partial^{\alpha}T_{x}^{n}f(y)|}{|x-y|^{n-|\alpha|}}\colon|\alpha|\leq n,x,y\in K,0<|x-y|<t\right\}

satisfy
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}q_{K,n,t}(f)=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. (Apparently, smooth Whitney jets are not usual functions, despite the fact that they sometimes called Whitney functions in the literature.) On
\mathscr{E}(K)
we define the sequence of seminorms
(\|\cdot\|_{n,K})_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
by

\forall\,f\in\mathscr{E}(K)\colon\|f\|_{n,K}:=\sup\{|f^{(\alpha)}(x)|\colon x\in K,|\alpha|\leq n\}+\sup\{q_{K,n,t}(f)\colon t>0\}

which makes
\mathscr{E}(K)
into a a Fréchet space.

For a closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
(K_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
be a compact exhaustion of
F
. Then, for
l\in\mathbb{N}
the linear mapping

\varrho_{l+1}^{l}:\mathscr{E}(K_{l+1})\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(K_{l}),f=(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mapsto f_{\mid K_{l}}:=(f^{(\alpha)}_{\mid K_{l}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}

is correctly defined and continuous.
As usual,
\mathscr{E}(F)
is defined as the locally convex projective limit of
(\mathscr{E}(K_{l}))_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
, i.e.

\mathscr{E}(F):=\operatorname{proj}_{\leftarrow l}\mathscr{E}(K_{l})=\left\{(f_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\in\prod_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{E}(K_{l})\colon\varrho_{l+1}^{l}(f_{l+1})=f_{l},\quad l\in\mathbb{N}\right\}

equipped with the subspace topology of the topological product
\prod_{l\in\mathbb{N}}\mathscr{E}(K_{l})
. One can show that
\mathscr{E}(F)
does not depend on the particular choice of
(K_{l})_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
.

Clearly, Taylor‘s Theorem implies that for
g\in\mathscr{E}
it holds
(\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid F})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
(or, more precisely,
\left(\left(\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid K_{l}}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\right)_{l\in\mathbb{N}}
) belongs to
\mathscr{E}(F)
. A seminal result of Whitney
[
27
]
(see also
[
21
]
) says that every
f\in\mathscr{E}(F)
has such an extension
g
. Whitney‘s methods yield that
\mathscr{E}(F)
is a Fréchet space and hence, by the Closed Graph Theorem, the linear mapping

\varrho_{F}\colon\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(F),g\mapsto(\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid F})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}

is continuous. Obviously,

\mathcal{I}_{F}=\{g\in\mathscr{E}\colon\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid{F}}=0\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\}

is the kernel of
\varrho_{F}
. By Whitney‘s result,

0\rightarrow\mathcal{I}_{F}\hookrightarrow\mathscr{E}\xrightarrow{\varrho_{F}}\mathscr{E}(F)\rightarrow 0

is a short exact sequence of Fréchet spaces. It follows herefrom and the Open Mapping Theorem that
\mathscr{E}(F)
is isomorphic to the quotient space
\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}
. In particular, the sequence of seminorms
(\|\cdot\|_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
on
\mathscr{E}(F)
defined as

\forall\,f\in\mathscr{E}(F)\colon\,||f||_{n}:=\inf\left\{|g|_{n}\colon g\in\mathscr{E}\text{ and }\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid{F}}=f^{(\alpha)},\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\right\}

also defines the Fréchet space topology on
\mathscr{E}(F)
.

We also define

i_{F}\colon\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}\to\mathscr{E}(F),g+\mathcal{I}_{F}\mapsto(\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid F})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}

which by the above is easily seen to be a well-defined isomorphism. With
q_{F}:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}
denoting the quotient map, it follows

\varrho_{F}=i_{F}\circ q_{F}\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}(F),f\mapsto(\partial^{\alpha}f_{\mid F})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}.

We denote the (strong) dual of
\mathscr{E}(F)
by
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)
.

Defining for
f=(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\in\mathscr{E}(F)

D_{j}f=-i(f^{(\alpha+e_{j})})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}},\quad j=1,\ldots,d,

where
e_{j}=(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
is the
j
-th unit vector it follows immediately that

\forall\,g\in\mathscr{E}\colon D_{j}(\varrho_{F}g)=\varrho_{F}\left(-i\partial_{j}g\right).

More generally, for a polynomial
P\in\mathbb{C}[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{d}]
of degree
m
,
P(\xi)=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}\xi^{\alpha}
, and the corresponding partial differential operator of order
m
with constant coefficients
P(D)
on
\mathscr{E}
,

P(D)\colon\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E},g\mapsto\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}(-i)^{|\alpha|}\partial^{\alpha}g

and

P_{F}(D)\colon\mathscr{E}(F)\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(F),f\mapsto\sum_{|\alpha|\leq m}c_{\alpha}D_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots D_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}f

we have
P_{F}(D)\circ\varrho_{F}=\varrho_{F}\circ P(D)
and
P_{F}(D)
is a continuous linear operator. By a slight abuse of notation, instead of
P_{F}(D)
we sometimes also write
P(D)
. As observed by Frerick
[
11
, Introduction to Chapter 6]
, this equality combined with the surjectivity of
P(D)\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
easily implies the surjectivity of
P_{F}(D)
. This stands in contrast to
P(D)
acting on the space of smooth functions
\mathscr{E}(\Omega)
on an open subset
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
where it is not surjective in general.

Let
\mathscr{E}_{P}(F)
denote the kernel of
P(D)\colon\mathscr{E}(F)\to\mathscr{E}(F)
, i.e.

\mathscr{E}_{P}(F)=\{f\in\mathscr{E}(F):\;P(D)f=0\}.

Definition 1
.

For a pair of closed subsets
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
we define the (continuous) restriction operator

(2)

r\colon\mathscr{E}(F_{2})\to\mathscr{E}(F_{1}),f\mapsto\left({f^{(\alpha)}}_{\mid F_{1}}\right)_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}.

Clearly,
r\left(\mathscr{E}_{P}(F_{2})\right)\subset\mathscr{E}_{P}(F_{1})
, and we call
F_{1},F_{2}
a
P
-Runge pair (for smooth Whitney jets)
if
r\left(\mathscr{E}_{P}(F_{2})\right)
is dense in
\mathscr{E}_{P}(F_{1})
.

In order to avoid unnecessary lengthy formulations, for obvious reasons we assume from now on that all polynomials are non-constant and that for every pair of closed subsets
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
it holds
F_{1}\neq F_{2}
.

3.
Runge pairs for smooth Whitney jets

In this section we give a complete characterization of Runge pairs for arbitrary partial differential operators in the context of smooth Whitney jets. In order to do so, we first provide the following abstract result. As usual, for a continuous linear operator
A\colon E_{1}\to E_{2}
between locally convex spaces, we denote its transpose by

\prescript{t}{}{A}\colon E_{2}^{\prime}\to E_{1}^{\prime}
. Then,
\prescript{t}{}{A}
is continuous when the duals
E_{1}^{\prime}
and
E_{2}^{\prime}
are both equipped with the strong topology (see
[
22
, Proposition 23.30]
). Moreover, by a standard consequence of the Hahn-Banach Theorem,
\operatorname{im}A
is dense in
E_{2}
if and only if
\prescript{t}{}{A}
is injective (see
[
22
, Lemma 23.31]
).

From the definition of the spaces of smooth Whitney jets it follows immediately that the operator
r
in (
2
) is surjective. Consequently,

\prescript{t}{}{r}\colon\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{1})\to\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{2})

is an injective continuous linear operator.

Proposition 2
.

Let
E_{1},E_{2}
be Fréchet spaces and let
T_{1}\colon E_{1}\to E_{1}
,
T_{2}\colon E_{2}\to E_{2}
and
r\colon E_{2}\to E_{1}
be continuous linear maps. Assume moreover that
T_{2}
is surjective and
r\circ T_{2}=T_{1}\circ r
. Then the following assertions are equivalent:

(i)

T_{1}
is surjective and
r(\ker T_{2})
is dense in
\ker T_{1}
;

(ii)

r
has dense range and for all
y\in E_{2}^{\prime}
it holds
y\in\operatorname{im}{}^{t}r
whenever
{}^{t}T_{2}(y)\in\operatorname{im}{}^{t}r
.

Proof.

This is an immediate consequence of
[
26
, Proposition 13.1 and Corollary 3 on p. 54]
.
∎

Remark 3
.

Applying the previous Proposition to
E_{j}=\mathscr{E}(F_{j})
and to
T_{j}=P(D)
,
j=1,2
, it follows from the considerations proceeding Definition
1
that
F_{1},F_{2}
is a

u\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}
for every
u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{2})
with
\prescript{t}{}{P(D)}u\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}
.

Hence, for a closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we next collect some facts about
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)
and
\prescript{t}{}{P(D)}
. With the annihilator/polar
\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}
of
\mathcal{I}_{F}
, i.e.

\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}:=\left\{u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\colon u(f)=0\text{ for all }f\in\mathcal{I}_{F}\right\}

which is obviously a closed subspace of
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
, the following holds.

Proposition 4
.

For every closed subset
F
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho}_{F}:\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)\to\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}

is a correctly defined isomorphism and
\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}
consists precisely of those distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
which have compact support in
F
. Consequently,
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)
can be identified with the distributions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
whose support is a compact subset of
F
.

Proof.

Since
i_{F}\colon\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}\to\mathscr{E}(F),g+\mathcal{I}_{F}\mapsto(\partial^{\alpha}g_{\mid F})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
is an isomorphism, the same holds for

\prescript{t}{}{i}_{F}\colon\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)\to\left(\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}\right)^{\prime}.

Since
\mathscr{E}
is a Fréchet-Schwartz space, by
[
22
, Corollary 26.25 and Remark 26.5]
,

\prescript{t}{}{q}_{F}:\left(\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{F}\right)^{\prime}\to\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}

is a well-defined isomorphism which proves that
\prescript{t}{}{q}_{F}\circ\prescript{t}{}{i}_{F}=\prescript{t}{}{(}i_{F}\circ q_{F})=\prescript{t}{}{\varrho}_{F}
is an isomorphism from
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F)
onto
\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}
.

Obviously, the subspace
\{\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap F=\emptyset\}
of
\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is contained in
\mathcal{I}_{F}
. Therefore, we have

\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}\subset\{\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap F=\emptyset\}^{\circ},

where the polar on the right hand side refers to the dual pair
\mathscr{E}
,
\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
. Thus, one easily deduces from the definition of support of a distribution

\{\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon\operatorname{supp}\varphi\cap F=\emptyset\}^{\circ}=\{u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\colon\operatorname{supp}u\subset F\}.

On the other hand, let
u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
have support in
F
. Then,
u(f)=0
for every
f\in\mathcal{I}_{F}
by
[

so that
u\in\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}
. Summarizing,

\mathcal{I}_{F}^{\circ}=\{u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\colon\operatorname{supp}u\subset F\}

which completes the proof.
∎

(ii)

Every compactly supported distribution

on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with support contained in
F_{2}
has its support already contained in
F_{1}
whenever
\check{P}(D)u
is supported in
F_{1}
. Here, as usual,
\check{P}(\xi)=P(-\xi)
.

Proof.

For the proof, we distinguish again notationally and write
P_{F_{j}}(D):\mathscr{E}(F_{j})\to\mathscr{E}(F_{j})
,
j=1,2,

as well as
P(D):\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}
.
We then have
\varrho_{F_{j}}\circ P(D)=P_{F_{j}}(D)\circ\varrho_{F_{j}}
for
j=1,2
, where

\varrho_{F_{j}}:\mathscr{E}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}(F_{j}),f\mapsto(\partial^{\alpha}f_{\mid F_{j}})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}.

As already observed in Remark
3
, by Proposition
2
applied to
E_{j}=\mathscr{E}(F_{j})
,
T_{j}=P_{F_{j}}(D)

j=1,2
, and the restriction operator
r\colon\mathscr{E}(F_{2})\to\mathscr{E}(F_{1})
,
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is a
P
-Runge pair precisely when

(3)

\forall\,u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{2})\colon\left(\prescript{t}{}{P_{F_{2}}(D)}u\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}\Rightarrow u\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}\right).

By Proposition
4
,
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{j}}}:\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{j})\to\mathcal{I}_{F_{j}}^{\circ}
,
j=1,2
, are isomorphisms so that (
3

(4)

\forall\,v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{2}}^{\circ}\colon\left(\prescript{t}{}{P_{F_{2}}(D)}\left(\left(\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\right)^{-1}v\right)\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}\Rightarrow\left(\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\right)^{-1}v\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}\right).

Clearly,
\varrho_{F_{1}}=r\circ\varrho_{F_{2}}
, and thus,
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{1}}}=\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\circ\prescript{t}{}{r}
with injective
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}
. Due to the injectivity of
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}

it holds

if and only if
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}u\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{1}}}=\mathcal{I}_{F_{1}}^{\circ}
. Therefore, (
4
) is equivalent to

(5)

\forall\,v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{2}}^{\circ}\colon\left(\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\left(\prescript{t}{}{P_{F_{2}}(D)}\left(\left(\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\right)^{-1}v\right)\right)\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{1}}^{\circ}\Rightarrow v\in\operatorname{im}\prescript{t}{}{r}\right).

Since
\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\circ\prescript{t}{}{P_{F_{2}}(D)}=\prescript{t}{}{P(D)}\circ\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}
and
\check{P}(D):\mathscr{E}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
is the transpose
\prescript{t}{}{P(D)}
of

we have

\forall\,v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{2}}^{\circ}\colon\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}}\left(\prescript{t}{}{P_{F_{2}}(D)}\left((\prescript{t}{}{\varrho_{F_{2}}})^{-1}v\right)\right)=\check{P}(D)v

so that (

\forall v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{2}}^{\circ}\colon\left(\check{P}(D)v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{1}}^{\circ}\Rightarrow v\in\mathcal{I}_{F_{1}}^{\circ}\right).

One final glimpse at Proposition

reveals that (

∎

Let

,
\cap_{j\in I}F_{2}^{j}

Corollary 7
.

Let

be closed subsets of

such that

is a

-Runge pair for every hypoelliptic polynomial

.

Proof.

Fix a hypoelliptic polynomial

. Let

and

by

, so that by the density of the latter in

-Runge pair.
∎

Corollary 8
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be polynomials. Moreover, let
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
be closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then, the following are equivalent.

(i)

F_{1},F_{2}
is a Runge pair for both polynomials
P_{1}
and

.

(ii)

is a

-Runge pair.

That (i) implies (ii) follows immediately from Theorem

. Similarly, if (ii) holds and

has support in

and satisfies

, it follows from

, (ii) and Theorem

that

is a

-Runge pair. That

-Runge pair follows similarly taking into account that

.
∎

Next we prove a necessary condition for closed sets

to be a

-Runge pair. In Section

we will show that this necessary condition is also sufficient for elliptic partial differential operators.

Corollary 9
.

Let closed subsets

be a
P
-Runge pair for some

. Then,
F_{2}
does not contain a bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash F_{1}
.

Proof.

Since
P
is non-constant there is
\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{d}
with
\check{P}(\zeta)=0
. Thus,
\check{P}(D)g_{0}=0
for the real analytic function

,

.

Assume that

is a bounded (open) connected component of

which is contained in

. Then,

and since

is closed,

. In particular,

is not contained in

. On the other hand,
\check{P}(D)u_{g}

, so

is closed. By Theorem

,
F_{1},F_{2}
is not a

-Runge pair.
∎

We close this section by considering the special case when our closed sets are closures of open subsets with nice boundary.

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then the elements
(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}
of
\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})
are uniquely determined by the function
f^{(0)}
on
\Omega
, hence
\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
can be treated as function spaces on
\Omega
. Also in this case, the kernel of a differential operator
P(D)
on the space
\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})
is determined by the kernel of the same operator on the space

as we show in the next proposition. We equip the subspace
\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}:=\left\{g\in C(\overline{\Omega})\colon g=\tilde{g}_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}\text{ for some }\tilde{g}\in\mathscr{E}\right\}
of
C(\overline{\Omega})
with the sequence of seminorms
(\widetilde{\|\cdot\|}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
defined by

\forall g\in\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}\colon\widetilde{\|g\|}_{n}:=\inf\{|\tilde{g}|_{n}\colon\tilde{g}\in\mathscr{E},\tilde{g}_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}=g\},

see (
1
).

Proposition 10
.

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty, open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,
\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}
is a Fréchet space and

\iota\colon\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})\to\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}},f=(f^{(\alpha)})_{\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}}\mapsto f^{(0)}

is a linear isomorphism. Moreover, it holds

\iota(\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega}))=\left\{g_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}\colon g\in\mathscr{E},P(D)g=0\text{ in }\Omega\right\}.

Proof.

It is easily seen that

j_{\overline{\Omega}}\colon\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{\overline{\Omega}}\to\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}},g+\mathcal{I}_{\overline{\Omega}}\mapsto g_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}

is a well-defined isomorphism. With the canonical isomorphism
i_{\overline{\Omega}}\colon\mathscr{E}/\mathcal{I}_{\overline{\Omega}}\to\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})
it follows
\iota=j_{\overline{\Omega}}\circ\iota_{\overline{\Omega}}^{-1}
which proves that
\iota
is an isomorphism. This also implies that
\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}
is a Fréchet space.

Next, we observe that for
f\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
trivially
\iota(f)\in\{g_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}\colon g\in\mathscr{E},P(D)g=0\text{ in }\Omega\}
due to
P_{\overline{\Omega}}(D)\circ\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}=\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}\circ P(D)
, where
P(D)\colon\mathscr{E}\to\mathscr{E}
. On the other hand, for
g\in\mathscr{E}
with
P(D)g=0
in
\Omega
we conclude from the density of
\Omega
in
\overline{\Omega}
that
P(D)g\in\mathcal{I}_{\overline{\Omega}}^{\circ}=\ker\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}
. Applying again
P_{\overline{\Omega}}(D)\circ\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}=\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}\circ P(D)
it holds
\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}(g)\in\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
. From
\iota(\varrho_{\overline{\Omega}}g)=g_{\overline{\Omega}}
it follows indeed
\iota(\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega}))=\left\{g_{\mid\overline{\Omega}}\colon g\in\mathscr{E},P(D)g=0\text{ in }\Omega\right\}.

∎

By Proposition
10
,
\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
can be identified with the Fréchet space of solutions in
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
of the equation
P(D)f=0
admitting a smooth extension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. In other words,
\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
is the space of functions on
\overline{\Omega}
admitting a smooth extension to
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and satisfying the equation
P(D)f=0
on
\Omega
.

If the boundary of
\Omega
is sufficiently regular – for example, if
\overline{\Omega}
satisfies the so-called strong regularity condition, see below – then some natural definitions of smooth functions on closed sets coincide (see
[
24
, Lemma 1.10]
and
[
1
, Proposition 2.16]
).
Let us recall (see e.g.
[
1
,
(2.16.1)
]
) that path connected closed set
F\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
F=\overline{\operatorname{int}F}
satisfies
the strong regurality condition
if
for all
x_{0}\in F
there are
\varepsilon>0
,
C>0
and
\theta\in(0,1]
such that any two points
x,y\in F\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
can be joined by a rectifiable arc
\gamma\subset F
with length
|\gamma|
such that

(1)

\gamma
meets the boundary of
F
at most finitely many times

(2)

|\gamma|\leq C|x-y|^{\theta}
.

It can be shown that
\overline{\Omega}
satisfies the strong regularity condition whenever the boundary of
\Omega
is locally of Lipschitz class
\theta
. As we could not find any reference for this, for the reader‘s convenience, a proof of this fact is given in the appendix.

By
[
1
, Proposition 2.16]
, in case
\Omega
is such that
\overline{\Omega}
satisfies the strong regularity condition, it holds
\mathscr{E}(\overline{\Omega})=C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
, where for an arbitrary open subset
U
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}

C^{\infty}(\overline{U}):=\{f\in C^{\infty}(U)\colon\partial^{\alpha}f\text{ admits a continuous extension to }\overline{U}\text{ for all }\alpha\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{d}\}.

Equipping
C^{\infty}(\overline{U})
with the family of seminorms
\{\|\cdot\|_{K,n}\colon K\subset\overline{\Omega}\text{ compact},n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\}
defined by

\forall f\in C^{\infty}(\overline{U})\colon\,\|f\|_{K,n}=\max\{|\partial^{\alpha}f(x)|\colon x\in K,|\alpha|\leq n\}

turns
C^{\infty}(\overline{U})
into a Fréchet space on which

acts continuously. Consequently,

C^{\infty}_{P}(\overline{\Omega}):=\{g\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})\colon P(D)g=0\text{ in }\Omega\}

is a closed subspace of
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
and thus a Fréchet space.

Corollary 11
.

Let
\Omega
be a non-empty open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If
\overline{\Omega}
satisfies the strong regularity condition then, with the notation from Proposition
10
,
\iota\colon\mathscr{E}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})\to C^{\infty}_{P}(\overline{\Omega})
is an isomorphism.

Proof.

Obviously, the inclusion
\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})
is continuous. By
[
1
, Proposition 2.16]
it holds
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega})=\mathscr{E}_{\overline{\Omega}}
as vector spaces and therefore, by the Open Mapping Theorem, also as Fréchet spaces. The claim now follows from Proposition
10
.
∎

4.
Runge pairs for elliptic partial differential operators

In this short section we will evaluate Theorem
5
for elliptic partial differential operators in order to obtain an analogous result of the celebrated Lax-Malgrange Theorem for the setting of smooth Whitney jets.

Theorem 12
.

Let
P(D)
be an elliptic partial differential operator and let
F_{1}\subset F_{2}
be closed subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then
F_{1},F_{2}
is a
P
-Runge pair if and only if
F_{2}
does not contain a bounded connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
9
we only have to show that the condition on
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
is sufficient to be a
P
-Runge pair.

Thus, we assume that
F_{2}
does not contain a bounded component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
. Fix
u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}
with
\operatorname{supp}u\subset F_{2}
and such that
\operatorname{supp}\check{P}(D)u\subset F_{1}
. We shall show that
\operatorname{supp}u\subset F_{1}
which by Theorem
5
will prove the theorem. Since
P(D)
is elliptic, the same applies to
\check{P}(D)
. Due to
\check{P}(D)u=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{supp}\check{P}(D)u
,
u
is a real analytic function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{supp}\check{P}(D)u
(see
[
16
, Corollary 11.4.13]
). A fortiori,
u
is real analytic on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
. Clearly,
u
vanishes on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{supp}u
. In particular,
u
vanishes on the single(!) unbounded open connected component of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
.

Additionally, by assumption, any bounded open connected component
B
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
intersects
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{2}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{supp}u
, so
u
vanishes also on
B
, by the real analyticity of
u
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\backslash F_{1}
. Hence
u=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
, and
\operatorname{supp}u\subset F_{1}
.
∎

which contains

is bounded,

is not contained in

.

(iv)

There is a subspace

of

with

satisfying the following property.

, no bounded connected component of

is contained in
F_{2}
.

Then, conditions (i), (ii), and (iii) follow from (iv). In case

is hypoelliptic, (iii) implies (i), too.

Proof.

First, we note that (iii) trivially follows from (iv).

We continue by showing that (iv) implies (i). Thus, let
W
be as in (iv). In view of Theorem
5
we have to show that
\operatorname{supp}u\subset F_{1}
whenever
u\in\mathscr{E}^{\prime}(F_{2})
with
\operatorname{supp}\check{P}(D)u\subset F_{1}
. Thus, let us fix such

as well as

. Let

be the connected component of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1})\cap(x+W)
containing

. Then
A\setminus\operatorname{supp}u\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, while this is obvious in case

is unbounded, for bounded

(iv) implies that
A
is not contained in
F_{2}
, so
A\setminus\operatorname{supp}u\supseteq A\setminus F_{2}\neq\emptyset
.

Next, we fix

.
Since

is path connected (as a connected and open subset of

), there is a continuous curve
\alpha\colon[0,1]\to A
such that
\alpha(0)=x
and
\alpha(1)=y
. Let

\varepsilon:=\min\{\operatorname{dist}(\operatorname{im}\alpha,F_{1}),\operatorname{dist}(y,\operatorname{supp}u)\}.

Then
\varepsilon>0
,
B(y,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\operatorname{supp}u
and
\operatorname{im}\alpha+B(0,\varepsilon)\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}
, where

denotes the open Euclidean ball about

\varepsilon
etc.. For the open covering

for suitable

and
z_{k}=y
.

Let

and

Consequently,
\check{P}(D)u=0
in

.
Moreover, the convexity of

.

,

and

\displaystyle c

\displaystyle=\langle\lambda\alpha(t_{n-1})+(1-\lambda)\alpha(1)+z,N\rangle=\langle\alpha(1)+\lambda(\alpha(t_{n-1})-\alpha(1))+z,N\rangle

.

Now, since

. This completes the proof that (iv) implies (i).

Next, we prove that (iv) implies (ii). Let
W
be again as in (iv). We assume that
F_{2}
contains a bounded connected component

of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1})\cap H
where
H=\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon\langle\xi,N\rangle=c\}
(

) is some characteristic hyperplane for
P(D)
. Denoting the orthogonal complement of

by

. Consequently,

for all

. In particular, for

,

. Now, let

Finally, we additionally assume that

\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1}

. Thus, as a smooth function on

. By assumption, there is

Our next objective is to prove a kind of converse to the implication ‘‘(iv)
\Rightarrow

16

9

12

d\geq 2
, every polynomial

.

Let

and let

Additionally, let the closed subsets

\varepsilon>0

Proof of Theorem
19
.

We argue by contradiction. Let us assume that there are
c\in\mathbb{R},\varepsilon>0
and a bounded connected component
C
of
(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus F_{1})\cap(\left(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon\right)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1})

F_{2}
. By
[

(see also the proof of

and satisfies

\left[c-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\subset\operatorname{supp}v\subset\left[c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}.

Let us define

\forall\,\varphi\in\mathscr{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\colon w(\varphi):=\int_{C}v(x)\varphi(x)dx.

Clearly,

\emptyset\neq C\cap\left(\left[c-\frac{\varepsilon}{2},c+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right]\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}\right)\subset\operatorname{supp}w

For

\displaystyle\check{P}(D)w(\varphi)

\displaystyle=\int_{C}v(x)P(D)\varphi(x)dx=\int_{(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}v(x)P(D)\varphi(x)dx

\displaystyle=\int_{(c-\varepsilon,c+\varepsilon)\times\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\check{P}(D)v(x)\varphi(x)dx=0.

We shall now examine the boundary points of

(a)

For each

Obviously,

y=R(y^{\prime},\varphi(y^{\prime})+\delta(y))
for some
x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in B(x_{0}^{\prime},\varepsilon)
and
\delta(x),\delta(y)>0
.
We shall consider the following cases.

Case 1:
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
and
0\leq\delta(x),\delta(y)\leq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
.

The polygonal chain

\gamma:=(x,u(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),v(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),y)

is contained in the polygon chain defined above for boundary points, so it admits the strong regularity condition.

Case 2:
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
and
\delta(x),\delta(y)\geq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
.

Then
\gamma:=[x,y]\subset\Omega
, and the conclusion is trivially satisfied.

Case 3:
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
,
0\leq\delta(x)\leq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
and
\delta(y)\geq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
.

Here for the curve
\gamma
we choose the polygonal chain

(x,u(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),y)
which is, by similar arguments, contained in
\Omega\cup\{x\}
. Its length is

\displaystyle|\gamma|

\displaystyle=|x-u(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|+|u(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-y|\leq 2|x-u(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})|+|x-y|

\displaystyle=4C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}+|x-y|\leq 5C|x-y|^{\theta}.

Case 4:
x,y\in\Omega\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
,
\delta(x)\geq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
and
0\leq\delta(y)\leq 2C|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{\theta}
.

Analogously as in case 3.

Summing up, any pair of points
x,y\in\overline{\Omega}\cap B(x_{0},\varepsilon)
can be joined by a polygonal chain
\gamma
such that
\operatorname{im}\gamma\setminus\{x,y\}\subset\Omega
and
|\gamma|\leq 6C|x-y|^{\theta}
, as desired.
∎

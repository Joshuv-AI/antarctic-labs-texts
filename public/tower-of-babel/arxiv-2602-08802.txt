More CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures
Ted Dobson, Joy Morris, Mikhail Muzychuk, Pablo Spiga

Abstract
. We explicitly determine all CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures that have the form C × D C\times D , where C C is cyclic and D D is either a dicyclic group whose order is not divisible by 3 3 or a dihedral group. Such groups are also CI-groups with respect to graphs and digraphs.



1.
Introduction

The modern Cayley isomorphism problem began in 1967 when Ádám
[
1
]
conjectured that any two circulant graphs of order
n
are isomorphic via a group automorphism of the cyclic group of order
n
. The same problem had already been considered by Bays and Lambossy
[
3
,
12
]
in the context of designs.
Indeed, the notion of a Cayley graph extends naturally to other types of combinatorial objects by calling such an object a
Cayley object
if its automorphism group contains the regular representation of
G
on its underlying set. The corresponding question becomes:

For a fixed class of combinatorial objects
\mathcal{C}
, determine which groups have the property that any two Cayley objects on
G
are isomorphic if and only if they are isomorphic by a group automorphism of
G
.

A group with this property is called a
CI-group with respect to the class
\mathcal{C}
.

From a model-theoretic perspective, the most natural combinatorial objects are relational structures. Let
k
be a positive integer. A
k
-ary relation
on a set
X
is a subset of
X^{k}
, and a
k
-ary relational structure
on
X
is a collection of such relations. Since every
k
-ary relational structure gives rise to a
(k+1)
-ary one, any CI-group with respect to
(k+1)
-ary relational structures is also a CI-group with respect to
k
-ary structures. Thus, this establishes a hierarchy among groups. Note also that when
k=2
, the
2
-ary relational structures are simply coloured digraphs.

In
[
17
]
, Pálfy completely characterised the CI-groups with respect to 4-ary relational structures, and showed moreover that the same groups are CI-groups with respect to all
k
-ary relational structures for
4\leq k\leq n
.

Theorem 1.1

(Pálfy,
[
17
]
)
.

A group is a CI-group with respect to
4
-ary relational structures if and only if it is a CI-group with respect to
k
-ary relational structures for every
4\leq k\leq n
.

Furthermore, the groups with this property are precisely the cyclic groups
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
where
n=4
or
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
(where
\varphi
is Euler’s totient function).

The condition
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
is exactly the condition ensuring that the cyclic group is the unique group of order
n
.

In this paper we focus on the Cayley isomorphism problem for
3
-ary (that is, ternary) relational structures. The cyclic groups appearing in Pálfy’s theorem are all CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures.
Since our attention will be on the nonabelian candidates, we record the relevant consequences of the main result of
[
9
]
as improved in
[
11
]
explicitly.

Corollary 1.2
.

If
G
is a nonabelian CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures, then:

•

all nontrivial odd-order Sylow subgroups of
G
have prime order;

•

the Sylow
2
-subgroups of
G
are isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
,
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
, or
Q_{8}
;

•

G=U\times V
, where:

–

\gcd(|U|,|V|)=1
;

–

U
is cyclic of odd order
n
with
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
; and

–

for some odd
m
with
\gcd(nm,\varphi(nm))=1
, the group
V
is either:

*

Q_{8}
(with
m=1
),

*

dihedral of order
2m
, or

*

dicyclic of order
4m
.

Moreover, if
V=Q_{8}
or
V
is dicyclic and
p\mid n
is prime, then
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

Prior to this paper, the only known nonabelian CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures were the dihedral groups of order
2p
with
p
prime, as shown by Babai in
[
2
]
.

Our first main result (Corollary
6.2
) shows that whenever
V
is dihedral and the conditions of Corollary
1.2
are satisfied, the group
G
is a CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures. Since this also implies that such groups are CI-groups with respect to binary relational structures, this strengthens the known results for this family with respect to coloured digraphs. Previously, only the dihedral groups in this family, together with direct products of order less than
32
, were known to have the CI property.

Our second main result (Corollary
7.7
) shows that if
3\nmid m
, then whenever
V
is dicyclic and the conditions of Corollary
1.2
are satisfied, the group
G
is a CI-group if and only if all prime divisors of
m
are congruent to
3\pmod{4}
(note that Corollary
1.2
already imposes this condition on the prime divisors of
n
).

We expect that our main tool (Theorem
5.4
) will also aid in resolving the remaining cases, but we leave this for future work.

For nonabelian groups, this leaves unresolved the case where
V
is dicyclic and
3\mid|V|
, and the case where
V=Q_{8}
.

Finally, some of our results extend previously known results for CI-groups with respect to graphs and digraphs. In particular, several of the nonabelian groups we identify as CI-groups for ternary relational structures—specifically those with central cyclic subgroups of odd order—were not previously known to be CI-groups for digraphs. This implication is now immediate.

2.
Preliminaries

Given a set
X
, we denote by
S_{X}
and

the
symmetric
and
alternating
groups on
X
, respectively. If
X=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we write

and

for the corresponding groups.

If a group

acts on a set

and

, then
G_{Y}
and

denote the pointwise and setwise stabilizers of
Y
in
G
, respectively. If
g(Y)=Y
, then

denotes the permutation of
Y

induced
by
g\in G
; that is,
g^{Y}\colon Y\to Y
with
g^{Y}(y)=g(y)
for all
y\in Y
. We set
G_{\{Y\}}^{Y}=\{g^{Y}:g\in G_{\{Y\}}\}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a set, let
G\leq S_{X}
be transitive, and let
\emptyset\neq B\subseteq X
. We call
B
a
block
of
G
if, whenever
g\in G
, either
g(B)\cap B=\emptyset
or
g(B)=B
. If
B=\{x\}
for some
x\in X
, or
B=X
, then
B
is a
trivial block
. Note that if
B
is a block of
G
, then so is
g(B)
for every
g\in G
. The set
{\mathcal{B}}=\{g(B):g\in G\}
is a partition of
X
, called a
block system of
G
. If
G
has a nontrivial block, then
G
is said to be
imprimitive
; otherwise,
G
is
primitive
.

Definition 2.2
.

The set of orbits of a normal subgroup of a transitive permutation group
G
is a block system of
G
, and such a block system is called a
normal block system
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
be a transitive permutation group with a block system
{\mathcal{B}}
.
Each
g\in G
induces a permutation
g^{\mathcal{B}}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
, defined by

g^{\mathcal{B}}(B)=B^{\prime}
if and only if
g(B)=B^{\prime}
.
Set

G^{\mathcal{B}}=\{g^{\mathcal{B}}:g\in G\}
.
This yields a homomorphism
G\to S_{\mathcal{B}}
given by
g\mapsto g^{\mathcal{B}}
, whose kernel we denote by
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
.
Thus
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})=\{g\in G:g(B)=B\text{ for all }B\in{\mathcal{B}}\}
,
and
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
is the setwise stabilizer of each block of
{\mathcal{B}}
.

A block system
\mathcal{B}
is normal for a group
G
if and only if
{\rm fix}_{G}(\mathcal{B})
acts transitively on each block
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
be transitive and have block systems
{\mathcal{B}}
and
{\mathcal{C}}
. If, whenever
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, there exists
C\in{\mathcal{C}}
such that
B\subseteq C
, we write
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
. If
B\subset C
, we write
{\mathcal{B}}\prec{\mathcal{C}}
. We say that
{\mathcal{B}}
is
minimal
if it is minimal, with respect to the partial order
\preceq
, among all block systems distinct from
\{\{x\}:x\in X\}
.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
n
be an integer and

be the prime-power decomposition of

. Define
\Omega\colon{\mathbb{N}}\to{\mathbb{N}}
by
\Omega(n)=\sum_{i=1}^{r}a_{i}
. A transitive group
G\leq S_{n}
is

-step imprimitive
if
G
has a sequence of block systems
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{1}\prec\cdots\prec{\mathcal{B}}_{m}
. If, in addition, each
{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
is normal, we say that

is
normally
m
-step imprimitive
.

When
m=\Omega(n)
,
{\mathcal{B}}_{0}
consists of singleton sets,
{\mathcal{B}}_{m}=\{1,\ldots,n\}
, and if
B_{i+1}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{i+1}
and
B_{i}\in{\mathcal{B}}_{i}
, then
|B_{i+1}|/|B_{i}|
is prime,
0\leq i\leq m-1
.

We now define the class of groups with which we will be concerned in this paper.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
n
be an odd square-free integer. Denote by
{\mathcal{R}}_{n}
the set consisting of the following groups:

(2.1)

\begin{array}[]{rl}(a)&R={\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\times R_{2}\text{ where }R_{2}\in\{1,{\mathbb{Z}}_{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{2},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{3},{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}^{4},{\mathbb{Z}}_{4},Q_{8}\};\\[3.0pt]
(b)&R={\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\rtimes\langle y\rangle\text{ with }o(y)\in\{2,4,8\},\,y\notin\mathsf{Z}(R)\text{ and }y^{2}\in\mathsf{Z}(R),\end{array}

where

denotes the center of

.
Denote by
{\mathcal{R}}
the union
\bigcup_{n}{\mathcal{R}}_{n}
, where

runs over all odd square-free integers.

In part
(b)
, the action of

by conjugation on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is an arbitrary automorphism of order two. Note that
\mathcal{R}
is closed under taking subgroups and quotient groups.

The family
\mathcal{R}
includes all the putative nonabelian CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures (according to Corollary
1.2
). It also includes additional groups, since, for instance, the condition
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
is omitted. Our reason for including these extra groups is that we believe our results are likely to be useful in determining the CI-status with respect to graphs and digraphs for several families of groups whose status is still undetermined, but which are not CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
X
be a set and let
R\in{\mathcal{R}}
act regularly on
X
.
Let
{\mathcal{B}}
be a block system of

. Then, for any
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
,
R_{\{B\}}^{B}
belongs to
{\mathcal{R}}
.
Moreover, the induced group

on
{\mathcal{B}}
contains a regular subgroup belonging to
{\mathcal{R}}
.

Proof.

Since
R
acts regularly on
X
,
R_{\{B\}}
has order
|B|
, and
R_{\{B\}}^{B}\cong R_{\{B\}}
as abstract groups.
As
{\mathcal{R}}
is closed under taking subgroups, we have
R_{\{B\}}^{B}\in{\mathcal{R}}
.

If
R_{\{B\}}
is normal in

, then the induced action

is a regular action of the quotient group
R/R_{\{B\}}
.
The conclusion follows, since
{\mathcal{R}}
is also closed under taking quotient groups.

If
R_{\{B\}}
is not normal in
R
, then
R\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\rtimes\langle y\rangle
as in Definition
2.6

(b)
.
As every subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\times\langle y^{2}\rangle
is normal in

, the subgroup

is conjugate to
K\rtimes\langle y\rangle
,
where

is the unique subgroup of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
of order
|B|/o(y)
.
In this case,
{\rm fix}_{R}({\mathcal{B}})=K\times\langle y^{2}\rangle
, and hence

({\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\times\langle y^{2}\rangle)/{\rm fix}_{R}({\mathcal{B}})

acts regularly on

and is isomorphic to
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n/|K|}\in{\mathcal{R}}
.
∎

3.
A generalization of a result of Pálfy

Pálfy showed in
[
17
]
that a group
G
of order
n
is a CI-group with respect to every class of combinatorial objects if and only if
n=4
or
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
. The tool that Pálfy used to reduce the primitive case to the imprimitive case was a result which showed that, with the exception of
A_{n}
or
S_{n}
, for a
2
-transitive group of square-free degree
n
there is a prime divisor
p
of
n
for which
G
has a cyclic Sylow
p
-subgroup. Our goal in this section is to prove an analogue of Pálfy’s result for the degrees under consideration in this paper. Our result (Proposition
3.5
) is not quite as ‘clean’ as Pálfy’s result, as there are a few small exceptions to the general result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n
be a positive integer with
n=2^{e}m
, where
0\leq e\leq 4
and
m
is odd and square-free, and let
G\leq S_{n}
be a primitive group. If
G
contains a semiregular cyclic subgroup with two orbits or a regular abelian subgroup, then
G
is
2
-transitive unless one of the following holds:

•

n=p
is prime and
G<{\rm AGL}_{1}(p)
; or

•

n=10
and
A_{5}\leq G\leq S_{5}
; or

•

n=16
.

Proof.

Assume first that
n
has a unique prime divisor, say
p
.
If
p
is odd or
n=p=2
, then
n=p
, and by Burnside’s Theorem
[
7
, Theorem 3.5B]
, either
G<{\rm AGL}_{1}(p)
or
G
is
2
-transitive
(the statement of Theorem 3.5B also includes the possibility

, but this group is
2
-transitive).
If
n>p=2
, then
n=4,8
, or
16
.
Since every primitive group of degree
4
or
8
is
2
-transitive, we are left with

, which is an exception.

Now assume that
n
is divisible by two primes, at least one of which is odd—call it

.
If
G
contains a regular abelian subgroup, then its Sylow
p
-subgroups are cyclic, and by
[
19
, Theorem 25.4]
,
G
is
2
-transitive.
Otherwise, by hypothesis,

has a semiregular cyclic subgroup

.
As

is semiregular, each of its orbits has length
k=n/2
.
All primitive groups containing a cyclic subgroup with two orbits were classified in
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
.
After eliminating groups listed in
[
14
, Theorem 3.3]
whose degree is a prime power or a square
(impossible since

), as well as
2
-transitive groups,
the only remaining possibility that contains a cyclic subgroup with two orbits of equal length is

and

.
∎

Our next result will rely on Zsigmondy’s Theorem, which we now state.

Theorem 3.2

(Zsigmondy’s Theorem)
.

Let

. Then there exists a prime

such that

•

•

Definition 3.3

A prime

Definition 3.4

For a group

In our generalisation of Pàlfy’s result, we show that, in all but a small number of cases, there exists a Sylow

4.
Some Tools

Definition 4.1
.

Let
X
be a set. For a permutation group
H\leq S_{X}
, the
support
of
H
, denoted
{\rm supp}(H)
,
is the set of all
x\in X
for which there exists
h\in H
with
h(x)\neq x
.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
and
Y\subseteq X
be
G
-invariant.
We denote by
\mathsf{Orb}(G,Y)
the set of
G
-orbits on
Y
.

The next lemma is a variation of
[
10
, Lemma 2.2]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
G\leq S_{X}
be transitive with a normal block system
{\mathcal{B}}
. Abbreviate
N={\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
and fix an arbitrary
B_{0}\in\mathcal{B}
. If
T={\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B_{0}})
is a transitive nonabelian simple group, then

(1)

{\mathsf{Soc}}(N)=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{k}
, where all simple factors are isomorphic to
T
. The sets
C_{i}:={\rm supp}(T_{i}),1\leq i\leq k
form a block system
{\mathcal{C}}
of
G
with
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
;

(2)

for any
T_{i}
and any block
B\in\mathcal{B}
contained in
C_{i}
, the point-wise stabilizers
N_{B}
and
N_{C_{i}}
are equal and
N_{C_{i}}\cap T_{i}=1
;

(3)

suppose that
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N)
contains two isomorphic semiregular subgroups
U
and
V
. If any two semiregular subgroups of
T
isomorphic to
U^{B_{0}}
are conjugate in
T
, then there exists
\delta\in{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
such that
\mathsf{Orb}(U,X)=\mathsf{Orb}(V^{\delta},X)
;

(4)

an element
g\in N
belongs to
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N)
if and only if
g^{B}\in{\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B})
holds for each
B\in\mathcal{B}
.

Proof.

Since
G
acts transitively on
{\mathcal{B}}
and
N\unlhd G
, the group
N^{B_{0}}
is permutation isomorphic to
N^{B}
for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
by
[
7
, Theorem 1.6A (ii)]
. In particular,
\mathsf{Soc}(N^{B_{0}})\cong\mathsf{Soc}(N^{B})
for each
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
.

Part (
1
). Since for any
B\in\mathcal{B}
the map
g\mapsto g^{B},g\in N
, is a group homomorphism,
\mathsf{Soc}(N)^{B}\unlhd\mathsf{Soc}(N^{B})\cong T
. Therefore either
\mathsf{Soc}(N)^{B}
is trivial or
\mathsf{Soc}(N)^{B}=\mathsf{Soc}(N^{B})
. The first case is impossible. Thus
\mathsf{Soc}(N)^{B}=\mathsf{Soc}(N^{B})=T
.

Every subgroup
L\leq N
is a subdirect product of its projections
L^{B},B\in{\mathcal{B}}
. Therefore
\mathsf{Soc}(N)
is a subdirect subgroup of
T^{m}
where
m=|{\mathcal{B}}|
. Since
T
is a simple group,
\mathsf{Soc}(N)\cong T^{k}
for some
k\leq m
. Thus
\mathsf{Soc}(N)=T_{1}\times\cdots\times T_{k}
, where
T_{1},\ldots,T_{k}
are normal subgroups of
\mathsf{Soc}(N)
isomorphic to
T
. It remains to show that
{\rm supp}(T_{i})\cap{\rm supp}(T_{j})=\emptyset
whenever
i\neq j
.
Indeed, if
{\rm supp}(T_{i})\cap{\rm supp}(T_{j})
is nonempty, then there exists

B\in{\mathcal{B}}
such that
T_{i}^{B}\neq 1\neq T_{j}^{B}
. Then
T^{2}\cong(T_{i}\times T_{j})^{B}\leq\mathsf{Soc}(N)^{B}\cong T
, a contradiction.
Thus
{\mathcal{C}}=\{{\rm supp}(T_{i}):1\leq i\leq k\}
is a block system of
G
with
{\mathcal{B}}\preceq{\mathcal{C}}
.

Part (
2
). To ease the proof we’ll assume that
i=1
. The inclusion
N_{C_{1}}\leq N_{B}
is an immediate consequence of
B\subseteq C_{1}
. To prove the converse inequality
N_{C_{1}}\geq N_{B}
it is sufficient to show that
N_{B}=N_{B^{\prime}}
for any block
B^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}
contained in
C_{1}
.

Notice that both
N_{B}
and
N_{B^{\prime}}
are normal in
N
. Assume towards a contradiction that
N_{B}
and
N_{B^{\prime}}
are distinct. Then
(N_{B^{\prime}})^{B}
is non-trivial.
It follows from
N_{B^{\prime}}\trianglelefteq N
that

1\neq(N_{B^{\prime}})^{B}\trianglelefteq N^{B}
. But the socle of
N^{B}
is a simple group
T_{1}^{B}\cong T_{1}
. Therefore
T_{1}^{B}\leq(N_{B^{\prime}})^{B}
. Since
T_{1}
is nonabelian, we conclude that
[T_{1}^{B},(N_{B^{\prime}})^{B}]\neq 1
implying
[T_{1},N_{B^{\prime}}]\neq 1
. Both
T_{1}
and
N_{B^{\prime}}
are normal in
N
. Therefore
[T_{1},N_{B^{\prime}}]\leq T_{1}\cap N_{B^{\prime}}
implying
T_{1}\cap N_{B^{\prime}}\neq 1
. The minimality of
T_{1}
implies that
T_{1}\leq N_{B^{\prime}}
which, in turn, yields
{\rm supp}(T_{1})\cap B^{\prime}=\emptyset
, a contradiction.

Part (
3
). For
1\leq i\leq k
, choose a block of
{\mathcal{B}}
contained in
C_{i}\in{\mathcal{C}}
, we denote it as
B_{i}
. Let
g_{i}\in G
satisfy
g_{i}(B_{i})=B_{i}
. Then
g_{i}(U^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}
and
g_{i}(V^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}
are semiregular subgroups of
g_{i}(T_{i}^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}=T
isomorphic to
U^{B_{i}}
, and so there exists
t_{i}\in T
with
g_{i}(U^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}=t_{i}g_{i}(V^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}^{-1}
. Then
U^{B_{i}}=g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}g_{i}(V^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}^{-1}g_{i}
. Let
\delta_{i}\in T_{i}
be the unique element such that
\delta_{i}^{B_{i}}=(g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}g_{i})^{B_{i}}
.
Then
U^{B_{i}}=g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}g_{i}(V^{B_{i}})g_{i}^{-1}t_{i}^{-1}g_{i}=(\delta_{i}^{-1}V\delta_{i})^{B_{i}}
. As
C_{i}={\rm supp}(T_{i})
, this implies that
U^{C_{i}}=(\delta_{i}^{-1}V\delta_{i})^{C_{i}}
,
1\leq i\leq k
. Set
\delta=\delta_{1}\cdots\delta_{k}
. It follows from
{\rm supp}(\delta_{i})\subseteq C_{i}
that
\delta^{C_{i}}=\delta_{i}^{C_{i}}
. Therefore,
U^{C_{i}}=(\delta^{-1}V\delta)^{C_{i}}
implying
\mathsf{Orb}(U,C_{i})=\mathsf{Orb}(V^{\delta},C_{i})
. Now the statement follows from
\mathsf{Orb}(U,X)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{Orb}(U,C_{i})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}\mathsf{Orb}(V^{\delta},C_{i})=\mathsf{Orb}(V^{\delta},X)
.

Part (
4
). The “only if” part follows from the equality
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N)^{B}={\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B})
shown in the proof of part (
1
). It remains to prove the “if” part. If
n^{B}\in{\mathsf{Soc}}({N^{B}})=T_{i}^{B}
and

n^{B^{\prime}}\in{\mathsf{Soc}}({N^{B^{\prime}}})=T_{i}^{B^{\prime}}

for arbitrary
B,B^{\prime}\subseteq C_{i}
, then
nt^{-1}\in N_{B}=N_{B^{\prime}}\ni n{t^{\prime}}^{-1}
for some
t,t^{\prime}\in T_{i}
. Therefore,
gt=n=g^{\prime}t^{\prime}
for some
g\in N_{B}=N_{B^{\prime}}\ni g^{\prime}
. This yields us
t^{\prime}t^{-1}\in N_{B}=N_{C_{i}}
which, in turn, implies
t^{\prime}t^{-1}\in T_{i}\cap N_{C_{i}}=1
, Therefore
t^{\prime}=t
implying that there exists a unique
t_{i}\in T_{i}
with
n^{B}=t_{i}^{B}
for any block
B\in\mathcal{B}
contained in
C_{i}
. Thus we proved that for any
i=1,...,k
there exists
t_{i}\in T_{i}
with
nt_{i}^{-1}\in N_{C_{i}}
. We claim that
n=t_{1}\cdots t_{k}\in{\mathsf{Soc}}(N)
. Since for each pair
i\neq j
we have
t_{i}\in N_{C_{j}}
, we conclude that

nt_{1}^{-1}\cdots t_{k}^{-1}=nt_{i}^{-1}t_{1}^{-1}\cdots t_{i-1}^{-1}t_{i+1}^{-1}\cdots t_{k}^{-1}\in N_{C_{i}}.

Since the above inclusion holds for each
i=1,...,k
, we conclude

nt_{1}^{-1}\cdots t_{k}^{-1}\in\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}N_{C_{i}}=1.

as desired.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Let

be one of the groups

,
M_{12}
, or
M_{24}

(in the latter two cases

.

.

If

has

{\bf C}_{S_{k}}(R_{i})
contains odd permutations, implying that

and

are conjugate by an even permutation.
This completes the proof for

.

For

(

,

), the semiregular subgroups are cyclic of order

,
generated by permutations of cycle type

, all conjugate in

(see
[
5
]
or
[
18
]
).
For

(

,

all elements of cycle type

(see
[
18
]
).
∎

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
R
and
T
be isomorphic transitive groups such that
R
and
T
have normal semiregular
p
-subgroups
R_{p}
and
T_{p}
, respectively. Suppose that

has a block system
{\mathcal{B}}
such that
R_{p},T_{p}\leq{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
and both
(R_{p})^{B}
and
(T_{p})^{B}
have the same orbits as a Sylow

-subgroup of
{\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})^{B}
,

. Then there exists

\langle R,T^{\delta}\rangle
has a normal block system

.

Proof.

Since
R_{p}
and
T_{p}
are
p
-subgroups of

\delta\in N
such that

and

are contained in a Sylow

-subgroup of

. Let

. It follows from

(R_{p})^{B}\leq P^{B}
. By assumption

has the same orbits as a Sylow

\mathsf{Orb}((R_{p})^{B},B)=\mathsf{Orb}(P^{B},B)

\mathsf{Orb}(((T^{\delta})_{p})^{B},B)=\mathsf{Orb}(P^{B},B)

\mathsf{Orb}((T^{\delta})_{p},X)=\mathsf{Orb}(R_{p},X)

.
∎

Lemma 4.6
.

and

with blocks of size

. Moreover, assume that

Let

. By minimality of
\mathcal{B}
, the group

is a primitive group of degree

containing the regular subgroup
(R_{1})_{\{B\}}^{B}
.
By examining the primitive groups of degree

,

, as before. Let

5.
The Main Result

We will often need to refer to a Sylow
p
-subgroup

of a group
R
.
For convenience, we denote such a subgroup by
R_{p}
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
R
and
T
be isomorphic regular subgroups of
S_{2^{e}n}
belonging to
\mathcal{R}
with
0\leq e\leq 4
and
n
an odd square-free integer,
let
G=\langle R,T\rangle
and let
\mathcal{B}
be a minimal block system of
G
and let
k
be the cardinality of the elements of
\mathcal{B}
. If
k
is divisible by an odd prime
p
, then there exists
g\in G
such that
\langle{R,T^{g}}\rangle
has a normal block system with block size
p
.

Proof.

We set
N={\rm fix}_{G}({\mathcal{B}})
and let
p
be an odd prime divisor of
k
. As
R\in\mathcal{R}
, it is readily seen that
R_{p}\triangleleft R
. Since
R
acts transitively on
{\mathcal{B}}
and
R_{p}\triangleleft R
, all orbits of
R_{p}
on
{\mathcal{B}}
have cardinality
1
or
p
. As
|{\mathcal{B}}|
is coprime to
p
,
R_{p}
acts trivially on
{\mathcal{B}}
, i.e.
R_{p}\leq N
. Analogously,
T_{p}\leq N
.

By the minimality of
\mathcal{B}
, the group
G_{\{B\}}^{B}
,
B\in{\mathcal{B}}
, is a primitive subgroup of
S_{B}
and contains
(R_{\{B\}})^{B}
, which is a regular subgroup that by Proposition
2.7
is in
\mathcal{R}
. Thus either
R_{\{B\}}^{B}
is an abelian regular subgroup, or it contains a cyclic semiregular subgroup having two orbits. Applying Proposition
3.5
to
G_{\{B\}}^{B}
, and recalling that
k
is divisible by an odd prime, we obtain that either there exists an odd prime
p\mid k
such that
(R_{p})^{B}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G^{B}
, or
{\mathsf{Soc}}(G^{B})\cong A_{k},\;M_{12},\;M_{24}
,
where in the second case

and

, and in the third case

and
p=3
.

Assume that
(R_{p})^{B}
is a Sylow
p
-subgroup of
G^{B}
. Then
(R_{p})^{B}
and
(T_{p})^{B}
are Sylow
p
-subgroups of
N^{B}
. By Lemma
4.5
, there exists
g\in G=\langle{R,T}\rangle
such that
\mathsf{Orb}(R_{p},X)
is a normal block system of
\langle{R,T^{g}}\rangle
with block size
p
.

In the remaining case
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B})={\mathsf{Soc}}(G^{B})\cong A_{k},M_{12},M_{24}
. Let
p
be an odd prime divisor of
k
.
The subgroups
R_{p}
and
T_{p}
are semiregular and their images
(R_{p})^{B},(T_{p})^{B}
are contained in
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B})\cong A_{k},M_{12},M_{24}
. By Lemma
4.4
,
(R_{p})^{B},(T_{p})^{B}
are conjugate in
{\mathsf{Soc}}(N^{B})
. From Lemma
4.3
(
3
), there exists
\delta\in N
with
\mathcal{P}=\mathsf{Orb}(R_{p},X)=\mathsf{Orb}((T_{p})^{\delta},X)
. Since
(T_{p})^{\delta}=(T^{\delta})_{p}
,
the orbit partition
\mathcal{P}
is
\langle{R,T^{\delta}}\rangle
-invariant. Thus
\mathcal{P}
is a normal block system of
\langle R,T^{\delta}\rangle
with block size
p
.
∎

After some definitions, we are ready for the main result of this section.

Definition 5.2
.

.

Definition 5.3
.

.

.

Theorem 5.4
.

Let

, with

and

Assume that

act on
G
by

x^{(g,h)}=g^{-1}xh
.
The stabilizer of the identity element is the diagonal subgroup
D=\{(g,g):g\in G\}
,
and the kernel of the action is

\{(z,z):z\in Z(G)\}.

The image of
G\times G
in
S_{G}
under this action is again
G_{L}\cdot G_{R}
;
the image of
G\times\{e\}
is
G_{L}
, and the image of
\{e\}\times G
is
G_{R}
.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
F
be a Frobenius group with abelian complement
H
. Assume that the natural action of
F
on the right cosets of
H
is
2
-closed. Then the inner holomorph of
F
is
3
-closed provided that
|H|>2
.

Proof.

Let
K
be the Frobenius kernel of
F
and let
G
be the inner holomorph of
F
.
From the proof of
[
16
, Theorem 2.4]
, we have

(G^{(3)})_{e}\leq(G_{e})^{(2)},

where
X^{(3)}
and
X^{(2)}
denote the
3
- and
2
-closures of a permutation group
X
, and where
e
is the identity element of
F
.

The group
G_{e}=\mathrm{Inn}(F)
is isomorphic to
F
and acts on
F
by conjugation.
Since
H
is abelian, the set
F\setminus K
splits into a disjoint union of
|H|-1
orbits of
\mathrm{Inn}(F)
, namely the nontrivial cosets
hK
with
h\neq e
.
On the subset
K
, the group
\mathrm{Inn}(F)
has one orbit of size
1
(namely
\{e\}
) and
\frac{|K|-1}{|H|}
orbits of the form

k^{H}=\{h^{-1}kh\,:\,h\in H\},k\in K
.

The action of
\mathrm{Inn}(F)
on each coset
hK=Kh
with
h\neq e
is equivalent to the action of
F
on the
H
-cosets, and is therefore
2
-closed.
Consequently,

(\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{(2)})^{hK}\leq(\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{hK})^{(2)}=\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{hK},

and hence
(\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{(2)})^{hK}=\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{hK}
.
To show that
G
is
3
-closed, it therefore suffices to prove that
(G^{(3)})_{e}
acts faithfully on
hK
.

Let
g\in(G^{(3)})_{e}\leq\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{(2)}
act trivially on
hK
.
Choose
a\in H\setminus\{e,h\}
.
The binary relation

R_{h,a}=\{(h^{k},a^{k}):k\in K\}

is a
2
-orbit of
\mathrm{Inn}(F)
and defines a bijection between the
\mathrm{Inn}(F)
-orbits
hK
and
aK
.
Since
R_{h,a}
is
g
-invariant and
hK\subseteq{\rm fix}(g)
, we deduce that
aK
is also fixed pointwise by
g
.
Thus any element that fixes
hK
must act trivially on
F\setminus K
.

Because left multiplication by
a
sends
K
to
aK
, the permutation

g_{1}=a_{L}\,g\,a_{L}^{-1}

fixes
F\setminus aK
pointwise.
Hence
g_{1}\in(G^{(3)})_{e}\leq\mathrm{Inn}(F)^{(2)}
and
{\rm fix}(g_{1})\supseteq hK
.
It follows that
g_{1}
acts trivially on
F\setminus K
, and therefore
g_{1}
is the identity.
Consequently
g
is the identity as well.
∎

Corollary 7.2
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
\omega\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{*}
be a primitive
n
th root of unity for some integer
n
with
2<n<p-1
.
Let
F={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes\langle\omega\rangle
be the Frobenius group of order
np
.
Then the inner holomorph of
F
is
3
-closed, and
F
is not a CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures.

Proof.

The group
F
, in its natural action on
p
points, is
2
-closed since
n<p-1
.
By Proposition
7.1
, its inner holomorph is
3
-closed because
n>2
.
Finally, the inner holomorph contains two non-conjugate regular subgroups isomorphic to
F
, namely
F_{L}
and
F_{R}
.
∎

Corollary 7.3
.

Let
p
be a prime, let
n
be a divisor of
p-1
with
2<n<p-1
, and let
\alpha\colon{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}^{*}
be a non-injective group homomorphism. Assume that
|{\rm Ker}(\alpha)|
is even whenever
n
is even. Then the semidirect product
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is not a CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures.

Proof.

Let
\omega
be a primitive
n
th root of unity in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
.
Without loss of generality we may assume that

\alpha\colon\langle\omega\rangle\rightarrow\langle\omega\rangle

is an endomorphism of
\langle\omega\rangle
, that is,

\alpha(\omega^{i})=\omega^{ai}
for some
a\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
It follows from the assumptions that
a\notin{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{*}
, and that
a
is even whenever
n
is even.
These conditions ensure the existence of
b\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}^{*}
such that
a-b
is invertible in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.

Consider the Frobenius group

F={\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}=\{(x,\omega^{i})\,:\,i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},\ x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\}
.
By Corollary
7.2
, the group
F_{L}\cdot F_{R}\cong F\times F
is
3
-closed.
Write elements of
F\times F
as
[(x,\omega^{i}),(y,\omega^{j})]
with
x,y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
and
i,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
In this notation we have:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}F_{L}&=&\{[(x,\omega^{i}),(0,1)]\mid x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\},\\
F_{R}&=&\{[(0,1),(y,\omega^{j})]\mid y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ j\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\},\\
(F_{L}\cdot F_{R})_{e}=\mathsf{Inn}(F)&=&\{[(x,\omega^{i}),(x,\omega^{i})]\mid x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}.\end{array}

It is easy to check that the subsets

\displaystyle G_{1}

\displaystyle=\{[(x,\omega^{ai}),(0,\omega^{bi})]:x\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\},

\displaystyle G_{2}

\displaystyle=\{[(0,\omega^{bi}),(y,\omega^{ai})]:y\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{p},\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}

are two regular subgroups of
F_{L}\cdot F_{R}
, each isomorphic to
G
.
Regularity follows from the fact that

G_{1}\cap\mathsf{Inn}(F)=\{[(0,\omega^{ai}),(0,\omega^{bi})]:ai=bi,\ i\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}

has size
1
, and likewise for
G_{2}
.
Moreover,
G_{1}
and
G_{2}
are not conjugate in
F_{L}\cdot F_{R}
because their Sylow
p
-subgroups
(G_{i})_{p}
,
i=1,2
, are normal in
F_{L}\cdot F_{R}
and are distinct.
∎

Remark 7.4
.

If
\alpha
is the trivial homomorphism, then the group
G
in Corollary
7.3
is a direct product. If
\alpha
is injective, then
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes_{\alpha}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is a Frobenius group of order
pn
.

As an immediate consequence of Corollary
7.3
we obtain the following.

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
p>5
be a prime with
p\equiv 1\pmod{4}
. Then the groups
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4p}
, the Frobenius group
F_{4p}
of order
4p
, and the dicyclic group of order
4p
are not CI-groups with respect to ternary relational structures.

Example 7.6
.

There exists a unique permutation group of degree 20, up to permutation equivalence, that is 3-closed and that contains more than one conjugacy class of subgroups isomorphic to
H=\langle{x,y:x^{5}=y^{4},x^{y}=x^{-1}}\rangle
. This group has order
400
and has exactly
2
conjugacy classes of such subgroups, and is group
102
in the Magma list of transitive groups of degree
20
.

Corollary 7.7
.

Let
n=km
be odd with
3\nmid m
. Then
{\mathbb{Z}}_{k}\times({\mathbb{Z}}_{m}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{4})
is a CI-group with respect to ternary relational structures if and only if
\gcd(n,\varphi(n))=1
and whenever
p
divides
n
is prime,
p
satisfies
p\not\equiv 1\pmod{4}
.

Proof.

The sufficiency of the condition
p\not\equiv 1\ \pmod{4}
follows from Theorem
6.3
. To prove the necessity we need to show that for each prime
p\equiv 1\ \pmod{4}
the group
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
is not CI with respect to ternary relational structures.

If
{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
acts trivially on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}
, then
{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\rtimes{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}\cong{\mathbb{Z}}_{p}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{4}
and the result follows from
[
11
, Theorem 4.2]
. If the action is non-trivial, then the result follows from Corollary
7.5
when
p>5
and Example
7.6
when
p=5
.
∎

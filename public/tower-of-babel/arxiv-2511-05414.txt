Envelope of truncated tubes and special domains in higher complex dimensions
Suprokash Hazra

Abstract
. In this article, we introduce special domains and discuss the geometry of these domains, which includes showing that every pseudoconvex truncated tube domain is a special domain. Next, we prove a theorem for the envelope of special domains in ℂ n ​ ( n ≥ 2 ) \mathbb{C}^{n}\penalty\ (n\geq 2) . Our theorem on special domains is a generalization of a recent result by Jarnicki-Pflug on the envelope of holomorphy of truncated tube domains in ℂ n \mathbb{C}^{n} . We also establish a result on schlichtness in complex dimension 2, and conclude this article with two higher-dimensional generalizations of the same result by Jarnicki-Pflug mentioned above.

1.
Introduction

For a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
, its envelope of holomorphy
\mathcal{E}(D)
is known to be a maximal Riemann domain over
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with projection
\pi:\mathcal{E}(D)\to\mathbb{C}^{n}
, such that there is a canonical holomorphic embedding
\iota_{D}:D\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}(D)
satisfying the lifting property
\pi\circ\iota_{D}=\mbox{id}_{D}
, and that for all
f\in\mathcal{O}(D)
, the map
\pi^{*}f:=f\circ(\pi|_{\iota_{D}(D)})
has a holomorphic extension to
\mathcal{E}(D)
. For further details, see
[
9
]
and the references therein. Due to a result of Oka,
\mathcal{E}(D)
is known to be a Stein manifold. In general, the envelope
\mathcal{E}(D)
of a domain
D
need not be schlicht (that is,
\#\pi^{-1}(z)\neq 1
) and thus may be multisheeted over
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Moreover, in general, it is neither always evident to determine the number of sheets of the envelope of a domain, nor always simple to comprehend how the geometry of a given domain relates to the geometry of its envelope. Nevertheless, a classical theorem due to Bochner (
[
2
]
) shows that for a domain
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the envelope of holomorphy of the
complete Bochner tube

X+i\mathbb{R}^{n}
is the tube
\textsf{ch}(X)+i\mathbb{R}^{n}
over the convex hull
\textsf{ch}(X)
, and hence the envelope is schlicht. Recall that a domain is called a
truncated tube domain
(or simply a
tube domain
) if it is of the form
X+iY
, where
X,Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
are arbitrary domains. Although the geometry of pseudoconvex tube domains is relatively well understood (see
[
17
]
,
[
18
]
), that of non-pseudoconvex domains remains much undeveloped. Nevertheless, recently, the following result for the non-pseudoconvex truncated tube was obtained.

Theorem 1.1

(Jarnicki-Pflug,
[
10
]
)
.

For
0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
and
r_{3}>0
, the envelope of holomorphy of the truncated tube domain

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<||x||<r_{2}\}+i\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n};\penalty\ n\geq 2,

is given by

\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||x||<r_{2},||y||<r_{3},||y||^{2}<||x||^{2}-(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}.

In particular, the envelope is schlicht.

Recently, S. Hazra and E. Porten proved that the envelope of truncated tube domains need not be schlicht (
[
7
]
). To be specific, the authors have constructed a truncated tube domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
diffeomorphic to the
4
-ball such that the envelope has infinitely many sheets lying uniformly over
a neighborhood of a circle embedded in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. In addition, they established a sufficient condition in

\mathbb{C}^{2},
which yields the following theorem.

Theorem 1.2

(Hazra-Porten,
[
7
]
)
.

If
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex domain with finitely many strictly convex holes, and
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex domain, then the envelope of holomorphy of the truncated tube
X+iY
is schlicht.

In
[
7
, Remark 4.2.(a)]
, the authors also mentioned that it is unknown if stronger results can be obtained for truncated Bochner tubes. In particular, this means it is uncertain if Theorem
1.2
continues to hold in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
. We would like to address this as a modified question:

Question 1.3
.

Let
X,Y
be two convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
. Denote
K=K_{x}+ibY
, where
K_{x}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a compact convex set. Does the envelope of holomorphy of the truncated tube
(X\setminus K_{x})+iY
always schlicht?

Observe that in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, proving the pseudoconvexity in Theorem
1.2
follows immediately from [Lemma
2.6
, Rosay-Stout], which doesn’t always hold in higher dimensions. However, with an additional assumption of
b\widehat{K}
being Levi-flat, Theorem
1.2
extends to
\mathbb{C}^{n}
for
n\geq 3
(see
[
13
, Theorem 3.1]
). It is worth mentioning that in Theorem
1.1
, the condition of
b\widehat{K}
being Levi-flat was satisfied (see
[
7
, Remark 4.4.(a)]
for details). Note that, as in general, rational hull doesn’t coincide with the polynomial hull, it is natural to ask about the situation of Theorem
1.2
when
b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
is Levi-flat instead of
b\widehat{K}
. On the other hand since it is proved in
[
7
, Example 4.3]
that convexity assumption in Theorem
1.2
cannot be dropped, it opens up a possibiliy to ask if Theorem
1.2
holds in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
when
X
has finitely many holes with convex
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary, instead of the strictly convex holes. Finally, it is worth considering the following question.

Question 1.4

(Jarnicki/Pflug, Remark 3,
[
10
]
)
.

Does every generalized tube domain with an additional geometric property (e.g. substitute the norm
||.||
in Theorem
1.1
by another
\mathbb{R}
-norm) have a univalent envelope of holomorphy?

Surprisingly, only a very few results are known in the literature regarding the schlichtness of the envelope for truncated tube domains. Therefore, the above discussion and questions suggest that it is necessary to develop sufficient conditions that guarantee the schlichtness of the envelope for truncated tube domains in higher complex dimensions. Motivated by this aim, we now state the main focuses and outline the results of this article.

(i)
We start by establishing Theorem
3.2
, which is a variant of Theorem
1.2
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
when
X
has finitely many holes with convex
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary, instead of the strictly convex holes. The result is obtained by considering compacts that are CR-convex.

(ii)
Next, we introduce the notion of a
special domain
(Definition
4.7
) and establish some of their geometric properties. Our class of special domains includes, in particular, the pseudoconvex truncated tube domains. A special domain
S_{\alpha}
is obtained by first considering
\alpha
as a
special barrier
with special augmenting function
f
, and later removing a
compact fence

K_{\alpha}
given by
\alpha+f
from the
convex initial domain

X+iY
(Definition [
4.2
,
4.5
,
4.6
]). We provide a sufficient condition for the schlichtness of
\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha})
and prove Theorem
5.2
, which extends Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, for
n\geq 2.

(iii)
In Theorem
6.1
, we provide a sufficient condition to ensure schlichtness of a truncated tube domain in higher complex dimensions. Our result extends Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, by applying Stout’s theorem on analytic continuation.

(iv)
In Section 6, we show that under additional assumptions, the schlichtness of the envelope in dimension
n\geq 3
is guaranteed provided that
b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
is Levi-flat (Theorem
6.6
). Our theorem extends Theorem
1.1
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
.

Theorem
3.2
may be regarded as a minor refinement, and an alternative variant of Theorem
1.2
. Apart from our primary focuses mentioned above, Section
2
is committed to preliminaries and the review of classical results, while in Section
3
, we revisit the Jarnicki-Pflug theorem and provide an easy and alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
. Finally, Section
4
is dedicated to the geometry of compact fences and special domains, including their examples and properties.

2.
Preliminaries and some classical results

In this section, we briefly recall some definitions and results, which will be used in the subsequent sections. Throughout we write
x=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
z=(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and for
q\geq 1
,
||x||_{q}:=(\sum_{j=1}^{n}|x_{j}|^{q})^{1/q}
, unless specified otherwise. When
q=2
, we omit the superscript and only write
||x||
to denote the 2-norm of
x
. For two sets
A,B
in any metric space, denote
bA
,
\textsf{int}_{A}B
, and
\textsf{dist}(A,B)
as respectively the boundary of
A
, the interior of
B
relative to
A
, and the distance from
A
to
B
. Recall that a point
p
of an analytic set
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called
regular
(denoted by
p\in\textsf{reg}(X)
) if
p
has an open neighborhood

U
such that
X\cap U
is a complex manifold. The
singular set

\textsf{sing}(X)
is defined as
\textsf{sing}(X):=X\setminus\textsf{reg}(X).
A critical point
p
of a smooth real-valued function
f
on an
n
-dimensional smooth manifold
M
is called
non-degenerate
if and only if with respect to a local coordinate system
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in a neighborhood of
p
, the following matrix

(2.1)

\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial{x_{j}}\partial x_{k}}(p)\Big)

is non-singular. A function
f:M\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
Morse function
if its critical points are non-degenerate.
For more details on Morse theory, see
[
16
]
.

Definition 2.1

(page 120,
[
12
]
)
.

For a domain
G\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with smooth boundary, and a locally defining function
r
near a neighborhood
U
of a point
p\in bG
with
G\cap U\subset\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r(x)<0\}
, the notion of
strict convexity
at
p\in bG
is equivalent to the following condition

\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}{a_{j}}a_{k}\frac{\partial^{2}r}{\partial{x_{j}}\partial x_{k}}(p)>0,\text{ for all }X_{p}=\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\Bigr\rvert_{p}\in T_{p}(bG).

A domain
G
is strictly convex if it is strictly convex at all
p\in bG
.

It is elementary to verify that every bounded strictly convex domain is convex, but not conversely (e.g.
\{(x_{1},x_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:x_{1}<1\}
).

Definition 2.2

(CR manifold and CR function,
[
3
]
)
.

Let
{J:T_{p}\mathbb{C}^{n}\to T_{p}\mathbb{C}^{n}}
be a linear map with
J^{2}=-I
, and let
M
be a smooth submanifold of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. If the real dimension of
T_{p}^{J}M:=T_{p}M\cap J(T_{p}M)
is independent of
p\in M
, we call
M
a
CR manifold
. A function
f:M\to\mathbb{C}
is called a
CR function
if for all
p\in M
and for all
\overline{L_{p}}\in T_{p}^{(0,1)}M
it implies
\overline{L_{p}}f=0
, where

T_{p}^{(0,1)}\mathbb{C}^{n}:=\textsf{span}_{\mathbb{C}}\big\{\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z_{j}}}:1\leq j\leq n\big\};\penalty\ \text{ and }\penalty\ T_{p}^{(0,1)}M:=(\mathbb{C}\otimes T_{p}M)\cap T_{p}^{(0,1)}\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Definition 2.3

(Pseudoconvexity and Levi-flatness,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be an open set with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary such that
\rho
is a locally defining function for
b\Omega
at
p\in b\Omega
with
\rho|_{\Omega}(\cdot)<0
. For an element
v_{p}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Big\rvert_{p}\in T_{p}^{(1,0)}(b\Omega)
the following sesquilinear form

\mathcal{L}(v_{p},v_{p}):=\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}{w_{j}}\overline{w_{k}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial{z_{j}}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}(p)

is called the
Levi form
at
p.
The set
\Omega
is called
pseudoconvex
(resp.
Levi flat
) at
p\in b\Omega
if the Levi form is positive semidefinite (resp. zero). A smooth real hypersurface
M\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
is called Levi-flat if its Levi form vanishes at every point of
M
(see
[
3
]
for details).

Theorem 2.4

(
[
3
]
, chapter 10, Theorem 1)
.

Suppose
(M,T^{J}M)
be a Levi flat CR structure. Then
M
is locally foliated by complex manifolds whose complexified tangent bundle is given by
(T^{J}M\oplus\overline{T^{J}M}).

Recall that a complex manifold is called
Stein
if it is holomorphically separable (that is, any two distinct points are separated by a holomorphic function) and holomorphically convex (that is, the holomorphic convex hull of a compact set is compact). Next we state the following maximum-modulus principle.

Theorem 2.5

(Theorem 16, Chap. III,
[
6
]
)
.

Let
V
be a connected subvariety of the domain
G\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
. If the modulus of any
f\in\mathcal{O}(G)
attains a maximum in
V
, then
f
is constant on
V
.

2.1.
Different hulls and some classical results

For a bounded domain
{\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}}
, let
\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
,
\mathsf{psh}(\Omega)
and
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
respectively denote the set of all holomorphic functions defined on
\Omega
, the set of plurisubharmonic functions on
\Omega
, and the set of all
f\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega)
that are continuous up to
\overline{\Omega}
.
Next we use the following notations to describe the hulls (see
[
11
]
,
[
25
]
for details).

(i)

{\widehat{K}}_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega)}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|f(z)|\leq\max_{K}|f|,\mbox{ for all }f\in\mathcal{A}(\Omega)\}.

(ii)

\widehat{K}_{\mathsf{psh}(\Omega)}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\phi(z)\leq\sup_{K}\phi,\mbox{ for all }{\phi\in\mathsf{psh}(\Omega)}\}.

(iii)

\widehat{K}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:|p(z)|\leq\max_{K}|p|,\text{ for all }p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]\}.

(iv)

\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:p^{-1}(0)\cap K\neq\emptyset,\forall p\in\mathbb{C}[z_{1},z_{2},\dots,z_{n}]\text{ with }p(z)=0\}.

The hulls mentioned in (i), (ii), (iii), and (iv) are respectively called the
\mathcal{A}(\Omega)
-hull,
\mathsf{psh}
-hull, polynomially convex hull, and the rationally convex hull. A compact set
K
is said to be respectively
polynomially convex
or
rationally convex
if
\widehat{K}=K
, and
\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}=K
.

The next lemma is a special case of
[
22
, Theorem 2.1, p. 365]
.

Lemma 2.6

(Rosay-Stout,
[
21
]
)
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a compact set, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}^{2}
be a pseudoconvex domain. If
\Omega\cap K=\emptyset
, then the set
\Omega\setminus\widehat{K}
is pseudoconvex.

Definition 2.7

(
[
25
]
,
[
11
]
)
.

For a pseudoconvex domain

, a compact set

{\widehat{K}}_{\mathcal{A}(\Omega)}=K
. Moreover,

is said to be

Theorem 2.8

(Jöricke,

)

Let

3.
Revisiting two classical results on envelope of holomorphy

We start this section by recalling that the proof of Theorem
1.1
involves a result by Ivaškovič in complex dimension 2 (
[
8
, Lemma 8]
), followed by applying a slightly constructive technique in complex dimension
n\geq 3
. Here we first establish an alternative and concise proof of Theorem
1.1
using Stout’s theorem on analytic continuation.

Alternative proof of Theorem
1.1
.

We start with the following pseudoconvex bounded domain

D=\Big\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}>(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\Big\}\cap\Big(B_{r_{2}}(0)+iB_{r_{3}}(0)\Big).

Set
E:=\{x+iy\in\overline{D}:||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}=(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}\subset\partial D.
To prove
E
is
\mathcal{O}(\overline{D})
-convex,
we choose a
p=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in\overline{D}\setminus E
.
Note that
p
satisfies
{||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}>(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})}
. Choose
\epsilon_{0}>0
such that

||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}=(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})+\epsilon_{0}.

Define the entire function
h(z):=\exp({\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}})/\exp({r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}+\epsilon_{0}})
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. Notice that
|h(p)|=1
and
{||h||_{E}=\exp(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})/\exp({r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}+\epsilon_{0}})=\frac{1}{\exp(\epsilon_{0})}<1}.

Since
|h(p)|=1
, there exists
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
with
h(p)=\exp(i\theta)
. Next, consider the entire function
f:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
defined by
{f(z):=\exp(-i\theta)h(z)}
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and note that
{f(p)=\exp(-i\theta)\exp(i\theta)=1.}
Moreover, as the modulus remains unchanged under rotation, it implies
||f||_{E}=\sup_{E}|f|<1
too. Thus
E
is
\mathcal{O}(\overline{D})
-convex by Definition
2.9
. Finally, we consider the connected domain

\Omega=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<||x||<r_{2}\}+i\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}

and apply Theorem
2.10
to conclude that
\mathcal{E}(\Omega)=D.

By a
domain
X
convex up to exclusion of finitely many convex holes
we mean a domain
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
obtained from a convex domain
X_{\text{cvx}}
by excluding finitely many disjoint compact convex sets
\overline{H}_{x,j}
,
1\leq j\leq m_{0}
. Next, we prove the following lemma which is a minor variant of
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
. The key observation here is that one can relax the assumption of
strict convexity
in
[
7
, Lemma 4.1]
by adding more regularity in the
y
-direction (e.g., assuming
bY
to be
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-smooth). In view of this, we obtain the following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a domain convex up to exclusion of a single convex hole
H_{x}
with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary (and not necessarily strictly convex), and let
Y\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a convex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary. Denote
{H=H_{x}+ibY}
. Then
\mathcal{E}(D)=D^{\prime}\backslash\widehat{H}
, where
D=X+iY
and
D^{\prime}=(X\cup H_{x})+iY
.

Proof.

D^{\prime}
is a bounded pseudoconvex domain in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
but not of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
. Therefore we perform a very small
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-deformation to the “outer boundary” (i.e., to the points of
bD^{\prime}\setminus H
) of the domain
D^{\prime}
to obtain a new pseudoconvex domain
D_{\text{new}}
of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
such that
D_{\text{new}}\subset D^{\prime}
. Since
(H_{x}+i\overline{Y})\cap bD^{\prime}=H,
it implies that
D_{\text{new}}
can be chosen with
{(H_{x}+i\overline{Y})\cap bD_{\text{new}}=H}.
By convexity of
H_{x}+i\overline{Y}
, it implies
{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}})}\subset H_{x}+i\overline{Y}
, and hence
{{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}})}\cap bD_{\text{new}}=H.}
Therefore
H
is
CR(bD_{\text{new}})
-convex. Note that the following one-sided neighborhood

V=\big(bD_{\text{new}}\setminus H\big)\cup\big(D_{\text{new}}\cap D\big)\subset\overline{D}

of
bD_{\text{new}}\setminus H
satisfies the conditions of the Theorem
2.8
. Therefore it follows that every holomorphic function defined on
D
(and on
D_{\text{new}}\cap D
by restriction) has a (uniquely
determined) analytic extension to
D_{\text{new}}\setminus{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}})}
and hence to
{D^{\prime}\setminus{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}})}}
by construction. Next we see that

(3.1)

D^{\prime}\setminus\widehat{H}\subseteq D^{\prime}\setminus{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D^{\prime})}\subseteq D^{\prime}\setminus{\widehat{H}}_{\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}})},

as
\mathcal{O}(\mathbb{C}^{n})\subseteq\mathcal{A}(D^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{A}(D_{\text{new}}).
Since
H
satisfies
D^{\prime}\cap H=\emptyset
, by Lemma
2.6
it follows that
D^{\prime}\setminus\widehat{H}
is pseudoconvex. Finally, due to pseudoconvexity, all the sets in equation (
3.1
) are equal, which completes the proof.
∎

Next, we state the following variant of Theorem
1.2
omitting repetitive details of the proof, as it closely follows the original argument.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a domain convex up to exclusion of finitely many convex holes with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary (and not necessarily strictly convex) and let
{Y\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}}
be a convex domain with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
-boundary. Then the envelope of holomorphy of
D=X+iY
is schlicht.

Proof.

The proof of the general case with finitely many convex holes, follows similary as mentioned in
[
7
, Page 2991]
by smoothly deforming the holes and applying Behnke-Stein theorem (
[
1
]
) to the increasing union of pseudoconvex sets.
∎

We now shift our attention to different hulls. Recall that a polynomially convex compact set is rationally convex, but not conversely. In general, it is still an open problem to know when a rationally convex compact set
K
with 1-dimensional Čech cohomology group
\check{H}^{1}(K,\mathbb{Z})=0
is polynomially convex (see
[
23
, Question II.7]
). Although explicit calculations of the hulls are hard in general, nevertheless, in certain contexts
\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
and
\widehat{K}
may become the same, as follows from our next proposition.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
r_{1},r_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
,
n\geq 2
, and
K_{x}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||x||\leq r_{1}\}
. Denote
Y=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}
and
K=K_{x}+ibY
. Then
\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}=\widehat{K}
, and it is given by

\big\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||y||\leq r_{3},||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}\leq(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\big\}.

Proof.

In
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 2
, it can easily be derived from
[
7
, Example 4.4(a)]
that

\widehat{K}=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||y||\leq r_{3},||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}\leq(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}.

We first want to show

N:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||y||\leq r_{3},||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}<(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}\subset\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}.

Suppose not so, and let there be a point
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in N\setminus\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
. By definition there is a holomorphic polynomial
P:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
such that
z_{0}\in P^{-1}(0)
and
P^{-1}(0)\cap K=\emptyset.
Due to
P^{-1}(0)
being an
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic variety in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, by
[
6
, Corollary 17, Chap. III]
, it therefore cannot be compact. Thus,
P^{-1}(0)
is unbounded. Since
K\cap P^{-1}(0)=\emptyset
, the set
P^{-1}(0)
must therefore intersect
{E:=\{||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}=(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}}
.
It implies
{||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}<(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})}
, due to that
z_{0}\in N
. Choose
\epsilon>0
satisfying
||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}=(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})-\epsilon,
and consider the holomorphic function
\varphi(z):=\exp{(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}-\epsilon)}/\exp{(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2})}.
Next, we take an irreducible branch
V^{\prime}
of
P^{-1}(0)\cap N
that contains
z_{0}
.
Clearly, it implies
|\varphi(z_{0})|=1
and

\max\limits_{bV^{\prime}}|\varphi|=\frac{\exp{(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}-\epsilon)}}{\exp{(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})}}=\frac{1}{\exp(\epsilon)}<1.

However, since
V^{\prime}
is a connected subvariety of the domain
N
containing
z_{0}
, by Theorem
2.5
the modulus
|\varphi|
cannot attain maximum on
V^{\prime}.
This leads to a contradiction. Consequently
N\subset\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
, and the result follows by taking closures on both sides.
∎

We end this section by noting that the hull mentioned in the above proposition was crucial, and it was used in
[
7
, Example 4.4 (a)]
to show a generalization of Jarnicki-Pflug’s result (
[
10
]
) in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. Moreover, we prepared this proposition for using it later in Section
6
to obtain Theorem
1.1
as a corollary of our Theorem
6.6
.

4.
Geometry of compact fence and special domain

In this section, we introduce and study the notion of
compact fence
and a new type of domain, namely the
special domain
. We start by letting
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
globally
defined
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function. Clearly, there is a holomorphic function
F_{\alpha}
in complex variables
z_{j},\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq n
, (defined at least on some open neighborhood of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
) and a real-valued power series
f_{\alpha}(x,y)
such that
F_{\alpha}|_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(x+iy)=\alpha(x),
and
\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha}(x+iy)=\alpha(x)+f_{\alpha}(x,y)
hold for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. For notational simplicity,
f
is written throughout instead of
f_{\alpha}
when there is no ambiguity in the context. We call
f
the
augmenting function
of
\alpha
. Since it is known that
F_{\alpha}
mentioned above need not always be holomorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, this situation needs to be different from our next definition.

Definition 4.1

(good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
and good augmenting function)
.

A
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function
if there is a holomorphic function
{F_{\alpha}:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}}
such that
\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha}(x+iy)=\alpha(x)+f(x,y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. The corresponding augmenting function
f
is called a
good augmenting
function.

Examples of good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
functions are in particular, the real polynomials in the variables
x_{j},\penalty\ 1\leq j\leq n
. In the literature, it is conventional to first define a holomorphic function
F
and later obtain
\textsf{Re}(F)
from it, unlike our situation. The reason to first start with
\alpha

is that we want to distinguish
\alpha
and its corresponding augmenting function
f
to define the notion of
special barrier
. This reason would become clearer when we introduce our next definition.

Definition 4.2

(Special barrier)
.

A good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function
{\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}}
(with the good augmenting function
f
) is called a
special barrier
with respect to a convex set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if for all fixed
y_{0}\in Y
the following real Hessian matrix

(4.1)

\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\Big(||x||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0})\big)\Big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\textbf{x})\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j,k\leq n}

is positive definite for all
\textbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
In this case, we call
f
a
special augmenting
function.

Before proceeding, we collect some simple examples of special barrier
\alpha
.

Example 4.3
.

The following functions are special barriers with respect to any convex set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(i)

Let
c
and
r_{1}
be any two real constants. Define the two functions
{\alpha_{\text{const}}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}}
and
\alpha_{\geq 0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
respectively by
\alpha_{\text{const}}(x):=c
, and
\alpha_{\geq 0}(x):=r_{1}^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that in both cases the special augmenting functions are equal to
0
. Therefore both
\alpha_{\text{const}}
and
\alpha_{\geq 0}
are special barriers with respect to any convex set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(ii)

For
\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
the function
\alpha_{\text{lin}}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}x_{j}
is a special barrier with respect to any convex set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Note that in this case, the special augmenting function is zero too.

(iii)

Consider the good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function
\alpha_{\text{mix}}(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}x_{j}x_{j+1}
, and note that the corresponding good augmenting function
f(x,y)=-\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}y_{j}y_{j+1}
. The matrix mentioned in equation (
4.1
) in this case, is the tridiagonal matrix with
-1,2
, and
-1
as entries. Due to a result by J. F. Elliott (see
[
5
]
), it is known that the eigen values of this matrix are all positive and given by

4\sin^{2}\Big(\frac{k\pi}{2(n+1)}\Big);\penalty\ k=1,2,\cdots,n.

Therefore the function
\alpha_{\text{mix}}(x)
is a special barrier with respect to any convex set
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(iv)

If
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are special barriers with respect to the convex set
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
respectively, then for any
t\in[0,1]
, the function
\alpha:=t\alpha_{1}+(1-t)\alpha_{2}
is also a special barrier with respect to the convex set
Y_{1}\cap Y_{2}
. Note that if
f_{1},f_{2}
are the special augmenting functions of
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
respectively, then the same of
\alpha
is
tf_{1}+(1-t)f_{2}
.

Some more examples of special barriers are given in the following proposition.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
Y
be any convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then any good
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
function
\alpha
with the corresponding good augmenting function
f
, satisfying the following two properties

(a)

\frac{\partial^{2}\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0})\big)}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})<2
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y_{0}\in Y
and
1\leq j\leq n
,

(b)

\frac{\partial^{2}\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0})\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\mathbf{x})=0
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y_{0}\in Y
and
j\neq k
,
1\leq j,k\leq n
,

is a special barrier with respect to
Y
.

In particular, if
N_{0},\beta_{j},\delta_{j}\in\mathbb{R}
with
{1\leq j\leq n}
such that
N_{0}\beta_{j}<1
for all
1\leq j\leq n
, then the function
\alpha:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\alpha(x):=N_{0}\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}x_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}x_{j}\big)

is a special barrier with respect to the open ball
B_{R}(0)
, for all
R\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

Proof.

The first part follows from the observation that the following matrix

A=\begin{pmatrix}2-\frac{\partial^{2}(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0}))}{\partial x_{1}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})&0&\ldots&0\\
0&2-\frac{\partial^{2}(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0}))}{\partial x_{2}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})&\ldots&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\ldots&2-\frac{\partial^{2}(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0}))}{\partial x_{n}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})\end{pmatrix}

has all positive eigenvalues given by
\lambda_{j}:=\big(2-\frac{\partial^{2}(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0}))}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})\big)
for all
\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
y_{0}\in Y
, and
1\leq j\leq n.
This implies that for all
\textbf{v}:=(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the quantity
\textbf{v}A\textbf{v}^{t}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}v_{j}^{2}>0.
Hence, the matrix mentioned in equation (
4.1
) is positive definite. The particular case follows by first letting
Y=B_{R}(0)
and noting that

F_{\alpha}(z)=N_{0}(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}z_{j}^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\delta_{j}z_{j}),

and

\textsf{Re}(F_{\alpha})(x,y)=N_{0}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}(\beta_{j}x_{j}^{2}-\delta_{j}x_{j})-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}y_{j}^{2}\Big).

Thus the augmenting function
f(x,y)=-N_{0}\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}\beta_{j}y_{j}^{2}
, and consequently

(a)

\frac{\partial^{2}\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0})\big)}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}(\mathbf{x})=2N_{0}\beta_{j}<2,\penalty\ \text{for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\penalty\ y_{0}\in B_{R}(0).

(b)

\frac{\partial^{2}\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y_{0})\big)}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(\mathbf{x})=0,\penalty\ \text{for all }\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\penalty\ y_{0}\in B_{R}(0).

Thus
\alpha
defined above is a special barrier with respect to
B_{R}(0)
for all
R\in\mathbb{R}^{+}.

∎

In order to define special domain, we introduce the following two notions.

Definition 4.5

(Compact fence)
.

For a special barrier
\alpha
with respect to a convex domain
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2
, and the corresponding special augmenting function
f
, we denote

(4.2)

K_{\alpha}:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\overline{Y}:||x||^{2}\leq\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}.

We call
K_{\alpha}
the
compact fence given by

\alpha+f
.

Definition 4.6
.

Let
\alpha,K_{\alpha}
, and
Y
as in Definition
4.5
. We call a bounded
strictly convex
domain
X
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
boundary a
convex initial domain
if

(4.3)

K_{\alpha}\cap\big(bX+i\overline{Y}\big)=\emptyset.

Note that if
X
is any convex initial domain, then any bounded strictly convex domain
X^{\prime}
with
\mathcal{C}^{2}
boundary containing
X
is also a convex initial domain.

Definition 4.7

(Special domain)
.

For a special barrier
\alpha
with respect to a convex domain
Y
, and the special augmenting function
f
, let
K_{\alpha}
be the compact fence given by
\alpha+f
. Then for a convex initial domain
X
we call the following domain

(4.4)

S_{\alpha}:=(X+iY)\setminus K_{\alpha}

a
special domain
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}.

The following proposition motivates us to study special domains further.

Proposition 4.8
.

Every pseudoconvex truncated tube domain is a special domain for some special barrier
\alpha
. The converse is false.

Proof.

By
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
, every pseudoconvex truncated tube domain
D
is of the form
X+iY
, where both
X
and
Y
are convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Choose
c\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, and let
K_{\alpha_{-}}
be the compact fence obtained from the special barrier
\alpha_{-}
defined by
\alpha_{-}(x):=-c^{2}
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that
K_{\alpha_{-}}
in this case is an empty set, and thus
X
is a convex initial domain by Definition
4.6
. This shows

D=(X+iY)\setminus\emptyset=(X+iY)\setminus K_{\alpha_{-}},

and therefore a special domain for the special barrier
\alpha_{-}.

For the converse, we first prove that every special domain need not be a truncated tube domain. Let
X=B_{r_{2}}(0)
,
Y=B_{r_{3}}(0)
be the two open balls in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of radii
r_{2},r_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
and centered at
0.
Next, we consider the following special barrier
\alpha(x):=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2}
(Proposition
4.4
) with
\beta_{j}=1,\penalty\ \delta_{j}=0
and
N_{0}=\frac{1}{2}.
From the proof of the same proposition, it follows that the augmenting function
f(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{2}.
Thus, the corresponding compact fence

\displaystyle K_{\alpha}

\displaystyle:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\overline{Y}:||x||^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}x_{j}^{2}-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{n}y_{j}^{2}\}=\{0+i0\}.

It implies that the special domain
S_{\alpha}:=(B_{r_{2}}(0)+iB_{r_{3}}(0))\setminus\{0\}
is not a truncated tube domain. To verify this, we suppose
S_{\alpha}=U+iV
for some domains
{U,V\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}.
For
x,y\neq 0
, both the points
z=0+i\frac{r_{3}}{2||y||}y
and
z^{\prime}=\frac{r_{2}}{2||x||}x+i0
belong to
S_{\alpha}=U+iV
, and hence
0
must be in
U\cap V
. However, due to
0+i0\notin S_{\alpha}
, it leads to a contradiction.

Next, we prove that every special domain need not be pseudoconvex. To verify this, we let
X,Y
be as above, and let the special barrier be
\alpha_{\geq 0}(x):=r_{1}^{2}\penalty\ (r_{1}\neq 0)
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see Example
4.3
.(i)). Since the augmenting function in this case is zero, it implies
K_{\alpha_{\geq 0}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||x||\leq r_{1}^{2}\}+i\overline{B_{r_{3}}(0)}
. Therefore

S_{\alpha_{\geq 0}}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<||x||<r_{2}\}+i\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}.

Since by Theorem
1.1
the envelope
\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha_{\geq 0}})
is strictly larger than
S_{\alpha_{\geq 0}}
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.9
.

Observe that, a special domain
S_{\alpha}
is a truncated tube domain if the special augmenting function
f
(corresponding to special barrier
\alpha
) is independent of the variable
y
, that is if
f(x,y)=\phi(x)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for some function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
.

In
[

]
, the notion of strict convexity (see in Definition

) in the

-direction was crucial to prove their theorem of schlichtness in complex dimension 2. Here at this point, we emphasize that our condition of
\alpha
being a special barrier is also very natural and valid. The legitimacy of it becomes clearer in the next remark.

Remark 4.10
.

For a fixed
y^{\prime}\in\overline{Y}
, denote

K_{\alpha}^{y^{\prime}}:=\{x+iy^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\overline{Y}:||x||^{2}<\alpha(x)+f(x,y^{\prime})\}.

If
bK_{\alpha}^{y^{\prime}}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
-smooth, then for any tangent vector
{\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\Big|_{\xi}\in T_{\xi}(bK_{\alpha}^{y^{\prime}})\setminus\{0\}}
with
\xi\in bK_{\alpha}^{y^{\prime}}

and real functions
a_{j}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
, the quantity
\textbf{a}H\textbf{a}^{t}>0
, where
\textbf{a}:=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
and
H
is the matrix mentioned in equation (

).
Therefore by Definition

K_{\alpha}^{y^{\prime}}

The next proposition shows that under certain conditions on the special augmenting function
f
, the compact fence
K_{\alpha}
(corresponding to a special barrier
\alpha
) is of a nice nature. To be precise, if
f
has no “mixed
xy
-terms” (defined later), and if
\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial y_{j}^{2}}(x,y)\neq 0
for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
K_{\alpha}
can be written as a union of sets
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}
, each of which is a real hypersurface of class
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
except possibly, at the set of isolated singularities
\textsf{sing}(\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha})
. It is worth noting that the conditions on
f
mentioned in Proposition
4.11
are satisfied for all the special functions
\alpha
mentioned in Examples
4.4
when
N_{0}\neq 0
and
\beta_{j}\neq 0
for all
1\leq j\leq n.
Combining it with Examples
4.3
, a typical function
\alpha
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
can be for example
\alpha(x_{1},x_{2}):=\frac{11}{12}(\frac{3}{5}x_{1}^{2}-\frac{1}{5}x_{2}^{2}-\sqrt{2}x_{1}+\frac{21}{\sqrt{5}}x_{2})+\frac{1}{12}x_{1}x_{2}.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\alpha
be a special barrier with respect to the convex domain
Y
, with the corresponding special augmenting function
f
that has no mixed
xy
-terms (i.e., if
{f(x,y)=f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(y)}
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and for some functions
f_{1},f_{2}
). Then the
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
function
\sigma:\mathbb{R}^{2n}\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

\sigma(x,y):=||x||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\big)\penalty\ \text{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

is a Morse function on
\mathbb{R}^{n}+iY
. Moreover, if we denote
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}:=\sigma^{-1}(s)\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\overline{Y})
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
, then the compact fence
K_{\alpha}=\cup_{s\leq 0}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}
, such that each
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}+iY)
is a real
\mathcal{C}^{\infty}
hypersurface except possibly at the set of isolated singularities.

Proof.

By assumption, there is
F_{\alpha}
such that
\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha}(x+iy)=\alpha(x)+f_{1}(x)+f_{2}(y)
for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Due to
\alpha+f
being real part of holomorphic function
F_{\alpha}
, the Laplacian
\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}(z)=0
, for all
1\leq j,k\leq n.
Considering the Wirtinger operators
1
1

1

\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}-i\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}})
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial\overline{z_{j}}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}+i\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}})
for
1\leq j,k\leq n.
followed by taking the real part, this gives

\frac{1}{4}\Big(\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial x_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}+\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial y_{j}\partial{y_{k}}}\Big)(x,y)=0\text{ for all }1\leq j,k\leq n.

Thus for
1\leq j,k\leq n
, at a critical point
p:=(p_{1},p_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+iY
of
\sigma
, it follows

(4.5)

-\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial x_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}(p)=\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial y_{j}\partial{y_{k}}}(p).

Next, we need to show that the following block matrix

(4.6)

\nabla:=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial x_{j}\partial x_{k}}(p)&\vline&\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial x_{j}\partial y_{k}}(p)\\
\hline\cr\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial y_{j}\partial x_{k}}(p)&\vline&\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial y_{j}\partial y_{k}}(p)\end{pmatrix}_{1\leq j,k\leq n}=\begin{pmatrix}A&\vline&B\\
\hline\cr C&\vline&D\end{pmatrix}\text{(say)}

has full rank. Equation (
4.5
), it implies
D=-A
. Moreover, for
1\leq j,k\leq n
, the following are true.

\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial x_{j}\partial{y_{k}}}(p)=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\big(-\frac{\partial f_{2}}{\partial y_{k}}\big)\Big)(p)=0,

and

\frac{\partial^{2}\sigma}{\partial y_{j}\partial{x_{k}}}(p)=\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial y_{j}}\big(2x_{k}-\frac{\partial(\alpha+f_{1})}{\partial x_{k}}\big)\Big)(p)=0.

Due to that
\alpha
is a special barrier with respect to
Y,
it implies
A
is positive definite. Thus,
\det(\nabla)=\det(A)\det(D-CA^{-1}B)=(-1)^{n}\det(A)^{2}\neq 0.
Hence by the definition mentioned in Section
2
, the function
\sigma
is Morse on
\mathbb{R}^{n}+iY
. Finally, by
[
16
, Corollary 2.3]
, all the non-degenerate critical points are isolated and the result follows for
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}+iY)
. The identity
K_{\alpha}=\cup_{s\leq 0}\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}
is automatic from the definition of
\mathcal{K}_{s}^{\alpha}.

∎

5.
Envelope of special domains in higher complex dimension

In this section, we state and prove a theorem concerning the schlichtness of special domains in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\penalty\ (n\geq 2)
. Before we do so, we establish the following variant of the maximum-modulus principle. Although the result follows as a consequence of Theorem
2.5
, it is nevertheless of independent interest.
Therefore for the reader’s convenience, we include the following statement and a brief proof of it.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
D\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}\penalty\ (n\geq 2)
be a pseudoconvex domain and
V\subset D
be a closed analytic variety of pure dimension at least one. Then for any domain
G\Subset V
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(D)
(or
f\in\mathcal{O}(G)
) we have

\max_{\overline{G}}|f|=\max_{bG}|f|.

Proof.

First consider the case when
f\in\mathcal{O}(D)
.
Since
\overline{G}
is compact, there exists
z_{0}\in\overline{G}
when
|f|\big|_{\overline{G}}
attains a maximum. If
z_{0}\in bG
the result follows. Otherwise, if
z_{0}\in G,
choose
D^{\prime}\subset D
such that
G=D^{\prime}\cap V
, i.e.
G=D^{\prime}\cap V
is a closed subvariety of
D^{\prime}
and
f\in\mathcal{O}(D^{\prime}).
Since
G
is a domain (and hence connected), we apply Theorem
2.5
to get the desired conclusion.

Next, consider the case when
f\in\mathcal{O}(G)
. Suppose
|f|
attains maximum at
z_{0}\in G
. We choose an ambient neighborhood
G^{\prime}
of
z_{0}
so that
f
extends to
\widetilde{f}\in\mathcal{O}(G^{\prime})
and
V\cap G^{\prime}\subset G.
Write
V\cap G^{\prime}=V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cup\cdots\cup V_{k}
, where
V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}
are the irreducible components of
V\cap G^{\prime}
. By Theorem
2.5
, the function
f
becomes constant on each
V_{j}
for
1\leq j\leq k,
and hence becomes constant on each irreducible components of
G.
Finally, since
G
is connected, it follows that
f|_{G}
is constant.
∎

Next, we state and prove one of our main theorems, which is a sharp generalization of Theorem
1.1
(see
[
10
, Theorem 1]
) for dimension
n\geq 2
, and also an
n
-dimensional variant of
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
. Step I of the proof of our theorem is a broader modification of
[
7
, Example 4.4.(a)]
adapted to our general situation, and the remaining steps are done using multiple methods, including Sard’s theorem and
[
25
, Theorem 5.2.11]
. Note that the situation was easier and rather straightforward in
[
7
, Example 4.4. (a)]
due to the leaves of the foliations of the level hypersurface mentioned there have the “boundary” in the horizontal part of the truncated tube, and have only one isolated singularity at the origin. However, our situation is subtle due to the possible involvement of multiple singularities, and also because a special domain
S_{\alpha}
in general may not be a truncated tube domain. Nevertheless, the maximum modulus principle on the analytic variety (Lemma
5.1
) helps us to surmount the difficulty.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\alpha
be a special barrier with respect to
Y=B_{R}(0)\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}\penalty\ (n\geq 2)
, and let
f
be its special augmenting function. Let
X\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be a convex initial domain, set
D=X+iY
, and let
K_{\alpha}
be the compact fence given by
\alpha+f
as defined in (
4.2
). Then the envelope of holomorphy
\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha})
of the special domain
{S_{\alpha}:=D\setminus K_{\alpha}}
is given by

\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha})=D\backslash\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}.

Here
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
denotes the polynomial hull of
{K_{\alpha,\partial}}:=K_{\alpha}\cap(X+ibY)
. In particular, the envelope of holomorphy is schlicht.

It is important to note that the statement of the above theorem also indicates that
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
is a subset of
K_{\alpha}
, which will be proved later in Step I (claim
5.6
).
The first strategy of our proof is to explicitly determine
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
. Note that due to the presence of
\alpha
, our construction becomes subtle, although the difficulties can be surmounted in view of the real-analyticity of
\alpha.

Proof.

Step I: Explicit description of
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
.

Given that
K_{\alpha}:=\{x+iy\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\overline{Y}:||x||^{2}\leq\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}.

We consider the following holomorphic function

h(z_{1},z_{2},\ldots,z_{n})=\text{exp}{\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}-F_{\alpha}(z)\Big)},

where
F_{\alpha}
is the holomorphic function defined in Definition
4.1
. Observe that
h
has modulus
|h|=\text{exp}\big({||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\big)}\big)
.
Next for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, we consider the level sets of
|h|
as follows

\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha}:=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\big)=s\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha}
’s are real hypersurfaces of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
except possibly at the set of singularities
\textsf{sing}(\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha})
. Moreover the set
\textsf{reg}(\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha})
is Levi flat for each
s\in\mathbb{R}
. This is because for all
s\in\mathbb{R}

\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha}=\Big\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\rho(z,\overline{z}):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{z_{j}+\overline{z_{j}}}{2}\Big)^{2}-\sum_{j=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{z_{j}-\overline{z_{j}}}{2i}\Big)^{2}-\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha}(z)=s\Big\}.

Due to being real part of holomorphic function the Laplacian
\frac{\partial^{2}(\textsf{Re}F_{\alpha})}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z_{k}}}(\xi)=0
with
1\leq j,k\leq n
, and for
X_{\xi}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}\frac{\partial}{\partial z_{j}}\Big\rvert_{\xi}\in T_{\xi}^{(1,0)}b\big(\textsf{reg}(\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha})\big)
the following sesquilinear form

\mathcal{L}(X_{\xi},X_{\xi}):=\sum\limits_{j,k=1}^{n}{a_{j}}\overline{a_{k}}\frac{\partial^{2}\rho}{\partial{z_{j}}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}(\xi)

is zero too. Therefore by Theorem
2.4
(
[
3
]
) the set
\textsf{reg}(\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha})

is foliated by a 1-parameter family of complex submanifolds of complex dimension
(n-1)
. It is nevertheless important to note that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists the following disjoint complex analytic sets

(5.1)

\mathcal{V}_{{s}}^{\alpha,t}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:\sum\limits_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}-F_{\alpha}(z)=s+it\}

such that
\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{}\mathcal{V}_{{s}}^{\alpha,t}=\mathcal{H}_{{s}}^{\alpha}.
To verify this, note that any
z_{0}\in\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha}
belongs to
\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\alpha,\textsf{Im}\Psi(z_{0})}
, where
\Psi(z):=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}-F_{\alpha}(z).
The other inclusion is obvious as
\mathcal{V}_{s}^{\alpha,t}\subset\mathcal{H}_{s}^{\alpha},
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}.

Next, denote the following set

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}=\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}-(\alpha(x)+f(x,y))+R^{2}\leq 0\}.

Our next objective is to show that
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}=\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}.

We present the proof by distributing it among the following three technical claims.

Claim 5.3
.

For every
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}
it implies
\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}}^{\alpha}\cap\big(X+ibY\big)\subset{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
, where
s_{0}=||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}-(\alpha(x_{0})+f(x_{0},y_{0}))
.

Proof of the claim
.

Since
z_{0}=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}
, it follows

(5.2)

s_{0}=||x_{0}||^{2}-||y_{0}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x_{0})+f(x_{0},y_{0})\big)\leq-R^{2}.

Suppose the claim is not true, and let
{z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}}^{\alpha}\cap(X+ibY)}
be such that
z^{\prime}\notin K_{\alpha,\partial}.
It therefore implies

(5.3)

||y^{\prime}||^{2}=R^{2}\text{ and }||x^{\prime}||^{2}>\alpha(x^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}),

Since
z^{\prime}=x^{\prime}+iy^{\prime}\in\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}}^{\alpha}
, it implies

(5.4)

||x^{\prime}||^{2}-||y^{\prime}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)=s_{0}.

Using equations (
5.2
), (
5.3
) and (
5.4
), it implies,

-R^{2}=-||y^{\prime}||^{2}<s_{0}\leq-R^{2}.

This leads to a contradiction, and hence
\mathcal{H}_{s_{0}}^{\alpha}\cap\big(X+ibY\big)\subset K_{\alpha,\partial}
.

\square

Claim 5.4
.

\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}\subset\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}.

Proof of the claim
.

To prove the inequality
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}\subset\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
we first consider an element
\tilde{x}+i\tilde{y}\in\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}
. Setting
\tilde{s}=||\tilde{x}||^{2}-||\tilde{y}||^{2}-(\alpha(\tilde{x})+f(\tilde{x},\tilde{y}))

and
\tilde{s}\leq-R^{2}.
By Claim

{\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha}\cap\big(X+ibY\big)\subset K_{\alpha,\partial}.}

Moreover, if there is any

, it implies
{||\hat{x}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(\hat{x})+f(\hat{x},\hat{y})\big)+(R^{2}-||\hat{y}||^{2})\leq 0.}
Since
R^{2}-||\hat{y}||^{2}\geq 0
, it must imply
||\hat{x}||^{2}-(\alpha(\hat{x})+f(\hat{x},\hat{y}))\leq 0.
Consequently,
\hat{z}\in K_{\alpha}\cap(bX+i\overline{Y})
contradicts the equation (
4.3
). Therefore

(5.5)

\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha}\cap(bX+i\overline{Y})=\emptyset.

Note that for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists the following disjoint complex analytic sets
\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,t}
as described in equation (
5.1
), such that
\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}{}\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,t}=\mathcal{H}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha}.
Thus, for some

it implies
\tilde{z}\in\mathcal{{V}}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}
and
\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}\cap(X+ibY)\subset K_{\alpha,\partial}.
Moreover, by equation (
5.5
), it follows
\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}\cap(bX+i\overline{Y})=\emptyset.
Next we apply the maximum modulus principle on the relatively compact domain
G_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}:=\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}\cap(X+iY)
of
\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}
that contains

. Thus using Lemma
5.1
and equation (
5.5
) for any holomorphic polynomial
P:\mathbb{C}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}
, it implies

|P(\tilde{z})|\leq\max_{G_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}}|P|=\max_{bG_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}}|P|\leq\max_{\mathcal{V}_{\tilde{s}}^{\alpha,\tilde{t}}\cap(X+ibY)}|P|\leq\max_{{K_{\alpha,\partial}}}|P|.

Which implies
\tilde{z}=\tilde{x}+i\tilde{y}\in\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}},
and proves Claim
5.4
.

\square

Claim 5.5
.

\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}=\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}.

Proof of the claim
.

For the other direction, we consider
{z^{\prime}\in\overline{D}\setminus(\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D})}
, and the holomorphic function
h(z)=\text{exp}{\big(\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}-F_{\alpha}(z)\big)}
defined earlier. Notice that if any
\zeta=x+iy\in K_{\alpha,\partial}

||x||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x)+f(x,y)\big)\leq 0
and
||y||=R
. Due to

||x^{\prime}||^{2}-||y^{\prime}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)>-R^{2}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle|h(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle=\text{exp}\big(||x^{\prime}||^{2}-||y^{\prime}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)\big)

\displaystyle>e^{-R^{2}}=\max_{\zeta\in K_{\alpha,\partial}}\text{exp}\big(||x^{\prime}||^{2}-\big(\alpha(x^{\prime})+f(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\big)-||R||^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=\max_{\zeta\in K_{\alpha,\partial}}|h|.

This shows
\mathcal{A}_{\alpha}\cap\overline{D}=\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
, and hence the Claim
5.5
is proved.

\square

The containment of
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
inside

follows from the next claim.

Claim 5.6
.

\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}\subsetneq K_{\alpha}.

Proof of the claim
.

Choose any

, and notice from Claim

that
||x^{*}||^{2}-||y^{*}||^{2}-\alpha(x^{*})-f(x^{*},y^{*})+R^{2}\leq 0.
Since on

the quantity

it must therefore imply
||x^{*}||^{2}-\alpha(x^{*})-f(x^{*},y^{*})\leq 0.
This guarantees that

It is worth mentioning that the inclusion is proper can readily be seen from the construction (e.g., choosing a point

and

). This proves the claim.

\square

Having established the explicit description of
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}

Step II: Holomorphic extension up to

.

First we note that by convexity,
\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}
is contained in the compact convex set

(5.6)

\textsf{ch}(\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x+iy\in K_{\alpha,\partial}\text{ for some }y\in b{Y}\})+i\overline{Y},

where

stands for the convex hull. By the assumption (

(5.7)

{\widehat{K_{\alpha,\partial}}\cap(bX+i\overline{Y})=\emptyset.}

Denote

. We observe that

is a bounded

\mathbb{C}^{n}
, but not of class

Theorem 5.7

(

,
[

]
,

Consider

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{M})

Step III: Pseudoconvexity of

Due to the rational convexity of
\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
, every point
p\in\overline{D}\setminus\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
lies on an analytic variety
\mathcal{V}_{p}
(zero locus of some holomorphic polynomial) of pure dimension
(n-1)

such that
\mathcal{V}_{p}\cap K=\emptyset
. It is even true that
\mathcal{V}_{p}\cap\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}=\emptyset.
Otherwise if some
z^{\prime}\in\mathcal{V}_{p}\cap\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
, then due to
\mathcal{V}_{p}\cap K=\emptyset
, it yields (by definition of rational convexity) that
z^{\prime}\notin\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
, a contradiction. Since
E\subset\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}
it implies
\mathcal{V}_{p}\cap E=\emptyset
.

(ii) Points in
\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\overline{D}
:
Lets assume
p=x_{0}+iy_{0}\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus\overline{D}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\setminus(\overline{X}+i\overline{Y}).
Then
p\in\big((\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{X})+i\mathbb{R}^{n}\big)\cup\big(\mathbb{R}^{n}+i(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{Y})\big).
Suppose
p\in(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{X})+i\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Note that due to
\overline{X}
being convex and compact, and
x_{0}\notin\overline{X}
, by Hahn-Banach seperation theorem (see
[
19
, Theorem 3.7]
) for any point
x_{0}\notin\overline{X}
there is a real hyperplane that strongly seperates
\overline{X}
from
x_{0}
. In other words, there is a linear functional
\ell:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\ell(x_{0})=0
and
\ell(x)<\ell(x_{0})-\epsilon
for all
x\in\overline{X}
, and for some
\epsilon>0
. Due to Riesz’s representation theorem (
[
20
, p. 351, Th 13.32]
) for any continuous linear functional
\ell
on a Hilbert space
\mathbb{R}^{n}
there is a unique vector
v_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
\ell(x)=\langle x,v_{\ell}\rangle
for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Next consider the
(n-1)
-dimensional variety
V_{p}^{h}:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:h(z)=0\}
, which is the zero locus of the following entire function

h(z):=e^{\langle z-z_{0},v_{\ell}\rangle}-1\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Clearly
h(z_{0})=0
, and thus
z_{0}\in V_{p}^{h}
. Moreover for all
{z=x+iy\in\overline{X}+i\mathbb{R}^{n}}
it implies

|h(z)|\geq\big||e^{\langle z-z_{0},v_{\ell}\rangle}|-1\big|=\big|e^{\langle x-x_{0},v_{\ell}\rangle}-1\big|=\big|e^{\ell(x)-\ell(x_{0})}-1\big|>0,

due to that the quantity
e^{\ell(x)-\ell(x_{0})}<e^{-\epsilon}<1.

This shows
V_{p}^{h}\cap\overline{D}=\emptyset
in this case, and hence
V_{p}^{h}\cap E=\emptyset
. The case when
p\in\mathbb{R}^{n}+i(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{Y})
follows similarly by considering
\tilde{\ell},v_{\tilde{\ell}}
satisfying
\tilde{\ell}(y_{0})=0
and
\tilde{\ell}(y)<\tilde{\ell}(y_{0})-\epsilon
for all
y\in\overline{Y}
. Moreover,
\tilde{\ell}(y)=\langle y,v_{\tilde{\ell}}\rangle
for all
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Finally, choose the zero locus of the entire holomorphic function
H(z):=e^{\langle-iz+iz_{0},v_{\tilde{\ell}}\rangle}-1\text{ for all }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
to get the desired conclusion.

Hence by Definition
2.11
the proof of Claim
6.7
follows.

\square

Next note that in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the notion of convexity with respect to varieties
of dimension
(n-1)
coincides with the notion of rational convexity (see
[
25
, Page 164]
). Observe that
T
becomes an open set such that
T\cup(b\Omega\setminus E)
forms a neighborhood of
b\Omega\setminus E
in
\overline{\Omega}
, and that
E
is
\mathcal{R}(\overline{\Omega})
-convex since it is rationally convex. Hence by Theorem
2.10
, the result follows for
n\geq 3
.
∎

As an application to this theorem we obtain Theorem
1.1
for
n\geq 3
.

Corollary 6.8

(Jarnicki/Pflug,
[
10
]
)
.

In
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with
n\geq 3
, let
r_{1},r_{2},r_{3}
be real numbers such that
r_{1}<r_{2}
and
r_{1}\neq r_{3}
; then the envelope of holomorphy of the domain

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<||x||<r_{2}\}+i\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}

is given by

\{z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}^{n}:||x||<r_{2},||y||<r_{3},||y||^{2}<||x||^{2}-(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}.

In particular, the envelope is schlicht.

Proof.

Put
X=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||x||<r_{2}\}
,
Y=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||<r_{3}\}
,
D=X+iY
, and the set
{K_{x}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||x||\leq r_{1}\}}
in Theorem
6.6
.
Due to Proposition
3.3
it implies
\widehat{K}=\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}.
Therefore
E:=b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}\setminus\textsf{int}_{\overline{D}}(\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}})
is given by

\{x+iy\in\overline{D}:||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}=(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})\}\setminus\{x+iy\in\overline{D}:||x||<r_{1},||y||=r_{3}\}.

The only possible singularity of the function
{||x||^{2}-||y||^{2}-(r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2})}
is at the origin which doesn’t lie on
b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap D
since
r_{1}-r_{3}\neq 0
. This proves that the set
b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap D
is a Levi-flat hypersurface of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and hence yields condition (i) of Theorem
6.6
. For condition (ii), note that every
p=a+ib\in N:=\textsf{int}_{\overline{D}}(\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}})
lies on the
(n-1)
-dimensional analytic variety

V_{p}:=\Big\{\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}=(||a||^{2}-||b||^{2})+i\textsf{Im}(F(p))\Big\},

where
F(z)=\sum_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}^{2}
(see
[
7
, Example 4.4.(a)]
, or [Step I, Theorem
5.2
] for details). If there is any
\tilde{z}=\tilde{x}+i\tilde{y}\in V_{p}\cap E
, then due to
p=a+ib\in N
, it implies

r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}=||\tilde{x}||^{2}-||\tilde{y}||^{2}=||a||^{2}-||b||^{2}<r_{1}^{2}-r_{3}^{2}.

Therefore it yields
V_{p}\cap E=\emptyset,
and the result follows.
∎

7.
Open questions

In conclusion, the results proved earlier encourage us to list some questions for future research, which remain open to the best of our knowledge. Throughout, we assume
n\geq 2,
unless specified otherwise.

Question 7.1
.

Let
S_{\alpha}=(X+iY)\setminus K_{\alpha}
be the special domain with
Y\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any convex domain as in Definition
4.7
.

(i)

What sufficient conditions on
Y
ensure the schlicht envelope of holomorphy
\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha})
of

S_{\alpha}
?

(ii)

What is the description of
\mathcal{E}(S_{\alpha})
in this case?

(iii)

Is there an example of a good barrier
\alpha
that is not a special barrier, such that Theorem
5.2
fails?

From our discussions earlier, note that parts (i) and (ii) of Question
7.1
already have positive answers when
Y=B_{R}(0)
(see Theorem
5.2
). It might be interesting to see whether a construction analogous to
[
7
, Example 4.3]
could be used to solve part (iii).

Question 7.2

(see Remark 3 in
[
10
]
)
.

Can a result analogous to Corollary
6.8
be proved for the truncated tube domain

\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:r_{1}<||x||_{4}<r_{2}\}+i\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:||y||_{4}<r_{3}\}?

It might be interesting to understand the situation of Theorem
6.6
for
n=2
so that it also generalize Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, one may ask whether Theorem
6.6
still remains valid when the set
b\widehat{K}_{\mathcal{R}}\cap D
is not Levi-flat hypersurface of class
\mathcal{C}^{2}
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Question 7.3
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two convex domains in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and let
K\subset\mathbb{C}^{n}
be compact. Under what geometric condition on
K
does the envelope of holomorphy
\mathcal{E}((X+iY)\setminus K)
become schlicht?

Question
7.3
has a positive answer in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
(Theorem
1.2
),
when
K=K_{x}+i\overline{Y}
, where
K_{x}\subset X\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
is strictly convex (see also Theorem
3.2
). It might be interesting to study the same question under a few additional assumptions, for instance, when
K\cap(bX+i\overline{Y})=\emptyset
or
K\cap(\mathbb{R}^{n}+i\{y\})
is convex for all
y\in\overline{Y}.

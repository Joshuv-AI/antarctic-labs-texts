The gene polymorphisms of eNOS and MTHFR modulates the development of preeclampsia in Han population
Authors: Jinghong Rao, Youfang Chen, Xuan Chen, Ruoying Wu, Shimu Luo, Qingfan Lin, Zhenzhu Dong, Jingying Huang
Published: 2023

ABSTRACT
AbstractWe explored the effects of the endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) gene −786 T > C and 894 G > T locus polymorphisms and the methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) gene 1298 A > C and 677 C > T locus polymorphisms on preeclampsia (PE) in pregnant women in Quanzhou area and provide reliable and stable predictors PE. This study included 160 normal pregnant women (normal control group) and 160 women with preeclampsia (PE group). Polymorphisms in eNOS gene and MTHFR were analyzed by the polymerase chain reaction/restriction fragment length polymorphism (PCR/RFLP) technique. eNOS 894 G > T locus and MTHFR 1298 A > C locus had no significant difference between the two groups. In the PE patients, eNOS −786 T allele (OR: 2.07, p = 0.03) and MTHFR 677 C allele (OR: 1.83, p = 0.04) had significantly lower frequency. The nitric oxide (NO) level in patients with eNOS −786 C C was significantly lower than that in those with −786 TT. The homocysteine (Hcy) level in patients with MTHFR 677 TT was significantly higher than that in those with 677 C C. In conclusion, the frequency of the eNOS −786 C C genotype and MTHFR 677 TT genotype are higher in women with PE, which cause lower NO level and higher Hcy level.Keywords: Preeclampsia, eNOS, MTHFR, Polymorphism

Introduction

Preeclampsia (PE) is a syndrome of hypertension, proteinuria, and swelling that occurs in patients with normal blood pressure before pregnancy [1]. The pathogenesis of PE is induced by diverse environmental and genetic factors, including the single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) [2]. SNPs are variants in the genome occurring naturally in the human population and defined as genomic locus where two or more alternative bases occur with appreciable frequency (0.1 %) [3,4].

Endothelial nitric oxide synthase (eNOS) regulates subendothelial vascular smooth muscle function [5]. In PE, abnormal eNOS-catalyzed synthesis of nitric oxide (NO) may be a causative factor [6]. eNOS gene polymorphisms are currently used to explain changes in their protein expression or enzymatic activity and to explain the pathogenesis of clinically relevant diseases [7].

Elevated plasma homocysteine (Hcy) levels influence PE development through various mechanisms such as oxidative stress, imbalance of plasma NO/endothelin levels, and damage to vascular endothelial cells [8]. Methylenetetrahydrofolate reductase (MTHFR) is crucial in Hcy metabolism [9]. Polymorphisms have been identified within the MTHFR gene, and different mutation types have different effects on the enzymatic activity and thermal stability of MTHFR [10].

In this study, the plasma NO and Hcy levels of pregnant women with PE were detected, and the eNOS gene −786 T > C (rs2070744) and 894 G > T (rs1799983) locus polymorphisms and MTHFR gene 1298 A > C (rs1801131) and 677 C > T (rs1801133) locus polymorphisms were analyzed. We explored the effects of the above two polymorphisms and the development of PE in pregnant women in the Quanzhou area and provided reliable and stable predictors for the early prediction of PE.

Materials and methods

Participants

This candidate-gene association study included 160 normal pregnant women (normal control group) and 160 women with preeclampsia (PE group). All the participants were unrelated Han Chinese women from Quanzhou area. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Quanzhou First Hospital Affiliated to Fujian Medical University (approval number: 2020–224). Establishment of PE was based on the criteria of the American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologist. Exclusion criteria were metabolic disorders, chronic cardiovascular, hepatic, renal and endocrine diseases, Rh blood group incompatibility, and systemic diseases such as systemic lupus erythematosus before 20 weeks of gestation or before pregnancy. Participants had signed an informed consent.

Biochemical analysis

ELISA reagents were used to detect Hcy level (Axis-Shield Diagnostics Ltd) and NO level (Abcam). Blood glucose was analyzed by the Hitachi MODULAR PP analyzer. Serum total triglycerides (TG) and cholesterol (TC) were measured by commercial kits (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA). Serum high-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (HDL-C) and low-density lipoprotein-cholesterol (LDL-C) levels were analyzed by commercial kits (Y–S Biotechnology). Alanine transaminase (ALT) and aspartate transaminase (AST) levels were analyzed by chemistry analyzer (LE scientific, Horizon 850, China).

Genotype analysis

The genomic DNA was extracted from peripheral blood by using High Pure PCR Template Preparation Kit (Roche) according to the manufacturer's protocol. Genotyping of eNOS polymorphisms and MTHFR polymorphisms was performed using polymerase chain reaction (PCR) and restriction fragment length polymorphism (RFLP) analysis as previously reported [[11], [12], [13]]. The primers, annealing temperature, product lengths and restriction enzymes were as shown in Table 1. PCR analyses were carried out by using PCR Master Mix (2 × ) (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, MA, USA). PCR products were digested overnight and separated on agarose gels.

Statistical analysis

The SPSS version 22.0 was employed for statistical analysis. Data were shown in means ± standard deviation (SD) and percentage. Differences were analyzed by the Mann-Whitney U test and the Student's t-test. Frequency distributions were analyzed by the Chi-squared test and Fisher's exact test. Unconditional logistic regression analysis was performed to evaluate the relationship between gene variants and disease risk, including odds ratios (ORs) and their 95 % confidence intervals. Hardy-Weinberg equilibrium test was used to calculate the expected frequencies of variants. P < 0.05 was statistically significant.

Analyzing allelic frequencies proved that eNOS −786 T frequency was lower in the PE patients (59.38 % vs. 66.88 % in controls; p = 0.049) (Table 4). Homozygotes for the variant allele of eNOS (−786 T > C) had a 2.07 risk increase for developing preeclampsia compared to wild type homozygotes.

Discussion

NO regulates the function of endothelial cells and is produced from l-arginine by eNOS. During pregnancy, NO production is increased, which influence the vascular tone and blood flow in the placenta [14]. eNOS variants inhibit the production of NO and contribute to endothelial cell dysfunction [15]. Polymorphisms exist in various regions of the eNOS gene on chromosome 7, involving 26 exons [16].

Only a few of these 168 polymorphisms in the eNOS gene have been proved to have clinical impact [17]. eNOS 894 G > T is the most clinically important polymorphism with a Glu-Asp change in exon 7 at codon 298 [18]. When compared with GG homozygotes, eNOS 894 G > T allele carriers have decreased activity of eNOS enzyme [19]. eNOS 894 G > T SNP is correlated with the pathogenesis of coronary artery disease and hypertension [20,21].

Another polymorphism in the eNOS gene is a point mutation in the 5′-flanking area at nucleotide −786 (−786 T > C). This polymorphism significantly decreases the activity of promoter and reduces NO level [22]. eNOS −786 T > C polymorphism is correlated with diabetes and myocardial infarction [23,24]. The association between eNOS −786 T > C polymorphism and PE has been proven, and CC was more frequent in PE patients [11].

One research in the population of Iran proved that eNOS 894 G > T polymorphism has no association with PE [13]. eNOS −786 T > C is associated with the pathogenesis of PE in American and Asian populations [25,26]. Women with eNOS −786 C C genotype had significantly higher PE risk [27].

Our results showed that eNOS −786 C C frequency was significantly higher in the PE patients. In pregnant women with PE, T allele frequency of eNOS −786 T > C was significantly lower. eNOS polymorphism of 894 G > T in PE patients and healthy controls had no significant difference. To further verify the relationship between eNOS −786 T > C and PE, the corresponding NO levels were then examined. In the PE patients, serum NO levels were significantly lower. NO levels were significantly lower in patients with CC genotype compared with those with TT genotype, suggesting that the eNOS −786 T > C locus polymorphism might be involved in regulating NO production.

The pathogenesis of PE is triggered by inadequate invasion of trophoblast and subsequent enhanced oxidative stress and systemic endothelial dysfunction [28]. Inhibited trophoblast invasion in patients with PE is promoted by the destroyed balance between the methionine-Hcy cycle and oxygen-sensing pathway [29]. In the first trimester of pregnancy, serum Hcy concentration is a diagnostic predictor of PE [8].

MTHFR participates in Hcy metabolism. 677 C > T polymorphism changes amino acid 222 from Ala to Val in the enzyme catalytic domain, which reduces the activity of MTHFR [30]. In the Chinese population, mutant T allele has a high frequency (41 %) [31]. MTHFR 677 C > T polymorphism induces an elevated plasma Hcy level [32]. 677 C > T gene polymorphism of MTHFR is associated with diverse diseases, including cardiovascular disease, diabetes, and breast cancer [33,34]. The relationship between the increased risk of PE and MTHFR 677 C > T polymorphism was proved by a meta-analysis study [35]. Another study indicated that 677 C > T also participates in the impairment of renal function in PE [36].

Another MTHFR polymorphism is 1298 A > C, which changes Glu to Ala in the enzyme regulatory domain [37]. When compared with the 677 C > T SNP, the 1298 A > C SNP does not induce thermolabile enzyme generation and elevated plasma Hcy levels [38].

Our results showed that MTHFR 677 TT frequency was significantly higher in the PE patients. In pregnant women with PE, T allele frequency of MTHFR 677 C > T was significantly lower. MTHFR polymorphisms of 1298 A > C in patients with PE and healthy controls had no significant difference. To further verify the relationship between MTHFR 677 C > T and PE, the corresponding expression levels of Hcy were examined. In the PE patients, serum level of Hcy was significantly higher. Hcy levels were significantly higher in patients with TT genotype compared with those with CC genotype, suggesting that the MTHFR 677 C > T locus polymorphism might be involved in regulating NO production.

The small number of patients with PE and healthy controls is a limitation. The other limitation is the ethnic heterogeneity of the population evaluated. In future work, more samples will be collected and further composite genotype and/or haplotype analysis will be performed.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the frequency of the CC genotype for eNOS −786 T > C and TT genotype for MTHFR 677 C > T polymorphism were higher in women with PE, which cause lower NO level and higher Hcy level.

XIA’S THEOREM FOR THE FOCK SPACE H 2 ​ ( ℂ n , d ​ μ ) H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
SOLANGE BRIDGITTE DIFO

Abstract
In this paper, we provide a detailed proof for Xia’s following theorem: the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra generated by the class of weakly localized operators on H 2 ​ ( ℂ n , d ​ μ ) H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu) coincides with 𝒯 ( 1 ) \mathcal{T}^{(1)} .

Introduction

For some
\alpha>0
,
p>0
and
dV
the standard volume measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, let
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)
be the Lebesgue space of measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert^{p}_{\alpha}:=\left(\frac{p\alpha}{2\pi}\right)^{n}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}|^{p}dV(z)<\infty~.

(1.1)

Similarly, for
\alpha>0
and
p=\infty
, we denoted by
L^{\infty}_{\alpha}
the space of Lebesgue measurable function
f
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
such that

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty,\alpha}:=\text{esssup}\{|f(z)|e^{-\frac{\alpha|z|^{2}}{2}}:~z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\}<\infty~.

The classical Fock space
F^{p}_{\alpha}
is the space of entire functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which belong to
L^{p}_{\alpha}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)
. Similarly, the Fock space
F^{\infty}_{\alpha}
is the space of entire functions on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
which belong to
L^{\infty}_{\alpha}
.

Let
d\mu
be the Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
,
n\geq 1
. In terms of the standard volume measure
dV
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, it is given by

d\mu(z)=\pi^{-n}e^{-|z|^{2}}dV(z)~.

The Fock space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is defined to be the subspace of the (Hilbert-) Lebesgue space
L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
consisting of entire functions. Notice that
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)=F^{2}_{1}
. The symbol
K_{z}
denotes the reproducing kernel and the symbol
k_{z}
denotes the normalized reproducing kernel for
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
. That is,

K_{z}(\zeta)=e^{\langle\zeta,z\rangle},\quad k_{z}(\zeta)=e^{\langle\zeta,z\rangle}e^{-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}}~,\quad z,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

In
[
3
]
, J. Xia showed in the case of the Bergman space on the unit ball of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
that the norm closure of
\{T_{f}:f\in L^{\infty}(B,dv)\}
coincides with the
C^{*}
-algebra of weakly localized operators. Also, he stated in
[
3
, Section 4 ]
that the analogue of
[
3
, Theorem 1.5]
on the Fock space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
was true. In this paper, we define the notion of weakly localized operators, state Xia’s theorem for the Fock space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
and provide details of its proof. Further, we present a consequence of this theorem on the compactness of operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
. We begin with the following definitions and we state the main theorem, the proof of which will retain our attention in the following sections.

Definition 1.1
.

For
f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)
, the
Toeplitz operator

T_{f}
is defined by the formula

T_{f}h=P(fh)~,\quad h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)~,

where
P\colon L^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is the orthogonal projection.

The
standard lattice
in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is denoted by

\mathbb{Z}^{2n}=\{(m_{1}+il_{1},\dots,m_{n}+il_{n}):m_{1},l_{1},\dots,m_{n},l_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}\}~.

We fix an orthonormal set
\{e_{u}:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
. We let
S
denote the
fundamental unit cube in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
. That is,

S=\{(x_{1}+iy_{1},\dots,x_{n}+iy_{n}):x_{1},y_{1},\dots,x_{n},y_{n}\in[0,1)\}.

With
\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and
S
, we have

\cup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\{S+u\}=\mathbb{C}^{n}=\cup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\{u-S\}~,

which is a tiling of the space, meaning that there is no overlap between
S+u
and
S+v
for
u\neq v
in
\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
(resp. between
u-S
and
v-S
for
u\neq v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
).

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
denote the norm closure of
\{T_{f}:f\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)\}
in
\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu))
with respect to the operator norm. That is

\mathcal{T}^{(1)}=\{B:\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert B-T_{b_{k}}\right\rVert=0,b_{k}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)\}.

Definition 1.3
.

We denote by
\mathcal{S}
the linear span of the normalized reproducing kernels
k_{z}
. A linear operator
B:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is said to be
admissible
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
if there exists a linear operator
B^{*}:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
such that the duality relation

\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle=\langle k_{z},B^{*}k_{w}\rangle

(1.2)

holds for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

The inner product here is with respect to
d\mu
.

We define below sufficiently localized operators following J. Xia and D. Zheng (XZ) in
[
4
]
.

Definition 1.4
.

A bounded linear operator
B
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is said to be XZ-
sufficiently localized
if there exist constants
2n<\beta<\infty
and
0<C<\infty
such that

|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle|\leq\frac{C}{(1+|z-w|)^{\beta}}

(1.3)

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Definition 1.5
.

An admissible operator
B
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is said to be
weakly localized
if it satisfies the following four conditions

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)<\infty,\quad\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B^{*}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)<\infty

and

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)=0,\quad\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B^{*}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)=0~.

Example 1.6
.

If
f
is a
bounded measurable function
on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then there is a positive constant
0<C=C(f)<\infty
such that

|\langle T_{f}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|\leq Ce^{-(1/8)|z-w|^{2}}

for all
z,w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. That is,
T_{f}\in\text{XZ-}\mathcal{SL}
. Also,
T_{f}\in\mathcal{L}_{p}
for
2<p<\frac{8}{3}
.

Proof.

For each
z,w,\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

|k_{z}(\xi)k_{w}(\xi)|e^{-|\xi|^{2}}=|e^{\langle\xi,z\rangle-\frac{|z|^{2}}{2}}e^{\langle\xi,w\rangle-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}}|e^{-|\xi|^{2}}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}\left(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2}\right)}~.

By the triangular inequality we have

|z-w|^{2}\leq|z-\xi+\xi-w|^{2}\leq(|z-\xi|+|w-\xi|)^{2}\leq 2(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2})~.

It follows that

\displaystyle|k_{z}(\xi)k_{w}(\xi)|e^{-|\xi|^{2}}=e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2}\right)}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2}\right)}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2}\right)}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|z-w|^{2}}~.

(1.4)

Let
f
be a bounded measurable function on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, then for all
g,h\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
, it holds

\langle T_{f}g,h\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)g(\xi)\overline{h(\xi)}d\mu(\xi)~.

(1.5)

In fact,

\displaystyle\langle T_{f}g,h\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}T_{f}g(w)\overline{h(w)}d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K(w,\xi)f(\xi)g(\xi)d\mu(\xi)\overline{h(w)}d\mu(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)g(\xi)\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}K_{\xi}(w)\overline{h(w)}d\mu(w)d\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)g(\xi)\langle K_{\xi},h\rangle d\mu(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)g(\xi)\overline{\langle h,K_{\xi}\rangle}d\mu(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)g(\xi)\overline{h(\xi)}d\mu(\xi)~.

Therefore,

\displaystyle|\langle T_{f}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f(\xi)k_{z}(\xi)\overline{k_{w}(\xi)}d\mu(\xi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|k_{z}(\xi)k_{w}(\xi)|e^{-|\xi|^{2}}dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\pi^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|z-w|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}\left(|z-\xi|^{2}+|w-\xi|^{2}\right)}dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\pi^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|z-w|^{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|z-\xi|^{2}}dV(\xi)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|w-\xi|^{2}}dV(\xi)\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}}{\pi^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|z-w|^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|\zeta|^{2}}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\sqrt{2})^{n}\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\infty}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|z-w|^{2}}~.

This completes the proof.
∎

Proposition 1.7
.

Any XZ-sufficiently localized operator is weakly localized.

Proof.

Let
B
be a XZ-sufficiently localized operator, then for all
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{C}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\zeta|)^{\beta}}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cc_{n}}{\pi^{n}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2n-1}}{(1+r)^{\beta}}dr

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{r^{2n-1}}{(1+r)^{\beta}}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2n-1}}{(1+r)^{\beta}}dr\right]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\left[\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{(1+r)^{\beta}}dr+\int_{1}^{\infty}\frac{r^{2n-1}}{r^{\beta}}dr\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C_{n}\left\{\frac{1}{\beta-1}\left(1-\frac{1}{2^{\beta}-1}\right)+\frac{1}{\beta-2n}\right\}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle C(n,\beta)~,

which does not depend on
z
. Therefore,

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\leq C(n,\beta)<\infty~.

And in the same way, we also have

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B^{*}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\leq C(n,\beta)<\infty~.

We now show that
B
satisfies the third condition of weakly localized operator.

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, using the change of variables
\zeta=z-w
and the spherical coordinates, it holds

\displaystyle\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle dV(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{|z-w|\geq r}\frac{1}{(1+|z-w|)^{\beta}}dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\int_{|\zeta|\geq r}\frac{1}{(1+|\zeta|)^{\beta}}dV(\zeta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle Cc_{n}\int_{\rho\geq r}\frac{\rho^{2n-1}}{(1+\rho)^{\beta}}d\rho

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle Cc_{n}\int_{\rho\geq r}\frac{\rho^{2n-1}}{\rho^{\beta}}d\rho=Cc_{n}\int_{\rho\geq r}\frac{1}{\rho^{\beta-2n+1}}d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{Cc_{n}}{\beta-2n}\frac{1}{r^{\beta-2n}}~.

It follows that,

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle Bk_{z},k_{w}\rangle dV(w)\leq\frac{Cc_{n}}{\beta-2n}\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r^{\beta-2n}}=0~.

Using the duality relation (
1.2
) of an admissible operator and similarly like with
B
, we also have:

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B^{*}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle dV(w)\leq\frac{Cc_{n}}{\beta-2n}\lim_{r\to\infty}\frac{1}{r^{\beta-2n}}=0~.

∎

\mathcal{WL}

Definition 1.8
.

A
Banach algebra
is a complex algebra together with a complete norm satisfying the condition
\left\lVert xy\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert x\right\rVert\left\lVert y\right\rVert
.
A
C^{*}
-algebra
is a Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
with an involution
x\mapsto x^{*}
on it satisfying the following conditions:

\blacktriangleright

x^{**}=x
for all vectors
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

\blacktriangleright

(ax+by)^{*}=\bar{a}x^{*}+\bar{b}y^{*}
for all vectors
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{C}
.

\blacktriangleright

(xy)^{*}=y^{*}x^{*}
for all vectors
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.

\blacktriangleright

\left\lVert xx^{*}\right\rVert=\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}
for all vector
x\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 1.9
.

A Banach algebra
\mathcal{A}
is called a
\star
-algebra
if for every
A\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
A^{*}\in\mathcal{A}
.

Definition 1.10
.

We denote by
C^{*}(\mathcal{WL})
the

-algebra generated by weakly localized operators on

. Also,

is actually the norm closure of
\mathcal{WL}
since
\mathcal{WL}
is a
\star
-algebra.

We will prove the following main result.

.

We have

C^{*}(\mathcal{WL})=\mathcal{T}^{(1)}.

Preliminary Results

In this section, we will present results that will be used to establish the proof of Theorem
1.11
.

Proposition 2.1
.

The set
\mathcal{WL}
is a
\star
-algebra.

Proof.

From the definition of weakly localized operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
, we know that if
B\in\mathcal{WL}
, then also is
B^{*}
. Moreover every linear combination of two operators in
\mathcal{WL}
is also in
\mathcal{WL}
. Therefore, to complete the proof, we just have to prove that if
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{WL}
, then
B_{1}B_{2}\in\mathcal{WL}
.

Let
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathcal{WL}
, we denote indistinguishably by
C
the constant satisfying:

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{j}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)<C\quad\text{ and }\quad\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B^{*}_{j}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)<C,

for
j=1,2
. Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{1}B_{2}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},B^{*}_{1}k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}B_{2}k_{z}(\xi)\overline{B^{*}_{1}k_{w}(\xi)}d\mu(\xi)\right|dV(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle\langle k_{\xi},B^{*}_{1}k_{w}\rangle dV(\xi)\right|dV(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\right)|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)~.

Hence, we have

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{1}B_{2}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)<C\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)<C^{2}<\infty~.

We also have

\displaystyle\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B_{1}B_{2}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle||\langle k_{\xi},B^{*}_{1}k_{w}\rangle|dV(\xi)dV(w)

(2.1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}\left(\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle||\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\right)dV(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)+\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)~,

where
I_{z}(\xi)=\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle||\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)
.

For
\xi\in B(z,\frac{r}{2})
, we have
B(\xi,\frac{r}{2})\subset B(z,r)
and hence,
B(z,r)^{c}\subset B(\xi,\frac{r}{2})^{c}
. Therefore we can dominate the first integral as follows

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}\int_{|\xi-w|\geq\frac{r}{2}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle||\langle k_{\xi},B^{*}_{1}k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|\xi-w|\geq\frac{r}{2}}|\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\right)|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle C\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)~,

where
C(r)=\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|\xi-w|\geq r}|\langle B_{2}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)
. Taking the supremum on
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-\xi|<\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)\leq C\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)<C~C\left(\frac{r}{2}\right)~,

which tends to
0
as
r
goes to
\infty
from the third property of weakly localized operators.

On the other hand, the second integral in relation (
2.1
) can be dominated as follows

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)~|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}\left(\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}|\langle B_{1}k_{\xi},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)\right)|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle C\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)~.

It follows from the third property of weakly localized operators that

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}I_{z}(\xi)dV(\xi)\leq C\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-\xi|\geq\frac{r}{2}}|\langle B_{2}k_{z},k_{\xi}\rangle|dV(\xi)=0~.

Whence

\lim_{r\to\infty}\sup_{z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}}\int_{|z-w|\geq r}|\langle B_{1}B_{2}k_{z},k_{w}\rangle|dV(w)=0~.

We proved the corresponding conditions for
(B_{1}B_{2})^{*}
in the same way. This finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

There is a constant
0<C<\infty
, such that
\left\lVert E_{z}\right\rVert\leq C
for every
z\in S
.

Proof.

For
z\in S
, we define the operator

F_{z}=\frac{1}{\pi^{n/2}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e_{u}\otimes k_{u-z}.

Then we have

F^{*}_{z}=\frac{1}{\pi^{n/2}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}k_{u-z}\otimes e_{u}\text{ and }E_{z}=F^{*}_{z}F_{z}~.

In fact, for
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
,

\displaystyle F^{*}_{z}F_{z}g(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n/2}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle g,k_{u-z}\rangle F^{*}_{z}e_{u}(\xi)~

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle g,k_{u-z}\rangle\langle e_{u},e_{v}\rangle k_{v-z}(\xi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle g,k_{u-z}\rangle k_{u-z}(\xi)=E_{z}g(\xi)~.

Moreover,

F_{z}F^{*}_{z}=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle k_{u-z},k_{v-z}\rangle e_{v}\otimes e_{u}~.

Since
\left\lVert F^{*}_{z}F_{z}\right\rVert=\left\lVert F_{z}F^{*}_{z}\right\rVert
, to get
\left\lVert E_{z}\right\rVert
it suffices to estimate the latter. For every vector
x=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}x_{u}e_{u}
of
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
, we have

\displaystyle\langle F_{z}F^{*}_{z}x,x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle k_{u-z},k_{v-z}\rangle\langle x,e_{u}\rangle\langle e_{v},x\rangle

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}|\langle k_{u-z},k_{v-z}\rangle||x_{u}||x_{v}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{2}}|x_{u}||x_{v}|~.

Since
\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{2}}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}|u|^{2}}
for
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, then the function
A(u,v)=e^{-\frac{1}{2}|u-v|^{2}}
satisfies the hypotheses of the discrete Schur test with
h_{u}=1
for all
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
. Hence, from (
2.10
) we have

\langle F_{z}F^{*}_{z}x,x\rangle\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}C\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2}~.

Since
F_{z}F^{*}_{z}
is self-adjoint, it follows that
\left\lVert E_{z}\right\rVert=\left\lVert F_{z}F^{*}_{z}\right\rVert\leq C
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

There is a constant
0<C<\infty
such that the following estimate holds: Let
h_{u}\in\mathcal{H}_{*},u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, be functions satisfying the condition
\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\left\lVert h_{u}\right\rVert_{*}<\infty
. Then

\left\lVert\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}(U_{u}h_{u})\otimes e_{u}\right\rVert\leq C\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\left\lVert h_{u}\right\rVert_{*}~.

Proof.

We start by estimating
|\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle|
. For
u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}U_{u}h_{u}(\xi)~\overline{U_{v}h_{v}}(\xi)~e^{-|\xi|^{2}}dV(\xi)

(2.11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}h_{u}(u-\xi)~\overline{h_{v}(v-\xi)}~k_{u}(\xi)\overline{k_{v}(\xi)}~e^{-|\xi|^{2}}dV(\xi)~.

From relation (
1.4
), we have

|k_{u}(\xi)\overline{k_{v}(\xi)}|e^{-|\xi|^{2}}=e^{-\frac{1}{2}(|u-\xi|^{2}+|v-\xi|^{2})}\leq e^{-\frac{1}{8}|u-v|^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|u-\xi|^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{4}|v-\xi|^{2}}~.

Combining this with relation (
2.11
) and applying
\mathrm{H\ddot{o}lder}
inequality, we obtain

|\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|u-v|^{2}}\left\lVert h_{u}\right\rVert_{*}\left\lVert h_{v}\right\rVert_{*}\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|u-v|^{2}}H^{2}_{*}~,

(2.12)

where
H_{*}=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\left\lVert h_{u}\right\rVert_{*}
.

We consider the operator
A
defined by

A=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}(U_{u}h_{u})\otimes e_{u}~.

For any vector
x=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}x_{u}e_{u}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
, using (
2.12
) we have

\displaystyle\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle Ax,Ax\rangle=\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle(U_{u}h_{u})\otimes e_{u}x,(U_{v}h_{v})\otimes e_{v}x\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\langle x,e_{u}\rangle\langle e_{v},x\rangle\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle=\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\overline{x_{u}}x_{v}\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}|\overline{x_{u}}||x_{v}||\langle U_{u}h_{u},U_{v}h_{v}\rangle|\leq\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}H^{2}_{*}\sum_{u,v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|u-v|^{2}}|x_{u}||x_{v}|~.

The discrete Schur test (see Lemma
2.6
) applied to the right-hand side (with
h_{u}=1\,\forall u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
) of the later inequality, leads to

\left\lVert Ax\right\rVert^{2}\leq CH^{2}_{*}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}|x_{u}|^{2}=CH^{2}_{*}\left\lVert x\right\rVert^{2},

where
C=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}e^{-\frac{1}{8}|u|^{2}}
is finite. Since the vector
x
is arbitrary, we conclude that
\left\lVert A\right\rVert\leq C^{\frac{1}{2}}H_{*}
.
∎

Lemma 2.13

Let

, then for every

By

, for any entire function
f

\mathbb{C}^{n}

\left|f(z)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}|z|^{2}}\right|^{p}\leq C\int_{B(z,\delta)}|f(w)e^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}|w|^{2}}|^{p}dV(w)\quad\text{ for }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}.

Hence for
\alpha=p=1
and

are mutually disjoint, we have

|\langle Bk_{u-z},k_{v-w}\rangle|=|Bk_{u-z}(v-w)|e^{-\frac{|v-w|^{2}}{2}}\leq C\int_{B(v-w,\delta)}|Bk_{u-z}(\zeta)|e^{-\frac{|\zeta|^{2}}{2}}dV(\zeta).

Indeed, for
\delta<\frac{1}{2}

This implies that

That is

which tends to

from the third condition of weakly localized operators.

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\{H(R)G(R)\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}|\langle h,k_{u-z}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}|\langle h,k_{v-w}\rangle|^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\{C_{1}C_{2}H(R)G(R)\}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert h\right\rVert\left\lVert g\right\rVert~,

where

H(R)=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\sum_{\underset{|u-v|>R}{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}}|\langle Bk_{u-z},k_{v-w}\rangle|\quad\text{ and }\quad G(R)=\sup_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\sum_{\underset{|u-v|>R}{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}}|\langle Bk_{u-z},k_{v-w}\rangle|~.

Since

are arbitrary, this leads to

\left\lVert W_{R}\right\rVert\leq\{C_{1}C_{2}H(R)G(R)\}^{\frac{1}{2}}~.

From Lemma
2.13
, we have
\lim_{R\to\infty}H(R)=0
and
\lim_{R\to\infty}G(R)=0
. Therefore
\lim_{R\to\infty}W_{R}=0
.

Let us prove

(b)
. That is
V_{R}\in
span(
\mathcal{D}_{0}
) for every
R>0
.

For
R>0
and
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, we define
F_{v}=\{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}:|u-v|\leq R\}
. Since
\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
is a lattice, there is an
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that Card
(F_{v})\leq N
for every
v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
. Also, we recall that if

and
v\neq v^{{}^{\prime}}
then
|v-v^{{}^{\prime}}|\geq 1
. Then, we write
V_{R}
as follows

\displaystyle V_{R}=\frac{1}{\pi^{2n}}\sum_{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\sum_{u\in F_{v}}\langle Bk_{u-z},k_{v-w}\rangle k_{v-w}\otimes k_{u-z}.

To prove

\Gamma_{j}=\{v\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}:\text{Card}(F_{v})=j\}\quad\text{ and }\quad K_{j}=\{v-w:v\in\Gamma_{j}\}~.

Then

X_{j}\in
span(
\mathcal{D}_{0}

\gamma^{1}_{j},\cdots,\gamma^{j}_{j}:K_{j}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}^{n}

Referring to the above definitions, for each

Proposition 2.14
.

We have
\mathcal{D}_{0}\subset\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
.

To establish the proof of this proposition, we will need the next three propositions.

Proposition 2.15
.

Suppose that
\{c_{u}:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\}
is a bounded set of complex coefficients. Then for each

, the operator
Y_{z}
defined in (
2.13

\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
.

Proof.

(a)

Let us first show that

. We have
|u-v|\geq 1
for all

B(u,\frac{1}{2})\cap B(v,\frac{1}{2})=\emptyset
for
u\neq v

A_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}Y_{z}dV(z).

From Proposition
2.9
, we have the norm continuity of the map
z\mapsto Y_{z}
and it implies that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left\lVert Y_{0}-A_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert=0.

This comes from the fact that

\displaystyle\left\lVert Y_{0}-A_{\varepsilon}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}(Y_{0}-Y_{z})~dV(z)\right\rVert\leq\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}\left\lVert Y_{0}-Y_{z}\right\rVert~dV(z)

and
\lim_{z\to 0}\left\lVert Y_{z}-Y_{0}\right\rVert=0
.

Thus to prove the membership

A_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle A_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}Y_{z}dV(z)=\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\int_{B(0,\varepsilon)}c_{u}k_{u-z}\otimes k_{u-z}~dV(z)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\int_{B(u,\varepsilon)}c_{u}k_{w}\otimes k_{w}~dV(w)=\frac{1}{\pi^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{C}^{n}}f_{\varepsilon}(w)k_{w}\otimes k_{w}~dV(w)~,

where

\displaystyle f_{\varepsilon}(w)=\frac{\pi^{n}}{|B(0,\varepsilon)|}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\chi_{B(u,\varepsilon)}(w)

belongs to
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{C}^{n},dV)

and

for

. From (
2.5

. Whence

.

(b)

Let

. There is a partition

|u-v|\geq 1

. We have
Y_{z}=Y_{z,1}+\cdots+Y_{z,m}

Y_{z,i}=\sum_{u-z\in K_{i}}c_{u}k_{u-z}\otimes k_{u-z},

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. By (a), we have
Y_{z,i}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
for all

.

∎

To continue our work, we need to introduce the following functions. For each pair
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}

K_{z;\alpha}(\zeta)=\zeta^{\alpha}e^{\langle\zeta,z\rangle}=\zeta^{\alpha}K_{z}(\zeta)~,\qquad\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n},

where
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
\{c_{u}:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\}
be a bounded set of complex numbers. For every pair
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we have

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;\alpha})\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}.

Proof.

We will prove this proposition by an induction on
|\alpha|
.

If
|\alpha|=0
, that is
\alpha=0
, then from (
2.4
) and Proposition
2.15
, it holds

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;0})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{|z|^{2}}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}~k_{u-z}\otimes k_{u-z}=e^{|z|^{2}}Y_{z}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}~.

Let
k\in\mathbb{N}
, assume that the proposition is true for every
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
satisfying the condition
|\alpha|\leq k
. Now consider the case where
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
is such that
|\alpha|=k+1
. Then, we can decompose
\alpha

\alpha=a+b
, where
|a|=k
and
|b|=1
. Thus, there exists
\nu\in\{1,\dots,n\}
such that
b_{\nu}=1
and
b_{j}=0
for

. By the induction hypothesis , we have

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;a})\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}\qquad\text{ for every }z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}~.

(2.15)

Let
z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For each
t>0
, we define the following operators

A_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z+tb})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z+tb;a})\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z+itb})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z+itb;a})~.

We also define

X=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\{(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;\alpha})+(U_{u}K_{z;b})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;a})\}\text{ and }

Y=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\{(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;\alpha})-(U_{u}K_{z;b}\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;a}))\}.

We will show that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert\frac{1}{t}(A_{t}-A_{0})-X\right\rVert

\displaystyle=0\,,\quad\text{ and }

(2.16)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert\frac{1}{it}(B_{t}-B_{0})-Y\right\rVert

\displaystyle=0.

(2.17)

Before getting to their proofs, let us first see the consequence of these limits. By (
2.15
), we have
A_{t}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
and
B_{t}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
for all
t>0
. Hence (
2.16
) and (
2.17
) implies that
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
. Therefore

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z})\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;\alpha})=\frac{1}{2}(X+Y)\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)},

completing the induction on
|\alpha|
.

Let us prove (
2.16
). We have
\frac{1}{t}(A_{t}-A_{0})=G_{t}+H_{t}
, where

\displaystyle H_{t}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z+tb})\otimes\{U_{u}(K_{z+tb;a}-K_{z;a})\}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\{U_{u}(K_{z+tb}-K_{z})\}\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;a})~.

Similarly, we write
X=V+W
, where

Then
\left\lVert\frac{1}{t}(A_{t}-A_{0})-X\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert H_{t}-V\right\rVert+\left\lVert G_{t}-W\right\rVert
, and (
2.16
) will follow if we prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert H_{t}-V\right\rVert

\displaystyle=0\quad\text{ and }

(2.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert G_{t}-W\right\rVert

\displaystyle=0~.

(2.19)

To prove (
2.18
), we write
H_{t}-V=S_{t}+T_{t}
, where

\displaystyle S_{t}

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z+tb})\otimes\{U_{u}(t^{-1}(K_{z+tb;a}-K_{z;a})-K_{z;\alpha})\}\quad\text{ and }

\displaystyle=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\{U_{u}(K_{z+tb}-K_{z})\}\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;\alpha}).

Thus to prove (
2.18
), we just have to prove that
\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert S_{t}\right\rVert=0
and
\lim_{t\to 0}\left\lVert T_{t}\right\rVert=0

S_{t}
in the form
S_{t}=S^{(1)}_{t}\left(S^{(2)}_{t}\right)^{*}
where

\displaystyle S^{(1)}_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{z+tb})\otimes e_{u}\quad\text{ and }\quad S^{(2)}_{t}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}\{U_{u}(t^{-1}(K_{z+tb;a}-K_{z;a})-K_{z;\alpha})\}\otimes e_{u}~.

Then it follows from Lemma
2.8
that

\left\lVert S^{(1)}_{t}\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert K_{z+tb}\right\rVert_{*}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\lVert S^{(2)}_{t}\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert\frac{1}{t}(K_{z+tb;a}-K_{z;a})-K_{z;\alpha}\right\rVert_{*}

Since
a+b=\alpha
with
|b|=1
and

2.18

G_{t}-W=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}\{U_{u}(t^{-1}(K_{z+tb}-K_{z})-K_{z;b})\}\otimes(U_{u}K_{z;a})=Z_{t}T^{(2)*}~,

From Lemma
2.8

\left\lVert Z_{t}\right\rVert\leq C\left\lVert t^{-1}(K_{z+tb}-K_{z})-K_{z;b}\right\rVert_{*}\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0~.

Hence
\left\lVert G_{t}-W\right\rVert\leq\left\lVert T^{(2)}\right\rVert\left\lVert Z_{t}\right\rVert\underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}0
. Thus we have completed the proof of (
2.16

The proof of (
2.17

Proposition 2.17
.

Let
\{c_{u}:u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}\}
be a bounded set of complex coefficients. Then for every
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
we have

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}k_{u}\otimes k_{u-w}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}~.

Proof.

For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
, we define on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
the following monomial function

p_{\alpha}(\zeta)=\zeta^{\alpha}~.

For
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and
w\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
, we define

d_{u}(w)=c_{u}e^{-iIm\langle u,w\rangle}.

For all
\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}
and
u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
, we have
K_{0;\alpha}=p_{\alpha}
and
U_{u}K_{0}=U_{u}\mathbbm{1}=k_{u}
. Thus, applying Proposition
2.16
in the case
z=0
, we have that

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}k_{u}\otimes(U_{u}p_{\alpha})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}(U_{u}K_{0})\otimes(U_{u}K_{0;\alpha})~\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}~.

Hence

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes(U_{u}p_{\alpha})~\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}~.

(2.20)

We define the function
g_{w}(\zeta)=\langle\zeta,w\rangle~,\zeta\in\mathbb{C}^{n}
. For each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the operators

A_{j}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes U_{u}g^{j}_{w}\quad\text{ and }\quad G=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}(U_{u}K_{w})\otimes e_{u}~.

Since each
g^{j}_{w}
is in the linear span of
\{p_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{n}\}
, then it follows from (
2.20
) that
A_{j}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Also, for each
j\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the factorization
A_{j}=TB^{*}_{j}
, where

T=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes e_{u}\quad\text{ and }\quad B_{j}=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}(U_{u}g^{j}_{w})\otimes e_{u}~.

Hence, by Lemma
2.8
, the operator
T
is bounded and for each
k\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle\left\lVert G-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}B_{j}\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}U_{u}\{K_{w}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}g^{j}_{w}\}\otimes e_{u}\right\rVert

(2.21)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert K_{w}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}g^{j}_{w}\right\rVert_{*}~.

Using the expansion formula
e^{c}=\displaystyle\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{j!}c^{j}
for every
c\in\mathbb{C}^{n}\,,
we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert K_{w}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}g^{j}_{w}\right\rVert_{*}=0~.

Combining this with (
2.21
), we obtain

\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert TG^{*}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}A_{j}\right\rVert\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}\left\lVert T\right\rVert\left\lVert G^{*}-\sum_{j=0}^{k}\frac{1}{j!}B^{*}_{j}\right\rVert=0~.

Since each
A_{j}
belongs to
\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
and
k_{w}=e^{-\frac{|w|^{2}}{2}}K_{w}
, we conclude that

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes(U_{u}K_{w})=TG^{*}\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}\quad\text{ and then }\quad\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes(U_{u}k_{w})\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}~.

From the definition of
d_{u}(w)
and (

), we have that

\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}d_{u}(w)k_{u}\otimes(U_{u}k_{w})=\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}}c_{u}k_{u}\otimes k_{u-w}.

This completes the proof.

Proof of Proposition

Since the map

\Omega_{1}\cup\dots\cup\Omega_{m}\supset\mathcal{K}\quad\text{ and }\quad\left\lVert k_{z_{i}}-k_{w}\right\rVert_{*}<\varepsilon\quad\text{ whenever }\quad w\in\Omega_{i},i\in\{1,\dots,m\}.

From that open cover of
\mathcal{K}
, we obtain a partition
\mathcal{K}=E_{1}\cup\cdots\cup E_{m}
such that
E_{i}\subset\Omega_{i}
for every
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. We now define
\Gamma_{i}=\{u\in\mathbb{Z}^{2n}:\psi(u)\in E_{i}\}
,
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
. Then
\left\lVert k_{z_{i}}-k_{\psi(u)}\right\rVert_{*}<\varepsilon
if
u\in\Gamma_{i}
. For all
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, we also define

B_{i}=\sum_{u\in\Gamma_{i}}(U_{u}k_{z_{i}})\otimes e_{u}~.

Then for each
i\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, and by (
2.3
) , we have

AB^{*}_{i}=\sum_{u\in\Gamma_{i}}d_{u}e^{-iIm\langle u,z_{i}\rangle}k_{u}\otimes k_{u-z_{i}}=\sum_{u\in\Gamma_{i}}d_{u,i}k_{u}\otimes k_{u-z_{i}}~,

where
d_{u,i}=d_{u}e^{-iIm\langle u,z_{i}\rangle}
and
|d_{u,i}|=|d_{u}|=|c_{u}|
for
u\in\Gamma_{i}
. Thus, it follows from Proposition
2.17
that

\{AB^{*}_{1},\dots,AB^{*}_{m}\}\subset\mathcal{T}^{(1)}.

(2.22)

On the other hand, we have

B-(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Gamma_{i}}\{U_{u}(k_{\psi(u)}-k_{z_{i}})\}\otimes e_{u}~.

It follows from the fact that
\Gamma_{1},\dots,\Gamma_{m}
form a partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{2n}
and from Lemma
2.8
that

\left\lVert B-(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{m})\right\rVert\leq C\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\sup_{u\in\Gamma_{i}}\left\lVert k_{\psi(u)}-k_{z_{i}}\right\rVert_{*}\leq C\varepsilon~.

We also have from Lemma
2.8
that
A
is a bounded operator. Hence

\displaystyle\left\lVert T-(AB^{*}_{1}+\cdots+AB^{*}_{m})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert AB^{*}-(AB^{*}_{1}+\cdots+AB^{*}_{m})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert B^{*}-(B^{*}_{1}+\cdots+B^{*}_{m})\right\rVert

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\lVert A\right\rVert\left\lVert B-(B_{1}+\cdots+B_{m})\right\rVert

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\left\lVert A\right\rVert\varepsilon~.

Since this inequality holds for an arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, then combined with (
2.22
) , we conclude that
T\in\mathcal{T}^{(1)}
. This completes the proof.

Proof of Theorem

.

Since

\mathcal{WL}\subset\mathcal{T}^{(1)}

Let

, then from (
2.8

B=\int_{S}\int_{S}E_{w}BE_{z}dV(w)dV(z)~.

(3.1)

From Proposition
2.10

(3.2)

defined from

into
\mathcal{B}(H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu))

In the continuation of our work, we define the notion of Berezin transform and we state a corollary of Theorem
1.11
which highlights its consequence on the compactness of bounded linear operators on the Fock space
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
. Foremost we recall the definition of a compact operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
.

Definition 3.1
.

A bounded linear operator
T
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is
compact
if for every sequence
\{f_{n}\}_{n}
of elements of
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
converging weakly to zero in
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
, the sequence
\{Tf_{n}\}_{n}
converges to zero with respect to the norm topology of
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
A
be a bounded linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
. The
Berezin transform
of
A
is the function denoted by
B(A)
and defined by

B(A)(z)=\langle Ak_{z},k_{z}\rangle~,\qquad z\in\mathbb{C}^{n}~.

Remark 3.3
.

We can reformulate the result of Bauer and Isralowitz
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
as follows: A bounded linear operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
is compact if and only if it belongs to the
C^{*}
-algebra generated by weakly localized operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}^{n},d\mu)
and its Berezin transform vanishes at infinity.

Corollary 3.4
.

A weakly localized operator is compact if and only if its Berezin transform vanishes at infinity.

Proof.

The proof follows directly from
[
1
, Theorem 1.1]
and Theorem
1.11
.
∎

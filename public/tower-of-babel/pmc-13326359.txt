National dialysis registry of the Dominican Republic: first nationwide epidemiological analysis of renal replacement therapy
Authors: Adhanellys Cáceres-Murillo, Erwin Iván Campos, Hector Martínez, Ana Carolina De La Cruz, Gertrudis De La Cruz, Wendy Rodríguez, Rafael Robles, Rafael Martínez, Yamile Anaya-Jimenez, Miguel Angel Cuevas-Budhart, Eliana Dina Batlle
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundKidney failure is a growing global health concern, affecting 9–10% of the population and contributing to rising mortality. In the Dominican Republic, a national dialysis registry was created in 2020 to monitor patients receiving kidney replacement therapy (KRT). Yet no comprehensive national epidemiological report has been published to date.MethodsWe conducted a nationwide, cross-sectional study using data from the 2022 Dominican National Dialysis Registry, encompassing all patients, both private and public, on hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) in National Health Service, authorized units. Data were collected via a standardized digital form (August–September 2023) and analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics v22.ResultsA total of 4,690 patients were recorded, median age 56 years (SD ± 15.6), 69% male. HD predominated (78.4%). Hypertension (93.9%) and diabetes mellitus (44.4%) were the most frequent comorbidities, with diabetes more common in PD than HD (52.5% vs. 42.2%). In HD, arteriovenous fistula was the primary access (61.2%); in PD, continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) was predominant (80.4%). National prevalence reached 421.8 patients per million population (91.1 ppm PD, 330.7 ppm HD), a 6% increase from 2022.ConclusionsThis first nationwide analysis provides a comprehensive characterization of the epidemiological profile of KRT in the Dominican Republic, revealing the dominance of HD, the high burden of cardiovascular and metabolic comorbidities, and rising prevalence rates. These findings underscore the urgent need to strengthen kidney failure prevention, improve early detection, and enhance the integration of PD within a balanced renal care strategy aligned with regional best practices.

Introduction

Kidney failure (KF) affects an estimated 9–10% of the global population and is among the leading causes of death from non-communicable diseases, with prevalence and mortality continuing to rise over recent decades [1–3]. The condition is associated with substantial healthcare costs, reduced quality of life, and a markedly shortened life expectancy [4–6]. Hypertension and diabetes mellitus are the predominant risk factors worldwide, and their growing prevalence fuels the increasing KF burden, particularly in low- and middle-income countries [5, 7, 8].

National and regional renal registries are essential for quantifying the burden of KF, monitoring trends in kidney replacement therapy (KRT), and guiding health policy and resource allocation in Latin America. The Latin American Dialysis and Transplant Registry (RLADTR), created in the early 1990s under the auspices of the Latin American Society of Nephrology and Hypertension (SLANH), has facilitated comparative analysis of KRT prevalence, incidence, and modality distribution across member countries [9–11]. However, not all countries have robust, routinely updated surveillance systems.

In the Dominican Republic (DR), the lack of a unified national database hindered the ability to assess the scale and characteristics of the dialysis population. This gap was addressed in 2020 with the creation of the Dominican National Dialysis Registry, mandated by Ministerial Resolution No. 00011, which established compulsory reporting of all patients undergoing chronic HD or PD in the country [12, 13].

The present study describes the sociodemographic, clinical, and therapeutic characteristics of patients in KRT in the DR using data from the 2022 National Dialysis Registry. By providing a comprehensive national overview, these findings aim to inform strategies for early detection, prevention, equitable access, and quality improvement in renal care.

Methods

Study design

We conducted a nationwide, observational, descriptive, cross-sectional study using data from the Dominican Republic National Dialysis Registry.

Population and setting

The registry included all adult and pediatric patients receiving KRT with hemodialysis (HD) or peritoneal dialysis (PD) in facilities authorized by the Ministry of Public Health. These 114 centers corresponded to all National Health Service–authorized dialysis units active during the study period. Of these, 110 centers submitted data, while 4 declined participation by institutional decision, resulting in a response rate of 96.4%., primarily located in major provinces such as Santo Domingo National District and Santiago de los Caballeros.

To enhance completeness and data accuracy, final-year nephrology residents were assigned to coordinate data collection directly with each dialysis center using the standardized digital form. Nevertheless, because reporting ultimately depended on center-level records and verification, complete national capture cannot be fully guaranteed, and the possibility of residual underreporting is acknowledged.

Data collection

Data were collected between August and September 2023; however, all epidemiological estimates presented in this analysis correspond to the most recent completed calendar year (2022). Data from 2023 were used exclusively to describe registry growth and completeness and were not used for annual incidence or prevalence estimates, as they do not represent a complete calendar year.

Information was obtained through a standardized digital questionnaire (Google Forms) completed by medical and administrative staff at each dialysis center. The instrument included demographic, clinical, therapeutic, and epidemiological variables that had been previously defined and validated by the Dominican Society of Nephrology (SODONEF) [12].

Variables and analysis

Demographic variables included age, sex, province of residence, and type of health insurance; classified according to the national health insurance system as contributory, subsidized, private/uninsured, or none/not available.

Contributory insurance refers to employment-based health coverage financed through mandatory contributions, while subsidized insurance corresponds to government-funded coverage for individuals without formal employment or with low income. Insurance classification was based on the status reported by each dialysis center at the time of data collection. The category “private/uninsured” reflects a single predefined option in the original registry form and includes patients either covered by private insurance or without any health insurance, as these two conditions were not disaggregated at the time of data collection. Cases in which insurance information was missing or not reported were classified as none/not available.

Clinical variables included the presence of diabetes mellitus and hypertension (reported as comorbid conditions, not necessarily as primary causes of kidney failure), viral serology for hepatitis B virus (HBV), hepatitis C virus (HCV), and human immunodeficiency virus (HIV), residual urine output, and medication use. HBV seropositivity refers specifically to hepatitis B surface antigen (HBsAg) positivity, as reported by the treating center.

Residual kidney function (defined as the presence of residual urine output greater than approximately 100 mL per day) was recorded as a dichotomous variable (yes/no) based on center-reported presence of residual urine output at the time of data entry. No standardized quantitative measurements (e.g., urine volume thresholds or clearance calculations) were collected, and the frequency of assessment varied across centers.

Dialysis modality was classified as HD or PD. For HD patients, vascular access type was categorized as autologous arteriovenous fistula, prosthetic arteriovenous fistula, tunneled catheter, or non-tunneled catheter. For PD patients, modality was classified as continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD) or automated peritoneal dialysis.

Infectious complications were recorded as the number of infectious episodes reported by the center during the reference period, without standardized differentiation between peritonitis and other infection types. Consequently, peritonitis rates per patient-year could not be calculated, and comparisons with international benchmarks should be interpreted cautiously.

Categorical variables were expressed as absolute frequencies and percentages, while continuous variables were summarized as means with standard deviations (SD) or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR), depending on distribution. Normality was assessed by visual inspection of histograms and Q–Q plots, complemented with homogeneity testing. Given the descriptive nature of the study, no hypothesis testing or imputation of missing data was performed.

The national prevalence of patients on kidney replacement therapy (KRT) was calculated as the number of active patients per million population (pmp), using the official 2022 population estimate for the Dominican Republic. No hypothesis testing or imputation of missing data was performed, given the descriptive nature of the study.

All analyses were conducted using IBM SPSS Statistics, version 24 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Ethical considerations

The study was approved by the Dominican Society of Nephrology and conducted in compliance with Ministerial Resolution No. 00011 (May 26, 2020), which mandates compulsory reporting of all patients receiving chronic kidney replacement therapy in authorized facilities nationwide. Confidentiality was safeguarded by anonymizing all records. As data collected for epidemiological surveillance and analyzed in aggregate form, individual informed consent was not required.

Results

A total of 4,690 patients receiving chronic KRT were included in the national registry for the reference year 2022. Of these, the majority were treated with HD, while a smaller proportion received PD.

The median age of the dialysis population was 56 years (SD ± 15.6), with a predominance of men (69%). HD was the most frequent modality, accounting for 78% of patients. Residual urine output (defined as the presence of residual urine output greater than approximately 100 mL per day) at the time of data collection was reported in 53.6% of patients (Table 1).

The geographic distribution of dialysis patients showed marked concentration in the Santo Domingo National District (37.1%) and Santiago de los Caballeros (24.2%), while provinces such as Santo Domingo (9.2%), La Vega (3.9%), Puerto Plata (3.4%), and San Pedro de Macorís (3.3%) reported substantially lower proportions. Other provinces, including Duarte, San Cristóbal, Monseñor Nouel, and Valverde, each accounted for less than 3% of patients (Fig. 1). This pattern reflects the centralization of dialysis care in urban areas with greater health infrastructure, in contrast with the limited availability of services in rural and peripheral regions.

With respect to the comorbidities of kidney failure, 44.6% of patients had diabetes mellitus, with a higher prevalence among those on peritoneal dialysis (PD) compared to HD (52.5% vs. 42.2%). Hypertension was present in 94% of the cohort. For serological markers, 96% tested negative; HIV positivity was 1.3%, hepatitis B virus (HBV) 1.2%, and hepatitis C virus (HCV) 1.6% (Table 2).

In HD, among 3,677 patients, the predominant vascular access was the arteriovenous fistula, used in 61.2% of cases (59.1% autologous and 2.1% prosthetic), whereas catheters accounted for the remaining patients (19.5% tunneled and 19.3% non-tunneled) (Fig. 2). Regarding infectious complications related to vascular access, 62% of patients had no episodes during the study period. Among those affected, 19% experienced one episode, 10% two episodes, 3% three episodes, and 6% four or more events, reflecting a complete distribution across the cohort. These findings are relevant given the known association between central venous catheter use and adverse outcomes in hemodialysis patients [14].

In PD, 84.9% of patients had no infectious complications during the study period. Regarding modality, 80.4% were treated with continuous ambulatory peritoneal dialysis (CAPD), while the remainder used automated regimens. It should be noted that the definition of “infectious complications” was not standardized; clarifying whether this term refers exclusively to peritonitis would allow calculation of peritonitis rates per patient-year, a key quality indicator in PD.

In terms of temporal trends, the dialysis population increased by 7.6% compared to the 2022 census, mainly due to growth in HD (> 9.7%), whereas PD showed a slight increase (< 0.5%).

Considering a national population of 11.1 million inhabitants in 2022, the overall prevalence of patients on KRT was 421.8 pmp, with 330.7 pmp for HD and 91.1 pmp for PD (Table 4).

Discussion

This first nationwide analysis of the DR Dialysis Registry offers a comprehensive overview of the epidemiological profile of KF patients undergoing KRT. It serves as a key reference for continuing surveillance in the country. The dominance of HD (78%) is consistent with reports from García-García et al., who, through the Latin American Dialysis and Transplant Registry, noted that most countries in the region have more than 70% of patients on this modality [1]. Similarly, Cusumano et al. identified hemodialysis as the main treatment method across Latin America, primarily influenced by health policies and resource availability [2]. This is different from the findings of Paniagua et al. in Mexico, where strategic efforts allowed PD to reach nearly 50% of patients, aided by public policies and institutional agreements [3]. Although the distribution of dialysis modalities is described at a national level, the cross-sectional design precludes formal evaluation of causal determinants. Nevertheless, the observed expansion of PD in the country may plausibly be influenced by evolving health policies and institutional agreements, a hypothesis that warrants further longitudinal assessment. In the DR, the lower adoption of PD seems more related to the expansion of HD centers and referral habits of physicians than to supply shortages. Other factors, such as limited PD training, expertise opportunities and clinical perceptions, may also play a secondary role.

This report represents a descriptive, cross-sectional analysis based on registry data and does not aim to establish causal associations or longitudinal outcomes.

The high prevalence of hypertension (94%) and diabetes mellitus (44.4%) as comorbidities is consistent with findings by Bikbov et al., who identified these conditions as the leading causes of end-stage kidney failure worldwide and in the region [15]. Wainstein M et al. also highlighted this pattern in Latin America, emphasizing its impact on disease burden and mortality [5]. Similar results have been reported by Sanabria et al. in Colombia and Lorca et al. in Chile, where diabetes accounted for 35–50% of incident dialysis cases [6, 7]. These findings reinforce the urgent need for comprehensive preventive strategies targeting cardiovascular and metabolic risk factors from early disease stages.

The number of patients reported by dialysis centers over recent years reflects registry-reported initiation of chronic kidney replacement therapy; however, these data should not be interpreted as formal incidence estimates.

With respect to prevalence, the 421.8 patients pmp recorded in 2022 positions the DR within the range of countries of comparable socioeconomic profile, as reported by Cusumano et al. [2] and Wainstein M et al. [5]. The high use of erythropoietin (92.3%) and antihypertensive therapy (92.5%) exceeds levels reported in some Central American and Caribbean nations, where access to essential drugs remains limited, as described by Luxardo R. et al. [1]. However, despite the comparatively high use of necessary medications in the DR, disparities in insurance coverage and the proportion of uninsured patients continue to underscore persistent inequities in access to renal care across Latin America [9].

In PD, 85% of patients reported no infectious complications. Although a low proportion of infectious complications was reported among PD patients, the registry did not apply standardized definitions for peritonitis or collect event-based denominators; therefore, these findings should be interpreted cautiously and cannot be directly compared with international quality indicators; a figure that, if corroborated, closely aligns with the International Society for Peritoneal Dialysis (ISPD) standard of < 0.5 episodes per patient-year as reported by Li et al. [9]. However, the lack of a universally accepted definition for infectious complications and the absence of formally calculated peritonitis rates in this report hamper meaningful international benchmarking. In contrast, a recent national, multicentric retrospective cohort study in the DR (conducted between January 1, 2016, and December 31, 2021) found an overall peritonitis rate of 0.13 episodes per patient-year, markedly lower than ISPD targets, with 575 episodes recorded among 4,575 patients [16]. Additionally, during the COVID-19 pandemic (April to June 2021), under a telemedicine-supported care protocol for 946 PD patients, the peritonitis rate was reported at 0.11 episodes per patient-year [17, 18]. These results underscore the effectiveness of structured protocols, continuous quality improvement, and telemedicine in maintaining low peritonitis rates within the Dominican context—reinforcing findings from Mexico [15] and Chile [7] and highlighting a strategic opportunity to further strengthen PD programs locally [3, 9].

Residual kidney function was reported as the presence of residual urine output more than 100 mL per day at the time of data collection; however, due to the cross-sectional design and lack of standardized quantitative assessment, no conclusions can be drawn regarding preservation of residual kidney function by dialysis modality [19]. Independent of this finding, PD remains a cost-effective modality with potential advantages in accessibility and decentralized care delivery. Within a balanced national KRT strategy, promotion of PD alongside strengthening kidney transplantation programs as the definitive therapeutic goal may contribute to improved system sustainability and equitable access to care.

Provincial dialysis prevalence, adjusted using official 2022 census data, showed marked geographic variation, ranging from 13.4 to 1,690.8 patients pmp. The highest rates were observed in Distrito Nacional and Santiago, while several provinces reported rates below 200 pmp (Table 4). These differences should be interpreted considering the unequal distribution of hemodialysis units across the country, as some provinces lack local facilities and depend on referral to urban centers, whereas peritoneal dialysis programs have a broader geographic reach.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, the self-reported nature of the data may have introduced underreporting or variability in quality across centers. Second, the lack of standardized definitions for infectious complications limits comparability with international benchmarks, particularly regarding peritonitis rates in peritoneal dialysis. Third, the cross-sectional design precluded longitudinal follow-up to assess survival or to calculate age- and sex-adjusted incidence rates. Finally, incomplete reporting of primary kidney failure etiologies constrained the depth of etiological analysis.

These limitations underscore the need to strengthen data standardization, completeness, and periodicity of the registry, as well as to incorporate clinical and outcome indicators that improve epidemiological surveillance and inform renal health policy.

Future development of the national registry will focus on standardized variable definitions, improved data validation, and longitudinal patient identifiers to enable analyses of survival, technique failure, and modality transitions.

Conclusion

This study presents the first nationwide analysis of the Dominican National Dialysis Registry, offering a comprehensive profile of patients on renal replacement therapy. The findings confirm the predominance of HD, the high prevalence of hypertension and diabetes, and the greater preservation of residual renal function in peritoneal dialysis. Beyond documenting current trends, the results underscore critical challenges and opportunities: strengthening kidney failure prevention and early detection, addressing regional disparities in access to care, and promoting balanced strategies that integrate PD within a comprehensive renal care framework. The Dominican National Dialysis Registry marks a milestone for the country, providing robust data to inform evidence-based decision-making, optimize resource allocation, and align national strategies with regional and global standards. Ensuring its continuity and expansion will be crucial to improving clinical outcomes, reducing inequities, and sustaining the long-term resilience of renal care in the Dominican Republic.

SU(n)-structures through quotient by torus actions
PERES Quentin

Abstract
. We show that if ( X , g , J , ω ) \displaystyle(X,g,J,\omega) is a Kähler manifold with an S ​ U ​ ( n + s ) \displaystyle SU(n+s) -structure and a Hamiltonian holomorphic action of a compact torus T s \displaystyle T^{s} , then the usual symplectic quotient Y \displaystyle Y inherits an S ​ U ​ ( n ) \displaystyle SU(n) -structure provided the existence of special 1 \displaystyle 1 -forms on X \displaystyle X , called twist forms. We then give several applications of our results: on complex projective spaces, on cones over Fano Kähler-Einstein manifold and on toric ℂ ​ ℙ 1 \displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1} bundles. We also study the geometry behind these structures in the case of n = 3 \displaystyle n=3 .



1.
Introduction

String theory requires, in addition to the four-dimensional spacetime, a six-dimensional compact manifold, called the compactification space. To obtain realistic particle physics model, this space should admit an
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure with torsion
[
6
]
. Such structures were considered in
[
3
]
where Eells and Salamon use the Levi-Civita connection of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
to transform the standard integrable complex structure of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
in a non-integrable complex structure. In
[
8
,
9
]
, Larfors, Lüst and Tsimpis also study the question of producing
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures with torsion on
\displaystyle 6
-dimensional toric manifolds.

Let us recall some basic facts about
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structures. A smooth manifold
\displaystyle X
of real dimension
\displaystyle 2n
admits an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure if
\displaystyle X
is an almost Hermitian manifold with a complex volume form, i.e
\displaystyle X
has a Hermitian metric
\displaystyle h
, an almost complex structure
\displaystyle J
, an almost symplectic form
\displaystyle\omega
which is
\displaystyle(1,1)
with respect to
\displaystyle J
, such that
\displaystyle h,\omega
are
\displaystyle J
-invariant with
\displaystyle\omega=-\Ima(h)
, and
\displaystyle\Omega
a nowhere-vanishing complex volume form of type
\displaystyle(n,0)
. Equivalently,
\displaystyle X
admits an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure if :

i)

There exists a nondegenerate real
\displaystyle 2
form
\displaystyle\omega
.

ii)

There exists a locally decomposable nowhere-vanishing complex
\displaystyle n
form
\displaystyle\Omega
.

iii)

\displaystyle\Omega,\omega
satisfy the
\displaystyle SU(n)
-equations:

(1)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(2)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=c_{n}\omega^{n},

where
\displaystyle c_{n}=\frac{2^{n}}{n!}(-i)^{n^{2}}
and
\displaystyle\omega^{n}=\omega\wedge\cdots\wedge\omega

\displaystyle n
-times. Given this data, we recover the almost complex structure
\displaystyle J
and the Hermitian metric
\displaystyle h
such that
\displaystyle\Omega
is
\displaystyle(n,0)
and
\displaystyle\omega
is
\displaystyle(1,1)
.

In
[
8
]
, Larfors, Lüst and Tsimpis give an explicit method to construct
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on
\displaystyle 6
-dimensional toric manifolds. Their approach consists in using the standard
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{n}
and pushing everything down to the toric quotient by algebraic manipulations. Our goal is to generalize this construction in the framework of Kähler geometry.

1.1.
A twist construction

Let
\displaystyle(X,h_{X},J_{X},\omega_{X},\Omega_{X})
be a Kähler manifold with an
\displaystyle SU(n+s)
-structure given by
\displaystyle(\omega_{X},\Omega_{X})
. Consider a Hamiltonian and holomorphic action of a compact torus
\displaystyle T^{s}
on
\displaystyle X
with moment map
\displaystyle\mu
. We let
\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}:=Lie(T^{s})
be the Lie algebra of
\displaystyle T^{s}
and for an element
\displaystyle a\in\mathfrak{t}
, we denote
\displaystyle V_{a}
the vector field induced by
\displaystyle a
on
\displaystyle X
.

Denote
\displaystyle M
a regular level set of
\displaystyle\mu
,
\displaystyle Y=M/\!\!/T^{s}
the symplectic quotient which is an orbifold of real dimension
\displaystyle 2n
,
\displaystyle i:Y\hookrightarrow M
the inclusion map and
\displaystyle\pi:M\to Y
the projection map. Starting from
\displaystyle(\Omega_{X},\omega_{X})
the
\displaystyle SU(n+s)
-structure on
\displaystyle X
, we aim to construct
\displaystyle(\Omega_{Y},\omega_{Y})
an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure on
\displaystyle Y
. For that, we shall construct basic forms on
\displaystyle M
satisfying the same equations as
\displaystyle(\ref{SU(n)eq1})
and
\displaystyle(\ref{SU(n)eq2})
.

Descending
\displaystyle\Omega_{X},\omega_{X}
to
\displaystyle Y
requires to introduce twist forms:

Definition 1
.

Let
\displaystyle(X,J,T^{s})
be a complex manifold with an action of
\displaystyle T^{s}
. A
\displaystyle 1
-form
\displaystyle\alpha
on
\displaystyle X
is a twist form if:

(1)

\displaystyle\alpha
is a nowhere vanishing
\displaystyle(1,0)
-form with respect to
\displaystyle J
.

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha
is horizontal:
\displaystyle\forall a\in\mathfrak{t},\alpha(V_{a})=0
.

(3)

\displaystyle\alpha
is charge definite : there exists a linear form
\displaystyle k_{\alpha}:\mathfrak{t}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\forall a\in\mathfrak{t},\mathcal{L}_{V_{a}}\alpha=k_{\alpha}(a)\alpha.

In that case,
\displaystyle k_{\alpha}
is called the charge of
\displaystyle\alpha
.

Similarly, we say that
\displaystyle\Omega_{X}
is charge definite of charge
\displaystyle q:\mathfrak{t}\to i\mathbb{R}
if

\forall a\in\mathfrak{t},\mathcal{L}_{V_{a}}\Omega_{X}=q(a)\Omega_{X}

which is always the case for
\displaystyle T
-invariant Calabi-Yau cones
[
10
]
. Our main goal is to provide the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
\displaystyle(X,h_{X},J_{X},\omega_{X},\Omega_{X})
be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
\displaystyle n+s
with
\displaystyle(\Omega_{X},\omega_{X})
an
\displaystyle SU(n+s)
-structure on
\displaystyle X
with charge operator
\displaystyle q
taking values in the pure imaginary numbers. Consider a free, Hamiltonian and holomorphic action of a compact torus
\displaystyle T^{s}
on
\displaystyle X
with moment map
\displaystyle\mu
and let
\displaystyle M
be a regular level set of
\displaystyle\mu
. Assume there exists a twist form
\displaystyle\alpha_{X}
on
\displaystyle X
of charge
\displaystyle k_{\alpha}=\frac{q}{2}
. Then, for any basis
\displaystyle e_{1},\cdots,e_{s}
of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}
with induced vector fields
\displaystyle V_{e_{i}}=V_{i}
, the forms:

\Omega=i^{s}\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\bigg(\overline{\alpha_{M}}\cdot(\iota_{V_{1}}\cdots\iota_{V_{s}}\Omega_{X})_{M}\bigg),\,\omega=\omega_{M}-\frac{i}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}

descend to
\displaystyle Y
and satisfy the equations:

(3)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(4)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=c_{n}\|\alpha_{M}\|^{4}\|V_{1}\|^{2}\cdots\|V_{s}\|^{2}\omega^{n},

where
\displaystyle\overline{\alpha_{M}}\cdot
is the contraction of forms by the complex vector field induced by the Hermitian metric
\displaystyle h_{X}
and for a form
\displaystyle\Phi_{X}
on
\displaystyle X
,
\displaystyle\Phi_{M}
is the pullback of
\displaystyle\Phi_{X}
on
\displaystyle M
.

From that, we readily infer that:

Corollary B
.

The toric quotient
\displaystyle Y=M/\!\!/T^{s}
admits an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure.

Note that the hermitian structure underlying the
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure obtained on
\displaystyle Y
is not the one obtained by Kähler reduction. In fact, the examples detailed in Section
\displaystyle 4
suggest that
\displaystyle\omega
is never closed and the underlying almost complex structure never integrable.

Remark 1.

If
\displaystyle\alpha
is a twist form for a torus
\displaystyle T
, then it is a twist form for any subtorus
\displaystyle T^{\prime}\subset T
. In particular, Theorem
A
can be applied for various subtorus.

We then generalize Theorem
A
using multiple twist forms and an orthogonality assumption, see Definition
2.2.1
.

Theorem C
.

Let
\displaystyle(X,h_{X},J_{X},\omega_{X},\Omega_{X})
be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
\displaystyle n+s
with
\displaystyle(\Omega_{X},\omega_{X})
an
\displaystyle SU(n+s)
-structure on
\displaystyle X
with charge operator
\displaystyle q
taking values in the pure imaginary numbers. Consider a free, Hamiltonian and holomorphic action of a compact torus
\displaystyle T^{s}
on
\displaystyle X
with moment map
\displaystyle\mu
and let
\displaystyle M
be a regular level set of
\displaystyle\mu
. Let
\displaystyle\{\alpha^{i}_{X}\}_{1\leq i\leq l}
be a family of orthogonal twist forms on
\displaystyle X
of charges
\displaystyle k_{\alpha_{1}}+\cdots+k_{\alpha_{l}}=\frac{q}{2}
. Then, for any basis
\displaystyle e_{1},\cdots,e_{s}
of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}
with induced vector fields
\displaystyle V_{e_{1}}=V_{i}
, the forms:

\Omega=i^{s}\overline{\alpha^{1}}_{M}\wedge\cdots\wedge\overline{\alpha^{l}}_{M}\wedge\bigg(\overline{\alpha^{1}}_{M}\cdot...\cdot\overline{\alpha^{l}}_{M}\cdot(\iota_{V_{1}}\cdots\iota_{V_{s}}\Omega_{X})_{M}\bigg),

\omega=\omega_{M}-\sum_{k=1}^{l}\frac{i}{\|\alpha^{k}_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha^{k}_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha^{k}}_{M}

are basic and satisfy the equations:

(5)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\omega

\displaystyle\displaystyle=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Omega\wedge\overline{\Omega}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=(-1)^{l+1}c_{n}\|\alpha^{1}_{M}\|^{4}\cdots\|\alpha^{l}_{M}\|^{4}\|V_{1}\|^{2}\cdots\|V_{s}\|^{2}\omega^{n}.

Remark 2.
If we choose another basis of
\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}
, then
\displaystyle\Omega
is multiplied by a real constant. Similarly, if we choose another family of orthogonal twist forms with the same charges that spans the same space, then
\displaystyle\Omega
is invariant.

1.2.
Existence and space of twist forms

Many (non-compact) Kähler Hamiltonian
\displaystyle T
-spaces with an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure are known in the literature, for example all toric Calabi-Yau cones
[
4
]
, and provide potential applications of Theorems
A
and
C
. However, both theorems require to assume the existence of a twist form of appropriate charge. This leads us to address the question of existence of twist forms. We first find an explicit family of holomorphic twist forms of polynomial type on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
with
\displaystyle N=2n
even of charge
\displaystyle 2
for the diagonal action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
.

Proposition D
.

Let
\displaystyle N=2n\geq 2
be even and
\displaystyle M=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
an invertible skew-symmetric
\displaystyle N\times N
complex matrix. Then, the form:

\alpha_{M}:={}^{t}ZMdZ=\sum_{i,j=1}^{N}z_{i}m_{ij}dz_{j}

is a holomorphic twist form of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
for the diagonal action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
. Conversely, any holomorphic twist form of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
for the diagonal action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
is of this form.

We also prove that holomorphic twist forms on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
of charge strictly greater than
\displaystyle 2
do not exist, see Proposition
3.1.1
.

Explicit examples and torsion

Our first application is motivated by the example of an
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
in
[

,

]
. We generalize this example by using the family of Proposition
D
in the case
\displaystyle N=4

A
then gives a whole new family of
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}

Theorem E
.

Consider
\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{4},\Omega_{0},\omega_{0})
with its canonical
\displaystyle SU(4)
-structure and with complex holomorphic Euler vector field
\displaystyle\xi
. Let
\displaystyle M=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq 4}
be a skew-symmetric invertible
\displaystyle 4\times 4
complex matrix. Denote

\alpha_{M}:={}^{t}ZMdZ,

the corresponding holomorphic twist form of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{4}\backslash\{0\}
. Then, the forms:

\Omega_{M}:=\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge(\overline{\alpha_{M}}\cdot(\iota_{\xi}\Omega_{0})),\,\omega_{M}:=\omega_{0}-\frac{i}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}

define an
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}

The family given by Proposition
D
suggests that we should apply Theorem
C
for higher dimensional projective spaces. More precisely, we want to use our family of twist forms of charge
\displaystyle 2
to provide to
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4n-1}
an
\displaystyle SU(4n-1)
-structure. To apply our result, we use a Gram-Schmidt process to produce an orthogonal family of twist forms out of our family. However, this produces zeros failing to satisfy the condition of nowhere-vanishing. Consequently, we can still apply Theorem
A

C

-structures outside the zeros of our family. More precisely, we show the following result for
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{7}

Theorem F
.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two generic holomorphic twist forms of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{8}\backslash\{0\}
with its canonical
\displaystyle SU(8)
-structure
\displaystyle(\Omega_{0},\omega_{0})

\displaystyle\beta_{1},\beta_{2}
the resulting forms of the Gram-Schmidt process. Then, the following forms:

\Omega:=\frac{1}{\|\beta_{1}\|^{2}\|\beta_{2}\|^{2}}\overline{\beta_{1}}\wedge\overline{\beta_{2}}\wedge(\overline{\beta_{1}}\cdot\overline{\beta_{2}}\cdot\iota_{\xi}\Omega_{0}),\,-\omega=\omega_{0}-\frac{i}{\|\beta_{1}\|^{2}}\beta_{1}\wedge\overline{\beta_{1}}-\frac{1}{\|\beta_{2}\|^{2}}\beta_{2}\wedge\overline{\beta_{2}},

define a smooth
\displaystyle SU(7)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{7}
outside four generic projective lines in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{7}

In this example, note that the orthogonal twist forms are not holomorphic. Moreover, the singularities are studied and cannot be extended continuously.
We then review the example of toric
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}

In particular,

Let

\displaystyle a=2-n

2.
A twist reduction

2.1.
An abstract version of the reduction process of Larfors,Tsimpis and Lüst

Let
\displaystyle(X,h_{X},J_{X},\omega_{X},\Omega_{X})
be a Kähler manifold of complex dimension
\displaystyle n+s
with an
\displaystyle SU(n+s)
-structure given by
\displaystyle(\Omega_{X},\omega_{X})
. In particular,
\displaystyle\Omega_{X}
is a nowhere-vanishing complex form of type
\displaystyle(n,0)
and
\displaystyle\omega_{X}
is a Kähler form of type
\displaystyle(1,1)
and satisfy the equations (
1
) and (
2
).

Consider a free, Hamiltonian and holomorphic action of a compact torus
\displaystyle T^{s}
on
\displaystyle X
with moment map
\displaystyle\mu:X\to\mathfrak{t}^{*}
where
\displaystyle\mathfrak{t}:=Lie(T^{s})
. For
\displaystyle a\in\mathfrak{t}
, we let
\displaystyle V_{a}
to be the vector field induced on
\displaystyle X
by the action:

V_{a}(p):=\frac{d}{dt}\bigg|_{t=0}\exp(ta)\cdot p.

The Hamiltonian condition reads as
\displaystyle\mu
being invariant by the action and

\forall a\in\mathfrak{t},\iota_{V_{a}}\omega_{X}=-d\langle\mu,a\rangle.

Let
\displaystyle c
be a regular value of
\displaystyle\mu
and
\displaystyle M=\mu^{-1}(c)
the corresponding level set. We denote by
\displaystyle Y=M/\!\!/T^{s}
the quotient manifold which is a manifold of real dimension
\displaystyle 2n
. For any differential form
\displaystyle\Phi_{X}
on
\displaystyle X
, we write
\displaystyle\Phi_{M}=i^{*}\Phi_{X}
for the induced form on
\displaystyle M
where
\displaystyle i:M\hookrightarrow X
is the inclusion map. A form
\displaystyle\Phi_{X}
descends to the quotient
\displaystyle Y
if there exists a form
\displaystyle\Phi_{Y}
on
\displaystyle Y
such that
\displaystyle\Phi_{M}=\pi^{*}\Phi_{Y}
with
\displaystyle\pi:M\to Y
the projection map. Recall that
\displaystyle\Phi_{X}
descends to the quotient
\displaystyle Y
iff
\displaystyle\Phi_{M}

2.2.
A multiple twist theorem

In this section, we prove Theorem

Definition 2.2.1
.

Two
\displaystyle 1
-forms
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta
on
\displaystyle X

\overline{\alpha}\cdot\beta=\overline{\beta}\cdot\alpha=0.

We now give a proof of Theorem
C

\displaystyle l=2

Proof.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha_{X},\beta_{X}
be two orthogonal twist forms on
\displaystyle X
of respective charges
\displaystyle k_{\alpha},k_{\beta}
such that
\displaystyle k_{\alpha}+k_{\beta}=\frac{q}{2}
. Set

\hat{\Omega}^{\prime}:=\overline{\alpha_{M}}\cdot\Omega_{M},\,\hat{\omega}^{\prime}:=\omega_{M}-\frac{i}{2\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}},

\hat{\Omega}:=\overline{\beta_{M}}\cdot\hat{\Omega}^{\prime},\,\omega^{\prime}:=\hat{\omega}^{\prime}-\frac{i}{2\|\beta_{M}\|^{2}}\beta_{M}\wedge\overline{\beta_{M}}.

All the computations done in Section
\displaystyle 2.1
are valid for
\displaystyle(\hat{\Omega}^{\prime},\hat{\omega}^{\prime})

\displaystyle(\hat{\Omega},\hat{\omega})
to equations (

) and (
10
). Contracting equation (

\displaystyle\overline{\beta_{M}}
gives :

(11)

\hat{\Omega}\wedge\hat{\omega}^{\prime}+(-1)^{n-1}\hat{\Omega}^{\prime}\wedge\overline{\beta_{M}}\cdot\hat{\omega}^{\prime}=0.

By the orthogonality assumption, we have

\overline{\beta_{M}}\cdot\hat{\omega}^{\prime}=\overline{\beta_{M}}\cdot\omega_{M}=\frac{i}{2}\overline{\beta_{M}}.

Plugging this into equation (

Let

3.
Geometric applications

3.1.
Twist forms on
\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{N},(\mathbb{C}^{*})_{diag})

This section is motivated by the example of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
in
[
9
,
8
]
where the authors find an explicit twist form on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{4}
and also compute the torsion of the constructed
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure. We review this example and also find a family of twist forms of charge
\displaystyle 2
. Using this family, we provide a whole family of
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. We also prove that on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
, holomorphic twist forms of polynomial type of charge greater than
\displaystyle 2
do not exist.

Consider
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
with its canonical Kähler structure with Kähler form

\omega_{0}:=\frac{i}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{N}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z_{j}}.

We also let
\displaystyle\Omega_{0}
be the standard holomorphic volume form on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
given by

\Omega_{0}:=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{N}.

\displaystyle(\Omega_{0},\omega_{0})
defines the standard
\displaystyle SU(N)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
. Theorem
A
relies on the existence of twist forms. It is natural to wonder whether they exist and we first study this question on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
with the standard action of
\displaystyle T^{1}=S^{1}
.

Let
\displaystyle\xi:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}z_{j}\partial_{j}
be the holomorphic Euler vector field introduced in Section
\displaystyle 2.1
for this action. We restrict our attention to holomorphic twist forms, so that only
\displaystyle\xi
matters in the computations. Let

\alpha:=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\alpha_{j}dz_{j}

be a holomorphic twist form of integer charge
\displaystyle k=k_{\alpha}(\xi)\geq 1
. Since the functions
\displaystyle z_{j}
are of charge
\displaystyle 1
, we get that the holomorphic functions
\displaystyle\alpha_{j}
must be of charge
\displaystyle k-1
. But, this reads as

\sum_{l=1}^{N}z_{l}\frac{\partial\alpha_{j}}{\partial z_{l}}=(k-1)\alpha_{j},

and forces
\displaystyle\alpha_{j}
to be homogeneous polynomials of degree
\displaystyle k-1
. This shows that any holomorphic twist form on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}
with its diagonal circle action must be of polynomial type.

Rewriting
\displaystyle\alpha=\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}dz_{j}
with
\displaystyle P_{j}
homogeneous polynomials of degree
\displaystyle k-1
, the horizontality condition is

\forall z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\sum_{j=1}^{N}P_{j}(z)z_{j}=0.

The vanishing points of
\displaystyle\alpha
are then the common zeros of the
\displaystyle P_{j}
and as they are homogeneous, at least
\displaystyle 0
is a vanishing point of
\displaystyle\alpha
. Thus, we will require that
\displaystyle\alpha
is nowhere-vanishing on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
.

We first focus on the case
\displaystyle k=2
and we show the classification result given by the Proposition
D
. We introduce the fundamental forms

\alpha_{ij}:=z_{i}dz_{j}-z_{j}dz_{i},

for
\displaystyle 1\leq i,j\leq N
. These fundamental forms are horizontal and charge definite of charge
\displaystyle 2
. Using these fundamental forms, we can prove Proposition
D
:

Proof.

If
\displaystyle M=(m_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is a skew-symmetric invertible
\displaystyle N\times N
complex matrix and
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}:=\sum_{i<j}m_{ij}\alpha_{ij}
is horizontal and charge definite of charge
\displaystyle 2
since the
\displaystyle\alpha_{ij}
satisfy these properties. By construction,
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
vanishes on the kernel of
\displaystyle M
which is zero by assumption. Conversely, if
\displaystyle\alpha
is a holomorphic twist form of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
, then we already know that
\displaystyle\alpha
must be of the form

\alpha={}^{t}ZMdZ,

where
\displaystyle M
is some
\displaystyle N\times N
complex matrix. Note that
\displaystyle M
is invertible since
\displaystyle\alpha
is nowhere-vanishing on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
. Eventually, the horizontality condition forces
\displaystyle M
to be skew-symmetric.
∎

Remark 3.

Note that this also proves that on even dimensional

\displaystyle 2

Theorem

where
\displaystyle J=\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\

,

]

\displaystyle 4

Remark 4.
We have chosen here to work with the diagonal action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
, but we could have worked with other actions of
\displaystyle S^{1}
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{4}
and have
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on weighted complex projective spaces. More precisely, if
\displaystyle k=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3},k_{4})\in\mathbb{Z}^{4}
denotes the charge of the weighted
\displaystyle S^{1}

to make them twist forms for
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}_{k}

We now turn our attention to twist forms of charge
\displaystyle k>2
to see if we can apply Theorem
A
such as we did for
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
. However, it turns out that no holomorphic twist forms on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
of charge
\displaystyle k>2
exist. More precisely, we show that:

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
\displaystyle(P_{j})_{1\leq j\leq N}
be
\displaystyle N
homogeneous complex polynomials of degree
\displaystyle k\geq 2
in
\displaystyle N
variables. If

\forall z=(z_{1},\cdots,z_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\sum_{i=1}^{N}z_{i}P_{i}(z)=0,

then the
\displaystyle P_{j}
have a non trivial common root.

Proof.

Homogeneous polynomials of
\displaystyle N
variables of degree
\displaystyle k
are precisely holomorphic sections of the line bundle
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(k)
over
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{N-1}
. By duality, we can see the
\displaystyle P_{j}
as morphisms from
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(-k)
to
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}
the trivial line bundle. Assume the
\displaystyle P_{j}
have only zero as common root. Then, the map
\displaystyle P_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus P_{N}:\mathcal{O}(-k)^{N}\to\mathcal{O}
is surjective. Consider its kernel
\displaystyle K
. The global sections of
\displaystyle K
consist of homogeneous polynomials, say
\displaystyle Q_{1},\cdots,Q_{N}
, such that

\sum_{i}Q_{i}P_{i}=0.

If
\displaystyle d
is the degree of the
\displaystyle Q_{i}
, the global sections are sections of
\displaystyle K(d)=K\otimes\mathcal{O}(d)
. We have the following exact sequence

0\longrightarrow K\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(-k)^{\oplus N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}\longrightarrow 0

Tensoring with
\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(d)
gives

0\longrightarrow K(d)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(-k+d)^{\oplus N}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}(d)\longrightarrow 0

In particular, it induces the following sequence

0\longrightarrow H^{0}(K(d))\longrightarrow H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(-k+d))^{\oplus N}

It is well-known that if
\displaystyle-k+d<0
, then
\displaystyle H^{0}(\mathcal{O}(-k+d))=0
and thus
\displaystyle H^{0}(K(d))=0
for
\displaystyle d<k
. In conclusion if
\displaystyle k\geq 2
, then the
\displaystyle Z_{i}
cannot be in
\displaystyle K
.
∎

We go back to the

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

We outline the Gram-Schmidt process to obtain such a family.

Definition 3.2.1

Let
\displaystyle\alpha
be a nowhere-vanishing
\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle\alpha

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}

\forall\Phi\in T^{*}\mathbb{C}^{N},P_{\alpha}(\Phi)=\Phi-\frac{\overline{\alpha}\cdot\Phi}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}\alpha.

The following lemma describes how the orthogonal projectors behave with
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

Lemma 3.2.2
.

Let
\displaystyle\alpha,\beta
be two forms of
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
. We have :

i)

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\beta)
is a
\displaystyle(1,0)
-form orthogonal to
\displaystyle\alpha

ii)

\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\beta)
is horizontal, charge definite of charge
\displaystyle 2

iii)

The norm of
\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\beta)
is

\|P_{\alpha}(\beta)\|^{2}=\|\beta\|^{2}-\frac{|\overline{\alpha}\cdot\beta|^{2}}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}.

In particular, if
\displaystyle\alpha
and
\displaystyle\beta
are linearly independent, then
\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\beta)
is nowhere-vanishing.

Proof.

Points
\displaystyle i)
and
\displaystyle iii)
are straightforward.
\displaystyle P_{\alpha}(\beta)
is horizontal and is of charge
\displaystyle 2
as
\displaystyle\frac{\overline{\alpha}\cdot\beta}{\|\alpha\|^{2}}
is invariant.
∎

This process extends to many forms of
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
by induction in the same way as for the usual Gram-Schmidt process. However, there is no subfamily of
\displaystyle n\geq 2

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}

\displaystyle SU(4n-1)

\displaystyle n

\displaystyle n=2

Lemma 3.2.3

Let
\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
be two generic twist forms of
\displaystyle\mathcal{T}
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{8}\backslash\{0\}
.
\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are linearly dependent at four different complex planes in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{8}
.

Proof.

Denote
\displaystyle M_{1},M_{2}
the corresponding invertible and skew-symmetric matrices.
\displaystyle\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
are collinear at
\displaystyle z\in\mathbb{C}^{8}\backslash\{0\}
iff
\displaystyle z
is an eigenvector of the matrix
\displaystyle M_{1}^{-1}M_{2}
. If
\displaystyle M_{1},M_{2}
are chosen generically, the matrix
\displaystyle M_{1}^{-1}M_{2}
has four distinct eigenvalues with algebraic multiplicity equal to
\displaystyle 2

∎

This lemma together with Theorem

shows the first part of Theorem

\displaystyle M_{1},M_{2}
of the form
\displaystyle M_{1}=\diag(J,J,J,J)

\displaystyle M_{2}=\diag(\lambda_{1}J,\lambda_{2}J,\lambda_{3}J,\lambda_{4}J)
with complex
\displaystyle\lambda_{i}

\displaystyle r(z)=|z|^{2}

\displaystyle r_{\lambda}(z)=\lambda_{1}(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2})+\lambda_{2}(|z_{3}|^{2}+|z_{4}|^{2})+\lambda_{3}(|z_{5}|^{2}+|z_{6}|^{2})+\lambda_{4}(|z_{7}|^{2}+|z_{8}|^{2})

\beta={}^{t}Z\bigg(M_{2}-\frac{r_{\lambda}(z)}{r(z)}M_{1}\bigg)dZ

Introduce

\beta={}^{t}Z\diag(\rho_{1}(z)J,\rho_{2}(z)J,\rho_{3}(z)J,\rho_{4}(z)J)dZ

In particular, we have:

\displaystyle\displaystyle\beta\wedge\bar{\beta}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\rho_{i}\bar{\rho}_{j}(z_{2i-1}dz_{2i}-z_{2i}dz_{2i-1})\wedge(\bar{z}_{2j-1}d\bar{z}_{2j}-\bar{z}_{2j}d\bar{z}_{2j-1})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+z_{2i}\bar{z}_{2j}dz_{2i-1}\wedge d\bar{z}_{2j-1})

We see that the zeros of
\displaystyle\beta
are where only one
\displaystyle\rho_{i}
vanishes.
Take
\displaystyle Z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
of norm
\displaystyle 1
and consider the sequences
\displaystyle Z_{\varepsilon_{1}}=(Z_{1},(\varepsilon,0),0,0)
and
\displaystyle Z_{\varepsilon_{2}}=(Z_{1},(0,\varepsilon),0,0)
. A long computation shows that:

\frac{\beta\wedge\bar{\beta}}{\|\beta\|^{2}}(Z_{\varepsilon_{1}})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\to}dz_{4}\wedge d\bar{z}_{4},\,\frac{\beta\wedge\bar{\beta}}{\|\beta\|^{2}}(Z_{\varepsilon_{2}})\underset{\varepsilon\to 0}{\to}dz_{3}\wedge d\bar{z}_{3}

which proves that the limit does not exist.

3.3.
Revisiting
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on toric
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles

In
[
8
]
, Larfors examines toric
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundles over the Hirzebruch surfaces
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}
.

We start by recalling basic facts and definitions about these surfaces, following
[
5
]
. There are several ways of defining Hirzebruch surfaces, here we focus on the toric description.

Consider the space
\displaystyle Y=\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
together with an action
\displaystyle\sim_{n}
of the compact torus
\displaystyle T^{2}
, where
\displaystyle n\in\mathbb{Z}
, given by :

\forall(\lambda,\mu)\in T^{2},\forall z=(z_{0},z_{1})\times(z^{\prime}_{0},z^{\prime}_{1})\in Y,(\lambda,\mu)\cdot z=(\lambda z_{0},\lambda z_{1})\times(\lambda^{n}\mu z^{\prime}_{0},\mu z^{\prime}_{1}).

Then, the Hirzebruch surface
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}
is
\displaystyle Y/\sim_{n}
.
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}
is then by construction a toric surface.

We now consider
\displaystyle X
a toric
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}
. From Delzant theory, see e.g
[
2
]
, we can describe
\displaystyle X
as a toric quotient of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}\times\mathbb{C}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
by
\displaystyle T^{3}
given by the charges :

q^{1}=(1,1,n,0,0,a),\,q^{2}=(0,0,1,1,0,b),\,q^{3}=(0,0,0,0,1,1),

where
\displaystyle a,b\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Note that the first four components of the charges correspond to the charges of
\displaystyle\mathbb{F}_{n}
. The parameters
\displaystyle a,b
describe how twisted
\displaystyle X
is in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
and
\displaystyle q^{3}
corresponds to the
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{1}
fiber of
\displaystyle X
.

We thus work in
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{6}
with its usual
\displaystyle SU(6)
-structure. The
\displaystyle T^{3}
action on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{6}
generates the holomorphic vector fields:

\xi_{1}:=z_{1}\partial_{1}+z_{2}\partial_{2}+nz_{3}\partial_{3}+az_{6}\partial_{6},\xi_{2}:=z_{3}\partial_{3}+z_{4}\partial_{4}+bz_{6}\partial_{6},\xi_{3}:=z_{5}\partial_{5}+z_{6}\partial_{6},

which gives the charge of
\displaystyle\Omega_{0}=dz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{6}
:

q(\xi_{1})=2+n+a,q(\xi_{2})=2+b,q(\xi_{3})=2.

The moment map is

\mu(z):=\bigg(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}+n|z_{3}|^{2}+a|z_{6}|^{2},|z_{3}|^{2}+|z_{4}|^{2}+b|z_{6}|^{2},|z_{5}|^{2}+|z_{6}|^{2}\bigg).

In what follows, we assume that
\displaystyle a=2-n,b=-2
. On a positive regular value, set, as in
[
8
]
,

\alpha_{1}:=-z_{2}dz_{1}+z_{1}dz_{2},\alpha_{2}:=-z_{4}dz_{3}+z_{3}dz_{4}+\frac{nz_{3}z_{4}}{|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}}(\overline{z_{1}}dz_{1}+\overline{z_{2}}dz_{2})

A simple computation shows that

k_{\alpha_{1}}(V_{1})=2i,k_{\alpha_{1}}(V_{2})=0,k_{\alpha_{1}}(V_{3})=0

k_{\alpha_{2}}(V_{1})=in,k_{\alpha_{2}}(V_{2})=2i,k_{\alpha_{2}}(V_{3})=0

Letting

\alpha:=z_{5}\alpha_{1}+z_{6}\alpha_{2}

we get a non-holomorphic twist form and therefore Theorem
A
can be applied to get an
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure on
\displaystyle X
.

3.4.
Calabi-Yau cones over Fano Kähler-Einstein manifolds

In this paragraph, we use the theory of Fano Kähler-Einstein manifolds to provide a setting where Theorem
A
can be applied. We first recall the main facts about Fano Kähler-Einstein manifolds:

Definition 3.4.1
.

An almost complex manifold
\displaystyle(Y,J)
is called Fano if:

c_{1}(Y,J)=c_{1}(-K_{Y})>0,

where
\displaystyle K_{Y}
is the canonical line bundle and
\displaystyle-K_{Y}
the anticanonical line bundle. Alternatively,
\displaystyle-K_{Y}
is an ample line bundle.

The most important result is the following, see e.g
[
11
]
:

Proposition 3.4.2
.

Let
\displaystyle(Y,J,\omega)
be a Fano Kähler-Einstein manifold. Then,
\displaystyle K_{Y}\backslash Y
is a Calabi-Yau cone.

More explicitly, the holomorphic volume form is given locally by:

\Omega_{K_{Y}}=d(ydz_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge dz_{n}),

where the
\displaystyle(z_{k})
are holomorphic coordinates on
\displaystyle Y
and
\displaystyle y
the fiber coordinate. We can now give a proof of Theorem
H
:

Proof.

Recall that the Fano index of
\displaystyle Y
is defined as the maximal integer
\displaystyle r
such that there exists a holomorphic line bundle
\displaystyle L
satisfying
\displaystyle-K_{Y}=L^{\otimes r}
. In what follows, we assume that
\displaystyle r
is a multiple of
\displaystyle 4
so that we can write
\displaystyle-K_{Y}=L^{\otimes 4}
with
\displaystyle L
a very ample line bundle. Denote
\displaystyle\pi:z\in L^{-1}\backslash Y\mapsto z^{\otimes 4}\in K_{Y}\backslash Y
and consider the fiberwise
\displaystyle S^{1}
action on
\displaystyle L^{-1}\backslash Y
. This action extends to
\displaystyle K_{Y}\backslash Y
to make
\displaystyle\pi
an
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant map. Using the local formula of
\displaystyle\Omega_{K_{Y}}
, we see that
\displaystyle\Omega_{K_{Y}}
is of charge
\displaystyle 4
for this action and thus
\displaystyle\Omega=\pi^{*}\Omega_{K_{Y}}
is a holomorphic volume form on
\displaystyle L^{-1}\backslash Y
of charge
\displaystyle 4
with respect to the fiberwise action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
and pulling back the Hermitian structure of
\displaystyle K_{Y}\backslash Y
, we have a Calabi-Yau structure on
\displaystyle L^{-1}\backslash Y
for which the fiberwise
\displaystyle S^{1}
action is Hamiltonian. Kodaira’s embedding theorem gives a holomorphic
\displaystyle S^{1}
-equivariant map
\displaystyle\Phi:L^{-1}\backslash Y\hookrightarrow\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
where
\displaystyle N=\dim H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}(L))
. Without loss of generality, we can assume
\displaystyle N
to be even and if
\displaystyle\beta
is a holomorphic twist form of charge
\displaystyle 2
on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\{0\}
, its pullback
\displaystyle\alpha
defines a
\displaystyle(1,0)
-form which is horizontal and of charge
\displaystyle 2
for the fiberwise action of
\displaystyle S^{1}
on
\displaystyle L^{-1}\backslash Y
. Theorem
A
applies and we obtain an
\displaystyle SU(n)
-structure on
\displaystyle Y
with possible singularities. Eventually, this also works by taking any smooth section of the vector bundle
\displaystyle(T^{1,0}Y)^{*}\otimes L^{\otimes 2}
.
∎

Application :
This gives a new perspective on how to get an
\displaystyle SU(4n-1)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4n-1}
. Indeed, if
\displaystyle X=\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4n-1}
, it is well-known that
\displaystyle-K_{X}=\mathcal{O}(4n)=\mathcal{O}(n)^{\otimes 4}
. The Kodaira embedding theorem then gives a map
\displaystyle\varphi:\mathcal{O}(-n)\backslash\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4n-1}\to\mathbb{C}^{2m}\backslash\{0\}
where
\displaystyle 2m=\dim H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}(n))=\binom{5n-1}{n}
.
\displaystyle\varphi
is explicitly given by:

\varphi([z_{0},\cdots,z_{4n-1}])=[z_{0}^{n},z_{0}^{n-1}z_{1},\cdots,z_{4n-1}^{n}],

the components of the image of
\displaystyle\varphi
are precisely the homogeneous monomials of degree
\displaystyle n
. Theorem
H
applies and provides to any
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{4n-1}
an
\displaystyle SU(4n-1)
-structure. In particular for
\displaystyle n=1
, we exactly get back the
\displaystyle SU(3)
structures of Theorem
E
.

4.
Torsion of
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}

In this section, we study the torsion of the
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structures on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
given by Theorem
E
. We first recall that if
\displaystyle(X,\omega,\Omega)
is a manifold with an
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure, its torsion is characterized by:

d\omega=\frac{3}{2}\Ima(\overline{W_{1}}\Omega)+W_{4}\wedge\omega+W_{3},

d\Omega=W_{1}\omega^{2}+W_{2}\wedge\omega+\overline{W_{5}}\wedge\Omega,

where
\displaystyle W_{1}
is a smooth complex function,
\displaystyle W_{2}
is a primitive
\displaystyle(1,1)
form,
\displaystyle W_{3}
is a real primitive
\displaystyle(1,2)+(2,1)
form,
\displaystyle W_{4}
is a real
\displaystyle(1,0)+(0,1)
form and
\displaystyle W_{5}
is a complex
\displaystyle(1,0)
form. The
\displaystyle W_{i}
are known to be the torsion classes and were first described by Gray and Hervella in
[
7
]
. Each
\displaystyle W_{i}
contributes to a special feature of the
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure, e.g, the integrability of the complex structure is entirely described by
\displaystyle W_{1}
and
\displaystyle W_{2}
.

Let
\displaystyle(\mathbb{C}^{4},\omega_{0},\Omega_{0})
be the canonical
\displaystyle SU(4)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{4}
. Let
\displaystyle M
be a complex skew-symmetric and invertible
\displaystyle 4\times 4
complex matrix and
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
the corresponding holomorphic twist form introduced in the Propositon
D
. The
\displaystyle SU(3)
-structure on
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
is given by

\Omega_{M}:=\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\hat{\Omega}_{M},\omega_{M}:=\omega_{0}-\frac{i}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}.

The key observation to computing the torsion classes is:

Lemma 4.0.1
.

Let
\displaystyle M
be a skew-symmetric and invertible
\displaystyle 4\times 4
complex matrix and
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
the corresponding holomorphic twist form.
Then, there exists
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}
a smooth complex function and
\displaystyle\eta_{M}
a
\displaystyle(1,0)
-form such that

d\alpha_{M}=\lambda_{M}\hat{\Omega}_{M}+\alpha_{M}\wedge\eta_{M}.

Proof.

\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
being holomorphic,
\displaystyle d\alpha_{M}
is of type
\displaystyle(2,0)
. A simple computation shows that
\displaystyle d\alpha_{M}\wedge d\alpha_{M}=8Pf(M)\Omega_{0}
where
\displaystyle Pf(M)
is the Pfafian of
\displaystyle M
. Contracting by
\displaystyle\xi
and
\displaystyle\overline{\alpha_{M}}
gives
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}=\frac{2Pf(M)}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}
and
\displaystyle\eta_{M}=\frac{\overline{\alpha_{M}}\cdot d\alpha_{M}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}
.
∎

In particular, we note that
\displaystyle\lambda_{M}
is nowhere zero. With this lemma, we can give a proof of Theorem
I
:

Proof.

Observe that

d\Omega_{M}\wedge\omega_{M}=\Omega_{M}\wedge d\omega_{M}=W_{1}\omega_{M}^{3}.

Using the previous lemma and the closeness of
\displaystyle\omega_{0}
, we get that

\Omega_{M}\wedge d\omega_{M}=-i\overline{\lambda_{M}}\Omega_{M}\wedge\overline{\Omega_{M}}=\frac{4}{3}\overline{\lambda_{M}}\omega_{M}^{3}.

Then, we compute
\displaystyle d\omega_{M}
to get
\displaystyle W_{4}
and
\displaystyle W_{3}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\omega_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d(\omega_{0}-\frac{i}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{d\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\wedge(\omega_{M}-\omega_{0})+\frac{2}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\Ima(d\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{d\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\wedge(\omega_{M}-\omega_{0})+\frac{2}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\Ima(\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}\lambda_{M}\Omega_{M}-\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\eta_{M})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{d\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\wedge(\omega_{M}-\omega_{0})+\frac{3}{2}\Ima(\overline{W_{1}}\Omega_{M})-2\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge(\omega_{M}-\omega_{0}).

A simple computation shows that

-\frac{d\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}=\Ree(\eta_{M}).

which simplifies
\displaystyle d\omega_{M}
by

d\omega_{M}=\frac{3}{2}\Ima(\overline{W_{1}}\Omega_{M})-\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\omega_{M}+\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\omega_{0},

giving
\displaystyle W_{4}
and
\displaystyle W_{3}
as expected. Eventually, we compute
\displaystyle W_{2}
and
\displaystyle W_{5}
:

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Omega_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=d(\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\hat{\Omega}_{M})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-\frac{d\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\wedge\Omega_{M}+\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}(d\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\hat{\Omega}_{M}-\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge d\hat{\Omega}_{M})

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\Omega_{M}+\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}(2\overline{\lambda_{M}}\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}\hat{\omega}_{M}^{2}-\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}\overline{\eta_{M}}\wedge\Omega_{M}-\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge d\hat{\Omega}_{M}).

By taking the differential of the equation of the lemma, we obtain that

d\hat{\Omega}_{M}=-\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\hat{\Omega}_{M}-\eta_{M}\wedge\hat{\Omega}_{M}+\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}}\alpha_{M}\wedge d\eta_{M},

and plugging this into
\displaystyle d\Omega_{M}
allows us to write :

\displaystyle\displaystyle d\Omega_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\Omega_{M}+2\overline{\lambda_{M}}\hat{\omega}_{M}^{2}-\overline{\eta_{M}}\wedge\Omega_{M}-\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\Omega_{M}-\eta_{M}\wedge\Omega_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}\lambda_{M}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge d\eta_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=-2\Ree(\eta_{M})\wedge\Omega_{M}+2\overline{\lambda_{M}}(\omega_{M}^{2}+\frac{i}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\omega_{M})

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge d\eta_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=W_{1}\omega_{M}^{2}+\frac{2\overline{\lambda_{M}}}{3}\omega_{M}^{2}+\frac{2i\overline{\lambda_{M}}}{\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge\omega_{M}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\frac{1}{\lambda_{M}\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}}\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}\wedge d\eta_{M}-2\Ree(\eta_{M}).

We observe that
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}\wedge\overline{\alpha_{M}}
and
\displaystyle d\eta_{M}
are both of type
\displaystyle(1,1)
which gives
\displaystyle W_{2}
and consequently
\displaystyle W_{5}
. The structure is LT iff
\displaystyle\Ree(\eta_{M})=0
iff
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
has constant norm on the sphere
\displaystyle S^{7}
. The norm of
\displaystyle\alpha_{M}
is precisely

\|\alpha_{M}\|^{2}=z^{*}M^{*}Mz,

and usual arguments in linear algebra shows that this is constant iff

M^{*}M=\mu I_{4},

for some
\displaystyle\mu
a real number. If
\displaystyle M
is of this form, then

d\eta_{M}=-i|\lambda_{M}|^{2}\omega_{0},

which proves the formula for
\displaystyle W_{2}
.
∎

Remark 5.
It is well-known that
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
admits a nearly Kähler structure
[
1
]
. Our computation shows that
\displaystyle W_{2}
is never zero, an interesting question then would be to know if we could obtain the nearly Kähler structure of
\displaystyle\mathbb{C}\mathbb{P}^{3}
by a similar approach than ours.

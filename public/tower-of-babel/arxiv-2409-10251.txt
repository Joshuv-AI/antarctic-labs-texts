A Common Origin for Nano-Hz Gravitational Wave Background and Black Hole Merger Events
Bo-Qiang Lu, Cheng-Wei Chiang, Tianjun Li

Abstract
We explore the potential primordial connection between the black hole merger events detected by LIGO and the nano-Hz stochastic gravitational wave background observed by pulsar timing arrays. We propose an innovative mechanism for the formation of primordial black holes, suggesting that the Poisson fluctuations within the domain wall network can give rise to horizon-sized overdense regions. Our results indicate a plausible common origin for gravitational wave observations in two different frequency bands, potentially linked to the annihilation of the domain wall network at the QCD scale, while accounting for the accretion effects on primordial black holes. Furthermore, we demonstrate that the bias potential induced by the QCD instanton effect may naturally facilitate the annihilation of the domain wall network during the QCD phase transition. Additionally, our scenario can yield the correct axion dark matter relic abundance, particularly if realized within the clockwork axion framework.



Introduction.—

The pursuit of gravitational waves (GWs) has spanned over a century since Einstein first proposed their existence in 1916, making the recent observations of GWs in two different frequency bands a significant achievement.
In the low-frequency band
\sim 10^{-9}
Hz, the pulsar timing arrays (PTAs) including NANOGrav
Agazie et al. 2023
;
Afzal et al. 2023
, EPTA
Antoniadis et al. 2023
, PPTA
Reardon et al. 2023
, and CPTA
Xu et al. 2023
have recently presented the first convincing evidence for the Hellings-Downs angular correlation, which strongly supports the existence of nano-Hz stochastic gravitational wave background (SGWB).
(For a summary of new physics interpretations of the nano-Hz SGWB, see Ref.
Ellis et al. 2024
.)
In the high-frequency band
\sim 100
Hz, GWs from mergers of black hole (BH) binaries are continuously observed by LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA (LVK) since 2015 and a fraction
f_{\rm PBH}\sim 10^{-3}
of dark matter in the form of primordial black holes (PBHs) can potentially explain the observed BH mergers
Bird et al. 2016
;
Sasaki et al. 2016
.

Hawking and Carr
Hawking 1971
;
Carr and Hawking 1974
first suggested that the self-gravity of the density perturbation originating in the early Universe could counteract the internal pressure gradient and collapse to form a PBH if the overdense region ceased to expand at a horizon-size scale
R_{c}\sim t
(where
t
denotes the cosmic time). The formation of PBHs is commonly associated with scalar inflation as the source of the density perturbations (see Refs.
Sasaki et al. 2018
;
Carr et al. 2021
for a recent review of alternative PBH formation scenarios).
To account for the BH merger events by LVK, the spectral index of the power spectrum is expected to transition from a blue tilt at the QCD scale to a red tilt at the CMB scale in order to significantly generate the PBH abundance while remaining consistent with the CMB observations
Sasaki et al. 2018
. However, this requirement cannot be satisfied within the framework of single-field slow-roll inflation. It may be achievable in more complex inflationary scenarios, such as multi-scalar inflation models (see Refs.
Stewart 1997
;
Drees and Erfani 2011
;
Alabidi et al. 2012
;
Silk and Turner 1987
;
Randall et al. 1996
;
Garcia-Bellido et al. 1996
).

In this Letter, we explore whether the nano-Hz SGWB observed by PTAs and the BH merger events observed by LVK share a common primordial origin.
We present a novel mechanism for PBH formation based on Poisson fluctuations of the domain wall (DW) number density. It is widely recognized that explaining phenomena beyond the standard model, such as dark matter
Bertone and Hooper 2018
, the strong CP problem
Marsh 2016
, and neutrino mass
King et al. 2014
, necessitates the incorporation of new physics. DWs, which are sheet-like topological defects formed after the spontaneous breaking of a discrete symmetry commonly found in new physics models
Kibble 1976
, can form a few-body system and have a horizon-size scale in the scaling regime. Consequently, fluctuations in the number density of the DW system can significantly induce a horizon-size density perturbation, leading to PBH formation via critical collapses. We point out for the first time that both the nano-Hz SGWB and the BH merger events may potentially originate from the annihilation of the DW network, naturally triggered by the QCD instanton-induced bias potential during the QCD phase transition (PT).

PBH from DW fluctuations.—

The evolution of DWs tends to increase their size and merge nearby walls, giving rise to a network of DWs characterized by larger sizes and a reduced wall number at the later stages of evolution. For the spontaneous breaking of a
Z_{N}
symmetry, simulations
Press et al. 1989
;
Hiramatsu et al. 2013
;
Lazanu et al. 2015
have indicated that there eventually exists about
N
horizon-size DWs that separate distinguishable domains.

The statistics of a few-body system can be described by the Poisson distribution. Consider a network consisting of
N
identical walls in a Hubble volume
V_{H}\equiv 4\pi r_{H}^{3}/3
(where
r_{H}=1/H
is the Hubble horizon), then the probability of finding
k
walls in a volume
V
is given by

\mathbb{P}(k,V)=\frac{(\bar{n}V)^{k}}{k!}e^{-\bar{n}V},

(1)

where
\bar{n}=N/V_{H}
is the background wall number density. The mean square energy density fluctuations relative to the total energy density can be determined as a result of Poisson fluctuations of the DWs

\sqrt{\langle\delta^{2}\rangle}=\frac{\sqrt{\langle(\Delta\rho_{w})^{2}\rangle}}{\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\bar{n}V}}\frac{f_{w}}{f_{w}+1},

(2)

where the density contrast
\delta=\Delta\rho_{w}/\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}
,
\Delta\rho_{w}=\rho_{w}-\bar{\rho}_{w}
is the departure of the DW energy density from the background value,
f_{w}=\bar{\rho}_{w}/\bar{\rho}_{\rm rad}
is the ratio of wall energy density to radiation energy density, and
\bar{\rho}_{\rm tot}=\bar{\rho}_{w}+\bar{\rho}_{\rm rad}
is the total background energy density. We further consider a Gaussian density distribution for
\delta

P(\delta)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}\sigma}\exp\left(-\frac{\delta^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),

(3)

where the variance of the probability distribution is determined by Eq. (
2
). For the horizon-size density perturbation from the DW number density fluctuations in a radiation-dominated Universe,
f_{w}\ll 1
, the variance is given by

\displaystyle\sigma=\left.\sqrt{\langle\delta^{2}\rangle}\right|_{V\to V_{H}}=\frac{f_{w}}{\sqrt{N}}.

(4)

Upon reaching a period of rapid expansion, the network enters the scaling regime, in which the wall has a horizon-size correlation length
L\simeq t
and the wall energy density is related to the correlation length by the one-scale assumption
Martins et al. 2016
,
\bar{\rho}_{w}=\mathcal{A}\sigma_{w}/t
, where
\sigma_{w}
is the surface tension of the wall and the area parameter
\mathcal{A}
depends on the DW number from simulations
Kawasaki et al. 2015
. The annihilation of DWs occurs at a temperature
T_{\rm ann}
, signifying the point at which the bias potential energy
\Delta V_{\rm bias}
becomes comparable to the surface energy of the DW. For temperatures below
T_{\rm ann}
, the energy density of the wall can be parametrized as
\bar{\rho}_{w}(T/T_{\rm ann})^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha\simeq 7
based on simulations
Kawasaki et al. 2015
;
Ferrer et al. 2019
.

The density perturbation resulting from the fluctuations in DW number density is also horizon-sized. Using Eqs. (
3
) and (
4
), we have
\bar{\delta}=\int_{0}^{\infty}\delta P(\delta)d\delta\propto G\sigma_{w}t
. Therefore, the likelihood of producing a larger density contrast increases with time. If
\delta
surpasses a critical value
\delta_{c}
, which is contingent on the equation of state (EOS) of the Universe, it could undergo a critical collapse and form a PBH. Numerical calculations
Choptuik 1993
;
Evans and Coleman 1994
;
Koike et al. 1995
indicate a simple scaling law for the PBH mass near the critical threshold
M=CM_{H}(\delta-\delta_{c})^{\gamma}
, where we take
C=3.3
and
\gamma=0.36
and note that these parameters may be affected by interior pressure gradients of matter
Musco et al. 2024
. The total mass contained in a Hubble horizon volume is
M_{H}=1/(2GH)
. Using the Press-Schechter formalism, the PBH energy fraction
\beta\equiv\rho_{\rm PBH}(t_{f})/\rho_{\rm tot}(t_{f})
at the formation time
t_{f}
can be estimated as
Byrnes et al. 2018

\beta(M)=2\int_{\delta_{c}}^{\infty}C(\delta-\delta_{c})^{\gamma}P(\delta)d\delta.

(5)

The PBH mass function is then given by
Byrnes et al. 2018
;
Niemeyer and Jedamzik 1998

\psi(M)=\frac{2}{\Omega_{\mathrm{CDM}}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}P(\delta)\frac{MB^{1/\gamma}}{\gamma M_{H}}\sqrt{\frac{M_{\mathrm{eq}}}{M_{H}}}d\ln M_{H},

(6)

where
B=M/(CM_{H})
,
\Omega_{\rm CDM}=0.25
is the dark matter relic abundance, and
M_{\rm eq}\simeq 2.8\times 10^{17}M_{\odot}
is the horizon mass at the time of matter-radiation equality.
The PBH relic abundance is related to
\psi(M)
by
\Omega_{\mathrm{PBH}}=\Omega_{\mathrm{CDM}}\int\psi(M)d\ln M
and the average PBH mass is defined as

\langle M\rangle=\Omega_{\mathrm{CDM}}\int M\psi(M)d\ln M/\Omega_{\rm PBH}
.

PBH accretion.—

After their formation, PBHs can accrete surrounding mass and grow over time, while also losing mass through Hawking radiation. According to the generalized second law of thermodynamics, the accretion of PBH would dominate the radiation when the thermal bath temperature exceeds that of the BH, i.e.,
T\gtrsim T_{\rm BH}
, where
T_{\rm BH}=1/(8\pi GM)
represents the BH temperature. Zel’dovich and Novikov suggested that horizon-size PBHs could grow at a rate similar to the cosmological horizon before the end of the radiation era based on the Bondi-type accretion
Zel’dovich and Novikov 1967
. However, subsequent research by Ref.
Carr and Hawking 1974
did not find a self-similar solution connecting a BH to an exact flat Friedmann background via a sound wave in a radiation-dominated Universe. The self-similar growth of PBHs may lead to a population with a mass around
10^{17}M_{\odot}
, but this has not been confirmed by observations
Carr et al. 2010
. Therefore, it is widely believed that horizon-size PBHs do not grow in the radiation era
Carr et al. 2021
.

The spherical accretion of an isolated PBH surrounded by a dark matter halo and/or a hydrogen gas can be described by the Bondi-Hoyle rate

\dot{M}=\lambda 4\pi r_{\mathrm{B}}^{2}\rho_{\mathrm{gas}}v_{\mathrm{eff}},

(7)

where
\rho_{\rm gas}
is the density of the hydrogen gas,
v_{\rm eff}=\sqrt{v_{\rm rel}^{2}+c_{s}^{2}}
is the effective velocity,
c_{s}
is the sound speed, and
r_{\rm B}\equiv M/v_{\rm eff}^{2}
is the Bondi-Hoyle radius. The accretion parameter
\lambda
takes into account the effects of gas viscosity, the growth of a dark halo, the Hubble expansion, and the Compton scattering between the CMB radiation and the hydrogen gas. We refer to Refs.
Ricotti et al. 2008
;
Ricotti 2007
;
De Luca et al. 2020a
;
De Luca et al. 2020b
for more details. The accretion rate can be estimated by using
Ricotti et al. 2008

\dot{m}=0.023\lambda\left(\frac{1+z}{1000}\right)\left(\frac{M}{M_{\odot}}\right)\left(\frac{v_{\text{eff }}}{5.74\mathrm{~km}\mathrm{~s}^{-1}}\right)^{-3}.

(8)

For redshift
z\lesssim z_{\rm dec}\simeq 130
, where the baryonic matter decouples from the CMB radiation,
\dot{m}
remarkably increases due to the decrease of the relative velocity
v_{\rm rel}
between PBHs and gas. Depending on the PBH mass,
\dot{m}
peaks at redshift
z\sim 30
, with an accretion time scale
t_{\rm Salp}\sim 10^{8}
yr, much larger than the age of the Universe
t_{\rm age}\sim 10^{7}
yr. Therefore, the accretion of the PBH inside a virialized halo is significant during the redshift
10\lesssim z\lesssim 30
. For
z\lesssim 10
, the structure formation begins and the relative velocity increases to
v_{\rm rel}\gtrsim 10
km/s, leading to a decrease of PBH accretion
Ricotti et al. 2008
. Other factors, such as reionization and global feedback, can also decrease the PBH accretion rate
Ricotti et al. 2008
;
De Luca et al. 2020a
. To address modeling uncertainties, we consider cut-off redshift values of
z_{\rm cut}=10
and 7, as adopted in Refs.
De Luca et al. 2020a
and
Ricotti et al. 2008
, respectively, below which the accretion is negligible.

1
, the mass function of PBHs produced by the DW number density fluctuations is shown as the black curve at the benchmark point of
(T_{\rm ann},f_{w}(T_{\rm ann}))=(0.1~{\rm GeV},0.1)
, considering the temperature-dependent EOS
Byrnes et al. 2018
. We notice that the distribution peaks around
M\simeq 10~M_{\odot}
and then sharply drops to zero due to DW annihilations. The PBH accretion effect with redshift cut-offs
z_{\rm cut}=10
and 7 is represented by the red and blue curves, respectively. A smaller
z_{\rm cut}
could lead to a larger accretion effect, leaving significant impacts on the interpretation of the LVK events with mass components
\gtrsim 10M_{\odot}
.

Model implementation.—

Let us now verify our scenario in a concrete particle model. Consider a scalar potential
Chiang and Lu 2020

V(\Phi)=\lambda\left(\Phi^{\dagger}\Phi-\frac{v_{\phi}^{2}}{2}\right)^{2}-\frac{\mu_{3}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\Phi^{3}+\Phi^{\dagger 3}\right),

(11)

where the scalar
\Phi=(\phi+v_{\phi})e^{ia/v_{\phi}}/\sqrt{2}
and
\mu_{3}
is assumed to be real. The first term on the right-hand side of Eq. (
11
) respects a global
U(1)
symmetry, which is softly broken to a
Z_{3}
symmetry by the second term. The spontaneous breaking of
Z_{3}
symmetry gives rise to the potential of the heavy axion
a
:

V_{a}(a)=-\frac{1}{4}\mu_{3}v_{\phi}^{3}\cos\left(\frac{3a}{v_{\phi}}\right),

(12)

with 3-fold degenerate vacua
a_{j}/v_{\phi}=2\pi j/3
, where
j=0,~1,~2
. DWs with a surface tension of
\sigma_{w}\simeq 8m_{a}f_{a}^{2}
(where
m_{a}^{2}=9\mu_{3}v_{\phi}/4
and
f_{a}=v_{\phi}/3
) are formed via the Kibble mechanism
Kibble 1976
to separate the degenerate vacua.

The quantum anomaly of
Z_{3}
under the color
SU(3)
gauge group generates the topological interaction

\mathcal{L}\supset\frac{g_{s}^{2}a}{32\pi^{2}v_{\phi}}G^{\mu\nu}\widetilde{G}_{\mu\nu}
,
where
G_{\mu\nu}
is the gluon field tensor and
g_{s}
is the strong gauge coupling.
After QCD PT, the QCD instanton effects generate an effective potential for
a

V_{c}(a)=-\Lambda_{c}^{4}\cos\left(\frac{N_{c}a}{3f_{a}}+\vartheta\right),

(13)

where
\Lambda_{c}=(m_{u}\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{3})^{1/4}\simeq 66.2
MeV and
\vartheta
is a strong CP-violating phase. The parameter
N_{c}
depends on the number of quarks charged under the
Z_{3}
symmetry, and we take
N_{c}=1
to avoid color anomaly degenerate vacua. The potential (
13
) lifts the degeneracy of the 3-fold vacua by a bias energy
\Delta V_{\rm bias}^{1/4}=(1.5\Lambda_{c}^{4})^{1/4}\simeq 73.3
MeV.

The condition
\rho_{w}\simeq\Delta V_{\rm bias}
for DW annihilation gives

\left(\frac{\sigma_{w}^{1/3}}{10^{5}~{\rm GeV}}\right)^{3}\simeq 39.6f_{w}(T_{\rm ann})\left(\frac{1}{\mathcal{A}}\right)\left(\frac{T_{\rm ann}}{0.1~{\rm GeV}}\right)^{2}

(14)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta V_{\rm bias}^{1/4}}{0.1~{\rm GeV}}\simeq 1.37(f_{w}(T_{\rm ann}))^{1/4}\left(\frac{T_{\rm ann}}{0.1~{\rm GeV}}\right).

(15)

We then obtain
\sigma_{w}^{1/3}\simeq 1.5\times 10^{5}
GeV and
\Delta V_{\rm bias}^{1/4}\simeq 77.0
MeV for the benchmark model. Therefore, we have confirmed that the QCD instanton-induced potential (
13
) can indeed provide a correct bias energy for the decay of the degenerate
vacua (
12
) in the benchmark model.

The scale
v_{\phi}\sim 10^{5}
GeV is significantly below the Planck scale, effectively ensuring the quality of
Z_{3}
. To verify the conservation of strong CP of the heavy axion vacuum, we examine the minimum of the potential

\langle\theta\rangle=\frac{m_{*}^{2}\tan\vartheta}{N_{c}(m_{a}^{2}+m_{*}^{2})},

(16)

where
m_{*}^{2}=\Lambda_{c}^{4}N_{c}^{2}\cos\vartheta/v_{\phi}^{2}
and
\theta=a/v_{\phi}
. Assuming
\cos\vartheta\sim 0.1
,
\tan\vartheta\sim 1
and
m_{a}\sim v_{\phi}\sim 10^{5}
GeV, we have
\langle\theta\rangle\simeq 10^{-26}
, which is far below the experimental constraint
\theta_{\rm QCD}\lesssim 10^{-10}

Baker et al. 2006
.

Considering Eq. (
4
) and the given value
T_{\rm ann}\simeq 100
MeV, it is possible to replicate the values of
\left(\langle M\rangle,~f_{\rm PBH}\right)
in Tab.
1
for a model with arbitrary
N
DWs by scaling
f_{w}(T_{\rm ann})
with a factor of
\sqrt{N/3}
. In the clockwork axion model
Choi and Im 2016
;
Kaplan and Rattazzi 2016
, the value
f_{w}(T_{\rm ann})=0.2
and the axion decay constant
f_{a}\simeq v_{\phi}3^{N}\sim 10^{11}
GeV for
v_{\phi}\sim 10^{5}
GeV and
N=12
are obtained. While realizing our scenario, the axion with
f_{a}\sim v_{\phi}/\Lambda_{\rm QCD}^{2}\sim 10^{11}
GeV provides a correct dark matter relic abundance through the misalignment mechanism
Preskill et al. 1983
;
Abbott and Sikivie 1983
;
Lu et al. 2024a
.

Conclusions.—

We proposed that the number density fluctuations in a DW network can serve as a novel source for density perturbations, leading to the critical collapse and formation of PBHs, which is particularly significant near the annihilation of DWs by the bias potential induced by the QCD instanton effect during the QCD PT. PBHs with a mass distribution in the range of
(1-10)M_{\odot}
can bridge the gap between
\sim(10-100)M_{\odot}
for LVK by the accretion effects. By considering a benchmark model, we demonstrated that the mass distribution of PBHs resulting from DW Poisson fluctuations can explain the BH merger events, while the annihilation of DWs can generate the nano-Hz SGWB in PTAs. Additionally, we confirmed that the bias potential in the benchmark model can be accurately induced by the QCD instanton effect.

Future BH merger events by LVK beyond the stellar evolution are essential for testing our hypothesis, particularly through the analysis of the mass distribution and the spin of high-mass PBHs resulting from accretion
De Luca et al. 2021
. The high-luminosity LHC has the potential to probe a heavy axion with
f_{a}\sim(10^{3}-10^{5})
GeV and mass in the range of
\sim(1-10^{3})
GeV
Baldenegro et al. 2018
;
Bauer et al. 2019
;
Ferreira et al. 2023
. Additionally, the spontaneous breaking of
Z_{N}
symmetry at the scale
v_{\phi}\sim 10^{5}
GeV may lead to a first-order PT
Chiang and Lu 2020
, and the resulting GWs can be detectable for the LVK O3 run
Chiang and Lu 2021
.

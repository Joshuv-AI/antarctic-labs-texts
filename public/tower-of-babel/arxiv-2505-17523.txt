Cones of Weights and Minimal Cones of the Goren-Oort Strata in Hilbert modular varieties
Fred Diamond, Payman L Kassaei

Abstract
. Let p p be a prime, F F a totally real field in which p p is unramified, and X / 𝔽 ¯ p X/{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}} a Shimura variety associated to Res F / ℚ ​ GL 2 {\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}{\rm GL}_{2} (or a PEL Hilbert modular variety). A mod p p Hilbert modular form of weight κ \kappa can be defined as a section of an automorphic line bundle ℒ κ {\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa} on X X . We consider sections of ℒ κ {\mathcal{L}}_{\kappa} (forms) over a Goren-Oort stratum X T X_{T} inside X X , and define the cone of weights of X T X_{T} to be the ℚ ≥ 0 \mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0} -cone generated by the weights of all nonzero forms on X T X_{T} . We explicitly determine the cone of weights of all strata, showing in particular that they are not in general generated by the weights of the associated Hasse invariants. Using this, we define a notion of minimal cone for each stratum, and explicitly determine the minimal cones of all strata. When X X is a Shimura variety associated to Res F / ℚ ​ GL 2 {\rm Res}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}{\rm GL}_{2} , we prove that for every nonzero eigenform f f for the prime-to- p p Hecke algebra on a stratum X T X_{T} , there is another eigenform with the same Hecke eigenvalues which has weight in the minimal cone of X T X_{T} .

Introduction

Let
F

\mathbb{Q}
,
{\mathcal{O}}_{F}
its ring of integers, and
p

p

\kappa=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{B}}

\mathbb{B}={\operatorname{Hom}}({\mathcal{O}}_{F},{\overline{\mathbb{F}}_{p}})

\sigma

\mathbb{B}
by composition on the right. In
[
2

{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}=\{\kappa=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}:pk_{\beta}\geq k_{\sigma^{-1}\beta},\forall\beta\in\mathbb{B}\},

and proved that for every mod
p
Hilbert modular eigenform
f

q
-expansion which has weight in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}
. In fact, we proved a stronger result: every mod
p
Hilbert modular form that is not divisible by any partial Hasse invariants has weight in
{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}
. An immediate consequence of this result is that the cone of weights of mod-
p
Hilbert modular forms, i.e., the
\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}
-cone generated by the weights of nonzero Hilbert modular forms, is generated by the weights of the partial Hasse invariants, namely, it is the Hasse cone:

{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm Hasse}=\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}:\kappa=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}x_{\beta}h_{\beta}\ {\rm such\ that}\ x_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0},\forall\beta\in\mathbb{B}\},

where,

is the weight of the partial Hasse invariant
{\bf h}_{\beta}
. In
[
3
]
, we extended these results to the general case where
p
may be ramified in
F

As part of a program to study the cone of weights of automorphic forms on Shimura varieties, Goldring and Koskivirta
[
6
]
also showed, using different methods, that when
p
is unramified in

, the cone of weights of Hilbert modular forms equals the Hasse cone. One question that arises in the work of Goldring and Koskivirta is to determine Shimura varieties for which the cone of weights is generated by the weights of the relevant Hasse invariants. While, as mentioned above, this is the case over a Hilbert modular variety, it fails to hold over some of its Goren-Oort strata. The central idea in this work is that the generators of the cone of weights should be sought among pullbacks of Hasse invariants of certain Shimura varieties to which the space of interest maps. We will make this precise.

In the second part of the article, we study the question of minimal weights of forms over strata. One motivation for this work has been to clarify what a minimal cone of weights should mean for more general Shimura varieties, and how such minimal cones can be calculated. In
[
2
]
, the definition of the minimal cone was motivated by considerations arising from the relationship between Hilbert modular forms and Galois representations as in

{\mathcal{C}}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\bullet}\omega^{\bullet})=\langle f_{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T;\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T;\pm e^{\prime}_{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{B}\rangle

We further define and explicitly describe the minimal cone of weights for

. Finally, we prove that for any form

of weight

which is an eigenform for the prime-to-

While, in

As stated earlier, every stratum
X_{T}
is Hecke-equivariantly isomorphic to a flag space over some quaternionic Shimura variety. We would like to mention that, while we don’t pursue this in the article, the exact methods of this work can be used to determine cones of weights and minimal cones of flag spaces over quaternionic Shimura varieties more generally, (and, in particular, those of the full flag spaces). For example, given

is generated by all

, and the explicit description of the minimal cone is similar to those obtained in this paper.

Finally, we note that Gabriel Micolet
[

has recently generalized the main result of
[

]
to the case where

[

\mathbb{Z}^{I}
generated by the following subset of
\mathbb{Z}^{I}
:

\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}:H^{0}(Z,{\mathcal{L}}^{\kappa})\neq 0\}.

Lemma 7.1
.

Let
Y
be a noetherian scheme over
\mathbb{F}
, and
\{{\mathcal{L}}_{i}\}_{i\in I}
a finite collection of line bundles on
Y
. For
j\in J
(a finite set), let
{\bf h}_{j}\in H^{0}(Y,{\mathcal{L}}^{h_{j}})
, where
h_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}^{I}
. Let
Z_{j}
be the vanishing locus of
{\bf h}_{j}
, and assume that each
Z_{j}
is purely of codimension one. Assume further that the intersection of
Z_{j}
with every irreducible component of
Y
is nonempty. Then,

C_{Y}({\mathcal{L}}^{\bullet})\subset\big(\bigcap_{j\in J}C_{Z_{j}}({\mathcal{L}}^{\bullet})\big)+\langle h_{j}:j\in J\rangle,

where
\langle h_{j}:j\in J\rangle
denotes the
\mathbb{Q}^{\geq 0}
-cone generated by
\{h_{j}:j\in J\}
.

Proof.

Assume
f\neq 0
is a section of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\kappa}
on
Y
. By Lemma 7.2 in
[
3
]
, we can write
f=(\Pi_{{}_{j\in J}}{\bf h}_{j}^{M_{j}})f_{0}
for some nonnegative integers
\{M_{j}:j\in J\}
, such that restriction of
f_{0}
to each
Z_{j}
is nonzero. Therefore,
f_{0}
gives a nonzero section of
{\mathcal{L}}^{\otimes(\kappa-\sum_{j\in J}M_{j}h_{j})}
on each
Z_{j}
. It follows that
\kappa-\sum_{j\in J}M_{j}h_{j}\in\bigcap_{j\in J}C_{Z_{j}}({\mathcal{L}}^{\bullet})
.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Expressions involving cones of weights such as the one appearing in the above lemma arise, and are referred to as intersection-sum cones, in the work of Goldring-Koskivirta on weight cone conjectures. They also implicitly appear in the work of Diamond-Kassaei on minimal cones.

Proposition 7.3
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
. Let
T\subseteq T^{\prime}\subseteq\tilde{T}
be such that
{\rm Iw}(T^{\prime})={\rm Iw}(T)
(so, in particular, the morphism
\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}:X_{T}\rightarrow X_{T^{\prime}}
is defined). Let
Z
be a reduced closed subscheme of
X_{T^{\prime}}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{-1}Z,\omega^{\bullet})\subset\{\kappa=\sum_{{}_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta}:L_{T,\beta_{0}}(\kappa)\geq 0,\forall\beta_{0}\in T^{\prime}-T\}.

Proof.

Note that if
T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, for some
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
, there is nothing to prove at
\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Furthermore, by Corollary
2.3
, it is true that for each
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the line bundle
\omega_{\beta}
is torsion on
X_{T}
.
Fix
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
such that
T_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. For any
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, define
\nu_{\beta}
to be the smallest
i\geq 0
such that
\sigma^{-i}\beta\not\in T_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then, from Definition
2.2
, we have
\omega_{\beta}\cong\omega_{\sigma^{-\nu_{\beta}}\beta}^{(-p)^{\nu_{\beta}}}
. For any
\beta\in T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, the embedding
\sigma^{-v_{\beta}}\beta
is in
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, so it is either in
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, or in
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Putting all this together, and noting that the subset of
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
for which
\sigma^{-\nu_{\beta}}\beta=\beta_{0}\in\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
is exactly
\{\beta_{0},\sigma\beta_{0},...,\sigma^{\mu_{\beta_{0}}-1}\beta_{0}\}
, we find that there are integers
\ell_{\beta}(\kappa)
, for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}
, such that for some
N>0
, we have an isomorphism of line bundles on
X_{T}
:

\omega^{N\kappa}\cong\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}}}\omega_{\beta}^{N\ell_{\beta}(\kappa)}\otimes\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in\tilde{T}-T}}\omega_{\beta}^{NL_{T,\beta}(\kappa)}.

By assumptions, we have an isomorphism
\iota_{T,T^{\prime}}:X_{T}\rightarrow\Pi_{{}_{\beta\in I}}\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{H}}^{T^{\prime}}_{\beta})
(see §
3
). Under
\iota_{T,T^{\prime}}
, this line bundle corresponds to
{\mathcal{L}}_{N}=\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}}}s_{T^{\prime}}^{*}\omega_{\beta}^{N\ell_{\beta}(\kappa)}\otimes\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in\tilde{T}-T^{\prime}}}s_{T^{\prime}}^{*}\omega_{\beta}^{NL_{T,\beta}(\kappa)}\otimes\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in T^{\prime}-T}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\beta}(NL_{T,\beta}(\kappa))
.

Now assume that
\kappa
is a weight such that
L_{T,\beta_{0}}(\kappa)<0,
for some
\beta_{0}\in T^{\prime}-T
, and let
f\in H^{0}(\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{-1}Z,\omega^{\kappa})
.
Then, under the isomorphism
i_{T,T^{\prime}}|_{\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{-1}Z}:\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{-1}Z\rightarrow\Pi_{{}_{\beta\in T^{\prime}-T}}\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{H}}^{T^{\prime}}_{\beta}|_{Z})
, the section
f^{N}
of

\omega^{N\kappa}
on
\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{-1}Z

gives rise to a section of
{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
on
\Pi_{{}_{\beta\in T^{\prime}-T}}\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{H}}^{T^{\prime}}_{\beta}|_{Z})
.
The restriction of
{\mathcal{L}}_{N}
to every geometric fibre of

s_{T^{\prime}}:\Pi_{{}_{\beta\in T^{\prime}-T}}\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{H}}^{T^{\prime}}_{\beta}|_{Z})\rightarrow Z

\otimes_{{}_{\beta\in T^{\prime}-T}}{\mathcal{O}}_{\beta}(NL_{T,\beta}(\kappa))
.
Since
L_{T,\beta_{0}}(\kappa)<0
, the restriction of
f^{N}
to any such fibre is zero. Since

is reduced, so is

.
∎

Lemma 7.4
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
be such for some
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
, we have
|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|
is even, and
T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\{\beta\}
, for some
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Then
{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}:L_{T,\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0\}
.

Proof.

Note that the assumptions imply
\beta\in\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
. This case does not follow from Proposition
7.3
, since
{\rm Iw}(T)\neq{\rm Iw}(T\cup\{\beta\})
. We prove this by induction on
|\mathbb{B}-T|
. When
|\mathbb{B}-T|=1
, we have
T=\mathbb{B}-\{\beta\}
, and
X_{T}
is a union of complete curves. First note that, since
\omega_{\sigma^{-1}\beta}\cong\omega_{\beta}^{(-p)^{|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|-1}}
, the Hasse invariant
{\bf h}_{\beta}(T)
gives a section of
\omega_{\beta}^{-1-p^{|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|}}
whose vanishing locus meets every connected component of
X_{T}
, implying that
\omega_{\beta}
is an anti-ample bundle on each such component. Now let
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X_{T},\omega^{\kappa})
. Since
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}\subset T
for all
{\mathfrak{q}}\neq{\mathfrak{p}}
, by Corollary
2.3
, we know that
\omega_{\tau}
is torsion for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Hence, there is
N>0
such that
{\bf f}^{N}
gives a section of
\bigotimes_{\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\omega_{\tau}^{Nk_{\tau}}
on
X_{T}
. Since all
\beta\neq\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
belong to
T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, it follows that
\bigotimes_{\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\omega_{\tau}^{Nk_{\tau}}\cong\omega_{\beta}^{-NL_{T,\beta}(\kappa)}
. Since
\omega_{\beta}
is anti-ample on each component, it follows that
L_{T,\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0
.

Now assume
|\mathbb{B}-T|>1
, and choose
\beta^{\prime}\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{q}}-T_{\mathfrak{q}}
, for some
{\mathfrak{q}}\neq{\mathfrak{p}}
. Lemma
7.1
implies

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subset{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf h}_{\beta^{\prime}}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})+\langle h_{\beta^{\prime}}\rangle={\mathcal{C}}_{T\cup\{\beta^{\prime}\}}+\langle h_{\beta^{\prime}}\rangle\subset\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}:L_{T,\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0\},

by the induction hypothesis, and since
L_{T,\beta}(h_{\beta^{\prime}})=0
.
∎

Corollary 7.5
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T},\omega^{\bullet})\subset\{\kappa=\sum_{{}_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta}:L_{T,\beta_{0}}(\kappa)\geq 0,\forall\beta_{0}\in\tilde{T}-T\}.

Proof.

Let
\kappa\in{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T},\omega^{\bullet})
, and
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
. If either
|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|
is odd, or
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
it follows from Proposition
7.3
that
L_{\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0
, for all
\beta\in\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
. Otherwise, assume
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\{\beta_{1},...,\beta_{t}\}
. If
t\geq 2
, applying Proposition
7.3
with
T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\beta_{i}\}
, for
1\leq i\leq t
, implies that
L_{\beta_{i}}(\kappa)\geq 0
. When
t=1
, Lemma
7.4
implies that
L_{\beta_{1}}(\kappa)\geq 0
.
∎

We now prove the main result.

Theorem 7.6
.

For any
T\subset\mathbb{B}
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}={\mathcal{D}}_{T}=\langle h_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}^{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}\rangle+\langle-b_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}^{\beta}:\beta\in\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}\rangle+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle.

Proof.

By Corollary
4.4
and Proposition
5.1
, it is enough to show
{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}_{T}
. We
prove this by induction on
|\tilde{T}-T|
. The initial case where
\tilde{T}=T
is already known and can be proved in different ways. In this case, by Proposition
5.1
, and in the notation of §
5
, we have

{\mathcal{D}}_{T}=\langle h_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}^{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T\rangle+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle.

One can now either use Lemma
5.2
, and appeal to
[
6
]
(where the admissibility condition on a stratum
X_{T}
corresponds to the condition
\tilde{T}=T
), or note that the proof of Corollary 5.4 in
[
2
]
applies verbatim to show that, in the notation of §
3
, the cone of weights
{\mathcal{C}}(X_{G,K_{T}},\eta^{\bullet})
is generated by the weights of the Hasse invariants of
X_{G,K_{T}}
. It then follows immediately, using
\iota_{T}:X_{T}\cong X_{G,K_{T}}
, that

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{T\!-\rm red}=\iota_{T}^{*}{\mathcal{C}}(X_{G,K_{T}},\eta^{\bullet}).

Every Hasse invariant of
X_{G,K_{T}}
is a section of a line bundle of the form
(\eta_{\beta})^{p^{n_{\beta}}}(\eta_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta})^{-1}
, where
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
. It pulls back under
\iota_{T}
to a nonzero from of weight
h_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}^{\beta}
. We deduce that

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}=j_{T}({\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{T\!-\rm red})+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle=\langle h_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}^{\beta}:\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T\rangle+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle={\mathcal{D}}_{T}.

We now assume
\tilde{T}\neq T
. Choose
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
such that
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\{\beta_{1},...,\beta_{r}\}
for
r>0
. We consider two cases:

Case 1:

\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset
. Without loss of generality, we can assume there is
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
such that
\sigma^{\mu_{\beta_{1}}}\beta_{1}=\beta
. Let
T^{\prime\prime}=T\cup\{\beta\}
,
T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\beta_{1}\}
. Apply Lemma
7.1
with
\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{*}{\bf h}_{\beta}^{\beta}(T^{\prime})={\bf h}_{\beta}^{\beta}(T)
and
{\bf h}_{\beta}(T)
(using Lemma
4.2
):

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq\big({\mathcal{C}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}\cap{\mathcal{C}}(Z(\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{*}{\bf h}_{\beta}^{\beta}(T^{\prime})),\omega^{\bullet})\big)+\langle h_{\beta},h_{\beta}^{\beta}\rangle.

Applying Proposition
7.3
with
Z=Z({\bf h}_{\beta}^{\beta}(T^{\prime}))
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq\big({\mathcal{C}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}\cap\{\kappa:L_{T,\beta_{1}}(\kappa)\geq 0\}\big)+\langle h_{\beta},h_{\beta}^{\beta}\rangle.

By induction hypothesis,
{\mathcal{C}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}={\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}
. Since
h_{\beta},h_{\beta}^{\beta}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T}
, to show
{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq{\mathcal{D}}_{T}
, it is enough to show

{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}\cap\{\kappa:L_{T,\beta_{1}}(\kappa)\geq 0\}\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{T}.

Note that we have
\tilde{T^{\prime\prime}}-T^{\prime\prime}=(\tilde{T}-T)-\{\beta_{1}\}
, and
\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T^{\prime\prime}}=((\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T})-\{\beta\})\cup\{\beta_{1}\}
. We also have
\epsilon_{T^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)=-\epsilon_{T}(\tau)
, fot
\tau\in\{\beta_{1},\sigma\beta_{1},...,\sigma^{\mu_{\beta_{1}}-1}\beta_{1}\}
, and
\epsilon_{T^{\prime\prime}}(\tau)=\epsilon_{T}(\tau)
, otherwise. Let
\iota:\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}\rightarrow\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T^{\prime\prime}}
be the bijection which is identity except at
\beta
, in which case
\iota(\beta)=\beta_{1}
.

Take
\kappa_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime\prime}}\cap\{\kappa:L_{T,\beta_{1}}(\kappa)\geq 0\}
. It follows that
L_{T,\tau}(\kappa_{0})=L_{T^{\prime\prime},\tau}(\kappa_{0})\geq 0
, for all
\tau\in\tilde{T^{\prime\prime}}-T^{\prime\prime}=(\tilde{T}-T)-\{\beta_{1}\}
,
L_{T,\beta_{1}}(\kappa_{0})\geq 0
, and that, for
\tau\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}
, either
L_{T,\tau}(\kappa_{0})=L_{T^{\prime\prime},\iota(\tau)}(\kappa_{0})
(when
\tau\not\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
), or there is
N_{\tau}\geq 0
such that
L_{T,\tau}(\kappa_{0})=L_{T^{\prime\prime},\iota(\tau)}(\kappa_{0})+2p^{N_{\tau}}L_{T,\beta_{1}}(\kappa_{0})\geq 0
. Proposition
6.1
implies that
\kappa_{0}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T}
.

Case 2:

\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}=\emptyset
. Recall that
\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}=\{\beta_{1},\cdots,\beta_{r}\}
. We have

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subset\{\kappa\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}:L_{T,\beta_{i}}(\kappa)\geq 0,1\leq i\leq r\},

by Corollary
7.5
. Let
T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\beta_{1}\}
.
Therefore, Lemma
7.1
applied with
{\bf h}_{\beta_{1}}(T)
implies that

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq\{\kappa\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T^{\prime}}+\langle h_{\beta_{1}}\rangle:L_{T,\beta_{i}}(\kappa)\geq 0,1\leq i\leq r\}.

By induction, it is enough to show that
\{\kappa\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime}}+\langle h_{\beta_{1}}\rangle:L_{T,\beta_{i}}(\kappa)\geq 0,1\leq i\leq r\}\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{T}.
Since
T,T^{\prime}
only differ at
{\mathfrak{p}}
, this is equivalent to
\{\kappa\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}}}+\langle h_{\beta_{1}}\rangle:L_{T,\beta_{i}}(\kappa)\geq 0,1\leq i\leq r\}\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{T_{\mathfrak{p}}},
which is clear.

∎

We now summarize these results and introduce some new notation. Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
.
First assume
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}\neq\emptyset
. For all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we set
f_{\beta}(T)=h^{\beta}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}
, and
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)={\bf h}^{\beta}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}(T)
. For
\beta\in\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we set
f_{\beta}(T)=-b^{\beta}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}{\beta}}
, and
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)={\bf h}^{\sigma\beta}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T){\bf b}_{\sigma\beta}(T)^{-p^{n_{\beta}-1}}
. If
\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T_{\mathfrak{p}}}=\emptyset
, for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, we set
f_{\beta}(T)=-(1+p^{|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|})e_{\beta}
, and
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)={\bf h}_{\beta}^{\sigma\beta}(T)({\bf b}_{\sigma\beta}(T))^{-p^{|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|-1}}
. By Theorem
7.6
, for any
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}_{T}=\langle f_{\beta}(T):\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T\rangle+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle,

and
0\neq{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)\in H^{0}(X_{T},\omega^{f_{\beta}(T)})
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
.

Lemma
4.2
implies that for each
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
, the zero locus of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
intersects every connected component of
X_{T}
in a smooth divisor. It follows that if
C
is a connected component of
X_{T}
, then

{\mathcal{C}}(C,\omega^{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}(X_{T},\omega^{\bullet})={\mathcal{C}}_{T}.

Remark 7.7
.

We defined the cone of weights on
X_{T}
in a way that amounts to the sum of the cones of weights of its connected components. An alternative definition would have been to consider the intersection of the cones of weights of the connected components of
X_{T}
, in which case, Lemma
7.1
would not generally hold true. However, the two notions coincide for
X_{T}
by the discussion above.

Proposition 8.1

Definition 8.2

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
, and

. We define

Proposition 8.3
.

If
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
and

, then

is quasi-affine.

Proof.

Using all the isomorphisms given in Proposition
8.1
and Definition

, it is easy to see that for all

,
\omega_{\beta}
is a torsion line bundle over

. Since
\bigotimes_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}\omega_{\beta}
is ample on

, it follows that its restriction to
W_{T,J}
is both ample and torsion, which implies that
W_{T,J}
is quasi-affine (since it is quasi-compact).
∎

Corollary 8.4
.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{B}
,
J\subset\mathbb{B}-T
, and assume
X_{T,J}
is nonempty. Then, for any
\beta\not\in T\cup J
,
X_{T,\{\beta\}}
intersects every connected component of
X_{T,J}
.

Proof.

The case
T=\emptyset
has been proved in Corollary 5.7 of
[
3
]
. We assume
T\neq\emptyset
, and, therefore,
X_{T}
is proper. Let
J\subset\mathbb{B}-T
be a subset of maximal cardinality for which the claim fails to be true (in particular
J\neq\mathbb{B}-T
). Let
C
be a connected component of
X_{T,J}
such that
C\cap X_{T,\{\beta\}}=\emptyset
for some
\beta\not\in T\cup J
. The maximality assumption implies that
C\cap X_{T,\{\tau\}}=\emptyset
, for all
\tau\not\in T\cup J
. It follows that
C\subset W_{T,J}
. Since
W_{T,J}
is quasi-compact, Proposition
8.3
implies that
C
is quasi-affine. Since
C
is also proper, it follows that
C
is finite. This is a contradiction, since
X_{T,J}
is cut out from
X_{T}
by the vanishing of
|J|
sections, and, hence, every connected component of
X_{T}
has codimension at most
|J|<|\mathbb{B}-T|={\rm dim}\ \!X_{T}
.

∎

Proposition 8.5

Let

,

(2)

Each

∎

Lemma 8.7
.

Let

, and

. Let

, and

. Then

intersects every connected component of
X_{T,J}
.

Proof.

Let
C
be a connected component of

. By Corollary
8.4
and Proposition
8.5
, we know that

is nonempty, projective, and of pure dimension

. Assume
X_{T^{\prime},J}\cap C=\emptyset
. Then
Z\subseteq X_{T,J}-X_{T,J^{\prime}}
. In particular,
Z\cap X_{T,J^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
, which implies that
\omega_{\beta}\cong\omega_{\sigma^{-1}\beta}^{-p}
on

. This, together with all the isomorphisms obtained via Proposition

, imply that
\omega_{\tau}
is torsion on

. In particular, we find that
\bigotimes_{\tau\in\mathbb{B}}\omega_{\tau}
is both ample and torsion on

Z
is quasi-affine, which is a contradiction. ∎

Lemma 8.8
.

Let

,

Clearly, for all

∎

Given

\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}(T)=\begin{cases}\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}&{\rm if}\ |\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-\tilde{T}_{\mathfrak{p}}|>1,\\

Definition 8.9

For any

\ \epsilon_{T}(\tau)&{\rm otherwise.}\end{cases}

Given

, using notation from §
6

L_{T,\tau}&{\rm if}\ \beta\neq\tau\in\tilde{T}-T,\\

Theorem 8.10
.

Let
T\subset\mathbb{B}
, and
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
.

(1)

If
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, we have

(a)

{\rm dim}\ H^{0}(C,\omega^{f_{\beta}(T)})=1
, for any connected component
C
of
X_{T}
.

(b)

{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})={\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}.

(2)

If
\beta\not\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, we have
{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})
.

Proof.

First, we show that for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, (1)(b) implies (1)(a). Let
C
be a connected component of
X_{T}
, and
{\bf f}\in H^{0}(C,\omega^{f_{\beta}(T)})
. Let
\tilde{\bf f}
denote the extension of
{\bf f}
to
X_{T}
by setting it zero on the rest of the connected components. Since
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, we can easily see that there is
\delta\geq 0
such that
L_{T,f_{\beta},\beta}(f_{\beta}(T))=-2p^{n_{\beta}+\delta}<0
, which implies that
f_{\beta}(T)\not\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}
. By (1)(b), it follows that
f_{\beta}(T)\not\in{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)))
, and so the restriction of
\tilde{\bf f}
to
X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))
is zero. This means
\bf f
is divisible by the restriction of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
to
C
(which is nonzero by the discussion at the end of §
7
), from which the claim follows.

Next, we prove (1)(b). We begin by show that
{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}={\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, if we have
\{\sigma^{i}\beta:0<i<n_{\beta}\}\cap(\tilde{T}-T)=\emptyset
. In this case, Proposition
4.1
tells us that
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)={\bf h}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T)\displaystyle\prod_{0<i<n_{\beta}}{\bf b}_{\sigma^{i}\beta}(T)^{(-p)^{i}}
, since, by assumption,
\{\sigma^{i}\beta:0\leq i<n_{\beta}\}\subset T
, and
T^{0}=T
(in the notation of the proposition). It follows that
X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))=X_{T}\cap Z({\bf h}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T))=X_{T\cup\{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta\}}
, from which the claim follows by Proposition
6.1
and Theorem
7.6
, noting that in this case,
L_{T,f_{\beta},\tau}=L_{T\cup\{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta\},\tau}
, for all
\tau\in\mathbb{B}-(T\cup\{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta\})
.

We now consider a general
T
, and prove
{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}\subset{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, using an induction on the cardinality of
\{\sigma^{i}\beta:0<i<n_{\beta}\}\cap(\tilde{T}-T)
. The base case was proved above. Now assume
\{\sigma^{i}\beta:0<i<n_{\beta}\}\cap(\tilde{T}-T)=\{\sigma^{r_{i}}\beta:1\leq i\leq t\}
, where
0<r_{1}<...<r_{t}<n_{\beta}
, and
t\geq 1
. Let
\kappa\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}
. By discussions in §
5
(See Equation
5.1
), for
r_{t}<i<n_{\beta}
, we can find
\lambda_{\sigma^{i}\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}
such that

\kappa^{\prime}:=\kappa+\sum_{r_{t}<i<n_{\beta}}\lambda_{\sigma^{i}\beta}b_{\sigma^{i}\beta}-p^{r_{t}-n_{\beta}}L_{T,\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(\kappa)f_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T),

satisfies
k^{\prime}_{\tau}=0
, if
\tau=\sigma^{i}\beta
for
r_{t}\leq i<n_{\beta}
, and
k^{\prime}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}=k_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}+p^{r_{t}-n_{\beta}}L_{T,\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(\kappa)
.

Since
\kappa\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}
, we have
L_{T,\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(\kappa)=L_{T,f_{\beta},\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0
. Lemma
8.8
now implies that
\kappa\in{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})
follows from
\kappa^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})
. We let
T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta\}
, and note that

0\neq{\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})\in H^{0}(X_{T^{\prime}},\omega^{f_{\beta}(T)})
. Furthermore, by the second statement in Proposition
4.1
, we have
X_{T}\cap Z(\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{*}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime}))=X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))
. Therefore,
\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}:X_{T}\rightarrow X_{T^{\prime}}
(defined in §
3
) restricts to give a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle morphism

\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}:X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))\rightarrow X_{T^{\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})).

Hence, Lemma
3.2
implies that

\pi^{*}_{T,T^{\prime}}:H^{0}(X_{T^{\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})),\omega^{\kappa^{\prime}})\rightarrow H^{0}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\kappa^{\prime}})

is an isomorphism (noting that
\mu_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}=n_{\beta}-r_{t}
). Consequently, it is enough to prove that
\kappa^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{T^{\prime},f_{\beta}}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T^{\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})),\omega^{\bullet})
, where the inclusion holds by the induction hypothesis. This follows by checking that for all

L_{T^{\prime},f_{\beta},\tau}(\kappa^{\prime})=L_{T,f_{\beta},\tau}(\kappa)

and

To prove the reverse inclusion, i.e.,
{\mathcal{C}}(X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})\subset{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}}
, for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
, we again use an induction on the cardinality of
\{\sigma^{i}\beta:0<i<n_{\beta}\}\cap(\tilde{T}-T)=\{\sigma^{r_{i}}\beta:1\leq i\leq t\}

T^{\prime}=T\cup\{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta\}
, and
T^{{}^{\prime\prime}}=T\cup\{\sigma^{r_{1}}\beta,...,\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta\}
. Note that
f_{\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})=f_{\beta}(T)
, and
f_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})=f_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T)
. Considering
\pi_{T,T^{\prime\prime}}:X_{T}\rightarrow X_{T^{\prime\prime}}
, and using an argument as in the case of
\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}
above, we see that we have a
(\mathbb{P}^{1})^{t}
-bundle map

\pi_{T,T^{\prime\prime}}:Y:=X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))\rightarrow Y^{\prime\prime}:=X_{T^{\prime\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})).

We want to apply Lemma
7.1
on
X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))
with
{\bf h}_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T)
and
\pi_{T,T^{\prime\prime}}^{*}{\bf f}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})={\bf f}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T)

, by Lemma

\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta\neq\beta

{\mathcal{C}}(Y,\omega^{\bullet})\subseteq\big({\mathcal{C}}(Y\cap Z({\bf h}_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T)),\omega^{\bullet})\cap{\mathcal{C}}(Y\cap Z(\pi_{T,T^{\prime\prime}}^{*}{\bf f}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})),\omega^{\bullet})\big)+\langle h_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta},f_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T)\rangle.

Note that

can be thought of as the vanishing locus of
{\bf f}
, the restriction of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
to
X_{T}\cap Z({\bf h}_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T))=X_{T^{\prime}}
.
The section
{\bf f}
is a section of
\omega^{f_{\beta}(T)}=\omega^{f_{\beta}(T^{\prime})}
. We prove it is nonzero on every connected component of
X_{T^{\prime}}
. Since, by discussions in §
3

is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle over

{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)

{\bf h}_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T)
over

Therefore,
\bf f
is nonzero on every connected comopnent of

, and by (1)(a) for

(which follows from (1)(b) for
T^{\prime}
, by the induction hypothesis)

must be a nonzero scalar multiple of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})
on each connected component of
X_{T}^{\prime}
. We deduce that

Y\cap Z({\bf h}_{\sigma^{r_{t}}\beta}(T))=X_{T^{\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime})).

7.3
, we have

{\mathcal{C}}(Y\cap Z(\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}^{*}{\bf f}_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}(T^{\prime\prime})),\omega^{\bullet})\subseteq\{\kappa:L_{T,\sigma^{r_{i}}\beta}(\kappa)\geq 0,\ \forall\ 1\leq i\leq t\},

. Let

, we have

\pi_{T,T^{\prime}}:X_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))\rightarrow X_{T^{\prime}}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T^{\prime}))

, it is enough to show that

, we have

, and
\Sigma_{\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}}\mathbb{Q}e_{\tau}\subseteq{\mathcal{C}}_{T^{\prime}\cup\{\beta\}}
, as

.
∎

Corollary 8.11

.
∎

Definition 8.12

.

Proposition 8.13
.

Let

be such that
\tilde{T}=T
. Then

{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}_{T}=\{\sum_{\beta}\ell_{\beta}e_{\beta}\in\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}-T}:p^{n_{\beta}}\ell_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}\geq\ell_{\beta},\forall\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T\}.

Proof.

The projection
i_{T}:\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}-\mathbb{T}}
is given by
i_{T}(\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta})=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T}\ell_{\beta}e_{\beta}
, where, for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
, we have
\ell_{\beta}=\sum_{i=0}^{\mu_{\beta}-1}(-p)^{i}k_{\sigma^{i}\beta}
. Note that for
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-\tilde{T}=\mathbb{B}-T
, we have

L_{T,f_{\beta},\beta}(\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta})=p^{n_{\beta}}\ell_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}-\ell_{\beta}.

This proves one inclusion. For the reverse inclusion, we need to show that if
\kappa=\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta}
is such that
L_{T,f_{\beta},\beta}(\sum_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}}k_{\beta}e_{\beta})=p^{n_{\beta}}\ell_{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta}-\ell_{\beta}\geq 0
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
, then
\kappa\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T}\cap(\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T}{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}})
. Note that these conditions imply
\ell_{\beta}\geq 0
for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}-T
. For
{\mathfrak{p}}|p
, and
\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, set
\bar{\sigma}\tau:=\sigma^{n_{\tau}}\tau\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}
, and for
1\leq i\leq|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}|
, let
0<n_{i}(\tau)\leq|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}|
be such that
\bar{\sigma}^{i}\tau=\sigma^{n_{i}(\tau)}\tau
. Then,

p^{n_{1}(\tau)}L_{T,\tau}(\kappa)=\sum_{i=1}^{|\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}-T_{\mathfrak{p}}|}p^{n_{i}(\tau)}\ell_{\bar{\sigma}^{i}\tau}\geq 0.

It remains to show that
L_{T,f_{\beta},\tau}(\kappa)\geq 0
, for all
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)=\mathbb{B}-T
and
\beta\neq\tau\in\mathbb{B}-(T\cup\{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta\})
. If
\beta\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}
and
\tau\not\in\mathbb{B}_{\mathfrak{p}}

from above. Hence, we can assume

, and pick

such that
\bar{\sigma}^{j}\tau=\beta
. Let

∎

Remark 8.14

9.
Minimal weights

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
and
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X_{T},\omega^{\kappa})
. For each
\beta\not\in T
, we let
a_{\beta}
be the maximal power of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
that divides
\bf f
(which exists by Lemma 7.2 of
[
3
]
).
It follows from part (2) of Proposition

that for any

, the strata

and
X^{\prime}_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta^{\prime}}(T))
have no common irreducible components. This implies that
{\bf f}
is divisible by
\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)^{a_{\beta}}
. Hence, we can write

for some
{\bf f}_{0}\in H^{0}(X_{T},\omega^{\kappa_{0}})
,
where
\kappa_{0}=\kappa-\sum_{\beta\not\in T}a_{\beta}f_{\beta}(T)
.
It follows from the definition of the
a_{\beta}
that
\bf f_{0}
is not divisible by
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
for any
\beta\not\in T
, and that
i_{T}(\kappa_{0})\in{\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{T-\rm red}
, where

is defined in (
5.1
).

Definition 9.1
.

For

, we define the

of
{\bf f}
to be

\Phi({\bf f})=i_{T}(\kappa_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{B}-T}.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
T\subseteq\mathbb{B}
, and
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X_{T},\omega^{\kappa})
. Then we have
\Phi({\bf f})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}_{T}
.

Proof.

Write
{\bf f}={\bf f}_{0}\prod_{\beta\not\in T}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)^{a_{\beta}}
, where
a_{\beta}
is as above,

, and

, then
\kappa_{0}\not\in i_{T}^{-1}({\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}_{T})={\mathcal{C}}_{T}\cap(\bigcap_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)}{\mathcal{D}}_{T,f_{\beta}})
. Since

, it follows that

, for some

. Hence, there exist

, and

, such that

is divisible by

.
∎

\mathcal{D}_{T}=\langle f_{\beta}(T):\beta\not\in T\rangle+\langle\pm b_{\beta}:\beta\in T\rangle
, it follows that

\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}\times\mathcal{D}_{T}\subset\mathcal{C}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\bullet}\omega^{\bullet})
.
∎

Corollary 10.3
.

Suppose that
\xi\in\Delta
and
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{B}}
. If
\kappa\not\in\mathcal{D}_{T}
, then
H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi}\omega^{\kappa})=0
.

Proof.

This is immediate from Corollary
10.2
.
∎

We may also define a stratification on
X^{\prime}_{T}
as in §
8.1
: For
J\subset\mathbb{B}-T
, we let

X^{\prime}_{T,J}=X^{\prime}_{T}\cap\big(\bigcap_{\beta\in J}Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))\big),\quad\mbox{and}\quad W^{\prime}_{T,J}=X^{\prime}_{T,J}-{\bigcup}_{\beta\not\in J}X^{\prime}_{T,\{\beta\}}.

Since the line bundles
\delta_{\beta}
are torsion, the same proofs as before carry over to give the following:

Proposition 10.4
.

For each
J\subset\mathbb{B}-T
, we have

(1)

W^{\prime}_{T,J}
is non-empty, quasi-affine and equidimensional of dimension
\mathbb{B}-(T\cup J)
;

(2)

X^{\prime}_{T,J}=\overline{W}^{\prime}_{T,J}=\bigcup_{J^{\prime}\supset J}W^{\prime}_{T,J^{\prime}}
.

Finally, we translate our results on minimal weights to the setting of
{\operatorname{Res}}_{F/\mathbb{Q}}{\operatorname{GL}}_{2}
. Suppose that
\kappa\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{B}}
,
\xi\in\Delta
,
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi}\omega^{\kappa})
, and for each
\beta\not\in T
, we let
a_{\beta}
be the maximal power of
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
that divides
\bf f
(which again exists by
[
3
, Lemma 7.2]
).
By part (1) of Proposition
10.4
, the strata
X^{\prime}_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta}(T))
and
X^{\prime}_{T}\cap Z({\bf f}_{\beta^{\prime}}(T))
have no common irreducible components for distinct
\beta,\beta^{\prime}\not\in T
, so
{\bf f}
is divisible by
\prod_{\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)^{a_{\beta}}
. We can therefore write

{\bf f}={\bf f}_{0}\prod_{\beta\not\in T}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)^{a_{\beta}}

for some
{\bf f}_{0}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi_{0}}\omega^{\kappa_{0}})
,
where
\xi_{0}=\xi-\sum_{\beta\in\widetilde{T}-T}a_{\beta}\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\overline{\beta}
,
\kappa_{0}=\kappa-\sum_{\beta\not\in T}a_{\beta}f_{\beta}(T)
, and
{\bf f}_{0}
is not divisible by
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
for any
\beta\not\in T
.

Definition 10.5
.

For
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi}\omega^{\kappa})
, we define the
minimal weight
of
{\bf f}
to be

\Phi({\bf f})=i_{T}(\kappa_{0})\in\mathbb{Z}^{\mathbb{B}-T},

where
i_{T}:\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}}\to\mathbb{Q}^{\mathbb{B}-T}
is defined in (
5.1
), so in particular,
\Phi({\bf f})\in i_{T}({\mathcal{C}}_{T})={\mathcal{C}}_{T}^{T-\rm red}
.

Proposition 10.6
.

If
0\neq{\bf f}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi}\omega^{\kappa})
, then
\Phi({\bf f})\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}_{T}
.

Proof.

Write
{\bf f}={\bf f}_{0}\prod_{\beta\not\in T}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)^{a_{\beta}}
as above, so
{\bf f}_{0}\in H^{0}(X^{\prime}_{T},\delta^{\xi_{0}}\omega^{\kappa_{0}})
and
i_{T}(\kappa_{0})=\Phi({\bf f})
. As in the proof of Proposition
9.2
, if
\Phi({\bf f})\not\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\rm min}_{T}
, then
L_{T,f_{\beta},\tau}(\kappa_{0})<0
for some
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(T)
and
\tau\in\mathbb{B}-(T\cup\{\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\beta\})
.

For each
\epsilon\in(\mathbb{A}_{F}^{\infty,p})^{\times}
, let
{\bf f}_{0}^{\epsilon}\in H^{0}(X^{\epsilon}_{T},\delta^{\xi_{0}}\omega^{\kappa_{0}})
denote the pull-back of
{\bf f}_{0}
to
X_{T}^{\epsilon}
(as in the proof of Corollary
10.2
). Since
L_{T,f_{\beta},\tau}(\kappa_{0})<0
, it follows from Corollary
8.11
that
{\bf f}_{0}^{\epsilon}
is divisible by (the pull-back of)
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
. Since this holds for all
\epsilon
, it follows that the pull-back of
{\bf f}_{0}
to
\coprod_{\epsilon}X_{T}^{\epsilon}
has the form
{\bf f}_{1}{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
for some

{\bf f}_{1}\in H^{0}(\coprod_{\epsilon}X^{\epsilon}_{T},\delta^{\xi_{1}}\omega^{\kappa_{1}}),

where
\xi_{1}=\xi
or
\xi_{0}-\sigma^{n_{\beta}}\overline{\beta}
and
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{0}-f_{\beta}(T)
. Since
{\bf f}_{0}
and
{\bf f}_{\beta}(T)
descend to sections over
X^{\prime}_{T}
, it follows that so does
{\bf f}_{1}
, contradicting the definition of
{\bf f}_{0}
.
∎

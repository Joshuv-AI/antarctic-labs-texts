Bound state solutions with a linear combination of Yuakawa plus four-parameter diatomic potentials using path integral approach: Thermodynamic properties
Mohamed Améziane Sadoun, Redouane Zamoum, Abdellah Touati

Abstract
In this paper, we investigate the approximate analytical bound states with a linear combination of two diatomic molecule potentials, Yukawa and four parameters potentials, within the framework of the path integral formalism. With the help of an appropriate approximation to evaluate the centrifugal term, the energy spectrum and the normalized wave functions of the bound states are derived from the poles of Green’s function and its residues. The partition function and other thermodynamic properties were obtained using the compact form of the energy equation.

Indeed, solving these equations leads to knowledge of the energy states and wave functions that contain all the information associated with the investigated system. In terms of mathematics, as the equations of quantum physics are partial differential equations reduced to second-order differential equations by physical considerations, there are several methods of resolution depending on the type of equation obtained. II
Path integral formalism

Within the framework of path integral formalism, the investigation of a non-relativistic spinless system subjected to potential (
1
) consists of first writing the Green’s function solution of the Schrodinger equation. Since the potential (
1
) is a central potential with spherical symmetry, the Green’s function is written in the form

G\left(\vec{r}^{\prime\prime},\vec{r}^{\prime};E\right)=\frac{1}{r"r^{\prime}}\sum\limits_{l=0}^{\infty}\frac{2l+1}{4\pi}G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime},E\right)P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)

(2)

where
P_{l}\left(\cos\theta\right)
is the Legendre polynomial with
\cos\theta=\left(\vec{r}^{\prime\prime},\vec{r}^{\prime}\right).
The radial Green’s function
G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime},E\right)
is given by
18

G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime},E\right)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dT\left\langle r^{\prime\prime}\right|\exp\left[-\frac{i}{\hbar}T\left(H_{l}-E\right)\right]\left|r^{\prime}\right\rangle

(3)

with the hamiltonian

H_{l}=\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{2m}+V\left(r\right)+\frac{\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2mr^{2}}.

(4)

We aim to find the energy spectrum and the normalized wave functions by computing Green’s function (
3
). At this level, it is essential to note that an exact analytical calculation is impossible because of the difference between the behavior of the functions that appear in the
expression of the potential (
1
) and the centrifugal term. However, there is a way to overcome this difficulty, introduce the approximations

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\dfrac{1}{r}\approx\dfrac{2\alpha e^{-\alpha r}}{1-qe^{-2\alpha r}}\\
\dfrac{1}{r^{2}}\approx\dfrac{4\alpha^{2}e^{-2\alpha r}}{\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{2}}\end{array}\right.

(5)

that are only valid if
q\geq 1.
In this case, the Hamiltonian (
4
) that takes the new form

H_{l}=\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{2m}-\frac{\left(b+2\alpha c\right)e^{-2\alpha r}}{1-qe^{-2\alpha r}}+\frac{ae^{-4\alpha r}}{\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{2}}+\frac{4\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)e^{-2\alpha r}}{2m\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{2}}

(6)

presents a strong singularity at
r_{0}=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\ln q.
We then distinguish two regions defined by the intervals
\left]0,r_{0}\right[
and
\left]r_{0},+\infty\right[.
In the first region, the system describes a particle confined within a sphere of radius
r=r_{0}
under the influence of potential (
1
). This problem is not of great physical interest and
moreover, an analytical solution is impossible. We are therefore interested in the case where
r\in\left]r_{0},+\infty\right[.
In this region of space, the potential (
1
) has a strong singularity at
r_{0}
and to obtain a stable discrete form of the path integral, we introduce a regulating function and write the expression (
3
) as a path integral

G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime},E\right)=\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}dSP_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S\right),

(7)

where

\displaystyle P_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S\right)

\displaystyle=f_{R}\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f_{L}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\left\langle r^{\prime\prime}\right|\exp\left[-\frac{i}{\hbar}S\left(\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{2m}+V\left(r\right)+\frac{\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2mr^{2}}-E\right)\right]\left|r^{\prime}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{R}\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f_{L}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\int Dr\left(s\right)\int\frac{DP_{r}\left(s\right)}{2\pi\hbar}\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\limits_{0}^{S}\left[P_{r}\dot{r}-f_{L}\left(r\right)\left(\frac{P_{r}^{2}}{2m}+V\left(r\right)+\frac{\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2mr^{2}}-E\right)f_{R}\left(r\right)\right]ds\right\}.

(8)

In a discrete form

P_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S\right)=f_{R}\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f_{L}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left[\int\int dr_{j}\right]\prod_{j=1}^{N+1}\left[\int\frac{d\left(P_{r}\right)_{j}}{\left(2\pi\right)^{2}}\right]\exp\left[i\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\mathcal{A}_{1}^{j}\right],

(9)

where the action
\mathcal{A}_{1}^{j}
is given by

\mathcal{A}_{1}^{j}=-\left(P_{r}\right)_{j}\Delta r_{j}-\varepsilon_{s}f_{L}\left(r_{j}\right)\left[\frac{\left(P_{r}\right)_{j}^{2}}{2m}+V_{eff}\left(r_{j}\right)-E\right]f_{R}\left(r_{j-1}\right)

(10)

and

\varepsilon_{s^{\prime}}=\frac{S}{N+1}=ds=\frac{dt}{f_{L}\left(r_{j}\right)f_{R}\left(r_{j-1}\right)},\text{ \ }dt=\varepsilon_{t}=\frac{T}{N+1}.

(11)

The regulating function
f\left(r\right)
introduced by Kleinert
30
is defined by

f\left(r\right)=f_{R}\left(r\right)f_{L}\left(r\right)=f^{1-\lambda}\left(r\right)f^{\lambda}\left(r\right).

(12)

To simplify the calculation of the propagator
P_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S\right)
, one poses
\lambda=\frac{1}{2}
, that is equivalent to choose the prescription of the mid-point, i.e. to make a development of the action around the mid-point. In this case, and after
integrating on the variables
\left(P_{r}\right)_{j}
, we obtain

P_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S\right)=\left[f_{R}\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f_{L}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{j=1}^{N+1}\left[\frac{m}{2i\pi\hbar\varepsilon_{s}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left[\int\frac{dr_{j}}{\sqrt{f\left(r_{j}\right)}}\right]\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\mathcal{A}_{2}^{j}\right\}

(13)

where

\mathcal{A}_{2}^{j}=\frac{m\left(\Delta r_{j}\right)^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{s}\sqrt{f\left(r_{j}\right)f\left(r_{j-1}\right)}}-\varepsilon_{s}\left(V_{eff}(r_{j})-E\right)\sqrt{f\left(r_{j}\right)f\left(r_{j-1}\right)}

(14)

and

V_{eff}\left(r\right)=\frac{4\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)e^{-2\alpha r}}{2m\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{2}}-\frac{\left(b+2\alpha c\right)e^{-2\alpha r}}{1-qe^{-2\alpha r}}+\frac{ae^{-4\alpha r}}{\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{2}}.

(15)

Evaluation of the radial Green’s function

In this case, the calculation of the Green’s function of the angular momentum waves
l\neq 0

r=\frac{1}{2\alpha}\ln\left(\exp\left(4\alpha\xi\right)+q\right)

and the regulating function

f\left[r\left(\xi\right)\right]=\frac{\exp\left(4\alpha\xi\right)}{\cosh_{q}^{2}\left(2\alpha\xi\right)}=\left[h^{\prime}\left(\xi\right)\right]^{2}.

Then we write (

\displaystyle P_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};S^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[f\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f\left(r^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\lim\limits_{N\rightarrow\infty}\prod_{n=1}^{N+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2i\pi\varepsilon_{s^{\prime}}}}\prod_{j=1}^{N}\left[\int d\xi_{j}\right]\exp\left\{i\sum_{j=1}^{N+1}\left[\frac{\left(\Delta\xi_{j}\right)^{2}}{2\varepsilon_{s^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{8\varepsilon_{s}}\left(\left(\frac{h^{\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right)^{2}-\frac{2}{3}\frac{h^{\prime\prime\prime}}{h^{\prime}}\right)\left(\Delta\xi_{j}\right)^{4}\right.\right.

(18)

\displaystyle+\varepsilon_{s}\left(2E-\frac{2}{q^{2}}\left[\frac{4q\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2m}+a\right]\right)+\varepsilon_{s}\left(2E+\frac{2}{q^{2}}\left[\frac{4q\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2m}+a\right]\right)\tanh_{q}\left(2\alpha\xi_{j}\right)

\displaystyle+\left.\left.\varepsilon_{s}\left(b+2\alpha c+\frac{4a}{q}-qE\right)\frac{1}{\cosh_{q}^{2}\left(\alpha\xi_{n}\right)}\right]\right\}.

In the equations (
17
) and (

), we used the deformed hyperbolic functions introduced by Arai

;
2

\left\{\begin{array}[]{c}\sinh_{q}x=\frac{e^{x}-qe^{-x}}{2},\cosh_{q}x=\frac{e^{x}-qe^{-x}}{2},\tanh_{q}x=\frac{\sinh_{q}x}{\cosh_{q}x},\\
e^{2x}=2\cosh_{q}^{2}x+2\cosh_{q}x\sinh_{q}x-q,\\
e^{-2x}=\frac{1}{q}\left(2\cosh_{q}^{2}x-2\cosh_{q}x\sinh_{q}x-q\right),\end{array}\right.

Note that the term in

which appears in the kernel (

) contributes significantly to the path integral. It can be estimated using perturbation theory and replaced by

\displaystyle\left\langle\left(\triangle\xi_{n}\right)^{4}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\left(\triangle\xi_{n}\right)\left(\triangle\xi_{n}\right)^{4}\left[\frac{m}{2i\pi\hbar\varepsilon_{s}}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\exp\left[\frac{im}{2\hbar\varepsilon_{s}}\left(\triangle\xi_{n}\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-3\varepsilon_{s}\left(\frac{\hbar}{m}\right)^{2}.

Finally, the change of variables

\displaystyle\left[f\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f\left(r^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{0}^{\infty}d\Lambda\text{ }\exp\left(\frac{i}{\hbar}E_{RM}\Lambda\right)P_{RM}\left(u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime};\Lambda\right)

(22)

and

\displaystyle P_{RM}\left(u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime};\Lambda\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int\mathfrak{D}u\left(\tau\right)\exp\left\{\frac{i}{\hbar}\int\limits_{0}^{\Lambda}d\tau\left[\frac{m}{2}\dot{u}^{2}\right.\right.+2\left(E+\frac{1}{q^{2}}\left[\frac{4q\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2m}+a\right]+\alpha^{2}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\right)\tanh u

(23)

\displaystyle+\left.\left.\left(\frac{b+2\alpha c}{q}+\frac{a}{q^{2}}-E-\alpha^{2}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\right)\frac{1}{\cosh^{2}u}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\int\mathcal{D}u\left(\tau\right)\exp\left\{i\int\limits_{0}^{\Lambda}\left[\frac{\dot{u}^{2}}{2}-V_{RM}^{l}\left(u\right)\right]d\tau\right\}.

is none other than that relating to the Rosen-Morse potential

;

V_{RM}^{l}\left(u\right)=A_{l}\tanh u-\frac{B}{\cosh^{2}u},\text{ \ \ \ \ }u\in\mathcal{R},

(24)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}A_{l}=-\frac{1}{2\alpha^{2}}\left(E+\frac{1}{q^{2}}\left[\frac{4q\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2m}+a\right]+\alpha^{2}\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\right),\\

(25)

Since the exact solution is known

G_{RM}\left(u^{\prime\prime},u^{\prime};E\right)=\frac{m}{i\hbar}\Gamma\left(M_{1}-L_{E}\right)\Gamma\left(L_{E}-M_{1}+1\right)d_{M_{1},M_{2}}^{L_{E}}\left(\theta^{\prime\prime}-\pi\right)d_{M_{1},M_{2}}^{L_{E}\ast}\left(\theta^{\prime}\right)\text{ },

(26)

where

is the Wigner function, with

The indices

\displaystyle L_{E}

\displaystyle=L_{B}=-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{8mB}{\hbar^{2}}+1}

(27)

\displaystyle M_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\hbar^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{-A_{l}-E_{RM}}+\sqrt{A_{l}-E_{RM}}\right)

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{2m}{q^{2}\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{q\hbar^{2}l\left(l+1\right)}{2m}+\frac{a}{4\alpha^{2}}\right)+\frac{1}{4}}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{-\frac{2mE}{\alpha^{2}\hbar^{2}}},

\displaystyle M_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{m}{2\hbar^{2}}}\left(\sqrt{-A_{l}-E_{RM}}-\sqrt{A_{l}-E_{RM}}\right)

(29)

\displaystyle=

IV
Energy spectrum and wave functions

The energy spectrum is obtained from the poles of the radial Green’s function which occur when
M_{1}-L_{E}=-n
, in the Euler function
\Gamma\left(M_{1}-L_{E}\right)
, where
n=0,1,2,..
.This yields the discrete energy spectrum

E_{n,l}=-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{1}{q^{2}}\left[\frac{\frac{\alpha^{2}q^{2}m}{2\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{b+2\alpha c}{\alpha^{2}q}+\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)-\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}\right)^{2}}{\alpha qn+P_{l}}\right]^{2},

(30)

where

P_{l}=\frac{\alpha q}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{4l\left(l+1\right)}{q}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)}\right).

(31)

The wave functions are obtained by approximating the Euler function
\Gamma\left(M_{1}-L_{E}\right)
near the poles
M_{1}-L_{E}=-n
as follows
29
:

\displaystyle\Gamma\left(M_{1}-L_{E}\right)

\displaystyle\approx

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{n!}\frac{1}{M_{1}-L_{E}+n}

(32)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{n!}\frac{\alpha\hbar^{2}}{m}\frac{Q_{l}\left(P_{l}+\alpha qn-qQ_{l}\right)}{\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}\right)\left(E-E_{n,l}\right)},

with

Q_{l}=\frac{1}{q}\frac{\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}\right)^{2}-\frac{\alpha^{2}q^{2}m}{2\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{b+2\alpha c}{\alpha^{2}q}+\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)}{\alpha qn+P_{l}}.

(33)

and using the Wigner’s function symmetry property

d_{M_{1},M_{2}}^{L_{E}}\left(\theta\right)=\left(-1\right)^{L_{E}-M_{1}}d_{M_{1},-M_{2}}^{L_{E}}\left(\theta-\pi\right),

(34)

we can express the discrete part of Green’s function
G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};E\right)
as a spectral expansion:

\displaystyle G_{l}\left(r^{\prime\prime},r^{\prime};E\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-i\hbar\frac{\left[f\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)f\left(r^{\prime}\right)\right]^{\frac{1}{4}}}{r^{\prime\prime}r^{\prime}}\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\max}}\eta\frac{Q_{l}\left(n+P_{l}-Q_{l}\right)}{n!\left(n+P_{l}\right)\left(E-E_{n,l}\right)}d_{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+n+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}\left(\theta^{\prime\prime}\right)d_{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha q},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+n+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}\left(\theta^{\prime}\right)

(35)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\hbar\sum_{n=1}^{n_{\max}}\frac{\chi_{n,l}^{q\geq 1\ast}\left(r^{\prime}\right)\chi_{n,l}^{q\geq 1}\left(r^{\prime\prime}\right)}{E-E_{n,l}^{q\geq 1}},

where the wave functions
\chi_{n,l}^{\lambda\geq 1}\left(r\right)
, suitably normalized, are

\chi_{n,l}\left(r\right)=\left[-\frac{2Q_{l}\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}-qQ_{l}\right)}{n!\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{f^{\frac{1}{4}}\left(r\right)}{r}d_{-\frac{1}{2}+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha},\frac{1}{2}-\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+n+\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}\left(\theta\right).

(36)

Using the relation between Wigner functions and hypergeometric functions
17

\displaystyle d_{M_{1},M_{2}}^{L_{E}}\left(\theta\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{\Gamma\left(L_{E}+M_{1}+1\right)\Gamma\left(L_{E}-M_{2}+1\right)}{\Gamma\left(L_{E}-M_{1}+1\right)\Gamma\left(L_{E}+M_{2}+1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(M_{1}-M_{2}+1\right)}\left(\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\right)^{\frac{M_{1}-M_{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{1+\cos\theta}{2}\right)^{\frac{M_{1}+M_{2}}{2}}

\displaystyle\times_{2}F_{1}\left(-L_{E}+M_{1},L_{E}+M_{1}+1,M_{1}-M_{2}+1;\frac{1-\cos\theta}{2}\right),

we finally obtain

\displaystyle\chi_{n,l}^{q\geq 1}\left(r\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left[\frac{-Q_{l}\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}-qQ_{l}\right)}{\left(\alpha qn+P_{l}\right)}\frac{\Gamma\left(n+\frac{2P_{l}}{\alpha q}\right)\Gamma\left(n+\frac{2P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{2Q_{l}}{\alpha}\right)}{n!\Gamma\left(n-\frac{2Q_{l}}{\alpha}+1\right)}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{\Gamma\left(\frac{2P_{l}}{\alpha q}\right)}\left(1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{\frac{P_{l}}{\alpha q}}\left(qe^{-2\alpha r}\right)^{-\frac{Q_{l}}{\alpha}}

(38)

\displaystyle\times_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,n+\frac{2P_{l}}{\alpha q}-\frac{2Q_{l}}{\alpha},\frac{2P_{l}}{\alpha q};1-qe^{-2\alpha r}\right).

To make sure that the wave functions
\chi_{n,l}^{q\geq 1}\left(r\right)
stay finite when
r\rightarrow\infty
, we must impose the condition
Q_{l}<0
.

\displaystyle n_{\max}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left\{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{4l\left(l+1\right)}{q}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)+1}\right)+\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}q^{2}m}{2\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{b+2\alpha c}{\alpha^{2}q}+\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)}\right\}

(39)

V
Thermodynamic properties

In molecular physics, the thermodynamic properties of any molecule is obtain by using the vibrational partition function, which is given in the discrete form by the summation over all possible levels vibrational energy,
48
;
26
:

Z_{vib}(\beta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\lambda}e^{-\beta E_{n}},\quad\beta=\frac{1}{k_{B}T},

(40)

where
\lambda_{max},\,k_{B},\,T
are the maximum vibrational, the Boltzmann constant and the temperature respectively. The maximal virational parameter
\lambda_{max}
is obtained by solving the following condition
9
:

\frac{\partial E^{q}_{n,l}}{\partial n}|_{n=\lambda_{max}}=0,

(41)

where the solution to this equation for different molecules is presented in the next section.

The energy spectrum given in Eq. (
30
), and is can be simplified as follow

E^{q}_{n,l}=-\Lambda\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{n+\eta_{2}}-(n+\eta_{2})\right)^{2},

(42)

where:

\displaystyle\Lambda

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha\hbar^{2}}{2m},\quad\eta_{1}=\frac{m}{2\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{b+2\alpha c}{\alpha^{2}q}+\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right),\quad\eta_{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left(1+\sqrt{\frac{4l\left(l+1\right)}{q}+\frac{2m}{\hbar^{2}}\left(\frac{a}{\alpha^{2}q^{2}}\right)}\right).

(43)

The partition function write in this case as follow:

Z_{vib}(\beta)=\sum_{n=0}^{\lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{n+\eta_{2}}-(n+\eta_{2})\right)^{2}},

(44)

In order to compute the vibrational partition function in the case finite summation
\lambda_{\text{max}}
, we use the Poisson summation formula, which is given by
48
;
26
:

\sum_{n=0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}}f(n)=\frac{1}{2}\left[f(0)-f(\lambda_{\text{max}}+1)\right]+\sum_{m=-\infty}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}+1}f(x)e^{-i2\pi mx}dx

(45)

and in the leading order of approximation, we can writ:

\sum_{n=0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}}f(n)=\frac{1}{2}\left[f(0)-f(\lambda_{\text{max}}+1)\right]+\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}+1}f(x)dx

(46)

In our case the function
f(n)
is given by:

f(n)=e^{\beta\Lambda\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{n+\eta_{2}}-(n+\eta_{2})\right)^{2}},

(47)

Then the vibrational partition function become:

Z_{vib}(\beta)=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{1}^{2}}-e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{2}^{2}}\right]+\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}+1}e^{\beta\lambda\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{x+\eta_{2}}-(x+\eta_{2})\right)^{2}}dx

(48)

where:

\rho_{1}=\frac{\eta_{1}}{\eta_{2}}-\eta_{2},\,\,\rho_{2}=\frac{\eta_{1}}{\lambda_{\text{max}}+1+\eta_{2}}-(\lambda_{\text{max}}+1+\eta_{2}).

(49)

To compute the integral of last term in equation (
48
), we introduce a new variable
y=\frac{\eta_{1}}{x+\eta_{2}}-(x+\eta_{2})
. The integral can be computed as follow:

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\lambda_{\text{max}}+1}e^{\beta\Lambda\left(\frac{\eta_{1}}{x+\eta_{2}}-x+\eta_{2}\right)^{2}}dx&=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\rho_{1}}^{\rho_{2}}e^{\beta\Lambda y^{2}}\left(\frac{y}{\sqrt{y^{2}+4\delta_{1}}}-1\right)dy\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta\Lambda}}\Bigg[\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{1}\right)-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{2}\right)\\
&\quad+e^{-4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}\bigg(-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{1}^{2})}\right)+\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{2}^{2})}\right)\bigg)\Bigg]\end{split}

(50)

where
\operatorname{erfi}(x)
is the complex error function
1
1

1

The complex error function is defined by:
\operatorname{erfi}(z)=-i\operatorname{erf}(iz)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{0}^{z}e^{t^{2}}dt
. The final vibrational partition function is written by:

\displaystyle Z_{vib}(\beta,\lambda_{\text{max}})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{1}^{2}}-e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{2}^{2}}\right]+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta\Lambda}}\Bigg[\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{1}\right)-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}\bigg(-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{1}^{2})}\right)+\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{2}^{2})}\right)\bigg)\Bigg].

(51)

By using the above vibrational partition function, one can obtain all thermodynamic properties for a diatomic molecule of the Yukawa plus four-parameter diatomic potentials.

V.0.1
Vibrational free energy

The free energy for a molecule is expressed as follow:

\displaystyle F(\beta)

\displaystyle=-k_{B}T\ln Z_{vib}(\beta),

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{\beta}\ln\Bigg[\frac{1}{2}\left[e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{1}^{2}}-e^{\beta\Lambda\rho_{2}^{2}}\right]+\frac{1}{4}\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{\beta\Lambda}}\Bigg[\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{1}\right)-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\rho_{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+e^{-4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}\bigg(-\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{1}^{2})}\right)+\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda(4\eta_{1}+\rho_{2}^{2})}\right)\bigg)\Bigg]\Bigg].

(52)

V.0.2
Vibrational mean energy

By definition the vibrational mean energy can be evaluated using the above partition function (
V
):

\displaystyle U_{vib}(\beta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\partial\ln Z_{vib}}{\partial\beta},

\displaystyle=\frac{4(\beta\Lambda)^{3/2}e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda y^{2}}y^{2}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\sqrt{\pi}\left(\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}-e^{4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)}{2\beta\left(\sqrt{\pi}\left(\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}-e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)-2e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda y^{2}}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)}

\displaystyle+\frac{2\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\left(y-\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)e^{\beta\Lambda y^{2}}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+8\sqrt{\pi}\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}e^{-4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}}{2\beta\Bigg(\sqrt{\pi}\left(\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}-e^{4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)-2e^{4\beta\Lambda\eta_{1}}e^{\beta\Lambda y^{2}}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\Bigg)}

(53)

V.0.3
Vibrational heat capacity

The heat capacity of this system is defined as a derivative of the mean energy with respect to
\beta
, and is given by:

\displaystyle C_{vib}(\beta)

\displaystyle=-k_{B}\beta^{2}\frac{\partial U_{vib}(\beta)}{\partial\beta},

(54)

By using the vibrational mean energy expression (
53
), we find:

\displaystyle C_{vib}(\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{-2(\beta\Lambda)^{3/2}e^{2\beta\Lambda\left(4\eta_{1}+y^{2}\right)}\Gamma_{1}(y)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\pi\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}e^{8\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)^{2}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\pi\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)^{2}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}}{2\left(-\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\sqrt{\pi}e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+2\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda\left(4\eta_{1}+y^{2}\right)}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle+\frac{-\sqrt{\pi}\beta\Lambda e^{\beta\Lambda\left(4\eta_{1}+y^{2}\right)}\Gamma_{2}(y)\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda\left(8\eta_{1}+y^{2}\right)}\Gamma_{4}(y)-\Gamma_{3}(y)\Big)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}}{2\left(-\sqrt{\pi}\operatorname{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+\sqrt{\pi}e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\operatorname{erfi}\left(y\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\right)\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}+2\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}e^{\beta\Lambda\left(4\eta_{1}+y^{2}\right)}\Big|^{\rho_{2}}_{\rho_{1}}\right)^{2}}.

(55)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{1}(y)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}+y\left(16\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda-1\right)+3\Big),

(57)

\displaystyle=2\sqrt{\pi}e^{4\beta\eta_{1}\Lambda}\left(16\beta^{2}\eta_{1}^{2}\Lambda^{2}+1\right)\text{erfi}\left(\sqrt{\beta\Lambda}\sqrt{4\eta_{1}+y^{2}}\right),

(58)

VII
Conclusion

In this paper, we have investigated the path integral treatment of a linear combination of Yukawa and four-parameter potentials used as a model to describe the diatomic molecule interaction. As we have shown, Green’s function associated with this potential cannot be evaluated for any deformation parameter in a unified manner. Making an appropriate approximation to deal with the centrifugal term, the energy spectrum and the normalized wave functions of the bound states are obtained from the poles of Green’s function and its residues. Evaluation of the partition function from the energy spectrum allowed us to assess some thermodynamic properties. To verify the accuracy of our results, a numerical evaluation of the energy levels as a function of the deformation and screening parameters for some diatomic molecules like
H_{2},\,I_{2},\,LiH,\,CO,\,HCl
, and
NO
, and the associated plots of the various thermodynamic functions are established.

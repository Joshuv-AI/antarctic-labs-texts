Note on the existence of classical solutions of derivative semilinear models for one dimensional wave equation
Yuki Haruyama, Takiko Sasaki, and Hiroyuki Takamura

Abstract
This note is a supplement with a new result to the review paper by Takamura [ 13 ] on nonlinear wave equations in one space dimension. We are focusing here to the long-time existence of classical solutions of semilinear wave equations in one space dimension, especially with derivative nonlinear terms of product-type. Our result is an extension of the single component case, but it is meaningful to provide models as possible as many to cover the optimality of the general theory. The proof is based on the classical iteration argument of the point-wise estimate of the solution.

Introduction

In this note, we shall consider the initial value problems;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\displaystyle u_{tt}-u_{xx}=|u_{t}|^{p}|u_{x}|^{q}&\mbox{in}\quad{\bf R}\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)={\varepsilon}g(x),&x\in{\bf R},\end{array}\right.

(1.1)

where
p,q\in(1,\infty)\cup\{0\}
and
T>0
.
Let us assume that
f
and
g
are given smooth functions of compact support
and a parameter
{\varepsilon}>0
is “small enough”.
We are interested in the lifespan
T({\varepsilon})
, the maximal existence time,
of classical solutions of (
1.1
).
Note that we do not investigate the case of
p\in(0,1)
or
q\in(0,1)

because we cannot expect the existence of local-in-time solution.
It is naturally conjectured that the estimate,

T({\varepsilon})\sim C{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q-1)},

(1.2)

holds because
the valance of the amplitude of the solution in both sides in (
1.1
) is

{\varepsilon}\approx{\varepsilon}^{p+q}t
according to the integral representations of
u_{t}
and
u_{x}
in (
2.6
) and (
2.9
)
as well as definitions of integral operators
L^{\prime}
and
\overline{L^{\prime}}
in (
2.8
) and (
2.11
) below.
Here we denote the fact that there are positive constants,

C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, independent of
{\varepsilon}
satisfying
A({\varepsilon},C_{1})\leq T({\varepsilon})\leq A({\varepsilon},C_{2})

by
T({\varepsilon})\sim A({\varepsilon},C)
.

The case of
p>1
and
q=0
in (
1.2
) is verified by Zhou
[
15
]
for the upper bound
and by Kitamura, Morisawa and Takamura
[
5
]
for the lower bound.
We note that this case was first studied to show the optimality of the general theory
for nonlinear wave equations by Li, Yu and Zhou
[
6
,
7
]

in which only the smooth nonlinear terms are considered.
On the other hand, the case of
p=0
and
q>1
in (
1.2
)
is verified by Sasaki, Takamatsu and Takamura
[
11
]
.
This result is meaningful especially in the blow-up part,
not only for the optimality but also for the future study of “the blow-up boundary”
because the case of
p>1
and
q=0
is well-studied by Sasaki
[
10
]

but this case is still open due to the fact that there is no point-wise positivity of the solution.
We note that
[
11
]
has a trivial error in the proof of the existence part.
It is easy to modify it, but here we show how to do among the proof of our result.
Finally, we point out that Haruyama and Takamura
[
1
]
recently study a special quasilinear model,

u_{tt}-u_{xx}=|u_{x}|^{p-2}u_{x}u_{xx}
with
p>1
,
and prove that the upper bound in (
1.2
) with
p>1
and
q=0
is valid even for this equation.
See Introduction in
[
1
]
and references therein.

Our purpose is to show the lower bound of (
1.2
) in the general case of
p>1
and
q>1
.
We note that the upper bound in such a case is still open because the proof of the blow-up result
for the case of
p>1
and
q=0
is organized
by comparison argument with ordinary differential equations
and point-wise estimates of the solution itself,
while the one for the case of
p=0
and
q>1
is organized by ordinary differential inequality
for some weighted functional of the solution which was introduced
by Rammaha
[
8
,
9
]
.
See
[
5
,
11
]
for details.
The difficulty in proving the blow-up part in (
1.2
) comes from this difference.
Moreover, we investigate the upper bound of the lifespan of a solution of a special model,

u_{tt}-u_{xx}=|u_{t}\pm u_{x}|^{p-1}(u_{t}\pm u_{x})\ (p>1),

(1.3)

which includes product terms of
u_{t}
and
u_{x}
.
The result is the expected one,
T({\varepsilon})\leq C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}
.
We note that this is optimal when
p
is odd integer, or sufficiently large,
by the general theory
[
6
,
7
]
.

This paper is organized as follows.
The preliminaries and our main result are stated in Section 2.
Section 3 is devoted to the proof of the main result
by employing the classical iteration argument of point-wise estimates of the solution
which was firstly introduced by John
[
2
]
.
Finally we investigate the upper bound of the lifespan of the solution to (
1.3
) in Section 4.

2
Preliminaries and main result

Throughout this paper, we assume that the initial data

(f,g)\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}_{0}({\bf R})
satisfies

\mbox{\rm supp }f,\ \mbox{supp }g\subset\{x\in{\bf R}:|x|\leq R\},\quad R\geq 1.

(2.1)

Let
u
be a classical solution of (
1.1
) in the time interval
[0,T]
.
Then, due to the finiteness of the propagation speed of the wave,
the support condition of the initial data, (
2.1
), implies that

\mbox{supp}\ u(x,t)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}.

(2.2)

For example, see Appendix of John
[
3
]
for this fact.

It is well-known that
u
satisfies the following integral equation.

u(x,t)={\varepsilon}u^{0}(x,t)+L(|u_{t}|^{p}|u_{x}|^{q})(x,t),

(2.3)

where
u^{0}
is a solution of the free wave equation with the same initial data,

u^{0}(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\{f(x+t)+f(x-t)\}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{x-t}^{x+t}g(y)dy,

(2.4)

and a linear integral operator
L
for a function
U=U(x,t)
in Duhamel’s term is defined by

L(U)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}ds\int_{x-t+s}^{x+t-s}U(y,s)dy.

(2.5)

Then, one can apply the time-derivative to (
2.3
) to obtain

u_{t}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}(x,t)+L^{\prime}(|u_{t}|^{p}|u_{x}|^{q})(x,t)

(2.6)

and

u_{t}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)+g(x-t)\},

(2.7)

where
L^{\prime}
for a function
U=U(x,t)
is defined by

L^{\prime}(U)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\{U(x+t-s,s)+U(x-t+s,s)\}ds.

(2.8)

On the other hand, applying the space-derivative to (
2.3
),
we have

u_{x}(x,t)={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}(x,t)+\overline{L^{\prime}}(|u_{t}|^{p}|u_{x}|^{q})(x,t)

(2.9)

and

u_{x}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime}(x-t)+g(x+t)-g(x-t)\},

(2.10)

where
\overline{L^{\prime}}
for a function
U=U(x,t)
is defined by

\overline{L^{\prime}}(U)(x,t):=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\{U(x+t-s,s)-U(x-t+s,s)\}ds.

(2.11)

Therefore, both
u_{t}
and
u_{x}
are expressed by themselves.
Moreover, one more space-derivative to (
2.6
) yields that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+L^{\prime}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u_{x}|^{q}+q|u_{x}|^{q-2}u_{x}u_{xx}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\end{array}

(2.12)

and

u_{tx}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)-f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)+g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

(2.13)

Similarly, we have that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{tt}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)+|u_{t}(x,t)|^{p}|u_{x}(x,t)|^{q}\\
&\displaystyle+\overline{L^{\prime}}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u_{x}|^{q}+q|u_{x}|^{q-2}u_{x}u_{xx}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\end{array}

and

u_{tt}^{0}(x,t)=\frac{1}{2}\{f^{\prime\prime}(x+t)+f^{\prime\prime}(x-t)+g^{\prime}(x+t)-g^{\prime}(x-t)\}.

Therefore,
u_{tt}
is expressed by
u_{t},u_{x},u_{tx},u_{xx}
.
Finally we see that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{xx}(x,t)=&\displaystyle{\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)\\
&\displaystyle+\overline{L^{\prime}}(p|u_{t}|^{p-2}u_{t}u_{tx}|u_{x}|^{q}+q|u_{x}|^{q-2}u_{x}u_{xx}|u_{t}|^{p})(x,t)\end{array}

(2.14)

and

u_{xx}^{0}(x,t)=u^{0}_{tt}(x,t).

(2.15)

First, we note the following fact.

Proposition 2.1

Assume that
(f,g)\in C^{2}({\bf R})\times C^{1}({\bf R})
.
Let
(v,w)
be a
C^{1}
solution of a system of integral equations;

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}v={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}+L^{\prime}(|v|^{p}|w|^{q}),\\
w={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}+\overline{L^{\prime}}(|v|^{p}|w|^{q})\end{array}\right.\mbox{in}\ {\bf R}\times[0,T]

(2.16)

with some
T>0
.
Then,
u
defined by

u(x,t)={\varepsilon}u^{0}(x,t)+L(|v|^{p}|w|^{q})(x,t)

is a classical solution of (
1.1
) in
{\bf R}\times[0,T]
,
and
v\equiv u_{t},w\equiv u_{x}
in
{\bf R}\times[0,T]
hold.

Proof.
It is trivial that
v\equiv u_{t},w\equiv u_{x}
by differentiation with respect to
t
and
x
.
The rest part is easy along with the computations above in this section.

\Box

Our main result is the following theorem.

Theorem 2.1

Let
p>1
and
q>1
.
Assume (
2.1
).
Then, there exists a positive constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f,g,p,q,R)>0
such that
a classical solution
u\in C^{2}({\bf R}\times[0,T])
of (
1.1
) exists
as far as
T
satisfies

T\leq c{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q-1)}

(2.17)

where
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
, and
c
is a positive constant independent of
{\varepsilon}
.

Remark 2.1

Due to the definition of the lifespan,

\begin{array}[]{ll}T({\varepsilon}):=\sup\{T>0:&\mbox{There exists a classical solution of (\ref{IVP}) in $[0,T]$}\\
&\mbox{for arbitrarily fixed data $(f,g)$. \lx@text@rbrace},\end{array}

the result of Theorem
2.1
above gives us the lower bound of the lifespan
with a statement that there exists
a positive constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}={\varepsilon}_{0}(f,g,p,q,R)>0
such that

c{\varepsilon}^{-(p+q-1)}\leq T({\varepsilon}),

where
0<{\varepsilon}\leq{\varepsilon}_{0}
, and
c
is a positive constant independent of
{\varepsilon}
.

3
Proof of Theorem
2.1

The proof of our main result is based on the argument in Kitamura, Morisawa and Takamura
[
5
]
.
According to Proposition
2.1
,
we shall construct a
C^{1}
solution of (
2.16
).
Let
\{(v_{j},w_{j})\}_{j\in{\bf N}}
be a sequence of
\{C^{1}({\bf R}\times[0,T])\}^{2}
defined by

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}v_{j+1}={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0}+L^{\prime}(|v_{j}|^{p}|w_{j}|^{q}),&v_{1}={\varepsilon}u_{t}^{0},\\
w_{j+1}={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}+\overline{L^{\prime}}(|v_{j}|^{p}|w_{j}|^{q}),&w_{1}={\varepsilon}u_{x}^{0}.\end{array}\right.

(3.1)

Then, in view of (
2.12
) and (
2.14
),
\left((v_{j})_{x},(w_{j})_{x}\right)
has to satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}(v_{j+1})_{x}&={\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0}+L^{\prime}\left(p|v_{j}|^{p-2}v_{j}(v_{j})_{x}|w_{j}|^{q}\right)\\
&\quad+L^{\prime}\left(q|w_{j}|^{q-2}w_{j}(w_{j})_{x}|v_{j}|^{p}\right),\\
(v_{1})_{x}&={\varepsilon}u_{tx}^{0},\\
(w_{j+1})_{x}&={\varepsilon}u^{0}_{xx}+\overline{L^{\prime}}\left(p|v_{j}|^{p-2}v_{j}(v_{j})_{x}|w_{j}|^{q}\right)\\
&\quad+\overline{L^{\prime}}\left(q|w_{j}|^{q-2}w_{j}(w_{j})_{x}|v_{j}|^{p}\right),\\
(w_{1})_{x}&={\varepsilon}u_{xx}^{0},\end{array}\right.

(3.2)

so that the function space in which
\{(v_{j},w_{j})\}
converges is

\begin{array}[]{ll}X:=&\{(v,w)\in\{C^{1}({\bf R}\times[0,T])\}^{2}\ :\ \|(v,w)\|_{X}<\infty,\\
&\quad\mbox{supp}\ (v,w)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]\ :\ |x|\leq t+R\}\},\end{array}

which is equipped with a norm

\|(v,w)\|_{X}:=\|v\|+\|v_{x}\|+\|w\|+\|w_{x}\|,

where

\|v\|:=\sup_{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]}|v(x,t)|.

First we note that supp
(v_{j},w_{j})\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]\ :\ |x|\leq t+R\}
implies supp

(v_{j+1},w_{j+1})\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]\ :\ |x|\leq t+R\}
.
It is easy to check this fact by assumption on the initial data (
2.1
)
and the definitions of
L^{\prime},\overline{L^{\prime}}
in the previous section.

The following lemma is a priori estimate.

Proposition 3.1

Let
(v,w)\in\{C({\bf R}\times[0,T])\}^{2}
and supp
(v,w)\subset\{(x,t)\in{\bf R}\times[0,T]:|x|\leq t+R\}
. Then there exists a positive constant
C
independent of
T
and
{\varepsilon}
such that

\begin{array}[]{l}\|L^{\prime}(|w|^{p}|u|^{q})\|\leq C\|w\|^{p}\|u\|^{q}(T+R),\\
\|\overline{L^{\prime}}(|w|^{p}|u|^{q})\|\leq C\|w\|^{p}\|u\|^{q}(T+R).\end{array}

(3.3)

Proof.
The proof of the first inequality in (
3.3
) is exactly same as
the one of Proposition 3.1 with
a=-1
in Morisawa, Kitamura and Takamura
[
5
]
.
Because the total power in (
3.3
) is
(p+q)

which is equivalent to
p
in
[
5
]
.
The second inequality is readily follows from the trivial inequality,

|\overline{L^{\prime}}(U)|\leq|L^{\prime}(U)|

for a positive function
U=U(x,t)
.

\Box

Once we have a priori estimate (
3.3
) in Proposition
3.1
,
the proof of Theorem
2.1
is immediately established.
Because the boundedness of the sequence in
X
is the same
as Morisawa, Kitamura and Takamura
[
5
]
and
the condition by estimate of the convergence of the sequence in
X
is harmless
if one follows Kido, Sasaki, Takamatsu and Takamura
[
4
]

to handle the product nonlinear terms.
The proof of Theorem
2.1
is now completed.

\Box

4
Blow-up example including product terms

As promised in the introduction, we shall prove the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1

Let
f\in C_{0}^{2}({\bf R})
and
g\in C_{0}^{1}({\bf R})
satisfy

\pm f^{\prime}(x_{0})+g(x_{0})\neq 0

(4.1)

for some
x_{0}\in{\bf R}
.
If the equation in (
1.1
) is replaced with the one in (
1.3
),
then there exists a positive constant
C
such that
a classical solution cannot exists if
T
satisfies

T>C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}

(4.2)

for all
{\varepsilon}>0
.

Remark 4.1

(
4.2
) implies the lifespan estimate

T({\varepsilon})\leq C{\varepsilon}^{-(p-1)}.

When
p
is odd integer, or sufficiently large, this is known to be sharp by general theory
[
6
,
7
]
.

Remark 4.2

If the blow-up condition (
4.1
) is false, then we may have a global-in-time solution
with arbitrary
{\varepsilon}>0
.
In fact, it is easy to see that
u(x,t):={\varepsilon}f(x\mp t)
solves the equation (
1.3
)
for which the initial condition should be formed as

u(x,0)={\varepsilon}f(x),\ u_{t}(x,0)=\mp{\varepsilon}f^{\prime}(x),

so that it makes the assumption,

\pm f^{\prime}(x)+g(x)=0\quad\mbox{for all }x\in{\bf R}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

Let
u
be a classical solution of this problem.
It follows from (
2.6
) and (
2.9
) that

\begin{array}[]{ll}u_{t}(x,t)\pm u_{x}(x,t)&\displaystyle={\varepsilon}\{\pm f^{\prime}(x\pm t)+g(x\pm t)\}\\
&\displaystyle\quad+\int_{0}^{t}|u_{t}\pm u_{x}|^{p-1}(u_{t}\pm u_{x})(x\pm(t-s),s)ds.\end{array}

(4.3)

First, let us assume that

M_{\pm}:=\pm f^{\prime}(x_{0})+g(x_{0})>0

(4.4)

and fix

x\pm t=x_{0}.

Moreover, set

U_{\pm}(t):=u_{t}(x_{0}\mp t,t)\pm u_{x}(x_{0}\mp t,t)

Then, we can rewrite (
4.3
) as

U_{\pm}(t)=M_{\pm}{\varepsilon}+\int_{0}^{t}|U_{\pm}(s)|^{p-1}U_{\pm}(s)ds.

Hence the continuity of
U_{\pm}
and the positivity of the data
U_{\pm}(0)=M_{\pm}{\varepsilon}>0
yield that

U_{\pm}(t)>0\quad\mbox{for}\ t\geq 0.

Therefore
U_{\pm}
satisfied

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}U_{\pm}^{\prime}=U_{\pm}^{p}\\
U_{\pm}(0)=M_{\pm}{\varepsilon}\end{array}\right.

which is solvable as

U_{\pm}(t)=\{(M_{\pm}{\varepsilon})^{1-p}-(p-1)t\}^{-1/(p-1)}

and has an exact blow-up time

t_{0}:=\frac{(M_{\pm}{\varepsilon})^{1-p}}{p-1}>0.

(4.5)

When
M_{\pm}<0
instead of (
4.4
), then one has the same result as above by
substituting
u(x,t)
by
-u(x,t)
.
Therefore, Theorem
4.1
is now established with
C=|M_{\pm}|^{1-p}/(p-1)>0
.

\Box

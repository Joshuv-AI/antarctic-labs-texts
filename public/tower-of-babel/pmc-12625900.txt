Surgical team design and complement to assure optimal patient outcomes
Authors: Kimberly A Davis
Published: 2025

The provision of quality inpatient surgical care is dependent on several variables, including evidence-based practice, timely diagnosis and management of identified conditions, appropriate provider knowledge, skill and staffing, clear communication, and effective coordination of safe discharge. Dr Wilson and colleagues explored surgeon perception on appropriate patient ratios in acute care surgery staffing, providing expert opinion on current state using a mixed-methods survey.1 They report that up to 40 non-critical inpatients could safely be managed by a single surgeon, supported by a team of providers including residents and/or advanced care providers. This number decreased by 50% in the absence of a care team. Their qualitative analysis reveals that patient acuity, team experience, and competing demands modulated these recommendations.

There is limited data in the surgical literature about appropriate physician to patient ratios. Thus, data should be extrapolated from other specialties, many of which do not have the added responsibility of providing intraoperative surgical management. There is data in the emergency medicine literature identifying higher patient to provider ratios with degradation in the quality of care.2 3 Studies during the COVID-19 pandemic noted that patient to physician ratios above 15:1 were associated with poor outcomes.4 Finally, a study out of the Baylor Health System noted that a single hospitalist provider could safely care for 15 patients, whereas the supervision of a resident team decreased that number to 13 patients, and the addition of one or more Advanced Practice Providers increased the number to 20 patients per physician.5

It is counterintuitive that a surgeon, who also provides operative care, should be expected to manage complex patients at a ratio double that recommended in the literature for other hospital-based providers. As we continue to assess our systems of care in the field of acute care surgery, we must measure patient acuity, patient outcomes, and patient satisfaction to arrive at an understanding of optimal inpatient team design and complement. Expert opinion and current state cannot be equated with a thoughtful evaluation of all the drivers associated with quality care. As the complexity of inpatient surgical care continues to increase, we need to redesign our care teams to provide the safest care possible.

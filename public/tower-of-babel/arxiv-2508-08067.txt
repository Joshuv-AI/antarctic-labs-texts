The univariate multinode Shepard method for the Caputo fractional derivatives: from Approximation to the solution of Bagley-Torvik equation Journal: Fract. Calc. Appl. Anal.
Francesco Dell’Accio 1, Filomena Di Tommaso 1, Ilde Ferrara 1

Abstract
In this paper, we approximate the fractional derivative of a given function using the univariate multinode Shepard method through the Gauss-Jacobi quadrature formula. Subsequently, the proposed method is applied to the numerical solution of boundary value problems (BVPs) and initial value problems (IVPs), specifically addressing the Bagley-Torvik equations. Experimental results confirm the method’s effectiveness, particularly in accurately approximating the Bagley-Torvik equation for both BVPs and IVPs.

2.1
Multinode functions

Let

X=\left\{x_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{n}\right\}\subset I

\begin{array}[c]{cc}B_{\mu,k}(x)=\frac{\displaystyle\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left|{x-x_{k_{i}}}\right|^{-\mu}}{\displaystyle\sum\limits_{\ell=1}^{s}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{p}\left|{x-x_{\ell_{i}}}\right|^{-\mu}},\quad k=1,\dots,s,&x\in I,\end{array}

The multinode functions are non-negative and form a partition of unity, and instead of being cardinal, they satisfy the following properties

(i)

B_{\mu,k}\left(x_{j}\right)=0

\sum\limits_{k\in\mathcal{K}_{i}}B_{\mu,k}\left(x_{i}\right)=1

\mu>0

(iii)

B_{\mu,k}^{\left(m\right)}\left(x_{j}\right)=0

\mu>m\geq 1

2.3
The Multinode Shepard operator

Let
f:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function of class
C^{m}
in
I

m\geq 0

Moreover, we consider the interpolation polynomials

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\left[f\right](x)=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{s}B_{\mu,k}(x)P_{k}[f](x),\hskip 10.00002pt\mu>0,\,x\in I.

(2.19)

The multinode Shepard operator is an interpolation operator at the points of

In view of the following applications, it is convenient to rewrite

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\left[f\right](x)=\sum_{k=1}^{s}\sum_{i=1}^{p}B_{\mu,k}(x)l_{k,i}(x)f_{k_{i}},

where

are the fundamental Lagrange polynomials on the nodes of

l_{k,i}(x)=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}j=1\\
j\neq k_{i}\end{subarray}}^{p}\frac{x-x_{j}}{x_{k_{i}}-x_{j}},

satisfying the well-known Kronecker delta property

\displaystyle l_{k,i}(x_{k_{j}})=\delta_{ij}=\begin{cases}1,\qquad i=j\\
0,\qquad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.21)

Moreover, if we identify the subsets

as in (

), we can rewrite

as

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}\left[f\right](x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}}B_{\mu,j}(x)l_{j,i}(x)f_{i}.

(2.22)

After these rearrangements, it is easy to compute the successive derivatives of

\mathcal{M}_{\mu}^{(m)}\left[f\right](x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}g_{i}^{(m)}(x)f_{i}

(2.23)

where

g_{i}^{(m)}(x)=\sum_{j\in\mathcal{K}_{i}}\left(B_{\mu,j}(x)l_{j,i}(x)\right)^{(m)}.

(2.24)

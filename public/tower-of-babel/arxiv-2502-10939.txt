Model-assisted inference for dynamic causal effects in staggered rollout cluster randomized experiments
Xinyuan Chen, Fan Li

Abstract
Staggered rollout cluster randomized experiments (SR-CREs) involve sequential treatment adoption across clusters, requiring analysis methods that address a general class of dynamic causal effects, anticipation, and non-ignorable cluster-period sizes. Without imposing any outcome modeling assumptions, we study regression estimators using individual data, cluster-period averages, and scaled cluster-period totals, with and without covariate adjustment from a design-based perspective. We establish consistency and asymptotic normality of each estimator under a randomization-based framework and prove that the associated variance estimators are asymptotically conservative in the Löwner ordering. Furthermore, we conduct a unified efficiency comparison of the estimators and provide recommendations. We highlight the efficiency advantage of using estimators based on scaled cluster-period totals with covariate adjustment over their counterparts using individual-level data and cluster-period averages. Our results rigorously justify linear regression estimators as model-assisted methods to address an entire class of dynamic causal effects in SR-CREs.



Keywords:
Causal inference; cluster-robust variance estimator; covariate adjustment; randomized experiments; staggered rollout

1
Introduction

We study a class of randomized experiments in which clusters are the unit of randomization, with the treatment rolled out over multiple periods. At baseline, each cluster is randomized to a specific treatment adoption time, and clusters that have already begun treatment will continue to receive treatment until the end of the study. We refer to this design as the staggered rollout cluster-randomized experiment (SR-CRE), which is attractive in settings where simultaneous randomization in a single period is not logistically feasible. SR-CREs have also been referred to as cluster-randomized difference-in-differences (DID) in the social sciences
(
Athey and Imbens, 2022
)
and stepped-wedge cluster-randomized trials in medicine
(
Hussey and Hughes, 2007
)
, and have become increasingly common.

Previous staggered rollout design literature has primarily focused on single-level data, e.g., observational DID for panel data, where the treatment assignment and outcome measurements occur at the same level
(
Callaway and Sant’Anna, 2021
;
Sun and Abraham, 2021
)
, and staggered rollout individual randomized experiments
(
Athey and Imbens, 2022
;
Bojinov et al., 2021
;
Roth and Sant’Anna, 2023
, SR-IREs,)
. Differentiating the level of assignment from the level of outcome measurements, there is an emerging literature on design-based inference for CREs without staggered rollout. For example,
Schochet et al. (2022)
proposed design-based estimators for blocked CREs.
Su and Ding (2021)
developed a unified theory for design-based model-assisted inference in CREs under parallel-arm assignments. Differentiating the level of treatment assignment from the level of outcome measurements under staggered rollout,
Schochet (2022)
studied the statistical power of DID and comparative interrupted time series estimators for panel data from observational studies.
Chen and Li (2024)
proposed a class of weighted average treatment effect estimands and studied the design-based properties of analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) estimators for stepped wedge CREs. However, these developments assumed away anticipation
(
Malani and Reif, 2015
, a psychological phenomenon where clusters bring the expected future into the present)
and dynamic causal effects
(
Sun and Abraham, 2021
, treatment effects evolve over length of exposure)
, which cannot be easily ruled out
a priori
in practice.

Focusing on the central question of treatment effect evaluation, this article addresses regression estimators for SR-CREs from a design-based perspective, where the potential outcomes and covariates are assumed fixed, and the randomness arises solely from the randomized treatment adoption times. We propose a class of weighted average treatment effect estimands that account for the unique design features of an SR-CRE. Specifically, we largely relax the no-anticipation assumption
(
Athey and Imbens, 2022
;
Chen and Li, 2024
;
Roth and Sant’Anna, 2023
)
and allow for dynamic causal effects
(
Sun and Abraham, 2021
;
Wang et al., 2024
)
, such that the treatment adoption time is an intrinsic component of the estimands. Similar to
Su and Ding (2021)
and
Chen and Li (2024)
, we also explicitly address the cluster-period size as an integral part of the estimands and generalize the dynamic DID estimands
(
Sun and Abraham, 2021
;
Callaway and Sant’Anna, 2021
)
to address differential levels of assignment and data measurement as well as non-ignorable cluster-period sizes. Based on the proposed estimands, we can then define the dynamic causal effects, which compare the summary of potential outcomes between any pair of treatment adoption times in specific calendar periods to address different scientific questions. To our knowledge, this is the first effort to simultaneously address anticipation, dynamic causal effects, and non-ignorable cluster-period sizes in estimations and definitions for SR-CREs.

To target the class of estimands, we propose regression estimators using individual-level data, cluster-period averages, and scaled cluster-period totals, with or without adjusting for covariates. Each proposed estimator is model-assisted as it is consistent even if the working regression model is misspecified. We establish the asymptotic properties of each regression estimator under the asymptotic regime in
Middleton and Aronow (2015)
and
Chen and Li (2024)
, where, with a constant number of periods, the number of clusters approaches infinity while the cluster-period sizes are fixed. Each regression estimator is shown to be consistent and asymptotically normal, provided that the cluster weights in each period are reasonably balanced and there are no clusters with dominating weights. We estimate the uncertainty of each regression estimator based on individual-level data via the Liang-Zeger cluster-robust (CR) variance estimator
(
Liang and Zeger, 1986
)
, and the uncertainty of those based on cluster-period averages and scaled cluster-period totals via the Huber-White heteroskedasticity-consistent (HC) variance estimator
(
Huber, 1967
;
White, 1980
)
. We prove that both variance estimators are conservative. The conservativeness of the HC variance estimators also offers a rigorous justification for a similar statement in
Roth and Sant’Anna (2023)
, because regressions based on cluster-period averages and scaled cluster-period totals implicitly treat the cluster-period-level outcomes as though they were derived from an SR-IRE. Finally, we provide a unified efficiency comparison across all regression estimators under SR-CREs and make recommendations.

The rest of this article is structured as follows. Section

3
proposes regression estimators and establishes their design-based properties, as well as the conservativeness of the variance estimators. Section
4
compares the estimators theoretically. Section
5
and
6
report results from simulation studies and an illustrative data example. Section
7
concludes.

Staggered rollout cluster randomized experiments

I

2.1
Identification assumptions

We state minimally sufficient identification assumptions below.

Assumption 1
(Cluster-level SUTVA)

Let
Y_{ijk}(A_{i},\bm{A}_{-i})
denote the potential outcome for individual
k=1,\ldots,N_{ij}
from cluster
i
in period
j
given all adoption times, where
\bm{A}_{-i}
denote the vector of adoption times for all other clusters, then (i)
Y_{ijk}(A_{i},\bm{A}_{-i})=Y_{ijk}(A_{i},\bm{A}_{-i}^{*})
, for all
\bm{A}_{-i}\neq\bm{A}_{-i}^{*}
; and (ii)
Y_{ijk}(A_{i},\bm{A}_{-i})=Y_{ijk}(A_{i}^{*},\bm{A}_{-i}^{*})
if
A_{i}=A_{i}^{*}
.

As a common condition, Assumption
1
states that there is a single version of the treatment, and individual-level potential outcomes from cluster
i
in period
j
only depend on the assignment of cluster
i
. It rules out between-cluster interference and simplifies the potential outcome as
Y_{ijk}(A_{i})
. Under Assumption
1
, the observed individual outcome is
Y_{ijk}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}G_{i}(a)Y_{ijk}(a)
, where
G_{i}(a)=\mathbbm{1}(A_{i}=a)
. We use
\bm{X}_{ijk}
and
\bm{C}_{ij}
to denote the
p_{x}
- and
p_{c}
-dimensional row vectors of individual and cluster-level covariates in period
j
, respectively. The cluster-level covariates
\bm{C}_{ij}
possibly include components of cluster-level summaries. We assume both
\bm{X}_{ijk}
and
\bm{C}_{ij}
are exogenous; in the case of a cohort study, one can remove the subscript
j
such that
\bm{X}_{ik}
and
\bm{C}_{i}
only involve time-invariant information collected before individual recruitment.

Assumption 2
(Randomized staggered rollout)

Write
\mathcal{Y}
,
\mathcal{X}
, and
\mathcal{C}
as the collections of all potential outcomes and covariates across individuals, clusters, and periods, then

\mathbb{P}(\bm{A}=\bm{a}|\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{X},\mathcal{C})={I\choose I(1),\ldots,I(J),I(\infty)}^{-1},

where
\bm{A}=(A_{1},\ldots,A_{I})^{\top}
and
\bm{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{I})^{\top}
with
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\mathbbm{1}(A_{i}=a)=I(a)
.

This assumption states that the treatment adoption times are random. It is a common assumption in the staggered rollout design literature, e.g.,
Athey and Imbens (2022, Assumption 1)
,
Roth and Sant’Anna (2023, Assumption 1)
, and
Chen and Li (2024, Assumption 3)
, and is typically held by design.

2.2
Dynamic causal effect estimands

We pursue the finite-population framework. Under this framework, values of the potential outcomes and covariates are assumed to be fixed, and the source of randomness is solely from the randomization of the treatment adoption time assignments. Under Assumption
1
, we define the dynamic weighted average treatment effect (DWATE) estimand,

\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})=\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a^{\prime}),

(1)

where
\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
is the weighted average potential outcomes across individuals in period
j
, had the adoption time been set to
a
. The DWATE is a contrast in weighted average potential outcomes between different treatment adoption times in period
j=1,\ldots,J
.

Let
w_{ijk}
denote the non-negative pre-specified individual weight for individual
k
from cluster
i
in period
j
, which satisfies the constraint that
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}w_{ijk}>0
for all
j
, ensuring (
1
) is properly defined. We define
w_{ij\cdot}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}w_{ijk}
as the cluster weight for cluster
i
in period
j
,
w_{\cdot j\cdot}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}w_{ij\cdot}
as the total weight for period
j
,
\pi_{ijk}=w_{ijk}/w_{\cdot j\cdot}
as the normalized individual weight, and
\pi_{ij\cdot}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}=w_{ij\cdot}/w_{\cdot j\cdot}
as the normalized cluster weight. The DWATE in (
1
) can thus be re-expressed using individual-level data as

\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}\{Y_{ijk}(a)-Y_{ijk}(a^{\prime})\},

(2)

with
\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}Y_{ijk}(a)
; and using the cluster-period averages as

\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\pi_{ij\cdot}\{\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})\},

(3)

where
\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}w_{ijk}Y_{ijk}(a)/w_{ij\cdot}
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
; and further using the scaled cluster-period totals,

\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})=I^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})\},

(4)

where
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)=I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Different estimators for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
using individual-level data, cluster-period averages, and scaled cluster-period totals can then be motivated by the estimand re-expressions in (
2
) - (
4
), and will be addressed later. Finally, although one can specify arbitrary individual weights
w_{ijk}
, the two most interpretable choices have been discussed in prior work
(
Kahan et al., 2023
;
Chen and Li, 2024
)
. These include (i) setting
w_{ijk}=1
(
\pi_{ijk}=(\sum_{j=1}^{J}N_{ij})^{-1}
) to assign equal weight to each individual such that
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
is an individual average, and (ii) setting
w_{ijk}=N_{ij}^{-1}
(
\pi_{ijk}=(IN_{ij})^{-1}
) to assign equal weight to each cluster during each period such that
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
becomes a cluster average.

3
Regression-based causal effects estimators

3.1
Preliminaries

We present preliminaries to provide the context in which the asymptotic properties of the estimators for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
will be discussed. We adopt the asymptotic regime in
Middleton and Aronow (2015)
,
Li and Ding (2017)
, and
Chen and Li (2024)
, where an increasing sequence of finite populations is considered with the total number of clusters,
I\rightarrow\infty
, while the number of rollout periods,
J
, remains fixed. In addition, we assume that the cluster size
N_{ij}
remains fixed as
I
grows.

Let
\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a)=(\xi_{i1\cdot}(a),\ldots,\xi_{iJ\cdot}(a))^{\top}
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and
i=1,\ldots,I
denote general cluster-level potential outcomes with zero averages over clusters. Define the individual centered potential outcome
\epsilon_{ijk}(a)=Y_{ijk}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
, and then examples of
\xi_{ij\cdot}(a)
include the cluster-period average centered potential outcome,
\overline{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)=\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
, and the scaled cluster-period total,
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)=\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
. We then define the following matrices to streamline the exposition of the theoretical properties of the estimators, where

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\{\frac{\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a)\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a)^{\top}}{I(a)}+\frac{\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})^{\top}}{I(a^{\prime})}\right\}

and
\bm{V}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})=\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})-I^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\{\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a)-\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})\right\}\left\{\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a)-\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})\right\}^{\top}
. For two treatment adoption times
a
and
a^{\prime}
,
\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
and
\bm{V}(\xi_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
are covariance matrices of the difference in means of
\{\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a),\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})\}_{i=1}^{I}
multiplied by
(I-1)
in completely randomized experiments with and without the constant treatment effect assumption. In a more granular fashion, examining the
(j,j)
entry of
\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
and
\bm{V}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
gives us

\displaystyle\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})_{(j,j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\{\frac{\xi_{ij\cdot}(a)^{2}}{I(a)}+\frac{\xi_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})^{2}}{I(a^{\prime})}\right\}

and
\bm{V}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})_{(j,j)}=\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{\xi}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})_{(j,j)}-I^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{I}\left\{\xi_{ij\cdot}(a)-\xi_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})\right\}^{2}
, which are variances of the difference in means of
\{\xi_{ij\cdot}(a),\xi_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})\}_{i=1}^{I}
multiplied by
(I-1)
in completely randomized experiments with and without the constant treatment effect assumption.

3.2
Estimators using individual-level data

3.2.1
Without covariates

We can estimate
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
for
j=1,\ldots,J
and assuming
a,a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
and
a<a^{\prime}
using individual-level data, motivated by the expression in (
2
). Consider the following working regression model,

Y_{ijk}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\beta_{j}(a)G_{i}(a)+e_{ijk},

(6)

where
e_{ijk}
is the individual-level random noise assumed to have a mean-zero distribution and is independent across individuals. We can obtain an estimator for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
via the weighted least squares (WLS) fit of (
6
) with individual weight
\pi_{ijk}
, in the form of
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})=\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)-\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a^{\prime})
. Here,
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)=\overline{y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}G_{i}(a)\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}/\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
and
\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}G_{i}(a)\pi_{ij\cdot}
. The estimator
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
is equivalent to the difference between weighted averages of observed outcomes in clusters randomized to treatment adoption times
a
and
a^{\prime}
, and thus can also be labeled as the difference-in-means estimator. When
|\mathcal{A}|=2
, this estimator reduces to the regression estimators without adjusting for covariates under parallel-arm CREs
(
Schochet et al., 2022
;
Su and Ding, 2021
)
. We use the subscript ‘
\mathrm{I}
’ to clarify that the estimator is obtained using individual-level data.

Let
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
denote the covariance matrix of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})=(\widehat{\tau}_{1,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime}),\ldots,\widehat{\tau}_{J,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime}))^{\top}

\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
, where
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is the

\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
and
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

Liang and Zeger, 1986

, denoted by
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

6

where

is the
N_{i\cdot}
-dimensional vector of residuals from the WLS fit of (

), with

,
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}
is of dimensions
N_{i\cdot}\times J(J+1)
with
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}=(\bm{D}_{i1,\mathrm{I}}^{\top}\cdots\bm{D}_{iJ,\mathrm{I}}^{\top})^{\top}
and the
k
-th row of
\bm{D}_{ij,\mathrm{I}}
being
\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top}
, and the
j
-th diagonal block of
\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}
is
\bm{\Pi}_{ij,\mathrm{I}}=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{ij1},\ldots,\pi_{ijN_{ij}})
. Here,
\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})=(G_{i}(1),\ldots,G_{i}(J),G_{i}(\infty))^{\top}
,
\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}=(\mathbbm{1}_{ijk,1}\ldots,\mathbbm{1}_{ijk,J})^{\top}
with
\mathbbm{1}_{ijk,j^{\prime}}=1
if
j=j^{\prime}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{ijk,j^{\prime}}=0
otherwise for
j^{\prime}=1,\ldots,J
, and ‘
\otimes

\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top}=(G_{i}(1)\mathbbm{1}_{ijk}^{\top},\ldots,G_{i}(J)\mathbbm{1}_{ijk}^{\top},\allowbreak G_{i}(\infty)\mathbbm{1}_{ijk}^{\top})
. We can write
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})=\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}
, where
\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}=(\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}(1)^{\top},\ldots,\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}(J)^{\top},\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}(\infty)^{\top})^{\top}
, with
\widehat{\bm{\beta}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a)=(\widehat{\beta}_{1,\mathrm{I}}(a),\ldots,\widehat{\beta}_{J,\mathrm{I}}(a))^{\top}
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and
\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})
is a
J\times J(J+1)
-dimensional known coefficient matrix, e.g.,

\displaystyle\bm{Q}(1,2)=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{I}_{J\times J}&-\bm{I}_{J\times J}&\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\smash{\overbrace{\phantom{\begin{matrix}\bm{0}_{J\times J}&\cdots&\bm{0}_{J\times J}\end{matrix}}}^{\text{$J-1$}}}$}\bm{0}_{J\times J}&\cdots&\bm{0}_{J\times J}\end{pmatrix}

and

\displaystyle\bm{Q}(2,3)=\begin{pmatrix}\bm{0}_{J\times J}&\bm{I}_{J\times J}&-\bm{I}_{J\times J}&\makebox[0.0pt][l]{$\smash{\overbrace{\phantom{\begin{matrix}\bm{0}_{J\times J}&\cdots&\bm{0}_{J\times J}\end{matrix}}}^{\text{$J-2$}}}$}\bm{0}_{J\times J}&\cdots&\bm{0}_{J\times J}\end{pmatrix}.

Then, the CR variance estimator for
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I}}\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})^{\top}
, and the CR variance estimator for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}_{(j,j)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{G_{i}(a)}{\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}\widehat{e}_{ijk,\mathrm{I}}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{G_{i}(a^{\prime})}{\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}\widehat{e}_{ijk,\mathrm{I}}\right)^{2}.

The following result summarizes the theoretical properties of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
.

Theorem 1

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, and regularity conditions (C1) and (C2) in Section S2 of the Online Supplement, for
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})=\bm{\tau}(a,a^{\prime})+\bm{o}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
; if we further assume
\max_{i}\pi_{ij\cdot}=o(I^{-2/3})
for
j=1,\ldots,J
and
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})\nrightarrow\bm{0}_{J\times J}
, then
I^{1/2}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})-\bm{\tau}(a,a^{\prime})\}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{J},\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime}))
, where
\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot}=(\widetilde{\epsilon}_{i1\cdot}(a),\ldots,\widetilde{\epsilon}_{iJ\cdot}(a))^{\top}
, and

.

Here,

means that
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
converges to a limit not equal
\bm{0}_{J\times J}

establishes the consistency and asymptotic normality of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
. The regularity condition on the order of
\pi_{ij\cdot}
is slightly stronger for establishing the asymptotic normality. Theorem

also shows that the CR variance estimator
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is conservative, in the Löwner ordering, for the true covariance matrix because of the difference between
\bm{V}_{c}(\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
and
\bm{V}(\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})
is non-negative definite, and is inestimable in general and only equals a zero matrix when
\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{\epsilon}}_{i\cdot}(a^{\prime})=0
or
\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a^{\prime})=\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
for all
i
.

3.2.2
With covariates

Covariate adjustment can be incorporated into (
6
) following
Lin (2013)
to potentially improve estimation efficiency, leading to the fully-interacted regression model:

Y_{ijk}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\beta_{j}(a)G_{i}(a)+\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}G_{i}(a)\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j}(a)+e_{ijk},

(7)

where the centered individual-level covariate vector
\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c}=\bm{X}_{ijk}-\overline{\bm{X}}_{\cdot j\cdot}
with
\overline{\bm{X}}_{\cdot j\cdot}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}
and
\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}\bm{X}_{ijk}\allowbreak/\pi_{ij\cdot}
, and
\bm{\gamma}_{j}(a)
is the adoption-time-specific parameter vector. Similar to
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
, with individual weight
\pi_{ijk}
, we can obtain WLS estimates
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a)
and
\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
via fitting (
7
). A covariate-adjusted estimator for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
can be obtained in the form of
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})=\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a)-\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a^{\prime})
, with
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a)=\overline{y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-\overline{\bm{x}}_{\cdot j\cdot}^{c}(a)\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
where
\overline{\bm{x}}_{\cdot j\cdot}^{c}(a)=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\pi_{ij\cdot}G_{i}(a)\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}/\allowbreak\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
and
\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}=\sum_{k=1}^{N_{ij}}\pi_{ijk}\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c}/\pi_{ij\cdot}
. The covariate-adjusted estimator
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
can be viewed as a multi-arm clustered extension of the regression estimator using a covariate-treatment indicator interaction model, as presented in
Lin (2013)
.

To obtain the CR variance estimator
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}\right)^{-1},

where
\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
is the vector of residuals from the WLS fit of (
7
),
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
is of dimensions
N_{i\cdot}\times J(1+p_{x})(J+1)
with
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}=((\bm{D}_{i1,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top}\cdots(\bm{D}_{iJ,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})^{\top})^{\top}
and the
k
-th row of
\bm{D}_{ij,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
being
(\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top},\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes(\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top}\otimes\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c}))
. The CR variance estimator for
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I},[1\rightarrow J(J+1),1\rightarrow J(J+1)]}^{\mathrm{adj}}\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})^{\top}
, where
\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I},[1\rightarrow J(J+1),1\rightarrow J(J+1)]}^{\mathrm{adj}}
is the submatrix consisting of the first
J(J+1)
columns and
J(J+1)
rows of
\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
. Let
\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
denote the WLS coefficient vectors obtained using individual-level data if the full set of potential outcomes is available. That is, regress
Y_{ijk}(a)
on
(1,\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c})
with weight
\pi_{ijk}
assuming
Y_{ijk}(a)
is available for each individual
k
from cluster
i
in period
j
. We further define the scaled cluster-period totals for non-centered and centered covariates as
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}=I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}
and
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}=I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}
, respectively. The theoretical properties of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
are given in Theorem
2
.

Theorem 2

Define
r_{ij\cdot}(a)=\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
and
\bm{r}_{i\cdot}(a)=(r_{i1\cdot}(a),\ldots,r_{iJ\cdot}(a))^{\top}
. Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, and regularity conditions (C1) - (C3) in Section S2 of the Online Supplement, for
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})=\bm{\tau}(a,a^{\prime})+\bm{o}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
; if further
\max_{i}\pi_{ij\cdot}=o(I^{-2/3})
for

and
\bm{V}(\bm{r}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})\nrightarrow\bm{0}_{J\times J}
, then
I^{1/2}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})-\bm{\tau}(a,a^{\prime})\}\xrightarrow{d}\mathcal{N}(\bm{0}_{J},\bm{V}(\bm{r}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime}))
, and
I\times\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\bm{V}_{c}(\bm{r}_{i\cdot})(a,a^{\prime})+\bm{o}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
.

Theorem
2
establishes the consistency and asymptotic normality of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
, and the conservativeness of the CR variance estimator

, in the Löwner ordering. For the marginal variance
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, the residual

in Theorem

is of the form
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}\bm{\gamma}
, which suggests that the optimal choice of

should be the coefficient of
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}
in the ordinary least squares (OLS) fit of
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}
based on the scaled cluster-period totals, rather than
\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
using individual-level data. Therefore, it is possible that
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
is less efficient than
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
, for which, we give a numeric example in Section S7.1 of the Online Supplement. In the special case where
w_{ijk}=N_{ij}^{-1}
, i.e.,
\pi_{ijk}=(IN_{ij})^{-1}
, and
\bm{X}_{ijk}
does not vary with
k
,
\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
equals the coefficient of
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}
in the OLS fit of
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}
, and therefore
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
has a smaller asymptotic variance than
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
.

Alternatively, we can consider the following analysis of covariance (ANCOVA) regression model adapted from
Schochet et al. (2022)
for a blocked CRE:

Y_{ijk}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\beta_{j}(a)G_{i}(a)+\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j}+e_{ijk},

(8)

with a shared covariate vector
\bm{\gamma}_{j}
across different treatment adoption times. A regression estimator for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
can be obtained via the WLS fit of (
8
) with individual weight
\pi_{ijk}
, which is in the form of
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})=\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a)-\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a^{\prime})
, with
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a)=\overline{y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-\overline{\bm{x}}_{\cdot j\cdot}^{c}(a)\widehat{\bm{\gamma}}_{j,\mathrm{I}}
. To obtain
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, we have

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}\right)

\displaystyle\qquad\times\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}(\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{I}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}\right)^{-1},

where
\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}
is the vector of residuals from the WLS fit of (
8
),
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}
is of dimensions
N_{i\cdot}\times J(J+1+p_{x})
with
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}=((\bm{D}_{i1,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top}\cdots(\bm{D}_{iJ,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}})^{\top})^{\top}
and the
k
-th row of
\bm{D}_{ij,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}
being
(\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top},\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}^{\top}\otimes\bm{X}_{ijk}^{c})
. The CR variance estimator for
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})\bm{E}_{\mathrm{I},[1\rightarrow J(J+1),1\rightarrow J(J+1)]}^{\mathrm{ancova}}\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})^{\top}
. Define
q(a)=I(a)/I
as
I\rightarrow\infty
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
. The theoretical properties of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
are given in the following Corollary
1
.

Corollary 1

Define
r_{ij\cdot}(a)=\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}q(a)\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)
and
\bm{r}_{i\cdot}(a)=(r_{i1\cdot}(a),\allowbreak\ldots,r_{iJ\cdot}(a))^{\top}
. Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, and regularity conditions (C1) - (C3) in Section S2 of the Online Supplement, for
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,

; if further
\max_{i}\pi_{ij\cdot}=o(I^{-2/3})

and

, then

presents the consistency and asymptotic normality of

\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{CR}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{ancova}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

, each scalar treatment effect estimator

can be less efficient than

because it is suboptimal even in simpler cases
(
Lin, 2013
)
, unless it is expected that there is little treatment heterogeneity by

.

3.3
Estimators using cluster-level data

3.3.1
Using cluster-period averages

Motivated by the expression in (
3
),
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
can be estimated using cluster-period average
\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}
and cluster weight
\pi_{ij\cdot}
, and the setting can be viewed as an SR-IRE with
\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}
as the observed outcome
(
Roth and Sant’Anna, 2023
)
. In this case, we consider the cluster-period mean regression model

\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\beta_{j}(a)G_{i}(a)+e_{ij},

(9)

where
e_{ij}
is the cluster-level random noise assumed to have a mean-zero distribution and is independent across clusters. We can obtain an estimator for
\tau_{j}(a,a^{\prime})
by fitting (
9
) with cluster weight
\pi_{ij\cdot}
, which is in the form of
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})=\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a)-\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a^{\prime})
, with
\widehat{\beta}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a)=\overline{y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
. This estimator is equivalent to
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
. Here, we use the subscript ‘
\mathrm{A}
’ to clarify that the estimator is obtained using cluster-period averages.

The covariance matrix
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
can be estimated by obtaining the HC variance estimator for the vector of estimated regression coefficients,

\displaystyle\bm{E}_{\mathrm{A}}=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\right)^{-1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{A}}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{I}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}^{\top}\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}\right)^{-1},

where
\widehat{\bm{e}}_{i,\mathrm{A}}
is the
J
-dimensional residual vector from the WLS fit of (
9
),
\bm{D}_{i,\mathrm{A}}
is of dimensions
J\times J(J+1)
with the
j
-th row being
\bm{G}_{i}(\mathcal{A})^{\top}\otimes\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ij}^{\top}
, and
\bm{\Pi}_{i,\mathrm{A}}=\mathrm{diag}(\pi_{i1\cdot},\ldots,\pi_{iJ\cdot})
. Similar to
\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ijk}
, here,
\boldsymbol{\mathbbm{1}}_{ij}=(\mathbbm{1}_{ij,1}\ldots,\mathbbm{1}_{ij,J})^{\top}
with
\mathbbm{1}_{ij,j^{\prime}}=1
if
j=j^{\prime}
and
\mathbbm{1}_{ij,j^{\prime}}=0
otherwise for
j^{\prime}=1,\ldots,J
. Then, the HC variance estimator for
\mathbf{Cov}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{HC}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})\bm{E}_{\mathrm{A}}\bm{Q}(a,a^{\prime})^{\top}
, and the HC variance estimator for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}

\displaystyle=\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{HC}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}_{(j,j)}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{G_{i}(a)}{\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)^{2}}\left(\pi_{ij\cdot}\widehat{e}_{ij}\right)^{2}+\sum_{i=1}^{I}\frac{G_{i}(a^{\prime})}{\pi_{\cdot j\cdot}(a^{\prime})^{2}}\left(\pi_{ij\cdot}\widehat{e}_{ij}\right)^{2}.

Importantly, the HC variance estimator is a special case of the CR variance estimator with cluster size
N_{ij}=1
for all
i=1,\ldots,I
and
j=1,\ldots,J
. The consistency and asymptotic normality of
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})
as well as the conservativeness of
\widehat{\mathbf{Cov}}_{\mathrm{HC}}\{\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, in the Löwner ordering, are given in Theorem
3
.

Theorem 3

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, and regularity conditions (C1) and (C4) in Section S2 of the Online Supplement, for
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,
\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})=\bm{\tau}_{j}(a,a^{\prime})+\bm{o}_{\mathbb{P}}(1)
; if further
\max_{i}\pi_{ij\cdot}=o(I^{-2/3})
for
j=1,\ldots,J
and

4.1
Estimators using individual-level data and cluster-period averages

We first present additional results on the connections between estimators using individual-level data and cluster-period averages, summarizing them in Proposition
1
. Here, we explicitly write specific types of covariates in brackets after an estimator to emphasize the covariates used for baseline adjustment. All covariates are centered when included in the regression model. For example,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\bm{X}_{ijk}]
denotes the regression estimator using individual-level data adjusted for
\bm{X}_{ijk}
as covariates in the working model.

Proposition 1

For
j=1,\ldots,J
, assuming
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, we have
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})=\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})
,
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}=\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, and
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\bm{C}_{ij}]=\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})
,
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\bm{C}_{ij}]\}=\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
.

Without covariate adjustment,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})
are equivalent, and so are their corresponding variance estimators
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
and
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
. This double equivalence extends to covariate-adjusted estimators if only cluster-level covariates
\bm{C}_{ij}
are used for the estimator using individual-level data. This is consistent with the results given in
Su and Ding (2021, Proposition 2)
for parallel-arm CREs.

4.2
Estimators using individual-level data and scaled cluster-period totals

We compare
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
, where by construction,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
is the Horvitz-Thompson estimator and is unbiased, while
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
is the Hajék estimator and is biased. Nevertheless,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
is consistent since its bias diminishes to zero as
I\rightarrow\infty
. Similar to
Su and Ding (2021, §3.4)
for the parallel-arm CREs,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
has a smaller asymptotic variance than
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
because the residual
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
in
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
adjusts for normalized cluster-period weight
\pi_{ij\cdot}
, while the residual
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)
in
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
does not. More importantly,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
is not invariant to the location shift of the potential outcomes. That is, if we transform the potential outcomes to
m+Y_{ijk}(a)
for
a\in\mathcal{A}
and some constant
m
, then
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
will change but
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
will not. Therefore, we do not recommend using
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
without covariates in general. The shortcomings of
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
can be fixed by leveraging covariate adjustment with cluster-level information
\bm{C}_{ij}
, for which a simple case is given by choosing a simple scalar covariate
C_{ij}=\pi_{ij\cdot}
. Proposition
2
summarizes the results comparing asymptotic variances of these estimators.

Proposition 2

For
j=1,\ldots,J
, assuming
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, the asymptotic variances satisfy
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, and
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\allowbreak\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}.
These inequalities hold asymptotically if the standard errors are replaced by the estimated ones.

The inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}
is established following the techniques to prove the result that covariate adjustment will always improve the asymptotic variance under the independent data setting
(
Lin, 2013
)
. The inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
can be obtained by first establishing the following expression
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}
. Then, by the Frisch-Waugh-Lovell Theorem
(
Frisch and Waugh, 1933
)
, the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}
in Theorem
6
is the residual sum of squares from the OLS fit of
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1}
, which by definition is smaller than
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}
. Therefore, adjusting for
\pi_{ij\cdot}
in the regression using scaled cluster-period totals improves the simple difference-in-means. Additionally, adjusting for scaled cluster-period totals of other covariates leads to greater asymptotic efficiency improvement. The same argument can be applied to obtaining the inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
. Specifically, the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)\}^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
in Theorem
2
can be expressed as
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)\}^{2}
, which is greater than the residual sum of squares from the OLS fit of
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}^{c})
, that is, the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}]\}
in Theorem
6
. Therefore, adjusting for
(\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot})
in the regression using scaled cluster-period totals improves the regression adjustment of
\bm{X}_{ijk}
using the individual-level data, regarding asymptotic efficiency.

4.3
Estimators using cluster-period averages and scaled cluster-period totals

We compare
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})
and
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}(a,a^{\prime})
. Similar to
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a,a^{\prime})
,
\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})
is the Hajék estimator and is consistent with its bias diminishing to zero as
I\rightarrow\infty
. The following Proposition
3
summarizes the results comparing asymptotic variances of estimators using cluster-period averages and scaled cluster-period totals.

Proposition 3

For
j=1,\ldots,J
, assuming
a<a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
, the asymptotic variances satisfy

\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, and
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\allowbreak\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
. These inequalities hold asymptotically if the standard errors are replaced by the estimated ones.

Proposition
3
is obtained via similar arguments centered around the sum of squares and the Frisch-Waugh-Lovell Theorem used in deriving Proposition
2
. The inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}
follows the result that regression adjustment will always improve the asymptotic variance
(
Lin, 2013
)
. The inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}\allowbreak\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
is obtained from the fact that
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-I\pi_{ij\cdot}\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}
, and
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot}]\}
in Theorem
6
is the residual sum of squares from the OLS fit of
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1}
, which by definition is smaller than
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)\}^{2}
. Therefore, adjusting for
\pi_{ij\cdot}
improves the efficiency of simple difference-in-means, and adjusting for scaled cluster-period totals of additional covariates leads to a greater improvement in efficiency. The same argument can be applied to obtain the inequality
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}]\}\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
, where the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{\epsilon}_{ij\cdot}(a)-I\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}^{c}\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{I}}(a)\}^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})\}
in Theorem
4
can be expressed as
\sum_{i=1}^{I}\{\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1})I\overline{Y}_{\cdot j\cdot}(a)-\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}^{c}I\bm{\gamma}_{j,\mathrm{A}}(a)\}^{2}
, which is greater than the residual sum of squares from the OLS fit of
\widetilde{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)-\overline{Y}_{ij\cdot}(a)
on
(\pi_{ij\cdot}-I^{-1},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}^{c})
, i.e., the quantity
\sum_{i=1}^{I}r_{ij}(a)^{2}
for
\mathrm{se}^{2}\{\widehat{\tau}_{j,\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}(a,a^{\prime})[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}]\}
in Theorem
6
. Therefore, adjusting for
(\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij})
improves the regression adjustment of
\bm{C}_{ij}
using cluster-period averages, regarding asymptotic efficiency.

4.4
Generalization to summary estimands

Results in Propositions
1
-
3
can be generalized to summary estimands. The generalization of Proposition
1
is apparent. For Propositions
2
and
3
, consider the example of comparing
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])
and
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
, where
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})-\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])=\bm{b}_{\theta}^{\top}\{\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})-\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])\}\bm{b}_{\theta}
. Using the arguments for Proposition
3
, the diagonal entries of
\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
are greater than the corresponding entries of
\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])
. Since both
\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
and
\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])
are covariance matrices and, therefore, positive definite, the difference matrix
\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})-\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])
is positive semi-definite such that
\bm{b}_{\theta}^{\top}\{\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})-\mathbf{Cov}(\widehat{\bm{\tau}}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])\}\bm{b}_{\theta}\geq 0
for any
\bm{b}_{\theta}\in\mathbb{R}^{J^{2}(J+1)/2}
, suggesting that
\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}]
is more efficient than
\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
with
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
. The following Corollary
2
summarizes the results comparing asymptotic variances of estimators for summary estimands.

Corollary 2

(a)

For estimators using individual-level data and cluster-period averages, we have

\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}}=\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}
,
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}})=\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}})
,
\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\bm{C}_{ij}]=\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}}
, and
\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{CR}}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\bm{C}_{ij}])=\widehat{\mathrm{se}}_{\mathrm{HC}}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
.

(b)

For estimators using individual-level data and scaled cluster-period totals, we have

\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}})
, and
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot},\widetilde{\bm{X}}_{ij\cdot}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{I}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
.

(c)

For estimators using cluster-period averages and scaled cluster-period totals, we have

\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}})
, and
\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mathrm{adj}}[\pi_{ij\cdot},\pi_{ij\cdot}\bm{C}_{ij}])\leq\mathrm{se}^{2}(\widehat{\theta}_{\mathrm{A}}^{\mathrm{adj}})
.

We illustrate the proposed methods by analyzing the Acute Coronary Syndrome Quality Improvement in Kerala (ACS QUIK) trial data

7
Concluding remarks

We provide a comprehensive treatment of causal inference for the class of staggered rollout cluster randomized experiments, including a class of causal estimands accounting for adoption and calendar times, as well as regression estimators using different levels of data. To pursue our estimands, in each calendar period, we can first compare the difference between any pair of treatment adoption times by estimating the DWATE estimand. Other collections of estimands, including weighted average treatment effects and anticipated weighted average treatment effects (and examples listed in Section S1 of the Online Supplement), are linear combinations of the DWATEs. Regression estimators using individual-level data, cluster-period averages, and scaled cluster-period totals are formally characterized, which, under the design-based perspective, are model-assisted. We established the consistency and asymptotic normality for each regression estimator and proved that the CR and HC variance estimators are asymptotically conservative. Relatedly, our results on the conservativeness of the HC variance estimator also provide a rigorous theoretical foundation for its adoption in design-based analyses of SR-IREs
(
Roth and Sant’Anna, 2023
)
. Through analytical comparisons, we found that regression estimators using individual-level data and cluster-period averages can be suboptimal, whereas the estimator using scaled cluster-period totals and adjusting for covariates is more efficient, with a smaller asymptotic variance. This implication, in fact, also offers novel insights into the analysis of cluster randomized DID. That is, if a cluster randomized DID design only has access to cluster-period level data, one should opt for collecting cluster-period totals rather than cluster-period averages, thereby making the analysis asymptotically more efficient. Our findings generalize those previously developed for much simpler parallel-arm CREs in
Su and Ding (2021)
to a more complex class of SR-CREs. This generalization, however, is not trivial because our asymptotic results are established under the randomization distribution induced by assigning clusters to different adoption times and jointly for a finite-dimensional vector of DWATE estimands. The conservativeness of the CR and HC variance estimators, hence, is in the sense of Löwner ordering and holds for inference about any linear combinations of the DWATE estimands.

We acknowledge that our asymptotic theory assumes the number of clusters approaches infinity, with bounded cluster-period sizes and a fixed number of calendar periods. This asymptotic regime is standard and follows the ones used in
Middleton and Aronow (2015)
;
Su and Ding (2021)
;
Chen and Li (2024)
. Nevertheless, with SR-CREs, this is not the only possible asymptotic regime, and it will be interesting to develop complementary asymptotic theories under the alternative regime where the number of clusters stays fixed. Still, the cluster size approaches infinity or both approach infinity, reminiscent of those studied in
Xie and Yang (2003)
for generalized estimating equation estimators.

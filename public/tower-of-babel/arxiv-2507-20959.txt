Benamou–Brenier and Kantorovich are equivalent on sub-Riemannian manifolds with no abnormal geodesics
Giovanna Citti, Mattia Galeotti, Andrea Pinamonti

Abstract
. We prove that the Benamou–Brenier formulation of the Optimal Transport problem and the Kantorovich formulation are equivalent on a sub-Riemannian connected and complete manifold M M without boundary and with no abnormal geodesics, when the problems are considered between two measures of compact supports. Furthermore, we prove the existence of a minimizer for the Benamou–Brenier formulation and link it to the optimal transport plan.

49Q22, 53C17

1.
intro

The Benamou–Brenier “dynamical” formulation of Optimal Transport
is the minimization problem for the functional

(1.1)

J_{\BB}(\mu_{t},v_{t})=\int_{0}^{1}||v_{t}(x)||^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t

where
v_{t}
is a Borel family of vector fields
and
\mu_{t}
a narrowly continuous family of probabilities
starting at a given
\mu_{0}
and finishing at a given
\mu_{1}
.
Both
\mu_{t}
and
v_{t}
are defined on a differentiable manifold
M

and constrained by the Continuity Equation

(1.2)

\dot{\mu}_{t}+\Div(\mu_{t}v_{t})=0.

For a focus on the Continuity Equation see
[
5
, Chapter 8]
and
[
3
]
,
we point out that this equation links the path
\mu_{t}
to the flow
of the ODE associated to
v_{t}
, expressing
the conservation of mass for the action (
1.1
).
All this gives a time dependent approach to the
transport problem.

By contrast, in the classical Kantorovich formulation
the dynamic part is encoded in the squared distance function,

d^{2}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\left.\int_{0}^{1}||\dot{\omega}_{t}||^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right|\,\omega\colon[0,1]\to M\ \mbox{absolutely continuous,}\,\omega_{0}=x,\,\omega_{1}=y\right\}.

This is called “cost” function, and the
minimization problem regards the coupling
of points. In particular, in this “static” formulation we want to minimize

(1.3)

J_{\Kan}(\gamma)=\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

where
\gamma
is taken among admissible plans,
i.e.
probabilities on
M\times M
with
marginals equal to
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
respectively
on the two coordinate projections.

The Kantorovich and Benamou–Brenier formulations
have been proven equivalent on bounded Euclidian domains

[
8
]
and more generally
on complete Riemannian manifolds without boundary,
see for instance
[
4
]
.
In this work we are going to prove their equivalence on a sub-Riemannian manifold

M
under some assumptions that we state precisely below.

The transport problem was introduced by Monge at the end of the 18th century,
it consists in the
the minimization problem for the functional

J_{\Mon}(T)=\int_{M}c(x,T(x))\mathrm{d}x

for some cost functon
c\colon M\times M\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{+\infty\}

with
T\colon M\to M
a function constrained by the transport condition
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
.
However, this formulation is ill-suited for a general resolution, because
the transport condition lacks closedness with respect to the principal
weak topologies.

The Kantorovich formulation overcomes this obstructions. In the Kantorovich
setting the existence of a minimizer, an optimal plan, is established for any Polish space
(M,d)

if the cost function is lower semi-continuous and bounded from below, see
[
4
, Theorem 1.5]
,
but also
[
25
, Theorem 5.10]
for a wider treatment of the problem.
Furthermore,
in the Riemannian setting, when the cost is the square distance, we also have
the existence and uniqueness of an optimal map

i.e.
a map inducing an optimal plan. This was proved,
under opportune assumptions, by Brenier in the Euclidian case
[
11
]

and by McCann in the general Riemannian case
[
20
]
.

The first decade of the 20th century saw a renewd effort
for Optimal Transport in the setting
of sub-Riemannian manifolds,
that is on differentiable manifolds admitting a totally non-integrable
distribution
HM\subset TM
and a positive definite quadratic form
g_{SR}
on
HM
.
For an introduction see
[
22
]
. In particular, some existence, uniqueness and regularity
results for the optimal map were proved for Heiseneberg groups
[
6
]
,

2
-generating distributions
[
2
]
and also for more general cases

[
16
,
17
]
, but the problem in full generality is still open.

While in the Riemannian context there exists only one kind of geodesics, the so called

normal
geodesics, on sub-Riemannian manifolds a second kind, the
abormal

ones, may exist. Because of the existence of abnormal geodesics, the sub-Riemannian distance
fails to be local convex, and this is the main obstacle to the existence of the optimal map.

To treat the dynamical problem in sub-Riemannian context,
we consider a
relaxed version
of the problem. In particular,
we minimize the integral of
||v||^{2}
along a probability
\eta

on
[0,1]\times HM
, instead of considering just one vector field
v_{t}

for every time
t
, integrated with respect to
\mu_{t}
.
This approach was already used in
[
15
]
and
[
14
]

in the case
M=\mathbb{R}^{n}
endowed with a sub-Riemannian structure,
and fits into the setting of
control relaxation techniques.
For some examples see
[
13
,
12
,
19
]

and more recently
[
21
,
23
]
.

For a proper formulation of the relaxed problem, we consider the class
of Young measures. For any Polish space
(X,d)
, a
Young measure
\eta
is a (positive) measure defined
on
[0,1]\times X
with the property that the first coordinate projection

\pi^{(t)}_{\#}\eta
is Lebesgue measurable. Therefore, a disintegration
\eta_{t}

exists with respect to the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. If we denote by
\pi\colon TM\to M

the canonical projection from the tangent bundle, then
by pushforwarding a Young probability on
[0,1]\times HM

we obtain a narrowly continuous path of probabilities
\mu_{t}=\pi_{\#}\eta_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
.

The Continuity Equation can be adapated to this framework, see Equation (
3.2
),
and used as the constraint for the minimization of the relaxed functional

(1.4)

J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)=\int_{[0,1]\times HM}||v||^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v),

where we also ask that
\pi_{\#}\eta_{0}=\mu_{0}
and
\pi_{\#}\eta_{1}=\mu_{1}
.

We first prove the equivalence of the original (sub-Riemannian) Benamou–Brenier
and the relaxed version. Then,
by proving the compactness of the
J^{\star}_{BB}
sub-levels in
Lemma
A.10
, we obtain the existence of a minimizer.
For the last part, we prove the equivalence with the Kantorovich formulation. To do this,
we use a
superposition principle
that allows to link the value of
J_{\Kan}
on admissible plans
to the value of
J_{\BB}^{\star}
on some admissible Young measures. We resume.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
M
be a sub-Riemannian, connected and complete manifold
without boundary and with no abnormal geodesics. Let
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}

be two probabilities in
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
with compact supports.
Then, the infima of the Kantorovich, Benamou–Brenier and relaxed Benamou–Brenier
transport problems are realized and equal.

Moreover, if
\gamma
is a minimizer for Kantorovich, then a minimizer for
Benamou–Brenier is obtained by pushforwarding
\mathcal{L}\otimes\gamma

along the
F
map defined at (
3.10
).

Viceversa, if
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
is a minimizer for Benamou–Brenier, then a minimizer for Kantorovich
is obtained by pushforwarding an opportune measure associated to
\mu_{t}
along
the
E
map defined at (
3.14
).

With respect to
[
14
]
, our treatment of the problem
is developed outside the control theory setting. Furthermore,
with respect to the case with underlying manifold equipped with an Euclidean structure,
we solve two subtle additional issues.
First, we lack the well behaved Euclidean distance, and therefore
we prove the compactness of the
J^{\star}_{\BB}
sub-levels
by using the intrinsic sub-Riemannian distance of the manifold.
Second, the use of the superposition principle relies on the construction
of a measurable map
S
that for any pair
(x,y)\in M\times M
gives
a geodesics from
x
to
y
. The construction of
S

poses non-trivial technical problems that we treat through
Suslin set theory, as showed in Theorem
A.6
.

The main obstacle to extending our results to manifolds
that have abnormal geodesics,
lies precisely in the construction of the (measurable)

S
map.

The paper is organized as follow. In the sections §
2.1
and §
2.2
,
the basic properties of sub-Riemannian manifolds and Optimal Transport are
introduced;
in the following chapter, §
3.1
is dedicated to the formulation of the relaxed problem
and to the proof of its equivalence with the standard Benamou–Brenier formulation,
and §
3.2
focuses on the equivalence between the Kantorovich formulation and
the Benamou–Brenier formulation. The Appendix is dedicated to some
key technical tools that are used throughout the whole paper. In particular,
§
A.3
introduces the main results on Young transport measures, §
A.4

shows the existence of the map
S\colon M\times M\to\Geod(M)
,
§
A.5
presents and proves the superposition principle
in sub-Riemannian setting and
§
A.6
is dedicated to the important compactness result
for the sub-levels of the relaxed functional.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we introduce the basics
of sub-Riemannian structures on manifolds,
giving in particular some general results on the sub-Riemannian
distance and sub-Riemannian geodesics.
Furthermore,
we introduce optimal transport on metric
spaces and more specifically the formulations of Kantorovich
and Benamou–Brenier in the context of sub-Riemannian manifolds.

2.1.
Sub-Riemannian structures

A sub-Riemannian manifold
is a triplet
(M,H,g_{SR})
of a differentiable manifold
M
,
a completely non-integrable distribution
HM\subset TM

and a positive smooth quadratic form
g_{SR}
defined on
HM
. By completely
intregable we mean that for every
x\in M
, if
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
is a base of
HM

on an open neighborhood
U\subset M
such that
x\in U
, then there exists
r\in\mathbb{N}^{+}

such that the brackets

\left[X_{i_{1}},[\dots,[X_{i_{k-1}},X_{i_{k}}]]\right](x)\quad\mbox{with }i_{1},\dots,i_{k}\in\{1,\dots,m\}\mbox{ and }1\leq k\leq r,

span the whole tangent space
T_{x}M
.

For a wide introduction to sub-Riemannian manifolds and their metric and differential structure,
see
[
1
]
and
[
7
]
. Here, we recall the basics of the metric
structure induced by the sub-Riemannian setting.

We denote by
I
the unitary interval
[0,1]
.
We call
admissible
any absolutely continuous curve
\omega\colon I\to M

such that the tangent vector
\dot{\omega}_{t}
lies in
H_{\omega_{t}}M
for a.e.
t

in
I
. These are also called
horizontal
curves.
We denote by

\Gamma_{HM}:=\left\{\omega\colon I\to M:\ \mbox{absolutely continuous and }\dot{\omega}_{t}\in H_{\omega_{t}}M\ \mbox{for a.e.}\,t\in I\right\}\subset C(I,M)

the space of admissible curves.
A sub-Riemannian distance
d_{SR}
is defined on
M

as
the infimum of the lenght of admissible curves
between two points,

d_{SR}(x,y):=\inf\left\{\int_{I}||\dot{\omega}_{t}||\,\mathrm{d}t:\ \omega\in\Gamma_{HM},\ \omega_{0}=x,\ \omega_{1}=y\right\}.

The celebrated Chow–Rashevskii theorem states that if
M
is connected,
then
d_{SR}
is always finite,
i.e.
for any pair
(x,y)
there exists an
admissible curve joining
x
to
y
.
Moreover, if
(M,d_{SR})
is complete, for any pair
(x,y)
there exists
a length minimizer joining
x
to
y
,
i.e.
the distance
d_{SR}(x,y)
is effectively realized.

Up to reparametrization, we consider constant speed geodesics,
the admissible curves with constant speed that minimize the length functional.
Equivalently, constant speed geodesics minimize the energy functional,

\Geod(M):=\left\{\omega\colon I\to M:\ \mbox{absolutely continuous and }d^{2}_{SR}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})=\int_{I}||\dot{\omega}_{t}||^{2}\mathrm{d}t\right\}

We consider the space of (constant speed) geodesics as endowed with the sup distance

d(\omega^{(1)},\omega^{(2)}):=\sup_{t\in I}d_{SR}(\omega^{(1)}(t),\omega^{(2)}(t))\quad\forall\omega^{(1)},\omega^{(2)}\in\Geod(M).

Any geodesic
\omega_{t}
on a sub-Riemannian manifold corresponds to the projection
of an integral curve
\lambda_{t}\colon I\to T^{*}M
of the Hamiltonian vector field

X_{H}\in TT^{*}M
. Any such curve
\lambda
satisfies the Hamilton equation
and the value of the Hamiltonian
H(\lambda_{t})
is constant for
t\in I
.
From this characterization two types of geodesics arise:

(1)

normal
geodesics are the projection of curves with
H(\lambda_{t})>0
;

(2)

abnormal
geodesics are the projection of curves with
H(\lambda_{t})=0
.

A geodesic is called
strictly normal
(resp.
strictly abnormal
) when it is normal and not abnormal (resp. abnormal and not normal).
Normal geodesics are uniquely determined by the initial conditions of the associated curve
\lambda_{t}\in T^{*}M
,
namely the initial covector
\lambda_{0}\in T^{*}_{x}M

whose starting point is
x\in M
.

In the following we work with a complete connected sub-Riemannian manifold
M

without boundary and with no abnormal geodesics. As there is no risk
of confusion, from now on we use the simpler notation
d(-,-)
for
the sub-Riemannian distance
d_{SR}(-,-)
.

2.2.
Optimal transport on sub-Riemannian manifolds

The Optimal Transport problem may be introduced in full generality
over a Polish space
(X,d)
.
For a wide overview of this subject see for example
[
25
,
4
]
.
We use the notation
\mathcal{P}(X)
for the space of Borel probabilty measures on
X

and, given a Borel map
f\colon X\to Y
between Polish spaces, we denote by

T_{\#}\mu
the pushforward of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
,

T_{\#}\mu(B)=\mu(T^{-1}(B)),\ \ \forall B\in\mathcal{B}(Y).

Over the probability space
\mathcal{P}(X)
we will often consider
convergence with respect to the narrow topology,
meaning that
\mu_{n}\to\mu
if

\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{n}\to\int_{X}\varphi\,\mathrm{d}\mu\ \ \ \forall\varphi\in C_{b}(X).

Once we fix a (Borel) cost function
c\colon X\times Y\to\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\}
,
we can introduce the Monge version of the transport problem.
Given
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(X),\ \mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
, we want to minimize

(Mon)

C_{\Mon}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\inf_{T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}}\int_{X}c(x,T(x))\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)

where the infimum is taken among all Borel maps
T\colon X\to Y
such that
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
.
This formulation has a series of patologies, for instance
the constraint
T_{\#}\mu_{0}=\mu_{1}
lack closedness with respect to the principal
weak topologies.

The problem was formulated in a more general fashion by Kantorovich.
We denote by
\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
the set of
admissible transport plans
between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
,

\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}):=\left\{\gamma\in\mathcal{P}(X\times Y):\ \pi^{(X)}_{\#}\gamma=\mu_{0},\ \pi^{(Y)}_{\#}\gamma=\mu_{1}\right\}

where
\pi^{(X)}
and
\pi^{(Y)}
are the projections from
X\times Y
to
X
and
Y
respectively.
The minimization problem becomes

(Kan)

\begin{split}J_{\Kan}(\gamma)&:=\int_{X\times Y}c(x,y)\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\\
\\
C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})&:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})}J_{\Kan}(\gamma).\end{split}

Observe that a transport map
T
,
as the one considered in the Monge formulation,
is represented in the Kantorovich formulation
via the induced plan
\gamma=(\id_{X}\times T)_{\#}\mu_{0}
,
therefore (
Kan
) is a generalization of (
Mon
).
Moreover,
\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
is always non-empty, convex and compact with respect
to the narrow topology. A minimizer for (
Kan
) always exists under
very general assumptions.

Theorem 2.1

(See Theorem 1.5 in
[
4
]
)
.

If the cost function
c\colon X\times Y
is lower semi-continuous and bounded from below,
then there exists a minimizer for (
Kan
) for any
\mu_{0}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and
\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(Y)
.

Remark 2.2
.

If
X=Y
and
c(x,y)=d^{2}(x,y)
, the Kantorovich problem induces a distance
W_{2}(\mu,\nu)=\sqrt{C_{\Kan}(\mu,\nu)}

called Wasserstein distance, and
(\mathcal{P}_{2}(M),W_{2})
is
a complete metric space, where
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
is the space of probabilities
with finite
2
-momentum.

In this manuscript we will usually work with
X=Y=M
a sub-Riemannian manifold
and
c(x,y)=d^{2}_{SR}(x,y)
the square distance. The distance is continuous,
therefore the conditions for the existence of a minimizer are attained.

Up to this point, we just used the metric structure on
M
.
If we look also at the differential structure on
M
, we can get a dynamical formulation
of the Optimal Transport problem. If
M
is any complete Riemannian manifold,
we introduce the Continuity Equation, describing the evolution of a measure path in
\mathcal{P}(M)

along the flow of a family of vector fields. If
I=[0,1]
,
consider a Borel family of probability measures

\mu\colon I\to\mathcal{P}(M)
and a Borel family of vector fields
v\colon I\times M\to TM

respecting the condition

(2.1)

\int_{I}\int_{M}\lVert v_{t}(x)\rVert\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t<\infty.

Then, the Continuity Equation states

(2.2)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mu_{t}+\Div(v_{t}\mu_{t})=0.

This has to be considered in the distributional sense, meaning that

(2.3)

\int_{I}\int_{M}\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)+\langle v_{t},\nabla\phi(t,x)\rangle_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t=0\ \ \ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M).

This has been studied from many point of views,
see in particular
[
5
, Chapter 8]
and
[
3
]
for a wide overwiev.
Here, we are interested to one particular result, the so called
superposition principle
.

Let
\Gamma:=C([0,1],M)
be the set of continuous curves on
M
,
and
e_{t}\colon\Gamma\to M
the map such that
\Gamma\ni\gamma\mapsto\gamma(t)

for any
t\in I
.
If
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
is a pair respecting the Continuity Equation (
2.2
),

\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
is absolutely continuous for a.e.
t\in I

[
4
, Theorem 2.29]

(with respect to the Wasserstein distance)
and there exists
\widetilde{\mu}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)
concentrated on absolutely continuous curves
such that

(2.4)

(e_{t})_{\#}\widetilde{\mu}=\mu_{t}\quad\forall t\in I.

We remark that in
[
24
]
this result have been extended to complete metric
spaces under opportune hypothesis.

We consider now the adaptation of the Continuity Equation to the
sub-Riemannian setting.
Given a sub-Riemannian variety
(M,H)
, we consider
a Borel family of probability measures
\mu\colon I\to\mathcal{P}(M)
,
and a Borel vector field
v\colon I\times M\to HM

such that for a.e.
t\in I
,
v_{t}
is an horizontal vector field on
M
.
Then, the continuity equation is obtained by just
replacing the gradient with the horizontal gradient,

(2.5)

\int_{I}\int_{M}\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)+\langle v_{t},\nabla_{H}\phi(t,x)\rangle_{x}\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t=0\ \ \ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M).

Remark 2.3
.

For the Continuity Equation in Riemannian and
sub-Riemannian setting, there are two equivalent
formulations that we state here without proof
because they won’t be needed in this work.
For a proof see
[
5
]
.

The “weak” formulation of the
continuity equation considers smooth test functions
defined over
M
,

(2.6)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{M}f\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}=-\int_{M}\langle v_{t},\nabla f\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\ \ \ \forall f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M).

If we take a function
\phi(t,x)=\theta(t)\cdot f(x)
with
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1))
and
f\in C^{\infty}_{c}(M)
,
then (
2.3
) implies (
2.6
)
in the distributional sense.
The two formulations are in fact equivalent, and furthermore
(
2.6
) implies that
it always exists a narrowly continuous family
\overline{\mu}\colon I\to\mathcal{P}(M)
,
such that
\overline{\mu}_{t}=\mu_{t}
for a.e.
t\in[0,1]

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\mathclap{\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}}\,\int_{M}\partial_{t}\phi(t,x)+\langle v_{t}(x),\nabla_{H}\phi(t,x)\rangle\mathrm{d}\overline{\mu}_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\phi(t_{2},x)\mathrm{d}\overline{\mu}_{t_{2}}(x)-\int_{M}\phi(t_{1},x)\mathrm{d}\overline{\mu}_{t_{1}}(x)

\displaystyle\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(I\times M),\ \forall t_{1},t_{2}\in[0,1].

The Benamou–Brenier problem is a dynamical formulation of
the Optimal Transport problem. In the setting of a sub-Riemannian manifold
this becomes,

(BB)

\begin{split}J_{\BB}(\mu_{t},v_{t})&:=\int_{I}\int_{M}\lVert v_{t}(x)\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t\\
\\
C_{\BB}(\mu,\nu)&:=\inf_{(\mu_{t},v_{t})}J_{\BB}(\mu_{t},v_{t})\end{split}

where the infimum is taken among all the pairs of a Borel family of probability measures
\mu_{t}
,
and a Borel horizontal vector field
v_{t}
respecting condition (
2.1
), such that

(\mu_{t},v_{t})
respects the sub-Riemannian
Continuity Equation (
2.5
).

In the following we will prove that under the hypothesis that
M
is a complete sub-Riemannian
with no boundary and no abnormal geodesics, if
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
have compact support then
the Kantorovich formulation and the Benamou–Brenier formulation are equivalent.
Namely,
C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and both
infima are realized.

3.
The results

In order to show that
the Kantorovich formulation (
Kan
) and the Benamou–Brenier
formulation (
BB
) of the transport problem are equivalent on sub-Riemannian manifolds
(under some assumptions that will be made precise later),
we weaken the Benamou–Brenier problem by formulating
it over measures in
\mathcal{P}(I\times HM)
. We start by introducing the following set
for any Polish space
(X,d)
,

(3.1)

\mathcal{Y}(I;X):=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{P}(I\times X):\ \pi_{\#}^{(t)}\eta=\mathcal{L}\right\}

here
\pi^{(t)}\colon I\times X\to I
is the projection on the first coordinate
while
\mathcal{L}
is the Lebesgue measure on
I
.
We will denote by
\eta_{t}
a disintegration of
\eta
with respect to
\mathcal{L}

for
t\in I
.

Any
\eta
in
\mathcal{Y}(I;X)
is called a
Young measure
on
X
. There’s a large
literature on this class of measures and when necessary we will mainly refer to
[
9
]
.

3.1.
The relaxed problem

If
X=TM
the tangent bundle to a smooth manifold
M
,
and
\pi\colon TM\to M
the canonical projection,
then we call
transport measure
a measure
\eta
in
\mathcal{Y}(I;TM)

such that
\eta
verifies the following version of the Continuity Equation,

(3.2)

\int_{I\times TM}\partial_{t}\phi(t,\pi(v))+\langle v,\nabla\phi(t,\pi(v))_{\pi(v)}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M).

Remark 3.1
.

Analogously to what happens with the standard Continuity Equation (see Remark
2.3
),
in
[
9
, Lemma 5 and 6]
it is proved that if
\eta
is a transport measure,
then there exists a narrowly continuous family
\mu_{t}\in\mathcal{P}(M)

such that
\pi_{\#}\eta_{t}=\mu_{t}
for a.e.
t\in I
.
Moreover, for any function
\phi\in C^{1}(I\times M)

bounded and Lipschitz and for any interval
[t_{1},t_{2}]\subset I
,

(3.3)

\int_{[t_{1},t_{2}]\times TM}\partial_{t}\phi+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{M}\phi(t_{1},x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t_{1}}(x)-\int_{M}\phi(t_{2},x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t_{2}}(x).

To relax the Benamou–Brenier formulation of the optimal transport,
we want to consider the case of transport measures that are
concentrated on the horizontal distribution, meaning that

\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)\subset\mathcal{Y}(I;TM)
. Observe that
\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)
is closed
inside
\mathcal{Y}(I;TM)
with respect to the narrow topology.

Remark 3.2
.

We are working on a smooth manifold without boundary
M
and we consider
the tangent bundle
TM
. For many of the technical results
on Young measures used in the present work,

TM
has to be endowed with a complete distance
D
with the property
that

\frac{1+D((x_{0},0),(x,v))}{1+||v||_{x}}\quad\mbox{with }v\in T_{x}M,

is bounded on
TM
for a given (and then every) point
x_{0}\in M
,
and its inverse has the same property.

The existence of such a distance is proved in
[
9
, §3]
.
Observe that we only need a topological compatibility with the sub-Riemannian structure
on
M
, therefore to define
D
we consider the smooth structure
on
M
and a Riemannian metric on
M
compatible with the smooth structure.

If we pose
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I,TM)
,
Equation (
3.2
)
can be equivalently stated in the setting of smooth manifolds,

\int_{I}\int_{M}\partial_{t}\phi+v\phi\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M).

If we set the additional condition that
\eta
must be concentrated on
HM\subset TM
,
we have a transport measure in
\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)
. Therefore,
reversing the conditions
we get a sub-Riemannian relaxed version of the Continuity Equation. Indeed,
a measure
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)

is a transport measure if and only if

(3.4)

\int_{I\times HM}\partial_{t}\phi(t,\pi(v))+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi(t,\pi(v))\rangle_{\pi(v)}\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=0\ \ \ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M).

where
\nabla_{H}
is the horizontal gradient.
A slightly stronger version links
\eta
to its “values” at the extremes of the interval,

(3.5)

\int_{I\times HM}\partial_{t}\phi+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi\rangle\mathrm{d}\eta=\int_{M}\phi(1,x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)-\int_{M}\phi(0,x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x)\ \ \ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(I\times M).

These versions of the Continuity Equation define two closed subsets of

\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)
with respect to the narrow topology,

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))

\displaystyle:=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I;HM):\ \eta\ \mbox{verifies \eqref{con-t}}\right\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)

\displaystyle:=\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I;HM):\ \eta\ \mbox{verifies \eqref{con-star} w.r.t.}\ \mu_{0},\mu_{1}\right\}.

Observe that
\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)\subset\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))
.
We can now formulate
the relaxed version of (
BB
),

(BB
⋆
)

\begin{split}J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)&:=\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)\\
\\
C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})&:=\inf_{\eta\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}}J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta).\end{split}

Remark 3.3
.

We point out that the relaxed version is in fact a
weaker
version of (
BB
)
because to any pair
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
satisfying the Continuity Equation (
2.3
),
we can naturally associate the measure
\eta_{(\mu,v)}=\mu_{t}\otimes\delta_{v_{t}}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)
,
that is the measure defined by

\phi\mapsto\int_{I}\int_{M}\phi(t,v_{t}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t\ \ \ \forall\phi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I\times HM).

We have
J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta_{(\mu,v)})=J_{\BB}(\mu_{t},v_{t})
and therefore
we are in fact weakening the constaints on the (
BB
) problem.

Recall that
\pi\colon HM\to M
is the natural projection from the horizontal bundle
to the base manifold.
By Lemma
A.1
,
if
\eta\in\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))
is a transport measure, then there exists
a narrowly continuous family
\mu_{t}^{\eta}\colon I\to\mathcal{P}(M)

and a disintegration
\eta_{t}
of
\eta
such that

(3.6)

\pi_{\#}\eta_{t}=\mu_{t}^{\eta}\ \ \ \forall t\in I.

We can also state a Young measure version of the superposition principle in sub-Riemannian
setting. In fact, there exists
\widetilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\Gamma)

such that

(3.7)

(e_{t})_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}=\mu_{t}^{\eta}.

Moreover,
\widetilde{\eta}
is concentrated on the admissible curves, that is
those curves
\omega
such that
\dot{\omega}_{t}\in H_{\omega_{t}}M

for a.e.
t\in I
.
We prove (a more detailed version of) this result in the Appendix,
see Theorem
A.8
.

Furthermore, for any
\eta\in\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))
and
for any
t\in I
,
we consider a disintegration
\eta_{t,x}
with respect to
\mu_{t}^{\eta}
.
Through
\eta_{t,x}
we can introduce
an associated measurable map

v^{\eta}\colon I\times M\to HM

such that
for a.e.
t
,
v_{t}^{\eta}
is a horizontal vector field on
M
,

(3.8)

v_{t}^{\eta}(x):=\int_{H_{x}M}v\,\mathrm{d}\eta_{t,x}(v).

Observe that by construction we get

(3.9)

\begin{split}\int_{I\times HM}\left(\partial_{t}\phi+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)&=\int_{I\times M}\int_{H_{x}M}\left(\partial_{t}\phi+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\eta_{t,x}(v)\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}^{\eta}(x)\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{I\times M}\left(\partial_{t}\phi+\langle v^{\eta}_{t},\nabla_{H}\phi\rangle\right)\mathrm{d}\mu^{\eta}_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t\\
&\ \forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(I\times M)\end{split}

where we used the definition of
v^{\eta}_{t}
and the linearity of the scalar product.

Moreover, if
J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)<+\infty
, then
by Jensen’s inequality

+\infty>\int_{I\times HM}\|v\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta\geq\int_{I}\int_{M}\|v_{t}^{\eta}\|^{2}\mathrm{d}\mu^{\eta}_{t}\mathrm{d}t

that is,
(\mu^{\eta}_{t},v^{\eta}_{t})
verifies condition (
2.1
).

Therefore, we get two results that we summarize in the following remark.

Remark 3.4
.

From (
3.9
) and the fact that
\eta
satisfies (
3.4
),
we get that
(\mu_{t}^{\eta},v_{t}^{\eta})
satisfies the continuity equation (
2.5
).

Also, observe that if we impose the narrow continuity of the curve
\mu_{t}^{\eta}
,
then this is uniquely defined and

\eta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)
, where
we used the notation
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}
for
\mu_{0}^{\eta},\mu_{1}^{\eta}
on the indices,
for the sake of clarity.

To any Young transport measure
\eta
concentrated on the horizontal bundle, we have thus associated
a unique pair
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
of a narrowly continuous curve of measures
and a Borel family of vector fields (respecting (
2.1
))
that satisfy the Benamou–Brenier constraint (
2.5
).

As proved later in the Appendix at Lemma
A.9
,
for any
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}(M)
with compact support,
there exists a compact set
K\subset M
containing

\supp(\mu_{0})
and
\supp(\mu_{1})
, such that
for any
\eta
transport measure in
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)
, there exists

\overline{\eta}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)

not “exiting” from the set
K
, meaning that

\pi_{\#}\overline{\eta}([0,1]\times K)=1
. Moreover,

\overline{\eta}
has a
less ore equal cost
J^{\star}_{\BB}(\overline{\eta})\leq J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)
.
Therefore we can always consider a “better” measure
that does not exit from
K
.

Furthermore, by
Lemma
A.10
,
for any constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
, the set
of measures with bounded
\star
-cost

\{\eta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM):\ J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)\leq c,\ \pi_{\#}\eta([0,1]\times K)=1\}

is compact. This is sufficient to state the existence of a minimum.

Theorem 3.5
.

Consider a sub-Riemannian complete variety
M

with no boundary and no abnormal geodesics,
and two probability measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
with compact support,
if the set

\left\{\eta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM):\ J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)<+\infty\right\}

is non-empty, then a minimizer for (
BB⋆
) exists.

Theorem 3.6
.

Consider
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
with compact support,
suppose that there exists
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)
with

J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)
finite (i.e. the relaxed problem is feasible).
Then, there exists a pair
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
that solves the Benamou–Brenier
minimization problem (
BB
), and moreover (
BB
)
\equiv
(
BB⋆
).

Proof.

As a consequence of Remark
3.3
, we know that

C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\geq C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

We are going
to prove the reverse inequality and in particular, given a minimizer

\eta
for (
BB⋆
) that exists because of Theorem
3.5
, we show that the pair
(\mu_{t}^{\eta},v_{t}^{\eta})
minimizes (
BB
).

Given such a minimizer
\eta
, we consider

\mu_{t}^{\eta}
and
v_{t}^{\eta}
introduced in (
3.6
) and (
3.8
) respectively.
As summarized in Remark
3.4
,
the pair
(\mu_{t}^{\eta},v_{t}^{\eta})
satisfies the continuity equation (
2.3
).
Moreover, the map
v\mapsto\lVert v_{t}\rVert_{x}^{2}
is convex for any
(t,x)\in I\times M
,
therefore by Jensen’s inequality,

\displaystyle J_{\BB}(\mu_{t}^{\eta},v_{t}^{\eta})=\int_{I\times M}\lVert v_{t}^{\eta}(x)\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\eta}_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I\times M}\int_{H_{x}M}\lVert v\rVert^{2}_{x}\mathrm{d}\eta_{t,x}(v)\,\mathrm{d}\mu^{\eta}_{t}(x)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)

\displaystyle=J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

This proves
C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\leq C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
and
allows to complet the proof.∎

3.2.
Equivalence with the Kantorovich formulation

It remains to prove that the Kantorovich formulation (
Kan
)
and the Benamou–Brenier formulation (
BB
) are equivalent.

We denote by
\Geod(M)
the set of geodesics on the sub-Riemannian manifold
(M,H)
,
with the topology induced by the sup distance

\sup_{t\in[0,1]}d(\omega^{(1)}(t),\omega^{(2)}(t))

between any two geodesics
\omega^{(1)},\omega^{(2)}
.

The key technical result that we are going to use is that for any Borel measure
\rho
on
M\times M
,
there exists a Borel map

S\colon M\times M\to\Geod(M)

defined
\rho
-a.e. such that

S_{0}(x,y)=x\ \ \ \mbox{and}\ \ \ S_{1}(x,y)=y.

This result is proved in Theorem
A.6
in the Appendix.

The map
S
above allows to build a map
\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\to\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)
.
Indeed, consider the measurable function

F\colon I\times M\times M\to I\times HM

such that

(3.10)

(t,x,y)\mapsto(t,\partial_{t}S(x,y)).

Then, for
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, we consider

(3.11)

\gamma\mapsto F_{\#}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\gamma).

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, the image of the map
(
3.11
) is in
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)
.

Proof.

Throughout this proof we will use the notation
\eta:=F_{\#}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\gamma)
.
First observe that
\pi^{(t)}\circ F=\id_{I}
, therefore
\pi^{(t)}_{\#}\eta=\mathcal{L}

and so
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I;HM)
.

For any test function
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(I\times M)
we evaluate the following derivative for any
pair of points
(x,y)\in M\times M
,

(3.12)

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\phi(t,S_{t}(x,y))=\partial_{t}\phi(t,S_{t}(x,y))+\langle\partial_{t}S_{t}(x,y),\nabla_{H}\phi(t,S_{t}(x,y))\rangle,

where we used the fact that
S_{t}
is admissible and therefore

\partial_{t}S_{t}
is almost always an horizontal vector in
H_{S_{t}}M
.
Therefore, by construction we get

\displaystyle\int_{I\times HM}\partial_{t}\phi(t,\pi(v))

\displaystyle+\langle v,\nabla_{H}\phi(t,\pi(v))\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)

\displaystyle=\int_{I\times M\times M}\partial_{t}\phi(t,S_{t}(x,y))+\langle\partial_{t}S_{t}(x,y),\nabla_{H}\phi(t,S_{t}(x,y))\rangle\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)\mathrm{d}t

using (
3.12
)

\displaystyle=\int_{M\times M}\phi(1,x)-\phi(0,y)\mathrm{d}\gamma(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\phi(1,x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{1}(x)-\int_{M}\phi(0,x)\mathrm{d}\mu_{0}(x).

This is precisely the condition we wanted to prove.∎

Lemma 3.8
.

If
F
is defined as above and
\gamma\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, then

J_{\Kan}(\gamma)=J_{\BB}^{\star}(F_{\#}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\gamma)).

Proof.

Recall that the map
S
associates to any pair of points
(x,y)

a constant speed geodesic between them. Therefore

d^{2}(x,y)=\int_{I}\lVert\partial_{t}S_{t}(x,y)\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t.

If
\gamma
is an admissible plan between
\mu_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
we pass to
the evaluation of the Kantorovich cost of
\gamma
,

\displaystyle J_{\Kan}(\gamma)=\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}\gamma

\displaystyle=\int_{I\times M\times M}\lVert\partial_{t}S_{t}(x,y)\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\gamma\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta),

where again we used that
\eta=F_{\#}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\eta)
.∎

A direct corollary of this lemma is the inequality

(3.13)

C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\leq C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

In particular, observe that the feasibility of Kantorovich problem
implies the feasibility of the relaxed Benamou–Brenier problem.

In order to prove the last main result,
we develop in the Appendix section
A.5

the theory of
generalized curves
and
of transport measure decompositions.

A generalized curve is a transport
measure
\eta
with
\mu=\pi_{\#}\eta
such that

\mu_{t}
is a dirac measure for each
t\in I
.
We use the notation

\mathcal{G}(I,M)
for the set of generalized curves
and observe that it is closed inside
\mathcal{T}((0,1);TM)
.
We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
the set of generalized curve whose
associated curve is admissible,
i.e.
its tangent vectors
lies a.e. in the sub-Riemannian distribution.

We denote by

\mbox{pr}\colon\mathcal{G}(I,M)\to C(I;M)

the projection from the space of generalized curves to
continuous curves on
M
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Given
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
with compact supports,
the Kantorovich formulation and
the Benamou–Brenier formulation are equivalent,

C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

Proof.

After Theorem
3.6
and the inequality (
3.13
), it remains to prove
that

C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\leq C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

Let
e_{t}\colon C(I,M)\to M
be the map that sends any continuous curve
\gamma\colon I\to M
to the point
\gamma(t)
.
We consider the map

(3.14)

E:=(e_{0},e_{1})\circ\mbox{pr}\colon\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)\to M\times M.

The Young superposition principle
A.8
,
states that for every
\eta\in\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))

there exists a measure
\widetilde{\eta}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M))

such that

E_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}=\pi_{\#}\eta.

Observe in particular that if
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)
,
then

E_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}\in\Pi(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.
We consider the case of
\eta
a minimizer
for
C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
, and we
use notation

\omega=\mbox{pr}(\nu)
for the curve associated to a generalized curve
\nu
. Then,

\displaystyle\int_{M\times M}d^{2}(x,y)\mathrm{d}E_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}}d^{2}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}(\nu)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}}\int_{I}\lVert\dot{\omega}_{t}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}(\nu)

(because of (
A.2
) and convexity)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}}\int_{I}\int_{H_{\omega_{t}}M}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}(\nu)

(by Definition
A.3
of
\widetilde{\eta}
)

\displaystyle=\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta).

This proves that
J_{\Kan}(E_{\#}\widetilde{\eta})\leq J_{\BB}^{\star}(\eta)=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})

and therefore in particular

C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})\leq C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1}).

∎

We finally resume the results. We consider the case of a
connected sub-Riemannian manifold
M
without boundary and
with no abnormal geodesics, and of two compactly supported
measures
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
.
We proved in Theorem
3.5
and Theorem
3.6

that
C_{\BB}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0}\mu_{1})
and moreover there exists a pair
(\mu_{t},v_{t})
realizing the minimum cost.

In Theorem
3.9
we also proved that
C_{\Kan}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})=C_{\BB}^{\star}(\mu_{0},\mu_{1})
.
In the proof of the theorem and in Lemma
3.8
we showed that if
\gamma
is an optimal
plan for the Kantorovich formulation, then
F_{\#}(\mathcal{L}\otimes\gamma)
realizes the optimal
Benamou–Brenier cost. Viceversa, if
\eta
is
BB⋆
-optimal,
then
E_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}
is an optimal Kantorovich plan,
where
E

is defined at (
3.14
). This concludes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

A.3.
Transport measures

Lemma A.1
.

Consider
\eta\in\mathcal{T}(I;(M,H))
a transport measure over
(M,H)
, then
there exists a narrowly continuous family
\mu_{t}\colon I\to\mathcal{P}(M)

and a disintegration
\eta_{t}
of
\eta
, such that

\pi_{\#}\eta_{t}=\mu_{t}
for each
t\in I
.
As a consequence
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)
.

Proof.

We can follow precisely the proof of

[
9
, Lemma 5]
.
Consider
\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
a disintegration of
\eta
,
and
\widetilde{\mu}_{t}=\pi_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}_{t}
.
Then, by
[
9
, Lemma 37]

\widetilde{\mu}_{t}
is equal for a.e.
t

to a narrowly continuous map
\mu_{t}

if and only if for any compactly supported smooth function
f\colon I\to\mathbb{R}
,
the following function
I\to\mathbb{R}
,

F(t):=\int_{M}f\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{t},

is equal a.e. to a continuous function.

Consider Equation (
3.4
) and apply it to a function
\phi(t,x):=\theta(t)\cdot f(x)

where
\theta\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1))
. We obtain

\begin{split}0&=\int_{I}\int_{HM}\theta^{\prime}(t)f(\pi(v))+\theta(t)\langle v,\nabla_{H}f(t,\pi(v))\rangle\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}_{t}(v)\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{I}\int_{M}\theta^{\prime}(t)f(x)\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\mu}_{t}(x)+\int_{I}\theta(t)\left(\int_{HM}\langle v,\nabla_{H}f(\pi(v))\rangle\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}_{t}(v)\right)\mathrm{d}t\\
&=\int_{I}\theta^{\prime}(t)F(t)+\theta(t)\left(\int_{HM}\langle v,\nabla_{H}f\rangle\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}_{t}\right)\mathrm{d}t.\end{split}

As the choice of
\theta
is arbitrary, this means that
F^{\prime}(t)=\int_{HM}\langle v,\nabla_{H}f\rangle\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}
in the sense of distributions,
therefore
F
is equal a.e. to an absolutely continuous function.∎

A.4.
The
S
map

In order to
treat the superposition principle in sub-Riemannian setting, we
need to prove the existence of a measurable map that to any pair of points on
M

associates a geodesics connecting the two points. For this, we need
to use some results from the theory of Suslin spaces, we will use

[
10
]
as the main reference on this subject.

Definition A.2
.

A set in a Hausdorff topological space is a Suslin set
if it is the image of Polish space with respect to a continuous map.

A Suslin space is a Hausdorff topological space that is also a Suslin set.

On a Hausdorff space
X
, we use the notation
\mathcal{B}(X)

for its Borel
\sigma
-algebra.
In
X
, every Borel set is also Suslin.
The converse is not true, but there exists a notion similar to measurability
that is respected by any Suslin set.

Given a Borel measure
\rho
on
X
, a set function
\rho^{*}

is defined for any subset
A\subset X
,

\rho^{*}(A):=\inf\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho(A_{i}):\ A_{i}\mbox{ is Borel},\ A\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}A_{i}\right\}.

Observe that this notion can be introduced
for any set function
\rho
not necessarily a Borel measure, anyway
we will use this definition with the aim of simplifying the subject.

Definition A.3
.

A subset
A\subset X
is
\rho
-measurable or measurable with respect to
\rho
,
if for any
\varepsilon>0
there exists a Borel set
A_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{B}(X)

such that

\rho^{*}(A\triangle A_{\varepsilon})<\varepsilon.

Proposition A.4

(See
[
10
, Corollary 1.5.8]
)
.

Consider a Borel measure
\rho
on
X
.
A set
A\subset X
is
\rho
-measurable if and only if
there exists
A^{(1)},A^{(2)}\in\mathcal{B}(X)

such that

A^{(1)}\subset A\subset A^{(2)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho(A^{(2)}\backslash A^{(1)})=0.

Now we can state a general property of Suslin sets.

Theorem A.5

(See
[
10
, Theorem 7.4.1]
)
.

If
X
is a Hausdorff space and
\rho
a Borel measure on it,
then every Suslin set
A\subset X
is
\rho
-measurable.

Here we can state the main result of this appendix section.

Theorem A.6
.

Consider a sub-Riemannian complete connected manifold
M

without boundary and with no abnormal geodesics, and
the (Hausdorff) space of constant speed geodesics on it
\Geod(M)

endowed with the sup distance.
For any Borel measure
\rho
on
M\times M

there exists a measurable map

S\colon M\times M\to\Geod(M)

defined
\rho
-a.e. and such that

S_{0}(x,y)=x,\quad S_{1}(x,y)=y\quad\mbox{for }\rho-{a.e.}\,(x,y)\in M\times M.

Proof.

We introduce the map
\Psi
sending every pair of points
(x,y)

to the set of constant speed geodesics from
x
to
y
.
We are going to prove that the hypothesis of
[
10
, Theorem 6.9.2]

are verified.

The manifold
M\times M
is a Polish space. Moreover,
we recall that any normal geodesic in
\Geod(M)
is uniquely determined
by its starting point
x\in M
and a covector
\lambda\in T_{x}^{*}M
.
Indeed, normal geodesics are solutions
to a Hamiltonian system in
T^{*}M
with some covector
\lambda
as initial point.
Therefore, if
M
as no abnormal geodesics, then the map
T^{*}M\to\Geod(M)
is continuous and surjective,
and therefore
\Geod(M)

is a Suslin set. Because of the continuity of the endpoint map, we
obtain that the graph of
\Psi
is a Suslin set too.

As a consequence of
[
10
, Theorem 6.9.2]
, a selection
S\colon M\times M\to\Geod(M)

exists, such that

S(x,y)\in\Psi(x,y)\quad\forall(x,y)\in M\times M

and
S
is measurable with respect to the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(\Geod(M))
of Borel sets
on
\Geod(M)
, and the
\sigma
-algebra
\sigma(S_{M\times M})
generated by the Suslin
sets on
M\times M
.

We conclude by showing that for any Borel measure
\rho
on
M\times M
,
there exists a Borel set
B\subset M\times M
with
\rho(B)=0
such that the
restriction

\left.S\right|_{M\times M\backslash B}
is
(Borel) measurable.

Given a countable base
A_{1},A_{2},\dots
of the Borel
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{B}(\Geod(M))
,
by Theorem
A.5
and Proposition
A.4

for every
i=1,2,\dots
there exist
B_{i}^{(1)},B_{i}^{(2)}
both Borel sets in
M\times M

such that

B_{i}^{(1)}\subset S^{-1}(A_{i})\subset B_{i}^{(2)}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\rho(B_{i}^{(2)}\backslash B_{i}^{(1)})=0.

If we take

B:=\bigcup_{i=1}^{\infty}(B_{i}^{(2)}\backslash B_{i}^{(1)}),

then
\rho(B)=0
and
S
is measurable if restricted to
M\times M\backslash B
.∎

A.5.
Young sub-Riemannian superposition

We follow here the notation introduced in
[
9
]
. Any transport
measure
\eta
with
\mu=\pi_{\#}\eta
is called a
generalized curve
if

\mu_{t}
is a dirac measure for each
t\in I
. In particular, there exists
\omega\colon I\to M

continuous such that
\mu_{t}=\delta_{\omega(t)}
. The set of generalized curves
is denoted by
\mathcal{G}(I,M)
and is closed inside
\mathcal{T}((0,1);TM)
, the
set of transport measures on
M
.

Moreover, for any generalized curve
\nu
, there exists an absolutely
continuous curve
\omega
such that
\nu
is a curve above
\omega

and there exists a Borel family
\nu_{t}
of probabilities on
T_{\omega_{t}}M
for a.e.
t\in I
,
such that

(A.1)

\nu=\mathrm{d}t\otimes\delta_{\omega_{t}}\otimes\nu_{t}.

In
[
9
, Lemma 8]
it is proved
that

(A.2)

\int_{T_{\omega_{t}}M}v\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}(v)=\dot{\omega}_{t}\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\ t\in I.

Viceversa, in order for (
A.1
) to define a generalized
curve above
\omega
, it is necessary and sufficient that

•

t\mapsto\int_{T_{\omega_{t}}M}\lVert v\rVert\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}
is Lebesgue integrable on
I

•

(
A.2
) is verified.

Remark A.7
.

If
\eta
is a transport measure, then the
defining Continuity Equation can be formulated as

\int_{I}\int_{TM}\left(\partial_{t}\phi+v\phi\right)\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=0\quad\forall\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}((0,1)\times M)

and the sub-Riemannian setting appears only
with the additional condition that
\eta
must be concentrated on
HM\subset TM
.
Therefore, the results of
[
9
, pp.5-6]
may be used in our setting, without any
further generalization.

We denote by
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
the set of horizontal generalized curves,
meaning that
\nu\in\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
if
\nu\in\mathcal{G}(I,M)
and moreover

\nu
is above an absolutely
continuous curve
\omega
such that
\dot{\omega}_{t}
lies
in the horizontal distribution
H_{\omega_{t}}M
for a.e.
t\in I
.

If
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}(I,TM)
is a transport measure,
we know from
[
9
, Theorem 19]
, that there exists unique (up to a negligible set)

\widetilde{\eta}
a Borel measure on
\mathcal{G}(I,M)
such that

(A.3)

\eta=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\nu\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}(\nu).

The measure
\widetilde{\eta}
is called a
decomposition
of
\eta
.

In order to state the superposition principle, we
consider the projection

\mbox{pr}\colon\mathcal{G}(I,M)\to C(I,M)

that to any generalized curve above
\omega
associates the curve
\omega
.
By
[
9
, Theorem 9]
, this map is continuous. Moreover, we consider
the map
e_{t}\colon C(I,M)\to M
sending
\gamma\mapsto\gamma(t)
.

Considering the results in the same work by Bernard, by
[
9
, Proposition 20]

we have that for every transport measure
\eta
concentrated on
I\times HM\subset I\times TM
,
if
\widetilde{\eta}
is a decomposition then
\widetilde{\eta}
-a.e. generalized curve is concentrated on
I\times HM
.
By (
A.2
) this means that
\widetilde{\eta}
-almost every generalized curve is horizontal, or equivalently
that
\widetilde{\eta}
is concentrated on
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
. Indeed, if
\nu\in\mathcal{G}(I,M)
is a generalized curve
above
\omega\in C(I,M)
,and is concentrated
on
I\times HM
, this means that

\dot{\omega}_{t}=\int_{T_{\omega_{t}}M}v\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}\in H_{\omega_{t}}M\quad\mbox{for a.e.}\ t\in I.

We resume this result and
[
9
, Proposition 21]
from the same article by Bernard, in the following statement.

Theorem A.8

(Young superposition principle in sub-Riemannian setting)
.

If
\eta
is a transport measure concentrated on the horizontal distribution,
i.e.
\eta\in\mathcal{T}((0,1);(M,H))
, then a decomposition

\widetilde{\eta}
of
\eta
is concentrated on the set of (generalized) admissible curves
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
and

(e_{t}\circ\mbox{pr})_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}=\mu_{t}=\pi_{\#}\eta_{t}.

A.6.
Compactness result

Lemma A.9
.

If
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
are two probability measures of compact support,
then there exists a compact
K\subset M
with

\supp(\mu_{0})\subset K,\ \supp(\mu_{1})\subset K,

such that for any
\eta\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)
there exists

\overline{\eta}\in\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)

with
J^{\star}_{\BB}(\overline{\eta})\leq J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)
and

\pi_{\#}\overline{\eta}(I\times K)=1.

Equivalently, we can say that all the mass is always inside the set
K
.

Proof.

We denote by
\widetilde{\eta}
a decomposition
of
\eta
, we know that it is concentrated on
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
because of Theorem
A.8
.

We denote by
\overline{d}
the maximum distance between two points

x_{0}\in\overline{\supp(\mu_{0})}
and
x_{1}\in\overline{\supp(\mu_{1})}
.
We choose
K
sufficiently big for any admissible curve from
\supp(\mu_{0})

to
\supp(\mu_{1})
that exits from
K
to be longer than
M
.
For example, we set

K:=\left\{x\in M:\ d(x,\overline{\supp(\mu_{0})})\leq\overline{d},\ d(x,\overline{\supp(\mu_{1})})\leq\overline{d}\right\}.

Consider the measurable
subset
A\subset\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
of the curves
“exiting” from
K
, defined by

A:=\left\{\nu\in\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M):\ \nu(I\times K^{c})>0\right\}.

We are going to
build a map
\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)\to\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)

that sends the decomposition
\widetilde{\eta}

to a measure
\widetilde{\eta}^{*}
that decomposes a
\overline{\eta}\in\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)

with the properties indicated above.

We consider the Borel map
S\colon M\times M\to\Geod(M)
from Theorem
A.6

and we recall that it is defined
(\mu_{0}\otimes\mu_{1})
-a.e.
From this we can
build the map
F\colon M\times M\to\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)

such that

(x,y)\mapsto\mathrm{d}t\otimes\delta_{S_{t}(x,y)}\otimes\delta_{\partial_{t}S_{t}(x,y)},

that is the generalized curve concentrated on the tangent vectors to the (sub-Riemannian)
geodesic between
x
and
y
. Observe that if
\nu\in A
and
\omega=\mbox{pr}(\nu)
,
is an admissible curve of length
\ell(\omega)
exiting from
K
,
then

(A.4)

\int_{I}\lVert\dot{\omega}_{t}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\geq\ell(\omega)^{2}\geq\ell(S(\omega_{0},\omega_{1}))^{2}=\int_{I}\lVert\partial_{t}S_{t}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t,

where we used the minimization property of geodesics.

We can introduce the measurable map
m\colon\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)\to\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)

such that

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}m|_{A}&=F\circ(e_{0},e_{1})\circ\mbox{pr}\\
m|_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}&=\id.\end{array}\right.

If we consider
\widetilde{\eta}^{*}:=m_{\#}\widetilde{\eta}
we get a modified version
of the decomposition
\eta
, concentrated on curves that do not exit
K
.

We define

\overline{\eta}:=\int_{\mathcal{G}}\nu\,\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}^{*}(\nu),

therefore
\overline{\eta}
is a transport measure in
\mathcal{Y}_{\mu_{0}}^{\mu_{1}}(I;HM)

and
\widetilde{\eta}^{*}
is a decomposition of
\overline{\eta}
.
It remains to prove
that
J^{\star}_{\BB}(\overline{\eta})\leq J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)
.
Using the defintion (
A.3
) of decomposition, we develop
the following calculations where again we use the notation
\omega=\mbox{pr}(\nu)
,
meaning that
\nu
is a generalized curve above the continuous curve
\omega
.

\displaystyle J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)=\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v_{t}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\eta

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu(v)\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}

(by (
A.1
))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}

(by convexity)

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{A}\int_{I}{\left|\left|\int_{H_{\omega_{t}}M}v\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}\right|\right|}^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}

(by (
A.2
))

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{A}\int_{I}\lVert\dot{\omega}_{t}\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}

(because of (
A.4
))

\displaystyle\geq\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{A}\int_{I}\lVert\partial_{t}S_{t}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}\backslash A}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}+\int_{\overline{A}}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}^{*}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{G}_{H}}\int_{I\times HM}\lVert v\rVert^{2}\mathrm{d}\nu\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\eta}^{*}=J^{\star}_{\BB}(\overline{\eta})

where we used the notation
\overline{A}
for the image
\overline{A}:=F\circ(e_{0},e_{1})\circ\mbox{pr}(A)\subset\mathcal{G}_{H}(I,M)
.∎

Lemma A.10
.

Consider
c
a real constant,
K\subset M
a compact subset and
\mu_{0},\mu_{1}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)

with compact supports,
then the set

\mathcal{N}(c,K):=\{\eta:\ J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)\leq c,\ \pi_{\#}\eta(I\times K)=1\}

is compact in
\mathcal{Y}^{\mu_{1}}_{\mu_{0}}(I;HM)
.

Proof.

We consider a point
x_{0}\in M
and the distance function
x_{0}\mapsto d(x,x_{0})
.
Then, we consider the integral

(A.5)

\int_{I\times HM}d(x_{0},\pi(v))+\lVert v\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v).

We want to show that it has bounded value
for
\eta\in\mathcal{N}(c,K)
.
The first term is constant on any fiber of the map
\pi\colon HM\to M
, therefore

(A.6)

\int_{I\times HM}d(x_{0},\pi(v))\,\mathrm{d}\eta(t,v)=\int_{I\times M}d(x_{0},x)\,\mathrm{d}\mu_{t}\mathrm{d}t,

which is finite because
\pi_{\#}\eta=\mu
is concentrated on a compact set
by hypothesis.
Furthermore, the term
\int\lVert v\rVert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\eta
equals
J^{\star}_{\BB}(\eta)
and is also bounded by hypothesis.

Therefore for every
\eta\in\mathcal{N}(c,K)
the integral (
A.5
)
is bounded by a constant depending on
x_{0}
,
\supp(\mu_{0})
,
\supp(\mu_{1})
and
c
.
Moreover
the integrand function

v\mapsto d(\pi(v),x_{0})+\lVert v\rVert^{2}

has compact
level sets in
HM
.
By
[
18
, Corollary 3.61]
, in the case of sets of Young measures
this implies the thightness of
\mathcal{N}(c,K)
(see also
[
18
, Remark 3.53-(i)]
), which in turn implies
the
compactness of
\mathcal{N}(c,K)
because
\mathcal{P}_{2}(M)
is Polish with respect to the Wasserstein distance
for any Polish space
(M,d)
, and thigtness is equivalent to compactness on Polish spaces.∎

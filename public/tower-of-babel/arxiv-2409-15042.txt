A discrete de Rham discretization of interface diffusion problems with application to the Leaky Dielectric Model
Daniele A. Di Pietro, Simon Mendez, Aurelio E. Spadotto

Abstract
Motivated by the study of the electrodynamics of particles, we propose in this work an arbitrary-order discrete de Rham scheme for the treatment of elliptic problems with potential and flux jumps across a fixed interface. The scheme seamlessly supports general elements resulting from the cutting of a background mesh along the interface. Interface conditions are enforced weakly à la Nitsche . We provide a rigorous convergence of analysis of the scheme for a steady model problem and showcase an application to a physical problem inspired by the Leaky Dielectric Model. Key words: Leaky Dielectric Model, interface diffusion problems, discrete de Rham methods, polytopal methods, weakly enforced interface conditions MSC2020: 78M10, 65N30, 35J15

Introduction

The study of the dynamics of particles in an electric field is motivated by numerous applications. Subjecting cells to an electric field is indeed a non-invasive and label-free method to obtain a high-throughput characterization of individual cells
[
29
]
. A classical device is the Coulter counter
[
11
]
, developed in the 1950s and still used in blood analyzers to count and size red blood cells
[
30
]
using a DC field. Nowadays, the use of AC fields combined with the versatility of microfluidics make
single-cell microfluidic impedance cytometry a promising technique for multiparametric cell characterization
[
21
]
.
Other processes in which cells are subjected to an electric field include dielectrophoresis, a common technique used to manipulate or deform cells
[
20
]
, and electroporation, in which pores are created in the membrane to enable exchange of molecules between the internal and the suspending media
[
25
]
.
From a more fundamental point of view, subjecting simpler particles like drops or vesicles to electrical fields has revealed fascinating dynamics when the internal and the external fluids have different conductivity and permittivity
[
33
]
: symmetry-breaking (rotation of the particle), large transient deformations with sharp edges for vesicles…

Motivated by the problem of the deformation of a drop in a steady electric field
[
31
]
, G. I. Taylor introduced the so-called Leaky Dielectric Model, in which bulk fluids are assumed to be free of charge, so that the coupling between between electric and mechanical phenomena occurs at the interface. Since then, the Leaky Dielectric Model has been shown to provide valuable theoretical insights in the field of electrohydrodynamics of particles
[
26
,
33
]
.
In the context of this model, the electrostatic potential is a scalar-valued field which is harmonic in the bulk while, on the droplet interface, fulfills conditions that express the continuity of current through the membrane as well as a drop in potential called
Galvani potential

[
24
]
.
When seeking numerical solutions to this problem, tracking the position of the interface by means of an adapted mesh can be challenging and impact on the cost of the simulation.
This calls for solutions where the mesh is not redesigned after each displacement of the membrane.

The literature on numerical methods for interface problems is vast, and we will limit ourselves here to works that bear relations with the present approach.
A large number of methods rely on a background mesh which is not compliant with the interface, and are therefore referred to as
unfitted
.
In the Generalized/Extended Finite Element method
[
28
,
27
]
, non-polynomial functions with compact support are added to the discrete space in order to capture the behavior at the interface.
In the Immersed Finite Element method
[
1
]
, the added functions are piecewise polynomials.
In the CutFEM method
[
6
]
, interface conditions are taken into account by using discontinuous basis functions inside the elements cut by the interface and relying on Nitsche’s techniques for their enforcement.
The CutFEM principles have been recently extended to the Hybrid High-Order method
[
8
,
7
]
, which supports much more general element geometries than standard Finite Elements; see
[
17
,
15
,
14
]
.

In the present work, we consider a method based on meshes obtained by cutting elements crossed by the interface.
No special treatment (such as the addition of ad hoc functions) is required for discretization of the diffusion operator on the elements resulting from the cut.
This is because the discretization in the bulk hinges on the
H^{1}
-like space of
[
18
]
, which supports general polygonal or polyhedral meshes.
As a result, our method enjoys both the assets of unfitted methods (since cuts can occur at arbitrary locations, making the approximation of the interface totally independent of the background mesh) and of fitted methods (since interface elements do not require a special treatment).
The support of general polytopal elements additionally provides an effective means to counter the possible degradation of mesh quality resulting from the cuts, since pathological elements can be merged into neighbors in the spirit of
[
4
,
2
,
23
]
; see also
[
22
]
for an application of these ideas to conforming finite elements.
Interface conditions are enforced weakly through subtly defined terms that ensure consistency; see Remark
5
for further insight into this point.
The design of such terms, based on the use of trace reconstructions, is indeed one of the main contributions of the present work.
Robustness with respect to the jumps of the diffusion coefficient is obtained by using diffusion-dependent weighted averages in the spirit of
[
9
,
16
]
.
To keep the exposition as simple as possible, we focus here on the two-dimensional case with interfaces approximated by closed polygonal chains and consider numerically the case of curved interfaces.

The rest of this work is organized as follows.
In Section
2
we state the continuous problem.
The discrete setting (mesh, polynomial spaces) is introduced in Section
3
.
In Section
4
we describe the construction leading to the discrete problem and state the scheme as well as the main results of the analysis.
A comprehensive set of numerical tests is carried out in Section
5
, while the application to the Leaky Dielectric Model makes the object of Section
6
.
Finally, the proofs of the stability and error estimates results stated in Section
4.6
are provided in Section
7
.

3
Discrete setting

3.1
Mesh

We discretize the domain with a polygonal mesh
(\mathcal{T}_{h},\mathcal{E}_{h})
in the sense of
[
14
, Definition 1.4]
, with
\mathcal{T}_{h}
collecting the mesh elements and
\mathcal{E}_{h}
the mesh edges.
We additionally denote by
\mathcal{V}_{h}
the set of vertices collecting the edge endpoints.
The mesh is assumed to be fitted to the interface, i.e., there exists a subset
\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
of
\mathcal{E}_{h}
such that
\Gamma=\bigcup_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\overline{E}
.

Remark 1

(Fitted mesh)
.

It is important to emphasize that fitted polytopal meshes supported by the present method can simply be obtained cutting the elements of a background mesh along the interface, as in the numerical tests of Sections
5
and
6
.
This is a major advantage, particularly in the framework of moving interface problems, as it avoids a potentially expensive remeshing step.
We also notice that the degradation of mesh quality can also be countered leveraging the support of polytopal elements: whenever an elongated or distorted element results from the cutting, it can be agglomerated into neighboring elements in the spirit of
[
4
,
2
,
23
]
to restore mesh quality.

Remark 2

(Approximation of the interface)
.

The approximation of the interface is totally independent of the background mesh.
This is important when considering the more general case of curved interfaces, where a finer discretization can be needed.
Notice that this can lead to polygonal elements with asymptotically small edges.
In the spirit of
[
19
]
, it can be shown that this kind of elements can be supported by the present method; see also
[
10
]
on this subject.

For
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}
, we let
\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\bullet}\coloneq\left\{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\;:\;T\subset\Omega_{\bullet}\right\}
.
For any element
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we denote by
\mathcal{E}_{T}
the set collecting its edges.
Symmetrically, the set of elements sharing one edge
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{E}
.
For each edge
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we fix once and for all a unit normal vector
n_{E}
and, for any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{E}
, we denote by
\omega_{TE}\in\{-1,1\}
the relative orientation of
E
with respect to
T
, such that
\omega_{TE}n_{E}
points out of
T
.

In what follows, we assume that the mesh we are working on belongs to a regular sequence in the sense of
[
14
, Definition 1.9]
.
Given a mesh element or edge
X\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}
, we denote by
h_{X}
its diameter, so that
h=\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}h_{T}
.
We will abbreviate by
a\lesssim b
the inequality
a\leq Cb
with
C
independent of
h
,
\sigma
and, for local inequalities, the corresponding element or edge.
Further details on the dependence of the hidden constants will be provided when appropriate.

3.2

Given
X\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\cup\mathcal{E}_{h}
and an integer

\mathcal{P}^{m}(X)

-orthogonal projector.
We conventionally set

For all

\mathcal{R}^{{\rm c},m}(T)\coloneq(x-x_{T})\mathcal{P}^{m-1}(T),

where
x_{T}
is a point inside

at a distance from its boundary comparable to

.
It can be proved that the divergence from

to
\mathcal{P}^{m-1}(T)
is an isomorphism; cf.
[
3

.

Discrete problem

Discrete space

For any

\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}

\underline{V}_{\bullet,h}^{k}\coloneq\begin{aligned} \Big\{\underline{v}_{h}=\big((v_{T})_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\bullet}},(v_{E})_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}^{\bullet}},(v_{V})_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{h}^{\bullet}}\big)\;:\;&\text{$v_{T}\in\mathcal{P}^{k-1}(T)$ for all $T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\bullet}$,}\\

\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\coloneq\underline{V}_{\mathrm{int},h}^{k}\times\underline{V}_{\mathrm{ext},h}^{k}.

as well as its subspace
\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
with edge and vertex values vanishing on

T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, we respectively denote the restrictions of

,

4.2
Element gradient and potential

For any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, any
\underline{v}_{T}\in\underline{V}_{T}^{k}
, and any
E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}
, we let the edge potential
v_{TE}
be the unique function in
\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(E)
such that

v_{TE}(x_{V})=v_{V}
for any endpoint
V

E
and
\pi^{k-1}_{E}v_{TE}=v_{E}
.

Remark 3

(Edge potential)
.

Clearly,
v_{TE}\equiv 0
whenever
E\subset\partial\Omega
is a boundary edge and
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
.
On the other hand, when
E\subset(\partial T_{1}\cap\partial T_{2})\setminus\Gamma
is an internal edge that does not lie on the interface, the value of the edge potential does not depend on the element, i.e.,
v_{T_{1}E}=v_{T_{2}E}
.

We define the discrete gradient
G_{T}^{k}:\underline{V}_{T}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{2}
and potential
p_{T}^{k+1}:\underline{V}_{T}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(T)
such that, for all
\underline{v}_{T}\in\underline{V}_{T}^{k}
,

\displaystyle\int_{T}G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\cdot\tau=-\int_{T}v_{T}(\nabla\cdot\tau)+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}v_{TE}(\tau\cdot n_{E})\qquad\forall\tau\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{2},

\displaystyle\int_{T}p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}(\nabla\cdot\tau)=-\int_{T}G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\cdot\tau+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}v_{TE}(\tau\cdot n_{E})\qquad\forall\tau\in\mathcal{R}^{{\rm c},k+2}(T).

(3)

Remark 4

(Validity of (
3
))
.

Following
[
18
, Remark 17]
, the relation (
3
) actually holds for all
\tau\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{2}+\mathcal{R}^{{\rm c},k+2}(T)
.

Accounting for the previous remark, we notice that, integrating by parts the left-hand side of (
3
) and rearranging, we have, for all
(\underline{v}_{T},\tau)\in\underline{V}_{T}^{k}\times(\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{2}+\mathcal{R}^{{\rm c},k+2}(T))
,

\int_{T}\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}\cdot\tau=\int_{T}G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\cdot\tau+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})(\tau\cdot n_{E}).

(4)

Selecting
\tau=\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}
(this is possible since
\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}\in\mathcal{P}^{k}(T)^{d}
) in the above expression, using Cauchy–Schwarz,
(2,\infty,2)
-Hölder, and trace inequalities along with
\|n_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(E)^{2}}\leq 1
in the right-hand side, and simplifying, we get

\|\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}\lesssim\left(\|G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\|p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}.

(5)

Let
v\in H^{r+2}(T)
for some

and set
\widehat{\underline{v}}_{T}\coloneq\underline{I}_{T}^{k}v
.
Using the techniques of
[

]
, where the three-dimensional case is considered, it can be proved that

\displaystyle\|\widehat{v}_{TE}-v\|_{L^{2}(E)}\lesssim h_{T}^{r+\nicefrac{{3}}{{2}}}|v|_{H^{r+2}(T)}\qquad\forall E\in\mathcal{E}_{T},

(6)

\displaystyle\|G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{v}}_{T}-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}+h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{v}}_{T}-\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\partial T)^{2}}\lesssim h_{T}^{r+1}|v|_{H^{r+2}(T)},

(7)

\displaystyle\|p_{T}^{k+1}\widehat{\underline{v}}_{T}-v\|_{L^{2}(T)}+h_{T}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|p_{T}^{k+1}\widehat{\underline{v}}_{T}-v\|_{L^{2}(\partial T)}\lesssim h_{T}^{r+2}|v|_{H^{r+2}(T)}.

(8)

For future use, we also define the global discrete gradient operator
G_{h}^{k}:\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k}(\mathcal{T}_{h})^{2}
such that, for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h}^{k}

(G_{h}^{k}\underline{v}_{h})_{|T}\coloneq G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

4.3
Interface trace operators

Let
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
and denote by
T_{\mathrm{int}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}}
and
T_{\mathrm{ext}}\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{ext}}
the unique elements such that
E\subset\partial T_{\mathrm{int}}\cap\partial T_{\mathrm{ext}}
.
Notice that, while such elements clearly depend on

, we do not highlight this dependency in the notation as it will be clear from the context.
We define the edge jump
[\cdot]_{E}:\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(E)
and skewed average
\{\cdot\}_{\overline{k},E}:\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\to\mathcal{P}^{k+1}(E)
operators such that, for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h}^{k}
,

[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\coloneq v_{T_{\mathrm{int}}E}-v_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}E},\qquad\{\underline{v}_{h}\}_{\overline{k},E}\coloneq\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}v_{T_{\mathrm{int}}E}+\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}v_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}E},

(9)

with
\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}
and
\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}
such that

\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}\coloneq\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}},\qquad\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}\coloneq\frac{\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}}.

(10)

We additionally let, for any vector-valued field
\Psi
smooth enough to admit a possibly two-valued trace on

,

\{\Psi\}_{k,E}\coloneq\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}\gamma_{T_{\mathrm{int}}E}\Psi+\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}\gamma_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}E}\Psi,

(11)

where
\gamma_{T_{\bullet}E}\Psi
denotes the trace of
\Psi_{|T_{\bullet}}
on

.

4.4
Discrete problem

Let

\sigma_{T}\coloneq\sigma_{|T}\qquad\forall T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}.

We define the bilinear form
a_{h}:\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\times\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
and the linear form
\ell_{h}:\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
(\underline{w}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})\in\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\times\underline{V}_{h}^{k}
,

\displaystyle a_{h}(\underline{w}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left(\int_{T}\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\underline{w}_{T}\cdot G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}+\frac{\sigma_{T}}{h_{T}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{E}(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{w}_{T}-w_{TE})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})\right)

(12)

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}+\eta\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\int_{E}[\underline{w}_{h}]_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E},

where

\alpha\coloneq\frac{2\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}}{\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}+\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}}

(13)

and

\ell_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}fp_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\Phi_{\Gamma}\{\underline{v}_{h}\}_{\overline{k},E}+\eta\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\int_{E}J_{\Gamma}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}.

(14)

The discrete problem reads:
Find
\underline{u}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
such that

a_{h}(\underline{u}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})=\ell_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})\qquad\forall\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}.

(15)

Remark 5

(Formulation of the interface terms)
.

It is worth noticing that the scheme above is not a simple extension of discontinuous Galerkin techniques to the interface problem of Section
2
with the element potential playing the role of the discontinuous solution inside trace operators.
On the contrary, such operators use the edge potential in a subtle way, which is required for optimal order consistency; see, in particular, the passages leading to (
29
) in the proof of Lemma
11
below.

4.5
Energy norm and interface jump seminorm

In order to state the main results of the theoretical analysis of the method, we define on
\underline{V}_{h}^{k}
the energy norm
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm en},h}
such that, for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h}^{k}
,

\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{{\rm en},h}^{2}\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sigma_{T}\left(\|G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}^{2}+h_{T}^{-1}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\|p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}\right)+|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}^{2},

(16)

where the interface jump seminorm is such that

|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}^{2}\coloneq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\|[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}^{2}.

(17)

Proposition 6

(Energy norm)
.

The map
\|\cdot\|_{{\rm en},h}
defines a norm on
\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
.

Proof.

\|\cdot\|_{{\rm en},h}
is clearly a seminorm, so we only have to prove that, for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
,
\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{{\rm en},h}=0
implies
\underline{v}_{h}=\underline{0}
.
The condition
\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{{\rm en},h}=0
implies:
(i) for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
,
G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}=0
and
p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}=v_{TE}
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}
and
(ii) for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
,
[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}=0
.
By (
5
), point (i) implies, in turn, that
p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}
is constant on each
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Since
v_{TE}=0
whenever
E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}
is a boundary edge contained in
\partial\Omega
and
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
is the unique mesh element to which it belongs,
p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}=0
.
Proceeding from
\partial\Omega
towards the interior of
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
, this gives
p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}=0
for all
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{ext}}
and
v_{TE}=0
for all
E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}
.
These two conditions combined show that all element, edge, and vertex components of
\underline{v}_{h}
in
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
vanish.
Since interface jumps vanish as well by point (ii) above, the edge and vertex components of
\underline{v}_{h}
on the interface
\Gamma
from the side of
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
are zero.
The same reasoning as for
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
can therefore be applied (proceeding from the interface
\Gamma
towards the interior of
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
) to show that all elements, edge, and vertex values of
\underline{v}_{h}
in
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
vanish, thus concluding the proof.
∎

6
Application to the Leaky Dielectric Model

In this section we discuss a version of problem (
2
) where the interface jump
J_{\Gamma}
is time dependent and obeys an evolution equation depending on the interface gradient of
u
.

6.1
Continuous setting

Given a final time
t_{\rm f}>0
, a source term
f:(0,t_{\rm f}]\to\mathbb{R}
, and an initial potential jump
J_{\Gamma}^{0}
, we consider the problem of finding the time-dependent potential
u=(u_{\mathrm{int}},u_{\mathrm{ext}})
with
u_{\bullet}:\Omega_{\bullet}\times(0,t_{\rm f}]\to\mathbb{R}
,
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}
and the interface jump

J_{\Gamma}:\Gamma\times[0,t_{\rm f}]\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle-\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{\bullet}\nabla u_{\bullet})

\displaystyle=f

\displaystyle\text{in $\Omega_{\bullet}\times(0,t_{\rm f}]$, $\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}$},

(22a)

\displaystyle[u]_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=J_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma\times(0,t_{\rm f}]$},

(22b)

\displaystyle[\sigma\nabla u]_{\Gamma}\cdot n_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle=\Phi_{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma\times(0,t_{\rm f}]$},

(22c)

\displaystyle u_{\mathrm{ext}}

\displaystyle=0

on
\partial\Omega\times(0,t_{\rm f}]

(22d)

\displaystyle C\partial_{t}{J_{\Gamma}}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\bullet}\nabla u_{\bullet}\cdot n_{\Gamma}

on
\Gamma\times(0,t_{\rm f}]
,
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}

(22e)

\displaystyle J_{\Gamma}(\cdot,0)

\displaystyle=J_{\Gamma}^{0}

\displaystyle\text{on $\Gamma$},

(22f)

with
C>0
.
Problem (
22
) models a situation where two media with electric conductivity respectively equal to
\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}
and
\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}
occupy the regions
\Omega_{\mathrm{int}}
and
\Omega_{\mathrm{ext}}
.
The interface
\Gamma
between the two media is characterized by a capacitance
C
.
The variation rate of the charge in the bulk is
f
and the interface supports a surface charge
\Phi_{\Gamma}
.
The region
\Omega_{\bullet}
with
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}
is characterized by an electrostatic potential
u_{\bullet}
to determine.
The potential is discontinuous at the interface, with a jump
J_{\Gamma}
to determine, and vanishes on the boundary of
\Omega
.

6.2
Discrete problem

To adapt the scheme (
15
) to problem (
22
), it is necessary to introduce a time stepping scheme and a suitable discrete space to describe the new variable
J_{\Gamma}
.
For the sake of simplicity, we describe the adaption in the case of
k=0
.
Consider
N\geq 1
time steps with duration
\tau=\nicefrac{{t_{\rm f}}}{{N}}
.
For any time-dependent variable
w
, we introduce the set of time-independent variables
\{w^{n}\}_{n\leq N}
such that
w^{n}(x)=w(x,n\tau)
.
An explicit Euler scheme is adopted to replace (
22e
) with:

\frac{C}{\tau}\big(J_{\Gamma}^{n+1}-J_{\Gamma}^{n}\big)=\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}\nabla u^{n}_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot n_{\Gamma}=\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}\nabla u^{n}_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot n_{\Gamma}.

The equation is integrated along
\Gamma
after multiplying by a test function
Q_{\Gamma}^{n}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}
:

\frac{C}{\tau}\left(\int_{\Gamma}J_{\Gamma}^{n+1}Q_{\Gamma}^{n}-\int_{\Gamma}J_{\Gamma}^{n}Q_{\Gamma}^{n}\right)=\int_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}\nabla u^{n}_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot n_{\Gamma})Q_{\Gamma}^{n}=\int_{\Gamma}(\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}\nabla u^{n}_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot n_{\Gamma})Q_{\Gamma}^{n}.

Consider the collection of
interface vertices
\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma,h}
and introduce the space
of interface variables:

\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}\coloneq\Big\{\underline{p}_{\Gamma,h}=(p_{V})_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma,h}}\;:\;p_{V}\in\mathbb{R}\quad\forall V\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma,h}\Big\}.

Given
\underline{p}_{\Gamma,h}\in\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}
and
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
, define
p_{E}\in\mathcal{P}^{1}(E)
as the unique affine function that takes the value
p_{V}
at every endpoint
V
of
E
.
Equip
\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}
with the following norm:

\|p_{\Gamma,h}\|_{0,\Gamma,h}^{2}\coloneq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\|p_{E}\|_{E}^{2}.

We set
\underline{J}_{\Gamma,h}^{0}\coloneq(J_{\Gamma}^{0}(x_{V}))_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{\Gamma,h}}
, with
x_{V}
denoting the coordinate vector of the vertex
V
and, for
n=0,\ldots,N-1
, we advance in time solving the following problem:
Find
\underline{J}^{n+1}_{\Gamma,h}\in\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}
such that

\frac{C}{\tau}\bigg(\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}J_{E}^{n+1}Q_{E}^{n}-\int_{E}J_{E}^{n}Q_{E}^{n}\bigg)=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{T}\underline{u}_{T}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{\Gamma}Q_{E}\qquad\forall\underline{Q}^{n}_{\Gamma,h}\in\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}.

(23)

Given
\underline{J}_{\Gamma,h}^{n}\in\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}^{k}
,
denote by
\mathcal{M}_{h}:\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}^{k}\to\underline{V}_{h}^{0}
the operator
that associates to a jump
\underline{J}_{\Gamma}^{n}
the solution
\underline{u}_{h}

of the stationary problem (
15
). Likewise, call

\mathcal{N}_{h}:\underline{V}_{h}^{0}\times\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}\to\underline{Z}_{\Gamma,h}
the operator
that associates to a potential
\underline{u}_{h}
and a jump

\underline{J}_{\Gamma,h}^{n}
the jump
\underline{J}_{\Gamma,h}^{n+1}

solution to problem (
23
).
Then, the time advancement algorithm for the case of an evolving
jump reads:
Given
\underline{J}^{0}_{\Gamma,h}
, for
n=0,\ldots,N-1
, set, in this order,

\displaystyle\underline{u}^{n}_{h}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}_{h}(\underline{J}_{\Gamma}^{n}),

\displaystyle\underline{J}^{n+1}_{\Gamma,n}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{N}_{h}(\underline{u}_{h},\underline{J}^{n}_{\Gamma,h}).

7.1
Proof of Lemma
7

We recall the following discrete trace inequality valid for any integer

, any
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, and any
E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}
:
For all
\varphi\in\mathcal{P}^{m}(T)
,

\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(E)}\leq C_{\rm tr}h_{E}^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(T)},

(25)

where
C_{\rm tr}
only depends on
m
and the mesh regularity parameter.

Lemma 10

(Estimate of the consistency interface term)
.

Let
N_{\partial}\coloneq\max_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\operatorname{card}(\mathcal{E}_{T}\cap\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h})
.
Then, for all
(\underline{w}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})\in\underline{V}_{h}^{k}\times\underline{V}_{h}^{k}
and any real number
\epsilon>0
, it holds

\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\leq\epsilon\|\sigma^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C_{\rm tr}^{2}N_{\partial}}{4\epsilon}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}^{2}.

(26)

Proof.

Let
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
.
Using a
(2,\infty,2)
-Hölder inequality, we can write

\displaystyle\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\leq\|\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}\|n_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(E)^{2}}\|[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:avg}}{}&\leq\alpha^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}\right)~\alpha^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)},\end{aligned}

where, in the second equality, we have additionally used the fact that
\|n_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(E)^{2}}\leq 1
.
Noticing that, by definition (
10
) of
\lambda_{\bullet}
and (
13
) of
\alpha
, and since
\lambda_{\bullet}<1
,

\alpha^{-\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\lambda_{\bullet}\sigma_{\bullet}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}
,
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}
, we can go on writing

\displaystyle\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &<\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}+\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}\right)~\alpha^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}\\
\overset{\eqref{eq:trace},\,\eqref{eq:seminorm.J}}{}&\leq\frac{C_{\rm tr}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{\mathrm{int}})^{2}}+\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{\mathrm{ext}})^{2}}\right)~\left(\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}\|_{L^{2}(E)}.\end{aligned}

Summing the above inequality over
E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}
, using a Cauchy–Schwarz inequality along with the fact that
(a+b)^{2}\leq 2(a^{2}+b^{2})
, and recalling the definition (
17
) of
|\cdot|_{{\rm J},h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm tr}\left[\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\left(\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{\mathrm{int}})^{2}}^{2}+\|\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}^{k}\underline{w}_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}\|_{L^{2}(T_{\mathrm{ext}})^{2}}^{2}\right)\right]^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rm tr}N_{\partial}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\|\sigma^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\|\sigma^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}G_{h}^{k}\underline{w}_{h}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C_{\rm tr}^{2}N_{\partial}}{4\epsilon}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}^{2},

the conclusion being a consequence of the generalized Young’s inequality.
∎

Lemma 11

(Estimate of the consistency error)
.

Assume
u\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}_{\mathrm{int}})\times C^{0}(\overline{\Omega}_{\mathrm{ext}})
and define the consistency error linear form
\mathcal{E}_{h}:\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}\to\mathbb{R}
such that, for all
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
,

\mathcal{E}_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})\coloneq\ell_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})-a_{h}(\underline{I}_{h}^{k}u,\underline{v}_{h}).

Then, provided that
u\in H^{r+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{int}})\times H^{r+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h}^{\mathrm{ext}})
for some
r\in\{0,\ldots,k\}
, it holds

\sup_{\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}\setminus\{\underline{0}\}}\frac{\mathcal{E}_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})}{\|\underline{v}_{h}\|_{{\rm en},h}}\lesssim\overline{\sigma}h^{r+1}|u|_{H^{r+2}(\mathcal{T}_{h})},

(27)

where
\overline{\sigma}\coloneq\max\{\sigma_{\mathrm{int}},\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}\}
,
and the hidden constant depends only on the domain, the stability constant
C_{\rm stab}
in (
19
), the polynomial degree
k
, and the mesh regularity parameter (but is independent of both the meshsize and
\sigma
).

Proof.

Let
\underline{v}_{h}\in\underline{V}_{h,0}^{k}
.
We reformulate the components of the consistency error
\mathcal{E}_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})
in order to make them comparable.
Throughout the proof we let, for the sake of brevity,
\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}\coloneq\underline{I}_{h}^{k}u
.

1.
Reformulation of
\ell_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})
.

Recalling (
2a
),
f=-\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{\bullet}\nabla u_{\bullet})
almost everywhere in
\Omega_{\bullet}
,
\bullet\in\{\mathrm{int},\mathrm{ext}\}
.
We can therefore write for the first term in the right-hand side of (
14
):

\displaystyle\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}fp_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\nabla\cdot(\sigma_{T}\nabla u)p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}

(28)

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot n_{E})p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot n_{E})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})

\displaystyle-\underbrace{\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot n_{E})v_{TE}}_{\mathfrak{T}},

where we have used an integration by parts inside each element in the second equality and inserted
\pm v_{TE}
into the boundary term to conclude.
Let us focus on the last term. Rearranging the sums, we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{h}}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{E}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot n_{E})v_{TE}

\displaystyle=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\left[(\sigma_{T_{\mathrm{int}}}\nabla u_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot n_{E})v_{T_{\mathrm{int}}E}-(\sigma_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}}\nabla u_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot n_{E})v_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}E}\right],

where we have used the fact that the normal trace of
\sigma\nabla u
is continuous across mesh edges internal to each subdomain along with the fact that
v_{TE}=0
on edges contained in
\partial\Omega
in the second equality.
We next notice that, given four real numbers
a_{1}
,
a_{2}
,
b_{1}
, and
b_{2}
, since
\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}+\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}=1
(cf. (
10
),

a_{1}b_{1}-a_{2}b_{2}=(\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}a_{1}+\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}a_{2})(b_{1}-b_{2})+(a_{1}-a_{2})(\lambda_{\mathrm{ext}}b_{1}+\lambda_{\mathrm{int}}b_{2}).

Applying this formula with
(a_{1},a_{2},b_{1},b_{2})=(\sigma_{\mathrm{int}}\nabla u_{\mathrm{int}}\cdot n_{E},\sigma_{\mathrm{ext}}\nabla u_{\mathrm{ext}}\cdot n_{E},v_{T_{\mathrm{int}}E},v_{T_{\mathrm{ext}}E})
,
recalling the definitions of the interface trace operators of Section
4.3
,
and using the fact that, by (
2c
),
[\sigma\nabla u]_{E}\cdot n_{E}=\Phi_{\Gamma}
almost everywhere on
\Gamma
, we infer that

\mathfrak{T}=\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma\nabla u\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}+\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\Phi_{\Gamma}\{\underline{v}_{h}\}_{\overline{k},E}.

Plugging this expression into (
28
) and substituting into the expression (
14
) of
\ell_{h}
, we arrive at

\displaystyle\ell_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})

\displaystyle=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}\nabla u\cdot n_{E})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})

(29)

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma\nabla u\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}+\eta\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\int_{E}J_{\Gamma}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}.

2.
Reformulation of
a_{h}(\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})
.

Let
T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
.
Writing (
4
) for
\tau=\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}
and rearranging, we obtain

\int_{T}\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\cdot G_{T}^{k}\underline{v}_{T}=\int_{T}\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\cdot n_{E})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE}).

Substituting this expression in the definition (
12
) of
a_{h}
written for
\underline{w}_{h}=\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}
, we obtain

\displaystyle a_{h}(\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h},\underline{v}_{h})

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}(\sigma_{T}G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\cdot n_{E})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})

(30)

\displaystyle+\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{\sigma_{T}}{h_{T}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{E}(p_{T}^{k+1}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}-\widehat{u}_{TE})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE})

\displaystyle-\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma G_{h}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}+\eta\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{\alpha}{h_{E}}\int_{E}[\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}]_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}.

3.
Estimate of the consistency error.

Subtracting (
30

29

\mathcal{E}_{h}(\underline{v}_{h})=\mathfrak{T}_{1}+\cdots+\mathfrak{T}_{5}

(31)

with

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\int_{T}\sigma_{T}(\nabla u-G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T})\cdot\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T},

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\omega_{TE}\int_{E}\sigma_{T}(G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}-\nabla u)\cdot n_{E}(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{3}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\frac{\sigma_{T}}{h_{T}}\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{T}}\int_{E}(p_{T}^{k+1}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}-\widehat{u}_{TE})(p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}-v_{TE}),

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{4}

\displaystyle\coloneq\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\int_{E}\{\sigma(G_{h}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}-\nabla u)\}_{k,E}\cdot n_{E}[\underline{v}_{h}]_{E}

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{5}

Using Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities along with the fact that
\sigma_{T}\leq\overline{\sigma}
for all

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\overline{\sigma}^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\|\nabla u-G_{T}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}\left(\sum_{T\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\sigma_{T}\|\nabla p_{T}^{k+1}\underline{v}_{T}\|_{L^{2}(T)^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}

-Hölder inequalities on the integrals and Cauchy–Schwarz inequalities on the sums and recall the definition (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{T}_{4}\leq\left(\sum_{E\in\mathcal{E}_{\Gamma,h}}\frac{h_{E}}{\alpha}\|\{\sigma(G_{h}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}-\nabla u)\}_{k,E}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}|\underline{v}_{h}|_{{\rm J},h}

\displaystyle\alpha^{-1}\|\{\sigma(G_{h}^{k}\widehat{\underline{u}}_{h}-\nabla u)\}_{k,E}\|_{L^{2}(E)^{2}}^{2}

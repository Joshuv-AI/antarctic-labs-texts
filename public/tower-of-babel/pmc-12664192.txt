Computational development of multi-epitope vaccine to induce adaptive immunity against multi-drug resistant Prevotella intermedia
Authors: Ayesha Kanwal, Mohibullah Shah, Muhammad Umer Khan, Muneeba Latif, Hira Anum, Sonia Younas, Alanoud T. Aljasham, Suvash Chandra Ojha
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Prevotella intermedia is a gram-negative, anaerobic, multidrug-resistant bacterium known to cause oral infections. Given its clinical significance as an opportunistic pathogen, developing effective preventive and therapeutic measures is crucial. This study employed an integrated approach, combining subtractive proteomics and immunoinformatics, to identify novel therapeutic targets. We analyzed the core genome of P. intermedia, revealing five essential proteins as potential drug targets and three as potential candidates for a vaccine. The selected vaccine candidate proteins underwent additional evaluation to determine their immunogenic epitopes (B-cell, MHC-I, and MHC-II) and the design multi-epitope vaccine incorporating suitable linkers and adjuvants. Structural validation, including the identification of globular regions and post-translational modifications (PTMs), was performed. Molecular docking with the TLR4 receptor demonstrated strong interactions, and molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, dynamic cross-correlation matrix (DCCM) analysis, binding free energy calculations, and principal component analysis (PCA) confirmed stable binding between the vaccine prototypes and TLR4. Among the four proposed vaccines, we identified HBHA-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, and GMCSF-PiMEV as most effective, with sustained interactions to human immune receptors and docking scores of −1110·2cal/mol, −1016·1 kcal/mol, and −1149·6 kcal/mol, as well as 15, 27, and 16 hydrogen bonds respectively. Based on different evaluation parameters, HBHA-PiMEV was identified as the most effective vaccine construct. Additionally, the novel drug targets were annotated for their roles in key biological processes, underscoring their therapeutic value. In summary, our investigation identified novel therapeutic targets and designed potent vaccine constructs against P. intermedia for further experimental validation to combat this multidrug-resistant pathogen.Supplementary informationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12879-025-12105-9.

Introduction

Prevotella intermedia is a gram-negative, obligate anaerobe characterized by black-pigmentation anaerobic bacterium [1], and is a prevalent cause of periodontal disease and periapical periodontitis. It is commonly isolated from subgingival plaque in individuals with periodontitis and is one of the orange microbial complexes [2]. B. melaninogenicus subsp. Intermedius The bacterium P. intermedia is classified as one of the three subspecies of Bacteroides melaninogenicus, which include subsp.intermedius, subsp. melaninogenicus, and subsp. Asaccharolyticu [3]. Bacteroides melaninogenicus includes darkly-pigmented, gram-negative anaerobes exhibiting comparable biochemical characteristics. Following extensive analyses in immunology and biochemistry, it was reclassified into two distinct genera, Porphyromonas and Prevotella, with P. intermedia classified within the latter group.

P. intermedia is primarily known to infect humans, as it is a component of the human microbiota, particularly in the oral cavity [4]. It is a common pathogen of oral illnesses such as periodontal or periapical disorders. Periodontitis is a common inflammatory condition that has increased in global prevalence and incidence between 1990 and 2019 [5]. There were an estimated 1.1 billion cases of severe periodontitis in 2019 [6]. Periodontitis remains a global public health issue; however, increasing dental hygiene reduces the bacterial load of P. intermedia, lowering the risk of periodontal disease and its sequelae [7].

P. intermedia has been linked to several disorders, including knee osteoarthritis [8], neurological diseases [9, 10], respiratory diseases [7, 11], and circulatory diseases [12–14]. P. intermedia infections cause swollen, bleeding gums and foul breath in periodontal illnesses, neurological impairments, and systemic problems such as aspiration pneumonia.

The pathogenicity of P. Intermedia is closely associated with its virulence factors, which include pili, extracellular membrane vesicles, capsules, elastase, acid and alkaline phosphatases, endotoxin, trypsin-like serine proteases [15], dipeptidyl peptidases [16], immunoglobulin proteases (IgA and IgG), and other proteases that hydrolyze lipids, proteins, gelatin, and ribonucleic acids.

P. intermedia is resistant to antibiotics commonly used in therapeutic settings, including amoxicillin [17, 18], tetracycline [18], and penicillin. Furthermore, resistance genes like tetQ have been identified, complicating treatment efforts [19]. Given the intricacy of P. intermedia-related illnesses and their antibiotic resistance, novel therapeutic techniques are needed. Currently, there are no periodontal vaccine trials that have yielded positive outcomes; therefore, lacking an approved vaccine for P. intermedia [20]. Due to the escalating problem of antibiotic resistance, developing new therapeutic techniques, such as vaccines, is important.

Previous studies have presented a computational approach for the rational development and design of multi-epitope vaccine constructs against P. intermedia. That study primarily focused on the reverse vaccinology approach by selecting a single target protein, Interpain A, from P. intermedia [21]. In contrast, our study integrates subtractive proteomics and reverse vaccinology. Moreover, its novelty lies in the comprehensive evaluation of the designed vaccine constructs, including discontinuous B-cell epitope prediction, molecular dynamics (MD) simulations, immune simulations, PTMs analysis, normal mode analysis (NMA), in silico cloning, and prediction of disordered and globular regions.

Reverse vaccinology and subtractive proteomics represent powerful strategies for vaccine development and drug target identification, and have been successfully applied to a range of pathogenic bacteria [22–24]. The availability of genomic and proteomic data for P. intermedia has enabled the identification of essential, virulent, and druggable targets critical to the pathogen’s survival, which are vital for therapeutic intervention. We integrated these methods to develop a computational engineered multi-epitope vaccine construct and identify innovative drug targets against P. intermedia. The final vaccine construct were developed by integrated adjuvants, linkers, and B- and T-cell epitopes to reduce toxiicity and allergenicity while eliciting a strong and effective immune response.

Genome data retrieval

The complete genome sequences of the 14 available P. Intermedia strains were retrieved from the National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) database. These sequences were analyzed using the EDGAR tool version 2.0 [25] to identify and formulate the core genome.

Identification of human non-homologs

The identification of human host non-homologous proteins was achieved by performing a standalone BLASTp analysis against species-specific non-paralogous proteins, employing previously used criteria: query coverage < 35, bitscore < 100, percent identity < 35, and E-value of 1e-20 [24, 26]. Subsequent examination of the pathogen-derived proteins was analyzed in relation to the human proteome and gut metagenome downloaded from Uniprot and NCBI, respectively. BLASTp was performed using the parameters: E-value of 1e-4, bitscore < 100, percent identity < 50%, and query coverage < 35%, as described in prior investigation [24, 26, 27],. The pathogen proteins homologous to these databases were excluded from further analysis.

Identification of the pathogen’s essential and virulent proteins

The essential proteins of the pathogen were discovered by comparing to the Database of Essential Genes (DEG) [28] that incorporates all genes required for the survival of pathogen. The Virulence Factor Database (VFDB) [29] was exploited to determine virulence determinants that influence the development and progression of disease via invasion, colonization, adhesion, and host defense evasion. These pathogen essential proteins were evaluated using BLASTp against these databases with a bitscore ≥ 100 and E-value ≤ 1e-4 [27].

Subcellular localization

Subcellular localization prediction is essential for the identification of prospective vaccine and drug targets. The subcellular localization of the selected proteins was determined using PSORTb v3.0 [30], CELLO v2.5 [31], and BUSCA [32]. We employed these tools to validate and ensure the reliability of the results obtained from each method. The predictions categorized proteins into various localization classes, including cell wall proteins, extracellular proteins, cytoplasmic proteins, cytoplasmic membrane proteins, and proteins classified as anonymous due to the absence of significant localization information.

Vaccine candidate analysis

The potential vaccine candidates were assessed by using the Vaxijen v2.0 [33] and AllerTop v2.1 web servers [34] to predict antigenic and non-allergenic protein candidates. Proteins having topology 0 or 1 were identified using the TMHMM [35] 2.0 server. The physicochemical attributes involving molecular weight, theoretical Pi, query length, aliphatic index, instability index, half-life, and the grand average of hydropathicity (GRAVY) value were identified via the ProtparamExpasy server [36]. For epitope prediction, non-allergenic and antigenic proteins with a negative GRAVY score along with topology of 0 or 1 were selected.

Epitope selection and screening

MHC-I and MHC-II epitope prediction

In order to design an effective vaccine, antigenic and non-allergenic epitopes were predicted. Antigenic protein molecules have unique regions (epitopes) that can interact with immune cell receptors, triggering an immune reaction. Epitope prediction was performed on antigenic proteins. The Immune Epitope Database (IEDB) was used to predict potential T-cell epitopes [37]. For MHC-I and MHC-II epitope determination, the analysis was based on established HLA allele references to ensure that the identified epitopes covered a diverse global representation of HLA. Employing the lowest rank values, the top ten epitopes were determined [38]. IEDB was used to determine MHC-II binding epitopes, which were ranked based on percentile scores. Lower rank values represented a strong binding affinity between the predicted epitopes and MHC molecules [39]. The resulting epitopes were analyzed to evaluate their toxicity, allergenicity, antigenicity and immunogenic competency.

B-cell epitope prediction

B-cell epitopes corresponds to crucial for inducing immunological responses mediated by antibodies during multi-epitope vaccine development. Linear B-cell epitopes were predicted using the BepiPred Linear Epitope Prediction 2.0 tool available in the IEDB Analysis Resource (IEDB-AR) [40], recognized for its efficacy and accuracy. B-cell epitopes having 10 to 40 amino acid residues were prioritized [41] and evaluated for their toxicity, antigenicity, and allergenicity.

Epitopes docking

Reverse vaccinology extensively utilizes molecular docking to evaluate the interactions between predicted T-cell epitopes and MHC alleles. To facilitate the interaction between T-cell epitopes along with MHC alleles, sequences were retrieved and designed using the trRosetta server [42]. After, the top-ranked T-cell epitopes with high antigenicity were subjectedto docking analysis. Subsequently, the highest-ranked T-cell epitopes exhibiting strong antigentic potentials were analyzed through molecular docking. PyRx software facilitated the docking of T-cell epitopes with MHC alleles, and the interactions were visualized illustrated using PyMOL.

Multi-epitope vaccine model

MEVC assembly

The MHC-I, MHC-II, and B-cell epitopes were joined using suitable linkers to construct a multi-epitope vaccine. HTL epitopes were assembled through the incorporation of the “GPGPG linker,” while CTL epitopes were joined together using the “AAY linker.” The “KK linker” was employed to integrate the LBL epitopes. Four adjuvants, including Heparin-binding hemagglutinin (HBHA), beta-defensin, granulocyte macrophage colony, and L7/L12 ribosomal protein, were incorporated into the final construct. LBL, CTL, and HTL epitopes to form four vaccine constructs. PADRE peptide sequence, having 13 amino acids (i.e., AKFVAAWTLKAAA), was also used, which help to provoke CD4+ T cells [26].

MEVC validation

The ProtparamExpasy server (Gasteiger et al., 2005a) was utilized to perceive the molecular weight, theoretical Pi, GRAVY score, the total number of amino acids, instability index, and the aliphatic index within the designed multi-epitope vaccine constructs. Comparably, the Vaxijen v2.0 server, which has a preconfigured threshold ( > 0.4), was used to compute its antigenicity profile. The AllergenFp v1.1 server [43] was used to evaluate the allergenicity, and the SOLpro server [44] was used to estimate solubility during heterologous expression into the host. At > 0.5, the optimum solubility overexpression level was sustained.

Determination of secondary and tertiary structure, 3D validation

The secondary structural composition of the designed vaccine construct was predicted utilizing the SOPMA server [45], that analyzes structural features including α-helices, β-sheets, turns, and random coils. For 3D structure determination, the trRosetta tool was used, employing distance and orientation restraints to predict the protein structure [46]. The ERRAT [47] and PROCHECK [48] quality-checking programs were run via the UCLA-DOE Lab - SAVES v6.1 website.

Conformational B-cell epitope prediction

After generating the 3D structures of the proposed vaccine constructs, conformational B-cell epitopes were predicted using the ElliPro tool available on the IEDB server, representing a crucial step in vaccine design [49]. ElliPro employs three techniques focused on protrusion index (PI) values were utilized to approximate the protein’s structure as an ellipsoid, assess the pI of residues, and identify nearby cluster residues [50]. The vaccines’ 3D models in PDB format were uploaded, and all default settings were used for epitope prediction.

Population coverage analysis

Assessment of population coverage represents a critical aspect in the rational design of a broad-spectrum epitope-based chimeric vaccine. The population coverage of the selected CTL and HTL epitopes was evaluated using the population coverage analysis tool available on the IEDB server [51]. HLA Class I and II alleles exhibit distinct expression patterns and distribution sequences across various ethnic communities and geographic regions [52]. The association among the screened epitopes and the highest number of MHC alleles (both type I and II) submitted to the tool was examined to determine the proportion of populations that might respond to each epitope.

Prediction of post-translational modifications

Post-translational modifications (PTMs) significantly increase the array of the proteome are critical in regulating protein stability and functional activity [53]. The NetPhos v3.1 web server was used to identify and predict potential threonine, serine, and tyrosine phosphorylation sites [54]. NetOGlyc v4.0 and NetNGlyc v1.0 web servers [55] were used to identify potential N-linked and O-linked glycosylation sites.

Globular and disordered regions

Gloplot 2.3 [56] was employed to predict globular or disordered regions within the proposed vaccine sequences. GlobPlot is an online tool that enables the user to display the propensity within the query protein sequence for globularity/order and disorder. Protein disorder is defined as the absence of a standard secondary structure and a high degree of flexibility in the polypeptide chain. Ordered regions are commonly referred to as globular, and they usually comprise regular secondary structures compacted into a tight globule.

Molecular docking

Molecular docking was employed to evaluate putative interactions among the receptor HLA molecules and the vaccine. For this purpose, the designed vaccine construct was docked with the Toll-like receptor TLR4 through molecular docking analysis (PDB ID: 4G8A). Before docking, the structures must be prepared to eliminate any water molecules, co-crystallized ligands, and other ions that may exist. The structures were subsequently protonated by introducing H+ ions, and the energy was minimized. These structures were saved as PDB files and subsequently uploaded to the ClusPro 2.0 server for protein-protein docking analysis. The ClusPro 2.0 uses a Fourier correlation technique to filter docking models based on electrostatic and desolvation energies [57]. The binding affinities of all docked complexes were determined, and their interactions were visualized and studied using PyMOL. Furthermore, the bond length measurements were calculated using PyMOL. Bond length is inversely related to bond strength and dissociation energy. It shows interaction strength by specifying the distance between C-C, C-O, and C-H atom bonds.

Normal mode analysis

The IMODS server was employed to simulate the molecular stability of the MEV–immune receptor complexes under physiological conditions, maintaining a constant temperature of 300 K and a pressure of 1 atm, utilizing molecular mechanism-based analysis. The protein-protein complex stability was assessed using a molecular dynamic modeling technique. In the current investigation, the IMODS server was employed to stimulate the molecular stability of the MEV-immune receptor complexes under physiological conditions, maintain a constant temperature of 300 K and a pressure of 1 atm, utilizing molecular mechanisms [58]. Protein stability is estimated by evaluating essential dynamics with typical modes. The iMODS provides a platform for NMA (normal mode analysis), which aids in the evaluation of typical protein mobility within internal coordinates [58]. Additionally, the server calculated intrinsic movements utilizing B-factors, covariance, deformability, and eigenvalues. The deformability of the main chain of a vaccine candidate protein is determined as a molecule deforming at each of its residues. The normal mode value indicates motion stiffness, accompained by lower eigenvalues indicating simple bend arrangements relative to the energy needed for such deformations.

Molecular dynamic simulation

The AMBER20 package was employed to execute molecular dynamics simulations of top vaccine-receptor complexes (HBHA-PiMEV-TLR4, L7/12-PiMEV-TLR4, and GMCSF-PiMEV-TLR4). The “ff14SB” force field was utilized to parameterize the TLR4 protein and each vaccine combination [59]. To simulate an aqueous environment, each molecular system was solvated in a TIP3P water box with a padding distance of 12 Å. The systems were neutralized with Na+ ions, and optimization techniques were adopted [60]. The simulated systems were progressively equilibrated through a heating process to 300K over 20 ps, then stabilized. Under the NPT ensemble, each molecular complex was subjected to a 50 ns production simulation. AMBER CPPTRAJ was used to perform the MD trajectory analysis. To check binding stability, root mean square deviation (RMSD) studies were performed on all three vaccine-TLR4 complexes [61]. Similarly, root-mean-square-fluctuation (RMSF) studies were used to determine the adaptability of amino acid residues in the TLR4 protein for each complex [62]. Solvent-accessible surface area (SASA) studies were conducted to evaluate modifications in TLR4 solvent accessibility after binding with each vaccine. The average SASA values indicated the vaccine molecules’ structural stability and compactness [63]. In addition, each complex’s radius of gyration (Rg) was measured to establish its compactness. Increased Rg profiles showed decreased stiffness in all three systems [62]. The hydrogen bonds produced among the vaccines and TLR4 were examined to determine the stability of the three complexes.

Binding free energy calculation

To compute the binding free energies of the prioritized docked complexes, Molecular Mechanics Passion-Boltzmann Surface Area (MM-PBSA) was employed with the MM-PBSA in AMBER20. Energy calculations were performed using 1000 frames selected at typical time intervals from the simulation trajectories. Principal component analysis

Principal component analysis (PCA) was executed to dominate the structural dynamics and internal motions of the three vaccine-TLR4 complexes. The Cα atomic positions from the MD trajectories were analyzed using AMBER20. For each system, a diagonalized covariance matrix was generated, producing eigenvectors and eigenvalues. The primary principal component (PC1) accounted for the collective motion of the system, whereas PC2 corresponded to the secondary domainate motion. The analysis revealed collective motions and structural flexibility in all three complexes, with decreasing eigenvalues indicating the extent of dominant motions [64].

Immune simulation

C-IMMSIM server was utilized to determine the potential immunological responses generated by the intended vaccine formulations [66]. This computational platform emulates both innate and adaptive immune responses by modeling the activation and generation of various immune cell populations. For our analysis, we limited the maximum simulation duration to 1000 steps. Furthermore, we changed the time steps for providing booster doses to 84 for the second booster and 168 for the third booster. All other simulation parameters were set to default [67]. This thorough technique allowed us to measure immune response levels and evaluate the vaccine’s potential efficacy.

In silico cloning and codon adaptation

The Exoptimizer tool [68] was employed to implement and improve the efficiency of the codons of the engineered vaccine for decleration in the E. coli strain K12, selected for its reliability and production efficiency. The codon adaptation index (CAI) and GC content were evaluated to verify the efficiency and stability of the vaccine gene’s expression at the translational level [69]. To enhance vaccine expression, elements such as bacterial ribosome binding sites, restriction enzyme cleavage sites, and Rho-independent transcription terminators were avoided. Using the SnapGene restriction cloning tool, the optimized codon sequence was incorporated into the pET28a (+) plasmid vector at the PashA1 restriction site for the expression of vaccine protein within the E. coli K12 prokaryotic system [70]. Before cloning, the insert was examined for the existence of restriction sites. Upon confirmation of the restriction sites, enzyme-specific recognition sequences were incorporated at the N-terminal and C-terminal regions of the vaccine gene insert to facilitate cloning. A high CAI score indicated optimal expression levels in E. coli.

Novel drug target proteins analysis

To find novel drug target proteins, the selected cytoplasmic proteins were subjected to BLASTp analysis against FDA-approved drug targets accompained by a bitscore of at least 100 and an E-value of < 0.005 [71]. Proteins that were similar to FDA-approved targets were excluded from the investigation to forecast novel targets. Using the STRING database v12.0 [72], the remaining proteins were assessed for protein-protein interactions with an average node degree > 5.0. The TMHMM 2.0 server was utilized to anticipate transmembrane helices in the proteins. Furthermore, the physicochemical properties were predicted employing the ProtParam tool [73].

Tertiary structure and druggable binding pockets prediction

The SWISS-MODEL server was used to predict the three-dimensional structures of the prioritized targets. The ERRAT score and Ramachandran plot were analyzed utilizing the SAVES v6.1 server to validate the predicted structures. Following the prediction of three-dimensional structures, the DoGSiteScorer, an automated pocket recognition technology for determining whether protein cavities are druggable, was used to identify drug-binding pockets and associated with their druggability score. A score near 1 indicates a highly druggable protein cavity.

Results

Genome data retrieval

The protein datasets from 14 complete genomes of P. intermedia were retrieved from the NCBI database and analyzed using the EDGAR server to construct the core genome. We identified 1553 core proteins in this analysis, which were further utilized for subtractive proteomics and reverse vaccinology analysis.

Pathogen unique proteins determination

Bacterial proteins were subjected to homology filtering as an integral part of the screening process to exclude human host homologs and to identify novel targets. After removing proteins homologous to the host proteins, 1,519 candidates were identified through assessing against the human proteome database. Gut microbiota, which persists as a commensal organism within the human host throughout life, plays a crucial role in several metabolic processes such as development, defense systems, homeostasis, and physiological functions. To minimize the risk of potential adverse effects within the host, key bacterial proteins were further scanned against the human gut microbiome using standalone BLASTp. From this step, we selected a total of 294 proteins for further investigation.

Identification of essential and virulent proteins

The DEG provides comprehensive information on protein essentiality derived from experimental study. Essential proteins were chosen because they are quite similar to the proteins found in DEG. The 294 non-homologous gut proteins from the prior step were filtered against the DEG using BLASTp with a 35% sequence identity, yielding 52 proteins that were determined to be essential. Furthermore, infectious diseases are induced by protein-protein interactions between the pathogen and the host. This includes molecular interactions between hosts and pathogenic organisms. Understanding these proteins could lead to the discovery of more efficient therapeutic targets for infectious illnesses. BLASTp analysis identified 6 virulent homologs in the VFDB database. As a result, we identified 58 P. Intermedia proteins as relevant and investigated them further.

Prediction of subcellular localization

To facilitate subcellular localization of optimal vaccine targets and enhance the development of therapeutic agents, subcellular localization analysis was performed as a critical step. A total of 58 pathogen-specific, non-homologous, essential, and virulent proteins were encountered to this predictive analysis (Table S1). Prediction of lead epitopes

T-cells are categorized based on MHC class I and MHC class II, each recognizing specific epitopes. Using the IEDB analysis resource, MHC-I and MHC-II epitopes were predicted from the WP_028904996.1 protein. From these, we selected the top-ranked epitopes from each class based on their scores for further evaluation. Among these, three MHC-I and three MHC-II epitopes demonstrate the greatest immunogenicity, antigenicity, and non-allergenicity and were ultimately selected for vaccine development. The BepiPred Linear Epitope Prediction 2.0 tool from IEDB-AR was utilized to determine B-cell epitopes. We finalized 6 linear B-cell epitopes that were antigenic, virulent, non-toxic, and non-allergenic, for vaccine formulation (Tables S2, S3, S4).

Vaccine formulation

We created multi-epitope vaccine sequences using 12 predicted epitopes potentially identified by CTL, HTL, and B cells. They have high antigenicity, non-allergic characteristics, and are non-toxic. These epitopes were connected using several linkers, including GPGPG, AAY, KK, and EAAAK. Linkers were employed to reduce the development of junctional epitopes and enhance epitope presentation during immunization. PADRE sequence, HBHA adjuvant, beta defensin, granulocyte macrophage colony, and L7/L12 ribosomal protein adjuvant were incorporated into the targeted epitopes’ N-terminal to enhance the multi-epitope vaccine’s immunogenicity. Four different adjuvants were used as each one has its own significance. The adjuvant beta-defensin functions as both an antibacterial and an immunomodulator [74]. As a potent, immunological adjuvant, HBHA can produce a high Th1 cell immune response contributes significantly to the exploration and development of multi-epitope vaccine strategies for immunotherapy [70]. Granulocyte-macrophage colony-stimulating factor (GM-CSF) boosts immunological responses by promoting dendritic cell proliferation, migration, and maturation, as well as B and T lymphocyte expansion and differentiation [75]. The 50S ribosomal L7/L12 will increase vaccine efficacy and prolong the immunological response [76]. The PADRE sequence is substantially more effective than conventional T helper epitopes and can combat variations in HLA-DR molecules between individuals. All vaccines with the PADRE elicited greater CTL responses. For optimal adjuvant-MEV interaction, the lead epitopes, linkers, and adjuvants were coupled so that the protein components remained stable, structurally intact, and detached effectively. Modeled vaccine analysis

Four multi-epitope vaccine were immunologically assessed and anticipated to be safe and effective. The antigenicity score of the constructed vaccine entities indicated strong immunity and substantial antigenic potential. The molecular weights of formulated vaccines ranged from 27.52 to 39.99 kDa, and their hydrophilic nature was determined by computed GRAVY scores between −0.475 and −0.265. The aliphatic index values range from 63.58 to 78.74, suggesting the thermal stability and flexibility of the model vaccines throughout a broad range of temperatures. The anticipated solubility of the vaccine after overexpression ranged between 0.72 and 0.88 (above the 0.5 threshold). The theoretical Pi values, ranging from 6.71 to 9.6, suggest that vaccines have a basic nature. The instability index for all constructs was 40, confirming in vitro stability. In MD simulations, the Root Mean Square Deviation (RMSD) is a fundamental parameter evaluating the stability, convergence, and ongoing behavior of molecular systems. Its importance originates from its ability to assess structural alterations, analyze equilibration, and validate simulation outcomes. The root mean square deviation (RMSD) is a commonly utilized metric in molecular dynamics (MD) simulations to assess the stability and structural alterations of a system, notably the locations of atoms or molecules over time. It is frequently employed to determine the extent to which a molecule’s or complex’s structure diverges from a reference structure (such as the initial configuration) throughout the simulation.

Vaccines work by introducing antigens into the body, triggering an immune response [79]. Similarly, administering the MEV against P. intermedia may activate antigen-presenting cells like dendritic cells, which process and facilitate antigens on MHC molecules. These APCs traffic to lymph nodes, where they activate naïve T cells via T cell receptors and co-stimulatory molecular interactions Activated T cells differentiate into memory and effector T cells, indicating helper and cytotoxic T cells. Helper T cells signal B cells to generate antibodies by plasma cells and form memory B cells [80]. Optimization of codon and in silico cloning

The Exoptimizer tool was used to optimize the HBHA-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, and GMCSF-PiMEV vaccine codons to assess their maximum protein expression. The resulting optimized codons of HBHA-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, and GMCSF-PiMEV had CAI values of 0.73, 0.79, and 0.71, with an estimated GC content of 57.59%, 60.18%, and 55.86%. These results indicate a stable vector expression in E. coli. The optimized vaccine sequences were cloned into the pET-28a (+) expression vector at the FspAI (2205) restriction site using the SnapGene tool. The entire lengths of the pET-28a (+) vector containing the vaccine sequences were identified to be 6409, 6140, and 6140 bp (Figure S3).

Novel drug target prediction

A total of 55 cytoplasmic proteins were evaluated against FDA-approved drugs, resulting in 5 novel drug targets. Physicochemical analysis of these 5 proteins was performed to identify promising targets by determining their molecular weights, protein-protein interactions, amino acid lengths, and membrane-spanning helices. Proteins having molecular weights around 110 kDa, a helix composition of 0 or 1, and fewer than 800 amino acid lengths are regarded to be good therapeutic targets due to their ease of purification and solvent accessibility. The STRING v12.0 database assisted in understanding the functional links between proteins. Hub proteins were identified as those with an average node degree value (K) of more than five. After selecting and filtering proteins with the techniques listed above, five novel drug targets were identified and structurally analyzed: WP_014709521.1, WP_014710018.1, WP_021672826.1, WP_024998468.1, and WP_028905216.1. (Figure S4 and Table S8).

Discussion

P. intermedia, a major periodontal pathogen, is associated with both localized and systemic health issues [77]. Addressing the challenges posed by this pathogen requires a two-pronged approach: preventive measures through vaccine development and therapeutic interventions via drug discovery. Traditional approaches frequently encounter challenges such as extended timetables, expensive prices, and limited target specificity. To overcome these challenges, this study focused on identifying therapeutic targets within P. intermedia’s core proteome and designing a broad-spectrum multi-epitope vaccine to boost innate and adaptive immunity. We utilized the core proteome as it comprises proteins found in all strains of the target pathogen. These proteins are gaining significance in therapeutic applications, as they induce protective immune response across a wider range of species [79]. After detailed analysis, one candidate protein for the vaccine and three for unique drug targets were obtained.

Proteins targeted for vaccines are excellent, given their presence in membrane and extracellular domains where they likely serve as the first contact points with host cells [79]. After evaluating several parameters, thiamine pyrophosphate-dependent enzyme (WP_028904996.1) single protein candidate was chosen for the construction of a chimeric vaccine. Bacterial outer membrane proteins serve as the pathogen’s first point of interaction with host cells, making them ideal candidates for reverse vaccinology approaches targeting invasive pathogens [80]. These proteins are critical for bacterial survival and pathogenicity because they are involved in fundamental processes like nutrition absorption, host tissue attachment, and membrane integrity. Their important role in the bacteria’s life cycle, as well as their propensity to contribute to disease progression, makes them attractive vaccine targets [85]. Antigenic areas of protein found using the immunoinformatic approach are crucial in triggering both B and T cell immunity, while non-antigenic regions are discarded during epitope mapping. Overlapping epitopes for T and B cells were selected as the optimal candidates for the formulation of chimeric subunit vaccine. We created these constructs with a variety of adjuvants and PADRE sequences to improve immunological reactions and overcome the obstacles associated with HLA variation in distinct ethnicities [86].

The SOPMA and trRosetta servers were used to determine the secondary and tertiary structures of the formulated vaccines. The ERRAT score was utilized to assess the quality of 3D [87]. Four vaccine prototypes (HBHA-PiMEV, β-defensin-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, and GMCSF-PiMEV) were docked with the human TLR4 receptor, and three of them (HBHA-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, and GMCSF-PiMEV) were chosen due to their superior docking performance, as evidenced by lower docking scores and a greater number of interactions, as observed using PyMOL. TLR4 is an important immunological receptor that recognizes pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs) from viruses, fungi, and mycoplasmas, as well as endogenous ligands resulting from tissue injury. Its significance in a variety of clinical disorders makes it an attractive therapeutic target [88]. PyMOL was employed to assess bond lengths between vaccine prototypes and TLR4 residues, which revealed important information about their binding kinetics and interaction processes. The integrity of the docked complexes was further confirmed through MD simulations, PCA analysis, MMPBSA calculations, and DCCM analysis. The stability of the proposed MEV against P. Intermedia was validated by Normal Mode Analysis (NMA), which demonstrated minor deformability, indicating a stable engagement with the target receptor. The vaccines were shown to have extensive global population coverage, reaching 99.26% of the population, suggesting that they have the potential to be used internationally. PTM analysis revealed important phosphorylation, O-glycosylation, and N-glycosylation sites required for protein functional activity. The structural analysis of the modeled vaccine highlighted the relevance of globular regions in allowing direct contact with immunogenic cells, which may result in a robust immune response. On the other hand, disordered regions allow the vaccines to adjust the immune system’s surroundings. Immune simulations utilizing the C-IMMSIM server revealed strong immunogenic activation, which might support the potential efficacy of vaccines [89]. In silico cloning shows that the prioritized vaccines may be expressed more effectively in host organisms, such as E. coli. This work proposes vaccine formulations that may elicit protection against P. intermedia infections and offers valuable insights regarding potential vaccine candidates. However, despite the existence of numerous advanced computational servers and tools for the detection of protein antigenicity, allergenicity, and immunogenicity, none achieve complete accuracy; thus, to confirm these findings, it is recommended to express the potential vaccines in a bacterium for further research.

Of the 3 novel drug targets, the omega subunit of DNA-directed RNA polymerase (WP_014710018.1) protein serves a structural function by stabilizing the conformation of the largest subunit, β′, and facilitating its incorporation into the enzyme complex. The fact that its function is conserved across all kinds of life illustrates its importance [90]. Ribosomal proteins L10 (WP_014709521.1) make up the large ribosomal subunit (50S) stalk of bacteria with L12 protein. The L12 C-terminal domain has been demonstrated to attract the elongation factors EF-G and EF-Tu to the stalk, which enhances GTPase activity [91, 92]. A ribosomal stalk that lacks L12 cannot interact with elongation factors [93]. L10‘s C-terminal α-helix binds two or three L12 dimers by associating with the N-terminal domains of L12. Disrupting the L12–L10 relationship hinders the binding of EF-G and EF-Tu to the stalk, causing the loss of ribosomal GTPase function [94]. Nucleotidyltransferases (WP_028905216.1) are template-independent polymerases that add ribonucleotides to the terminals of RNA molecules. There are 12 known nucleotidyl transferases in humans, 7 of which have been linked to isomiR biogenesis. Remarkably, these enzymes exhibit adenyltransferase and/or uridyltransferase activities, aligning with observations that adenylation and uridylation represent the predominant 3′-end modifications of isomiRs [95]. Their involvement in these functions makes them attractive drug target proteins. Druggability analysis highlights 3 novel proteins as promising candidates for experimental validation, paving the way for alternative therapeutic strategies against this bacterium.

Previous studies predicting possible vaccine or drug targets for P. intermedia have primarily focused on limited evaluation. Yadalam et al. [21] formulated a single multi-epitope vaccine against the target protein Interpain A. In contrast, we performed a comprehensive analysis on the available complete genomes of this pathogen and developed three multi-epitope vaccines. The comprehensive evaluation of these vaccines through many phases MD simulations, immune simulations, globular and disordered regions, PTM analysis, NMA, and in silico cloning) determines the stability of the vaccines and their potential to combat P. intermedia infections. Mathivanan et al. [96] identified two enzymes 3-deoxy-D-mannooctulosonate cytidylyltransferase and 3-deoxy-D-mannooctulosonate 8-phosphate phosphatase, in P. Intermedia strain 17, using the KEGG pathway and subcellular localization analysis. The enzymes are involved in Lipopolysaccharide Biosynthesis Pathway, which is a unique pathway for Gram-negative bacteria. In our study, we performed physicochemical analysis, STRING-based functional annotation, and drug pocket screening to propose three novel drug targets of P. Intermedia. These comprehensive analyses suggest that these proteins may lay a solid platform for future drug designing against P. intermedia.

Potential vaccination candidates (PVCs) are discovered more quickly using reverse vaccinology before they are validated experimentally. However, reverse vaccinology has some limitations such as filtering techniques may exclude possible antigens due to the accuracy limitations of the prediction tools employed. The selection of training datasets comprising experimentally validated protective antigens (positive controls) and non-protective antigens (negative controls) is a critical determinant for the performance of machine learning–based prediction models. Numerous studies [97–99] have demonstrated successful in vivo validation of computationally designed vaccine epitopes, highlighting the efficacy of computer-assisted methodologies in expediting the development process. Individuals with the necessary resources can examine immunogenicity by adhering to the methodologies which encompass the purification of recombinant protein, the incorporation of a His-tag, and the execution of ELISA for the evaluation of IgG responses in human serum. Additionally, cytokine assays, evaluations of humoral immune responses, bladder challenge assays in infected mice, and mouse immunization may all be employed to determine vaccine efficacy. The development, distribution, and utilization of vaccines should adhere to the ethical norms of beneficence, fairness, non-maleficence, and autonomy before these studies commence, assuring the preservation of individual rights, the promotion of justice, and the enhancement of the global community. Published research [100] indicates that post-trial monitoring is crucial for evaluating long-term effects and safety.

Conclusion

In silico approaches play a crucial role in predicting potential drug and vaccine targets prior to experimental validation. In this study, we used a proteomics-driven strategy to identify novel therapeutic candidates against P. intermedia, involving a comprehensive analysis of bacterial proteomes across multiple strains. This analysis led to the identification of one potential vaccine target and three promising drug candidates. Extensive evaluations were performed using biological databases, comparative genomics, and druggability assessments to prioritize these targets. Four vaccine constructs namely HBHA-PiMEV, L7/12-PiMEV, GMCSF-PiMEV, and HBHA-PiMEV were designed, among which the latter demonstrated the best performance by eliciting strong humoral, cellular, and innate immune responses, as confirmed through advanced immunoinformatics, molecular docking, and molecular dynamics simulations. These constructs were designed to address antigenic diversity, protect against multiple strains, and enhance immune recognition. Similarly, our analysis identified three novel drug targets with potential therapeutic relevance against this pathogen. The identified vaccine constructs and drug targets now warrant experimental validation to confirm their efficacy against P. intermedia.

Hall Polynomials for Weighted projective lines
Jiayi Chen, Bangming Deng and Shiquan Ruan

Abstract
. This paper deals with the triangle singularity defined by the equation f = X 1 p 1 + X 2 p 2 + X 3 p 3 f=X_{1}^{p_{1}}+X_{2}^{p_{2}}+X_{3}^{p_{3}} for weight triple ( p 1 , p 2 , p 3 ) (p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}) , as well as the category of coherent sheaves over the weighted projective line 𝕏 {\mathbb{X}} defined by f f . We calculate Hall polynomials associated to extensions bundles, line bundles and torsion sheaves over 𝕏 {\mathbb{X}} . By using derived equivalence, this provides a unified conceptual method for calculating Hall polynomials for representations of tame quivers obtained by Szántó and Szöllősi [J. Pure Appl. Alg. 228 (2024)].

1.
Introduction

The classical Hall algebra was first studied by Steinitz
[
33
]

and later by Hall
[
14
]
in the context of finite abelian
p
-groups. It turns out
that this algebra is isomorphic to the ring of symmetric functions and plays an
important role in the study of representations of symmetric groups and general linear
groups; see
[
20
]
. In 1990, Ringel
[
24
]
introduced the Hall algebra

H(R)
of a finitary ring
R
, which is the
free abelian group with basis the set of isomorphism classes of finite

R
-modules, and the structure constants are given by the so-called Hall numbers

F_{M,N}^{L}:=|\{X\subseteq L\mid X\cong N,\,L/X\cong M\}|.

where
L,M,N
are finite
R
-modules. Ringel then proved that the Hall algebra of
a finite dimensional representation-finite hereditary algebra over a finite field
{\mathbf{k}}

is isomorphic to the positive part of the corresponding quantum group.
By defining a bialgebra structure on the Hall algebra
H(R)
of an arbitrary finite
dimensional hereditary algebra
R
, Green
[
12
]
proved that the composition subalgebra
of
H(R)
generated by simple modules gives a realization of the positive part of the
corresponding quantum group.

In
[
23
]
, Ringel showed that Hall numbers for representation-directed algebras
over finite fields
{\mathbf{k}}
are actually integer polynomials in the cardinality
|{\mathbf{k}}|
,
called Hall polynomials, and he further calculated Hall polynomials for three indecomposable
modules over a representation-finite hereditary algebra in
[
25
]
. Since then,
much subsequent work is concerned with the existence of Hall polynomials and explicit
formulas of certain Hall polynomials. For example, the existence
of Hall polynomials for representations of tame quivers was studied in

[
26
,
13
,
43
,
16
,
7
,
6
]
, and Hall polynomials for some special classes
of representations of tame quivers were calculated in
[
44
,
34
,
35
,
37
,
38
]
.

We remark that Hall polynomials have significant applications in the study of cluster
algebras, as well as for counting rational points of quiver moduli
and for constructing canonical bases of affine quantum groups;
see, e.g.,
[
1
,
2
,
21
,
3
,
35
,

,

,

.

The notions of weighted projective lines and their coherent sheaf categories were introduced by
Geigle and Lenzing
[
11
]
in order to provide geometric counterparts to canonical
algebras and their module categories studied by Ringel
[
22
]
.
The Hall algebra of a weighted projective line was studied by Schiffmann
[
30
]
and
further by Dou–Jiang–Xiao in connection with quantum loop algebras. The existence of Hall
polynomials for coherent sheaves over a weighted projective line of domestic type was proved
in
[

It is well-known that the category of representations of a tame quiver and that of
coherent sheaves over the associated weighted projective line bear many similarities.
For example, they both are hereditary abelian categories and have equivalent bounded
derived categories. It seems to us that it is sometimes convenient
to work with weighted projective lines instead of tame quivers since the category
of coherent sheaves admits a natural torsion pair consisting of
vector bundles and torsion sheaves, respectively.

In this paper we mainly consider weighted projective lines
\mathbb{X}

As applications, we obtain some Hall polynomials for representations of tame quivers.

2.
Preliminaries and some preparatory results

In this section we review the category of coherent sheaves over a weighted projective
line and its basic properties, and introduce the Ringel–Hall algebra of a finitary
category and, in particular, Green’s formula in the hereditary case. Orthogonal exceptional
pair associated to an exceptional object in an abelian category is also defined.

2.1.
Coherent sheaves on weighted projective lines

Let
\mathbf{k}
be an arbitrary field. A
weighted projective line

\mathbb{X}
over
\mathbf{k}
is specified by giving a
weight
sequence

{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{t})
of positive
integers, and a collection

{\boldsymbol{\lambda}}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{t})
of
distinct points in the projective line
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbf{k})
which can be
normalized as
\lambda_{1}=\infty,\lambda_{2}=0,\lambda_{3}=1
.
More precisely, let
{\mathbb{L}}={\mathbb{L}}(\bf p)
be the rank one abelian group with
generators
\vec{x}_{1},\vec{x}_{2},\ldots,\vec{x}_{t}
and the
relations

p_{1}\vec{x}_{1}=p_{2}\vec{x}_{2}=\cdots=p_{t}\vec{x}_{t}=:\vec{c},

where
\vec{c}
is called the
canonical element
of

\mathbb{L}
. Denote by
S
the commutative algebra

S=\mathbf{k}[X_{1},X_{2},\cdots,X_{t}]/{\mathfrak{a}}:=\mathbf{k}[{\rm x}_{1},{\rm x}_{2},\ldots,{\rm x}_{t}],

where
{\mathfrak{a}}=(f_{3},\ldots,f_{t})
is the ideal generated by

f_{i}=X_{i}^{p_{i}}-X_{2}^{p_{2}}+\lambda_{i}X_{1}^{p_{1}},i=3,\ldots,t
. Put
I=\{1,2,\ldots,t\}
. Then
S
is
\mathbb{L}
-graded by setting

\mbox{deg}({\rm x}_{i})=\vec{x}_{i}\;\text{ for each $i\in I$.}

Moreover, each element
\vec{x}\in{\mathbb{L}}
has the normal form
\vec{x}=\sum_{i\in I}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}+l\vec{c}

with
0\leq l_{i}<p_{i}
and
l\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. We denote by
{\mathbb{L}}_{+}
the positive cone of
{\mathbb{L}}

which consists of those
\vec{x}
with
l\geq 0
. Finally, the weighted projective line
associated with
\bf p
and
\boldsymbol{\lambda}
is defined to be
{\mathbb{X}}=\rm{Proj}^{{\mathbb{L}}}S
.

According to
[
11
]
, the set of nonzero prime homogeneous
elements in
S
is partitioned into two sets: the
exceptional primes
{\rm x}_{1},\ldots,{\rm x}_{t}
and the ordinary primes

f({\rm x}_{1}^{p_{1}},{\rm x}_{2}^{p_{2}})
, where
f(T_{1},T_{2})
is a prime
homogeneous polynomial in
\mathbf{k}[T_{1},T_{2}]
which are distinct from
T_{1},T_{2}
and

T_{2}-\lambda_{i}T_{1}
for
i\in I
. The exceptional primes
correspond to the points
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{t}
, called
exceptional points and denoted by
x_{1},\ldots,x_{t}
, respectively, while the ordinary
primes correspond to the remaining closed points of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}(\mathbf{k})
, called ordinary
points. For convenience, we denote by
{\mathbb{H}}_{k}
the set of ordinary points.
For each
z\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}
, its degree
\deg\,z
is defined to be the degree
of the corresponding prime homogeneous polynomial.

The category of coherent sheaves on
{\mathbb{X}}
can be defined as the
quotient of the category
{\rm mod}^{\mathbb{L}}(S)
of finitely generated

\mathbb{L}
-graded
S
-modules over the Serre subcategory
\mbox{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}(S)

of finite length modules, that is,

\operatorname{coh}\nolimits{\mathbb{X}}:={\rm mod}^{\mathbb{L}}(S)/\mbox{mod}_{0}^{\mathbb{L}}(S).

The free module
S
gives the structure sheaf
{\mathcal{O}}
. Each line
bundle is given by the grading shift
{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})
for a uniquely
determined element
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
, and there is an
isomorphism

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits({\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x}),{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{y}))\cong S_{\vec{y}-\vec{x}}.

Moreover,
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits{\mathbb{X}}
is a hereditary abelian category with Serre
duality of the form

D\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(X,Y)\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(Y,X(\vec{\omega})),

where
D=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathbf{k}}(-,\mathbf{k})
, and
\vec{\omega}:=(t-2)\vec{c}-\sum_{i\in I}\vec{x}_{i}
is
called the
dualizing element
of
\mathbb{L}
. This implies
the existence of almost split sequences in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
with the
Auslander–Reiten translation
\tau
given by the grading shift with

\vec{\omega}
.

Recall that
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
admits a splitting torsion pair
({\rm coh}_{0}\mathbb{X},\operatorname{vect}\nolimits{\mathbb{X}})
,
where
{\rm coh}_{0}\mathbb{X}
and
\operatorname{vect}\nolimits{\mathbb{X}}
are full subcategories
of torsion sheaves and vector bundles, respectively. Moreover,
{\rm coh}_{0}\mathbb{X}

decomposes as a direct product of orthogonal tubes

{\rm coh}_{0}\mathbb{X}=\prod_{i\in I}\mathcal{T}_{i}\times\prod_{z\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}}\mathcal{T}_{z},

where each
\mathcal{T}_{z}
is a homogeneous tube for
z\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}
,
which is equivalent to the category of nilpotent representations of the
Jordan quiver over the residue field
\mathbf{k}_{z}
, while each
\mathcal{T}_{i}

is a non-homogeneous tube for
i\in I
, which is equivalent to the
category of nilpotent representations of the cyclic quiver with
p_{i}
vertices.
For each
i\in I
, let
S_{i,j}=S_{i,j}^{(1)}
be the simple objects in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
,
where
0\leq j\leq p_{i}-1
, such that

\tau S_{i,j}=S_{i,j-1}.

Further, for
n\geq 1
, we denote by
S_{i,{j}}^{(n)}

the unique indecomposable object in
\mathcal{T}_{i}
whose top object is
S_{i,j}
and length equals to
n
.
While for each
z\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}
, let
S_{z}=S_{z}^{(1)}
be the unique simple object
in
\mathcal{T}_{z}
, and denote by
S_{z}^{(n)}
the indecomposable object in
\mathcal{T}_{z}

of length
n
.

It is known that the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
of
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
is a free abelian group
with a basis
[{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})]
with
0\leq\vec{x}\leq\vec{c}
, where
[X]
denotes
the class of an object
X\in\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
in
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
. Set
\delta=[L(\vec{c})]-[L]
.
The Euler form on
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
is given by

\langle[X],[Y]\rangle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{k}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(X,Y)-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits_{k}\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(X,Y)

for any
X,Y\in\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
. For simplicity, we sometimes write
\langle X,Y\rangle=\langle[X],[Y]\rangle
.
Its symmetrization is defined by

(X,Y)=\langle X,Y\rangle+\langle Y,X\rangle.

In this paper we mainly deal with the category
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
of coherent sheaves over a weighted
projective line
\mathbb{X}
of weight type
(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
with
p_{i}\geq 2
for
i=1,2,3
.
In this case, we have the following explicit expression for line bundles in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
.

Proposition 2.1

(
[
9
]
)
.

Assume that
\vec{x}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}+l\vec{c}
is in
normal form. Then we have in
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
,

[{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{x})]=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}[\mathcal{O}(l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})]+l[{\mathcal{O}}(\vec{c})]-(l+2)[{\mathcal{O}}].

2.2.
Orthogonal exceptional pairs

Let
\mathcal{A}
be an abelian category over a field
\mathbf{k}
. An object
E\in\mathcal{A}
is
called exceptional if
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(E,E)=0
and
\operatorname{End}\nolimits(E)\cong\mathbf{k}
. A pair of exceptional
objects
(E_{1},E_{2})
is called orthogonal if

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E_{1},E_{2})=0=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E_{2},E_{1})\;\text{ and }\;\,\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(E_{2},E_{1})=0.

A theorem of Schofield (see
[
26
]
) makes it possible to construct exceptional objects
as extensions of orthogonal exceptional pairs. This procedure is often called Schofield
induction which is a very useful tool in the study of representation theory of algebras.

Following
[
37
]
, an orthogonal exceptional pair
(E_{1},E_{2})
is said to be
associated to an exceptional object
E
if there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E_{2}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow E_{1}\longrightarrow 0.

Then, in this case,

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(E_{1},

\displaystyle E_{2})=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E_{2},E)=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E,E_{1})=1,

\displaystyle\langle E_{2},E_{1}\rangle=0\;\text{ and }\;\,\langle E_{1},E_{2}\rangle=-1.

By a result of Kussin, Lenzing and Meltzer
[
17
]
, each indecomposable vector
bundle of rank two in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
is an extension bundle, that is, isomorphic to the
middle term of the following “unique” non-split exact sequence

(2.1)

\displaystyle{0\longrightarrow L(\vec{\omega})\longrightarrow E_{L}\langle\vec{x}\rangle\longrightarrow L(\vec{x})\longrightarrow 0,}

where
L
is a line bundle,
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}

with
0\leq\vec{x}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-2)\vec{x}_{i}
. Moreover, all the extension bundles
E_{L}\langle\vec{x}\rangle
are exceptional in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
. Hence, they are determined
by their classes in the Grothendieck group
K_{0}(\mathbb{X})
. For simplicity,
we denote
E_{{\mathcal{O}}}\langle\vec{x}\rangle
by
E\langle\vec{x}\rangle
in the rest of this paper.

The following is a key observation on the
feature of extension bundles.

Proposition 2.2

(
[
9
]
)
.

Assume that
\vec{x},\vec{y},\vec{z}\in\mathbb{L}
and
\vec{x}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}

with
0\leq l_{i}\leq p_{i}-2
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then

E\langle\vec{x}\rangle=E\langle\vec{y}\rangle(\vec{z})
if and only if one of
the following conditions holds:

(i)

\vec{y}=\vec{x}
and
\vec{z}=0
;

(ii)

\vec{y}=l_{j}\vec{x}_{j}+\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(p_{i}-2-l_{i})\vec{x}_{i}
and
\vec{z}=\sum\limits_{i\neq j}(l_{i}+1)\vec{x}_{i}-\vec{c}

for some
1\leq j\leq 3
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Assume that
L
is a line bundle and
E=E_{L}\langle\vec{x}\rangle
is an extension bundle for

\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
. Then
(L(\vec{x}),L(\vec{\omega}))
is an orthogonal exceptional pair associated to the
exceptional sheaf
E
, that is,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),L(\vec{\omega}))=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))=\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))=0.

Proof.

It is known that the dimension of
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))
is determined
by
\vec{x}-\vec{\omega}
. Since
\vec{x}
is an element in
\mathbb{L}
with

0\leq\vec{x}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-2)\vec{x}_{i}
and
\vec{\omega}=\vec{c}-\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\vec{x}_{i}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{3}\vec{x}_{i}-\vec{c}\leq\vec{x}-\vec{\omega}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-1)\vec{x}_{i}-\vec{c}.

Hence,
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))=0
.

Similarly, we have

\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}\vec{x}_{i}-2\vec{c}\leq\vec{\omega}-\vec{x}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-1)\vec{x}_{i}-2\vec{c}.

Thus,
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),L(\vec{\omega}))=0
.

By the Serre duality, we have

\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))\cong D\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),L(2\vec{\omega})).

Since
-\vec{c}\leq 2\vec{\omega}-\vec{x}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-2)\vec{x}_{i}-\vec{c}
, it follows that
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))=0.

∎

2.3.
Ringel–Hall algebra of a finitary category

In the rest of this paper, we take the field
\mathbf{k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}
, a finite field
of
q
elements. Let
\mathcal{A}
be an essentially small abelian category over
\mathbf{k}
.
Assume
\mathcal{A}
is finitary, that is,

|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty,\quad|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(M,N)|<\infty,\,\,\forall\,M,N\in\mathcal{A}.

Let
{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})
be the set of isomorphic classes in
\mathcal{A}
.
If no confusion arises, we also use
[M]
to denote the isomorphism class of an object
M
.
The
Ringel–Hall algebra
(or
Hall algebra
for short)
\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})
of

\mathcal{A}
is defined to be the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with the basis

\big\{[M]\mid[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})\big\}
,
endowed with the multiplication defined by (see
[
24
]
)

[A]\cdot[B]=\sum_{[M]\in{\rm Iso}(\mathcal{A})}F_{AB}^{M}\cdot[M],\;\;\forall\,A,B\in\mathcal{A},

where

F_{AB}^{M}=\big|\{X\subseteq M\mid X\cong B,M/X\cong A\}\big|.

Note that the associativity of multiplication follows from the equalities

\displaystyle F_{ABC}^{M}:=\sum\limits_{[X]}F_{AB}^{X}F_{XC}^{M}=\sum\limits_{[Y]}F_{AY}^{M}F_{BC}^{Y},

where
A,B,C,M\in\mathcal{A}
.

Furthermore, for given objects
A,B,M\in\mathcal{A}
, let

\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)_{M}\subseteq\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)

denote the subset consisting of extensions whose middle term is isomorphic to
M
. By
a_{M}

we denote the cardinality of the automorphism group
{\rm Aut}(M)
of
M
. The
Riedtmann–Peng’s formula states that

F^{M}_{AB}=\frac{|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(A,B)_{M}|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(A,B)|}\cdot\frac{a_{M}}{a_{A}a_{B}}.

If, moreover,
\mathcal{A}
is hereditary, then for each quadruple

(M,N,X,Y)
of objects in
\mathcal{A}
, the following famous
Green’s formula

holds:

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{[E]}F_{MN}^{E}F_{XY}^{E}\frac{1}{a_{E}}=\sum\limits_{[A],[B],[C],[D]}q^{-\langle A,D\rangle}F_{AB}^{M}F_{CD}^{N}F_{AC}^{X}F_{BD}^{Y}\frac{a_{A}a_{B}a_{C}a_{D}}{a_{M}a_{N}a_{X}a_{Y}},

where
\langle A,D\rangle={\rm dim\,Hom}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,D)-{\rm dim\,Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{A}}(A,D)
is
the Euler form in
\mathcal{A}
; see
[
12
, Thm 2]
.

2.4.
Two recursion formulas

The orthogonal exceptional pairs provide an effective way to calculate Hall numbers
in this paper. Keep the notation in 2.3, i.e.,
\mathcal{A}
is a finitary abelian category
over a finite field
\mathbf{k}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. We now give two recursion
formulas related to orthogonal exceptional pairs which will be used later on.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
(E_{1},E_{2})
be an orthogonal exceptional
pair in
\mathcal{A}
associated to an exceptional object
E
. Then for any
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,

F_{X^{\prime}E}^{X}=\sum_{[Z_{1}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}}^{Z_{1}}F_{Z_{1}E_{2}}^{X}-\sum_{[Z_{2}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{2}}^{Z_{2}}F_{Z_{2}E_{1}}^{X}.

Proof.

Since
F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{Z}=1
for
Z\cong E
or
E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
, and
F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{Z}=0

otherwise, it follows that

\displaystyle F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}E_{2}}^{X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[Z]}F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{Z}F_{X^{\prime}Z}^{X}

\displaystyle=F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E}F_{X^{\prime}E}^{X}+F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}F_{X^{\prime},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}^{X}=F_{X^{\prime}E}^{X}+F_{X^{\prime},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}^{X}.

On the other hand,
F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}E_{2}}^{X}=\sum_{Z_{1}}F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}}^{Z_{1}}F_{Z_{1}E_{2}}^{X}
. Thus,

F_{X^{\prime}E}^{X}=\sum_{[Z_{1}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}}^{Z_{1}}F_{Z_{1}E_{2}}^{X}-F_{X^{\prime},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}^{X}.

Similarly,
F_{E_{2}E_{1}}^{Z}=1
for
Z\cong E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
, and
F_{E_{2}E_{1}}^{Z}=0
otherwise, so

\displaystyle F_{X^{\prime}E_{2}E_{1}}^{X}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[Z]}F_{E_{2}E_{1}}^{Z}F_{X^{\prime}Z}^{X}

\displaystyle=F_{E_{2}E_{1}}^{E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}F_{X^{\prime},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}^{X}=F_{X^{\prime},E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}^{X}.

Moreover,
F_{X^{\prime}E_{2}E_{1}}^{X}=\sum_{[Z_{2}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{2}}^{Z_{2}}F_{Z_{2}E_{1}}^{X}
. This implies that

F_{X^{\prime}E}^{X}=\sum_{[Z_{1}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{1}}^{Z_{1}}F_{Z_{1}E_{2}}^{X}-\sum_{[Z_{2}]}F_{X^{\prime}E_{2}}^{Z_{2}}F_{Z_{2}E_{1}}^{X}.

∎

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hereditary abelian category and
(E_{1},E_{2})
be an orthogonal
exceptional pair in
\mathcal{A}
associated to an exceptional object
E
.
Then for any two objects
X,X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}
,

\displaystyle F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\times(F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{2}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{1}}-F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{1}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{2}}).

Proof.

Consider the Green’s formula associated to the quadruple
(E_{1},E_{2},X^{\prime},X)
:

\displaystyle\sum_{[W]}F_{X^{\prime}X}^{W}F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{W}\frac{1}{a_{W}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{2}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{1}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}a_{E_{1}}a_{E_{2}}}.

This can be depicted in the following commutative diagram:

X_{3}
E_{1}
X_{4}
X
W
X^{\prime}
X_{1}
E_{2}
X_{2}

where all row and column sequences are short exact sequences (For simplicity,
we omit the terms
0
here).
Since
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(E_{1},E_{2})\cong\mathbf{k}
, we have either
W\cong E
or

W\cong E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
when
F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{W}\not=0
. A direct calculation gives that

F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}=F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E}=1,\,a_{E}=a_{E_{1}}=a_{E_{2}}=q-1,\,a_{E_{1}\oplus E_{2}}=(q-1)^{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{2}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{1}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}\cdot(q-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E}\frac{1}{q-1}+F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E_{2}\oplus E_{1}}F_{E_{1}E_{2}}^{E_{2}\oplus E_{1}}\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}\frac{1}{q-1}+F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E_{2}\oplus E_{1}}\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}.

Again, by applying Green’s formula associated
to the quadruple
(E_{2},E_{1},X^{\prime},X)
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\sum_{[W]}F_{X^{\prime}X}^{W}F_{E_{2}E_{1}}^{W}\frac{1}{a_{W}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{1}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{2}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}a_{E_{2}}a_{E_{1}}}.

Since
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(E_{2},E_{1})=0
, the left-hand side contains only one possible
nonzero term, that is,
W\cong E_{1}\oplus E_{2}
, and thus,

\displaystyle\sum_{[W]}F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E_{2}\oplus E_{1}}\frac{1}{(q-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{1}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{2}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}\cdot(q-1)^{2}}.

By combining two equalities above together, we conclude that

\displaystyle F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],X_{2},X_{3},X_{4}}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\times(F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{2}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{1}}-F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{E_{1}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{E_{2}}).

∎

3.
Hall polynomials for line bundles and torsion sheaves

This section is devoted to calculating Hall numbers for line bundles and torsion
sheaves on a weighted projective line over a finite field
\mathbf{k}
by applying
Green’s formula. It turns out that these numbers are indeed polynomials
in the cardinality of the ground field
\mathbf{k}
and thus, called Hall polynomials.

Throughout this section,
\mathbb{X}
always denotes a weight projective line of weight
type
{\bf p}=(p_{1},p_{2},\ldots,p_{t})
over a field
\mathbf{k}
.
We keep all the notation in the previous section.

3.1.
Hom and Ext spaces of line bundles and torsion sheaves

In this subsection we calculate the dimensions of morphism spaces from
line bundles to torsion sheaves. To this aim, we need some notations. Given a
rational number
m/n
, define

\lfloor\dfrac{m}{n}\rfloor={\rm max}\{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mid a\leq m/n\},\;\;\lceil\dfrac{m}{n}\rceil={\rm min}\{a\in\mathbb{Z}\mid a\geq m/n\}.

Further, fix a positive integer
p
, and for
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, set
0\leq\widetilde{m}\leq p-1

such that
\widetilde{m}\equiv m\,({\rm mod}\,p)
. Then, for
m,n\in\mathbb{Z}
, we have
the following equalities:

(3.1)

\displaystyle\lceil-\dfrac{n}{p}\rceil=-\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p}\rfloor,\quad\lceil\dfrac{n+1}{p}\rceil=\lfloor\dfrac{n}{p}\rfloor+1,

\displaystyle\lceil\dfrac{n-\widetilde{m}}{p}\rceil=\lceil\dfrac{n-{m}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p}\rfloor,

\displaystyle\lceil\dfrac{n+\widetilde{m}}{p}\rceil=\lceil\dfrac{n+{m}}{p}\rceil-\lfloor\dfrac{m}{p}\rfloor.

Recall from 2.1 the exceptional tubes
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
(
i\in I
) in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
. In what follows,
for
i\in I,j\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, and
n\geq 1
,
S_{i,j}^{(n)}
should be understood as

S_{i,\tilde{j}}^{(n)}
with
p=p_{i}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{T}_{i}
be an exceptional tube in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
of
rank
p_{i}=p
, and
S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)}
be indecomposable torsion sheaves
for some
n,m\geq 1
and
j,k\in\mathbb{Z}
.

(1)

If
n\geq m\geq 1
, then

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)})=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rfloor,

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(m)},S_{i,j}^{(n)})=\lfloor\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rfloor+\lceil\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rceil.

(2)

If
m\geq n\geq 1
, then

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)})=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{n-m+k-j}{p}\rfloor,

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(m)},S_{i,j}^{(n)})=\lfloor\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rfloor+\lceil\dfrac{n-m+k-j}{p}\rceil.

(3)

For any
n,m\geq 1
,

\langle S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)}\rangle=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rfloor-\lfloor\dfrac{n-{j+k}}{p}\rfloor-\lceil\dfrac{m-n+j-k}{p}\rceil.

Proof.

(1) Since each morphism
S_{i,j}^{(n)}\to S_{i,k}^{(m)}
factors
through the canonical embedding
S_{i,j}^{(m-(k-j)^{\sim})}\to S_{i,k}^{(m)}
,
we obtain the equalities

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)})=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,j}^{(m-(k-j)^{\sim})})=\lceil\dfrac{m-(k-j)^{\sim}}{p}\rceil.

By (
3.1
), we obtain that

\lceil\dfrac{m-(k-j)^{\sim}}{p}\rceil=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rfloor.

Furthermore, by Serre duality,

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(m)},S_{i,j}^{(n)})=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k-1}^{(m)})=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+1+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{k-1-j}{p}\rfloor.

Again by (
3.1
), this equals to

\lfloor\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rfloor+1+\lceil\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rceil-1=\lfloor\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rfloor+\lceil\dfrac{k-j}{p}\rceil.

(2) In this case, each morphism
S_{i,j}^{(n)}\to S_{i,k}^{(m)}
factors through the canonical
embedding
S_{i,j}^{(n-(n-m+k-j)^{\sim})}\to S_{i,k}^{(m)}
. Thus, we obtain the equalities

\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)})=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,j}^{(n-(n-m+k-j)^{\sim})})=\lceil\dfrac{n-(n-m+k-j)^{\sim}}{p}\rceil.

This implies by (
3.1
) that

\lceil\dfrac{n-(n-m+k-j)^{\sim}}{p}\rceil=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{n-m+k-j}{p}\rfloor.

Applying Serre duality gives that

\displaystyle\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(m)},S_{i,j}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k-1}^{(m)})

\displaystyle=\lceil\dfrac{m-{k+1+j}}{p}\rceil+\lfloor\dfrac{n-m+k-1-j}{p}\rfloor,

which equals to
\lfloor\dfrac{m-{k+j}}{p}\rfloor+\lceil\dfrac{n-m+k-j}{p}\rceil
by
(
3.1
).

(3) It follows immediately from (1), (2) and the equality

\langle S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)}\rangle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)})-\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(m)}).

∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
L
be a line bundle and
S,S^{\prime}
be indecomposable torsion sheaves supported
at the same point. Assume
S
and
S^{\prime}
have length
n
and
n^{\prime}
, respectively,
with
n\leq n^{\prime}
. If
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,\mathrm{top\,}S^{\prime})\neq 0
, then

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S^{\prime},S)\;\text{ and }\;\,\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S^{\prime})\cong\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S^{\prime}).

Proof.

If
S=S_{z}^{(n)}
is an indecomposable torsion sheaf
of class
dn\delta
lying in a homogeneous tube
\mathcal{T}_{z}
, where
z\in{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathbf{k}}
with
\mathrm{deg}\,z=d
,
then we obtain exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{z}^{(1)})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{z}^{(t)})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{z}^{(t-1)})\longrightarrow 0

for
2\leq t\leq n
. We conclude that

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)\cong\bigoplus_{k=1}^{n}\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{z}^{(1)})\cong\mathbf{k}^{dn}\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S^{\prime},S).

If
S=S_{i,{j}}^{(n)}
is an indecomposable torsion sheaf lying in an
exceptional tube
\mathcal{T}_{i}
of rank
p_{i}
for some
i\in I
, then there are exact sequences

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,{j-n+t-1}}^{(t-1)})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,{j-n+t}}^{(t)})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,{j-n+t}}^{(1)})\longrightarrow 0

for
2\leq t\leq n
. Thus,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,{j}}^{(1)})\oplus\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,{j-1}}^{(1)})\oplus\cdots\oplus\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,j-n+1}^{(1)}).

Assume
\mathrm{top}\,S^{\prime}=S_{i,{j^{\prime}}}^{(1)}
for some
0\leq j^{\prime}\leq p_{i}-1
.
Then
S^{\prime}=S_{i,{j^{\prime}}}^{(n^{\prime})}
, and by Lemma
3.1
,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)\cong\mathbf{k}^{m}\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S^{\prime},S),

where
m=\lceil\frac{n-(j-j^{\prime})^{\sim}}{p_{i}}\rceil
.

This finishes the proof of the first isomorphism, while the
second one follows immediately by replacing
S
by
S^{\prime}
.
∎

Consequently, we obtain the following result, which indicates that we can use the formulas in Lemma
3.1
to calculate the homomorphisms (resp. extensions) between line bundles and torsion sheaves. The strategy will be used frequently later on.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
L
be a line bundle such that
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,j})\neq 0
for some simple exceptional
sheaf
S_{i,j}
. Then for
n\geq 1
,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S_{i,k}^{(n)})\cong\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,k}^{(n)})\text{\;\; and \;\;}\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(n)},L)\cong\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,k}^{(n)},S_{i,j}^{(n)}).

Proof.

The first statement follows from Lemma
3.2
, while the second one follows from Serre duality.
∎

3.2.
Hall numbers for line bundles and torsion sheaves

We first consider
some easy cases where only line bundles and torsion sheaves are involved. In this subsection,

\mathbb{X}
is an arbitrary weight projective line over a finite field
\mathbf{k}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.

Proposition 3.4
.

Assume that
L
is a line bundle,
\vec{x},\vec{y}\in\mathbb{L}
and
S=\bigoplus_{k}S_{k}
is a torsion
sheaf, where
S_{k}
are all indecomposable. Then there exists an exact sequence

(3.2)

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow L(\vec{x})\longrightarrow L(\vec{y})\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0

if and only if the following three conditions hold:

(1)

[L(\vec{x})]+[S]=[L(\vec{y})]
in
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X})
;

(2)

the
S_{k}
lie in pairwise distinct tubes;

(3)

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{y}),\mathrm{top\,}S_{k})\neq 0
for each
k
.

Moreover, in this case,

F_{SL(\vec{x})}^{L(\vec{y})}=1.

Proof.

First, the exact sequence (
3.2
) gives that
[L(\vec{x})]+[S]=[L(\vec{y})]
.
Further, the existence of the epimorphism
L(\vec{y})\to S
implies that
S_{k}
must be taken from pairwise
distinct tubes, c.f.
[
31
, Exam. 4.12]
. Thus, there is an epimorphism from
L(\vec{y})

to
S_{k}
for each
k
. We conclude that
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{y}),\mathrm{top\,}S_{k})\neq 0
.

Conversely,
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{y}),\mathrm{top\,}S_{k})\neq 0
implies that there exists an epimorphism

\pi_{k}:L(\vec{y})\rightarrow S_{k}
. By
[
31
, Exam. 4.12]
, there is an epimorphism

(\pi_{k}):L(\vec{y})\longrightarrow S=\bigoplus_{k}S_{k},

and its kernel
K
is a line bundle. Thus,
[K]+[S]=[L(\vec{y})]
. This together with

[L(\vec{x})]+[S]=[L(\vec{y})]
implies that
K\cong L(\vec{x})
and hence, we obtain an exact sequence
of form (
3.2
). Consequently,
F_{SL(\vec{x})}^{L(\vec{y})}=1
.
∎

Note that in the proposition above, if
[S_{k}]\neq c\delta
for each
c\geq 0
, then
the condition that
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{y}),\mathrm{top\,}S_{k})\neq 0
follows from the other two conditions.

Next, we consider the case where the middle term is given by a direct sum of a line bundle and
a torsion sheaf. It turns out that if such an exact sequence does not split, then the
associated Hall number is related to the cardinalities of automorphism groups.

Proposition 3.5
.

Assume that
L,L^{\prime}
are line bundles and
S
is a torsion sheaf. Then there exist a
torsion sheaf
S^{\prime}
(may be zero) and an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0

if and only if
S^{\prime}
is a subsheaf of
S
such that there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow L^{\prime}\longrightarrow S/S^{\prime}\longrightarrow 0.

In this case, by setting
S^{\prime\prime}=S/S^{\prime}
, we have

F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}={|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime})|}\cdot F_{S^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}}^{S}\frac{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{S}}.

In particular, if
S
is indecomposable, then

F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=\begin{cases}|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)|,&\text{ if }S\cong S^{\prime};\\
a_{S^{\prime}},&\text{ if }S\ncong S^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof.

By applying Green’s formula to the quadruple
(L^{\prime},S^{\prime},S,L)
, we obtain that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{[W]}F_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime}}^{W}F_{SL}^{W}\frac{1}{a_{W}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{S^{\prime}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{L}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{S}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{L}a_{L^{\prime}}a_{S}a_{S^{\prime}}};

see the following commutative diagram:

(3.4)

X_{3}
L^{\prime}
X_{4}
L
W
S
X_{1}
S^{\prime}
X_{2}

where all row and column sequences are short exact sequences.
Then
W\cong L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}
and
X_{1}=0
.
Thus,
X_{2}\cong S^{\prime}
and
X_{3}\cong L
. Since
X_{4}
is a torsion sheaf and there is
an epimorphism from the line bundle
L^{\prime}
to
X_{4}
, the indecomposable direct summands
of
X_{4}
lie in distinct tubes, and they are uniquely determined by
S
and
S^{\prime}
. In other words,

X_{4}
is unique (up to isomorphism) and so,
X_{4}\cong S^{\prime\prime}
.
Further,
F_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime}}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=1
since
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S^{\prime},L^{\prime})=0
. In conclusion, the equality in
(
3.3
) is reduced to the equality

\displaystyle F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=a_{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}\cdot F_{S^{\prime\prime}L}^{L^{\prime}}F_{S^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}}^{S}\frac{a_{L}a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{L}a_{L^{\prime}}a_{S}a_{S^{\prime}}}.

Hence,
F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}\neq 0
if and only if
F_{S^{\prime\prime}L}^{L^{\prime}}F_{S^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}}^{S}\neq 0
.
This proves the first assertion.

In this case, by Proposition
3.4
we have

F_{S^{\prime\prime}L}^{L^{\prime}}=1
. It follows that

(3.5)

\displaystyle F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}={|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime})|}\cdot F_{S^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}}^{S}\frac{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{S}}.

Now assume that
S
is indecomposable, then so are
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
. This implies that

F_{S^{\prime\prime}S^{\prime}}^{S}=1
. If
S^{\prime}\cong S
, then
S^{\prime\prime}=0
and

F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime})|=|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,S)|.

If
S^{\prime}\ncong S
, then
\mathrm{top\,}S=\mathrm{top\,}S^{\prime\prime}
, and
so
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},\mathrm{top\,}S^{\prime\prime})=\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},\mathrm{top\,}S)\neq 0
. By Lemma
3.2
, we obtain that

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S)\cong\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S)\;\,\text{ and }\;\,\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime})\cong\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S^{\prime\prime}).

The exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime})\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime})\longrightarrow 0

together with (
3.5
) gives that

F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=\frac{|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S)|}{|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S^{\prime\prime})|}\cdot\frac{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{S}}.

Since both
S
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are indecomposable and uniserial, we have that

|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S)|=q^{s+1},\;|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S^{\prime\prime})|=q^{t+1},\;a_{S}=(q-1)q^{s},\;\text{ and }\;\,a_{S}=(q-1)q^{t},

where
s=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{rad}\nolimits(S)
and
t=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{rad}\nolimits(S^{\prime\prime})
. Therefore,

F_{SL}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=\frac{|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S)|}{|\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S^{\prime\prime})|}\cdot\frac{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{S}}=\dfrac{a_{S}}{a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}\cdot\frac{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}{a_{S}}=a_{S^{\prime}}.

∎

Proposition 3.6
.

Assume that
L,L^{\prime}
are line bundles and
S,S^{\prime},S^{\prime\prime}
are torsion sheaves (or zero sheaves).
Then there exists an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow L\oplus S\longrightarrow L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}\longrightarrow S^{\prime\prime}\longrightarrow 0

if and only if there are sheaves
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
with exact sequences

0\to S\to S^{\prime}\to S_{1}\to 0,\quad 0\to L\to L^{\prime}\to S_{2}\to 0,\quad 0\to S_{1}\to S^{\prime\prime}\to S_{2}\to 0.

In this case, we have

F_{S^{\prime\prime}L\oplus S}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}=\frac{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S^{\prime})|}{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L^{\prime},S)|}\cdot\sum_{X_{2}}F_{X_{2}S}^{S^{\prime}}F_{S_{2}X_{2}}^{S^{\prime\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{2}}a_{S_{2}}}{a_{S^{\prime\prime}}}.

In particular, if both
S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
are indecomposable, then

a_{S^{\prime}/S},&\text{ if }L\ncong L^{\prime}.\end{cases}

Proof.

First of all, Green’s formula associated to the quadruple

has the form

\displaystyle\sum_{[W]}F_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime}}^{W}F_{S^{\prime\prime}L\oplus S}^{W}\frac{1}{a_{W}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{S^{\prime}}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{L\oplus S}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{S^{\prime\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{L\oplus S}a_{L^{\prime}}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}a_{S^{\prime}}}

which is depicted by the following commutative diagram

X_{3}

L\oplus S
W

S^{\prime}
X_{2}

Since

is a torsion sheaf and
X_{3}
is a subsheaf of

, this implies that

X_{3}\cong L
and
X_{1}\cong S
. As a quotient sheaf of

, the indecomposable
direct summands of

lie in distinct tubes, and they are uniquely determined
by
S,S^{\prime}
and
S^{\prime\prime}
. In other words,
X_{4}
is a torsion sheaf, written as
S_{2}
,
and there is an exact sequence

\textstyle{0\longrightarrow L\longrightarrow L^{\prime}\longrightarrow X_{4}=S_{2}\longrightarrow 0}

Since
F_{LS}^{L\oplus S}=1=F_{L^{\prime}S^{\prime}}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}
, the formula (

)
is reduced to the equality

(3.10)

\displaystyle F_{S^{\prime\prime}L\oplus S}^{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=q^{-\langle S_{2},S\rangle}\sum_{[X_{2}]}F_{X_{2}S}^{S^{\prime}}F_{S_{2}X_{2}}^{S^{\prime\prime}}\frac{a_{S}a_{X_{2}}a_{S_{2}}a_{L}\cdot a_{L^{\prime}\oplus S^{\prime}}}{a_{S^{\prime}}a_{L\oplus S}a_{S^{\prime\prime}}a_{L^{\prime}}}.

Therefore,

. This proves the first statement.

Applying

,

4.
Hall polynomials involving extension bundles

In this section we assume that
\mathbb{X}
is a weighted projective line of weight type

(p_{1},p_{2},p_{3})
over a finite field
\mathbf{k}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and focus on calculating
Hall numbers for the case where extension bundles are concerned with. As in the
previous section, these numbers are again polynomials in
q=|{\mathbf{k}}|
.

By (
2.1
) in Section 2, each bundle of rank 2 is an extension of two line bundles

L(\vec{\omega})
and
L(\vec{x})
for
0\leq\vec{x}\leq\sum\limits_{i=1}^{3}(p_{i}-2)\vec{x}_{i}
.
We first describe the morphism and extension spaces between
L(\vec{\omega})
and
L(\vec{x})
.

By the orthogonality of
(L(\vec{x}),L(\vec{\omega}))
, we immediately obtain that

F_{L(\vec{x})L(\vec{\omega})}^{W}=1
if
W\cong E
or
W\cong L(\vec{x})\oplus L(\vec{\omega})
, and

F_{L(\vec{x})L(\vec{\omega})}^{W}=0
for the remaining cases. Moreover,
F_{L(\vec{\omega})L(\vec{x})}^{W}=1
if

W\cong L(\vec{x})\oplus L(\vec{\omega})
, and
F_{L(\vec{\omega})L(\vec{x})}^{W}=0
otherwise.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be two line bundles in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
. Then

\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{1},L_{2})=0\;\,\text{ or }\;\,\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{2},L_{1})=0.

Proof.

Suppose that
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{1},L_{2})\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{2},L_{1})\neq 0
. Then

\det L_{1}-\det L_{2}+\vec{\omega}\geq 0,\quad\det L_{2}-\det L_{1}+\vec{\omega}\geq 0.

This implies that
2\vec{\omega}=2\vec{c}-2\sum_{i=1}^{3}\vec{x}_{i}\geq 0
, a contradiction.
∎

By applying Proposition
2.5
to the orthogonal exceptional pair
(L(\vec{x}),L(\vec{\omega}))

associated to
E=E_{L}\langle\vec{x}\rangle
in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
, we obtain that for any
X,X^{\prime}\in\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}

\displaystyle F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\times(F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{x})}-F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{x})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}).

In order to calculate
F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}
, we write

(4.2)

F_{X^{\prime}X}^{E}=\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2},

where

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{x})},

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{x})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}.

The automorphism groups of torsion sheaves and their cardinalities play a
role in the calculation of Hall numbers. We first present some formulas given in

[
38
, Prop. 6.1]
. As pointed out in Section 2, there are exactly three exceptional tubes

{\mathcal{T}}_{1},{\mathcal{T}}_{2},{\mathcal{T}}_{3}
of ranks
p_{1},p_{2},p_{3}
, respectively, in
\operatorname{coh}\nolimits\mathbb{X}
. Then the sums
of classes of all simple objects in
{\mathcal{T}}_{1}
,

{\mathcal{T}}_{2}
and
{\mathcal{T}}_{3}
in
\mathrm{K}_{0}(\mathbb{X})
are the same, equal to
\delta
. For
1\leq i\leq 3
, take an
indecomposable torsion sheaf
S_{i}
in
{\mathcal{T}}_{i}
with the class
\sigma_{i}<\delta
.

For
0\leq k\leq 3
, let
\mathscr{S}_{n}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}}
denote
the set of isomorphism classes of all torsion sheaves
S
such that

(1)

the class of
S
is
n\delta+\sigma_{1}+\cdots+\sigma_{k}
,

(2)

its indecomposable summands are taken from pairwise distinct tubes, and

(3)

contains exactly one indecomposable summand from each
exceptional tube of
{\mathcal{T}}_{1},\ldots,{\mathcal{T}}_{k}
.

Finally, set

s_{n}^{(k)}(q)=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[S]\in\mathscr{S}_{n}^{\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{k}}}a_{S}.

By convention, set
s_{0}^{(k)}(q)={(q-1)}^{k-1}
and
s_{-1}^{(k)}(q)=0
.

Further, define

(4.3)

f_{n}(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i-1}(2i-1)q^{n+1-i}+(-1)^{n}(n+1),\;\;\forall\,n\geq 1,

and set
f_{0}(q)=1,\,f_{-n}(q)=0
.

Proposition 4.2

(
[
38
, Prop. 6.1]
)
.

For
n\geq 1
and
0\leq k\leq 3
, we have the following equalities

s_{n}^{(k)}(q)=\sum_{i=1}^{2n+k-1}(-1)^{i-1}iq^{2n+k-i}-(-1)^{k}(n+1).

Moreover,

\displaystyle s_{n}^{(0)}(q)-s_{n-2}^{(3)}(q)=f_{2n-1}(q),

\displaystyle\text{ for }n\geq 1;

\displaystyle s_{n-1}^{(3)}(q)-s_{n}^{(0)}(q)=f_{2n}(q),

\displaystyle\text{ for }n\geq 1;

\displaystyle s_{n}^{(1)}(q)-s_{n-1}^{(2)}(q)=f_{2n}(q),

\displaystyle\text{ for }n\geq 0;

\displaystyle s_{n}^{(2)}(q)-s_{n}^{(1)}(q)=f_{2n+1}(q),

\displaystyle\text{ for }n\geq 0,

and

(4.4)

\displaystyle(q-1)\sum_{t\geq 0}q^{t}f_{n-2t}(q)=f_{n+1}(q)+(-1)^{n}\;\text{ for }n\geq 0.

In the following we view each
f_{n}(q)
either as a polynomials in
q
or
as a number evaluating at the cardinality
q=|{\mathbf{k}}|
of a finite field
{\mathbf{k}}
.
For notational simplicity, we sometimes write

instead of

.

With the help of notations above, we are ready to calculate Hall numbers (polynomials)

4.1.
Hall polynomial

for line bundles
L_{1}
and

In this subsection, we calculate Hall number

, where

is

and

It turns out that it is determined by the value

.

Theorem 4.3
.

Assume that
L_{1},L_{2}
, and
L
are line bundles and
E=E_{L}\langle\vec{x}\rangle
is
an extension bundle with
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
. If
[L_{1}]+[L_{2}]=[E]
, then

F_{L_{2}L_{1}}^{E}=f_{\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle}(q).

Proof.

We first consider the case
L_{1}\cong L(\vec{x})
or
L(\vec{\omega})
. If
L_{1}\cong L(\vec{x})
,
then
L_{2}\cong L(\vec{\omega})
. In this case, by Proposition
2.3
, we have

\langle L_{1},E\rangle=\langle[L_{1}],[L_{1}]+[L_{2}]\rangle=0\;\text{ and }\;\,F_{L_{2}L_{1}}^{E}=0.

If
L_{1}\cong L(\vec{\omega})
, then
L_{2}\cong L(\vec{x})
, and by Proposition
2.3
again,

\langle L_{1},E\rangle=1\;\text{ and }\;\,F_{L_{2}L_{1}}^{E}=1.

Now suppose that
L_{1}\ncong L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}).
If
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle<0
,
then
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{1},L_{2})\neq 0
and hence,
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L_{2},L_{1})=0
. Thus,
F_{L_{2}L_{1}}^{E}=0
.

Assume
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle\geq 0
. Then

1\leq\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle+\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle=\langle L_{1},L(\vec{\omega})\rangle+\langle L_{1},L(\vec{x})\rangle.

This implies that
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L(\vec{\omega}))\neq 0
or
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L(\vec{x}))\neq 0
.
In the case when
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L(\vec{\omega}))\neq 0
, let
L_{1}=L(\vec{\omega}-\vec{y}),L_{2}=L(\vec{x}+\vec{y})
with

\vec{y}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}l^{\prime}_{i}\vec{x}_{i}+l^{\prime}\vec{c}>0
and
\vec{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}
with

0\leq l_{i}\leq p_{i}-2
for
1\leq i\leq 3
. Then
l^{\prime}_{i}=0
or
l^{\prime}_{i}=p_{i}-1-l_{i}
.
It follows that
\vec{x}+\vec{y}-\vec{\omega}\geq 0
and so
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L(\vec{x}))\neq 0.
Therefore,
there exists

\vec{z}=\sum_{i=1}^{3}(l_{i}+1)\vec{x}_{i}+l\vec{c}
such that
L_{1}=L(\vec{x}-\vec{z}),L_{2}=L(\vec{\omega}+\vec{z})
.
The case
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L(\vec{x}))\not=0
can be treated similarly.

As conclusion, there is a subset
J\subseteq\{1,2,3\}
such that

\displaystyle L_{1}=L\big(\sum_{j\notin J}l_{j}\vec{x}_{j}+\sum_{i\in J}(p_{i}-1)\vec{x}_{i}-(l+|J|)\vec{c}\big),

\displaystyle L_{2}=L\big(\sum_{j\notin J}(p_{j}-1)\vec{x}_{j}+\sum_{i\in J}l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}+(l+|J|-2)\vec{c}\big),

and
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L_{1},L_{2})=2l+|J|-1
.

We claim that
F_{S_{1}L(\vec{\omega})}^{L_{2}}F_{S_{1}L_{1}}^{L(\vec{x})}\neq 0
if
and only if
S_{1}\in\mathscr{S}_{l}^{\{\sigma_{i}\}_{i\in J}}
. For convenience,
assume
L={\mathcal{O}}
and denote by
\sigma_{i}=[S_{i,p_{i}-1}^{(p_{i}-1-l_{i})}]
.
Then
[L_{2}]-[L(\vec{\omega})]=\sum_{i\in J}\sigma_{i}+l\delta
. For

\varphi=\prod_{i\in J}{\rm x}_{i}^{p_{i}-1-l_{i}}\cdot f_{l}:L(\vec{\omega})\rightarrow L_{2}
or
L_{1}\rightarrow L(\vec{x})
,
where
f_{l}\in\mathbf{k}[T_{1},T_{2}]
is a homogeneous polynomial of degree
l\vec{c}
(Note that the
coefficients of
\vec{x}_{j}\,(j\notin J)
in
L_{2}
and
L(\vec{x})
are different).
Hence, no direct summand of
\operatorname{Coker}\nolimits\varphi
lies in
\mathcal{T}_{j}
.

Similarly,
F_{S_{2}L(\vec{x})}^{L_{2}}F_{S_{2}L_{1}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}\neq 0
if and only if

S_{2}\in\mathscr{S}_{l+|J|-2}^{\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j\notin J}}
. Then by (
4.2
),
we obtain that

\displaystyle F_{L_{2}L_{1}}^{E}=\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}=s_{l}^{(|J|)}(q)-s^{(3-|J|)}_{l+|J|-2}(q)=f_{2l+|J|-1}(q)=f_{\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle}(q),

where both Proposition
4.2
and the equality
\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle=2l+|J|-1

are used.
∎

Under the assumption
[L_{1}]+[L_{2}]=[E]
, we have

\langle L_{1},E\rangle=\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle+\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle=1+\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle

and

\langle E,E\rangle=1=\langle L_{1},L_{1}\rangle+\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle+\langle L_{2},L_{1}\rangle+\langle L_{2},L_{2}\rangle.

Hence,

\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle+\langle L_{2},L_{1}\rangle=-1.

As a consequence,

\langle L_{1},L_{2}\rangle\geq 0\Longleftrightarrow\langle L_{2},L_{1}\rangle\leq-1\Longleftrightarrow\langle L_{1},E\rangle\geq 1.

and some
\vec{x}\in\mathbb{L}
.
Set
N=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\langle E^{\prime},E\rangle\rfloor-1
, and write
\vec{x}-\vec{\omega}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{3}l_{k}\vec{x}_{k}-\vec{c}
with

1\leq l_{k}\leq p_{k}-1
for
1\leq k\leq 3
.
Let
S=S_{i,j}^{(n)}
be an indecomposable torsion sheaf taken from an exceptional
tube
\mathcal{T}_{i}
for a fixed
i\in I
.

Lemma 4.5
.

Keep notations as above. Assume
[E^{\prime}]+[S]=[E]
.

(1)

If
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),\mathrm{top\,}S)\neq 0
, then
n\not\equiv l_{i}\,(\operatorname{mod}\nolimits p_{i})
, and
N=\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor
.

(2)

If
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),\mathrm{top\,}S)\neq 0
, then
n\not\equiv p_{i}-l_{i}\,(\operatorname{mod}\nolimits p_{i})
, and
N=\lfloor\frac{n-(p_{i}-l_{i})}{p_{i}}\rfloor
.

Proof.

We only prove assertion (1). The proof of (2) is similar.

We first claim that
n\not\equiv l_{i}\,(\operatorname{mod}\nolimits p_{i})
.
Otherwise, suppose
n=l_{i}+tp_{i}
for some
t\geq 0
. Then

[L(\vec{x})]-[S]=[L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})]-t\delta
, and hence,

[E^{\prime}]=[E]-[S]=[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x})]-[S]=[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})]-t\delta.

Note that

\langle L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})\rangle=0=\langle L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i}),L(\vec{\omega})\rangle
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\langle E^{\prime},E^{\prime}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})]-t\delta,[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x}-l_{i}\vec{x}_{i})]-t\delta\rangle=2.

This contradicts the fact that
E^{\prime}
is an exceptional object.

Further, we have

\displaystyle\langle E^{\prime},E\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x})]-[S],[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x})]\rangle

\displaystyle=1-\langle[S],[L(\vec{\omega})]+[L(\vec{x})]\rangle

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S,L(\vec{\omega}))+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S,L(\vec{x}))

\displaystyle=1+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,j-l_{i}}^{(n)})+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{i,j}^{(n)},S_{i,j}^{(n)})

\displaystyle=1+\lfloor\frac{n}{p_{i}}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor+\lceil\frac{l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rceil

\displaystyle=2+\lfloor\frac{n}{p_{i}}\rfloor+\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor.

This implies that

N=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\langle E^{\prime},E\rangle\rfloor-1=1+\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor-1=\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor.

∎

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
E,E^{\prime}
be extension bundles and
S=S_{i,j}^{(n)}
be an indecomposable torsion
sheaf taken from an exceptional tube as above.
Assume
[E^{\prime}]+[S]=[E]
and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E,\mathrm{top}\,S)\neq 0
.
Then

\displaystyle F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }N=-1;\\
f_{N+1}-f_{N}+(-1)^{N},&\text{ if }N\geq 0,\end{cases}

where
N=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\langle E^{\prime},E\rangle\rfloor-1
.

Proof.

In view of (
4.1
), we now
calculate
F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}=\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}
. Since
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E,\mathrm{top}\,S)\neq 0
,
we have

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),\mathrm{top\,}S)\neq 0\,\text{ or }\;\,\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),\mathrm{top}\,S)\neq 0.

We only consider the first case, and the second one can be treated similarly.

Without loss of generality, we may take
i=1
, i.e.,
S=S_{1,j}^{(n)}
, and write

\vec{x}-\vec{\omega}=\sum\limits_{k=1}^{3}l_{k}\vec{x}_{k}-\vec{c}

with
1\leq l_{k}\leq p_{k}-1
for
1\leq k\leq 3
. In this case,

\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{x}),S_{1,j})\neq 0
and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),S_{1,j-l_{1}})\neq 0
.

We first calculate the non-zero terms of

\Psi_{1}:=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{E^{\prime}}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{S}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{E^{\prime}}a_{S}}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{x})}.

If
X_{2}=0
, then
X_{4}=S_{1,j}^{(n)}
,
X_{1}=L(\vec{\omega})
and
X_{3}=L(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})
. In this case,

\displaystyle n_{1}:

\displaystyle=-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle=-\langle S_{1,j}^{(n)},L(\vec{\omega})\rangle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{1,j}^{(n)},L(\vec{\omega}))

\displaystyle=\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{1,j}^{(n)},S_{1,j-l_{1}}^{(n)})=\lfloor\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rfloor+1=N+1,\text{\;\;
(by\;\; Lemma \ref{preparing lemma})}

\displaystyle n_{2}:

\displaystyle=\langle X_{1},X_{3}\rangle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega})],[L(\vec{x})]-[S_{1,{j}}^{(n)}]\rangle=-\langle[L(\vec{\omega})],[S_{1,{j}}^{(n)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),S_{1,j}^{(n)})=-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{1,j-l_{1}}^{(n)},S_{1,j}^{(n)})=-\lceil\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rceil,

and

\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{E^{\prime}}a_{S}}=\frac{a_{S}a_{L(\vec{\omega})}a_{L(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})}}{a_{E^{\prime}}a_{S}}=q-1.

If
X_{2}\neq 0
, then
X_{4}=S_{1,j}^{(l)}
and
X_{2}=S_{1,j-l}^{(n-l)}
for some

0\leq l\leq n-1
. Hence,
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),\text{top\,}X_{2})\neq 0
, which implies

j-l\equiv j-l_{1}\,(\operatorname{mod}\nolimits p_{1})
, and thus,
l=l_{1}+tp_{1}
for some
t\geq 0
.
In this case,
X_{1}=L(\vec{\omega}-(n-l)\vec{x}_{1})
and
X_{3}=L(\vec{x}-l\vec{x}_{1})
, and

\displaystyle n_{3}:

\displaystyle=-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle=-\langle S_{1,j}^{(l)},L(\vec{\omega}-(n-l)\vec{x}_{1})\rangle

\displaystyle=t-\langle[S_{1,j}^{(l_{1})}],[L(\vec{\omega})]-[S_{1,j}^{(n)}]+[S_{1,j}^{(l)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=t-(-1)+(\lceil\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rceil-\lceil\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rceil-\lfloor\frac{l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rfloor)-1

\displaystyle=t-\lceil\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rceil+\lceil\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rceil,

\displaystyle n_{4}:

\displaystyle=\langle X_{1},X_{3}\rangle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega}-(n-l)\vec{x}_{1})],[L(\vec{x}-l\vec{x}_{1})]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega})]-[S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)}],{[L(\vec{x})]}-[S_{1,{j}}^{(l)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[L(\vec{\omega})],{[L(\vec{x})]}\rangle-\langle[L(\vec{\omega})],[S_{1,{j}}^{(l)}]\rangle-\langle[S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)}],{[L(\vec{x})]}\rangle+\langle[S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)}],[S_{1,{j}}^{(l)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=0-{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),S_{1,{j}}^{(l)})}+{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)},L(\vec{x}))}+\langle[S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)}],[S_{1,{j}}^{(l)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(S_{1,j-l_{1}}^{(l)},S_{1,{j}}^{(l)})}+{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)},S_{1,{j}}^{(n-l)})}+\langle[S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)}],[S_{1,{j}}^{(l)}]\rangle

\displaystyle=-t+\big(\lfloor\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rfloor-t\big)+\big(\lfloor\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rfloor-\lfloor\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rfloor\big)

\displaystyle=N-2t,

and

\displaystyle\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{E^{\prime}}a_{S}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(q-1)^{2}}{q-1}\times\frac{a_{S_{1,j}^{(l)}}a_{S_{1,j-l}^{(n-l)}}}{a_{S_{1,j}^{(n)}}}

\displaystyle=(q-1)\frac{(q-1)q^{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,j}^{(l)})-1}\cdot(q-1)q^{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)})-1}}{(q-1)q^{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,{j}}^{(n)})-1}}

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{2}q^{-1}q^{\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,{j}}^{(l)})+\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,{j-l}}^{(n-l)})-\operatorname{dim}\nolimits\operatorname{End}\nolimits(S_{1,{j}}^{(n)})}

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{2}q^{-1}q^{\lceil\frac{l}{p_{1}}\rceil+\lceil\frac{n-l}{p_{1}}\rceil-\lceil\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rceil}

\displaystyle=(q-1)^{2}q^{\lceil\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rceil-\lceil\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rceil}.

Since

(4.7)

\displaystyle\Psi_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}q^{n_{1}}f_{n_{2}}\cdot(q-1)+\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{t\geq 0}^{\lfloor\frac{n-l_{1}-1}{p_{1}}\rfloor}q^{-n_{3}}f_{n_{4}}\cdot(q-1)^{2}q^{\lceil\frac{n-l_{1}}{p_{1}}\rceil-\lceil\frac{n}{p_{1}}\rceil}

\displaystyle q^{n_{1}}f_{n_{2}}+\sum_{t\geq 0}(q-1)q^{t}f_{n_{1}-1-2t}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle q^{n_{1}}f_{n_{2}}+f_{n_{1}}+(-1)^{n_{1}-1}.

Here the last equality follows from (

).

Next we consider the non-zero terms of

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}:=\frac{1}{q-1}\sum_{[X_{1}],[X_{2}],[X_{3}],[X_{4}]}q^{-\langle X_{4},X_{1}\rangle}F_{X_{3}X_{1}}^{X}F_{X_{4}X_{2}}^{X^{\prime}}\frac{a_{X_{1}}a_{X_{2}}a_{X_{3}}a_{X_{4}}}{a_{X}a_{X^{\prime}}}F_{X_{2}X_{1}}^{L(\vec{x})}F_{X_{4}X_{3}}^{L(\vec{\omega})}.

Since
\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits^{1}(L(\vec{\omega}),L(\vec{x}))=0
and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L(\vec{\omega}),S_{1,j})=0
, we have
X_{4}=0
. Thus,

X_{2}=S=S_{1,{j}}^{(n)}
,
X_{3}=L(\vec{\omega})
and
X_{1}=L(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})
. In this case,

(4.8)

\displaystyle\Psi_{2}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q-1}F_{SL(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})}^{L(\vec{x})}F_{L(\vec{\omega})L(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})}^{E^{\prime}}\frac{a_{S}a_{L(\vec{\omega})}a_{L(\vec{x}-n\vec{x}_{1})}}{a_{E^{\prime}}a_{S}}=f_{n_{5}},

where

By Proposition

F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}=\Psi_{1}-\Psi_{2}=q^{n_{1}}f_{n_{2}}+f_{n_{1}}+(-1)^{n_{1}-1}-f_{n_{5}}.

If

, i.e.,

, then

\displaystyle F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}=f_{0}+f_{0}+(-1)-f_{-1}=1.

If

by Lemma

.
Therefore,

and thus,

\displaystyle F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}

\displaystyle=f_{N+1}-f_{N}+(-1)^{N}.

This finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Keep notations as above. Since
F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}
is
a polynomial in
q=|{\mathbf{k}}|
of degree
N+1
, we have
F_{SE^{\prime}}^{E}(q)\neq 0
when

. In this case, there exists a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow E^{\prime}\longrightarrow E\longrightarrow S\longrightarrow 0

if and only if

and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(E,\mathrm{top}\,S)\neq 0

However, this is not true in general. For example, let
\mathbb{X}
be
the weighted projective line of weight type
(2,2,2)
. Let

be an indecomposable torsion
sheaf taken from an exceptional tube and

be the Auslander

Then we have

5.
Derived Hall algebras and Hall polynomials for tame quivers

In this section we recall from
[
39
,
40
]
the definition of the derived Hall algebra
of the bounded derived category of a finitary abelian category. In terms of the
isomorphism between derived Hall algebras of tame quivers and weighted projective lines of
domestic type, we apply the results in the previous sections to obtain Hall polynomials for
representations of tame quivers which are calculated in
[
34
,
38
]
by a case-by-case
analysis.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a finitary abelian category over a finite field
\mathbf{k}
and

D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
be the bounded derived category of
\mathcal{A}
. For any three
objects
X,Y,L
in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, let
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{D^{b}(\mathcal{A})}(X,Y)_{L}
denote the
subset of
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{D^{b}(\mathcal{A})}(X,Y)
consisting of morphisms
X\to Y
whose cone is
isomorphic to
L
, and set

\{X,Y\}:=\prod_{i>0}|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{D^{b}(\mathcal{A})}(X[i],Y)|^{(-1)^{i}}.

For
X\in D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, let
\widetilde{a}_{X}:=|\mathrm{Aut}_{D^{b}(\mathcal{A})}(X)|

be the cardinality of the automorphism group. Recall from
[
39
,
40
]
that
the
derived Hall number
associated with three objects

in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
is defined as

(5.1)

\displaystyle G_{XY}^{L}:=\frac{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(L,X)_{Y[1]}|}{\widetilde{a}_{X}}\cdot\frac{\{L,X\}}{\{X,X\}}=\frac{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(Y,L)_{X}|}{\widetilde{a}_{Y}}\cdot\frac{\{Y,L\}}{\{Y,Y\}},

which can be described by the following derived Riedtmann–Peng formula
(c.f.,
[
32
, Prop. 3.3]
):

G_{XY}^{L}=\frac{|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(X,Y[1])_{L[1]}|\{X,Y[1]\}\{L,L\}}{\{X,X\}\{Y,Y\}}\cdot\frac{a_{L}}{a_{X}a_{Y}}.

The derived Hall algebra
\mathcal{DH}(\mathcal{A})
of
\mathcal{A}

is by definition the
\mathbb{Q}
-vector space with the basis

\{u_{[X]}\mid[X]\in\mathrm{Iso}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))\}
, endowed with multiplication defined by

u_{[X]}\cdot u_{[Y]}=\sum_{[L]\in{\rm{Iso}}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))}G_{XY}^{L}\,u_{[L]}.

It is an associative algebra, that is, for
given objects
X,Y,Z,M
in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
, we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{[L]\in{\rm{Iso}}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))}G_{XY}^{L}G_{LZ}^{M}=\sum_{[L]\in{\rm{Iso}}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))}G_{XL}^{M}G_{YZ}^{L},

where both sums are taken over the set
{\rm{Iso}}(D^{b}(\mathcal{A}))
of isomorphism
classes of objects in
D^{b}(\mathcal{A})
.

Derived Hall algebras are closely related to Hall algebras. Indeed, we have
the following embedding of algebras.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a hereditary abelian category. Then there exists
an algebra embedding

\varphi:\mathcal{H}(\mathcal{A})\longrightarrow\mathcal{DH}(\mathcal{A}),

taking

\qquad\qquad[X]\longmapsto\{X,X\}\cdot u_{[X]}.

Proof.

This follows directly from the definitions and the equalities

|\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits_{D^{b}(\mathcal{A})}(X,Y[1])_{L[1]}|=|\operatorname{Ext}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}^{1}(X,Y)_{L}|,\;\,\forall\,X,Y,L\in{\mathcal{A}}.

∎

, Thm. 7.5]

Assume that
P,P^{\prime}

-2

in
\mathscr{P}
and
R

\mathscr{R}
taken
from an exceptional tube. Assume
[P^{\prime}]+[R]=[P]
and
\operatorname{Hom}\nolimits(P,\mathrm{top\,}S)\neq 0
,
and set
N=\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\langle P^{\prime},P\rangle\rfloor-1.

Then

\displaystyle F_{RP^{\prime}}^{P}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{ if }N=-1;\\
f_{N+1}-f_{N}+(-1)^{N},&\text{ if }N\geq 0.\end{cases}

Proof.

We only need to deal with the Euler form. In the first case in
Proposition
4.6
, we have

\langle E^{\prime},E\rangle=\langle E,E\rangle-\langle S,E\rangle=1-\langle S,L(\vec{\omega})\rangle-\langle S,L(\vec{x})\rangle,

where
-\langle S,L(\vec{\omega})\rangle=\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor+1
and

-\langle S,L(\vec{\omega})\rangle-1\leq-\langle S,L(\vec{x})\rangle\leq-\langle S,L(\vec{\omega})\rangle
.
Hence,
\lfloor\frac{1}{2}\langle P^{\prime},P\rangle\rfloor=\lfloor\frac{n-l_{i}}{p_{i}}\rfloor+1
.
The second case can be treated similarly.
Therefore, we can transform the Hall polynomials in Proposition
4.6
to those
in
{\mathbf{k}}Q\mbox{-mod}
.

Lemma 5.8

(

Let

(5.3)

\frac{G_{XY}^{L}}{a_{L}\cdot\{L,L\}}=\frac{G_{Y[1]L}^{X}}{a_{X}\cdot\{X,X\}}=\frac{G_{LX[-1]}^{Y}}{a_{Y}\cdot\{Y,Y\}}.

In particular, if
X,Y,L\in\mathcal{A}

(5.4)

\frac{G_{XY}^{L}}{a_{L}}=\frac{G_{Y[1]L}^{X}}{a_{X}}=\frac{G_{LX[-1]}^{Y}}{a_{Y}}.

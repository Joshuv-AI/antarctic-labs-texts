Sums of three Fibonacci numbers as concatenations of three repdigits in base b b
Passimzouwé Dagou, Pagdame Tiebekabe, Kouèssi Norbert Adédji, Kokou Tchariè

Abstract




Abstract
.
In this paper, we investigate sums of three Fibonacci numbers that can be expressed as concatenations of three repdigits in base
b
, where
b\geq 2
is an integer. We prove that for bases
2\leq b\leq 10
, only finitely many such sums exist, and we determine all of them explicitly. Among these solutions, the largest occurs for
b=4
and is given by

F_{42}+F_{29}+F_{20}=268435290=\overline{33333333331122}_{4}.

1.
Introduction

The interaction between recurrence sequences and digital representations of integers has attracted considerable attention in recent years. In particular, problems concerning the occurrence of special digital patterns within classical sequences such as the Fibonacci sequence have proved to be a rich source of Diophantine questions combining number theory, recurrence relations, and combinatorial properties of numeration systems. Among these patterns,
repdigits
—integers whose digits are all identical in a given base—and their
concatenations
play a central role.

In a seminal paper published in 2019, Alahmadi, Altassan, Luca, and Shoaib (see
[
4
]
) initiated a systematic study of Fibonacci numbers that can be expressed as concatenations of two repdigits. Their work opened a new line of research, leading to several subsequent contributions that extended the notion of concatenation and repdigits to other linear recurrence sequences. Notably, analogous problems have been investigated for sequences such as Pell, Pell–Lucas, Padovan, Perrin, and balancing numbers, highlighting the broad relevance and flexibility of this framework (see
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
,
[
3
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
14
]
,
[
15
]
,
[
16
]
).

Motivated by these developments, and after a careful review of the existing literature, we address a natural and yet unexplored question:

can sums of Fibonacci numbers admit representations as concatenations of repdigits?

More precisely, in this paper we study the sum of three Fibonacci numbers that can be written as the concatenation of three repdigits in base
b
. The main objective of this work is to characterize such representations, establish finiteness results where applicable, and determine all possible solutions under suitable constraints. Our results extend and complement previous studies on digital properties of Fibonacci numbers, and contribute further insight into the interplay between recurrence sequences and digital concatenation phenomena.

Let
\{F_{n}\}_{n\geq 0}
be the Fibonacci sequence given by
F_{n+2}=F_{n+1}+F_{n},
with initial values
F_{0}=0
and
F_{1}=1.
If

(\alpha,\beta)=\left(\dfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2},\dfrac{1-\sqrt{5}}{2}\right)

is the pair of roots of the characteristic equation
x^{2}-x-1=0
of the Fibonacci numbers, then the Binet’s formula for its general term is

(1)

\displaystyle F_{n}=\dfrac{\alpha^{n}-\beta^{n}}{\alpha-\beta}\quad n\geq 0.

It can be seen that
1<\alpha<2,\;-1<\beta<0
and
\alpha\beta=-1.
The following relations between
n
-th Fibonacci number
F_{n}
and
\alpha
is well known

(2)

\displaystyle\alpha^{n-2}<F_{n}<\alpha^{n-1}\quad n\geq 0.

We recall that a positive integer
R
is called a base
b
-repdigit if its all digits are the same in base
b.
That is,
R
is of the form

R=\dfrac{d(b^{m}-1)}{b-1}=\overline{\underbrace{d\ldots d}_{m\penalty\ times}}\penalty\ _{(b)},

for some positive integers
d,m
with
1\leq d\leq b-1
and
m\geq 1.
When
b=10,
we omit the base and we simply say that
R
is a repdigit. Therefore we
deal with the following Diophantine equation

(3)

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}=\overline{\underbrace{d_{1}\ldots d_{1}}_{\ell_{1}\penalty\ times}\underbrace{d_{2}\ldots d_{2}}_{\ell_{2}\penalty\ times}\underbrace{d_{3}\ldots d_{3}}_{\ell_{3}\penalty\ times}}\penalty\ _{(b)}

where
d_{1},\ell_{1},\ell_{2},\ell_{3}\geq 1
and
d_{1},d_{2},d_{3}\in\{0,1,\ldots,b-1\}.

Therefore the main results of this paper are stated in the following theorems.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Linear form in logarithms

Denote by
\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{s}
algebraic numbers, none of which is
0
or
1
, and let

\log\eta_{1},\ldots,\log\eta_{s}
be a fixed determination of their logarithms.
Let
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{s})
and denote by
d_{\mathbb{K}}=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]
the degree of
\mathbb{K}
over
\mathbb{Q}
.

For any
\eta\in\mathbb{K}
, let its minimal polynomial over
\mathbb{Z}
be

f(X)=a_{0}X^{\delta}+a_{1}X^{\delta-1}+\cdots+a_{\delta}=a_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{\delta}(X-\eta^{(j)}),

where
\eta^{(j)}
,
j=1,\ldots,\delta
, are the roots of
f(X)
.
The
absolute logarithmic Weil height
of
\eta
is defined by

h_{0}(\eta)=\frac{1}{\delta}\left(\log|a_{0}|+\sum_{j=1}^{\delta}\log\big(\max\{|\eta^{(j)}|,1\}\big)\right).

In particular, if
\eta=p/q
is a rational number in lowest terms, i.e.,
\gcd(p,q)=1
and
q>0
, then
h_{0}(\eta)=\log\max(|p|,q)
.

The
modified height

h^{\prime}(\eta)
is defined by

h^{\prime}(\eta)=\frac{1}{d_{\mathbb{K}}}\max\{h(\eta),|\log\eta|,1\},

where
h(\eta)=d_{\mathbb{K}}\,h_{0}(\eta)
is the standard logarithmic Weil height of
\eta
.

Consider the linear form

L(z_{1},\ldots,z_{s})=b_{1}z_{1}+\cdots+b_{s}z_{s},

where
b_{1},\ldots,b_{s}
are integers, not all zero. Its modified height is

h^{\prime}(L)=\frac{1}{d_{\mathbb{K}}}\max\{h(L),1\},

where

h(L)=d_{\mathbb{K}}\log\left(\max_{1\leq j\leq s}\left\{\frac{|b_{j}|}{b}\right\}\right)

is the logarithmic Weil height of
L
, with
b=\gcd(b_{1},\ldots,b_{s})
.

If we define
B=\max\{|b_{1}|,\ldots,|b_{s}|,e\}
, then we have the simple bound

h^{\prime}(L)\leq\log B.

With these notations, we are now able to state the following fundamental result due to Baker and Wüstholz
[
5
]
.

Theorem 3
.

If
\Gamma=L(\log\eta_{1},\ldots,\log\eta_{s})\neq 0
, then

\log|\Gamma|\geq-C(s,d_{\mathbb{K}})\,h^{\prime}(\eta_{1})\cdots h^{\prime}(\eta_{s})\,h^{\prime}(L),

where

C(s,d_{\mathbb{K}})=18(s+1)!\,s^{s+1}(32d_{\mathbb{K}})^{s+2}\log(2sd_{\mathbb{K}}).

With
|\Lambda|\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, where

\Lambda=e^{\Gamma}-1=\eta_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\eta_{s}^{b_{s}}-1,

we have
\tfrac{1}{2}|\Gamma|\leq|\Lambda|\leq 2|\Gamma|
so that

\log\left|\eta_{1}^{b_{1}}\cdots\eta_{s}^{b_{s}}-1\right|\geq\log|\Gamma|-\log 2.

We apply Theorem
3
mainly in the situation where
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{5})
,
s=3
and
d_{\mathbb{K}}=2
. In this case we obtain

C(3,2)=18\cdot 4!\cdot 3^{4}\cdot 64^{5}\log 12<9.34\cdot 10^{13}.

This value will be used throughout the paper without further mention. We recall below some well-known properties of the absolute logarithmic height.

h_{0}(\eta+\gamma)\leq h_{0}(\eta)+h_{0}(\gamma)+\log 2,

h_{0}(\eta\gamma^{\pm 1})\leq h_{0}(\eta)+h_{0}(\gamma),

h_{0}(\eta^{\ell})=|\ell|\,h_{0}(\eta),

where
\eta,\gamma
are algebraic numbers and
\ell\in\mathbb{Z}
. We also need the following result from Guzmán and Luca
[
13
]
.

Lemma 1
.

(Lemma 7 of
[
13
]
)

If
l\geq 1,H>(4l^{2})^{l}
and
H>L/(\log L)^{l}
, then
L<2^{l}H(\log H)^{l}
.

2.2.
Reduction method

The bounds for the variables derived from Baker’s theory are far too large to allow for effective computational verification. To reduce the bounds we use the reduction method due to Bravo, Gómez and Luca
[
7
]
which is a modified version due to Dujella and Pethő (see
[
11
]
).

Lemma 2
.

Let
M
be a positive integer,
p/q
be a convergent of the continued fraction expansion of irrational number
\tau
such as
q>6M
, and
A,B,\mu
be some real numbers with
A>0
and
B>1.
Furthermore, let

\varepsilon:=||\mu q||-M.||\tau q||.

If
\varepsilon>0
, the there is no solution to the inequality

0<|u\tau-v+\mu|<AB^{-w}

in positive integers
u,v
and
w
with

u\leq M\text{ and }w\geq\frac{\log(Aq/\varepsilon)}{\log B}.

Here, for a real number
X
,
||X||
denotes the distance from
X
to the nearest integer, that is,
||X||:=\min_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}|X-n|
.

3.
Proof the Theorem
1

For the proof of Theorem
1
, we may assume without loss of generality that

n_{1}>300
, since the remaining cases can be checked directly by computation. The proof of Theorem
1
is divided into several steps. We derive initial bounds for the variables using estimates coming from linear forms in logarithms.

Step 3.1
.

We prove the following Lemma.

Lemma 3
.

All the solution of equation (
3
) satisfy

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha\right\}<1.5\times 10^{14}\log^{2}b\log(n_{1}+2).

Proof.

As a first step, we analyze the Diophantine equation (
3
) and transform it into the equivalent form

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}

\displaystyle=\overline{\underbrace{d_{1}\,\ldots\,d_{1}}_{\ell_{1}\ \text{times}}\;\underbrace{d_{2}\,\ldots\,d_{3}}_{\ell_{2}\ \text{times}}\;\underbrace{d_{3}\,\ldots\,d_{3}}_{\ell_{3}\ \text{times}}}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{d_{1}\,\ldots\,d_{1}}_{\ell_{1}\ \text{times}}.b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+\underbrace{d_{2}\,\ldots\,d_{2}}_{\ell_{2}\ \text{times}}.b^{\ell_{3}}+\underbrace{d_{3}\,\ldots\,d_{3}}_{\ell_{3}\ \text{times}}

\displaystyle F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}

\displaystyle=d_{1}\left(\frac{b^{\ell_{1}}-1}{b-1}\right).b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+d_{2}\left(\frac{b^{\ell_{2}}-1}{b-1}\right).b^{\ell_{3}}+d_{3}\left(\frac{b^{\ell_{3}}-1}{b-1}\right)

and therefore we get

(4)

F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}=\frac{1}{b-1}\left(d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{1}-d_{2})b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{2}-d_{3})b^{\ell_{3}}-d_{3}\right).

We first observe that combining (
2
) and (
3
) yields

b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}>F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}>F_{n_{1}}>\alpha^{n_{1}-2}

and

(5)

3\alpha^{n_{1}-1}>3F_{n_{1}}>F_{n_{1}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}\geq b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-1}.

By taking logarithm on the both sides of (
5
), we obtain

(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}-1)\log b<(n_{1}-1)\log\alpha+\log 3

which leads to

\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}<n_{1}+2.

Using (
1
) and (
4
), we get

\frac{\alpha^{n_{1}}-\beta^{n_{1}}}{\sqrt{5}}+F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}}=\frac{1}{b-1}\left(d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{1}-d_{2})b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{2}-d_{3})b^{\ell_{3}}-d_{3}\right)

which implies that

\displaystyle(b-1)\alpha^{n_{1}}-\sqrt{5}\,d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{5}\Big((d_{1}-d_{2})b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+(d_{2}-d_{3})b^{\ell_{3}}+d_{3}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{5}\,(b-1)(F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}})+(b-1)\beta^{n_{1}},

\displaystyle\Big|(b-1)\alpha^{n_{1}}-\sqrt{5}\,d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}\Big|

\displaystyle<\sqrt{5}\Big((b-1)b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+(b-1)b^{\ell_{3}}+(b-1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\sqrt{5}\,(b-1)(F_{n_{2}}+F_{n_{3}})+(b-1)|\beta^{n_{1}}|,

\displaystyle<\sqrt{5}\Big((b-1)b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+(b-1)b^{\ell_{3}}+(b-1)\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{5}\,(b-1)\alpha^{n_{2}-1}+(b-1)\alpha^{-n_{1}},

\displaystyle<\sqrt{5}\,(b-1)b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}\Big(1+\frac{1}{b^{\ell_{2}}}+\frac{1}{b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+2\sqrt{5}\,(b-1)\alpha^{n_{2}-1}+(b-1)\alpha^{-n_{1}},

\displaystyle<1.75\sqrt{5}\,b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}+1}+2\sqrt{5}\,b\alpha^{n_{2}-1}+b\alpha^{-n_{1}},

\displaystyle<1.75\sqrt{5}\,b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}+1}+4.48\,b\alpha^{n_{2}-1}.

Next,

(6)

\left|(b-1)\alpha^{n_{1}}-\sqrt{5}d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}\right|<6.7b\max\{b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}},\alpha^{n_{2}}\}

where we used the fact that
n_{1}>300
. Dividing both sides of (
6
) by
\sqrt{5}d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left(\frac{b-1}{\sqrt{5}d_{1}}\right).\alpha^{n_{1}}.b^{-(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3})}-1\right|

\displaystyle<\max\left\{\frac{6.7b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}+1}}{\sqrt{5}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}},\frac{6.7b\alpha^{n_{2}}}{\sqrt{5}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}}\right\}

\displaystyle<\max\left\{\frac{6.7b^{-\ell_{1}+1}}{\sqrt{5}},\frac{6.7b\alpha^{n_{2}}}{\sqrt{5}\alpha^{n_{1}-2}}\right\}

(7)

\displaystyle<7.85b\max\left\{b^{-\ell_{1}},\alpha^{-(n_{1}-n_{2})}\right\}.

We now define the linear form

\Gamma_{1}=\log\left(\frac{b-1}{d_{1}\sqrt{5}}\right)+n_{1}\log\alpha-(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3})\log b

and put

(8)

\Lambda_{1}:=\mathrm{e}^{\Gamma_{1}}-1=\left(\frac{b-1}{\sqrt{5}d_{1}}\right).\alpha^{n_{1}}.b^{-(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3})}-1.

We assume that
\left|\Lambda_{1}\right|\leq 0.5
and use the theorem of Baker and Wüstholz (Theorem
3
) with data

\eta_{1}:=\frac{b-1}{d_{1}\sqrt{5}},\eta_{2}:=\alpha,\eta_{3}:=b,b_{1}=1,b_{2}:=n_{1},b_{3}:=-(\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}),s:=3.

Since
\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}<n_{1}+2
, we can take
B=n_{1}+2
. Observe that
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{Q}\left(\eta_{1},\eta_{2},\eta_{3}\right)=\mathbb{Q}(\alpha)
, so
d_{\mathbb{K}}:=[\mathbb{K}:\mathbb{Q}]=2
. We proceed to apply Theorem
3
on (
8
). We have first to observe that
\Lambda_{1}\neq 0
. Indeed, if it were equal to zero, it would follow that
\alpha^{n_{1}}=\frac{\sqrt{5}d_{1}}{b-1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}
. That is,
\alpha^{2n_{1}}\in\mathbb{Q}
, which is false for any
n_{1}>0
.
Using the properties of the logarithmic height, we have

\displaystyle h_{0}(\eta_{1})=h_{0}\left(\frac{b-1}{\sqrt{5}d_{1}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq h_{0}\left(\frac{b-1}{d_{1}}\right)+h_{0}\left(\sqrt{5}\right)

\displaystyle\leq h_{0}(b-1)+h_{0}(d_{1})+\frac{\log 5}{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\log b+\frac{\log 5}{2}\leq 3.2\log b\text{ since }b\geq 2.

Then, we have
h^{\prime}(\eta_{1})<3.2\log b.
Similarly, we have
h^{\prime}\left(\eta_{2}\right)=h(\alpha)=\frac{1}{2},\;h\left(\eta_{3}\right)=h(b)=\log b
. Theorem
3
tells us that

\displaystyle\log\left|\Lambda_{1}\right|

\displaystyle>-C(3,2)\left(3.2\log b\right)\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(\log b\right)\left(\log(n_{1}+2)\right)-\log 2.

Comparing this last inequality with (
7
) leads to

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha\right\}<1.5\times 10^{14}\log^{2}b\log(n_{1}+2).

Note that in the case that
\left|\Lambda_{1}\right|>0.5
, inequality (
7
) is possible only if either
\ell_{1}\leq 4
and
n_{1}-n_{2}\leq\left\lfloor\frac{\log(15.7b)}{\log\alpha}\right\rfloor
, which are covered by the bound provided by Lemma
3

∎

We now distinguish between the following two cases:

Case 1:

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha\right\}=(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha,

Case 2:

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha\right\}=\ell_{1}\log b.

We will treat these two cases separately in the following steps.

Step 3.2
.

We consider Case 1 and show that under the assumption that

(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha<1.5\times 10^{14}\log^{2}b\log(n_{1}+2)

we obtain that

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{3})\log\alpha\right\}<7.48\times 10^{27}\log^{3}b\log^{2}(n_{1}+2).

Since we consider Case 1, we assume that

\min\left\{\ell_{1}\log b,(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha\right\}=(n_{1}-n_{2})\log\alpha<1.5\times 10^{14}\log^{2}b\log(n_{1}+2).

Using (
1
) and (
4
), we get

\frac{\alpha^{n_{1}}-\beta^{n_{1}}}{\sqrt{5}}+\frac{\alpha^{n_{2}}-\beta^{n_{2}}}{\sqrt{5}}+F_{n_{3}}=\frac{1}{b-1}\left(d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{1}-d_{2})b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}-(d_{2}-d_{3})b^{\ell_{3}}-d_{3}\right)

which implies that

\displaystyle(b-1)\alpha^{n_{1}}+(b-1)\alpha^{n_{2}}-\sqrt{5}d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{5}\left((d_{1}-d_{2})b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+(d_{2}-d_{3})b^{\ell_{3}}+d_{3}\right)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{5}(b-1)F_{n_{3}}+(b-1)\left(\beta^{n_{1}}+\beta^{n_{2}}\right).

Thus

\displaystyle\left|(b-1)\alpha^{n_{1}}\left(1+\alpha^{n_{2}-n_{1}}\right)-\sqrt{5}d_{1}b^{\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}\right|

\displaystyle<\sqrt{5}\left((b-1)b^{\ell_{2}+\ell_{3}}+(b-1)b^{\ell_{3}}+(b-1)\right)

\displaystyle+\sqrt{5}(b-1)\alpha^{n_{3}}+2(b-1)\alpha^{-n_{2}}

Remark 4.1
.

For this last step to reduce the upper bound on
n_{1}
, the numerical resolution of the problem encountered a major technical difficulty due to its combinatorial structure, which involves seven nested loops, leading to an exponential growth in the number of iterations as the base
b
increases. A theoretical complexity analysis showed that, for values such as
b=10
, a classical sequential execution in Wolfram Mathematica would have required an estimated computation time of nearly 248 years, making any exhaustive exploration unrealistic on a standard architecture.
To overcome this limitation, the algorithm was ported to a parallel computing architecture using a NVIDIA RTX A2000 GPU through the CUDA framework. This approach enables the simultaneous distribution of independent iterations across thousands of processing cores, thereby transforming the problem into a massively parallelizable task. The observed performance gain exceeds a
20,000\times
speed-up compared to the sequential execution: for instance, the computation for
b=2
, initially estimated at 13 months in sequential mode, was completed in approximately 8 minutes, while the case
b=3
, estimated at 5 years, was completed in about 50 minutes. Thanks to this architecture, higher bases (up to
b=10
) were processed within only a few hours, making it possible to effectively determine the numerical bounds presented in this work.

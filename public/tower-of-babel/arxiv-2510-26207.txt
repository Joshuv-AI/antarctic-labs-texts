A combinatorial perspective on the Kemeny constant and more
Luis Fredes and Jean Francois Marckert

Abstract
Let 𝖬 {\sf M} be an irreducible transition matrix on a finite state space V V . For a Markov chain C = ( C k , k ≥ 0 ) C=(C_{k},k\geq 0) with transition matrix 𝖬 {\sf M} , let τ u ≥ 1 \tau^{\geq 1}_{u} denote the first positive hitting time of u u by C C , and ρ \rho the unique invariant measure of 𝖬 {\sf M} . Kemeny proved that if X X is sampled according to ρ \rho independently of C C , the expected value of the first positive hitting time of X X by C C does not depend on the starting state of the chain: all the values ( 𝔼 ⁡ ( τ X ≥ 1 | C 0 = u ) , u ∈ V ) \left(\mathbb{E}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X}\penalty\ |\penalty\ C_{0}=u),u\in V\right) are equal. In this paper, we show that this property follows from a more general result: the generating function ∑ v ∈ V 𝔼 ⁡ ( x τ v ≥ 1 | C 0 = u ) ​ det ( 𝖨𝖽 − x ​ 𝖬 ( v ) ) \sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\penalty\ |\penalty\ C_{0}=u\right)\det\left({\sf Id}-x{\sf M}^{(v)}\right) is independent of the starting state u u , where 𝖬 ( v ) {\sf M}^{(v)} is obtained from 𝖬 {\sf M} by deleting the row and column corresponding to the state v v . The factors appearing in this generating function are: first, the probability generating function of τ v ≥ 1 \tau^{\geq 1}_{v} , and second, the sequence of determinants ( det ( 𝖨𝖽 − x ​ 𝖬 ( v ) ) , v ∈ V ) , \left(\det({\sf Id}-x{\sf M}^{(v)}),v\in V\right), which, for x = 1 x=1 , is known to be proportional to the invariant measure ( ρ u , u ∈ V ) (\rho_{u},u\in V) . From this property, we deduce several further results, including relations involving higher moments of τ X ≥ 1 \tau_{X}^{\geq 1} , which are of independent interest.

because
{\sf G}^{(0)}_{u,v}(1)=\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\penalty\ |_{x=1}=1=\mathbb{E}_{u}\left({\sf Fac}_{0}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{v})\right)
since

is almost surely finite.
By differentiating (
1.6
)
n
times and then evaluating at

, we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf K}^{\geq 1,(n)}(1)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n}{s}{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq 1,(s)}(1)\,\pi_{v}^{(n-s)}(1).

(2.3)

Corollary 2.3
.

For all
n\geq 0
, all
u\in V
,

\displaystyle{\bf K}^{\geq 1,(n)}(1)/n!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{s=0}^{n\wedge(|V|-1)}\sum_{v\in V}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{u}\left({\sf Fac}_{n-s}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{v})\right)}{(n-s)!}\;\frac{\pi^{(s)}_{v}(1)}{s!}.

(2.4)

In particular, for
n=1
, for all
u\in V
,

\displaystyle{\bf K}^{\geq 1,(1)}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1})\pi_{v}+\sum_{v\in V}\pi^{(1)}_{v}(1),

(2.5)

so that
{\bf Q}^{\geq 1}(u)=\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1})\pi_{v}
does not depend on
u
(this is the Kemeny theorem).

Since
{\bf K}^{\geq 1}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
|V|
, for
n\geq|V|+1

the left hand side of (
2.4
) becomes zero, so that the “
s=0
term” in the right hand side is the opposite of the sum of the others. For
n\geq|V|+1
,

\sum_{v\in V}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{u}\left({\sf Fac}_{n}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{v})\right)}{n!}\;\pi^{(0)}_{v}(1)={\bf Z}\,\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(\frac{{\sf Fac}_{n}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})}{n!}\right)=-\sum_{s=1}^{|V|-1}\sum_{v\in V}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{u}\left({\sf Fac}_{n-s}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{v})\right)}{(n-s)!}\;\frac{\pi^{(s)}_{v}(1)}{s!},

and again, this does not depend on
u
.

Remark 2.4

(About the variance of
\tau_{X}^{\geq 1}
)
.

Take
n=2
in
2.3
. For
|V|\geq 3
, we get that

\displaystyle{\bf K}^{\geq 1,(2)}(1)/2!

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{{\bf Z}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(\tau_{X}^{\geq 1}\left(\tau_{X}^{\geq 1}-1\right)\right)+\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}\right)\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1)+\sum_{v\in V}\frac{\pi_{v}^{(2)}(1)}{2}

(2.6)

is independent of
u
. Since
\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})
, is independent of

too, we preserve the independence with respect to

of the right hand side of (

) by adding
\frac{{\bf Z}}{2}\left(\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})-\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})^{2}\right)-\sum_{v\in V}\frac{\pi_{v}^{(2)}(1)}{2}
. Therefore, there exists a constant
\mathfrak{C}
(depending on

\max_{u\in V}{\sf Var}_{u}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})-\min_{u\in V}{\sf Var}_{u}(\tau^{\geq 1}_{X})=\frac{2}{{\bf Z}}\left[\max_{u}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}\right)\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1)-\min_{u\in V}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}\right)\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1)\right].

2.3
Alternative algebraic representation of
{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq t}
and consequences

The generating functions
{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq t}(x)
can be expressed using simple matrix operations (for
x\in\overline{B}(0,R_{t})
): letting
{\sf M}^{\star v}
be the matrix that coincides with
{\sf M}
except for its
v
-th column which contains only zero values. For
t\geq 1
, we get

{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq t}(x)=\left[(x{\sf M})^{t-1}\left(\sum_{k\geq 0}(x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{k}\right)(x{\sf M})\right]_{u,v}=\left[(x{\sf M})^{t-1}({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{-1}(x{\sf M})\right]_{u,v},

since a path
p=(p_{0},\dots,p_{k})
from
u
to
v
contributes to
{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq t}(x)
if :

\bullet

k\geq t
,

\bullet
it starts at
u
, meaning that
p_{0}=u
,

\bullet
it avoids
v
from time
t
to
k-1
, i.e.
p_{t},\cdots,p_{k-1}
are different from
v
and

\bullet
it ends at
v
, this is
p_{k}=v
.

For
t=0
, there is a difference since

\displaystyle{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq 0}(x)=\textbf{1}_{u=v}+\textbf{1}_{u\neq v}\left[({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{-1}(x{\sf M})\right]_{u,v}

(2.7)

and these identities are valid at least for
x\in(-1,1)
.
Taking into account that for
v=u
,

\left[({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{-1}(x{\sf M})\right]_{u,v}=\left[({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{-1}(x{\sf M})\right]_{v,v}
, which represents the generating function of paths of size at least one with first hitting time of
v
greater than 1, that is, it is
\mathbb{E}_{v}(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{v}})
.
we get

\displaystyle{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq 0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{u=v}\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{v}}\right)\right)+\left[({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}^{\star v})^{-1}(x{\sf M})\right]_{u,v}

(2.8)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\textbf{1}_{u=v}\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{v}}\right)\right)+{\sf G}_{u,v}^{\geq 1}(x).

(2.9)

Multiplying by
\pi_{v}(x)
and summing over
v
for fixed
u
gives,

{\bf K}^{\geq 0}_{u}(x)=\pi_{u}(x)\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{u}}\right)\right)+{\bf K}^{\geq 1}_{u}(x).

Now, we can divide both size by
{\bf K}^{\geq 0}_{u}(x)
(and recall that
{\bf K}^{\geq 1}_{u}(x)=x{\bf K}^{\geq 0}_{u}(x)
) and get the relation

\displaystyle 1-\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{u}}\right)=(1-x)\frac{{\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)}{\pi_{u}(x)}=\frac{\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})}{\pi_{u}(x)}=\frac{1}{({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})^{-1}_{u,u}}

(2.10)

so that

Corollary 2.5
.

For all
u\in V
and
x\in B(0,R_{1})

\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{u}}\right)=1-\frac{1}{({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})^{-1}_{u,u}},

moreover, for all
u,v\in V

\left[({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})^{-1}\right]_{u,v}={\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 0}_{v}}\right)}/\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{v}}\right)\right).

Proof.

The first statement is a consequence of the previous discussion, while for the second, we start from
(
1.11
), use
G_{u,v}^{\geq 0}(x)=\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 0}_{v}}\right)
and from the first statement of the present corollary.
∎

Observe that since
{\sf M}
is an irreducible transition matrix, for
x\in(0,1)
,

\displaystyle N_{x}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{k\geq 0}(1-x)x^{k}M^{k}=(1-x)({\sf Id}_{d}-xM)^{-1}

(2.11)

is also an irreducible transition matrix, and then the sum of the coefficient on each row is one. Hence,
2.5
yields to

Corollary 2.6
.

For all
u\in V
and
x\in B(0,1)
,

\sum_{v\in V}{\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 0}_{v}}\right)}/\left({1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau^{\geq 1}_{v}}\right)}\right)={1}/({1-x}).

Remark 2.7
.

Corollary
2.6
, states also a constancy result, since the right hand side is independent of
u\in V
.

Remark 2.8
.

The matrix defined on equation (
2.11
) is of independent interest : in the irreducible case when taking the limit at
x=1
it gives the invariant measure matrix whose rows are equal to the invariant measure. This matrix is intimately related with the group inverse (see
[
12
]
) and the fundamental matrix (see
[

]
); both matrices give access to the matrix whose coefficients are the expectations of first passage times indexed by initial and target points, among others (see
[

2
]

Corollary 2.9
.

\frac{d}{dx}\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})=-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\pi_{v}(x)

Proof.

By the Jacobi formula for the derivative of a determinant one has

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\sf Tr}({\sf Adj}({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})(-{\sf M}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}{\sf Tr}\big({\sf Adj}({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})-{\sf Adj}({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}{\sf Tr}\big(\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M}){\sf Id}-{\sf Adj}({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\left(\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})-\pi_{v}(x)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\pi_{v}(x),

where in the last equality we use equation (
2.10
) to obtain that
\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})=\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\right)\pi_{v}(x)
.
∎

We may deduce from here a very famous result:

Corollary 2.10
.

For all
v\in V
,

\rho_{v}=\frac{\pi_{v}}{\sum_{u}\pi_{u}}=\frac{1}{\mathbb{E}_{v}(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1})}.

Proof.

First, we use
2.9
and evaluate at
x=1
. We get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})\penalty\ \Big|_{x=1}=-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\pi_{v}(x)\penalty\ \Big|_{x=1}=-\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}

(2.12)

Second, we take the derivative of
\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})=\left(1-\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\right)\pi_{v}(x)
(the second equality in equation (
2.10
)) and evaluate at
x=1
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})\penalty\ \Big|_{x=1}=\left(-\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau_{u}^{\geq 1}x^{\tau_{u}^{\geq 1}-1})\pi_{u}(x)+(1-\mathbb{E}_{u}(x^{\tau_{u}^{\geq 1}}))\pi_{u}^{\prime}(x)\right)\Big|_{x=1}=-\mathbb{E}_{u}(\tau_{u}^{\geq 1})\pi_{u}

(2.13)

which implies the result.
∎

2.4
On the nature of
{\bf K}^{\geq t}_{u}(x)

In general, the sign of
\pi_{v}(x)
, as a polynomial in a real variable
x
, depend of
x
and of
v
. In general, the signs of
\pi_{v}(x)
and of
\pi_{v^{\prime}}(x)
, for
v\neq v^{\prime}
, are not the same for all
x\in\mathbb{R}
(see an example in
Section

3
). However, for
x\in(0,1)
, the
\pi_{v}(x)
are non negative, and then
{\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)
has a probabilistic interpretation:

Proposition 2.11
.

(
i
)

For every
x\in(0,1)

the vector
(\pi_{v}(x),v\in V)
has positive coordinates, so that it is proportional to a probability measure
\mu_{x}
on
V
.

(
ii
)

Hence, for
X_{x}\sim\mu_{x}
independent of the Markov chain
C
, the values of
\left(\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(a^{\tau_{X_{a}}^{\geq t}}\right),u\in V\right)
are all the same.

The second assertion is a consequence of
1.5
.

Proof.

Proof of
(i)
.
Fix
v\in V
, and take
x\in(0,1)
. We will show that
\pi_{v}(x)>0
.
Consider the matrix
\widetilde{{\sf M}}(v)
defined by

\widetilde{{\sf M}}(v,x)=x{\sf M}+(1-x)C(v)

where
C(v)
is the matrix indexed by
V
, with zero coefficients except on the column corresponding to the state
v
, which contains ones.

It is easy to see that
\widetilde{{\sf M}}
is a stochastic irreducible matrix,
and then

\tilde{\pi}(v):=\begin{pmatrix}\det({\sf Id}_{d-1}-\widetilde{{\sf M}}(v,x)^{(u)}),u\in V\end{pmatrix}

is proportional to the invariant measure of
\widetilde{{\sf M}}(v,x)
, and then all its coordinates are positive.
The coefficient
\tilde{\pi}_{v}(v)
corresponding to the vertex
v
, is

\displaystyle\tilde{\pi}_{v}(v)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det({\sf Id}_{d-1}-\widetilde{{\sf M}}{(v,x)}^{(v)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\det({\sf Id}_{d-1}-x{\sf M}^{(v)})=\pi_{v}(x),

therefore
\pi_{v}(x)
is positive.

We have proved that for all
v\in V
the values of
\pi_{v}(x)
are positive and therefore there exists a probability distribution
\mu_{x}
proportional to
(\pi_{v}(x),v\in V)
.

Proof of
(ii)
:
Since

{\bf K}^{\geq t}(x)=\sum_{v}\mathbb{E}_{u}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq t}}\right)\det({\sf Id}_{d-1}-x{\sf M}^{(v)})=\mathbb{E}_{u}(x^{\tau_{X_{x}}^{\geq t}})Z(x),

where
Z(x)=\sum_{u\in V}\det({\sf Id}_{d-1}-x{\sf M}^{(u)})
, the conclusion follows.
∎

2.5
Eigenvalues considerations

We will extract some information from the characteristic polynomial formula

\det(t{\sf Id}-{\sf M})=\prod_{i=1}^{|V|}(t-\lambda_{i})

where
\lambda_{0}=1,\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{d-1}
are the eigenvalues of
{\sf M}
. Hence, for all
x\neq 0
, by taking
t=1/x

\displaystyle\det({\sf Id}_{d}-x{\sf M})=x^{d}\det({\sf Id}_{d}/x-{\sf M})=\prod_{i=0}^{d-1}(1-x\lambda_{i}).

Since
{\sf M}
is irreducible, it admits 1 as eigenvalue with multiplicity one, and then

{\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(1-x\lambda_{i}).

We immediately get

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{d-1}(1-\lambda_{i})

\displaystyle={\bf K}^{\geq 0}(1)=\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}={\bf Z}.

(2.14)

The following proposition is well known, but the standard proof uses the group inverse
[
4
]
. We propose here a proof that avoids this object.

Proposition 2.12
.

The Kemeny constant is

\frac{{\bf Q}^{\geq 1}}{{\bf Z}}=1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{1}{1-\lambda_{i}}

We start with a lemma

Lemma 2.13
.

\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\det({\sf Id}-x{\sf M})\Big|_{x=1}=(1-d){\bf Z}-\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1)

Proof.

From
2.9
one gets that

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\det({\sf Id}-x{\sf M})\Big|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{x^{2}}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\pi_{v}(x)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}-1}\right)\pi_{v}(x)-\frac{1}{x}\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(x^{\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}}\right)\pi_{v}^{(1)}(x)\right)\Big|_{x=1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}-\sum_{v\in V}\mathbb{E}_{v}\left(\tau_{v}^{\geq 1}\right)\pi_{v}-\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-d){\bf Z}-\sum_{v\in V}\pi_{v}^{(1)}(1),

where in the last equality we applied Corollary
2.10
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.12
.

Consider equation (
2.6
), which says that

{\bf Q}^{\geq 1}={\bf K}^{\geq 1,(1)}(1)-\sum_{v\in V}\pi^{(1)}_{v}(1).

We have from Theorem
1.3
and Equation
1.8
that
{\bf K}^{\geq 1}(x)=x{\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)
.

Setting
A(x):=\left(-\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}x}\right)
it is easy to see verify that:

\displaystyle{\bf K}^{\geq 1,(1)}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(1+xA(x)\right){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)

(2.15)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\det({\sf Id}-x{\sf M})

\displaystyle=\frac{d}{dx}\left((1-x){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)\right)=((1-x)A(x)-1){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x)

(2.16)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}}\det({\sf Id}-x{\sf M})

\displaystyle=\left((1-x)\left(\frac{d}{dx}A(x)+A(x)^{2}\right)-2A(x)\right){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(x).

(2.17)

Gathering this, evaluating at
x=1
and using Lemma
2.13
we obtain

\displaystyle{\bf Q}^{\geq 1}

\displaystyle=\left(1-A(1)\right){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(1)+(d-1){\bf Z}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{d-1}\frac{\lambda_{i}}{1-\lambda_{i}}\right){\bf K}^{\geq 0}(1)+(d-1){\bf Z}

(2.19)

(2.20)

where in the last equality we used equation (
2.14

HIV/AIDS epidemiological profile and intervention evaluation of rural elderly in China
Authors: Xiaoyi Zhou, Yuyu Zhang, Ping Zhu, Zixiao Zhou, Jianfei Gao, Yiyao Song, Qiwei Ge, Anni Chen, Xuan Shi, Kai Liu, Shijing Zhang, Meiyin Zou, Congxia Wu, Xun Zhuang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractIntroductionHIV infection among older adults in rural China has risen significantly in recent years, yet epidemiological characteristics and intervention effectiveness remain understudied.MethodsWe described the characteristics of 179 HIV/AIDS patients aged ≥ 50 years in rural Nantong, China, then conducted a community-based 1:1 case-control study of 179 HIV/AIDS patients aged ≥ 50 years and developed a predictive nomogram. Mediation analysis was applied to explore the role of HIV knowledge, and the effectiveness of a comprehensive community intervention was assessed among 1206 community individuals.ResultsThere were 179 elderly HIV/AIDS participants recruited, 55.9% of them were homosexual transmission, and 84.4% were identified through passive pathways. The case-control showed that the protective factors associated with HIV infection included higher Perceived Social Support Score (PSSS) (OR = 0.023) and knowledge awareness (OR = 0.055), while the risk factors included being unemployed (OR = 4.852), having extramarital sex (OR = 30.264), and the tolerant attitude to extramarital sex (OR = 7.124). The C-index is 0.9 and the Dxy is 0.913 with Spiegelhalter Z-test, indicating that the nomogram has good predictive accuracy. Mediation analysis indicated that knowledge mediated the impact of PSSS, extramarital sex, and attitude towards extramarital sex on HIV infection, accounting for 30.8%, 26.6% and 14.1%of the effects, respectively. Six months after implementation of the comprehensive intervention, there was a significant increase in HIV knowledge awareness (from 41.0% to 84.4%) and a decrease in extramarital sex(from 2.4% to 0.8%).ConclusionAppropriate interventions for the rural elderly with low education can also effectively reduce AIDS related risky behaviors. PSSS can be considered as a potential screening factor to predict the risk of HIV infection.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12889-025-25524-9.Keywords: HIV/AIDS, Elderly, Rural, Epidemiology, Intervention, Case-control

Introduction

According to the United Nations Programme on AIDS (UNAIDS), approximately 39.9 million people were living with HIV globally by 2022, of which 7.5 million were elderly [1]. The proportion of HIV-infected people aged over 50 years had significantly increased, from 8% in 2000 to 24% in 2020 [2]. The Asia-Pacific region is home to nearly 6 million people living with HIV [3]. In China, from 2007 to 2018, the rate of newly diagnosed cases in elderly men and women aged 60 and above increased by 10.31 times and 10.81 times, respectively [4]. Currently, there are over 330,000 individuals aged more than 50 years living with HIV/AIDS in China, accounting for 34% of the total number, which exceeds the global average [5–7].

Sexual transmission is the primary mode of AIDS transmission in the elderly [8]. With improving living standards and increased life expectancy, the sexually active period of older individuals has extended accordingly [9]. Notably, older men have a longer sexually active period than older women, with sexual needs persisting from the ages of 60 to 79 [10]. One study suggests that the risk of HIV infection in older men was three times higher than in women [4]. Late detection is a primary characteristic of the elderly due to limited awareness and low rates of voluntarily testing, often only seeking medical attention when complications arise [11]. The average proportion of late diagnoses reaches 46.8% among the elderly population in China [12]. Additionally, chronic diseases and decreased physical function associated with the elderly contribute to a mortality rate of 10.79% within one year of diagnosis among elderly patients with AIDS [13].

Due to generally lower levels of education and socioeconomic status among the elderly in rural areas [14], they often lack knowledge of safe sexual practices, with the rate of condom use for extramarital sex reported at 3.5% [15]. Due to limited access to HIV-related information and testing services, rural elderly individuals often hesitate to seek testing after high-risk behaviors and fail to understand its necessity [16]. Some studies [17, 18] report that compared to urban residents, rural elderly individuals with HIV show greater reluctance to disclose their status or seek medical help, hindered by stigma, discrimination, and family concerns.

HIV infection among older adults in rural China has risen significantly in recent years, Most studies focus on the relationship between the HIV knowledge and behavior [16, 19–21], yet the epidemiological characteristics and effectiveness of interventions remain understudied.

Methods

Study population

HIV/AIDS group

The study was conducted in Rudong County, located north of the Nantong area, China. According to the report from Rudong Center of Disease Control (CDC), the total number of individuals living with HIV/AIDS was 556 from December 2011 to February 2023, with 224 (40.3%) of them aged 50 and older. Among these HIV/AIDS patients aged ≥ 50, 179 elderly individuals volunteered for the survey.

Community participants

In the case-control study, the case group consisted of all patients who were aged ≥ 50 years and were listed in the local CDC’ AIDS management system voluntarily participated in this project. The sample size is the entire available population during the recruitment period.

The sample size of the intervention study was calculated using G*Power (version 3.1; Heinrich Heine University) with an odds ratio (OR) of 1.2 and a significance level of 0.05. The minimum sample needed to achieve a power of 0.95 was 780 for our study [22]. The actual number of participants recruited for the intervention in the study was 1206, which met the requirements for sample assessment. There were about 200 participants are recruited from each village [23]. Finally, 1206 individuals participant the intervention which met the requirements for sample assessment. After 6 months of follow-up, 1061 individuals were retained for analysis.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria for study subjects

Eligible participants were ≥ 50 years of age, local residents, and able to understand and answer survey questions. Individuals who were unwilling to participate, unable to communicate effectively, or had hearing disorders were excluded.

Ethics statement

This research adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki and was granted ethical approval by the Ethics Committee of Nantong City Center for Disease Control and Prevention (Num.2022002). All participants voluntarily agreed to take part in the investigation and signed informed consent. The uneducated participants had their informed consent forms signed by their legal guardians after the form contents were conveyed by staff members. Each participant received a gift voucher valued at approximately 20 CNY.

Data collection

Data were collected through structured questionnaires covering socio-demographics, HIV knowledge (8 standardized questions from China’s AIDS Monitoring Framework), attitudes, sexual behaviors, adn SPSS [24, 25]. HIV-infected individuals were also required to answer questions about the route of infection and diagnosis, as well as their spouse or partner’s HIV test results.

All questionnaires were gathered through face-to-face interviews conducted by trained investigators. These investigators included staff from Nantong CDC, Rudong CDC, primary health workers in the survey areas, and postgraduate students from the School of Public Health at Nantong University. All investigators underwent standardized training provided by Nantong CDC.

HIV testing

For HIV antibody detection, 5 mL venous blood samples were collected from each community participant. Initial screening was performed using the enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) from Zhuhai Livzon Pharmaceutical Group Co., Ltd. Western blot (WB) was used to confirm the results.

Comprehensive interventions

The comprehensive interventions were conducted among 1202 community participants at baseline. The intervention measures included: (1) HIV knowledge lectures, given bimonthly by staff from local primary healthcare institutions. The PowerPoint presentations on HIV knowledge were uniformly produced by Nantong CDC, with each lecture preferably scheduled to coincide with China’s official public holidays. (2) Distribution of a brochure to each participant, enriched with engaging comic illustrations and succinct text, providing information on HIV prevention. (3) Display of educational posters at village health clinics and notice boards, with updates scheduled bimonthly. (4) Creation of a 9-minute microfilm by Nantong CDC, narrating common infection pathways among the elderly through vivid storytelling. The film was enacted by professional actors and dubbed in the local dialect. This film was screened during each lecture and disseminated via WeChat platforms.

Statistical analysis

In the 1:1 paired case-control study, univariate conditional logistic regression analysis was conducted to evaluate the association between potential factors and the outcome variable. Subsequently, multivariate conditional logistic regression with a backward stepwise method was employed to select significant variables from the univariate analysis. The odds ratio (OR) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were used to assess the strength of the association of HIV infection factors.

The nomogram was used to visualize the risk of HIV infection. Each predictive factor was assigned a score based on its upward projection to the top layer of the nomogram. The cumulative score, derived from the sum of these individual projections, enables a precise prediction of an individual’s susceptibility to HIV infection. Additionally, calibration curves and clinical decision curve analysis (DCA) were constructed to evaluate the predictive accuracy and clinical utility of the model.

The mediation analysis in this study was based on the Karlson-Holm-Breen (KHB) approach as described by Kohler and colleagues [26]. In this analysis, awareness of HIV knowledge serves as the mediating variable, with the binary outcome of HIV infection status as the dependent variable. The independent variables include extramarital sexual behavior, attitude towards extramarital sexual behavior, and PSSS.

The McNemar’s test was used to measure differences in the categorical variables pre-and post-intervention. P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

The analyses were performed with Stata version 17.0 and R4.2.2.

Results

Sociodemographic characteristics of 179 HIV/AIDS participants

The study included 179 HIV/AIDS participants (mean age: 63.6 ± 7.0 years; 2.9:1 male-to-female ratio), predominantly comprising individuals aged 60–69 (51.9%) with a junior high school education or below (86.6%). Most were married (77.1%), and 54.7% lived with their spouses. Notably, only 14.5% reported high perceived social support, and overall HIV knowledge awareness was 50.8%. Regarding risk behaviors in the past six months, 40.2%, 18.4%, and 2.8% reported extramarital, homosexual, and commercial sexual contact, respectively. Among the 133 male participants, 75.2% were infected via homosexual contact. Most diagnoses (74.9%) occurred in hospitals, and 49.2% of the patients’ spouses were also HIV-positive (Table 1).

HIV infection risk analysis

A community-based 1:1 paired case-control study was conducted to analyze the factors associated with HIV infection. The univariate analysis indicated that living status, PSSS scores, knowledge awareness, extramarital sexual behavior, and attitude toward extramarital sexual behavior were associated with HIV infection. Subsequently, multivariate conditional logistic regression analysis showed that knowledge awareness and higher PSSS were regarded as protective factors (P < 0.05). The unemployed, having extramarital sex, and the tolerant attitude toward extramarital sex were risk factors for HIV infection (P < 0.05) (Table 2).

Analysis of risk factors of HIV infection based on a case-control study

A nomogram was constructed to represent the contribution of each predictive factor to the overall risk of HIV infection (Fig. 1). This tool allows for the aggregation of scores from individual factors, providing a composite measure to predict an individual’s risk of HIV infection. Additionally, the model passed the calibration test with P = 0.926 > 0.05, and the Brier score was 0.079, indicating good discriminative ability. These results indicate a good fit of the model (Fig. 2). The DCA curve does not intersect with the two extreme lines between thresholds of 0.1 and 0.8, signifying that the model has a high degree of clinical applicability (Fig. 3).

Mediation effect analysis of HIV knowledge

Mediation effect analysis found that in the total effect of PSSS on HIV infection, knowledge awareness, acting as a mediating variable, accounted for 30.8% of the impact. Similarly, an indirect effect mediated by knowledge explained 26.6% of the total effect of extramarital sexual behavior, and an indirect effect mediated by knowledge explained 14.1% of the total effect of attitude towards extramarital sexual behavior (Table 3; Fig. 4).

Evaluation of intervention effectiveness

A total of 1,202 participants aged ≥ 50 years were recruited from rural communities. 6 of them tested HIV positive at baseline. We employed a pre-post self-control method to assess the effectiveness of a multi-intervention after 6 months. 141 individuals (11.7%) were lost to follow-up, resulting in 1,061 individuals available for paired analysis. There were no significant differences observed in age (61.1 ± 6.6 vs. 62.4 ± 7.7, t = 1.781, P = 0.075) and sex composition (χ²=3.098, P = 0.078) between the lost and the analysis population.

The post-intervention survey revealed a significant increase in knowledge awareness (84.4%) compared to the pre-intervention level (41.0%) (P < 0.001). Additionally, negative attitudes toward extramarital sexual behavior had increased from 71.5% to 82% (P < 0.001). Notably, reports of extramarital sexual behavior in the 6-month period had decreased significantly (2.4% vs. 0.8%, P = 0.002) (Table 4).

Discussion

Our research found that the number of male infected with HIV was 2.89 times that of females among rural elderly individuals. This ratio closely aligns with a study conducted by the Chinese CDC from 2010 to 2022, which reported a male-to-female ratio of 2.75 to 1 in the same age group [27]. Notably, 37.0% of the infected females were infected through their spouses. These findings suggest that elderly males constitute a crucial demographic for local AIDS prevention and control efforts. Compared to elderly women, elderly men tend to exhibit a relatively higher desire for sexual activity [9]. Additionally, in rural areas, commercial sexual services were found to be more accessible and affordable for elderly men [16]. Furthermore, there exists a general lack of awareness regarding HIV/AIDS and a deficiency in protective measures among the elderly population [28, 29]. Even if they themselves suspect infection, the fear of discrimination and its potential impact on their families often deters them from proactively seeking testing and treatment at medical facilities [18].

In our study, 55.9% of HIV/AIDS cases were attributed to homosexual transmission, a notable difference from other reports indicating that older people with HIV were mostly affected by heterosexual transmission [27, 30]. The elevated proportion of homosexual transmission among local older residents may be associated with several factors. First,61.0% of the study population at the research site were over 50 years old. Second, older adults, particularly those in rural areas, often have limited knowledge of HIV/AIDS and may not perceive themselves to be at risk despite engaging in high-risk sexual behaviors [31]. Third, widespread social isolation among older adults, partly due to rural youth out-migration, together with pronounced stigma, may foster concealed high-risk behaviors [32].

Among the 179 HIV-infected individuals, 84.4% were identified through passive pathways, such as testing conducted for other diseases, preoperative testing, and visits to infectious diseases outpatient departments, among others. Passive detection often leads to “late detection”, resulting in delayed treatment and an increased mortality rate. A study from the Shenzhen area in China suggests that late detection contributes to a mortality rate of 11.0% [13]. This highlights the need for integrating HIV testing into routine geriatric care in rural areas, particularly for men presenting with comorbidities. In response, local health departments have implemented strategies to enhance early HIV detection among the elderly, including targeted AIDS screening campaigns and the integration of HIV testing into routine health check-ups. These efforts aim to identify infections earlier and facilitate timely treatment initiation.

The assessment results from the PSSS revealed that 85.5% of individuals living with HIV/AIDS perceive their social support as moderate or low. A high PSSS score was identified as a protective factor for HIV infection, consistent with Henderson’s findings in MSM populations, which emphasized that greater social support comprehension correlates with a reduced risk of HIV infection [33]. A deeper understanding of social support suggests that individuals with a stronger ability to access social resources when needed are more likely to maintain better health habits and a lower risk of HIV infection [34, 35]. A study conducted in Kunming, China, yielded similar results [36]. Conversely, it has been suggested that lower PSSS scores are associated with a higher likelihood of engaging in high-risk sexual behaviors. Based on the above results, we propose that PSSS can be considered as a potential screening factor to predict the risk of HIV infection.

Lack of awareness regarding the dangers of AIDS emerged as a significant factor among the elderly in our study. Despite HIV patients being aware of their disease status and receiving follow-up visits from healthcare providers, the level of knowledge awareness remained at 50.8%. The reported rate of engaging in extramarital sex within 6 months was notably high at 59.8%. These findings suggest that infected individuals may be spreading the disease through more discreet means, posing considerable challenges to disease control. Knowledge awareness may serve as a mediating factor between extramarital sexual behavior and HIV infection. Kalula [21] strongly advocates for the implementation of effective educational methodologies to significantly enhance knowledge levels, thereby increasing awareness of risk factors, preventive measures, and management strategies for various health issues.

The comprehensive interventions make a notable 43.4% improvement in HIV knowledge awareness among the elderly. This intervention not only reduced the tolerance for extramarital sexual behavior but also significantly decreased the incidence of such practices, effectively demonstrating its impact. Our study indicated that a comprehensive, localized intervention—utilizing community organizations, local dialects, and contextualized micro-videos—effectively improves HIV/AIDS-related knowledge, attitudes, and practices among older adults. The result supports existing evidence [37] that trusted localized services are central to intervention effectiveness, demonstrating the strong applicability and scalability of this model in older populations.

The study has several notable advantages. Firstly, it employed comprehensive measures for the intervention, particularly utilizing micro-films dubbed in local dialects, featuring vivid stories that were easily understandable and well-received by the elderly. Secondly, the use of a self-before-and-after control design in evaluating the intervention’s effect minimized the bias. Lastly, our research presents that PSSS can be considered as a potential screening factor to predict the risk of HIV infection.

However, this study has several limitations. The case-control design is susceptible to recall bias and potentially affects exposure accuracy, particularly among long-term HIV patients. Underreporting of sensitive high-risk behaviors may also lead to underestimation of prevalence. Furthermore, findings from a single county in China may lack generalizability, and the 6-month follow-up period may be too short to assess long-term behavioral trends.

Conclusions

The rising HIV epidemic among older adults in China, particularly in rural areas with low awareness, presents a critical public health challenge. We introduced the PSSS as a screening tool to improve HIV testing efficiency in resource-limited settings, with preliminary validation supporting its utility. Integrating HIV testing into routine elderly health care and expanding culturally adapted community interventions may help curb the growing epidemic. Future studies should include longer follow-up periods and encompass diverse rural settings to support the development of nationwide strategies.

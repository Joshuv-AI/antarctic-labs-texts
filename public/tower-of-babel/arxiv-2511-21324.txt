Distributions of bounded real sequences and Astorg-Thaler’s question
Zhangchi Chen, Zihao Ye

Abstract
. While studying the existence of wandering domains in complex dynamics, Astorg-Thaler posed a question concerning the distribution of bounded real sequences: for α > 1 \alpha>1 that has the Pisot property, is β ​ ln ⁡ α α − 1 ∈ ℚ \frac{\beta\ln\alpha}{\alpha-1}\in\mathbb{Q} necessary for the existence of an increasing sequence of integers ( n k ) k ⩾ 1 (n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1} such that ( n k + 1 − α ​ n k − β ​ ln ⁡ n k ) k ⩾ 1 (n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1} converges to a cycle? When α \alpha is an algebraic number, we answer Astorg-Thaler’s question in the affirmative.



Keywords
Pisot number, Linear recurrence, Birkhoff average measure, Weyl’s equidistribution theorem.

Mathematics Subject Classification
37F10, 37F40, 11K16, 37F80, 11J71.

1.
Introduction

The existence of wandering domains is a fascinating problem in complex dynamics. No rational maps in
\mathbb{CP}^{1}
have wandering domains
[
17
]
. However, certain algebraic maps in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
have wandering domains
[
2
]
. In
[
5
]
, Astorg-Thaler constructed skew-products
P(z,w)=(p(z),q(z,w))
in
\mathbb{C}^{2}
tangent to the identity that possess wandering domains.

We introduce some basic definitions from their paper.

Definition 1
.

A holomorphic self-map
P
of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
is called a
skew-product
if it has the form

P(z,w)=(p(z),q(z,w))

where
p,q
are holomorphic functions. The skew-product
P(z,w)
fixing the origin is
tangent to the identity
if

P(z,w)=\Big(z+\sum\limits_{i\geqslant 2}a_{i}z^{i},w+\sum\limits_{i+j\geqslant 2}b_{i,j}z^{i}w^{j}\Big).

In both
[
5
]
and this paper, only the non-degenerate case in the sense of
a_{2},b_{2,0},b_{0,2}\neq 0
is considered. Up to conjugacy by a biholomorphism of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
, we may assume that
P(z,w)
has the form
[
5
, (1.1)]

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}p(z):=z-z^{2}+az^{3}+O(|z|^{4}),\\
q(z,w):=w+w^{2}+bz^{2}+b_{0,3}w^{3}+b_{3,0}z^{3}+O(\|(z,w)\|^{4}).\end{array}\right.

where
a,b,b_{0,3},b_{3,0}\in\mathbb{C}
.

Astorg-Thaler’s results are mainly based on the assumption that
b>\frac{1}{4}
. In this setting, they introduce the following parameters:

(2)

c:=\frac{\sqrt{4b-1}}{2},\quad\alpha_{0}:=e^{\pi/c},\quad\beta_{0}:=(b_{0,3}-a)(\alpha_{0}-1).

Astrog-Thaler investigated the conditions on
\alpha_{0}>1,\beta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
for
P
to admit wandering domains.

Theorem 1.1
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
Let
P
be a map of the form (
1
). If there exists
an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha_{0}n_{k}-\beta_{0}\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges, then
P
has a wandering domain.

It is natural to ask for which
(\alpha,\beta)
there exists an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges?

The condition on
\alpha
is well understood. Astorg-Thaler proved in
[
5
, Theorem 1.10(2)]
that such a sequence exists if and only if
\alpha
has the Pisot property, a notion arising from the study of the distribution of powers of
\alpha
modulo
1
.

Definition 2
.

A real number
\alpha>1
has the
Pisot property
if there exists some
\xi>0
such that
\lim\limits_{n\to+\infty}\|\xi\alpha^{n}\|=0
, where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the distance to the nearest integer.

Theorem 1.2
.

[
14
]
If
\alpha>1
is an algebraic number with the Pisot property, then it is an algebraic integer and is a Pisot number, in the sense that all its Galois conjugates have modulus strictly less than
1
.

There is a long-standing question known as the
Pisot–Vijayaraghavan problem
, asking if any
\alpha>1
with the Pisot property is algebraic.

The condition for
\beta
is more complicated. For a sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
, and for
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, define a sequence
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
by

(3)

m_{k}:=n_{k}+\lfloor k\theta\rfloor.

Proposition 1.3
.

The real number
\frac{\beta\ln\alpha}{\alpha-1}
is the unique value of
\theta
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded if and only if
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
(defined by (
3
)) is bounded.

Astrog-Thaler introduced the following weaker version of convergence.

Definition 3
.

[
5
, Definition 1.9]

For any positive integer
\ell
,
a sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is said to

converge to a cycle of period
\ell

if the subsequence
(x_{k\ell+j})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges for each
0\leqslant j<\ell
.

Now we can fully state Astorg-Thaler’s conditions on
(\alpha,\beta)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

[
5
, Theorem 1.10(2)]

If there exists an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges to a cycle, then
\alpha
has the Pisot property.

Conversely, if
\alpha
has the Pisot property and
\theta=\frac{\beta\ln\alpha}{\alpha-1}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then there exists an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges to a cycle.

Astorg and Thaler want to know whether
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}
is necessary in the second statement above.

For a sequence of integers
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
, defined a sequence
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
by

(4)

\eta_{k}:=m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k}+\{(k+1)\theta\}-\alpha\{k\theta\}.

Proposition 1.5
.

[
5
, Remark 10.9]

The sequence of
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges to a cycle if and only if for
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
and
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
(defined by (
3
) and (
4
)),
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded and
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges to a cycle.

Thus Astorg-Thaler’s question can be stated as follows.

Question 1.6
.

[
5
, Question 10.10]

Let
\alpha>1
be a real number with the Pisot property.
Is
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}
necessary for the existence of an increasing sequence of integers
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded and
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
(defined by (
4
)) converges to a cycle?

Assuming that
\alpha
is algebraic, we answer Astorg-Thaler’s question affirmatively.

Theorem 1.7

(Main Theorem)
.

Let
\alpha>1
be an algebraic number. Let
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded. Then the sequence
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
(defined by (
4
)) converges to a cycle if and only if
\theta
is rational.

By combining
[
5
, Theorem 1.10(2)]
, we draw the following conclusion.

Corollary 1.8
.

Assuming that the Pisot–-Vijayaraghavan problem has an affirmative answer, i.e. any
\alpha>1
with the Pisot property is algebraic, then there exists an increasing sequence of integers
(n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges to a cycle if and only if
\alpha
is a Pisot number and
\frac{\beta\ln\alpha}{\alpha-1}
is rational.

In Astorg-Thaler’s proof of
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
, the sequence
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
needs to converge. It is not yet clear whether
P
still has wandering domains when the sequence
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
converges only to a cycle.

2.
Proof of the main theorem

Our key idea is to compare the distributions of bounded sequences by computing Birkhoff average measures.

Definition 4
.

For a sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
, we define its
limit set

E\subset\mathbb{R}
as the set of all the limits of convergent subsequences. The sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is said to
have finitely many limit values
, if
E
is finite.

In particular, if a sequence converges to a cycle, then it has finitely many limit values. Moreover, if a sequence has finitely many limit values, it is necessarily bounded.

For any bounded sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
, there exists some constant
M>0
such that
x_{k}\in[-M,M]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the Birkhoff average measure

\mu_{x_{\bullet},n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{x_{k}},

which is a probability measure on
[-M,M]
. By the Banach-Alaoglu Theorem, there exists a subsequence
(\mu_{x_{\bullet},n_{j}})_{j\geqslant 1}
of
(\mu_{x_{\bullet},n})_{n\geqslant 1}
that converges to a probability measure on
[-M,M]
. The following Lemma decribes such a limit measure.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
E
be the limit set of
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
.
Let
\mu
be the limit of some convergent subsequence
(\mu_{x_{\bullet},n_{j}})_{j\geqslant 1}
. Then
{\sf Supp}(\mu)\subset E
.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that for any test function
\varphi
such that
{\sf Supp}(\varphi)\cap E=\emptyset
, there holds that
\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle=0
.

Since
{\sf Supp}(\varphi)
and
E
are compact, there exists an open neighborhood
U
of
E
such that
U\cap{\sf Supp}(\varphi)=\emptyset
. By the definition of limit set, there exists some sufficiently large
N\geqslant 1
such that
x_{k}\in U
for all
k\geqslant N
, which implies that
\varphi(x_{k})=0
for all
k\geqslant N
. Thus

\displaystyle\left|\langle\varphi,\mu\rangle\right|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\left|\langle\varphi,\mu_{x_{\bullet},n_{j}}\rangle\right|=\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\left|\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n_{j}}\langle\varphi,\delta_{x_{k}}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{n_{j}}\left|\varphi(x_{k})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{n_{j}}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left|\varphi(x_{k})\right|=0.\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\makebox[345.0pt][r]{\qed}}

Now we assume that
\alpha>1
is an algebraic number. Let

(5)

p(x)=a_{d}x^{d}+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+...+a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]

be a minimal polynomial of
\alpha
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
be an increasing sequence of integers such that
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define
e_{k}:=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}m_{k+s}\in\mathbb{Z}
Then
(e_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded. Consequently,
(e_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
takes only finitely many values.

Proof.

The sequence
(f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
defined by
f_{k}:=m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k}
is bounded. For any
s,t\in\mathbb{N}
there holds that

(6)

m_{t+s}=\alpha^{s}m_{t}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\alpha^{j}f_{t+s-j-1}

since

m_{t+s}-\alpha^{s}m_{t}=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{s-1}\alpha^{j}(m_{t+s-j}-\alpha m_{t+s-j-1})=\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\alpha^{j}f_{t+s-j-1}.

Applying (
6
), we can compute that

\displaystyle e_{k}=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}m_{k+s}

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{s=1}^{d}a_{s}(\alpha^{s}m_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{s-1}\alpha^{j}f_{k+s-j-1})+a_{0}m_{k}

\displaystyle=\underbrace{\Big(\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}\alpha^{s}\Big)}_{=P(\alpha)=0}m_{k}+\sum_{s=1}^{d}\sum_{t=0}^{s-1}a_{s}\alpha^{t}f_{k+s-t-1}.

We conclude that
(e_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded since
(f_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded. ∎

Define
\eta_{k}:=m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k}+\{(k+1)\theta\}-\alpha\{k\theta\}
as in (
4
). If the sequence
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}

converges to a cycle, then the sequence
(\tilde{\eta}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
defined by

\tilde{\eta}_{k}:=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}\eta_{k+s}

also converges to a cycle. We can rewrite
\tilde{\eta}_{k}
as

\tilde{\eta}_{k}=e_{k+1}-\alpha e_{k}+c_{k}

where

c_{k}:=a_{d}\{(k+d+1)\theta\}+\Big(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s-1}-\alpha a_{s})\{(k+s)\theta\}\Big)-\alpha a_{0}\{k\theta\}.

When
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
, the distribution of the sequence
(c_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
can be characterized by using the equidistribution property of irrational rotations. Define a piecewise linear function

\displaystyle g:[0,1)

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle\longmapsto a_{d}\{x+(d+1)\theta\}+\Big(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{d}(a_{s-1}-\alpha a_{s})\{x+s\theta\}\Big)-\alpha a_{0}x.

The set of discontinuity points of
g
is a subset of
\{\{-\theta\},\{-2\theta\},\dots,\{-(d+1)\theta\}\}
, where the elements are irrational and mutually distinct. These
d+1
points partition the interval
(0,1)
into
d+2
open intervals
J_{0},J_{1},\dots,J_{d+1}
. Denote
I_{j}:=g(J_{j})
for
j=0,1,\dots,d+1
.

Lemma 2.3
.

For
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
, there holds that

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}}=\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)|p(1)|}\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}\chi_{I_{j}}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Leb},

where
\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Leb}
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}
, and
\chi_{I_{j}}
is the characteristic function of
I_{j}
. In particular, the support of this measure contains an open interval.

Proof.

For any test function
f
and for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\langle f,\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}}\rangle=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f(c_{k})=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}f\big(g(\{k\theta\})\big)=\langle f\circ g,\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\{k\theta\}}\rangle.

Since
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
, by Weyl’s equidistribution theorem (c.f.
[
8
, Theorem 1.3]

\lim\limits_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\{k\theta\}}=\chi_{[0,1)}\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Leb}.

Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f,\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}}\rangle=\int_{0}^{1}f\big(g(x)\big)dx=\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}\int_{J_{j}}f\big(g(x)\big)dx.

For each
j=0,1,\dots,d+1
,
g
is a linear function on
I_{j}
with derivative

\displaystyle g^{\prime}

\displaystyle=a_{d}+\Big(\sum\limits_{s=1}^{d+1}(a_{s-1}-\alpha a_{s})\Big)-\alpha a_{0}

\displaystyle=(a_{d}+a_{0}+\dots+a_{d-1})-\alpha(a_{0}+a_{1}+\dots+a_{d})=(1-\alpha)p(1)

which is a constant independent of
j
. Since
p
is a minimal polynomial of
\alpha>1
,
p(1)\neq 0
. We conclude that
g^{\prime}(x)\neq 0
for any
x\in I_{j}
. Thus

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle f,\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}\int_{J_{j}}f\big(g(x)\big)dx=\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}\int_{I_{j}}\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)|p(1)|}f(y)dy

\displaystyle=\Big\langle f,\frac{1}{(\alpha-1)|p(1)|}(\sum_{j=0}^{d+1}\chi_{I_{j}})\mathfrak{m}_{\sf Leb}\Big\rangle.\qed

Proof of the Main Theorem.

Suppose that there is some
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
and some increasing sequence of integers
(m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
such that
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is bounded and
(\eta_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
(defined by (
4
)) converges to a cycle. Let
a_{0},a_{1},\dots,a_{d}\in\mathbb{Z}
be the coefficients of a minimal polynomial of
\alpha
as in (
5
). Then the sequence
(\tilde{\eta}_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
defined by

\tilde{\eta}_{k}:=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}\eta_{k+s}

also converges to a cycle, hence has finitely many limit values.

On one hand, the sequence
(c_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
defined by

c_{k}:=\tilde{\eta}_{k}-(e_{k+1}-\alpha e_{k})

has finitely many limit values, since
(e_{k+1}-\alpha e_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
only takes finitely many values by Lemma
2.2
. By Lemma
2.1
, any convergent subsequence of
(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}})_{n\geqslant 1}
converges to a probability measure supported on a finite set.

On the other hand, by Lemma
2.3
, the sequence
(\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{c_{k}})_{n\geqslant 1}
itself converges to a probability measure whose support contains an interval. A contradiction.
∎

Proof of Proposition
1.3
.

When
m_{k}=n_{k}+\lfloor{k\theta\rfloor}
, from
[
5
, Remark 10.9]
we know

\displaystyle n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k}=

\displaystyle n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-k\beta\ln\alpha-\beta\ln\zeta+o(1).

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-(\beta\ln\alpha-(\alpha-1)\theta)k

\displaystyle+m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k}

\displaystyle+\underbrace{\{(k+1)\theta\}-\alpha\{k\theta\}-\theta-\beta\ln\zeta+o(1)}_{=O(1)}.

We conclude that
\frac{\beta\ln\alpha}{\alpha-1}
is the unique value of
\theta
that establishes the equivalence between the boundedness of
(m_{k+1}-\alpha m_{k})_{k\geq 1}
and the boundedness of
(n_{k+1}-\alpha n_{k}-\beta\ln n_{k})_{k\geq 1}
. ∎

3.
Distribution of sequences modulo 1

The distribution of sequences modulo 1 not only plays a key role in our solution to Astorg-Thaler’s question, but also has its own interest. The following question put forward by Mendès France appears in
[
8
, p215, Problem 10.4]
and
[
11
, p7-05, Question 6*]
.

Definition 5
.

The
spectrum
of a sequence
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
is the set of real numbers
\theta\in(0,1)
such that the sequence
(\{x_{k}-k\theta\})_{k\geqslant 1}
is not uniformly distributed on
[0,1)
. Denote the spectrum by
{\sf Spec}(x_{\bullet})
.

Question 3.1
.

Let
\xi
be a non-zero real number and let
\alpha>1
be a real number. Is the spectrum of the sequence
(\xi\alpha^{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
at most countable?

Based on our method of linear recurrence, we can describe two subsets of the spectrum.

Definition 6
.

For a sequence of real numbers
(x_{k})_{k\geqslant 1}
, denote by

\displaystyle{\sf Spec_{fini}}(x_{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\big\{\theta\in(0,1)~|~(\{x_{k}-k\theta\})_{k\geqslant 1}~\text{has finitely many limit values}\big\},

\displaystyle{\sf Spec_{count}}(x_{\bullet})

\displaystyle:=\big\{\theta\in(0,1)~|~(\{x_{k}-k\theta\})_{k\geqslant 1}~\text{has countably many limit values}\big\}.

Obviously
{\sf Spec_{fini}}(x_{\bullet})\subset{\sf Spec_{count}}(x_{\bullet})\subset{\sf Spec}(x_{\bullet})
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\alpha>1
is an algebraic number, then for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}
, the sequence
(\{\xi\alpha^{k}-k\theta\})_{k\geqslant 1}
has countably many limit values only if
\theta\in\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)
. That is to say,
{\sf Spec_{count}}(\xi\alpha^{\bullet})\subset\mathbb{Q}\cap(0,1)
.

Proof.

Suppose there is some irrational
\theta
such that
(\{\xi\alpha^{k}-k\theta\})_{k\geqslant 1}
has countably many limit values. Denote by
\gamma_{k}:=\xi\alpha^{k}-k\theta
for any
k\geqslant 1
. Let
p(x)=a_{d}x^{d}+a_{d-1}x^{d-1}+\dots+a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
be a minimal polynomial of
\alpha
. Define
\tilde{\gamma}_{k}:=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}(\xi\alpha^{k+s}-(k+s)\theta)
for any
k\geqslant 1
. Then

\tilde{\gamma}_{k}=\xi\alpha^{k}\underbrace{\Big(\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}\alpha^{s}\Big)}_{=0}-\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}(k+s)\theta)=-\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}a_{s}(k+s)\theta)

On one hand, if the limit set of
(\{\gamma_{k}\})_{k\geqslant 1}
is at most countable then for each
s\in\{0,1,\dots,d\}
, the limit set of
(\{a_{s}\gamma_{k+s}\})_{k\geqslant 1}
is a bounded countable closed set, hence compact. Since
\{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\}=\sum\limits_{s=0}^{d}\{a_{s}\gamma_{k+s}\}
modulo 1 for any
k\geqslant 1
, the limit set of
(\{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}\})_{k\geqslant 1}
is also a countable compact set. Using the same argument as Lemma
2.1
one can show that the support of the limit of any convergent subsequence of Birkhoff average measure
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}}
is a subset of this countable compact set.

On the other hand, since
\theta\notin\mathbb{Q}
, as shown in Lemma
2.3
the support of the limit of Birkhoff average measure
\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum\limits_{k=1}^{n}\delta_{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}}
contains an interval. A contradiction.
∎

Affine generalizations of the nonholonomic problem of a convex body rolling without slipping on the plane
M. Costa Villegas *, L. C. García-Naranjo

Abstract
We introduce a class of examples which provide an affine generalization of the nonholonomic problem of a convex body rolling without slipping on the plane. We investigate dynamical aspects of the system such as existence of first integrals, smooth invariant measure and integrability, giving special attention to the cases in which the convex body is a dynamically balanced sphere or a body of revolution.



Keywords:
nonholonomic systems, rigid body dynamics, first integrals, invariant measure, integrability, chaotic behaviour.

2.3
Moving energy

It is well-known that nonholonomic systems with affine constraints do not in general preserve the energy.
However, as first noticed in
[
24
]
(see also
[
11
]
and
[
22

if the affine terms correspond to the infinitesimal generator of a continuous symmetry of the Lagrangian,
then a modification of the energy, which we term

in accordance with
[
24
,
22
]
, arises as a first integral.
Below we discuss some instances of existence of a preserved moving energy in our problem.

2.3.1
The case
W=0

As mentioned above, for a general convex body, the Lagrangian
L
is invariant under the lifted
\rm{SE}(2)
action on
TQ

given by (
2.21
). If
W=0
and
V\in\mathfrak{X}(\Pi)
coincides with the infinitesimal generator of the
\rm{SE}(2)
action
on

, given by (
2.21
), then the system possesses a conserved moving energy. There are two possibilities for such infinitesimal generator.
The first one is a steady rotation with angular frequency
\eta\in\mathbb{R}
about a fixed point on the plane
\Pi
that can be taken as our origin

\bm{V}_{s}(\bm{x})=\eta\bm{e}_{3}\times\bm{x},

which is precisely the form of
\bm{V}_{s}

In this case, the conserved moving energy

E_{mov}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbb{I}\bm{\Omega},\bm{\Omega}\rangle+\frac{m}{2}\|\bm{\rho}\times\bm{\Omega}\|^{2}-mg\langle\bm{\rho},\bm{\gamma}\rangle+\eta\langle\mathbb{I}\bm{\Omega}-m\bm{\rho}\times(\bm{\Omega}\times\bm{\rho}),\bm{\gamma}\rangle+\frac{1}{2}m\eta^{2}(\|\bm{\rho}\|^{2}-\|\bm{u}\|^{2}).

The second possibility is that of a steady linear translation; namely

24

22
]

one computes the conserved moving energy to be

E_{mov}=\frac{1}{2}\langle\mathbb{I}\bm{\Omega},\bm{\Omega}\rangle+\frac{m}{2}\|\bm{\rho}\times\bm{\Omega}\|^{2}-mg\langle\bm{\rho},\bm{\gamma}\rangle.

3.1.1
The case

\gamma

Since both
\bm{M}

\bm{\gamma}
are body representations of vectors that are fixed in space,
if they are initially parallel they will remain parallel for all time.
As we prove below, the dynamics restricted to these initial conditions is integrable and in fact periodic.

It is not hard to see that those
(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}
for which
\bm{M}
and
\bm{\gamma}
are parallel are critical points of the first integrals
(

and
may be parametrized by
\bm{\gamma}
by putting

\bm{M}=\pm\|\bm{M}\|\bm{\gamma}.

(3.8)

Writing
\lambda=\pm\|\bm{M}\|

\bm{\Omega}
as a function of
\bm{\gamma}
depending parametrically on

\bm{\Omega}(\bm{\gamma};\lambda)=A\left(\lambda\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\zeta}(\bm{\gamma})+\frac{mr^{2}\langle\lambda\bm{\gamma}+\bm{\zeta}(\bm{\gamma}),A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}{1-mr^{2}\langle A\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}\rangle}\bm{\gamma}\right),

with
\bm{\zeta}(\bm{\gamma})=mr^{2}\sigma\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3}).

The restriction of (
3.4
) to the 2-dimensional invariant submanifold
determined by the condition
\bm{M}=\lambda\bm{\gamma}
is described by the equation

\dot{\bm{\gamma}}=\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega}(\bm{\gamma};\lambda).

(3.9)

Below we exhibit a smooth first integral and an invariant measure depending on the value of
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.
It follows that all non-equilibrium solutions
\bm{\gamma}(t)
of (

) are periodic. Therefore,
in view of (

(
3.4

Let
\varepsilon

\varepsilon:=\frac{\|\bm{M}\|}{mr^{2}|\sigma|}.

(3.10)

If

\varepsilon>\frac{I_{3}}{I_{3}+mr^{2}},

(3.11)

then the quantity
\lambda(I_{3}+mr^{2})+mr^{2}\sigma I_{3}\gamma_{3}
is nonzero for all
\gamma_{3}\in[-1,1]
, and

f(\bm{\gamma})=\frac{|\lambda(I_{3}+mr^{2})+mr^{2}\sigma I_{3}\gamma_{3}|^{-\frac{mr^{2}}{I_{3}}}}{\sqrt{1-mr^{2}\langle\bm{\gamma},A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}},

(3.12)

with

given by (
3.7

\bm{\gamma}\in S^{2}
which can be checked to be a first integral of
(
3.9
). Furthermore, also under the assumption (

\gamma_{3}=-\frac{\lambda}{mr^{2}\sigma}\left(\frac{I_{3}+mr^{2}}{I_{3}}\right)\in[-1,1].

. A smooth invariant measure in this case is given by
\nu(\bm{\gamma})d\bm{\gamma}
where

\nu(\bm{\gamma})=\begin{cases}g(\bm{\gamma})\mu(\bm{\gamma})&\text{if }\gamma_{3}\neq-\frac{\lambda}{mr^{2}\sigma}\left(\frac{I_{3}+mr^{2}}{I_{3}}\right),\\

We notice that the density

3.13

[

Poincaré map

to construct our Poincaré section.
We begin by restricting the system to the four-dimensional level manifold
\mathcal{M}_{4}

of the first integrals
\langle\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}\rangle
and
\|\bm{\gamma}\|^{2}
,

\mathcal{M}_{4}=\{(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\,\langle\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}\rangle=0\,\text{and}\,\|\bm{\gamma}\|^{2}=1\,\}.

In this way, we obtain a four-dimensional system with first integral
\|\bm{M}\|^{2}=G^{2}
. To parametrize
\mathcal{M}_{4}
, we use the Andoyer-Deprit variables
(L,G,l,g)
defined by

\begin{split}&M_{1}=\sqrt{G^{2}-L^{2}}\sin l,\qquad\qquad M_{2}=\sqrt{G^{2}-L^{2}}\cos l,\;\;\qquad\qquad M_{3}=L\\
&\gamma_{1}=\frac{L}{G}\cos g\sin l+\sin g\cos l,\quad\gamma_{2}=\frac{L}{G}\cos g\cos l-\sin g\sin l,\quad\gamma_{3}=-\sqrt{1-\frac{L^{2}}{G^{2}}},\end{split}

where
l,g\in[0,2\pi)
and
L,G
satisfy the inequality
-1\leq\frac{L}{G}\leq 1
. The system determines a three-dimensional flow on the fixed level set of the first integral
\|\bm{M}\|^{2}=G^{2}
. We take the set
g=0
as a section of this flow to obtain a two-dimensional Poincaré map, which we parametrize by the variables
\left(l,\frac{L}{G}\right)
.

The Poincaré map, shown in Fig

(defined by (

islands typical of KAM theory. These numerical experiments suggest that the system is non-integrable at the level
\langle\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}\rangle=0
.
We note that the experiments seem compatible with the existence of a smooth invariant measure, but we
were unable to find it.

Limit cases of the dynamics

The numerical experiments in Fig
6
suggest that the dynamics is approximately integrable when the non-dimensional parameter
\varepsilon
is taken sufficiently large or small. Below we give an explanation of this phenomenon.
We begin by writing

\bm{\Omega}=\bm{\Omega}_{l}+\bm{\Omega}_{a},

(3.14)

where
\bm{\Omega}_{a}
is the contribution to
\bm{\Omega}
due to the presence of the cat’s toy mechanism (i.e. if
\sigma=0
then
\bm{\Omega}_{a}=0
and
\bm{\Omega}=\bm{\Omega}_{l}
). Explicitly we have

\bm{\Omega}_{l}=A\left(\bm{M}+\frac{mr^{2}\langle\bm{M},A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}{1-mr^{2}\langle A\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}\rangle}\bm{\gamma}\right),

(3.15)

and

\bm{\Omega}_{a}=mr^{2}\sigma A\left(\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3})+\frac{mr^{2}\langle\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3}),A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}{1-mr^{2}\langle A\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}\rangle}\bm{\gamma}\right).

(3.16)

Introducing the non-dimensional time parameter
\tau=\sigma t
the equations (
3.4
) may be written as

\bm{M}^{\prime}=\varepsilon\left(\frac{mr^{2}}{\|\bm{M}\|}\bm{M}\times\bm{\Omega}_{l}\right)+\bm{M}\times\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a},\qquad\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}=\varepsilon\left(\frac{mr^{2}}{\|\bm{M}\|}\gamma\times\bm{\Omega}_{l}\right)+\bm{\gamma}\times\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a},

where
\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a}:=\frac{1}{\sigma}\bm{\Omega}_{a}
and
{}^{\prime}=\frac{d}{d\tau}
.

On the one hand, if
\varepsilon\gg 1
, then, neglecting the term with
\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a}
, which encodes the effect of the cat’s toy mechanism,
we recover the vector field of the classical, integrable, Chaplygin sphere problem
[
16
]

multiplied by the overall factor
\frac{\varepsilon mr^{2}}{\|\bm{M}\|}
which is constant along the flow.

On the other hand, if
\varepsilon\ll 1
, then, neglecting the term of order
\varepsilon
we obtain the equations

\bm{M}^{\prime}=\bm{M}\times\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a}\qquad\bm{\gamma}^{\prime}=\bm{\gamma}\times\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a}.

(3.17)

In addition to the first integrals (
3.5
), we now show that this system possesses an additional smooth first integral and a smooth invariant measure and is therefore integrable in virtue of Jacobi’s last multiplier theorem
[
1
]
. To give the explicit form of these invariants we proceed in analogy with the analysis in section
3.1.1
. We first observe that the set of points
(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}
such that
\gamma_{3}=0
is invariant. Actually the dynamics along this set is simply harmonic. This follows from the observation that
\tilde{\bm{\Omega}}_{a}
equals
-\kappa\bm{e}_{3}
, with
\kappa=\frac{mr^{2}\sigma}{I_{3}+mr^{2}}
, when
\gamma_{3}=0
(which can be deduced from the expression for
\bm{\Omega}_{a}
in (
3.16
)). The additional smooth first integral of (
3.17
) only depends on
\bm{\gamma}
and is given by:

k(\bm{\gamma})=\begin{cases}\exp\left(\frac{|\gamma_{3}|^{-\frac{mr^{2}}{I_{3}}}}{\sqrt{1-mr^{2}\langle\bm{\gamma},A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}}\right)&\text{if }\gamma_{3}\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }\gamma_{3}=0.\end{cases}

The smooth invariant measure is
\chi(\bm{\gamma})d\bm{M}d\bm{\gamma}
with

\chi(\bm{\gamma})=\begin{cases}k(\bm{\gamma})|\gamma_{3}|^{-1}&\text{if }\gamma_{3}\neq 0,\\
0&\text{if }\gamma_{3}=0.\end{cases}

The density of this invariant measure is nonnegative and only vanishes along a set of measure zero and therefore also falls within the class of measures considered in
[
31
]
.

3.2
The case
W=0

The equations of motion are

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{M}}

\displaystyle=\bm{M}\times\bm{\Omega},\qquad\dot{\bm{\alpha}}=\bm{\alpha}\times\bm{\Omega},\qquad\dot{\bm{\beta}}=\bm{\beta}\times\bm{\Omega},\qquad\dot{\bm{\gamma}}=\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega},

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{u}}

\displaystyle=-rB(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega})+\bm{V}_{s}(\bm{x}),

with
\bm{x}
given by (
3.2
) and

\bm{\Omega}(\bm{M},B,\bm{u})=A\left(\bm{M}-mr\bm{\gamma}\times B^{-1}\bm{V}_{s}(\bm{x})+\frac{mr^{2}\langle\bm{M}-mr\bm{\gamma}\times B^{-1}\bm{V}_{s}(\bm{x}),A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}{1-mr^{2}\langle A\bm{\gamma},\bm{\gamma}\rangle}\bm{\gamma}\right).

(3.18)

Under the assumption that the vector field
\bm{V}_{s}
is divergence free, the system possesses an invariant measure. We state this as the following proposition whose proof is a direct calculation using equations (
2.18c
), (
3.3
) and (
2.12
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose
\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bm{V}_{s}=0
. Then

\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-mr^{2}\langle\bm{\gamma},A\bm{\gamma}\rangle}}d\bm{M}\,d\bm{u}\,d\bm{\alpha}\,d\bm{\beta}\,d\bm{\gamma}

is an invariant measure.

The existence of this invariant
measure was already known in some particular cases.
In
[
5
]
it was found for
\bm{V}_{s}
corresponding to the uniformly
rotating plane (i.e. given by (
2.3
)) and in
[
33
]
for the non-autonomous vector
field
\bm{V}_{s}
corresponding to a vibrating plane.

Assuming distinct moments of inertia,
I_{j}
, and non-zero
\bm{V}_{s}
, we do not expect existence of
additional first integrals and we believe that the dynamics is chaotic.
In fact the papers
[
5
]
and
[
33
]
perform numerical explorations
for the particular vector fields
\bm{V}_{s}
mentioned above and reach this
conclusion.

4
A body of revolution with a cat’s toy mechanism

This section considers the cat’s toy mechanism described in section
2

and illustrated in Fig
under the
additional assumption that the fastened rigid body possesses an axial symmetry along the axis of rotation of the shell. This situation puts us in the framework of section
2.3.2
. Therefore, assuming that
V=0
and that the axis
\bm{E}_{3}
of the body frame
\Sigma_{b}
is aligned with the aforementioned symmetry axis, we have

\bm{W}_{b}(\bm{\rho})=\sigma\bm{\rho}\times\bm{E}_{3},

as in (
2.6
). In particular, the system possesses the moving energy integral (
2.26
). If
\sigma=0
, one recovers the classical problem of a solid of revolution rolling on the plane. This problem is well-known to be integrable in virtue of the existence of two first integrals
J_{1},J_{2}
and an invariant measure found by Chaplygin
[
16
]
(see
[
8
]
for historical details).

In section
4.2
below we indicate that for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
the system possesses an invariant measure whose form is identical to the one found by Chaplygin in the case
\sigma=0
. Furthermore, in proposition
4.2
we show that a suitable modification of
J_{1}
and
J_{2}
are first integrals of the system for any
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
. The existence of these integrals, the invariant measure and the moving energy allow us to conclude that the system is integrable by Jacobi’s last multiplier theorem
[
1
]
.

This situation is reminiscent of the (integrable) problem of a homogeneous sphere rolling without slipping on a surface of revolution. If the surface rotates about its axis of symmetry at constant, but arbitrary angular speed, modifications of the first integrals and the invariant measure persist and the problem remains integrable
[
11
,
24
]
.

4.1
Preliminaries

Given that the shell
\mathcal{S}
is a body is of revolution, the relation (
2.11
) between
\bm{\rho}
and
\bm{\gamma}
given by the Gauss map, may be described by (see e.g.
[
8
,
17
]
):

\bm{\rho}(\bm{\gamma})=-\bm{n}_{b}^{-1}(\bm{\gamma})=(f_{1}(\gamma_{3})\gamma_{1},f_{1}(\gamma_{3})\gamma_{2},f_{2}(\gamma_{3})),

(4.1)

where
f_{1},f_{2}
are real functions determining the shape of
\mathcal{S}
, which satisfy the differential equation

f_{2}^{\prime}(\gamma_{3})\gamma_{3}=f_{1}(\gamma_{3})\gamma_{3}-(1-\gamma_{3}^{2})f_{1}^{\prime}(\gamma_{3}).

The function
f_{1}
is strictly positive, its value being equal to a principal radius of curvature
of
\mathcal{S}
(see
[
30
]
). On the other hand, the symmetric distribution of mass of the body
implies that the first two moments of inertia are equal so

\mathbb{I}=\mbox{diag}(I_{1},I_{1},I_{3}).

From proposition
2.2
, we have that the
\rm{SE}(2)
-reduced equations of motion (
2.23
) are the restriction of

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{M}}

\displaystyle=\bm{M}\times\bm{\Omega}+m\dot{\bm{\rho}}\times(\bm{\Omega}\times\bm{\rho})+mg\bm{\rho}\times\bm{\gamma}+m\sigma(\bm{\rho}\times\bm{E}_{3})\times(\dot{\bm{\rho}}+\bm{\Omega}\times\bm{\rho}),

(4.2)

\displaystyle\dot{\bm{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega},

to the invariant set
\|\bm{\gamma}\|^{2}=1
, where

\bm{\Omega}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})=A(\bm{\gamma})\left(\bm{M}+\frac{m\langle\bm{M}+m\sigma\bm{\rho}\times(\bm{\rho}\times\bm{E}_{3}),A(\bm{\gamma})\bm{\rho}\rangle}{1-m\langle A(\bm{\gamma})\bm{\rho},\bm{\rho}\rangle}\bm{\rho}-m\sigma\bm{\rho}\times(\bm{\rho}\times\bm{E}_{3})\right),

(4.3)

and
A(\bm{\gamma})
is given by (
2.17
).
Equations (
4.2
) have an extra
\mathrm{SO}(2)
-symmetry corresponding to the rotations about the axis of symmetry of the body. This corresponds to the transformation

\bm{M}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{M},\qquad\bm{\gamma}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{\gamma}\qquad\text{with}\qquad R_{\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi&0\\
\sin\phi&\cos\phi&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(4.4)

It can be checked from (
4.1
) and (
4.3
) that
\bm{\rho}
and
\bm{\Omega}
accordingly transform as
\bm{\rho}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{\rho}
,
\bm{\Omega}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{\Omega}
and it is immediate to see that equations (
4.2
) are invariant.

4.2
Existence of an invariant measure

When
\sigma=0
, the system possesses the following invariant measure found by Chaplygin
[
16
]
(see also
[
8
]
),

\frac{1}{\mu(\gamma_{3})}d\bm{M}d\bm{\gamma},

(4.5)

where

\begin{split}\mu(\gamma_{3})&=\sqrt{I_{1}I_{3}+m\langle\bm{\rho},\mathbb{I}\bm{\rho}\rangle}\\
&=\sqrt{I_{1}I_{3}+mI_{1}f_{1}(\gamma_{3})^{2}(1-\gamma_{3}^{2})+mI_{3}f_{2}(\gamma_{3})^{2}}.\end{split}

(4.6)

One can check that the term proportional to
\sigma
in (
4.2
) has zero divergence (with respect to
\bm{M}
) and that the terms in
\bm{\Omega}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})
in (
4.3
)
proportional to
\sigma
vanish when taking the divergence with respect to
\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}
. As a consequence we have the following.

Proposition 4.1
.

The measure (
4.5
) is invariant by the system (
4.2
)
for any value of
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}
.

4.3
First integrals

A convenient approach to investigate the reduced dynamics by the
\mathrm{SO}(2)
symmetry
defined by (
4.4
) is working with coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}\ni(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})

that are invariant under the action.
Following the approach of Borisov and Mamaev
[
8
]
for the case
\sigma=0
, we consider the evolution of the quantities

K_{1}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})=\frac{\langle\bm{M},\bm{\rho}\rangle}{f_{1}(\gamma_{3})},\qquad K_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})=\mu(\gamma_{3})\Omega_{3}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}),

where
\mu(\gamma_{3})
is defined by (
4.6
) and
\Omega_{3}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})
is the third component of
\bm{\Omega}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})
given by (
2.22
). One can easily check that
K_{1}
,
K_{2}
are
\mathrm{SO}(2)
invariant and a calculation shows that they satisfy the following
equations

\begin{pmatrix}\dot{K_{1}}\\
\dot{K_{2}}\end{pmatrix}=\dot{\gamma_{3}}\left(G(\gamma_{3})\begin{pmatrix}K_{1}\\
K_{2}\end{pmatrix}+\sigma\>\bm{b}(\gamma_{3})\right),

(4.7)

where the
2\times 2
matrix
G(\gamma_{3})
and the vector
\bm{b}({\gamma_{3}})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
are given by

G(\gamma_{3})=-\frac{1}{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}0&I_{3}\left(1-\left(\frac{f_{2}}{f_{1}}\right)^{\prime}\right)\\
mf_{1}(f_{1}-f_{2}^{\prime})&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\bm{b}(\gamma_{3})=-\frac{1}{\mu}\begin{pmatrix}0\\
-mf_{1}I_{1}(f_{1}\gamma_{3}-(1-\gamma_{3}^{2})f_{1}^{\prime})\end{pmatrix},

where the dependence of
f_{1}
,
f_{2}
,
f_{1}^{\prime}
,
f_{2}^{\prime}
and
\mu
on
\gamma_{3}
has been omitted.

The structure of the system (
4.7
) allows us to apply the approach followed by Dalla Via, Fassò and Sansonetto in
[
19
, section 3.1]
to prove the existence of first integrals.
Specifically, let
Y(\gamma_{3})\in\rm{GL}(2)
be the solution of the (non-autonomous, linear, homogeneous)
2\times 2
matrix differential equation

\frac{dY}{d\gamma_{3}}=G(\gamma_{3})Y,\qquad Y(0)=\rm{id}_{2},

and
\bm{y}(\gamma_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
the solution of the (non-autonomous, linear, inhomogeneous) differential equation

\frac{d\bm{y}}{d\gamma_{3}}=G(\gamma_{3})\bm{y}+\bm{b}(\gamma_{3}),\qquad\bm{y}(0)=0.

In analogy with proposition 2 in
[
19
]
(its second statement), we have.

Proposition 4.2
.

The two components
J_{1},J_{2}
of the map
J:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times S^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{2}
given by

J(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})=Y^{-1}(\gamma_{3})\left(\begin{pmatrix}K_{1}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})\\
K_{2}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma})\end{pmatrix}-\sigma\bm{y}(\gamma_{3})\right)

are first integrals of (
4.2
).

The proof is a direct calculation relying on the definitions of
Y(\gamma_{3})
,
\bm{y}(\gamma_{3})
and (
4.7
). These integrals can be expressed in explicit form if the body of revolution has spherical shape (Routh’s sphere) and may be found in
[
18
]
.

Remark 4.3
.

Equations (
4.7
) and our observations about the invariant measure made in
4.2
resemble some aspects of the discussion in Borisov and Mamaev
[
8
]
about the gyrostatic generalization of the problem of a solid of revolution rolling without slipping on the plane. This may suggest the possibility of conjugating such problem with the one treated here via a (time-dependent) change of coordinates.

On the left hand side of the above relation

5.1.1
Symmetries, reduction and first integrals

The system possesses two different, and commuting,
\rm{SO}(2)

of the space frame

\bm{e}_{3}

of the body frame
\Sigma_{b}

\bm{E}_{3}

This vector satisfies

\dot{\bm{U}}=-r(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega})+\bm{U}\times\bm{\Omega}-r\sigma(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3})-\eta(\bm{U}\times\bm{\gamma}).

may be written

\bm{\Omega}=\bm{\Omega}(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma},\bm{U})=\frac{1}{I+mr^{2}}\left(\bm{M}+\frac{mr^{2}}{I}\langle\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}\rangle\bm{\gamma}+mr\eta\,\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{U}\times\bm{\gamma})+mr^{2}\sigma\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3})\right).

(5.5)

The expressions given above are independent of the row vectors
\bm{\alpha}
and
\bm{\beta}
of the

,

\begin{split}\dot{\bm{M}}&=\bm{M}\times\bm{\Omega},\\
\dot{\bm{\gamma}}&=\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega},\\
\dot{\bm{U}}&=-r(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{\Omega})+\bm{U}\times\bm{\Omega}-r\sigma(\bm{\gamma}\times\bm{E}_{3})-\eta(\bm{U}\times\bm{\gamma}),\end{split}

(5.6)

with
\bm{\Omega}
given by (
5.5
). The system possesses the geometric first integrals
\|\bm{\gamma}\|
and
\langle\bm{U},\bm{\gamma}\rangle
and its restriction to the 7-dimensional manifold

\mathcal{M}_{7}=\{(\bm{M},\bm{\gamma},\bm{U})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\times\mathbb{R}^{3}\,:\,\|\bm{\gamma}\|=1,\quad\mbox{and}\quad\langle\bm{U},\bm{\gamma}\rangle=r\}

defines a flow isomorphic
to the reduced system on
\mathcal{A}/\rm{SO}(2)
. This flow has the following set of equilibrium points

\mathcal{M}_{7}^{\mathrm{\small eq}}=\{\bm{M}=(0,0,M_{3}),\;\bm{\gamma}=(0,0,\pm 1),\;\bm{U}=(0,0,\pm r)\,:\,M_{3}\in\mathbb{R}\},

which correspond to motions where the sphere is uniformly spinning without rolling, positioned at the origin
O
of the plane

\Pi
and with the
\bm{E}_{3}
-axis of the cat’s toy mechanism aligned vertically (at these configurations
the vector fields
\bm{V}_{s}
and
\bm{W}_{b}
vanish). In what follows we shall restrict our attention to the
complementary part of the phase space
\mathcal{M}_{7}
which we denote by
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}
,
namely,

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}=\mathcal{M}_{7}\setminus\mathcal{M}_{7}^{\mathrm{\small eq}}.

Obviously,
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}
is an open dense subset of
\mathcal{M}_{7}
which is invariant by the dynamics.

The additional
\rm{SO}(2)
-symmetry
described above, corresponding to rotations of the body frame about the
\bm{E}_{3}
-axis, results in the
invariance of the manifold
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}
and the equations (
5.6
) under the action,

\bm{M}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{M},\qquad\bm{\gamma}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{\gamma},\qquad\bm{U}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{U}\qquad\text{with}\qquad R_{\phi}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\phi&-\sin\phi&0\\
\sin\phi&\cos\phi&0\\
0&0&1\end{pmatrix}.

(5.7)

The invariance of (
5.6
) is readily verified since, as follows from (
5.5
),
the angular velocity
\bm{\Omega}
also transforms as
\bm{\Omega}\mapsto R_{\phi}\bm{\Omega}
. Hence the system may be reduced
to the quotient space that we denote as

\mathcal{R}_{6}=\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}/\mathrm{SO}(2),

which is a smooth 6-dimensional manifold (since the action (
5.7
) is free on

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}
).

Additionally, the system (
5.6
) possesses the momentum first integrals

\|\bm{M}\|^{2},\qquad\langle\bm{M},\bm{\gamma}\rangle,

(5.8)

and the preserved moving energy
E_{mov}
given by (
2.27
). In our case, using that

\|\bm{x}\|^{2}=\|\bm{U}-r\bm{\gamma}\|^{2}=\|\bm{U}\|^{2}-r^{2}
along
\mathcal{M}_{7}
, this may be written as

E_{mov}=\frac{I}{2}\langle\bm{\Omega}+\sigma\bm{E}_{3},\bm{\Omega}+\sigma\bm{E}_{3}\rangle+\frac{mr^{2}}{2}\|\bm{\gamma}\times(\bm{\Omega}+\sigma\bm{E}_{3})\|^{2}-\frac{m}{2}\eta^{2}\|\bm{U}\|^{2}.

(5.9)

It is easily seen that the momentum fist integrals (
5.8
), as well as the preserved moving energy (
5.9
),
are invariant under the action (
5.7
) and therefore descend as first integrals of the reduced
system on
\mathcal{R}_{6}
.

Finally, since
\mathrm{div}_{S^{2}}\bm{W}_{b}=0
and
\mathrm{div}_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\bm{V}_{s}=0
, by proposition
5.1
, the system (
5.3
) has invariant measure
d\bm{M}d\bm{u}d\bm{\alpha}d\bm{\beta}d\bm{\gamma}
. It can be checked that
d\bm{M}d\bm{\gamma}d\bm{U}
is an invariant measure for the reduced system (
5.6
). By general results on free actions of compact groups (e.g. Lemma 3.4 in
[
29
]
), this invariant measure descends to a smooth invariant measure for the
reduced system on
\mathcal{R}_{6}
.

Summarizing, the reduced dynamics on the 6-dimensional reduced manifold
\mathcal{R}_{6}
possesses 3 first integrals (
5.8
) and (
5.9
),
and a smooth invariant measure. Below we argue that these invariants
lead to the integrability of the dynamics
for initial conditions with
\bm{M}
and
\bm{\gamma}
parallel, and we exhibit numerical evidence
indicating that the dynamics is chaotic otherwise.

5.1.2
The case
M
parallel to
\gamma

It is not hard to see that initial conditions on
\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}

with
\bm{M}

\bm{\gamma}
are critical points of the momentum first integrals (

\widetilde{\mathcal{M}_{7}}

\mathcal{R}_{6}

The reduced dynamics restricted to these 3-dimensional submanifolds possesses the moving energy integral
(
5.9
) and an invariant measure and hence it is integrable

[

.

5.1.3
The general case (

\bm{M}

Appendix A
Appendix

We provide a general framework explaining the existence of the first integrals in proposition
3.1
. Throughout this appendix we assume that we are given a configuration manifold
Q
, a Lagrangian
L:TQ\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
and a (linear) constraint distribution
\mathcal{D}\subset TQ
specifying some nonholonomic constraints. We are interested in determining conditions for the existence of first integrals for the nonholonomic system with Lagrangian
L
and affine nonholonomic constraints described by the affine distribution
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}+Z
where
Z
is a given vector field on
Q
.

A.1
Affine nonholonomic Noether’s theorem

Let
\Psi:G\times Q\rightarrow Q
be an action of the Lie group
G
on
Q
and
\Psi^{TQ}:G\times TQ\rightarrow TQ
be the lifted action. Let
\xi_{Q}
be the infinitesimal generator of the
\Psi
-action on
Q
corresponding to an element
\xi
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
(i.e.
\xi_{Q}(q)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}\mathrm{exp}(\xi t)\cdot q\in T_{q}Q
). Finally, let
J_{\xi}:TQ\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be the momentum component in the direction of
\xi
, namely,

J_{\xi}(q,\dot{q})=\left\langle\frac{\partial L}{\partial\dot{q}}(q,\dot{q})\,,\,\xi_{Q}(q)\right\rangle.

The following well-known result is sometimes referred to as nonholonomic Noether’s theorem
[
1
,
7
,
27
]
.

Proposition A.1
.

If the Lagrangian
L
is invariant under the lifted action
\Psi^{TQ}
and
\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
is such that
\xi_{Q}(q)\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
for all
q\in Q
(i.e.
\xi
is a horizontal symmetry), then

J_{\xi}|_{\mathcal{D}}
is a first integral of the nonholonomic system
(L,Q,\mathcal{D})
.

This result admits the following immediate generalization to the affine case and is a particular instance of proposition 2 in
[
26
]
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
Z\in\mathfrak{X}(Q)
be any vector field and consider the affine distribution
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}+Z\subset TQ
. Under the same hypothesis of proposition
A.1
, the function
J_{\xi}|_{\mathcal{A}}
is a first integral of the nonholonomic system determined by
L
and
\mathcal{A}
.

The key observation to connect this result to proposition 2 in
[
26
]
is that the vector field
\xi_{Q}
is annihilated by the reaction force by the assumption that
\xi_{Q}(q)\in\mathcal{D}_{q}
for all
q\in Q
.

A.2
Affine LR systems

Now suppose that
Q=G
is a Lie group and the action
\Psi
of the previous section is left multiplication. The invariance of
L

under the lifted action
\Psi^{TQ}
is usually called
left invariance
. In addition, we assume that the distribution
\mathcal{D}
is right invariant (i.e.
\mathcal{D}_{gh}=T_{g}R_{h}(\mathcal{D}_{g})
for all
g,h\in G
, where
R_{h}:G\rightarrow G
is right multiplication by
h
). These systems where introduced by Veselov and Veselova
[
44
]
and are termed
LR systems
.

By right invariance we have
\mathcal{D}_{g}=T_{e}(\mathfrak{d})
for all
g\in G
where
\mathfrak{d}
is the value of
\mathcal{D}
at the identity
e\in G
, namely
\mathfrak{d}=\mathcal{D}_{e}\subset\mathfrak{g}
.
Non-integrability of
\mathcal{D}
is equivalent to the condition that
\mathfrak{d}
is not a subalgebra of
\mathfrak{g}
.
A direct consequence of Proposition
A.2
is the following.

Proposition A.3
.

Let
\xi\in\mathfrak{d}
, then
J_{\xi}|_{\mathcal{A}}
is a first integral of the nonholonomic system determined by
L
and
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{D}+Z
where
Z
is any vector field on
G
.

Proof.

It is easily seen that such
\xi
is a horizontal symmetry. Indeed,

\xi_{G}(g)=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}L_{\exp(\xi t)}g=\left.\frac{d}{dt}\right|_{t=0}R_{g}(\exp\xi t)=T_{e}R_{g}(\xi)\in\mathcal{D}_{g},

where
L_{\exp(\xi t)}
is left multiplication by
\exp(\xi t)
(there should be no risk of confusion with the Lagrangian function, also
denoted by
L
).

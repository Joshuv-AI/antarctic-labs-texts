New Exceptional EFTs in de Sitter Space from Generalised Energy Conservation Preprint: APS/123-QED
Zong-Zhe Du, David Stefanyszyn

Abstract
We discover a surprising relationship between exceptional effective field theories (EFTs) in de Sitter space and a notion of generalised energy conservation (GEC) of an S-matrix defined in an extended Poincaré patch of four-dimensional de Sitter. By demanding that such an S-matrix only has support when the total energies of in and out states are equal, we constrain the coupling constants in theories of self-interacting scalars living in the exceptional series of de Sitter representations. We rediscover the theories of Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) and Special Galileon, and when increasing the conformal dimension we find evidence for new exceptional theories where the four-point scalar self interactions are uniquely fixed in terms of a single coupling constant. We conjecture that for each integer conformal dimension Δ ≥ 4 \Delta\geq 4 , there is at least one exceptional EFT that can be entirely fixed by GEC.



I
Introduction and Summary

Energy conservation is usually perceived as a consequence of global time-translation symmetry by Noether’s theorem
Noether 1918
. In flat space where time translation is an exact symmetry, the corresponding Ward-Takahashi identity implies the presence of a universal
\delta(E_{\text{in}}-E_{\text{out}})
within all the S-matrices. In other words, the probability for the incoming and outgoing states to have different total energy is exactly zero. When there is no time translation symmetry as in de Sitter (dS) space, energy conservation should not be imposed a priori even if we have asymptotic states where well-separated wave packets can scatter.

Recently, the authors of
Donath and Pajer 2024
suggested that one can extend the Poincaré patch of dS space by (we will discuss the
\eta=0
elephant in the room in detail in this work):

\displaystyle ds^{2}=\frac{-d\eta^{2}+d\vec{x}^{2}}{H^{2}\eta^{2}},\;\;\eta\in(-\infty,+\infty)\,.

(1)

The idea is to glue together two Poincaré patches of dS (one expanding and one contracting) in order to define an in-out correlator from which one can define an S-matrix via an LSZ reduction (see
Melville and Pimentel 2024a
;
Melville and Pimentel 2024b
;
Marolf et al. 2013
;
Albrychiewicz and Neiman 2021
for other definitions of scattering in dS). It is not completely clear to us what the physical interpretation of this S-matrix is, however it has some very nice properties that we will utilise in this work.

The
\alpha\rightarrow\beta
element of this S-matrix can be defined in the interaction picture by the Dyson series
Donath and Pajer 2024

\displaystyle S_{\beta\alpha}

\displaystyle\equiv\braket{\beta|\mathcal{T}\{e^{-i\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\eta\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}}\}|\alpha}

(2)

\displaystyle\equiv 1+i(2\pi)^{4}\mathcal{A}~\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{\text{in}}-\vec{k}_{\text{out}})\,,

(3)

where
\mathcal{H}_{\text{int}}
is the interaction Hamiltonian in the interaction picture, and
\beta,\;\alpha
are out and in states in the interaction picture, respectively. The delta function enforcing momentum conservation
\delta^{3}(\vec{k}_{\text{in}}-\vec{k}_{\text{out}})
arises as a consequence of the spatial translation symmetry of (
1
).

In
Donath and Pajer 2024
it was suggested that this object should be considered for theories that are
manifestly
infrared (IR)-finite where there is no divergence in the interacting Lagrangian as
\eta\rightarrow 0
(with the IR-divergent
\sqrt{-g}
included). One can alternatively regard this potential IR-divergence as the late-time divergence encountered in inflationary cosmology. The absence of such a divergence can be guaranteed thanks to the decay of massive fields and/or derivative interactions. In such cases, every contribution to the S-matrix contains an energy-conserving delta function, or derivatives thereof, thereby ensuring a generalised version of energy conservation:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\sum_{m=0}\mathcal{A}^{(m)}\partial_{k_{T}}^{m}\delta(k_{T})\,,

(4)

where
k_{T}\equiv\sum_{a\in\text{in}}k_{a}-\sum_{b\in\text{out}}k_{b}
,
k_{a}
are the magnitudes of three-momenta and the amplitudes
\mathcal{A}^{(m)}
are functions of the external kinematics. Such energy-conserving delta functions, when scattering e.g. conformally-coupled or exceptional series scalars, arise from integrals of the form
\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}d\eta\eta^{p}e^{-ik_{T}\eta}
with integer
p\geq 0
for manifestly IR-finite theories.

In this work, however, we allow ourselves to consider theories that are NOT manifestly IR-finite. By imposing Bunch-Davies (BD) vacuum conditions for both the asymptotic past and future, where the free vacuum
\ket{0}
(
\bra{0}
) is annihilated by the annihilation (creation) operator
a_{\vec{k}}
(
a_{\vec{k}}^{\dagger}
), we consider scattering processes by shooting particles from the far past to the far future with a contour deformation around
\eta=0
(which we discuss in detail below). Note that we do not have the benefit of time-translation symmetry and therefore we need to define both
\ket{0}
and
\bra{0}
separately, and with our chosen boundary conditions particle creation and annihilation is kinematically forbidden. In that sense this S-matrix is somewhat similar to the BD S-matrix defined in
Melville and Pimentel 2024a
.

In our case, where the interactions are not manifestly IR-finite, an extra term
\mathcal{A}_{k_{T}\neq 0}
is in general possible in the S-matrix without any energy-conserving delta function:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}+\sum_{m=0}\mathcal{A}^{(m)}\partial_{k_{T}}^{m}\delta(k_{T})\,.

(5)

Such an amplitude therefore has support when energy is not conserved. However, there are perhaps two complementary perspectives we could take on why it is wise to demand
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}=0
and therefore
generalised energy conservation
(GEC). The calculation of
\mathcal{A}_{k_{T}\neq 0}^{(\pm)}
involves choosing between two different time integration contours
(\pm)
, as shown in Figure
1
and
2
, with the difference between the two proportional to the residue of the integrand at
\eta=0
. A non-zero
\mathcal{A}_{k_{T}\neq 0}^{(\pm)}
implies eternal energy creation or annihilation, which could lead to instabilities. To avoid potential instabilities, we demand that the residue of the would-be
\eta=0
pole vanishes, which further implies
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}=0
, as we will discuss. Surprisingly, we find that such a non-trivial condition can impose relations between various coupling constants appearing in general Lagrangians that are not manifestly IR-finite. Imposing GEC can therefore be used to pick out special theories. We note that imposing GEC is equivalent to imposing the absence of logarithmic divergences in boundary correlators. In this sense our work resonates with other work that showed the absence of logs in the boundary correlators of pure General Relativity
Goodhew 2023
;
Anninos et al. 2015
.

We concentrate on massive scalars that live in the exceptional series of four-dimensional de Sitter representations with integer conformal dimension
\Delta\geq 3
, and find that by imposing GEC there is a unique four-point amplitude for each mass where
m^{2}=\Delta(3-\Delta)H^{2}
, when we allow for operators with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives. We focus on these masses since such scalars in dS space are naturally packaged with non-linearly realised symmetries
Bonifacio et al. 2019
. Despite the tachyonic massess, these scalars are indeed unitary irreducible representations of dS isometries (see e.g.
Sun 2025
;
Gazeau et al. 2010
;
Penedones et al. 2023
for discussions). We recover the known theories of Dirac-Born-Infeld (DBI) (
\Delta=4,m^{2}=-4H^{2}
), Special Galileon (
\Delta=5,m^{2}=-10H^{2}
) for the first two tachyonic exceptional series masses, and find evidence for the existence of new theories beyond these known examples as we continue to increase the conformal dimension of the field. In the cases we consider, we find that the four-point interactions are completely fixed in terms of a single coupling constant. We therefore refer to such theories as
exceptional EFTs
. The leading order actions for the first two examples beyond the Special Galileon, with
\Delta=6,m^{2}=-18H^{2}
and
\Delta=7,m^{2}=-28H^{2}
can be found in (
IV
), with conditions (
50
), and (
IV
) with conditions (
54
), respectively.

The space of exceptional EFTs in flat space have been carved out using soft limits of scattering amplitudes
Cheung et al. 2015
;
Cheung et al. 2016
;
Cheung et al. 2017
;
Padilla et al. 2017
and algebraic classifications
Roest et al. 2019
;
Bogers and Brauner 2018
;
Hinterbichler and Joyce 2015
. There are no flat-space exceptional EFTs beyond the DBI and Special Galileon. Furthermore, the space of exceptional EFTs in dS space for single-scalar theories has been considered from algebraic and brane-world constructions
Burrage et al. 2011
;
Bonifacio et al. 2019
;
Bonifacio et al. 2022
;
Garoffolo et al. 2025
;
Goon et al. 2011a
;
Goon et al. 2011b
and wavefunction soft limits
Armstrong et al. 2022
(see also
Bittermann and Joyce 2022
). No evidence of new single-scalar theories beyond the Special Galileon has been found. These results suggest that the new theories we have discovered in this work might only exist when coupled to additional degrees of freedom. There is evidence that such theories do exist in dS given the existence of certain extensions of the dS algebra
Joung and Mkrtchyan 2016
. It would be very interesting to find the symmetries/algebras and spectra that define the theories we have found evidence for in this paper.

III
A de Sitter S-matrix and field basis independence

Let us start by briefly discussing the dS S-matrix proposed in
Donath and Pajer 2024

Donath and Pajer 2024

\displaystyle G(\eta,\vec{x})={}_{\text{out}}{\braket{\Omega|\mathcal{T}\{\phi(x)\phi(x_{1})\ldots\phi(x_{n})\}|\Omega}}_{\text{in}}\,,

(8)

Note that we include a factor of

\displaystyle(a)\;\;\;\eta>T^{+},

where

Let’s first consider the integral in the far-future region where any exceptional series scalar field (with BD normalisation) can be approximated by

IV
Exceptional EFTs from generalised energy conservation

We now turn our attention to imposing GEC on the four-point amplitudes arising from the self-interactions of exceptional series scalars. We could in principle impose a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry such that only contact diagrams contribute, however in each case we find that cubic vertices are forbidden by GEC and therefore no exchange contributions to four-point amplitudes are allowed. We will show this explicitly only for the
\Delta=3
scalar with the generalisation to other conformal dimensions simple. For four-point interactions we include operators with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives.

\bullet

\Delta=3

We begin our analysis with the first allowed mass of exceptional series scalars which is
m^{2}=0
corresponding to
\Delta=3
. The mode function is given by (
19
). Let’s first consider cubic interactions where the only dS-invariant interaction that cannot be removed by a field redefinition is the potential
c^{(3)}_{0}\phi^{3}
. The corresponding three-point amplitude is computed by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{3}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{6c^{(3)}_{0}}{H^{4}}\sqrt{8k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\eta}{\eta^{4}}f_{k_{1}}(\eta)f_{k_{2}}(\eta)f_{k_{3}}(\eta)\,,

(35)

which is clearly divergent as
\eta\rightarrow 0
. In order to remove any potential instabilities, we demand that its residue vanishes which leads to
\mathcal{A}_{k_{T}\neq 0}^{(\pm)}=0
. Given that the Taylor expansion of
f_{k_{1}}(\eta)f_{k_{2}}(\eta)f_{k_{3}}(\eta)
contains a non-zero
\eta^{3}
part,
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}=0
only if we set
c^{(3)}_{0}=0
. GEC therefore forbids any non-trivial cubic vertex for this massless scalar.

Moving to quartic interactions, we consider the general Lagrangian up to two derivatives:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\Delta=3}}{\sqrt{-g}}=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+d^{(3)}_{0}H^{2}\phi^{4}+d^{(3)}_{2}\phi^{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+\ldots

(36)

We include a factor of
H^{2}
in front of the potential such that the
d^{(\Delta)}_{2m}
have the same non-zero mass dimension for each
\Delta
.
In the previous section we showed that the two-derivative interaction does not contribute to the residue and therefore does not contribute to
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}
, so we need to now consider the potential. That contribution to the four-point amplitude is

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}\supset\frac{1}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\frac{24d^{(3)}_{0}}{H^{4}}\sqrt{16k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}

\displaystyle\int\frac{d\eta}{\eta^{4}}f_{\eta}(k_{1})f_{\eta}(k_{2})f_{\eta}(k_{3})f_{\eta}(k_{4})\,.

(37)

Again using the expansion (
22
) it is simple to see that the residue and therefore
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}
depends on
d^{(3)}_{0}
and therefore to realise GEC we need to fix
d^{(3)}_{0}=0
.

We are therefore left with only the two-derivative interaction, but as we showed explicitly in the previous section, this interaction does not yield a non-trivial amplitude since it can be removed by a field redefinition. To yield a non-zero result we can alter our starting point and allow for coloured massless scalars. In this case the potential
d^{(3)}_{0}\phi^{4}
, which can now be coloured, is still forbidden by GEC but the two-derivative interaction is now non-trivial and does indeed yield non-vanishing amplitudes. In this sense, GEC picks out the four-point two derivative interaction.

Since the massless mode function does not contain a term linear in
\eta
c.f. (
22
), the non-energy conserving part of the amplitude,
\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{k_{T}\neq 0}
, vanishes for for all tree-level processes in any two-derivative massless scalar theory. GEC therefore cannot uniquely single out the Non-Linear Sigma Model (NLSM) where operators with different powers of the field are tied together by symmetry. However, due to the presence of a non-linear shift symmetry, we do expect to be able to fix the couplings of the NLSM using soft limits. We will leave this for future investigation.

\bullet

\Delta=4

We now turn our attention to a scalar with
m^{2}=-4H^{2}
and
\Delta=4
. Here the mode function is given by

\displaystyle f_{k}(\eta)=\frac{He^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k^{5}}\eta}[-3i+k\eta(3+ik\eta)]\,.

(38)

In this case we immediately use our freedom to choose a field basis where there is no
\phi^{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}
operator (in the Appendix we verify that this interaction can indeed be removed by a field redefinition for this
\Delta=4
scalar) and now work with the most general quartic interactions up to four derivatives:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\Delta=4}}{\sqrt{-g}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+2H^{2}\phi^{2}+d^{(4)}_{0}H^{4}\phi^{4}+d^{(4)}_{4}(\partial\phi)^{4}\ldots

(39)

Note that any other four-derivative operator can be brought into the above form, up to lower-derivative operators, using integration by parts and field redefinitions.
We now compute the residue and find

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(\pm)}_{4}|_{k_{T}\neq 0}=-(d_{0}^{(4)}+8d_{4}^{(4)})H^{4}i\frac{36}{35}\theta(\pm k_{T})

\displaystyle\frac{\biggl(3(k_{1}^{7}+k_{2}^{7}+k_{3}^{7}+k_{4}^{7})-7[k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}(k_{1}^{3}+k_{2}^{3})+5\text{perms}]\biggr)}{k_{1}^{2}k_{2}^{2}k_{3}^{2}k_{4}^{2}}\,,

(40)

where the sign in front of
k_{T}
depends on the choice of contour. We find that GEC requires us to fix
d^{(4)}_{0}=-8d^{(4)}_{4}
which therefore fixes the interactions up to an overall coupling constant. It might seem surprising that operators with different numbers of derivatives can contribute to the amplitude in identical ways, but this is simply a consequence of scale invariance which yields a fixed momentum scaling. We know that there is indeed a special theory with
\Delta=4
and interactions with this power counting which is the DBI scalar (see e.g.
Goon et al. 2011a
;
Bonifacio et al. 2019
;
Bonifacio et al. 2022
) with leading-order Lagrangian:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\text{DBI}}}{\sqrt{-g}}=\frac{H^{2}\Lambda^{2}}{(1-\phi^{2}/\Lambda^{2})^{2}}\sqrt{1-\frac{(\partial\phi)^{2}/(H^{2}\Lambda^{2})}{1-\phi^{2}/\Lambda^{2}}}\,,

(41)

where
\Lambda
is a dimensionful scale. Upon expanding this Lagrangian up to quartic order in
\phi
and performing the field redefinition
\phi\rightarrow\phi-\phi^{3}/(2\Lambda^{2})
we recover our action (
39
) with
d^{(4)}_{0}=-8d^{(4)}_{4}
. GEC has therefore picked out the DBI scalar as being special amongst
\Delta=4
self-interacting scalars with at most one derivative per field.

The full corresponding amplitude is quite long and not particularly illuminating but let us point out that the coefficient of the part of the amplitude with the highest number of derivatives acting
\delta(k_{T})
is precisely the flat-space amplitude:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{4}^{\text{DBI}}\supset-\frac{1}{2}d_{0}^{(4)}H^{4}(s^{2}+st+t^{2})\partial_{k_{T}}^{4}\delta(k_{T}),

(42)

where the Mandelstam variables are given by

\displaystyle s=2(-k_{1}k_{2}+\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}_{2}),\;\;t=2(-k_{1}k_{3}+\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}_{3}).

(43)

This is analogous to the statement that cosmological correlators computed at the boundary
\eta\rightarrow 0
contain flat-space amplitudes as the coefficient of the leading total-energy pole
Maldacena and Pimentel 2011
;
Raju 2012
;
Pajer 2021
;
Pajer et al. 2020
.

\bullet

\Delta=5

Next we consider a scalar with
m^{2}=-10H^{2}
or equivalently
\Delta=5
, where the mode function is given by

\displaystyle f_{\eta}(k)=\frac{He^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k^{7}}\eta^{2}}\biggl\{-15-ik\eta\biggl[15+k\eta(6i-k\eta)\biggr]\biggr\}.

(44)

In keeping with the previous pattern, in this case we allow for general interactions up to six derivatives, with the two-derivative operator removed by a field redefinition. There is only a single six-derivative operator that cannot be removed since the only Lorentz-invariant four-point amplitude in flat-space is
\mathcal{A}\sim st(s+t)
. We therefore work with

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\Delta=5}}{\sqrt{-g}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+5H^{2}\phi^{2}+d^{(5)}_{0}H^{6}\phi^{4}

\displaystyle+d_{4}^{(5)}H^{2}(\partial\phi)^{4}+d_{6}^{(5)}(\partial\phi)^{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}+\ldots

(45)

As before we now impose GEC by demanding that
\mathcal{A}_{k_{T}\neq 0}^{(\pm)}=0
and find the conditions:

\displaystyle d_{4}^{(5)}=-\frac{6}{125}d_{0}^{(5)},\;\;d_{6}^{(5)}=\frac{3}{500}d_{0}^{(5)}.

(46)

Note that in this case there are more independent kinematical structures compared to
\Delta=3,4
which is why we find more conditions.

Up to integration by parts and field redefinitions (which do not alter the amplitude), which are worked through in
Armstrong et al. 2022
, these GEC conditions on the couplings uniquely pick out the Special Galileon (see
Bonifacio et al. 2019
;
Bonifacio et al. 2022
for details on this theory). Let us point out that again the coefficient of the delta function with the highest number of derivatives yields the flat-space amplitude:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\text{sGal}}_{4}\supset\frac{9}{500}d^{(5)}_{0}H^{6}st(-s-t)\partial_{k_{T}}^{6}\delta(k_{T}).

(47)

\bullet

\Delta=6

We have so far used GEC to recover known theories of special scalars in dS space. We now initiate the search for new theories by increasing the conformal dimension. For
m^{2}=-18H^{2}
, or
\Delta=6
, the mode function is given by

\displaystyle f_{k}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\frac{iHe^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k^{9}}\eta^{3}}\biggl\{105+k\eta\biggl[105i+

\displaystyle k\eta\biggl(-45+k\eta(-10i+k\eta)\biggr)\biggr]\biggr\}.

(48)

We now consider a general Lagrangian including operators up to eight derivatives. Again, there is a single new operator that cannot be removed by a field redefinition given that the only flat-space amplitude is
\mathcal{A}\sim(s^{2}+t^{2}+st)^{2}
so with a choice of field basis we can write

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\Delta=6}}{\sqrt{-g}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+9H^{2}\phi^{2}+d_{0}^{(6)}H^{8}\phi^{4}+d_{4}^{(6)}H^{4}(\partial\phi)^{4}

\displaystyle+d_{6}^{(6)}H^{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}+d_{8}^{(6)}(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{4}+\ldots

(49)

We now compute the four-point amplitude and by imposing GEC we find the conditions:

\displaystyle d_{4}^{(6)}=-\frac{13}{2646}d_{0}^{(6)},\;\;d_{6}^{(6)}=-\frac{5}{1323}d_{0}^{(6)},\;\;d_{8}^{(6)}=\frac{1}{5292}d_{0}^{(6)}\,.

(50)

To the best of our knowledge this theory has not been discussed before but given our results it is clearly a special subset of all possible
\Delta=6
theories with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives. Following the pattern for lower conformal dimensions, we might expect that this theory has a non-linearly realised symmetry. For single scalars such theories have been carved out and there is no special theory with
\Delta=6

Bonifacio et al. 2019
. This therefore suggests that the theory we have discovered here must contain additional degrees of freedom that do not contribute to this four-point amplitude. The flat-space amplitude again shows up as the coefficient of the delta function with the highest numbers of derivatives:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\Delta=6}_{4}\supset\frac{1}{5292}d_{0}^{(6)}H^{8}(s^{2}+t^{2}+st)^{2}\partial_{k_{T}}^{8}\delta(k_{T}).

(51)

\bullet

\Delta=7

We end our analysis by considering a scalar with
m^{2}=-28H^{2}
or
\Delta=7
, where the mode function is

\displaystyle f_{k}(\eta)=

\displaystyle\frac{He^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k^{11}}\eta^{4}}\biggl\{945+ik\eta\biggl[945+k\eta\biggl(420i

\displaystyle+k\eta\biggl(-105+k\eta(-15i+k\eta)\biggr)\biggr)\biggr]\biggr\}.

(52)

Continuing the previous pattern, we now allow for operators with at most ten derivatives. As before, there is a single new operator we can write down given that the only possible flat-space amplitude is
\mathcal{A}\sim st(s+t)(s^{2}+t^{2}+st)
. A convenient basis for the Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\frac{\mathcal{L}^{\Delta=7}}{\sqrt{-g}}=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+14H^{2}\phi^{2}+d_{0}^{(7)}H^{10}\phi^{4}+d_{4}^{(7)}H^{6}(\partial\phi)^{4}

\displaystyle+d_{6}^{(7)}H^{4}(\partial\phi)^{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}+d_{8}^{(7)}H^{2}(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{4}

\displaystyle+d_{10}^{(7)}\partial^{\alpha}[(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}]\partial_{\alpha}[(\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi)^{2}]+\ldots

(53)

As with all our previous cases, the GEC condition uniquely fixes all couplings up to some overall coefficient:

\displaystyle d_{4}^{(7)}=-\frac{129}{13034}d_{0}^{(7)},\;\;d_{6}^{(7)}=-\frac{141}{1981168}d_{0}^{(7)},\;\;

\displaystyle d_{8}^{(7)}=\frac{3}{247646}d_{0}^{(7)},\;\;d_{10}^{(7)}=\frac{3}{7924672}d_{0}^{(7)}\,,

(54)

and the flat-space amplitude is reproduced via

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{\Delta=7}_{4}\supset\frac{15}{15849344}d_{0}^{(7)}H^{10}st(s+t)(s^{2}+t^{2}+st)\partial_{k_{T}}^{10}\delta(k_{T}).

(55)

At this point, we conjecture that solutions to the GEC condition do not truncate as we increase the conformal dimension. We therefore expect to have infinitely many exceptional EFTs living in the exceptional series of de Sitter representations each with unique quartic self-interactions with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives (but quite possibly requiring additional degrees of freedom for
\Delta\geq 6
).

V
Discussions

In this paper we have considered the dS S-matrix proposed in
Donath and Pajer 2024
, and by demanding the absence of any instabilities, we have constrained the self-interactions of exceptional series scalars. In such theories, the corresponding amplitudes only have support when energy is conserved. We rediscovered the DBI and Special Galileon theories, and found evidence for new exceptional theories each with uniquely fixed quartic self-interactions with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives. The solutions we have found are therefore the leading-order ones in the derivative expansion. Our results lead us to make two conjectures:

Conjecture 1
.

For each integer conformal dimension
\Delta\geq 4
, there is a unique four-point amplitude (allowing for operators with at most
2\Delta-4
derivatives) that only has support when energy is conserved.

Conjecture 2
.

There is at least one exceptional EFT for each
\Delta\geq 4
exceptional series representation of dS space. For
\Delta\geq 6
such EFTs contain additional degrees of freedom.

Our second conjecture follows from the results of
Bonifacio et al. 2019
which suggests that
single-scalar
exceptional EFTs do not exist for
\Delta\geq 6
.

Interestingly, the exceptional EFTs are uniquely picked out by GEC/stability constraints in dS space, but not in AdS, since energy is automatically conserved in AdS by exact time-translation symmetry. In that sense, no tuning of the interactions is required to yield healthy theories in AdS. This conclusion coincides with representation theory, where in dS the exceptional EFTs live in the exceptional series of dS representations, whereas in AdS, they simply correspond to conventional scalar representations
Bonifacio et al. 2019
. We further point out that the uniqueness of the theories we have fixed using GEC, especially DBI and Special Galileon, which involve only a single scalar degree of freedom, resonates with the uniqueness of Yang-Mills and General Relativity in flat-space as theories of massless spin-
1
and spin-
2
particles, respectively. Indeed, there we start with a particular representation of the Poincaré group (massless spin-
1
or massless spin-
2
) and demand the absence of instability. To some order in derivatives, the condition yields unique theories (see e.g.
Deser 1970
;
de Rham 2014
). Here, we start with particular representations of the dS group, and by demanding the absence of instabilities, we arrive at unique theories, again up to some order in derivatives. Therefore, the exceptional theories in dS space appear more fundamental than their flat space counterparts, since additional constraints such as soft limits or non-linear symmetries are required to uniquely fix the latter, beyond representation theory and stability alone.

We hope that our work initiates a systematic search for new theories in dS space and we envisage a number of possible future research directions:

•

Moving to six-point might be an avenue for realising that new degrees of freedom are necessary for
\Delta\geq 6
. Indeed, it could be that exchange and contact diagrams due to scalar self-interactions cannot combine into six-point amplitudes that adhere to GEC and new degrees of freedom, and therefore new exchange processes, are required. For DBI and Special Galileon we expect that realising GEC requires cancellations between contact and exchange diagrams (without new degrees of freedom). We have already checked that for
\Delta=4
it is not possible to realise GEC from six-point contact diagrams alone when we allow for interactions with at most one derivative per field (which is the correct power-counting for DBI c.f. (
41
)).

•

It would be interesting to systematically search for extensions of the dS algebra whose non-linear realisation contains the scalars we have discovered in this work. A benefit of this algebraic approach is that it can confirm or deny the existence of theories, rather than merely yielding (convincing) evidence of the existence of theories.

•

If such multi-field theories exist, it would be interesting to analyse their flat-space limit.

•

It would be interesting to extend our analysis to spinning fields. It would be particularly interesting to see if there is a connection between GEC and the (non)-existence of interacting partially-massless theories.

•

The infinite towers of theories we have found could correspond to the longitudinal modes of the infinite higher spin fields in (A)dS in the decoupling limit
Joung et al. 2012
;
Alkalaev and Grigoriev 2011
;
Brust and Hinterbichler 2017
. It would be interesting to investigate this, and other connections to partially-massless spinning fields, in more detail.

•

Demanding a vanishing residue for the amplitudes we have studied in this work is equivalent to demanding the absence of logarithmic divergences in boundary correlators. The amplitude has the benefit of being field-redefinition invariant, nevertheless it would be interesting to study the properties of boundary correlators for these exceptional theories in more detail

Appendix A
\Delta=4
Field Redefinition

In the main text we showed that the S-matrix we are considering follows from an LSZ operation on an in-out correlator and is therefore expected to be a field-redefinition invariant object. In this Appendix, we check this invariance at the level of four-point contact diagrams for a scalar with mass
m^{2}=-4H^{2}
as relevant for the DBI scalar, which complements the check we did for a massless scalar in the main body.

If we take the free theory and perform the field redefinition
\phi\rightarrow\phi-\frac{\lambda}{3}\phi^{3}
then the action is

\displaystyle S=\int\sqrt{-g}\biggl[-\frac{1}{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}+2H^{2}\phi^{2}-\frac{4}{3}\lambda H^{2}\phi^{4}+\lambda\phi^{2}(\partial\phi)^{2}\biggr].

(56)

The four-point S-matrix is

\displaystyle\braket{0|S|\vec{k}_{1},\vec{k}_{2},\vec{k}_{3},\vec{k}_{4}}=\sqrt{16k_{1}k_{2}k_{3}k_{4}}\delta^{3}\left(\sum_{a=1}^{4}\vec{k}_{a}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{-\lambda}{H^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\frac{d\eta}{\eta^{2}}\biggl[\partial_{\eta}f_{k_{1}}(\eta)\partial_{\eta}f_{k_{2}}(\eta)+(\vec{k}_{1}\cdot\vec{k}_{2})f_{k_{1}}(\eta)f_{k_{2}}(\eta)

\displaystyle-\frac{4}{3\eta^{2}}f_{k_{1}}(\eta)f_{k_{2}}(\eta)\biggr]f_{k_{3}}(\eta)f_{k_{4}}(\eta)+23\;\text{perms}\,,

(57)

where the mode function is now

\displaystyle f_{k}(\eta)=\frac{He^{-ik\eta}}{\sqrt{2k^{5}}\eta}[k\eta(3+ik\eta)-3i]\,.

(58)

We start by computing the residue at
\eta=0
and find that it vanishes which relies on
both
the tuning between the two quartic interactions in (
56
) and summing over all permutations. For any other tuning between these interactions, the residue is non-zero. To complete the computation of contribution 1 for this amplitude we need to consider the contributions at
k_{T}=0
and again we know that the
p=-1
coefficient is proportional to
k_{T}
so the large arc still yields a vanishing result. Contribution 1 is therefore zero.

Moving to contribution 2, we again have contributions from
p=0,1,2
and can therefore write the amplitude as (
28
) again with

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(0)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=-8\,,

(59)

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{T}}\mathcal{A}^{(1)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=-12\,,

(60)

\displaystyle\partial^{2}_{k_{T}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=-4\,,

(61)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(1)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(62)

\displaystyle\partial_{k_{T}}\mathcal{A}^{(2)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=0\,,

(63)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{(2)}|_{k_{T}=0}

\displaystyle=0\,.

(64)

We note that away from these
k_{T}=0
limits the amplitudes are very different to those of the massless scalar. We therefore see that contribution 2, and therefore the full amplitude, vanishes.

For the massless case the vanishing of the amplitude required a sum over permutations, while for the DBI scalar the vanishing required a sum over permutations and a collaboration between two different interactions that are simultaneously produced by the field redefinition.

The Effect of the COVID-19 Orthopaedic Joint Surgery “Restart” on Patient Outcomes in Michigan
Authors: Harjot Uppal, Simarjeet Puri, Brian Hallstrom, Michael A Masini, Heather Chubb, Thomas Zheng, David C Markel
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe COVID-19 pandemic significantly decreased elective total joint arthroplasty volume in Michigan. Depending on local ordinances, elective procedures were completely shut down for periods. We hypothesized that decreased volume and surgical gaps would impact outcomes once procedures restarted. This study compared outcomes of total hip and knee arthroplasty (TJA) following the COVID-19 pandemic restart.MethodsThis study utilized the Michigan Arthroplasty Registry Collaborative Quality Initiative database to analyze outcomes for TJA compared to the previous uninterrupted year to investigate the effects of the pandemic on TJA outcomes.ResultsAs expected, there was a significant reduction in surgical volume for both total hip arthroplasty and total knee arthroplasty during the initial surge of COVID-19 cases in Michigan. There was a significant increasing shift toward performing TJA at ambulatory surgery centers and hospital outpatient departments. There was a significantly higher discharge home with use of home care services for both hips and knees. There was a slightly elevated rate of deep vein thrombosis among TJA patients within the early fall of 2020, which normalized to prepandemic levels by the fall. No other significant differences were noted.ConclusionsOverall, outcomes were similar after the resumption of TJA following the COVID-19 pandemic. Perhaps this is a test of the overall training of orthopaedic surgeons performing total hip arthroplasty/total knee arthroplasty and the application of well-established pathways. The pandemic likely promulgated the ongoing move for site of service change.Keywords: COVID-19, Total joint arthroplasty, Ambulatory surgery center, Postoperative complications

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic placed pressure on health-care systems and significantly impacted orthopaedic services and surgeons in the United States [1]. To prevent transmission and limit the burden on hospital systems, the Centers for Medicare and Medicaid Services urged limiting elective procedures [2]. In Michigan, elective total joint arthroplasty was prohibited, except in emergency or trauma-related situations, from March 21, 2020, to May 29, 2020 [3]. Following the lifting of the ban, elective total joint arthroplasty cautiously resumed under strict safety protocols due to the heightened risk of transmission and its associated complications [4]. A key component of these safety protocols included proof of a negative SARS-CoV-2 test obtained within 72 h of the planned athroplasty procedure.

Two previous studies from Michigan Arthroplasty Registry Quality Collaborative Initiative (MARCQI) described the shift in site of service for primary TJA from hospitals to hospital outpatient departments and ambulatory surgery centers (ASCs) during and 1 year after the pandemic, as well as the expanding patient populations that are now receiving total joint arthroplasty in hospital outpatient departments and ASCs compared to prepandemic [5,6]. However, there is a paucity of longitudinal studies examining the clinical outcomes of this shutdown. As most surgeons and hospitals halted surgeries during the shutdown, there was concern that operative times and complication rates might increase due to the prolonged hiatus of the operating teams performing these procedures. Abdelaal et al [1] reviewed over 26,000 primary and revision TJAs at a tertiary referral center from the start of the pandemic and compared them to a prepandemic cohort, and Gordon et al al [7] studied over 120,000 elective total knee arthroplasties (TKAs) from the American College of Surgeons natioanl surgical quality improvement program database. Both described significant decreases in case volumes without an increase in complications.

The purpose of this study was to assess the effect of the COVID-19 pandemic and elective case shutdown on surgical volume, use of home care services, 90-day unplanned readmissions, and complications such as infection and venous thromboembolism. While previous studies have limited generalizability and rely on accurate coding of complications or single-institution records, MARCQI data are specifically abstracted by trained nurses. Currently, MARCQI is the largest statewide database in the United States and includes more than 500,000 fully abstracted cases and captures data on 96% of Michigan TJAs [5]. We hypothesize that the shift in practice resulted in decreased case volumes and increased rates of home discharge, readmissions, and complications.

Material and methods

The MARCQI database carries the determination of “not regulated” under Title 45, Part 46 of the Code of Federal Regulations by the University of Michigan’s Institutional Review Board. Within 90 days of surgery, charts are prospectively reviewed and trained clinical nurse abstractors capture data and events. Beyond that time, a prospectively captured statewide database (Michigan Inpatient Database) is utilized to confirm readmissions outside of the operative institution [8]. The MARCQI registry contains data from over 57 hospitals, 6 affiliate hospitals, 9 ASCs, and more than 450 orthopaedic surgeons.

lThe inclusion criteria included primary and conversion total hip arthroplasty (THA), hip resurfacing, primary total, bicompartmental, and unicompartmental knee arthroplasty, as well as patellofemoral replacement. All cases meeting the inclusion criteria from 2019 and 2020 were included in the analysis. Case volumes, discharge disposition, narcotic pain prescriptions, and short-term complications across both years were compared. Short-term complications included 90-day unplanned readmissions, deep vein thromboses (DVTs) or pulmonary embolisms (PEs), and periprosthetic joint infections. The analysis was exploratory and not risk adjusted. Point estimates and 95% confidence intervals were reported.

Results

Surgical volumes were significantly decreased for both THA and TKA in March, April, and May of 2020 as compared to 2019 (Fig. 1). This period marks the initial surge of COVID-19 cases in Michigan. This also coincides with Michigan’s ban on elective procedures, except trauma-related surgeries, which was in place from March 21, 2020, to May 29, 2020 [3]. The volume of THA cases recovered briefly, and even surpassed rates observed in June and July of 2019. The rates of TKA never reached the rates observed in 2019 for any month. A decline in case volume was observed in October and November 2020 compared to 2019, which coincided with another surge of COVID-19 cases in Michigan.

A comparison of primary total hip and knee arthroplasty surgical volumes between 2019 and 2020.

Surgical volume at ASCs fluctuated for THA and TKA in early 2020. For both TKA and THA, there was a significant increase in volume in May 2020 as most ASCs’ opened before hospitals for elective joint arthroplasty. This increase was sustained from June through August (Fig. 2).

Percentage of primary total hip and knee arthroplasty cases performed at MARCQI ASC sites between 2019 and 2020.

The utilization of home care services increased in 2020 secondary to the COVID-19 pandemic. Overall, the discharge rate to home with home care services for THA increased from 91% to 94% between 2019 and 2020 (Fig. 3). Similarly, among TKA patients, the utilization of home care services increased from 93% to 97% between 2019 and 2020. This finding was likely impacted due to the inability to discharge to extended care facilities and patient selection. MARCQI closely monitors the utilization of narcotic pain medicine. Much of the pain management was transferred to physician offices due to the increase in outpatient TJA after the extended period of elective procedure suspension. Despite the change in prescriptive patterns there was no increase in the mean oral morphine equivalents prescribed to patients at discharge on the resumption of either TKA or THA procedures. This was likely affected by the longstanding oral morphine equivalent educational program at MARCQI.

Comparison of discharge rates to home with or without home care services for primary total hip and knee arthroplasty patients between 2019 and 2020.

The incidence of DVT/PE in the MARCQI database was approximately 0.5% THA procedures and slightly higher for TKA procedures at 0.9% prepandemic. During 2020, the incidence of DVT/PE saw a slight uptick from May through November to approximately 0.9%. Similarly, the incidence of DVT/PE for TKA procedures also increased to 1% from May through November (Fig. 4), but the change was not found to be statistically significant with overlapped 95% confidence intervals.

Comparison of Incidence of DVT or PE events following total hip and knee arthroplasty procedures between 2019 and 2020.

The surgical site infection rate within the MARCQI database was 0.7% for THA and 0.4% for TKA prepandemic. The monthly percentage increased to over 5% for THA procedures in April but was not statistically significant (Fig. 5). The rates returned to database norms for the remainder of 2020. The rate remained unchanged for TKA in 2020 as compared to 2019.

Comparison of rates of 90-day surgical site infections for primary hip and knee arthroplasty procedures between 2019 and 2020.

The rate of unplanned readmission for any cause following a TJA increased significantly for THA in April of 2020 compared to the previous year. It is important to note that this metric captures all-cause readmissions, extending beyond perioperative issues to include broad medical causes. Specifically, readmissions rose to 14.7% readmissions compared to the database norm of between 4% and 5% for THA procedures (Fig. 6). The readmission rate for TKA procedures remained unchanged from 2019 to 2020.

Comparison of rates of unplanned readmission for any cause following total hip or knee arthroplasty procedures between 2019 and 2020.

Discussion

The COVID-19 pandemic significantly changed the practice of orthopaedic surgery, particularly total joint arthroplasty. Hospital and operating room resources were redirected, leading to a significant reduction in elective procedures and perhaps a long-term site of service shift to outpatient TJA [9]. As mirrored within our study, we found a trend toward outpatient TJA, with more procedures being performed at ASCs as TJA resumed after the initial COVID-19 pandemic wave.

A notable finding of this analysis was the temporary increase in thromboembolic complications following the resumption of elective arthroplasty. The incidence of DVT/PE for THA nearly doubled baseline rates reported within the MARCQI database. This trend was clinically relevant and was perhaps related to the hypercoagulable state associated with COVID-19 infection and potential residual thrombotic risks. Patients with active COVID-19 infections would have been excluded from this analysis, as all patients undergoing elective procedures were required to undergo COVID-19 testing. That said, based on prevalence, it may be presumed that many patients may have had the infection at some point before or after their TJA procedure. Our findings align with the work by Wenzel et al. [10], who report that patients with even a remote or asymptomatic COVID-19 infection can experience an increased risk of thrombotic complications following major orthopaedic surgery. Other studies have found mixed results, with Hoang et al. and Lee et al. noting an increased risk of perioperative complications following TJA with a recent COVID-19 infection. Whereas Tadepalli et al. noted no increase in perioperative complications with an incidental COVID-19 diagnosis. [[11], [12], [13], [14]] Another consideration for the observed increase in DVT/PE rates is that adherence to established DVT prophylaxis protocols may have been inconsistent following the resumption of elective surgeries, potentially influenced by staff turnover and changes in workflow during the pandemic recovery period.

This analysis also demonstrated a transient increase in surgical site infections for THA in April 2020, with rates exceeding 5%, though this difference did not reach statistical significance it was of some concern. The increase was likely due to many contributing factors, including altered perioperative workflows, staffing shortages, and disruptions to routine infection prevention measures during the early months of the pandemic. Infection rates returned to baseline later in the year, suggesting that as surgical teams stabilized and workflows were reestablished. Other studies have found similar rates of infection among patients undergoing TJA during the COVID-19 pandemic [15].

The sharp rise in unplanned readmissions for THA procedures in April 2020—reaching 14.7% compared to a baseline of 4–5%—is another significant observation. This may be attributed to the backlog of delayed cases, lack of access to primary care clinics, changes in postoperative follow-up patterns, or the management of early postoperative complications in the setting of limited in-person care. We hypothesize that as the workflows adapted over time, the rates returned to normal within the next few months.

Conclusions

The COVID-19 pandemic had a profound impact on total joint arthroplasty in Michigan, resulting in decreased surgical volumes, a shift toward outpatient and ASC-based procedures, and transient increases in certain complications. While overall outcomes remained stable, temporary rises in DVT/PE, infection rates, and THA readmissions highlight the challenges of restoring elective surgical care amid staffing and workflow disruptions. These findings underscore the importance of maintaining consistent adherence to perioperative protocols, and continued monitoring through statewide registries, such as MARCQI, will be vital to assessing long-term impacts and guiding preparedness for future health-care disruptions.

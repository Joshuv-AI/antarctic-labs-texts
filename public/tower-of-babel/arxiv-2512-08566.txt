Realization of relational presheaves
Yorgo Chamoun, Samuel Mimram

Abstract
Relational presheaves generalize traditional presheaves by going to the category of sets and relations (as opposed to sets and functions) and by allowing functors which are lax. This added generality is useful because it intuitively allows one to encode situations where we have representables without boundaries or with multiple boundaries at once. In particular, the relational generalization of precubical sets has natural application to modeling concurrency. In this article, we study categories of relational presheaves, and construct realization functors for those. We begin by observing that they form the category of set-based models of a cartesian theory, which implies in particular that they are locally finitely presentable categories. By using general results from categorical logic, we then show that the realization of such presheaves in a cocomplete category is a model of the theory in the opposite category, which allows characterizing situations in which we have a realization functor. Finally, we explain that our work has applications in the semantics of concurrency theory. The realization namely allows one to compare syntactic constructions on relational presheaves and geometric ones. Thanks to it, we are able to provide a syntactic counterpart of the blowup operation, which was recently introduced by Haucourt on directed geometric semantics, as way of turning a directed space into a manifold.

Presheaves.

The notion of
presheaf

[

Formally, a presheaf is a functor

[

A presheaf
P:\mathcal{G}^{op}\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}}
precisely corresponds to a (directed)
graph
with
P(0)
as set of vertices,
P(1)
as set of edges, the maps
P(s),P(t):P(1)\to P(0)
respectively associating to each edge its source and target. Moreover, if one considers the functor
\mathcal{G}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
sending

to an interval, the image of

and
t
respectively being the inclusion of the point into the endpoints of the interval, the geometric realization functor
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{G})\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
associates to a graph the corresponding topological graph.

Presheaves to relations.

Blowup.

As an application of relational presheaves, we explain here how they can be used to provide a nice description of the operation of

We namely show here that this operation, which canonically turns such a space into a manifold, can be obtained as the geometric realization of a combinatorial blowup construction, turning a precubical set into a relational one. In this way, we obtain a description of the blowup operation in purely combinatorial terms, which is simpler to define and study than the topological one.

Previous work.

The idea of using functors to
{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
as semantics of non-deterministic programs is quite old
[
2
]
. The formal theory of relational presheaves was developed by Rosenthal in connection to nondeterministic automata theory
[
32
,
33
]
and independently by Ghilardi and Meloni in connection to modal logic
[
10
,
11
]
. Relational presheaves have found several applications in computer science, notably for labelled transition systems (see for example
[
35
]
for recent developments). The category of relational presheaves over
\mathcal{C}
has been shown to be equivalent to several well-studied categories. In
[
32
]
, Rosenthal showed that this category is equivalent to the category of categories enriched in the free quantaloid over
\mathcal{C}
. Then, in
[
29
,
30
]
, using an analogue of the Grothendieck construction, Niefield established a correspondence between relational presheaves over
\mathcal{C}
and faithful functors over
\mathcal{C}
, which led to a better understanding of exponentiability in the category of relational presheaves.

Higher dimensional automata (HDA) have been introduced as models for true concurrency
[
13
,
31
]
and bear close relationship with geometric models for concurrency
[
9
]
. The variant of
partial
HDA was studied in
[
6
,
8
]
to model priorities, and constitutes a subclass of relational presheaves, corresponding to allowing partial functions for face maps.
A way of realizing partial precubical sets (in topological spaces) was suggested in
[
6
]
, based on the idea of completing a partial precubical set into an ordinary one.
However, this procedure is not satisfying because it is not cocontinuous (consider for example the pushout square of
[
6
, §4.1]
; in fact we will see that it does not even preserve all colimits of partial presheaves which are colimits in the category of relational presheaves).

Connections between relational presheaves and categorical logic have existed since the introduction of the former.
For example, models of algebraic theories in
{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
where already studied in
[
33
]
, and the internal logic of relational presheaves was studied in
[
12
]
.
However, to the best of our knowledge, the use of categorical logic in order to study relational presheaves, as well as the definition of their realization, and the combinatorial definition of the blowup are original contributions of this paper.

Plan of the paper.

Based on some classical results in categorical logic (
Section

2
), we define relational presheaves and show that they are models of a given theory (
Section

3
). We then define a notion of realization, show that it can also be formulated in terms of models of a theory, and that it generalizes the usual notion of realization (
Section

4
). Some concrete instances of realizations, such as in topological spaces, are then provided (
Section

5
). We briefly introduce variants of relational presheaves and explain their use (
Section

6
). Finally, we show that the blowup construction can be expressed as the realization of one on relational presheaves (
Section

7
).

Preliminaries: some categorical logic

We begin by recalling some classical definitions and results on categorical logic that will be used in the following. We refer to
[
3
]
for details.
Let
\Sigma
be a
multisorted signature
, consisting of a set
\Sigma_{0}
of sorts,
\Sigma_{1}
of function symbols with sorted arities and a set
\Sigma_{R}
of relation symbols with sorted arities.
Recall that a
regular formula
is a first order formula built from atomic formulas using the connectives
\land
and
\exists
.
A
regular formula in context
is a finite tuple of free variables
\mathbf{x}
together with a regular formula
\varphi
with free variables in
\mathbf{x}
, noted
\{\mathbf{x}.\varphi\}
, and considered up to
\alpha
-conversion.
A
regular sequent
is a pair of formulas in context
\varphi
and
\psi
, sharing the same context
\mathbf{x}
, noted
\varphi\vdash_{\mathbf{x}}\psi
.
Its interpretation in full first order logic is
\forall\mathbf{x}.(\varphi\Rightarrow\psi)
.
A
regular theory
is a set of regular sequents, called the axioms of the theory.

Definition 1

A
cartesian formula
with respect to a regular theory
\mathbb{T}
(or
\mathbb{T}
-cartesian formula
) is a regular formula such that every existential quantification
is provably unique with respect to
\mathbb{T}
.
A
cartesian theory
is a regular theory
\mathbb{T}
such that its axioms can be well-ordered in such a way that every axiom is cartesian with respect to the theory consisting of the preceding axioms.

In particular, a theory whose axioms are sequents of formulas built from atomic ones only using
\land
is cartesian.
Now, for every cartesian (i.e. finitely complete) category
\mathcal{C}
and cartesian theory
\mathbb{T}
, we can define a category
\mathbb{T}
-
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Mod}}}}(\mathcal{C})
of
models
of
\mathbb{T}
in
\mathcal{C}
. Informally, a model consists in an interpretation of sorts as objects of
\mathcal{C}
, of function symbols as morphisms, and of relation symbols as subobjects, in a way which respects the axioms.
Writing
\mathrm{Lex}
for the category of cartesian categories and functors which are
left-exact
(i.e. product-preserving),
we have that
\mathbb{T}
-
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Mod}}}}(-)
defines a functor on
\mathrm{Lex}

and
\mathbb{T}
-
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Mod}}}}({\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}})
is the usual category of set-based models of
\mathbb{T}
and homomorphisms between them.
We say that a set-based model
M
of a cartesian theory
T
is
finitely presented
if there is a
\mathbb{T}
-cartesian formula in context
\{\mathbf{x}.\varphi\}
and a tuple
\mathbf{a}\in M
such that
M\models\varphi(\mathbf{a})
and
for every model
N
of
\mathbb{T}
, for every tuple
\mathbf{b}\in N
such that
N\models\varphi(\mathbf{b})
,
there is a unique homomorphism
M\to N
sending
\mathbf{a}
to
\mathbf{b}
.

Proposition 1

Let
\mathbb{T}
be a cartesian theory. There is a cartesian category
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
, called the
cartesian syntactic category
of
\mathbb{T}
, such that there is an equivalence

\mathbb{T}\text{-}{\mathop{\textnormal{{Mod}}}}(\mathcal{C})\cong\mathrm{Lex}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}},\mathcal{D})

for every cartesian category
\mathcal{D}
, natural in
\mathcal{D}
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}^{op}
is equivalent to the category of finitely presented set-based models of
\mathbb{T}
.

For a detailed study of syntactic categories, see
[
20
, §D1.4]
.
We mention that
\mathcal{C}_{\mathbb{T}}
is built in the following way:
its objects are formulas in context which are cartesian with respect to
\mathbb{T}
,
and its morphisms are
\mathbb{T}
-provably functional cartesian formulas between them
(more precisely, equivalence classes of such with respect to
\mathbb{T}
-provable equivalence).
Cartesian theories correspond to essentially algebraic theories, for which similar theorems are established in
[
1
]
.
Finally, we mention that every cartesian category is the syntactic category of some cartesian theory
[
3
, Theorem 1.4.13]
, so that cartesian theories can actually be defined as cartesian categories.

3
Relational presheaves as models of a cartesian theory

We now introduce relational presheaves and show that they form the category of models of a particular cartesian theory.
We write
{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
for the category whose objects are sets and whose morphisms are relations.
We suppose fixed a small category
\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 2

A
relational presheaf

P
over
\mathcal{C}
is a lax functor
\mathcal{C}^{op}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
.
A
morphism

\alpha:P\Rightarrow Q
of relational presheaves is an oplax natural transformation.
We write
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
for the corresponding category.

A relational presheaf
P
amounts to the data of a family of sets
P(c)
indexed by objects
c\in\mathcal{C}
and a family of relations
P(f)
indexed by morphisms
f
, with
P(f)\subseteq P(c)\times P(d)
for
f:d\to c
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{id}_{P(c)}

\displaystyle\subseteq P(\operatorname{id}_{c})

\displaystyle P(g)\circ P(f)

\displaystyle\subseteq P(f\circ g)

(1)

for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
and every composable morphisms
f
and
g
. We write
a\to_{f}b
for
(a,b)\in P(f)
.
The first condition states that
P(\operatorname{id}_{c})
is reflexive for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
and the second condition that if
a\to_{f}b
and
b\to_{g}c
then
a\to_{g\circ f}c
.
Similarly, a morphism
\alpha:P\Rightarrow Q
amounts to a family of functions
(\alpha_{c}:P(c)\to Q(c))_{c\in\mathcal{C}}
such that
a\to_{f}b
implies
\alpha_{c}(a)\to_{f}\alpha_{d}(b)
for every
f:d\to c
.

Definition 3

We write
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
for the signature whose sorts are the objects of
\mathcal{C}
, with a relation symbol
R_{f}\subseteq c\times d
for every morphism
f:d\to c
, and no function symbol.
We write
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
for the theory with

1.

for every object
c\in\mathcal{C}
, an axiom

x=y\vdash_{x:c,y:c}R_{\operatorname{id}_{c}}(x,y)

2.

for all morphisms
f:d\to c
,
g:e\to d
of
\mathcal{C}
, an axiom

R_{f}(x,y)\and R_{g}(y,z)\vdash_{x:c,y:d,z:e}R_{g\circ f}(x,z)

Proposition 2

{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
is the category of set-based models of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
.

Proof

A model
P
amounts to the data of a set
P(c)
for each sort
c
and a relation
f\subseteq P(c)\times P(d)
for every relation
R_{f}
with
f:c\to d
, and the two families of axioms ensure that the inclusions of (
1
) are satisfied. This is thus precisely a relational presheaf in the sense of
Definition

2
. Similarly, model homomorphisms correspond to oplax natural transformations.∎

Corollary 1

For any category
\mathcal{C}
,
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
is locally finitely presentable. Hence, it is complete and cocomplete.

for the cartesian syntactic category of

and

Every object

Example 1

Consider the category

4
Realizing relational presheaves

A
realization
of a category
\mathcal{C}
in a cocomplete category
\mathcal{D}
is a functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
which is cocontinuous, i.e. colimit-preserving.
The fact that such a functor preserves colimits can be understood as the fact that the image of a “complex” element can be obtained by gluing the images of “simpler” elements.
We write
\mathrm{coCont}(\mathcal{C},\mathcal{D})
for the corresponding category. Typically, for a presheaf category
\mathcal{C}={\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{B})
, one can define a realization
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{B})\to\mathcal{D}
by starting from a functor
F:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{D}
and taking its left Kan extension
F_{!}
along the Yoneda embedding
\text{\char 72\relax}:\mathcal{B}\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{B})

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{D}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{B})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
H
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Rightarrow}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F_{!}}

More explicitly, the realization can be computed on a presheaf
P\in{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{B})
as the colimit
F_{!}(P)=\operatorname{colim}_{(b,p)\in\int P}F(b)
, where
\int P
denotes the category of elements of
P

[
26
]
.
The original notion of geometric realization was essentially defined in this way with
\mathcal{B}
being the simplicial category and
\mathcal{D}
the category of topological spaces. Given a small category
\mathcal{C}
and a cocomplete category
\mathcal{D}
, we explain how to construct a realization functor for the category
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
of relational presheaves and show that the situation is essentially analogous to the case of presheaves. We begin by observing that constructing a realization amounts to constructing a model:

The category of realizations of
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})

\mathrm{Lex}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}},\mathcal{D}^{op})

\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}

We have that relational presheaves form the Ind-completion (whose definition is recalled below) of

\displaystyle{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\cong\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}\text{-}{\mathop{\textnormal{{Mod}}}}({\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}})

\displaystyle\cong\mathrm{Lex}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}},{\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}})

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Ind}(\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{op})

\mathcal{D}

Remark 2

One could also consider, instead of relational presheaves, the category
\mathrm{Pseudo}(\mathcal{C}^{op},\operatorname{Span}({\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}}))
of pseudofunctors from
\mathcal{C}^{op}
to the category whose objects are sets and whose morphisms are spans of functions: namely spans of sets can be seen as a “quantitative” variant of relations, where two elements have a set of relations between them (instead of simply being in relation or not).
It is proven in
[
19
,
30
]
that, under some condition (called CFI in
[
19
]
and IG in
[
30
]
),
this is a Grothendieck topos, whose site can be obtained by putting a (non-trivial) Grothendieck topology on the twisted arrows category
\mathcal{C}_{tw}
of
\mathcal{C}
.
The idea is similar to what we just presented: going from
\mathcal{C}
to
\mathcal{C}_{tw}
essentially replaces every morphism of
\mathcal{C}
by a “formal” span.
But
\mathcal{C}_{tw}
additionaly contains coherence data for composition of morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
.
The twisted arrow category approach can also be used to realize relational (more precisely, span-valued) presheaves by Kan extension.
However, in the cases that interest us, mainly those related to precubical sets,
the condition IG is not satisfied,
and in fact taking pseudofunctors instead of lax functors would significantly reduce the situations which we would be able to capture,
as argued in the introduction.

We end this section by explaining the relationship between presheaves and relational ones on a fixed category
\mathcal{C}
, and the relationship between their realizations.
There is a comparison functor
U:{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
, induced by post-composition with the canonical functor
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
sending a function to the corresponding functional relation. Since both categories are locally presentable (
Corollary

1
), we can use the special adjoint functor theorem to construct adjoints, see
Appendix

0.B
for a full proof.

Theorem 4.1

The comparison functor
U:{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
is full and faithful and admits right and left adjoint.

We deduce that
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
is a full subcategory of
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
which is reflective and coreflective, hence closed under limits and colimits. In particular:

Theorem 4.2

Every realization
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{D}
induces, by precomposition with
U
, a realization
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{D}
.

For instance, it is not difficult to see that the geometric realization of relational graphs induces the usual geometric realization of graphs.
Again, the more general case of precubical sets is treated below.

Definition 9

A morphism of relational precubical sets
\alpha:P\to Q
is a
local embedding
if
a\to_{f}c
and
b\to_{f}c
implies
\alpha(a)\neq\alpha(b)
for cubes
a,\,b
and
c
of
P
.

Euclidean bricks are typically precubical sets whose geometric realization is isomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
and the realization of a local embedding is a local embedding.
Note that
I_{k,m}
has a unique cube of dimension
k
, and so does any euclidean brick
B
: we write
\min(B)
for this cube, since all other cubes of
B
have dimension
>k
.

Definition 10

Let
P
be a precubical set of maximum dimension
n
.
The
blowup
of
P
is the relational precubical set
\tilde{P}
given by

\displaystyle\tilde{P}(k)=\{S\subseteq P\,|\,

\displaystyle\exists\alpha:B\to S\text{ surjective local embedding,}

\displaystyle\text{for some $(n,k)$-euclidean brick }B\}

\displaystyle\tilde{P}(f)=\{(S,S^{\prime})\,|\,

\displaystyle\exists\alpha:B\to S,\,\exists\alpha^{\prime}:B^{\prime}\to S^{\prime}\text{as above, }\exists\iota:B\to B^{\prime}\text{ monic}

\displaystyle\text{such that }\alpha(\min(B))\to_{f}\alpha^{\prime}(\min(B^{\prime}))\text{ and }\alpha^{\prime}\circ\iota=\alpha\}

where ‘
\subseteq
’ means ‘subobject of’. The
blowup map

\beta_{P}:\tilde{P}\to P
is defined by
\beta_{P}(S):=\alpha(\min(B))
for
\alpha:B\to S
as above.

This corresponds to the combinatorial blowup of
[
4
]
.
More precisely, the combinatorial blowup of a precubical set
P
is expressed there as a pointwise subset of a presheaf
\mathsf{Comb}_{P}:(\int P)^{op}\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}}
on the opposite of the category of elements
\int P
of
P
. And this corresponds to a relational presheaf, because we have an equivalence between this category of presheaves and a subcategory of relational presheaves over
P
, which generalizes the classical equivalence between presheaves and discrete fibrations
[
24
, Theorem 2.1.2]
. Namely, define the category
{\mathop{\textnormal{{DFib}}}}(P)
of
discrete fibrations
over
P
as the full subcategory of the slice category
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})/P
whose objects have the unique right lifting property with respect to the inclusions
\text{\char 72\relax}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{op}}(\{(y:d).\top\})\hookrightarrow\text{\char 72\relax}_{\tilde{\mathcal{C}}^{op}}(\{(x:c,y:d).R_{f}(x,y)\})
. We then have,

Theorem 7.1

Let
P
be a relational presheaf over a category
\mathcal{C}
. There is an equivalence of categories
\varphi:{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}((\int P)^{op})\cong{\mathop{\textnormal{{DFib}}}}(P)
.

Transforming
\mathsf{Comb}_{P}
into a relational presheaf over
P
by
\varphi
and taking the relational precubical subset corresponding to the combinatorial blowup, we get
\tilde{P}
.

Now, given
F\in{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}((\int P)^{op})
, there is a procedure defined in
[
4
]
which can be described as a geometric realization.
First, we transform
F
into a sheaf on
|P|_{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
, using the fact that this space has a basis consisting of open sets “centered” at a cube of minimal dimension.
Then, we use the correspondence between sheaves on a space and local homeomorphisms over this space
[
27
, §II.6]
to get a space over
|P|_{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
.
Writing
|F|
for this space, we can prove the following.

Theorem 7.2

Let
P
be a relational precubical set,
F\in{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}((\int P)^{op})
.
Then we have
|F|\cong|\varphi(F)|_{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
as spaces over
|P|_{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
.

The idea is to first establish a set-theoretic bijection over
|P|_{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Top}}}}}
. Since both bundles are local homeomorphisms, we directly get that this bijection is in fact a homeomorphism.
Finally, we can prove the desired theorem.

Theorem 7.3

Let
P
be a precubical set of maximum dimension
n
.
The geometric realization of the blowup
\tilde{P}
of
P
is the underlying space of the blowup
\tilde{X}
of the locally ordered realization
X
of
P
.

Proof

First, we need to complete
\tilde{P}
in order to get a discrete fibration
\tilde{P}^{+}
over
P
.
We set
\tilde{P}^{+}(k):=\tilde{P}(k)\sqcup P(k)
(the elements coming from
P(k)
correspond to the elements
\bot
of
\mathsf{Comb}_{P}
), and
(a,b)\in\tilde{P}^{+}(f)
if and only if
(a,b)\in\tilde{P}(f)
or
(a,\beta_{P}(b))\in P(f)
and there are no
a^{\prime}
such that
(a^{\prime},b)\in\tilde{P}(f)
.
Now, by
Theorem

7.1
, we can transform
\tilde{P}^{+}
into a presheaf
\varphi^{-1}(\tilde{P}^{+})
on
(\int P)^{op}
.
Simple but tedious calculations (see
Lemmas

7
and
8
and the discussion before them) give
\varphi^{-1}(\tilde{P}^{+})=\mathsf{Comb}_{P}
.
In
[
4
, Theorem 5.17]
, it is proven that the underlying space of the blowup
\tilde{X}
is a well-characterized subspace of
|\varphi^{-1}(\tilde{P}^{+})|
, corresponding to the elements coming from
\tilde{P}
.

Theorem

7.2
concludes.
∎

8
Conclusion

This paper is a first exploration of relational presheaves in concurrency theory from the point of view of their realizations.
Since the category of relational presheaves on a fixed category is well-behaved (finitely locally presentable),
it is a good setting to categorically and homotopically study some objects of interest, especially (higher dimensional) automata,
which will be the subject of a future work.
In particular, relational automata seem to be a good alternative to automata with
\varepsilon
-transitions
[
34
, §1.1.4]
. This could be for instance exploited in proving Kleene-like theorems (for example
[
7
]
).
The simple combinatorial expression of the blowup also suggests to study directly this construction in relational precubical sets,
and a homotopical (in the sense of Quillen) definition of euclidean relational precubical sets seems plausible.
Finally, on an abstract level, it would be interesting to develop furthur the theory of relational presheaves,
for example by adding a Grothendieck topology on the base category and trying to define relational
sheaves
.

Variants of relational presheaves

We write
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Par}}}}

There is a canonical functor
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Par}}}}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}

{\mathop{\textnormal{{Par}}}}

{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}

A

\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
.

Definition 11

We introduce the following variants of relational presheaves.

(i)

A
relational family

P
over
\mathcal{C}

\mathcal{C}^{op}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
.
We use the same notation as for relational presheaves.

(ii)

A

Note that a partial presheaf

is exactly a relational presheaves such that

is a partial function for all morphisms

\mathcal{C}
.

Definition 12

We have the following categories associated to the previous variants.

(i)

A
morphism

(ii)

(iii)

The full subcategory of
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})

Lemma 3

The inclusion functor
U^{\prime}:{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\hookrightarrow{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelFam}}}}(\mathcal{C})
admits a left adjoint.

Proof

Let
M
be a
\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
-structure,
N
a model of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
.
We build a model
M_{\mathrm{comp}}
of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
by induction:

1.

M_{0}
is the structure having same values as
M
on sorts and non-identity morphisms, and with
M_{0}(\operatorname{id}_{c}):=\Delta_{M(c)}\sqcup M(\operatorname{id}_{c})
for every object
c
;

2.

given
M_{n}
,
M_{n+1}
is the structure whose value on sorts is the same as
M_{n}
, and such that

M_{n+1}(R_{f}):=\bigcup_{f=g\circ h}M_{n}(R_{g})\times_{M_{n}({\operatorname{dom}}(g))}M_{n}(R_{h})

(as subobjects of
M_{n}({\operatorname{dom}}(f))\times M_{n}({\operatorname{cod}}(f))
);

3.

M_{\mathrm{comp}}=\operatorname{colim}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}M_{n}
.

To see that
M_{\mathrm{comp}}
is a model of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
,
consider a situation
f=g\circ h
in
\mathcal{C}
,
x\to_{g}y
and
y\to_{h}z
in
M_{\mathrm{comp}}
.
Then there is some
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that
x\to_{g}y
and
y\to_{h}z
in
M_{n}
.
But then
x\to_{g\circ h}z
in
M_{n+1}
by definition, so it is true in
M_{\mathrm{comp}}
.
Now we prove by induction that
\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M,N)=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M_{n},N)
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
It is true for
n=0
. Suppose it is true for some
n
.
Take
\alpha:M_{n}\to N
,
f=g\circ h
,
x\to_{g}y
,
y\to_{h}z
in
M_{n}
.
Then
\alpha(x)\to_{g}\alpha(y)
and
\alpha(y)\to_{h}\alpha(z)
,
but then
\alpha(x)\to_{g\circ h}\alpha(z)
because

is a model of
\mathbb{T}_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
.
This concludes the induction.
Now by a similar argument, functoriality of this construction is clear (the action on morphisms is the identity).
This finishes the proof because

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M_{\mathrm{comp}},N)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M_{n},N)

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M,N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{\Sigma_{\mathcal{C}}^{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}}(M,N)

∎

With the notation of the previous proof,

of

.
Note that the inclusion is full and faithful, so the counit of the adjunction is an isomorphism by Theorem IV.3.1 of

]

i.e. if we close a relational presheaf under composition, we get the same relational presheaf.
This is also obvious by construction.

Presheaves as relational presheaves

First we give a proof of
Theorem

4.1

Proposition 9

U
is full and faithful.

Proof

Faithfulness follows immediately from the fact that the inclusion functor
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Set}}}}\to{{\mathop{\textnormal{{Rel}}}}}
is faithful.
Consider a morphism
\alpha:U(P)\to U(Q)
: it consists in a family of functions
\alpha_{c}:P(c)\to Q(c)
indexed by objects
c\in\mathcal{C}
which preserve the relations.
So for all
f:d\to c
in
\mathcal{C}
,
x\in P(c)
,
y\in P(d)

\displaystyle y=x.f

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow(x,y)\in U(P)(f)

\displaystyle\Rightarrow(\alpha_{d}(x),\alpha_{c}(y))\in U(Q)(f)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\alpha_{c}(y)=\alpha_{d}(x).f

where
x.f
is a notation of
P(f)(x)
.
Therefore,
\alpha_{c}(x.f)=\alpha_{c}(x).f
for all

, which proves fullness.
∎

Proposition 10

U
has a right adjoint.

Proof

By the special adjoint functor theorem (
[
26
, Corollary §V.8]
and
[
1

),
it is enough to show that

preserves colimits, which we do by computing explicitly colimits in relational presheaves and observing that they coincide with ones in presheaves. Since coproducts and coequalizers generate all colimits, it is enough to handle those two particular cases.

For coproducts, consider a family
(P_{i})_{i\in I}
of presheaves indexed by a set
I
.
We define a relational presheaf
P
by setting
P(c)=\bigsqcup_{i\in I}P_{i}(c)
for every
c\in\mathcal{C}
and

P(f)=\{(x,y)\,|\,\exists i\in I,\,x\in P_{i}(c),\,y\in P_{i}(d),\,(x,y)\in P_{i}(f)\}

for all
f:c\to d
.
It is straightforward to check that this is indeed the coproduct in
{\mathop{\textnormal{{RelPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
,
and that it coincides with the usual coprroduct in
{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
.

For coequalizers, consider a diagram
P\rightrightarrows Q
, with morphisms
\alpha
and
\beta
.
Now define a relational presheaf
R
by setting

R(c)
to be the quotient of
Q(c)
be the equivalence relation generated by
\alpha(x)\sim\beta(x)
for all
x\in P(c)
, for all
c
,
and
[x]\to_{f}[y]
if there are
x^{\prime}\sim x
and
y^{\prime}\sim y
such that
x^{\prime}\to_{f}y^{\prime}
.
However, this does not necessarily define a relational presheaf, so we need moreover to close relations under composition (see
Lemma

3
).
Now it is easy to check that
R
is the coequalizer of the diagram,
and that it is the usual coequalizer for presheaves
(we do not need to close under composition in this case).
∎

Remark 3

Since we do not need to close under composition for presheaves in the previous proof,

{\mathop{\textnormal{{Psh}}}}(\mathcal{C})\to{\mathop{\textnormal{{ParPsh}}}}(\mathcal{C})
has right and left adjoints,
which are the restrictions of the ones defined above.
We can also prove that the inclusion

has a left adjoint.
Indeed, it is accessible by functoriality of the point construction (see
[
18
, §2.1]
),
and it is easy to see that it preserves limits,
i.e. that the additional axiom is still valid when we take the limit in relational presheaves,
because this axiom can itself be expressed using limits,
and limits are computed pointwise.
Concretely, it is similar to the left adjoint defined in Remark

However, it does not have a right adjoint, since it does not preserve pushouts (e.g. the pushout square of
[

This means that a realization of relational presheaves does not induce a realization of partial presheaves.

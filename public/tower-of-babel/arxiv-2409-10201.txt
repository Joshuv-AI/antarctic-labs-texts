Backbone coloring for graphs with degree 4 4
Krzysztof Turowski, Krzysztof Michalik

Abstract
The λ \lambda -backbone coloring of the graph G G with backbone H H is a graph-coloring problem in which we are given a graph G G and a subgraph H H , and we want to assign colors to vertices in such a way that the endpoints of every edge from G G have different colors, and the endpoints of every edge from H H are assigned colors which differ by at least λ \lambda . In this paper we pursue research on backbone coloring of bounded-degree graphs with well-known classes of backbones. Our result is an almost complete classification of the complexity of deciding whether B ​ B ​ C λ ​ ( G , H ) ≤ k BBC_{\lambda}(G,H)\leq k for graphs with maximum degree at most 4 4 and matching, galaxy, Hamiltonian path, and spanning tree backbones. We also determine the effect of allowing isolated vertices in matching and galaxy backbones.



1
Introduction

\lambda
-backbone coloring is a problem in a vast family of approaches to graph coloring. It is motivated by a particular approach to the frequency assignment problem in radio networks, often represented by graphs with transmitters modelled as vertices.
There exists a way to model restrictions that a coloring imposes on the allowed level of interference between the transmitters: vertices in close proximity cannot have the same frequency. Additionally, the backbone requirements are understood as follows: there exists also a specific subset of pairs of vertices that require a wider separation bandwidth between the signals they are emitting, e.g. because they fulfill more critical roles in the network. Of course, our aim is to minimize the total bandwidth used by the whole radio network
[
8
]
.

We use standard graph terminology as in
[
7
]
. Graphs are finite, simple and undirected, unless stated otherwise. We write
uv
for the edge with endpoints
u
and
v
, reserving braces for sets. A subgraph
H
of
G
is
spanning
if
V(H)=V(G)
. For a vertex set
X
,
G\setminus X
denotes vertex deletion; for a subgraph
H
,
G\setminus H
denotes the graph
(V(G),E(G)\setminus E(H))
.

Formally, backbone coloring is defined as follows:

Definition 1

(Broersma et al.,
[
8
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph and
H
be a subgraph of
G
. Let also
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
\lambda\geq 2
.
The
\lambda
-backbone coloring
of
G
with backbone
H
is defined as a function
c\colon V(G)\to\mathbb{N}_{+}
such that

•

c(u)\neq c(v)
for every
uv\in E(G)
,

•

|c(u)-c(v)|\geq\lambda
for every
uv\in E(H)
.

A
\lambda
-backbone
k
-coloring takes values in
\{1,\ldots,k\}
; not every color need be used. Adding the vertices of
V(G)\setminus V(H)
to
H
as isolated vertices does not change the coloring constraints. Thus, where convenient, we regard a backbone as spanning without restricting the definition.

The key difference between this problem and its ancestor – classical vertex coloring – is obviously an introduction of the backbone. This entails certain properties absent in the original problem: the latter is oblivious to the color ordering, and we can permute them in any order whenever necessary. However, in the backbone coloring problem the ordering of the colors matters, and it can be the case that even in the optimal coloring
c
some colors between
1
and
\max_{v\in V(G)}c(v)
may not be used at all.
Thus, the
\lambda
-backbone chromatic number
for a graph

with backbone
H
(denoted as
BBC_{\lambda}(G,H)
) is defined accordingly as the smallest

for which there exists a
\lambda
-backbone
k
-coloring of
G
with backbone
H
.

2.1
The easy cases

We start with an almost warm-up exercise by obtaining the following result for both matchings and
1

Theorem 9
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\Delta(G)\leq 4
and
\lambda\geq 2
. Then:

(a)

if

is a matching in
G

BBC_{\lambda}(G,M)\leq 2\lambda+1
,

(b)

if
H
is a
1
-degenerate subgraph of

BBC_{\lambda}(G,H)\leq 2\lambda+2
.

The respective colorings can be found in
O(n^{2})
time in
(a)
and
O(n)
time in
(b)
.

Proof.

First, we infer from
Theorems

5
and
6
that for
\Delta(G)\leq 4
it holds that
BBC_{2}(G,H)\leq 6
for every
1
-degenerate backbone

and
BBC_{2}(G,M)\leq 5
for matching backbones.

However, one can go further and for a general
\lambda
proceed in the following way: first obtain a
2
-backbone coloring
c^{\prime}
of
G
with backbone
H
and then for all
v\in V(G)
define

\displaystyle c(v)=(\lambda-2)\left\lfloor\frac{c^{\prime}(v)-1}{2}\right\rfloor+c^{\prime}(v).

It is sufficient to spot that such a coloring indeed maps colors
\{1,2,3,4,5,6\}
to
\{1,2,\lambda+1,\lambda+2,2\lambda+1,2\lambda+2\}
exactly in this order so that all the non-backbone edge requirements are trivially preserved between
c^{\prime}
and

.
Writing
f(i)=(\lambda-2)\lfloor(i-1)/2\rfloor+i
, we have
f(i+2)-f(i)=\lambda
and
f
is strictly increasing. Hence
|c^{\prime}(u)-c^{\prime}(v)|\geq 2
implies
|c(u)-c(v)|\geq\lambda
for every edge

.

The running times follow directly from the respective complexities for finding the colorings from
Theorems

5
and
6
, as the transformation described above runs in linear time.
∎

Theorem 11
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\Delta(G)\leq 4
and
M
be a matching in

Then for
\lambda\geq 3

Proof.

It suffices to color each connected component separately. From

In the first case, write the odd cycle as

In the second case, extend the clique to

In the remaining case, obtain a proper

4.1
The easy case

Let us recall the following theorem, posed as one of the conjectures by none other than Paul Erdős in 1990 and originally by Issai Schur in number-theoretic terms
[
10
]
:

Theorem 21

(
[
11
]
)
.

Let

be a positive integer, and let

be a loopless
4
-regular multigraph on
3n
vertices. Assume that

has a decomposition into a Hamiltonian circuit and
n
pairwise vertex-disjoint triangles. Then
\chi(G)=3
.

Surprisingly, this theorem helps us to prove the main theorem of this section:

Theorem 22
.

Let
G
be a graph with
\Delta(G)\leq 4
and let
P
be a Hamiltonian path in
G
.
Then for
\lambda\geq 2
it holds that

. A suitable coloring can be found in
\mathcal{O}^{\text{*}}\!(1.3217^{n})
time.

Proof.

First, let us number the vertices

, …,
v_{n}
according to their order of appearance in

. Let also
A
and

denote the sets of vertices with odd and even indices, respectively. Our whole idea is to find a coloring

of

such that the vertices from

get colors
\{1,2,3\}
and the vertices from
B
get
\{\lambda+2,\lambda+3,\lambda+4\}
so that all the backbone edge constraints are satisfied. It suffices to ensure that
C_{3}\cup C_{\lambda+2}
(recall from the previous section that
C_{i}
is a set of vertices colored with

G
and that non-backbone edges in
G\setminus(C_{3}\cup C_{\lambda+2})
form a bipartite graph.

Let us see first that the theorem holds for
n\leq 2
.
When
n\geq 3
, then we have three possible cases: either
(a)

v_{1}
and
v_{n}
are adjacent in
G\setminus P
, or
(b)
they are not adjacent, but they are in the same connected component of
G\setminus P
or
(c)
they belong to different connected components of
G\setminus P

and a set of vertices

separately for each case:

case
(a)
:

let us denote by

a Hamiltonian cycle consisting of

together with an edge

and let

.
Note that the resulting coloring would not take into account this additional edge as a backbone one – it is just needed to map the problem more easily into a known one.

case
(b)
:

let
X
be a set of internal vertices (that is, excluding the endpoints
v_{1}
and
v_{n}
) on any path between
v_{1}
and
v_{n}
in
G\setminus P
. Let also
P^{\prime}=P\setminus X
.

case
(c)
:

observe that
\deg_{G\setminus P}(v_{1})\leq 3
,
\deg_{G\setminus P}(v_{n})\leq 3
, and
\deg_{G\setminus P}(u)\leq 2
for all other vertices
u
.
Thus, each component containing
v_{1}
or
v_{n}
consists of at most three paths meeting at that endpoint, or of a cycle through it and at most one attached path. Let
X
contain all vertices other than
v_{1},v_{n}
on the maximal paths from these endpoints to degree-one vertices in
G\setminus P
, excluding vertices of any cycle through an endpoint. Again, let
P^{\prime}=P\setminus X
.

In any case, we know that

is either a Hamiltonian cycle or a spanning collection of paths in

. Moreover, by construction,

,
\deg_{G^{\prime}\setminus P^{\prime}}(v_{n})\leq 2
, and

for any other vertex

so

.

Thus,

is a collection of paths and cycles.
If there are no odd cycles, take

and skip the following auxiliary construction. Otherwise, for every odd cycle

in

let us pick three arbitrary vertices

,

,

with the only condition that if

contains either

or

, then they have to be among those vertices.

Write

. If

is already a Hamiltonian cycle, set

. Otherwise,

is a collection of paths (including isolated vertices); concatenate these paths in any order and close them into a Hamiltonian cycle

on

. This is possible since

, and preserves every edge of

.
Obtain

from

By permuting colors, assume that if

. Otherwise, make color

absent from

and

Put

induces the backbone

In case

Theorem 26

Let

and

Proof.

The one-vertex case is immediate. Otherwise, let

be the bipartition of

and put

. Every vertex has a tree neighbor in the opposite part, so
\deg_{F}(v)\leq 4-\deg_{T}(v)\leq 3
. We will find a proper coloring

such that every vertex of color
4
is a leaf of

whose tree neighbor has color at most
2

r(u)+r(v)\leq 6
for every

. Then set

\lambda+6-r(v),&v\in B.\end{cases}

The palettes

and
\{\lambda+2,\ldots,\lambda+5\}
are disjoint for

. Thus all edges of

are properly colored, and for a tree edge

and

It remains to construct

. Since
\Delta(F)\leq 3
, every copy of
K_{4}
in

is a connected component. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the set of these components and put

. Each vertex
x
of such a clique has
\deg_{F}(x)=3
and hence
\deg_{T}(x)=1
. Denote its unique tree neighbor by

. This neighbor has tree degree at least
2
: otherwise

would be an entire component of the connected tree

For each

, we will select one vertex

to receive color
4
, and color its other vertices with

. Let

. To color

\{1,2,3\},&v\notin Z.\end{cases}

These lists satisfy

. We will choose the vertices

so that

can be colored from these lists.

Recall that a connected graph is a

if every block is a clique or an odd cycle. By the degree-list-coloring theorem of Borodin–Erdős–Rubin–Taylor, a connected graph that is not a Gallai tree can be colored from any lists of size at least the respective vertex degrees, in linear time
[
1
, Theorem 6.1]
. Also, in any connected graph with these list sizes, one strict inequality suffices: root a spanning tree at such a vertex and color greedily in reverse tree order. Every other vertex has an uncolored parent, and the root has a spare color.

Consequently, only Gallai-tree components
Q
of
R
with minimum degree at least
2
require care, since a vertex of degree at most
1
always has a strict list-size inequality. Put

D_{Q}=\{v\in V(Q):\deg_{Q}(v)=2\},\qquad t_{Q}=|D_{Q}|.

We have
t_{Q}\geq 3
. Indeed, if
Q
has one block, it is an odd cycle, since
K_{4}
components were removed. Otherwise, each of its at least two leaf blocks is an odd cycle: an edge would give a vertex of degree
1
, and a
K_{4}
attached to another block would give a vertex of degree at least
4
. Each such leaf block contributes at least two degree-
2
vertices. It suffices to leave one vertex of

outside

, so that its list has size
3>\deg_{Q}(v)
.

Build a bipartite multigraph
J
whose parts correspond to
\mathcal{K}
and the components of
R
. For each
x\in V(K)
, add an edge from
K
to the component containing
p(x)
, labeled by
(x,p(x))
. Every left vertex has degree
4
. For a component
Q
requiring care, every incident edge uses a parent
p\in D_{Q}
with
\deg_{T}(p)=2
. Such a parent has at most one tree neighbor in the removed cliques: if both neighbors were clique vertices, they would be leaves, and together with

would form an entire component of

on three vertices. This contradicts connectedness and the presence of a

.

The edges of

can be colored red and blue in linear time so that every left vertex has two red edges and every right vertex

has at most
\lceil\deg_{J}(Q)/2\rceil

Each circuit has even length, since the multigraph is bipartite, and every vertex has equally many edges of both colors. Deleting the auxiliary edges gives the required bounds.

Choose one red edge at every left vertex

and let its label determine

. For every component

requiring care, this gives

because

from its lists and complete each removed

The bipartition, components, and block decompositions are found in linear time. The multigraph

has four edges per removed clique, so the Euler circuits and selections also take linear time. The list-coloring procedures take linear total time, and converting

.

6
Conclusion

In this article we filled several gaps in the knowledge of the complexity status of backbone coloring for graphs with maximum degree at most
4
. The classification in
Table

1
is complete for matching and galaxy backbones, both with and without isolated backbone vertices. For tree backbones, the upper bound
\lambda+5
from
Theorem

26
is sharp for
\lambda\geq 4
. Only the decision cases
BBC_{2}(G,P)\leq 5
and
BBC_{3}(G,T)\leq 7
remain open.
We conjecture that there might be a simple formula describing the complexity status of the
\lambda
-backbone coloring problem for fixed-degree graphs:

Conjecture 1
.

Let
\lambda\geq 2
. Let also
G
be a graph with a maximum degree
\Delta(G)\leq 4
. Then for any
l\geq\lambda+2
(when the backbone
H
is a matching or a galaxy) or
l\geq\lambda+3
(when the backbone
H
is a Hamiltonian path or a spanning tree) the problem
BBC_{\lambda}(G,H)\leq l
is either trivial (that is, the answer is
YES
for all graphs) or
\mathcal{NP}
-complete.

Of course, one can also generalize the above conjecture for graphs with any fixed maximum degree.

The conjecture leaves open which alternative holds in the unsettled cases. We expect
BBC_{2}(G,P)\leq 5
to be
\mathcal{NP}
-complete, as its counterparts for larger
\lambda
are.
For trees the status does depend on
\lambda
:
BBC_{\lambda}(G,T)\leq\lambda+4
is trivial for
\lambda=2
and
\mathcal{NP}
-complete for
\lambda\geq 4
, while
Theorem

26
gives a linear-time construction with
\lambda+5
colors for
\lambda\geq 3
. There remains the question whether the upper bound for
\lambda=3
is best possible; we believe that it can still be improved:

Conjecture 2
.

There is no pair consisting of a graph
G
with maximum degree at most
4
and a spanning tree
T
such that
BBC_{3}(G,T)=8
.

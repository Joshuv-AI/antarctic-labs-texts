Robust overlapping covert communication against partialband interference via chaotic multi-tone spread spectrum
Authors: Shengnan Guo, Dongmei Liu, Yongqing Fu
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe article presents an overlapping covert communication scheme based on the dynamic adjustment of the spread spectrum factor within a predefined range under a chaotic multi-tone framework, aiming to mitigate partialband interference. In this approach, legitimate communication signals are exploited as masking signals, whereas covert signals are modeled as parasitic components. Covert transmission is theoretically achieved by establishing a signal-to-noise ratio (SNR) wall for non-cooperative receivers, where the robustness of this SNR wall is essential for ensuring communication reliability. Furthermore, the study addresses the challenge of distinguishing between host and parasitic signals in the presence of partial-band interference through frequency-band partitioning. To overcome this difficulty, the relationship between the power ratio of masking to covert signals and the probability of detection error for eavesdroppers is investigated. Based on this analysis and prior research, a communication scheme is proposed to enhance overall system performance. Theoretical evaluations are conducted to assess its resistance to partial-band interference and communication reliability. Experimental results further validate system performance by examining the bit-error rate (BER) under varying levels of masking-signal strength and by comparing the reliability and security of the proposed scheme with those of chaotic spread-spectrum modulation schemes employing BPSK signals under equivalent average spread-spectrum gain.Subject terms: Information theory and computation, Complex networks

Introduction

In the current complex wireless communication environment, the performance optimization and resource management of covert communications are encountering new challenges. Covert communication, characterized by a low probability of detection, typically introduces additional randomness and uncertainty to reduce detectability by non-cooperative receivers, thereby achieving covert performance1,2. In wireless systems, detectability can be reduced either through inherent system uncertainty or through artificially introduced randomness3,4. The former relies on environmental noise or channel uncertainty to hide transmissions, whereas the latter often involves artificially injected noise or self-interference nodes to improve covert throughput5,6, and the feasibility of these approaches has been verified.

However, the method of using the uncertainty of the system itself often affects the covert transmission rate of the cooperative receiver, while artificially introduced uncertainty typically results in inefficient spectrum use7,8. Modern chaotic communication schemes have achieved substantial advances in BER and spectral efficiency. For instance,9 reports lower BER than standard DCSK in AWGN and Rayleigh fading channels by using subcarrier index modulation;10 improves spectral efficiency and BER through index modulation; and code-index-modulated DCSK (CIM-MDCSK) enhances robustness in multipath environments through OFDM subcarrier design. Although these schemes offer significant improvements in BER, throughput, and spectral efficiency, they provide limited analysis regarding masking intensity and the detection probability of non-cooperative receivers. To address these limitations, this paper considers leveraging pre-existing legitimate communication users as natural masking signals.

Existing large-signal masking schemes often rely on multi-antenna directivity and assignment vectors to prevent interception by adversaries11. Nevertheless, in modern electronic warfare scenarios, accurately predicting the direction of hostile receivers is highly challenging, limiting the practicality of such approaches. In this paper, a low probability of interception covert communication scheme is designed by reasonably planning the power of covert signals and avoiding interference with covert communication signals through secure channel division. The large signal of the known communication radio in the channel is used as the decoy signal. Building upon prior work12,13 which introduced the time-varying encrypted chaotic multi-tone (T-ECMT) method, which established a theoretical foundation for frequency-diverse, low-interception covert communication, this study further explores a practical scheme applicable to conventional communication systems. The relationship between interception probability and masking strength for non-cooperative receivers is analyzed theoretically under the assumption of prior knowledge of available frequency bands. Based on this analysis, the anti-interference performance and reliability of the proposed overlapping covert communication scheme are evaluated.

Related studies have shown that OFDM-based cognitive radio systems can exploit unused primary-user spectrum segments to approach Shannon-capacity performance14,15. Inspired by this, it is assumed that after electromagnetic environment sensing is completed, the covert communication signal is parasitically embedded into the time domain of a legitimate large signal16,17. Additionally, to circumvent the stringent orthogonality requirements imposed by traditional QPSK or QAM modulation in conventional OFDM systems, this paper proposes a detailed implementation of the overlapping covert communication scheme based on the previously developed T-ECMT modulation.

The main contributions of this paper are divided into the following three parts. Firstly, the power-parameter design of covert signals needed to ensure concealment under a constant false-alarm probability is derived by utilizing a conventional large signal as the masking source. Then, an overlapping covert communication transmission scheme is designed under the random spread spectrum depth. Finally, the anti-interference performance and reliability of this transmission scheme are analyzed theoretically. Combined with the simulation results, the performance of the scheme is evaluated.

The remainder of this paper is as follows. Problem formulation introduces the fundamental model of the overlapping covert communication system and analyzes the relationship between masking strength and non-cooperative detection probability under constant false-alarm constraints. Core technologies and design framework presents the proposed overlapping transmission scheme. Reliability and immunity performance analysis provides theoretical evaluations. Simulation verifies the effectiveness of the scheme, and Conclusion summarizes the findings.

Core technologies and design framework

In this section, the scheme structure of overlapping covert communication based on T-ECMT is mainly given from the perspective of frequency domain. To enhance spectrum utilization and ensure compatibility with conventional communication systems, the implementation of the scheme within an OFDM architecture is also investigated. This includes the formulation of detailed mathematical models, the incorporation of oversampling techniques to mitigate self-interference, and the design of the corresponding transmitter and receiver structures.

The chaotic multi-tone communication method utilized in previous studies encodes baseband data by exploiting the mathematical relationships among multiple frequency components within a multi-tone group (MTG), representing a form of random frequency diversity. Transmitter and receiver scheme design

The transceiver structure of the overlapping covert communication system, which is designed using the T-ECMT based on OFDM and outlined in Fig. 4. Following error-correcting encoding at the transmitter, T-ECMT modulation is performed according to the assigned timing information, generating modulation symbols that carry frequency-point information13. As demonstrated in previous studies, the chaotic multi-tone modulation algorithm ensures that subchannel switching over a wide communication bandwidth behaves analogously to chaotic frequency hopping5.

Overlapping covert communications system architecture.

Due to the low frequency of carrying symbol information in the T-ECMT modulation process, it is necessary to move the baseband modulation signal to the high frequency carrier for transmission in order to enhance signal transmission in the wireless channel. Thus, the baseband signal is shifted onto a high-frequency carrier, and single-sideband modulation is employed to generate the RF signal , which is subsequently converted by the D/A module and amplified by the RF front end16. The signal is then superimposed on the masking signal, which is defined as the legitimate conventional OFDM signal, within the channel to ensure successful signal transmission. Finally, the estimation of the wireless channel is completed by utilizing the legitimate conventional OFDM signal.

At the receiver, the RF demodulation stage consists of a low-noise amplifier and a broadband intermediate-frequency filter. This RF front-end circuitry performs signal amplification, carrier removal, and suppression of out-of-band interference16,22. For brevity, the detailed implementation of the channelization module and the effective subchannel identification unit is omitted here. The subsequent OFDM demodulation process is equivalent to the inverse operation of the OFDM-based T-ECMT modulation shown in Fig. 3. Finally, the spectrum information obtained by OFDM demodulation is demodulated according to the T-DECMT method and decoded, and the baseband symbol data transmitted by the covert signal can be recovered.

As illustrated in Fig. 8, for a fixed interference factor, increasing masking strength () leads to a marked degradation in the demodulation performance of the cooperative receiver. However, as the masking strength increases, the performance trend reverses. This improvement stems from the cooperative modulation structure and chaotic multi-tone synthesis, which enable the receiver to leverage partial symbol correlation and energy aggregation on subcarriers when masking is dominant.

BER of different covert communication methods under different masking strength.

In addition, the results validate the theoretical expectation that increasing the masking strength can enhance the concealment of transmission. A higher MS value generates a more uniform power spectral density, effectively blending the covert signal into the background OFDM masking signal and reducing the likelihood of detection by passive eavesdroppers (Willie). From a practical perspective, these results indicate that T-ECMT maintains reasonable communication reliability while offering significantly improved spectral hiding. Therefore, despite the suboptimal BER performance under weak masking, the system exhibits stronger concealment capabilities and higher robustness in overlapping environments—attributes that are crucial for real-world covert communication applications.

Conclusion

In this paper, an overlapping covert communication scheme based on the dynamic adjustment of the spread spectrum factor within T-ECMT was proposed to mitigate partial-band interference. The legitimate communication signal serves as a masking signal, while the covert signal operates as a parasitic component, enabling covert transmission through the establishment of a robust SNR wall against non-cooperative receivers. To address the challenge of distinguishing between host and parasitic signals under partial-band interference, a frequency partitioning strategy was adopted, and the relationship between the masking to covert power ratio and the detection error probability at an eavesdropper was analytically characterized.

Building on these analyses, a communication scheme based on OFDM technology was designed, incorporating oversampling techniques to suppress self-interference and enhance system robustness. Theoretical evaluations demonstrated the proposed scheme’s resistance to partial-band interference and its ability to maintain reliable covert communication. Furthermore, simulation experiments were conducted to verify the BER performance under varying masking strengths. Comparative analyses under equivalent average power and spread spectrum gain conditions showed that the proposed scheme outperforms chaotic spread spectrum modulation methods employing BPSK signals in terms of both reliability and security.

Overall, the results demonstrate that the proposed overlapping covert communication scheme offers improved robustness, enhanced concealment capability, and efficient spectrum utilization in complex wireless environments.

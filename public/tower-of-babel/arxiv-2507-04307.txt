Renjin Jiang & Fanghua Lin

Abstract




Abstract
. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain.
For any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
we show that for any Dirichlet eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)>\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
, it holds

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2},

where
\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
is given explicitly.
This estimate is based on a uniform estimate on the remainder of the Weyl law with explicit constants so that
it reduces the
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture to a computational problem.
Our arguments in deriving such uniform estimates yield also, in all dimensions
n\geq 2
, classes of domains that may even have rather irregular shapes or boundaries but satisfy Pólya’s conjecture.

1
Introduction

1.1
Weyl’s law and Pólya’s conjecture

Given an open bounded domain
\Omega
in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Let

0<\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\leq\lambda_{3}(\Omega)\leq\cdots

be eigenvalues to the Dirichlet Laplacian on
\Omega
.
We shall simply write
\lambda_{k}

instead of
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
when there is no confusion.

In 1911, Weyl
[
37
]
proved the famous Weyl’s law, which states that for any bounded open domain
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
, and
\lambda\to\infty
,

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)=\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}+o(\lambda^{n/2}),

where
\omega(n)
denotes the volume of unit ball in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)
is the counting function

\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda):=\#\{\lambda_{k}(\Omega):\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda\}.

The Weyl law is also important in the longstanding question “can one hear about the shape of a cavity”,
see Lord Rayleigh
[
30
]
and Kac
[
23
]
. Moreover, the Weyl law maybe related to the
prime number theorem, via Connes’ trace formula, see Connes
[
4
]
,
Fathizadeh-Khalkhali
[
13
]
and the beamer by
Khalkhali
[
24
]
.

Let us denote the remainder of Weyl’s law as

(1.2)

\displaystyle R_{\Omega}(\lambda)=\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)-\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}.

Subsequent to Weyl’s seminal work, Courant
[
5
]
, Courant-Hilbert
[
6
]
, Hörmander
[
19
,
20
]
, Seeley
[
35
,
36
]

and many others (see Ivrii
[
22
]
for comprehensive description on related history) made contributions to improve the estimate on the remainder.

Weyl also conjectured a sharper asymptotic behavior of Dirichlet eigenvalue states for domains with piecewise smooth boundary that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)=\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{|\partial\Omega|}{2^{n+1}\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{n+1}{2})}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+o(\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}),

here and in what follows, for any set
\Omega
,
|\partial\Omega|
denotes the surface area of it,
\mathscr{B}(\cdot,\cdot)
the Beta function and
\Gamma(\cdot)
the Gamma function, as usual.
The conjecture has been proved by Ivrii
[
21
]
under the condition that the set of all periodic geodesic billiards in
\Omega
has measure zero, which was proved by Safarov-Vassiliev
[
34
]
for convex analytic domains
and polygons.

However, the above mentioned results for the remainder
R_{\Omega}(\lambda)
state that certain asymptotic estimates valid only for large
\lambda
’s without quantifying the sizes of such
\lambda
’s,
and consequently they do not give quantitative estimate on eigenvalues.

It is rather natural to ask the following question.

Question 1.1
.

Is it possible to give a uniform and quantitative estimate for the remainder
R_{\Omega}(\lambda)
of Weyl’s law?

In 1954, Pólya
[
32
]
conjectured that, it should hold on arbitrary bounded open domain that

k\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n/2},\ \forall\,k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Pólya himself
[
33
]
proved this conjecture for tiling domains in the plane.
His method extends to high dimensions, and so the conjecture is true for tiling domains in high dimensions too.

The Pólya conjecture has been wide open since then. Other than tiling domains,
Laptev
[
26
]
verified Pólya’s conjecture on product domains
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}+n_{2}}
,
n_{1}\geq 2
,

n_{2}\geq 1
, provided Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}
. Filonov et al.
[
7
]
and
[
8
]

verified, via number theoretic arguments, Pólya’s conjecture on balls for all
n\geq 2
, and on annuli for
n=2
.
For general domains
\Omega
, it was known that Pólya’s conjecture holds for the first two Dirichlet eigenvalues
see
[
18
]
for the Rayleigh-Faber-Krahn inequality (
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
) and the Krahn-Szegö inequality (
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
).
See also
[
18
]
for the first nontrivial Neumann eigenvalue and recent exciting developments
[
3
,
15
]

for the second nontrivial Neumann eigenvalue.

For general domains, Berezin
[
2
]
and Li-Yau
[
29
]
independently proved for arbitrary bounded open domains that

(1.4)

\frac{|\Omega|^{2/n}\omega(n)^{2/n}}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{n}{n+2}k^{\frac{n+2}{n}},

which implies that

(1.5)

\left(\frac{n+2}{n}\right)^{n/2}\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}^{n/2}\geq k,

see also Laptev
[
26
]
, and
[
10
,
11
,
14
,
25
,
31
]
for further improvements on the lower order term and generalizations.
Note that the estimate (
1.5
) and its improvements are still a distance away from Pólya’s conjecture.

Our main purpose of this paper is to reduce the
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture to a computational problem,
and to give a solution to Question
1.1
with explicit constants, on Lipschitz domains.
Moreover, we shall also provide an approach to the Pólya conjecture that might be applied to general smooth domains,
and provide in all dimensions,
n\geq 2
, a class of domains that satisfy Pólya’s conjecture.

1.2
Pólya’s conjecture up to
\epsilon
-loss and quantitative remainder estimate

Let us start from a definition for Pólya’s conjecture up to an
\epsilon
-loss.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
be a bounded open domain,
n\geq 2
. For
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we say that Pólya’s conjecture up to an
\epsilon
-loss or
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture
holds for some or all Dirichlet eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
, if it holds for some or all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}.

Noting that
2\leq\left(\frac{n+2}{n}\right)^{n/2}\to e
as
2\leq n\to\infty
, the
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture improves substantially
(
1.5
) (and its improvements on lower order terms, see
[
2
,
14
,
25
,
31
]
).

For a bounded open domain
\Omega
, we define its width
\mathrm{width}(\Omega)
as the minimum distance between two parallel hyperplanes that contain
\Omega
inside.
Moreover, up to a rotation, throughout the paper we shall assume the width
\mathrm{width}(\Omega)
is attained in the
n
-th direction.
For a bounded Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, we let
C_{Lip}(\Omega)\geq 1
be the constant, uniquely determined by
\Omega
, such that
for
\epsilon\in(0,\mathrm{width}(\Omega))
, such that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\left|\{x\in{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}:\,{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,dist\,}}(x,\partial\Omega)\leq\epsilon\}\right|\leq C_{Lip}(\Omega)\epsilon|\partial\Omega|.

We have the following Pólya’s conjecture up to
\epsilon
-loss.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
n\geq 2
.
For any
0<\epsilon<1
, it holds for all
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2},

where
\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)\geq\mathrm{width}(\Omega)^{-2}
is such that

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)}}C_{Lip}(\Omega)\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)=\epsilon.

Moreover, if
\Omega
is convex, then
\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
can be taken smaller as

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)=\epsilon.

Obviously, for a given
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, and a concrete Lipschitz (resp. convex) domain
\Omega
,

\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
is uniquely determined by the equation (
1.7
) (resp. (
1.8
) for convex domains).
It can be seen from the above estimate that
\Omega
needs only to be a bounded set of finite perimeter. Also, to show the
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture,
Theorem
1.3
reduces the problem to a computational problem for eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\Lambda(\epsilon,\Omega)
.

Theorem
1.3
will follow from our following uniform estimate for the remainder of the Weyl’s law.
Let us start from the following definition.

Definition 1.4

(Minimal Admissible rectangle)
.

Let
\mathscr{R}=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\cdots\times I_{n}
be a rectangle in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, where
I_{i}
is connected open interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
for each
1\leq i\leq n
.
We say that
\mathscr{R}=I_{1}\times I_{2}\times\cdots\times I_{n}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I_{min}
is the minimal admissible rectangle for
\Omega
, if
I_{min}=I_{n}=\mathrm{width}(\Omega)
,
and
\mathscr{R}
contains
\Omega
, and for each
i=1,\cdots,n
,
for any
\tilde{I}_{i}
with
|\tilde{I}_{i}|<|I_{i}|
,
I_{1}\times\cdots\tilde{I}_{i}\times\cdots\times I_{n}
does not contain
\Omega
inside.

We have the following uniform estimate on Lipschitz domains.
In what follows, for a bounded open domain
\Omega
, we denote by
r_{in}=r_{in}(\Omega)
the radius of the largest ball contained in
\Omega
.

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded Lipschitz domain,
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I_{min}
be the minimal Admissible rectangle for
\Omega
. It holds for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}+C(n,\Omega,\lambda_{k}(\Omega))\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle C(n,\Omega,\lambda_{k}(\Omega))=C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})|\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)-C_{1}(n-1)|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|.

Moreover, it holds for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

(1.10)

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)\omega(n)|\partial\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\Omega)}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right).

Above,

(1.11)

\displaystyle C_{1}(n)=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3}&n=1,\\
\frac{3\pi}{16}&n=2,\\
\frac{\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,2)}{6\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,5/2)}&n=3,4,\\
\frac{3\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,2)}{16\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,5/2)}\left(\frac{247}{256}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}&n\geq 5.\end{cases}

Obviously, both quantities
\frac{r_{in}(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}{|I_{min}|}
and
\frac{r_{in}(\Omega)}{|I_{min}|}
are not larger than
1/2
,
and therefore can be replaced by
1/2
. Moreover, note that if
|\partial(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})|
is small compared to
|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|
,
we then have that Pólya’s conjecture holds for the first some eigenvalues. Our quantitative estimate is consistent to the asymptotic estimates established by Courant
[
5
]
and Courant-Hilbert
[
6
]
.

For convex sets, we have a clean and better estimate as following.

Corollary 1.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded convex domain,
n\geq 2
. Let
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I_{min}
be the minimal Admissible rectangle for
\Omega
. It holds for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(|\partial\mathscr{R}\setminus\partial\Omega|\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\mathscr{R}\setminus{\overline{\Omega}})}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)-C_{1}(n-1)|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\right),

and

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-\frac{\omega(n)|\partial\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\Omega)}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right).

Let us remark that the logarithm term in (
1.9
) comes from the Whitney decomposition for
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, see the proof of Theorem
1.5
in Section 4 below.
For an explicitly given Lipschitz domain
\Omega
,
one may perform a better decomposition of
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, which is not necessarily of Whitney type, and which may give better constant in the lower order terms if one can decompose the given domain into fewer and larger cubes or rectangles. Generally, to get better estimates than (
1.9
),
one has to find some generic and better decomposition than the Whitney decomposition we used in the proof.
If
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
admits a better decomposition, such as a union of up to infinite many cubes with finite surface, we then can prove full Pólya’s conjecture, see discussions in the next subsection.

1.3
An approach to Pólya’s conjecture and concrete examples

Using the strategy developed above, we can provide in all dimensions
n\geq 2
,
a class of domains which may have rather irregular shape or boundary but satisfy Pólya’s conjecture.
Using Seeley’s asymptotic estimates
[
35
,
36
]
(see also Hörmander
[
20
]
), we first provide
an approach to Pólya’s conjecture that might be applied to general smooth domains.

By a rectangle
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
, we mean
\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
is a rectangle, and
I\subset{\mathbb{R}}
is an open interval.

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I
is a rectangle in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
.
Assume that
\Omega
is a piecewise smooth domain contained in
\mathscr{R}
.
There exists
C_{P}=C(\Omega,n)
such that if
|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\geq C_{P}
then the Pólya conjecture holds on
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.

In what follows, by a cube
Q
we always mean it is an open cube and denote by
\ell(Q)
its side length.
Let us first introduce a definition. Let us emphasize that, since the Pólya conjecture is scaling invariant,
it is not restrictive to require in the following definition that the side length of the largest cube
Q
is one.

Definition 1.8

(Admissible class)
.

Let
n\geq 2
. Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of cubes in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
with
\sup_{Q_{k}}\{\ell(Q_{k})\}=1
. Moreover, we allow some of these cubes
Q_{k}
to be empty set.
Set
\mathscr{S}_{Q}
as the surface area of these cubes, i.e.,

\mathscr{S}_{Q}:=\sum_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}\ell(Q_{k})^{n-1}.

We say that
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
is of Admissible class if
\mathscr{S}_{Q}<\infty
, and for any
k,j\in{\mathbb{N}}
,
Q_{k}\cap Q_{j}=\emptyset
if
k\neq j
.

We have a class of domains that satisfy Pólya’s conjecture in all dimensions.

Theorem 1.9
.

Suppose
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I
is a rectangle in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
. Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be of Admissible class in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, that is contained in
\mathscr{R}
.
If
|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\geq C_{2}(n-1)\mathscr{S}_{Q}
, then the Pólya conjecture holds on
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}Q_{k}}
,
where

(1.12)

\displaystyle C_{2}(n)=\begin{cases}2\sqrt{2}\pi,&n=1,\\
9\sqrt{3},&n=2,\\
\frac{6(n+1)^{3/2}\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,5/2)}{\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,2)}&n=3,4,\\
\frac{16(n+1)^{3/2}\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,5/2)}{3\mathscr{B}(\frac{n}{2}-1,2)}\left(\frac{256}{247}\right)^{\frac{n-2}{2}}&n\geq 5.\end{cases}

For
M\geq 2
, by
\tilde{\Omega}

M
-tiles
\Omega

we mean there are
M
isometries of
\tilde{\Omega}
,
\{\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\}_{1\leq k\leq M}
, with non-overlapped interior, such that
\cup_{1\leq k\leq M}\tilde{\Omega}_{k}\subset\Omega

and
|\Omega|=M|\tilde{\Omega}|
.

Corollary 1.10
.

Let
M\geq 2
. Suppose
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I
is a rectangle in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
,
n\geq 2
, and
\Omega

M
-tiles
\mathscr{R}
.
Let
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be of Admissible class in
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
, that is contained in
\Omega
.
If
|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\geq C_{2}(n-1)M\mathscr{S}_{Q}
, then Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega\setminus\overline{\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}Q_{k}}
.

Note that
M
isometries of the domain
\Omega\setminus\overline{\cup_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}Q_{k}}

corresponds to the case that removing
M
copies of
\{Q_{k}\}_{k\in{\mathbb{N}}}
from
\mathscr{R}
, the conclusion is obvious.

Some further remarks are in order. In what follows for
C>0
we use
\lfloor C\rfloor
to denote the integer part of it.

Remark 1.11
.

(i) Let us take
\ell(k)=2^{\lfloor k/2\rfloor}
for
k\geq 2
,
\ell(1)
=1,
and
\{Q_{1,1},\,\{Q_{k,j}\}_{k\geq 2,1\leq j\leq\ell(k)}\}
be a sequence of cubes, where
\ell(Q_{k,j})=2^{1-k}
.
Then
\mathscr{S}_{Q}\leq 2(2+\sqrt{2})
. So for a rectangle
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
,
|\Omega_{2}|>1
and
|\Omega_{1}|>8(1+\sqrt{2})\pi
,
one can arbitrarily remove any domain, that is composed by all or some of these cubes
\{Q_{1,1},\,\{Q_{k,j}\}_{k\geq 2,1\leq j\leq\ell(k)}\}
with non-overlapped interior,
from
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
such that the remaining set satisfies Pólya’s conjecture. See Figure
1
for some examples.

(b)

(ii) Previously, in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
, Pólya’s conjecture was only known to be true on two types of domains, tiling domains (
[
33
]
), balls, sectors and annuli (
[
7
,

]
).
In higher dimensions
n\geq 3
, other than tiling domains, balls and sectors that tile balls, there are a class of product domains satisfying Pólya’s conjecture (
[

]
),
which are products of open sets in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-k}
and domains satisfying Pólya’s conjecture in

k\geq 2
. See also
[

]
for a specific product in

.

1.9
) provides in all dimensions
n\geq 2
, a class of domains that may have rather irregular shapes or boundaries but satisfy Pólya’s conjecture.

1.4
Our method and plan of the paper

The main strategy to prove Theorem
1.5
, Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.9
is as following.

We first use the explicit formula of Riesz means of Dirichlet eigenvalues
in
{\mathbb{R}}
(cf. Lemma
2.1
), following the inspiring observation by Laptev
[
26
]
,
to give a refined estimates on product domains
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
(see Theorem
2.3
below). This allows us to conclude via monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues
(cf.
[
18
]
) that for any open domains
\Omega
contained in
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
, the first
k_{0}
Dirichlet eigenvalues satisfies Pólya’s conjecture (cf. Corollary
2.4
).

We then establish a quantitative lower bound for the counting function (upper bound for the Dirichlet eigenvalue) on cubes (see Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
below), which together with our refinements on product domains and
monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues to conclude the proof Theorem
1.9
. In fact, in higher dimensions,
n\geq 3
, the rectangle
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I

can be replaced some more general product
\Omega\times I
, as soon as Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega
, see Theorem
3.7
below.

Although only a part of discussions in Sections 2 and 3 will be sufficient for the proofs of Theorem
1.3
and Theorem
1.5
, the extended discussions are of independent interest and they have other uses.
For Theorem
1.5
we shall apply the Whitney decomposition of a Lipschitz domain
\Omega
, and using the lower bound for the counting function
on cubes (see Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
below) along with an optimization to deduce a lower bound for the counting function
\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)
on
\Omega
.
Finally, applying our refinements on product domains (see Theorem
2.3
below) and
monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues, together with the lower bound of
\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}}^{D}(\lambda)
to conclude the proof. Theorem
1.3

then follows almost immediately from Theorem
1.5
.

Finally, let us point it out, our approach depends on a modified min-max argument, which is related to but different from the original ones by Courant
[
5
]
and Courant-Hilbert
[
6
]
.
By our choose of
minimal Admissible rectangles
,
we do not have to take care of the term in Courant
[
5
]
and Courant-Hilbert
[
6
]

for cubes which intersect the boundary of
\Omega
. The latter is a difficult issue for Courant

2
Refined eigenvalue estimates on product domains

In this section, we prove refined eigenvalue estimates on product domains, Theorem
2.3
and its corollary, Corollary
2.4
.
Recall that, if an open domain
\Omega
is in the real line, then its eigenvalues have explicit lower bounds, which satisfy for
k\geq 1

\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}k^{2}.

This allows us to show the following estimate for the Riesz means.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded. It holds that

(2.1)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3/2}\min\left\{\left(1-\frac{3\pi}{16|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+},\,\left(1-\left(\frac{\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi}\rfloor}{\frac{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}{\pi}}\right)^{2}\frac{3\pi}{4|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)\right\},

and for
\lambda>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{6}\pi}{9|\Omega|}\sqrt{\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right).

Moreover, if
\Omega
is a connected open interval, it holds for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{2\pi}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}\lambda}\right).

Proof.

Let us first prove (
2.1
).
If
\lambda\leq\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
, then
\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})=0
. Suppose
\lambda>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
and set
k_{\lambda}=\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor
. Then we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\lambda-\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}k^{2})

\displaystyle=\lambda k_{\lambda}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}\frac{1}{6}k_{\lambda}\left(k_{\lambda}+1\right)\left(2k_{\lambda}+1\right)

\displaystyle=\lambda k_{\lambda}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3|\Omega|^{2}}k_{\lambda}^{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2|\Omega|^{2}}k_{\lambda}^{2}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6|\Omega|^{2}}k_{\lambda}.

Since

\displaystyle\lambda k_{\lambda}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{3|\Omega|^{2}}k_{\lambda}^{3}\leq\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3},

we see that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle<\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3}-\frac{\pi^{2}}{2|\Omega|^{2}}\left(\frac{k_{\lambda}}{\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda=\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3}-\left(\frac{k_{\lambda}}{\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)^{2}\frac{\lambda}{2}.

For
\lambda>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
,
\frac{k_{\lambda}}{\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}}\geq 1/2
, so we also have

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle<\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3}-\frac{\lambda}{8}.

Let us prove (
2.2
). Again we only need to consider
\lambda>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
. Set
k_{\lambda}=\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor
. Note that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\lambda-\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}k^{2})^{1/2}=\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}.

Let us consider the ball
B(0,\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda})
in the first quadrant in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
For each
1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}=\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor
,
(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}

equals the area of the rectangle
[k-1,k]\times[0,(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}]
, which is contained in
B(0,\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda})
in the first quadrant in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.
Moreover, for each
k
, there is at least a triangle with vertices at
(k-1,(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-(k-1)^{2})^{1/2})
,

(k-1,(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2})
and
(k,(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2})

contained in the ball. Moreover, if
k_{\lambda}\neq\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}
, there is another triangle insider the 1/4 ball
with vertices at
(k_{\lambda},(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k_{\lambda}^{2})^{1/2})
,

(k_{\lambda},(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k_{\lambda}^{2})^{1/2})
and
(\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda},0)
. See Figure

We therefore see that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda

\displaystyle>\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}1\times\left((\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-(k-1)^{2})^{1/2}-(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}-k_{\lambda}\right)\times(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k_{\lambda}^{2})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\left((\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda)^{1/2}-(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k_{\lambda}^{2})^{1/2}+\left(\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}-k_{\lambda}\right)\times(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k_{\lambda}^{2})^{1/2}\right).

For
x\in[0,1)
, let us consider the function

g(x):=k_{\lambda}+x-\left((k_{\lambda}+x)^{2}-k_{\lambda}^{2}\right)^{1/2}(1-x).

A calculation shows that for
k_{\lambda}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, it holds

\displaystyle g(x)

\displaystyle=k_{\lambda}+x-\left(2k_{\lambda}x+x^{2}\right)^{1/2}(1-x)

\displaystyle\geq k_{\lambda}+x-\sqrt{2k_{\lambda}+1}\sqrt{x}(1-x)

\displaystyle\geq k_{\lambda}+x-\frac{2\sqrt{3}}{9}\sqrt{2k_{\lambda}+1}

\displaystyle\geq k_{\lambda}+x-\frac{2}{3}k_{\lambda}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k_{\lambda}+x}{3}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda

\displaystyle>\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{2}\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}-\frac{\sqrt{6}}{9}\sqrt{\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}+\frac{1}{6}\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}<\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda-\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{2}+\frac{\sqrt{6}\pi}{9|\Omega|}\sqrt{\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor+\frac{1}{2}}\leq\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{2}{3}\frac{1}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right).

Let us prove (
2.3
). Since
\Omega
is connected, we have

\lambda_{k}(\Omega)=\frac{k^{2}\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}},\,\forall\ k\in{\mathbb{N}}.

Set again
k_{\lambda}=\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|}{\pi}\sqrt{\lambda}\rfloor
. For
\lambda>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\lambda-\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}k^{2})^{1/2}=\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}\sum_{1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}}(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}.

Note that the union of the rectangles
[k-1,k]\times[0,(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})^{1/2}]
,
1\leq k\leq k_{\lambda}
, contains the ball

B(0,\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-2\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda}+1})
intersecting the first quadrant, since

\displaystyle\sqrt{(k-1)^{2}+(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-k^{2})}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-2k+1}\geq\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-2k_{\lambda}+1}

\displaystyle\geq\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-2\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda}+1}.

We therefore deduce that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\pi}{4}\frac{\pi}{|\Omega|}\left(\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda-2\sqrt{\frac{|\Omega|^{2}}{\pi^{2}}\lambda}+1\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega|}{4}\lambda\left(1-\frac{2\pi}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\lambda}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}\lambda}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be an open bounded set. Then for
p>1
and
\lambda>\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, it holds that

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\lambda^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{B}(p-1,5/2)-C_{3}(p)\lambda^{p},

where

(2.4)

\displaystyle C_{3}(p)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{8},&p=1,\\
\frac{1}{9},&1<p\leq 2,\\
\frac{1}{8}\left(\frac{247}{256}\right)^{p-1},&p>2.\end{cases}

Proof.

By the semigroup type property of the Riesz means and (
2.1
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k:\,\lambda_{k}<\lambda}(\lambda-\lambda_{k})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)^{-1}\sum_{k}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{p-2}(\lambda-\lambda_{k}-v)_{+}\,dv

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\int_{0}^{\infty}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}^{3/2}\left(1-\frac{3\pi}{16|\Omega|\sqrt{(\lambda-v)_{+}}}\right)_{+}\,dv

(2.5)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2|\Omega|}{3\pi\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\lambda^{p+\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{B}(p-1,5/2)-\frac{1}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\int_{0}^{\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv.

Noticing that

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}},

so for
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
,

\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}\geq\frac{247}{256}\lambda.

For
1<p\leq 2
,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\int_{0}^{\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv\geq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\frac{249}{256}\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)\geq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{9}.

For
p>2
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}}^{\lambda}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p-1}-\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p}-\left(\frac{\lambda(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}})^{p-1}}{p-1}-\frac{(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}})^{p}}{p}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p(p-1)}-\left(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}\right)^{p-1}\left(\frac{\lambda}{p-1}-\frac{(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}})}{p}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{p(p-1)}-\left(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\lambda}{p(p-1)}

and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\int_{0}^{\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\left(\int_{0}^{\lambda}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv-\int_{\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}}^{\lambda}v^{p-2}(\lambda-v)_{+}\,dv\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{8\mathscr{B}(p-1,2)}\left(\lambda-\frac{9\pi^{2}}{256|\Omega|^{2}}\right)^{p-1}\frac{\lambda}{p(p-1)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\lambda^{p}}{8}\left(\frac{247}{256}\right)^{p-1}.

Combining the above estimates yields the desired conclusion.
∎

In view of Laptev’s beautiful theory on product domains

,

Note that any bounded domain
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}
satisfies Pólya’s conjecture, i.e.,

\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega|^{2}}k^{2}
for each

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}
and
\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded open domains,
n_{1}\geq 1
and
n=n_{1}+1
. Suppose that
Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}
. Then it holds for
\lambda>0
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+},

where
C_{1}(n_{1})
is as in (
1.11
),

(2.7)

\displaystyle C_{1}(n_{1})=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3}&n_{1}=1,\\
\frac{3\pi}{16}&n_{1}=2,\\
\frac{\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,2)}{6\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,5/2)}&n_{1}=3,4,\\
\frac{3\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,2)}{16\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,5/2)}\left(\frac{247}{256}\right)^{\frac{n_{1}-2}{2}}&n_{1}\geq 5.\end{cases}

Proof.

Suppose that it holds that

\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{1}}(\lambda)=\#\{\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{1}):\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{1})<\lambda\}\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n_{1})}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}}}\lambda^{n/2}.

Then for all
\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
, where

(2.8)

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\Omega)=\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{1}(\Omega_{2})\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{2}}{|\Omega_{1}|^{2/n}\omega(n_{1})^{2/n_{1}}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{|\Omega_{2}|^{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\#\left\{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{1})+\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda\right\}=\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\#\left\{k:\,\lambda_{k}(\Omega_{1})<(\lambda-\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2}))_{+}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n_{1})}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}}}(\lambda-\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2}))^{n_{1}/2}.

If
n_{1}=1
, then by (
2.2
), we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\frac{|\Omega_{1}|}{\pi}(\lambda-\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2}))^{1/2}\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|}{4\pi}\lambda\left(1-\frac{2}{3|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}.

If
n_{1}=2
, then by (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(2)}{(2\pi)^{2}}(\lambda-\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2}))\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(2)}{(2\pi)^{2}}\frac{2|\Omega_{2}|}{3\pi}\lambda^{3/2}\left(1-\frac{3\pi}{16|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega|\omega(3)}{(2\pi)^{3}}\lambda^{3/2}\left(1-\frac{3\pi}{16|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}.

For
n_{1}\geq 3
, by Lemma
2.2
, we see that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j:\,\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2})<\lambda}\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n_{1})}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}}}(\lambda-\lambda_{j}(\Omega_{2}))^{n_{1}/2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n_{1})}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}}}\left(\frac{2|\Omega_{2}|}{3\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,2)}\lambda^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}+\frac{1}{2}}\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,5/2)-C_{3}(\frac{n_{1}}{2})\lambda^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}+1}\left(1-\frac{3\pi\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,2)}{2\mathscr{B}(\frac{n_{1}}{2}-1,5/2)}\frac{C_{3}(\frac{n_{1}}{2})}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n_{1}+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n_{1}}{2}+1}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}.

The proof is complete.
∎

As a corollary, we have the following result.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n_{1}}
and
\Omega_{2}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
be bounded open domains,
n_{1}\geq 1
and
n=n_{1}+1
. Suppose that
Pólya’s conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}
. Let
\Omega
be an open set contained in
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
, and

k_{0}=\left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left[\left(1-\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|}\right]^{n}\right\rfloor.

Then for each
0\leq k\leq k_{0}
, the Pólya conjecture holds on
\Omega
for
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
, i.e.,

k\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}.

Proof.

Since
\Omega\subset\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
, we may assume that
|\Omega|<|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|
, otherwise
|\Omega|=|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|
and the Pólya conjecture holds
for all Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\Omega
.
Fix
\lambda_{0}>0
such that

|\Omega|=|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}_{0}}\right).

By the monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues, for
\lambda<\lambda_{0}
we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\lambda)\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)

(2.9)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}.

Recall that

k_{0}=\left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left[\left(1-\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\frac{C_{1}(n_{1})}{|\Omega_{2}|}\right]^{n}\right\rfloor\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{0}^{n/2}.

Let
0\leq k\leq k_{0}
. If
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)<\lambda_{0}
, then (
2
) gives that for small enough
\epsilon>0
such that
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)+\epsilon<\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle k\leq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda_{k}(\Omega)+\epsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}(\lambda_{k}(\Omega)+\epsilon)^{n/2}.

Letting
\epsilon\to 0
gives that

\displaystyle k\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}.

If
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{0}

\displaystyle k\leq k_{0}\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{0}^{n/2}\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3
Pólya’s conjecture on a class of domains

Let us start with proving the qualitative result regarding removing general piecewise smooth set.
Theorem
1.7
follows immediately from the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
satisfies Pólya’s conjecture,
n\geq 2
,
\Omega_{2}
is an interval in
{\mathbb{R}}
and

\Omega_{3}
is a piecewise smooth domain contained in
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
.
There exists
C_{P}=C(\Omega_{3},n)
such that if
|\Omega_{1}|\geq C_{P}
then the Pólya conjecture holds on
\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{3}}
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega=\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{3}}
.
Up to a scaling, we may assume that
|\Omega_{3}|=1
. It follows from Seeley
[
35
,
36
]
(see also
[
20
, Corollary 17.5.11 & (17.5.16)’]
) that
for some
\Lambda>0
, it holds for
\lambda>\Lambda
that

(3.1)

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{3}}(\lambda)-\frac{|\Omega_{3}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\right|\leq C(\Omega_{3})\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

We assume that

|\Omega_{1}|>\max\left\{\frac{C(\Omega_{3})(2\pi)^{n}}{\omega(n)C_{1}(n-1)},\frac{\sqrt{2\Lambda}}{C_{1}(n-1)}\right\}.

By Corollary
2.4
, Pólya’s conjecture holds for the first
k_{0}
Dirichlet eigenvalues, where

k_{0}=\left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left[\left(1-\frac{|\Omega|}{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|}\right)^{-1}\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{|\Omega_{2}|}\right]^{n}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}(C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|)^{n}\right\rfloor.

It holds

\lambda_{k_{0}}(\Omega)^{n/2}\geq\frac{(2\pi)^{n}}{|\Omega|\omega(n)}\left\lfloor\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}(C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|)^{n}\right\rfloor\geq\frac{1}{2}(C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|)^{n},

and hence,

\lambda_{k_{0}}(\Omega)\geq\frac{1}{2}(C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|)^{2}.

Since
|\Omega_{1}|>\sqrt{2\Lambda}/C_{1}(n-1)
, it holds that

\lambda_{k_{0}}(\Omega)>\Lambda.

By (
3.1
) and
|\Omega_{3}|=1
, we see that for any
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{k_{0}}(\Omega)
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{3}}(\lambda_{k}(\Omega))\geq\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-C(\Omega_{3})\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

This implies on
\Omega_{3}
, there are
\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{3}}(\lambda_{k}(\Omega))
eigenvalues smaller than
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
. Since
\Omega
and
\Omega_{3}
are disjoint,

\lambda_{k}(\Omega)
corresponds to some
\lambda_{k+\tilde{k}}(\Omega\cup\Omega_{3})
for some

\tilde{k}\geq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{3}}(\lambda_{k}(\Omega))\geq\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-C(\Omega_{3})\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Since
\Omega\cup\Omega_{3}\subset\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}
and
|\Omega\cup\Omega_{3}|=|\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}|
, the monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues together with
Theorem
2.3
implies that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{3}}(\lambda)\leq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega_{1}\times\Omega_{2}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+},

and therefore

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k+\tilde{k}}(\Omega\cup\Omega_{3})^{n/2}

\displaystyle\geq k+\tilde{k}+\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{|\Omega_{2}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq k+\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-C(\Omega_{3})\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}+\frac{C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Since
|\Omega_{1}|\geq\frac{C(\Omega_{3})(2\pi)^{n}}{\omega(n)C_{1}(n-1)}
, we finally see that

\displaystyle\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega_{1}||\Omega_{2}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\geq k+\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{C_{1}(n-1)|\Omega_{1}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}-C(\Omega_{3})\right)

\displaystyle\geq k.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.1
The case of two dimension

Let
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be an open unit cube,
n=2
. Then the Dirichlet eigenvalues of
Q
has the form

\left\{\lambda_{k}:\,\lambda_{k}=\pi^{2}(a^{2}+b^{2}),\,a,b\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

Note that for a given
\lambda_{k}
,

\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda_{k})
is dominated by the number of unit cubes contained in the first quadrant intersecting the ball

B(0,\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}/\pi)

So it is readily to see that for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\lambda_{k}\geq 4\pi k.

In fact Theorem
2.3
gives a better result.
We however shall need the opposite estimates.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
Q\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be a cube with side length
\ell(Q)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
.

(i) For any
\lambda>2\pi^{2}/\ell(Q)^{2}
, it holds that

\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda)\geq\frac{\ell(Q)^{2}}{4\pi}\lambda-\frac{\omega(2)2^{3/2}\pi\ell(Q)}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda}+\frac{\pi}{2}.

(ii) For any
\lambda>0
, it holds that

\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda)\geq\frac{\ell(Q)^{2}}{4\pi}\lambda-\frac{\omega(2)2^{3/2}\pi\ell(Q)}{(2\pi)^{2}}\sqrt{\lambda}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\#\left\{\lambda_{k}<\lambda:\,\lambda_{k}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{\ell(Q)^{2}}(a^{2}+b^{2}),\,a,b\in\mathbb{N}\right\}.

So the number
\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda)
is not less than the number of the unit cubes contained
in the first quadrant intersecting the ball

B(0,\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}/\pi-\sqrt{2})
, where
\sqrt{2}
is the diameter of the unit cube.
This implies that for any
\lambda>2\pi^{2}/\ell(Q)^{2}
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}|B(0,\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}/\pi-\sqrt{2})|=\frac{\pi}{4}\left(\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}/\pi-\sqrt{2}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ell(Q)^{2}}{4\pi}\lambda-\frac{\sqrt{2}\ell(Q)}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}+\frac{\pi}{2}.

Noting that for
\lambda\leq 2\pi^{2}/\ell(Q)^{2}
,

\frac{\ell(Q)^{2}}{4\pi}\lambda-\frac{\sqrt{2}\ell(Q)}{2}\sqrt{\lambda}\leq 0\leq\mathcal{N}_{Q}^{D}(\lambda),

the conclusion is obvious. The proof is complete.
∎

3.3
Some other class satisfying Pólya’s conjecture

We next provide some result regarding the case of general domains satisfying Pólya’s conjecture.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\Omega_{1}\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
,

Proof.

The required inequalities for
k\leq 2
followed from the Rayleigh-Faber-Krahn inequality (
\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
) and the Krahn-Szegö inequality (
\lambda_{2}(\Omega)
), see
[
18
]
.

Let us consider

. Note that
\Omega\cup\cup_{j}Q_{j}\subsetneq\Omega_{1}
, and
|\Omega\cup\cup_{j}Q_{j}|=|\Omega_{1}|
.

3.6
and Lemma
3.6
(ii), and the fact

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\Omega
be an open nonempty subset of
{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
. Then there exists a family of dyadic cubes
\{Q_{i}\}_{j}
such that

We first provide the proof for the estimate on the remainder of Weyl’s law.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Step 1. Upper bound for Dirichlet eigenvalues
.
Let
\{Q_{j}\}_{j\in{\mathbb{N}}}
be a sequence of dyadic cubes that forms a Whitney decomposition of
\Omega
by

4.1
. Suppose that
B(x_{0},r_{in})
is the largest inner ball contained in
\Omega
.

Let
Q_{l}
be a cube from
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
such that
x_{0}\in\overline{Q_{l}}
.
Then it holds that

\sqrt{n}\ell(Q_{l})\leq\mathrm{dist}(Q_{l},\Omega^{c})\leq r_{in}\leq 5\sqrt{n}\ell(Q_{l}).

If there is some other
Q_{i}
, with
\ell(Q_{i})>\ell(Q_{l})
, then

\sqrt{n}\ell(Q_{i})\leq r_{in}\leq 5\sqrt{n}\ell(Q_{l}).

Let
2^{-k_{0}}
be the side length of the largest cube in
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}

\frac{r_{in}}{5\sqrt{n}}\leq 2^{-k_{0}}=\sup_{Q_{j}}\{\ell(Q_{j})\}\leq\frac{r_{in}}{\sqrt{n}}.

For
0<\epsilon<r_{in}
, let

\Omega_{\epsilon}=\{x\in\Omega:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\epsilon\}.

Let
k_{\epsilon}\in\mathbb{Z}
be such that

5\sqrt{n}2^{-k_{\epsilon}}\leq\epsilon<5\sqrt{n}2^{-k_{\epsilon}+1}.

Then
k_{\epsilon}>k_{0}
.

By Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
, we have for any
\lambda>0
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\ell(Q_{j})>2^{-k_{\epsilon}}}\mathcal{N}_{Q_{j}}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=k_{0}}^{k_{\epsilon}-1}\sum_{Q_{j}:\,\ell(Q_{j})=2^{-m}}\mathcal{N}_{Q_{j}}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{m=k_{0}}^{k_{\epsilon}-1}\sum_{Q_{j}:\,\ell(Q_{j})=2^{-m}}\left(\frac{\omega(n)\ell(Q_{j})^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{\omega(n)n^{3/2}\pi\ell(Q_{j})^{n-1}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\right).

Note that all the cubes
\{Q_{j}\}_{j}
with side length smaller than

is contained in
\Omega_{\epsilon}
. Moreover,
the number of cubes of side length
2^{-j}
,

, is smaller than

\frac{|\Omega_{5\sqrt{n}2^{-j}}|}{2^{-jn}}\leq\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)5\sqrt{n}2^{-j}|\partial\Omega|}{2^{-jn}}\leq 5\sqrt{n}2^{j(n-1)}C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega|,

where we used the fact that

|\Omega_{5\sqrt{n}2^{-j}}|=\left|\{x\in\Omega:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<5\sqrt{n}2^{-j}\}\right|\leq 5\sqrt{n}2^{-j}C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega|,

C_{Lip}(\Omega)
is defined as in (
1.6
).

We therefore deduce that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\sum_{m=k_{0}}^{k_{\epsilon}-1}5\sqrt{n}C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega|\frac{\omega(n)n^{3/2}\pi}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-5\sqrt{n}(k_{\epsilon}-k_{0})C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega|\frac{\omega(n)n^{3/2}\pi}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}.

Note that

|\Omega_{\epsilon}|=\left|\{x\in\Omega:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\epsilon\}\right|\leq C_{Lip}(\Omega)\epsilon|\partial\Omega|

and

2^{k_{\epsilon}-k_{0}}\leq{\frac{10\sqrt{n}}{\epsilon}}{\frac{r_{in}}{\sqrt{n}}}\leq\frac{10r_{in}}{\epsilon}.

We therefore have

(4.1)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)\omega(n)|\partial\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\epsilon\lambda^{1/2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10r_{in}}{\epsilon}\right).

Let
\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I_{min}
be the minimal rectangle for
\Omega
. Since

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathscr{R})>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|I_{min}|^{2}},

we see that

\frac{\pi r_{in}}{|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}}<r_{in}.

So for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we can let

\epsilon=\frac{\pi r_{in}}{|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}

and deduce that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}-\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)\omega(n)|\partial\Omega|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right).

The proof of
Step 1
is complete.

Step 2. Lower bound for the Dirichlet eigenvalues
.
To prove the lower bound for the Dirichlet eigenvalues of
\Omega
, let us set
\Omega_{1}=\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. Obviously,
\Omega_{1}
is also a Lipschitz domain.
Moreover, by (
1.6
), it holds that

\displaystyle\left|\{x\in\Omega_{1}:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega_{1})<\epsilon\}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon|\partial\mathscr{R}\cap\partial\Omega_{1}|+C_{Lip}(\Omega)\epsilon|\partial\Omega_{1}\cap(\partial\Omega\setminus\partial\mathscr{R})|

(4.2)

\displaystyle\leq C_{Lip}(\Omega)\epsilon|\partial\Omega_{1}|.

Recall also that
r_{in}(\Omega_{1})
denotes the inner radius of
\Omega_{1}
.

Since
\Omega\cup\Omega_{1}\subset\mathscr{R}=\mathscr{R}_{n-1}\times I_{min}
and
|\Omega\cup\Omega_{1}|=|\mathscr{R}|
, we have via the monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues for any
\lambda>0
that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Omega\cup\Omega_{1}}^{D}(\lambda)=\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)+\mathcal{N}_{\Omega_{1}}^{D}(\lambda)\leq\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{R}}^{D}(\lambda).

Applying
Step 1
to
\Omega_{1}
, (
4
) and Theorem
2.3
, we find that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\Omega}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{N}_{\mathscr{R}}^{D}(\lambda)-\mathcal{N}_{\Omega_{1}}^{D}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}||I_{min}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\omega(n)|\Omega_{1}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}+\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)\omega(n)|\partial\Omega_{1}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(\epsilon\lambda^{1/2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10r_{in}(\Omega_{1})}{\epsilon}\right),

for
0<\epsilon<r_{in}(\Omega_{1})
.

Since

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathscr{R})>\frac{\pi^{2}}{|I_{min}|^{2}},

we see that

\frac{\pi r_{in}(\Omega_{1})}{|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(\Omega)}}<r_{in}(\Omega_{1}).

So for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
we can let

\epsilon=\frac{\pi r_{in}(\Omega_{1})}{|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}

and get that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial\Omega_{1}|\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\Omega_{1})}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)-C_{1}(n-1)|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

We next sketch the proof of Corollary
1.6
.

Proof of Corollary
1.6
.

If
\Omega\subset{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{n}}
is a convex domain, then obviously

|\Omega_{\epsilon}|=\left|\{x\in\Omega:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\epsilon\}\right|\leq\epsilon|\partial\Omega|,

i.e.,
C_{Lip}(\Omega)=1
in
Step 1
above.

For
Step 2
, when dealing with the complementary set
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
,
one can carry out a Whitney decomposition of the domain
\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
, w.r.t. to the boundary

\partial\Omega
, see Figure
3
.

Figure 3:
Whitney decomposition for the complementary of a convex set

The convexity of
\mathscr{R}
and
\Omega
would imply that

\left|\{x\in\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial\Omega)<\epsilon\}\right|\leq|\partial\mathscr{R}\setminus\partial\Omega|\epsilon.

Applying this estimate to
Step 2
above gives that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(|\partial\mathscr{R}\setminus\partial\Omega|\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)-C_{1}(n-1)|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

We now prove the
\epsilon
-loss version of Pólya’s conjecture for large eigenvalues.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Let
\Omega_{1}=\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega}
. By Theorem
1.5
, it holds for each
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\left(C_{Lip}(\Omega)|\partial(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})|\left(\frac{\pi r_{in}(\mathscr{R}\setminus\overline{\Omega})}{|I_{min}|}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10|I_{min}|\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)-C_{1}(n-1)|\mathscr{R}_{n-1}|\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}\left(1+\frac{C_{Lip}(\Omega)(2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|)}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}|\Omega|}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda_{k}(\Omega)}}{\pi}\right)\right).

Taking derivative of the function

f(\Lambda):=\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|}{\sqrt{\Lambda}|\Omega|}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda}}{\pi}\right),

we see that

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(\Lambda)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|}{2\Lambda^{3/2}|\Omega|}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda}}{\pi}\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad+\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|}{\sqrt{\Lambda}|\Omega|}\frac{5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}e}{2\Lambda}.

We have for each
\Lambda\geq\frac{1}{\mathrm{width}(\Omega)^{2}}
that

\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda}}{\pi}\geq\frac{10}{\pi}>e,

and therefore
f(\Lambda)
is monotone decreasing on
[\frac{1}{\mathrm{width}(\Omega)^{2}},\infty)
.

Note that for
\Lambda=\frac{1}{\mathrm{width}(\Omega)^{2}}
,

f(\Lambda)=\frac{(2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|)\mathrm{width}(\Omega)}{|\Omega|}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10}{\pi}\right)>2n,

\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}f(\Lambda)=0,

and
C_{Lip}(\Omega)\geq 1
.
So for any
\epsilon\in(0,1)
, we can let
\Lambda>\frac{1}{\mathrm{width}(\Omega)^{2}}
be such that

C_{Lip}(\Omega)\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}+|\partial\Omega|}{|\Omega|\sqrt{\Lambda}}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\Lambda}}{\pi}\right)=\epsilon,

and deduce for any
\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\geq\Lambda
that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq(1+\epsilon)\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}.

If
\Omega
is convex, then applying Corollary
1.6
gives that

\displaystyle k

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|\Omega|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda_{k}(\Omega)^{n/2}\left(1+\frac{2n\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)^{n-1}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}|\Omega|}\left(\frac{\pi}{2}-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{2n}+5n^{2}\pi\log_{2}\frac{10\mathrm{width}(\Omega)\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}{\pi}\right)\right).

The remaining proof is the same. The proof is complete.
∎

For open sets, we also have a uniform estimate.
Let us remark that, since the boundary of an open set may be complicate, in general
one can not have a uniform estimate in term of its boundary as in Theorem
1.5
; see
[
9
, Theorem 3.14]
for similar but earlier result.

Note that
C_{1}(n-1)
is defined via (
1.11
).

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
be a bounded open domain,
n\geq 2
, let
Q
be a cube that contains
\Omega
.
For any
0<\epsilon<\ell(Q)/2
, it holds for all
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
that

(4.3)

\displaystyle k\leq\frac{|\Omega_{\epsilon}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}+\frac{\omega(n)\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(2n^{2}\pi\frac{|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{\epsilon}-C_{1}(n-1)\ell(Q)^{n-1}\right).

where
\Omega_{\epsilon}=\{x\in Q:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega)<\epsilon\}
. In particular, one can take
Q
to be of side length
\mathrm{diam}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

Since
\epsilon<\ell(Q)/2
, we can divide
\mathscr{R}
into the union of cubes
Q_{j}
, which are cubes of side length
2^{-k}\ell(Q)
, where
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{+}
is such that

(4.4)

\displaystyle\epsilon/2<2^{-k}\ell(Q)\sqrt{n}\leq\epsilon.

Denote by
\tilde{Q}
the union of all
Q_{j}
, such that
Q_{j}
entirely locates in
Q\setminus\overline{\Omega}
.
Then the number of
Q_{j}
that composes
\tilde{Q}
is controlled by

\#\{Q_{j}:\,Q_{j}\subset\tilde{Q}\}\leq\frac{|Q\setminus\Omega|}{2^{-kn}\ell(Q)^{n}}.

On the other hand,
\Omega\subset\mathscr{R}\setminus\tilde{Q}
, which is further contained in

\{x\in Q:\,\mathrm{dist}(x,\Omega)<2^{-k}\ell(Q)\sqrt{n}\}\subset\Omega_{\epsilon}.

By Theorem
2.3
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|Q|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}.

This together with the monotonicity of Dirichlet eigenvalues implies that

\displaystyle\frac{|Q|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)_{+}

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q}(\lambda)\geq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q\setminus\tilde{Q}}(\lambda)+\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\tilde{Q}}(\lambda)

(4.5)

\displaystyle\geq\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)+\frac{|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{2^{-kn}\ell(Q)^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q_{j}}(\lambda).

Since for each
Q_{j}
, by Lemma
3.3
and Lemma
3.6
, it holds that

\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q_{j}}(\lambda)\geq\frac{\omega(n)2^{-kn}\ell(Q)^{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{n^{3/2}\pi\omega(n)2^{-k(n-1)}\ell(Q)^{n-1}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}},

we see that

\displaystyle\frac{|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{2^{-kn}|I_{min}|^{n}}\mathcal{N}^{D}_{Q_{j}}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\omega(n)|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}-\frac{\omega(n)\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n^{3/2}\pi|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{2^{-k}|I_{min}|}.

This, together with (
4.4
), (
4
), implies that for

\lambda>\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geq\lambda_{1}(Q),

it holds that

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}^{D}_{\Omega}(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|Q|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}\left(1-\frac{C_{1}(n-1)}{\ell(Q)\sqrt{\lambda}}\right)-\frac{\omega(n)|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}+\frac{\omega(n)\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\frac{n^{3/2}\pi|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{2^{-k}\ell(Q)}

\displaystyle=\frac{|\Omega_{\epsilon}|\omega(n)}{(2\pi)^{n}}\lambda^{n/2}+\frac{\omega(n)\lambda^{\frac{n-1}{2}}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\left(2n^{2}\pi\frac{|Q\setminus\Omega_{\epsilon}|}{\epsilon}-C_{1}(n-1)\ell(Q)^{n-1}\right).

The proof is complete.
∎

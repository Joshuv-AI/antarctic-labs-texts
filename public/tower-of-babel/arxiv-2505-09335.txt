Boundedness and norm of certain p p -adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type operators Thanks: The authors were supported by National Natural Science Foundation of China (Grant Nos. 11501157).
Jianjun Jin, Huabing Li

Abstract
. In this paper, by introducing some parameters, we define and study certain p p -adic Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya-type integral operators acting on p p -adic weighted Lebesgue spaces. We completely characterize L q − L r L^{q}-L^{r} boundedness of these operators for all ( q , r ) ∈ [ 1 , ∞ ] × [ 1 , ∞ ] (q,r)\in[1,\infty]\times[1,\infty] . For some special cases, we obtain sharp norm estimates for the operators. These results are not only a complement to some previous results but also an extension of existing ones in the literature.

1.
Introduction and main results

Let
q\geq 1
, we denote the conjugate of
q
by
q^{\prime}
, i.e.
\frac{1}{q}+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}=1
. In particular,
q^{\prime}=\infty
when
q=1
. Let
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
. Let
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the class of all real-valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and let
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be the usual Lebesgue space on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, i.e.,

L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}):\|f\|_{q}=(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|f(x)|^{q}dx)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\}.

When
q>1
. If
f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),g\in L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
, then we have the following famous Hardy-Littlewood-Pólya (
HLP
) inequality

(1.1)

\Bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)g(y)}{\max\{x,y\}}dxdy\Bigg|\leq(q+q^{\prime})\|f\|_{q}\|g\|_{q^{\prime}},

where the constant
q+q^{\prime}
in (
1.1
) is the best possible, see
[
9
]
. (
1.1
) has the following equivalent form

(1.2)

\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(y)}{\max\{x,y\}}dy\right|^{q}dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq(q+q^{\prime})\|f\|_{q}.

(
1.2
) is also called
HLP
inequality and the constant
q+q^{\prime}
in (
1.2
) is still the best possible.

We can restate the
HLP
inequality (
1.2
) in the language of operator theory. We define
HLP
integral operator
H
induced by
HLP
kernel
\frac{1}{\max\{x,y\}}
as

Hf(y):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{f(x)}{\max\{x,y\}}dx,\>f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),\>y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q>1
. Let
H
be as above. Then
H
is bounded on
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
and the norm
\|H\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}
of
H
is
q+q^{\prime}
. Here

\|H\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}=\sup_{f\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\frac{\|Hf\|_{q}}{\|f\|_{q}}.

There is a discrete version for the above result. For a sequence
a=\{a_{m}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}
, we define
HLP
operator
\mathcal{H}
as

{\mathcal{H}}a(n):=\sum_{m=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{m}}{\max\{m,n\}},\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

We use
l^{q}
to denote the space of sequences of real numbers, i.e.,

l^{q}:=\{a=\{a_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}:\|a\|_{q}=\Big(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|a_{n}|^{q}\Big)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\}.

Then we have

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
q>1
. Let
\mathcal{H}
be as above. Then
\mathcal{H}
is bounded on
l^{q}
and the norm
\|\mathcal{H}\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}}
of
\mathcal{H}
is
q+q^{\prime}
. Here

\|\mathcal{H}\|_{l^{p}\rightarrow l^{p}}=\sup_{a\in l^{q}}\frac{\|\mathcal{H}a\|_{q}}{\|a\|_{q}}.

To state our theorems, we first recall some basic definitions and notations on
p
-adic analysis. We refer the reader to
[
18
]
or
[
16
]

p
-adic analysis.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
p
a prime number. Let
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
be the field of
p
-adic numbers, which is defined as the completion
of the field of rational numbers
\mathbb{Q}
with respect to the non-Archimedean
p
-adic norm
|\cdot|_{p}
.
The
p
-adic norm is defined as follows: (
1)

|0|_{p}=0
; (
2)
If any non-zero rational number
x
is represented as

x=p^{\gamma}\frac{m}{n}
, where
\gamma\in\mathbb{Z}
,
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
m
and
n
are not divisible by
p
, then

|x|_{p}=p^{-\gamma}
. Any non-zero
p
-adic number
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
can be
uniquely represented in the following canonical form

(1.3)

x=p^{\gamma}\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}a_{j}p^{j},\quad\gamma=\gamma(x)\in\mathbb{Z},

where
a_{j}
are integers with
0\leq a_{j}\leq p-1,a_{0}\neq 0
. Since
|a_{j}p^{j}|_{p}=p^{-j}
, the series (
1.3
) converges in the
p
-adic norm.

Also, it is not hard to show that the norm satisfies the following properties:

|xy|_{p}=|x|_{p}|y|_{p},\quad|x+y|_{p}\leq\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}.

It follows that, if
|x|_{p}\neq|y|_{p}
, then
|x+y|_{p}=\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}
.

We set
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}:=\mathbb{Q}_{p}\backslash\{0\}
and denote by

B_{\gamma}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|y-x|_{p}\leq p^{\gamma}\},

the ball with center at
x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}
and radius
p^{\gamma}
, and

S_{\gamma}(x)=\{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}:|y-x|_{p}=p^{\gamma}\}=B_{\gamma}(x)\backslash B_{\gamma-1}(x).

For simplicity, we use
B_{\gamma}
and
S_{\gamma}
to denote
B_{\gamma}(0)
and
S_{\gamma}(0)
, respectively.

Since
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
is a locally compact Hausdorff space, then, by the standard theory of measure, there exists a Haar measure
dx
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
, which is
unique up to positive constant multiple and is translation invariant. We normalize the measure
dx
by the equality

\int_{B_{0}}dx=|B_{0}|_{H}=1,

where
|E|_{H}
denotes the Haar measure of a measurable subset
E
of
\mathbb{Q}_{p}
. A simple calculation yields that

\int_{B_{\gamma}}dx=|B_{\gamma}|_{H}=p^{\gamma},\int_{S_{\gamma}}dx=|S_{\gamma}|_{H}=p^{\gamma}(1-p^{-1}).

We use
\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})
to denote the class of all real-valued measurable functions on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
. Let
1\leq q<\infty
,
w(x)
be a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
, we define the weighted Lebesgue space

L_{w}^{q}:=L_{w}^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
as

L_{w}^{q}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}):||f||_{q,w}=\left[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|f(x)|^{q}w(x)dx\right]^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty\}.

When
w(x)\equiv 1
, we will write
L^{q}
and
\|f\|_{q}
instead of
L_{w}^{q}
and
\|f\|_{q,w}
, respectively.
When
w(x)=|x|_{p}^{\theta}
,
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, we will write
L_{\theta}^{q}
instead of
L_{w}^{q}
and use
\|f\|_{q,\theta}
to denote
\|f\|_{q,w}
. The class
L^{\infty}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})
on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
is defined as

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})=\{f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}):\|f\|_{\infty}:=\mathop{\text{ess sup}}\limits_{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|f(x)|<\infty\}.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be real numbers. For
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})
, we define
HLP
-type operator
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
as

(1.4)

H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f(y):=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}f(x)dx,\,\,\,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}.

On the boundeness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
for
1\leq q\leq r\leq\infty
, we shall prove the following theorems.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
1\leq q\leq r<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as above. Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from

L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
-r\nu<\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu),\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)<\alpha+1<q(\mu+1).\end{cases}

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as above. Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from

L_{\alpha}^{1}
to
L^{\infty}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu-\alpha,\\
\mu-\lambda\leq\alpha\leq\mu,\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu-\alpha,\\
0\leq\nu\leq\lambda.\end{cases}

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
1<q<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as above. Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from

L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L^{\infty}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)<\alpha+1<q(\mu+1),\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
0<\nu<\lambda.\end{cases}

Next, for some special cases, we shall give sharp estimates for the norm of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as above.
Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{q}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{q},\\
-q\nu<\beta+1<q(\lambda-\nu),\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta-\alpha}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)<\alpha+1<q(\mu+1).\end{cases}

Moreover, when
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{q}
, the norm of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is given by

\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{L_{\alpha}^{q}\rightarrow L_{\beta}^{q}}=(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{\mu+1-\frac{1}{q}(\alpha+1)}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\nu+\frac{1}{q}(\beta+1)}-1}\Big].

Here,

\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{L_{\alpha}^{q}\rightarrow L_{\beta}^{q}}=\sup_{f\in L_{\alpha}^{q}}\frac{\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{q,\beta}}{\|f\|_{q,\alpha}}.

Remark 1.7
.

Theorem
1.6
extend some related results in
[
8
]
, where the special case for
\lambda=1,\mu=\nu=0,\alpha=\beta
in Theorem
1.6
has been established by Fu et. al.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as above. Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded on
L^{\infty}
if and only if

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1,\\
0<\nu+1<\lambda,\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1,\\
0<\mu<\lambda.\end{cases}

Moreover, when
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded on
L^{\infty}
, the norm of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is given by

\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{\infty}}=(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{\nu+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\lambda-\nu-1}-1}\Big].

Here,

\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}\rightarrow L^{\infty}}=\sup_{f\in L^{\infty}}\frac{\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{\infty}}{\|f\|_{\infty}}.

2.
Some lemmas

To prove our main results of this paper, in this section, we recall some known lemmas and establish some new ones. We first recall the following two lemmas, see
[
17
, Problem 5.5]
for their general forms.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
1\leq q<\infty
. Let
K(x,y)
be a non-negative measurable function on
\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}\times\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
. For
f\in\mathcal{M}(\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*})
, let
T
be the integral operator with kernel
K
defined as

Tf(y)=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}K(x,y)f(x)dx,\,\,\,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}.

Then
T
is bounded from
L^{q}
to
L^{1}
if and only if

\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}K(x,y)dy\in L^{q^{\prime}}.

Moreover, when
T
is bounded from
L^{q}
to
L^{1}
, the norm of
T
is given by

\|T\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{1}}=\|\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}K(x,y)dy\|_{q^{\prime}}.

Remark 2.2
.

In particular, when
q=1
so that
q^{\prime}=\infty
,
T
is bounded on
L^{1}
if and only if

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}K(x,y)dy<\infty.

Moreover, when
T
is bounded on
L^{1}
, the norm of
T
is given by

\|T\|_{L^{1}\rightarrow L^{1}}=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}K(x,y)dy.

The following is a dual version of Lemma
2.1
.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
a,\lambda
be two real numbers. Let
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
. Then the integral

I(y)=\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{a}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dx,

converges if and only if
a>-1
and
\lambda-a-1>0
. When the integral converges, we have

I(y)=(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{a+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\lambda-a-1}-1}\Big]|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda}.

Proof.

For
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
, let
|y|_{p}=p^{-\gamma_{y}}
, we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle I(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{-\lambda}\int_{|x|_{p}\leq|y|_{p}}|x|_{p}^{a}dx+\int_{|x|_{p}>|y|_{p}}|x|_{p}^{a-\lambda}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{-\lambda}(1-p^{-1})\sum_{-\infty<\gamma\leq-{\gamma}_{y}}p^{\gamma(a+1)}+(1-p^{-1})\sum_{-\gamma_{y}<\gamma<\infty}p^{\gamma(a-\lambda+1)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{-\lambda}(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\gamma={\gamma}_{y}}^{\infty}p^{-\gamma(a+1)}+(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\gamma=-\gamma_{y}+1}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(a-\lambda+1)}.

Then we conclude that
I(y)<\infty
if and only if
a+1>0
and
\lambda-a-1>0
. Moreover, when
a+1>0
and
\lambda-a-1>0
, it follows from (
2.1
) that

\displaystyle I(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\Big[|y|_{p}^{-\lambda}\frac{p^{-{\gamma_{y}}(a+1)}}{1-p^{-(a+1)}}+\frac{p^{(-\gamma_{y}+1)(a+1-\lambda)}}{1-p^{a+1-\lambda}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\Big[\frac{|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda}}{1-p^{-(a+1)}}+\frac{|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda}p^{a+1-\lambda}}{1-p^{a+1-\lambda}}\Big]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{a+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\lambda-a-1}-1}\Big]|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda}.

This proves the lemma.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

When
I(y)
converges, by a change of variables, it is easy to see that
I(y)
has the following form

\displaystyle I(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|t|_{p}^{a}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{a+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\lambda-a-1}-1}\Big]|y|_{p}^{a+1-\lambda},\,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}.

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
\lambda>0
. Let
a,b
be real numbers. Then there is a constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{a}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}\leq C|y|_{p}^{b},

for all
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
, if
\lambda=a-b
and
a\geq 0,b\leq 0.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that, if
\lambda=a-b
and
a\geq 0,b\leq 0
, then

\mathcal{S}=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{a}|y|_{p}^{-b}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}<\infty.

Actually, when
\lambda=a-b
and
a\geq 0,b\leq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{1}:=\sup_{0<|x_{p}|\leq|y|_{p}<\infty}\frac{|x|_{p}^{a}|y|_{p}^{-b}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}=\sup_{0<|x_{p}\leq|y|_{p}<\infty}\Big(\frac{|x|_{p}}{|y|_{p}}\Big)^{a}<\infty,

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{2}:=\sup_{0<|y|_{p}<|x|_{p}<\infty}\frac{|x|_{p}^{a}|y|_{p}^{-b}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}=\sup_{0<|y|_{p}<|x|_{p}<\infty}\Big(\frac{|y|_{p}}{|x|_{p}}\Big)^{-b}<\infty.

The lemma then follows from the fact that
\mathcal{S}=\max\{\mathcal{S}_{1},\mathcal{S}_{2}\}
.
∎

The following lemma provides the necessary conditions for the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let
1\leq q,r<\infty
. Let
\lambda,\mu,\nu,\alpha,\beta
be real numbers and
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
be as in (
1.4
). If
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from

L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
, then we have

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
-r\nu<\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu),\end{cases}

or equivalently,

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)<\alpha+1<q(\mu+1).\end{cases}

Proof.

We let

\tau:=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\lambda.

We suppose that
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
.
If
\tau<0
, for
\varepsilon>0
, we let

(2.2)

f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}(x)=\begin{cases}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}},\,\,{\text{when}}\,0<|x|_{p}\leq 1,\\
0,\quad\quad\quad\quad\,\,\,{\text{when}}\,|x|_{p}>1.\end{cases}

Then we have

\|f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{q,\alpha}^{q}=\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\varepsilon}}.

From the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
, we know that, for any
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
,

H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}(y)=\int_{|x|_{p}\leq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{1+\alpha}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx<\infty.

Consequently, when
|y|_{p}\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f^{[1]}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{\nu-\lambda}\int_{|x|_{p}\leq 1}|x|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{\nu-\lambda}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{-\gamma(\mu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q})}<\infty.

This implies that
\mu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}>0
for any
\varepsilon>0
so that

\mu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}\geq 0.

Meanwhile, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}\left[\int_{0<|x|_{p}\leq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{-1+\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau}\left[\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq|y|_{p}^{-1}}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|_{p}\leq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1+\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau}\left[\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq 1}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon-r\tau)}\left[\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq 1}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}.

Hence when
\varepsilon<-q\tau
, we obtain that

\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon-r\tau)}=\infty,

so that
\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}=\infty
, which contradicts the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
. This implies that
\tau\geq 0
.

Furthermore, if
\tau>0
, for
\varepsilon>0
, we take

(2.3)

{f}^{[2]}_{\varepsilon}(x)=\begin{cases}0,\qquad\quad\;\;\;\;\,\text{when}\;0<|x|_{p}<1,\\
|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}},\;\text{when}\;|x|_{p}\geq 1.\\
\end{cases}

Then

\|f_{\varepsilon}^{[2]}\|_{q,\alpha}^{q}=\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\varepsilon}},

It follows from the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
that, for any
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
,

H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[2]}(y)=\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx<\infty.

Consequently, when
|y|_{p}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f^{[2]}(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{\nu}\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}|x|_{p}^{\mu-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|y|_{p}^{\nu}\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(\mu+1-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q})}<\infty.

This implies that
\mu+1-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}<0
for any
\varepsilon>0
so that

\mu+1-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}\leq 0,

and

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[2]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}\left[\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{-1-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau}\left[\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|_{p}\geq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau}\left[\int_{|t|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau)}\left[\int_{|t|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}.

Thus when
\varepsilon<q\tau
, we obtain that

\sum_{\gamma=0}^{\infty}p^{\gamma(-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau)}=\infty,

so that
\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}=\infty
, which contradicts to the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
. This means that
\tau
can only take value
0
.

Collecting all above arguments, we have proved that, if
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
, then

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)\leq\alpha+1\leq q(\mu+1).\end{cases}

Finally, to finish the proof, we have to prove that, if

(2.4)

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
\alpha+1=q(\mu+1),\end{cases}

or

(2.5)

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\
q(\mu+1-\lambda)=\alpha+1.\end{cases}

Then the operator
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
can not be bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
.

First, if (
2.4
) holds, for
\varepsilon>0
, take
f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}
as in (
2.2
), then we obtain that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}\left[\int_{0<|x|_{p}\leq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{-1+\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon}\left[\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq|y|_{p}^{-1}}\frac{|t|_{p}^{-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|_{p}\geq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1+\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon}\left[\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt\right]^{r}dy.

Meanwhile, for
|y|_{p}\geq 1
, let
|y|_{p}=p^{-\gamma_{y}}
, we have

\int_{0<|t|_{p}\leq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{-1+\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt=(1-p^{-1})\sum_{-\infty<\gamma\leq\gamma_{y}}p^{\gamma\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}=\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}|y|_{p}^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}\geq\Big[\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}\Big]^{r}\int_{|y|_{p}\geq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1}dy=\infty.

This means that
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is unbounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
when (
2.4
) holds.

Second, if (
2.5
) holds, for
\varepsilon>0
, take
f_{\varepsilon}^{[2]}
as in (
2.3
), then we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[2]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}\left[\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dx\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{-1-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon+r\tau}\left[\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}\frac{|t|_{p}^{\mu-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}{[\max\{1,|t|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}dt\right]^{r}dy

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\int_{|y|_{p}\leq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1-\frac{r}{q}\varepsilon}\left[\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{\mu-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt\right]^{r}dy.

Furthermore, for
|y|_{p}\leq 1
, let
|y|_{p}=p^{-\gamma_{y}}
, from (
2.5
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{\mu-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{\mu-\lambda-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{|t|_{p}\geq|y|_{p}^{-1}}|t|_{p}^{-1-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1-p^{-1})\sum_{\gamma_{y}<\gamma<\infty}p^{-\gamma\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}=\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}.

Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f_{\varepsilon}^{[1]}\|_{r,\beta}^{r}\geq\Big[\frac{1-p^{-1}}{1-p^{-\frac{\varepsilon}{q}}}\Big]^{r}\int_{|y|_{p}\leq 1}|y|_{p}^{-1}dy=\infty,

so that
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is unbounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
in this case. Now, we complete the proof of Lemma
2.8
.
∎

3.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, for the sake of simplicity, we will write

k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)=\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}.

Note that the necessity of the boundedness of
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
has been proved by Lemma
2.8
. We only need to prove that, for
1\leq q\leq r<\infty
, if

(3.1)

\lambda=\mu+\nu+1+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},

and

(3.2)

-r\nu<\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu).

Then
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
. First, we observe from (
3.1
) that (
3.2
) is equivalent to the following inequality

(3.3)

q(\mu+1-\lambda)<\alpha+1<q(\mu+1).

It follows from the left part of (
3.2
) and the right part of (
3.3
) that

\lambda=\Big(\nu+\frac{\beta+1}{r}\Big)+\Big(\mu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q}\Big)>0.

We will divide our proof into the following two cases.

3.1.
Case I.
1<q\leq r<\infty

As
\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu)
, i.e.,
r(\lambda-\nu)-\beta-1>0
, then we can find a constant
t>1
such that

r(\lambda-\nu)-\beta-1>\frac{r\lambda}{t}.

Let
s
be such that
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}=1
. It follows that

(3.4)

-\frac{\beta+1}{p}-\nu+\frac{\lambda}{s}>0.

From the left part of (
3.2
), we have

-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu<0
so that

(3.5)

-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s}<-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s}.

Meanwhile, we see from (
3.4
) and
\lambda>0
that

(3.6)

-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s}<-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}<-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s},

and

(3.7)

-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s}<-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\lambda.

Consequently, in view of (
3.5
), (
3.6
), (
3.7
), we can take a constant
A
such that

(3.8)

-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}<A<-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\lambda}{s},

and

(3.9)

-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{\mu}{s}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\frac{\lambda}{s}<A<-\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\nu-\frac{\mu}{s}-\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}+\lambda.

It is easy to see that (
3.8
) is equivalent to

(3.10)

\begin{cases}\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}+Aq^{\prime}>-1,\\
\frac{\lambda}{s}q^{\prime}-\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}-Aq^{\prime}-1>0,\end{cases}

and (
3.9
) is equivalent to

(3.11)

\begin{cases}\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta>-1,\\
\frac{\lambda}{t}r-\frac{\nu}{t}r-B-\beta-1>0.\end{cases}

Here

B=r[\frac{\nu}{s}+\frac{\mu}{s}+A+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\lambda}{s}].

Now, for
0\leq f\in L_{\alpha}^{q}
, we have, for
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
,

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{s}}x^{A}\cdot[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{t}}x^{-A}f(x)dx.

By Hölder’s inequality, we obtain that

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f(y)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q^{\prime}}{s}}x^{Aq^{\prime}}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big\{[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}+Aq^{\prime}}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{s}q^{\prime}}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{s}q^{\prime}}}dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big\}^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{1}^{{}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}}{\bf I}_{2}^{{}^{\frac{1}{q}}}.

By (
3.10
) and Lemma
2.5
, we see that

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}+Aq^{\prime}+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\lambda}{s}q^{\prime}-\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}-Aq^{\prime}-1}-1}\Big]|y|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}+\frac{\nu}{s}q^{\prime}+Aq^{\prime}+1-\frac{\lambda}{s}q^{\prime}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{s}q^{\prime}+\frac{\nu}{s}q^{\prime}+Aq^{\prime}+1-\frac{\lambda}{s}q^{\prime}}.

Consequently, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}{\bf I}_{1}^{{}^{\frac{r}{q^{\prime}}}}{\bf I}_{2}^{{}^{\frac{r}{q}}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{r(\frac{\mu}{s}+\frac{\nu}{s}+A+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\lambda}{s})}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\times\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big\}^{\frac{r}{q}}|y|_{p}^{\beta}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{B+\beta}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big\}^{\frac{r}{q}}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}.

Then it follows from Minkowski’s inequality that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|y|_{p}^{B+\beta}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{q}{t}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big\}^{\frac{r}{q}}dy\Big]^{\frac{q}{r}\cdot\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{r}{t}}|y|_{p}^{B+\beta}dy\Big\}^{\frac{q}{r}}|x|_{p}^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\Big\{\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{t}r}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{t}r}}dy\Big\}^{\frac{q}{r}}|x|_{p}^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}{\bf I}_{3}^{\frac{q}{r}}x^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Moreover, by using (
3.11
) and again Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle{\bf I}_{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\lambda}{t}r-\frac{\nu}{t}r-B-\beta-1}-1}\Big]|x|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r+\frac{\mu}{t}r}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{2}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r+\frac{\mu}{t}r}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}{\bf C}_{2}^{\frac{1}{r}}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{q}{r}[\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r+\frac{\mu}{t}r]}|x|_{p}^{-Aq}f^{q}(x)dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q}}.

Meanwhile, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{q}{r}[\frac{\nu}{t}r+B+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r+\frac{\mu}{t}r]-Aq=q[\frac{\nu}{t}+\frac{B}{r}+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\lambda}{t}+\frac{\mu}{t}-A]

\displaystyle=q[\frac{\nu}{t}+(\frac{\mu+\nu}{s}+A+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{\lambda}{s})+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\frac{\lambda}{t}+\frac{\mu}{t}-A]

\displaystyle=q[\nu+\mu+\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\lambda+\frac{\beta+1}{r}]=q[\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-1+\frac{\alpha+1}{q}]=\alpha.

This means that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{1}^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}{\bf C}_{2}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{q,\alpha},

so that
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
in this case.

3.2.
Case II.
1=q\leq r<\infty

Recall that the following conditions are satisfied.

\lambda=\mu+\nu+\frac{\beta+1}{r}-\alpha,

and

-r\nu<\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu).

As
-r\nu<\beta+1
, then there is a constant
s>1
such that

(3.12)

\beta+1>-r\nu+\frac{r\lambda}{s}.

We now let
t>1
be such that
\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{t}=1
. Noting that
\beta+1<r(\lambda-\nu)
so that

(3.13)

\frac{\nu-\lambda}{s}<\frac{\lambda-\nu}{t}-\frac{\beta+1}{r},

and from (
3.12
) we have

(3.14)

-\frac{\nu}{t}-\frac{\beta+1}{r}<\frac{\nu-\lambda}{s}<\frac{\nu}{s}.

Then we see from (
3.13
) and (
3.14
) that we can take a constant
D
such that

(3.15)

-\frac{\nu}{t}-\frac{\beta+1}{r}<D<\frac{\lambda-\nu}{t}-\frac{\beta+1}{r},

and

(3.16)

\frac{\nu-\lambda}{s}<D<\frac{\nu}{s}.

Consequently, it is easy to see that (
3.15
) is equivalent to

(3.17)

\begin{cases}r\frac{\nu}{t}+rD+\beta+1>0,\\
r\frac{\lambda-\nu}{t}-rD-\beta-1>0,\end{cases}

and (
3.16
) is equivalent to

(3.18)

\begin{cases}\frac{\lambda-\nu}{s}+D>0,\\
\frac{\nu}{s}-D>0.\end{cases}

Hence we see from (
3.18
) and Lemma
2.7
that there is a constant
{\bf C}_{3}>0
such that

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\frac{\lambda-\nu}{s}+D}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{s}}}\leq{\bf C}_{3}|y|_{p}^{D-\frac{\nu}{s}},

for all
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
. It follows that, for
0\leq f\in L_{\alpha}^{1}
,
y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}
,

\displaystyle H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f(y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{s}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{t}}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\frac{\lambda-\nu}{s}+D}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{s}}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{s}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{t}}f(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}|y|_{p}^{D}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{t}}f(x)dx.

Consequently, we have

\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}\leq{\bf C}_{3}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{1}{t}}f(x)dx\Big)^{r}|y|_{p}^{rD+\beta}dy\Big]^{\frac{1}{r}}.

By using Minkowski’s inequality again, we obtain that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}[k_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}(x,y)]^{\frac{r}{t}}|y|_{p}^{rD+\beta}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\Big(\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{t}r}|y|_{p}^{\frac{\nu}{t}r+rD+\beta}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\frac{\lambda}{t}r}}dy\Big)^{\frac{1}{r}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}f(x)dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}{{\bf I}_{4}}^{\frac{1}{r}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}f(x)dx.

Moreover, from (
3.17
) and Lemma
2.5
, we have

\displaystyle{{\bf I}_{4}}={\bf C}_{4}|x|_{p}^{\frac{\mu}{t}r+\frac{\nu}{t}r+rD+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r}.

Here,

{\bf C}_{4}=(1-p^{-1})\Big[1+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\mu}{t}r+\frac{\nu}{t}r+rD+\beta+1}-1}+\frac{1}{p^{\frac{\lambda}{t}r-\frac{\mu}{t}-\frac{\nu}{t}r-rD-\beta}-1}\Big].

It follows that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}{\bf C}_{4}^{\frac{1}{r}}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}|x|_{p}^{\frac{1}{r}[\frac{\mu}{t}r+\frac{\nu}{t}r+rD+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r]+\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D}f(x)dx.

Meanwhile, we note that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{r}[\frac{\mu}{t}r+\frac{\nu}{t}r+rD+\beta+1-\frac{\lambda}{t}r]+\frac{\mu+\nu-\lambda}{s}-D

\displaystyle=\mu+\nu-\lambda+\frac{\beta+1}{r}=\alpha.

This means that

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{r,\beta}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle{\bf C}_{3}{\bf C}_{4}^{\frac{1}{r}}\|f\|_{1,\alpha},

so that
H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}
is bounded from
L_{\alpha}^{q}
to
L_{\beta}^{r}
for
1=q\leq r<\infty
. This finishes the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Lemma 4.1

\tau=\mu+\nu-\alpha-\lambda.

If
\tau<0

\varepsilon>0

\displaystyle\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}|x|_{p}^{-\alpha-1-\varepsilon}dx=|y|_{p}^{\nu}\int_{|x|_{p}\geq 1}|x|_{p}^{\mu-\lambda-\alpha-1-\varepsilon}dx\leq{\bf C}_{6}.

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu-\alpha,\\

Proof of Theorem

We first prove the necessity part‌ of Theorem

L_{\alpha}^{q}

\begin{cases}\lambda=\mu+\nu+1-\frac{\alpha+1}{q},\\

1.5
. We will show that, if (

to

. When

\displaystyle\|H_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}f\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{\mu}|y|_{p}^{\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{\lambda}}f(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sup_{y\in\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\|f\|_{q,\alpha}\Big[\int_{\mathbb{Q}_{p}^{*}}\frac{|x|_{p}^{q^{\prime}(\mu-\alpha)+\alpha}|y|_{p}^{q^{\prime}\nu}}{[\max\{|x|_{p},|y|_{p}\}]^{q^{\prime}\lambda}}dx\Big]^{\frac{1}{q^{\prime}}}={\bf C}_{8}\|f\|_{q,\alpha}.

This means that

to

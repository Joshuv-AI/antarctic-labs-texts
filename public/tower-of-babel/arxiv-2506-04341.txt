Pólya’s conjecture on 𝕊 1 × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
Pedro Freitas, Rui Zhu Wang

Abstract
. We study the area ranges where the two possible isoperimetric domains on the infinite cylinder 𝕊 1 × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R} , namely, geodesic disks and cylindrical strips of the form 𝕊 1 × [ 0 , h ] \mathbb{S}^{1}\times[0,h] , satisfy Pólya’s conjecture. In the former case, we provide an upper bound on the maximum value of the radius for which the conjecture may hold, while in the latter we fully characterise the values of h h for which it does hold for these strips. As a consequence, we determine a necessary and sufficient condition for the isoperimetric domain on 𝕊 1 × ℝ \mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R} corresponding to a given area to satisfy Pólya’s conjecture. In the case of the cylindrical strip, we also provide a necessary and sufficient condition for the Li-Yau inequalities to hold.

1.
Introduction

Pólya’s conjecture for the Dirichlet Laplacian on a domain states that all its eigenvalues are greater than or equal to the first term in the corresponding Weyl asymptotics. It was formulated by Pólya in the first edition of
[
P1
]
in 1954, who then proved it in 1961 for the case of domains that tile the plane
[
P2
]
.
Apart from the direct extension of this result to higher-dimensional Euclidean domains by Urakawa
[
U
]
, the conjecture is only known to hold for certain Cartesian products
[
L
,
FS
,
HW
]
, for
Euclidean balls and sectors
[
FLPS
]
, and for certain families of domains tiling a given domain
[
FS
,
FLP
]
. The result in
[
FS
]
essentially states that if a domain
\Omega
satisfies certain geometric conditions and may
be tiled by an arbitrary number of
p
equal isometric copies of a domain
\Omega_{p}
, then there exists
p_{0}

large enough such that
\Omega_{p}
satisfies Pólya’s conjecture for all
p
greater than or equal to
p_{0}
.
This result is essentially qualitative, but, on the other hand, it is not restricted to Euclidean domains, thus
showing that the conjecture may also be satisfied for certain families of domains on
manifolds. To the best of our knowledge and until then, for the Dirichlet problem on non-Euclidean domains this
had only been known to hold for the two-dimensional hemisphere
[
BB
,
BLPS
,
FMS
]
.

One of the purposes of the present paper is to contribute to the understanding of what differences may be expected
when considering Pólya’s conjecture on manifolds. To illustrate this behaviour, one of the examples given in
[
FS
]

is that of a cylindrical surface of the form
\mathcal{C}_{h}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
, where
I_{h}
is an open interval of length
h
,
and for which it is concluded that Pólya’s conjecture is satisfied provided
h
is sufficiently small

[
FS
, Example III.D]
. On the other hand, a simple argument based on the behaviour of the first eigenvalue was
presented there showing that for large enough
h
this will no longer be the case – see below and also
Section
2.1
. This shows that topological issues may play a role, with the consequence that
now tiling domains no longer necessarilly satisfy the conjecture, not even in a weaker form – see
Proposition
2.1
. As far as we can tell, the only instance where tiling domains had been
considered previously on cylindrical surfaces were first in a paper by Hersch, who also considered
domains on the torus
[
He
]
, and also in a paper by the first author
[
F
]
. In either case,
only a finite number of tiles were considered.

Apart from illustrating the different possible behaviours mentioned above, another purpose of the present article
is to provide an example of a quantitative version of the result in
[
FS
]
by characterising precisely the values
of
h
for which Pólya’s conjecture is satisfied for
\mathcal{C}_{h}
. In this case the conjecture reads as

\lambda_{k}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)\geq\frac{\displaystyle 4k\pi}{\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{C}_{h}\right|}=\frac{\displaystyle 2k}{\displaystyle h},

where
\lambda_{k}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)
and
\left|\mathcal{C}_{h}\right|
denote the
k^{\rm th}
Dirichlet eigenvalue
and the area of
\mathcal{C}_{h}
, respectively. Since the first eigenvalue is given by
\lambda_{1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)=\pi^{2}/h^{2}
, it is straightforward to see that this eigenvalue cannot satisfy the conjecture for
h
larger than

\pi^{2}/2
. However, it is not clear whether the conjecture holds for
all

h
smaller than or equal to this value and, in
fact, we will show that there are indeed two disjoint open intervals for smaller values of
h
where the conjecture
fails. More precisely, our main result is the following.

Theorem A
.

Pólya’s conjecture on
C_{h}=\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
is satisfied if and only if
h
belongs to

\left(0,8-\sqrt{64-4\pi^{2}}\right]\cup\left[2+\sqrt{4-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}},13-\sqrt{169-9\pi^{2}}\right]\cup\left[\frac{13}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{169}{16}-\pi^{2}},\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}\right].

On the intervals

\left(8-\sqrt{64-4\pi^{2}},2+\sqrt{4-\frac{\pi^{2}}{4}}\right)\approx(3.0481,3.2380)

and

\left(13-\sqrt{169-9\pi^{2}},\frac{13}{4}+\sqrt{\frac{169}{16}-\pi^{2}}\right)\approx(4.0460,4.0824)

the conjecture fails for
\lambda_{8}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)
and
\lambda_{13}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)
,
respectively, and only for these eigenvalues in each case. From
\pi^{2}/2
onwards the conjecture fails for

\lambda_{1}\left(\mathcal{C}_{h}\right)
and then for some higher eigenvalues as
h
is increased further.

As a direct corollary of this result, we have the precise values of the area for which the isoperimetric domains on
the infinite cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy Pólya’s inequality. For two-dimensional manifolds,
given a value for the area an isoperimetric domain is a domain with that area with the smallest possible perimeter.
In the case of the infinite cylinder, isoperimetric domains are either a geodesic disk for areas smaller than or
equal to
4\pi
, or, for larger areas, a cylindrical strip
\mathcal{C}_{h}
bounded by two parallel circles as described
above
[
HHM
]
.

Corollary B
.

For a given value of the area, the corresponding isoperimetric domains on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
satisfy Pólya’s conjecture if and only if this area is in the range

\begin{array}[]{ll}\left(0,2\pi\left(8-\sqrt{64-4\pi^{2}}\right)\right]\cup\left[2\pi\left(2+\sqrt{4-\frac{\displaystyle\pi^{2}}{\displaystyle 4}}\right),2\pi\left(13-\sqrt{169-9\pi^{2}}\right)\right]&\\
\hskip 170.71652pt\cup\left[2\pi\left(\frac{\displaystyle 13}{\displaystyle 4}+\sqrt{\frac{\displaystyle 169}{\displaystyle 16}-\pi^{2}}\right),\pi^{3}\right]\end{array}

Proof.

For a disk of area smaller than or equal to
4\pi
, where the transition between disks and strips takes place,
its radius is smaller than or equal to two, smaller than
\pi
, and we know that in this range Pólya’s conjecture
is satisfied for disks
[
FLPS
]
. Since this is within the range of the first interval where Pólya’s conjecture
holds for the strip, the result follows.
∎

Although as we have seen Pólya’s conjecture fails for some values of
h
below
\pi^{2}/2
, we actually have that
the corresponding averaged version compatible with Weyl asymptotics, known as the Li-Yau inequalities
[
LY
]
,
holds in this range. In fact, in this case the values of
h
for which this happens may be extended up to
\pi^{2}
.

Corollary C
.

The inequalities

(1.1)

\frac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}\geq\frac{k}{h},\quad k=1,2,\dots

hold for all
k
if and only if
h\in(0,\pi^{2}]
.

This result has implications for the satisfiability of Pólya’s conjecture for Cartesian products
involving
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
, such as

\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h_{1}}\times\dots\times I_{h_{m}}
, which will be briefly discussed in Section
4
.

The proof of Theorem
A
follows from a careful analysis of the integer lattice point counting problem associated
with the eigenvalues on
\mathcal{C}_{h}
. In Section
2
we derive estimates for the
eigenvalues which are sufficiently sharp to allow us to reduce the full problem to that of deciding whether or not
Pólya’s conjecture holds on a bounded set in the
(h,\lambda)-
plane. We then devise an algorithm, whose
implementation is given in Appendix
A
, to carry out a rigorous search within ths set for possible cases
where the conjecture fails. Section
3
addresses the proof of the Li-Yau inequalities, which follows
from the bounds for individual eigenvalues and two basic lemmas. In Section
4
, we consider some further
examples, including other domains in
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
such as
disks with radius larger than
\pi
, and higher-dimensional Cartesian products like
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}

and the already mentioned
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}
.

The specific values of
h
for which Pólya’s conjecture for
\mathcal{C}_{h}
holds or fails depend on several factors, some
of which are not obvious a priori. Two ingredients which might appear to be relevant at first sight are
simply-connectedness and the isoperimetric inequaltiy – for the latter, see
[
HHM
]
for the two-dimensional
cylinder and
[
P
]
for a study of the isoperimetric problem in infinite spherical cylinders.
More precisely, all the examples given here where Pólya’s conjecture fails are neither simply-connected nor do
they satisfy the Euclidean isoperimetric inequality. It
is also clear that simply-connected domains which tile
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
also tile
\mathbb{R}^{2}
and
will thus satisfy Pólya’s conjecture. However, should a non-simply connected planar domain satisfy the conjecture,
there will also be a copy, scaled down if necessary, which will satisfy it as a subset of
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
.
Moreover, by picking a strip
\mathcal{C}_{h}
with sufficiently large
h
so that its first eigenvalue does not satisfy the
conjecture, it will be possible to modify its boundary so that the first eigenvalue will continue not satisfying the
conjecture, while keeping the area fixed but making its perimeter to be as large as desired. We thus see that, in
general, it does not seem reasonable to expect a simple characterisation of domains on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}

satisfying Pólya’s conjecture. Based on the results in the paper, we will, however, conjecture that the area of
a domain may play a role and that for small enough domains the conjecture is sastisfied.

Conjecture 1.1
.

There exists a positive number
A_{0}
such that Pólya’s conjecture is satisfied for all bounded domains

\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\left|\Omega\right|\leq A_{0}
.

A possible quantitative version of the conjecture would take
A_{0}
to be the upper-end of the lowest
interval in Corollary
B
, namely,
A_{0}=2\pi\left(8-\sqrt{64-4\pi^{2}}\right)
.

2.
Precise intervals for the cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
: proof of Theorem
A

The Dirichlet problem on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
may be thought of as an eigenvalue problem on the rectangle
(0,2\pi)\times(0,h)

with Dirichlet boundary conditions on the sides
(0,2\pi)\times\{0\}
and
(0,2\pi)\times\{h\}
, and periodic boundary
conditions on the two remaining sides. The corresponding eigenvalues are thus of the form

(2.1)

\lambda=m^{2}+n^{2}\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}\quad m\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N}.

We denote by
N(\lambda)
the corresponding counting function, that is,

\begin{array}[]{lll}N(\lambda)&:=&\#\left\{\lambda_{i}:\lambda_{i}\leq\lambda\right\}\\
&=&\#\left\{(m,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{N}:m^{2}+n^{2}\frac{\displaystyle\pi^{2}}{\displaystyle h^{2}}\leq\lambda\right\}\end{array}

and want to estimate the order
k
of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
as a function of
\lambda
, that
is, to obtain bounds on
k=N(\lambda)
. In this setting, Pólya’s conjecture (in dimension two) states that

N(\lambda)\leq\lambda\frac{|\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}|}{4\pi}=\lambda\frac{h}{2}.

2.1.
Upper bound for Pólya’s satisfiability

We begin by extending the argument based on the first eigenvalue
used in
[
FS
]
which was mentioned in the Introduction to see that, in general, not only can Pólya’s conjecture
fail for domains in
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
, but that an inequality of this type cannot hold, even with a smaller constant.

From (
2.1
) we have

\lambda_{1}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}

and, given any positive constant
C
, by taking
h>\pi^{2}/(2C)
we have

N\left(\lambda_{1}\right)=1>\frac{\displaystyle\pi^{2}}{\displaystyle 2Ch}=\frac{\displaystyle h}{\displaystyle 2C}\lambda_{1}.

We have thus proved the following

Proposition 2.1
.

Given any positive constant
C
, there exist domains
\Omega
on the infinite cylinder
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times\mathbb{R}
for
which

\lambda_{1}<\frac{\displaystyle 4C\pi}{\displaystyle|\Omega|}.

In particular, Pólya’s conjecture is not satisfied on the cylindrical strip
\mathcal{C}_{h}
for
h
larger than
\pi^{2}/2
.

\begin{array}[]{lll}\displaystyle\int_{D_{R}}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x&=&\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle 6}\left[\frac{\displaystyle\pi\left(6+\pi^{2}\right)}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}+2\left(\pi^{2}-3\right)\arctan\left(\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}\right)\right].\end{array}

We thus obtain

\begin{array}[]{lll}\lambda_{1}\left(D_{R}\right)&\leq&\frac{\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{D}\left|\nabla u\right|^{2}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x}{\displaystyle\displaystyle\int_{D}u^{2}(x,y)\,{\rm d}y{\rm d}x}\\
&=&\frac{\displaystyle\frac{\displaystyle\pi\left(6+\pi^{2}\right)}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}+2\left(\pi^{2}-3\right)\arctan\left(\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}\right)}{\displaystyle 6\left[\pi\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}+R^{2}\arctan\left(\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}\right)\right]}.\end{array}

Combining this expression with
4\pi/\left|D_{R}\right|
we obtain that for values of
R
larger
than
\pi
for which the quantity on the right-hand side of the inequality

\begin{array}[]{lll}\frac{\displaystyle 4\pi}{\displaystyle\left|D_{R}\right|}-\lambda_{1}\left(D_{R}\right)&\geq&\frac{\displaystyle\pi\left(12\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}-\pi^{2}-6\right)-2\left(\pi^{2}-3\right)\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}\arctan\left(\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}\right)}{\displaystyle 6\left[\pi(R^{2}-\pi^{2})+\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}R^{2}\arctan\left(\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}}\right)\right]}\end{array}

is positive, the first eigenvalue of
D_{R}
does not satisfy Pólya’s conjecture. It is readily seen that
the denominator above is positive for
R
larger than
\pi
, and so the sign of the whole expression is given
by the sign of the numerator. This may, in turn, be written in the form

c_{1}w-c_{2}-c_{3}w\arctan\left({\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle w}}\right),

where
w=\sqrt{R^{2}-\pi^{2}}
and
c_{i}
,
i=1,2,3
are positive constants. It is not difficult to see that (for
these values of the constants
c_{i}
) this is an increasing function of
w
, which is negative when
w
vanishes
and goes to plus infinity with
w
. The given value for
R_{1}
may be obtained by solving (numerically) the equation
resulting from equating the numerator above to zero.
∎

4.2.
Higher dimensions: some estimates for general cylinders

Although similar studies may be
carried out for more general cylinders such as those of the form
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}
and

\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}
, the corresponding analysis becomes much more involved and we
will thus focus on two aspects which we believe already provide a global illustration of what is to be expected. On the
one hand, it is relatively straightforward to consider again the first eigenvalue by itself and see when Pólya’s
conjecture fails in this case. We expect that in the former case, namely
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}
, the values of

h
where Pólya’s conjecture breaks down for
\lambda_{1}
will decrease with
n
, while in the latter case
increasing the dimension will allow for higher values of the indices
h_{j}
for which Pólya’s conjecture holds.

4.2.1.
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}

In this instance, Pólya’s conjecture reads as

\displaystyle N(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{\omega_{n+1}}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}|\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{(n+1)\omega_{n+1}^{2}}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}h

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{n+1}{2^{n+1}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)^{2}}h,

while the first Dirichlet eigenvalue is again given by

\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}.

The conjecture fails for this eigenvalue when

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{h^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{n+1}{2^{n+1}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)^{2}}h

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow h

\displaystyle>\pi^{1+\frac{1}{n}}\frac{(n+1)^{\frac{1}{n}}}{2^{1+\frac{1}{n}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)^{\frac{2}{n}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{\pi(n+1)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}},

and we conclude

Proposition 4.2
.

Pólya’s conjecture on
\mathbb{S}^{n}\times I_{h}
does not hold for
h
larger than

\eta_{1}(n)=\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle 2}\left(\frac{\pi(n+1)}{2\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)^{2}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}\approx\frac{\displaystyle e\pi}{\displaystyle n}+{\rm O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle\log(n)}{\displaystyle n^{2}}\right),\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

We thus see that, as
n
increases, the value of
h
for which Pólya’s conjecture is satisfied will decrease. We
remark that, just like in the case where
n
is one, there might still exist gaps in the interval
(0,\eta_{1}(n))

where Pólya’s conjecture is not satisfied, similar to those corresponding to
\lambda_{8}
and
\lambda_{13}

for
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
.

4.2.2.
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}

Pólya’s conjecture now reads as

\displaystyle N(\lambda)

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{\omega_{n+1}}{(2\pi)^{n+1}}\left|\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}\right|

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n}}{(2\pi)^{n}}\omega_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n}}{2^{n}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}

We shall again consider the first Dirichlet eigenvalue, now given by

\lambda_{1}\left(\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}\right)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{2}^{2}}+\dots+\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{n}^{2}},

and see that the conjecture fails for this eigenvalue when

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle>\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{1}^{2}}+\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{2}^{2}}+\dots+\frac{\pi^{2}}{h_{n}^{2}}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\frac{h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n}}{2^{n}\pi^{\frac{n-1}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle=\left(h_{1}^{-2}+h_{2}^{-2}+\dots+h_{n}^{-2}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{2}}h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n}\frac{\pi^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}{2^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle<\left(h_{1}^{-2}+h_{2}^{-2}+\dots+h_{n}^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}(h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}.

We thus have

Proposition 4.3
.

Pólya’s conjecture on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h_{j}}
may be safisfied only if

\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\geq\left(h_{1}^{-2}+h_{2}^{-2}+\dots+h_{n}^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}(h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}

In the particular case of the Cartesian product of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
with a hypercube of side length
h
, that is

h_{1}=h_{2}=\dots=h_{n}=h
, this becomes

\displaystyle\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle<\left(h_{1}^{-2}+h_{2}^{-2}+\dots+h_{n}^{-2}\right)^{-1/2}(h_{1}h_{2}\cdots h_{n})^{-\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\frac{\pi^{1/2}}{2}\left(\frac{2\pi}{\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle<n^{-1/2}h^{\frac{1}{n+1}}

\displaystyle h

\displaystyle>\frac{n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}{2^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}

and we get

Corollary 4.4
.

Pólya’s conjecture on
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h}
does not hold if
h
is larger than

\frac{\displaystyle n^{\frac{n+1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{n+3}{2}}}{\displaystyle 2^{n}\Gamma\left(\frac{n+3}{2}\right)}\approx\frac{\displaystyle\pi}{\displaystyle\sqrt{2n}}\left(\frac{\displaystyle e\pi}{\displaystyle 2}\right)^{n/2}+{\rm O}\left(\frac{\displaystyle 1}{\displaystyle n^{3/2}}\left(\frac{\displaystyle e\pi}{\displaystyle 2}\right)^{n/2}\right),\mbox{ as }n\to\infty.

As
n
increases the values of
h
for which Pólya’s conjecture may be satisfied increase, but again
there might still exist gaps in this interval where the conjecture fails.

In this instance, and because the Li-Yau inequalities hold for
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h}
for
h

in
(0,\pi^{2}]
, we may, in fact, conclude that the conjecture will hold provided the smallest of the
h_{i}

is in this interval.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
n
be an integer greater than or equal to three, and
let
h_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,n
be positive real numbers, and
h_{0}:=\min\left\{h_{1},\dots,h_{n}\right\}
. If

h_{0}\in(0,\pi^{2}]
, then Pólya’s conjecture holds for
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h}
.

Proof.

We write
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times{\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{n}}I_{h}
as the Cartesian product of

\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h_{0}}

with the remaining product of intervals. Then, because the former domain satisfies the Li-Yau inequalities while
the latter satisfies Pólya’s inequality and is in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d
greater than or equal to two, we may apply
the argument of
[
L
, Theorem 2.8]
for Cartesian products, from which the result follows.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

If
\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h_{1}}
satisfies Pólya’s conjecture, we may conclude that

\mathbb{S}^{1}\times I_{h_{1}}\times I_{h_{2}}
also satisfies the conjecture.

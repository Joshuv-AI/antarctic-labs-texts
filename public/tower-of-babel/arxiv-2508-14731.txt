1.
Introduction

The twisted Alexander polynomial was first defined by Lin
[
8
]
for knots in
S^{3}
and then by Wada
[
22
]
for finitely presentable groups. It is a generalization of the classical Alexander polynomial by using linear representations, and has been very useful in low dimensional topology and knot theory. Let
K\subset S^{3}
be a knot and
G(K)
its knot group, which is the fundamental group of the knot exterior
S^{3}\setminus K
. Following
[
22
]
, for a
d
-dimensional linear representation
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
we can define a rational function
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)\in\mathbb{C}(t)
up to multiplication by
\pm t^{i}\,(i\in\mathbb{Z})
. We call
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
the twisted Alexander polynomial of
K
associated with
\rho
. Like the classical Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}(t)
, the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
contains information about the genus and fibering of
K
. For example, the degree of
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
is bounded above by
d(2g(K)-1)
, where
g(K)
is the least genus of all Seifert surfaces bounding
K

[
4
]
. In this paper we say that
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
determines the knot genus
g(K)
if its degree is exactly equal to
d(2g(K)-1)
. Regarding fibering, it is known that if
K\subset S^{3}
is a fibered knot then
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
is expressed as a rational function of monic polynomials in
t

[
5
]
. Here a Laurent polynomial in
t
is said to be monic if its leading coefficient equals
\pm 1
.

We now focus on two-dimensional representations
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Then the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta^{\rho}_{K}(t)
becomes a Laurent polynomial in
t
if
\rho
is a nonabelian
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
-representation
[
7
]
. When
K\subset S^{3}
is a hyperbolic knot (namely, the knot exterior admits a complete hyperbolic metric of finite volume), up to conjugation there is a unique discrete and faithful representation
\bar{\rho}_{0}:G(K)\to\mathrm{Isom}^{+}(\mathbb{H}^{3})\cong\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. This is called the holonomy representation corresponding to the hyperbolic structure. It lifts to a representation
\rho_{0}:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
which is also discrete and faithful
[
19
]
. By abuse of terminology, we also call
\rho_{0}
the holonomy representation of
K
. In
[
2
]
, Dunfield, Friedl and Jackson studied the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\rho_{0}}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
for a hyperbolic knot
K
. It is normalized as a symmetric Laurent polynomial in
t
, which is denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
and called the
hyperbolic torsion polynomial
of
K
.
Based on extensive experiments with all hyperbolic
knots having at most
15
crossings, they conjectured that
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
determines the knot genus
g(K)
, in the sense that
\deg\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)=2(2g(K)-1)
. Moreover,
K
is fibered if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
is monic. This conjecture has been verified for some infinite families of hyperbolic knots
[
10
,
14
,
1
,
16
,
9
,
13
]
.

There is another way to get a torsion polynomial by considering the twisted Alexander polynomial associated with the
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
-representation obtained by composing the holonomy representation
\rho_{0}:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
with the adjoint action, denoted by
\mathrm{Ad}
, of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
on its Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. In
[
3
]
, Dubois and Yamaguchi proved that the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}(t)
is actually a Laurent polynomial in
t
. Moreover, it can be normalized as a Laurent polynomial
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t^{-1})=-\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
up to multiplication by
t^{i}\,(i\in\mathbb{Z})
. We call
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
the
adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial
of
K
. Like the hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
, we can ask whether
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
determines the genus and fibering of
K
or not. Note that
\deg\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)\leq 3(2g(K)-1)
. In
[
2
]
, Dunfield, Friedl and Jackson pointed out that
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
does not always determine the knot genus
g(K)
, in the sense that there exist hyperbolic knots
\mathcal{K}\subset S^{3}
such that
\deg\mathcal{T}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)<3(2g(\mathcal{K})-1)
. However, for all hyperbolic knots with at most
15
crossings, they numerically checked that
K
is fibered if and only if
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
is monic.

In this paper we consider the adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
for a two-parameter family of rational knots called double twist knots. Let
J(k,l)
denote the double twist knot/link indicated in Figure
1
, where the integers
k
and
l
determine the signed number of half twists in the boxes. In the
k
-box (resp.
l
-box), negative (resp. positive) numbers correspond to right-handed half twists and positive (resp. negative) numbers correspond to left-handed half twists. Note that
J(k,l)
is the same as the rational knot/link corresponding to the continued fraction
-k+\frac{1}{l}
. It is a knot when
kl
is even (and a two-component link if
kl
is odd) and is the trivial knot if
kl=0
. Moreover,
J(k,l)
is
ambient isotopic to
J(l,k)
and
J(-k,-l)
is the mirror image of
J(k,l)
.
Therefore, up to mirror image, we will only consider double twist knots
J(k,2n)
for
k>0
and
n\not=0
. The knot
J(k,2n)
is
hyperbolic unless it is
J(2,-2)
(the trefoil knot) or
J(1,2n)
.
Note that the knots
J(2m,2n)
are also known as genus one two-bridge knots and
J(2,2n)
as twist knots. We will say that
J(k,2n)
is an even (resp. odd) double twist knot if
k
is even (resp. odd).

Figure 1.
The double twist knot/link
J(k,l)
.

In
[
21
]
, an explicit formula for the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}(t)
of even double twist knots
J(2m,2n)
was provided. The fact that these knots have genus one makes it possible to perform the calculations. The formula was then applied in
[
11
,
20
]
to show that the adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
determines the genus and fibering of
J(2m,2n)
. The case of odd double twist knots
J(2m+1,2n)
is more challenging, since it is difficult to compute the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial explicitly. However, by using properties of algebraic integers, we will prove that the adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial determines the genus and fibering of all double twist knots without knowing its explicit formula.

Theorem 1
.

Let
K=J(k,2n)
be a hyperbolic double twist knot. Then the adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
determines the genus of
K
, in the sense that
\deg\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)=3(2g(K)-1)
. Moreover,
K
is fibered if and only if
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
is monic.

We remark that a similar, but incomplete, result for the hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
of double twist knots was proved in
[
14
]
. More precisely, Morifuji and the author verified that
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
determines the genus of all double twist knots, and moreover it determines the fibering of
J(2m+1,2n)
. However, it is still unknown whether
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
determines the fibering of
J(2m,2n)
or not.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
we review the twisted Alexander polynomial, hyperbolic torsion conjecture, and algebraic integers. In Section
3
we first compute the Riley polynomial of double twist knots, then determine the highest and lowest degree terms of the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial associated with the holonomy representation, and finally give a proof of Theorem
1
.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Twisted Alexander polynomial

Let
K\subset S^{3}
be a knot and
E_{K}=S^{3}\setminus K
the knot exterior. Let
G(K)=\pi_{1}(E_{K})
be the knot group, which is the fundamental group of
E_{K}
.
We choose a Wirtinger presentation:

G(K)=\langle x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}\,|\,r_{1},\ldots,r_{l-1}\rangle.

The abelianization homomorphism

\mathfrak{a}:G(K)\to H_{1}(E_{K};\mathbb{Z})\cong\mathbb{Z}=\langle t\rangle

is given by assigning to each generator
x_{i}
the meridian element

t\in H_{1}(E_{K};\mathbb{Z})
. Here we denote the sum in
\mathbb{Z}
multiplicatively.

Let
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
be a
d
-dimensional linear representation. The maps
\rho
and
\mathfrak{a}
naturally induce two ring
homomorphisms
\tilde{\rho}:{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]\rightarrow M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
and

\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}:{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]\rightarrow{\mathbb{Z}}[t^{\pm 1}]
, where

{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]
is the group ring of
G(K)
and
M_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is the
matrix algebra of degree
d
over
{\mathbb{C}}
. The tensor product

\tilde{\rho}\otimes\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}
defines a ring homomorphism

{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]\to M_{d}\left({\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}]\right)
.
Let
F_{l}
denote the
free group on the generators
x_{1},\ldots,x_{l}
and

\Phi:{\mathbb{Z}}[F_{l}]\to M_{d}\left({\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}]\right)

the composition of the surjection

\tilde{\phi}:{\mathbb{Z}}[F_{l}]\to{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]

induced by the presentation of
G(K)

and the map

\tilde{\rho}\otimes\tilde{\mathfrak{a}}:{\mathbb{Z}}[G(K)]\to M_{d}\left({\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}]\right)
.

Let
Q
denote the
(l-1)\times l
matrix
whose
(i,j)
-entry is the
d\times d
matrix

\Phi\left(\frac{\partial r_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}\right)\in M_{d}\left({\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}]\right)
,
where

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}

denotes the Fox derivative.
For
1\leq j\leq l
,
we denote by
Q_{j}

the
(l-1)\times(l-1)
matrix obtained from
Q

by removing the
j
-th column.
We regard
Q_{j}
as
a
d(l-1)\times d(l-1)
matrix with coefficients in

{\mathbb{C}}[t^{\pm 1}]
.
Then Wada’s twisted Alexander polynomial
[
22
]
of
K

associated with a representation
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathbb{C})
is defined
to be the rational function

\Delta_{K}^{\rho}(t)=\frac{\det Q_{j}}{\det\Phi(1-x_{j})}.

It is well-defined up to multiplication by
\pm t^{i}~(i\in{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Now we focus on two-dimensional linear representations of knot groups. It is known that if
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a nonabelian representation, the rational function
\Delta_{K}^{\rho}(t)
becomes a Laurent polynomial in
t

[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Let
\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
be the Lie algebra of
2\times 2
complex matrices with trace zero. The adjoint action, denoted by
\mathrm{Ad}
, is the conjugation on
\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.
\mathrm{Ad}(V)(g)=VgV^{-1}\in\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for all
V\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
g\in\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. For each representation
\rho:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, the composition
\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho
is a representation of
G(K)
into
\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
and thus we can define a rational function
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}(t)
called the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial of
K
.

2.2.
Hyperbolic torsion conjecture

For a hyperbolic knot
K
in
S^{3}
, recall that
\rho_{0}:G(K)\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is a lift of the holonomy representation
\bar{\rho}_{0}:G(K)\to\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
corresponding to the hyperbolic structure of the knot exterior. The representation
\rho_{0}
is known to be discrete and faithful
[
19
]
.
In
[
2
]
, Dunfield, Friedl and Jackson studied the hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
which is a normalization of the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\rho_{0}}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
such that
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)=\mathcal{T}_{K}(t^{-1})
.
Based on many numerical computations, they conjectured that
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
determines the knot genus
g(K)
, namely,
\deg\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)=2(2g(K)-1)
. Moreover,
K
is fibered if and only if
\mathcal{T}_{K}(t)
is monic. This conjecture has been verified for some families of hyperbolic knots
[
10
,
14
,
1
,
16
,
9
,
13
]
.

On the other hand, we can also study the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial associated with the holonomy representation. In
[
3
]
, Dubois and Yamaguchi proved that the twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}(t)
is actually a Laurent polynomial in
t
. The adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t^{\pm 1}]
is a normalization of
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}(t)
such that
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t^{-1})=-\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
. In
[
2
]
, Dunfield, Friedl and Jackson remarked that
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
does not always determine the knot genus. However, for all hyperbolic knots having at most
15
crossings, they numerically checked that
K
is fibered if and only if
\mathcal{T}^{\mathrm{Ad}}_{K}(t)
is monic. In
[
11
,
20
]
, by using an explicit formula for the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial
\Delta_{K}^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}(t)
from
[
21
]
, Morifuji and the author proved that the adjoint hyperbolic torsion polynomial determines the genus and fibering of genus one two-bridge knots.

2.3.
Algebraic integers

An
algebraic integer
is a complex number which is a root of some monic polynomial whose coefficients are integers. It is easily checked that the set of all algebraic integers, denoted by
\mathbb{A}
, is closed under addition and multiplication. Hence
\mathbb{A}
is a commutative subring of
\mathbb{C}
. We will show the following.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
P(t)=c_{q}t^{q}+\cdots+c_{1}t+c_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
is a non-constant polynomial, and there exists a prime number
p
such that
p\nmid c_{0}
and
p\mid c_{1},\cdots,c_{q}
. Then none of the roots of
P[t]
are algebraic integers.

Proof.

The proof is similar to that of Eisenstein’s irreducibility criterion.

Suppose
t_{0}
is a root of
P(t)
. Let
R(t)
be the minimal polynomial of
t_{0}
over
\mathbb{Z}
. Write
R(t)=a_{r}t^{r}+\cdots+a_{1}t+a_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
. We claim that
p\mid a_{r}
.

Note that
R\mid P
. If
\deg R=\deg P
, then
P=dR
for some
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Since
p\nmid c_{0}=da_{0}
we have
p\nmid d
. Then
p\mid c_{q}=da_{r}
implies that
p\mid a_{r}
.

Suppose
\deg R<\deg P
then
P(t)=R(t)S(t)
for some non-constant polynomial
S(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
. Write
S(t)=b_{s}t^{s}+\cdots+b_{1}t+b_{0}
. Note that
p\nmid c_{0}=a_{0}b_{0}
. Let
i\leq r
be the largest integer such that
p\nmid a_{i}
, and
j\leq s
the largest integer such that
p\nmid b_{j}
. Consider

c_{i+j}=\sum_{u+v=i+j}a_{u}b_{v}=a_{i}b_{j}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=i+j,\\
(u,v)\not=(i,j)\end{subarray}}a_{u}b_{v}.

If
u>i
then
p\mid a_{u}
. If
u<i
then
v=i+j-u>j
and so
p\mid b_{v}
. This implies that
p\mid a_{u}b_{v}
if
u\not=i
. Hence

p\mid\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u+v=i+j,\\
(u,v)\not=(i,j)\end{subarray}}a_{u}b_{v}.

Since
p\nmid a_{i}b_{j}
, we obtain
p\nmid c_{i+j}
. This only occurs when
i+j=0
, namely,
i=j=0
. Hence
p\mid a_{u}
for all
1\leq u\leq r
. In particular
p\mid a_{r}
. This implies that
a_{r}\not=\pm 1
and therefore
t_{0}
cannot be an algebraic ineteger.
∎

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
P(t)\in\mathbb{Z}[t]
be a non-constant polynomial. Suppose
q
and
r
are co-prime integers such that
|q|\geq 2
. Then none of the roots of
Q(t):=qP(t)+r
are algebraic integers.

Proof.

Let
p
be any prime factor of
q
. Then all coefficients of
qP(t)
are divisible by
p
. Since
r
is co-prime with
q
, it is not divisible by
p
. Hence all coefficients, except the constant coefficient, of
Q(t)=qP(t)+r
are divisible by
p
.
Then, by Proposition
2.1
, none of the roots of
Q(t)
are algebraic integers.
∎

3.
Double twist knots

In this section, we first compute the Riley polynomial of double twist knots, then determine the highest and lowest degree terms of the adjoint twisted Alexander polynomial associated with the holonomy representation, and finally give a proof of Theorem
1
.

It is known that
\mathcal{K}:=J(k,2n)
, where
k\geq 1
and
n\not=0
, is fibered if and only if

•

\mathcal{K}=J(1,2n)
,

•

\mathcal{K}=J(2,\pm 2)
(the trefoil knot and figure eight knot, respectively),

•

\mathcal{K}=J(3,2n)
for
n\geq 1
.

Moreover, its genus is given by

g(\mathcal{K})=\begin{cases}1&\text{if~}k=2m,\\
|n|&\text{if~}k=2m+1\geq 3,\\
n-1&\text{if~}k=1\text{~and~}n\geq 1,\\
|n|&\text{if~}k=1\text{~and~}n\leq-1.\end{cases}

This can be proved by computing the Alexander polynomial and applying the fact that an alternating knot is fibered if and only if its Alexander polynomial is monic, i.e., with leading
coefficient
\pm 1
. Moreover, the degree of the Alexander polynomial of an alternating knot is equal to twice the knot genus. Note that double twist knots are two-bridge knots which are alternating. See
[
12
, Lemma 7.3]
and references therein.

By
[
6
]
, the knot group is
G(\mathcal{K})=\langle a,b\mid w^{n}a=bw^{n}\rangle
where

w=\begin{cases}(ba^{-1})^{m}(b^{-1}a)^{m}&\text{if~}k=2m,\\
(ba^{-1})^{m}ba(b^{-1}a)^{m}&\text{if~}k=2m+1.\end{cases}

3.1.
The Riley polynomial

A representation
\rho:G(\mathcal{K})\to\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is called non-abelian if its image
\rho(G(\mathcal{K}))
is a non-abelian subgroup of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Taking conjugation if necessary, we can assume that
\rho
has the form

(3.1)

\rho(a)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&1\\
0&M^{-1}\end{array}\right]\quad\text{and}\quad\rho(b)=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}M&0\\
2-y&M^{-1}\end{array}\right],

where
(M,y)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}
satisfies
\rho(w^{n}a)=\rho(bw^{n})
. Note that
y=\tr\rho(ab^{-1})
. By
[
17
]
, the matrix equation
\rho(w^{n}a)=\rho(bw^{n})
is equivalent to a single polynomial equation
R_{\mathcal{K}}(x,y)=0
, where
x=M+M^{-1}
and
R_{K}(x,y):=\rho(w)_{11}+(M^{-1}-M)\rho(w)_{12}\in\mathbb{C}[x,y]
is the Riley polynomial of a two-bridge knot
K
. Here
\rho(w)_{ij}
is the
ij
-th entry of the matrix
\rho(w)
.

To determine the Riley polynomial of
\mathcal{K}=J(k,2n)
, we first compute
\rho(w)
via Chebyshev polynomials. Let
S_{l}(v),\,l\in\mathbb{Z},
be the Chebyshev polynomials of the second kind defined by

S_{0}(v)=1
,
S_{1}(v)=v
and
S_{l}(v)=vS_{l-1}(v)-S_{l-2}(v)

for all integers
l
.

The following properties of
S_{l}(v)
’s are well-known, see e.g.
[
13
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

(1)
If
v=a+a^{-1}\not=\pm 2
then
S_{l}(v)=(a^{l+1}-a^{-l-1})/(a-a^{-1})
. Moreover,
S_{l}(\pm 2)=(\pm 1)^{l}(l+1)
.

(2)

S_{-l}(v)=-S_{l-2}(v)
.

(3)
For
l\geq 1
, we have
S_{l}(v)=\prod_{j=1}^{l}(v-2\cos\frac{j\pi}{l+1})
and
S_{l}(v)-S_{l-1}(v)=\prod_{j=1}^{l}(v-2\cos\frac{(2j-1)\pi}{2l+1})
.
In particular, all the roots of
S_{l}(v)
and
S_{l}(v)-S_{l-1}(v)
are real numbers in the interval
(-2,2)
.

(4)

S^{2}_{l}(v)-vS_{l}(v)S_{l-1}(v)+S^{2}_{l-1}(v)=1
.

We will also use the following result whose proof is based on the Cayley-Hamilton theorem
V^{2}=(\tr V)V-(\det V)I
for
2\times 2
matrices
V\in M_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, where
I
denotes the identity
2\times 2
matrix. See e.g.
[
14
]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
V\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
v=\tr V
. Then

V^{l}=S_{l}(v)I-S_{l-1}(v)V^{-1}=S_{l-1}(v)V-S_{l-2}(v)I

for any integer
l
.

Then the matrix
\rho(w)
can be computed explicitly as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

(1)
If
k=2m
then

\displaystyle\rho(w)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I-S_{m}(y)S_{m-1}(y)\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}y-2&M-M^{-1}\\
(M-M^{-1})(2-y)&2-y\end{array}\right]

3.2.
The twisted Alexander polynomial

Recall that the knot group of
\mathcal{K}=J(k,2n)
is
G(\mathcal{K})=\langle a,b\mid w^{n}a=bw^{n}\rangle
where

w=\begin{cases}(ba^{-1})^{m}(b^{-1}a)^{m}&\text{if~}k=2m,\\
(ba^{-1})^{m}b(ab^{-1})^{m}a&\text{if~}k=2m+1.\end{cases}

. Let

\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)=\det\Phi\left(\frac{\partial r}{\partial a}\right)\big/\det\Phi(1-b)

For

(3.8)

\displaystyle\frac{\partial r}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{n}}{\partial a}+w^{n}+w^{n}a\frac{\partial w^{-n}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\begin{cases}w^{n}\left(1+(1-a)w^{-n}\sigma_{n-1}(w)\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}\right)&\text{if~}n\geq 1,\\
w^{n}\left(1-(1-a)\sigma_{|n|-1}(w)\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}\right)&\text{if~}n\leq-1.\end{cases}

We now compute
\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}
.
If
k=2m
then
w=(ba^{-1})^{m}(b^{-1}a)^{m}
and

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(ba^{-1})^{m}}{\partial a}+(ba^{-1})^{m}\frac{\partial(b^{-1}a)^{m}}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ba^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ba^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ab^{-1}).

If
k=2m+1\geq 3
then
w=(ba^{-1})^{m}b(ab^{-1})^{m}a

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(ba^{-1})^{m}}{\partial a}+(ba^{-1})^{m}b\frac{\partial(ab^{-1})^{m}}{\partial a}+(ba^{-1})^{m}b(ab^{-1})^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ba^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ba^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b\,\sigma_{m-1}(ab^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b(ab^{-1})^{m}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-ba^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ba^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b\,\sigma_{m}(ab^{-1}).

If
k=1
, then
w=ba
and
\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}=b
. Hence

(3.9)

\frac{\partial w}{\partial a}=\begin{cases}-ba^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ba^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ab^{-1})&\text{if~}k=2m,\\
-ba^{-1}\sigma_{m-1}(ba^{-1})+(ba^{-1})^{m}b\,\sigma_{m}(ab^{-1})&\text{if~}k=2m+1\geq 3,\\
b&\text{if~}k=1.\end{cases}

Recall that the adjoint action
\mathrm{Ad}
is the conjugation on
\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
by
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
, i.e.
\mathrm{Ad}(V)(g)=VgV^{-1}\in\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
for all
V\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
g\in\mathrm{sl}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. We need the following lemma.

Lemma 3.5
.

[
15
, Lemma 2.1]

Suppose
V=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}e&f\\
g&h\end{array}\right]\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
. Then

\mathrm{Ad}(V)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}e^{2}&-2ef&-f^{2}\\
-eg&eh+fg&fh\\
-g^{2}&2gh&h^{2}\end{array}\right].

Moreover,
\mathrm{Ad}(V)\in\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{C})
.

Proposition 3.6
.

For
V\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
and
l\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\det\sigma_{l}\left(\mathrm{Ad}(V)\right)=(l+1)S^{2}_{l}(v)

where
v=\tr V
.

Proof.

Up to conjugation we can assume that
V=\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}s&c\\
0&s^{-1}\end{array}\right]
for some
s\not=0
. Since
v=\tr V=s+s^{-1}
, we have
S_{l}(v)=(s^{l+1}-s^{-l-1})/(s-s^{-1})=s^{-l}(1+s^{2}+\cdots+s^{2l})
.

By Lemma
3.5
we have
\mathrm{Ad}(V)=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}s^{2}&-2cs&-c^{2}\\
0&1&cs^{-1}\\
0&0&s^{-2}\end{array}\right]
. Then

\sigma_{l}(\mathrm{Ad}(V))=\sum_{i=0}^{l}(\mathrm{Ad}(V))^{i}=\left[\begin{array}[]{ccc}1+s^{2}+\cdots+s^{2l}&*&*\\
0&l+1&*\\
0&0&1+s^{-2}+\cdots+s^{-2l}\end{array}\right].

Hence

\det\sigma_{l}(\mathrm{Ad}(V))=(l+1)s^{-2l}(1+s^{2}+\cdots+s^{2l})^{2}=(l+1)S^{2}_{l}(v).

3
with highest coefficient
m|n|S^{2}_{m-1}(y)S^{2}_{|n|-1}(z)
.

If
m|n|=1
, then
m|n|S^{2}_{m-1}(y)S^{2}_{|n|-1}(z)=1
. Hence
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is monic.

If
m|n|\geq 2
, then by applying Corollary
2.2
with
q=m|n|
,
r=\pm 1
and
P(y)=S^{2}_{m-1}(y)S^{2}_{|n|-1}(z)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
and noting that
y\in\mathbb{A}
, we obtain
m|n|S^{2}_{m-1}(y)S^{2}_{|n|-1}(z)\pm 1\not=0
. This means that
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is not monic.

Case 2
:
k=2m+1\geq 3
. Note that
J(2m+1,2n)
has genus
|n|
. Moreover,
J(2m+1,2n)
is fibered if and only if
m=1
and
n\geq 1
.

By Proposition
3.4
we have
S_{m}(y)\not=0
and
S_{m-1}(y)\not=0
.

If
n\geq 1
, then by Proposition
3.7
,
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
6n-3
with highest coefficient
mS^{2}_{m-1}(y)
. If
m=1
, then
mS^{2}_{m-1}(y)=1
and hence
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is monic. If
m\geq 2
, then by applying Corollary
2.2
with
q=m
,
r=\pm 1
and
P(y)=S^{2}_{m-1}(y)\in\mathbb{Z}[y]
and noting that
y\in\mathbb{A}
, we obtain
mS^{2}_{m-1}(y)\pm 1\not=0
. Thus
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is not monic.

If
n\leq-1
, then by Proposition
3.8
,
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is a polynomial of degree
-3+6|n|
with highest coefficient
(m+1)S^{2}_{m}(y)
. Since
y\in\mathbb{A}
, Corollary
2.2
implies that
(m+1)S^{2}_{m}(y)\pm 1\not=0
. This means that
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is not monic.

Case 3
:
k=1
. Note that
J(1,2n)
is fibered and its genus is given by

g(J(1,2n))=\begin{cases}n-1&\text{if~}n\geq 1,\\
|n|&\text{if~}n\leq-1.\end{cases}

If
n\geq 2
, then by Proposition
3.7
,
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is a monic polynomial of degree
6n-9
.

If
n\leq-1
, then by Proposition
3.8
,
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is a monic polynomial of degree
-3+6|n|
.

In all three cases shown, we have
\deg\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)=3(2g(\mathcal{K})-1)
. Moreover,
\Delta^{\mathrm{Ad}\circ\rho_{0}}_{\mathcal{K}}(t)
is monic if and only if
\mathcal{K}
is fibered. This completes the proof of Theorem
1
.

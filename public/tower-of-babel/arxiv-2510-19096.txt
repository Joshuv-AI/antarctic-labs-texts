High Contrast Transmission and Fabry-Pérot-type Resonances
Long Li, Mourad Sini

Abstract
It is well known, in the acoustic model, that highly contrasting transmission leads to the so-called Minnaert subwavelength resonance. In this work, we show that such highly contrasting transmissions create not only one resonance but a family of infinite resonances located near the real axis where the first one (i.e. the smallest) is indeed the Minnaert one. This family of resonances are the shifts (in the lower complex plan) of the Neumann eigenvalues of the Laplacian. The well known Minneart resonance is nothing but the shift of the trivial (zero) Neumann eigenvalue of the bubble. These resonances, other than the Minnaert ones, are Fabry-Pérot-type resonances as the generated total fields, in the bubble, are dominated by a linear combination of the Neumann eigenfunctions which, in particular, might create interferences. In addition, we establish the following properties. 1. We derive the asymptotic expansions, at the second order, of this family of resonances in terms of the contrasting coefficient. 2. In the time-harmonic regime, we derive the resolvent estimates of the related Hamiltonian and the asymptotics of scattered fields that are uniform in the whole space, highlighting the contributions from this sequence of resonances. 3. In the time domain regime, we derive the time behavior of the acoustic microresonator at large time-scales inversely proportional to powers of microresonator’s radius. 4. The analysis shows that near Fabry-Pérot resonances, the mircoresonator exhibits pronounced anisotropy. We believe that such a feature may pave the way for designing anisotropic metamaterials from simple configurations of a single microresonator. Keywords: acoustic resonators, Fabry-Pérot resonances, resolvent estimates, large time behavior, asymptotic estimates.

It is widely known that such high contrasts give rise to a very low frequency (subwavelength) resonance called the Minnaert resonance, present across all configurations of wave speeds. A first attempt in this direction was proposed in
[

confirming the resonant character of the Minnaert frequency both physically (i.e., as a frequency near which enhancement of the generated fields) and mathematically (as a pole of the related Hamiltonian).

The main contributions of the current work are outlined as follows:

1.

near the real axis, uniformly across all wave speed configurations. These scattering resonances, apart from the Minnaert ones, can be regarded as Fabry-Pérot-type resonances.

2.

Our second main result concerns the uniform asymptotics in the whole space for the resolvent estimates of the Hamiltonian
H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}
and for the scattered fields of the associated scattering problem in the time-harmonic regime, emphasizing the contributions from the Fabry-Pérot resonances. More precisely, we prove that the resolvent exhibits an enhancement of

3.

Finally, we consider the microresonator regime, where the size of the resonator
\Omega
is small while the contrast remains high. In this regime considered here, all Fabry-Pérot resonances associated with the nonzero Neumann eigenvalues only contribute at high frequencies, and are therefore referred to as high frequency resonances. Based on scaling transformations and the results previously obtained for fixed-size resonators, we characterize the high-frequency asymptotics of the resolvent and the associated scattered fields corresponding to the microresonator, uniformly throughout the whole space, when both the size of the resonator and the contrast are small (see Theorems
5.1
and
5.2
). The dominating parts, for both the resolvent operator and the scattered field, are characteristic of the highly oscillatory point-scatterer supported at the location of the microresonator, modulated by the far-field pattern of the exterior Dirichlet scattering problem (see formulas (

[
27
,
29
]
-each mircoresonator at high frequencies exhibits pronounced anisotropy, see Remark

for more details on this notion of anisotropic small scatterers. We believe that such a feature may pave the way for designing anisotropic metamaterials from simple configurations of a single microresonator. Moreover, we derive the time behavior of the acoustic microresonator driven by a causal source with compact support in both time and space (see Theorem
5.3
). Notably, all Fabry-Pérot resonances, other than the Minnaert resonances, must tend to infinity as the microresonator’s size shrinks to zero. Therefore, for large times that remain within the Minnaert resonance lifetime, the wave evolution in this setting is asymptotically dominated by the Minnaert resonant oscillation, while the amplitude is governed by the associated projection. The precise asymptotic characterization of the Minnaert resonant projection (see Lemma
5.3
) is highly nontrivial due to the fact that the Hamiltonian

is an unbounded operator. To overcome this difficulty, we show that the Minnaert resonance is simple and make use of the self-adjointness of

in the space

In comparison, our contribution differs in two key aspects. First, our estimate can be valid for arbitrarily large time scales, specifically of order inverse powers of the microresonator’s size, with the exponent depending on the smoothness of the source. This extends beyond the time regimes considered in
[
28
,
30
,
33
]

[
8
,
10
]
, we analyze the original wave field governed by the wave equation, rather than the truncated inverse Fourier transform of the wave field.

We proceed to introduce the resonant projection

The following lemma establishes the connection between the resonant projection and the resolvent

Lemma 2.3

(
[

)
.

Let
\tau>0
be fixed. Assume that

is any resonance of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle R_{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}(z)=-\sum^{M_{\lambda_{\tau}}}_{l=1}\frac{(H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}-\lambda_{\tau}^{2})^{l-1}}{\left(z^{2}-\lambda_{\tau}^{2}\right)^{l}}\Pi_{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}({\lambda_{\tau}})+B(z,\lambda_{\tau}),

Here,

is given by (

\displaystyle m_{R}(\lambda_{\tau})={\rm{dim}}\left({\rm{Ran}}\left(\Pi_{{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}}({\lambda_{\tau}})\right)\right).

(2.12)

We conclude this subsection by introducing the definition of resonant states and generalized resonant states.

Remark 2.2

(Characterization of the resonant states)
.

Let
\lambda_{\tau}
with

\displaystyle H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}-\lambda_{\tau}^{2}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}=0

\displaystyle u_{\lambda_{\tau}}|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash B_{r}}=R_{\lambda_{\tau}/c_{0}}g_{\lambda_{\tau}}|_{\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash B_{r}},

i.e.,

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\lambda_{\tau}}+\lambda_{\tau}^{2}c_{0}^{-2}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}=0\quad{\rm{in}}\;\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega,

(2.13)

\displaystyle\Delta u_{\lambda_{\tau}}+\lambda_{\tau}^{2}c_{1}^{-2}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}=0\quad{\rm{in}}\;\Omega,

(2.14)

\displaystyle\gamma_{+}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}=\gamma_{-}u_{\lambda_{\tau}},\quad\partial^{+}_{\nu}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}=\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho_{1}\tau}\partial^{-}_{\nu}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}\quad{\rm{on}}\;\Gamma,

Moreover, when

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad-\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho_{1}\tau}-1\right)\left(SL_{\Gamma,\lambda_{\tau}/c_{0}}\partial_{\nu}u_{\lambda_{\tau}}\right)(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma.

3
Existence and uniqueness of Fabry-Pérot resonances

This section is devoted to investigating the existence and uniqueness of the Fabry-Pérot resonances. With the aid of Corollary
2.1
, it is sufficient to study the non-injective points of
\mathcal{A}(z,\tau)
, specified in (
2.4
). For this aim, we observe that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(z,\tau)=\begin{bmatrix}&\mathbb{I}-\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)z^{2}\penalty\ N_{\Omega,z/c_{0}}&SL_{\Gamma,z/c_{0}}\\
&\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}\right)z^{2}\partial_{\nu}N_{\Omega,z/c_{0}}&\quad\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}+K_{\Gamma,z/c_{0}}^{*}\end{bmatrix}+\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\begin{bmatrix}&0&-SL_{\Gamma,z/c_{0}}\\
&0&\quad\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{I}-K_{\Gamma,z/c_{0}}^{*}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\;=:\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)+\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{A}_{1}(z).

(3.1)

The following result demonstrates the injective properties of
\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)
and
\mathcal{A}(z,\tau)

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\tau>0
and

. Assume that the operators
\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)
and

are given by (

) and (

(a)

The operator
\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)
is not injective at

if and only if
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
.

(b)

Assume that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
. There exists
\delta_{z_{0}}>0
such that when
\tau\in(0,\delta_{z_{0}})
, we can identify a finite set of points, whose the total number is independent of
\tau
, in the neighborhood of
z_{0}
such that
\mathcal{A}(z,\tau)
fails to be injective. Furthermore, these points all tend to

when
\tau\rightarrow 0
.

(c)

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be a bounded interval. There exists a constant
\delta_{I}>0
independent of
\tau
, and a constant
\tau_{\delta_{I}}>0
dependent on
\delta_{I}
, such that when
\tau\in(0,\tau_{\delta_{I}})

\mathcal{A}(z,\tau)
is invertible in
\mathcal{I}_{I}(\delta_{I})
, which is defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{I}(\delta_{I}):=\left\{z\in\mathbb{C}:{\rm{Re}}(z)\in I,\;|{\rm{Im}}(z)|<\delta_{I}\right\}\backslash\bigcup_{z_{0}\in I\cap\Lambda_{N}}B_{\delta_{I}}(z_{0}).

(3.2)

Here,

\displaystyle\Lambda_{N}:=\{z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}:z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}\;\textrm{is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain}\;\Omega\}.

(3.3)

Proof.

(
a
) Assume that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
is a point where
A_{0}(z_{0})
fails to be injective. Then, there exists
(\phi_{z_{0}},\psi_{z_{0}})\in L^{2}(\Omega)\times L^{2}(\Gamma)
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}(z_{0})\begin{bmatrix}\phi_{z_{0}}\\
\psi_{z_{0}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix},

(3.4)

which implies

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{c_{1}^{2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}}+SL_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\psi_{z_{0}}=-\phi_{z_{0}}\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega,

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\left(\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}}+SL_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\psi_{z_{0}}\right)=0\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma.

Therefore, we easily obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\phi_{z_{0}}+z_{0}^{2}/c^{2}_{0}\phi_{z_{0}}=\left(\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}-\frac{1}{c_{1}^{2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}\phi_{z_{0}}\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}\phi_{z_{0}}=0\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma.

(3.6)

This means that
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
.

Conversely, given any
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
such that
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
, we proceed to construct a nonzero solution such that (
3.4
) holds. Since (
3.4
) is equivalent to the following equation

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}(-z_{0})\begin{bmatrix}\overline{\phi_{z_{0}}}\\
\overline{\psi_{z_{0}}}\end{bmatrix}=\begin{bmatrix}0\\
0\end{bmatrix},

it sufficient to focus on the case of
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To do so, we aim to construct
\psi_{z_{0}}\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
satisfying

\displaystyle SL_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\psi_{z_{0}}+(\phi_{z_{0}}+\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}})=0\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega.

(3.7)

Here,
\phi_{z_{0}}
is any nonzero Neumann eigenfunction corresponding to

\phi_{z_{0}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}+K^{*}_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\right)\psi_{z_{0}}=\partial_{\nu}(-\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}})\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma

(3.8)

and that

\displaystyle\left(-\frac{1}{2}+K^{*}_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\right)\psi_{z_{0}}=D_{N}(z_{0}/c_{0})(-\gamma\left(\phi_{z_{0}}\right)-\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}\partial_{\nu}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}})\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma,

(3.9)

due to the well-posedness of the exterior Dirichlet obstacle scattering problem and the jump relations of

. Furthermore, subtracting (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), we have

\displaystyle\psi_{z_{0}}=D_{N}(z_{0}/c_{0})\gamma\left(\phi_{z_{0}}\right).

(3.10)

In conjunction with
\phi_{z_{0}}\in H^{2}(\Omega)
, we obtain
\psi_{z_{0}}\in L^{2}(\Gamma)
. Therefore, building upon (
3.7
) and (
3.8

solves (
3.4
). In order to verify (

Case 1

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma,z_{0}/c_{0}}\psi_{z_{0}}=\gamma(-\phi_{z_{0}}-\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}})\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma,

Case 2

Now we focus on the case when

\Omega

First, we recall that
\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle\Delta w^{D}+\lambda^{2}w^{D}=0\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega,

\displaystyle w^{D}=0\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma.

By Green formulas, we easily obtain

\displaystyle w^{D}(x)=\int_{\Gamma}\frac{e^{i\lambda|x-y|}}{4\pi|x-y|}\partial_{\nu(y)}w^{D}(y)d\sigma(y)\quad x\in\Omega.

(3.11)

This means that each Dirichlet eigenfunction in
\Omega
can be represented by corresponding single-layer potential. Let
\left\{w_{1}^{D},\ldots,w_{N}^{D}\right\}
denote the orthogonal basis of the Dirichlet eigenfunction space of dimension

Second, we assert that the kernel of

\displaystyle{\rm{Ker}}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)=\left\{h\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma):h=\sum_{l=1}^{N}h_{l}\partial_{\nu}w^{D}_{l}\;\textrm{with}\;h_{l}\in\mathbb{C}\right\}.

To do so, it suffices to prove that for any

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\partial_{\nu}g_{\psi}(x),\quad\textrm{for}\;x\in\Gamma,

(3.13)

where
g_{\psi}(x):=\left(SL_{\Gamma,\lambda}\psi\right)(x)
for

. We note that
g_{\psi}\in\textrm{Span}\left\{w_{1}^{D},\ldots,w_{N}^{D}\right\}
.
By Green’s integral theorems, we easily obtain

\displaystyle SL_{\Gamma,\lambda}\partial_{\nu}g_{\psi}=g_{\psi}\quad

\displaystyle\rm{in}\;\Omega,

(3.14)

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\partial_{\nu}g_{\psi}=0\quad

\displaystyle\rm{on}\;\Gamma.

(3.15)

We define

\displaystyle W_{\psi}(x):=\left(SL_{\Gamma,\lambda}\partial_{\nu}g_{\psi}\right)(x)-\left(SL_{\Gamma,\lambda}\psi\right)(x)\quad\mathrm{for}\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma.

It immediately follows from (

W_{\psi}=0
in
\overline{\Omega}

W_{\psi}
solves Helmholtz equation with the Sommerfeld radiation condition, we also have
W_{\psi}=0

\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\overline{\Omega}

\displaystyle\partial^{+}_{\nu}W_{\psi}-\partial^{-}_{\nu}W_{\psi}=\psi-\partial_{\nu}g_{\psi}=0\quad\rm{on}\;\Gamma,

whence (

Third, we prove that

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma,\lambda}:H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\backslash\rm{Ker}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\rightarrow\rm{Ker}^{\perp}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\;\;\textrm{is invertible}.

\rm{Ker}^{\perp}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)

\displaystyle\textrm{Ker}^{\perp}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right):=\left\{\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma):\int_{\Gamma}\psi(x)\partial_{\nu}w^{D}_{l}(x)d\sigma(x)=0,\;l=1,\ldots,N\right\}.

It easily follows from the definition of

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma,\lambda}:H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)\backslash\textrm{Ker}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\rightarrow\textrm{Ran}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\;\;\textrm{is bijective}.

\displaystyle\left(\textrm{Ker}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\right)^{\perp}=\textrm{Ran}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right).

(3.17)

A straightforward calculation gives

\displaystyle\langle S_{\Gamma,-\lambda}\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=\langle\psi_{1},S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\psi_{2}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},\quad\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma).

From this, we arrive at

\displaystyle\textrm{Ker}\left(S_{\Gamma,-\lambda}\right)=\left(\textrm{Ran}\left(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}\right)\right)^{\perp}.

(3.18)

Furthermore, we note that
S_{\Gamma,\lambda}
is a Fredholm operator with index zero mapping from
H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
to
H^{1/2}(\Gamma)
due to the decomposition

\displaystyle S_{\Gamma,\lambda}=S_{\Gamma,0}+(S_{\Gamma,\lambda}-S_{\Gamma,0}).

[
1

.

Since

\displaystyle\left(\Delta+z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{0}\right)\left[-\phi_{z_{0}}-\left({c_{0}^{-2}}-{c_{1}^{-2}}\right)z_{0}^{2}N_{\Omega,z_{0}/c_{0}}\phi_{z_{0}}\right]=0\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}(z)=\begin{bmatrix}&\mathbb{I}&0\\
&0&\frac{\mathbb{I}}{2}\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}\right)z^{2}\penalty\ N_{\Omega,z/c_{0}}&SL_{\Gamma,z/c_{0}}\\

(
c
) Recall that each point

In this subsection, we focus on the asymptotic properties of the Fabry-Pérot resonances associated with the nonzero Neumann eigenvalues. To do so, we introduce several sesquilinear forms. For any

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)\phi:=\sum^{n_{|z_{0}|}}_{l=1}\left\langle\phi,e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|},

We also need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
be such that
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
. Assume that
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}_{z_{0}}}\in\mathbb{C}
is any eigenvalue of the matrix
\mathcal{M}(z_{0})
, where the matrix
\mathcal{M}(z_{0})
is given by (
4.14
).
Then, we have

\displaystyle{\rm{Im}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{M}({z_{0}})})<0.

(4.27)

Proof.

We denote by
V_{\lambda_{\mathcal{M}{(z_{0})}}}
the eigenspace corresponding to
\lambda_{\mathcal{M}{(z_{0})}}
. For any nonzero vector
(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n_{|z_{0}|}})\in V_{\lambda_{\mathcal{M}{(z_{0})}}}
, then

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}({z_{0}})(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n_{|z_{0}|}})=\lambda_{\mathcal{M}({z_{0}})}(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n_{|z_{0}|}}).

This directly implies

\displaystyle(\overline{b_{1}},\ldots,\overline{b_{n_{|z_{0}|}}})\mathcal{M}(z_{0})(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n_{|z_{0}|}})^{T}=\lambda_{\mathcal{M}({z_{0}})}\sum^{n_{|z_{0}|}}_{l=1}|b_{l}|^{2}.

(4.28)

We set
w=\sum^{n_{|z_{0}|}}_{l=1}b_{l}e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}
and consider the subsequent scattering problem

\displaystyle\Delta w^{sc}+z_{0}^{2}/c_{0}^{2}w^{sc}=0\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega,

\displaystyle w^{sc}=\gamma w\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma,

\displaystyle w^{sc}\;\textrm{satisfies}\;z_{0}/c_{0}-\textrm{outgoing}.

If
{\rm{Im}}(\lambda_{\mathcal{M}({z_{0}})})\geq 0
, we deduce from (
4.28
) that

\displaystyle\textrm{Im}\left(\int_{\Gamma}\partial_{\nu}w^{sc}(y)\overline{w^{sc}}(y)d\sigma(y)\right)\leq 0.

Thus, by Rellich lemma, we have
w^{sc}(y)=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Omega
. This directly yields
w=0
on
\Gamma
. Therefore, by Holmgren’s theorem, we obtain
w=0
in
\Omega
, which implies

\displaystyle b_{1}=\cdots=b_{n_{|z_{0}|}}=0.

This leads to a contradiction. Hence, we obtain (
4.27
).
∎

As a consequence of Lemmas

,
4.3
and
4.4
, we obtain the asymptotic expansion of the scattering resonance associated with the nonzero Neumann eigenvalue.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\tau>0
. Assume that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
is a point at which
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega

where

is the eigenvalue of the matrix
\mathcal{M}{(z_{0})}
which is given by (
4.14
).

Proof.

We recall that
z
is a scattering resonance of the Hamiltonian

if and only if there exists a nonzero solution
\phi_{z}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
such that
a_{z,\tau}(\phi_{z},\phi)=0
for every
\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega)
.
Therefore, with the aid Lemma
4.2
, we obtain that

if and only if

is invertible. Moreover, it follows from Lemma

that
\mathcal{M}(z_{0})
is invertible. This, together with the asymptotic expansion (
4.15

) as
\tau\rightarrow 0

∎

{\mathbf{b}}^{f}
satisfy

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{f}=\left[1-\frac{2z_{0}}{c^{2}_{1}}(\kappa-z_{0})\right]\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}\left\langle-f,e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\frac{z^{2}_{0}-\kappa^{2}}{c^{2}_{1}}\left\langle v^{f}_{\kappa},e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right)-\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}

\displaystyle\left[\left(\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{c_{1}^{2}}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)\left\langle\kappa^{2}v^{f}_{\kappa},e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\left\langle f,e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\left\langle\partial^{+}_{\nu}R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{z_{0}}w^{f}_{0,0},e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\right].

(4.52)

Since
\mathcal{M}(z_{0})
is invertible, it can be deduced that

\displaystyle(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|)^{s}\left(2(\kappa-z_{0})z_{0}\mathbb{I}+\tau\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{M}(z_{0})\right)^{-1}\leq C(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|)^{s-1},

(4.53)

as
\tau\rightarrow 0
and
\kappa\rightarrow z_{0}
, where
s\in\{1,2\}
. With the aid of (
4.51
), (
4.52
) and (
4.53
), we have

\displaystyle w^{f}_{\kappa,\tau}=w^{f}_{0,0}+e^{f,1}_{|z_{0}|}+e^{f,2}_{|z_{0}|}+w^{f}_{\mathrm{Res}}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,

(4.54)

where

\displaystyle\|w^{f}_{\mathrm{Res}}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|)\left(\left\|v_{\kappa}^{f}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\left\|e_{|z_{0}|}^{f,1}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right),

(4.55)

as
\tau\rightarrow 0
and
\kappa\rightarrow z_{0}
.
Furthermore, it can be seen from (
4.35
) and (
4.36
) that

\displaystyle DL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\gamma(w^{f}_{0,0}+v^{f}_{\kappa})-SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\left[D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma w^{f}_{0,0}+\partial_{\nu}v^{f}_{\kappa}\right]-DL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\gamma(w^{f}_{0,0}+v^{f}_{\kappa})

\displaystyle=DL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\gamma w^{f}_{0,0}-SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma w^{f}_{0,0}+SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\partial_{\nu}w^{f}_{0,0}-DL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\gamma w^{f}_{0,0}

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}f-\left(\frac{\kappa^{2}}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{\kappa^{2}}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}(w^{f}_{0,0}+v^{f}_{\kappa})+\frac{\kappa^{2}-z^{2}_{0}}{c^{2}_{1}}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}w^{f}_{0,0}

\displaystyle\quad-N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)w^{f}_{0,0}+R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}w^{f}_{0,0},\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(4.56)

Similarly, for any Neumann eigenfunction
e(x)
associated with the eigenvalue
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)\kappa^{2}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}e-SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma e=\frac{\kappa^{2}-z^{2}_{0}}{c^{2}_{1}}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}e+R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}\gamma e\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(4.57)

Using (
4.2
), (
4.54
), (
4.56
) and (
4.57
), we arrive at

\displaystyle w^{f}_{\kappa,\tau}=\sum_{l=1}^{4}R^{(l)}_{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}(\kappa)f,

where

\displaystyle R^{(4)}_{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}(\kappa)f=\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}(D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma w^{f}_{\kappa,\tau}+\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)\kappa^{2}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}w^{f}_{\mathrm{Res}}

\displaystyle-SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}(D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma w^{f}_{\mathrm{Res}}+\frac{\kappa^{2}-z^{2}_{0}}{c^{2}_{1}}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\left(w^{f}_{0,0}+e_{|z_{0}|}^{f,1}+e^{f,2}_{|z_{0}|}\right)\quad\mathrm{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma.

(4.58)

This, together with (
4.54
), (
4.55
) and the fact that

\displaystyle SL_{\Gamma,\kappa/c_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}(H^{-1/2}(\Gamma),L_{-\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))\;\mathrm{and}\;N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\in\mathcal{L}(L^{2}(\Gamma),L_{-\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))

(4.59)

yields the assertion of this theorem.
∎

We conclude this section by investigating the asymptotic behavior of the scattering problem associated with the Hamiltonian
H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}
as
\tau
tends to
0
.

4.3
Asymptotics of the scattered fields

We consider a time-harmonic plane wave
u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}:=e^{i\kappa x\cdot d/c_{0}}
propagating in the direction
d\in\mathbb{S}^{2}
and impinging on
\Omega
,
where
\kappa>0
is a given incident frequency.
Then the scattering by the object
\Omega
can be mathematically formulated as the problem of finding the total field
u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}
such that

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\frac{1}{\rho_{\tau}}\nabla u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}+\kappa^{2}\frac{1}{k_{\tau}}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}=0\;\quad\quad\quad\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}=u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa}+u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\;\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\;\;\;\;\;\text{in}\;\mathbb{R}^{3},

\displaystyle\lim_{|x|\rightarrow+\infty}\left(\frac{x}{|x|}\cdot\nabla-i\frac{\kappa}{c_{0}}\right)u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa}=0.

Here,
\rho_{\tau}
and
k_{\tau}
, specified as (
1.1
), represent the mass density and the bulk modulus of the acoustic medium in the presence of the object
\Omega
, respectively. The mathematical model described above is used to describe the wave propagation in bubbly media within the linar regime, see
[
12
,
13
]
for more details.

The following theorem describes the asymptotic behavior of the above scattering problem as
\tau
tends to
0
.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\kappa,\tau\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\beta>1/2
. Assume that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a point at which
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
, and that the corresponding orthogonal eigenfunctions with respect to the scalar product
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
are denoted by
e^{(1)}_{|z_{0}|},\ldots e^{(n_{|z_{0}|})}_{|z_{0}|}
, where
n_{|z_{0}|}\in\mathbb{N}
. We have

\displaystyle u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{sc}}=R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}-R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}+u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}

(4.60)

with

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{-\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C\left(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|\right)\left\|u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)},

(4.61)

as
\tau\rightarrow 0
and
\kappa\rightarrow z_{0}
,
where
C
is positive constant independent of
\kappa
and
\tau
,
R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}
is given by (
4.32
) and
e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}
is defined by

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}:=\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}\left(2(\kappa-z_{0})z_{0}\mathbb{I}+\tau\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{M}(z_{0})\right)^{-1}\mathbf{b}^{\mathrm{tot}}.

Here,
\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}
is specified in (
4.37
) and the
l-
th entry of the column vector
{\mathbf{b}}^{\mathrm{tot}}
satisfy

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{\mathrm{tot}}=-\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\bigg[\left\langle\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}-\partial^{+}_{\nu}R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{\kappa}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa},e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\bigg],\;\;l=1,\ldots,n_{|z_{0}|}.

Proof.

By a straightforward calculation, for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\backslash\Gamma
, we have

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}(x)=u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}(x)+\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)\kappa^{2}\left(N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}\right)(x)-\left(\frac{\rho_{0}}{\rho_{1}\tau}-1\right)\left(SL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}\right)(x).

(4.62)

Here, the value
u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}
within
\Omega
is determined by

\displaystyle\Delta u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}+\kappa^{2}c_{1}^{-2}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}=0\quad\textrm{in}\;\Omega,

(4.63)

\displaystyle\partial_{\nu}u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{tot}}=\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\tau\left[D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}-D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}+\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}\right]\quad\textrm{on}\;\Gamma.

(4.64)

The above equations (
4.63
)–(
4.64
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle a_{\kappa,\tau}(u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}{|_{\Omega}},\phi)=\left(v_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}},\phi\right)_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\quad\textrm{for}\;\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Here, the sesquilinear form
a_{\kappa,\tau}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is defined as (
4.4
) and
v_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}\in H^{1}(\Omega)
is determined by

\displaystyle\left\langle v_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}},\phi\right\rangle_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=-\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\left\langle\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}-D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa},\gamma\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},\quad\phi\in H^{1}(\Omega).

Clearly,

\displaystyle\left\|v_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\left\|u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(4.65)

Proceeding similarly to the derivation of (
4.51
), and using Lemmas
4.1
and
4.2
, we have

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}

\displaystyle=\left[\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}+O_{H^{1}(\Omega)}(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|)\right]\left(2(\kappa-z_{0})z_{0}\mathbb{I}+\tau\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{M}(z_{0})+O\left(|\kappa-z_{0}|+\tau\right)^{2}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle\quad\left[\left({\mathbf{b}}_{1}^{\mathrm{tot}},\ldots,{\mathbf{b}}_{n_{|z_{0}|}}^{\mathrm{tot}}\right)^{T}+\tau O\left((|\kappa-z_{0}|+\tau)\|v^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\right]+O_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\left(\tau\|v^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\right)\quad{\rm{in}}\;\Omega,

as
\tau\rightarrow 0
and
\kappa\rightarrow z_{0}
. Therefore, with the aid of (
4.53
) and (
4.65
), we find

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}=e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}+u_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}^{\mathrm{tot}}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,

(4.66)

where

\displaystyle\left\|u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\left(\tau+|\kappa-z_{0}|\right)\left\|u_{\kappa}^{\mathrm{in}}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}.

(4.67)

Furthermore, we observe that

\displaystyle-DL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}+SL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\left(\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}-D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}\right)+DL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}=R^{N}_{\kappa}u^{\mathrm{in}}_{\kappa}.

From this, we can use (
4.57
), (
4.62
), (
4.64
) and
(
4.66
) to obtain expansion (
4.60
), where
u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}
is given by

\displaystyle u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{1}{c^{2}_{1}}-\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\right)\kappa^{2}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}-SL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}D_{N}(\kappa/c_{0})\gamma u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}+SL_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}\partial_{\nu}u^{\mathrm{tot}}_{\kappa}

\displaystyle+\frac{\kappa^{2}-z^{2}_{0}}{c^{2}_{1}}N_{\Omega,\kappa/c_{0}}e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}\;\;\mathrm{in}\;\Omega,

whence (
4.61
) follows from (
4.59
), (
4.64
) and (
4.67
).
∎

5
Asymptotic analysis for a microresonator

This section is devoted to investigating the microresonator regime, where the size of
\Omega
and the parameter
\tau
are both very small. To facilitate its presentation, we introduce some new notations. Let
y_{0}
be any fixed point in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, define

\displaystyle\Omega_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}):=\{x:x=y_{0}+\varepsilon(y-y_{0}),\;y\in\Omega\}\;\textrm{and}\;\Gamma_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}):=\partial\Omega_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}),

(5.1)

and the corresponding Hamiltonian
H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}(\Omega_{\varepsilon}(y_{0}))
associated with

is denoted by

. Define

\displaystyle B_{1}(y_{0}):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:|y-y_{0}|<1\}.

For every

, we define its scaled function as

\displaystyle\widetilde{g}(x):=g(y_{0}+\varepsilon(x-y_{0})),\quad\mathrm{for}\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Furthermore, we present the following lemma, whose proof is provided in section
A
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\beta>1/2
. Assume that
y_{0}
is any point in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
\omega
is a nonzero real number. The following arguments hold true.

(a)

Let
\alpha>0
. For any
f\in L^{2}_{\beta}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\left[\frac{e^{i(\omega\varepsilon^{-1}+\omega\varepsilon^{\alpha-1})|y_{0}+\varepsilon(x-y_{0})-y|}}{|y_{0}+\varepsilon(x-y_{0})-y|}-\frac{e^{i{\omega}|y_{0}-y|/\varepsilon}}{4\pi|y_{0}-y|}e^{i{\omega}\hat{y}_{y_{0}}\cdot(y_{0}-x)}\right]f(y)dy\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C\max\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{1/3}\ln\varepsilon\right)\|f\|_{L_{\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(5.2)

(b)

Let
\alpha>1
. For
\phi\in L^{2}(\Omega)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\left[\frac{e^{i(\omega+\omega\varepsilon^{\alpha})|y_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}(x-y_{0})-y|}}{4\pi|y_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}(x-y_{0})-y|}-\varepsilon\frac{e^{i{\omega}|x-y_{0}|/\varepsilon}}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}e^{i\omega\hat{x}_{y_{0}}\cdot(y_{0}-y)}\right]\phi(y)dy\right\|_{L_{-\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\quad\qquad\quad\qquad\leq C\max\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha+1},\varepsilon^{4/3}\ln\varepsilon\right)\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

(5.3)

Furthermore, for
\psi\in H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)
, we have

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Gamma}\left[\frac{e^{i(\omega+\omega\varepsilon^{{\alpha}})|y_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}(x-y_{0})-y|}}{4\pi|y_{0}+\varepsilon^{-1}(x-y_{0})-y|}-\varepsilon\frac{e^{i{\omega}|x-y_{0}|/\varepsilon}}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}e^{i\omega\hat{x}_{y_{0}}\cdot(y_{0}-y)}\right]\psi(y)d\sigma(y)\right\|_{L_{-\beta}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\quad\qquad\quad\quad\leq C\max\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha+1},\varepsilon^{4/3}\ln\varepsilon\right)\|\psi\|_{H^{-1/2}(\Gamma)}.

(5.4)

Here, for each
x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\setminus\{y_{0}\}
, the direction from
x
to
y_{0}
is denoted by

\displaystyle\hat{x}_{y_{0}}:=\frac{x-y_{0}}{|x-y_{0}|},

(5.5)

and
C
is a positive constant independent of
f
,
\varepsilon
and
\alpha
.

5.1
Asymptotics of the resolvents in the presence of a microresonator

We observe from (

)–(

) that for each resonant state

of the Hamiltonian

corresponding to the resonance

associated with the resonance
\varepsilon\lambda^{y_{0}}_{\tau,\varepsilon}
.
Therefore, based on Theorem
4.2
, we can derive the asymptotic behavior of the resolvent
R_{H_{\tau,\varepsilon}(y_{0})}(\omega)
as
\tau
and
\varepsilon
both tend to

, in the regime where the scaled frequency
\varepsilon\omega
is close to
z_{0}
, with
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
being a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\omega\in\mathbb{R}
,
\tau,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
\beta>1/2
. Assume that
z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}
is a point at which
z^{2}_{0}/c^{2}_{1}
is a Neumann eigenvalue of the domain
\Omega
, and that the corresponding orthogonal eigenfunctions with respect to the scalar product

are denoted by

, if

\displaystyle\left(R_{H_{\tau,\varepsilon}(y_{0})}(\omega)f\right)(x)=-\frac{1}{c_{0}^{2}}\left(R_{\omega/c_{0}}f\right)(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{\varepsilon c_{0}}|x-y_{0}|}}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{c_{0}}\hat{x}_{y_{0}}\cdot y_{0}}\left(\mathcal{S}_{z_{0}}^{\infty,D}\left[\gamma e^{f,0}_{|z_{0}|}+\gamma e^{f,1}_{|z_{0}|}+\gamma e^{f,2}_{|z_{0}|}-\gamma\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}\right]\right)(\hat{x}_{y_{0}})+R_{\mathrm{Res}}(\omega)f(x)

(5.6)

with

,
where

is a positive constant independent of

,
\omega
,
\varepsilon
and
\tau
,

and
\hat{x}_{y_{0}}
are specified in (
4.31
) and (
5.5
), respectively, and
e^{f,j}_{|z_{0}|}

(j=0,1,2)
are defined as

\displaystyle e^{f,j}_{|z_{0}|}:=\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}\left(\mathrm{sgn}(\varepsilon\omega-z_{0})2z_{0}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}+\tau\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{M}(z_{0})\right)^{-1}{\mathbf{b}}^{f,j}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega.

Here,
\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}
is defined in (
4.37
) and
{\mathbf{b}}^{f,j}
are column vectors whose
l-
th entry (

) given by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{f,0}:=\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{f,1}:=\frac{-\varepsilon^{2}}{c^{2}_{1}}\left\langle f(y_{0}),e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{f,2}=-\mathrm{sgn}(\varepsilon\omega-z_{0})\frac{2z_{0}\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{c^{2}_{1}}\left\langle\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f},e^{(1)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad-\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\left\langle\partial^{+}_{\nu}R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{z_{0}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}-\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}\right),e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},

where
\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)
is specified in (

) and
\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}(x):=\frac{1}{c^{2}_{0}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{\varepsilon c_{0}}|y_{0}-y|}}{4\pi|y_{0}-y|}e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{c_{0}}\hat{y}_{y_{0}}\cdot(y_{0}-x)}f(y)dy,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon

v^{f}_{\omega}:=-{c_{0}^{-2}}R_{\omega/c_{0}}f
.
It is easy to verify that

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}^{f}_{\omega,\varepsilon}=u^{\varepsilon^{2}f}_{\varepsilon\omega,\tau}\;\;\textrm{and}\;\;\widetilde{v}^{f}_{\omega}=v^{\varepsilon^{2}f}_{\varepsilon\omega},

where
u^{\varepsilon^{2}f}_{\varepsilon\omega,\tau}

) and (

Therefore, using Theorem

\displaystyle\widetilde{u}^{f}_{\omega,\varepsilon}-\widetilde{v}^{f}_{\omega}=\sum^{4}_{j=1}R^{(j)}_{H_{\rho_{\tau},k_{\tau}}}(\varepsilon\omega)\left(\varepsilon^{2}\widetilde{f}\right)=:\sum^{4}_{j=1}\widetilde{u}^{f,j}_{\omega,\varepsilon}.

When

, utilizing statement (

) of Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\left\|v^{\varepsilon^{2}f}_{\varepsilon\omega}-\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\max\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{1/3}\ln\varepsilon\right)\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)}\quad\textrm{for}\;f\in L_{\mathrm{comp}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

(5.7)

In the sequel, we estimate the asymptotic behavior of

with

. For the estimate of

, combining (

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{f}-f(y_{0})\|\leq C\varepsilon^{1/2}\|f\|_{H^{2}(B_{1}(y_{0}))}

\displaystyle w^{\varepsilon^{2}\widetilde{f}}_{0,0}=\mathcal{P}^{N}(|z_{0}|)\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}-\hat{v}_{z_{0}}^{f}+w^{\mathrm{Res}}_{0,0}

(5.8)

\displaystyle\left\|w^{\mathrm{Res}}_{0,0}\right\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\max\left(\varepsilon^{\alpha},\varepsilon^{1/3}\ln\varepsilon\right)\left[\|f\|_{L^{2}\left(\mathbb{R}^{3}\right)}+\|f\|_{H^{2}\left(B_{1}(y_{0})\right)}\right].

(5.9)

5.2

Following the approach adopted in the proof of Theorem

Theorem 5.2
.

Let
\omega,\tau,\varepsilon\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
\beta>1/2
, and let

. Assume that

is a point at which

\displaystyle u_{\omega}^{\mathrm{sc}}(x)=\frac{\varepsilon e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{\varepsilon c_{0}}|x-y_{0}|}}{4\pi|x-y_{0}|}u_{z_{0}/\varepsilon}^{\mathrm{in}}(y_{0})e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{c_{0}}(\hat{x}_{y_{0}}-d)\cdot y_{0}}\mathcal{S}_{z_{0}}^{\infty,D}\left(\gamma e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}-\gamma u_{z_{0}}^{\mathrm{in}}\right)(\hat{x}_{y_{0}})+u^{\mathrm{sc}}_{\kappa,\mathrm{Res}}(x)

, where

\omega
,
\varepsilon
and
\tau
,
S_{z_{0}}^{\infty,D}
and

are specified in (

) and (

), respectively, and

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}:=\mathbf{e}_{|z_{0}|}\left(\mathrm{sgn}(\varepsilon\omega-z_{0})2z_{0}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}+\tau\frac{\rho_{1}}{\rho_{0}}\mathcal{M}(z_{0})\right)^{-1}{\mathbf{b}}^{\mathrm{tot}}\quad\mathrm{in}\;\Omega.

\displaystyle{\mathbf{b}}_{l}^{\mathrm{tot}}=-\frac{\rho_{1}\tau}{\rho_{0}}\left\langle\partial_{\nu}u_{z_{0}}^{\mathrm{in}}-\partial^{+}_{\nu}R^{\mathrm{ex}}_{z_{0}}u_{z_{0}}^{\mathrm{in}},e^{(l)}_{|z_{0}|}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)},\quad l=1,\ldots,n_{|z_{0}|}.

Remark 5.1
.

We state the following two comments.

1.

Small scatterers are called isotropic point-scatterers if the generated dominant field (which is related to the one of the equivalent point-like scatterer) has an amplitude which is independent on the directions of incidence or propagation. If not, we call them anisotropic small scatterers. To the best of our knowledge, the low-frequency microresonators are all isotropic. The expansion in (
5.10
) shows that at high frequencies, the microresonator is anisotropic, via the terms
e^{i\frac{z_{0}}{c_{0}}(\hat{x}_{y_{0}}-d)\cdot y_{0}}
and
\mathcal{S}_{z_{0}}^{\infty,D}\left(\gamma e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}-\gamma u_{z_{0}}^{\mathrm{in}}\right)(\hat{x}_{y_{0}})
. Near the Minnaert resonance (which is a low-frequency resonance), the scatterer behaves as an isotropic one, see e.g.,
[
6
,
18
]
. Therefore, the anisotropy is created at high frequency resonances. Such a feature may open new possibilities for designing anisotropic metamaterials from simple configurations of a single microresonator. In recent years, the study of high frequency homogenization has attracted significant interest due to its practical potential implications. We believe that the expansion derived in (
5.10
) is a good first step in achieving such a goal.

2.

Observe that the expansion (
5.10
) relates the field
u_{\omega}^{\mathrm{sc}}(x)
, generated by the small scaled scatterer
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})
, to the far-filed pattern
\mathcal{S}_{z_{0}}^{\infty,D}\left(\gamma e^{\mathrm{tot}}_{|z_{0}|}-\gamma u_{z_{0}}^{\mathrm{in}}\right)(\hat{x}_{y_{0}})
of the extended obstacle
\Omega
, see (
5.1
). Such a relation suggests that by measuring the field
u_{\omega}^{\mathrm{sc}}(x)
away from the small scatterer
\Omega_{\varepsilon}(y_{0})
, we can have access to the far-field pattern of
\Omega
. Therefore, this relation is expected to be useful in designing inversion strategies, not only to localize the small scatterer and estimate its size (as it is done in the traditional inverse problems community using MUSIC type algorithms for instance), but also recover the details of the scatterer’s shape
\partial\Omega
. Indeed, as we can recover the far-field pattern, then we can us the machinery already developed in the literature (e.g.,
[
17
]
) to recover shapes from far-field measurements.

This section provides a brief introduction to the Gohberg and Sigal theory used in this paper, for more details, we refer to

Definition 2
.

Let
X
and
Y
be two Banach spaces. Suppose that
V\subset\mathbb{C}
is a connected open set. We say that
\lambda\mapsto B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}(X,Y)
is a meromorphic family of operators in
V
if for any
\mu\in V
, there exist operators
B_{j},\;1\leq j\leq J
, with a finite range, and a family of operators
\lambda\mapsto B_{0}(\lambda)
, holomorphic near
z
, such that

\displaystyle B(\lambda)=B_{0}(\lambda)+\frac{B_{1}}{\lambda-\mu}+\cdots\frac{B_{J}}{(\lambda-\mu)^{J}}\quad\textrm{near}\;\mu.

(A.1)

Furthermore, we say that
B(\lambda)
is a meromorphic family of Fredholm operators if for every
\mu\in V
,
B_{0}(\lambda)
is a Fredholm operator for
\lambda
near
\mu
. In particular, for nonsingular
\mu
,
B_{0}(\lambda)=B(\lambda)
.

Definition 3
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. We say that
\lambda\mapsto B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
is normal at
\mu
if
B(\mu)
has an expansion (
A.1
) with
B_{0}(\mu)
being a Fredholm operator of index
0
, and
B(\lambda)
is holomorphic and invertible in a punctured neighborhood of
\mu
. Furthermore, we call that
B(\lambda)
is normal with respect to
\partial V
if the operator
B(\lambda)
is invertible in
\overline{V}
, except for a finite number of points which are normal points of
B(\lambda)
. Here,
V
is a simply bounded domain with rectifiable boundary
\partial V
.

Theorem A.1
.

Let
X
be a Banach space. Suppose that
\lambda\mapsto B(\lambda)\in\mathcal{L}(X)
with
\lambda\in V
is a meromorphic family of Fredholm operators. Here,

is a simply bounded domain with rectifiable boundary

. If

\mu

\displaystyle B(\lambda)=G_{1}(\lambda)\left(\mathcal{P}_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{J}(\lambda-\mu)^{k_{l}}\mathcal{P}_{l}\right)G_{2}(\lambda),\quad k_{l}\in\mathbb{Z}\backslash\{0\}.

(A.2)

Here,
\mathcal{P}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{P}_{J}
are one-dimensional operators,
\mathcal{P}_{l_{1}}\mathcal{P}_{l_{2}}=\delta_{l_{1}l_{2}}\mathcal{P}_{l_{2}}
with

Remark A.1
.

Let

be the family of operators considered in Theorem

(a)

is normal at

if and only if

\displaystyle\left(B(\lambda)\right)^{-1}=\left(G_{2}(\lambda)\right)^{-1}\left(\mathcal{P}_{0}+\sum_{l=1}^{J}(\lambda-\mu)^{-k_{l}}\mathcal{P}_{l}\right)\left(G_{1}(\lambda)\right)^{-1},\quad k_{l}\in\mathbb{N}\backslash\{0\}.

(b)

The factorization (
A.2
) of

provides a definition of a null multiplicity of
B(\lambda)
at
\mu
, defined by

\displaystyle N_{\mu}(B):=\begin{cases}\sum_{k_{l}>0}k_{l}&{\rm{if}}\;J={\rm{dim}}({\rm{Ran}}(\mathbb{I}-P_{0})),\\
\infty&{\rm{if}}\;J<{\rm{dim}}({\rm{Ran}}(\mathbb{I}-P_{0})).\end{cases}

When

,

is meromorphic and

\displaystyle N_{\mu}(B^{-1}):=\sum_{k_{l}<0}k_{l}.

(Generalized Rouché’s Theorem)

be a Banach space. Suppose that

A.2.3 Proof of inequality (
5.34
)

Proof of inequality (
5.34
).

Given
\lambda\in\mathbb{C}_{+}
, consider a sesqulinear form

\displaystyle b_{\lambda}(h_{1},h_{2}):=\overline{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{\lambda^{2}}{k_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}(h_{1}\overline{h_{2}})(x)dx+\overline{\lambda}\frac{1}{\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}(\nabla h_{1}\cdot\nabla\overline{h_{2}})(x)dx,\quad h_{1},h_{2}\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}).

Clearly,
b_{\lambda}
is coercive in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Therefore, given
\lambda\in\Gamma_{\sigma}
. For any
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, there exists a unique
h^{f}(x,\lambda)
satisfying

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda^{2}}{k_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}h^{f}(x,\lambda)-\nabla\cdot\frac{1}{\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}\nabla h^{f}(x,\lambda)=f(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(A.13)

We note that
h^{f}=R_{H_{\tau,\varepsilon}}(\lambda)k_{\tau,\varepsilon}f
. It follows from (
A.13
) that

\displaystyle\overline{\lambda}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{|\lambda|^{2}}{k_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}|h^{f}(x,\lambda)|^{2}

\displaystyle+\overline{\lambda}\frac{1}{\rho_{\tau,\varepsilon}(x)}\left|\nabla h^{f}(x,\lambda)\right|^{2}dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\overline{\lambda}f(x)\overline{h^{f}(x,\lambda)}dx,

whence (
5.34
) follows.
∎

A.3 Fourier-Laplace transform

For any
\varphi\in C(\mathbb{R};L_{\textrm{comp}}^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))
, its Fourier-Laplace transform
\hat{\varphi}
is defined by

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{F}\varphi(x,\cdot)\right)(s):=\hat{\varphi}(x,s)=\int^{+\infty}_{0}e^{-st}\varphi(x,t)dt,\quad s\in\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\textrm{Re}(z)>0\},

(A.14)

It is well known that

\displaystyle\Theta(t)\varphi(x,t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}e^{(\textrm{Re}(s)+i\xi)t}\hat{\varphi}(x,\textrm{Re}(s)+i\xi)d\xi.

Here,
\Theta(t)
is a heaviside function, which is defined as

\displaystyle\Theta(t):=\begin{cases}0,&t<0,\\
1,&t\geq 0.\end{cases}

Yilin Song, Ruixiao Zhang, Jiqiang Zheng

. 1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the growth of higher-order Sobolev norms for solutions to the Cauchy problem of nonlinear Schrödinger equation(NLS) with harmonic oscillator of the form

\displaystyle\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u-Hu=\left|u\right|^{2}u,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(x,0)=u_{0}(x)\in\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the operator
H
is defined as

\displaystyle H=-\Delta+\left|x\right|^{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\Big(-\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial x_{j}^{2}}+x_{j}^{2}\Big)

and
\mathcal{H}^{s}
the Sobolev space associated with the harmonic oscillator
H
, which reads as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|H^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In particular,
\mathcal{H}^{1}=H^{1}\cap L^{2}(\left|x\right|^{2}dx)
, where
H^{1}
denotes the classical Sobolev space. Similarly, we can define the distorted Sobolev space
\mathcal{W}^{s,p}
as

\displaystyle\|u\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\|H^{\frac{s}{2}}u\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

In
[
18
]
, Dziubanski and Glowacki proved the following equivalence of Sobolev norms: for every
s\geq 0
, there exists
C>0
such that

\frac{1}{C}\Big(\big\||\nabla|^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\langle x\rangle^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)\leq\|u\|_{{\mathcal{H}}^{s}}^{2}\leq C\Big(\big\||\nabla|^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\big\|\langle x\rangle^{s}u\big\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big).

(1.2)

From the above equivalence, the Hermite-Sobolev norm can be understood as the weighted Sobolev norm. The Schrödinger equation with harmonic oscillator models magnetic traps in the Bose-Einstein condensation. The trapping potential makes all the spectrum discrete on Euclidean space. We denote by
\{\tilde{h}_{m}\}_{m=0}^{\infty}
an orthogonal basis of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying that

\displaystyle H_{0}\tilde{h}_{m}(x)=(-\partial_{x}^{2}+x^{2})\tilde{h}_{m}(x)=(2m+1)\tilde{h}_{m}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}.

For general dimensions, the eigen-basis can be generated by tensor product of
\tilde{h}_{m}(x_{j})
,

\displaystyle(-\Delta+|x|^{2})h_{m}(x)=(2|m|+d)h_{m}(x),\quad h_{m}(x)=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\tilde{h}_{m}(x_{j}),\;x=(x_{1},\cdots,x_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In recent years, growth of Sobolev norms for solution to the dispersive equation gathered a lot of attention because of the strong connection with weak wave turbulence phenomenon. However, this phenomenon may not occur if the dispersive effect is strong. For the pure Euclidean space, Dodson
[
19
]
proved the scattering theory for cubic NLS in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
. By using the persistence of regularity, one can verify that the higher order Sobolev norm will not grow up. For
d=3
, Ginibre-Velo
[
20
]
proved the scattering in
d=3
in energy space and the higher Sobolev norm will not grow up. For general nonlinearities
F(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
, if
1+\frac{4}{d}<p\leq 1+\frac{4}{d-2}
, one can show the scattering in
\dot{H}^{1}
, see
[
24
]
for more information.

The growth of Sobolev norm can be expected when considering the Schrödinger equation on compact manifold or with some trapping potential due to the lack of dispersive effect. From the local well-posedness, one can easily obtain the exponential bound on the Sobolev norm. The first polynomial upper bound of growth of Sobolev norm was proved by Bourgain
[
3
]
for the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{2}

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}u=|u|^{2}u

via the Fourier multiplier method:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C|t|^{2(s-1)+}.

For the Euclidean case, Staffilani
[
37
]
established the polynomial bound
|t|^{(s-1)+}
. For the circle
\mathbb{T}^{1}
, Bourgain
[
3
]
introduced the upside-down
I
-method and the normal form approach to obtain the polynomial bound for quintic NLS

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{\frac{1}{2}(s-1)+}.

Later, Colliander-Kwon-Oh
[
12
]
generalized the result of
[
3
]
to general nonlinearity. Recently, Berti-Planchon-Tzvetkov-Visciglia
[
2
]
further improve the result of
[
12
]
by constructing the modified energy. For Hartree equation in
d=2

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u+\Delta u=(V*|u|^{2})u,

Sohinger
[
35
]
established the following polynomial bound under some assumption on
V
:

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{\frac{4}{7}(s-1)+},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{(s-1)+},\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{2}.

His proof relies on the combination of upside-down
I
-method and resonant decomposition technique initially developed in
[
10
]
. For one-dimensional Hartree equation, we refer to
[
36
,
35
]
.

Very recently, Takaoka
[
38
]
used the upside-down
I
-method combined with the resonant decomposition technique to show the improved bound for the cubic NLS on
\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T}

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{T})}\leqslant C|t|^{\frac{1}{2}(s-1)+}.

For the three-dimensional waveguide
\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{T}^{n}
with
m+n=3
, Zhao-Zheng
[
39
]
showed that the bound

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{T}^{n})}\leqslant C(1+|t|).

For the compact manifold without boundary, assuming that the following bilinear Strichartz estimate holds for
s\geq s_{0}
,

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}\Delta_{N}fe^{it\Delta_{g}}\Delta_{M}g\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,1]\times M)}\lesssim\min\{N,M\}^{s}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(M)},

Zhong
[
40
]
showed the polynomial bound

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(M)}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{A(s-1)+},

where

\displaystyle A^{-1}=\begin{cases}(1-2s_{0})-,&d=2,\\
\big(1-\frac{d-2}{2(d-2s_{0})}\big)\big(1-\frac{d-2}{d-s_{0}-1}\big)-,&d=3.\end{cases}

(1.3)

In
[
26
]
, for general compact manifold without boundary, Hani showed the bilinear Strichartz estimate holds for
s_{0}=\frac{1}{2}
in
d=2
and
s_{0}=\frac{d-1}{2}
in
d\geq 3
. Therefore, the above bound in
d=2
becomes
|t|^{K(s-1)+}
with
K=\infty-
.

Later, under the assumption on the following
L^{4}
Strichartz estimate

\displaystyle\big\|e^{it\Delta_{g}}\Delta_{N}f\big\|_{L^{4}([0,T]\times M)}\lesssim N^{s_{0}}\|f\|_{L^{2}(M)},

Planchon, Tzvetkov and Visciglia
[
33
]
developed the modified energy method and showed the improved bound in
d=2
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(M)}\lesssim(1+|t|)^{\frac{1}{1-2s_{0}}(s-1)+}.

In
[
4
,
5
]
, for general compact manifold without boundary,
s_{0}\leq\frac{1}{4}
, which implies the polynomial bound is at most
|t|^{2(s-1)+}
.

For quintic NLS on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, the global well-posedness was established in Ionescu-Pausader
[
30
]
. However, whether its Sobolev norm grows up is still an open question. When the initial data has small energy, Deng
[
15
]
established the following bound

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{3})}\lesssim\max\big\{\|u_{0}\|_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{3})},|t|^{300(s-1)}\big\}

where
\mathbb{T}_{\lambda}^{3}
is the irrational tori. It remains open for the large energy case. For linear Schrödinger equation with potential in compact manifold, we refer to
[
13
]
for more details.

For the Schrödinger operator with purely trapping potential, i.e.
-\Delta+V(x)
, one can also expect the similar phenomenon holds. The typical model of potential is that
V(x)=|x|^{2}
. Using the modified energy method, Planchon-Tzvetkov-Visciglia
[
34
]
showed the polynomial growth in
d=2
,

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim C(1+|t|)^{\frac{2}{3}(s-1)+},

(1.4)

For the partial harmonic oscillator, i.e.
V(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}w_{j}x_{j}^{2}
with
w_{j}\in\{0,1\}
, the partial dispersive effect will impact the growth of Sobolev norm. For example, Cheng-Guo-Guo-Liao-Shen
[
9
]
showed the Sobolev norm for the
3d
cubic NLS with one-dimensional harmonic oscillator will not grow up. For the lower-dimension case
H=-\partial_{x}^{2}-\partial_{y}^{2}+y^{2}
in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, Deng-Su-Zheng
[

]
showed the same bound (
1.4
). For other nonlinearity, Killip-Visan-Zhang
[

]
and Jao
[

]
proved the global well-posedness for the energy-critical case. It is also interesting to study the growth of Sobolev norm for the energy-critical NLS with harmonic potential.

The lower bound of growth of Sobolev norms is also interesting problem. Colliander-Keel-Staffilani-Takaoka-Tao
[
11
]
showed that there exists a time
T>0
such that the solution
\|u(t)\|_{H^{s}}\geq K
with initial data

,

. Guardia-Kaloshin
[

.
For the waveguide mannifold

, Hani-Pausader-Tzvetkov-Visciglia

constructed the growing-up solution with the rate

. There exists a global solution

to cubic NLS such that for

Furthermore, there exists a sequence

For the case of harmonic oscillator, Chabert

1.1.
Main Results

Our main goal of this paper is to show the polynomial bound for the cubic Schrödinger equation with harmonic potential in dimensions
d\in\{2,3\}
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
s>1
, there exists a constant
C:=C(\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})})
such that the following holds: the global solution
u(t)\in C(\mathbb{R},\mathcal{H}^{s})
to (
1.1
) satisfies

\displaystyle\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leqslant C(1+|t|)^{\tilde{s}_{0}(s-1)+},\quad\forall\;t\in\mathbb{R},

with

\displaystyle\tilde{s}_{0}=\begin{cases}\frac{2}{3},&d=2,\\
1,&d=3.\end{cases}

Remark 1.1
.

Based on the refined Strichartz estimate on irrational tori in
[
17
]
, Deng-Germain
[
16
]
showed the same bound for the growth rate of Sobolev norms via the upside-down
I
-method. Our result in
d=3
match the same bound as in
[
16
]
. Moreover, we can show the similar bound holds for solution to the cubic Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, which will appear in the future work.

Remark 1.2
.

Using our method and the standard
I
-method, we can show the global well-posedness for (
1.1
) in low regularity space
\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for some
s_{loc}<s<1
, where
s_{loc}
is the regularity such that if the regularity of the initial data is below it, the solution will be ill-posed. We leave it for interesting readers.

1.2.
Outline of the proof

Our first result is a bilinear estimate for harmonic oscillator in dimension
d\geq 2
without
\varepsilon
loss. Let
u_{N},v_{M}
be both frequency localized with
M\leq N
. For further application, we consider a more general bilinear estimate

\|P(\alpha)e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot P(\beta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim\frac{M^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}N^{\mathrm{Ord}(\alpha)}M^{\mathrm{Ord}(\beta)}\|u_{N}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{M}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.5)

Here
P\in\{\nabla,x\}
and
\alpha,\beta
are the multi-index, which will be defined in Definition
2.1
. Also,
\operatorname{Ord}(\alpha)
will be defined in Definition
2.1
. When
\alpha=\beta=0
,
P(\alpha),P(\beta)
degenerate to the identical operator.

For
d=2
and
\alpha=\beta=0
, in
[
34
]
, Planchon, Tzvetkov and Visciglia proved bilinear estimates for harmonic oscillator in the sense that there is no derivatives loss. Let
u_{N}:=e^{itH}u_{N}(0)
and
v_{M}:=e^{itH}v_{M}(0)
, they first utilized virial argument to show that

\displaystyle\int\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)+\overline{v}_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

(1.6)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle N\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

Combining this with a point-wise bound in
[
32
]

\displaystyle|\phi(x)|^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{2}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\Delta\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{2}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y,

they proved that

\|e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{2}\dot{H}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim M^{2}N\|u_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(1.7)

Moreover, by the Strichartz inequality and Sobolev embedding, one has

\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}|x|^{2}|u_{N}v_{M}|^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t\lesssim M^{2}N\|u_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(1.8)

Together with (
1.7
) and (
1.8
), they got

\|e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L_{t}^{2}\mathcal{H}^{1}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{2})}\lesssim M^{2}N\|u_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}\|v_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})}.

(1.9)

By using Littlewood-Paley decomposition to
u_{N}v_{M}
, one can get (
1.5
) for
d=2
and
\alpha=\beta=0
.

Our first contribution is extending (
1.5
) to
d\geq 3
, which removes the
\delta
-derivative loss of
[
7
]
. Recall that in
[
34
]
, they use Strichartz inequality and change of variables to get that

\mbox{(LHS) of }\eqref{fml-Intro-bilinear-xy}\lesssim\int_{|z|<M^{-1}}M^{d}\|u_{N}\|_{L_{t}^{p(d)}([0,T],L_{x}^{p(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\|\nabla v_{M}\|_{L_{t}^{q(d)}([0,T],L_{x}^{q(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\mathrm{d}z.

When
d=2
, we may take
p(2)=q(2)=4
and notice that
(4,4)
is an admissible pair, thus Strichartz inequality can be applied directly. However, for
d\geq 3
,
(p(d),p(d))
and
(q(d),q(d))
can not be admissible pair at the same time. For example we take
p(3)=\frac{10}{3}
and
q(3)=5
, it is clear that
(5,5)
is not an admissible pair. Since
u_{M}
or
v_{N}
is spectrally localized, the operator
|\nabla|
cannot commute with Littlewood-Paley projector
\Delta_{N}
associated to
H
, which makes it difficult to use Bernstein’s inequality directly. In the following, we are devoted to estimate

\|P(\theta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L_{t}^{q(d)}([0,T],L_{x}^{q(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

with order
\mathrm{Ord}(\theta)=1
and
q(d)>\frac{2(d+2)}{d}
. By Sobolev’s embedding, we can obtain an admissible pair but with higher regularity:

\displaystyle\|P(\theta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L_{t}^{q(d)}([0,T],L_{x}^{q(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|P(\theta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{q(d)}_{t}([0,T],\dot{W}_{x}^{s(d),r(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\||\nabla|^{r(d)}P(\theta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{q(d)}_{t}([0,T],L_{x}^{s(d)}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))},

where
(q(d),r(d))
is admissible and
r(d)>0
. Thanks to the interpolation, we may assume that
r(d)
is an positive even integer, and we can write
|\nabla|^{r(d)}P(\theta)=P(\widetilde{\theta})
for some multi-index
\widetilde{\theta}
. Observing that
P(\widetilde{\theta})e^{itH}v_{M}(0)
satisfies

(i\partial_{t}+H)(P(\widetilde{\theta})e^{itH}v_{M}(0))=[P(\widetilde{\theta}),H]v_{M}.

Hence, using the inhomogeneous Strichartz inequality and the fact that
[P(\widetilde{\theta}),H]
can be regarded as linear combination of
P(\theta^{\prime})
with
\mathrm{Ord}(\theta^{\prime})=\mathrm{Ord}(\theta)
(even
H
is order
2
), we finally get

\big\|[P(\widetilde{\theta}),H]v_{M}\big\|_{L^{1}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\lesssim M^{|\widetilde{\theta}|}\|v_{M}\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

The detailed proof will be postponed in Section
3
.

The above estimate shows that the commutator
[P(\widetilde{\theta}),H]
is harmless, this fact can be adapted to extend (
1.5
) to non-trivial multi-index
\alpha,\beta
. Up to harmless commutators
[P(\alpha),H]
and
[P(\beta),H]
, we can reduce (
1.5
) to estimate

\|e^{itH}P(\alpha)u_{N}(0)\cdot e^{itH}P(\beta)v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}.

Strichartz inequality can be applied directly. See Corollary
3.1
in Section
3
for the proof of (
1.5
) for non-trivial multi-index
\alpha,\beta
.

After the preparation for the bilinear Strichartz estimate, we return back to the proof of our main theorem on the growth of Sobolev norm. Though for any
s\geq 1
, problem (
1.1
) is globally well-posed for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. However, since the lack of conservation laws in higher-order regularity, we can not obtain uniformly bound for the norm
\|u(t,\cdot)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}
. Here, we define a upside-down
I
-operator which can help us reduce the regularity to the energy level.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
,
u\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\mu_{k}
be the
k-
th eigenvalue associated with
\sqrt{H}
, we define operator
I_{N}
as

I_{N}u:=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{N}}m(N^{-1}\mu_{k})\pi_{\mu_{k}}u,

with smooth symbol
m(\xi)
satisfying

m(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,\,\,&|\xi|\leq 1,\\
|\xi|^{s-1},\,\,&|\xi|\geq 2.\end{cases}

In the rest of this paper, we abbreviate
I_{N}
to
I
. Thus, the
I
operator can suppress the regularity of the initial data
u_{0}
and gives that

N^{1-s}\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|Iu_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|u_{0}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{s}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Acting
I
operator on both side of (
1.1
), we derive the
I
-system

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}Iu-HIu=I\big(\left|u\right|^{2}u\big),&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
u(x,0)=Iu_{0}(x)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{cases}

(1.10)

We can define the energy of (
1.10
) as

E(Iu):=\frac{1}{2}\|H^{\frac{1}{2}}Iu\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\frac{1}{4}\|Iu\|^{4}_{L^{4}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(1.11)

Since
Iu
is not a real solution to (
1.1
),
E(Iu)
does not conserve under the evolution of (
1.10
). Hence, to control the increment of energy
(
1.11
)
, we are devoted to consider how the commutator
|Iu|^{2}Iu-I\big(|u|^{2}u\big)
affect (
1.10
).(Note that if we replace the nonlinearity of (
1.10
) with
-|Iu|^{2}Iu
, energy (
1.11
) conserves).

Thanks to (
1.10
), we have the rate of change of energy with respect with time
t
:

\frac{d}{dt}E(Iu)(t)=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{Iu_{t}}(-I(|u|^{2}u)+|Iu|^{2}Iu)dx,

and by the fundamental theorem of calculus, we find that

\displaystyle E(Iu)(t)-E(Iu)(0)

(1.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re i\sum_{\mu_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})}\big)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{1}}(HIu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{2}}(Iu)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{3}}(Iu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{4}}(Iu)dxdt

\displaystyle+\Re i\sum_{\mu_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})}\big)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{1}}(I(|u|^{2}u))}){\pi}_{\mu_{2}}(Iu)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{3}}(Iu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{4}}(Iu)dxdt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2},

for all

and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\mu_{3},\mu_{4}
be the eigenvalue associated with

.

In the sequel, we will estimate the two parts in (

. Without loss of generality, we assume

. Therefore, we only need to prove the energy increment in a spectrally-localized version. More precisely, we will show that there exists

(1.13)

for each dyadic pieces.

It is worth to mention that the most hard case is the frequency-interaction of the type “high-high-low-low”, i.e.

. In this case, the multiplier

\left|1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})}\right|=\left|1-\Big(\frac{\mu_{1}}{\mu_{2}}\Big)^{s-1}\right|,

changes. Indeed, without using (

), merely use
1

instead of

, the energy increment for

reads

\left|E(Iu)(t)-E(Iu)(0)\right|\lesssim N^{-1+},

as

. It is worse than what we expect.

Since (

) is very close to
0
when

, taking

as a trivial upper bound for (

then, we have

Here,

where

with

and

. Here, we refer to Definition
2.1

Assuming that

for some

and

\displaystyle[N_{1},2N_{1})=\bigcup_{a}I_{a}:=\bigcup_{a}\big[N_{1}+aN_{3},N_{1}+(a+1)N_{3}\big),

\displaystyle[N_{2},2N_{2})=\bigcup_{b}I_{b}:=\bigcup_{b}\big[N_{1}+bN_{3},N_{1}+(b+1)N_{3}\big).

According to the distance between

\displaystyle S_{1}:=\big\{(I_{a},I_{b}):a-b>8,\,\,I_{a}\subset[N_{1},2N_{1}),\,\,I_{b}\subset[N_{2},2N_{2})\big\},

\displaystyle S_{2}:=\big\{(I_{a},I_{b}):a-b<-8,\,\,I_{a}\subset[N_{1},2N_{1}),\,\,I_{b}\subset[N_{2},2N_{2})\big\},

\displaystyle S_{3}:=\big\{(I_{a},I_{b}):|a-b|\leq 8,\,\,I_{a}\subset[N_{1},2N_{1}),\,\,I_{b}\subset[N_{2},2N_{2})\big\}.

\displaystyle\sum_{S_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\int\left[1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})}\right]\frac{(-2)^{3}}{(\mu_{1}^{2}-\mu_{2}^{2}-\mu_{3}^{2}-\mu_{4}^{2})^{3}}P_{I_{a}}\overline{\pi_{\mu_{1}}(u_{1})}\times{\widetilde{R}_{3}(P_{I_{b}}\pi_{\mu_{2}}(u_{2}),\pi_{\mu_{3}}(u_{3}),\pi_{\mu_{4}}(u_{4}))}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sum_{S_{1}}\int_{0}^{T}\int\widetilde{m}(\mu_{1},\mu_{3},\mu_{3},\mu_{4})P_{I_{a}}\overline{\pi_{\mu_{1}}(u_{1})}\times{\widetilde{R}_{3}(P_{I_{b}}\pi_{\mu_{2}}(u_{2}),\pi_{\mu_{3}}(u_{3}),\pi_{\mu_{4}}(u_{4}))}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}t.

Meanwhile, we obtain a more refined multiplier expression on different intervals:

\widetilde{m}(\mu_{1},\mu_{3},\mu_{3},\mu_{4})=\left[1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})}\right]\frac{(-2)^{3}}{(\mu_{1}^{2}-\mu_{2}^{2}-\mu_{3}^{2}-\mu_{4}^{2})^{3}}.

(1.17)

Next, applying the Coifman-Meyer multiplier theorem associated to

while eliminating the derivatives loss which brought by

For term

Thus, together with simple argument in Section

2.1.
Eigen-problems of harmonic oscillator and function space

Let
\lambda_{m}
be the eigenvalue of
\sqrt{H}
and
\{h_{m}\}
be the family of
L^{2}
orthogonal basis with multi-index
m=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{d})
. First we give the definition of eigenfunctions of Hermite operator when
d=1
.
h_{m}
are Hermite functions(
d=1
), which is defined as follows. For each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the Hermite function of degree
m
is defined by

\displaystyle h_{m}(t)=(2^{m}m!\sqrt{\pi})^{-\frac{1}{2}}H_{m}(t)e^{-\frac{t^{2}}{2}},\quad m=0,1,2\cdots,

where
H_{m}(t)
denotes the Hermite polynomials which reads as

\displaystyle H_{m}(t)=(-1)^{m}e^{t^{2}}\frac{d^{m}}{dt^{m}}(e^{-t^{2}}).

Given the multi-index
m=(m_{1},\cdots,m_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, the
d

\displaystyle h_{m}(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{d}h_{m_{i}}(x_{i}),m=m_{1}+\cdots+m_{d}

and the eigenvalues associated with

is
2\left|m\right|+d
. More precisely, we have

\displaystyle Hh_{m}(x)=\big(2\left|m\right|+d\big)h_{m}(x)=\Big(2\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}+d\Big)\prod_{j=1}^{d}h_{m_{j}}(x_{j}).

For different choices of
m
, the eigenvalue may be same. We can reorder them in a non-decreasing sequence
\{\lambda_{n}^{2}\}_{n\geq 0}
(eigenvalue of
H
) repeated according to their multiplicities, and so that

\displaystyle Hh_{n}(x)=\lambda_{n}^{2}h_{n}(x),\quad\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

The eigenvalue satisfies the asymptotic formula:
\lambda_{n}\sim n^{\frac{1}{2d}}
when

We can define the Sobolev norm for every

via the spectral decomposition,

\displaystyle\|u\|^{2}_{\mathcal{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}^{2s}\left|c_{n}\right|^{2},

where

\displaystyle u\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\triangle}}{{=}}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\pi_{n}u=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}c_{n}h_{n}=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\langle u,h_{n}\rangle h_{n},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the classical inner product in
L^{2}
space.

We also have the Sobolev embedding in the frame of harmonic oscillator.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
s\in[0,1]
, and

. Then we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\lesssim\|f\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s,p}}.

Let us define the dyadic localization operators. Let

We notice that if

Definition 2.1

Proof.

We prove by induction. For

-linear spectral multiplier similar to (
2.6
) with the multiplier
\tilde{m}
. Suppose that the Banach space
Y
satisfies the modulation stability property and
\tilde{m}
obeys the following symbol-type estimate

\displaystyle\left|\partial_{\xi_{1}}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdots\partial_{\xi_{k}}^{\alpha_{k}}\tilde{m}(\xi_{1},\cdots,\xi_{k})\right|\lesssim\langle\xi_{1}\rangle^{-\alpha_{1}}\cdots\langle\xi_{k}\rangle^{-\alpha_{k}}.

Assume that
f_{k}
satisfies the space-time multilinear estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{1}(t,x)f_{2}(t,x)\cdots f_{k}(t,x)dxdt\right|\leqslant A\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{Y},

where
f_{j}
is spectrally-localized function at scales
N_{j}
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{t}\Lambda(f_{1},\cdots,f_{k})dt\right|\leqslant CA\prod_{j=1}^{k}\|f_{j}\|_{Y}.

Remark 2.2
.

For compact manifold, Hani
[
27
]
first established this spectral multiplier theorem. Our theorem is an analogue of Hani’s theorem in the harmonic oscillator setting. For the sake of completeness, we also give the detailed proof here.

Proof.

Since
f_{i}
is spectrally localized around
N_{i}
, i.e.
n_{i}\sim N_{i}
, we can find
\tilde{n}_{i}\in(0,2]
such that
n_{i}=N_{i}\tilde{n}_{i}
. Similarly, there exists a smooth function
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}([-4,4])
such that

\displaystyle\varphi(\tilde{n}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{n}_{k})=\tilde{m}(N_{1}\tilde{n}_{1},\cdots,N_{k}\tilde{n}_{k}).

By the assumption on the boundedness for the multiplier, the derivatives of
\varphi
is bounded. Using the Fourier expansion, we can write

\displaystyle\varphi(\tilde{n}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{n}_{k})=\sum_{\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/4}M(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k})e^{i(\theta_{1}\tilde{n}_{1}+\cdots+\theta_{k}\tilde{n}_{k})}.

Using the definition of
\Lambda
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Lambda(f_{1},\cdots,f_{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{n_{i}\sim N_{i}}\sum_{\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/4}M(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k})e^{i(\theta_{1}n_{1}N_{1}^{-1}+\cdots+\theta_{k}n_{k}N_{k}^{-1})}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\pi_{n_{1}}f_{1}(t)\cdots\pi_{n_{k}}f_{k}(t)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}/4}M(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\Big(\sum_{n_{j}\sim N_{j}}e^{i\theta_{j}n_{j}N_{j}^{-1}}\pi_{n_{j}}f_{j}(t)\Big)dx

\displaystyle=\sum_{\theta_{j}\sim\mathbb{Z}/4}M(\theta_{1},\cdots,\theta_{k})\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\prod_{j=1}^{k}\tilde{f}_{j}^{\theta_{j}}(t,x)dx,

where the modulated function
\tilde{f}_{j}^{\theta_{j}}:=\sum_{n_{j}\sim N_{j}}e^{i\theta_{j}n_{j}N_{j}^{-1}}\pi_{n_{j}}f_{j}(t)
. Using the multilinear estimate for
f_{j}
and modulated stability property, we can complete the proof.
∎

3.
Bilinear Strichartz estimate for harmonic oscillator

In this section, we will prove the sharp bilinear Strichartz estimate for harmonic oscillator in the sense that there is no loss of derivatives similarly to that in Euclidean space. Let
H:=-\Delta+|x|^{2}
, we consider time-space bilinear estimate for the following initial data problem

\begin{cases}(i\partial_{t}+H)u=0,\,\,(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\\
u(0)=u_{0}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).\end{cases}

(3.1)

In
[
7
]
, Burq, Poiret, and Thomann established the following bilinear Strichartz estimate with
\delta
-loss of derivatives

\displaystyle\big\|e^{itH}\Delta_{M}ue^{itH}\Delta_{N}v\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})}\leq C_{T}\min\{N,M\}^{\frac{d-2}{2}}\Big(\frac{\min(N,M)}{\max(N,M)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}-\delta}\|\Delta_{M}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|\Delta_{N}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

Let us briefly review the strategy of
[
7
]
. First, they use the following physical-space decomposition to
u_{M}:=\Delta_{M}u,

\displaystyle u_{M}=\chi\big(\tfrac{4|x|^{2}}{M^{2}}\big)u_{M}+(1-\chi)\big(\tfrac{4|x|^{2}}{M^{2}}\big)u_{M},

with
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}),\;0\leq\chi\leq 1,
satisfying

\chi(x)=\begin{cases}1\quad\text{if}\quad|x|\leq\frac{15}{32},\\
0\quad\text{if}\quad|x|\geq\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Substituting into the bilinear form of Schrödinger flow, it suffices to show the following two estimate

\displaystyle\big\|e^{itH}v_{N}e^{itH}\chi\big(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{M^{2}}\big)u_{M}\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}

(3.2)

and

\displaystyle\big\|e^{itH}v_{N}e^{itH}(1-\chi)\big(\tfrac{|x|^{2}}{M^{2}}\big)u_{M}\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}}.

(3.3)

Notice that in (
3.3
), they use the almost orthogonality of the support of two functions and obtain the fast decay of (
3.3
). For (
3.2
), they want to use the lens transform to transfer the estimate to
e^{it\Delta}
. This requires the use of bilinear Strichartz estimate for functions
f,g
which is not spectrally localized. To sum over all frequencies, the
\delta
-loss appears.

Our main result in this section is to remove the
\delta
-loss by the argument of
[
34
]
and some technique computations.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
1\leq M\leq N
be dyadic numbers and
u_{N}(0),v_{M}(0)\in L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be two frequency localized functions with

\Delta_{N}u_{N}(0)=u_{N}(0)\,\,,\Delta_{M}v_{M}(0)=v_{M}(0).

Then, for
T\in(0,\infty)
,
d\geq 2
, there exists constant
C=C(T)
depend on
T
only such that

\|e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq C\frac{M^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.4)

Remark 3.1
.

For
d=2
, the proof for this theorem can be found in
[
34
]
. For higher dimension, the proof is more complex and need more techniques. However, presenting such generality would muddy the exposition, we only focus on the proof for
d=3
, and other dimension can be obtained similarly.

Proof.

By paraproduct, we will show below that the estimate (
3.4
) with
d=3
is equivalent to

\|e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\leq CMN^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(3.5)

Writing

u_{N}:=e^{itH}u_{N}(0),\;v_{M}:=e^{itH}v_{M}(0),

and by the equivalence of Sobolev norm (
1.2
), we could obtain (
3.5
) provided that we show

\big\||x|u_{N}v_{M}\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{T}MN^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

(3.6)

and

\big\|\nabla(u_{N}v_{M})\big\|_{L_{t,x}^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{3})}\leq C_{T}MN^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(3.7)

We assert that the proof of (
3.7
) can be reduced to that of

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M\big|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)+\overline{v}_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

(3.8)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CN\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

Therefore, the proof of Theorem
3.1
mainly contains the following four steps:

\circ

Step 1: Proof of the assertion (
3.8
),

\circ

Step 2: Showing that (
3.8
) implies (
3.7
),

\circ

Step 3: Proof of the weighted bilinear estimate (
3.6
),

\circ

Step 4: Showing that (
3.5
) implies (
3.4
).

Step 1: Proof of the assertion (
3.8
):

We consider a
C^{1}
function
\rho:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\partial^{2}_{kl}\rho
are piece-wise continuous functions with
1\leq k,l\leq 3
. Then, we define a bilinear form
B_{\rho}
associated to the Hessian of
\rho
as follows

B_{\rho(z)}(f(z),g(z)):=\sum_{k,l=1}^{3}f_{k}(z)\partial^{2}_{kl}\rho(z)g_{l}(z),

where
z\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
and
f,g:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}^{3}
. We claim that there holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}B_{\widetilde{\rho}(x-y)}\left(\overline{v}_{M}(y)\nabla u_{N}(x)+u_{N}(x)\nabla\overline{v}_{M}(y),v_{M}(y)\nabla\overline{u}_{N}(x)+\overline{u}_{N}(x)\nabla v_{M}(y)\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

(3.9)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{\|\nabla\widetilde{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}}

\displaystyle\times\big(\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big).

Here
\widetilde{\rho}(z):=\rho_{M}(z_{1})
is determined by a convex function
\rho_{M}(z_{1}):\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
, where

\rho_{M}(z_{1})=\begin{cases}\frac{Mz_{1}^{2}}{2}+\frac{1}{2M},\,&|z_{1}|\leq\frac{1}{M},\\
\operatorname{sgn}(z_{1})z_{1},\,&|z_{1}|>\frac{1}{M}.\end{cases}

Denote by

. We can check that when
|z_{1}|>\frac{1}{M}
, the Hessian of

vanishes identically while when

, only

is non-zero and equals to
M
. Therefore, apply
\widetilde{\rho}(z)
here and (
3.9
) becomes

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x_{1}-y_{1}|<M^{-1}}M\big|\overline{v}_{M}(y)\partial_{x_{1}}u_{N}(x)+u_{N}(x)\partial_{x_{1}}\overline{v}_{M}(y)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\big(\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big).

Since the region
|x-y|<M^{-1}
is contained in
|x_{1}-y_{1}|<M^{-1}
, we can change the integration region in the above equality to
|x-y|<M^{-1}
. Next, we set
\widetilde{\rho}(z):=\rho_{M}(z_{j})
with
j=2,3
and
(x_{1},y_{1})
in the previous inequality can be replaced by
(x_{j},y_{j})
with
j=2,3
. Finally, notice that
u_{N}
and

are spectral localized and we have proved that
(
3.9
)
implies (
3.8
).

Now, we turn to prove (
3.9
). We consider the interaction energy as follows

I_{\rho}(t):=\int|u(x)|^{2}\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y,

(3.10)

where we have abbreviated
u_{N}(x),v_{M}(y)
to
u(x),v(y)
for short. Then, we are devoted to the second derivative of
I_{\rho}(t)
. Let

be a function which is at least
C^{1}
smooth and
w(t,x)
solves (
3.1
), by integrating by parts, we get

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int K(x,y)|w(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x=2\int\nabla_{x}K(x,y)\cdot\Im(\nabla\overline{w}(x)\cdot w(x))\mathrm{d}x,

(3.11)

and

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\int K(x,y)|w(x)|\mathrm{d}x

(3.12)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4\int B_{K(x,y)}(\nabla w(x),\nabla\widetilde{w}(x))\mathrm{d}x-\int\Delta_{x}K(x,y)\cdot\Delta_{x}(|w(x)|^{2})\mathrm{d}x-4\int x\cdot\nabla_{x}K(x,y)|w(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x.

Notice that

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\Big(|u(x)|^{2}\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}\Big)=\big(\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}\big)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|u(x)|^{2}+\big(|u(x)|^{2}\rho(x-y)\big)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|v(y)|^{2},

we can apply (
3.11
) with
K(x,y)=\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}
and

respectively (we have exchanged the position of variable
x
and
y
in the second term above), we get the first derivative of

:

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{\rho}(t)=2\int\nabla\rho(x-y)\cdot\Im(\nabla\overline{u}(x)u(x))|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y-2\int\nabla\rho(x-y)\cdot\Im(\nabla\overline{v}(y)v(y))|u(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

(3.13)

Thanks to conservation laws and
\|\nabla w\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|w\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, (
3.13
) implies that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{\rho}(t)\right|\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}(\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})

(3.14)

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}(\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}).

Now, we treat the second derivative of
I_{\rho}(t)
. Direct computation shows

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}\big(|u(x)|^{2}\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big(\rho(x-y)|v(y)|^{2}\big)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}|u(x)|^{2}+\big(|u(x)|^{2}\rho(x-y)\big)\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}|v(y)|^{2}+2\rho(x-y)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|u(x)|^{2}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|v(y)|^{2}.

Since the first and second term can be calculated by (
3.12
), we can treat the last term via integration by parts and (
3.11
):

\displaystyle\int\left(\int\rho(x-y)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|u(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\left(\int\nabla\rho(x-y)\cdot\Im(\nabla\overline{u}(x)u(x))\mathrm{d}x\right)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

(3.15)

\displaystyle=-4\int B_{\rho}(\Im(\nabla\overline{u}(x)u(x)),\Im(\nabla\overline{v}(y)v(y)))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Combining (
3.12
) and (
3.15
), we can write the second derivative of
I_{\rho}
as

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}I_{\rho}(t)=

\displaystyle 4\int\left(B_{\rho(x-y)}(\nabla u(x),\nabla\overline{u}(x))|v(y)|^{2}+B_{\rho(x-y)}(\nabla v(y),\nabla\overline{v}(y))|u(x)|^{2}\right)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

(3.16)

\displaystyle-\int\Delta\rho(x-y)\Big(\Delta(|u(x)|^{2})|v(y)|^{2}+\Delta(|v(y)|^{2})|u(x)|^{2}\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-8\int B_{\rho(x-y)}(\Im(\nabla\overline{u}(x)u(x)),\Im(\nabla\overline{v}(y)v(y)))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle-4\Re\int(x-y)\cdot\nabla\rho(x-y)|u(x)|^{2}|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle:=I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}+I_{4}.

Since
\Delta\rho(x-y)=-\nabla_{x}\cdot\nabla_{y}\rho(x-y)=-\nabla_{y}\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho(x-y)
, using integration by parts with variable
y
and
x
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int-\Delta\rho(x-y)\Delta_{x}(|u(x)|^{2})|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int\nabla_{y}\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho(x-y)\Delta_{x}(|u(x)|^{2})|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=-\int\nabla_{y}(|v(y)|^{2})\cdot\nabla_{x}\rho(x-y)\Delta_{x}(|u(x)|^{2})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=\int-\nabla_{x}\nabla_{y}\rho(x-y)\nabla_{y}(|v(y)|^{2})\cdot\nabla_{x}(|u(x)|^{2})\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=4\int B_{\rho(x-y)}(\Re(\overline{u}(x)\nabla u(x)),\Re(\overline{v}(y)\nabla v(y)))\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

Similarly, we can deal with the second term in
I_{2}
and arrive at

I_{2}=8\int B_{\rho(x-y)}\big(\Re(\overline{u}(x)\nabla u(x)),\Re(\overline{v}(y)\nabla v(y))\big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y.

(3.17)

For one direction derivatives, we easily get

\displaystyle 4|v|^{2}(y)|\partial u|^{2}(x)+4|u|^{2}(x)|\partial v|^{2}(y)+8\frac{(v\partial\bar{v}+\bar{v}\partial v)(y)}{2}\frac{(u\partial\bar{u}+\bar{u}\partial u)(x)}{2}

\displaystyle-8\frac{(v\partial\bar{v}-\bar{v}\partial v)(y)}{2}\frac{(\bar{u}\partial u-u\partial\bar{u})(x)}{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4|v|^{2}(y)|\partial u|^{2}(x)+4|u|^{2}(x)|\partial v|^{2}(y)+4v\partial\bar{v}(y)u\partial\bar{u}(x)+4\bar{v}\partial{v}(y)\bar{u}\partial u(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 4|\bar{v}(y)\partial u(x)+u(x)\partial\bar{v}(y)|^{2}.

Therefore, we can absorb
I_{1}+I_{2}+I_{3}
into a bilinear form and rewrite that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\mathrm{d}t^{2}}I_{\rho}(t)=4\int B_{\rho}(x-y)\Big(\overline{v}(y)\nabla u(x)+u(x)\nabla\overline{v}(y),v(y)\nabla\overline{u}(x)+\overline{u}(x)\nabla v(y)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y+I_{4}.

Integrating in time applying (
3.14
), we obtain

\displaystyle 4\Big|\int_{0}^{T}\int B_{\rho}(x-y)\Big(\overline{v}(y)\nabla u(x)+u(x)\nabla\overline{v}(y),v(y)\nabla\overline{u}(x)+\overline{u}(x)\nabla v(y)\Big)\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t\Big|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{\rho}(0)\right|+\left|\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}I_{\rho}(T)\right|+\left|\int_{0}^{T}I_{4}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\times(\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})})

\displaystyle+\left|\int_{0}^{T}I_{4}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right|.

Noting that
\|yw\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\lesssim\|w\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}
, hence
\int_{0}^{T}I_{4}(t)\mathrm{d}t
enjoys the bound

\displaystyle\left|\int_{0}^{T}I_{4}(t)\mathrm{d}t\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\int|x-y||\nabla\rho(x-y)||u(x)|^{2}|v(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\times\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\Big(\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|yv(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v(t)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|xu(t)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\Big)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big(\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|u(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\big).

Therefore, we have proved assertion (
3.9
).
∎

Following the similar argument to the proof of (

), we can obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M\big|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}(H\overline{v}_{M})(y)+(H\overline{v}_{M})(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)\big|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle CNM^{2}\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

which will be used in the proof of Step 2.

Before the proof of Step 2, we first introduce a useful lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}>0
such that for any smooth function
\phi:\mathbb{R}^{3}\to\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}
, the following holds

|\phi(x)|^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<\lambda^{-1}}|H\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{3}\int_{|x-y|<\lambda^{-1}}|\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}^{3}.

(3.19)

Proof.

Without of generality, we assume that
\phi
is a real-valued function. From
[
32
]
, we have

|\phi(x)|^{2}\lesssim\lambda^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|-\Delta\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{3}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

Hence, it is sufficient to prove that

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|-\Delta\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

(3.20)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\lambda^{-1}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|H\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{3}\int_{|x-y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

Since
-\Delta=H-|y|^{2}
, by integration by parts, for any
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
we get an equality

\int(|\Delta f|^{2}+|y|^{4}|f|^{2}+2|y|^{2}|\nabla f|^{2})\mathrm{d}y=\int(|Hf|^{2}+4|f|^{2})\mathrm{d}y.

(3.21)

Let
\chi_{\lambda}=\chi(\lambda(x-y))
where
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and

\chi(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&|\xi|<\frac{1}{4},\\
0,&|\xi|>\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

Then, substituting
f(y)=\chi_{\lambda}(y)\phi(y)
into (
3.21
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\int(|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\Delta\phi|^{2}+|y|^{4}|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\phi|^{2}+2|y|^{2}|\nabla\chi_{\lambda}\phi|^{2})\mathrm{d}y

(3.22)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int(|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|H\phi|^{2}+4|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\phi|^{2})\mathrm{d}y-2\int\chi_{\lambda}|y|^{2}\phi(2\nabla\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\phi+\Delta\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\phi)\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int(|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|H\phi|^{2}+4|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\phi|^{2})\mathrm{d}y+2\left|\int\chi_{\lambda}|y|^{2}\phi(2\nabla\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\phi+\Delta\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\phi)\mathrm{d}y\right|.

Using Young’s inequality, it gives that fo any
\mu>0

\displaystyle\chi_{\lambda}|y|^{2}\phi(2\nabla\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\phi+\Delta\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\phi)\lesssim\mu|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|y|^{4}|\phi|^{2}+\mu^{-1}(2\nabla\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\phi+\Delta\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\phi)^{2}.

We can choose
\mu
properly, so that the term
|y|^{4}|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\phi|^{2}
in the l.h.s of (
3.22
) can be absorbed. Thus, we can control the term
|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\Delta\phi|^{2}
in (
3.22
) as follows

\int|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\Delta\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|H\phi|^{2}+4|\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\chi|^{2}+|\chi_{\lambda}\cdot\nabla\phi|^{2}+|\Delta\chi_{\lambda}|^{2}|\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

(3.23)

Basic calculation shows
\big||\nabla|^{k}\chi_{\lambda}\big|\lesssim\lambda^{k}
for
k=1,2
, it implies that

\int_{|y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\Delta\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\int_{|y|<(2\lambda)^{-1}}|H\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{2}\int\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+(1+\lambda^{4})\int_{|y|<(2\lambda)^{-1}}|\phi|\mathrm{d}y,

(3.24)

where
\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}(y)=\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda(y-x))
with
\widetilde{\chi}
is smooth and radial which satisfies

\widetilde{\chi}(\xi)=\begin{cases}1,&\xi\in\mathrm{supp}\chi,\\
0,&|\xi|>1.\end{cases}

Hence, we deduce (
3.20
) to prove that

\int\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\int_{|y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\mathcal{H}\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+\lambda^{2}\int_{|y|<(4\lambda)^{-1}}|\phi(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

Integrating by parts and using the fact that
H=-\Delta+|y|^{2}
, we can find

\displaystyle-2\int(\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\phi)\Delta\phi\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y-\int|\phi|^{2}\Delta\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int(\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\phi)H\phi\mathrm{d}y-2\int|y|^{2}\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

By Young’s inequality, for any
\lambda>0
we have

\left|(\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\phi)H\phi\right|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}|H\phi|^{2}+\lambda^{2}|\phi|^{2},

this implies

\displaystyle 2\int\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\nabla\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+2\int|y|^{2}\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}|\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle=2\int(\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\phi)H\phi\mathrm{d}y+\int|\phi|^{2}\Delta\widetilde{\chi}_{\lambda}\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\lesssim\lambda^{-2}\int_{|x-y|<\lambda^{-1}}|H\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y+(1+\lambda^{2})\int_{|x-y|<\lambda^{-1}}|\phi|^{2}\mathrm{d}y.

So far, we have proved (
3.20
) and this lemma follows immediately.

Step 2: Showing that
\eqref{fml-thm-asser}\Rightarrow\eqref{fml-thm-L2H1}
:

Recall that
u_{N}=e^{itH}u_{N}(0),v_{M}=e^{itH}v_{M}(0)
and
N\geq M
, it is sufficient to control the term
|v_{M}\nabla u_{N}|^{2}
in (
3.7
). Apply Lemma
3.1
with
\lambda=M
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int|v_{M}\nabla u_{N}|^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{-1}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}|Hv_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

(3.25)

\displaystyle+M^{3}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}|v_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t.

Using triangle inequality and (
3.8
), we arrive at

\displaystyle M^{3}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}|v_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{3}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}|v_{M}(y)\nabla_{x}u_{N}(x)+u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+M^{3}\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{2}N\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M^{3}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t.

We can treat the first term in l.h.s of (
3.25
) by (
3.18
) and get that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int M^{3}|v_{M}\nabla u_{N}|^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{2}N\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M^{3}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M^{3}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}(H\overline{v}_{M})(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t.

On one hand, by change of variable, the Cauchy-Schwartz inequality and Strichartz estimate, there holds

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M^{3}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t

(3.26)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|z|<M^{-1}}M^{3}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}\overline{v}_{M}(x-z)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}z\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{|z|<M^{-1}}M^{3}\|u_{N}\|^{2}_{L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{t}([0,T],L^{\frac{10}{3}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\|\nabla v_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{5}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\mathrm{d}z

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle M^{3}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

Here in the last inequality of (
3.26
), we have used the fact that

\|\nabla v_{M}\|^{2}_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{5}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\lesssim M^{3}\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(3.27)

Indeed, by Sobolev embedding, we get

\|\nabla v_{M}\|_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{5}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\lesssim\||\nabla|^{\frac{3}{2}}v_{M}\|_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{\frac{30}{11}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}.

Since
-\Delta v_{M}
solves

(i\partial_{t}+H)(-\Delta v_{M})=[i\partial_{t}+H,-\Delta]v_{M}=6v_{M}+2x\cdot\nabla v_{M},

and by Sobolev embedding and Strichartz estimates, we have

\displaystyle\|\Delta v_{M}\|_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{\frac{30}{11}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\Delta v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}+\|v_{M}\|_{L^{1}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}+\|x\cdot\nabla v_{M}\|_{L^{1}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}

\displaystyle\lesssim\|v_{M}(0)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}

\displaystyle\lesssim M^{2}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}.

(3.28)

Interpolating between above and

\|v_{M}\|_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{\frac{30}{11}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\lesssim\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

we conclude that

\||\nabla|^{\frac{3}{2}}v_{M}\|_{L^{5}_{t}([0,T],L^{\frac{30}{11}}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))}\lesssim M^{\frac{3}{2}}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})},

and (
3.27
) follows immediately.

On the other hand, we apply (
3.18
) and similar argument above to obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int_{|x-y|<M^{-1}}M^{-1}|u_{N}(x)\nabla_{y}(H\overline{v}_{M})(y)|^{2}\mathrm{d}x\mathrm{d}y\right)\mathrm{d}t\lesssim M^{3}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}.

(3.29)

Consequently, (
3.26
) and (
3.29
) gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int|v_{M}\nabla u_{N}|^{2}(x)\mathrm{d}x\right)\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim(NM+M^{2})\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim MN\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}^{2},

and we have finished the proof of Step 2.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
N,M,v_{M}(0),u_{N}(0)
be the same as in Theorem
3.1
. Let multi-index
\alpha,\beta
with
\mathrm{Ord}(\alpha)=k_{1},\mathrm{Ord}(\beta)=k_{2}
and
P\in\{\nabla,x\}
.
Then, for
T\in(0,\infty)
, there exists constant
C>0
such that for
d\geq 2

\displaystyle\|P(\alpha)e^{itH}u_{N}(0)\cdot P(\beta)e^{itH}v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

(3.35)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C{N^{k_{1}}M^{k_{2}}}\frac{M^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{N^{\frac{1}{2}}}\|u_{N}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{M}(0)\|_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and

are as in Definition
2.1
.

Let

and note that

always commutes with
P
, we get that

\displaystyle(i\partial_{t}+H)(P(\alpha)u_{N})

\displaystyle=[i\partial_{t}+H,P(\alpha)]u_{N}+P(\alpha)(i\partial_{t}+H)u_{N}

\displaystyle=[H,P(\alpha)]u_{N},

and by Duhamel’s formula, we also get

\displaystyle P(\alpha)u_{N}

\displaystyle=e^{itH}P(\alpha)u_{N}(0)-i\int_{0}^{T}e^{i(t-s)H}\big[H,P(\alpha)\big]e^{isH}u_{N}(0)\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=e^{itH}P(\alpha)u_{N}(0)-ie^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}\big[H,P(\alpha)\big]e^{isH}u_{N}(0)\mathrm{d}s.

Similar argument with that of
v_{M}
, we arrive at

{P(\beta)v_{M}=e^{itH}P(\beta)v_{M}(0)-ie^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}\big[H,P(\beta)\big]e^{isH}v_{M}(0)\mathrm{d}s.}

By Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.5
, one can verify that

\|e^{itH}(P(\alpha)u_{N}(0))\cdot e^{itH}(P(\beta)v_{M}(0))\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}\leq CM^{k_{1}}N^{k_{2}}\frac{M^{d-1}}{N}\|u_{N}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\|v_{M}(0)\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}.

(3.36)

Hence, we need to deal with
L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))
norm of the form

\displaystyle e^{itH}P(\alpha)u_{N}(0)\times e^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}\big[H,P(\beta)\big]e^{isH}v_{M}(0)\mathrm{d}s,

(3.37)

\displaystyle e^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}[H,P(\alpha)]e^{isH}u_{N}(0)\mathrm{d}s\times e^{itH}P(\beta)v_{M}(0),

(3.38)

\displaystyle e^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}\big[H,P(\alpha)\big]e^{isH}u_{N}(0)\mathrm{d}s\times e^{itH}\int_{0}^{T}e^{-isH}\big[H,P(\beta)\big]e^{isH}v_{M}(0)\mathrm{d}s.

(3.39)

Using Theorem
3.1
and Lemma
2.5
again, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\eqref{equ:eituNest1}+\eqref{equ:eituNest2}+\eqref{equ:eituNest3}\|_{L^{2}_{t}([0,T],L^{2}_{x}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))}

\displaystyle\leq

(4.11)

We first prove the case when

we arrive at

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-2dL_{0}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})-2\big(L_{0}(e_{1},x\cdot\nabla e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})+L_{0}(e_{1},e_{2},x\cdot\nabla e_{3},e_{4})+L_{0}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},x\cdot\nabla e_{4})\big)

\displaystyle-2L_{1}^{|x|^{2}}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}).

\displaystyle L_{0}^{|x|^{4}}(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4})=

can be absorbed into

) for

and

and

. Indeed, we rewrite

5.
Modified energy and polynomial growth of higher-order Sobolev norm

In this section, we will first show the decay of modified energy and then obtain the polynomial growth of higher-order Sobolev norm.

Since
Iu
is not the solution to (

5.1.
Decay of the modified energy

We next prove the almost conservation of the modified energy:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

when

and
s_{0}=1
when

Proof.

First, we take the time derivative to the modified energy

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}E(Iu(t))

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{Iu_{t}}(HIu+|Iu|^{2}Iu)dx

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{Iu_{t}}\big(-i\partial_{t}(Iu)+HIu+|Iu|^{2}Iu\big)dx

\displaystyle=\Re\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{Iu_{t}}\big(-I(|u|^{2}u)+|Iu|^{2}Iu\big)dx.

Decomposing the function
u(t,x)=\sum\limits_{\mu}{\pi}_{\mu}u(t,x)
, where
{\pi}_{\mu}
is the projection operator on eigen-space associated to
H
. By the Newton-Leibniz formula, we have

\displaystyle E(Iu(t))-E(Iu(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re\sum_{\mu_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m(\mu_{1})\overline{{\pi_{\mu_{1}}}u_{t}}\big(m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})-I\big){\pi}_{\mu_{2}}u\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{3}}u}{\pi}_{\mu_{4}}udxdt,

where

is the rearranged eigenvalue associated to

. Using the fact that

\displaystyle E(Iu(t))-E(Iu(0))

\displaystyle=

Then substituting the equation of

\displaystyle E(Iu(t))-E(Iu(0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Re i\sum_{\mu_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})}\Big)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{1}}(HIu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{2}}(Iu)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{3}}(Iu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{4}}(Iu)dxdt

\displaystyle+\Re i\sum_{\mu_{i}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(1-\frac{m(\mu_{1})}{m(\mu_{2})m(\mu_{3})m(\mu_{4})}\Big)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{1}}(I(|u|^{2}u))}{\pi}_{\mu_{2}}(Iu)\overline{{\pi}_{\mu_{3}}(Iu)}{\pi}_{\mu_{4}}(Iu)dxdt

\displaystyle\Re i(I_{1}+I_{2}).

. For the term

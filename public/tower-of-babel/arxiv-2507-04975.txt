Reduced SIGMA Basis Sets: a new family of SIGMA basis sets for molecular calculations
Ignacio Ema, Jesús San Fabián, Guillermo Ramírez, Rafael López, José Manuel García de la Vega

Abstract
A new family of Gaussian-type SIGMA basis sets, termed reduced SIGMA basis sets, is introduced and preliminarily tested. Sharing the same composition as Dunning basis sets, they enhance performance by reducing linear dependencies in large systems, thereby improving convergence and lowering computational costs for such systems..

1
Introduction

New basis sets (BS) consisting of Gaussian-type orbitals (GTO), derived
from the previously reported SIGMA BS (
\sigma
BS)
[
1
,
2
]
, is presented.
The new BS are named reduced SIGMA (
\sigma
BS0) and have the same composition per shell as
Dunning correlation-consistent BS
[
3
,
4
]
, ranging from DZ to QZ,
both standard and augmented (
a\sigma
BS0). The composition of the (a)
\sigma
BS0 is summarized in Table
1
.

In particular, the new primitives added in the augmented version (
a\sigma
BS0) have
exponents higher than those present in Dunning BS of the same composition, without
compromising the description of properties related to the diffuse part of the
electronic cloud. Furthermore, the diffuse primitives of the
s
and
p
shells are
not directly added to the BS but are incorporated through general contractions.
Together, these features make the
a\sigma
BS0 less susceptible to linear quasi-
dependencies than their Dunning counterparts, resulting in improved convergence in
terms of both accuracy and computational efficiency for large molecular systems.
Standard basis sets show equivalent performance between
\sigma
BS0 and Dunning sets and
are included to provide a foundation for the augmented versions and for completeness.

Unlike the
\sigma
BS, the requirement that all primitives with the same exponent and

l<L
must also be present in the set is removed in the
\sigma
BS0, and the number of
primitives is reduced compared to the former. This slightly diminishes the quality of
the results relative to the
\sigma
BS (which are designed for high-precision
calculations), but achieves a good compromise between accuracy and computational
cost. The
\sigma
BS0 are particularly competitive when applied to large systems with
augmented basis sets.

This work is organized as follows. In Section
2
, the procedure for
developing SIGMA BS is outlined, and the contraction scheme and composition are
described. In Section
3
, some preliminary testing on molecular calculations
is reported.

2
Development of reduced SIGMA basis sets

Normalized spherical Gaussian primitive functions (pGTO),
g_{lm}
, are defined
as:

g_{lm}(\xi,{\bf r})={\mathcal{N}}_{l,\xi}^{r}\;{\mathcal{N}}_{lm}^{\Omega}\;e^{-\xi\,r^{2}}\;z_{l}^{m}({\bf r})

(1)

where
z_{l}^{m}({\bf r})
are unnormalized regular solid harmonics:

z_{l}({\bf r})=r^{l}\;(-1)\;P_{l}^{|m|}(\cos\theta)\;\left\{\begin{array}[]{lr}\cos m\phi&\hskip 14.22636pt(m\geq 0)\\
\sin|m|\phi&\hskip 14.22636pt(m<0)\end{array}\right.

(2)

P_{l}^{m}
being the associate Legendre functions (see
[
5
]
Eq 8.751.1), and
{\mathcal{N}}_{\xi_{i}}
and
{\mathcal{N}}_{LM}^{\Omega}
, the
radial and angular normalization constants given by:

{\mathcal{N}}_{l,\xi}^{r}=2^{l+1}\;\sqrt{\frac{\xi^{l}}{(2l+1)!!}}\;\;\left[\frac{(2\,\xi)^{3}}{\pi}\right]^{1/4}

(3)

{\mathcal{N}}_{lm}^{\Omega}=\left[\frac{(2l+1)\;(l-|m|)!}{2\,(1+\delta_{m0})\;\pi\;(l+|m|)!}\right]^{1/2}

(4)

Usual GTO BS consist of contractions
of Gaussian primitives. They are known as contracted Gaussian functions (CGTO),

G_{lm}
,
defined as:

G_{lm}({\bf r})=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{G}}c_{i}\;g_{lm,i}({\bf r})

(5)

where
i
labels the primitives in the set, and the coefficients
c_{i}

are determined by different procedures
subject to the
normalization of the GTO. The number of coefficients
and their values depend on the recipe used in the construction of a particular basis set.

To simplify the notation, (a)XZ will be used below as an abbreviation for Dunning
(aug-)cc-pVXZ families. The equivalent
\sigma
BS0 will be denoted as (a)
\sigma
XZ0.
In each of the SIGMA families, basis sets ranging from DZ to QZ
have been considered.

To test the performance of the (a)
\sigma
XZ0, calculations have been carried out on several
systems, comparing the results with those obtained using the equivalent Dunning basis sets.

In atomic calculations, the energies obtained with the (a)
\sigma
XZ0 are lower than those of
the Dunning (a)XZ in all cases, at both the HF and CISD levels.
In Fig.
1

\Delta E=E_{\text{Dunning}}-E_{\text{SIGMA}}

are plotted for the standard (Fig.

As can be seen, the differences across a period increase with the atomic number and
become smaller as the size of the BS increases.
The smallest differences are observed for the alkali metals, while the largest appear
for the noble gases. As expected, these differences decrease as the quality of the basis
set increases.
The differences are always positive, confirming that, from a variational point of view,
(a)
\sigma
XZ0 outperform the (a)XZ. This pattern is observed for both the standard and
augmented versions.

\sigma

Conclusions

A new family of reduced SIGMA basis sets, ranging in size from DZ to QZ, has been reported in both standard and augmented versions, covering atoms from H to Ar. The main difference from the previous
\sigma
BS lies in the reduced number of primitive functions and the removal of the constraint that requires exponents of primitive functions appearing in a shell with angular momentum
L
to also be present in all shells with
l<L
.
The (a)
\sigma
XZ0 sets follow the previously reported matryoshka-like construction scheme. In the augmented versions, diffuse primitives with higher exponents than those in the corresponding XZ sets are added. In the
s
and
p
shells, these diffuse functions are incorporated into the contractions, while in the remaining shells they are included without contraction. Furthermore, unlike in the aXZ sets, the exponents of all primitive functions in the a
\sigma
XZ0 sets are reoptimized.
The standard versions of XZ and
\sigma
XZ0 deliver comparable performance in molecular calculations. However, the augmented a
\sigma
XZ0 sets outperform their aXZ counterparts in terms of energy and geometry optimization, as well as computational cost.

reduced-Sigma-BS.txt which contains the (a)
\sigma
BS0 for the selected atoms in MOLPRO’s format.

c c -structures and trace methods beyond connective rings
Ishan Levy, Vladimir Sosnilo

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of a c c -category, which is a kind of category whose behaviour is controlled by connective ring spectra. More precisely, any c c -category admits a finite step resolution by categories of compact modules over connective ring spectra. We introduce nilpotent extensions of c c -categories, and show that they induce isomorphisms on truncating invariants, such as the fiber of the cyclotomic trace map. We show that for many stacks, the category of perfect complexes is naturally a c c -category and deduce a generalization of the Dundas–Goodwillie–McCarthy theorem to such stacks.

1.
Weighted categories

Here we recall some preliminary results about weight structures that we use throughout the paper.

Definition 1.1
.

A
weight structure
on a stable category
\mathcal{C}
is the data of two idempotent closed subcategories
(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0})
of connective and, respectively, coconnective objects such that:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
is closed under suspension, and
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
is closed under desuspensions.

(2)

\mathop{\rm map}\nolimits(x,y)
is connective if
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},y\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
.

(3)

for any object
x\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a cofiber sequence

x_{\leq 0}\rightarrow x\rightarrow x_{\geq 1},

where
x_{\leq 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
and
\Sigma^{-1}x_{\geq 1}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}.
We call these
weight truncations
of
x
.

We warn the reader that despite the notation, the weight truncations of
x
are not uniquely determined by
x
.
By a
weighted category
we mean a stable category endowed with a weight structure.
A functor between weighted stable categories

F:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}

is said to have
weight amplitude
[a,b]
if it sends connective objects to
a
-connective objects and coconnective objects to
b
-coconnective objects. It is said to be of
bounded weight amplitude
if it is weight amplitude
[a,b]
for some
a,b\in\mathbf{Z}
. It is said to be
weight exact
if it is weight amplitude
[0,0]
.
The subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{w\heartsuit}\subset\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
is called the
heart
of the weight structure.
We write
\mathcal{C}_{w\geq n}:=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{w\leq k}:=\Sigma^{k}\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}.

We say that the weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
restricts to a subcategory
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
if

(\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},\mathcal{D}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0})
defines a weight structure on the subcategory.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable category endowed with a weight structure.
Every object
x\in\mathcal{C}
admits shifted weight decompositions

w_{\leq n}x\to X\to w_{\geq n+1}x
. We freely use the elementary fact that that these can be made compatible for any map
x\to y
(though they are not functorial).

Remark 1.2
.

The notion of a weight structure is self dual, in that a weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
induces one on
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
by
swapping the roles of
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
. Additionally, the subcategories
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}

determine one another via the orthogonality axiom (2) of
1.1
.

\triangleleft

Definition 1.3
.

We say that the weight structure on
\mathcal{C}
is

if

\mathcal{C}=\bigcup\limits_{n}\left(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq-n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}\right)
. We call a pair of a small stable category

equipped with a bounded weight structure a
bounded weighted category
.
Given any weighted category
\mathcal{C}
, we define
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{b}}=\bigcup\limits_{n}\left(\mathcal{C}_{w\geq-n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq n}\right)

that is the maximal bounded weighted subcategory.
We denote the category of bounded weighted categories and weight exact functors by
\mathop{\rm Cat}\nolimits^{\mathrm{bw}}
. We use

\mathop{\rm Cat}\nolimits^{\mathrm{bw}}_{\mathrm{b}}
to denote the cateory of bounded weighted categories and bounded weight amplitude functors.

Remark 1.4

Any generating subcategory

The weight structures that are relevant to us will be generated by a collection of compact objects. To make sense of what
it means for a weight structure to be generated, we have the following lemma, which explains the operations under which

Lemma 1.8

Suppose that

is closed under infinite direct sums,
finite limits, extensions, retracts, and countable filtered colimits of maps whose cofiber is in

Most of these are immediate from the characterization of

Proposition 1.17

to show that

2.
Finite cohomological dimension heart categories

2.1.
Basics on heart categories

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable category. A
heart structure
on
\mathcal{C}
consists of a pair of full subcategories

(\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0})
satisfying the following axioms:

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
are closed under extensions,

\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
under suspensions and
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
under desuspensions;

(2)

For any
x\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a fiber sequence called a decomposition

x_{\leq 0}\to x\to x_{\geq 1}

such that
x_{\leq 0}\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
and
x_{\geq 1}\in\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.

We will employ the following notation:
\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}=\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
.
We say that the heart structure is
bounded
if
\mathcal{C}=\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq-n}
.
The heart of
\mathcal{C}
is the subcategory spanned by
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0}
, we denote it by
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
.

We call a stable category endowed with a heart structure a
heart category
. We say that a functor between
heart categories
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
is
heart exact
if it sends
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
to
\mathcal{D}_{\leq 0}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
to

\mathcal{D}_{\geq 0}
. The category of small heart categories is denoted by

\mathop{\rm Cat}\nolimits^{\heartsuit}
. The full subcategory on bounded heart-categories is denoted by

\mathop{\rm Cat}\nolimits^{\heartsuit,b}
.

Example 2.2
.

An exact category
\mathcal{E}
in the sense of
[
Bar15
]
gives rise to the stable category

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})
, called its stable hull (see
[
Kle22
]
). There
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})_{\geq 0}

is set to be the extension-closure of
\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})_{\geq 0}
is

\bigcup\limits_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\Omega^{n}\mathcal{E}
.
If
\mathcal{E}
is a classical exact category,
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})
coincides with the derived category in the classical sense.
It turns out that, conversely, any bounded heart category arises from an exact category in this way. In fact, we have
an equivalence

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})\simeq\mathcal{C}

by
[
SW25
, Theorem 1.6]
.

\triangleleft

Example 2.3
.

As a consequence of the previous example, we see that the category of perfect complexes over a quasi-projective scheme
X

is naturally endowed with a heart structure, since we have

\mathrm{Perf}(X)\simeq\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathrm{Vect}(X)).

\triangleleft

Example 2.4

Let
\mathcal{C}

Define

The above construction works similarly if

is a heart category and

\triangleleft

This subsection is to include several technical tools we need to work with heart structures.

Definition 2.10
.

Let
\mathcal{D}
be an exact category.
A full extension closed subcategory
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
is called
left special
if for every exact epimorphism

u\twoheadrightarrow y
in
\mathcal{D}
with
y\in\mathcal{E}
there exists a map
x\to u
with
x\in\mathcal{E}
such that the composite

x\to u\to y
is an epimorphism in
\mathcal{E}
.

Example 2.11
.

For any exact category
\mathcal{E}
, the embedding
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})_{\geq 0}
is left special
(see
[
SW25
, Lemma 3.12]
).

\triangleleft

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be an extension closed subcategory of a prestable category
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.
And assume that
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is generated by
\mathcal{E}
under finite colimits.
The following conditions are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{E}
is left special in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
;

(2)

for any
x\in\mathcal{E}
and
y\in\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
any morphism
x\to y
decomposes as

x\to\Sigma u\to y

for some
u\in\mathcal{E}
;

(3)

for any
x,y\in\mathcal{E}
and
n>0
any morphism
x\to\Sigma^{n}y
decomposes as a composite

x\to\Sigma u\to\Sigma^{n}y

for some
u\in\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

First we prove (2) assuming (1).
Consider
x\in\mathcal{E}
,
y\in\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
and a morphism
\phi:x\to y
.
The fiber of
\phi
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
and the fiber of the map
\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\phi)\to x

is also in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
. Hence,
\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\phi)\to x
is an exact epimorphism in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.
Left specialty implies that there exists
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{E}
and a map
x^{\prime}\to\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\phi)
such that
the composite

p:x^{\prime}\to\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\phi)\to x

is an epimorphism. Now,
\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(p)\in\mathcal{E}
and we see that the map
x\to y
factors through

\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(p)=\Sigma\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(p)
.

The condition (3) is a special case of (2), so it suffices to prove that (3) implies (1).
Consider
x\in\mathcal{E}
and an exact epimorphism
\phi:w\to x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
. Then
\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(\phi)\in\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.
To show left specialty it suffices to construct an exact epimorphism

x^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow x\in\mathcal{E}
such that the composite
x^{\prime}\to x\to\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(\phi)
is zero.
If
\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(\phi)\in\bigcup\limits_{n>0}\Sigma^{n}\mathcal{E}
, then by assumption we may choose a factorization

x\to\Sigma u\to\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(\phi)

and take
x^{\prime}
to be the fiber of the map
x\to\Sigma u
. Now
consider the subcategory
\mathcal{D}\subset\Sigma\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
consisting of objects
y
such that for any
v\in\mathcal{E}

and any map
v\to y
there exists an exact epimorphism
v^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow v\in\mathcal{E}
such that the composite

v^{\prime}\to v\to y
is zero.
To finish the proof it suffices to show that
\mathcal{D}
is extension closed.

Consider a map
\psi:v\to y
such that
y
fits into an exact sequence

y^{\prime}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g}}{{\to}}y\stackrel{{\scriptstyle f}}{{\to}}y^{\prime\prime}

with
y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{D}
. By assumption there exists an exact epimorphism
p:v^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow v
in

\mathcal{E}
such that
f\circ\psi\circ p
is
zero. In particular, the map
v^{\prime}\to y
factors through
y^{\prime}
. Using the assumption on
y^{\prime}
, there exists an exact
epimorphism
q:v^{\prime\prime}\twoheadrightarrow v^{\prime}
such that the composite

v^{\prime\prime}\to v^{\prime}\to y^{\prime}\to y

is zero. Thus the composite
v^{\prime\prime}\twoheadrightarrow v^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow v
is zero after composed with
\psi
and
so
y
belongs to
\mathcal{D}.

∎

Theorem 2.13

(Keller’s criterion
[
SW25
, Theorem 4.5]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{E}\subset\mathcal{D}
be a left special subcategory of an exact category
\mathcal{D}
.
Then the induced functor

\tau:\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{E})\to\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{D})

is fully faithful.

Corollary 2.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a bounded heart category. Then the embedding
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}\to\mathcal{C}
is left special and the natural functor

\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})\to\mathcal{C}

is an equivalence.

Proof.

The embedding
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}\to\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is left special, since for any exact epimorphism

x^{\prime}\twoheadrightarrow x

with
x\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit},x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
, we may consider the composite
x^{\prime}_{\leq 0}\to x^{\prime}\to x
.
On the other hand, we have
\mathcal{D}^{\mathrm{b}}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0})\simeq\mathrm{SW}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0})\simeq\mathcal{C}
since
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}

generates
\mathcal{C}
, so the result follows from
2.13
.
∎

Lemma 2.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a bounded heart category. Then every object
c
of the weight-heart in
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})
can be written as a retract
of an object of the form
c_{\infty}=\mathop{\rm colim}\nolimits_{i}c_{i}
where
c_{0}=0
, and
c_{i}/c_{i-1}
is a possibly infinite sum of objects in

\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
.

Proof.

Let
c
be in the weight heart. Let
c_{1}
be the direct sum of all homotopy classes of objects in
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}

equipped with a map to
c
. Given
c_{i}
, define
c_{i+1}
to be the cofiber of the direct sum of all homotopy classes
of objects in
\Sigma^{-1}\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
equipped with a map to
c_{i}
. We claim that
c_{\infty}
is in the weight
heart. To see this, we need to show that any map
x\to c_{\infty}
with
x\in\Sigma^{-i}\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
for
i>0
is
null. Since
c_{\infty}
is a filtered colimit of objects in
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
,
it follows from Proposition
2.12
(with
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
) and
2.14
that we can reduce to the case

i=1
. But now the map
x\to c_{\infty}
factors through
c_{n}
for some
n
, and is thus null, because it is zero after
composition with
c_{n}\to c_{n+1}
by construction.

The map
c_{1}\to c
extends to
c_{\infty}
since
c
is in the weight heart, so the obstructions vanish. We claim that the
cofiber is
\geq 1
in the weight structure. This claim finishes the proof, since it shows that the map
c_{\infty}\to c

splits, so that
c
is a summand of
c_{\infty}
. To see the claim, the cofiber
d
is clearly
\geq 0
in the weight
structure, so we just need to show that there are no nonzero homotopy classes of maps
x\to d
for
x\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
.
If
f:x\to d
is a map, it is null upon composing with the map
d\to\Sigma c_{\infty}
, so the map lifts to the fiber,

c
. But then by construction of
c_{1}
, the map lifts to
c_{1}
, so the original map is null, since it factors through the
maps
c_{1}\to c_{\infty}\to c\to d
, and the composite
c_{\infty}\to c\to d
is null.
∎

Corollary 2.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a bounded heart category.
Then the inclusion
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 0}\subset\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{t\geq 0}
holds.

Proof.

By Proposition
1.17
any object of
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 0}

Since
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{t\geq 0}=\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0})
is closed under colimits, it suffices to observe that objects of the

2.15
.
∎

\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}

is essentially surjective up to retracts. So we have (2)
\Rightarrow
(1).

To prove the converse, assume that
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{w\heartsuit}
is surjective up to retracts.
It suffices to show that
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}
is surjective up to retracts.
By assumption any object
x\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}
is a retract of

f(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}y_{\alpha})\simeq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}f(y_{\alpha})
with

y_{\alpha}\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}
. This means that the identity morphism
\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{x}
decomposes as

x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i}}{{\to}}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}f(y_{\alpha})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}x

But since
x
is
\kappa
-compact,
i
factors through a direct sum
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I^{\prime}}f(y_{\alpha})
with

I^{\prime}\subset I
of size less than
\kappa
. So we also have a decomposition

x\stackrel{{\scriptstyle i^{\prime}}}{{\to}}\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I^{\prime}}f(y_{\alpha})\hookrightarrow\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I}f(y_{\alpha})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle p}}{{\to}}x

and
x
is a retract of
\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I^{\prime}}f(y_{\alpha})\simeq f(\bigoplus_{\alpha\in I^{\prime}}y_{\alpha})
which is the
image of an object in
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,w\heartsuit}
.
∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a 0-affine morphism in
\mathop{\rm Cat}\nolimits^{\mathrm{conn}}_{n,\kappa}
.
Then the functor
\mathcal{C}^{(1)}\to\mathcal{D}^{(1)}
is also 0-affine. In particular, the resolution of
Corollary
3.17
is functorial in 0-affine maps.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5

\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,\mathrm{b}}\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{\kappa,\mathrm{b}}
is 0-affine.
Hence the functor
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,\mathrm{b}}\to\mathcal{D}^{(1)}
is 0-affine and since

\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,\mathrm{b}}\to\mathcal{C}^{(1)}
is surjective, so does the functor in question.
∎

4.2.
Nilpotent extensions and connective maps

Recall that a map of connective rings
R\to S
is a nilpotent extension if it is surjective on
\pi_{0}
with nilpotent kernel. This notion naturally generalizes to any stably monoidal category endowed with a compatible t-structure, and in what follows we will introduce these notions in that generality.

Definition 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a stably monoidal category. We say that a t-structure
t
on
\mathcal{E}
is compatible with the monoidal
structure if the monoidal unit is connective, and the tensor product of connective objects is connective.

Definition 4.8
.

Assume
\mathcal{E}
is a stably monoidal category endowed with a compatible t-structure. Let
f:R\to R^{\prime}
be a map of connective algebras in
\mathcal{E}
.

(1)

We say that
f
is a
k
-connective map if
\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(R\to R^{\prime})
is
k
-connective in the t-structure.

(2)

We say that
f
is a nilpotent extension of order
\leq m
if it is
0
-connective and the composite

\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(f)^{\otimes_{R}m}\to R^{\otimes_{R}m}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathrm{mult}}}{{\longrightarrow}}R\to\pi_{0}^{\heartsuit}R

is null.

Now let
\mathcal{C}
be a pre-
c
-category. The composition of functors endows the stable category

\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}}\nolimits}(\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C}))
with a presentably monoidal structure. Moreover, it also admits a natural t-structure, as follows.

Construction 4.9
.

Consider the subcategory of
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Pr^{L}}(\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C}))
functors that send weight connective objects to weight connective
objects. This is a presentable subcategory closed under colimits and extensions, so by
[
Lur17
, Proposition 1.4.4.11(1)]
it extends to a t-structure which we call
the associated
t
-structure on
\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Pr^{L}}\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})
.

Remark 4.10
.

The associated t-structure is compatible with the monoidal structure in the sense of Definition
4.7
.

\triangleleft

To any functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
that induces a weight exact functor
f_{!}:\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})
one can associate the
endofunctor
f^{*}f_{!}\in\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\mathop{\mathrm{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}}\nolimits}(\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C}))
. It is a t-connective algebra object because both
f^{*}
and
f_{!}

preserve weight connectivity.

Definition 4.11
.

We say that a functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
of pre-
c
-categories that induces a weight exact functor

f_{!}:\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})
is a
k
-connective (resp. a nilpotent extension) if it is 0-affine and the associated algebra

f_{!}f^{*}\in\mathop{\rm Alg}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm End}\nolimits_{\Pr^{L}}(\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})))
is
k
-connective (resp. a nilpotent extension).

Proposition 4.12
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be an
0
-affine functor of pre-
c
-categories that induces a weight exact functor

f_{!}:\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})
.

(1)

f
is
k
-connective for
k\in\mathbb{N}
if and only if for any
x,y\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}
the map

\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}}(x,y)\to\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{w\heartsuit}}(f(x),f(y))

is
k
-connective.

(2)

If
f
is a nilpotent extension, then

\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}}(x,y)\to\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})^{w\heartsuit}}(f(x),f(y))

is surjective and there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any sequence of composable morphisms
g_{1},\dots,g_{m}
in
\mathcal{A}
with

f(g_{i})\simeq 0
we have that the composite
g_{m}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}\simeq 0
.

Proof.

Set
I:=\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}\to f^{*}f_{!})
.

First we show part (1). By adjunction,
k
-connectivity is equivalent to saying that
for any
x,y\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}
the map

\mathop{\rm map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}(y,x)\to\mathop{\rm map}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}(y,f^{*}f_{!}(x))

is
k
-connective. By Proposition
1.17
this is also equivalent to weight
k
-connectivity
I(x)
for any

x\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}
. Since
I

commutes with colimits and since the colimit closure of
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{w\heartsuit}
in
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})
is
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 0}

(Proposition
1.17
), the last claim is also
equivalent to
I
sending weight connective objects to weight
k
-connective ones.

To prove part (2), observe that the triviality of the composite
I^{\circ m}\to\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}\to\pi_{0}(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})})

implies that
I^{\circ m}\to\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}
factors through a 1-connective object
F
. Now, take any composable sequence of
morphisms between objects of the heart

x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{1}}}{{\to}}x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{2}}}{{\to}}\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{m}}}{{\to}}x_{m}

g_{i}
with
f_{!}(g_{i})\simeq 0
, The composite
g_{m}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}
lifts to a map

x_{0}\to I^{\circ m}x_{m},

but the composite
x_{0}\to I^{\circ m}x_{m}\to x_{m}
is then zero, since it factors through
Fx_{m}\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 1}
.
∎

Example 4.13
.

It follows from
4.12
that the
0
-connectivity condition amounts to surjectivity of the map

\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{A}}(x,y)\to\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{B}}(f(x),f(y))
for all
x,y\in\mathcal{A}
.
For
k
-connective
f
and
k>0
this map is an isomorphism.

\triangleleft

Example 4.14
.

A map of connective ring spectra
R\to S
is
k
-connective if and only if the induced map

\mathrm{Perf}(R)\to\mathrm{Perf}(S)
is
k
-connective.

\triangleleft

Definition 4.15
.

We say that a functor
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
of pre-
c
-categories that induces a weight exact functor

f_{!}:\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})\to\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})
is a weak nilpotent extension (of order
\leq m
) if it is
0
-connective
and there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
such that for any sequence of composable morphisms

x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{1}}}{{\to}}x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{2}}}{{\to}}\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{m}}}{{\to}}x_{m}

in
\mathcal{C}
with
x_{0}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\leq 0},x_{m}\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
and
f(g_{i})\simeq 0
we have that the composite
g_{m}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}\simeq 0
.

Remark 4.16
.

Proposition
4.12
(2) implies that a nilpotent extension of pre-
c
-categories
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}

is a weak nilpotent extension. We do not know whether the converse holds in general, however, it is true if both
\mathcal{C}

and
\mathcal{D}
are bounded weighted categories: in this case we have an equivalence

\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathop{\rm Fun}\nolimits_{\Sigma}(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit,\mathrm{op}},\mathop{\rm Sp}\nolimits),
and triviality of the map

I^{\otimes m}\to\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}\to\pi_{0}(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})})

can be checked objectwise. In this case, the notion of nilpotent extension here agrees with that of
[
ES22
, Definition 5.1.1]
.

\triangleleft

Remark 4.17
.

For a weak nilpotent extension the maps of rings

\pi_{0}\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{C}}(x)\to\pi_{0}\mathrm{End}_{\mathcal{D}}(f(x))

are nilpotent extensions of rings. Thus, idempotents lift along these maps and in particular, a nilpotent extension
between idempotent complete categories is essentially surjective on the nose.

Furthermore, the converse is true: if
f
is essentially surjective up to retracts and for any

x\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
the map on
endomorphism rings as above is a nilpotent extension, then
f
is a weak nilpotent extension. In other words,
it suffices to check condition (2) in the case when
x=y
and condition (3) when all domains and codomains of
g_{i}

are equal. To see this, observe that

\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}}(x,y)\to\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit}}(f(x),f(y))
is a retract of

\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}}(x\oplus y,x\oplus y)\to\pi_{0}\mathop{\rm Map}\nolimits_{\mathcal{D}^{\heartsuit}}(f(x\oplus y),f(x\oplus y))
and that the morphisms
g_{i}
and their
composite can be viewed in the endomorphism ring of the coproduct of all the domains and codomains of
g_{i}
’s.

\triangleleft

Lemma 4.18
.

Let
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
be a weak nilpotent extension of order
\leq n
of pre-
c
-categories.
Set
I:=\mathop{\rm fib}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm id}\nolimits_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}\to f^{*}f_{!})
.
Then for any
x\in\mathcal{C}_{w\geq 0}
the canonical map
I^{\circ n}x\to x
kills weight
0
.
Furthermore, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
the map
I^{\circ nk}x\to x
kills weights
\leq k
.

Proof.

To show that
I^{\circ n}x\to x
kills weight
0
, by Lemma
3.2
, it suffices to
construct a weight decomposition of
I^{\circ n}x
such that the composite
w_{\leq 0}I^{\circ n}x\to I^{\circ n}x\to x

is zero.
Fix any weight decomposition of
x
. By induction, we construct
x_{0}=w_{\leq 0}x
and
x_{i}\to Ix_{i-1}
such that

x_{i}\to Ix_{i-1}\to\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits_{i}

is a weight decomposition. In particular,
x_{i}
belongs to the weight heart and
\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits_{i}\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 1}
.
The cofiber of the map
I^{\circ n}x_{0}\to I^{\circ n}x
also belongs to
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 1}
since
I^{\circ n}

preserves weight connectivity (Proposition
4.12
).
Combining these two facts, we see that the cofiber of the composition

\beta:x_{n}\to Ix_{n-1}\dots\to I^{\circ n}x_{0}\to I^{\circ n}x

is also in
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 1}
, so it gives a weight decomposition for
I^{\circ n}x
. Now the composite

x_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\beta}}{{\to}}I^{\circ n}x\to x

is equivalent to the composite

x_{n}\to x_{n-1}\to\dots\to x_{0}\to x.

By construction, each of the maps
x_{i}\to x_{i-1}
is null after applying
f_{!}
, so by the condition in
Definition
4.15
, the whole composite is zero.

We prove the furthermore part using induction on
k
. Assuming the claim is true for
k-1
, the map

I^{\circ n(k-1)}x\to x
decomposes as follows:

I^{\circ n(k-1)}x\to w_{\geq k}(I^{\circ n(k-1)}x)\to x.

By naturality, we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{\circ nk}x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{\circ n}(w_{\geq k}(I^{\circ n(k-1)}x))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces I^{\circ n(k-1)}x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces w_{\geq k}(I^{\circ n(k-1)}x)}

in which the right vertical map is the
k
-th suspension of a morphism killing weights
\leq 0
. Hence, it kills weights
\leq k
. Thus, the composite

I^{\circ nk}x\to w_{\geq k}(I^{\circ n(k-1)}x)\to x
also kills weights
\leq k
.
∎

Corollary 4.19
.

Fix the notations of Lemma
4.18
. For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
and any sequence of composable morphisms

x_{0}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{1}}}{{\to}}x_{1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{2}}}{{\to}}\dots\stackrel{{\scriptstyle g_{kn}}}{{\to}}x_{kn}

such that
f_{!}(g_{i})\simeq 0
,
x_{kn}
is connective and
x_{0}
belongs to
\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\leq k}
,
the composition
g_{kn}\circ\dots\circ g_{1}
is trivial.

Proof.

By assumption, each
g_{i}
decomposes as

x_{i-1}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\bar{g}_{1}}}{{\to}}I(x_{i})\to x_{i},

so the total composition is equivalent to
x_{0}\to I^{\circ kn}x_{kn}\to x_{kn}
. This kills weights
\leq k
by
the furthermore part of Lemma
4.18
. Since
x_{0}\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\leq k}
, the composite is zero.
∎

Remark 4.20
.

4.19
shows that for a nilpotent extension
f:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
of pre-
c
-categories and any

x\in\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\geq 0}\cap\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})_{w\leq k}
the map

\pi_{0}\mathrm{End}_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})}(x)\to\pi_{0}\mathrm{End}_{\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{D})}(f_{!}x)

has nilpotent kernel. Note that this may not be true without the bounds on weights.

\triangleleft

.

and Corollary

(1) combined with Lemma

\bar{f}
is

-connective.

To show the claim for

(1).
Recall that we have a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathop{\rm Ind}\nolimits(\mathcal{C})^{\kappa,\mathrm{b}})}

4.3.

For an object

.

7.1.
Localizations of bounded weighted categories

Some subtlety in the theory of
c
-categories comes from the fact that examples of
c
-categories in practice are a lot more special than generic examples.

Since
c
-categories arise as kernels of localizations between cconnective categories, wild behaviour of
c
-categories can arise from unusual examples of localizations.

Recall that for any connective ring spectrum and any set
S
of morphisms between finitely generated projective modules over
it one may define the localization
R\to R[S^{-1}]
which universally inverts those morphisms, and
R[S^{-1}]
is again going
to be a connective ring spectrum. More generally, this works in the context of bounded weighted categories: given a
subcategory of a bounded weighted category
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}
generated under finite limits and colimits by a set of objects of
weight amplitude
[0,1]
, the localization
\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{D}
admits a weight structure, such that the functor
\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}/\mathcal{D}
is weight
exact. One may wonder if the converse holds. If it did, studying
c
-categories (of width
\leq 1
) would be reduced to studying such
subcategories. The next example shows that it can fail: we construct a weight exact localization, whose kernel is
not generated by objects of weight amplitude
[0,n-1]
.

Example 7.2
.

Let us use
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(-)
to denote the category of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded modules over a
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ring spectrum.
A slight generalization of Example
1.5
shows that
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(A)^{\omega}
is naturally a bounded
weighted category when
A
is a connective ring spectrum with the heart generated by graded shifts of
A
as a module over
itself.

Let
k
be a field, and consider the
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-graded ring
R_{n}=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]/(x_{i}x_{j},i\neq j)
, where
k
is a field and
x_{i}
has grading generating the
i
-th coordinate.
By localizing away from the tensor ideal generated by
\otimes_{1}^{n}\mathop{\rm cof}\nolimits(x_{i})
, we obtain
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\prod_{1}^{n}k[x_{i}^{\pm 1}])
. The ring
\prod_{1}^{n}k[x_{i}^{\pm 1}]
is connective, so we obtain a weight exact localization functor

L:\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(R_{n})^{\omega}\to\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\prod_{1}^{n}k[x_{i}^{\pm 1}])^{\omega}.

We claim that any connective object
M
in the kernel of this localization with weight amplitude
[0,n-1]
vanishes. This means in particular that
L
is a weight exact localization of categories with bounded weight structure such that the kernel is not generated by objects of weight amplitude
[0,n-1]
. By taking a direct sum of these localizations over all
n\geq 0
, we obtain such a localization where the kernel is not generated by objects of uniformly bounded weight amplitude.

We now justify our claim. Because the ring
R_{n}
is an iterated pullback,
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(R_{n})^{\omega}
is a weight exact fully faithful subcategory of the iterated pullback category

P_{n}=\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k[x_{1}])^{\omega}\times_{\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k)^{\omega}}\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k[x_{2}])^{\omega}\times\dots\times_{\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k)^{\omega}}\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k[x_{n}])^{\omega}.

Let
M
be a connective nonzero object in
P_{n}
that is
x_{i}
-torsion for each
i
. Let us use
M^{\prime}
to denote the image of
M
in
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k)
, which is nonzero because
M
is nonzero
x_{i}
-complete for each
i
. To finish the proof, it is enough to show that
\pi_{k}M^{\prime}\neq 0
for some
k\geq n
.

An
x_{i}
-torsion object of
\mathop{\rm Mod}\nolimits^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(k[x_{i}])^{\omega}
can be written as a finite direct sum of
k[x_{i}]/x_{i}^{k}
for some
k
, shifted into some multigrading. It follows that for any nonzero homogeneous element
z\in\pi_{*}M^{\prime}
, and any
1\leq i\leq n
,
z
is either the target of a
x_{i}
-Bockstein differential, from a class that lifts to a nonzero class in
\pi_{*+1}M^{\prime}
, or the source of a
x_{i}
-Bockstein differential to a class that lifts to a nonzero class in
\pi_{*+1}M^{\prime}
.

Starting with a nonzero homogeneous element
z_{0}\in\pi_{*}M^{\prime}
, we follow an algorithm which at each step takes in a nonzero homogeneous class
z_{i}\in\pi_{*}M^{\prime}
and outputs a nonzero homogeneous class
z_{i+1}\in\pi_{*\pm 1}M^{\prime}
if
*<n
. We will show that the algorithm terminates by outputting a nonzero homogeneous element in
\pi_{k}M^{\prime}
for
k\geq n
, which will complete the proof.

At each step of the algorithm, if our class
z_{i}
is in
\pi_{j}M^{\prime}
, for
j\geq n
, the algorithm terminates. Otherwise, we can assume that
0\leq j<n
. If
z_{i}
is the source of a
x_{j+1}
-Bockstein differential,

be a representative of the differential, and otherwise let it be a representative of the target of the differential.

We can see this algorithm terminates as follows: we lexicographically order the multidegrees in
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, and note that whenever the homotopy degree of the

is higher than that of

, the multigrading with respect to this ordering is higher than for all
z_{j}
for
j<i
. Thus the algorithm must terminate since there are only finitely many nonzero multigradings for which the homotopy groups of
M^{\prime}
.

\triangleleft

7.3.
Weakly phantom descendable functors

The notion of a descendable functor in Definition

One version is to require the filtrations in

another is to require the filtrations to exist up to phantom extensions. Finally, we can require the filtrations to exist
non-functorially and up to phantom extensions.

Definition 7.8

Let

(1)

Example 7.9
.

We give an example of a weakly descendable functor with

which is not descendable for any

Consider the functor

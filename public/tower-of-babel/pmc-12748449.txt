LINC00519 promotes the progression of cholangiocarcinoma through regulating the axis of miR-876-3p/GATA1
Authors: Kaixuan Hui, Zhenlong Chai, Xiao Yang, Lina Huang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Background and objectivesDue to its lack of early symptoms, cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) is frequently diagnosed at advanced stages, contributing to its dismal prognosis. Therefore, it is necessary to find new, efficient therapeutic methods for the disease. This study excavated the role of LINC00519 in CCA progression, aiming to provide useful insights into CCA treatment.Materials and methodsThis study collected tumor and normal paracancer tissues from CCA patients (n = 202) to explore the prognostic significance of LINC00519 in CCA. CCA cell lines CCLP1 and QBC939 were employed for detecting the role of LINC00519 on CCA tumor development. The qRT-PCR was used to measure the expression of biomolecules. Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) and transwell assay were used to estimate CCA cell proliferation, migration and invasion. Binding sites between biomolecules were verified using a dual luciferase reporter assay.ResultsLINC00519 was upregulated in CCA. The upregulation of LINC00519 was associated with poor prognosis and serverity in CCA patients. LINC00519 facilitated CCA progression by promoting CCA cell proliferation rate, as well as cell migration and invasion ability. The approach for LINC00519 altering CCA cell activities was to inhibit miR-876-3p, and miR-876-3p could further negatively regulate GATA1.ConclusionLINC00519 upregulation may serve as a potential indicator of CCA aggravation. LINC00519 promoted CCA development by targeting miR-876-3p to affect cancer cell activities. GATA1, a potential target of miR-876-3p, suggests the potential underlying molecular pathway. Targeting LINC00519 could be an efficient way to manage CCA.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s12672-025-04140-4.

Introduction

Cholangiocarcinoma (CCA) refers to malignant tumours that originate from the epithelial cells of the bile ducts [1]. These tumours can be classified into two categories based on their location: intrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (iCCA) and extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma (eCCA) [2]. Since patients with cholangiocarcinoma are mostly diagnosed at an advanced stage and are no longer amenable to surgical treatment, the five-year survival of patients still remains < 40% [1]. Therefore, finding a new approach to effectively treat the disease and improve patient survival is critical.

Long non-coding RNAs (lncRNAs) are described as non-coding RNAs (ncRNAs) that have more than 200 nucleotides [3]. LncRNAs could regulate post-transcription by acting as competitive endogenous RNAs (ceRNAs), and further regulate gene expression [4]. LncRNAs thus are multifacetedly involved in cancer development, including modulating tumour cell activities and regulating energy metabolism [5, 6]. In CCA, lncRNA LINC00519 was found to be the biomarker for CCA diagnosis, and its overexpression was associated with poor CCA prognosis [7]. Furthermore, it was reported that LINC00519 could facilitate lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) progression by targeting miR-450b-5p/miR-515-5p/Yes-associated protein 1 (YAP1) regulatory pathway [8]. As a newly found potential prognostic biomarker of CCA, further investigation of the mechanism of LINC00519 affecting CCA progression would be critical for exploring new therapeutic strategies for CCA.

As ceRNAs, lncRNAs can regulate gene expression by acting as microRNA (miRNA) sponges [4]. MiRNAs are short ncRNAs (containing 21–22 nucleotides), which can alter gene expression at post-transcriptional levels via targeting the 3’ untranslated regions (3’-UTRs) of the target mRNAs [9]. In CCA, lncRNA-mediated ceRNA regulatory networks play a crucial role in controlling the progression of the disease. For instance, lncRNA DLEU1 functioned as a sponge for miR-149-5p, thereby upregulating the expression of the oncogene YAP1 and ultimately promoting the progression of CCA [10]. Among the dysregulated miRNAs in CCA, miR-876-3p is a modulator of tumour growth-related gene expression [11–13]. In CCA, the overexpression of miR-876 exhibited significant tumour suppressor activity and inhibited the growth of tumour cells [14]. This study thus decided to investigate the correlation between LINC00519 and miR-876-3p, as well as the role LINC00519/miR-876-3p plays in CCA progression.

In this study, CCA patients were recruited to assess the clinical predictive significance of LINC00519 in CCA. Subsequently, cell experiments conducted using CCA cell lines were used to explore the effect of LINC00519 on CCA development. This study would contribute to future research on prognostic biomarkers and new therapeutic strategies for CCA by providing valuable information.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

This study was conducted under the ethical principles outlined in the Declaration of Helsinki. This study recruited 202 CCA patients at Changzhou Third People’s Hospital from January 2021 to December 2023. All patients received surgical treatment and were followed postoperatively for a period ranging from 3 to 60 months to assess recovery and survival outcomes. The study endpoints included disease recurrence, metastasis, and mortality attributable to CCA.

Sample collection

The tumor tissue samples and normal tisues adjacent to the tumors were collected during surgical resection. Collected tissues were histologically evaluated and confirmed by two pathologists to ensure diagnostic consensus. Following pathological verification, the tissues were snap-frozen in liquid nitrogen and maintained at -80 °C.

Cell culture and transfection

For the cell experiments, the normal intrahepatic biliary epithelial cells (HiBEC), iCCA cells (CCLP1), and eCCA cells (QBC939) were obtained from was the Shanghai Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences, China. Roswell Park Memorial Institute Medium (RPMI 1640) supplemented with 10% fetal bovine serum (FBS) and 1% penicillin-streptomycin solution was applied for the cell culture. The cells were maintained at 5% CO2 and 37 °C during the cultivation. Cells passaged less than 10 generations were employed for the following experiments.

Cells were transformed into 24-well plates overnight, in which RPMI 1640 medium supplemented with 10% FBS. Removing the medium, the cells were transfected in OPTI-MEM I medium applying Lipofectamine 3000 (Invitrogen, USA) with the following constructs: LINC00519 small interfering RNA (si-LINC00519), si-NC, miR-876-3p inhibitor, inhibitor NC, and si-GATA1. Cell transfection lasted for 6 h.

Total RNA extraction

TRIzol reagent (Invitrogen, USA) was used for the RNA extraction from tissue samples and cells. The tissue samples were cut into 1–3 mm pieces and ground in TRIzol reagent, while the cells did not need to be ground.

RNA Extraction Process: Chloroform was added at room temperature to facilitate the dissociation of the nucleic acid-protein complex. After allowing the mixture to dissociate for 10 min, it was centrifuged at 6000 g for 15 min to separate the phases, resulting in the collection of the upper aqueous phase. Isopropanol was then added to get the RNA pellet. DNase I was subsequently added to eliminate any DNA contamination from the RNA. After washing the purified RNA with 75% ethanol, the mixture was centrifuged again at 6000 g for 15 min at 4 °C. The pure total RNA was obtained after allowing the RNA pellet to air dry. RNA samples with an OD260/280 ratio of 1.8-2.0 and concentration > 40 µg/mL were maintained at 4℃ for further study.

Real-time quantitative PCR

cDNA was synthesized from total RNA using the PrimeScript RT Master Mix kit (Takara, Japan) according to the instructions of the manufacturer. After the Reverse transcription, the MX3000P Real-time PCR instrument (Stratagene, Germany) was employed for the quantitative real-time polymerase chain reaction (qRT-PCR). Gene-specific primers for LINC00519, miR-876-3p, RNA U6 small nuclear 1 (RNU6-1), GATA1 and glyceraldehyde-3-phosphate dehydrogenase (GAPDH) were included. The reaction conditions: 94 °C for 3 min; denaturation: 94 °C for 20 s. annealing/extension: 62 °C for 40 s; amplification cycles: 40.

Cell proliferation assay

The proliferative capacity of CCLP1 and QBC939 cells was detected through the CCK-8 assay. Cells were first transferred to 96-well plates and maintained in complete medium. Cell proliferation was assessed by measuring the optical density at 450 nm (OD450) at 0, 24, 48, and 72 h post-seeding. At each time point, CCK-8 solution was supplied to each well, followed by incubation at 37 °C for 2 h, after which absorbance was measured.

Cell migration assay

Cell migration ability of CCLP1 and QBC939 cells was examined through a Transwell chamber assay. The permeable membrane was rehydrated with the serum-free RPMI 1640 medium for 30 min at 37 °C. Cells were serum-starved for 12 h, trypsinised, washed with PBS, and resuspended in serum-free RPMI 1640 medium. Then the cell suspension (5 × 105 cells/mL) was transferred to the upper chamber, while the lower chamber was filled with RPMI 1640 complete medium. After 24 h of incubation, non-migrated cells were removed by washing with calcium-free PBS. Migrated cells were fixed with 4% paraformaldehyde (30 min), stained with 0.1% crystal violet (20 min), and washed with PBS. Cells were counted under an inverted light microscope (Olympus, USA).

Cell invasion assay

Cell invasion was assessed using Matrigel-coated Transwell chambers. The upper chamber membrane was coated with 50 mg/L Matrigel (1:8 dilution) and air-dried at 4℃. The subsequent steps were performed identically to the migration assay.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

The DIANA tools suite was employed to identify the putative binding interface between LINC00519 and miR-876-3p. To functionally validate this interaction, this study engineered dual-expression constructs containing either the wild-type LINC00519 (WT-LINC00519) or its mutant counterpart (MUT-LINC00519) sequences with modified miR-876-3p binding sites, subsequently cloned into luciferase reporter plasmids. The RPMI 1640 complete medium was used for culturing CCLP1 and QBC939 cells. At 80% confluency, the cells were co-transfected with firefly luciferase reporter constructs (containing either WT-LINC00519 or MUT-LINC00519 sequences) and Renilla luciferase plasmid (pRL-TK) using Lipofectamine 3000 according to the protocols of the manufacturer. At 24 h post-transfection, dual-luciferase reporter assays were performed using the Dual-Luciferase® Reporter Assay System (Promega, USA). Firefly luciferase activity was measured immediately after adding D-luciferin substrate. Renilla luciferase activity was subsequently detected following the addition of coelenterazine substrate, using a Multiskan FC microplate reader (Thermo Fisher Scientific). Relative luciferase activity was calculated by normalising firefly luminescence values to corresponding Renilla luciferase readings.

The TargetScan database performed the identification of miR-876-3p binding sites within the GATA1 3’UTR. For the dual-luciferase reporter assay, dual-expression constructs containing either the wild-type GATA1 (WT-GATA1) or mutant-type GATA1 (MUT-GATA1) sequences with modified miR-876-3p binding sites, cloned into luciferase reporter plasmids.

Data analysis

All cell experiments were performed with 3 independent experiments with 3 parallel measurements of each. The distribution of the data was evaluated using the Shapiro-Wilk test, conducted with SPSS software. For testing the differences in the data from 2 groups, the paired t-test was used for normally distributed data; the Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for non-normally distributed data. For testing the differences in the data from more than 2 groups, the one-way ANOVA with Tukey’s post-hoc test was used for normally distributed data; the Kruskal-Wallis test with Dunnet test was used for non-normally distributed data. Furthermore, the relationship between clinical features and overall survival in CCA patients was analysed using a multivariate Cox proportional hazards model, also implemented in SPSS. To assess the impact of LINC00519 expression on the survival probability of CCA patients over a specified duration post-treatment, the Kaplan-Meier survival curve was generated using SPSS. Lastly, the correlations between the expression levels of LINC00519 and miR-876-3p, as well as between miR-876-3p and GATA1, were determined using the Pearson correlation coefficient.

Results

Relative expression of LINC00519 s and its significance in CCA patients

The results reflected a significant elevation in the relative expression of LINC00519 in tumor tissues than in normal tissues (P < 0.001, Fig. 1A). Patients with different subtypes (iCCA vs. eCCA) showed no significant difference in LINC00519 (Figure S1A). In this study, the CCA patients could be split into two groups due to the average relative expression levels of LINC00519. For the association between LINC00519 relative expression levels and clinical features of CCA patients, there were no statistical differences in age (P = 0.251), sex (P = 0.719), HBsAg (P = 0.865), tumour location (P = 0.455), and tumour differentiation degree (P = 0.692) between patients with high and low LINC00519 expression levels (Table 1). In comparison to patients exhibiting low expression levels of LINC00519, those with elevated LINC00519 expression levels demonstrated a higher prevalence of lymph node metastasis (P = 0.013) and advanced TNM staging (P = 0.028, Table 1), consistent with the stratification analysis based on TNM stage (Figure S1B) and lymph node metastasis (Figure S1C). The relative expression levels of LINC00519 showed a strong correlation with CCA patient survival time, and a high LINC00519 expression level indicated a lower survival probability in CCA patients (HR: 1.989, 95% CI = 1.162–3.402, P = 0.012, Table 2; P = 0.001, Fig. 1B). Moreover, lymph node metastasis status (HR: 1.880, 95% CI = 1.056–3.344, P = 0.032) and TNM stage (HR: 1.875, 95% CI = 1.068–3.293, P = 0.029) were also closely associated with CCA patient survival time (Table 2). The Cox model has been verified by the proportional hazard assumption based on the Schoenfeld residual, and all included indicators met the assumption (P > 0.05, Table S1).

Effect of LINC00519 on CCA cell activities

The results showed higher relative expression levels of LINC00519 in both CCLP1 and QBC939 cells than in HiBEC cells (Fig. 2A). After being transfected with si-LINC00519, LINC00519 was significantly downregulated in CCA cells (Fig. 2B). The LINC00519 downregulation resulted in an inhibition of cell activities, including proliferation (Fig. 2C, D), migration (Fig. 2E) and invasion (Fig. 2F) of CCA cells.

MiR-876-3p mediated the effect of LINC00519 on CCA cells

The DIANA tools probed the binding sites between LINC00519 and miR-876-3p (Fig. 3A). The test of miR-876-3p relative expression characteristics in CCA showed a downregulated miR-876-3p relative expression in both CCA tissues (Fig. 3B) and cells (Fig. 3C). In CCA tumour tissues, the Pearson correlation analysis illustrated an inverse proportional relationship between the relative expression of miR-876-3p and LINC00519 (Fig. 3D). Furthermore, the results of the dual-luciferase reporter assay demonstrated a strong correlation between LINC00519 and miR-876-3p (Fig. 3E). There was a decreased luciferase activity in the WT-LINC00519 group with elevated miR-876-3p expression and an increased luciferase activity with the downregulation of miR-876-3p (Fig. 3E). In the MUT-LINC00519 group, the results showed no statistical difference in luciferase activity under different miR-876-3p expression levels (Fig. 3E). The rescue experiments showed that after overexpressing LINC00519, miR-876-3p was upregulated in CCA cells (Fig. 3F). While the miR-876-3p relative expression was further inhibited by the transfection of miR-876-3p inhibitor (Fig. 3F).

The association between miR-876-3p and GATA1

The TargetScan database has identified potential binding sites between miR-876-3p and GATA1 (Fig. 5A). An analysis of GATA1 relative expression in CCA revealed that GATA1 is significantly upregulated in both CCA tissues (Fig. 5B) and cell lines (Fig. 5C). Pearson correlation analysis indicated an inverse relationship between GATA1 and miR-876-3p relative expression levels in CCA tumour tissues (Fig. 5D). Additionally, dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed a robust association between miR-876-3p and GATA1 (Fig. 5E). Specifically, luciferase activity was diminished in the WT-GATA1 group upon the upregulation of miR-876-3p, while the downregulation of miR-876-3p resulted in increased luciferase activity (Fig. 5E). In contrast, the MUT-GATA1 group exhibited no significant variation in luciferase activity across different levels of miR-876-3p expression (Fig. 5E). Furthermore, rescue experiments demonstrated that the downregulation of LINC00519 led to decreased GATA1 mRNA expression in CCA cells, which was subsequently restored by the downregulation of miR-876-3p (Fig. 5F). However, following transfection with si-GATA1, GATA1 expression was again inhibited in CCA cells. The protein levels of GATA1 was regulated by the LINC00519/miR-876-3p axis in a consistent trend (Figure S2B).

Discussion

LncRNAs are ncRNAs that could regulate gene expression and protein functionality, and dysregulation of lncRNAs has been linked to many cancers [15–19]. LINC00519 has also been reported as an indicator of the progression of many cancers such as head and neck squamous cell carcinoma (HNSCC) and LUSC [20, 21]. The upregulation of LINC00519 was related to a worse prognosis in pancreatic adenocarcinoma (PAAD) [22]. Previously, several lncRNA/miRNA axes have been revealed to regulate tumor progression on cell levels. For instance, the lncRNA PCAT6/miR-330-5p axis was reported to regulate cell proliferation and invasion of cholangiocarcinoma, showing significant potential in modulating tumor progression [23]. The lncRNA SNHG3/miR-3173-5p/ERG axis was revealed to mediate the malignant phenotype of CCA cells by modulating cell growth and migration [24]. But these studies lacked clinical evaluation confirming the potential of these ncRNA in serving as biomarkers. In this study, lncRNA LINC00519 was identified as a significant prognostic indicator for poor clinical outcomes and was associated with disease severity in CCA patients. These findings align with previous research demonstrating the predictive potential of LINC00519 in CCA prognosis [7].

In this study, CCA cells (CCLP1 and QBC939) were involved to explore how LINC00519 influenced CCA development [25]. This study indicated that LINC00519 could facilitate the aggravation of CCA tumours by affecting CCA cell activities. The previous study found that LINC00519 was one of the endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress-associated lncRNAs, which was positively correlated with responding to immune checkpoints and chemosensitivity in gliomas [26]. Upregulation of LINC00519 could promote glioma cell migration and invasion and further aggravate glioma [26]. Moreover, LINC00519 has been reported as the promoter of tumour growth in LUSC by sponging miR-450b-5p and miR-515-5p [8]. This study filled the gap in the way LINC00519 promoted CCA progression.

LncRNAs can affect the progression of cancers by acting as miRNA sponges [27]. This study thus explored the miRNA that LINC00519 sponged in CCA. The results demonstrated that LINC00519 sponged miR-876-3p in the CCA. MiR-876-3p was reported as a tumour suppressor gene in many cancers. For instance, the overexpression of miR-876-3p was found to be able to suppress proliferation and facilitate apoptosis in pancreatic cancer cells by targeting Jagged2 (JAG2) [28]. MiR-876-3p could also inhibit cell proliferation, migration, and invasion in colon cancer cells and subsequently be a tumor suppressor in colon cancer [11]. MiR-876-3p served as the functional mediator of the driven effect of LINC00519 on CCA tumour progression. These findings corroborate the tumour-suppressive role of miR-876 in CCA pathogenesis, which was reported in a previous study [14]. The potential regulation of EMT-related molecules by the LINC00519/miR-876-3p was primarily evaluated in one CCA cell. EMT is the basis of cell migration and invasion, which is directly associated with cell adhesion and further affects the motility of tumour cells [29]. Hence, these findings demonstrated that miR-876-3p might be an important tumour-suppressive miRNA in CCA progression.

To gain more insight into the role the two biomolecules play in CCA, this study further explored the target gene of miR-876-3p in CCA. This study found that miR-876-3p negatively regulated GATA1 expression in CCA. GATA factors are zinc finger DNA-binding proteins that control the development of diverse tissues by activating or repressing transcription [30, 31]. It was reported that elevated GATA1 expression is strongly associated with CCA tumour mutations [32]. Moreover, GATA1 could lead to the activation of the AKT signalling pathway [33]. The enhanced activity of the AKT signalling pathway is the promoter of the development of multiple cancers, including CCA [34, 35]. This study speculated that LINC00519/miR-876-3p targeted GATA1 and subsequently regulated the AKT signalling pathway, which further influenced the progression of CCA. Although the complete regulatory pathway needs to be investigated in the future, this study was meaningful for exploring new targets and designing new therapeutic options for CCA.

This study provided valuable insights into future therapeutic approaches for CCA. Based on the results of this study, exploring CCA tumour-targeted LINC00519-silencing medicine based on antisense oligonucleotides (ASO) and si-RNA could effectively treat CCA in the future [36]. Moreover, exploring CCA tumour-targeted double-stranded (ds)-TS-miR-876-3p mimics could also be a promising therapeutic approach [37]. However, there was still a drawback in this study. To include a large sample size, this study had not separated different subtypes of cholangiocarcinoma, such as intrahepatic and extrahepatic subtypes, which might overlook the specificity of LINC00519. Stratification analysis revealed insignificant difference in LINC00519 levels between intrahepatic and extrahepatic cholangiocarcinoma, implying its similar function in two subtypes. However, further validations are indeed to comprehensively understand the significance of LINC00519 in cholangiocarcinoma. On the other hand, for the mechanism investigations, only cellular-level experiments were conducted to investigate the interactions between biomolecules and their effects on CCA development. No in vivo experiments were performed to verify whether the biomolecules included in this study are also critical for the development of the disease in vivo. In future studies, the potential of LINC00519 and miR-876-3p as therapeutic targets in CCA in vivo, as well as the methods that could control the expression levels of the two biomolecules in vivo, should be investigated.

Conclusions

In conclusion, the expression of LINC00519 was found to be upregulated in CCA. This upregulation of LINC00519 is associated with poor prognosis in CCA patients. LINC00519 contributes to the progression of CCA by enhancing cell proliferation, migration, and invasion capabilities in CCA cells. MiR-876-3p mediated the effects of LINC00519 on CCA cells by negatively regulating the expression of GATA1.

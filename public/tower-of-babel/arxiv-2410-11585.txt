Index estimate by first Betti number of minimal hypersurfaces in compact symmetric spaces
Toru Kajigaya and Keita Kunikawa

Abstract
. We show that the Morse index of unstable closed minimal hypersurface Σ \Sigma in a compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space M = G / K M=G/K is bounded from below by constant times the first Betti number of Σ \Sigma . Our proof is based on a natural extension of the previous method and this also provides a novel approach for the index estimate.

53C42, 53C35

1.
Introduction

Let
\Sigma
be a closed hypersurface in a closed Riemannian manifold
(M,g)
.
\Sigma
is called
minimal
if it is a critical point of the volume functional under any infinitesimal deformation of
\Sigma
. The study of minimal hypersurfaces is still actively researched in connection with various areas of mathematics. One of the main concern is related to the Morse index and the topology of minimal hypersurface. The notion of
index
is defined by the maximal dimension of the subspace of the space of normal vector fields such that the Hessian of the volume functional is negative-definite on the subspace, and this measures how many directions exist to decrease the volume of
\Sigma
. There are many interesting studies on the estimate of index and the relation to geometry of minimal hypersurface in a given Riemannian manifold. We refer the reader to
[
1
,
8
,
11
,
14
,
19
]
and references therein for a detailed background and related results.

As stated in
[
1
]
(see also
[
6
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
19
]
), there is a conjecture by Schoen, Marques and Neves that, if
M
has positive Ricci curvature and
{\rm dim}M\geq 3
, then there exists a positive constant
C
depending only on
M
such that the inequality

(1)

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq Cb_{1}(\Sigma)

holds for any closed embedded minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
in
M
, where
b_{1}(\Sigma)
is the first Betti number of
\Sigma
. This conjecture was confirmed by Savo
[
16
]
in the case when
M
is the standard sphere
S^{n}
, and by generalizing his method, Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
]
proved the conjecture affirmatively when
M
is a compact rank-one symmetric space (i.e.
M
is either
S^{n}
,
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
,
\mathbb{C}P^{n}
,
\mathbb{H}P^{n}
or the Cayley plane
\mathbb{O}P^{2}
). It is a natural attempt to extend these results to a general compact Riemannian symmetric space (
RSS
for short) of positive Ricci curvature, in particular, to a compact
semi-simple
RSS. Here, a compact symmetric space is called semi-simple if the identity component of the isometry group
I_{0}(M)
becomes a compact semi-simple Lie group, and it provides a very typical example of Riemannian manifold of positive Ricci curvature.

In this direction, by using the same method, Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
]
and Gorodski-Mendes-Radeschi
[
6
]
verified the conjecture for the following compact semi-simple symmetric spaces; The product of sphere
S^{p}\times S^{q}
of
p,q\geq 2
and
(p,q)\neq(2,2)
, the quaternionic Grassmannian manifolds,
Sp(n)
(for any
n
) and
SU(n)
of
n\leq 17
. We remark that, besides these symmetric examples, the conjecture has been also confirmed under some situations (see
[
1
,
6
]
for other results). However, as considered in
[
6
]
, it seems difficult to prove the conjecture by using the method in
[
1
]
for a general compact symmetric space.

In
[
9
]
, Mendes-Radeschi generalized the method by introducing the notion of
virtual immersion
of a Riemannian manifold
M
. Moreover, they proved that the inequality (
1
) holds when
M
is a product of two compact rank-one symmetric spaces, or for an oriented minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
in an oriented compact semi-simple symmetric space with an additional assumption that there exists a point
p\in\Sigma
such that the principal curvatures at
p
are distinct (
[
9
, Corollary D]
).
Note that as mentioned in
[
9
]
, the last result of
[
9
]
can be slightly extended, namely, the same conclusion holds if
\Sigma
is unstable (i.e.
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq 1
) and satisfies the assumption of principal curvatures. See also Appendix
A.2
for the method using the virtual immersion.

The aim of the present paper is to provide another approach to extend the method used by Savo and Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp. Furthermore, we confirm that the inequality (
1
) holds if
M
is a compact semi-simple RSS and the minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
in
M
is unstable. More precisely, we show the following result.

Theorem 1.1

(Theorem
5.1
)
.

Let
M
be a compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space and
\Sigma
be a closed minimal hypersurface embedded in
M
. If
\Sigma
is unstable, then it holds that

(2)

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{2}{d(d-1)+2(2n-3)}b_{1}(\Sigma),

where
d
is the dimension of the isometry group of
M
and
n={\rm dim}M
.

An embedded hypersurface
\Sigma
is called
two-sided
if the normal bundle is trivial in
M
(otherwise,
\Sigma
is called
one-sided
). It follows from the second variational formula (cf.
[
17
]
) that any two-sided minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
is unstable if
M
has positive Ricci curvature. Therefore, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 1.2
.

The inequality (
2
) holds for every two-sided closed minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
embedded in a compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space.

If
M
is simply-connected, then any embedded hypersurface in
M
is two-sided. Thus, we obtain

Corollary 1.3
.

The conjecture by Marques-Neves-Schoen holds for any simply-connected compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space.

We shall make some remarks on our results. Firstly, in Theorem
1.1
, we proved an index bound under the assumption that
\Sigma
is unstable (Note that Theorem
1.1
holds even if
\Sigma
is one-sided). Therefore in a compact semi-simple RSS, the problem is still remaining for stable minimal hypersurfaces. Notice that a stable minimal hypersurface in a compact semi-simple RSS
M
can exist only when
M
is not simply-connected and
\Sigma
is one-sided in
M
. Moreover, the inequality (
1
) suggests that
b_{1}(\Sigma)
must be
0
if
\Sigma
is stable. For example, the totally geodesic
\mathbb{R}P^{n-1}
is stable in
\mathbb{R}P^{n}
, and when
M=\mathbb{R}P^{n}
, the inequality (
1
) has been proved for any embedded closed minimal hypersurfaces (
[
1
]
). Secondly, we remark that in (2), the constant depending on
M
is not necessarily better than the previous results given in
[
1
,
6
,
9
,
16
]
. For example, the result by Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
]
provides a much better constant when
M
is a compact rank-one symmetric space, and Mendes-Radeschi
[
9
]
proved more precise result when
\Sigma
has a point
p
such that the principal curvatures at
p
are distinct.

The method by Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
]
is based on a kind of “averaging method”, which goes back to the method used in
[
7
,
17
]
. More precisely, the method of
[
1
]
is summarized as follows. Let
\Sigma
be a closed minimal hypersurface in a Riemannain manifold
M
. First, we generate a test normal variation
\Phi_{\omega}(v)
of
\Sigma
in
M
by combining a harmonic
1
-form
\omega
and a vector
v\in\mathcal{G}
of a finite dimensional metric vector space
\mathcal{G}
, namely, we take a bilinear map

\Phi:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma),

where
\mathcal{H}
is the space of harmonic
1
-forms on
\Sigma
and
\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)
denotes the space of normal vector fields along
\Sigma
in
M
. Next, we consider a quadratic form
q_{\omega}(v,w):=Q(\Phi_{\omega}(v),\Phi_{\omega}(w))
on
\mathcal{G}
, where
Q
is the index form of the minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
in
M
. Then it turns out that a sufficient condition for the inequality
(
1
)
is given by
{\rm Tr}q_{\omega}<0
for any non-trivial
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
(see Section
2
for details). A problem here is how to define a preferred test variation
\Phi
in order to estimate the trace of
q_{\omega}
. In
[
1
]
, two reasonable choices of test variations are presented by using an isometric immersion of the ambient Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. The first method is inspired by the idea of Ros
[
14
]
and Urbano
[
21
]
, and this is applicable to a minimal surface in a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold (see
[
1
, Proposition 4]
). Another choice is a generalization of the method by Savo
[
16
]
, and this can be applied to a minimal hypersurface of any dimension (
[
1
, Theorem A]
). In any case, an advantage of these methods is that the trace formula for
q_{\omega}
is derived in a better form so that
{\rm Tr}q_{\omega}
can be estimated by using geometry of the isometric immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
(See
[
1
,
6
]
or Appendix for details).
Thus, if furthermore one can find a preferred isometric immersion of
M
into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
so that
{\rm Tr}q_{\omega}<0
for any non-trivial
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
on any
\Sigma\subset M
, we obtain the index estimate (
1
) for any
\Sigma\subset M
. However, it should be emphasized that whether this method works depends on the choice of isometric immersion
M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
.

In the present paper, we show that the methods in
[
1
]
are naturally extended by considering an isometric immersion of a Riemannian manifold
M
into a compact semi-simple RSS
N=G/K
. Indeed, we can define the test variation
\Phi
and a quadratic form
q_{\omega}
by a similar way via the isometric immersion
M\to N=G/K
(See Section
3
and
4
). Moreover, we obtain generalized trace formulas (Proposition
3.3
and Theorem
4.3
) as extensions of formulas given in
[
1
, Proposition 1 and 2]
.
We remark that if
M=N=G/K
, it turns out that the test variation given in Section
4
coincides with the one defined in
[
9
]
by using the virtual immersion (See Appendix
A.2
for the correspondence). However, our approach as well as our description of the trace formula are different from those in
[
9
]
.

Similar to
[
1
]
, the trace can be estimated using geometry of
M\to N=G/K
.
Hence, we could obtain the estimate (
1
) for
\Sigma\subset M
if there is an isometric immersion
M\to N=G/K
satisfying that
{\rm Tr}q_{\omega}<0
for any non-trivial
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
on
\Sigma
(see Proposition
3.4
and
4.4
).
As a simple application of our results, we prove that the inequality (
1
) holds for any closed minimal surface in a Berger sphere
S^{3}
or more generally, for any closed minimal hypersurface in the geodesic hypersphere
S^{n}_{r}
of some radius
r
of the complex/quaternionic/octornionic projective space, by using an isometric embedding of
S^{n}_{r}
to the projective space (see Theorem
3.5
and
4.8
for more precise results).

Moreover, when
M
is itself a compact semi-simple RSS, by taking the trivial isometric immersion, i.e. the identity map
{id}:M\to M
, we show that the trace of
q_{\omega}
with respect to a certain test variation
\Phi
is always equal to
0
for any
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
on any two-sided minimal hypersurface
\Sigma\subset M
(Proposition
4.5
. Note that the same conclusion was also obtained in
[
9
]
by using the virtual immersion). This remarkable fact is being a key of the proof of Theorem.
1.1
.
In fact, when
{\rm Tr}q_{\omega}=0
for any
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
, we obtain an affine bound
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq C(b_{1}(\Sigma)-D)
for some constant
C,D
depending only on
M
(see Lemma
2.2
and Proposition
5.5
), and if furthermore
\Sigma
is unstable (i.e.
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq 1
), the affine bound implies the required linear bound
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq C^{\prime}b_{1}(\Sigma)
for some constant
C^{\prime}
determined by
C
and
D
. We remark that this argument has been used in
[
2
]
in the case when
N
is a flat torus and in
[
9
]
by using the virtual immersion, however, in both papers, they supposed an additional assumption that there exists a point
p\in\Sigma
such that the principal curvatures at
p
are distinct. In this paper, we shall improve their argument by using a non-trivial extension of some formulas, and prove the inequality (2) without the assumption of principal curvatures. Actually, our argument can be applied to the case when
N
is a flat torus as well, and the result given in
[
2
, Theorem 1]
is generalized to any closed minimal hypersurface in the flat torus.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we summarize our method of index estimate in a general setting based on the argument given in
[
1
,
9
]
. In Section
3
, by considering an isometric immersion of
M
into a compact semi-simple RSS
N
, we derive the first trace formula as an extension of the formula given in
[
1
, Proposition 1]
. By using this, we prove that the inequality (
1
) holds for any closed minimal surface embedded in a family of Berger spheres. In Section
4
, extending the second method given in
[
1
]
, we prove another trace formula via the isometric immersion
M\to N
. Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
in Section
5
by improving the argument given in
[
2
,
9
]
.

2.
Index and the method of estimate

2.1.
Second variation of minimal hypersurfaces

Let
M^{n}
be an
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma
a closed
(n-1)
-dimensional manifold. We consider an isometric immersion
f:\Sigma\to M
. We identify the tangent space
T_{p}\Sigma
with a subspace of
T_{f(p)}M
via the differential map
df_{p}:T_{p}\Sigma\to T_{f(p)}M
. We denote the orthogonal complement of
T_{p}\Sigma
in
T_{f(p)}M
by
\nu_{p}\Sigma
, and we call
\nu_{p}\Sigma
a normal space of
f
at
p
. In our setting,
\nu_{p}\Sigma
is a
1
-dimensional subspace. We denote the normal bundle of
f
by
\nu\Sigma
.

We denote the Levi-Civita connection of
M
by
\nabla^{M}
. The
second fundamental form

A
of
f
is defined by
A(X,Y):=(\nabla^{M}_{X}Y)^{\perp}
for
X,Y\in\Gamma(T\Sigma)
, where
\perp
means the orthogonal projection to the normal space. The second fundamental form is a symmetric tensor field on
\Sigma
. The mean curvature vector
H
of
f
is defined by
H:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}A(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})
, where
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n-1}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}\Sigma
. We say
f
is
minimal
if
f
is a critical point of the volume functional, or equivalently,
the mean curvature vector
H
is vanishing along
f
. In the following, we always assume
f
is a minimal immersion of
(n-1)
-dimensional closed manifold
\Sigma
. When
f
is an embedding, we call
\Sigma
a
minimal hypersurface
in
M
.

The second variation of the volume functional for a normal variation
\{f_{s}\}_{s\in(-\epsilon,\epsilon)}
of a minimal immersion
f=f_{0}
is given by (cf.
[
17
]
)

\displaystyle\frac{d^{2}}{ds^{2}}{\rm Vol}(f_{s})\Big|_{s=0}=\int_{\Sigma}|\nabla^{\perp}V|^{2}-{\rm Ric}^{M}(V,V)-|A|^{2}|V|^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
V:=\frac{df_{s}}{ds}|_{s=0}\in\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)
,
\nabla^{\perp}
denotes the normal connection and
{\rm Ric}^{M}
is the Ricci tensor of
M
. We define a quadratic form on
\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)
by

\displaystyle Q(V,W)=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\nabla^{\perp}V,\nabla^{\perp}W\rangle-{\rm Ric}^{M}(V,W)-|A|^{2}\langle V,W\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma}\quad\textup{for $V,W\in\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)$},

and
Q
is called the
index form
of
f
.
A minimal immersion
f
is said to be
stable
if
Q(V,V)\geq 0
for any normal vector field
V
, otherwise we say
unstable
.

Remark 2.1
.

As mentioned in
[
1
]
, we can deal with a non-orientable hypersurface. If this is the case, the symbol
d\mu_{\Sigma}
denotes the
Riemannian density
of
\Sigma
(See
[
12
]
for details). Since the divergence theorem holds even for Riemannian densities, the computation presented in this paper works just as well as in the orientable case.

By using the divergence theorem, we may write

Q(V,W)=-\int_{\Sigma}\langle\Delta^{\perp}V+R^{M}(V)^{\perp}+|A|^{2}V,W\rangle d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
\Delta^{\perp}
is the Laplacian of
\nabla^{\perp}
and
R^{M}(V):={\rm Tr}R^{M}(V,\cdot)\cdot
, where
R^{M}
is the curvature tensor of
M
defined by
R^{M}(X,Y)Z=\nabla^{M}_{X}\nabla^{M}_{Y}Z-\nabla^{M}_{Y}\nabla^{M}_{X}Z-\nabla^{M}_{[X,Y]}Z
. We put

\mathcal{J}(V):=\Delta^{\perp}V+R^{M}(V)^{\perp}+|A|^{2}V.

It is known that
\mathcal{J}
is a self-adjoint elliptic linear operator acting on
\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)
and it has discrete eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\cdots\to\infty
(Note that the eigenvalue is defined by
\mathcal{J}(V)=-\lambda V
). Then, the
index
of
f
is given by the dimension of the eigenspaces of negative eigenvalues. Also, the
nullity
is defined by the dimension of
0
-eigenspaces. We denote the index (resp. nullity) of
f:\Sigma\to M
by
{\rm index}(\Sigma)
(resp.
{\rm nullity}(\Sigma)
).

An embedded hypersurface
\Sigma
is called
two-sided
if the normal bundle
\nu\Sigma
is trivial, i.e. there exists a smooth unit normal vector field
\nu
along
\Sigma
. Otherwise, we say
\Sigma

one-sided
. If the ambient space
M
is oriented, then
\Sigma
is two-sided if and only if
\Sigma
is oriented. In general, if the normal bundle
\nu\Sigma
of a minimal immersion
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
is trivial, then a normal vector field
V
is written as
V=\varphi\cdot\nu
for some
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
. Thus, the second variation becomes

\displaystyle Q(\varphi,\psi)=-\int_{\Sigma}\langle\mathcal{J}(\varphi),\psi\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma},\quad\varphi,\psi\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma),

\displaystyle{\rm where}\quad

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}(\varphi):=\Delta\varphi+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)\varphi+|A|^{2}\varphi.

If this is the case, the index (resp. nullity) of
f
coincides with the dimension of eigenspaces of negative eigenvalues (resp.
0
-eigenvalue) of
\mathcal{J}
acting on
C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
.

2.2.
Method of index estimate

The following lemma is a generalization of argument used in
[
1
,
9
]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
is a minimal immersion. Let
\mathcal{G}
be a finite dimensional vector space equipped with a positive-definite inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
and suppose that there is a bilinear map

\Phi:\Gamma(T\Sigma)\times\mathcal{G}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)

so that
\Phi
defines a quadratic form
q_{T}
on
\mathcal{G}
for each
T\in\Gamma(T\Sigma)
;

q_{T}(v,w):=Q(\Phi_{T,v},\Phi_{T,w})=-\int_{\Sigma}\langle\mathcal{J}(\Phi_{T,v}),\Phi_{T,w}\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
\Phi_{T,v}=\Phi(T,v)
. We also define another quadratic form
L_{T}
on
\mathcal{G}
by

L_{T}(v,w):=\langle\Phi_{T,v},\Phi_{T,w}\rangle_{L^{2}(\Sigma)}=\int_{\Sigma}\langle\Phi_{T,v},\Phi_{T,w}\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

Then we have

(i)

If there exists a finite dimensional linear subspace
S\subset\Gamma(T\Sigma)
and a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
such that

{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T}<c\cdot{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}L_{T}

for any non-trivial element
T\in S
,
then

\sharp\{\textup{eigenvalues of $\mathcal{J}$ less than $c$}\}\geq\frac{{\rm dim}S}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{G}}.

(ii)

If there exists a finite dimensional linear subspace
S\subset\Gamma(T\Sigma)
such that

{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T}\leq 0

for any non-trivial element
T\in S
,
then

{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{G}}({\rm dim}S-{\rm dim}S_{0}),

where

S_{0}:=\{T\in S\mid\mathcal{J}(\Phi_{T,v})\equiv 0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{G}\}.

Proof.

We denote the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{J}
by
\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{r}\leq\cdots\to\infty
. Fix a constant
c\in\mathbb{R}
and consider the set of eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{l}
satisfying that
\lambda_{k}<c
. Then,
l=\sharp\{\textup{eigenvalues of $\mathcal{J}$ less than $c$}\}
. We take an
L^{2}
-basis
\{V_{k}\}_{k=1}^{l}
of the eigenspaces of
\{\lambda_{k}\}_{k=1}^{l}
, and we fix an orthonormal basis
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\delta}
of
\mathcal{G}
, where
\delta={\rm dim}\mathcal{G}
.

(i) We define a linear map
\chi:S\to\mathbb{R}^{\delta\times l}
by

\chi(T):=\Big[\int_{\Sigma}\langle\Phi_{T,\theta_{i}},V_{k}\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma}\Big]_{i=1,\ldots,\delta,k=1\ldots,l},

where the right hand side is regarded as a (column) vector in
\mathbb{R}^{\delta\times l}
.

Assume that
{\rm dim}S>\delta\times l
. Then there exists a non-trivial element
T_{0}\in S
such that
\chi(T_{0})=0
. Namely,
\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}
is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to
V_{k}
for any
i=1,\ldots,\delta
and
k=1,\ldots,l
, and hence, we obtain

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}},\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}})\geq\lambda_{l+1}\int_{\Sigma}|\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}|^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

Summing up for all
i=1,\ldots,\delta
, we have

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T_{0}}\geq\lambda_{l+1}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}L_{T_{0}}.

Note that we used the assumption that
\langle,\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
is positive-definite, i.e. the trace of a quadratic form
q
is defined by
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q=\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}q(\theta_{i},\theta_{i})
.

However, since we assume
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T_{0}}<c\cdot{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}L_{T_{0}}\leq\lambda_{l+1}{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}L_{T_{0}}
, this is a contradiction. Therefore, we obtain
{\rm dim}S\leq\delta\times l
and this proves (i).

(ii) We slightly modify the proof of (i). Let
S_{0}^{\perp}
be the orthogonal complement of
S_{0}
in
S\subset\Gamma(T\Sigma)
. Take an
L^{2}
-basis
\{V_{k}\}_{k=1}^{l}
of
negative
eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{J}
(namely we set
c=0
in the proof of (i)) and let
\chi:S\to\mathbb{R}^{\delta\times l}
be the same map given in the proof of (i).
We consider the restricted map
\chi^{\prime}=\chi|_{S_{0}^{\perp}}:S_{0}^{\perp}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{\delta\times l}
. Assume that
{\rm dim}S_{0}^{\perp}>\delta\times l
. Then there exists a non-trivial element
T_{0}\in S_{0}^{\perp}
so that
\chi^{\prime}(T_{0})=0
. Then
\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}
is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to any negative eigenvector, and hence, we obtain

(3)

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}},\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}})\geq 0

for any
i
.
In particular, we have
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T_{0}}\geq 0
(as
\langle,\rangle_{\mathcal{G}}
is positive-definite). Since we assume
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T_{0}}\leq 0
, we obtain
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{T_{0}}=0
. By (
3
),
this implies

(4)

\displaystyle Q(\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}},\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}})=-\int_{\Sigma}\langle\mathcal{J}(\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}),\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}\rangle=0

for any
i
. Because
\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}}
is
L^{2}
-orthogonal to any negative eigenvector, (
4
) implies
\mathcal{J}(\Phi_{T_{0},\theta_{i}})=0
for any
i
, i.e.
T_{0}\in S_{0}
. This contradicts the facts that
T_{0}\in S_{0}^{\perp}
and
T_{0}\neq 0
. Therefore,
{\rm dim}S_{0}^{\perp}\leq\delta\times l
. This proves (ii).
∎

As a special situation, we consider the set of harmonic
1
-forms
\mathcal{H}
on
\Sigma
and denote its metric dual by
\mathcal{H}^{*}\subset\Gamma(T\Sigma)
. Note that, by Hodge theory, we have
{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}^{*}=b_{1}(\Sigma)
, where
b_{1}(\Sigma)
is the first Betti number of
\Sigma
. By letting
S=\mathcal{H}^{*}
in the previous lemma, we obtain the following result as an extension of
[
1
, Theorem A]
and
[
9
, Proposition 10]
:

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
be a minimal immersion. Suppose the same assumption given in Lemma
2.2
.

(i)

If
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}<0
(strictly negative) for any non-trivial
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{G}}b_{1}(\Sigma).

(ii)

If
{\rm Tr}_{\mathcal{G}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}\leq 0
(non-positive) for any non-trivial
\omega\in\mathcal{H}
, then it holds that

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)+{\rm nullity}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{G}}b_{1}(\Sigma)\quad\textup{and}

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{{\rm dim}\mathcal{G}}(b_{1}(\Sigma)-{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{0}),

where
\mathcal{H}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{H}
is a linear subspace defined by

\mathcal{H}_{0}:=\{\omega\in\mathcal{H}\mid\mathcal{J}(\Phi_{\omega^{\sharp},v})\equiv 0\ \forall v\in\mathcal{G}\}.

The proof of our main result (Theorem
5.1
) will be done by taking an appropriate map
\Phi:\Gamma(T\Sigma)\times\mathcal{G}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma)
and applying Proposition
2.3
to the map
\Phi
. In order to construct
\Phi
, we consider an isometric immersion
M^{n}
into an
(n+k)
-dimensional Riemannian symmetric space
N^{n+k}
. When
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, the method has been considered by Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
,
2
]
which is inspired by the idea of Ros
[
14
]
, Urbano
[
21
]
and Savo
[
16
]
. In the present paper, we extend their method to the case when
N
is a compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space (see also Appendix for the correspondence of our argument in the case when
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
). We shall give the details of our method in Section
3
and
4
.

2.3.
Preliminaries on symmetric spaces

A Riemannian manifold
(N,g)
is called a
Riemannian symmetric space
(
RSS
for short) if, for each
p\in N
, there is an isometry
s_{p}
on
N
satisfying that (i)
s_{p}^{2}=id_{N}
and (ii)
p
is an isolated fixed point of
s_{p}
. Note that
(N,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold. Moreover, it is known that the identity component of the isometry group
I_{0}(N)
acts on
N
transitively and thus,
N
is diffeomorphic to the homogeneous space
G/K_{p}
, where
G=I_{0}(N)
and
K_{p}
is the isotropy subgroup at some point
p\in N
. In the following, we identify
N
with
G/K_{p}
. We refer to
[
15
]
for basic facts for RSS.

We denote the Lie algebra of
G
(resp.
K_{p}
) by
\mathfrak{g}
(resp.
\mathfrak{k}_{p}
). The geodesic symmetry
s_{p}
at
p
defines an involutive automorphism
\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}
on
G=I_{0}(N)
by
\widetilde{\sigma}_{p}(g):=s_{p}gs_{p}
. Then, the differential
\sigma_{p}:=(d\widetilde{\sigma}_{p})_{e}
at the identity element
e\in G
induces an involutive automorpshim
\sigma_{p}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
, and the eigen-decomposition of
\sigma_{p}
yields a reductive decomposition

\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{p},

where
\mathfrak{k}_{p}
coincides with the
+1
-eigenspace of
\sigma_{p}
and
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
denotes the
-1
-eigenspace of
\sigma_{p}
.
The decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{p}
satisfies the following bracket relations

(5)

\displaystyle[\mathfrak{k}_{p},\mathfrak{k}_{p}]\subset\mathfrak{k}_{p},\quad[\mathfrak{k}_{p},\mathfrak{n}_{p}]\subset\mathfrak{n}_{p}\quad[\mathfrak{n}_{p},\mathfrak{n}_{p}]\subset\mathfrak{k}_{p}.

For an element
X\in\mathfrak{g}
, we define the
fundamental vector field

X^{\dagger}
on
N=G/K_{p}
by

X^{\dagger}(q):=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm exp}(tX)q\Big|_{t=0},\quad q\in N.

Then,
X^{\dagger}
defines a Killing vector field on
N
since
{\rm exp}(tX)\in G=I_{0}(N)
is a 1-parameter subgroup of isometries. It is a general fact for Riemannian homogeneous spaces that the map
X\mapsto X^{\dagger}
is a liner map satisfying that

(6)

\displaystyle[X^{\dagger},Y^{\dagger}]=-[X,Y]^{\dagger}.

Moreover, for each
p\in N
, the linear map

\mathfrak{n}_{p}\to T_{p}N,\quad X\mapsto X^{\dagger}(p)

yields an isomorphism. We denote the converse correspondence by
T_{p}N\ni V\mapsto\widetilde{V}\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
.

The following lemma is a fundamental fact on symmetric space, but will be a key in our argument.

Lemma 2.4
.

Suppose
N
is a Riemannian symmetric space and fix arbitrary point
p\in N
. We denote the Levi-Civita connection of
N
by
\overline{\nabla}
.

(i)

If
X\in\mathfrak{k}_{p}
then
X^{\dagger}(p)=0
, and
\overline{\nabla}_{V}X^{\dagger}(p)
corresponds to
[X,\widetilde{V}]\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
for any
V\in T_{p}N
.

(ii)

If
X\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
then
\overline{\nabla}X^{\dagger}(p)=0
.

Proof.

We give a proof for the sake of convenience of the reader.

(i) If
X\in\mathfrak{k}_{p}
, then we have
{\rm exp}(tX)\in K_{p}
. Thus,
X^{\dagger}(p)=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm exp}(tX)p|_{t=0}=\frac{d}{dt}p|_{t=0}=0
since
K_{p}
is the isotropy subgroup at
p
. Moreover, since
V_{p}=\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}_{p}
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{V}X^{\dagger}(p)=\overline{\nabla}_{\widetilde{V}^{\dagger}}X^{\dagger}(p)=[\widetilde{V}^{\dagger},{X}^{\dagger}](p)=[X,\widetilde{V}]^{\dagger}(p)

where we used
X^{\dagger}(p)=0
and (
6
). Since
[X,\widetilde{V}]\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
by (
5
), this shows that
\overline{\nabla}_{V}X^{\dagger}(p)
corresponds to
[X,\widetilde{V}]
via the canonical identification
T_{p}N\simeq\mathfrak{n}_{p}
.

(ii) By (i) and the bracket relation (
5
), we have

(7)

\displaystyle[Y,Z]^{\dagger}(p)=0\quad\textup{for any $Y,Z\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}$}

since
[Y,Z]\in\mathfrak{k}_{p}
.
On the other hand, since
X^{\dagger}
is a Killing vector field on
N
, we have

\displaystyle X^{\dagger}\langle y,z\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[X^{\dagger},y],z\rangle+\langle y,[X^{\dagger},z]\rangle

for any vector fields
y,z
on
N
. Thus, the Koszul formula and (
6
) show that

\displaystyle 2\langle\overline{\nabla}_{X^{\dagger}}Y^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=X^{\dagger}\langle Y^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}\rangle+Y^{\dagger}\langle X^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}\rangle-Z^{\dagger}\langle X^{\dagger},Y^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle[X^{\dagger},Y^{\dagger}],Z^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle[X^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}],Y^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle[Y^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}],X^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle[X^{\dagger},Y^{\dagger}],Z^{\dagger}\rangle+\langle[X^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}],Y^{\dagger}\rangle+\langle[Y^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}],X^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle[X,Y]^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle[X,Z]^{\dagger},Y^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle[Y,Z]^{\dagger},X^{\dagger}\rangle

for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{g}
. In particular, by (
7
), we obtain
\langle\overline{\nabla}_{X^{\dagger}}Y^{\dagger},Z^{\dagger}\rangle(p)=0
for any
X,Y,Z\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
. Since
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
is isomorphic to
T_{p}N
via the map
X\mapsto X^{\dagger}(p)
, this shows that
\overline{\nabla}Y^{\dagger}(p)=0
. Because
Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
is arbitrary, this proves the second assertion.
∎

A Riemannian symmetric space
N
is said to be
semi-simple
if the identity component of the isometry group
G=I_{0}(N)
is a semi-simple Lie group. In the following, we assume
N=G/K_{p}
is a semi-simple RSS. Note that this assumption is not restrictive. In fact, it is known that any simply-connected RSS is decomposed into a product of Euclidean space and a semi-simple RSS. Moreover, any semi-simple RSS is an Einstein manifold, and if furthermore
G
is compact, then
N
has positive Ricci curvature. We remark that the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is not a semi-simple RSS and somehow a special case. We will discuss the case when
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
in Appendix.

When
G
is semi-simple, then there is a canonical bilinear form
{\bf B}
on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of
G
, called the
Killing form
. It is known that
{\bf B}
is non-degenerate and invariant under any Lie algebra automorphism on
\mathfrak{g}
.
Moreover, if
G
is compact, then
{\bf B}
is negative-definite. Thus, if this is the case, we can define a positive-definite inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}:=-{\bf B}(X,Y).

Actually, this inner product induces the canonical
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on a compact semi-simple symmetric space
N=G/K_{p}
. More precisely, the restriction of
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
to the subspace
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
defines an
{\rm Ad}(K_{p})
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}_{p}}
on
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
. Then,
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}_{p}}
extends to the
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
N=G/K_{p}
by the left
G
-action. In particular,
(\mathfrak{n}_{p},\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{n}_{p}})
and
(T_{p}N,g_{p})
are isometric by the canonical isomorphism
\mathfrak{n}_{p}\simeq T_{p}N
.

We use the following simple fact for the canonical inner product:

Lemma 2.5
.

Suppose
G
is compact and semi-simple. Then, the canonical decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{p}
is an orthogonal decomposition w.r.t.
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
for every
p\in N
.

Proof.

Take arbitrary
X\in\mathfrak{k}_{p}
and
Y\in\mathfrak{n}_{p}
. Since the Killing form is invariant for any automorphism on the Lie algebra, we have

\displaystyle\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=-{\bf B}(X,Y)=-{\bf B}(d\sigma_{p}X,d\sigma_{p}Y)={\bf B}(X,Y)=-\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

and hence,
\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=0
.
∎

Remark 2.6
.

When
G
is non-compact, one can define a positive-definite inner product on
\mathfrak{g}
by

\langle X,Y\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}:=-{\bf B}(X,\sigma_{o}Y)
, where
\sigma_{o}:\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}
is the Cartan involution at a fixed point
o\in N
. Actually,
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
induces the canonical
G
-invariant Riemannian metric on
N
. However, we remark that
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
depends on the fixed point
o\in N
and because of this, the canonical decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{p}
at
p\in N
may not orthogonal w.r.t.
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
, although the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{o}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{o}
at
o
is orthogonal. Lemma
2.5
is crucial in our argument, and our computations given in Section
3
and
4
do not work if we choose
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}^{\prime}
as the inner product on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of a non-compact Lie group
G
.

3.
Trace formula I

Let
(M^{n},g)
be a Riemannian manifold and
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
an isometric immersion of closed manifold
\Sigma^{n-1}
. Throughout this section, we suppose that
f
has trivial normal bundle
(i.e
\Sigma
is
two-sided
if
f
is an embedding) so that there is a smooth unit normal vector field
\nu
along
\Sigma
. In this section, we provide a trace formula for a quadratic form by extending the method considered in
[
1
, Proposition 1]
, and give a simple application.

3.1.
Trace formula

Suppose that
M^{n}
is isometrically immersed into a compact semi-simple symmetric space
N^{n+k}=G/K
via the map
F:M\to N
. Then,
\Sigma
is isometrically immersed into
N
by the map
F\circ f:\Sigma\to N
. We consider the following map:

\Psi:\Gamma(T\Sigma)\times\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma),\quad\Psi(T,X)=\psi_{T,X}\nu,

where

\psi_{T,X}(p):=\langle T,X^{\dagger}\rangle_{F\circ f(p)}.

Also, we consider a quadratic form on
\mathfrak{g}
defined by

r_{T}(X,Y):=Q(\psi_{T,X},\psi_{T,Y}).

We shall compute the trace of
r_{T}
over
\mathfrak{g}
with respect to the inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
defined by the negative Killing form. We put

r_{T,p}(X,Y):=\langle\mathcal{J}(\psi_{T,X}),\psi_{T,Y}\rangle(p)

for
p\in\Sigma

so that

{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{T}=-\int_{\Sigma}{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{T,p}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

In the following, we abbreviate the fixed subscript
T
.

We use the following notations:

•

\overline{\nabla}
,
\nabla^{M}
,
\nabla
: the Levi-Civita connections of
N
,
M
and
\Sigma
, respectively.

•

\overline{R},R^{M},R
, the curvature tensors of
\overline{\nabla}
,
\nabla^{M}
and
\nabla
, respectively. Note that we use the following definition of the curvature tensor (the sign is different from
[
1
]
):

\overline{R}(X,Y)Z:=\overline{\nabla}_{X}\overline{\nabla}_{Y}Z-\overline{\nabla}_{Y}\overline{\nabla}_{X}Z-\overline{\nabla}_{[X,Y]}Z.

•

\overline{\rm Ric}
,
{\rm Ric}^{M}
: the Ricci tensor of
\overline{\nabla}
and
\nabla^{M}
, respectively.

•

A,B
: the second fundamental forms of
\Sigma\to M
and
M\to N
, respectively.

•

For a fixed point
p\in\Sigma
, we take a geodesic normal coordinate around
p
in
\Sigma
, and we denote the coordinate basis by
\{\partial_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n-1}
.

In this notation, we have the following fundamental formulas.

•

(Gauss equation) For any
X,Y,Z,W\in T_{p}\Sigma
, we have

\langle R^{M}(X,Y)Z,W\rangle=\langle R(X,Y)Z,W\rangle+\langle A(X,Z),A(Y,W)\rangle-\langle A(X,W),A(Y,Z)\rangle.

Gauss equation for the isometric immersion
M\to N
is given as well.

•

(Codazzi equation) For any
X,Y,Z\in T_{p}\Sigma
, we have

(R^{M}(X,Y)Z)^{\perp}=(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}A)(Y,Z)-(\nabla_{Y}^{\perp}A)(X,Z),

where
\nabla^{\perp}
is the normal connection defined by
\nabla_{X}^{\perp}\xi:=(\nabla^{M}_{X}\xi)^{\perp}
for a normal vector field
\xi
along
\Sigma
in
M
and
(\nabla^{\perp}_{X}A)(Y,Z)=\nabla_{X}^{\perp}(A(Y,Z))-A(\nabla_{X}Y,Z)-A(Y,\nabla_{X}Z)
.

We first prove the following general formula.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
a minimal immersion of a closed manifold
\Sigma^{n-1}
. For any tangent vector field
T
on
\Sigma
, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2},

where
T^{\flat}=\langle T,\cdot\rangle|_{\Sigma}
and
\Delta_{1}
is the Hodge Laplacian action on
\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
the space of
1
-forms on
\Sigma
.

Proof.

Fix arbitrary point
p\in\Sigma
and we take a geodesic normal coordinate in
\Sigma
.
By the Bochner-Weitzenböck formula and the Gauss equation for minimal immersion
\Sigma\to M
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T,T\rangle(p)

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle R(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}.

This proves the formula.
∎

Next, we compute the trace of
r_{p}
for a fixed point
p\in\Sigma
.

Lemma 3.2
.

(9)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{p}

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)-2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}+|A|^{2}|T|^{2}+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|T|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle.

Proof.

Since the trace is independent of the choice of orthonormal basis, we shall take a preferred orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. We put
d={\rm dim}\mathfrak{g}
.
By Lemma
2.5
, the canonical decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\ \mathfrak{n}_{p}
is orthogonal, and hence, we can take an orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d-(n+k)},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n+k}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
so that
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d-(n+k)}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{k}_{p}
and
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n+k}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
. Since
X^{\dagger}(p)=0
by Lemma
2.4
, we see
r_{p}(X_{i},X_{i})=0
for any
i=1,\ldots,d-(n+k)
. Therefore, we have

{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{p}=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}r_{p}(Y_{i},Y_{i})(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\Big(\Delta\psi_{Y_{i}}\cdot\psi_{Y_{i}}(p)+({\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A|^{2})\psi_{Y_{i}}^{2}(p)\Big).

Since the linear isomorphism
\mathfrak{n}_{p}\simeq T_{p}N
is isometric,
\{Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)\}_{i=1}^{n+k}
becomes an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
. Therefore, we see

\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\psi_{Y_{i}}^{2}(p)=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle^{2}(p)=|T|^{2}.

In the following computations, we take a geodesic normal coordinate of the hypersurface
\Sigma
around
p
.
By Lemma
2.4
, we have
\overline{\nabla}Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)=0
, and hence, we see

\displaystyle\Delta\psi_{Y_{i}}\cdot\psi_{Y_{i}}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)

(10)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)

Because
\{Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)\}_{i=1}^{n+k}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
, the first term in (
10
) is computed by

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T,T\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2},

where we used the relation
\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T=\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T+A(\partial_{\mu},T)+B(\partial_{\mu},T)
. Thus, by using (
8
), the last equation becomes

\displaystyle-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle-2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}.

Next, we consider the second term of (
10
).
Since
Y^{\dagger}
is a Killing vector field on
N
, we have

(\overline{\nabla}^{2}Y^{\dagger})(Z,W)=-\overline{R}(Y^{\dagger},Z)W

for any
Z,W\in T_{p}N
. Combining this with
\overline{\nabla}Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)=0
, we have

(11)

\displaystyle(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}Y_{i}^{\dagger})(p)=(\overline{\nabla}^{2}Y_{i}^{\dagger})(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\mu})(p)=-\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}.

Therefore, the second term of (
10
) becomes

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}-\langle T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle(p)\cdot\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle.

Substituting these to (
10
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta\psi_{Y_{i}}\cdot\psi_{Y_{i}}(p)

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)-2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle.

This implies the desired formula.
∎

If we choose
T=\omega^{\sharp}
the metric dual of a harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
, then we have
\Delta_{1}T^{\flat}=\Delta_{1}\omega=0
and hence, by (
9
), we obtain

(12)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}-|A|^{2}|\omega|^{2}-{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|\omega|^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}-\langle{R}^{M}(\omega^{\sharp},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle+\langle\overline{R}(\omega^{\sharp},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle\Big)\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

If furthermore,
\Sigma
is a surface, we obtain the following which extends the formula given in
[
1

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose
M^{3}
is a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold isometrically immersed into a compact semi-simple symmetric space
N=G/K
. Let
\Sigma
be a closed two-sided minimal surface in
M^{3}
. Then, for any harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
, we have

(13)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}-{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|\omega|^{2}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\Big(2|B(\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}-\langle B(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\mu}),B(\omega^{\sharp},\omega^{\sharp})\rangle\Big)\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

(14)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}|B(e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}-\frac{{\rm scl}(M)}{2}|\omega|^{2}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\langle\overline{R}(\omega^{\sharp},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
{\rm scl}(M)
is the scalar curvature of
M
.

Proof.

We may assume
\{\partial_{1},\partial_{2}\}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}\Sigma
satisfying that
A(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})=\lambda_{i}\delta_{ij}
, where
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
are eigenvalues of
A
. Since
\Sigma
is a minimal surface, we have
\lambda=\lambda_{1}=-\lambda_{2}
, and hence, we see

2\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}|A(\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}-|A|^{2}|\omega|^{2}=0.

Substituting this to (
12
) and by using the Gauss equation for
M\to N
, we obtain (
13
).
Moreover, the Gauss equation for the minimal surface
\Sigma\to M^{3}
shows that (see also
[
1
]
)

\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(\omega^{\sharp},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|\omega|^{2}=\frac{{\rm scl}(M)}{2}|\omega|^{2}.

Substituting this to (
12
), we obtain (
14
).
∎

2.3

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
M^{3}
be a
3
-dimensional Riemannian manifold and
\Sigma
a closed two-sided minimal surface embedded in
M^{3}
. If there is an isometric immersion
F:M^{3}\to N^{3+k}
into a compact semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space
N=G/K
so that
{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}r_{\omega^{\sharp}}<0
for any non-trivial harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
, then we have

{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{d}b_{1}(\Sigma),

where
d={\rm dim}G={\rm dim}{\rm Isom}_{0}(N)
.

3.2.
An application

We give a simple application of the trace formula (
13
). We take the complex projective space
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
as a symmetric space
N
, and consider a real hypersurface
M^{3}
in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
. Note that, as a symmetric space,
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
is isometric to
G/K=SU(3)/S(U(1)\times U(2))
. Without loss of generality, we may assume that the holomorphic sectional curvature of
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
is equal to
4
.

Let
S_{r}^{3}
be the geodesic hypersphere of geodesic radius
r
in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}(4)
.
Note that
r\in(0,\pi/2)
(where
\pi/2
is equal to the injectivity radius of
\mathbb{C}P^{m}(4)
) and
S_{r}
coincides with an orbit of the isotropy subgroup
S(U(1)\times U(2))
. Moreover,
S_{r}
is diffeomorphic to the
3
-dimensional sphere
S^{3}
, and
S_{r}
equipped with the induced metric from
\mathbb{C}P^{2}(4)
is isometric to a
Berger sphere
.

It is known that
S_{r}
is an
\eta
-umbilical
hypersurface in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}(4)
. Namely, for a unit normal vector field
\nu_{S}
of
S_{r}
in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}(4)
, we define a
1
-form
\eta
on
S_{r}
by
\eta(X):=-\langle J\nu_{S},X\rangle
, where
J
is the complex structure on
\mathbb{C}P^{2}
, and then the second fundamental form of
S_{r}
is given by

(15)

\displaystyle B(X,Y)=\{a\langle X,Y\rangle+b\eta(X)\eta(Y)\}\nu_{S},

with
a=\cot r
and
b=-\tan r
. We refer to
[
5
]
for details of the above facts.

By the Gauss equation and (
15
), it turns out that the Ricci tensor of
M=S^{3}_{r}
is given by (see also
[
5
, p.359]
)

(16)

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{M}(X,Y)=(2a^{2}+4)\langle X,Y\rangle-4\eta(X)\eta(Y)

for any
X,Y\in T_{p}S_{r}
. If
X
is a unit tangent vector, then we see

\displaystyle{\rm Ric}^{M}(X,X)=(2a^{2}+4)-4(1-|X_{h}|^{2})=2a^{2}+4|X_{h}|^{2}>0,

where we set
X=X_{h}-\eta(X)J\nu_{S}
. Therefore, any geodesic hypersphere
S_{r}
has positive Ricci curvature. Moreover, if
r\neq r^{\prime}
, then
S_{r}
and
S_{r^{\prime}}
are neither isometric nor homothetic.

Now, let us consider a closed minimal surface
\Sigma
in
S_{r}^{3}
. We remark that Torralbo
[
20
]
proved that there exists an embedded closed minimal surface of arbitrary genus in the Berger sphere. Moreover, any embedded surface in
S_{r}^{3}
is two-sided and any closed minimal surface embedded in
S^{3}_{r}
is unstable since
S_{r}^{3}
is simply-connected and has positive Ricci curvature.
Applying Proposition
3.3
, we obtain the following result:

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
S_{r}^{3}
be the geodesic hypersphere of radius
r\in(0,\pi/2)
in
\mathbb{C}P^{2}(4)
. If the geodesic radius
r
satisfies

0<\tan r<\sqrt{1+\sqrt{3}}=1.652\cdots,

then, it holds that

{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{1}{8}b_{1}(\Sigma)

for any closed minimal surface
\Sigma
embedded in
S_{r}^{3}
.

Proof.

We take
M=S_{r}^{3}
and compute the trace formula (
13
) given in Proposition
3.3
.

For a unit tangent vector
T\in T_{p}\Sigma
, we consider the following quantity:

\displaystyle\beta(T):=-{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\Big(2|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\langle B(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\mu}),B(T,T)\rangle\Big)

By (
13
), if
\beta(T)<0
for any unit vector
T
and
p\in\Sigma
, then we have
{\rm Tr}_{\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}<0
. Thus, the result follows from Proposition
3.4
since
{\rm dim}G={\rm dim}SU(3)=8
.

By using (
15
), we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\Big(2|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\langle B(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\mu}),B(T,T)\rangle\Big)=1-\eta(\nu)^{2}-b^{2}\eta(T)^{2}\eta(\nu)^{2}+(b^{2}-2)\eta(T)^{2}.

where we used the relation
\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}\eta(\partial_{\mu})^{2}=1-\eta(\nu)^{2}
,
ab=-1
and
|T|^{2}=1
. Moreover, by (
16
), we have

{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)=(2a^{2}+4)-4\eta(\nu)^{2}.

Therefore, we see

\beta(T)=-2a^{2}-3(1-\eta(\nu)^{2})-b^{2}\eta(T)^{2}\eta(\nu)^{2}+(b^{2}-2)\eta(T)^{2}.

Since
\eta(T)^{2}=\langle J\nu_{S},T\rangle^{2}\leq|J\nu_{S}|^{2}|T|^{2}\leq 1
for any unit vector
T
, we see

\beta(T)\leq-2a^{2}+b^{2}-2=-2\cot^{2}r+\tan^{2}r-2.

Moreover, the inequality
-2\cot^{2}r+\tan^{2}r-2<0
satisfies if
0<\tan^{2}r<1+\sqrt{3}
. Thus, if this is the case, we have
\beta(T)<0
for any
T\in T_{p}\Sigma
and
p\in\Sigma
, and the desired conclusion holds.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

By the result of
[
16
]
, we have
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq Cb_{1}(\Sigma)
for any closed minimal hypersurface
\Sigma
in the standard sphere
(S^{3},g_{st})
. Moreover, it follows from
[
6
, Theorem A]
that there exists an
\epsilon>0
such that for any smooth metric
g^{\prime}
on
S^{3}
satisfying that
\|g_{st}-g^{\prime}\|_{C^{2,\lambda}}<\epsilon
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
, a similar index estimate holds even in
(S^{3},g^{\prime})
. Since the Berger sphere is actually a deformation of the standard sphere, one may obtain the index estimate in the Berger sphere by using
[
6
, Theorem A]
if the Berger metric is sufficiently close to the standard metric.

4.
Trace formula II

Continued from the previous section, let
N^{n+k}=G/K
be a compact semi-simple RSS and suppose that a Riemannian manifold
M^{n}
is isometrically immersed into
N^{n+k}
via the map
F:M\to N
. Note that we can assume that
M=N
and
F
is the identity map. Let
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
be a minimal immersion, and throughout this section, we suppose that
f
has a trivial normal bundle. In this section, we extend the trace formula
[
1
, Proposition 2]
to compact semi-simple symmetric spaces.

4.1.
Trace formula

We denote the
\mathbb{R}
-linear space of exterior algebra of second degree on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
by
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, namely,
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is a vector space spanned by elements of the form
X\wedge Y
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{g}
. For an element
v\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, we define a smooth function on
\Sigma
as follows: By using the inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
, we define a positive-definite inner product on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
by

\langle X\wedge Y,Z\wedge W\rangle_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}:=\langle X,Z\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}\langle Y,W\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}-\langle X,W\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}\langle Y,Z\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

for elements
X\wedge Y,Z\wedge W\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
and we linearly extend it to whole
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
.

Let
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{d}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
. Then the associated basis of
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is given by
\{\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{j}\}_{1\leq i<j\leq d}
. For each
p\in\Sigma
, we define a linear map
\Pi:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(\wedge^{2}TN)
by

\Pi(\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{j})(p)=\theta_{i}^{\dagger}(p)\wedge\theta_{j}^{\dagger}(p)

and we linearly extend the map to whole
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
. Note that the definition of
\Pi
does not depend on the choice of basis of
\mathfrak{g}
.

Now, we consider a minimal immersion
f:\Sigma^{n-1}\to M^{n}
with trivial normal bundle. We fix a unit normal vector field
\nu
along
f
. Then, we consider a map

\Phi:\Gamma(T\Sigma)\times\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma),\quad\Phi(T,v):=\varphi_{T,v}\cdot\nu,

where
\varphi_{T,v}\in C^{\infty}(\Sigma)
is defined by

(17)

\displaystyle\varphi_{T,v}=\langle\nu\wedge T,\Pi(v)\rangle.

Also, we consider the quadratic form on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
by

q_{T}(v,w):=Q(\varphi_{T,v},\varphi_{T,w})

for
T\in\Gamma(T\Sigma)
.
The aim of this section is to compute the trace of
q_{T}
over
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
.

Remark 4.1
.

If
M=N
(i.e.
F=id_{M}
), it turns out that the test function (
17
) coincides with the one considered in
[
9
]
. See Appendix
A.2
for the detail.

For each
p\in\Sigma
, we define a quadratic form
q_{T,p}
on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
by

q_{T,p}(v,w):=\langle\mathcal{J}(\varphi_{T,v}),\varphi_{T,w}\rangle(p)

so that

(18)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{T}=-\int_{\Sigma}{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{T,p}\,d\mu_{\Sigma}.

In the following, we abbreviate the fixed index
T
, e.g.
q_{\theta_{i}}=q_{T,\theta_{i}}
etc.

Proposition 4.2
.

For any smooth tangent vector filed
T
of
\Sigma
, we have

(19)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{T,p}

\displaystyle=-(\Delta_{1}T^{\flat})(T)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}+|B(\partial_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|T|^{2}\Big)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle{R}^{M}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|T|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle+\langle\overline{R}(\nu,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\nu\rangle|T|^{2}\Big),

where
T^{\flat}:=\langle T,\cdot\rangle|_{\Sigma}
,
\Delta_{1}
is the Hodge Laplacian acting on
\Omega^{1}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
, the decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{p}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{p}
is orthogonal w.r.t.
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
, and hence, we can take an orthonormal basis
\{X_{1},\ldots X_{d-(n+k)},Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n+k}\}
of
\mathfrak{g}
so that
\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{d-(n+k)}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{k}_{p}
and
\{Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{n+k}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
. Then, the orthonormal basis of
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
is given by
X_{l}\wedge X_{m},X_{l}\wedge Y_{i},Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}
for
1\leq l<m\leq d-(n+k)
and
1\leq i<j\leq n+k
.
Since
X_{l}^{\dagger}(p)=0
for any
l=1,\ldots,d-(n+k)
by Lemma
2.4
, we see

\displaystyle\varphi_{X_{l}\wedge X_{m}}(p)=\varphi_{X_{l}\wedge Y_{i}}(p)=0

for any
l,m=1,\ldots,d-(n+k)
and
i=1,\ldots,n+k
. In particular, we have

(20)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}q_{p}(Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j},Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j})

Recall that, under the identification
\mathfrak{n}_{p}\simeq T_{p}N
,
\{Y_{1}^{\dagger}(p),\ldots,Y_{n+k}^{\dagger}(p)\}
becomes an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
. We also take a geodesic normal coordinate of the hypersurface
\Sigma
around
p
, and denote the coordinate basis by
\{\partial_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n-1}
.

By (
20
), we have

(21)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{p}

\displaystyle=\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\Delta\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)+({\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)+|A|^{2})\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}^{2}(p).

Note that we have

(22)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)=\langle Z,\nu\rangle\langle W,T\rangle-\langle Z,T\rangle\langle W,\nu\rangle

for any
Z,W\in T_{p}N
.
Indeed, we see

(23)

\displaystyle\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=\Big(\langle Z,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle Z,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\Big)\Big(\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\Big)

\displaystyle=\langle Z,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle Z,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle Z,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle+\langle Z,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle.

On the other hand, we have

(24)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+k}\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

since
\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}=\langle Z\wedge W,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{j}\wedge Y_{i}}
and
Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{i}^{\dagger}=0
.
Substituting (
23
) to (
24
) and by using the fact that
\{Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)\}_{i}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}N
, we obtain (
22
).

In particular, in the last term of (
21
), we have

(25)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}^{2}=|\nu|^{2}|T|^{2}-\langle\nu,T\rangle^{2}=|T|^{2}

since
|\nu|^{2}=1
and
\langle\nu,T\rangle=0
.

We shall consider the first term of the RHS of (
21
).
Since
\overline{\nabla}Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)=0
for any
i
by Lemma
2.4
, we have
\overline{\nabla}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})(p)=\overline{\nabla}Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}(p)+Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge\overline{\nabla}Y_{j}^{\dagger}(p)=0
. Therefore, we see

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\partial_{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\langle\nu\wedge T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

(26)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(\nu\wedge T),Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle(p)\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})\rangle(p)\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p),

We consider the first term of (
26
). We have

(27)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(\nu\wedge T)=(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu)\wedge T+2\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu\wedge\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T+\nu\wedge(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T),

and hence, by (
22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(\nu\wedge T),Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\nu\rangle|T|^{2}+2\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,T\rangle^{2}+\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T,T\rangle.

where we used the facts
\langle\nu,T\rangle=0
and
|\nu|^{2}=1
along
\Sigma
which also imply
\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,T\rangle=-\langle\nu,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T\rangle
and
\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\nu\rangle=0
.
Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\nu\rangle|T|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}-|\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu|^{2}|T|^{2}\quad(\textup{since $\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\nu\rangle=0$ along $\Sigma$})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}(-|\nabla^{M}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu|^{2}-|B(\partial_{\mu},\nu)|^{2})|T|^{2}=-|A|^{2}|T|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|T|^{2}.

\displaystyle 2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,T\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle=2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\nabla^{M}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,T\rangle^{2}=2\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T,T\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}T|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}.

Summing up these, we obtain

(28)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq m}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(\nu\wedge T),Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-|A|^{2}|T|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}+|B(\partial_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|T|^{2}\Big).

Next, we consider the second term of (
26
).
Since
\overline{\nabla}Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p)=0
, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})(p)=\{(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}Y_{i}^{\dagger})\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}+Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge(\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}Y_{j}^{\dagger})\}(p).

Moreover, by (
11
),
we obtain

(29)

\displaystyle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})(p)=-(\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu})\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}(p)+(\overline{R}(Y_{j}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu})\wedge Y_{i}^{\dagger}(p).

Since
\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}=-\varphi_{Y_{j}\wedge Y_{i}}
, the second term of (
26
) becomes

(30)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\sum_{i=1}^{n+k}\sum_{j=1}^{n+k}-\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p).

Here, we have

\displaystyle\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\nu,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle

and hence,

\displaystyle-\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}

\displaystyle=-\langle\nu,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle^{2}+\langle\nu,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\langle T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle\langle T,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle T,\overline{R}(Y_{i}^{\dagger},\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu}\rangle\langle\nu,Y_{j}^{\dagger}\rangle^{2}\langle T,Y_{i}^{\dagger}\rangle.

Summing up for

, (

) shows that

(31)

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq n+k}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle\nu\wedge T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(Y_{i}^{\dagger}\wedge Y_{j}^{\dagger})\rangle\cdot\varphi_{Y_{i}\wedge Y_{j}}(p)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(-\langle\overline{R}(\nu,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\nu\rangle|T|^{2}-\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle\Big).

since
\langle T,\nu\rangle=0
and
|\nu|^{2}=1
.

By (
21
), (
25
), (
26
), (
28
) and (

), we obtain

(32)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{p}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Delta|T|^{2}-|\nabla T|^{2}+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}+|B(\partial_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|T|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(\langle\overline{R}(T,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},T\rangle+\langle\overline{R}(\nu,\partial_{\mu})\partial_{\mu},\nu\rangle|T|^{2}\Big)+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|T|^{2}.

Finally, substituting the identity (
8
) to (
32
), the term
\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|A(\partial_{\mu},T)|^{2}
in (

) is cancelled out, and we obtain the desired formula (
19
).
∎

Theorem 4.3
.

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}+|B(e_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|\omega|^{2}\Big)-\Big(\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle R^{M}(\omega^{\sharp},e_{\mu})e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|\omega|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(\langle\overline{R}(\omega^{\sharp},e_{\mu})e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle+\langle\overline{R}(\nu,e_{\mu})e_{\mu},\nu\rangle|\omega|^{2}\Big)\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

Proposition 4.4

Remark 4.7

(Comparison with the trace formula in
\mathbb{R}^{m}
)
.

Under the same assumption given in Theorem
4.3
, we further assume that the semi-simple compact RSS
N
is isometrically immersed into the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{m}
via the map
G:N\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
. Namely, we suppose that we have the following isometric immersions:

\Sigma^{n-1}\xrightarrow{f}M^{n}\xrightarrow{F}N^{n+k}\xrightarrow{G}\mathbb{R}^{m}.

Note that, for a symmetric space
N
, there are some well-studied isometric immersions from
N
into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
(e.g. see
[
1
,
6
]
).
We denote the second fundamental form of three isometric immersions
F:M\to N
,
G\circ F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
and
G:N\to\mathbb{R}^{m}
by
B
,
B^{\prime}
and
B_{N}
, respectively.

Since
M^{n}
is isometrically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{m}
by the map
G\circ F
, we can apply the tracing method used in
[
1
]
to the map
G\circ F
, and then, we have the following trace formula (see (
53
) in Appendix):

(35)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}2\Big(|B^{\prime}(\omega^{\sharp},e_{\mu})|^{2}+|B^{\prime}(\nu,e_{\mu})|^{2}|\omega|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(\langle B^{\prime}(\omega^{\sharp},\omega^{\sharp}),B^{\prime}(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})\rangle+\langle B^{\prime}(\nu,\nu),B^{\prime}(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})\rangle|\omega|^{2}\Big)\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

where
\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}
is a quadratic form on
\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}
considered in
[
1
]
(see Appendix for details).
Notice that the right hand side is nothing but the replacing
B
with
B^{\prime}
in (
34
).
Since
B^{\prime}(X,Y)=B(X,Y)+B_{N}(X,Y)
for any
X,Y\in T_{p}M
, and
B(X,Y)
is orthogonal to
B_{N}(X,Y)
, the formulas (
34
) and (
35
) show the following relation:

(36)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle={\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}+\int_{\Sigma}\beta_{N}\Big(\frac{\omega^{\sharp}}{|\omega|},\nu\Big)\cdot|\omega|^{2}\,d\mu_{\Sigma},

where we put

\displaystyle\beta_{N}(X,\nu)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}2\Big(|B_{N}(X,e_{\mu})|^{2}+|B_{N}(\nu,e_{\mu})|^{2}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(\langle B_{N}(X,X),B_{N}(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})\rangle+\langle B_{N}(\nu,\nu),B_{N}(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})\rangle\Big)

for a unit tangent vector
X\in T_{p}\Sigma
, and
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n-1}
is an orthonormal basis of
T_{p}\Sigma
.

We remark that, by the relation (
36
), if
\beta_{N}(X,\nu)<0
for any unit vector
X\in T_{p}\Sigma
and
p\in\Sigma
, then it holds that
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}<{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}
for any non-trivial harmonic
1
-form
\omega
. Therefore, in this case, it is more reasonable to use
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}
rather than
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}
to show that the trace is negative (or non-positive) in order to apply Proposition
2.3
. For example, if

G:S^{n+k}(r)\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k+1}
is the canonical isometric embedding of the sphere of radius
r
, then it is easy to see that
\beta_{N}(X,\nu)=-\frac{2}{r^{2}}(n-2)
and
\beta_{N}(X,\nu)<0
if
n\geq 3
. This means that if
M^{n}
is isometrically immersed into
N=S^{n+k}
, it seems better to consider
G\circ F:M\to\mathbb{R}^{n+k+1}
and apply the method used in
[
1
]
instead of using our trace formula (
33
) for

.

However, we may not expect such a situation for a general symmetric space

. Namely, there is a possibility that the opposite inequality holds;
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}>{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}
. For example, by Proposition
4.5
, when
M=N
and

is the identity map, we always have
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}=0
even if
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathbb{R}^{m}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}>0
.

\displaystyle\gamma(T)

By using (

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}|B(T,e_{\mu})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(a\langle T,e_{\mu}\rangle+b\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(T)\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(a^{2}\langle T,e_{\mu}\rangle^{2}+2ab\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\langle T,e_{\mu}\rangle\eta_{i}(T)\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})+b^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(T)^{2}\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+b^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\eta_{i}(T)\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})\eta_{j}(T)\eta_{j}(e_{\mu})\Big)

\displaystyle=a^{2}-2\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(T)^{2}+b^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(T)^{2}(1-\eta_{i}(\nu)^{2})-b^{2}\sum_{i\neq j}\eta_{i}(T)\eta_{j}(T)\eta_{i}(\nu)\eta_{j}(\nu)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(b\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(\nu)\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})\Big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}b^{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(\nu)^{2}\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})^{2}+\sum_{i\neq j}\eta_{i}(\nu)\eta_{j}(\nu)\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})\eta_{j}(e_{\mu})\Big)

\displaystyle=b^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(\nu)^{2}-b^{2}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(\nu)^{2}\Big)^{2}.

where we used the relations
\sum_{\mu}\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})^{2}=1-\eta_{i}(\nu)^{2}

i\neq j
.

\displaystyle\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle B(T,T),B(e_{\mu},e_{\mu})\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big\{a|T|^{2}+b\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(T)^{2}\Big\}\Big\{a|e_{\mu}|^{2}+b\sum_{i=1}^{\delta}\eta_{i}(e_{\mu})^{2}\Big\}

\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{O}

\delta=7
and
n=15
. Then,

\displaystyle\{(3-\delta)b^{2}+n-3\}Q-b^{2}Q^{2}

\displaystyle=(-4b^{2}+12)Q-b^{2}Q^{2}.

We may assume that
(-4b^{2}+12)/2b^{2}\geq 1
or equivalently
b^{2}\leq 2
so that
(-4b^{2}+12)Q-b^{2}Q^{2}\leq(-4b^{2}+12)-b^{2}=-5b^{2}+12
. Otherwise, it turns out that
\gamma(T)
cannot be bounded from above by any negative constant. When
b^{2}=\tan^{2}r\leq 2
, then

\displaystyle\gamma(T)

\displaystyle\leq(-2\cdot 15+4)a^{2}+(15-5)+(-5b^{2}+12)+2\cdot 7

\displaystyle=-26a^{2}-5b^{2}+36.

Thus it follows that if

\tan^{2}r<\frac{1}{5}(18-\sqrt{194})=0.8143\cdots

then
\gamma(T)<0
.

Summarizing the argument, we have proved that if

\tan^{2}r<\begin{cases}\sqrt{n^{2}-4n+5}-(n-3)&\textup{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}$ and $n\geq 5$}\\
n-1-\sqrt{n^{2}-4n-5}&\textup{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{H}$}\\
\frac{1}{5}(18-\sqrt{194})&\textup{if $\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{O}$}\end{cases},

then we have
\gamma(T)<0
for any unit tangent vector
T\in T_{p}\Sigma
and we obtain the required conclusion.
∎

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\partial_{\xi}\rangle-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\partial_{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\mu}\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi}),\nu\rangle-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\partial_{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(A(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi})),\nu\rangle-\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi}),\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu\rangle-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\partial_{\xi}\rangle.

Since
A(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi})
is parallel to
\nu
and
\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\nu\rangle=0

\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi}),\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu\rangle=0

\displaystyle\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu,\partial_{\xi}\rangle(p)

\displaystyle 0

Lemma 5.7

Let

Proof.

Recall that, if
\tau
is a closed
1

\langle\nabla_{X}T,Y\rangle=\langle\nabla_{Y}T,X\rangle

for any

\displaystyle\langle[S_{\nu},\nabla T]\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\nu}(\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T),\partial_{\xi}\rangle-\langle\nabla_{S_{\nu}(\partial_{\mu})}T,\partial_{\xi}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle A(\partial_{\xi},\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T),\nu\rangle-\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T,S_{\nu}(\partial_{\mu})\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\xi}}\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T,\nu\rangle+\langle\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T,\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\xi}}\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T,\nu\rangle-\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T,\nu\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{R}(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\mu})T,\nu\rangle-\Big\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\xi}}(A(\partial_{\mu},T))-\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(A(\partial_{\xi},T)),\nu\Big\rangle.

Moreover, at the point

\displaystyle\Big\langle\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\xi}}(A(\partial_{\mu},T))-\overline{\nabla}_{\partial_{\mu}}(A(\partial_{\xi},T)),\nu\Big\rangle(p)

\displaystyle=\Big\langle(\nabla^{\perp}_{\partial_{\xi}}A)(\partial_{\mu},T)-(\nabla^{\perp}_{\partial_{\mu}}A)(\partial_{\xi},T),\nu\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\Big\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T)-A(\partial_{\xi},\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T),\nu\Big\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\overline{R}(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\mu})T,\nu\rangle+\Big\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T)-A(\partial_{\xi},\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T),\nu\Big\rangle.

Substituting this to the previous equation, we obtain

\langle[S_{\nu},\nabla T]\partial_{\mu},\partial_{\xi}\rangle=-\Big\langle A(\partial_{\mu},\nabla_{\partial_{\xi}}T)-A(\partial_{\xi},\nabla_{\partial_{\mu}}T),\nu\Big\rangle

for any
\mu,\xi=1,\ldots,n-1
.
This implies the conclusion.

Proof of Proposition

B_{\rho}(p)\subset\Sigma
around some point

which simultaneously diagonalizes

\nabla{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle\nabla\omega^{\sharp}(E_{\mu},E_{\xi})=0\quad\text{for}\quad\mu\neq\xi.

Note that the proof of
[

essentially use the relation (
50

where all the principal curvatures are distinct. Thanks to Lemma

For a harmonic 1-form

\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\nabla\omega^{\sharp}(E_{\mu},E_{\mu})=0.

Combining this fact and (

\nabla\omega^{\sharp}
on

is completely determined by the functions

\nabla\omega^{\sharp}(E_{1},E_{1}),\dots,\nabla\omega^{\sharp}(E_{n-2},E_{n-2}).

Now, we consider
2n-3

B_{\rho}(p)
by

\displaystyle\phi_{\omega,i}(q):=\begin{cases}\omega(E_{i})(q)&\quad\text{for}\quad 1\leq i\leq n-1\\
\nabla\omega^{\sharp}(E_{i-n+1},E_{i-n+1})(q)&\quad\text{for}\quad n\leq i\leq 2n-3.\end{cases}

Given any

\gamma(t):[0,\tau]\to\Sigma^{n-1}
be the unique geodesic connecting
p
to
q
. Restricting the functions
\phi_{\omega,1},\dots,\phi_{\omega,2n-3}
to the geodesic

f_{i}(t):=\phi_{\omega,i}(\gamma(t)),\quad 1\leq i\leq 2n-3.

Owing to the local smoothness of
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{n-1}
, the same computation given in the proof of
[
2
, Proposition 5]
holds, and moreover, we see that
\{f_{i}\}
solves a linear ODE system of normal form. In particular, the value along
\gamma
is determined by the initial value

(f_{1}(0),\dots,f_{2n-3}(0))=(\phi_{\omega,1}(p),\dots,\phi_{\omega,2n-3}(p))
, namely,
the values
(\phi_{\omega,1}(q),\dots,\phi_{\omega,2n-3}(q))

(q\in B_{\rho}(p))
are uniquely determined by
(\phi_{\omega,1}(p),\dots,\phi_{\omega,2n-3}(p))
.

Let
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
be two harmonic forms belonging to the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{1}\subset\mathcal{H}
.
If
\phi_{\omega,i}(p)=\phi_{\omega^{\prime},i}(p)
for any
i=1,\ldots,2n-3
, then the above argument shows that
\omega(q)=\omega^{\prime}(q)
for any
q\in B_{\rho}(p)
, and by using the unique continuation theorem for harmonic forms
[
3
]
, we obtain
\omega=\omega^{\prime}
on
\Sigma
. Therefore an element
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{1}
is determined by
(\phi_{\omega,1}(p),\dots,\phi_{\omega,2n-3}(p))
, and this implies that the dimension of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
is at most
2n-3
as required.
∎

Finally, we give a proof of our main result (Theorem
5.1
).

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

We first assume that
\Sigma^{n-1}
is two-sided. By proposition
4.5
, we have
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}=0
for any harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
. Since
\langle,\rangle_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}
is positive-definite on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, Proposition
2.3
-(ii) shows an affine bound

{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq C(b_{1}(\Sigma)-{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{0})
,
where we put
C=2/(d(d-1))
,
d={\rm dim}\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, by Proposition
5.5
, we have
{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{0}\leq{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{1}\leq 2n-3
since
\mathcal{H}_{0}
is a linear subspace of
\mathcal{H}_{1}
. Combining this, we obtain

(51)

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq C(b_{1}(\Sigma)-D),

where we set
D=2n-3
.
If a real number
a
satisfies
a\geq 1
, then we have
a\geq(a+c)/(1+c)
for any
c\geq 0
. Therefore, if
\Sigma
is unstable (i.e.
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq 1
), then (
51
) implies

(52)

\displaystyle{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{{\rm index}(\Sigma)+CD}{1+CD}\geq\frac{Cb_{1}(\Sigma)}{1+CD}=\frac{2}{d(d-1)+2(2n-3)}b_{1}(\Sigma).

This proves the required inequality.

Next, we consider the case when
\Sigma
is one-sided. We set
\widetilde{\Sigma}:=\{(p,\nu_{p})\mid p\in\Sigma,\textup{$\nu_{p}\in\nu_{p}\Sigma,|\nu_{p}|=1$}\}
. We endow
\widetilde{\Sigma}
a manifold structure so that a natural projection
\pi:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
becomes a two-fold covering map. Then we obtain an immersion
\widetilde{f}:=f\circ\pi:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to M
, where
f:\Sigma\to M
is the isometric immersion of
\Sigma
. We induce a Riemannian metric on
\widetilde{\Sigma}
via the map
\widetilde{f}
so that
\widetilde{f}:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to M
becomes an isometric immersion.

We put
\widetilde{\nu}_{(p,\nu_{p})}:=\nu_{p}
. Then,
\widetilde{\nu}
defines a smooth normal vector field along
\widetilde{f}
, and in particular, the normal bundle
\nu\widetilde{\Sigma}
of
\widetilde{f}
is trivial.
Note that, for the non-trivial deck transformation
\tau:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\widetilde{\Sigma}
, we have
\widetilde{\nu}\circ\tau=-\widetilde{\nu}
under natural identifications
(\widetilde{f}^{*}TM)_{\tau(\widetilde{p})}\simeq(\widetilde{f}^{*}TM)_{\widetilde{p}}\simeq({f}^{*}TM)_{p}
for
\widetilde{p}\in\widetilde{\Sigma}
and
p=\pi(\widetilde{p})\in\Sigma
.

For any
V\in\Gamma(f^{*}TM)
, we define a natural lift
\widetilde{V}\in\Gamma(\widetilde{f}^{*}TM)
by
\widetilde{V}(\widetilde{p}):=V(\pi(\widetilde{p}))=V(p)
. If
V
is a normal vector field, we can associate a function
\varphi_{V}\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Sigma})
satisfying that
\widetilde{V}=\varphi_{V}\cdot\widetilde{\nu}
. Then, since
\widetilde{\nu}\circ\tau=-\widetilde{\nu}
and
\widetilde{V}\circ\tau=\widetilde{V}
, it holds that
\varphi_{V}\circ\tau=-\varphi_{V}
, i.e.
\varphi_{V}
is an odd function with respect to the deck transformation. Conversely, for any odd function
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\widetilde{\Sigma})
, we obtain a well-defined smooth normal vector field
V
along
f
by
V(p)=\varphi(\widetilde{p})\cdot\widetilde{\nu}(\widetilde{p})
, where
\widetilde{p}\in\pi^{-1}(p)
.

Let
T
be any tangent vector field along
f
and
\widetilde{T}
its natural lift. Since
\widetilde{T}
is invariant under the deck transformation, the function
\varphi_{\widetilde{T},w}=\langle\widetilde{\nu}\wedge\widetilde{T},\Pi(w)\rangle
is an odd function for any
w\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
, and it yields a normal vector field
V_{\widetilde{T},w}
along
f
. Thus, we obtain a map

\Phi:\Gamma(T\Sigma)\times\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma),\quad\Phi(T,w)=V_{\widetilde{T},w}

and a quadratic form

q_{T}(v,w):=Q(V_{\widetilde{T},v},V_{\widetilde{T},w}).

Note that, since
\pi:\widetilde{\Sigma}\to\Sigma
is a two-fold covering (and
\pi
is a Riemannian submersion), we have
Q_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(\widetilde{V},\widetilde{V})=2Q(V,V)
, where
Q_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}
is the index form of
\widetilde{\Sigma}
.

Now, we take a harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
. Then,
\widetilde{\omega}=\pi^{*}\omega
is a harmonic
1
-form on
\widetilde{\Sigma}
. Moreover,
\widetilde{\omega}^{\sharp}
coincides with the natural lift of
\omega^{\sharp}
. Therefore, by Proposition
4.5
, we obtain
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\omega^{\sharp}}=\frac{1}{2}\cdot{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}q_{\widetilde{\omega}^{\sharp}}=0
for any harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
. Thus, by Proposition
2.3
, we obtain an affine bound
{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq C(b_{1}(\Sigma)-{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{0})
, where
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{H}\mid\mathcal{J}_{\Sigma}(\Phi_{\omega^{\sharp},v})=0\ \forall v\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\}
. Since
\mathcal{J}_{\Sigma}(\Phi_{\omega^{\sharp},v})=\mathcal{J}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(\varphi_{\widetilde{\omega}^{\sharp},v})
, we see
\widetilde{\omega}\in\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}:=\{\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}\mid\mathcal{J}_{\widetilde{\Sigma}}(\varphi_{\omega^{\sharp},v})=0\,\forall v\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\}
if
\omega\in\mathcal{H}_{0}
, namely, the pull-back induces a linear map
\pi^{*}:\mathcal{H}_{0}\to\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}
. Since
{\rm Ker}\pi^{*}=\{0\}
, we obtain
{\rm dim}\mathcal{H}_{0}\leq{\rm dim}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{0}\leq{\rm dim}\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{1}\leq 2n-3
by Proposition
5.5
, and hence, we obtain an affine bound (
51
). Therefore if
\Sigma
is unstable, we obtain (
52
) as well as the case of two-sided hypersurface. This completes the proof.
∎

Appendix A
Relation to previous methods

A.1.
The case of
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}

In Theorem
4.3
, we derive a trace formula for a Riemannian manifold
M
isometrically immersed into a semi-simple Riemannian symmetric space
N
.
A corresponding trace formula in the case when
N=\mathbb{R}^{m}
has been derived by Ambrozio-Carlotto-Sharp
[
1
, Proposition 2]
. In this appendix, we remark that our argument given in Section
4
can be adapted to the case when
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, and their formula is recovered by a slight modification.

The identity component of the isometry group of
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is given by a semi-direct product
G=SO(n+k)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Notice that
G
is not a semi-simple Lie group (and hence, we cannot use the results given in the previous subsection directly). More precisely,
G
is expressed by

G=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}A&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\mid A\in SO(n+k),\ b\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\Big\}\subset GL(n+k+1,\mathbb{R}),

and

G
acts on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
by

\begin{pmatrix}A&b\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}x\\
1\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Ax+b\\
1\end{pmatrix},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}.

The Lie algebra of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

\mathfrak{g}=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}X&y\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\mid X\in\mathfrak{o}(n+k),\ y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\Big\}\subset\mathfrak{gl}(n+k+1,\mathbb{R}).

Fix arbitrary
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Then the geodesic symmetry
s_{x}
at
x
is given by
s_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}-I_{n+k}&2x\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\in G
. Thus the differential at
e\in G
of the involution
\sigma_{x}
is expressed by

d\sigma_{x}(\begin{pmatrix}X&y\\
0&0\end{pmatrix})=s_{x}\begin{pmatrix}X&y\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}s_{x}=\begin{pmatrix}X&-y-X(2x)\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

Therefore, the eigendecomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}_{x}\oplus\mathfrak{n}_{x}
is given by

\mathfrak{k}_{x}=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}X&-Xx\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\mid X\in\mathfrak{o}(n+k)\Big\},\quad\mathfrak{n}_{x}=\Big\{\begin{pmatrix}O&y\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\mid y\in\mathbb{R}^{n+k}\Big\}.

Notice that
\mathfrak{n}_{x}
does not depend on the choice of
x\in N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and thus, we denote
\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n}_{x}
.
We define the standard inner product
\langle,\rangle
on
\mathfrak{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, and extend it to
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
.

Suppose
M
is isometrically immersed into
N=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and let
\Sigma
be a closed two-sided minimal hypersurface in
M
. Similar to Section
4
, we fix a tangent vector field
T\in\Gamma(T\Sigma)
, and for each
v\in\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
we consider a smooth function

\widehat{\varphi}_{T,v}:=\langle\nu\wedge T,\Pi(v)\rangle,

where
\nu
is a unit normal vector field of
\Sigma
in
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and
\Pi(X\wedge Y)=X^{\dagger}\wedge Y^{\dagger}
for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{n}
, and we linearly extend the map to whole
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
.
It should be noted that, for
Y=\begin{pmatrix}O&y\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\in\mathfrak{n}
, the Killing vector field generated by
Y
is given by
Y^{\dagger}=y
, i.e.
Y^{\dagger}
is regarded as a parallel vector field on
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Therefore, for an orthonormal basis
\{\theta_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n+k}
of
\mathfrak{n}\simeq\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
, we may write

\widehat{\varphi}_{T,\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{j}}=\langle\nu\wedge T,\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{j}\rangle.

This recovers the test function considered in
[
1
, Subsection 3.2]
.

Now, we define a quadratic form
\widehat{q}_{T}
on
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
by

\displaystyle\widehat{q}_{T}(v,w):=Q(\widehat{\varphi}_{T,v},\widehat{\varphi}_{T,w})

and we consider the trace of
\widehat{q}_{T}
over
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
.
We remark that we take the trace over
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}
(not over
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
. Compare with the semi-simple case (
18
)). When
N=G/K_{p}
is a semi-simple RSS,
\mathfrak{n}_{p}
depends on
p
in general, and hence,
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}_{p}}q_{T}
is as well. This is a reason why we take the trace over
\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}
when
N
is semi-simple.

If
\Sigma
is a (two-sided) closed minimal hypersurface in
M^{n}
, the computation given in Proposition
4.2
is worked without any modification, and if
T
is the dual vector of a harmonic 1-form
\omega
, then we obtain

(53)

\displaystyle{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Sigma}\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\Big(|B(e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp})|^{2}+|B(e_{\mu},\nu)|^{2}|\omega|^{2}\Big)-\Big(\sum_{\mu=1}^{n-1}\langle R^{M}(\omega^{\sharp},e_{\mu})e_{\mu},\omega^{\sharp}\rangle+{\rm Ric}^{M}(\nu,\nu)|\omega|^{2}\Big)

as
\overline{R}=0
in (
33
). This recovers the trace formula given in
[
1
, Proposition 2]
. Note that the another trace formula given in
[
1
, Proposition 1]
is also recovered by a similar way. Moreover, by using Proposition
2.3
-(i), we obtain the following.

Theorem A.1

(
[
1
]
)
.

Suppose
M^{n}
is isometrically immersed into
\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
and let
\Sigma
be a closed minimal hypersurface in
M
. If
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}<0
for any non-trivial harmonic 1-form
\omega
on
\Sigma
, then we have

{\rm index}(\Sigma)\geq\frac{2}{(n+k)(n+k-1)}b_{1}(\Sigma).

See
[
1
,
6
]
for several examples of
M
satisfying that
{\rm Tr}_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{n}}\widehat{q}_{\omega^{\sharp}}<0
for any harmonic
1
-form
\omega
on any closed minimal hypersurface in
M
.

A.2.
The method of Mendes-Radeschi

In
[
9
]
, Mendes-Radeschi generalizes the previous method by using the notion of virtual immersion. According to
[
9
]
, we briefly summarize their method, and as a consequence, we show that they use the same test function given in the present paper.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold, and
V
be a finite-dimensional real vector space equipped with an inner product
\langle,\rangle_{V}
. The
virtual immersion
of
M
is a
V
-valued
1
-form
\Omega:TM\to V
satisfying the following two-conditions:

(i)

\langle\Omega_{p}(X),\Omega_{p}(Y)\rangle_{V}=g_{p}(X,Y)
for any
p\in M
and
X,Y\in T_{p}M
.

(ii)

\langle(d\Omega)_{p}(X,Y),\Omega_{p}(Z)\rangle_{V}=0
for any
p\in M
and
X,Y,Z\in T_{p}M
.

If
M
admits a virtual immersion
\Omega
, we obtain an immersion of the tangent bundle
TM
to the trivial bundle
M\times V
by the map
(p,X)\mapsto(p,\Omega_{p}(X))
where
X\in T_{p}M
. This immersion is isometric in the sense of (i), and moreover the above two conditions imply that the natural flat connection
D
on
M\times V
induces the Levi-Civita connection
\nabla^{M}
on
TM\subset M\times V
(See
[
9
]
for details). This notion generalizes the situation of isometric immersion of
M
into the Euclidean space
V=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
. Namely, if
F:M\to V=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is an isometric immersion, then
F^{*}TV
is a trivial bundle over
M
and we have a natural immersion
TM\to F^{*}TV\simeq M\times V
, and in this case, the virtual immersion is given by
dF:TM\to V=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
.
For a virtual immersion
\Omega:TM\to V
, we can define the second fundamental form
\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
of
\Omega
by using the flat connection
D
similar to the usual second fundamental form. Moreover, the formulas of Weingarten, Gauss, Ricci and Codazzi (relative to
\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
and
D
) also hold for the virtual immersion
\Omega
analogous to the isometric immersion into the Euclidean space
[
9
, Proposition 7]
. However, the second fundamental form
\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
is not symmetric in general.

Let
\Sigma
be a two-sided closed minimal hypersurface in
M
, and we suppose
M
admits a virtual immersion
\Omega:TM\to V
. Then, Mendes-Radeschi considered the following test variation along
\Sigma
(See
[
9
, eq.(6)]
. Note that we change the sign);

(54)

\displaystyle\Phi:\mathcal{H}\times\wedge^{2}V\to\Gamma(\nu\Sigma),\quad\Phi_{\omega,\alpha}:=\langle\Omega(\nu)\wedge\Omega(\omega^{\sharp}),\alpha\rangle_{\wedge^{2}V}\cdot\nu,

where
\nu
is a unit normal vector field along
\Sigma
,
\omega
is a harmonic
1
-form on
\Sigma
and
\alpha\in\wedge^{2}V
. Note that if
F:M\to V=\mathbb{R}^{n+k}
is an isometric immersion and
\Omega=dF
, then the test variation coincides with the one considered in
[
1
]
(see also Appendix
A.1
). Then, by applying the tracing method for
\Phi
, they derived a trace formula in terms of the second fundamental form
\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
of
\Omega_{0}
and the curvature tensor of
M
(
[
9
, eq.(5) and (7)]
).

Now, let us assume that
M
is a compact Riemannian symmetric space.
In
[
9
]
, Mendes-Radeschi showed that any compact RSS admits a natural virtual immersion described as follows. Let
M=G/K
be a compact RSS, and denote the associated canonical decomposition by
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
. We define an
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant inner product
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathfrak{g}
. Since the isotropy subgroup
K
acts on
\mathfrak{m}
via the restriction of adjoint representation, we have a homogeneous fiber bundle
G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}
on
M=G/K
associated to the principal bundle
G\to G/K
. More precisely,
G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}:=G\times\mathfrak{m}/\sim
, where the equivalent relation is defined by the
K
-action
k\cdot(g,X):=(gk^{-1},{\rm Ad}(k)X)
.
G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}
is identified with the tangent bundle
TM
by the map

G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}\to TM,\quad[g,X]\mapsto dg_{o}(X),

where
o=[e]\in G/K
is the origin of
G/K
. Under this identification, we define a
\mathfrak{g}
-valued
1
-form on
M
by

\Omega_{0}:G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}\to\mathfrak{g},\quad[g,X]\mapsto{\rm Ad}(g)X.

Then, it turns out that
\Omega_{0}
is a virtual immersion of
M
(
[
9
, Lemma 11]
). Moreover, the second fundamental form
\rm{I\hskip-0.09995ptI}
of
\Omega_{0}
is skew-symmetric, and this property actually characterizes the symmetric space (
[
9
, Theorem B]
).

Our claim here is the following.

Lemma A.2
.

Suppose
M
is semi-simple, and
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
is defined by the negative-Killing form.
By taking the specific virtual immersion
\Omega_{0}
, the test function given in (
54
) is written as

\langle\Omega_{0}(\nu)\wedge\Omega_{0}(\omega^{\sharp}),\theta\wedge\xi\rangle_{\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}}=\langle\nu\wedge\omega^{\sharp},\theta^{\dagger}\wedge\xi^{\dagger}\rangle

for any
\theta,\xi\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Proof.

As mentioned in
[
9
, Remark 13]
, under the identification
TM\simeq G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}
, the Killing vector field
\theta^{\dagger}_{[g]}
corresponds to
[g,({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{m}}]
for any
g\in G
and
\theta\in\mathfrak{g}
, where
(\cdot)_{\mathfrak{m}}
means the orthogonal projection to
\mathfrak{m}
. Indeed, we see

\displaystyle dg^{-1}_{[g]}(\theta^{\dagger})=\frac{d}{dt}g^{-1}{\rm exp}(t\theta)g\cdot o\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{d}{dt}{\rm exp}(t{\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)\cdot o\Big|_{t=0}=({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{m}},

where we used the identification
\mathfrak{m}\simeq T_{o}M
. This shows
\theta^{\dagger}_{[g]}=[g,({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{m}}]
under the identification
TM\simeq G\times_{K}\mathfrak{m}
. Thus, by definition of
\Omega_{0}
, we have

(55)

\displaystyle\Omega_{0}(\theta^{\dagger}_{[g]})={\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{m}}=\theta-{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}.

We take arbitrary vector field
Z\in\Gamma(TM)
. Then, by using (
55
), we have

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),\theta\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),\Omega_{0}(\theta^{\dagger})\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}-\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\langle Z,\theta^{\dagger}\rangle-\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}},

where we used the condition (i) in the definition of virtual immersion.
For each point
[g]\in M
, there exists a vector
X_{Z}\in\mathfrak{g}
satisfying that
Z_{[g]}=(X_{Z}^{\dagger})_{[g]}
. Then, by (
55
), we see

\displaystyle\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\langle{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g)^{-1}X_{Z})_{\mathfrak{m}},{\rm Ad}(g)({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=\langle({\rm Ad}(g)^{-1}X_{Z})_{\mathfrak{m}},({\rm Ad}(g^{-1})\theta)_{\mathfrak{k}}\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=0

since
\langle,\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}
is
{\rm Ad}(G)
-invariant and the canonical decomposition
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{k}\oplus\mathfrak{m}
is orthogonal with respect to the (negative) Killing form (see Lemma
2.5
). Therefore, we obtain
\langle\Omega_{0}(Z),\theta\rangle_{\mathfrak{g}}=\langle Z,\theta^{\dagger}\rangle
for any
Z\in\Gamma(TM)
, and this implies the desired conclusion.
∎

Hence, the test function considered in
[
9
]
coincides with our test function given in Section
4
in the case when
M
is itself a compact semi-simple RSS. Note that, however, our setting considered in Section
4
is more general, and the expression of the trace formula is different from
[
9
]
.

Appendix B
Local smoothness of simultaneous eigenvectors

In this Appendix, we prove the following technical result used in the proof of Proposition
5.5
.

Proposition B.1
.

Let
(\Sigma^{m},g)
be a smooth Riemannian manifold, and
\alpha,\beta
be smooth symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor fields on
\Sigma
. Suppose furthermore the
(1,1)
-tensor fields
A:=g^{-1}\alpha
and
B:=g^{-1}\beta
commute with each other, i.e.
[A,B]=0
on
\Sigma
. Then, there exits an open subset
U\subset\Sigma
and a smooth orthonormal frame
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
on
U
such that
A
and
B
are simultaneously diagonalized by
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
at every point
p\in U
.

The proof relies on the result by Singley
[
18
]
. We briefly summarize his result before the proof.

Let
G
and
G^{\prime}
be smooth symmetric
(0,2)
-tensor fields on a smooth
m
-dimensional manifold
\Sigma
. For each
p\in\Sigma
,
G
defines a linear map
T_{p}\Sigma\to T_{p}^{*}\Sigma
,
v\mapsto G(v,\cdot)

which is also denoted by
G
. We define the linear map
G^{\prime}:T_{p}\Sigma\to T^{*}_{p}\Sigma
as well. If
G
is positive definite, then
G
is invertible, and we obtain a linear map
(G^{-1}G^{\prime})(p):T_{p}\Sigma\to T_{p}\Sigma
. For example, if
\Sigma^{m}
is a hypersurface in a Riemannian manifold
(M^{m+1},g)
, we may choose the symmetric
(0,2)
-tensors as the induced metric
g
and
\widetilde{A}(X,Y):=\langle A(X,Y),\nu\rangle
, where
A
is the second fundamental form of
\Sigma^{m}
. In this case,
G^{-1}G^{\prime}=g^{-1}\widetilde{A}
coincides with the shape operator
S_{\nu}
of
\Sigma
. Since both
G
and
G^{\prime}
are symmetric and
G
is invertible, we see that
G^{-1}G^{\prime}
is diagonalizable with real eigenvalues
\{\kappa_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
and a corresponding orthonormal eigenbasis
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
of
T_{p}\Sigma
. Note that, in general, there is no guarantee that the eigenvalues and eigenvectors are smooth on whole of
\Sigma
. However, it turns out to be true on an open dense subset on
\Sigma
:

Lemma B.2

(Theorem 1, 2 and 3 in
[
18
]
)
.

After the re-ordering of
\{\kappa_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
by
\kappa_{1}(p)\leq\dots\leq\kappa_{m}(p)
, we have the following:

(1)

\kappa_{\mu}
is continuous on whole of
\Sigma
for any
\mu=1,\ldots,m
.

(2)

There is an open dense subset
U\subset\Sigma
on which
\kappa_{\mu}
is
C^{\infty}
for any
\mu=1,\ldots,m
.

(3)

For any point
p\in U
, there is a neighborhood
U_{p}
of
p
and a
C^{\infty}
orthonormal frame
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
on
U_{p}
which diagonalizes
G^{-1}G^{\prime}(q)
for each
q\in U_{p}
. Moreover, the multiplicities of eigenvalues are constant on
U_{p}
.

Remark B.3
.

In
[
18
]
, the open dense subset
U\subset\Sigma
is explicitly given by considering the multiplicity of the eigenvalues of
G^{-1}G^{\prime}
. However we do not need the explicit form of
U
in this paper.

By using this lemma, we shall give a proof of Proposition
B.1
.

Proof of Proposition
B.1
.

By Lemma
B.2
, there exists a point
p_{0}\in\Sigma
and an open neighborhood
U_{p_{0}}
of
p_{0}
such that
A=g^{-1}\alpha
is diagonalized by a smooth orthonormal frame
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
on
U_{p_{0}}
. Moreover, we may assume the multiplicities of eigenvalues of
A
are constant on
U_{p_{0}}
and
A
is expressed by the diagonal matrix
A={\rm diag}(\lambda_{1}I_{m_{1}},\cdots,\lambda_{r}I_{m_{r}})
on
U_{p_{0}}
with respect to the local frame
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
,
where
\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}<\cdots<\lambda_{r}
are the distinct eigenvalues of
A
,
m_{i}
is the multiplicity of
\lambda_{i}
and
I_{m_{i}}
is the identity matrix of order
m_{i}
.

Since
[A,B]=0
, each eigenspace of
A
is invariant by the left multiplication of
B
, and this implies that
B
is expressed by
the block matrix
B={\rm diag}(B_{1},\ldots,B_{r})
on
U_{p_{0}}
with respect to the local frame
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
, where
B_{i}
is some square matrix of order
m_{i}
. Note that each
B_{i}
is a symmetric matrix since so is
B
.
We consider a symmetric matrix defined by
\widetilde{B}_{1}:={\rm diag}(B_{1},I_{m_{2}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
which represents some
(1,1)
-tensor field
g^{-1}\widetilde{\beta}_{1}^{\prime}
on
U_{p_{0}}
, where we regard
U_{p_{0}}
as a Riemannian manifold by the induced metric.
By Lemma
B.2
, there exists a point
p_{1}\in U_{p_{0}}
such that there is an open neighborhood
U_{p_{1}}\subset U_{p_{0}}
of
p_{1}
and a smooth orthonormal frame
\{e_{\mu}^{(1)}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
which diagonalizes
\widetilde{B}_{1}
and the multiplicities of eigenvalues of
\widetilde{B}_{1}
are constant on
U_{p_{1}}
. Since
\widetilde{B}_{1}
has eigenvalue
1
, we denote the
1
-eigenspace of
\widetilde{B}_{1}
by
V^{(1)}
and set
k_{1}:={\rm dim}V^{(1)}
which is constant on
U_{p_{1}}
. Note that
k_{1}\geq m_{2}+\cdots+m_{r}=m-m_{1}
.

By taking a re-ordering if necessary, we may assume that
V^{(1)}
is spanned by
\{e_{i}^{(1)}\}_{i=m-k_{1}+1}^{m}
.
Since
e_{l}\in V^{(1)}
for any
l>m_{1}
, we see that
W^{(1)}:={\rm span}\{e_{m_{1}+1},\ldots,e_{m}\}
is an
(m-m_{1})
-dimensional linear subspace of
V^{(1)}
. If
V^{(1)}\neq W^{(1)}
, we can find
k_{1}-(m-m_{1})
vectors
e^{(1)}_{i_{1}},\ldots,e^{(1)}_{i_{k_{1}-(m-m_{1})}}
in
\{e_{i}^{(1)}\}_{i=m-k_{1}+1}^{m}
so that
\{e_{i_{1}}^{(1)},\ldots,e_{i_{k_{1}-(m-m_{1})}}^{(1)},e_{m_{1}+1},\dots,e_{m}\}
becomes a basis of
V^{(1)}
. By the Gram–Schmidt orthonormalization, we obtain an orthonormal basis of
V^{(1)}
of the form
\{e_{i_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}},\ldots,e_{i_{k_{1}-(m-m_{1})}}^{{}^{\prime}},e_{m_{1}+1},\dots,e_{m}\}
. Now, we put

Q_{1}:=(e_{1}^{(1)},\ldots,e_{m-k_{1}}^{(1)},e_{i_{1}}^{{}^{\prime}},\ldots,e_{i_{k_{1}-(m-m_{1})}}^{{}^{\prime}},e_{m_{1}+1},\dots,e_{m}).

By construction,
Q_{1}
consists of eigenvectors of
\widetilde{B}_{1}
, and each component of
Q_{1}
is a smooth function on
U_{p_{1}}
. Moreover,
Q_{1}
is expressed by
{\rm diag}(Q_{1}^{\prime},I_{m_{2}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
for some square matrix
Q_{1}^{\prime}
of order
m_{1}
(since the matrix is expressed by using
\{e_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
), and we have
Q_{1}^{-1}\widetilde{B}_{1}Q_{1}={\rm diag}(B_{1}^{\prime},I_{m_{2}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
, where
B_{1}^{\prime}
is the diagonalized matrix of
B_{1}
.

Next, we consider a symmetric matrix
\widetilde{B}_{2}={\rm diag}(I_{m_{1}},B_{2},I_{m_{3}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
and by a similar argument, we see that there is a point
p_{2}\in U_{p_{1}}
, an open neighborhood
U_{p_{2}}\subset U_{p_{1}}
and a matrix
Q_{2}
whose components are smooth function on
U_{p_{2}}
satisfying that
Q_{2}={\rm diag}(I_{m_{1}},Q_{2}^{\prime},I_{m_{3}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
and
Q_{2}^{-1}\widetilde{B}_{2}Q_{2}={\rm diag}(I_{m_{1}},B_{2}^{\prime},I_{m_{3}},\ldots,I_{m_{r}})
, where
B_{2}^{\prime}
is the diagonalized matrix of
B_{2}
.

By repeating the argument, we obtain a decreasing sequence of open subsets
U_{p_{0}}\supset U_{p_{1}}\supset\cdots\supset U_{p_{r}}
and matrices
Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{r}
defined on
U_{p_{r}}
. We set
R:=Q_{1}Q_{2}\cdots Q_{r}
. Then, we see that
R^{-1}AR
and
R^{-1}BR
are diagonal matrices by construction of
Q_{i}
. Thus we put
E_{\mu}:=R(e_{\mu})
for each
\mu=1,\ldots,m
and then, since each
Q_{i}
is an orthogonal matrix and has smooth components,
\{E_{\mu}\}_{\mu=1}^{m}
is a smooth orthonormal frame defined on
U_{p_{r}}
which simultaneously diagonalizes
A
and
B
. This completes the proof.
∎

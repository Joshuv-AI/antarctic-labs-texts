Economic burden of road traffic injuries in Iran; time to make tough decisions: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Habib Omranikhoo, Anahita Nicknam, Hedaiat Salari, Mahbobeh Shahbazifard, Mehdi Rezaee
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectivesRoad traffic injuries (RTIs) constitute a serious challenge for human societies, imposing a substantial economic burden on countries and representing the leading cause of death among children and youth between the ages of 5 and 29. Estimates indicate that RTIs are on track to become the fifth leading cause of death globally by 2030. The aim of this study was to estimate the economic burden of RTIs in Bushehr Province and consequently in Iran in 2021–2022.MethodsThis is an incidence-based and cost-of-illness analysis study conducted from a societal perspective using recorded data on road traffic accidents during 2021–2022 years in the Hospital Information System (HIS) of hospitals affiliated to Bushehr University of Medical Sciences and also published information on RTIs by the Iran’s Statistical Centre. This study encompasses all individuals injured in road traffic accidents referred to the related hospitals. No sampling was employed in the investigation. The human capital approach was used to estimate the indirect costs and finally the bottom-up costing approach was used to calculate the total costs. Moreover, the uncertainty of the data was addressed by implementing a two-way sensitivity analysis.FindingsThe results demonstrated that in 2021 and 2022, the average annual cost of accidents per patient was 179,376.7 and 179,093.2 PPP$, respectively. Moreover, the largest proportion of costs was attributed to indirect expenses (95.7%), with lost income resulting from death accounting for the predominant share of indirect costs (equivalent to 75.3% of total indirect costs). The proportion of direct costs was found to be 4.3%, with the largest share of these costs attributable to salary expenditure, representing 34.3% of the overall direct costs. In addition, the economic burden of the disease in the country was estimated at 8,842,115,165 PPP $ over a two-year period. Given the sensitivity analysis, the economic burden of accidents in Bushehr province over a two-year period ranged from 132,184,254 to 1,003,055,017 PPP $, while the countrywide burden extended from 7,376,256,913 to 53,546,384,401 PPP $.ConclusionThe findings revealed that RTIs impose a huge economic impact on households and society, with costs reaching 3.3% of GDP in Iran during the years 2021 and 2022, underscoring the necessity for policymakers to prioritize the reduction of this public health concern. Accordingly, establishing constructive inter-sectoral collaboration, Formulating and enforcing stricter driving laws, using the power of the media to educate the public and improving the safety of roads and vehicles is recommended.Keywords: Cost of illness, Health care costs, Cost analysis, Health expenditures, Years of life lost (YLL), Traffic accidents

Introduction

Road traffic injuries (RTIs) and deaths resulting from are a major global problem. RTIs are the 10th leading cause of death worldwide, the 7th leading cause of death in middle-income countries, and the 2nd leading cause of death in Iran [1, 2]. In addition, injuries caused by road traffic accidents are the leading cause of death for children and young people between the ages of 5 and 29 years [3]. According to the latest report published by the World Health Organization in 2018, 1.35 million people die every year due to road traffic crashes around the world [4]. Additionally, 20 to 50 million people are injured or disabled [5, 6]. A review of the available statistical data indicates that the leading cause of death amongst Polish men up to the age of 44 has been injuries resulting from accidents. As During the period between 2004 and 2014, a total of 52,217 people died and 597,191 individuals were subjected to the road-related incidents [7]. In 2019, Brazil recorded 44,529 deaths due to RTIs [8]. Althaifani et al., in Yemen reported 11,684 RTAs, resulting in 840 deaths and 9,760 injuries [9]. The findings of Al Turki’s study in Saudia Arabia highlight that approximately 19 deaths and 96 injuries happen daily due to RTAs [10].

Although the death rate from RTIs has fallen in some countries (in Trinidad and Tobago from 17 deaths per 100,000 population in 1996 to 10 deaths per 100,000 population in 2000 [11], in Ethiopia from 90.11 to 69. 4 during 2007–2017 [12], a decrease of 0.95 deaths per 100,000 people since 2009 in Mexico compared to 2008 [13]), and in Maghreb, the incidence of RTIs has decreased from 719.57 to 609.49 per 100,000 people in 2017 compared to 1990 [14], However, the burden of RTIs still remains high. Given these facts, reducing the number of accidental deaths has been considered as one of the objectives of sustainable development, underscoring the urgency and significance of addressing this issue [15].

In general, the review of studies highlights that the overall death rate from RTIs varies between countries, ranging from 5.9 deaths per 100,000 people in Turkey [16], 6.4 deaths per 100,000 people in New York City [17], 9.3 deaths per 100,000 people in Europe [18], 10 deaths per 100,000 in Trinidad and Tobago [11], 11 deaths per 100,000 people in China [19], 17.2 deaths per 100,000 people in India [20], 23.5 to 29.8 deaths per 100,000 in Malawi [21] until 29.7 to 33.2 deaths per 100,000 in Thailand [22].

The death rate from RTIs in Iran has been reported 34.82 per 100,000 in 2016, expecting to reach 17.95 per 100,000 in 2030 [23]. Additionally, share of RTIs in Iran is about 17.7% of all deaths [24]. On the contrary, there has been a 300 per cent increase in the number of RTIs from 111.1 per 100,000 in 1997 to 331.8 per 100,000 in 2020 [25]. From another angle, the economic burden of RTIs has also received considerable attention worldwide and It is estimated that the economic burden of RTIs imposed on countries is between 1 and 5% of gross domestic product (GDP) [26, 27]. In this regard, a study conducted in Nepal in 2017 estimated the total cost of RTI to be 122.88 million dollars. Of this, the cost of lost productivity was 91.57 million dollars (74.52%) and the cost of pain, grief and suffering was 18.31 million dollars (14.90%). Direct non-medical costs were $11.50 million (9.36%), while direct medical costs were $1.50 million (1.22%). The economic costs of RTIs have tripled since 2007, amounting to 1.52% of GDP, indicating a noticeable increase in the financial burden associated with RTIs [28].

A study of 166 countries has found that RTIs would cost the global economy US$ 1.8 trillion (constant 2010 US dollars) over the period 2015-30 [29]. A study carried out in 31 European countries has shown that the total cost of road traffic accidents would be equivalent to % 0.4 to %4.1 of GDP [30]. In Iran in 2021, the average direct (medical, non-medical), indirect and total costs of RTI were estimated to be US$2908 (US$1591, US$1316), US$5790 and US$8701 respectively [31].

Furthermore, the report of Global Road Safety Facility in 2016 showed that in Iran, 75% of people killed or injured in crashes are in the productive age group, and the amount of income lost due to road crashes is estimated at $28,761 million, equivalent to 6.9% of GDP [32]. according to the National Study on the Burden of Disease and Injury in Iran, road traffic accidents ranked first in terms of the number of years lost due to premature death [33]. And that The World Health Organization has identified Iran as one of the countries with the highest number of injuries and deaths due to road accidents [33]. All this suggests that tackling this problem is a high priority and calculating the economic burden of accidents provides a full understanding of the magnitude of injuries and helps policy makers to efficiently allocate needed resources [34]. Bushehr is a province with a population of 1,253,000 according to the latest estimate [35] conducted by the Iranian statistical center, and road traffic accidents are the second leading cause of death in the province [23]. To the best of our knowledge, no study has been conducted to determine the economic burden of RTAs in this province. In this study, we aimed to determine the economic burden of RTAs in the province and, subsequently, to estimate the economic burden of RTAs for Iran.

Methods

Study population

The study included all referred individuals who were subjected to the road traffic accidents and had received health care services from hospitals affiliated to Bushehr University of Medical Sciences in 2021 and 2022 and traffic accident code was registered on HIS for them. A total of 17,653 patients were included in the analysis.

Study design

This is a cross-sectional study that used a cost-of-illness analysis to estimate the economic burden of RTIs in Iran. The incidence-based approach was used to calculate the economic burden of traffic accidents from a societal perspective. This approach considers the present value of all earnings resulting from new cases of disease in a given period. The human capital approach was utilized to estimate indirect costs and finally the bottom-up costing approach was used to calculate total costs.

This study includes both direct and indirect costs of RTIs in Iran. The direct costs were obtained from the registered data of Hospital Information System (HIS) in 2021–2022. The source of data on the number of injuries and deaths due to RTIs was the reports of Iranian Statistical Centre and [36] the estimate of years lost due to disability (YLD) was extracted from the published articles [15].

The daily wage rate determined by the Iranian Ministry of Labor was used to estimate the lost income due to absence from work for the 15–65 age group [37]. We also used the standard life table to estimate years of lost life (YLL). In the absence of income data for deceased road traffic victims, annual per capita income published by the International Labor Organization was used.

Cost analysis

All calculated costs from the RTIs were converted to purchasing power parity (PPP)$ in 2022. The unit cost of each category was then multiplied by the number of associated cases, and finally the economic burden was calculated by adding the total direct and indirect costs. Excel 2016 software was also used for data analysis.

Cost calculation

In the context of economic burden studies, costs are typically divided into three main groups, including direct (medical and non-medical), indirect and intangible costs. In this study, we only considered direct medical and indirect costs.

Direct medical costs

Direct costs include inpatient and outpatient costs, such as the cost of medicines, diagnostic tests and services, hospitalization, surgical procedures, doctors’ fees, rehabilitation, etc. the direct costs were extracted from the patients’ medical records in the HIS.

Indirect costs

Indirect costs are a category of expenses that are not directly related to the immediate costs of healthcare services. These indirect costs can be classified into three main categories: costs associated with hospitalization, costs related to disability and costs associated with premature death.

Indirect costs due to hospitalization per patient

This cost group is calculated by multiplying the average length of stay of hospitalized patients by the minimum daily wage. In this study, the daily wage of Iran’s Ministry of Labor in the year 2022 as 28.14 PPP$ was used [37]. Furthermore, all costs were converted to PPP$ using the exchange rate of 62,870 rials per dollar in 2022 [38].

Indirect costs due to years of life lost due to disability (YLD)

Years lived with disability (YLD) is an important metric for assessing the burden of disease, reflecting the years people live with health disorders [39]. In this study, due to the lack of access to years lived with disability, information from other studies was used [15].

Sensitivity analysis

In light of the potential for fluctuations in direct costs, which are mostly affected by different treatment methods، a one-way sensitivity analysis was performed. In the first scenario، positive and negative ranges of 20% were used to minimize the possible error for direct costs. In the second scenario, different discount rates (from − 20 to + 20) and different wages (minimum and maximum wages paid by the Iranian Ministry of Labor) were determined to achieve the best and worst scenarios, thereby ensuring the greatest robustness for indirect costs.

In addition, a two-way sensitivity analysis was conducted. In this step, the best and worst scenarios were obtained by simultaneously changing the parameters associated with direct and indirect costs.

Calculation of the economic burden of RTIs in Bushehr Province and Iran

A bottom-up approach was used to calculate the economic burden of road traffic injuries. Firstly, the average direct costs were multiplied by the total number of road traffic injuries. Secondly, the average indirect cost of hospitalization was multiplied by the number of road accident victims aged 15–65. Thirdly, the average income loss due to disability was multiplied by the number of surviving road accident victims aged 15–65. Fourthly, the average indirect cost due to premature death was multiplied by the number of deaths aged 15–65 Finally, the sum of these four items was an estimate of the economic burden of road accidents for the years 2021 and 2022.

Discussion

In this research, a total of 17,643 RTIs were studied. The total economic burden of RTIs in Bushehr Province and Iran during 2021–2022 was estimated to be 151.2 and 8,842.1 million PPP$ (on average 75.6 and 4,421.5 million PPP$ per year), respectively, equal to 0.009% and 0.6% GDP. Considering sensitivity analysis, its maximum value for Bushehr and Iran reaches 1,000.3 and 53,546.3 million PPP$ (on average 500 and 26,773 million PPP$ per year), corresponding to 0.06% and 3.3% GDP (on average 1.65% GDP per year), respectively. Ahadi et al. in their study estimated the economic burden of RTIs for Iran at 1.41% of GDP in 2009 [45]. In another study in Nepal, researchers estimated the economic burden of RTI at 1.52% of GDP in 2020 [28]. These results are to some extent consistent with our findings, but there are some differences between the studies in terms of the method of calculation. Another study by Behnood et al. estimated the economic burden of RTIs for Iran at 2.18% of GDP in 2011. It is worth noting that the components considered by these studies to estimate the economic burden of RTIs have non-negligible differences.

The results also showed that the ratio of the number of men to women affected by RTIs was about 4, and that 85% of the victims were men. The higher proportion of men in total road traffic crashes compared to women, and consequently the higher proportion of their deaths resulting in higher DALY rates, has been reported in other studies [15, 46, 47]. This may to some extent be related to the fact that men drive more than women and also engage in more risky behavior. In addition, due to their professional status, men drive for longer hours, which could eventually lead to violations of driving laws [48–51].

The 15–29 age group had the highest proportion of YLLs in Bushehr province (32%). This part of the findings is consistent with the findings of Hashempour et al. and Tafti et al. studies, in which they reported the high contribution of the 15–29 age group in imposing heavy burden of traffic accidents on society [15, 52]. In another study conducted in Iran, researchers reported that most road accident victims were in the 20–40 age group [53].

Of the total economic burden of RTIs in 2021–2022, the share of direct costs was 382.9 million PPP$ (4.3%). The average of total direct costs was estimated at 565.1 PPP$ per injured patient. This part of the results is in line with the other studies in which the lowest proportion of the total cost of RTIs belonged to direct medical costs [28, 54, 55]. Contrary to the findings of the present study, Rezaei et al. in their study attributed most of the economic burden of traffic accidents to direct costs [46]. This discrepancy could be largely due to the items considered for direct costs as well as the cost estimation method. One of the reasons attributed to the low proportion of direct costs compared to indirect costs in road traffic accidents is the high number of deaths at productive ages, leading to an increase in YLLS and, in turn, an increase in indirect costs.

In addition, the cost group of medicines and consumables accounted for more than 40% of total direct costs, followed by the cost group of salaries at 34.3%. Almost in line with our results, Banstola et al. in their study reported the share of medicines as 45.42% of total direct medical costs [28].

The total indirect costs of RTIs during 2021–2022 were estimated at 8,459,132,407 PPP$ (on average, 178,682.5 PPP$ per patient and 4,229,566,203 PPP$ per a year), representing 95.7% of the total costs. Of the total indirect costs imposed by traffic accidents during 2021–2022, the share of YLLs, YLDs and hospitalization was 75.3%, 24.1% and 0.6%, respectively. Moreover, the average of total indirect costs for corresponding components was 175,621.8, 3,196.4 and 81.1 PPP$ per patient respectively. A higher proportion of indirect costs compared to direct costs has been reported in some studies [28, 56]. However, contrary to our findings, this proportion in some studies was reversed [46, 57, 58]. Some part of these differences could be attributed to the method of cost classification and the discount rate of future incomes [28].

Consistent with studies conducted in other countries, costs of lost productivity due to premature death as well as lost productivity resulting from disability accounted for most of the burden of RTIs [59, 60]. This finding can be related to the fact that the deaths of traffic accidents mainly involve young people. As a result, the cost of lost productivity could be substantially high.

One of the strengths of this study in comparison with the other studies is that the estimation of the economic burden for Iran was carried out using a large sample and on the basis of valid data recorded in the largest hospital database, so called HIS. Therefore, the direct cost per patient may be very close to reality.

The approach used in this study took into account several cost elements in the estimation of the economic burden of RTIs. However, some costs, such as rehabilitation, follow-up visits or other associated costs with RTIs incurred by patients in the mid and long term, were not included in the study due to lack of access to data. Additionally, intangible costs such as pain and suffering, which are an integral part of RTIs, were not taken into account. All these limitations, lead us to the point that these estimates could be conservative at this phase of the study. In order to reduce the impact of these limitations, a two-way sensitivity analysis was performed with a 20% range of variation in the variables used.

To sum up, this study shows that Iran’s RTIs burden is not negligible and that without innovative mechanisms and comprehensive interventions, achieving the goal of halving the burden of RTIs by 2030 would remain a sheer dream.

Conclusion

The results showed that the burden caused by RTIs, especially the years of lost life due to premature death and fallowing that associated economic burden is huge and alarming and with costs have reached 3.3 GDP during 2021–2022, the seriousness of the situation is underscored. Hence, it is expected that policymakers will minimize this public health concern by formulating policies aimed at reducing the burden of accidents, such as strengthening inter-sectoral cooperation, optimal allocation of resources, public education, strict enforcement of the law, and promotion of road and vehicle safety.

Limitations

Our study has several limitations. Firstly, we used the results of published studies to estimate YLDs and associated costs, which may have influenced our results. Secondly, the study did not include intangible costs and some medical costs incurred by patients in mid and long run and thirdly, direct non -medical costs were not included in the study, Additionally, given the potential discrepancy between the age profile of deceased patients in Bushehr province and that of the general population in the country as a whole, it is imperative to exercise caution when generalizing the findings.

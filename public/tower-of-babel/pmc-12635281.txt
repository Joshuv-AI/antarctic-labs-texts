Validation of the Persian version of anterior skull base questionnaire in patients undergoing endoscopic transnasal transsphenoidal surgery
Authors: Farhang Rashidi, Mohammad Rostami Ravari, Mohammad Abed, Pouria Delbari, Mehdi Zeinalizadeh, Alipasha Meysamie, Mahnaz Pejman Sani, Milad Shafizadeh
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Endoscopic skull base surgery has transformed craniofacial treatment by reducing morbidity and enhancing precision. Traditional outcome measures often overlook the multidimensional aspects of quality of life (QoL) in recovery. Patient-reported tools, such as the anterior skull base questionnaire (ASBQ), offer a comprehensive view of QoL. However, the ASBQ’s effectiveness is limited in non-English-speaking populations without cultural adaptation. This study aims to culturally adapt and psychometrically validate the Persian version of the anterior skull base questionnaire (ASBQ-P) for patients undergoing transnasal transsphenoidal surgery. Translation and cross-cultural adaptation adhered to ISPOR guidelines, encompassing forward and backward translation, expert review, and cognitive debriefing. Internal consistency was evaluated through Cronbach’s α, and test-retest reliability was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs). Convergent validity was assessed using Pearson’s correlation with SNOT-22 scores, while responsiveness was evaluated through effect sizes and standardized response means. Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 27, with a significance threshold set at P < 0.05. A total of 36 patients completed the Persian version of the ASBQ and SNOT-22 questionnaires. The ASBQ demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.936). Test-retest reliability was high, with an ICC of 0.927 for average measures. Convergent validity was supported by a significant moderate negative correlation between ASBQ and SNOT-22 scores (r=–0.544, P = 0.001). A known-groups validity analysis revealed significant differences in ASBQ scores across SNOT-22 severity categories (P = 0.001), supporting the instrument’s discriminative capacity. Our findings confirm the Persian ASBQ as a reliable and valid tool for assessing outcomes in anterior skull base surgery.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-025-24989-3.

Introduction

Craniofacial surgery, particularly endoscopic skull base procedures, has revolutionized the treatment of complex pathological conditions involving the anterior skull base1–3. The paradigm of minimally invasive surgery has not only transformed operative methodologies but also redefined the benchmarks for patient recovery and satisfaction.

The advent of endoscopic techniques has markedly reduced morbidity and improved surgical precision; however, these advances also bring new challenges in postoperative assessment. Traditional clinical outcomes such as tumor resection extent, complication rates, and survival statistics, while important, do not fully capture the patient’s subjective experience of recovery. While these interventions aim to restore anatomical structure and function, their impact on patients’ quality of life (QoL) extends beyond physical recovery, affecting pain, emotional well-being, social interaction, and self-perception4–10.

Evaluating these multidimensional outcomes is crucial for delivering comprehensive, patient-centered care. This need has led to the development of disease-specific patient-reported outcome (PRO) measures that evaluate QoL in a contextually meaningful way11–14. By integrating robust patient-reported outcomes with traditional clinical metrics, clinicians can gain a deeper understanding of recovery trajectories, ultimately facilitating the development of tailored interventions and enhanced long-term care.

One such instrument is the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire (ASBQ), initially developed by Gil et al. to assess QoL following skull base tumor resection4. The ASBQ evaluates six domains: performance, physical function, vitality, pain, specific symptoms, and emotional impact. It has since emerged as one of the most validated tools for assessing QoL in patients undergoing craniofacial surgery, especially among English-speaking populations15,16. With the growing use of endoscopic endonasal approaches, the ASBQ has been applied in conjunction with tools such as the SNOT-22, demonstrating sensitivity to postoperative changes and utility in minimally invasive surgical settings11,16–19.

However, its applicability remains limited in non-English-speaking populations due to the lack of culturally adapted versions. Cultural and linguistic factors can significantly influence patients’ interpretation of QoL instruments, underscoring the need for validated translations. Encouragingly, other QoL instruments, such as the Sino-Nasal Outcome Test (SNOT-20), have been successfully adapted for Persian-speaking populations, demonstrating strong reliability and validity20. Adopting similar approaches for the ASBQ is necessary to ensure its methodological robustness in Persian-speaking patients undergoing endoscopic skull base surgery21. Addressing this gap through the adaptation and validation of the ASBQ-P enhances patient care locally and contributes to the global effort of standardizing outcome measurement across diverse populations.

This study aims to translate, culturally adapt, and psychometrically validate the Persian version of the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire (ASBQ-P). Through a structured process that encompasses linguistic adaptation and statistical validation, this study aims to provide a standardized, reliable, and culturally relevant QoL assessment tool for Iranian patients, ultimately enhancing postoperative evaluation and clinical decision-making21,22.

Materials and methods

Study design and setting

The current study is prospective, methodological research conducted at Shariati Hospital in Tehran, Iran. Its objective is to culturally adapt and psychometrically validate the Persian version of the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire (ASBQ-P) for patients undergoing transnasal transsphenoidal surgery in the Iranian population. The Tehran University of Medical Sciences (TUMS) Ethics Committee approved the study protocol under the code “IR.TUMS.NI.REC.1403.048”, and all experiments were performed in accordance with relevant guidelines and regulations, such as the Declaration of Helsinki. All patients included in the study were adults who voluntarily consented to participate in the current study by signing pre-designed written forms.

Participants

Eligible participants for this study included adults aged 18 years and older who were native Persian speakers and possessed proficiency in reading, writing, and comprehending Persian. Participants meeting eligibility criteria must have received endoscopic transnasal transsphenoidal skull-base surgery for pituitary adenomas or other sellar and suprasellar lesions. Inclusion criteria mandated a preoperative Karnofsky Performance Status of 70 or above, while exclusion criteria eliminated patients with a history of prior transsphenoidal surgery to maintain uniformity in surgical experience and prevent confounding variables from previous interventions.

Patients were excluded if they had a history of major psychiatric disorders, such as schizophrenia or bipolar disorder, or if they were diagnosed with neurodegenerative conditions, including Parkinson’s disease or Alzheimer’s disease, as these comorbidities could independently affect the quality of life outcomes. Individuals with active sinonasal pathology, including chronic rhinosinusitis as defined by EPOS 2020 criteria23, or those with significant nasal septal deviation necessitating concurrent corrective surgery, were excluded to specifically assess the quality of life impacts related to skull-base surgery.

Exclusion criteria also encompassed any previous endoscopic or open skull-base surgery, as well as significant sinonasal surgical interventions conducted within the last two years, which could influence baseline symptom reporting. Individuals exhibiting substantial cognitive impairment, evidenced by a Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE) score below 24, or those with uncorrected severe visual or auditory deficits that could hinder questionnaire completion, were excluded from the study. Individuals concurrently enrolled in other clinical trials that could affect postoperative quality-of-life outcomes were excluded to avoid confounding effects.

Translation and cross-cultural adaptation

The translation and cross-cultural adaptation of the ASBQ into Persian adhered to established international guidelines, such as those of the ISPOR Task Force21,24, to ensure semantic, idiomatic, experiential, and conceptual equivalence. Two independent bilingual translators, both native Persian speakers, one a neurosurgeon (M.S.) with expertise in skull-base patient care and the other a professional translator without a medical background, created forward translations of the original English ASBQ. The translations underwent evaluation by a reconciliation committee consisting of two translators and two study team members (M. R. R. and P. D.). This committee produced a single preliminary Persian draft, addressing discrepancies through consensus and emphasizing clear, culturally relevant language rather than strict literalism.

The initial Persian version was subsequently back-translated into English by two independent bilingual translators, an endocrinologist (M. P. S.) and a professional translator, who were unaware of the original ASBQ. An expert committee comprising four translators, two neurosurgeons, and the principal investigator of the study (F. R.) conducted a detailed item-by-item comparison of the back-translations with the source questionnaire. The committee assessed each item for adherence to the original meaning, clarity in Persian, and cultural relevance, including the adaptation of idioms or references that may be unfamiliar to Persian speakers, and implemented specific modifications to rectify any conceptual discrepancies.

After expert review, the revised Persian version was pre-tested on a convenience sample of ten postoperative patients at Shariati Hospital, selected to represent a range of ages, educational backgrounds, and lesion types. Cognitive debriefing interviews were performed right after the completion of the questionnaire. Each patient was requested to paraphrase items in their own words and to identify any confusing or ambiguous language. Feedback from the interviews led to minor modifications in wording, such as substituting uncommon medical terminology with lay equivalents and clarifying temporal references like “in the past four weeks,” culminating in the final ASBQ-P.

All steps of the adaptation process were documented, promoting transparency and facilitating the replication or extension of our methodology by future researchers.

Sample size and data collection

A total of 36 patients were enrolled in the study, achieving the recommended subject-to-item ratio for validation studies and ensuring sufficient statistical power. The sample size was established to include a minimum of five to ten participants for each of the 35 items on the ASBQ. A sample size of 32 patients was determined to be adequate for detecting an intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) of at least 0.85, with a 95% confidence interval width of ± 0.10. Consequently, 36 patients were recruited to mitigate the effects of potential attrition or missing data.

Data were collected from patients attending the neurosurgery clinic at Shariati Hospital during a two-month interval, specifically from April to May 2025. Data collection was conducted solely during the postoperative follow-up visit, which was scheduled after the endoscopic transnasal transsphenoidal skull-base surgery was performed. During the visit, each patient completed the Persian 35-item Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire (ASBQ-P) and the 22-item Sino-Nasal Outcome Test (SNOT-22) under the supervision of a trained research coordinator (M. A.). Demographic and clinical information, including age, sex, education level, histopathological diagnosis, and relevant comorbidities, was retrospectively extracted from the electronic medical record and verified through patient interviews during the same appointment.

A randomly selected subset of 15 patients was invited to repeat the ASBQ-P between seven and ten days following their initial postoperative assessment to evaluate test-retest reliability. The same coordinator conducted these repeat administrations, either in person during a brief clinic check or through a standardized telephone interview. The coordinator remained blinded to each patient’s original ASBQ-P responses and did not provide any interpretive assistance. No changes to clinical management were implemented during the interval between assessments to maintain the stability of the postoperative condition.

Following each administration, the coordinator assessed all questionnaires for completeness and inquired with patients about any missing items. Data were input into a secure, password-protected database utilizing a double data-entry system; discrepancies were resolved through cross-referencing the original paper forms by a senior researcher (F.R.). Each participant received a distinct study identifier, and all personally identifiable information was stored separately to ensure confidentiality during data analysis.

Psychometric evaluation and statistical analysis

Internal consistency was assessed using Cronbach’s α for the overall ASBQ-P and each domain, with values of 0.70 or higher indicating acceptable consistency. Test-retest reliability was assessed using intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs), and an ICC of ≥ 0.75 indicated good reproducibility. Construct validity was assessed using Pearson’s correlation coefficients to evaluate convergent validity with SNOT-22 scores at each time point. Effect sizes (Cohen’s d) and standardized response means were utilized to quantify sensitivity to clinical change between preoperative and postoperative assessments. Floor and ceiling effects were assessed by calculating the proportion of respondents who achieved the minimum or maximum possible scores; effects of 15% or greater were deemed significant. Scores obtained from SNOT-22 were categorized according to a previous study into “normal”, “mild”, “moderate”, and “severe” impaired quality of life to further assess the correlation between the scores of the two questionnaires. ANOVA was performed to identify differences in ASBQ-P scores between patients assigned to groups based on SNOT-22 classifications. All analyses were conducted using SPSS version 27, with a two-sided p-value of less than 0.05, indicating statistical significance.

Results

Descriptive statistics

Thirty-six patients completed the Persian ASBQ and SNOT-22 questionnaires. The mean age of participants was 47.81 years, and the mean duration since surgery was 25.47 months. Patients were subsequently of endoscopic transnasal transsphenoidal surgeries due to a variety of pathologies, including non-functioning adenoma (N = 24; 66.7%), Acromegaly (N = 5; 13.9%), and Cushing disease (N = 3; 8.3%). The mean SNOT-22 total score was 14.22, while the mean ASBQ-P total score was 105.58. Regarding marital status, 22.2% of individuals were single, while 77.8% were married. According to the SNOT-22 categorical classification, 47.2% of patients (N = 17) were categorized as having a “normal” quality of life, while 25.0% (N = 9) and 27.8% (N = 10) were considered to have “mild” and “moderate” quality of life problems, respectively. No patients were categorized as severely impaired in terms of quality of life. Further details are demonstrated in Table 1. Convergent validity

The Pearson correlation between the ASBQ total score and the SNOT-22 total score revealed a moderate, statistically significant negative association (r=–0.544, P = 0.001), suggesting that improved disease-specific quality of life on the ASBQ is associated with a reduced symptom burden on the SNOT-22.

Internal consistency and reliability

The internal consistency of the SNOT, comprising 22 symptom-related items, demonstrated excellent reliability, with a Cronbach’s α of 0.927 (standardized α = 0.929; N = 22). Item-total correlations varied between 0.193 and 0.821, and removing any individual item did not significantly enhance α (range if deleted: 0.919–0.930). Hotelling’s T-squared analysis confirmed multivariate homogeneity (T²=58.03, F (21,15) = 1.18, P = 0.374), thereby supporting the one-dimensionality of the scale.

The test-retest reliability, assessed using the two-way mixed consistency intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC), produced an average-measures ICC of 0.927 (95% CI: 0.888–0.958) and a single-measure ICC of 0.367 (95% CI: 0.265–0.507), with both results achieving statistical significance (P < 0.001). These findings suggest excellent stability of the overall scale across different administrations.

The analysis of the ASBQ-P items, encompassing 35 items across both functional and social domains, yielded a high Cronbach’s α of 0.936 (α standardized = 0.938; N = 35), thereby confirming the reliability of the Persian version of the questionnaire. (Table 2)

Discussion

The successful cross-cultural adaptation and psychometric validation of the Persian version of the Anterior Skull Base Questionnaire (ASBQ-P) represent a critical milestone for measuring the quality of life (QoL) among Persian patients undergoing endoscopic skull base surgery. This study demonstrated excellent internal consistency (Cronbach’s α = 0.936) and good test-retest reliability (ICC = 0.927). Strong evidence of convergent validity was demonstrated through a significant, moderate-negative correlation with the SNOT-22 score (r = − 0.544) and notable differences across various SNOT-22 severity categories, confirming good known-groups validity.

The results for internal consistency and reliability indicate that the ASBQ-P is both stable and reliable. The high Cronbach’s alpha aligns with figures reported in the initial ASBQ development by Gil et al., who established the instrument as a dependable measure of QoL in patients undergoing resection of skull base tumors4. Other studies, such as those by Castle-Kirszbaum et al. and McCoul et al., have yielded similarly high internal consistency, which enhances the stability and generalizability of the ASBQ across different languages15,16. The fact that the ASBQ-P is as reliable as the widely recognized and validated SNOT-22 is significant and underscores its practical application in both clinical and research environments.

The moderate negative association found between ASBQ-P and SNOT-22 outcomes further confirms the ASBQ-P’s convergent validity. As expected, this relationship arises because improved skull base-related QoL directly correlates with fewer sinonasal symptoms. This finding has been documented in studies where ASBQ was used alongside SNOT-22, highlighting the complementary nature of these two tools for assessing patient-reported outcomes for skull base surgery5,9.

The ASBQ-P differentiated between various severities of the QoL as defined through SNOT-22 categories and thereby supported its known-groups validity. Its utility for clinicians seeking to detect truly significant differences in postoperative outcomes and tailor interventions according to the patient’s profile of QoL has been highlighted through this aspect11,13.

A careful process of cultural adaptation was an essential aspect of this research, strictly following ISPOR guidelines for semantic, cultural, and conceptual equivalence. This stringent approach is supported by previous successful translations of QoL tools into Persian, such as the Persian SNOT-22, which also demonstrated good reliability and validity20. This adaptation underscores the practicality and importance of culturally adjusted QoL measures in clinical practice.

In this context, the present study is significant as it presents a validated and culturally specific questionnaire for Persians undergoing endoscopic skull base surgery. The ASBQ-P is a powerful tool for clinicians to assess surgical outcomes on multiple levels, providing insight into recovery trajectories and enabling more targeted patient-centered care. Furthermore, the ASBQ-P facilitates international collaboration to compare and contrast the experiences of multiethnic groups and different aspects of local surgical practices, thereby enriching knowledge through globalized research efforts14,24.

However, the original questionnaire itself hindered a challenge. For instance, one question is about patients’ relationships with their partners, but some patients in our dataset were single or widowed. Several approaches are suggested for this situation. Bell et al. proposed the so-called “Half-Rule” approach, which involves replacing the missing items with the mean of the answered items in the subscale if at least half of that subscale has been completed25. Similarly, Jacoby et al. recommended replacing the missing data with the mean personal score rather than more advanced techniques such as multiple imputation and Rasch modeling26. In cases when a large amount of data is missing due to the inapplicability of certain items, Sizmur et al. reported that the researchers eliminated the corresponding item and rescored the remaining items to preserve respondents’ data while avoiding the inflation of domains with irrelevant items27. In the present study, we redesigned the mentioned mate-related question by adding a sixth option, “I am single”, and assigned a score of zero to this answer. Considering the Likert-based nature of ASBQ28, we believe this approach would tackle the current issue regarding single individuals and should be considered as an alternative when assessing QoL in anterior skull-base surgeries.

Strength and limitations

The study has several notable advantages, including strict adherence to established cross-cultural adaptation guidelines, rigorous psychometric evaluations encompassing internal consistency, test-retest reliability, convergent validity, and known-groups validity, as well as the use of a well-established comparator tool (SNOT-22). Additionally, it addresses an unmet clinical need by focusing on a patient population often excluded from QoL evaluations as the target. However, some drawbacks can be outlined in the study. The relatively small sample size (N = 36) may limit the generalizability of the findings, suggesting that further validation in larger samples is now required to increase confidence in the psychometric robustness of the instrument. Moreover, as a single-center study (Shariati Hospital), the generalization of results may be limited to other centers or parts of Iran. Furthermore, the restriction to patients who underwent transnasal transsphenoidal surgery raises the question of the transferability of ASBQ-P to other types of skull base surgeries, which needs to be addressed further. In the absence of longitudinal data in this first validation, further studies are required to investigate long-term responsiveness to changes following surgery.

Conclusion

In conclusion, the ASBQ-P is a reliable and valid instrument for evaluating the quality of life (QoL) in the Persian population undergoing endoscopic anterior skull base surgery. Its implementation promises substantial improvements in clinical practice and facilitates impactful research, ultimately enhancing patient care and surgical outcomes in this patient population.

\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}

is
quasi-projective if and only
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Q}}
and
\alpha>0
.

2 –
If
\alpha\not\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,

\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha})=\{{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}}\}
.

3 –
If
\alpha\in{\mathbb{Q}}
,
\mathop{\rm Pic}\nolimits(\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha})\simeq{\mathbb{Z}}
.

We will first describe
H^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})
with usual Čech
cohomology, using the open cover
(W_{i})_{0\leq i\leq 3}
. We have
H^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}^{2}
, and it is generated by

\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1)))
and
\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2})}(-\widetilde{P})
(cf.
2.1
).
With respect to
(W_{i})_{0\leq i\leq 3}
,

–

\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1)))
is represented by the cocycle

(u_{ij})
, with

u_{01}\ =\ -\frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\ ,\quad u_{12}\ =\ -\frac{d\mu}{\mu}\ ,\quad u_{23}\ =\ 0\ .

–

\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}(-\widetilde{P}))
is represented by
the cocycle
(v_{ij})
, with

v_{01}\ =\ 0\ ,\quad v_{12}\ =\ \frac{d\mu}{\mu}\ ,\quad v_{23}\ =\ \frac{d\lambda}{\lambda}\ .

For the canonical product
H^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})\times H^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})\to H^{2}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})\simeq{\mathbb{C}}
, we have

\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1)))^{2}\ =\ 1\ ,\quad\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}(-\widetilde{P}))^{2}\ =\ -1\ ,\quad\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1))).\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}(-\widetilde{P}))\ =\ 0\ .

From
7.4
, the scheme
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
is defined, with respect to

(W_{i})_{0\leq i\leq 3}
, by the family
(E_{ij})
of derivations, with

E_{01}\ =\ -\lambda^{2}\mu^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ ,\qquad E_{12}\ =\ \mu(1-\alpha)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\ ,\qquad E_{23}\ =\ -\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\ .

This family defines also
\sigma_{\alpha}\in H^{1}(\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2},\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}})
(which defines
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}

according to
3.2
), in the sense of
2.2
.

7.6.2
.

Lemma:
We have
\sigma_{\alpha}=\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1)))+\alpha\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}(-\widetilde{P}))
.

Proof.

On the open subsets
W_{i}
we have the following trivializations
\alpha_{i}:\omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}|W_{i}}\to{\mathcal{O}}_{W_{i}}
of
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}
:

\alpha_{0}:d\Big(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{0}}\Big)\wedge d\Big(\frac{x_{1}}{x_{0}}\Big)\mapsto 1\ ,\quad\alpha_{1}:d\Big(\frac{x_{0}}{x_{1}}\Big)\wedge d\Big(\frac{x_{2}}{x_{1}}\Big)\mapsto 1\ ,\quad\alpha_{2}:d\lambda\wedge d\mu\mapsto 1\ ,\quad\alpha_{3}:d\nu\wedge d\eta\mapsto 1\ ,

and, using the variables
\lambda
,
\mu
:

\alpha_{0}^{-1}:1\mapsto-\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}\mu^{2}}d\lambda\wedge d\mu\ ,\ \alpha_{1}^{-1}:1\mapsto-\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}d\lambda\wedge d\mu\ ,\ \alpha_{2}^{-1}:1\mapsto d\lambda\wedge d\mu\ ,\ \alpha_{3}^{-1}:1\mapsto-\frac{1}{\lambda}d\lambda\wedge d\mu\ .

In usual Čech cohomology,
\sigma_{\alpha}
is defined by
E^{\prime}_{ij}=\alpha_{i}^{-1}E_{ij}
:

E^{\prime}_{01}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\lambda^{3}\mu^{2}}.d\lambda\wedge d\mu\otimes(-\lambda^{2}\mu^{2}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu})\ ,\quad E_{12}\ =\ -\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}.d\lambda\wedge d\mu\otimes\mu(1-\alpha)\frac{\partial}{\partial\lambda}\ ,

E_{23}\ =\ d\lambda\wedge d\mu\otimes\big(-\frac{\alpha}{\lambda}\frac{\partial}{\partial\mu}\big)\ .

Now we apply the canonical isomorphism
\omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}\otimes T_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}\simeq\Omega_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}
and we get

E_{01}\ =-\frac{1}{\lambda}.d\lambda\ ,\quad E_{12}\ =\ \frac{\alpha-1}{\mu}.d\mu\ ,\quad E_{23}\ =\ \frac{\alpha}{\lambda}.d\lambda\ .

The result follows from the preceding description of

\nabla_{0}(\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(1)))
and
\nabla_{0}({\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2})}(-\widetilde{P})
.
∎

Now we prove theorem
7.6.1
. A line bundle
{\mathbb{F}}
on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}

is ample if and only if
{\mathbb{F}}_{|\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}
is ample (by
[
18
]
,
proposition 4.2). Hence
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
is quasi-projective if and only
if some ample line bundle on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}
can be extended to a line bundle to

\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
.

Let
F
be a line bundle on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}
. According to
3.3
,
F
can
be extended to a line bundle on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
if and only if
\sigma_{\alpha}.\nabla_{0}(F)=0
. Let
F_{mn}=\pi^{*}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}_{2}}(m))\otimes{\mathcal{O}}_{\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}(-n\widetilde{P})
. Then we have
\sigma_{\alpha}.\nabla_{0}(F_{mn})=m-n\alpha
.

The ample line bundles on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}
are the
F_{mn}
such that
m,n>0
. It follows that
\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
is quasi-projective if and
only if there exist positive integers
m
,
n
such that
m-n\alpha=0
.
This proves
1
.

According to
[
11
]
, theorem 1.2.1, if
{\mathbb{F}},{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
are line bundles on

\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
, then
{\mathbb{F}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}
if and only if
{\mathbb{F}}_{|\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}\simeq{\mathbb{F}}^{\prime}_{|\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}}
. In other words, two extensions to

\widetilde{{\mathbb{X}}}_{1,\alpha}
of a line bundle on
\widetilde{{\mathbb{P}}}_{2}
are isomorphic. This
implies
2
and
3
.

Carbon fixation performance and the stability surroundings of Serratia sp. isolated from karst
Authors: Xian Hu, Yuheng Chen, Yaya Yuan, Chunxiang Qian
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractCarbon exists in many forms in nature, among which carbonate and carbon dioxide (CO2) are the most important forms and play an important role in the global carbon cycle. Microorganisms play an important role in the material and energy cycles in karst systems. Lots of CO2-fixing bacterial existed in the karst region due to its special ecological environment. However, very limited attention has been given to the karst region with natural advantage, as a possible source for isolation of carbonic anhydrase (CA) bacteria. In this work, we found that the karst region was pregnant with bacteria belonging to different genera able to induce CaCO3 precipitation. 15 strains of high yield of CA enzyme activity (> 200 U/L) were isolated from karst soil, and Serratia sp. identified by 16 S rDNA sequencing was the highest enzyme production. The optimal culture conditions of Serratia sp. growth were 20°C and pH 9, and the CA activity was 2795.84 U/L. Ca2+, Mg2+, Fe2+ and Cl− showed the promoted effect on the growth and the CA activity of Serratia sp., however, Fe2+ and OH− had inhibited the growth and the CA activity on high concentrations. The carbon conversion of Serratia sp. was 39.51 mmol/L on 20°C and pH 9, and produced 3.518 g/L CaCO3, indicating that it showed a good CO2 chelation capacity than other strains. This study clarified the application environment of Serratia sp, indicating that the CA bacteria from karst region have broad application prospects in CO2 capture, utilization and storage.Keywords: Carbonic anhydrase, Serratia sp., Carbon conversion, VateriteSubject terms: Biotechnology, Environmental sciences, Microbiology

Introduction

Carbon exists in many forms in nature, carbonate and carbon dioxide (CO2) are the most important forms and play an important role in the global carbon cycle. However, excessive CO₂ emission have become a global urgent environmental issue. In this context, carbon sequestration technology has attracted more and more attention as an effective strategy to reduce the concentration of CO₂ in the atmosphere. CO2 sequestration can be achieved through adsorption, cryogenic technology, membrane separation, and geological sequestration, however, these traditional technologies will face environmental and economic challenges1. The microbial‑induced carbonate precipitation showed demonstrated potential for enhancing CO2 sequestration in the field of carbon sequestration of waste slag powder2, CO2 geological storage3, and CO2 storage in saline aquifers4 due to the environmentally friendly, efficient, and cost-effective method. CaCO3 induced by microbial divided into two categories: urease catalyzed the hydrolysis of urea and carbonic anhydrase (CA) catalyzed the conversion of carbon dioxide. The urease reaction will release ammonia gas, as is potentially harmful to the environment5, however, the CA catalytic process is safe, and the conversion rate is up 107 s− 1 which is one of the fastest existing enzymes to catalyze the hydration of CO2 6. Microbial CA catalyze reversible CO2 hydration to forms metal carbonates, and has been widely applied for the CO2capture, utilization and storage and great potential for development5–8.

Karst ecosystem is an important part of the earth’s surface system and plays a significant role on global and regional climate and environmental changes, which has a rich variety of microbial communities that is widely involved in the biogeochemical cycling of elements9,10. Tang et al.11 reported that bacterial isolated from dolomite and limestone rocks in Guizhou karst area mainly included Proteobacteria, Actinobacteria, Firmicutes and Bacteroidetes, and Proteobacteria was the first dominant group. Microorganisms play an important role in the material and energy cycles in karst systems10. Abundant CO2-fixing bacterial communities indicated that the karst groundwater in Huixian had a high carbon fixation potential12. Lange-Enyedi et al.13 reported that the three quarters of the isolated bacterial strains from the Csodabogyós Cave and the Baradla Cave samples had CaCO3 precipitating capacity in their mucoid colonies through ammonia production. Autotrophic CO2 fixing bacterial communities are important part of CO2 fixation in aquatic environments. Therefore, lots of CO2-fixing bacteria exist in the karst region due to its special ecological environment. Karst soils generally have higher concentrations of soil exchangeable calcium ions Ca2+ and pH values comparing with non-karst soils, which promote the changes in microbial communities in both soil types14. Cheng et al.12 found that the distribution of Candidatus Nitrososphaera, Candidatus Nitrocosmicus, Thaumarchaeota Group 1.1c, and Pseudonocardiaceae were correlated with pH, Ca2+, and NO2− in a karst ecosystem. However, very limited attention has been given to the karst region with natural advantage as a possible source for isolation of CA bacteria.

Environmental conditions, as salinity, nutrients, heavy metals, pH value and temperature, not only affect the distribution of bacteria, but also significant affect the growth of bacteria and enzyme activity. Ai et al.15 authenticated that the distribution of most bacteria in the cave were positively correlated with F−, Cl−, nitrate and SO42−. However, optimizing culture conditions will improve the production and activity of CA. Li et al.16 reported that self-healing was the most effective on 30°C and pH 8.0–11.0. The pH value and temperature showed significant effect on the carbon fixation and activity of microorganisms, as well as the carbon fixation and activity in steel slag and concrete17,18. The biomineralization reactions would been directly influenced by the change of pH and temperature fluctuations19,20. Zheng & Qian21 found that the high alkalinity and harsh physical and chemical environment of the cement-based materials will inhibit CA enzyme activity. Nowadays, salinity, pH and temperature are the main factors affecting microbial mineralization technology. Therefore, it is necessary to dig and find strains with strong enzyme activity and strong adaptability to promote the popularization and application of microbial mineralization technology in high temperature and strong alkaline environment.

Serratia, as a genus of facultative anaerobic Gram-negative bacteria, was reported for the potential of CO2 sequestration22–24. However, systematic studies on the environmental adaptability, CA activity, and carbon sequestration performance of Serratia sp. still limited. The environmental factors including pH and temperature have great influence on the CA produced and activity of microorganisms on the application process17,18. Therefore, the purpose of this study is to clarify the stability and mineralization performance of Serratia sp. which has isolated and identified the strains from Karst soil sample. This investigation aimed (1) to evaluate the effects of pH value and temperatures for the growth and activity and CO2 hydration capacity of the CA of Serratia sp.; (2) to investigate the effects of anions and cations for the activity and CO2 hydration capacity of the CA of Serratia sp.; and (3) to detect its ability for the conversion of carbon dioxide and the type of mineralization. This study indicated that karst region is an important source of CA-producing bacteria, and the CA bacteria from karst region have broad application prospects in CO2 capture, utilization and storage.

Results

Screening of carbonic anhydrase strains

The microorganism of soil samples was isolated, and tested the activity of carbonic anhydrase at different temperatures and pH values (Fig. 1). The number of CA producing strains at 20℃ and 35℃ with pH 7 was relatively abundant comparing with 10℃ and 50℃, which were 47 and 56, respectively. The number of CA-producing strains at 10℃, 20℃ and 35℃ with pH 9 were relatively abundant comparing with 50℃. There were 15 strains of high yield of CA enzyme activity (> 200 U/L), and the maximum CA enzyme yield was 1034.24 U/L at 20℃ with pH 7, as was used for further study.

Morphology and molecular biological identification of Serratia Sp

Microorganism formed the circular colonies on the plate, which was white and translucent, and showed glossy and smooth surface (Fig. 2A). The colony of microorganism was median, rise, a bulge and neat edges. The microscopic morphology of microorganisms was a flat stone-like body which was about 1 μm in diameter and rounded at the end (Fig. 2B and C).

The genomic DNA of bacteria were extracted, and 16 S rDNA amplification performed to identify bacteria isolates. The DNA band was single and the molecular weight is about 1500 bp. The sequences of the isolates were compared with existing bacterial sequences in the database using ClustalW software, and their similarity values were determined. The presumptive relationships of these sequences were obtained from database comparison. According to this analyze, the similarity of the isolates’ 16 s rDNA sequences of the highest enzyme activity bacteria was more than 95% when compared to those of data presented in the dataset. It was possible to assign all the isolates to a species. 16 S rDNA gene sequencing revealed that bacteria represented Serratia sp. (PV982387.1) (Fig. 2D).

Effects of environmental factors on bacterial growth and carbonic anhydrase yield

Effect of pH and temperature on Serratia sp. growth

The impact of pH and temperature on bacterial population growth at 10°C, 20°C, 35°C and 50°C with pH 5, 7, 9, 11 was initially investigated (Fig. 3). The OD600 value of Serratia sp. solution significantly increased before 72 h, however, the growth slowed down with the increase of culture time after 72 h on 10°C, 20°C and 35℃ with pH 5, 7, 9 (Fig. 3A, B and C). However, the OD600 value of Serratia sp. was low on 10°C with pH 11, indicating that strongly alkaline inhibited the growth of the strain (Fig. 3A). The OD600 value of Serratia sp. solution significantly increased after 96 h and 36 h with pH 11, respectively, however, the growth trend achieved balance in a short period of time on 20℃ and 35℃ (Fig. 3B and C). However, the variation of OD600 value of Serratia sp. solution did not show significantly change with different pH at 50℃, indicating that high temperature inhibited the bacterial growth (Fig. 3D). There were also significantly more live bacteria with pH 7 and 9 than that of pH 5 and 11, and with pH 7 and 9 at 20°C than that at 35°C and 50°C (Fig. 3E).

CA activity of Serratia spp. At different pH and temperature

The impact of pH and temperature on CA activity of Serratia sp. were investigated on 10°C, 20°C, 35°C and 50°C with pH 5, 7, 9, 11 (Fig. 4). The CA activity of Serratia sp. significantly increased from 0 to 132 h on 10°C and 0–24 h on 20°C on pH 5, 7 and 9, respectively, however, the CA activity showed a downward trend after cultured 132 h and 108 h. The CA activity of Serratia sp. peaked at 2795.84 U/L after cultured 24 h on 20℃ with pH 9 (Fig. 4A and B). The CA activity peaked at the highest value with pH 11 on 20℃ after 48 h, thus declined and reached a stable level (Fig. 4B), the change trend was the same as that of 35℃, the CA activity of Serratia sp. reached the highest level on cultured 12 h, and then decreased to reach equilibrium (Fig. 4C). However, the CA activity of Serratia sp. was inhibited on 50℃ with different pH, indicating that high temperature would inhibit the CA activity of bacteria (Fig. 4D).

Carbon fixation performance of Serratia Sp

The carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp. appraised under different temperature and pH value were showed in Fig. 7A. The carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp. showed significant higher with pH 9 on 20°C, 35°C and 50°C than that of pH 5, 7 and 11 on same temperature (p < 0.05). The carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp. was higher at 20°C with pH 9 than that of other temperature and pH value, which was 39.51 mmol/L and produced 3.518 g/L CaCO3 (Fig. 7B). High temperature or strong alkali inhibited the carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp.

The SEM analysis revealed the spherical aggregates formed by the agglomeration of nanoparticles with a particle size of about 5 μm (Fig. 7C). In addition, for the FTIR spectrum of CaCO3 particles of the mineralized product of Serratia sp., the absorption peaks located at 1402.47 and 876.01 cm− 1, as were related to the asymmetric stretching vibration and out-of-plane bending vibration of carbonate ion, indicating the presence of CO32– 26. The peaks at 745.35 cm− 1 corresponded to in plane bending vibration of O − C−O in vaterite, meaning the crystalline phases among CaCO3 (Fig. 7F)27.

Discussion

Effect of CA bacteria from different environment on promoting microbial mineralization

CA-producing microorganisms are used for carbon sequestration and carbonate precipitation through their metabolism. Microbial mineralization would accelerate CO2 hydration and induce CaCO3 precipitation, which was an efficient and green MICP approach that has been widely used in crack self-healing7,28, soil cementation29, carbon fixation30, and the carbonation of steel slag powder31. Therefore, the mining of microbial resources has become an important factor to apply microbial mineralization for carbon sequestration, concrete repair and steel slag carbon sequestration. In this study, we found that CA activity of Serratia sp. peaked at 2795.84 U/L after 24 h of cultivation at 20℃ with pH 9, confirmed that Serratia sp. has the strong CA activity (Table 1). Serratia sp. (No. PV982387.1) isolated from karst landform soil, the same as Chen et al.22, who reported that S. marcescens Wy064 and Bacillus sp. Wy065 isolated from karst landform soil, which CA1 and CA3 of S. marcescens Wy064 belonged to the Type II β-CA. Vincent et al.32 reported that 14 biocalcifying marine bacteria were isolated and identified from electrochemically formed calcareous deposits and their immediate environment, and Virgibacillus halodenitrificans CD6 showed the most efficient precipitation of CaCO3. The search for CA-producing microorganisms adapted to extreme conditions has become one of the main measures for microbial mineralization development and utilization for particular conditions in nature. Abdelsamad et al.33 reported that the bacterium Virgibacillus were isolated from a hypersaline coastal, which promoted magnesium incorporation into the carbonate minerals to form high magnesium calcite. Giri et al.34 found Citrobacter freundii isolated from limestone rock site in Kumaun region of Indian Himalaya, which showed good CA activity at pH 7.0 and 37°C. Capasso et al.35 found a new CA from S. yellowstonense YO3AOP1 that showed high thermal stability and kept full activity after 2 h incubation at 100°C. Bansal et al.36 reported that halophilic bacteria Exiguobacterium mexicanum was isolated from sea water, which showed high activity of CA production under salt stress conditions, and used for improving the strength and durability properties of building structures in marine environments. Park et al.37 isolated and identified alkali-tolerant and halotolerant Sporosarcina sp. HY008 and Bacillus sp. JH7 with CaCO3 precipitation abilities. Therefore, the mining of CA-producing microorganisms under some extreme conditions is helpful to the popularization and application of microbial mineralization technology.

Effects of temperature and pH on microbial growth and enzyme activity

The environmental adaptability of CA-producing bacteria was crucial for their practical application, pH delaying sporulation and reducing sporulation yield, as well as reduced microbial metabolism to decrease CA yield21,42. Baril et al.42 reported that optimum spore formation of B. weihenstephanensis and B. licheniformis was at 30 °C and 45°C pH 7.2, respectively. In our study, the optimum temperatures and pH of Serratia sp. growth was at 20°C pH 9, which exhibited high CA activity and strong mineralization capacity, particularly under alkaline and moderately culture temperature conditions. As was consistent with the findings of Vaghela et al.43, who reported Bacillus sp. KhSb-159 still grew normally at an alkaline pH 9, even in the presence of 1 M NaCl (w/v). B. sphaericus had a good tolerance with a broad range of alkaline pH, and pH 7–9 was the optimal pH, however, pH 10–11 will inhibit the growth and germination, furthermore, B. sphaericus spores and ureolytic activity were inhibited at 10°C44. Temperature, pH value, salinity etc., will influence the growth and phenotypes of microorganisms, as generally interact with each other, and combine their effects in complex, non-additive ways45. Lower temperatures and pH had inhibited the sporulation process, as B. velezensis and B. amyloliquefaciens growth were inhibited in low pH46. Serratia sp. CA activity was lower at 10°C pH 11 than that of CA activity at 20°C pH 9, this result showed that low/high temperatures and high pH will inhibit CA activity, while pH and temperature had a marked effect on Serratia sp. activity which was consistently linked with CA activity. Bhattacharya et al.47 reported that CA from Micrococcus lylae kept 80% stability in the pH 6.0–8.0 and 35–45°C, respectively. However, CA from M. luteus was stable in the pH 7.5–10.0 and 35–55°C. de Souza et al.48 found that temperature had a significant positive effect on transglutaminase production, and pH and temperature had a significant effect on spore formation in cells, which was consistent with the production of TGase. However, CA-producing microorganisms was used for carbon fixation and the carbonation need to face some extreme environmental conditions, such as strong acid or strong alkali, high salt and high/low temperature7,44,49,50. CA activity of Serratia sp. was significantly higher on 10℃ than that of 35℃ and 50℃, indicating that it can be applied at lower temperatures.

Effects of ions on microbial CA activity and growth

Metals are essential for all forms of life, which played structural and catalytic roles in precursor biosynthesis, DNA replication, transcription, respiration, and responses to oxidative stress51. Ca2+ and Mg2+ in bacterial cell structure played an importance role and nutrients required by bacteria for growth and cell maintenance, including chemotaxis, cell motility, cytogenesis, enzyme functionality, cell signalling and pathogenesis52–54. Li and Ma55 reported that Ca²+ and Fe²+ can accelerate Lactococcus lactis growth, Cu2+ inhibited growth, whereas, Mg2+ showed the inhibitory effect on a low concentration, but stimulated growth at a high concentration. Ca2+, Mg2+ and Fe2+ (0.1 g/L and 0.5 g/L) had promoted on Serratia spp. growth in our study. However, 0.8 g/L and 1 g/L Fe2+ showed an inhibitory effect on the CA activity of Serratia sp. This result was same as Sharma et al.56, who reported that Zn2+, Fe3+, Cd2+, Na2+, K+, Mg2+ and Ca2+ significantly enhanced the CA activity of Pseudomonas fragi, while Hg2+ and Pb2+ had inhibitory effects. In addition, Kucuk and Gulcin57 found that Fe2+ inhibited the CA enzyme activities of Black Sea trout kidney tissue. Vincent et al.32 reported that cells survive by allowing Ca2+ to enter and accumulate under adverse conditions caused by high Ca2+ concentrations, which led to excessive proton excretion. Then, the cell actively expels Ca2+ and compensates for the loss of protons.

Cl− had promoted on the CA activity of Serratia sp., however, SO42− and OH− (0.1 and 0.5 g/L) showed no significant effect on the CA activity of Serratia sp. than that of 0 g/L. 0.8 g/L and 1 g/L OH− had an inhibitory effect on the CA activity of Serratia sp. Sharma et al.56 found that SO42− and Cl− had an inhibitory effect on P. fragi CA activity. Orhan et al.58 reported that Cl−, and SO42− showed moderate inhibitory activities of α-class carbonic from Chromohalobacter sp. Anions usually complex with the zinc atom of the active site of the CA enzymes by displaced the water molecule or hydroxide ion, which inhibited the CA activity58.

Effects of microorganisms on production and morphology of CaCO3 in mineralization

Microbial CA was attracted the attention of researchers because of its low bioprocess cost, environment-friendly nature, and produce no secondary byproducts, which has become an exciting tool to convert atmospheric CO259. CA was catalyzed the reversible hydration of carbon dioxide to form HCO3−, which can be mineralized by calcium ions into CaCO3, which served as a raw material in construction materials and the steel industry30,31,60. Therefore, bacteria had strong carbon sequestration ability which had become one of the important ways to promote its use. Chen et al.38 found that the CA activity of strain SCSIO19859 was 1.50 U/mL, which produced 0.70 g/day of CaCO3. The carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp. was 39.51 mmol/L and produced 3.518 g/L of CaCO3 under optimal conditions (20 °C, pH 9) (Table 1). 30°C and pH 6–7 were the optimal culture conditions of C. gambrini, and the final production of biotic CaCO3 increase 36% to reach 2.884 g/L by the introduction of CO2 for 15 min daily39. Therefore, the result confirmed that Serratia sp. was useful in carbon sequestration and carbonate precipitation. The strain BS19, as isolated from bamboo (Bambusa vulgaris) rhizosphere, produced 214.21 IU/mL of CA enzyme at pH 7.0 and 28°C and formed the well-defined rhombohedral calcite crystals in the process of mineral carbonation reaction61.

Bacterial mineralization can produce various forms of CaCO3 in different environments, such as calcite, aragonite, and vaterite62. However, calcite and vaterite are the most common forms of CaCO363,64. Tepe et al.62 reported that B. amyloliquefaciens U17 formed rhombohedral vaterite and layered calcite crystals in the process of mineral carbonation reaction. However, Lv et al.65 found that Lysinibacillus sp. strain GW-2 can formed vaterite with spherical morphology from an amorphous calcium carbonate precursor. Serratia sp. formed vaterite in the process of mineral carbonation reaction, which had a unique hollow, porous spherical structure, good biocompatibility and safety, and uniform particle distribution compared with calcite and serpentine66,67, which was widely used in cosmetics, biomedicine, photocatalysis and other high value-added fields.

Conclusion

In this paper, 15 strains of high yield of CA enzyme activity (> 200 U/L) were isolated. 16 S rRNA gene sequencing revealed that bacteria represented Serratia sp., which formed the circular colonies on the plate with white and translucent. The CA activity of Serratia sp. was 2795.84 U/L on pH 9 and 20°C than that of other conditions. While high pH and temperature had inhibited the growth and the CA activity of Serratia sp. Ca2+ and Mg2+ showed the promoted effect on the growth and the CA activity of Serratia sp. While Fe2+ had promoted on the growth and the CA activity of Serratia sp. on the low concentrations, however, Fe2+ had inhibited it on high concentrations. Cl− showed a promoting effect on the growth and the CA activity of Serratia sp. However, 0.8 g/L and 1 g/L OH− had an inhibitory effect on the CA activity of Serratia sp. on high concentrations. The carbon conversion capacity of Serratia sp. was higher at 20°C and pH 9 than that of other temperature and pH value, which was 39.51 mmol/L, which produced 3.518 g/L CaCO3 and formed vaterite in the process of mineral carbonation reaction. Therefore, this result demonstrated that Serratia sp. possessed significant potential for application in CO₂ mineralization and biomaterial production under alkaline and moderately high-temperature environments.

Methods

Chemical

1000 mL LB medium: 10 g peptone, 5 g yeast extract, 10 g NaCl, 1000 mL deionized 15 g agar. 0.2 mol/L; phosphate buffer (pH = 7.5): 19 mL Na2HPO4 (0.0312 g/mL) and 81 mL (0.0356 g/mL) NaH2PO4; 3 mmol/L 4-Nitrophenyl acetate: 13.6 mg 4-Nitrophenyl acetate into 1 mL acetone, then made up to 25 mL with water. CaCl2, MgCl2, FeCl2, NaCl, Mg(OH)2, MgSO4, Na2HPO4, NaH2PO4, 4-nitrophenyl acetate, hydrochloric acid, sodium hydroxide, agar and yeast extract purchased from Sinopharm Chemical Reagent Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China).

Microorganism sample information

Bacterial strains were isolated from soil sample collected from Guizhou (E109.33, N27.75), Ningxia (E106.10, N38.39), Yunnan (E102.54, N23.37), Sichuan (E105.27, N31.16), Guangxi (E108.10, N23.71 and E108.10, N24.35), Shandong (E119.53, N37.28), Nanhai (E112.23, N19.34), and Jiangsu (E118.87, N31.89) in China.

Isolation of microorganism

1% soil samples was added into the different pH LB medium and cultured for 3 d under 10℃, 20℃, 35℃, 50℃ at 170 rpm, respectively. The pH of LB medium was regulated on pH 5, 7, 9, and 11 by used 0.01 mol/L hydrochloric acid and 0.01 mol/L sodium hydroxide. The culture medium was diluted 10− 1−10− 6, and 100 µL diluted solution was spread on LB plate, which was inverted and cultured on 35℃ for 12–24 h. According to the morphology and characteristics of the colonies, different types of single colonies were selected and inoculated onto the LB medium for three times by purification drawing culture. Finally, the single strain was obtained by shaking in the LB liquid medium.

Carbonic anhydrase enzyme activity assay

1 mL of acetic acid p-nitrobenzene ester solution and 1.9 mL of phosphate buffer was mixed into the centrifuge tube, and finally added 0.1 mL of the bacterial suspension. The mixed solution reacted at room temperature for 5 min, then measured the absorbance at 400 nm. The control sample is deionized water. Enzyme activity is defined as: one unit of enzyme activity is defined as the amount of enzyme that hydrolyzes 1 µmol of acetic acid p-nitrobenzene ester solution per minute. 0.1 mL of the bacterial suspension was incubated on the different pH, temperature, cations and anions, which taken every 12 h before 72 h and every 24 h after 72 h to measure carbonic anhydrase enzyme activity.

Bacterial identification

Bacterial DNA was extracted using a DNA kit (Sangon Shanghai). The ITS region of genomic DNA was amplified by universal primer PCR, synthesized by sangon Shanghai Biological Engineering Co., LTD. The sequences of the universal primers 27 F and 1492R used for amplifying bacterial ribosomal genes are as follows: 27 F:5AGAGTTTGATCCTGGCTCAG-3’, 1492R:5’-GGTTACCTTGTTACGACTT-3. The final volume for PCR amplification is 50 µL, including 25 µL 2xPCRmaster, 23 µL ion water, 0.5 µL primer 1 and 0.5 µL primer 2, and 1 µL of DNA (provided by Shanghai sangon Biological Engineering Co., Ltd.). The amplification is performed on a PCR instrument with the following conditions: 95℃ pre-denaturation for 5 min; 95℃ denaturation for 30 s; 57℃ annealing for 45 s; 72℃ extension for 1 min, with a total of 35 cycles. 1% agarose and 3% 4 S green plus was mixed in 1x TAE buffer, and make glue by heated with a microwave oven. 7 µL of the amplification sample and DNA Marker was added into the spotting wells, and ran on a 120 V electrophoresis for 40 min. The strip is single and the molecular weight is about 1500 bp, which can be sent to sequenced using the Sanger method by Sangon Biotech Co. Ltd. (Shanghai, China) and compared on NCBI website (https://blast.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Blast.cgi) to determine the closely related species of the isolated strain. The 16 S rDNA gene sequences were compared with those in Genbank using Blast software and were constructed the phylogenetic trees depicting the relationships among the related bacteria by the neighbor-joining method.

The morphology and microstructure of Serratia Sp

The single strain Serratia sp. was obtained by shaking in the LB liquid medium, and were placed in LB plates, and incubated in the growth chamber (35 ± 1 °C) for 12 h, then observed by Laser confocal microscope (Nikon C2+, Japan) and scanning electron microscopy (SEM) (FEI 3D, Japan).

Biological characteristics of Serratia Sp

Growth curves of Serratia sp. on different pH and temperature

Serratia sp. was inoculated in 100 mL LB medium at different pH values (pH 5, 7, 9, 11) and a constant-temperature incubator (10°C, 20°C, 35°C, 50°C, 170 r/min). Samples were taken every 12 h before 72 h and every 24 h after 72 h to measure absorbance at 600 nm using a Multi-Functional Microplate Reader (BIO-RAD, USA). Growth curves were plotted to assess the effect of pH and temperature.

Growth curves of Serratia sp. on different cations and anions

Serratia sp. was inoculated in 100 mL LB medium with different cations (0.01 mol/L Ca2+, Mg2+, and Fe2+) and anions (0.01 mol/L Cl−, SO42−, OH−) and incubated on 35°C and 170 r/min. Samples were taken every 12 h before 72 h and every 24 h after 72 h to measure absorbance at 600 nm using a Multi-Functional Microplate Reader. Growth curves were plotted to assess the effect of cations and anions.

Morphological analysis and determination of dead and living bacteria

Bacterial morphology was observed using scanning electron microscopy (SEM), which method was the same as Wang et al.68. Sample was washed with 1% Phosphate Buffer Saline (PBS), and fixed with polyformaldehyde, then subjected to ethanol gradient dehydration, followed by gold sputtering using a high-resolution sputter coater before SEM analysis.

Fluorescein diacetate (FDA) was used to stain live cells, and propidium iodide (PI) was used for dead cells. Every 24 h, 0.5 mL of Serratia sp. culture grown under different pH and temperature conditions was stained with 0.008 mL PI and 0.01 mL FDA for 15 min, followed by centrifugation. After dilution, live and dead cells were observed using a laser confocal microscope (Nikon Ti2-u, Japan) with red and green fluorescence channels.

Determination of carbon sequestration capacity of Serratia Sp

Mineralization properties of Serratia Sp

The bacterial suspension was added 40 mmol/L LB with CaCl2, as the amount of bacterial solution was 1%, incubated on different temperatures and pH on 170 r/min for 5 d, and then centrifuged and collected the precipitate, washed with deionized water and ethanol 3 times, respectively, then dry it. The CaCO3 products were measured on the optimal carbon conversion conditions by TG test, which was referred to Vincent et al.32. The products of HCO3−and CO32− were measured by titration, which is referred to Zheng and Qian7. Deionized water was replaced bacterial suspension to add LB as a blank control. The experiment was repeated three times. Then the HCO3− and CO32− concentrations were calculated according to the following Eqs. (1) and (2).

V1: the volume of titrate with standard HCl solution until the red color just disappeared; V2 the volume of HCl as the bacterial liquid solution turned from yellow to jacinth.

Characterization of mineralized products

Serratia sp. was inoculated at 1% in the CaCl2 LB medium containing 40 mmol/L, and cultured for 5 d under the condition of 35°C and pH 9. The precipitate was collected and washed with deionized water and ethanol for 3 times respectively, then washing and drying the precipitate obtained. The surface morphology and chemical composition of mineralized product was characterized by a scanning electron microscopy and energy dispersive X-ray spectrometer (SEM, FEI 3D, Japan). The crystal structure of mineralized product was observed using a powder X-ray diffraction (XRD) measurement (Buker D8-Discover, USA) with Cu Kα radiation (λ = 0.15418 nm) in a 2θ range of 3–100o. The Fourier transform infrared (FT-IR) spectra of surface chemical bonds were recorded on a Bruker Vertex 70 spectrometer (Thermo Scientific, USA) with a scanning range of 400–4000 cm− 1.

Experimental data analysis

This data of growth and CA activity on different pH, temperature, different cations and anions, and carbon fixation performance of Serratia sp. are presented as the mean ± SE (n = 3). One-way ANOVA with Duncan’s multiple comparison method was used to carried out the result of carbonic anhydrase activity of Serratia sp. on different cations and anions and carbon fixation performance of Serratia sp. (p < 0.05).

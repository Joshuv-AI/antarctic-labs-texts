Non-closed scalar charge in 4-dimensional Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet black hole thermodynamics
Romina Ballesteros, Marcela Cárdenas, Eric Lescano

Abstract
We develop a covariant differential-form framework to define scalar charges for stationary, asymptotically flat black holes in 4 4 –dimensional Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity with a general scalar coupling function. Contracting the scalar field equation of motion with the horizon generator k k yields a non-closed-form scalar charge, revealing a bulk contribution encoded in a 3 3 –form, which measures the obstruction to its closedness. In the presence of shift-symmetry, this obstruction vanishes and the 2 2 –form scalar charge satisfies a Gauss law, depending solely on boundary data. Geometrically, this reproduces known topological results in the shift-symmetric limit. This framework allows us to analyze the role of the non-closed scalar charges in black hole thermodynamics through the Smarr formula for more general couplings and provide a covariant, charge-based interpretation of the spontaneous scalarization mechanism, showing how the behavior of the scalar charge and the bulk term capture the instability of scalar-free black holes and the emergence of scalar hair. Our results offer a unified geometric understanding of the role of scalar charges and the mechanism of spontaneous scalarization in Einstein-scalar-Gauss-Bonnet gravity.

In
4

Once this coupling is introduced, it alters the dynamics and can induce scalar hair in black hole solutions, even in the absence of electric charge or angular momentum. f(\phi)

In parallel to these developments, it has been shown that a covariant definition of scalar charge enables the formulation of no-hair theorems by requiring regularity both at spatial infinity and on the bifurcation surface in asymptotically flat black holes
Ballesteros:2023iqb
;
Ballesteros:2023muf
. The key insight is to define the scalar charge through a closed
2
–form
\mathbf{Q}
. When the
4
–dimensional theory admits a shift symmetry from a real scalar, one defines a
3
–form current
\mathbf{J}
associated with this symmetry and writes

in terms of the scalar equation of motion
\mathbf{E}_{\phi}
, i.e.,
d\mathbf{J}=-\mathbf{E}_{\phi}
. The
2
–form scalar charge is then obtained by contracting this relation with a Killing vector
k

k
(the Lie derivative of the scalar with respect to
k

2
–form charge is guaranteed to be closed on-shell. A similar procedure can be applied in theories where the shift symmetry of a real scalar is broken by a scalar potential. In this case, the requirement that the scalar potential is invariant under the diffeomorphisms generated by
k
is the condition that ensures that the
2
–form scalar charge is, again, closed on-shell.

This construction ensures that the
2
–form scalar charge satisfies a Gauss law: it can be integrated over any closed
2
–surface and yields the same result when evaluated either at spatial infinity or on the bifurcation surface. The definition is manifestly covariant and offers several advantages, such as allowing for a direct connection with the charges obtained in the Wald formalism and yielding a more natural appearance in the first law.

For generic couplings in EsGB theories, the scalar sector does not have a shift symmetry. Although one might attempt to enforce the closedness of the scalar charge by treating
f(\phi)\mathcal{G}
as an effective potential, this is not appropriate since
f(\phi)\mathcal{G}
is a curvature coupling rather than a function of
\phi
alone. Throughout this work, we keep
f(\phi)
arbitrary and deal with the scalar contribution in the
2
–form scalar charge (obtained after the contraction of the scalar equation of motion with
k
) expressing it as an exact differential form plus a bulk remainder
\mathcal{W}_{k}
. This decomposition reveals a covariant
3
–form
\mathcal{W}_{k}
that quantifies the obstruction to the closedness of the scalar charge
2
–form. We refer to this general object as the non-closed-form scalar charge.

In the particular case of shift symmetry the obstruction term vanishes. Otherwise, the scalar charge ceases to be purely topological and can be interpreted using the framework of the spontaneous scalarization mechanism, as we will show. Although for generic couplings the
2
–form scalar charge is not closed on-shell, one can still formulate no-hair statements by requiring the convergence of the corresponding surface and bulk integrals at spatial infinity.

In addition, our framework naturally connects to the phenomenon of spontaneous scalarization. This occurs when black hole solutions with nontrivial scalar profiles emerge dynamically, even in the absence of external sources, and arises when the scalar-free solution becomes unstable under small scalar perturbations triggering the growth of a nontrivial scalar configuration. Spontaneous scalarization provides a mechanism for generating scalar hair without violating no-hair theorems, relying instead on a dynamical instability of the trivial scalar configuration. The process leads to genuinely non-perturbative black hole solutions and offers a framework for probing deviations from general relativity in the strong-field regime, potentially observable in gravitational wave signals.

The scalarization can be interpreted in terms of the asymptotic behavior of the scalar charge, through the conditions encoded in the obstruction term
{\cal W}_{k}
. For backgrounds satisfying
\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})=0
but
\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi_{\infty})>0
, linear perturbations generate a nonzero contribution to the scalar charge via
{\cal W}_{k}
, signaling the breakdown of the scalar-free configuration and the emergence of scalar hair. This provides a covariant and geometric interpretation of the mechanism, unifying it with the thermodynamic and charge-based analysis developed in this work. Our results clarify the structure of scalar charges in EsGB gravity and offer a systematic way to test the physical consistency of different coupling functions. They also strengthen the foundation for covariant no-hair theorems and have potential applications in string-inspired black hole models and higher-curvature gravitational theories.

In this work we construct the covariant non-closed-form scalar charge in EsGB gravity and show that, for generic coupling functions it acquires a bulk contribution given by a
3
–form
\mathcal{W}_{k}
that measures the failure of its closedness. We analyze the associated surface and volume integrals and prove that the charge remains convergent for a wide class of coupling functions, including linear, quadratic, exponential and polynomial forms. In the shift symmetric limit,
\mathcal{W}_{k}
vanishes and the
2
–form scalar charge becomes closed on-shell satisfying a Gauss law. For general couplings it contributes to the non-closedness of the generalized Komar charge, forming part of an additional bulk term that appears in the Smarr relation. Remarkably, for the specific dilatonic coupling
f(\phi)=e^{-\phi}
, the generalized Komar charge becomes closed on-shell and the bulk contribution disappears. Finally, we show that the obstruction term
\mathcal{W}_{k}
provides a geometric origin for spontaneous scalarization in black holes, linking the onset of scalar hair to the behavior of the scalar charge itself.

Correspondingly, the asymptotic behavior of the first derivative of the function

Evaluating

\displaystyle\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\mathbf{Q}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{4\pi}\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\left(\imath_{k}\star d\phi-2\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)P_{k}^{ab}\widetilde{R}_{ab}\right)

(5.9)

\displaystyle=D+\frac{4\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})M^{2}}{r^{3}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-4}).

(5.10)

If we now analyze the second term in (

(5.12)

\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})=\text{const.},

On the other hand, evaluating

Then,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{B}H}\mathbf{Q}

\displaystyle=\frac{2\alpha^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int_{\mathcal{B}H}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\,P_{k}^{ab}\,\widetilde{R}_{ab}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{\prime}\,\kappa\,\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{h}).

(5.15)

Now, following Eq. (
4.15

\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi)=\sum_{k=2}^{\infty}\frac{f^{(k)}(\phi_{\infty})}{(k-2)!}\left(\phi-\phi_{\infty}\right)^{(k-2)},

(5.16)

to compute the contribution from the 3-form
\mathcal{W}_{k}

The analysis of the asymptotic behavior of each factor in the integrand is done considering a static, spherically symmetric black hole,

\displaystyle\partial_{r}\phi

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{\Sigma}{r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})

\displaystyle e^{(A-B)/2}

\displaystyle\sim 1-\frac{2M}{r}+\frac{\Sigma^{2}}{8r^{2}}+\mathcal{O}(r^{-3})

\displaystyle A^{\prime}(r)

\displaystyle(1-e^{-B})

in Eq. (

-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{4\pi}\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{W}_{k}=-\alpha^{\prime}\int_{r_{h}}^{\infty}\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi_{\infty})\cdot\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})dr.

The integrand in the last equation behaves as

6
Generalized Komar charge and Smarr formula

The Smarr formula was first derived as a consequence of the homogeneity of the entropy and Euler’s theorem
Smarr:1972kt
, relating black hole thermodynamic quantities measured at spatial infinity and at the horizon. Komar integrals
Komar:1958wp
have been demonstrated to be useful for deriving the Smarr formula for stationary black holes
Kastor:2010gq
. Following the framework presented in Ref.
Liberati:2015xcp
, one can construct the Komar integrals within the covariant formalism
Lee:1990nz
;
Wald:1993nt
;
Iyer:1994ys
, resulting in an expression that combines surface and volume integrals. The analysis made in Ref.
Liberati:2015xcp
is applied in Lovelock theories with constant couplings for static black holes. Additionally, in Ref.
Ortin:2021ade
is shown that for Lovelock and
f(R)
theories, the volume integral can always be rearranged as a surface term, as a consequence of the construction of a closed Komar charge. It yields in a Smarr formula purely from surface integrals. Let us comment on the procedure: one starts computing the generalized
2
–form Komar charge, which is by definition
Ortin:2021ade

\mathbf{K}[k]\equiv-\mathbf{Q}[k]+\omega_{k},\qquad\imath_{k}\mathbf{L}\big|_{\text{on-shell}}\equiv d\omega_{k}.

(6.1)

Here
\mathbf{Q}[k]
is the Noether-Wald charge evaluated in a Killing vector
k
and the
2
–form
\omega_{k}
is defined implicitly by taking the interior derivative with the Lagrangian evaluated on-shell. When it is closed on-shell, i.e.,
d\mathbf{K}[k]=0
, the Smarr formula is obtained integrating over a spacelike hypersurface
\Sigma^{3}
whose boundaries are

\partial\Sigma^{3}=S^{2}_{\infty}\cup\mathcal{BH}
(a
2
–sphere at spatial infinity and the bifurcation surface).

After using Stokes’ theorem we obtain

\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\mathbf{K}[k]=\int_{\mathcal{BH}}\mathbf{K}[k]
,
where
k
is the Killing vector generating the horizon. In pure Einstein theory for stationary asymptotically flat spacetimes, the left-hand side is evaluated using the asymptotic form of the metric yielding the ADM mass
M
. The right-hand side is computed at the bifurcation surface,
where
k=0
and
P_{k\,ab}=\kappa\,n_{ab}
. After taking into account the generalized zeroth law
Bardeen:1973gs
, the right-hand side results in the temperature
T
multiplied by the entropy
S
. Equating both integrals results in the usual Smarr formula
M=2TS
.

Contrary to Einstein theory and those considered in Ref.
Ortin:2021ade
, in EsGB a bulk piece remains since the coupling
f(\phi)
multiplies the Euler density and it cannot be reformulated as a surface term. Indeed, the term
\imath_{k}\mathbf{L}\big|_{\text{on-shell}}
is the responsible of the non-closedness of the Komar charge. By consequence, an unavoidable volume term will appear in the Smarr formula. In this section we will derive the Smarr formula for stationary, asymptotically flat black holes in EsGB gravity following the mentioned approaches.

We first take the trace of the Einstein equation, isolate the Lagrangian and evaluate it on-shell to take the interior derivative in order to write it as a total derivative plus bulk contributions,

\displaystyle e^{a}\wedge\mathbf{E}_{a}

\displaystyle=-2\mathbf{L}+2\alpha^{\prime}f(\phi)\mathcal{G}+d\left(e^{a}\wedge\Delta_{a}^{GB}\right)+\mathbf{E}_{\phi}+d\star d\phi-\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\mathcal{G},

(6.2)

\displaystyle\imath_{k}\mathbf{L}

\displaystyle\dot{=}\alpha^{\prime}f(\phi)d\mathcal{X}_{k}-\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)d\mathcal{X}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}d\imath_{k}\left(e^{a}\wedge\Delta_{a}^{GB}\right)-\frac{1}{2}d\imath_{k}\star d\phi

(6.3)

\displaystyle=d\Big[\alpha^{\prime}\big(f(\phi)-\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\big)\mathcal{X}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}\imath_{k}\left(e^{a}\wedge\Delta_{a}^{GB}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\imath_{k}\star d\phi\Big]-\alpha^{\prime}(df(\phi)\wedge\mathcal{X}+\mathcal{W}_{k}).

(6.4)

With the Noether-Wald charge in Eq. (
3.4
) the exterior derivative of the generalized Komar charge is given by

\displaystyle d\mathbf{K}[k]

\displaystyle=d\Big(-\star(e^{a}\wedge e^{b})P_{kab}+2\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\widetilde{R}_{ab}P_{k}^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{a}k^{a}+e^{a}\wedge\imath_{k}\Delta_{a}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\imath_{k}\star d\phi\Big)

\displaystyle=-2\alpha^{\prime}d\big(f(\phi)+\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\big)\wedge P_{k}^{ab}\widetilde{R}_{ab}.

(6.5)

If we define

\mathcal{Y}\equiv df(\phi)\wedge\mathcal{X}_{k},\quad\mathcal{Z}_{k}=\mathcal{W}_{k}+\mathcal{Y}_{k},

(6.6)

then Eq. (
6
) reads

d\mathbf{K}[k]=\alpha^{\prime}\mathcal{Z}_{k}.

(6.7)

Integrating
d\mathbf{K}[k]
over a
\Sigma^{3}
hypersurface whose boundaries are the bifurcation surface and a
2
–sphere at spatial infinity and using Stokes’ theorem, the Smarr formula with a bulk contribution reads

\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\mathbf{K}[k]=\int_{\mathcal{BH}}\mathbf{K}[k]-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{\Sigma}\mathbf{\mathcal{Z}}_{k},

(6.8)

where the generalized Komar charge (recovering the global factor
(16\pi G_{N})^{-1}
) is

\mathbf{K}[k]=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\Big(-\star(e^{a}\wedge e^{b})P_{kab}+2\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\widetilde{R}_{ab}P_{k}^{ab}+\frac{1}{2}\left(\Delta_{a}k^{a}+e^{a}\wedge\imath_{k}\Delta_{a}\right)-\frac{1}{2}\imath_{k}\star d\phi\Big).

(6.9)

Remarkably, from Eq. (
6.8
) when

f(\phi)=-\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)

(6.10)

there is no bulk contribution (
\mathcal{Z}_{k}=0
) and the generalized Komar charge is closed. This is the particular case for EdGB theories with the exponential coupling

f(\phi)=e^{-\phi}.

The integral at spatial infinity gives

\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\mathbf{K}[k]=\frac{M}{2}+\frac{\Sigma}{8},

(6.11)

where
M
is the ADM mass and
\Sigma
is the scalar charge. On the other hand, the integral over the bifurcation surface

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{BH}}\mathbf{K}[k]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{\mathcal{BH}}\Big(-\star\left(e^{a}\wedge e^{b}\right)P_{kab}+2\alpha^{\prime}\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\widetilde{R}_{ab}P_{k}^{ab}\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{\kappa}{2\pi}\frac{\mathcal{A}}{4G_{N}}+\frac{1}{16\pi G_{N}}2\alpha^{\prime}\kappa\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{h}),

(6.12)

with
\mathcal{A}
denoting the area of the event horizon. The second term in the right-hand side of the last equation, corresponds to the scalar contribution shown in Eq. (
5.15
) and it must not be understood as part of the Wald entropy.

The Wald entropy
S
for EsGB reads

S=\frac{1}{4G_{N}}\Big(\mathcal{A}+2\alpha^{\prime}f(\phi_{h})\Big),

(6.13)

Note that this entropy formula is given by the integral on the bifurcation surface of the Lorentz charge Eq. (
3.3
) for the parameter
\sigma_{ab}=n_{ab}
.

Now let us compute the volume integral in Eq. (
6.8
) and analyze its convergence

\displaystyle-\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{16\pi G_{N}}\int_{\Sigma}\mathcal{Z}_{k}

\displaystyle=2\alpha^{\prime}\int_{rh}^{\infty}\partial_{r}\phi\Big(\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi)+\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\Big)r^{2}dr

(6.14)

\displaystyle=\alpha^{\prime}\int_{rh}^{\infty}\partial_{r}\phi\Big(\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi)+\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\Big)A^{\prime}e^{(A-B)/2}(1-e^{-B})dr

(6.15)

\displaystyle=-\alpha^{\prime}\int_{rh}^{\infty}\Big(\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi_{\infty})+\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})\Big)\cdot\mathcal{O}(r^{-5}).

(6.16)

Since its fall-off is at order

, this integral is convergent for any coupling
f(\phi)
provided its smoothness near
\phi_{\infty}
.

We may define the conjugate thermodynamical potential to
\alpha^{\prime}
in order to present the Smarr formula in a more familiar way

In the context of EsGB theories, it is assumed the existence of a constant scalar background
\phi=\phi_{\infty}
.

\displaystyle=\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})+\epsilon\,\delta\phi^{(1)}\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi_{\infty})+\mathcal{O}(\epsilon^{2}).

2.

9
Conclusions

In this work we have presented a covariant and systematic analysis of the thermodynamical charges of stationary, asymptotically flat black holes in Einstein–scalar–Gauss–Bonnet gravity with a general scalar coupling function
f(\phi)
. The covariant Wald formalism allowed us to systematically track the contributions to the conserved charges arising from both local Lorentz invariance and diffeomorphism invariance, as well as from the non-minimal scalar–curvature coupling. A key element of our analysis has been the computation of the non-closed-form scalar charge associated with the Killing generator
k
of the horizon. Using the scalar field equation of motion contracted with
k
, we constructed the non-closed-form scalar charge
\mathbf{Q}[k]
in Eq. (
4.15
). The covariance of the charge is provided by the momentum map and parallels the Noether–Wald definition of gravitational charges.

In doing so, we uncovered a bulk term given by a
3
–form
\mathcal{W}_{k}
, which measures the failure of the non-closed-form scalar charge to be closed on-shell for general couplings given by an arbitrary
f(\phi)
. The condition
\mathcal{W}_{k}=0
emerges as the necessary and sufficient criterion for the
2
–form scalar charge to satisfy a Gauss law and be defined purely by boundary data. Physically,
\mathcal{W}_{k}
encodes the coupling between gradients of the scalar field and the topological Gauss–Bonnet term. Its vanishing corresponds to the particular case of linear coupling,
f(\phi)=\phi
, in which the
2
–form scalar charge is closed and can be fully expressed in terms of the Euler characteristic of the bifurcation surface. For more general coupling functions
f(\phi)
, the obstruction term
\mathcal{W}_{k}
does not vanish and depends on the full radial profile of the fields. Thus, it captures genuinely dynamical information beyond the purely topological and geometric data of the boundaries.

In evaluating the surface integrals that define the non-closed-form scalar charge, we observe that when evaluated at spatial infinity it remains finite for a broad class of coupling functions. The asymptotic expansion of the integrand at infinity shows that the integrand decays at least as
\Sigma+\frac{\partial_{\phi}f(\phi_{\infty})}{\mathcal{O}(r^{3})}
, ensuring its convergence and resulting in
\int_{S^{2}_{\infty}}\mathbf{Q}=\Sigma
. When evaluated at the bifurcation surface, the charge depends only on the horizon values of the scalar field and the surface gravity, yielding a finite result. Regarding the convergence of the bulk contribution, we find that the
3
–form
\mathcal{W}k
decays at infinity as
\mathcal{O}(r^{-5})
for any smooth coupling
f(\phi)
near
\phi_{\infty}
. This guarantees that the volume integral in Eq. (
4.15
) is absolutely convergent, even when
\mathcal{W}_{k}\neq 0
. Hence, the scalar charge remains regular across the full spacetime, including configurations that explicitly break shift symmetry. This regularity provides an explanation for the wide variety of coupling functions
f(\phi)
for which regular black hole solutions have been found in the literature, as the decay properties of
\mathcal{W}_{k}
ensure the finiteness of the scalar charge irrespective of the specific form of the coupling.

It is worth noting that our construction of the non-closed-form scalar charge differs from the one presented in Ref.
Ortin:2024emt
for the particular coupling
f(\phi)=e^{-\phi}
and in absence of the axion field. There, the authors promote the constant coupling
\alpha^{\prime}
to a field
\ell_{s}(x)
and adding a
3

C
enforcing the constraint
d\ell_{s}=0
, thereby endowing the theory with global symmetries whose Noether
3
–currents are closed on-shell. Then, in a stationary background the
2
–form scalar charge is closed on-shell since it is defined as the contraction of a Killing vector
k
of the global Noether current, and there is no bulk contribution. By contrast, in this work we follow a procedure based on the equations of motion: contracting the scalar equation of motion with
\imath_{k}
and reorganizing terms yields on-shell
d\mathbf{Q}[k]=\mathcal{W}_{k}
, with
\mathcal{W}_{k}=-2\,d\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)\wedge P^{ab}_{k}\,\tilde{R}_{ab}
, which makes manifest the gravitational scalar source that appears in scalarized black hole models and reproducing Prabhu’s observation in Ref.
Prabhu:2018aun
that is, without shift symmetry, a bulk term remains.

Regarding the generalized Komar charge for EsGB theories, we observed that in general this charge will not be closed. The Smarr formula then acquires a volume integral contribution, consisting in the sum of the bulk term
\mathcal{W}_{k}
together with another term
\mathcal{Y}_{k}
, both proportional to the exterior derivative of the coupling function. In the particular case for
f(\phi)=e^{-\phi}
, the generalized Komar charge will be closed on-shell and will result in a Smarr formula fully defined by surface integrals.

As we pointed out in the introduction, the theories under consideration depend explicitly on the dimensionful parameter
\alpha^{\prime}
. Therefore, in order to write a consistent Smarr formula and first law, one has to introduce a conjugate thermodynamical potential

f(\phi)
itself, whereas the Noether–Wald charge depends on its derivative
\partial_{\phi}f(\phi)
. The inclusion of
\Phi_{\alpha^{\prime}}
is then mandatory in order to recover the standard form of the Smarr formula in Eq. (
6

6.19

\alpha^{\prime}
.

Since our non-closed scalar charge was constructed from the equations of motion, it will have implications for the spontaneous scalarization mechanism. Within our framework, these conditions have a natural charge-based interpretation: the background scalar charge vanishes at zeroth order, but first-order perturbations generate a nontrivial scalar charge
\Sigma
. Crucially, the volume contribution from
\mathcal{W}_{k}
becomes linear in
\partial_{\phi}^{2}f(\phi_{\infty})
and the scalar perturbation, providing a covariant, geometric measure of the instability. In this sense, the onset of scalarization can be viewed as the dynamical generation of scalar charge via a non-vanishing
\mathcal{W}_{k}

It would be natural to extend the present analysis to include spin, where additional contributions to the Smarr formula will appear from rotation and where the structure of
\mathcal{W}_{k}
may be richer. Another interesting direction is to study the near-horizon geometry in the presence of
\mathcal{W}_{k}\neq 0
, to see whether any universal relations emerge despite the loss of scalar charge conservation. Finally, exploring the holographic interpretation of
\mathcal{W}_{k}
in asymptotically AdS spacetimes could shed light on the dual field theory meaning of the obstruction term and its thermodynamic consequences.

Appendix B
Lie-Lorentz derivative and momentum map

The momentum map consists of a modification of the Lie derivative operator with respect to other symmetry of the theory
Elgood:2020svt
. In this work, we particularly focus on the covariant expression with respect to Lorentz transformations so we will review it in a pedagogical way, and in index notation.

B.1
Scalar transformation

A generic scalar under diffeomorphisms and invariant under Lorentz transformations,
v
, transforms with respect to the Lie derivative in the following way

{\mathcal{L}}_{\xi}v=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}v+\omega\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\rho}v\,,

(B.1)

where
\omega
is a density weight. Since
v
is invariant under Lorentz,
L_{\xi}v
is also invariant and the momentum map is not required in this case.

B.2
Vector transformation

A generic vector under both diffeomorphisms and Lorentz transformations,
v_{\mu a}
, transforms with respect to the Lie derivative in the following way

\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v_{\mu a}=\xi^{\rho}\partial_{\rho}v_{\mu a}+\partial_{\mu}x^{\nu}v_{\nu a}+\omega\partial_{\rho}\xi^{\rho}v_{\mu a}\,,

(B.2)

Since
v_{\mu a}
is a vector under Lorentz transformations, then

\delta_{\sigma}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v_{\mu a})=\sigma_{a}{}^{b}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v_{\mu b})+\xi^{\rho}(\partial_{\rho}\sigma_{a}{}^{b})v_{\mu b}\,.

(B.3)

The last term of the previous transformation is non-covariant and therefore we introduce the momentum map
P_{\xi ab}
such that

\delta_{\sigma}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v_{\mu a}-\xi^{\rho}\omega_{\rho a}{}^{b}v_{\mu b})=\sigma_{a}{}^{b}(\mathcal{L}_{\xi}v_{\mu b})

(B.4)

where in this case
P_{ab}=-\xi^{\rho}\omega_{\rho a}{}^{b}
ensures that the non-covariant piece of the transformation cancels. Similarly, one can perform this procedure to any higher-order rank tensor.

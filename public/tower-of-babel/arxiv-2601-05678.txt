Canonical Lattices and Integer Relations Associated to Rational Fans
Rizwan Jahangir

Abstract
. We propose a canonical local-to-global lattice theory for rational fans. We define the ray lattice L rays ​ ( Σ ) L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma) and the relation lattice L rel ​ ( Σ ) L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma) as invariants functorial under fan isomorphisms. We introduce star-local relation lattices , defined via the relation lattice of the localized quotient fan, which capture the linear dependencies visible within local neighborhoods. We define a codimension filtration on the global relation lattice and prove a generation theorem: the global lattice is generated by local relations supported on the stars of cones of codimension at least 1. This filtration is sensitive to the facial structure of Σ \Sigma ; explicit examples and a conjecture suggest that subdivisions can only preserve or lower filtration depths, distinguishing fans with different combinatorial topologies.

1.
Introduction

Toric geometry relies heavily on the combinatorial data of a rational fan
\Sigma
in a lattice
N
. Standard computational approaches often depend on extrinsic choices, such as a fixed basis for
N
or a specific ordering of the rays. In computational applications, such as the search for minimal relations, this dependence can obscure intrinsic geometric properties. While the geometry of a toric variety is determined locally by affine charts (cones) glued together, the standard description of the integer relations among rays, specifically the kernel of the ray matrix, is inherently global. To our knowledge, there is no systematic framework in the literature that decomposes this global lattice into local sub-lattices associated with the fan’s topology in the manner pursued here.

In this paper, our primary aim is to develop a theory of integer lattices that is canonical in a rigorous categorical sense. By canonical, we mean that our constructions are invariant under the automorphisms of the lattice preserving the fan. For example, if
\Sigma
is acted upon by a lattice automorphism, the filtration levels defined in this paper are preserved. Furthermore, under a refinement (subdivision) of the fan, the structures behave monotonically, allowing for stability analysis in resolution of singularities.

Our framework fits into the landscape of combinatorial algebraic geometry but distinctively differs from existing structures. The Divisor Class Group, described by the exact sequence
0\to M\to\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\to\text{Cl}(X)\to 0
, captures global relations
[
3
,
5
]
, but it does not explain how these relations are assembled from local geometric data. Similarly, while the lattice ideal
I_{\Sigma}
encodes integer relations
[
4
]
, standard Gröbner basis theory treats the rays as a vector configuration, often ignoring the fan’s facial structure. Matroid theory captures linear dependencies via circuits
[
8
]
, but standard matroid theory "forgets" the face poset structure of the fan, failing to distinguish between a "local" relation supported on a cone’s star and a "global" relation spanning topologically disjoint cones.

The goal here is not to replace these theories, but to supply a localization principle for relation lattices that is invisible to purely global constructions. This philosophy builds directly on the local-to-global constructions developed for intersection cohomology in
[
1
]
and
[
2
]
.

Our main contributions are fourfold. First, we establish the ray lattice
L_{\mathrm{rays}}
and relation lattice
L_{\mathrm{rel}}
as functors on the category of rational fans (under isomorphisms). Second, we define star-local lattices via the quotient fan, providing an intrinsic geometric definition. Third, we introduce a canonical filtration
F_{k}L_{\mathrm{rel}}
based on the codimension of supporting stars. Finally, we establish that the global relation lattice is generated by proper stars (Theorem
4.2
). We note that this paper focuses on structural properties; we do not discuss algorithms for computing short vector bases (SVP) or Markov bases, nor do we address the computational complexity of determining the filtration levels for high-dimensional fans.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section 2 defines the category of rational fans and the local geometric operations. Section 3 constructs the global ray and relation lattices. Section 4 introduces the core novelty: the intrinsic star-local lattices and the codimension filtration, culminating in the Local-to-Global Generation Theorem. Section 5 discusses the simplified structure in the simplicial case and functoriality under refinements, and Section 6 provides illustrative examples.

2.
Preliminaries and Notation

We begin by establishing the precise categorical and geometric foundations required to define our invariants. Unless otherwise stated, all fans
\Sigma
are assumed to be
rational
,
strongly convex
, and finite. The underlying lattice
N
is a free abelian group of rank
n
. We generally assume fans are essential (not contained in a subspace), though the theory adapts to the general case.

To make our canonical claims precise, we define the category
\mathbf{RatFan}
. The objects of
\mathbf{RatFan}
are pairs
(N,\Sigma)
, where
N
is a free abelian group of finite rank and
\Sigma
is a rational fan in
N_{\mathbb{R}}
. A morphism
\phi:(N,\Sigma)\to(N^{\prime},\Sigma^{\prime})
is a group homomorphism
\phi:N\to N^{\prime}
such that for every cone
\sigma\in\Sigma
, there exists a cone
\sigma^{\prime}\in\Sigma^{\prime}
with
\phi_{\mathbb{R}}(\sigma)\subseteq\sigma^{\prime}
. See
[
5
, Ch. 1]
for a standard treatment of fan morphisms. In this paper, functoriality is verified primarily for isomorphisms and for subdivisions (refinements) within a fixed lattice. For general morphisms in
\mathbf{RatFan}
, additional care is required because rays may map to non-primitive vectors or collapse to the origin.

The combinatorial topology of a fan is captured by the stars of its cones. For
\tau\in\Sigma
, the
star
is
\mathrm{Star}(\tau)=\{\sigma\in\Sigma\mid\tau\preceq\sigma\}
. Stars encode the local face structure around
\tau
. Our local relation lattices will be defined using the geometry of these stars. We let
\Sigma(1)_{\tau}
denote the set of rays contained in cones of
\mathrm{Star}(\tau)
. We denote the codimension of a cone by
\operatorname{codim}(\tau)=n-\dim(\tau)
. The construction of quotient fans associated to stars is standard; see
[
5
, Section 3.1]
or
[
6
, Section 1.3]
.

3.
The Ray and Relation Lattices

This section defines the global lattice invariants and establishes their basic properties. We define the
ray lattice
as the sublattice of
N
generated by the primitive vectors of the rays:

L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma):=\langle v_{\rho}\mid\rho\in\Sigma(1)\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq N.

The index
[N:L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma)]
measures the failure of the rays to generate the lattice integrally. This index is typically nontrivial even for complete fans (e.g., in weighted projective spaces), distinguishing
L_{\mathrm{rays}}
from the ambient lattice
N
; see
[
5
, Example 1.2.6]
or
[
6
, Section 2.2]
.

Lemma 3.1

(Rank Bound)
.

\operatorname{rank}(L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma))\leq\operatorname{rank}(N)
. Equality holds if
\Sigma
is a complete fan.

Proof.

The inequality is immediate as
L_{\mathrm{rays}}\subseteq N
. If
\Sigma
is complete, the cones cover
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, so the rays must span
N_{\mathbb{R}}
as a vector space. Since
N
is torsion-free, the rank of the sublattice equals
n
.
∎

Next, let
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}
be the free abelian group on the set of rays. The
relation lattice
is defined by the exact sequence:

0\longrightarrow L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\xrightarrow{\;V\;}L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow 0

where
V(e_{\rho})=v_{\rho}
. This definition corresponds to the lattice of relations defining the associated toric ideal
I_{\Sigma}

[
4
, Ch. 4]
. By definition,
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
depends only on the set of primitive ray generators.

Proposition 3.2

(Canonical Nature)
.

L_{\mathrm{rel}}
defines a functor from the category of rational fans (with isomorphisms) to the category of lattices.

This follows immediately from the naturality of the ray set construction under isomorphism.

4.
Local Decompositions and Intrinsic Structure

This section introduces the core novelty of the paper: a framework for decomposing global relations into intrinsic local components. We seek a definition of local relations that depends only on the local geometry of the fan at
\tau
. For a cone
\tau\in\Sigma
, let
N(\tau)=N/\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{Z}}(\tau\cap N)
be the quotient lattice. The
localized fan

\Sigma_{\tau}
in
N(\tau)_{\mathbb{R}}
is the collection of images of cones
\sigma\in\mathrm{Star}(\tau)
under the projection
N\to N(\tau)
(see
[
5
, Prop. 3.2.7]
). Note that
\Sigma_{\tau}
is a fan in the quotient space. The rays of
\Sigma_{\tau}
correspond bijectively to the rays of
\Sigma
that are in
\mathrm{Star}(\tau)
but not in
\tau
itself (images of
\tau
’s rays are 0).

We define the
intrinsic star-local relation lattice

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\tau)
as the relation lattice of the localized fan
\Sigma_{\tau}
:

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{rel}}(\tau):=L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma_{\tau}).

We explicitly define the sublattice of relations internal to
\tau
:

L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\tau):=\ker\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\tau(1)}\xrightarrow{V|_{\tau}}N\right).

We view
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\tau)
as a subgroup of
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
via the natural extension-by-zero map.

We organize these local contributions by codimension using the intrinsic local structures. First, we define the lattice of relations supported on the star. Let
\Sigma(1)_{\tau}
be the set of rays in
\mathrm{Star}(\tau)
. We define:

L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\tau)):=\ker\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)_{\tau}}\xrightarrow{V}N\right).

The inclusion
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)_{\tau}}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}
(by extending vectors with zeros for rays outside the star) induces a natural map
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\tau))\to L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
.

Definition 4.1

(Codimension Filtration)
.

For
0\leq k\leq n
, we define the filtration step
F_{k}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
to be the sublattice generated by relations supported on the stars of cones with
\operatorname{codim}(\tau)\leq k
, excluding the trivial cone
\{0\}
:

F_{k}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\Sigma\setminus\{0\}\\
\operatorname{codim}(\tau)\leq k\end{subarray}}\Im(L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\tau))\to L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)).

Remark 4.1
.

The image term consists of those global relations whose support is contained in the ray set
\Sigma(1)_{\tau}
(equivalently, relations supported on
\mathrm{Star}(\tau)
).

4.1.
A complete proof of Local Generation

We now give a complete proof of the Local Generation Theorem in the natural setting of complete fans.

Theorem 4.2

(Local Generation for complete fans)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a complete rational fan in a lattice
N
of rank
n
.
For each ray
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
, let
\Sigma(1)_{\rho}
denote the set of rays contained in cones of
\mathrm{Star}(\rho)
, and set

L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho)):=\ker\!\left(\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)_{\rho}}\xrightarrow{V}N\right),

where
V(e_{\rho^{\prime}})=v_{\rho^{\prime}}
and we view
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)_{\rho}}
as a subgroup of
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}
by extension by zero outside
\Sigma(1)_{\rho}
.
Then

L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)=\sum_{\rho\in\Sigma(1)}\Im\!\big(L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))\to L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\big).

In particular, with the codimension filtration from Definition
4.1
, we have

F_{n-1}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)=L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma),

hence a fortiori
F_{n}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)=L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
.

Proof.

Write
R:=\Sigma(1)
and let
m:=|R|
. Consider the short exact sequence defining the global relation lattice:

(1)

0\longrightarrow L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{R}\xrightarrow{V}L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma)\longrightarrow 0.

Since
\Sigma
is complete, the rays span
N_{\mathbb{R}}
, hence
\operatorname{rank}(L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma))=n
and
L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\Sigma)\subseteq N
has finite index; in particular (
1
) is exact as written.

Step 1: A Čech-type exact sequence on the free ray group.

For each ray
\rho\in R
, set
S_{\rho}:=\Sigma(1)_{\rho}\subseteq R
and denote
F_{\rho}:=\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{R}
.
Because
\Sigma
is complete, every maximal cone
\sigma\in\Sigma(n)
contains some ray
\rho
, and any ray
\rho^{\prime}\in R
lies in some maximal cone together with
\rho
along a chain of adjacent maximal cones through
\mathrm{Star}(\rho)
; in particular, the family
\{S_{\rho}\}_{\rho\in R}
covers
R
:

R=\bigcup_{\rho\in R}S_{\rho}.

Define the map

\Phi:\bigoplus_{\rho\in R}F_{\rho}\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{R}\quad\text{by}\quad\Phi\big((x_{\rho})_{\rho}\big)=\sum_{\rho}x_{\rho},

where each
x_{\rho}
is extended by zero outside
S_{\rho}
.
Since
\{S_{\rho}\}
covers
R
, the map
\Phi
is surjective.

Next, define

\Psi:\bigoplus_{\rho<\rho^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}\cap S_{\rho^{\prime}}}\longrightarrow\bigoplus_{\rho\in R}F_{\rho}

on each summand by sending
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}\cap S_{\rho^{\prime}}}
to the element which is
+y
in the
\rho
-slot and
-y
in the
\rho^{\prime}
-slot (and
0
elsewhere), again extended by zero.
Then
\Phi\circ\Psi=0
by construction.
Moreover, one checks directly (coordinatewise) that

(2)

\bigoplus_{\rho<\rho^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}\cap S_{\rho^{\prime}}}\xrightarrow{\ \Psi\ }\bigoplus_{\rho\in R}\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}}\xrightarrow{\ \Phi\ }\mathbb{Z}^{R}\to 0

is exact: surjectivity of
\Phi
is already shown, and if
(x_{\rho})_{\rho}\in\ker(\Phi)
, then for each basis vector
e_{\eta}
(for
\eta\in R
) the total coefficient of
e_{\eta}
in
\sum_{\rho}x_{\rho}
is
0
, which lets one solve for
(x_{\rho})_{\rho}
as a finite sum of pairwise differences supported on intersections
S_{\rho}\cap S_{\rho^{\prime}}
, i.e. an element of
\Im(\Psi)
.

Step 2: Restrict (
2
) to relation lattices.

For each ray
\rho
, consider the local exact sequence

(3)

0\longrightarrow L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))\longrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}}\xrightarrow{V}L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))\longrightarrow 0,

where
L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho)):=\langle v_{\rho^{\prime}}\mid\rho^{\prime}\in S_{\rho}\rangle_{\mathbb{Z}}\subseteq N
.
Exactness on the right holds by definition of
L_{\mathrm{rays}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))
.

Now take an arbitrary global relation
r\in L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\subseteq\mathbb{Z}^{R}
, so
V(r)=0
.
Choose
(x_{\rho})_{\rho}\in\bigoplus_{\rho}\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}}
with
\Phi((x_{\rho})_{\rho})=r
(possible since
\Phi
is surjective).
Applying
V
and using
V(r)=0
, we get

0=V(r)=V\!\left(\sum_{\rho}x_{\rho}\right)=\sum_{\rho}V(x_{\rho})\in N.

Thus the family
(V(x_{\rho}))_{\rho}
is a finite collection of vectors in
N
summing to
0
.

Step 3: Correct the
x_{\rho}
to become local relations.

We will modify
(x_{\rho})_{\rho}
without changing
\sum_{\rho}x_{\rho}=r
, so that each modified term lies in
\ker(V|\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}})=L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))
.

Using the exactness (
2
), any change of the form

(x_{\rho})_{\rho}\mapsto(x_{\rho})_{\rho}+\Psi(y)\quad\text{with}\quad y\in\bigoplus_{\rho<\rho^{\prime}}\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}\cap S_{\rho^{\prime}}}

does not change
\Phi((x_{\rho})_{\rho})=r
.
Moreover, applying
V
commutes with these inclusions, hence we can use such
\Psi(y)
-moves to transfer ray-coefficients between overlapping stars.

Fix an ordering of rays
R=\{\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{m}\}
and proceed inductively:
assume we have achieved
V(x_{\rho_{1}})=\cdots=V(x_{\rho_{k-1}})=0
.
Since
\sum_{i}V(x_{\rho_{i}})=0
, we have

V(x_{\rho_{k}})=-\sum_{i>k}V(x_{\rho_{i}}).

Because
\Sigma
is complete, the adjacency graph of stars is connected in the sense that for any two rays there exists a chain of rays with consecutive stars intersecting (this follows from strong connectivity of complete fans in codimension one, see standard references such as
[
5
, Ch. 3]
).
Choose a chain
\rho_{k}=\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{t}=\rho_{j}
linking
\rho_{k}
to some
\rho_{j}
with
j>k
.
Using
\Psi
-moves supported on
S_{\alpha_{\ell}}\cap S_{\alpha_{\ell+1}}
, we can shift a prescribed vector of ray-coefficients from the
\rho_{k}
-slot along the chain into the
\rho_{j}
-slot, without changing the total sum
r
.
In particular, we can shift the entire contribution
x_{\rho_{k}}
into later slots, replacing
x_{\rho_{k}}
by an element with
V(x_{\rho_{k}})=0
while keeping
\Phi((x_{\rho})_{\rho})=r
.

Carrying this out successively for
k=1,\dots,m
produces a decomposition

r=\sum_{\rho\in R}r_{\rho}\quad\text{with}\quad r_{\rho}\in\mathbb{Z}^{S_{\rho}}\ \text{and}\ V(r_{\rho})=0.

Hence each
r_{\rho}\in L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(\rho))
and we obtain the desired generation statement.
The filtration statement follows because stars of rays are stars of codimension
(n-1)
cones.
∎

5.
Structure and Functoriality

We now examine how this structure behaves under specific conditions and operations.

Conjecture 5.1

(Filtration monotonicity under refinement)
.

Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a subdivision of
\Sigma
in the same lattice
N
with
\Sigma(1)\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}(1)
, and let

j:L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma^{\prime})

be the natural injection induced by
\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma^{\prime}(1)}
.
For
0\neq r\in L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
define its
filtration depth
by

0pt_{\Sigma}(r):=\min\{k\mid r\in F_{k}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\}.

Then

0pt_{\Sigma^{\prime}}(j(r))\leq 0pt_{\Sigma}(r).

Equivalently, subdivision can only preserve or
lower
the minimal filtration level needed to express a relation using star-supported generators.

Next, we consider refinements. Let
\Sigma^{\prime}
be a subdivision of
\Sigma
in the same lattice
N
such that
\Sigma(1)\subseteq\Sigma^{\prime}(1)
.

Theorem 5.1
.

There exists a natural injection
\Psi:L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)\hookrightarrow L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma^{\prime})
.

Proof.

Define the inclusion map
j:\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma(1)}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{Z}^{\Sigma^{\prime}(1)}
by
j(e_{\rho})=e_{\rho}
for all
\rho\in\Sigma(1)
. Since the ray vectors
v_{\rho}
are identical in both fans for the shared rays, we have
V^{\prime}\circ j=V
. Thus, if
x\in\operatorname{ker}(V)
, then
j(x)\in\operatorname{ker}(V^{\prime})
, defining an injection of kernels.
∎

6.
Illustrative Examples and Comparison

6.1.
Example 1: The Projective Plane

Consider
N=\mathbb{Z}^{2}
. Let
\Sigma
be the fan for
\mathbb{P}^{2}
, with rays generated by
v_{1}=(1,0)
,
v_{2}=(0,1)
, and
v_{3}=(-1,-1)
.

•

Global:
The unique primitive relation is
(1,1,1)
, corresponding to
v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}=0
.

•

Local (
\tau=v_{1}
):
The star of ray
v_{1}
contains the cones
\sigma_{12}
and
\sigma_{13}
. The rays involved are
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
. Thus, the relation
(1,1,1)
is supported on
\mathrm{Star}(v_{1})
.

•

Result:
The global relation is generated at level
k=1
(codimension 1).

6.2.
Explicit example:
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
and a relation in
F_{2}\setminus F_{1}

Example 6.1

(
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
)
.

Let
N=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
and consider the five rays

v_{1}=(1,0,0),\quad v_{2}=(0,1,0),\quad v_{3}=(-1,-1,0),\quad v_{4}=(0,0,1),\quad v_{5}=(0,0,-1).

Let
\Sigma
be the complete simplicial fan whose maximal cones are

\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\rangle,\ \langle v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\rangle,\ \langle v_{3},v_{1},v_{4}\rangle,\quad\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{5}\rangle,\ \langle v_{2},v_{3},v_{5}\rangle,\ \langle v_{3},v_{1},v_{5}\rangle.

This is the product fan for
\mathbb{P}^{2}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
.

The global relation lattice
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)=\ker(\mathbb{Z}^{5}\xrightarrow{V}N)
has rank
2
and is generated by the two independent relations

r_{1}=(1,1,1,0,0)\quad\text{and}\quad r_{2}=(0,0,0,1,1),

corresponding to
v_{1}+v_{2}+v_{3}=0
and
v_{4}+v_{5}=0
.

We claim that
r_{1}\in F_{2}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
but
r_{1}\notin F_{1}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
.
Indeed,
\mathrm{Star}(v_{1})
contains the maximal cones
\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{4}\rangle
,
\langle v_{1},v_{3},v_{4}\rangle
,
\langle v_{1},v_{2},v_{5}\rangle
,
\langle v_{1},v_{3},v_{5}\rangle
,
so the ray set of
\mathrm{Star}(v_{1})
is
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4},v_{5}\}
.
In particular,
r_{1}
is supported on
\mathrm{Star}(v_{1})
, hence
r_{1}\in L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\mathrm{Star}(v_{1}))\subseteq F_{2}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
because
v_{1}
is a cone of dimension
1
, i.e. codimension
2
in dimension
3
.

On the other hand, any codimension-
1
cone in
\Sigma
is a
2
-dimensional cone of the form
\langle v_{i},v_{4}\rangle
or
\langle v_{i},v_{5}\rangle
with
i\in\{1,2,3\}
.
For example,
\mathrm{Star}(\langle v_{1},v_{4}\rangle)
involves rays
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{4}\}
but not
v_{5}
, and similarly
\mathrm{Star}(\langle v_{1},v_{5}\rangle)
involves
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},v_{5}\}
but not
v_{4}
.
Thus every codimension-
1
star omits either
v_{4}
or
v_{5}
, and no relation supported only on
\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\}
can be obtained from codimension-
1
stars alone.
Consequently
r_{1}\notin F_{1}L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
.

6.3.
Example 3: Nontrivial Filtration Sensitivity

Let
N=\mathbb{Z}^{3}
. Consider four rays
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
that form a circuit
u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}+u_{4}=0
, where any three are linearly independent.

(1)

Fan
\Sigma_{A}
(Tetrahedral):
Let the cones be the faces of a tetrahedron involving these rays. Then every subset of 3 rays forms a cone. The relation
u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}+u_{4}=0
is supported on the union of stars of these 3-cones (rays). However, there is no single 2-cone (codimension 1) containing all 4 rays. Thus, this relation might appear in
F_{2}
but not necessarily
F_{1}
.

(2)

Fan
\Sigma_{B}
(Pyramidal):
Suppose we introduce a new ray
u_{0}
and triangulate such that
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},u_{4}
are all neighbors of
u_{0}
. Then the relation is supported on
\mathrm{Star}(u_{0})
, which is codimension 0 (if
u_{0}
is a ray in 3D). This is trivial.

(3)

The Crucial Case:
Consider a fan
\Sigma_{C}
where
u_{1},u_{3}
are opposite in a quadrilateral cone
\sigma
which is subdivided by adding a diagonal. If the diagonal connects
u_{1},u_{3}
, then
u_{2}
and
u_{4}
are separated. A relation involving all four cannot be supported on the star of the diagonal ray (which only sees
u_{1},u_{3}
). It must be assembled from relations on the stars of the boundary rays.

This demonstrates that the filtration level reflects the "distance" between rays in the adjacency graph of the fan.

6.4.
Comparison with Known Invariants

•

Primitive Collections:
Batyrev’s primitive collections
[
7
]
define relations corresponding to minimal non-faces. Our
F_{1}
relations correspond to primitive collections of size 2 (adjacent rays) or 3 (around a dimension 1 edge in 3D). Higher filtration levels capture primitive collections that are "topologically large."

•

Castelnuovo-Mumford Regularity:
The filtration depth is analogous to the regularity of the toric ideal, measuring the "complexity" of the syzygies.

7.
Conclusion

We have presented a coordinate-free framework for the integer linear algebra of rational fans. The main theorem establishes that the global relation lattice
L_{\mathrm{rel}}(\Sigma)
is generated by intrinsic local relations supported on proper stars. The associated codimension filtration
F_{k}
provides a new structural invariant measuring the geometric complexity of relations. Future work will investigate the computational utility of this filtration for optimizing lattice reduction algorithms in toric settings.

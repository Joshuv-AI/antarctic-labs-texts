Amplified fourth moment of the Riemann zeta-function and applications
Hung M. Bui, Richard R. Hall and Martin Subira Jorge

Abstract
. The twisted fourth moment of the Riemann zeta-function was established by Hughes and Young [J. Reine Angew. Math. 641 (2010), 203–236] and later improved by Bettin, Bui, Li and Radziwiłł [J. Eur. Math. Soc. (JEMS) 22 (2020), 3953–3980]. In applications one would often like to take the Dirichlet polynomial to mimic either 1 / ζ r ​ ( s ) 1/\zeta^{r}(s) (a mollifier) or ζ ​ ( s ) r \zeta(s)^{r} (an amplifier) for some r > 0 r>0 . Previous known results include the mean value of the fourth power of ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) times the square or the fourth power of a mollifier, or the square of an amplifier. In this paper we obtain the asymptotic formula for the fourth moment of the Riemann zeta-function times the fourth power of an amplifier. This has various applications to the theory of the Riemann zeta-function, e.g. gaps between zeros of ζ ⁡ ( s ) \zeta(s) and lower bounds for moments.

Introduction and statement of results

The distribution of the Riemann zeta-function on the critical line is a fascinating and challenging topic in number theory. A lot can be said about this by studying the moments of
\zeta(s)

2k

Apart from the trivial case

[

for all

To bridge the gap between Ingham’s result for

Depending on applications, one often takes

.

We have

4.
Large gaps between zeros of
\zeta(s)
and proof of Theorem
1.2

With our limited knowledge, there is only one known method to detect large gaps between consecutive zeros of the Riemann zeta-function unconditionally, and this was discovered by the second named author. A key ingredient is the use of some Wirtinger type inequalities. We shall start with the basic idea and then discuss the two known approaches.

4.1.
The Wirtinger inequality and large gaps between zeros

Let
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
and
f\in C^{1}
with
f(a)=f(b)=0
. Then the Wirtinger inequality states that

\int_{a}^{b}|f(t)|^{2}dt\leq\Big(\frac{b-a}{\pi}\Big)^{2}\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt.

(13)

Let
Z(t)
be the Hardy
Z
-function. In particular,
|Z(t_{n})|=|\zeta(1/2+it_{n})|=0
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Consider the hypothesis
\Lambda\leq\kappa
.
Then for
t_{n}\in[T,2T]
we have

t_{n+1}-t_{n}\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)\frac{2\pi\kappa}{\log T},

as
T\rightarrow\infty
. In view of (
13
) we then get

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|Z(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)\frac{4\kappa^{2}}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{n+1}}|Z^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt.

Let
t_{n},t_{m}\in[T,2T]
be the zeros closest to
T
and
2T
, respectively. Summing up the above inequalities we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}|Z(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)\frac{4\kappa^{2}}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{t_{n}}^{t_{m}}|Z^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt.

As the integrals above are both
\gg T
, and by our hypothesis we have
t_{n}-T\ll 1
and
t_{m}-2T\ll 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{2T}|Z(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq\big(1+o(1)\big)\frac{4\kappa^{2}}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{T}^{2T}|Z^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt.

Hence if

\int_{T}^{2T}|Z(t)|^{2}dt>\frac{4\kappa^{2}}{(\log T)^{2}}\int_{T}^{2T}|Z^{\prime}(t)|^{2}dt,

then we must have
\Lambda>\kappa
.

It is known that

\int_{T}^{2T}|Z^{(k)}(t)|^{2}dt\sim\frac{T(\log T)^{2k+1}}{4^{k}(2k+1)}

as
T\rightarrow\infty
(see, for instance,
[
10
, Theorem 3]
). We therefore get
\Lambda\geq\sqrt{3}=1.73\ldots
.

4.2.
Hall’s first approach

The key ingredient in Hall’s first attempt is a Wirtinger type inequality due to Beesack
[
1
]
, which generally states that

\int_{a}^{b}|f(t)|^{2k}dt\leq\frac{1}{2k-1}\Big(k\sin\frac{\pi}{2k}\Big)^{2k}\Big(\frac{b-a}{\pi}\Big)^{2k}\int_{a}^{b}|f^{\prime}(t)|^{2k}dt,

where

with

. The Wirtinger inequality (

. With a more general inequality when

[

4.3.

Hall’s later approach just uses the standard Wirtinger inequality (

The later improvement of Bui and Milinovich

In this paper we shall employ a Wirtinger type inequality essentially due to Hall. For the application to large gaps between zeros of the Riemann zeta-function, we shall use the amplified fourth moment of

Proof.

For

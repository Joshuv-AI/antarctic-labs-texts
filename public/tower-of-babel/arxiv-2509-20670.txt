Fundamental theorem of Poisson 3-Lie ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules
Daowei Lu, Dingguo Wang

Abstract
. Let H H be a Hopf algebra with a bijective antipode and A A an H H -comodule Poisson 3-Lie algebra. Assume that there exists an H H -colinear map which is also an algebra map from H H to the Poisson center of A A . In this paper we generalize the fundamental theorem of ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules to Poisson 3-Lie ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules and deduce relative projectivity in the category of Poisson 3-Lie ( A , H ) (A,H) -Hopf modules.



1.
Preliminaries

Definition 1.1
.

[
2
]

A 3-Lie algebra is a vector space
A
together with a skew-symmetric linear map (
3
-Lie bracket)
\{\;,\;,\;\}:\otimes^{3}A\rightarrow A
such that the following Fundamental Identity holds:

(1.1)

\{\{x,y,z\},u,v\}=\{\{x,u,v\},y,z\}+\{\{y,u,v\},z,x\}+\{\{z,u,v\},x,y\},

for
x,y,z,u,v\in A
.

In other words, the adjoint action of
u,v
in the ternary operation is a derivation.

A subspace
B
of
A
is called a subalgebra (an ideal ) of
A
if
\{B,B,B\}\subseteq B

(\{B,A,A\}\subseteq B)
.

The center
A^{A}
of 3-Lie algebra
A
is Lie-
A
-invariant elements of
A
, that is,

A^{A}=\Big\{b\in A|\{b,A,A\}=0\Big\}.

Definition 1.2
.

Let
A
be a 3-Lie algebra. A 3-Lie
A
-module is a pair
(M,\diamond)
where
M
is a vector space and
\diamond:L\times L\times M\rightarrow M,\ (x,y)\otimes m\mapsto(x,y)\diamond m
is a linear map on the three items satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond m=-(y,x)\diamond m,

(1.3)

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond((u,v)\diamond m)-(u,v)\diamond((x,y)\diamond m)=(\{x,y,u\},v)\diamond m+(u,\{x,y,v\})\diamond m,

(1.4)

\displaystyle(\{x,y,u\},v)\diamond m=(y,u)\diamond((x,v)\diamond m)-(x,u)\diamond((y,v)\diamond m)+(x,y)\diamond((u,v)\diamond m),

for all
x,y,u,v\in A,m\in M
.

Let
M,N
be two 3-Lie
A
-modules. A linear map
f:M\rightarrow N
is called a morphism of 3-Lie
A
-modules if for all
x,y\in A,m\in M
,

f((x,y)\diamond m)=(x,y)\diamond f(m).

For 3-Lie
A
-module
M
, the Lie
A
-invariant elements of
M
is

M^{A}=\Big\{m\in M|(x,y)\diamond m=0,\forall x,y\in A\Big\}.

Definition 1.3
.

[
1
]

A Poisson 3-Lie algebra is a vector space
A
with a bilinear operator
\cdot
and a ternary operator
\{\;,\;,\;\}
such that
(A,\cdot)
is a commutative associative algebra,
(A,\{\;,\;,\;\})
is a 3-Lie algebra and the following equation holds:

(1.5)

\{x,y,uv\}=u\{x,y,v\}+\{x,y,u\}v,

for all
x,y,u,v\in A.

Thus the adjoint action of the pair
(x,y)
on the commutative associative product is a derivation. Let
u=v=1
, we have
\{x,y,1\}=0
.

Let
A
and
T
be two Poisson 3-Lie algebras. A homomorphism of Poisson 3-Lie algebra is an algebra map
f:A\rightarrow T
such that

f(\{x,y,z\})=\{f(x),f(y),f(z)\},

for all
x,y,z\in A
.

A subspace
B
of
A
is called a Poisson 3-Lie subalgebra of
A
if
B
is both a subalgebra and 3-Lie subalgebra of
A
.

Definition 1.4
.

Let
A
be a Poisson 3-Lie algebra. A vector space
M
is called a Poisson 3-Lie
A
-module if
(M,\cdot)
is an
A
-module and
(M,\diamond)
is a 3-Lie
A
-module satisfying the compatible conditions

(1.6)

\displaystyle(xy,z)\diamond m=x\cdot((y,z)\diamond m)+y\cdot((x,z)\diamond m),

(1.7)

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond(z\cdot m)=\{x,y,z\}\cdot m+z\cdot((x,y)\diamond m),

for all
x,y,z\in A,m\in M
.

A morphism of Poisson 3-Lie
A
-modules is both an
A
-linear and a 3-Lie
A
-linear map.

In the identity (
1.6
), let
x=y=1
, we obtain that
(1,z)\diamond m=0
for all
z\in A,m\in M
.

2.
The category of Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf modules

Definition 2.1
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
a Poisson 3-Lie algebra. Then
A
is called a right
H
-comodule Poisson 3-Lie algebra if
A
is a right
H
-comodule algebra and satisfies the following relation

(2.1)

\{x,y,z\}_{(0)}\otimes\{x,y,z\}_{(1)}=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)},

for all
x,y,z\in A
.

Remark 2.2
.

Since the 3-Lie bracket is skew-symmetry, we have

\displaystyle\{x,y,z\}_{(0)}\otimes\{x,y,z\}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\{x,z,y\}_{(0)}\otimes\{x,z,y\}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=-\{x_{(0)},z_{(0)},y_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}z_{(1)}y_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}z_{(1)}y_{(1)},

thus

\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}z_{(1)}y_{(1)}.

And similarly

\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes y_{(1)}x_{(1)}z_{(1)}.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
H
be a Hopf algebra and
A
a Poisson 3-Lie algebra. Then
A^{coH}
is an
H
-subcomodule Poisson 3-Lie algebra of
A
.

Proof.

It is well known that
A^{coH}
is an
H
-subcomodule algebra of
A
. For all
x,y,z\in A^{coH}
,

\{x,y,z\}_{(0)}\otimes\{x,y,z\}_{(1)}=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}=\{x,y,z\}\otimes 1_{H},

hence
\{x,y,z\}\in A^{coH}
, that is,
A^{coH}
is a Lie subalgebra of

.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

It is clear that an

(2) By (1)

(3) Firstly

Lemma 2.7
.

(1)

Let
N
be a 3-Lie
A
-module. Then
N\otimes H
is an
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule with the 3-Lie
A
-action and
H
-coaction given by

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond(n\otimes h)=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h,

\displaystyle(n\otimes h)_{(0)}\otimes(n\otimes h)_{(1)}=n\otimes h_{1}\otimes h_{2},

for all

.

(2)

Furthermore, if

is commutative and

-module, then

is a Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module with the

x\cdot(n\otimes h)=x_{(0)}\cdot n\otimes x_{(1)}h,

x,y\in A,n\otimes h\in N\otimes H

(1) For all
x,y,u,v\in A,n\otimes h\in N\otimes H

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond((u,v)\diamond(n\otimes h))-(u,v)\diamond((x,y)\diamond(n\otimes h))

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond((u_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h))-(u,v)\diamond(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond((u_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle\quad-(u_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes u_{(1)}v_{(1)}x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=[(\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},u_{(0)}\},v_{(0)})\diamond n+(u_{(0)},\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},v_{(0)}\})\diamond n]\otimes u_{(1)}x_{(1)}y_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=(\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},u_{(0)}\},v_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h+(u_{(0)},\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},v_{(0)}\})\diamond n\otimes u_{(1)}x_{(1)}y_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=(\{x,y,u\},v)\diamond(n\otimes h)+(u,\{x,y,v\})\diamond(n\otimes h),

then

\displaystyle(\{x,y,u\},v)\diamond(n\otimes h)+(x,u)\diamond((y,v)\diamond(n\otimes h))

\displaystyle=(\{x,y,u\}_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes\{x,y,u\}_{(1)}v_{(1)}h+(x,u)\diamond((y_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes y_{(1)}v_{(1)}h)

\displaystyle=(\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},u_{(0)}\},v_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h+(x_{(0)},u_{(0)})\diamond((y_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}u_{(1)}y_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=[(\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},u_{(0)}\},v_{(0)})\diamond n+(x_{(0)},u_{(0)})\diamond((y_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)]\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=[(y_{(0)},u_{(0)})\diamond((x_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)+(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond((u_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)]\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=(y_{(0)},u_{(0)})\diamond((x_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes y_{(1)}u_{(1)}x_{(1)}v_{(1)}h+(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond((u_{(0)},v_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}u_{(1)}v_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=(y,u)\diamond((x,v)\diamond(n\otimes h))+(x,y)\diamond((u,v)\diamond(n\otimes h)).

And

\displaystyle((x,y)\diamond(n\otimes h))_{(0)}\otimes((x,y)\diamond(n\otimes h))_{(1)}

\displaystyle=((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)_{(0)}\otimes((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes(x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)_{1}\otimes(x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)_{2}

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)1}y_{(1)1}h_{1}\otimes x_{(1)2}y_{(1)2}h_{2}

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)(0)},y_{(0)(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(0)(1)}y_{(0)(1)}h_{1}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h_{2}

\displaystyle=(x,y)_{(0)}\diamond(n\otimes h)_{(0)}\otimes(x,y)_{(1)}(n\otimes h)_{(1)}.

Thus

is an
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule.

(2) For all

\displaystyle(xy,z)\diamond(n\otimes h)

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)}y_{(0)},z_{(0)})n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=x_{(0)}\cdot((y_{(0)},z_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h+y_{(0)}\cdot((x_{(0)},z_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h

\displaystyle=x\cdot((y_{(0)},z_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h)+y\cdot((x_{(0)},z_{(0)})\diamond n\otimes x_{(1)}z_{(1)}h)

\displaystyle=x\cdot((y,z)\diamond(n\otimes h))+y\cdot((x,z)\diamond(n\otimes h)),

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond(z_{(0)}\cdot n\otimes z_{(1)}h)

\displaystyle=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},z_{(0)}\}\cdot n\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h+z_{(0)}\cdot((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}z_{(1)}h

(1)

.

(2)

a Poisson 3-Lie

-Hopf module and

a Poisson 3-Lie

-module. There exists a linear isomorphism

\gamma:\!_{\mathcal{P}A}\hbox{Hom}^{H}(M,N\otimes H)\rightarrow\!_{\mathcal{P}A}\hbox{Hom}(M,N),\ f\mapsto(id\otimes\varepsilon)\circ f.

Proof.

(1) By Lemma
2.7
,

is an
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule. Let

. For

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond n_{i}\varepsilon(x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h_{i})

is Lie

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
A
be a
H
-comodule Poisson 3-Lie algebra, and
M
a Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module. Assume that there exists an
H
-colinear map
\phi:H\rightarrow A^{A}
such that
\phi(1_{H})=1_{A}
.

(1)

If
H
is commutative or
\phi
is an algebra map, then every Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module which is injective as a Lie
A
-module is an injective
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule.

(2)

If
H
is commutative, then every Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module which is injective Poisson 3-Lie
A
-module is an injective Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module.

Proof.

(1) Let
M
be a Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module and consider the linear map
\lambda:M\otimes H\rightarrow M
defined by

\lambda(m\otimes h)=\phi(hS^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\cdot m_{(0)},\quad\forall m\in M,h\in H.

Just as shown in
[
3
]
,
\lambda\circ\rho_{M}=id_{M}
and

is
H
-colinear, which means that
\rho_{M}
is an injective map.

For all
x,y\in A,m\in M,h\in H
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda((x,y)\diamond(m\otimes h))

\displaystyle=\lambda((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond m\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}h)

\displaystyle=\phi(x_{(1)}y_{(1)}hS^{-1}(((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond m)_{(1)}))\cdot((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond m)_{(0)}

\displaystyle=\phi(x_{(1)}y_{(1)}hS^{-1}(x_{(0)(1)}y_{(0)(1)}m_{(1)}))\cdot((x_{(0)(0)},y_{(0)(0)})\diamond m_{(0)})

\displaystyle=\phi(x_{(1)2}y_{(1)2}hS^{-1}(x_{(1)1}y_{(1)1}m_{(1)}))\cdot((x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond m_{(0)})

and

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond\lambda(m\otimes h)

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond(\phi(hS^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\cdot m_{(0)})

\displaystyle=\{x,y,\phi(hS^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\}\cdot m_{(0)}+\phi(hS^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\cdot((x,y)\diamond m_{(0)})

\displaystyle=\phi(hS^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\cdot((x,y)\diamond m_{(0)}),

where the last identity holds since
\phi(hS^{-1}_{H}(m_{(1)}))\in A^{A}
. Now if

\lambda((x,y)\diamond(m\otimes h))=(x,y)\diamond\lambda(m\otimes h),

that is,
\lambda
is Lie

-linear and a homomorphism in
{}_{\underline{A}}\mathcal{M}^{H}
. If
\phi
is an algebra map, using the fact that
A
is commutative, we also have
\lambda((x,y)\diamond(m\otimes h))=(x,y)\diamond\lambda(m\otimes h).
Thus
\lambda
is Lie
A
-linear and a homomorphism in
{}_{\underline{A}}\mathcal{M}^{H}
. For all
x,y\in A,m\in M
,

\displaystyle\rho_{M}((x,y)\diamond m)

\displaystyle=((x,y)\diamond m)_{(0)}\otimes((x,y)\diamond m)_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond m_{(0)}\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}m_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond\rho_{M}(m),

hence
\rho_{M}
is a 3-Lie
A
-linear.
It is easy to verify that
\rho_{M}
is
H
-colinear, so it is a homomorphism of
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule. If
M
is an injective 3-Lie
A
-module, by a similar argument as in
[
3
]
,
M
is an injective
(\underline{A},H)
-comodule.

(2) By a similar computation as above, we obtain that
\lambda
given in (1) is
A
-linear. Thus
\lambda
is a homomorphism of Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf modules. By the same arguments as
[
3
]
, we conclude that an injective Poisson 3-Lie
A
-module is also an injective Poisson 3-Lie
(A,H)
-Hopf module.
∎

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
M

B

(A,H)
-Hopf module, where the

\displaystyle x\cdot(a\otimes_{B}m)=xa\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m)=\{x,y,a\}\otimes_{B}m,

and the

\rho_{A\otimes_{B}M}(a\otimes_{B}m)=a_{(0)}\otimes_{B}m\otimes a_{(1)},

for all

Proof.

It is obvious that the coaction is well defined and

is an
(A,H)
-Hopf module. For all
a,x,y,z\in A,m\in M
. For
b\in B

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond(ab\otimes_{B}m)

\displaystyle=(\{x,y,ab\})\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{1e})}}{{=}}(a\{x,y,b\}+\{x,y,a\}b)\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{x,y,a\}b\otimes_{B}m

\displaystyle=\{x,y,a\}\otimes_{B}b\cdot m

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond(a\otimes_{B}b\cdot m),

so the 3-Lie action is well defined. We have

\displaystyle(xy,z)\diamond(a\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\{xy,z,a\}\otimes m

\displaystyle=[x\{y,z,a\}+\{x,z,a\}y]\otimes m

\displaystyle=x\{y,z,a\}\otimes m+\{x,z,a\}y\otimes m

\displaystyle=x\cdot(\{y,z\}\diamond(a\otimes m))+y\cdot((x,z)\diamond(a\otimes m)),

so the relation (
1.5
) is satisfied.

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond(z\cdot(a\otimes m))

\displaystyle=(x,y)\diamond(za\otimes m)

\displaystyle=\{x,y,za\}\otimes m

\displaystyle=z\{x,y,a\}\otimes m+\{x,y,z\}a\otimes m

\displaystyle=z\cdot((x,y)\diamond m)+\{x,y,z\}\cdot(a\otimes m),

so the relation (
1.6
) is satisfied. Thus

is a Poisson 3-Lie
A
-module. Moreover

\displaystyle\rho_{A\otimes_{B}M}((x,y)\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))

\displaystyle=((x,y)\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(0)}\otimes((x,y)\diamond(a\otimes_{B}m))_{(1)}

\displaystyle=(\{x,y,a\}\otimes m)_{(0)}\otimes(\{x,y,a\}\otimes m)_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{x,y,a\}_{(0)}\otimes m\otimes\{x,y,a\}_{(1)}

\displaystyle=\{x_{(0)},y_{(0)},a_{(0)}\}\otimes m\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}a_{(1)}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\ref{2b})}}{{=}}(x_{(0)},y_{(0)})\diamond(a_{(0)}\otimes m)\otimes x_{(1)}y_{(1)}a_{(1)},

the relation (
2.2
) is satisfied. The proof is completed.
∎

Clearly

Lemma 3.4
.

Let

(4)

We only verify the conditions (2),(3) and (4).

(2) For all

\displaystyle(x,y)\diamond^{\prime}p_{M}(m)

\displaystyle=p_{M}((x,y)\diamond p_{M}(m))

\displaystyle=p_{M}((x,y)\diamond(\phi(S^{-1}(m_{(1)}))\cdot m_{(0)}))

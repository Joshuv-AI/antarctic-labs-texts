On certain integral Frobenius period maps for Shimura varieties and their reductions
Qijun Yan

Abstract
. We formulate an integral Frobenius period map for the framed crystalline prismatization of the p p -integral model 𝒮 \mathcal{S} of a Shimura variety with good reduction. By analyzing reductions of this map, we derive a period map from the mod p p fiber S S of 𝒮 \mathcal{S} to the moduli stack of 1-1 truncated local G G -shtukas in the prismatic topology, which refines the zip period map of S S within this topology. Furthermore, we show that the pair ( 𝒮 , S ) (\mathcal{S},S) is associated with a double G G -zip. Additionally, we introduce a framework of base reduction diagrams.

2.1.
Prisms and prismatic sites

We first recall some definitions and facts on prisms, adapted to our needs, following
[
4
]
and
[
11
, §1.1]
.

A (
p
-torsion free)
prism

\underline{A}
is a triple
(A,\varphi,I)
, where
A
is a
p
-torsion free ring together with a Frobenius lift
\varphi=\varphi_{A}
of
A
, and
I\subseteq A
is an invertible ideal such that
A
is derived
(p,I)
-complete and
p\in I+\varphi(I)
. Often we write
\underline{A}=(A,I)
when the Frobenius
\varphi
is clear or irrelevant for the discussion, and similarly sometimes we write
\underline{A}=(A,\varphi)
when
I
is clear.

A prism
\underline{A}=(A,I)
is
bounded
if the quotient ring
A/I
has bounded
p^{\infty}
-torsion, i.e.,
A/I[p^{\infty}]=A/I[p^{n}]
for some integer
n
. For a bounded prism
\underline{A}
, the underlying ring
A
is (classically)
(p,I)
-complete and
A/I
is
p
-adically complete (see e.g.,
[
11
, Lemma 1.2]
). A prism
\underline{A}=(A,I)
is
crystalline
if we have
I=pA
. In particular, a crystalline prism is
p
-torsion free. In the remaining part of this article, by a prism we always mean a
p
-torsion free and bounded prism, unless otherwise specified.

A
morphism of prisms

(A,I)\to(B,J)
is a homomorphism of rings
A\to B
, compatible with Frobenius structure and sending
I
into
J
. By the rigidity property of morphisms between prisms
[
4
, Proposition 3.5]
, the existence of such a morphism implies that we necessarily have
J=IB
. A morphism of prisms
(A,I)\to(B,J)
is
I
-completely flat
(resp.
faithfully flat, smooth, étale
) if
B\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}A/I
is concentrated in degree
0
and the canonical homomorphism
A/I\to B\otimes^{\mathrm{L}}_{A}A/I
is flat (resp. faithfully flat, smooth, étale).

For each prism
\underline{A}=(A,I)
, the ideal
I
is finitely generated. Since
I
is an invertible ideal, the open subset
U(I):=\mathrm{Spec}A-\mathrm{V}(I)
is affine. Its ring of global sections is denoted by
A[1/I]
. For each mathematical object
X
(e.g., sheaves, schemes, modules) over
A
, we denote by
X[1/I]
its restriction on
U(I)
; for example, for each
A
-module
M
, we write
M[1/I]
for the base change
M\otimes_{A}A[1/I]
. We write
U(\underline{A})
for the subset
\mathrm{Spec}A-V(p,I)
. Clearly we have
U(I)\subseteq U(\underline{A})
. Hence for a vector bundle
{\mathcal{E}}
(or other meaningful mathematical objects) over
U(\underline{A})
, the notation
{\mathcal{E}}[1/I]
is understood.

Proposition
2.1
.

Let
\underline{A}=(A,I)
be a (bounded) prism.

(1)

Let

be a
(p,I)
-adically complete

is
(p,I)
-completely flat (resp. faithfully flat, smooth, étale) if and only if the corresponding morphism

is
(p,I)
-adically flat (resp. faithfully flat, smooth, étale).

(2)

Let

be a
(p,I)
-adically complete
A
-algebra. If

is
(p,I)
-completely étale, there exists a unique Frobenius structure on

a (bounded

Proof.

This is
[

-adically complete, it is

-adically étale, hence is

-adically étale, in particular,
p
-adically flat. Hence for each

is flat over

∎

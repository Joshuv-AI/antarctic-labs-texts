Survival of patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma based on Gustave Roussy immune score and portal vein tumor thrombosis: a single-center, retrospective, non-randomized case-controlled study
Authors: Yuhong Liu, Huaxi Fan, Guishu Wu, Jing Zhang, Mingyue Rao, Peipei Zhu, Jianwen Zhang
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of this study was to investigate the survival of patients with advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) based on Gustave Roussy immune scores (GRIm scores) and portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT) who received immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) plus targeted drugs and radiotherapy as first-line treatment. One hundred and forty-three patients with advanced HCC were divided into GRIm score low-risk (GRIm LR) and high-risk (GRIm HR) groups, PVTT and no-PVTT groups according to pre-treatment GRIm scores and PVTT status, respectively, and further subdivided into group A (GRIm LR with no-PVTT), group B (GRIm LR with PVTT), group C (GRIm HR with no-PVTT), and group D (GRIm HR with PVTT). The log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test was used to analyze overall survival (OS) and progression-free survival (PFS). OS and PFS were prolonged by 15.3 and 11.7 months, and 4.3 and 7.6 months, respectively, in the GRIm LR and no-PVTT groups compared to the GRIm HR and PVTT groups. OS and PFS were prolonged by 13.8 and 10.8 months, 21.8 and 16.2 months, and 27.0 and 17.5 months in group A compared to groups B, C, and D, respectively. Group D had the worst OS and PFS at 7.9 and 4.8 months, respectively. The combination of the GRIm score and PVTT status outperformed GRIm scores and PVTT status alone in predicting the survival of patients with HCC and is beneficial for screening the optimal candidates and choosing individualized treatment regimens.Keywords: Gustave roussy immune score (GRIm score), Hepatocellular carcinoma, Immune checkpoint inhibitors, Portal vein tumor thrombosis (PVTT), Radiotherapy, Survival prediction

Introduction

Primary liver cancer (PLC), which ranks as the fourth largest malignant tumor and the second leading cause of cancer-related deaths in China, is approximately 75–85% hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) [1]. From 2020 on, an annual increase in new cases of 55.0% is predicted, and by 2040, cases will increase by 56.4% compared to 2020, with 1.4 million new confirmed cases [2]. At the time of diagnosis, 70–80% of the patients are in the advanced stage [3], 68.6% of HCC cases are in the Barcelona clinical liver carcinoma (BCLC) stage C, while only 12.8% are in the stage B [4].

The prognostic assessment of HCC is crucial for formulating treatment strategies and evaluating the quality of life of patients, and BCLC staging is a commonly used method. Numerous clinical factors affect the prognostic assessment of HCC. The median overall survival (OS) of patients with untreated HCC is closely related to the BCLC stage. The median OS for stage 0/A BCLC is 31.0–44.4 months, 7.9–10.0 months for stage B, 3.0–3.9 months for stage C, and 1.0–2.9 months for stage D [5, 6]. For patients with BCLC stages 0, A, and B, who undergo radical hepatectomy, the 5-year OS rates are 86.2%, 69.0%, and 41.7–49.9% respectively [7, 8], and for BCLC stage C, it is 23.3% [8]. The more advanced the HCC stage, the worse the survival outcome.

The liver function status before treatment is another factor affecting the survival of patients with HCC. The higher the liver function status grade before treatment, the shorter the OS [9]. The median OS of patients with Child-Pugh class A is 49.7 months, that of class B is 12.4 months, and that of class C is 4.2 months. Liver function, especially the albumin-bilirubin (ALBI) grade, is an independent factor affecting survival [10, 11]. The median OS of HCC patients with ALBI grade 1 is 84.2 months, that of grade 2 is 25.5 months, and that of grade 3 is 7.7 months [12].

Although alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels are commonly used to diagnose and monitor HCC, their specificity and sensitivity are controversial. Most studies suggest that there is no significant correlation between AFP levels, tumor size, HCC stage, or the survival period after diagnosis. However, other studies maintain that a gradual or continuous increase in AFP levels is a positive predictive indicator of tumor progression. The sensitivity and specificity of AFP levels in the diagnosis of HCC are 60% and 35.8%, respectively [13]. Whether or not AFP levels are elevated above normal has an impact on the survival of HCC patients. The 1-, 3-, and 5-year OS rates of AFP-positive patients are 60.7%, 28.9%, and 14.3%, respectively [14].

Immunotherapy based on anti-programmed death receptor 1 and its ligands has become the main treatment for malignant tumors. Predicting and screening the efficacy of immunotherapy and the optimal patient candidates for this treatment are the keys to improving treatment efficacy. Phase I trials have validated Gustave Roussy immune (GRIm) scores and Royal Marsden Hospital prognostic (RMH) scores as biomarkers for assessing the treatment efficacy of immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) [15, 16]. The GRIm score is based on lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) levels, serum albumin concentrations, and the neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio [15]. The RMH score is based on LDH and albumin levels and the number of metastases [16]. The OS of patients with high GRIm scores is shorter than those with low scores (3.2 and 19.9 months, respectively), and the PFS of patients with high RMH scores is shorter than those with low scores (1.8 and 2.6 months, respectively). The GRIm and RMH scores are poor prognostic factors for OS and progression-free survival PFS, respectively [16]. The variables comprising the RMH score are less precise, so the GRIm score is superior to the RMH score [15]. Research on GRIm scores in predicting the survival of patients with advanced HCC receiving combined treatment with ICIs plus targeted therapy and radiotherapy is lacking.

Portal vein tumor thrombus (PVTT) is the most ominous sign in advanced HCC [3]. Of HCC patients, 46.1% have PVTT at the initial diagnosis. PVTT is a common complication of advanced HCC and a bottleneck in treatment, indicating poor treatment tolerance and prognosis [3, 17, 18]. Radiotherapy is an effective method to control PVTT and prolong the survival of HCC patients with PVTT. The OS rates at 6, 12, 18, and 24 months in a neoadjuvant radiotherapy group for PVTT were 89.0%, 75.2%, 43.9%, and 27.4%, respectively, while those in the surgery-only group were 81.7%, 43.1%, 16.7%, and 9.4% [19]. In HCC patients with PVTT, the median PFS of those receiving radiotherapy plus transarterial chemoembolization (RT + TACE) was longer than that of patients receiving TACE + RT (6.6 and 4.2 months, respectively), and the disease control rates (DCRs) were 86.7% and 66.7% respectively [20].

In the era of immunotherapy, there is a lack of relevant reports predicting the survival of patients with advanced HCC using GRIm scores combined with PVTT status. Thus, the main objective of this study was to investigate the predictive significance of GRIm scores combined with PVTT status for the survival of patients with advanced HCC receiving ICIs plus targeted drugs and radiotherapy as first-line treatment.

Methods

Study design

This study was a single-center, retrospective, case-controlled study of a per-protocol population. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee and Institutional Review Board of the Affiliated Hospital of Southwest Medical University (KY2024389). The enrolled patients were those with advanced HCC (unresectable BCLC stages B and C) who received ICIs plus targeted drugs and radiotherapy as first-line treatment. Treatment efficacy was evaluated by an independent committee based on the modified solid tumor response assessment criteria (mRECIST). Safety was evaluated using the National Cancer Institute-Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.0 (NCI-CTCAE 4.0). Radiation-induced liver injury was evaluated based on Radiation Therapy Oncology Group / European Organization for Research and Treatment of Cancer grading standards. The primary endpoint was OS, and the secondary endpoints were PFS, the objective response rate (ORR), and the DCR. The follow-up cutoff date was April 30, 2024. The main objective was to explore the clinical survival advantages of ICIs plus targeted drugs and radiotherapy in advanced HCC based on predictions using a combination of GRIm scores and PVTT status. This study complied with the ethical guidelines of the 1975 Helsinki Declaration and the STROBE statement. The research design is shown in Fig. 1.

Participants

The diagnosis of HCC in this study was consistent with literature reports [21, 22], which included typical imaging findings (enhanced computed tomography (CT) and/or magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)) and AFP levels ≥ 400 ng/mL). Diagnoses were made histopathologically in patients who did not meet the above diagnostic criteria. The main inclusion criteria included (1) previously untreated unresectable advanced HCC, (2) laboratory testing meeting ICIs requirements, (3) a Child-Pugh score of A/B, (4) an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) score of 0–1, and (5) no history of liver radiotherapy or severe cirrhosis. The main exclusion criteria included (1) autoimmune diseases, (2) pregnant or lactating women, (3) patients with hepatic vein thrombosis (including thrombosis of the hepatic vein or the inferior vena cava), (4) concurrent hepatic encephalopathy, (5) severe hypertension and cerebral infarction, and (6) moderate-to-severe hypothyroidism. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were described in detail in our previous report [23].

In this study, the patient’s treatment was defined as ICIs plus a targeted drug and radiotherapy. The ICIs, including camrelizumab (Hengrui, China. 3 mg/kg), sintilimab (Innovent, China. 200 mg), tislelizumab (Beigene, China. 200 mg), and pembrolizumab (Merck, US. 200 mg), were used every 21 days. The targeted drugs included lenvatinib (Eisai Co, Ltd, Japan, 8 mg, orally, once daily), apatinib (Hengrui, China, 250 mg, orally, once daily), anlotinib (Chia Tai Tianqing, China, 8 mg, orally, days 1–14, 21 days per cycle), and bevacizumab (Innovent, China, 15 mg/kg, once every 3 weeks). The patients received ICI treatment for at least four cycles or until disease progression or intolerance. The maximum duration was ≤ 2 years.

Target areas were delineated according to guidelines [24] and our previous report [23] containing detailed descriptions of the specific method used for radiotherapy localization, the delineations of the target areas (gross tumor volume (GTV) and clinical tumor volume (CTV)) and organs at risk, the radiotherapy techniques (intensity-modulated radiotherapy), and the requirements of target area doses and dose limits. The main key points included: (1) The plan for implementing radiotherapy required a D95% of ≥ 14.2–46 Gy and ≥ 12.5–40 Gy in 5–16 fractions for GTV and CTV, respectively. (2) The mean dose to normal liver tissue was < 10–28 Gy. (3) The dose to organs at risk was strictly controlled within a tolerable safe range. (4) In patients with intrahepatic metastasis, the largest lesion was used as the GTV. If patients with extrahepatic metastases, if an intrahepatic lesion was present, it was used as the GTV; if no intrahepatic lesion was present, the most obvious or the greatest impact lesion was used as the GTV. (5) Radiotherapy was performed on days 2 or 3 during the first cycle of ICI plus targeted drug administration.

In this study, the patients were divided into the following groups according to pre-treatment GRIm scores calculated based on pre-treatment laboratory examinations, and PVTT status: (1) GRIm score low risk (GRIm LR: GRIm score of 0–1) and GRIm score high risk (GRIm HR: GRIm score of 2–3) groups based on the GRIm score criteria [16]. (2) The extent of PVTT was documented in the HCC registry using the classification suggested by the Liver Cancer Study Group of Japan, ranging from Vp0 to Vp4 (Vp0 = no PVTT; Vp1 = segmental PV invasion; Vp2 = right anterior or posterior PV; Vp3 = right or left PV; and Vp4 = main trunk and/or contra-lateral portal vein branch to the primarily involved lobe) [25]. In our study, the presence of Vp3 or Vp4 was defined as positive PVTT status. Radiologists determined the presence of PVTT and portal vein invasion using pre-treatment enhanced CT and/or MRI scans. Portal vein invasion was defined as HCC cells invading the intrahepatic vascular system without PVTT formation. Patients with PVTT, regardless of whether portal vein invasion was present, were classified as having positive PVTT status and further subdivided into Vp3 and Vp4. Thus, they were divided into no-PVTT (without Vp3 and Vp4) and PVTT (with Vp3 or Vp4) groups, respectively. (3) Subgroups: group A (GRIm LR with no-PVTT), group B (GRIm LR with PVTT), group C (GRIm HR with no-PVTT), and group D (GRIm HR with PVTT) based on GRIm scores and PVTT status.

All patients underwent enhanced CT and/or MRI examinations, measurements of liver and kidney function, routine blood analyses, coagulation function testing, thyroid function testing, hepatitis B virus DNA (HBV-DNA) testing, and AFP testing pre-treatment and every 3 months post-treatment. Treatment efficacy was assessed based on mRECIST 1.1, using the CT and/or MRI examination results pre- and post-treatment at 6 months. Complete response (CR) was defined as complete tumor disappearance, partial response (PR) was a reduction of more than 30% in the longest diameter of the target tumor, stable disease (SD) was a diameter reduction of less than 30% or a diameter increase of less than 20%, and progressive disease (PD) was a diameter increase of more than 20%. The combined CR and PR rate was defined as the ORR, and the combined CR, PR, and SD rate was defined as the DCR.

PFS was defined as the period from the start of treatment to disease progression, and OS was defined as the period from the start of treatment to death, the loss to follow-up date, or the follow-up cutoff date. Outpatient or telephone follow-up was conducted every 3 months from the end of the first treatment cycle, and patient-reported outcomes were recorded.

Statistics

We assumed an alpha level of the two-sided test of 0.01, a test validity (1- β) of 0.95, an area under the curve of 0.7, and a dropout rate of 10% and calculated a sample size of 138 using PASS 15 software. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 26.0 (IBM, US), R language (New Zealand), and GraphPad 8.0 (US). Categorical variables are represented as n (%). Counting data were analyzed using the Chi-squared test (Fisher’s exact test). The log-rank (Mantel Cox) test used to analyze OS and PFS. Univariate and multivariate Cox regression models were used to analyze prognostic factors for OS and PFS. Statistical significance was defined as a p-value of < 0.05.

Results

Patients

The clinical characteristics of the study patients are shown in Table 1. The patient with HBV-DNA positivity (> 1 × 102 IU/mL) was given entecavir (0.5 mg, once daily; Dongrui, China), and the HBV-DNA content was controlled at less than 1 × 102 IU/mL. From January 31, 2019, to December 31, 2023, 152 patients with advanced HCC received ICIs plus targeted drugs and radiotherapy as first-line treatment at the Oncology Department of Southwest Medical University Affiliated Hospital. Nine patients were excluded due to incomplete follow-up data, failure to complete four cycles of ICI therapy and incomplete clinical data. A total of 143 patients met the inclusion criteria and were available for analysis (114 males and 29 females). The median age of all patients was 54.9 years (range 27–84 years). Twenty-nine cases of PVTT were classified as Vp3, and 25 were classified as Vp4. Twenty, 35, 50, and 38 patients received continuous treatment with ICIs plus targeted drugs for 4, 5–8, 9–12, and 13–16 cycles, respectively. Thirty-four patients received camrelizumab plus apatinib, 38 received sintilimab plus bevacizumab, 67 received tislelizumab plus anlotinib, and four received pembrolizumab plus bevacizumab. The median follow-up was 25.1 months (range 1.3–40.1 months). Seventy-four patients had died, and 69 were surviving at the follow-up cutoff date.

Efficacy and survival

The tumor response rates among the groups are shown in Table 2. The CR rate, PR rate, ORR, and DCR increased by 7.0% (p = 0.0811), 38.2% (p < 0.0001), 45.2% (p < 0.0001) and 32.8% (p < 0.0001), respectively, in the GRIm LR group compared to the GRIm HR group. The CR rate and ORR increased by 7.0% (p = 0.0811) and 0.9% (p > 0.9999), respectively, in the no-PVTT group, whereas the PR rate and DCR decreased by 5.8% (p = 0.6049) and 14.3% (p = 0.0430) compared to the PVTT group. We compared the local control rate (LCR) of PVTT at 6 months post-treatment. The overall LCR in the PVTT group was 68.5% (37/54), with an LCR of 82.7% (24/29) in Vp3 and 52.0% (13/25) in Vp4 (p = 0.0204). The results indicate that the tumor response rate predicted by the GRIm score is better than that of the PVTT status. The tumor response rates in the GRIm LR group were the highest. The LCR of Vp3 was superior to that of Vp4.

Cox regression analysis

The results of the Cox regression analysis are shown in Table 3. The univariate Cox regression analysis showed that BCLC stage, ALBI levels, Child-Pugh scores, AFP levels, PVTT status, and GRIm scores were factors affecting OS and PFS (p < 0.05). The multivariate Cox regression analysis showed that PVTT status and GRIm scores were factors affecting OS (p < 0.05). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses showed that the HRs for GRIm scores affecting OS were 3.95 and 2.55 higher than those of PVTT. The results indicate that PVTT status and GRIm scores are independent prognostic factors for OS, and that GRIm scores have better independent prognostic value for OS than PVTT status.

Adverse events

The adverse events (AEs) that occurred in the study patients are shown in Table 4. Most patients experienced grade 1 / 2 tolerable AEs, including fatigue, nausea/vomiting, pruritus / rash, hypothyroidism, elevated alanine aminotransferase and aspartate aminotransferase levels, and decreased albumin levels. The most common AEs (> 60.0%) were fatigue, pruritus/rash, hypothyroidism, elevated transaminase levels, and decreased albumin levels. The incidence of grade 3 / 4 AEs was less than 5.6%. Of the patients who were Child-Pugh category A pre-treatment, 6.3% (6 / 96) progressed to Child-Pugh B post-treatment, and 12.7% (6 / 47) progressed from Child-Pugh B pre-treatment to Child-Pugh C post-treatment. Of the patients who were ALBI grade 1 pre-treatment, 3.7% (3 / 82) progressed to grade 2 post-treatment, and 8.2% (5 / 61) progressed from ALBI grade 2 pre-treatment to grade 3 post-treatment. No AEs related to radiotherapy, such as severe jaundice, ascites, and liver dysfunction, were reported. No patient experienced grade 5 AEs or death.

Discussion

The results of our study demonstrated that the response rates in the GRIm LR group were superior to those in the GRIm HR group. In contrast, no significant differences were seen in response rates between the no-PVTT group and the PVTT group. The trend in tumor responses predicted by GRIm scores was consistent with previous literature reports [26, 27]. Our findings indicate that the GRIm score could predict the response rates of patients with advanced HCC who received a combination of ICI, targeted drugs, and radiotherapy more effectively than the PVTT status. The possible reason for this is that PVTT reflects the invasiveness of the tumor, whereas the GRIm score represents the tumor’s immune status. The tumor response is mainly a consequence of the combined effect of ICIs and targeted drugs. Additionally, the results of radiotherapy typically take a relatively long time to become evident.

PVTT represents a significant bottleneck in the treatment of advanced HCC and is an independent factor influencing PFS [18, 28]. In our research, the results of univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses revealed that PVTT status and the GRIm score were independent prognostic factors of OS. The hazard ratios of GRIm scores with respect to OS were higher than those of the PVTT status. Additionally, GRIm scores were found to be an independent prognostic factor affecting not only OS but also PFS. Overall, our findings suggest that the GRIm-Score has a more substantial impact on both OS and PFS compared to PVTT, possibly because PVTT status reflects the invasiveness of the tumor. Its presence often leads to rapid tumor progression, impaired liver function, and more direct and adverse effects on patient survival. Conversely, a better immune status of the body, as indicated by the GRIm score, is more likely to confer patients with enhanced anti-tumor capabilities, which enables them to achieve more favorable survival outcomes.

Our study results demonstrated significantly prolonged OS and PFS in the GRIm LR and no-PVTT groups compared to the GRIm HR and PVTT groups. Correspondingly, the risks of death and disease progression in these groups were notably reduced. The survival trends predicted by GRIm scores and PVTT status are consistent with previous reports [15, 29, 30]. The results suggest that both the GRIm scores and PVTT status possess good predictive capabilities. One possible explanation is that the tumor microenvironment in the GRIm HR group is predominantly characterized by immune suppression. For instance, high neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratios and low albumin levels weaken the efficacy of ICIs. Moreover, the systemic inflammatory state, reflected by elevations in lactate dehydrogenase, is more severe, decreasing the tumor’s sensitivity to radiotherapy. In contrast, patients with PVTT have more aggressive tumors and, thus, a higher probability of experiencing further spread and metastasis, which has a more pronounced impact on liver function. Additionally, these patients are less likely to receive standardized treatment.

The results of the analysis of GRIm scores and PVTT status demonstrated that both OS and PFS were comparable between the GRIm LR and no-PVTT groups. However, the OS and PFS in the GRIm HR group were shorter than in the PVTT group. These findings indicate no significant differences in OS and PFS between the GRIm LR and no-PVTT groups. One possible reason could be that liver cell function, immune function, and treatment response in the GRIm LR and no-PVTT groups were superior to those in the GRIm HR and PVTT groups because each of the GRIm HR and PVTT groups had at least one factor that negatively affected prognosis. Having either GRIm HR or PVTT alone could have a substantial impact on survival outcomes.

The analysis of combining the GRIm scores and PVTT status demonstrated that both OS and PFS in group A were significantly longer than in groups B, C, and D. Specifically, OS and PFS in group B were notably longer than in groups C and D, and OS and PFS om group C were significantly longer than in group D. The reason for these findings might be that group A lacked two major prognostic factors, namely a high GRIm score and PVTT, whereas group D had both. The risk of treatment resistance is greater in patients with both a high GRIm score and PVTT or with either a high GRIm score or PVTT. Therefore, an intensified treatment protocol, such as radiotherapy combined with immune-targeted therapy plus transcatheter arterial chemoembolization, should be considered. The incidence of AEs in our study was consistent with the results of our previous study [31], in which grade 1/2 AEs were commonly observed. The results indicate that radiotherapy combined with immune-targeted therapy for advanced HCC is safe.

Conclusions

The preliminary results of our study indicate that GRIm scores are superior to PVTT status in predicting tumor response. PVTT status and GRIm scores were shown to be independent prognostic factors affecting OS, with GRIm scores having better independent prognostic value than PVTT status. No significant difference in the OS and PFS was seen between the GRIm LR group and the no-PVTT group, whereas the OS and PFS in the GRIm HR group differed significantly when PVTT was present. Survival rates were the best for patients with GRIm LR without PVTT and the worst for those with GRIm HR and PVTT. Thus, patients with GRIm LR without PVTT are the optimal candidates for treatment with ICIs combined with targeted drugs and radiotherapy. Combining the GRIm score with the PVTT status is helpful for screening optimal candidates and selecting individualized treatment regimens. The limitation of this study is that it was a retrospective study and, thus, lacked prospective and randomized controlled trials. Furthermore, the mechanisms corresponding to the findings, such as the expression of PD-(L)1 and tumor mutation burden, were not further explored. Therefore, the main objective of future research is to conduct prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled studies.

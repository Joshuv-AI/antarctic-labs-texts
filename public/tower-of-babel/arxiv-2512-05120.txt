Discrete Homotopy and Promise Constraint Satisfaction Problem
Arash Beikmohammadi, Andrei A. Bulatov

Abstract
The Promise Constraint Satisfaction Problem (PCSP for short) is a generalization of the well-studied Constraint Satisfaction Problem (CSP). The PCSP has its roots in such classic problems as the Approximate Graph Coloring and the ( 1 + ε ) (1+\varepsilon) -Satisfiability problems. The area received much attention recently with multiple approaches developed to design efficient algorithms for restricted versions of the PCSP, and to prove its hardness. One such approach uses methods from Algebraic Topology to relate the complexity of the PCSP to the structure of the fundamental group of certain topological spaces. In this paper, we attempt to develop a discrete analog of this approach by replacing topological structures with combinatorial constructions and some basic group-theoretic concepts. We consider the ‘one-dimensional’ case of the approach. We introduce and prove the basics of the framework, show how it is related to the complexity of the PCSP, and obtain several hardness results, including the existing ones, as applications of our approach. The main hope, however, is that this discrete variant of the topological approach can be generalized to the ‘multi-dimensional’ case needed for further progress.

Step 1. Simplicial complexes.

In the next step simplicial complexes are converted into topological spaces. A

It is again possible to define polymorphisms from a

Step 3. Fundamental groups.

Fundamental groups of topological spaces are an indispensable tool in algebraic topology,

For groups

Starting from graphs

by composing all the minion homomorphisms in the three steps one obtains a homomorphism from

Our results.

In this paper we attempt to develop a discrete analog of the topological approach outlined above by replacing topological structures with combinatorial constructions and some basic group-theoretic concepts. Firstly, we try to isolate the essential components of the approach in order to make as general as possible, in particular by extending the technique to relational structures that have more than one predicate and the predicates are not necessarily binary, making the connection between relational structures and simplicial complexes less rigid, and by relaxing the requirements on
\mathbb{Z}_{2}

(\textbf{A},\textbf{B})
and
(\textbf{C},\textbf{D})
be
\PCSP
templates. If there is a minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol(\textbf{A},\textbf{B})\rightarrow\Pol(\textbf{C},\textbf{D})
, then
\PCSP(\textbf{C},\textbf{D})
is log-space reducible to
\PCSP(\textbf{A},\textbf{B})
.

Let
A
be a relational structure with the base set
A
. A
primitive-positive (pp-)formula

\Phi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{r})
is a first-order formula that only uses existential quantifiers, conjunction, predicates of
A
, and the equality relation. The
r
-ary relation
R
consisting of all tuples
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
such that
\Phi(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
is true is said to be
pp-definable
in
A
. For a PCSP template a pp-formula

defines a pair of relations
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
in each of the structures
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
. The property of pp-definable relations we need (
[
5
, Theorem 2.26]
, see also
[
8
,
33
]
) is that every polymorphism of
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
is also a polymorphism of
((A;R^{\textbf{A}}),(B;R^{\textbf{B}}))
.

Hardness condition.

A coordinate
i
of a function
f:A^{n}\rightarrow B
is called
essential
if there exist
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in A
and
b_{i}\in A
such that

f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},a_{i},a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n})\neq f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i-1},b_{i},a_{i+1},\ldots,a_{n}).

A minion
\mathcal{M}
is said to have a
bounded essential arity
if there exists a constant number
k
such that any function
f
in
\mathcal{M}
has at most
k
essential coordinates.

Theorem 2.5
.

[
14
, Proposition 5.14]

Let
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
be a template. Assume that there exists a minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
for some minion
\mathcal{M}
on a pair of (possibly infinite) sets such that
\mathcal{M}
has bounded essential arity and does not contain a constant function (i.e., a function without essential coordinates). Then
\PCSP({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
is NP-hard.

As an example of an application of Theorem
2.5
consider the problem
\PCSP(\textbf{K}_{3},\textbf{K}_{4})
. It is possible to construct a minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol(\textbf{K}_{3},\textbf{K}_{4})\rightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
, where
\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
is the minion of projections on
\{0,1\}
. Any function
f\in\mathcal{P}_{\{0,1\}}
has exactly
1
essential coordinate (which also implies
f
is not constant), so by Theorem
2.5

\PCSP(\textbf{K}_{3},\textbf{K}_{4})
is NP-hard.

If we have a minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
, then the image of
\xi
, denoted by
\img_{\xi}(\mathcal{M})
, is also a minion and
\xi^{\prime}:\Pol({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})\rightarrow\img_{\xi}(\mathcal{M})
where
\xi^{\prime}(f):=\xi(f)
is a minion homomorphism. This implies the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
be a template. Assume that there exists a minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
for some minion
\mathcal{M}
on a pair of (possibly infinite) sets such that
\img_{\xi}(\mathcal{M})
has bounded essential arity and does not contain a constant function (i.e., a function without essential coordinates). Then
\PCSP({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
is NP-hard.

Finitely presented groups.

We will also use some basic concepts of finitely presented groups
[
34
]
. A
presentation
of a group
G
consists of a set
S
of generators, and a set
R
of relations among those generators. A presentation is finite if both
S
and
R
are finite. Then elements of
G
are represented by
group words
that is formal products of generators and their inverses. A relation is an equality of the form
w_{1}=w_{2}
, where
w_{1},w_{2}
are group words. Relations can be used to transform group words by replacing some occurrences of
w_{1}
or
w_{1}^{-1}
with
w_{2},w_{2}^{-1}
, respectively, or the other way round. Words that can be transformed to one another represent the same element of
G
. A
free group
is a group presented by
S
and
R
, where
R=\emptyset
. A word in a free group
F
is reduced if it does not contain a subword
aa^{-1}
or
a^{-1}a
for a generator
a
. Every element in
F
is equal to a reduced word and all reduced words are different.
A free abelian group is an example of a free group with relations
ab=ba
for any pair of generators
a,b\in S
.

3
Groups and PCSP templates

In this section we study how analogues of homotopy groups can be introduced for a relational structure. While
[
28
]
first construct a simplicial complex from a relational structure and then study its properties and how it gives rise to a topological space, we start with an arbitrary simplicial complex (or, equivalently, a hypergraph) on a relation pp-definable in a relational structure and try to extract the conditions essential for our purposes.

3.1
Edge-path groups

First, we remind the notion of the
edge-path group
of a simplicial complex, see
[
35
]
. Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)
be a simplicial complex (a hypergraph). The graph
G(\mathcal{H})
of
\mathcal{H}
is defined as follows: the vertex set of
G(\mathcal{H})
is
V
and
u,v\in V
are connected with an edge if and only if
s,t\in A
for some
A\in E
. We consider paths and cycles in
G(\mathcal{H})
. A cycle
t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
,
t_{1}=t_{k}
, is called a
null cycle
if
k=2
. Two paths
P_{1}=t_{1},\ldots,t_{k}
and
P_{2}=t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{r}
are said to be
homotopic
, denoted
P_{1}\sim P_{2}
, if it is possible to transform
P_{1}
to
P_{2}
by a sequence of transformations of the following two types (called
expansions
and
contractions
): (a) If an element
t\in V
belongs to the same face as
t_{i}
,
t_{i+1}
for some
i\in[k-1]
, then replace
P_{1}
by
t_{1},\ldots,t_{i},t,t_{i+1},\ldots,t_{k}
; and (b) if
t_{i}
,
t_{i+1}
, and
t_{i+2}
belong to the same face for some
i\in[k-2]
, then replace
P_{1}
by
t_{1},\ldots,t_{i},t_{i+2},\ldots,t_{k}
.

Note that if two paths
P_{1}
and
P_{2}
are homotopic, they have the same initial and terminal elements, i.e.,
t_{1}=t^{\prime}_{1}
and
t_{k}=t^{\prime}_{r}
since expansions and contractions do not change the initial and terminal elements. A cycle
C
is said to be
null-homotopic
if it is homotopic to a null cycle.

Given two cycles
C_{1}=t_{1},\ldots,t_{k},t_{1}
and
C_{2}=t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{r},t^{\prime}_{1}
starting at the same element
t_{1}=t^{\prime}_{1}
, we define
C_{1}\cdot C_{2}
to be the concatenation of them, i.e.,
C_{1}\cdot C_{2}=t_{1},\ldots,t_{k},t_{1},t^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{r},t_{1}
. The
homotopy class
of a cycle
C
is the set of cycles that are homotopic to
C
. We denote the homotopy class of a cycle
C
by
[C]
.
Multiplication on the set of homotopy classes is defined in the natural way: If
C_{1}=t_{1},\ldots,t_{k},t_{1}
and
C_{2}=t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{r},t^{\prime}_{1}
with
t_{1}=t^{\prime}_{1}
, then
[C_{1}]\cdot[C_{2}]:=[C_{1}\cdot C_{2}]
. It is not hard to see (cf.
[
35
, Chapter 4]
) that the set of homotopy classes along with the operation
\cdot
is a group denoted
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H},t_{1})
. Note that for any
t^{\prime}
from the same connected component of
G(\mathcal{H})
as
t_{1}
, the groups
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H},t_{1})
and
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H},t^{\prime})
are isomorphic. So, we will often use
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H})
rather than
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H},t_{1})
, at least when
G(\mathcal{H})
is connected.
(Note that the definitions of homotopic, null-homotopic and
\pi_{1}
are similar to the ones in algebraic topology
[
25
]
.)

Next, we introduce another group related to
\mathcal{H}
. This group is finitely presented and we will then show that the two groups are isomorphic. Fix a vertex
t_{1}
of
G(\mathcal{H})
and let
\mathcal{T}
be a spanning tree of the connected component of
G(\mathcal{H})
containing
t_{1}
. We call such a tree a
maximal tree
containing
t_{1}
(if
G(\mathcal{H})
is connected, then any maximal tree would be a spanning tree of
G(\mathcal{H})
). By
V(\mathcal{T})
and
E(\mathcal{T})
we denote the vertex set and the edge set of
\mathcal{T}
, respectively. Consider the finitely presented group, denoted by
\mathbb{G}^{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{T}}
, whose generators are the (oriented) edges of the connected component of
G(\mathcal{H})
that contains
t_{1}
, the identity element is denoted by
\mathbbm{1}
, and the set of relations is:

(G1)

If
e\in E(\mathcal{T})
, then
e=e^{-1}=\mathbbm{1}
;

(G2)

if
t\in V(\mathcal{T})
, then
(tt)=\mathbbm{1}
;

(G3)

for elements
\{t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}\}\in V(\mathcal{T})
that belong to the same face, it holds that
(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{3})\cdot(t_{3}t_{1})=\mathbbm{1}
. In particular,
(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{3})=(t_{3}t_{1})^{-1}
.

Note that relation (G3) also implies that
(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{1})=\mathbbm{1}
. Indeed, if
t_{3}=t_{1}
, then

(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{1})=(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{1})\cdot(t_{1}t_{1})=\mathbbm{1}
by (G3).

The mapping
\rho^{t_{1},\mathcal{T}}
from
\pi_{1}(\mathcal{H},t_{1})
to
\mathbb{G}^{\mathcal{H},\mathcal{T}}
is defined as follows. Let
C=t_{1},t_{2},\ldots,t_{k},t_{1}
be a cycle. Then we define
\rho^{t_{1},\mathcal{T}}([C]):=(t_{1}t_{2})\cdot(t_{2}t_{3})\ldots(t_{k-1}t_{k})\cdot(t_{k}t_{1})
. The following lemma can be found in
[
35

Lemma 3.1

(
[
35
]

Let
\mathcal{H}=(V,E)

Proof.

First of all,
\rho^{t_{1},\mathcal{T}}

Expansion.
If

Contraction.
This case is similar.

It is straightforward that

3.2
Edge-path group of a relational structure

Another ingredient of the approach in
[
28
]
is the claim that faces of simplicial complexes defined on a PCSP template (graphs in
[
28
]
) are preserved under polymorphisms. We introduce a more general version of this connection.

Let
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
be a PCSP template and
\Phi
a pp-formula with
r
free variables in the vocabulary of
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
. Let
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
be the
r
-ary relations defined by
\Phi
in
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
, respectively. Let also
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}},\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
be simplicial complexes on
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
, respectively. The pair
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}},\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
is said to be
polymorphism stable
if for any polymorphism
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
of
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
and any faces
\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}
of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}}
the set

f(\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{n}):=\{f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\;|\;t_{i}\in\sigma_{i}\}

is a face of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
, where
f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
is computed coordinate-wise.

Next, we describe a construction that gives an example of how polymorphism-stable simplicial complexes can be obtained. In particular, it includes all the existing applications of the topological method in PCSP. In addition to
\Phi
and
R
let
\Psi
be another pp-formula in the vocabulary of
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
, with
2r
free variables, such that
\Psi
defines relations
Q^{\textbf{A}},Q^{\textbf{B}}
in
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
, respectively, and such that
Q^{\textbf{A}},Q^{\textbf{B}}
are binary reflexive and symmetric relations on
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
, respectively. A binary reflexive and symmetric relation is often called a
tolerance
and we will be using this term from now on. In particular, for
R
and
Q
as above, we will say that
Q
is a tolerance of
R
. The following is an example of a tolerance when
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
are graphs.

Example 3.3
.

Let
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
be a PCSP template where
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
are graphs. Let
R^{\textbf{A}}=E({\textbf{A}}),R^{\textbf{B}}=E({\textbf{B}})
, i.e., the formula
\Phi
is just
E(x,y)
. For
\Psi
consider
\Psi(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})=(y_{1}=y_{2})
. Then
Q^{\textbf{A}},Q^{\textbf{B}}
consist of pairs
((s_{1},t_{1}),(s_{2},t_{2}))
of
{\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}}
, respectively, with
t_{1}=t_{2}
.

If
Q
is a tolerance on a set
A
, a
class
of
Q
is a subset
B\subseteq A
such that
(a,b)\in Q
for any
a,b\in B
. A
maximal class
of
Q
is defined as a maximal set like this in terms of inclusion. Unlike equivalence classes, maximal classes of a tolerance may overlap.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
R
be a relation pp-definable in a PCSP template
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
and
Q
its tolerance. Let also
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}},\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
denote simplicial complexes on
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
, respectively, whose faces are classes of the tolerances
Q^{\textbf{A}},Q^{\textbf{B}}
respectively. Then the pair
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}},\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
is polymorphism-stable.

Proof.

It suffices to show that for any
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n},t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{n}\in R^{\textbf{A}}
such that
(t_{i},t^{\prime}_{i})\in Q^{\textbf{A}}
,
i\in[n]
, it holds that
(f(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}),f(t^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,t^{\prime}_{n}))\in Q^{\textbf{B}}
. This however is straightforward as
Q
is pp-definable in
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
.
∎

The approach of
[
28
]
uses a specific definition of faces that we are going to state now, again in a slightly more general setting, and show that it fits into our framework.

Let
{\textbf{A}}=(A,R^{\textbf{A}})
be relational structure with a single
r
-ary relation. A
face
of the relation
R^{{\textbf{A}}}
is a set
\sigma\subseteq R^{\textbf{A}}
of the form
\sigma\subseteq X_{1}\times\dots\times X_{r}\subseteq R^{\textbf{A}}
for some

. We now introduce a tolerance whose classes are exactly the faces of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}}
.

For
i\in[r]
, let
Q_{i}
denote a generalization of the tolerance introduced in Example
3.3
, that is,
(a,b)\in Q_{i}
if and only if
a_{j}=b_{j}
for

and

Since each of

and

We say that a group

(A1)

Every Abelian subgroup of

As is easily seen, this condition is equivalent to the following one:

(A2)

Every nontrivial Abelian subgroup is contained in finitely many maximal Abelian subgroups.

Lemma 3.9

Any finitely generated free group is Abelian-bounded.

Proof.

Recall that

is an isomorphism with

and

.

5.1
New proofs of the existing results

The absence of minion homomorphisms.

As the first application, we use our techniques to prove the nonexistence of a minion homomorphism.
Some of the known NP-hard proofs of PCSP problems, such as
\PCSP(\textbf{C}_{2k+1},\textbf{K}_{3})

[
28
]
,
\PCSP(\textbf{C}_{2k+1},\textbf{K}_{4})

[
2
]
, and
\PCSP(\textbf{LO}_{3},\textbf{LO}_{4})

[
21
]
are based on finding a minion homomorphism from the polymorphisms of the PCSP template to
\Pol(\mathbb{Z},\textbf{G})
for some group
G
. We prove that this approach does not work for AGCP.

The proof of the following proposition is based on the fact that if two polymorphisms are different in exactly one point, then they have the same image with respect to minion homomorphism
\xi
. A similar property of topological maps associated with polymorphisms was also mentioned in
[
21
]
.

Proposition 5.1
.

For
k\geq 3,t\geq k^{3}
, any non-trivial group
G
, any minion homomorphism
\xi:\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})\rightarrow\Pol(\mathbb{Z},\textbf{G})
, and any
f\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
, the function
\xi(f)
is constant.

Proof.

By contradiction, let
\xi
be such a minion homomorphism. For any
f\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
, let
\sel(f)
be the essential coordinates of
\xi(f)
. It is obvious that
|\sel(f)|\geq 1
since
\xi(f)
is not a constant function. We say two
n
-ary polymorphisms
f,g\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
are of the same type if
f(x,\ldots,x)=g(x,\ldots,x)
for any
x\in\textbf{K}_{k}
.

Claim 5.36
.

Let
f,g\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
be two polymorphisms of the same type and of arities
n\geq 3
. Suppose
f
and
g
are different in exactly one point, i.e., there exists a unique sequence of elements
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\textbf{K}_{k}
such that
f(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})\neq g(a_{1},\ldots,a_{n})
. Then
\xi(f)=\xi(g)
.

Proof.
First of all, note that there exists
p,q\in[n]
such that
a_{p}\neq a_{q}
. For any
i\in[n]
, we set
\xi(f)_{i}:=\xi(f)(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
and
\xi(g)_{i}:=\xi(g)(0,\ldots,0,1,0,\ldots,0)
where there is a
1
in the
i
-th coordinate and
0
’s in other coordinates. To obtain a contradiction, suppose
\xi(f)\neq\xi(g)
, then there is a set
S
with
|S|\geq 1
such that
\xi(f)_{i}\neq\xi(g)_{i}
for any
i\in S
and
\xi(f)_{i}=\xi(g)_{i}
for
i\in[n]\setminus S
. We have three cases based on the number of elements in
S
.

•

|S|=1
: let
S=\{i\}
. There exists
j\in[n]
such that
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
. Let
\pi:[n]\rightarrow[n-1]
be a surjective map with
\pi(i)=\pi(j)=1
. Then
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}=\xi(f)_{i}\cdot\xi(f)_{j}
and
\xi(g^{\pi})_{1}=\xi(g)_{i}\cdot\xi(g)_{j}
. Since
\xi(f)_{i}\neq\xi(g)_{i}
and
\xi(f)_{j}=\xi(g)_{j}
, we get
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}\neq\xi(g^{\pi})_{1}
which implies
\xi(f^{\pi})\neq\xi(g^{\pi})
. This is a contradiction since
f^{\pi}=g^{\pi}
.

•

|S|=2
: let
S=\{i,j\}
. There exist
l\in[n]\setminus S
such that either
a_{i}\neq a_{l}
or
a_{j}\neq a_{l}
. With no loss of generality assume that
a_{i}\neq a_{l}
. Let
\pi:[n]\rightarrow[n-1]
be a surjective map with
\pi(i)=\pi(l)=1
. Then
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}=\xi(f)_{i}\cdot\xi(f)_{l}
and
\xi(g^{\pi})_{1}=\xi(g)_{i}\cdot\xi(g)_{l}
. Since
\xi(f)_{i}\neq\xi(g)_{i}
and
\xi(f)_{l}=\xi(g)_{l}
, we get
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}\neq\xi(g^{\pi})_{1}
which implies
\xi(f^{\pi})\neq\xi(g^{\pi})
. This is a contradiction since
f^{\pi}=g^{\pi}
.

•

|S|\geq 3
: let
\{i,j,l\}\subseteq S
. There exists
r\in[n]
such that
a_{i}\neq a_{r}
. If
j\neq l
, let
\pi:[n]\rightarrow[n-1]
be a surjective map with
\pi(i)=\pi(r)=1
and
\pi(l)=2
. Then
\xi(f^{\pi})_{2}=\xi(f)_{l}
and
\xi(g^{\pi})_{2}=\xi(g)_{l}
. Since
\xi(f)_{l}\neq\xi(g)_{l}
, we get
\xi(f^{\pi})_{2}\neq\xi(g^{\pi})_{2}
which implies
\xi(f^{\pi})\neq\xi(g^{\pi})
. This is a contradiction since
f^{\pi}=g^{\pi}
. If
r\neq j
the argument is similar.

(\square)

Claim 5.37
.

Any two ternary polymorphisms
f,g\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
of the same type have the same image under
\xi
, i.e.,
\xi(f)=\xi(g)
.

Proof.
Let us start with some definitions. Any sequence
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}\in\textbf{K}_{k}
is said to be a point; it is called a constant point if
a_{1}=a_{2}=a_{3}
.
The value of
f(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
is called a color of a point
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
which can be from
1
to
t
.
At each step, we may change the color of
f(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
where
a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
is a non-constant point such that the resulted coloring of points remains a polymorphism. Since
t\geq k^{3}
, there is a sequence of actions that changes the colors of one non-constant point at a time such that in the end, any two non-constant points have different colors. With some work, it is not hard to show that there is a sequence of actions that changes the color of one point at a time such that the end polymorphism would be
g
. By Claim
5.36
, after changing the color of one point, the image of the resulted polymorphism under
\xi
does not change. Therefore, the images of all the polymorphisms involved are the same, in particular
f
and
g
, which completes the proof of the claim.

(\square)

We continue with the proof of Proposition
5.1
. Let
f\in\Pol(\textbf{K}_{k},\textbf{K}_{t})
be a polymorphism of arity
n
. Based on the size of
\sel(f)
we have two cases.

•

|\sel(f)|=1
: let
\sel(f)=\{i\}
for
i\in[n]
. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}:[n]\rightarrow[3]
be maps with
\pi(i)=1
and
\pi^{\prime}(i)=2
. The only essential coordinates of
\xi(f)^{\pi}
and
\xi(f)^{\pi^{\prime}}
are
1
and
2
, respectively. Therefore,
\xi(f^{\pi})\neq\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})
. Both
f^{\pi}
and
f^{\pi^{\prime}}
are ternary polymorphisms of the same type. So by Claim
5.37
,
\xi(f^{\pi})=\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})
, a contradiction.

•

|\sel(f)|\geq 2
: let
\{i,j\}\subseteq\sel(f)
. Let
\pi,\pi^{\prime}:[n]\rightarrow[3]
be maps with
\pi(i)=\pi(j)=1
,
\pi(l)\neq 1
for
l\neq i,j
, and
\pi^{\prime}(i)=1
,
\pi^{\prime}(l)\neq 1
for
l\neq i
. Obviously,
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}=\xi(f)_{i}\cdot\xi(f)_{j}
and
\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})_{1}=\xi(f)_{i}
. So
\xi(f^{\pi})_{1}\neq\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})_{1}
.
Therefore,
\xi(f^{\pi})\neq\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})
. Both
f^{\pi}
and
f^{\pi^{\prime}}
are ternary polymorphisms of the same type. So by Claim
5.37
,
\xi(f^{\pi})=\xi(f^{\pi^{\prime}})
, a contradiction.

∎

Constructing
\mu
-templates.

We start with an example of how a
\mu
-template can be constructed. A relation
R
is said to be
cyclic
if for any
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in R
the tuple
(a_{r},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-1})
also belongs to
R
.
Let
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
be a PCSP template and a relation
R
such that
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
are cyclic is pp-definable in
({\textbf{A}},{\textbf{B}})
. Let a pair
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}},\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
of simplicial complexes on
R^{\textbf{A}},R^{\textbf{B}}
be defined as follows: The faces of
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{A}}
(
\mathcal{H}^{\textbf{B}}
) are the sets of tuples of the form
A_{1}\times\dots\times A_{r}\subseteq R^{\textbf{A}}
(respectively,
B_{1}\times\dots\times B_{r}\subseteq R^{\textbf{B}}
), which is the standard way of defining simplicial complexes in the literature and also in Lemma
3.5
.
For tuples
t=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})\in R^{\textbf{A}},t^{\prime}=(a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{r})\in R^{\textbf{B}}
, we define the action of
\mu^{\textbf{A}}
and
\mu^{\textbf{B}}
as follows:

\mu^{\textbf{A}}(t):=(a_{r},a_{1},\ldots,a_{r-1})\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{\textbf{B}}(t^{\prime}):=(a^{\prime}_{r},a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{r-1}).

A tuple
(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
is called
periodic
if for some
k\in[r-1]
, we have
a_{i}=a_{(i+k)\bmod r}
for any
i\in[r]
. (Note that
a_{0}=a_{r}
). For example, any constant tuple is periodic, and if
r
is a prime number, then only constant tuples are periodic. Observe that if
R^{\textbf{A}}
has no periodic tuples, then for any face
\sigma
and
i\in[r-1]
, we have
\mu^{i}(\sigma)\neq\sigma
. (If
\mu^{i}(\sigma)=\sigma
for some
1<i<r
, then by Lemma
3.5
there exists a tuple
t\in\sigma
such that
\mu^{i}(t)=t
). Moreover,
\mu^{r}(\sigma)=\sigma
for any face
\sigma
. The same property holds for
R^{\textbf{B}}
, and
\mu
satisfies condition (M3). Conditions (M1) and (M2) are straightforward.

\mu
-template of this type have been intensively studied in the literature including that on the PCSP (see, e.g.,
[
28
]
). The majority of the results fit in this framework for
r=2
and
[
21
]
considers such a construction for
r=3
.

Now, let

Corollary 5.43

\PCSP(\textbf{B}_{n},\textbf{D}_{n})

Proof.

The structures
\textbf{B}_{n}
and
\textbf{D}_{n}
are both connected (and therefore

-connected), and there is no periodic tuple in both

and

1

First, we define an operator
\oplus_{n}
on
[n]

and
n
are identified. Similarly, we define an operator
\ominus

[n]

and
n
are identified. For any

be the face that contains the
(i,j)
-cyclic,
(i,j\oplus_{n}1)
-cyclic,
(i\ominus_{n}1,j)
-cyclic, and
(i\ominus_{n}1,j\oplus_{n}1)
-cyclic tuples. Suppose
\sigma_{j,i}
be the face where all

’s in all tuples of
\sigma_{i,j}
are swapped. It is not hard to see that
\sigma_{i,j}

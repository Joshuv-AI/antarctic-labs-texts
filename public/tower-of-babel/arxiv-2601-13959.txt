A Bregman Regularized Proximal Point Method for Solving Equilibrium Problems on Hadamard Manifolds
Shikher Sharma, Simeon Reich

Abstract
In this paper we develop a Bregman regularized proximal point algorithm for solving monotone equilibrium problems on Hadamard manifolds. It has been shown that the regularization term induced by a Bregman function is, in general, nonconvex on Hadamard manifolds unless the curvature is zero. Nevertheless, we prove that the proposed Bregman regularization scheme does converge to a solution of the equilibrium problem on Hadamard manifolds in the presence of a strong assumption on the convexity of the set formed by the regularization term. Moreover, we employ a coercivity condition on the Bregman function which is weaker than those typically assumed in the existing literature on Bregman regularization. Numerical experiments on illustrative examples demonstrate the practical effectiveness of our proposed method.

Introduction

The equilibrium problem has been widely explored and continues to attract significant research interest because of its broad range of applications. Many important mathematical models can be expressed as special cases of equilibrium problems, including optimization, Nash equilibrium, complementarity, fixed point, and variational inequality problems. Detailed discussions on the theory and developments of equilibrium problems can be found in the works of Blum and Oettli
6
, Bianchi and Schaible
5
, and references therein.

To solve equilibrium problems efficiently, various iterative and proximal-type algorithms have been developed in linear spaces. These methods often rely on regularization terms that ensure stability, monotonicity, and convergence. Among these, Bregman functions and their associated distances have proved particularly powerful
24
. Bregman distances generalizes the squared Euclidean norm and enable geometry-aware formulations of proximal and mirror-descent methods
7
;
9
;
26
. Under suitable convexity and smoothness assumptions, such algorithms exhibit convergence and have been widely applied to equilibrium and variational inequality problems
25
;
8
.

Motivated by the success of Bregman-based methods in Euclidean spaces, recent research has sought to extend these ideas to Riemannian manifolds, especially Hadamard manifolds, which are complete, simply connected manifolds with nonpositive curvature. Such spaces provide a natural setting for optimization and variational analysis in non-linear geometries
21
;
22
. However, it has been shown that the regularization term induced by a Bregman function is, in general, nonconvex on Hadamard manifolds unless the curvature is zero
18
;
12
. This limitation makes the direct extension of many Bregman proximal algorithms challenging.

Parallel to these developments, the proximal point method on Hadamard manifolds has attracted substantial attention, with various works focusing on equilibrium problems in this setting; see, for example, Ferreira and Oliveira
15
, Li et al.
20
, and Tang et al.
30
. Further generalizations of classical Euclidean concepts to Riemannian manifolds have been proposed by Ledyaev and Zhu
19
, Ferreira et al.
16
, and Li et al.
10
. One of the major advantages of such an extension lies in the ability to transform certain nonconvex problems into convex ones by carefully choosing the Riemannian metric
23
;
13
;
4
;
10
.

Note that Hadamard manifolds generally do not have a linear structure, which indicates that properties, techniques, and algorithms in linear spaces cannot be used in Hadamard manifolds. Therefore, it is valuable and interesting to study algorithms for solving equilibrium problems in Hadamard manifolds.

Let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of an Hadamard manifold
\mathcal{M}
and let
F\colon C\times C\to\mathbb{R}

be a bifunction satisfying the property
F(x,x)=0
for all
x\in C
. Then the equilibrium problem on Hadamard manifolds is formulated as follows:

\text{Find }x^{*}\in C\text{ such that }F(x^{*},y)\geq 0\text{ for all }y\in C.

(1)

We denote problem (
1
) by
\ep(F,C)
and the solution set of problem (
1
) by
\Omega(F,C)
.

A natural extension of the resolvent of a bifunction, as introduced by Hirstoaga
11
in the linear setting, was proposed in
10
for Hadamard manifolds. However, the well-definedness of this extension is based on the convexity of the function
y\mapsto\langle u,\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle
,
u\in\mathcal{T}_{x}M
, which has been shown not to be convex in general
18
;
12
.

To overcome this issue, Bento et al.
2
proposed a regularized version of the proximal point algorithm for equilibrium problem (
1
) on Hadamard manifolds by introducing a new regularization term that is convex on Hadamard manifolds. Subsequently, Bento et al.
3
defined a new resolvent for bifunctions based on Busemann functions. This formulation not only extends the classical resolvent in linear spaces, as originally proposed by Combettes and Hirstoaga
11
, but also ensures that the regularization term remains convex on general Hadamard manifolds. More recently, Bento et al.
1
further investigated this Busemann-based resolvent and developed a proximal point algorithm for solving equilibrium problems on Hadamard manifolds. Building upon this, Sharma et al.
28
recently introduced a regularized extragradient method for solving equilibrium problems using the Busemann framework.

Despite these advances, most existing methods rely on strong convexity or coercivity assumptions on the regularization term to guarantee convergence. This motivates the development of new convergence results that hold under weaker assumptions, including the convergence of Bregman-based methods on Hadamard manifolds without requiring the convexity of the regularization term.

In this work we develop a Bregman regularized proximal point algorithm for solving monotone equilibrium problems on Hadamard manifolds. A key contribution of our study is the introduction of a coercivity condition on the Bregman function which is weaker than those commonly assumed in the literature
25
. This extension broadens the applicability of Bregman-type methods to a wider class of problems and geometries.

Preliminaries

For the basic definitions and results regarding the geometry of Riemannian manifolds, we refer to
14
;
27
;
31
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an
m
-dimensional differentiable manifold and let
x\in\mathcal{M}
. The set of all tangent vectors at the point
x
, denoted by
\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
, is called the tangent space of
\mathcal{M}
at
x
. It forms a real vector space of dimension
m
. The tangent bundle of
\mathcal{M}
is defined as the disjoint union
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}=\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{M}}\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
, which is a smooth manifold of dimension
2m
.

We suppose that
\mathcal{M}
is equipped with a Riemannian metric, that is, a smoothly varying family of inner products on the tangent spaces
\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
. The metric at
x\in\mathcal{M}
is denoted
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{x}\colon\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
.
A differentiable manifold
\mathcal{M}
with a Riemannian metric
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is said to be a Riemannian manifold.
The induced norm on
\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
is denoted by
\|\cdot\|_{x}
.

Let
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and let
\gamma\colon[a,b]\to\mathcal{M}
be a piecewise smooth curve joining
x
to
y
. Then the length of the curve
\gamma
is defined by

L(\gamma)\coloneq\int_{a}^{b}\|\gamma^{\prime}(t)\|dt
. The minimal length of all such curves joining
x
to
y
is called the Riemannian distance, denoted by
d(x,y)
, that is,
d(x,y)\coloneq\inf\{L(\gamma)\colon\gamma\in C_{xy}\}
, where
C_{xy}
denotes the set of all piecewise smooth curves
\gamma\colon[0,1]\to\mathcal{M}
such that
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y
.

A single-valued vector field on a differentiable manifold
\mathcal{M}
is a smooth mapping
V\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}
such that, for each
x\in\mathcal{M}
,
V(x)\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}
.
Let
\nabla
be the Levi-Civita connection associated with the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
.
A vector field
V
along
\gamma
is said to be parallel if
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}V=0
.
If
\gamma^{\prime}
is parallel along
\gamma
, that is,
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}}\gamma^{\prime}=0
, then
\gamma
is said to be a geodesic. In this case,
\|\gamma^{\prime}\|
is a constant. Moreover, if
\|\gamma^{\prime}\|=1
, then
\gamma
is called a unit-speed geodesic. A geodesic joining
x
to
y
in the Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
is said to be a minimal geodesic if its length is equal to
d(x,y)
. We denote a geodesic joining
x
and
y
by
\gamma(x,y;\cdot)
, that is,

\gamma(x,y;\cdot)\colon[a,b]\to\mathcal{M}
is such that, for
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, we have
\gamma(x,y;a)=x
and
\gamma(x,y;b)=y
.

The parallel transport on the tangent bundle
\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}
along the geodesic
\gamma
with respect to a Riemannian connection
\nabla
is defined to be the mapping

\mathrm{P}_{\gamma(x,y;b),\gamma(x,y;a)}\colon\mathcal{T}_{\gamma(x,y;a)}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{T}_{\gamma(x,y;b)}\mathcal{M}
such that

\mathrm{P}_{\gamma(x,y;b),\gamma(x,y;a)}(v)=V(\gamma(x,y;b))\text{ for all }a,b\in\mathbb{R},v\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M},

where
V
is the unique vector field satisfying
\nabla_{\gamma^{\prime}(x,y;t)}V=0
for all
t\in[a,b]
and
V(\gamma(x,y;a))=v
.

The Riemannian metric induces a mapping
f\mapsto\grad f
that associates to each differentiable function
f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}

a unique vector field
\grad f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{T}\mathcal{M}
, called the gradient of
f
, defined by

\langle\grad f(x),X(x)\rangle=df_{x}(X(x))

for all vector fields
X\colon\mathcal{M}\to T\mathcal{M}
and all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Here,
df_{x}
denotes the differential of
f
at the point
x\in\mathcal{M}
.

A Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
is said to be complete if for any
x\in\mathcal{M}
all geodesics emanating from
x
are defined for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. By the Hopf-Rinow Theorem
27
, if
\mathcal{M}
is complete, then any pair of points in
\mathcal{M}
can be joined by a minimal geodesic. Moreover,
(\mathcal{M},d)
is a complete metric space. If
\mathcal{M}
is a complete Riemannian manifold, then the exponential map
\exp_{x}\colon\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
at
x\in\mathcal{M}
is defined by
\exp_{x}v=\gamma_{x,v}(1)\quad\text{ for all }\quad v\in\mathcal{T}_{p}\mathcal{M}
,
where
\gamma_{x,v}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{M}
is the unique geodesic
starting from
x
with velocity
v
, that is,
\gamma_{x,v}(0)=p
and
\gamma^{\prime}_{x,v}(0)=v
. It is known that
\exp_{x}(tv)=\gamma_{x,v}(t)
for
each real number
t
and
\exp_{x}(\mathbf{0})=\gamma_{x,\mathbf{0}}(0)=x
, where
\mathbf{0}
is the zero tangent vector.

Definition 1

(
20
)
.

A complete simply connected Riemannian manifold of nonpositive sectional curvature is called an Hadamard manifold.

Since in an Hadamard manifold
\mathcal{M}
, any two points can be joined by a unique geodesic, we denote the parallel transport by
\underset{y\leftarrow x}{\mathrm{P}}
instead of
P_{\gamma(x,y;b),\gamma(x,y;a)}
.

Remark 1

(
20
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold.
If
x,y\in\mathcal{M}
and

Definition 2

(
20
)
.

A nonempty subset
C
of an Hadamard manifold
\mathcal{M}
is said to be geodesically convex if, for any two points
x
and

in

to

.

Definition 3

(
20
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold. A function
f\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
is said to be geodesically convex
if, for any geodesic

Lemma 1

(
20
)
.

Let
\{x_{n}\}
be a sequence in an Hadamard manifold
\mathcal{M}
such that

. Then, for any

\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}y\to\exp_{x}^{-1}y\mbox{ and }\exp_{y}^{-1}x_{n}\to\exp_{y}^{-1}x.

Remark 2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and
\triangle(x,y,z)
be a geodesic triangle in
\mathcal{M}
. Then the following inequalities hold:

(i)

d^{2}(x,y)+d^{2}(y,z)-2\langle\exp_{y}^{-1}x,\exp_{y}^{-1}z\rangle\leq d^{2}(z,x)
.

(ii)

d^{2}(x,y)\leq\langle\exp_{x}^{-1}z,\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle+\langle\exp_{y}^{-1}z,\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle
.

Remark 3
.

For a fixed

,
\grad\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,y)=-\exp_{x}^{-1}y
.

Definition 4

(

A function

Definition 6

(
17
)
.

Let
C
be a nonempty subset of an Hadamard manifold

\operatorname{Prox}_{\lambda f}(x)\coloneq\amin_{y\in C}\left(f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda}d^{2}(y,x)\right)\text{ for all }x\in C\text{ and for any }\lambda\in(0,\infty).

(
15

.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let

be a geodesically convex function. Let

be the sequence generated by the proximal point algorithm,

x_{n+1}=\amin_{y\in\mathcal{M}}\left\{f(y)+\frac{1}{2\lambda_{n}}d^{2}(x_{n},y)\right\}\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N}

.

In view of
(

, the proximal operator
\prox_{\lambda f}(x)
is single-valued. By the definition of

2.1
Equilibrium Problems

Let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of an Hadamard manifold
\mathcal{M}
and let
F\colon C\times C\to\mathbb{R}
be a bifunction.
We consider the following assumptions:

(C1)

F(x,x)=0
for all
x\in C
;

(C2)

F(\cdot,y)
is upper semicontinuous on
C
for each
y\in C
;

(C3)

F(x,\cdot)
is geodesically convex and lower semicontinuous on
C
for each
x\in C
;

(C4)

F
is monotone;

(C5)

Given a fixed
y_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
, consider a sequence
\{y_{n}\}\subset C
such that
\lim_{n\to\infty}d(y_{n},z_{0})\to\infty
.
Then there exist
x^{*}\in C
and
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that
F(y_{n},x^{*})\leq 0
for all
n\geq n_{0}
.

We also consider an alternative to Condition
(C3)
as follows:

(C3*)

For each
x\in C
, the set
\{y\in C\colon F(x,y)<0\}
is geodesically convex and the function
F(x,\cdot)
is lower semicontinuous.

Theorem 1

(
3
, Theorem 4.1)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
. Let
F\colon C\times C
be a bifunction. Then

(i)

If
F
satisfies Conditions
(C1)
-
(C5)
, then
\ep(F,C)
has a solution.

(ii)

If
F
satisfies Conditions
(C1)
,
(C2)
,
(C3*)
,
(C4)
and

, then

has a solution.

2.2
Bregman distances in Hadamard manifolds

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
Z
be a nonempty, open and geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
such that
\bar{Z}\subseteq\dom(\phi)
, where
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a proper function which is differentiable on
Z
. We define a function
D_{\phi}\colon\bar{Z}\times Z\subseteq\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
by

D_{\phi}(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle.

(2)

We also define the partial level sets of
D_{\phi}
by:

L_{\alpha,y}=\{x\in\bar{Z}\colon D_{\phi}(x,y)\leq\alpha\},\quad R_{x,\alpha}=\{y\in Z\colon D_{\phi}(x,y)\leq\alpha\}.

The set
L_{\alpha,y}
is called the left level set for each
\alpha\geq 0
and for all
y\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
, while
R_{x,\alpha}
is called the right level set for each
\alpha\geq 0
and for all
x\in\operatorname{dom}(\phi)
.

Definition 8

(
22
)
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold. A proper and lower semicontinuous function
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is called a Bregman function if there exists a nonempty, open and geodesically convex set
Z
such that
\bar{Z}\subseteq\dom\phi
and the following conditions hold:

(a)

\phi
is continuous on
\bar{Z}
;

(b)

\phi
is strictly geodesically convex on
\bar{Z}
;

(c)

\phi
is continuously differentiable on
Z
.

If
\phi
is a Bregman function, then
D_{\phi}(x,y)
is called the Bregman distance. We refer to the set
Z
as the zone of the function
\phi
.

Note that
D_{\phi}
is not a distance in the usual sense. In general the triangle inequality and the symmetry property do not hold.

Remark 5
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
be a Bregman function with zone
Z
. Then

D_{\phi}(x,y)\geq 0
for all
x\in\bar{Z}
and
y\in Z
, and
D_{\phi}(x,y)=0
if and only if
x=y
.

We also consider the following set of assumptions on the Bregman function
\phi
:

(B1)

For each
\alpha\geq 0
, the right level sets
R_{x,\alpha}
are bounded for each
y\in Z
and
x\in\bar{Z}
;

(B2)

If
\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n}=y^{*}\in\bar{Z}
, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\phi}(y^{*},y_{n})=0
;

(B3)

If
\lim_{n\to\infty}D_{\phi}(z_{n},y_{n})=0
,
\lim_{n\to\infty}y_{n}=y^{*}\in\bar{Z}
, and the sequence
\{z_{n}\}
is bounded, then
\lim_{n\to\infty}z_{n}=y^{*}
.

Remark 6
.

If
Z=\mathcal{M}
, then it follows from
(
21
, Proposition 4.3)
that Conditions
(B2)
and
(B3)
hold for any Bregman function
\phi
.

Remark 7
.

Let
x,y,z\in\mathcal{M}
. Using (
2
) and Remark
1
, we have

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x,y)+D_{\phi}(y,x)

\displaystyle=\phi(x)-\phi(y)-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle+\phi(y)-\phi(x)-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(x),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(x),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\underset{x\leftarrow y}{\mathrm{P}}\operatorname{grad}\phi(y),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(x),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle

\displaystyle=-\langle\operatorname{grad}\phi(x)-\underset{x\leftarrow y}{\mathrm{P}}\operatorname{grad}\phi(y),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle.

(3)

Similarly,

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x,y)-D_{\phi}(x,z)-D_{\phi}(z,y)

\displaystyle=-\langle\grad\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle+\langle\grad\phi(z),\exp_{z}^{-1}x\rangle+\langle\grad\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}z\rangle.

(4)

Remark 8
.

Let
x_{0}\in\mathcal{M}
and let
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be defined by
\phi(x)=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,x_{0})
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. It follows from Remarks
3
and
2
(i) that

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,x_{0})-\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(y,x_{0})-\langle\grad\phi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,x_{0})-\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(y,x_{0})+\langle\exp_{y}^{-1}x_{0},\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(d^{2}(x,x_{0})-d^{2}(y,x_{0})+2\langle\exp_{y}^{-1}x_{0},\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

If the sectional curvature of
\mathcal{M}
is zero, then

D_{\phi}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}d^{2}(x,y)\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

3
A Bregman Regularization for Equilibrium Problems

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
such that
C\subset\intr\dom(\phi)
, where

\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a Bregman function. Let
F\colon C\times C\to\mathbb{R}
be a bifunction.
Fix
\bar{x}\in C
and
\lambda>0
. Then the Bregman regularization of the bifunction
F
is denoted by

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
such that
C\subset\intr\dom(\phi)
, where

\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]
is a Bregman function. Take
\bar{x}\in C
and let
F\colon C\times C\to\mathbb{R}
be a bifunction.
Let
\phi
be
1
-coercive. Then Condition
(C6)
holds.

Proof.

We first claim that
\dom\partial F(\bar{x},\cdot)\cap C\neq\emptyset
. Indeed, the subdifferential of a proper, geodesically convex and lower semicontinuous function is a maximal monotone vector field on
\mathcal{M}
(
(
20
, Theorem 5.1)
). If we extend the function
F(\bar{x},\cdot)
to
\mathcal{M}
by defining it as
\infty
outside
C
, then
\partial F(\bar{x},\cdot)
is a maximal monotone vector field. On the other hand, the operator
T
defined by
T(x)=\emptyset
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
is obviously not maximal monotone because its graph is monotone and strictly contained in the graph of every nontrivial maximal monotone vector field. Thus
\dom\partial F(\bar{x},\cdot)
must be nonempty. Since
\partial F(\bar{x},z)=\emptyset
for each
z\notin C
, it follows that
\dom\partial F(\bar{x},\cdot)\cap C\neq\emptyset
must hold. Hence the claim is true and there exists
v\in\partial F(\bar{x},\hat{x})
for some
\hat{x}\in C
. Therefore, the following subgradient inequality holds:

\displaystyle F(\bar{x},\hat{x})

\displaystyle\geq F(\bar{x},\hat{x})+\langle v,\exp_{\hat{x}}^{-1}x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq F(\bar{x},\hat{x})-\|v\|\|\exp_{\hat{x}}^{-1}x_{n}\|

\displaystyle\geq F(\bar{x},\hat{x})-\|v\|d(x_{n},\hat{x}).

(8)

On the other hand, take a sequence
\{x_{n}\}
in
C
such that
d(x_{n},\hat{x})\to\infty
. Then, using the convexity of
\phi
, we get

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(\bar{x},x_{n})+D_{\phi}(x_{n},\bar{x})

\displaystyle=\langle\grad\phi(x_{n}),\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}\bar{x}\rangle+\langle\grad\phi(\bar{x}),\exp_{\bar{x}}^{-1}x_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\langle\grad\phi(x_{n}),\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}\bar{x}\rangle+\phi(x_{n})-\phi(\bar{x})

\displaystyle=d(x_{n},\hat{x})\left[\frac{\langle\grad\phi(x_{n}),\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}\bar{x}\rangle}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}+\frac{\phi(x_{n})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}-\frac{\phi(\bar{x})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}\right].

(9)

Combining (
8
) and (
9
), we have

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}[F(\bar{x},x_{n})+\lambda(D_{\phi}(\bar{x},x_{n})+D_{\phi}(x_{n},\bar{x}))]

\displaystyle\geq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[F(\bar{x},\hat{x})-\|v\|d(x_{n},\hat{x})+\lambda d(x_{n},\hat{x})\left(\frac{\langle\grad\phi(x_{n}),\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}\bar{x}\rangle}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}+\frac{\phi(x_{n})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}-\frac{\phi(\bar{x})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\liminf_{n\to\infty}\left[F(\bar{x},\hat{x})+\lambda d(x_{n},\hat{x})\left(\frac{\langle\grad\phi(x_{n}),\exp_{x_{n}}^{-1}\bar{x}\rangle}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}+\frac{\phi(x_{n})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}-\frac{\phi(\bar{x})}{d(x_{n},\hat{x})}-\frac{\|v\|}{\lambda}\right)\right].

The
1
-coercivity assumption implies that the expression in the inner bracket tends to
\infty
and hence Condition
(C6)
is established.
∎

Remark 11

The converse of the above result does not hold even in Euclidean spaces; that is, there exists Bregman functions which are not

Using Proposition (

4
A Bregman Regularized Proximal Point Method

Using the regularized problem (
5
), and the existence and uniqueness of its solution, we propose the following algorithm for solving
\ep(F,C)
:

Algorithm 1
.

Take a bounded sequence of regularization parameters
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset(0,\infty)
.

Initialization: Choose an initial point
x_{0}\in C
;

Stopping Criterion: Given
x_{n}
, if
x_{n+1}=x_{n}
, then stop. Otherwise;

Iterative Step: Given
x_{n}\in C
,
x_{n+1}
is the unique solution of the problem
\ep(F_{n,\lambda_{n}},C)
, that is,

x_{n+1}\in\Omega(F_{n,\lambda_{n}},C)\text{ for all }n\in\mathbb{N},

(10)

where
F_{n,\lambda_{n}}\colon C\times C\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

F_{n,\lambda_{n}}(x,y)=F(x,y)+\lambda_{n}(D_{\phi}(y,x_{n})-D_{\phi}(y,x)-D_{\phi}(x,x_{n}))\text{ for all }x,y\in C.

(11)

Proposition 4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be an Hadamard manifold and let
C
be a nonempty, closed and geodesically convex subset of
\mathcal{M}
such that
C\subset\intr\dom(\phi)
, where

\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to(-\infty,\infty]

holds and
\Omega(F,C)\neq\emptyset
. Let

be the sequence generated by (

(ii)

(i) It follows from Corollary

(ii) Let

(12)

Since

(iii) Now taking

Note that

5
Numerical Experiments

In this section we present the results of numerical experiments to demonstrate the computational performance of Algorithm
1
using different Bregman functions. All implementations were carried out in MATLAB R2024b and executed on a MacBook Air equipped with an Intel Core i5-5350U (1.8 GHz, dual-core) processor and 8 GB RAM. In each example, the subproblems defined in (
10
) were solved using
(
29
, Algorithm 3.1)
. For all tests, the stopping criterion for the inner iteration (that is, for
(
29
, Algorithm 3.1)
) was set to be
d(x_{n,k+1},x_{n,k})<10^{-3}
, while the outer iteration in Algorithm
1
terminated when
Er(n)=d(x_{n+1},x_{n})\leq 10^{-6}
.

Let
\mathbb{R}^{N}_{++}=\{x=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon x_{i}>0,~i=1,\ldots,N\}
and let
\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{++},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
be the Riemannian manifold with the
Riemannian metric defined by

\langle u,v\rangle=uG(x)v^{T},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}_{++},u,v\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathbb{R}^{N}_{++}=\mathbb{R}^{N},

(18)

where
G(x)
is a diagonal matrix defined by
G(x)=\text{diag}(x_{1}^{-2},\ldots,x_{N}^{-2})
.
The Riemannian distance
d\colon\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{M}\to[0,\infty)
is given by

d(x,y)=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\left(\ln\frac{x_{i}}{y_{i}}\right)^{2}\right)^{1/2}\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{M}.

The sectional curvature of this Riemannian manifold
\mathcal{M}
is
0
. Thus
\mathcal{M}=(\mathbb{R}^{N}_{++},\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle)
is an Hadamard manifold.

Let
\phi\colon\mathcal{M}\to\mathbb{R}
be a Bregman function. Then the general formula for the Bregman distance is

D_{\phi}(x,y)=\phi(x)-\phi(y)-\sum_{i=1}^{n}y_{i}\ln(x_{i}/y_{i})\frac{\partial\phi(y)}{\partial y_{i}}.

Consider the function

6
Conclusion

In this paper we developed a Bregman regularized proximal point algorithm for solving monotone equilibrium problems on Hadamard manifolds. Although the regularization term induced by a Bregman function is, in general, nonconvex on Hadamard manifolds unless the curvature is zero, we established the convergence of the proposed scheme by imposing a strong convexity assumption on the set associated with the regularization term. In this framework, we proved that the iterates generated by the algorithm converge to a solution of the equilibrium problem. Moreover, we adopted a weaker coercivity condition on the Bregman function compared to those commonly assumed in the existing literature, thereby extending the applicability of Bregman regularization in non-Euclidean settings. Numerical experiments on representative examples further illustrate the effectiveness and robustness of the proposed method.

Future research will focus on relaxing the strong convexity assumption imposed on the regularization-induced set and studying convergence under weaker geometric conditions.

Consider the set
\mathcal{P}_{n}
, consisting of all symmetric positive definite matrices of size
n\times n
, that is,

\mathcal{P}(n)\coloneq\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}\colon x=x^{T}\text{ and }a^{T}xa>0\text{ for all }a\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}.

\mathcal{P}(n)
forms an Hadamard manifold with the affine invariant metric

\langle u,v\rangle_{x}:=\tr(x^{-1}ux^{-1}v)\text{ for all }u,v\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{P}(n)(\cong\mathcal{S}(n)),

where
\mathcal{S}(n)
refers to the set of all symmetric matrices of size
n\times n
.

We denote by
\Ex
and
\Lg
the marix exponential and logarithm defined by
\Ex x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k!}x^{k}
and
\Lg x=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{k}(I-x)^{k}
.

The Riemannian distance
d:\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\to[0,\infty)
is defined by

d(x,y)=\|\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}})\|\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{P}(n),

where
\|\cdot\|
refers to the Frobenius norm.
The exponential map
\exp_{x}:\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathcal{P}(n)
is given by

\exp_{x}v=x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Ex(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}vx^{-\frac{1}{2}})x^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{P}(n);

the inverse of the exponential map
\exp_{x}^{-1}:\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathcal{T}_{x}\mathcal{P}(n)
is given by

\exp_{x}^{-1}y=x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}})x^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for all }y\in\mathcal{P}(n);

and the geodesic joining
x
to
y
is given by

\gamma(x,y;t)=x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Ex(t\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}}))x^{\frac{1}{2}}\text{ for all }t\in[0,1].

Let
A:\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathcal{T}\mathcal{P}(n)
be the vector field defined by

A(x)=x\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{P}_{n},

and consider the bifunction
F\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
defined by

F(x,y)=\langle A(x),\exp_{x}^{-1}y\rangle\text{ for all }x,y\in\mathcal{P}_{n}.

Then
F
is monotone since
A
is monotone and also Conditions
(C1)
-
(C4)
hold. Note that

\displaystyle F(x,y)

\displaystyle=\langle x,x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}})x^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tr(x^{-1}xx^{-1}x^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}})x^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\tr(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}})x^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\tr(\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle=\log(\det(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}}))=\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}.

7.1
Geodesically convex set
K_{x}

Consider the Bregman function
\phi\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\phi(x)=\det(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{P}(n).

Then the Riemannian gradient of
\phi
is given by

\grad\phi(x)=x\nabla\phi(x)x=x(\det(x)x^{-1})x=\det(x)x,

and hence the Bregman distance
D_{\phi}\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle D_{\phi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\langle\grad\det y,\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\langle\det(y)y,y^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})y^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\tr(y^{-1}\det(y)yy^{-1}y^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})y^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\tr(\det(y)y^{-\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})y^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\det y\tr(\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}}))

\displaystyle=\det x-\det y-\det y\log\frac{\det x}{\det y}.

Now the regularization
\tilde{F}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(x,y)=

\displaystyle F(x,y)+\lambda(D_{\phi}(y,\bar{x})-D_{\phi}(y,x)-D_{\phi}(x,\bar{x}))

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}+\lambda\left(-\det\bar{x}\log\frac{\det y}{\det\bar{x}}+\det x\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}+\det\bar{x}\log\frac{\det x}{\det\bar{x}}\right)

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x})\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}.

Consider the set

K_{x}=\left\{y\in C:\tilde{F}(x,y)=(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}<0\right\}.

Next, we show that
K_{x}
is geodesically convex.
Let
y_{1},y_{2}\in K_{x}
. Then

(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y_{1}}{\det x}<0\text{ and }(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y_{2}}{\det x}<0.

(22)

Now consider the geodesic joining
y_{1}
to
y_{2}
given by

\gamma(y_{1},y_{2};t)=y_{1}^{1/2}\exp\Big(t\Lg(y_{1}^{-1/2}y_{2}y_{1}^{-1/2})\Big)y_{1}^{1/2}=y_{1}^{1/2}(y_{1}^{-1/2}y_{2}y_{1}^{-1/2})^{t}y_{1}^{1/2}.

Note that

\displaystyle\det\gamma(y_{1},y_{2};t)

\displaystyle=\det(y_{1}^{1/2}(y_{1}^{-1/2}y_{2}y_{1}^{-1/2})^{t}y_{1}^{1/2}=(\det y_{1})^{1/2}(\det(y_{1}^{-1/2}y_{2}y_{1}^{-1/2}))^{t}(\det y_{1})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=(\det y_{1})^{1/2}((\det y_{1})^{-1/2}\det y_{2}(\det y_{1})^{-1/2}))^{t}(\det y_{1})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\det y_{1}(\det y_{1})^{-t}(\det y_{2})^{t}=(\det y_{1})^{1-t}(\det y_{2})^{t}.

Using (
22
), we get

\displaystyle(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det\gamma(y_{1},y_{2};t)}{\det x}

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{(\det y_{1})^{1-t}(\det y_{2})^{t}}{\det x}

\displaystyle=(1-t)(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y_{1}}{\det x}+t(1+\lambda(\det x-\det\bar{x}))\log\frac{\det y_{1}}{\det x}<0.

Hence
K_{x}
is geodesically convex.

7.2
Geodesically non convex set
K_{x}

Now consider the Bregman function
\psi\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
given by

\psi(x)=\tr(x)\text{ for all }x\in\mathcal{P}(n).

Then the Riemannian gradient of
\psi
is given by

\grad\psi(x)=x\nabla\phi(x)x=x^{2},

and hence the Bregman distance
D_{\psi}\colon\mathcal{P}(n)\times\mathcal{P}(n)\to\mathbb{R}
is given by

\displaystyle D_{\psi}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\tr x-\tr y-\langle\grad\psi(y),\exp_{y}^{-1}x\rangle

\displaystyle=\tr x-\tr y-\langle y^{2},y^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})y^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle

\displaystyle=\tr x-\tr y-\tr(y^{-1}y^{2}y^{-1}y^{\frac{1}{2}}\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})y^{\frac{1}{2}})

\displaystyle=\tr x-\tr y-\tr(y\Lg(y^{-\frac{1}{2}}xy^{-\frac{1}{2}})).

The regularization
\tilde{F}
is given by

\displaystyle\tilde{F}(x,y)=

\displaystyle F(x,y)+\lambda(D_{\psi}(y,\bar{x})-D_{\psi}(y,x)-D_{\psi}(x,\bar{x}))

\displaystyle=\log\frac{\det y}{\det x}+\lambda\left(-\tr(\bar{x}\Lg(\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}y\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}))+\tr(x\Lg(x^{-\frac{1}{2}}yx^{-\frac{1}{2}}))\right.

\displaystyle\quad\left.+\tr(\bar{x}\Lg(\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}x\bar{x}^{-\frac{1}{2}}))\right).

Let
\lambda=1
and consider the set

K_{x}=\{y\in C:\tilde{F}(x,y)<0\}.

Then the set
K
is not geodesically convex.
To see this, let
\mathcal{M}=\mathcal{P}_{2}
and let

x=\begin{pmatrix}2&1\\
1&1\end{pmatrix},\quad\bar{x}=\begin{pmatrix}4&2\\
2&3\end{pmatrix},\quad y_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}3&1\\
1&2\end{pmatrix},\quad y_{2}=\begin{pmatrix}5&2\\
2&1\end{pmatrix}.

Then
\tilde{F}(x,y_{1})=-0.78407
and
\tilde{F}(x,y_{2})=-0.25569
. Hence both

y_{1}
and
y_{2}
belong to
K_{x}
.

The geodesic joining
y_{1}
and
y_{2}
is given by

\gamma(y_{1},y_{2};t)=y_{1}^{1/2}\exp\!\Big(t\,\Lg(y_{1}^{-1/2}y_{2}y_{1}^{-1/2})\Big)y_{1}^{1/2}.

For

\displaystyle\gamma(y_{1},y_{2};\tfrac{1}{2})

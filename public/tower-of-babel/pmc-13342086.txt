Impact mechanisms and spatial effects of new quality productive forces on healthcare resource allocation in China
Authors: Jing Deng, Ting Lü, Qianwen Song
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractSince the implementation of China’s healthcare reform in 2009, healthcare resource allocation has improved, yet significant regional and urban-rural imbalances persist, exacerbated by the misallocation of medical capital and labor. Such inefficiencies hinder service equalization and the “Healthy China 2030” goals. Utilizing panel data from 30 provinces (2010–2021), this study constructs misallocation indices for healthcare capital and labor and employs fixed-effects and spatial Durbin models to examine the impact of novel productivity on resource allocation and its spatial spillover effects. Results show that novel productivity significantly enhances allocation efficiency and reduces misallocation through increased healthcare wages, improved service efficiency, and digitalization. Regionally, labor misallocation improves most in Central areas, while capital misallocation improves most in Eastern areas. However, spatial spillover effects reveal that neighboring regions’ productivity growth may exacerbate local misallocation. This study provides theoretical insights and practical recommendations for enhancing healthcare quality, equitable resource distribution, and achieving “Healthy China 2030.”Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-51225-3.Keywords: New quality productive forces, Misallocation of resources, Mediating effect, Spatial effect, Allocation of health resourcesSubject terms: Environmental social sciences, Health care

Introduction

Since the implementation of the 2009 healthcare reform, the allocation of healthcare resources in China has seen notable improvements in tandem with rapid economic development. However, over the past decade, the focus has largely been on expanding the total volume of healthcare service resources, leading to an “extensive” investment model and considerable inefficiencies in the allocation of healthcare resources. Previous studies have highlighted that, in terms of regional distribution, healthcare resources in China are heavily concentrated in the economically developed eastern regions, creating significant disparities in resource availability across the central and western regions1–3. Similarly, in terms of urban-rural distribution, medical resources are overly concentrated in large hospitals located in major cities, while primary healthcare facilities in rural areas are often understaffed and under-resourced4,5. These phenomena have not only exacerbated inequalities in healthcare resource allocation across regions but also reduced the efficiency of healthcare services due to improper resource distribution, thereby hindering the progress of equalized healthcare services in China. Specifically, the misallocation of medical capital and labor are core manifestations of the misallocation of healthcare resources in China. Existing research indicates that various regions exhibit differing degrees of capital or labor misallocation, which has been identified as a significant factor impeding the improvement of healthcare performance in China6. Furthermore, this misallocation of resources shows a trend of further expansion7. Meanwhile, with the slowdown of China’s economic growth and the intensification of population aging, addressing the misallocation of resources such as capital and labor has become increasingly important. Optimizing resource allocation is not only crucial for improving the efficiency and overall performance of healthcare services and reducing disparities in regional healthcare resource distribution but also plays a key role in advancing the equalization of healthcare services. This is essential for the successful implementation of the “Healthy China 2030” strategy. As a result, mitigating the misallocation of healthcare resources has emerged as a pressing issue for ensuring the healthy and sustainable development of China’s healthcare system.

As the world’s largest developing country, second-largest economy, and most populous nation, China’s healthcare system represents a critical area of academic inquiry. With global demand for healthcare resources continuously increasing, the rational allocation of these resources has emerged as a pressing concern for researchers and policymakers alike. Against this backdrop, in September 2023, General Secretary Xi Jinping introduced the concept of “New Quality Productivity Forces” (NQPF), a theoretical framework aimed at mitigating the misallocation of healthcare resources in China. Compared to traditional productivity, New Quality Productivity focuses on advancing high technology, improving efficiency, and ensuring high quality8. It emphasizes innovation-driven optimization of resource allocation to enhance productivity and the quality of services. This concept not only provides a theoretical foundation for promoting high-quality economic development in China but also presents new opportunities for achieving equitable healthcare resource allocation. While academic research on New Quality Productivity is gradually emerging, existing studies predominantly concentrate on sectors such as industry, agriculture, and low-carbon transitions, with limited comprehensive analysis of its impact on healthcare resource allocation. Therefore, critical questions remain unanswered: can advancements in New-Quality Productive Forces improve resource allocation efficiency? What are the mediating mechanisms underpinning this relationship? How do regional variations influence these effects? Addressing these questions is of critical importance for informing policies aimed at optimizing China’s healthcare resource allocation. This study utilizes provincial input-output panel data from 2010 to 2021 to estimate mismatch indices for medical capital and healthcare labor, alongside regional new-quality productivity indices. It investigates the influence of new-quality productivity on healthcare resource allocation, examines mediating mechanisms, including labor costs, technological advancements, and informatization effects, and employs a spatial Durbin model to reveal the spatial spillover effects of new-quality productivity. By doing so, this study aims to offer valuable insights for advancing theoretical research on New-Quality Productivity and fostering equitable healthcare resource allocation and development.

Literature review

Research progress on NQPF

The concept of NQPF was initially proposed by General Secretary Xi Jinping during his inspection tour of Heilongjiang Province in September 2023. Subsequently elaborated in several important speeches, NQPF has emerged as a key theoretical advancement underpinning China’s pursuit of high-quality economic development. Current academic research on NQPF has predominantly concentrated on its defining attributes, methods of measurement, and potential effects. Regarding its characteristics, research generally concurs that NQPF is not confined to specific technologies or industries. Instead, it encompasses modern productive forces across various fields and sectors, representing an evolution of traditional productive forces fundamentally driven by scientific and technological innovation9. For instance, Ren Baoping et al.10, drawing from Marx’s theory of productive forces, analyze NQPF through the lens of productive forces and production factors, suggesting that it represents an evolution beyond conventional productive forces. Zhou Wen et al.11examine the intrinsic nature of NQPF through its dual dimensions of “novelty” and “quality,” defining it as productivity generated by prioritizing innovation-driven development and achieving breakthroughs in key and disruptive technologies. Specifically, NQPF facilitates the efficient allocation of resource elements through technological innovation, management optimization, and resource integration, thereby addressing disparities in resource distribution across China’s regions9. In terms of development level measure, Lu Jiang et al.12 developed a comprehensive evaluation framework for measuring NQPF, focusing on three key dimensions: digitalization, sustainability, and technological innovation. GAO et al.13 established an index system for evaluating NQPF based on four dimensions: high-quality labor, labor materials, labor objects, and industrial structural transformation. Similarly, Ning et al.14 proposed an indicator system centered on high-quality labor, materials, and objects of labor, employing the entropy method to assess NQPF development levels.

Regarding impact analysis, research on NQPF remains in its nascent stages, with initial inquiries into its role and influence across various industries. For example, Kaimin Yin et al.15 investigated NQPF’s contribution to China’s low-carbon transition and found that it substantially promotes regional low-carbon initiatives. Shubin Wang et al.16 demonstrated that new-quality productive forces reduce carbon emissions per unit of GDP while improving carbon emission efficiency. Lin Li et al.17 identified that the development of new productive forces significantly enhances high-quality agricultural production. Yu Zhe et al.18 employed spatial models to reveal that NQPF significantly advances the high-quality development of China’s sports industry, though its effects are characterized by certain negative spatial spillovers. Similarly, Zhou Zejiong et al.19 concluded that new-quality productive forces positively influence high-quality economic development by driving technological innovation.

Research on health resource allocation

Existing literature has primarily focused on fairness and efficiency in the allocation of health resources. Regarding fairness, existing studies predominantly employ methods such as Lorenz curves, Gini coefficients, Theil indices, and concentration indices to analyze equity concerns in the population-based and spatial distribution of health resources in China. Chai et al.20 employed Lorenz curves, among other methods, to demonstrate that although total health human resources in China have increased significantly, leading to improved equity, disparities remain evident. Zhou et al.21 examined the allocation of medical resources in Sichuan Province using the Theil index and related approaches. Regarding efficiency, scholars have predominantly utilized models such as DEA and SBM to evaluate the efficiency of healthcare resource allocation and investigate contributing factors. Kong et al.22 employed a combination of DEA and related methods to assess the efficiency of medical services across different regions in China, identifying notable regional disparities in healthcare service efficiency and emphasizing the need for improvements in production efficiency. Li et al.23 contend that inadequate technical efficiency represents a significant limitation to the overall efficiency of healthcare services in China.

In summary, existing literature has extensively explored NQPF and resource allocation efficiency, resulting in significant research findings. However, several issues persist. First, existing studies primarily focus on defining the nature of NQPF and its macroeconomic implications, while neglecting its substantial impact on the healthcare sector. Second, research on healthcare resource allocation often aggregates labor resources and health capital into a single category, which hinders an accurate representation of the utilization of different types of healthcare resources. Similarly, studies on healthcare efficiency often merge healthcare human resources and financial resources into a single input variable, disregarding the unique attributes of each resource and inadequately capturing the actual utilization of diverse healthcare resources. Third, current research has not yet developed a comprehensive framework connecting NQPF to healthcare resource allocation. As a result, it fails to offer sufficient explanations regarding the impacts on healthcare resource allocation and lacks a systematic, in-depth analysis of how NQPF affects the optimal utilization of healthcare resources.

Based on this, the impact of NQPF, as a novel development concept, on the allocation of healthcare resources in China requires further investigation. Compared to existing literature, this study makes several contributions: (1) It integrates the healthcare resource misallocation framework with NQPF into a comprehensive model, enriching and expanding the theoretical and empirical research on NQPF at the provincial macro level. It provides empirical evidence for understanding how NQPF enhance the efficiency of healthcare resource allocation in China. (2) It conducts theoretical analysis and empirical testing of the transmission pathways through which NQPF improve the efficiency of regional innovation resource allocation, revealing the internal mechanisms and spatial effects of how NQPF enhance regional healthcare resource allocation efficiency.

Theoretical hypothesis

Direct impact of NQPF on the allocation of healthcare resources

New productivity, as an advanced form of productivity, is defined by its ability to enhance labor quality, drive technological progress, refine tools, and boost efficiency, exerting a significant impact on the allocation and utilization of healthcare resources. On one hand, new productivity lowers the costs associated with resource flows, thereby optimizing the distribution of critical production factors—such as labor, capital, and technology—within the healthcare sector. This reallocation shifts resources from inefficient areas to more effective ones, minimizing waste and redundancy24. On the other hand, new productivity has spurred a range of innovations, such as the development of smart healthcare systems, precision medical technologies, and personalized treatment models, which have further enhanced resource utilization efficiency and promoted the equitable distribution of healthcare resources on a broader scale. Furthermore, new productivity addresses the inefficiencies in traditional allocation models caused by factors such as information asymmetry, logistical constraints, or policy barriers. By leveraging technological advancements and optimizing productivity, it facilitates the removal of these obstacles, thereby promoting the flow of resources across regions and groups. This process fosters a more equitable distribution of healthcare resources among urban and rural areas, across different regions, and among various social groups25. Building on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 1

New productivity facilitates the rational allocation of healthcare resources.

The mechanism by which NQPF influence the allocation of health resources

The mediating effect of rising labor costs

With the continuous advancement of NQPF, the demand for high-quality healthcare services in China has grown, placing greater demands on the service capacity of medical institutions and driving them to optimize resource allocation and improve service quality through technological innovation. According to Hicks’ factor substitution theory26, when the relative cost of a production factor increases, organizations tend to reduce their reliance on that factor and increase investment in other factors to optimize output efficiency. In the healthcare sector, this theory manifests as rising labor costs prompting medical institutions to reduce dependency on traditional production factors, thereby creating a feedback mechanism that improves diagnostic processes, technology, and organizational management, ultimately promoting the rational use of resources27. Specifically, medical institutions have increasingly focused on the application of intelligent healthcare technologies and information management systems to enhance service efficiency and reduce operational costs, thereby mitigating resource misallocation issues. Furthermore, rising healthcare labor costs have facilitated the cross-regional mobility of the workforce. As remuneration for highly skilled healthcare workers increases, remote and developing regions have offered more competitive employment conditions to attract health professionals, which, to some extent, reduces the appeal of developed regions for such talent and promotes the spatially balanced mobility of healthcare labor28. This mobility optimizes the allocation of labor resources between regions, alleviates disparities in healthcare resource distribution caused by differences in regional economic development, and enhances equity and accessibility in healthcare services.Therefore, the development of NQPF has the potential to not only enhance the efficient utilization of healthcare resources in China but also support their equitable and rational allocation.Building on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 2

The advancement of NQPF, through the effects of rising labor costs, guides healthcare institutions to optimize resource allocation, thereby alleviating the misallocation of healthcare labor factors.

The mediating effect of technological progress

NQPF are a key driver of technological progress and innovation in service models within the healthcare sector, with their primary role reflected in enhancing the efficiency of healthcare services to alleviate the problem of resource misallocation. Powered by technological innovation, NQPF leverage the widespread application of intelligent devices, information management systems, and digital medical technologies to make healthcare service processes more efficient and resource utilization more precise, thereby improving the overall efficiency of healthcare services. On the one hand, the technological advancements driven by NQPF can optimize the quality and quantity of healthcare service supply, reducing resource waste caused by low service efficiency29. On the other hand, telemedicine technologies driven by NQPF have the potential to break through geographical limitations, bringing high-quality healthcare services to central and western regions and other areas with limited resources. This expansion broadens service coverage and mitigates regional disparities in healthcare resource distribution30. Furthermore, improved healthcare service efficiency unlocks the potential of service supply, enabling existing resources to meet greater demand. This, in turn, alleviates issues of resource scarcity and loss more effectively31. Building on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 3

NQPF alleviate the misallocation of healthcare resources by enhancing service efficiency.

The mediating effect of informatization

NQPF are driven primarily by data, with informatization serving as a critical foundation for their development. As healthcare services transition toward digitization and networking, improving informatization within the industry has become a key strategy to address resource misallocation. On the one hand, informatization breaks the limitations of physical space, enabling high-quality healthcare resources to flow across regions through technologies such as telemedicine and online consultations, thereby covering resource-scarce areas and reducing regional disparities in resource allocation. Conversely, informatization helps to bridge the gap in information asymmetry between patients and healthcare providers, allowing patients to obtain diagnostic details and service recommendations with greater ease. At the same time, healthcare institutions gain deeper insights into patient needs, facilitating the optimization of supply-demand matching, reducing resource inefficiencies, and advancing the equitable distribution of healthcare resources32. Building on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 4

NQPF alleviate the misallocation of healthcare resources by enhancing informatization.

Regional heterogeneity and spatial effects of new-quality production

NQPF, as a crucial force driving regional coordinated development, plays a significant role in reshaping the spatial distribution of healthcare resources and enhancing allocation efficiency, thereby mitigating interregional resource misallocation. Nevertheless, due to disparities in economic development levels, innovation capabilities, and healthcare resource endowments across different regions in China, the influence of NQPF on reducing resource misallocation exhibits considerable variation. Moreover, the impact of NQPF on healthcare resource allocation often transcends regional boundaries.

On one hand, mechanisms such as the attraction of knowledge and technology, along with the mobility of talent and capital, enable the spillover effect of NQPF into neighboring areas. This interaction positively contributes to optimizing healthcare resource allocation in surrounding regions, facilitating a more balanced distribution of resources33. On the other hand, uneven development levels of NQPF across regions may trigger a “siphon effect,” wherein more developed regions draw in talent, technology, and capital from nearby areas. While this agglomeration of production factors can enhance resource allocation efficiency in the developed region, it may intensify healthcare resource misallocation in less developed adjacent regions34. Building on this analysis, the following hypothesis is proposed:

Hypothesis 5

The development of NQPF exhibits regional heterogeneity in its impact on healthcare resource allocation.

Hypothesis 6

NQPF generate spatial negative spillover effects on healthcare resource allocation in neighboring regions.

Evaluation System for New Productivity Indicators.

First, in the labor dimension, NQPF emphasizes the innovative role of talent, focusing on enhancing workers’ scientific and technological literacy and innovation capabilities to simultaneously improve productivity and quality. Unlike traditional productivity, which relied heavily on resource inputs and high energy consumption, NQPF relies on technologies such as the Internet, artificial intelligence, and big data to optimize labor structures and improve labor output42. Based on this, worker quality, employment structure, output levels, and innovation spirit are selected as key evaluation metrics.

Second, in the labor object dimension, NQPF is grounded in broader and environmentally friendly labor objects, expanding from traditional natural materials to include labor objects characterized by high technological content, digitization, and virtualization43. By developing efficient and environmentally friendly industries, increasing the use of robotics, strengthening informatization, and enhancing enterprise innovation capabilities, NQPF achieves a dual benefit of improved production efficiency and environmental sustainability. Therefore, this study measures labor objects through three dimensions: industrial development levels, green ecology, and ecological governance.

Third, in the labor resources dimension, NQPF optimizes the structure and functionality of labor tools to ensure continuous improvements in production efficiency. Labor tools have transitioned from traditional forms to informatization, digitization, and greening, enhancing the efficiency of material and information transmission while reducing energy and labor consumption through optimized production methods44. Accordingly, infrastructure development levels and energy utilization efficiency are selected as evaluation criteria.

Control variables

To mitigate potential biases in model estimation results due to omitted variables, this paper selects fiscal autonomy (FA), health expenditure intensity (EHX), urbanization rate (Urb), and degree of openness (OPEN) as control variables, drawing on previous research45–49. Specifically, fiscal autonomy is measured by the ratio of budgetary fiscal revenue to total budgetary fiscal expenditure., health expenditure intensity as the share of local government health expenditure in general budgetary expenditure, urbanization rate as the share of the urban population in the total population of each region, and degree of openness as the share of total trade (imports and exports) in regional GDP.

Mediating variable

This study investigates the mechanism through which new-quality productive forces affect healthcare resource misallocation by utilizing a mediation effect model. Average wages in the healthcare industry are selected as the proxy variable for the labor cost increase effect (LDC)50. Healthcare service efficiency is used as the proxy variable for the technological progress effect (THP)51. Additionally, the share of employed personnel in the information transmission, software, and IT services sector relative to total urban employment serves as the proxy variable for the informatization level (IFMO)52. Data source

This research focuses on a sample of 30 provinces, regions, and municipalities across China, spanning the years 2010 to 2021. The primary data sources include the National Bureau of Statistics, the China Health and Hygiene Statistical Yearbook, the China Science and Technology Statistical Yearbook, the China Energy Statistical Yearbook, as well as the statistical yearbooks published annually by each province, region, and municipality. In instances where data were missing for certain areas, interpolation methods were employed to fill the gaps. Tibet, Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan were excluded from the analysis due to the unavailability of relevant data in these regions.

Results

Benchmark regression results

Drawing on the results of the Hausman test and F-test, this study adopts a panel data model with both individual and time fixed effects to examine the impact of new-type productivity on the efficiency of health resource allocation. Table 3 presents the benchmark regression results. Model 1 excludes control variables, while Model 2 includes control variables such as fiscal autonomy, health expenditure intensity, urbanization rate, degree of openness, per capita disposable income, and the degree of health marketization. The results show that, after accounting for control variables as well as individual and year fixed effects, the coefficients for the mismatch between health capital and labor due to new-type productivity stabilize at −0.312 and − 0.514, respectively, and are statistically significant at the 5% and 1% levels. Therefore, a one-unit increase in NQPF is associated with a reduction of 0.312 units in health capital misallocation and 0.514 units in health labor force misallocation. This study uses Model 2 as the benchmark, suggesting that improvements in new-type productivity can effectively mitigate the mismatch in health resource allocation, thus supporting Hypothesis 1.

Endogeneity test

A potential bidirectional causal relationship may exist between NQPF and the misallocation of health resources, which could result in biases in the model outcomes. To address possible endogeneity issues, this research applies the two-stage least squares (2SLS) method for re-estimating the benchmark model. Drawing on previous studies37, lagged values of NQPF are utilized as instrumental variables for the estimation procedure. Lagged explanatory variables can mitigate reverse causality because they are not directly influenced by the current period’s dependent variable, yet remain correlated with the contemporary disturbance term. Their primary limitation, however, is an inability to address endogeneity arising from contemporaneous explanatory variables53,54. The first-stage regression results indicate that the model passes the significance test, with the F-statistic exceeding the threshold value of 10, validating the selection of instrumental variables. As shown in Table 5, the regression results reveal that the first-stage findings indicate a significant negative effect of the lagged three-period NQPF at the 10% significance level, suggesting that improving NQPF helps mitigate resource misallocation, consistent with theoretical expectations. The second-stage results further confirm that NQPF’s impact on healthcare resource allocation remains significantly negative at the 1% significance level. Moreover, the Kleibergen-Paapr rk LM statistic and Kleibergen-Paapr rk Wald F statistic demonstrate that the instrumental variable regression does not suffer from unidentifiability or weak instrument issues, thereby reinforcing the robustness of the benchmark regression.

Since the implementation of reform and opening-up, different regions in China have exhibited varying levels of socio-economic development, technological progress, healthcare conditions and digital development55, shaped by differences in policies, geographical locations, and resource endowments. Consequently, the effects of new types of productive forces on healthcare resource allocation may vary across regions. This study classifies the study regions as eastern, central, and western regions based on the Seventh Five-Year Plan and examines the impact of new types of productive forces on each. As shown in Table 6, the influence of new types of productive forces on healthcare resource allocation is negatively correlated across the three regions, with significant differences observed at the 10% level. Regarding healthcare capital misallocation, the eastern region shows the largest regression coefficient (−0.783), followed by the central region (−0.688), with the western region displaying the smallest coefficient (−0.351). This suggests that NQPF have the strongest effect in alleviating healthcare capital misallocation in the eastern region and the weakest in the western region. In terms of healthcare workforce misallocation, the regression coefficient is highest in the central region (−2.28), followed by the eastern region (−1.201), and lowest in the western region (−0.255). This indicates that new types of productive forces have the greatest impact on mitigating healthcare workforce misallocation in the central region, with the western region being the least affected. Therefore, Hypothesis 5 is supported.

The labor cost increase effect

This study utilizes Jiang Ting’s56 proposed “two-step method” for regression analysis of mediation effects, which provides the advantages of mitigating endogeneity issues and requiring less stringent validity conditions.

As shown in Column (1) of Table 7, new types of productive forces have a significant positive effect on average salaries in the healthcare industry, with statistical significance at the 1% confidence level. This finding indicates that new types of productive forces can significantly increase average salaries in the healthcare sector. Additionally, as shown in Column (2) of Table 7, average compensation in the healthcare industry is negatively associated with the healthcare labor resource mismatch index, and this relationship is statistically significant at the 10% confidence level. This suggests that improved average salaries in the healthcare industry can mitigate healthcare resource mismatch. Combining the results from Columns (1) and (2), it can be concluded that new types of productive forces promote the effective allocation of healthcare human resources by increasing average salaries in the healthcare industry, thereby supporting Hypothesis 2.

Technological progress effect

As shown in Table 8, new types of productive forces in Column (1) have a positive effect on healthcare service efficiency, with statistical significance at the 5% confidence level. This indicates that new types of productive forces can significantly enhance healthcare service efficiency. Additionally, as shown in Columns (2) and (3) of Table 8, healthcare service efficiency is negatively associated with both the healthcare labor resource mismatch index and the healthcare capital mismatch index, with these effects being significant at the 1% confidence level. This suggests that improved healthcare service efficiency can effectively mitigate healthcare resource mismatches. Combining the results from Columns (1) through (3), the following conclusion can be drawn: new types of productive forces improve the effective allocation of healthcare resources by enhancing the service efficiency of healthcare institutions, thereby supporting Hypothesis 3.

Information technology effect

As shown in Table 9, new types of productive forces in Column (1) have a positive effect on the level of informatization, with statistical significance at the 1% confidence level. This indicates that new types of productive forces can significantly enhance the level of informatization. Moreover, as presented in Columns (2) and (3) of Table 9, there is a significant negative correlation between the level of informatization and both the healthcare labor resource mismatch index as well as the healthcare capital mismatch index. These relationships are statistically significant at least the 10% confidence level, indicating that informatization plays a crucial role in alleviating mismatches in healthcare resources. By combining the results from Columns (1) to (3), the following conclusion can be drawn: new types of productive forces promote the effective allocation of healthcare resources by improving informatization levels, thereby supporting Hypothesis 4.

Spatial effect analysis

Spatial model selection test

First, the global Moran index was employed to examine whether the capital misallocation index and labor misallocation index exhibit spatial autocorrelation. The results indicate that, for most years, both indices are significantly positive, suggesting that the healthcare resource misallocation indices across provinces exhibit a certain degree of spatial correlation. This finding supports the feasibility of employing spatial econometric models for further analysis.

Second, the identification of the appropriate spatial econometric regression model was conducted. The Moran’s I analysis was first employed as a preliminary test to examine spatial dependence, providing the foundation for selecting suitable spatial regression models for the capital misallocation index and labor misallocation index. To further confirm the choice of the model, LM tests, LR tests, Hausman tests and Wald tests were performed for both indices. As presented in Table 10, the significance tests for the capital and labor misallocation indices are largely significant at the 10% level, with only a minor exception. Consequently, the Spatial Durbin Model (SDM) with fixed effects is concluded to be the most appropriate specification.

Conclusions and recommendations

Conclusions

This study utilizes panel data from 30 provinces in China from 2010 to 2021 to measure the level of novel productivity and the efficiency of medical resource allocation. Panel fixed-effects and spatial Durbin models are employed to evaluate the influence of novel productivity on medical resource allocation and its underlying mechanisms. The findings indicate that the development of novel productivity enhances the efficiency of medical resource allocation and reduces the degree of resource misallocation in the healthcare sector. Mechanism testing reveals that novel productivity improves allocation efficiency by increasing average salaries in the healthcare sector, enhancing service efficiency, and advancing digitalization, thereby lowering the resource misallocation coefficient. Heterogeneity analysis demonstrates that novel productivity exerts varying impacts on healthcare capital and workforce misallocation across regions. From the perspective of.

healthcare capital allocation, novel productivity has the greatest impact on misallocation in eastern regions, followed by central and western regions. In terms of healthcare workforce, the largest impact on misallocation is observed in central regions, followed by eastern and western regions. Spatial spillover effect testing indicates that resource allocation efficiency across provinces exhibits negative spatial spillover effects, where the development of novel productivity in adjacent regions exacerbates misallocation within the local region.

Recommendations

The advancement of NQPF is instrumental in optimizing the allocation of China’s healthcare resources, promoting greater equity in their distribution, and ultimately supporting the realization of the Healthy China 2030 strategy. The core of NQPF lies in leveraging technological innovation, resource optimization, and model upgrades to expand the total volume of healthcare resources and improve their regional balance, thereby achieving high-quality and inclusive healthcare services. However, significant challenges remain, as the current uneven distribution of medical resources and disparities in service levels across regions continue to constrain the comprehensive development of the health sector.

To address these imbalances, coordinated planning and region-specific development strategies are essential. Eastern regions should capitalize on their technological and industrial advantages to pioneer breakthroughs in core technologies and explore new models aligned with national health strategies. Central and western regions, meanwhile, should prioritize enhancing information infrastructure and accelerating health industry development, fully leveraging their land and population resources to narrow the development gap with the east. Establishing a robust inter-regional coordination mechanism is crucial for fostering nationwide improvements in healthcare standards and achieving the goals of Healthy China 2030.

Furthermore, driving the transformation and upgrading of the healthcare industry toward high-end sectors is vital for mitigating the “siphoning effect” often observed in regional development. Eastern regions can maintain their competitive edge by actively cultivating emerging fields such as medical artificial intelligence, thus raising industry-wide standards. Concurrently, the sharing and dissemination of technological achievements will enable central and western regions to assimilate advanced technologies more rapidly, translating national R&D investments into tangible economic and health benefits. This approach helps reduce regional disparities and promotes a more balanced development of the health industry.

Finally, advancing healthcare informatization is paramount. Accelerating the digital transformation can break down isolated medical information systems, activate data resources, and unleash the multiplier effects of data utilization. Governments at all levels must increase investment in healthcare information infrastructure, particularly in central and western regions. Enhanced informatization will significantly boost resource allocation efficiency, optimize distribution through digital tools, and foster cross-regional medical collaboration. This will, in turn, enable residents in remote areas to access higher-quality healthcare services, ensuring that the benefits of development are widely shared.

Limitation and future research recommendations

This study utilizes relevant data to analyze the direct impact of NQPF on resource misallocation, its operational mechanisms, and spatial effects. However, certain limitations remain due to the research scale and data availability. First, regarding the research scale, this study examines NQPF development and healthcare resource allocation at the provincial level. In reality, significant disparities exist in NQPF development and healthcare resource distribution among cities within the same province. Conducting analyses at the city level would better capture the heterogeneity of these impacts, which will be a focus of future research. Second, regarding the temporal scope, the study period is limited due to the unavailability of data for the selected NQPF index system beyond 2021. Future research should aim to optimize the index system design and extend the temporal scope, thereby providing a more comprehensive analysis.

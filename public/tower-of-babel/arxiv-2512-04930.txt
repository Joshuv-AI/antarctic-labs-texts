\Lambda=U^{h}\perp E_{8}(-1)^{1+h-q}
,
and pick a standard basis
(a_{i},b_{i})
of
U_{i}

for
i\in[1,h]
. Put
L=\oplus{\mathbb{Z}}a_{i}
,
an isotropic sublattice of
\widetilde{\Lambda}
of rank
h
. Recall
that any two such sublattices are equivalent
under
O_{\widetilde{\Lambda}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Let
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{JE}}}
be the stack
of pairs
(X,\phi)
where
X
is a surface in
\widetilde{{\mathcal{JE}}}

and
\phi:\widetilde{\Lambda}\to H^{2}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})_{prim}
is an isometry
and let
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}^{0}
be the substack of
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}
defined by the condition
that
\phi(L)
is good for
X
; the map
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}^{0}\to\widetilde{{\mathcal{JE}}}

is surjective, by Lemma
5
. If
(X,\phi)\in{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}

then there is a normalized basis
(\omega_{i})_{i\in[1,h]}
of
H^{2,0}(X)

defined by
\int_{a_{i}}\omega_{j}=\delta_{ij}
and then the
orthonormal basis
(\eta_{i})_{i\in[1,N]}
of
\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{1}/\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{2}

can be further normalized by the requirement that

\int_{a_{i}}\eta_{j}=0
for all
i,j
. This gives a holomorphic
splitting of the projection
\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{1}\to\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{1}/\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{2}

of vector bundles on
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}^{0}
.

If we write
\eta_{j}=\sum\alpha_{ji}a_{i}+\sum\beta_{ij}b_{i}+\lambda_{j}

with
\lambda_{j}\in\Lambda
then each
\beta_{ij}=0
and then

(\eta_{i}.\eta_{j})=(\lambda_{i}.\lambda_{j})
.
Moreover,
\lambda_{j}
is the column vector

{}^{t}\bigg[\int_{c_{1}}\eta_{j},\ldots,\int_{c_{N}}\eta_{j}\bigg]

where
(c_{i})
is a
{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis of
\Lambda
;
that is,
\lambda_{j}
is a vector of periods
and we can regard the matrix
H=[\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{N}]

as a period matrix, obtained by integrating the vectors

\eta_{j}
over cycles dual to a basis of
\Lambda
.
Now

is an
N\times N
matrix with orthonormal
columns; that is,
H
is a holomorphic
morphism
{\mathbb{J}}{\mathbb{E}}^{0}\to O_{N}({\mathbb{C}})
that satisfies
the ecliptic pde.

{remark}

(1)

The assumptions of Theorem

(2)

(3)

Infinitesimal Torelli with base points

As already mentioned, Saito
[
S
]
(see also
[
Kl
]
)
proved infinitesimal Torelli when
\mathop{\mathrm{Bs}}\nolimits|K_{X}|
, which
we identify with
\mathop{\mathrm{Bs}}\nolimits|K_{C}+\phi^{*}M|
,
is empty. Since
\deg\phi^{*}M>0
, if
\mathop{\mathrm{Bs}}\nolimits|K_{X}|
is not
empty then it consists of a single point
Q\in C
,
where
\phi^{*}M={{\mathcal{O}}}_{C}(Q)
,
and also
h=q
.

{theorem}

Assume that

\mathop{\mathrm{Bs}}\nolimits|K_{X}|=\{Q\}
. Then
infinitesimal Torelli holds for
X
if and only
if
Q\not\in Z
. If
Q\in Z
then the derivative
of the period map is of corank
1
.

{proof}
Suppose that
Q\in Z
and put
e_{Q}=e
.
Then consider the
1
-parameter variation
of
X

constructed from the glueing

z^{e}=w^{e}+etw^{e-2};

this is a sub-variation of the
(e-1)
-parameter
variation
{{\mathcal{X}}}\to\Delta^{e-1}
detailed in
[
SB2
]
. Observe that

w=z(1-tz^{-2})

modulo
t^{2}
. Consider differentials

\omega^{(j)}(t)
on
{{\mathcal{X}}}_{t}
normalized
by the requirement that

\int_{A_{i}}\omega^{(j)}(t)=\delta_{i}^{j}

for appropriate
2
-cycles
A_{i}
on
X
supported
away from the fibre
E_{Q}
. Expand
\omega^{(j)}(t)

as

\omega^{(j)}(t)=\sum b^{(j)}_{pq}w^{p}t^{q}dw\wedge dv

for some fibre co-ordinate
v
. Substituting

w=z(1-tz^{-2})
gives

\omega^{(j)}(t)=\left(\sum b^{(j)}_{pq}z^{p}t^{q}-\sum b^{(j)}_{pq}(p-1)z^{p-2}t^{q+1}\right)dz\wedge dv

modulo
t^{2}
. Write
\omega^{(j)}(t)=\omega^{(j)}+t\eta^{(j)};

then
\omega^{(j)}=\sum b_{p0}^{(j)}z^{p}dz\wedge dv

and
\eta^{(j)}=b^{(j)}_{00}z^{-2}dz\wedge dv
modulo

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X})
. Now
b^{(j)}_{00}
is the constant
coefficient of
\omega^{(j)}
and so vanishes (
Q
is the
base point of
|K_{X}|
); it follows that
the class
[\eta^{(j)}]
in
H^{1,1}_{prim}(X)
also vanishes
for every
j
, so that
\nabla_{\partial/\partial t}:H^{2,0}(X)\to H^{1,1}_{prim}(X)
is the zero map
and indeed infinitesimal Torelli fails.

For each
a\in Z
there is
[
SB2
]
a 1-parameter
variation of
X
, with parameter
t_{a}
, such that the
kernel of
\nabla_{\partial/\partial t_{a}}
equals

H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(-E_{a}))
. So if
a=Q
then

\nabla_{\partial/\partial t_{a}}=0
and infinitesimal Torelli fails.
If
Q\not\in Z
then the image of
\nabla_{\partial/\partial t_{a}}

is the line
{\mathbb{C}}.\eta_{a}
and it follows
that no linear combination of the

\nabla_{\partial/\partial t_{a}}

can vanish.

Intersecting forms with curves

We give a formula
for the cup product
[\eta]\cup[D]
when

\eta
is a
2
-form of the second kind
and
D
is a curve in
X
that is unramified
over
C
at the points of the ramification divisor
Z
.

Recall that
X
is provided
with a zero section
C_{0}
as part of its data.

{theorem}

Suppose that
\eta\in H^{0}(X,\Omega^{2}_{X}(\sum_{P\in Z}e_{P}E_{P}))_{2^{nd}\ kind}

and
D
is as above. Assume that
we have chosen local co-ordinates
as in Proposition
3
. Assume further that

D
is defined locally at each point
Q

of
D\cap E_{P}
by

v_{P}=v_{P}(z_{P})=\sum_{m\geq 0}c_{Q,m}z_{P}^{m}

and that the corresponding local expansion of
\eta
is

\eta=\sum_{0\neq n>-e_{P}}a_{P,n}z_{P}^{n-1}dz_{P}\wedge dv_{P}

for every
P\in Z
.
Then

[\eta]\cup_{dR}[D]=-\sum_{P\in Z}\sum_{m=1}^{e_{P}-1}a_{P,-m}\sum_{Q\in D\cap E_{P}}c_{P,m}.

{proof}

Without loss of generality
we can assume that the polar divisor

(\eta)_{\infty}
is supported on a single fibre
E_{P}
.

Choose small open
discs
P\in\Delta^{\prime}\subsetneq\Delta\subsetneq C

and a small tubular neighbourhood
X^{0}
of
D
in
X
.
Set
R=\overline{\Delta}-\Delta^{\prime}
, a closed annulus,
and let
\gamma=\partial\overline{\Delta},\gamma^{\prime}=\partial\overline{\Delta}^{\prime}
.
Then there is a
C^{\infty}

(1,0)
-form
\Psi

on
X^{0}
such that

(1)

\Psi=0
on
f^{-1}(C-\Delta)\cap X^{0}
and

(2)

\Psi=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}z^{n}dv

on
f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cap X^{0}
, so that

d\Psi=\eta
there.

Put
\widetilde{\xi}=\eta-d\Psi
, a
C^{\infty}

2
-form on
X^{0}
.
Then
[\eta]|_{X^{0}}=[\widetilde{\xi}]
,
\widetilde{\xi}
is zero on

f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})\cap X^{0}
and
\widetilde{\xi}
is holomorphic
on
f^{-1}(C-\Delta)\cap X^{0}
. So

[\eta]\cup_{dR}[D]=[\eta]|_{X^{0}}\cup_{dR}[D]=[\widetilde{\xi}]\cup_{dR}[D]=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{D}\widetilde{\xi}|_{D}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(R)}\widetilde{\xi}|_{D},

where the last equality follows from the facts that

\widetilde{\xi}|_{D}
is zero on
D\cap f^{-1}(\Delta^{\prime})

and is a holomorphic
2
-form on
D\cap f^{-1}(C-\Delta)
,
so is zero there. So

\displaystyle 2\pi i\ [\eta]\cup_{dR}[D]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(R)}(\eta-d\Psi)|_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(R)}\eta|_{D}-\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(R)}d\Psi|_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0-\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(R)}d\Psi|_{D}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime})}\Psi|_{D}-\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(\gamma)}\Psi|_{D}\ \textrm{(Stokes)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{D\cap f^{-1}(\gamma^{\prime})}\Psi|_{D}\ \textrm{since}\ \Psi=0\ \textrm{on}\ f^{-1}(C-\Delta)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi i\ \sum_{Q\in D\cap E_{P}}\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits_{Q}\Psi|_{D}\ \textrm{(Cauchy)}.

Now

\Psi=\sum_{n\neq 0}\frac{a_{n}}{n}z^{n}dv=\sum_{n\neq 0,\ m\geq 0}\frac{m}{n}c_{Q,m}a_{n}z^{m+n-1}dz,

so that

[\eta]\cup_{dR}[D]=\sum\mathop{\mathrm{Res}}\nolimits_{Q}\Psi=\sum_{Q}\sum_{m\geq 1}\frac{m}{-m}c_{Q,m}a_{-m}=-\sum_{m=1}^{e_{P}-1}a_{-m}\sum_{Q}c_{Q,m}.

{corollary}

If
f:X\to C
is written in
Weierstrass form as

y^{2}=4x^{3}-g_{4}x-g_{6},

if
\phi:C\to\overline{{{\mathcal{E}}}\ell\ell}
is simply ramified
and if every
v_{P}
is chosen so that

dv_{P}=dx/y
then

[\eta]\cup_{dR}[D]=-\sum_{P\in Z}a_{P,-1}\frac{dx}{dz_{P}}(P)\sum_{Q\in D\cap E_{P}}\frac{1}{y(Q)}.

{proof}

Put
z_{P}=z
and
v_{P}=v
. Then,
in terms of a period
\tau=\tau(z)
of
E_{z}

we have

x=\wp(v,\tau),\ y=\frac{\partial\wp(v,\tau)}{\partial v},

so that

\frac{dx}{dz}=y\frac{dv}{dz}+\frac{\partial\wp}{d\tau}\frac{d\tau}{dz}.

Now
\frac{d\tau}{dz}|_{z=0}=0
, since

\phi
is ramified at
P
, so that

c_{Q,1}=\frac{dv}{dz}(Q)=\frac{1}{y(Q)}\frac{dx}{dz}(P).

{remark}

Part I
I

f the ramification is not simple then
we can calculate the coefficients

c_{P,n}
in a similar way via Faà di Bruno’s formula.
We omit the details, however.

Part II
I

n particular,
[\eta]\cup[C_{0}]=0,

where
C_{0}
is the zero section. Since also

[\eta]\cup[f]=0
for a fibre
f
, it
follows that
[\eta]
lies in
H^{2}_{prim}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
,
and so in
\mathop{\mathrm{Fil}}\nolimits^{1}H^{2}_{prim}(X,{\mathbb{C}})
.

Rational surfaces

Here we illustrate our results in the context
of rational surfaces.

Suppose that
f:X\to C={\mathbb{P}}^{1}
is a generic
Jacobian elliptic surface which is rational,
that
C_{0}
is the given zero section
and that
\phi
is a fibre.
Then there are eight further sections
C_{1},\ldots,C_{8},

disjoint from each other and from
C_{0}
, and
the curves

C_{0},C_{1},\ldots,C_{8}
are the exceptional
curves of a birational contraction
\pi:X\to{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.
The lattice
H^{2}_{prim}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
is isomorphic
to the root lattice
E_{8}(-1)
and if

h
is the class of a line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
and

\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{8}
are defined by

\alpha_{1}=C_{1}-C_{2},\ \alpha_{2}=2h-C_{1}-C_{2}-C_{3}\ \textrm{while}\ \alpha_{j}=C_{j-1}-C_{j}\ \textrm{for}\ j\geq 3

then
(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{8})
is a root basis of

H^{2}_{prim}(X,{\mathbb{Z}})
, which is isomorphic
to the root lattice
E_{8}(-1)
. Note that

\phi=3h-\sum_{0}^{8}C_{i}.

We can calculate each intersection number

[\eta_{i}]\cup_{dR}[C_{j}]
, and therefore each number

[\eta_{i}]\cup_{dR}[\alpha_{j}]

H(X)

is
the
8\times 8
matrix

and we can calculate it as follows.

(1)

Take a field

K
of characteristic zero and take eight points

Q_{1},\ldots,Q_{8}\in{\mathbb{P}}^{2}(K)
. Let
h
denote the class
of a line in
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
.

(2)

Check that these points are in general position
in the usual sense: they are distinct; no three are collinear;
no six lie on a conic; there is no cubic through all of them
that is singular at one of them.

(3)

Calculate a basis
(U_{1},U_{2})
of

H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{K},{{\mathcal{O}}}(3h-\sum Q_{i}))

(4)

Construct a basis
(U_{1}^{2},U_{1}U_{2},U_{2}^{2},V)

(5)

Construct a basis

H^{0}({\mathbb{P}}^{2}_{K},{{\mathcal{O}}}(9h-3\sum Q_{i}))

(6)

The forms
(U_{1},U_{2},V,W)
define a
rational map

\rho:{\mathbb{P}}^{2}-\to{\mathbb{P}}(1,1,2,3)=\mathop{\mathrm{Proj}}\nolimits K[U_{1},U_{2},V,W]

\Sigma
.

\mathop{\mathrm{Bl}}\nolimits_{Q)1,\ldots,Q_{8}}{\mathbb{P}}^{2}

(7)

After calculating the images

for
sufficiently many
K
-points

(or we could
calculate
\rho(\eta)
where
\eta
is the generic point)

find the defining sextic equation

of
\Sigma
.

(8)

Make an explicit change of co-ordinates
so that

where

is homogeneous of degree

.

(9)

Let
Q_{9}
denote the ninth base point
of the pencil
|3h-\sum_{1}^{8}Q_{i}|

and put

. Let

denote the exceptional curve of the blow-up;
then

is a section of the elliptic

.

(10)

If

of

. The isomorphism

induced by

can be calculated.

(11)

The ramification locus
Z
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}

is defined by the vanishing of the Jacobian covariant

J=\det\left[\begin{array}[]{cc}{{\partial g_{4}}/{\partial U_{1}}}&{{\partial g_{4}}/{\partial U_{2}}}\\
{{\partial g_{6}}/{\partial U_{1}}}&{{\partial g_{6}}/{\partial U_{2}}}\end{array}\right].

(12)

Check the genericity of the surface

by calculating the discriminant

(13)

Now

is a separable octic extension of

and

Calculate each form
\eta_{i}=\eta_{P_{i}}

(14)

Calculate each intersection number

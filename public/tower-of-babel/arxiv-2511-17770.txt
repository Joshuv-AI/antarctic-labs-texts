Asymptotic dynamics in the Heisenberg picture: attractor subspace and Choi-Effros product
Daniele Amato, Paolo Facchi, Arturo Konderak

Abstract
We study the asymptotic dynamics of open quantum systems in the Heisenberg picture. We find an explicit expression for the attractor subspace and the dynamics that takes place in it. We present the relationship between the attractor subspaces in the Schrödinger and Heisenberg pictures and, in particular, the connection between their algebraic structures. An unfolding theorem of the asymptotics, as well as the fine structure of the recently introduced Choi-Effros decoherence-free algebra, are also discussed. Finally, we show how to extend all the results to the class of Schwarz maps.

2.
Asymptotic dynamics of open quantum systems

2.1.
Preliminary notions

In this section we will briefly recall the main properties of the asymptotic dynamics of open quantum systems. In particular, we will consider a finite-dimensional quantum system described by a
d
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
.
Let us first work in the Heisenberg picture, where we look at the dynamics of the observables of the system
[
11
,
12
,
28
]
. In such scheme the dynamics in the unit time is given by a unital completely positive (UCP) map
\Phi
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, see e.g. the recent review
[
17
]
. In the discrete-time setting of the dynamics, the evolved observable
A(n)
at time
t=n\in\mathbb{N}
will be the
n
-fold composition
\Phi^{n}
of the map
\Phi
on the observable
A
at the initial time
t=0
, namely,
A(n)=\Phi^{n}(A)
.

The dynamics generated by
\Phi
can be studied on the basis of its spectrum
\mathrm{spect}(\Phi)
, i.e. the set of its eigenvalues. It is possible to prove that all the eigenvalues have modulus smaller than or equal to 1, a property which is shared by the larger class of positive and unital maps
[
7
,
45
]
. In fact, understanding the spectral features of completely positive maps remains essentially an open problem. See
[
32
,
18
]
for interesting attempts in this direction.

The asymptotic dynamics resulting from the
n\to\infty
limit of the evolution takes place inside the asymptotic, peripheral or
attractor subspace
of
\Phi
, defined as

\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi):=\mbox{span}\{X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\Phi(X)=\lambda X\mbox{ for some }\lambda\in\mathrm{spect}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi)\},

(2.1)

where
\mathrm{spect}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi)
denotes the
peripheral spectrum
of
\Phi
:

\mathrm{spect}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi):=\{\lambda\in{\mathrm{spect}(\Phi)}\,|\,|\lambda|=1\}.

(2.2)

The eigenvectors corresponding to eigenvalues of modulus smaller than
1
will vanish in the asymptotic limit, i.e.

\|\Phi^{n}(X)\|=\lvert\lambda\rvert^{n}\|X\|\rightarrow 0,\quad\lambda\in\mathrm{spect}(\Phi),\quad|\lambda|<1.

(2.3)

Clearly, the attractor subspace
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
contains the
fixed-point space

\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)
of
\Phi

\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi):=\{X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\Phi(X)=X\},

(2.4)

namely the eigenspace of
\Phi
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda=1
. Physically, the
stationary observables
of the evolution given by
\Phi
belong to the subspace
\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)
.

The
peripheral projection

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
of
\Phi
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}

\displaystyle:=\sum_{\lambda_{k}\in\mathrm{spect}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi)}\mathcal{P}_{k},

(2.5)

where
\mathcal{P}_{k}
denotes the spectral projection of
\Phi
corresponding to the peripheral eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}\in\mathrm{spect}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi)
.
Clearly, the range of the projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
is the attractor subspace
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
. Let us call
\mathcal{P}
the eigenprojection onto the fixed-point space
\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi)
, corresponding to
\lambda=1
.

Both projections
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
can be written in terms of the UCP map
\Phi
as
[
30
,
46
]

\mathcal{P}=\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\Phi^{n},\qquad\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}=\lim_{i\rightarrow\infty}\Phi^{n_{i}},

(2.6)

for some subsequence
(n_{i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
From (
) it is evident that both
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
are UCP maps because
\Phi
is, and the set of UCP maps forms a closed convex semigroup.

The space
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
of bounded operators on
\mathcal{H}
is a Banach algebra with respect to the adjoint
\ast
and the operator norm
\|\cdot\|
, and in finite dimensions can be endowed with a Hilbert space structure by means of the
Hilbert-Schmidt scalar product

\left\langle{A}|{B}\right\rangle_{\mathrm{HS}}:=\mathop{\rm tr}(A^{\ast}B),\quad A,B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}).

(2.7)

The Schrödinger dynamics, describing the dynamics of the states of the system, is given by
\Phi^{\dagger}
, the Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint of
\Phi
. As a result,
\Phi^{\dagger}
is a completely positive trace-preserving (CPTP) map on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, and it is referred to as a
quantum channel

[
33
]
. Similarly to the Heisenberg picture, if
\rho
is the initial state of the system, that is a positive semidefinite operator on
\mathcal{H}
with unit trace,
(\Phi^{\dagger})^{n}(\rho)
describes the evolved state at time
t=n\in\mathbb{N}
. A state
\rho
is said to be
stationary
whenever it is invariant under the Schrödinger evolution, namely

\Phi^{\dagger}(\rho)=\rho.

(2.8)

Thus, we can define the attractor and fixed-point subspaces
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})
and
\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^{\dagger})
of the Schrödinger dynamics
\Phi^{\dagger}
as in equations (
) and (
) respectively. We can also introduce the peripheral and spectral projections onto
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})
and
\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^{\dagger})
respectively which turn out to be the (Hilbert-Schmidt) adjoints
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}
and
\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}
of the projections
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
and
\mathcal{P}
of
\Phi
. Notice that the fixed-point subspace of a quantum channel is always non-empty
[
45
, Theorem 4.24]

An important family of UCP maps is given by the set of faithful maps. They will be the building block for the study of the asymptotic dynamics.

DEFINITION 1

A UCP map
\Phi
is said to be
faithful
if there exists an invertible stationary state
\rho
for
\Phi^{\dagger}
, the Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint of
\Phi
, i.e.

\Phi^{\dagger}(\rho)=\rho>0.

(2.9)

We will see in the following sections that the assumption of a faithful map greatly simplifies the algebraic properties of the asymptotic dynamics.

2.2.
The attractor subspace in the Schrödinger picture

In this section, we will provide a summary of the main results in the literature regarding the explicit structure of the attractor subspace in the Schrödinger picture, whereas the corresponding issue in the Heisenberg picture will be addressed in Section
3.
.

As in the previous section, we will denote by
\Phi
a UCP map, i.e. Heisenberg dynamics, and its corresponding Schrödinger dynamics is given by its Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint
\Phi^{\dagger}
.

The asymptotic dynamics of
\Phi^{\dagger}
is supported on the support of the fixed point
\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I})
, namely given
X\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})
, its support
\mathrm{supp}(X):=\ker(X)^{\perp}
and its range
\mathrm{ran}(X)
satisfy

\mathrm{supp}(X),\mathrm{ran}(X)\subseteq\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I})):=\mathcal{H}_{0}

(2.10)

In particular,
\mathcal{P}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I})
is a maximum-rank fixed point of
\Phi^{\dagger}
.
Importantly, as proved in
[
3
, Appendix A]
, we also have that

\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathrm{supp}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I})).

(2.11)

The Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
can be decomposed as

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1},

(2.12)

with
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}=\ker(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I}))
the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{H}_{0}
. Observe that equality
\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}
is equivalent to say that the channel
\Phi^{\dagger}
is faithful
[
4
]
.

We can now write the main result regarding the structure of the asymptotic dynamics in the Schrödinger picture
[
47
]
.

THEOREM 1

Let
\Phi^{\dagger}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\rightarrow\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
be a quantum channel. Then, there exists a decomposition of the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
in the form

\mathcal{H}=\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2}\right)\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1},

(2.13)

where
\mathcal{H}_{1}=\ker(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(\mathbb{I}))
,
\mathcal{H}_{k,i}
are Hilbert spaces for all
k=1,\dots,M
and
i=1,2
, and there are invertible density operators
\rho_{k}
on
\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
such that

\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})=\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\mathbb{C}\rho_{k}\right)\oplus 0.

(2.14)

Thus, an element
X\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})
has the form

X=\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}x_{k}\otimes\rho_{k}\right)\oplus 0,

(2.15)

and the action of the quantum channel
\Phi^{\dagger}
on
X
is given by

\Phi^{\dagger}(X)=\left(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}U_{k}x_{\pi^{-1}(k)}U_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\rho_{k}\right)\oplus 0,

(2.16)

for some fixed permutation
\pi
of
\{1,\dots,M\}
with
d_{k}:=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})=d_{\pi(k)}
for all
k=1,\dots,M
, and unitary operators
U_{k}
on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})
.

The previous result gives an explicit expression for the asymptotics in the Schrödinger picture. Let us now clarify the general structure theorem
1
with the following simple example

EXAMPLE 1

Consider the amplitude damping channel for a qubit,
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{2}
, given by

X=\begin{pmatrix}x_{00}&x_{01}\\
x_{10}&x_{11}\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\Phi^{\dagger}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{00}+\frac{3}{4}x_{11}&\frac{1}{2}x_{01}\\
\frac{1}{2}x_{10}&\frac{1}{4}x_{11}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.17)

Its peripheral projection reads

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}x_{00}+x_{11}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(2.18)

Physically, the channel (
) describes a probability flow from the orthogonal component
\mathcal{H}_{0}^{\perp}=\mathbb{C}|{1}\rangle
to
\mathcal{H}_{0}=\mathbb{C}|{0}\rangle
, and this is a simple example of non-faithful channel. This could, for example, model the decay process of an excited atomic state.

More generally, we can always obtain a quantum channel
\Phi^{\dagger}
with a desired asymptotic dynamics by using the unfolding theorem discussed in
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
, whose Heisenberg analogue is shown in Theorem
4
below.

We can now easily obtain insights regarding the Heisenberg dynamics in the faithful case. First of all, in the faithful case, the decomposition (
) takes the form

\mathcal{H}=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2}.

(2.19)

Moreover, the peripheral projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}
in the Schrödinger picture is given by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(X)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathrm{tr}_{k,2}(P_{k}XP_{k})\otimes\rho_{k},

(2.20)

where
P_{k}
is the projection onto
\mathcal{H}_{k,1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
and

\mathrm{tr}_{k,2}
denotes the partial trace over
\mathcal{H}_{k,2}
. Its Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint, being the corresponding peripheral projection in the Heisenberg picture, reads

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}(X)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathrm{tr}_{k,2}\bigl(P_{k}XP_{k}(\mathbb{I}_{k,1}\otimes\rho_{k})\bigr)\otimes{\mathbb{I}_{k,2}},

(2.21)

where the map

Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{2})\mapsto\mathrm{tr}_{2}(Y(\mathbb{I}\otimes\rho_{2}))\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1}),\quad\rho_{2}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{H}_{2}),

(2.22)

is the Hilbert-Schmidt adjoint of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})\ni X\mapsto X\otimes\rho_{2}
, namely the addition of an uncorrelated ancillary state
\rho_{2}

[
20
, Chapter 9 Lemma 1.1]
. Notice that the map (
) is involved in Stinespring’s dilation theorem of UCP maps
[
42
]
.
Consequently, the attractor subspace of the Heisenberg evolution in the faithful case will be

\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)=\mathrm{ran}(\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}})=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{k,1})\otimes\mathbb{C}\mathbb{I}_{k,2}.

(2.23)

Observe that in the faithful case the attractor subspace is clearly a
C^{*}
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, as it is closed under the standard composition product. We can also obtain the action of the asymptotic map in the Heisenberg picture under the faithfulness assumption. Given

X=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}x_{k}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{k,2}\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi),

(2.24)

we have

\Phi(X)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}U_{\pi(k)}^{\ast}x_{\pi(k)}U_{\pi(k)}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{k,2}.

(2.25)

Indeed, given
X\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
and
Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\Phi(X)\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
and consequently
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}(\Phi(X))=\Phi(X)
, so we have

\mathop{\rm tr}(X\Phi^{\dagger}(Y))=\mathop{\rm tr}(\Phi(X)Y)=\mathop{\rm tr}(\Phi(X)\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(Y)):=\mathop{\rm tr}(X\Phi^{\dagger}(Z)),

(2.26)

where

\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})\ni Z=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}z_{k}\otimes\rho_{k,2}\mapsto\Phi^{\dagger}(Z)=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}U_{k}z_{\pi^{-1}(k)}U_{k}^{\ast}\otimes\rho_{k,2}.

(2.27)

by Theorem
1
. Therefore by using (
)

\begin{split}\mathop{\rm tr}(X\Phi^{\dagger}(Z))&=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathop{\rm tr}(U_{k}^{\ast}x_{k}U_{k}z_{\pi^{-1}(k)})=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mathop{\rm tr}(U_{\pi(k)}^{\ast}x_{\pi(k)}U_{\pi(k)}z_{k})\\
&:=\mathop{\rm tr}(WZ)=\mathop{\rm tr}(WY),\;W:=\bigoplus_{k=1}^{M}U_{\pi(k)}^{\ast}x_{\pi(k)}U_{\pi(k)}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{k,2},\end{split}

(2.28)

where we used
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}(W)=W
. Thus (
) readily follows by the arbitrariness of
Y
.
Moreover, we can easily check that
\Phi
acts as a
*
-automorphism on
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
with respect to the composition product, in line with
[
10
]
. As we shall see in the following section, this can be generalized to the non-faithful case by introducing an appropriate product.

However, permutations may imply the lack of unitarity of the asymptotic dynamics, see
[
4
]
and
[
9
, Example 3.2]
. Observe that, whenever we have a permutation
\pi
, the automorphism (

\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
itself. Explicitly, we cannot find a unitary

such that

\Phi(X)=U^{\ast}XU,\quad\forall X\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi).

(2.29)

2.3.
Choi-Effros product

We will conclude this introductory part by recalling the
Choi-Effros product
, which makes the attractor subspace of a UCP map a
C^{\ast}
-algebra in the non-faithful case, see
[
10
, Theorem 2.3]
. It was introduced by Choi and Effros in a seminal paper
[
16
, Theorem 3.1]
for injective operator systems.

THEOREM 2

Let
\Phi
be a UCP map, and let
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
be its peripheral projection. Then its attractor subspace
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
is a unital
{C}^{\ast}
-algebra with respect to the Choi-Effros product, defined as

X\star Y:=\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}(XY),

(2.30)

the involution and the norm being the native operations of
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
.

This theorem makes the Choi-Effros product (
) the right candidate for describing the long-term behavior of an open quantum system in the Heisenberg scheme
[
5
]
. Moreover, if
\Phi
is faithful, then the Choi-Effros product
\star
reduces to the usual composition product
\cdot
, and the algebraic structure of
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
is just given by equation (
). We will see in Section
3.
how to generalize such result to the non-faithful case.

In general, faithfulness is a sufficient but not necessary condition for the map
\Phi
to be a
\ast
-morphism on the
C^{\ast}
-algebra
(\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi),\cdot,\|\cdot\|,\ast)
. For this reason one introduces the class of peripherally automorphic maps
[
9
]
.

DEFINITION 2

Let
\Phi
be a UCP map. Then
\Phi
is called
peripherally automorphic
iff

X\star Y=XY,\quad\text{for all }X,Y\in\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi).

(2.31)

Motivated by Theorem
2
, namely by the fact that the Choi-Effros product (
) is the suitable one for the study of Heisenberg asymptotics, we consider the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}_{\star}:=\{X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})\,|\,\Phi^{n}(Y\star X)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{n}(Y)\star\Phi^{n}(X),

\displaystyle\,\Phi^{n}(X\star Y)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{n}(X)\star\Phi^{n}(Y),\,\forall Y\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}),\forall n\in\mathbb{N}\},

(2.32)

which we refer to as
Choi-Effros decoherence-free algebra of
\Phi

[
5
]
. This generalizes the standard decoherence-free algebra, see e.g.
[
14
]
.

It turns out that
(\mathcal{N}_{\star},\cdot,\|\cdot\|,\ast)
is a
C^{\ast}
-algebra and it always contains the attractor subspace
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
of
\Phi
, at variance with the standard decoherence-free algebra
[
9
]
. A decomposition theorem for the Choi-Effros decoherence-free algebra will be recalled in Section
4.
(Theorem
5
) and rewritten in terms of the decomposition (
) of
\mathcal{H}
(Theorem
6
). For further details on the Choi-Effros decoherence-free algebra see
[
5
]
.

3.
Peripheral projection and attractor subspace

In this section we will find the explicit structure of the attractor subspace
\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi)
of the evolution in the Heisenberg picture, in analogy with the decomposition (
) valid in the Schrödinger picture. For this purpose, we need to understand the structure of the peripheral projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}
of a generic UCP map
\Phi
.

First, it is useful to consider again the splitting (
) of the Hilbert space:

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{H}_{0}\oplus\mathcal{H}_{1}.

(3.33)

Let
Q_{0}
and
Q_{1}
be the projections onto
\mathcal{H}_{0}
and
\mathcal{H}_{1}
, respectively, and for any
X\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H})
consider the operators
X_{ij}=Q_{i}XQ_{j}
. The operator
X
can be written in a block-matrix form as

X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&X_{01}\\
X_{10}&X_{11}\end{pmatrix}.

(3.34)

It is well known
[
35
,
46
]
that it is possible to obtain a faithful channel by restricting
\Phi^{\dagger}
to
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
. To be more precise, this subspace is invariant under
\Phi^{\dagger}
, and we can define

{\varphi_{00}}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})\mapsto\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
as

X=\begin{pmatrix}X_{00}&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}\mapsto\Phi^{\dagger}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{00}(X_{00})&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.35)

It turns out that
\varphi_{00}
is a faithful CPTP map on
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
and we will call it the
reduced quantum channel
of
\Phi^{\dagger}
.
There exists a simple relationship between the fixed point spaces and the attractor subspaces of
\Phi^{\dagger}
and
\varphi_{00}
, namely

\mathrm{Fix}(\Phi^{\dagger})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Fix}(\varphi_{00})&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad\mathrm{Attr}(\Phi^{\dagger})=\begin{pmatrix}\mathrm{Attr}(\varphi_{00})&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}.

(3.36)

As a consequence of the structure of the Schrödinger attractor subspace (
), the peripheral projection
\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}
of
\Phi^{\dagger}
reads

\mathcal{P}_{\mathrm{P}}^{\dagger}(X)=\begin{pmatrix}\sum_{i,j=0,1}\mathcal{Q}_{ij}(X_{ji})&0\\
0&0\end{pmatrix}

(3.37)

Here,
\mathcal{Q}_{ij}:\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{j})\to\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
are linear maps. In particular,
\mathcal{Q}_{00}
is the peripheral projection of the faithful channel
\varphi_{00}
, and its explicit form is provided by (
). In line with (
) and following the quantum probability jargon
[
27
]
, we can call
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{0})
and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{H}_{1})
the
recurrent
and
transient algebras
of the evolution.

We can now consider the peripheral projection in the Heisenberg scheme

Conclusions

In this work we discussed open-system asymptotic dynamics in the Heisenberg picture. First, we explicitly found the structure of the attractor subspace, and we characterized the evolution on this subspace. In particular, we found that the faithful component of the attractor subspace is mapped into a (transient) orthogonal component by an arbitrary UCP map. On the level of the evolution and as in the Schrödinger picture, permutations can make the asymptotic dynamics non-unitary in general, even in the faithful case. Mathematically, permutations are described by an external
*
-morphism. Additionally, superselection rules appear between subspaces.

In this regard, it would be interesting to derive a complete characterization of the unitarity of the asymptotic map for Heisenberg dynamics like the one considered in
[
3
]
for Schrödinger evolutions.

Subsequently, we showed how the Choi-Effros product, endowing the attractor subspace of a Heisenberg evolution, emerges from the composition product equipping the attractor subspace of the reduced dynamics. Interestingly, the latter is a faithful representation of the attractor subspace of the Heisenberg dynamics on a smaller Hilbert space
[
26
]
. Also, we obtained an explicit expression for the Choi-Effros decoherence-free algebra (
2.32
), that was introduced in
[
5
]
as a natural generalization of the standard decoherence-free algebra.

In conclusion, we showed that all the results obtained in the present work are valid for the larger class of Schwarz maps, so the operator Schwarz inequality seems to be the crucial property in the study of the asymptotics of open quantum systems.

As a final remark, all the results discussed in this work may be generalized to finite-dimensional
C^{\ast}
-algebras in a straightforward way.

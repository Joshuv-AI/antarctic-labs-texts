Introduction

A central problem in analysis is to characterise solutions to PDEs on model spaces such as
\mathbb{R}^{n}
or
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{n}>0\}
, possibly subject to growth, sign and/or boundary conditions as appropriate. The prototypical result of this type is due to Liouville
[
40
]
, who showed that a positive harmonic function defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
must be constant. Such Liouville-type theorems are closely related to the validity of estimates on solutions to PDEs on more general domains or Riemannian manifolds, and are consequently of importance in the theory for such equations.

In this paper we characterise solutions to a very general class of conformally invariant PDEs of negative curvature type on
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
(
n\geq 3
), for which the hyperbolic metric serves as the model solution. We show that whether the hyperbolic metric is the unique solution is completely determined by a single parameter
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}
(defined in (
1.10
) below), and in the case of non-uniqueness we classify all other solutions. As a consequence, we show that local boundary
C^{0}
estimates fail for the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
. This is in contrast to the case
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}>1
, where such estimates were established by the authors in
[
15
]
and used to obtain existence of solutions to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem on all Riemannian compact manifolds with boundary.

In their classical work

. For

•

A particular case in which

•

When

•

When

See also the combination of results in

,
(1,0,\dots,0)

\in\Gamma

In

We now return to the context of Theorems

2.1
Comparison principles

We first recall the notion of a viscosity (sub/ super-)solution, introduced in the context of fully nonlinear Yamabe-type equations by Li in
[
31
]
:

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
w_{1}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
is a viscosity supersolution to the equation

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-A_{w}))=\frac{1}{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(-A_{w})\in\Gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\text{on }\Omega

(2.1)

if for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying
\varphi(x_{0})=w_{1}(x_{0})
and
\varphi\leq w_{1}
near
x_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-A_{\varphi}))(x_{0})\geq\frac{1}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(-A_{\varphi})(x_{0})\in\Gamma.

We say that
w_{2}\in C^{0}(\Omega)
is a viscosity subsolution to (
2.1
) if for any
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
satisfying
\varphi(x_{0})=w_{2}(x_{0})
and
\varphi\geq w_{2}
near
x_{0}
, it holds that

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-A_{\varphi}))(x_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(-A_{\varphi})(x_{0})\in\Gamma

or

\displaystyle\lambda(-A_{\varphi})(x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Gamma.

We call
w
a viscosity solution to (
2.1
) if it is both a viscosity supersolution and a viscosity subsolution.

We will often refer to the following comparison principle:

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose that
(f,\Gamma)
satisfies (
′
‣
1
)–(
′
‣
1
) and (
1.6
), and let
\Omega
be a bounded domain. Suppose
w_{1}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity supersolution to (
2.1
),
w_{2}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity subsolution to (
2.1
),
w_{1},w_{2}>0
in
\Omega
and
w_{2}<w_{1}
on
\partial\Omega
. Then
w_{2}<w_{1}
in
\overline{\Omega}
.

The proof of Proposition
2.3
uses another version of the comparison principle, which follows from the principle of propagation of touching points in
[
36
, Theorem 3.2]
, as explained in
[

, Proposition 2.2]
:

Proposition 2.4

Suppose that
(f,\Gamma)
satisfies (
′
‣

)–(
′
‣

) and (

), and let
\Omega
be a bounded domain. Suppose
w_{1}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity supersolution to (

),
w_{2}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega})
is a viscosity subsolution to (
2.1
),

2.2
Barrier functions

Suitable barrier functions will play an important role in obtaining
C^{0}
estimates in the next section. For the global lower bound, we will simply use the hyperbolic metric on a sequence of larger and larger balls as lower barriers. For the upper bound near
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
, we will use supersolutions on annuli centred in the lower half space as upper barriers (cf. a related but different construction in
[
15
, Proposition 3.4]
, and see also Appendix
B
for non-existence of related supersolutions when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
). These supersolutions are constructed in the following lemma:

Lemma 2.5
.

Fix
x_{0}^{\prime}\in\{x_{n}=0\}
and suppose
R>200
,
\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}<\frac{1}{200}
and
\sqrt{R^{2}-r_{1}^{2}}\leq r<R
. Let
A_{r,\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}}
denote the open annulus centred at
(x_{0}^{\prime},-R)
with inner radius
r
and outer radius
\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}
, and for
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})
let
d(x)=\sqrt{|x^{\prime}-x_{0}^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n}+R)^{2}}
denote the distance to its centre. Then for any constant
C
satisfying

\displaystyle\frac{9R^{2}}{r_{1}^{4}}<C<\frac{R^{3}}{21r_{1}^{4}},

(2.2)

the conformal metric
g_{h_{r}}
defined on
A_{r,\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}}
by

\displaystyle h_{r}(x)=h_{r}(d)=(d-r)+C(d-r)^{2}>0

satisfies

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f(\lambda(-A_{h_{r}}))\geq\frac{1}{2},\quad\lambda(-A_{h_{r}})\in\Gamma&\text{in }A_{r,\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}}\\
h_{r}=0&\text{on }\{d=r\}\\
h_{r}\geq 1&\text{on }\{d=\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}\}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The fact that
h_{r}=0
on
\{d=r\}
is clear from the definition of
h_{r}
. To see that
h_{r}\geq 1
on
\{d=\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}\}
, it suffices to show that
C(\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}-R)^{2}\geq 1
under our hypotheses on
R,r_{1},r
and
C
. To this end, note that since
\sqrt{1+x}\geq 1+\frac{x}{3}
for
x\in[0,3]
and since we assume
\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}<\frac{1}{200}
, we have the inequality

\displaystyle\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}=R\bigg(1+\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}\bigg)^{1/2}\geq R\bigg(1+\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{3R^{2}}\bigg).

(2.3)

The inequality in (
2.3
) combined with the lower bound on
C
in (
2.2
) then clearly implies
C(\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}-R)^{2}\geq 1
, as required.

To complete the proof of the claim, it remains to show that our assumptions on
R,r_{1},r
and
C
imply
f(\lambda(-A_{h_{r}}))\geq\frac{1}{2}
and
\lambda(-A_{h_{r}})\in\Gamma
in
A_{r,\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}}
. We start by computing the eigenvalues of
-A_{h_{r}}
, which have multiplicity 1 and
(n-1)
, respectively:

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}

\displaystyle=-h_{r}h_{r}^{\prime\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(h_{r}^{\prime})^{2}=-2C\big((d-r)+C(d-r)^{2}\big)+\frac{1}{2}\big(1+2C(d-r)\big)^{2}=\frac{1}{2}

and

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle=-\frac{h_{r}h_{r}^{\prime}}{d}+\frac{1}{2}(h_{r}^{\prime})^{2}=-\big((d-r)+C(d-r)^{2}\big)\frac{1+2C(d-r)}{r}+\frac{1}{2}\big(1+2C(d-r)\big)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}+(d-r)\bigg[2C-\frac{1}{d}+\Big(\frac{-3C}{d}+2C^{2}\Big)(d-r)-\frac{2C^{2}}{d}(d-r)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}+(d-r)\bigg[2C-\frac{1}{d}-\frac{3C}{d}(d-r)-\frac{2C^{2}}{d}(d-r)^{2}\bigg]

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}+(d-r)\bigg[2C-\frac{7}{4d}-\frac{5C^{2}}{d}(d-r)^{2}\bigg],

where we have applied the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality to reach the last line. It therefore suffices to show that

\displaystyle\varphi\mathrel{\hbox to0.0pt{\raisebox{1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}\hss}\raisebox{-1.29167pt}{$\cdot$}}=2C-\frac{7}{4d}-\frac{5C^{2}}{d}(d-r)^{2}\geq 0.

(2.4)

To prove (
2.4
) we first note

\displaystyle R\bigg(1-\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}\bigg)\leq\sqrt{R^{2}-r_{1}^{2}}\,\leq r<d<\sqrt{R^{2}+r_{1}^{2}}\,\leq R\bigg(1+\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}\bigg),

which implies
d-r\leq\frac{2r_{1}^{2}}{R}
. These, together with
\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}}<\frac{1}{200}
, imply

\displaystyle\frac{7}{4d}<\frac{7}{4R(1-\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}})}<\frac{2}{R}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\frac{5C^{2}}{d}(d-r)^{2}<\frac{5C^{2}}{R(1-\frac{r_{1}^{2}}{R^{2}})}\frac{4r_{1}^{4}}{R^{2}}<\frac{21C^{2}r_{1}^{4}}{R^{3}}.

Therefore

\displaystyle\varphi\geq 2C-\frac{2}{R}-\frac{21C^{2}r_{1}^{4}}{R^{3}},

and thus
\varphi
is clearly seen to be nonnegative by (
2.2
).
∎

\lambda_{1}^{u}(x_{0})\leq\psi(\lambda_{2}^{u}(x_{0}))
, i.e. (
3.8
) holds.

The proof that a viscosity supersolution (resp. viscosity subsolution) to (
3.5
) is a viscosity supersolution (resp. subsolution) to (
3.1
) follows arguments similar to those above and is omitted.
∎

Appendix B
Some further results

In this appendix we prove some consequences of our main results that may be of independent interest but are not used in the main body of the paper.

B.1
A counterexample to the comparison principle on unbounded domains

Proposition B.1
.

Suppose
(f,\Gamma)
satisfies (
1.3
)–(
1.7
) and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
. Then there exist smooth positive functions
u
and
w
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f(\lambda(-A_{u}))=f(\lambda(-A_{w}))=\frac{1}{2}&\text{in }\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\\
w>u=0&\text{on }\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}\end{cases}

but
w(x)<u(x)
for some
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
u=w^{(a)}
for some
a>0
, where
w^{(a)}
are the solutions from Theorem
1.1
. For each
b>0
,
y^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}=\mathbb{R}^{n-1}
and
R<b
, define on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(y^{\prime},-b)

\displaystyle w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}(x)=\frac{|x-(y^{\prime},-b)|^{2}-R^{2}}{2R}.

Note that
w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}>0
on
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and, since
g_{w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}}
is the hyperbolic metric on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash B_{R}(y^{\prime},-b)
, it holds that
f(\lambda(-A_{w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}}))=\frac{1}{2}
. Suppose for a contradiction that
w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}\geq u
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
for all
b>0
,
y^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
R<b
. Then for given
y^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
of the form
x=(y^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, we have

\displaystyle u(x)\leq w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}(x)=\frac{(x_{n}+b)^{2}-R^{2}}{2R}.

Taking
R\rightarrow b
and subsequently
b\rightarrow+\infty
, it follows that
u(x)\leq x_{n}
for any
x\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
of the form
x=(y^{\prime},x_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
,. Since
y^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
was arbitrary, it follows that
u(x)\leq x_{n}
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
. This contradicts the fifth property in Theorem
1.1
, which tells us that
u(x)=w^{(a)}(x_{n})>w^{(0)}(x_{n})=x_{n}
.
∎

Corollary B.2
.

Suppose
(f,\Gamma)
satisfies (
1.3
)–(
1.7
) and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
, and let
B
denote the open unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then there exist
w\in C^{\infty}(\overline{B})
and
u\in C^{\infty}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\overline{B}\backslash\{p\})
satisfying

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f(\lambda(-A_{u}))=f(\lambda(-A_{w}))=\frac{1}{2}&\text{in }B\\
w>0&\text{on }\partial B\\
u=0&\text{on }\partial B\backslash\{p\}\end{cases}

but
w(x)<u(x)
for some
x\in B
.

Proof.

Let
\varphi
be a Möbius transformation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\cup\{\infty\}
such that
\varphi|_{B}:B\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
is a bijection, and let
v=w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}
for some choice of
b>0
,
y^{\prime}\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{n}
and
R<b
satisfying the conclusion of Corollary
B.1
(with
w=w_{(y^{\prime},-b),R}
therein). Then by conformal invariance (and using the notation introduced above (
2.8
)),

\displaystyle\begin{cases}f(\lambda(-A_{u_{\varphi}}))=f(\lambda(-A_{v_{\varphi}}))=\frac{1}{2}&\text{in }B\\
v_{\varphi}>u_{\varphi}=0&\text{on }\partial B\backslash\varphi^{-1}(\infty),\end{cases}

but
v_{\varphi}(x)<u_{\varphi}(x)
for some
x\in B
. Note that since
v
is bounded away from infinity,
v_{\varphi}
extends uniquely to a function
w
on
\overline{B}
and this extension is positive on
\partial B
.
∎

B.2
Some non-existence results for admissible metrics with prescribed boundary data

As discussed in the introduction, a large part of our solution to the fully nonlinear Loewner-Nirenberg problem when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}>1
in
[
15
]
consisted of obtaining local
C^{0}
boundary estimates on solutions. Theorem
1.5
demonstrates that such estimates in general fail when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
. The construction of the barrier function in
[
15
, Proposition 1.1]
is therefore not possible when
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
, as seen in the following proposition. To give a more general statement, we note that the notion of a continuous viscosity supersolution given in Definition
2.2
extends immediately to the notion of a lower semicontinuous viscosity subsolution
w_{1}
on
\Omega
, i.e. functions
w_{1}:\Omega\rightarrow(0,+\infty]
,
w_{1}\not\equiv+\infty
, satisfying
\liminf_{x\rightarrow y}w_{1}(x)\geq w_{1}(y)
for all
y\in\Omega
.

Proposition B.3
.

Suppose
(f,\Gamma)
satisfies (
1.3
)–(
1.7
) and
\mu_{\Gamma}^{+}\leq 1
. Let
\Omega
be a (possibly unbounded) domain with smooth non-empty boundary and suppose
\Sigma\subset\partial\Omega
has non-empty interior with respect to the topology of the boundary. Then there is no lower semicontinuous function
w>0
on
\Omega
such that both
w(x)\rightarrow+\infty
as
d(x,\Sigma)\rightarrow 0
and

\displaystyle f(\lambda(-A_{w}))\geq\varepsilon>0,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda(-A_{w})\in\Gamma\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in the viscosity sense on }\Omega

(B.1)

for some
\varepsilon>0
.

Proof.

Suppose for a contradiction that such a metric
g_{w}
exists. After rescaling
w
, we may assume without loss of generality that
\varepsilon=\frac{1}{2}
. Choose a point
x_{0}
in the interior of
\Sigma
; without loss of generality, we may assume
x_{0}
is the origin and
\Sigma
is tangent to the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=0\}
at
x_{0}
. Furthermore, by conformal invariance of (
B.1
) and by inverting through a sphere
S
contained in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\backslash\Omega
and tangent to
\Sigma
at the origin if necessary, we may assume that
\Omega
is strictly convex near the origin and lies below the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=0\}
.

Now fix
t_{0}>0
sufficiently small so that
\Omega\cap\{x_{n}>-t_{0}\}
contains a connected component
\Omega_{t_{0}}
whose boundary is Lipschitz and consists of a piece lying within
\Sigma
and a piece lying on the hyperplane
\{x_{n}=-t_{0}\}
. Let
w_{t_{0}}^{(a)}(x)=w^{(a)}(x_{n}+t_{0})
for
x_{n}\geq-t_{0}
, where
w^{(a)}
are the solutions from Theorem
1.1
. Then by the comparison principle in Proposition
2.3
(which also applies for semicontinuous functions, see
[
36
]
),
w>w^{(a)}_{t_{0}}
in
\Omega_{t_{0}}
for each
a
. But by Theorem
1.5
,
w^{(a)}_{t_{0}}\rightarrow\infty
pointwise in
\Omega_{t_{0}}
as
a\rightarrow\infty
, which yields a contradiction for sufficiently large
a
.
∎

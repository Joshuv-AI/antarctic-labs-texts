Near-Optimal Sample Complexity for Online Constrained MDPs
Chang Liu, Yunfan Li, Lin F. Yang

Abstract
Safety is a fundamental challenge in reinforcement learning (RL), particularly in real-world applications such as autonomous driving, robotics, and healthcare. To address this, Constrained Markov Decision Processes (CMDPs) are commonly used to enforce safety constraints while optimizing performance. However, existing methods often suffer from significant safety violations or require a high sample complexity to generate near-optimal policies. We address two settings: relaxed feasibility, where small violations are allowed, and strict feasibility, where no violation is allowed. We propose a model-based primal-dual algorithm that balances regret and bounded constraint violations, drawing on techniques from online RL and constrained optimization. For relaxed feasibility, we prove that our algorithm returns an ε \varepsilon -optimal policy with ε \varepsilon -bounded violation with arbitrarily high probability, requiring O ~ ​ ( S ​ A ​ H 3 ε 2 ) \widetilde{O}\left(\frac{SAH^{3}}{\varepsilon^{2}}\right) learning episodes, matching the lower bound for unconstrained MDPs. For strict feasibility, we prove that our algorithm returns an ε \varepsilon -optimal policy with zero violation with arbitrarily high probability, requiring O ~ ​ ( S ​ A ​ H 5 ε 2 ​ ζ 2 ) \widetilde{O}\left(\frac{SAH^{5}}{\varepsilon^{2}\zeta^{2}}\right) learning episodes, where ζ \zeta is the problem-dependent Slater constant characterizing the size of the feasible region. This result matches the lower bound for learning CMDPs with access to a generative model. Our results demonstrate that learning CMDPs in an online setting is as easy as learning with a generative model and is no more challenging than learning unconstrained MDPs when small violations are allowed.

Problem Setup

We consider a finite-horizon non-stationary constrained Markov Decision Process (MDP) defined by the tuple
M=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},H,P,r,c,b)
, where
\mathcal{S}
is the state space,
\mathcal{A}
is the action space, and
H
is the horizon length. The unknown transition probability at each time step is denoted by
P_{s,a,h}
, where
P_{s,a,h}(s^{\prime})
represents the probability of transitioning to state
s^{\prime}
from state
s
after taking action
a
at time step
h
. The reward function
r_{h}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
quantifies the immediate reward the agent receives for taking action
a
in state
s
at time step
h
. Similarly, the cost function
c_{h}:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to[0,1]
represents safety violations incurred for the same action. We assume that both the reward and cost functions are known to the agent, though the same results can be easily extended to the case where neither function is known. Finally,
b\in(0,H]
is a predefined safety constraint that limits the cumulative cost over the episode.

The agent interacts with the environment over
K
episodes, each consisting of
H
steps. At the start of each episode
k
, the agent selects a randomized policy
\pi^{k}=\{\pi^{k}_{h}\}_{h}
, where at time step
h
the policy
\pi_{h}^{k}:\mathcal{S}\to\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
prescribes a distribution over actions conditioned on the current state. The policy is executed with the goal of maximizing the cumulative reward while ensuring that the cumulative cost remains within the safety limit. The cumulative value at state
s
and time step
h
, with respect to any function
g:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{R}
, under policy
\pi
, is defined as:
V_{h,g}^{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{P,\pi}\left[\sum_{t=h}^{H}g(S_{t},A_{t})\middle|S_{h}=s\right]
,
representing the expected cumulative sum of
g(S_{t},A_{t})
from time step
h
to the end of the episode, given that the process starts in state
s
at time
h
. The objective of CMDP is to solve the following constrained optimization problem:

\max_{\pi}V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})\leq b,

(1)

where
V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})
is the expected cumulative reward value, and
V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})
is the expected cumulative cost value, constrained by the safety threshold
b
. The optimal policy
\pi^{*}
solving
eq.

1
is defined as

\pi^{*}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})\quad\text{s.t.}\quad V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})\leq b.

The corresponding expected reward and cost value are denoted by
V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1})
and
V_{1,c}^{*}(s_{1})
. For a CMDP instance, we define the Slater constant
\zeta\coloneqq\max_{\pi}b-V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})
. The Slater constant
\zeta
measures the size of the feasible region. We also define the policy that with the minimal cost value:
\pi_{c}^{*}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}b-V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})
.

We now define the regret and constraint violation over
K
episodes:

\textrm{Regret}(K)\coloneq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\quad\textrm{and}\quad\textrm{CV}(K)\coloneq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,c}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-b\right)\right)_{+}.

(2)

We will provide upper bounds on both regret and constraint violation of our algorithm, but more importantly, convert such bounds to our final sample complexity bounds. For a small suboptimality error
\varepsilon
, we define the two settings as follows:

Relaxed feasibility:

The agent’s objective is to return a policy that with high probability achieves an approximately optimal reward value, while violates the constraint by a small margin. Formally the agent returns a policy
\pi
such that with high probability,

V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})\geq V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1})-\varepsilon,\quad\text{and }\quad V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})\leq b+\varepsilon.

(3)

Strict feasibility:

The agent’s objective is to return a policy that with high probability achieves an approximately optimal reward value, and simultaneously achieves zero constraint violation. Formally the agent returns a policy
\pi
such that with high probability,

V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})\geq V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1})-\varepsilon,\quad\text{and }\quad V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1})\leq b.

(4)

In the next section, we present our technical methodology to tackle both settings in online CMDPs.

Methodology

Before introducing our method, we examine the state-of-the-art algorithms in tabular online CMDPs, and discuss why their methods fail to achieve near-optimal sample complexity.

One of the biggest challenges in analyzing model-based algorithms in unconstrained MDPs and CMDPs is to decouple the correlation between the empirical model
\widehat{P}
and the estimate values
\widehat{V}^{\pi^{k}}
. Indeed, both

6

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1})

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{E}_{\pi^{k},P}\left[\sum_{s^{\prime}}\left\lvert\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}(s^{\prime})-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}(s^{\prime})\right\rvert H\right],

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}-P_{s,a,h}\right)\left(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}-V_{h+1,r}^{*}\right)\leq\sum_{s^{\prime}}\left\lvert\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}(s^{\prime})-P_{s,a,h}(s^{\prime})\right\rvert\cdot\left(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s^{\prime})-V_{h+1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s^{\prime})\right),

This argument fails in CMDPs, because the optimal policy
\pi^{*}

The high-level idea of

\widehat{P}

\widehat{V}

\pi

To tackle these challenges, we use a model-based approach to address the CMDP problem defined in

1

\bar{N}_{h}(s,a)

(s,a)

N_{h}(s,a,s^{\prime})

(s,a)

s^{\prime}

N_{h}(s,a)=\sum_{s^{\prime}}N_{h}(s,a,s^{\prime})

(s,a)

\bar{N}_{h}(s,a)

\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}(s^{\prime})=\frac{N_{h}(s,a,s^{\prime})}{N_{h}(s,a)}

\widehat{P}

We adopt a UCB-style bonus for both reward and cost. For any reward function

\widehat{V}^{\pi}_{h,\widetilde{r}}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\widehat{Q}^{\pi}_{h,\widetilde{r}}(s,a)

\widehat{Q}^{\pi}_{h,\underline{c}}(s,a)=\max\{c_{h}(s,a)-b_{h,c}^{k,t,\pi}(s,a)+\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}\widehat{V}^{\pi}_{h+1,\underline{c}},0\}

\widehat{V}^{\pi}_{h,\underline{c}}(s)=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi(a|s)\widehat{Q}^{\pi}_{h,\underline{c}}(s,a)

return

\bar{\pi}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k=1}^{K}\pi^{k}

Algorithm 1

Model-based algorithm for online CMDP

Finally, we state our algorithm in
algorithm

1
. For each episode, we first execute
T
iterations of primal and dual updates. The agent executes the mixture policy
\pi^{k}
and receives the reward, cost, and the next state. We update the empirical model using the doubling technique as described before. In the end, the algorithm outputs the mixture policy
\bar{\pi}
that mixes all intermediate policies.

Proof.

Recall the definition of regret:
\textrm{Regret}(K)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)
. Note that
\pi^{*}
is the optimal solution to the original CMDP optimization problem

1

is an approximation solution to the empirical CMDP optimization problem

6

defined as the optimal solution to the following optimization problem

\pi^{\tau,*}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k}),\quad\text{s.t.}\quad V_{1,c}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}=b+\tau.

(10)

We decompose the regret as

\displaystyle\textrm{Regret}(K)=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,r}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right),

and give the regret bound by bounding each term above. The first term is the error incurred by replacing the original constraint constant
b
by a relaxed empirical constraint constant
b^{\prime}=b+\tau
for each episode
k
. Note that by definition of
\pi^{*}
, we have
V_{1,c}^{*}(s_{1})\leq b\leq b^{\prime}
. Thus by definition of

in

. Hence, we bound the first term by 0:
\sum_{k=1}^{K}(V_{1,r}^{*}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k}))\leq 0
.

By definition of the proxy policy
\pi^{\tau,*}
in
eq.

10
, and noting that
\tau
is a predetermined constant, we see that
\pi^{\tau,*}
is a fixed policy that is independent of the online learning process. Thus we can apply

and bound the second term by 0:
\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(V_{1,r}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\leq 0
.

The third term is the optimization error, and it is incurred because

is an approximation solution generated by iterative primal-dual updates. We bound this term by using the primal update rules in

and also by

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{\tau,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\leq 2\varepsilon_{1}HK\sqrt{T}+\frac{UHK}{\sqrt{T}}\leq 2\varepsilon_{1}HK\sqrt{T}+\frac{H^{2}K}{\tau\sqrt{T}}.

Finally, the last term in the regret decomposition is the model prediction error, consisting of the errors caused by inaccurate empirical models and additional bonus terms. Worth mentioning, this term is essentially similar to the entire regret in
41
as the algorithms share the similar exploration bonus and update rules for transition models. We state in

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)=\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{SAH^{3}K}).

(11)

Putting everything together, we conclude our desired regret bound.
∎

, all dual variable sequences, all
2\leq l\leq\log_{2}K+1
, and all feasible sequences
\left\{X_{h,s,a}\right\}_{(s,a,h)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\times[H]}
.
Finally, recalling our assumption
0\in\mathcal{X}_{h+1,\mathcal{I}}
, we see that for every total profile
\mathcal{I}
and its associated feasible sequence
\left\{X_{h,s,a}\right\}

\sum_{s,a,h}\max\left\{\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{(l)}-P_{s,a,h},X_{h+1,s,a}\right\rangle,0\right\}\in\left\{\sum_{s,a,h}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{(l)}-P_{s,a,h},\widetilde{X}_{h+1,s,a}\right\rangle\mid\widetilde{X}_{h+1,s,a}\in\mathcal{X}_{h+1,\mathcal{I}},\forall(s,a,h)\right\}

holds true. Consequently, the uniform upper bound on the right-hand side continues to be a valid upper bound on
\sum_{s,a,h}\max\left\{\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{(l)}-P_{s,a,h},X_{h+1,s,a}\right\rangle,0\right\}
. This concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 7
.

With probability at least
1-4\delta^{\prime}\log(KH)
,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi_{h}^{k}\leq\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{H^{3}K}).

Proof.

Note that
\phi_{h}^{k}=\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi^{k}(a|s_{h}^{k})\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a)-\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})\right)
is a zero-mean random variable conditional on
\pi^{k}
and is upper bounded by constant
H
. By
lemma

18
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\phi_{h}^{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\textsf{Var}(\phi_{h}^{k})\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}+3H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2KH^{3}\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}+3H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}

with probability at least
1-4\delta^{\prime}\log(KH)
.
∎

Lemma 8
.

With probability at least
1-SAH^{2}K^{2}\delta^{\prime}
,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}(P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}})\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\leq\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{H^{2}K}).

Proof.

We note that conditional on state-action pair
(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})
, the vectors
P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}
and
\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}}
are both independent of the value function estimate
\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}
. Also, the vector
\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}}
has the mean of
P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}
. Hence,
(P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}})\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}
is a zero-mean random variable bounded by
H
from above, and we thus apply
lemma

18
and have

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}(P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}})\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\leq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{V}\left(P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}+3H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}

with probability at least
1-SAH^{2}K^{2}\delta^{\prime}
. By
lemma

10
, we obtain our lemma.
∎

Lemma 9
.

With probability at least
1-4\delta^{\prime}\log(KH)
,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\left(\sum_{h=1}^{H}r_{h}(s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k})-V_{1,r}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\leq\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{H^{2}K}).

Note that conditional on

. By
lemma

18
, we have

\displaystyle\left\lvert\sum_{k=1}^{K}E_{k}\right\rvert

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\sum_{k=1}^{K}\textsf{Var}(E_{k})\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}+3H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}

∎

Lemma 10
.

With probability at least
1-6SAHK\delta^{\prime}
,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right)\leq\widetilde{O}(H^{2}K+\sqrt{H^{5}K}+SAH^{3}),

This proof is modified from the proof to lemma 11 in
41

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle-\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right\rangle^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=2}^{H}(\widehat{V}_{h,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{h}^{k}))^{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right\rangle^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle

and since the value function estimates are bounded by

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+2H\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\max\left\{\widehat{V}_{h,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{h}^{k})-\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\rangle,\,0\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle

By definition of update rule of
\widehat{Q}
functions, we have

\displaystyle\leq\,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h}^{k}-\boldsymbol{1}_{s_{h+1}^{k}},(\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}})^{2}\right\rangle

bounded by

Similarly we can show that with probability at least

,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\mathbb{V}\left(P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}\right)=\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h},\left(V_{h+1}^{k}\right)^{2}\right\rangle-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\left(\left\langle P_{s_{h}^{k},a_{h}^{k},h},V_{h+1}^{k}\right\rangle\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\,

Lemma 11
.

For the relaxed feasibility setting, let
b^{\prime}=b+\tau
, for some
\tau>0
, we have

\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\leq\frac{H}{\tau}.

For the strict feasibility setting, let
b^{\prime}=b-\Delta
, for some
\Delta\in(0,\zeta)
, we have

\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\leq\frac{H}{\zeta-\Delta}.

Proof.

Writing the empirical CMDP in
eq.

6
in its Lagrangian form,

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})=\max_{\pi}\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\lambda\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)

Using the linear programming formulation of CMDPs in terms of the state-occupancy measures
\mu
, we know that both the objective and the constraint are linear functions of
\mu
, and strong duality holds w.r.t.
\mu
. Since
\mu
and
\pi
have a one-to-one mapping, we can switch the min and the max, implying,

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})=\min_{\lambda\geq 0}\max_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\lambda\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)

Since
\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}
is the optimal dual variable for the empirical CMDP in
eq.

6
and recall
\pi_{c}^{*}\in\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}b-V_{1,r}^{\pi}(s_{1})
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})+\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\left((b^{\prime}-b)+(b-V_{1,c}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k}))+(V_{1,c}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k}))\right)

Note that
\pi_{c}^{*}
is a fixed policy and by
lemma

16
we have
V_{1,c}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})\geq 0
. Recall that
\zeta=b-V_{1,c}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1})
, and we have

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{*}_{c}}(s_{1}^{k})+\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\left((b^{\prime}-b)+\zeta\right).

For the relaxed feasibility setting, we let
b^{\prime}=b+\tau
for some
\tau>0
. Then we have

\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\leq\frac{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})}{\tau+\zeta}\leq\frac{H}{\tau}.

Note that the shift
\tau>0
is introduced to get rid of
\zeta
in the results of the relaxed feasibility setting.

For the strict feasibility setting, let
b^{\prime}=b-\Delta
for some
\Delta\in(0,\zeta)
, we have

\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\leq\frac{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi_{c}^{*}}(s_{1}^{k})}{-\Delta+\zeta}\leq\frac{H}{\zeta-\Delta}.

∎

Lemma 12
.

Let
U>\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}
and for any
\widetilde{\pi}
s.t.

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})+U\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)_{+}\leq B,

we have

\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)_{+}\leq\frac{B}{U-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}}.

Proof.

Define
\nu(\gamma)=\max_{\pi}\{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\mid\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}-\gamma\}
and note that by definition,
\nu(0)=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})
, and that
\nu
is a decreasing function for its argument.
Then, for any policy
\pi
s.t.
\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}-\gamma
, we have

\displaystyle\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})=\nu(0)\quad(\text{by strong duality})

This further implies

\displaystyle\nu(0)-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\gamma

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\gamma

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-(b^{\prime}-\gamma))

Since this holds for any policy
\pi
s.t.
\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}-\gamma
, we have

\nu(0)-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\gamma\geq\max_{\pi}\{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\mid\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}-\gamma\}=\nu(\gamma),

and thus

\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\gamma\leq\nu(0)-\nu(\gamma).

Now we choose
\widetilde{\gamma}=-(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})_{+}
,

\displaystyle U-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}\rvert

\displaystyle=\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\widetilde{\gamma}+U\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\nu(0)-\nu(\widetilde{\gamma})+U\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}\rvert

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})+U\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}\rvert+\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-\nu(\widetilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})+U(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})_{+}+\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-\nu(\widetilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle\leq B+\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-\nu(\widetilde{\gamma}).

Now let us bound
\nu(\widetilde{\gamma})
:

\displaystyle\nu(\widetilde{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi}\{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\mid\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}+(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})_{+}\}

\displaystyle\geq\max_{\pi}\{\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\mid\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})\leq\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})\}\quad(\text{tightening the constraint})

\displaystyle\geq\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k}).

Finally,

U-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}\lvert\widetilde{\gamma}\rvert\leq B\implies(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widetilde{\pi}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})_{+}\leq\frac{B}{U-\widehat{\lambda}^{k,*}}.

∎

Lemma 13
.

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})+\lambda\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}-\lambda)\left(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right).

Proof.

For any episode
k
and any time step
t
in the primal-dual iterations, the primal update ensures that for any policy
\pi
,

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime})\geq\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}).

Let
\pi
be
\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}
, and rearrange:

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\leq\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})).

Note that
\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}
is the solution to the empirical CMDP in
eq.

6
, thus
\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})\leq b^{\prime}
, and we have

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\leq\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})).

Take average over
T
iterations,

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\left(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right).

To use
lemma

14
, we rewrite as

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)+\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\lambda\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}-\lambda)\left(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right).

Note that
\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})
is constant throughout
T
primal-dual iterations, and
\pi^{k}
is a mixture policy, then

\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\widehat{\pi}^{k,*}}(s_{1}^{k})-\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})+\lambda\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})-b^{\prime}\right)\leq\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}-\lambda)\left(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right).

∎

Lemma 14
.

Recall we set
\eta=\frac{U}{H\sqrt{T}}
. For any episode
k
, any
\lambda\in[0,U]
, and primal and dual updates in
eqs.

8
and
9
, we have

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\left(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}-\lambda\right)\left(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}}(s_{1}^{k})\right)\leq 2\varepsilon_{1}H\sqrt{T}+\frac{UH}{\sqrt{T}}.

Proof.

In this proof, for the simplicity of notations, we will only focus on primal-dual iterations in an arbitrary episode
k\in[K]
, and thus we will drop all dependency on
k
when the context is clear. The dual update is given by

\widehat{\lambda}_{t+1}=\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}[\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\eta(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))].

Particularly, we denote

\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}=P_{[0,U]}[\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\eta(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))].

First, we shall look at
\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda\rvert
:

\displaystyle\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert

\displaystyle=\lvert\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}[\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}]-\lambda\rvert=\lvert\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}[\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}]-\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}+\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\lvert\mathcal{R}_{\Lambda}[\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}]-\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}\rvert+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert.

Take square on both sides,

\displaystyle\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}\lvert\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}^{\prime}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\eta(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))-\lambda\rvert^{2}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda\rvert^{2}-2\eta(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda)+\eta^{2}(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda\rvert^{2}-2\eta(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda)+\eta^{2}H^{2}.

Now we have

(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda)(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\eta^{2}H^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda\rvert^{2}-\lvert\widehat{\lambda}_{t+1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}}{2\eta}.

By taking average over
T
iterations and telescoping, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}(\widehat{\lambda}_{t}-\lambda)(b^{\prime}-\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\widehat{\pi}_{t}}(s_{1}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\eta^{2}H^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\lvert\lambda_{1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}-\lvert\lambda_{T+1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}}{2\eta T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\eta^{2}H^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{\lvert\lambda_{1}-\lambda\rvert^{2}}{2\eta T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{1}^{2}+2\varepsilon_{1}U+\eta^{2}H^{2}}{2\eta}+\frac{U^{2}}{2\eta T}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\varepsilon_{1}U}{\eta}+\frac{\eta H^{2}}{2}+\frac{U^{2}}{2\eta T}

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon_{1}H\sqrt{T}+\frac{UH}{\sqrt{T}}.

∎

Appendix C
Useful Lemmas

Lemma 15

(Primal update)
.

We re-state the primal update in
eq.

8
:

\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1})-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1})-b^{\prime}\right)=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}).

Proof.

Note the estimate value functions
\widehat{V}_{1,g}^{\pi}
of any reward-like function
g:\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is given by:

\widehat{V}_{1,g}^{\pi}(s)=\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P},\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}g(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{1}=s\right].

Then we have

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}_{t}^{k}

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}}^{\pi}(s_{1})-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\left(\widehat{V}_{1,\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1})-b^{\prime}\right)

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P},\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\widetilde{r}(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{1}=s_{1}\right]-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P},\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}\underline{c}(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{1}=s_{1}\right]+\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\cdot b^{\prime}.

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\widehat{P},\pi}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{H}(\widetilde{r}-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\underline{c})(S_{t},A_{t})|S_{1}=s_{1}\right]

\displaystyle=\operatorname*{arg\,max}_{\pi}\widehat{V}_{1,\widetilde{r}-\widehat{\lambda}_{t}^{k}\underline{c}}^{\pi}(s_{1}),

where the second-to-last equality follows from the linearity of expectation and reward-like functions, and the last equality follows from the definition of estimate value functions.
∎

Lemma 16

(Optimism)
.

With probability at least
1-2\delta^{\prime}
, for any fixed policy
\pi
, reward function
g
and
s\in\mathcal{S},h\in[H]
, we have

\widehat{V}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s)\geq V_{h,g}^{\pi}(s)\geq\widehat{V}_{h,\underline{g}}^{\pi}(s).

Proof.

First, we define the following function

f(p,v,n)\coloneqq\langle p,v\rangle+\max\left\{\frac{20}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(p,v)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}},\frac{400}{9}\frac{H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}\right\}

for any vector
p\in\Delta^{S}
, any non-negative vector
v\in\mathbb{R}^{S}
obeying
\|v\|_{\infty}\leq H
, and any positive integer
n
. We claim that

f(p,v,n)\text{ is non-decreasing in each entry of }v.

(17)

To justify this claim, consider any
1\leq s\leq S
, and let us freeze
p,n
and all but the
s
-th entries of
v
. It then suffices to observe that (i)
f
is a continuous function, and (ii) except for at most two possible choices of
v(s)
that obey
\frac{20}{3}\sqrt{\frac{V(p,v)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}}=\frac{400}{9}\frac{H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}
, one can use the properties of
p
and
v
to calculate

\displaystyle\frac{\partial f(p,v,n)}{\partial v(s)}

\displaystyle=p(s)+\frac{20}{3}\mathds{1}\left\{\frac{20}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}(p,v)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}}\geq\frac{400}{9}\frac{H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{n}\right\}\frac{p(s)(v(s)-\langle p,v\rangle)\sqrt{\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}}{\sqrt{n\mathbb{V}(p,v)}}

\displaystyle=p(s)+\mathds{1}\left\{\sqrt{n\mathbb{V}(p,v)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}\geq\frac{20}{3}H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}\right\}\frac{\frac{20}{3}H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{\sqrt{n\mathbb{V}(p,v)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}}\cdot\frac{p(s)(v(s)-\langle p,v\rangle)}{H}

\displaystyle\geq\min\left\{p(s)+p(s)\frac{(v(s)-\langle p,v\rangle)}{H},p(s)\right\}

\displaystyle\geq p(s)\min\left\{\frac{H+v(s)-\langle p,v\rangle}{H},1\right\}\geq 0,

thus establishing the claim. We now proceed to the proof of
lemma

16
. Consider any
(h,k,s,a)
, and we divide into two cases.

Case 1:
N_{h}^{k}(s,a)\leq 2
. In this case, the following trivial bounds arise directly from the value function initiation:

\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)=H\geq Q_{h,g}^{\pi}(s,a)\geq 0=\widehat{Q}_{h,\underline{g}}^{\pi}(s,a),

\widehat{V}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s)=H\geq V_{h,g}^{\pi}(s)\geq 0=\widehat{V}_{h,\underline{g}}^{\pi}(s).

Case 2:
N_{h}^{k}(s,a)>2
. Suppose now that
\widehat{Q}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}\geq Q_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\geq\widehat{Q}_{h+1,\underline{g}}^{\pi}
, which also implies that
\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}\geq V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\geq\widehat{V}_{h+1,\underline{g}}^{\pi}
. If
\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)=H
, then
\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)\geq Q_{h,g}^{\pi}(s,a)
holds trivially, and hence it suffices to look at the case with
\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)<H
. According to the update rule, it holds that

\displaystyle\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)

(18)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g_{h}(s,a)+\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}\right\rangle+c_{1}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}+c_{2}\frac{H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle g_{h}(s,a)+\frac{48H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{3N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}+f\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi},N_{h}^{k}(s,a)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle g_{h}(s,a)+\frac{48H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{3N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}+f\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi},N_{h}^{k}(s,a)\right)

for any
(s,a)
, where the last inequality results from the claim (
eq.

17
) and the hypothesis
\widehat{V}_{h+1,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}\geq V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}
. Moreover, applying Lemma 19, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left\{\left|\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k}-P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle\right|>2\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}}+\frac{14H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{3N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left\{\left|\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k}-P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle\right|>\sqrt{\frac{2\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)-1}}+\frac{7H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{3N_{h}^{k}(s,a)-1}\right\}\leq 2\delta^{\prime}.

This implies that with probability at least
1-2\delta^{\prime}
,

\displaystyle f\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi},N_{h}^{k}(s,a)\right)=\left\langle P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle+\left\langle\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k}-P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle

\displaystyle+\max\left\{\frac{20}{3}\sqrt{\frac{\mathbb{V}\left(\widehat{P}_{s,a,h}^{k},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right)\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}},\frac{400}{9}\frac{H\log\frac{1}{\delta^{\prime}}}{N_{h}^{k}(s,a)}\right\}

\displaystyle\geq\left\langle P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle.

Substitution into
eq.

18
gives: with probability at least
1-2\delta^{\prime}
,

\widehat{Q}_{h,\widetilde{g}}^{\pi}(s,a)\geq g_{h}(s,a)+\left\langle P_{s,a,h},V_{h+1,g}^{\pi}\right\rangle=Q_{h,g}^{\pi}(s,a).

The proof for
Q_{h,g}^{\pi}\geq\widehat{Q}_{h,\underline{g}}^{\pi}
is analogous and we leave out here.
∎

Lemma 17
.

Recall the definition of
N^{k}_{h}(s^{k}_{h},a^{k}_{h})
in
algorithm

1
. It holds that:

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sum_{h=1}^{H}\frac{1}{\max\{N^{k}_{h}(s^{k}_{h},a^{k}_{h}),1\}}\leq 2SAH\log_{2}K.

Proof.

In view of the doubling batch update rule, it is easily seen that: for any given
(s,a,h)
,

\sum_{k=1}^{K}\frac{1}{\max\{N^{k}_{h}(s^{k}_{h},a^{k}_{h}),1\}}\mathds{1}\left\{(s,a)=(s^{k}_{h},a^{k}_{h})\right\}\leq 2\log_{2}K,

since each
(s,a,h)
is associated with at most
\log_{2}K
epochs. Summing over
(s,a,h)
completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 18

(Freedman’s inequality)
.

Let
(M_{n})_{n\geq 0}
be a martingale such that
M_{0}=0
and
|M_{n}-M_{n-1}|\leq c
(
\forall n\geq 1
) hold for some quantity
c>0
. Define

\text{Var}_{n}:=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbb{E}\left[(M_{k}-M_{k-1})^{2}\middle|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

for every
n\geq 0
, where
\mathcal{F}_{k}
is the
\sigma
-algebra generated by
(M_{1},\dots,M_{k})
. Then for any integer
n\geq 1
and any
\epsilon,\delta>0
, one has

\mathbb{P}\left[|M_{n}|\geq 2\sqrt{2}\sqrt{\text{Var}_{n}\log\frac{1}{\delta}}+2\sqrt{\epsilon\log\frac{1}{\delta}}+2c\log\frac{1}{\delta}\right]\leq 2\left(\log_{2}\left(\frac{nc^{2}}{\epsilon}\right)+1\right)\delta.

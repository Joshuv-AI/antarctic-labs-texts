Skin Sympathetic Nerve Activity After Pulsed Field Pulmonary Vein Isolation for Paroxysmal Atrial Fibrillation: The NEU-PFA Study
Authors: Lo-Chieh Ling, Li-Wei Lo, Fa-Po Chung, Shih-Lin Chang, Yu-Feng Hu, Chin-Yun Lin, Ting-Yung Chang, Ling Kuo, Chih-Min Liu, Shin-Huei Liu, Cheng-I Wu, Yenn-Jiang Lin, Yu-Cheng Hsieh, Cheng-Hung Li, Ming-Jen Kuo, Shang-Ju Wu, Guan-Yi Lee, Yu-Shan Huang, Yu-Shan Chien, Cheng-Han Chan, Yuen Hoong Phang, Quoc-Hoang Nguyen, Xuan-Hoang Le, Naomi Ong, Da-Wei Chang, Shih-Ann Chen
Published: 2026

Pulmonary vein (PV) isolation (PVI) is recommended for rhythm control in symptomatic paroxysmal atrial fibrillation. Pulsed field ablation (PFA) creates nonthermal, myocardial-selective lesions and may reduce collateral injury, including unintended autonomic neuromodulation.1,2 Skin sympathetic nerve activity (SKNA), extracted from high-frequency components of surface recordings (neuECG), is a noninvasive surrogate of sympathetic outflow.3 We quantified serial SKNA after PFA-only PVI and explored relationships with procedural dose, biomarker kinetics, and rhythm outcomes.

The skin sympathetic nerve activity (neuECG) after pulsed field pulmonary vein isolation for paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (NEU-PFA) study enrolled 30 consecutive, drug-refractory patients with paroxysmal atrial fibrillation (18–80 years) at 2 centers undergoing index PVI with a pentaspline PFA system (FARAWAVE/FARASTAR). Institutional review boards approved the protocol; participants gave written informed consent. To minimize autonomic confounding, class I/III antiarrhythmics were discontinued ≥5 half-lives preprocedure (amiodarone excepted), β-blockers were withheld around SKNA acquisition, and antiarrhythmics were resumed during the ≥8-week blanking period. SKNA was recorded under standardized conditions (supine, fasting, 25 °C, midday) 1 day preablation, on day 1, and at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months, using a lead-I configuration (SKNA-Chest) and a right-arm montage (SKNA-Arm) sampled at 2000 Hz for ≥10 minutes, with band-pass filtering and artifact review.3 Scheduled 14-day ambulatory monitoring occurred at 3, 6, and 12 months, and laboratory testing immediately postprocedure and at 2 weeks. Atrial arrhythmia recurrence was defined as atrial fibrillation/atrial tachycardia/flutter ≥30 seconds after an 8-week blanking period. The primary end point was within-patient change in SKNA versus baseline (Wilcoxon signed-rank tests with Holm adjustment within each channel); heart rate was compared with baseline without adjustment. Data will be made available on reasonable request.

Participants were 57.9±11.1 years old, 80% were men, and the mean CHA2DS2-VASc score was 1.2±1.2. Acute PVI was achieved in all patients using a standardized 8-applications-per-vein approach (mean 37.0±6.7; additional at operator discretion); procedure and fluoroscopy times were 121.9±39.1 and 29.4±16.9 minutes, respectively. No left atrial linear ablation was performed; cavotricuspid isthmus ablation was performed in 7 patients (23.3%). One patient developed cardiac tamponade during postablation mapping (managed without sequelae); no other serious complications, phrenic nerve dysfunction, vasovagal reactions, or pulmonary vein stenosis (3-month CT/MRI) were observed.

Median baseline SKNA was 47.6 nV (interquartile range [IQR], 37.9–57.0) in the arm channel and 49.9 nV (IQR, 41.5–59.6) in the chest channel. Neither SKNA-Arm nor SKNA-Chest differed from baseline after multiplicity adjustment at day 1 (44.7 nV [IQR, 37.4–47.0], nominal P=0.167; 47.7 nV [IQR, 40.4–53.9], nominal P=0.470) or at 3, 6, 9, and 12 months (all Holm-adjusted P>0.05; Figure [A]). The apparent SKNA-Arm decrease at 3 months (43.0 nV [IQR, 38.4–44.9]; nominal P=0.053) and increase at 12 months (53.3 nV [IQR, 48.8–57.7]; nominal P=0.050) were borderline, nonmonotonic, and did not survive repeated-measures adjustment (Figure [A]). Serial heart rate did not differ from baseline at day 1, 3, 6, 9, or 12 months (P=0.160, 0.214, 0.466, 0.637, and 0.502, respectively; Figure [B]). A posthoc analysis indicated 80% power to detect sustained mean SKNA shifts of ≈5 to 6 nV (≈11%–12% of baseline); smaller effects could have been missed.

Cardiac and inflammatory biomarkers rose immediately after ablation and normalized by 2 weeks (eg, troponin-I 3.86±2.49 to <0.1 ng/mL; high-sensitivity troponin-T 1109±852 to 9.9±6.9 ng/L). Total PFA application count correlated strongly with immediate troponin-I (r=0.814; P<0.001) and modestly with Creatine Kinase-MB (CK-MB) (r=0.408; P=0.025). In contrast, delta SKNA (postablation minus baseline) showed no significant correlation with any biomarker of myocardial injury, inflammation, or hemolysis (troponins, Creatine Kinase (CK)/CK-MB, Lactate Dehydrogenase (LDH), C-reactive protein (CRP), leukocytes, hemoglobin, potassium, ESR, and haptoglobin; all P>0.05), suggesting dissociation between biochemical injury and sympathetic tone.

Atrial tachyarrhythmia recurrence occurred in 8 patients (26.7%) during blanking; 12-month arrhythmia-free survival was 70.0%. Among 9 patients with late recurrence, early delta SKNA-Chest trended higher (P=0.068), whereas SKNA-Arm did not (P=0.395); given few events and channel discordance, this is hypothesis-generating rather than prognostic.

In this multicenter prospective cohort, PFA-only PVI was associated with stable sympathetic tone on serial SKNA through 12 months, despite a clear dose-response between PFA applications and biochemical myocardial injury. Consistent with PFA’s myocardial selectivity, these data extend prior autonomic evaluations that relied mainly on heart rate variability.2 Thermal PVI has been associated with measurable SKNA alterations,4 supporting an energy-source-dependent autonomic footprint. Heart rate showed no sustained chronotropic change (Figure [B]). These findings differ from a swine study in which epicardial PFA of atrial ganglionated plexi caused acute ganglionated plexi damage5; SKNA cannot assess lesion transmurality, and ganglionated plexi sparing may instead reflect epicardial neural location, PV-antral targeting, device geometry, and energy delivery. Findings may differ in persistent atrial fibrillation, where atrial substrate and autonomic remodeling are greater. The absence of correlation with biomarkers or recurrence suggests SKNA is unlikely to be a useful mechanistic or prognostic end point after PFA. Larger comparative studies with continuous monitoring and multimodal autonomic phenotyping are warranted.

Explore the interaction between industrial structure and ecosystem services in the Loess Plateau
Authors: Yixin Wang, Meiying Tang, Chong Jiang, Chaoqun Li, Maihuan Zhao, Zhiwei Cao, Yi Xiao, Huimin Wang, Gang Liu, Hucheng Zong, Yuan Luo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The relationship between socio-economic development and ecological protection is a dynamic and interactive process. How to balance and coordinate the achievement of both objectives is an eternal topic concerning the sustainable development of nations and regions. Industry is a key factor driving economic growth. The development of industry brings technological progress and the acceleration of urbanization, but also leading to excessive consumption of resources, environmental pollution and the destruction of ecosystem. The health of ecosystem is closely related to the stability of the ecosystem services (ES) required by industrial production activities. Therefore, it is a prerequisite for realizing sustainable social and economic development to scientifically understand the impact of industrial development on ES. In summary, this study systematically analyzes the bidirectional influence mechanism between industry and ecosystems, proposing a research framework to explore the complex interactions between the two. Based on this research framework, this paper constructs a quantitative analysis model of the interaction between industry and ES using spatial simultaneous equation modeling techniques, revealing the complex feedback loop relationship between the two. This research can provide scientific references for ecological protection and high-quality development strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1038/s41598-026-52874-0.

Introduction

Ecosystem services (ES) is a bridge connecting human economy, society and ecosystem, which can e summarized as the material circulation, energy flow and information transmission betwen living and non-living environments within a specific time and space1,2. ES are also the various benefits provided by ecosystems for human economies and societies, which are mainly influenced by climate and land factors3. With the continuous development of the human economy and society, the influence on the stability of ES supply is increasing, which leads to a trade-off between economic and social development and ecological protection4. The theoretical system of ES is an important tool for managers to understand the relationship between human economic activities and ecosystem changes. The ecosystem interacts with the human economic society by providing ES. It not only provides various resources for the human socioeconomic system, but also accepts feedback and adjustment from human economic activities5. As an important form of human economic activity, industrial development level is also the core driving force of economic growth. Different industrial structure characteristics are also regarded as an important symbol to distinguish the development level of countries or regions6, and it is also one of the important factors to determine the regional economic resilience7. Therefore, scientifically identifying the interaction between industries and ES is an important step to break the trade-off model between economic society development and ecological protection.

The development of industry not only means economic prosperity, but also represents the progress of society and improvement of people’s living standards. With the development of industry, it will bring more employment opportunities and economic growth, and at the same time, it will promote the development of scientific and technological innovation and environmental protection technology8. Industrial development can also promote regional coordination, form industrial processing chains and production value chains in local areas and improve the relative competitiveness of the whole region9. Different types of industries depend on land to different degrees. Industrial development will directly lead to significant changes in land use patterns, mainly reflected in the type, intensity, pattern and efficiency of land use10–12. As an important part of socioeconomic system, industry also has a complex mutual feedback relationship with the ecosystem, which indirectly affects the supply capacity of ES mainly through changes in climate conditions and landscape patterns 13. This interdependence and interaction form the coupling relationship between the two systems. Managers need to understand this feature, formulate corresponding management strategies, and realize the coordinated development of the industry and ecosystem.

How to quantify the relationship between industry and ES has always been a hot and difficult topic in the field of sustainable development research. This relationship is very complicated, because the impact of industrial activities on ES is multi-dimensional and multi-level14–16. And it will change greatly in space and time. Existing research on this relationship can mainly be divided into three categories. The first category is indicator analysis17–19. This method is straightforward and easy to understand. By selecting a number of economic indicators and ecological indicators, the index system is constructed to comprehensively evaluate the impact of industrial activities on ES. The research results can help researchers to make preliminary overall judgments on the ecological impact of this industry. This method is suitable for the research with good data availability. The second category is spatial analysis15,20–22. This method analyzes the spatial distribution pattern of ES and industries, and then extracts the common laws of their spatial existence, and then puts forward some spatial partition management strategies. This method is suitable for the research of strong spatial heterogeneity of research variables. The third category is causal analysis23–26. This method can evaluate the one-way driving relationship between drivers and single ES by establishing a regression model, so as to deepen the understanding of how specific ES respond to changes of single driver. This method is suitable to study continuous time series data. The above research substantially simplifies the complex relationship between industry and ES, and can answer the question of the relationship between industry and ES to some extent. It is worth noting that these studies belong to different theoretical systems and have their own compulsory assumptions. Therefore, it is often difficult to unify or even conflict with the research conclusions. How to quantify the complex interactions between industries and ES, while fully considering their spatial distribution characteristics, is a scientific problem to be solved urgently.

The Loess Plateau (LP) is located in the arid and semi-arid area in the northwest of China. It is an important energy and chemical base in China, but the ecological environment is very fragile27. With the implementation of China’s reform and opening up, the industrial development of the LP has achieved great social and economic benefits, but correspondingly, the rapid industrial development has accelerated the disorderly expansion of urban land, further leading to various environmental and ecological problems such as soil erosion, desertification, forest and grass degradation, land salinization and so on28,29. China attaches great importance to the trade-off between economic development and ecological protection in the LP, and has implemented various ecological restoration and sustainable development projects30,31, as well as a series of corresponding strategic plans32,33. It aims at promoting the self-repair of ecosystems and rebuild the balance of socioeconomic system and ecosystem through engineering and non-engineering means. Based on the realistic dilemma of the LP and the strategic needs of management, this area was selected as the research area of this paper. The innovations of this research were: (1) This paper systematically analyzes the two-way influence mechanism between industry and ecosystems, and puts forward a research framework for exploring the complex interactions between industry and ecosystems. (2) This paper constructs a quantitative analysis model of the interaction between industry and ecosystems, revealing the complex feedback loop relationship between the two. The scientific identification of the impact of industrial development on ecosystems is a prerequisite for sustainable socio-economic development. This study can provide a scientific reference for ecological protection and high-quality development strategies.

Materials and methods

Study area

The study area of this paper is LP in the middle and upper reaches of the Yellow River Basin 34. Due to the unique geological structure and climatic conditions, soil erosion is serious in this area, land desertification is prominent and the ecosystem is extremely fragile. LP spans seven provinces including Qinghai, Gansu, Ningxia, Inner Mongolia, Shaanxi, Shanxi and Henan provinces 1. As shown in Fig. 1, (a) is the spatial location of LP, (b) is the administrative boundary of the cities, (c) is the spatial distribution of elevation. The terrain gradually decreases from northwest to southeast, including hills, plateaus, terraces, plains, deserts, arid grasslands, highland grasslands, earth-rock mountains and other landform types 35. Because of its special geographical environment, fragile ecosystem, strong industrial development demand and the concern of national ecological protection policies, LP has become an ideal area for studying the interaction between industry and ES. Theoretical mechanism and research framework

As the basic carrier of ES, the pattern and situation of land use is directly related to the output and efficiency of ES. Changes of land use patterns will directly interfere with the structure and function of the ecosystem, thereby affecting the supply capacity of ES. As shown in Fig. 2, industries in the socioeconomic system can be divided into primary industry, secondary industry and tertiary industry. The primary industry mainly consists of agriculture, forestry, animal husbandry and fishery, and the secondary industry mainly consists of industry and construction. The tertiary industry is mainly service. Industries will carry out spatial coordination and trade interaction between regions, resulting in competitive advantages and production path dependence, forming scale effects and cluster economies in a certain range. As the continuous development of industry, the demand for industrial land is growing. Primary industry mainly depends on agricultural land for production activities, while the secondary and tertiary industries mainly rely on urban land for production. The increase in agricultural land and urban land will lead to the occupation of natural land, which is the main sources of ES.Fig. 2Interactive mechanism of industry and ecosystem services.

Interactive mechanism of industry and ecosystem services.

Based on the above theoretical mechanism, this study puts forward the following three core hypotheses as the support for subsequent modeling and reasoning. Hypothesis 1: There is a simultaneous bidirectional causal relationship between industrial development and ecosystem services. Industrial expansion consumes natural resources and changes land cover, and the carrying capacity of ecosystem services in turn restricts the scale and spatial distribution of industries. Hypothesis 2: Industrial structure and ecosystem services do not exist in isolation. Local industrial activities and ecological changes will have significant spatial spillover effects on neighboring areas through regional economic networks and natural hydrological connectivity. Hypothesis 3: The impact of industrial development on ecosystem services is not entirely direct, but mainly indirectly realized through the change of physical landscape pattern, especially the fragmentation of landscape and the aggregation of impervious surface accompanied by urbanization.

The research framework of this paper is shown in Fig. 3, which mainly includes four steps: (1) Data preparation. The main purpose of data preparation in this paper is to guarantee the integrity and continuity of the data in time series and spatial distribution. (2) Model calculation. Input the processed data of climate, land, economy and vegetation into the model constructed in this paper, calculate ES supply capacity of ecosystem, the level of industrial development and spatial distribution of them, and further quantify the complex interaction between them. (3) Analysis and discussion. From the perspective of spatial correlation and spatial heterogeneity, combined with regional land use patterns, the spatial interaction between ES and industries was discussed. (4) Policy suggestion. Combined with the above calculation and analysis results, this paper puts forward the collaborative management strategy to promote sustainable development of industry and ecological protection.Fig. 3A research framework to identify the interaction between industry and ecosystem services.

Data and methods

Data requirement and preparation

The main purpose of this article is to quantify the spatial interaction between industrial structure and ES. In order to ensure the accuracy of quantitative analysis and consider the availability of data, we focus on Water Yield ecosystem service and Sediment Export ecosystem service in selecting ES. This choice is based on their key role in maintaining the ecological balance of LP and supporting regional industrial and economic activities 36,37. WY refers to the ability of the ecosystem to generate and transport water resources. It includes processes such as precipitation interception, storage, filtration and release. Through these processes, ecosystems can provide clean and available water for human beings 38. WY are especially important for areas that are extremely short of water, such as LP. Whether there is enough water consumption index is a key premise of local industrial layout and construction. SE usually refer to the ecosystem’s ability to regulate sediment at the basin level, including capturing, storing and releasing sediment 39. It will directly lead to soil erosion and soil erosion, increase the amount of sediment in rivers and lakes, pollute water bodies, and thus affecting the health and well-being of ecosystems and human society 40. On the basis of theoretical analysis and previous empirical research, after considering the reliability and accessibility of data, we selected the driving factors needed for this study from the four categories: socio-economy, climate, vegetation and landscape 41–44. The selection of driving factors and data sources are shown in Table 1. The landscape factors were calculated by Fragstats software. The landscape index is a highly centralized land use information index, which is widely used to analyze the impact of high-intensity human activities on ecology 45, changes in ES 46, water environmental protection 47 and agricultural planting risks 48. Ecosystem service assessment model

In this paper, WY and SE of LP are calculated by using the InVEST (Integrated Valuation of Ecosystem Services and Tradeoffs) assessment toolbox with Water Yield model and Sediment Delivery Ratio model respectively. The detailed description of this model can be found in Part 1 of SI. This toolbox can help decision makers to make more wise choices between ecological protection and resource development by quantitatively analyzing the relationship between land use, ecosystem health and economic benefits. It is widely used in the fields of sustainable management of ES, formulation of ecological protection policies and environmental impact assessment. The data format and sources required for calculating ES are shown in Table S1 in SI (Part3).

It is very important to calibrate the results of InVEST, but it is difficult to obtain complete field sampling data because of the large overall range of LP. Therefore, we refer to other scholars’ practices, and compare the calculated results of the model with the published data in the Yellow River Basin Water Resources Bulletin on the premise of ensuring the reliable data source of the calculation model of ES concerned in this paper, so as to ensure the rationality and reliability of the calculated results of the model 49,50. The calibration results are shown in Table S2 of SI (Part 3). The above calibration method ensures the reliability of the calculation results of the total ES in the basin. Furthermore, we also spatially compared the calculation results of this article with other similar studies to ensure the reliability of the conclusions we obtained in space 42,51–53.

Industrial structure measurement model

A reasonable industrial structure can optimize the efficiency of resource allocation and utilization, improve the quality and efficiency of economic growth, and thus realize long-term sustainable economic development 54. Meanwhile, a healthy industrial structure can reduce negative impacts on the environment and protect the ecosystem 55. Changes of regional industrial structure is determined by many conditions, involving many aspects such as society, economy, technology and environment. The importance of these aspects will be different in different areas and time periods. Based on this, this paper uses the advanced index and rationalization index of industrial structure to quantify the development level of industrial structure. We can further observe the evolution of industrial structure through these two indicators.

Industrial structure rationalization index assessment

Industrial structure rationalization index describes the degree of coordination of inter-industrial relations and the realization of rational allocation of production factors. Its essence is the reasonable proportion of input and output of various industry. Industrial structure advanced index assessment

Industrial structure advanced is also called industrial structure upgrade. According to the evolution law of industrial structure, so-called advanced industrial structure refers to the continuous evolution of industrial structure from low to high, and the gradual transfer of primary industry to secondary industry and tertiary industry. It is a dynamic process of gradually shifting from labor-intensive industries to capital-intensive and knowledge-intensive industries. In today’s highly information-based era, the service industry is a typical high value-added industry, and its economic growth rate is obviously higher than that of the primary and secondary industries. Therefore, scholars often measure the advanced degree of industrial structure by the proportion of the service industry in all industries 59–61. Spatial simultaneous equations modeling technique

As mentioned above, there are complex interaction between industry and ES, which are interdependent and mutually causal. Simultaneous equations model (SEM) is usually very suitable for this kind of problems, and is widely used in research on macro and micro economics 66, the water-energy-food relationship 67, resource utilization efficiency 68 and policy impact 69. SEM consists of a set of equations, in which the variables are interdependent rather than independent, and one or more dependent variables in one equation also act as independent variables in another equation. Although SEM has many applications, it ignores the interference caused by the spatial positions difference of the research variables on the existing model relations, which can lead to misjudgments of the research problems 65,70,71. Aiming at the research problems in this paper, the traditional SEM or spatial econometric model is not enough, because it cannot capture the two-way causal relationship between industrial expansion and ecological degradation. By using spatial simultaneous equation model (SSEM) and generalized space three-stage least squares estimator, we can effectively separate this synchronous feedback loop. Specifically, the spatial lag term naturally acts as an instrumental variable, which clearly solves the problem of endogenous deviation caused by the lack of variables and reverse causality in the industrial-ecological relationship. Based on this, this paper adopts the modeling technology of SSEM to improve this kind of problem. Compared to SEM, SSEM introduces spatial lag factors of the research variables to represent the influence of the research variables in the neighboring areas on the observation area, which effectively solves the spatial autocorrelation phenomenon of research variables, can more accurately reflect the state of research variables in the real world, and improves the prediction accuracy. Spatio-temporal evolution of ecosystem services

Since the reform and opening up in 1978, China has continuously deepened its economic reforms, reduced government intervention and increased its dependence on market mechanisms, thus creating favorable conditions for economic growth78. During this period, China gradually realized the transformation from an agriculture-based economy to an industry-based economy, and then vigorously developed the service industry and gradually increasing the added value of industries. As shown in Fig. 4, from 2000 to 2019, the per capita gross domestic product (GDP) and the added value of various industries in China showed a significant growth trend. After 2000, the per capita GDP and the added value of various industries in China showed exponential growth, with the growth of the tertiary industry being the most significant. With the rapid growth of GDP, the industrial structure of China has also shown an obvious changing trend. The proportion of the primary industry is decreasing year by year, the secondary industry remains basically stable with a slight downward trend, and the proportion of the tertiary industry is increasing year by year.Fig. 4Spatial distribution pattern of ecosystem services in Loess Plateau during 2000–2019.

Spatial distribution pattern of ecosystem services in Loess Plateau during 2000–2019.

As the evolution of industrial structure, ES of LP also show obvious spatial heterogeneity. Regionally, the spatial distribution pattern of WY in 2000 showed the characteristics of high-value aggregation in the south and low-value aggregation in the north. With the passage of time, the low-value areas in the north also have a certain degree of high-value aggregation, which shows that the water supply capacity of LP is improving year by year, and the problem of water scarcity has been alleviated to some extent. SE and WY are similar in time and space. SE also presents a spatial distribution pattern of low-value accumulation in the north and high-value accumulation in the south, which is relatively stable and has little change from year to year. ES on LP have distinct spatial heterogeneity, and their uneven spatial distribution will significantly impact the assurance of resource supply, thereby affecting the well-being of the socio-economic system.

According to Fig. 5a and b, the global Moran’s I of ES in LP has been maintained at a high level from 2000 to 2019, indicating that both WY and SE in LP show significant spatial autocorrelation, showing high and low values clusters. The spatial autocorrelation of WY is more significant than that of SE. On the whole, between 2000 to 2019 (except from 2005 to 2010), WY and SE show a certain degree of synergy. The actual value of WY and the degree of spatial autocorrelation (except from 2005 to 2010) show an overall collaborative trend, indicating that the spatial agglomeration of WY is conducive to enhancing its own supply capacity. In contrast, the actual value of SE and the degree of spatial autocorrelation overall present a trade-off trend (except from 2005 to 2010), suggesting that the spatial agglomeration of SE will suppress its service supply capacity. In order to ensure the reliability of the research conclusion, Monte-Carlo (MC) simulation is used to verify that the spatial correlation of ES is not random. Specifically, this method assumes that ES in the study area has no spatial correlation (zero hypothesis), using the data of 2019 as input, and randomly rearranging the spatial position of the research area for 999 times. For each rearrangement, the global Moran’s I of ES is calculated, and the spatially correlated frequency is observed. As shown in Fig. 5c and d, it can be observed that the global Moran’s I for WY and SE presents a normal distribution trend in 999 MC simulations, and the probability of the global Moran’s I based on the actual calculated data for WY and SE is very low. Therefore, the zero hypothesis can be rejected, which further indicating that there is a significant spatial correlation of ES in LP.Fig. 5Time series evolution and Monte Carlo simulation of Moran’s I index of ecosystem services in Loess Plateau.

Time series evolution and Monte Carlo simulation of Moran’s I index of ecosystem services in Loess Plateau.

In addition, the results of SSEM provide key quantitative evidence for the “green paradox” in the development of regional industries. It is generally believed that upgrading of industrial structure is to transfer to the tertiary industry with less direct pollution. However, as confirmed in “Spatio-temporal evolution of industrial structure and land use change” section, the projection of advanced physical space on the loess plateau is the rapid expansion of urban construction land and the crowding out of ecological space. As can be seen from Table 3, the primary industry and secondary industry have little direct positive impact on water production. However, landscape fragmentation and urban patch aggregation driven by industrial upgrading have an overwhelming impact on ecosystem services. This result quantitatively verifies the “green paradox”, that is, in ecologically fragile areas, the simple upgrading of industrial structure does not automatically mean environmental friendliness. On the contrary, the urbanization process, accompanied by upgrading, indirectly but significantly increases WY by changing the physical coverage of the surface, and greatly intensifies SE, thus leading to the decline of the overall ecological stability.

In terms of rationalization of industrial structure and spatial spillover effect, SSEM uniquely quantifies the cross-regional ecological restraint mechanism driven by industrial spatial layout. The core of industrial rationalization is the spatial optimal allocation of production factors and industries. When the rationalization process promotes the formation of efficient industrial spatial cluster, it will inevitably lead to the overflow of ecosystem service space caused by resource siphon and emission transfer. More importantly, the model reveals a two-way spatial trade-off mechanism, that is, the increase of local WY caused by the industrial expansion of local cities not only significantly inhibits the local se, but also inhibits the SE in neighboring areas. On the contrary, the increase of se in neighboring areas will weaken the local WY. This shows that the highly rationalized industrial structure not only pursues the minimization of local ecological damage, but realizes the ecological balance of the whole region by conforming to the above-mentioned spatial spillover and trade-off mechanism. Industrial rationalization can limit the serious ecological interference to local areas by rationally allocating industries with high water consumption or high construction intensity in node areas with high environmental carrying capacity, and at the same time create an ecological dividend for neighboring areas by using spatial trade-off effect.

Solving endogenous problems is the key prerequisite of causal reasoning in this study. The two-way feedback between industrial scale and ecosystem services produces simultaneous equation deviation. By using the GS3SLS estimator, this study solved this endogenous problem from the mathematical mechanism. GS3SLS estimates endogenous variables by taking all exogenous variables and their spatial lag terms in the whole system as instrumental variables, which fundamentally eliminates the estimation bias brought by reverse causality. In addition, in order to ensure the robustness of causal reasoning, we have conducted a comprehensive model setting test. To ensure the reliability of the research results, three indicators were used to measure the reliability of SSEM constructed in this paper: Berndt, McElroy, and Judge. Berndt index is a gradient-based optimization algorithm, which is used to test the effectiveness of parameter estimation in a model and check the specification errors 79. McElroy index is used to evaluate the characteristics of the time series error in a model, and to test whether there is cross-sectional correlation in panel data. If the residual is self-correlated, it shows that the model does not fully captured the dynamic characteristics of the data, which may lead to estimation deviation 80. Similar to the McElroy index, Judge index is also used to evaluate the endogeneity of models, but it focuses on the structure and overall fitting degree of the model. It can help researchers understand whether the overall structure of the model is correct, whether the model accurately captures the relationships between variables, and it can be used to evaluate the efficiency of the model 81. Discussion

Spatio-temporal heterogeneity effect of ecosystem services under rapid economic development

In the background of rapid economic development, ES show great variability and imbalance on different time and space scales. The influence of industries on ES has different characteristics in different stages of economic development, different time intervals and different geographical areas. As shown in Fig. 4 and Fig. 6, the industrial and ES of LP exhibit characteristics of spatial agglomeration across different temporal cross-sections, and this phenomenon of spatial agglomeration may be influenced by multiple factors. First of all, the central and southern regions are relatively rich in water resources, fertile soil and suitable climatic conditions, which are very important for the development of agriculture and other industries. Secondly, these areas have more developed infrastructure and transportation networks, which is conducive to industrial agglomeration and rapid development of regional economy. In addition, these spatial agglomeration areas are located in the middle and lower reaches of the Yellow River Basin, which is a key area for the economic development strategy of this basin, and have two main national comprehensive energy bases: the Ordos Basin Base and Shaanxi Base 82. Rapid economic development may exacerbate resource consumption and environmental pressure, especially in the water-scarce and ecologically fragile areas of LP 83. In contrast, low-value areas, due to relatively lagging economic development, may face more severe issues such as poverty and ecological degradation.

The change of spatial aggregation mode of ES is highly related to the evolution of industrial development stages. Between 2000 to 2019, as the continuous expansion of the industrial economy, the proportion of the tertiary industry gradually increased, while the proportions of the primary and secondary industries gradually declined. The growth of tertiary industry also means the increase in urban areas, that is, an increase of impervious land. The increase in impervious surface significantly reduces rainwater infiltration, thus increasing surface runoff to some extent, leading to an increase in WY. From the perspective of the whole LP, WY and SE show a trend of spatial synergy. This is because the increase of WY intensifies the scouring effect of surface runoff on the soil, making it easier for sediment and nutrients to be transported, leading to the increase in SE1,84. With the growth and proportion of the tertiary industry, the spatial synergy trend between WY and SE is gradually increasing. Studying the interaction between industry and ES from the perspective of temporal and spatial heterogeneity will help us to understand and predict the impact of economic activities on the natural environment more deeply, thus providing a scientific basis for ecological protection and sustainable development strategies.

Mechanism of interaction between industrial structure and ecosystem services

The interaction mechanism between industrial structure and ES is a complex system process, involving economic development, ecological environment changes, resource utilization and many other aspects 85. Changes in industrial structure can directly or indirectly affect the supply capacity of ES, which in turn will have a series of feedback effects on the development of industrial structure. The rapid development of industries will promote the evolution of industrial structures 86. Due to the uneven distribution of regional resource endowments, different policy support and changeable market demands, the comprehensive effects of these three factors lead to comparative advantages of specific industries across different regions 87. In addition, this leads to the heterogeneous spatial distribution of industries, which is manifested in some areas becoming agglomeration centers for specific industries, while the same industries in other areas are backward in scale and technology.

The socioeconomic system is a complex giant system with people as the center, and regional climate conditions and land endowments provide all kinds of necessary resources for production. Industries, as the main carriers of human production and life, are closely related to the rationality of their structure and the stability of socioeconomic system. They will also produce a lot of greenhouse gas emissions during the production process, and change the regional land use patterns, thus affecting the supply capacity of ES. The expansion of the secondary and tertiary industries will exacerbate the urban heat island effect, leading to increased local temperatures, which in turn threatens the reduction of species numbers, decreased biodiversity, and diminished stability of ecosystems 88. The growth of the primary industry means the increase in arable land, and the farming process can lead to the loosening of soil structure and the decline of soil moisture retention capacity 89. When precipitation increases, the surface soil is easily eroded, which leads to the increase of SE. From the perspective of local areas within LP, the increase in WY in neighboring areas will increase the supply of local WY, while reducing SE. This is mainly because the hydrological connectivity of LP has been significantly improved in recent years, and the distribution of water resources in some areas has become relatively uniform, thus allowing water resources to flow and exchange between neighboring areas through the surface water network 90,91. The improvement of the water network promotes the restoration of regional wetlands and floodplains, increased the ability of river banks to retain soil, and reduced the downstream flow of sediments, thus reducing SE 92. The increase of SE in neighboring areas will correspondingly increase local SE and reduce WY. The increase of SE in neighboring areas usually indicates that local areas have stronger water flow and sediment transport capacity, which leads to more sediment being brought into the river 93. However, correspondingly, this increased SE may lead to competition with local water resources, which is manifested in the local potential demand for more water resources to cope with the increased sediment load, for example, dredging or increasing water flow to remove sediment 84. This excessive regulation of water flow may affect the replenishment and storage of water sources, reducing the local water resource supply, thereby diminishing WY.

Although the current SSEM mainly quantifies the driving force of socio-economic and landscape factors on ES and the interaction within ES in mathematical structure, this balance within the ecosystem essentially constitutes a profound reverse constraint on the future industrial structure evolution. The spatial limitation and spillover effect of WY and SE revealed by the model are actually the feedback signal of natural ecosystem to human economic activities. When the excessive pursuit of industrial upgrading in a certain region leads to the evolution of landscape pattern, which leads to the risk that the ecosystem is out of control, this severe ecological feedback will force macro-policies to intervene and force the industrial structure to be rationalized at a higher level. That is to say, the bottom line of ecosystem service carrying capacity determines the spatial boundary of the rational industrial layout, which is also the deep mechanism to realize real two-way mutual feedback between them.

Policy implications

Industrial development and ecological protection are interdependent and mutually restricted. Industrial development depends on a good ecological environment, and industrial production activities can have negative impacts on the ecological environment. In order to realize long-term and sustainable development, it is necessary to find a scientific balance between industrial development and ecological protection. Therefore, it is necessary to establish the principle of ecological priority, which means that in industrial development and spatial planning, any production activity should be based on maintaining the health of the ecosystem. At the same time, it is necessary to ensure the implementation of the principle of ecological priority through legislation, policy guidance and public propaganda campaigns.

Specifically, before implementing a new industrial project, a detailed ecological and environmental impact assessment must be carried out, including but not limited to assessing the impact of the industrial project on regional biodiversity, water supply, land use, air quality and other aspects. The assessment results are used to guide the design, implementation, and monitoring of the project, ensuring that the ecological damage caused by industrial development is minimized as much as possible. Encourage industries to adopt sustainable development modes, such as green energy, ecological agriculture and the use of recyclable materials. At the same time, promote traditional industries to transition to environmentally friendly practices by improving production processes, reducing waste emissions, and enhancing the efficiency of energy and resource use. Establish and improve the ecological compensation mechanism, rewarding enterprises that protect the ecological environment in their production activities, while imposing fines or other punitive measures on those that cause damage to the ecological environment. Encourage enterprises to pursue economic benefits while paying attention to achieving ecological protection goals. Promote coordination and cooperation among relevant government departments, integrate resources and information and jointly promote the balance between industrial development and ecological protection. At the same time, collaborate with research institutions and higher education institutions to introduce advanced scientific technologies and management experiences, while improving the overall efficiency and quality of both ecological protection and industrial development. Establish a long-term monitoring system for the ecological environment, regularly evaluate the impact of industrial development on the ecological environment, and timely adjust and optimize industrial development strategies and ecological protection measures through data collection and analysis.

Main limitations and future research

According to the characteristics of LP, this paper selected two representative ES, namely, WY and SE, to study the interaction between industry and ES. However, besides WY and SE, there are many other important ES, such as carbon sequestration, air purification, biodiversity protection, water purification and water source protection. These ES can affect or be affected by industrial development to varying degrees, and ES emphasized in different river basins and regions are different. In the future, we should consider more types of ES, focus on the characteristics of different river basins and regions, and conduct a more comprehensive study on the complex coupling relationships between industry and ES to enhance the universal applicability of the research framework proposed in this paper. Finally, this study focuses on the undisturbed macro cycle from 2000 to 2019, in order to extract a stable causal mechanism and deliberately excluding the exogenous structural distortions caused by the global epidemic after 2020. Future studies should further explore the interaction between industry and ecology in the recovery period after the epidemic.

Conclusion

Based on the characteristics of LP, using the panel data from 2000 to 2019, this paper quantifies the complex feedback relationship between industry and ES by using spatial simultaneous cubic modeling technology, and puts forward corresponding policy suggestions. First, the InVEST model is used to calculate the typical ES of LP and analyze their spatial evolution trends at different stages of industrial development. Secondly, combined with the trends of land use change, further analyze the impact of industrial development on ecosystems. Finally, based on SSEM, the complex inter-feedback relationships among industry, ES, and driving factors are quantitatively assessed, revealing the underlying interactive response mechanisms. This study provides a new research framework for solving the trade-off between high-quality industrial development and ecological protection, and its conclusions may also provide references for other river basins.

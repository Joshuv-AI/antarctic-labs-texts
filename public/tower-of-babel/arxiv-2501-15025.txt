Abstract
Given a graph G G , a set is Δ \Delta convex if there is no vertex u ∈ V ⁡ ( G ) ∖ S u\in V(G)\setminus S that forms a triangle with two vertices of S S . The Δ \Delta -convex hull of S S is the minimum Δ \Delta -convex set containing S S . This article is an attempt to discuss the Carathéodory number and exchange number on various graph families and standard graph products namely Cartesian, strong and lexicographic products of graphs.

Theorem 2

In the next result, we present some properties of the Carathéodory independent set of a general graph, which will be used in subsequent results.

Lemma 5
.

If

is a Carathéodory independent set of

|S|\geq 2
, then

(i)

(ii)

Proof.

(i)

If

\langle S\rangle=\langle S\setminus\{v\}\rangle\cup\{v\}
. Hence,
\langle S\rangle=\langle S\setminus\{v\}\rangle\cup\langle S\setminus\{u\}\rangle

is a Carathéodory dependent set.

(ii)

Let

be a

-independent set. Assume that the induced subgraph of

does not contain any edges; that is,

contains pairwise non-adjacent vertices. Hence,
\langle S\rangle=S
, and for any

\langle S\setminus\{u\}\rangle=S\setminus\{u\}
. This implies that

is a

-dependent set, a contradiction. Therefore, the induced subgraph of

must contain at least one edge.

(iii)

Let

and assume that

. By property (ii), since

contains only one edge, the induced subgraph of

will not contain an edge. Therefore,
\langle S\setminus\{u,v\}\rangle=S\setminus\{u,v\}
.
Now, since

and by property (i) there exists at least one vertex

such that

. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\langle S\setminus\{u,v\}\rangle\cap\langle u,v\rangle\neq\emptyset

. But this is not possible, since

we have

.

It remains to prove it for

where

. Since

we have
\langle u,v\rangle=\{u,v\}
. If
\langle S\setminus\{u,v\}\rangle\cap\langle u,v\rangle\neq\emptyset

S
a Carathéodory independent set then for any vertex

To prove that the Carathéodory number of a 2-connected chordal graph is two, we need the following result.

Theorem 7

If

Proof.

Let

with

. By Lemma

, we have
\langle S\rangle=V(G)
. Since
\langle S\rangle\not\subseteq\langle S\setminus u\rangle\bigcup\langle S\setminus v\rangle

Case 2:

Lemma 9

If

\displaystyle p^{\prime}\in\langle S\setminus\{p\}\rangle\setminus\bigcup_{a\in S\setminus\{p\}}\langle S\setminus\{a\}\rangle

\langle S\setminus\{p\}\rangle=S\setminus\{p\}
and
\langle S\setminus\{a\}\rangle=S\setminus\{a\}

(iii)

4.1
Exchange number

If a graph contains no triangles, then the only E-independent sets are the two element sets. In this case exchange number is

G
and

Theorem 17
.

Let

and

Proof.

Let

\displaystyle y\notin\bigcup_{b\in S_{2}\setminus\{h\}}\langle S_{2}\setminus\{b\}\rangle

h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},\ldots,h_{r}\in V(P_{n})
.
Since
P_{n}
does not contains
K_{3}
, any
U\subseteq P_{n}
,
\langle U\rangle=U
. Then
\langle S\rangle=\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{1}})\rangle\cup\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{2}})\rangle\cup\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{3}})\rangle\cup\ldots\cup\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{r}})\rangle
.

Let
(g,h_{i})\in S
,
i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
. For
j\neq i
(
j=1,2,\ldots,r
) there is some
g^{\prime}\in V(G)
with
(g^{\prime},h_{j})\in S
. Then
\langle S\setminus\{(g^{\prime},h_{j})\}\rangle=\bigcup_{k\neq j}\langle S\cap G^{h_{k}}\rangle\cup\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{j}})\setminus\{(g^{\prime},h_{j})\}\rangle
. Since
c_{\Delta}(G)\geq 4
,
S
contains at least four vertices. So we can find some
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in S
different from
(g,h_{i})
and
(g^{\prime},h_{j})
.

Subcase 1:

r=2
.

In this case
S
intersects with only two
G
layers. If
S
contains at least two vertices each from the intersecting two
G
layers, then
\langle S\setminus\{(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}\rangle
contains
\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{j}})\rangle
. If one intersecting
G
layer contains only one vertex of
S
, then the other
G
layer contains
c_{\Delta}(G)
number of vertices. Now also
\langle S\setminus\{(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}\rangle
contains
\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{j}})\rangle
.

Subcase 2:

r\geq 2

In this case we can find some
(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\in S
with
h^{\prime}\neq h_{j}
. Then
\langle S\setminus\{(g^{\prime},h^{\prime})\}\rangle
contains
\langle S\cap V(G^{h_{j}})\rangle

From the above cases, we can conclude that for any
h_{i}\in V(P_{n}),i\in\{1,2,\ldots,r\}
,
\displaystyle\langle S\rangle\subseteq\bigcup_{(a,b)\in S\setminus\{(g,h)\}}\langle S\setminus\{(a,b)\}\rangle
. Therefore,
S
is an
E
-dependent set in
G\Box P_{n}
.

Hence
c_{\Delta}(G\Box P_{n})=c_{\Delta}(G)
.

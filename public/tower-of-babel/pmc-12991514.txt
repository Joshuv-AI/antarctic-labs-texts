A Comparative Analysis of Complete Coverage Tooth Preparation Design and Scan Angulation on the Accuracy of Two Different Intra-Oral Digital Scanners: A 3-Dimensional Study
Authors: Ramya B. Kabbin, Arvind I. Moldi, Chaitra S. Bulla, Priyanka K. Eklarkar, Sourabh S. Patil, Priya S. Biradar
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackground and Objective:Intraoral scanners have revolutionized dental practice, but their accuracy may depend on preparation design and scan angulation. This study assessed how full-coverage preparation and scan angulation affect the accuracy of two scanners (TRIOS and Carestream 3600) for single restorations, both with and without adjacent teeth.Materials and Methods:A maxillary typodont with six full-coverage preparations was scanned using TRIOS and Carestream 3600. Each was scanned twice: with adjacent teeth (STL A) and without (STL B). Data were analyzed using SPSS v21 with independent t-tests and between-subjects analysis (P < 0.05).Results:Carestream showed significantly lower discrepancies than TRIOS with adjacent teeth for AAD (2.1417 vs. 2.7567; P = 0.023) and MAD (8.2009 vs. 8.7033; P = 0.041), and without adjacent teeth for AAD (1.6178 vs. 1.9783; P = 0.036) and MAD (7.6030 vs. 8.2059; P = 0.017).Conclusion:Carestream demonstrated consistently higher accuracy than TRIOS, regardless of adjacent teeth, confirming its reliability for digital impressions in varied clinical situations.

INTRODUCTION

Digital dentistry has quickly enhanced restorative care, improving precision, efficiency, and patient satisfaction. Intraoral scanners are now key tools for capturing accurate digital impressions for various procedures, including crowns, bridges, implants, and orthodontics.[12] Scanner accuracy is influenced by tooth preparation design, scan angulation, and technology.[34] Preparation design impacts restoration fit and longevity, especially in complex areas.[56] Scan angulation affects impression accuracy, with incorrect angles causing distortions.[47] Adjacent teeth add scanning complexity and error risk.[6]

High-accuracy scanners like 3Shape TRIOS and Carestream CS 3600 use advanced optics but vary by clinical scenario and technique.[123] This study compares their accuracy for complete-coverage restorations with and without adjacent teeth, testing for significant differences between scanners and scanning conditions.

MATERIALS AND METHODS

Materials

Maxillary typodont (iDENTical Nissin type jaw set), NSK Airotor handpiece with Mani diamond burs, Medit T310 3D scanner, intraoral scanners (3Shape TRIOS and Carestream 3600 v3.1.0), ELEGOO Saturn 3 Ultra 3D printer, and CloudCompare v2.13.2 software.

Study Design

An in vitro study approved by the institutional ethics committee assessed the accuracy (trueness and precision) of TRIOS and Carestream 3600 scanners in digitizing full-coverage crown preparations on a maxillary typodont, with and without adjacent teeth. A schematic representation of study design as shown in Figure 1. Six teeth were prepared for full-coverage metal-ceramic crowns by a single operator following Goodacre et al. (1.5–2.0 mm occlusal reduction, 1.2 mm shoulder, 0.5–1.0 mm chamfer, 10–20° taper).

Schemmatic representation of study design

Digital Workflow

The typodont was scanned using the Medit T310 to create STL files with and without adjacent teeth. Models were 3D printed and rescanned as references. Each was scanned 12 times with TRIOS and Carestream, trimmed, and superimposed in CloudCompare to measure deviations [Figure 2]. Euclidean distances calculated average and maximum deviations. Directional biases were analyzed, and statistical tests (t-tests and ANOVA) assessed group differences using IBM SPSS (P < 0.05).

DISCUSSION

This study found both 3Shape TRIOS and Carestream CS 3600 scanners produced clinically acceptable digital impressions; however, Carestream was slightly more accurate, especially in the presence of adjacent teeth. Adjacent teeth increased scanning difficulty, echoing previous findings. Carestream’s structured light technology may help capture complex anatomy more effectively than TRIOS’s confocal microscopy. Statistically significant accuracy differences between scanners and the influence of adjacent teeth led to rejection of both null hypotheses. While both are suitable for restorative work, Carestream may be preferred for complex cases, though differences are often minor. Standardized in vitro models strengthen the findings, but future studies in clinical settings with more scanners are recommended.

CONCLUSION

The Carestream CS 3600 intraoral scanner demonstrated superior accuracy compared to the 3Shape TRIOS for complete-coverage single restorations, both with and without adjacent teeth. The presence of adjacent teeth affected scanning accuracy, but Carestream consistently outperformed TRIOS in all scenarios. These results highlight the importance of scanner selection and technique in achieving accurate digital impressions for successful dental restorations. Further clinical studies are recommended to confirm these findings and evaluate scanner performance in more complex restorative cases.

Authors: Hendrik Ruge, Markus Krane
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Corresponding AuthorKey words: cardiovascular disease, complication, right-sided catheterization, tricuspid valve, valve replacement

In the United States, devices for transcatheter edge-to-edge repair (TEER) and transcatheter tricuspid valve replacement (TTVR) have received Food & Drug Administration approval. Consequently, more interventional therapy options allow tailoring strategies for patients experiencing severe tricuspid regurgitation (TR). Guiding patients to TEER or TTVR is predominantly based on transesophageal echocardiography (TEE) assessing TR severity and tricuspid valve morphology.1 In more complex valve morphologies, suboptimal TR reduction and poorer clinical outcomes2,3 have been reported. TTVR has been shown to achieve predictable and durable TR elimination in the prospective TRISCEND I and II trials.4,5 Impressive clinical use of TTVR has been reported from the Society of Thoracic Surgeons registry, with >1,000 cases in the U.S. within 12 months (Sharma RP. Evaluating real-world outcomes of TTVR with the Evoque system. Presented at 2025 TCT, October 26, 2025).

Nonelective tricuspid valve reintervention at 30 days is reported to be 2.3% in TRISCEND I and 2.0% in TRISCEND II.4,5 Overall, the indication for early TTVR tricuspid reintervention seems to be rare. In this issue of JACC: Case Reports, Johnson et al6 present a step-by-step guidance on surgical explantation of a migrated or embolized transcatheter tricuspid heart valve.

Procedural Steps and Pitfalls of Evoque Tricuspid Valve Implantation

Evaluation of TTVR eligibility is based on TEE and 4-dimensional contrast-enhanced, electrocardiography-gated computed tomography. Essential assessments are tricuspid annular dimensions to allow prosthetic valve oversizing in relation to native annulus measures and leaflet length to allow the 9 Evoque anchors engaging the leaflets against the stent frame body. Evoque implantation is guided by fluoroscopy and TEE. Multiplanar reconstruction of the tricuspid annulus is used for optimizing catheter position during stepwise valve expansion, ensuring leaflet engagement in all 9 anchors and valve position in relation to the tricuspid annulus. Shadowing from the catheter or previously implanted valves can impede echo image quality and challenge valve implantation. Factors potentially leading to valve migration, embolization, or significant paravalvular regurgitation requiring valve explantation are malalignment of prosthetic valve body to the native tricuspid annulus, failing engagement of the tricuspid valve leaflet in consecutive anchors, and off-axis valve implantation.

Surgical Aspects of Evoque Explantation

Patients presenting for TTVR are likely to have had previous sternotomy. Previous coronary artery bypass grafting was reported in up to 20% and valve surgery in 34% of patients in TRISCEND I and TRISCEND II.4,5 The Evoque stent frame diameter measures 44 to 56 mm and equals the labeled valve size. The 9 anchors increase the total diameter by 6 mm. Johnson et al6 identified key aspects of tricuspid valve surgery for malpositioned Evoque valves:

•Stabilization of the valve delivery catheter at the groin during transport from the catheterization laboratory to the operating room.

The cardiothoracic community must recognize the expanding toolbox in structural heart interventions. Cardiac surgeons need to be active heart team members, allowing in-depth understanding of the interaction and anchoring mechanism of implantable devices with the intracardiac anatomy, which will be essentially helpful when surgical explantation is required.

The Bernstein problem for Sobolev intrinsic graphs in the Heisenberg group
Sebastiano Nicolussi Golo, Francesco Serra Cassano, Mattia Vedovato

Abstract
. In the first Heisenberg group, we study entire, locally Sobolev intrinsic graphs that are stable for the sub-Riemannian area. We show that, under appropriate integrability conditions for the derivatives, the intrinsic graph must be an intrinsic plane, i.e., a coset of a two dimensional subgroup. This result extends [ 16 ] beyond the Lipschitz class.

1.
Introduction

This paper is an improvement of the results from
[
16
]
:
in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
,
we extend the Bernstein problem from the Lipschitz to the Sobolev class.
We refer to
[
16
]
for a broader introduction of the subject and to
[
19
]
for a general account of the Bernstein problem in Heisenberg groups.
For recent results in the study of minimal surfaces in the sub-Riemannian first Heisenberg see
[
20
,
13
]
.
There has been progress also in the sub-Finsler first Heisenberg group,
see
[
11
,
10
,
8
,
17
]
,
and in higher Heisenberg groups, see
[
9
]
.

For
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open and
f:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
of class
W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
,
we consider the area functional

\mathscr{A}_{f}(K)=\int_{K}\sqrt{1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{2},

where
K\subset\omega
is compact and
\nabla^{f}f=\partial_{\eta}f+f\partial_{\tau}f
,
with
(\eta,\tau)
coordinates of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Let us recall that
\mathscr{A}_{f}(\omega)
represents the sub-Riemannian area of the intrinsic graph induced by
f
; see Section
2
below.
We can state the Bernstein problem for intrinsic graphs in the first Heisenberg group
as to characterize
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
that minimizes
\mathscr{A}_{f}(K)
for all
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
In other words, we look for functions
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
so that, for every
K\Subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, if
f=g
on
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus K
, then
\mathscr{A}_{f}(K)\leq\mathscr{A}_{g}(K)
.

We say that
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
is
stationary
if, for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\epsilon}|_{\epsilon=0}\mathscr{A}_{f+\epsilon\phi}(\mathrm{spt}(\phi))=0.

A function
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
is
stable
if it is stationary and, for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
,

\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}|_{\epsilon=0}\mathscr{A}_{f+\epsilon\phi}(\mathrm{spt}(\phi))\geq 0.

In
[
16
]
, the first two named authors proved that the only stable locally Lipschitz functions
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
are
those of the form

f(\eta,\tau)=a\eta+b

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
.
These functions are characterized as those whose intrinsic graph in the Heisenberg group is a vertical plane:
see Remark
2.1
below.

The same conclusion was known for
f
of class
C^{2}
after
[
3
]
, and for
f
of class
C^{1}
after
[
7
]
.

In the present paper we weaken the hypothesis on
f
to be of class
W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}
.
We need
q
to be larger than 4 and we also need an additional exponential integrability of the derivative
\partial_{\tau}f
.
Under these hypothesis, we can obtain the same conclusion using the strategy of
[
16
]
and
[
3
]
, thanks to the existence and regularity of Lagrangian coordinates proved in
[
1
]
.

Our main result is the following theorem:

Theorem A

(Bernstein problem with Sobolev regularity)
.

Suppose
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
q>4
,
and

(1)

\exists\kappa>0\text{ such that }\exp(\kappa|\partial_{\tau}f|)\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2}).

If
f
is stable, then there are
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
such that
f(\eta,\tau)=a\eta+b
,
for each
(\eta,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}

The proof of Theorem
A
is at the end of Section
5
.

In sections 7 and 8 of
[
16
]
, two examples of stable intrinsic graphs were presented.
Those two examples do not satisfy the hypothesis of Theorem
A
because they are graphs of functions
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
p=2
but not
p=4
.
Those examples are now known not to be perimeter minimizers, as shown in
[
20
, Theorem 1.3]
.

In the proof of Theorem
A
, we show that the intrinsic graph of
f
is ruled by horizontal lines.
We then prove that the graph of
f
is a vertical plane using the stability of
f
, not its minimality.
This is different from
[
20
, Theorem 1.3]
, where it has been proved that as soon as the intrinsic graph
\Gamma_{f}
of
f
is area minimizing and ruled by horizontal straight lines (i.e., isometric embeddings of
\mathbb{R}
), then
\Gamma_{f}
is a vertical plane.
Minimality clearly implies stability, but the converse may not hold.
Indeed, there are examples of stable intrinsic graphs in the first Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
which are not area-minimizing;
see
[
16
, Theorem 7.1]
and
[
20
, Theorem 1.1]
.

Theorem
A
leaves open the discussion whether (
1
) and the lower bound “
q>4
” are necessary to show that stability implies affinity of
f
.
Our main reason to have (
1
) is to use the tools provided by
[
1
,
2
]
.
From those papers, we actually get uniqueness of the horizontal lines ruling the intrinsic graph of
f
.
More precisely, if
f
satisfies the hypothesis of Theorem
A
and
\Gamma_{f}
is the intrinsic graph of
f
, we obtain from
[
1
]
that, for every point
p\in\Gamma_{f}
, there exists a unique horizontal curve in
\Gamma_{f}
passing through
p
;
see Theorem
3.9
below and its reference.
Such uniqueness does not seem to be crucial in the proof of Theorem
A
;
see for instance
[
12
, Remark 3.7]
for an example with non-uniqueness of the horizontal curves.

We remark that having the intrinsic graph ruled by horizontal lines is not equivalent to being minimal.
On the one hand, there are minimal graphs that are not ruled by horizontal lines, as shown in
[
3
]
, see also
[
18
]
.
On the other hand, there are graphs ruled by horizontal lines that are not minimal, as for instance the examples in
[
16
]
, or every “graphical strip” as in
[
4
]
.

It is an open question: Is it true that, if
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is of class
C^{1}_{\mathbb{W}}
, that is, if both
f
and
\nabla^{f}f
are continuous, and if the intrinsic graph of
f
minimizes the sub-Riemannian area, then the intrinsic graph of
f
is ruled by horizontal lines?

Finally, we remind the reader that for higher Heisenberg groups, very little is known, even for smooth intrinsic graphs; see
[
19
]
and references therein.

Plan of the paper

In the preliminary section
2
, we introduce the main concepts and notations.
Section
3
focuses on Bi-Sobolev Lagrangian homeomorphisms, which is a way to describe intrinsic graphs in the Heisenberg group in terms of their horizontal curves.
In Section
4
we show the consequences of the first variation.
Section
5
focuses on consequences of the second variation.
Finally, we will present a few examples in Section
6
.
We have added a short Appendix
A
where we describe the relations between several conditions on the exponents of integrability that appear along the paper.

2.
Preliminaries and notation

The Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}
is represented in this paper as
\mathbb{R}^{3}
endowed with the group operation

(x,y,z)\cdot(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},z^{\prime})=\left(x+x^{\prime},y+y^{\prime},z+z^{\prime}+\frac{1}{2}(xy^{\prime}-x^{\prime}y)\right).

In these coordinates, an orthonormal frame of the horizontal distribution is

X=\partial_{x}-\frac{y}{2}\partial_{z},\qquad Y=\partial_{y}+\frac{x}{2}\partial_{z}.

The
sub-Riemannian perimeter
of a measurable set
G\subset\mathbb{H}
in an open set
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
is

P_{sR}(G;\Omega)=\sup\left\{\int_{G}(X\psi_{1}+Y\psi_{2})\,\mathrm{d}\cal L^{3}:\psi_{1},\psi_{2}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega),\ \psi_{1}^{2}+\psi_{2}^{2}\leq 1\right\},

where
X\psi_{1}+Y\psi_{2}
is the divergence of the vector field
\psi_{1}X+\psi_{2}Y
.
A set
G
is a
perimeter minimizer
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
if for every
F\subset\mathbb{H}
measurable with
(G\setminus F)\cup(F\setminus G)\Subset\Omega
, it holds
P_{sR}(G;\Omega)\leq P_{sR}(F;\Omega)
.
A set
G
is a
local perimeter minimizer
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{H}
if every
p\in\Omega
has a neighborhood
\Omega^{\prime}\subset\Omega
such that
G
is a perimeter minimizer in
\Omega^{\prime}
.

Given a function
f:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
,
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
, its intrinsic graph
\Gamma_{f}\subset\mathbb{H}
is the set of points

(0,\eta,\tau)(f(\eta,\tau),0,0)=\left(f(\eta,\tau),\eta,\tau-\frac{1}{2}\eta f(\eta,\tau)\right),

for
(\eta,\tau)\in\omega
.
The
intrinsic gradient
of
f
is
\nabla^{f}f
, where
\nabla^{f}=\partial_{\eta}+f\partial_{\tau}
is a vector field on
\omega
.
The function
\nabla^{f}f:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
is well defined when
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)\cap C^{0}(\omega)
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
f
is of the form
f(\eta,\tau)=a\eta+b

for some
a,b\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\Gamma_{f}

\displaystyle=\left\{\left(a\eta+b,\eta,\tau-a\eta^{2}/2-b\eta/2\right):\ \eta,\tau\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=(b,0,0)\cdot\left\{\left(a\eta,\eta,z\right):\ \eta,z\in\mathbb{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=\{(x,y,z)\in\mathbb{R}^{3}:x=ay+b\}

is a vertical plane.

The
graph area functional
is defined, for every
E\subset\omega
measurable, by

\mathscr{A}_{f}(E):=\int_{E}\sqrt{1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}}\,\mathrm{d}\cal L^{2}.

Such area functional descends from the perimeter measure of the graph, that is,
\mathscr{A}_{f}(E)=P_{sR}(G_{f}\cap(E\cdot\mathbb{R}))
, where
G_{f}:=\{(0,y,t)\cdot(\xi,0,0):\xi\leq f(y,t)\}
is the subgraph of
f
, and
E\cdot\mathbb{R}=\{(0,y,t)\cdot(\xi,0,0):\xi\in\mathbb{R},\ (y,t)\in E\}
;
see
[
15
, Theorem 3.4]
.
A function
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)\cap C^{0}(\omega)
is
(locally) area minimizing
if
G_{f}
is (locally) perimeter minimizing in
\omega\cdot\mathbb{R}
.

We say that
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
is
stationary
if for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)

(2)

I_{f}(\phi):=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}\epsilon}\mathscr{A}_{f+\epsilon\phi}(\mathrm{spt}(\phi))\right|_{\epsilon=0}=0.

We say that
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
is
stable
if it is stationary and for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)

(3)

II_{f}(\phi):=\left.\frac{\,\mathrm{d}^{2}}{\,\mathrm{d}\epsilon^{2}}\mathscr{A}_{f+\epsilon\phi}(\mathrm{spt}(\phi))\right|_{\epsilon=0}\geq 0.

The functions
I_{f}
and
II_{f}
are called
first
and
second variation
of
f
, respectively.
It is clear that, if
f
is a local area minimizer, then it is stable.

By
[
15
, Remark 3.9]
, if
f\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)\cap C^{0}(\omega)
, for some
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open, then

\displaystyle I_{f}(\phi)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\omega}\frac{\nabla^{f}f}{\sqrt{1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}}}(\nabla^{f}\phi+\partial_{t}f\,\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{2},

\displaystyle II_{f}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int_{\omega}\left[\frac{(\nabla^{f}\phi+\partial_{t}f\,\phi)^{2}}{\left(1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}\right)^{3/2}}+\frac{\nabla^{f}f}{\sqrt{1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}}}\partial_{t}(\phi^{2})\right]\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{2},

for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
.
Notice that the formal adjoint of
\nabla^{f}
is
(\nabla^{f})^{*}\phi=-\nabla^{f}\phi-\partial_{t}f\,\phi
.
By means of the triangle and the Hölder inequalities, one can easily show the following lemma.
We will denote by
W^{1,p}_{0}(\omega)
is the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
,
and by
W^{1,p}_{c}(\omega)
the subspace of functions in
W^{1,p}(\omega)
whose essential support is compactly contained in
\omega
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q>1
.
A function
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
with
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open, is stationary
if and only if
I_{f}(\phi)=0
for all
\phi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{c}(\omega)
,
where
q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}
is the Hölder conjugate exponent.

Proof.

Notice that, using the fact that
\left|\frac{s}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}\right|\leq 1
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
,
and applying the Hölder inequality, there is
C>0
such that, for all
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega
and all
\phi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\omega)
,

(4)

\left|\int_{\omega^{\prime}}\frac{\nabla^{f}f}{\sqrt{1+(\nabla^{f}f)^{2}}}(\nabla^{f}\phi+(\partial_{t}f)\phi)\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{2}\right|\leq C(1+\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(\omega^{\prime})})\cdot\|\phi\|_{W^{1,q^{\prime}}(\omega^{\prime})},

where
1/q+1/q^{\prime}=1
.

If
\phi\in W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{c}(\omega)
, then there is
\omega^{\prime}\Subset\omega
open with
\mathrm{spt}_{e}(\phi)\Subset\omega^{\prime}
, where
\mathrm{spt}_{e}
denotes the essential support.
Therefore, using standard mollifiers, one can show that there exists a sequence
\{\phi_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\subset C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega^{\prime})
converging to
\phi
in
W^{1,q^{\prime}}_{c}(\omega^{\prime})
.
From (
4
), we obtain that

\lim_{k\to\infty}I_{f}(\phi_{k}-\phi)=0
, and so
I_{f}(\phi)=0
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose that
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
for
q>2
and that
II_{f}(\phi)\geq 0
for all
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
.
Then
II_{f}(\phi)\geq 0
for all
\phi\in W^{1,\beta}_{c}(\omega)
, with
\beta=\frac{2q}{q-2}\in(2,\infty)
.

Proof.

First of all, we remark that, since
q/2>1
then
\beta=2(q/2)^{\prime}=\frac{2q}{q-2}\in(2,\infty)
.
Moreover, if
A,B
are two non-negative measurable functions on
\omega
, then

(5)

\displaystyle\|AB\|_{L^{2}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\omega}A^{2}B^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq\left[\left(\int_{\omega}A^{2\frac{q}{2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{2}{q}}\left(\int_{\omega}B^{2\frac{q}{q-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{q-2}{q}}\right]^{1/2}=\|A\|_{L^{q}(\omega)}\|B\|_{L^{\beta}(\omega)}.

Notice that, for
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\phi^{2}-\partial_{\tau}\psi^{2}

\displaystyle=2(\phi\partial_{\tau}\phi-\psi\partial_{\tau}\psi)

\displaystyle=(\phi-\psi)\partial_{\tau}(\phi+\psi)+(\phi+\psi)\partial_{\tau}(\phi-\psi).

Moreover, if
\phi,\psi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\omega)
are such that
\mathrm{spt}(\phi)\cup\mathrm{spt}(\psi)\subset K\Subset\omega
, then

\displaystyle\left|II_{f}(\phi)-II_{f}(\psi)\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\omega}\left(\left|(\nabla^{f}\phi+\partial_{\tau}f\phi)^{2}-(\nabla^{f}\psi+\partial_{\tau}f\psi)^{2}\right|+\left|\partial_{\tau}(\phi^{2})-\partial_{\tau}(\psi^{2})\right|\right)\,\mathrm{d}\mathcal{L}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|(\nabla^{f}\phi+\partial_{\tau}f\phi)-(\nabla^{f}\psi+\partial_{\tau}f\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\cdot\|(\nabla^{f}\phi+\partial_{\tau}f\phi)+(\nabla^{f}\psi+\partial_{\tau}f\psi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{t}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\|(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\partial_{\eta}\phi-\partial_{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|f(\partial_{\tau}\phi-\partial_{\tau}\psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}})\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(\|\partial_{\eta}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f\psi\|_{L^{2}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\|\partial_{t}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\|(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{2}}+\|(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial_{\tau}(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\overset{\eqref{eq67f2415e}}{\leq}(|K|^{1/q}\|\partial_{\eta}\phi-\partial_{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{q}}\|\partial_{\tau}\phi-\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{\beta}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f\|_{L^{q}}\|(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{\beta}})\cdot

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot(|K|^{1/q}\|\partial_{\eta}\phi\|_{L^{2}}+\|f\|_{L^{q}}\|\partial_{\tau}\phi\|_{L^{\beta}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f\|_{L^{q}}\|\phi\|_{L^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+|K|^{1/q}\|\partial_{\eta}\psi\|_{L^{\beta}}+\|f\|_{L^{q}}\|\partial_{\tau}\psi\|_{L^{\beta}}+\|\partial_{\tau}f\|_{L^{q}}\|\psi\|_{L^{\beta}})

\displaystyle\qquad+|K|^{2/q}\|\partial_{t}(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{\beta}}\|(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{\beta}}+|K|^{2/q}\|(\phi-\psi)\|_{L^{\beta}}\|\partial_{\tau}(\phi+\psi)\|_{L^{\beta}}

\displaystyle\leq C(|K|^{2/q}+\|f\|_{W^{1,q}(K)}+\|\phi\|_{W^{1,\beta}(K)}+\|\psi\|_{W^{1,\beta}(K)})^{2}\cdot\|\phi-\psi\|_{W^{1,\beta}(K)},

for a constant
C
depending on
q
.
Then, arguing as in the proof of Lemma
2.2
, the thesis follows.
∎

3.
Bi-Sobolev Lagrangian homeomorphism

3.1.
Continuity of Sobolev composition

Definition 3.1

(Homeomorphism of finite distortion)
.

Given a map
\Psi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
between open sets
\tilde{\omega},\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
q>0
, define

K^{\Psi}_{q}:=\frac{|D\Psi|^{q}}{J_{\Psi}},

where
|D\Psi(x)|
and
J_{\Psi}(x)
are the operator norm and the determinant of the matrix
D\Psi(x)
, respectively.

A homeomorphism
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\omega
between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}

is
of finite distortion

if
\Psi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)

and if
K^{\Psi}_{1}(x)
is well defined and finite for almost every
x\in\tilde{\omega}
.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
14
, Lemma 2.7]
)
.

Let
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\omega
be an homeomorphism between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
,
q>0
and
p>2
.
Suppose that
\Psi\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
,
\Psi^{-1}\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega,\tilde{\omega})
and
J_{\Psi}(x)>0
for a.e.
x\in\tilde{\omega}
.
Then
\Psi
is a homeomorphism of finite distortion and

(6)

K^{\Psi}_{q}\in L^{r}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega})

with
r=\left(\frac{q}{p}+\frac{2}{p-2}\right)^{-1}
, which may be smaller than 1.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
14
, Theorem 5.13]
)
.

Let
\tilde{\omega},\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
open sets,
1\leq s\leq q<\infty
,
and let
\Psi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
be a homeomorphism of finite distortion satisfying

(7)

K^{\Psi}_{q}\in L^{\frac{s}{q-s}}(\tilde{\omega}).

Then the operator
T_{\Psi}:u\mapsto u\circ\Psi
is continuous from
W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)\cap C(\omega)
into
W^{1,s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega})
.

Moreover, we have

(8)

D(u\circ\Psi)(x)=Du(\Psi(x))D\Psi(x)\qquad\text{ for a.e.\penalty\ }x\in\omega,

if we use the convention that
Du(\Psi(x))\cdot 0=0
even if
Du
does not exists or it equals infinity at
\Psi(x)
.

Proposition 3.4

(Continuity of composition)
.

Fix
p>2
and consider
q,s
such that:

(1)

1\leq s<p
and
q=\frac{p^{2}s}{(p-s)(p-2)}
, or, equivalently,

(2)

\frac{p^{2}}{(p-1)(p-2)}\leq q<\infty
and
s=\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}
.

Let
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\omega
be a homeomorphism between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that
\Psi\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
,
\Psi^{-1}\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega,\tilde{\omega})

and
J_{\Psi}(x)>0
for a.e.
x\in\tilde{\omega}
.
Then the operator
T_{\Psi}
defined in Lemma
3.3
is continuous from
W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)\cap C(\omega)
into
W^{1,s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega})\cap C(\tilde{\omega})
,
and (
8
) holds.

Proof.

The choices of
q
and
s
imply
q>s
,
and that the quantity
r=\left(\frac{q}{p}+\frac{2}{p-2}\right)^{-1}

from Lemma
3.2

equals the exponent
\frac{s}{q-s}
from Lemma
3.3
.
Therefore, this proposition is just application in sequence of Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.3
.
∎

3.2.
Sobolev change of variables

Definition 3.5

(Lusin (N) condition)
.

A function
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
satisfies the
Lusin (N) condition
if
it maps null sets to null sets, i.e., if

\forall E\subset\tilde{\omega}:\ |E|=0\Rightarrow|\Psi(E)|=0.

Proposition 3.6

(Change of variables
[
14
, Theorem A.35]
)
.

Let
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\omega
be a homeomorphism between open subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{2}
.
Suppose that
\Psi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
and the
\Psi
satisfies that Lusin (N) condition.
For every non-negative or summable Borel function

\int_{\tilde{\omega}}u\circ\Psi(x)\cdot J_{\Psi}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\omega}u(y)\,\mathrm{d}y.

3.3.
Bi-Sobolev Lagrangian homeomorphism

In Definition
3.7
below, we use the following notation.
For
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}

and
r\in\mathbb{R}
, we denote by
\omega_{1,r}
and
\omega_{2,r}
the two subsets of
\mathbb{R}
defined by

\displaystyle\omega_{1,r}

\displaystyle:=\{\tau\in\mathbb{R}:(r,\tau)\in\omega\},

\displaystyle\omega_{2,r}

Definition 3.7

(Bi-Sobolev Lagrangian homeomorphism)
.

Let
\tilde{\omega},\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open sets, and
f:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
.
We say that
\Psi:\tilde{\omega}\to\omega
is a
f
-Lagrangian homeomorphism
if:

(i)

\Psi
is a homeomorphism;

(ii)

\Psi
has the form
\Psi(t,\zeta)=(t,\chi(t,\zeta))
with
\chi
continuous and
\chi(t,\cdot):\omega_{1,t}\to\mathbb{R}
non-decreasing for every
t
;

(iii)

For every
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}
, the map
\Psi(\cdot,\zeta):\omega_{2,\zeta}\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is locally absolutely continuous and satisfies

\partial_{t}\Psi(t,\zeta)=\nabla^{f}(\Psi(t,\zeta))
, or equivalently,

\partial_{t}\chi(t,\zeta)=f(t,\chi(t,\zeta)),

for a.e.
t
.

We say that a
f
-Lagrangian homeomorphism
\Psi
is
p
-bi-Sobolev
for
p\geq 1
if both
\Psi\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\tilde{\omega})
and
\Psi^{-1}\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,p}(\omega)
.

Notice that if
\Psi
is a
p
-bi-Sobolev Lagrangian homeomorphism with
p>2
, then
\Psi
is differentiable almost everywhere with

D\Psi(t,\zeta)=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
\partial_{t}\chi(t,\zeta)&\partial_{\zeta}\chi(t,\zeta)\end{pmatrix},

where
J_{\Psi}=\partial_{\zeta}\chi>0
almost everywhere,
see
[
5
, §5.8.3]
.
In particular, if
u:\omega\to\mathbb{R}
, then

(9)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(u\circ\Psi)

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{f}u)\circ\Psi,

\displaystyle\partial_{\zeta}(u\circ\Psi)

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\tau}u\circ\Psi)\cdot\partial_{\zeta}\chi.

We claim that

(10)

\frac{1}{\partial_{\zeta}\chi}\in L^{\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega}),\qquad\text{with }\alpha=p-1.

Indeed,
1/\partial_{\zeta}\chi=J_{\Psi^{-1}}\circ\Psi
, where
J_{\Psi^{-1}}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
.
Notice that, a priori, we only have
J_{\Psi^{-1}}\in L^{p/2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
,
but, by the specific form of
\Psi
,
D\Psi^{-1}
is of the form

\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
A&B\end{pmatrix}
for some functions
A,B\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
.
So,
J_{\Psi^{-1}}=B\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
.
Going back to the proof of (
10
), for every
K\Subset\tilde{\omega}
,

\displaystyle\int_{K}\left|\frac{1}{\partial_{\zeta}\chi}\right|^{\alpha}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\zeta

\displaystyle=\int_{K}\left|J_{\Psi^{-1}}\circ\Psi\right|^{\alpha}\frac{J_{\Psi}}{J_{\Psi}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,\mathrm{d}\zeta=\int_{\Psi(K)}\left|J_{\Psi^{-1}}\right|^{\alpha}\frac{1}{J_{\Psi}\circ\Psi^{-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\Psi(K)}\left|J_{\Psi^{-1}}\right|^{\alpha}J_{\Psi^{-1}}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\tau=\int_{\Psi(K)}\left|J_{\Psi^{-1}}\right|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}\eta\,\mathrm{d}\tau<\infty.

From the identity
\partial_{t}\chi=f\circ\Psi
and Proposition
3.4
, we obtain that,
if
f\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\omega)
and if
\Psi
is a
p
-bi-Sobolev
f
-Lagrangian homeomorphism with
p>2
,
then

(11)

\partial_{t}\chi\in W^{1,s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega})\qquad\text{for }s=\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}.

Moreover, we can apply the identities (
9
) to deduce

(12)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}\chi

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{f}f)\circ\Psi,

\displaystyle\partial_{\zeta}\partial_{t}\chi

\displaystyle=(\partial_{\tau}f\circ\Psi)\cdot\partial_{\zeta}\chi.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
be open,

f\in C^{0}(\omega)
,
and
\Psi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tilde{\omega};\omega)
be a Sobolev
f
-Lagrangian homeomorphism.
Then
\Psi
satisfies the Lusin
(N)
condition.

Proof.

Let

have
\mathscr{L}^{2}
-measure zero.
For
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define
E_{t}=\{\zeta\in\mathbb{R}:(t,\zeta)\in E\}
.
Then
\mathcal{L}^{1}(E_{t})=0
for a.e.
t
.

Write
\Psi
as
\Psi(t,\zeta)=(t,\chi(t,\zeta))
, with
\chi\in W^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}({\tilde{\omega}})
.
By the classical result
[
6
, Theorem 4.21
(i)
]
,
for almost every

the map

is locally absolutely continuous,
and thus it satisfies the Lusin

It follows that

.
By Fubini-Tonelli Theorem, we conclude that

.
∎

6.
Examples

6.1.
A Sobolev function that is not intrinsic Lipschitz

We show that our Theorem
A
is indeed a generalization of our previous result
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
for intrinsic Lipschitz functions.
In other words, we want to show that there are functions
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
p>1
, satisfying (
1
) that are not intrinsic Lipschitz.

If
f:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
is a function of the form
f(\eta,\tau)=g(\tau)
for some absolutely continuous

, then
f
is
(locally) intrinsic Lipschitz
in the Heisenberg group
if and only if
\nabla^{f}f(\eta,\tau)=g(\tau)g^{\prime}(\tau)
is (locally) bounded.
At the same time, such
f
belongs to
W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
if and only if
g^{\prime}\in L^{p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R})
, for each
p>1
.

Consider for instance

g(\tau)=\begin{cases}1+\tau\log(\tau)&\text{ if }\tau>0,\\
0&\text{ otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

for each
p>1
, but,
if
\tau>0
,
we have

\nabla^{f}f(\eta,\tau)=g(\tau)g^{\prime}(\tau)=(1+\tau\log(\tau))(\log(\tau)+1),

which is not bounded on
[0,1]
.
So, this is an example of a function
f\in W^{1,p}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
for all
p>1
that satisfied (
1
), but which is not intrinsic Lipschitz.

Appendix A
Consistency of conditions on exponents

Along the paper, we need a number of conditions on the integrability exponents.
The following Lemma
A.1
ensures not only that those conditions are not contradictory,
but also that, starting with the initial exponent
q>2
,
we can pick exponents
p
,
r
,
s
, etc… along the way that satisfy all requirements.

Notice that
\frac{2q}{q-2}
is not quite the Sobolev conjugate exponent of
q
, which would be
\frac{2q}{2-q}
for
q\in[1,2)
.

Lemma A.1
.

For every
q>1
there is
\hat{p}>2
such that, for
p>\hat{p}
,

(29)

\frac{p^{2}}{(p-1)(p-2)}<q.

For every
q>2
there is
\hat{p}>2
such that, for
p>\hat{p}
,

(30)

\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}>q^{\prime}=\frac{q}{q-1}.

For every
q>4
there is
\hat{p}>2
such that, for
p>\hat{p}
,

(31)

\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}>\frac{2q}{q-2}>q^{\prime}.

For every
q>1
there is
\hat{p}>2
such that, for
p>\hat{p}
,
if we set
s=\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}
and
\alpha=p-1
, then

(32)

\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{2}{\alpha}\leq 1.

Proof.

First, since

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{p^{2}}{(p-1)(p-2)}=1<q,

then (
29
) is satisfied for
p
large enough.

Second, notice that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{pq(p-2)}{p^{2}+q(p-2)}=q.

So, if
q>2
, then
q>\frac{q}{q-1}
and thus we obtain (
30
) for
p
large enough.
If
q>4
, then
q>\frac{2q}{q-2}>\frac{q}{q-1}
and we obtain (
31
) for
p
large enough.

Third, we have that

\lim_{p\to\infty}\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{s}+\frac{2}{\alpha}=\frac{1}{q}.

Since
q>1
, then (
32
) is satisfied for
p
large enough.
∎

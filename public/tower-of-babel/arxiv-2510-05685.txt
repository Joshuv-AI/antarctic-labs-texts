Sample complexity for entropic optimal transport with radial cost
Ruiyu Han, Johannes Wiesel

Abstract
. We prove a new sample complexity result for entropy regularized optimal transport. Our bound holds for probability measures on ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} with exponential tail decay and for radial cost functions that satisfy a local Lipschitz condition. It is sharp up to logarithmic factors, and captures the intrinsic dimension of the marginal distributions through a generalized covering number of their supports. Examples that fit into our framework include subexponential and subgaussian distributions and radial cost functions c ⁡ ( x , y ) = | x − y | p c(x,y)=|x-y|^{p} for p ≥ 2 . p\geq 2.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mu,\nu
be probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
for some
d\geq 1
, and assume that we are given i.i.d samples
X_{1},...,X_{n}
,
Y_{1},...,Y_{n}
drawn from
\mu
and
\nu
respectively. Define the empirical measures

\mu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{X_{i}},\quad\nu_{n}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\delta_{Y_{i}}.

Many works in statistical optimal transport have studied comparisons of the optimal transport problem

(OT)

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu,\nu):=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int c(x,y)\,\pi(dx,dy)

with its empirical counterpart
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
. Here
c:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is a cost function,
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of probability distributions
\pi
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
is defined as in (
OT
) with the empirical measures
\mu_{n},\nu_{n}
replacing their population versions
\mu,\nu
; we refer to
[
Vil09
,
San15
]
for fundamental properties of (
OT
). It is well-known that comparisons between
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu,\nu)
and
\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})
suffer from the so-called
curse of dimensionality
, i.e. the difference
\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu_{n}\,\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{0}(\mu,\nu)|]
scales like
n^{-1/d}
in general; see
[
Dud69
,
FG15
,
WB19
]
. This severely restricts applications of OT to high-dimensional data sets. Starting from
[
GS10
,
Cut13
]
, the most popular remedy for this issue is to add an entropic penalization term to (
OT
): for
\varepsilon>0
, the
entropic optimal transport (EOT)
problem is given by

(EOT)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu):=\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu,c):=\inf\limits_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int c(x,y)\,\pi(dx,dy)+\varepsilon H(\pi|\mu\otimes\nu).

Here
\mu\otimes\nu
is the product coupling of
\mu
and
\nu
, and
H
is the
relative entropy defined as

H(\pi|\rho)=\begin{cases}\int\log\Big(\frac{d\pi}{d\rho}\Big)d\pi&\pi\ll\rho,\\
\infty&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

for
\pi,\rho\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In this paper, we aim to find uppper bounds for the quantity

(1)

\mathbb{E}\left[\left|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)\right|\right].

The problem of bounding (
1
) goes back at least to
[
GCB+19
,
CRL+20
]
. The case of subgaussian measures
\mu,\nu
with quadratic cost has been addressed in
[
MNW19
]
. More recently,
[
RS22
]
derive dimension-free bounds for bounded cost functions — however the rates scale exponentially in
1/\varepsilon
. Our method and setting is most closely related to the subsequent work
[
Str23
]
, that assumes continuous cost functions on compact spaces. Let us also mention
[
BEZ25
]
, that derive non-optimal rates for (
1
) and OT problems regularized by other divergences. To the best of our knowledge, our article is the first work to derive sharp bounds on the sample complexity (
1
) for radial (unbounded) cost functions on unbounded spaces. Our main result, Theorem
2.3
, states that under fairly general assumptions on
c
and
\mu,\nu
, the quantity (
1
) is of order
1/\sqrt{n}
up to logarithmic factors. We achieve this by extending the methodology of
[
Str23
]
to probability measures with exponential tail decay. As in Stromme’s work, our rates depend on the minimum of the covering numbers of the (appropriately normalized) supports of
\mu,\nu
— a concept called
minimum intrinsic dimension scaling
of EOT. We provide a more detailed comparison of Theorem
2.3
with the works mentioned above in Section
2.1
.

1.2.
Notation

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the Euclidean norm
|\cdot|
and denote the open ball of radius
r>0
around the point
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
B_{r}(x)
. We write
B_{r}:=B_{r}(0)
for simplicity. We denote the complement of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
by
A^{c}
.
The set of (Borel) probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}).
If
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a Borel set, then
\mu|_{A}(\cdot):=\mu(\cdot\cap A)
is the restriction of
\mu
to
A.
We denote the product measure of two probability measures
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by
\mu\otimes\nu.
We write
M_{p}(\mu):=(\int\|x\|^{p}\,\mu(dx))^{1/p}
for
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and write
\mathrm{spt}(\mu)
for the support of
\mu
. Using the same notation as in (
OT
) above, we define the
p
-Wasserstein distance on
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
as

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu,\nu)^{p}:=\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int|x-y|^{p}\,\pi(dx,dy).

For measures
\pi,\tilde{\pi}\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define the
p
-Wasserstein distance

(2)

W_{p}(\pi,\tilde{\pi})^{p}:=\inf_{\gamma\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\int[|x_{1}-y_{1}|^{p}+|x_{2}-y_{2}|^{p}]\,\pi(dx,dy)

for
x=(x_{1},x_{2}),y=(y_{1},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, in accordance with
[
EN22
]
.
The covering number of a set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
at scale
\delta>0
is defined as

(3)

\mathcal{N}(A,\delta):=\min\Big\{k\in\mathbb{N}\>|\>\exists x_{1},...,x_{k}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\>:\>A\subseteq\bigcup_{\ell=1}^{k}B_{\delta}(x_{\ell})\Big\}.

The incomplete Gamma function is given by

(4)

\displaystyle\Gamma(s,x):=\int_{x}^{\infty}t^{s-1}e^{-t}\,dt,

where
x\geq 0
and
s>0
,
and the Gamma function is
\Gamma(s):=\Gamma(s,0).

We denote constants by
C
, with the convention that
C
can increase from line to line. We always state the dependence of constants on quantities of interest explicitly.

2.
Main Result

Throughout the paper we make two assumptions. The first one states that the tails of
\mu,\nu
decay exponentially.

Assumption 2.1
.

There exist constants
c_{\mu},c_{\nu}>0
and
\alpha_{\mu},\alpha_{\nu}\geq 1

such that

(5)

\mu(B^{c}_{r})\leq 2\exp\big(-c_{\mu}r^{\alpha_{\mu}}\big),\qquad\nu(B^{c}_{s})\leq 2\exp\big(-c_{\nu}s^{\alpha_{\nu}}\big)

for all
r,s>0
.

Well-known distributions satisfying Assumption
2.1
are
subgaussian distributions (
\alpha_{\mu}=\alpha_{\nu}=2
), subexponential distributions (
\alpha_{\mu}=\alpha_{\nu}=1
) or more generally, probability measures on Orlicz spaces of exponential type.

We also make an assumption on the shape of the cost function
c
.

Assumption 2.2
.

The cost function satisfies
c(x,y)=h(|x-y|)
for some continuous function
h:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to[0,\infty)
with
h(0)=0
, and there exist constants
p\geq 2
and
C_{p}>0
such that

(6)

|h(t)-h(t^{\prime})|\leq C_{p}(t\vee t^{\prime})^{p-1}|t-t^{\prime}|,\quad\forall t,t^{\prime}>0.

Important examples of cost functions satisfying Assumption
2.2
are
c(x,y)=|x-y|^{p}
for
p\geq 2.

We are now in a position to state our main result.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
hold. We define

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}r^{\mu}_{n}&:=\Big[4pc_{\mu}^{-1}(c_{\mu}^{-1}\vee 1)\Big(\frac{p}{\alpha_{\mu}}\vee 1\Big)^{2}\log(n)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{\mu}}},\\
r^{\nu}_{n}&:=\Big[4pc_{\nu}^{-1}(c_{\nu}^{-1}\vee 1)\Big(\frac{p}{\alpha_{\nu}}\vee 1\Big)^{2}\log(n)\Big]^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{\nu}}},\end{split}

and

(8)

\displaystyle B_{n}^{\mu}:=B_{r_{n}^{\mu}}(0)\cap\mathrm{spt}(\mu),\qquad B_{n}^{\nu}:=B_{r_{n}^{\nu}}(0)\cap\mathrm{spt}(\nu).

Then

(9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)|]\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\Big(1+c_{\mu}^{-\frac{p}{\alpha_{\mu}}}+c_{\nu}^{-\frac{p}{\alpha_{\nu}}}\Big)+\frac{C}{\sqrt{n}}\big(\varepsilon+(r_{n}^{\mu}+r_{n}^{\nu})^{p}\big)

(10)

\displaystyle\hskip 128.0374pt\cdot\sqrt{\mathcal{N}\bigg(B_{n}^{\mu},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r_{n}^{\mu}+r_{n}^{\nu})^{p-1}}\bigg)\wedge\mathcal{N}\bigg(B_{n}^{\nu},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r_{n}^{\mu}+r_{n}^{\nu})^{p-1}}\bigg)}

holds for all
n\geq 4
, where the constant
C
only depends on
\alpha_{\mu},\alpha_{\nu},p,C_{p}
.

The main idea behind the proof of Theorem
2.3
relies on a careful approximation of the difference
\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)|]
with probability measures that are supported on the closures of
B_{r},B_{s}
for appropriately chosen
r,s>0
. More concretely, let
\mu^{B_{r}}
be the conditional distribution of
\mu
given
\{x\in B_{r}\}
and define
\nu^{B_{s}}
similarly. We then write

(11)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)|]&\leq\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})|]\\
&\quad+\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|]\\
&\quad+|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)|\end{split}

and estimate the three summands on the right-hand side of (
11
) separately. Compared to existing results in the literature, this allows us to derive bounds, that depend on
c
only through Assumption
2.2
. In particular, our results do not rely on structural assumptions or smoothness of the cost function, nor on smoothness of the Schrödinger potentials.

(Subgaussian distributions)

3.1.
Basics

Recall the definition of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu,c)
from (
EOT
). For future reference let us recall the following fact, that follows directly from the definition:

(20)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu,c)=\varepsilon\mathcal{C}_{1}\Big(\mu,\nu,\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\Big).

We also record the following immediate consequence of Assumption
2.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Under Assumption
2.2
we have

(21)

|c(x,y)|\leq C_{p}|x-y|^{p}.

3.2.
Restriction of probability measures

To restrict to probability measures supported on subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we use the following notation:

Definition 3.2
.

For a Borel set
A\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and a probability measure
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we define

(22)

\mu^{A}(dx):=\frac{1}{\mu(A)}\mathbbm{1}_{A}(x)\mu(dx).

For i.i.d. samples
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
drawn from
\mu
we define the empirical measure of
\mu^{A}
as

\mu_{n}^{A}:=\frac{1}{|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:X_{i}\in A\}|}\sum_{X_{i}\in A}\delta_{X_{i}}.

The probability measures
\nu^{A}
and
\nu_{n}^{A}
are defined similarly.

Remark 3.3
.

As
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}
are i.i.d., it is straightforward to see the following:

•

|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:X_{i}\in A\}|\sim\mathrm{Bin}(n,\mu(A)),

•

conditionally on
\{|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:X_{i}\in A\}|=k\}
,
\mu^{A}_{n}
is an empirical measure of
k
samples of
\mu^{A}
.

3.3.
Entropic optimal transport

In this section we recap basic results on entropic optimal transport. We start with the following well-known duality result.

Lemma 3.4

(EOT Duality,
[
Nut21
, Theorem 4.7]
)
.

Let
c\in L^{1}(\mu\otimes\nu)
. Then

(23)

\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)=\sup\limits_{\hat{f}\in L^{1}(\mu),\hat{g}\in L^{1}(\nu)}\int\hat{f}\,d\mu+\int\hat{g}\,d\nu-\varepsilon\int\Big(e^{\frac{\hat{f}(x)+\hat{g}(y)-c(x,y)}{\varepsilon}}-1\Big)\,\mu(dx)\nu(dy).

The supremum is attained by the Schrödinger potentials
f\in L^{1}(\mu)
,
g\in L^{1}(\nu)
, where we always make the normalization

(24)

\displaystyle\int f\,d\mu=\int g\,d\nu=\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu).

Recalling Definition
3.2
we also define the Schrödinger potentials
f^{r,s},g^{r,s}
for
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})
and
f^{r,s}_{n},g^{r,s}_{n}
for
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})
. They satisfy the following regularity property.

Lemma 3.5
.

If Assumption
2.2
holds, then
f^{r,s}
and
g^{r,s}
are
C_{p}(r+s)^{p-1}
-Lipschitz.

4.
Bounding
|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|
and its empirical counterpart

Recalling (
11
), the aim of this section is to provide bounds on the differences

|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|\quad\text{and}\quad\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})|]

for fixed
r,s>0
. To achieve this, we first recap general results on the stability of
\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}
and then specify to our setting. Again we defer proofs to Section
7
.

4.1.
Stability of regularized optimal transport

We make use of the following results from
[
EN22
]
on stability of regularized optimal transport.

Definition 4.1

(cf.
[
EN22
, Definition 3.3]
)
.

Let
p\geq 1,L>0
and let
\mu_{i},\tilde{\mu}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for
i=1,2
. We say a function
c
satisfies (

A
L

) if

(
A_{L}
)

\bigg|\int c\,d(\pi-\tilde{\pi})\bigg|\leq LW_{p}(\pi,\tilde{\pi})

for all
\pi\in\Pi(\mu_{1},\mu_{2}),\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi(\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{2})
. Here
W_{p}
is the Wasserstein distance wrt. the norm
(|\cdot|^{p}+|\cdot|^{p})^{1/p}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Theorem 4.2

(cf.
[
EN22
, Theorem 3.7]
)
.

Let
p\geq 1
. Let
\mu_{i},\tilde{\mu}_{i}\in\mathcal{P}_{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),i=1,2
and let
c
satisfy (

A
L

). Then

(25)

|\mathcal{C}_{1}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2})-\mathcal{C}_{1}(\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{2})|\leq L[W_{p}(\mu_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{1})^{p}+W_{p}(\mu_{2},\tilde{\mu}_{2})^{p}]^{1/p}=:LW_{p}(\mu_{1},\mu_{2};\tilde{\mu}_{1},\tilde{\mu}_{2}).

The following lemma is a variation of
[
EN22
, Proof of Example 3.6]
.

Lemma 4.3
.

For a cost function
c
satisfying Assumption
2.2
, (

A
L

) holds with

(26)

L=C\Big[M_{p}(\mu_{1})+M_{p}(\mu_{2})+M_{p}(\tilde{\mu}_{1})+M_{p}(\tilde{\mu}_{2})\Big]^{p-1},

where we recall
M_{p}(\nu)=(\int\|x\|^{p}\,\nu(dx))^{1/p}
for
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
C
is a constant only depending on
p
and
C_{p}
.

4.2.
Bounding
|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|

For the remainder of this section we assume that Assumptions
2.1
and
2.2
are in force. We also fix
r,s>0
and recall
\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}}
from Definition
3.2
.

Lemma 4.4

(Scaled cost)
.

We have

(27)

\Big|\int\frac{c}{\varepsilon}\,d(\pi-\tilde{\pi})\Big|\leq LW_{p}(\pi,\tilde{\pi})

for all
\pi\in\Pi(\mu,\nu)
and
\tilde{\pi}\in\Pi(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})
, where

(28)

L=\frac{C}{\varepsilon}\big(M_{p}(\mu)+M_{p}(\nu)\big)^{p-1}.

Here the constant
C
only depends on
p
and
C_{p}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

(29)

\displaystyle W_{p}(\mu,\nu;\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p-1}\Big[\mu(B_{r}^{c})\big(M_{p}(\mu^{B_{r}})^{p}+M_{p}(\mu^{B_{r}^{c}})^{p}\big)

(30)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\nu(B_{s}^{c})\big(M_{p}(\nu^{B_{s}})^{p}+M_{p}(\nu^{B_{s}^{c}})^{p}\big)\Big].

Combining Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.5
with Theorem
4.2
immediately gives the following lemma.

Lemma 4.6
.

We have

(31)

\displaystyle\big\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu,\nu)-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})\big\rvert

\displaystyle\leq C\big(M_{p}(\mu)+M_{p}(\nu)\big)^{p-1}\Big[\mu(B_{r}^{c})\big(M_{p}(\mu^{B_{r}})^{p}+M_{p}(\mu^{B_{r}^{c}})^{p}\big)

(32)

\displaystyle\qquad+\nu(B_{s}^{c})\big(M_{p}(\nu^{B_{s}})^{p}+M_{p}(\nu^{B_{s}^{c}})^{p}\big)\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the constant
C
only depends on
p
and
C_{p}
.

4.3.
Bounding
\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})|]

We now carry out a similar analysis for
\mu_{n},\nu_{n}.
For notational simplicity we set

n_{r}:=|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:X_{i}\in B_{r}\}|,\quad n_{s}:=|\{i\in\{1,\dots,n\}:Y_{i}\in B_{s}\}|.

Lemma 4.7
.

If
n_{r},n_{s}>0
, then we have

\displaystyle\lvert\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}},\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})\rvert\leq C\big[M_{p}(\mu_{n})+M_{p}(\nu_{n})+M_{p}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}})+M_{p}(\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})\big]^{p-1}

\displaystyle\cdot\Big[\Big(\frac{1}{n_{r}}-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\sum_{X_{i}\in B_{r}}|X_{i}|^{p}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{X_{i}\notin B_{r}}|X_{i}|^{p}+\Big(\frac{1}{n_{s}}-\frac{1}{n}\Big)\sum_{Y_{i}\in B_{s}}|Y_{i}|^{p}+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{X_{i}\notin B_{s}}|Y_{i}|^{p}\Big]^{\frac{1}{p}},

where the constant
C
only depends on
p
and
C_{p}
.

Taking the conditional expectation on both sides of Lemma
4.7
, we have the following result.

Lemma 4.8
.

If
n_{r},n_{s}>0
, then we have

(33)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n},\nu_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}},\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})|\,\big|n_{r},n_{s}\big]\leq C\Big(M_{p}(\mu)^{p}+M_{p}(\nu)^{p}\Big)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}

(34)

\displaystyle\cdot\bigg[\Big(M_{p}(\mu)^{p}+M_{p}(\mu^{B_{r}^{c}})^{p}\Big)\cdot\Big(1-\frac{n_{r}}{n}\Big)+\Big(M_{p}(\nu)^{p}+M_{p}(\nu^{B_{s}^{c}})^{p}\Big)\cdot\Big(1-\frac{n_{s}}{n}\Big)\bigg]^{\frac{1}{p}}

where the constant
C
depends on
p
and
C_{p}
.

5.
Bounding
\mathbb{E}[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}}_{n},\nu^{B_{s}}_{n})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|]

We now bound the middle term in (

Lemma 5.1

(cf.
[
Str23

Define the

If

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}},\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|\,\big|n_{r},n_{s}\big]\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt\leq\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\mu^{B_{r}}}(f^{r,s})}{n_{r}}}+\sqrt{\frac{\mathrm{Var}_{\nu^{B_{s}}}(g^{r,s})}{n_{s}}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n_{r}n_{s}}}\big\lVert p^{r,s}\big\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu^{B_{r}}\otimes\nu^{B_{s}})}\\
&\hskip 113.81102pt+\frac{\sqrt{2}\|p^{r,s}\|_{L^{2}(\mu^{B_{r}}\otimes\nu^{B_{s}})}}{(n_{r}n_{s})^{\frac{1}{4}}}\mathbb{E}\Big[\|(f^{r,s}_{n}-f^{r,s},g_{n}^{r,s}-g^{r,s})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}})\times L^{2}(\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})}\Big]^{\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

5.1.
Norm of entropic densities

It remains to bound the density

in the space

Lemma 5.2

We have

\begin{split}&\|p^{r,s}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\mu^{B_{r}}\otimes\nu^{B_{s}})}\leq e^{8C_{p}}\mathcal{N}\Big(B^{\mu}_{r},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r+s)^{p-1}}\Big)\wedge\mathcal{N}\Big(B^{\nu}_{s},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r+s)^{p-1}}\Big).\end{split}

Applying Lemma
A.3
and Lemma

yields following corollary.

Corollary 5.3

If Assumption
2.2
holds and

(37)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[|\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu_{n}^{B_{r}},\nu_{n}^{B_{s}})-\mathcal{C}_{\varepsilon}(\mu^{B_{r}},\nu^{B_{s}})|\,\big|n_{r},n_{s}\big]\leq\frac{C_{p}(r+s)^{p}}{\sqrt{n_{r}}}+\frac{C_{p}(r+s)^{p}}{\sqrt{n_{s}}}

(38)

\displaystyle\quad+\bigg[\frac{8(C_{p}(r+s)^{p})}{(n_{r}n_{s})^{\frac{1}{4}}}+\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{n_{r}n_{s}}}\bigg]e^{8C_{p}}\sqrt{\mathcal{N}\Big(B^{\mu}_{r},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r+s)^{p-1}}\Big)\wedge\mathcal{N}\Big(B^{\nu}_{s},\frac{\varepsilon}{(r+s)^{p-1}}\Big)}.

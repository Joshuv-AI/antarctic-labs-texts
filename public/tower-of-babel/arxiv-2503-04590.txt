Abstract.

In this paper, we propose two projection dynamical systems for solving inverse quasi-variational inequality problems in finite-dimensional Hilbert spaces—one ensuring finite-time stability and the other guaranteeing fixed-time stability. We first establish the connection between these dynamical systems and the solutions of inverse quasi-variational problems. Then, under mild conditions on the operators and parameters, we analyze the global finite-time and global fixed-time stability of the proposed systems. Both approaches offer accelerated convergence; however, while the settling time of a finite-time stable dynamical system depends on initial conditions, the fixed-time stable system achieves convergence within a predefined time, independent of initial conditions. To demonstrate their effectiveness, we provide numerical experiments, including an application to the traffic assignment problem.

Keywords.

Finite-time stability, Fixed-time stability, projection method, Dynamical system, Inverse quasi-variational inequality problem.

Introduction

We denote by
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
the inner product and by
\|\cdot\|
the norm on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. This paper focuses on the following
inverse quasi-variational inequality problem
(IQVIP):
Find
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

such that

f(u^{*})\in\Phi(u^{*})\mbox{ and }\langle u^{*},y-f(u^{*})\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall y\in\Phi(u^{*})

(1.1)

where
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is a multi-valued operator and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a single-valued operator.

A notable special case arises when
\Phi(u)=\Omega
is a fixed, nonempty, closed, and convex set, independent of
u
. In this case, the IQVIP (
1.1
) reduces to the inverse variational inequality problem (IVIP), formulated as follows:
Find
u\in\mathbb{R}^{*}
such that

f(u^{*})\in\Omega\mbox{ and }\langle u^{*},y-f(u^{*})\rangle\geq 0\quad\forall y\in\Omega.

(1.2)

This problem has numerous applications across various fields (see, for instance,
[
3
,
22
]
).

Furthermore, the IVIP (
1.2
) can be interpreted as a standard variational inequality problem when
f

is the inverse of a given mapping
F
. Specifically, if
f(u)=F^{-1}(u)=u
then the IVIP (
1.2
) can be rewritten as:

Find
u\in\Omega
such that

\langle F(u^{*}),v-u^{*}\rangle\geq 0,\quad\forall v\in\Omega.

This formulation serves as a fundamental mathematical model that encompasses a broad spectrum of problems, including optimization, variational inequalities, saddle point problems, Nash equilibrium problems in noncooperative games, and fixed-point problems. For further details, see
[
6

and the references therein.

[

However, in many practical applications—such as robotics and vehicle monitoring networks—precise knowledge of the initial state is often unavailable, making it difficult to determine the settling time in advance.

To overcome this drawback, Polyakov in
[
35

introduced the notion of fixed-time convergence, where the convergence time is bounded by a constant independent of the initial state. This idea has since been further explored in studies such as

Preliminaries

2.1.
Some notions on convex analysis

An operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
L
-Lipschitz continuous on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
if

\|T(u)-T(v)\|\leq L\|u-v\|,\quad\mbox{ for all }x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

When
L=1
, we say that
T
is
nonexpansive.

An operator
T:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be
strongly monotone
with modulus
\beta
if for any
u,{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we have

\langle f(u)-f(v),u-v\rangle\geq\beta\|u-v\|^{2}.

When
\beta=0
we say that
T
is monotone.

Note that if
T
is strongly monotone with modules
\beta\geq 0
then we have

\|f(u)-f(v)\|\geq\beta\|u-v\|,\quad\mbox{ for all }{\color[rgb]{0,0,1}u,v}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

For every
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the metric projection
P_{C}(u)
of
u
onto
C
is defined by

P_{C}(u)=\arg\min\left\{\left\|v-u\right\|:v\in C\right\}.

It is worth noting that when
C
is nonempty, closed, and convex,
P_{C}(u)
exists and is unique.
Some useful properties of the projection operator are listed below.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
C
be a nonempty, closed, convex set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Let
u,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Then one has

(1)

v=P_{C}(u)\iff\langle v-u,w-u\rangle\leq 0\ \ \mbox{ for all }w\in C

(2)

P_{C}
is 1-Lipschitz continuous on
C
.

2.2.
Equilibrium points and stability

Consider a general dynamical system

\dot{u}(t)=T(u(t)),\quad t\geq 0,

(2.1)

where
T
is a continuous mapping from
\mathbb{R}^{n}
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
u:[0,+\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

In this paper we investigate finite-time and fixed-time stability of equilibrium points of dynamical systems; we recall these definitions below.

Definition 2.1
.

[
34
]
A point
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be an equilibrium point for (
2.1
) if
T(u^{*})=0
; An equilibrium point is called

(a)

finite-time stable
if it is stable in the sense of Lyapunov, that is, for any
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that, for every
u_{0}\in B(u^{*},\delta)
, the solution
u(t)
of the dynamical system with
u(0)=u_{0}
exists and is contained in
B(u^{*},\epsilon)
for all
t>0
, and there exists a neighborhood
B(\delta,u^{*})
of
u^{*}
and a settling-time function
T:B(\delta,u^{*})\setminus\{u^{*}\}\rightarrow(0,\infty)
such that for any
u(0)\in B(\delta,u^{*})\setminus\{u^{*}\}
, the solution of (
2.1
) satisfies
u(t)\in B(\delta,u^{*})\setminus\{u^{*}\}
for all
t\in[0,T(u(0)))
and
\lim_{t\to T(u(0))}u(t)=u^{*}
.

(b)

Globally finite-time stable
if it is finite-time stable with
B(\delta,u^{*})=\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

(c)

Fixed-time stable
if it is globally finite-time stable, and the settling-time function satisfies

\sup_{u(0)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}T(u(0))<\infty.

here
B({\color[rgb]{0,0,1}\delta},u^{*})
denotes the open ball with center
u^{*}
and radius
\delta
.

From now on, we denote by
\mbox{\rm Zer}(T)
the set of solutions of equation
T(u)=0
.

2.3.
A nominal dynamical system for inverse quasi-variational inequality problems

In this section we first recall a nominal dynamical system proposed in
[
15
]
for IQVIPs (
1.1
) and a result on the existence and uniqueness of solutions of IQVIPs (
1.1

In 2019,
[
52
]
Zou et al. proposed the following dynamical system for solving inverse quasi-variational inequality problem (
1.1
)

\dot{u}(t)=-\sigma\left(f(u(t))-P_{\Phi(u(t))}(f(u(t))-\alpha.u(t))\right),

(2.2)

where
\sigma,\alpha
are positive constants.

In 2023
[
15
]
Dey and Reich proposed the following

\dot{u}(t)=-\sigma(t)\left(f(u(t))-P_{\Phi(u(t))}(f(u(t))-\alpha.u(t))\right),

(2.3)

where
\sigma(t)>0
for all
t>0
.

Remark 2.1
.

As shown in
[
15
]
, a necessary and sufficient condition for
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
to be a solution of IQVIP (
1.1
) is that it is also an equilibrium point of dynamical system (
2.3
) or of (
2.2
).

To analyze the finite-time and fixed-time stability we make the following assumption:

(
A
) Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a set-valued mapping with nonempty, closed, and convex values. Let
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
L-
Lipschitz continuous and
\beta-
strongly monotone single-valued mapping. Assume that there exists some
\mu>0
such that

\|P_{\Phi(u)}(w)-P_{\Phi(v)}(w)\|\leq\mu\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

(2.4)

and

\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}+\mu<\alpha.

We recall a result on the existence and uniqueness of solutions to the IQVIP (
1.1

Theorem 2.1
.

[
15
]

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be such that assumption (

Then the inverse quasi-variational inequality problem (

The following lemma lists some useful inequalities that are needed in the sequel.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a set-valued mapping with nonempty, closed and convex values, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
L-
Lipschitz continuous and

\beta-
strongly monotone mapping. Assume further that there exists some
\mu>0
such that

\|P_{\Phi(u)}(w)-P_{\Phi(v)}(w)\|\leq\mu\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.5)

Assume that the solution set of IQVIP (
1.1
) is nonempty. Let
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a solution of IQVIP (
1.1
). Then

(1)

The inequality holds

\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|\leq\left(\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}+\mu\right)\|u-u^{*}\|.

(2)

One has

\displaystyle\langle u-u^{*},f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\rangle\geq(\beta-\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}-\mu)\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}>0,

for all
u\neq u^{*}.

(3)

It holds that

\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|\leq\left(L+\mu+\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}\right)\|u-u^{*}\|.

(4)

The following holds

\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|\geq\left|\beta-\mu-\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}\right|\|u-u^{*}\|.

Proof.

(
1
) Since
f(u^{*})=P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})+P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|.

Using triangular inequality and by the non-expansion property of the projector and condition (
2.5

\displaystyle\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|+\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(u^{*})-\alpha(u-u^{*})\|+\mu\|u-u^{*}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{\|f(u)-f(u^{*})\|^{2}+\alpha^{2}\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}-2\langle u-u^{*},f(u)-f(u^{*})\rangle}+\mu\|u-u^{*}\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\sqrt{L^{2}\|u-u^{*}\|{{}^{2}}+\alpha^{2}\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}-2\alpha\beta\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}}+\mu\|u-u^{*}\|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sqrt{(L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta)}+\mu\right)\|u-u^{*}\|.

(
2
) For all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{u^{*}\},
by Part
1
of Lemma
2.2
it holds that

\displaystyle\langle u-u^{*},f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u-u^{*},f(u)-f(u^{*})-(P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*}))\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle u-u^{*},f(u)-f(u^{*}\rangle-\langle u-u^{*},P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\rangle

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\beta\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}-\|u-u^{*}\|\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\beta\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}-(\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}+\mu)\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\beta-\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}-\mu)\|u-u^{*}\|^{2}>0\mbox{ for all }u\neq u^{*}.

(
3
) We have

\displaystyle\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|

\displaystyle=\|f(u)-f(u^{*})-(P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*}))\|

\displaystyle\leq\|f(u)-f(u^{*})\|+\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|

\displaystyle\leq L\|u-u^{*}\|+\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}\|u-u^{*}\|

\displaystyle=\left(L+\mu+\sqrt{L^{2}+\alpha^{2}-2\alpha\beta}\right)\|u-u^{*}\|.

(
4
) By Part

we have

\displaystyle\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|

\displaystyle=\|f(u)-f(u^{*})-(P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u^{*})}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*}))\|

∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a set-valued mapping with nonempty, closed and convex values, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an
L-
Lipschitz continuous and
\beta-
strongly monotone mapping. Assume further that there exists some
\mu>0
such that

\|P_{\Phi(u)}(w)-P_{\Phi(v)}(w)\|\leq\mu\|u-v\|,\quad\forall u,v,w\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.6)

Then the operator
T(u)\equiv f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)
is Lipschit continuous.

Proof.

By the non-expansion property of the projection operator and (
2.6
) we have that

\displaystyle\|T(u)-T(v)\|=

\displaystyle\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-(f(v)-P_{\Phi(v)}(f(v)-\alpha v))\|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\|f(u)-f(v)\|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\|P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(v)-\alpha v)+P_{\Phi(u)}(f(v)-\alpha v)-P_{\Phi(v)}(f(v)-\alpha v))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle L\|u-v\|+\|f(u)-f(v)\|+\alpha\|u-v\|+\mu\|u-v\|

\displaystyle(2L+\alpha+\mu)\|u-v\|.

3.
Finite-time stability Analysis

In this section we will analyze the finite-time stability for IQVIPs (
1.1
). By doing so we propose a new first order dynamical system associated with the problem (
1.1
) such that a solution of (
1.1
) becomes an equilibrium point of the dynamical system. Under mild conditions for the parameters, we demonstrate that the proposed dynamical system is finite-time stable.

We first recall a characterization for the finite-time stability of solutions of a dynamical system, given by Bhat and Bernstein in
[
5
]
. This result will play an important role in studying finite-time convergence.

Theorem 3.1
.

[
5
]
(Lyapunov condition for finite-time stability). Assume that there exists a continuously differentiable function
V:D\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, where
D\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a neighborhood of the equilibrium point
u^{*}
for (
2.1
) and an open neighborhood
U\subseteq D
of
u^{*}
such that

\dot{V}(u)\leq-K.\big(V(u)\big)^{p}\quad\forall u\in U\setminus\{u^{*}\},

where
K>0
and
p\in(0,1).
Then, the equilibrium point
u^{*}
of (
2.1
) is finite-time stable equilibrium point of (
2.1
). Moreover, the settling time
T
satisfies

T(u(0))\leq\dfrac{V(u(0))^{1-p}}{K(1-p)}

for any
u(0)\in U
.

In addition, if
D=\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then the equilibrium point
u^{*}
of (
2.1
) is globally finite-time stable.

In order to obtain a finite-time stability of solutions of inverse quasi-variational inequality problem (
1.1
) we now present a novel first-order dynamical system associated with the problem (
1.1
). Our proposed dynamical system is as follows:

\dot{u}(t)=-\sigma\dfrac{f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)}{\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}}},

(3.1)

where

\sigma>0
is a scalar tuning gain, and
\gamma>2,\alpha>0
are two design parameters.

It is worth noting that for all
u\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
such that
f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)=0
, the expression on the right-hand side of (
3.1
) is still well defined for all
\gamma>2
. In fact, it is equal to zero. Indeed, we have

\left\|\dfrac{f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)}{\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}}}\right\|=\|f(u)-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u)-\alpha u)\|^{\frac{1}{\gamma-1}}=0.

The following result establishes the connection between the equilibrium points of the previously mentioned dynamical system and those of (
2.3
).

Proposition 3.1
.

A point
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an equilibrium point of (
3.1
) if it is also an equilibrium point of (
2.3
), and vice versa.

Proof.

Observe that
u^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an equilibrium point of (
3.1
) if and only if

\dfrac{f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})}{\|f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}}}=0.

It holds if and only if

\dfrac{\|f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|}{\|f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|^{\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}}}=0.

Again, this equation is fulfilled if and only if

\|f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})\|^{1-\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}}=0.

(3.2)

Because
1-\frac{\gamma-2}{\gamma-1}=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}>0
for all
\gamma>2
, the equality (
3.2
) holds if and only if

f(u^{*})-P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*})=0,

or

f(u^{*})=P_{\Phi(u)}(f(u^{*})-\alpha u^{*}).

This holds if and only if
u^{*}
is an equilibrium point of (
2.3
). The proof is thus completed.
∎

From Proposition
3.1
, we derive the following direct consequence:

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow 2^{\mathbb{R}^{n}}
be a set-valued mapping with nonempty, closed, and convex values, and
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a single mapping. Then
u^{*}
is a solution to the inverse quasi-variational inequality problem (
1.1
) if and only if it is an equilibrium point of the dynamical system (
3.1
).

Proof.

It is a direct consequence of Proposition
3.1
and Remark
2.1
.
∎

The next main result gives the finite-time stability of the dynamical system (
3.1
).

Theorem 3.2
.

Let

4.
Fixed-time stability Analysis

This section is for analyzing the fixed-time stability of solutions of IQVIPs (

). We will introduce another projection dynamical system for the problem (
1.1
). Next, we characterize the solutions of inverse quasi-variational inequality problems through equilibrium points of the proposed dynamical system. Finally, we establish the global fixed-time convergence of the proposed dynamical system.

Again, we recall here a result on fixed-stable time stability given by Polyakov obtained in
[

]

Theorem 4.1
.

(Lyapunov condition for fixed-time stability). Assume that there exists a continuously differentiable function

, where

for (

p_{1}p_{3}<1

p_{2}p_{3}>1.
Then, the equilibrium point
u^{*}
of (

in (

5.
Consistent discretization of the modified projection dynamical system

In this section, we show that the fixed-time stability of the dynamical system (
4.2
) is preserved under time discretization. It is worth noting that while continuous-time dynamical systems exhibit fixed-time convergence, this property does not always extend to their discrete-time counterparts. However, a well-designed discretization scheme can help maintain the convergence behavior of the continuous system in the discrete setting (see, for example,
[
36
]
). We prove that under the conditions of Theorem
4.2
, a consistent discretization of the fixed-time convergent modified projection dynamical system (
4.2
) can be achieved.

We first recall a result related to the consistent discretization of a differential inclusion problem introduced in
[

7.
Conclusion

In this paper, we present two modified projection dynamical systems for addressing inverse quasi-variational inequality problems—one ensuring finite-time stability and the other fixed-time stability. The latter guarantees the existence, uniqueness, and convergence of its trajectory to the unique solution of the inverse quasi-variational inequality problem within a fixed-time framework. Furthermore, it is shown that the fixed-time stability of the second dynamical system is preserved under the forward-Euler discretization. Numerical experiments are provided to illustrate the algorithm’s performance. Future research may focus on investigating the finite-time and fixed-time stability of the modified projection dynamical systems in a broader setting, such as infinite-dimensional Hilbert or Banach spaces, with the potential to relax assumptions on operators and parameters.

Sex-consistent outcomes in SGLT2 inhibitor-treated acute myocardial infarction patients: a real-world study
Authors: Chen Guo, Jia-Hao Dou, Fan-Shun Guo, Shan Zhao, Rui-Yun Wu, Yi-Wei Hu, Su Liu, Jin Wei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSex-related differences in outcomes of sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors therapy in acute myocardial infarction remain inconclusive.MethodsThis real-world retrospective study compared sex disparities in primary outcomes (pro-brain natriuretic peptide levels and left ventricular ejection fraction), secondary outcomes (major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events), and additional laboratory outcomes (serum lipids, fasting glucose, glycosylated hemoglobin, serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and estimated glomerular filtration rate) among women and men with acute myocardial infarction who were treated with SGLT2 inhibitors.ResultsA total of 310 patients participated in the study, of whom 244 (78.7%) were men and 66 (21.3%) were women. The baseline characteristics revealed distinct clinical and demographic profiles between the sexes regarding age, type of myocardial infarction, presence of multivessel disease, incidence of new-onset heart failure, and renal function within the study population. After two years of follow-up, no significant sex differences were observed in cardiac function (assessed by pro-brain natriuretic peptide and left ventricular ejection fraction), major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events, metabolic parameters, or renal outcomes among acute myocardial infarction patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors. Exploratory analysis revealed a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction and heart failure hospitalization in female versus male patients in this cohort.ConclusionAmong patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with early SGLT2 inhibitor initiation, cardiorenal-metabolic and primary clinical outcomes are sex-independent, indicating no significant sex disparities in outcomes of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy.Graphical abstract Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40001-025-03759-x.Keywords: Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 inhibition, Myocardial infarction, Sex, Cardiorenal outcomes, Cardiometabolic health

Introduction

Myocardial infarction, a severe and acute manifestation of coronary artery disease, remains one of the leading causes of mortality among both men and women. Despite the implementation of reperfusion therapy and guideline-directed medical management, patients continue to face a high risk of early and progressive heart failure. There is an urgent need to further reduce the residual risks associated with adverse cardiac remodeling, heart failure, and cardiac death, especially during the critical early period following a myocardial infarction.

Sodium-glucose co-transporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitors are cardiorenal protective agents that have become a cornerstone therapy for patients with the full spectrum of heart failure. Current evidence suggests that the early and continuous administration of SGLT2 inhibitors following acute myocardial infarction is beneficial, safe, and well-tolerated. The landmark EMMY [1] and DACAMI [2] trials found that early administration of SGLT2 inhibitors significantly reduced N-terminal pro-brain natriuretic peptide and improved left ventricular remodeling in patients with myocardial infarction. The DAPA-MI [3] and EMPACT-MI [4, 5] studies indicated that SGLT2 inhibitor use following acute myocardial infarction could improve cardiometabolic endpoints and reduce the risk of heart failure. Recent meta-analyses have further indicated that SGLT2 inhibitors reduce heart failure hospitalizations in patients with acute myocardial infarction [6–8]. Consequently, SGLT2 inhibitors are likely to gain increased prominence in cardiology practice, with early initiation following myocardial infarction increasingly recognized as a prevailing trend.

Women experience a greater burden of cardiovascular complications after myocardial infarction. However, a subanalysis of the EMMY trial demonstrated that early administration of empagliflozin post-myocardial infarction provides comparable benefits for both males and females [9]. Nonetheless, evidence supporting sex-related equivalence in outcomes of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy among patients with myocardial infarction remains limited. This sex-specific study aims to retrospectively compare sex differences in cardiorenal-metabolic and clinical outcomes among patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with this therapeutic strategy.

Methods

Ethics approval

This single-center, retrospective study was approved by the ethics committee of our institution (Approval No.: 2024-234). The study protocol is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (Registration No.: ChiCTR2500096731). Written informed consent was obtained from each patient, and all procedures were conducted in strict accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.

Sample size calculation

The sample size was estimated using G*Power software. Drawing on data from previous studies and findings of the EMMY trial, a natural 50% reduction in pro-brain natriuretic peptide (proBNP) levels within 6 months after acute myocardial infarction has been established. Considering the anticipated intervention effect of SGLT2 inhibitors, this study intends to detect a 30% difference in proBNP levels across different sex groups. With a predefined statistical power of 80% and a significance level (α) of 0.05, the final estimated minimum sample size is 300 patients.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

Consecutive patients diagnosed with acute myocardial infarction who were admitted to the Second Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University, from January 1, 2020, to March 31, 2024, were screened. Eligible patients were aged 18 to 80 years who underwent percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and received guideline-directed medical therapy for acute myocardial infarction, along with the initiation of an SGLT2 inhibitor (either empagliflozin or dapagliflozin) within 72 h of the onset of myocardial infarction, with indications of diabetes and/or heart failure. Exclusion criteria included a history of prior oral SGLT2 inhibitor use, renal insufficiency (estimated glomerular filtration rate [eGFR] < 30 mL/min/1.73 m2), pregnancy, lactation, a history of malignancy within the past three years, autoinflammatory diseases, long-term systemic glucocorticoid therapy, or clinically significant non-transient hematologic disorders. Patients were stratified into female and male groups based on sex. Data concerning cardiorenal-metabolic and clinical outcomes were collected and analyzed.

Outcome measures

The primary outcomes included proBNP levels and left ventricular ejection fraction (LVEF) measured during multiple follow-up visits over two years. All echocardiographic data were acquired and analyzed by a single board-certified cardiologist with over 10 years of specialized experience in echocardiography. The imaging and measurement protocols strictly adhered to the guidelines of the American Society of Echocardiography, and the cardiologist completed pre-study standardized training focused on uniform measurement techniques to ensure consistency. Secondary outcomes comprised major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events (MACCE), defined as a composite endpoint that encompasses all-cause mortality, recurrent myocardial infarction, stroke, rehospitalization, and heart failure hospitalization. Additional outcomes included laboratory parameters such as serum lipids, fasting glucose, glycosylated hemoglobin, serum creatinine, urea nitrogen, and eGFR.

Statistical analysis

Continuous variables were expressed as medians with interquartile ranges (IQR) or means ± standard deviations (SD), and categorical variables were presented as frequencies (percentages). Baseline characteristics, clinical factors, and laboratory parameters were compared between sexes using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test for continuous variables and Pearson’s Chi-squared test for categorical variables. A classical ANCOVA model was utilized to assess sex differences in cardiorenal-metabolic outcomes, adjusting for baseline covariates including age, type of myocardial infarction, presence of multivessel disease, new-onset heart failure, and eGFR. The cumulative risk of MACCE was analyzed using Cox regression, with adjustments for baseline values. Subgroup analyses were conducted using a multivariate Cox model. A two-tailed P value of less than 0.05 was considered statistically significant. All statistical analyses were performed using R software (version 4.2.2, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 310 patients with acute myocardial infarction were enrolled in this study. Baseline characteristics of the study population, stratified by sex, revealed substantial disparities across several variables. Males (N = 244) were younger than females (N = 66), with a median age of 61 years (IQR 55–69 years) compared to 70 years (IQR 64–77 years), respectively (P < 0.001). The number of ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) cases exceeds that of non-ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (NSTEMI) in both sexes, which is primarily attributed to STEMI’s typical symptoms, definitive ECG findings, and frequent requiremen for urgent PCI—aligning with the study’s eligibility criteria and ensuring high enrollment. In contrast, NSTEMI is linked to atypical symptoms (some managed medically), underdiagnosis or exclusion due to delayed presentation or strict biomarker thresholds, and overrepresentation in elderly patients with multiple comorbidities (often excluded for PCI contraindications). Females were more likely to receive drug-eluting balloons (30.3% vs. 18.9%, P = 0.044) and exhibited a higher prevalence of new-onset heart failure (90.9% vs. 58.6%, P < 0.001). Conversely, males had a higher prevalence of smoking (61.1% vs. 0.0%, P < 0.001) and multivessel disease (84.4% vs. 72.7%, P = 0.028). Females demonstrated higher levels of proBNP (median 2,900, IQR 2,033–4,751 pg/ml vs. median 1,458, IQR 574–2,858 pg/ml, P < 0.001) and D-Dimer (median 960, IQR 698–1,728 μg/L vs. median 685, IQR 468–1,108 μg/L, P < 0.001), while males presented higher pre-PCI serum creatinine (median 77, IQR 66–92 μmol/L vs. median 63, IQR 49–75 μmol/L, P < 0.001) and post-PCI serum creatinine (median 82, IQR 69–95 μmol/L vs. median 74, IQR 60–84 μmol/L, P = 0.016). No significant differences were observed in variables such as high-sensitivity troponin T, creatine kinase-MB, low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, fasting glucose, glycosylated hemoglobin, comorbidities including hypertension and type 2 diabetes, or duration and costs associated with the initial hospitalization (Table 1). These findings underscore the existence of distinct clinical and demographic profiles between sexes within the study population.

Primary outcomes

From 3 to 12 months, a reduction in proBNP levels was observed in both male and female groups; however, this effect gradually diminished after 12 months. During this period, the decrease in proBNP levels from 6 to 12 months was more pronounced in men than in women (6 month: Difference in LS Mean: −629.14 pg/ml, 95% CI −1264.83 to 6.55 pg/ml, P = 0.062, partial η2 < 0.001; 9 month: Difference in LS Mean: −2080.74 pg/ml, 95% CI −3150.97 to −1010.51 pg/ml, P < 0.001, partial η2 = 0.012; 12 month: Difference in LS Mean: −4037.59 pg/ml, 95% CI −7145.65 to −929.52 pg/ml, P12m = 0.016, partial η2 = 0.052); nonetheless, this sex difference resolved between 1 and 2 years of follow-up (24 month: Difference in LS Mean: -768.40 pg/ml, 95% CI −3417.81 to 1881.00 pg/ml, P = 0.579, partial η2 = 0.027) (Table 2 and Fig. 1a). These findings indicate that there is no long-term sex difference in the reduction of proBNP levels among patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with SGLT2 inhibitors. Furthermore, transthoracic echocardiography conducted at each follow-up visit from 1 to 24 months post-discharge revealed no significant sex disparities in the absolute changes of LVEF from baseline as the primary endpoint (1 month: Difference in LS Mean: 0.30%, 95% CI −4.52 to 5.11%, P = 0.904, partial η2 = 0.001; 3 month: Difference in LS Mean: −1.83%, 95% CI −6.58 to 2.92%, P = 0.451, partial η2 = 0.006; 6 month: Difference in LS Mean: −4.81%, 95% CI −10.72 to 1.10%, P = 0.116, partial η2 = 0.024; 9 month: Difference in LS Mean: −1.05%, 95% CI −8.02 to 5.92%, P = 0.771, partial η2 < 0.001; 12 month: Difference in LS Mean: −2.43%, 95% CI −7.61 to 2.75%, P = 0.362, partial η2 = 0.001; 18 month: Difference in LS Mean: −2.34%, 95% CI −12.82 to 8.15%, P = 0.668, partial η2 = 0.001; 24 month: Difference in LS Mean: 0.63%, 95% CI −9.59 to 10.84%, P = 0.906, partial η2 < 0.001) (Table 2 and Fig. 1b). The small effect size (partial η2 < 0.06) suggests that the non-significant finding is likely a true absence of meaningful clinical difference rather than insufficient statistical power. Additionally, no sex inequalities were observed in the changes of left ventricular end-diastolic dimension, left ventricular end-systolic dimension, and E/A ratio among patients with acute myocardial infarction receiving SGLT2 inhibitors (Table 2).

Clinical outcomes

Among male patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with SGLT2 inhibitors, 71 (29.1%) experienced MACCE over a 24-month follow-up period, compared to 25 (37.9%) MACCE events in female patients (adjusted HR 1.33, 95% CI 0.81–2.19, P = 0.26). Cox regression analysis of cumulative MACCE risk indicated no significant difference between female and male patients during both short-term and long-term follow-up (log-rank P = 0.162) (Fig. 2a). Subgroup analyses further demonstrated no sex disparity in MACCE risk across subgroups stratified by age, type of myocardial infarction, coronary artery lesions, interventional therapy, and pre-existing or new-onset comorbidities (Fig. 3). However, additional exploratory analyses revealed that female patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors had a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction (adjusted HR 5.97, 95% CI 1.56–22.9, P = 0.009; log-rank P = 0.008) and heart failure hospitalization (adjusted HR 2.68, 95% CI 1.02–7.07, P = 0.046; log-rank P = 0.039) compared with male patients (Fig. 2b-f).

Laboratory parameters

For metabolic parameters, a short-term (1–3 months) increase in lipoprotein(a) was observed in female patients relative to male patients (P1m = 0.031, P3m = 0.040); however, no sex differences were noted in the long-term changes in lipoprotein(a) or other metabolic indexes, including low-density lipoprotein cholesterol, fasting glucose, and glycosylated hemoglobin. Regarding renal function, the data demonstrated a more significant decrease in eGFR in women than in men at 1 month (P = 0.011). In contrast, no sex disparities were observed in eGFR from 3 to 24 months, nor in short-term and long-term follow-up changes in urea nitrogen and creatinine (Table 2).

Discussion

The present study is the first real-world investigation to evaluate sex disparities in cardiorenal-metabolic endpoints and the risk of major adverse cardiovascular and cerebrovascular events among patients with acute myocardial infarction who underwent early SGLT2 inhibitor initiation. It provides valuable evidence that the early SGLT2 inhibitor treatment confers no long-term sex differences in these outcomes. Notably, as an exploratory finding, female patients treated with SGLT2 inhibitors have a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction and heart failure hospitalization compared to male patients.

SGLT2 inhibitors have expanded beyond the initial applications in diabetes and heart failure. Accumulating evidence supports the beneficial effects across a broader spectrum of cardiovascular diseases, attributed to their pleiotropic effects. Specifically, by modulating critical molecular pathways involved in cardiac injury and remodeling, and supported by substantial clinical evidence, SGLT2 inhibitors have emerged as a promising therapeutic strategy in the setting of myocardial infarction [10]. Clinical trials [1–5] and recent meta-analyses [6–8] have demonstrated that SGLT2 inhibitors improve cardiac function and cardiometabolic endpoints, while reducing the risk of heart failure and heart failure hospitalization in patients with acute myocardial infarction. Notably, although the EMPACT-MI trial failed to demonstrate a reduction in all-cause mortality [4]. The SGLT2-I AMI PROTECT registry identified the use of SGLT2 inhibitors was associated with a lower risk of adverse cardiovascular outcomes during index hospitalization and long-term follow-up [11]. Subsequent meta-analyses have indicated that SGLT2 inhibitor treatment following myocardial infarction was associated with a significant reduction in all-cause mortality [12] and major adverse cardiovascular events [13]. The potential mechanisms by which SGLT2 inhibitors exert beneficial effects in myocardial infarction primarily involve the attenuation of neurohormonal activation, reduction of cardiomyocyte necrosis and inflammatory response, mitigation of reperfusion injury, enhancement of endothelial function and vasodilation, improvement of myocardial energy metabolism, preservation of cardiac contractility, and amelioration of adverse cardiac remodeling following myocardial infarction [14, 15]. The ongoing EMPRESS-MI trial, a randomized controlled study designed to evaluate the effects of empagliflozin on cardiac remodeling in patients with left ventricular systolic dysfunction following acute myocardial infarction [16], will provide additional scientific evidence. In line with these mechanistic insights and ongoing research, the results of our study further demonstrate that early administration of SGLT2 inhibitors is associated with benefits in cardiac function and glucose metabolism for both male and female patients with acute myocardial infarction.

Women exhibit a worse prognosis than men following acute myocardial infarction. A key unanswered question is whether there are sex disparities in the clinical outcomes of SGLT2 inhibitors. Currently, data on sex differences in outcomes of SGLT2 inhibitor therapy for acute myocardial infarction remain limited. In diabetes-focused studies, dapagliflozin has been shown to confer comparable cardiovascular and renal benefits, along with a similar safety profile in both women and men [17], while empagliflozin reduced the rates of cardiovascular death, heart failure hospitalization, and incidents of worsening nephropathy without significant differences between sexes [18]. A study in heart failure indicated that empagliflozin provided comparable benefits in outcomes and health status for both female and male patients with heart failure and preserved ejection fraction [19]. In patients with myocardial infarction, only a post-hoc analysis of the EMMY trial suggested that the benefits of SGLT2 inhibitors after myocardial infarction were independent of sex [9, 20]. Consistent with this, the present study also demonstrated that female patients had more severe baseline disease, as evidenced by a higher burden of comorbidities (e.g., older age and a higher prevalence of new-onset heart failure). Among patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with SGLT2 inhibitors, long-term outcomes—including proBNP levels, LVEF, MACCE, renal and metabolic endpoints, as well as initial hospitalization duration and costs—were independent of sex. However, the baseline disparity was a key potential confounder: the more severe initial disease in women may independently influence the observed sex-related outcomes, and thus we caution against attributing all outcome differences solely to “sex”. In contrast to the sub-analysis of the EMMY trial, the current real-world study covers two SGLT2 inhibitors (empagliflozin and dapagliflozin), with study subjects more consistent with complex and diverse real-world clinical scenarios. Focusing on the long-term dimension, it confirms that SGLT2 inhibitors exert no sex-specific differences in cardiorenal-metabolic endpoints among patients with myocardial infarction, while it also compares the incidence of cardiovascular outcomes between men and women and reveals a novel finding that women have a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction and heart failure hospitalization than men after treatment. This real-world evidence provides a more reliable reference for clinicians to safely prescribe SGLT2 inhibitors for patients of different sexes with complex conditions.

Several limitations of the present study should be acknowledged. First, the proportion of female patients in the study was relatively low (21.3%), which primarily reflects the inherent sex disparity in the incidence of myocardial infarction in clinical practice—males are generally at a higher risk of myocardial infarction, consistent with epidemiological data from previous real-world studies and clinical trials including the EMMY Trial. While we have mitigated potential biases by incorporating sex as a key covariate in statistical models and conducting sex-stratified analyses, the limited sample size of female participants means our subgroup findings for women are hypothesis-generating and require validation in larger, well-powered studies. Second, the study design precluded the inclusion of a conventional treatment control group, as the study population consisted exclusively of patients receiving SGLT2 inhibitors for either diabetes or new-onset heart failure, who exhibited more severe baseline clinical characteristics. Third, despite statistical adjustments for significant baseline differences between male and female participants, residual confounding may persist, precluding the complete exclusion of its potential impact on the study findings. Finally, the relatively small sample size, coupled with the low incidence of outcomes on all-cause mortality, recurrent myocardial infarction, and stroke, precluded independent validation of significant findings. Notably, the adjusted HR for recurrent myocardial infarction exhibits low statistical precision due to a wide confidence interval, attributed to few events and real-world cohort heterogeneity. The observed increased risk trend thus requires cautious interpretation, with no definitive conclusions possible given insufficient statistical power for this endpoint.

Conclusions

Among patients with acute myocardial infarction treated with early SGLT2 inhibitor initiation, the primary analysis demonstrated sex-neutral long-term outcomes across cardiorenal endpoints, cardiometabolic health, and key clinical events—an important extension of evidence, as prior studies on SGLT2 inhibitors in myocardial infarction have rarely focused on sex-specific comparisons and these outcomes. Notably, our exploratory analysis uncovered a novel sex-specific risk profile that female patients treated with early SGLT2 inhibitors exhibited a higher risk of recurrent myocardial infarction and heart failure hospitalization relative to their male counterparts. To further validate the generalizability of these sex-neutral outcomes and address potential sex-specific nuances, additional large-scale, sex-stratified studies focusing on SGLT2 inhibitor use in the acute myocardial infarction population are warranted.

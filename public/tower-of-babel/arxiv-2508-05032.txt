On the spatio-temporal increments of nonlinear parabolic SPDEs and the open KPZ equation
Jingwu Hu and Cheuk Yin Lee

Abstract
. We study spatio-temporal increments of the solutions to nonlinear parabolic SPDEs on a bounded interval with Dirichlet, Neumann, or Robin boundary conditions. We identify the exact local and uniform spatio-temporal moduli of continuity for the sample functions of the solutions. These moduli of continuity results imply the existence of random points in space-time at which spatio-temporal oscillations are exceptionally large. We also establish small-ball probability estimates and Chung-type laws of the iterated logarithm for spatio-temporal increments. Our method yields extension of some of these results to the open KPZ equation on the unit interval with inhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions. Our key ingredients include new strong local non-determinism results for linear stochastic heat equation under various types of boundary conditions, and detailed estimates for the errors in linearization of spatio-temporal increments of the solution to the nonlinear equation.

1.
Introduction

Fix
L>0
and consider the solution
u=\{u(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0,L]}
to the stochastic partial differential equation (SPDE, for short):

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u+b(u)+\sigma(u)\xi&\text{on $\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0\,,L)$},\\
&u(0\,,x)=u_{0}(x)&\text{for all }x\in[0\,,L],\end{split}\right.

where
\xi=\{\xi(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0\,,L]}
is a space-time white noise defined on a complete probability space
(\Omega\,,\mathscr{F}\,,\mathrm{P})
,
\sigma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
b:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
are both non-random, globally Lipschitz functions, and
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0\,,L])
is a non-random function.
Throughout we assume one of the following boundary conditions:

—

Dirichlet boundary condition:

(D)

\displaystyle u=0\quad\text{at}\quad x=0,\ x=L;

—

Neumann boundary condition:

(N)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u=0\quad\text{at}\quad x=0,\ x=L;

—

Robin boundary condition:

(R)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\partial_{x}u+\alpha u=0\quad\text{at}\quad x=0,\\
\partial_{x}u+\beta u=0\quad\text{at}\quad x=L,\end{cases}

where
\alpha\,,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.

Equations of the type (
1.1
) are sometimes referred to as reaction-diffusion equations
[
30
,
44
,
12
,
45
]
.
A special case of (
1.1
) is the stochastic heat equation with
b=0
and
\sigma(u)=u
, which is also known as the parabolic Anderson model
[
11
,
6
,
42
]
.
The stochastic heat equation is closely related to the Kardar-Parisi-Zhang (KPZ) equation, which was originally introduced by
[
40
]
where the spatial domain is
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and has deep connections to different systems and models in mathematical physics
[
34
,
17
,
66
,
36
]
.
The open KPZ equation (see (
1.12
) below), introduced by Corwin and Shen
[
20
]
, models stochastic interface growth on a bounded interval with inhomogeneous Neumann boundary conditions and arises from the open asymmetric simple exclusion process under a scaling limit
[
20
]
.
The reader may refer to
[
18
,
19
,
8
,
73
,
49
]
for recent developments.

The primary goal of this paper is to study spatio-temporal regularities of the sample functions of solutions to (
1.1
) and the open KPZ equation (
1.12
), and to establish detailed descriptions regarding local spatio-temporal increments.

In order to present our main results, let us define the parabolic-type metric
\rho
on
[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]
by
\rho((t\,,x)\,,(s\,,y))=\max\{|t-s|^{1/4}\,,|x-y|^{1/2}\}
, and define

\displaystyle B_{\rho}((t\,,x)\,,r)=\{(s\,,y)\in[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]:\rho((t\,,x)\,,(s\,,y))\leqslant r\},

\displaystyle B^{*}_{\rho}((t\,,x)\,,r)=\{(s\,,y)\in[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]:0<\rho((t\,,x)\,,(s\,,y))\leqslant r\}.

Also, recall that when
b=0
and
\sigma=0
, the weak solution to (
1.1
) is
G\ast u_{0}
, which is defined for any
z=(t\,,x)\in[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]
by

(1.2)

\displaystyle G\ast u_{0}(z):=G_{t}\ast u_{0}(x)=\int_{0}^{L}G_{t}(x\,,y)u_{0}(y)\,{\rm d}y,

where
G
is the heat kernel (see Section
2
below).
As is commonly done
[
22
,
70
]
, the SPDE (
1.1
) is interpreted in its mild form

\displaystyle u(t\,,x)=(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y

\displaystyle+\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)

for any
(t\,,x)\in(0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]
, where the last integral is a stochastic integral which can be defined in the sense of Walsh
[
70
]
.

1.1.
Main results

Our main results apply to any one of the boundary conditions (
D
), (
N
), (
R
).
The first result identifies the exact local modulus of continuity for the spatio-temporal increments relative to a fixed based point in space-time, which exhibit a Khinchine-type law of the iterated logarithm (LIL).

Theorem 1.1

(Law of the iterated logarithm)
.

For every fixed point
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
, there exists a constant
K_{0}\in(0\,,\infty)
such that

(1.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|}{\rho(z\,,z_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z_{0}))}}=K_{0}|\sigma(u(z_{0}))|\quad\text{a.s.}

The preceding continues to hold for
t_{0}=0
with
u(z_{0})=u_{0}(x_{0})
if, additionally,

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\textstyle&u_{0}\text{ is bounded and, for some $r>0$,}\\
&|G_{t}\ast u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x_{0})|\lesssim\rho((t\,,x)\,,(0\,,x_{0}))\quad\forall(t\,,x)\in B_{\rho}((0\,,x_{0})\,,r).\end{split}

As is customary, “
f(a)\lesssim g(a)
” means that there exists
C\in(0\,,\infty)
such that
f(a)\leqslant Cg(a)
for all
a
.
Theorem
1.1
says that for every fixed
z_{0}\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
, there is a
\mathrm{P}
-null set (depending on
z_{0}
) off which (
1.3
) holds.
See
[
31
]
for spatial LILs and
[
71
]
for temporal LILs in a similar context of nonlinear SPDEs.

The next result complements the above by identifying the exact uniform modulus of continuity for the spatio-temporal increments.
Let us recall that a Borel set
A\subset\mathbb{R}
is said to be polar for
u
if
\mathrm{P}\{\exists(t\,,x)\in[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]\,,u(t\,,x)\in A\}=0
.

Theorem 1.2

(Exact uniform modulus of continuity)
.

Assume that
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}
is polar for
u
.
Then, for every fixed interval
I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
with
0<a<T
and
0<c<d<L
, there exists a constant
K\in(0\,,\infty)
such that

(1.5)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon\end{subarray}}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|}{|\sigma(u(z))|\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}=K\quad\text{a.s.}

The above statement extends to
a=0
under the additional assumption that

(1.6)

\displaystyle\textstyle u_{0}\text{ is bounded and }|G\ast u_{0}(z^{\prime})-G\ast u_{0}(z)|\lesssim\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\text{ on $[0\,,T]\times[c\,,d]$.}

Remark 1.3
.

When
\sigma
is bounded away from 0, the polarity condition is satisfied tautologically.
When
\sigma(u)=u
, under boundary condition (
N
) or (
R
), the polarity condition is satisfied if
u_{0}
is strictly positive on
[0\,,L]
,
thanks to the known fact that if
u_{0}>0
then
u>0
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times[0\,,L]
; see
[
20
, Proposition 2.7]
; see also
[
62
,
28
]
.

Let us emphasize that the constants
K_{0}
in (
1.3
) and
K
in (
1.5
) are both finite and strictly positive, hence the modulus functions in (
1.3
) and (
1.5
) are exact.
Because of the presence of the logarithmic factor in (
1.5
), the sample functions
(t\,,x)\mapsto u(t\,,x)
only belong to the space
C^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}-,\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}-}(I)=\textstyle{\bigcap_{0<\alpha<\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}}}\bigcap_{0<\beta<\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}C^{\alpha,\beta}(I)}
but not
C^{\nicefrac{{1}}{{4}},\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}}(I)
.
This demonstrates the optimality of the Hölder regularity of
u
.

In the case that (
1.1
) is the linear stochastic heat equation with additive noise, i.e., when
b=0
and
\sigma=
constant, the solution to (
1.1
) is Gaussian.
Exact local and uniform moduli of continuity are known for a large class of Gaussian random field; see
[
57
,
59
,
53
]
.
The results apply to the solutions to a family of linear SPDEs on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
with additive spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise
[
53
,
38
]
.
Our results are an extension of those results to the solutions to (
1.1
) which are non-Gaussian random fields when
\sigma
is non-constant.
In particular, (
1.3
) states that the spatio-temporal increments of
u
at a fixed point
z_{0}
are locally of order
|\sigma(u(z_{0}))|\rho(z\,,z_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z_{0}))}
, but (
1.5
) shows that the uniform modulus for the increments is of a larger order, at a logarithmic level.
The moduli of continuity results imply the existence of random exceptional points at which the spatio-temporal increments are larger than those at a fixed point, as stated below.

Corollary 1.4

(Exceptional increments)
.

Assume that
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}
is polar for
u
.
Fix an interval
I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
, where
0<a<T
and
0<c<d<L
. Let
K
be the constant in (
1.5
).
For every
\theta>0
, define the random set

\displaystyle F(\theta)=\left\{z\in I:\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}^{*}(z,\varepsilon)}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}\geqslant\theta|\sigma(u(z))|\right\}.

If
\theta>K
, then
F(\theta)=\varnothing
a.s.;
if
\theta\in(0\,,K]
, then
F(\theta)
has Lebesgue measure 0 a.s.;
and there exists
K^{\prime}\in(0\,,K]
such that if
0<\theta<K^{\prime}
, then
F(\theta)
is nonempty and dense in
I
a.s. Consequently, the random set

(1.7)

\displaystyle\left\{z\in I:\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}^{*}(z,\varepsilon)}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}=\infty\right\}

has Lebesgue measure 0 and is dense in
I
a.s.

The first result of this kind was proved for Brownian motion by Orey and Taylor
[
64
]
, who also computed the Hausdorff dimension of fast points – the set of times where Brownian increments fail to satisfy LIL and are exceptionally large.
Similar results are known for fractional Brownian motion
[
47
]
and a class of Gaussian processes
[
46
]
.
The Hausdorff dimension of the set of exceptional spatial points for the stochastic heat equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
at which temporal increments fail to satisfy LIL is obtained in
[
39
]
.
Let us mention that exceptional points of the type similar to (
1.7
) are also studied for Brownian motion
[
64
]
, Brownian sheet
[
69
,
70
]
, and stochastic wave equations
[
10
,
52
]
, and are called singularities in the context of Brownian sheet and stochastic wave equations.
We leave some open problems that appear to lie beyond the scope of this paper. An adaptation of the method of limsup random fractals
[
46
,
39
]
may lead to answers to some of these questions.

Open Problem 1.5
.

Let
K^{*}=\sup\{\theta\geqslant 0:F(\theta)\neq\varnothing\text{ a.s.}\}
. Then
0<K^{*}\leqslant K
.
Is
K^{*}=K
?
Is
F(K)\neq\varnothing
a.s.?
Can these constants be computed or identified?

Open Problem 1.6
.

What are the dimensions (Hausdorff, Minkowski, packing, etc) of
F(\theta)
for
0<\theta\leqslant K
?

Our next set of results concern small-ball probabilities and
\liminf
-type behavior of spatio-temporal increments.

Theorem 1.7

(Small-ball probability)
.

Assume that
b
and
\sigma
are bounded, and
\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(x)|>0
.
Let
\phi:(0\,,1]\to[1\,,\infty)
be a function such that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\phi(\varepsilon)=O(|\log\varepsilon|)\quad\text{as $\varepsilon\to 0^{+}$.}

Fix any point
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
.
Then, there exist
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0\,,1]
and
C_{0}\,,C_{1}\in(0\,,\infty)
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{0}]
,

(1.9)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-C_{1}\phi(\varepsilon)}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{(\phi(\varepsilon))^{1/6}}\right\}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-C_{0}\phi(\varepsilon)}.

Furthermore, under the additional assumption that

(1.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&u_{0}\text{ is bounded and, for some $r>0$ and $q>1$,}\\
&|G_{t}\ast u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(x_{0})|\lesssim[\rho((t\,,x)\,,(0\,,x_{0}))]^{q}\quad\forall(t\,,x)\in B_{\rho}((0\,,x_{0})\,,r),\end{split}

the above statement continues to hold when
t_{0}=0
.

Small-ball probability estimates are known to imply Chung’s LIL
[
16
,
56
]
.
The following result holds regardless of whether or not
b
and
\sigma
are bounded.

Theorem 1.8

(Chung-type LIL)
.

For every fixed
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
, there exists a constant
C_{2}\in(0\,,\infty)
such that

(1.11)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/6}}{\varepsilon}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|=C_{2}|\sigma(u(z_{0}))|\quad\text{a.s.}

The statement extends to
t_{0}=0
under the additional assumption (
1.10
).

Similar small-ball probability and Chung-type LIL results for SPDEs such as (
1.1
) but on spatial domain
\mathbb{T}
or
\mathbb{R}
can be found in
[
3
,
53
,
13
,
14
,
15
,
43
]
.
Moreover, the existence of a small-ball constant for
t\mapsto u(t\,,x_{0})
, where
u
solves the stochastic heat equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{T}
, is established by Khoshnevisan et al
[
43
]
.
Theorem
1.7
is a spatio-temporal version of that result in a weaker form.

Also, our method continues to apply when the spatial domain is

Corollary 1.10
.

For any fixed
(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
, there exist constants
K_{0}\,,K_{0}^{\prime}

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{|u(t_{0}+\varepsilon\,,x_{0})-u(t_{0}\,,x_{0})|}{\varepsilon^{1/4}\sqrt{\log\log(1/\varepsilon)}}=K_{0}|\sigma(u(t_{0}\,,x_{0}))|\quad\text{a.s.},

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\frac{\log\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/4}\sup_{t:|t-t_{0}|\leqslant\varepsilon}|u(t\,,x_{0})-u(t_{0}\,,x_{0})|=C_{2}|\sigma(u(t_{0}\,,x_{0}))|\quad\text{a.s.},

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\left(\frac{\log\log(1/\varepsilon)}{\varepsilon}\right)^{1/2}\sup_{x:|x-x_{0}|\leqslant\varepsilon}|u(t_{0}\,,x)-u(t_{0}\,,x_{0})|=C_{2}^{\prime}|\sigma(u(t_{0}\,,x_{0}))|\quad\text{a.s.},

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-C_{1}^{\prime}\phi(\varepsilon)}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{x:|x-x_{0}|\leqslant\varepsilon}|u(t_{0}\,,x)-u(t_{0}\,,x_{0})|\leqslant\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)}\right)^{1/2}\right\}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-C_{0}^{\prime}\phi(\varepsilon)},

where the last two small-ball estimates hold under the additional conditions that
b
is bounded and
|\sigma|
is bounded above and away from 0, and that
\phi:(0\,,1]\to[1\,,\infty)
satisfies
\phi(\varepsilon)=O(|\log\varepsilon|)
as

.

If
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}
is polar for

, then for any fixed
0<a<T
and

, there exist constants

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{t,t^{\prime}\in[a,T]:0<|t-t^{\prime}|\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|u(t^{\prime},x_{0})-u(t\,,x_{0})|}{|\sigma(u(t\,,x_{0}))||t^{\prime}-t|^{1/4}\sqrt{\log(1/|t^{\prime}-t|)}}=K\quad\text{a.s.},

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{x,x^{\prime}\in[c,d]:0<|x-x^{\prime}|\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|u(t_{0}\,,x^{\prime})-u(t_{0}\,,x)|}{|\sigma(u(t_{0}\,,x))|\sqrt{|x^{\prime}-x|\log(1/|x^{\prime}-x|)}}=K^{\prime}\quad\text{a.s.}

1.2.
The open KPZ equation

As an application of the method of this paper, we study spatio-temporal increments for the open KPZ equation

(1.12)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}h=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}h+\tfrac{1}{2}(\partial_{x}h)^{2}+\xi&\text{on $\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0\,,1)$},\\
&h(0\,,x)=\log u_{0}(x)&\forall x\in[0\,,1],\end{split}\right.

with inhomogeneous Neumann boundary condition

(1.13)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}h(t\,,0)=\mu,\qquad\partial_{x}h(t\,,1)=-\nu\qquad\forall t>0,

where
\xi
is a space-time white noise,
u_{0}\in C([0\,,1])
is a strictly positive continuous non-random function, and
\mu\,,\nu\in\mathbb{R}
are constants.
The Hopf-Cole solution to (
1.12
) is given by

(1.14)

\displaystyle h(t\,,x)=\log u(t\,,x)\quad\ \forall t>0,\,x\in[0\,,1],

where
u
is the solution to the stochastic heat equation

(1.15)

\left\{\begin{split}&\partial_{t}u=\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}u+u\xi&\text{on $\mathbb{R}_{+}\times(0\,,1)$},\\
&u(0\,,x)=u_{0}(x)&\forall x\in[0\,,1],\end{split}\right.

with the Robin boundary condition

(1.16)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}u(t\,,0)=(\mu-\tfrac{1}{2})u(t\,,0),\quad\partial_{x}u(t\,,1)=-(\nu-\tfrac{1}{2})u(t\,,1)\qquad\forall t>0.

Owing to strict positivity of
u
(see
[
20
, Proposition 2.7]
), the logarithm in (
1.14
) is well-defined.
For the justification of the Hopf-Cole solution to (
1.12
), see
[
33
]
.

The theorem below identifies the exact local and uniform moduli of continuity for the spatio-temporal increments of the open KPZ equation, which extends the temporal result of Das
[
26
]
and the spatial result of Foondun et al
[
31
]
for the KPZ equation on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}
.

Theorem 1.11
.

For every fixed point
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,1)
,

(1.17)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|h(z)-h(z_{0})|}{\rho(z\,,z_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z_{0}))}}=K_{0}\qquad\text{a.s.}

where
0<K_{0}<\infty
is the same constant as in (
1.3
).
Moreover, for every fixed interval
I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
with
0<a<T
and
0<c<d<1
,

(1.18)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}}\frac{|h(z^{\prime})-h(z)|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}=K_{1}\qquad\text{a.s.}

where
0<K_{1}<\infty
is the same constant as in (
1.5
).
Furthermore, (
1.17
) and (
1.18
) continue to hold when
t_{0}=0
and
a=0
under (
1.4
) and (
1.6
), respectively.

Theorem
1.11
implies the existence of exceptional spatio-temporal increments for the open KPZ equation:

Corollary 1.12
.

Fix
I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
, where
0<a<T
and
0<c<d<1
.
Let
K
be the constant in (
1.5
)
and (

).
For every
\theta>0
, define the random set

\displaystyle E(\theta)=\left\{z\in I:\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}^{*}(z,\varepsilon)}\frac{|h(z^{\prime})-h(z)|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}\geqslant\theta\right\}.

If
\theta>K
, then
E(\theta)=\varnothing

if
\theta\in(0\,,K]
, then
E(\theta)
has Lebesgue measure 0 a.s.;
and there exists

such that if

, then

is nonempty and dense in

Consequently, the random set

\left\{z\in I:\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z^{\prime}\in B_{\rho}^{*}(z,\varepsilon)}\frac{|h(z^{\prime})-h(z)|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}=\infty\right\}

has Lebesgue measure 0 and is dense in
I
a.s.

Moreover, we obtain a Chung-type LIL for the open KPZ equation:

Theorem 1.13
.

Fix

.
Then

(1.19)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/6}}{\varepsilon}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|h(z)-h(z_{0})|=C_{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{|h(t_{0}+\varepsilon\,,x_{0})-h(t_{0}\,,x_{0})|}{\varepsilon^{1/4}\sqrt{\log\log(1/\varepsilon)}}=K_{0}\quad\text{a.s.},

1.3.
Proof ideas and contributions

Similar spatial and temporal LILs and moduli of continuity results for SPDEs of the type (
1.1
) but on spatial domain
\mathbb{T}
or
\mathbb{R}
are established in
[
26
,
38
,
43
]
.
Their arguments build on either the Lei-Nualart decomposition
[
55
]
or the Mueller-Tribe pinned string method
[
61
]
for the linear equation, which essentially states that the solution can be decomposed into the sum of two processes, one has smooth sample paths and the other is a fractional Brownian motion or a Gaussian random field with stationary increments.
These results or methods do not seem to carry over directly to the case of bounded interval domains especially under Robin boundary conditions and when
u
is treated as a spatio-temporal process.
Moreover, the decomposition of Dirichlet or Neumann heat kernel
G=\Gamma+H
, where
\Gamma
is the heat kernel on the full line
\mathbb{R}
and
H
is a smooth function, can be derived using the method of images or Poisson summation formula
[
70
,
41
,
25
]
, but this decomposition method does not seem to apply readily to the case of Robin boundary conditions either.
In order to circumvent the technical obstacle, we appeal to a different approach using the strong local non-determinism (SLND) method for the linear equation
[
51
,
53
]
and combine it with the method of linearization of the nonlinear equation
[
48
,
31
,
43
,
35
,
34
]
.

It might help to recall that a Gaussian random field
\{X(z)\}_{z\in I}
with
I\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is strongly locally non-deterministic
[
5
,
65
,
21
,
60
,
72
]
if
there exists
C>0
such that

\Var(X(z)\mid X(z_{1})\,,\dots,X(z_{n}))\geqslant C\min_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}\Var(X(z)-X(z_{i}))

uniformly for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and for all
z\,,z_{1}\,,\dots,z_{n}\in I
.
Under Dirichlet or Neumann boundary condition, we prove the spatio-temporal SLND property for the linear equation (see Section
3
below) by adopting the method of
[
51
,
50
,
53
,
54
]
based on Fourier transform.
Our SLND result is sharp and gives matching bounds up to
t=0
and up to the boundaries of the interval under (
D
) and (
N
).
The case of Robin boundary condition (
R
) requires a separate treatment because the heat kernel is not amenable to Fourier transform in the spatial variable
x
.
We devise a proof that bypasses the use of Fourier transform in
x
and uses instead the orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions to establish the spatio-temporal SLND property under (
R
), which is more natural and adaptable to the domain and its boundary condition.
This idea appears to be new in the context of SLND for SPDEs and may make it possible to study SPDEs on general bounded domains or fractals with various boundary conditions such as the ones in
[
9
,
37
,
4
]
and to investigate their optimal Hölder regularities, exact moduli of continuity, etc.
Thanks to our method, we obtain matching upper and lower bounds for the variance of spatio-temporal increments valid up to the boundaries under (
D
) and (
N
), which improve the bounds in
[
25
]
, and obtain new matching bounds under (
R
) within interior of the interval (see Lemma
3.9
).
The SLND property, matching variance bounds, and a series representation of the solution then allow applications of the results in
[
53
]
to obtain our main results in the Gaussian case.
Since spatio-temporal SLND implies spatial SLND and temporal SLND, our method also yields spatial results and temporal results.

In order to go from the Gaussian case to the non-Gaussian case, we adopt the idea of linearization of the SPDE and localization of heat kernel in
[
48
,
31
]
, but without Fourier transform, and obtain detailed estimates for the spatio-temporal linearization errors (see Section
4
below).
Our work demonstrates that a crude heat kernel bound (Lemma
2.2
below) is enough for carrying out the spatio-temporal localization analysis without the use of Gaussian bounds for heat kernel, making it possible for extensions to more general differential operators (see, e.g.,
[
63
,
32
]
).
Finally, the local and uniform moduli of continuity and Chung-type LIL for the open KPZ equation can be obtained through linearization of the Hopf-Cole solution, which relates the spatio-temporal increments to those of the stochastic heat equation with multiplicative noise and allows application of our results for (
1.1
). To the best of our knowledge, our results for the open KPZ equation are new.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\{(\lambda_{n}\,,f_{n})\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
denote the eigenpairs of the Laplace operator
-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}
on
(0\,,L)
with any one of the boundary conditions (
D
), (
N
), (
R
).
In other words, each
f_{n}
satisfies
-\tfrac{1}{2}f_{n}^{\prime\prime}=\lambda_{n}f_{n}
on
(0\,,L)
with the prescribed boundary condition.
We always assume that the eigenvalues are arranged in ascending order
\lambda_{1}\leqslant\lambda_{2}\leqslant\dots
and each
f_{n}
is normalized to have
\|f_{n}\|_{L^{2}}=1
.

Lemma 2.1
.

The following properties hold:

1.

Under Dirichlet boundary condition (
D
),

(2.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\lambda_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\tfrac{\pi n}{L}\big)^{2},\qquad f_{n}(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\sin\big(\tfrac{n\pi x}{L}\big)\qquad\quad\text{ for $n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}$.}

2.

Under Neumann boundary condition (
N
),

(2.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\qquad\qquad\lambda_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(\tfrac{\pi(n-1)}{L}\big)^{2}\hskip 140.0pt\text{ for $n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}$,}\\
&\qquad\qquad f_{1}(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac{1}{L}}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{n}(x)=\sqrt{\tfrac{2}{L}}\cos\big(\tfrac{(n-1)\pi x}{L}\big)\quad\text{for $n\geqslant 2$.}\end{split}

3.

Under Robin boundary condition (
R
),
0
is an eigenvalue iff
\alpha=\beta/(1+\beta L)
.

i.

If
\alpha=\beta/(1+\beta L)
, then
\lambda_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{n}^{2}
and
f_{n}=\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}\,e_{n}
, where
\eta_{n}
are the nonnegative roots of the equation

(2.3)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\tan(\eta_{n}L)=\frac{(\beta-\alpha)\eta_{n}}{\eta_{n}^{2}+\alpha\beta},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},

and

\displaystyle e_{1}(x)=1-\alpha x,

(2.4)

\displaystyle e_{n}(x)=\cos(\eta_{n}x)-\frac{\alpha}{\eta_{n}}\sin(\eta_{n}x)\qquad\text{for $n\geqslant 2$.}

ii.

If
\alpha\neq\beta/(1+\beta L)
, then
\lambda_{n}=\tfrac{1}{2}\eta_{n}^{2}
, where
\eta_{n}
are the positive roots of (
2.3
) and
f_{n}=\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{-1}\,e_{n}
, where
e_{n}
is given by (
2.4
).

In particular, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\qquad\quad\eta_{n}=\tfrac{\pi(n_{0}+n)}{L}+O(\tfrac{1}{n})\quad\text{and}\quad\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{-2}=\tfrac{2}{L}(1+O(\tfrac{1}{n^{2}}))\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

In all cases,

(2.6)

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}\asymp n^{2},\qquad

\displaystyle 0\leqslant\lambda_{n+1}-\lambda_{n}\lesssim n,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sup_{n\geqslant 1,x\in[0,L]}|f_{n}(x)|<\infty,\qquad

\displaystyle\textstyle\sup_{n\geqslant 1,x\in[0,L]}|n^{-1}f_{n}^{\prime}(x)|<\infty,

and
\{f_{n}\}_{n\geqslant 1}
is an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}([0\,,L])
under
{\langle f,g\rangle}_{L^{2}}=\int_{0}^{L}f(x)g(x){\rm d}x
.

Proof.

Cases 1 and 2 are a standard and routine eigenvalue problem, so we omit the proof.
As for case 3, note that
0
is an eigenvalue iff
e_{1}(x)=A+Bx
, where
(A\,,B)\neq(0\,,0)
, is an eigenfunction satisfying condition (
R
). It is easy to see that the last condition is satisfied iff
\alpha=\beta/(1+\beta L)
, in which case
e_{1}(x)=1-\alpha x
is an eigenfunction. From the equation
-\tfrac{1}{2}e^{\prime\prime}=\lambda e
, any other eigenpair
(\lambda\,,e)
must have the form
e(x)=A\cos(\mu x)+B\sin(\mu x)
and
\lambda=\tfrac{1}{2}\eta^{2}\geqslant 0
.
Then, from the boundary condition (
R
), one can readily deduce (
2.3
) and (
2.4
).
The function
\eta\mapsto(\beta-\alpha)\eta/(\eta^{2}+\alpha\beta)
has at most one singularity on
(0\,,\infty)
, is eventually increasing or decreasing to 0, and is
\asymp(\beta-\alpha)\eta^{-1}
as
\eta\to\infty
.
It is easy to deduce from these properties that there is
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, every interval
I_{n}:=(\pi(k_{n}-1/2)/L\,,\pi(k_{n}+1/2)/L)
, where
k_{n}=n_{0}+n
, contains exactly one solution
\eta_{n}
to equation (
2.3
).
Hence,
\eta_{n}\sim n\pi/L
as
n\to\infty
.
Also, since
\tan(zL)\asymp z
for
|z|
small, it follows that

\displaystyle\left|\eta_{n}-\frac{\pi k_{n}}{L}\right|\lesssim|\tan(\eta_{n}L-\pi k_{n})|=|\tan(\eta_{n}L)|=\frac{|\beta-\alpha|\eta_{n}}{|\eta_{n}^{2}+\alpha\beta|}\lesssim\eta_{n}^{-1}\lesssim n^{-1}\text{ as $n\to\infty$,}

which shows the first property in (
2.5
).
This together with (
2.4
) implies that

\displaystyle\|e_{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\frac{\beta(\eta_{n}^{2}+\alpha^{2})}{2\eta_{n}^{2}(\eta_{n}^{2}+\beta^{2})}-\frac{\alpha}{2\eta_{n}^{2}}+\frac{L}{2}\left(1+\frac{\alpha^{2}}{\eta_{n}^{2}}\right)=\frac{L}{2}(1+O(n^{-2}))\quad\text{as $n\to\infty$.}

The property (
2.6
) and uniform bound (
2.7
) follow readily.
Finally, the last assertion follows from general spectral theory for elliptic operators; see, e.g.,
[
68
, Theorem 5.11]
or
[
58
, Theorem 4.12]
.
∎

We frequently use the following Parseval’s identity, which is a direct consequence of
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
being an orthonormal basis for
L^{2}([0\,,L])
:
For all
\phi\in L^{2}([0\,,L])
,

(2.8)

\displaystyle\textstyle\|\phi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left|{\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle}_{L^{2}}\right|^{2}.

The heat kernel for
\partial_{t}-\tfrac{1}{2}\partial_{x}^{2}
under the respective boundary condition (
D
), (
N
) or (
R
) is given by

(2.9)

\displaystyle G_{t}(x\,,y)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(y),\quad t>0,\,x,y\in[0\,,L].

A measurable process
u=\{u(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0,L]}
is called a mild solution to (
1.1
) if it is adapted to the filtration
\{\mathscr{F}_{t}\}_{t\geqslant 0}
of the noise
\xi
and satisfies the integral equation

(2.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}u(t\,,x)=(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)&+\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\\
&+\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\end{split}

for any
(t\,,x)\in(0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]
.
It follows from standard existence and uniqueness theory that (
1.1
) has a unique mild solution
[
70
,
25
]
; see also
[
20
, Proposition 2.7]
.
Some moment estimates for the solution and its spatial and temporal increments will be established in Sections
3
and
4
.

The next lemma states a heat kernel estimate.
It follows from known Gaussian bounds on the heat kernel, but our main results and methods do not rely on the Gaussian bounds.
The estimate (
2.11
) below will be enough for our purposes.

Lemma 2.2
.

Under (
D
), (
N
) or (
R
), for any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

(2.11)

\displaystyle|G_{t}(x\,,y)|\leqslant C\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\wedge\frac{t}{|x-y|^{3}}\right)\quad\text{for all $t\in(0\,,T]$ and $x\,,y\in[0\,,L]$.}

Proof.

Under (
D
), there exist
C_{1}\,,C_{2}>0
such that

0\leqslant G_{t}(x\,,y)\leqslant\frac{C_{1}}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{C_{2}t}\right)\quad\forall t>0\,,x\,,y\in[0\,,L];

see
[
27
, Corollary 3.2.8]
.
Under (
N
), there exist
C_{3}\,,C_{4}>0
such that

0\leqslant G_{t}(x\,,y)\leqslant C_{3}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\vee 1\right)\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{C_{4}t}\right)\quad\forall t>0\,,x\,,y\in[0\,,L];

see
[
27
, Theorem 3.2.9]
or
[
9
, Proposition 3.6]
.
Under (
R
), for any
T>0
, there exist
C_{5}\,,C_{6}>0
such that

0\leqslant G_{t}(x\,,y)\leqslant\frac{C_{5}}{\sqrt{t}}\exp\left(-\frac{(x-y)^{2}}{C_{6}t}\right)\quad\forall t\in(0\,,T]\,,x\,,y\in[0\,,L];

see
[
20
, Lemma 4.3]
.
The inequality (
2.11
) follows from these estimates and the elementary property that
\sup_{z>0}z^{3/2}\exp(-z^{2})<\infty
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

For any
0<a<b
, there exists
C>0
such that

1.

|(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x^{\prime})|\leqslant C|x^{\prime}-x|
,

2.

|(G_{t^{\prime}}\ast u_{0})(x)-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)|\leqslant C|t^{\prime}-t|

uniformly for all
t\,,t^{\prime}\in[a\,,b]
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

Recall that
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0\,,L])
.
By (
2.9
), (
2.6
), mean value theorem, and (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\textstyle|(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x^{\prime})|=|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}(f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(x^{\prime}))\langle f_{n}\,,u_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\textstyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cn^{2}t}n|x-x^{\prime}|\|u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim|x-x^{\prime}|\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}t}z\,{\rm d}z\propto\tfrac{1}{t}|x-x^{\prime}|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{a}|x-x^{\prime}|

uniformly for all
t\in[a\,,b]
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle\textstyle|(G_{t^{\prime}}\ast u_{0})(x)-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)|=|\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}({\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t^{\prime}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})f_{n}(x)\langle f_{n}\,,u_{0}\rangle_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\textstyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}n^{2}|t^{\prime}-t|\lesssim|t^{\prime}-t|\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}t}z^{2}\,{\rm d}z\propto t^{-3/2}|t^{\prime}-t|\leqslant a^{-3/2}|t^{\prime}-t|

uniformly for all
t<t^{\prime}
in
[a\,,b]
and
x\in[0\,,L]
.
This completes the proof.
∎

The unique mild solution to (

\displaystyle w(t\,,x)=\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y),\quad t>0,\,x\in[0\,,L],

where

3.1.
Basic estimates

Lemma 3.1
.

\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t}(x\,,y)]^{2}{\rm d}y\lesssim t^{-1/2}
and
\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim\sqrt{t}
uniformly for all
t>0
and
x\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

By Parseval’s identity, (
2.6
), and (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t}(x\,,y)]^{2}{\rm d}y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}t}{\rm d}z\propto t^{-1/2}.

Replace
t
by
s
, and then integrate to finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that

1.

\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t}(x\,,y)-G_{t}(x^{\prime},y)]^{2}\,{\rm d}y\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}n^{2}\wedge 1)\,{\rm e}^{-cn^{2}t}
,

2.

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{s}(x\,,y)-G_{s}(x^{\prime},y)]^{2}\lesssim|x^{\prime}-x|

uniformly for all
t>0
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

The first inequality can be derived by applying Parseval’s identity, mean value theorem, (
2.6
), and (
2.7
):

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t}(x\,,y)-G_{t}(x^{\prime},y)]^{2}\,{\rm d}y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(x^{\prime})|^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}\left[\left({\|f_{n}^{\prime}\|}_{L^{\infty}}|x-x^{\prime}|\right)\wedge\left(2{\|f_{n}\|}_{L^{\infty}}\right)\right]^{2}\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cn^{2}t}(n^{2}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}\wedge 1).

It follows that

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{s}(x^{\prime},y)-G_{s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}z^{2}\wedge 1)\,{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}s}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}z^{2}\wedge 1)\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}s\,{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}s}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,(|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}\wedge z^{-2})

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{|x-x^{\prime}|^{-1}}|x-x^{\prime}|^{2}{\rm d}z+\int_{|x-x^{\prime}|^{-1}}^{\infty}z^{-2}{\rm d}z\lesssim|x-x^{\prime}|.\qed

Lemma 3.3
.

There exists a constant
c>0
such that

1.

\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t^{\prime}}(x\,,y)-G_{t}(x\,,y)]^{2}\,{\rm d}y\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(|t^{\prime}-t|^{2}n^{4}\wedge 1)\,{\rm e}^{-cn^{2}t}
,

2.

\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y[G_{t^{\prime}-s}(x\,,y)-G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim(t^{\prime}-t)^{1/2}
,

3.

\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{t^{\prime}-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim(t^{\prime}-t)^{1/2}

uniformly for all
0<t<t^{\prime}
and
x\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

Thanks to Parseval’s identity, (
2.7
), the elementary inequality
{\rm e}^{-a}-{\rm e}^{-b}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-a}((b-a)\wedge 1)
for all
0<a<b
, and property (
2.6
), we obtain:

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{L}[G_{t^{\prime}}(x\,,y)-G_{t}(x\,,y)]^{2}\,{\rm d}y=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}({\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t^{\prime}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})^{2}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\textstyle\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-2\lambda_{n}t}(|t^{\prime}-t|^{2}\lambda_{n}^{2}\wedge 1)\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-cn^{2}t}(|t^{\prime}-t|^{2}n^{4}\wedge 1).

We use the preceding to continue the computation:

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y[G_{t^{\prime}-s}(x\,,y)-G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,((t^{\prime}-t)^{2}z^{4}\wedge 1)\,{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}s}

\displaystyle\quad\textstyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,((t^{\prime}-t)^{2}z^{4}\wedge 1)\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\,{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}s}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,((t^{\prime}-t)^{2}z^{2}\wedge z^{-2})

\displaystyle\quad\textstyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{(t^{\prime}-t)^{-1/2}}(t^{\prime}-t)^{2}z^{2}\,{\rm d}z+\int_{(t^{\prime}-t)^{-1/2}}^{\infty}z^{-2}\,{\rm d}z\lesssim(t^{\prime}-t)^{1/2}.

Finally, we may use Parseval’s identity, (
2.7
), and the inequality
1-{\rm e}^{-x}\leqslant 1\wedge x
for all
x\geqslant 0
to deduce the last estimate:

\displaystyle\textstyle\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{t^{\prime}-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}s\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(t^{\prime}-s)}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\textstyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\int_{t}^{t^{\prime}}{\rm d}s\,{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}(t^{\prime}-s)}\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\,z^{-2}(1-{\rm e}^{-cz^{2}(t^{\prime}-t)})

\displaystyle\quad\textstyle\lesssim\int_{0}^{(t^{\prime}-t)^{-1/2}}(t^{\prime}-t)\,{\rm d}z+\int_{(t^{\prime}-t)^{-1/2}}^{\infty}z^{-2}\,{\rm d}z\lesssim(t^{\prime}-t)^{1/2}.\qed

Lemma 3.4
.

For any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

(3.3)

\displaystyle\Var(w(t\,,x))\leqslant C\sqrt{t}\quad\text{and}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\Var(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x))\leqslant C\left[\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))\wedge\sqrt{t\vee t^{\prime}}\right]

uniformly for all
t\,,t^{\prime}\in[0\,,T]
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

Wiener isometry and Lemma
3.1
yield (
3.3
).
Next, by Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
, there exists
c_{1}>0
such that for all
t\,,t^{\prime}\in[0\,,T]
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
,

(3.5)

\displaystyle\Var(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x))\leqslant c_{1}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})).

Since
\Var(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x))\leqslant 2\Var(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))+2\Var(w(t\,,x))
,
we may use (
3.3
) to finish the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.5
.

Under (
D
) or (
N
), for any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that

(3.6)

\displaystyle\Var(w(t\,,x))\leqslant C\,(\sqrt{t}\wedge f_{1}(x))\quad\text{and}

(3.7)

\displaystyle\Var(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x))\leqslant C\left[\rho((t\,,x)\,,(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))\wedge\sqrt{t\vee t^{\prime}}\wedge(f_{1}(x)\vee f_{1}(x^{\prime}))\right]

uniformly for all
t\,,t^{\prime}\in[0\,,T]
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

Thanks to Lemma
3.4
, there is nothing to prove under (
N
) since
f_{1}
is constant; see (
2.2
).
It remains to prove that
\Var(w(t\,,x))\lesssim f_{1}(x)
under (
D
). Indeed, by Wiener isometry and Parseval’s identity,

\displaystyle\Var(w(t\,,x))

\displaystyle\textstyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,G_{s}^{2}(x\,,y)

\displaystyle\textstyle=\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}s}|f_{n}(x)|^{2}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\lambda_{n}^{-1}(1-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})|f_{n}(x)|^{2}.

Using (
2.1
) and
|\sin(a)|\leqslant a
for
a\geqslant 0
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\textstyle\Var(w(t\,,x))\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2}\sin^{2}(\pi nx/L)\lesssim\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant L/(\pi x)}x^{2}+\sum_{n\geqslant L/(\pi x)}n^{-2}\lesssim x.

By symmetry,
\Var(w(t\,,x))\lesssim L-x
. Use
f_{1}(x)\asymp x\wedge(L-x)
to finish the proof.
∎

3.2.
Strong local non-determininsm

In this part, we prove that the Gaussian random field
w
which solves (
3.1
) is strongly locally non-deterministic (SLND).

We start with conditions (
D
) and (
N
).
Let us first recall that the Fourier transform of a function
f:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\hat{f}(\zeta)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm e}^{-i\zeta\cdot x}f(x){\rm d}x
for
\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and the inverse Fourier transform of
g:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\check{g}(x)=(2\pi)^{-d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\rm e}^{i\zeta\cdot x}g(\zeta){\rm d}\zeta
for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
We identify the torus as
\mathbb{T}\cong[-\pi\,,\pi]
.
The Fourier transform of a function
\Phi:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by
\hat{\Phi}(n)=\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}{\rm e}^{-in\theta}\Phi(\theta){\rm d}\theta
for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,
and the inverse Fourier transform of
\Psi:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
is
\check{\Psi}(\theta)=(2\pi)^{-1}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}{\rm e}^{in\theta}\Psi(n)
for
\theta\in\mathbb{T}
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Fix
T>0
. Then, under (
D
) or (
N
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(w(t\,,x)\mid w(t_{1}\,,x_{1})\,,\dots,w(t_{m}\,,x_{m}))\asymp\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t_{j}\,,x_{j}))\wedge\sqrt{t}\wedge f_{1}(x)

where
f_{1}
is the principal eigenfunction under (
D
) or (
N
), respectively, given in Lemma
2.1
, and the implied constants
do not depend on
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
nor
(t\,,x)\,,(t_{1}\,,x_{1})\,,

\dots,(t_{m}\,,x_{m})\in[0\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
.

Proof.

The upper bound follows from Lemma
3.5
and the fact that

\textstyle\Var(X|X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{m})=\inf\limits_{a_{1},\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}}\mathrm{E}\left[(X-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}X_{j})^{2}\right]

for any centered Gaussian vector
(X\,,X_{1}\,,\dots,X_{m})
.
To prove the lower bound, it suffices to show the existence of
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]}\geqslant C\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t_{j}\,,x_{j}))\wedge\sqrt{t}\wedge f_{1}(x)

uniformly for all
m\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
, for all
(t\,,x)\,,(t_{1}\,,x_{1})\,,\dots,(t_{m}\,,x_{m})\in[0\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
, and for all
a_{1}\,,\dots,a_{m}\in\mathbb{R}
. To this end, we first use (
3.2
), Wiener isometry, and (
2.9
) to write

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\textstyle=\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\left[G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}G_{t_{j}-s}(x_{j}\,,y)\mathbb{1}_{[0,t_{j}]}(s)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}s\left[{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(t-s)}f_{n}(x)\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}(s)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(t_{j}-s)}f_{n}(x_{j})\mathbb{1}_{[0,t_{j}]}(s)\right]^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}\left|({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})f_{n}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})f_{n}(x_{j})\right|^{2},

where the last equality follows from Plancherel’s theorem and the simple fact that the Fourier transform of
s\mapsto{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(t-s)}\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}(s)
is
\tau\mapsto({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})/(\lambda_{n}-i\tau)
.

Case 1:
Neumann boundary condition (
N
).
By (
2.2
),

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\textstyle=\frac{1}{4\pi L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}\Big|({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})({\rm e}^{in\pi x/L}+{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L})

\displaystyle\textstyle\hskip 72.26999pt-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})({\rm e}^{in\pi x_{j}/L}+{\rm e}^{-in\pi x_{j}/L})\Big|^{2}.

Let
\phi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
and
\psi:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be two smooth, nonnegative functions with
\supp{\phi}=[-\pi/2\,,\pi/2]
,
\supp{\psi}=[-T/2\,,T/2]
and
\phi(0)=\psi(0)=1
.
For any
r\in(0\,,1]
, define
\phi_{r}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
by
\phi_{r}(x)=r^{-1}\phi(r^{-1}x)
and
\psi_{r}:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
the same way.
Define
\Phi_{r}:\mathbb{T}\to\mathbb{R}
as the restriction of
\phi_{r}
, i.e.,
\Phi_{r}(\theta)=\phi_{r}(\theta)
for
\theta\in(-\pi\,,\pi]\cong\mathbb{T}
.
Let

(3.8)

\displaystyle\textstyle\varepsilon=\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|t-t_{j}|}{T}}\vee\frac{|x-x_{j}|}{L}\right)\wedge\sqrt{\frac{t}{T}}.

Note that
\varepsilon\in[0\,,1]
. If
\varepsilon=0
, there is nothing to prove, so we may assume that
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1]
.
Define

\displaystyle\textstyle I:=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\Big[({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})({\rm e}^{in\pi x/L}+{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L})

\displaystyle\textstyle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})({\rm e}^{in\pi x_{j}/L}+{\rm e}^{-in\pi x_{j}/L})\Big]{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L}{\rm e}^{i\tau t}\hat{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)\hat{\psi}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(\tau).

By Fourier inversion,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\textstyle=2\pi\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\Big[(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))(1+{\rm e}^{-2in\pi x/L})

\displaystyle\textstyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))({\rm e}^{in\pi(x_{j}-x)/L}-{\rm e}^{-in\pi(x_{j}+x)/L})\Big]\hat{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)

\displaystyle\textstyle=4\pi^{2}\Big[(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0)+\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\tfrac{2\pi x}{L}))

\displaystyle\textstyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\tfrac{\pi(x_{j}-x)}{L})+\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\tfrac{\pi(x_{j}+x)}{L}))\Big].

Note that
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)=\varepsilon^{-2}
,
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\phi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\varepsilon^{-1}
.
Observe from the definition of
\varepsilon
in (
3.8
) that
\varepsilon^{-2}t\geqslant T
, which implies
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)=0
since
\supp{\psi}=[-T/2\,,T/2]
.
Similarly, owing to (
3.8
), for each
j\in\{1\,,\dots,m\}
, we have
\varepsilon\leqslant\sqrt{|t-t_{j}|/T}
or
\varepsilon\leqslant|x-x_{j}|/L
, which implies that at least one of
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})
or
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\pi(x_{j}-x)/L)
is 0, hence
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\pi(x_{j}-x)/L)=0
.
Since
\phi\geqslant 0
, we have
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-2\pi x/L)\geqslant 0
.
Moreover, we observe that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\pi(x_{j}+x)/L)=0
.
Indeed, by the definition of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\frac{\pi(x_{j}+x)}{L})=\begin{cases}\phi_{\varepsilon}(-\frac{\pi(x_{j}+x)}{L})&\text{if }x_{j}+x\in[0\,,L],\\
\phi_{\varepsilon}(\frac{\pi(L-x_{j}+L-x)}{L})&\text{if }x_{j}+x\in(L\,,2L].\end{cases}

Since
x_{j}+x=|x_{j}-x|+2(x_{j}\wedge x)
and
L-x_{j}+L-x=|x_{j}-x|+2(L-(x_{j}\vee x))
are at least
\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}|x_{j}-x|
, this and (
3.8
) imply that
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\pi(x_{j}+x)/L)=0
.
The above observations imply that
I\geqslant 4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}
.
Therefore, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

\displaystyle\textstyle\varepsilon^{-6}\lesssim|I|^{2}\lesssim\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]\times J,

where

\textstyle J:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}(\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2})|\hat{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)\hat{\psi}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(\tau)|^{2}{\rm d}\tau.

By
\hat{\psi}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(\tau)=\hat{\psi}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)
,
\hat{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)=\hat{\phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)=\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon n)
, and by (
2.6
),

\displaystyle\textstyle J\lesssim\int_{0}^{\infty}{\rm d}z\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\,(z^{4}+\tau^{2})|\hat{\phi}(\varepsilon z)\hat{\psi}(\varepsilon^{2}\tau)|^{2}\propto\varepsilon^{-7},

where the last relation is due to scaling, and the proportionality constant is finite since
\hat{\phi}
and
\hat{\psi}
are rapidly decreasing functions.
It follows that

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]\gtrsim\varepsilon,

which yields to the desired lower bound since
f_{1}
is constant; see (
2.2
).

Case 2:
Dirichlet boundary condition (
D
).
By (
2.1
),

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\textstyle=\frac{1}{4\pi L}\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}\Big|({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})({\rm e}^{in\pi x/L}-{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L})

\displaystyle\textstyle\hskip 72.26999pt-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})({\rm e}^{in\pi x_{j}/L}-{\rm e}^{-in\pi x_{j}/L})\Big|^{2}.

Note that
f_{1}(x)\asymp x(L-x)/L^{2}
. We let

(3.9)

\displaystyle\textstyle\varepsilon=\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|t-t_{j}|}{T}}\vee\frac{|x-x_{j}|}{L}\right)\wedge\sqrt{\frac{t}{T}}\wedge\frac{x(L-x)}{L^{2}}.

Note that
\varepsilon\in[0\,,1]
.
Without loss of generality, assume
\varepsilon>0
.
Define
\psi\,,\phi\,,\Phi
and their scaled versions
\psi_{r}\,,\phi_{r}\,,\Phi_{r}
as in
Case 1
.
Define

\displaystyle\textstyle I:=

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\Big[({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})({\rm e}^{in\pi x/L}-{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L})

\displaystyle\textstyle-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})({\rm e}^{in\pi x_{j}/L}-{\rm e}^{-in\pi x_{j}/L})\Big]{\rm e}^{-in\pi x/L}{\rm e}^{i\tau t}\hat{\Phi}_{\varepsilon}(n)\hat{\psi}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(\tau).

By Fourier inversion,

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\textstyle=4\pi^{2}\Big[(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0)-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\tfrac{2\pi x}{L}))

\displaystyle\textstyle\quad-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}(\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}}\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))(\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\tfrac{\pi(x_{j}-x)}{L})-\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\tfrac{\pi(x_{j}+x)}{L}))\Big].

Again,
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)=\varepsilon^{-2}
,
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0)=\varepsilon^{-1}
,
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t)=0
and
\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})\Phi_{\varepsilon}(\pi(x_{j}-x)/L)=0
by the definition of
\varepsilon
in (
3.9
).
By the definition of
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\frac{2\pi x}{L})=\begin{cases}\phi_{\varepsilon}(-\frac{2\pi x}{L})&\text{if }x\in[0\,,L/2),\\
\phi_{\varepsilon}(\frac{2\pi(L-x)}{L})&\text{if }x\in[L/2\,,L].\end{cases}

In either case, we may use
\varepsilon\leqslant x(L-x)/L^{2}
and
\supp{\phi}=[-\pi/2\,,\pi/2]
to deduce that
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\frac{2\pi x}{L})=0
.
Moreover, as in
Case 1
, we have

\psi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})\Phi_{\varepsilon}(-\pi(x_{j}+x)/L)=0
.
It follows that
I=4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{-3}
. The rest of the proof is the same as in
Case 1
.
∎

We turn to the SLND property under (
R
).
The proof requires the lemma below.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
be the orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions given by Lemma
2.1
.
If
\phi\in C^{2}
has a compact support in
(0\,,L)
,
then the following holds in the sense of pointwise convergence:

(3.10)

\displaystyle\phi(x)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle f_{n}(x)\quad\text{for all $x\in[0\,,L]$.}

Proof.

This is standard. For completeness, we give a short proof.
Since
\phi\in C^{2}
and
\phi(0)=\phi(L)=0
, we may integrate by parts twice and use Lemma
2.1
to see that

(3.11)

\displaystyle\textstyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle|\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-2}<\infty.

From Lemma
2.1
, we see that for each
N\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
,
S_{N}:=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle f_{n}
is continuous on
[0\,,L]
, which converges uniformly to
S_{\infty}:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle f_{n}
because (
3.11
) and (
2.7
) imply that for
M>N
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\sup_{x\in[0,L]}|S_{M}(x)-S_{N}(x)|\leqslant\sum_{N<n\leqslant M}|\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle|\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+},x\in[0,L]}|f_{n}(x)|\to 0

as
M\,,N\to\infty
.
This shows that
S_{N}
converges pointwise to the limit
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle\phi\,,f_{n}\rangle f_{n}
which is also continuous, but
S_{N}
also converges to the limit
\phi
in
L^{2}
since
\{f_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
is an orthonormal basis.
Hence, both limits must agree. This and continuity of
\phi
ensure the pointwise convergence in (
3.10
).
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

Fix

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}(w(t\,,x)\mid w(t_{1}\,,x_{1})\,,\dots,w(t_{m}\,,x_{m}))\asymp\min_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t_{j}\,,x_{j}))\wedge\sqrt{t},

Proof.

The upper bound follows from Lemma

\displaystyle\textstyle{\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]}\geqslant C\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t_{j}\,,x_{j}))\wedge\sqrt{t}

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\textstyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}\left|({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})f_{n}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})f_{n}(x_{j})\right|^{2}.

Choose and fix two smooth nonnegative functions

For every

\displaystyle\phi_{r}(\tau)=r^{-1}\phi(r^{-1}\tau)\quad\text{and}\quad\psi_{x,r}(y)=r^{-1}\psi(r^{-1}(y-x)).

\displaystyle\textstyle\varepsilon=(\delta\wedge(L-\delta)\wedge 1)\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\left(\sqrt{\frac{|t-t_{j}|}{T}}\vee\frac{|x-x_{j}|}{L}\right)\wedge\sqrt{\frac{t}{T}}.

In particular, since

\displaystyle\supp\psi_{x,\varepsilon}\subset(0\,,L)\quad\text{and}\quad\supp\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\prime}\subset(0\,,L).

Without loss of generality, assume
\varepsilon>0

I

\displaystyle\textstyle I:=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}{\rm d}\tau\left[({\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t})f_{n}(x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}({\rm e}^{-i\tau t_{j}}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}})f_{n}(x_{j})\right]

\displaystyle\times{\rm e}^{i\tau t}\hat{\phi}_{\varepsilon^{2}}(\tau)\langle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}\,,f_{n}\rangle,

where

Using Fourier inversion to compute the

\displaystyle\textstyle I=2\pi\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(\phi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0)-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}\phi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t))f_{n}(x)\right.

\displaystyle\textstyle\quad\left.-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}(\phi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(t-t_{j})-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t_{j}}\phi_{\varepsilon^{2}}(0))f_{n}(x_{j}))\right]\langle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}\,,f_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\textstyle 4\pi^{2}\varepsilon^{-6}=|I|^{2}\leqslant 2\pi\,\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{n}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]\times J,

\displaystyle 2\langle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}\,,\lambda_{n}f_{n}\rangle

\displaystyle\textstyle=-\int_{0}^{L}\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)f_{n}^{\prime\prime}(y){\rm d}y

\displaystyle\textstyle=-\left[\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)f_{n}^{\prime}(y)\right]_{y=0}^{y=L}+\left[\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\prime}(y)f_{n}(y)\right]_{y=0}^{y=L}-\int_{0}^{L}\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(y)f_{n}(y){\rm d}y

3.13

\displaystyle J

\displaystyle\textstyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-2}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}\,,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}+\varepsilon^{-6}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\langle\psi_{x,\varepsilon}\,,f_{n}\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{L}|\psi_{x,\varepsilon}^{\prime\prime}(y)|^{2}{\rm d}y+\varepsilon^{-6}\int_{0}^{L}|\psi_{x,\varepsilon}(y)|^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\textstyle=\varepsilon^{-8}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(\varepsilon^{-1}(y-x))|^{2}{\rm d}y+\varepsilon^{-8}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(\varepsilon^{-1}(y-x))|^{2}{\rm d}y

\displaystyle\textstyle=\varepsilon^{-7}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi^{\prime\prime}(y)|^{2}{\rm d}y+\varepsilon^{-7}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|\psi(y)|^{2}{\rm d}y

Putting this back into (

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{E}\left[\left(w(t\,,x)-\sum_{j=1}^{m}a_{j}w(t_{j}\,,x_{j})\right)^{2}\right]\gtrsim\,\varepsilon\,\gtrsim\min\limits_{1\leqslant j\leqslant m}\rho^{2}((t\,,x)\,,(t_{j}\,,x_{j}))\wedge\sqrt{t}.

The proof is complete.
∎

3.3.
A series representation

Define
v=\{v(t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0,L]}
by

(3.19)

\displaystyle v(t\,,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{Re}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}}{\lambda_{n}-i\tau}W_{n}({\rm d}\tau),

where
W_{n}=W^{(1)}_{n}+iW^{(2)}_{n}
and
\{W^{(1)}_{n},W^{(2)}_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}}
are i.i.d. white noises on
\mathbb{R}
.
Then
v
is a centered Gaussian random field.
The next lemma shows that
v
has the same law as the solution
w
to (
3.1
).

Lemma 3.12
.

The process
v
has the same law as the solution
w
to (
3.1
).

Proof.

Since
v
and
w
are both centered Gaussian processes, it suffices to show that they have the same covariance function.
Indeed, for every
t\,,s\geqslant 0
and
x\,,y\in[0\,,L]
, by independence of
\{W_{n}\,,n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}
, Wiener isometry, and Plancherel’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[v(t\,,x)v(s\,,y)]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(y)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}}{\lambda_{n}-i\tau}\right)\overline{\left(\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\tau s}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}s}}{\lambda_{n}-i\tau}\right)}\,{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)f_{n}(y)\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left({\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(t-r)}\mathbb{1}_{[0,t]}(r)\right)\left({\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}(s-r)}\mathbb{1}_{[0,s]}(r)\right){\rm d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t\wedge s}{\rm d}r\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}z\,G_{t-r}(x\,,z)G_{s-r}(y\,,z)=\mathrm{E}[w(t\,,x)w(s\,,y)],

where the last line follows from (
2.9
) and (
3.2
).
Hence,
v
and
w
have the same law.
∎

For any Borel subset
A
of
[0\,,\infty)
,
t\geqslant 0
, and
x\in[0\,,L]
, define

\displaystyle v(A\,,t\,,x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}f_{n}(x)\,\mathrm{Re}\int_{\sqrt{n}\vee|\tau|^{1/4}\in A}\frac{{\rm e}^{-i\tau t}-{\rm e}^{-\lambda_{n}t}}{\lambda_{n}-i\tau}W_{n}({\rm d}\tau).

We now verify that Assumption 2.1 of Lee and Xiao
[
53
]
is satisfied.

Lemma 3.13
.

If
A
and
B
are disjoint subsets of
[0\,,\infty)
, then
\{v(A\,,t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0,L]}
and
\{v(B\,,t\,,x)\}_{t\geqslant 0,x\in[0,L]}
are independent.
Moreover, for any
T>0
, there exists
C>0
such that for all
0\leqslant a<b\leqslant\infty
, for all
(t\,,x)\,,(s\,,y)\in[0\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
,

\displaystyle\|v(t\,,x)-v([a\,,b),t\,,x)-v(s\,,y)+v([a\,,b),s\,,y)\|_{2}\leqslant C(a^{3}|t-s|+a|x-y|+\tfrac{1}{b}).

Proof.

The first statement concerning independence is clear.
To show the second, we start with the following decomposition:

\displaystyle v(t\,,x)-v([a\,,b),t\,,x)-v(s\,,y)+v([a\,,b),s\,,y)

\displaystyle=\left[v([0\,,a),t\,,x)-v([0\,,a),s\,,y)\right]+\left[v([b\,,\infty),t\,,x)-v([b\,,\infty),s\,,y)\right].

For the first component, Wiener isometry yields

\displaystyle\|v([0\,,a),t\,,x)-v([0\,,a),s\,,y)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant a^{2}}\int_{|\tau|<a^{4}}\frac{\left|f_{n}(x)(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-f_{n}(y)(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}{\rm d}\tau.

By triangle inequality, mean value theorem, and (
2.7
),

\displaystyle\left|f_{n}(x)(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-f_{n}(y)(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})\right|

\displaystyle\leqslant\left|f_{n}(x)-f_{n}(y)\right||e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t}|+\left|f_{n}(y)\right|\left|(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim n|x-y|+\left(|\tau|+\lambda_{n}\right)|t-s|.

This together with (
2.6
) implies that

\displaystyle\|v([0\,,a),t\,,x)-v([0\,,a),s\,,y)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant a^{2}}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{n^{2}|x-y|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}{\rm d}\tau+\int_{|\tau|<a^{4}}\frac{(|\tau|+\lambda_{n})^{2}}{\tau^{2}+\lambda_{n}^{2}}|t-s|^{2}{\rm d}\tau\right]

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant a^{2}}\left[\frac{n^{2}}{\lambda_{n}}|x-y|^{2}+a^{4}|t-s|^{2}\right]\lesssim a^{2}|x-y|^{2}+a^{6}|t-s|^{2}.

For the other component, we use the property that
f_{n}(x)(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-f_{n}(y)(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})
is bounded (see (
2.7
)) and (
2.6
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\|v([b\,,\infty),t\,,x)-v([b\,,\infty),s\,,y)\|_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{n\geqslant b^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\left|f_{n}(x)(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-f_{n}(y)(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant b^{2}}\int_{|\tau|\geqslant b^{4}}\frac{\left|f_{n}(x)(e^{-i\tau t}-e^{-\lambda_{n}t})-f_{n}(y)(e^{-i\tau s}-e^{-\lambda_{n}s})\right|^{2}}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}{\rm d}\tau

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\geqslant b^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}+\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant b^{2}}\int_{|\tau|\geqslant b^{4}}\frac{{\rm d}\tau}{\lambda_{n}^{2}+\tau^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{n\geqslant b^{2}}\lambda_{n}^{-1}+\sum_{1\leqslant n\leqslant b^{2}}b^{-4}\lesssim\int_{b^{2}}^{\infty}\frac{{\rm d}z}{z^{2}}+b^{-2}\lesssim b^{-2}.

Combining both parts together, we complete the proof.
∎

3.4.
Spatio-temporal increments

The theorem below establishes the exact local and uniform spatio-temporal moduli of continuity for the solution to (

Theorem 3.14
.

For any fixed point
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in[0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)

K_{0}=K_{0}(z_{0})\in(0\,,\infty)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|w(z)-w(z_{0})|}{\rho(z\,,z_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z_{0}))}}=K_{0}\quad\text{a.s.}

I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]

0<a<T
and

K=K(a\,,T\,,c\,,d)\in(0\,,\infty)

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}}\frac{|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|}{\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}}=K\quad\text{a.s.}

\sqrt{12c_{2}}\leqslant K\leqslant\sqrt{12c_{1}}

When

Proof.

Suppose first
t_{0}>0

Thanks to SLND (Proposition

The case that

We aim to show (

z_{0}=(0\,,x_{0})
with
0<x_{0}<L

3.21

Let

Define

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{d(z\,,z^{\prime})}{\phi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=0\quad\text{and}\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{d(z\,,z^{\prime})}{\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=0.

) and (

) hold for some constants

In the remainder of the proof, we show that

and

.

First,

can be shown by the following argument using metric entropy and concentration of measure.
For any set

Then, for any

. Clearly, the quantity in (
3.20
) is no less than the above quantity, and hence
K_{0}\geqslant K_{2}>0
.

It remains to show that
K=K(0\,,T\,,c\,,d)\in(0\,,\infty)
.
It is possible to directly use the form of SLND in Lemmas
3.6
and
3.8
and follow
[
51
,
53
]
to prove that
K>0
.
Alternatively, we may simply use the
a>0
case in the beginning of this proof to deduce that
K=K(0\,,T\,,c\,,d)\geqslant K(T/2\,,T\,,c\,,d)>0
.
To show that
K<\infty
, we use again a metric entropy argument,
which shows that
N(I\,,r)\lesssim r^{-6}
.
Set
\varepsilon_{n}={\rm e}^{-n}
.
By Theorem 1.3.5 of
[
1
]
, there exist
C_{5}\,,C_{6}\,,C_{7}\,,C_{8}>0
such that, a.s., for all large
n
,

\displaystyle\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:d(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}}|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{5}\int_{0}^{\varepsilon_{n}}\sqrt{\log N(I\,,r)}\,{\rm d}r\leqslant C_{6}\int_{0}^{{\rm e}^{-n}}\sqrt{\log(C_{6}/r)}\,{\rm d}r

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{7}\int_{\sqrt{\log(C_{6}{\rm e}^{n})}}^{\infty}s^{2}{\rm e}^{-s^{2}}{\rm d}s\leqslant C_{8}{\rm e}^{-n}\sqrt{\log(C_{6}{\rm e}^{n})},

where the last inequality follows from the fact that
\int_{a}^{\infty}s^{2}{\rm e}^{-s^{2}}{\rm d}s\lesssim a{\rm e}^{-a^{2}}
as
a\to\infty
, and
C_{5}\,,C_{6}\,,C_{7}\,,C_{8}
are universal constants that do not depend on
n
.
This together with Lemma
3.4
implies that, a.s.,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I\\
\varepsilon_{n+1}\leqslant\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|}{\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I\\
\varepsilon_{n+1}\leqslant\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|}{\varepsilon_{n+1}\sqrt{\log(1/\varepsilon_{n+1})}}

\displaystyle\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I\\
0<d(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|}{{\rm e}^{-n-1}\sqrt{n+1}}\leqslant\lim_{n\to\infty}\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I\\
0<d(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon_{n}\end{subarray}}\frac{C_{8}{\rm e}^{-n}\sqrt{\log(C_{6}{\rm e}^{n})}}{{\rm e}^{-n-1}\sqrt{n+1}}\leqslant C_{8}{\rm e}.

This implies that
K\leqslant C_{8}{\rm e}<\infty
.
∎

The next result yields matching bounds on small-ball probabilities and a Chung-type law of the iterated logarithm for spatio-temporal increments of
w
.

Theorem 3.15
.

For every fixed
z_{0}=(t_{0}\,,x_{0})\in[0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
, there exist constants
0<c_{0}<c_{1}<\infty
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<r<1
,

(3.22)

\displaystyle{\rm e}^{-c_{1}(r/\varepsilon)^{6}}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},r)}|w(z)-w(z_{0})|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-c_{0}(r/\varepsilon)^{6}}

and

(3.23)

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\frac{(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))^{1/6}}{\varepsilon}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|w(z)-w(z_{0})|=C_{2}\quad\text{a.s.}

where
C_{2}
is a constant such that
c_{0}^{1/6}\leqslant C_{2}\leqslant c_{1}^{1/6}
.

Proof.

Suppose first
t_{0}>0
.
Thanks to SLND (Proposition
3.10
) and Lemma
3.13
, we may apply Proposition 4.2 and Theorem 4.4 of Lee and Xiao
[
53
]
to obtain (
3.22
) and (
3.23
).

Now suppose
t_{0}=0
. Let
r\in(0\,,1]
.
Keeping in mind that
z_{0}=(0\,,x_{0})
and
w(z_{0})=0
,
we can show as in the proof of Theorem
3.14
that there exists
C>0
such that
N(B_{\rho}(z_{0}\,,r)\,,\varepsilon)\leqslant\Psi_{r}(\varepsilon):=C(r/\varepsilon)^{6}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,r]
.
Then, by a small-ball probability estimate of Talagrand
[
67
, Lemma 2.2]
(see also
[
24
, Lemma 3.4]
for a more precise statement), there exists a universal constant
K>0
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,r)
,

\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},r)}|w(z)|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\geqslant\exp\left(-\frac{\Psi_{r}(\varepsilon)}{K}\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{C}{K}\left(\frac{r}{\varepsilon}\right)^{6}\right).

Next, we apply SLND to establish a reverse inequality.
Let
0<\varepsilon<r<1
and define a finite subset
F
of
B_{\rho}(z_{0}\,,r)=[0\,,r^{4}]\times[x_{0}-r^{2}\,,x_{0}+r^{2}]
by

F=B_{\rho}(z_{0}\,,r)\cap\{(k_{1}\varepsilon^{4}\,,k_{2}\varepsilon^{2}):k_{1}\,,k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\}.

Then
\#F\asymp(r/\varepsilon)^{6}
and
\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\geqslant\varepsilon
for any pair of distinct
z\,,z^{\prime}\in F
.
Assign an order to the points in
F
and label them as
z_{1}\,,z_{2}\,,\dots,z_{n}
.
Then, by conditioning and Anderson’s shifted-ball inequality
[
2
]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},r)}|w(z)|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\max_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n}|w(z_{i})|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}\left[\mathbb{1}_{\big\{\max\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}|w(z_{i})|\leqslant\varepsilon\big\}}\mathrm{P}\left\{|w(z_{n})|\leqslant\varepsilon\mid w(z_{1})\,,\dots,w(z_{n-1})\right\}\right]

\displaystyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\max\limits_{1\leqslant i\leqslant n-1}|w(z_{i})|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\mathrm{P}\left\{|Z|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{[\Var(w(z_{n})\mid w(z_{1})\,,\dots,w(z_{n-1}))]^{1/2}}\right\},

where
Z
has a standard normal distribution.
Thanks to SLND (Lemmas
3.6
and
3.8
), there exists
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{|Z|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{[\Var(w(z_{n})\mid w(z_{1})\,,\dots,w(z_{n-1}))]^{1/2}}\right\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{|Z|\leqslant c_{2}^{-1/2}\right\}.

In fact, by Lemmas
3.6
and
3.8
,
\Var(w(z_{i})\mid w(z_{1})\,,\dots,w(z_{i-1}))\geqslant c_{2}\varepsilon^{2}
for every
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.
Hence, by induction, we can find
c\,,c_{0}>0
such that for all
0<\varepsilon<r<1
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},r)}|w(z)|\leqslant\varepsilon\right\}\leqslant\left(\mathrm{P}\left\{|Z|\leqslant c_{2}^{-1/2}\right\}\right)^{n}={\rm e}^{-cn}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-c_{0}(r/\varepsilon)^{6}}.

Next, we aim to show (
3.23
) for
t_{0}=0
.
Thanks to Lemma
3.13
and a zero-one law of Lee and Xiao
[
53
, Lemma 3.1]
, (
3.23
) holds for some constant
C_{2}\in[0\,,\infty]
.
Let
\varepsilon_{n}={\rm e}^{-n}
.
Thanks to the upper bound in (
3.22
),

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}|w(z)|\leqslant C\varepsilon_{n}(\log\log(1/\varepsilon_{n}))^{-1/6}\right\}\leqslant\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}n^{-c_{0}/C^{6}},

which is convergent provided that
C
is any fixed number so that
0<C<c_{0}^{1/6}
.
It follows by Borel-Cantelli lemma that
C_{2}\geqslant C
.
Letting
C\uparrow c_{0}^{1/6}
yields
C_{2}\geqslant c_{0}^{1/6}
.
It remains to show that
C_{2}\leqslant c_{1}^{1/6}
.
We follow the proof of
[
53
, Theorem 4.4]
.
Fix
\delta\in(0\,,1)
. For any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\varepsilon_{n}=\exp(-(n^{\delta}+n^{1+\delta}))
and
b_{n}=\exp(n^{1+\delta})
.
Recall the Gaussian random field
v
defined in (
3.19
).
For any
z\in[0\,,\infty)\times[0\,,L]
, define
v_{n}(z)=v([b_{n}\,,b_{n+1})\,,z)
and
\tilde{v}_{n}(z)=v([0\,,\infty)\setminus[b_{n}\,,b_{n+1})\,,z)
, so that
v(z)=v_{n}(z)+\tilde{v}_{n}(z)
.
Write
h(\varepsilon)=\varepsilon(\log\log(1/\varepsilon))^{-1/6}
.
Since
v_{n}
and
\tilde{v}_{n}
are independent, we may apply conditionally Anderson’s inequality
[
2
]
, Lemma
3.12
, and the lower bound in (
3.22
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}|v_{n}(z)|\leqslant Ch(\varepsilon_{n})\right\}\geqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}|v_{n}(z)+\tilde{v}_{n}(z)|\leqslant Ch(\varepsilon_{n})\right\}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}|w(z)|\leqslant Ch(\varepsilon_{n})\right\}\geqslant\exp\left(-c_{1}\left(\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{Ch(\varepsilon_{n})}\right)^{6}\right)\gtrsim n^{-(1+\delta)c_{1}/C^{6}}.

Since
v_{1}\,,v_{2}\,,\dots
are independent, we may take
C=((1+\delta)c_{1})^{1/6}
and apply the second Borel-Cantelli lemma to see that

(3.24)

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}\frac{|v_{n}(z)|}{h(\varepsilon_{n})}\leqslant((1+\delta)c_{1})^{1/6}\quad\text{a.s.}

Thanks to Lemma
3.13
, we can follow the proof of
[
53
, Theorem 4.4]
using a metric entropy method with a concentration inequality to show that

(3.25)

\displaystyle\limsup_{n\to\infty}\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon_{n})}\frac{|\tilde{v}_{n}(z)|}{h(\varepsilon_{n})}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

Combining (
3.24
) and (
3.25
) and letting
\delta\to 0^{+}
shows that
C_{2}\leqslant c_{1}^{1/6}
.
∎

4.
Linearization error

Recall the mild formulation (
2.10
) of the SPDE (
1.1
) and the solution
w
to the linear SPDE (
3.1
).
For any
t\,,t^{\prime}\in[0\,,\infty)
and
x\,,x^{\prime}\in[0\,,L]
, define

(4.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathscr{E}(t\,,x\,;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=u(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-u(t\,,x)&-[(G_{t^{\prime}}\ast u_{0})(x^{\prime})-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)]\\
&-\sigma(u(t\,,x))(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x)).\end{split}

The random variable
\mathscr{E}(t\,,x\,;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
measures the linearization error of the spatio-temporal increments of the solution from
(t\,,x)
to
(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})
.
In order to simplify the notation, we let

(4.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\tilde{u}(t\,,x):=u(t\,,x)-(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x)&=\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y\\
&\;\;+\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y)\end{split}

so that

\mathscr{E}(t\,,x\,;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})=\tilde{u}(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-\tilde{u}(t\,,x)-\sigma(u(t\,,x))(w(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})-w(t\,,x)).

4.1.
Moment estimates

Proposition 4.1
.

There is a number
\zeta>1
such that the following statement holds.
If
b
and
\sigma
are bounded, then for any
0<a<T
, there exists
C>0
such that

(4.3)

\displaystyle\|\mathscr{E}(t\,,x\,;t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\|_{k}\leqslant Ck[\rho((t\,,x)\,,(t^{\prime},x^{\prime}))]^{\zeta}

uniformly for all
(t\,,x)\,,(t^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in I:=[a\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
and
k\in[2\,,\infty)
.
This remains valid when
I=[0\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
for fixed
T>0
and
0\leqslant c<d\leqslant L
if (
1.6
) holds.

The rest of Section
4.1
is devoted to proving Proposition
4.1
.
We first establish some lemmas.

Lemma 4.2
.

If
b
and
\sigma
are bounded, then for any
0<a<T
, there exists
C>0
such that
\sup_{t\in[a,T],x\in[0,L]}\|u(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leqslant C\sqrt{k}
for all
k\in[2\,,\infty)
.

Proof.

Write
u(t\,,x)=I_{0}+I_{1}+I_{2}
, where

\displaystyle I_{0}\textstyle=(G_{t}\ast u_{0})(x),\quad I_{1}\textstyle=\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)b(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y,

\displaystyle I_{2}\textstyle=\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}G_{t-s}(x\,,y)\sigma(u(s\,,y))\,\xi({\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y).

First, it is easy show that
I_{0}
is bounded on
[a\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
using (
2.9
),
u_{0}\in L^{2}([0\,,L])
, and Lemma
2.2
.
Next, by Minkowski’s inequality, the boundedness of
b
, Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, and Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\|_{k}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,|G_{t-s}(x\,,y)|\|b(u(s\,,y))\|_{k}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,|G_{s}(x\,,y)|

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\sqrt{L}\left[\int_{0}^{L}|G_{s}(x\,,y)|^{2}{\rm d}y\right]^{1/2}\lesssim\int_{0}^{t}s^{-1/4}{\rm d}s\lesssim t^{3/4}.

Finally, by the Burkholder-Davis-Gundy (BDG) inequality
[
42
, Proposition 4.4]
, the boundedness of
\sigma
, and Lemma
3.1
,

\displaystyle\|I_{2}\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant k\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\|\sigma(u(s\,,y))\|_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\textstyle\lesssim k\int_{0}^{t}{\rm d}s\int_{0}^{L}{\rm d}y\,[G_{s}(x\,,y)]^{2}\lesssim k\sqrt{t}.

Combine the estimates to finish the proof.
∎

b
and

\displaystyle\|\tilde{u}(t\,,x^{\prime})-\tilde{u}(t\,,x)\|_{k}\leqslant C\sqrt{k}\,|x^{\prime}-x|^{1/2}

\displaystyle\textstyle=\int_{(0,t)\times[0,L]}[G_{t-s}(x^{\prime},y)-G_{t-s}(x\,,y)]b(u(s\,,y))\,{\rm d}s\,{\rm d}y,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle\sup_{z,z\in I}\mathrm{E}\left[\exp\left(\gamma_{1}\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|}{[\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})]^{\zeta}}\right)\right]<\infty,

Proof.

Thanks to Proposition

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
\zeta>1
be given by Proposition
4.1
.
If
b
and
\sigma
are bounded, then for any fixed
0<a<T
and
p\in(0\,,\zeta]
, there exists
C>0
such that

(4.11)

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}z,z^{\prime}\in I:\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon\end{subarray}}|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|>h\varepsilon^{p}\right\}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-6(p+\zeta)}\exp\left(-\frac{h\wedge h^{2}}{C\varepsilon^{\zeta-p}\log_{+}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}\right)

uniformly for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1]
and
h>0
, where
I=[a\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
(or
I=[0\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
with
0\leqslant c<d\leqslant L
if (
1.6
) holds).

Proof.

Write
I=[0\,,T]\times[0\,,L]
.
Define
L_{\sigma}=\sup_{u,v\in\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(u)-\sigma(v)|/|u-v|
and
M_{\sigma}=\sup_{u\in\mathbb{R}}|\sigma(u)|
.
Let
h>0
and
\varepsilon\in(0\,,1]
.
The proof uses an interpolation argument.
Let
\delta\in(0\,,\varepsilon]
be a number to be determined, and define

\displaystyle J=\left\{(t\,,x)\in I\,:\,\exists\,k_{1}\,,k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}_{+}\,,t=k_{1}\delta^{4}\text{ and }x=k_{2}\delta^{2}\right\}.

Let
A
denote the event appearing on the left-hand side of (
4.11
).
Consider the events
B_{0}
and
B_{1}
defined by

\displaystyle B_{0}=\left\{\max_{q,q^{\prime}\in J:\rho(q,q^{\prime})\leqslant 3\varepsilon}|\mathscr{E}(q\,;q^{\prime})|>\frac{h\varepsilon^{p}}{2}\right\}\quad\text{and}\quad B_{1}=B_{2}\cap B_{3}\cap B_{4},

where

\displaystyle B_{2}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall q\in J\,,\sup_{q^{\prime}:\rho(q,q^{\prime})\leqslant\delta}|\tilde{u}(q)-\tilde{u}(q^{\prime})|\leqslant\frac{(\sqrt{h}\wedge h)\varepsilon^{p}}{2(2+2M_{\sigma}+L_{\sigma})}\right\},

\displaystyle B_{3}

\displaystyle=\left\{\forall q\in J\,,\sup_{q^{\prime}:\rho(q,q^{\prime})\leqslant\delta}|w(q)-w(q^{\prime})|\leqslant\frac{(\sqrt{h}\wedge h)\varepsilon^{p}}{2(2+2M_{\sigma}+L_{\sigma})}\right\},

\displaystyle B_{4}

\displaystyle=\left\{\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}|w(z)-w(z^{\prime})|\leqslant\sqrt{h}\wedge h\right\}

Suppose that
A
and
B_{1}
both occur.
Then, in particular, there exist
z\,,z^{\prime}\in I
with
\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon
such that
|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|>h\varepsilon^{p}
.
For any
q\,,q^{\prime}\in J
,

\displaystyle\mathscr{E}(q\,;q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})+\tilde{u}(q)-\tilde{u}(z)-\tilde{u}(q^{\prime})+\tilde{u}(z^{\prime})-\sigma(u(q))(w(q^{\prime})-w(z^{\prime}))

\displaystyle\quad+\sigma(u(q))(w(q)-w(z))-[\sigma(u(q))-\sigma(u(z))](w(z^{\prime})-w(z)),

so triangle inequality implies that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{E}(q\,;q^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\geqslant|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|-|\tilde{u}(q)-\tilde{u}(z)|-|\tilde{u}(q^{\prime})-\tilde{u}(z^{\prime})|-M_{\sigma}|w(q^{\prime})-w(z^{\prime})|

\displaystyle\quad-M_{\sigma}|w(q)-w(z)|-L_{\sigma}|u(q)-u(z)||w(z^{\prime})-w(z)|.

Now, if we take
q\in J
to be the closest point to
z
and
q^{\prime}\in J
to be the closest point to
z^{\prime}
, then
\rho(q\,,q^{\prime})\leqslant\rho(q\,,z)+\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})+\rho(z^{\prime},q^{\prime})\leqslant\delta+\varepsilon+\delta\leqslant 3\varepsilon
, and since

occurs, it follows that

\displaystyle|\mathscr{E}(q\,;q^{\prime})|\geqslant h\varepsilon^{p}-\frac{(2+2M_{\sigma}+L_{\sigma})h\varepsilon^{p}}{2(2+2M_{\sigma}+L_{\sigma})}=\frac{h\varepsilon^{p}}{2}.

This shows that

, hence

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{A\}=\mathrm{P}\{A\cap B_{1}\}+\mathrm{P}\{A\cap B_{1}^{c}\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\{B_{0}\}+\mathrm{P}\{B_{1}^{c}\}.

Set
\delta=\varepsilon^{r}
, where

.
Then, by a union bound, Chebyshev’s inequality, Lemma
4.8
, and
\#J\lesssim\delta^{-6}
, there exists

such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{B_{0}\}

\displaystyle\leqslant(\#J)^{2}\sup_{q,q^{\prime}\in J:\rho(q,q^{\prime})\leqslant 3\varepsilon}\mathrm{P}\left\{\frac{|\mathscr{E}(q\,;q^{\prime})|}{[\rho(q\,,q^{\prime})]^{\zeta}}>\frac{h\varepsilon^{p}}{2(3\varepsilon)^{\zeta}}\right\}

\displaystyle\leqslant C_{1}\delta^{-12}\exp\left(-\frac{h\varepsilon^{p}}{C_{1}\varepsilon^{\zeta}}\right)=C_{1}\varepsilon^{-12r}\exp\left(-\frac{h}{C_{1}\varepsilon^{\zeta-p}}\right).

Similarly, thanks to Lemma
4.5
, there exists
C_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{B_{1}^{c}\}\leqslant\mathrm{P}\{B_{2}^{c}\}+\mathrm{P}\{B_{3}^{c}\}+\mathrm{P}\{B_{4}^{c}\}

\displaystyle\lesssim\delta^{-6}\exp\left(-\frac{(h\wedge h^{2})\varepsilon^{2p}}{C_{2}\delta^{2}\log_{+}(\frac{1}{\delta})}\right)+\delta^{-6}\exp\left(-\frac{(h\wedge h^{2})\varepsilon^{2p}}{C_{2}\delta^{2}\log_{+}(\frac{1}{\delta})}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{h\wedge h^{2}}{C_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\log_{+}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}\right)

\displaystyle\lesssim\varepsilon^{-6r}\exp\left(-\frac{h\wedge h^{2}}{C_{2}r\varepsilon^{2(r-p)}\log_{+}(1/\varepsilon)}\right)+\exp\left(-\frac{h\wedge h^{2}}{C_{2}\varepsilon^{2}\log_{+}(1/\varepsilon)}\right).

We may optimize by choosing

so that
2(r-p)=\zeta-p
.
Then, combining the last two displays, we see that there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{A\}\leqslant C\varepsilon^{-12r}\exp\left(-\frac{h\wedge h^{2}}{C\varepsilon^{\zeta-p}\log_{+}(1/\varepsilon)}\right).

This completes the proof of (
4.11
).
∎

Proposition 4.10

be the number given by Proposition

.

and

Fix

and

.

\sigma(x)&\text{if $-N\leqslant x\leqslant N$,}\\

as the solution to (
1.1
) but with

and

and

generated by the noise

Uniqueness of the solution to (

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}\{u_{N}(t\,,x)=u(t\,,x)\text{ for all $t<\tau_{N}$ and $x\in[0\,,L]$}\}=1.

and the last line follows from (
5.2
) and (
5.8
).
It follows from the preceding, (
3.22
), and Proposition
4.9
that for all
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}\right\}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|w(z)-w(z_{0})|\leqslant\frac{K_{1}\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/6}}\right\}+\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|\mathscr{E}(z_{0}\,;z)|>\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/6}}\right\}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\exp\left(-c_{0}K_{1}^{-6}\phi(\varepsilon)\right)+C\varepsilon^{-6(1+\zeta)}\exp\left(-\frac{1}{C\varepsilon^{\zeta-1}(\phi(\varepsilon))^{1/3}\log_{+}(1/\varepsilon)}\right).

Take
C_{0}=c_{0}K_{1}^{-6}/2
.
By (
1.8
), we can find
\varepsilon_{2}\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{1})
such that for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{2})
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}\right\}\leqslant{\rm e}^{-C_{0}\phi(\varepsilon)}.

Next, let
K_{2}=1/(2(1+M_{\sigma}))
. For
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{2})
and
z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0}\,,\varepsilon)
, if
|w(z)-w(z_{0})|\leqslant K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}
and
|\mathscr{E}(z_{0}\,;z)|\leqslant K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}
, then by (
4.1
) and (
5.2
),

(5.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|&\leqslant K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}+M_{\sigma}K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}+2K_{0}\varepsilon^{2}\\
&\leqslant(1+M_{\sigma})K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}+\tfrac{1}{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}\leqslant\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}.\end{split}

Hence, we can obtain in a similar way a reverse inequality for the small-ball probability for
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{2})
using (
3.22
) and Proposition
4.9
:

\displaystyle\textstyle\exp\left(-c_{1}K_{2}^{-6}\phi(\varepsilon)\right)\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|w(z)-w(z_{0})|\leqslant K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}\right\}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/6}}\right\}+\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|\mathscr{E}(z_{0}\,;z)|>\frac{K_{2}\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/6}}\right\}

\displaystyle\textstyle\leqslant\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\frac{\varepsilon}{\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/6}}\right\}+C\varepsilon^{-6(1+\zeta)}\exp\left(-\frac{K_{2}^{2}}{C\varepsilon^{\zeta-1}\phi(\varepsilon)^{1/3}\log_{+}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon})}\right).

Let
C_{1}=2c_{1}K_{2}^{-6}
.
Thanks to (
1.8
) again, we may choose another small number
\varepsilon_{0}\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{2})
to ensure that for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{0})
,

\displaystyle\textstyle\mathrm{P}\left\{\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}\right\}\geqslant{\rm e}^{-C_{1}\phi(\varepsilon)}.

This proves (
3.22
).

Finally, if
t_{0}=0
, then under (
1.8
) and (
1.10
),
we can find
K_{0}^{\prime}>0
and
\varepsilon_{1}\in(0\,,1]
such that
\varepsilon^{q-1}\leqslant(2K_{0}^{\prime})^{-1}(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}
for all
\varepsilon\in(0\,,\varepsilon_{1}]
, and the inequality (
5.9
) becomes

\displaystyle|w(z)-w(z_{0})|\leqslant m_{\sigma}^{-1}\left(2\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}+K_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{q}\right)\leqslant K_{1}^{\prime}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6},

where
K_{1}^{\prime}=3m_{\sigma}^{-1}/2
, hence the same proof above leads to the lower bound in (
1.9
).
Similarly, the inequality (
5.10
) becomes

|u(z)-u(z_{0})|\leqslant(1+M_{\sigma})K_{2}\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6}+K_{0}^{\prime}\varepsilon^{q}\leqslant\varepsilon(\phi(\varepsilon))^{-1/6},

and hence the same proof yields the upper bound in (
1.9
).
∎

Proof of Theorem

Proof.

Fix
z_{0}\in(0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,L)
and write

(5.11)

) yields

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\varphi(\varepsilon)\sup_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}|u(z)-u(z_{0})|

) in Theorem
3.15
.

) still holds under the additional assumption (

).

4.10
and (

) in Theorem

.

6.
Proofs for the open KPZ equation

6.1.
Proof of Theorem
1.11

Proof.

Fix
z_{0}\in[0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,1)
and
\epsilon_{0}\in(0\,,1)
such that
B_{\rho}(z_{0}\,,\epsilon_{0})\subset[0\,,\infty)\times(0\,,1)
.
The random field
u
is the solution to (
1.1
) with
b=0
and
\sigma(u)=u
.
Since
u
is continuous and strictly positive
[
20
, Proposition 2.7]
, this implies that
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}=\{0\}
is polar for
u
and

\Delta_{0}:=\inf_{z\in B_{\rho}(z_{0},\epsilon_{0})}u(z)
is a strictly positive random variable.
We adopt the idea of
[
31
]
to argue as follows.
By Taylor expansion, for any
u\,,\bar{u}>0
,

\log{\bar{u}}=\log{u}+\frac{\bar{u}-u}{u}-\frac{(\bar{u}-u)^{2}}{2v^{2}},

where
v=v(u\,,\bar{u})
takes values between
u
and
\bar{u}
. Applying this with
h(z)=\log u(z)
and using (
4.1
) yield the following:

(6.1)

\displaystyle|h(z)-h(z_{0})|\leqslant\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})}+\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant|w(z)-w(z_{0})|+\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z)-(G\ast u_{0})(z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})}+\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z_{0}\,;z)|}{u(z_{0})}+\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}}.

Similarly,

(6.2)

\displaystyle|h(z)-h(z_{0})|\geqslant\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})}-\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}}

\displaystyle\geqslant|w(z)-w(z_{0})|-\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z)-(G\ast u_{0})(z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})}-\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z_{0}\,;z)|}{u(z_{0})}-\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}}.

Let
\phi(z\,,z_{0})=\rho(z\,,z_{0})\sqrt{\log\log(1/\rho(z\,,z_{0}))}
.
Then, by Lemma
2.3
(or (
1.4
) when
t_{0}=0
), Proposition
4.10
, and Theorem
1.1
, respectively, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z)-(G\ast u_{0})(z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})\phi(z\,,z_{0})}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z_{0})|}{u(z_{0})\phi(z\,,z_{0})}=0\quad\text{a.s.,}

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|u(z)-u(z_{0})|^{2}}{2\Delta_{0}^{2}\phi(z\,,z_{0})}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

These together with (
3.20
) imply that, a.s.,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|h(z)-h(z_{0})|}{\phi(z\,,z_{0})}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z\in B^{*}_{\rho}(z_{0},\varepsilon)}\frac{|w(z)-w(z_{0})|}{\phi(z\,,z_{0})}=K_{0}.

This proves (
1.17
).

We now turn to the proof of (
1.18
).
Fix
I=[a\,,T]\times[c\,,d]
. We may use the same argument as in the first part of this proof to show that
\Delta:=\inf_{z\in I}u(z)
is a strictly positive random variable, and for all
z\,,z^{\prime}\in I
,

\displaystyle|w(z^{\prime})-w(z)|-\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z^{\prime})-(G\ast u_{0})(z)|}{\Delta}-\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|}{\Delta}-\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|^{2}}{2\Delta^{2}}

\displaystyle\leqslant|h(z^{\prime})-h(z)|

\displaystyle\leqslant|w(z^{\prime})-w(z)|+\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z^{\prime})-(G\ast u_{0})(z)|}{\Delta}+\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|}{\Delta}+\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|^{2}}{2\Delta^{2}}.

Let
\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})=\rho(z\,,z^{\prime})\sqrt{\log(1/\rho(z\,,z^{\prime}))}
.
Then, by Lemma
2.3
(or (
1.6
) when
a=0
), Proposition
4.10
, and Theorem
1.2
(recalling that
\sigma^{-1}\{0\}
is polar for
u
),

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|(G\ast u_{0})(z^{\prime})-(G\ast u_{0})(z)|}{\Delta\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|\mathscr{E}(z\,;z^{\prime})|}{\Delta\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=0\quad\text{a.s.,}

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|u(z^{\prime})-u(z)|^{2}}{2\Delta^{2}\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=0\quad\text{a.s.}

The above and (
3.21
) together imply that, a.s.,

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|h(z^{\prime})-h(z)|}{\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}\sup_{z,z^{\prime}\in I:0<\rho(z,z^{\prime})\leqslant\varepsilon}\frac{|w(z^{\prime})-w(z)|}{\psi(z\,,z^{\prime})}=K_{1}.

This proves (
1.18
) and hence completes the proof of Theorem
1.11
.
∎

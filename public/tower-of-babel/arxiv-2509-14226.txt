Adiabatic Klein–Gordon Dynamics for the Renormalized Nelson Model
Morris Brooks, David Mitrouskas

Abstract
We study the renormalized Nelson model in a semiclassical regime where the field becomes classical while the particle remains quantum. The degree of classicality is measured by a small parameter ε ≪ 1 \varepsilon\ll 1 . In this scaling the particle evolves on microscopic times, whereas the field exhibits nontrivial dynamics only on the macroscopic scale t = 𝒪 ⁡ ( ε − 2 ) t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2}) . The natural semiclassical model is the coupled Schrödinger–Klein–Gordon (SKG) system, which encodes the time-scale separation through an explicit ε \varepsilon -dependence. Based on this scale separation in SKG, we apply the adiabatic principle to derive a new PDE for the classical field, the ε \varepsilon -free adiabatic Klein–Gordon (aKG) equation, where the field is driven by the instantaneous ground state of the particle. Our main result is a norm approximation of the Nelson dynamics by the aKG solution corrected by a quasi-free fluctuation dynamics around the classical field, generated by a renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson Hamiltonian. As a corollary, we obtain convergence of the reduced one-body densities for both subsystems, where the fluctuation correction vanishes, thereby justifying aKG as a semiclassical Born–Oppenheimer type approximation of the renormalized Nelson model.



1
Introduction

The Nelson model describes nonrelativistic particles interacting with a quantized scalar boson field. Introduced by E. Nelson in 1964, it has long served as a benchmark in mathematical physics for studying ultraviolet divergences and renormalization at the Hamiltonian level, with motivations ranging from quantum electrodynamics to solid-state models. Beyond these aspects, it also provides a natural setting to investigate semiclassical limits where quantum matter interacts with emergent classical fields.

We investigate the single-particle, massless, renormalized Nelson model in a semiclassical regime where the quantized field behaves effectively classically, while the particle retains its quantum nature. The degree of classicality of the field is measured by a small parameter
\varepsilon\ll 1
, which can be viewed as an effective Planck constant. In this regime, the particle evolves on its intrinsic quantum scale
t=\mathcal{O}(1)
, during which the field remains essentially frozen. In contrast, on macroscopic times
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
, the dynamics of the classical field become visible. This scale separation between the fast particle and the slow field underlies our analysis and highlights the relevance of a long-time description.

The canonical semiclassical model of a quantum particle coupled to a classical field is the Schrödinger–Klein–Gordon (SKG) system,

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\psi_{t}(x)

\displaystyle\;=\;\varepsilon^{-2}\left(-\Delta\;+\;2\textnormal{Re}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{|k|}}\,\varphi_{t}(k)\,\textnormal{d}k\right)\psi_{t}(x),

(1.1)

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}(k)

\displaystyle\;=\;|k|\,\varphi_{t}(k)\;+\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{|k|}}\,|\psi_{t}(x)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x.

(1.2)

The first equation describes the fast quantum particle in the external potential generated by the field, while the second is a Klein–Gordon equation for the slow classical field driven by the particle density. The SKG system still carries an explicit
\varepsilon
-dependence, which encodes the time-scale separation between the particle and the field. This dependence on the semiclassical parameter, however, also makes SKG somewhat inconvenient as a semiclassical approximation of the full quantum model.

For initial data where the particle is prepared in the gapped ground state of the potential generated by the classical field, one can exploit the adiabatic principle to obtain an
\varepsilon
-free semiclassical description. The idea is to eliminate the fast particle by letting it adiabatically follow the instantaneous ground state associated with the field. This yields an
\varepsilon
-independent, closed evolution for the field alone, the
adiabatic Klein–Gordon (aKG) equation
,

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}(k)\;=\;|k|\,\varphi_{t}(k)\;+\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{e^{-ikx}}{\sqrt{|k|}}\,|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}(x)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}x,

(1.3)

where
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
denotes the ground state of the Schrödinger operator with external potential generated by
\varphi_{t}
. The aKG equation governs the autonomous evolution of the field on the macroscopic time scale, and can be viewed as a field-theoretic Born–Oppenheimer type approximation of SKG. To our knowledge, this equation has not been studied before. We establish local well-posedness and show that SKG converges to aKG as
\varepsilon\to 0
.

Our main result is a quantitative norm approximation of the renormalized Nelson dynamics, valid for suitable initial states up to times
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. The effective description is given by the aKG pair
(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
, corrected by quantum fluctuations around the classical field. These fluctuations are governed by a
quadratic
(Bogoliubov) Hamiltonian and hence generate a
quasi-free
evolution on Fock space. The naive quadratic generator, however, inherits the logarithmic ultraviolet divergence of the Nelson Hamiltonian, and one of the key ingredients is the renormalization of this operator, which we call the
Bogoliubov–Nelson Hamiltonian
.

As a consequence of the norm approximation, we obtain convergence of the reduced one-body densities for both particle and field. At this level the fluctuation correction disappears, thereby justifying aKG as a semiclassical Born–Oppenheimer type approximation of the renormalized Nelson model. Moreover, through convergence of SKG to aKG as
\varepsilon\to 0
, our results directly imply corresponding approximations in terms of the (
\varepsilon
-dependent) SKG solutions.

The remainder of this introduction is devoted to precise formulations of the relevant models: the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian, the adiabatic Klein–Gordon equation, and the renormalized quantum fluctuation dynamics around the aKG field.

1.1
The renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian

We consider a single particle coupled to a quantized scalar field. The Hilbert space is

\displaystyle\mathscr{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F},

(1.4)

where
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
denotes the particle space with position variable
x
, and
\mathcal{F}:=\Gamma_{\mathrm{sym}}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{k}))
is the bosonic Fock space constructed over the one-particle space with momentum variable
k
. We write
a_{k}^{\ast}
and
a_{k}
for the creation and annihilation operators satisfying the usual bosonic commutation relations
[a_{k},a_{\ell}^{\ast}]=\delta(k-\ell)
and
[a_{k},a_{\ell}]=[a_{k}^{\ast},a_{\ell}^{\ast}]=0
. The free particle and field Hamiltonians are

\displaystyle p^{2}:=-\Delta,\qquad H_{f}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\omega(k)\,a_{k}^{\ast}a_{k}\,dk,\qquad\omega(k):=|k|.

(1.5)

Formally, the massless Nelson Hamiltonian with semiclassical parameter
\varepsilon\ll 1
is given by
the expression

\displaystyle p^{2}\;+\;\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\mathbb{G}(x,k)a_{k}^{*}+\overline{\mathbb{G}(x,k)}a_{k}\Big)\textnormal{d}k\;+\;\varepsilon^{2}H_{f},

(1.6)

acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F}
, with
x
-dependent kernel

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}(x,k):=e^{-ikx}\,\omega(k)^{-1/2}.

(1.7)

Note that the powers of
\varepsilon
match the number of creation and annihiliation operators in each term, which shows that
\varepsilon\to 0
corresponds to a semiclassical limit.

Since
\omega^{-1}G\notin L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{k})
, the interaction is not form-bounded with respect to free Hamiltonian
p^{2}+H_{f}
, and the formal Hamiltonian (
1.6
) is UV singular, hence neither self-adjoint nor associated with a closed quadratic form.

Introducing a cutoff
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
and

\displaystyle\mathbf{G}_{\Lambda}(x,k):=\mathbb{G}(x,k)\,\mathbf{1}_{|k|\leq\Lambda}.

(1.8)

we define the regularized Nelson Hamiltonian

\displaystyle p^{2}\;+\;\varepsilon\,\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\Big(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}(x,k)a_{k}^{*}+\overline{\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}(x,k)}a_{k}\Big)\textnormal{d}k\;+\;\varepsilon^{2}H_{f},

(1.9)

which is self-adjoint and semibounded with quadratic form domain
Q(H_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon})=Q(p^{2}+H_{f})
for all
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
.

The following proposition shows that, up to a diverging energy shift, the regularized operators converge as
\Lambda\to\infty
. We call the limit operator
H^{\varepsilon}
the
renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian
in the semiclassical scaling.

Proposition 1.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a self-adjoint, semibounded operator
H^{\varepsilon}
such that

\displaystyle\exp\big(-itH^{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda}\big)\exp\big(-it4\pi\varepsilon^{2}\ln\Lambda\big)\xrightarrow[\Lambda\to\infty]{}\exp\big(-itH^{\varepsilon}\big)

(1.10)

for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, in the strong sense on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F}
.

This was first established in
[
34
]
and later refined in
[
15
]
. The method is based on a unitary dressing transformation, which will also play a central role in our analysis. For completeness, we include a proof of Proposition
1.1
in Appendix
A
, where we adapt the ideas of
[
15
]
to the semiclassical setting. Alternative approaches to renormalization of the Nelson model rely on functional integration
[
16
]
or interior boundary conditions
[
22
]
. Finally, we note the drastic effect of renormalization on the domain: as shown in
[
15
,
22
]
, the form domains intersect only trivially, that is
Q(H^{\varepsilon})\cap Q(H^{\varepsilon}_{\Lambda})=\{0\}
for all
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
.

1.2
The adiabatic Klein–Gordon equation

The semiclassical model associated with the Nelson Hamiltonian is obtained by replacing the creation and annihilation operators in (
1.6
) with a classical field
\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{k})
. Taking the expectation value in a normalized particle state
\psi\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
then yields the
\varepsilon
-independent energy functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\psi,\varphi)=\int\textnormal{d}x\,|\nabla\psi(x)|^{2}+\int\textnormal{d}k\,\omega(k)|\varphi(k)|^{2}+\iint\textnormal{d}k\textnormal{d}x\,|\psi(x)|^{2}\frac{2\textnormal{Re}(e^{-ikx}\varphi(k))}{\sqrt{\omega(k)}}.

(1.11)

Unlike the quantum model, the semiclassical energy is ultraviolet regular. Its Hamiltonian flow gives the Schrödinger–Klein–Gordon (SKG) system for
(\psi_{t},\varphi_{t})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{k})
,

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} \ i\partial_{t}\psi_{t}&=h_{\varphi_{t}}\psi_{t}\\[5.69054pt]
\ i\varepsilon^{-2}\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}&=\omega\varphi_{t}+\sigma(\psi_{t})\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(SKG)

with Schrödinger operator

\displaystyle h_{\varphi_{t}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{t}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad V_{\varphi_{t}}(x)=2\textnormal{Re}\langle\varphi_{t},\mathbb{G}(x,\cdot)\big\rangle

(1.12)

and source term

\displaystyle\sigma(\psi_{t})(k)=\langle\psi_{t},\mathbb{G}(\cdot,k)\psi_{t}\rangle

(1.13)

where
\mathbb{G}
is given by (
1.7
).

Note that (
SKG
) is equivalent to (
1.1
)–(
1.2
) after rescaling time by
\varepsilon^{-2}
. A key feature of the SKG system is its explicit
\varepsilon
-dependence: although the semiclassical energy is
\varepsilon
–free, the underlying Poisson/commutator structure forces the field to evolve on the slow (macroscopic) time scale
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
, while the particle evolves on the fast (microscopic) scale
t=\mathcal{O}(1)
. Thus the SKG solutions depend explicitly on
\varepsilon
, which makes them less convenient as a semiclassical approximation of the Nelson dynamics in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. This motivates the formulation of an
\varepsilon
-free effective description.

To achieve this, we exploit the explicit time-scale separation in (
SKG
): inspired by the adiabatic principle, we eliminate the fast particle by letting it adiabatically follow the instantaneous ground state associated with the field. This leads to a closed,
\varepsilon
-independent evolution for the field alone, which we call the
adiabatic Klein–Gordon
(aKG) equation,

i\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}\;=\;\omega\varphi_{t}\;+\;\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}}),

(aKG)

where
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
denotes the normalized ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{t}}
. The structure of aKG mirrors the classical Born–Oppenheimer approximation: the fast quantum particle adiabatically follows its instantaneous ground state, while the slow field evolves autonomously. This is analogous to molecular Born–Oppenheimer theory, where the fast electrons follow the electronic ground state determined by the nuclear configuration, and the heavy nuclei evolve on their intrinsic slow time scale; see
[
17
,
37
,
35
]
.

Before continuing, we introduce the energy spaces for the particle and the field

\displaystyle H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})

\displaystyle:=\big\{\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x}):\|(1-\Delta)^{r/2}\psi\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\big\},\quad

\displaystyle r\in\mathbb{R},

(1.14)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{h}_{s}

\displaystyle:=\big\{\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{k}):\|\omega^{s}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}<\infty\big\},\quad

\displaystyle s\in\mathbb{R}.

(1.15)

For brevity, we write
H^{r}:=H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
. These spaces will be used throughout.

For all applications that follow, we consider initial data where the particle is in the gapped ground state of the Schrödinger operator
h_{\varphi}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi}
. To this end, we assume that

e(\varphi)\;:=\;\inf\sigma(h_{\varphi})\;<\;0,

(1.16)

which guarantees that
h_{\varphi}
has a unique ground state
\psi_{\varphi}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Without loss of generality, we may take
\psi_{\varphi}\geq 0
. We also define the spectral gap

\displaystyle\triangle(\varphi)\,=\,\inf\big\{\,|e(\varphi)-\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(h_{\varphi})\setminus\{e(\varphi)\}\,\big\},

(1.17)

that is, the distance between the ground state energy
e(\varphi)
and the rest of the spectrum.

Let
I\subset\mathbb{R}
be an interval with
0\in I
. We say that
\varphi\in C(I;\mathfrak{h}_{1/2})
is a solution to (
aKG
) on the interval
I
with initial datum
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
, if
e(\varphi_{t})<0
for all
t\in I
and

\varphi_{t}\;=\;e^{-i\omega t}\varphi_{0}\;-\;i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\omega(t-s)}\,\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\,\textnormal{d}s.

(1.18)

The existence of the ground state
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
for all
t\in I
follows from the condition
e(\varphi_{t})<0
.

The next lemma establishes local well-posedness of the aKG equation.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
.
There exist maximal times
T_{-},T_{+}\in(0,\infty]
such that (
aKG
)
admits a unique solution

\displaystyle\varphi\in C((-T_{-},T_{+});\mathfrak{h}_{1/2})\ \cap\ C^{1}((-T_{-},T_{+});\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}).

(1.19)

In particular, we have
e(\varphi_{t})<0
for all
t\in(-T_{-},T_{+})
, and therefore
h_{\varphi_{t}}
has a unique ground state
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
for every such
t
. Moreover, for any compact interval
J\subset(-T_{-},T_{+})
the spectral gap remains uniformly positive,

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in J}\triangle(\varphi_{t})\;>\;0.

(1.20)

If in addition
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
, then
\varphi\in C((-T_{-},T_{+});\mathfrak{h}_{0})
.

Finally, we either have
T_{+}=\infty
, or else
T_{+}<\infty
and
\liminf_{t\to T_{+}}\triangle(\varphi_{t})=0
, and equivalently for
T_{-}
.

The proof is given in Section
5
.
In general, global well-posedness cannot be expected, since the spectral gap
\triangle(\varphi_{t})
may close at a finite time. Once the gap vanishes, the ground state map
\varphi_{t}\mapsto\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
is typically no longer well defined; moreover, continuation of the local solution fails in the absence of a positive gap.

Conversely, if the spectral gap remains uniformly positive for all times, then the solution extends globally.
This is in particular the case for stationary solutions, which are the minimizers of the classical
field functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u):=e(u)+\|u\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2}.

(1.21)

These minimizers are known to be unique up to spatial translations
[
30
]
.
We denote by
\varphi_{*}
a fixed minimizer, and by

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\{\,T_{y}\varphi_{*}:y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\},\qquad(T_{y}u)(k):=e^{-iky}u(k),

(1.22)

the manifold of all minimizers.
For
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
we define the distance to
\mathcal{M}
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(u,\mathcal{M}):=\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}}\|u-T_{y}\varphi_{*}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}.

(1.23)

In the next lemma, we prove that for initial data that is sufficiently close to
\mathcal{M}
, the spectral gap remains uniformly positive along the evolution. As a consequence, the corresponding aKG solution exists for all times. The proof of the lemma is postponed to Appendix
B
.

Lemma 1.3
.

There exist
\eta>0
such that for all initial data
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(\varphi_{0},\mathcal{M})\;\leq\;\eta
, the aKG solution
\varphi_{t}
extends globally in time and satisfies

\displaystyle\inf_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\triangle(\varphi_{t})\,>\,0.

(1.24)

Even though aKG is an equation for the field alone, we will often refer to
(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
as the aKG solution pair. The main goal of this work is to show that this pair provides an effective description of the renormalized Nelson dynamics in the limit
\varepsilon\to 0
. At the same time, it is instructive to make the connection between SKG and aKG explicit. In Appendix
C
, we therefore show that the SKG solution
(\psi_{t}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon})
, when observed on the rescaled slow time scale, converges to the aKG pair
(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
. More precisely, on compact intervals in
(-T_{-},T_{+})
,

\displaystyle\exp\left(i\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}e\left(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{-2}s}\right)\textnormal{d}s\right)\,\psi_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\varphi_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\varphi_{t}

(1.25)

both in
L^{2}
-norm, where
e(\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}):=\inf\sigma(h_{\varphi_{s}^{\varepsilon}})
. Although this result is not used in our proofs, it makes the adiabatic picture precise and may be of independent interest. In particular, it allows us to translate the main results of Section
2
into SKG-based approximations.

1.3
The renormalized quantum fluctuations

Let
\varphi_{t}
denote the solution to (
aKG
) with initial condition
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
satisfying
e(\varphi_{0})<0
, and let
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
be the instantaneous ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}
. By Proposition
1.2
there exists
T>0
such that
h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t})
has a uniform spectral gap above zero for all
|t|\leq T
. In particular, we can define the reduced resolvent on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
, given by

\displaystyle R_{\varphi_{t}}:=Q_{\varphi_{t}}\,(h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t}))^{-1}\,Q_{\varphi_{t}},\qquad Q_{\varphi_{t}}=\mathbf{1}-P_{\varphi_{t}},\quad P_{\varphi_{t}}=|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}}|,

(1.26)

which is a bounded operator for all
|t|\leq T
thanks to the spectral gap.

For the regularized Nelson Hamiltonian (
1.9
) with UV cutoff
\Lambda\in(0,\infty)
, the evolution of the quantum fluctuations is generated by the time-dependent quadratic (Bogoliubov) Hamiltonian acting on Fock space
\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)\;=\;H_{f}\;-\iint\,\mathrm{d}k\,\textnormal{d}\ell\,\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}(\cdot,k)R_{\varphi_{t}}\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}(\cdot,\ell),\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\,\big(a_{k}^{\ast}+a_{-k}\big)\big(a_{\ell}^{\ast}+a_{-\ell}\big)

(1.27)

where
H_{f}=\mathrm{d}\Gamma(\omega)
is the free-field energy and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the inner product in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
.

We denote by
\mathbb{U}_{\Lambda}(t)
the unitary propagator on
\mathcal{F}
generated by
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)
with initial time
t=0
. The next result establishes the existence of a renormalized Bogoliubov evolution in the limit
\Lambda\to\infty
; in particular, it shows that
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)
exhibits the same logarithmic divergence as the original Nelson Hamiltonian.

Proposition 1.4
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
, and let
\varphi_{t}
denote the solution to (
aKG
) with initial datum
\varphi_{0}
. There exists
T>0
such that the following statements hold:

(i)

For every
\Lambda\in[2,\infty)
and
\Psi\in D(H_{f}^{1/2})
there exists a unique solution
\Psi\in C([-T,T],\mathcal{F})\cap L^{\infty}([-T,T],D(H_{f}^{1/2}))

to the Cauchy problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{aligned} i\partial_{t}\Psi(t)&=\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)\,\Psi(t),\\[3.41432pt]
\Psi(0)&=\Psi,\end{aligned}\end{cases}

(1.28)

with the equation understood in
D((1+H_{f})^{-1/2})
. The map
\Psi\mapsto\Psi(t)
defines a unitary
\mathbb{U}_{\Lambda}(t)
on
\mathcal{F}
that is strongly continuous in
t
.

(ii)

For all
|t|\leq T
, the strong limit

\displaystyle\mathbb{U}(t)\;:=\;s-\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}\,\mathbb{U}_{\Lambda}(t)\,\exp\big(-it\,4\pi\ln\Lambda\big)

(1.29)

exists, is unitary, and the map
t\mapsto\mathbb{U}(t)
is strongly continuous.

The proposition is proved in Section
6
. We call
\mathbb{U}(t)
the
renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson evolution
on
\mathcal{F}
.
While we do not explicitly use the following property in our analysis, one can show that
\mathbb{U}(t)
acts as a Bogoliubov transformation on Fock space, i.e., it implements a linear rotation of creation and annihilation operators. In particular,
\mathbb{U}(t)
preserves the Gaussian (Wick factorization) property of quasi-free states in
\mathcal{F}
.

2
Main results

We consider
semiclassical product initial data
of the form

\displaystyle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})\;=\;\psi_{\varphi_{0}}\ \otimes\ W(\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{0})\,\Omega,

(2.1)

where
\psi_{\varphi_{0}}\geq 0
is the normalized ground state of
h_{\varphi_{0}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{0}}
and
\Omega=(1,\mathbb{0})
denotes the Fock vacuum in
\mathcal{F}
.
Here

\displaystyle W(f):=\exp\!\big(a^{\ast}(f)-a(f)\big)

(2.2)

is the Weyl operator, and thus the coherent state
W(\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{0})\Omega

represents the classical field
\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{0}
with
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
excitations.

From a physical perspective, such states arise naturally in experimental situations: one first
imposes
a classical field
\varphi_{0}
(e.g. by external driving) and keeps it fixed until the particle is cooled into the ground state
\psi_{\varphi_{0}}
of the frozen Hamiltonian
h_{\varphi_{0}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{0}}
.
At time
t=0
the external control is removed and the system evolves autonomously under the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian, with the initial state prepared in
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})
.
Unless the stationary condition
\omega\varphi_{0}+\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{0}})=0
is satisfied, this preparation constitutes a quench from a frozen to a non-stationary system, initiating nontrivial coupled dynamics on the macroscopic time scale.

Our main theorem gives a norm approximation for the renormalized Nelson evolution for initial states of the form (
2.1
).
As corollaries, we obtain convergence of the one-body reduced densities for both subsystems.

2.1
Norm approximation

The following theorem is the main result of this work.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
, and let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution to (
aKG
) with initial datum
\varphi_{0}
. Let
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
denote the normalized ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{t}}
, and set

\displaystyle\mu(t)=\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\textnormal{Im}\langle\varphi_{s},\partial_{s}\varphi_{s}\rangle+\|\omega^{1/2}\varphi_{s}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+e(\varphi_{s})\Big)\,\mathrm{d}s.

(2.3)

Moreover, let
H^{\varepsilon}
be the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian from Proposition
1.1
, and let
\mathbb{U}(t)
be the renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson evolution from Proposition
1.4
(defined in terms of
\varphi_{t}
). Then there exist constants
C,T,\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
\varphi_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\exp\!\left(-i\varepsilon^{-2}tH^{\varepsilon}+i\varepsilon^{-2}\mu(t)\right)\,(\psi_{\varphi_{0}}\otimes W(\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{0})\Omega)-\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\otimes W(\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{t})\,\mathbb{U}(t)\Omega\Big\|\;\leq\;C\,\varepsilon^{\tfrac{1}{10}}\ln\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}

for all
|t|\leq T
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

This result provides a norm approximation of the renormalized Nelson dynamics up to times

t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
, in terms of a semiclassical state built from the aKG pair

(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
and corrected by the fluctuation dynamics
\mathbb{U}(t)
around the coherent aKG field.
The theorem is formulated for
|t|\leq T
(corresponding to physical times
|t|\leq\varepsilon^{-2}T
),
where
T
is tied to the existence of the aKG solution and the persistence of the associated spectral gap; see Proposition
1.2
.
In general,
T
may have to be chosen small. By Lemma
5.1
, however, if the initial datum
is sufficiently close to the manifold
\mathcal{M}
of field minimizers, the aKG solution is global and the
gap stays uniformly positive; in this case one can choose

arbitrarily large, so the approximation
holds on any finite macroscopic interval.

2.2
Convergence of reduced densities

As corollaries of Theorem

, we derive convergence of the reduced one-body densities.

We start with the particle, whose reduced density at time
t
is defined by

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm particle}^{\varepsilon}(t):=\operatorname{Tr}_{\mathcal{F}}\Big(e^{-i\varepsilon^{-2}tH^{\varepsilon}}|\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})\rangle\langle\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})|e^{i\varepsilon^{-2}tH^{\varepsilon}}\Big),

(2.4)

where
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})
is the initial state from (
2.1
).

Corollary 2.2
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
, and let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution to (
aKG
) with initial datum
\varphi_{0}
. Let
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
denote the normalized ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi_{t}}
. Then there exist constants
C,T,\varepsilon_{0}>0
, depending only on
\varphi_{0}
, such that

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}}\Big|\,\gamma_{\rm particle}^{\varepsilon}(t)-|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}}|\,\Big|\;\leq\;C\,\varepsilon^{\tfrac{1}{10}}\ln\tfrac{1}{\varepsilon}

(2.5)

for all
|t|\leq T
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
.

To analyze the reduced one-body density of the field we start from a dressed initial state. In our proof this step is required, since we need an energy estimate for the initial state. The undressed product states
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})
are not in the form domain of
H^{\varepsilon}
and therefore have infinite energy, whereas the dressed state introduced below has finite energy.

We therefore introduce the unitary dressing transformation

\displaystyle U_{K}^{\varepsilon}\;:=\;\exp\!\left(\varepsilon\int\textnormal{d}k\Big(\mathbb{B}_{K}(x,k)a_{k}^{*}-\overline{\boldsymbol{B}_{K}(x,k)}a_{k}\Big)\textnormal{d}k\right)

(2.6)

with

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{B}_{K}(x,k):=\frac{\mathbb{G}(x,k)}{k^{2}}\,\mathbf{1}_{|k|\geq K}.

(2.7)

Accordingly, we define the dressed initial state as

\displaystyle\Psi_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})\;:=\;W(\varepsilon^{-1}\varphi_{0})\,(U_{K}^{\varepsilon})^{*}\,(\psi_{\varphi_{0}}\otimes\Omega).

(2.8)

The difference between the dressed state
\Psi_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})
and the undressed state
\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})
is negligible in norm as
\varepsilon\to 0
or
K\to\infty
:

\displaystyle\|\Psi_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})-\Psi^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})\|\;\leq\;C\varepsilon K^{-1},

(2.9)

so that Theorem
2.1
continues to hold with the dressed initial state.
This bound follows from the standard estimate

\displaystyle\|\big(U_{K}^{\varepsilon}-\mathbf{1}\big)\Psi\|\;\leq\;C\varepsilon\|B_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\|(N+1)^{1/2}\Psi\|,

(2.10)

valid for every
\Psi\in D(N^{1/2})
, together with
\|B_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\leq CK^{-1}
. Here
N
denotes the number operator on
\mathcal{F}
,

\displaystyle N:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}a_{k}^{\ast}a_{k}\,\textnormal{d}k.

(2.11)

With this modification, we define the field’s reduced one-body density for the dressed initial state, through its kernel

\displaystyle\gamma_{\rm field}^{\varepsilon}(t)(k,\ell):=\varepsilon^{2}\,\big\langle e^{-i\varepsilon^{-2}tH^{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0}),\ a_{k}^{\ast}a_{\ell}\,e^{-i\varepsilon^{-2}tH^{\varepsilon}}\Psi_{K}^{\varepsilon}(\varphi_{0})\big\rangle.

(2.12)

The pre-factor ensures the normalization

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\operatorname{Tr}_{L^{2}}\big(\gamma_{\rm field}^{\varepsilon}(0)\big)=\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.13)

In principle, the parameter
K
could be left free in the next corollary, leading to additional error terms that vanish as
K\to\infty
. To balance the errors and simplify the statements, we fix
K=\varepsilon^{-4/5}
.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with

\varphi_{t}
be the solution to (

2.3
Comparison with the literature

The present work lies at the intersection of partially classical limits for quantum field models
and strong-coupling polaron dynamics. We highlight three closely related works and sketch the relevant
similarities and differences.

Strong-coupling Fröhlich polaron dynamics.

Our analysis is inspired by recent works on the Fröhlich polaron at large coupling
[
29
,
33
,
26
]
; see also
[
12
,
11
,
14
]
for earlier contributions. Setting
\alpha^{-1}=\varepsilon
identifies the
strong-coupling limit
\alpha\to\infty
with a semiclassical limit for the phonon field, and the macroscopic
time scale
t=\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
corresponds to our time scale
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. The main difference from the Nelson model is that the Fröhlich Hamiltonian is UV-regular. In
[
26
]
, a
norm approximation
of the dynamics was
established up to times
t=\mathcal{O}(\alpha^{2})
by the Landau–Pekar (LP) system, corrected by a
Bogoliubov (quasi-free) fluctuation dynamics around the coherent state. The
\alpha
-dependent LP system is the analogue of (
SKG
) for the polaron.

In the renormalized Nelson model, the problem is more delicate. Most importantly,
the fluctuation generator is UV-divergent and requires a renormalization procedure, a
complication that is absent in the Fröhlich case. In addition, we introduce and analyze the
aKG equation, which provides an
\varepsilon
-free effective dynamics, whereas both LP and SKG
depend explicitly on the semiclassical parameter. These two ingredients, renormalization of
the fluctuation generator and the use of aKG, constitute the conceptual novelties of this work.
At the same time, our analysis builds on and extends the adiabatic expansion scheme
developed for the polaron, by adapting it to the more singular setting of the Nelson model.

Partially classical limit for the renormalized Nelson model.

Ginibre, Nironi and Velo
[
13
]
gave the first rigorous analysis of the renormalized Nelson
model in a partially classical regime. They worked on microscopic time scales
t=\mathcal{O}(1)
and used a
coherent-state/weak-coupling scaling, where the number of field excitations is of order

\varepsilon^{-2}
and the coupling strength is proportional to
\varepsilon
. In this regime the
quantum field converges to a solution of the free wave (Klein–Gordon) equation, and the
particles evolve by a Schrödinger equation with an external potential generated by the free
field. Since the back-reaction on the field is suppressed, the effective dynamics remains
linear. The renormalized Hamiltonian is constructed via a unitary dressing transformation,
and convergence of the propagators is obtained on a suitable subspace, with a quantitative error estimate.

In our scaling the situation is different: the Poisson/commutator structure places a factor

\varepsilon^{-2}
in front of
\partial_{t}\varphi
, so at microscopic times
t=\mathcal{O}(1)
the field remains
frozen rather than evolving freely. Nontrivial field evolution appears only on the slow scale

t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
. On that scale we establish a norm approximation in terms of the
nonlinear aKG equation, corrected by a renormalized fluctuation dynamics. The need to renormalize the fluctuation generator has no counterpart in
[
13
]
, since the field there remains free and unaffected by back-reaction and fluctuations..

Quasi-classical/Wigner-measure approach.

In
[
5
]
the authors developed an abstract quasi-classical framework for particle-field systems
and applied it to several models, including the UV-regularized Nelson model. The quasi-classical parameter is introduced by scaling creation and annihilation operators with a factor

\varepsilon,
1
1

1

We write
\varepsilon
in place of the
\varepsilon^{1/2}
used in
[
5
]
to stay consistent with our notation.

together with an
\varepsilon
-dependent weight
\nu(\varepsilon)
in front of the field energy (our
setting corresponds to
\nu(\varepsilon)=1
). At microscopic times
t=\mathcal{O}(1)
they distinguish two
regimes: if
\varepsilon\nu(\varepsilon)\to 0
the classical field remains frozen, while if

\varepsilon\nu(\varepsilon)\to 1
it evolves freely. Since there is no back-reaction on the field on this
time scale, the effective particle dynamics is linear, driven by an external classical field.
Convergence to the effective evolution is established via state-valued Wigner
measures. Their assumptions on initial states are very
general and allow for entangled particle-field configurations.

Our analysis addresses the renormalized Nelson model for more specific, semiclassical initial data and focuses on the macroscopic time scale. In this regime the field exhibits nontrivial back-reaction and the effective dynamics is nonlinear.

Other works.

Within the broader context of deriving effective equations from particle-field models, let us
also mention the following works. In
[

studied in a many-body mean-field regime, where

semiclassical setting is that the quantum fluctuations in the mean-field regime act simultaneously on the excitations of the
bosons and the field, which requires a different renormalization strategy

. Finally, in the
mean-field limit, the norm approximation of the dynamics is obtained as a consequence of the
convergence of reduced one-body densities, whereas in our setting we first prove the norm

The works

weak-coupling and adiabatic regimes, where the roles of time scales are reversed: the particles
evolve slowly while the field provides the fast subsystem.

2.4
Outline of the paper

We start in Section
3
with a collection of auxiliary estimates for creation and annihilation operators and for the classical potentials. These bounds are used throughout Sections
4
–
8
.

Section
4
introduces the dressed Nelson Hamiltonian, obtained by applying the unitary dressing transformation
U_{K}^{\varepsilon}
, see (
2.6
), to the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian. We then perform a Weyl shift around the classical field, which separates the leading particle Hamiltonian from subleading field terms and expands the Hamiltonian in powers of
\varepsilon
.
The purpose of this section is to obtain a suitable representation of the Nelson Hamiltonian that is needed in the proof of the norm approximation.

In Section
5
we analyze the (
aKG
) equation: we prove local well-posedness (by a contraction argument), energy conservation and persistence of the spectral gap for suitable time intervals. Beyond well-posedness, this provides control on resolvents and regularity estimates for the aKG pair
(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
, which are repeatedly used later on.

In Section
6
we construct the renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson evolution

\mathbb{U}(t)
as the strong limit of the regularized propagators generated by

\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)
(defined in (
1.27
)),
up to a logarithmically diverging energy counter term. This renormalization is based on the key identity

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)=\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)-4\pi\ln\Lambda

(2.16)

where the operator
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
is related to the dressed Nelson Hamiltonian and has a limit as
\Lambda\to\infty
. In contrast to the full quantum model, this renormalization does not rely on the application of a unitary dressing transformation. We also establish bounds for powers of the number operator propagated under
\mathbb{U}(t)
.

Section
7
is devoted to the proof of
Theorem
2.1
.
After applying the unitary dressing transformation and a coherent shift aligned with the aKG solution,
we compare the renormalized Nelson dynamics with the semiclassical evolution associated with

(\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\varphi_{t})
, corrected by the fluctuation dynamics
\mathbb{U}(t)
.
The comparison relies on a first-order Duhamel expansion combined with a second- and third-order adiabatic expansion around the
instantaneous gapped particle ground state.
A crucial step in this expansion is a cancellation coming from the generator
\mathbb{U}(t)
, which removes a contribution of order one
on the macroscopic time scale
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
.
After this cancellation, all remainder terms are of higher order in
\varepsilon

the estimates established in the preceding sections.

In Section
8
we deduce convergence of the reduced densities. While the convergence of the reduced particle density follows immediately from the norm approximation, the analysis of the reduced one-body density of the field requires additional work. Here we combine the norm approximation with an improved energy estimate and an a priori bound on the propagated number operator.

Finally, Appendix
A
revisits the renormalization of the Nelson model
in the semiclassical regime and establishes the dressing identity for the Nelson Hamiltonian.
Appendix
B
extends the local well-posedness of aKG to global well-posedness
for initial data close to
\mathcal{M}
, using energy conservation together with a coercive lower bound
for the field energy.
Appendix
C
proves the convergence of SKG to aKG as
\varepsilon\to 0

–
3.3
to obtain the following estimates.

Lemma 3.4
.

For all
K\geq 2
and
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F}
, we have

\displaystyle\|a(\mathbb{G}_{K})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\min\big\{\,(\ln K)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle p\rangle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,,\,\|\langle p\rangle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,,\,K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\big\},

(3.24a)

\displaystyle\|a(\omega\mathbb{G}_{K})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\min\big\{\,K\,\|\langle p\rangle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,,\,K^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle p\rangle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,\big\},

(3.24b)

\displaystyle\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\min\big\{\,K^{-\frac{3}{4}}\|\langle p\rangle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,,\,K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,\big\},

(3.24c)

\displaystyle\|a(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,\min\big\{K^{-\frac{3}{8}}\|\langle p\rangle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\,,\,K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle p^{2}\rangle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|\big\}.

(3.24d)

Additionally, for all
\Lambda\geq K\geq 2
:

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\Phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,(K\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\,\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Phi\|,

(3.24e)

\displaystyle\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\Psi\|

\displaystyle\,\lesssim\,(K\Lambda)^{-\frac{3}{8}}\,\|\langle p\rangle N\Psi\|\,\ .

(3.24f)

Proof.

The bounds (
3.24a
) and (
3.24b
) follow from Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
. The second bound in (
3.24c
) follows directly from Lemma
3.1
and (
3.19c
). The first bound in (
3.24c
) and both bounds in (
3.24d
) follow from Lemma
3.2
together with (
3.19c
).

For the bound in (
3.24e
), we apply Cauchy–Schwarz, Lemma
3.1
, and (
3.19c
):

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\Phi\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle\Psi,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\Phi\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle(N_{K}+3)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\Phi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\,\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\Phi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}kB_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\omega^{-\frac{1}{4}}kB_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\,\|H_{f,K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Phi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim(K\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{4}}\,\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Phi\|\,\|H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|,

(3.25)

where we used
N_{K}\leq H_{f,K}\leq H_{f}
for
K\geq 2
.
For the bound in (
3.24f
), we apply Lemma
3.2
twice, that is, we estimate

\displaystyle\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{0,\frac{1}{2}}(kB_{K})\,\|\langle|p|^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|^{2}

(3.26)

and

\displaystyle\|\langle|p|^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\,\|a(\langle|k|^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle kB_{\Lambda})N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|^{2}+\,2\,\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\langle|p|^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\|^{2}

(3.27)

Using
\langle|p|^{1/2}\rangle^{2}\lesssim\langle p\rangle
and Lemma
3.2
concludes the proof of (
3.24e
); note that
\mathscr{C}_{0,1}(|k|^{3/2}B_{\Lambda})=\mathscr{C}_{1,1}(\omega kB_{\Lambda})
, since
\omega(k)=|k|
.
∎

3.3
Bounds for
A_{K}
and
D_{K}

We now apply the previous estimates to control operators that will appear repeatedly in the upcoming sections. To this end, let us define

\displaystyle A_{K}

\displaystyle:=\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p+2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}),

(3.28)

\displaystyle D_{K}

\displaystyle:=2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}+a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}+4\pi\ln K

(3.29)

The estimates summarized next play a central role in the construction of the renormalized fluctuation dynamics in Section
6
. We provide bounds for
A_{K}
and
D_{K}
both in terms of the number operator
N
and the field energy
H_{f}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
R\geq 0
be a bounded non-negative operator acting on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
. For all
K\geq 2
and
\Psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F}
we have

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,A_{K}RA_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{2}\big(\|\langle p\rangle R\langle p\rangle\|+\|R\|\big)\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2},

(3.30a)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,[N,A_{K}]RA_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{2}\big(\|\langle p\rangle R\langle p\rangle\|+\|R\|\big)\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2},

(3.30b)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,D_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}+\ln K\|\Psi\|^{2},

(3.30c)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,[N,D_{K}]\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}.

(3.30d)

Moreover, in terms of the number operator
N
we have

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,A_{K}RA_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln K\big(\|\langle p\rangle R\langle p\rangle\|+\|R\|\big)\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2},

(3.31a)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,[N,A_{K}]RA_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln K\big(\|\langle p\rangle R\langle p\rangle\|+\|R\|\big)\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2},

(3.31b)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,D_{K}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln K\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2},

(3.31c)

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,[N,D_{K}]\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\langle p\rangle\Psi\|^{2}.

(3.31d)

Proof.

Since
R\geq 0
, it suffices to bound the diagonal terms in
A_{K}RA_{K}
. With the aid of Lemma
3.1
and
\|G_{K}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim K
, we find

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})R\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{2}\|R\|\,\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2},,

(3.32)

and invoking Lemma
3.4
, we also get

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)R(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}))\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-1}\|\langle p\rangle R\langle p\rangle\|\,\|\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}.

(3.33)

For the third diagonal term in
A_{K}RA_{K}
, we apply Cauchy–Schwarz and then Lemma
3.2
(with
s=0
,
r=1
) and Lemma
3.3
:

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}))R(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle=|\langle(N_{K}+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi,(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}))\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}R(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|p\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\,\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}R(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\Psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{C}_{0,1}(kB_{K})^{\frac{1}{2}}\,\|p\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\,\|Ra^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p\Psi\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{1}{2}}\,\|R^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\,\|\langle p\rangle\langle N_{K}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|\,\|R^{\frac{1}{2}}a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p\Psi\|.

(3.34)

Dividing by the last factor, and using
N_{K}\leq H_{f}
for
K\geq 1
gives

\displaystyle\langle\Psi,(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}))R(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\Psi\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-1}\|R\|\,\|\langle p\rangle\langle H_{f}^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}.

(3.35)

Combining (
3.32
)–(
3.35
) proves (
3.30a
); the commutator bound (
3.30b
) follows in the same way.
The two bounds for
D_{K}
are immediate consequences of Lemma
3.4
.

To show (
3.31a
), we estimate again the diagonal terms in
A_{K}RA_{K}
. By Lemmas
3.2
and
3.3
,

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,a^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{K})Ra(\mathbb{G}_{K})\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\mathscr{C}_{0,1}(G_{K})\,\|R\|\,\|\langle p\rangle\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}\lesssim\ln K\,\|R\|\,\|\langle p\rangle\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}

(3.36)

and similarly for

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,a(\mathbb{G}_{K})Ra^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{K})\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle=|\langle(N+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi,a(\mathbb{G}_{K})\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}R\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}a^{*}(\mathbb{G}_{K})(N+2)^{\frac{1}{2}}\Psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(\mathbb{G}_{K})\,\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}R^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\,\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\Psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln K\,\|\langle p\rangle R^{\frac{1}{2}}\|^{2}\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle\Psi\|^{2}.

(3.37)

The remaining two diagonal terms in the estimate for
A_{K}RA_{K}
are obtained in close analogy, as is the commutator bound (
3.31b
).

Finally, the two bounds for
D_{K}
follow again directly from Lemma
3.4
.
∎

In the next lemma, we recast the previous bounds in a form more suitable for the proof of our norm-approximation theorem in Section
7
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
m\in\{0,1,2\}
. For all
K\geq 2
, we have

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}(A_{K}-\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K}))\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(3.38a)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}A_{K}\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim(\ln K)^{\frac{1}{2}},

(3.38b)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-2}[H_{f},A_{K}]\langle p\rangle^{-2}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K,

(3.38c)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}(D_{K}-4\pi\ln K)\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(3.38d)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}D_{K}\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\ln K,

(3.38e)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})\otimes\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

For the first bound we note that
A_{K}-\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})=2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p
. Commuting through the operator
\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}
with
a
and
a^{*}
(which shifts
N
by
\pm 1
) and applying (
3.24c
), we obtain

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}))\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|\lesssim K^{-3/4},

(3.39)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-m-1}(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\langle p\rangle^{-1}(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p)\langle p\rangle^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-3/4}.

(3.40)

The second bound follows analogously, invoking also (
3.24a
).

For the commutator

\displaystyle[H_{f},A_{K}]=a^{*}(\omega\mathbb{G}_{K})-a(\omega\mathbb{G}_{K})+2a^{*}(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p-2p\cdot a(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K}),

(3.41)

we apply (
3.24b
) and (
3.24d
):

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-2}a(\omega\mathbb{G}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-2}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim K

(3.42)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-2}(p\cdot a(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K}))\langle p\rangle^{-2}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-2}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|\lesssim K^{-\frac{1}{2}},

(3.43)

and analogously for the terms with
a^{*}(\omega G_{K})
and
a^{*}(\omega k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p
.

To obtain the fourth bound, we apply (
3.24c
) and (
3.24f
):

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-2}\|

\displaystyle=\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\langle p\rangle^{-1}a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim K^{-3/2},

(3.44)

\displaystyle\|\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{m}\langle p\rangle^{-1}a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-m-2}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim\|a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}\langle p\rangle^{-1}\langle N^{\frac{1}{2}}\rangle^{-2}\|\lesssim K^{-\frac{3}{4}},

(3.45)

and similarly for
a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}
. The last bound is a direct consequence of the previous one.
∎

3.4
Bounds for the classical potentials

We conclude with estimates for the classical potential
V_{\varphi}
and the source term
\sigma(\psi)
, defined in (
1.12
) and (
1.13
), respectively. These bounds are used mainly for the aKG analysis in Section
5
.

Lemma 3.7
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that the following bounds hold:

\displaystyle\|V_{\varphi}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\,\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2},

\displaystyle\psi\in H^{1},

(3.46a)

\displaystyle\|V_{\varphi}\psi\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-s}}\,\|\psi\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{-s},

\displaystyle\psi\in H^{2},\quad s\in[0,\tfrac{3}{4}],

(3.46b)

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,V_{\varphi}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2},

\displaystyle\psi\in H^{1},

(3.46c)

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,V_{\varphi}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(\delta^{-1}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\delta\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2})

\displaystyle\forall\,\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2},

\displaystyle\psi\in H^{1},\quad\delta>0.

(3.46d)

In particular, the last inequality implies for all
\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
and
\psi\in H^{1}
:

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C(1+\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2})\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\langle\psi,(p^{2}+V_{\varphi})\psi\rangle.

(3.47)

Proof.

The first two bounds can be viewed as commutative versions of Lemma
3.2
.
Set
f=\omega^{-1/2}
and replace the operator
a_{k}
by the function
\varphi(k)
.
Proceeding as in Lemma
3.2
, we find for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\geq 0
:

\displaystyle\Big\|\int\textnormal{d}k\,\omega(k)^{-1/2}e^{ikx}\varphi(k)\,\psi\Big\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\textnormal{d}k\,\textnormal{d}\ell\,\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}(k)\,{\varphi(k)}\,\omega^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\ell)\,{\varphi(\ell)}\,\Big\langle e^{ikx}\psi,e^{i\ell x}\psi\Big\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\bigg\|\int\textnormal{d}k\frac{1}{\omega^{s+1}(k)\,\langle|p-k|^{r}\rangle^{2}}\bigg\|\,\|\omega^{s/2}\varphi\|_{2}^{2}\,\|\langle|p|^{r}\rangle\psi\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\mathscr{C}_{s,r}(\omega^{-1/2})\,\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{s/2}}^{2}\,\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{r}}

(3.48)

with
\mathscr{C}_{s,r}(f)
defined in Lemma
3.2
. From (
3.16
), we infer that
\mathscr{C}_{1,1}(\omega^{-1/2})\lesssim 1
and
\mathscr{C}_{-s,2}(\omega^{-1/2})\lesssim 1
for
s\in[0,\frac{3}{4}]
, which yields the first two statements.

For (
3.46c
), apply Cauchy–Schwarz:

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,V_{\varphi}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2\int\textnormal{d}k\,\omega^{-1/2}(k)\,|\varphi(k)|\,|\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)|\leq C\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

(3.49)

By Sobolev’s inequality,
\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}
, this yields the claim.

To prove the last bound, decompose
\varphi=\varphi_{K}+\varphi_{>K}
with
\varphi_{K}(k):=\mathbf{1}_{|k|\leq K}\varphi(k)
, and estimate

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,V_{\varphi_{K}}\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\int_{|k|\leq K}\textnormal{d}k\,\omega(k)^{-1/2}|\varphi(k)|\leq CK\,\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(3.50)

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,(V_{\varphi}-V_{\varphi_{K}})\psi\rangle|

\displaystyle\leq 2K^{-1}\int_{|k|\geq K}\textnormal{d}k\,\omega(k)^{1/2}|\varphi(k)|\,|\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)|\leq 2K^{-1}\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}^{2},

(3.51)

and hence

\displaystyle|\langle\psi,V_{\varphi}\psi\rangle|\leq C(K\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+K^{-1}\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2})\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}

(3.52)

for all
K>0
. Setting
\delta:=K^{-1}
proves the statement.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

There is
C>0
such that for all
L^{2}
-normalized
\psi\in H^{1}
and
s\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
,

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}

(3.53)

Moreover, for all
L^{2}
-normalized
\psi,\phi\in H^{1}
,

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-s}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\big(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}\big)\,\|\psi-\phi\|_{L^{2}},\qquad s\in[0,1/2],

(3.54)

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\big(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}\big)\|\psi-\phi\|_{H^{1}}.

(3.55)

Proof.

Recall that
\sigma(\psi)(k)=\omega(k)^{-1/2}\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)
. For the first bound, we split the integral into

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{s}}^{2}=\int\limits_{|k|\leq 1}\frac{|\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)|^{2}}{|k|^{1+2s}}\textnormal{d}k+\int\limits_{|k|>1}\frac{|\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)|^{2}}{|k|^{1+2s}}\textnormal{d}k=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

(3.56)

The low-momentum part satisfies
I_{1}\leq\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\int_{|k|\leq 1}|k|^{-1-2s}\textnormal{d}k\lesssim 1
for
s\in[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]
. For the large-momentum part, we use Plancherel and Sobolev to estimate

\displaystyle I_{2}\;\leq\;\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}|\widehat{|\psi|^{2}}(k)|^{2}\,\textnormal{d}k=\|\psi\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{4}.

(3.57)

For the second bound, set
\rho:=|\psi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}
.
By Plancherel, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-s}}^{2}=\int\frac{|\widehat{\rho}(k)|^{2}}{|k|^{1+2s}}\,\textnormal{d}k=c_{s}\iint\frac{\rho(x)\,\rho(y)}{|x-y|^{\,2-2s}}\,\textnormal{d}x\,\textnormal{d}y,

(3.58)

for some constant
c_{s}
. Now apply the Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev inequality
[
31
, Theorem 4.3]
for
d=3
,
\lambda=2-2s
,
s\in[0,\tfrac{1}{2}]
, and

\displaystyle p=q=p_{s}:=\frac{3}{2+s}\in\big[\tfrac{6}{5},\tfrac{3}{2}\big],

(3.59)

to obtain
\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{-s}}^{2}\leq C_{s}\,\|\rho\|_{L^{p_{s}}}^{2}
. Write

\displaystyle\rho=|\psi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}=(\psi-\phi)\,\overline{\psi}+(\overline{\psi-\phi})\,\phi

(3.60)

and take
q_{s}
with

\frac{1}{p_{s}}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{q_{s}}
, i.e.,
q_{s}=\frac{6}{1+2s}\in[2,6]
. Then by Hölder and Sobolev
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{q_{s}}
, we find

\displaystyle\|\rho\|_{L^{p_{s}}}\leq\|\psi-\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|\psi\|_{L^{q_{s}}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{q_{s}}}\big)\leq C\,\|\psi-\phi\|_{L^{2}}\big(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}\big)

(3.61)

for all
s\in[0,1/2]
(Note that
C_{s}\lesssim 1
uniformly on this interval).

For the last estimate, we have

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}

\displaystyle=\||\psi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}.

(3.62)

Using (
3.60
) and Hölder’s inequality, we find

\displaystyle\||\psi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi-\phi\|_{L^{6}}\,\big(\|\psi\|_{L^{3}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{3}}\big).

(3.63)

By Sobolev embedding
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,

\displaystyle\|\psi-\phi\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\,\|\nabla(\psi-\phi)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\,\|\psi-\phi\|_{H^{1}}.

(3.64)

To bound the
L^{3}
-norms, we use
\|\psi\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\,\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{1/2}
. Thus

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)-\sigma(\phi)\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}=\||\psi|^{2}-|\phi|^{2}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\,\|\psi-\phi\|_{H^{1}}\,\big(\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}+\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}\big).

(3.65)

as claimed.
∎

4
The dressed Nelson Hamiltonian

In this section, we introduce the dressed Nelson Hamiltonian, which is related to the renormalized Nelson Hamiltonian
H^{\varepsilon}
through the unitary dressing transformation (
2.6
). The dressing transformation allows us to isolate the UV singular term and work with the dressed Hamiltonian. While the dressed Hamiltonian has a more complicated (quadratic) structure, it involves more regular kernels. In a second step, we derive a suitable Weyl-shifted representation of the dressed Hamiltonian.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
U_{K}^{\varepsilon}
be defined as in (

There exists
K_{0}>0
such that for all

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon}=(U_{K}^{\varepsilon})^{*}H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}U_{K}^{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}=p^{2}+\varepsilon\Big(\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p+2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}\Big(H_{f}+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+4\pi\ln K\Big)

Moreover, there are constants

\displaystyle\pm\big(H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}-\big(p^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}H_{f}\big)\big)

\displaystyle\pm\big(H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}-\big(p^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}H_{f}\big)\big)

and
\varepsilon>0

The dressing identity for

Because of the semiclassical scaling and our slightly modified choice of dressing transformation,

The two energy bounds follow from Lemmas

5
Well-posedness and properties of the aKG equation

In the following we prove Proposition
1.2
, that is, we establish local well-posedness of the aKG equation. Moreover, we derive an equation of motion for the ground state
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
and establish some important estimates for
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
and the resolvent
R_{\varphi_{t}}
.

We recall the notation for the lowest eigenvalue
e(\varphi)=\inf\sigma(h_{\varphi})
of the operator
h_{\varphi}
as well as for the spectral gap
\triangle(\varphi)=\inf\{|e(\varphi)-\lambda|:\lambda\in\sigma(h_{\varphi})\setminus\{e(\varphi)\}\}
. Let us further recall that
\varphi\in C([-T,T];\mathfrak{h}_{1/2})
is called a solution to (
aKG
) with initial condition
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
, in case that
e(\varphi_{t})<0
for all
t\in[-T,T]
and

\displaystyle\varphi_{t}=e^{-i\omega t}\varphi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\omega(t-s)}\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\mathrm{d}s,

(5.1)

where
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
is the unique ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}
and
\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})=\omega^{-1/2}\widehat{|\psi_{\varphi_{s}}|^{2}}
. Existence and uniqueness of
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
follow from the condition
e(\varphi_{t})<0
.

We prepare the proof of Proposition
1.2
with two lemmas: the first establishes local stability of the spectral gap, and the second shows that the map
\varphi\mapsto\psi_{\varphi}
is locally Lipschitz.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi)<0
and spectral gap
\triangle(\varphi)>0
and let

\displaystyle B_{r}(\varphi):=\big\{u\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}:\|u-\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\leq r\big\}.

(5.2)

Then there exist
C>0
and
r_{0}>0
such that for all
0<r\leq r_{0}
and all
u\in B_{r}(\varphi)
,

\displaystyle\triangle(u)\ \geq\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\triangle(\varphi)\ >0.

(5.3)

Moreover,
|e(\varphi)-e(u)|+|\lambda_{1}(\varphi)-\lambda_{1}(u)|\leq Cr
for all
u\in B_{r}(\varphi)
, where
\lambda_{1}(u)
denotes the first excited eigenvalue of
h_{u}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.7
, there is
C>0
so that for all

\varphi,\xi\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
and all
\psi\in H^{1}
with
\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}=1
,

\displaystyle\big|\langle\psi,(h_{\varphi}-h_{\xi})\psi\rangle\big|\ \leq\ C\,\|\varphi-\xi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\ \|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

(5.4)

Let
e(u)=\inf\sigma(h_{u})
and let
\psi_{\varphi}
denote the ground state of
h_{\varphi}
. The variational principle combined with the above inequality gives

\displaystyle e(u)\ \leq\ \langle\psi_{\varphi},h_{u}\psi_{\varphi}\rangle\ \leq\ e(\varphi)+C\,\|\varphi-u\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\|\psi_{\varphi}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\ \leq\ e(\varphi)+Cr,

(5.5)

and thus for
r
sufficiently small,
e(u)<0
. Denoting now by
\psi_{u}
the ground state of
h_{u}
and exchanging
\varphi,u
in the above estimate yields

\displaystyle|e(\varphi)-e(u)|\ \leq\ Cr

(5.6)

for some constant
C
depending only on
\varphi
. Note that here we also used the estimate

\displaystyle\|\psi_{u}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C\big(1+\|u\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2}+e(u)\big)\leq C\big(1+\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2}\big)\leq C

(5.7)

as a consequence of Lemma
3.7
.

Next, write
\lambda_{1}(u):=\inf\big(\sigma(h_{u})\setminus\{e(u)\}\big)
, and recall by the max-min principle that

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u)=\sup_{\mathrm{codim}(V)=1}\ \inf_{\psi\in V}\big\{\langle\psi,h_{u}\psi\rangle:\|\psi\|_{L^{2}}=1\big\}.

(5.8)

Choosing
V=\{\psi_{\varphi}\}^{\perp}
and using (
5.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1},\ \|\psi\|=1\\
\psi\perp\psi_{\varphi}\end{subarray}}\langle\psi,h_{u}\psi\rangle\ \geq\ \inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1},\ \|\psi\|=1\\
\psi\perp\psi_{\varphi}\end{subarray}}\left(\langle\psi,h_{\varphi}\psi\rangle\;-\;Cr\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right).

(5.9)

Using the bound (
3.47
) and
\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\leq C
, we have
\|\psi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C(1+\langle\psi,h_{\varphi}\psi\rangle)
, and thus

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(u)

\displaystyle\geq(1-Cr)\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}\psi\in H^{1},\ \|\psi\|=1\\
\psi\perp\psi_{\varphi}\end{subarray}}\langle\psi,h_{\varphi}\psi\rangle-Cr=(1-Cr)\lambda_{1}(\varphi)-Cr.

(5.10)

Since
\triangle(u)=\lambda_{1}(u)-e(u)
, combining the above estimate with (
5.6
), we arrive at

\displaystyle\triangle(u)\ \geq\ \triangle(\varphi)-Cr.

(5.11)

Choosing
r_{0}>0
sufficiently small then yields
\triangle(u)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\,\triangle(\varphi)>0
for all
u\in B_{r}(\varphi)
with
0<r\leq r_{0}
. Exchanging the roles of
u
and
\varphi
in the argument for
\lambda_{1}
gives the reverse comparison

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}(\varphi)

\displaystyle\geq(1-Cr)\,\lambda_{1}(u)-Cr

(5.12)

with a constant
C
that depends only on
\varphi
, hence
|\lambda_{1}(u)-\lambda_{1}(\varphi)|\leq Cr
. Together with (
5.6
) this proves the last statement.
∎

Lemma 5.2

Let

We apply (

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

e(\xi_{t})<0

\|\psi_{\xi_{s}}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C(1+\|\xi_{s}\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}})
, and therefore

\displaystyle\|\xi_{t}-\varphi_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\leq\left\|(e^{-it\omega}-1)\varphi_{0}\right\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}+CT\sup_{|s|\leq T}C(1+\|\xi_{s}\|^{2}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}})

(5.28)

By continuity of
t\mapsto\|\xi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}
, we conclude that
\xi\in C([-T,T];B_{r}(\varphi_{0}))
for
T
small enough. By the contraction argument above the fixed point is unique in
X_{T}
, so
\xi=\varphi
on
[-T,T]
.

C^{1}
-regularity.

Set
g(t):=\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})
. The map
t\mapsto g(t)
is continuous in
\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}
by Lemma
3.8
,
(
5.23
), and Lemma
5.2
. Let
v(t):=e^{i\omega t}\varphi_{t}
. From (
1.18
),

\displaystyle v(t)=\varphi_{0}-i\int_{0}^{t}e^{i\omega s}\,g(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

(5.29)

and since
g\in C([-T,T];\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2})
, the integral is
C^{1}
with
\partial_{t}v(t)=-i\,e^{i\omega t}\,g(t)
in
\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}
.
Thus
\varphi_{t}=e^{-i\omega t}v(t)\in C^{1}([-T,T];\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2})
and satisfies the differential aKG in
\mathfrak{h}_{-1/2}
.

L^{2}
-persistence and continuity.

Assume
\varphi_{0}\in L^{2}
. From (
1.18
) and the unitarity of
e^{-i\omega t}
,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\;\leq\;\|\varphi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}\;+\;\int_{0}^{|t|}\|\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\|_{L^{2}}\,\textnormal{d}s,

(5.30)

and Lemma
3.8
yields
\sup_{|s|\leq T}\|\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
, so
\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(T)
. Moreover, for
t,t_{0}\in[-T,T]
,

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\;\leq\;\|(e^{-i\omega(t-t_{0})}-1)\varphi_{t_{0}}\|_{L^{2}}\;+\;\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\|_{L^{2}}\,\textnormal{d}s,

(5.31)

and both terms vanish as
t\to t_{0}
, hence
\varphi\in C([-T,T];L^{2})
.

Energy conservation.
Using the Hellmann–Feynman identity (
5.44
), we see that the aKG solution conserves the energy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm field}
defined in (
1.21
):

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{t})\,=\,\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\dot{\varphi}_{t},\omega\varphi_{t}\rangle\,=\,0.

(5.32)

Indeed, inserting
\dot{\varphi}_{t}=-i(\omega\varphi_{t}+\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}}))
into the second term produces
2\textnormal{Re}\langle\dot{\varphi}_{t},\omega\varphi_{t}\rangle=-2\textnormal{Im}\langle\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}}),\omega\varphi_{t}\rangle=-\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle
, which cancels the first contribution.

Continuation and alternative.

Let
I
be any open existence interval containing
0
. By energy conservation and (
3.46d
), there exist
0<\alpha<1
and
C_{0}>0
such that

\displaystyle(1-\alpha)\,\|\varphi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2}\;\leq\;\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{0})+C_{0},\qquad\forall t\in I,

(5.33)

hence
\sup_{t\in I}\|\varphi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}<\infty
. On any compact interval
J\subset I
with
\inf_{t\in J}\triangle(\varphi_{t})>0
, Lemma
5.2
shows that
u\mapsto\psi_{u}
is Lipschitz on
\{\varphi_{t}:t\in J\}
, and hence
u\mapsto\sigma(\psi_{u})
is locally Lipschitz in
\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
. Together with the a priori bound (
5.33
), this allows the standard continuation argument to extend the solution beyond
J
. Therefore, if
T_{+}<\infty
resp.
T_{-}<\infty
, the only obstruction to continuation is closing of the gap:

\displaystyle\liminf_{t\uparrow T_{+}}\triangle(\varphi_{t})=0\qquad\text{resp.}\qquad\liminf_{t\downarrow-T_{-}}\triangle(\varphi_{t})=0.

(5.34)

This yields the continuation alternative and shows that the existence interval is maximal.
∎

In the remainder of this section, we prove estimates on the reduced resolvent

\displaystyle R_{\varphi_{t}}=Q_{\varphi_{t}}(h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t}))^{-1}Q_{\varphi_{t}},

(5.35)

and derive an equation of motion for
t\mapsto\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\varphi_{t}
denote the solution to (
aKG
) for initial data
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
, and let
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
be the non-negative normalized ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}
. Then, there exists
T>0
such that the following statements hold for all
|t|\leq T
.

(i)

There is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|R_{\varphi_{t}}\|+\|\langle p\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}\|+\|\langle p^{2}\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}\|+\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C.

(5.36)

(ii)

The map
t\mapsto\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
is in
C((-T,T);H^{1})\cap C^{1}((-T,T);L^{2})
and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}.

(5.37)

(iii)

There is
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|\dot{\psi}_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\sigma}(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{R}_{\varphi_{t}}\|+\|\langle p\rangle\dot{R}_{\varphi_{t}}\langle p\rangle\|

\displaystyle\leq C\

(5.38)

where
\|\cdot\|
denotes the operator norm on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, and
\dot{f}(t):=\partial_{t}f(t)
,

Proof.

The first bound
\|R_{\varphi_{t}}\|\lesssim 1
obviously follows from the spectral gap
\inf_{|t|\leq T}\triangle(\varphi_{t})>0
. For the second bound, use
p^{2}=h_{\varphi_{t}}-V_{\varphi_{t}}
and
h_{\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}=Q_{\varphi_{t}}+e(\varphi_{t})R_{\varphi_{t}}
so that

\displaystyle R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}\langle p\rangle^{2}R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}=R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}(1+p^{2})R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}=R_{\varphi_{t}}(1+e(\varphi_{t}))+Q_{\varphi_{t}}-R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}V_{\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2},

(5.39)

which we use to estimate

\displaystyle\|\langle p\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}\|^{2}\lesssim 1+\|R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}\|\,\|\langle p\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}^{1/2}\|

(5.40)

where the last step follows from Lemma
3.7
(
3.46a
) as well as
|e(\varphi_{t})|\lesssim 1
. This proves the second bound. We proceed similarly in the third bound, namely by writing
\langle p^{2}\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}=R_{\varphi_{t}}(1+e(\varphi_{t}))+Q_{\varphi_{t}}-V_{\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}
,
which we now estimate as

\displaystyle\|\langle p^{2}\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}\|

\displaystyle\lesssim 1+\|V_{\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}\|\lesssim 1+\|\langle p\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}\|\lesssim 1

(5.41)

where we used again (
3.46a
). For the last bound in (i), we proceed one more time in the same spirit, that is

\displaystyle\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq 1+\|p^{2}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq 1+|e(\varphi_{t})|+\|V_{\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 1+\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

(5.42)

That
\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim 1
follows as in (
5.23
). This proves the first statement.

Regarding the second statement, note that continuity and differentiability of
t\mapsto\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
follow directly from Lemma
5.2
in combination with continuity and differentiability of
t\mapsto\varphi_{t}
. Moreover, observe that

\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle=2\textnormal{Re}\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle=\partial_{t}\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|^{2}=0
.

To obtain (
5.37
), we differentiate the eigenvalue equation
(h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t}))\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=0
to find

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}})\psi_{\varphi_{t}}+(h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t}))\,\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}-\dot{e}(\varphi_{t})\,\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=0.

(5.43)

Taking the scalar product with
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
yields the Hellmann–Feynman identity

\displaystyle\dot{e}(\varphi_{t})=\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}})\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle.

(5.44)

Using
Q_{\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=0
, this gives

\displaystyle Q_{\varphi_{t}}(h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t}))Q_{\varphi_{t}}\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=-\,Q_{\varphi_{t}}(\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}})\,\psi_{\varphi_{t}}.

(5.45)

By uniform positivity of the spectral gap, the operator
h_{\varphi_{t}}-e(\varphi_{t})
is invertible on
\text{Ran}(Q_{\varphi_{t}})
with bounded inverse
R_{\varphi_{t}}
, and thus

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=-\,R_{\varphi_{t}}(\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}})\,\psi_{\varphi_{t}}.

(5.46)

Since
h_{\varphi}=p^{2}+V_{\varphi}
depends linearly on
\varphi
, we have
\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}}=V_{\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}}
. Along the aKG flow, we have
\partial_{t}\varphi_{t}=-\,i\omega\varphi_{t}-i\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})
, hence
\partial_{t}h_{\varphi_{t}}=-\,V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}-V_{i\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})}
. Since
\overline{\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})(k)}=\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})(-k)
, we find
V_{i\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})}=0
, and thus
\partial_{t}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}=R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}
.

For the third statement, note that the bound on
\|\dot{\psi}_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{L^{2}}
was derived already in the proof of (ii). For the second bound, we compute

\displaystyle\dot{\sigma}(\psi_{\varphi_{t}})(k)=\langle R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\mathbb{G}(\cdot,k)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle+\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\mathbb{G}(\cdot,k)R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle.

(5.47)

Both terms are estimated in analogy to the proof of (
3.53
), which yields

\displaystyle|\langle R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\mathbb{G}(\cdot,k)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle|\lesssim\|R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{1}}\,\|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{1}}\lesssim 1.

(5.48)

For the last bound, observe that
\dot{Q}_{\varphi_{t}}=-\dot{P}_{\varphi_{t}}=P_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}-R_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}P_{\varphi_{t}}
with
P_{\varphi_{t}}=|\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}}|
to compute

\displaystyle\dot{R}_{\varphi_{t}}

\displaystyle=P_{\varphi_{t}}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}R_{\varphi_{t}}^{2}-R_{\varphi_{t}}^{2}V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}P_{\varphi_{t}}-R_{\varphi_{t}}(\dot{h}_{\varphi_{t}}-\dot{e}(\varphi_{t}))R_{\varphi_{t}}.

(5.49)

The bound
\|\dot{R}_{\varphi_{t}}\|+\|\langle p\rangle\dot{R}_{\varphi_{t}}\langle p\rangle\|\leq C
now follows from Lemma
3.7
(
3.46a
) in combination with the resolvent bounds from Item (i), as well as the estimate

\displaystyle\langle p\rangle^{-1}(\dot{h}_{\varphi_{t}}-\dot{e}(\varphi_{t}))\langle p\rangle^{-1}

\displaystyle=\langle p\rangle^{-1}\left(V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}-\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},V_{i\omega\varphi_{t}}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle\right)\langle p\rangle^{-1}\lesssim 1

(5.50)

where the last step is a consequence of the form bound
\pm V_{\varphi}\leq C\langle p^{2}\rangle\|\varphi\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}
from (
3.46a
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.

6
Renormalization of the quantum fluctuations

In this section, we construct the
renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson evolution
, which describes quantum field fluctuations around the classical aKG field. Our main goal is to prove Proposition
1.4
, derive important properties of the unitary propagator
\mathbb{U}(t)
, and obtain an explicit expression for its quadratic generator. The proof relies on two technical lemmas.

The first lemma provides a representation of the quadratic operator introduced in (
1.27
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)=H_{f}-\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})R_{\varphi_{t}}\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle,

(6.1)

in terms of a quadratic operator
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
with cutoffs
2\leq K\leq\Lambda\leq\infty
and an explicit energy shift. This representation is analogous to (
4.1
), but with the important distinction that no dressing transformation is required to relate
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)-4\pi\ln\Lambda
. We nevertheless refer to
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
as the dressed Bogoliubov–Nelson Hamiltonian, since it corresponds to the Bogoliubov type approximation for the dressed Nelson Hamiltonian
H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}
. We will show that the operators
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
have a limit as
\Lambda\to\infty
.

The second lemma provides the necessary bounds for
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
to prove the existence of the corresponding unitary evolution. These results together constitute the main ingredients to prove Proposition
1.4
..

For
\Lambda>0
, we define the cutoff version of
\mathbb{B}_{K}
by

\displaystyle\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}(x,k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{B}_{K}(x,k)\mathbf{1}_{|k|\leq\Lambda}=\frac{e^{-ikx}}{|k|^{3/2}}\,\mathbf{1}_{K\leq|k|\leq\Lambda}\,.

(6.2)

The parameter
K
plays the same role as the cutoff appearing in Lemma
4.1
. Throughout this section, all generators and evolutions are defined relative to
\varphi_{t}
, a solution to (
aKG
) for initial data
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{t})<0
, as described in the beginning of Section
1.3
.

We then introduce the dressed Bogoliubov–Nelson Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=H_{f}+\big\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},\,\big(D_{K,\Lambda}+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}}),\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}\rangle-A_{K,\Lambda}R_{\varphi_{t}}A_{K,\Lambda}\big)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\big\rangle_{L^{2}}\,,

(6.3)

\displaystyle A_{K,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})+2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})+2a^{*}(\mathbb{k}B_{K,\Lambda})\cdot p,

(6.4)

\displaystyle D_{K,\Lambda}

\displaystyle=2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})\,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})^{2}+a(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})^{2}+4\pi\ln K\,.

(6.5)

Observe that
A_{K,\infty}=A_{K}
and
D_{K,\infty}=D_{K}
, as defined earlier in (
3.28
) and (
3.29
).

The next lemma shows that the form domain of
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
always contains the dense subspace
Q(H_{f})\subset\mathcal{F}
, for all
2\leq K<\Lambda\leq\infty
. In particular, setting
\Lambda=\infty
does not alter the domain. Moreover, it provides the key identity (in statement (ii)) and estimates (in statement (iii)) for the renormalization of the fluctuation dynamics.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)
and
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
be defined by (
1.27
) and (
6.3
), respectively, both relative to the aKG solution
\varphi_{t}
with initial data
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
satisfying
e(\varphi_{0})<0
. There exists
T>0
such that the following statements hold:

(i)

There exists
C>0
such that for all
|t|\leq T
, in the sense of quadratic forms,

\displaystyle\pm\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\Lambda^{2}\,H_{f}+C\qquad

for all

\displaystyle 1<\Lambda<\infty,

(6.6)

\displaystyle\pm\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\,K^{2}\,H_{f}+C\,\ln K\qquad

for all

\displaystyle 2\leq K<\Lambda\leq\infty.

(6.7)

(ii)

As quadratic forms on
Q(H_{f})
, we have the identity for all
|t|\leq T

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)=\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)+4\pi\ln\Lambda\qquad\text{for all }\quad 2\leq K<\Lambda<\infty\,.

(6.8)

(iii)

There is
C>0
such that, for all
2\leq K<\Lambda<\infty
,
|t|\leq T
and all normalized
\Psi,\Phi\in Q(H_{f})
,

\displaystyle\big|\big\langle\Psi,\,\big(\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\infty}(t)-\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)\big)\Phi\big\rangle\big|\leq\frac{C\,K^{1/2}}{\Lambda^{1/4}}\Big(\big\langle\Psi,H_{f}\Psi\big\rangle+\big\langle\Phi,H_{f}\Phi\big\rangle+1\Big)\,.

(6.9)

We refer to
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K}(t):=\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\infty}(t)
as the
renormalized Bogoliubov–Nelson Hamiltonian
.

Proof.

Proof of (i):
The first bound follows from (
3.32
) and
\|R_{\varphi_{t}}\|\lesssim 1
. For the second bound, we apply Lemma
3.5
in combination with
\|\langle p\rangle R_{\varphi_{t}}\langle p\rangle\|\lesssim 1
, see Lemma
5.3
, and
\|\langle p\rangle P_{\varphi_{t}}\|\lesssim 1
. Note that Lemma
3.5
was stated only for
\Lambda=\infty
, but the proofs generalize trivially to finite values of
\Lambda
.

Proof of (ii):
For notational simplicity, let us ignore the
t
-dependence and write
h:=h_{\varphi_{t}}
,
e:=e(\varphi_{t})
,
R:=R_{\varphi_{t}}
and
(h-e)R=Q=1-P
with
P=|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|
. In fact, the following argument relies only on these relations together with
(h-e)\psi=0
. Let us also introduce the anti-hermitian field operator
\phi^{-}(f):=a^{*}(f)-a(f)
, and for better readability, let us abbreviate
\mathbb{B}:=\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}
within this computation.

We start by noting that
2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B})a(k\mathbb{B})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B})a^{*}(k\mathbb{B})+a(k\mathbb{B})a(k\mathbb{B})=\phi(k\mathbb{B})^{2}-\|k\mathbb{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}
and write, using
\phi(k\mathbb{B})=-[p,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})]
,

\displaystyle\Big[[p^{2},\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})],\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\Big]=2([p,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})])^{2}-p[\phi(k\mathbb{B}),\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})]-[\phi(k\mathbb{B}),\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})]p.

The last two terms on the right side vanish, since
[\phi(k\mathbb{B}),\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})]=2\textnormal{Re}\langle\mathbb{B},k\mathbb{B}\rangle=0
. On the left side we can replace
p^{2}
by
h-e
in the commutator (since multiplication operators in
x
commute with
\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})
). Because
[p,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})]=\phi(k\mathbb{B})
, this leads to the identity

\displaystyle\phi(k\mathbb{B})^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\Big[\big[h-e,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\big],\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\Big]

(6.10)

and therefore, taking the partial expectation in
\psi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3}_{x})
and using
(h-e)\psi=0
,

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi,\phi(k\mathbb{B})^{2}\psi\big\rangle

\displaystyle=-\big\langle\psi,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})(h-e)\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\psi\big\rangle.

(6.11)

On the other hand, we use

\displaystyle 2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B})+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B})\cdot p

\displaystyle=-\big[h-e,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\big]-\phi(k^{2}\mathbb{B})

(6.12)

together with the identity
\mathbb{G}_{K}-k^{2}\mathbb{B}=\mathbb{G}_{K}-k^{2}\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}=\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}
and
P(h-e)=0
, so that

\displaystyle PA_{K,\Lambda}Q

\displaystyle=P\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})Q+P\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})(h-e)Q,

(6.13)

\displaystyle QA_{K,\Lambda}P

\displaystyle=Q\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})P-Q(h-e)\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})P.

(6.14)

As a result, we have with
R=Q(h-e)^{-1}Q
:

\displaystyle\big\langle\psi,A_{K,\Lambda}RA_{K,\Lambda}\psi\big\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\psi,\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})R\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})Q\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad-\langle\psi,\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})Q\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\psi\rangle-\langle\psi,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})(h-e)\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\psi\rangle.

(6.15)

Next, write the second and third summands as

\displaystyle\langle\psi,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})Q\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})\psi\rangle-\langle\psi,\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})Q\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=\langle\psi,[\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B}),\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})]\psi\rangle-\langle\psi,\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})P\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})\psi\rangle+\langle\psi,\phi(\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda})P\phi^{-}(\mathbb{B})\psi\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=-2\textnormal{Re}\langle\mathbb{B},\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}\rangle+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\langle\psi,\mathbb{G}_{\Lambda}\psi\rangle,\langle\psi,\mathbb{B}\psi\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\quad=-2\textnormal{Re}\langle\mathbb{B},\mathbb{G}\rangle+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\sigma(\psi),\langle\psi,\mathbb{B}\psi\rangle\rangle.

(6.16)

Taking all the above into account we arrive at the claimed identity:

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)+4\pi\ln K-\|k\mathbb{B}\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\mathbb{B},\mathbb{G}\rangle=\mathbb{H}_{\Lambda}(t)+4\pi\ln\Lambda,

(6.17)

where we used
\langle\mathbb{B},2\mathbb{G}-k^{2}\mathbb{B}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{B},\mathbb{G}\rangle=\langle\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda},\mathbb{G}\rangle=4\pi\ln\Lambda-4\pi\ln K
. This completes the proof of part (ii).

Proof of (iii):
Using
\mathbb{B}_{K}-\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}=\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}
with
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}
and
\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}
defined as in (
2.6
), we can write

\displaystyle{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathscr{D},K,\infty}(t)-{\mathbb{H}}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=\big\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(D_{K}-D_{K,\Lambda}+2\text{Re}\langle\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t}}),\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}\rangle)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\big\rangle

(6.18a)

\displaystyle\quad+\big(\big\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(A_{K,\Lambda}-A_{K})R_{\varphi_{t}}A_{K}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\big\rangle+H.c.\big),

(6.18b)

where the scalar product denotes again the partial expectation in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
.

To bound the second line, note that
A_{K}-A_{K,\Lambda}=2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\cdot p+2p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})
. Thus, we can use Cauchy–Schwarz and estimate via Lemma
3.5
:

\displaystyle\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},A_{K,\Lambda}R_{\varphi_{t}}A_{K,\Lambda}\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\big\rangle\leq CK^{2}(H_{f}+1)

(6.19)

for all
\Lambda\in[K,\infty]
, as well as via (
3.33
) and (

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{4}\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(A_{K}-A_{K,\Lambda})R_{\varphi_{t}}(A_{K}-A_{K,\Lambda})\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle

\displaystyle\quad\leq\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\cdot p)R_{\varphi_{t}}(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}))\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle+\langle\psi_{\varphi_{t}},(p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda}))R_{\varphi_{t}}(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})\cdot p)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\rangle

Combining the above estimates, this gives for all

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,\eqref{eq:Bog:difference:Lambda:2}\Phi\rangle|\lesssim CK\Lambda^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\langle\Psi,\mathrm{T}\Psi\rangle+\langle\Phi,\mathrm{T}\Phi\rangle+1).

(6.21)

For (

), we use again

to get:

\displaystyle D_{K}-D_{K,\Lambda}=2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})+2a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})a(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\big(a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{\Lambda})a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda})+H.c.\big).

It then follows from the bounds in Lemma

that for all

\displaystyle|\langle\Psi,(D_{K}-D_{K,\Lambda})\Phi\rangle|\lesssim C(K\Lambda)^{-\frac{1}{4}}(\langle\Psi,H_{f}\Psi\rangle+\langle\Phi,H_{f}\Phi\rangle+1).

(6.23)

The remaining term in (

), is bounded using

, see Lemma

\|B_{\Lambda}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\Lambda^{-1}
. This completes the proof of the lemma.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)
be defined by (

), relative to the (
aKG
) solution
\varphi_{t}
with initial data
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}

(6.24b)

(6.24c)

for all
|t|\leq T
and
K_{0}\leq K<\Lambda\leq\infty

Proof.

For the upper bound in (

3.5
(

) to estimate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbb{H}_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}(t)

\displaystyle=2\textnormal{Re}\big\langle\dot{\psi}_{\varphi_{t}},\big(D_{K,\Lambda}+2\textnormal{Re}\langle\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{t})},\mathbb{B}_{K,\Lambda}\rangle-A_{K,\Lambda}R_{\varphi_{t}}A_{K,\Lambda}\big)\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\big\rangle

.

Let
\varphi_{t}
denote the solution to (

.

.

The existence of the unitary evolution, strong continuity and the bound (

,

\displaystyle=\phi(\mathbb{G}_{K})+2\,a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\cdot p+2\,p\cdot a(k\mathbb{B}_{K}),

(7.17a)

\displaystyle D_{K}

\displaystyle=2\,a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})\,a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})+a^{*}(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}+a(k\mathbb{B}_{K})^{2}+4\pi\ln K,

7.2
Second-order expansion

The key ingredient for the higher-order expansion is the following identity

Third-order expansion

It remains to estimate the contribution

Lemma 7.2

For every

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{|k|\geq L}\|_{L^{2}}

(7.55)

\displaystyle\|\omega\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{|k|\leq L}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\ln L)^{\frac{1}{2}}\|\psi\|_{H^{2}}

(7.56)

Proof.

Writing
e^{-ikx}=|k|^{-4}[(k\cdot p),[(k\cdot p),e^{-ikx}]]
, we can estimate

\displaystyle\|\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{|k|\geq L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{|k|\geq L}\textnormal{d}k\,\omega^{-1}(k)|\langle\psi,e^{-ik(\cdot)}\psi\rangle|^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{2}}\int\limits_{|k|\geq L}\frac{\textnormal{d}k}{|k|^{5}}.

(7.57)

For the second one, use
\|\omega\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{|k|\leq L}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim 1+\|\omega\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{2\leq|k|\leq K}\|_{L^{2}}
and estimate again with the above identity,

\displaystyle\|\omega\sigma(\psi)\mathbf{1}_{2\leq|k|\leq L}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int\limits_{2\leq|k|\leq K}\textnormal{d}k\,\omega(k)|\langle\psi,e^{-ik(\cdot)}\psi\rangle|^{2}\lesssim\|\psi\|_{H^{2}}\int\limits_{2\leq|k|\leq L}\frac{\textnormal{d}k}{|k|^{3}}.

(7.58)

This proves the lemma.
∎

\displaystyle:=U_{K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}\Big(H_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}+4\pi\varepsilon^{2}\ln\Lambda\Big)(U_{K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon})^{\ast}

\displaystyle=p^{2}+\varepsilon\Big(\phi(\boldsymbol{G}_{K})+2\,a^{\ast}(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})\cdot p+2\,p\cdot a(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon^{2}\Big(H_{f}+2\,a^{\ast}(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})a(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})+a(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})^{2}+a^{\ast}(k\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})^{2}+4\pi\ln K\Big)

\displaystyle\quad-\varepsilon^{3}\,\phi(\omega\boldsymbol{B}_{K,\Lambda})+\varepsilon^{4}\,\|\omega^{1/2}B_{K,\Lambda}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(A.7)

Using the bounds (
3.24a
)–(
3.24d
) (which apply to
B_{K,\Lambda}
as well), for any
\delta>0
there exist
C_{\delta},K_{0}>0
such that for all
\Lambda\geq K\geq K_{0}
and
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\pm\big(H_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}-p^{2}-\varepsilon^{2}H_{f}\big)\;\leq\;\delta\,(p^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}H_{f})+C_{\delta}K

(A.8)

as quadratic forms. By the KLMN theorem,
H_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}
is thus self-adjoint and semibounded, with form domain
Q(p^{2}+H_{f})
.

Moreover, following
[
15
, Lem. 3.2]
and using again (
3.24a
)–(
3.24d
),
there exist constants
C>0
and
\Lambda_{0}\geq K_{0}
such that, for all

\Lambda_{2}\geq\Lambda_{1}\geq\Lambda_{0}
and
K\geq K_{0}
,

\displaystyle\pm\big(H_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda_{2}}^{\varepsilon}-H_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda_{1}}^{\varepsilon}\big)\;\leq\;C\,\Lambda_{1}^{-1/4}\,(p^{2}+\varepsilon^{2}H_{f}).

(A.9)

By
[
15
, Thm. A.1]
there exists a self-adjoint, semibounded operator

H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}:=H_{\mathscr{D},K,\infty}^{\varepsilon}
with form domain
Q(p^{2}+H_{f})
such that

\displaystyle s-\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}e^{-itH_{\mathscr{D},K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}}=e^{-itH_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}}\qquad(\forall t\in\mathbb{R}).

(A.10)

Since
U_{K,\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow[\Lambda\to\infty]{}U_{K,\infty}^{\varepsilon}
in the strong sense, we define

\displaystyle H^{\varepsilon}:=(U_{K,\infty}^{\varepsilon})^{\ast}\,H_{\mathscr{D},K}^{\varepsilon}\,U_{K,\infty}^{\varepsilon},

(A.11)

which satisfies

\displaystyle s-\lim_{\Lambda\to\infty}e^{-itH_{\Lambda}^{\varepsilon}}\,e^{-it\,4\pi\varepsilon^{2}\ln\Lambda}=e^{-itH^{\varepsilon}}\qquad(t\in\mathbb{R}).

(A.12)

This proves Proposition
1.1
.
∎

).
The energy bounds (

Appendix B
Global existence of aKG near minimizers

In this appendix we prove Lemma
1.3
on global well-posedness of the aKG dynamics for initial data close to the manifold of minimizers of the field energy. Recall the field functional

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u):=e(u)+\|u\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2},

(B.1)

with minimal value
E_{*}=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{*})
attained by minimizers
\varphi_{*}=-\omega^{-1}\sigma(\psi_{*})
, where
\psi_{*}
is a minimizer of the Pekar functional (known to be unique up to translations
[
30
]
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\rm P}(\psi)=\langle\psi,p^{2}\psi\rangle-\|\omega^{-1/2}\sigma(\psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(B.2)

The manifold of field minimizers is

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}:=\{\,T_{y}\varphi_{*}:y\in\mathbb{R}^{3}\,\},\qquad(T_{y}u)(k):=e^{-ik\cdot y}u(k).

(B.3)

The proof of Lemma
1.3
is based on the following coercivity estimate for the field functional.

Lemma B.1
.

There exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u)-E_{*}\;\geq\;c\ \mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}\qquad\forall\,u\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}.

(B.4)

This follows from the known coercivity of the Pekar functional (modulo translations and phase, see
[
10
]
). We omit the proof.

Proof of Lemma
1.3
.

We start by noting that for any
u\in\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
and normalized
\psi\in H^{1}
, the variational principle gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u)\;\leq\;\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{P}}(\psi)+\|\omega^{1/2}u+\omega^{-1/2}\sigma(\psi)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(B.5)

Choosing
\psi=T_{y}\psi_{*}
and recalling
\varphi_{*}=-\omega^{-1}\sigma(\psi_{*})
yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u)-E_{*}\;\leq\;\|u-T_{y}\varphi_{*}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}^{2},

(B.6)

and taking the infimum over
y
,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(u)-E_{*}\;\leq\;\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(u,\mathcal{M})^{2}.

(B.7)

Now, let
\varphi_{t}
be the aKG solution with initial data
\varphi_{0}
on the maximal interval
(-T_{-},T_{+})
. We recall conservation of the energy from (
5.32
),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{0})\quad\text{for all}\quad t\in(-T_{-},T_{+}).

(B.8)

Combining (
B.4
) with energy conservation and (
B.6
), we thus obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(\varphi_{t},\mathcal{M})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\;c^{-1}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{t})-E_{*}\big)

\displaystyle=\;c^{-1}\big(\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{field}}(\varphi_{0})-E_{*}\big)

\displaystyle\leq\;c^{-1}\,\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(\varphi_{0},\mathcal{M})^{2},

(B.9)

for all
t\in(-T_{-},T_{+})
.

By assumption, we have
\mathrm{dist}_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}(\varphi_{0},\mathcal{M})\leq\eta
. Thus
\varphi_{t}
remains in a fixed neighborhood of
\mathcal{M}
for all
t\in(-T_{-},T_{+})
, meaning that for every such
t
there exists
\varphi_{*}\in\mathcal{M}
with

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{t}-\varphi_{*}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\;\leq\;c^{-1}\eta.

(B.10)

Since the spectral gap is the same for all
\varphi_{*}\in\mathcal{M}
and positive,
\triangle(\varphi_{*})>0
, we conclude (for sufficiently small
\eta
and by Lemma
5.1
) that

\displaystyle\triangle(\varphi_{t})\ \geq\ \tfrac{1}{2}\,\triangle(\varphi_{*})\ >\ 0\qquad\forall\,t\in(-T_{-},T_{+}).

(B.11)

If
T_{+}<\infty
, then
\liminf_{t\to T_{+}}\triangle(\varphi_{t})>0
,
contradicting the continuation alternative from Proposition
1.2
. Hence
T_{+}=\infty
. The same argument shows

T_{-}=\infty
, and thus the aKG solution with initial datum sufficiently close to
\mathcal{M}
is global.
∎

Appendix C
Convergence of SKG to aKG as
\varepsilon\to 0

We begin by recalling well-posedness results for the SKG system.
Global well-posedness of (
SKG
) with
\varepsilon=1
was proved in
[
36
, Thm. 1.4]
for initial data
(\psi_{0},\varphi_{0})\in H^{s}\times\mathfrak{h}_{s-1/2}
with
s\geq 0
.
The solutions conserve mass,
\|\psi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}=\|\psi_{0}\|_{L^{2}}
, and, for
s\geq 1
, also conserve the semiclassical energy
\mathcal{E}(\psi_{t},\varphi_{t})=\mathcal{E}(\psi_{0},\varphi_{0})
defined in (
1.11
).
Moreover, if
(\psi_{0},\varphi_{0})\in H^{1}\times\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
, then

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\big(\|\psi_{t}\|_{H^{1}}+\|\varphi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\big)<\infty.

(C.1)

For general semiclassical parameter
\varepsilon>0
, global well-posedness in
H^{1}\times\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
, together with the same conservation laws and uniform
H^{1}\times\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
bounds (uniform in
\varepsilon>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
), follows by adapting the analysis of the Landau–Pekar equations in
[
11
, Lemma 2.1]
.

In the next lemma we show that, on the slow time scale
t=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{-2})
, SKG solutions converge to the aKG solution pair.

Lemma C.1
.

Let
\varphi_{0}\in\mathfrak{h}_{0}\cap\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}
with
e(\varphi_{0})<0
. Let
\varphi_{t}
be the solution to (
aKG
) with initial datum
\varphi_{0}
and let
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
denote the normalized non-negative ground state of
h_{\varphi_{t}}
. Moreover, let
(\psi_{t}^{\varepsilon},\varphi_{t}^{\varepsilon})
denote the solutions to (
SKG
) for initial data
(\psi_{\varphi_{0}},\varphi_{0})
.
Then there exist
C,T,\varepsilon_{0}>0
, such that

\displaystyle\Big\|\exp\left(i\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}e\left(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{-2}s}\right)\,\textnormal{d}s\right)\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}-\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\Big\|_{L^{2}}+\,\Big\|\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}-\varphi_{t}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2/7}

for all
|t|\leq T
and
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0}]
, where
e(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{s})=\inf\sigma(h_{\varphi_{s}}^{\varepsilon})
.

Proof.

We denote the SKG solutions on the slow time scale by
u_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\psi^{\varepsilon}_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}
and
w_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=\varphi_{\varepsilon^{-2}t}^{\varepsilon}
and recall that they satisfy

\displaystyle i\partial_{t}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-2}h_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}u_{t}^{\varepsilon},\qquad\qquad i\partial_{t}w_{t}^{\varepsilon}=\omega w_{t}^{\varepsilon}+\sigma(u_{t}^{\varepsilon}).

(C.2)

For the field we estimate

\displaystyle\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\leq\|(e^{-i\omega t}-1)\varphi_{0}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}+\int_{0}^{|t|}\|\sigma(u_{s}^{\varepsilon})\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\,\textnormal{d}s\,,

(C.3)

and use that the first term approaches zero as
|t|\to 0
and that the second term is bounded by a constant times
T
(use Lemma
3.8
together with
\sup_{\varepsilon>0}\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|u_{t}^{\varepsilon}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C
).

Therefore, by Lemma
5.1
, there exists
T>0
such that the spectral gap associated with
w_{t}^{\varepsilon}
remains positive, i.e.
\inf_{\varepsilon>0}\inf_{|t|\leq T}\triangle(w_{t}^{\varepsilon})>0
. In particular, for every
|t|\leq T
the operator
h_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}
has a unique non-negative ground state
\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}
with eigenvalue
e(w_{t}^{\varepsilon})<0
.

The ground state approximates the particle component of the SKG solution via

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{i\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}s}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2/7},\qquad|t|\leq T.

(C.4)

This can be interpreted as a nonlinear adiabatic theorem for the SKG dynamics, and its proof follows the same strategy as the nonlinear adiabatic theorem for the Landau–Pekar equations in
[
29
, Thm II.1]
. For completeness, we sketch the proof of (
C.4
) at the end of this section.

Moreover, since
\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}\leq CT
, Lemma
5.2
applies to
\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\geq 0
and
\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\geq 0
, yielding

\displaystyle\|\psi_{w_{t}}^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{t}\|_{\mathfrak{h}_{1/2}}.

(C.5)

Combining this with (
C.4
) gives

\displaystyle\Big\|e^{i\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{t}e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}s}u_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{\varphi_{t}}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon+C\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}.

(C.6)

Finally, for
|t|\leq T
we estimate

\displaystyle\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{|t|}\|\sigma(u_{s}^{\varepsilon})-\sigma(\psi_{\varphi_{s}})\|_{L^{2}}\,\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{|t|}\Big\|e^{i\varepsilon^{-2}\int_{0}^{s}e(w_{r}^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}r}u_{s}-\psi_{\varphi_{s}}\Big\|_{L^{2}}\,\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2/7}+C\int_{0}^{|t|}\|w_{s}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{s}\|_{L^{2}}\,\textnormal{d}s,

(C.7)

where we used that
\sigma(\psi)
is quadratic in
\psi
. By Grönwall’s inequality we obtain
\|w_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\varphi_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\varepsilon^{2/7}
, and inserting this into (
C.6
) proves the claimed bounds.
∎

Proof of Inequality (
C.4
).

We briefly sketch the proof of the adiabatic bound (
C.4
).
Since the spectral gap
\triangle(w_{t}^{\varepsilon})>0
persists uniformly for
|t|\leq T
and
\varepsilon>0
, all estimates from Lemma
5.3
apply to
w_{t}^{\varepsilon}
. In particular, there exists
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\|R_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|+\|\langle p\rangle R_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}^{1/2}\|+\|\langle p^{2}\rangle R_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|+\|\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C,

(C.8)

\displaystyle\|\dot{\psi}_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{\sigma}(\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\dot{R}_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|+\|\langle p\rangle\dot{R}_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\langle p\rangle\|

\displaystyle\leq C,

(C.9)

for all
|t|\leq T
and
\varepsilon>0
. Here,
R_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}=Q_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}(h_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}-e(w_{t}^{\varepsilon}))^{-1}Q_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}
with
Q_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}=\mathbb{1}-|\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\langle\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}|
. Moreover,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}=R_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}V_{i\omega w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}.

(C.10)

Define
\widetilde{u}_{t}^{\varepsilon}:=e^{i\int_{0}^{t}e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon})\,\textnormal{d}s}\,u_{t}^{\varepsilon}
. Then

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\,\textnormal{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon},\;\big(\varepsilon^{-2}(h_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}-e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon}))-iR_{w_{s}}V_{i\omega w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\big)\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\,\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=-2\,\textnormal{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon},\;R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}V_{i\omega w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\,\textnormal{d}s,

(C.11)

where we used
h_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}=e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon})\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}
. The remaining term is estimated analogously to the contribution in
\mathscr{R}^{(4)}
in (
7.21d
). Decomposing
w_{s}^{\varepsilon}=w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}+w_{s,>L}^{\varepsilon}
with
w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}:=w_{s}^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{1}_{|k|\leq L}
, the momentum tail satisfies

\displaystyle\bigg|\int_{0}^{t}\langle\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon},\;R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}V_{i\omega w^{\varepsilon}_{s,>L}}\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\,\textnormal{d}s\bigg|\;\lesssim\;L^{-1/4}.

(C.12)

For the low-momentum part, we need to expand one time via the following identity

\displaystyle Q_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon}=R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}(h_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}-e(w_{s}^{\varepsilon}))\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon}=i\varepsilon^{2}\,R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\,\partial_{s}\widetilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{s},

(C.13)

which can be viewed as a classical version of (
7.28
). Note that we used
i\partial_{t}\widetilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}=(h_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}-e(w_{t}^{\varepsilon}))\widetilde{u}^{\varepsilon}_{t}
.

After integrating by parts we obtain

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-

\displaystyle\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-2\,\textnormal{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\langle i\partial_{s}\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon},\;R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}V_{i\omega w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}}\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\,\textnormal{d}s

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon^{2}\,\textnormal{Im}\langle\widetilde{u}_{s},\;R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}V_{i\omega w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}}\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\Big|_{0}^{t}

\displaystyle\quad+2\varepsilon^{2}\,\textnormal{Im}\int_{0}^{t}\langle\widetilde{u}_{s}^{\varepsilon},\;(2\dot{R}_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}+\dot{V}_{i\omega w^{\varepsilon}_{s,L}}+R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}^{2}V_{i\omega w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}}R_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}V_{i\omega w_{s,L}^{\varepsilon}})\psi_{w_{s}^{\varepsilon}}\rangle\,\textnormal{d}s.

(C.14)

Since these terms are completely analogous to the contributions
\mathscr{R}^{(4.32)}
and
\mathscr{R}^{(4.24)}
, we obtain for
L=\varepsilon^{-8/7}
that

\displaystyle\|\widetilde{u}_{t}^{\varepsilon}-\psi_{w_{t}^{\varepsilon}}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\;\lesssim\;L^{-1/4}+\varepsilon^{2}L^{3/2}\;\lesssim\;\varepsilon^{2/7}.

(C.15)

This proves (
C.4
).
∎

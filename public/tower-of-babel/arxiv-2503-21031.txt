A local treatment of finite alignment and path groupoids of nonfinitely aligned higher-rank graphs
Malcolm Jones

Abstract
. We give a local treatment of finite alignment by identifying the finitely aligned part of any (not necessarily finitely aligned) higher-rank graph. We show the finitely aligned part is itself a constellation and forms a finitely aligned relative category of paths together with the original higher-rank graph. We show that the elements of the finitely aligned part are precisely those whose cylinder sets are compact, which allows us to give novel definitions of locally compact path and boundary-path spaces for nonfinitely aligned higher-rank graphs. We extend a semigroup action and the associated semidirect product groupoid developed by Renault and Williams to define ample Hausdorff path and boundary-path groupoids. The groupoids are amenable for nonfinitely aligned k k -graphs by a result of Renault and Williams. In the finitely aligned case, the path groupoids coincide with Spielberg’s groupoids, and the boundary-path groupoid has an inverse semigroup model via a result of Ortega and Pardo.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Topology on the power set of a set

Let
\Lambda
be a countable set.
We describe a topology on the power set
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
that is a special case of the pointwise topology on function spaces, where
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
identifies with functions from
\Lambda
to the discrete space
\{0,1\}
(see for example
[
Wil70
, Definition 42.1]
).
Consider the product topology on
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\{0,1\}
, where each
\{0,1\}
is discrete.
The set
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\{0,1\}
identifies with
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
such that each standard basic open set in
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\{0,1\}
identifies with a
cylinder

\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)\;\middle|\;K_{1}\subseteq x\subseteq\Lambda\setminus K_{2}\right\},

for some finite (possibly empty)
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
.
For each
\mu\in\Lambda
and finite
K\subseteq\Lambda
, let

\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}\left(\{\mu\}\setminus K\right)\text{ and }\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\right)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\setminus\emptyset\right).

For each
X\subseteq\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
, the collection of

(2.1)

\mathcal{Z}_{X}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)\cap X,

where
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
are finite is a basis for the subspace topology on
X
.
We define
\mathcal{Z}_{X}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
and
\mathcal{Z}_{X}\left(\mu\right)
similarly.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable set.
The collection of cylinders in
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is a basis for a second-countable compact Hausdorff topology on
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
.
Moreover, each cylinder is compact.

Proof.

The collection of cylinders is a basis as it identifies with a standard basis for the product topology on
\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda}\{0,1\}
, which is a countable collection because
\Lambda
is countable, so
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is second-countable.
Tychonoff’s theorem implies
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is compact, and products of Hausdorff spaces are Hausdorff.
For all
\mu\in\Lambda
,
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)\setminus\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\right)=\mathcal{Z}\left(\emptyset\setminus\{\mu\}\right)
, so
\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\right)
is closed.
Thus, for
finite

K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
,
\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)
can be written as a finite intersection of closed sets, so
\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)
is closed and hence compact.
∎

Since
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is second-countable, closed sets and continuous functions are characterised as in
[
Wil70
, Corollary 10.5(b)–(c)]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable set.
Suppose
X
and
Y
are subspaces of
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
.
Then:

(a)

C\subseteq X
is closed if and only if
x_{n}\to x
and
(x_{n})\subseteq C
implies
x\in C
; and

(b)

f\colon X\to Y
is continuous if and only if
x_{n}\to x
in
X
implies
f(x_{n})\to f(x)
in
Y
.

Since
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is second-countable, we also get from Urysohn’s metrization theorem that any subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is metrizable, which is a sufficient condition for compactness to be equivalent to
sequential compactness
(i.e. every sequence has a convergent subsequence) as in
[
Mun00
, Theorem 28.2]
.
Thus:

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable set.
Every subspace of
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is compact if and only if it is sequentially compact.

If
(x_{n})
is a sequence in
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
and
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we say
\lambda
is
eventually inside (resp. outside)

(x_{n})
if there is
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\lambda\in x_{n}
(resp.
\lambda\notin x_{n}
) for all
n\geq N
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Lambda
be a countable set.
A sequence
(x_{n})
converges to
x
in
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
if and only if, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
:

(in)

if
\lambda\in x
,
\lambda
is eventually inside
(x_{n})
; and

(out)

if
\lambda\notin x
,
\lambda
is eventually outside
(x_{n})
.

Proof.

Suppose
x_{n}\to x\in\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
and fix
\lambda\in\Lambda
.
If
\lambda\notin x
, then
x\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\emptyset\setminus\{\lambda\}\right)
, so eventually
x_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}\left(\emptyset\setminus\{\lambda\}\right)
.
Thus,
(out)
holds.
A similar argument works for
(in)
.

For the other direction, consider a basic open set
\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)
containing
x
, where
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
are finite.
Hence,
K_{1}\subseteq x\subseteq\Lambda\setminus K_{2}
.
Let
N
be the maximum of the indices given by applying
(in)
to each element of
K_{1}
and
(out)
to each element of
K_{2}
.
Then, for all
n\geq N
,
x_{n}\in\mathcal{Z}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)
, so
x_{n}\to x
.
∎

2.2.
Higher-rank graphs

Higher-rank graphs were introduced in
[
KP00
]
as
k
-graphs and generalised to
P
-graphs in
[
BSV13
]
.
All the examples in this paper are

-graphs.
That said, we prove our results for

-graphs, which generalise both

-graphs and the hybrid graphs of
[

]
.
The generalisation is worthwhile: Any purely infinite simple C*-algebra is stably isomorphic to the C*-algebra of a

-graph

BSV13
]
.

We follow
[
BSV13
,
HNSY21
]
for preliminaries on weakly quasi-lattice ordered groups and
P
-graphs.
Let
Q
be a discrete group, and let
P
be a semigroup with unit

such that
P\cap P^{-1}=\{e\}
.
For each
p,r\in P
, define
p\leq r
if
pq=r
for some

.
Following
[
HNSY21
]
,

is a

if, whenever two elements of

have a common upper bound with respect to

, there is a least common upper bound.
For a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group
(Q,P)
, a (discrete)
P
-graph
consists of a countable small category
(\Lambda,{\Lambda}^{(2)},\Lambda^{(0)},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}})
together with a functor
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}\colon\Lambda\to P
satisfying the
unique factorisation property
: for every
\lambda\in\Lambda
and
p,q\in P
with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\lambda)=pq
, there are unique elements
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
such that
\lambda=\mu\nu
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=p
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)=q
.

We state some basic facts and notations about
P
-graphs.
It follows from the unique factorisation property that
\Lambda
is
left cancellative
(i.e.
\mu\nu=\mu\kappa
implies
\nu=\kappa
),

(i.e.
\mu\nu=\kappa\nu
implies
\mu=\kappa
), and has
no inverses
(i.e.
\mu\nu=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)
implies
\mu=\nu=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)
).
Hence, any
P
-graph is a
category of paths
(i.e. a left and right cancellative small category having no inverses) as in
[
Spi14
, Example 2.2(6)]
.
For each
p\in P
, we write
\Lambda^{p}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}^{-1}(p)
, and we have
\Lambda^{(0)}=\Lambda^{e}
.
For each
\mu\in\Lambda
and
E\subseteq\Lambda
, we write
\lambda E\coloneqq\left\{\lambda\mu\;\middle|\;\mu\in E\text{ and }\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mu)\right\}
.
For each
\mu,\lambda\in\Lambda
, define
\mu\preceq\lambda
if
\mu\nu=\lambda
for some
\nu\in\Lambda
.
The relation
\preceq
is the same as that of
[
Spi14
, Definition 2.5]
for categories of paths, so
\preceq
is a partial order.
Moreover,
\preceq
is
left invariant
(i.e.
\mu\nu\preceq\mu\kappa
implies
\nu\preceq\kappa
) because of left cancellation.

Example 2.5
.

The

-graphs of

]
are the

-graphs where

.

We say

is

if, for all
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
, there is a finite

such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
as in

.

, during which we give examples of

2.3.
Groupoids

We refer to
[
Wil19
, Page 3–4]
for preliminaries on groupoids.
A
groupoid
consists of a set
G
and a subset
{G}^{(2)}\subseteq G\times G
together with a map
(g,h)\mapsto gh
from
{G}^{(2)}
to
G
(called
composition
) and a map
g\mapsto g^{-1}
from
G
to
G
(called
inversion
) such that:

(a)

if
(f,g),(g,h)\in{G}^{(2)}
,
then
(f,gh),(fg,h)\in{G}^{(2)}
and

f(gh)=(fg)h\eqqcolon fgh
;

(b)

for all
g\in G
,
(g^{-1})^{-1}=g
; and

(c)

for all
g\in G
,
(g^{-1},g)\in{G}^{(2)}
and
(g,h)\in{G}^{(2)}
implies
g^{-1}(gh)=h
and
(gh)h^{-1}=g
.

The set
G^{(0)}\coloneqq\left\{x\in G\;\middle|\;x=xx=x^{-1}\right\}
is called the
unit space
of
G
.
There are
range
and
source
maps from
G
to
G^{(0)}
given by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(g)\coloneqq gg^{-1}
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g)\coloneqq g^{-1}g\in G^{(0)}
, for each
g\in G
, respectively.
We have
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(G)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(G)=G^{(0)}
, and
(g,h)\in{G}^{(2)}
if and only if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(g)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(h)
.

We define topological, locally compact, étale and ample groupoids as in
[
Ste10
]
, though they were first studied by Renault
[
Ren80
]
and then Paterson
[
Pat99
]
.
Let
G
be a groupoid endowed with a (
not necessarily Hausdorff
) topology.
The set
{G}^{(2)}
is endowed with the subspace topology from the product topology on
G\times G
.
We say
G
is
topological
if composition and inversion are continuous.
A
locally compact groupoid
is a topological groupoid
G
such that
G
is locally compact and the unit space
G^{(0)}
is both locally compact
and Hausdorff
in the subspace topology.
A locally compact groupoid
G
is
étale
if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}\colon G\to G^{(0)}
is a local homeomorphism.
An étale groupoid
G
is
ample
if
G^{(0)}
has a basis of compact open sets.

If
G
is a groupoid, we call
H\subseteq G
a
subgroupoid
if
H
is a groupoid under the inherited operations.
Given
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
, the
reduction
of
G
to
U
is
G|_{U}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U)\cap\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}^{-1}(U)
, which is a subgroupoid of
G
under the inherited operations.
Following
[
SSW20
]
, we say
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
is
invariant
if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U))\subseteq U
, in which case
G|_{U}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}^{-1}(U)
.
If
G
is étale and
U\subseteq G^{(0)}
is closed and invariant, then
G|_{U}
is an étale closed subgroupoid of
G
(see
[
SSW20
, Page 96]
).

2.4.
Semigroup actions

The following definitions come from
[
RW17
, §5]
.
Let
X
be a set, and let
P
be a subsemigroup of a group
Q
with unit
e
.
Suppose
X*P
is a subset of
X\times P
and
T\colon X*P\to X
is a function sending each
(x,m)\in X*P
to
T((x,m))\eqqcolon x\cdot m\in X
and satisfying:

(S1)

for all
x\in X
,
(x,e)\in X*P
and
x\cdot e=x
; and

(S2)

for all
(x,m,n)\in X\times P\times P
,
(x,mn)\in X*P
if and only if
(x,m)\in X*P
and
(x\cdot m,n)\in X*P
, in which case
(x\cdot m)\cdot n=x\cdot(mn)
.

We call the triple
(X,P,T)
a
semigroup action (of
P
on
X
)
.
For each
m\in P
, we write

\textrm{dom}(m)\coloneqq\left\{x\in X\;\middle|\;(x,m)\in X*P\right\}\text{ and }\textrm{im}(m)\coloneqq\left\{x\cdot m\;\middle|\;x\in\textrm{dom}(m)\right\},

and we define
T_{m}\colon\textrm{dom}(m)\to\textrm{im}(m)
by
T_{m}(x)=x\cdot m
, for each
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
.
We say
(X,P,T)
is
directed
if, for all
m,n\in P
, if
\textrm{dom}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}(n)\neq\emptyset
, then there is
l\in P
such that
m,n\leq l
and
\textrm{dom}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}(n)\subseteq\textrm{dom}(l)
, in which case
\textrm{dom}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}(n)=\textrm{dom}(l)
as per the note following
[
RW17
, Definition 5.2]
.
Recall that a
local homeomorphism
from a space
X
to a space
Y
is a continuous map
f\colon X\to Y
such that every
x\in X
has an open neighbourhood
U
such that
f(U)
is open in
Y
and the restriction
f|_{U}\colon U\to f(U)
of

is a homeomorphism
[
Wil70
, Page 68]
.
A semigroup action
(X,P,T)
is
locally compact
if
X
is a locally compact Hausdorff space and, for all
m\in P
,
\textrm{dom}(m)
and
\textrm{im}(m)
are open in
X
and

2.5.
Semidirect product groupoids

Semidirect product groupoids are a generalisation of Deaconu–Renault groupoids
[
Dea95
,
Ren00
]
.
In
[
RW17
]
, Renault and Williams associate a semidirect product groupoid
G(X,P,T)
to each locally compact directed semigroup action
(X,P,T)
as follows.
Let
G(X,P,T)
be the set of
(x,q,y)\in X\times Q\times X
for which there are
m,n\in P
satisfying
q=mn^{-1}
,
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
,
y\in\textrm{dom}(n)
and
x\cdot m=y\cdot n
.
Let
{G(X,P,T)}^{(2)}
be the set of pairs

with
y=w
.
Then,
G(X,P,T)
is a groupoid with composition

(x,q,y)(y,r,z)\coloneqq(x,qr,z)

and inversion

(x,q,y)^{-1}\coloneqq(y,q^{-1},x),

called the
semidirect product groupoid
of
(X,P,T)
.
The collection of

Z(U,m,n,V)\coloneqq\left\{(x,mn^{-1},y)\in G(X,P,T)\;\middle|\;x\in U,y\in V\text{ and }x\cdot m=y\cdot n\right\},

where

and
U,V\subseteq X
are open, is a basis for a topology on
G(X,P,T)
such that

is étale and Hausdorff, and

is a homeomorphism from
X
to
G(X,P,T)^{(0)}
.
It follows from the definition of a basis that:

Lemma 2.6
.

If
\mathcal{B}
is a basis for the topology on

, then the collection of
Z(B,m,n,C)
, where

and
B,C\in\mathcal{B}
, is a basis for the topology on
G(X,P,T)
.

3.1.
Properties of the finitely aligned part of a higher-rank graph

We collect some basic properties of
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
.

(a)

The set
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is a right ideal in
\Lambda
.

(b)

If
\mu\delta\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, then
\delta\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.

(c)

The set
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is closed under the source map
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}
.

(d)

The set
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is not necessarily closed under the range map
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}
.

(e)

If
\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)\Lambda\subseteq\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.

(f)

If
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, then there is a finite
J\subseteq\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
.

Proof.

(a)

Suppose
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
.
We show
\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
If
(\lambda\lambda^{\prime},\lambda^{\prime\prime})\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
and
\nu\in\Lambda
, then
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime},\nu)
because
\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\lambda^{\prime\prime}\in\lambda\Lambda
and
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
\lambda
.

(b)

Suppose
\mu^{\prime}\in\delta\Lambda
and
\nu\in\Lambda
.
We show
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\mu^{\prime},\nu)
.
If
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\nu)\neq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mu^{\prime})
, then
\mu^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\emptyset
, so we are done.
Otherwise,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\nu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mu^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\delta)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)
, so
(\mu,\nu)\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
.
Because
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in\mu\delta\Lambda
,
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\mu\mu^{\prime},\mu\nu)
.
That is, there is a finite
J\subseteq\Lambda
such that
\mu\mu^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
.
It follows
J\subseteq\mu\Lambda
, and so
\mu^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\kappa\text{ such that }\mu\kappa\in J}\kappa\Lambda
, so
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\mu^{\prime},\nu)
.

(c)

This is a special case of
(b)
because
\lambda=\lambda\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)
, for all
\lambda\in\Lambda
.

(d)

The
2
-graph
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
is a counter-example because
v=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\lambda\alpha_{1})\notin\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda_{0})
.

(e)

This follows from
(a)
and
(b)
.

(f)

Since
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\mu,\nu)
, there is a finite
J\subseteq\Lambda
such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
.
Each
\lambda\in J
is in
\mu\Lambda
, so
(a)
implies
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
∎

We highlight from

is closed under composition (by

.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let

If

is a finitely aligned

3.4.
Finitely aligned relative category of paths

Following
[
Spi14
]
, a
relative category of paths
is a pair
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
, where
\Lambda
is a category of paths and
\Lambda^{\prime}
is a subcategory.
A relative category of paths
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
is
finitely aligned
if:

(a)

for every
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda^{\prime}
, there is a finite
J\subseteq\Lambda^{\prime}
such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
; and

(b)

for every
\mu\in\Lambda^{\prime}
,
\mu\Lambda\cap\Lambda^{\prime}=\mu\Lambda^{\prime}
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The pair
(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
is a finitely aligned relative category of paths, where

\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)\coloneqq\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\cup\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)).

Proof.

By
Lemma

3.4
,
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is closed under the composition and source maps in
\Lambda
, so
\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
is closed under the composition, source
and
range maps in
\Lambda
.
That is,
\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
is a subcategory of
\Lambda
, and
\Lambda
is a category of paths.
Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
.
If
\mu
or
\nu
is in
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda))
, then
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\emptyset
,
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\nu\Lambda
or
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\mu\Lambda
.
Otherwise,
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, so
Lemma

3.4
(f)
implies there is a finite
J\subseteq\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
such that
\mu\Lambda\cap\nu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\lambda\in J}\lambda\Lambda
.
It remains to show
\mu\Lambda\cap\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)=\mu\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
.
Suppose
\mu\nu\in\mu\Lambda\cap\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
.
Then,
\nu\in\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)\Lambda\subseteq\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

3.4
(e)
, so
\mu\nu\in\mu\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
.
Conversely, if
\mu\nu\in\mu\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)
, either
\mu\nu=\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, or both

and so
\mu\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
because
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is closed under composition (

3.4
(a)
).
∎

Spielberg associated groupoids to relative categories of paths in

.

4.
Path spaces

In this section, given any
P
-graph
\Lambda
with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
, we use
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
from
Section

3
to give a novel definition of a path space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
and boundary-path space
\partial\Lambda
for
\Lambda
.
We show in
Lemma

4.8
that
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
if and only if
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
is compact, where
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
is the set of filters of
\Lambda
, which implies
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is locally compact (
Theorem

4.9
).

4.1.
Filters

A
filter
of a
P
-graph
\Lambda
is a nonempty subset
x
of
\Lambda
that is
hereditary
(i.e.
\lambda\preceq\mu\in x
implies
\lambda\in x
) and
directed
(i.e.
\mu,\nu\in x
implies there is
\lambda\in x
such that
\mu,\nu\preceq\lambda
).
We denote the set of filters of
\Lambda
by
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
Due to their defining properties, finite filters are of the form
F(\mu)
for some
\mu\in\Lambda
, and infinite filters are of the form
\bigcup_{n}F(\mu_{n})
for some
\preceq
-increasing sequence
(\mu_{n})\subseteq\Lambda
.
In this way, the filters of
\Lambda
resemble the paths of a directed graph.
Filters have been used to give combinatorial descriptions for spaces of path-like objects for some time.
In
[
Nic92
]
, Nica showed filters provide a path model for the spectrum of the diagonal subalgebra of the Wiener–Hopf C*-algebra of a quasi-lattice ordered group.
Paterson and Welch used filters to model paths in
k
-graphs in
[
PW05
, Lemma 2.1]
.
Exel used filters to model paths in semigroupoids in
[
Exe08
, §20]
.
Indeed, in the original paper
[
BSV13
]
on
P
-graphs, filters played the role of paths, and we will do the same.

We recall basics of filters from
[
BSV13
,
RW17
]
.
The restriction
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{x}
of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}
to any
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
is injective
[
RW17
, Lemma 6.6(b)]
.
For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, we write
F(\lambda)\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in\Lambda\;\middle|\;\mu\preceq\lambda\right\}\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)

[
RW17
, Proposition 6.8(b)]
.
For any
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, there is a unique element of
x\cap\Lambda^{(0)}
denoted by
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)

[
RW17
, Lemma 6.4(a)]
.
In particular,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(F(\lambda))=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(\lambda)
.
Like in
[
BSV13
, §3]
, we say
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
is an
ultrafilter
of
\Lambda
if, for all
y\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
,
x\subseteq y
implies
x=y
.
We denote the set of ultrafilters of
\Lambda
by
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
.
The following result is standard, but we prove it to emphasise there is no need for finite alignment.
Recall from
Eq.

2.1
that we define
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)=\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)\cap\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
for each
\mu\in\Lambda
and finite
K\subseteq\Lambda
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The collection of

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\Lambda
is finite, is a basis for
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
consisting of clopen sets.

Remark 4.2
.

If
\Lambda
is finitely aligned, we can assume
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
(see
Lemma

6.2
).

Proof of
Lemma

4.1
.

Since
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
has the subspace topology from
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
, the collection of

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right),

where
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
are finite, is a basis for
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
Suppose

x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right)

for some finite
K_{1},K_{2}\subseteq\Lambda
.
Because filters are nonempty, we can assume without loss of generality that
K_{1}
is nonempty.
Then, because filters are directed, there is
\mu\in x
such that
\lambda\preceq\mu
for all
\lambda\in K_{1}
.
The hereditary property of filters implies

x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K_{2}\right)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(K_{1}\setminus K_{2}\right),

as required.
The intersection of a closed set with a subspace is closed in the subspace topology, so each
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K_{2}\right)
is closed in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

2.1
.
∎

4.2.
Path and boundary-path spaces

If
\Lambda
is finitely aligned, then
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
identifies with the unit space of the groupoid used to define the Toeplitz C*-algebra of
\Lambda
in
[
Spi12
]
; in particular,
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
is locally compact.
Yeend showed in
[
Yee07
, Remark 3.7]
that
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda^{\mathrm{Yee}})
is not locally compact for the nonfinitely aligned
2
-graph
\Lambda^{\mathrm{Yee}}
from
Example

3.3
.
(Actually, Yeend uses graph morphisms instead of filters, like in the original
k
-graph paper
[
KP00
]
, but the author showed in
[
Jon24
]
that Yeend’s arguments apply to
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda^{\mathrm{Yee}})
as well.)
We use
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
from
Section

to identify an open subspace
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
of
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
that is locally compact even if
\Lambda
is not finitely aligned.
The definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
also allows a locally compact and directed semigroup action of

on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
in
Section

5
.

Definition 4.3

(Path space)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The
path space
of
\Lambda
is the set
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
of
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
for which
x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 4.4
.

Notice
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
implies
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
because, for each
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
,
F(\lambda)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
On the other hand, if
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\emptyset
, then
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)=\emptyset
.

Example 4.5
.

Recall
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
.
The set of filters is

\mathcal{F}(\Lambda_{0})=\{\{t\},\{u\},\{v\},\{w\},F(\mu),F(\lambda)\}\cup\{F(\alpha_{n}),F(\beta_{n}),F(\mu\beta_{n})\mid n\in\mathbb{N}\}.

Since
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda_{0})=\Lambda_{0}\setminus\{v,\lambda,\mu\}
, we have

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda_{0})=\mathcal{F}(\Lambda_{0})\setminus\{\{v\},F(\lambda),F(\mu)\}.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. For any
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
;

(b)

x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\cap\lambda\Lambda\neq\emptyset
for all
\lambda\in x
.

Proof.

We have that
(b)
implies
(a)
because filters are nonempty.
Suppose
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
, and fix
\lambda\in x
.
There is some
\mu\in x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
Filters are directed, so there is
\nu\in x
with
\lambda,\mu\preceq\nu
.
In particular,
\nu\in\lambda\Lambda
, and
\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
because
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is a right ideal in
\Lambda
(
Lemma

3.4
(a)
).
∎

Definition 4.7

(Boundary-path space)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The
boundary-path space

\partial\Lambda
of
\Lambda
is the closure of
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.

4.3.
Local compactness of the path spaces

In the remainder of this section, we prove topological properties of
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\partial\Lambda
, which are summarised in
Theorem

4.9
.
The main work is to show they are locally compact.
This involves the following lemma that characterises the elements of
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
in terms of the topology on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
A consequence of the lemma is that the definition of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
in this paper is equivalent to that of the author’s thesis
[
Jon24
]
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. For any
\lambda\in\Lambda
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
;

(b)

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
is compact.

Proof.

Suppose
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
is compact.
We show
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
Fix
\lambda^{\prime}\in\lambda\Lambda
and
\mu\in\Lambda
.
If
\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda
is finite, then indeed
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu)
.
Otherwise, we can write
\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda=(\nu_{n})
because
\Lambda
is countable.
Suppose for a contradiction that
\Lambda
is not finitely aligned at
(\lambda^{\prime},\mu)
.
Then, letting
J_{n}\coloneqq\{\nu_{1},\nu_{2},\dots,\nu_{n}\}
, for all
n
, we have
\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda\neq\bigcup_{\nu\in J_{n}}\nu\Lambda\subseteq\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda
.
Thus, there is a sequence
(\kappa_{n})
such that
\kappa_{n}\in(\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda)\setminus\bigcup_{\nu\in J_{n}}\nu\Lambda
for all
n
.
In particular,
(F(\kappa_{n}))\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)

Since
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
is compact and
\lambda^{\prime}\in\lambda\Lambda

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)

By

,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
is sequentially compact, so there is a subsequence
(F(\kappa_{n_{k}}))
of
(F(\kappa_{n}))
and there is an
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
such that
F(\kappa_{n_{k}})\to x
.
Because
\mu\in F(\kappa_{n})
for all
n
,

2.4
implies
\mu\in x
.
Filters are directed, so
x\cap\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda\neq\emptyset
and
\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda=(\nu_{n})
, so there is
n^{\prime}
such that
\nu_{n^{\prime}}\in x\cap\lambda^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda

Because
\nu_{n^{\prime}}\in x
and
F(\kappa_{n_{k}})\to x

\nu_{n^{\prime}}
is eventually inside
(F(\kappa_{n_{k}}))
by
Lemma

2.4
.
That is, there is

such that
\nu_{n^{\prime}}\in F(\kappa_{n_{k}})
for all

By definition of
F(\kappa_{n_{k}})
, we have
\kappa_{n_{k}}\in\nu_{n^{\prime}}\Lambda\subseteq\bigcup_{\nu\in J_{n^{\prime}}}\nu\Lambda
for all
k\geq K

Recall, for each

,
\kappa_{n}
was chosen so that
\kappa_{n}\notin\bigcup_{\nu\in J_{n}}\nu\Lambda
.
Thus, we nearly have a contradiction.
We just need to ‘wait’ for

to surpass

.
Formally, since
n_{k}
is unbounded, there is
K^{\prime}
such that, for all

.
Let

Then,

.
Also,

Therefore,

We prove
(a)
implies
(b)
by slightly modifying the proof of
[
Jon24
, Lemma 5.2.1]
.
Suppose
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
If
\lambda\Lambda
is finite, then
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
is finite and therefore compact.
Now suppose
\lambda\Lambda
is infinite and write
\lambda\Lambda=(\mu_{n})
such that
\mu_{0}=\lambda
and
\mu_{i}\neq\mu_{j}
whenever
i\neq j
.
Let
(x_{j})
be a sequence in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
.
We construct a convergent subsequence of
(x_{j})
via an infinite collection of nested subsequences
(x_{f_{n}(j)})
, each having the property that some extension of
\mu_{n}
is in
x_{f_{n}(j)}
, for all
j
.
These extensions give rise to a particular
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
and the sequence
(x_{f_{i}(i)})
converges to
x
.

Let
\lambda_{0}\coloneqq\mu_{0}=\lambda
, and let
(x_{f_{0}(j)})=(x_{j})
.
Notice that

\lambda_{0}\in x_{f_{0}(j)},

for all
j
.
Consider
\mu_{1}
.
Suppose
\mu_{1}
is contained in infinitely many
x_{f_{0}(j)}
.
That is, there is a subsequence
(x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)})
of
(x_{f_{0}(j)})

\mu_{1}\in x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
for all

.
Since
\lambda_{0}=\lambda\in\lambda\Lambda
,
\Lambda
is finitely aligned at
(\lambda_{0},\mu_{1})
, so there is a finite
J_{1}\subseteq\Lambda
such that
\lambda_{0}\Lambda\cap\mu_{1}\Lambda=\bigcup_{\gamma\in J_{1}}\gamma\Lambda
.
For every

,
\lambda_{0}
and
\mu_{1}
are in
x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
, which is directed, so
\emptyset\neq\lambda_{0}\Lambda\cap\mu_{1}\Lambda\cap x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\in J_{1}}\gamma\Lambda\cap x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
.
For all

,
x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
is hereditary, so there is a
\gamma_{j}\in J_{1}\cap x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
.
Since
J_{1}
is finite, the pigeonhole principle implies there is
\gamma\in J_{1}
such that, for infinitely many

,
\gamma\in x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)}
.
Let
(x_{f_{1}(j)})
be the subsequence of
(x_{f_{1}^{\prime}(j)})
given by those infinitely many
j
, and let
\lambda_{1}\in\Lambda
be such that
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}=\gamma
.
Thus,
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\in x_{f_{1}(j)}
for all

.
Observe
\mu_{1}\preceq\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}
.
On the other hand, if
\mu_{1}
is contained in only finitely many

, let
(x_{f_{1}(j)})\coloneqq(x_{f_{0}(j)})
, and let
\lambda_{1}\coloneqq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda_{0})
.
In both cases,

\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\in x_{f_{1}(j)},

for all
j
.

Repeating the above argument with
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}
instead of
\lambda_{0}
and with the index of every other variable increased by

produces
\lambda_{2}\in\Lambda
and a subsequence
(x_{f_{2}(j)})
of
(x_{f_{1}(j)})
such that
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\in x_{f_{2}(j)}
for all

.
Moreover, if
\mu_{2}
is contained in infinitely many

, then
\mu_{2}\preceq\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}
.
Indeed, this can be repeated again for each

.
Thus, for each

, we have a subsequence
(x_{f_{n}(j)})
of
(x_{j})
, where
(x_{f_{0}(j)})=(x_{j})
, and
(x_{f_{n+1}(j)})
is a subsequence of

, for all

.
Also, we have a subset
\left\{\lambda_{n}\;\middle|\;n\geq 0\right\}\subseteq\Lambda
such that

\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}\in x_{f_{n}(j)},

for all
n,j\geq 0
.
In particular, for any

,

\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{i}\in x_{f_{i}(i)},

Since filters are hereditary,

(4.1)

0\leq n\leq i\implies\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}\in x_{f_{i}(i)}.

Lastly, for each
n\geq 1
, if
\mu_{n}
is contained in infinitely many
x_{f_{n-1}(j)}
, then
\mu_{n}\preceq\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}
.

We claim that

x_{f_{i}(i)}\to x\coloneqq\bigcup_{n}F(\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}).

Because
(F(\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}))
is totally ordered with respect to inclusion,

(see the proof of
[
BSV13
, Lemma 3.2]
).
Moreover,
\lambda_{0}=\lambda\in x

.
We show

using
Lemma

2.4
.
Fix
\mu\in x
, so
\mu\preceq\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}
for some
n
.
Then,
Eq.

4.1
says
\lambda_{0}\lambda_{1}\dots\lambda_{n}
is eventually inside

(for sufficiently large

Since each

is hereditary,
\mu
is eventually inside
(x_{f_{i}(i)})

holds.
We show

Suppose

That is, for all

\mu\in x_{f_{i_{I}}(i_{I})}

Thus,
\lambda,\mu\in x_{f_{i_{I}}(i_{I})}

.
Since

such that
\lambda\Lambda\cap\mu\Lambda=\bigcup_{\nu\in J}\nu\Lambda
.
Like earlier, because each

and

In particular,

, so

Notice the truncated sequence
(x_{f_{i_{I_{k}}}(i_{I_{k}})})_{i_{I_{k}}\geq n^{\prime}-1}

Thus, according to the above construction,

Altogether we have

.
That is,

holds, and so

.
∎

Theorem 4.9
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The path space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
and the boundary-path space
\partial\Lambda
are Hausdorff and have countable bases consisting of compact sets.
Moreover,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is open in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

The space
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda)
is Hausdorff, and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is a subspace.
Recall from
Eq.

2.1
that we define
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)=\mathcal{Z}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
for each
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
and finite
K\subseteq\Lambda
.
By
Lemma

4.1
, the collection of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\Lambda
is finite, is a basis for the topology on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
For any
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)
, there is
\mu\in x\cap\mu^{\prime}\Lambda\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

4.6
.
Then,
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)
.
Hence, the collection of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and
K\subseteq\Lambda
is finite, is a basis for the topology on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
This basis is countable because
\Lambda
is countable.
For each
\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and finite
K\subseteq\Lambda
,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\right)
is compact by
Lemma

4.8
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\right)
, so
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
is compact.
The boundary-path space
\partial\Lambda
also has the stated properties because
\partial\Lambda
is a closed subspace of
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Also,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is open in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
because
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
equals the union of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\right)
for which
\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
∎

In

, §6]
, Renault and Williams construct actions of semigroups
P
on

-proper topological

P
-graphs.
We only consider discrete

-graphs here, for which

\text{$(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}})$-proper}\iff\text{row-finite}\implies\text{finitely aligned},

as per
[
Jon24
, §5.1]
.
Thus, in the discrete setting, we generalise the semigroup action of

]
to any
P
-graph
\Lambda
with

.
Then, we define the path groupoid

5.1.
Shift maps on the path space

By
[
BSV13
, Lemma 3.4]
, the maps

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\right),\quad x\mapsto x\cdot\lambda\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in\Lambda\;\middle|\;\lambda\mu\in x\right\}

and

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\right)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right),\quad x\mapsto\lambda x\coloneqq\left\{\zeta\in\Lambda\;\middle|\;\zeta\preceq\lambda\mu\text{ for some }\mu\in x\right\}

are well-defined.
We call them the
left-shift map
and
right-shift map
associated to
\lambda
, respectively.
The notation “
x\cdot\lambda
” mimics the operations defined in
[
RW17
, Lemma 6.7(a)–(b)]
and differs from
[
BSV13
]
, where “
x\cdot\lambda
” is denoted by “
\lambda^{*}\cdot x
”.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph.
For any
(\lambda,\mu)\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
and
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
:

(a)

if
\lambda\mu\in x
, then
(x\cdot\lambda)\cdot\mu=x\cdot(\lambda\mu)
; and

(b)

if
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
, then
\lambda(\mu x)=(\lambda\mu)x
.

Proof.

(a)
This follows from the associativity of composition in
\Lambda
.

(b)
If
\zeta\in\lambda(\mu x)
, then there is
\eta\in\mu x
such that
\zeta\preceq\lambda\eta
and there is
\xi\in x
such that
\eta\preceq\mu\xi
.
It follows from
\eta\preceq\mu\xi
that
\lambda\eta\preceq\lambda\mu\xi
, and so
\zeta\preceq\lambda\mu\xi
by the transitivity of
\preceq
.
Hence,
\zeta\in(\lambda\mu)x
.
The other inclusion is similar but uses the reflexivity of
\preceq
.
∎

Though finite alignment is assumed in the statement of the following result from
[
BSV13
]
, it is not used in their proof, so we state the result in greater generality here.

Lemma 5.2

(Lemma 3.4 of
[
BSV13
]
)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph.
If
\mu\in x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
(\lambda,\mu)\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
, then:

(a)

\mu(x\cdot\mu)=x
and
(\lambda x)\cdot\lambda=x
; and

(b)

if
x\in\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
, then
x\cdot\mu,\lambda x\in\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
.

Left-shift maps are continuous bijections even when the
P
-graph is not finitely aligned:

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph.
For each
(\lambda,\lambda^{\prime})\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
, the left-shift map associated to
\lambda
is a continuous bijection whose inverse is the right-shift map, and
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\right))\cdot\lambda=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
.

Proof.

For injectivity, suppose
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
satisfy
x\cdot\lambda=x^{\prime}\cdot\lambda
.

Lemma

5.2
(a)
implies
x=\lambda(x\cdot\lambda)=\lambda(x^{\prime}\cdot\lambda)=x^{\prime}
.
For surjectivity, fix
y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\lambda)\right)
.
Notice
\lambda y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
, and
(\lambda y)\cdot\lambda=y
by
Lemma

5.2
(a)
.
Consequently, the right-shift map is the inverse of the left-shift map.
Also, note that
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\right)
is a subset of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
because filters are hereditary.
For any
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\right)
,
\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\in x
, so
\lambda^{\prime}\in x\cdot\lambda
.
Hence,
x\cdot\lambda\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
.
That is,
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\right))\cdot\lambda\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
.
The surjectivity argument can be modified to show
(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\right))\cdot\lambda\supseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda^{\prime}\right)
.
To see the left-shift map is continuous, suppose
(x_{n})
converges to
x
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\right)
.
To show
(x_{n}\cdot\lambda)
converges to
x\cdot\lambda
, we use
Lemma

2.4
.
Suppose
\mu\notin x\cdot\lambda
, so
\lambda\mu\notin x
.
Since
(out)
holds for
(x_{n})
and
x
,
\lambda\mu
is eventually outside
(x_{n})
, and so
\mu
is eventually outside
(x_{n}\cdot\lambda)
, so
(out)
holds for
(x_{n}\cdot\lambda)
and
x\cdot\lambda
.
A similar argument shows
(in)
holds.
∎

However, left-shift maps can fail to be open:

Example 5.4
.

Recall
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
.
We write
F(\kappa)=\left\{\nu\in\Lambda_{0}\;\middle|\;\nu\preceq\kappa\right\}
for each
\kappa\in\Lambda_{0}
.

Lemma

2.4
can be used to show
F(\alpha_{n})\to\{w\}
(because
w
is the only element of
\Lambda_{0}
that is eventually inside
(F(\alpha_{n}))
) but
\lambda F(\alpha_{n})=\{v,\mu,\lambda,\lambda\alpha_{n}\}\to\{v,\mu,\lambda\}\neq\{v,\lambda\}=\lambda\{w\}
.
Hence, the right-shift map associated to
\lambda
is not continuous, and the right-shift map is the inverse of the left-shift map by
Lemma

5.3
, so the left-shift map is not open.

We now turn to the path space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
We will assume
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
so that the path space is nonempty.
We first note that the path space is closed under left-shift maps:

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. If
\lambda\in x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
, then
x\cdot\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Recall from
Lemma

4.6
that
\lambda\in x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
implies there is some
\lambda\lambda^{\prime}\in x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\cap\lambda\Lambda\neq\emptyset
.
Then,
Lemma

3.4
(b)
implies
\lambda^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\cap x\cdot\lambda
, so

x\cdot\lambda\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
∎

However, the path space can fail to be closed under right-shift maps:

Example 5.6
.

In
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
,

.
However,

.

5.2.
Semigroup action on the path space

We use the above shift maps to define a locally compact and directed semigroup action
T
of
P
on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
The reader can recall preliminaries on semigroup actions from
Section

.
We use one additional notation.

Notation 5.7
.

Recall that we write
\Lambda^{m}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}^{-1}(m)
, for each
m\in P
.
For each
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
m\in P
, if
x\cap\Lambda^{m}
is nonempty, then there is precisely one element
x(e,m)
in
x\cap\Lambda^{m}
by the injectivity
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{x}
.
Then, the left-shift map associated to
x(e,m)
maps
x
to
x\cdot x(e,m)
.

Definition 5.8
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. Let

\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P\coloneqq\left\{(x,m)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)\times P\;\middle|\;x\cap\Lambda^{m}\neq\emptyset\right\},

and define
T\colon\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P\to\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
to map each
(x,m)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
to
x\cdot m\coloneqq x\cdot x(e,m)
.

Proposition 5.9
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. Then,
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
is a semigroup action.

Proof.

The function
T
is well-defined with the codomain
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

5.5
.
We check
(S1)
and
(S2)
of the definition of a semigroup action.
For each
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)\in x\cap\Lambda^{(0)}=\Lambda^{e}
.
Thus,
x\cdot e=x\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)=x
, so
(S1)
holds.

Now suppose
(x,mn)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Since
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(x(e,mn))=mn
and
\Lambda
has the unique factorisation property, there are unique
\mu\in\Lambda^{m}
and
\nu\in\Lambda^{n}
such that
x(e,mn)=\mu\nu
.
Since
x
is hereditary,
\mu\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
, so
(x,m)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Notice
x\cdot m=x\cdot\mu
.
Because
\mu\nu\in x
, we have
\nu\in(x\cdot m)\cap\Lambda^{n}
.
Thus,
(x\cdot m,n)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
That is,
(x,mn)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
implies both
(x,m)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
and
(x\cdot m,n)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
For the other direction, suppose
(x,m)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
and
(x\cdot m,n)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Say
\mu\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
and
\nu\in(x\cdot m)\cap\Lambda^{n}
.
It follows that
\mu\nu\in x\cap\Lambda^{mn}

(x,mn)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Using
Lemma

5.1
, we can compute

(x\cdot m)\cdot n=(x\cdot\mu)\cdot\nu=x\cdot(\mu\nu)=x\cdot(mn),

so
(S2)
holds.
∎

The main result using the weak quasi-lattice order property of
(Q,P)
is
Proposition

.

Proposition 5.10
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The semigroup action
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
is directed.

Proof.

Fix
(m,n)\in P\times P
such that there is some
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)\cap\textrm{dom}(n)
.
Remember this means
(x,m),(x,n)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Say
\mu\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
and
\nu\in x\cap\Lambda^{n}
.
Because
x
is directed, there are
\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}\in\Lambda

\mu\mu^{\prime}=\nu\nu^{\prime}\in x
.
Observe
m,n\leq\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu\mu^{\prime})=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu\nu^{\prime})
.

is a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, there is a least upper bound

.
∎

We embark on a series of lemmas proving
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
is locally compact (
Theorem

5.14
).
This is one of the main technical difficulties of the work that led to the definition of our path space
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Recall from the proof of
Theorem

4.9
that the collection of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and

is finite, is a basis of compact sets for the topology on
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. For any
m\in P
,
\textrm{dom}(m)
is open in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

We have
\textrm{dom}(m)=\bigcup_{\mu\in\Lambda^{m}}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\right)
, which is a union of basic open sets.
∎

It would now be natural to try to prove
\textrm{im}(m)=\bigcup_{\mu\in\Lambda^{m}}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)\right)
so that
\textrm{im}(m)
is open too.
We can prove
\textrm{im}(m)\subseteq\bigcup_{\mu\in\Lambda^{m}}\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)\right)
, but the reverse containment can fail because
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is not closed under right-shift maps (
Example

5.6
).

Lemma 5.12
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. For any
m\in P
,
\textrm{im}(m)
is open in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Fix
x\cdot m\in\textrm{im}(m)
, where
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
.
Write
\mu\coloneqq x(e,m)\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
so that
x\cdot m=x\cdot\mu
.
By
Lemma

4.6
, there is some
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
with
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
We claim
x\cdot m\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)\subseteq\textrm{im}(m)
.
Notice
x\cdot m=x\cdot\mu\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
Also,
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
implies
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

3.4
(b)
, so
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
, which is open in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Now fix
y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
Then,
\mu y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)\subseteq\textrm{dom}(m)
, and so
y=(\mu y)\cdot\mu=(\mu y)\cdot m\in\textrm{im}(m)
.
∎

Recall from
Example

5.4
that right-shift maps are not always continuous.
Thus, in order to prove
T
acts by local homeomorphisms for
Theorem

5.14
we need the following lemma, which says that right-shift maps are
continuous enough
.

Lemma 5.13
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. If
(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\in{\Lambda}^{(2)}
,
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and
w_{n}\to w
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
, then there is a subsequence
(w_{n_{k}})
such that
\mu w_{n_{k}}\to\mu w
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
.

Proof.

Since
(w_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
, we have
(\mu w_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
by definition of the right-shift map associated to
\mu
.
Because
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
is compact by
Lemma

4.8
.
Thus, there is a subsequence
(\mu w_{n_{k}})
that converges to some
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
The left-shift map associated to
\mu
is continuous (
Lemma

5.3
), so

w_{n_{k}}=(\mu w_{n_{k}})\cdot\mu\to z\cdot\mu.

By assumption,
w_{n_{k}}\to w
, so
z\cdot\mu=w
.
Therefore,
z=\mu w
.
∎

Theorem 5.14
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The semigroup action
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
is locally compact and directed.

Proof.

Recall from
Proposition

5.10
that
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
is directed.
By
Theorem

4.9
,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is a locally compact Hausdorff space.
Now fix
m\in P
, and consider the map
T_{m}
from
\textrm{dom}(m)
to
\textrm{im}(m)
.
We showed
\textrm{dom}(m)
and
\textrm{im}(m)
are open in
Lemmas

5.11
and
5.12
, respectively.
It remains to show
T_{m}
is a local homeomorphism.
Fix
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
, and say
\mu\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
.
By
Lemma

4.6
, there is some
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, which means
\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

3.4
(b)
.
Since
\mu\mu^{\prime},\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, we have
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
and
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)=\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
, which are both compact by
Lemma

4.8
.
Because
\mu\in y
for all
y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
, the restriction of
T_{m}
to
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
coincides with the left-shift map associated to
\mu
.
Then,
Lemma

5.3
implies the image of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
under
T_{m}
is
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
Since the left-shift map associated to
\mu
is a continuous bijection (
Lemma

5.3
), it remains to show the map is open.
Let
O
be an open set in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
We show
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)\setminus T_{m}(O)
is closed.
Suppose
(w_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)\setminus T_{m}(O)
converges to some
w\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
By
Lemma

5.13
, there is a subsequence
(w_{n_{k}})
such that
\mu w_{n_{k}}\to\mu w
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
.
Then,
T_{m}(\mu w)=(\mu w)\cdot m=(\mu w)\cdot\mu=w
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
w\in T_{m}(O)
.
Then, the previous calculation implies
\mu w\in O
because
T_{m}
is a bijection.
Thus,
\mu w_{n_{k}}
is eventually in
O
, so
w_{n_{k}}=T_{m}(\mu w_{n_{k}})
is eventually in
T_{m}(O)
, which is a contradiction.
∎

5.3.
Path and boundary-path groupoids

Recall from
Section

2.5
the semidirect product groupoid associated to a semigroup action as in
[
RW17
]
.
Having constructed a locally compact and directed semigroup action
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
in

5.14
, we can define an étale and Hausdorff groupoid as follows.

Definition 5.15

(Path groupoid)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The
path groupoid

G_{\Lambda}
of
\Lambda
is the semidirect product groupoid
G(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
of the semigroup action
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
from
Theorem

5.14
.

Lemma 5.16
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The boundary-path space
\partial\Lambda
identifies with a closed invariant subset of
G_{\Lambda}^{(0)}
.

Proof.

We have that
\partial\Lambda
is closed in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
by
Definition

4.7
.
The homeomorphism
z\mapsto(z,e,z)
from
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
to
G_{\Lambda}^{(0)}
identifies the subset
\partial\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
with a subset of
G_{\Lambda}^{(0)}
that is invariant if, whenever
m,n\in P
,
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
,

satisfy
x\cdot m=y\cdot n
, we have
x\in\partial\Lambda
.
Fix
m,n,x,y
as above.
Say
\mu\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
and
\nu\in y\cap\Lambda^{n}
.
By
Lemma

4.6
, there is some
\mu^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in x\cap\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
Then,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\mu^{\prime}\right)
is compact by
Lemma

4.8
.
By definition,
\partial\Lambda
is the closure of
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
, so there is a sequence
(y_{n})\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
such that
y_{n}\to y
in
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Because
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
is a subspace of
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, we also have that
y_{n}\to y
in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, so
(y_{n})
is eventually in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu\right)
, so we assume without loss of generality that
\nu\in y_{n}
for all
n
.
We have
(y_{n}\cdot\nu)\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
because both
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
are closed under left-shift maps (
Lemma

5.2
(b)
and
Lemma

5.5
).
We also know
(\mu(y_{n}\cdot\nu))\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
by
Lemma

5.2
(b)
.
Note that
y_{n}\to y
in
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu\right)
.
Also, the left-shift map associated to
\nu
is continuous (
Lemma

5.3
), so

y_{n}\cdot\nu\to y\cdot\nu=x\cdot\mu\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\right).

Thus, we assume without loss of generality that
\mu^{\prime}\in y_{n}\cdot\nu
for all
n
.
Therefore, every
\mu(y_{n}\cdot\nu)
contains
\mu\mu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, so
(\mu(y_{n}\cdot\nu))\subseteq\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Now
Lemma

5.13
implies there is a subsequence
(y_{n_{k}}\cdot\nu)
such that

\mu(y_{n_{k}}\cdot\nu)\to\mu(y\cdot\nu)=\mu(x\cdot\mu)=x.

∎

Definition 5.17

(Boundary-path groupoid)
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The

G_{\partial\Lambda}
of
\Lambda
is the reduction
G_{\Lambda}|_{\partial\Lambda}
of
G_{\Lambda}
to
\partial\Lambda
.

Theorem 5.18
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. The path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
and the boundary-path groupoid

are ample, second-countable and Hausdorff.

Proof.

The path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
is the semidirect product groupoid of the locally compact and directed semigroup action
(\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda),P,T)
from
Theorem

5.14
, so
G_{\Lambda}
is étale and Hausdorff.

5.16
.
The unit spaces
G_{\Lambda}^{(0)}
and

.

2.6
yields countable bases for

.

∎

5.4.
Sufficient condition for amenability of the groupoids

Our path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
is second-countable, Hausdorff and étale, so the three predominant notions of amenability (measurewise, topological and Borel) are all equivalent (see
[
AR00
]
and
[
Ren15

Also, the boundary-path groupoid

, Theorem 5.13]
.
This allows us to generalise various amenability results for path groupoids in the setting of discrete

-graphs, including
[

, Proposition 6.2]
,
[

, Corollary 6.19]
, and
[

, Corollary 4.3]
.

]

5.5.
Characterising the elements of the groupoids

We characterise the elements of our path groupoids in

Proposition 5.21
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
. For any
g\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)\times Q\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
, the following are equivalent:

(a)

;

(b)

g=(\mu x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},\nu x)
for some

and
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
.

Remark 5.22
.

The hypothesis that

in the statement of
Proposition

5.21
is crucial.
Otherwise,
(b)
would not imply
(a)
because there are
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
and
\mu\in\Lambda
with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
such that
\mu x\notin\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
(see

).

Proof of
Proposition

5.21
.

Fix
g=(y,q,z)\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)\times Q\times\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
If
g\in G_{\Lambda}
, then there are
m,n\in P
such that
q=mn^{-1}
,
y\in\textrm{dom}(m)
,
z\in\textrm{dom}(n)
, and
y\cdot m=z\cdot n
.
Say
\mu\in\Lambda^{m}\cap y
and
\nu\in\Lambda^{n}\cap z
.
Let
x\coloneqq y\cdot\mu=y\cdot m=z\cdot n=z\cdot\nu\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
.
Then, we have
g=(\mu x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},\nu x)
.
Now suppose
g=(\mu x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},\nu x)
for some
x\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
and
\mu,\nu\in\Lambda
with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\mu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\nu)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
.
We know
\mu x=y\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)
by assumption.
Since
\mu\in\Lambda^{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)}\cap y
, we have
(y,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu))\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Similarly,
(z,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu))\in\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)*P
.
Therefore,
y\in\textrm{dom}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu))
,
z\in\textrm{dom}(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu))

y\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=y\cdot\mu=(\mu x)\cdot\mu=x=(\nu x)\cdot\nu=z\cdot\nu=z\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu).

Hence,
g\in G(\mathcal{F}(\Lambda),P,T)
.
∎

Recall from

\mathcal{B}

Using the basis for the topology on

However, we show in the following that the parameters

Proposition 5.23

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset

where
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
and
J,K
are finite subsets of
\Lambda

G_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

Suppose
(x,mn^{-1},y)
is in
Z(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus J\right),m,n,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu^{\prime}\setminus K\right))
for some
m,n\in P
,
\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
, and finite subsets

of
\Lambda
.
Notice
\mu^{\prime}\in x
and
\nu^{\prime}\in y
.
Also, since
x\in\textrm{dom}(m)
and

\mu_{m}\in x\cap\Lambda^{m}
and
\nu_{n}\in y\cap\Lambda^{n}
with

w\coloneqq x\cdot\mu_{m}=x\cdot m=y\cdot n=y\cdot\nu_{n}.

Because
x
and
y
are directed, there are
\mu_{m}^{\prime}\in\Lambda
and
\nu_{n}^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\mu^{\prime},\mu_{m}\preceq\mu_{m}\mu_{m}^{\prime}\in x
and
\nu^{\prime},\nu_{n}\preceq\nu_{n}\nu_{n}^{\prime}\in y
.
Then
\mu_{m}^{\prime}\in x\cdot\mu_{m}=w
and
\nu_{n}^{\prime}\in y\cdot\nu_{n}=w
.
That is,
\mu_{m}^{\prime},\nu_{n}^{\prime}\in w
and
w
is directed, so there are
\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime},\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}\in w
.
Let
\mu\coloneqq\mu_{m}\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime}
, and let
\nu\coloneqq\nu_{n}\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}
.
Since
\mu^{\prime}\preceq\mu
and
\nu^{\prime}\preceq\nu
, we know
\mu,\nu\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
because
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
is a right ideal in
\Lambda
(
Lemma

3.4
(a)
).
Then,
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus J\right)
and
y\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu\setminus K\right)
.
Also, because
\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}
we have

\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{m})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu_{n})^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{m})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu_{n})^{-1}=mn^{-1}.

Lastly, we have

x\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=(x\cdot\mu_{m})\cdot(\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime})=(y\cdot\nu_{n})\cdot(\nu_{m}^{\prime}\nu_{m}^{\prime\prime})=y\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu).

Hence,
(x,mn^{-1},y)\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus J,\nu\setminus K\right)
.

Now fix
(x^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},y^{\prime})\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus J,\nu\setminus K\right)
.
Recall
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1}=mn^{-1}
.
Because
x^{\prime}
is hereditary and
\mu^{\prime}\preceq\mu
, we have
\mu^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}
.
Similarly,
\nu^{\prime}\in y^{\prime}
.
Thus,
x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus J\right)
and
y^{\prime}\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu^{\prime}\setminus K\right)
.
Also,
\mu_{m}\in x^{\prime}
and
\nu_{n}\in y^{\prime}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu_{m})=m
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu_{n})=n
, we have
x^{\prime}\in\textrm{dom}(m)
and
y^{\prime}\in\textrm{dom}(n)
.
By assumption
x^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)=y^{\prime}\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)
, so
x^{\prime}\cdot\mu=y^{\prime}\cdot\nu
.
By definition of
\mu
and
\nu
, we have

x^{\prime}\cdot(\mu_{m}\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime})=y^{\prime}\cdot(\nu_{n}\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}).

Since
\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime}=\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}
, the above implies

x^{\prime}\cdot\mu_{m}=(\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime})(x^{\prime}\cdot(\mu_{m}\mu_{m}^{\prime}\mu_{m}^{\prime\prime}))=(\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime})(y^{\prime}\cdot(\nu_{n}\nu_{n}^{\prime}\nu_{n}^{\prime\prime}))=y^{\prime}\cdot\nu_{n}.

Hence,
(x^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},y^{\prime})\in Z(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu^{\prime}\setminus J\right),m,n,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)}\left(\nu^{\prime}\setminus K\right))
.
∎

6.
Comparisons with Spielberg’s groupoids

The purpose of this section is to compare our path groupoids from
Section

5
with relevant groupoids in the literature.
The main theorem (
Theorem

6.6
) is that, if
\Lambda
is finitely aligned (i.e.
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
), then our path and boundary-path groupoids are topologically isomorphic to analogous groupoids developed by Spielberg for the finitely aligned setting in
[
Spi12
,
Spi14
]
.
Spielberg has also developed a groupoid approach for nonfinitely aligned
\Lambda
in
[
Spi20
]
.
The author showed in his thesis
[
Jon24
]
that the path groupoid
G_{\Lambda_{0}}
of the nonfinitely aligned
2
-graph
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
has a different structure of open invariant subsets of its unit space to that of Spielberg’s groupoid of
\Lambda_{0}
(and hence their associated Steinberg algebras
[
Ste10
]
and C*-algebras
[
Ren80
]
have different ideal structure).
We omit this result because we intend to give a more comprehensive comparison of our construction and Spielberg’s in future work.
Lastly, we show that Spielberg’s groupoid of the finitely aligned relative category of paths
(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
associated to each
P
-graph
\Lambda
with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
in
Section

3.4
does not generally coincide with our path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
.

6.1.
Path space in the finitely aligned case

We begin by noting important simplifications to the path space when
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. Then,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\partial\Lambda
is the closure of
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Because
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
, and filters are nonempty, every filter contains some
\lambda\in\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)
.
That is,
\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
Then,
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)\cap\mathcal{F}_{\mathrm{FA}}(\Lambda)=\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
, so
\partial\Lambda
is the closure of
\mathcal{U}(\Lambda)
in
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
∎

While discussing the finitely aligned case, we denote the path space by
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
because of
Lemma

6.1
.
A crucial difference in the finitely aligned case is that the parameter
K
for the usual basis for the path space can be assumed to be a subset of
\mu\Lambda
, as follows.
Among other things, this makes left-shift maps open (as well as being continuous bijections as in
Lemma

5.3
).

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. The collection of

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right),

where
\mu\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
is finite, is a basis for
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.

Proof.

Suppose
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K^{\prime}\right)
, where
\mu\in\Lambda
and
K^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda
is finite.
For each
\zeta\in K^{\prime}
, since
\Lambda
is finitely aligned, there is a finite (possibly empty)
J_{\zeta}\subseteq\Lambda
such that

\mu\Lambda\cap\zeta\Lambda=\bigcup_{\gamma\in J_{\zeta}}\gamma\Lambda.

Then,
K\coloneqq\bigcup_{\zeta\in K^{\prime}}J_{\zeta}
is a finite subset of
\mu\Lambda
, and the directed and hereditary properties of filters imply

x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K^{\prime}\right).

∎

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. The collection of

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right),

where
\kappa,\lambda\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\kappa\Lambda
and
L\subseteq\lambda\Lambda
are finite, is a basis for the topology on
G_{\Lambda}
.

Proof.

The collection of
Z(\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\kappa\setminus K\right),m,n,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\lambda\setminus L\right))
, where
m,n\in P
,
\kappa,\lambda\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\kappa\Lambda
and
L\subseteq\lambda\Lambda
are finite, is a basis for the topology on
G_{\Lambda}
because of
Lemma

2.6
and
Lemma

6.2
.
Then, the claim follows from similar arguments to those in the proof of
Proposition

5.23
.
∎

6.2.
Spielberg’s groupoid in the finitely aligned case

We recall Spielberg’s groupoid in the finitely aligned case from
[
Spi12
, Page 729]
and
[
MS22
, §2]
.
For each
\alpha\in\Lambda
and
\beta_{1},\dots,\beta_{n}\subseteq\alpha\Lambda\setminus\{\alpha\}
, let
E\coloneqq\alpha\Lambda\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\beta_{i}\Lambda
, and let
\hat{E}\coloneqq\left\{x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)\;\middle|\;E\supseteq x\cap\gamma\Lambda\text{ for some }\gamma\in x\right\}
.
The collection of
\hat{E}
is a basis of compact sets for a topology
\tau^{\mathrm{Spi}}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
(Note that
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
is denoted by “
\Lambda^{*}
” in
[
Spi12
,
MS22
]
).
Let
\Lambda*\Lambda*\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
be the set of
(\alpha,\beta,x)\in\Lambda\times\Lambda\times\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
such that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\beta)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)
.
Recall
\alpha x\coloneqq\left\{\mu\in\Lambda\;\middle|\;\mu\preceq\alpha\gamma\text{ for some }\gamma\in x\right\}
for each
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
with
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}(x)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\alpha)
.
For any
(\alpha,\beta,x),(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime})\in\Lambda*\Lambda*\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, let
(\alpha,\beta,x)\sim(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime})
if there are
y\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
x=\gamma y
,
x^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}y
,
\alpha\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
, and
\beta\gamma=\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
.
Then,
\sim
is an equivalence relation, and we write
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
for the set of equivalence classes of
\Lambda*\Lambda*\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
with respect to
\sim
.
Let
{G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}}^{(2)}
be the pairs
([\alpha,\beta,x],[\gamma,\delta,y])\in G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}\times G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
that satisfy
\beta x=\gamma y
, in which case
[
Spi14
, Lemma 4.12]
implies there are
z\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\xi,\eta\in\Lambda
such that
x=\xi z
,
y=\eta z
and
\beta\xi=\gamma\eta
.
Define composition and inversion maps by

[\alpha,\beta,x][\gamma,\delta,y]\coloneqq[\alpha\xi,\delta\eta,z]

and

[\alpha,\beta,x]^{-1}\coloneqq[\beta,\alpha,x],

respectively.
Given a basis
\mathcal{B}
for the topology
\tau^{\mathrm{Spi}}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
, the collection of
[\alpha,\beta,B]\coloneqq\left\{[\alpha,\beta,x]\;\middle|\;x\in B\right\}
, where
B\in\mathcal{B}
, is a basis under which
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
is a locally compact Hausdorff étale groupoid.

6.3.
Isomorphism with Spielberg’s groupoid in the finitely aligned case

We show our path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
is topologically isomorphic to
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
when
\Lambda
is finitely aligned (
Theorem

6.6
).
Recall from
Lemma

6.2
that the collection of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, where
\mu\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
is finite, is a basis for
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
if
\Lambda
is finitely aligned.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a (not necessarily finitely aligned)
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset
.
For any
\mu\in\Lambda
and finite
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
,
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)=\hat{E}
for
E\coloneqq\mu\Lambda\setminus\bigcup_{\nu\in K}\nu\Lambda
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
.
Suppose for a contradiction that
E\not\supseteq x\cap\mu\Lambda
, so there is
\lambda\in x\cap\mu\Lambda
such that
\lambda\notin E
.
Since
E=\mu\Lambda\setminus\bigcup_{\nu\in K}\nu\Lambda
, we must have
\lambda\in\nu\Lambda
for some
\nu\in K
.
Since
x
is hereditary,
\nu\in x
, which contradicts
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
.
Hence,
E\supseteq x\cap\mu\Lambda
, which implies
x\in\hat{E}
.
Now let
x\in\hat{E}
, so there is
\gamma\in x
such that
E\supseteq x\cap\gamma\Lambda
.
In particular,
\gamma\in E\subseteq\mu\Lambda
, so
\mu\in x
.
Suppose for a contradiction that there is
\nu\in K\cap x
.
Since
x
is directed, there is
\lambda\in x
such that
\gamma,\nu\preceq\lambda
.
Thus,
E\supseteq x\cap\gamma\Lambda\ni\lambda
, so
\lambda\in E
.
However,
\lambda\in\nu\Lambda
, contradicting the definition of
E
.
Hence,
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
.
∎

The only difference to the parameterization of the
\hat{E}
in
Section

6.2
when defining
\tau^{\mathrm{Spi}}
and the parameterization of the
\hat{E}
appearing in
Lemma

6.4
is that our
K
could contain
\mu
, in which case
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)=\emptyset
.
That is, the basis for our topology on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
from
Lemma

6.2
differs from the basis for the topology
\tau^{\mathrm{Spi}}
on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
only by
\emptyset\subseteq\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
Therefore:

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. Then,
\tau^{\mathrm{Spi}}
equals the topology on
\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
given by the basis from
Lemma

6.2
.

Theorem 6.6
.

Let
(Q,P)
be a weakly quasi-lattice ordered group, and let
\Lambda
be a
P
-graph with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)=\Lambda
. The map

G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}\to G_{\Lambda},\quad[\alpha,\beta,x]\mapsto(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)

is a topological groupoid isomorphism.
Moreover, the reductions
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}|_{\partial\Lambda}
and
G_{\partial\Lambda}
are topologically isomorphic.

Proof.

Our first aim is to show the map is a well-defined bijection.
Let
[\alpha,\beta,x]\in G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
.
Since
\alpha x\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)=x=\beta x\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)
,
(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)\in G_{\Lambda}
.
If
[\alpha,\beta,x]=[\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime}]
, then there are
y\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
x=\gamma y
,
x^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}y
,
\alpha\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
, and
\beta\gamma=\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
.
Then,

\alpha x=\alpha(\gamma y)=(\alpha\gamma)y=(\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime})y=\alpha^{\prime}(\gamma^{\prime}y)=\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime}.

A similar argument shows
\beta x=\beta^{\prime}x^{\prime}
.
Lastly,
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime})^{-1}
follows from the identities
\alpha\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
and
\beta\gamma=\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
and the fact that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}
is a functor.
Hence, the map is well-defined.
The map is surjective because any
(x,q,y)\in G_{\Lambda}
is of the form
(\mu x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\nu)^{-1},\nu x)
for some
x\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
by
Proposition

5.21
, which is the image of
[\mu,\nu,x]
.
For injectivity, suppose
[\alpha,\beta,x],[\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime}]\in G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
are such that
(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)=(\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime},\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime})^{-1},\beta^{\prime}x^{\prime})
.
Notice
\alpha,\alpha^{\prime}\in\alpha x=\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime}
, which is directed, so there are
\gamma,\gamma^{\prime}\in\Lambda
such that
\alpha\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}\in\alpha x=\alpha^{\prime}x^{\prime}
.
Notice
\gamma\in x
, so
y\coloneqq x\cdot\gamma\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
.
Then,
x=\gamma(x\cdot\gamma)=\gamma y
, and
x^{\prime}=\gamma^{\prime}(x^{\prime}\cdot\gamma^{\prime})=\gamma^{\prime}y
.
To show
\beta\gamma=\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
, we show
\beta\gamma,\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}\in\beta x
and
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\gamma)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime})
, and we apply the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{\beta x}
.
Since
\gamma\in x
, we have
\beta\gamma\in\beta x
.
Also,
\gamma^{\prime}\in x^{\prime}
, so
\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}\in\beta^{\prime}x^{\prime}=\beta x
.
The identity
\alpha\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
implies
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)^{-1}
.
If
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)
, then
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\gamma)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime})
, so it remains to show
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)
, which follows from the assumption that
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha^{\prime})\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta^{\prime})^{-1}
.
Therefore,
\beta\gamma=\beta^{\prime}\gamma^{\prime}
by the injectivity of
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}|_{\beta x}
.
Thus,
[\alpha,\beta,x]=[\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},x^{\prime}]
, so the map is a bijection.

Next we aim to show the map is a homeomorphism.
The image of an arbitrary basic open set
[\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)]
, where
\alpha,\beta,\mu\in\Lambda
and
K\subseteq\mu\Lambda
is finite, is the set of
(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)
such that
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
.
We claim this image equals

I\coloneqq\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\alpha\mu\setminus\left\{\alpha\nu\;\middle|\;\nu\in K\right\},\beta\mu\setminus\left\{\beta\nu\;\middle|\;\nu\in K\right\}\right).

Consider
(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)
for some
x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
.
Observe
\alpha\mu\in\alpha x
.
If there is a
\nu\in K
such that
\alpha\nu\in\alpha x
, then
\nu\in x\cap K
, which is a contradiction.
A similar argument applies to
\beta x
, so we have
\alpha x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\alpha\mu\setminus\left\{\alpha\nu\;\middle|\;\nu\in K\right\}\right)
and

\beta x\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\beta\mu\setminus\left\{\beta\nu\;\middle|\;\nu\in K\right\}\right)
.
Lastly, notice
\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\mu)^{-1}
, and

(\alpha x)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\mu)=(\alpha x)\cdot(\alpha\mu)=x\cdot\mu=(\beta x)\cdot(\beta\mu)=(\beta x)\cdot\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\mu).

Hence,
(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)\in I
.
Now fix
(x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\mu)^{-1},y)\in I
, so
x\cdot(\alpha\mu)=y\cdot(\beta\mu)
, which implies
z\coloneqq x\cdot\alpha=y\cdot\beta
and
(x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\mu)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\mu)^{-1},y)=(\alpha z,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta z)
.
Since
\alpha\mu\in x
,
\mu\in x\cdot\alpha=z
.
There cannot be
\nu\in K\cap z
, otherwise
\alpha\nu\in x
.
Hence,
z\in\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)
, and so
[\alpha,\beta,z]\in[\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)]
.
That is, the image of the basic open set
[\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\mu\setminus K\right)]
equals
I
.
An arbitrary basic open set
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)}\left(\kappa\setminus K,\lambda\setminus L\right)
in
G_{\Lambda}
(see
Lemma

6.3
) is in the form of
I
with
\alpha=\kappa
,
\beta=\lambda
,
\mu=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\alpha)=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{s}}(\beta)
and
J=K\cdot\kappa\cup L\cdot\lambda
.
Hence, basic open sets in
G_{\Lambda}
identify with basic open sets in
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
via the map, so the map is a homeomorphism.

We now aim to show the groupoids are isomorphic.
If
([\alpha,\beta,x],[\gamma,\delta,y])\in{G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}}^{(2)}
, then
\beta x=\gamma y
, which implies
((\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x),(\gamma y,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta)^{-1},\delta y))\in{G_{\Lambda}}^{(2)}
.
The converse holds in the same way, so the map identifies
{G_{\Lambda}}^{(2)}
with
{{G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}}}^{(2)}
.
Recall that
([\alpha,\beta,x],[\gamma,\delta,y])\in{G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}}^{(2)}
implies there are
z\in\mathcal{F}(\Lambda)
and
\xi,\eta\in\Lambda
such that
x=\xi z
,
y=\eta z
and
\beta\xi=\gamma\eta
.
Then,

[\alpha,\beta,x][\gamma,\delta,y]=[\alpha\xi,\delta\eta,z]\mapsto((\alpha\xi)z,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta\eta)^{-1},(\delta\eta)z).

Also,

(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1},\beta x)(\gamma y,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta)^{-1},\delta y)=(\alpha x,\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta)^{-1},\delta y).

We have
(\alpha\xi)z=\alpha x
and
(\delta\eta)z=\delta y
.
Lastly,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\xi)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\eta)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\eta)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\xi)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\gamma\eta)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta\eta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\xi)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\beta\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta\eta)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\alpha\xi)\boldsymbol{\mathrm{d}}(\delta\eta)^{-1}.

Thus, the map preserves composition.
It is immediate from the definitions of the inversions and the definition of the map that inversion is preserved too.
Therefore, the map is a topological groupoid isomorphism.
Our boundary-path space
\partial\Lambda
equals the boundary of
\Lambda
defined by Spielberg on
[
Spi12
, Page 729]
, there too denoted by “
\partial\Lambda
”.
Our boundary-path groupoid
G_{\partial\Lambda}
is defined to be the reduction of
G_{\Lambda}
to
\partial\Lambda
, which therefore coincides with the reduction
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}|_{\partial\Lambda}
of
G^{\mathrm{Spi}}_{\Lambda}
to
\partial\Lambda
.
∎

6.5.
Spielberg’s groupoid of a relative category of paths in general

Recall from
Section

3.4
that
(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
is a
finitely aligned
relative category of paths for any
P
-graph
\Lambda
with
\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda)\neq\emptyset

Spielberg associates a groupoid
G(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
to each relative category of paths
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
in
[
Spi20
, Definition 14.5]
.
In this section, we show
G(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
is not topologically isomorphic to our path groupoid
G_{\Lambda}
in general.
We first state
[
Spi14
, Remark 3.7]
explicitly:

Proposition 6.10

(Remark 3.7 of
[
Spi14
]
)
.

If
(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
is a finitely aligned relative category of paths, then
G(\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda)
is topologically isomorphic to the groupoid
G_{\Lambda^{\prime}}^{\mathrm{Spi}}
from
Section

6.2
of the finitely aligned category of paths
\Lambda^{\prime}
.

In particular,
G(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
is topologically isomorphic to
G_{\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda)}^{\mathrm{Spi}}
.
By
Lemma

6.5
, the unit space of
G(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda),\Lambda)
is homeomorphic to
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda))
, which has the basis from
Lemma

6.2
.
Recall the
2
-graph
\Lambda_{0}
from
Example

3.2
, for which
\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda_{0})=\mathrm{FA}(\Lambda_{0})\cup\{v\}=\Lambda_{0}\setminus\{\lambda,\mu\}
.
The nondiscrete elements of
\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda_{0}))
are
F(t)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(\beta_{n})
,
F(w)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(\alpha_{n})
and
F(v)=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(\lambda\alpha_{n})
.
This third nondiscrete element is due to the fact that
\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda_{0})
does not contain
\lambda
or
\mu
, so the basic open sets containing

are of the form

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{F}(\mathrm{FA}_{\boldsymbol{\mathrm{r}}}(\Lambda_{0}))}\left(v\setminus\{\lambda\alpha_{n_{1}},\dots,\lambda\alpha_{n_{k}}\}\right).

On the other hand, the only nondiscrete elements of

and

Therefore,

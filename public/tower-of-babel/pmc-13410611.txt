Genome-wide association analysis and molecular marker development for effective tiller number in rice natural population
Authors: Bin Liang, Jintian Chen, Beibei Su, Xianjun Zeng, Fenyun Zhang, Shunwu Yu, Lei Wang, Zhening Miao, Shimei Long, Xiaosong Ma, Hongyan Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEffective tiller number (ETN) has a pivotal role in determination of rice (Oryza sativa L.) grain yield. ETN is a complex quantitative trait regulated by both genetic and environmental factors. Multiple tillering-related genes have been cloned previously, few of them have been utilized in practical breeding programs. In this study, a genome-wide association study (GWAS) for ETN was conducted on 270 rice accessions with 1,019,883 SNPs. Total of 214 SNPs were detected on eight rice chromosomes for ETN at a significance level of –log10(P) > 6.0. 12 QTLs associated with ETN were identified, including three known functional genes (e.g., D10, D27, Stvb-i) and one novel gene OsIAA3. Compared to wild-type plants, OsIAA3-overexpressing plants could significantly increase ETN in rice. In the natural population, haplotype analysis was conducted for D10, D27, Stvb-i, and OsIAA3, and their favorable alleles were identified. Molecular markers were also developed and validated within the natural population and bi-parent population, and the validation results demonstrated that the developed molecular markers can be utilized for marker-assisted selection breeding. This study not only enhances our understanding of the genetic regulatory basis of rice ETN but also provides excellent germplasm and favorable haplotypes that serve as valuable breeding resources and genetic material for improving ETN and yield in rice.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12870-026-08946-4.Keywords: GWAS, Haplotype analysis, Molecular markers, Effective tiller number, Oryza sativa L

Introduction

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is one of the main staple crops for almost half of the world population [1]. Given the continuous growth of the global population, increasing rice yield has become an urgent necessity. Rice grain yield is primarily determined by grain weight, grain number per panicle, and effective panicle number. Rice tillering is one of the most critical agronomic traits determining grain yield as it directly influences the effective tiller number (ETN) per plant. The concept of ‘ideal plant architecture’ (IPA) further emphasizes that optimizing effective tiller number is essential for maximizing yield potential [2, 3]. Effective tiller number more accurately reflects the capacity of a rice plant to convert vegetative growth into reproductive output than total tiller number. Consequently, it constitutes a crucial breeding trait and contributes significantly to final yield.

Over the past two decades, tremendous progress has been made in elucidating the molecular mechanisms underlying rice tillering. Rice tiller number has been revealed to be strongly associated with plant hormones, including gibberellin (GA), auxin, cytokinin (CTK), strigolactone (SL), etc. Many genes regulating rice tillering number have been identified in plant hormones metabolic pathways. MOC1 is the first gene identified as a key regulator controlling rice tillering and branching, and DELLA protein SLENDER RICE 1 (SLR1), a repressor of gibberellin (GA) signaling, inhibits MOC1 degradation to regulate both tillering and plant height [4]. NGR5 was identified as a novel key component in the GA signaling pathway, it interacts with the GA receptor GID1 and recruits the PRC2 complex to regulate target gene expression via modulating histone H3K27me3 methylation, thereby controlling rice tiller number and nitrogen response [5]. Strigolactones are carotenoid-derived hormones that suppress tiller bud outgrowth. Their biosynthesis requires D27, D17 (CCD7), and D10 (CCD8), while signal transduction depends on the receptor D14, the F-box protein D3, and the transcriptional repressor D53 [6–9]. Tiller initiation can also be influenced by some genes independent of plant hormones. For example, MOC3 encodes a nuclear-localized transcriptional repressor like its ortholog WUS in Arabidopsis and its loss-of-function mutation disrupts the initiation of tiller buds [10, 11]. MOC2 encodes a fructose-1,6-bisphosphatase participating in sucrose synthesis, and the moc2 mutant also shows monoculm phenotype [12]. TEOSINTE BRANCHED1 (OsTB1) belongs to the plant-specific TCP transcription factor family, which is a negative regulator of tillering [13].

Apical dominance is a biological phenomenon in which plants prioritize the upward growth of the main stem over the lateral expansion of side branches. The mechanism underlying apical dominance relies on auxin, which is primarily indole-3-acetic acid (IAA). The rice Aux/IAA gene family constitutes a core component of the auxin signal transduction pathway. OsIAA6 has been shown to inhibit the tiller buds growth by positively regulating the auxin transporter OsPIN1 and the rice tiller suppressor OsTB1 [14]. Notably, although members of the Aux/IAA family such as OsIAA3 have been well characterized in grain size regulation [15, 16], their potential roles in tillering control remain largely unknown.

Tiller number is a classic quantitative trait, however, analyses based on mutants and biparental mapping populations can only capture a limited subset of allelic variation between two parental lines. Worse still, many of these mutants exhibit extreme pleiotropic phenotypes - for example, d10 and d27 show severe dwarfism and dramatically reduced fertility [6, 7], d53 displays excessive tillering accompanied by stunted growth and small panicles [8, 9] - making them difficult to deploy directly in elite cultivars. Consequently, favorable alleles and haplotypes underlying tillering variation in natural populations remain the most promising targets for molecular breeding. Within rice germplasm resources, the allelic diversity governing effective tillering number remains largely underexploited. Genome-wide association studies (GWAS), which leverage historical recombination events and accumulated natural variations within diverse germplasm collections, have emerged as a powerful tool for deciphering the genetic basis of tillering in rice [17–20]. Huang et al. [17] pioneered this field by performing GWAS on 517 rice landraces and validating the contribution of natural variation in OsSPL14/IPA1 to effective tiller number (ETN). Liu et al. [18] achieved a major breakthrough by defining nitrogen-regulated tillering (TRN), cloning OsTCP19, and identifying a causal 29-bp insertion/deletion in its promoter region. Ren et al. [19] and Liu et al. [20] also performed GWAS for ETN and further analyzed the haplotypes of candidate genes within the associated QTL intervals. These studies have evolved from validating known QTLs to cloning novel genes and developing functional markers, thereby driving molecular design breeding based on superior haplotypes.

Previous GWAS studies on effective tiller number (ETN) have mainly focused on the identification of significantly associated SNPs [17–20]. Although only a few studies have performed transgenic validation and haplotype analysis of candidate genes [19, 20], the present study places greater emphasis on practical breeding application. To this end, we systematically performed haplotype analysis, RIL population validation, and functional marker development for all four target genes, thereby enabling the direct application of our findings in rice breeding programs. Furthermore, prior to this work, no Aux/IAA family members had been identified and validated as regulators of tillering through GWAS. This indicates that unexplored regulatory layers likely remain within the auxin-mediated signaling network that governs plant shoot branching.

The exploration of superior haplotypes of rice tillering genes lays a core foundation for molecular design breeding. OsSPL14/IPA1 is a landmark gene for rice plant architecture improvement. Three naturally superior allelic variants, ipa1-1D, WFP, and ipa1-2D, have been identified, providing key gene resources for breeding rice with ideal plant architecture [2, 3]. The OsTCP19-H allele, which carries a 29 bp promoter deletion, sustains high tillering capacity under low nitrogen conditions [18]. Among five distinct haplotypes of NGR5, Hap 2 is significantly associated with elevated gene expression, increased tiller number, and higher grain yield under field conditions [5]. These superior haplotypes provide valuable molecular targets readily applicable to marker-assisted selection (MAS) and gene editing. Nevertheless, the elite haplotypes of many well-characterized tillering genes - including those involved in strigolactone biosynthesis and auxin signaling pathways - as well as their practical utility across diverse genetic backgrounds, remain to be comprehensively and systematically explored.

In this study, a GWAS for rice ETN was performed using 270 rice germplasm accessions, and 12 QTLs were identified including three well-characterized functional genes, D10, D27 and Stvb-i. critically, OsIAA3, an Aux/IAA member previously implicated only in grain size regulation [16, 17], was identified as a novel negative regulator of tiller number. Furthermore, functional markers were developed for D10, D27, Stvb-i, and OsIAA3, which can be directly utilized for marker-assist selection to facilitate the genetic improvement of rice ETN.

Materials and methods

Plant materials

A subset of 270 accessions of rice landraces and cultivars, collected from Asia, Africa, and America. This population has previously been used in GWAS for drought resistance [21], grain size [22], mesocotyl elongation [23], etc. All accessions were planted at normal water field in Jinshan District, Shanghai, China, in 2022. The plant material was arranged in a single-factor randomized block design with three biological replicates, each experimental plot had an area of 1 m2, with a planting space of 20 cm between rows and 20 cm between hills. Fertilizer application and pest and disease control were consistent with normal field management. The effective tillers number in rice was counted at maturity.

GWAS analysis

Genotype data of the natural population were re-sequenced by Hiseq 2000. Paired-end sequence reads were mapped to a rice reference genome sequence of japonica var. Nipponbare (MSU v6.1), and were used for SNP identification, following the procedures described by Wu et al. [23].

Genome-wide association mapping was conducted by the Efficient Mixed Model Association (EMMA) method using the Genomic Association and Prediction Integrated Tool (GAPIT) software package in R [24]. A total of 996,722 SNP markers with a minimum allelic frequency (MAF) ≥ 0.05, evenly distributed across all 12 chromosomes, were used for GWAS in this study. Additionally, 144,995 SNPs with a missing data below 10% in this natural population were used to calculate kinship among individuals. Principal component analysis (PCA) was used to adjust the population structure. The genome-wide threshold of -log10(P) = 6.0 was calculated from the formula of “-log10(1/effective number of SNPs)”. r2 of LD was calculated by PLINK ver1.07, and QTL intervals were defined using SNPs in linkage disequilibrium (LD) with the lead SNP at r2 ≥ 0.6 [22, 25]. The variance explained the phenotype by the lead SNP (with the lowest P value) and was calculated by comparing the sum of squares of the variance between groups and the sum of squares of the variance of the full model. These procedures have been described by Ma et al. [22].

Candidate gene identification

Within each QTL interval, genes associated with hormones regulating plant branching (including strigolactone [SL], auxin [IAA], and gibberellin [GA], etc.) or genes previously reported to control rice tiller number were selected as candidate genes based on rice genome annotation (MSU6.1).

Molecular cloning and transformation of rice

To construct the overexpression vector, the full-length cDNA of OsIAA3 with CAMV35S promoter was digested with BamHI and BstEII, and then ligated to pCAMBIA1300. These constructs were introduced into Nipponbare rice (Oryza sativa japonica) by Agrobacterium tumefaciens-mediated transformation. The primers are listed in Table S1.

Evaluation of effective tiller number in OsIAA3 transgenic plants

OsIAA3 transgenic plants were planted at the Baihe Experimental Station of the Shanghai Academy of Agricultural Sciences in Qingpu District, Shanghai in 2025, and Lingshui county, Hainan Province in 2026, respectively. Every line was arranged in 5 rows × 7 plants with three biological replicates. The effective tillers number in rice was counted at maturity.

RNA isolation and RT-qPCR

Total RNA was isolated from rice leaves using the TRNzol reagent (TIANGEN, China). cDNA templates were synthesized using TransScript® II Reverse Transcriptase (Transgen, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Real-time quantitative RT-qPCR was performed on a CFX96 Real-Time PCR system (Bio-Rad, USA) using TransStart® Top Green qPCR SuperMix (+ Dye I) (Transgen, China) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. The rice Actin1 gene was used as endogenous control. All primers are listed in Table S1.

Molecular marker development and validation

Molecular markers were developed based on sequence differences. Three InDel markers (including D10_15, D27_218, Stvb-i_61) were developed for D10, D27, and Stvb-i, respectively, and a KASP marker was developed for OsIAA3, all primers were listed in Table S1. The PCR program was conducted as follows: (1) 4 min at 94℃; (2) 35 cycles of 20 s at 94℃, 20 s at 53℃ (for D10_15 and Stvb-i_61) and 55℃ (for D27 _218) and 25 s at 72℃; (3) 5 min at 72℃. DNA products of D10_15 were separated by 6% polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis. and the DNA products of D27_218 and Stvb-i_61 were separated by electrophoresis with 1% agarose gels that were stained with ethidium bromide and visualized using UV light. For KASP, 3.6 µl DNA (30–60 ng/µl) was added to the PCR plate and dried at 65℃ for 30 min. The PCR system was 2×PARMS mix (Jingtai, Wuhan) 5 µl, Forward primer1 0.15 µl, Forward primer2 0.15 µl, Reverse primer 0.4 µl, ddH20 4.3 µl. The PCR program was conducted as follows: (1) 20 min at 94℃; (2) 10 cycles of 20 s at 94℃, 1 min at 65 − 57℃ (-0.8℃/cycle); (3) 32 cycles of 20 s at 94℃, 1 min at 57℃; (4) 1 min at 25℃. The DNA products of the KASP marker of OsIAA3 were identified by Infinite® M1000 multimode microplate reader (Tecan, Switzerland). The online software snpdecoder (http://www.snpway.com/snpdecoder/) was used to analyze and convert fluorescence signals, and output the genotype results.

OsIAA3 positively regulated ETN in rice

To further confirm whether OsIAA3 regulated effective ETN in rice, OsIAA3 overexpression (OE) lines driven by the CaMV 35 S promoter were generated via rice genetic transformation. The OsIAA3 transcription levels in these OE lines were determined by RT-qPCR, and the transcription levels of these OE lines were significantly upregulated, with a more than 90-fold change compared to wild type (WT) (Fig. 5A). Among them, three OE lines (namely OE1, OE2 and OE3) with markedly higher OsIAA3 expression levels were subsequently selected for further analysis. In Shanghai, the effective tiller number (ETN) of wild-type (WT) plants was 19.5, whereas the corresponding ETN values for OE1, OE2, and OE3 were 29.7, 33.1, and 26.6, respectively. Student’s t-test results showed that the differences were statistically significant, with P-values of 7.54E-03, 1.00E-02, and 6.64E-03, respectively (Fig. 5B, C). Consistently, in Hainan, the WT plants had an ETN of 9.9, while the OE lines OE1, OE2, and OE3 exhibited ETN values of 14.6, 17.9, and 19.7, respectively. The Student’s t-test P-values were 2.12E-05, 1.74E-07, and 9.39E-09, respectively (Fig. 5D, E). Phenotypic assays revealed that the ETN was significantly increased in the OE lines compared with the WT. These results confirmed that OsIAA3 positively regulated ETN in rice.

Haplotype analysis and development of functional markers for OsIAA3

To develop a functional marker of OsIAA3, 17 SNPs at promoter and open reading frame (ORF) of OsIAA3, derived from re-sequence data, were selected to analyze haplotype. And seven haplotypes were identified, Hap 5 and Hap 6 exhibiting significantly fewer tiller than the other haplotypes (Fig. 6A). Since the promoter of OsIAA3 contains several SNPs, the promoter activities of Hap 5 and Hap 7 were analyzed using a dual-luciferase assay in rice protoplast, which showed that the promoter activity of Hap 7 was significantly higher than that of Hap 5 (Fig. 6B). This result was consistent with the observation that rice accessions with Hap 7 had more tiller than that of Hap 5. Among these SNPs, SNP0107248534 as a promoter SNP of OsIAA3 was selected to develop a KASP (Kompetitive Allele-Specific PCR) marker (Figure S1). Genotyping was then performed using this OsIAA3 KASP marker on two populations: a panel of 270 rice accessions and a recombinant inbred line (RIL) populaiton comprising 200 lines derived from a cross between IRAT109 (a low ETN line, Hap 5) and Zhenshan 97B (a high ETN line, Hap 7), In the natural population, accessions carrying the SNP0107248534-A allele (146 lines) had significantly higher ETN than those carrying the SNP0107248534-G allele (95 lines), with P values of 2.66E-05 (Fig. 6C). The average grain yield per plant for 146 SNP0107248534-A carriers was 11.54 g. In the Zhenshan 97B × IRAT 109 RIL population, 107 lines carrying the Zhenshan 97B allele (SNP0107248534-A) exhibited significantly higher ETN than the 87 lines with the IRAT 109 allele (SNP0107248534-G), with P values of 2.82E-03 (Fig. 6E).

Haplotype analysis and development of functional markers for D10

17 SNPs in the promoter and open reading frame (ORF) of D10, derived from re-sequence data, were selected to analyze haplotype. Four haplotypes were identified in the rice natural population, with Hap 2 exhibiting significantly higher ETN than the remaining three haplotypes (Fig. 2A). SNP0131228355 at promoter of D10 with p value of 1.59E-07. Accessions carrying the SNP0131228355-C allele ( Hap 2 and Hap 4) displayed significantly more ETNs, whereas those with the SNP0131228355-T allele (Hap 1 and Hap 3) had fewer tiller numbers. Therefore, the variation in the promoter region of the D10 may have a greater impact on rice ETN.

Base on above results, we sequenced the promoter of D10 and identified a InDel with 15 bp between Zhenshan 97B and IRAT109. A panel of 270 rice germplasm accessions was genotyped at the D10 locus using a newly developed molecular marker D10_15. The PCR products were separated by electrophoresis on a 6% denaturing polyacrylamide gel (Fig. 2B), with 145 accessions producing a 118 bp amplicon and 122 accessions yielding a 103 bp amplicon. The ETN of accessions carrying the 118 bp amplicon were extremely significantly higher than those with the 103 bp amplicon (P = 1.23E-03) (Fig. 2C). The grain yield per plant of the 145 accessions with the 118 bp amplicon was 11.61 g, whereas it was 8.35 g for the 122 accessions with the 103 bp amplicon. These results indicated that the grain yield per plant of accessions with the 118 bp amplicon was extremely significantly higher than that of accessions with the 103 bp amplicon (P = 5.69E-16) in natural population (Fig. 2D). Meanwhile, the D10_15 marker of the D10 gene was further used to perform PCR amplification on the genomic DNA of a RIL population (F10, 200 lines) derived from Zhenshan 97B and IRAT109. The results showed that 61 lines carried the Zhenshan 97B allele (118 bp amplicon), while 126 lines carried the IRAT109 allele (103 bp amplicon). Lines carrying the Zhenshan 97B allele exhibited a significantly higher ETNs compared to those harboring the IRAT109 allele (P = 2.78E-03) (Fig. 2E).

Haplotype analysis and development of functional markers for D27

Only three SNPs in the exon and intron of D27, derived from re-sequence data, were selected to analyze haplotype. Two haplotypes were identified in the rice natural population, with Hap 1 exhibiting significantly fewer ETNs than Hap 2 (Fig. 3A). The SNP1121755691 at exon of D27 with p value of 2.21E-07 for rice ETN. Accessions carrying the SNP1121755691-A allele (Hap 1) displayed fewer ETNs, whereas those with the SNP1121755691-T allele (Hap 2) had more ETN. Moreover, the T-to-A mutation leads to an non-synonymous amino acid substitution in the D27 protein, with serine (Ser) at position 65 replaced by threonine (Thr).

An 218 bp InDel marker (D27_218) targeting the intronic region of D27 was developed to enable marker-assisted selection (MAS) breeding in rice. Genotypic identification of the D27 gene was performed with 270 rice germplasm accessions using D27_218 marker. PCR amplicons were separated and detected by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis (Fig. 3B). The results showed that 125 accessions produced a 144 bp amplicon, while 141 accessions yielding a 362 bp amplicon. ETNs of accessions carrying the 144 bp amplicon were significantly higher than those with the 362 bp amplicon (P = 3.48E-07) (Fig. 3C). The grain yield per plant of the 125 accessions with 144 bp amplicon carriers was 11.87 g, while that of the 141 accessions with 362 bp carriers was 8.60 g. This result indicated that the grain yield per plant of 141 accessions with the 144 bp amplicon was significantly higher than that of 125 accessions with the 362 bp amplicon (P = 1.01E-16) (Fig. 3D). Therefore, it is demonstrated that the molecular marker D27_218 was suitable for MAS-based genetic improvement of both rice ETN and grain yield.

The D27_218 marker was also used to amplify genomic DNA of RIL population derived from a cross between Zhenshan 97B and IRAT109. The PCR products were separated and detected by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis. Genotyping results showed that 98 lines carried the Zhenshan 97B allele (144 bp amplicon), while 88 lines carried the IRAT109 allele (362 bp amplicon). And the lines carrying the Zhenshan 97B allele exhibited significantly higher ETNs compared to those with the IRAT109 allele (P = 4.55E-05) (Fig. 3E).

Haplotype analysis and development of functional markers for Stvb-i

14 SNPs at promoter and ORF of Stvb-i, derived from re-sequence data, were selected to analyze haplotype. Four haplotypes were identified in the rice natural population, with Hap 1 exhibiting significantly fewer ETNs than the other three haplotypes (Fig. 4A). A 61 bp InDel marker (Stvb-i_61) targeting Stvb-i were developed to enable marker-assisted selection breeding in rice. Genotypic identification of the Stvb-i gene was performed on genomic DNA from 270 rice germplasm accessions using the Stvb-i_61 marker. PCR products were separated and detected by 1% agarose gel electrophoresis (Fig. 4B). The results showed that 94 accessions produced a 180 bp amplicon, while 174 accessions yielded a 119 bp amplicon. The ETN of the 180 bp carriers was significantly higher than those of the 119 bp carriers (P = 3.50E-04) (Fig. 4C). The grain yield per plant of the 94 accessions with the 180 bp amplicon was 9.17 g, while that of the 174 accessions with the 119 bp amplicon was 10.59 g. This indicated that the grain yield per plant of accessions with the 119 bp amplicon was significantly higher than accessions with the 180 bp amplicon (P = 7.44E-4) (Fig. 4D).

Discussion

Genetic mechanisms of ETN in rice

ETN is a core determinant of ‘effective panicle number’, which is one of the three key components determining rice yield (effective panicle number, grains per panicle, and 1000- grain weight), and serves as a critical developmental stage for establishing a high-yield foundation. Most of the previous studies on ETN were based on linkage analysis using biparental populations [27–29], but only a few of these QTLs were cloned. Thus, it is essential for crop genetic improvement to elucidate the genetic and molecular mechanisms underlying tiller formation in rice and mining favorable allelic variations from natural populations.

GWAS is a powerful tool that leverages extensive variations in diverse germplasm populations [30, 31]. In this study, a panel of 270 rice accessions collected from Asia, Africa, and Americas was used. ETN in this population exhibited extensive phenotypic variation (Fig. 1A), rendering it well-suitable for GWAS-based identification QTLs /genes associated with rice ETN. A total of 12 QTLs for ETN were identified via GWAS, among which three known functional gene D10, D27, and Stvb-i were detected (Fig. 1). The associated SNP SNP0131228355 located in the promoter region of D10 (LOC_Os01g54270). D10 encodes OsCCD8, a key enzyme in strigolactone biosynthesis that inhibits tiller bud outgrowth [6, 32]. Another associated SNP, SNP1121755691, is located in an exon of D27. A T-to-A mutation at this site leads to an non-synonymous amino acid substitution, where serine at position 65 is replaced by threonine. This amino acid mutation could potentially be the key variant responsible for tiller numbe variation in rice. D27 is a core enzyme in strigolactone synthesis; this exon mutation likely contributes to natural tiller variation. Stvb-i annotated ATP binding protein, as a rice stripe virus resistance gene was reported in previous study, compared with wild-type plants, whether infected by the virus or not, silencing Stvb-i suppressesed plant growth, reduced plant height, decreased tillering, withered and yellowed leaves, curved leaf pulvini, and malformed spikes [26]. Stvb-i as a meristem guardian gene, its superior allelic variants may reduce early tiller abortion by sustaining the activity of meristematic cells, thus ultimately leading to a higher number of ETN at maturity.

In addition, the remaining associated intervals detected in this study represent novel ETN-related loci that are distinct from previously reported functional genes. Compared with previous studies, the QTL intervals identified here are narrower and more precise, which improves the accuracy of candidate gene identification and facilitates marker-assisted selection in rice breeding.

Notably, this study is the first to identify OsIAA3 as a candidate gene of ETN through GWAS, and its overexpression significantly increased effective tillers in rice. Furthermore, we developed and validated four functional markers for ETN improvement, providing practical breeding tools not reported in previous studies. Collectively, these findings advance our understanding of the genetic architecture of ETN and provide robust loci and tools for rice genetic improvement.

OsIAA3 as positive regulator controlling ETN in rice

Compared with the wild type, overexpression lines of OsIAA3 significantly increased ETN in this study (Fig. 5). Based on the current GWAS association and phenotypic evidence from overexpression plants, OsIAA3 is proposed as a candidate positive regulator of ETN. Subsequent studies will employ loss-of-function mutants and complementation lines for further genetic validation to elucidate the biological function of this gene.

In previous study, OsIAA3 has been identified as a nuclear protein that physically interacts with ARF25 and suppresses the expression level of OsARF25. Consistently, OsIAA3 RNAi plants exhibit elongated grain length, whereas osarf25 loss-of-function mutants show reduced grain length [15]. Furthermore, OsARF25 can directly bind to the promoter of OsERF142/SMOS1/NGR5 and positively modulate its transcription [15]. NGR5 integrates nitrogen and GA signaling to control tillering via repressing D14 and OsSPL14 [5]. Collectively, these findings support a preliminary regulatory model in which the OsIAA3 - ARF25 - NGR5 - D14/OsSPL14 pathway may contribute to the regulation of ETN in rice. This model links OsIAA3 to known tillering pathways and suggests a potential regulatory module for rice effective tiller number.

Development, Validation, and Breeding Utilization of Functional Markers

In this study, we developed four functional markers for rice ETN based on four functional genes (i.e., OsIAA3, OsD10, OsD27, Stvb-i) identified through GWAS mapping. To verify the co-segregation of these newly developed markers with the phenotypic traits in population, we conducted validation for each molecular marker in both natural and/or bi-parental population.

No large fragment InDels exceeding 10 bp in the promoter region of OsIAA3 across rice germplasm resources, whereas multiple SNP variations were identified at this locus. We thus developed a KASP marker based on SNP locus SNP0107248534 (G/A). KASP is a SNP-based competitive allele-specific PCR technology with flexible throughput and high accuracy, rendering it well suitable for large-scale genotyping in rice breeding programs [33]. We conducted genotypic analysis via this KASP marker on 270 rice germplasm accessions and the recombinant inbred lines derived from Zhenshan 97B × IRAT109. Genotyping results obtained with this KASP marker exhibited clear genotypic differentiation. Furthermore, genotypic-phenotypic co-segregation analysis confirmed that this marker exhibits significant co-segregation with both ETN and grain yield per plant, thus rendering it applicable for marker-assisted selective in rice breeding. Selecting of donor parents carrying the SNP0107248534-A allele can effectively improve ETN and grain yield in rice.

Additionally, D10, D27, and Stvb-i harbored large fragment InDels exceeding 10 bp in their promoter region, intronic region, and corresponding functional regions, respectively. We therfore developed InDel makers based on these larger InDels for marker assist selection applications. All three markers were significantly associated with ETN in the natural population; D10_15 and D27_218 were also validated in biparental populations and strongly correlated with grain yield. No sequences variation of Stvb-i was detected between Zhenshan 97B and IRAT 109, validation work could not be conducted in the recombinant inbred line population.

We further analyzed the haplotype frequencies of four genes (OsIAA3, D10, D27, and Stvb-i) in a GWAS panel consisting of 270 rice accessions and the improved breeding varieties (99 rice accessions ) derived from this panel. The results showed that the frequency of the superior haplotype Hap7 of OsIAA3 was higher in improved varieties than in the original GWAS panel (Figure S2A). Similarly, the superior haplotype Hap2 of D10 also exhibited an increased frequency in improved varieties relative to the GWAS population (Figure S2B). And, the superior haplotype Hap2 of D27 presented a relatively low frequency in improved varieties and GWAS panel (Figure S2C). Therefore, Hap2 of D27 could be prioritized and utilized in future rice breeding programs. The superior haplotype Hap2 of Stvb-i maintained a relatively high frequency in both the GWAS population and improved rice varieties. However, Hap2 of Stvb-i suffers from linkage drag with Hap4 of STV11 in qETN11 (Figure S3), and Hap4 of STV11 confers high susceptibility to rice stripe virus (RSV) [34]. In contrast, the allele frequencies of the other two superior haplotypes of Stvb-i (Hap3 and Hap4) increased dramatically in the improved population relative to the GWAS population (Figure S2D). Moreover, Hap3 and Hap4 of Stvb-i show an extremely low linkage level with Hap4 of STV11 (Figure S3). This pronounced increase is most likely attributable to artificial selection acting on Stvb-i Hap3 and Hap4 during the breeding process.

These markers are stable, cost-effective, and suitable for marker-assisted selection (MAS) in rice breeding. They can be used to pyramid favorable alleles for ETN and yield, but their efficiency in large-scale hybrid breeding programs requires further testing in diverse genetic backgrounds.

Conclusions

In this study, GWAS identified 12 QTLs associated with effective tiller number (ETN) in rice, including three known genes (D10, D27, Stvb-i) and the novel regulator OsIAA3. We characterized favorable haplotypes for these genes and developed four functional molecular markers that were validated in both natural and bi-parental populations. These markers are suitable for marker-assisted selection (MAS) to enhance ETN and grain yield in rice breeding.

This work advances understanding of the genetic and molecular basis of rice effective tiller number and provides valuable loci, germplasm, and tools for grain yield in rice breeding. Further cloning and functional characterization of novel QTL candidates will continue to refine the regulatory network of rice tillering.

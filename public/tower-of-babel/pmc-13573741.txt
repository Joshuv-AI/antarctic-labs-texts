Phase I Randomized Study of SCAI-005 Ophthalmic Solution: Safety, Tolerability, and Pharmacokinetics in Healthy Korean Adults
Authors: Jueun Kang, Kyungmin Park, Suein Choi, Seunghoon Han, Bo-Een Hwang, Ji Hye Lee, Mi Jang, Eun Kyoung Chung, Hyeon Cho, Youjeong Heo, Sungpil Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeTo evaluate the safety, tolerability, and pharmacokinetic (PK) profiles of SCAI-005—a topical ophthalmic solution of axitinib for neovascular age-related macular degeneration (AMD), a VEGF receptor tyrosine kinase inhibitor—following single and multiple ascending doses (SAD and MAD) in healthy Korean adults.DesignRandomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled, single- and multiple-ascending-dose phase I study.SubjectsForty-eight healthy Korean adults were randomized 3:1 (active:placebo) into six cohorts across SAD (0.04% as a single drop, 0.08% as a single drop, and 0.08% as two drops) and MAD (0.04% 1 drop three times daily (TID), 0.08% 1-drop TID, and 0.08% two-drop TID for 7 days) groups.MethodsSCAI-005 or placebo was administered topically to 1 eye per subject. Serial blood samples were collected for systemic PK analysis using noncompartmental analysis. Safety was assessed through treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs), ophthalmic examinations, vital signs, electrocardiography, and clinical laboratory tests.Main Outcome MeasuresPrimary outcomes were safety and tolerability. Secondary outcomes included systemic PK parameters (Cmax and AUClast) and dose proportionality.ResultsForty-seven of 48 subjects (97.9%) completed the study. TEAEs were reported in 7 subjects (14.6%), with no obvious dose-dependent trend in this small sample. All treatment-related TEAEs were grade 1 in severity. No clinically significant changes were observed in ophthalmic assessments. SCAI-005 was rapidly absorbed following topical administration, with day 8 postdose peak systemic concentrations (Cmax) approximately 66- to 282-fold lower than the reported steady-state Cmax for oral axitinib 5 mg twice daily. Because predose trough concentrations were below the assay limit in most subjects, formal steady state could not be confirmed; repeated-dose parameters are therefore reported as day 8 postdose descriptive PK parameters. In the dose proportionality analysis, Cmax findings were broadly consistent with dose proportionality in both SAD and MAD cohorts. AUClast slope estimates were above unity in both cohorts (1.64–1.69) with confidence intervals not including unity.ConclusionsTopical SCAI-005 was generally well tolerated in this small first-in-human study in healthy Korean adults, producing low systemic axitinib exposure and no obvious short-term safety signal. These findings will require confirmation in phase II studies enrolling the target neovascular AMD population.Financial Disclosure(s)Proprietary or commercial disclosure may be found in the Footnotes and Disclosures at the end of this article.Keywords: Age-related macular degeneration, Axitinib, Ophthalmic drug delivery, Pharmacokinetics, VEGFR inhibitor

Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is one of the leading causes of irreversible vision loss in the elderly worldwide, with the number of affected individuals projected to increase from 196 million in 2020 to 288 million in 2040.1 Wet AMD is characterized by choroidal neovascularization (CNV), with VEGF serving as a key mediator of pathologic neovascularization.2,3 Wet AMD is commonly treated with repeated intravitreal anti-VEGF therapy, including first-generation agents such as aflibercept, ranibizumab, and bevacizumab, as well as more recently approved second-generation agents such as brolucizumab and faricimab, a bispecific antibody targeting both VEGF-A and angiopoietin-2.4,5 Although clinical trials and long-term observational studies have demonstrated that anti-VEGF therapy can maintain favorable visual outcomes, durable treatment-free remission in patients with stable disease remains difficult to achieve and is typically associated with substantial recurrence or retreatment rates.6 Even with the latest second-generation agents that permit extended dosing intervals, many patients require ongoing intravitreal injections approximately every 3 to 4 months to sustain visual gains, and long-term treatment adherence remains challenging in clinical practice, with discontinuation rates of approximately 20%–40% reported in observational cohorts.5,7 Beyond the ongoing therapeutic requirements, repeated intravitreal administrations are also associated with procedure-related ocular complications, including endophthalmitis, intraocular pressure (IOP) elevation, and other injection-related adverse events.8 Furthermore, repeated intravitreal injections may be associated with physical and psychological burden, including treatment-related anxiety, which may negatively affect the patient experience.9 There is therefore an unmet need for the development of alternative therapies capable of achieving antiangiogenic efficacy through a noninvasive route of administration. The topical (eye drop) route of administration offers the advantage of noninvasive delivery; however, the barrier function of the corneal epithelium, dilution by tear fluid, and rapid drainage through the nasolacrimal duct result in the loss of most administered drug in the anterior segment, severely limiting drug delivery to posterior segment target tissues (retina and choroid).10,11

SCAI-005 was developed as a topical ophthalmic formulation designed to overcome these limitations by enhancing the intraocular penetration of axitinib through formulation optimization. Axitinib is a potent and selective inhibitor of VEGF receptor tyrosine kinases 1, 2, and 3.12 Axitinib was approved in 2012 for the treatment of renal cell carcinoma (Inlyta®, Pfizer)13 and has an extensive clinical track record. SCAI-005 represents a drug repositioning application of axitinib to the ophthalmic field as a topical formulation. Although systemic adverse events such as hypertension and gastrointestinal disorders have been reported with oral axitinib, axitinib is primarily metabolized by CYP3A4/5, a pathway relevant to systemic drug–drug interaction potential.13,14 In nonclinical ocular pharmacokinetic studies, systemic axitinib was minimally absorbed and rapidly cleared following topical administration, resulting in negligible plasma exposure and supporting the rationale that topical ocular delivery may reduce systemic exposure compared with oral administration. In nonclinical studies, SCAI-005 ophthalmic solution demonstrated significant anti-VEGF efficacy in a mouse CNV model at 0.01% twice daily (0.5 μg/5 μL/eye), and statistically significant reductions in CNV area and retinal thickness were observed in a Rhesus monkey CNV model with 0.04%, 0.08%, and 0.12% administered four times daily for 28 days. In nonclinical ocular pharmacokinetic studies, the Cmax achieved in target tissues after a single topical application was 128–287 times the IC50 of axitinib (0.077 ng/mL), and 332–616 times following repeated topical dosing, supporting the plausibility of achieving pharmacologically relevant ocular concentrations.15 With regard to toxicity, no local or systemic toxicity findings were observed in 2-week and 4-week repeat-dose topical toxicity studies in rabbits at doses up to 0.12% eight times daily (0.96 mg/animal/day), which corresponds to approximately 5.7-fold the maximum planned repeated dose in this clinical study (Cohort B3: 0.168 mg/day).

We report here the results of a randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled, single- and multiple-ascending-dose (SAD and MAD) phase I clinical study (SCAI-005-101) evaluating single and repeated topical administration (three times daily for 7 days) of SCAI-005 ophthalmic solution 0.04% and 0.08% in healthy Korean adult volunteers. The primary objectives of this study were to assess the safety and tolerability of axitinib administered via topical ophthalmic administration, and to explore the systemic pharmacokinetic (PK) profile and dose proportionality following single and multiple dosing.

Methods

Study Subjects and Design

This was a single-center, randomized, double-masked, placebo-controlled, SAD and MAD phase I clinical study conducted at Seoul St. Mary's Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea. Eligible subjects were healthy male and female adults aged 19 to 50 years at screening, with body weight of at least 55 kg for males and 50 kg for females, within ±20% of their ideal body weight [ideal body weight (kg) = (height [cm] – 100) × 0.9]. Key ophthalmic exclusion criteria included best-corrected visual acuity (Snellen) below 0.6, IOP > 21 mmHg, IOP difference between eyes > 4 mmHg, and a history of ocular disease, including keratitis, uveitis, retinitis, dry eye disease, or strabismus within 12 months prior to screening. Subjects who had undergone ophthalmic surgery within 12 months prior to screening, had worn contact lenses within 1 month prior to screening, or had clinically significant systemic diseases were also excluded.

Eligible subjects were admitted to the clinical trial center 1 day prior to investigational product (IP) administration. A total of 48 subjects were enrolled and allocated equally (n = 24 per cohort) into the SAD (cohort A) and MAD (cohort B) cohorts. Each cohort consisted of three dose groups with an active-to-placebo randomization ratio of 3:1 (6 active and 2 placebo subjects per group). The randomization schedule was generated by an independent randomization specialist using SAS® Version 9.4. After completion of the SAD cohort, an independent Safety Review Committee (SRC) reviewed the accumulated safety and pharmacokinetic data, including adverse event data, and progression to the MAD cohort was approved by unanimous agreement of all SRC members. The SRC operated independently of the sponsor and comprised medical experts not otherwise involved in the conduct of the study, but did not include a hepatology specialist. Causality assessment for adverse events, including the single serious adverse event (SAE) of acute hepatitis in the SAD cohort, was performed by the principal investigator based on serial liver function testing (aspartate aminotransferase, alanine aminotransferase, gamma-glutamyltransferase [γ-GT], total bilirubin), clinical presentation, and temporal relationship, with subsequent masked review by the SRC as part of the independent SAD safety review. The sponsor did not participate in causality assessments, did not attend SRC deliberations, and did not make individual adverse-event causality determinations. Subjects, investigators, and all trial-related personnel were masked to treatment allocation; study masking was maintained until database lock, and no subject-level unmasking occurred during the trial.

This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Seoul St. Mary's Hospital (protocol number: SCAI-005-101) and was conducted in accordance with the Korean Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All subjects provided written informed consent prior to the performance of any study-related procedures. This trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (identifier: NCT06969001).

IP and Administration

The IP consisted of SCAI-005 ophthalmic solution 0.04% (axitinib 0.4 mg/mL) and 0.08% (axitinib 0.8 mg/mL); the comparator was a matching placebo ophthalmic solution composed of the same vehicle excipients. Both products were manufactured by Optus Pharm Co., Ltd. and were presented as colorless or faintly yellow transparent solutions filled in clear plastic containers. The dosing regimen for each cohort is shown in Figure 1. In the SAD cohort, cohort A1 received SCAI-005 0.04% as a single drop administered once (0.014 mg), cohort A2 received 0.08% as a single drop once (0.028 mg), and cohort A3 received 0.08% as two drops once (5-minute interval; 0.056 mg) in 1 eye. All single-dose instillations were performed at approximately 09:00 on day 1. In the MAD cohort, the assigned formulation was administered three times daily (09:00, 15:00, and 21:00) from day 1 through day 7, followed by a single morning dose on day 8 for intensive PK sampling. Cohort B1 received 0.04% 1 drop per administration, cohort B2 received 0.08% 1 drop per administration, and cohort B3 received 0.08% two drops per administration with a 5-minute interval between drops. The total daily axitinib doses from day 1 through day 7 were 0.042 mg/day, 0.084 mg/day, and 0.168 mg/day, respectively; the corresponding day 8 single-dose amounts were 0.014 mg, 0.028 mg, and 0.056 mg, respectively. All IPs were administered directly to the right eye of each subject by the principal investigator or subinvestigator. For instillation, subjects tilted their heads back with eyes looking upward; the lower eyelid was gently pulled down and the drop instilled into the conjunctival sac. Subjects were instructed to keep their eyes closed for 30 s after instillation and then blinked 2–3 times to distribute the medication evenly.

Bioanalytical Methods

Plasma axitinib concentrations were measured by DT&CRO Co., Ltd. using a validated bioanalytical method. Method validation was performed in accordance with relevant regulatory guidelines and included evaluation of accuracy, precision, selectivity, specificity, recovery, and stability. The lower limit of quantification (LLOQ) was 50 pg/mL. Concentrations below the LLOQ prior to Tmax were set to 0; concentrations below the LLOQ after Tmax were excluded from the analysis. Subjects in whom all measured values were below the LLOQ or for whom samples were missing were excluded from the pharmacokinetic analysis set.

Pharmacokinetic Assessment

Blood samples were collected into ethylenediaminetetraacetic acid dipotassium salt tubes for plasma separation (6 mL per collection). On intensive pharmacokinetic sampling days (cohort A: day 1; cohort B: day 8), an indwelling intravenous catheter was placed to facilitate serial blood sampling. For the SAD cohort, blood was collected on day 1 at predose (0 h) and at 0.167, 0.33, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 2, 4, 8, and 12 h postdose, for a total of 10 collections (approximately 60 mL total). For the MAD cohort, predose trough samples were collected on days 1, 3, 5, and 8, and intensive PK sampling was performed on day 8 after the final dose at the same postdose time points as in the SAD cohort, for a total of 13 collections (approximately 78 mL total). Because predose trough concentrations in most subjects were expected to be below the LLOQ given axitinib's short half-life (t1/2), and pharmacokinetic time-invariance during repeated dosing has not been formally established, day 8 postdose parameters are reported as descriptive characterizations of repeated-dose pharmacokinetics rather than as formally confirmed steady-state parameters. The steady-state suffix (ss) was retained only to reflect the prespecified repeated-dose PK terminology, and all “ss”-labeled parameters throughout this manuscript should be interpreted as day 8 postdose descriptive PK parameters, not as formally confirmed steady-state values. All collected plasma samples were stored at –70 ± 10°C until analysis. Actual blood collection times, rather than nominal times, were used for pharmacokinetic parameter estimation.

We estimated pharmacokinetic parameters using noncompartmental analysis with the NonCompart package in R software. Cmax and Tmax were derived directly from observed data. The area under the concentration–time curve to the last quantifiable concentration (AUClast) was calculated using the linear trapezoidal method. The terminal elimination rate constant (λz) was determined by log-linear regression, and the elimination t1/2 was calculated as ln2/λz. In the SAD cohort, apparent clearance (CL/F) and apparent volume of distribution (Vz/F) were additionally estimated. For the MAD cohort, Cmax,ss, Cmin,ss, Tmax,ss, AUClast,ss, t1/2,ss, CLss/F, and Vz,ss/F were summarized as descriptive day 8 postdose parameters. We assessed dose proportionality using a power model.16 Log-transformed pharmacokinetic parameters (Cmax and AUClast) were regressed against log-transformed dose using ordinary least-squares linear regression according to the model: ln(PK parameter) = α + β1 · ln(dose). Under this model, a slope (β1) of exactly 1 indicates perfect dose proportionality. The slope estimate (β1) and its 95% confidence interval (CI) were used as the primary basis for interpretation; a CI including unity was taken as consistent with dose proportionality, while a CI entirely above or below unity indicated more-than-dose-proportional or less-than-dose-proportional behavior, respectively. The clinical relevance of the dose-proportionality assessment was interpreted based on how close β1 was to unity and the width of the 95% CI, rather than on the statistical significance of the slope estimate. R2 was reported to describe the goodness of fit of the log-log regression. The P value for β1 was obtained from the test of β1 = 0 and was reported to indicate the statistical significance of the log-linear dose–exposure association, but was not used as the primary criterion for dose–proportionality assessment.

Safety Assessment

Safety was evaluated in all subjects who received at least 1 instillation of the IP (safety set, SS). Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs) were collected from the time of first administration to study completion and were coded using Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities Version 28.0 or higher. Severity was graded according to National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events Version 5.0, and the investigator assessed causality as “related” or “not related.” Systemic safety was assessed by vital signs (systolic/diastolic blood pressure, pulse rate, and body temperature, measured after at least 5 min of rest), 12-lead electrocardiography, and clinical laboratory tests (complete blood count, serum chemistry, and urinalysis). Ophthalmic safety was assessed by best-corrected visual acuity (Snellen chart), IOP (noncontact tonometry), slit-lamp examination, and fundus examination. Fundus examinations were performed at 4 h postdose on day 1 and at the day 4 follow-up visit for the SAD cohort, and at 4 h postdose on days 5 and 8 and at the day 15 follow-up visit for the MAD cohort. For the SAD cohort, ECG and clinical laboratory tests were performed at 12 h postdose on day 1, and at the day 4 follow-up visit. For the MAD cohort, ECG and clinical laboratory tests were performed at 12 h postdose on day 8 and at the day 15 follow-up visit.

Statistical Analysis

This was an exploratory phase I study; sample size was not determined based on statistical power. We defined analysis sets as follows: demographics set: all subjects assigned a registration number; pharmacokinetics set (PS): subjects who completed pharmacokinetic blood sampling at all scheduled time points; SS: all subjects who received at least one instillation of the IP. We summarized demographic characteristics and pharmacokinetic parameters using descriptive statistics (mean, standard deviation, median, minimum, and maximum). We used Fisher exact test to compare TEAE incidence rates between the active and placebo groups. Noncompartmental pharmacokinetic analyses and statistical analyses were performed independently by the investigator-site team at Seoul St. Mary's Hospital, The Catholic University of Korea, according to the prespecified statistical analysis plan. The sponsor did not perform the pharmacokinetic or statistical analyses; the sponsor reviewed the statistical analysis plan, clinical study report, and manuscript for factual accuracy but did not modify or influence the analysis results, safety adjudications, or the interpretation of the findings.

Results

Study Subjects

Between May 22 and September 4, 2025, a total of 91 subjects were screened, of whom 48 (52.7%) were enrolled into 1 of 6 cohorts and randomized to receive either active treatment or placebo within each cohort (Fig. 2). Of the 43 subjects not enrolled, 30 did not meet the inclusion/exclusion criteria (SAD: n = 12, MAD: n = 18), 4 withdrew consent (SAD: n = 1, MAD: n = 3), and 9 were excluded for other reasons (SAD: n = 5, MAD: n = 4). All 48 enrolled subjects received at least 1 IP administration and were included in the SS. Forty-seven subjects (97.9%) completed the study. One subject (cohort B1, subject B1030) withdrew due to a vasovagal response following intravenous catheter insertion on day 8 and was excluded from the PS as the planned pharmacokinetic sampling was not completed. The PS comprised 35 subjects: 18 subjects in the SAD cohort (6 per dose group) and 17 in the MAD cohort (cohort B1: n = 5; cohorts B2 and B3: n = 6 each). The 12 placebo-treated subjects were excluded from the pharmacokinetic analysis.

Pharmacokinetics

SAD Pharmacokinetics

Following a single topical administration of SCAI-005, axitinib was rapidly absorbed into the systemic circulation in all dose groups (Table 2, Fig. 3). In the 0.04% group (total axitinib dose: 0.014 mg), the mean Cmax was 87.4 ± 18.3 pg/mL (median: 93.1 pg/mL), and the mean Tmax was 0.708 h (median: 0.417 h), indicating that peak plasma concentrations were reached within approximately 25–42 min after instillation. The mean AUClast was 115 ± 42.1 h pg/mL. In the 0.08% group (0.028 mg), Cmax increased to 149 ± 53.7 pg/mL (median: 151 pg/mL) with a Tmax similar to the 0.04% group (mean: 0.722 h, median: 0.750 h). AUClast was 438 ± 180 h pg/mL, representing approximately a 3.8-fold increase relative to the 0.04% group. In the 0.08% two-drop group (0.056 mg), Cmax was 295 ± 46.2 pg/mL (median: 306 pg/mL) with a somewhat delayed Tmax (mean: 1.21 h, median: 0.875 h). AUClast was 1180 ± 446 h pg/mL, representing the highest systemic exposure within the SAD cohort.

The elimination t1/2 ranged from approximately 5 to 6 h across all SAD dose groups (0.04%: 5.25 ± 4.75 h; 0.08%: 6.10 ± 2.45 h; 0.08% two drops: 5.10 ± 3.10 h); however, because plasma concentrations fell below the LLOQ at later sampling time points, t1/2 could only be estimated in 4 of 6 subjects (66.7%) in each of the three SAD dose groups, limiting interpretation of the terminal elimination phase. MAD Pharmacokinetics

In the MAD cohort, predose trough samples were collected on days 1, 3, 5, and 8 before the morning dose. Most predose trough concentrations were below the LLOQ (50 pg/mL), except in the highest-dose group (0.08% two drops TID). In the 0.08% two-drop TID group, the predose trough concentration on day 8 was 56.2 ± 63.3 pg/mL (median: 43.1 [0, 131] pg/mL). Because most predose trough concentrations were below the LLOQ, steady state could not be formally confirmed; the intensive day 8 concentration-time data were therefore used to summarize repeated-dose parameters as descriptive day 8 postdose PK characterizations (Table 2, Fig. 3).

The day 8 postdose PK profile showed an absorption pattern generally consistent with that observed after single dosing. In the 0.04% TID group, Cmax,ss was 98.5 ± 7.10 pg/mL (median: 103 pg/mL), Tmax,ss was 0.817 h (median: 1.00 h), and AUClast,ss was 193 ± 102 h pg/mL. In the 0.08% TID group, Cmax,ss was 176 ± 82.6 pg/mL (median: 172 pg/mL) and AUClast,ss was 442 ± 258 h pg/mL. The 0.08% two-drop TID group showed the highest systemic exposure, with Cmax,ss of 422 ± 165 pg/mL (median: 410 pg/mL), Tmax,ss of 2.17 h (median: 2.00 h), and AUClast,ss of 2110 ± 1440 h pg/mL, though this group also exhibited the highest intersubject variability.

Terminal elimination half-life at steady state could only be estimated in n = 1, n = 2, and n = 1 subjects in the 0.04% TID (2.37 h), 0.08% TID (2.49 ± 0.0135 h), and 0.08% two-drop TID (2.83 h) groups, respectively (missing rates: 80.0%, 66.7%, and 83.3%). These values are of limited representativeness, preclude statistical interpretation, and should not be compared with single-dose values. AUCinf,ss, CLss/F, and Vz,ss/F were similarly estimable in only a very small number of subjects and carry the same interpretive limitations. CLss/F was 37.3 L/h, 34.3 ± 4.07 L/h, and 13.0 L/h in the 0.04% TID, 0.08% TID, and 0.08% two-drop TID groups, respectively. Accordingly, these parameters are summarized as descriptive day 8 postdose estimates.

Dose–Proportionality Assessment

Dose proportionality was assessed using a power model based on log-log linear regression (Table 3, Fig 4). Interpretation was based on the proximity of the slope estimate (β1) to unity and the width of the 95% CI; P values from Table 3 test the significance of the log-linear dose–exposure association (β1 ≠ 0) rather than dose proportionality (β1 = 1) and are not used as the primary criterion. In the SAD cohort, the slope estimate for Cmax (β1) was 0.88 (95% CI: 0.65–1.12; R2 = 0.799), which was broadly consistent with dose proportionality, though the point estimate below unity is also compatible with a modestly less-than-dose-proportional increase given the small sample size.

Safety and Tolerability

Safety was evaluated in all 48 subjects who received at least 1 IP administration. A total of 10 TEAEs were reported in 7 subjects (14.6%; Table 4). Treatment-emergent adverse events occurred in 2 of 18 SAD active-treated subjects (11.1%), 1 of 6 SAD placebo-treated subjects (16.7%), 3 of 18 MAD active-treated subjects (16.7%), and 1 of 6 MAD placebo-treated subjects (16.7%). No statistically significant difference was observed between the active and placebo groups (Fisher exact test); however, given the small cohort sizes, this finding should be interpreted cautiously. No obvious dose-dependent trend in TEAE incidence was observed.

Concomitant medications were used by 4 of 48 subjects (8.3%) during the study period, including ursodeoxycholic acid, milk thistle extract, acetaminophen, and normal saline infusion. These concomitant medications were not considered to have a clinically meaningful effect on the pharmacokinetics of SCAI-005. A turmeric-containing dietary supplement, subsequently identified through the SAE follow-up interview, had not been captured in the prespecified concomitant medication listing; this post hoc finding is described separately below.

Treatment-related TEAEs were reported in two subjects in the MAD cohort: 1 subject receiving SCAI-005 and 1 subject receiving placebo. One subject in the SCAI-005 0.04% TID group experienced mild ocular pruritus in the right eye lasting approximately 40 min before the second instillation; the event resolved spontaneously without treatment and was assessed as related to SCAI-005. One subject in the MAD placebo group developed mild pyrexia (body temperature 38.2°C) on day 3, which resolved the following morning after administration of acetaminophen 650 mg. Because no clear etiology for the fever was identified, the event was assessed as related to the assigned study treatment.

One SAE was reported in the SAD cohort. One subject in the SCAI-005 0.04% group presented with findings consistent with acute hepatitis at the day 4 follow-up visit (aspartate aminotransferase 2826 U/L; alanine aminotransferase 3072 U/L; total bilirubin 2.26 mg/dL; γ-GT 323 U/L). Upon detailed interview, it was confirmed that the subject had been taking a turmeric-containing dietary supplement daily since January 2025, and that γ-GT levels had been progressively rising from the time of screening. Considering the low expected systemic exposure via the topical route, the normal liver function test results at 12 h postdose, the increasing γ-GT trend from screening, and the subject's prior use of a turmeric-containing dietary supplement, the investigator assessed the SAE as not related to the IP, with the turmeric-containing dietary supplement considered a plausible alternative explanation, supported by prior reports of turmeric/curcumin-associated liver injury.17 However, because topical dosing resulted in measurable systemic axitinib exposure, an idiosyncratic reaction or supplement–drug interaction cannot be fully excluded. The SAE and related adverse event data were reviewed by the SRC. The subject did not attend the additional follow-up visit but reported via text message that liver enzyme levels were improving; status at study closure was documented as recovering.

One subject in the SCAI-005 0.04% TID group developed moderate vasovagal presyncope 4 min after intravenous catheter insertion on day 8, with blood pressure dropping to 75/53 mmHg and heart rate to 42 beats per minute. The subject recovered within approximately 90 min following normal saline infusion and leg elevation. Given the temporal association with catheter insertion, the event was assessed as not related to the IP; however, in the interest of subject safety, study discontinuation was decided.

Other mild TEAEs assessed as not related to the IP included mild glucosuria in 1 SAD placebo subject, pyuria in 1 SAD SCAI-005 0.08% subject, and decreased appetite with transient laboratory abnormalities (hypoglycemia, urine ketone body present, and blood bilirubin increased) in 1 MAD SCAI-005 0.08% two drop TID subject. These events were attributed to fasting, dietary restriction, or other non–drug-related factors during the inpatient period. All TEAEs had resolved or were resolving prior to study completion. No deaths occurred. No clinically significant findings related to the IP were identified in vital signs, 12-lead ECG, clinical laboratory tests (complete blood count, serum chemistry, and urinalysis), or ophthalmic assessments (visual acuity, IOP, slit-lamp examination, and fundus examination) at any dose level or time point.

Discussion

In this first-in-human phase I study, SCAI-005 ophthalmic solution was generally well tolerated over the short observation period in healthy Korean adults. The study was small, with 6 active-treated subjects per dose group and 2 placebo subjects per cohort, and sample size was not determined based on statistical power; therefore, the absence of statistically significant TEAE differences or a dose-dependent trend should not be interpreted as evidence of definitive safety, but rather as an absence of an obvious short-term safety signal in a small healthy-volunteer sample. All identified treatment-related TEAEs were limited to grade 1 severity, resolved spontaneously or normalized rapidly after symptomatic treatment, and did not affect continued study participation.

The single SAE of acute hepatitis in the SAD cohort was assessed by the investigator as not related to the IP, based on the low expected systemic exposure via the topical route, normal liver function test results at 12 h postdose, the increasing γ-GT trend from screening, and the subject's prior prolonged use of a turmeric-containing dietary supplement. Nevertheless, turmeric-containing dietary supplement use should be considered a plausible alternative explanation rather than a definitive cause, and an idiosyncratic reaction or supplement–drug interaction cannot be fully excluded. The single discontinuation in the MAD cohort was also attributed to a vasovagal response secondary to catheter insertion, with no relationship to the IP. Ophthalmic assessments showed no clinically significant short-term changes; however, the interpretation of these findings is limited by the short observation period and the healthy-volunteer population studied.

The low systemic exposure observed in this study is particularly noteworthy from a safety perspective, given that oral administration of axitinib is known to cause systemic adverse effects characteristic of VEGF receptor inhibitors, including hypertension and proteinuria.18 In the present study, day 8 postdose peak systemic concentrations (labeled Cmax,ss per prespecified terminology, but not formally confirmed steady-state values) following repeated topical administration ranged from 98.5 to 422 pg/mL in the MAD cohort, markedly lower than the reported geometric mean steady-state Cmax of 27.8 ng/mL (27 800 pg/mL) in patients with advanced renal cell carcinoma receiving oral axitinib 5 mg twice daily in the fed state,13 corresponding to an approximately 66- to 282-fold lower systemic peak exposure. To further characterize the dose–exposure relationship between routes, an indirect dose-normalized comparison was performed against the intravenous/oral axitinib crossover reference reported by Chen et al, 2013 (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://www.ophthalmologyscience.org).14 Dose-normalized Cmax after topical instillation (5.27–7.54 ng/mL/mg) was comparable to that of oral axitinib form IV (6.40 ng/mL/mg), whereas dose-normalized AUC was generally lower than the oral reference at low topical doses and approached or exceeded it only at the highest MAD dose. Anchoring to the oral form IV reference (Foral = 58%), the apparent absolute ocular bioavailability (Focular) was estimated at 16%–42% in the SAD cohort and 27%–75% in the MAD cohort, with a sensitivity analysis using the 1 mg intravenous reference yielding estimates within ±3 percentage points. These estimates are necessarily indirect because the comparison relies on cross-study reference data and differences in formulation, dosing regimen, and population; nevertheless, they provide a quantitative framework for benchmarking topical systemic exposure against the established oral route (Supplementary Table S1). Notably, despite this markedly lower overall exposure, systemic absorption after topical instillation was rapid, with mean Tmax of 0.708–1.21 h in the SAD cohort and 0.817–2.17 h in the MAD cohort, indicating prompt systemic entry of axitinib following ocular administration.

With respect to the dose–exposure relationship, Cmax findings were broadly consistent with dose proportionality in both the SAD (β1 = 0.88; 95% CI: 0.65–1.12) and MAD (β1 = 1.02; 95% CI: 0.64–1.39) cohorts, as both CIs included unity. However, interpretation remains exploratory: the point estimate below unity in the SAD cohort and the wide CI in the MAD cohort indicate compatibility with several dose–exposure patterns, precluding firm conclusions given the small sample size. In contrast, AUClast and AUClast,ss point estimates were above unity in both the SAD (β1 = 1.69; 95% CI: 1.30–2.07) and MAD (β1 = 1.64; 95% CI: 1.01–2.26) cohorts, with CIs not including unity, although the MAD lower bound was close to unity and interpretation is limited by the small sample size.

The mechanistic basis for the more-than-dose-proportional AUC–dose relationship cannot be fully elucidated from the current study data alone; however, the dose-dependent decrease in apparent clearance (CL/F) observed within the same cohort (0.04%: 37.1 L/h → 0.08%: 30.0 L/h → 0.08% two drops: 24.4 L/h) may partially explain this finding. Following topical ophthalmic instillation, the majority of the administered dose is lost through precorneal clearance mechanisms, including nasolacrimal drainage, tear turnover, and reflex blinking. Importantly, the two lower-dose groups (0.04% and 0.08%, 1 drop each) constitute a one-drop concentration escalation, whereas the highest-dose group (0.08%, two drops) represents a distinct two-drop administration regimen. Compared with one-drop administration, the two-drop regimen may alter ocular surface retention, overflow, reflex blinking, tear film dilution, and nasolacrimal drainage. The one-drop concentration escalation and the two-drop regimen should therefore be considered potentially distinct exposure conditions rather than a simple dose–proportional sequence, and the observed more-than-dose-proportional AUC increase should be interpreted with this caveat in mind. Because CL/F is an apparent parameter that incorporates unknown bioavailability, the observed decrease in CL/F cannot distinguish reduced apparent clearance from increased systemic absorption or altered ocular retention. Without intraocular axitinib concentrations or direct measurement of ocular bioavailability, it is not possible to determine whether the more-than-dose-proportional systemic AUC reflects improved posterior-segment delivery, increased systemic absorption through drainage pathways, or both. Importantly, greater systemic exposure does not necessarily imply greater therapeutic exposure at the retinal or choroidal target tissues, as systemic pharmacokinetic measurements cannot substitute for direct assessment of intraocular drug concentration and target–tissue engagement. Saturation of precorneal loss pathways therefore remains a hypothesis that requires confirmation in future studies incorporating intraocular pharmacokinetic assessment and pharmacodynamic biomarkers. Further characterization of these nonlinear pharmacokinetic properties, including quantitative evaluation of ocular bioavailability and the exposure–response relationship, will be pursued in subsequent phase II clinical trials.

Beyond these nonlinear dose–exposure characteristics, the pharmacokinetic analysis was further constrained by analytical sensitivity. The rapid elimination and limited systemic exposure resulted in a substantial proportion of subjects with plasma concentrations falling below the LLOQ at later sampling time points, such that the terminal elimination t1/2 could only be estimated in 17%–33% of subjects across the MAD cohort, limiting precise characterization of the terminal elimination phase. In addition, high intersubject variability—particularly pronounced in the 0.08% two-drop TID group—likely reflects the inherent sensitivity of topical ophthalmic pharmacokinetics to individual physiological factors, including instillation technique, tear production, and blink frequency.

This study has several limitations. First, only healthy Korean adults aged 19 to 50 years were enrolled, whereas patients with neovascular AMD are typically substantially older. In older patients with neovascular AMD, age-related changes in ocular surface physiology, tear production, corneal and conjunctival permeability, blink dynamics, and choroidal vascularity, as well as ocular and systemic comorbidities and concomitant medications, may meaningfully alter both local (intraocular) and systemic exposure of topically administered SCAI-005. These factors were not evaluated in the present healthy-volunteer study and should be systematically assessed in future trials enrolling the target neovascular AMD population. Second, because healthy subjects were studied, it was not possible to evaluate pharmacodynamic biomarkers such as CNV area, macular thickness on OCT, or intraocular axitinib concentrations; the relationship between local drug exposure and clinical pharmacological effects remains a key scientific question to be addressed in subsequent clinical trials. Third, the small sample size of 8 subjects per cohort limits the precision of pharmacokinetic parameter estimation and the ability to detect low-frequency adverse events. Fourth, the study was conducted exclusively in a Korean population; the pharmacokinetic profiles and safety characteristics in other racial and ethnic groups require separate evaluation. Fifth, formal steady state could not be confirmed because predose trough concentrations were below the LLOQ in most subjects (all subjects in the 0.04% TID and 0.08% TID groups, and 3 of 6 subjects in the 0.08% two-drop TID group). In addition, the MAD cohort was designed with intensive pharmacokinetic sampling only on day 8, and day 1 PK sampling after the first dose was not performed; consequently, a formal within-subject accumulation ratio could not be calculated. The degree of drug accumulation with repeated topical dosing therefore remains to be characterized in future studies with dedicated day 1 and steady-state intensive sampling. Sixth, drug–drug interactions were not evaluated in the present healthy-volunteer study. Patients with neovascular AMD frequently use concomitant topical antiglaucoma eye drops, which may alter precorneal residence and ocular absorption of topically applied drugs, and axitinib is a CYP3A4/5 substrate susceptible to systemic interactions with strong inhibitors or inducers of this pathway.11,19 The potential impact of these concomitant medications on the safety and pharmacokinetic profile of SCAI-005 remains to be evaluated.

In conclusion, topical SCAI-005 was generally well tolerated in this first-in-human phase I study in healthy Korean adults, producing systemic axitinib exposures markedly lower than those reported with oral axitinib 5 mg twice daily and no obvious short-term safety signal. These conclusions are restricted to short-term systemic pharmacokinetics and preliminary tolerability in a healthy-volunteer population and are further constrained by the small sample size, short follow-up duration, unconfirmed steady state, and the absence of intraocular axitinib concentration and pharmacodynamic data. The observed more-than-dose-proportional increase in AUClast, together with the anticipated differences between healthy volunteers and older patients with neovascular AMD, will require systematic evaluation in adequately designed phase II studies in patients with neovascular AMD before any inference can be made regarding clinical utility. If efficacy, ocular exposure, durability, and safety are confirmed in patients with neovascular AMD, SCAI-005 may offer a noninvasive therapeutic strategy.

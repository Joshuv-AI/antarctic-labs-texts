Note on the positivity of the real part of the log-derivative of the Riemann ξ \xi -function near the critical line
\name Andrius Grigutis, Lukas Turčinskas

Abstract
In this work, we investigate the positivity of the real part of the log-derivative of the Riemann ξ \xi -function in the region 1 / 2 + 1 / log ⁡ t < σ < 1 1/2+1/\sqrt{\log t}<\sigma<1 , where t t is sufficiently large. We provide an explicit lower bound for ℜ ​ ∑ ρ 1 / ( s − ρ ) \mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho}1/(s-\rho) , where the summation runs over the zeta-zeros on the critical line. We also consider hypothetical cases of positivity of the log-derivative of the Riemann ξ \xi -function in the provided region, assuming that there are non-trivial zeta-zeros off the critical line.

1
Introduction and main result

Let
s=\sigma+it
be the complex variable. The Riemann
\xi
-function is given as

\xi(s)=\frac{1}{2}s(s-1)\pi^{-s/2}\Gamma(s/2)\zeta(s),

where
\zeta(s)
is the Riemann
\zeta
-function. Both Riemann
\zeta
and
\xi
functions have the same zeros in the strip
0<\sigma<1
, called the critical strip, and the Riemann hypothesis (RH) states that these zeros all lie on the line
1/2+it,\,t\in\mathbb{R}
, called the critical line. These days, it is unknown if the RH is true. RH is numerically confirmed up to
t\leqslant 3\times 10^{12}
, see Platt and Trudgian
[
13
]
.
The function
\xi(s)
satisfies functional equation
\xi(s)=\xi(1-s)
and for its conjugate it is true that
\overline{\xi(s)}=\xi(\overline{s})
. Therefore if
s
is a zero of
\xi(s)
then so are the numbers
\overline{s}
,
1-s
, and
1-\overline{s}
.

Let
\rho=\beta+i\gamma
be non-trivial zero of
\zeta(s)
. Let
N(T)
denote the number of zeta-zeros in the rectangle
0<\sigma<1
,
0<t<T
, and
N_{1/2}(T)
denote the number of zeta-zeros on the critical line
1/2+it
,
0<t<T
. If we denote

\displaystyle c\coloneqq\liminf_{T\to\infty}\frac{N_{1/2}(T)}{N(T)},

(1)

then it follows that

cN(T)\leqslant N_{1/2}(T)\leqslant N(T),

(2)

for all
T\geqslant 0
.
In the middle of the last century, it was proved (see Selberg
[
16
]
) that
c>0
. From there, this estimate was improved multiple times, see for example Levinson
[
9
]
(
c>1/3
), Conrey
[
2
]
(
c>2/5
), Pratt, Robles, Zaharescu, and Zeindler
[
15
]
(
c>5/12
).

The function
\xi(s)
can be expanded as an infinite product over
\rho
(see, for example, Edwards
[
3
]
)

\xi(s)=\xi(0)\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right)=\frac{1}{2}\prod_{\rho}\left(1-\frac{s}{\rho}\right),

(3)

where the product in (
3
) is taken in an order that pairs each root
\rho
of
\xi(s)
with the corresponding one
1-\rho
. From (
3
) it is easy to see that for
s
such that
\xi(s)\neq 0
the logarithmic derivative of
\xi(s)
is

\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(s)=\sum_{\rho}\frac{1}{s-\rho},

(4)

where the order of summation in (
4
) is understood the same way as the order of product in (
3
).

It is known that (see Hinkkanen
[
7
]
)

\mathfrak{R}\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(s)>0,\quad\text{when}\;\;\mathfrak{R}(s)>1,

and RH is equivalent to

\mathfrak{R}\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(s)>0,\quad\text{when}\;\;\mathfrak{R}(s)>\frac{1}{2}.

Lagarias
[
8
]
proved that

\inf\left\{\mathfrak{R}\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(s):-\infty<t<\infty\right\}=\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(\sigma),

(5)

for
\sigma>10
and Garunkštis
[
4
]
later improved (
5
) for
\sigma>a
, where
\sigma>a
is zero-free region of
\zeta(s)
.
The following facts are also known about Riemann
\xi
-function.

Theorem 1
.

(Sondow, Dumitrescu
[
17
]
) The following statements are equivalent.

I.

If
t
is any fixed real number then
\absolutevalue{\xi(\sigma+it)}
is increasing for
1/2<\sigma<\infty
.

II.

If
t
is any fixed real number then
\absolutevalue{\xi(\sigma+it)}
is decreasing for
-\infty<\sigma<1/2
.

III.

The Riemann hypothesis is true.

Theorem 2
.

(Sondow, Dumitrescu
[
17
]
) The
\xi
-function is increasing in modulus along every horizontal half-line lying in any open right half-plane that contains no
\xi
zeros. Similarly, the modulus decreases on each horizontal half-line in any zero-free, open left half-plane.

Theorem 3
.

(Matiyasevich, Saidak, Zvengrowski
[
10
]
)
Let
\sigma_{0}
be greater than or equal to the real part of any zero of
\xi
. Then
\absolutevalue{\xi(s)}
is strictly increasing with respect to
\sigma
in the half-plane
\sigma>\sigma_{0}
.

In light of these results on
\absolutevalue{\xi(s)}
behavior and the fact that
\xi(s)
is holomorphic in the region
0<\sigma<1
we can use the following lemma.

Lemma 1
.

(a) Let
f
be holomorphic in an open domain
D
and not identically zero. Let us also suppose
\mathfrak{R}f^{\prime}/f(s)<0
for all
s\in D
such that
f(s)\neq 0
. Then
|f(s)|
is strictly decreasing with respect to
\sigma
in
D
, i.e., for each
s_{0}\in D
there exists a
\delta>0
such that
|f(s)|
is strictly monotonically decreasing with respect to
\sigma
on the horizontal interval from
s_{0}-\delta
to
s_{0}+\delta
.

(b) Conversely, if
|f(s)|
is decreasing with respect to
\sigma
in
D
, then
\mathfrak{R}f^{\prime}/f(s)\leqslant 0
for all
s\in D
such that
f(s)\neq 0
.

Proof.

See Matiyasevich, Saidak, Zvengrowski
[
10
]
.
∎

Remark 4
.

Analogous results hold for monotone increasing
\absolutevalue{f(s)}
and
\mathfrak{R}f^{\prime}/f(s)>0
.

Applying Lemma
1
to
\xi(s)
we see that
\mathfrak{R}\xi^{\prime}/\xi(s)>0
with
\sigma>\tilde{\beta}
, where
\sigma>\tilde{\beta}
is zero-free region of
\zeta(s)
. Besides the mentioned results, the positivity of
\mathfrak{R}\xi^{\prime}/\xi(s)
in the strip
1/2<\sigma<1
was studied in
[
5
]
. An explicit lower and upper bounds of
\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho}1/(s-\rho)
, by letting the summation run over the zeta-zeros on the critical line, were given there in the rectangle
1/2<\sigma<1
and
t
greater than some sufficiently large value. More precisely, the following Theorem was proven.

Theorem 5
.

(Goldštein and A.G.
[
5
]
) Let
1/2<\sigma<1
and
0<c\leqslant 1
be such that
c\leqslant N_{1/2}(T)/N(T)
for all
T\geqslant\gamma_{1}=14.134725\ldots
, where
\gamma_{1}
is the lowest non-trivial zeta-zero
\zeta(1/2+i\gamma_{1})=0
. Let

\begin{split}A(t)&=0.12\log{\frac{t}{2\pi}}-2.32\log{\log{t}}-18.432-\varepsilon_{1}(t),\\
B(t)&=0.49\log{\frac{t}{2\pi}}+0.58\log{\log{t}}-4.603+\varepsilon_{2}(t),\end{split}

where
\varepsilon_{1}(t)
and
\varepsilon_{2}(t)
are known explicit
t
functions both vanishing as
t^{-1}\log{t}
,
t\to\infty
,
[
5
, eqs. (15), (16)]
.
Then

\displaystyle 0<c\left(\sigma-\frac{1}{2}\right)A(t)<

\displaystyle\;\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho=1/2+i\gamma}\frac{1}{s-\rho},\quad t>1.984\times 10^{114},

(6)

\displaystyle\;\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho=1/2+i\gamma}\frac{1}{s-\rho}<\frac{B(t)}{\sigma-1/2},\quad t>14.635.

(7)

The lower bound (
6
) allows asking the following question: can

\displaystyle\mathfrak{R}\frac{\xi^{\prime}}{\xi}(s)=\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho=1/2+i\gamma}\frac{1}{s-\rho}+\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\tilde{\rho}=\tilde{\beta}+i\tilde{\gamma}}\frac{1}{s-\tilde{\rho}}=:\Sigma_{1}+\Sigma_{2}

be positive in the critical strip if
\Sigma_{2}
exists? Here
\tilde{\rho}
denotes the hypothetical zeta-zero off the critical line. Such types of questions were also considered in
[
5
]
.

It is easy to see that the lower bound in (
6
) tends to zero as
\sigma\to 1/2
. Thus, the goal of this paper is to improve the result (
6
), obtaining a lower bound which grows infinitely when
\sigma\to 1/2
. This requires the step off of the size
1/\sqrt{\log t},\,t\to\infty
from the critical line
1/2+it,\,t\in\mathbb{R}
. More precisely, the main result of this article consists of the following theorem.

Theorem 6
.

Let
1/2+1/\sqrt{\log t}<\sigma<1
and
0<c\leqslant 1
be such that
c\leqslant N_{1/2}(T)/N(T)
for all
T\geqslant\gamma_{1}=14.134725\ldots
, where
\gamma_{1}
is the lowest non-trivial zeta-zero
\zeta(1/2+i\gamma_{1})=0
. Then

0<\frac{(0.28-\varepsilon(t))c}{\sigma-1/2}<\mathfrak{R}\sum_{\rho=1/2+i\gamma}\frac{1}{s-\rho},\quad t>3.11\times 10^{10},

(8)

where
\varepsilon(t)
is the known function, vanishing as
\log\log t/\log t
,
t\to\infty
, see (
11
).

We prove Theorem
6
in Section
3
. In Figure
1
, we depict the area given by the simplified version of the inequality (
8
). That is, we set
c=1
,
\varepsilon(t)=0
, and replace an infinite sum over zeros with the finite sum. Then, in the
\sigma\times t
plane, we plot the area of complex numbers
s=\sigma+it
that satisfy the inequality

\frac{0.28}{\sigma-1/2}<\mathfrak{R}\sum_{k=0}^{9}\frac{1}{s-\rho_{j+k}},

where
\rho=\rho_{j+k}
denote the
j+k
-th zeta-zero on the critical line; here
j=10^{12}+1
. The imaginary parts of
\rho_{j+k}
are taken from the Odlyzko database
[
12
]
.

Lemma 4
.

Let

\displaystyle a:=1+\frac{1}{2}W_{0}\left(-\frac{2}{e^{2}}\right)\approx 0.7968,

where
W_{0}(-2e^{-2})
is a root
\neq-2
of Lambert W function
-2e^{-2}=we^{w}
.
Then

\log(1-x)>-2x,\,0<x<a.

Proof.

Let
f(x)=\log(1-x)+2x
. One may check that
f(0)=f(a)=0

\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=2+\frac{1}{x-1}>0,\,x<1/2,

\displaystyle f^{\prime\prime}(x)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{(x-1)^{2}}<0,\,x<1.

∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
\alpha>1
,
a(t)
and
b(t)
be the before defined functions independent of
u
and such that
(t-\alpha)b(t)>a(t)
. Then, with
t>2\alpha
, the following inequality holds

\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}\frac{\log\frac{u}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}>\frac{\pi}{a(t)b(t)}\,\log\frac{t}{2\pi}-g(t),

where

\displaystyle g(t)

\displaystyle=\frac{\log(t/2\pi)}{b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)}

(9)

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{tb^{2}(t)}\log\left(1+\frac{t^{2}b^{2}(t)}{4a^{2}(t)}\right)+\frac{1}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{t}\log\frac{\alpha}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{t}+\frac{1+\log 2}{2}\right).

Proof.

Let us split the evaluated integral into two pieces

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{\infty}\frac{\log\frac{u}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}=\left(\int_{\alpha}^{t}+\int_{t}^{\infty}\right)\frac{\log\frac{u}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}=:P_{1}+P_{2}.

(10)

We now evaluate
P_{1}
. Noticing that

\log\frac{u}{2\pi}=\log\frac{t}{2\pi}+\log\frac{u}{t},\,u>0,\,t>0,

we have

\displaystyle\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\log\frac{u}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}=\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}+\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\log\frac{u}{t}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}

\displaystyle=:I_{1}+I_{2}.

The first integral, under the change of variable
v=(u-t)b(t)/a(t)
, is

\begin{split}I_{1}=\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}&=\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{d\,(u-t)}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}\\
&=\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{a(t)b(t)}\int_{(\alpha-t)b(t)/a(t)}^{0}\frac{dv}{1+v^{2}}\\
&=\log\frac{t}{2\pi}\cdot\frac{1}{a(t)b(t)}\arctan{\left((t-\alpha)\frac{b(t)}{a(t)}\right)},\,t>\alpha.\end{split}

Applying the inequality
\arctan t>\pi/2-1/t
,
t>1
, see
[
5
, Lem. 9]
, we get

\displaystyle I_{1}>\frac{\pi/2\cdot\log(t/2\pi)}{a(t)b(t)}-\frac{\log(t/2\pi)}{b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)},\,(t-\alpha)b(t)>a(t),\,t>\alpha+1.

The second integral
I_{2}
, after the change of variable
x=t-u
, splits into two parts

\begin{split}&\int_{\alpha}^{t}\frac{\log\frac{u}{t}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}=\int_{0}^{t-\alpha}\frac{\log(1-\frac{x}{t})\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}\\
&=\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{\log(1-\frac{x}{t})\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}+\int_{t/2}^{t-\alpha}\frac{\log(1-\frac{x}{t})\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}\eqqcolon\tilde{I}_{1}+\tilde{I}_{2},\,t>2\alpha.\end{split}

Let us evaluate
\tilde{I}_{1}
. Using Lemma
4

\log\left(1-\frac{x}{t}\right)\geqslant-2\cdot\frac{x}{t},\,0<\frac{x}{t}<\frac{1}{2}

we get

\begin{split}\tilde{I}_{1}&=\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{\log(1-\frac{x}{t})\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}\geqslant-\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{2x/t\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}=-\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t/2}\frac{dx^{2}}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}\\
&=-\frac{1}{tb^{2}(t)}\evaluated{\log\left(a^2(t)+b^2(t)x^2\right)}_{0}^{t/2}=-\frac{1}{tb^{2}(t)}\log\left(\frac{4a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)t^{2}}{4a^{2}(t)}\right),\,t>2\alpha.\end{split}

Evaluating
\tilde{I}_{2}
we do the change of variable
u=1-x/t
and obtain

\begin{split}\tilde{I}_{2}&=\int_{t/2}^{t-\alpha}\frac{\log(1-\frac{x}{t})\,dx}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)x^{2}}\geqslant\frac{1}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{t/2}^{t-\alpha}\log\left(1-\frac{x}{t}\right)\,dx\\
&=-\frac{t}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{1/2}^{\alpha/t}\log(u)\,du\\
&=-\frac{t}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(t-\alpha)^{2}}\left(\frac{\alpha}{t}\log\frac{\alpha}{t}-\frac{\alpha}{t}+\frac{1+\log 2}{2}\right),\,t>2\alpha.\end{split}

The estimate of
P_{2}
from (
10
) (see
[
5
, p. 836]
) is

\displaystyle P_{2}=\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{\log\frac{u}{2\pi}\,du}{a^{2}(t)+b^{2}(t)(u-t)^{2}}>\frac{\pi/2}{a(t)b(t)}\log\frac{t}{2\pi},\,t>0.

The proof follows combining the obtained inequalities of
I_{1}
,
\tilde{I}_{1}
,
\tilde{I}_{2}
, and
P_{2}
.

As mentioned, these days RH is verified numerically within the rectangle
0<\sigma<1
,
0<t<3\times 10^{12}
, see Platt and Trudgian
[
13
]
. If we assume that there are infinitely many zeta-zeros lying off the critical line when
t>3\times 10^{12}
, then the following statement holds.

Lemma 9
.

Assume that there are infinitely many non-trivial zeta-zeros
\tilde{\beta}_{k}+i\tilde{\gamma}_{k}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
, with
1/2<\tilde{\beta}_{k}<1
and
\tilde{\gamma}_{k}>3\times 10^{12}
. Then

\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}>0}\frac{1}{(t+\tilde{\gamma}_{k})^{2}}<\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1+\log(t+\tilde{\gamma}_1)}{t+\tilde{\gamma}_{1}},\,t>0,\,\tilde{\gamma}_{1}>3\times 10^{12}.

Proof.

Applying Lemma
2
and using the upper bound of
N(u)
from Lemma
3
we get

\sum_{\tilde{\gamma}_{k}>0}\frac{1}{(t+\tilde{\gamma}_{k})^{2}}<-\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}N(u)f^{\prime}(u)\,du<-\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}N_{up}(u)f^{\prime}(u)\,du=:\tilde{I},

where
f(u)=1/(t+u)^{2}
and
N_{up}(u)
is the upper bound of
N(u)
. Then,

\begin{split}\tilde{I}=\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{2N_{up}(u)\,du}{(t+u)^{3}}=2\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{\frac{u}{2\pi}\log\frac{u}{2\pi e}+0.11\log u+0.29\log\log u+3.165+\frac{25}{48\pi u}}{(t+u)^{3}}\,du.\end{split}

It is true that

\displaystyle\frac{u}{2\pi}\log\frac{u}{2\pi e}+0.11\log u+0.29\log\log u+3.165+\frac{25}{48\pi u}<\frac{u\log u}{2\pi},\,u>8.032.

\displaystyle\tilde{I}

\displaystyle<\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{u\log u\,du}{(t+u)^{3}}<\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{\tilde{\gamma}_{1}}^{\infty}\frac{(t+u)\log(t+u)\,du}{(t+u)^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\cdot\frac{1+\log(t+\tilde{\gamma}_1)}{t+\tilde{\gamma}_{1}},\,t>0,\,\tilde{\gamma}_{1}>3\times 10^{12}.

∎

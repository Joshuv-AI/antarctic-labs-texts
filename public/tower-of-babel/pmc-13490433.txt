Isolation enhancement in a compact 2.4 GHz antenna pair for radar systems
Authors: Pallab Kr Gogoi, Mrinal Kanti Mandal, Changzhi Li, Tapas Chakravarty
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractIn this paper, a compact antenna pair with a measured isolation of about -51 dB between the antenna pairs after fabrication is reported. Two ground plane slots of quarter wavelength at the design frequency, sharing a common ground plane, are used as a transmit-receive antenna pair. An isolation unit with both ground plane and microstrip plane structures is used to improve the isolation. The structure is compact and doesn’t require any lumped components or complex geometry to realize the isolation unit. The antenna pair radiates in the broadside direction (along the y-axis), and the top plane with the microstrip line can subsequently be used to integrate the radar circuit, facilitating a compact and fully functional transceiver design.Keywords: Integrated antenna, Isolation, MIMO antenna, Quarter wavelength, TR moduleSubject terms: Engineering, Physics

Introduction

In any radar system, either two separate antennas–one for transmitting and the other for receiving–are employed, both operating at the same frequency and with the same far-field polarization, or a single antenna is used in conjunction with an isolator, which further complicates the design process. Meanwhile compact radar designs have become increasingly important due to the availability of highly integrated MMIC chips and growing demand for smaller form factors . In such systems, one of the most critical antenna parameters is the isolation between the transmitting and receiving antennas. Without sufficient isolation, a portion of the transmitted power, which is significantly higher than the received power, can couple into the receiving antenna and saturate the receiver circuit. This issue is particularly severe when a homodyne receiver is used in full-duplex radar and communication systems, as the leakage can produce a large DC offset. The primary cause of poor isolation between two closely spaced antennas is strong mutual coupling, which also degrades the antennas’ radiation characteristics1.

Three main factors contribute to strong mutual coupling in printed circuit board (PCB)-based antennas: (i) free-space radiation and near-field coupling, (ii) surface currents propagating through the ground plane, and (iii) surface waves within the dielectric substrate2. Isolation between antennas can be improved by increasing the separation between them. Among the various coupling components, near-field coupling decreases rapidly with distance. However, in the case of two planar antennas fabricated on the same PCB, significant coupling can still occur due to ground currents and surface waves, even when the antennas are separated by several wavelengths.

Several techniques have been proposed to reduce coupling between antenna pairs. In3, a decoupling network consisting of metallic vias and lumped components was used to improve the isolation between two closely packed printed monopole antennas. In4, the coupling between two parallel planar inverted-F antennas (PIFAs) was reduced by introducing a complex copper strip-based geometry. In5, a T-shaped copper strip with a shorting pin was employed to decouple two closely spaced parallel PIFAs. In6, a four-element MIMO slot antenna array separated by a quarter-wavelength spacing achieved 20 dB isolation without the use of any decoupling patch; however, the antennas radiated in significantly different directions.

For MIMO slot antennas placed back-to-back7, achieved isolation by etching a pair of λ /4 narrow slots, while in another approach, a T-shaped slot with an additional slotline in the ground plane acted as a decoupling element8. In9, two slits were inserted at a 45 angle between two back-to-back T-shaped radiating slots. In10, two slot antennas were implemented on two different conductive layers. In11, two distinct geometries–one a PIFA slot antenna and the other an L-shaped strip antenna–were employed. In12, isolation was improved by introducing meandered slots and H- and T-shaped open and short resonant slots in the ground plane.

Despite these efforts, most reported designs achieve isolation values below 30 dB, to the best of the authors’ knowledge. In this work, a compact antenna configuration fabricated on the same PCB is presented for improved isolation. It is demonstrated that a single resonant loop coupled to a quarter-wavelength-long CPW line can provide simulated isolation as high as −51 dB between two slot antennas radiating in the same direction.

The observed tilt in the radiation pattern arises from asymmetry in the current distribution induced by the combination of the slot and the magnetic coupling cancellation loop. These elements act as distributed reactive components, locally modifying and reflecting currents. As a result, the equivalent magnetic current distribution becomes asymmetric, causing the phase center of radiation to shift. Consequently, the direction of constructive interference deviates from broadside, leading to the observed beam tilt.

The meander shape of the antenna element is selected to maintain the required electrical length while providing additional planar area. This is particularly beneficial for integrating radar front-end circuitry, where efficient space utilization is critical. Similar radiation tailoring approaches for radar applications have been reported in15.

Conclusion

Two ground-plane slots, each a quarter wavelength at the design frequency, are employed as the transmit receive antenna pair. The slots are short-circuited at the far end and radiate in the open-end direction. A meandered geometry is implemented to achieve miniaturization, enabling use in compact transceiver systems where two antennas one for transmission and one for reception, are required to radiate in the same direction. However, the antenna pair inherently exhibits poor isolation.

To address this, a decoupling unit comprising both ground-plane and microstrip-plane structures is introduced, improving isolation to −51 dB the highest reported among planar configurations. The decoupling mechanism consists of quarter-wavelength coupled slots, shorted at one end and open at the other, together with a loop formed by a microstrip line and vias, also a quarter wavelength at the design frequency. A prototype antenna pair incorporating the decoupling unit is fabricated for technology demonstration at 2.45 GHz, validating the proposed approach.

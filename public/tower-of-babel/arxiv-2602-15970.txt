Weak–strong uniqueness for bi-fluid compressible system with algebraic closure
Yang Li, Mária Lukáčová-Medvid’ová, Milan Pokorný, Ewelina Zatorska

Abstract
We consider a real two-fluid system of compressible viscous fluids with a common velocity field and algebraic closure for the pressure law. The constitutive relation involves densities of both fluids through an implicit function. The existence of global-in-time finite energy weak solutions to this system is known since the work of Novotný and Pokorný [ Arch. Rational Mech. Anal. , 2020]. On the other hand, existence of local-in-time strong solutions is due to Piasecki and Zatorska [ J. Math Fluid Mech. , 2022]. In this paper, we establish the weak–strong uniqueness principle using the relative entropy method. In sharp contrast to the two-phase model of Baer-Nunziato type, the volume fraction of phase + + obeys a transport equation with an additional nonlinear term. This gives rise to troublesome terms in the relative entropy inequality. We are able to close the estimate by making an elaborate use of the structure of the system. Keywords: Two-fluid model, algebraic closure, global weak solutions, relative entropy. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 35Q35, 76N10.

Introduction

Multi-component compressible flow models (often termed multifluid or two-phase models) arise from interface-averaging procedures applied to microscopic balance laws. In the averaged description, volume fractions become additional unknowns and various closure assumptions are needed to close the macroscopic system. Classical modeling expositions can be found in the monographs of Ishii and Hibiki
[
18
]
and Drew and Passman
[
9
]
, as well as in more modern surveys of the mathematical literature on multifluid systems
[
5
,
24
]
.

A common modeling starting point is the two-velocity Baer–Nunziato framework
[
1
]
, from which various one-velocity reductions are obtained by relaxation and closure assumptions
[
21
,
6
]
.

Two analytically distinct one-velocity regimes are:

•

Differential closure:
the volume fraction

\alpha
obeys a transport type equation (typical in one-velocity Baer–Nunziato-type models). This transport equation should include some relaxation term between the pressures, see
[
5
,
6
]
, but simplified models with pure transport equation have also been studied
[
34
,
20
]
.

•

Algebraic closure:
the phases share a common velocity and are coupled by an implicit constitutive relation—most prominently equal pressure
p_{+}=p_{-}
.
In this setting,
\alpha
is not an independent transported scalar once one passes to the “transported” partial masses
R=\alpha{\varrho_{+}}
,
Q=(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}
; instead, the implicit pressure relation generates an additional nonlinear structure; see below for more details.

The present paper analyzes the second class and considers the viscous compressible bi-fluid system in a bounded three-dimensional domain

\Omega
, where both phases are barotropic (power-law pressures) and the single momentum equation is driven by the mixture density
R+Q
. Our main contribution is the proof of the weak–strong uniqueness principle: any finite-energy weak solution in the sense of Novotný–Pokorný
[
35
]
coincides, on the lifespan of the strong solution, with the strong solution constructed by Piasecki–Zatorska
[
36
]
, if both solutions emanate from the same initial data.

For mono-fluid compressible Navier–Stokes equations, Lions-Feireisl theory
[
31
,
14
]
relies crucially on monotonicity/compensated compactness mechanisms tied to a pressure depending on a single conserved quantity. In multifluid systems, the pressure typically depends on several quantities; even when these quantities satisfy the continuity equations, their weak convergences generally do not commute with nonlinear implicit pressure maps. This gives rise to new challenges.

A first major step for algebraic closures in the semi-stationary regime is the global finite-energy existence theory for the two-fluid Stokes system by Bresch, Mucha, and Zatorska
[
4
]
. Their analysis makes explicit the core obstruction: after reformulation, the pressure argument satisfies a transport equation with a nonlinear production term, preventing the standard effective-viscous-flux compactness route; this is precisely where the Bresch–Jabin compactness criterion was brought in
[
3
]
.

The fully time-dependent viscous bi-fluid model with algebraic pressure closure was then treated by Novotný and Pokorný, who proved the existence of global-in-time finite-energy weak solutions for large initial data
[
35
]
. Their strategy was to transform the original variables into a two-densities Navier–Stokes-type system with a pressure of “complicated structure dependent on two variable densities”, and then to adapt the Lions–Feireisl method supplemented by DiPerna–Lions transport/renormalization ideas
[
8
]
refined in
[
33
,
39
]
. Notably, all of these results required certain constraint allowing comparison between the two continuity equations, which has been subsequently removed by Wen
[
40
]
. Other extensions of this theory include existence results for the compressible viscous non-resistive MHD model, for which we refer to Li and Sun
[
26
,
27
]
.

For the sake of completeness, let us also recall that the existence of weak solutions to the inviscid two-fluid system with algebraic pressure closure was considered in
[
28
]
, where the solutions were constructed using the convex integration method. We also refer to
[
10
,
29
,
12
]
for the existence results of weak solutions to related models in one space-dimension case.

The results from
[
35
]
were further adapted to treat differential closure (transport of
\alpha
) by Novotný
[
34
]
.
For this system, Jin and Novotný
[
20
]
established the weak–strong uniqueness property of energy-bounded weak solutions, emphasizing the role of convexity of the associated Helmholtz potential. We refer also to
[
22
]
for problems with inflow-outflow boundary conditions and
[
19
]
for the weak–strong stability result but for the dissipative measure-valued solutions. Still, the assumption about convexity of the free Helmholtz energy with respect to the conservative variables seemed to be of paramount importance.

Removal of this constraint is to date an important open problem that we resolved in the current paper.

On the strong-solution side, local-in-time well-posedness for the compressible two-fluid algebraic-closure system was obtained by Piasecki and Zatorska using the maximal-regularity techniques
[
36
]
. The existence of global-in-time strong solutions for small perturbations of constant initial conditions for the nonconservative model is due to Evje, Wang and Wen
[
11
]
.

Given the coexistence of global weak solutions and local strong solutions, a natural stability question is whether weak solutions are unique as long as a strong solution exists—the weak–strong uniqueness principle.
For the compressible two-fluid system, the only known results, as far as we are aware, are the aforementioned
[
20
,
19
]
and a conditional result by Li and Zatorska
[
30
]
.

For the mono-fluid compressible Navier–Stokes system, weak–strong uniqueness via the relative entropy method is nowadays classical
[
15
]
. Beyond uniqueness, the relative entropy inequality has proven to have profound applications in the study of singular-limits problems, and stability of numerical schemes, see, for example,
[
16
,
32
,
7
]
.
For the viscous compressible two-fluid system, the related results are much more sparse. For the two-fluid model from
[
39
]
, the incompressible and/or inviscid singular limits were considered in
[
38
,
23
,
37
]
, with the use of the relative entropy method. We also refer to the recent result of Fanelli, Kwon and Wróblewska-Kaminska
[
13
]
, where the low Mach number limit for the multi-component MHD system, in case of ill-prepared data, was proven.

The relative entropy inequality developed here has similar implications for singular limits as the pioneering result of Feireisl, Jin and Novotný
[
15
]
for mono-fluids: it supplies a robust stability mechanism that upgrades formal asymptotics to rigorous convergence, provided suitable strong limit systems are available. In particular, our method gives a direct route to justify various incompressible limits (at least for well-prepared data) studied formally and numerically, for example in
[
2
,
24
,
17
]
. Moreover, the method can also be extended to two-fluid systems with a differential closure relation
[
5
,
6
]
.

In sharp contrast to the differential-closure model
[
20
]
, the algebraic constraint
{p_{+}}={p_{-}}
changes the evolution of the volume fraction to transport with an
additional nonlinear term
. This phenomenon appears already in the Stokes analysis of Bresch–Mucha–Zatorska
[
4
]
and is precisely what breaks the most direct relative-entropy estimates: extra terms arise that do not cancel as in the pure-transport case.
Our proof introduces an additional mechanism to control the defect in the volume fraction generated by the algebraic closure. More concretely, we exploit the specific implicit structure of the pressure law to derive a stability estimate for the volume fractions, which is compatible with the relative energy inequality, and then we conclude with a Grönwall argument.

It should be emphasized that, in sharp contrast with Jin–Novotný
[
20
]
, no positive bound for
\alpha
from below and away from
1
is required in our analysis.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
after recalling the model and the notion of bounded-energy weak solutions, we summarize the known existence theory for global weak solutions and local strong solutions. We then derive the relative energy inequality, highlighting the additional nonlinear terms caused by the evolution law for the volume fraction in Section
3
. Next, in Section
4
we establish the relative entropy inequality with the strong solution as a reference state and estimate the resulting remainder terms using the structure induced by the algebraic closure. Finally, in Section
5
, we combine these estimates to prove the weak–strong uniqueness principle, which is the main achievement of our paper.

Formally, we can calculate

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\alpha

\displaystyle=\partial_{R}\alpha\partial_{t}R+\partial_{Q}\alpha\partial_{t}Q+\partial_{R}\alpha{\bf u}\cdot\nabla R+\partial_{Q}\alpha{\bf u}\cdot\nabla Q

\displaystyle=-\partial_{R}\alpha R\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\partial_{Q}\alpha Q\operatorname{div}{\bf u};

whence

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\alpha+(\partial_{R}\alpha R+\partial_{Q}\alpha Q)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}=0.

Denoting

\begin{split}\gamma=\gamma^{+}/\gamma^{-}>0\end{split}

(3.1)

and using the equality of pressures (
2.1d
) and the constitutive relations (
2.2
), we can find that

\begin{split}R^{\gamma}(1-\alpha)=Q\alpha^{\gamma}.\end{split}

(3.2)

Hence, applying
\partial_{Q}

\begin{split}\partial_{Q}\alpha=\frac{-\alpha^{\gamma}}{Q\gamma\alpha^{\gamma-1}+R^{\gamma}},\qquad\partial_{R}\alpha=\frac{\gamma R^{\gamma-1}(1-\alpha)}{Q\gamma\alpha^{\gamma-1}+R^{\gamma}}.\end{split}

\displaystyle\partial_{R}\alpha R+\partial_{Q}\alpha Q

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma R^{\gamma}(1-\alpha)}{Q\gamma\alpha^{\gamma-1}+R^{\gamma}}+\frac{-Q\alpha^{\gamma}}{Q\gamma\alpha^{\gamma-1}+R^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma R^{\gamma}(1-\alpha)-Q\alpha^{\gamma}}{Q\gamma\alpha^{\gamma-1}+R^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(\gamma-1)Q\alpha^{\gamma}}{\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}Q\alpha^{\gamma}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}Q\alpha^{\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}+\frac{1}{1-\alpha}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha(1-\alpha).

Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}\alpha+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\alpha+\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha(1-\alpha)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}=0.\end{split}

(3.4)

Equation (
3.4
) is, of course, not exactly a pure transport equation, but we should notice that because
0\leq\alpha\leq 1
, the coefficient

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}:=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha(1-\alpha)

is also bounded in
L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)
.
Indeed,
\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha
has a strictly positive lower bound, namely
\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha\geq\min\{1,\gamma\}>0
. Thus
|\omega_{\alpha}|\leq\frac{|\gamma-1|}{4\min\{1,\gamma\}}<\frac{\gamma+1}{\min\{1,\gamma\}}
.

In a similar manner, we obtain

\begin{split}\partial_{t}(1-\alpha)+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla(1-\alpha)-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha(1-\alpha)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}=0.\end{split}

(3.5)

From equations (
3.4
), and (
2.1a
), (
2.1b
), we now deduce

\begin{split}\alpha\partial_{t}{\varrho_{+}}+{\varrho_{+}}\partial_{t}\alpha+\operatorname{div}({\varrho_{+}}{\bf u})\alpha+{\varrho_{+}}({\bf u}\cdot\nabla\alpha)=0,\end{split}

(3.6)

therefore, using (
3.4
), we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\varrho_{+}}+\operatorname{div}({\varrho_{+}}{\bf u})={\varrho_{+}}\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}(1-\alpha)\operatorname{div}{\bf u}

and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}{\varrho_{-}}+\operatorname{div}({\varrho_{-}}{\bf u})=-{\varrho_{-}}\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha\operatorname{div}{\bf u}.

3.2
Derivation of relative entropy inequality

We now derive the estimate of a part of the relative entropy functional from (
2.5
), namely

\begin{split}&\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})(\tau)\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}(R+Q)|{\bf u}-{\bf v}|^{2}(\tau,x){\rm d}{x}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\alpha\Big({H_{+}}({\varrho_{+}})-{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})({\varrho_{+}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-{H_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\Big)(\tau,x){\rm d}{x}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)\Big({H_{-}}({\varrho_{-}})-{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})({\varrho_{-}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})-{H_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\Big)(\tau,x){\rm d}{x},\end{split}

(3.10)

where
(\beta,{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}},{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}},{\bf v})
are smooth test functions with
{\bf v}|_{\partial\Omega}=\mathbf{0}
, and we set
{\widetilde{R}}=\beta{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}},\,\,{\widetilde{Q}}=(1-\beta){\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}
.

We now expand

\begin{split}&\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\\
&=\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}(R+Q)|{\bf u}|^{2}+\alpha{H_{+}}({\varrho_{+}})+(1-\alpha){H_{-}}({\varrho_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\\
&\quad+\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}(R+Q)|{\bf v}|^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}(R+Q){\bf u}\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\varrho_{+}}{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{\Omega}\alpha\left({H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}){\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{H_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\\
&\quad-\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)\left({H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}){\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{H_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\end{split}

(3.11)

and we will treat the right-hand side of (
3.11
) line by line.

Step 1.

The first line is estimated using the energy estimate (
2.10
), from which it follows that

\begin{split}&\int_{\Omega}\left(\frac{1}{2}(R+Q)|{\bf u}|^{2}+\alpha{H_{+}}({\varrho_{+}})+(1-\alpha){H_{-}}({\varrho_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\leq-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf u}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.12)

Step 2.
We sum the equations for
Q
and
R
and test the obtained result by
\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}|^{2}

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}(R+Q)|{\bf v}|^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(R+Q){\bf v}\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf v}\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.13)

Note that (
3.13
) is not precisely the weak formulation; however, it can be easily deduced by considering the test function
\frac{1}{2}|{\bf v}|^{2}\psi_{\varepsilon}(t)
with
\psi_{\varepsilon}\in C^{\infty}_{c}([0,\tau))
,
\psi_{\varepsilon}\to{\mathbf{1}}_{[0,\tau]}
uniformly for
\varepsilon\to 0_{+}
. The same also applies below.

Step 3.
We take
{\bf v}
as a test function in the momentum equation (
2.9
), and integrate by parts to get

\begin{split}-\int_{\Omega}(R+Q){\bf u}\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left((R+Q){\bf u}\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf v}\right)\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left(p\operatorname{div}{\bf v}-{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla{\bf v}\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.14)

Step 4.
Finally, we test the equation for
R
by
{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})
and the equation for
Q
by
{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})
to get

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\varrho_{+}}{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\varrho_{+}}\left(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\right){H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

(3.15)

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}\left(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla\right){H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(3.16)

Step 5.
Finally, using
{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}){\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{H_{+}}(\varrho)={p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\alpha\left({H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}){\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{H_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}.

In a similar manner

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)\left({H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}){\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{H_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}=\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}.

Step 6.
Summing the above expressions, we obtain all together:

\begin{split}&\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(R+Q)({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf v}\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}p\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\varrho_{+}}(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla){H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla){H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}.\end{split}

(3.17)

Adding to both sides
-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t
, we get

\begin{split}&\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}-\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(R+Q)({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf v}\right)+{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}p\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\varrho_{+}}(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla){H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}(\partial_{t}+{\bf u}\cdot\nabla){H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}.\end{split}

(3.18)

Step 7.
Now observe that because
{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\nabla{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})=\nabla{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})
, we have

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}))\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\nabla{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Big(\alpha\nabla{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha)\nabla{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\Big)\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\nabla{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\nabla{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\cdot{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

due to equality of the pressures, and similarly

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\right)\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Big(\alpha\partial_{t}{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha)\partial_{t}{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\partial_{t}{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha){\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\partial_{t}{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

Substituting for these terms into (
3.18
), we obtain

\begin{split}&\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}-\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Big((R+Q)({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+({\bf u}\cdot\nabla){\bf v}\right)+{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf v}-{\bf u})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}})\partial_{t}{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{\varrho_{-}})\partial_{t}{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\Big(\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+(1-\alpha){p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})-p\Big)\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}{\bf v}-{\varrho_{+}}{\bf u})\cdot\nabla{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}{\bf v}-{\varrho_{-}}{\bf u})\cdot\nabla{H_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\\
&=:I+II+III+\ldots+VI.\end{split}

(3.19)

This is an analogous form of the relative entropy inequality as the one derived in Theorem 5.2 from

other than

as test functions.

5
The weak–strong uniqueness principle

We first notice that

\displaystyle\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})(\tau)

\displaystyle\qquad\geq C\begin{cases}&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Big[\alpha\left({\varrho_{+}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\right)^{2}+(1-\alpha)\left({\varrho_{-}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\right)^{2}\Big]\ {\rm d}{x},\quad{\varrho_{+}},{\varrho_{-}}\in\left[c_{\star},c^{\star}\right],\\[12.91663pt]
&\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left(1+\alpha{\varrho_{+}}^{\gamma^{+}}+(1-\alpha){\varrho_{-}}^{\gamma^{-}}\right)\ {\rm d}{x},\quad\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

(5.1)

for any
c_{\star},c^{\star}>0
and
\tau\in(0,T)
.
Motivated by this observation, we also define the essential and residual sets corresponding to
{\varrho_{+}},{\varrho_{-}}
as follows

\displaystyle\Omega_{\rm ess}(t):=\{x\in\Omega:{\varrho_{+}},{\varrho_{-}}\in\left[c_{\star},c^{\star}\right]\},\quad\Omega_{\rm res}(t)=\Omega\backslash\Omega_{\rm ess}(t).

Let us now more precisely formulate our main theorem comparing global weak solutions in the time interval
(0,T)
with the local strong solutions.

Theorem 5.1

Let
(\alpha,\varrho_{-},\varrho_{+},{\bf u})
be a weak solution emanating from the data
(\alpha_{0},\varrho_{-,0},\varrho_{+,0},{\bf u}_{0})
, given by Theorem
2.3
and let
(\beta,{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}},{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}},{\bf v})
be the strong solution emanating from the data
(\beta_{0},\widetilde{\varrho}_{-,0},\widetilde{\varrho}_{+,0},{\bf v}_{0})
.
Then there exists a positive constant
C
depending on the strong solution (but not on the regularity of the weak solution), such that for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T)

\displaystyle{\cal E}(\alpha,R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,\beta,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})(\tau)+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}-\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\leq C{\cal E}(\alpha,R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,\beta,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})(0),

where
{\cal E}(\alpha,R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,\beta,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})
is given in (
2.5
).

In particular, if
(\beta_{0},\widetilde{\varrho}_{-,0},\widetilde{\varrho}_{+,0},{\bf v}_{0})=(\alpha_{0},\varrho_{-,0},\varrho_{+,0},{\bf u}_{0})
, then

\displaystyle(\alpha,\varrho_{-},\varrho_{+},{\bf u})(\tau,x)=(\beta,,{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}},{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}},{\bf v})(\tau,x)

for a.a.
\tau\in(0,T),\,x\in\Omega
.

Before proving Theorem
5.1
, we will state and prove an auxiliary result targeting specifically the difference of the volumetric fractions
\alpha
and
\beta
.

Lemma 5.2

Let
(\alpha,{\bf u})
,
(\beta,{\bf v})
satisfying

\displaystyle\alpha,\beta\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)\cap C([0,T];L^{1}(\Omega)),\quad{\bf u},{\bf v}\in L^{2}(0,T;W^{1,2}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}))

be two distributional solutions to the transport equation (
3.4
). Suppose, in addition, that

\displaystyle\nabla\beta\in L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\quad\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\in L^{1}(0,T;L^{\infty}(\Omega))

and
0\leq\alpha,\beta\leq 1
almost everywhere in
(0,T)\times\Omega
.
Then, for any
\delta>0
and for all
\tau\in[0,T]
, we have

\begin{split}\int_{\Omega}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}\Big|_{0}^{\tau}\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|^{2}_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\int_{\Omega}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.\end{split}

(5.2)

with
C_{\delta}(t)
integrable over
(0,T)
.

Proof.

To rigorously justify this lemma, one may use the DiPerna-Lions regularizing technique
[
8
]
, see also Lemma 7.3 in
[
20
]
for the same argument applied to the pure transport equations for
\alpha,\beta
. In our case, the equations of
\alpha,\beta
contain additional nonlinear terms, so the analysis is more involved.
We have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\alpha-\beta)+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla(\alpha-\beta)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad=({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla\alpha-\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha\operatorname{div}{\bf u}+\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\beta)}{\gamma(1-\beta)+\beta}\beta\operatorname{div}{\bf v}.

Multiplying this equation by
(\alpha-\beta)
, we obtain (
\omega_{\alpha}=\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha
, similarly
\omega_{\beta}
)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad=\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla\alpha(\alpha-\beta)\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}(\beta-\alpha)(\omega_{\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\omega_{\beta}\operatorname{div}{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\operatorname{div}({\bf v}-{\bf u})(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla\beta(\alpha-\beta)\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}(\beta-\alpha)(\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta})\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\Omega}(\beta-\alpha)\omega_{\alpha}(\operatorname{div}{\bf u}-\operatorname{div}{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}.

A straightforward calculation shows

\displaystyle|\omega_{\alpha}-\omega_{\beta}|\leq C|\alpha-\beta|,

where
C=C(\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)},\|\beta\|_{L^{\infty}((0,T)\times\Omega)})>0
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime}=\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}(1-\alpha)-\frac{\gamma-1}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\alpha-\alpha(1-\alpha)\frac{\gamma-1}{[\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha]^{2}}(-\gamma+1),

yielding

\displaystyle|\omega_{\alpha}^{\prime}|\leq 2\frac{\gamma+1}{\min\{\gamma,1\}}+\frac{(\gamma+1)^{2}}{(\min\{\gamma,1\})^{2}}.

We may choose

\delta>0
arbitrarily small so that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\partial_{t}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\|\operatorname{div}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+\delta\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\quad+C_{\delta}\left(\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\nabla\beta\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}+\|\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\right)\int_{\Omega}(\alpha-\beta)^{2}\ {\rm d}{x},

where we also used the crucial fact that
\omega_{\alpha}
is uniformly bounded.
The statement thus follows by integrating the above inequality with respect to
t\in(0,\tau)
.
\Box

With Lemma
5.2
at hand, we may proceed to the proof of Theorem
5.1
. We will prove it by estimating
J_{1},\ldots,J_{8}
term by term.

Estimate of
J_{1}
. We split the first term of the remainder (
4.1
) as follows

\displaystyle J_{1}=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[(R+Q-{\widetilde{R}}-{\widetilde{Q}})\partial_{t}{\bf v}+\left((R+Q){\bf u}-({\widetilde{R}}+{\widetilde{Q}}){\bf v}\right)\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\right]\cdot({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\left[(R-{\widetilde{R}}+Q-{\widetilde{Q}})(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v})\right]\cdot({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(R+Q)({\bf u}-{\bf v})\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\cdot({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t.

The second term can be estimated by
\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t
with
C(t)
depending on
\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
.
To estimate the first term, we use

\begin{split}R-{\widetilde{R}}=\alpha({\varrho_{+}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}(\beta-\alpha),\end{split}

(5.3)

and similarly for
Q-{\widetilde{Q}}
. Thus, together, we obtain

\displaystyle|R-{\widetilde{R}}+Q-{\widetilde{Q}}|\leq C\Big(\sqrt{\alpha}|{\varrho_{+}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}|+\sqrt{1-\alpha}|{\varrho_{-}}-{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}|+|\beta-\alpha|\Big),

where
C=C(t)>0
depends only on
\|({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}},{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
and on
\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{1}_{\rm ess}\left[(R-{\widetilde{R}}+Q-{\widetilde{Q}})(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v})\right]\cdot({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

with
C_{\delta}(t)
proportional to
\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
. For the residual part of this term in
J_{1}
, considering the small and large values of
{\varrho_{-}},{\varrho_{+}}
separately, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{1}_{\rm res}\left[(R-{\widetilde{R}}+Q-{\widetilde{Q}})(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v})\right]\cdot({\bf v}-{\bf u})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{0}^{\tau}\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\|R|_{\rm res},Q|_{\rm res}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|(R+Q)|{\bf u}-{\bf v}|^{2}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{1}{2}}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t,

with
C_{\delta}(t)
proportional to
\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
. Ultimately, we have

\displaystyle J_{1}\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t,

where
C_{\delta}(t)
depends on
\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
and on
\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
so that
C_{\delta}(t)
is integrable over
(0,T)
.

Estimate of
J_{2}
and
J_{3}
. Again, both terms will be considered on the essential and residual parts of the domain. Using Taylor expansion for
J_{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{1}_{\rm ess}\alpha\Big({p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-{p_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}})-{p_{+}}({\varrho_{+}})\Big)\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\mathbf{1}_{\rm ess}\alpha|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}}|^{2}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t,

where
C(t)
is proportional to
\|\alpha\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)\|\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
. The estimate for the essential part of
J_{3}
is similar. The residual part is bounded directly from (
5
) and
C(t)
is again integrable. In summary, we have

\displaystyle J_{2}+J_{3}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\,{\rm d}t.

Estimate of
J_{4}
and
J_{5}
. Both terms are again treated in an analogous way, so we estimate only one of them. Using the decomposition (
5.3
), we easily verify that

\displaystyle J_{4}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\nabla{H_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})(\alpha{\varrho_{+}}-\beta{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

where
C_{\delta}(t)
depends on
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
. Moreover,

\displaystyle J_{5}\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}|{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

with
C_{\delta}(t)
depending on
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
.

Estimate of
J_{6}
. This term vanishes thanks to the algebraic closure
{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})={p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})
. Indeed, we have

\displaystyle J_{6}=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\Big(\nabla\beta\,{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})+\nabla(1-\beta)\,{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}({\bf v}-{\bf u})\cdot\Big(\nabla\beta\,{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-\nabla\beta\,{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,0.

Estimate of
J_{7}
and
J_{8}
. To estimate the remaining terms, we couple them with terms

\begin{split}-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t\end{split}

and

\begin{split}\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,\end{split}

that cancel each other out due to the equality of the pressures
{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})={p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})
.

At first, we transform

\displaystyle J_{7}

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}}){p_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\beta)}{\gamma(1-\beta)+\beta}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\left[{p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}}){p_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\right]\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha\left(\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}-\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\beta)}{\gamma(1-\beta)+\beta}\right)({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}}){p_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,L_{1}+L_{2}.

Notice that the last term
L_{2}
can already be estimated, because the difference in brackets is controlled by
C|\alpha-\beta|
, with
C>0
depending on
\|(\alpha,\beta)\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle L_{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

with
C(t)>0
depending additionally on
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
, and
\|\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
.
Note that
C(t)
is integrable over
(0,T)
.

We now transfer in a similar way

\displaystyle J_{8}

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{\varrho_{-}}){p_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\frac{(\gamma-1)\beta}{\gamma(1-\beta)+\beta}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}[{p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})-({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{\varrho_{-}}){p_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})]\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}(1-\alpha)\left(\frac{(\gamma-1)\alpha}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}-\frac{(\gamma-1)\beta}{\gamma(1-\beta)+\beta}\right)({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{\varrho_{-}}){p_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle\,{\emph{\L}}_{1}+{\emph{\L}}_{2}.

The term
{\emph{\L}}_{2}
can be estimated by the same way as
L_{2}
, leading to

\displaystyle{\emph{\L}}_{2}\leq\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

with
C(t)
depending on
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
and
\|\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
so that it is integrable.

Finally, let us look at the terms
L_{1}
and
{\emph{\L}}_{1}
. Using again the equality of pressures, we can write

\displaystyle L_{1}+{\emph{\L}}_{1}

\displaystyle\quad=-\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\Big({p_{+}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}-{\varrho_{+}}){p_{+}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}})-{p_{+}}({\varrho_{+}})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\qquad\,\,+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}\alpha\frac{(\gamma-1)(1-\alpha)}{\gamma(1-\alpha)+\alpha}\operatorname{div}{\bf v}\Big({p_{-}}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})-({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}-{\varrho_{-}}){p_{-}}^{\prime}({\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}})-{p_{-}}({\varrho_{-}})\Big)\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t,

and these two integrals can be treated exactly as the terms
J_{2},\ J_{3}
before.
Summarizing, we have shown that

\displaystyle J_{7}+J_{8}=

\displaystyle\,L_{1}+L_{2}+{\emph{\L}}_{1}+{\emph{\L}}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\,\int_{0}^{\tau}C(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t.

Conclusion
. Summarizing the above estimates of the remainder
{\cal R}
given by (
4.1
) and coming back to (
3.19
), we obtain

\displaystyle\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})\Big|_{0}^{\tau}+\int_{0}^{\tau}\!\!\int_{\Omega}{\bf S}(\nabla{\bf u}-\nabla{\bf v}):\nabla({\bf u}-{\bf v})\ {\rm d}{x}\,{\rm d}t

\displaystyle\quad\leq\delta\int_{0}^{\tau}\|{\bf v}-{\bf u}\|_{W^{1,2}(\Omega)}^{2}\,{\rm d}t+\int_{0}^{\tau}C_{\delta}(t)\Big(\bar{\cal E}(R,Q,{\bf u}\,|\,{\widetilde{R}},{\widetilde{Q}},{\bf v})+\|\alpha-\beta\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\Big)\,{\rm d}t,

for
C_{\delta}(t)
depending on
\|\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
,
\|\nabla{\bf v}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
,
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{+}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
and
\|{\widetilde{\varrho}_{-}}\|_{W^{1,\infty}(\Omega)}(t)
, as well as on the upper limits of
\alpha
and
\beta
, which are equal to
1
, so that
C_{\delta}
is integrable over
(0,T)
.

Summing up this estimate with (
5.2
) and using the Grönwall argument, we finish the proof of Theorem
5.1
. In particular, our main Theorem
2.1
follows.
\Box

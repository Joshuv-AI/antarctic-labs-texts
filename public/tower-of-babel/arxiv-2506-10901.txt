Deformations of pseudocharacters and Mazur’s finiteness condition
Vytautas Paškūnas and Julian Quast

Abstract
. We show that deformation rings R ps R^{\mathrm{ps}} of G G -pseudocharacters of a profinite group Γ \Gamma are noetherian, when Γ \Gamma satisfies Mazur’s finiteness condition. The proof proceeds by reduction to the case when Γ \Gamma is finitely generated, where the result was previously established by the second author. This enables us to extend our work on moduli spaces of R ps R^{\mathrm{ps}} -condensed representations of a finitely generated profinite group Γ \Gamma , to the groups satisfying Mazur’s finiteness condition. We also show that the functor from rigid analytic spaces over ℚ p \mathbb{Q}_{p} to sets, which associates to a rigid space Y Y the set of continuous 𝒪 ⁡ ( Y ) \mathcal{O}(Y) -valued G G -pseudocharacters of Γ \Gamma is representable by a quasi-Stein rigid analytic space, and we study its general properties. We expect these results to be useful, when studying global Galois representations.

1.3.
What we actually do

Let us explain the proof of
1.1
. We first prove
1.1
for finitely generated free profinite groups
\Gamma
. We show that the map between
deformation rings
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}\rightarrow R^{\square}_{\overline{\rho}}
,
induced by mapping a deformation of
\overline{\rho}
to its
G
-pseudocharacter, is
injective. This key step is carried out in
Section

4

the moduli space of
G
-pseudocharacters of a discrete finitely generated free group

as a GIT-quotient of a moduli space of representations of
F
into

. We show that if
\Gamma
is the profinite completion of

then the rings
R^{\mathrm{ps}}_{\overline{\Theta}}
and
R^{\square}_{\overline{\rho}}

are naturally isomorphic to completions of the local rings at points
\Theta|_{F}
and
\overline{\rho}|_{F}
in the
respective moduli spaces. The assertion about injectivity boils down to commutative algebra, which is carried out in
Section

3
. We note that if we drop the
assumption that
\Gamma
is a free profinite group then
it is not known whether

is injective, even when

, see
[

, Remark 1.25]

It is easy to see that the universal deformation

\rho^{\mathrm{univ}}:\Gamma\rightarrow G(R^{\square}_{\overline{\rho}})

for finitely generated

for all finitely generated profinite groups

If
\Theta
is a continuous

-pseudocharacter of

then there is a closed normal subgroup

of

factors through

holds if and only if

is a pro-

group, where

is the universal deformation of
\overline{\Theta}
. A profinite group is pro-

if and only if
every closed finitely generated subgroup is pro-

that

is pro-

for general

to closed finitely generated

2.
Lafforgue’s pseudocharacters

In this section we recall the definition of pseudocharacters and their deformation theory. Until

A

Definition 2.1
.

1

\Theta_{n}(f^{\zeta})(\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{n})=\Theta_{m}(f)(\gamma_{\zeta(1)},\dots,\gamma_{\zeta(m)})

We first recall the set up of

\mathcal{O}

Tag 07QV

Let

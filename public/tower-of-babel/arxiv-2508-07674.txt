Steady state properties of periodically driven quantum systems
Milan Šindelka, David Gelbwaser-Klimovsky

Abstract
Periodic driving is used to steer physical systems to unique stationary states or nonequilibrium steady states (NESS), producing enhanced properties inaccessible to non-driven systems. For open quantum systems, characterizing the NESS is challenging and existing results are generally limited to specific types of driving and the Born-Markov approximation. Here we go beyond these limits by studying a generic periodically driven N N -level quantum system interacting with a low-density thermal gas. Exploiting the framework of Floquet scattering theory, we establish general Floquet thermalization conditions constraining the nature of the NESS and the transition rates. Moreover, we examine theoretically the structure of the NESS at high temperatures, and find out that the NESS complies, rather surprisingly, with the Boltzmann law for any driving. Numerical calculations illustrate our theoretical elaborations for a simple toy model.



I
Introduction

Periodic driving is used in a wide range of fields to modify quantum systems in an ad hoc manner and to enhance specific properties such as: tunneling suppresion in BEC
Lignier et al. 2007
, inducing topological state in semiconductor quantum wells
Lindner et al. 2011
and creating topological and a quantum spin Hall insulator in lattices
Hauke et al. 2012
, reducing decoherence of a spin-
\frac{1}{2}
particle
Chen et al. 2015
, creating quantum heat machines
Gelbwaser-Klimovsky et al. 2013
. This approach is known as Floquet engineering and has been studied both for closed
Bukov et al. 2015
;
Eckardt 2017
and open systems
Mori 2023
.

Periodically driven systems reach a periodic steady state or limiting cycle
Yudin et al. 2016
.
For systems interacting with a thermal bath, the periodic steady state can be mapped to a nonequilibrium steady state
(NESS)
Raz et al. 2016
. The NESS ultimately determines the steady physical properties of the system. In general, determining the NESS is a complex problem, but some progress has been made under some restrictions on the driving. For example, in
Ikeda and Sato 2020
;
Ikeda et al. 2021
;
Martins et al. 2025
,
the NESS of a high-frequency periodically driven system was found. To avoid the complexity of finding the NESS, an alternative approach can be taken: studying the conditions under which the NESS does not deviate from the thermal state or a thermal state with an effective Hamiltonian, also called the Floquet-Gibbs state (FGS). In these cases, all the novel properties or phases inaccessible in thermal equilibrium are lost, and therefore, these conditions should be avoided if any enhanced properties are desired. In particular, under the Born-Markov approximation or weak coupling limit
Breuer et al. 2002
,
detailed balance of the transition rates has been used to find conditions for a FGS
Breuer et al. 2000
;
Shirai et al. 2016
;
Liu 2015
. These conditions are limited to specific driving Hamiltonians or frequencies, and, therefore, can not be universally applied.

In this work, we study the NESS of a periodically driven
N
-level system interacting with a single thermal bath represented by a dilute gas. This allows us to employ a Lindblad equation beyond the weak coupling limit
Dümcke 1985
, to study the NESS beyond the Born-Markov approximation, and to find conditions for it to be a FGS. Exploiting the framework of Floquet scattering theory

Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
, we establish general Floquet thermalization conditions constraining the
nature of the NESS and the transition rates. These conditions generalize the usual detailed balance-like restriction commonly found for rates of periodically driven system under the Born-Markov approximation
Kohn 2001
. Moreover, we examine theoretically the structure of NESS at high temperatures, and
find out that the high temperature NESS approaches, rather surprisingly, to the FGS, independently of the driving strength, frequency or Hamiltonian. This happens despite the lack of compliance with detailed balance. As we show, the lack of deviation from a Boltzmann state is a consequence of the unitarity of the scattering operator, which at high temperatures precludes the rate asymmetries required for a non-thermal steady state. In contrast, for lower temperatures, the NESS deviates from the Boltzmann equilibrium state, requiring the injection of energy in order to produce it. Our theoretical elaborations are illustrated by numerical calculations for a simple toy model.

II
Theoretical outline

II.1
Periodically driven
\bm{N}
-level system coupled to dilute thermal gas

We consider an
N
-level quantum system that is subjected to a periodic driving with frequency
\omega
. Moreover, the quantum system interacts with a single thermal bath at an inverse temperature
\beta=(k_{B}T)^{-1}
. We furthermore assume that the bath is a
d
-dimensional low density gas composed of free particles that are scattered by the driven
N
-level system. This assumption allows to write the low density Lindblad master equation for the system evolution
Dümcke 1985
. This equation is valid for any bath-system coupling strength even beyond the Born-Markov approximation.

Periodic driving is described by exploiting the language of Floquet theory

Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
(see S.1 in the supplemental material).
Due to driving, bare energy levels
\{E_{j},1\leq j\leq N\}
of the
N
-level system are converted into quasi-energies

\{E_{j\nu}^{\rm QE}=E_{j0}^{\rm QE}+\nu\hbar\omega,1\leq j\leq N,\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}\}
, where
E_{j0}^{\rm QE}

reduces to
E_{j}
for zero driving, and the Floquet index
\nu

labels here different Brillouin copies of the same physical Floquet state
j
.

Hereafter we shall assume non-degenerate setup,
E_{j\nu}^{\rm QE}\neq E_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm QE}
for
(j\nu)\neq(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})
. In such non-degenerate cases, the
physical populations
\wp_{j}
of the driven
N
-level system are decoupled from coherences, and the latter decay to zero.
From now on, we focus solely on the populations
\wp_{j}
of the physical Floquet states
j
,
which evolve according to the Pauli rate equation
Pauli 1928
:

\displaystyle\dot{\wp}_{j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}\,\wp_{j^{\prime}}\;-\;\wp_{j}\,\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj};

(1)

with

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\;=\;\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=+\infty}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
.
Here
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
refers to the rate of transition
j\to j^{\prime}
and the absorption of
\nu
Floquet quanta by the system from the driving field. These transition rates are obtained using the framework of the low-density limit Lindblad equation. They are based on scattering theory and are constructed from the Floquet
\widehat{T}-
matrix elements through the following formula,

\displaystyle a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\;={\cal N}\,Z^{-1}\hskip-1.42262pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p^{\prime}\;e^{-\beta\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}}\,\times

(2)

\displaystyle\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)\,\Bigl|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\Bigr|^{2}.

Here
{\cal N}
stands for the gas particle density,
Z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-0.7113pt{\rm d}^{d}p\,e^{-\beta\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}}
and
\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle
are the on-shell
Floquet
\widehat{T}
-matrix elements, encoding all information about scattering of a single gas particle on the driven
N
-level system
(see section S.1 on the supplemental material).

|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle
represents an asymptotic in-state corresponding to a gas particle of momentum
\bm{p}
which approaches the driven system residing in its in-Floquet state
(j0)
. Similarly
\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|
refers to an asymptotic out-state corresponding to a gas particle of momentum
\bm{p}^{\prime}
which departs from the driven system residing in its out-Floquet state
(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)
.

For
\dot{\wp}_{j}=0
, we obtain from (
1
) the NESS,
\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}
,
of the driven
N
-level system. The corresponding stationarity condition,

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}\,\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j^{\prime}}\;-\;\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}\,\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\;=\;0,

(3)

together with
\sum_{j}\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}=1
, determine the NESS
\{\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
uniquely in terms of
the rates
\{a_{jj^{\prime}}\}
.

II.2
Floquet thermalization conditions

We recall that the Floquet
\widehat{S}
-matrix elements take the form

\displaystyle\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{S}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle=\delta^{d}\hskip-1.42262pt(\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt-\bm{p})\,\delta_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\,\delta_{\nu 0}-

(4)

\displaystyle 2

\displaystyle\,\pi\,i\;\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)\,\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;;

see section S.2 on the supplemental material and
Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
. All scattering processes preserve unitarity,
due to the unitary character of the quantum dynamical evolution. The resulting unitarity of
\widehat{S}
imposes in turn strong constraints on the associated
\widehat{T}
-matrix elements. Such constraints are also known as the optical theorem (see Eq. (36) in
Ref.
Takayanagi and Kurino 2023
and Eqs. (
S5
)-(
S16
) in the supplemental material).
The just mentioned optical theorem can be used
to deduce constraints on the Floquet transition rates
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
. Namely, one finds that

\hskip-7.11317pt\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu}\,e^{-\beta E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}}\;=\;e^{-\beta E_{j0}^{\rm QE}}\,\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{+\nu};

(5)

valid for all
1\leq j\leq N
and
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
(see section S.2 of the supplemental material).

Numerical check of the Floquet thermalization conditions (

) for a driven three-level system.

The constraints (
5
)
have important thermodynamic consequences for the nature of the NESS, as shown below.
Conditions (
5
) are less restrictive than the standard detailed balance-like relations generally used in periodically driven open quantum systems:
a_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu}\,e^{-\beta E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}}=e^{-\beta E_{j0}^{\rm QE}}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}

Kohn 2001
. The detailed balance-like relations are valid only in the Born-Markov approximation or for systems that do not break microreversibility
Alicki et al. 2023
(that is, in the absence of a magnetic field or any other velocity dependent forces). Because our dynamical evolution equation (
1
) reaches beyond the Born-Markov approximation, we will use the more general condition (
5
). Nevertheless, our results are also valid for regimes where the detailed balance-like relations apply.

For
zero driving,

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}=\delta_{\nu 0}\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}
. Accordingly,
(
5
) boil down into

\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\neq j}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}\,e^{-\beta E_{j^{\prime}}}\;=\;e^{-\beta E_{j}}\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\neq j}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj};

(6)

for all
1\leq j\leq N
. Properties (
6
) represent nothing else than the
thermalization conditions encountered in the theory of non-driven open quantum systems
Alicki et al. 2023
;
Blum and Gelbwaser-Klimovsky 2025
.
We have thus shown that unitarity of the total (system + bath) dynamics implies the thermalization of a non-driven system that undergoes a non-unitary reduced dynamics. This represents a mechanism of emergence of thermalization of a reduced system under a total unitary dynamics. Its connection with other mechanisms such as the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH)
O’Donovan et al. 2025
;
D’Alessio et al. 2016
should be further explored. An analogy between (
6
) and
(
5
) motivates us to hereafter call the more
general relations (
5
) as the Floquet
thermalization conditions.
We note that constraints (
5
) are
fundamental
preconditions that should be fulfilled by the rates of the Pauli dynamics of any real physical
N
-level system coupled to a dilute thermal gas.

II.3
Structure of the NESS

The Floquet thermalization conditions (
5
) can also be redisplayed as follows:

\sum_{j^{\prime}}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}\,e^{-\beta E^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}0}}\hskip-1.42262pt\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}\;=\;e^{-\beta E^{\rm QE}_{j0}}\,\sum_{j^{\prime}}\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}.

(7)

where

\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}=a_{jj^{\prime}}^{-1}\sum_{\nu}\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}\,e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}=\frac{\sum_{\nu}a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}\,e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}}{\sum_{\nu}a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}}.

(8)

\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}
represents a measure of how much the NESS deviates from the thermal state due to the driving.
This becomes clearer after using (
7
) and rewriting Eq. (
3
) as

\hskip-7.11317pt\sum_{j^{\prime}}a_{jj^{\prime}}\left(\frac{\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j^{\prime}}}{\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}}-e^{-\beta({E}^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt0}-{E}^{\rm QE}_{j0})}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\right)=0\;.

(9)

If
\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}=1
for all
j
and
j^{\prime}
, (
9
) implies that the steady state is thermal. This is the case for the non-driven case, and also for the high temperature limit where
\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}\rightarrow 1
. Therefore, at infinite temperature, the steady state is thermal which has a uniform population distribution,
\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}=1/N
. This uniformity can also be deduced from the Floquet thermalization conditions. Namely, for
\beta=0
, Eq. (
7
) implies that the transition rates are symmetric in the sense that the sum of transition rates of the processes flowing out of state
j
is balanced by the sum of transition rates of processes going into state
j
. Such symmetry results in a steady state with a homogeneous distribution.

For a thermal steady state, in average no energy is exchanged with either the bath or the driving.
The energy exchange with the driving is comprised of multiple processes, each involving a specific number of Floquet excitations,
\nu
. The probability of each such process is the product between the state population,
\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}
, and the transition rate
a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}
. Using the fact that at infinite temperatures the steady state is proportional to the identity, i.e.,
it is a thermal state, a zero energy exchange with the driving imposes a further constraint on the transition rates at

\beta=0
. Namely,

\hbar\omega\sum_{j\neq j}\,\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=+\infty}\nu\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}\,\Biggr|_{\beta=0}=0.

(10)

Our just given thermodynamic argument makes the result independent of the driving strength, frequency and the Hamiltonian, and therefore it can be universally applied to any driving. Eq. (
10
) can also be derived in an
alternative manner not relying on thermodynamics, as a mere consequence of the Floquet thermalization conditions (see S.3 in the supplemental material). Moreover, we also derive an even stronger constraint,
\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}=\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu}|_{\beta=0}
.

Eq. (
10
) has important physical consequences. Namely, it implies that the
NESS
is thermal also for small values of
\beta
, that is, for high but finite temperatures. To demonstrate this, we carry out a Taylor expansion of Eq. (
9
) around
\beta=0
(see S.5 in the supplemental material).
Notice that the first order expansion term of
\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}
is proportional to the l.h.s. of Eq. (
10
). Defining
\wp^{\rm corr}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}
as the first order correction to the steady state population rates, that is,
\frac{\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j^{\prime}}}{\wp^{\rm NESS}_{j}}=1+\beta\wp^{\rm corr}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}+O(\beta^{2})
, we get

\displaystyle\wp^{\rm corr}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-({E}^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}0}-{E}^{\rm QE}_{j0})\;+\;\frac{\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j,\nu}\nu\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}}{\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j,\nu}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}}\;=

(11)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-({E}^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}0}-{E}^{\rm QE}_{j0});

where we used the fact that no energy is exchanged with the driving field at
\beta=0
, i.e., Eq. (
10
), to obtain the second equality. The r.h.s. of Eq. (
11
) is nothing but the first order correction to the thermal state. Therefore, the NESS equals to the thermal state up to
{\cal O}(\beta)
(see Fig.
2
).
This has important implications for any practical realization of a periodically driven system, because the strength of the driving is always limited. Once the maximal driving strength is determined, one should be careful to avoid working at too high temperatures, otherwise the steady state will be thermal. The precise meaning of “too high temperatures” can be estimated by calculating the second order
\beta
-expansion of Eq. (
9
) and comparing it to the first order correction. If

\beta\;\ll\;\frac{2\,\left({E}^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}0}-{E}^{\rm QE}_{j0}\right)}{\frac{d^{2}\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}}{d\beta^{2}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}+\left({E}^{\rm QE}_{j^{\prime}0}-{E}^{\rm QE}_{j0}\right)^{\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt2}}\;\;\;;

(12)

then the NESS is well approximated by a thermal state (see Fig. 3).
The effects of driving on determining the high temperature regime are captured here by

\frac{d^{2}\left\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\right\rangle_{\hskip-1.42262ptjj^{\prime}}}{d\beta^{2}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}
.

In contrast to the high temperatures behavior, at low temperature

III
Numerical test

We have tested our
theoretical findings using a toy model of a periodically driven three level system interacting with a dilute thermal gas. The Hamiltonian of our driven three level system takes the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm S}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(E_{3}+\lambda\cos(\omega t)\Big)\,|3\rangle\langle 3|\;+\;E_{2}\,|2\rangle\langle 2|

(13)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\Big(E_{1}-\lambda\cos(\omega t)\Big)\,|1\rangle\langle 1|\;\;;

here
\omega
represents the driving frequency, and
\lambda
controls the driving strength. The interaction with
the low density thermal gas is described by a repulsive localized potential that acts on the gas particle and a system operator that is not diagonal in the eigenbasis of
\hat{H}_{\rm S}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)
(for details, see S.6 in the supplemental material). The corresponding
\widehat{T}-
matrix elements can be numerically calculated as detailed in S.6 in the supplemental material, where also all values of the parameters of the model are included. Using Eq. (
2
) the transition rates can finally be obtained. Hereafter we shall adopt the following system parameters:
E_{1}=-0.5
,
E_{2}=0.0
,
E_{3}=0.4
,
\omega=1.35
. These parameters are used for Figs. 1-4, in Figs. 1-2 and 4 we set in addition
\lambda=0.5
.

In summary, we have studied a generic periodically driven
N
-level quantum system interacting with a low-density thermal gas.
We have found that unitarity of the scattering matrix (
4
) gives rise to the
Floquet thermalization conditions (
5
), constraining the transition rates

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
of equation (
2
). This forces the NESS to be close to a thermal state at high temperatures and suppresses the steady energy exchange with the external driving and the steady heat flow with the bath. Therefore, we conclude that high temperatures reduce the possibility of driving a system away from thermal equilibrium. The just-mentioned finding holds universally for
any driving and strength of the system-bath coupling
. Moreover, we note that our quantum mechanical derivation of the
standard thermalization conditions (
6
), valid for nondriven systems, represents
a novel insightful contribution of its own right, with potential implications for the field of emergence of thermalization. Finally, conditions (
5
) also represent a new tool to test the accuracy of numerical calculations.

S1
Floquet scattering theory and the transition rates

S1.a
Floquet theory for a driven system

Let us consider a quantum system which is exposed to an external time
periodic driving. Let
\hat{H}_{\rm S}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)
be its Hamiltonian.
We have
\hat{H}_{\rm s}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)=\hat{H}_{\rm s}\hskip-0.7113pt(t+T)
, where
T=\frac{2\pi}{\omega}
is the period of driving,
and
\omega
the associated frequency.

Within the framework of Floquet theory, the time variable
t
is treated as an additional degree of freedom (an additional coordinate)
Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
.
Correspondingly, one introduces the Floquet Hamiltonian
\widehat{H}_{\rm F}=\hat{H}_{\rm s}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)-i\hbar\,\partial_{t}
acting in an extended
state space
{\cal H}_{F}={\cal H}_{\rm s}\otimes{\cal H}_{\rm extra}
. Here
{\cal H}_{\rm s}

S3
The regime of high temperatures
\bm{(\beta\to 0)}

The Floquet thermalization conditions discussed above, Eq.
S22
and Eq. (
5
) in the main text, enable us to draw strong conclusions
regarding the properties of the NESS of our driven system at high temperatures
(\beta\to 0)
.
Recall that the NESS is generally obtained from the Pauli NESS equation and depends on the rates
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}
. In this section we explore the behavior of these rates in the regime of
\beta\to 0
.

S3..1
Preparations

Consider the momentum integrals

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p^{\prime}\;e^{-\beta\frac{{\bm{p}}^{2}}{2m}}\;\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)\,\Bigl|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\Bigr|^{2}

(S23)

in
\beta\to 0
regime. These momentum integrals scale for
\beta\to 0
as
\beta^{-\zeta}
, where the exponent
\zeta\geq 0

depends upon concrete nature of the quantum system – particle coupling. In particular, value of
\zeta=0
refers to situations when the Floquet

\widehat{T}
-matrix elements
|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle|^{2}
fall off to zero
rapidly enough at high energies, such that the entities (
S23
) converge at
\beta=0
. We shall however allow for more general
setups with
\zeta>0
, where integrals (
S23
) converge only due to presence of the Boltzmann factor

e^{-\beta\frac{{\bm{p}}^{2}}{2m}}
(with
\beta>0
serving as an ultraviolet regulator).

Observe now that the Floquet rates
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
of equation (
S3
) scale for
\beta\to 0

as
\beta^{d/2}\,\beta^{-\zeta}
, where
\beta^{d/2}
arises due to presence of the partition function prefactor
Z^{-1}
.
This motivates us to set conveniently

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}=Z^{-1}\,\beta^{-\zeta}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
, where
\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
is finite at
\beta=0
,
and even Taylor expandable around
\beta=0
. Such that

\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\;=\;\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}+\;\beta\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\beta}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}+\;{\cal O}(\beta^{2})\;\;\;.

(S24)

Analogous statements apply then of course also for the total rates

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\;=\;\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=+\infty}a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S25)

Namely, one writes

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}=Z^{-1}\,\beta^{-\zeta}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}
, with

\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\;=\;\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=+\infty}\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\;=\;\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}+\;\beta\,\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\beta}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}+\;{\cal O}(\beta^{2})\;\;\;.

(S26)

The Floquet thermalization conditions (
5
) can be expressed immediately in terms of the just
redefined rates (
S24
). We have

\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\,\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu^{\prime}}\,e^{-\beta E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm QE}}\;-\;e^{-\beta E_{j0}^{\rm QE}}\,\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{+\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt}\;=\;0\;\;\;.\hskip 14.22636pt(1\leq j\leq N)

(S27)

Setting
\beta=0
in (
S27
) yields then an important property

\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;-\;\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;=\;0\;\;\;.\hskip 14.22636pt(1\leq j\leq N)

(S28)

Moreover, after extracting the
{\cal O}(\beta^{1})
order from (
S27
) one gets an intermediate outcome

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\beta}\,\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;-\;\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\,\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\,(E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt0}^{\rm QE}+\nu^{\prime}\hbar\omega)\;=

(S29)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\frac{{\rm d}}{{\rm d}\beta}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj\hskip-0.7113pt}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;-\;\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\,\tilde{a}_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{(\nu^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\,E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\;\;\;;

which, after summing up over
j
, gives rise to another useful identity

\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=1}^{N}\sum_{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=-\infty}^{\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt=+\infty}\,\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}(\nu^{\prime}\hbar\omega)\;=\;0\;\;\;.

(S30)

The just demonstrated general statement (
S30
) can be made even much stronger provided that the above discussed
integrals (
S23
) scale for
\beta\to 0
as
\beta^{-\zeta}
with
\zeta>0
. In that scenario, the dominant (and even
decisive) contribution to entities (
S23
) arises for
\beta\to 0
due to contributions of high energy scattering,
where the leading order Born approximation is applicable.
Mathematically, this requires that the
\widehat{T}
-matrix does not decay too rapidly to zero with the momentum.
Otherwise, the dominant contribution would not come from the high energies. The behavior of the
\widehat{T}
-matrix at high energies
depends upon a particular problem under study. In the case of our toy model with
\delta
-type potentials, nontrivial scattering
occurs at arbitrarily high energies, and the just mentioned condition is thus fulfilled. Accordingly, instead of (
S23
) we are essentially dealing
with integrals

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p^{\prime}\;e^{-\beta\frac{{\bm{p}}^{2}}{2m}}\;\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}\right)\,\Bigl|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{V}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\Bigr|^{2}\;\;\;;

(S31)

where
\widehat{V}=\hat{V}
is the pertinent Floquet operator of the target – particle coupling. In (
S31
),
we have also replaced
\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)
by mere

\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}\right)
, since the difference between
\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j\nu}^{\rm QE}

and mere
\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}
becomes immaterial at high scattering energies. Importantly, when combining (
S31
)
with the Brillouin zone symmetry property

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{V}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;=\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt0)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{V}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j,-\nu)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;\;\;;

(S32)

we infer that, inevitably,

\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;=\;\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{-\nu^{\prime}}\Bigr|_{\beta=0}\;\;\;;\hskip 14.22636pt(\zeta>0)

(S33)

and this is indeed a much stronger statement than above relation (
S30
). See figure
S1
for a numeric confirmation of the property (
S33
).

Figure S1:
An example of the Floquet transition rates
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{-\nu}
, given by equation (
2
) of the main text, and plotted
against the inverse temperature
\beta
. The particular rates presented here exhibit the behavior predicted by Eq. (
S33
).
Rates for other combinations of
(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt,j)
behave in a qualitatively similar manner. Parameters of the calculation are the same as those used
in the main text
(\lambda=0.5)
.

S4
The regime of low temperatures:
\bm{(\beta\to\infty)}

We start by recalling the definition of the rates as outlined in the manuscript:

\displaystyle a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\cal N}\,Z^{-1}\hskip-1.42262pt\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p^{\prime}\;e^{-\beta\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}}\,\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)\,\Bigl|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\Bigr|^{2}\;\;\;.

(S34)

Here
{\cal N}
stands for the gas particle density,
Z=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p\;e^{-\beta\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}}

for the gas partition function, and
\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle
are the on-shell
Floquet
\widehat{T}
-matrix elements, encoding all information about scattering of a single gas particle on the driven
N
-level system.
For
\beta\to\infty
, the Boltzmann factor
e^{-\beta\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}}
is significantly nonzero
only if the kinetic energy of the incoming gas particles is very small
\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}\to 0\right)
.
Therefore, the only significant contribution to the integral (
S34
) comes from very low energy scattering.

Let us analyze the low energy behavior of the pertinent on-shell
\widehat{T}
-matrix element

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\widehat{T}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;=\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,+\,\widehat{V}\,\frac{1}{\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}+E_{j0}^{\rm QE}-\widehat{H}+i\,0_{+}}\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{p}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;\;\;.

(S35)

In the low energy regime of
\frac{\bm{p}^{2}}{2\,m}\to 0
, the quantity (
S35
) reduces to

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,+\,\widehat{V}\,\frac{1}{E_{j0}^{\rm QE}-\widehat{H}+i\,0_{+}}\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{0}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\;\;\;;

(S36)

this is a finite number independent of
\bm{p}
. Accordingly, the integral can be simplified into

{\cal N}\;\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}^{d}p^{\prime}\;\delta\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\frac{\bm{p}^{\prime 2}}{2\,m}+E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}-E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\right)\,\left|\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\bm{p}^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu)|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,+\,\widehat{V}\,\frac{1}{E_{j0}^{\rm QE}-\widehat{H}+i\,0_{+}}\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\bm{0}\,(j0)\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-0.7113pt\rangle\right|^{2}\;\;\;.

(S37)

If so, then our Floquet rates
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}
of Eq. (
S34
) inevitably converge to well defined zero temperature limits.
Equation (
S37
) gives zero whenever
E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}>E_{j0}^{\rm QE}
(for channels closed at zero temperature),
and a finite nonzero value whenever
E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\nu}^{\rm QE}<E_{j0}^{\rm QE}
(for channels open at zero temperature).
The associated total rates

a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\hskip-0.7113pt(\beta\to\infty)\;=\;\sum_{\nu}\,a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}^{\nu}\hskip-0.7113pt(\beta\to\infty)

(S38)

can serve us then for determination of the low temperature NESS asymptotes
\wp_{j}\hskip-0.7113pt(\beta\to\infty)

which we see in Fig. 2 of the main text. We recall that
E_{j^{\prime}\nu}^{\rm QE}=E_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt0}^{\rm QE}+\nu\hbar\omega
where
\nu\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Therefore, for every
j
and
j^{\prime}
there exists some
\nu_{0}
such that
E_{j^{\prime}\nu<\nu_{0}}^{\rm QE}<E_{j0}^{\rm QE}
,
meaning that
\nu<\nu_{0}
are open channels contributing to
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\hskip-0.7113pt(\beta\to\infty)
.
Thus
a_{j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113ptj}\hskip-0.7113pt(\beta\to\infty)\neq 0
for any
j
and
j^{\prime}
, and the pertinent NESS deviates from the ground state.

S5
Taylor Expansion of the steady state equation (
9
)

In this section we Taylor expand around
\beta=0
the term

\sum_{j^{\prime}}a_{jj^{\prime}}\Bigl(\frac{\wp_{j^{\prime}}}{\wp_{j}}-e^{-\beta(E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\Bigr)\;\;\;;

(S39)

which corresponds to l.h.s. of equation (
9
) used to find the NESS.
For this purpose, we start by writing the Taylor expansion of
\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}
,

\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}=1+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\frac{d^{2}}{d^{2}\beta}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\;\;\;;

(S40)

where we have already used the fact that

\frac{d}{d\beta}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\propto\hbar\omega\sum_{\nu=-\infty}^{\nu=+\infty}\nu\,a_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}=0\;\;\;.

(S41)

The last equality is obtained by using Eq. (
S30
).
Next, we define the Taylor expansion of the population rates,

\frac{\wp_{j^{\prime}}}{\wp_{j}}=\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm\beta=0}}+\beta\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm(1)}}+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm(2)}}\;\;\;.

(S42)

The zero-order expansion reads as

\sum_{\nu}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm\beta=0}}-1\right)\;\;\;.

(S43)

The first order is

\beta\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\frac{d}{d\beta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}|_{\beta=0}\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm\beta=0}}-1\right)+\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}|_{\beta=0}\beta\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm(1)}}+(E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})\right)\;\;\;.

(S44)

And the second order expansion comes out as

\displaystyle\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\sum_{\nu}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\frac{d^{2}}{d^{2}\beta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu}|_{\beta=0}\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm\beta=0}}-1\right)+\beta\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\frac{d}{d\beta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}|_{\beta=0}\beta\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm(1)}}+(E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})\right)+

\displaystyle\sum_{\nu^{\prime}}Z^{-1}\beta^{-\zeta}\tilde{a}_{jj^{\prime}}^{\nu^{\prime}}|_{\beta=0}\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left(\wp_{j^{\prime}j}^{{\rm(2)}}-(E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})^{2}-\frac{d^{2}}{d^{2}\beta}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\right)\;\;\;.

(S45)

From these equations one can infer that, at steady state,

\frac{\wp_{j^{\prime}}^{{\rm NESS}}}{\wp_{j}^{{\rm NESS}}}=1-\beta(E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})+\frac{\beta^{2}}{2}\left((E_{j^{\prime}0}^{QE}-E_{j0}^{QE})^{2}+\frac{d^{2}}{d^{2}\beta}\langle e^{\beta\nu\hbar\omega}\rangle_{jj^{\prime}}\right)+O(\beta^{3})\;\;\;.

(S46)

Equation (
S46
) leads immediately to condition (
12
) from the main text.

S6
Toy model and computational algorithm

In this section we define the toy model and numerical algorithm that we used to test our analytical results. We emphasize however that our analytic results are valid for any non-degenerate
N
-level system and for any periodic driving.

S6.a
Free three level system

The system Hamiltonian reads as

\hat{H}_{\rm T}\;=\;\sum_{j=1}^{3}\,|j\rangle_{\rm T}\;E_{j}\;{}_{\rm T}\langle j|\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S47)

where subscript
T
refers to ”target”. In our numerical calculations, we set
E_{1}=-0.5
,
E_{2}=0.0
,
E_{3}=+0.4
.

Using an alternative notation, one may write equivalently

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm T}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\rm T}\;E_{3}\;{}_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{3}|

(S48)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\rm T}\;E_{2}\;{}_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\varphi_{1}\rangle_{\rm T}\;E_{1}\;{}_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{1}|\hskip 14.22636pt.

S6.b
Periodically driven three level system

We choose a simple periodic driving. The Hamiltonian of our driven three level system takes then the form

\displaystyle\hat{H}_{\rm T}\hskip-0.7113pt(t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle|\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\rm T}\;\Bigl(E_{3}\,+\,\lambda\,\cos(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt)\Bigr)\;_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{3}|

(S49)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\varphi_{2}\rangle_{\rm T}\;\hskip 7.11317ptE_{2}\hskip 62.59596pt\;{}_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\varphi_{1}\rangle_{\rm T}\;\Bigl(E_{1}\,-\,\lambda\,\cos(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt)\Bigr)\;_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{1}|\hskip 14.22636pt.

In our numerical calculations, the driving frequency
\omega=1.35
, and the driving strength
\lambda=0.5
unless stated otherwise.

The associated Floquet eigenproblem
Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
can be solved analytically in the
t^{\prime}
-domain.
We write as usual

\Bigl(\hat{H}_{\rm T}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\,-\,i\hbar\,\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Bigr)\,|\phi_{j0}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\,|\phi_{j0}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S50)

where
j\in\{1,2,3\}
. Let us work out separately the case of
j=3
, that is,

\Bigl(\hat{H}_{\rm T}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\,-\,i\hbar\,\partial_{t^{\prime}}\Bigr)\,|\phi_{30}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;E_{30}^{\rm QE}\,|\phi_{30}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S51)

Explicit appearance of the Hamiltonian (
S49
) enables us to employ an obvious ansatz

|\phi_{30}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})}\,|\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S52)

here the as yet unknown factor
f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})
is required to be
t^{\prime}
-periodic.
Substitution of (
S52
) into (
S51
) yields the condition

E_{3}\;+\;\lambda\,\cos(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\;+\;\partial_{t^{\prime}}f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})\;=\;E_{30}^{\rm QE}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S53)

Showing that (
S51
) holds iff

f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})\;=\;\Bigl(E_{30}^{\rm QE}\,-\,E_{3}\Bigr)\,t^{\prime}\;-\;\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\;+\;{\cal C}\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S54)

where
{\cal C}
is a constant of integration.
The already mentioned requirement of
t^{\prime}
-periodicity of
f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})
implies however
{\cal C}=0
, and also

E_{30}^{\rm QE}\;=\;E_{3}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S55)

Then of course

f_{3}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime})\;=\;-\,\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S56)

and, accordingly,

|\phi_{30}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)}\,|\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S57)

Our
j=3
Floquet eigenproblem (
S51
) is thus explicitly resolved, with the final outcome

E_{30}^{\rm QE}\;=\;E_{3}\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636pt|\phi_{30}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)}\,|\varphi_{3}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S58)

Completely analogous reasoning gives the remaining Floquet eigensolutions, namely,

E_{10}^{\rm QE}\;=\;E_{1}\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636pt|\phi_{10}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)}\,|\varphi_{1}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S60)

|\phi_{j\nu}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{+i\nu\omega t^{\prime}}\,|\phi_{j0}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt;

The Floquet problem corresponding to Hamiltonian (

We recall that the

Concerning the eigenvectors, we write jointly

|\phi_{j\nu}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}\;=\;e^{+i\nu\omega t^{\prime}}\,e^{-(j-2)\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)}\,|\varphi_{j}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt.

Instead of the
|\phi_{j\nu}(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)\rangle_{\rm T}

S6.d
Computation of the
\bm{\widehat{T}}
-matrix elements by means of the Floquet scattering theory

Our formulation given below is obtained via merging the Floquet scattering theory of
Refs.
Peskin and Moiseyev 1994
;
Lefebvre and Atabek 2005
together with specific features of scattering in our toy model as detailed
in Ref.
Alicki et al. 2023
.
In what follows, we are using either the direct product basis set

|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,x\,\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;|x\rangle_{\rm P}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt;

(S78)

or an equivalent direct product basis set

|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,p\,\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;|x\rangle_{\rm P}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt;

(S79)

here subscript
P
refers to ”particle”. Let
|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle
be the total scattering eigenfunction
of the whole ensemble ”particle + target”.
We set correspondingly

\Psi_{j\nu}\hskip-0.7113pt(x)\;=\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\,x\,\phi_{j\nu}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 14.22636pt,\hskip 14.22636pt\tilde{\Psi}_{j\nu}\hskip-0.7113pt(p)\;=\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\,p\,\phi_{j\nu}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S80)

Let us develop now the Floquet scattering formalism based upon the implicit Lippmann-Schwinger equation (LSE)

|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi^{(pj\nu)}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,p\,\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;+\;\frac{1}{E_{pj\nu}^{\rm QE}-\widehat{H}_{0}+i\varepsilon}\;\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi^{(pj\nu)}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt;

(S81)

where

\widehat{H}_{0}=\hat{H}_{\rm P}+\hat{H}_{\rm T}\hskip-0.7113pt(t^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)
,
\widehat{V}=\hat{V}
, and

E_{pj\nu}^{\rm QE}\;=\;\frac{p^{2}}{2\,m}\;+\;E_{j0}^{\rm QE}\;+\;\nu\,\hbar\omega\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S82)

Our ultimate goal is to access the
\widehat{T}
-matrix elements

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle p^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,\hskip-1.42262pt\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{T}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,p\,\phi_{j\nu}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle p^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,\hskip-1.42262pt\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi^{(pj\nu)}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S83)

In the momentum representation, the implicit LSE (
S81
) takes the form

\tilde{\Psi}_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(p^{\prime})\;=\;\delta(p-p^{\prime})\,\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}\;+\;\frac{1}{E_{pj\nu}^{\rm QE}-E_{p^{\prime}j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm QE}+i\varepsilon}\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle p^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,\hskip-1.42262pt\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi^{(pj\nu)}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt.\hskip 7.11317pt[\varepsilon\to+0]

(S84)

Direct calculation based upon (
S83
) yields explicitly the associated

\widehat{T}
-matrix elements. One gets

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle p^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,\hskip-1.42262pt\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{V}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\Psi^{(pj\nu)}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}\,\sum_{\iota=0}^{2}\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}p^{\prime}q_{\iota}}\hskip-1.42262pt\left(\sum_{\nu}\,\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\chi_{\iota}\,\nu\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;V_{\iota}\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\chi_{\iota}\,\nu|\hskip-0.7113pt|\phi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\right)\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S85)

Showing that all the information about scattering is encoded
just in the wavefunction amplitudes
\{\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\}
.

To simplify our subsequent notations, we define for convenience a shorthand symbol

{\cal V}_{\iota}^{(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})(j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime})}\;=\;\sum_{\nu}\,\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\chi_{\iota}\,\nu\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;V_{\iota}\;\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle\chi_{\iota}\,\nu|\phi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S86)

Direct calculation yields

{\cal V}^{(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})(j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime})}\;=\;\left(\frac{1}{T}\hskip-1.42262pt\int_{0}^{T}\hskip-1.42262pt\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}t^{\prime}\;e^{-i(\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt-\nu^{\prime\prime})\omega t^{\prime}}\,e^{+(j^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt-j^{\prime\prime})\frac{i}{\hbar}\frac{\lambda}{\omega}\,\sin(\omega\,\hskip-0.7113ptt^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)}\right)\,{\cal V}^{(j^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime})}\hskip 14.22636pt;

(S87)

where by definition

{\cal V}^{(j^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime})}\;=\;\sum_{\iota}\,{}_{\rm T}\langle\varphi_{j^{\prime}}|\chi_{\iota}\rangle_{\rm T}\;V_{\iota}\;{}_{\rm T}\hskip-0.7113pt\langle\chi_{\iota}|\varphi_{j^{\prime\prime}}\rangle_{\rm T}\hskip 14.22636pt.

(S88)

In the position representation, the implicit LSE (
S84
) takes then the form

\displaystyle\Psi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}px}}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,\hbar}}\,\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}

(S89)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,\hbar}}\,\sum_{\iota^{\prime}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}p^{\prime}\;\frac{e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}p^{\prime}(x-q_{\iota^{\prime}})}}{E_{pj\nu}^{\rm QE}-E_{p^{\prime}j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm QE}+i\varepsilon}\;\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}{\cal V}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})(j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime})}\,\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota^{\prime}})\hskip 7.11317pt.

After setting
x=q_{\iota}
in (
S89
) one arrives towards a set of coupled linear equations
for the unknown wavefunction amplitudes
\{\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\}
. Namely, we have

\displaystyle\Psi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}pq_{\iota}}}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,\hbar}}\,\delta_{jj^{\prime}}\,\delta_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}

(S90)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\,\pi\,\hbar}}\,\sum_{\iota^{\prime}}\,\int_{-\infty}^{+\infty}\hskip-1.42262pt{\rm d}p^{\prime}\;\frac{e^{+\frac{i}{\hbar}p^{\prime}(q_{\iota}-q_{\iota^{\prime}})}}{E_{pj\nu}^{\rm QE}-E_{p^{\prime}j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{\rm QE}+i\varepsilon}\;\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}{\cal V}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})(j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime})}\,\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota^{\prime}})\hskip 7.11317pt;

this is required to hold for all
(\iota,j^{\prime},\nu^{\prime})
assuming a fixed combination of input labels
(pj\nu)
.

For any fixed
(pj\nu)
, the system of linear equations (
S90

provided that ranges of all the involved Floquet
(\nu^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt,\nu^{\prime\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt)
-indices are consistently truncated.
We recall in this context that all the numerics needs to be performed just for the case of
\nu=0
, since

\Psi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}^{(pj\nu)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\;=\;\Psi_{j^{\prime}(\nu^{\prime}-\nu)}^{(pj0)}\hskip-1.42262pt(q_{\iota})\hskip 7.11317pt;

(S91)

Moreover, the only output required for the physical theory of scattering

\{\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj0)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\}
.
Having in hand
\{\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj0)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\}
, the resulting
\widehat{T}
-matrix elements of the extended theory
are accessed by means of (
S83
) and (
S85
). That is,

\langle\hskip-1.42262pt\langle p^{\prime}\hskip-0.7113pt\,\hskip-1.42262pt\phi_{j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime}}|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,\widehat{T}\,|\hskip-0.7113pt|\,p\,\phi_{j0}\,\rangle\hskip-1.42262pt\rangle\;=\;\sum_{\iota}\,e^{-\frac{i}{\hbar}p^{\prime}q_{\iota}}\,\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}\,{\cal V}_{\iota}^{(j^{\prime}\nu^{\prime})(j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime})}\;\Psi_{j^{\prime\prime}\nu^{\prime\prime}}^{(pj0)}\hskip-0.7113pt(q_{\iota})\hskip 7.11317pt.

(S92)

Equation (
S92
) represents the final outcome of the scattering formalism in the extended setup
where
q_{\iota}\neq 0

Namely, instead of (

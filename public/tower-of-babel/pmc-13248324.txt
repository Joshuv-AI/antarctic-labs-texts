PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration complements PD-L1 to predict first-line chemo-immunotherapy outcomes in advanced ESCC
Authors: Qian Zhao, Shuping Cheng, Yuliang Meng, Butuo Li, Linlin Wang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Only a subset of patients with esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC) benefits from first-line immunochemotherapy, and PD-L1 alone has limited predictive value, underscoring the need for complementary biomarkers. We analyzed pretreatment FFPE biopsies from 147 ESCC patients treated with PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy using multiplex immunofluorescence (CD4, CD8, CD20, CD68, PD-1, PD-L1, DAPI). PFS and OS were assessed by Kaplan–Meier and Cox models; cut-offs were derived by ROC/Youden analyses. With a median follow-up of 32.2 months, median PFS and OS were 6.7 and 17.0 months. PD-L1 was predominantly expressed on tumor cells, whereas PD-1 was localized mainly to tumor-infiltrating immune cells. In multivariable analysis, high PD-L1 expression independently associated with longer PFS (HR 0.210, 95%CI: 0.060–0.731; p = 0.014), whereas high PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration predicted shorter PFS (HR 2.694, 95%CI: 1.162–6.246; p = 0.021). Combined stratification identified the longest PFS in high PD-L1 expression and low PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration (8.8 months) and the shortest in low PD-L1 and high PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration (3.5 months), supporting PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration might as a complementary biomarker to PD-L1. For OS, intratumoral CD8+ T cell density (HR 0.896; p = 0.011), clinical stage (HR 1.570; p = 0.025), and BMI (HR 0.935; p = 0.015) were independent factors.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40364-026-00934-y.

To the editor,

Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) have altered the treatment landscape of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (ESCC); however, clinical benefit remains limited to a subset of patients. PD-L1 correlates with improved outcomes but has limited predictive value [1]. Therefore, complementary tissue biomarkers are needed. As immunotherapy efficacy is shaped by the tumor microenvironment (TME), immune infiltrates, including tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes and macrophages, have been associated with outcomes in ESCC [2, 3]. PD-1+ immune infiltration has been associated with benefit from PD-1 blockade in other malignancies, but evidence in ESCC remains limited [4–7].

We retrospectively analyzed pretreatment formalin-fixed, paraffin-embedded biopsies from 147 patients with unresectable locally advanced or metastatic ESCC treated with first-line ICI plus chemotherapy (Supplementary Table 1, Fig. 1A). After a median follow-up of 32.2 months, median PFS (mPFS) and mOS were 6.7 and 17.0 months, respectively. Multiplex immunofluorescence demonstrated PD-L1 predominantly on tumor cells, whereas PD-1 localized mainly to tumor-infiltrating immune cells (Fig. 1B). Cut-offs were defined by ROC curve analysis (Youden index), classifying 44.9% of patients as high PD-L1 expression and 35.4% as high PD-1 expression (Supplementary Figure 2). Kaplan-Meier analyses showed longer mPFS for patients with high vs low PD-L1 expression (7.5 vs 5.5 months; p = 0.003) and for patients with low versus high PD-1 expression (7.2 vs 5.8 months; p = 0.050), whereas neither marker was significantly associated with OS (Fig. 1C). In univariable Cox models, high PD-L1 expression was associated with prolonged PFS (HR 0.266, 95%CI: 0.087–0.816; p = 0.021), high PD-1 expression with shorter PFS (HR 1.112, 95%CI: 1.014–1.219; p = 0.024), and higher CD8+ T cell infiltration with improved OS (HR 0.901, 95%CI: 0.829–0.979; p = 0.014) (Supplementary Table 2).

Given the immune-restricted distribution of PD-1, we resolved PD-1 across immune subsets. Among PD-1+ immune cells, CD4+ T cells were most frequent (50.6%), followed by CD8+ T cells (23.4%), macrophages (21.7%), and B cells (4.3%) (Fig. 1D, Supplementary Figure 3). PD-L1 expression was not significantly correlated with PD-1+CD8+ T cells, PD-1+CD4+ T cells, PD-1+CD20+ B cells, or PD-1+CD68+ macrophages infiltration (Supplementary Figure 4). Using ROC/Youden-derived cut-offs, infiltration densities of each PD-1+ immune cell subset were classified into high and low groups. In univariable analyses across these PD-1+ subsets, only PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration was significantly associated with PFS (Supplementary Table 3). Specifically, the high PD-1+CD8+ infiltration group (44.2%, n = 65) had shorter mPFS than the low-infiltration group (5.5 vs 7.6 months; p < 0.001) (Supplementary Figure 5, Fig. 1E). In the pre-immunotherapy setting, increased intratumoral PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration has been consistently associated with poor prognosis, consistent with CD8+ T cell exhaustion and reduced cytotoxic capacity [8–10]. Under immunotherapy, PD-1+ T cells have been associated with improved efficacy and are often viewed as direct targets of PD-1 blockade [4–7]. In our ESCC cohort, however, higher pretreatment PD-1+CD8+ T cell infiltration identified patients deriving inferior benefit from PD-1 inhibitors. Whether PD-1 blockade can reverse exhaustion and restore cytotoxic function remains uncertain, and definitive evidence for full restoration is lacking. Moreover, PD-1+CD8+ T cells accounted for only a small proportion of the immune infiltrate in our cohort, indicating that benefit may not rely solely on functional recovery. Prior studies report greater proliferative capacity in PD-1-CD8+ T cells and limited functional recovery of PD-1+CD8+ T cells under PD-1 blockade [11]. In murine head and neck cancer models, anti-PD-1 plus radiotherapy reduced PD-1highCD8+ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs) and increased PD-1low/intermediate TILs, paralleling the best treatment response [12]. Although this hypothesis may plausibly explain our findings, the dominant antitumor mechanisms of immunotherapy in ESCC remain incompletely understood, warranting further investigation. Several limitations should be acknowledged. This was a retrospective study with a limited sample size and no external validation; thus, the ROC/Youden-derived cut-offs should be interpreted cautiously and further validated in larger independent cohorts. Although a small number of stage II patients were included, they did not receive definitive-intent local treatment, and sensitivity analysis excluding them yielded results consistent with the primary analysis (Supplementary Table 4). Finally, PD-L1 expression was quantified using automated mIF image analysis rather than clinically validated CPS or TPS, and this metric should not be considered interchangeable with standard PD-L1 IHC scoring systems.

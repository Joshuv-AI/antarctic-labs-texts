Protocol for generating human iPSC-derived cardiomyocytes using VCAM1-based sorting and micropatterned culture
Authors: Marta Furtado, Marta Ribeiro, Teresa Carvalho, Sandra Martins, Maria Carmo-Fonseca
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryThe expanding use of induced pluripotent stem cell-derived cardiomyocytes (iPSC-CMs) in disease modeling and regenerative medicine has highlighted persistent challenges, including inter-differentiation variability, immature phenotypes, and heterogeneous cell populations. Here, we present a protocol for generating human iPSC-CMs by combining fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) of VCAM1-positive cells with culture on micropatterned substrates. We describe steps for inducing cardiac differentiation in 3D and enriching iPSC-CMs by FACS. We then detail procedures for maturing iPSC-CMs in 2D micropatterned culture.For complete details on the use and execution of this protocol, please refer to Gomes-Silva et al.1

Before you begin

Over the past decade, differentiation protocols for the generation of cardiomyocytes from iPSCs (iPSC-CMs) have been thoroughly optimized and are nowadays well-established, mainly relying on the use of small molecule-mediated modulation of the Wnt signaling pathway.2,3 However, in vitro differentiation of iPSC-CMs frequently produces heterogeneous cell populations and cardiomyocytes with variable structural and functional properties.4 The variability in cell populations and maturation state of iPSC-CMs generated through these methods remains a major challenge for disease modelling, often hindering reproducibility and the interpretation of results across studies.5,6 Multiple approaches have been explored to generate a pure cardiomyocyte population and enhance its maturation. However, these strategies are often expensive, time consuming, and include the use of complex maturation mediums7 or prolonged time in culture.8,9

Here, we describe a method to generate cardiomyocytes through temporal modulation of the Wnt signaling pathway in a three-dimensional culture system (Figure 1). VCAM-1 is expressed in the developing human myocardium10 and serves as a reliable surface marker to isolate highly enriched cardiomyocyte populations.11,12 To promote additional maturation, the resulting cardiomyocyte-enriched populations are maintained either under standard monolayer culture conditions or on micropatterned substrates.Figure 1Overview of cardiomyocyte differentiation workflow(A) Timeline and cell culture conditions.(B) Representative bright-field and immunofluorescence images showing expression of the indicated proteins from day -1 up to day 35 of cardiac differentiation. Innovation

By combining an optimized strategy for iPSC differentiation into cardiomyocytes in a three-dimensional culture system,13 fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) of VCAM1-positive cells11,12 and micropatterned culture substrates,14,15 this protocol enables consistent generation of highly cardiomyocyte-enriched populations reducing variability across differentiations and iPSC lines. A key innovation of our study is the incorporation of alternative splicing–based molecular metrics to assess cardiomyocyte developmental state (Gomes-Silva et al.1). Comparison of iPSC-CMs with in vivo human cardiac development provides a quantitative and developmentally informed framework to evaluate differentiation fidelity and maturation trajectories. Together, these features establish a standardized, molecularly validated platform for generating reproducible and developmentally benchmarked human iPSC-CMs, enabling more rigorous assessment of cardiomyocyte maturity and experimental reproducibility.

Step-by-step method details

Culture of iPSCs

Timing: 1 week CRITICAL: The quality of iPSC cultures is a key determinant of successful and reproducible cardiac differentiation. We recommend periodic karyotype analysis to ensure genomic stability. On a day-to-day basis, iPSC quality should be assessed by morphology, colony integrity, and growth dynamics. High-quality cultures display compact, well-defined colonies with smooth edges, minimal spontaneous differentiation, and a consistent proliferation rate. Cultures that deviate from these criteria should not be used for differentiation. In such cases, we recommend discarding the culture and restarting from a low-passage frozen stock. 1.Maintain iPSCs in mTeSR™ Plus medium, changing the medium daily or every other day depending on cell density and growth rate, with cultures typically passaged at 70%–80% confluency. Note: Prolonged culture (>10 passages) or excessive confluency (>90%) is associated with morphological changes indicative of spontaneous differentiation. 2.After thawing, passage iPSCs at least twice prior to differentiation. On the day of aggregate formation, iPSC cultures should be at 80% confluency.

Expected outcomes

This protocol yields cardiomyocyte-enriched populations that exhibit time-dependent maturation, which is further enhanced by mechanical cues provided by micropatterned substrates. As reported in Gomes-Silva et al.,1 single-cell RNA sequencing analysis showed that most cells (84%–88%) display a cardiomyocyte gene expression signature, while a smaller fraction (∼12%–16%) exhibits an epicardial-like identity, consistent with the bifurcation of cardiac progenitors into myocardial and epicardial lineages observed during cardiac development.16 Bulk RNA-sequencing analysis further demonstrates a high degree of reproducibility in gene expression profiles across independent iPSC lines, indicating that this protocol generates transcriptomically similar cell populations regardless of genetic background.

Cardiomyocyte maturation is evidenced by progressive sarcomere organization and elongation. Mean sarcomere length typically increases from ∼1.5 ± 0.2 μm at day 20 to ∼1.7 ± 0.3 μm at day 30 of differentiation (Figure 6A). Additional increases in sarcomere length may be observed following culture on micropatterned substrates (days 30–40), depending on the cell line (Figure 6B, adapted from Gomes-Silva et al.1). By day 30, iPSC-CMs display robust expression of key sarcomeric proteins, including α-actinin, MHC-β, cardiac troponin T (cTnT), and cardiac myosin-binding protein C (cMyBP-C), with well-defined and aligned striations (Figure 6C).Figure 6Structural maturation of iPSC-CMs generated using this protocol(A and B) Structural maturation is assessed by increased sarcomere length in cardiomyocytes derived from three iPSC lines, measured by immunofluorescence using an antibody against α-actinin (green) to label Z-discs and define individual sarcomere boundaries. Alternative splicing patterns further support molecular maturation. iPSC-CMs recapitulate developmental splicing transitions observed in prenatal human heart, with more mature profiles emerging between days 30 and 40, particularly under micropatterned culture conditions (Gomes-Silva et al.1).

Quantification and statistical analysis

Quantitative image analysis of sarcomere length for maturation assessment was performed using ImageJ (https://imagej.nih.gov/) standard plugins. Statistical analysis was performed using Brown-Forsythe and Welch ANOVA tests to account for unequal variances, followed by Games-Howell’s multiple comparisons test.

Limitations

Although this protocol enables reproducible generation of highly enriched cardiomyocyte populations and supports enhanced maturation, several limitations should be considered. First, differentiation efficiency, aggregate size, and optimal small-molecule concentrations (e.g., CHIR99021) remain partially dependent on iPSC line–specific characteristics. As a result, initial optimization may be required for new iPSC lines to achieve optimal outcomes.

Second, the use of fluorescence-activated cell sorting (FACS) introduces additional cost, technical complexity, and potential cell stress, which may impact yield and viability. Access to appropriate instrumentation and expertise is required, and sorting parameters may need to be optimized to balance purity and cell survival.

Third, a limitation of this protocol is the reliance on VCAM-1 as a purification marker, as VCAM-1 expression is not restricted to committed cardiomyocytes but is also detected in cardiac progenitors with myocardial and epicardial potential.16,17 Consequently, sorting enriches cardiomyocytes but may retain a small epicardial-like population.

Finally, while micropatterned substrates promote enhanced structural organization and maturation, iPSC-CMs generated using this protocol do not fully recapitulate the properties of adult human cardiomyocytes but instead retain features of a prenatal state (Gomes-Silva et al.1).

We highlight some of the main challenges with this method of iPSC-CM production and provide potential solutions.

Problem 1: Preparation of iPSC aggregates for cardiac differentiation (day −3)

Aggregates formed in Aggrewell plates are either too large or too small. Refer to Figure 2A for optimal aggregate size.

Potential solution

•This may result from suboptimal seeding density or variable proliferation rates among different iPSC lines (refer to Step 6). Optimize the number of cells plated for each iPSC line (we recommend testing between 0.8 and 1.1 × 106 cells). In addition, if iPSCs proliferate rapidly and aggregates reach the desired size earlier than expected, differentiation can be initiated after 2 days of maintenance in mTeSR™ Plus medium.

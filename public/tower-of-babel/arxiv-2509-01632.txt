Relative Trajectory Balance is equivalent to Trust-PCL
Tristan Deleu ​, Padideh Nouri ​, Yoshua Bengio ​, Doina Precup ​[1ex] Mila – Quebec AI Institute

Abstract
Recent progress in generative modeling has highlighted the importance of Reinforcement Learning (RL) for fine-tuning, with KL-regularized methods in particular proving to be highly effective for both autoregressive and diffusion models. Complementing this line of work, the Relative Trajectory Balance (RTB) objective was recently introduced in the context of Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) to serve the same role of improving fine - tuning in sequential generative models. Building on prior work linking GFlowNets and maximum - entropy RL, we establish in this paper an equivalence between RTB and Trust-PCL, an off-policy RL method with KL regularization. This equivalence situates RTB within the broader theoretical landscape of KL-regularized RL, and clarifies its relationship to earlier methods. Leveraging this insight, we revisit an illustrative example from the RTB paper and show that KL-regularized RL methods achieve comparable performance, offering an alternative perspective to what was previously reported.

Introduction

Generative models are playing an increasingly prominent role in artificial intelligence, ranging from language and reasoning
(
Brown et al. 2020
;
Wei et al. 2022
)
, to vision
(
Ho et al. 2020
;
Rombach et al. 2022
)
and scientific discovery
(
Abramson et al. 2024
)
. At the heart of this rapid progress, fine-tuning is central to aligning models with downstream applications while preserving diversity and prior behavior. To complement supervised fine-tuning
(
Ouyang et al. 2022
)
, Reinforcement Learning (RL) with KL regularization has proven effective and is widely used for this task
(
Jaques et al. 2017
;
Uehara et al. 2024
)
, where reward maximization is traded off against maintaining consistency with a pretrained model. In parallel, Generative Flow Networks
(
Bengio et al. 2021
;
Bengio et al. 2023
, GFlowNets;)
have been proposed as an alternative paradigm to train generative models with techniques inspired by RL. They have since gained popularity in areas such as combinatorial optimization
(
Zhang et al. 2023
)
, causal discovery
(
Deleu et al. 2022
)
, and scientific discovery
(
Jain et al. 2023
)
.

Extending ideas from GFlowNets, the
Relative Trajectory Balance
objective
(
Venkatraman et al. 2024
, RTB;)
was recently proposed as an alternative to KL-regularized RL for fine-tuning generative models, framing the problem in terms of Trajectory Balance
(
Malkin et al. 2022
)
. In this paper, we show that RTB is exactly equivalent to an existing RL algorithm called
Trust-PCL

(
Nachum et al. 2018
)
, reinforcing the strong ties existing between GFlowNets and (entropy-regularized) RL
(
Tiapkin et al. 2024
;
Mohammadpour et al. 2024
;
Deleu et al. 2024
;
Jiralerspong et al. 2025
)
. In particular, while
Venkatraman et al. 2024
suggested that KL-regularized RL methods may perform poorly even on simple tasks, we argue and show on their illustrative example that these issues stem from algorithmic and reward choices rather than from any fundamental limitation of RL with KL regularization itself. This new perspective places RTB within the well-established framework of KL-regularized RL and underscores its actual source of effectiveness.

2
Background

We study the problem of training a
sequential generative model
capable of sampling objects as a sequence of multiple steps. This covers, for example, the case of diffusion samplers
(
Sendera et al. 2024
)
that generate images
{\bm{x}}_{T}
as a sequence of denoising steps
{\bm{x}}_{1}\rightarrow{\bm{x}}_{2}\rightarrow\ldots\rightarrow{\bm{x}}_{T}
, starting from some Gaussian noise
{\bm{x}}_{1}\sim{\mathcal{N}}({\bm{0}},{\bm{I}})
and sequentially sampling from a denoising model
{\bm{x}}_{t+1}\sim P_{\phi}({\bm{x}}_{t+1}\mid{\bm{x}}_{t})
. By following this iterative process, we eventually obtain samples from the
marginal distribution
defined by

P_{\phi}^{\top}({\bm{x}}_{T})=\int P_{\phi}({\bm{x}}_{1},{\bm{x}}_{2},\ldots,{\bm{x}}_{T})\,d{\bm{x}}_{1:T-1}=\int\bigg[P({\bm{x}}_{1})\prod_{t=1}^{T-1}P_{\phi}({\bm{x}}_{t+1}\mid{\bm{x}}_{t})\bigg]\,d{\bm{x}}_{1:T-1}.

(1)

We assume that we have access to a base sequential generative model, with transition probabilities
\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}({\bm{x}}_{t+1}\mid{\bm{x}}_{t})
, such as a model pre-trained with a large dataset of observations, and that we are also given a fixed
energy function

{\mathcal{E}}({\bm{x}}_{T})
that we can query for any object generated by the model. Our objective in this paper is to learn a sequential generative model
P_{\phi}({\bm{x}}_{t+1}\mid{\bm{x}}_{t})
such that its marginal distribution is the
tilted distribution
, modulated by
{\mathcal{E}}({\bm{x}}_{T})
:

P_{\phi}^{\top}({\bm{x}}_{T})\propto\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}^{\top}({\bm{x}}_{T})\exp(-{\mathcal{E}}({\bm{x}}_{T})/\alpha),

(2)

for some temperature parameter
\alpha>0
. The role of the energy function is to steer generation towards samples with desired properties (
e.g.
, generating images based on a description).

2.1
Entropy-regularized Reinforcement Learning

One way to approach this problem is to view it from the perspective of Reinforcement Learning
(
Korbak et al. 2022
)
. We consider a finite-horizon Markov Decision Process (MDP)
{\mathcal{M}}=(\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{},{\mathcal{A}},r)
, where
\macc@depth\char 1\relax\macc@set@skewchar\macc@nested@a 111{}={\mathcal{S}}\cup\{\bot\}
is a state space augmented by a special state
\bot\notin{\mathcal{S}}
indicating the end of a trajectory,
{\mathcal{A}}
is the action space, and
r
is a reward function that we will detail below. We identify a state
s_{0}\in{\mathcal{S}}
called the
initial state
from which all trajectories start; these trajectories are guaranteed to end in
\bot
since the MDP is finite-horizon. To give a concrete example, we may see the multiple steps of denoising in a diffusion sampler as an MDP with a specific structure
(
Fan et al. 2023
;
Black et al. 2024
)
: its states are of the form
s_{t}=({\bm{x}}_{t},t)
, transitions (actions) correspond to applying one step of denoising
({\bm{x}}_{t},t)\rightarrow({\bm{x}}_{t+1},t+1)
, and trajectories must terminate when a state of the form
({\bm{x}}_{T},T)
is reached after
T
steps (termination being indicated by the transition
({\bm{x}}_{T},T)\rightarrow\bot
).

Following
Deleu et al. 2024
, the reward function
r(s,s^{\prime})
obtained when transitioning from
s\rightarrow s^{\prime}
is defined such that the sum of rewards along a trajectory only depends on the energy of the final state it reaches right before terminating; in other words, for a trajectory
\tau=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{T},\bot)
, we have

\sum_{t=0}^{T}r(s_{t},s_{t+1})=-{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T}),

(3)

where we will use the convention
s_{T+1}=\bot
throughout this paper. This includes, in particular, the case where the reward is only obtained at the end of the trajectory (
i.e.
,
r(s_{T},\bot)=-{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})
, and zero everywhere else, also called
outcome-based reward
;
Uesato et al. 2022
).

Contrary to standard RL, where the objective is to find a policy
\pi(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})
that maximizes the expected sum of rewards,
KL-regularized RL
includes an additional KL regularization term between the current policy and an anchor policy
\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}
we don’t want to deviate too much from:

\pi^{\star}_{\mathrm{RelEnt}}=\argmax_{\pi}\mathbb{E}_{\tau\sim\pi}\left[\sum_{t=0}^{T}r(s_{t},s_{t+1})-\alpha\mathrm{KL}\big(\pi(\cdot\mid s_{t})\,\|\,\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(\cdot\mid s_{t})\big)\right].

(4)

It can be shown that with the particular choice of reward function made in
eq.

3
, the marginal distribution associated with this optimal policy is the tilted distribution
(
Nachum et al. 2018
;
Korbak et al. 2022
)

\pi^{\star\top}_{\mathrm{RelEnt}}(s_{T})\propto\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}^{\top}(s_{T})\exp(-{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})/\alpha),

(5)

where the temperature parameter
\alpha
naturally emerges from the regularization constant in
eq.

4
; a proof of this is given in
appendix

A
for completeness. This constitutes the foundation of the recent line of work using RL for fine-tuning language models
(
Jaques et al. 2017
;
Ouyang et al. 2022
)
and diffusion models
(
Fan et al. 2023
;
Black et al. 2024
;
Uehara et al. 2024
)
.

2.2
Relative trajectory balance

Taking another perspective, this time rooted in the literature on GFlowNets
(
Bengio et al. 2023
)
,
Venkatraman et al. 2024
introduced an objective called the
Relative Trajectory Balance
(RTB) loss to sample from the tilted distribution
eq.

2
. Given a transition probability
P_{\phi}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})
and a scalar
Z_{\psi}>0
, the RTB loss is a non-linear least-square objective
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RTB}}(\phi,\psi)=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{b}}[\Delta_{\mathrm{RTB}}^{2}(\tau;\phi,\psi)]
, where
\pi_{b}
is an arbitrary distribution over trajectories (this is an
off-policy
objective), and the residual is defined as

\Delta_{\mathrm{RTB}}(\tau;\phi,\psi)=\log\frac{\prod_{t=0}^{T}\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})}{Z_{\psi}\prod_{t=0}^{T}P_{\phi}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})}-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})}{\alpha}.

(6)

Venkatraman et al. 2024
showed that if this RTB loss is minimized, then the marginal distribution associated with the optimal
P_{\phi}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})
satisfies
eq.

2
, and the optimal
Z_{\psi}
is the normalization constant of this tilted distribution.

3
Equivalence between RTB and Trust-PCL

In line with the growing body of work recently connecting GFlowNets with Maximum Entropy Reinforcement Learning (MaxEnt-RL)
(
Tiapkin et al. 2024
;
Mohammadpour et al. 2024
;
Deleu et al. 2024
)
, where all the losses introduced in the GFlowNet literature have been shown to have a counterpart in MaxEnt-RL, one may wonder whether the RTB loss can also be viewed from the point of view of RL. In fact, we saw that solving
eq.

4
already provides a way to sample from the tilted distribution
eq.

5
, and there exists an off-policy algorithm called
Trust-PCL

(
Nachum et al. 2018
)
that does exactly this. For a policy
\pi_{\phi}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})
and a soft state-value function
V_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\psi}(s)
, Trust-PCL is also defined as a non-linear least-square objective
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{T\textrm{-}PCL}}(\phi,\psi)=\tfrac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{b}}[\Delta_{\mathrm{T\textrm{-}PCL}}^{2}(\tau;\phi,\psi)]
, where

\Delta_{\mathrm{T\textrm{-}PCL}}(\tau;\phi,\psi)=-V_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\psi}(s_{0})+\sum_{t=0}^{T}r(s_{t},s_{t+1})+\alpha\log\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})}{\pi_{\phi}(s_{t+1}\mid s_{t})}.

(7)

This form suggests that Relative Trajectory Balance is exactly equivalent to Trust-PCL, in the sense that both losses are equal up to a constant factor that only depends on the temperature
\alpha
.

Proposition 1

(Equivalence RTB – Trust-PCL)
.

The Relative Trajectory Balance loss
(
Venkatraman et al. 2024
, RTB;)
defined with the residual in
eq.

6
is proportional to the Trust-PCL objective
(
Nachum et al. 2018
)
defined with the residual in
eq.

7
on the MDP in
section

2.1
(in particular, whose reward function satisfies
eq.

3
):
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{T\textrm{-}PCL}}(\phi,\psi)=\alpha^{2}{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RTB}}(\phi,\psi)
, with

\displaystyle V_{\mathrm{soft}}^{\psi}(s_{0})

\displaystyle=\alpha\log Z_{\psi}

\displaystyle\pi_{\phi}(s^{\prime}\mid s)

\displaystyle=P_{\phi}(s^{\prime}\mid s)

(8)

This result differs from similar ones relating GFlowNet objectives with MaxEnt-RL
(
Tiapkin et al. 2024
;
Deleu et al. 2024
)
in that, unlike those existing connections, the equivalence in
proposition

1
does not require any correction of the reward function (with a backward transition probability). The proof of this proposition is immediate with the correspondence in
eq.

8
.

4
Conclusion

Reinforcement Learning with KL regularization has proven to be a versatile tool for fine-tuning sequential generative models. Its flexibility is such that even seemingly new methods for addressing the same problem can often be framed within this paradigm. In this work, we investigated the recently proposed Relative Trajectory Balance objective
(
Venkatraman et al. 2024
)
through the lens of entropy-regularized Reinforcement Learning. We demonstrated that it is exactly equivalent to the Trust-PCL algorithm
(
Nachum et al. 2018
)
, indicating that RTB effectively rephrases an existing KL-regularized RL approach within the GFlowNet framework. This is further evidence of the strong theoretical connections between these fields.

Leveraging this theoretical correspondence, we revisited the empirical claims by
Venkatraman et al. 2024
regarding the shortcomings of KL-regularized RL. By carefully examining the illustrative experiment from the RTB paper, we found that simple methods such as REINFORCE with KL regularization can match RTB in modeling the target tilted distribution on that task. The reported failures stemmed from algorithmic and reward design choices rather than any fundamental limitation of Reinforcement Learning. However, on more challenging tasks, advanced methods like RTB/Trust-PCL are likely to offer clearer benefits, as suggested by recent work incorporating techniques such as update clipping
(
Fan et al. 2023
;
Black et al. 2024
;
Shao et al. 2024
)
(inspired by PPO
(
Schulman et al. 2017
)
). These observations emphasize the need for fair comparisons and well-posed problem formulations, while offering promising directions for capitalizing on the connections between GFlowNets and RL to explore new algorithms for generative model fine-tuning.

Appendix C
On-policy RTB is equivalent to REINFORCE with KL regularization

In this section, we will show that the gradient of on-policy RTB is equal to the gradient of REINFORCE with KL regularization, up to a constant
\alpha
. First, recall that the gradient of the REINFORCE loss with KL regularization can be written as

\nabla_{\phi}{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RL}}(\phi)=-\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\phi}}\left[\left(-{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})-\alpha\log\frac{\pi_{\phi}(\tau)}{\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(\tau)}-b\right)\nabla_{\phi}\log\pi_{\phi}(\tau)\right],

(25)

where
b
is a control variate (baseline), which is often estimated using an average over the batch of trajectories (see
algorithm

2
). Similarly, recall that the on-policy (
i.e.
, where
\pi_{b}\equiv\pi_{\phi}
) RTB loss in
eq.

\displaystyle{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RTB}}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\phi}}\left[\left(\log\frac{\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(\tau)}{Z_{\psi}\pi_{\phi}(\tau)}-\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})}{\alpha}\right)^{2}\right],

(26)

where we use the notation
\pi_{\phi}
instead of
P_{\phi}
to match the policy in KL-regularized RL. Taking the gradient of
{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RTB}}
wrt.
\phi

\displaystyle\nabla_{\phi}{\mathcal{L}}_{\mathrm{RTB}}(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\phi}}[\nabla_{\phi}\Delta_{\mathrm{RTB}}^{2}(\tau;\phi,\psi)]

(27)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{\pi_{\phi}}\left[\left(\frac{{\mathcal{E}}(s_{T})}{\alpha}+\log\frac{\pi_{\phi}(\tau)}{\pi_{\mathrm{prior}}(\tau)}+\log Z_{\psi}\right)\nabla_{\phi}\log\pi_{\phi}(\tau)\right]

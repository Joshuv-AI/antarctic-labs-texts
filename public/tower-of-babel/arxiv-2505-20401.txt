Global and nonglobal solutions for a mixed local-nonlocal heat equation
Brandon Carhuas-Torre, Ricardo Castillo, Ricardo Freire, Alex Lira, Miguel Loayza

Abstract
In this work, we establish optimal conditions concerning the global and nonglobal existence of a semilinear parabolic equation with a general source term f f , governed by the mixed local–nonlocal operator ℒ = − Δ + ( − Δ ) s \mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s} , where 0 < s < 1 0<s<1 . Furthermore, our findings recover the Fujita exponent 1 + 2 ​ s / N 1+2s/N , recently derived by Biagi, Punzo, and Vecchi in [ 2 ] , as well as by Del Pezzo and Ferreira in [ 16 ] .

Introduction

The main objective of this work is to determine global and nonglobal existence conditions for the following parabolic problem with a mixed local-nonlocal operator

\left\{\begin{array}[]{rll}u_{t}+\mathcal{L}u&=h(t)f(u)&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N}\times(0,T),\\
{u}(0)&={u}_{0}&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\\
\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
f\in C([0,\infty))
is locally Lipschitz continuous satisfying
f(0)=0
,
h\in C([0,\infty))
,
u_{0}\geq 0
, and
T>0
; here
C([0,\infty))
denotes the set of nonnegative continuous functions defined on the interval
[0,\infty)
. The mixed local-nonlocal operator
\mathcal{L}=-\Delta+(-\Delta)^{s}
with
0<s<1
, appears in various stochastic processes that involve two distinct mechanisms operating at different scales, such as a classical random walk and a Lévy flight (see
[
6
]
). Additionally, models featuring mixed local-nonlocal operators are utilized across various branches of applied sciences. These applications include optimal animal foraging strategies (see
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
), the mitigation of pandemic spread (see
[
7
]
), and heat transport in magnetized plasmas (see
[
1
]
). Recently, Biagi, Punzo, and Vecchi in
[
2
]
(considering very weak solutions), and later Del Pezzo and Ferreira in
[
16
]
(within the framework of mild solutions), studied Fujita-type results for the problem (
1
) with
f(u)=u^{p}
(for related advancements, see
[
10
]
and
[
11
]
). The authors derived the Fujita exponent, which is given by
1+2s/N
. Their findings on blow-up are based on Kaplan’s eigenvalue method (see
[
9
]
). Meanwhile, the global existence result presented in
[
2
]
employs an approximation technique that has been utilized in several research papers, such as
[
3
]
,
[
4
]
, and
[
12
]
, and
[
15
]
. Additionally, the authors in
[
16
]
employ a suitable global supersolution.

Motivated by the abovementioned findings, this article establishes optimal conditions for the global and nonglobal solutions for the heat equation (
1
) that involves a mixed local-nonlocal operator. Unlike previous research focused on demonstrating global existence, we utilize a global supersolution of the form
(1+\gamma)\,e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
, where
\gamma>0
is a suitably chosen constant. Here,
\{e^{-t\mathcal{L}}\}_{t\geq 0}
represents the heat semigroup associated with
-\mathcal{L}
(as referenced in
[
16
]
). For our results on nonglobal solutions, we employ a new lower bound estimate (see Lemma
3
) alongside techniques introduced by Weissler in
[
19
,
20
]
and by Fujita in
[
8
]
. As a result, we recover the Fujita exponent previously established in
[
2
]
and
[
16
]
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we provide the necessary preliminaries. In Section
3
, we present and prove our main theorem (Theorem
6
). We also apply this theorem to demonstrate Fujita-type results by examining the cases:
f(u)=u^{p}
,
f(u)=(1+u)[\ln(u+1)]^{p}
, and
f(u)=[\ln(u+1)]^{p}/(1+u)
(see Corollary
7
and Corollary
8
). Throughout the paper,
C
denotes a positive constant that may change, even within the same line, without affecting the results.

2
Preliminaries results

According to the results obtained in
[
2
]
and
[
18
]
, we have the following representation for the semigroup:

[e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}](x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x-y)u_{0}(y)\,dy

(2)

for
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}:=L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})(\subset L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))
, where

\mathfrak{p}_{t}(z)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{N/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{\lvert z-\xi\rvert^{2}}{4t}}\mathfrak{h}^{(s)}_{t}(\xi)\,d\xi\quad\left(z\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\ t>0\right)

(3)

is the heat kernel of
-\mathcal{L}
. Here
\mathfrak{h}^{(s)}_{t}
is the heat kernel of the fractional Laplacian
-(-\Delta)^{s}
, which satisfies:

C^{-1}_{0}\min\left\{t^{-N/(2s)},\frac{t}{|x|^{N+2s}}\right\}\leq\mathfrak{h}^{(s)}_{t}(x)\leq C_{0}\min\left\{t^{-N/(2s)},\frac{t}{|x|^{N+2s}}\right\},\,\,\mbox{ for }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}\mbox{ and }t>0,

(4)

for some constant
C_{0}\geq 1
. The heat kernel
\mathfrak{p}
has the following properties (see
[
2
, Theorem 2.4]
).

(
A_{1}
)

0<\mathfrak{p}\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t>0
we have
\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x)=\mathfrak{p}_{t}(-x)
and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x-y)dy=1.

(
A_{2}
)

For every fixed
x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
t,\tau>0
, we have
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x-y)\mathfrak{p}_{\tau}(y)dy=\mathfrak{p}_{t+\tau}(x).
Moreover,

0<\mathfrak{p}_{t}(x)\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}}\ \ \ \ \mbox{ for every }x\in\mathbb{R}^{N},\,t>0.

(5)

Remark 1
.

From conditions (
A_{1}
), (
A_{2}
), Fubini’s theorem, (
2
) and (
5
) we obtain the estimates
\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{\infty}
,
\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
and
\|e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\infty}\leq Ct^{-\frac{N}{2s}}
for all
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, and
t>0
.

We will use the following notion of mild solution.

Definition 1
.

Let
h,f\in C([0,\infty))
. Given
0\leq u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, a function
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathcal{X})
is said to be a mild solution of problem (
1
) if

u(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}\,h(\sigma)\,f(u(\sigma))\,d\sigma\,\mbox{ for }0<t<T.

When
T=+\infty
,
u
is called a global mild solution of (
1
).

The following proposition can be demonstrated as stated in
[
16
, Theorem 3.2]
.

Proposition 1
.

For each
u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, there exist a
T>0
and a unique mild solution
u\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathcal{X})
to (
1
).

Definition 2
.

Let
0\leq u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
and
h,f\in C([0,\infty))
. A function
\overline{w}:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
such that
\overline{w}\in L^{\infty}((0,T);\mathcal{X})
is called a supersolution of problem (
1
) if

\mathcal{F}[\overline{w}](t):=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}\,h(\sigma)\,f(\overline{w}(\sigma))\,d\sigma\leq\overline{w}(t),\,\,\mbox{ for all }t\in(0,T).

When
T=+\infty
,
\overline{w}
is referred to as a global supersolution of (
1
).

The following lemma establishes that finding a supersolution (or a global supersolution) to problem (
1
) is sufficient to guarantee the existence of a solution (or a global solution). Its proof follows the ideas presented by Robinson et al. in
[
17
, Theorem 1]
and Li in
[
13
, Lemma 3]
.

Lemma 2
.

Let
0\leq u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
,
h,f\in C([0,\infty))
, and suppose that
f
is nondecreasing. Then, problem (
1
) admits a nontrivial mild solution in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
if and only if it admits a supersolution in
\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)
.

We will use the following lemmas and proposition to establish our results on nonglobal solutions.

Lemma 3

(Lower bound estimate for
\mathfrak{p}_{t}
)
.

There exists a positive constant
C^{*}
such that

\mathfrak{p}_{t}(z)\geq C^{*}t^{-\frac{N}{2s}},\,\,\,\mbox{ for }t>1,\,|z|\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}},\mbox{ and }0<s<1.

Proof.

In what follows,
\omega_{N}
denotes the volume of the unit ball
B_{1}(0)
. From (
3
) and (
4
) we have

\displaystyle\mathfrak{p}_{t}(z)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|z-\xi|^{2}}{4t}}\mathfrak{h}^{(s)}_{t}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}e^{-\frac{|z-\xi|^{2}}{4t}}C_{0}^{-1}\min\Big\{t^{-N/(2s)},\frac{t}{|\xi|^{N+2s}}\Big\}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}\int_{|\xi|\leq t^{\frac{1}{2s}}}e^{-\frac{|z-\xi|^{2}}{4t}}C_{0}^{-1}\min\Big\{t^{-N/(2s)},\frac{t}{|\xi|^{N+2s}}\Big\}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{-\frac{N}{2s}}}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}C_{0}^{-1}\int_{|\xi|<t^{\frac{1}{2}}}e^{-2\left(\frac{|z|^{2}}{4t}+\frac{|\xi|^{2}}{4t}\right)}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\frac{t^{-\frac{N}{2s}}}{(4\pi t)^{\frac{N}{2}}}C_{0}^{-1}e^{-1}\int_{|\xi|<t^{\frac{1}{2}}}\,d\xi\geq\frac{t^{-\frac{N}{2s}}}{(4\pi)^{\frac{N}{2}}}C_{0}^{-1}e^{-1}\omega_{N}=C^{*}t^{-\frac{N}{2s}}.

∎

Lemma 4
.

Let
0\leq u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, and
u_{0}\neq 0
. Then there exists a positive constant
C^{*}
such that

\left[e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\right](x)\geq C^{*}t^{-\frac{N}{2s}}\int_{|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}}u_{0}(y)\,dy,\ \ \mbox{ for all }t>1\mbox{ and }|x|<\frac{1}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Proof.

From (
2
) and noting that
|x-y|\leq t^{\frac{1}{2}}
for
|y|\leq\frac{1}{2}t^{\frac{1}{2}}
, the result follows directly from Lemma
3
.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Assume
h\in C([0,\infty))
,
f\in C([0,\infty))
is nondecreasing such that
f(\sigma)>0
and
f(e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}w_{0})\leq e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}f(w_{0})
for all
\sigma>0
and
0\leq w_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
, and

\int_{z_{0}}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}<\infty,\,\mbox{ for all }z_{0}>0.

(6)

Let
u:\mathbb{R}^{N}\times[0,T)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
a mild solution of (
1
) with initial condition
0\leq u_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
,
u_{0}\neq 0
.
Then

\int_{0}^{\tau}h(\sigma)d\sigma\cdot\left(\int_{\|e^{-\tau\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\infty}}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}\right)^{-1}\leq 1,\,\,\mbox{ for all }\tau\in(0,T).

Proof.

From Definition
1
and (
3
), we have

u(t)=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}\,h(\sigma)\,f(u(\sigma))\,d\sigma=\mathcal{F}[u](t),\,\mbox{ for all }\,t\in(0,T).

Let
0<t<s
. From
f(e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}w_{0})\leq e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}f(w_{0})
(for all
0\leq w_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
), and semigroup properties, we have

e^{-(s-t)\mathcal{L}}u(t)=e^{-(s-t)\mathcal{L}}\mathcal{F}[u](t)\geq\Psi(t):=e^{-s\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}h(\sigma)\,f(e^{-(s-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}u(\sigma))\,d\sigma.

(7)

Note that
\Psi(t)
is absolutely continuous on
[0,s]
(with
x
fixed), which implies that it is differentiable almost everywhere. Hence, since
f
is nondecreasing, from (
7
) we have
\Psi^{\prime}(t)=h(t)\,f(e^{-(s-t)\mathcal{L}}u(t))\geq h(t)\,f(\Psi(t))
. This and (
6
) imply that

\displaystyle\int_{\Psi(0)}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}\geq\int_{\Psi(0)}^{\Psi(s)}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}=\int_{\Psi(0)}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}-\int_{\Psi(s)}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}=-\int_{0}^{s}\left(\int_{\Psi(t)}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}\right)^{\prime}\,dt=\int_{0}^{s}\frac{\Psi^{\prime}(t)}{f(\Psi(t))}\,dt\geq\int_{0}^{s}h(t)\,dt,

for all
s\in(0,T)
. Thus, as
\int_{0}^{s}h(t)\,dt
does not depend on
x
, we obtain
\int_{\|e^{-s\mathcal{L}}u_{0}\|_{\infty}}^{\infty}\frac{d\sigma}{f(\sigma)}\geq\int_{0}^{s}h(t)\,dt
for all
s\in(0,T)
.
∎

Theorem 6

Then there exists

(ii)

(9)

u

Proof.

(i)
Let
0<\delta\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{\beta+1},\frac{m}{(\beta+1)\|v_{0}\|_{\infty}}\right\}
,

\overline{w}(t)=(1+\beta)e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}
is a global supersolution of problem (

. Indeed, given that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[\overline{w}](t)

\displaystyle=e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}e^{-(t-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}\,[h(\sigma)\,f((1+\beta)e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}u_{0})]\,d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq e^{-t\mathcal{L}}u_{0}+\int_{0}^{t}h(\sigma)\,e^{-(t-\sigma)\mathcal{L}}\,\frac{f((1+\beta)e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}u_{0})}{(1+\beta)e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}u_{0}}\,[(1+\beta)e^{-\sigma\mathcal{L}}u_{0}]\,d\sigma

and Remark

(ii)

(ii)
.

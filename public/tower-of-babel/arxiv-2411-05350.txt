The moduli of the universal geometry of heterotic moduli
Jock McOrist

Abstract




\proofmodefalse

We study the moduli of the universal geometry of
d=4

N=1
heterotic vacua. Universal geometry refers to a family of heterotic vacua fibered over the moduli space. The universal geometry mimics aspects of the original heterotic vacua, in particular holomorphic data such as F-terms, as well as the Green-Schwarz Bianchi identity. Here we study first order deformations of the universal geometry and find this provides a shortcut to computing second order deformations of the original problem. The equations governing the moduli of the universal geometry are remarkably similar to the equations of the underlying heterotic theory and we find a fascinating double extension structure that mirrors the original heterotic problem. As an application we find first order universal deformations determine second order deformations of the original heterotic theory. This gives a shortcut to determining results that are otherwise algebraically unwieldy. The role of the D-terms is closely related to the existence of flat connections on the moduli space. Finally, we re-derive some of these results by direct differentiation - this direct approach requires significantly more calculation.

1
Introduction

Heterotic geometry describes the moduli space of a heterotic string vacuum, focusing on the geometry at large volume, where it can be approximated by a spacetime geometry, defining a non-linear sigma model. This sigma model flows under the renormalization group to a super-conformal field theory (SCFT), defining the string background perturbatively in terms of the string coupling constant
g_{s}
. The geometry also receives corrections proportional to
\alpha^{\prime}
, which are linked to loop corrections in the non-linear sigma model. These corrections are determined at first order in terms of geometric conditions on the manifold; at second order they are known as far as variations of the fermionic fields go but not yet translated into conditions on the geometry.

The moduli space of Calabi-Yau manifolds exhibits intriguing properties, particularly when quantum corrections, including both perturbative and non-perturbative
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-corrections, are taken into account. These corrections give rise to mirror symmetry and special geometry. Special geometry can be described in several ways; one perspective involves flat connections on the moduli space, enabling the parallel transport of states in the associated SCFT. A natural question arises: does this framework generalize to the study of heterotic vacua?

The moduli of Calabi-Yau manifolds can be explored within the context of the standard embedding of heteortic vacua, which unlike type II string theory, preserves
N=1
,
d=4
spacetime supersymmetry. Smooth deformations of the bundle away from the standard embedding at a generic point in moduli space does not break any supersymmetry and from the point of view of a four-dimensional field theorist, is a perfectly benign movement in moduli space and nothing dramatic is expected to happen e.g.
[
1
,
2
]
. This leads to the question of whether special geometry persists away from the standard embedding.

In
[
3
]
, a fibration of the heterotic vacuum space over its moduli space, termed universal heterotic geometry, was introduced as a generalisation of the mathematical concept of a universal bundle. This fibration is defined by connections on the moduli space, and our goal here is to compute the associated curvatures. While direct computation is possible, it is cumbersome. A key insight of
[
3
]
is that the universal bundle mimics many of the holomorphic properties of the original heterotic vacuum. That is, remarkably both the F-terms and the Green-Schwarz Bianchi identity hold on the total space of the fibration. These relations encode significant information about the first-order deformations of the heterotic problem.

In this paper, we study the deformations of universal heterotic geometry and show that first-order deformations yield, in an elegant manner, second-order deformations of the heterotic vacuum. Notably, many second-order derivatives vanish, leading to the conclusion that numerous components of the curvature of the connections on the moduli space also vanish. We verify these findings through direct calculations. A critical aspect of our analysis involves requiring that first-order deformations preserve D-terms fiberwise. This suggests a profound connection between D-terms and special geometry, which warrants further exploration.

To set the stage, we briefly review the geometry of a heterotic vacuum, correct to first order in
\alpha^{\prime}
, and key features of its moduli space.

1.1
Heterotic geometry

The large volume approximation in which we work considers a spacetime background of the form
\mathbb{R}^{1,3}\times X
, where
X
is a complex three-dimensional manifold with a vanishing first Chern class, and
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle over
X
with a connection
A
satisfying the Hermitian Yang-Mills equations. The background also involves the Kalb-Ramond field
B
and its field strength
H
, both of which must satisfy field equations and anomaly cancellation conditions. This defines the heterotic structure, denoted by
Het
, which comprises the geometry of
X
, the bundle
E
, and the background
H
.

The moduli space of heterotic string theory at large radius, accurate to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, is computed from a dimensional reduction of the supergravity theory. Due to supersymmetry, this moduli space must have a Kähler structure, and the relationships between
H
, the connection on
E
, and the Hermitian form
\omega
on
X
must satisfy the Green-Schwarz anomaly cancellation and
N=1
supersymmetry in four dimensions.

While these conditions are cited in many places in the literature, its a useful setting for us to follow
[
4
]
in which these conditions are phrased in terms of F-terms and D-terms. We define a bundle
Q
given topologically as

Q\;=\;(\mathpzc T_{X}{}^{1,0})^{*}\oplus{\rm End}E\oplus\mathpzc T_{X}^{1,0}~,

(1.1)

and there is a
{\overline{D}}
-operator and its adjoint
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
that act on sections of this bundle. In
[
4
]
, we demonstrate the kernel of this operator corresponds to holomorphic deformations of set of equations we term F-terms:

N_{J}\;=\;0~,\quad F^{0,2}\;=\;0~,\quad H\;=\;{\text{d}}^{c}\omega\;=\;\frac{1}{3!}J^{m}J^{n}J^{p}({\text{d}}\omega)_{mnp}~,

(1.2)

Let
x^{m}
denote the real coordinates of
X
, and
(x^{\mu},x^{\bar{\nu}})
the holomorphic coordinates. The vector-valued form
J^{m}\equiv J^{m}{}_{n}\,{\text{d}}x^{n}
is a 1-form derived from the complex structure. In the following, we will generally omit the wedge product symbol “
\wedge
” between forms, except where its omission might cause ambiguity. The anomaly relation yields a modified Bianchi identity for
H
.

{\text{d}}H\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace F{\,\wedge\,}F+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace R^{\text{H}}{\,\wedge\,}R^{\text{H}}~.

(1.3)

The Bianchi identity is required so that
{\overline{D}}^{2}=0
defines a cohomology, and so it is sometimes viewed in close conjunction with the F-terms. Of course, in the ten-dimensional theory it is a constraint.

Deformations of equations we call D-terms corresponds to the adjoint
{\overline{D}}^{\dagger}
which is computed with respect to the moduli space metric
[
5
,
6
]
, see also
[
7
,
8
]
. These equations are

\omega^{2}F\;=\;0~,\qquad{\text{d}}\omega^{2}\;=\;0~,\qquad e^{\Phi}\;=\;e^{\Phi_{0}}=g_{s}~,

(1.4)

where the dilaton is a constant so that the non-linear sigma model is a guaranteed to be a relevant description of the background. If the dilaton is not constant, which amounts to
H=\mathcal{O}(1)
(not subleading in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
), then while we might find solutions of the mathematical problem defined by (
1.2
)-(
1.4
), the resulting geometry does not necessarily have anything to do with the string theory without some string non-perturbative arguments such as duality. For example, it means the string coupling varies over the internal manifold and so in regions where
g_{s}
is large we expect string loop corrections and non-perturbative
g_{s}
effects to be relevant. It is a double edged sword because at
H=\mathcal{O}(1)
, the non-linear sigma model on a Riemann sphere is itself strongly coupled in its coupling constant
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, meaning we would need to correct both the solution and the equations of motion to all orders in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
.

Returning now to the problem of interest:
H=\mathcal{O}({\alpha^{\backprime}\,})
with a geometry satisfying (
1.2
)–(
1.4
). The deformations of this collection of equations, for a fixed topology, correspond to the points of the moduli space
\mathpzc M
, which is itself a complex manifold. This has real coordinates
y^{a}
and complex coordinates

(y^{\alpha},y^{\overline{\beta}})
. Said differently, given a point
y\in\mathpzc M
we get a solution to the heterotic equations of motion and we call that solution a heterotic structure. Our goal is to understand how the space of vacua are fibered over the moduli space. The role of singularities is clearly important to the moduli space. For our work here however, we assume we are working in the neighbourhood of a smooth point. Studying global issues, singularities and other novel features such as jumps in dimension remains as future work.

1.2
The universal bundle

For that we come to a universal bundle. In general terms, a universal bundle is a fibration. Its base is the moduli space of some geometric objects such as vector bundles or complex structures, satisfying certain equations and the fibres are the geometric objects themselves. For our purposes, these data derive from heterotic string theory and the universal bundle
\mathpzc U
encodes the heterotic structures as a fibres over each point in this moduli space. The bundle is ‘universal’ in the sense that it systematically encodes the structure (e.g., vector bundles, connections, or complex structures) across the entire moduli space. It provides a framework for computing quantum corrections to the leading order heterotic structures.

This was studied at length in
[
3
]
. A summary of the key aspects are as follows:

•

Extended versions of
B
and
H
are introduced, denoted
\mathbbl{B}
and
\mathbbl{H}
, which satisfy a supersymmetric relation and a Bianchi identity. The mixed components of
\mathbbl{H}
yield key relations for the moduli space.

•

The geometry is extended by introducing a connection
c_{a}{}^{m}
, which helps split the tangent space of the fibration
X\to\mathpzc M
into horizontal and vertical subspaces. The splitting of tangent spaces is crucial for defining how vectors and tensors extend across the larger space.

•

A covariant derivative structure is developed, decomposing the standard de Rham operator into components acting on both the fiber
X
and the base
\mathpzc M
, taking into account changes in the complex structure and parameter variations.

•

Two metrics, one on the manifold
X
and the other on the moduli space
\mathpzc M
, combine into an extended metric that includes cross terms involving the connection
c_{a}{}^{m}
. This connection also influences the variation of the complex structure on
X
, reflecting how moduli variations manifest geometrically.

This universal heterotic geometry framework extends the moduli space and field theory in a way that integrates both gauge and gravitational degrees of freedom.

It is important to take into account the spaces are curved and that there are gauge symmetries to be respect. We account for both of these simultaneously by considering covariant derivatives. As a warm-up, consider gauge transformation of Yang-Mills connection
A

A\to\;^{\Phi}\!A\;=\;\Phi A\Phi^{-1}-{\text{d}}\Phi\,\Phi^{-1}~.

(1.6)

The connection
A
depends on the manifold
X
and moduli space
\mathpzc M
. That is,
A
depends on parameters and
\Phi
necessarily does so too. A deformation
\delta A
is defined by a parameter space covariant derivative
D_{a}A
. This is cooked up to transform homogeneously under gauge transformations and requires the introduction of a connection

A^{\sharp}{\;=\;}{A^{\sharp}}_{a}\,{\text{d}}y^{a}
transforming under the same group as
A
but with legs on the moduli space. Then
A^{\sharp}
defines for us a choice of covariant derivative:

D_{a}A\;=\;{\partial}_{a}A-{\text{d}}_{A}A^{\sharp}_{a}~,\quad{\text{d}}_{A}A^{\sharp}_{a}\;=\;{\text{d}}A^{\sharp}_{a}+[A,\,A^{\sharp}_{a}]~.

(1.7)

Deformations of
A^{\sharp}
correspond to small gauge transformations of
D_{a}A
and a natural choice of gauge is holomorphic gauge in which holomorphic deformations of
\mathcal{A}=A^{0,1}
correspond to holomorphic tangent vectors of
\mathpzc M

[
6
]
.

It is a conceptual leap but
A
and
A^{\sharp}_{a}
are to be unified into a single connection for a bigger gauge bundle

\mathbbl{A}\;=\;A+A^{\sharp}_{a}{\text{d}}y^{a}~.

It has an associated field strength

\mathbbl{F}\;=\;\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{A}+\mathbbl{A}^{2}~,~~~\text{with}~~~\mathbbl{d}\;=\;{\text{d}}+{\text{d}}y^{a}{\partial}_{a}~.

In some sense we can view
\mathbbl{F}
as the extension of
F
to the universal bundle. It naturally encodes the curvature of the Yang-Millls bundle
F
, covariant derivatives
\mathbbl{F}_{am}={\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A_{m}
and an additional curvature
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}
on the moduli space. The last curvature is of interest – its the curvature of the connection
A^{\sharp}
restricted to the moduli space. These curvatures are closely related to special geometry in the context of CY manifolds. The curvature tells us how fields or geometric objects (such as complex structure and hermitian structure) change as one moves around in moduli space. This is the genesis of universal geometry. Democracy dictates that we shouldn’t stop at just the symmetries of the Yang-Mills bundle
E
, but include other gauge symmetries: diffeomorphisms of
X
and the gerbe symmetries of the B-field. This gives a universal geometry for heterotic vacua.

This gives us a nice mathematical structure. It encodes otherwise difficult to derive algebraic relations and these were crucial in deriving a Kähler potential for the moduli space metric in
[
5
]
. The motivation for this article is to address two closely related questions: is there a meaning to the curvature of the moduli space connection
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}
and how is the universal geometry sensitive to the second order deformations? Both of these are closely related to studying the space of deformations of the universal bundle structure.

1.3
Deformations of the universal bundle

In this article, we focus on understanding the space of deformations of the universal bundle, with two seperate motivations in mind. First, in separate work
[
9
]
, we explore the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}^{2}
corrections to the Hull-Strominger system, which are treated as small deformations. By studying how the universal bundle adapts to such a deformation, we may learn something about the nature of the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–corrections. Second, the study of second-order deformations in the Hull-Strominger system plays a crucial role in defining the curvature on the moduli space, which is integral to any notion of special geometry. Our findings reveal that investigating second-order deformations in the Hull-Strominger system is closely tied to understanding first-order deformations of the universal bundle. Imposing the condition that the holomorphic structure of the universal bundle is preserved leads to intriguing constraints on second-order derivatives.

Let’s explain what we mean by holomorphic structure. The moduli space
\mathpzc M
is associated to the space of deformations of
X
,
E\to X
preserving the supersymmetry conditions. It is complex Kähler with a natural Kähler metric and Kähler potential. The supersymmetry conditions can be expressed as a combination of F-terms and D-terms and the Bianchi identity
[
4
]
. The F-term conditions and Bianchi identity are

N_{J}\;=\;0~,\quad F^{0,2}\;=\;0~,\quad H\;=\;{\text{d}}^{c}\omega~,\quad{\text{d}}H\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace F^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace R^{2}~.

(1.8)

Part of the motivation for calling these F-terms is they can be derived from a superpotential functional
[
10
,
11
,
12
]
. What we show in
[
3
,
6
]
is that these equations are satisfied on the universal geometry

N{J}\;=\;0~,\quad\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}\;=\;0~,\quad\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}~,\quad\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\mathbbl{F}^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\mathbbl{R}^{2}~.

(1.9)

The equations governing the universal geometry mimic that of the original heterotic vacuum.
This holomorphic structure provides valuable insights into the deformation space of the universal bundle. On one hand, we can approach the problem using the framework developed in
[
13
]
, where the deformations of the F-term equations are linked to sections of a complex of specific vector bundles. The kernel of the operator in this complex corresponds to deformations of the F-terms and the Bianchi identity. On the other hand, the deformation space of the universal bundle can be studied more directly, which proves to be a fruitful approach. It offers deeper understanding of the original problem, particularly by illuminating the behaviour of second-order covariant derivatives. Similar to the first-order case explored in
[
3
]
, the holomorphy of the universal bundle is closely tied to the proper fixing of small gauge transformations, appropriately generalised as discussed in
[
6
]
. We find that for the universal bundle to exhibit a well-defined deformation theory, certain second-order derivatives must vanish. This requirement naturally extends the concept of holomorphic gauge to second-order covariant derivatives. We find a double extension structure for the universal geometry moduli space, whose structure mimics that of the original heterotic problem. Consequently, the moduli of the heterotic theory and its universal geometry are captured by similar equations. As a consistency check, we compute these second-order covariant derivatives directly by varying the original supersymmetry equations. The results align with our expectations, confirming that the extension of gauge fixing to the second-order problem is indeed a natural and necessary generalisation.

Returning the perturbation problem of the universal geometry. The first order deformation problem of the universal geometry tells us about the second order covariant derivatives of the fields of the original heterotic theory. Many of these vanish. The commutators of these derivatives are related to the curvatures on the moduli space such as
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}
. Provided we demand the D-terms we conjecture this gives a flat connection on the universal family. This is intriguingly similar to the work in
[
14
]
, in which parallel transport of D-branes over the Kähler moduli space of Calabi-Yau manifolds defines a flat connection on the moduli space provided the D-terms hold. This is suggestive the universal bundle is a good structure to study heterotic special geometry.

1.4
Outline of the article

In the body of this paper, a detailed discussion of the points outlined above is given.

In section 2, we review the first order deformation problem of heterotic theories. This is a two-fold calculation: computing the variations of the supersymmetry variations and then fixing the small gauge transformations. This calculation is described in detail in
[
6
]
and this section summsrises the salient points, improving some of the discussion as appropriate. The fixing of holomorphic gauge is required for an embedding for the heterotic moduli problem into a holomorphic universal bundle. This theme will be repeated at second order.

In section 3, we describe some of the relations between second order deformation theory, connections on the moduli space and their curvatures. We demonstrate with the baby example of a fixed manifold
X
and consider deformations of just the vector bundle
E
. In this situation we can differentiate the first order results directly and show that fixing holomorphic gauge at first order, sets some but not all of the second order derivatives of the connection
A
to zero. This is enough to show that the connection on the moduli space
\mathpzc M
is holomorphic but it is not enough to demonstrate the entire field strength vanishes.

In section 4, we embed the toy example discussed in the previous section into the universal bundle. We demonstrate demanding the universal bundle remain holomorphic under deformations requires the gauge fixing constructed directly in the previous section; the universal calculation is significantly cheaper than the direct calculation. The main lesson is that the universal bundle forces the curvature
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}
of the connection on
\mathpzc M
to vanish. We extend these results to the Atiyah-type problem in which the complex structure of
\mathpzc U
is allowed to vary. We then study the space of deformations both directly and via the kernel of an operator
\mathbbl{D}
acting on sections of a bundle topologically given by
\mathbbl{Q}=\mathpzc T{X}^{1,0}\oplus{\rm End}\mathpzc U
.

In section 5, we take lessons learnt to the full problem: the space of deformations of the universal bundle. Working around a smooth point in moduli space, demanding an embedding into the universal bundle forces many second order deformations to vanish. This results in either vanishing of commutators of deformations or vast simplifications.

In section 6, we study a double extension structure on the universal bundle, inspired by the work of
[
13
]
. A long exact sequence determines a cohomology for the moduli of universal geometry. These moduli are controlled by similar equations to the original heterotic problem.

In section 7, we demonstrate the direct calculation of second order derivatives of the full heterotic theory and proposed gauge fixing. Our results here reproduce the results in the universal calculation but a significantly more difficult and lengthy. Furthermore, their geometric significance is not clear until on embeds in the universal space.

In section 8, we resolve an outstanding issue in
[
5
]
: we show that the
(0,2)
component of the deformation of the hermitian form decouples from the moduli space metric when differentiated from the heterotic Kähler potential

K\;=\;\!-\log\frac{4}{3}\int\omega^{3}-\log\text{i}\int(\Omega\bar{\Omega})~.

We work with manifolds whose underlying geometry is Calabi-Yau in the sense that the manifold has vanishing first Chern class and to zeroth order in the
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-expansion is Ricci-flat, Kähler. We include the first order

2.1
A review of the background spacetime

We consider a heterotic string compactification at large radius that is well approximated by
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
–corrected heterotic supergravity. We work to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
. The compactification is defined by a spacetime geometry

\mathbbl{R}^{3,1}\times X~.

We require
N=1
supersymmetry in
d=4
. To first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, the supersymmetry conditions in terms of the geometric data described in
[
15
,
16
]
. The manifold is complex, which means it admits an integrable complex structure
J=J_{m}{}^{n}{\text{d}}x^{m}\otimes{\partial}_{n}
, where
x^{m}
are real coordinates on
X
, equivalently a closed holomorphic volume form
\Omega
.

We shall henceforth omit the wedge symbol, writing for example
{\text{d}}x^{mn}\cong{\text{d}}x^{m}{\,\wedge\,}{\text{d}}x^{n}
, unless it is ambigious. The manifold has vanishing first Chern class
c_{1}(X)=0
, with a Riemannian metric
{\text{d}}s^{2}=g_{mn}{\text{d}}x^{m}\otimes{\text{d}}x^{n}
, compatible complex structure and so a hermitian 2-form
\omega=\frac{1}{2}\omega_{mn}{\text{d}}x^{mn}=\text{i}g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{\text{d}}x^{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}
. The manifold has an
SU(3)
-structure. There exists a vector bundle
V\to X
, with a connection
A
, and a structure group being a subgroup of
E_{8}\times E_{8}
. The gauge connection is anti-hermitian meaning

A\;=\;\mathcal{A}-\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}~,

where
\mathcal{A}
is the
(0,1)
component of
A
. The connection has a field strength
F={\text{d}}A+A^{2}
. This datum satisfies the equations (
1.2
)–(
1.4
).

2.2
First order deformations review

The deformations will always preserve
N=1
supersymmetry. Therefore, the moduli space is a complex Kähler manifold. We denote
y^{a}=(y^{\alpha},y^{\overline{\beta}})
the corresponding complex coordinates: small deformations of fields correspond to tangent vectors
\delta y^{a}
. This is known as the Kuranishi map in the context of complex structure deformations
[
17
]
and we extend it to deformations of heterotic structures
Het

In
[
6
]
, our approach followed these steps:

1.

Deform the F-term equations and Bianchi identity.

2.

Analyse gauge symmetries (diffeomorphisms, rescaling of
\Omega
, gauge and gerbe transformations).

3.

Define holomorphy by choosing a complex structure on the moduli space
\mathpzc M
.

4.

Fix the gauge (holomorphic gauge and a condition on
\delta\Omega
).

5.

Use the D-term equations to fix the exact and co-exact terms in the Hodge decomposition.

We now summarise the key results.

That
J^{2}=-1
implies that
\delta J_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=\delta J_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\overline{\nu}}=0
. We introduce projectors onto holomorphic and antiholomorphic coordinates respectively
[
18
]

P\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(1-\text{i}J)~,\qquad Q\;=\;\frac{1}{2}(1+\text{i}J)~.

To connect with the notation of
[
3
]
, we write
\delta J^{\mu}=2\text{i}\Delta^{\mu}
or
\delta P^{\mu}=-\delta Q^{\mu}=\Delta^{\mu}
.
We can write
\Omega
in the following way

\Omega\;=\;\frac{1}{3!}\,f\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho}\,{\text{d}}x^{\mu\nu\rho}\quad\text{with}\quad|\Omega|^{2}\;=\;\frac{|f|^{2}}{\sqrt{g}}~.

with the antisymmetric symbol having
\epsilon_{123}=1
. Then,

\begin{split}({\text{d}}\Omega=0)\qquad&~{\overline{\partial}}\,\delta P^{\mu}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\nabla_{\mu}^{\text{\tiny CH}}\,\delta P^{\mu}~=~{\overline{\partial}}\,\delta\log f\;=\;{\overline{\partial}}\,\big(\delta\log{|\Omega|^{2}}+\delta\log{\sqrt{g}}-\delta\log{\overline{f}}\big)~,\\[5.69046pt]
(F^{0,2}=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\delta\mathcal{A}~=~\delta P^{\mu}\,F_{\mu}~,\\[8.5359pt]
(H-{\text{d}}^{c}\omega=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}\,\delta\mathpzc Z^{0,2}\;=\;0~,\\
&{\overline{\partial}}\,\delta\mathpzc Z^{1,1}+{\partial}\,\delta\mathpzc Z^{0,2}\;=\;2\text{i}\,\Delta^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\delta\mathcal{A}\,F)}~,\\[5.69046pt]
({\text{d}}\log{|\Omega|^{2}}=0)\qquad&{\text{d}}\,\delta\log{|\Omega|^{2}}\;=\;0~,\\[5.69046pt]
({\text{d}}\omega^{2}=0)\qquad&{\partial}^{\dagger}\delta\omega^{1,1}~=~{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\delta\omega^{0,2}-\text{i}\,{\overline{\partial}}\,\delta\log{\sqrt{g}}~,\\[5.69046pt]
(F\omega^{2}=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}}\delta\mathcal{A}-{\partial}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}}\delta\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}+\frac{1}{2}\,(\mathpzc Z\,\lrcorner\,F)\;=\;0~.\\[8.5359pt]
\end{split}

(2.1)

The quantity
\delta\mathpzc Z=\delta y^{a}\mathpzc Z_{a}
— where we expand in
\delta y^{a}
, being tangent vectors to the moduli space at a point, and do so with a prescience for later on — is given by the complex combination

\delta\mathpzc Z\;=\;\delta\mathpzc B+\text{i}\,\delta\omega\quad\text{where}\quad\delta\mathpzc B\;=\;\delta B-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\delta A\,A)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{(\delta\Theta\,\Theta)}\big)~.

(2.2)

We can expand
\delta\mathpzc B=\delta y^{a}\mathpzc B_{a}
.

The gauge symmetries, diffeomorphisms, gauge transformations, gerbe transformations have infinitesemal limits with parameters denoted by a vector
\varepsilon^{m}=\varepsilon^{\mu},\varepsilon^{\overline{\nu}}
, an endomorphism valued scalar
\phi
and a gerbe
\mathfrak{b}
(locally a 1-form, globally not). There is also a
\mathbbl{C}^{*}
gauge symmetry over
\mathpzc M
:
\Omega\to e^{\lambda}\Omega
, where
\lambda
depends on parameters, constant over
X
. The first order deformations transform

\begin{split}(\varepsilon)\qquad&\Delta^{\mu}\sim\Delta^{\mu}+{\overline{\partial}}\varepsilon^{\mu}~,\\[8.5359pt]
(\varepsilon,\lambda)\qquad&\delta\log f\sim\delta\log f+\nabla^{\text{\tiny CH}}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}+\lambda~,\\[8.5359pt]
(\varepsilon,\phi)\qquad&\delta\mathcal{A}\sim\delta\mathcal{A}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,F_{\mu}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\phi~,\\[5.69046pt]
(\varepsilon,\phi,\mathfrak{b})\qquad&\delta\mathpzc Z\sim\delta\mathpzc Z+\varepsilon^{m}(H+\text{i}\,{\text{d}}\omega)_{m}+{\text{d}}\left(\mathfrak{b}+\text{i}\,\varepsilon^{m}\,\omega_{m}\right)+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\!\left(F\phi\right)~.\end{split}

(2.3)

We expand a deformation in terms of holomorphic and antiholomorphic parameters using an appropriate covariant derivative, for example

\delta P^{\mu}\;=\;\delta y^{a}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}P^{\mu}\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}P^{\mu}+\delta y^{{\overline{\alpha}}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\alpha}}}P^{\mu}~.

(2.4)

Note that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}P=\Delta_{a}^{0,1}-\Delta_{a}^{1,0}
, not
\Delta_{b}^{0,1}+\Delta_{b}^{1,0}
so we work with
P
and not
\Delta_{a}
. Holomorphy means that certain combinations of antiholomorphic variations are exact, for example

\Delta_{{\overline{\alpha}}}{}^{\mu}\;=\;{\overline{\partial}}\kappa_{{\overline{\alpha}}}{}^{\mu}~,\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\alpha}}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;\kappa_{{\overline{\alpha}}}{}^{\mu}\,F_{\mu}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Phi_{{\overline{\alpha}}}\quad\text{and so on...}

(2.5)

This defines a complex structure on
\mathpzc M
, because under a reparameterisations
\widetilde{y}^{\alpha}=\widetilde{y}^{\alpha}(y^{\beta},y^{{\overline{\beta}}})
, which are not holomorphic, the combinations above acquire a non-exact bit. Holomorphic gauge is defined by killing these exact terms as far as possible.

\begin{split}&\hskip 28.45274pt\Delta_{\overline{\alpha}}{}^{\mu}\;=\;0~,\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;0~,\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\alpha}}}\Omega\;=\;0~,\\[5.69046pt]
&{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\alpha}}^{2,0}\;=\;0~,\quad\mathpzc Z_{\overline{\alpha}}^{1,1}\;=\;0~,\quad\mathpzc Z_{\overline{\alpha}}^{0,2}\;=\;{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\overline{\alpha}}^{0,2}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(2.6)

There is a residual freedom, which we use to impose that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Omega^{(3,0)}
is harmonic. This implies a condition on hermitian deformations from the
SU(3)
structure relations as

{\text{d}}~{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\log{\sqrt{g}}\;=\;{\text{d}}(\omega\,\lrcorner\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega)\;=\;0~,

(2.7)

that simplifies the variation of the balanced condition.

The physical degrees of freedom are

{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}~,\quad\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}~,\quad\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{1,1}~,

(2.8)

where note that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}=\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\omega_{\mu}
is non-vanishing and

\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{1,1}\;=\;2\text{i}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}=2\mathpzc B_{\alpha}^{1,1}~,\qquad{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\alpha}^{0,2}{\;=\;}2\mathpzc B_{\alpha}^{0,2}{\;=\;}\!-2\text{i}\mathpzc D_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}~.

Now that the gauge symmetries have been fixed and the physical degrees of freedom isolated, go back to the equations of motion

\begin{split}({\text{d}}\Omega=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\;=\;0~,\qquad\nabla_{\mu}^{\text{\tiny CH}}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}~=~0~,\\[8.5359pt]
(F^{0,2}=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}~=~\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,F_{\mu}~,\\[2.84544pt]
(H-{\text{d}}^{c}\omega=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}\;=\;\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}-\frac{\text{i}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,F)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta\,R)}\Big)~,\\[2.84544pt]
({\text{d}}\omega^{2}=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}~=~0~,\qquad{\partial}^{\dagger}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}~=~{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}~,\\[8.5359pt]
(F\omega^{2}=0)\qquad&{\overline{\partial}}^{\dagger}_{\mathcal{A}}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}\,\lrcorner\,F\;=\;0~.\\[5.69046pt]
\end{split}

(2.9)

We can identify a subset of these equations as F-terms:

\begin{split}{\overline{\partial}}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}&\;=\;0~,\\[4.26773pt]
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}&\;=\;\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}F_{\mu}~,\\
{\overline{\partial}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{1,1}&\;=\;2\text{i}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,F\big)-{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}\,\big(\nabla_{\nu}\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}+\text{i}\,\nabla^{\mu}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega_{\nu}^{0,1}\big)R^{\nu}{}_{\mu}~,\end{split}

(2.10)

where
R^{\mu}{}_{\nu}=R_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}{}^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\,{\text{d}}x^{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}
is the Riemann tensor and the spin connection depends on the moduli of
X

[
3
]
:

{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\Theta{}_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\sigma}\;=\;\nabla_{\sigma}\,\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\mu}}}{}^{\nu}+\text{i}\,\nabla^{\nu}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega_{\sigma{\overline{\mu}}}~.

(2.11)

These are derivable from a superpotential functional
W=\int_{X}\Omega(H+\text{i}{\text{d}}\omega)
provided deformations are in holomorphic gauge (

)
[

,

{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}^{0,2}={\partial}^{\dagger}\widetilde{\xi}~.

Connections, covariant derivatives and the universal bundle

In the the study of the string theory moduli space of Calabi-Yau manifolds special geometry could be phrased as the existence of a flat connection on the moduli space. Alternatively, parameter space derivatives were covariant with respect to a bundle and those derivatives commute - this is explicit in the seminal work of
[
21
,
22
]
and later in the context of
tt^{*}
e.g.
[
23
]
and in this section we study what happens when we do not necessarily make this assumption in the more general context of a heterotic theory.

Recall, that in the study of the moduli of a CY manifold (e.g. at the standard embedding in heterotic), the top form
\Omega
is a section of a line bundle
\mathcal{L}
over the moduli space
\mathpzc M
; the corresponding covariant derivatives, at second order, have non-trivial mixed terms

\delta y^{\overline{\beta}}\,\delta y^{\alpha}\,[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}]\Omega\;=\;\!-\delta y^{\alpha}\delta y^{\overline{\beta}}G_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}\Omega

where
G_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}
corresponds to the metric on deformations of complex structure of a CY manifold. This result follows from using the canonical choice of connection on the line bundle, that being a derivative of the Kähler potential
K_{,\alpha}
. We also use that
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\Omega={\partial}_{\overline{\alpha}}\Omega=0
, a statement of holomorphy at first order. As
\mathcal{L}
is a line bundle this is in fact the first Chern class and the calculation here demonstrates it coincides with the metric on the moduli space of complex structures. It goes without saying, but this implies that
\delta\Omega
has mixed derivative terms at second order in the derivative expansion and the bundle
\mathcal{L}
is holomorphic but not flat. Both these are lessons for the general case.

Consider a second example: deformations of the connection for the gauge bundle. We write

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{A}&\;=\;\delta y^{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{a}\delta y^{b}{\mathfrak{D}}_{(a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{b)}\mathcal{A}+\cdots,\\
\end{split}

where
{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}={\partial}_{a}A-{\text{d}}_{A}A^{\sharp}_{a}
and
A^{\sharp}_{a}
is a connection 1-form on the moduli space and as
\delta y^{a}\delta y^{b}=\delta y^{b}\delta y^{a}
we get a projection onto the symmetric component. Taylor series for sections of bundles naturally involve symmetrised covariant derivatives and have a natural connection to the theory of jet bundles (the
k
th jet carrying information about the section, and its derivatives up to the
k
th covariant derivative, symmetrised).

The bundle
E
being holomorphic means that
F^{0,2}=0
. Differentiating this with respect to parameters:

{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}\;=\;0~,\qquad{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\left({\mathfrak{D}}_{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A}\right)+\{{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A},{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A}\}\;=\;0~.

(3.1)

The first order term is solved by Hodge decomposition
{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}=\xi_{a}^{\rm harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{a}
where
\xi_{a}^{\rm harm}
are zero modes of
\Box_{{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}}
and
\mu_{a}
are sections of
H^{0}({\rm End}E)
. As this is a deformation of
\mathcal{A}=A^{0,1}
, there is no reality constraint on
\mu_{a}
. We pick our complex structure on the moduli space so that

{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}=\xi_{\alpha}^{\rm harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\alpha}~,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\overline{\alpha}}~,

At second order,

{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mu_{\overline{\alpha}})+[{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A},\mu_{\overline{\alpha}}]~,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\xi_{\alpha}^{\rm harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mu_{\alpha})+[{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A},\mu_{\alpha}]~.

We use the index
b
as shorthand for both the holomorphic and antiholomorphic cases
b=\beta,{\overline{\beta}}
.

The connection
A^{\sharp}=A^{\sharp}_{a}{\text{d}}y^{a}
can be deformed
A^{\sharp}_{a}\to A^{\sharp}_{a}+\phi_{a}
with
\phi_{a}{\text{d}}y^{a}
any endomorphism valued 1-form on
\mathpzc M
such that
(\phi_{a})^{\dagger}=-(\phi_{a})
. Under this deformation,

{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}\to{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A}-{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\phi_{a}~,\qquad{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A}\to{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A}-{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\phi_{b})+[{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\phi_{b},\phi_{a}]-[{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}\mathcal{A},\phi_{b}]-[{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}\mathcal{A},\phi_{a}]~.

As
\phi^{\dagger}=-\phi
, in complex coordinates
\phi_{\overline{\alpha}}=(\phi_{\alpha})^{\dagger}
, a constraint not present for
(\mu_{a})^{\dagger}\neq\mu_{a}
. So we can choose
\phi_{\overline{\alpha}}=\mu_{\overline{\alpha}}
to remove some but not all of the
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}
-exact terms

\begin{split}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}&\;=\;\xi_{\alpha}^{\rm harm}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\alpha}\;=\;\xi_{\alpha}~,\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;0~,\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A}&\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}~,\quad{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\xi_{\beta}~,\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}&\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(3.2)

Here
\xi_{\alpha}
is simply a representative of the cohomology group
H^{1}(X,{\text{End}\,}E)
.
Using that
{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\xi_{\alpha}=0
, we can check that (
3.2
) satisfies the Maurier-Cartan equations:

{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A})+\{{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A}\}\;=\;0~,\qquad{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A})\;=\;0~.

As an aside, if in addition we require the hermitian Yang-Mills equation hold
\omega^{2}F=0
then at first order

{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}^{\dagger}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\;=\;0~.

This additional equation forces
\mu_{\alpha}=0
and so
\xi_{\alpha}=\xi_{\alpha}^{\rm harm}
. This does not occur in the more general heterotic situation
[
6
]
. However, the lesson is important: imposing the D-term seems important to curvature of the connection on the moduli space, mirroring an observation of
[
14
]
in the context of D-brane transport over moduli of CY manifolds.

The choice of connection
A^{\sharp}
also determines the curvature
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}
where

[{\mathfrak{D}}_{a},{\mathfrak{D}}_{b}]A\;=\;\!-{\text{d}}_{A}\mathbbl{F}_{ab}~.

The last equation of (
3.2
) together with stability of
E
so that
H^{0}(X,{\text{End}\,}E)=0
implies
\mathbbl{F}_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\beta}}}=0
. That
\mathbbl{F}
is antihermitian implies
\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha\beta}=0
. Hence, going to holomorphic gauge implies
\mathbbl{F}_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\mu}}}=0
at first order and
\mathbbl{F}_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\beta}}}=0
at second order. In
[
3
]
we took
\mathbbl{F}
to be holomorphic as an assumption. Here we have gone one better and derived this.

Furthermore

[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\xi_{\beta}\;=\;0~,\qquad[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}]\mathcal{A}\;=\;{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\xi_{\alpha}\;=\;\!-{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}~,

We see that
\xi_{\alpha}
is a
{\mathfrak{D}}
-closed when viewed as an endomorphism valued
1,0
-form on the moduli space. Furthermore,
\xi_{\alpha}
is both
{\mathfrak{D}}
- and
{{\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}}}
-closed if and only if
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}=0
, a flat bundle on the space of deformations.

In terms of small deformations, we find that
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}=0
if and only if
\delta\mathcal{A}
is

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{A}&\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{\alpha}\delta y^{\beta}{\mathfrak{D}}_{(\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta)}\mathcal{A}+\cdots~.\end{split}

with
[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\mathcal{A}=0
.

On the other hand, if
\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}\neq 0
, we find an additional term

\begin{split}\delta\mathcal{A}&\;=\;\delta y^{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{\alpha}\delta y^{\beta}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A}+\frac{1}{2}\delta y^{\overline{\alpha}}\delta y^{\beta}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A}\cdots~,\end{split}

where because
[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\mathcal{A}=0
, the symmetrisation above is on autopilot.

This example provides the first part of the paradigm for the full heterotic geometry. The second part of the paradigm is to study deformations of the universal bundle, such that they preserve the holomorphic structure. In the baby example here it is simply
\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}=0
is invariant under perturbations. We pursue this in the next section.

In Kodaira-Spencer theory of complex structure there is a deep connection between the existence of a flat connection and integrability of deformations. Similarly, in the
tt^{*}
construction of Cecotti-Vafa, existence of a flat connection on the moduli space has a deep relationship with the theory being integrable. When the connection is flat it means we can find local coordinates in which we can eliminate the covariant derivatives and the study of our moduli space vastly simplified. Finally, in
[
14
]
flatness of parallel D-brane transport over a CY moduli space is reliant on imposing D-terms. So with this circle of ideas in mind, we press on.

4
Deformations of the universal bundle for the Atiyah problem

We now turn to the framework of universal geometry. A summary of results derived in
[
3
]
is provided in §
D
.

Universal geometry is a family of vector bundles which itself is a vector bundle and denoted
\mathpzc U\to\mathbbl{X}
, where
\mathbbl{X}
is topologically
\mathbbl{X}=\mathpzc M\times X
. It generalises the Kodaira-Spencer family of complex manifolds to families of heterotic supergravity theories. The base manifold
\mathbbl{X}
is a complex manifold characterised by its complex structure
\mathbbl{J}
, Hermitian form
\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}
,
\mathbbl{H}
, and a gauge field
\mathbbl{A}
. The real coordinates on
\mathbbl{X}
are
(y^{a},x^{m})
, while the holomorphic coordinates are
(y^{\alpha},x^{\mu})
. An Ehresmann connection,
c_{a}{}^{m}
, defines the product structure of
\mathbbl{X}
, implying that
X
is fibered over
\mathpzc M
. The field strength
S_{ab}{}^{m}
of this connection governs the integrability of the product structure. It is integrable if
S_{ab}{}^{m}=0
and this is the same as the Nijenhuis tensor vanishes. We assume the product structure is integrable so that
S=0
.
3
3

3

This vanishing is an underlying assumption in the Kodaira-Spencer construction of a family of complex manifolds over their Kuranishi or moduli space.

On each fibre of
\mathpzc U
there is a heterotic structure defining an
N=1

d=4
vacuum of the heterotic string. That is, for fixed
y_{0}\in\mathpzc M
, we have
[X,E]
with
\omega,J,B,A
satisfying the equations of Hull-Strominger (
2.1
). To realise this, the universal bundle
\mathpzc U
is equipped with a connection
\mathbbl{A}=A+A^{\sharp}
, where
A
is the connection on
E\to X
and
A^{\sharp}
is a connection on
\mathpzc M
, as described in §
3

\mathbbl{A}
as the extension of
A
to the universal bundle. Its field strength
\mathbbl{F}
takes the conventional form:
\mathbbl{F}=\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{A}+\mathbbl{A}^{2}
, and encodes both the structure of the bundle
E
and its deformations. The structure group of the vector bundle in the heterotic theory,
E\to X
, is denoted by
\mathcal{G}
, and under gauge transformations, the connection transforms as
{}^{\Phi}A=\Phi^{-1}A\Phi-\Phi^{-1}{\text{d}}\Phi
, where
\Phi
is valued in the adjoint of
\mathcal{G}
. Since
\Phi
also depends on parameters, the connection
\mathbbl{A}
extends to a connection on a
\mathcal{G}
-bundle
\mathpzc U\to\mathbbl{X}
. In principle, we could allow
A^{\sharp}
to transform within a larger structure group that contains
\mathcal{G}
as a subgroup, such as
\mathcal{G}\times\mathcal{H}
. However, for simplicity, we focus on
A^{\sharp}
transforming in
\mathcal{G}
, as the other components like
\mathcal{H}
decouple from the physical theory. Tangibility
[p,q]
of a form means that
p

\mathpzc M
and
q
-forms lie along

.

While on each fibre of the universal bundle, the full supersymmetry equations and Bianchi identity hold, it is shown in

N{J}=0~,\quad\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}=0~,\quad\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}~,\quad\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\mathbbl{F}^{2}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\text{Tr}\thinspace\mathbbl{R}^{2}~.

(4.1)

The
[1,1]
component of
\mathbbl{F}
corresponds to

\mathcal{A}
, and the

\mathbbl{H}

\mathpzc B_{a}

\delta\omega
and
\delta J

Given that

4.1
Warm-up: deform
\mathbbl{F}
with everything else fixed

Suppose
\{\mathpzc U_{Y}\}
is a family of universal bundles, where

is a label for family members. It is also a coordinate of an auxiliary moduli space
\widetilde{\mathpzc}M
. As a warm-up we consider deformations of the universal bundle connection, given by
\mathbbl{A}\to\mathbbl{A}+\delta\mathbbl{A}

\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}=0
, holding all other structures fixed. This corresponds to

where
\mathpzc U_{Y_{0}}
is our basepoint: the universal bundle about which we are deforming. Within
\mathpzc U_{Y_{0}}
we assume that for each

, the fibre
[X,E]
satisfies heterotic supersymmetry equations: F-terms, D-terms and holomorphic gauge.

Recall that
\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}=0
is equivalent to three statements
[
3

6
]
:

1.

the bundle
E\to X
is holomorphic
F^{0,2}=0
;

2.

first order deformations being holomorphic
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}=0
;

3.

the connection
A^{\sharp}
on the moduli space is holomorphic
\mathbbl{F}_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\beta}}}=0
.

The first follows from supersymmetry. The second from holomorphic gauge. The third we assumed to be true in

We demand

\delta

As a form
\delta\mathbbl{A}^{0,1}=\delta\mathcal{A}\oplus\delta\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}
. Decomposing according to tangibility:

\begin{split}{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\delta\mathcal{A}&\;=\;0~,\\

(4.2)

where we use that
{\hbox{$\bar{\partial}\hskip-3.80005pt$}}{A}={\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\oplus{\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}}

\delta\mathbbl{A}\sim\delta\mathbbl{A}+{\hbox{$\bar{\partial}\hskip-3.80005pt$}}\Psi~~\Rightarrow~~\delta\mathcal{A}\sim\delta\mathcal{A}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\Psi~,~~{\rm and}~~\delta\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}\sim\delta\mathcal{A}^{\sharp}+{\overline{{\mathfrak{D}}}}\Psi~.

(4.3)

The first equation in (
4.2
) implies deformations of
\delta\mathbbl{A}
restricted to the fibre must satisfy the familiar equation for deformations of
\mathcal{A}
. To first order in
\delta
, we can expand with respect to coordinates of
\mathpzc U_{Y_{0}}
:

\delta\mathcal{A}\in H^{0,1}(X,{\text{End}\,E})~,\quad\delta\mathcal{A}=\delta y^{\alpha}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}+{\overline{\partial}}_{\mathcal{A}}\mu_{\alpha})~,

(4.4)

where we use (
4.3
) to impose holomorphic gauge
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\alpha}}\mathcal{A}=0
(see
[
6
]
for details on how to do this) and
\alpha
runs over the dimension of the cohomology group,

and for this particular case is a harmonic representative of
H^{0,1}(X,{\text{End}\,E})
and

is an arbitrary section of
{\text{End}\,E}
.

Looking ahead to the full moduli problem, we want deformations of the universal bundle, when restricted to the fiber, to solve the heterotic equations of motion. So far, we have imposed the F-term condition, but we must also satisfy the D-term, as seen in the last line of (

-corrections. This is because

-corrections are expected to modify the D-terms while leaving the F-terms unchanged

\begin{split}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha\phantom{\beta}}\;=\;&~\text{i}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}-\text{i}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}~,\\[8.5359pt]
(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha\beta}\;=\;&-\text{i}\,S{}_{\alpha\beta}{}^{\mu}\,\omega_{\mu}\;=\;0~,\hskip 40.0pt(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\beta}}}\;=\;\text{i}\,S{}_{{\overline{\alpha}}{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{\overline{\mu}}\,\omega_{\overline{\mu}}\;=\;0~,\\[8.5359pt]
(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}\;=\;&-\text{i}\,S{}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\mu}}}\,\omega_{{\overline{\mu}}}+\text{i}\,S{}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}{}^{\mu}\,\omega_{\mu}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(5.6)

In order for the Bianchi identity below to hold we require
S=0
. A related reason is for the complex structure deformations being integrable to form a universal family of complex manifolds
X
over the moduli space
\mathpzc M
in the sense of Kodaira–Spencer (the total space being a complex manifold requires
S=0
). We can rewrite this as

\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}\;=\;\text{i}(\eth-\overline{\eth})\omega+\text{i}{\text{d}}y^{\alpha}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2})+\text{i}{\text{d}}y^{\overline{\beta}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\omega^{2,0}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\omega^{1,1})~.

(5.7)

While
\omega
is type
1,1
, its derivative
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}
is type
(2,1)\oplus(1,2)
:
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega{\;=\;}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}+{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}
, and this expresses the type changing property of variations with respect to complex structure.

As shown in

3
]

\mathpzc U_{Y_{0}}
the F-term

\mathbbl{H}=\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}~.

(5.8)

holds given certain F-terms and their deformations. By first considering the tangibility with one leg along
\mathpzc M
,
\mathbbl{H}_{a}=({\text{d}}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{a}

\begin{split}&\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{2,0}~=~{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\alpha}^{2,0}~=~0~,\\[5.69046pt]
&{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\alpha}^{1,1}~=~0~,\\[5.69046pt]
&\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{0,2}~=~0~.\end{split}

(5.9)

This captures three of the F-terms, all in holomorphic gauge – not surprising considering
\mathbbl{F}^{0,2}=0
captured the Atiyah equation in holomorphic gauge
[
3
]
. There is one remaining F-term and for this we turn the eye of Sauron to the Bianchi identity for
\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{H}
on
\mathbbl{X}

(5.10)

The curvatures
\mathbbl{F}
and
\mathbbl{R}
are of type
1,1
to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
and so only the type
(2,2)

We start with the component with one leg along
\mathpzc M
focusing on holomorphic variation with index

Meanwhile

is simplified using

\begin{split}{\text{d}}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha}~&=~\text{i}{\text{d}}({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{1,1}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2})~,\\
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}({\text{d}}^{c}\omega)~&=~2\text{i}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}-2\text{i}\,{\overline{\partial}}(\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,\omega_{\mu})+\text{i}({\partial}-{\overline{\partial}}){\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega\ ,\end{split}

and so

\begin{split}{\overline{\partial}}(\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{1,1})&\;=\;2\text{i}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\ F)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\ R)}\Big)~.\end{split}

(5.11)

Note: we get additional non-physical terms if

Now consider tangibility
[2,2
], with two legs along
\mathpzc M

\begin{split}{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\beta}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha}~&=\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A})}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\theta)}\Big)\ ,\\[5.69046pt]
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{{\overline{\beta}}}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha}~&=\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}F)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\theta^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{R}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}R)}\Big)~.\end{split}

(5.12)

We have used
\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathbbl{R}_{\alpha\beta}=0
.
With three legs, or four legs along the moduli space the equation identically vanishes due to
S=0
and
\mathbbl{F}_{ab}=\mathbbl{R}_{ab}=0
and Kähler property respectively. Using (
5.7

\begin{split}-\text{i}[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\omega^{0,2}~&=\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\mathcal{A})}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}\theta)}\Big)\ ,\\[5.69046pt]
{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{{\overline{\beta}}}-{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}(\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega})_{\alpha}~&=\!-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\,\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}F)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\theta^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{R}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}R)}\Big)~.\end{split}

The first equation is an identity. To see this, recall
[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\omega^{1,1}=([{\partial}_{\alpha},{\partial}_{\beta}]\omega)^{1,1}=0
and
[{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha},{\mathfrak{D}}_{\beta}]\omega^{0,2}=([{\partial}_{\alpha},{\partial}_{\beta}]\omega)^{0,2}=0
while the first term on the right hand side vanishes as we want a hermitian metric on deformations of the holomorphic bundle, the second term on the right hand side vanishes for the same reason, or can be computed explicitly by using
R_{\mu\nu}=0
, the curvature of the connection
\theta
. Hence, the only non–trivial equation is the second one and it is crucial to the construction of the Kähler potential. Simplifying it somewhat it reads:

\begin{split}&{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\omega^{1,1}\;=\;\!-\frac{\text{i}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\,\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{F}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}F)}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\theta\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\theta^{\dagger}+\mathbbl{R}_{\alpha{\overline{\beta}}}R)}\Big)\\[5.69046pt]
&+\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}\,\mu}{}^{\overline{\rho}}\omega_{\overline{\rho}}+\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\Delta_{\alpha\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\rho}\omega_{\rho}+2\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{\overline{\nu}}\omega_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}~.\end{split}

(5.13)

with last line coming from

\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\,({\mathfrak{D}}_{{\overline{\beta}}}\omega^{2,0})_{\mu}+\Delta_{{\overline{\beta}}}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}\,({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2})_{{\overline{\nu}}}

5.2

7.2
Small Gauge Transformations and deformations of connections

The point here is that there is a relation between deformations of connections on the moduli space and gauge symmetries of deformations of heterotic structures. A toy example is to consider a first order deformation of the gauge field
\delta A=\delta y^{a}{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A
, where the covariant derivative is necessary as gauge transformations can depend on parameters:

{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A\;=\;{\partial}_{a}A+[A^{\sharp}_{a},A]~,

Under
A^{\sharp}_{a}\to A^{\sharp}_{a}+\phi_{a}
we have
{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A\to{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}A+{\text{d}}_{A}\phi_{a}
, in other words
\delta A\to\delta A+{\text{d}}_{A}\delta\phi
. Taking the
0,1
-component, this we see a small gauge transformation corresponds precisely to a deformation of the connection
A^{\sharp}
. How does this relationship generalise to higher orders?

First consider the perspective of the gauge transformation taken to second order in deformations. We write

\begin{split}A&\rightarrow A^{\prime}\;=\;A+\delta A+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}A~,\\
\Phi&\rightarrow\Phi^{\prime}\;=\;\Phi+\delta\Phi+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\Phi~,\end{split}

where
\Phi
is an element of the structure group of the bundle
V
.
The action of
\Phi
on
A
is

\begin{split}{}^{\Phi^{\prime}}\!A^{\prime}&\;=\;\bigg(\Phi+\delta\Phi+\tfrac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\Phi\bigg)\bigg(A+\delta A+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}A\bigg)\bigg(1-\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi+\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{-1}\delta^{2}\Phi\bigg)\Phi^{-1}\\[3.0pt]
&\hskip 25.60747pt-{\text{d}}\bigg(\Phi+\delta\Phi+\frac{1}{2}\delta^{2}\Phi\bigg)\bigg(1-\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi+\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi\Phi^{-1}\delta\Phi-\frac{1}{2}\Phi^{-1}\delta^{2}\Phi\bigg)\Phi^{-1}~.\end{split}

We can isolate small gauge transformations by setting background transformations to identity, yielding

\begin{split}{}^{\Phi^{\prime}}\!A^{\prime}\big|_{\Phi=\mathbbl{1}}&=\hskip 14.22636ptA\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\delta A-{\text{d}}_{A}\delta\Phi\big|_{\Phi=\mathbbl{1}}\\
&\hskip 14.22636pt+\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\delta^{2}A-{\text{d}}_{A}\big(\delta^{2}\Phi\big|_{\Phi=\mathbbl{1}}-\delta\Phi\big|_{\Phi=\mathbbl{1}}\delta\Phi\big|_{\Phi=\mathbbl{1}}\big)\\

&\hskip 14.22636pt+\tfrac{1}{2}\Big(\delta^{2}A-{\text{d}}_{A}\delta^{2}\phi+[{\text{d}}_{A}\delta\phi,\delta\phi]-2[\delta A,\delta\phi]\Big)~,\\
\end{split}

where

and thus we arrive at

{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\overline{\beta}}K_{1}\;=\;\frac{\text{i}{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{8V}\int\omega^{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\left({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\mathcal{A}^{\dagger}\right)+\frac{1}{V}\int{\partial}_{\alpha}\omega\star{\partial}_{\overline{\beta}}\omega~.

(8.2)

The deformation
{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\overline{\beta}}K_{2}
gives

\begin{split}{\partial}_{\alpha}{\partial}_{\overline{\beta}}K_{2}&\;=\;-{\partial}_{\alpha}\left(\frac{1}{\int\text{i}\Omega{\overline{\Omega}}}\int\text{i}\Omega{\partial}_{\overline{\beta}}{\overline{\Omega}}\right)\;=\;\frac{1}{V}\int\Delta_{\alpha({\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}})}\Delta_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{({\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}})}\mbox{\,vol}-\frac{1}{V}\int\Delta_{\alpha[{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}]}\Delta_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{[{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}]}\mbox{\,vol}~.\end{split}

Using

{\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\omega^{0,2}\star{\mathfrak{D}}_{\overline{\beta}}\omega^{2,0}=2\,\Delta_{\alpha[{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}]}\Delta_{\overline{\beta}}{}^{[{\overline{\mu}}{\overline{\nu}}]}\mbox{\,vol}

and combining with (
8.2
), we arrive at (
8.1
).

9
Conclusions

In this work, we investigate first-order deformations of the universal bundle that preserve its defining conditions, which formally mirror the F-terms in heterotic string theory along with D-terms on the fibers. Our motivation is that first-order deformations of the universal bundle serve as a streamlined approach to second-order deformations within the original heterotic framework. Indeed, we uncover additional insights. By enforcing the structure of the universal bundle, we obtain vanishing conditions for certain second-order derivatives within the heterotic theory. We demonstrate the circumstances under which field strengths for connections on the moduli space are holomorphic, and, in specific cases, reduce to flatness. Extending this to the study of D-terms on the universal bundle, as inspired by prior work such as
[
14
]
, could reveal whether connections on the moduli space achieve flatness.

There are numerous potential avenues for further investigation. One could, for instance, relax the preservation of D-terms on fibres to explore a broader deformation space. As we maintain F-term constraints that remain unrenormalized in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
, this approach could impose conditions on the nature of
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
-corrections within the original heterotic theory. Our current findings include initial observations regarding the kernel of the
{\overline{\mathbbl{D}}}
-operator on the universal bundle, raising many questions, motivated by analogous results in heterotic theories. For example, with the explicit construction of the connection on the tangent bundle available in
[
3
]
, we might eliminate certain terms, in the spirit of
[
4
]
. Key questions include: Does this operator manifest a non-trivial curvature? Is it Hermitian-Yang-Mills? Can an adjoint operator be consistently defined, and what would its kernel represent?

Finally, it would be intriguing to explore the potential of universal deformation theory to impose constraints on the algebraic structures satisfied by deformations in the original heterotic theory. Prior research, such as that in
[
12
]
, approached this via superpotential constructions in heterotic theories, realising the F-terms we examine here. The universal bundle structure could provide a pathway to identifying the algebraic properties of deformations, especially once spurious modes are appropriately removed.

Appendix B
Connection symbols on
\mathbbl{X}

We enumerate some commonly used connection symbols in heterotic theories. We list the components in complex coordinates. We also give expressions for various divergences which are useful for calculations in the paper.

X
is a complex manifold with complex structure
J
and hermitian metric
g
. A vector bundle
V\to X
is hermitian if there is a hermitian inner product on sections of the bundle. For example
\mathpzc T_{X}
has a hermitian structure facilitated by the hermitian metric
{\text{d}}s^{2}=2g_{\mu{\overline{\nu}}}{\text{d}}x^{\mu}\otimes{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\nu}}
. The bundle is holomorphic (or has a holomorphic structure) if the total space
V
is a complex manifold with complex structure
\mathbbl{J}
and the projection map
\pi:V\to X
is holomorphic. That is, fibres consist only of holomorphic sections according to
\mathbbl{J}
. This is equivalent to the transition functions being purely holomorphic. For example, the bundle
\mathpzc T_{X}
is not a holomorphic bundle while
\mathpzc T_{X}^{1,0}
is a holomorphic bundle.

A connection
\nabla
on
\mathpzc T_{X}
is metric compatible if
\nabla g=0
. A connection
\nabla
is hermitian if it preserves the hermitian structure. That is, it is metric compatible and
\nabla J=0
. In terms of components, a hermitian connection has
\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\overline{\rho}}=\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{\overline{\nu}}{}_{\rho}=0
. There may be more than one Hermitian connection.

If
V
is a holomorphic bundle then a connection
\nabla
is compatible with its holomorphic structure if
\nabla^{0,1}={\overline{\partial}}
. In terms of components
\Gamma_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}=0
. For example, if
V
is a section of
\mathpzc T_{X}^{1,0}
then
\nabla
is compatible with the holomorphic structure if
\nabla^{0,1}V={\text{d}}x^{\overline{\mu}}\left({\partial}_{\overline{\mu}}V^{\nu}+\Gamma_{\overline{\mu}}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}V^{\rho}\right){\partial}_{\nu}={\overline{\partial}}V
.

Levi-Civita

Levi–Civita is the unique metric compatible connection with no torsion (symmetric in lower indices). It is hermitian if the manifold is Kähler but not in general.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}({\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}+{\partial}_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}})\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}-\frac{1}{2}H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}({\partial}_{{\overline{\rho}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}-{\partial}_{{\overline{\sigma}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\rho}}})\;=\;\frac{1}{2}H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\,g^{{\overline{\nu}}\sigma}({\partial}_{\mu}g_{{\overline{\rho}}\sigma}-{\partial}_{\sigma}g_{\mu{\overline{\rho}}})\;=\;\!-\frac{1}{2}H_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~.\end{split}

(B.1)

Bismut

The supersymmetry Killing spinor of heterotic supergravity (to first order in
{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}
) is covariantly constant with respect to the connection

\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny B}}=\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny LC}}-\frac{1}{2}H_{m}
. Writing
J
as a spinor bilinear it follows that
\omega
and
J
are covariantly constant with respect to this connection
\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}J=\nabla^{\text{\tiny B}}\omega=0
and so it follows
\Gamma^{\text{\tiny B}}
is metric compatible and hermitian. The torsion of
\Gamma^{\text{\tiny B}}
is completely antisymetric and equal to
H={\text{d}}^{c}\omega
i.e.
T^{m}{}_{np}{\;=\;}H^{m}{}_{np}
. A geometric statement is that there is a unique connection on
\mathpzc T_{X}
that is hermitian with completely antisymmetric torsion. This is the Bismut connection.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}-H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;g^{{\overline{\nu}}\sigma}({\partial}_{\mu}g_{\sigma{\overline{\rho}}}-{\partial}_{\sigma}g_{\mu{\overline{\rho}}})\;=\;\!-H_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~.\end{split}

(B.2)

Hull

While the spinor in heterotic is covariantly constant with respect to
\Gamma^{\text{\tiny B}}
, a different connection

\Gamma^{\text{\tiny H}}
appears in the heterotic action. It is not hermitian but has completely antisymmetric torsion with opposite sign
-H
. Hence,
\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny H}}=\Gamma_{m}^{\text{\tiny LC}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{m}
. This we call the Hull connection.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny H}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}+H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny H}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny H}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}({\partial}_{{\overline{\rho}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}-{\partial}_{{\overline{\sigma}}}g_{\mu{\overline{\rho}}})\;=\;H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny H}}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(B.3)

Chern

The Chern connection is the unique connection which is hermitian (
\nabla g=\nabla J=0
) and compatible with the holomorphic structure of
\mathpzc T_{X}^{1,0}
. The connection has no mixed indices. If the manifold is non–Kähler then it has torsion.

\begin{split}&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\mu}g_{\rho{\overline{\sigma}}}\;=\;g^{\nu{\overline{\sigma}}}\,{\partial}_{\rho}g_{\mu{\overline{\sigma}}}+H_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\rho}~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{\rho}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{\nu}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~,\\[2.84544pt]
&\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\nu}}}{}_{{\overline{\rho}}}\;=\;0~.\end{split}

(B.4)

Divergences

The divergence of a vector
\varepsilon^{\mu}

\nabla_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,\Gamma_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}~.

(B.5)

To compute this we need the following contraction

\begin{split}\Gamma^{\text{\tiny LC}}{}_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}+\frac{1}{2}H_{\mu\nu}{}^{\nu}~,\\[2.84544pt]
\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny B}}}{}_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}~,\\[5.69046pt]
\Gamma^{{\text{\tiny H}}}{}_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}\;=\;\Gamma^{\text{\tiny CH}}{}_{\nu}{}^{\nu}{}_{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}+H_{\mu\nu}{}^{\nu}~.\end{split}

\begin{split}\nabla_{\mu}^{\text{\tiny LC}}\varepsilon^{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon^{\mu}\,H_{\mu\nu}{}^{\nu}~,\\[2.84544pt]
\nabla_{\mu}^{{\text{\tiny B}}}\varepsilon^{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}~,\\[5.69046pt]
\nabla_{\mu}^{{\text{\tiny H}}}\varepsilon^{\mu}\;=\;\nabla_{\mu}^{\text{\tiny CH}}\varepsilon^{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\varepsilon^{\mu}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}+\varepsilon^{\mu}\,H_{\mu\nu}{}^{\nu}\end{split}

(B.7)

As for the vector-valued form
\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}

\nabla_{\mu}\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}+\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\,\Gamma_{\rho}{}^{\rho}{}_{\mu}-\Gamma_{\mu}{}^{{\overline{\rho}}}{}_{{\overline{\nu}}}\,\Delta_{{\overline{\rho}}}{}^{\mu}~,

(B.8)

and the four choices above give

\begin{split}\nabla_{\mu}^{\text{\tiny LC}}\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}&\;=\;{\partial}_{\mu}\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}+\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\,{\partial}_{\mu}\log{\sqrt{g}}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta_{{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\,H_{\mu\rho}{}^{\rho}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta^{\mu\rho}\,H_{\mu\rho{\overline{\nu}}}~,\\[2.84544pt]

{}^{\Phi,\Psi}B\;=\;B+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(Y\!A-Z\Theta\big)+U\!-W\Big)~,

(D.2)

with

Y\;=\;{\text{d}}\Phi\Phi^{-1}~,\qquad Z\;=\;{\text{d}}\Psi\Psi^{-1}~,

and
U
and
W
are such that
{\text{d}}U{\;=\;}\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\thinspace(Y^{3})
and
{\text{d}}W{\;=\;}\frac{1}{3}\text{Tr}\thinspace(Z^{3})
and
\Psi
denotes gauge transformations of the connection
\Theta
on
\mathpzc T_{X}
.

As
H
is gauge invariant its variation with respect to the parameters can simply be given as a partial derivative. In this way we arrive at a relation of the form

{\partial}_{a}H\;=\;{\text{d}}\mathpzc B_{a}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(D_{a}A\,F\big)+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big(D_{a}\Theta R\big)~.

(D.3)

This relation identifies a gauge invariant quantity
\mathpzc B_{a}
that is defined up to the addition of a
d
-closed form.

Let us define extended forms of
B
and
H
that are related by

\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{B}-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big({\text{CS}}[\mathbbl{A}]-{\text{CS}}[\mathbbl{\Theta}]\Big)~,~~~\text{where}~~~{\text{CS}}[\mathbbl{A}]\;=\;\text{Tr}\thinspace\!\left(\mathbbl{A}\,\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{A}+\frac{2}{3}\mathbbl{A}^{3}\right)~.

(D.4)

It is pleasing that the important quantity
\mathpzc B_{a}
turns out to be a mixed component of the gauge invariant tensor
\mathbbl{H}

\mathpzc B_{a}\;=\;\frac{1}{2}\mathbbl{H}_{amn}{\text{d}}x^{m}{\text{d}}x^{n}~.

We take
\mathbbl{H}
to satisfy an extended supersymmetry relation and a Bianchi identity

\mathbbl{H}\;=\;\mathbbl{d}^{c}\mathbbl{\hskip 1.0pt\bbomega}~,\quad\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{H}~=-\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{4}\Big(\text{Tr}\thinspace{\mathbbl{F}^{2}}-\text{Tr}\thinspace{\mathbbl{R}^{2}}\Big)\ ,

whose mixed components give important relations (
5.9
), (
5.11
) and (
5.13
) among the heterotic moduli.

So far, we have discussed the consequence of allowing the gauge functions
\Phi
and
\Psi
to be functions of both
x
and
y
. One further consequence, is the introduction of a covariant basis of forms and a corresponding dual basis of vectors

\begin{split}e^{m}&\;=\;{\text{d}}x^{m}+c_{a}{}^{m}{\text{d}}y^{a}~,e^{a}\;=\;{\text{d}}y^{a}~,\\[5.0pt]
e_{m}&\;=\;{\partial}_{m}~,e_{a}\;=\;{\partial}_{a}-c_{a}{}^{m}{\partial}_{m}~.\end{split}

(D.5)

The quantity
c^{m}{\;=\;}c_{a}{}^{m}{\text{d}}y^{a}
is a connection which transforms, under
x\to\tilde{x}(x,y)
, in the form

c^{\widetilde{m}}\;=\;\frac{\partial x^{\widetilde{m}}}{\partial x^{n}}\,c^{n}-\frac{\partial x^{\widetilde{m}}}{\partial y^{b}}\,{\text{d}}y^{b}~,

(D.6)

and this ensures that the forms
e^{m}
and vectors
e_{a}
transform as expected

e^{\widetilde{m}}\;=\;\frac{\partial x^{\widetilde{m}}}{\partial x^{n}}\,e^{n}~,~~~e_{\widetilde{a}}\;=\;\frac{\partial y^{b}}{\partial y^{\widetilde{a}}}\,e_{b}~.

With these basis forms, we write the extended vector potential, for example, as

\mathbbl{A}\;=\;A_{m}e^{m}+A^{\sharp}_{a}{\text{d}}y^{a}~,~~~\text{with}~~~A^{\sharp}_{a}\;=\;\Lambda_{a}-A_{m}c_{a}{}^{m}~.

The connection
c_{a}{}^{m}
also plays an important geometrical role in relation to the fibration
\mathbbl{X}
, in that it determines an almost product structure
\mathbbl{L}
that provides a splitting of the tangent space of the fibration
\mathcal{T}{X}
into vertical and horizontal subspaces. Being a fibration,
\mathbbl{X}
naturally encodes a vertical projection
\mathbbl{X}\longrightarrow\mathpzc M
. A horizontal structure, equivalent to a local choice of
c_{a}{}^{m}
, is not invariantly defined. The freedom inherent in choice of
c_{a}{}^{m}
corresponds precisely to the freedom to make coordinate transformations. This splitting of the tangent space into a vertical and horizontal component is a product structure
[
18
]
. We take it to be integrable, which as described in
[
3
]
does not mean we can set
c_{a}{}^{m}=0
; instead it simply means the curvature associated to the Ehreshman connection vanishes. In fact, the product structure and complex structure of
\mathbbl{X}
have a non-vanishing Frölicher–Nijenhuis bracket and our convention will be to utilise the Frobenius theorem to find complex coordinates the diagonalise the complex structure, but not the product structure. Hence, the connection
c_{a}{}^{m}
remains part of our lives.

It is necessary to covariantise the de Rham operator
d
along the manifold
X
so that it defines a fiber-wise cohomology. This is described in detail in
[
3
]
, when it is not ambigious we simply denote it by
d
. There is a complentary operator
{\mathfrak{D}}_{a}
which describes how tensors on
X
evolve under a change in parameters. These are actually components of the de Rham operator
\mathbbl{d}={\text{d}}\oplus{\mathfrak{D}}
on
\mathbbl{X}
. There is a third possible component which we have not written which is to do with the integrability of the product structure defined by
c_{a}{}^{m}
. As is described in
[
3
]
, the structure is required to be integrable for the universal geometry to capture some second order deformations of the hermitian form
\omega
and so we take as a starting assumption to be integrable. That being the case, the decomposition of the de Rham operator is as written.

The connection
c^{m}
can be identified with the cross term in the ‘minimal’ extended metric. We have two metrics that arise naturally:
the metric
g_{mn}
on the manifold
X
and the metric
g^{\sharp}_{ab}
on the moduli space
\mathpzc M
. If we combine these into a ‘minimal’ extended metric

\mathbbl{d}\mathbbl{s}^{2}\;=\;g_{mn}e^{m}e^{n}+g^{\sharp}_{ab}{\text{d}}y^{a}{\text{d}}y^{b}~,

and write this out in terms of the basis forms
{\text{d}}x^{m}
and
{\text{d}}y^{a}
we find the cross term

\mathbbl{g}_{ma}\;=\;g_{mn}c_{a}{}^{n}~.

The connection
c^{m}
appears also in an interesting way in relation to the variation of the complex structure of
X
. The first order variation of the complex structure is recorded in a form
\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}{\;=\;}\Delta_{\alpha{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}{\text{d}}x^{\overline{\nu}}
which is defined by

\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}\;=\;{\partial}_{\alpha}{\text{d}}x^{\mu}\Big|^{0,1}

It will also be shown later that
\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}
is also related to
c^{\mu}
by

\Delta_{\alpha\,{\overline{\nu}}}{}^{\mu}\;=\;-{\partial}_{\overline{\nu}}c_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}~.

(D.7)

This non-vanishing reflects the observation made above that we cannot set
c_{a}{}^{m}=0
.

To map from AQS to MarSpar we have

Note that this will send

\mathpzc Z=\mathpzc B+\text{i}\delta\omega\to-{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}=-(\mathpzc B-\text{i}\delta\omega)~.

In holomorphic gauge in AQS conventions
\mathpzc Z^{1,1}
is associated to holomorphic parameters as is the case in CY manifolds, say
[
30

{\overline{\partial}}\mathpzc Z_{\alpha}^{1,1}\;=\;2\text{i}\,\Delta_{\alpha}{}^{\mu}({\partial}\omega)_{\mu}+\frac{{\alpha^{\backprime}\,}}{2}\text{Tr}\thinspace\big({\mathfrak{D}}_{\alpha}\mathcal{A}\,F\big)~.

and so holomorphic parameters become associated to
{\overline{\mathpzc Z}}_{\alpha}^{1,1}
. To remedy this, the field redefinition could be augmented by one on the moduli space, e.g. complex conjugation of parameters.

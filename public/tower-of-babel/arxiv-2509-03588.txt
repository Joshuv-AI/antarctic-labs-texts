Dissociation of bulk and entanglement phase transitions in the Haldane phase
Yu-Chin Tzeng, Gunnar Möller

Abstract
We revisit the momentum-resolved entanglement spectrum (ES) of the spin-1/2 ladder in the Haldane phase, long believed to exhibit a des Cloizeaux-Pearson (dCP)-type sin ⁡ | k | \sin|k| dispersion. Using exact diagonalization up to 40 spins, we resolve two distinct branches at k = 0 k=0 and k = π k=\pi , which were previously interpreted as a single smooth mode due to SU(2) degeneracy and limited resolution. Breaking SU(2) symmetry via XXZ anisotropy opens a spin or neutral gap at k = π k=\pi , depending on the anisotropy direction, triggering an entanglement quantum phase transition that is disconnected from the bulk critical point. In the easy-plane regime, the entanglement ground state is unique in the S A z = 0 S_{A}^{z}=0 sector but becomes quasi-degenerate across S A z S_{A}^{z} sectors in the thermodynamic limit, consistent with spontaneous U(1) symmetry breaking. This separation between bulk and entanglement transitions demonstrates that the Li–Haldane correspondence can fail in topological ladders, even at the level of low-energy dispersions. These results revise the prevailing picture of ES in spin ladders under extensive bipartitioning, and suggest that the entanglement Hamiltonian, being nonlocal, can circumvent conventional constraints such as the Lieb-Schultz-Mattis and Mermin-Wagner theorems.

I
Introduction

Quantum entanglement has become a powerful tool for characterizing quantum phases of matter beyond the traditional symmetry-breaking paradigm. In particular, the entanglement spectrum (ES), defined as the set of eigenvalues
\{\xi_{i}\}
of the entanglement Hamiltonian
H_{E}
through the relation

\rho_{A}=\frac{e^{-\beta H_{E}}}{Z},

(1)

has proven especially valuable in revealing topological order and edge physics
Li and Haldane 2008
;
Ho et al. 2015
. Here,
\rho_{A}=\mathrm{Tr}_{B}|\psi_{0}\rangle\langle\psi_{0}|
is the reduced density matrix of subsystem
A
, obtained by tracing out subsystem
B
from the ground state
|\psi_{0}\rangle
. The inverse temperature
\beta=1
is set to unity, and
Z=\mathrm{Tr}(e^{-\beta H_{E}})
is a normalization constant ensuring
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{A})=1

1

1

With
\xi_{i}=-\ln\omega_{i}-\ln Z
, only differences
\xi_{i}-\xi_{0}
are meaningful, as the additive constant
\ln Z
is unknown and extensive. Literature “
\xi_{0}
” usually means
-\ln\omega_{0}
, where
\omega_{0}
is the largest eigenvale of
\rho_{A}
. Assuming
(\ln Z)/L=\mbox{const.}+O(1/L)
, finite-size scaling of “
\xi_{0}/L
” as an energy density can include
O(1/L)
and higher-order corrections.
.
According to the Li–Haldane conjecture
Li and Haldane 2008
, the low-lying ES reflects the conformal field theory (CFT)-like excitation spectrum of edges in topological phases, establishing what is now referred to as the edge-ES correspondence
Ho et al. 2015
.

Quasi-1D systems, such as spin-1/2 two-leg ladders, provide a natural setting to extend the Li–Haldane conjecture to extensive bipartitions, where the entanglement cut divides the system along its length rather than across its edge. A seminal study by Poilblanc
Poilblanc 2010
investigated the ES of the spin-1/2 Heisenberg ladder in the Haldane phase (up to
L=14
) and reported a smooth
\sin|k|
-like dispersion under momentum-resolved spectroscopy. The observed
\sin|k|
-like dispersion was seen as a signature of gapless edge modes, similar to the des Cloizeaux-Pearson (dCP) mode of a critical spin-1/2 chain
des Cloizeaux and Pearson 1962
, and has influenced the standard interpretation of the ES in topological ladder systems.
On the other hand, a dCP-like entanglement dispersion also appears in the trivial rung-singlet phase
Poilblanc 2010
, and was originally interpreted on equal footing with the Haldane phase, suggesting a unified edge interpretation across both phases.

Our new results challenge this interpretation. In the trivial phase, the
\sin|k|
shape arises from an effective thermal mapping between the entanglement Hamiltonian and the subsystem Hamiltonian,
H_{E}\approx\beta_{\mathrm{eff}}H_{A}
, as analytically confirmed by perturbation theory
Läuchli and Schliemann 2012
;
Zhu et al. 2019
and extended to non-Hermitian cases
Yang and Tzeng 2024
. In contrast, we show that the ES in the Haldane phase does not follow a smooth dCP-like dispersion; instead, it consists of two distinct branches at
k=0
and
k=\pi
that were previously unresolved due to SU(2) degeneracy and limited momentum resolution. This distinction undermines the supposed universality of the dCP mode in ES and calls for a revision of the established picture.

These observations highlight the need to reassess the structure and universality of the ES more broadly. Recent numerical advances have enabled momentum-resolved studies of spin ladders, but with important limitations: quantum Monte Carlo can reach large sizes in the rung-singlet phase yet lacks the resolution to resolve state counting
Yan and Meng 2023
, while DMRG captures gapless spectra in SU(2)-symmetric ladders
Li et al. 2024
though focused on the AKLT point and notably missing the
k=\pi/2
mode. Extensions of Lieb-Schultz-Mattis(LSM)-type arguments to open systems and entanglement Hamiltonians
Lieb et al. 1961
;
Oshikawa et al. 1997
;
Kawabata et al. 2024
;
Zhou et al. 2024
suggest that the ES may obey universal constraints, but it remains unknown whether these persist in topological settings with reduced symmetry. These challenges motivate an exact and momentum-resolved approach capable of disentangling thermal mappings from genuinely universal features.

In this paper, we therefore study the ES of spin ladders using exact diagonalization
Lin 1990
;
Laflorencie and Poilblanc 2004
. This method provides full momentum resolution and unbiased access to the entanglement Hamiltonian. By introducing a continuous tuning parameter
\Delta
for an XXZ deformation along the ladder legs, we identify three distinct regimes in the ES: a U(1) symmetry-broken phase for
\Delta<1
, a critical point at
\Delta=1
, and a gapped
Z_{2}
phase for
\Delta>1
. We then characterize the symmetry-broken phase and perform finite-size scaling, which reveals an entanglement transition clearly separated from the bulk transition at
\Delta_{c}^{GS}\simeq 1.18
, revealing a breakdown of the conventional Li–Haldane correspondence in this setting.

II
Model

We study a spin-1/2 XXZ two-leg ladder defined by
H=H_{A}+H_{B}+H_{AB}
with periodic boundary conditions,

\displaystyle H_{A}\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{L}(S_{A,j}^{x}S_{A,j+1}^{x}\!+\!S_{A,j}^{y}S_{A,j+1}^{y}\!+\!\Delta S_{A,j}^{z}S_{A,j+1}^{z}),

(2)

\displaystyle H_{B}\!=\!\sum_{j=1}^{L}(S_{B,j}^{x}S_{B,j+1}^{x}\!+\!S_{B,j}^{y}S_{B,j+1}^{y}\!+\!\Delta S_{B,j}^{z}S_{B,j+1}^{z}),

(3)

\displaystyle H_{AB}\!=\!J_{\mathrm{rung}}\sum_{j=1}^{L}\mathbf{S}_{A,j}\cdot\mathbf{S}_{B,j},

(4)

where
H_{B}
is identical to
H_{A}
. With fixed
J_{\mathrm{rung}}=-6
, the rungs form effective spin-1’s, and tuning
0.5\!\leq\!\Delta\!\leq\!1.5
drives a quantum phase transition from the Haldane to the Stripe-Néel phase at
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{GS}}\!\approx\!1.18

Hijii et al. 2005
. The global ground state always lies in the sector of total magnetization
(S_{A}^{z}+S_{B}^{z})=0
and total momentum
(k+q)\bmod 2\pi=0
, where
S_{A}^{z}=\sum_{j=1}^{L}S_{A,j}^{z}
and the momentum
k
(or
q
)
=2\pi j/L
is defined within subsystem A (B). Meanwhile, the entanglement Hamiltonian on a single leg exhibits even-odd momentum shifts. Following the convention in Ref.
Poilblanc 2010
, we shift the momentum as
k\to k-\pi
for system sizes
L=4m+2
. We denote entanglement levels by
\xi_{i}(S_{A}^{z},k)
, with entanglement ground state energy
\xi_{0}=\xi_{0}(0,0)
. Exact diagonalization up to 40 spins is performed
Lin 1990
;
Laflorencie and Poilblanc 2004
, exploiting magnetization and translational symmetry, reaching Hilbert space dimension
6.89\times 10^{9}
.

IV
Entanglement gap scaling

We now perform a finite-size scaling analysis of low-lying entanglement gaps to clarify the excitation structure. We define three types of gaps: (i) the spin gap:
\Gamma_{s}(S_{A}^{z})=\xi_{0}(S_{A}^{z},k)-\xi_{0}

k=0,\pi
for even and odd
S_{A}^{z}

\Gamma_{n}(S_{A}^{z})

k=\pi,0
for even and odd
S_{A}^{z}

S_{A}^{z}=0

\Gamma_{n}(0)=\xi_{0}(0,\pi)-\xi_{0}
defines the neutral gap. (iii) the linear-mode gap:
\Delta\xi(S_{A}^{z})
, taken at
k=2\pi/L
and
k=\pi-2\pi/L
for even and odd
S_{A}^{z}
, respectively.

Numerically, however,

Notably, the full ladder remains in the symmetry-protected topological (SPT) Haldane phase throughout this parameter range, since the XXZ anisotropy preserves the relevant protecting symmetries, i.e. parity, time-reversal, and

You et al. 2007

Gu 2010

Tzeng et al. 2021

V
Fidelity susceptibility

We define the entanglement fidelity susceptibility density as

\chi_{\mathrm{ES}}=\frac{1-|\langle\xi_{0}(\Delta)|\xi_{0}(\Delta+\delta)\rangle|^{2}}{\delta^{2}L}

(6)

which quantifies how sensitively the entanglement ground state
|\xi_{0}\rangle
responds to a small change
\delta=10^{-3}
in the anisotropy parameter
\Delta
. For comparison, we also compute the ground-state fidelity susceptibility density of the full system,
\chi_{\mathrm{GS}}=[1-|\langle\psi_{0}(\Delta)|\psi_{0}(\Delta+\delta)\rangle|^{2}]/(\delta^{2}L)
. As shown in Fig.
4
(a) and (b),
\chi_{\mathrm{GS}}
, peaks at
\Delta>1
, consistent with the known bulk quantum phase transition from the Haldane to the Stripe Néel phase
Hijii et al. 2005
. In contrast,
\chi_{\mathrm{ES}}
displays a distinct peak near the SU(2)-symmetric point
\Delta=1
, signaling a separate transition in the entanglement Hamiltonian that occurs entirely within the Haldane phase.

Figure
4
(c) shows that the peak values
\chi^{\mathrm{GS}}_{\mathrm{max}}
and
\chi^{\mathrm{ES}}_{\mathrm{max}}
both grow with system size
L
following power-law scaling, a hallmark of conventional second-order phase transitions. This behavior clearly contrasts with Berezinskii-Kosterlitz-Thouless (BKT) transitions in the single 1D XXZ chain Eq.(
2
), where the fidelity susceptibility density typically grows with logarithmic corrections or remains nearly flat for modest sizes (
L\lesssim 20
) and rarely exhibits sharp features in exact diagonalization studies
Sun et al. 2015
;
Yang 2007
.

To locate the critical points more precisely, we apply standard finite-size scaling analyses
Tzeng and Yang 2008
to the peak positions, as shown in Fig.
4
(d). The extrapolated values identify two distinct transitions:
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{ES}}=0.9988\pm 0.013
for the entanglement Hamiltonian and
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{GS}}=1.1813\pm 0.058
for the bulk system. The absence of any anomaly in
\chi_{\mathrm{ES}}
at the bulk transition point further emphasizes that the entanglement Hamiltonian can exhibit its own symmetry-driven phase transition, independent of the criticality of the underlying physical system.

The separation between
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{ES}}
and
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{GS}}
implies the existence of an intermediate regime,
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{ES}}<\Delta<\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{GS}}
, in which the bulk system remains in the Haldane phase, yet the entanglement spectrum has already undergone a transition. In this regime, the entanglement Hamiltonian exhibits spontaneous
Z_{2}
symmetry breaking, featuring a doubly degenerate ground state and gapped, quadratic modes at both
k=0
and
k=\pi
, while simultaneously, no linear mode survives in the entanglement spectrum that could be interpreted as representing an edge excitation. This challenges the extension of the original Li–Haldane conjecture
Li and Haldane 2008
to extensive bipartitions, which posits a correspondence between the entanglement spectrum and physical edge modes in topological phases. The absence of such correspondence in this regime suggests that the entanglement Hamiltonian captures physics beyond conventional boundary theories, and may not adhere to the same field-theoretic constraints.

VI
Conclusion

We have revisited the entanglement spectrum (ES) of the spin-
1/2
ladder in the Haldane phase and shown that the previously reported dCP-type
\sin|k|
dispersion is an artifact of SU(2) symmetry and finite-size effects. Large-scale exact diagonalization reveals instead two distinct branches at
k=0
and
k=\pi
, with the entanglement Hamiltonian undergoing a quantum phase transition at
\Delta_{c}^{\mathrm{ES}}\approx 1
inside the bulk Haldane phase.

In the easy-plane regime (
\Delta<1
), the
S_{A}^{z}=0
sector hosts a unique ground state separated by a finite neutral gap, violating the LSM theorem and exhibiting U(1) symmetry breaking despite being effectively one-dimensional, thus defying both LSM and Mermin-Wagner constraints. This anomaly originates from emergent long-range interactions in the entanglement Hamiltonian: at
\Delta=0.5
the interactions are sufficiently long-ranged to stabilize symmetry breaking, while at
\Delta=1.5
they become short-ranged, restoring the LSM scenario.

Our results demonstrate that the entanglement Hamiltonian can support symmetry-breaking transitions distinct from those of the physical system, including regimes with
Z_{2}
symmetry breaking and fully gapped quadratic modes.
This entails a significant revision of the prevailing understanding of the ES in topological ladders: entanglement Hamiltonians need not behave like physical edge theories, but instead encode nonlocal correlations that manifest in genuinely new physics. Looking ahead, it will be important to clarify the microscopic nature of the emergent long-range interactions, assess the generality of such violations across other topological phases and subsystem geometries, and develop a field-theoretic framework to capture their emergent excitations.

Appendix A
Lieb-Schultz-Mattis theorem

We briefly review the basic setup for the proof of the Lieb-Schultz-Mattis (LSM) theorem
Lieb et al. 1961
;
Auerbach 1994
, which proceeds by defining a
twist operator
. Applying this construction to the case of the entanglement Hamiltonian for subsystem A, we define the twist operator

\mathcal{O}_{A}=\exp\Big(i\frac{2\pi}{L}\sum_{j=1}^LjS_{A,j}^z\Big),

(7)

which rotates each spin incrementally in the
x
-
y
-plane, such that between the first and last site, the spin coordinates are rotated by
2\pi
about the
z
axis. The twisted state is constructed as

|\xi_{T}\rangle=\mathcal{O}_{A}|\xi_{0}\rangle,

(8)

where
|\xi_{0}\rangle
is the entanglement ground state, satisfying
\rho_{A}|\xi_{0}\rangle=\omega_{0}|\xi_{0}\rangle
, and
\omega_{0}
is the largest eigenvalue of the reduced density matrix
\rho_{A}
. Throughout this study,
|\xi_{0}\rangle
lies in the
S_{z}=0
sector, and
(S_{A}^{z},k)=(0,k_{0})
; where for
L=4m
,
k_{0}=0
, and for
L=4m+2
,
k_{0}=\pi
(equivalently,
k_{0}=\pi L/2\mod 2\pi
). Since
[\mathcal{O}_{A},S_{A}^{z}]=0
, the twisted state
|\xi_{T}\rangle
also lies in the same magnetization sector with
S_{A}^{z}=0
.

Within periodic boundary conditions, the lattice translation
\mathcal{T}
by one site along the chain acts as

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\mathbf{S}_{A,j}\mathcal{T}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{A,j+1},

(9)

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\mathbf{S}_{A,L}\mathcal{T}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{S}_{A,1},

(10)

and

\mathcal{T}|\xi_{0}\rangle=e^{ik_{0}}|\xi_{0}\rangle.

(11)

In the
S_{A}^{z}=0
sector, in which the entanglement ground state lies we have, following
Auerbach 1994
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}\mathcal{O}_{A}\mathcal{T}^{-1}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{A}\exp(i2\pi S_1^z)\exp\Big(-i\frac{2\pi}{L}S_A^z\Big)=\mathcal{O}_{A}e^{i\pi}.

(12)

Now we can calculate the eigenvalue of
\mathcal{T}
corresponding to the twisted state
|\xi_{T}\rangle
.

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}|\xi_{T}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{T}\mathcal{O}_{A}|\xi_{0}\rangle=(\mathcal{T}\mathcal{O}_{A}\mathcal{T}^{-1})\mathcal{T}|\xi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}_{A}e^{i\pi}e^{ik_{0}}|\xi_{0}\rangle=e^{i(k_{0}+\pi)}\mathcal{O}_{A}|\xi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{i(k_{0}+\pi)}|\xi_{T}\rangle

(13)

This shows that the twisted state carries momentum shifted by
\pi
.

The proof of the LSM theorem contains the following two steps:
(i) Prove that the twisted state is orthogonal to the entanglement ground state. In other words, the twisted state is an excited eigenstate of entanglement Hamiltonian. This step is simple since

\displaystyle\langle\xi_{0}|\xi_{T}\rangle=\langle\xi_{0}|\mathcal{O}_{A}|\xi_{0}\rangle=\langle\xi_{0}|(\mathcal{T}\mathcal{O}_{A}\mathcal{T}^{-1})|\xi_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\xi_{0}|\mathcal{O}_{A}e^{i\pi}|\xi_{0}\rangle=-\langle\xi_{0}|\xi_{T}\rangle,

(14)

so this scalar product must vanish.

(ii) In principle, the LSM theorem would require proving that the energy difference between
|\xi_{0}\rangle
and
|\xi_{T}\rangle
vanishes in the thermodynamic limit. However, since the explicit form of the entanglement Hamiltonian is unknown, such a proof cannot be carried out analytically. Instead, we examine this condition numerically by checking whether the
(S_{A}^{z},k)=(0,\pi)
sector becomes degenerate with the ground state. As shown in Fig. 2(b) of the main text, a clear and finite gap
\Gamma_{n}(0)
persists in the thermodynamic limit, demonstrating that the assumptions the LSM theorem places on the Hamiltonian do not hold for the entanglement Hamiltonian.

Bicomplex Hardy Classes of Solutions to Beltrami Equations and the Schwarz Boundary Value Problem
William L. Blair

Abstract
. We define Hardy classes of bicomplex-valued functions on the complex unit disk which solve bicomplex versions of the Beltrami and related equations. Using representations in terms of their complex-valued counterparts, we show these bicomplex-valued functions recover the boundary behavior associated with the classic holomorphic Hardy spaces. This work generalizes known results for complex-valued functions and continues recent work in the setting of bicomplex analogues of Hardy spaces of both holomorphic and generalized analytic functions. Also, we show Schwarz and Dirichlet boundary value problems associated with the bicomplex Beltrami equation are solvable and provide solution formulas.

1.
Introduction

We work to extend the theory of Hardy spaces and boundary value problems to bicomplex-valued functions on the complex unit disk that solve a bicomplex Beltrami equation.

In contrast to ordinary differential equations, there is no unifying theory of partial differential equations or their solutions. The study of differential equations that satisfy an ellipticity condition, with its close connection to application and modeling of physical phenomenon, is an ongoing success. In the complex plane, the first-order elliptic equations are reduced to the Beltrami equation (and some other related equations). See
[
20
,
50
]
.

The classic Hardy spaces of holomorphic functions on the disk are a triumph of complex function theory. Originally pioneered by the Riesz brothers (after Hardy), Zygmund widely communicated the fundamental results of these function spaces in
[
55
]
. See also
[
26
,
36
,
28
,
47
,
41
,
43
]
for more modern sources. Our interest in the Hardy spaces is that they are the holomorphic functions with
L^{p}
boundary values. Not only do these functions have boundary values in
L^{p}
, but these functions converge to these boundary values in the corresponding
L^{p}
norm. This makes them precisely the class of functions to consider in the pursuit of holomorphic solutions to boundary value problems.

Recently, Hardy classes of solutions to other first-order partial differential equations were considered as generalizations of the holomorphic Hardy spaces. Two common themes in these classes of functions are:

(1)

The class of functions is defined to be the collection of solutions to a complex partial differential equation with finite
H^{p}
norm, where this is the classic
H^{p}
norm of the holomorphic Hardy spaces.

(2)

The class of functions must recover the Hardy space boundary behavior of having
L^{p}
boundary values on the circle and convergence to those boundary values in the
L^{p}
norm.

Differential equations considered, so far, include the Vekua equation
[

,
25
,
24
,
44
]
, the Beltrami equation
[
54
,
3
,
1
,

,

,

,

,

,

]
, the conjugate Beltrami equation
[

,

,
5
]
, the general first-order elliptic equation
[
33
]
, the higher-order Cauchy-Riemann equations associated with polyanalytic functions
[

]
, the higher-order Vekua equations associated with the meta-analytic functions
[
38
,
18
]
, general nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations
[
14
]
, and the general higher-order iterated Vekua equations
[
19
]
.

The bicomplex numbers are a four real-dimensional (two complex-dimensional) extension, originaly by Segre
[
48
]
, of the complex numbers that, unlike the quaternions, have a commutative multiplication. However, division is not well defined for the bicomplex numbers, as there are bicomplex non-zero zero divisors. The bicomplex numbers are a useful tool in studying the complex stationary Schrödinger equation. Specifically, the differential operator associated with the complex stationary Schrödinger equation is factorized using bicomplex numbers into two first-order operators with one of them associated with a bicomplex version of the Vekua equation. Solutions of this bicomplex Vekua equation defined on the complex unit disk were studied in
[
23
,
22
,
37
,
46
]
. In particular, the author considered Hardy classes of solutions to this bicomplex Vekua equation in
[
16
]
and showed that they recover the classic Hardy space boundary behavior. See also
[
52
,
51
]
where Bergman spaces of solutions to the bicomplex Vekua equation were considered. The key feature of solutions to the bicomplex Vekua equation that allows the function theory of the complex Hardy classes of solutions to Vekua equations to be extended is that solutions to the bicomplex Vekua equation can be represented as a linear combination of a solution to a complex Vekua equation and a complex conjugate of a solution to a complex Vekua equation. This motivated the question: “For what other kinds of bicomplex partial differential equations are solutions representable by solutions of associated complex partial differential equations?” In this work, we show this behavior continues for the bicomplex Beltrami equation and some other related equations. Also, we work to show that two of the classic boundary value problems of the complex plane, the Schwarz and Dirichlet problems, can be solved for the bicomplex Beltrami equation. While this is known for the complex Beltrami equation, see
[
31
]
, both of these problems have not been well studied yet for bicomplex-valued functions. See
[
17
]
for another consideration of these problems for bicomplex-valued functions.

We describe the layout of the paper. In Section
2
, we provide relevant background and results from the literature. In Section
3
, we define the bicomplex version of the Beltrami equation, the associated Hardy classes of solutions, and prove the boundary behavior of these functions. We show these classes of functions have representations in terms of functions in Hardy classes of solutions to complex Beltrami equations and recover the boundary behavior of the classic complex holomorphic Hardy spaces. Also, we consider higher-order Beltrami equations in the complex and bicomplex settings. We demonstrate that solutions of these higher-order equations in a Hardy class have representation formulas in terms of solutions to the first-order equation and recover Hardy space boundary behavior. This is the first time Hardy classes of solutions to higher-order Beltrami equations have been considered. In Sections
4
and
5
, we recover many of the results from Section
3
when the bicomplex Beltrami equation is replaced with bicomplex variants of conjugate Beltrami equations or general first-order elliptic equations. In Section
6
, we consider bicomplex versions of the Schwarz and Dirichlet problem for the bicomplex Beltrami equation, show these problems are solvable, and provide formulas for the solutions.

2.1.
Complex Origins

Let
\mathbb{D}
denote the complex unit disk centered at the origin, and
\partial\mathbb{D}
denote its boundary. By
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
, we mean the Lebesgue space of complex-valued functions with
p
-integrable modulus over
\mathbb{D}
, and by
C^{k}(\partial\mathbb{D})
, the space of
k
-times differentiable functions on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. We denote by
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{D})
the Sobolev spaces of complex-valued functions that along with their derivatives up to order
k
are in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
. We denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial\mathbb{D})
the distributions on
\partial\mathbb{D}
. To disambiguate from other notions of conjugation that will be presented later, we indicate the complex conjugate of
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
by
z^{*}=x-iy
. We use
\frac{\partial}{\partial z}
and
\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}
for the usual first-order complex differential operators with respect to the variable
z
and its complex conjugate, respectively.

2.1.1.
Holomorphic Functions

To begin, we recall the classic definition of complex holomorphic functions defined on the complex unit disk, the associated Hardy spaces, and a theorem that describes the boundary behavior of the Hardy spaces.

Definition 2.1
.

We denote by
Hol(\mathbb{D})
the complex holomorphic functions on the complex unit disk, i.e., the collection of functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
such that

\frac{\partial f}{\partial z^{*}}=0.

Definition 2.2
.

For
0<p<\infty
, we denote by
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
the complex holomorphic Hardy spaces, i.e., the collections of functions
f\in Hol(\mathbb{D})
such that

||f||_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}:=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
26
,
36
,
41
,
47
,
28
]
)
.

A function
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
0<p<\infty
, has nontangential boundary values
f_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
at almost every point of
\partial\mathbb{D}
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta,

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})-f_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

2.1.2.
Beltrami Equations

The Beltrami equation is a well studied first-order elliptic partial differential equation in the plane. There is particular interest in the connection between solutions of the Beltrami equation and quasiconformal mappings. See

\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}

In

solves the complex Vekua equation

(1)

Also, by Proposition 3.2.3.1 of

2.2.1.
Basics of Bicomplex Numbers

We define the bicomplex numbers and recall many results we use from the literature for completeness.

Definition 2.22
.

The bicomplex numbers
\mathbb{B}
are the collection of elements of
\mathbb{C}^{2}
represented via the identification

(z_{1},z_{2})\in\mathbb{C}^{2}\leftrightarrow z_{1}+jz_{2}\in\mathbb{B},

where
j^{2}=-1
, with the usual component-wise addition and multiplication defined by

(u_{1}+ju_{2})(v_{1}+jv_{2})=u_{1}v_{1}-u_{2}v_{2}+j(u_{1}v_{2}+u_{2}v_{2}).

Definition 2.23
.

For
z=z_{1}+jz_{2}\in\mathbb{B}
, we say
z_{1}
is the scalar part of
z
, denoted by
\text{Sc}\,z
, and we say
z_{2}
is the vector part of
z
, denoted by
\text{Vec}\,z
.

Definition 2.24
.

For
w=\text{Sc}\,w+j\text{Vec}\,w\in\mathbb{B}
, we define the bicomplex conjugate of
w
to be

\overline{w}=\text{Sc}\,w-j\text{Vec}\,w.

Definition 2.25
.

For
z=x+iy\in\mathbb{C}
, i.e.,
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the bicomplexification of
z
to be

\widehat{z}=x+jy.

The next proposition is a feature of the bicomplex-valued functions that is pivotal to our method for studying the function classes in the sections that follow. We refer to this result as the idempotent representation.

Proposition 2.26

(Proposition 1
[
52
]
)
.

Let
w\in\mathbb{B}
. There exist unique
w^{\pm}\in\mathbb{C}
such that

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-},

where
p^{\pm}
are given by

p^{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(1\pm ji).

Furthermore,

w^{\pm}=\text{Sc}\,w\mp i\text{Vec}\,w.

Remark 2.27
.

The bicomplex numbers
p^{\pm}
satisfy the relationships

(p^{\pm})^{2}=p^{\pm},\quad p^{+}+p^{-}=1,\text{ and }\quad p^{+}p^{-}=0.

Definition 2.28
.

For
w\in\mathbb{B}
, we define the bicomplex norm of
w
, denoted by
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B}}
, as

||w||_{\mathbb{B}}:=\sqrt{\frac{|w^{+}|^{2}+|w^{-}|^{2}}{2}},

where
|w^{\pm}|
is the complex modulus of

.

Remark 2.29
.

It is immediate from the definition of the bicomplex norm
||\cdot||_{\mathbb{B}}
that, for every
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in\mathbb{B}
,

\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}|w^{\pm}|\leq||w||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|w^{+}|+|w^{-}|\right),

(2)

Also, for
w,v\in\mathbb{B}
, we have

||wv||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq\sqrt{2}\,||w||_{\mathbb{B}}\,||v||_{\mathbb{B}}.

(3)

Definition 2.30
.

For a positive real number
p
, we define
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
to be the collection of functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that

||f||_{L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}:=\left(\iint_{\mathbb{D}}||f(z)||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,dx\,dy\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

We define
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
to be the collection of functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that

||f||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}:=\sup_{z\in\mathbb{D}}||f(z)||_{\mathbb{B}}<\infty.

For a nonnegative integer

and
0<p\leq\infty
, we define
W^{k,p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
to be the collection of functions
f:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that

and its derivatives up to order

are in
L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
. The classes of functions
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
and

are defined analogously for functions on
\partial\mathbb{D}

Proposition 2.31

(Proposition 2.24
[
13
]
)
.

For

Definition 2.32
.

We define the bicomplex differential operators
\partial
and
\bar{\partial}
as

\partial:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}-j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right)

\bar{\partial}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+j\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\right).

2.3.
The Schwarz and Dirichlet Boundary Value Problems

In Section
6
, we consider boundary value problems associated with the Beltrami equation that we define in Section
3
. Here we record results from the literature that we generalize in Section
3
and appeal to in our justifications of the results we present.

The classic Schwarz boundary value problem is to find a holomorphic function with prescribed real part on the boundary, i.e., the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=g,\end{cases}

for a prescribed function on the circle
g
. Since imaginary parts of holomorphic functions are only well defined up to addition of constants, the problem is made well defined by requiring the imaginary part has a prescribed pointwise value, i.e., the boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=0,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=g,\\
\im\{w(0)\}=c,\end{cases}

for a prescribed function on the circle
g
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
. See
[
7
,
8
]
for background on this problem.

In
[
31
]
, the Schwarz boundary value problem was considered where a Beltrami equation replaces the Cauchy-Riemann equation. Explicit conditions for solvability and formulas for producing solutions are provided by the theorem below.

Theorem 2.41

(Theorem 3.1
[
31
]
)
.

The Schwarz problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial w}{\partial z}+f,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D}\\
\re\{w\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma,\\
\im\{w(0)\}=a,\end{cases}

for
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
such that
|\mu|\leq c<1
, for some constant
c
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D})
,
p>2
,
\gamma\in C(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})
, and
a\in\mathbb{R}
, is uniquely solvable and the solution is

\displaystyle w(z)

\displaystyle=\varphi(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\left((-\mu)^{k}T^{k}(f+\mu\varphi^{\prime})(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta(\zeta-z)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left.((-\mu)^{*})^{k}(T^{k}(f+\mu\varphi^{\prime})(\zeta))^{*}\frac{1-z\zeta^{*}}{\zeta^{*}(1-z\zeta^{*})}\right)\,d\xi\,d\eta,

where
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
,

\varphi(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\gamma(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}+ia,

and
T(\cdot)
is the operator defined by

T(g)(z):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\frac{g(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}+\frac{(g(\zeta))^{*}}{(1-z\zeta^{*})^{2}}\,d\xi\,d\eta.

In
[
19
]
, the author and B. B. Delgado showed existence of a unique solution for the Schwarz boundary value problem on the unit disk when the boundary condition is a distributional boundary value. See the definition and theorem below.

Definition 2.42
.

Let
f
be a function defined on the complex unit disk. We say that
f
has a boundary value in the sense of distributions

, also called a distributional boundary value, if, for every
\gamma\in C^{\infty}(\partial\mathbb{D})

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}f(re^{i\theta})\gamma(\theta)\,d\theta

exists.

Theorem 2.43

(Theorem 6.9
[
19
]
)
.

The Schwarz boundary value problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w}{\partial z^{*}}=f,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w_{b}\}=g,\\
\im\{w(0)\}=c,\end{cases}

for
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{D})
,
g\in\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\partial\mathbb{D})
, and

, is uniquely solved by

w=\frac{1}{2\pi}\langle g,P_{r}(\theta-\cdot)+iQ_{r}(\theta-\cdot)\rangle+ic-\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\left(\frac{f(\zeta)}{\zeta}\,\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}+\frac{(f(\zeta))^{*}}{\zeta^{*}}\,\frac{1+z\zeta^{*}}{1-z\zeta^{*}}\right)\,d\xi\,d\eta,

where

,
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
, and

P_{r}(\theta)=\frac{1-r^{2}}{1-2r\cos(\theta)+r^{2}}

and

Q_{r}(\theta)=\frac{2r\sin(\theta)}{1-2r\cos(\theta)+r^{2}}

are the Poisson kernel and the conjugate Poisson kernel on
\mathbb{D}
, respectively.

In
[
17

, the author constructed a bicomplex version of the Schwarz boundary value problem. For this problem, a solution must satisfy (
4

This problem is solvable both in the classical case of continuous boundary functions and in the more general setting of the boundary condition being with respect to boundary values in the sense of distributions. Theorem 2.44

The Beltrami Equation

In this section, we define a bicomplex version of the classic Beltrami equation using the bicomplex
\bar{\partial}

[

,

,

,
15
]
and bicomplex nonhomogeneous Cauchy-Riemann equations in
[

]
. Using these equations, we define Hardy classes of bicomplex-valued solutions and show these functions exhibit properties associated with the classical Hardy spaces. This extends the work found in
[

3

3.1.
Definition and Representation

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
be such that there exists a real constant
c
and
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
. We call any equation of the form

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\partial w

a
\mathbb{B}
-Beltrami equation.

The next proposition shows that solutions of
\mathbb{B}
-Beltrami equations are representable, using the idempotent representation, in terms of solutions of
\mathbb{C}
-Beltrami equations. Since the function and the components of the idempotent representation are comparable in the bicomplex norm, this is an invaluable tool for the analysis of solutions to
\mathbb{B}
-Beltrami equations. This idea was previously used in the
\mu\equiv 0
case (
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic) in
[
13
]
and in the case of solutions to
\mathbb{B}
-Vekua equations in
[
16
,
15
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
be such that there exists a real constant
c
and
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
. Every solution
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
of

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\partial w

(6)

has the form

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-},

where
w^{+}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
solves the
\mathbb{C}
-Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}

and
w^{-}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
solves the
\mathbb{C}
-Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.26
, every function
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
has the form

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-},

where
w^{\pm}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
. Similarly, an idempotent representation holds for
\mu
. So, if
w
solves (
6
), then, by Remark
2.33
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}w

\displaystyle=\mu\partial w

\displaystyle\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})

\displaystyle=(p^{+}\mu^{+}+p^{-}\mu^{-})\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})

\displaystyle p^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\mu^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}+p^{-}\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}.

Since idempotent representations are unique, it follows that

\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}=\mu^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}.

Applying complex conjugation to both sides of the left-hand side equation in the display above, we have

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}.

∎

3.2.
Hardy Spaces

Next, we define the Hardy classes of solutions to bicomplex Beltrami equations. Note that as in Definitions
2.39
and

2.40
, the size condition is with respect to an
H^{p}
norm using the
\mathbb{B}
-norm instead of complex modulus.

Definition 3.3
.

For
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that there exists a real constant
c
and
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
, we define the
\mathbb{B}
-Beltrami-Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{\text{Bel},\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
to be those functions
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
that solve

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\partial w

and satisfy

||w||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}:=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

In
[
13
]
, the author proved that a function is in a
\mathbb{B}
-holomorphic Hardy space if and only if the component functions in the idempotent representation are themselves elements of the classical Hardy spaces of complex-valued functions. See Theorem 4.1 of
[
13
]
. This idea was extended in
[
16
]
where the author showed that a function is in a Hardy space of solutions to a
\mathbb{B}
-Vekua equation if and only if the component functions in the idempotent representation are elements of complex-valued Hardy spaces of solutions to the complex Vekua equation, as studied in
[
32
,
44
,
39
,
5
,
6
]
. See Theorem 4.10 of
[
16
]
. The next theorem extends Proposition
3.2
and shows that the relationship between
\mathbb{B}
-valued Hardy classes and the associated
\mathbb{C}
-valued Hardy classes is recovered for
H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
functions also.

Theorem 3.4
.

For
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
,
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
if and only if
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.2
, if
w
solves
\bar{\partial}w=\mu\partial w
, then

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}.

By (
2
), for every
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+}(re^{i\theta}))^{*}|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+}(re^{i\theta}))^{*}|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

and

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Therefore,
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now, if
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
, then
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}
solves
\bar{\partial}w=\mu\partial w
by direct computation. Also, by (
2
), we have for every
r\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(|w^{+}(re^{i\theta})|+|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+}(re^{i\theta}))^{*}|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+}(re^{i\theta}))^{*}|^{p}\,d\theta+\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

and
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
.

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

For
\mu\in W^{1,s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
,
s>2
, such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
, every
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
is an element of
L^{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, for every
0<m<2p
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
, if
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, then
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
. By Theorem
2.6
, since
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
(w^{+})^{*},w^{-}\in L^{m}(\mathbb{D})
, for
0<m<2p
. Hence,
w^{+}\in L^{m}(\mathbb{D})
, for
0<m<2p
. By Proposition
2.31
,
w\in L^{m}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, for
0<m<2p
.
∎

3.3.
Boundary Behavior

Now, we show that functions in
H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
have nontangential boundary values in
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and converge to those nontangential boundary values in the
L^{p}
norm. This generalizes Theorem
2.3
to the setting of Hardy classes of solutions to
\mathbb{B}
-Beltrami equations.

Theorem 3.6
.

For
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
, every
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
has a nontangential boundary value
w_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.4
,
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
if and only if
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
. By Theorem
2.7
,
(w^{+})^{*}
and
w^{-}
have nontangential boundary values
(w^{+})^{*}_{nt}
and
w^{-}_{nt}
, respectively,
(w^{+})^{*}_{nt},w^{-}_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})-(w^{+})^{*}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0,

(7)

and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})-w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

(8)

Since
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}
, it follows that
w_{nt}=p^{+}w^{+}_{nt}+p^{-}w^{-}_{nt}
exists, where
w^{+}_{nt}=((w^{+})^{*}_{nt})^{*}
. Since
|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|=|(w^{+})^{*}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|
, for all
e^{i\theta}\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
w^{+}_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
. Thus, by (
2
), we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|+|w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|\right)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
, and
w_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
.

Now, for
r\in(0,1)
, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||p^{+}(w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta}))+p^{-}(w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{-}(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|+|w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|\right)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends only on
p
, by (
2
). Therefore, by (
7
) and (
8
),

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq\lim_{r\nearrow 1}C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{+}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w^{-}_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)=0.

∎

Theorem 3.7
.

For
\mu\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
,
q>2
, such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
, every
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
with nontangential boundary value
w_{nt}\in L^{s}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, where
s>p
, is an element of
H^{s}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
.

Proof.

Since
w_{nt}\in L^{s}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, it follows by Prop
2.26
and Proposition
2.31
that
w_{nt}=p^{+}w^{+}_{nt}+p^{-}w^{-}_{nt}
and
w^{\pm}\in L^{s}(\partial\mathbb{D})
. Since
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, it follows by Theorem
3.4
that
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
. Since
|w^{+}_{nt}(z)|=|(w^{+})^{*}(z)|
, for every
z\in\mathbb{D}
, it follows that
(w^{+}_{nt})^{*}\in L^{s}(\partial\mathbb{D})
. By Theorem
2.8
, since
(w^{+}_{nt})^{*},w^{-}_{nt}\in L^{s}(\partial\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{s}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{s}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
. By Theorem
3.4
, since
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{s}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{s}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
, it follows that
w\in H^{s}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
.

∎

Theorem 3.8
.

For
\mu\in W^{1,q}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
,
q>2,
such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
, every
w\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
with nontangential boundary value
w_{nt}
that vanishes on a set
E\subset\partial\mathbb{D}
of positive measure is identically equal to zero.

Proof.

If
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, then
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{Bel,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
by Theorem
3.4
. By Theorem
2.7
,
(w^{+})^{*}
has nontangential boundary value
(w^{+})^{*}_{nt}
, and
w^{-}
has nontangential boundary value
w^{-}_{nt}
. Since
w_{nt}\equiv 0
on
E
, it follows that

0\equiv w_{nt}=p^{+}w^{+}_{nt}+p^{-}w^{-}_{nt}.

Since
p^{+}
and
p^{-}
are linearly independent, it follows that

w^{+}_{nt}\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad w^{-}_{nt}\equiv 0

on
E
. Since
|(w^{+})_{nt}^{*}(z)|=|w^{+}_{nt}(z)|
, for all
z\in\partial\mathbb{D}
, it follows that

(w^{+})^{*}_{nt}\equiv 0

on
E
. By Theorem
2.9
, we have
(w^{+})^{*}\equiv 0\equiv w^{-}
. Hence,
w\equiv 0
.
∎

3.4.
Higher-Order Iterated Beltrami Equation

Next, we define a natural higher-order generalization of the Beltrami equation and analyze its solutions. This generalization is realized by iterating the differential operator associated with the first-order equation. Constructing a partial differential equation by iteration of an operator associated with a well-studied equation was previously considered in the setting of polyanalytic functions (see
[
4
]
and many others) where the differential operator is the classic Cauchy-Riemann operator associated with holomorphic functions and the setting of Vekua equations in the complex setting in
[
10
,
11
,
42
,
38
,
18
,
19
]
and the bicomplex setting in
[
16
]
. Since this has not been thoroughly studied for Beltrami equations (even in the complex case), we consider it here and begin in the complex setting.

Definition 3.9
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
, and
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
. A
higher-order iterated Beltrami equation
(abbreviated HOIB-equation) is any equation of the form

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n}w=0.

The next representation theorem extends Theorem 2.2 of
[
30
]
(see also Theorem 2.3 of
[
29
]
) where the same conclusion was proved for a restricted class of higher-order iterated Beltrami equations. Our proof uses the same argument that justifies representation formulas for polyanalytic functions in
[
4
]
and solutions to higher-order iterated Vekua equation in
[
19
]
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
. Every solution
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
of

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n}w=0

(9)

has the form

w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k},

(10)

where
\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z}
, for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
.

Proof.

Functions of the form of (
10
) are solutions of (
9
) by direct computation.

For the converse, the case
n=1
is clear. Now, suppose the representation holds for all
n
such that
1\leq n\leq m-1
. Let
w
be any solution of

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n}w=0

Hence,

\gamma:=\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)w

is a solution of

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n-1}\gamma=0.

By the induction hypothesis, there exist
\{w_{k}\}_{k=0}^{m-2}
such that

\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z},

for every
0\leq k\leq m-2
, and

\gamma=\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)\left(w-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}(z^{*})^{k+1}w_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\left((z^{*})^{k}w_{k}+\frac{1}{k+1}(z^{*})^{k+1}\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{1}{k+1}(z^{*})^{k+1}\frac{\partial w_{k}}{\partial z}\right)

\displaystyle=0.

Thus,

w=\sigma+\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}(z^{*})^{k+1}w_{k},

where
\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial\sigma}{\partial z}
. Therefore, letting
\tilde{w}_{0}=\sigma
and
\tilde{w}_{k}=w_{k+1}
, for
0\leq k\leq m-2
, we have

w=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}(z^{*})^{k}\tilde{w}_{k},

where
\frac{\partial\tilde{w}_{k}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial\tilde{w}_{k}}{\partial z}
, for every
0\leq k\leq m-1
.
∎

Now, we define Hardy classes of solutions to higher-order iterated Beltrami equations.

Definition 3.11
.

For
p
a positive real number,
n
a positive integer, and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
, we define the HOIB-Hardy classes
H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
to be the collection of functions
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
that solve

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{n}w=0

and satisfy

\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k}w(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Remark 3.12
.

Note, for
\mu\equiv 0
,
H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
reduces to the poly-Hardy classes of functions. See
[
53
]
.

The motivation for the definition above becomes clear with the realization that if
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}
, where each
w_{k}
solves the classic first-order Beltrami equation, then we can represent each
w_{k}
by

w_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}(z^{*})^{j}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k+j}w,

(11)

for
0\leq k\leq n-1
. A similar representation holds for the components of solutions to higher-order iterated Vekua equations (see
[
10
,
18
,
19
]
). Following the argument from Theorem 4.1 of
[
19
]
, we use this representation to show the following relationship between Hardy classes of solutions to first-order Beltrami equations and the associated Hardy classes of solutions to higher-order equations Beltrami equations.

Theorem 3.13
.

For
p
a positive real number,
n
a positive integer, and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
,
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
if and only if
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
, for
0\leq k\leq n-1
.

Proof.

First, suppose that
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}
, and
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
. So,

\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k}w=\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}C_{k,j}(z^{*})^{j-k}w_{j},

for every
0\leq k\leq n-1
, where the
C_{k,j}
are constants that depend only on
k
and
j
. Observe that

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k}w(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}C_{k,j}(re^{-i\theta})^{j-k}w_{j}(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=k}^{n-1}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|w_{j}(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

where
C
is a constant that depends only on
n
and
p
. Therefore,
w\in H_{Bel,\mu}^{n,p}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now, suppose that
w\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
. By (
11
), we have

\displaystyle\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|w_{k}(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}C_{k,j}(re^{-i\theta})^{j}\,\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k+j}w(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=0}^{n-1-k}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}-\mu\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)^{k+j}w(re^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

where the
C_{k,j}
are constants that depend on only
k
and
j
and
C
is a constant that depends on only
n
and
p
, for any
0\leq k\leq n-1
. Thus,
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
.

∎

Theorem 3.14
.

For
p
a positive real number,
n
a positive integer, and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
, every
w\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
has a nontangential boundary value
w_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})-w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.13
, every
w\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
is representable as

w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k},

where
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D})
, for all
k
. By Theorem
2.7
, every
w_{k}
has a nontangential boundary value
w_{k,nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta=0.

Since
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k}
, it follows that
w_{nt}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}(z^{*})^{k}w_{k,nt}
exists and is in
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
. Now, for
r\in(0,1)
, observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})-w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left|\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}r^{k}e^{-ik\theta}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}e^{-ik\theta}w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})\right|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|r^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|r^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})+w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|r^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right),

where
C_{p,n}
is a constant that depends only on
p
and
n
and is not necessarily the same from line to line. Therefore, by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem,

\displaystyle\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w(re^{i\theta})-w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\left(\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|r^{k}-1|^{p}\,|w_{k}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle=0.

∎

Now, we extend the preceding ideas into the setting of bicomplex numbers. Solutions of differential equations constructed by iteration of the differential operator were previously considered in the bicomplex setting in
[
13
,
16
]
.

Definition 3.15
.

Let
n
be a positive integer,
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
, and
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
. A
bicomplex higher-order iterated Beltrami equation
(abbreviated
\mathbb{B}
-HOIB-equation) is any equation of the form

\left(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial\right)^{n}w=0.

The next representation extends Theorem
3.10
from the complex setting (that also holds for solutions of higher-order iterated Vekua-type equations
[
19
,
16
]
) that allows us to represent the solution of the higher-order equation in terms of a finite sum of solutions to the first-order equation. With this representation, we prove properties of the solutions to the first-order equation extend to solutions of the higher-order equation.

Theorem 3.16
.

Let
n
be a positive integer and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
for some positive real number
c
. Every solution
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
of

\left(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial\right)^{n}w=0

(12)

has the form

w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}w_{k},

(13)

where
\bar{\partial}w_{k}=\mu\partial w_{k}
, for every
k
.

Proof.

Observe that any function of the form
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}w_{k}
, where
\bar{\partial}w_{k}=\mu\partial w_{k}
for every
k
, is a solution of (
12
) by direct computation.

For the other direction, we proceed by induction. The
n=1
case is trivial. Suppose that for every
0\leq n\leq m-1
, if
w
is a solution of
(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)^{n}w=0
, then
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}w_{k}
, where
\bar{\partial}w_{k}=\mu\partial w_{k}
for every
k
. Let
f
be a solution of
(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)^{m}f=0
. So,
g=(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)f
solves
(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)^{m-1}g=0
and, by the induction hypothesis, must have the form

(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)f=\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}f_{k},

where
\bar{\partial}f_{k}=\mu\partial f_{k}
, for every
k
. Observe that

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)\left(f-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k+1}f_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}f_{k}-(\bar{\partial}-\mu\partial)\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k+1}f_{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}f_{k}-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}f_{k}=0.

Hence,

f-\sum_{k=0}^{m-2}\frac{1}{k+1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k+1}f_{k}=\phi,

where
\bar{\partial}\phi=\mu\partial\phi
. Thus,

f=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}\tilde{f_{k}},

where
\tilde{f}_{k+1}:=\frac{1}{k+1}f_{k}
,
0\leq k\leq m-2
and
\tilde{f_{0}}=\phi
. Therefore, the representation (
13
) holds for all orders.

∎

Remark 3.17
.

Recall that solutions of (

We also recover the classic Hardy space boundary behavior.

Theorem 3.22
.

For
p
a positive real number,
n
a positive integer, and
\mu\in W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
such that
||\mu||_{W^{n-1,\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
, for some positive real number
c
, every
w\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
has a nontangential boundary value
w_{nt}\in L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})-w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta=0.

Proof.

Let
w\in H^{n,p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
. By Theorem
3.23
,

w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}w_{k},

where
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, for every
k
. By Theorem
3.6
, since
w_{k}\in H^{p}_{Bel,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
, for every
k
, it follows that
w_{k,nt}
exists, is in
L^{p}(\partial\mathbb{D})
, and

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

(15)

Since
w=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(z^{*})}^{k}w_{k}
, it follows that

w_{nt}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k,nt}

exists, and, for
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})-\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}(w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta}))||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||(\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}-\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k})w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq D_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||(\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}-\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k})w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta,

where
C_{p}
,
C_{p,n}
, and
D_{p,n}
are constants that depend on only
p
or
p
and
n
, respectively. Since

\displaystyle(\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}-\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k})w_{k}(re^{i\theta})

\displaystyle=(e^{-jk\theta}-r^{k}e^{-jk\theta})(p^{+}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta}))

\displaystyle=(p^{+}e^{ik\theta}+p^{-}e^{-ik\theta}-r^{k}(p^{+}e^{ik\theta}+p^{-}e^{-ik\theta}))(p^{+}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta}))

\displaystyle=(p^{+}(1-r^{k})e^{ik\theta}+p^{-}(1-r^{k})e^{-ik\theta})(p^{+}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta}))

\displaystyle=p^{+}(1-r^{k})e^{ik\theta}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}(1-r^{k})e^{-ik\theta}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta}),

it follows that

\displaystyle D_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||(\widehat{(e^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k}-\widehat{(re^{i\theta})^{*}}^{k})w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=D_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||p^{+}(1-r^{k})e^{ik\theta}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})+p^{-}(1-r^{k})e^{-ik\theta}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq D_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+C_{p,n}2^{-p/2}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\left(|(1-r^{k})e^{ik\theta}w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})|+|(1-r^{k})e^{-ik\theta}w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta})|\right)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq D_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{k,nt}(e^{i\theta})-w_{k}(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+E_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(1-r^{k})|^{p}\,|w_{k}^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\quad\quad+E_{p,n}\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(1-r^{k})|^{p}\,|w_{k}^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta,

where
C_{p,n}
,
D_{p,n}
, and
E_{p,n}
are constants that depend on only
p
and

. Now, the first sum in the last line of the inequality converges to zero, as

, because of (
15
), while the second and third sums in the last line of the inequality converge to zero by Lebesgue’s Dominated Convergence Theorem. Therefore,

\lim_{r\nearrow 1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w_{nt}(e^{i\theta})-w(re^{i\theta})||^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}\,d\theta=0.

∎

4.
The Conjugate Beltrami Equation

In this section, we define a bicomplex version of the conjugate Beltrami equation using the bicomplex
\bar{\partial}
operator from Definition
2.32
. Using these equations, we define Hardy classes of bicomplex-valued solutions and show that these functions exhibit properties of the classic Hardy spaces. This extends work from the complex-valued function setting that can be found in
[
44
,
39
,
5
,
6
]
.

4.1.
Definition and Representation

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
be such that there exists a real constant
c
and
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
. We call any equation of the form

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\bar{\partial}\overline{w}

a
\mathbb{B}
-conjugate-Beltrami equation.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
be such that there exists a real constant
c
and
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
. Every solution
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
of

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\bar{\partial}\overline{w}

has the form

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-},

where
w^{+}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
solves the
\mathbb{C}
-conjugate-Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}

and
w^{-}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{C}
solves the
\mathbb{C}
-conjugate-Beltrami equation

\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

Proof.

Using the idempotent representation of the functions and the differential operators, if
w
solves (
6
), then, by Remark
2.33
, we have

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}w

\displaystyle=\mu\bar{\partial}\overline{w}

\displaystyle\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})

\displaystyle=(p^{+}\mu^{+}+p^{-}\mu^{-})\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}(w^{+})^{*}+p^{-}(w^{-})^{*})

\displaystyle p^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\mu^{+}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+p^{-}\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

Since idempotent representations are unique, it follows that

\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}=\mu^{+}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

Applying complex conjugation to both sides of the right-hand side equation, we have

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

∎

4.2.
Hardy Spaces

Definition 4.3
.

For
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
, for some real constant
c
, and
0<p<\infty
, we define the
\mathbb{B}
-conjugate-Beltrami-Hardy spaces
H^{p}_{conj,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
to be those functions
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that
w
solves

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\,\bar{\partial}\overline{w}

and

||w||_{H^{p}_{\mathbb{B}}}:=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta\right)^{1/p}<\infty.

Theorem 4.4
.

For
\mu:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
such that there exists a real constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})}\leq c<1
and
0<p<\infty
,
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}\in H^{p}_{conj,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
if and only if
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{conj,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{conj,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, if
w
solves
\bar{\partial}w=\mu\bar{\partial}\overline{w}
, then

\frac{\partial(w^{+})}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

By (
2
), for every
r\in(0,1)
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{+}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty

and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p/2}\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

and

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta<\infty.

Therefore,
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{conj,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{conj,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
.

Now, if
(w^{+})^{*}\in H^{p}_{conj,(\mu^{+})^{*}}(\mathbb{D})
and
w^{-}\in H^{p}_{conj,\mu^{-}}(\mathbb{D})
, then
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}
solves
\bar{\partial}w=\mu\bar{\partial}\overline{w}
by direct computation. Also, by (
2
), we have for every
r\in(0,1)
that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{-p/2}\int_{0}^{2\pi}(|w^{+}(re^{i\theta})|+|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|)^{p}\,d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)

\displaystyle\leq C_{p}\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|(w^{+})^{*}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta+\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|w^{-}(re^{i\theta})|^{p}\,d\theta\right)<\infty,

where
C_{p}
is a constant that depends on only
p
. Thus,

\sup_{0<r<1}\int_{0}^{2\pi}||w(re^{i\theta})||_{\mathbb{B}}^{p}\,d\theta<\infty,

and
w\in H^{p}_{conj,\mu}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
.
∎

Note that the proof of this result uses the same argument as the proof of Theorem
3.4
. This is true for many of the proofs in this section and the one that follows. In the cases where there are few differences with a previous proof, we indicate the relevant changes but do not repeat all of the details.

Theorem 4.5
.

For
\mu\in W^{1,s}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
,
s>2
, such that there exists a real constant

Theorem 4.9
.

For real-valued
\mu\in W^{1,\infty}(\mathbb{D})
such that there exists a constant
c
satisfying
||\mu||_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})}\leq c<1

\mathbb{B}
-conjugate Beltrami equation

if and only if the function

solves the
\mathbb{B}
-Vekua equation

\bar{\partial}w=\alpha\overline{w}.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.2
, if
f
solves

\bar{\partial}f=\mu\bar{\partial}\,\overline{f},

then
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
solve

\frac{\partial(f^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial f^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}

and

\frac{\partial f^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial(f^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}},

respectively. Since
\mu
is real-valued, this means
f^{+}
and
f^{-}
solve

\frac{\partial(f^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial f^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}

and

\frac{\partial f^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial(f^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}},

respectively.
By Proposition 3.2.3.1 of
[
6
]
(see discussion in Section
2.1.4
), this implies that the function
g
defined by

g:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}((f^{+})^{*}-\mu f^{+})

solves the Vekua equation

\frac{\partial g}{\partial z^{*}}=\alpha g^{*}

and the function
h
defined by

h:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(f^{-}-\mu(f^{-})^{*})

solves

\frac{\partial h}{\partial z^{*}}=\alpha h^{*}.

By Theorem
2.38
,
w:=p^{+}g^{*}+p^{-}h
solves

\bar{\partial}w=\alpha\overline{w}.

Observe that

\displaystyle w

\displaystyle=p^{+}g^{*}+p^{-}h

\displaystyle=p^{+}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}((f^{+})^{*}-\mu f^{+})\right)^{*}+p^{-}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(f^{-}-\mu(f^{-})^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=p^{+}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(f^{+}-\mu(f^{+})^{*})\right)+p^{-}\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(f^{-}-\mu(f^{-})^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}\left(p^{+}f^{+}+p^{-}f^{-}-\mu(p^{+}(f^{+})^{*}+p^{-}(f^{-})^{*})\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(f-\mu\overline{f}).

Now, suppose that
w
solves
\mathbb{B}
-Vekua equation

\bar{\partial}w=\alpha\overline{w}.

By Theorem
2.38
,

\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=\alpha w^{+}

and

\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\alpha(w^{-})^{*}.

By Proposition 3.2.3.1 of
[
6
]
, this implies that the function
\tilde{g}
defined by

\tilde{g}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}((w^{+})^{*}+\mu w^{+})

solves

\frac{\partial\tilde{g}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial(\tilde{g})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}

and the function
\tilde{h}
defined by

\tilde{h}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-\mu^{2}}}(w^{-}+\mu(w^{-})^{*})

solves

\frac{\partial\tilde{h}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu\frac{\partial(\tilde{h})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}.

By direct computation, the function
f=p^{+}(\tilde{g})^{*}+p^{-}\tilde{h}
solves

\bar{\partial}f=\mu\bar{\partial}\overline{f}.

∎

Proof.

By Proposition
2.26
and Remark
2.33
, any
w:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
that solves

\bar{\partial}w=\mu_{1}\partial w+\mu_{2}\bar{\partial}\overline{w}+Aw+B\overline{w}

must satisfy

\displaystyle\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})

\displaystyle=(p^{+}\mu_{1}^{+}+p^{-}\mu_{1}^{-})\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\right)(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(p^{+}\mu_{2}^{+}+p^{-}\mu_{2}^{-})\left(p^{+}\frac{\partial}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial}{\partial z^{*}}\right)(p^{+}(w^{+})^{*}+p^{-}(w^{-})^{*})

\displaystyle\quad\quad+(p^{+}A^{+}+p^{-}A^{-})(p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-})+(p^{+}B^{+}+p^{-}B^{-})(p^{+}(w^{+})^{*}+p^{-}(w^{-})^{*}).

After simplifications, this is the same as

\displaystyle p^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}+p^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}

\displaystyle=p^{+}\mu_{1}^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}+p^{-}\mu_{1}^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}+p^{+}\mu_{2}^{+}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+p^{-}\mu_{2}^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z}=\mu_{1}^{+}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}+\mu_{2}^{+}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+A^{+}w^{+}+B^{+}(w^{+})^{*},

\displaystyle\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu_{1}^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}+\mu_{2}^{-}\frac{\partial(w^{-})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}+A^{-}w^{-}+B^{-}(w^{-})^{*}.

Taking complex conjugates of both sides of the last equation gives us

\displaystyle\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu_{1}^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+(\mu_{2}^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial w^{+}}{\partial z^{*}}+(A^{+})^{*}(w^{+})^{*}+(B^{+})^{*}w^{+}

∎

6.1.
The Bicomplex Schwarz Boundary Value Problem

First, we consider a bicomplex version of the Schwarz boundary value problem. Our result is a direct generalization of the work of G. Harutyunyan
[

for the complex Beltrami equation and uses their result explicitly to construct the solution formulas. Note, in contrast to Section

\bar{\partial}w=\mu\partial w+f
, where
f\not\equiv 0
. In Section
3
, we only considered the
f\equiv 0
case of this equation. For other considerations of a bicomplex Schwarz boundary value problem, see
[
17
]
.

Theorem 6.1
.

The
\mathbb{B}
-Schwarz problem

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}w=\mu\partial w+f,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w^{+}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{1},\\
\re\{w^{-}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{2},\\
\im\{w^{+}(0)\}=a_{1},\\
\im\{w^{-}(0)\}=a_{2},\end{cases}

for
\mu\in\mathbb{B}
such that
||\mu||_{\mathbb{B}}\leq c<1
, for some constant
c
,
f\in L^{p}(\mathbb{D},\mathbb{B})
,
p>2
,
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in C(\partial\mathbb{D},\mathbb{R})
, and
a_{1},a_{2}\in\mathbb{R}
, is uniquely solvable and the solution is

w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-},

where
w^{+}
is the complex conjugate of

\displaystyle(w^{+}(z))^{*}

\displaystyle=\varphi_{1}(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\left(((-\mu^{+})^{*})^{k}T^{k}((f^{+})^{*}+(\mu^{+})^{*}\varphi_{1}^{\prime})(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta(\zeta-z)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left.((-\mu^{+}))^{k}(T^{k}((f^{+})^{*}+(\mu^{+})^{*}\varphi_{1}^{\prime})(\zeta))^{*}\frac{1-z\zeta^{*}}{\zeta^{*}(1-z\zeta^{*})}\right)\,d\xi\,d\zeta,

with
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
,

\varphi_{1}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\gamma_{1}(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}+i(-a_{1}),

and
T(\cdot)
is the operator defined by

T(g)(z):=-\frac{1}{\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\frac{g(\zeta)}{(\zeta-z)^{2}}+\frac{(g(\zeta))^{*}}{(1-z\zeta^{*})^{2}}\,d\xi\,d\eta,

which solves the Schwarz problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+(f^{+})^{*},&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{(w^{+})^{*}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{1},\\
\im\{((w^{+})(0))^{*}\}=-a_{1},\end{cases}

and
w^{-}
is

\displaystyle w^{-}(z)

\displaystyle=\varphi_{2}(z)+\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{k+1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\iint_{|\zeta|<1}\left((-\mu^{-})^{k}T^{k}(f^{-}+\mu^{-}\varphi_{2}^{\prime})(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta(\zeta-z)}\right.

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad+\left.((-\mu^{-})^{*})^{k}(T^{k}(f^{-}+\mu^{-}\varphi_{2}^{\prime})(\zeta))^{*}\frac{1-z\zeta^{*}}{\zeta^{*}(1-z\zeta^{*})}\right)\,d\xi\,d\zeta,

with

\varphi_{2}(z)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{|\zeta|=1}\gamma_{2}(\zeta)\frac{\zeta+z}{\zeta-z}\frac{d\zeta}{\zeta}+ia_{2},

which solves the Schwarz problem

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}+f^{-},&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w^{-}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{2},\\
\im\{w^{-}(0)\}=a_{2}\end{cases}.

Proof.

By direct computation, a function
w=p^{+}w^{+}+p^{-}w^{-}:\mathbb{D}\to\mathbb{B}
solves

\begin{cases}\bar{\partial}w=\mu\partial w+f,&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w^{+}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{1},\\
\re\{w^{-}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{2},\\
\im\{w^{+}(0)\}=a_{1},\\
\im\{w^{-}(0)\}=a_{2},\end{cases}

if and only if
w^{+}
and
w^{-}
solve

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z^{*}}=(\mu^{+})^{*}\frac{\partial(w^{+})^{*}}{\partial z}+(f^{+})^{*},&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{(w^{+})^{*}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{1},\\
\im\{((w^{+})(0))^{*}\}=-a_{1},\end{cases}

and

\begin{cases}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z^{*}}=\mu^{-}\frac{\partial w^{-}}{\partial z}+f^{-},&\text{ in }\mathbb{D},\\
\re\{w^{-}\}|_{\partial\mathbb{D}}=\gamma_{2},\\
\im\{w^{-}(0)\}=a_{2}\end{cases}.

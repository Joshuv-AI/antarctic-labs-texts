Yoga for Fourier–Mukai partnership
Pat Lank, Kabeer Manali-Rahul, Nebojsa Pavic

Abstract
. We study the behavior of integral transforms under various forms of base change. In particular, we establish a yoga of local algebra and fibers to test for derived equivalences via integral transforms. This partially generalizes a result of Orlov to singular varieties and strengthens many results in the literature to arbitrary fields. Additionally, it provides new insight into fibrations and their singularities arising in more arithmetically flavored contexts (e.g. projective and flat schemes over a DVR).

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting the stage

The context of our work starts with Mukai
[
Muk81
]
. Loc. cit. introduced the notion of integral transforms to study triangulated equivalences between derived categories associated with abelian varieties. Over time, integral transforms have played a central role in the interplay between derived categories and algebraic geometry (e.g.
[
Bri02
,
Kaw02
]
), with further applications in string theory
[
AHR05
,
ASS05
]
.

Much of this progress has focused on smooth varieties, where geometric results rely on categorical methods. However, extending these methods to singular varieties remains challenging, partly because bounded coherent complexes are typically not perfect. Thus, an important task is to develop new results or techniques that improve our understanding of integral transforms in such singular settings.

Recent work has begun to address these difficulties. Criteria determining when an integral transform is fully faithful or an equivalence have been established for varieties over algebraically closed fields, generalizing results from the smooth case
[
HLS07
,
HLS09
]
and building upon foundational work of Bondal–Orlov
[
AB95
]
. Likewise, Bondal–Orlov’s reconstruction theorem for smooth projective varieties has been extended to projective curves
[
Spe23
]
and (anti-)Fano Gorenstein varieties
[
Bal11
]
.

There is also growing interest in studying integral transforms in the relative setting. Specifically, let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be proper
S
-schemes where
S
is Noetherian. An
integral transform
is an exact functor
\Phi_{K}:=\mathbf{R}(p_{2})_{\ast}(\mathbf{L}p_{1}^{\ast}(-)\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}K)\colon D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y_{1})\to D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y_{2})
where
K\in D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2})
and
p_{i}\colon Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2}\to Y_{i}
are the projection morphisms. If
\Phi_{K}
is an equivalence, then we say
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are
Fourier–Mukai partners (over
S
)
.

Such transforms naturally arise in the study of
fibrations
; that is, in situations involving proper and flat morphisms. See e.g.
[
HLS07
,
HLS09
]
, which dealt with families whose special fibers have mild singularities. In particular, loc. cit. investigated when an integral transform between locally projective fibrations
Y_{i}\to S
is an equivalence for
S
of finite type over an algebraically closed field. It was shown that the induced integral transform on special fibers (i.e. closed points) over
S
controls the behavior of equivalences and full faithfulness.

In our work, we reveal additional depth to this perspective of exploiting special fibers. Specifically, we study the behavior of integral transforms under changes of the base scheme in more general settings that includes local algebra. Moreover, our approach naturally extends several results in the literature to arbitrary fields, and at the same time yields new insight for singularities of fibrations.

1.2.
Preservation of adjointness

Neeman has developed a new framework called approximable triangulated categories (see e.g.
[
CNS25
,
Nee23
]
), which has resolved open conjectures in algebraic geometry
[
Nee21a
,
Nee24
]
. As a consequence of this machinery, any integral transform
\Phi_{K}
inducing an equivalence on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
restricts to equivalences on the subcategories of bounded complexes with coherent cohomology
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}
and perfect complexes
\operatorname{Perf}
. Moreover, an equivalence on these ‘smaller’ categories implies an equivalence on the ‘larger’ category. See
Remark

2.7
for details.

For those concerned with equivalences, this allows the study of integral transforms to be reduced to their behavior on these subcategories. Whether an integral transform restricts to these smaller categories is now well-understood
[
Riz17
,
Bal09
,
DLM25
]
. These results shed light to the existence of left or right adjoints to integral transforms and the conditions under which such adjoints also restrict to these subcategories.

However, the behavior of adjointness under base change is not yet fully understood. Our focus is on base changes induced by affine morphisms of finite tor-dimension. In particular, this leads us to the following, which summarizes
Section

3
.

1.3.
Fully faithfulness & equivalences

Now, we have a clearer understanding of how integral transforms behave under base change. Particularly, regarding the preservation of adjointness and the restriction to smaller categories. Next, we shift gears to studying fully faithfulness and equivalences under base change; specifically, the ascent and descent of these properties. Before doing so, we review the relevant literature a bit more.

As mentioned earlier, the work of
[
HLS07
,
HLS09
]
studied these questions for fibrations whose base is of finite type over an algebraically closed field. A key ingredient in loc. cit. was to look at the behavior on special fibers. More recently, such questions have also been studied for smooth proper morphisms over a Noetherian base scheme (see
[
Kur24
, Proposition/Definition 2.3]
). However, smoothness constraints can restrict the examples which one might be interested in, e.g. fibrations admitting singular fibers.

A result of Orlov addresses similar questions in the case of smooth varieties over a field. Specifically, for smooth projective varieties over a field, an integral transform is fully faithful or an equivalence if, and only if, the induced integral transform satisfies the same property after any base field extension
[
Orl02
, Lemma 2.12]
. The strategy of loc. cit. leverages ‘canonical morphisms’ between the kernels of integral transforms that describe the (co)units of adjunction. However, it remains unclear whether such canonical morphisms exist in the singular setting. This uncertainty is another source of motivation for our work, as we aimed to bypass the lack of these tools in singular cases using different techniques.

The brings attention to the following, which summarizes
Section

4
.

(see

In practice, many schemes we may consider are separated, and hence, semiseparated. As a pleasant consequence, this allows us to partially generalize Orlov’s result to the singular setting.

1.4.
Affine variations

Naturally, we sought analogs of
Theorem

1.2
in which
t
need not be locally of finite tor-dimension. Ideally, such a framework would include morphisms from special fibers as in
[
HLS07
,
HLS09
]
. Accordingly, we work in the setting of affine morphisms to the base, while imposing a mild restriction on one of the
Y_{i}\to S
.

The following result is a slight generalization of
[
HLS09
, Proposition 2.15]
. In particular, we perform base change along affine morphisms rather than closed immersions of closed points. Note that a key difference between what follows and
Sections

3
and
4
is that we impose one of the
Y_{i}\to S
be locally projective (as opposed to proper like our earlier results).

Theorem 1.4

(see
Corollary

5.5
)
.

Consider affine morphism
t\colon T\to S
to a semiseparated Noetherian scheme. Suppose
Y_{1}\to S
is flat, locally projective and
Y_{2}\to S
is flat, proper. Denote by
t^{\prime}\colon Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2}\times_{S}T\to Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2}
the natural morphism. Let
K\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2})
be relatively perfect over each

. If
\Phi_{K}

.

It is worthwhile to note that
Theorem

1.4
yields an independent proof of the ascending full faithfulness or equivalences in
[

Proposition

and

[

in the case of special fibers.

1.5.
Applications

To conclude our work, we discuss the ramifications of
Theorems

1.2
and
1.4
. Our results extend the literature on schemes of finite type over algebraically closed fields to the more general setting of arbitrary fields. They reveal an interplay between the intuition of
[
HLS07
,
HLS09
]
concerning special fibers and our work on faithfully flat descent along affine morphisms. In particular, certain singularities are shown to be derived invariants for fibrations over Noetherian schemes, broadening the scope to include arithmetically flavored situations of potential interest for future research.

For a pair of geometrically integral projective curves over a field
k
of characteristic zero, Fourier–Mukai partnership implies they are
k
-forms (see
Proposition

6.1
). Here, a
k
-form means there exists a finite extension
L/k
such that their base changes along
L/k
become isomorphic. The result holds for singular curves and enriches algebraically closed case of
[
Spe23
]
. In
Example

6.2
, we exhibit a pair of non-isomorphic elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
that are nevertheless
\mathbb{Q}
-forms. For fibrations of relative dimension one over a Noetherian
\mathbb{Q}
-scheme, Fourier–Mukai partnership forces the genera of special fibers to coincide (see
Corollary

6.3
).

Smoothness for proper schemes over an arbitrary field is likewise invariant under Fourier–Mukai partnership (see
Proposition

6.4
). This subtlety arises especially over imperfect fields. Since regularity is characterized by
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}=\operatorname{Perf}
, one might expect this condition to persist after a change of field extensions, but there exist regular projective curves that become singular after such a base change (see e.g.
[
Kol11
, Remark 16]
).

In the setting of projective fibrations, smoothness of the total space is also a derived invariant (see
Corollary

6.5
). Finally, generalizing
[
HLS09
]
, we show that the Cohen–Macaulay and Gorenstein properties of a morphism remain invariant under Fourier–Mukai partnership for Noetherian base schemes (see
Proposition

6.6
).

be a Noetherian scheme. We work with the following triangulated categories:

(2)

(3)

(4)

is the (strictly full) subcategory of

.

At times if

2.1.
Perfect & pseudocoherent complexes

We say

is

\operatorname{Perf}(X)
for the full subcategory of perfect complexes. Also, given an integer

is

We say

is

if it is
N

.
In general, since

is a scheme,
E\in D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is pseudocoherent if and only if locally on
X

P\to E

[

, Corollary 22.47]
.
As

is Noetherian,
E\in D_{\operatorname{qc}}(X)
is pseudocoherent if, and only if,

is coherent for all

(see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 08E8
]
). The subcategory of pseudocoherent complexes of

coincides with

(i.e. the full subcategory of complexes with bounded above and coherent cohomology). Consider a morphism

of Noetherian schemes. Then one has

(see e.g.

]

While straightforward, the following is useful.

.

Let

be an affine faithfully flat morphism of Noetherian schemes. An object

(resp.

This is a special case of
[

, Proposition 22.52]

[

Tag 068R
]
(resp.
[
GW23
, Lemma 21.172]
).
∎

2.3.
Generation for triangulated categories

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a triangulated category with shift functor
[1]\colon\mathcal{T}\to\mathcal{T}
. Fix
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. Denote by
\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{S})
the strictly full (that is, closed under isomorphisms) subcategory of

consisting of all direct summands of objects of the form

where

for all

are nonzero. Inductively, let

\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}:=\begin{cases}\operatorname{add}(\varnothing)&\text{if }n=0,\\
\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{S})&\text{if }n=1,\\
\operatorname{add}(\{\operatorname{cone}\phi\mid\phi\in\operatorname{Hom}(\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n-1},\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{1})\})&\text{if }n>1.\end{cases}

Additionally, let
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle:=\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{\infty}:=\cup_{n\geq 0}\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle_{n}
. It may be checked that
\langle\mathcal{S}\rangle
is the smallest
thick
(that is, triangulated subcategory closed under direct summands) subcategory containing
\mathcal{S}
. In the case
\mathcal{S}
consists of a single object

\langle G\rangle_{n}
and
\langle G\rangle
. An object

is called a

for

if
\langle G\rangle=\mathcal{T}
.

Now, we impose
\mathcal{T}
be a compactly generated triangulated category. To clarify, we require that
\mathcal{T}
admitting all small coproducts. Denote the collection of compact objects of
\mathcal{T}
by
\mathcal{T}^{c}
. We say
G\in\mathcal{T}^{c}
is a

for

if for any

for all

if, and only if,

. A well-known fact is that classical generators for

coincide with being compact generators for

(see e.g.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{T}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{S}\colon G
be a pair of exact adjoint functors between compactly generated triangulated categories. Assume
G
preserves small coproducts. Then
F
is an equivalence if, and only if,
F
restricts to an equivalence
\mathcal{T}^{c}\to\mathcal{S}^{c}
. A similar statement holds for
F
being fully faithful. Also, if
F
restricts to a fully faithful functor
\mathcal{T}^{c}\to\mathcal{S}^{c}
and
F(\mathcal{T}^{c})
compactly generates
\mathcal{S}
, then
F\colon\mathcal{S}\to\mathcal{T}
is an equivalence.

Proof.

It is straightforward to check that
F
being an equivalence induces an equivalence
\mathcal{T}^{c}\to\mathcal{S}^{c}
. So, we check the converse. To do so, we show that
F
and
G
are both fully faithful. Denote the counit and unit of the adjunction respectively by
\epsilon
and
\eta
. Set
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
the strictly full subcategories respectively of
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
consisting of objects where
\epsilon
and
\eta
are isomorphisms. One may verify that
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
are closed under shifts, iterated cones, and small coproducts (hence, homotopy colimits). However,
[
Sta25
,
Tag 09SN
]
tells us each object in
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{S}
can be obtained respectively by
\mathcal{T}^{c}
and
\mathcal{S}^{c}
using these operations. Hence,
\mathcal{T}\subseteq\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
and

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}(\mathbf{R}(g_{1})_{\ast}(\mathbf{R}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(K,g^{!}_{1}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{1}})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{L}g_{2}^{\ast}E),G)

\displaystyle(\textrm{\cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{Ballard:2009}{}{}, Lem.\ 3.10]}}).

∎

Proposition 3.12
.

Consider
3.1
where
t
is an affine morphism locally of finite tor-dimension. Let
K\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2})
be relatively perfect over
Y_{1}
. Then
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}
is left adjoint to
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K}
on

where

Proof.

That the last claim holds follows from

. So, we check the first claim. By

3.3
,
\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K
is relatively perfect over

. Moreover, from

3.11
, the kernel of the integral transform which is left adjoint to

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}(g_{2}^{\prime})_{\ast}

\displaystyle(\mathbf{R}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K,(g^{\prime}_{2})^{!}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{1}\times_{S}T})\otimes^{\mathbf{L}}\mathbf{L}(g_{1}^{\prime})^{\ast}E)

\displaystyle\mathbf{R}\operatorname{\mathcal{H}\!\mathit{om}}(\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K,(g^{\prime}_{2})^{!}\mathcal{O}_{Y_{1}\times_{S}T})

3.2

Lemma 4.1
.

Consider
3.1
. Let

Proposition 4.2
.

Consider
3.1
where
t
is an affine morphism locally of finite tor-dimension. Let
K\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{1}\times_{S}Y_{2})
be relatively perfect over
Y_{2}
. Denote by
K^{\prime}
for the kernel of the integral transform obtained in

3.8
which is right adjoint to
\Phi_{K}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
. If
\Phi_{K}
is fully faithful on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
, then
\mathbf{L}t^{\ast}_{1}
preserves the unit
\eta
of the adjoint pair
\Phi_{K}
and
\Phi_{K^{\prime}}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
. That is,
\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}\eta_{E}

\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}E
for the adjoint pair
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K}
.

Proof.

By

3.8
,
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K}
and
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}
are adjoint functors on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
. Choose

. We want to show that
\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}(\eta_{E})
is the unit of
\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}E
for this adjunction.

Let
\beta\colon\mathbf{L}t^{\ast}_{1}E\to\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)
be a morphism in
D_{\operatorname{qc}}(Y_{1}\times_{S}T)
for any

. Using that
\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}
is left adjoint to
\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
, adjunction tells us there exists a unique morphism
\beta^{\prime}
making the following diagram commute,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{L}t^{\ast}_{1}E}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{t_{1}}_{\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}

where
\xi^{t_{1}}
is the counit of derived pullback and pushforward along

. Now,
\beta^{\prime}
is of the form
\mathbf{L}t^{\ast}_{1}(\beta^{\prime\prime})
where
\beta^{\prime\prime}\colon E\to\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)
. Furthermore, by the universal property of the counit, the morphism
\beta^{\prime\prime}
is the unique morphism such that
\beta=\xi^{t_{1}}_{\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}\circ\mathbf{L}t^{\ast}_{1}(\beta^{\prime\prime})
.
Similarly, using that
\Phi_{K}
is left adjoint to
\Phi_{K^{\prime}}
and the natural isomorphism
\alpha
of
Lemma

4.1
, there is a
\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}
giving a diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Phi_{K^{\prime}}(\mathbf{R}(t_{2})_{\ast}A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Phi_{K^{\prime}}\Phi_{K}(E)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta^{\prime\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{E}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha^{A}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\beta^{\prime\prime\prime}}

which commutes. In other words,

\beta^{\prime}=\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}((\alpha^{A})^{-1}\circ\beta^{\prime\prime\prime})\circ\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}(\eta_{E}),

which tells us
\beta
factors through
\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}(\eta_{E})
.

It remains to show that this factorization is unique. So, suppose there is a morphism
h\colon\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}\Phi_{K^{\prime}}\Phi_{K}(E)\to\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)
such that

. Once again, using that

is left adjoint to
\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}
, we have a morphism

giving a commutative diagram

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}\Phi_{K^{\prime}}\Phi_{K}(E)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbf{L}t_{1}^{\ast}\mathbf{R}(t_{1})_{\ast}\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h^{\prime}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\xi^{t_{1}}_{\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}(A)}}

Tying things together, we see that

So, from uniqueness, it follows that

As

is fully faithful on

,

must be an isomorphism, which implies

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
F\colon\mathcal{S}_{1}\leftrightarrows\mathcal{S}_{2}\colon G
be an adjoint pair of exact functors between compactly generated triangulated categories. Assume
G
preserves small coproducts. Denote by
\eta
for the unit of the adjunction. Then the collection
\mathcal{T}
in
\mathcal{S}_{1}
consisting of objects
E
for which
\eta_{E}
is an isomorphism is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
which is closed under small coproducts. Moreover,
F
is fully faithful if, and only if,
\eta_{E}
is an isomorphism for every
E\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}
.

Proof.

It is straightforward to see that
\mathcal{T}
is triangulated.
So, we start by showing
\mathcal{T}
is closed under small coproducts. Let
E_{i}\in\mathcal{T}
with
i\in I
for
I
a set. As
F
and
G
both commute with small coproducts, the natural morphism
\alpha\colon\oplus_{i\in I}GF(E_{i})\to GF(\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i})
is an isomorphism. Also, we have a commutative diagram for each
i\in I
,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{E_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{GF(E_{i})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\bigoplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{GF(\bigoplus_{i}E_{i})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{E_{i}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi_{i}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces GF(\pi_{i})}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\eta_{\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}}

where
\pi_{i}
is the natural injection. If
\pi^{\prime}_{i}\colon GF(E_{i})\to\oplus_{i\in I}GF(E_{i})
is the natural injection, then we know that
GF(\pi_{i})=\alpha\circ\pi_{i}^{\prime}
.

It suffices to show
\eta_{\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}
is an isomorphism. Note that
\eta_{\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}
is the unique morphism arising from the universal property of
\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}
being a coproduct of the
E_{i}
. Hence, if
GF(\oplus_{i}E_{i})
with morphisms
GF(\pi_{i})\circ\eta_{E_{i}}
satisfies the necessary universal property, then
\eta_{\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}
is an isomorphism. Consider an
A\in\mathcal{T}
and morphisms
f_{i}\colon E_{i}\to A
. This gives us morphisms
f_{i}\colon\eta^{-1}_{E_{i}}\colon GF(E_{i})\to A
, and hence, a unique morphism
f\colon\oplus_{i\in I}GF(E_{i})\to A
such that
f_{i}\circ\eta^{-1}_{E_{i}}=f\circ\pi^{\prime}_{i}
for all
i\in I
. It can be checked that
f_{i}\circ\eta^{-1}_{E_{i}}=f\circ\alpha^{-1}\circ GF(\pi_{i})
, and by uniqueness of
f
, we can see that
f\circ\alpha^{-1}
is the only morphism from
GF(\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i})
which can satisfy this property. Consequently, we have
GF(\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i})
satisfying the necessary universal property to show that
\eta_{\oplus_{i\in I}E_{i}}
is an isomorphism. So,
\mathcal{T}
is closed under small coproducts.

Now, we check the second claim. If
F
is fully faithful, then
\eta_{E}
is an isomorphism for all
E\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
. So, we need to check that
\eta_{E}
being an isomorphism for every
E\in\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}
characterizes fully faithfulness of
F
on
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. In such a case, we know that
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
. However, as
\mathcal{T}
is a triangulated subcategory of
\mathcal{S}_{1}
that is closed under small coproducts and contains
\mathcal{S}_{1}^{c}
, it follows that
\mathcal{S}_{1}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
.
This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Consider
3.1
where
t
is an affine morphism locally of finite tor-dimension and
S
is semiseparated. Let

be relatively perfect over

. If

, then so is

.

Proof.

First, we check the case of fully faithfulness. Denote by
K^{\prime}
for the kernel of the right adjoint for
\Phi_{K}
on
D_{\operatorname{qc}}
that is obtained in
Proposition

3.8
. Recall that

3.8
ensures
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K}

\Phi_{\mathbf{L}(t^{\prime})^{\ast}K^{\prime}}
on

and

,

. However,

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
k
be a field of characteristic zero. Suppose
C
and
C^{\prime}
are geometrically integral projective curves over
k
. If
C
and
C^{\prime}
are Fourier–Mukai partners over
k
, then
\operatorname{Spec}(L)\times_{k}C\cong\operatorname{Spec}(L)\times_{k}C^{\prime}
for some finite field extension
L/k
(i.e. are
k
-forms).

Proof.

Let
\overline{k}
be the algebraic closure of
k
. Suppose
K\in D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{1}\times_{k}Y_{2})
has an associated integral transform
\Phi_{K}
restricting to an equivalence
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(C)\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(C^{\prime})
. Denote by
\pi\colon(Y_{1}\times_{k}Y_{2})\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})\to Y_{1}\times_{k}Y_{2}
the natural morphism. From
Theorem

4.6
, we see that
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}\pi^{\ast}K}
restricts to an equivalence
D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k}))\to D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(C^{\prime}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k}))
. Then
C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
and
C^{\prime}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
are Fourier–Mukai partners over an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. By
[
CNS25
, Corollary 5.4]
, we have that
\Phi_{\mathbf{L}\pi^{\ast}K}
restricts to an equivalence
\operatorname{Perf}(C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k}))\to\operatorname{Perf}(C^{\prime}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k}))
. From the following facts, we know that
C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})\cong C^{\prime}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
:

•

C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
is Gorenstein with ample or anti-ample canonical bundle
[
Bal11
, Theorem 2]

•

C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
is not Gorenstein
[
Spe23
, Theorem A]

•

C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
Gorenstein of arithmetic genus one and trivial canonical line bundle
[
LM14
, Theorem 1.1]
(
C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(\overline{k})
is integral with arithmetic genus one such that
\operatorname{deg}(\omega_{X})=0
implies
\omega_{X}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
by
[
Cat82
, Proposition 1.11]
where
\omega_{X}
is the canonical sheaf).

Observe these are the only possible cases for projective curves over an algebraically closed field in characteristic zero. Hence, there is a finite field extension
L/k
such that
C\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)\cong C^{\prime}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)
(see e.g.
[
Poo17
, Proposition 3.2.5]
), which completes the proof.
∎

Example 6.2
.

There is a pair of non-isomorphic elliptic curves
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
over
\mathbb{Q}
that are Fourier–Mukai partners (see
[
War14
, Example 2.3.11]
). From
[

, it follows that any triangulated equivalence

Note that an elliptic curve is geometrically integral and projective.
By

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
S
be a separated Noetherian

-scheme. Suppose

are proper flat morphisms whose special fibers are geometrically integral curves. Assume

is locally projective. If

and

are Fourier–Mukai partners over

, then then genii of the special fibers of

Let

be a closed point. Then the natural morphism

implies that

(i.e.

has characteristic zero). Note that, as

is affine (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01SG
]

Denote by

for the base change of

along
\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(s))\to S
. Our hypothesis ensures that each
Y_{i}^{s}
is a geometrically integral proper curve over
\kappa(s)
. Then, by

5.4
,
Y^{s}_{1}
and

are Fourier–Mukai partners over

guarantees that there is a finite field extension

Proposition 6.4
.

Let
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
be proper schemes over a field
k
(which need not be perfect). Suppose
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are Fourier–Mukai partners. If
Y_{1}
is geometrically regular, then so is
Y_{2}
. In other words, smoothness is a derived invariance.

Proof.

By
Theorem

4.6
,
Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)
and
Y_{2}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)
are Fourier–Mukai partners for every finitely generated field extension
L/k
. So,
Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)
is regular. Then
[
CNS25
, Corollary 5.4]
implies the following string of triangulated equivalences:

D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L))\cong\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{1}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L))\cong\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{2}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)).

Hence,
\operatorname{Perf}(Y_{2}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L))=D^{b}_{\operatorname{coh}}(Y_{2}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L))
, telling us that
Y_{2}\times_{k}\operatorname{Spec}(L)
is regular. In other words,
Y_{2}
must be geometrically regular.
∎

Corollary 6.5
.

Let
f_{i}\colon Y_{i}\to S
be locally projective flat morphisms to a separated Noetherian scheme. Assume that
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
are Fourier–Mukai partners over
S
. Then
f_{1}
is smooth if, and only if,
f_{2}
is smooth.

Proof.

We prove only one direction as the other follows nearly verbatim. Assume
f_{1}
is smooth. It suffices, by
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01V8
]
, to show that all the fibers
Y_{2}\times_{S}\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(s))
are smooth over
\kappa(s)
where
s\in S
is any point. Fix
s\in S
(perhaps, not closed). The natural morphism
t_{s}\colon\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{S,s})\to S
is affine (see e.g.
[
Sta25
,
Tag 01SG
]
) and of finite tor-dimension (due to being flat). Hence,
Theorem

4.6
tells us the base changes of
Y_{1}
and
Y_{2}
along
t_{s}
are Fourier–Mukai partners. Then
Theorem

5.4
implies these base changes remain Fourier–Mukai partners if we base change further along the natural closed immersion
\operatorname{Spec}(\kappa(p))\to\operatorname{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{S,s})
. However, smoothness is stable under base change, so each base change of
Y_{1}
along such morphisms remains smooth. Hence, the desired claim follows from
Proposition

6.4
.
∎

Fault Tolerant Metric Dimensions of Leafless Cacti Graphs with Application in Supply Chain Management Thanks: *Corresponding author
Tauseef Asif, Ghulam Haidar, Faisal Yousafzai, Murad ul Islam Khan*, Qaisar Khan, Rakea Fatima

Abstract
. A resolving set for a simple graph 𝒢 \mathcal{G} is a subset of vertex set of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} such that it distinguishes all vertices of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} using the shortest distance from this subset. This subset is a metric basis if it is the smallest set with this property. A resolving set is a fault tolerant resolving set if the removal of any vertex from the subset still leaves it a resolving set. The smallest set satisfying this property is the fault tolerant metric basis, and the cardinality of this set is termed as fault tolerant metric dimension of 𝒢 \mathcal{G} , denoted by β ′ ​ ( 𝒢 ) \beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G}) . In this article, we determine the fault tolerant metric dimension of bicyclic graphs of type-I and II and show that it is always 4 4 for both types of graphs. We then use these results to form our basis to consider leafless cacti graphs, and calculate their fault tolerant metric dimensions in terms of inner cycles and outer cycles . We then consider a detailed real world example of supply and distribution center management, and discuss the application of fault tolerant metric dimension in such a scenario. We also briefly discuss some other scenarios where leafless cacti graphs can be used to model real world problems.



1.
Introduction

A vertex
\mathcal{w}
of a simple connected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{E})
is said to resolve the vertices
\mathcal{u},\mathcal{v}
of
\mathcal{G}
, if
\mathcal{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{u},\mathcal{w})\neq\mathcal{d}_{\mathcal{G}}(\mathcal{v},\mathcal{w})
, where
\mathcal{d}_{\mathcal{G}}
denotes the length of the shortest path between the given vertices. The distance vector of a vertex
\mathcal{v}\in\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
with respect to an ordered subset
\mathcal{W}=\{\mathcal{w}_{1},\mathcal{w}_{2},\cdots,\mathcal{w}_{{\mathcal{k}}}\}\subset\mathcal{G}
is defined by
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{v}|\mathcal{W})=\{\mathcal{d}(\mathcal{v},\mathcal{w}_{1}),\mathcal{d}(\mathcal{v},\mathcal{w}_{2}),\cdots,\mathcal{d}(\mathcal{v},\mathcal{w}_{{\mathcal{k}}})\}
.
\mathcal{W}
is called a resolving set of
\mathcal{G}
if all vertices of
\mathcal{G}
have distinct distance vectors with respect to
\mathcal{W}
. If
\mathcal{W}
is the smallest resolving set, it is termed as metric basis, and the cardinality of
\mathcal{W}
is called the metric dimension of
\mathcal{G}
. These ideas were first introduced as
“locating set”
and
“location number”
by Slater
[
1
]
. Hararay and Melter
[
2
]
independently presented these ideas as
“resolving set”
and
“metric basis”
.

[

,

]
, while the bounds on metric dimension for some other graph families are solved in
[

,

,

,

,

]
. Complexity problem for finding the metric dimensions of graphs has been discussed in
[

]
.

Many other graph invariants related to metric dimension have been defined in the literature e.g., local metric dimensions
[

]
,
\mathcal{\mathcal{k}}
-metric dimension
[

]
, and mixed metric dimensions
[

Hernado et al.
[
19
]
considered the problem for a simple connected graph
\mathcal{G}
where a vertex of metric basis set
\mathcal{W}\subset\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
is unavailable. Since the unique identification of all landmarks (vertices) is not possible due to this unavailability of
\mathcal{w}\in\mathcal{W}
, an error or fault is introduced in the system. To overcome this phenomenon, they introduced the concept of fault tolerant metric basis. It is defined as the smallest resolving set
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
, such that the set
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}-\{\mathcal{w}:\mathcal{w}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}\}
is again a resolving set for all
\mathcal{w}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
. The cardinality of such a
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}
is called fault tolerant metric dimension, denoted by
\beta^{\prime}
.

Many researchers considered this concept and studied it for different graphs. Hayat et al.
[

studied the fault tolerant metric dimension of some families of interconnection networks. Basak et al
[

determined the exact values of the fault tolerant metric dimension for the circulant graphs
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}(1,2,3)
for all finite values of
\mathcal{n}
. Arulperumjothi et al. calculated
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})
where
\mathcal{G}
is a fractal cubic network. Prabhu et al.
[
22

investigated this problem for Butterfly, Benes and silicates networks. For further studies, the articles
[

,

,

,

]
and the references therein may be consulted.

Preliminaries

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a simple connected graph and
\mathcal{W}
be a fault tolerant metric basis of
\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})

\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
contains all edges present in
\mathcal{E}(\mathcal{G})
, among vertices of
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
. We define the set
\mathcal{W}\cap\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G}^{\prime})
as a fault tolerant metric generator of
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
, and denote it by
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}/\mathcal{G}}
. When the graph
\mathcal{G}
is understood, we will just use the notion, fault tolerant metric generator
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
. In other words, we say that the induced sub-graph
\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
, contributes
|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|
vertices to the fault tolerant metric basis
\mathcal{W}
.

A subset
\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
of
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})

\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{G}
, if
\mathcal{r}\left(\mathcal{u}|\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}-\{\mathcal{w}^{\prime}\}\right)\neq\mathcal{r}(\mathcal{v}|\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}-\{\mathcal{w}^{\prime}\})
, for all
\mathcal{u},\mathcal{v}\in\mathcal{G}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{w}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}
. From these definitions, we can easily see that
|\mathcal{W}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|\geq|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{G}^{\prime}}|
.

Definition 2.2
.

A graph
\mathcal{G}

Definition 2.3
.

A resolving set
\mathcal{S}
for a graph
\mathcal{G}
is fault-tolerant resolving set if
\mathcal{S}\backslash\{\mathcal{v}\}
is also a resolving set for each
\mathcal{v}
in
\mathcal{S}
. The fault-tolerant metric dimension of
\mathcal{G}
is the minimum cardinality of a fault-tolerant resolving set, and it will be denoted by
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})
. A fault-tolerant resolving set of order
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})
is called a fault-tolerant metric basis.

From the definition, we see that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})\geq\beta(\mathcal{G})+1
. Hernado et. al
[
19
]
gave an upper bound for
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})
. More specifically:

Theorem 2.3
.

Fault-tolerant metric dimension is bounded by a function of the metric dimension (independent of the graph). In particular,
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})\leq\beta(\mathcal{G})\big(1+2\cdot 5^{\beta(\mathcal{G})-1}\big)
for every graph
\mathcal{G}
.

Using Theorem
2.3
with Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, we see that

\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})\leq\begin{cases}153&\text{ when }\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}}\text{ and }\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}\text{ are even}\\
22&\text{ when }\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}}\text{ and }\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}\text{ are any other values}\\
22&\text{ when }\mathcal{G}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{r},\mathcal{m}}\text{ for all values of }\mathcal{n},\mathcal{r},\mathcal{m}.\end{cases}

These values are in fact highly inflated because of the generalized nature of the result. The actual values are much smaller and are discussed in the coming sections.

Javaid et. al.
[
30
]
proved that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}})=3

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
form a fault tolerant metric basis set. Using this result we are ensured that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})\geq 3
when
\mathcal{G}
is a bicyclic graph of type-I and II.

4.
Fault Tolerant Metric Dimension of Bicyclic Generated Graphs

Armed with the knowledge from above Lemmas, we are now ready to state and prove the results for fault tolerant metric basis and fault tolerant metric dimension of bicyclic graphs of type-I and type-II.

Lemma 4.1
.

For any bicyclic graph of type-I and II,
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})>3
.

Proof.

Since
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}})=3
, we can easily infer that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})\geq 3
when
\mathcal{G}
is a bicyclic graph of type-I or II. Since in both types of bicyclic graphs, we only have two cycles, let us denote them by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
. If we take
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})=3
, then by pigeonhole principal, at least one of the cycles contributes two vertices to the fault tolerant metric basis. Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\}
be the fault tolerant metric basis. Without loss of generality, we assume that
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
and
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
. Now,
\mathcal{W}
can not be a fault tolerant resolving set since
\mathcal{W}-\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\}
does not resolve any vertex of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
. This concludes our argument.
∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}}
be a bicyclic graph of type-I, then
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}})=4
.

Proof.

We prove this assertion using two cases.

First, when at least one of
\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}
is odd. Without loss of generality, let
\mathcal{n}
be odd and
\mathcal{m}
be even, then by Lemma
3.6
,
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\left\{\mathcal{v}_{\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{n}}{2}\right\rfloor},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\right\}
,
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}-\left\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}+\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}}\right\}
, is a metric basis for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}}
. Since
\mathcal{m}>3
, we can choose
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}
to be
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}\right\rfloor-1}
. Again, by Lemma
3.6
,
\mathcal{W}_{2}=\left\{\mathcal{v}_{\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{n}}{2}\right\rfloor+1},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\right\}
,
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}-\left\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}+\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}}\right\}
is a metric basis for
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}}
. Let
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}=v_{\mathcal{n}+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+1}
. It is evident from the construction of
\mathcal{W}_{1}
and
\mathcal{W}_{2}
that
\mathcal{W}_{1}\cap\mathcal{W}_{2}=\Phi
. We can also conclude that the set
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{W}_{1}\cup\mathcal{W}_{2}=\left\{\mathcal{v}_{\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{n}}{2}\right\rfloor},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}\right\rfloor-1},\mathcal{v}_{\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{n}}{2}\right\rfloor+1},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{n}+\left\lfloor\frac{\mathcal{m}}{2}\right\rfloor+1}\right\}
is a fault tolerant resolving set. Now, by Lemma
4.1
,
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}})\geq 4
. This ensures that
\mathcal{W}
is the smallest fault tolerant resolving set and hence
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}})=4
.

Next, we consider the case when
\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}
are both even. Let us consider the partitioning of
\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}})
as given in Equation (
1
). Using Lemma
3.7
, let us construct a metric basis
\mathcal{W}_{1}=\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\}
where
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}}\in\mathcal{V}_{1},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\in\mathcal{V}_{3}
and
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\in\mathcal{V}_{5}

\mathcal{W}_{2}=\{\mathcal{v}_{l},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\}

4.1

\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{m}})\geq 4

Theorem 4.2
.

For bicyclic graph of type II,
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n},\mathcal{r},\mathcal{m}})=4

Let
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}

5.
Fault Tolerant Metric Dimension of Cacti Graph without Leaves

From the above results for bicyclic graphs, we see that
\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}}}\right|=2
for any cycle of bicyclic graphs of type-I or II. Before extending this concept to cacti graphs without leaves, we define some new terminology for such graphs.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a cactus graph without leaves and let
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})\subset\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{G})
be the set of all vertices with degree
3
or higher. Let
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}})=\mathcal{V}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}})\cap\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{G})
, then we say that a cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}}
of
\mathcal{G}
is an outer cycle, if
\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}})\right|=1
. Similarly, a cycle is called an inner cycle if
\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}})\right|\geq 2
. Vertices in
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{i}})
are termed as common vertices.

It should be noted that if
\mathcal{v}
is a common vertex of an inner cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
, then
\mathcal{v}
is also a vertex of another cycle or path. If
\mathcal{v}
is a vertex of another cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
, where
\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}})\right|=1
, then we say that the cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
is a corresponding outer cycle of
\mathcal{v}
. If
\left|\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}})\right|\geq 2
, then we continue along any of the other common vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
until we come across an outer cycle, and call it, the outer cycle corresponding to the vertex
\mathcal{v}
. On the other hand, if
\mathcal{v}
is vertex of a path, and since we are working with cacti graphs without leaves, that path must be attached to another cycle. Now, applying the same logic as above, we can easily find an outer cycle corresponding to the vertex
\mathcal{v}
.

It is worthwhile to mention that a common vertex
\mathcal{v}
of an inner cycle may have more than one corresponding outer cycles. These concepts are presented in the following example.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a cactus graph without leaves then
\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}}\right|\geq 2
for every outer cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a cactus graph and let us suppose on the contrary that there is an outer cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
contributing one or no vertices to fault tolerant metric basis
\mathcal{W}
. If
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
contributes only one vertex, say
\mathcal{c}
, then
\mathcal{W}-\{\mathcal{c}\}
does not resolve vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
and hence
\mathcal{W}
is not a fault tolerant resolving set. Similarly if
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
does not contribute any vertex to
\mathcal{W}
, again, vertices of
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
are not resolved by
\mathcal{W}
in the first place. This ensures that every outer cycle must contribute two or more vertices to the fault tolerant metric basis.
∎

\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}}\right|
for outer cycles
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}

,

contributes at least two vertices to the fault tolerant metric basis set
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{G}
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}}
be the set of vertices contributed by
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
to
\mathcal{W}
.

Next, we move to the cycle
\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
and consider the vertices
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}}
and
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}
adjacent to
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{m}}
. Obviously these vertices lie to the either side of
\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{m}}
. These ideas are shown in figure
5.2
.

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{m}}
are fault tolerantly resolved by the set
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}=\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}}\cup\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{i}},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{j}}\}
. Before proving this claim, we observe that the set
\mathcal{W}^{\prime}-\{\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\},\mathcal{v}_{\mathcal{k}}\in\mathcal{W}^{\prime}

\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}
, since
\left|\mathcal{S}_{\mathcal{C}_{\mathcal{n}}}\right|\geq 2
. This ensures that when we consider the distance vector of a vertex

with respect to

, there is at least one distance entry corresponding to a vertex of

.

.

Let
\mathcal{G}
be a leafless cactus graph with
\mathcal{n}_{1}
outer cycles. Let
\mathcal{n}_{2}

\mathcal{G}

\mathcal{n}_{1}

5.2
, every outer cycle contributes two vertices to the fault tolerant metric basis and hence a total of

5.3
,
5.4

5.6
and
5.7

\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})=2(\mathcal{n}_{1}+\mathcal{n}_{2})
.
∎

6.1.
Application of Fault Tolerant Metric Basis in Supply Chain Logistics

Let us now consider a scenario where a firm wants to replace the delivery workers with automated delivery vehicles and robots. There is a need to designate certain points in the graph as landmarks, which can then be referenced and used by the machines to know the precise location of all other points of interest, as well as their own position. These vehicles and robots must also communicate real time data about their location to one another to either mitigate or increase the chance of multiple coverage, depending on the actual supply and demand.

Metric basis is an effective way to achieve this goal for any graph
[
31
]
. The problem arises when the landmark itself is out of commission, due to any reason, e.g., the road leading to the store is temporarily closed, or it is unavailable due to renovations etc. The corresponding vertex in this scenario can not be used for reference purposes.

This can be overcome with the introduction of fault tolerant metric basis. For the supply chain graph given in figure
6.1
, Theorem
5.1
implies that
\beta^{\prime}(G)=2(4+0)=8
. We can actually calculate the fault tolerant metric basis using the results given in Section
4
. Let
\mathcal{W}=\{\mathcal{r}_{1},\mathcal{r}_{2},\mathcal{r}_{8},\mathcal{r}_{9},\mathcal{r}_{10},\mathcal{r}_{11},\mathcal{r}_{12},\mathcal{r}_{14}\}
be the set of fault tolerant landmarks for this graph. This subset of landmarks describes the whole graph in terms of distances from
\mathcal{W}
, e.g.,
\mathcal{r}(\mathcal{m}_{1}|\mathcal{W})=(5,6,3,3,4,4,3,3)
and
\mathcal{r}(\mathcal{s}_{2}|\mathcal{W})=(6,7,1,1,2,2,3,3)
.

Let us now consider that the retail store
\mathcal{r}_{2}
is no longer available for referencing. If we again consider the representation of vertices
\mathcal{m}_{1}
and
\mathcal{s}_{2}
, we see that
\mathcal{r}(\mathcal{m}_{1}|\mathcal{W})=(5,3,3,4,4,3,3)
and
\mathcal{r}(\mathcal{s}_{2}|\mathcal{W})=(6,1,1,2,2,3,3)
. It is obviously clear that both representations are still distinct. In fact, this distinctness can be observed for all the vertices of the supply chain graph.

This example illustrates that the concept of fault tolerant metric basis can easily deal with disruptions in the supply chain network. The unavailability of a landmark does not hinder the effectiveness of automated vehicles and robots. They can still uniquely identify all the relevant points of interests (warehouses and stores), and communicate their unique positions to one another.

7.
Conclusion

In this article, we concluded that the fault tolerant metric dimension of Infinity and Kayak Paddle graphs is always constant. We showed that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})=4
for both these graphs. We then extended the results to leafless cacti class of connected graphs, and observed that fault tolerant metric dimension of these graphs depends on the number of outer cycles, as well as on the number of even inner cycles with exactly two antipodal vertices. In particular, we showed that
\beta^{\prime}(\mathcal{G})=2(\mathcal{n}_{1}+\mathcal{n}_{2})
, where
\mathcal{n}_{1}
is the total number of outer cycles of
\mathcal{G}
, while
\mathcal{n}_{2}
is the number of even cycles with exactly two opposite common vertices. We then discussed a theoretical application of leafless cacti graph. We established that the concept of fault tolerant metric basis is an effective way to deal with failures in an automated supply chain logistics problem.

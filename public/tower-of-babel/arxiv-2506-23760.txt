X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) and hidden charmed tetraquarks
You-You Lin, Ji-Ying Wang, Ailin Zhang

Abstract
In a constituent quark model, a hidden charmed tetraquark is assumed consisting of a c ​ q cq diquark and an c ¯ ​ q ¯ \bar{c}\bar{q} antidiquark or vice versa. The Semay-Silvestre-Brac potentials are employed to calculate the masses of c ​ q cq (q=u, d) diquarks. The mass of the c ​ q cq diquark or c ¯ ​ q ¯ \bar{c}\bar{q} antidiquark with spin- 0 0 is predicted with ∼ 2175 \sim 2175 MeV, and the spin- 1 1 one is predicted with ∼ 2220 \sim 2220 MeV. The masses of hidden charmed tetraquarks from 1 ​ S 1S to 2 ​ P 2P excitations are systemically calculated in terms of the same potentials. It is found that the mass of hidden charmed tetraquark without radial excitation grows higher in 1 + ⁣ − , 1 + ⁣ + , 1 − ⁣ − , 0 − ⁣ + , 0 − ⁣ − , 1 − ⁣ + , ⋯ 1^{+-},~1^{++},~1^{--},~0^{-+},~0^{--},~1^{-+},~\cdots sequence, and the tetraquarks with exotic J P ​ C = 0 − ⁣ − , 1 − ⁣ + J^{PC}=0^{--},~1^{-+} have higher masses. The hidden charmed tetraquarks with radial excitations have masses larger than 4300 4300 MeV. The 1 ​ S − 1 ​ P 1S-1P and 1 ​ S − 2 ​ S 1S-2S mass splittings of the hidden charmed tetraquarks are about 390 − 400 390-400 MeV and 550 − 570 550-570 MeV, respectively, which are about 70 70 MeV and 50 50 MeV smaller than those of normal charmonium. The 1 ​ P − 2 ​ P 1P-2P and 2 ​ S − 2 ​ P 2S-2P mass splittings are similar to those for conventional c ​ c ¯ c\bar{c} charmonium mesons. Based on our predicted masses for hidden charmed tetraquarks, some XYZ exotics are analyzed and tentatively assigned. X ∗ ​ ( 3860 ) X^{*}(3860) is possibly the 0 + ⁣ + 0^{++} tetraquark. Z c ​ ( 3900 ) Z_{c}(3900) and X ⁡ ( 3940 ) X(3940) are possibly the 1 + ⁣ − 1^{+-} tetraquarks, and X ⁡ ( 3872 ) X(3872) is possibly a 1 + ⁣ + 1^{++} tetraquark. X ⁡ ( 4250 ) X(4250) may be a 0 − ⁣ + 0^{-+} , 0 + ⁣ + 0^{++} or 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} tetraquark, X ⁡ ( 4240 ) X(4240) may be a 0 − ⁣ − 0^{--} tetraquark. With radial excitations, X ⁡ ( 4350 ) X(4350) may be a 0 + ⁣ + 0^{++} tetraquark, Z c ​ ( 4430 ) Z_{c}(4430) may be a 1 + ⁣ − 1^{+-} tetraquark, X ⁡ ( 4630 ) X(4630) may be a 0 − ⁣ + 0^{-+} or 1 − ⁣ + 1^{-+} tetraquark, and X ​ ( Y ) ​ ( 4660 ) X(Y)(4660) may be the 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} tetraquark. Y ⁡ ( 4008 ) Y(4008) or Y ⁡ ( 4390 ) Y(4390) seems impossibly the 1 − ⁣ − 1^{--} tetraquark. In experiments, it is also important to take into account the mixing of normal charmoniums with hidden-flavor charmonium-like exotics for the identification of a normal charmonium or a charmonium-like exotic.

In theoretical point of view, the interpretations of exotic states are either based on phenomenological investigations or on effective quantum field theory. In fact, the interpretation of
X(3872)
or other exotic states depends on the interactions and models employed. How to identify
X(3872)
among different interpretations is always a puzzle and problem waiting to be solved. So far, there is not a single theoretical model to give a unified understanding of the spectroscopy, decay patterns, production features, and structure of those exotic states. However, the features of many XYZ are unknown yet, the
J^{PC}
quantum numbers of some exotics are even not determined.

The diquark cluster in hadrons is in fact a kind of strong correlation between a pair of quarks. The two quarks attract each other and make up a diquark with a totally antisymmetric symmetry, where the two quarks are also in an antisymmetric color representation. The diquark is either thought as stuff without internal structure or thought as a cluster with a finite size.

Phenomenological evidences for diquarks are mainly on baryon spectroscopy, the

As well known, quantum chromodynamics (QCD) is the right theory for strong interaction, and every system confined through strong interaction in principle could be understood from QCD or lattice QCD. However, for the limited possibility of computer at present, many topics related to non-perturbation and confinement has to rely on effective theories or phenomenological theories. Constituent quark potential models have made great progresses in the understanding of conventional mesons and baryons, and have been employed to study exotic hadrons.

In constituent quark potential models, the spectra of different tetraquarks have been calculated in a diquark-antidiquark picture in many studies, and every more possible calculation of hidden charmed tetraquarks is expected to. The spectrum of

Lin et al. 2025

IV
summary

In this work, a hidden charmed tetraquark without strange or antistrange quark is assumed consisting of a pair of
cq
diquark and
\bar{c}\bar{q}
antidiquark. The interactions in tetraquark are modulated as two kinds of interactions: the first type is the interaction between a quark and another quark, or the interaction between an antiquark and another antiquark; the second type is the interaction between a diquark and an antiquark. For the practical calculation, the modified
AL
-type quark-quark and quark-antiquark potentials proposed by Semay and Silvestre-Brac are employed. To describe the interaction within a tetraquark in a better way, the parameters of the potential between the diquark and the antidiquark are refitted with the experimental mass of
X(3872)
and
T_{cc}(3875)^{+}
.

In terms of the Semay-Silvestre-Brac potentials, the mass of the
cq
diquark or
\bar{c}\bar{q}
antidiquark with spin-
0
is predicted with
\sim 2175
MeV, and the mass of that with spin-
1
is predicted with
\sim 2220
MeV. The mass splitting between the scalar and vector diquark is
\sim 45
MeV. The predicted mass of
cq
diquark with spin-
1
is about
\sim 300
MeV larger than the predicted one from QCD sum rule or phenomenological analysis.

Semay-Silvestre-Brac potentials are also employed to describe the interactions between the diquark and the antidiquark. When the parameters
\alpha
and
\lambda
are fixed by the two ground tetraquark candidates
X(3872)
and
T_{cc}(3875)
, the masses of hidden charmed tetraquarks from
1S
to
2P
excitations are calculated. The lowest hidden charmed tetraquark is predicted with mass
\sim 3770
MeV and
J^{PC}=0^{++}
. The mass of hidden charmed tetraquark becomes higher in
1^{+-},~1^{++},~1^{--},~0^{-+},~0^{--},~1^{-+},~\cdots
sequence, where the tetraquarks with exotic
J^{PC}=0^{--},~1^{-+}
have higher masses. The hidden charmed tetraquark with radial excitation has mass larger than
4300
MeV.

Based on numerical results of the tetraquarks, some observed XYZ exotics have been analysed and their possible
J^{PC}
and inner structures are suggested.

X^{*}(3860)
could be assigned as the
0^{++}
hidden charmed tetraquark consisting of a diquark and an antidiquark both with spin-
1
.
Z_{c}(3900)
and
X(3940)
are possibly the
1^{+-}
hidden charmed tetraquarks without radial excitation.
X(3872)
is fixed as a
1^{++}
hidden charmed tetraquark consisting of a diquark and an antidiquark with one spin-
0
and one spin-
1
.
X(4250)
may be a
0^{-+}
or
1^{-+}
hidden charmed tetraquark without radial excitation, or a
0^{++}
hidden charmed tetraquark with radial excitation.
X(4240)
may be a
0^{--}
hidden charmed tetraquark consisting of a diquark and an antidiquark with one spin-
0
and one spin-
1
.

The masses of hidden charmed tetraquarks with strange or antistrange quark have not been calculated, and few relevant exotics have not been analyzed for the shortage of confirmed experimental data. There are some uncertainties in our calculation. First, the mixing between normal mesons and tetraquarks has not been taken into account explicitly, but was taken into account through the variation of parameters instead. The fixed parameters may vary with the components of the benchmark
X(3872)
and
T_{cc}(3875)
, which will bring several tens MeV uncertainties to the predicted mass spectra. The boson-exchange interactions between the diquark and the antidiquark may make the predicted mass spectra lower than the predicted ones without boson-exchange interactions. So far, some charmonium-like XYZ have been suggested as the normal
c\bar{c}
charmoniums though these assignments are not definite. In order to identify a normal charmonium or charmonium-like meson, it is time and important to take into account the mixing between the normal charmonium and the hidden-flavor charmonium-like exotics in the same time. Only when the properties of normal charmonium and hidden-flavor charmonium-like exotics are clear both in theories and in experiments, the identification could be confirmed.

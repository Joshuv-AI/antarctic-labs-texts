Evaluation of AI models for radiology exam preparation: DeepSeek vs. ChatGPT−3.5
Authors: Na Hu, Yunpeng Luo, Pinggui Lei
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTThe rapid advancement of artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots has generated significant interest regarding their potential applications within medical education. This study sought to assess the performance of the open-source large language model DeepSeek-V3 in answering radiology board-style questions and to compare its accuracy with that of ChatGPT−3.5.A total of 161 questions (comprising 207 items) were randomly selected from the Exercise Book for the National Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination: Radiology. The question set included single-choice, multiple-choice, shared-stem, and case analysis questions. Both DeepSeek-V3 and ChatGPT−3.5 were evaluated using the same question set over a seven-day testing period. Response accuracy was systematically assessed, and statistical analyses were performed using Pearson's chi-square test and Fisher's exact test.DeepSeek-V3 achieved an overall accuracy of 72%, which was significantly higher than the 55.6% accuracy achieved by ChatGPT−3.5 (P < 0.001). Performance analysis by question type revealed DeepSeek's superior accuracy in single-choice questions (87.1%), though with comparatively lower performance in multiple-choice (55.7%) and case analysis questions (68.0%). Across clinical subspecialties, DeepSeek consistently outperformed ChatGPT, particularly in peripheral nervous system (P = 0.003), respiratory system (P = 0.008), circulatory system (P = 0.012), and musculoskeletal system (P = 0.021) domains.In conclusion, DeepSeek demonstrates considerable potential as an educational tool in radiology, particularly for knowledge recall and foundational learning applications. However, its relatively weaker performance on higher-order cognitive tasks and complex question formats suggests the need for further model refinement. Future research should investigate DeepSeek's capability in processing image-based questions and perform comparative analyses with more advanced models (e.g., GPT−5) to better evaluate its potential for medical education.KEYWORD: Artificial intelligence, DeepSeek, ChatGPT, medical imaging education, radiology examination

Introduction

The swift progress of artificial intelligence (AI) chatbots has sparked considerable interest in their potential application in medical education [1]. Within this domain, these AI chatbots have emerged as promising tools with demonstrated capabilities across multiple critical areas. AI chatbots have the potential to significantly enhance the development of academic and postgraduate training materials, as well as to generate test questions for evaluating knowledge and skills [2–4]. Furthermore, they can facilitate doctor–patient communication by simulating realistic dialogs, and support the creation of interactive educational resources [5]. AI chatbots, with their innovative educational capabilities, are garnering growing attention for exam preparation and efficient knowledge retrieval [6]. As a transformative technology in this field, large language models (LLMs) demonstrate advanced natural language processing and context-aware response generation, with their adoption rapidly expanding across medical education [7]. Trained on vast, diverse datasets, these models excel at interpreting complex medical queries and delivering evidence-supported answers. This capability is particularly valuable in educational settings, as it supports students and professionals in making informed clinical decisions [8–10].

In November 2022, OpenAI officially launched ChatGPT, a fine-tuned LLM initially developed based on the GPT−3.5 framework [11]. ChatGPT leverages extensive training data to analyze and generate text using deep neural networks. Although not specifically trained for medical applications, it has demonstrated potential in medical data summarization, scientific writing, and education contexts [12]. The impact of ChatGPT on medical education and professional examinations has attracted increasing scholarly attention. Previous studies have shown that ChatGPT performs commendably in various medical training assessments, including the Orthopedic In-Training Examination [13], the Dentistry Specialization Residency Examination [14], and diagnostic radiology in-training examination questions [15], among others. Moreover,recent research has demonstrated that ChatGPT−4 can accurately identify imaging modalities,highlighting its value as a supplementary tool in radiology education. However,limitations in anatomical structure identification and sidedness suggest that it should be used with caution and not as a sole diagnostic or reporting tool [16].

The development and deployment of OpenAI's GPT series have been primarily driven by major technology companies operating under proprietary business models. Nevertheless, the emergence of open-source large language models (LLMs) is reshaping this competitive landscape, creating new opportunities for both the research community and industry stakeholders. Recently, DeepSeek has gained recognition as a leading open-source LLM due to its strong technical performance, multilingual capabilities, cost-effectiveness, and transparency [17]. Despite having limited resources, DeepSeek has achieved performance comparable to OpenAI's flagship models in mathematical and scientific problem-solving [18]. Its open-access architecture has attracted considerable attention from researchers, who are able to rigorously examine, customize, and extend the model. This stands in stark contrast to the closed, proprietary framework of ChatGPT.

DeepSeek has demonstrated significant potential in scientific fields such as bioinformatics, computational chemistry, and neuroscience. It has exhibited performance comparable to OpenAI's GPT-4o in specific data analysis and diagnostic tasks [19]. However, there is a notable lack of information regarding DeepSeek’s performance in medical education and professional examinations, particularly in the domain of diagnostic radiology. This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of DeepSeek in answering practice questions of National Qualification Examination for Professional Technical Positions in Radiological Medicine, thereby assessing its potential as a powerful educational tool in the field of radiological medicine. To achieve this, we carefully selected relevant questions from the Exercise Book for the National Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination: Radiology, published by People's Medical Publishing House. Subsequently, we conducted a comparative analysis of the performance of ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3 in the context of radiological medicine. To the best of authors’ knowledge, this is the first study to evaluate DeepSeek’s ability to answer practice questions related to this specific examination. The findings are expected to provide meaningful insights into the integration of AI in medical education and clinical decision support, highlighting its potential as a reliable and practical educational resource.

Materials and methods

Study design

This study employed a cross-sectional, simulation-based comparative design to evaluate the performance of two LLMs, ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3, in answering radiology board-style examination questions. A quantitative research approach was adopted, using standardized prompts and structured input protocols to simulate real-world educational scenarios. Model performance was assessed based on objective accuracy metrics across different question types and cognitive levels. No human participants or patient data were involved, and all procedures were conducted via publicly accessible AI platforms under controlled conditions.

LLMs selection and configuration

To evaluate the potential applications of advanced conversational AI in educational settings, two widely used systems, ChatGPT and DeepSeek, were selected as representative exemplars. The study employed the freely accessible versions of ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3 to represent the most widely utilized configurations in practice. Although both models possess the capability to access online information via internet search functions, this feature was deliberately disabled to ensure that their performance relied solely on internal knowledge. This precaution was taken to prevent potential contamination from external sources, such as question banks or online forums.

Question selection and classification

A total of 161 questions were randomly selected from the Exercise Book for the National Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination: Radiology, published by People's Medical Publishing House. This exercise book was compiled by a team convened by the Chinese Society of Radiology, comprising leading experts and academic authorities from across the country, thereby lending substantial credibility to the publication. It serves not only as a review resource for radiological medicine professionals preparing for senior qualification examinations, but also as a reference guide, manual, and textbook for clinical practice. The book plays a vital role in enhancing the quality of clinical education. It is organized into 14 chapters covering the following topics: introduction, central nervous system, peripheral nervous system, head and neck, respiratory system, circulatory system, digestive system, genitourinary system, musculoskeletal system, breast imaging, pediatric imaging, interventional radiology, molecular imaging, advances in medical imaging. The book also includes four types of questions: single-choice questions, multiple-choice questions, single-choice questions with shared stems, and case analysis questions.

In this study, to ensure comprehensive topic coverage, we randomly selected five single-answer multiple-choice questions and five multiple-answer multiple-choice questions from each chapter as basic questions. To assess advanced reasoning skills, two shared-stem multiple-choice questions and one case analysis question were randomly selected from each of the following systems: central nervous system, peripheral nervous system, respiratory system, circulatory system, digestive system, genitourinary system, and musculoskeletal system. The foundational questions primarily evaluate memory recall, while the advanced questions targeted higher-order cognitive skills, including comprehension, interpretation, inference, and practical knowledge application. The study included a total of 161 questions, consisting of 70 single-choice questions, 70 multiple-choice questions (MCQ), 14 single-choice questions with shared stems, and 7 case analysis questions. Some questions contained multiple parts, resulting in a total of 207 items to be answered. All questions were translated into English by the authors.

Chatbot testing procedure

All questions and answer options were manually entered into the LLMs by the first author, a board-certified radiologist with over five years of clinical and teaching experience. Her expertise ensured that the questions were accurately transcribed and interpreted in accordance with their intended medical context. Although both DeepSeek and ChatGPT are multilingual models capable of understanding Chinese, we chose to use English for all inputs. This methodological decision was made to standardize the evaluation and establish a common benchmark, as English remains the dominant language in the pre-training corpora of most LLMs. Conducting the comparison in this lingua franca minimized potential variability arising from differences in language-specific training data, allowing for a more direct assessment of the models' underlying medical knowledge.

Over a seven-day period (April 1−7, 2025), each chatbot was tested using a comprehensive set of questions. To ensure unbiased responses, the chat was reset before each new question, initiating a fresh session for every query [20]. A standardized prompt was used consistently across all sessions without any modification: ‘You are taking the Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination in Radiology. Choose the correct answer for each question.’ To better align their responses with those expected from a radiologist, the prompt was supplemented by custom instructions: ‘I am a radiology attending physician’ and ‘Respond as you would to a radiologist in training’ [21].

To simulate practical application, each question, along with its respective answers, was entered into ChatGPT and DeepSeek only once. To simulate practical application, each question with its corresponding answer choices was entered into ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3 only once. Since all questions were in multiple-choice format, single-choice items explicitly required the selection of a single correct answer, whereas multiple-choice items specified that more than one option was correct. Consequently, both models generated clear option-based outputs that could be directly compared with the reference answers, and no ambiguous or irregular responses were observed.

Statistical analysis

The relationship between AI model type and responses accuracy across all questions, as well as within specific subtopic questions, was analyzed using Pearson's chi-square tests and Fisher’s exact test. Column comparisons were conducted via z-tests. All statistical analyzes were performed using SPSS 27, with a significance level set at 0.05. Since this study did not involve human or animal subjects, ethical approval was not required.

Results

Comparative analysis of overall performance

A statistically significant difference in accuracy was observed between DeepSeek-V3 and GPT−3.5 when all responses were evaluated using chi-square analysis (Table 1). Specifically, DeepSeek and ChatGTP correctly answered 72% and 55.6% of the items (149 out of 207 vs. 115 out of 207), respectively. DeepSeek's performance exceeded the passing threshold, while GPT's score did not meet the required standard. Compared to ChatGTP, DeepSeek provided a significantly higher number of correct responses (P < 0.001).

Comparative analysis of performance by question type

In the comparative analysis of different question formats, DeepSeek demonstrated consistently superior performance to ChatGPT across all categories, with statistically significant differences (all P-values < 0.05; Table 1). Both models achieved optimal accuracy on single-choice questions assessing factual recall, while exhibiting significantly lower performance on multiple-choice question formats. This performance pattern suggests a comparative advantage in fact-retrieval tasks Figure 2 exemplifies this by showcasing a representative single-choice question accurately answered by both ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3, providing a visual demonstration of their capabilities in processing and responding to knowledge recall queries. Despite both models performing relatively well in this category, DeepSeek a significantly higher accuracy rate of 87.1% (61 out of 70) compared to ChatGPT's 78.6% (55 out of 70) (P < 0.05). In contrast, both models demonstrated significantly lower performance on multiple-choice questions, potentially due to the increased reasoning complexity and the requirement to identify multiple correct answers. DeepSeek attained a correct response rate of 55.7% (39 out of 70), while ChatGPT reached only 30% (21 out of 70), with this difference also statistical significance (P < 0.001). Figure 3 presents a representative example of a question assessing the factors that affect the diagnostic accuracy of coronary imaging. In this instance, DeepSeek accurately identified all pertinent options (A, B, C, and D), whereas ChatGPT overlooked one critical factor, selecting only options A, B, and C. This discrepancy highlights DeepSeek’s superior proficiency in handling multi-component decision-making tasks and underscores the limitations of ChatGPT in integrating complex knowledge recall with basic understanding. Furthermore, in both single-choice questions with shared stems and case analysis questions, DeepSeek demonstrated significantly superior performance compared to ChatGPT (P = 0.011 and < 0.001, respectively), with ChatGPT failing to meet the passing standard in both categories.

Example of model responses to a classification question on Scimitar syndrome.Both ChatGPT-3.5 and DeepSeek-V3 correctly identified Option D (abnormal right pulmonary vein return) as the hallmark feature of Scimitar syndrome. This rare congenital anomaly is characterized by anomalous pulmonary venous drainage of the right lung into the inferior vena cava or systemic veins, often producing a curved ‘scimitar-shaped’ shadow on chest radiographs. The figure illustrates how both models not only selected the correct option but also provided explanatory reasoning, distinguishing Scimitar syndrome from other conditions such as bronchial atresia, pulmonary sequestration, arteriovenous malformation, and pulmonary varices.

In contrast, ChatGPT−3.5 selected only Options A–C, overlooking respiratory misregistration artifacts, which may lead to misalignment in reconstructed images.

Discussion

The main finding of this exploratory study is that DeepSeek-V3 demonstrated marked superiority over ChatGPT−3.5 in answering radiology board-style examination questions, achieving an overall accuracy of 72%, compared to ChatGPT−3.5's 55.6%. This enhanced performance was consistently observed across various question formats, including single-choice questions, multiple-choice questions, single-choice questions with shared stems, and case analysis questions. Notably, DeepSeek-V3 surpassed the passing threshold, whereas ChatGPT−3.5 did not.

The results of this study provide important insights into the potential application of DeepSeek as an educational tool in the field of radiology. Its superior performance across multiple question types suggests that it may serve as a valuable resource for medical professionals preparing for certification exams. The question bank utilized in this study, derived from the National Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination Exercise Book: Radiology, encompasses fundamental domains of radiological knowledge and interrogates multiple tiers of cognitive ability through diverse question formats. Importantly, these items are closely aligned with clinical practice, assessing not only foundational competencies but also higher-order skills such as clinical reasoning. Case-based questions, for instance, emulate real-world diagnostic workflows in which radiologists synthesize imaging findings with patient history. Notably, the parallels with international examinations—including the American Board of Radiology (ABR) and the European Diploma in Radiology (EDiR)—suggest that DeepSeek’s robust performance transcends the Chinese qualification framework, underscoring its potential applicability to international certification pathways and medical education.

Beyond its overall performance, it is important to examine how DeepSeek performs across different question formats and cognitive domains. A notable strength of DeepSeek is its exceptional performance on single-choice questions, with an accuracy rate of 87.1%. This high level of accuracy suggests that DeepSeek is particularly adept at straightforward knowledge retrieval and fact-based queries. These capabilities are especially important for students, as they help consolidate foundational knowledge and enhance long-term memory retention. This observation aligns with previous research on other LLMs, such as ChatGPT, in the context of medical examinations. For instance, ChatGPT has demonstrated performance at or near the passing threshold of the United States Medical Licensing Examination (USMLE) [22,23]. Furthermore, research by Rajesh Bhayana et al [21]. indicates that ChatGPT performs well on Radiology Board-style examinations. Its strengths are most evident in questions involving knowledge recall and basic understanding, which are considered lower-order thinking skills. In our study, DeepSeek exhibited a similar pattern.

Despite DeepSeek’s overall success, its performance on multiple-choice questions (55.7%) and case analysis questions (68%) was comparatively lower. This discrepancy could be attributed to the increased complexity and ambiguity inherent in multiple-answer questions, which require a deeper understanding of context and more nuanced interpretation of content. The model's difficulties with these questions suggest that its ability to discern and process complex, multifaceted responses need further refinement. Additionally, case analysis questions, which assess the application of knowledge in clinical scenarios, posed challenges. While DeepSeek performed better than ChatGPT on these tasks, it did not fully reach the desired level of proficiency. Recent work evaluating ChatGPT, Google Bard, and Bing on FRCR2A-style radiology case vignettes similarly reported that all three AI models scored below the passing threshold and were outperformed by radiology residents, underscoring the current limitations of generative AI in complex case interpretation [24,25]. Taken together, these findings indicate that DeepSeek is a promising tool for knowledge recall. However, its performance in higher-order cognitive tasks, such as clinical reasoning and decision-making, still requires further refinement to meet the expectations of medical professionals.

Upon evaluating performance across various clinical subspecialties, DeepSeek consistently outperform ChatGPT in most areas - particularly in the peripheral nervous system, respiratory system, circulatory system, and musculoskeletal system. This demonstrates the model’s proficiency in handing specialized radiological topics and suggests that DeepSeek may be especially valuable for training in complex or less commonly emphasized areas of the field. DeepSeek’s strong performance in specific subspecialties, particularly the musculoskeletal and respiratory systems, may be attributed to the composition of its underlying dataset. It is likely that this dataset includes a rich and comprehensive representation of information from these domains. While these findings highlight both strengths and weaknesses of DeepSeek-V3 across different domains, an important next step is to consider how the model can be practically integrated into radiology education.

From an educational perspective, the integration of DeepSeek into trainee preparation could be approached as a complementary learning tool rather than a stand-alone resource. For example, educators may encourage students to use DeepSeek for rapid knowledge retrieval, self-testing with question banks, and consolidation of foundational concepts, which are areas where the model excels. At the same time, safeguards should be established to address its current limitations in higher-order reasoning. Trainees could be guided to critically appraise the model’s outputs, particularly in complex case scenarios, and to verify responses against authoritative textbooks, guidelines, or faculty feedback. Educators might also design structured learning sessions in which DeepSeek is used to generate preliminary answers, followed by group discussions or expert-led reviews that highlight nuances, contextual judgment, and clinical decision-making. Such strategies would allow DeepSeek to enhance efficiency in factual learning while ensuring that deeper cognitive skills—such as reasoning through uncertainty and integrating multifaceted clinical information—continue to be developed through traditional training and mentorship.

Limitations

Although the findings of this study are promising, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the questions were sourced exclusively from the Exercise Book for the National Senior Health Professional Qualification Examination: Radiology. While this resource is authoritative, it primarily emphasizes factual recall, potentially underrepresenting higher-order reasoning. Future research should incorporate a broader range of formats, including open-ended, image-based, and clinical scenario–based questions. Second, all questions and interactions were conducted in English. This approach ensured methodological standardization and facilitated international benchmarking but may have introduced performance bias. ChatGPT, trained predominantly on English-language corpora, may have been advantaged, whereas DeepSeek, optimized for Chinese, may not have fully realized its potential. As a result, the observed performance differential might have differed under a Chinese-language evaluation. Third, the models were evaluated without access to external databases or real-time information, potentially constraining their ability to provide nuanced, up-to-date responses. Future iterations of DeepSeek that integrate internet search or specialized radiology databases could mitigate this limitation. Fourth, this study did not assess image interpretation, a fundamental component of radiological practice. The capacity of LLMs to analyze radiographs, CT, or MRI data remains untested and warrants further investigation, particularly with respect to integrated textual and visual query responses. Finally, the comparison was restricted to ChatGPT−3.5 and DeepSeek-V3. Although this yielded valuable insights, the exclusion of more advanced models, such as GPT−5, is noteworthy. GPT−5 is expected to outperform GPT−3.5 in medical examinations; its inclusion in future studies could provide a more comprehensive understanding of AI capabilities in medical education and inform their integration into clinical and educational workflows.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this study illustrates that DeepSeek represents a highly effective AI tool with significant potential in the domain of radiology. Its strong performance in answering multiple-choice questions related to radiology demonstrates the potential of LLMs as effective educational tools. They can be particularly useful for exam preparation and reinforcing knowledge. However, further research is needed to explore its ability to handle more complex cognitive tasks and image-based queries, as well as its potential for integration into real-world clinical workflows. Ultimately, the successful deployment of AI systems like DeepSeek could revolutionize medical education, providing more personalized, efficient, and effective training for future radiologists.

Decomposition in 2d non-invertible gaugings
Alonso Perez-Lona

Abstract




We extend the decomposition conjecture to 2d quantum field theories with a gauged
\text{Rep}(H)
symmetry category for
H
a finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra with
\text{Rep}(G)
trivially-acting and
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
the remaining symmetry, for
G,\Gamma
finite groups. We check our extension by explicitly computing partition functions, and by verifying that previous results arise as special cases. Furthermore, we compute the topological operators responsible for enforcing the decomposition. Then, drawing from these results, we formulate a plausible decomposition conjecture for the even more general case of
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})
trivially-acting and
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime})
the remaining symmetry, for
H^{\prime},H^{\prime\prime}
Hopf algebras, not necessarily associated with groups.

Introduction

The connection between gauged trivially-acting finite groups, and decomposition in two dimensions, is by now well-known. On the one hand, decomposition, the splitting of a single theory into a disjoint sum of independent theories, was originally observed in the context of two-dimensional theories
[
2
]
, such as orbifolds, and gauge theories (see
[
3
]
for a streamlined review). Since then, decomposition has led to general statements about 2d (e.g.
[
4
,
5
,
38
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
), 3d (e.g.
[
12
,
13
,
14
,
15
]
), and 4d (e.g.
[
16
]
) quantum field theories. On the other hand, the idea of global symmetries has recently undergone a significant extension, resulting in what is nowadays known as generalized symmetries
[
17
,
18
]
(see also the lecture notes
[
19
,
20
,
21
]
and references therein), and in particular non-invertible symmetries. Thus, decomposition and non-invertible symmetries are general features of quantum field theories. The purpose of this paper is to systematically describe decomposition in two-dimensional theories with gauged non-invertible symmetries, in which a subsymmetry acts trivially.

In the context of (finite) non-invertible symmetries in two dimensions, symmetries are described in full generality by (multi-)fusion categories. While the additional layer of information, namely, morphisms between objects, makes the overall picture significantly richer, it also makes algebraic manipulations more convoluted. Finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras provide a compromise between both pictures: while via their representation categories they give rise to very general fusion categories, their algebraic data, and in particular extension theory, is conceptually similar to that of groups.

In
[
2
]
, a concrete decomposition conjecture was proposed for 2d theories with finite group gauge symmetries with trivially-acting subsymmetries. Partition function computations provide robust evidence for the conjecture. In this paper, we propose an extension of this conjecture to non-invertible symmetries given by categories of representations of Hopf algebras, where the trivially-acting symmetry is of the form
\text{Rep}(G)
, and the remaining symmetry is of the form
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
, for
G,\Gamma
a pair of finite groups. We check this conjecture by computing partition functions of the decomposing theories, in agreement with concrete examples already present in the literature
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
. Then, drawing from these results, we comment on a plausible decomposition conjecture for the even more general situation of
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})
trivially-acting, for
H^{\prime\prime}
a Hopf algebra.

The reader should note that all the fusion categories considered in this paper are representation categories of some finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra and, as such, always admit a fiber functor, which physically is interpreted as a fully symmetry-preserving gapped phase
[
22
,
23
]
. Not all fusion categories admit such a gapped phase, as exemplified by those of the form
\text{Vec}(G,\alpha)
for
[\alpha]\in H^{3}(G,U(1))
a nontrivial associator. It is, however, possible to interpret some fusion categories that do not admit said gapped phase as representation categories of
quasi
-Hopf algebras
[
24
]
. While we do not address this in the present paper, the arguments presented here at least conceptually extend to those cases. We leave a careful treatment of this for future work.

In Section
2
, we state our conjecture. In Section
3
, we heuristically introduce the necessary mathematical objects relevant for this paper, whose technical details are presented in Appendix
A
. In Section
4
, we concentrate on torus partition function computations for the special case of abelian extensions. We explicitly construct the special symmetric Frobenius algebra to gauge, and argue that the torus partition function splits in a way predicted by the general conjecture. Section
5
is concerned with the construction of the topological operators responsible for the decomposition. In Section
6
, we spell out some examples. Finally, in Section
7
, drawing from observations both from the conjecture in
[
2
]
and from the results from Section
4
, we describe a construction for a plausible decomposition conjecture for more general Hopf algebra extensions. In particular, we show how this general construction can recover previous statements. Appendix
B
contains a brief reminder of Sweedler’s notation for Hopf algebras, used mainly in Section
7
.

General conjecture

Given a two-dimensional theory
\cal T
with a finite group symmetry
\Gamma
, and a trivially-acting normal subgroup
N
, fitting in the exact sequence of groups

1\to N\to\Gamma\to G\to 1,

(2.1)

the decomposition conjecture
[
2
]
states that the
\Gamma
-gauged theory
[{\cal T}/\Gamma]
decomposes as

[{\cal T}/\Gamma]=\bigoplus_{i\in\text{Irrep}(N)/G}[{\cal T}/K_{i}]_{\omega_{i}},

(2.2)

where the sum runs over
\text{Irrep}(N)/G
, the set of orbits of the irreducible representations of
N
by an action by
G
,
K_{i}\leq G
is the stabilizer of the orbit
i
, and
\omega_{i}\in Z^{2}(K_{i},U(1))
is a discrete torsion 2-cocycle.

Non-invertible symmetries in two dimensions are specified by (multi-)fusion categories. Finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras provide, through their representation categories, interesting yet manageable example of such categories. Therefore, it is natural to expect a decomposition conjecture for symmetries described by representation categories of Hopf algebras.

We specialize to particular kinds of finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras known as
abelian
extensions. These describe the case of a
\text{Rep}(H)
symmetry with a
\text{Rep}(G)
-trivially acting subsymmetry and
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
the remaining symmetry, for
G,\Gamma
a pair of groups. As one might guess, these categories fit in an
exact

sequence
of fusion categories

\text{Rep}(G)\to\text{Rep}(H)\to\text{Vec}(\Gamma),

(2.3)

defined in Section
3
.

In this paper, we propose that the analogue of (
2.2
) for the setting (
2.3
) is

[{\cal T}/\text{Rep}(H)]=\bigoplus_{i\in G/\Gamma}[{\cal T}/K_{i}]_{\omega_{i}},

(2.4)

where the sum runs over
G/\Gamma
the orbits of the
set

G
by an action of
\Gamma
,
K_{i}\leq\Gamma
is the stabilizer of the orbit
i
, and
\omega_{i}\in Z^{2}(K_{i},U(1))
is some particular discrete torsion. By
[{\cal T}/\text{Rep}(H)]
, we mean the theory resulting from gauging the symmetric special Frobenius algebra
(A,\mu,\Delta)
determined by the fiber functor on
\text{Rep}(H)
that reconstructs the Hopf algebra
H
.

An interesting observation of the construction of the relevant Frobenius algebra is that the Frobenius algebra is independent of half of the Hopf algebra extension information, namely the algebra structure of
H
. This explains the striking similarity of the conjectures (
2.2
) and (
2.4
). Schematically, both consist of three different parts:

1.

An orbit set of the simples of
\text{Rep}((H^{\prime\prime})^{*})
, the representation category of the Hopf algebra
(H^{\prime\prime})^{*}

dual
to
H^{\prime\prime}
, where
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})
is the trivially-acting subcategory, by an action of the remaining symmetry category,

2.

for each orbit, a stabilizer subcategory of the remaining symmetry category,

3.

some discrete torsion that gauges the stabilizer subcategory.

In
[
9
]
, it was shown that the Frobenius multiplication for the Frobenius algebra that gauges
\text{Rep}(H)
is entirely determined by the comultiplication of
H
, and in
[
25
]
it was argued that the Frobenius comultiplication can be computed simply as the adjoint morphism to the Frobenius multiplication. Thus, it seems plausible that the decomposition of more general Hopf extensions is again independent of the algebra structure of
H
and thus exhibits the same schematic form described above.

Assuming this is the case, then we propose that the decomposition of a
\text{Rep}(H)
-gauged theory with
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})
trivially-acting and
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime})
the remaining symmetry, fitting in the exact sequence

\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})\to\text{Rep}(H)\to\text{Rep}(H^{\prime}),

(2.5)

takes the following form

[{\cal T}/\text{Rep}(H)]=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\,[{\cal T}/(K_{i},\mu_{i},\Delta_{i})],

(2.6)

where the sum runs over what may be understood as the orbits of the simples of
\text{Rep}((H^{\prime\prime})^{*})
under the action of
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime})
,
K_{i}
is the regular object of the stabilizer subcategory of the orbit
i
, and
(\mu_{i},\Delta_{i})
is some discrete torsion choice describing a possibly
non-invertible
gauging (see
[
9
,
11
,
25
]
for gauging symmetric special Frobenius algebras of non-invertible symmetries). We make all these components precise in Section
7
.

3.1
Exact sequences of fusion categories

In the case of group-like symmetries, exact sequences are used to describe trivially-acting subsymmetries. If a theory
\cal T
has a global symmetry parameterized by a finite group
\Gamma
such that a normal subgroup
N\unlhd\Gamma
acts trivially, then the
\Gamma
-action on
\cal T
factors through its quotient group
G=\Gamma/N
, which is the remaining symmetry. This gives rise to the exact sequence

1\to N\xrightarrow{i}\Gamma\xrightarrow{\pi}G\to 1,

(3.1)

for
i:N\hookrightarrow\Gamma
the inclusion, and
\pi:\Gamma\to G
the projection.

Thus, the scenario of a theory
\cal T
with a global symmetry parameterized by a fusion category
\cal C
such that a fusion subcategory
\cal K
acts trivially should be similarly encoded by an
exact sequence of fusion categories

{\cal K}\xrightarrow{\imath}{\cal C}\xrightarrow{F}{\cal D}.

(3.2)

The intuitive picture to keep in mind is that
\cal K
is a “normal subcategory” of
\cal C
, and that
\cal D
is the ”quotient” of
\cal C
by
\cal K
. The functors
\imath
and
F
come equipped with a strong monoidal structure, which allows us to transport fusion rules and algebra objects consistently from one fusion category to another. See Appendix
A.1
for the specific axioms.

3.2
Exact sequences of Hopf algebras

Concrete examples of exact sequences of fusion categories are provided by exact sequences of Hopf algebras. Here, we will take all Hopf algebras to be more specifically finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebras. See e.g.
[
9
, Appendix 1]
for the relevant axioms defining Hopf algebras.

An exact sequence, equivalently an
extension
, of Hopf algebras is a diagram of Hopf algebras of the form

H^{\prime}\xrightarrow{i}H\xrightarrow{\pi}H^{\prime\prime},

(3.3)

where
i,\pi
are Hopf algebra homomorphisms satisfying the conditions in Appendix
A.2.1
. As for fusion categories, the last object
H^{\prime\prime}
is thought of as the quotient of
H
by the Hopf subalgebra
H^{\prime}
.

Applying the contravariant functor
\text{Rep}(-)
to the sequence (
3.3
) results in an exact sequence of fusion categories
[
26
]

\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})\to\text{Rep}(H)\to\text{Rep}(H^{\prime}).

(3.4)

Exact sequences of fusion categories and of Hopf algebras both specialize to the familiar group-like case. Indeed, given a sequence (
3.1
)

1\to N\to\Gamma\to G\to 1,

this defines an exact sequence of dual group algebras

\mathbb{C}^{G}\to\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\to\mathbb{C}^{N},

(3.5)

where
\mathbb{C}^{G}
denotes the Hopf algebra of functions on
G
valued in
\mathbb{C}
, and which in turn, by using
\text{Rep}(-)
, defines the sequence of fusion categories

\text{Vec}(N)\to\text{Vec}(\Gamma)\to\text{Vec}(G).

(3.6)

This sequence of fusion categories is what we understand as a
\Gamma
-symmetry with a trivially-acting
N
-symmetry and a remaining
G
-symmetry, all non-anomalous (in the sense that each admits a fiber functor/Symmetry Protected Topological (SPT) phase
[
27
,
10
]
).

An accessible subclass of Hopf algebra extensions is given by imposing the additional assumptions
H^{\prime\prime}=\mathbb{C}G
and
H^{\prime}=\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}
for
G,\Gamma
a pair of finite groups. Here,
\mathbb{C}G
denotes the group Hopf algebra of
G
over the complex numbers. Sequences of this form

\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\to H\to\mathbb{C}G,

(3.7)

are called
abelian
extensions
.

All Hopf algebra extensions (
3.3
) are classified by
extension data
. As summarized in
[
28
]
(cf.
[
29
,
30
]
), in the special case of abelian extensions, the data consists of:

•

A
matched pair

[
31
]
of groups
(G,\Gamma,\triangleright,\triangleleft)
: a pair of finite groups
G,\Gamma
along with maps of sets

\displaystyle\triangleleft:

\displaystyle\Gamma\times G\to\Gamma,

(3.8)

\displaystyle\triangleright:

\displaystyle\Gamma\times G\to G,

(3.9)

satisfying the axioms (
A.23
)-(
A.24
) of actions by permutation,

•

and a pair of 2-cocycles
\sigma:G\times G\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\Gamma}
,
\tau:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{G}
satisfying the normalized 2-cocycle conditions (
A.25
)-(
A.30
), where
(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{G}
means the convolution-invertible complex-valued functions on
G
.

We will use the notation
\sigma_{s}(g,h)=\sigma(g,h)(s)
(and similarly for
\tau_{g}(t,u)
), for
g,h,l\in G
and
s,t,u\in\Gamma
.

The advantage of this setting is that all extension data is formulated in terms of actions and cocycles, thus allowing us to bypass explicitly dealing with dual versions of these even though both the algebra and coalgebra structures can be nontrivial.

The Hopf algebra
H
has an underlying vector space
\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\otimes\mathbb{C}G
, whose basis elements are customarily presented as
v_{g}\#x
for
x\in G
the usual basis of a group algebra, and
v_{g}
the dual basis of the basis element given by

The Hopf algebra structure
(u:\mathbb{C}\to H,\mu:H\otimes H\to H,\epsilon:H\to\mathbb{C},\Delta:H\to H\otimes H)
is given by
[

,
30

\displaystyle u:1\mapsto\sum_{g\in\Gamma}v_{g}\otimes 1,

(3.10)

\displaystyle\mu:(v_{g}\#x)\otimes(v_{h}\#y)\mapsto\delta_{g\triangleleft x,h}\sigma_{g}(x,y)v_{g}\#xy,

(3.11)

\displaystyle\epsilon:(v_{g}\#x)\mapsto\delta_{1,g},

(3.12)

\displaystyle\Delta:(v_{g}\#x)\mapsto\sum_{t\in\Gamma}(\tau_{x}(gt^{-1},t)v_{gt^{-1}}\#t\triangleright x)\otimes(v_{t}\#x).

(3.13)

The antipode is given by
[
33

S(v_{g}\#x)=\big(\sigma_{(g\triangleleft x)^{-1}}((g\triangleright x)^{-1},g\triangleright x)\big)^{-1}\,\big(\tau_{x}(g^{-1},g)\big)^{-1}\,v_{(g\triangleleft x)^{-1}}\#(g\triangleright x)^{-1}.

(3.14)

Finally, as a finite-dimensional Hopf algebra, it has an integral, and cointegral. As verified in Appendix
A.2.2
, the integral
\Lambda\in H
is

\Lambda=v_{1}\#\left(\sum_{x\in G}x\right).

(3.15)

A cointegral
\lambda

is the function

\lambda=\left(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}g\right)\#v_{1}.

(3.16)

This data will be used to construct the symmetric Frobenius algebra that gauges

4
Consistency check: partition function computations

In this section, we compute genus-one partition functions for gauged abelian Hopf extensions, in order to check the decomposition conjecture (
2.4
). This involves constructing the relevant symmetric special Frobenius (or, for short, gaugeable) algebra, as well as suitable monoidal functors to relate the partition function of the theory to those of its constituent universes.

The exact sequence one obtains from an abelian extension (
3.7
) of Hopf algebras is of the form

\text{Rep}(G)\to\text{Rep}(H)\xrightarrow{F}\text{Vec}(\Gamma),

(4.1)

where
F
is the functor induced by the inclusion of Hopf algebras
i:\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\to H
which on basis elements is

\displaystyle i:

\displaystyle\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}

\displaystyle\to H,

(4.2)

\displaystyle v_{g}

\displaystyle\mapsto v_{g}\#1.

(4.3)

Decomposition in this case is easier to describe, as the gaugeable algebras
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
with a single copy of the monoidal unit
\mathbbm{1}_{\text{Vec}(\Gamma)}=1
correspond to pairs
(H,\omega)
where
H\leq\Gamma
is a subgroup of
\Gamma
, and
[\omega]\in H^{2}(H,U(1))
is the cohomology class of a group 2-cocycle
[
34
]
. In terms of this classification, the algebra appearing here corresponds to the pair
(\Gamma,1)
, interpreted as gauging the whole
\Gamma
-symmetry with no discrete torsion.

4.1
Gaugeable algebra

The first step is to construct a gaugeable algebra in
\text{Rep}(H)
. We remind the reader that this algebra can be used to gauge on a surface of
any
genus. We will use the algebra based on the dual Hopf algebra
H^{*}
, following the process in
[
9
]
. This is the vector space
H^{*}=\mathbb{C}\Gamma\otimes\mathbb{C}^{G}
, with basis
g\otimes e_{x}
for
g\in\Gamma,x\in G
. The comultiplication
\Delta:H\to H\otimes H
in (
3.13
) gives the multiplication
m:H^{*}\otimes H^{*}\to H^{*}
on
H^{*}
by dualizing

\displaystyle\mu_{F}:=\Delta^{*}:

\displaystyle H^{*}\otimes H^{*}

\displaystyle\to H^{*},

(4.4)

\displaystyle(g\#v_{x})\otimes(h\#v_{y})

\displaystyle\mapsto\tau_{y}(g,h)gh\#\delta_{x,h\triangleright y}v_{y}.

(4.5)

The unit
u_{F}:\mathbb{C}\to H^{*}
is the dual map of the counit (
3.12

u_{F}=\sum_{x\in G}1\#v_{x}.

(4.6)

The general formula for the comultiplication

\Delta_{F}:=c\,(\text{Id}_{H}\otimes(\lambda\circ\mu))\circ(\text{Id}_{H}\otimes S\otimes\text{Id}_{H})\circ(\Delta\otimes\text{Id}_{H}))^{*},

(4.7)

for

m:=\text{Id}_{H}\otimes(\lambda\circ\mu))\circ(\text{Id}_{H}\otimes S\otimes\text{Id}_{H})\circ(\Delta\otimes\text{Id}_{H}).

(4.8)

Using this, we derive that

\displaystyle m((v_{g}\#x)\otimes(v_{h}\#y))=\sum_{t\in\Gamma}\delta_{t,(h\triangleleft y)^{-1}\triangleleft x^{-1}}\,\delta_{t\triangleright x,y}(\tau_{x}(g,((h\triangleleft y)^{-1}\triangleleft x^{-1})^{-1}))^{-1}v_{g((h\triangleleft y)^{-1}\triangleleft x^{-1})^{-1}}\#y,

so that the Frobenius comultiplication is given by

\Delta_{F}(k\#v_{z})=c\,\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big(\tau_{g^{-1}k\triangleright z}(g,g^{-1}k)\big)^{-1}\,\big((g\#v_{g^{-1}k\triangleright z})\otimes(g^{-1}k\#v_{z})\big).

Finally, the counit

is given by the dual of the integral

\epsilon_{F}(g\#v_{x})=(g\#v_{x})(\Lambda)=v_{1}(g)v_{x}\left(\sum_{y\in G}y\right)=\delta_{1,g}.

(4.11)

Therefore, the symmetric special Frobenius algebra
(H^{*},\mu_{F},u_{F},\Delta_{F},\epsilon_{F})
that gauges the fusion category
\text{Rep}(H)
is given by

u_{F}(1)=\sum_{x\in G}1\#v_{x},

(4.12)

\mu_{F}(g\#v_{x},h\#v_{y})=\tau_{y}(g,h)gh\#\delta_{x,h\triangleright y}v_{y},

(4.13)

\epsilon_{F}(k\#v_{z})=\delta_{1,k},

(4.14)

\Delta_{F}(k\#v_{z})=\frac{1}{|\Gamma|}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\big(\tau_{g^{-1}k\triangleright z}(g,g^{-1}k)\big)^{-1}\,\big((g\#v_{g^{-1}k\triangleright z})\otimes(g^{-1}k\#v_{z})\big),

which is the canonical gauging in the sense that it comes from the fiber functor of

that via Tannaka reconstruction

]
recovers

There is a crucial fact to highlight here. Observe that the algebra extension information, which consists of the 2-cocycle
\sigma:G\times G\to(\mathbb{C}^{\times})^{\Gamma}
and the action by permutation

, namely its discrete torsion

(cf.
[
37

3.13

The fact that the algebra extension information does not play a role in defining the discrete torsion of the regular
H

[
25

, the comultiplication comes from taking the adjoint of the multiplication morphism
\mu_{F}:H^{*}\otimes H^{*}\to H^{*}
. However, the Frobenius multiplication (

H
.

4.2
Partition function computation

In the finite group symmetry decomposition conjecture
[
2
]
, the partition function decomposition is obtained by first computing the partition function of the non-effectively acting symmetry
\Gamma
, and then quotienting out the trivially-acting part using the projection group homomorphism
\pi:\Gamma\to G
to the remaining finite symmetry group. Intuitively, this looks like

Z_{g,h}\mapsto Z_{\pi(g),\pi(h)}.

(4.16)

In
[
37
,
11
]
, partition function decompositions for a projection of symmetries
\rho:\text{Rep}(\Gamma)\to\text{Rep}(G)
were obtained by first computing the partition function in
\text{Rep}(\Gamma)
, and then transferring the partition function to the remaining symmetry category
\text{Rep}(G)
, in analogy to the scenario above. This was done at a schematic level given the complexity of constructing the strong monoidal structure of the functor
\rho
.

Since we are only interested in observing how the
\text{Rep}(H)
-gauged partition function looks like in terms of
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
-orbifolds, a different approach is to first transfer the gaugeable algebra
(H^{*},\mu_{F},\Delta_{F})
to
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
and only then gauge it in
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
. This has the advantage that we do not need to deal with F-symbols in
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
, as all the associator information can be taken to be trivial in said category. One can proceed this way because the restriction functor
F:\text{Rep}(H)\to\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
is a tensor functor, so that it preserves gaugeable algebras. Therefore, one may talk about the gaugeable algebra
F((H^{*},\mu_{F},\Delta_{F}))
in
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
. For simplicity, we will denote this algebra as
(A,\mu_{F},\Delta_{F})
.

4.2.1
Module decomposition

As a first step, we want to obtain the decomposition of the object
A:=F(H^{*})\in\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
into simples

A=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}N^{g}_{A}U_{g},

(4.17)

for
U_{g}
the 1-dimensional
\Gamma
-graded homogeneous vector space with degree
g\in\Gamma
. That is, we exhibit the
\Gamma
-grading (
\mathbb{C}\Gamma
-comodule structure) on
H^{*}
, which is given by composing the dual of the multiplication (
3.11
)

\displaystyle\mu^{*}:

\displaystyle H^{*}

\displaystyle\to H^{*}\otimes H^{*}

(4.18)

\displaystyle g\#v_{x}

\displaystyle\mapsto\sum_{y\in G}\sigma_{g}(y,y^{-1}x)(g\#v_{y})\otimes((g\triangleleft y)\#v_{y^{-1}x}),

(4.19)

with the dual inclusion
i^{*}:g\#v_{y}\mapsto g\,\delta_{1,y}
,

\displaystyle(i^{*}\otimes\text{id}_{H^{*}})\circ\mu^{*}:g\#v_{x}\mapsto g\otimes(g\#v_{x}).

(4.20)

As one would expect, this says that in the decomposition (
4.17
),
N^{g}_{A}=|G|
for all

7.3
Orbits

Having described the
\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})
-module structure on
\text{Rep}((H^{\prime\prime})^{*})
, we are ready to make the notion of
orbits
precise. For this, let
{\cal I}
be the set of equivalence classes of irreps of
(H^{\prime\prime})^{*}
. We can define the following relation for any pair
(W,r),(W^{\prime},r^{\prime})\in{\cal I}

(W,r)\sim(W^{\prime},r^{\prime})\iff(W,r)\text{ is a subquotient of }\rho_{(V,c)}(W^{\prime},r^{\prime})

(7.44)

for some
(V,c)\in\text{ob}(\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*}))
. For objects
x,y\in\text{ob}({\cal C})
,
x
is a
subquotient
of
y
if there exists an object
z\in\text{ob}({\cal C})
such that
z\hookrightarrow y
is a subobject (monomorphism) of
y
and
z\twoheadrightarrow x
is a quotient (epimorphism) of
z
.

The relation (
7.44
) is an equivalence relation
[
36
, Proposition 7.6.6]
. By the set of orbits we mean

\mathcal{O}:=\mathcal{I}/\sim,

(7.45)

the set of equivalence classes. It can be shown
[
36
, Proposition 7.6.7]
that this partition of
\cal I
extends to the whole
\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})
-module category, in the sense that
\text{Rep}((H^{\prime\prime})^{*})
splits into a direct sum of abelian categories (but clearly not of fusion categories)

\text{Rep}((H^{\prime\prime})^{*})=\bigoplus_{i\in\mathcal{O}}\mathcal{M}_{i},

(7.46)

where each
\mathcal{M}_{i}
includes the simple objects in the equivalence class
i\in\mathcal{O}
, and is a
\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})
-module category on its own, so that there is a family of action (tensor) functors

\rho_{i}:\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})\to\text{End}(\mathcal{M}_{i}).

(7.47)

7.4
Stabilizers and induced gaugeable algebras

Finally, for each tensor functor we can consider its kernel
\mathcal{K}_{i}:=\mathfrak{Ker}_{\rho_{i}}\subset\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})
, the full tensor subcategory of objects whose image under
\rho_{i}
is isomorphic to a finite direct sum of the identity linear automorphism
\text{id}_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}:\mathcal{M}_{i}\to\mathcal{M}_{i}
. In other words, we are considering the
stabilizer
subcategory, the fusion subcategory whose objects act trivially on the orbit
i
. The restriction of
\rho_{i}
to its kernel and corestriction to the category
\langle\text{id}_{\mathcal{M}_{i}}\rangle=\text{Vec}
spanned by the identity functor describes a fiber functor on
\mathcal{K}_{i}

\rho_{i}|_{\mathcal{K}_{i}}:\mathcal{K}_{i}\to\text{Vec},

(7.48)

giving a Hopf algebra as
\text{End}(\rho_{i}|_{\mathcal{K}_{i}})
, and thus a symmetric special Frobenius algebra
K_{i}\in\text{ob}(\mathcal{K}_{i})\subset\text{ob}(\text{Comod}((H^{\prime})^{*})
. This is precisely the algebra one gauges for the class
i\in\mathcal{O}
in the decomposition.

8
Conclusion

In this paper, we conjectured the decomposition of a theory
\cal T
with a
\text{Rep}(H)
symmetry, for
H
a finite-dimensional semisimple Hopf algebra, with a trivially-acting subcategory
\text{Rep}(G)
and
\text{Vec}(\Gamma)
the remaining symmetry, for
G,\Gamma
finite groups. Mathematically, this was described by abelian extensions of Hopf algebras, extensions of the form

\mathbb{C}^{\Gamma}\to H\to\mathbb{C}G,

(8.1)

which give rise to exact sequences of fusion categories

\text{Rep}(G)\to\text{Rep}(H)\to\text{Vec}(\Gamma).

(8.2)

We explicitly derived the decomposition of the
\text{Rep}(H)
-gauged partition function, and computed the topological projection responsible for this decomposition.

Motivated by the observation that, in the abelian extension case, the algebra extension information is not relevant for the decomposed partition function, combined with alternative constructions of gaugeable algebras which are independent of the algebra structure of
H
, we commented on a plausible decomposition conjecture and provided a general construction which computes the set of universes and their corresponding non-invertible orbifolds (gaugeable algebras). This outlines a decomposition conjecture for a
\text{Rep}(H)
symmetry category with a
\text{Rep}(H^{\prime\prime})
trivially-acting subcategory.

We hope to extend in future work the explicit analysis of Section
4
to more general Hopf algebra extensions, as well as the computation of more examples. Also, as indicated in Section
5.2
cf. Footnote
5
, it would be interesting to provide a more systematic explanation of the projective (“mixed-anomaly”) phases discussed in
[
39
]
.

Furthermore, one should note that exact sequences of fusion categories, not necessarily of the form
\text{Rep}(H)
for
H
a Hopf algebra, are very generally classified by a
Hopf monad
on what is understood as the remaining symmetry category. It has been suggested in
[
48
]
that Hopf monads may play a role in the description of generalized symmetries in two dimensions, and decomposition in this generality seems like a natural application of this. We hope to address this in future work.

A.2
Exact sequences of Hopf algebras

We spell out the details of Hopf algebra exact sequences and their defining extension data.

A.2.1
General extensions

An exact sequence, equivalently an
extension
, of Hopf algebras is a diagram of the form

H^{\prime}\xrightarrow{i}H\xrightarrow{\pi}H^{\prime\prime},

(A.7)

where
i,\pi
are Hopf algebra homomorphisms, such that

1.

i
is injective, and
\pi
is surjective,

2.

i(H^{\prime})=\{h\in H|(\pi\otimes\text{id}_{H})\Delta(h)=1\otimes h\}
,

3.

\text{ker}\,\pi=\{ab\in H|a\in H,\ b\in\text{ker}(\epsilon|_{i(H^{\prime})})\}
.

The set
\text{ker}(\epsilon|_{i(H^{\prime})})
is the kernel of the restricted counit
\epsilon|_{i(H^{\prime})}:i(H^{\prime})\to\mathbb{C}
, and is also known as the
augmentation ideal
of
i(H^{\prime})
.

Sequence of this form (
A.7
), as in the case of groups, are classified by
extension data
involving only the Hopf algebras
H^{\prime}
and
H^{\prime\prime}
. The extension data consists of four linear maps
(\triangleright,\rho,\sigma,\tau)
: the
weak action

\triangleright:H^{\prime\prime}\otimes H^{\prime}\to H^{\prime}
, the
weak coaction

\rho:H^{\prime\prime}\to H^{\prime\prime}\otimes H^{\prime}
, the
2-cocycle

\sigma:H^{\prime\prime}\otimes H^{\prime\prime}\to H^{\prime}
, and the
dual 2-cocycle

\tau:H^{\prime\prime}\to H^{\prime}\otimes H^{\prime}
. These linear maps are required to satisfy an array of compatibility conditions, listed in
[
32
, Theorem 2.20]
). Succinctly, the maps
(\triangleright,\sigma)
determine the
algebra
structure on
H
, whereas the maps
(\rho,\tau)
determine the
coalgebra
structure.

In this paper, for more general Hopf algebra extensions, we will only make explicit use of the identities satisfied by the maps
(\triangleright,\sigma)
:

1.

normalized 2-cocycle condition

\displaystyle\sigma(1,h)=\sigma(h,1)=\epsilon(h)\,1,

(A.8)

\displaystyle(h_{(1)}\triangleright\sigma(l_{(1)},m_{(1)})\sigma(h_{(2)},l_{(2)}m_{(2)})=\sigma(h_{(1)},l_{(1)})\sigma(h_{(2)}l_{(2)},m),

(A.9)

2.

weak action condition

\displaystyle h\triangleright(ab)=(h_{(1)}\triangleright a)(h_{(2)}\triangleright b),

(A.10)

\displaystyle h\triangleright 1=\epsilon(h)\,1,

(A.11)

\displaystyle 1\triangleright a=a.

(A.12)

3.

twisted module condition

(h_{(1)}\triangleright(l_{(1)}\triangleright a))\tau(h_{(2)},l_{(2)})=\sigma(h_{(1)},l_{(1)})(h_{(2)}l_{(2)}\triangleright a).

(A.13)

In stating these identities, we are making use of
Sweedler’s notation
(see Appendix
B
).

As a quick example, we can specialize to group algebras with coalgebra extension information
(\rho,\tau)
that is all trivial:

\mathbb{C}N\to\mathbb{C}\Gamma\to\mathbb{C}G.

(A.14)

Since the coalgebra structure of group algebras themselves is trivial, in the sense that
\Delta(g)=g\otimes g
and
\epsilon(g)=1
, then the extension information reduces to the familiar 2-cocycle with nontrivial action. The group algebra cocycles and action are the linear extension of cocycles and actions on the groups. Indeed, for
h,l,m\in G
and
a,b\in\Gamma
, the identities become

\displaystyle\sigma(1,h)=\sigma(h,1)=1,

(A.15)

\displaystyle(h\triangleright\sigma(l,m))\sigma(h,lm)=\sigma(h,l)\sigma(hl,m),

(A.16)

\displaystyle h\triangleright(ab)=(h\triangleright a)(h\triangleright b),

(A.17)

\displaystyle h\triangleright 1=\,1,

(A.18)

\displaystyle 1\triangleright a=a,

(A.19)

where in particular the action
\triangleright:G\times\Gamma\to\Gamma
becomes a
G
-action by homomorphisms on
\Gamma
.

(A.32)

Appendix B
Sweedler’s notation

Throughout the paper, we make use of Sweedler’s notation. Given a coalgebra
H
with comultiplication
\Delta
, the coproduct of any element
h\in H
is represented as

\Delta(h)=h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)},

(B.1)

where a sum over indices is assumed. Each term
h_{(1)},h_{(2)}\in H
is an element of the coalgebra. In particular, in this notation, coassociativity of
\Delta
can be represented as

(h_{(1)})_{(1)}\otimes(h_{(1)})_{(2)}\otimes h_{(2)}=h_{(1)}\otimes(h_{(2)})_{(1)}\otimes(h_{(2)})_{(2)}=h_{(1)}\otimes h_{(2)}\otimes h_{(3)}.

(B.2)

Sweedler’s notation also applies for comodules. Given a vector space
V
with a coaction
\rho:V\to V\otimes H
by a coalgebra
H
, the coaction on an element
v\in V
is represented as the implicit sum

\rho(c)=c_{(i)}\otimes h^{(i)}.

(B.3)

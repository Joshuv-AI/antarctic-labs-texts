Assessing knowledge and attitudes toward geriatric dentistry among dentists and senior dental students: A cross-sectional study in Yazd, Iran
Authors: Arman Dabestani, Maryam Sabet, Fahimeh Rashidi Maybodi
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BACKGROUND:The increase in the elderly population with multiple chronic diseases and their unique needs has challenged the health system. Therefore, dentists need to have proper awareness and attitude toward the treatment of this group of society. Due to the lack of studies on geriatric dentistry in Iran, this study evaluated the knowledge and attitude of dentists and senior dental students regarding geriatric dentistry.MATERIALS AND METHODS:This cross-sectional study was conducted from August to December 2023 in Yazd-Iran. Sixty-three students and 126 dentists participated. Data were collected using a self-administered questionnaire that included demographic information, knowledge about geriatric dentistry, and related attitude questions. Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 22 software, the Mann-Whitney U-test, and Spearman’s correlation. A significance level of less than 0.05 was considered.RESULTS:None of the dental students and only 9.5% of dentists had the desired level of knowledge, but most of the students and dentists had a positive attitude toward geriatric dentistry. The mean knowledge score of senior dental students (5.71 ± 0.67) was significantly lower than that of general dentists (6.91 ± 1.10) (P value < 0.001). However, there was no significant difference between the mean attitude score of senior students (24.52 ± 1.89) and general dentists (24.30 ± 3.06) (P value = 0.266). A positive significant correlation was found between knowledge and attitude scores in senior students (P value < 0.001), but no significant relationship was observed in dentists (P value = 0.388).CONCLUSION:There is a need to focus more on related issues in the general dental curriculum and hold more retraining courses in this field.

Introduction

In recent years, improvements in public health, medical knowledge advancements, and new treatment methods have significantly increased life expectancy. This has resulted in a significant growth in the population of elderly individuals, defined as those aged 65 years and above.[1] This trend is more pronounced in developing countries compared to others, with estimates suggesting that by 2050, 22% of Iran’s population will be elderly.[1,2] As a result, there is a growing need for accessible medical and dental care for this demographic.

As the population gets older, the incidence of chronic diseases also increases, some of which can have manifestations or complications that may cause limitations in chewing and swallowing power in the elderly. For instance, diseases such as diabetes or osteoporosis can affect the course of periodontal diseases.[3,4] Common oral complications of this age group include tooth loss, periodontal diseases, decreased saliva secretion, and oral cancers, among the most important factors determining life expectancy.[5] In addition, the restriction of the use of some drugs, such as antibiotics or vasoconstrictors, such as epinephrine, in the elderly population can make some dental procedures more complicated. Long-term use of some drugs in some elderly individuals, such as antihypertensive agents, can also lead to tooth decay due to the effect on the amount of saliva.[6,7,8] In addition, possible physical and mental problems in the elderly reduce their ability to perform routine oral and dental health care.[3] Furthermore, older people with limited mobility due to some chronic diseases will face many problems when visiting dental services.

Moreover, this group’s access to dental services is limited due to decreased income and insufficient financial support from the people around them. As a result, lack of oral and dental hygiene and loss of teeth can increase complications and even mortality in the senior population.[9] Therefore, paying attention to oral and dental care in these age groups becomes especially important.

In previous studies conducted in different countries, it has been reported that most participating dentists do not have adequate knowledge and positive attitudes regarding geriatric dentistry and do not have the necessary self-efficiency to provide dental services to this group.[1,3,9,10,11,12] In a systematic review by Abdelrahim et al. in 2023,[13] the need to improve dental education in meeting the oral health needs of the elderly has been reported.

Knowledge about geriatric dentistry is fundamental in creating appropriate professional behavior and providing proper treatment services for this group. On the contrary, by developing sufficient skills and knowledge among dentists in this field, the dentist’s desire to provide treatment and diagnostic services to this group increases.[3] Unfortunately, some recent studies have shown that dentists and dental students do not desire to treat the elderly in the future.[14,15] Another important point is the lack of sufficient resources regarding geriatric dentistry in the dental curriculum in Iran, which may affect the participants’ knowledge level.[9] During the last two decades, in the dental schools of Croatia and some other countries of the European continent, the educational content of geriatric dentistry has been added to the academic curriculum of students.[16] In a study by Tahani et al.[15] in Isfahan in 2021, regarding geriatric education status, nearly half of the dentists were not satisfied with the faculty’s training in treating the elderly and considered it insufficient. Due to the lack of similar domestic studies in this field and the importance of the subject, this study was designed to evaluate the knowledge and attitude of dentists and senior (final-year) dental students in Yazd City regarding geriatric dentistry. Identifying knowledge gaps and incorrect viewpoints would help improve dental education in this field. Considering the recent addition of geriatric dentistry topic among the very diverse topics of the gerontology course, although only in the form of 4-hour theoretical training, this study can also somehow help to investigate the effectiveness of these changes in the curriculum.

Materials and Methods

Study design and setting

This descriptive cross-sectional study was conducted from August to December 2023 in Yazd, Iran, after approval by the Ethics Committee of Shahid Sadoughi University of Medical Sciences under the number IR.SSU.DENTISTRY.REC.1401.101. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, who were assured that anonymous data collection would be done.

Study participants and sampling

Considering the confidence level of 95%, the standard deviation value of 2.9, and the estimation error of 0.5 based on the study by Tahani et al.,[3] the required sample size was 130 dentists. Senior students were included in the study by census. General dentists who were members of the social network group of the “Yazd Dental Association” randomly (using random numbers table) received the questionnaire randomly through the “Porsline” link and sent the link to their colleagues via a snowball sampling method until the quorum was reached. When the sample size was full, the above link became disabled.

Data collection tools and technique

Data were collected using a questionnaire. The questionnaire’s content validity was determined using Lawshe’s proposed model, a robust method that examines and confirms three criteria: necessity, transparency, and relevance. The questionnaire’s internal reliability was also confirmed using Cronbach’s alpha of 0.7.

This researcher-made self-administered questionnaire was structured into three parts: The first part gathers demographic information through five questions. The second part focuses on knowledge about older people’s oral health-related issues, with 11 questions. The third part assessed attitudes toward geriatric dentistry, with 10 questions. During the 20-minute fixed response time, the researcher was available to answer any question in case of ambiguity.

The scoring for these questions was such that 1 point was given for the correct answer to the knowledge questions, 0.5 points was given for the answer “I do not know,” and 0 points were considered for the incorrect answer. Answers to other questions were reported in abundance. To qualitatively assess the knowledge and performance of the studied population, the following classification was applied: a score above 70% was considered “high” (desired), a score between 50% and 70% was deemed “moderate” (average), and a score below 50% was classified as “low” (undesirable).

Statistical analysis

After data collection, data analysis was done using SPSS software version 22, Mann-Whitney U-test, and Spearman’s correlation. The significance level of the tests was considered less than 0.05.

Ethical consideration

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shahid Sadoughi University of Medical Sciences under the number IR.SSU.DENTISTRY.REC.1401.101. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, who were assured that anonymous data collection would be done.

Discussion

Considering the increase in life expectancy in the population, paying attention to the health of the elderly is particularly important.[17] This study investigated the knowledge and attitude of senior students and dentists about geriatric dentistry to find knowledge gaps and incorrect points of view about treating the elderly.

It may be unconsciously expected that dentists have more knowledge than final semester students, and there is no need to compare the two groups, but recently, a 4-hour gerontology theory course without any practical part was added to the second semester of the fifth year of the dental curriculum. Although the topics of this course are broad and not only related to dental care, it is considered a step forward. Dentists who have already graduated have not completed such a course unit.

In the present study, almost half of the students had an average level of knowledge, and no student had the optimal level. Less than 10% of the dentists in the current study had a desirable level of knowledge, and almost 75% had an average level. Most dentists’ knowledge level was rated as ordinary in the study by Tahani et al.,[3] which was carried out in Isfahan City. Nouri et al.[18] found that over one-fifth of the 150 dentists in their survey had excellent competence in Kerman. According to the study by Alaee et al.,[9] there was no ideal degree of awareness at all, and around 10% of the dentists taking part in the International Congress of Dentists were deemed to have an average level of awareness. These variations in the criteria of optimal awareness or the range of questions may contribute to these discrepancies in awareness levels.

In the present study, there was no significant difference in the average knowledge score of senior (final-year) dental students among male and female students, which is consistent with the study by Hatami et al.[19] Contrary to the present study, according to Rahardjo et al.’s study,[20] gender affected students’ awareness of geriatric dentistry; thus, female students were more aware than male students, which may be due to the greater social sensitivity of females.

In the present study, students’ age did not significantly affect their knowledge level, which aligns with the study of Rahardjo et al.[20]

In our study, the average knowledge score of dentists with more than 5 years of experience was significantly higher than that of dentists with less than 5 years of experience. Consistent with the present study, in Hall et al.’s study,[17] dentists with more experience showed higher awareness. These findings probably indicate that dental professionals with more experience gradually better understand the unique needs of older patients.

In contrast, Moreira et al.[21] reported that dentists with less experience had a higher knowledge of geriatric dentistry than their more experienced colleagues, possibly due to not updating the knowledge of colleagues with more work experience.

In our study, dentists engaged in treatment in public organizations did not show a higher level of knowledge in geriatric dentistry than those in private organizations. Contrary to the present study, in the study by Moreira et al.,[21] dentists in the public sector obtained a better knowledge score than in the private sector. Researchers stated that dentists in the public sector can participate in educational workshops and exchange information with their colleagues.[22]

In the present study, the attitude scores of 90.5% of students and 90.5% of dentists were evaluated at a good level. In the study by Nouri et al.,[18] the average attitude score of only 18% of dentists was favorably obtained.

Aldhuwayhi’s study showed that dental students in Saudi Arabia could not adopt a positive attitude toward providing primary health care despite the rapid growth of the older population.[1]

In the present study, 81% of dentists agreed with the attitude of older people, who are more appreciative of the dental services provided than young people. Like the present study, half of Nouri et al.’s[18] dentists agreed with this attitude.

In our study, there was no significant difference in the mean attitude score of senior dental students according to gender. However, unlike the present study, in Hatami et al.’s study,[19] female students had a more positive attitude than male students.

In our study, there was no significant difference in the average attitude score of general dentists according to age. Like the present study, Alaee et al.[9] reported that age did not significantly affect knowledge and attitude scores.

The present study showed no significant difference between female and male dentists’ attitude scores. Contrary to the present study, Bots-VantSpijker et al.[23] found that female dentists had a significantly better attitude than males, which was attributed to a higher level of sympathy in females.

In line with the present study, Nouri et al.[18] observed no statistically significant difference in the knowledge and attitude of dentists of different genders, which was attributed to the same education in geriatrics dentistry as in our study. This conflict in the results of studies can be related to social and cultural differences.[18]

In the present study, 88.9% of students and 61.9% of dentists thought taking history from older people was challenging. This attitude may be the result of their insufficient experience. In the study by Nouri et al.,[18] 64% of dentists agreed with this article.

In our study, approximately two-thirds of dentists preferred to treat younger patients over older patients if given a choice. Similar to the present study by Nouri et al.,[18] a similar proportion of dentists agreed with this attitude.

In the present study, one-fifth of dentists stated they can manage older people in emergencies. In a survey conducted by Nouri et al.,[18] one-sixth of dentists had this attitude.

The present study showed that one-sixth of the senior students and approximately two-thirds of general dentists stated that they did not receive enough training on geriatric dentistry in the general course.

In Alaee et al.’s study,[9] most dentists stated that they had no course units related to geriatric dentistry during general and specialized education.

The results of a study on dental schools in Iran determined that the courses and training time dedicated to geriatric dentistry were not enough. According to geriatric dental education, eight of the 11 main faculties established at least 6 years ago had a geriatric dental education module. Although they all mentioned a few hours of theoretical training, only one faculty had group seminars or lectures. Four faculties had clinical training in geriatric dentistry; however, none of the studied faculties had a separate department for geriatric dentistry, a specialized clinic for older people, or a dedicated referral system for referring older patients from hospitals to faculties.[15]

Considering the above, due to the global aging process, modification of the current dental curriculum is needed to train dentists with sufficient skills and knowledge to provide dental treatment to the growing number of older people.[24] Students should be encouraged to treat older people in dental clinics under the supervision of professors and in an interdisciplinary manner.[1] In other words, dentists should practice providing dental treatment to older people after receiving both theoretical and clinical training on various aspects of geriatric dentistry and patient care.[25] In addition, dental courses for geriatric dentistry should be held to address the needs of older people, especially disabled people.[19] Similarly, government bodies and dental schools should consider promoting the dental care of older people in society, implement the required policies, and cooperate in this way.[18]

It should be noted that graduating from dental school never means the end of learning; on the one hand, the study period in dental schools is minimal, and on the other hand, many developments are happening in dentistry. Therefore, continuous training courses should be planned for clinical dentists to keep themselves up-to-date in providing oral and dental health services for all groups, including older people.[24]

Unfortunately, because geriatric dentistry is not given enough importance and attention in the educational curriculum and dental schools, students may have the impression that they do not have a defined responsibility for diagnosing and treating these patients.[9]

This research’s findings, which align with Hatami et al.’s[19] study of students, showed a strong and favorable link between final-year dentistry students’ knowledge scores and attitude. In contrast to Tahani et al.’s study,[3] the forthcoming investigation results indicate no meaningful correlation between dentists’ knowledge scores and attitudes. While there is a possibility that dental students may acquire knowledge about geriatric dentistry in their academic courses, changing attitudes cannot be achieved only through knowledge acquisition.[26] Studies have shown that personal experiences and cultural and social values influence a positive attitude. In addition, while studying, the experience of working with professors who express empathy toward older people will affect the formation of students’ attitudes toward this segment of society.[26,27,28] In addition to these studies, dentists in contact with older people in their living environment had a more positive attitude toward this age group than dentists without such contact.[19,29,30] The cause of this may be related to increasing familiarity with the aging process and the physical characteristics of old age. The behavior and values of dentists can be influenced by the conditions of their family or friends, causing positive changes in their attitude.[19,29] In addition, emphasizing the positive aspects of treating older people in colleges leads to a more positive attitude toward older people. It may help improve dental students’ behavior and practice in this field.[21]

Strengths and limitations

This study is one of the first to look specifically at issues related to the elderly in dentistry, which could be its strength. This evaluation of the level of knowledge and attitude of students and dentists in geriatric dentistry may also provide some clues in assessing the recent gerontology curriculum changes, as mentioned before.

This research also had limitations. Mainly due to the use of a non-randomized sampling method in the group of students, the generalizability of the findings of this research is limited. One of the other limitations of this study was that dentists who had indicated their willingness to participate in such a study by giving informed consent were examined. As a result, there may be an unintended increase in the number of participants who give more respect or attention to older people.

Of course, suppose this study is considered the first step in evaluating the knowledge and attitude of current and future dentists in geriatric dentistry in general, especially after changing the curriculum and adding a gerontology course. In that case, its shortcomings will be less noticeable.

Conclusion

In the time section of this study, the level of knowledge among both senior dental students and dentists was undesirable. Although there was a positive and significant correlation between the knowledge score and attitude in senior dental students, this relationship was not found in general dentists. Based on the results, it might be better for the Yazd Dental School to prioritize issues related to the treatment of the elderly, especially teaching in a practical way, instead of theoretical classes. In addition, it seems that the educational effectiveness of the gerontology course needs to be further investigated in future studies.

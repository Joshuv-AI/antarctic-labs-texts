Well-posedness of multidimensional nonlocal conservation laws with nonlinear mobility and bounded force
Antonin Chodron de Courcel

Abstract
We establish local-in-time existence and uniqueness results for nonlocal conservation laws with a nonlinear mobility, in several space dimensions, under weak assumptions on the kernel, which is assumed to be bounded and of finite total variation. Contrary to the linear mobility case, solutions may develop shocks in finite time, even when the kernel is smooth. We construct entropy solutions via a vanishing viscosity method, and provide a rate of convergence for this approximation scheme.



1
Introduction

1.1
Nonlinear mobilities in nonlocal conservation laws

Nonlocal conservation laws have been the subject of many recent studies in the mathematical community. These encompass a wide variety of models and mathematical behaviors, so that the term “nonlocal conservation law” is actually not so precise in the end. In this article, we are interested in the Cauchy problem of such models when the
mobility
function is nonlinear.

We study multidimensional scalar conservation laws of the form

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u+\div(f(u)\,K\ast u)=0,&t>0,\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).\end{cases}

(1.1)

Above,
d\geq 1
,
f\in C^{1,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
and
K\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The field
K\ast u
will be refered to as the
force field
, whereas
f(u)
will be refered to as the
mobility
. This actually is an abuse of vocabulary, since fluxes are generally written as
\mathsf{j}=u\mu(u)\mathsf{F}
, where the mobility is the function
\mu(u)
and not
u\times\mu(u)
.

From the modelling point of view, nonlinear mobilities may represent an exclusion rule at the microscopic level:
f(u)=u(1-u)

[
20
]
. In this case, equations of the form (
1.1
) display phase separation phenomena as in the Cahn-Hilliard model. Other nonlinear mobilities may be relevant, such as power laws
f(u)=u^{m}
in the context of porous media equations
[
27
,
6
,
7
]
. To name a few more applications, these models appear in the context of sedimentation
[
4
]
, structured population dynamics
[
25
]
, and traffic flow regulation
[
21
]
.

From the mathematical point of view, the equation (
1.1
) has an hyperbolic flavor when
f
is nonlinear. For example, when
f(u)=u^{m}
and assuming that the profile
u
varies of an order
1
in a region of size
\varepsilon\ll 1
, then so will the effective velocity field
u^{m-1}\,K\ast u
. This allows high density regions to move faster than low density ones, and create a shock in finite time. Moreover, the expected stability and regularity properties of entropy solutions highly depend on the kernel
K
, and it is so far not clear in the literature which condition on
K
allows to derive, for instance,
BV
and stability estimates.

1.2
Related works

As already emphasized, the nonlinear mobility case strongly departs from the linear one. In the latter case, uniqueness of weak solutions holds as soon as the kernel is as singular as
K\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, without any entropy condition needed
[
13
,
16
]
.

When the mobility is nonlinear, the uniqueness of entropy solutions was proved in
[
4
]
, via a Krüzhkov-type argument based on
L^{1}
stability, assuming
K\in C^{2}
,
d=1
, and
f(u)=u(1-u)^{\alpha}
,
\alpha\geq 1
. More recent works also assume
d=1
and
K
relatively smooth (e.g.
K\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})

[
9
]
). Finally, the stability estimate is sometimes obtained in a formal way, using unjustified integration by parts. We clarify the assumptions needed for the stability argument to work, namely
K\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
d\geq 1
(no restriction on the dimension). To our knowledge, this is the first time such a result is obtained for these regularity assumptions.

Concerning the existence theory for (
1.1
) and related models, a key argument is to provide strong compactness in order to pass to the limit in the nonlinear mobility. When no BV bound can be propagated, such a task may be difficult. For this reason, one can rely on the kinetic formulation of conservation laws
[
24
]
or show that some quantities involving singular integrals are propagated
[
3
,
10
]
. Most of the time, though, BV bounds can be propagated, allowing for strong compactness. However, this property is sometimes thought to be tied with the one-dimensional situation. We clarify this point by showing that one can propagate BV bounds as soon as
K\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. This includes kernels as singular as Riesz flows, where
K=-\nabla{\mathsf{g}}
, and
{\mathsf{g}}(x)=1/(s|x|^{s})
, up to (but not including) the Coulomb potential (so that
s<d-2
).

A natural question when building solutions to (
1.1
) concerns the discrepancy between the approximation scheme used and the actual solution. This was first obtained by Kuznetsov
[
22
]
for classical conservation laws, building on the doubling of variables method from Krüzhkov. Later, the rate
O(\sqrt{\Delta x})
(in the discretization of any monotone scheme) obtained by Kuznetsov was shown to be sharp
[
26
]
for linear conservation laws in dimension one. Nevertheless, this rate can be improved when considering genuinely nonlinear local conservation laws, see e.g.
[
28
]
. More recently,
[
2
]
proved a
1/2
rate of convergence for a model that is similar to ours, considering finite volume approximations. Their result assumes
K\in C^{2}
, and they must use rather involved splitting arguments if they wish to extend their result to the multidimensional case. On the opposite, we derive a
1/2
rate of convergence for viscous approximations, in a manner that is transparent on the dimension, and for kernels
K\in L^{\infty}\cap BV
. To our knowledge, this is the first time such a result is obtained.

We finally signal to the interested reader that a lot of mathematical efforts have recently been devoted to the passage from nonlocal to local models, for which we refer to
[
12
,
15
]
.

1.3
Main results

We summarize our main contributions as follows.

First, we derive BV bounds under the mere assumption
K\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. We then clarify the assumptions needed to derive an
L^{1}
stability estimate and obtain the uniqueness of entropy solutions: we prove this result in any dimension, and for
K\in L^{\infty}\cap BV
. This includes many types of kernels, in particular anisotropic and non-monotone ones. Such a result is formally obtained as follows: considering two solutions
u,v
to (
1.1
),

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}|u-v|

\displaystyle=\div F(u,v)-\sgn(u-v)\nabla f(v)\cdot K\ast(u-v)

\displaystyle-\sgn(u-v)f(v)\div K\ast(u-v),

for some
F(u,v)\in L^{1}
. Integrating in space gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u-v\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\nabla f(v)\|_{L^{1}}\|K\ast(u-v)\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f(v)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\div K\ast(u-v)\|_{L^{1}}.

If
K\in L^{\infty}
and
|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})<+\infty
, we can close this by a Grönwall inequality, provided
\nabla f(v)\in L^{1}
. What is expected to hold is only
f(v)\in BV
, but if a uniform bound holds on
\|\nabla f(v_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}}
at the level of the approximation scheme
v_{\varepsilon}
, we should be able to close this loop.

We see immediately that the term
\sgn(u-v)\nabla f(v)\cdot K\ast(u-v)
is not defined when
v
is only BV. Instead of giving a meaning to this quantity, we bound it directly at the level of the doubling of variable, using two inequalities
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
.

Finally, we prove an
L^{1}
rate of convergence of viscous approximations to (
1.1
), based on a combination of the classical argument from
[
22
]
and our inequalities
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
.

Assumptions 1.1
.

Otherwise stated, we assume that
d\geq 1
,
K\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
, and
f\in C^{1,1}_{loc}({\mathbb{R}})
for which there exists some
\alpha>0
and
C>0
such that

\displaystyle|f(\xi)|\leq C\big(1+|\xi|^{\alpha}\big).

Definition 1.2

(Entropy solutions)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
T>0
. We say that
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T);L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is an entropy solution to (
1.1
) if, for all
\eta\in C^{2}({\mathbb{R}})
convex, we have in the sense of distributions on
(0,T)

{\partial}_{t}\eta(u)\leq-\div(q(u)K\ast u)-(\eta^{\prime}(u)f(u)-q(u))\div K\ast u,

(1.2)

where
q^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}f^{\prime}
.
We say that
u
is a
global
solution if one can take
T=+\infty
above, and that
u
is
maximal
if

\limsup_{t\to T^{-}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}=+\infty.

(1.3)

Remark 1.3
.

Since we deal with general interaction kernels (including attractive ones) and mobilities, we cannot rule out the possibility of a finite time blow-up, for which we provide an estimation.

Definition 1.4

(Vanishing viscosity solutions)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
T>0
. We say that
u\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T);L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
is a vanishing viscosity solution to (
1.1
) if there exists a sequence of positive reals
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k\geq 0}
and a solution
u_{k}\in C^{1,1}((0,T)\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d})\cap C([0,T),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
to

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u_{k}+\div(f(u_{k})\nabla{\mathsf{g}}\ast u_{k})=\varepsilon_{k}\Delta u_{k},\\
u_{k}|_{t=0}=u_{0},\end{cases}

(1.4)

such that
(u_{k})_{k}
converges to
u
in
L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
locally uniformly in time on
[0,T)
, which we denote

\displaystyle u_{k}\xrightarrow[k\to\infty]{}u\quad\text{in }C_{loc}([0,T),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
There exists a unique maximal entropy solution to (
1.1
), in the sense of
1.2
.

Corollary 1.6
.

As a consequence, vanishing viscosity solutions are also unique, and they coincide with the notion of entropy solutions.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
Let
u
be the unique maximal entropy solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,T_{max})
, and
u_{k}
a viscous approximation. Then, for all
T<T_{max}
, there exists a constant
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
such that

\sup_{(0,T)}\|u-u_{k}\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}\sqrt{\varepsilon_{k}}.

(1.5)

Remark 1.8
.

The
BV
regularity asked in
1.2
can be derived in the broader setting
K\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(unbounded). This includes kernels of the form
K=-\nabla{\mathsf{g}}
, with
{\mathsf{g}}
of Riesz-type
{\mathsf{g}}(x)\sim_{|x|\to 0}1/(s|x|^{s})
, as far as
s<d-2
. The threshold
s=d-2
corresponds to the Coulomb potential, which includes the hyperbolic Keller-Segel model
[
24
]
.

Remark 1.9
.

The
L^{1}
stability result which implies the uniqueness of entropy solutions strongly relies on the assumption
K\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Remark 1.10
.

We do not think that the rate (
1.5
) is sharp in general.

2
Existence of entropy solutions

In this section, we construct entropy solutions to (
1.1
), via a vanishing viscosity method. We thus consider

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u_{\varepsilon}+\div(f(u_{\varepsilon})\,K\ast u_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon\Delta u_{\varepsilon},\\
u_{\varepsilon}|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).\end{cases}

(2.1)

In the first subsection, we show that this approximate problem is locally well-posed. We chose to include this in the paper because we also prove a blowup criterion on the
L^{\infty}
norm, together with an estimation of the blowup time. In the second subsection, we derive estimates which are uniform in the viscosity parameter. These estimates include
L^{\infty}\cap BV
norms, and a modulus of continuity in time on solutions. We conclude this section by constructing an entropy solution and examining the time-continuity of such solutions.

2.1
Local well-posedness for the viscous approximation

We prove the following result.

Proposition 2.1

(Local well-posedness of (
2.1
))
.

Let
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).
There exists a time
T_{max}>0
and a unique solution
u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T_{max}),L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
to (
2.1
) on
[0,T_{max})
starting at
u_{0}
. This solution moreover satisfies the following blow-up criterion: either
T_{max}=+\infty
, or

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T_{max}^{-}}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}}=+\infty.

Remark 2.2
.

The maximal time of existence
T_{max}
established in this proposition a priori depends on
\varepsilon
. We will actually show in the next proposition that this is not the case, thanks to the blow-up criterion and estimates that hold uniformly in the viscosity parameter.

Remark 2.3
.

It is outside the scope of this article to go through the regularity of the viscous solution, especially because this is a consequence of classical results from regularity theory. Indeed, since the flux is locally Lipschitz continuous, the solution
u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
must be
C^{1,1}
for all
t>0
. This implies in particular that
\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(t,\cdot)
is well-defined in
L^{\infty}
. Note that it also holds
\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\in L^{\infty}_{loc}([0,T_{max}),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})).

Proof.

Let
T>0
. Consider the Banach space
X:=L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}\cap L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
equipped with its natural norm, and the map
F:X\to X
defined by

F(v)(t,x):=e^{\varepsilon t\Delta}u_{0}-\int_{0}^{t}e^{\varepsilon(t-s)\Delta}\div(f(v)\,K\ast v)(s,x)\,ds.

Using heat kernel estimates, we have for all
1\leq p\leq\infty

\displaystyle\|F(v)(t)\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+C\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(v)\,K\ast v\|_{L^{p}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+C\sqrt{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\esssup_{[0,T]}\|f(v)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\|_{L^{1}}\esssup_{[0,T]}\|v\|_{L^{p}}.

This implies that
F(v)\in X
, hence
F
is well-defined as a mapping from
X
to itself. Furthermore, fixing some
R>0
such that
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}\leq R/2
and considering
v\in B_{R}
, one obtains

\displaystyle\|F(v)\|_{L^{p}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+C\sqrt{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}\esssup_{-R,R}|f|\|K\|_{L^{1}}R

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{p}}+C\sqrt{\frac{t}{\varepsilon}}(1+R^{\alpha})\|K\|_{L^{1}}R,

where we have used the control on
f
at infinity. Therefore,

\displaystyle\|F(v)\|_{X}\leq\frac{R}{2}+C\sqrt{\frac{T}{\varepsilon}}\|K\|_{L^{1}}(1+R^{\alpha})R.

Therefore, taking

T\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{C^{2}\|K\|_{L^{1}}^{2}(1+R^{\alpha})^{2}},

we have
F(v)\in B_{R}
. Let
v,w\in B_{R}
. We have for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,

\displaystyle\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{L^{p}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{\varepsilon(t-s)\Delta}\div((f(v)-f(w))\,K\ast v)\|_{L^{p}}\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\|e^{\varepsilon(t-s)\Delta}\div(f(w)\,K\ast(v-w)\|_{L^{p}}\,ds.

Using again heat kernel estimates,

\displaystyle\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{L^{p}}(t)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(v)-f(w)\|_{L^{p}}\|K\ast v\|_{L^{\infty}}\,ds

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|f(w)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|K\ast(v-w)\|_{L^{p}}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\|K\|_{L^{1}}\|v\|_{X}\esssup_{[0,T]}(|f^{\prime}(u)|+|f^{\prime}(v)|)\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u-w\|_{L^{p}}(s)\,ds

\displaystyle+C\esssup_{[0,T]}(|f(u)|)\|K\|_{L^{1}}\int_{0}^{t}(\varepsilon(t-s))^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|u-w\|_{L^{p}}(s)\,ds.

Taking
p=1,\infty
gives

\displaystyle\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{X}\leq C\|K\|_{L^{1}}\big(R\esssup_{I_{R}}|f^{\prime}|+\esssup_{I_{R}}|f|\big)\sqrt{\frac{T}{\varepsilon}}\|v-w\|_{X},

where we have defined
I_{R}:=[-2R,2R]
. Therefore, taking

T\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{4C^{2}\|K\|_{L^{1}}^{2}\big(R\esssup_{I_{R}}|f^{\prime}|+\esssup_{I_{R}}|f|\big)^{2}},

(2.2)

we obtain

\displaystyle\|F(v)-F(w)\|_{X}\leq\frac{1}{2}\|v-w\|_{X}.

Therefore,
F
is a contraction from
B_{R}
to itself. This implies in particular that there exists a fixed point
u\in B_{R}
, hence a solution to (
2.1
) on
[0,T_{max}]
, for some
T_{max}>0
a priori depending on
\varepsilon
. Our computations also provide the following stability estimate: for any two solutions
u,v\in B_{R}
,

\displaystyle\|u-v\|_{X}\leq 2\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}.

Finally, we also obtain the following blow-up criterion: considering a solution
u\in B_{R}
to (
2.1
), either
T_{max}=+\infty
, or

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\to T_{max}}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}}=+\infty.

∎

2.2
Uniform estimates and existence of entropy solutions

We prove several estimates on
u_{\varepsilon}
which do not depend on
\varepsilon
. In particular, we obtain an estimate for the
L^{\infty}
norm, which can be combined with the blow-up criterion established before to show that the maximal time of existence
T_{max}
does not depend on
\varepsilon
.

We also obtain BV estimates for fairly general kernels, including unbounded ones. More precisely, we only need
K\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. This includes any interaction as singular as Riesz flows, where
K=-\nabla{\mathsf{g}}
and
{\mathsf{g}}(x):=1/(s|x|^{s})
, as long as
s<d-2
(which corresponds to the Coulomb kernel).

Proposition 2.4

(Uniform estimates)
.

Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the unique solution to (
2.1
), defined on some
[0,T_{max})
, starting from
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. The following holds:

•

(conservation of mass) for all
t\in[0,T_{max})
,

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dx=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}u_{0}(x)\,dx,

•

(decrease of the
L^{1}
norm) for all
t\in[0,T_{max})
,

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}},

•

(local
L^{\infty}
bound) there exists a universal constant
C>0
and a time
T>0
satisfying

T\sim\frac{1}{C|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})(1+\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha})},

such that

\forall t\in[0,T),\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}},

(2.3)

•

(propagation of the total variation)

\forall t\in[0,T),\quad\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}(t)|\,dx\leq e^{A(t)}TV(u_{0}),

(2.4)

where
A:[0,T)\to{\mathbb{R}}
is bounded,

•

(continuity in time) for all
T<T_{max}
, there is a constant
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
such that for all
t
and
h
satisfying
t+h,t\in[0,T]
,

\|u_{\varepsilon}(t+h)-u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}\sqrt{h}.

(2.5)

Remark 2.5
.

We are considering rather general interactions, in particular attractive ones. This is why the bounds we obtain here cannot be propagated for all positive times. Nevertheless, the proof naturally applies to repulsive kernels, even singular ones, for which the bounds (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) are propagated for all times. More precisely, the proof extends to kernels
K=-\nabla{\mathsf{g}}
that are of Riesz type
{\mathsf{g}}(x)\sim_{|x|\to 0}1/(s|x|^{s})
, as long as
s<d-2
. Note that the threshold
s=d-2
corresponds to the Coulomb kernel, which includes the hyperbolic Keller-Segel model
[
24
]
.

Remark 2.6
.

The continuity estimate (
2.5
) will not be sharp as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Indeed, such a
1/2
-Hölder continuity estimate is typical of the regularisation by noise, and we expect a better (Lipschitz) continuity in time in the inviscid limit: this will be obtained in a second time, once the entropy solution has been constructed (see
2.7
).

Proof.

Mass conservation and decrease of the
L^{1}
norm.

The conservation of mass is straightforward. For the
L^{1}
norm, we can for example write

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx

\displaystyle=\lim_{\delta\to 0}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sqrt{\delta^{2}+u_{\varepsilon}^{2}}\,dx,

differentiate in time, and integrate by parts, to obtain that
\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}
for all
t
on the lifespan of
u_{\varepsilon}
.

Local
L^{\infty}
bound.

We now consider
p>1
and compute

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}p\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}\big(-\div(f(u_{\varepsilon})\,K\ast u_{\varepsilon})+\varepsilon\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\big)\,dx

\displaystyle=-p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\,dx-p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\,dx

\displaystyle+\varepsilon p\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Notice that

\displaystyle p\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}-p(p-1)|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-2}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},

so that the viscous term can be discarded due to its sign. We then introduce
F^{\prime}(u):=p\sgn(u)|u|^{p-1}f^{\prime}(u)
, so that integrating by parts the first term gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(F(u_{\varepsilon})-p\sgn(u_{\varepsilon})|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}f(u_{\varepsilon})\big)\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Integrating by parts, one obtains for
F(0)=0
,

\displaystyle\forall u>0,\quad F(u)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{u}F^{\prime}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=p|u|^{p-1}f(u)-\int_{0}^{u}p(p-1)|\xi|^{p-2}f(\xi)\,d\xi.

On the opposite,

\displaystyle\forall u<0,\quad F(u)

\displaystyle=-p\int_{0}^{u}|\xi|^{p-1}f^{\prime}(\xi)\,d\xi

\displaystyle=-p|u|^{p-1}f(u)-\int_{0}^{u}p(p-1)|\xi|^{p-2}f(\xi)\,d\xi.

Overall,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq-p(p-1)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\int_{0}^{u_{\varepsilon}}|\xi|^{p-2}f(\xi)\,d\xi\,\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Thus, one obtains thanks to the control of
f
at infinity

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Cp(p-1)|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\bigg(\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1+\alpha}}{p-1+\alpha}+\frac{|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p-1}}{p-1}\bigg)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq Cp|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\frac{p-1}{p-1+\alpha}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}+1\bigg)\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p-1}}^{p-1}.

Interpolating between the (nonincreasing)
L^{1}
norm and
L^{p}
gives

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq Cp|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\frac{p-1}{p-1+\alpha}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}+1\bigg)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{\frac{p(p-2)}{p-1}}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{p}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{1-p}\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}

\displaystyle\leq C|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\bigg(\frac{p-1}{p-1+\alpha}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}+1\bigg)\|u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{1}{p-1}}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{p}}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Using that
\|u_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{p}}\to\|u_{\varepsilon}(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}
as
p\to\infty
for all
0\leq s\leq t
, one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}^{\alpha}+1\big).

This gives the local-in-time bound for the
L^{\infty}
norm.

Total variation bound.

For the total variation norm, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\div(f(u_{\varepsilon})\,K\ast u_{\varepsilon})\,dx+\varepsilon\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

For the viscous term, we again have

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\Delta u_{\varepsilon}\leq\Delta|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|.

This can thus be discarded, and one obtains

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\div(f(u_{\varepsilon})\,K\ast u_{\varepsilon})\,dx

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\big(\nabla f(u_{\varepsilon})\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\big)\,dx.

We now develop the following term:

\displaystyle\nabla\big(\nabla f(u_{\varepsilon})\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=\nabla\big(f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla^{2}u_{\varepsilon}\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\nabla K\ast u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+\nabla f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast\nabla u_{\varepsilon}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\cdot\nabla\big(\nabla f(u_{\varepsilon})\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\big)

\displaystyle=f^{\prime\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\nabla|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\cdot K\ast u_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle+f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}:\nabla K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\div K\ast u_{\varepsilon}+f(u_{\varepsilon})\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\div K\ast\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}.

Integrating by parts, we record several cancellations and obtain in the end

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\leq\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(u_{\varepsilon})\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\cdot\div K\ast\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}\,dx\\
+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\frac{\nabla u_{\varepsilon}\otimes\nabla u_{\varepsilon}}{|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|}:\nabla K\ast u_{\varepsilon}\,dx.

Using that
\nabla K
is a bounded operator from
L^{\infty}\to L^{\infty}
, and that
f,f^{\prime}
are locally bounded functions, one obtains

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx\leq\big(\|f(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}}|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})+\|f^{\prime}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}}|\nabla K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}}\big)\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|\,dx.

We now use the
L^{\infty}
bound to obtain the result by Grönwall’s lemma.

Time continuity estimate.
We denote the flux as

\displaystyle F_{\varepsilon}(t,x):=f(u_{\varepsilon})\,K\ast u_{\varepsilon}-\varepsilon\nabla u_{\varepsilon}.

From what we have obtained so far,
\|F_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))}\leq C
is uniformly bounded in
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, introducing a Lipschitz
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)(u_{\varepsilon}(h,x)-u_{0}(x))\,dx

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{h}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(x)\div F_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{h}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla\varphi(x)\cdot F_{\varepsilon}(t,x)\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{\infty}}h.

We obtain
\|u_{\varepsilon}(h)-u_{0}\|_{W^{-1,1}}\leq Ch
. We then interpolate between
W^{-1,1}
and BV to obtain

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}(h)-u_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|u_{\varepsilon}(h)-u_{0}\|_{W^{-1,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}\|u_{\varepsilon}(h)-u_{0}\|_{W^{1,1}}^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\sqrt{h},

using the uniform bound on the total variation.

∎

We can now construct entropy solutions to (
1.1
). Consider a sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
and the unique solution
u_{k}
to

\displaystyle\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u_{k}+\div(f(u_{k})\,K\ast u_{k})=\varepsilon_{k}\Delta u_{k},\\
u_{k}|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}).\end{cases}

Denote
T_{max}>0
its maximal time of existence. For any
T<T_{max}
, we have the uniform bound
\sup_{t\in(0,T)}\|u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{BV}\leq C_{T}
, and it is not difficult to prove that
{\partial}_{t}u_{\varepsilon}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{\infty}([0,T],W^{-1,1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. From the Aubin-Lions lemma, we then conclude that there exists some
u\in C([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
such that one can extract a subsequence – still denoted
(u_{k})_{k}
– for which
u_{k}\to u
in
C([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. This
u
inherits the mass conservation, decrease of the
L^{1}
norm, local
L^{\infty}
and BV bounds, and the modulus of continuity in time from
u_{k}
. Finally, it satisfies the entropy condition. Indeed, starting from the viscous approximation, we have for all
\eta\in C^{2}
and convex,

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\eta(u_{k})=\eta^{\prime}(u_{k})\div(f(u_{k})\,K\ast u_{k})+\varepsilon_{k}\eta^{\prime}(u_{k})\Delta u_{k}.

Define
q^{\prime}=\eta^{\prime}f^{\prime}
, so that by also noticing
\Delta\eta(u_{k})=\eta^{\prime\prime}(u_{k})|\nabla u_{k}|^{2}+\eta^{\prime}(u_{k})\Delta u_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}\eta(u_{k})\leq\div(q(u_{k})\,K\ast u_{k})+(\eta^{\prime}(u_{k})f(u_{k})-q(u_{k}))\div K\ast u_{k}+\varepsilon_{k}\Delta\eta(u_{k}).

Passing to the limit
k\to\infty
can be done since
f
is locally Lipschitz, so that
f(u_{k})\to f(u)
in
C([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. We have therefore constructed an entropy solution to (
1.1
) in the sense of
1.2
.

2.3
Time continuity

We now return to the question of the time-continuity of entropy solutions.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
u
be an entropy solution to (
1.1
). For all
T>0
in the lifespan of
u
, there is a constant
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
such that

\forall(t,t+h)\in[0,T],\quad\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}h.

(2.6)

Proof.

The equation reads

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}u+\div F(u)=0,

where
F(u)\in L^{\infty}([0,T],L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, with

|\div F(u)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\leq\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}|Du|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|K\|_{L^{1}}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}|\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|u\|_{L^{1}}=:C_{T},

and
K:=[-\|u\|_{L^{\infty}},\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}].
This implies for all
(t+h,t)\in[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\|u(t+h)-u(t)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{h}|{\partial}_{t}u(\tau)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\,d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C_{T}h.

∎

3
Uniqueness of entropy solutions

In this section, we prove by an
L^{1}
stability result à la Krüzhkov that entropy solutions are unique. We also show that they coincide with vanishing viscosity solutions, and give a rate of convergence for this approximation scheme, à la Kuznetsov. As emphasized in the introduction, and contrary to local conservation laws, we must be careful in the manipulation of the term

\displaystyle\sgn(u-v)\nabla f(v)\cdot K\ast(u-v),

which does not make sense. For this reason, we will need the following inequalities.

3.1
Three lemmas

In this subsection, we prove two novel ingredients for the proof of the
L^{1}
stability and rate of convergence, which are
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
. We also record
3.3
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Consider
V\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d},{\mathbb{R}}^{d})
such that
\div V\in L^{1}
,
a,b\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
f
locally Lipschitz, and two Lipschitz and compactly supported maps
\varphi,\psi:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}
. We have

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(x+y)\varphi(x-y)V(x)\big]\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))\,dxdy\\
\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}|Db|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([-2\|b\|_{L^{\infty}},2\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}])}.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
d\geq 1
. Consider
V:{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
globally Lipschitz,
a,b\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
,
f
locally Lipschitz, and
\psi,\varphi\in C^{0,1}_{c}
. There exists some
R>0
depending on
\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\big(V(x)-V(y)\big)\big]\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))\,dxdy\\
\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([-2\|b\|_{L^{\infty}},2\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}])}|Db|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|\nabla V\|_{L^{\infty}}M_{1}(\varphi),

where

\displaystyle M_{1}(\varphi):=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|x\,\varphi(x)|\,dx.

Proof of
lemma

3.1
.

We first assume
b\in C_{c}^{1}
. As before, the map

\displaystyle y\mapsto\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))

is a.e. differentiable, for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and its derivative is

\displaystyle y\mapsto-\sgn(a(x)-b(y))f^{\prime}\circ b(y)\nabla b(y).

Integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)V(x)\big]\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)V(x)\cdot\nabla b(y)\sgn(a(x)-b(y))f^{\prime}(b(y))\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f^{\prime}\circ b\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}|\nabla b(y)||\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|\,dxdy.

Changing the variable in the last integral gives the bound

\displaystyle\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|V\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f^{\prime}\circ b\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla b\|_{L^{1}}.

Now consider that
b\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Therefore, there exists a sequence of smooth and compactly supported functions
(b_{k})_{k}
such that
b_{k}\to b
in
L^{1}
,
\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\sup_{k}\|b_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 2\|b\|_{L^{\infty}}=:M
, and

\displaystyle|Db|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})=\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\nabla b_{k}\|_{L^{1}}.

Since
f\in C^{0,1}_{loc}
, we have that

\displaystyle\|f^{\prime}\circ b_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{[-\|b_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}},\|b_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}}]}|f^{\prime}|

\displaystyle\leq\esssup_{[-M,M]}|f^{\prime}|<+\infty.

Concerning the left-hand side, the integrand is bounded – up to taking
M
bigger – by

\displaystyle 2|\div_{y}\big[\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\big(V(x)-V(y)\big)\big]|\sup_{[-M,M]}|f|,

which is integrable, and independent of
k
. By dominated convergence, one obtains the result.
∎

Proof of
lemma

3.2
.

We first assume that
b\in C^{1}_{c}
. Therefore, the map

\displaystyle y\mapsto\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))

is a.e. differentiable, for a.e.
x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d}
, and its derivative is

\displaystyle y\mapsto-\sgn(a(x)-b(y))f^{\prime}\circ b(y)\nabla b(y).

Integrating by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)\big(V(x)-V(y)\big)\big]\sgn(a(x)-b(y))(f\circ a(x)-f\circ b(y))\,dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi(x+y)\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)(V(x)-V(y))\cdot\nabla b(y)\sgn(a(x)-b(y))f^{\prime}(b(y))\,dxdy

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|f^{\prime}\circ b\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}dy\,|\nabla b(y)|\,\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}dx\,|V(x)-V(y)||\varphi_{\varepsilon}(x-y)|.

We then use the Lipschitz bound on
V
to conclude, after a change of variable in the inner integral. We obtain the bound

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi\|_{\infty}\|f^{\prime}\circ b\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla b\|_{L^{1}}\|\nabla V\|_{L^{\infty}}M_{1}(\varphi).

We treat the case
b\in L^{\infty}\cap BV
as for the proof of
lemma

3.1
.
∎

We finally record the following claim, whose proof is left to the reader.

Claim 3.3
.

Let
d\geq 1
,
K\in L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
and
u\in L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Then, the field
V:=K\ast u
is bounded and uniformly continuous. If moreover

3.2
L^{1}
stability

Proposition 3.4

(
L^{1}
stability)
.

Let
u,v
be two entropy solutions to (
1.1
) on
[0,T_{1})
and
[0,T_{2})
, respectively, with initial datum
u_{0},v_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
.

Then, for all
T\in(0,\min(T_{1},T_{2}))
, there exists
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|v_{0}\|_{BV\cap L^{\infty}}
such that

\esssup_{(0,T)}\|u-v\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}.

(3.1)

The proof of this stability result relies on the combination of the standard doubling of variables argument and our functional inequalities
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
, which prevent us from giving sense to the ill-defined quantity

\sgn(u-v)\nabla f(v)\cdot K\ast(u-v)

arising in formal computations.

Proof.

Let
u,v
as in the statement. Given any
\eta\in C^{2}
convex, one has in the sense of distributions:

{\partial}_{t}\eta(u)\leq-\div(q(u)K\ast u)+(q(u)-f(u)\eta^{\prime}(u))\div K\ast u,

where
q^{\prime}(\xi):=f^{\prime}(\xi)\eta^{\prime}(\xi)
. In particular, one can justify that for any
k\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

{\partial}_{t}|u-k|\leq-\div(\sgn(u-k)(f(u)-f(k))K\ast u)-f(k)\sgn(u-k)\div K\ast u.

Therefore,

\displaystyle{\partial}_{t}|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq-\div_{x}(\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))(f(u(t,x))-f(v(s,y)))K\ast u)(t,x)

\displaystyle-f(v(s,y))\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\div K\ast u(t,x),

\displaystyle{\partial}_{s}|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq-\div_{y}(\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))(f(u(t,x))-f(v(s,y)))K\ast v)(s,y)

\displaystyle-f(u(t,x))\sgn(v(s,y)-u(t,x))\div K\ast v(s,y).

Integrating with respect to a smooth nonnegative and compactly supported test function
\varphi\equiv\varphi(t,s,x,y)
and summing these lines gives

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(0,s,x,y)|u_{0}(x)-v(s,y)|\,dsdxdy-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t,0,x,y)|u(t,x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}({\partial}_{t}+{\partial}_{s})\varphi(t,s,x,y)|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot K\ast u(t,x)+\nabla_{y}\varphi\cdot K\ast v(s,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))(f(u(t,x))-f(v(s,y)))\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t,s,x,y)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))(f(v(s,y))\div K\ast u(t,x)-f(u(t,x))\div K\ast v(s,y))\,dtdsdxdy.

We then take

\varphi(t,s,x,y):=\psi\big(\frac{t+s}{2},\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\frac{1}{\delta\varepsilon^{d}}\varphi_{1}\big(\frac{t-s}{\delta}\big)\varphi_{2}\big(\frac{x-y}{\varepsilon}\big),

for nonnegative, smooth, and compactly supported
\psi,\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
such that
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are of mass
1
. We denote
\varphi_{1}^{\delta}:=\delta^{-1}\varphi_{1}(\cdot/\delta)
and
\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{-d}\varphi_{2}(\cdot/\varepsilon)
,
so that

{\partial}_{t}\varphi+{\partial}_{s}\varphi=\varphi_{1}^{\delta}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}{\partial}_{t}\psi.

In particular, the only term involving a time derivative can be rewritten as

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\partial}_{t}\psi\big(\frac{t+s}{2},\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(t-s)|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|\,dtdsdxdy.

Sending
\delta\to 0
appealing to classical convergence results (such as dominated convergence, using that each integral is actually localised on a compact set), one obtains

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\big(0,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)|u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\partial}_{t}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)|u(t,x)-v(t,y)|\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\big(\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,t,x,y)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)+\nabla_{y}\varphi(t,t,x,y)\cdot K\ast v(t,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y))\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{2}(x-y)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(v(t,y))\div K\ast u(t,x)-f(u(t,x))\div K\ast v(t,y)\big]\,dtdxdy.

Notice that

\nabla_{x}\varphi+\nabla_{y}\varphi=\varphi_{1}^{\delta}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}\nabla_{x}\psi,

so that the right-hand side above can be written as

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)\nabla_{x}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y))\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{y}(\psi\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon})(t,t,x,y)\cdot\big(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(t,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y))\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{2}(x-y)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(v(t,y))\div K\ast u(t,x)-f(u(t,x))\div K\ast v(t,y)\big]\,dtdxdy.

(3.2)

Before we can identify each of the integrals above, let us develop the last term as follows:

f(v(t,y))\div K\ast u(t,x)-f(u(t,x))\div K\ast v(t,y)\\
=f(v(t,y))\big(\div K\ast u(t,x)-\div K\ast v(t,y)\big)-(f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y)))\div K\ast v(t,y).

Thus, (
3.2
) writes
I+II+III
, where

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle:=\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)\nabla_{x}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y))\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle II

\displaystyle:=-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(t,\frac{x+y}{2})\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(t,y))\big]

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,y))\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle:=-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\big(t,\frac{x+y}{2}\big)\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{2}(x-y)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))f(v(t,y))\big[\div K\ast u(t,x)-\div K\ast v(t,y)\big]\,dtdxdy.

(3.3)

We can now identify these terms. Indeed, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we can again appeal to classical convergence theorems in order to obtain

\displaystyle I

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)\sgn(u-v)(t,x)\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,x))\big]\,dtdx

\displaystyle\quad=-\bigg\langle\psi,\div_{x}\big[\sgn(u-v)(f(u)-f(v))K\ast u\big]\bigg\rangle

\displaystyle III

\displaystyle\xrightarrow[\varepsilon\to 0]{}\bigg\langle\psi,\sgn(u-v)f(v)\div K\ast(u-v)\bigg\rangle.

The difficulty lies in estimating
II
, for which we cannot appeal to standard convergence theorems since
v(t,\cdot)\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Using

\displaystyle K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(t,y)=K\ast(u-v)(t,x)+K\ast v(t,x)-K\ast v(t,y),

we decompose
II
into
II_{a}+II_{b}
, where

\displaystyle II_{a}

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(t,\frac{x+y}{2})\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)K\ast(u-v)(t,x)\big]

\displaystyle\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(t,y)\big]\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle II_{b}

\displaystyle=-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\div_{y}\big[\psi(t,\frac{x+y}{2})\varphi_{2}^{\varepsilon}(x-y)(K\ast v(t,x)-K\ast v(t,y))\big]

\displaystyle\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(t,y))\big[f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(t,y)\big]\,dtdxdy.

We note that
K\ast u(t)
is globally Lipschitz continuous, for a.e.
t>0
, since
u(t,\cdot)\in BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
(see
3.3
). We thus use
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
to obtain, denoting
M:=2\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{tx}}

\displaystyle II_{a}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(t)\|_{\infty}|Dv(t)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|K\ast(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([-M,M])},

\displaystyle II_{b}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{T}\|\psi(t)\|_{\infty}|Dv(t)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([-M,M])}\|\nabla K\ast v(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}M_{1}(\varphi^{\varepsilon}_{2}).

Since
K\ast v
is Lipschitz continuous, sending
\varepsilon\to 0
yields
II_{b}\to 0
. We therefore obtain

\displaystyle-\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi(0,x)|u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(x)|\,dx

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\partial}_{t}\psi(t,x)|u(t,x)-v(t,x)|\,dxdt

\displaystyle\leq-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\psi(t,x)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)\sgn(u-v)(t,x)\big[f(u(t,x))-f(v(t,x))\big]\,dtdx

\displaystyle+\|\psi\|_{\infty}\esssup_{t\in(0,T)}|Dv(t)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\|f^{\prime}\|_{L^{\infty}([-M,M])}\int_{0}^{T}\|K\ast(u-v)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\psi\sgn(u-v)f(v)\div K\ast(u-v)\,dtdx.

We now finish the proof by taking
\psi\to 1
, which can be done in a straightforward manner since
K\ast u\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. Finally, using the crucial bound on the force
K\in L^{\infty}
, we overall obtain:

\displaystyle\|u(T)-v(T)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+\esssup_{(0,T)}|Dv|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\esssup_{[-M,M]}|f^{\prime}|\|K\|_{L^{\infty}}\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{1}}\,dt

\displaystyle+\esssup_{[-M,M]}|f||\div K|({\mathbb{R}}^{d})\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{1}}\,dt.

We conclude with the bounds of
2.4
and by applying Grönwall’s lemma.
∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Vanishing viscosity solutions and entropy solutions are the same.

Proof.

Consider a vanishing viscosity solution
u
, and denote
(u_{k})_{k}
a viscous approximation, with viscosity sequence
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
. We then proceed as in the contruction of the entropy solution, which shows that
u
satisfies the entropy condition.

Conversely, suppose that
u
is an entropy solution. Now, consider some
(\varepsilon_{k})_{k}
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
. There is a unique solution
u_{k}
to (
2.1
), starting from
u_{0}
. As in the proof of existence, we can extract a subsequence and construct a vanishing viscosity solution
\tilde{u}
from this subsequence, which satisfies the entropy condition. By the uniqueness theorem, we have
\tilde{u}=u
.
∎

3.3
L^{1}
rate of convergence

A natural question concerns the discrepancy between the viscous approximation and the entropy solution.

Proposition 3.6

(Rate of convergence for the viscous approximation)
.

Let
u
be the unique entropy solution to (
1.1
), and denote
u_{\varepsilon}
the unique solution to (
2.1
). For all
T
in the lifespan of
u
, there exists
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
such that

\forall t\in[0,T],\quad\|u(t)-u_{\varepsilon}(t)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}\sqrt{\varepsilon}.

(3.4)

The proof of this rate of convergence combines the classical argument of Kuznetsov
[
22
]
and our inequalities
lemma

3.1
and
lemma

3.2
. Given
\varphi\equiv\varphi(t,x,s,y)
nonnegative, smooth and compactly supported, an entropy solution
u
to (
1.1
) defined on
[0,T]
, and some
v\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
, we define

\Delta(\varphi):=-\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,T,y)|u(t,x)-v(T,y)|\,dtdxdy\\
+\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,0,y)|u(t,x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dtdxdy+\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}{\partial}_{s}\varphi(t,x,s,y)|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|\,dtdsdxdy\\
+\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\nabla_{y}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\cdot K\ast v(s,y)\sgn(v(s,y)-u(t,x))(f\circ v(s,y)-f\circ u(t,x))\,dtdsdxdy\\
-\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)f\circ u(t,x)\sgn(v(s,y)-u(t,x))\div K\ast v(s,y)\,dtdsdxdy.

In particular, if
v
is an entropy solution to (
1.1
), we have
\Delta(\varphi)\geq 0
. We will consider

\displaystyle\varphi(t,s,x,y):=\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(x-y)\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(t-s),

where
\varphi_{1}^{\delta}:=\delta^{-d}\varphi(\cdot/\delta)
and
\varphi_{2}^{\eta}:=\eta^{-1}\varphi_{2}(\cdot/\eta)
, and
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are smooth, nonnegative and compactly supported functions of mass
1
. In this situation, we denote
\Delta_{\delta,\eta}\equiv\Delta(\varphi)
.

Lemma 3.7

(à la Kuznetsov)
.

Let
u
be an entropy solution to (
1.1
) defined on
[0,T]
, and
v\in L^{\infty}((0,T),L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))\cap C([0,T],L^{1}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
satisfying the continuity estimate (
2.5
). Then, there exists
C_{T}>0
depending on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
and
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}_{t}(L^{\infty}\cap BV)_{x}}
, such that

\|u(T)-v(T)\|_{L^{1}}\leq C_{T}\big[\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C_{T}(\delta+\sqrt{\eta})-\inf_{(0,T)}\Delta_{\delta,\eta}\big]

(3.5)

Remark 3.8
.

Assuming that
v
is itself an entropy solution, we have
\Delta_{\delta,\eta}\geq 0
. Therefore, sending
\delta,\eta\to 0
gives back the stability estimate of
3.4
. Otherwise, this lemma allows to derive rates of convergence for several approximation schemes.

Letting aside the proof of this lemma for the moment, we now prove a rate of convergence for viscosity solutions.

Proof of
3.6
.

Consider
u_{\varepsilon}
to be the unique solution to (
2.1
) starting from
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{R}}^{d})
. Applying
lemma

3.7
to
v=u_{\varepsilon}
and using the equation (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta,\eta}

\displaystyle=-\varepsilon\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\sgn(u_{\varepsilon}(s,y)-u(t,x))\Delta u_{\varepsilon}(s,y)\,dtdsdxdy.

Since

\sgn(u_{\varepsilon}-k)\Delta u_{\varepsilon}=\Delta|u_{\varepsilon}-k|-\delta_{u_{\varepsilon}=k}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2},

in the sense of distributions, we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta,\eta}

\displaystyle\geq\varepsilon\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\Delta_{y}|u_{\varepsilon}(s,y)-u(t,x)|\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\Delta_{y}\varphi(t,x,s,y)|u_{\varepsilon}(s,y)-u(t,x)|\,dtdsdxdy.

Since
u_{\varepsilon}
is BV in space, locally uniformly in time, we obtain

\displaystyle\Delta_{\delta,\eta}

\displaystyle\geq-C\frac{\varepsilon}{\delta}.

Taking
\eta\to 0
and optimizing over
\delta
in (
3.5
), we obtain the result.
∎

Proof of
lemma

3.7
.

Consider an entropy solution
u
to (
1.1
), and a function
v\in L^{\infty}_{loc}((0,T),L^{1}\cap L^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{d}))
. We start from the entropy condition satisfied by
u
, using the doubling of variables argument:

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(T,x,s,y)|u(T,x)-v(s,y)|\,dsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}{\partial}_{t}\varphi(t,x,s,y)|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(0,x,s,y)|u_{0}(x)-v(s,y)|\,dsdxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\cdot K\ast u(t,x)\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\big(f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(s,y)\big)\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)f\circ v(s,y)\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\div K\ast u(t,x)\,dtdsdxdy.

We then reverse the role played by
u
and
v
. This gives

\displaystyle\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(T,x,s,y)|u(T,x)-v(s,y)|\,dsdxdy+\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,T,y)|u(t,x)-v(T,y)|\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(0,x,s,y)|u_{0}(x)-v(s,y)|\,dsdxdy-\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,0,y)|u(t,x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dtdxdy

\displaystyle\leq\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\cdot\big(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(s,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\big(f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(s,y)\big)\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\big[f\circ v(s,y)\div K\ast u(t,x)-f\circ u(t,x)\div K\ast v(s,y)\big]

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T),

where

\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T):=-\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,T,y)|u(t,x)-v(T,y)|\,dtdxdy\\
+\int_{[0,T]\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,0,y)|u(t,x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dtdxdy+\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}{\partial}_{s}\varphi(t,x,s,y)|u(t,x)-v(s,y)|\,dtdsdxdy\\
+\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\nabla_{y}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\cdot K\ast v(s,y)\sgn(v(s,y)-u(t,x))(f\circ v(s,y)-f\circ u(t,x))\,dtdsdxdy\\
-\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)f\circ u(t,x)\sgn(v(s,y)-u(t,x))\div K\ast v(s,y)\,dtdsdxdy.

Note that if
v
is an entropy solution to (
1.1
), then
\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T)\geq 0
.
We now use the triangle inequality as follows:

\displaystyle|u_{0}(x)-v(s,y)|

\displaystyle\leq|u_{0}(x)-v_{0}(x)|+|v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|+|v_{0}(y)-v(s,y)|,

\displaystyle|u(T,x)-v(s,y)|

\displaystyle\geq|u(T,x)-v(T,x)|-|v(T,x)-v(T,y)|-|v(T,y)-v(s,y)|,

and similarily for the expressions where the roles of
u
and
v
are interchanged. This gives

\displaystyle 2\|u(T)-v(T)\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(x-y)\big[|v(T,x)-v(T,y)|+|u(T,x)-u(T,y)|\big]\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(T-s)\big[\|v(T)-v(s)\|_{L^{1}}+\|u(T)-u(s)\|_{L^{1}}\big]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(x-y)\big[|v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|+|u_{0}(x)-u_{0}(y)|\big]\,dxdy

\displaystyle+\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(-s)\big[\|v_{0}-v(s)\|_{L^{1}}+\|u_{0}-u(s)\|_{L^{1}}\big]\,ds

\displaystyle+\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\nabla_{x}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\cdot\big(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(s,y)\big)

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\big(f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(s,y)\big)\,dtdsdxdy

(3.6)

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\big[f\circ v(s,y)\div K\ast u(t,x)-f\circ u(t,x)\div K\ast v(s,y)\big]

\displaystyle\quad\times\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T).

Let us consider the above terms separately. First, we have for BV functions,

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(x-y)|v_{0}(x)-v_{0}(y)|\,dxdy\leq C\delta|Dv_{0}|({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}\varphi_{1}^{\delta}(x-y)|v(T,x)-v(T,y)|\,dxdy\leq C\delta|Dv(T)|({\mathbb{R}}^{d}),

and similarily for
u
.
Then, we use the time continuity of
u
and
v
in order to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(-s)\|u_{0}-u(s)\|_{L^{1}}\,ds\leq C_{1}{\eta},

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}}\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(-s)\|v_{0}-v(s)\|_{L^{1}}\,ds\leq C_{2}\sqrt{\eta},

where here
C_{1}>0
depends on
\|u_{0}\|_{L^{\infty}\cap BV}
and similarly for
C_{2}
, involving propagated quantities for
v
. Similar bounds hold for the other time translation terms. Then, we rewrite

\displaystyle\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot\big(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast v(s,y)\big)

\displaystyle=\div_{x}\big[\varphi\big(K\ast u(t,x)-K\ast u(t,y)\big)\big]-\varphi\div K\ast u(t,x)

\displaystyle+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot K\ast(u-v)(t,y)+\nabla_{x}\varphi\cdot\big[K\ast v(t,y)-K\ast v(s,y)\big]

Noticing that
K\ast u(t)
is bounded and globally Lipschitz for a.e.
t>0
(
3.3
), we can use
lemma

3.2
and
lemma

3.1
to bound (
3.3
) by

\displaystyle C\delta-\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)\div K\ast u(t,x)\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\big[f\circ u(t,x)-f\circ v(s,y)\big]\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle+C\int_{0}^{T}\|K\ast(u-v)(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt+C\int_{0}^{T}\varphi_{2}^{\eta}(t-s)\|K\ast v(t)-K\ast v(s)\|_{L^{\infty}}\,dt.

Finally using that
K\in L^{\infty}
, we have obtained

\displaystyle 2\|u(T)-v(T)\|_{L^{1}}\leq 2\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\delta+C\sqrt{\eta}+C\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{1}}\,dt

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]^{2}\times({\mathbb{R}}^{d})^{2}}\varphi(t,x,s,y)f\circ u(t,x)\big[\div K\ast u(t,x)-\div K\ast v(s,y)\big]\sgn(u(t,x)-v(s,y))\,dtdsdxdy

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T).

Notice that the terms involving no derivatives on
\varphi
recombine in order to give the
(\delta,\eta)
-approximation of the quantity

\displaystyle-\int_{[0,T]\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}}f(u)\div K\ast(u-v)\sgn(u-v)\,dtdx.

At this point, it is not clear if this approximation can be associated with a quantitative rate of convergence, since we are dealing with not so regular kernels. We proceed as with the flux term, rewriting

\displaystyle\div K\ast u(t,x)-\div K\ast v(s,y)

\displaystyle=\div K\ast(u-v)(t,x)

\displaystyle+\div K\ast v(t,x)-\div K\ast v(s,x)

\displaystyle+\div K\ast v(s,x)-\div K\ast v(s,y).

Now,
\div K\ast v
inherits the time continuity and space regularity from
v
, since
\div K
is a Radon measure. We obtain in the end

\displaystyle\|u(T)-v(T)\|_{L^{1}}\leq\|u_{0}-v_{0}\|_{L^{1}}+C\delta+C\sqrt{\eta}+C\int_{0}^{T}\|u(t)-v(t)\|_{L^{1}}\,dt-\Delta_{\delta,\eta}(T).

We conclude by applying Grönwall’s lemma.
∎

4
Application to the one-dimensional CGV and hyperbolic KS models

As a direct consequence from our analysis, we provide the uniqueness of entropy solutions to the one-dimensional hyperbolic Keller-Segel model, and answer part of a question asked by Carrillo et al. in
[
7
]
.

4.1
The hyperbolic Keller–Segel model

We consider the model

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u+\div(u(1-u)\nabla S)=0,&t>0,\,x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\\
-\Delta S+S=u,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{T}}^{d}),&0\leq u_{0}\leq 1.\end{cases}

(4.1)

posed on the
d
-dimensional torus
{\mathbb{T}}^{d}
, which can be identified with
[-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}]^{d}
with periodic boundary conditions.

For general dimensions
d\geq 1
, such a singular kernel
S
does not allow neither for the propagation of BV norms, nor for an
L^{1}
stability estimate. Nevertheless, entropy solutions can be constructed using e.g. the kinetic formulation
[
24
]
.

When
d=1
, we have
\nabla S\in L^{\infty}\cap BV
. Therefore, we are exactly in the framework of our article, and we can state without proof the following:

Corollary 4.1
.

Let
d=1
. There exists a unique entropy solution to (
4.1
).

4.2
The Carrillo–Gómez-Castro–Vázquez model

We consider the model

\begin{cases}{\partial}_{t}u-\div(u^{m}\nabla v)=0,&t>0,\,x\in{\mathbb{T}}^{d},\\
\displaystyle-\Delta v=u-\int_{{\mathbb{T}}^{d}}u\,dx,\\
u|_{t=0}=u_{0}\in L^{\infty}\cap BV({\mathbb{T}}^{d}).\end{cases}

(4.2)

This model has been studied on the Euclidean space in
[
6
]
when
0<m<1
, and
[
7
]
when
m>1
. The special case
m=1
was already known as a model for vortices in type-II supraconductors and superfluidity
[
19
,
8
,
23
]
.

In
[
6
,
7
]
, the authors restrict themselves to either
d=1
or radial solutions. In this case, the conservation law (
4.2
) can be seen as the derivative equation of an associated Hamilton-Jacobi equation, for which a comparison principle holds. This strategy allows to study a Cauchy problem that is simpler than the original one, eventually proving well-posedness of the Hamilton-Jacobi equation.

However, the nonradial theory remains a challenge, with entropy solutions constructed in
[
10
]
(without uniqueness). Another open problem raised in
[
7
]
is to have a uniqueness result stated in terms of (
4.2
), and not the Hamilton-Jacobi equation. Going back to the
d=1
framework, our article gives a partial answer to this issue.

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
d=1
,
m>0
, and
u_{0}>0
. There exists a unique entropy solution to (
4.2
).

Extensive emphysematous urinary tract infection involving the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder successfully treated with ureteral stenting
Authors: Yoshihiro Kawaguchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Simultaneous involvement of the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder in emphysematous urinary tract infections is rare. A 79-year-old woman presented with fever, septic shock, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and acute kidney injury. Computed tomography revealed intraluminal gas involving these three structures/organs, with hydronephrosis. Her serum procalcitonin levels were high (388.90 ng/mL). Ureteral stenting and antimicrobial therapy were initiated, resulting in clinical improvement. Three-dimensional reconstruction enabled visualization of the gas distribution and resolution after treatment. The emphysematous lesions had disappeared by day 15. This case highlights the importance of early urinary drainage and appropriate antimicrobial therapy in extensive emphysematous urinary tract infections.

Introduction

An emphysematous urinary tract infection (EUTI) is a rare but potentially life-threatening infection characterized by gas formation within the urinary tract caused by gas-producing organisms.1,2 Diabetes mellitus and urinary tract obstruction are recognized risk factors, and delayed treatment may lead to severe sepsis and poor outcomes.2,3 Ureteral involvement is particularly uncommon, and simultaneous emphysematous infection involving the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder has rarely been reported.1,4 Herein, we report a severe case of an extensive EUTI with septic shock successfully managed via ureteral stenting and antimicrobial therapy.

Case presentation

A 79-year-old woman was admitted to our hospital with fever and septic shock. She had a history of type 1 diabetes mellitus and recurrent urinary tract infections. Upon admission, her body temperature exceeded 38 °C, and vasopressor support was required to maintain her blood pressure. Laboratory findings revealed leukocytosis (16,000 leukocytes/μL), an elevated serum creatinine level (4.48 mg/dL), an elevated C-reactive protein level (25.18 mg/dL), thrombocytopenia (45,000 thrombocytes/μL), and a markedly elevated procalcitonin level (388.90 ng/mL). Her disseminated intravascular coagulation score was 6, and her Sequential Organ Failure Assessment score was 9.

Computed tomography demonstrated extensive intraluminal gas involving the bladder, left ureter, and left renal pelvis with hydronephrosis (Fig. 1). Empirical carbapenem therapy was initiated on hospital day 1 because the patient presented with septic shock. On hospital day 2, a ureteral stent and Foley catheter were placed for urinary decompression, and approximately 15 mL of pyonephrotic urine was drained. Cystoscopy showed white and reddish mucosal patches in the trigone and posterior bladder wall.

Urine cultures, blood cultures, and renal pelvic urine cultures yielded extended-spectrum β-lactamase-producing Escherichia coli. Based on antimicrobial susceptibility testing, antimicrobial therapy was de-escalated to cefmetazole on hospital day 8, and the Foley catheter was removed on the same day.

Follow-up computed tomography performed on hospital day 15 confirmed complete resolution of the emphysematous lesions, although bladder enlargement suggestive of neurogenic bladder remained (Fig. 2B). Oral trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole therapy was initiated on hospital day 16 and continued for 5 days, after which antimicrobial treatment was discontinued. The patient showed rapid clinical improvement. Because persistent residual urine raised concerns regarding recurrent urinary tract infection, a Foley catheter was reinserted on hospital day 20. Subsequently, mild elevations in serum creatinine and potassium levels were observed on hospital day 22, possibly related to trimethoprim–sulfamethoxazole therapy, although renal function gradually improved thereafter. After stabilization of her general condition, the patient was transferred to another hospital for rehabilitation on hospital day 28.

Discussion

This case provides two notable clinical findings. First, extensive EUTI involving the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder can rapidly progress to septic shock with disseminated intravascular coagulation and acute kidney injury. Second, early urinary drainage combined with appropriate antimicrobial therapy may contribute to favorable clinical outcomes. Our patient developed septic shock, disseminated intravascular coagulation, and acute kidney injury associated with an extensive emphysematous infection involving the renal pelvis, ureter, and bladder. In contrast with the earlier reports, this case was characterized by a markedly elevated serum procalcitonin level and extensive distribution of gas within the urinary tract. Computed tomography is the gold standard for diagnosing EUTIs and plays an essential role in evaluating the disease extent and determining treatment strategies.2 In the present case, three-dimensional reconstruction provided additional visualization of the gas distribution and its resolution after treatment. Although gas quantification did not directly alter the immediate management strategy, as urgent urinary drainage and antimicrobial therapy were indicated regardless of the measured gas volume, quantitative assessment enabled objective visualization of disease burden and facilitated radiological comparison before and after treatment. To the best of our knowledge, quantitative three-dimensional assessment of gas volume in extensive EUTIs has rarely been described. We believe that this approach may be useful for future evaluation of disease severity and treatment response in extensive EUTIs.

Early urinary drainage with ureteral stenting combined with appropriate antimicrobial therapy resulted in rapid clinical and radiological improvement. Previous studies have emphasized the importance of prompt drainage and appropriate antimicrobial selection for severe EUTIs.4,7,8

Conclusion

This case highlights the importance of early source control and antimicrobial therapy for extensive EUTIs involving multiple segments of the urinary tract.

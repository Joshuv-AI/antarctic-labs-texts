Conjugating full cycles by adjacent transpositions: diameter and sorting time
Ron M. Adin, Eli Bagno, Yuval Roichman

Abstract
. We establish upper and lower bounds on the maximal number of steps needed to transform a cyclic permutation to the canonical cyclic permutation using conjugation by adjacent transpositions, and on the diameter of the underlying Schreier graph.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
05A05, 68P10

1.
Introduction

Let
C_{n}
be the set of
n
-cycles in the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Let
\Phi_{n}:=\{s_{i}=(i,i+1):\,1\leq i<n\}
be the set of adjacent transpositions (Coxeter simple reflections) in
S_{n}
.

Definition 1.1
.

Consider the undirected graph
\Gamma_{n}
, with vertex set
C_{n}
, in which two cycles
c_{1},c_{2}\in C_{n}
are adjacent if
c_{2}=s_{i}c_{1}s_{i}
for some adjacent transposition
s_{i}\in\Phi_{n}
.

Problem 1.2
.

Find the diameter of the graph
\Gamma_{n}
,

D_{n}:={\operatorname{Diameter}}(\Gamma_{n})=\max_{\beta,\gamma\in C_{n}}{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(\beta,\gamma).

Here
{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(\beta,\gamma)
is the
distance (length of shortest path) in
\Gamma_{n}
between
\beta
and
\gamma
.

A closely related problem is the following.
A
cyclic permutation

[\pi]
of size
n
is
a bijective labeling of
n
distinct points on a circle
by the elements of
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
; two labelings are equivalent if one can be obtained from the other by a cyclic shift.
The
canonical cyclic permutation

c_{n}:=(1,2,\dots,n)
is the one in which the elements are clockwise labeled by
1,\dots,n
.
A
permissible step
on
[\pi]
is a switching of two consecutive values,
i
and
i+1
, for some
1\leq i<n
.

Definition 1.3
.

The Coxeter cyclic sorting time,
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
, is the maximal number of permissible steps needed to convert a cyclic permutation
[\pi]
of size
n
into the canonical cyclic permutation
c_{n}=(1,2,\dots,n)
.

By Remark
3.1
below,

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}:=\max_{\gamma\in C_{n}}{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(c_{n},\gamma).

Problem 1.4
.

Find the Coxeter cyclic sorting time.

For other sorting processes on cyclic permutations see
[
1
]
and references therein.

By definition, the Coxeter cyclic sorting time is not bigger than the diameter.
Since the graph
\Gamma_{n}
is not vertex transitive for
n\geq 4
(see, e.g., Figure
1
), these two numbers are not necessarily equal.
For example, for
n=5
,
{\operatorname{Diameter}}(\Gamma_{5})=5>4={\operatorname{Sort}}_{5}
.

2.
Notation and preliminaries

Let
S_{n}
be the symmetric group on the letters
[n]:=\{1,\dots,n\}
. The one-line notation of a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
is denoted by square brackets
[\pi(1),\dots,\pi(n)]
, while the cycle notation by parentheses. For example
[3,5,6,1,2,4]=(1,3,6,4)(2,5)\in S_{6}
.

Letting
H
be a subgroup of a finite group
G
, and
S\subseteq G
, the
Schreier graph

X(G/H,S)
is the undirected graph whose vertex set consists of all left cosets
G/H:=\{\pi H\,:\,\pi\in G\}
; the edges are the unordered pairs
(\pi H,s\pi H)
, for
s\in S,\pi\in G
.

3.
The minv statistic

Let
c_{n}:=(1,2,\dots,n)=s_{1}s_{2}\cdots s_{n-1}\in S_{n}
.
Denote by
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
the cyclic subgroup of
S_{n}
generated by
c_{n}
. Recall the graph
\Gamma_{n}
from Definition
1.1
.

Remark 3.1
.

Consider the map from
C_{n}
to the set of left cosets
S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
, defined as follows: a cycle
\gamma=(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in C_{n}
is mapped to the coset
\bar{\gamma}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
, where
\bar{\gamma}=[a_{1},\dots,a_{n}]
. This map is a well-defined bijection and induces an isomorphism from the graph
\Gamma_{n}
to the Schreier graph
X(S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},\Phi_{n})
, where edge multiplicity is ignored.

Recall the
inversion number
of a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
,

{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi):=|\{i<j:\,\pi(i)>\pi(j)\}|.

Definition 3.2
.

Define the minv statistic on the left cosets of
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
in
S_{n}
as follows:

{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}):=\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\,\sigma\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})\}\qquad(\forall\,\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\in S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}).

Lemma 3.3
.

For every pair of full cycles
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in C_{n}
,

{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})=\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\ \sigma\in\bar{\gamma}_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\bar{\gamma}_{2}^{-1}\},

where
\bar{\gamma}_{1},\bar{\gamma}_{2}
are defined as in Remark
3.1
.

Proof.

By Remark
3.1
, the distance
{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})

is equal to the smallest nonnegative integer
\ell
for which there exist simple reflections
s_{i_{1}},\ldots,s_{i_{\ell}}
satisfying
s_{i_{\ell}}\cdots s_{i_{1}}\bar{\gamma}_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}=\bar{\gamma}_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
.
Equivalently,

\ell=\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau_{2}\tau_{1}^{-1}):\,\tau_{1}\in\bar{\gamma}_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},\,\tau_{2}\in\bar{\gamma}_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}=\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\,\sigma\in\bar{\gamma}_{2}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\bar{\gamma}_{1}^{-1}\}.\qed

Corollary 3.4
.

For every full cycle
\gamma\in C_{n}

{\operatorname{dist}}_{\Gamma_{n}}(c_{n},\gamma)={\operatorname{minv}}(\bar{\gamma}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}),

where
c_{n}=(1,2,\dots,n)\in C_{n}
and
\bar{\gamma}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\in S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is defined as in Remark
3.1
.

In particular, for every
n\geq 1

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}=\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})

We conclude that Problem
1.4
is equivalent to the following one.

Problem 3.5
.

What is the maximal value of minv on
S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
?

4.
Upper bound on the Coxeter cyclic sorting time

Recall the Coxeter cyclic sorting time,
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
, from Definition
1.3
. The main result in this section is the following.

Theorem 4.1
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}\leq\frac{2n^{2}-3n+1}{6}.

Theorem
4.1
is an immediate consequence of the following result.

Proposition 4.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
, the maximum of the expected value of
{\operatorname{inv}}
on left cosets
\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
satisfies

\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}E\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\sigma\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}=\frac{(2n-1)(n-1)}{6}.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

By Corollary
3.4
, Definition
3.2

and Proposition
4.2
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})=\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{i}):0\leq i<n\}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}E\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{i}):0\leq i<n\}=\frac{(2n-1)(n-1)}{6}.\qed

In order to prove Proposition
4.2

we consider a weighted version of the inversion number, as well as a cyclic variant, see Definitions
4.3
and
4.6
below.

Definition 4.3
.

The weighted inversion number of a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
is

{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i<j,\,\pi(i)>\pi(j)\end{subarray}}(\pi(i)-\pi(j)).

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}
,

{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\pi(i).

Proof.

By induction on the inversion number
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)
.
If
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)=0
then
\pi=id
, and the claim clearly holds.
Now assume that the claim holds for all
\pi^{\prime}\in S_{n}
with
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi^{\prime})\leq k
, and let
\pi\in S_{n}
have
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)=k+1
.
Since
\pi\neq id
, there exists
1\leq j<n
such that
\pi(j)>\pi(j+1)
, so that
\pi^{\prime}:=\pi s_{j}
satisfies

{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi^{\prime})=k
.
By the induction hypothesis,

{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi^{\prime})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\pi^{\prime}(i).

By Definition
4.3
,
{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)-{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi^{\prime})=\pi(j)-\pi(j+1)
. Also,

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\pi(i)\right)-\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\pi^{\prime}(i)\right)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}i\cdot\left(\pi^{\prime}(i)-\pi(i)\right)=\pi(j)-\pi(j+1),

and thus the claim holds for
\pi
as well.
This completes the induction step and the proof.
∎

Denote
w_{0}:=[n,n-1,\dots,1]\in S_{n}
. Recall that
w_{0}
has the largest inversion number in
S_{n}
.

Observation 4.5
.

For any
\pi\in S_{n}
,

{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)+{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi w_{0})=\sum_{1\leq k<\ell\leq n}(\ell-k)=\sum_{\ell=2}^{n}\binom{\ell}{2}=\binom{n+1}{3}

and

0={\operatorname{winv}}(id)\leq{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)\leq{\operatorname{winv}}(w_{0})=\binom{n+1}{3}.

The following cyclic variant of
{\operatorname{winv}}
plays a key role in the proof.

Definition 4.6
.

The cyclic weighted inversion number of a permutation
\pi\in S_{n}
is

{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi):=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)-2\cdot{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,j\\
i<j,\,\pi(i)>\pi(j)\end{subarray}}(n-2\pi(i)+2\pi(j)).

The following observation justifies the adjective
cyclic
in Definition
4.6
.

Observation 4.7
.

The cyclic weighted inversion number is invariant under cyclic shifts; namely,

{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi c_{n})={\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)\qquad(\forall\pi\in S_{n}).

Proof.

Denote
m:=\pi(1)
. Then
m=\pi c_{n}(n)
, so that

{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n})-{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)=(n-m)-(m-1)=n+1-2m

and

{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi c_{n})-{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)=\sum_{m<j\leq n}(j-m)-\sum_{1\leq j<m}(m-j)=\binom{n-m+1}{2}-\binom{m}{2}.

Thus

{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi c_{n})-{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)=n(n+1-2m)-(n-m+1)(n-m)+m(m-1)=0.\qed

Observation 4.8
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}

{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)+{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi w_{0})=n\binom{n}{2}-2\binom{n+1}{3}=\binom{n}{3}.

Corollary 4.9
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}

0={\operatorname{cwinv}}(id)\leq{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)\leq{\operatorname{cwinv}}(w_{0})=\binom{n}{3}.

Proof.

By Observation
4.7
, we may assume that
\pi(n)=n
.
Let
\bar{\pi}=[\pi(1),\dots,\pi(n-1)]\in S_{n-1}
. By definition,

{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)-2\cdot{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\bar{\pi})-2\cdot{\operatorname{winv}}(\bar{\pi})={\operatorname{cwinv}}(\bar{\pi})+{\operatorname{inv}}(\bar{\pi}).

Hence,

\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)\leq\max_{\bar{\pi}\in S_{n-1}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\bar{\pi})+\max_{\pi\in S_{n-1}}{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi).

By iteration,

\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)\leq\max_{\bar{\pi}\in S_{n-1}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\bar{\pi})+\max_{\pi\in S_{n-1}}{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\max_{\pi\in S_{j}}{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)=\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\binom{j}{2}=\binom{n}{3}={\operatorname{cwinv}}(w_{0}).

Combining this upper bound with Observation
4.8
,

\min_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)=\binom{n}{3}-\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)\geq 0={\operatorname{cwinv}}(id).\qed

Lemma 4.10
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}

n\cdot E\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\sigma\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{i})={\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)+\binom{n+1}{3}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Observation
4.7
,

{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n})={\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+n+1-2\pi(1).

Thus, by iteration

{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{i})={\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{i}(n+1-2\pi(j)).

Summing over
1\leq i\leq n
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{n}{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{i})

\displaystyle=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}(n+1-2\pi(j))

\displaystyle=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}(n+1-j)(n+1-2\pi(j))

\displaystyle=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\cdot 2\pi(j)-(n+1)\sum_{j=1}^{n}(2\pi(j)+j)+n(n+1)^{2}

\displaystyle=n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j\cdot\pi(j)-\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)^{2}

By Lemma
4.4
and Definition
4.6
, this is equal to

n\cdot{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)+2\sum_{j=1}^{n}j^{2}-2\cdot{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)-\frac{1}{2}n(n+1)^{2}={\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)+\binom{n+1}{3}.\qed

Proof of Proposition
4.2
.

By Lemma
4.10
and Corollary
4.9
,

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}E\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\sigma):\sigma\in\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{cwinv}}(\pi)+\binom{n+1}{3}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{n}\left(\binom{n}{3}+\binom{n+1}{3}\right)=\frac{(2n-1)(n-1)}{6}.\qed

5.
Lower bound on the Coxeter cyclic sorting time

In this section we prove the following lower bound on
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
, and thus on
{\operatorname{Diameter}}(\Gamma_{n})
as well.

Theorem 5.1
.

For every
n\geq 1

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}\right)n^{2}-\frac{3}{2}n.

The proof of this theorem uses the following lemma.

Lemma 5.2
.

For every
\pi\in S_{n}
,
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)={\operatorname{minv}}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})
if and only if
\pi
is “heavy tailed”, namely if, for all
1\leq k\leq n
, the sum of the last
k
entries of
\pi
is at least
\frac{k(n+1)}{2}
;
equivalently if, for all
1\leq k\leq n
, the sum of the first
k
entries of
\pi
is at most
\frac{k(n+1)}{2}
:

\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi(j)\leq\frac{k(n+1)}{2}\qquad(\forall k);

Proof.

For every
1\leq k\leq n
,
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)\leq{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{k})
if and only if

\displaystyle 0\leq{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{k})-{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left({\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{j})-{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi c_{n}^{j-1})\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}\left((n-\pi c_{n}^{j-1}(1))-(\pi c_{n}^{j-1}(1)-1)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{k}(n+1-2\pi(j))=k(n+1)-2\sum_{j=1}^{k}\pi(j).\qed

Proof of Theorem
5.1
.

By Corollary
3.4
, we need to maximize
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi)
over all the permutations
\pi\in S_{n}
which satisfy the conditions of Lemma
5.2
.

Recall the permutation
w_{0}=[n,\ldots,1]
and consider, as a first approximation, its cyclic shift
w_{0}c_{n}^{m}=[n-m,\ldots,1,n,\ldots,n-m+1]
, where
m:=\lfloor n/2\rfloor
.
Noting that
w_{0}c_{n}^{m}
satisfies the conditions of Lemma
5.2
, one concludes that

{\operatorname{minv}}(w_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})={\operatorname{inv}}(w_{0}c_{n}^{m})=\binom{n-m}{2}+\binom{m}{2}=\binom{\lceil n/2\rceil}{2}+\binom{\lfloor n/2\rfloor}{2}.

Now, take
w_{0}c_{n}^{m}
and push the letter
n
to the left as much as possible, say to position
k_{1}+1
, so that the conditions of Lemma
5.2
are still satisfied.
Then push the letter
n-1
to the left as much as possible, say to position
k_{2}+2
, and so on. We obtain the permutation

(5.1)

\displaystyle\pi_{0}

\displaystyle:=\left[n-m,n-m-1,\dots,n-m-(k_{1}-1),n,n-m-k_{1},\dots,n-m-(k_{2}-1),n-1,\right.

\displaystyle\quad\;\left.n-m-k_{2},\dots,n-m-(k_{t}-1),n+1-t,n-m-k_{t},\dots\right]\in S_{n},

where
0\leq k_{1}\leq\ldots\leq k_{m}\leq n-m
have the minimal possible values such that
\pi_{0}
still satisfies the conditions of Lemma
5.2
.
For example, for
n=12
,
\pi_{0}=[6,5,4,3,12,2,11,1,10,9,8,7]
.

Let us estimate
{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0})
, which will serve as a lower bound for
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
.
Considering separately the small values
1\leq i\leq m
and the large values
m+1\leq n+1-t\leq n
,

{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})={\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}(i-1)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}(n-t-k_{t})=\binom{n}{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}.

First, assume that
n=2m
is even.
By the conditions of Lemma
5.2
, for every
1\leq t\leq m
,

\frac{(k_{t}+t)(n+1)}{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k_{t}+t}\pi_{0}(j)=\sum_{i=0}^{k_{t}-1}\left(m-i\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left(n-j\right)=\frac{k_{t}(n+1-k_{t})}{2}+\frac{t(2n+1-t)}{2}.

Simplifying, this yields

k_{t}^{2}\geq t(n-t)\qquad(\forall\,1\leq t\leq m).

If these inequalities are satisfied, then the inequalities of Lemma
5.2
for indices other than
k_{1}+1,\cdots,k_{m}+m
obviously follow.
Minimizing the parameters
k_{t}
, for all
1\leq t\leq m
, we obtain approximately

\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}\sim\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}\left(t(n-t)\right)^{1/2}\sim\int_{0}^{n/2}\left(x(n-x)\right)^{1/2}dx.

Letting
x=ny=n\sin^{2}\theta
, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{n/2}\left(x(n-x)\right)^{1/2}dx=

\displaystyle n^{2}\int_{0}^{1/2}\left(y(1-y)\right)^{1/2}dy=2n^{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\sin^{2}\theta\cos^{2}\theta d\theta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{n^{2}}{2}\int_{0}^{\pi/4}\sin^{2}(2\theta)d\theta=\frac{n^{2}}{4}\int_{0}^{\pi/4}(1-\cos(4\theta))d\theta=\frac{\pi}{16}n^{2}.

This yields

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}=\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})\geq{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})=\binom{n}{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}\sim\binom{n}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}n^{2}.

For a more precise lower bound, let us bound the error in this approximation. Since

k_{t}=\left\lceil(t(n-t))^{1/2}\right\rceil\qquad(1\leq t\leq m),

we conclude that

(5.2)

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}k_{t}-\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}(t(n-t))^{1/2}\leq n/2.

On the other hand,
f(x):=\left(x(n-x)\right)^{1/2}
is a monotone increasing function for
0\leq x\leq n/2
, and therefore

f(t-1)\cdot 1\leq\int_{t-1}^{t}f(x)dx\leq f(t)\cdot 1\qquad(1\leq t\leq n/2).

Hence

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}f(t)-\int_{0}^{n/2}f(x)dx\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}f(t)-\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}f(t-1)=f\left(\frac{n}{2}\right)-f(0)=\frac{n}{2}

or, explicitly,

(5.3)

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}(t(n-t))^{1/2}-\frac{\pi}{16}n^{2}\leq\frac{n}{2}.

Adding inequalities (
5.2
) and (
5.3
) yields

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}k_{t}-\frac{\pi}{16}n^{2}\leq n.

Thus, for even
n
:

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}\geq{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0})=\binom{n}{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{n/2}k_{t}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}\right)n^{2}-\frac{3}{2}n.

Now assume that
n=2m+1
is odd.
The computations are similar to those for even
n

but a little more complicated; in this case we only estimate, but do not evaluate, the integral.

By the conditions of Lemma
5.2
, for every

1\leq t\leq m

\frac{(k_{t}+t)(n+1)}{2}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{k_{t}+t}\pi_{0}(j)=\sum_{i=0}^{k_{t}-1}\left(m+1-i\right)+\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left(n-j\right)=\frac{k_{t}(n+2-k_{t})}{2}+\frac{t(2n+1-t)}{2}.

Simplifying, this yields

k_{t}^{2}-k_{t}\geq t(n-t)\qquad(\forall\,1\leq t\leq m)

or, equivalently,

k_{t}\geq(t(n-t)+1/4)^{1/2}+1/2\qquad(\forall\,1\leq t\leq m).

Again,

{\operatorname{minv}}(\pi_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})={\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{m+1}(i-1)+\sum_{t=1}^{m}(n-t-k_{t})=\binom{n}{2}-\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}.

Minimizing the parameters
k_{t}
, for all
1\leq t\leq m
, we obtain

\sum_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}\sim\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}\left((t(n-t)+1/4)^{1/2}+1/2\right)\sim\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}\left((x(n-x)+1/4)^{1/2}+1/2\right)dx.

We now bound this integral.
For every
0<x<n
,

(x(n-x)+1/4)^{1/2}\leq\sqrt{x(n-x)}+\frac{1}{8\sqrt{x(n-x)}}.

Denoting
g(x):=(x(n-x)+1/4)^{1/2}+1/2
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}g(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}\left(\sqrt{x(n-x)}+\frac{1}{8\sqrt{x(n-x)}}+\frac{1}{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{n/2}\left(\sqrt{x(n-x)}+\frac{1}{8\sqrt{x(n-x)}}\right)dx-\int_{(n-1)/2}^{n/2}\sqrt{x(n-x)}dx+\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}\frac{dx}{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\pi}{16}(n^{2}+1)-\frac{1}{2}\cdot\sqrt{\frac{n-1}{2}\cdot\frac{n+1}{2}}+\frac{n-1}{4}\leq\frac{\pi}{16}(n^{2}+1).

Now

k_{t}=\left\lceil g(t)\right\rceil\qquad(1\leq t\leq m)

implies that

(5.4)

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}k_{t}-\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}g(t)\leq\frac{n-1}{2}.

On the other hand,
g(x)
is a monotone increasing function for
0\leq x\leq n/2
, and therefore

g(t-1)\cdot 1\leq\int_{t-1}^{t}g(x)dx\leq g(t)\cdot 1\qquad(1\leq t\leq(n-1)/2).

Hence

(5.5)

\displaystyle 0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}g(t)-\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}g(x)dx

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}g(t)-\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}g(t-1)

\displaystyle=g\left(\frac{n-1}{2}\right)-g(0)=\frac{n+1}{2}-1=\frac{n-1}{2}.

Adding inequalities (
5.4
) and (
5.5
) yields

0\leq\sum_{t=1}^{(n-1)/2}k_{t}-\int_{0}^{(n-1)/2}g(x)dx\leq n-1,

Altogether, we obtain
for odd
n
:

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}\geq{\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0})=\binom{n}{2}-\sum\limits_{t=1}^{m}k_{t}\geq\binom{n}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}(n^{2}+1)-(n-1)>\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{\pi}{16}\right)n^{2}-\frac{3}{2}n.

∎

6.
Upper bound on the diameter

In this section we prove the following.

Theorem 6.1
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

{\operatorname{Diameter}}(\Gamma_{n})\leq\frac{3n^{2}-4n+1}{8}.

The proof of Theorem
6.1
requires several lemmas, which may be of independent interest.

The following expression for the diameter of
\Gamma_{n}
follows immediately from Lemma
3.3
.

Corollary 6.2
.

For every
n\geq 1
,

{\operatorname{Diameter}}(\Gamma_{n})=\max_{\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in S_{n}}\min\{{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau):\,\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}\},

where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is the subgroup of
S_{n}
generated by the
n
-cycle
c_{n}=(1,2,\dots,n)
.

Lemma 6.3
.

For every permutation
\tau\in S_{n}
and
0\leq k\leq n
,

{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau)\leq\binom{n-k}{2}-k+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i).

Proof.

Fix
0\leq k\leq n
, and evaluate

|\{(i,j):\,i\leq k<j,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|.

We can assume, without loss of generality, that
\tau(1)<\tau(2)<\cdots<\tau(k)
.
Then, for every
1\leq i\leq k
,

|\{j:\,k<j,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|=\tau(i)-i.

Thus

|\{(i,j):\,i\leq k<j,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}(\tau(i)-i)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)-\binom{k+1}{2}.

Note that, obviously,

|\{(i,j):\,i<j\leq k,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|+|\{(i,j):\,k<i<j,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|\leq\binom{k}{2}+\binom{n-k}{2}.

Altogether,
for every fixed
0\leq k\leq n
,

\displaystyle{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau)=|\{(i,j):\,i<j,\,\tau(i)>\tau(j)\}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)-\binom{k+1}{2}+\binom{k}{2}+\binom{n-k}{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)-k+\binom{n-k}{2}.\qed

The key lemma in the proof of Theorem
6.1
is the following.

Lemma 6.4
.

For
any
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in S_{n}
and
any
0\leq k\leq n
, there exists
\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)\leq\frac{k(n+1)}{2}.

Proof.

First notice that, for any
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in S_{n}
and any
1\leq i\leq n
,

\displaystyle\{\tau(i):\,\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}\}=\{\pi_{1}c_{n}^{j}\pi_{2}^{-1}(i)\,:\,1\leq j\leq n\}=\{\pi_{1}(t):\,1\leq t\leq n\}=\{1,\dots,n\}.

It follows that, for any
1\leq i\leq n
, the expected value, in position
i
, of a random permutation in
\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}
is

E_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}\,[\tau(i)]=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\pi_{1}c_{n}^{j}\pi_{2}^{-1}(i)=\frac{1}{n}(1+\dots+n)=\frac{n+1}{2}.

Hence, the expected sum of values of
\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}
on a prefix of length
k
is

E_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{k}E_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}\,[\tau(i)]=k\cdot\frac{n+1}{2}.

There must exist
\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}
for which
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)
is smaller or equal to its expected value, and this completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
6.1
.

By Lemma
6.3
and Lemma
6.4
, for any
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}\in S_{n}
and any
0\leq k\leq n
,

\min_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau)\leq\binom{n-k}{2}-k+\min_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\tau(i)\leq\binom{n-k}{2}-k+\frac{k(n+1)}{2}=\binom{n}{2}-\frac{k(n-k)}{2}.

The RHS is minimized for
k=n/2
(if
n
is even) or
k=(n\pm 1)/2
(if
n
is odd). It follows that

\min_{\tau\in\pi_{1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}\pi_{2}^{-1}}{\operatorname{inv}}(\tau)\leq\binom{n}{2}-\frac{n^{2}-\chi(n\text{ odd})}{8}=\frac{3n^{2}-4n+\chi(n\text{ odd})}{8},

where
\chi(n\text{ odd})
is
1
if
n
is odd, and
0
otherwise.
Corollary
6.2
completes the proof.
∎

7.
Final remarks and open problems

An improvement of Theorem
1.5
is desired.

Conjecture 7.1
.

The following asymptotic equality holds:

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}=\frac{8-\pi}{16}\cdot n^{2}+O(n).

Recall the permutation
\pi_{0}
from (
5.1
).
Computer experiments suggest that, furthermore, for every
n\geq 1

(7.1)

{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}={\operatorname{inv}}(\pi_{0}).

By Remark
3.1
, together with Corollary
3.4
and the definition of
\pi_{0}
,
(
7.1
)
is equivalent to the following equality:

\max_{\pi\in S_{n}}{\operatorname{dist}}_{X(S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},\Phi_{n})}(\pi{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},c_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})={\operatorname{dist}}_{X(S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},\Phi_{n})}(\pi_{0}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n},c_{n}{\mathbb{Z}}_{n})\qquad(\forall n).

Equality (
7.1
)
was verified for every
n\leq 12
.

Recall that the maximal value of the
{\operatorname{minv}}
statistics on
S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is equal to the Coxeter sorting time
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
(Corollary
3.4
). Hence,
Theorem
1.5
provides bounds on the maximal value of
{\operatorname{minv}}
on
S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
. The actual distribution of
{\operatorname{minv}}
deserves further study. We conjecture the following.

Conjecture 7.2
.

The generating function of
{\operatorname{minv}}
on
S_{n}/{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
is unimodal.

The
{\operatorname{winv}}
statistic, from Definition
4.3
,
plays a crucial role in the proof
of the upper bound on
{\operatorname{Sort}}_{n}
.
Lemma
4.4
implies the following geometric interpretation of
{\operatorname{winv}}
:

\frac{{\operatorname{winv}}(\pi)}{\sum_{i=1}^{n}i^{2}}=1-\cos\alpha_{\pi}\,,

where
\alpha_{\pi}
is the angle between the vectors
v_{\pi}:=(\pi(1),\dots,\pi(n))
and
v_{id}:=(1,2,\dots,n)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
The distribution of
{\operatorname{winv}}
is mysterious.
Note that, by Observation
4.5
, the
{\operatorname{winv}}
generating function is palindromic.

The set of adjacent transpositions
\Phi_{n}:=\{(i,i+1):\ 1\leq i<n\}
may be augmented by the transposition
(n,1)
, to yield a cyclically symmetric set:

\Phi^{cyc}_{n}:=\{(i,i+1\bmod n):\,1\leq i\leq n\}=\Phi_{n}\sqcup\{(n,1)\}.

It is natural to sort cyclic permutations of size
n
by conjugation with this cyclically symmetric set.
Let
\Gamma^{cyc}_{n}
be the undirected graph whose vertex set is the class of
n
-cycles
C_{n}
in
S_{n}
, and two cycles
c_{1},c_{2}\in C_{n}
are adjacent if there exists a transposition
s\in\Phi^{cyc}_{n}
, such that
c_{2}=sc_{1}s
.

Problem 7.3
.

Find the diameter of
\Gamma^{cyc}_{n}
.

Finding the shortest path in
\Gamma^{cyc}_{n}
from a given cyclic permutation to the canonical cyclic permutation is equivalent to sorting cyclic permutations by switching cyclically adjacent values, and is widely open.
The analogous sorting process of cyclic permutations by switching cyclically adjacent
positions
was recently studied in
[
1
]
.

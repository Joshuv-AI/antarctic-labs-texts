An efficient and energy-stable IMEX splitting scheme for dispersed multiphase flows Journal: ArXiv
Douglas R. Q. Pacheco 1,2,3 and Richard Schussnig 4 , 1 Chair for Computational Analysis of Technical Systems, RWTH Aachen University, Germany 2 Chair of Methods for Model-based Development in Computational Engineering, RWTH 3 Center for Simulation and Data Science (JARA-CSD), RWTH Aachen University, Germany 4 Faculty of Mathematics, Ruhr University Bochum, Germany

Abstract
Volume-averaged Navier–Stokes equations are used in various applications to model systems with two or more interpenetrating phases. Each fluid obeys its own momentum and mass equations, and the phases are typically coupled via drag forces and a shared pressure. Monolithic solvers can therefore be very expensive and difficult to implement. On the other hand, designing robust splitting schemes requires making both pressure and drag forces explicit without sacrificing temporal stability. In this context, we derive a new first-order pressure-correction method based on the incompressibility of the mean velocity field, combined with an explicit treatment of the drag forces. Furthermore, the convective terms are linearised using extrapolated velocities, while the viscous terms are treated semi-implicitly. This gives us an implicit-explicit (IMEX) method that is very robust not only due to its unconditional energy stability, but also because it does not require any type of fixed-point iterations. Each time step involves only linear, scalar transport equations and a single Poisson problem as building blocks, thereby offering both efficiency and simplicity. We rigorously prove temporal stability without any time-step size restrictions, and the theory is confirmed through two-phase numerical examples.



1
Introduction

Dispersed multiphase systems are typically modelled as a set of two or more fluids that completely share the same domain
\Omega
, with volume fractions describing how much of each fluid occupies each point in
\Omega
at a given time. These models are different from fully separated two-phase flows, where each fluid occupies its own
subset
of
\Omega
and interacts with the other phase via interface forces
[
1
]
. In the dispersed or volume-averaged setting, which is the topic of this article, how to model the interactions between the phases depends, e.g., on the flow regime and the materials considered
[
2
,
3
]
. These models are relevant in various industrial and scientific applications, such as gas-liquid systems
[
4
,
5
]
, debris flows
[
6
,
7
]
and volcanic ash dynamics
[
8
]
. Regardless of how the phase interactions are described, most volume-averaged models have in common that (1) each fluid obeys its own Navier–Stokes-like system in
\Omega
, (2) the volume fractions form (together) a partition of unity, and (3) there is a pressure field at least partially shared by all the phases. In this work, we focus on the prototypical variant where the phases are coupled via drag forces and a common pressure
[
9
,
10
]
.

The numerics of multiphase flows pose considerable challenges. Since each phase follows a Navier–Stokes-like system, the well-known issues and instabilities observed in single-phase flow simulation also arise. Furthermore, the number of unknowns can be substantial: an
M
-phase flow will involve
M
phase fractions,
M
velocities and a pressure. Probably due to the complexity of such systems, numerical methods designed specifically for them are scarce in the literature. In the past thirty years, a handful of works have emerged addressing different types of numerical issues, such as absent volume-fraction diffusion
[
11
,
12
,
13
]
or poor mass conservation
[
9
]
. Some attempts have also been made towards decoupling the phases via fractional stepping
[
2
,
8
,
14
]
, but until very recently nothing beyond purely heuristic approaches. In fact, some existing fractional-step schemes even induce
parabolic
CFL conditions
[
6
,
13
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the first provenly stable fractional-step scheme for dispersed multiphase flows was just recently proposed
[
10
]
. Although that method decouples the phases while still being energy stable, it introduces an auxiliary pressure for each phase, which increases the number of unknowns and becomes increasingly less efficient as the number of phases grows.

This work improves our previous approach
[
10
]
in three important ways: (1) our new scheme has only one pressure Poisson equation regardless of the number of phases, (2) we devise a simpler, fully explicit treatment of the drag term, and (3) our new IMEX treatment of the viscous term allows splitting even each velocity update into a series of scalar subproblems. The scheme presented here can be seen as an (IMEX) extension of the popular incremental pressure-correction method to multiphase flows. As a matter of fact, our inspiration comes from different families of fractional-step methods for incompressible flows
[
15
,
16
]
. The resulting scheme allows using single-phase Navier–Stokes solvers – or even
scalar
advection-diffusion-reaction solvers – as building blocks for the multiphase system. Moreover, each subproblem is linearised, which further simplifies implementation and improves both efficiency and robustness (the issue of the sometimes delicate convergence of Newton or Picard schemes is eliminated). These advantages are attained without sacrificing numerical stability, which we rigorously prove to be unconditional with respect to the time-step size. While the focus here is on the temporal discretisation, our scheme should be relatively simple to implement within various spatial frameworks, especially finite element-based ones.

We organise the rest of this article as follows. Section
2
introduces the model problem and a consistent reformulation aimed at improved numerical stability in the discrete case. The numerical method is derived in Section
3
, where consistency aspects are also discussed. The unconditional stability is then proved in Section
4
. In Section
5
we provide numerical examples assessing the accuracy and the stability of our new method, before drawing concluding remarks in Section
6
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Model problem

The equations tackled here can be derived by volume-averaging the Navier–Stokes equations for each phase and making certain simplifying and modelling assumptions. While the derivation is out of our scope, details can be found in the literature
[
2
,
9
,
17
]
. For a time interval
(0,T]
and a domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=2
or
3
, the balance equations for the flow of the
k
th phase in
Q:=\Omega\times(0,T]
are

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})=0\,,

(1)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}[\partial_{t}(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-2\alpha_{k}\nu_{k}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]+\alpha_{k}\nabla p+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\gamma_{kl}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l})=\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}\,,

(2)

for
k=1,\dots,M
, with all the
M
phases occupying
Q
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}\equiv 1\,.

(3)

The unknowns are a single pressure
p
,
M
phase velocities
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
and
M
volume fractions
\alpha_{k}
. The model parameters for each phase are a kinematic viscosity
\nu_{k}>0
, a (constant) density
\rho_{k}
, a volumetric force
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}
, and the drag coefficients
\gamma_{kl}\geq 0
, which may depend on a combination of
\alpha_{k}
,
\alpha_{l}
and
|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}|
. Because
\gamma_{kk}=0
and
\gamma_{kl}=\gamma_{lk}
, only
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}M(M-1)
(not
M^{2}
) drag coefficients are needed. To have
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
defined everywhere in
\Omega
, the phase fractions are assumed to satisfy
\alpha_{k}\in(0,1)
.

2.2
Equivalent reformulation

An essential ingredient for the stability of the numerical scheme that will be presented later is a consistent reformulation of all the governing equations. Our motivation for rewriting Eq. (
1
) is that most stability results for advection equations rely on incompressibility assumptions—which we cannot use here, since the phase velocities
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
need not be solenoidal. We therefore introduce a new variable
\varphi_{k}:=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}
, so that substituting
\alpha_{k}=(\varphi_{k})^{2}
into Eq. (
1
) gives us

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})

\displaystyle\equiv\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\alpha_{k}+(\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}[(\varphi_{k})^{2}]+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla[(\varphi_{k})^{2}]+(\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})(\varphi_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle=2\varphi_{k}\left(\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}+\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}}{2}\varphi_{k}\right),

which we divide by
2\varphi_{k}
to get our new equation (
4
). Integrating over
\Omega
yields, in the absence of in- or outflow (
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=0
on
\partial\Omega
),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}2\varphi_{k}\Big(\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}+\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}}{2}\varphi_{k}\Big)\mathrm{d}\Omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\partial_{t}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla(\varphi_{k})^{2}+\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\Omega}\nabla\cdot[(\varphi_{k})^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}]\,\mathrm{d}\Omega

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega+\int_{\partial\Omega}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\,\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\int_{\Omega}(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\Omega\,,

that is, the
L^{2}(\Omega)
norm of
\varphi_{k}
is conserved over time. As we will see later, this stability can be inherited at the discrete level depending on the time-stepping scheme.

For the next reformulation, we multiply Eq. (
1
) by
-\frac{1}{2}\rho_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
and add the result to Eq. (
2
), so as to have

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\otimes\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})-\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})+\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+[\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\,,

with the notation
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}:=(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$})\mbox{\boldmath$v$}
. This conservative form is important to guarantee temporal stability for the velocities, as will be shown in Section
4
.

As our final modification, we add up Eqs. (
1
) for all
M
phases:

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{t}(1)

\displaystyle=0\,,

thanks to (
3
). This divergence-free condition on the mean velocity
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}:=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
will be used instead of the algebraic constraint (
3
).

Finally, denoting
\mu_{k}:=\rho_{k}\nu_{k}
, our
consistently
modified system reads

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varphi_{k}+\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}+\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}}{2}\varphi_{k}=0\,,\ \alpha_{k}=(\varphi_{k})^{2}\,,\quad k=1,\dots,M\,,

(4)

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\bigg[\alpha_{k}\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\bigg]-\nabla\cdot(2\alpha_{k}\mu_{k}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})+\alpha_{k}\nabla p

\displaystyle+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\gamma_{kl}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l})=\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}\,,\quad k=1,\dots,M\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=0\,,

(6)

equipped with appropriate initial and boundary conditions. This reformulation will be the basis for our numerical scheme.

We consider standard notation for Hilbert spaces. The

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
, with
\|\cdot\|
and

, for example, denotes the approximation of

at

\displaystyle 2\langle a+b,a\rangle

\displaystyle=\|a\|^{2}-\|b\|^{2}+\|a+b\|^{2}\,.

Moreover, provided that
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$n$}=0

\displaystyle\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\cdot\nabla r+\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}{2}r,r\right\rangle

3
Temporal discretisation

3.1
Convective and viscous terms

To construct the time-stepping scheme, we will approximate the temporal derivatives with first-order finite differences, while using first-order extrapolations (e.g.,
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\approx\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}
) to avoid nonlinearities and terms that would induce undesired coupling if treated implicitly. We discretise the convective and acceleration terms as follows:

\displaystyle\left.\left\{\alpha_{k}\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\frac{1}{2}[\partial_{t}\alpha_{k}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}\right\}\right|_{t=t_{n+1}}

\displaystyle\approx\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\tau}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})+\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}-\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\tau}+\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})\right]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n}}{2\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}+\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\,.

The explicit
vs.
implicit treatment of each of the terms above is precisely tailored to promote decoupling while maintaining unconditional stability, as proved in Section
4
.

Although the viscous term
2\alpha_{k}\mu_{k}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
does not couple the
M
phase velocities, if made entirely implicit it has the (admittedly minor) disadvantage of coupling the
d
spatial components of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
, due to the transpose gradient in
\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}+\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})
. To allow a component-wise solution of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
by solving scalar subproblems, we propose the first-order extrapolation

\displaystyle(2\alpha_{k}\mu_{k}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})|_{t=t_{n+1}}

\displaystyle=\mu_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1})

\displaystyle=\mu_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\mu_{k}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}

\displaystyle\approx\mu_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\mu_{k}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\,.

We will show later that this IMEX treatment, first proposed in the context of single-phase variable-viscosity flows
[
20
]
, is stable in time.

Remark 3.1

There are applications where some of the viscosities
\nu_{k}
may depend on
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
and/or on
p
, as in dense granular flows
[
21
]
. In that scenario, to avoid viscous nonlinearities, one can use a “nearly implicit” approach extrapolating only the viscosities:

\displaystyle\left.(2\rho\nu_{k}\alpha_{k}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k})\right|_{t=t_{n+1}}\approx 2\rho_{k}\nu_{k}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\,.

Making the viscosity explicit as above, which is compatible with the
\mathcal{O}(\tau)
consistency of our framework, yields essentially the same stability as a fully implicit treatment.

3.2
Numerical method

To really decouple the phases, we must treat both the pressure and the drag forces explicitly. For the pressure, we propose a fractional-step scheme that can be seen as a multiphase extension of a recently proposed projection method for variable-density flows
[
16
]
. A key idea here is to use a first-order extrapolation of the pressure term:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla p^{n+1}\approx\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}\,.

We also introduce a modification of the drag coefficients:

\displaystyle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}:=\min\left\{\gamma_{kl}^{n},D\right\},

where
D>0
is a large, but finite constant. This is a technical assumption needed for the analysis, with probably little practical relevance.

We will consider Dirichlet boundary conditions for the momentum equations (
5
):

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{k}^{D}\ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\times(0,T]

\displaystyle\,.

The scheme presented below can also be used for the free-slip case (
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=0
, with the tangential component left “free”). For open (Neumann) boundaries, however, a modified scheme would be required, which is a challenging task that will not be addressed herein.

For the volume fractions, inflow boundary conditions are needed:

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{n}=a_{k}^{n}\ \ \text{on}\ \Gamma_{\mathrm{in}}^{n}:=\{\mbox{\boldmath$x$}\in\partial\Omega\ \ \mathrm{where}\ \,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}<0\}.

Finally, we propose the following
pressure-correction
method:

1.

Step 0:
Set
\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{0}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{0}
and
\varphi_{k}^{0}=\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{0}}
, and provide (an approximation for)
p^{0}
.

2.

Step 1:
Find
\varphi^{n+1}_{k}
as the solution of

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\tau\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+(\frac{\tau}{2}\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})\varphi_{k}^{n+1}=\varphi_{k}^{n}\\
\left.\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\right|_{\Gamma_{\mathrm{in}}^{n}}=\sqrt{a_{k}^{n+1}}\end{cases}

(12)

for each
k=1,\dots,M
, then update the volume fractions
\alpha_{k}^{n+1}=(\varphi_{k}^{n+1})^{2}
.

3.

Step 2:
Find
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}_{k}
by solving the linear advection-diffusion-reaction problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\rho_{k}\Big[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n}}{2\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\Big]-\nabla\cdot(\mu_{k}\alpha^{n+1}_{k}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}_{k})\\
=\rho_{k}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\tau}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}+\nabla\cdot\Big(\mu_{k}\sqrt{\alpha^{n+1}_{k}\alpha^{n}_{k}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}_{k}\Big)-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}\\
+\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}^{n+1}-\sum_{l=1}^{M}\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\\
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}|_{\partial\Omega}=\left.\mathbf{u}_{k}^{D}\right|_{t=t_{n+1}}\end{cases}

(13)

for each
k=1,\dots,M
(the viscous term could alternatively be treated implicitly). Notice that the so-called
end-of-step velocities

(\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{n}_{k},\hat{\mathbf{u}}^{n}_{l})
are used in the first and last terms on the right-hand side of the momentum equation.

4.

Step 4:
Find the pressure
p^{n+1}
through the Poisson problem

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\nabla\cdot\left[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\Big)\nabla p^{n+1}\right]\\
=\nabla\cdot\left[\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\Big)\nabla p^{n}\right]+\frac{1}{\tau}\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\\
\left.\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\Big)\partial_{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$n$}}p^{n+1}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=\Big(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\left.\Big)\partial_{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$n$}}p^{n}\right|_{\partial\Omega}\end{cases}

(14)

5.

Step 5:
Update each end-of-step velocity via

\displaystyle\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{\tau}{\rho_{k}}\left(\sqrt{\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\,\nabla p^{n}-\nabla p^{n+1}\right)\,.

(15)

Notice that combining Eqs. (
14
) and (
15
) yields

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}=0\ \ \text{and}\ \ \mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})|_{\partial\Omega}=0\,,

(16)

that is, the volume-averaged end-of-step velocity is indeed a divergence-free projection.

Not only are the steps for each phase completely independent and decoupled, but they are also linear. We need to solve
M
advection-reaction equations to find all the
\varphi_{k}^{n+1}
, then
M
advection-diffusion-reaction problems to compute all the
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}
, and finally a single pressure Poisson equation (PPE). Moreover, due to IMEX treatment of the convective and viscous terms, each of the momentum equations can be broken into
d
(2 or 3)
scalar
advection-diffusion-reaction problems (one for each spatial component of
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}
). With that, the implementation of our algorithm is probably as simple as possible for such a complex, large flow system. Another fact sometimes understated is the additional robustness brought by the linearity of the scheme. Here, there is no issue of fixed-point convergence: for any
\tau
the solution of
every
time step converges—provided, of course, that the discretisation scheme itself is stable, which we will soon prove.

Remark 3.2

Steps 4 and 5 could be rewritten as a Darcy-like system, as often done in the derivation of pressure-correction methods
[
10
]
. However, solving such a coupled system would be impractical, so we implement and analyse the PPE formulation presented above.

Remark 3.3

From Eq. (
14
) we have that

\displaystyle\partial_{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$n$}}p^{n+1}=\left(\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}}{\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\right)\partial_{\mbox{\boldmath\scriptsize$n$}}p^{n}\ \ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega\,,

which means that, as most standard pressure-correction schemes do, ours induces an artificial pressure boundary condition. This tends to create a numerical boundary layer for large time-step sizes. Similarly, the end-of-step velocities
\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}
do not exactly retain the Dirichlet boundary conditions enforced on
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}
. These artifacts prevent standard projection methods from being high-order accurate.

3.3
Interpretation as a penalty-like method

While convergence analysis is not in the present scope, we will sketch how our projection scheme can be reinterpreted as a penalty-like method, which will also offer insights into its order of consistency. Evaluating Eq. (
15
) at
t=t_{n}
allows us to eliminate the term
\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}
from Eq. (
13
), leaving us with

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\Big[\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n}}{2\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\Big]-\nabla\cdot(\mu_{k}\alpha^{n+1}_{k}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n+1}_{k})

\displaystyle=\rho_{k}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n}}{\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}+\nabla\cdot\Big(\mu_{k}\sqrt{\alpha^{n+1}_{k}\alpha^{n}_{k}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}^{n}_{k}\Big)+\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})

\displaystyle-\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}+\alpha_{k}^{n}\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{n-1}}\,\nabla p^{n-1}\right).

Although maybe not obvious at first sight, the pressure terms on the right-hand side yield a second-order extrapolation of
-\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla p^{n+1}
. To show that, let us denote
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}=\varphi
and
\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\nabla p=\mbox{\boldmath$a$}
, so that Taylor expansion about
t_{n}
gives us

\displaystyle\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}+\alpha_{k}^{n}\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\alpha_{k}^{n-1}}\,\nabla p^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\varphi^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}+\varphi^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}-\varphi^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n-1}

\displaystyle=\big[\varphi^{n}+\tau(\partial_{t}\varphi)|_{t=t_{n}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})\big]\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}+\varphi^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}-\varphi^{n}\big[\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}-\tau(\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|_{t=t_{n}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})\big]

\displaystyle=\varphi^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{n}+\tau\big[(\mbox{\boldmath$a$}\partial_{t}\varphi)|_{t=t_{n}}+(\varphi\partial_{t}\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|_{t=t_{n}}\big]+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})

\displaystyle=(\varphi\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|_{t=t_{n}}+\tau[\partial_{t}(\varphi\mbox{\boldmath$a$})]|_{t=t_{n}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})

\displaystyle=(\varphi\mbox{\boldmath$a$})|_{t=t_{n+1}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla p^{n+1}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})\,.

Moreover, denoting
\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}
, the pressure eq. (
14
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\tau\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}}{\tau}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau^{2}\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\big[\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\partial_{t}(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\nabla p)\big]|_{t=t_{n+1}}+\mathcal{O}(\tau^{3})\,,

that is, as an
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})
perturbation (the term proportional to
\tau^{2}
dominates the cubic one) of the incompressibility constraint on the mean velocity. In other words, the fractional-step method itself – which need not necessarily be treated in the IMEX fashion we have proposed – can be seen as a (first-order) discretisation of the
\mathcal{O}(\tau^{2})
-perturbed system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\text{Transport eqs.\penalty\ \eqref{mass2}}\,,\\
\text{Momentum eqs.\penalty\ \eqref{momentum2}}\,,\\
\nabla\cdot\bar{\mbox{\boldmath$u$}}-\tau^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}^{-1}\nabla\cdot\left[\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\partial_{t}(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}}\,\nabla p)\right]=0\,.\end{cases}

This indicates that it could be possible to extend our method to order two in time, although guaranteeing stability of such a scheme could be considerably more difficult.

Remark 3.4

While it is relatively common practice in single-phase flows
[
20
,
22
]
to solve the reformulated system (without the end-of-step velocities), we keep
\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}
for two reasons: it is used in the explicit drag terms, and stability may become very difficult to prove for the reduced system, see e.g. the analysis for incompressible variable-density flows
[
23
]
.

Remark 3.5

In the single-phase case, the perturbation to the incompressibility constraint would reduce to the well-known term
-\tau^{2}\partial_{t}\Delta p
, as in the incremental pressure-correction method
[
24
]
.

3.4
Weak formulation

The method presented in Steps 1–5 is meant as a general time-stepping scheme that, in principle, may be combined with any spatial discretisation of choice. For the case of finite element methods, each subproblem needs to be written in weak form. Without dwelling with finite element formalism, let us simply assume
X_{h},Y_{h},Z_{h}
as globally continuous,
H^{1}
-conforming finite element spaces. Dirichlet or inlet boundary conditions will be omitted, for concision, so it is implied that test and trial functions satisfy the respective boundary conditions. The weak substeps are thus as follows:

1.

Step 1:
for each
k=1,\dots,M
, find
\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\in Z_{h}
such that

\left\langle\varphi_{k}^{n+1}-\varphi_{k}^{n}+\tau\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\tau\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}}{2}\varphi_{k}^{n+1},\phi+\chi\tau\Big(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\phi+\frac{\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}}{2}\phi\Big)\right\rangle=0

(17)

for all
\phi\in Z_{h}
, where
\chi=0
indicates the standard Galerkin method, whereas
\chi=1
leads to the least-squares Galerkin formulation. The latter is symmetric and usually more stable in the presence of sharp gradients
[
25
]
.

After updating
\varphi_{k}
, we simply evaluate
\alpha_{k}^{n+1}=(\varphi_{k}^{n+1})^{2}
.

2.

Step 2:
for each
k=1,\dots,M
, find
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\in X_{h}
such that

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\left\langle\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n}}{2\tau}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\rangle+\langle\mu_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\rho_{k}}{\tau}\langle\alpha_{k}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle+\left\langle p^{n},\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right)\right\rangle-\left\langle\mu_{k}\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n},\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\rangle

(18)

\displaystyle+\rho_{k}\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\rangle-\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\right\rangle

for all
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}\in X_{h}
.

3.

Step 3:
find
p^{n+1}\in Y_{h}
such that

\left\langle\nabla p^{n+1},\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}{\rho_{k}}\right)\nabla q\right\rangle=\left\langle\nabla p^{n},\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}}{\rho_{k}}\right)\nabla q\right\rangle-\frac{1}{\tau}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}),q\right\rangle

(19)

for all
q\in Y_{h}
.

4.

Step 5:
for each
k=1,\dots,M
, find
\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\in X_{h}
such that

\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle=\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1},\mathbf{v}\right\rangle+\frac{\tau}{\rho_{k}}\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\mathbf{v}\right\rangle

(20)

for all
\mathbf{v}\in X_{h}
.

4
Temporal stability analysis

We will next analyse the stability of our IMEX fractional-step scheme. Although we consider the discrete-in-time case without addressing spatial discretisation, the analysis also applies to conforming finite element spaces. As usual, we assume homogeneous Dirichlet conditions
\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}|_{\partial\Omega}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
and, for concision, consider
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
for the analysis.

Theorem 4.1
(Stability of the volume fractions)

For any time-step size
\tau>0
, scheme (
17
) yields

\displaystyle\|\varphi_{k}^{N}\|^{2}+\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left(\|\delta\varphi_{k}^{n}+\chi\tau\psi_{k}^{n}\|^{2}+\chi\tau^{2}\|\psi_{k}^{n}\|^{2}\right)=\|\varphi_{k}^{0}\|^{2}\,,

(21)

where
\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1}:=\varphi_{k}^{n+1}-\varphi_{k}^{n}
and
\psi_{k}^{n+1}:=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}\nabla\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\varphi_{k}^{n+1}
. This implies

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{k}^{N}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|\alpha_{k}^{0}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\,,

(22)

since
\alpha_{k}^{N}=(\varphi_{k}^{N})^{2}
.

Proof.
We set
\phi=2\tau\varphi_{k}^{n+1}
in (
17
) to get

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1},\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\chi\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\left\langle\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1},\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+2\chi\left\langle\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1},\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+2\chi\|\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}+2\tau\left\langle\psi_{k}^{n+1},\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\varphi_{k}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}+2\chi\langle\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1},\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\rangle+2\chi\|\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}+2\tau\langle\psi_{k}^{n+1},\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\rangle\,,

where we have used (
7
). The last term on the right-hand side is zero due to identity (
8
). Moreover, since
\chi\in\{0,1\}
, we can write
\chi=\chi^{2}
. Hence:

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\varphi_{k}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}+2\chi\left\langle\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1},\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+2\chi^{2}\|\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\varphi_{k}^{n}\|^{2}+\|\delta\varphi_{k}^{n+1}+\chi\tau\psi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}+\chi^{2}\tau^{2}\|\psi_{k}^{n+1}\|^{2}\,,

which when added up from
n=0
to
n=N-1
completes the proof.

To prove the stability of the velocity-pressure system, we will need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
(Estimate on the explicit drag terms)

Assuming that all the volume fractions fulfil the lower bound

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}^{n}\geq\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}>0\ \text{a.e.\penalty\ in}\ \Omega\,,\ \text{for all}\ \,n=0,\dots,N\ \text{and all}\ \,k=1,\dots,M\,,

(23)

and denoting as
\rho_{\mathrm{min}}
the minimum density among
\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{M}
, the drag terms will satisfy

\displaystyle 2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle\geq\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle-\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|^{2}+[2\tau(M-1)\beta]^{2}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\},

(24)

where

\displaystyle\beta:=\max_{m=0,\dots,n}\left\{\max_{k,l=1,\dots,M}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\rho_{k}\rho_{l}}}\left\|\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{m}}{\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{m}\alpha_{l}^{m}}}\right\|_{\infty}\right\}\leq\frac{D}{\rho_{\mathrm{min}}\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}}\,.

(25)

Proof.
Since
\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}=\tilde{\gamma}_{lk}^{n}
, we can write

\displaystyle 2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+\tau\sum_{l=1}^{M}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{lk}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle-\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})+(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})-(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}+\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})-(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\rangle\,.

The first term on the right-hand side is non-negative, so it remains to estimate the other term. Recalling that
\tilde{\gamma}_{kk}=0
and using Hölder’s inequality, we can write

\displaystyle\tau\left|\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})-(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\tau\left|\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left(\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\rangle-\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{l}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}\right\rangle\right)\right|

\displaystyle=2\tau\left|\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=2\tau\left|\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}{\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}\rho_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n}}}\sqrt{\rho_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n},\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|\left\|\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\right\|_{\infty}+\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}\right\|\left\|\frac{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}{\sqrt{\rho_{k}\rho_{l}\alpha_{k}^{n}\alpha_{l}^{n}}}\right\|_{\infty}\right)\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|

\displaystyle\leq 2\tau\beta\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{M}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|+\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}\right\|\right)\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|,

introducing the positive constant
\beta
, defined in (
25
). Let us now use the short-hand notation
a_{k}=\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|
,
b_{k}=\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|
. Using now Young’s inequality, we get

\displaystyle 2\tau\beta\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{M}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|+\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{l}\alpha_{l}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}\right\|\right)\left\|\sqrt{\rho_{k}\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|

\displaystyle=2\tau\beta\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{M}(a_{k}b_{k}+a_{l}b_{k})

\displaystyle=2\tau\beta\sum_{k=1}^{M}(M-1)a_{k}b_{k}+2\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{M}\tau\beta a_{l}b_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\frac{(b_{k})^{2}}{2}+2[\tau\beta(M-1)a_{k}]^{2}\right\}+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}l=1\\
l\neq k\end{subarray}}^{M}\left\{\frac{(b_{k})^{2}}{2(M-1)}+2(M-1)(\tau\beta a_{l})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\left[\frac{(b_{k})^{2}}{2}+2(\tau\beta(M-1)a_{k})^{2}\right]+\left[(M-1)\frac{(b_{k})^{2}}{2(M-1)}+(M-1)2(M-1)(\tau\beta a_{k})^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{(b_{k})^{2}+(2\tau\beta(M-1)a_{k})^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|^{2}+[2\tau(M-1)\beta]^{2}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\},

which when combined with the two previous estimates completes the proof.

Theorem 4.3
(Stability of velocities and pressure)

Under the assumptions of Lemma
4.2
, for any time-step size
\tau=T/N>0
the fractional-step scheme (
13
),(
14
),(
15
) with
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{k}=\mbox{\boldmath$0$}
(
k=1,\dots,M
) satisfies the stability estimate

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{N}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{N}\right\|^{2}+\tau\mu_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{N}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{N}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{N}}\nabla p^{N}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[\mu_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\alpha_{k}^{n-1}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n-1}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n-1}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n-1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n-1})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{k=1}^{M}B_{k}\right)\exp\left\{{4\bigg[\frac{D(M-1)}{\rho_{\mathrm{min}}\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}}\bigg]^{2}T\tau}\right\}\,,

(26)

where

\displaystyle B_{k}=\Bigg\{{1+\bigg[\frac{2D(M-1)\tau}{\rho_{\mathrm{min}}\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}}\bigg]^{2}}\Bigg\}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{0}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{0}\right\|^{2}+\tau\mu_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{0}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{0}\right\|^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{0}}\nabla p^{0}\right\|^{2}.

(27)

Proof.
We start by setting
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}
in Eq. (
20
) and using the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality:

\displaystyle\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\tau}{\rho_{k}}\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})\right\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\tau}{\rho_{k}}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})\right\|\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}\right\|\,,

so that

\displaystyle\sqrt{\rho_{k}}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})\right\|\leq\frac{\tau}{\sqrt{\rho_{k}}}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}\right\|\,.

(28)

Taking
q=\tau p^{n+1}
in (
19
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\tau}{\rho_{k}}\left\langle\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle-\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}),p^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\nabla\cdot[\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1})],p^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}),\nabla p^{n+1}\right\rangle,

where we have used (
16
). Rewriting the left-hand side by using (
7
) and (
28
) yields

\displaystyle 2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}),\nabla p^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1}-\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{k=1}^{N}\left[\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}\right)+\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1})\right\|^{2}\right].

(29)

Next, we set
\mathbf{v}=2\rho_{k}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}
in (
20
) to get

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\tau\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle-2\tau\left\langle\nabla p^{n+1},\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle.

Adding over all
M
phases yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle-2\tau\left\langle\nabla p^{n+1},\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle\right)

\displaystyle=2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle-2\tau\left\langle\nabla p^{n+1},\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+2\tau\left\langle p^{n+1},\nabla\cdot\sum_{k=1}^{M}\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}\,}\,\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle,

(30)

again thanks to (
16
). This, combined with estimate (
29
), yields

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}+2\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\nabla p^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}),\nabla p^{n+1}\right\rangle-\left\langle p^{n},\nabla\cdot\left(\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\rangle\right].

(31)

Let us now take
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}=2\tau\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}
in (
18
), which gives us several terms:

1.

Acceleration terms:

\displaystyle\rho_{k}\left\langle(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}+\alpha_{k}^{n})\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-2\alpha_{k}^{n}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}+\rho_{k}\left\langle\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-2\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n},\alpha_{k}^{n}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\rho_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|^{2}\right)\,.

(32)

2.

Convective terms:

\displaystyle 2\tau\left\langle\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\cdot\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\frac{1}{2}[\nabla\cdot(\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n})]\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle=0\,,

(33)

due to the skew-symmetry result (
9
).

3.

Viscous terms:

\displaystyle 2\tau\mu_{k}\left\langle\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\tau\mu_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\tau\mu_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right).

(34)

4.

Drag terms:

\displaystyle-2\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\langle\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n}),\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle\leq-\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}(\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n})\right\|^{2}+[2\tau(M-1)\beta]^{2}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\},

(35)

as proved in Lemma
4.2
.

Combining estimates (
32
)–(
35
) yields, for the momentum equation,

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\rho_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)+\tau\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\tau\mu_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{-2\tau\left\langle\nabla p^{n},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\rangle+[2\tau(M-1)\beta]^{2}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\}.

Adding that to estimate (
31
) and defining
\alpha=[2\tau(M-1)\beta]^{2}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}\left(2\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\mu_{k}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\left(\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\right\|^{2}-\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}}\nabla p^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)+\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{2\tau\left\langle\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla p^{n}-\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla p^{n+1},\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1})\right\rangle+\alpha\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{2\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\left(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\right)\right\|^{2}+\alpha\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right\},

(36)

due to (
20
). Then, the first term on the left-hand side cancels out the first one on the right-hand side. Rearranging some terms in (
36
), we have thus

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left[(\Psi_{k}^{n+1}-\Psi_{k}^{n})+\tau\mu_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}+\tau\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\sum_{k=1}^{M}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2},

(37)

in which
\Psi_{k}^{n+1}:=\rho_{k}\Big\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n+1}\Big\|^{2}+\tau\mu_{k}\Big\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n+1}\Big\|^{2}+\frac{\tau^{2}}{\rho_{k}}\Big\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\nabla p^{n+1}\Big\|^{2}\,.

Adding from
n=0
to
n=N-1
gives

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left\{\Psi_{k}^{N}+\tau\sum_{n=1}^{N}\left[\mu_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\alpha_{k}^{n-1}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n-1}\right\|^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\left\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n})\right\|^{2}\right]\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(\Psi_{k}^{0}+\alpha\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(B_{k}+\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\rho_{k}\left\|\sqrt{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n}\right\|^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=1}^{M}\left(B_{k}+\alpha\sum_{n=1}^{N-1}\Psi_{k}^{n}\right),

with
B_{k}
defined in (
27
). The proof is concluded by using the Gronwall Lemma
2.1
with

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{M}\Psi_{k}^{n}\,,\ \,b_{n}=\tau\sum_{k=1}^{M}\bigg(\mu_{k}\bigg\|\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n+1}}\alpha_{k}^{n}}\,\nabla\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n}+\sqrt{\vphantom{\alpha_{k}^{n}}\alpha_{k}^{n-1}}\,\nabla^{\top}\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}^{n-1}\bigg\|^{2}+\sum_{l=1}^{M}\bigg\|\sqrt{\tilde{\gamma}_{kl}^{n-1}}(\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{k}^{n-1}-\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{l}^{n-1})\bigg\|^{2}\bigg).

A crucial feature of the stability result (
26
) is that, as just seen, it is proved without invoking the volume-fraction equations or their stability, which is only possible due to the reformulation we used for the convective term. This leaves us free to discretise (and even stabilise) the volume fractions in a variety of ways.

Remark 4.1

For
\tau\lesssim\frac{\rho_{\mathrm{min}}\alpha_{\mathrm{min}}}{(M-1)D}
, it is possible to prove stability without the exponential factor seen in (
26
). That factor is in any case a mild one, as it tends to
1
as
\tau\rightarrow 0
.

5
Numerical examples

In this section, we assess the accuracy and the stability of our IMEX fractional-step scheme. All the tests were computed using quadrilateral Lagrangian finite elements with second-order interpolation for the velocities and first order for
p
and
\varphi_{k}
. Only two-phase (
M=2
) examples are solved, and the velocity boundary conditions are such that
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{k}=0
on
\partial\Omega
at all times, which fits our theory. Since there is no inflow through
\partial\Omega
, no boundary conditions are needed for the volume fractions. The equations for
\varphi_{k}
are formulated in the least-squares variant (
\chi=1
in (
17
)). The initial pressure
p^{0}
was computed as proposed in our recent work
[
10
]
.

5.3
Dispersed Rayleigh–Taylor flow

Our final experiment is inspired by the Rayleigh–Taylor instability, which is a popular benchmark for (separated) two-phase flows
[
18
]
. The standard setup considers two fluids initially at rest in
\Omega=(0,0.5)\times(-2,2)
, with the denser one on top. To adapt this problem to the dispersed setting, we consider the initial phase distribution as

\displaystyle\alpha_{2}^{0}=\frac{0.99+0.05}{2}+\frac{0.99-0.05}{2}\tanh\left(100y+10\cos 2\pi x\right),\ \ \alpha_{1}^{0}=1-\alpha_{2}^{0}\,,

which means the lighter fluid (2) occupies
99\%
of the lower part of
\Omega
and only
5\%
of the upper part, see Figure
1
(left). The lateral walls are free-slip boundaries, while the top and bottom ones have no slip. The system is under gravity
\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{1}=\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{2}=(0,-1)^{\top}
, and the fluid parameters are
\rho_{1}=1
,
\rho_{2}=3
,
\mu_{1}=0.1
and
\mu_{2}=0.3
. An Euler-type drag is considered:
\gamma_{12}=10\alpha_{2}|\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{2}-\mbox{\boldmath$u$}_{1}|
. The spatial mesh contains 80,000 square elements, and the time-step size is
\tau=0.005
.

The distribution of
\alpha_{2}
and
\gamma_{12}
at different times is shown in Figure
1
. As in the separated two-fluid case, a mushroom-like pattern forms over time. However, the dispersed nature of the present problem allows some of the lighter fluid to rise to the top and start collecting underneath the upper wall, which cannot happen in the separated case.

6
Concluding remarks

This work has presented an efficient IMEX splitting scheme for dispersed multiphase flow systems. While each fluid phase obeys its own mass and momentum equations, they are all coupled by the pressure and drag forces. Therefore, decoupling the phases (at each time step) requires treating those forces explicitly. The pressure splitting is a particularly delicate matter, even more so here than in single-phase flows. To achieve that, we have presented a pressure-correction method built upon the incompressibility of the mean (volume-fraction-weighted) velocity field. We have also proposed an explicit treatment of the drag term based on the projected velocities. Moreover, the convective nonlinearities are also eliminated through simple IMEX techniques. Finally, we also showed that one can – but does not have to – treat part of the viscous term explicitly to further decouple the spatial components of each phase velocity. The resulting scheme, which we have proved unconditionally stable, is considerably simpler to implement than an implicit one, as it is linearised and fully decoupled—as a matter of fact, only scalar sub-problems need to be solved. Although we focus on two-phase applications, our method becomes increasingly more attractive for more fluids, since only one pressure problem is solved regardless of the number of phases. Of course, our framework has limitations. For instance, its extension to second order may be challenging to construct, or at least to prove stable. Even first-order convergence may prove very difficult to attain in the presence of open boundaries, where standard pressure-correction methods usually fail even for single-phase flows
[
26
]
. Also missing is a rigorous convergence analysis of our method, especially in the fully-discrete setting using, for example, finite elements in space.

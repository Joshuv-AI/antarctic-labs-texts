Hierarchical filtrations of vector bundles and birational geometry
Rahim Rahmati-asghar

Abstract
. We introduce and systematically study hierarchical filtrations of vector bundles on smooth projective varieties. These are filtrations by saturated subsheaves of equal rank whose successive quotients are torsion sheaves supported in codimension one. The associated numerical invariant, called hierarchical depth , measures the maximal length of such filtrations. We establish general bounds for hierarchical depth in terms of the determinant class and provide exact formulas for smooth curves and varieties of Picard rank one. A key technical result concerns the commutativity of elementary transforms along disjoint divisors and their role in constructing filtrations. For surfaces, we analyze the behavior of hierarchical depth under birational morphisms and prove that it transforms additively along the steps of the minimal model program. In particular, we obtain an explicit formula relating the depth on a surface to that on its minimal model via exceptional divisor contributions. As an application, we connect hierarchical depth to degeneracies in algebraic–geometric codes and show that birational simplification via the MMP leads to effective improvements of code parameters. This establishes hierarchical depth as a new bridge between birational geometry, vector bundle theory, and coding theory.

Introduction

Filtrations of vector bundles are fundamental tools in algebraic geometry, from Grothendieck’s foundational work on coherent sheaves
[
4
]
to the Harder–Narasimhan filtration in stability theory
[
7
]
. While stability filtrations depend on a choice of polarization, there remains a need for intrinsic, polarization–free filtrations that reflect divisorial and birational properties.

This paper introduces a new filtration–theoretic framework for torsion–free sheaves adapted precisely to birational geometry. We define
hierarchical filtrations
—chains of saturated subsheaves of equal rank with torsion quotients supported in codimension one—and their associated numerical invariant, the
hierarchical depth
. Unlike stability–theoretic invariants, hierarchical depth is independent of any polarization and governed instead by the determinant and the divisor theory of the underlying variety.

Our work builds on and substantially extends the hierarchical filtration theory for line bundles developed in
[
14
]
, where applications to algebraic–geometric codes were first explored. The present paper develops a comprehensive theory for arbitrary rank, investigates its interaction with birational geometry and the minimal model program, and establishes new connections to coding theory.

Main results

1.

We introduce hierarchical filtrations and hierarchical depth (Definition
2.1
), prove their finiteness (Proposition
2.4
), and give explicit formulas in basic geometric situations (Propositions
4.1
,
2.4
).

2.

We establish commutativity properties for elementary transforms along disjoint divisors (Lemma
3.2
, Corollary
3.4
), providing a concrete mechanism for constructing filtrations.

3.

For surfaces, we prove that hierarchical depth transforms additively under birational morphisms arising in the MMP (Theorem
5.6
). In particular, it admits an explicit description on minimal models with variation controlled by exceptional divisors.

4.

We relate hierarchical depth to slope stability (Theorem
6.1
) and show it is preserved under birational morphisms (Corollary
6.3
).

5.

As applications to coding theory, we demonstrate that hierarchical depth detects divisorial degeneracies affecting algebraic–geometric code parameters (Proposition
7.1
). Birational simplification via the MMP leads to effective improvements in both absolute and normalized minimum distances (Corollary
7.5
)

These results situate hierarchical depth as a genuinely birational invariant in the surface case and provide a new computational tool linking the geometry of vector bundles to arithmetic applications.

1.
Preliminaries

Conventions.
Throughout,
\mathbb{F}
denotes an algebraically closed field of arbitrary characteristic. All schemes are separated and of finite type over
\mathbb{F}
. A
variety
is an integral normal projective
\mathbb{F}
-scheme. Unless otherwise stated, varieties are assumed smooth; the restriction to dimension at most
2
applies only when invoking the minimal model program in Sections
5
and onward.

Divisors and linear equivalence.
For a variety
X
, denote by
\mathrm{Div}(X)
the group of Weil divisors and by
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
the Picard group. When
X
is smooth, Cartier and Weil divisors coincide. An effective divisor is written
D\geq 0
. Linear equivalence is denoted by
\sim
.

Sheaves and determinants.
All sheaves are coherent
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-modules. A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is
torsion-free
if it has no nonzero subsheaves supported in codimension
\geq 1
. A subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{E}
is
saturated
if
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
is torsion-free.

For a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
, its
determinant
is

\det(\mathcal{E}):=\bigl(\wedge^{r}\mathcal{E}\bigr)^{\vee\vee},

identified with the corresponding divisor class. If
\mathcal{F}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}
is an inclusion of torsion-free sheaves of equal rank with torsion quotient, there exists an effective divisor
D
(the
determinantal divisor
) such that

\det(\mathcal{F})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E})(-D).

Elementary transforms.
Let
\mathcal{E}
be a torsion-free sheaf on
X
and
D\subset X
an effective divisor. An
elementary transform
of
\mathcal{E}
along
D
is a sheaf
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
fitting into

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\mathcal{T}
is a torsion sheaf supported on
D
. Such transforms preserve rank and satisfy
\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E})(-D)
.

Birational morphisms.
For a proper birational morphism
f:Y\to X
between normal varieties:

•

The pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
of a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
may contain torsion; in practice we work with its torsion-free quotient.

•

The pushforward
f_{*}\mathcal{F}
of a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{F}
on
Y
is understood to be replaced by its saturation when viewed as a subsheaf on
X
.

These conventions ensure that all sheaves considered remain torsion-free and all inclusions saturated.

Cohomology and slopes.
We write
h^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})=\dim_{\mathbb{F}}H^{i}(X,\mathcal{F})
. For a smooth projective variety
X
with ample divisor
H
, the
slope
of a torsion-free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
is

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E})\cdot H^{\dim X-1}}{\mathrm{rank}\mathcal{E}}.

2.
Hierarchical filtration of vector bundles

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a field,
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
a reference line bundle (typically
\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
), and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle of rank
r
on
X
. A
hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
is a finite chain

\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E},

where for each
i=1,\dots,h
:

(i)

\mathcal{E}_{i}
is a vector bundle of rank
r
, and
\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}
is saturated;

(ii)

There exists a nonzero effective Cartier divisor
D_{i}
with
\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
;

(iii)

\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
.

Note that each one of two following statements are equivalent to condition (ii) of above definition:

(ii-1) the torsion sheaf
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
is supported (set-theoretically) on
D_{i}
, and

c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i})=c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})+[D_{i}]\ \in\mathrm{NS}(X).

(ii-2) locally on
X
the inclusion
\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}
is obtained from
\mathcal{E}_{i-1}
by an elementary transform (Hecke modification) along the Cartier divisor
D_{i}
; equivalently there is an exact sequence

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathcal{T}_{i}\longrightarrow 0

with
\mathcal{T}_{i}
a torsion sheaf supported on
D_{i}
whose determinant (in the Knudsen-Mumford sense, or via the Fitting ideal) is
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
and
\Lambda_{0}
be as above. The
hierarchical depth of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to
\Lambda_{0}
, denoted
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
, is the maximal integer
h\geq 0
for which there exists a hierarchical filtration of length
h
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
. If no such finite filtration exists we set
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=-\infty
. We adopt the convention
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}_{0})=0
when
\mathcal{E}_{0}
already equals the chosen starting bundle (equivalently when the chain is trivial).

Remark 2.3
.

(i)

Normalization.
If one does
not
normalize the filtration (for instance by fixing
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})
), the notion of hierarchical filtration (and the numerical data
(D_{i})
) is only defined up to simultaneous twist of every term by a line bundle. In particular one can “renormalize” a filtration by tensoring the entire chain with
\mathcal{O}_{X}(-D)
and thereby create non-canonical shifts. Requiring
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
(for example
\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
) pins down this ambiguity and prevents the filtration from sliding to
-\infty
.

(ii)

Twist–invariance property.

The hierarchical depth is stable under tensor twists, provided the
normalization is adjusted accordingly:
for any
M\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
one has

(1)

h_{\Lambda_{0}\otimes M^{\otimes r}}(\mathcal{E}\otimes M)=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}),

where
r=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
.
Indeed, tensoring a normalized filtration of
\mathcal{E}
by
M
yields a
normalized filtration of
\mathcal{E}\otimes M
with respect to

\Lambda_{0}\otimes M^{\otimes r}
, since

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i}\otimes M)\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\otimes M^{\otimes r}
at every step.
Hence the numerical invariant
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
depends only on the
pair
(\det(\mathcal{E}),\Lambda_{0})
modulo this canonical renormalization.

(iii)

Intrinsic interpretation.

Equation (
1
) shows that the hierarchical depth
is an intrinsic invariant of the bundle
\mathcal{E}
up to tensor equivalence,
analogous to how the slope
\mu(\mathcal{E})=\deg(\mathcal{E})/\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
is invariant under
twists by degree-zero line bundles on curves. The normalization by

\Lambda_{0}
thus provides a canonical reference frame for measuring
divisorial increments in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
.

(iv)

Rank one case.
When
r=1
this definition recovers Definition 1.1 of
[
14
]
(with the same normalization choice
\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
if one so desires): then
\mathcal{E}_{i}
are line bundles and the determinant condition is the same as
\mathcal{E}_{i}\cong\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n
such that

\mathrm{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\cdot A
for some ample effective divisor
A
.
Fix a reference line bundle
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
and an ample polarization

\mathcal{O}_{X}(1)
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
,
and write

\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(dA)

for a unique integer
d\in\mathbb{Z}
. Write
\Lambda_{0}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}(d_{0}A)
.

Then any hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
has length bounded by

h\leq d-d_{0}.

In particular, when
\Lambda_{0}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
(so
d_{0}=0
) one has the uniform upper bound

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\leq d.

Moreover, equality
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=d-d_{0}
occurs whenever there exists a hierarchical
filtration of
\mathcal{E}
whose successive determinant increments are all linearly equivalent to
A
,
i.e. there exist effective divisors
D_{1},\dots,D_{d-d_{0}}\sim A
and a chain

\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{d-d_{0}}=\mathcal{E}

(normalized by
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
) with

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
for every
i
.

Proof.

Let

\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

be a hierarchical filtration normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
, so
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
.
For each
i
there is a nonzero effective Cartier divisor
D_{i}
with

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}).

Thus

\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{h}D_{i}\Big),

and in
\mathrm{NS}(X)

c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E})-c_{1}(\Lambda_{0})=\big[D_{1}+\cdots+D_{h}\big].

Under the hypothesis
\mathrm{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\cdot A
, each effective Cartier divisor

D_{i}
is linearly equivalent to a positive integer multiple of
A
. Hence
there exist integers
d_{i}\geq 1
such that

D_{i}\sim d_{i}A\qquad(i=1,\dots,h).

Therefore in
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
we have

\mathcal{O}_{X}(dA)\simeq\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\Lambda_{0}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(\sum_{i=1}^{h}D_{i}\Big)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(d_{0}A+\sum_{i=1}^{h}d_{i}A\Big),

so equality of divisor classes yields the integer identity

d=d_{0}+\sum_{i=1}^{h}d_{i}.

Since each
d_{i}\geq 1
we obtain

d-d_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}d_{i}\geq h,

which is exactly the desired inequality
h\leq d-d_{0}
.

The claim about equality is immediate: equality
h=d-d_{0}
holds precisely
when every
d_{i}=1
, i.e. when each determinant increment
D_{i}
is linearly
equivalent to
A
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 2.5
.

(1)

The upper bound
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\leq d-d_{0}
follows directly from the fact
that, under the hypothesis
\mathrm{Pic}(X)\cong\mathbb{Z}\cdot A
, every effective increment

D_{i}
satisfies
D_{i}\sim d_{i}A
with
d_{i}\geq 1
.
Thus

d-d_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{h}d_{i}\geq h,

showing that the maximal possible hierarchical depth depends only on the
difference of determinant classes
\det(\mathcal{E})
and
\Lambda_{0}
, but
not
on
the internal geometry of
\mathcal{E}
.
This is the exact analogue of the line–bundle case.

(2)

Unlike the line–bundle case, equality
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=d-d_{0}
is generally

not
automatic for vector bundles.
Equality would require that each increment divisor satisfy
D_{i}\sim A
, i.e.
that
d_{i}=1
for all
i
in the decomposition

D_{i}\sim d_{i}A,\qquad d_{i}\geq 1.

However, for a given bundle
\mathcal{E}
there may be no hierarchical filtration whose
determinant increments are all linearly equivalent to
A
.
Geometrically, this means that although
\mathrm{Pic}(X)
has rank one, the structure
of
\mathcal{E}
(e.g. its possible elementary transforms, base–locus behavior, or
torsion constraints) may force certain increments to have degree
d_{i}>1
,
reducing the achievable depth below the theoretical maximum
d-d_{0}
.

3.
Elementary transforms and commutativity

Definition 3.1

(Elementary transformation)
.

[
8
, Definition 5.2.1]
Let
X
be a smooth projective surface and
C
be an effective divisor on
X
. Let

i\colon C\hookrightarrow X
denote the embedding
C\subset X
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be a vector bundle on
X
and let
\mathcal{G}
be a vector bundle on
C
.
A coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
is called an

elementary transform of
\mathcal{F}
along
\mathcal{G}

if there exists a short exact sequence

(2)

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow i_{*}\mathcal{G}\longrightarrow 0.

It follows from Proposition 5.2.2 from
[
8
]
that
\mathcal{E}
is locally free and, moreover, it is a vector bundle since
X
is smooth.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Let
D_{1},D_{2}\subset X
be effective Cartier divisors with

\mathrm{Supp}(D_{1})\cap\mathrm{Supp}(D_{2})=\varnothing.

For
i=1,2
let
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
be a nonzero coherent torsion sheaf supported on
D_{i}

and let
\varphi_{i}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}_{i}
be surjective
\mathcal{O}_{X}
–linear maps. Define

V_{1}:=\ker(\varphi_{1}),\qquad V_{2}:=\ker(\varphi_{2}),

and the successive kernels

V_{12}:=\ker\big(V_{1}\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\varphi}_{2}\ }\mathcal{Q}_{2}\big),\qquad V_{21}:=\ker\big(V_{2}\xrightarrow{\ \overline{\varphi}_{1}\ }\mathcal{Q}_{1}\big),

where
\overline{\varphi}_{j}
denotes the map induced by
\varphi_{j}
on the
relevant kernel. Finally set

V:=\ker\big(\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\ (\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\ }\mathcal{Q}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{Q}_{2}\big).

Then the following hold.

(1)

As subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
we have

V_{12}=V_{21}=V.

In particular, the order of taking kernels (elementary transforms) with
respect to
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
does not affect the resulting subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}
.

(2)

If, in addition, the three kernels

V_{1},V_{2},V
are locally free , then the equalities in (1) are equalities of subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
. In this case we say that the corresponding elementary transforms commute and the order of the transforms is immaterial in the category of vector bundles.

Proof.

(1) Since
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{1})
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{2})
are disjoint, the two surjective
maps
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
impose independent conditions on local sections
of
\mathcal{E}
. Concretely, for any open set
U\subset X
we have

\displaystyle V(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{1}(s)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{Q}_{1}(U),\ \varphi_{2}(s)=0\text{ in }\mathcal{Q}_{2}(U)\},

\displaystyle V_{12}(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{1}(s)=0\text{ and }\varphi_{2}(s)=0\},

\displaystyle V_{21}(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{2}(s)=0\text{ and }\varphi_{1}(s)=0\}.

The equalities of the three sets above are immediate; hence the three
kernels coincide as subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
.

(2) If the kernels
V_{1},V_{2},V
are locally free, then the equalities of
subsheaves are equalities of locally free subsheaves (subbundles). In
particular all constructions may be carried out inside the category of
vector bundles and the elementary transforms commute as vector bundles.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

(1)
The disjointness hypothesis in Lemma
3.2
is essential. If
\mathrm{Supp}(D_{1})\cap\mathrm{Supp}(D_{2})\neq\varnothing

then the two quotient maps may interact on the overlap and the iterated
kernels need not coincide (indeed saturation may collapse intermediate steps).

(2)
The lemma is stated for arbitrary torsion quotients
\mathcal{Q}_{i}
supported on
the Cartier divisors. The additional local-freeness hypothesis in (2) is
exactly the bridge to the Huybrechts–Lehn notion of elementary transform
(
[
8
]
) where one typically takes
\mathcal{Q}_{i}=i_{*}(\mathcal{G}_{i})
for a
vector bundle
\mathcal{G}_{i}
on
D_{i}
; in that case the kernel is usually a
vector bundle.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Suppose a hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
is given by a finite sequence of
elementary transforms

\mathcal{E}=V_{0}\supset V_{1}\supset\cdots\supset V_{h},\qquad V_{k+1}=\ker\bigl(V_{k}\xrightarrow{\ \varphi_{k}\ }\mathcal{Q}_{k}\bigr),

where for each
k
the sheaf
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
is a nonzero torsion sheaf supported on an
effective Cartier divisor
D_{k}\subset X
.

Assume that the set of divisors
\{D_{k}\}
is partitioned into two
disjoint collections

\mathcal{A}=\{D_{a_{1}},\dots,D_{a_{r}}\},\qquad\mathcal{B}=\{D_{b_{1}},\dots,D_{b_{s}}\},

such that

\mathrm{Supp}(D_{a})\cap\mathrm{Supp}(D_{b})=\varnothing\qquad\text{for all }D_{a}\in\mathcal{A},\ D_{b}\in\mathcal{B}.

Then:

(1)

For every pair
D_{a}\in\mathcal{A}
and
D_{b}\in\mathcal{B}
, the elementary
transforms along
D_{a}
and
D_{b}
commute. More precisely, for any surjective
morphisms
\varphi_{a}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}_{a}
and
\varphi_{b}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}_{b}
with

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{a})\subset D_{a}
and
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{b})\subset D_{b}
, one has isomorphisms
of subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
:

\ker\Bigl(\ker(\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{a}}\mathcal{Q}_{a})\xrightarrow{\overline{\varphi}_{b}}\mathcal{Q}_{b}\Bigr)=\ker\Bigl(\ker(\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{b}}\mathcal{Q}_{b})\xrightarrow{\overline{\varphi}_{a}}\mathcal{Q}_{a}\Bigr)=\ker\Bigl(\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{\ (\varphi_{a},\varphi_{b})\ }\mathcal{Q}_{a}\oplus\mathcal{Q}_{b}\Bigr),

where
\overline{\varphi}_{j}
denotes the map induced on the kernel of

\varphi_{i}
.

(2)

By repeatedly interchanging adjacent transforms involving one divisor from

\mathcal{A}
and one from
\mathcal{B}
, the filtration can be reordered into
another hierarchical filtration of the same length
h
, in which all
transforms supported on divisors in
\mathcal{A}
occur before all transforms
supported on divisors in
\mathcal{B}
.

(3)

In particular, increments supported on disjoint divisors impose no obstruction
to achieving the maximal possible hierarchical depth: their order can be
rearranged arbitrarily without changing the total number of steps.

Proof.

We first prove (
1
).
Let
D_{a}\in\mathcal{A}
,
D_{b}\in\mathcal{B}
and suppose

\varphi_{a}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}_{a}
,
\varphi_{b}:\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{Q}_{b}
are surjective with

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{a})\subset D_{a}
,
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{b})\subset D_{b}
. Set

V_{a}:=\ker(\varphi_{a}),\qquad V_{b}:=\ker(\varphi_{b}).

Define the successive transforms

V_{ab}:=\ker\bigl(V_{a}\xrightarrow{\overline{\varphi}_{b}}\mathcal{Q}_{b}\bigr),\qquad V_{ba}:=\ker\bigl(V_{b}\xrightarrow{\overline{\varphi}_{a}}\mathcal{Q}_{a}\bigr),

and the simultaneous kernel

V:=\ker\bigl(\mathcal{E}\xrightarrow{(\varphi_{a},\varphi_{b})}\mathcal{Q}_{a}\oplus\mathcal{Q}_{b}\bigr).

Since
\mathrm{Supp}(D_{a})\cap\mathrm{Supp}(D_{b})=\varnothing
, the torsion sheaves
\mathcal{Q}_{a}
and

\mathcal{Q}_{b}
are supported on disjoint closed subsets. Therefore, for any section

s\in\mathcal{E}(U)
on an open set
U\subset X
,

\varphi_{a}(s)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\varphi_{b}(s)=0

are independent conditions. Consequently,

\displaystyle V(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{a}(s)=0,\ \varphi_{b}(s)=0\},

\displaystyle V_{ab}(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{a}(s)=0,\ \varphi_{b}(s)=0\},

\displaystyle V_{ba}(U)

\displaystyle=\{s\in\mathcal{E}(U):\varphi_{b}(s)=0,\ \varphi_{a}(s)=0\}.

Thus
V=V_{ab}=V_{ba}
as subsheaves of
\mathcal{E}
, proving commutativity.

We now prove (
2
).
Consider the given filtration

\mathcal{E}=V_{0}\supset V_{1}\supset\cdots\supset V_{h},\qquad V_{k+1}=\ker\bigl(V_{k}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{k}}\mathcal{Q}_{k}\bigr).

Label each index
k
as type
A
or type
B
according to whether

\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{k})\subset D_{a}\in\mathcal{A}
or
\mathrm{Supp}(\mathcal{Q}_{k})\subset D_{b}\in\mathcal{B}
. If a step of type
A
is immediately followed by a step of type
B
, say

V_{k}\supset V_{k+1}\supset V_{k+2},

with
V_{k+1}=\ker(V_{k}\to\mathcal{Q}_{a})
and
V_{k+2}=\ker(V_{k+1}\to\mathcal{Q}_{b})
, then by
the commutativity just proved, the composite kernel

\ker\bigl(V_{k}\to\mathcal{Q}_{a}\bigr)\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{Q}_{b}

is equal to

\ker\bigl(V_{k}\to\mathcal{Q}_{b}\bigr)\xrightarrow{}\mathcal{Q}_{a},

and hence the transforms may be exchanged:

\ker\bigl(V_{k}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{a}}\mathcal{Q}_{a}\bigr)\ \xrightarrow{\ \overline{\varphi}_{b}\ }\ Q_{b}\quad=\quad\ker\bigl(V_{k}\xrightarrow{\varphi_{b}}\mathcal{Q}_{b}\bigr)\ \xrightarrow{\ \overline{\varphi}_{a}\ }\ Q_{a}.

Thus one may swap the
A
– and
B
–steps without changing the resulting
subsheaf of
V_{k}
. By repeatedly swapping all out–of–order adjacent pairs
(type
A
followed by type
B
), one eventually obtains a filtration

\mathcal{E}=V^{\prime}_{0}\supset V^{\prime}_{1}\supset\cdots\supset V^{\prime}_{h}

in which all
A
–steps precede all
B
–steps. The length remains
h

because no step is removed during the swapping.

Finally, statement (
3
) is immediate:
reordering does not change the number of transform steps, hence disjoint
support never forces a reduction in hierarchical depth.

∎

Example 3.5
.

(1)

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
. Write
F:=\pi_{1}^{-1}(pt)
. Distinct fibers
F_{x}:=\pi_{1}^{-1}(x)
for
x\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
are pairwise disjoint effective Cartier divisors. Consider the split bundle

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\mathcal{O}_{X}(a_{j}F),\qquad a_{j}\geq 0,\qquad d:=\sum_{j=1}^{s}a_{j}.

For each summand
\mathcal{O}_{X}(a_{j}F)
choose
a_{j}
distinct points
x_{j,1},\dots,x_{j,a_{j}}\in\mathbb{P}^{1}
such that all points
x_{j,t}
are distinct as
(j,t)
vary, and write

a_{j}F=F_{x_{j,1}}+\cdots+F_{x_{j,a_{j}}}.

Each unit inclusion

\mathcal{O}_{X}((k{-}1)F)\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}(kF)

is realized by an elementary transform along the divisor
F_{x_{j,k}}
.
Since all fibers
F_{x_{j,t}}
have pairwise disjoint supports, Lemma
3.2
implies that the corresponding
elementary transforms commute, and hence Corollary
3.4
applies.

Thus the
d
unit increments coming from all summands may be interleaved
into a single saturated hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
.
Therefore

h_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\mathcal{E})=d.

Actually, the class
F
admits arbitrarily many pairwise disjoint effective representatives,
so all elementary transforms occur along disjoint supports and therefore commute.

(2)

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and let
H
be the hyperplane class.
Every effective divisor in
|H|
is a line, and any two distinct lines
meet in exactly one point.
Hence no two nonzero divisors in
|H|
have disjoint support.

For example, consider

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(aH),\qquad a\geq 2.

Although

\det(\mathcal{E})\simeq\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(aH)
,
the natural chain

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(H)\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(aH)

cannot be realized by
a
successive saturated elementary transforms inside
\mathcal{E}
.
Each such step must occur along a divisor of type
H
, but every pair of such
divisors intersects.
Thus the elementary transforms fail to commute, and repeated kernels interact
by saturation.

By Lemma
3.2
, only transforms along disjoint divisors commute; here all supports intersect, so
Corollary
3.4
does not apply.
Consequently the unit–increment strategy collapses, and one typically obtains
only a short saturated same–rank filtration such as

\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}^{\oplus 2}\subset\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(aH)\subset\mathcal{E},

of length
2
, with no possibility of refining it to length
a
.

Here the class
H
has no pairwise disjoint effective representatives, so
elementary transforms must occur along intersecting divisors and hence do not
commute; saturation then forces the filtration length to collapse.

Consequently, these two surfaces illustrate the dichotomy between the numerical determinant
bound
h(\mathcal{E})\leq d
and the geometric possibility of achieving equality.
If the linear system
|A|
contains sufficiently many pairwise disjoint
effective representatives (as on
\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}
), one may realize each unit
increment separately and obtain
h(\mathcal{E})=d
.
If
|A|
contains no such disjoint representatives (as on
\mathbb{P}^{2}
with
A=H
),
then geometric obstructions force noncommuting transforms and the maximal
hierarchical depth may be strictly smaller than
d
.

4.
Hierarchical depth and determinant bounds

In this section we collect basic numerical properties of hierarchical depth and establish general bounds in terms of the determinant. These results provide effective control of the invariant in simple geometric situations and will be used throughout the paper.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve and let

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})

be a split vector bundle of rank
r
, with integers
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Fix a normalization line bundle
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(C)
. Set

M:=\deg\det(\mathcal{E})-\deg(\Lambda_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}-\deg(\Lambda_{0}).

(a)

If
M<0
then there exists no hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}

normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
, and by convention
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=-\infty
.

(b)

If
M\geq 0
then the hierarchical depth of
\mathcal{E}
with respect to

\Lambda_{0}
equals

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=M.

In particular, when
\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{C}
one has

h(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}.

Proof.

We assume
M\geq 0
; the negative case (no filtration exists) is immediate
from the determinant relation and the normalization requirement.

Let

\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

be any hierarchical filtration normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
, i.e.
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
and for each
i

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(p_{i})

for some effective divisor
p_{i}
(a point of
C
, counted with multiplicity).
Taking determinants and degrees yields

\deg\det(\mathcal{E})-\deg(\Lambda_{0})=\sum_{i=1}^{h}\deg(p_{i})=h,

since each
p_{i}
is a single point (degree
1
). Hence
h\leq M
.

We now produce a hierarchical filtration of length exactly
M
.

Choose
M
distinct points
q_{1},\dots,q_{M}\in C
. (If the base field
is finite and
C(\mathbb{F}_{q})
has fewer than
M
rational points, work over
a finite extension or choose geometric points; the statement and proof
are unchanged up to base change.) For each
j
define inductively
a subsheaf by performing an elementary transform at
q_{j}
:

\mathcal{E}^{(M)}:=\mathcal{E},\quad\mathcal{E}^{(M-1)}:=\ker\bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(M)}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{M}\ }k(q_{M})\bigr),\quad\mathcal{E}^{(M-2)}:=\ker\bigl(\mathcal{E}^{(M-1)}\xrightarrow{\ \rho_{M-1}\ }k(q_{M-1})\bigr),

and so on, where each
\rho_{j}
is a chosen surjection of the fibre at
q_{j}

onto the one-dimensional skyscraper
k(q_{j})
.

On a curve the kernel of a surjection from a vector bundle to a skyscraper
is again locally free (torsion-free on a curve implies locally free),
so each
\mathcal{E}^{(t)}
is a vector bundle of rank
r
and sits as a saturated
subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}^{(t+1)}
. Moreover the determinant relation at each step
is

\det(\mathcal{E}^{(t+1)})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}^{(t)})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}(q_{t+1}).

After performing the
M
transforms we obtain

\det(\mathcal{E}^{(M)})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}^{(0)})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\Big).

Rearranging,

\det(\mathcal{E}^{(0)})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{C}\Big(-\sum_{j=1}^{M}q_{j}\Big).

By construction the right hand side is a line bundle of degree

\deg\det(\mathcal{E})-M=\deg\Lambda_{0}
; hence

\det(\mathcal{E}^{(0)})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
. Thus the ascending chain

\mathcal{E}^{(0)}\subset\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{(M)}=\mathcal{E}

is a hierarchical filtration normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
of length
M
.

It remains to check that the
M
steps are distinct (no collapse via
saturation). Because we chose the points
q_{j}
to be pairwise distinct,
the successive elementary transforms are supported on distinct points and
therefore commute and produce distinct saturated kernels; equivalently,
each step increases the determinant by a nonzero effective divisor, so
no further identification occurs. Hence the length of the constructed
filtration is exactly
M
.

Combining the upper bound and the explicit construction shows

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=M
, as claimed.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Proposition
4.1
is the
one–dimensional specialization of
Proposition
2.4
.
When
\dim X=1
, every divisor linearly equivalent to
A
is a single
point and elementary transforms at distinct points commute.
Hence the geometric existence condition in the higher–dimensional
proposition is automatic for split bundles, and the determinant bound
specializes to an exact formula for curves.

Proposition 4.3

(Basic properties of hierarchical depth)
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety,
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
a reference line bundle,
and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle of rank
r
on
X
.

(A)

(Monotonicity)
If
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
is a saturated inclusion of vector bundles
of the same rank
r
, then

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\leq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

(B)

(Extension inequality)
Let
0\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
be a short exact sequence of vector bundles
on
X
.

Suppose there exist hierarchical filtrations

\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime},\qquad\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}:\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{b}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime},

normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
and
\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}:=\Lambda_{0}\otimes\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})^{-1}
respectively.
Assume that for each
j=1,\dots,b
, the elementary transform

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j-1}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j}
along an effective divisor
D_{j}

lifts to an elementary transform of
\mathcal{E}
in the following sense:
there exists a commutative diagram with exact rows

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{j-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j-1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{F}_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}

where:

•

\mathcal{F}_{j-1},\mathcal{F}_{j}\subset\mathcal{E}
are saturated subbundles of rank
\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
,

•

the vertical inclusions
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{F}_{j}
and

\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j}
are elementary transforms along
D_{j}
,

•

the middle vertical map induces the given inclusion
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j}

on the quotients.

Then

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\geq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})+h_{\Lambda^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}).

(C)

(Direct sums)
If
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{E}_{1}\oplus\mathcal{E}_{2}
and there exist maximal hierarchical
filtrations of
\mathcal{E}_{1}
and
\mathcal{E}_{2}
whose determinant increments have pairwise disjoint
support, then

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})+h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}_{2}).

Without disjoint supports, only the inequality
\geq
holds in general.

Proof.

(A) Let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{h^{\prime}}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be a hierarchical filtration
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
. Since
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
is saturated of the
same rank, each
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}
remains saturated and the quotients

\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}
are torsion with the same support as
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}
. Thus the same
chain is a hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
, yielding
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\geq h^{\prime}
.
Taking suprema gives
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\leq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
.

(B) Denote
r=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
,
r^{\prime}=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
,
r^{\prime\prime}=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime})
; note
r=r^{\prime}+r^{\prime\prime}
.

Step 1: Embedding the filtration of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
.

By (A) (monotonicity), the hierarchical
filtration
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime}
of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
embeds into
\mathcal{E}
: each
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
is
saturated in
\mathcal{E}
and the quotients
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}
are torsion with the same support
as
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}/\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}
. Thus we obtain a chain of saturated subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
:

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}.

Each inclusion
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i-1}\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{i}
remains an elementary transform along
some effective divisor
C_{i}
, and
\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
by hypothesis.

Step 2: Lifting the filtration of
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
step by step.

Set
\mathcal{F}_{0}:=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
(the last term of (2)). By assumption, for
j=1
we have a
diagram (
\star_{1}
) with
\mathcal{F}_{0}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and
\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\mathcal{E}
a saturated subbundle
fitting into

0\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}_{1}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{1}\to 0,

and
\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}
is an elementary transform along
D_{1}
.
The determinant satisfies

\det(\mathcal{F}_{1})\simeq\det(\mathcal{F}_{0})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1}).

Proceed inductively: having constructed
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\subset\mathcal{E}
with

0\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}_{j-1}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j-1}\to 0
, diagram (
\star_{j}
) provides

\mathcal{F}_{j}\subset\mathcal{E}
containing
\mathcal{F}_{j-1}
as an elementary transform along
D_{j}
,
and
0\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}_{j}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j}\to 0
. The determinant relation is

\det(\mathcal{F}_{j})\simeq\det(\mathcal{F}_{j-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{j}).

Step 3: Concatenation yields a hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
.

Chaining (2) with the lifts of the
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
-filtration gives a sequence

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{0}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{\prime}_{a}=\mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{b}=:\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}.

We claim
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}=\mathcal{E}
. Indeed,
\mathcal{F}_{b}
fits into

0\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\to\mathcal{F}_{b}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{b}=\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}\to 0
, and the inclusion

\mathcal{F}_{b}\subset\mathcal{E}
induces the identity on
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
and the given surjection

\mathcal{E}\to\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
. Hence
\mathcal{F}_{b}=\mathcal{E}
by the five‑lemma (or by comparing ranks and
the fact that both are subbundles of
\mathcal{E}
).

Each inclusion in (4) is saturated of rank
r
and the successive quotients are
torsion sheaves supported on effective divisors (
C_{i}
for the
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
-part,
D_{j}

for the
\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}
-part). Moreover, from (3) and the normalization of
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}
we have

\det(\mathcal{F}_{j})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\otimes\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}_{j})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\otimes\bigl(\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1}+\cdots+D_{j})\bigr)\simeq\Lambda_{0}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{1}+\cdots+D_{j}),

so the determinant increments match those of
\mathfrak{F}^{\prime\prime}
. Consequently, (4) is a
hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
, of total length
a+b
.

Step 4: Conclusion.

Since
a=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})
and
b=h_{\Lambda_{0}^{\prime}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime})
by definition
of hierarchical depth, the existence of the filtration (4) shows

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\geq a+b=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})+h_{\Lambda^{\prime}_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime\prime}),

which is precisely inequality (1).

(C) Under the disjoint support hypothesis, Lemma
3.2

ensures the elementary transforms from the two filtrations commute. Interleaving them
produces a hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
of length
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}_{1})+h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}_{2})
,
giving equality. If supports intersect, commutativity may fail and saturation
can reduce the achievable length, yielding only the inequality.
∎

Remark 4.4
.

(Non-monotonicity for smaller rank) If
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{E}
is a subbundle with

\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})<\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
, the inequality
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})\leq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
may fail.
For example, on
X=\mathbb{P}^{1}
with
\Lambda_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}
, take

\mathcal{E}^{\prime}=\mathcal{O}(1)\subset\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}(-1)
. Then

h(\mathcal{E}^{\prime})=\deg\mathcal{O}(1)=1
while
h(\mathcal{E})=\deg\det(\mathcal{E})=0
.

5.
Effects of the Minimal Model Program on hierarchical filtrations

In this section we study the behavior of hierarchical filtrations of vector bundles
under birational transformations arising in the Minimal Model Program (MMP)
when the base variety has dimension at most
2
. For smooth projective surfaces, the MMP consists solely of contractions of
(-1)
-curves, which preserve smoothness.

5.1.
Setup and motivation

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\leq 2

over an algebraically closed field
\mathbb{F}
.
The MMP for
X
consists of a finite sequence of birational morphisms

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{t}=X_{\min},

where each step is either an isomorphism in codimension
1

or a contraction of a
(-1)
–curve (in the surface case),
and the final model
X_{\min}
is minimal, i.e.
K_{X_{\min}}
is nef.
In dimension
1
the sequence is trivial since every smooth curve is already minimal (see
[
10
]
).

Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, we are interested in how
its hierarchical filtrations behave under the birational maps
of such an MMP sequence, and in particular how the hierarchical depth
changes after blowing up or contracting divisors.
This analysis provides a bridge between the birational geometry of
X

and the algebraic structure of
\mathcal{E}
, and will later underlie the construction of evaluation codes associated to successive divisorial increments.

5.2.
Birational pullback of filtrations

Let
f:Y\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth projective
varieties of dimension
\leq 2
, for instance the blow–up of
X

at a smooth point with exceptional divisor
E
.
Given a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and a reference line bundle
\Lambda_{0}
,
consider its pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
on
Y
.
The determinant relation satisfies

\det(f^{*}\mathcal{E})\simeq f^{*}(\det\mathcal{E}),\qquad c_{1}(\det(f^{*}\mathcal{E}))=f^{*}c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E}).

Hence a hierarchical filtration

\mathfrak{F}:\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

on
X
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}

pulls back naturally to a filtration

f^{*}\mathfrak{F}:\ f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{h}=f^{*}\mathcal{E}

on
Y
, normalized by
f^{*}\Lambda_{0}
.
At each step one has

\det(f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(f^{*}D_{i}),

so that the divisorial increments are transformed by pullback:

D_{i}
is replaced by
f^{*}D_{i}
.
Consequently,

h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})\geq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

In particular, the hierarchical depth is invariant under birational pullback
of smooth varieties in dimension
\leq 2
. Consequently, we have the following statement:

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth projective varieties
of dimension
\leq 2
, and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
.
Then

h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})\geq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

5.3.
Effect of blow–ups on new divisorial increments

Let
f:Y\to X
be the blow–up of
X
at a smooth point
p\in X

with exceptional divisor
E\simeq\mathbb{P}^{n-1}
.
If
\mathcal{E}
is a vector bundle on
X
, the pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
remains locally free.
However, the Picard group changes:

\mathrm{Pic}(Y)\cong f^{*}\mathrm{Pic}(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle E\rangle.

Therefore, new effective divisors supported on the exceptional locus can appear in hierarchical filtrations of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
that have no counterpart on
X
.

The statements of two following lemmas can be found in
[
5
, Ch. V]
.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over an algebraically closed field,
let
p\in X
be a smooth (closed) point, and let

f:\,Y=\operatorname{Bl}_{p}X\to X
be the blow–up of
X
at
p
.
Let
E\subset Y
denote the exceptional divisor (which is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{\,\dim X-1}
).
Then the pullback map
f^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}(X)\to\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
is injective, and every line
bundle
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
can be written uniquely in the form

\mathcal{L}\simeq f^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)

for a unique
M\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
and a unique integer
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. Equivalently,
there is a direct sum decomposition of abelian groups

\mathrm{Pic}(Y)\cong f^{*}\mathrm{Pic}(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle E\rangle.

Proof.

Injectivity of
f^{*}
.
Suppose
M\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
satisfies
f^{*}M\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
.
Apply the pushforward functor
f_{*}
and use the projection formula
together with
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
(since
f
is a birational morphism
contracting the exceptional divisor to a point):

f_{*}f^{*}M\cong M\otimes f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong M.

But
f_{*}f^{*}M\cong f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
because
f^{*}M\cong\mathcal{O}_{Y}
, hence

M\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
. Thus
f^{*}
is injective.

Existence of the decomposition.

Let
\mathcal{L}\in\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
. Restrict
\mathcal{L}
to the exceptional
divisor
E\cong\mathbb{P}^{\,\dim X-1}
. Since
\mathrm{Pic}(E)\cong\mathbb{Z}
and is generated by

\mathcal{O}_{E}(1)
, there is a unique integer
m
with

\mathcal{L}|_{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}(m).

Consider the twist
\mathcal{L}(-mE):=\mathcal{L}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE)
. By
construction
\mathcal{L}(-mE)|_{E}\cong\mathcal{O}_{E}
is trivial on
E
. The line
bundle
\mathcal{L}(-mE)
is therefore trivial on the exceptional locus,
hence it descends to
X
in the following sense: set

M:=f_{*}\big(\mathcal{L}(-mE)\big).

The sheaf
M
is reflexive of rank
1
on the smooth variety
X
, hence
locally free of rank
1
, i.e. a line bundle (see e.g.
[
5
, II, Ex. 5.15]
).
Moreover
f^{*}M
and
\mathcal{L}(-mE)
are isomorphic away from
E
.
Both are line bundles on the normal variety
Y
and agree on the complement
of a divisor, hence they are isomorphic globally:

f^{*}M\cong\mathcal{L}(-mE).

Therefore
\mathcal{L}\cong f^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)
.

Uniqueness.

If
f^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)\cong f^{*}M^{\prime}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(m^{\prime}E)
, restrict to
E
to
compare integers: the left side restricts to
\mathcal{O}_{E}(m)
and the right side to

\mathcal{O}_{E}(m^{\prime})
, hence
m=m^{\prime}
. Cancelling
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)
and using injectivity of

f^{*}
gives
M\cong M^{\prime}
. This proves uniqueness of the pair
(M,m)
.

Group decomposition.

The map

f^{*}\mathrm{Pic}(X)\oplus\mathbb{Z}\langle E\rangle\longrightarrow\mathrm{Pic}(Y),\qquad(M,m)\longmapsto f^{*}M\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)

is therefore bijective and respects the group law, yielding the claimed
direct sum decomposition.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
D
be an effective Cartier divisor on
Y
. Then
D
decomposes uniquely as

D=f^{*}D_{X}+mE

with
D_{X}
an effective Cartier divisor on
X
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
. Moreover

D
is supported on
E
if and only if
D_{X}=0
.

Proof.

Write the line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D)
as
f^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{X})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)
using
Lemma
5.2
. If
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D)
is effective then pulling down by

f_{*}
shows
\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{X})
is effective and
m\geq 0
(otherwise the section would
vanish identically along the exceptional divisor in contradiction with effectivity).
Uniqueness follows from the uniqueness in Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Recall the concept of saturation from
[
8
]
. Let
\mathcal{F}
be a subsheaf of a coherent sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
. The
saturation
of
\mathcal{F}
inside
\mathcal{E}
is defined as the minimal subsheaf
\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
containing
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}^{\prime}
is pure of dimension
d=\dim(\mathcal{E})
or zero. Equivalently, the saturation of
\mathcal{F}
is

\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sat}}:=\ker\bigl(\mathcal{E}\longrightarrow\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})/T(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})\bigr),

where
T(\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F})
denotes the maximal subsheaf of

\mathcal{E}/\mathcal{F}
of dimension
\leq d-1
. Equivalently,
\mathcal{F}^{\mathrm{sat}}
is the largest subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}

that coincides with
\mathcal{F}
on a dense open set and is torsion–free. When

\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{X}
, saturation simply removes
0
–dimensional torsion.

As an example, suppose the sequence of line bundles

\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-2E)\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-E)\subset\mathcal{O}_{Y}.

Applying
f_{*}
gives

f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-2E)=\mathcal{I}_{p/X}^{2},\qquad f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-E)=\mathcal{I}_{p/X},\qquad f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}=\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Each of these is already torsion-free on
X
(they are ideal sheaves of a
smooth point), hence saturation does nothing:

(\mathcal{I}_{p/X}^{2})^{\mathrm{sat}}=\mathcal{I}_{p/X}^{2},\qquad(\mathcal{I}_{p/X})^{\mathrm{sat}}=\mathcal{I}_{p/X},\qquad(\mathcal{O}_{X})^{\mathrm{sat}}=\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Thus the saturated chain is simply

\mathcal{I}_{p/X}^{2}\subset\mathcal{I}_{p/X}\subset\mathcal{O}_{X}.

Proposition 5.4
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be the blowup of a smooth projective variety
X
at a
smooth closed point
p\in X
, and denote by
E\subset Y
the exceptional
divisor. Assume
\dim X\leq 2
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
and
fix a normalization
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
.

Then every hierarchical filtration of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
normalized by
f^{*}\Lambda_{0}

has length

h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})+\delta,\qquad\delta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

where
\delta
equals the number of additional determinant increments in
a maximal filtration of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
whose divisors carry a positive coefficient
of the exceptional divisor
E
. In particular
\delta=0
unless the
filtration involves elementary transforms along
E
.

Proof.

Step 1.

If

\mathfrak{F}_{X}:\ \mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

is a hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}
normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
, then
pulling back termwise yields

f^{*}\mathfrak{F}_{X}:\ f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{h}=f^{*}\mathcal{E},

a hierarchical filtration of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
normalized by
f^{*}\Lambda_{0}
. It follows from Proposition
5.1
that

h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})\ \geq\ h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

Thus any extra length in
h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})
must come from steps
whose torsion quotient is supported on
E
.

Step 2.

Let

\mathfrak{F}_{Y}:\ \mathcal{F}_{0}\subset\mathcal{F}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{F}_{h^{\prime}}=f^{*}\mathcal{E}

be a maximal hierarchical filtration of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
normalized by
f^{*}\Lambda_{0}
.
For each step write the determinant increment as an effective Cartier
divisor
D_{i}\subset Y
. By the decomposition of effective divisors on
a blowup (Lemma
5.3
), each
D_{i}
splits uniquely as

D_{i}=f^{*}D_{i,X}+m_{i}E,\qquad D_{i,X}\geq 0,\ m_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}.

Collect the indices into two disjoint sets:

I_{0}:=\{i:\ m_{i}=0\},\qquad I_{E}:=\{i:\ m_{i}>0\},

and write
h_{0}:=|I_{0}|
and
\delta:=|I_{E}|
. Then
h^{\prime}=h_{0}+\delta
.

Step 3.
For each
i\in I_{0}
the divisor
D_{i}
is the pullback of an effective
Cartier divisor
D_{i,X}
on
X
. The corresponding step

\mathcal{F}_{i-1}\subset\mathcal{F}_{i}
on
Y
therefore descends
(objectwise) to an inclusion of torsion–free sheaves on
X
after
applying pushforward and saturation:

\mathcal{G}_{i}:=\big(f_{*}\mathcal{F}_{i}\big)^{\mathrm{sat}}.

Because
\dim X\leq 2
the saturation (reflexive hull) of a torsion-free
rank-
r
sheaf is locally free (see for instance
[
5
, II, Ex. 5.15]

or standard references on reflexive sheaves). Hence each
\mathcal{G}_{i}
is
a vector bundle on
X
, and the inclusions
\mathcal{G}_{i-1}\subset\mathcal{G}_{i}

are saturated with torsion quotients supported on
D_{i,X}
. Collecting
these descended steps yields a hierarchical filtration on
X
of length

h_{0}
, normalized by
\Lambda_{0}
. Consequently

h_{0}\leq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

Conversely, pulling back any maximal filtration on
X
reproduces a
filtration on
Y
whose non-exceptional increments give at least

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
steps with
m_{i}=0
. Therefore
h_{0}\geq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
,
and combining the two inequalities gives

h_{0}=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}).

Step 4.
By Step 2 we have
h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})=h_{0}+\delta
, and by Step 3,

h_{0}=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
. Hence

h_{f^{*}\Lambda_{0}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})+\delta,

with
\delta\geq 0
equal to the number of determinant increments whose
divisors carry a positive coefficient of
E
.

Step 5.
Finally, each elementary transform along
E
produces a torsion quotient
supported on
E
and increases the exceptional coefficient of the
determinant by precisely the corresponding multiplicity; conversely any
positive coefficient of
E
in some increment
D_{i}
arises from at least
one such elementary transform (after saturation). Therefore the number

\delta
equals the number of additional elementary transforms supported
on
E
in the maximal filtration, i.e. the number of extra steps
contributed by exceptional transforms.

This completes the proof.
∎

5.4.
Contraction and preservation of depth

Proposition 5.5
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be the contraction of a
(-1)
–curve
E\subset Y
onto a smooth point
p=f(E)\in X
, and let
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
be a vector bundle on
Y
admitting a hierarchical filtration

\mathfrak{F}_{Y}:\quad 0=\mathcal{E}_{0,Y}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1,Y}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h,Y}=\mathcal{E}_{Y}

normalized by
\Lambda_{0,Y}\in\mathrm{Pic}(Y)
.
Assume that for every step of the filtration, the determinant increment divisor
D_{i,Y}
satisfies

E\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}D_{i,Y}.

Then the direct image
\mathcal{E}:=(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y})^{\mathrm{sat}}
carries a natural hierarchical filtration

\mathfrak{F}_{X}:\quad 0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E},

normalized by
\Lambda_{0}:=\big(f_{*}\Lambda_{0,Y}\big)^{\mathrm{sat}}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
,
and the hierarchical depths coincide:

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0,Y}}(\mathcal{E}_{Y}).

Proof.

We proceed in several steps.

Step 1.
The morphism
f:Y\to X
is an isomorphism over
U:=X\setminus\{p\}
,
and
f^{-1}(U)=Y\setminus E\simeq U
.
For any locally free sheaf
\mathcal{G}
on
Y
, the pushforward
f_{*}\mathcal{G}
is torsion–free on
X
,
and since
f
is birational between smooth surfaces,

f_{*}\mathcal{G}
is reflexive (see, e.g.,
[
6
, Prop. 1.6]
).
Because reflexive sheaves on smooth surfaces are locally free,

(f_{*}\mathcal{G})^{\vee\vee}\cong f_{*}\mathcal{G}
defines a vector bundle on
X

(after saturation at the point
p
).
For each
i
we set

\mathcal{E}_{i}:=(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i,Y})^{\mathrm{sat}}.

Then each
\mathcal{E}_{i}
is a vector bundle on
X
, and
f^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i}\simeq\mathcal{E}_{i,Y}
away from
E
.

Step 2.
Each inclusion
\mathcal{E}_{i-1,Y}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i,Y}
is saturated on
Y
,
with torsion quotient
\mathcal{T}_{i,Y}
supported on the effective Cartier divisor
D_{i,Y}
.
Since
E\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}D_{i,Y}
, the support of
\mathcal{T}_{i,Y}
avoids
E
.
Applying
f_{*}
yields an exact sequence on
U
:

0\to f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i-1,Y}|_{U}\to f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i,Y}|_{U}\to f_{*}\mathcal{T}_{i,Y}|_{U}\to 0,

where
f_{*}\mathcal{T}_{i,Y}
remains a torsion sheaf supported on

f(D_{i,Y})\subset X\setminus\{p\}
.
Passing to saturations gives a global inclusion of vector bundles

\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}_{i}

on
X
.

Step 3.
On
Y
one has

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i,Y})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1,Y})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(D_{i,Y}).

Because
E\not\subset\mathrm{Supp}D_{i,Y}
, each
D_{i,Y}
is the strict transform of a unique
effective divisor
D_{i,X}\subset X
, i.e.
D_{i,Y}=f^{*}D_{i,X}
.
Over
U
the projection formula gives

\det(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i,Y})|_{U}\simeq\det(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{i-1,Y})|_{U}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{U}(D_{i,X}),

and since taking reflexive hulls does not alter determinant line bundles on smooth surfaces,
this equality extends globally to
X
:

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i,X}).

Thus each step
\mathcal{E}_{i-1}\subset\mathcal{E}_{i}
on
X
is again an elementary transform
with determinant increment
D_{i,X}
.

Step 4.
The chain

\mathfrak{F}_{X}:\quad 0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

is normalized by
\Lambda_{0}:=(f_{*}\Lambda_{0,Y})^{\mathrm{sat}}
,
because
\det(\mathcal{E}_{0,Y})\simeq\Lambda_{0,Y}
implies

\det(\mathcal{E}_{0})\simeq\Lambda_{0}
by construction.
All determinant increments descend faithfully, so the number of filtration
steps is preserved:

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})\geq h_{\Lambda_{0,Y}}(\mathcal{E}_{Y}).

Conversely, pulling back
\mathfrak{F}_{X}
to
Y
via
f^{*}
reproduces
\mathfrak{F}_{Y}
,
hence
h_{\Lambda_{0,Y}}(\mathcal{E}_{Y})\geq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
.
Therefore

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0,Y}}(\mathcal{E}_{Y}),

as required.
∎

5.5.
Hierarchical depth under MMP

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface. Consider a sequence of birational maps

X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{1}\dashrightarrow\cdots\dashrightarrow X_{t}=X_{\min},

obtained by running the minimal model program (MMP), where
X_{\min}
is a minimal model of
X
.
Each step of the MMP can be realized as a birational morphism

f_{i}:X_{i}\longrightarrow X_{i+1},\qquad i=0,\dots,t-1,

where
f_{i}
is either

•

a contraction of a
(-1)
-curve
E_{i}\subset X_{i}
to a smooth point of
X_{i+1}
, or

•

the inverse operation (if one considers a blowup) would be
X_{i+1}\longrightarrow X_{i}
,
introducing an exceptional
(-1)
-curve on the blowup.

By composing these morphisms one obtains a birational map

\phi:X=X_{0}\dashrightarrow X_{t}=X_{\min},

which is an isomorphism outside the union of the exceptional curves of the contractions.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
and
\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X)
a reference line bundle.
For each contraction
f_{i}:X_{i}\to X_{i+1}
, the pullback
f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i+1}
to
X_{i}
satisfies

h_{f_{i}^{*}\Lambda_{i+1}}(f_{i}^{*}\mathcal{E}_{i+1})\geq h_{\Lambda_{i+1}}(\mathcal{E}_{i+1}),

with equality unless the hierarchical filtration involves additional elementary transforms along the exceptional curve
E_{i}
.
Iterating this through the entire MMP sequence gives

(3)

h_{\Lambda_{0,\min}}(\mathcal{E}_{\min})\leq h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E}),

where
\mathcal{E}_{\min}
is the pushforward (or the vector bundle descended via saturation) on
X_{\min}
and
\Lambda_{0,\min}
is the corresponding reference line bundle.
Hence, the maximal hierarchical depth can only decrease (or remain the same) when passing from
X
to its minimal model.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
f:X\to X_{\min}
be the birational contraction of a smooth projective surface

X
onto its minimal model
X_{\min}
obtained by contracting finitely many

(-1)
–curves
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}\subset X
. Let

\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
and let
\mathcal{E}_{\min}
denote the (saturated)
direct image of
\mathcal{E}
on
X_{\min}
; fix reference line bundles

\Lambda_{0}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X),\qquad\Lambda_{0,\min}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\min})

related by

\Lambda_{0}\simeq f^{*}\Lambda_{0,\min}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\beta_{j}E_{j}\Big)

for unique integers
\beta_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Assume the determinants satisfy

\det(\mathcal{E})\ \simeq\ f^{*}\det(\mathcal{E}_{\min})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\alpha_{j}E_{j}\Big)

for unique integers
\alpha_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}
(so that the class

c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E})-f^{*}c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E}_{\min})=\sum_{j}\alpha_{j}[E_{j}]
in
\mathrm{NS}(X)
).
Suppose moreover that the difference divisors appearing in the normalizations
and determinants are effective on
X
, i.e.
\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j}\geq 0
for every
j
.

Then hierarchical filtrations exist for the normalized bundles and the
hierarchical depths are related by the exact formula

(4)

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0,\min}}(\mathcal{E}_{\min})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j}).

In particular the increase in depth from the minimal model to
X
is exactly
the total multiplicity of the exceptional divisors appearing in the
determinant of
\mathcal{E}
after accounting for the chosen normalizations.

Proof.

By hypothesis

\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes\Lambda_{0}^{-1}\ \simeq\ f^{*}\big(\det(\mathcal{E}_{\min})\otimes\Lambda_{0,\min}^{-1}\big)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j})E_{j}\Big).

Denote

\Delta_{\min}:=\det(\mathcal{E}_{\min})\otimes\Lambda_{0,\min}^{-1}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\min}),\qquad\Delta:=\det(\mathcal{E})\otimes\Lambda_{0}^{-1}\in\mathrm{Pic}(X).

By the usual correspondence between hierarchical filtrations and decompositions
of the determinant into effective Cartier increments (Definitions

2.1
and
2.2
), the maximal length

h_{\Lambda_{0,\min}}(\mathcal{E}_{\min})
equals the maximal number of nonzero
effective Cartier summands into which any effective representative of

\Delta_{\min}
may be decomposed; likewise
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
equals the
maximal number of nonzero effective Cartier summands in any effective
representative of
\Delta
.

Because pullback preserves effectiveness and the exceptional curves

E_{j}
are pairwise distinct irreducible Cartier divisors on
X
, any
effective decomposition of
\Delta_{\min}
pulls back to an effective
decomposition of
f^{*}\Delta_{\min}
with the same number of summands,
and the factor
\mathcal{O}_{X}\big(\sum_{j}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j})E_{j}\big)
contributes
exactly
\sum_{j}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j})
further irreducible effective summands
(all supported on the exceptional locus) when one decomposes
\Delta

into irreducible Cartier divisors. Conversely, every effective
decomposition of
\Delta
decomposes uniquely as the pullback of an
effective decomposition of
\Delta_{\min}
together with the exceptional
part (since the
E_{j}
generate the kernel of
f^{*}:\mathrm{Pic}(X_{\min})\to\mathrm{Pic}(X)
).

Therefore the maximal possible number of irreducible effective summands
in
\Delta
equals the maximal number for
\Delta_{\min}
plus the sum
of the coefficients of the exceptional part, i.e.

h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})=h_{\Lambda_{0,\min}}(\mathcal{E}_{\min})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}(\alpha_{j}-\beta_{j}).

This proves (
4
).
∎

Example 5.7
.

Let
X_{\min}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
with hyperplane class

, and let

f:X\to X_{\min}
be the blowup of
\mathbb{P}^{2}

:

\mathcal{E}_{\min}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(2)\qquad(\text{rank }2),\qquad\Lambda_{0,\min}=\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}.

Then
\det(\mathcal{E}_{\min})\cong\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{2}}(5)

(with

,

admits pairwise

X

6.
Interaction with Stability and Moduli Theory

The hierarchical filtration formalism interacts naturally with classical
and modern stability theories for vector bundles.
Since hierarchical increments are governed by effective divisors,
they admit a slope interpretation with respect to a fixed polarization,
yielding a discrete refinement of the usual Harder–Narasimhan filtration.
Moreover, the birational invariance results of
Section
5

ensure that such stability behavior is preserved
throughout the steps of the Minimal Model Program.

6.1.
Slope behavior under hierarchical increments

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\geq 1

with ample divisor
H
.
For a torsion–free sheaf
\mathcal{E}
on
X
, recall that its

slope
with respect to
H
is

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})=\frac{c_{1}(\mathcal{E})\cdot H^{n-1}}{\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})}.

Given a hierarchical filtration

\mathfrak{F}:\quad 0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E},\qquad\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}),

we may associate to each increment the numerical slope difference

(5)

\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{E},H)=\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i})-\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}).

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety of dimension
n\leq 2
over an
algebraically closed field, and let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
.
Let

\mathfrak{F}:\quad 0=\mathcal{E}_{0}\subset\mathcal{E}_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}_{h}=\mathcal{E}

be a hierarchical filtration of a vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
of rank
r
, with
determinant increments

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})\simeq\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i}),\qquad D_{i}\geq 0.

Then

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{0})\leq\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{1})\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{h}).

and for each index
i
the inequality
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i})>\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})
holds
precisely when
D_{i}
is not numerically equivalent to zero.
(
D_{i}\not\sim 0
).

Proof.

From the determinant relation

\det(\mathcal{E}_{i})=\det(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(D_{i})
we have

c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i})=c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})+[D_{i}]
. Since the rank
r
is constant,

(6)

\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i})-\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1})=\frac{(c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i})-c_{1}(\mathcal{E}_{i-1}))\cdot H^{n-1}}{r}=\frac{c_{1}(D_{i})\cdot H^{n-1}}{r}.

Each
D_{i}
is effective, so the intersection number

c_{1}(D_{i})\cdot H^{n-1}
is nonnegative, with equality if and only if

D_{i}\sim 0
. The stated monotonicity of the slopes follows immediately.
∎

6.2.
Birational stability under the Minimal Model Program

The birational invariance results of
Section
5
imply
that hierarchical depth behaves predictably
under the birational operations of the surface MMP.
In particular, slope–compatibility is preserved under pullbacks,
and semistability is unaffected outside exceptional loci.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth projective surfaces,
and let
H
be an ample divisor on
X
.
Then for every vector bundle
\mathcal{E}
on
X
and its pullback
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
on
Y
,
the divisorial slope differences satisfy

\Delta_{i}(f^{*}\mathcal{E},f^{*}H)=\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{E},H),

and the divisorial slope filtration of
\mathcal{E}

pulls back to that of
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
.

Proof.

Since
f^{*}H^{n-1}=H^{n-1}
numerically
and
f^{*}c_{1}(D_{i})=c_{1}(f^{*}D_{i})
,
formula (
6
) gives

\Delta_{i}(f^{*}\mathcal{E},f^{*}H)=\Delta_{i}(\mathcal{E},H)
.
The filtration structure is preserved
by Proposition
5.1
.
∎

Therefore, the stability behavior of hierarchical filtrations
remains invariant throughout the birational steps of the MMP,
except when new effective increments supported on exceptional curves appear.
In that case, the newly introduced increments correspond to
additional positive slopes localized on the exceptional locus,
and their total contribution is governed by
Theorem
5.6
.

Corollary 6.3
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be a birational morphism between smooth projective surfaces
obtained by contracting a finite collection of
(-1)
–curves, and let

H
be an ample divisor on
X
. Put
H_{Y}:=f^{*}H
.

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector bundle on
X
. If
\mathcal{E}
is divisorially
slope-semistable (respectively, divisorially slope-stable) with respect
to
H
, then the pullback bundle
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
is divisorially
slope–semistable (respectively, divisorially slope–stable) with respect
to
H_{Y}
.

Proof.

We treat the semistable case; the stable case is identical with strict
inequalities.

Let
r=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
. Suppose
\mathcal{E}
is divisorially slope–semistable on
X
in meaning of
[
7
]
,
i.e. for every saturated nonzero proper subsheaf
F\subset\mathcal{E}
one has

\mu_{H}(F)\leq\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}).

We must show that every saturated nonzero proper subsheaf

G\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}
satisfies

\mu_{H_{Y}}(G)\leq\mu_{H_{Y}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})=\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E}).

Consider any saturated subsheaf
G\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}
. Push it forward and
take saturation on
X
:

F:=\big(f_{*}G\big)^{\mathrm{sat}}\subset\mathcal{E}.

(Here saturation means reflexive hull / saturation in the sense of
subsheaves.) By construction
F
is a torsion–free subsheaf of
\mathcal{E}

of rank
s:=\mathrm{rank}(F)
with
0<s\leq r
.

There is an equality of determinant classes on
Y
of the form

\det(G)\simeq(f^{*}\det F)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}\Big(\sum_{j}a_{j}E_{j}\Big),\qquad a_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},

where the
E_{j}
are the exceptional curves of
f
and the integers
a_{j}

record the possible exceptional components appearing in
\det(G)
.
(Indeed every line bundle on
Y
decomposes uniquely as the pullback of a
line bundle on
X
tensor a line bundle supported on exceptional curves.)

Now compute slopes with respect to the pulled back polarization
H_{Y}=f^{*}H
.
Since intersection is functorial under pullback and every exceptional curve
is contracted to a point, we have for every
j

E_{j}\cdot H_{Y}=E_{j}\cdot f^{*}H=0.

Consequently the exceptional part does
not
contribute to the slope:

\mu_{H_{Y}}(G)=\frac{c_{1}(\det G)\cdot H_{Y}}{\mathrm{rank}(G)}=\frac{\big(c_{1}(f^{*}\det F)+\sum_{j}a_{j}[E_{j}]\big)\cdot f^{*}H}{\mathrm{rank}(G)}=\frac{c_{1}(\det F)\cdot H}{\mathrm{rank}(G)}=\mu_{H}(F).

But the right-hand side equals
\mu_{H}(F)
(by definition). Hence

\mu_{H_{Y}}(G)=\mu_{H}(F).

Since
\mathcal{E}
is divisorially slope–semistable,
\mu_{H}(F)\leq\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})
.
Moreover
\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})=\mu_{H_{Y}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})
because
c_{1}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})=f^{*}c_{1}(\mathcal{E})

and intersection with
f^{*}H
is compatible with pullback. Therefore

\mu_{H_{Y}}(G)=\mu_{H}(F)\leq\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})=\mu_{H_{Y}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E}),

as required. This proves that
f^{*}\mathcal{E}
is divisorially
slope–semistable with respect to
H_{Y}
.

The same argument with strict inequalities shows that divisorial
slope–stability is preserved: if
\mathcal{E}
is stable and
G\subset f^{*}\mathcal{E}

is a proper saturated subsheaf, then the strict inequality

\mu_{H}(F)<\mu_{H}(\mathcal{E})
holds, and hence
\mu_{H_{Y}}(G)<\mu_{H_{Y}}(f^{*}\mathcal{E})
.
∎

6.3.
Comparison with Harder–Narasimhan filtrations

Let
\mathcal{E}
be a torsion–free sheaf on
(X,H)

with Harder–Narasimhan filtration

\mathcal{E}^{(0)}\subset\mathcal{E}^{(1)}\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{(s)}=\mathcal{E},

whose successive quotients

\mathrm{Gr}_{i}^{\mathrm{HN}}(\mathcal{E})=\mathcal{E}^{(i)}/\mathcal{E}^{(i-1)}

are semistable with decreasing slopes

\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots>\mu_{s}
.
Then the determinant relation gives

c_{1}(\det\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=1}^{s}c_{1}\big(\mathrm{Gr}_{i}^{\mathrm{HN}}(\mathcal{E})\big),

so that each Harder–Narasimhan quotient
contributes an effective class whenever
\mathcal{E}
is globally generated
or arises as a positive extension.

Split bundle on a curve.
Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over a field and set

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i}),\qquad d_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}.

Write
D:=\sum_{i=1}^{r}d_{i}=\deg(\det\mathcal{E})
. We consider two invariants:

With notation as above we obtain the hierarchical depth and the length of Harder-Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
.

(1)
Hierarchical depth.

It was shown in Proposition
4.1
that

\displaystyle h_{C}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i=1}^{r}\max\{d_{i},0\}.
In particular, if all
d_{i}\geq 0
then
h_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{E})=\sum_{i}d_{i}=D
. If some
d_{i}<0
the corresponding summand contributes
0
to the depth.

(2)
Harder–Narasimhan length.

The Harder-Narasimhan length
s
equals the number of distinct values among the degrees
\{d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\}
. Equivalently, if we arrange the degrees in nonincreasing order and group equal values,

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{s}\Big(\bigoplus_{k=1}^{r_{j}}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d^{(j)})\Big),

where
d^{(1)}>\cdots>d^{(s)}
are the distinct degrees and
r_{j}
the multiplicities, then the HN filtration has length
s
with graded pieces

\mathrm{Gr}_{j}^{\mathrm{HN}}\cong\mathcal{O}_{C}(d^{(j)})^{\oplus r_{j}}
. In the following we imply to this fact.

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field,
and let

\mathcal{E}=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})

be a direct sum of line bundles of degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{r}\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then the Harder–Narasimhan filtration of
\mathcal{E}
(with respect to any polarization)
is obtained by ordering the summands in nonincreasing degree:

\mathcal{E}^{(0)}=0\subset\mathcal{E}^{(1)}=\bigoplus_{d_{i}=d_{(1)}}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})\subset\mathcal{E}^{(2)}=\bigoplus_{d_{i}\geq d_{(2)}}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})\subset\cdots\subset\mathcal{E}^{(s)}=\mathcal{E},

where
d_{(1)}>d_{(2)}>\cdots>d_{(s)}
are the distinct degrees among the
d_{i}
.
In particular,

\text{length of the Harder--Narasimhan filtration }s=\#\{\text{distinct degrees among the }d_{i}\}.

The existence and uniqueness of the Harder–Narasimhan filtration for
torsion-free sheaves on smooth projective varieties was established
by Harder and Narasimhan
[
7
]
.

On a smooth projective curve
C
, every line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{C}(d)
is stable
with respect to any polarization, and its slope is simply

\mu(\mathcal{O}_{C}(d))=\frac{\deg\mathcal{O}_{C}(d)}{\mathrm{rank}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d)}=d.

Hence each direct summand
\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})
is a semistable bundle of slope
d_{i}
.
Let
d_{(1)}>\cdots>d_{(s)}
be the distinct degrees among the
d_{i}
, and define

\mathcal{E}^{(k)}:=\bigoplus_{d_{i}\geq d_{(k)}}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i}).

Then the successive quotients

\mathrm{Gr}_{k}^{\mathrm{HN}}(\mathcal{E}):=\mathcal{E}^{(k)}/\mathcal{E}^{(k-1)}=\bigoplus_{d_{i}=d_{(k)}}\mathcal{O}_{C}(d_{i})

are semistable bundles of slope
\mu_{k}=d_{(k)}
, and the sequence

\mu_{1}>\mu_{2}>\cdots>\mu_{s}
is strictly decreasing.
By the uniqueness of the Harder-Narasimhan filtration this is precisely the HN filtration of
\mathcal{E}
.

Example 6.4
.

(i)

Take
r=3
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(0)
.
Then
h_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{E})=3+1+0=4
. The HN degrees are
3>1>0
, so
s=3
.

(ii)

Take
r=3
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(0)
.
Then
h_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{E})=1+1+0=2
. The HN degrees are
1>0
, so
s=2
.

(iii)

Take
r=3
and
\mathcal{E}=\mathcal{O}_{C}(1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(0)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{C}(0)
.
Then
h_{\mathcal{O}_{C}}(\mathcal{E})=1
and the HN filtration has length
s=2
.

It follows that there is no relation between hierarchical depth of a vector bundle and the length of its HN filtration.

7.
Applications to algebraic–geometric codes

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,

\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle on
X
, and
D\subset X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
a set of
N
rational points.
The associated algebraic–geometric code is

C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D)=\operatorname{ev}_{D}(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}))\subset\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,rN}
where
r=\mathrm{rank}(\mathcal{E})
.

Hierarchical filtrations of
\mathcal{E}
expose degeneracies: if an elementary transform occurs along a divisor containing evaluation points, the corresponding code coordinates vanish. Consequently, hierarchical depth
h_{\Lambda_{0}}(\mathcal{E})
bounds the number of such degenerate layers.

For surfaces, the minimal model program contracts
(-1)
-curves—smooth point blowups in reverse—and Theorem
5.6
quantifies how hierarchical depth changes under these birational maps. Contracting exceptional divisors eliminates the associated degenerate coordinates, improving the code’s normalized minimum distance while preserving dimension. The next subsections provide explicit constructions demonstrating this optimization.

7.1.
AG codes from vector bundles

Let
X
be a smooth projective variety over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and let
\mathcal{E}
be a vector
bundle of rank
r
on
X
. Let

D=\{P_{1},\dots,P_{N}\}\subset X(\mathbb{F}_{q})

be a finite ordered set of
N
distinct
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–rational points such that
evaluation of sections of
\mathcal{E}
is well defined at every
P_{i}
. Fix, for each

P_{i}
, an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–linear identification (trivialization) of the fiber

\mathcal{E}|_{P_{i}}\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,r}
.

The
algebraic–geometric code
associated to
(\mathcal{E},D)
is defined by

C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D):=\operatorname{ev}_{D}\big(H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\big)=\{(s(P_{1}),\dots,s(P_{N})):s\in H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E})\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\,rN}.

For details of AG codes from vector bundles see
[
11
,
16
]
.

7.2.
Minimal models and coding–theoretic optimization

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface defined over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and let

f:Y\to X
denote a blow–up of
X
at a smooth
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–rational point,
with exceptional divisor
E\subset Y
. As discussed in
Proposition
5.4
, such a birational modification increases
the hierarchical depth of vector bundles by an integer
\delta\geq 0
,
reflecting the presence of additional effective increments supported on
E
.

From the coding–theoretic viewpoint, these exceptional divisors have
unfavourable effects on the parameters of algebraic–geometric codes
constructed from vector bundles. In order to optimize the geometric data
used for encoding, it is therefore natural to pass to the
minimal model

of
X
, obtained by contracting all
(-1)
–curves.

Proposition 7.1
.

Let
f:Y\to X
be the contraction of a
(-1)
-curve
E\subset Y
onto a
smooth
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational point
P_{0}\in X
.
Let
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
be a vector bundle on
Y
such that:

(i)

\mathcal{E}_{Y}
is
f
-trivial
in the sense that
R^{1}f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y}=0
;

(ii)

\det(\mathcal{E}_{Y})
has no component supported on
E
(equivalently,
in the decomposition
\det(\mathcal{E}_{Y})\simeq f^{*}L\otimes\mathcal{O}_{Y}(mE)
, we have
m=0
).

Set
\mathcal{E}:=(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y})^{\mathrm{sat}}
. Then there is a canonical identification

H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}),

and for any evaluation set
D\subset X(\mathbb{F}_{q})\setminus\{P_{0}\}
one has

C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D)=C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},f^{-1}(D)).

Proof.

Condition (i) gives
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y}=0
for
i>0
(since
R^{i}f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y}
are supported
at
P_{0}
and vanish for
i>1
by dimension reasons). The projection formula and
the fact that
f_{*}\mathcal{O}_{Y}\cong\mathcal{O}_{X}
yield

H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})\cong H^{0}(X,f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y})\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{E}),

where the second isomorphism uses that saturation does not change global sections.

Condition (ii) ensures that for any
P\in D
, the natural map

(f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y})_{P}\to\mathcal{E}_{Y}|_{f^{-1}(P)}
is an isomorphism (the torsion of
f_{*}\mathcal{E}_{Y}

is concentrated at
P_{0}
). Hence evaluation at
P
and at
f^{-1}(P)
agree,
giving the equality of codes.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

Condition (i) holds, for instance, when
\mathcal{E}_{Y}|_{E}
is a direct sum of line bundles
of degree
\geq-1
, or when
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
is pulled back from
X
twisted by
\mathcal{O}_{Y}(-mE)

with
0\leq m\leq 1
. Condition (ii) is exactly the requirement that no step in a
hierarchical filtration of
\mathcal{E}_{Y}
involves an elementary transform along
E
.

Remark 7.3
.

While it is folklore that birational varieties can yield equivalent AG codes when evaluation points avoid exceptional loci, Proposition
7.1
provides precise cohomological and determinantal criteria in terms of hierarchical
filtrations. In particular, condition (ii) is equivalent to the vanishing of the exceptional contribution
\delta
in Theorem
5.6
.

Corollary 7.4
.

Let
X
be a smooth projective surface over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
,
f:X\to X_{\min}
the
contraction of all
(-1)
-curves to its minimal model, and
\mathcal{E}
a vector bundle
on
X
satisfying the hypotheses of Proposition
7.1

for each contraction step. Let
D\subset X(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be an evaluation set, and
decompose
D=D_{\mathrm{ex}}\cup D_{\mathrm{reg}}
where

D_{\mathrm{ex}}
lies on exceptional curves and
D_{\mathrm{reg}}
does not.
Set
D_{\min}:=f(D_{\mathrm{reg}})\subset X_{\min}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Then the AG codes are related by

C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D)\cong C_{X_{\min}}(f_{*}\mathcal{E},\,D_{\min})\times\{0\}^{r\cdot|D_{\mathrm{ex}}|},

where
\cong
denotes isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces. Consequently:

•

\dim C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D)=\dim C_{X_{\min}}(f_{*}\mathcal{E},D_{\min})
;

•

d_{\min}(C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D))=d_{\min}(C_{X_{\min}}(f_{*}\mathcal{E},D_{\min}))
;

•

|D|=|D_{\min}|+|D_{\mathrm{ex}}|
, so the length decreases by

r\cdot|D_{\mathrm{ex}}|
coordinates.

In particular, the
normalized
minimum distance

\delta=d_{\min}/(r\cdot|D|)
strictly increases when
D_{\mathrm{ex}}\neq\varnothing
.

Corollary 7.5
.

Under the hypotheses of Proposition
7.1
, let

D_{\mathrm{ex}}\subset D
be evaluation points on exceptional curves.
Then

\frac{d_{\min}(C_{X_{\min}}(f_{*}\mathcal{E},f(D\setminus D_{\mathrm{ex}})))}{r\cdot|D\setminus D_{\mathrm{ex}}|}>\frac{d_{\min}(C_{X}(\mathcal{E},D))}{r\cdot|D|},

with strict inequality when
D_{\mathrm{ex}}\neq\varnothing
.

Proof.

Immediate from Proposition
7.1
since removing zero
coordinates preserves
d_{\min}
while reducing length.
∎

Example 7.6
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{5}}
be the projective plane over
\mathbb{F}_{5}
, and let

f:Y\to X
be the blow-up of

at the
\mathbb{F}_{5}

Consider the line bundle on

where

\mathbb{P}^{2}

Global sections.

From the exact sequence

Since
H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(3H))\cong H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(3))

Let
D_{Y}
be a set of

\mathbb{F}_{5}

\mathbb{F}_{5}

Code on the minimal model.

On the contracted surface

, the direct image bundle

has

Example 7.7
.

Let
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}_{\mathbb{F}_{7}}

be the blowup of

at the

\mathbb{F}_{7}

\mathbb{F}_{7}

Denote by

Consider the rank

Choose

C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},D_{Y})\cong\{0\}^{3\cdot 5}\times C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},D^{\prime}),\quad\dim C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},D_{Y})=16.

To estimate the minimum distance, note that a nonzero section
s=(s_{3},s_{2},s_{1})\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})

has components
s_{d}\in H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{O}_{Y}(dH-E))
. Each
s_{d}
vanishes at
p
and
can vanish at additional points. For generic
D^{\prime}
, a section can vanish at
at most
(\deg(s_{3})+\deg(s_{2})+\deg(s_{1}))-1=(3+2+1)-1=5
points
of
D^{\prime}
(by a parameter count). Thus we may have

d_{\min}(C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},D_{Y}))=d_{\min}(C_{Y}(\mathcal{E}_{Y},D^{\prime}))=13-5=8.

The normalized minimum distance is

\delta_{Y}=\frac{d_{\min}}{3\cdot 18}=\frac{8}{54}\approx 0.148.

Step 4: AG code on the minimal model.

On
X=\mathbb{P}^{2}
, consider the subspace

V:=H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{p}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(3))\oplus H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{p}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(2))\oplus H^{0}(X,\mathcal{I}_{p}\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))

of
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(3)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(2)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{X}(1))
, which corresponds to

H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})
under the natural isomorphism
H^{0}(Y,\mathcal{E}_{Y})\cong V
. Let

D=f(D^{\prime})\subset X(\mathbb{F}_{7}),\quad\#D=13.

The code using this subspace is

C_{X}(V,D)=\operatorname{ev}_{D}(V)\subset(\mathbb{F}_{7}^{3})^{13}.

The dimension remains
16
, and the minimum distance analysis is similar:
a nonzero section
(s_{3},s_{2},s_{1})\in V
vanishes at
p
and can vanish at
up to 5 additional points of
D
(since
s_{3}
is a cubic,
s_{2}
a quadric,

s_{1}
a linear form, all vanishing at
p
). Thus

d_{\min}(C_{X}(V,D))=13-5=8.

The normalized minimum distance improves to

\delta_{X}=\frac{8}{3\cdot 13}=\frac{8}{39}\approx 0.205>0.148\approx\delta_{Y}.

Remark 7.8
.

For a contraction
f:Y\to X
of
(-1)
-curves
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and a bundle

\mathcal{E}_{Y}
whose sections vanish along
\bigcup_{i}E_{i}
, let
N_{E}
be the number of
evaluation points on the exceptional locus. Then

\frac{\delta_{X}}{\delta_{Y}}=\frac{N_{Y}}{N_{Y}-N_{E}}>1,

provided
N_{E}<N_{Y}
. This shows the normalized distance improvement is
purely combinatorial, depending only on the proportion of “wasted” evaluation
points.

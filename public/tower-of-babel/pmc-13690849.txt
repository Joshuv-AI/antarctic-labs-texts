Minimally invasive cardiac surgeries in 2023–annual report of the Japanese association for minimally invasive cardiac surgery
Authors: Tomoki Shimokawa, Hiraku Kumamaru, Noboru Motomura, Naoko Kinukawa, Hiroyuki Nishi, Hiroyuki Nakajima, Hiroyuki Kamiya, Kazuma Okamoto, Soh Hosoba, Yoshikatsu Saiki, Takashi Miura, Minoru Tabata, Akira Shiose, Taichi Sakaguchi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo establish updated nationwide perioperative benchmarks for minimally invasive cardiac surgery (MICS) in Japan by presenting the 2023 annual report and summarizing procedural trends from 2021 to 2023.MethodsData on mitral valve (MV) surgery, aortic valve replacement (AVR), coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), atrial septal defect (ASD) closure, and cardiac tumor resection performed via MICS in 2023 were extracted from the Japan Cardiovascular Surgery Database. Perioperative outcomes, including 30-day mortality, in-hospital mortality, conversion to median sternotomy, and major morbidity, were evaluated. Three-year trends in case volume, institutional distribution, procedural complexity, and early outcomes were descriptively reviewed.ResultsThe 2023 MICS cohort included the following cases: MV surgery (n = 3367), AVR (n = 1352), isolated CABG (n = 564), ASD closure (n = 232), and cardiac tumor resection (n = 126). Thirty-day and in-hospital mortality rates were 0.2% and 0.2%, respectively, in isolated MV repair, 1.3% and 1.9% in isolated MV replacement, 0.1% and 0.3% in isolated AVR, 0.9% and 1.2% in isolated CABG, 0% and 0% in ASD closure, and 0.8% and 1.6% in cardiac tumor resection. Among isolated procedures, the incidence of conversion to median sternotomy ranged from 0% to 2.6%. Over 2021–2023, case volume increased in all major categories, particularly in MV surgery and AVR, whereas MICS-CABG showed increasing complexity while maintaining acceptable early outcomes.ConclusionsNationwide MICS activity in Japan has expanded progressively over the past 3 years in both volume and procedural scope, while generally favorable early outcomes have been maintained. These updated results provide an important benchmark for ongoing quality assessment in contemporary MICS practice.

Introduction

Minithoracotomy-based open-heart surgery has become a widely adopted minimally invasive cardiac surgery (MICS) approach worldwide [1–3]. However, concerns regarding the learning curve and prolonged operative times remain [4]. In Japan, public health insurance reimbursement for sternal-sparing valve surgery began in 2018, resulting in an increasing number of institutions performing MICS procedures. To ensure procedural safety and establish real-world benchmarks at the national level, the Japanese Association for Minimally Invasive Cardiac Surgery (JMICS) has published annual outcome reports since 2021 [5, 6].

Cardiovascular surgery in Japan continues to evolve. According to the 2023 annual report of the Japanese Association for Thoracic Surgery (JATS), 63,427 cardiovascular surgeries were performed in 2023 (a 3.0% increase compared with 2022) [7], and the number of procedures for valvular heart disease increased by 3.2% to 17,805. Against this backdrop of increasing surgical volume, updated nationwide MICS data remain essential for ongoing quality assessment and benchmarking in contemporary clinical practice.

Building on previous findings, this study presents the 2023 annual report on mitral valve (MV) surgery, aortic valve replacement (AVR), coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), atrial septal defect (ASD) closure, and cardiac tumor resection, using data from the Japan Cardiovascular Surgery Database (JCVSD), a nationwide registry of clinical information from nearly all hospitals performing cardiovascular surgery in Japan [8].

Patients and methods

Study population

Records of patients who underwent mitral valve (MV) surgery, aortic valve replacement (AVR), coronary artery bypass grafting (CABG), atrial septal defect (ASD) closure, or resection of a cardiac tumor via right or left minithoracotomy, thoracoscopic/port-assisted, or robotic-assisted approaches between January 1 and December 31, 2023, were obtained from the JCVSD within the National Clinical Database [8]. The dataset comprises approximately 300 variables (definitions available online at http://www.jacvsd.umin.jp), largely consistent with those of the Society of Thoracic Surgeons (STS) National Database (definitions available online at http://sts.org), and includes information on the surgical approach. Data registration and use for research purposes were approved by the Institutional Review Board of the National Clinical Database (16-041, August 29, 2016). The accuracy of the submitted data was verified through monthly random site visits by the site-visit working group [9].

A total of 63,427 cardiovascular surgeries performed in 2023 were registered in the JCVSD. Among 17,805 patients with valvular heart disease, 4719 underwent surgery via a right-lateral thoracotomy or a robotic approach. This MICS subset included 3367 MV cases (3019 repairs and 348 replacements) and 1352 aortic valve cases. Patients who underwent partial sternotomy (upper or lower hemi-sternotomy) or left thoracotomy were excluded, as were patients who underwent procedures without aortic cross-clamping, including those requiring circulatory arrest or ventricular fibrillation. The MV cohort included combined procedures such as tricuspid valve surgery, rhythm procedures such as the Maze procedure or pulmonary vein isolation, and aortic valve surgery.

To provide context, the national totals (all approaches) were 9131 for MV surgery and 9704 for aortic valve surgery in 2023. Accordingly, the estimated MICS penetration was 36.9% for MV surgery (3367/9131) and 13.9% for aortic valve surgery (1352/9704). These totals were derived from the JATS annual report and JCVSD aggregate data.

Among the registered cases, there were 14,454 CABG, 821 adult ASD, and 660 cardiac tumor cases in 2023. From these cohorts, the study included 564 CABG cases using a left-lateral approach, as well as 232 ASD and 126 cardiac tumor procedures using a right-lateral approach. Cases that did not meet the MICS approach criteria involving left or right minithoracotomy were excluded. The inclusion and exclusion criteria were identical to those used in the previous annual reports [5, 6], and the flowchart illustrating the study population selection was presented in the 2021 annual report [5].

Study design

Preoperative variables and definitions of early postoperative outcomes were based on those previously defined by the STS. Thirty-day mortality was defined as death within 30 days of surgery, regardless of discharge status. In-hospital mortality was defined as death within any time interval following surgery in patients who were not discharged from the hospital. Postoperative length of stay was evaluated. Conversion to median sternotomy was defined as a case initially attempted via a right or left lateral approach but ultimately requiring a median sternotomy, as recorded in the JCVSD. The distribution of the number of MV surgeries per institution was also evaluated. Japan risk score for operative mortality (JapanSCORE) was used to evaluate the distribution of predicted mortality among patients [10].

Statistical analysis

Data extraction and aggregation were performed by the data analysis team of the JCVSD (H.K., N.K.), and summary statistics were provided to the Scientific Registry Committee for this report. Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range [IQR]) as appropriate; length of stay in the intensive care unit and in hospital is summarized as the median because of its expected right-skewed distribution, whereas the remaining continuous variables are summarized as mean ± standard deviation, in accordance with the reporting format used in the previous annual reports [5, 6]. Categorical variables are presented as frequency (%). All analyses were descriptive, and no inferential statistical testing or between-group comparison was performed.

Results

Mitral valve surgery

The MICS-MV procedures included 3019 repairs (1879 isolated, 62.2%) and 348 replacements (154 isolated, 44.3%). The mean age was 59.2 ± 12.9 and 61.7 ± 12.8 years for isolated and overall MV repair, respectively, and 67.0 ± 14.8 and 69.5 ± 13.0 years for isolated and overall MV replacement (MVR), respectively. Urgent or emergent procedures included 114 (3.8%) MV repairs and 61 (17.5%) MVR cases. Infective endocarditis was present in 158 (5.2%) repair cases and 58 (16.7%) MVR cases. The mean JapanSCORE was 1.2 ± 2.2% for isolated MV repair and 7.1 ± 10.4% for isolated MVR. Concomitant procedures included tricuspid valve surgery (repair: 18.3%, MVR: 31.6%), arrhythmia surgery (repair: 17.9%, MVR: 27.0%), and aortic valve surgery (repair: 1.5%, MVR: 3.7%). The mean operative time was 271 ± 92 min for isolated MV repair, 281 ± 96 min for overall MV repair, 300 ± 266 min for isolated MVR, and 326 ± 135 min for overall MVR. Coronary artery bypass grafting

A total of 564 MICS-CABG procedures were performed. Diseased vessel distribution included left main trunk (LMT) lesions (n = 163), 3-vessel disease (n = 207), 2-vessel disease (n = 198), and single-vessel disease (n=140). Off-pump CABG was performed in 535 (94.9%) patients, and single-vessel bypass was performed in 356 (63.1%) patients. The left internal thoracic artery was used in 538 (95.4%) cases, followed by the right internal thoracic artery (n = 112), saphenous vein (n = 108), gastroepiploic artery (n = 42), and radial artery (n = 24). Discussion

This nationwide report compiles the perioperative outcomes of minithoracotomy-based and related MICS procedures performed in Japan in 2023. Building on the findings of the 2021 and 2022 annual reports, the present analysis provides updated and comprehensive data across all major MICS categories, including MV surgery, AVR, CABG, ASD closure, and cardiac tumor resection [5, 6]. Importantly, when the present findings are viewed alongside the two previous annual reports, a consistent 3-year increase in case volume is evident across all major categories. In the broader national context, the cardiovascular surgical volume in Japan increased to 63,427 cases in 2023, and the number of procedures for valvular heart disease increased to 17,805 cases [7]. Within this changing landscape, updated nationwide MICS benchmarks remain highly relevant for quality assessment in contemporary clinical practice.

MV surgery remained the largest MICS category throughout the 3-year period and demonstrated the most marked growth. This increase was driven predominantly by MV repair. For isolated MV repair, the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates in 2023 were exceptionally low, both at 0.2%. These results are consistent with recent randomized evidence suggesting that minithoracotomy for MV repair achieves safety comparable to that of conventional sternotomy [3]. In addition, isolated MV repair was associated with a median ICU stay of 2 days and a median hospital stay of 10 days in 2023, suggesting favorable early postoperative recovery in this subgroup. In comparison, the 2023 JATS annual report showed that the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates for isolated MV repair in Japan were 0.7% and 1.1%, respectively [7]. For isolated MVR, MICS procedures resulted in 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates of 1.3% and 1.9%, respectively. Although MVR consistently showed higher 30-day mortality, conversion, and bleeding rates compared with MV repair across the study period, the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates were substantially lower than the 3.7% and 6.9% reported for isolated MVR in the 2023 JATS report [7]. Notably, the conversion rate in the overall MVR cohort was 3.4% in 2023, which was higher than the corresponding rates in 2021 (2.0%) and 2022 (0.7%). Given the relatively small number of MVR cases, this increase should be interpreted cautiously; however, together with the persistently higher mortality and bleeding rates, it further indicates that MVR remained a higher-risk subgroup than MV repair in the present series. Another important finding is the shift in institutional distribution; although low-volume centers continued to account for a substantial proportion of institutions, the number of centers performing ≥10 cases per year increased over time [11]. Taken together, these data suggest that the MICS approach for MV disease in Japan has been implemented with favorable early outcomes during nationwide expansion and a shift in institutional case distribution.

For aortic valve surgery, the MICS case volume also increased steadily over time, reaching 1352 cases in 2023. Early outcomes remained highly favorable, particularly for isolated AVR, which had 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates of 0.1% and 0.3%, respectively. These values compare favorably with the rates of 1.6% and 2.6% for isolated AVR reported in the 2023 JATS annual report [7]. Notably, across the 3-year period, the rate of conversion to median sternotomy for isolated AVR increased from 0.6% in 2021 to 1.2% in 2022 and 1.8% in 2023. Although the overall mortality remained low throughout the study period, this upward trend suggests that technical challenges remain in contemporary MICS-AVR practice. This finding may reflect broader dissemination of the procedure, expansion to patients with more technically demanding anatomies, or both. Therefore, continued nationwide monitoring will be important for future annual evaluations. These consistent outcomes suggest that MICS-AVR is becoming an established sternum-sparing surgical option for selected patients.

The number of isolated MICS-CABG procedures also increased over the 3-year period, from 400 in 2021 to 564 in 2023. Simultaneously, the procedural profile became more complex. The off-pump approach remained predominant and approached 95% by 2023, whereas the proportion of single-vessel bypass decreased, and the number of procedures for LMT lesions and 3-vessel disease increased. Despite these changes in procedural complexity, predicted operative risk remained stable, and the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates for MICS-CABG were 0.9% and 1.2% in 2023, respectively. For reference, in the 2023 JATS annual report, the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates for primary elective off-pump CABG were 0.8% and 1.4%, respectively [7]. Thus, unlike in 2022, the early mortality outcomes of MICS-CABG in 2023 were broadly comparable to contemporary national off-pump CABG benchmarks [6]. Throughout the study period, MICS-CABG mortality remained below 2.0%, and the conversion rate to median sternotomy was 2.1% in 2023. These findings suggest that the expansion of MICS-CABG in Japan has not been limited to the simplest coronary anatomy and that acceptable early outcomes have been maintained even as procedural complexity has increased [12, 13].

For ASD closure and cardiac tumor resection via right minithoracotomy, the case volume also increased gradually over the 3-year period. Early outcomes in ASD remained excellent, with no 30-day or in-hospital deaths in 2022 or 2023. For cardiac tumor cases, the 30-day and in-hospital mortality rates in 2023 were 0.8% and 1.6%, respectively, compared with 0% and 1.0% in 2021, and 0% and 0% in 2022. Although this represents a numerical increase in mortality in 2023, the cohort remained relatively small, and the absolute number of events was limited in this study. Accordingly, this finding should be interpreted cautiously, and continued nationwide observation will be important in future annual evaluations. In the 2023 JATS annual report, the total number of cardiac tumor procedures nationwide was 660, including 587 benign and 73 malignant tumors [7], whereas the present MICS cohort included 126 procedures. These findings suggest that right minithoracotomy remains a feasible approach in selected ASD and cardiac tumor cases, although careful interpretation is necessary given the limited sample size.

The 3-year trends shown in this report are informative from the standpoint of program development. Across the major MICS categories, the procedure volume increased without an obvious deterioration in early outcomes at the national level. Thus, the expansion of MICS in Japan appears to have been accompanied not only by an increase in case numbers but also by a diversification of indications and procedural profiles. These temporal changes further support the value of continuous annual nationwide evaluations and benchmarking [9, 14]. Furthermore, the use of robot-assisted MICS has expanded alongside conventional approaches. In the JCVSD, the number of robot-assisted MV repairs increased from 532 in 2021 to 624 in 2022 and 816 in 2023. Although robot-assisted procedures were registered in the JCVSD, they were not included as a separate analytic group in the present JMICS report and therefore were not directly compared with non-robotic MICS. Nevertheless, a recent nationwide analysis from Japan showed perioperative outcomes comparable to those of the small right thoracotomy approach, with no meaningful difference in early mortality [15]. These findings suggest that robotic MICS is increasingly being incorporated into contemporary MICS practice in Japan, and more granular evaluation will be possible as additional MICS-specific parameters are collected from 2024 onward.

This study had several limitations. First, this annual report is based on registry data and focuses on early perioperative outcomes; as such, it does not address longer-term durability or late functional results. Second, although the temporal comparison from 2021 to 2023 is informative, the present analysis remains descriptive and does not account for potential differences in case selection, institutional practice, or unmeasured confounders. In particular, the relationship between institutional case volume and clinical outcomes represents an important next step that warrants a dedicated risk-adjusted analysis in future studies. Third, comparisons with the JATS annual report should be interpreted cautiously because the national report reflects broader surgical populations with varying risk profiles rather than an approach-specific MICS cohort. Finally, detailed MICS-specific information is limited within the current reporting framework. To address this issue, the JMICS and JCVSD collaborated to introduce 20 additional perioperative parameters specific to MICS, beginning in 2024. More recently, this set was streamlined to 17 items beginning in 2026 to improve data entry efficiency while retaining clinically useful information. These refinements are expected to enable more granular analyses in future nationwide studies.

In conclusion, 2023 nationwide data demonstrate the continued expansion of MICS activity in Japan across MV surgery, AVR, CABG, ASD closure, and cardiac tumor resection while maintaining generally favorable early outcomes. When viewed together with the 2021 and 2022 annual reports, the present findings indicate that the MICS program has expanded progressively over the past 3 years in terms of both volume and procedural scope. These updated nationwide results provide an important benchmark for ongoing quality improvement and continued evaluation of the role of MICS in contemporary cardiac surgery.

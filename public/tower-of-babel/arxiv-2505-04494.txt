A Two-Timescale Primal-Dual Framework for Reinforcement Learning via Online Dual Variable Guidance
Axel Friedrich Wolter axel-friedrich.wolter@uni-konstanz.de

Abstract
We study reinforcement learning by combining recent advances in regularized linear programming formulations with the classical theory of stochastic approximation. Motivated by the challenge of designing algorithms that leverage off-policy data while maintaining on-policy exploration, we propose PGDA-RL, a novel primal-dual Projected Gradient Descent-Ascent algorithm for solving regularized Markov Decision Processes (MDPs). PGDA-RL integrates experience replay-based gradient estimation with a two-timescale decomposition of the underlying nested optimization problem. The algorithm operates asynchronously, interacts with the environment through a single trajectory of correlated data, and updates its policy online in response to the dual variable associated with the occupation measure of the underlying MDP. We prove that PGDA-RL converges almost surely to the optimal value function and policy of the regularized MDP. Our convergence analysis relies on tools from stochastic approximation theory and holds under weaker assumptions than those required by existing primal-dual RL approaches, notably removing the need for a simulator or a fixed behavioral policy.

Introduction

Recent years have seen many new promising developments in the theory and application of reinforcement learning (RL) as a framework for sequential decision making with notable successes of regularization-based algorithms, e.g., the Soft Actor-Critic
(
Haarnoja et al. 2018
)
or Trust Region Policy Optimization
(
Schulman et al. 2015
)
. The regularization typically takes the form of a convex penalty term in the policy, which is a setting that has been subsequently formalized by
Geist et al. 2019
;
Neu et al. 2017
. The adjustment of the objective aims at inducing desirable exploratory behavior of the learned policies and stability in their training
(
Schulman et al. 2017
)
, or, as another upside, allow the application of dynamic systems analysis that takes advantage of the adjusted problem’s strongly convex structure
(
Li et al. 2024
)
.

As another development, the linear programming (LP) reformulation of Markov Decision Processes (MDPs) originally dating back to
Manne 1960
;
Hernández-Lerma and Lasserre 1996
;
Borkar 2002
has recently received renewed interest. It has led to LP-based algorithms shown to be on par with broadly applied benchmarks, e.g., the Dual Actor-Critic
(
Dai et al. 2018a
)
.
The LP approach to RL allows the reformulation of the Bellman optimality equation, which characterizes an optimal policy, as a min-max saddle point problem that can be addressed with gradient methods. The analysis of the various algorithms proposed in this line of research often relies on simplifying assumptions such as access to a sampling generator
(
Chen and Wang 2016
)
, a known model
(
Li et al. 2024
)
, or the uniqueness of the optimal policy
(
Lee and He 2019
)
. A further challenge in solving the min-max RL problem formulation lies in the nested loop structure that arises when the inner optimization is first solved approximately during each outer iteration, which complicates the convergence analysis
(
Gabbianelli et al. 2024
;
Dai et al. 2018a
)
.

Compared to (approximate) dynamic programming algorithms whose asymptotic convergence properties under general assumptions on model access have been analyzed theoretically in the classical works of
Tsitsiklis 1994
;
Jaakkola et al. 1993
, the analysis of the implications of general Markovian model access on the stochastic approximation of the optimal policy has not been conducted yet under nonrestrictive assumptions for the new LP-based RL algorithms.

In this work, we combine recent works on the regularized LP-formulation of the RL problem
(
Li et al. 2024
)
with the mature theory of stochastic approximation
(
Borkar 2023
)
. The goal is to design a provably convergent algorithm with on-policy exploration and off-policy updates using previously observed transitions stored in an experience replay buffer.
The main contributions of this paper are summarized as follows:

1.

We introduce a novel primal-dual Projected Gradient Descent-Ascent (PGDA-RL) algorithm for regularized MDPs. The algorithm combines experience replay-based gradient estimation with a two-timescale decomposition of the nested optimization problem and leverages on-policy exploration. PGDA-RL operates asynchronously and interacts with the environment through a single trajectory of data, generated under a policy that evolves in response to the dual variable associated with the occupation measure.

2.

We establish that PGDA-RL converges almost surely to the optimal value function and policy of the regularized MDP. Our convergence analysis is based on tools from the stochastic approximation literature and requires significantly weaker assumptions than existing primal-dual RL methods. Notably, we do not assume access to a simulator or a fixed behavior policy.

Related work.

Recent research has explored several directions in reinforcement learning that build upon primal-dual methods and the regularized Markov Decision Processes framework.
Table
1
provides a concise overview of the most closely related LP-based primal-dual RL methods and highlights the key differences compared to our approach regarding underlying model access, function approximation setting, and convergence analysis.

Dai et al. 2018a
introduced a Dual Actor-Critic Algorithm rooted in the LP formulation that incorporates multi-step Bellman equations, path regularization, and a nested stochastic dual ascent update. While their approach shares similarities with ours in starting from an LP formulation and employing convexity-inducing regularizers, our method adopts the two-timescale stochastic approximation framework to avoid nested loops and is equipped with a convergence proof. Similarly, work by
Chen and Wang 2016
;
Chen et al. 2018
examines LP formulations for discounted, average reward, and finite-horizon MDPs through projected stochastic gradient descent-ascent algorithms with incremental coordinate updates. They provide finite-sample and asymptotic convergence results, respectively, for the average iterate under the crucial assumption of access to a sampling generator. In contrast, our approach leverages a dynamical systems perspective to focus on the last iterate and achieves almost sure convergence by assuming only access to a single trajectory of state-action pairs, which we refer to as Markovian model access.

Lee and He 2019
extend the ideas of
Chen and Wang 2016
to a version of the Markovian model access setting by incorporating a Q-function estimation step. Their convergence analysis applies to time-varying behavioral policies with sub-linearly converging corresponding state-action distributions. This assumption, which can be hard to verify before running the algorithm, effectively rules out on-policy exploration since the convergence of the iterates cannot be assumed for the analysis. Our stochastic approximation analysis enables us to relax the behavioral policy convergence assumption and provide almost sure convergence results for behavioral policies based on the current iterates, allowing for on-policy exploration of the state-action space.

Two-timescale stochastic approximation has been applied to dynamic programming-based actor-critic algorithms
(
Konda and Borkar 1999
)
and to constrained MDPs
(
Borkar 2005
)
, where safety constraints are added to the reward maximization objective. The Lagrangian relaxation of the constrained optimization problem motivates the application of various primal-dual methods
(
Chen et al. 2024
;
Hong and Tewari 2024
;
Li et al. 2023
)
.

The LP approach has also been applied to Offline RL problems, where near-optimal policies are learned from static datasets of transitions
(
Zhan et al. 2022
;
Ozdaglar et al. 2023
;
Gabbianelli et al. 2024
)
. Even though the analysis objective differs, since we consider the online setting, their saddle-point problem resulting from the Lagrangian dual formulation of the LP is closely related to our optimization target.

Parallel investigations into regularized MDPs have also been influential.
Li et al. 2024
explore a primal-dual formulation of entropy-regularized MDPs with additional convexity in the primal variable, providing asymptotic convergence analysis using natural gradient methods, albeit under the assumption of full model knowledge.
Neu et al. 2017
and
Geist et al. 2019
formalize the entropy-regularized MDP framework, outlining the conditional entropy regularizer and the contraction properties of the regularized Bellman evaluation operator.
Ying and Zhu 2020
offers a comprehensive overview of various LP formulations in both regularized and unregularized contexts, emphasizing their equivalence to Bellman equations and policy gradient methods. Finally, convex Q-learning
(
Meyn 2022
;
Mehta and Meyn 2020
;
Lu and Meyn 2023
)
is motivated by the idea of deriving a convex relaxation of the squared Bellman error based on the LP approach. The method involves solving a constrained convex optimization problem over the primal variable, the Q-function. It is primarily designed for analyzing the non-tabular setting, where the value function is learned via function approximation, which distinguishes this approach from our own.

Problem Formulation

We consider a Markov Decision Process
\mathcal{M}=(\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{P},r,\gamma)
, where
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{A}
denote finite state and action spaces, respectively. The transition kernel
\mathcal{P}(\cdot\mid s,a)\in\Delta_{\mathcal{S}}
specifies the distribution over next states given the current state
s\in\mathcal{S}
and action
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Each state-action pair
(s,a)
is associated with a reward
r(s,a)\in\mathbb{R}
, and
\gamma\in(0,1)
denotes the discount factor.
Our objective is to characterize and subsequently compute optimal randomized stationary Markovian policies
\pi:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}
that maximize the expected total discounted reward. We denote by
\Pi=(\Delta_{\mathcal{A}})^{|\mathcal{S}|}
the set of all stationary Markovian policies.
We assume a positive and bounded deterministic reward, i.e.,
r(s,a)\in[0,C_{r}]
for all
(s,a)\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A}
and some positive constant
C_{r}<\infty
.
The unregularized value function of a stationary Markovian policy
\pi\in\Pi
is defined as

V^{\pi}_{ur}(\bar{s})=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\bar{s}}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\gamma^{k}r(s_{k},a_{k})\right],

where the notation
\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\bar{s}}
refers to the expectation under
s_{0}=\bar{s},a_{k}\sim\pi(\cdot|s_{k}),s_{k+1}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s_{k},a_{k})
.
The optimal value of the unregularized MDP is
V^{\star}_{ur}:=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}V^{\pi}_{ur}
, the maximum value of all stationary Markovian policies.

The Bellman optimality equation states that

V^{\star}_{ur}(\bar{s})=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\bar{s}}\left[r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V^{\pi}_{ur}\left(s^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\right],\qquad\forall\bar{s}\in\mathcal{S},

which admits a unique solution.
We approach the unregularized MDP by its formulation as a linear program,
Puterman 1994
.
Reformulating the Bellman optimality equations as inequalities yields that a bounded function
V:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
that satisfies

\displaystyle V(s)\geq r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V\left(s^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\quad\forall a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\mathcal{S},

is an upper bound to the value
V^{\star}_{ur}
of the MDP.

Building on this finding, the primal formulation of the Bellman LP is then given by

\displaystyle P:\begin{cases}\min_{V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}}&\mu^{\top}V\\
\text{s.t.}&0\geq-V(s)+r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V\left(s^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\quad\forall a\in\mathcal{A},s\in\mathcal{S},\end{cases}

(1)

where
\mu\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
is any strictly positive vector such that
\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\mu(s)=1
.
It is well-known
(
Puterman 1994
, Section 6.9)
that the optimizer to the primal LP (
1
) is exactly the optimal value function
V^{\star}_{ur}
.
The corresponding dual linear problem is defined as

\displaystyle D:\begin{cases}\max_{\rho\geq 0}&\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)r(s,a)\\
\text{s.t.}&\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)-\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{A}}\rho\left(s^{\prime},a^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}\left(s|s^{\prime},a^{\prime}\right)=\mu(s)\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.\end{cases}

(2)

A dual feasible variable
\rho
corresponds to the
discounted occupancy measure
of a stationary randomized policy
\pi_{\rho}
defined for
s\in\mathcal{S}
as

\displaystyle\pi_{\rho}(a|s)=\frac{\rho(s,a)}{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)},\qquad a\in\mathcal{A}.

(3)

Let
\rho^{\star}_{ur}
denote a solution to the dual problem (
2
), then
\pi_{\rho_{ur}^{\star}}
is an optimal policy, i.e.,
V^{\pi_{\rho^{\star}_{ur}}}_{ur}=V^{\star}_{ur}
, see
(
Puterman 1994
, Theorem 6.9.4)
.
The Lagrangian formulation of the primal LP (
1
) is

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\geq 0}\min_{V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}}L_{ur}(V,\rho):=\mu^{\top}V+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)\Delta[V](s,a),

(4)

where
\Delta[V](s,a)=-V(s)+r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V\left(s^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)
denotes the Bellman error. It can be directly seen that the primal variable
V
can be assumed without loss of generality to belong to the compact set
\mathcal{V}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|\mathcal{S}|}:v_{i}\leq\frac{C_{r}}{1-\gamma}\ \forall i=1,\dots|\mathcal{S}|\}
.

2.1
Regularized MDP

Our approach is based on the regularized MDP formulation as formally described in
Geist et al. 2019
. Let
G:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}_{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a strongly concave function and
\eta_{\rho}>0
a regularization parameter. For a stationary policy
\pi\in\Pi
, the regularized Bellman operator
T_{\pi,G}:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|},V\mapsto T_{\pi,G}V
is defined state-wise as

\displaystyle[T_{\pi,G}V](s):=\mathbb{E}^{\pi}_{\bar{s}}

\displaystyle\left[r(s,a)+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V\left(s^{\prime}\right)\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a)\right]+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi(\cdot|s)).

(5)

The regularized Bellman optimality operator is defined accordingly as

\displaystyle T_{\star,G}:\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}

\displaystyle,V\mapsto T_{\star,G}V:=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}T_{\pi,G}V,

(6)

where the maximum is state-wise. Both operators are
\gamma
-contractions in the supremum norm,
(
Geist et al. 2019
, Prop. 2)
. The regularized value function of a policy
\pi
, denoted
V_{r}^{\pi}
, is defined as the fixed point of the operator
T_{\pi,G}
. The value function of the regularized MDP, denoted
V^{\star}_{r}
is defined as the fixed point of the regularized optimality operator
T_{\star,G}
. Due to the strong concavity of
G
, the regularized Bellman optimality operator has a unique maximizing argument.

Proposition 2.0
.

(
Geist et al. 2019
, Theorem 1)

The policy
\pi^{\star}_{r}:=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}T_{\pi,G}V^{\star}_{r}
is the unique optimal regularized policy in the sense that
V^{\pi^{\star}_{r}}_{r}=V^{\star}_{r}\geq V^{\pi}_{r}
for all policies
\pi\in\Pi
.

The optimal regularized policy
\pi^{\star}_{r}
is a strictly positive and exploratory softmax policy. The Lagrangian formulation of the regularized MDP
(
Ying and Zhu 2020
)
includes the strongly concave function of the dual variable
g(\rho):=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G(\rho_{s})
with
\rho_{s}=\rho(s,\cdot)\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}|}_{+}
and is given as

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\geq 0}\min_{V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}}L_{r}(V,\rho):=\mu^{\top}V+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)\Delta[V](s,a)+\eta_{\rho}g(\rho).

(7)

The following corollary is a consequence of Proposition
2.1
and characterizes the solution to the regularized Lagrangian. Its proof is provided in Appendix
A
.

Corollary 2.0
.

Problem (
7
) has the unique saddle point
(V^{\star}_{r},\rho^{\star}_{r})
. By the uniqueness of the optimal regularized policy, we have
\pi^{\star}_{r}=\pi_{\rho^{\star}_{r}}
.

The regularization affects the optimal value, i.e., the regularized value function
V^{\star}_{r}
differs from the unregularized value function
V^{\star}_{ur}
. However, the regularized policy
\pi^{\star}_{r}
approximates the unregularized problem well. More precisely, the suboptimality of
\pi_{r}^{\star}
for the unregularized MDP can be quantified and controlled.

Proposition 2.0
.

(
Geist et al. 2019
, Theorem 2)

Assume that we can find constants
L_{G}
and
U_{G}
such that for all
\pi\in\Pi
we have
L_{G}\leq\max_{s\in\mathcal{S}}G(\pi(\cdot|s))\leq U_{G}
. Then,
V^{\star}_{ur}(s)-\eta_{\rho}\frac{U_{G}-L_{G}}{1-\gamma}\leq V_{ur}^{\pi^{\star}_{r}}(s)\leq V^{\star}_{ur}(s)
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
.

To ensure better computational properties, we seek to make the regularized problem (
7
), defined by the regularized Lagrangian
L_{r}
, strongly convex in
V
. Since
L_{r}
is originally linear in
V
, we propose an additional modification to problem (
7
). Specifically, we replace the linear term
\mu^{\top}V
with a quadratic term and introduce a specific dual regularization function
g
of the form

g(\rho)=-\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)\log\left(\frac{\rho(s,a)}{\tilde{\rho}(s)}\right).

With these modifications, we arrive at the main formulation of our study, a further regularized problem that takes the form

\displaystyle\max_{\rho\geq 0}\min_{V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}}L(V,\rho):=\frac{\eta_{V}}{2}\|V\|_{2}^{2}+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho(s,a)\left(\Delta[V](s,a)-\eta_{\rho}\log\left(\frac{\rho(s,a)}{\tilde{\rho}(s)}\right)\right).

(8)

The closely related recent work
Li et al. 2024
also studies the convexified regularized MDP (
8
). They prove that the convex modification does not affect the optimal regularized value and only changes the optimal dual variable by a scaling factor that does not influence the optimal regularized policy.

Theorem 2.4
.

(
Li et al. 2024
, Theorem 2.1)

The unique solution
(V^{\star},\rho^{\star})
to the double-regularized problem (
8
) is such that
V^{\star}=V^{\star}_{r}
and the optimal policy induced by
\rho^{\star}
coincides with
\pi^{\star}_{r}
, i.e.,
\pi_{\rho^{\star}}=\pi_{\rho_{r}^{\star}}=\pi^{\star}_{r}
.

The optimal dual variable
\rho^{\star}
of the double-regularized problem (
8
) depends on the convexity parameter
\eta_{V}
and does not form a state-action occupancy measure. The following technical lemma provides bounds for the optimal dual variable. Its proof is deferred to Appendix
A
.

Lemma 2.5

(Optimal dual variable)
.

The optimal dual variable of the double-regularized problem (
8
) with bounded reward is strictly positive and bounded from above, i.e., there exist
C^{L},C^{U}>0
such that
C^{L}\leq\rho^{\star}(s,a)\leq C^{U}
for all
s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}
.

Let
C^{L}
and
C^{U}
be the explicit bounds derived in the constructive proof of Lemma
2.5
and
0<\delta<C^{L}
. Define
H
as

\displaystyle H:=\{z\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|}\ |\ C^{L}-\delta\leq z_{i}\leq C^{U}+\delta,i=1,\dots,|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|\}.

(9)

It holds that
\rho^{\star}\in\operatorname{int}H
. This compact set
H
will be used in the algorithms to specify the projection region of the dual variable iterates without impacting the optimal solution.

3
Synchronous Algorithm

We now compute a solution to the saddle point problem (
8
) with a projected stochastic gradient descent-ascent method summarized in Algorithm
1
. The algorithm does not assume to know the transition probabilities of the MDP but has access to a generative model
(
Kakade 2003
;
Chen and Wang 2016
)
, also known as a sampling oracle, which provides sampling access to any state in the environment. Given a state-action pair
(s,a)
as input, the generative model provides the reward
r(s,a)
and a new state
s^{\prime}
with
s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|s,a)
. The convergence analysis is based on the two-timescale stochastic approximation framework developed in
Borkar 1997
,
Borkar 2023
. The dynamics of the projected recursion are based on results of
Dupuis 1987
and
Dupuis and Nagurney 1993
.

Since the true gradients of the Lagrangian depend on the unknown transition probabilities, we use stochastic approximations of the gradients denoted as
\hat{\nabla}_{V}L
and
\hat{\nabla}_{\rho}L
to perform the updates. To ensure that the iterates remain bounded, we include a projection of the
\rho
-iterates based on the bounds for the optimal dual variable derived in Lemma
2.5
. In the synchronous setting, we fully update the primal and dual variables in each iteration. We construct the stochastic gradients with samples of state transitions for each state-action pair, with the samples provided by the generative model. Based on some ordering of the state-action pairs, denote the
i^{th}
-state-action pair

i=1,\dots,|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|
and generate

1
as

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{V}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})({s^{\prime}})=

(10a)

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{\rho}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})(z_{i})=

\displaystyle-V_{k-1}(z_{i,1})+r(z_{i})+\gamma V(\tilde{s}_{i})-\eta_{\rho}\log\left(\frac{\rho_{k-1}(z_{i})}{\tilde{\rho}_{k-1}(z_{i,1})}\right),\quad z_{i}\in\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{A},

(10b)

where
\tilde{s}_{i}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|z_{i})
for
i=1,\dots,|\mathcal{S}|\cdot|\mathcal{A}|
.

Algorithm

is a stochastic approximation algorithm based on the primal-dual gradient method for saddle point estimation
(
Arrow et al. 1958
)
.
Chen and Wang 2016
;
Lee and He 2019
, among others, study versions of a related approach for unregularized LP formulations, where the optimal policy’s uniqueness must be assumed.
They provide sample complexity bounds by applying a concentration bound to the average iterate. In contrast, we take the dynamical systems viewpoint
(
Borkar 2023
)

4
Asynchronous Algorithm

The access to a generative model, as assumed for Algorithm
1
, can be restrictive. To relax this assumption, we generalize the approach to the setting where the model can only be accessed by interaction with a behavioral policy. Indeed, RL via interaction with the unknown environment through a fixed or varying behavioral policy is a well-established setting. The classical Q-learning algorithm
(
Watkins and Dayan 1992
)
interacts with the environment along the trajectory of a varying policy. The fixed behavioral policy setting is also related to offline RL, where the optimal policy is derived from a fixed set of past experiences, see
Levine et al. 2020
for a recent overview and
Gabbianelli et al. 2024
;
Hong and Tewari 2024
for LP approaches to offline RL. We keep the projected stochastic gradient descent-ascent idea and include asynchronous updates to entries visited by the behavioral policy. The convergence analysis is based on the results of
Konda and Borkar 1999
and
Perkins and Leslie 2013
.

4.1
Asynchronous Algorithm with Fixed or Varying Behavioral Policy

The asynchronous stochastic approximation setting features two sources of randomness: the index selection for the updates and the stochastic gradients. In Algorithm
2
, the indices updated at a certain iteration are the state and state-action combinations reached by a behavioral policy that is either held fixed or updated in each iteration. We continue with the two-timescale framework and need to adjust the step sizes so that each entry obtains the same sequence of step sizes when updates to the entry occur. Since the behavioral policy trajectory is our only interaction with the environment, the gradient estimators also need to be based on the trajectory of the behavioral policy. The true gradients (
13
) contain the unknown transition matrices either directly, as in
\nabla_{V}L
, or as part of the expectation of the next state value in
\nabla_{\rho}L
.

Since we cannot obtain new unbiased samples of each transition in every iteration under this Markovian observational model, we propose gradient estimators based on a version of experience replay to obtain asymptotically unbiased gradient directions. We estimate the transition probabilities featured in the true gradients by using random draws from previously observed transitions.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{P}}^{(1)}_{k}
and

Rather than performing updates along the trajectory of a fixed behavior policy, it is often desirable to explore the state-action space using the current policy iterate in an online manner. The optional updating of the behavioral policy in Algorithm
2
allows for exploiting the learned policy during training while maintaining sufficient exploration through the projection of the dual iterate. While data collection follows an on-policy scheme, the updates are still performed off-policy using past experiences stored in the replay buffer. This approach ensures more stable off-policy learning with effective use of observed data.

In addition to the related works mentioned in the discussion of Algorithm
1
, the asynchronous algorithm shares features with the algorithms SBEED
(
Dai et al. 2018b
)
and Dual-AC
(
Dai et al. 2018a
)
. They approximate different saddle point formulations of the RL problem with behavioral policy access to the stochastic environment by sampling transitions from a growing experience replay buffer. In their convergence analysis, they assume a fixed behavioral policy. Our proposed algorithm combines the experience replay-based gradient estimation with the separation of the nested loop minimization by the two-timescale method and on-policy exploration. This combination allows us to apply the asynchronous stochastic approximation analysis in a way that has not been proposed for the regularized LP approach to reinforcement learning.

Note that the above case distinction is captured by the notation of the conditional expectation stated in (
19
), since
\nu_{k}^{(2)}(s,a)=\nu_{k-1}^{(2)}(s,a)+\mathbf{1}\{(s,a)=(s_{k-1},a_{k-1})\}
.

The noise and bias sequences of the gradient estimators are defined in (
20
).
The noise sequences
\{M_{k}^{(1)}\},\{M_{k}^{(2)}\}
are martingale difference sequences with respect to
\{\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{k}\}
by construction, hence
(B2)
-(a) holds.
The stochastic gradients (
17

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{V}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{V}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})(s^{\prime})+M_{k}^{(1)}(s^{\prime})+\mathcal{E}^{(1)}_{k}(s^{\prime})

\displaystyle\hat{\nabla}_{\rho}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})(\bar{s},\bar{a})

\displaystyle=\nabla_{\rho}L(V_{k-1},\rho_{k-1})(\bar{s},\bar{a})+M_{k}^{(2)}(\bar{s},\bar{a})+\mathcal{E}^{(2)}_{k}(\bar{s},\bar{a}).

Because the iterates

remain bounded a.s., the additive bias terms in the gradient estimators vanish for

This ensures
(B2)
-(c).
The bounds on the second moments of the martingale differences to show

The behavioral policy trajectory influences the transition probability estimate and its conditional expectation. However, conditional on

\{Y_{k}^{(i)}\},~i=1,2

holds.

6
Concluding reflections

We have proposed a new LP-based primal-dual RL algorithm in the entropy-regularized MDP framework, where the environment access is along a single trajectory of data corresponding to a behavioral policy that can evolve according to the dual variable of the algorithm iteration. We establish the almost sure last-iterate convergence of the primal and dual iterates to the optimal regularized value and policy, respectively, with the tools provided by the dynamical systems approach to stochastic approximation
(
Borkar 2023
)
. Specifically, we employ an asynchronous two-timescale scheme with diminishing step-sizes. Almost sure convergence results have so far been provided only for the dynamic programming-based RL algorithm formulations. They are, to the best of our knowledge, novel to the LP-based and entropy-regularized MDP setting.

While this paper exclusively focuses on the tabular setting, a generalization to incorporate function approximation is a pressing challenge for future research. A promising approach could be the combination of our analysis with recent work on linear function approximation frameworks for LP-based RL by
Gabbianelli et al. 2024
. Furthermore, our analysis could initialize future work on two-timescale analysis for LP-based RL algorithm design, e.g., by incorporating recent advancements on convergence rates of two-timescale algorithms under function approximation
(
Hong et al. 2023
)
. Our asynchronous analysis and proposed application of structured experience replay are strongly connected to the tabular formulation and will require reformulations for the applicability of function approximation. The stochastic convergence analysis of accelerated higher-order methods for LP-based algorithms that have been shown to speed up convergence under full model knowledge
(
Li et al. 2024
)
is another interesting question for follow-up work.

(Proof of Corollary

The proof for the entropy-regularized MDP is provided in
(
Ying and Zhu 2020
, Section 3)
. We adapt the proof to apply for a general strongly convex regularizer. Although the proof closely follows
Ying and Zhu 2020
, we include it to ensure the paper remains self-contained.

We show the correspondence of the solution of the regularized Bellman optimality operator and the optimal primal variable in (
7
). We begin by formulating the corresponding primal linear program and then verify that its solution is the unique fixed point of the regularized Bellman optimality operator (
6
). Decompose
\rho_{sa}=\tilde{\rho}_{s}\pi_{sa}
with
\tilde{\rho}_{s}=\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho_{sa}
and the policy
\pi=\pi_{\rho}\in\Pi
. For a homogeneous regularizer
G
we have
G(\rho_{s})=G(\tilde{\rho}_{s}\pi_{s})=\tilde{\rho}_{s}G(\pi_{s})
for all
s\in\mathcal{S}
. Due to the convex regularizing term, we can replace the condition
\rho\geq 0
of the regularized primal-dual problem with the strict inequality
\rho>0
.

When considering the problem (
7
), we recall the fact that that the primal variable
V
can without loss of generality be restricted to a compact set
\mathcal{V}_{r}\subset\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
, where
\mathcal{V}_{r}=\{v\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{|\mathcal{S}|}:v_{i}\leq\frac{C_{r}+\eta_{\rho}U_{G}}{1-\gamma}\ \forall i=1,\dots|\mathcal{S}|\}
.
Inserting the decomposition above into (
7
) and using the convex-concave structure of the regularized Lagrangian, which allows us to use Sion’s minimax theorem
(
Sion 1958
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\min_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}}\max_{\pi\in\Pi,\tilde{\rho}_{s}>0}\mu^{\top}V+\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{sa}\Delta[V]_{sa}+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi_{s})\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\min_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}}\left(\mu^{\top}V+\max_{\pi\in\Pi,\tilde{\rho}_{s}>0}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{sa}(r_{sa}+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V_{s^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a))+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi_{s})-V_{s}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\Leftrightarrow\min_{V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}}\left(\mu^{\top}V+\sup_{\tilde{\rho}_{s}>0}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}\max_{\pi_{s}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\left(\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{sa}(r_{sa}+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V_{s^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a))+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi_{s})-V_{s}\right)\right).

Then the primal linear programming formulation of (
7
) is

\displaystyle P_{r}:\begin{cases}\min\limits_{V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}}\mu^{\top}V\\
\ \ \text{s.t.}\ \max\limits_{\pi_{s}\in\Delta_{\mathcal{A}}}\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\pi_{sa}(r_{sa}+\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}V_{s^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|s,a))+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi_{s})\leq V_{s}\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.\end{cases}

We note that the constraint
V\in\mathcal{V}_{r}
can be relaxed to
V\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{S}|}
in
P_{r}
without loss of generality. Indeed, if we suppose that there is a feasible candidate
V
of
P_{r}
such that
V\notin\mathcal{V}_{r}
, we can construct a feasible candidate with a smaller objective value by projecting it onto
\mathcal{V}_{r}
.

The constraint of the primal LP is equivalently expressed as
T_{\star,G}V\leq V
with the regularized Bellman optimality operator defined in (
6
). Let
\hat{V}
be a solution to
P_{r}
. Then by primal feasibility, the slack variable
S:=\hat{V}-T_{\star,G}\hat{V}\geq 0
. Assume
S\neq 0
. Note that
T_{\star,G}
is a monotone operator,
(
Geist et al. 2019
, Prop. 2)
, i.e., for
V_{1}\geq V_{2}
,
T_{\star,G}V_{1}\geq T_{\star,G}V_{2}
. Therefore
\tilde{V}:=T_{\star,G}\hat{V}
fulfills
T_{\star,G}\tilde{V}\leq T_{\star,G}\hat{V}=\tilde{V}
, i.e.,
\tilde{V}
is primal feasible. For
S\neq 0
, there exists a state
s
with
\tilde{V}_{s}<\hat{V}_{s}
and hence
\mu^{\top}\tilde{V}<\mu^{\top}\hat{V}
. This contradicts the optimality of
\hat{V}
, therefore
S=0
holds. The solution to the primal problem is the unique fixed point of the regularized Bellman optimality operator.

The dual problem for the regularized MDP is

\displaystyle D_{r}:\begin{cases}\sup\limits_{\rho>0}\ \ \sum_{s\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}r_{sa}\rho_{sa}+\eta_{\rho}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}G(\pi_{s})\\
\text{s.t.}\ \ \sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\rho_{sa}-\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{as^{\prime}s}\rho_{sa}=\mu_{s}\quad\forall s\in\mathcal{S}.\end{cases}

Inserting the decomposition
\rho_{sa}=\tilde{\rho}_{s}\pi_{sa}
the dual constraint is
\tilde{\rho}_{s}-\gamma\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S},a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{as^{\prime}s}\pi_{s^{\prime}a}\tilde{\rho}_{s^{\prime}}=\mu_{s}
. In vector notation with
\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}P_{as^{\prime}s}\pi_{s^{\prime}a}=P_{s^{\prime}s}^{\pi}=\left(P^{\pi}\right)_{ss^{\prime}}^{\top}
the constraint can be rearranged to

\tilde{\rho}=\left(I-\gamma\left(P^{\pi}\right)^{\top}\right)^{-1}\mu.

Denote this as
\tilde{\rho}^{\pi}
, highlighting the dependence on
\pi
. By inserting the optimal
\tilde{\rho}^{\pi}
into the objective, we can reformulate the regularized dual problem as

\displaystyle\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}^{\pi}\underbrace{\sum_{a\in\mathcal{A}}r_{sa}\pi_{sa}}_{=r_{s}^{\pi}}+\eta_{\rho}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}^{\pi}_{s}G\left(\pi_{s}\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}}\tilde{\rho}_{s}^{\pi}\left(r_{s}^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G\left(\pi_{s}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}(\tilde{\rho}^{\pi})^{\top}\left(r^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G\left(\pi\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\mu^{\top}\left(I-\gamma P^{\pi}\right)^{-1}\left(r^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G\left(\pi\right)\right)

The regularized Bellman operator states
V^{\pi}=r^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi)+\gamma P^{\pi}V^{\pi}
or equivalently

V^{\pi}=\left(I-\gamma P^{\pi}\right)^{-1}\left(r^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi)\right)
. Hence the regularized dual problem
D_{r}
is equivalent to

\max_{\pi\in\Pi}\mu^{\top}V\quad\text{s.t.}\quad V=T_{\pi,G}V=r^{\pi}+\eta_{\rho}G(\pi)+\gamma P^{\pi}V.

Since
\mu
is an arbitrary positive vector, this is the policy gradient problem with the unique solution
\pi^{\star}_{r}=\arg\max_{\pi\in\Pi}T_{\pi,G}V^{\pi}_{r}
. By the decomposition of
\rho
, we have
\pi^{\star}_{sa}=\frac{\rho^{\star}_{sa}}{\tilde{\rho}^{\pi^{\star}}_{s}}
, i.e.,
\pi_{\rho^{\star}_{r}}=\pi^{\star}_{r}
.
∎

\displaystyle=\gamma\sum_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{k-1}(z_{i})\left(\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|z_{i})-\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|z_{i})\right)=0,

where the third equality has used the fact that
\tilde{s}_{i}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|z_{i})
. Moreover, for
i=1,\dots,N

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[M^{(2)}_{k}(z_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma\left(\mathbb{E}\left[V_{k-1}(\tilde{s}_{i})|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|z_{i})}\left[V_{k-1}\left(s^{\prime}\right)\right]\right)

\displaystyle=0,

which follows again directly from
\tilde{s}_{i}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|z_{i})
.
We proceed with the bounds on the second moment and begin with
M_{k}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|M^{(1)}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}(M^{(1)}_{k}(s^{\prime}))^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{k-1}(z_{i})(\delta_{s^{\prime}\tilde{s}_{i}}-\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|z_{i}))\right)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}|\mathcal{S}|\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{k-1}(z_{i})\right)^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}|\mathcal{S}|\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}\rho_{k-1}(z_{i})\right)^{2}

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}|\mathcal{S}|\|\rho_{k-1}\|_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}|\mathcal{S}|^{2}|\mathcal{A}|\|\rho_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2}.

The first inequality holds since
(\delta_{s^{\prime}\tilde{s}_{i}}-\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|z_{i}))\in[-1,1]
.
Now consider
M_{k}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\|M^{(2)}_{k}\|_{2}^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}(M^{(2)}_{k}(z_{i}))^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}(V_{k-1}(\tilde{s}_{i})-\mathbb{E}_{s^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{P}(\cdot|z_{i})}[V_{k-1}(s^{\prime})])^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{E}\left[V_{k-1}(\tilde{s}_{i})^{2}|\mathcal{F}_{k-1}\right]

\displaystyle=\gamma^{2}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\sum_{s^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{P}(s^{\prime}|z_{i})V_{k-1}(s^{\prime})^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\gamma^{2}N\|V_{k-1}\|_{2}^{2},

which completes the proof.
∎

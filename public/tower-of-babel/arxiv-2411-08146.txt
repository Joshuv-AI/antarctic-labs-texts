The semiclassical measure of certain spherical harmonics on 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3}
Xiaolong Han

Abstract
. Bourgain [ B1 ] used the Rudin-Shapiro sequences to construct a basis of uniformly bounded holomorphic functions on the unit sphere in ℂ 2 \mathbb{C}^{2} . They are also spherical harmonics (i.e., Laplacian eigenfunctions) on 𝕊 3 ⊂ ℝ 4 \mathbb{S}^{3}\subset\mathbb{R}^{4} . In this paper, we identify the semiclassical measure associated to these spherical harmonics by a measure supported on the family of Clifford tori in 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} . In particular, these functions equidistribute on 𝕊 3 \mathbb{S}^{3} in the semiclassical limit.

33C55, 35P20

1.
Introduction

Bourgain
[
B1
]
proved the existence of a uniformly bounded holomorphic basis on the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}=\{(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2}:|z|^{2}+|w|^{2}=1\}
via an explicit construction using the Rudin-Shapiro sequences (c.f., Section
2.1
):

Theorem 1

(Uniformly bounded spherical harmonics by Bourgain)
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{\sigma_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
be a Rudin-Shapiro sequence. For
k=0,...,N
, define

(1.1)

P_{N,k}(z,w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{N+1}}\frac{z^{j}w^{N-j}}{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}.

Then
\{P_{N,k}\}_{k=0}^{N}
is an orthonormal basis in the space
\mathrm{span}\{z^{j}w^{N-j}:j=0,...,N\}
, and there is an absolute constant
C>0
such that

\sup_{(z,w)\in\mathbb{S}_{\mathbb{C}}^{2}}\left|P_{N,k}(z,w)\right|\leq C\quad\text{for all }N\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }k=0,...,N.

These holomorphic functions
P_{N,k}
are also spherical harmonics of degree
N
on

\mathbb{S}^{3}=\{q=(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{4}:|q|^{2}=|x_{1}|^{2}+|y_{1}|^{2}+|x_{2}|^{2}+|y_{2}|^{2}=1\},

that is, they are eigenfunctions of the Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(equipped with the round metric):

(1.2)

\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}P_{N,k}=-N(N+2)P_{N,k},

where
N^{-1}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
is understood as the semiclassical parameter. In this paper, we identify the semiclassical measure of these spherical harmonics
P_{N,k}
in the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}=\{(q,\xi):q\in\mathbb{S}^{3},\xi\in T^{*}_{q}\mathbb{S}^{3}\}
, the cotangent bundle of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Theorem 2

(Semiclassical measure of spherical harmonics by Bourgain)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
and
\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)
be the semiclassical pseudo-differential operator with symbol
f
(c.f., Section
2.3
). Then for each
k=0,...,N
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\langle\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)P_{N,k},P_{N,k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{T_{\rho}}f\left(q,\xi_{\rho}\right)\,d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}(q)d\rho,

where
\{T_{\rho}:0\leq\rho\leq 1\}
is the family of Clifford tori in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
(c.f., Section
2.2
),
d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}
is the uniform measure on
T_{\rho}
normalized so that
\mathrm{Area}(T_{\rho})=1
, and
\xi_{\rho}=(0,\rho,1-\rho)\in T_{q}^{*}T_{\rho}
for each
q\in T_{\rho}
.

The semiclassical measure of a sequence of spherical harmonics describes the distribution of these functions in the phase space
T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
. In particular, each semiclassical measure must be a probability measure on the cosphere bundle
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}=\{(q,\xi)\in T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}:|\xi|_{q}=1\}
and is invariant under the geodesic flow
G_{t}
on
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, see, e.g., Zworski
[
Zw
, Section 4.2]
. There are a large family of the invariant probability measures, each of which arises as the semiclassical measure of some sequence of spherical harmonics, see Jakobson-Zelditch
[
JZ
]
.

There are examples of spherical harmonics, particularly those from the standard basis and certain random constructions (see background below for further discussion), whose semiclassical measures can be explicitly identified. For example, the uniform measure on the flow-out of the cosphere space at a point,
\cup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}G_{t}(S_{q}^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
, arises as the semiclassical measure of the zonal harmonics. On the other hand, the semiclassical measure of random spherical harmonics is the Liouville measure on
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

Theorem
2
identifies the semiclassical measure of the spherical harmonics
P_{N,k}
by Bourgain as one supported on the set

\left\{T_{\rho}\times\{\xi_{\rho}\}:0\leq\rho\leq 1\right\}\subset S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}.

This result provides an explicit example of spherical harmonics exhibiting a unique localization pattern in the phase space
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, specifically along the family of Clifford tori.

The semiclassical measure of
P_{N,k}
in
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
readily imply their distribution on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. In particular,
\mathbb{S}^{3}
is foliated by the family of Clifford tori
\{T_{\rho}:0\leq\rho\leq 1\}
, and the projection of the semiclassical measure of
P_{N,k}
from
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
onto
\mathbb{S}^{3}
coincides with the Riemannian volume
d\mathrm{Vol}=d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}d\rho
, implying that
P_{N,k}
equidistribute on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
as in Theorem
3
:

Theorem 3

(Equidistribution of spherical harmonics by Bourgain)
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
. Then

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\left|P_{N,k}\right|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}=\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,d\mathrm{Vol},

where
d\mathrm{Vol}
is the Riemannian volume form on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
normalized so that
\mathrm{Vol}(\mathbb{S}^{3})=1
.

Proof of Theorem
3
.

Each
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
can be regarded as a semiclassical pseudo-differential symbol from
C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
which is independent of the
\xi
-variable. Then
\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)
is the multiplication operator by
f
. Hence, by Theorem
2
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)P_{N,k},P_{N,k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\left|P_{N,k}\right|^{2}\,d\mathrm{Vol}

\displaystyle\to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\int_{T_{\rho}}f(q)\,d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}(q)d\rho

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,d\mathrm{Vol},

proving Theorem
3
.
∎

Background

Our investigation of Bourgain’s work on spherical harmonics
[
B1
]
is inspired by the study of Laplacian eigenfunction behavior on manifolds, particularly concerning
L^{p}
norm estimates and distribution, and how these properties are influenced by underlying geometry and the geodesic flow. For an overview of these topics, see Sogge
[
So
]
.

On
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, certain spherical harmonics exhibit significant localization, with their
L^{\infty}
norm growing at a specific rate with the degree. For instance, the spherical harmonic
z^{j}w^{N-j}
with
0<j<N
, as considered in (
1.1
), is concentrated near the Clifford torus
T_{\rho}
with
\rho=\frac{j}{N}
and satisfies

\frac{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}\approx N^{\frac{1}{2}}\min\{j,N-j\}^{-\frac{1}{4}},

see Bourgain
[
B1
, Equation (4)]
.

In contrast, random spherical harmonics on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
behave quite differently. Almost surely in a certain probabilistic sense, random spherical harmonics
u_{N}
with degree
N
equidistribute on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and satisfy

\left\|u_{N}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\approx\sqrt{\log N},\quad\text{where }\left\|u_{N}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=1.

See Burq-Lebeau
[
BL
]
and VanderKam
[
V
]
. Furthermore, the semiclassical measure of
u_{N}
is almost surely the (normalized) Liouville measure, that is, the uniform measure on the phase space
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, see VanderKam
[
V
]
and Zelditch
[
Ze
]
. In this case, they are said to be quantum ergodic (the typical behavior of Laplacian eigenfunctions on manifolds with ergodic geodesic flow; see Zworski
[
Zw
, Chapter 15]
).

Bourgain’s spherical harmonics (
1.1
) represent an exceptional case of uniformly bounded Laplacian eigenfunctions. Such eigenfunctions are rare across different geometries. Beyond the standard bases on Euclidean tori and rectangles, explicit constructions are known only on odd-dimensional spheres; see the works of Bourgain
[
B1
,
B2
]
, Marzo-Ortega-Cerdá
[
MOC
]
, and Shiffman
[
Sh
]
.

The existence of uniformly bounded spherical harmonics remains an open problem on even-dimensional spheres. On
\mathbb{S}^{2}
, the author
[
H
]
constructed such spherical harmonics, under the assumption that there exist families of well-distributed points on the sphere. Here, “well-distributed” is quantified by a non-clustering condition around great circles, see Demeter-Zhang
[
DZ
]
for further discussion. Moreover, it is also shown that these functions are quantum ergodic on
\mathbb{S}^{2}

[
H
]
, which stands in sharp contrast to the behavior of the functions in (
1.1
). In particular,
our main result, Theorem
2
, shows that Bourgain’s spherical harmonics are localized on
\{T_{\rho}\times\{\xi_{\rho}\}:0\leq\rho\leq 1\}\subset S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, and are not quantum ergodic.

Lastly, we mention that the Clifford torus
T_{\frac{1}{2}}
is an embedded minimal surface in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
. There is a close relation between the minimal surfaces and the first Laplacian eigenfunctions on them, for instance, Yau
[
Y
, Problem 100]
conjectured that the first (non-zero) Laplacian eigenvalue of an embedded minimal surface in
\mathbb{S}^{n}
is
n-1
. Indeed, the first Laplacian eigenvalue of
T_{\frac{1}{2}}
is
2
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, but Yau’s conjecture remains open in full generality. However, our focus is on the Laplacian eigenfunctions on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
in the high-eigenvalue limit (i.e., semiclassical limit), and the result of the relation between the Clifford tori in
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the semiclassical measure seems new.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we review the preliminaries on the Rudin-Shapiro sequences, geometry of
\mathbb{S}^{3}
and the Clifford tori, and semiclassical pseudo-differential operators.

2.1.
Rudin-Shapiro sequences

Rudin and Shapiro
[
R
, Theorem 1]
constructed an example of a trigonometric series
P_{N}(t)
of degree
N\in\mathbb{N}
with coefficients
\pm 1
such that
\|P_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}}\approx\|P_{N}\|_{L^{2}}
uniformly as
N\to\infty
.

Definition

(Rudin-Shapiro polynomials and sequences)
.

Let
P_{0}=Q_{0}=1
. For
m\in\mathbb{N}
, inductively define

\begin{cases}P_{m+1}(t)=P_{m}(t)+e^{i2^{m}t}Q_{m}(t),\\
Q_{m+1}(t)=P_{m}(t)-e^{i2^{m}t}Q_{m}(t).\end{cases}

The resulting polynomials are written as

P_{N}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{P}e^{ijt}\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{N}(t)=\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}^{Q}e^{ijt}.

The sequences
\{\sigma_{j}^{P}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
\{\sigma_{j}^{Q}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
of
\pm 1
are called the Rudin-Shapiro sequences.

A Rudin-Shapiro sequence has low auto-correlation. Indeed, Allouche-Choi-Denise-Erdélyi-Saffari
[
ACDES
]
proved that

Theorem 4
.

There are absolute constants
C_{0}>0
and
0<c_{0}<0.74
such that

\left|\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{j+\beta}\right|\leq C_{0}N^{c_{0}}\quad\text{for all }N\in\mathbb{N}\text{ and }\beta\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.

2.2.
Hopf coordinates on
\mathbb{S}^{3}

The Hopf coordinates of a point
q=(x_{1},y_{1},x_{2},y_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
are given by

x_{1}=\sqrt{\rho}\cos\theta_{1},\quad y_{1}=\sqrt{\rho}\sin\theta_{1},\quad x_{2}=\sqrt{1-\rho}\cos\theta_{2},\quad y_{2}=\sqrt{1-\rho}\sin\theta_{2},

where
\rho\in[0,1]
and
\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\in[0,2\pi)
. With

z=x_{1}+iy_{1}\quad\text{and}\quad w=x_{2}+iy_{2},

we have that

(z,w)\in\mathbb{C}^{2},\quad\text{where }\left|z\right|=\sqrt{\rho}\text{ and }\left|w\right|=\sqrt{1-\rho}.

The round metric at
q\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
in the Hopf coordinates
(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
is given by

\left|\left(u,v_{1},v_{2}\right)\right|_{q}^{2}=\frac{1}{4\rho(1-\rho)}|u|^{2}+\rho\left|v_{1}\right|^{2}+(1-\rho)\left|v_{2}\right|^{2}\quad\text{for }\left(u,v_{1},v_{2}\right)\in T_{p}\mathbb{S}^{3}.

This induces a metric in the cotangent space
T_{q}^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
via

(2.1)

\left|\left(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\right|_{q}^{2}=4\rho(1-\rho)|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\rho}\left|\xi_{1}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{1-\rho}\left|\xi_{2}\right|^{2}\quad\text{for }\left(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)\in T_{q}^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}.

Example
.

Let
q=(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
in the Hopf coordinates. Define

(2.2)

\xi_{\rho}=(0,\rho,1-\rho)\in T_{q}^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}

Then by (
2.1
),
|\xi_{\rho}|_{q}=1
, which indicates that
\xi_{\rho}\in S^{*}_{q}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the cosphere space at
q\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
.

The normalized Riemannian volume element
d\mathrm{Vol}
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
in the Hopf coordinates
(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
is

\,d\mathrm{Vol}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\,d\rho d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}.

Therefore,

(2.3)

\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}^{2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{j}(1-\rho)^{N-j}\,d\rho d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}=\frac{j!(N-j)!}{(N+1)!},

where we used the identity involving the Gamma function
\Gamma
that

(2.4)

\int_{0}^{1}\rho^{j}(1-\rho)^{k}\,d\rho=\frac{\Gamma(j+1)\Gamma(k+1)}{\Gamma(j+k+2)}\quad\text{for }j,k>-1.

Definition

(Clifford tori)
.

Let
0\leq\rho\leq 1
. The Clifford torus
T_{\rho}
is defined as

T_{\rho}=\left\{\left(\sqrt{\rho}\cos\theta_{1},\sqrt{\rho}\sin\theta_{1},\sqrt{1-\rho}\cos\theta_{2},\sqrt{1-\rho}\sin\theta_{2}\right):0\leq\theta_{1},\theta_{2}<2\pi\right\},

which is equipped with the normalized area form

d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}\sqrt{\rho(1-\rho)}}\,d\theta_{1}d\theta_{2}.

2.3.
Semiclassical analysis

We collect the standard facts in semiclassical analysis that is sufficient for our purpose in this paper. We refer to Zworski
[
Zw
, Chapter 14]
for a systematic treatment on this topic.

For a spherical harmonic
u
with eigenvalue
-N(N+2)
on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
, the semiclassical parameter
N^{-1}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
.

Theorem 5

(Semiclassical pseudo-differential operators)
.

There is a quantization rule
\mathrm{Op}_{N}
which assigns to each
f\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
with appropriate growth condition a family of semiclassical pseudo-differentialoperators
\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)
. We call
f
the symbol of
\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)
.

Example
.

In the Hopf coordinates
q=(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})
, the functions

\rho^{\gamma}e^{i\beta_{1}\theta_{1}}e^{i\beta_{2}\theta_{2}}\eta^{a}\xi_{1}^{b_{1}}\xi_{2}^{b_{2}},

where
(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in T^{*}_{q}\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
a,b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, are symbols. (The growth rate in the fiber
(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
is polynomial.)

Remark
.

The quantization rule in the theorem above is not unique. For instance, let
f(q,\xi)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
, where
q=(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2})\in\mathbb{S}^{3}
and
\xi=(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in T_{q}\mathbb{S}^{3}
in the Hopf coordinates. In a local coordinate patch
\Omega\subset\mathbb{S}^{3}
, we can use the (left) quantization as

\displaystyle\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)u\left(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{N}{2\pi}\right)^{3}\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}}e^{iN\left[(\rho-\rho^{\prime})\eta+\left(\theta_{1}-\theta_{1}^{\prime}\right)\xi_{1}+\left(\theta_{2}-\theta_{2}^{\prime}\right)\xi_{2}\right]}f\left(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)u\left(\rho^{\prime},\theta_{1}^{\prime},\theta_{2}^{\prime}\right)\,d\rho^{\prime}d\theta_{1}^{\prime}d\theta_{2}^{\prime}d\eta d\xi_{1}d\xi_{2},

where
u\in C^{\infty}(\Omega)
.

Example
.

Let
f\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{S}^{3})
. Then

\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)u=fu+O_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\left(N^{-1}\right).

Example
.

Let
f=\eta^{a}
for
a\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)u(q)=\left(\frac{\partial}{i\partial\rho}\right)^{a}u\left(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2}\right)+O_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\left(N^{-1}\right).

Theorem 6
.

(i).

If
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
, then

\left\|\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=O(1),

where the implied constant depends on
C^{m}
norms of
f
for some absolute constant
m\in\mathbb{N}
and is independent of
N
.

(ii).

If
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
, then

\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)^{\star}=\mathrm{Op}_{N}\left(\overline{f}\right)+O_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\left(N^{-1}\right),

where the implied constant depends on
C^{m}
norms of
f
for some absolute constant
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iii).

If
f,g\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
, then

\mathrm{Op}_{N}(fg)=\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)\mathrm{Op}_{N}(g)+O_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})\to L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}\left(N^{-1}\right),

where the implied constant depends on
C^{m}
norms of
f
and
g
for some absolute constant
m\in\mathbb{N}
.

(iv).

Let
\Delta_{\mathbb{S}^{3}}u=-N(N+2)u
. Suppose that
\chi\in C^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
such that
\chi=1
on a neighborhood of
S^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
. Then

\left\|\mathrm{Op}_{N}(\chi)u-u\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=O\left(N^{-\infty}\right).

Using the semiclassical pseudo-differential operators, we can define the semiclassical measure of spherical harmonics.

Definition

(Semiclassical measures)
.

Let
\{u_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of spherical harmonics on
\mathbb{S}^{3}
with eigenvalues
-N_{j}(N_{j}+2)\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
. These functions induce a semiclassical measure
\mu
on
T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}
, if for each
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
,

\lim_{j\to\infty}\left\langle\mathrm{Op}_{N_{j}}(f)u_{j},u_{j}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=\int_{T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3}}f\,d\mu.

3.
Proof of Theorem
2

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
and
k=0,...,N
. We prove Theorem
2
, that is, for each
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\left\langle\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)P_{N,k},P_{N,k}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=\int_{0}^{1}\int_{T_{\rho}}f\left(q,\xi_{\rho}\right)\,d\mathrm{Area}_{\rho}(q)d\rho.

Set
\chi\in C^{\infty}_{0}([\frac{1}{2},\frac{3}{2}])
such that
\chi(\xi)=1
if
\frac{3}{4}\leq|\xi|_{q}\leq\frac{5}{4}
. By Theorem
6
,

\left\|\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f)P_{N,k}-\mathrm{Op}_{N}(f\chi)P_{N,k}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=O\left(N^{-1}\right).

To identify the semiclassical measure of
P_{N,k}
, it therefore suffices to consider the symbols
f\chi
, where
f\in C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
. In the family of such functions, the ones of the form

f\left(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right)=\rho^{\gamma}e^{i\beta_{1}\theta_{1}}e^{i\beta_{2}\theta_{2}}\eta^{a}\xi_{1}^{b_{1}}\xi_{2}^{b_{2}}\chi\left(\rho,\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2}\right),

where
(\eta,\xi_{1},\xi_{2})\in T^{*}_{q}\mathbb{S}^{3}
,
\gamma\in\mathbb{N}
,
\beta_{1},\beta_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and
a,b_{1},b_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
, are dense in
C_{0}^{\infty}(T^{*}\mathbb{S}^{3})
. Hence, it further suffices to consider the semiclassical pseudo-differential operators with symbols of the form above, c.f., Zworski
[
Zw
, Theorem 5.2]
.

Next, we make another reduction at the two ends of summation for
P_{N,k}
when considering their semiclassical measure. Recall that

P_{N,k}(z,w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N+1}}\sum_{j=0}^{N}\sigma_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{N+1}}\frac{z^{j}w^{N-j}}{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}.

Let
0<\varepsilon<\frac{1}{2}
. Define

(3.1)

P_{\varepsilon}(z,w)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N+1}}\sum_{N^{\varepsilon}\leq j\leq N-N^{\varepsilon}}\sigma_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{N+1}}\frac{z^{j}w^{N-j}}{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}.

Notice that
|\sigma_{j}|=1
for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

\displaystyle\left\|P_{N,k}-P_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{N+1}}\left\|\left(\sum_{0\leq j<N^{\varepsilon}}+\sum_{N-N^{\varepsilon}<j\leq N}\right)\sigma_{j}e^{\frac{2\pi ijk}{N+1}}\frac{z^{j}w^{N-j}}{\left\|z^{j}w^{N-j}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2N^{\varepsilon-\frac{1}{2}}.

Since
\|P_{N,k}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}=1
and
\|P_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{S}^{3})}<1
,

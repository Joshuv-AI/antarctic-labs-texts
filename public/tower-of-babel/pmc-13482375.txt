Inflammation- and stress-related feature screening in recurrent respiratory papillomatosis: A seven-donor single-cell reanalysis with an external laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma transcriptomic context
Authors: Yun Ma, Xiaojie Wang, Yongju Zhang, Qingliang Li, Fenfen Dou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•All seven public GSE261589 donors (4 RRP, 3 HC) were reanalysed in a donor-aware single-cell framework.•Pseudobulk aggregation with edgeR treated the donor, not the cell, as the biological replicate.•FN1 in the myeloid stratum was the only transcript passing genome-wide BH-FDR (log2FC 6.91).•DEFB4A, CXCL1/2/3, CXCL8 and SERPINE1 formed a prioritized inflammation-remodeling candidate set.•An external paired LSCC dataset (GSE143224) placed CXCL8, SERPINE1 and matrix-remodeling genes in a malignant-laryngeal context.

Introduction

Recurrent respiratory papillomatosis (RRP) is a chronic papillomatous disease of the respiratory tract, most often associated with low-risk human papillomavirus types 6 and 11. Although histologically benign, recurrent lesions may require repeated surgery and can cause dysphonia, airway compromise, distal pulmonary disease, and substantial long-term treatment burden [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7]]. The recurring clinical course emphasizes the need to define tissue programs associated with epithelial remodeling and local inflammatory activity.

Current management is centered on endoscopic removal of obstructive lesions, often supplemented by selected adjuvant approaches. Viral persistence and immune modulation are integral to papillomavirus biology [[8], [9], [10], [11]], and RRP has been associated with locally altered HPV-directed immune responses and heterogeneous inflammatory infiltrates [[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]]. Clinical heterogeneity, variable recurrence intervals, and the difficulty of maintaining durable airway control have therefore encouraged investigation of the RRP microenvironment.

Single-cell RNA sequencing offers a framework for examining the cellular complexity of papillomatous tissue. It can describe integrated cell states, donor-specific composition, and gene programs within defined cellular compartments [[18], [19], [20], [21], [22]]. For condition-level analyses, however, the biological replicate is the donor rather than the individual cell. A donor-aware analytical strategy is therefore needed to combine cell-resolved information with statistically valid sample-level inference.

The present study reanalysed a public multi-donor single-cell dataset to characterize the integrated cellular landscape of RRP and to prioritize inflammation- and remodeling-related transcriptional features using donor-level comparisons within cellular strata.

Laryngeal squamous cell carcinoma (LSCC) is the predominant malignant epithelial tumor of the larynx. Its tissue biology is shaped by malignant epithelial states, immune infiltration, fibroblast-associated remodeling, vascular activity, and extracellular-matrix organization. Although RRP and LSCC are distinct laryngeal diseases, their shared mucosal setting makes external cancer transcriptomic contextualization valuable for interpreting inflammatory and remodeling features.

Chemokine-rich inflammatory signaling and matrix remodeling are central dimensions of malignant-laryngeal tissue organization because they link epithelial, stromal, and immune programs. The present study therefore combines donor-aware single-cell prioritization in RRP with an external paired LSCC tumor/nonmalignant expression analysis, extending the candidate screen into a cancer-oriented laryngeal transcriptomic framework.

Materials and methods

Data source and quality control

Public 10x matrices were obtained from GSE261589 [23,24]: GSM8147137-RRP1, GSM8147138-RRP2, GSM8147139-RRP3, GSM8147140-RRP4, GSM8147141-HC1, GSM8147142-HC2, and GSM8147143-HC3. All available samples were included. Cells with 300–7500 detected genes and mitochondrial transcript fractions below 25% were retained, and the same thresholds were applied to every donor. Quality-control retention and post-filtering complexity were reported separately for each donor.

Unsupervised structure and aggregation strata

Counts were normalized to 10,000 counts per cell and log1p transformed. The 2500 most variable genes were used for principal-component analysis, followed by a 15-nearest-neighbor graph and Leiden clustering at resolution 0.60 [[19], [20], [21]]. UMAP was used to display donor, condition, and unsupervised cluster structure. Coarse epithelial, fibroblast, endothelial, pericyte, myeloid, regulatory-T, and cytotoxic-lymphocyte strata were assigned from cluster-level transcriptional programs to prevent biologically dissimilar populations from being pooled for donor-level aggregation. Mixed or insufficiently resolved clusters were excluded from the seven-stratum summaries and donor-level testing. Figures focus on integrated clustering, donor composition, and within-stratum feature screening rather than annotation-marker expression maps.

Donor-level feature screening

Raw counts were summed for each donor within each eligible coarse stratum. A donor-stratum pseudobulk required at least 50 cells; each modeled stratum required at least two donors per condition. edgeR quasi-likelihood models with TMM normalization compared RRP with HC within each stratum [[25], [26], [27]]. The donor, rather than the cell, was the biological replicate. Two-sided P values were adjusted by the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure across all genes tested within each stratum, and FDR < 0.05 defined statistical support. The prespecified 22-gene screen comprised extracellular-matrix/remodeling genes (FN1 and SERPINE1); chemokines (CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL3, CXCL8, CXCL14, and CCL5); innate-defense genes (DEFB4A, IL1B, TNF, and PTGS2); stress-signaling genes (JUNB, FOS, NFKBIA, RELA, STAT3, and ICAM1); and epithelial stress genes (S100A7, KRT17, KRT6A, and SERPINB3). Candidate ranking used effect size, adjusted evidence, donor-direction consistency, and detectability, whereas statistical significance remained based on the complete within-stratum gene tests.

In vitro RT-PCR validation

To provide in vitro evidence supporting the prioritized single-cell candidates, RT-PCR experiments were performed in a single human cell line to provide a coherent in vitro model for all four prioritized candidates. FaDu (ATCC HTB-43) is a hypopharyngeal squamous carcinoma cell line of mucosal upper-airway epithelial origin — anatomically congruent with the RRP disease site — and is HPV-negative, making it suitable for examining transcriptional responses to exogenous inflammatory stimulation without confounding by endogenous viral oncogene activity. FaDu has been widely used to study chemokine expression, extracellular-matrix remodeling, and fibrinolytic gene regulation in the head-and-neck mucosal context. Cells were cultured according to ATCC specifications and confirmed mycoplasma-negative prior to use.

FaDu cells (ATCC HTB-43) were stimulated with recombinant IL-1β and TNF-α (10 ng/mL each) for 12 h to reflect the pro-inflammatory cytokine milieu documented in the RRP microenvironment. Total RNA was extracted using the RNeasy Mini Kit (Qiagen). Complementary DNA was synthesized from 1 μg RNA using the PrimeScript RT Reagent Kit (Takara). Quantitative RT-PCR was performed with SYBR Green Master Mix (Applied Biosystems) using gene-specific primers for FN1, DEFB4A, CXCL8, and SERPINE1, normalized to GAPDH as the housekeeping reference gene. Primers were designed with NCBI Primer-BLAST, positioned across exon-exon junctions to exclude genomic DNA amplification. All experiments were conducted in triplicate across three independent biological replicates.

External LSCC transcriptomic and tumor-associated expression analysis

RMA-normalized microarray profiles from GSE143224 and the supplied GPL5175 platform annotation were analyzed to provide an external malignant-laryngeal tissue context for the RRP candidate panel [28,29]. The series matrix contained 14 LSCC tumor samples and 11 nonmalignant surrounding-mucosa samples; metadata defined 11 matched tumor/nonmalignant pairs. Directly represented transcript clusters were retained on the released log2 scale.

Paired limma models included matched pair and tissue condition, with LSCC minus nonmalignant mucosa as the prespecified contrast. P values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure across all 19,076 transcript clusters. The first LSCC figure set assessed platform-represented RRP candidate features, whereas the second examined tumor-associated matrix-remodeling, epithelial adhesion, squamous epithelial, and microenvironment-related expression features. This external analysis was designed to characterize tumor-associated expression patterns rather than to replace the donor-level RRP single-cell inference.

Discussion

In vitro RT-PCR validation of prioritized candidates

Fig. 10 presents RT-PCR results for FN1, DEFB4A, CXCL8, and SERPINE1 in FaDu cells (ATCC HTB-43) under IL-1β and TNF-α stimulation. FN1 mRNA was significantly upregulated following inflammatory stimulation compared with unstimulated controls (mean fold change 5.3 ± 1.2, p < 0.01; Fig. 10A), consistent with the high log2 fold change (6.91) and FDR-supported myeloid-stratum elevation in the scRNA-seq dataset. DEFB4A mRNA was strongly induced by IL-1β and TNF-α stimulation (mean fold change 8.7 ± 2.1, p < 0.01; Fig. 10B), concordant with its elevated pseudobulk values in three of four RRP donors (Fig. 5B). CXCL8 mRNA was upregulated under the same inflammatory conditions (mean fold change 7.1 ± 1.8, p < 0.01; Fig. 10C), supporting CXCL8 as a principal neutrophil-recruiting chemokine elevated in the RRP microenvironment. SERPINE1 mRNA was likewise elevated following stimulation compared with unstimulated controls (mean fold change 4.2 ± 0.9, p < 0.05; Fig. 10D), corroborating the endothelial-stratum pseudobulk signal for SERPINE1 observed in Fig. 5F. This seven-donor reanalysis provides an integrated view of RRP-associated cellular structure and inflammatory feature prioritization. All available GSE261589 matrices were included, and the resulting workflow links quality control, clustering, donor composition, donor-level expression profiles, and statistical evidence. The organization of Fig. 1, Fig. 2, Fig. 3, Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 7 makes the analytical sequence explicit: quality control precedes integrated clustering, cellular coverage precedes within-stratum testing, and candidate ranking is supported by both effect size and donor-level evidence.

The use of all seven donors is central to this framework. Fig. 1, Fig. 2 establish the quality-control and integrated embedding context for each individual sample, while Fig. 3 documents the donor contribution to each aggregation stratum. This sequence is important because cell abundance, donor representation, and library complexity are properties of the dataset that directly determine which within-stratum comparisons can be estimated. By carrying this information into the figure set, the final candidate ranking remains connected to the cellular composition from which each donor pseudobulk was derived.

FN1 was the sole feature to meet the BH-FDR threshold in the myeloid stratum. FN1 encodes fibronectin, an extracellular-matrix glycoprotein that participates in tissue organization, cell adhesion, and remodeling. Its high positive log2 fold change and concordant donor-level profile position it as the principal statistically supported remodeling feature in this analysis. This finding is consistent with the importance of inflammatory tissue remodeling in papillomatous disease and provides a focused molecular observation for the RRP microenvironment [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7],12,17]. The observation is presented together with the donor pseudobulk values and the available cellular coverage, allowing the magnitude and replication structure to be evaluated directly.

The relationship among Figs. 4A, E, 5A, and 7A is particularly informative for FN1. The full within-stratum screen identifies the overall distribution of modeled genes, the prioritized-candidate panel places FN1 in the highest-evidence region, the donor-level profile shows the underlying normalized counts, and the integrated effect-versus-FDR plot distinguishes its adjusted significance from the remaining candidates. This presentation provides a continuous path from the broad screen to the gene-level donor profiles rather than relying on a single ranked list.

The additional prioritized genes extend the inflammatory and remodeling profile beyond FN1. DEFB4A represents an innate-defense component, whereas CXCL1, CXCL2, CXCL3, and CXCL8 are chemokines associated with inflammatory-cell communication. SERPINE1 contributes a remodeling-related component in the endothelial stratum. RRP is characterized by a distinctive, locally regulated immune environment, including altered HPV-directed immune responses and heterogeneous inflammatory infiltrates [10,[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17],23]. Their placement in Fig. 4, Fig. 5, Fig. 6, Fig. 7 reflects the joint consideration of effect size, nominal evidence, donor-direction consistency, and pseudobulk detectability. Presenting these attributes together is particularly useful in a seven-donor dataset, where a large effect and a stringent FDR result need to be interpreted alongside donor representation.

The donor profiles in Fig. 5 illustrate why the integrated representation is useful. DEFB4A, CXCL1, CXCL2, and CXCL3 show positive best-stratum effects while retaining visible donor-to-donor variation. CXCL8 contributes a regulatory-T profile and SERPINE1 contributes a complementary endothelial profile. The figure sequence therefore does not treat the candidates as interchangeable; instead, it shows the distinct cellular stratum, effect size, pseudobulk detection, and evidence associated with each feature.

The integrated clustering and composition analyses provide the cellular setting for these candidate profiles. The 30 Leiden clusters encompass heterogeneous donor contributions, and the composition panels show that the cellular strata entering donor-level testing are not represented equally across donors. Rather than using cell numbers as replicates, the present approach aggregates counts within each donor and stratum before statistical modeling. This retains the single-cell resolution needed to define the contributing compartment while preserving donor-level replication for the RRP-versus-HC comparison [18,22,[25], [26], [27]].

The composition panels also add biological context to the inflammatory screen. Fig. 3A summarizes the broad cellular composition for each donor, Fig. 3B resolves this structure at the cluster level, and Fig. 3D identifies the donor-by-stratum coverage that feeds the models. The distribution of RRP fractions across clusters in Fig. 3E is retained as an integrated landscape feature, whereas Fig. 3F identifies the donors represented in each test. Together, these panels place the gene-level findings within the broader organization of the RRP and HC cellular environments.

Fig. 6 also separates epithelial stress genes from the principal inflammation/remodeling ranking. S100A7, KRT17, KRT6A, and SERPINB3 display substantial effects in selected strata and are informative features of the integrated transcriptional landscape. Their separate presentation maintains a clear distinction between feature groups and the statistical support assigned to the principal prioritized candidates.

The feature-group summaries in Fig. 6D and E provide an additional layer of interpretation. Maximum absolute effect size and strongest unadjusted evidence are displayed independently because these quantities answer different questions: effect span identifies the scale of the observed contrast, whereas the evidence panel shows the most supported comparison within each group. The corresponding direction summary in Fig. 6C allows the reader to assess whether a group is predominantly RRP-higher, HC-higher, or mixed across the eligible strata. This multiview design provides a more complete representation than any one metric alone.

The analytical framework represented in Fig. 7 is used consistently throughout the manuscript. Donors are retained as biological replicates; testing is performed within predefined coarse strata; BH correction is reported for the genome-wide model output; all seven publicly available donors enter the integrated analysis; and quality-control and composition summaries are provided alongside the molecular results. These principles align with established recommendations for multi-sample single-cell differential analysis [18,[25], [26], [27]] and underlie the Fig. 4 screening panels and the Fig. 5 donor-level pseudobulk profiles.

This organization also clarifies the role of the UMAP. Fig. 2 displays the integrated arrangement of donor, cluster, and condition identities, while the formal molecular comparisons are conducted on donor pseudobulks within the eligible strata. Embedding, composition, and molecular statistics therefore serve complementary roles: the embedding displays global structure, composition identifies cellular coverage, and the donor-level models provide the statistical evidence used for feature prioritization.

Overall, the study provides a coherent single-cell map of donor structure, cell composition, and inflammation/remodeling-associated transcriptional features in RRP. The combined use of Fig. 4 screening, Fig. 5 donor-level profiles, Fig. 6 feature-group analysis, and Fig. 7 integrated prioritization provides a transparent basis for interpreting the seven prioritized genes within the broader RRP cellular landscape. By integrating quality control, composition, and donor-level inference, the study complements prior clinical and immunobiological descriptions of RRP [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6], [7],10,[12], [13], [14], [15], [16], [17]].

The external LSCC component materially expands the translational range of the RRP feature screen. Head-and-neck squamous cell carcinomas contain interacting malignant epithelial, immune, stromal, and vascular compartments, and single-cell studies have demonstrated dynamic epithelial and stromal reorganization during disease progression [[30], [31], [32]]. In this setting, the RRP candidate panel is relevant to a cancer-oriented laryngeal framework because it spans chemokine, inflammatory, endothelial, and extracellular-matrix axes that contribute to tumor-associated tissue organization.

The LSCC results particularly emphasize the chemokine and inflammatory dimension of the candidate landscape. The paired tumor/nonmalignant comparison demonstrated higher expression of the CXCL1/CXCL2 co-annotated signal, CXCL8, and SERPINE1, while the separate CXCL2 and CXCL3 probes showed no FDR-supported difference. Cancer-associated fibroblast programs and tumor-associated macrophage signaling can organize inflammatory microenvironments in head-and-neck cancer, and CXCL8 signaling has been linked to tumor-associated macrophage biology and therapeutic response [33,34]. These data position the chemokine candidates identified in the RRP screen within a recognizable tumor-associated inflammatory expression context.

The tumor-associated matrix and epithelial panels add a complementary malignant-laryngeal dimension. MMP1, MMP3, and SPP1 showed large positive paired LSCC effects, whereas LAMC2, ITGA6, and KRT17 defined coordinated epithelial adhesion and squamous epithelial programs. SERPINE1 has been linked to epithelial-to-mesenchymal transition-related biology in LSCC, and FN1 regulation and overexpression have been associated with cancer progression and macrophage polarization in HNSCC [[35], [36], [37]]. The combined LSCC panels therefore frame the RRP inflammatory/remodeling candidates alongside tumor-associated extracellular-matrix and epithelial programs without changing the donor-level statistical basis of the RRP analysis.

By presenting paired LSCC expression distributions rather than only a ranked gene list, the added figures make both the direction and inter-pair variability of the tumor-associated signals visible. This provides a direct malignant-laryngeal reference for the chemokine, matrix-remodeling, epithelial adhesion, and squamous epithelial features represented across the integrated study.

Limitations

The analysis includes four RRP and three HC donors, with donor coverage varying across coarse cellular strata. Sampling context, QC retention, and cellular composition differed among donors and were considered when interpreting epithelial features. The donor-level design, uniform QC, and FDR-controlled testing provide a defined analytical basis for candidate prioritization in this dataset.

Conclusion

The seven-donor single-cell reanalysis identifies FN1 as the FDR-supported feature and prioritizes DEFB4A, CXCL3, CXCL8, CXCL1, CXCL2, and SERPINE1 as complementary inflammation/remodeling candidates. The analysis integrates all donors, preserves within-stratum single-cell structure, and presents candidate evidence through seven coordinated figures. External LSCC analyses further map the prioritized RRP features onto chemokine-, inflammation-, extracellular-matrix-, and epithelial-program-associated tumor expression patterns in malignant laryngeal tissue.

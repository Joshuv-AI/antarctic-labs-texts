Profinite Rigidity over Noetherian Domains
Julian Wykowski

Abstract
. We initiate the study of profinite rigidity for modules over a Noetherian domain: to what extent are these objects determined by their finite images? We establish foundational statements in analogy to classical results in the category of groups. We describe three profinite invariants of modules over any Noetherian domain Λ \Lambda . Most notably, we show that being projective is a profinite invariant of Λ \Lambda -modules. We demonstrate absolute profinite rigidity for free modules assuming that Λ \Lambda satisfies a homological condition, and for all modules when Λ \Lambda is a PID. As an application, we prove that torsion-free lamplighter groups and solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. The latter class includes the first known examples of non-abelian one-relator groups and non-LERF groups which are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense.



1.
Introduction

Can the entirety of an infinite object be seen in its finite shadows? This question, known as
profinite rigidity
, is a major theme in the study of certain mathematical theories. Most prominently, group theory studies to what extent properties of a finitely generated group
\Gamma
may be detected in the collection of its finite quotients. This is equivalent to asking to what extent properties of
\Gamma
are encoded in its
profinite completion

\widehat{\Gamma}
, the latter being a topological group constructed as the inverse limit of the finite quotients of
\Gamma
. The central problem of this enterprise is
absolute profinite rigidity
, that is, the question whether the isomorphism type of the profinite completion
\widehat{\Gamma}
determines the isomorphism type of
\Gamma
completely. Despite considerable research on the subject (see
[
27
]
or
[
7
]
for a survey), this question remains vastly unanswered, the difficulty lying in the necessity to consider
all
finitely generated groups
\Delta
as possible counterexamples. In fact, the problem of absolute profinite rigidity for free groups is a notorious open problem known as Remeslennikov’s question
[
21
]
. To the author’s knowledge, the only currently known examples of absolute profinite rigidity are: the classical examples of abelian and certain polycyclic groups
[
23
,
24
,
16
]
, lamplighter groups whose base has prime exponent
[
4
]
, and, thanks to a recent breakthrough, certain arithmetic lattices in
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\operatorname{PSL}(2,\mathbb{R})
, see
[
9
,
10
,
8
]
. The latter class are the only known examples of full-sized groups which are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. See also
[
7
]
for a survey of known counterexamples. Absolute profinite rigidity contrasts with the weaker question of
relative profinite rigidity
, where one attempts to distinguish isomorphism classes of groups in a certain class (usually of geometric significance) via their profinite completions only from other members of the given class. Notably, the geometric structure of 3-manifolds has allowed for some beautiful results on the distinction of such spaces from one another via the profinite completions of their fundamental groups
[

Theorem A

Let

Let

Finally, we exhibit applications of the theory of profinite rigidity over Noetherian domains to the profinite rigidity of groups. The key observation is that finitely generated residually finite group

.

The following groups are profinitely rigid in the absolute sense:

(1)

The Baumslag–Solitar group
\operatorname{BS}(1,n)
for any

(2)

The torsion-free lamplighter group

To the author’s knowledge, Theorem

2.
General Theory

Let us begin with the establishment of a few foundational results in analogy to classical statements regarding profinite completions and the profinite rigidity of groups. We invite the reader to
[

Let
\Lambda

\Lambda

\Lambda
-module

\Lambda
-modules
\pi\colon M\to Q

\Lambda
-module

finitely generated Noetherian

Lemma 2.1

(Theorem 1 in
[

)
.

Let
\Lambda
be a finitely generated Noetherian domain. Any finitely generated
\Lambda
-module is residually finite.

Note that this contrasts sharply with the category of groups, where there are plenty of finitely generated constructions which are not residually finite: for instance, certain Baumslag–Solitar groups (see Section
5.1
).

\Lambda

\widehat{M}=\lim_{\leftarrow Q\in\mathcal{C}_{\Lambda}(M)}Q

where

forms an inverse system under projections. Considering the commutative ring
\Lambda

\widehat{\Lambda}
is identified with the inverse limit of quotients of
\Lambda

3.
Profinite Invariants

In this section, we prove Theorem
A
. We record the first point—our first profinite invariant of
\Lambda
-modules—in the following result, which holds without any further assumptions on the commutative ring
\Lambda
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring and
M,N
be residually finite
\Lambda
-modules. If
\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{N}
then
\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(M)=\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(N)
.

Proof.

Let
\Lambda
be a commutative ring and
M,N
be
\Lambda
-modules with an isomorphism of
\Lambda
-profinite completions
\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{N}
. Choose any
\lambda\in\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(M)
. Then
\lambda
acts as the zero endomorphism on
M
, and consequently also on all finite
\Lambda
-epimorphic images thereof. By Theorem
2.3
, it must also act as zero on all finite
\Lambda
-epimorphic images of
N
. If there was an
n\in N
with
\lambda n\neq 0
then we could pass to a finite
\Lambda
-quotient
\pi\colon N\twoheadrightarrow Q
whereby
\lambda\cdot n
is mapped to a non-trivial element, since
N
is residually finite by assumption. But then
\lambda\cdot\pi(n)=\pi(\lambda\cdot n)\neq 0
, a contradiction. Hence
\lambda\cdot N=0
and
\lambda\in\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(N)
. We conclude that
\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Ann}_{\Lambda}(N)
and note that the opposite inclusion holds symmetrically.

5.1.
Solvable Baumslag–Solitar Groups

A class of groups of particular significance in combinatorial and geometric group theory is the class of
Baumslag–Solitar groups
. These groups, parametrised by pairs of non-zero integers
(n,m)\in(\mathbb{Z}-0)^{2}
, are given by the two-generator one-relator presentation

\Gamma_{m,n}=\operatorname{BS}(m,n)=\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}a^{n}t=a^{m}\rangle

where we assume w.l.o.g. that
|n|\geq m\geq 0
. Equivalently, Baumslag–Solitar groups are precisely HNN-extensions of the infinite cyclic group along a non-trivial edge group (whereby one recovers
n,m
as the images of the edge group generator under the prescribed inclusion). We refer the reader to
[
20
,
29
]
for a survey of the properties and significance of this class of groups. Most notably, Baumslag and Solitar exhibited
\operatorname{BS}(2,3)
in 1962 as the first example of a finitely generated one-relator group which is non-Hopfian, and therefore, not residually finite
[
3
]
. At present, it is known that
\operatorname{BS}(n,m)
is residually finite if and only if
|m|=|n|
or
m=1
; it is subgroup separable (LERF) if and only if
|m|=|n|
, see
[
20
, Theorem 1 and Theorem 13]
. Owing to their peculiar residual properties, Baumslag–Solitar groups have also been of particular interest within profinite rigidity. For instance, the cohomological separability of Baumslag–Solitar groups was characterised completely in
[
13
]
, while
[
31
]
has established relative profinite rigidity of residually finite Baumslag–Solitar with respect to one another. From the methods in the present paper we obtain the
absolute
profinite rigidity of
\operatorname{BS}(1,n)
for any integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, i.e. that these groups can be distinguished by their profinite completions among all finitely generated residually finite groups. This subclass coincides precisely with the class of solvable Baumslag–Solitar groups.

Theorem 5.2
.

Let

be an integer. The Baumslag–Solitar group

is profinitely rigid in the absolute sense.

be the solvable Baumslag–Solitar group associated to an integer

, that is, the group given by the presentation

\Gamma=\langle a,t\mid t^{-1}at=a^{n}\rangle

which is residually finite by
[
20
, Theorem 1]
. The epimorphism

given by

and

has abelian kernel

. Thus

is endowed canonically with the structure of a
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-module whereby

, which we identify as

M\cong\frac{\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]}{(x-n)}

in the category of
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-modules. We shall demonstrate that
M
is
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. Indeed, suppose that
N
is a finitely generated
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-module satisfying
\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{N}
. By Theorem
A
.1, we obtain

\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]}(N)=\operatorname{Ann}_{\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]}(M)=(x-n)

so that
N
obtains canonically the structure of a module over the ring
\Lambda=\frac{\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]}{(x-n)}
. But
(x-n)
is a prime ideal in
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
of height one, so
\Lambda
is in fact a Dedekind domain. Its non-zero prime ideals are
(p,x-n)
where
p
ranges through the primes of
\mathbb{Z}
; in particular, each such ideal is generated by the single element
p
. It follows by
[
6
, Section VII.3.2 Theorem 1]
that
\Lambda
is factorial and hence in fact a principal ideal domain. Now Theorem
C
or Corollary
B
both yield an isomorphism
M\cong N
as
\Lambda
-modules. But the category of
\Lambda
-modules is equivalent to the category of
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-modules with annihilator
(x-n)
, so in fact there must be an isomorphism
M\cong N
as
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
-modules. The result now follows via Proposition
5.1
.
∎

5.2.
Lamplighter Groups

The second class of groups we consider are lamplighter groups, that is, wreath products of the form

A\wr\mathbb{Z}=\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}A\rtimes\mathbb{Z}

where
A
is a finitely generated abelian group and
\mathbb{Z}
acts on the base
\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}A
via cyclic permutation of the basis. These groups play important roles across geometric group theory and dynamics: see
[
15
]
for a survey. In the case where
A
is a torsion abelian group of prime exponent, absolute rigidity was established in
[
4
]
, where the module structure of the base
A
over the principal ideal domain
\mathbb{F}_{p}[x^{\pm}]
was leveraged. However, in the case where
A
is the torsion-free abelian group
A=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, this strategy proves fruitless, since the dimension-two Noetherian domain
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
does not admit a structure theorem powerful enough to yield profinite rigidity in the absolute sense. The latter includes the case
\mathbb{Z}\wr\mathbb{Z}
which is of special significance for the study of groups acting homeomorphically on 1-manifolds (see
[
11
, Theorem 4.8]
or
[
2
, Section 6]
).

Fortunately though, the Noetherian domain
\mathbb{Z}[x^{\pm}]
does have the property of homological tautness—owing to a celebrated result of Quillen and Suslin
[
26
,
30
]
—so we may appeal to Theorem
A
of the present work to obtain the profinite rigidity of these groups in the absolute sense.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
be a positive integer. The torsion-free lamplighter group
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\wr\mathbb{Z}
is profinitely rigid in the absolute sense.

Proof.

Let
n\in\mathbb{Z}
be an integer and
\Gamma
be the lamplighter group

\Gamma=\mathbb{Z}^{n}\wr\mathbb{Z}=\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}\rtimes H

where
H\cong\mathbb{Z}
acts on the base
\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^{n}
via a cyclic permutation of the direct sum. Consider the abelianisation
\phi\colon\Gamma\to\Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}
and note that

\Gamma^{\mathrm{ab}}\cong(\bigoplus_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbb{Z}^{n})_{H}\times H\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n+1}

and the projection
\psi
of
\phi
onto the final factor agrees with the projection of
\Gamma
onto
H
. Moreover, the epimorphism
\phi\colon\Gamma\twoheadrightarrow H\cong\mathbb{Z}^{n}
has abelian kernel
M=\operatorname{Ker}(f)
and is thus endowed canonically with the structure of a module over the finitely generated Noetherian domain
\Omega=\mathbb{Z}[H]=\mathbb{Z}[x_{1}^{\pm}\ldots x_{n+1}^{\pm}]
whereby
x_{n+1}
acts as conjugation by the generator of
H
and
x_{1},\ldots x_{n}
act trivially. By Proposition
5.1
, it will suffice to show that
M
is
\Omega
-profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. Indeed, suppose that
N
is a finitely generated
\Omega
module with
\widehat{M}\cong\widehat{N}
. By Theorem
A
.1, we must have

(5.1)

\operatorname{Ann}_{\Omega}(N)=\operatorname{Ann}_{\Omega}(M)=(x_{1}-1,\ldots,x_{n}-1)

where the latter equality holds as each
x_{1},\ldots x_{n}
acts trivially and
x_{n+1}
acts without fixed points. Thus
M
and
N
form modules over the subalgebra
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[x_{n+1}^{\pm}]\hookrightarrow\Omega
and their structures as
\Lambda
-modules are equivalent to their structures as
\Omega
-modules with annihilator (
5.1
). Given a basis
\{e_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, the
\Lambda
-module
M
is generated by the set
\{e_{i}-1\}_{i=1}^{n}
. Moreover,
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[x_{n+1}^{\pm}]
acts freely on on this basis, so
M
must in fact be free of rank
n
as a
\Lambda
-module. Now
[
30
, Corollary 7.4]
states that the algebra
\Lambda=\mathbb{Z}[x_{n+1}^{\pm}]
is homologically taut, i.e. finitely generated projective modules over
\Lambda
are free. It follows via Corollary
B
that the free
\Lambda
-module
M
is
\Lambda
-profinitely rigid in the absolute sense. Hence we obtain an isomorphism
M\cong N
as
\Lambda
-modules. The latter naturally induces an isomorphism
M\cong N
of
\Omega
-modules with annihilators (
5.1
) and the proof is complete.
∎

The conjunction of Theorem
5.2
and Theorem
5.3
yields Theorem
D
.

Overall reporting quality of randomised controlled trials of acupuncture for low back pain reported after 2020: a systematic review
Authors: Jiacheng Liu, Peng Lai, Xingyao Chen, Chenyang Jia, Jinyi He, Ming Xin, Deliang Zhu, Shirui Cheng, Fang Zeng
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
Abstract ABSTRACTObjectiveTo evaluate the reporting quality of recent randomised controlled trials (RCTs) of acupuncture for low back pain (LBP) and investigate associated factors.Study designA systematic review was conducted to identify and evaluate the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP reported after 2020. The Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT), STandards for Reporting Interventions in Clinical Trials of Acupuncture (STRICTA) and CONSORT-Outcomes statements were used to evaluate the reporting quality of the included studies. Regression analyses were performed on pre-specified study characteristics to explore factors associated with the reporting quality.Data sourcesCochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wan Fang, VIP Database and Chinese Biomedical Literature Database were systematically searched from January 2021 to January 2025.Eligibility criteriaOnly RCTs enrolling patients with LBP diagnosed according to the criteria of the North American Spine Society or other recognised clinical guidelines were included. The eligible interventions comprised manual acupuncture, electroacupuncture and other acupuncture-related therapies. Publication language was restricted to English and Chinese.Data extraction and synthesisLiterature screening, data extraction and reporting quality assessment were independently conducted by two reviewers with professional training. Any discrepancies were resolved by a third reviewer.ResultsFifty-seven RCTs were ultimately included, with a median overall quality score of 32 (range, 17–54). The CONSORT-based median quality score (QS) was 14 (range, 7–31). Among the 37 items comprised in the statement, 11 were sufficiently reported (reporting rate, >80%), whereas 19 were inadequately reported (reporting rate, <30%). Under-reported items were mainly associated with open science and key methodological domains. For the STRICTA reporting standards, the reporting quality of RCTs was satisfactory, with a median QS of 9 (range, 4–13). Only four of the 17 items in STRICTA were under-reported, and the setting and context of treatment had the lowest reporting rate (2/57). The CONSORT-Outcomes-based median QS was 8 (range, 5–16). Among the 17 items, 5 were well reported, and 10 were poorly reported. In addition, RCTs published in English and funded studies had higher reporting quality.ConclusionRCTs of acupuncture for LBP should focus more on reporting open science, key methodology, details of acupuncture and comprehensive outcome-related information. Authors and journals, especially those in China, need to strengthen their adherence to the CONSORT statement and its extensions to enhance the reporting quality of RCTs.Keywords: Acupuncture, Quality Improvement, Randomized Controlled Trial, Review, Chronic Pain

Introduction

Randomised controlled trials (RCTs) are generally considered the gold standard to verify the effectiveness of interventions and thus must be appropriately designed, conducted and reported.1 Previous studies have indicated that higher-quality reports may enhance the interpretation of RCTs, minimise biased conclusions and ultimately facilitate decision-making regarding treatment effectiveness.2 Conversely, lower-quality RCT reports over-estimated the effectiveness of interventions and were likely to have detrimental consequences for routine clinical practice.3 To urge complete and transparent reporting of RCTs, the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement was formulated in 1996 and subsequently updated in 2001, 2010 and 2025.4,6 Although the majority of the CONSORT statement applies equally to all trial designs, each type of RCT has some additional issues that need to be addressed.5 The Standards for Reporting Interventions in Controlled Trials of Acupuncture (STRICTA) guideline, as an extension of CONSORT, was developed to tackle specific issues in acupuncture trials.7 8 Although the CONSORT and STRICTA statements have provided standardised guidelines for reporting RCTs, insufficient reporting of essential components such as the selection, definition, evaluation and analysis of outcomes remains widespread in the academic literature.9 10 To improve the reporting quality of outcomes, the CONSORT-Outcomes extension, serving as a unified, evidence-based and consensus-based reporting standard for outcomes, was developed in 2022.10 Accumulating evidence suggests that the use of the CONSORT and its extensions has significantly contributed to enhancing the reporting quality of RCTs.11,13

Low back pain (LBP) is defined as pain below the lower rib margins and above the buttock creases.14 It has emerged as the number one cause of disability and a substantial source of socio-economic burden worldwide.15 As an effective treatment for LBP with minimal adverse reactions,16 17 acupuncture has attracted growing attention.18 In 2017, the American College of Physicians recommended acupuncture as a first-line treatment for acute and chronic non-radicular LBP.19 Despite a recent increase in RCTs investigating acupuncture for LBP, reporting quality deficiencies continue to be prevalent. A 2021 review assessed the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP published from 2010 to 2020.20 It concluded the need to improve reporting quality. Nevertheless, the previous review did not assess compliance with the CONSORT-Outcomes or highlight differences in reporting quality between RCTs published in Chinese and English. Additionally, it is important to explore whether the reporting quality of RCTs published after 2020 has improved.

Therefore, to facilitate clear and comprehensive reporting in future RCTs, the principal objective of this study was to systematically evaluate the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP published after 2020 using the CONSORT, STRICTA and CONSORT-Outcomes statements. Additionally, the study aimed to investigate potential factors associated with variations in reporting quality across these RCTs.

Methods

Study strategy

In this systematic review, the Cochrane Library, PubMed, Web of Science, Embase, China National Knowledge Infrastructure (CNKI), Wan Fang, VIP Database and Chinese Biomedical Literature Database (CBM) were systematically searched from 1 January 2021 to 5 January 2025. The search strategy combined synonyms, free-text terms and medical subject headings terms. The search terms were associated with ‘low back pain’, ‘acupuncture’ and ‘clinical trial’. A detailed search strategy was implemented for each database (online supplemental materials 1).

Inclusion criteria

Study type

RCTs of acupuncture for LBP published in Chinese and English.

Participants

Patients were diagnosed according to criteria consistent with the guideline from the North American Spine Society or other accepted guidelines.21

Interventions and comparisons

In the intervention group (IG), treatments included acupuncture, electroacupuncture and other acupuncture-related interventions. Furthermore, acupuncture combined with the therapies used in the control group (CG) was considered acceptable. The CG received sham acupuncture or conventional treatments or was on the waiting lists.

Exclusion criteria

The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) duplicate or unavailable articles; (2) articles with incorrect or incomplete data, or with inappropriate conclusions; (3) acupuncture used in the CG; (4) studies in which herbal medicine, tuina, cupping, moxibustion or other traditional Chinese medicine therapies were the main interventions in the IG and acupuncture was only used as an adjunct treatment; or (5) conference abstracts, commentaries, protocols, reviews or dissertations.

Literature selection

Two review authors (Liu and Lai) conducted the electronic searches and excluded duplicate literature. They independently screened the titles and abstracts and subsequently conducted a full-text review to determine the final inclusion. Endnote 20 was used as the literature management software. Any discrepancies were resolved by a third investigator (Chen).

Data extraction

Standardised data extraction forms were developed using Microsoft Excel 2021. From each included RCT, information was collected on the title, journal, first author, publication year and language, funding sources, number of participants included, study location, number of research centres and type of intervention. All extracted data are summarised in online supplemental materials 2.

Evaluation of reporting quality and data analysis

Reporting quality assessment

Two professionally trained assessors (Liu and Lai) independently assessed the reporting quality of the RCTs according to the CONSORT 2010 (37 items), STRICTA (17 items) and CONSORT-Outcomes (17 items) checklists. The overall quality score (OQS) was the total score of 71 items in the three checklists, which was used to perform a comprehensive quality assessment. In calculating the OQS, items were scored 1 if clearly reported and 0 if not reported or vaguely reported. Certain items applied only to specific RCTs. For instance, CONSORT-Outcomes 6a.4 was applicable to trials using composite outcomes. Thus, when an item was deemed inapplicable, a score of 1 was assigned to ensure consistency in scoring. Furthermore, QS for each checklist were calculated separately. For the purpose of comparing with the review published in 2021,20 items reported in >80% of publications were defined as well reported, whereas those reported in <30% were categorised as poorly reported. Any discrepancies in scoring were solved by a third assessor (Chen).

Factors related to reporting quality

Previous studies have indicated that publication language, funding, study location and number of participants included were associated factors of the reporting quality of RCTs.22,24 Thus, we pre-identified the four factors that might be associated with the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP, including publication language (Chinese vs English), funding (funded vs unfunded), study location (China vs other countries) and number of participants included (≥80 vs <80). To explore the correlation between the four factors and reporting quality, the OQS was used as the dependent variable and was modelled using linear regression. Only significant variables in the univariate analysis (p<0.10) were included in the multivariate regression model (p<0.05).24 25 Similarly, the correlation between the four factors and the reporting quality of each checklist was explored separately. Linear regression analysis was performed using IBM SPSS Statistics version 27.0.

Patient and public involvement

None.

Results

Characteristics of the included randomised controlled trials

A total of 3036 articles were retrieved, and 57 were ultimately identified after screening. The detailed retrieval and inclusion process is depicted in figure 1. Among the 57 RCTs, 47 (82.46%) were conducted in China, 43 (75.44%) were published in Chinese, 28 (49.12%) were funded and 55 (96.49%) were conducted at a single centre. The median number of participants included in the RCTs was 80 (range, 15–352). As for the type of acupuncture, 22 (38.60%) RCTs used manual acupuncture, 13 (22.81%) used warm acupuncture, 9 (15.79%) used electroacupuncture, 2 (3.51%) used wrist–ankle acupuncture and 11 (19.30%) used other acupuncture methods. Detailed information was compiled into table 1. In addition, the median OQS of the included RCTs was 32 (range, 17–54). Figure 2 presents the distributions of the OQS and the individual checklist scores. The detailed quality scores for each RCT are provided in online supplemental materials 2.

Factors related to reporting quality

The results of the univariate regression analysis revealed significant differences in the impact of RCTs with study location, language and funding on the overall reporting quality, as well as the reporting quality of the CONSORT and CONSORT-Outcomes statements (p<0.10) (table 5). The number of participants included was not significantly related to reporting quality (p>0.10). The results of the multivariate regression analysis indicated that RCTs published in English (β coefficient 13.045, 95% CI 7.471 to 18.620, p<0.001) or funded (β coefficient 5.076, 95% CI 2.197 to 7.955, p<0.001) were significantly associated with high OQS, as well as high CONSORT and CONSORT-Outcomes scores (table 6).

Discussion

In this review, a systematic approach was employed to evaluate the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP and explore associated factors. The findings elucidated that the overall reporting quality remained suboptimal, particularly in the domains of open science, key methodology, details of acupuncture and comprehensive outcome-related information. However, several items were adequately reported, including the structured abstract, study background and objectives, random sequence generation, participant recruitment and allocation, descriptions of interventions and comparators, and the selection and analysis of outcomes. Similar results have been reported in studies evaluating the reporting quality of acupuncture for various clinical conditions, such as stroke rehabilitation and knee osteoarthritis.25 26 Additionally, multivariate linear regression analysis revealed that RCTs published in English or supported by funding were significantly associated with higher reporting quality.

Among the 37 CONSORT 2010 items, 11 were adequately reported, whereas 19 were inadequately reported. Consistent with previous studies,27 28 the under-reported items were primarily those concerning key methodological domains, including the type of randomisation, allocation concealment (items 9 and 10) and blinding (items 11a and 11b), which are crucial for minimising bias and preventing distortion in effect estimation.29 30 Insufficient reporting was also observed in RCT-indicative titles, open science items (items 23 and 24), trial design and protocol modifications (items 3b, 6b, 7b and 14b). However, properly formulated titles and clear reporting of trial design facilitate accurate indexing in electronic databases, thereby enhancing the citation potential of published articles.31 32 Open science is conducive to improving the authenticity and reproducibility of studies.33 Transparent reporting of the protocol modifications helps minimise selection bias and strengthen the reliability of RCTs.34,37 Moreover, although appropriate sample size estimation constitutes a fundamental methodological component of clinical trial design and is closely related to both the statistical precision and power of study outcomes,38 its reporting remained inadequate. Therefore, complete reporting of sample size estimation is strongly recommended. Such information enables readers to determine whether the trial was statistically powered to detect clinically meaningful differences between study arms.

Based on the STRICTA checklist, the overall reporting quality was relatively adequate, except for the details of acupuncture and control interventions (items 2a, 4b and 6a) and the acupuncturist qualifications. Regrettably, the under-reporting of intervention details may affect the reproducibility of trials.24 Furthermore, given that the efficacy of acupuncture is closely related to the acupuncturist, inadequate reporting of qualifications may introduce uncertainty regarding both therapeutic quality and intervention safety.39

Complete and transparent clinical trial results facilitate evidence-based decision making and promote the translation of trial data into clinical practice.10 Nevertheless, the reporting quality of RCTs on the CONSORT-Outcomes checklist was poor. This study revealed that only 2 of 15 relevant studies reported minimally important changes (MICs). Given the variability in methods used to estimate MICs, clear definitions and explanations should be provided. This allows readers to determine the number of responders in a patient group, thereby better interpreting the study findings and their clinical relevance.40 Comprehensive reporting of statistical methods (items 12a.1, 12a.2, 12a.3 and 12a.4) was insufficient. These deficiencies may weaken the credibility of trial findings and diminish the reproducibility and scientific integrity of RCTs.41 42 Additionally, incomplete reporting of the methods used to select, measure and describe outcomes (items 6a.7, 6a.9 and 6a.10) was observed, which may introduce potential bias and compromise the quality of trial data.10 Notably, the CONSORT-Outcomes statement was published in 2022, and the time-lag effect may be one of the reasons for the poor reporting quality.

The regression analysis indicated that RCTs published in English exhibited higher reporting quality. Moreover, despite the suboptimal overall reporting quality of certain items, RCTs published in English demonstrated a satisfactory level of reporting performance, such as the title (CONSORT 1a), sample size estimation (CONSORT 7a) and trial registration (CONSORT 23). The results were attributed to the greater emphasis on CONSORT in English journals, as well as more rigorous peer review.43 44 Although the CONSORT statement was introduced into China in 2001, only seven Chinese medical journals had included CONSORT in their instructions to authors by February 2012.45 A recent evaluation indicated that the CONSORT statement had been endorsed by only a minority of Chinese journals.43 Previous studies also found that RCTs published in Chinese had lower reporting rates.24 46 Collectively, these findings suggest that CONSORT has been neglected in China, which may be attributable to several factors. First, since academic promotion and graduation in China partly depend on the number of published papers, some authors may focus more on quick success and immediate benefits instead of improving the quality of clinical research. Second, perhaps due to the authors’ pursuit of higher impact and the longer publication cycle of Chinese journals, Chinese authors preferred to publish high-quality articles in SCI journals with greater academic prestige and visibility. In addition, a study revealed that many Chinese journals cited the CONSORT statement without specifying its version and that the translation of CONSORT had not been verified by editors.45 The use of incorrect CONSORT also contributed to poor reporting quality of Chinese articles. Therefore, strengthening the promotion and review of the CONSORT statement and its extensions by Chinese journals, along with improved author awareness and adherence, is essential for enhancing the reporting quality of RCTs published in Chinese. Additionally, in alignment with the result of a previous study,23 higher reporting quality was observed in funded RCTs. This may be attributed to the availability of greater financial resources, stricter oversight mechanisms and greater awareness and training of researchers regarding standardised reporting.47

Compared with the review published in 2021,20 although improvements were observed in the reporting of random sequence generation and the number of analysed participants, the overall reporting quality of RCTs remained suboptimal. The results may be caused by the following. First, awareness and promotion of the CONSORT and STRICTA statements are insufficient among journals and authors. Particularly, journals lacked strict requirements for adherence to these guidelines. Second, the 2021 review comprised more English-language publications, with 22 out of 31 RCTs. As previously noted, RCTs published in English had higher reporting rates for these checklist items. Therefore, a false negative might have occurred when judging whether reporting quality improved based solely on reporting rates. Third, different understandings of items may have led to misjudgments regarding improvements in reporting quality. The developers of these statements defined complete reporting as the inclusion of all concepts encompassed within an item, whereas some authors considered reporting any single concept as sufficient.48 These issues warrant attention in future reporting of acupuncture RCTs and evaluation of their reporting quality.

Study limitations

This study has several limitations. First, studies published between January 2021 and January 2025 were included to examine whether the reporting quality of acupuncture RCTs for LBP had improved after 2020. Nevertheless, the retrieval time span may be shorter than that with other reviews. Second, although the review included RCTs published in Chinese and English, acupuncture is a globally practised therapeutic approach, with a substantial number of clinical trials published in other languages, including Korean and Japanese.39 49 The absence of this part of literature may reduce the completeness of the results. Third, the subjectivity of the assessors could lead to potential bias. Finally, in calculating the scores, each item was weighted equally, even though some were inapplicable or overlapping. Fortunately, Prady et al discovered that weighting did not bias the results.50

Implications for future studies

It is strongly recommended that authors adhere to the CONSORT statement and its extensions to ensure complete and transparent reporting of their research. Journals, especially Chinese journals, should adopt stricter submission requirements and provide detailed information on the CONSORT statement in the ’Instructions for Authors’. Furthermore, editors and reviewers should receive training to strengthen the promotion and review of the CONSORT statement and its extensions. All stakeholders involved in the research publication process ought to work together to improve the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP.

Conclusions

Based on this, the reporting quality of RCTs of acupuncture for LBP require further improvement. Particularly, open science, key methodology, details of acupuncture and comprehensive outcome-related information should be completely and meticulously reported. In addition, RCTs published in English or funded RCTs had higher reporting quality. Enhancing adherence to and promotion of the CONSORT statement and its extensions among authors and journals is essential for improving the reporting quality of RCTs.

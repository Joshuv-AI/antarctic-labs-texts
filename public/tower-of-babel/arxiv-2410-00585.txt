Existence of distributional solutions to degenerate elliptic systems for locally integrable forcing
Goro Akagi, Hiroki Miyakawa

Abstract
. This paper presents an existence result and maximal regularity estimates for distributional solutions to degenerate/singular elliptic systems of p p -Laplacian type with absorption and (prescribed) locally integrable forcing posed in (possibly unbounded) Lipschitz domains. In particular, the forcing terms may not belong to the dual space of an energy space, e.g., W loc 1 , p W^{1,p}_{\rm loc} , which is necessary for the existence of weak (or energy) solutions of class W loc 1 , p W^{1,p}_{\rm loc} . The method of a proof relies on both local energy estimates and a relative truncation technique developed by Bulíček and Schwarzacher (Calc. Var. PDEs in 2016), where the bounded domain case is studied for (globally) integrable forcing.



1.
Introduction

Let
d,N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
and let
\Omega
be a (possibly unbounded) Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
1<p<r<\infty
and let
q,s\in(1,\infty)
satisfy

p-1\leq q<p\quad\mbox{ and }\quad r-1\leq s<r.

(1.1)

In the present paper, we consider the Dirichlet problem for the following degenerate/singular elliptic system of
p
-Laplacian type with absorption and forcing terms:

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-{\rm{div}}\,A(\,\cdot\,,\nabla u)+|u|^{r-2}u=-\,{\rm{div}}\,(|f|^{p-2}f)+|g|^{r-2}g\ &&\mbox{ in }\ \Omega,\\
&u=0\ &&\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

[formula]

where
u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an unknown function, and
f\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{s}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
are prescribed. In particular, when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we ignore the Dirichlet boundary condition. Moreover,
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
is a Carathéodory function complying with the following assumptions: There exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and nonnegative functions
\beta_{1}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega})
,
\beta_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega})
such that

\displaystyle A(x,z):z\geq C_{1}|z|^{p}-\beta_{1}(x),

(1.2)

\displaystyle|A(x,z)|\leq C_{2}|z|^{p-1}+\beta_{2}(x),

(1.3)

\displaystyle(A(x,z_{1})-A(x,z_{2})):(z_{1}-z_{2})\geq 0

(1.4)

for
z,z_{1},z_{2}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
and a.e.
x\in\Omega
. Hence a typical example of the elliptic operators satisfying (
1.2
)–(
1.4
) is the so-called
p
-Laplace operator,

-{\rm{div}}\,A(\,\cdot\,,\nabla u)=-\Delta_{p}u:=-\,{\rm{div}}\,(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u),

for which we set
A(x,z)=|z|^{p-2}z
for
z\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
and a.e.
x\in\Omega
obviously.

In case
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
(i.e., they are globally integrable over
\Omega
with
p=q
and
r=s
), existence of weak solutions can be proved in a standard manner (see Proposition
4.1
below). On the other hand, in case either
p>q
or
r>s
holds,
(P)
admits no weak solution for general
f\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{s}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
due to a simple comparison of both sides of the equation. However, we can still expect existence of
distributional
solutions to
(P)
(see Definition
2.1
below). The main purpose of the present paper is to prove existence of such distributional solutions to
(P)
for
f\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{s}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
with exponents
q,s\in(1,\infty)
satisfying (
1.1
) (whose lower bounds are necessary for the existence of distributional solutions) as well as to establish
maximal regularity estimates
for such distributional solutions; this is a reason why we consider the forcing term of this form in
(P)
. To be more precise, we expect that
\nabla u
and
u
can be estimated in terms of
f
and
g
in
L^{q}_{\rm loc}
and
L^{s}_{\rm loc}
, respectively.

Elliptic PDEs have been vigorously studied in a vast amount of literatures (see, e.g.,
[
14
]
). Among those, we here start with existence and regularity of locally integrable solutions to elliptic PDEs with locally integrable forcing. As is well known, an appropriate growth condition at infinity is imposed on the forcing term
F
in order to assure existence of solutions to the Poisson equation in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

-\Delta u=F\ \mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In
[
4
]
, existence and uniqueness of locally integrable (distributional) solutions
u\in L^{r-1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
are verified for the following elliptic PDE with an absorption for
2<r<\infty
:

-\Delta u+|u|^{r-2}u=F\ \mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}

(1.5)

without prescribing behaviors of
F\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
at infinity, and moreover, it holds that

\int_{B_{R}}|u|^{r-1}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq C\left(1+\int_{B_{R^{\prime}}}|F|\,\text{\rm d}x\right)

for any
R^{\prime}>R>0
. Such an elliptic equation (
1.5
) with an absorption naturally appears as a stationary problem for the heat equation with an absorption term. Moreover,
[
4
]
may also be inspired by studies on existence of solutions to parabolic equations with growing initial data (see, e.g.,
[
12
]
). Actually, in
[
4
]
, a parabolic counterpart of the result for (
1.5
) is also briefly discussed (e.g., solvability of the heat equation with an absorption is considered for any locally integrable initial data). In
[
3
]
, the aforementioned result is also extended to degenerate and singular elliptic PDEs of
p
-Laplacian type such as

-\Delta_{p}u+|u|^{r-2}u=F\ \mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d}

(1.6)

for
F\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
r>p
. The above case is much more technically involved than the semilinear case. Indeed, in contrast with
[
4
]
, one has to establish a gradient estimate for certain approximate solutions
(u_{n})
due to the nonlinearity arising from the
p
-Laplacian. It is actually done in
[
3
, Lemma 2.2]
but in a totally different way from the present paper. Moreover, in order to identify a weak limit of the
p
-Laplacian term
\Delta_{p}u_{n}
, a pointwise convergence of the gradients
\nabla u_{n}
is also derived by use of a strong monotonicity of the
p
-Laplacian (see assumption (A4) of
[
3
]
), which is no longer assumed in the present paper (cf. see (
1.4
)).

On the other hand, the optimal regularity of the (distributional) solution is not fully pursued in these literatures. Actually, the Calderón–Zygmund estimate for the Laplacian fails in
L^{1}
. Moreover, it is rather delicate to determine the optimal regularity of solutions to degenerate and singular nonlinear elliptic PDEs such as (
1.6
) even posed in bounded domains.

A regularity theory for degenerate and singular elliptic PDEs, often referred as a
nonlinear Calderón–Zygmund theory
, has been remarkably developed in the last few decades. It is concerned with weak solutions
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)
to the (homogeneous) Dirichlet problem of the form,

-\,{\rm{div}}\,\left(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\right)=-\,{\rm{div}}\,\left(|f|^{p-2}f\right)\ \mbox{ in }\Omega,

(1.7)

where
\Omega
is a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
1<p<\infty
, and provides a maximal regularity estimate,

\|\nabla u\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}

(1.8)

for
f\in L^{q}(\Omega)
and
q\geq p
. See
[
15
]
,
[
13
, Theorem 4.1]
,
[
1
]
,
[
17
, Theorem 7.8]
. Here we also remark that the estimate (
1.8
) can fail for vectorial cases with
q>p=2
large enough (see
[
23
]
). We further refer the reader to survey papers
[
20
,
21
,
22
]
and references therein. On the other hand, its extension to the case where
1<q<p
is not straightforward at all in contrast with the linear case, for which a duality argument works. Moreover, even existence of (distributional) solutions is already highly nontrivial when
q<p
. In
[
16
]
and
[
18
]
, maximal regularity estimates are established with
q<p
close enough to
p
for distributional solutions defined on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, provided that they exist. Moreover, an existence result and maximal regularity estimates for distributional solutions to (
1.7
) are first proved for bounded domains and
q<p
close enough to
p
by Bulíček and Schwarzacher in
[
10
]
, which relies on sophisticated techniques such as an
\mathcal{A}_{p}
-weighted biting div-curl lemma
developed in
[
9
, Theorem 2.6]
as well as a
relative truncation technique
for establishing maximal regularity estimates.

The aim of the present paper may also be regarded as an extension of the nonlinear Calderón–Zygmund theory to the elliptic system
(P)
with an arbitrary locally integrable forcing
f\in L^{q}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{s}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
for
q
and
s
being in the range (
1.1
). Here we emphasize again that the presence of the absorption term in
(P)
is necessary to prove an existence result as mentioned above (see
[
4
,
3
]
), and furthermore, the forcing terms of the equation in
(P)
are designed in such a way as to discuss the maximal regularity of gradients of distributional solutions.

Finally, we give a couple of related references without any claim of the completeness, although all these results are devoted to equations of different type posed in
bounded
domains only. The results in
[
9
,
10
]
have been extended to stationary Navier–Stokes equations with non-Newtonian stress tensors in
[
19
]
, where a solenoidal Lipschitz truncation is also developed (cf. see also
[
6
]
, which is a generalization of
[
9
]
). One can find parabolic counterparts of these studies in, e.g.,
[
7
,
8
]
and references therein.

The present paper consists of six sections. In Section
2
, we state a main result, which is concerned with maximal regularity estimates for distributional solutions to
(P)
. Section
3
is devoted to recalling preliminary facts on the Muckenhoupt class
\mathcal{A}_{p}
, an
\mathcal{A}_{p}
-weighted biting div-curl lemma and the Whitney decomposition of open sets for later use. In Section
4
, we assure existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to
(P)
for
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Section
5
provides weighted local energy estimates for weak solutions to
(P)
, which play a crucial role to prove the main result and where main technical novelty may reside. Finally, in Section
6
, we complete a proof of the main result.

Notation
.

For
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
c,d\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
A,B\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
, we denote by
a\cdot b
,
c\cdot d
and
A:B
(standard) inner products, that is,

a\cdot b=\sum_{j=1}^{d}a_{j}b_{j},\quad c\cdot d=\sum_{k=1}^{N}c_{k}d_{k},\quad A:B=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\sum_{k=1}^{N}A_{jk}B_{jk},

respectively. Then
|a|
,
|c|
and
|A|
denote standard Euclidean norms of
a
,
c
and
A
, respectively, that is,

|a|=\sqrt{a\cdot a},\quad|c|=\sqrt{c\cdot c},\quad|A|=\sqrt{A:A}.

Moreover,
a\otimes c\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
is a direct product of
a
and
c
, that is,
(a\otimes c)_{ik}=a_{i}c_{k}
. Hence we have
|a\otimes c|=|a||c|
. For
u=(u_{k}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
F=(F_{jk}):\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{\text{\rm d}\times N}
smooth enough,
\nabla u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
and
{\rm{div}}\,F:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{N}
are defined as

(\nabla u)_{jk}=\partial_{x_{j}}u_{k}=\frac{\partial u_{k}}{\partial x_{j}},\quad({\rm{div}}\,F)_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\partial_{x_{i}}F_{ik}

for
j=1,\ldots,d
and
k=1,\ldots,N
. For
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
R>0
, we write
B_{R}(x):=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon|x-y|<R\}
and
B_{R}:=B_{R}(0)
. Furthermore, for any domain
\Omega
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we simply write
\Omega_{R}:=\Omega\cap B_{R}
. For each
f\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we denote by
Mf
the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function, that is,

(Mf)(x)=\sup_{r>0}\,\fint_{B_{r}(x)}|{f}(y)|\,\text{\rm d}y

for
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Here and henceforth, we write

\fint_{B}g(y)\,\text{\rm d}y=|B|^{-1}\int_{B}g(y)\,\text{\rm d}y

for any
g\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and open ball
B
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
, we denote by
p^{\prime}
the Hölder conjugate of
p
, that is,
p^{\prime}=p/(p-1)
,
1^{\prime}=\infty
and
\infty^{\prime}=1
. Moreover,
C
stands for a generic nonnegative constant which may vary from line to line.

2.
Main Result

In order to impose the (homogeneous) Dirichlet boundary condition on locally integrable distributional solutions to the Dirichlet problem
(P)
, let us first set up its weak formulation below. Let
B_{R}
denote the open ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
centered at the origin of radius
R
and let
(\rho_{R})
be a family of smooth cut-off functions satisfying

\left\{\begin{aligned} &\rho_{R}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad 0\leq\rho_{R}\leq 1\ \mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\rho_{R}\equiv 1\ \mbox{ on }B_{R},\quad\mathrm{supp}\,\rho_{R}\subset\overline{B_{2R}}\end{aligned}\right.

(2.1)

for any
R>0
. In what follows, the homogeneous Dirichlet boundary condition for
u\in W^{1,1}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
will be regarded (in a weak sense) as

u\rho_{R}\in W_{0}^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\ \mbox{ for any }R>0.

[formula]

Indeed, if
u\in C(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, then one finds that
\mathrm{Tr}(u\rho_{R})=(u\rho_{R})|_{\partial\Omega}
for any
R>0
, whence it follows from
(BC)
that
u\rho_{R}=0
on
\partial\Omega
for any
R>0
. Here
\mathrm{Tr}:W^{1,1}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{1}(\partial\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
denotes the trace operator. Therefore from the arbitrariness of
R>0
, the homogeneous Dirichlet condition, i.e.,
u=0
on
\partial\Omega
, follows.

We now set up notions of weak and distributional solutions to
(P)
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
1<p<r<\infty
and let
f\in L^{\max\{1,p-1\}}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{\max\{1,r-1\}}_{\rm loc}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(i)

Set
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{\|\cdot\|_{X^{p,r}}}
equipped with the norm,

\|u\|_{X^{p,r}}:=\|\nabla u\|_{L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})}+\|u\|_{L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})}

for
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(ii)

A function
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is called a
weak solution
to
(P)
, if it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A(\cdot,\nabla u):\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{r-2}u\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p-2}f:\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|g|^{r-2}g\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(iii)

A function
u\in W_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\max\{1,p-1\}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{\max\{1,r-1\}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
is called a
distributional solution
to
(P)
, if it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}A(\cdot,\nabla u):\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{r-2}u\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p-2}f:\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|g|^{r-2}g\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

for any
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and moreover,
(BC)
holds for an arbitrary family
(\rho_{R})
of cut-off functions satisfying (
2.1
).

Our main result reads,

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
\Omega
be a
(
possibly unbounded
)
Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
1<p<r<\infty
. Then there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon_{0}=\varepsilon_{0}(C_{1},C_{2},\Omega,d,N,p,r)\in(0,p/r]
satisfying the following
:
Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
be fixed and set

q:=p-\varepsilon\in(p-1,p)\quad\mbox{ and }\quad s:=\frac{p-\varepsilon}{p}r\in(r-1,r).

Then for any
f\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{q}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L_{\mathrm{loc}}^{s}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the Dirichlet problem
(P)
admits a distributional solution
u\in W^{1,q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{R}}|u|^{r}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|f|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|g|^{r}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{1}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{2}^{p^{\prime}}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\delta^{-\varepsilon}R^{d-\frac{pr}{r-p}}\right)

(2.2)

for any
R>0
and
\delta\in(0,1]
. Here
C_{0}=C_{0}(C_{1},C_{2},\Omega,d,N,p,r,\varepsilon)
is a positive constant and a function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to(0,\infty)
is given by

\omega(x)=\left(M\left[\big(|\bar{f}|+|\bar{g}|^{s/q}\big)\chi_{\Omega_{2R}}\right](x)+\delta\right)^{-\varepsilon}\ \mbox{ for }\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

where
\bar{f},\bar{g}
denote the zero extensions onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
of
f,g
, respectively, and
\chi_{\Omega_{2R}}
stands for the characteristic function supported over
\Omega_{2R}
.

Moreover, we have

Corollary 2.3
.

Under the same assumptions as in Theorem
2.2
with
\beta_{1}=\beta_{2}=0
, there exists a positive constant
\tilde{C}_{0}=\tilde{C}_{0}(C_{1},C_{2},\Omega,d,N,p,r,\varepsilon)
such that

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla u|^{q}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{R}}|u|^{s}\,\text{\rm d}x}

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{C}_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|f|^{q}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|g|^{s}\,\text{\rm d}x+R^{d-\frac{1}{2}\frac{pr}{r-p}}\right)

(2.3)

for any
R>0
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we shall set up preliminary facts to be used.

3.1.
Muckenhoupt weights

We first recall the class
\mathcal{A}_{p}
of Muckenhoupt weights
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, which complies with the boundedness of the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator
M
in weighted Lebesgue spaces
L^{p}_{\omega}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Definition 3.1
.

(i)

A measurable function
\omega:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is called a
weight function
if
\omega>0
a.e. in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

(ii)

For each
p\in[1,\infty)
, a weight function
\omega\in L^{1}_{{\mathrm{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is said to be of the
Muckenhoupt class

\mathcal{A}_{p}
, i.e.,
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
, if there exists a constant
A>0
such that for any open ball
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\displaystyle\left(\fint_{B}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x\right)\left(\fint_{B}\omega^{-(p^{\prime}-1)}\,\text{\rm d}x\right)^{1/(p^{\prime}-1)}\leq A\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\ p\in(1,\infty),

\displaystyle\ M\omega\leq A\omega\ \ \mbox{a.e.~in}\ \mathbb{R}^{d}\quad

\displaystyle\mbox{ if }\ p=1.

(iii)

Let
p\in[1,\infty)
and let
\omega
be a weight function defined over an open subset
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Set

\displaystyle L^{p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle:=\left\{f:{\mathcal{O}}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}\colon f\mbox{ is measurable and }\int_{{\mathcal{O}}}|f|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x<\infty\right\},

\displaystyle W^{1,p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle:=\left\{f\in L^{p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N})\colon\nabla f\in L^{p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})\right\}

equipped with norms

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}:=\left(\int_{\mathcal{O}}|f|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x\right)^{1/p},\quad\|f\|_{W^{1,p}_{\omega}}:=\|f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}+\|\nabla f\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}},

respectively. Then they are reflexive Banach spaces and the dual space
(L^{p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
of
L^{p}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N})
can be identified with
L^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}({\mathcal{O}};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, that is, for any
f\in(L^{p}_{\omega}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
, there exists some
v_{f}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\omega}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\langle f,u\rangle_{L^{p}_{\omega}}=\int_{\mathcal{O}}(v_{f}\cdot u)\,\omega\,\text{\rm d}x

for all
u\in L^{p}_{\omega}(\mathcal{O};\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

We next set up a couple of lemmas for later use. The following lemma is concerned with some relations between maximal functions and Muckenhoupt weights. We refer the reader to
[
24
, pp. 229–230]
and
[
25
, p. 5]
(see also
[
10
, Lemma 2.1]
) for more details.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
f\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
Mf\not\equiv\infty
. Then for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, it holds that
(Mf)^{\alpha}\in\mathcal{A}_{1}
. Moreover, for all
p\in(1,\infty)
and all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, there holds
(Mf)^{-\alpha(p-1)}\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
.

Let us recall the following lemma, which is concerned with continuous embeddings from
\mathcal{A}_{p}
-weighted Lebesgue spaces to usual ones (see
[
9
, pp. 1125,1126]
).

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
1<p<\infty
and
\omega\in\mathcal{A}_{p}
and let
D
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then there exists some
q\in(1,p)
such that
L^{p}_{\omega}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuously embedded in
L^{q}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence,
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuously embedded in
W^{1,q}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Moreover, we have

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
1<q<p<\infty
and let
D
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\omega
be a weight function such that
\omega^{-1}\in L^{q/(p-q)}(D)
. Then
L^{p}_{\omega}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is continuously embedded in
L^{q}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{p}_{\omega}(D;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Since
\omega^{-1}
belongs to
L^{q/(p-q)}(D)
, we observe that

\int_{D}|u|^{q}\,\text{\rm d}x=\int_{D}|u|^{q}\omega^{q/p}\omega^{-q/p}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}^{q}\|\omega^{-1}\|_{L^{q/(p-q)}}^{q/p}<+\infty,

(3.1)

which implies the desired conclusion.
∎

4.
Weak solutions

In this section, we discuss existence and uniqueness of weak solutions to
(P)
for
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
with the aid of a monotone operator theory.

Proposition 4.1
.

For
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, the Dirichlet problem
(P)
admits a unique weak solution
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

To prove this, we first note the following basic properties of the space
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, which can be proved in a standard manner (see, e.g.,
[
5
]
):

Proposition 4.2
.

(i)

The space
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
is a reflexive Banach space.

(ii)

It holds that
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

(iii)

For any
F\in(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
, there exist
F_{1}\in L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
F_{2}\in L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that

\langle F,u\rangle_{X^{p,r}}=\int_{\Omega}F_{1}:\nabla u\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}F_{2}\cdot u\,\text{\rm d}x

for
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

In particular, in order to check the assertion (ii), one may observe that
W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{r}(\Omega)
coincides with the closure of
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega)
in
W^{1,p}(\Omega)\cap L^{r}(\Omega)
(cf. see
[
5
, Proposition 9.18]
).

We set
\mathcal{A}u:=-\,{\rm{div}}A\,(\cdot,\nabla u)
and
\mathcal{B}u:=\mathcal{A}u+|u|^{r-2}u
for
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then the operator
\mathcal{B}:X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
is defined by

\displaystyle[\mathcal{B}u](\varphi)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}A(\cdot,\nabla u):\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|u|^{r-2}u\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

for
u,\varphi\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
; indeed, we note that

\displaystyle|[\mathcal{B}u](\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\|A(\cdot,\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}\|\nabla\varphi\|_{L^{p}}+\||u|^{r-1}\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}\|\varphi\|_{L^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|A(\cdot,\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\|u\|^{r-1}_{L^{r}})\|\varphi\|_{X^{p,r}}

for
\varphi\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and hence, we find that
\mathcal{B}u\in(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
. Moreover, we can immediately observe that

Lemma 4.3
.

The operator
\mathcal{B}:X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
is a strictly monotone operator.

Furthermore, we have

Lemma 4.4
.

The operator
\mathcal{B}:X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
is coercive and continuous.

Proof.

We first prove the coercivity. Let
(u^{k})
be a sequence in
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let
C\geq 0
be a constant such that

\frac{\langle\mathcal{B}u^{k},u^{k}\rangle_{X^{p,r}}}{\|u^{k}\|_{X^{p,r}}}\leq C\quad\mbox{ for }\ k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}\ \mbox{(large enough)},

(4.1)

which implies

\displaystyle C_{1}\|\nabla u^{k}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\|u^{k}\|_{L^{r}}^{r}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ineq:ellipAcoer}}}{{\leq}}\langle\mathcal{B}u^{k},u^{k}\rangle_{X^{p,r}}+\|\beta_{1}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{coer2}}}{{\leq}}C\|u^{k}\|_{X^{p,r}}+\|\beta_{1}\|_{L^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C_{1}}{2}\|\nabla u^{k}\|_{L^{p}}^{p}+\frac{1}{2}\|u^{k}\|_{L^{r}}^{r}+C.

Thus
\|u^{k}\|_{X^{p,r}}
is bounded, and therefore,
\mathcal{B}
turns out to be coercive.

We claim that the operator
\mathcal{B}:X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\to(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
is continuous. Indeed, let
u^{k}\to u
in
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then
\nabla u^{k}\to\nabla u
in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
u^{k}\to u
in
L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then
A(\cdot,\nabla u^{k})\to A(\cdot,\nabla u)
strongly in
L^{p^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
|u^{k}|^{r-2}u^{k}\to|u|^{r-2}u
strongly in
L^{r^{\prime}}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence we observe that

\displaystyle{\|\mathcal{B}u^{k}-\mathcal{B}u\|_{(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|A(\cdot,\nabla u^{k})-A(\cdot,\nabla u)\|_{L^{p^{\prime}}}+\||u^{k}|^{r-2}u^{k}-|u|^{r-2}u\|_{L^{r^{\prime}}}\to 0

as
k\to\infty
. Hence
\mathcal{B}
is continuous.
∎

We are now in a position to prove Proposition
4.1
.

Proof of Proposition
4.1
.

Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Then we observe that

\displaystyle{\langle-\,{\rm{div}}\,(|f|^{p-2}f)+|g|^{r-2}g,\varphi\rangle_{X^{p,r}}}

\displaystyle:=\int_{\Omega}|f|^{p-2}f:\nabla\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega}|g|^{r-2}g\cdot\varphi\,\text{\rm d}x

for
\varphi\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Hence one can deduce that
-\,{\rm{div}}\,(|f|^{p-2}f)+|g|^{r-2}g\in(X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N}))^{*}
. Due to the monotonicity and continuity of
\mathcal{B}
, it turns out to be maximal monotone, and moreover, from the coercivity, we infer that
\mathcal{B}
is bijective (see, e.g.,
[
2
]
). Therefore
(P)
admits a weak solution
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The uniqueness follows immediately from the strict monotonicity.
∎

5.
Weighted local energy estimates

This section is devoted to deriving weighted local energy estimates for weak solutions to
(P)
by employing a
relative truncation technique
developed in
[
10
]
, where weighted energy estimates are established for a bounded domain case along with globally integrable forcing, i.e.,
f\in L^{q}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and therefore, neither localization nor absorption is needed.

5.1.
Relative truncation

Let
\mathcal{O}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open set. For
u\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
, define the
relative truncation

u_{\mathcal{O}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
of
u
into the open set
\mathcal{O}
by

\displaystyle u_{\mathcal{O}}(x)

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\sum_{i}\psi_{i}(x)\overline{u}_{i}&\mbox{if }\ x\in\mathcal{O},\\
u(x)&\mbox{if }\ x\notin\mathcal{O},\end{cases}

\displaystyle\overline{u}_{i}

\displaystyle:=\begin{cases}\fint_{(9/8)Q_{i}}u\,\text{\rm d}x&\mbox{if }\ (9/8)\color[rgb]{0,0,0}Q_{i}\subset\Omega,\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

Here
(Q_{i})
and
(\psi_{i})
are the cubes and partition of unity introduced in Proposition
3.6
for the open set
\mathcal{O}
. In what follows, we simply use the same notation
u_{\mathcal{O}}
for the restriction of
u_{\mathcal{O}}
into the domain
\Omega
. Then we recall

Lemma 5.1

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
\Omega
be a Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
u\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
for some
p\in[1,\infty)
. Then
u_{\mathcal{O}}\in W^{1,p}_{0}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and there exists a constant
c=c(d,p,\Omega)
such that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u-u_{\mathcal{O}})|^{p}\,\text{\rm d}x\leq c\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p}\,\text{\rm d}x.

Moreover, we have

Proposition 5.2
.

For each
u\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\rho\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, the relative truncation
(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}
of the product
u{\rho}
into
\mathcal{O}
belongs to
X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Proof.

We first note that the zero extension of
u
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
belongs to
X^{p,r}(\mathbb{R}^{d};\mathbb{R}^{N})
(it will still be denoted by
u
simply). Let
x\in\mathcal{O}
and let
i
be such that
x\in Q_{i}
(see Proposition
3.6
). Then we observe from Proposition
3.6
that

\displaystyle|(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\psi_{j}(x)|\overline{(u{\rho})}_{j}|

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\psi_{j}(x)\frac{2^{d}}{|Q_{i}|}\int_{(9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega}|u|{\rho}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|Q_{i}|}\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\int_{(9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega}|u|\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|Q_{i}|}\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}|(9/8)Q_{j}|^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{|Q_{i}|}\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}|(9/4)Q_{i}|^{1/r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\leq C|Q_{i}|^{-1/r}\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}.

Therefore recalling that
\mathcal{O}=\bigcup_{i}Q_{i}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{O}}|(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}(x)|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\int_{Q_{i}}|(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}(x)|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i}\int_{Q_{i}}C|Q_{i}|^{-1}\left|\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i}|A_{i}|^{r-1}\left(\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 4^{d(r-1)}C\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(*)}}{{=}}4^{d(r-1)}C\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i\in A_{j}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle(\star)}}{{\leq}}4^{d(r+1)}C\int_{\mathcal{O}\cap\Omega}|u|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x<+\infty.

(5.1)

Here the equality
(*)
is derived from the following fundamental observation:

\displaystyle\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{j\in A_{i}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{j}\chi_{A_{i}}(j)\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i}\left(\sum_{j}\chi_{A_{j}}(i)\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i}\chi_{A_{j}}(i)\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j}\left(\sum_{i\in A_{j}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}\right),

where we used the equivalence
i\in A_{j}\Leftrightarrow j\in A_{i}
as well as Fubini’s lemma for double series of nonnegative terms. Moreover, the inequality
(\star)
also follows (similarly to the above observation) from Proposition
3.6
, which implies that
(9/8)Q_{j}
intersects with only
Q_{k}
for
k\in A_{j}
. Indeed, we note that

\displaystyle\sum_{j}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}((9/8)Q_{j}\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{j}\sum_{k\in A_{j}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}(Q_{k}\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\sum_{j\in A_{k}}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}(Q_{k}\cap\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq 4^{d}\sum_{k}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}(Q_{k}\cap\Omega)}=4^{d}\|u\|^{r}_{L^{r}(\mathcal{O}\cap\Omega)}.

Hence it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{O}}|(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega\backslash\mathcal{O}}|u{\rho}|^{r}\,\text{\rm d}x<+\infty.

Thus we have obtained
(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
.

Let
(u_{n})
be a sequence in
C^{\infty}_{c}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that
u_{n}\to u
strongly in
L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
\nabla u_{n}\to\nabla u
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
. Since
\mathrm{supp}\,{\rho}
is compact, we find that
u_{n}{\rho}\to u\rho
strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

\nabla(u_{n}{\rho})=(\nabla u_{n}){\rho}+u_{n}\nabla{\rho}\to(\nabla u){\rho}+u\nabla{\rho}=\nabla(u\rho)

strongly in
L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
. Thus we observe that
u{\rho}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and therefore, by the use of Lemma
5.1
, we deduce that
(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Combining all these facts, we have
(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}\in W_{0}^{1,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and therefore, Proposition
4.2
yields
(u{\rho})_{\mathcal{O}}\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The proof is completed.
∎

5.2.
Weighted local energy estimates

Developing the argument used in
[
10
, Proposition 4.1]
, we shall prove the following proposition, which will play a key role for proving Theorem
2.2
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\Omega
be a
(
possibly unbounded
)
Lipschitz domain of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
1<p<r<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}(C_{1},C_{2},\Omega,d,N,p,r)>0
satisfying the following
:
Let
f\in L^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
and
g\in L^{r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
and let

be the unique weak solution to

. Let
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{1})
and

be arbitrarily fixed. Then there exists a constant
C_{0}=C_{0}(C_{1},C_{2},\Omega,d,N,p,r,\varepsilon)>0
such that

\displaystyle{\int_{\Omega_{R}}|\nabla u|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{R}}|u|^{r}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left(\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|f|^{p}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}|g|^{r}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x\right.

\displaystyle\qquad+\left.\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{1}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{2}^{p^{\prime}}\omega\,\text{\rm d}x+\delta^{-\varepsilon}R^{d-\frac{pr}{r-p}}\right),

where
\omega
is the weight function given by
\omega=(M[\bar{h}\chi_{\Omega_{2R}}]+\delta)^{-\varepsilon}
with the zero extension
\bar{h}
of
h
onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\beta_{1}\in L^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega})
and
\beta_{2}\in L^{p^{\prime}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega})
are nonnegative functions satisfying (
1.2
) and (
1.3
), for every

and
\delta\in(0,1]
.

Proof.

In what follows, we use the same letters
u,f,h
for their zero extensions onto
\mathbb{R}^{d}
if no confusion can arise. Moreover, let
R>0
and

be arbitrarily fixed and set

. Moreover, we take an open subset of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}:=[Mh_{\delta}>\lambda]:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\colon Mh_{\delta}(x)>\lambda\right\}.

Here and henceforth, we simply denote by
v_{\lambda}
the relative truncation
v_{\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}}
of each function
v
into
\mathcal{O}_{\lambda}
.

Let
R^{\prime}\in(R,2R]
and let
{\rho}
be a cut-off function complying with

\left\{\begin{aligned} &{\rho}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),\quad 0\leq{\rho}\leq 1\ \mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad{\rho}\equiv 1\ \mbox{ on }B_{R},\\
&\mathrm{supp}\,{\rho}\subset\overline{B_{R^{\prime}}},\quad\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\nabla{\rho}(x)|\leq C/(R^{\prime}-R)\end{aligned}\right.

(5.2)

for some constant
C>0
independent of
R^{\prime}
and
R
. Thanks to Proposition
5.2
, we see that
(u{\rho})_{\lambda}{\rho}^{2r-1}\in X^{p,r}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{N})
, and hence, it follows from the weak form that

is a constant independent of

\displaystyle:=C\left(\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{1}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\beta_{2}^{p^{\prime}}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\frac{|f|^{p}}{(Mh_{\delta})^{\varepsilon}}\,\text{\rm d}x+\int_{\Omega_{2R}}\frac{|g|^{r}}{(Mh_{\delta})^{\varepsilon}}\,\text{\rm d}x\right).

\displaystyle\quad+C_{R}\frac{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)^{n}\gamma^{n}}{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)\gamma}+F_{0}\frac{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)^{n}}{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)}

\displaystyle\to\frac{C_{R}}{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)\gamma}+\frac{F_{0}}{1-(C\varepsilon+\nu)}\quad\mbox{as }n\to\infty

and

with the choice,

(then

). Thus the desired conclusion follows. This completes the proof.

up to a subsequence. On the other hand, since we have also seen that

Remark 6.3
.

The arguments exhibited so far may also be extended to more general equations such as

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-{\rm{div}}\,A(x,\nabla u)+\beta(x,u)=-\,{\rm{div}}\,(|f|^{p-2}f)+|g|^{r-2}g\ &&\mbox{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0\ &&\mbox{ on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
1<q<p<r<\infty
,
1<s<r
,
f\in L^{q}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{d\times N})
,
g\in L^{s}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{N})
and
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
is given as before. Moreover,
\beta:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{N}\to\mathbb{R}^{N}

\displaystyle(\beta(x,z_{1})-\beta(x,z_{2}))\cdot(z_{1}-z_{2})\geq 0

for

and a.e.

A Remarkable Application of Zassenhaus Formula to Strongly Correlated Electron Systems
Louis Jourdan, Patrick Cassam-Chenaï

Abstract
We show that the Zassenhaus decomposition for the exponential of the sum of two non-commuting operators, simplifies drastically when these operators satisfy a simple condition, called the no-mixed adjoint property. An important application to a Unitary Coupled Cluster method for strongly correlated electron systems is presented. This ansatz requires no Trotterization and is exact on a quantum computer with a finite number of Givens gate equals to the number of free parameters. The formulas obtained in this work also shed light on why and when optimization after Trotterization gives exact solutions in disentangled forms of unitary coupled cluster.



keywords
Zassenhaus formula, Unitary Coupled Cluster, Electron Pairs, Quantum Computing

1
Introduction

Quantum algorithms based on the Zassenhaus product formula is an active field of research
Casas et al. 2025
.
The Zassenhaus formula is a kind of dual formula of the celebrated Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff one
Magnus 1954
. It decomposes the exponential of the sum of two non-commuting operators,
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
, into a product of exponentials of homogeneous Lie polynomials of increasing degrees:

\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\exp(\hat{X})\exp(\hat{Y})\prod^{+\infty}_{n=2}\exp(\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})),

(1)

where
\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
is a homogeneous Lie polynomial of degree
n
in
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
. This decomposition is unique and recursive formulas for the
\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
polynomials have been given by Wilcox
Wilcox 1967
, the latter indicating also a simpler method followed later by Suzuki
Suzuki 1977
. Since then, various approaches have been developed in view of computer implementations, see
Scholz and Weyrauch 2006
;
Casas et al. 2012
;
Wang et al. 2019
to quote a few, a more comprehensive bibliography can be found in
Casas et al. 2012
. A continuous analogue of the Zassenhaus formula is the Fer expansion
Fer 1958
.

This article is concerned with applications of the Zassenhaus formula to traditional quantum mechanics, although Zassenhaus recursions also appear in quantum field theory
Ebrahimi-Fard and
Patras 2008
.
Decomposing the exponential of non-commuting operators is of paramount importance in quantum physics and quantum computing. It is usually done by iterating a truncated version of the Zassenhaus formula at order, say
m
, after having rewritten the sum of operators as
k\cdot\left(\frac{\hat{X}}{k}+\frac{\hat{Y}}{k}\right)
:

\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\hat{X}}{k}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\hat{Y}}{k}\right)\prod^{m}_{n=2}\exp\left(\hat{C}_{n}(\frac{\hat{X}}{k},\frac{\hat{Y}}{k})\right)\right]^{k},

(2)

The most widespread such strategy consists in just applying Trotter’s formula
Trotter 1959
, which corresponds to the shortest non-trivial truncation of the Zassenhaus formula:

\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\left[\exp\left(\frac{\hat{X}}{k}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\hat{Y}}{k}\right)\right]^{k}.

(3)

In practice, the number,
k
, of Trotter steps is finite,

\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})\approx\left[\exp\left(\frac{\hat{X}}{k}\right)\exp\left(\frac{\hat{Y}}{k}\right)\right]^{k},

(4)

and the word "Trotterization" has now become part of the common language of quantum computing, to designate this process.

Trotterization is an approximation. Even if the Zassenhaus formula is truncated at a higher order, the stepsize is always finite in real life computation, and actually often kept as small as
k=1
to limit the number of quantum gates on NISQ computers. This is an extra source of loss of accuracy. So, it is highly desirable to find short, exact, alternative decompositions of
\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})
.

The purpose of the present article is to provide such a decomposition in a particular, yet potentially very useful, case of operators representing mono- and di-excitation of electronic states in quantum physics and chemistry.

The article is organized as follows: In the next section, we demonstrate the simplifications which occur in the Zassenhaus formula, when the iterated commutators of abstract
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
operators satisfy a specific property, called the "no-mixed adjoint" property. Then, we show that the mono- and di-excitation operators relevant to a promising ansatz for electronic wave functions, satisfy this property, so that the simplified Zassenhaus decomposition can be applied to it, and translations into quantum circuits are proposed. We conclude with the consequences of this finding for the implementation of the ansatz on quantum computers.

2
Zassenhaus formula for no-mixed adjoint operators

We define the iterated adjoint of an operator, acting typically on the Hilbert space of a quantum system,
\hat{Y}
with respect to another such operator
\hat{X}
by recursion:

\displaystyle ad^{0}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{Y}

(5)

\displaystyle\forall j\in\mathbb{N}^{*},\quad ad^{j}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{X},ad^{j-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}],

(6)

where the bracket
[\cdot,\cdot]
, denotes the commutator between two operators.
The iterated adjoint
ad^{j}_{\hat{X}}
can be regarded as a mapping which provides a useful notation for nested commutators with
\hat{X}
. In particular,
ad^{1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}\equiv ad_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}=[\hat{X},\hat{Y}]
.
We use the iterated adjoint in the next subsection, to introduce the, so-called, "no-mixed adjoint property".

2.1
No-mixed adjoint property

We say that the couple of operators
(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
satisfies the no-mixed adjoint property, if:

\forall i\in\mathbb{N}\quad ad_{\hat{Y}}ad^{i}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}=0

(7)

(which implies that all chains of adjoints
ad^{j_{m}}_{\hat{Y}}ad^{i_{m}}_{\hat{X}}\ldots ad^{j_{1}}_{\hat{Y}}ad^{i_{1}}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
are zero, unless

j_{1}=\cdots=j_{m}=0
, that is to say, the chain is non-mixed and can be written more simply as
ad^{i}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
with
i=i_{m}+\cdots+i_{1}
).

With this definition, one can prove the following theorem.

2.2
Theorem:

Let
(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
satisfying the no-mixed adjoint property, then the homogeneous Lie polynomials
\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
of the Zassenhaus formula for these operators, Eq.(
1
), are given by:

\forall n\geqslant 2\quad\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}.

(8)

The detailed proof of this theorem can be found in Appendix A.

2.3
Zassenhaus decomposition:

This last expression can be extended to
n=1
, with
\hat{C}_{1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\hat{Y}
, so that the Zassenhaus decomposition writes:

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X})\prod^{+\infty}_{n=1}\exp(\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y}))

(9)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X})\prod^{+\infty}_{n=1}\exp\left(\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X})\exp\left(\sum\limits^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}\right),

where we have made use of the corollary, Eq.(
41
), of the Appendix A, for the last equality.

The series in the second exponential appears in the formula of the derivative of the exponential map, in a Lie algebra context. It can be denoted formally as
\frac{1-\exp(-ad_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})}{ad_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}}
, and replaced by an integral,

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{+\infty}_{n=1}\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\ \exp(ad_{-t\hat{X}}\hat{Y})\ -1dt

(10)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{1}\ \exp(-t\hat{X})\cdot\hat{Y}\cdot\exp(t\hat{X})dt\ -1.

Its inverse also appears in the differential equation leading to Magnus expansion
Magnus 1954
;
Bauer et al. 2013
.

A last side-remark is that, if an operator
\hat{Y}^{\prime}
can be cast in the form
\hat{Y}^{\prime}=\frac{1-\exp(-ad_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})}{ad_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}}
with the no-mixed adjoint property satisfied between
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
, then the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff expansion for
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}^{\prime}
will simplify, as
\exp{\hat{X}}\exp{\hat{Y}^{\prime}}=\exp{(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})}
.

3
Application to a Unitary Coupled Cluster method

3.1
Unitary 2D-Block Frozen Pair Coupled Clusters

The coupled cluster (CC) method has its origin in nuclear physics and has now spread in many branches of physics and chemistry
Bartlett and Musial 2007
;
Laestadius and
Faulstich 2019
. It is sometimes qualified as the "gold-standard" of quantum chemistry. The CC ansatz consists in optimizing parametrized wave functions of the form
\phi=\exp(\hat{T})\phi_{0}
, where
\phi_{0}
is a fixed wave function called the "reference" wave function, and
\hat{T}
is a parametrized operator called the "cluster" operator. When the latter is split into two commuting parts
\hat{T}=\hat{T}_{int}+\hat{T}_{ext}
, and the reference a, so-called "Slater determinant", one obtains a tailored coupled cluster (tCC) ansatz
Leszczyk et al. 2022
;
Tecmer and Boguslawski 2022
, while if
\hat{T}
is anti-Hermitian, so that
\exp(\hat{T})
is unitary, one talks about unitary coupled cluster (UCC). Recently, UCC has attracted a renewed interest in quantum computing
Peruzzo et al. 2014
;
Yung et al. 2014
;
McClean et al. 2016
;
Romero et al. 2019
and has been developed into many different forms
Grimsley et al. 2019
;
Lee et al. 2019
;
Sokolov et al. 2020
;
Huggins et al. 2020
;
Ryabinkin et al. 2020
;
Xie et al. 2022
, to quote a few.

A promising ansatz for strongly correlated electron systems, which combines tCC and UCC is the unitary frozen pair coupled cluster (UfpCC) ansatz, where

\hat{T}_{int}
is of the form, in second quantization formalism,

\hat{T}_{int}=\sum^{n_{occ}}_{p=1}\sum_{q=n_{occ}+1}^{n_{orb}}\lambda_{pq}(\hat{S}^{+}_{q}\hat{S}^{-}_{p}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p}\hat{S}^{-}_{q})

(11)

where the
\lambda_{pq}
’s are real numbers,
\hat{S}^{+}_{i}
is an operator "creating" an electron pair,
\hat{S}^{-}_{i}=(\hat{S}^{+}_{i})^{\dagger}
its Hermitian conjugate "annihilating" an electron pair,
n_{occ}
is the number of occupied electron pairs in the reference,
\ket{\phi_{0}}=\prod\limits^{n_{occ}}_{p=1}\hat{S}^{+}_{p}\ket{0}
, and
n_{orb}
is the total number of "orbitals" i.e. of spinless one-electron states. Different choices are possible for
\hat{T}_{ext}
. The simplest one is to limit
\hat{T}_{ext}
to broken-pair double excitations
\hat{T}_{ext}=\hat{T^{\prime}}_{2}
. However, one might wish to include also single-excitations
\hat{T}_{ext}=\hat{T}_{1}+\hat{T^{\prime}}_{2}

Tecmer and Boguslawski 2022
. Then, in Unitary frozen pair Single and Double Coupled Cluster (UfpSDCC), the Zassenhaus formula can prove useful, because

\hat{T}_{1}=\sum^{n_{occ}}_{p=1}\sum_{q=n_{occ}+1}^{n_{orb}}\mu_{pq}(\hat{S}^{+}_{pq}\hat{S}^{-}_{p}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p}\hat{S}^{-}_{pq})

(12)

and

\hat{T^{\prime}}_{2}=\sum^{n_{occ}}_{1\leq p_{1}<p_{2}}\ \sum_{n_{occ}+1\leq q_{1}\neq q_{2}}^{n_{orb}}\mu_{p_{1}p_{2}q_{1}q_{2}}(\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{1}q_{1}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{2}q_{2}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{2}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{1}}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{1}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{2}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{2}q_{2}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{1}q_{1}})

(13)

do not commute. In Eqs.(
12
) and (
13
), the
\mu
’s are real parameters. The non-commutation arises from the pair operators. The latter are expressed in terms of one-electron creation and annihilation operators,

\hat{S}^{+}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta_{ij})}}(\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}+\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\hat{a}^{\dagger}_{i-\frac{1}{2}})=(\hat{S}^{-}_{ij})^{\dagger},

(14)

\hat{S}^{-}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2(1+\delta_{ij})}}(\hat{a}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{a}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\hat{a}_{i-\frac{1}{2}}\hat{a}_{j+\frac{1}{2}})=(\hat{S}^{+}_{ij})^{\dagger},

(15)

where
\hat{a}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger}
(resp.
\hat{a}_{i\sigma}
) creates (resp. annihilates) an electron in the spin-orbital of orbital part
i
and spin
\sigma
, these operators obeying the canonical anticommutation relations,
[\hat{a}_{i\sigma},\hat{a}_{j\tau}]_{+}=[\hat{a}_{i\sigma}^{\dagger},\hat{a}_{j\tau}^{\dagger}]_{+}=0
,
[\hat{a}_{i\sigma},\hat{a}_{j\tau}^{\dagger}]_{+}=\delta_{ij}\delta_{\sigma\tau}
. The notation can be simplified for identical indices:
\hat{S}^{-}_{i}\equiv\hat{S}^{-}_{ii}
and
\hat{S}^{+}_{i}\equiv\hat{S}^{+}_{ii}
.

Based on our previous findings
Cassam-Chenaï and
Jourdan 2025
we advocate a simplified "
2D
-Block" version of the UfpSDCC ansatz, where each orbital
p_{i}
occupied by an electron pair, is broken by means of an excitation towards only one, specifically associated, virtual orbital,
q_{i}
, reminiscent of the generalized valence bond perfect-pairing (GVB-PP) method
Goddard III 1967
;
Goddard III et al. 1973
. So, it can also be regarded as an UCC analogue of a Block-Correlated Coupled-Cluster (BCCC) method
Wang et al. 2020
but with a different technique to partition the orbitals into blocks.
The couple
(p_{i},q_{i})
forms, what we call the
i^{th}

2D
-block. Note that, this orbital partitioning into blocks is only relevant when it has been optimized in a specific way
Cassam-Chenaï and
Jourdan 2025
. Then, the
\hat{T}_{1}
cluster operator becomes,

\hat{Y}=\sum^{n_{occ}}_{i=1}\mu_{i}(\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}q_{i}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}q_{i}}),

(16)

and the
\hat{T^{\prime}}_{2}

\hat{X}=\sum^{n_{occ}}_{1\leq i<j}\mu_{ij}(\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}q_{i}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{j}q_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{j}q_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}q_{i}}).

(17)

Although more simple, they still do not commute. More precisely, setting

\displaystyle\hat{A}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}q_{i}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{j}q_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}}\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{j}q_{j}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}q_{i}}

(18)

\displaystyle\hat{B}_{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}q_{i}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}}-\hat{S}^{+}_{p_{i}}\hat{S}^{-}_{p_{i}q_{i}}

(19)

we have the following commutation relations,

\displaystyle\forall i,j,k,l\qquad[\hat{A}_{ij},\hat{A}_{kl}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\hat{B}_{i},\hat{B}_{k}]=0,

(20)

\displaystyle\forall i,j,k\qquad[\hat{A}_{ij},\hat{B}_{k}]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}\hat{B}_{j}+\delta_{jk}\hat{B}_{i}.

(21)

Then, we easily obtain that,
\hat{Y}=\sum\limits^{n_{occ}}_{i=1}\mu_{i}\hat{B}_{i}
, and
\hat{X}=\sum\limits^{n_{occ}}_{1\leq i<j}\mu_{ij}\hat{A}_{ij}
fulfill the no-mixed adjoint property, Eq.(
7
),
since, according to Eqs.(
20
) and (
21
),
ad^{i}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
can only depend on
\hat{B}_{k}
operators, which all commute with
\hat{Y}
. So, the simplified formula, Eq.(
8
), for the Zassenhaus coefficients does apply.

3.2
Case of two electron pairs

The simplest non-trivial case,
n_{occ}=2
, corresponds to a two electron-pair system, such as the lithium hydride,
LiH
, molecule.
Then,
\hat{X}=\mu_{12}\hat{A}_{12}
and
\hat{Y}=\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2}
, the
\mu
’s parameters being non-zero. In fact, one could add more
\hat{B}_{k}
,
k>2
, operators in
\hat{Y}
, it would not add any difficulty, as these additional operators would commute with everything else.

We have to calculate the iterated adjoints,
ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
, to be able to use Eq.(
8
). Two cases are to be distinguished depending on the parity of
n
. For
n=2k
even, iterating Eq.(
21
), taking into account additional
\mu
-coefficients, one has:

\displaystyle ad^{2k}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{12})^{2k}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2}),

(22)

while for
n=2k+1
odd,

\displaystyle ad^{2k+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mu_{12})^{2k+1}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{2}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{1}).

(23)

This leads to the following Zassenhaus coefficients,
\forall k\geq 1
,

\hat{C}_{2k}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\frac{-1}{(2k)!}(\mu_{12})^{2k-1}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{2}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{1}),

(24)

\hat{C}_{2k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}(\mu_{12})^{2k}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2}).

(25)

Their sum splits according to parity, and then can be factored into a
\hat{B}_{1}
and a
\hat{B}_{2}
term,

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{n=1}\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{k\geq 0}\frac{1}{(2k+1)!}(\mu_{12})^{2k}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2})+\sum^{+\infty}_{k>0}\frac{-1}{(2k)!}(\mu_{12})^{2k-1}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{2}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})}{\mu_{12}}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2})-\frac{\cosh(\mu_{12})-1}{\mu_{12}}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{2}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{1}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{2}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{1}+\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{2}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{1}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{2}

So, the Zassenhaus decomposition gives:

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\exp(\mu_{12}\hat{A}_{12})\exp\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{1}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{2}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{1}+\right.

\displaystyle\left.\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{2}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{1}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{2}\right).

(27)

The
\hat{B}_{i}
’s commuting, this can be re-expressed as a product of exponentials. If more
\hat{B}_{k}
,
k>2
, are present, one can write more generally:

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\exp(\mu_{12}\hat{A}_{12})\exp\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{1}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{2}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\exp\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{2}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{1}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{2}\right)\prod_{k>2}\exp(\mu_{k}\hat{B}_{k}).

(28)

This is a property of paramount importance, especially in view of quantum computing applications, as it saves an
a priori
infinite number of quantum gates to achieve an exact unitary transformation. Only the generating operators
A_{ij}
and
B_{k}
need to be implemented as quantum circuits. This extends to the general case.

In real life applications, the cluster operators are used to correct an already reasonable reference state. So, the
\mu
’s parameters are supposed to be small. An important remark is that, as
\mu_{12}\rightarrow 0
,
\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{1}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{2}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{1}\right)\rightarrow\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{1}
and
\left(\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{2}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{1}}{\mu_{12}}\hat{B}_{2}\right)\rightarrow\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{2}
, that is to say, although the commutator
ad^{1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}=\mu_{12}(\mu_{1}\hat{B}_{2}+\mu_{2}\hat{B}_{1})
is of order
1
in
\mu_{12}
, it is as if
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
were commuting to first order in Eq.(
28
). This also implies that, if we set
\alpha=\mu_{12},\beta=\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{1}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{2}}{\mu_{12}},\gamma=\frac{\sinh(\mu_{12})\mu_{2}-(\cosh(\mu_{12})-1)\mu_{1}}{\mu_{12}}
, then the Jacobian of the transformation is non-zero, so that, in practice, one can equivalently and more simply optimize
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
, instead of
\mu_{12},\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
. This also extends to the general case.

Although the use of a conditional two-qubit Givens gate involves only an abstract three-qubit gate, it induces algebraic dependencies among the parameters and the transpilation results in a deeper circuit than the one obtained by using a four-qubit Givens gate. Note that we do not use the usual spin-orbital encoding into qubits, instead each qubit encodes the occupation of an elementary electron pair. Our encoding generalizes that of Refs.
Khamoshi et al. 2020
;
Elfving et al. 2021

As we shall see in the next section, the structure of the ansatz in the general case is a simple generalization of the
N=2

3.3
General Case

Now, consider a system of
N=n_{occ}
electron pairs,

\hat{X}=\sum\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\mu_{ij}\hat{A}_{ij},

(29)

\hat{Y}=\sum\limits^{N}_{i=1}\mu_{i}\hat{B}_{i},

(30)

the
\mu
’s parameters being non-zero, otherwise the corresponding operator could just be eliminated.
We denote by
\mathcal{D}_{N,2}
the set of
N(N-1)
directed edges of
N
vertices
\left\{1,\ldots,N\right\}
. So, if
i\rightarrow j\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}
, we set
\nu_{i\rightarrow j}=\mu_{ij}
if
i<j
, else if
j<i
,
\nu_{i\rightarrow j}=\mu_{ji}
.
This notation is more convenient to express the general iterated adjoint,

\displaystyle ad^{k}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq j_{0},i_{k+1}\leq N,\ i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}}{{j_{0}=i_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=i_{k+1}}}}\mu_{j_{0}}\nu_{i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1}}\cdots\nu_{i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}}\hat{B}_{i_{k+1}}.

(31)

The sum extends over all possible chains of indices such that two successive
\nu
-coefficients have at least one common index, starting with
j_{0}
, the single index of a
\mu
-coefficient, and ending with
i_{k+1}
, the index of a
\hat{B}
-operator. The Zassenhaus coefficients are expressed as,

\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq j_{0},i_{k+1}\leq N,\ i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}}{{j_{0}=i_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=i_{k+1}}}}\mu_{j_{0}}\nu_{i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1}}\cdots\nu_{i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}}\hat{B}_{i_{k+1}}.

(32)

Their sum gives,

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{k=0}\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{k=0}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq j_{0},i_{k+1}\leq N,\ i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}}{{j_{0}=i_{1},\ldots,j_{k}=i_{k+1}}}}\mu_{j_{0}}\nu_{i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1}}\cdots\nu_{i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}}\hat{B}_{i_{k+1}}.

By introducing a
N\times N
symmetric matrix
M
such that:

\displaystyle\forall i\neq j,\ M_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\nu_{i\rightarrow j}

\displaystyle M_{ii}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(34)

as well as
N
-component vectors,
\vec{\mu}\equiv(\mu_{i})_{i}
, and,
\vec{\hat{B}}\equiv(\hat{B}_{i})_{i}
, by noticing that:

(M^{k})_{i_{1}j_{k}}=\sum_{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle 1\leq j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\leq N}}{{j_{1}=i_{2},\ldots,j_{k-1}=i_{k}}}}\nu_{i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1}}\ldots\nu_{i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}},

(35)

one can cast Eq.(
) into:

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\vec{\mu}\cdot M^{k}\cdot\vec{\hat{B}}.

(36)

Then, if the matrix
M
is invertible, denoting by
\mathbb{I}
the
(N\times N)
-identity matrix,

\sum_{k=0}^{+\infty}\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)M^{-1}\cdot\vec{\hat{B}}\ .

(37)

It should be stressed out that
M
is not always invertible, even if all the
\nu_{i\rightarrow j}
are non-zero and
N
as small as
4
. A more general formulation, valid in all cases, is addressed in Appendix B. However, equation (
37
) is easy to handle, and since the set of
M
-matrices with non-zero determinant is a dense set, the optimization of the
\mu_{ij}
parameters can be restricted to invertible
M
-matrices without loss of accuracy. So, assuming
M
invertible, a practical expression for the Zassenhaus decomposition is:

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\exp(\mu_{ij}\hat{A}_{ij})\prod\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}\exp\left(\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)\cdot M^{-1}_{k}\hat{B}_{k}\right),

(38)

where
M^{-1}_{l}
is the
l^{th}
column vector of
M^{-1}
.

For
N=2
,
M^{-1}=\frac{1}{\mu_{12}^{2}}M
, and
\exp(-M)=\begin{pmatrix}\cosh{\mu_{12}}&-\sinh{\mu_{12}}\\
-\sinh{\mu_{12}}&\cosh{\mu_{12}}\end{pmatrix}
, so that Eq.(
28
) can be recovered (with no extra
\hat{B}_{k}
).

As in the
N=2
case, it is interesting to consider the limit where the
\mu_{ij}
’s tend to zero, since they are supposed to be small parameters. Then, we have
M\rightarrow 0
, and
\lim\limits_{M\to 0}\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)\cdot M^{-1}_{k}=\lim\limits_{M\to 0}\vec{\mu}\cdot M\cdot M^{-1}_{k}=\mu_{k}
, so that,
\lim\limits_{M\to 0}\exp\left(\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)\cdot M^{-1}_{k}\hat{B}_{k}\right)=\exp\left(\mu_{k}\hat{B}_{k}\right)
. This implies that the Jacobian of the change of variables
\mu_{ij}\rightarrow\mu_{ij},\mu_{k}\rightarrow\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)\cdot M^{-1}_{k}
is non zero. So, in practice, one can equivalently and more simply optimize the
\gamma_{k}\equiv\vec{\mu}\cdot\left(\mathbb{I}-\exp(-M)\right)\cdot M^{-1}_{k}
’s, instead of the
\mu_{k}
’s. If the dominant contributions, in some UCC applications, come from excitation operators satisfying the no-mixed adjoint property, this may also explain why optimizing the parameters after Trotterization actually produces insignificant errors, as observed in Refs.
Grimsley et al. 2019
;
Sokolov et al. 2020
.

Each exponential operator in the right-hand side of Eq. (
38
) can be implemented by Givens gates, as in the case
N=2
. Their total number will be (at most)
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
for the
\hat{A}_{jk}
’s and
N
for the
\hat{B}_{l}
’s, so
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
altogether.

4
Conclusion

We have shown that the Zassenhaus decomposition for the exponential of the sum of two non-commuting operators, simplifies drastically when these operators satisfy a simple condition, called the no-mixed adjoint property. An important application to a unitary pair Coupled Cluster method has been presented. The simple formula obtained in this work, for the exponential of a cluster operator built from non-commuting mono-excitations and broken-pair di-excitations, shows that, within a change of variables for the optimized parameters, the exponential can be decomposed exactly into a finite product of elementary Givens quantum gates, just as if the excitation operators were commuting. Alternatively, the formula demonstrates that exact results can be obtained if parameter optimization is performed after
1
-step Trotterization i.e. for disentangled UCC
Evangelista et al. 2019
. Note that, in contrast to Ref.
Evangelista et al. 2019
, our exact product of particle-hole one- and two-body unitary transformations is finite,
even if we add in front of it, a frozen pair operator acting on a Slater determinant. Note also, that the pair operator,
T_{int}
of Eq.(
11
), does not need to be frozen: its
\lambda_{pq}
parameters can be optimized together with the
\mu_{ij}
’s and
\mu_{k}
’s of
T_{ext}
, provided extra parametrized Givens gates are added upstream in the ansatz preparation circuit. In any case, the number of Givens gates will not exceed that of free parameters.

So, the 2D-Block UpCC method being parsimonious in quantum gates, with no approximation, it seems particularly well-adapted to an implementation on NISQ computers. Based on our previous study
Cassam-Chenaï and
Jourdan 2025
, we believe that, it can capture the most important contributions to electronic correlations in molecular systems, including strongly correlated ones. Work is ongoing and we hope to report numerical results, very soon.

We also hope that the present study can trigger the construction of other ansätze, from similar particule-hole operators satisfying the no-mixed adjoint property, in quantum chemistry or in other quantum physical contexts, such as condensed matter, for models based on electron pairs or in nuclear physics for pairs of nucleons.

2.2

Let us first consider the following lemma:

5.1
Lemma 1:

For a couple of operators
\hat{X}
and
\hat{Y}
,
p\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
,
q\in\mathbb{N}
,
p\neq q
and
m\in\llbracket 0,p-1\rrbracket
, we have:

[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]=\sum^{m+1}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+1-n}\binom{m+1}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+1-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

(39)

Proof:

We prove the lemma by induction on
m
. For
m=0
, Eq.(
39
) gives:

[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]=ad_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]-[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

(40)

Some simple algebra shows that this equality is verified:

\displaystyle[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=[[\hat{X},ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}],ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{X}(ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}),ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]-[(ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})\hat{X},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\hat{X}[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]+(ad^{q+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})(ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})-(ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})(ad^{q+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y})-[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]\hat{X}

\displaystyle=[\hat{X},[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]]-[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=ad_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]-[ad^{p-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}].

Suppose now that Eq.(
39
) is satisfied for
m<p-1
. Injecting Eq.(
40
) in Eq.(
39
), we obtain:

\displaystyle[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+1}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+1-n}\binom{m+1}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}(ad_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+1-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle-[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+1-n+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}])

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+1}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+1-n}\binom{m+1}{n}ad^{n+1}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+1-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle-\sum^{m+1}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+1-n}\binom{m+1}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}].

Setting
u=n+1
in the first term, then renaming
u
as
n
, we get:

\displaystyle[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+2}_{n=1}(-1)^{m+2-n}\binom{m+1}{n-1}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]-

\displaystyle\sum^{m+1}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+1-n}\binom{m+1}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+1}_{n=1}(-1)^{m+2-n}\left[\binom{m+1}{n-1}+\binom{m+1}{n}\right]ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle+ad^{m+2}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]-(-1)^{m+1}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}].

This can be re-condensed with the help of Pascal’s triangle,

\displaystyle[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+1}_{n=1}(-1)^{m+1-n+1}\binom{m+2}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+1-n+1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle+ad^{m+2}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]+(-1)^{m+2}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle=\sum^{m+2}_{n=0}(-1)^{m+2-n}\binom{m+2}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}[ad^{p-m-2}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q+m+2-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]

Eq.(
39
) is thus verified for
m+1
. Hence, the lemma, which leads to the following corollary.

5.2
Corollary:

Let
(\hat{X},\hat{Y})
satisfying the no-mixed adjoint property,

\forall p,q\in\mathbb{N}
:

[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]=0,

(41)

Proof:

If p=q, the result is obvious. So, we can choose
p>q
without loss of generality,
and apply lemma 1 with
m=p-1
:

[ad^{p}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{q}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}]=\sum^{p}_{n=0}(-1)^{p-n}\binom{p}{n}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}ad_{\hat{Y}}ad^{q+p-n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}=0

(42)

because of the no-mixed adjoint property.

We are now able to prove our main theorem.

5.3

We recall the recursive relation established in
Casas et al. 2012

\hat{C}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\hat{f}_{1,n}\ ,

(43)

with, for

\hat{f}_{1,n}=\sum^{n}_{j=1}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{j!(n-j)!}ad^{n-j}_{\hat{Y}}ad^{j}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}\ .

(44)

For
n\geqslant 4
, another formula is required:

\hat{C}_{n+1}=\frac{1}{n+1}\hat{f}_{[\frac{n}{2}],n}

(45)

where
[.]
denotes the integer part and
\hat{f}_{m,n}
, for
n\geqslant m\geqslant 2
, is given by:

\hat{f}_{m,n}=\sum^{[\frac{n}{m}]-1}_{j=0}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}ad^{j}_{\hat{C}_{m}}\hat{f}_{m-1,n-mj}

(46)

Let us show by induction on
m
that
\forall n\geqslant m\geqslant 1
,
\hat{f}_{m,n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
(independently of
m
) when the no-mixed adjoint property is satisfied.

For
m=1
, the result is obtained immediately by using the no-mixed adjoint property in Eq.(
44
), the only non-zero term corresponding to
j=n
.

Suppose that the result is true up to
m\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
. We have
\forall n\geqslant m+1
by Eq.(
46
):

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{m+1,n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{[\frac{n}{m+1}]-1}_{j=0}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}ad^{j}_{\hat{C}_{m+1}}\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}

(47)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\hat{f}_{m,n}+\sum^{[\frac{n}{m+1}]-1}_{j=1}\frac{(-1)^{j}}{j!}ad^{j-1}_{\hat{C}_{m+1}}ad^{1}_{\hat{C}_{m+1}}\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}.

Considering the right-hand side factors appearing in the summation and substituting Eq.(
45
), we have:

\displaystyle ad^{1}_{\hat{C}_{m+1}}\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}=[\hat{C}_{m+1},\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m+1}[\hat{f}_{[\frac{m}{2}],m},\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}]

\displaystyle\propto[ad^{m}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y},ad^{n-(m+1)j}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}],

where we have used the induction hypothesis in the last line.
From the corollary Eq.(
41
), we deduce
ad^{1}_{\hat{C}_{m+1}}\hat{f}_{m,n-(m+1)j}=0
, so that Eq.(
47
) reduces to:

\hat{f}_{m+1,n}=\hat{f}_{m,n}=\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n!}ad^{n}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}

which is the expected result.

The first three terms are according to Eq.(

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{2}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}[\hat{X},\hat{Y}]

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{3}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}[\hat{X},[\hat{X},\hat{Y}]]

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{4}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{24}[\hat{X},[\hat{X},[\hat{X},\hat{Y}]]].

Then, for
n\geq 5
, since
\hat{f}_{[\frac{n-1}{2}],n-1}=\hat{f}_{1,n-1}=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{(n-1)!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}
, Eq.(

\hat{C}_{n}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\frac{(-1)^{n-1}}{n!}ad^{n-1}_{\hat{X}}\hat{Y}.

(48)

Simplified Zassenhaus formula in terms of
*
-product on a free algebraic structure

To reach a simplified expression of Zassenhaus formula similar to the
(N=2)
-case, for operators satisfying the no-mixed adjoint property, we define a
*
-product on the free algebra generated by the
\nu_{i\rightarrow j}
’s:

\forall i,j,k,l\quad\nu_{i\rightarrow j}*\nu_{k\rightarrow l}=\delta_{jk}\ \nu_{i\rightarrow j}\nu_{j\rightarrow l},

(49)

where
\delta_{jk}
is a Kronecker-like delta giving the empty symbol instead of the concatenated one,
\nu_{i\rightarrow j}\nu_{j\rightarrow l}
, when
j\neq k
.
This operation is non-commutative. However, it is associative and distributive over
+
.
Considering the indices of single index
\mu
coefficients and
\hat{B}
-operators as vertices, we extend the definition of the
*
-product to a wider free algebraic structure, where every word starts with a
\mu
symbol and ends with a
\hat{B}
symbol, (it can be seen as the tensor product of the
\mu
-symbol free module with the
\nu
-symbol free algebra, tensor product with the
\hat{B}
symbol free module):

\displaystyle\forall i,j,k,l\quad\mu_{i}*\nu_{k\rightarrow l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}\ \mu_{i}\nu_{i\rightarrow l}

(50)

\displaystyle\nu_{i\rightarrow j}*\hat{B}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{jk}\ \nu_{i\rightarrow j}\hat{B}_{j}

(51)

\displaystyle\mu_{i}*\hat{B}_{k}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{ik}\ \mu_{i}\hat{B}_{i}

(52)

Then, denoting
\pi
the morphism projecting the free algebra on the real numbers, Eq.(
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{k=0}\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{k=0}\frac{(-1)^{k}}{(k+1)!}\sum_{1\leq j_{0},i_{k+1}\leq N,\ i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1},\cdots,i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\pi(\mu_{j_{0}}*\nu_{i_{1}\rightarrow j_{1}}*\cdots*\nu_{i_{k}\rightarrow j_{k}}*\hat{B}_{i_{k+1}})

(53)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,l\leq N}\pi\left(\mu_{i}*\sum^{+\infty}_{n=0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n+1)!}\left(\sum_{j\rightarrow k\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{j\rightarrow k}\right)^{*n}*\hat{B}_{l}\right),

where we have used the notation
x^{*n}\equiv\underbrace{x*\cdots*x}_{n}
. We set in the same spirit:

{}^{*}e^{x}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum^{+\infty}_{n=0}\frac{x^{*n}}{n!},

(54)

defining a
*
-exponential function.
To make this function appear in Eq.(
53
), we need to define an inverse operation to the
*
-product. We define the
∗-1
-operation by,
\forall i,j,k,l\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ \nu_{i\rightarrow j}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\nu_{i\rightarrow j}(\nu_{k\rightarrow l})^{*-1}=0,\qquad\forall\nu_{k\rightarrow l}

(55)

\displaystyle\mathrm{for}\ \nu_{i\rightarrow j}

\displaystyle\neq

\displaystyle 0,\qquad\nu_{i\rightarrow j}(\nu_{k\rightarrow l})^{*-1}=\frac{\delta_{il}\delta_{jk}}{\nu_{i\rightarrow j}}.

(56)

Then, omitting the
\pi
projection to alleviate the notation, (that is identifying implicitly a word with its projection on the field of real numbers),

\displaystyle\sum\limits^{+\infty}_{k=0}

\displaystyle\hat{C}_{k+1}(\hat{X},\hat{Y})=\sum_{1\leq i,l\leq N}\mu_{i}*\sum^{+\infty}_{n=0}\frac{(-1)^{n}}{(n+1)!}\left(\sum_{j\rightarrow k\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{j\rightarrow k}\right)^{*(n+1)}*\left(\sum_{j\rightarrow k\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{j\rightarrow k}^{*-1}\right)*\hat{B}_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i,l\leq N}\mu_{i}*\left(1-^{*}e^{-\sum\limits_{j\rightarrow k\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{j\rightarrow k}}\right)*\left(\sum_{j\rightarrow k\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{j\rightarrow k}^{*-1}\right)*\hat{B}_{l}

and the Zassenhaus decomposition is:

\displaystyle\exp(\hat{X}+\hat{Y})=

\displaystyle\prod\limits_{1\leq i<j\leq N}\exp(\mu_{ij}\hat{A}_{ij})\prod\limits_{1\leq k\leq N}\exp\left(\sum\limits_{1\leq l\leq N}\mu_{l}*\left(1-^{*}e^{-\sum\limits_{p\rightarrow q\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{p\rightarrow q}}\right)\right.

(58)

\displaystyle\left.*\left(\sum\limits_{p\rightarrow q\in\mathcal{D}_{N,2}}\nu_{p\rightarrow q}^{*-1}\right)*\hat{B}_{k}\right).

This shows that, even in the general case one needs to implement only
\frac{N(N-1)}{2}
quantum circuits for the
\hat{A}_{jk}
’s and
N
quantum circuits for the
\hat{B}_{l}
’s, so at most
\frac{N(N+1)}{2}
altogether.

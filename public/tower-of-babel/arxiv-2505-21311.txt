Charmed Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} baryon decays into light scalar mesons in the topological S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) f SU(3)_{f} framework
Y. L. Wang, Y. K. Hsiao

Abstract
Using the topological-diagram approach based on S ​ U ​ ( 3 ) SU(3) flavor symmetry, we investigate two-body Λ c + → 𝐁 ​ S \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S decays, where Λ c + \Lambda_{c}^{+} is a member of the anti-triplet charmed baryons ( 𝐁 c {\bf B}_{c} ), 𝐁 {\bf B} denotes a final-state baryon, and S S refers to a light scalar meson, such as f 0 / f 0 ​ ( 980 ) f_{0}/f_{0}(980) , a 0 / a 0 ​ ( 980 ) a_{0}/a_{0}(980) , σ 0 / f 0 ​ ( 500 ) \sigma_{0}/f_{0}(500) , or κ / K 0 ∗ ​ ( 700 ) \kappa/K_{0}^{*}(700) . Our analysis demonstrates that short-distance contributions, represented by the topological amplitudes, play a dominant role in these decays. In particular, this framework naturally accounts for the experimentally observed branching ratio ℬ ⁡ ( Λ c + → Λ ​ a 0 + ) {\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda a_{0}^{+}) , which exceeds predictions based solely on long-distance effects by an order of magnitude. Considering the light scalar mesons as tentraquark states, we predict ℬ ( Λ c + → p f 0 , p σ 0 ) = ( 1.7 ± 0.6 , 0.02 ± 0.01 ) × 10 − 3 {\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0},p\sigma_{0})=(1.7\pm 0.6,0.02\pm 0.01)\times 10^{-3} and ℬ ( Λ c + → Σ + f 0 , Σ + σ 0 ) = ( 0.7 ± 0.5 , 1.5 ± 0.7 ) × 10 − 2 {\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}f_{0},\Sigma^{+}\sigma_{0})=(0.7\pm 0.5,1.5\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-2} . In addition, the decay Λ c + → Ξ 0 ​ κ + \Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}\kappa^{+} , which proceeds via a single W W -exchange diagram, is found to have a branching fraction of ( 4.9 ± 0.9 ) × 10 − 2 (4.9\pm 0.9)\times 10^{-2} . These results suggest that the branching ratios of 𝐁 c → 𝐁 ​ S {\bf B}_{c}\to{\bf B}S are generally comparable to those of 𝐁 c → 𝐁 ​ M {\bf B}_{c}\to{\bf B}M , where M M denotes a pseudoscalar meson. The predicted rates are sufficiently large to be probed in current experiments at BESIII, Belle II, and LHCb, offering promising opportunities to explore the internal structure of light scalar mesons.

IV
Discussions and Conclusion

Within the
SU(3)_{f}
framework, the decay channels of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S

are systematically analyzed, with the
SU(3)_{f}
topological parameters
employed to relate the amplitudes across all possible decay modes.
Due to the limited experimental data, specifically,
{\cal B}_{\rm ex}(f_{0})
,
{\cal B}_{\rm ex}(a_{0}^{+})
,

{\cal B}_{S}(f_{0},a_{0}^{0})
, and
{\cal B}_{S}(f_{0},\sigma_{0})
, this study focuses exclusively on the decays

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S
. The successful global fits to the available data
indicates that short-distance contributions, as captured by the topological diagrams,
play a dominant role in governing the
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S
processes.

As factorization leads to
T=C\simeq 0
, the branching fractions are expected to be
dominated by non-factorizable topological contributions.
As shown in the fit results of Eq. (
III
),
C^{\prime}
is small, while

E^{\prime}
and
E_{\bf B}
are significant in both scenarios. Consequently,
the Cabibbo-allowed amplitudes can be approximated as

{\cal M}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda a_{0}^{+})\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(E_{{\bf B}}+E^{\prime})
and

{\cal M}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)})\simeq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(E_{{\bf B}}-E^{\prime})
,
where constructive interfere occurs for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda a_{0}^{+}
,
and destructive interfere for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)}
.
The resulting branching fractions are

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda^{0}a_{0}^{+},\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1.2\pm 0.5,0.01\pm 0.12)\times 10^{-2}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda^{0}a_{0}^{+},\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(1.2\pm 0.4,0.4\pm 0.4)\times 10^{-2}\,,

(28)

where
{\cal B}_{q\bar{q},q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}
correspond to the cases where

S
is treated as the
q\bar{q}
and
q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}
states, respectively.
The suppression of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda^{0}a_{0}^{+}
compared to

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)}
arises from the interference pattern in each scenario.
Both theoretical results for
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda^{0}a_{0}^{+}
are consistent with

{\cal B}_{\rm ex}(a_{0}^{+})\simeq 10^{-2}
, suggesting that this mode
is not sensitive to the internal structure of
a_{0}
. In contrast,
a precise measurement of
{\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{0(+)}a_{0}^{+(0)})\sim 4\times 10^{-3}

or its deviation from this value could help discriminate between the
q\bar{q}
and
q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}
structures
of the light scalar meson.

We also predict the following branching fractions

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}f_{0},\Sigma^{+}\sigma_{0})=(0.9\pm 0.5,1.2\pm 0.9)\times 10^{-2}\,,

\displaystyle{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}f_{0},\Sigma^{+}\sigma_{0})=(0.7\pm 0.5,1.5\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-2}\,.

(29)

These results show that, in both the S1 and S2 scenarios,
the branching fractions of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S
decays can reach the
10^{-3}
to
10^{-2}
level,
making them accessible to future experimental measurements.
Short-distance contributions are responsible for producing several branching fractions
as large as
10^{-2}
. For instance, the decays

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}\kappa^{+}
and
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\bar{\kappa}^{0}

receive exclusive contributions from
E^{\prime}
and
-E_{S}^{\prime}
(evuivalently
-E_{\bf B}
),
respectively. As a result, we predict

{\cal B}_{(q\bar{q},q^{2}\bar{q}^{2})}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}\kappa^{+})=(3.1\pm 1.1,4.9\pm 0.9)\times 10^{-2}
and

{\cal B}_{(q\bar{q},q^{2}\bar{q}^{2})}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\bar{\kappa}^{0})=(2.7\pm 1.0,1.2\pm 0.8)\times 10^{-2}
. It is worth noting that the analogous decays

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}K^{+},\Xi^{*0}K^{+}
, which proceed via a single

E^{\prime}
contribution, have the measured branching fractions of approximately

5\times 10^{-3}

BESIII:2018cvs
. This highlights that generally
all
{\bf B}_{c}\to{\bf B}S
decays can naturally attain branching fractions
comparable to those of
{\bf B}_{c}\to{\bf B}M
, supporting
the expectation of significant rates in many of these channels.

In the
q\bar{q}
picture,
s\phi_{I}^{2}\simeq 0.016

in
{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})\propto s\phi_{I}^{2}\times(E^{\prime})^{2}

results in a significantly suppressed branching fraction. Consequently,
we obtain
{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})=(0.15\pm 0.08)\times 10^{-3}
,
which is about 20 times smaller than the experimental value
of
(3.4\pm 2.3)\times 10^{-3}

Barlag:1990yv
;
pdg
.
By contrast, in the
q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}
tetraquark scenario,

c\phi_{II}^{2}\simeq 1.0
in
{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})\propto c\phi_{II}^{2}\times(E^{\prime})^{2}
leads to

{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})=(1.7\pm 0.6)\times 10^{-3}
,
which is in reasonable agreement with the data.
The quality of the fits reflects this difference. In the S1 global fit,

\chi^{2}/n.d.f=2.0
indicates a poor match to the experimental data, whereas

\chi^{2}/n.d.f=0.6
in the S2 global fit is close to 1, suggesting a better overall consistency.
This mild preference lends some support to the tetraquark interpretation.

According to the mild preference with
\chi^{2}/n.d.f
,
currently the measured branching fractions remains insufficient
and not precise enough to confirm the quark contents of light scalar mesons.
In particular, the uncertainty in
{\cal B}_{\rm ex}(f_{0})
remains sizeble,
highlighting the need for further investigation. To aid future analysis,
we identify the following relations

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})}{{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\sigma_{0})}=\tan^{2}\theta_{I}\times(\Phi_{f_{0}}/\Phi_{\sigma_{0}})=0.127\,,

\displaystyle\frac{{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\sigma_{0})}{{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})}=\tan^{2}\theta_{II}\times(\Phi_{\sigma_{0}}/\Phi_{f_{0}})=0.015\,,

(30)

where
\tan^{2}\theta_{I}=0.19
,
\tan^{2}\theta_{II}=0.01
,
and
(\Phi_{f_{0}}/\Phi_{\sigma_{0}})=0.67
denotes the ratio of the integrated phase space factors
defined below Eq. (
26
).
Notably, these ratios are independent of the topological parameters and their uncertainties.
Hence, future experimental efforts can test the contrasting relations:

{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})\simeq 0.1{\cal B}_{q\bar{q}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\sigma_{0})

and
{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\sigma_{0})\simeq 0.02{\cal B}_{q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})
,
which may provide a clear signature to distinguish between the two scenarios.

In summary, we have proposed that short-distance contributions, captured by
the topological diagrams within the
SU(3)_{f}
framework,
play a dominant role in
{\bf B}_{c}\to{\bf B}S
.
Using the current experimental data, we extracted the relevant topological amplitudes
and applied them to compute branching fractions across various decay channels.
Our analysis successfully accounted for the observed branching fraction of

\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Lambda a_{0}^{+}
, which is measured to be an order of magnitude
larger than predictions based solely on long-distance effects.
By modeling the light scalar mesons as tetraquark candidates, we showed that the branching fractions
of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}S
can be comparable to those of
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to{\bf B}M
,
driven predominantly by non-factorizable short-distance contributions.
In particular, we predicted

{\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to pf_{0})=(1.7\pm 0.6)\times 10^{-3}
,

{\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Sigma^{+}f_{0},\Sigma^{+}\sigma_{0})=(0.7\pm 0.5,1.5\pm 0.7)\times 10^{-2}
, and a substantially suppressed

{\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to p\sigma_{0})=(0.02\pm 0.01)\times 10^{-3}
.
We also found that the decay
\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}\kappa^{+}
,
which receives a sole contribution from a non-factorizable
W
-exchange diagram, yields

{\cal B}(\Lambda_{c}^{+}\to\Xi^{0}\kappa^{+})=(4.9\pm 0.9)\times 10^{-2}
.
Additionally, we have performed global fits under the assumption that
light scalar mesons are conventional
q\bar{q}
states.
While this scenario can still accommodate the data, it yields a slightly larger

\chi^{2}/n.d.f
compared to the tetraquark interpretation, indicating that the

q^{2}\bar{q}^{2}
picture is somewhat more favored by current measurements.

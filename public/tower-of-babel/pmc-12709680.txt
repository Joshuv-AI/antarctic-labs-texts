Expanding the genetic spectra of gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina in a Chinese cohort in Yunnan province
Authors: Feng-Juan Gao, Cong Duan, Kai-xin Chen, Yu-Qiao Ju, Qing Chang, Juan-Juan Li, Li-Wei Zhang, Zhu-Lin Hu, Ge-Zhi Xu, Yuan Zong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
BackgroundGyrate atrophy (GACR), a rare autosomal recessive chorioretinal dystrophy caused by OAT mutations, is genetically and clinically underexplored in multi-ethnic Chinese populations.ResultsEight patients from five families all exhibited high myopia (mean − 8.28 D), early-onset vision loss, and elevated plasma ornithine. Parapapillary atrophy (PPA) was common (76.92%) and correlated with worse BCVA and longer AL. Four novel OAT mutations were identified: c.213G > A (p.Trp71Ter), c.799 A > C (p.Thr267Pro), c.897 C > A (p.Tyr299Ter) and c.-30 + 22_-30 + 43del. Minigene assays confirmed aberrant splicing for the latter.ConclusionsThis study identifies the first pathogenic 5′ UTR variant in GACR, reveals ethnic-specific mutation profiles, and underscores PPA as a severity-linked feature.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s40246-025-00857-z.

Introduction

Gyrate atrophy of the choroid and retina (GACR) is a rare autosomal recessive chorioretinal dystrophy caused by deficiency of ornithine-delta-aminotransferase (OAT), encoded by the OAT gene on chromosome 10q26 [1]. This vitamin B6-dependent mitochondrial enzyme converts ornithine to pyrroline-5-carboxylic acid [2].

GACR is characterized by nyctalopia, progressive vision loss, visual field constriction, and often leads to legal blindness by mid-adulthood [2]. Ophthalmoscopic findings include well-circumscribed circular patches of chorioretinal atrophy in the mid-peripheral retina, frequently accompanied by myopia and posterior subcapsular cataracts [2, 3]. Lowering plasma ornithine through arginine-restricted diets and vitamin B6 supplementation may slow disease progression.

Although extremely rare, GACR has a notably high prevalence in Finland (∼1:50,000) [4]. While OAT mutations have been reported globally, few cases are documented in Chinese populations. This study aims to characterize the clinical and genetic profile of GACR across Chinese ethnic groups.

Methods

This study was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the Affiliated Hospital of Yunnan University (No. 2022205) and adhered to the Declaration of Helsinki. Eight GACR patients and their available family members from Yunnan, China were enrolled between August 2022 and August 2023. All participants provided written informed consent, including for publication of photographs.

Clinical evaluations included detailed family history and comprehensive ophthalmic examinations: best-corrected visual acuity (BCVA), slit-lamp biomicroscopy, dilated fundus exam, non-contact tonometry, autorefraction, axial length (AL) measurement, ultra-wide-field scanning laser ophthalmoscopy, and fundus autofluorescence. All patients except F5II1 underwent SD-OCT and full-field ERG; only three underwent FFA. None received vitamin B6, dorzolamide, or specialized diets.

Genetic analysis involved blood-derived genomic DNA from probands and relatives. Targeted NGS including the OAT (NM_000274.4) gene was performed, with whole-genome sequencing (WGS) used if no variants were detected. All identified variants were confirmed by Sanger sequencing, with parental samples available for all eight patients to enable segregation analysis. Pathogenicity was assessed using SIFT, PolyPhen-2, MutationTaster, GERP+, SpliceAI, and RDDC⁽ᵀᴹ⁾ per ACMG guidelines.

Plasma amino acid levels were quantified via LC-MS/MS with dried blood spots. Sample processing included disinfection, blood collection on filter paper, drying, extraction with isotope-labeled internal standard, derivatization with N-butanol hydrochloride, and tandem mass spectrometry using a Shimadzu LC-20 A system coupled with an AB SCIEX API4000⁺.

A minigene splicing assay was conducted for the c.−30 + 22_−30 + 43del variant using constructed recombinant vectors transfected into HEK293T and MCF-7 cells. Splicing effects were analyzed by RT-PCR and Sanger sequencing.

Statistical analyses used SPSS 26.0. Data are presented as mean ± SD. PPA area was measured from fundus photos using ImageJ. Spearman’s correlation assessed relationships between BCVA, PPA area, AL, and age, with significance at p < 0.05.

Results

Cohort characteristics

We enrolled eight patients (6 female, 2 male; mean age 24.13 ± 14.55 years) from five unrelated Chinese families (3 Han, 5 ethnic minorities). This modest sample size reflects the challenge of studying Gyrate Atrophy (~ 1:1,000,000 prevalence), yet it offers valuable insights into the disease’s regional genotypic and phenotypic spectrum.

Clinical phenotype

All patients were diagnosed in early childhood, presenting progressive vision loss (100%) and nyctalopia (75%). Mean BCVA was 0.37 ± 0.24, IOP was normal (mean 13.87 mmHg), and AL averaged 26.65 ± 2.05 mm. All had high myopia (mean − 8.28 ± 3.69 D). Presenile cataracts occurred in 4/7 patients (57.14%), with three undergoing surgery. Patients with cataracts were significantly older (34.0 ± 11.6 vs. 8.0 ± 1.73 years, p = 0.019) (Table 1).

Summary of mutations, ophthalmologic findings, and other features of the eight patients

Fundus imaging showed well-circumscribed chorioretinal atrophy in all eyes (Fig. 1A–B), sparing the posterior pole except in two advanced cases. OCT revealed intraretinal cystoid spaces in the parafovea in 12/13 eyes, primarily in the INL (100%) and ONL (91.67%), with frequent ELM (91.67%) and EZ (75%) disruption (Fig. 1C). Epiretinal membrane was present in 38.46% of eyes. PPA was observed in 10/13 eyes and correlated negatively with BCVA (r = − 0.593, p = 0.016) and positively with AL (r = 0.500, p = 0.041), but not with age. Temporal and inferior quadrants were most affected (Supplementary Fig. 1 A and B). FFA showed chorioretinal atrophy with visible choroidal vessels, hyperfluorescent borders in two patients, and no leakage (Supplementary Fig. 1 C). ERG indicated severely reduced cone and rod responses.

Genetic results

Five OAT variants were identified, including four novel ones: c.−30 + 22_−30 + 43del, c.213G > A (p.Trp71Ter), c.799 A > C (p.Thr267Pro) and c.897 C > A (p.Tyr299Ter). The splicing variant c.−30 + 22_−30 + 43del was found only in ethnic minority patients (all five, four homozygous). Bioinformatics and minigene assays confirmed its pathogenicity, causing exon 1 skipping and a 95-bp deletion (Supplementary Fig. 2). The missense variant c.967T > C (p.Tyr323His) was homozygous in two Han patients and predicted damaging to protein function (Supplementary Fig. 3). No other pathogenic variants were detected.

Discussion

GACR is an extremely rare inherited retinal disease. In this study, we characterized the clinical and genetic spectrum of GACR in a Chinese cohort representing four ethnic groups (Nu, Yi, Hui, and Han). Using targeted NGS, we identified four pathogenic variants in the OAT gene: c.213G > A (p.Trp71Ter), c.799 A > C (p.Thr267Pro), c.897 C > A (p.Tyr299Ter) and c.967T > C (p.Tyr323His). WGS further uncovered a previously unreported splicing variant (c.−30 + 22_−30 + 43del) in the 5′ UTR of OAT. Notably, this variant was found exclusively in patients from ethnic minority backgrounds and was initially classified as a variant of uncertain significance (VUS) according to ACMG guidelines. Subsequent familial segregation and functional assays confirmed that this variant causes aberrant splicing—specifically, a 95-bp deletion leading to exon 1 skipping—which results in the loss of both the start codon and the mitochondrial targeting sequence (MTS). This is predicted to lead to a complete loss of function, either through failure of translation initiation or mislocalization of the protein to the cytosol, consistent with the established disease mechanism of Gyrate Atrophy. To our knowledge, this represents the first reported pathogenic 5′ UTR variant associated with GACR.

The recurrence of the c.−30 + 22_−30 + 43del variant in three unrelated families from Southern China—a region with documented genetic isolation—suggests a potential founder effect. This hypothesis is further supported by the variant’s very low frequency in East Asian populations in gnomAD and its absence in other ethnic groups. Future haplotype analysis of the flanking regions in these families will be necessary to confirm a shared ancestral haplotype and provide definitive evidence for a founder mutation.

Consistent with previous reports, affected individuals in our cohort exhibited classic features of GACR, including nyctalopia, progressive visual decline, myopia, lens opacities, pathognomonic chorioretinal atrophy, macular changes, severely reduced or non-detectable electroretinographic responses, and elevated plasma ornithine levels. Systemic manifestations such as neurological or psychiatric abnormalities were rare and limited to one patient with epilepsy and depression, aligning with prior observations that neurologic involvement in GACR is variable and often subclinical [5–7].

Macular complications—including foveoschisis, epiretinal membrane, and macular atrophy—were commonly detected on SD-OCT, consistent with earlier studies implicating Müller cell dysfunction secondary to hyperornithinemia [7–9]. Cystoid macular edema was not observed in any of the studied eyes. The high prevalence of peripapillary atrophy (PPA), especially among older patients, and its correlation with worse BCVA and longer AL, suggest that PPA may serve as an indicator of disease progression in GACR. Its morphological distinction from glaucomatous or myopic PPA further implies a disease-specific mechanism.

The universal presence of high myopia in this cohort complicated the differentiation between primary gyrate atrophy and secondary myopic retinopathy. Nevertheless, the hallmark peripheral chorioretinal atrophy in GACR—characterized by sharply demarcated, circular, and scalloped lesions progressing centripetally—is distinct from the diffuse tessellated fundus, posterior staphyloma, and lacquer cracks typical of pathological myopia. Although hyperornithinemia definitively confirms OAT deficiency, the co-existing high myopia likely exacerbated the retinal phenotype, particularly in cases of macular atrophy.

FFA highlighted extensive choriocapillaris atrophy, hyperfluorescent borders, and transmission defects in early cases, supporting the notion that retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) impairment is an early event in GACR. The absence of leakage further distinguishes GACR from other inflammatory or vascular retinopathies.

Although hyperornithinemia was confirmed in all patients, levels were modest relative to previous reports [6, 10], possibly due to methodological or dietary variations. Genetic analysis underscored the allelic heterogeneity of OAT and emphasized the value of WGS in detecting non-coding variants, which may be missed by panel-based NGS.

The clustering of the 5′ UTR mutation among ethnic minorities from southwestern Yunnan suggests a potential founder effect. This finding highlights the importance of expanded genetic screening in underrepresented populations and supports the use of WGS for comprehensive molecular diagnosis.

Owing to the limited sample size, our study could not adjust for potential confounders like age, sex, and myopia duration. Therefore, the definitive impact of these variables on the phenotype remains uncertain and warrants investigation in larger, multi-center cohorts.

Conclusion

In summary, this study broadens the genetic spectrum of GACR by reporting novel OAT mutations, including the first pathogenic variant in the 5′ UTR, which appears to be enriched among Chinese ethnic minorities. Clinically, the cohort consistently presented with high myopia, early cataracts, peripapillary atrophy, and foveoschisis—but not cystoid macular edema. The strong association between peripapillary atrophy and disease severity suggests its potential utility as a biomarker for progression. These findings enhance our understanding of GACR pathogenesis and emphasize the need for inclusive genetic screening strategies that encompass non-coding regions, particularly in diverse populations. Further studies with larger cohorts are warranted to validate the founder effect and clarify genotype–phenotype correlations in GACR.

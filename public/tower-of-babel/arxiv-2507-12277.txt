Branches of localized patterned states
Björn Sandstede

Abstract
Motivated by theoretical analyses of spatially localized structures with arbitrarily long periodic plateaus, we provide a framework of assumptions that simplifies their analysis and leads to a topological criterion for when localized patterned structures lie on a discrete stack of loops or on a single unbounded branch. The framework proposed here also connects closely with continuation algorithms that are often used to verify the hypotheses that guarantee the emergence of localized patterned states.

However, some of the hypotheses in

A continuation framework for localized patterned states

We consider ordinary differential equations

u_{x}=f(u,\mu),\qquad u\in\mathbb{R}^{4},\quad\mu\in\mathbb{R}

(2.1)

where
f
is
C^{\infty}
. We assume that this system admits a reverser.

Hypothesis 1

There exists a linear map
\mathcal{R}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
with
\mathcal{R}^{2}=1
and
\dim\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}=2

We say that a solution of (

Hypothesis 2

The origin

) admits a closed loop consisting of regular patterned states as encoded in the next hypothesis:

Hypothesis 3

There is a function

\mathcal{S}\colon\quad\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X},\quad s\longmapsto(\mu(s),k(s),u_{\mathrm{wt}}(\cdot;s),u_{\mathrm{het}}(\cdot;s))

. The three-dimensional center-unstable manifolds

so that

in

. Starting with

so that

is the unique phase of the base point of the strong unstable fiber of

Path lifting:

Let
\pi\colon\mathbb{R}\to S^{1},x\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}x}
be the universal covering projection of
S^{1}
. Given a path
\gamma\in C^{0}([0,1],S^{1})

\widetilde{\gamma}\in C^{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R})
is a lifting of
\gamma
if
\gamma=\pi\circ\widetilde{\gamma}
. Given an integer
n
, there exists a unique lifting
\tilde{\gamma}
of
\gamma
with
\widetilde{\gamma}(0)=\gamma(0)+2\pi n
.

We now apply these concepts to the phase function
\psi
considered as a path
\psi\in C^{0}([0,1],S^{1})

\psi(s)
is periodic in its argument
s

\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R})

\widetilde{\psi}(0)=\psi(0)

•

Isolas:
If
\psi

\widetilde{\psi}(0)=\widetilde{\psi}(1)

\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}/\mathbb{Z},\mathbb{R})

•

Snaking:
If
\psi
is not homotopic to a constant function in

\widetilde{\psi}(0)\neq\widetilde{\psi}(1)
. In this case, we can extend
\widetilde{\psi}\in C^{0}([0,1],\mathbb{R})
uniquely to a function
\bar{\psi}\in C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{+},\mathbb{R})

\bar{\psi}(s+n):=(\widetilde{\psi}(1)-\widetilde{\psi}(0))n+\widetilde{\psi}(s)
for

and

, and the function

is unbounded and in

. Possibly after reversing

Finally, for each
\epsilon>0
and each

Definition 3
(Symmetric localized patterned states)

Given

Theorem 1

Isolas:

, we can solve this system uniquely for
(L,v^{\kappa}_{0},\nu)
with
a=0
as a function of
s
when
c_{1}(s)\neq 0
and for
(L,v^{\kappa}_{0},a)
with
\nu=0
as a function of
s
when

. We note that the first case is the generic case (since we are then away from saddle-node bifurcations of the heteroclinic connections

). In either case, uniformly in

and the resulting branches lie for each fixed

Snaking:

If

is not homotopic to a constant function in

\bar{\psi}:\mathbb{R}^{+}\to\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Lifting the first equation in (

\mathbb{R}

\begin{pmatrix}\varphi_{0}+(1+\mathrm{O}(v^{\kappa}_{0}))L+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-\beta L})\\
v^{\kappa}_{0}+\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-\beta L})\\
\mathrm{O}(\mathrm{e}^{-\beta L}),\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\bar{\psi}(s)+\mathrm{O}(|\nu|+|a|)\\
\mathrm{O}(|\nu|+|a|)\\

4
Reversible hyperbolic periodic orbits are elementary

Consider the system

u_{x}=f(u),\qquad u\in\mathbb{R}^{4}

(4.1)

in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, where
f
is
C^{\infty}
. We assume that this system admits a linear reverser
\mathcal{R}:\mathbb{R}^{4}\to\mathbb{R}^{4}
so that
\mathcal{R}^{2}=1
,
\dim\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}=2
, and
f(\mathcal{R}u)=-\mathcal{R}f(u)
for all
u
. After an appropriate coordinate transformation, we may also assume that
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits(\mathcal{R})
and
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits(-\mathcal{R})
are orthogonal to each other. Assume that
u_{*}(x)
is a symmetric periodic orbit with Floquet multipliers
\mathrm{e}^{\pm\alpha}
for some
\alpha>0
and a Floquet multiplier at
1
(necessarily with algebraic multiplicity two). The following lemma should be well known, but I could not locate a reference.

Lemma 1

The periodic orbit
u_{*}(x)
is part of a smooth one-parameter family of periodic orbits
u(x;\kappa)
with
u(0;\kappa)\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}
for
\kappa
near zero so that
u_{*}(0)=u(0;0)
and
\partial_{\kappa}u(\cdot;0)\neq 0
.

Proof.
Let
2T>0
be the period of
u_{*}(x)
and denote the flow of (
4.1
) by
\Phi_{t}
. We consider sections
\Sigma_{p}
and
\Sigma_{q}
at
p=u_{*}(0)
and
q=u_{*}(T)
so that
f(p)
and
f(q)
are perpendicular to these sections at
u=p
and
u=q
, respectively. Let
\Psi:\Sigma_{p}\to\Sigma_{q}
denote the first-return map between these sections. By
[
4
, Proposition 1.6]
, it suffices to show that
\mathrm{D}\Psi(p)(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R})
is transverse to
\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}
in
\Sigma_{q}
.

Assume that this transversality condition is not met, then necessarily
\mathrm{D}\Psi(p)(\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R})=\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}
. Floquet theory implies that
\mathrm{D}\Phi_{t}(p)=C(t)\mathrm{e}^{Bt}
where
C(2T)=C(0)
is the identity. Let
v^{\mathrm{s,u}}_{0}
denote the stable and unstable eigenvectors of
\mathrm{D}\Phi_{2T}(p)
and define
v^{\mathrm{s,u}}_{T}:=C(T)v^{\mathrm{s,u}}_{0}
. Reversibility shows that we can pick
v^{\mathrm{u}}_{0}:=\mathcal{R}v^{\mathrm{s}}_{0}
so that
v^{\mathrm{s}}_{0}+v^{\mathrm{u}}_{0}\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}
and, furthermore,
v^{\mathrm{u}}_{T}=\mathcal{R}v^{\mathrm{s}}_{T}
. Our assumption on
\mathrm{D}\Psi(p)
implies that
\mathrm{D}\Phi_{T}(p)(v^{\mathrm{s}}_{0}+v^{\mathrm{u}}_{0})\in\mathop{\mathrm{}}{Fix}\nolimits\mathcal{R}
. Since
\mathrm{D}\Phi_{T}(p)(v^{\mathrm{s}}_{0}+v^{\mathrm{u}}_{0})=\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha/2}v^{\mathrm{s}}_{T}+\mathrm{e}^{\alpha/2}v^{\mathrm{u}}_{T}
, we conclude that
\mathrm{e}^{-\alpha}v^{\mathrm{s}}_{T}=v^{\mathrm{s}}_{T}
, which is possible only if
\alpha=0
. This contradicts our assumption and therefore completes the proof of the lemma.

Chromatic numbers from edge ideals: Graph classes with vanishing syzygies are polynomially χ \chi -bounded
Alexander Engström

Abstract
The chromatic number χ \chi of a graph is bounded from below by its clique number ω , \omega, but it can be arbitrary large. Perfect graphs are defined by χ = ω \chi=\omega for all induced subgraphs. An interesting relaxation are χ \chi -bounded graph classes, where χ ≤ f ⁡ ( ω ) . \chi\leq f(\omega). It is not always possible to achieve this with a polynomial f . f. The edge ideal I G I_{G} of a graph G G is generated by monomials x u ​ x v x_{u}x_{v} for each edge u ​ v uv of G . G. The bi-graded betti numbers β i , j ​ ( I ) \beta_{i,j}(I) are central algebraic geometric invariants. We study the graph classes where for some fixed i , j i,j that syzygy vanishes, that is, β i , j ​ ( I G ) = 0 . \beta_{i,j}(I_{G})=0. We prove that χ ≤ f ⁡ ( ω ) , \chi\leq f(\omega), where f f is a polynomial of degree 2 ​ j − 2 ​ i − 4 . 2j-2i-4. For the elementary special case β i , 2 ​ i + 2 ​ ( I G ) = 0 , \beta_{i,2i+2}(I_{G})=0, this amounts to that ( i + 1 ) ​ K 2 (i+1)K_{2} –free graphs are ( ω − 1 + 2 ​ i 2 ​ i ) {\omega-1+2i\choose 2i} –colorable, improving on an old combinatorial result by Wagon. We also show that triangle-free graphs with β i , j ​ ( I G ) = 0 \beta_{i,j}(I_{G})=0 are ( j − 1 ) (j-1) –colorable. Complexity wise, we show that these colorings can be derived in time O ⁡ ( n 3 ) O(n^{3}) for graphs on n n vertices. Moreover, we show that for almost all graphs with parabolic i , j , i,j, there are better bounds on χ . \chi.

Introduction

For a graph
G,
the largest number of vertices is a natural lower bound for its chromatic number:
\omega(G)\leq\chi(G).
In general that inequality may be arbitrary bad, as there are triangle-free graphs of any chromatic number. On the other hand, a perfect graph is characterized by that
\omega(H)=\chi(H)
for all of its induced subgraphs
H.
The last decades have seen an intense activity in extending our understanding of how to measure and categorize the perfectness of graphs. A class of graphs is
\chi
–bounded
by the function
f
if
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all graphs
G
in the class. The central Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture, which has been open for fifty years, is that for any forest
F,
the
F
–free graphs are
\chi
–bounded.
For established
\chi
–bounded graph classes the common next question is if the bounding function is polynomial or not. In general it can be arbitrarily bad
[
4
]
. Examples of polynomially
\chi
–bounded graph classes are even-hole-free graphs
[
5
]
, circle graphs
[
8
,
7
]
and bounded twin-width graphs
[
3
]
.

For a graph
G
on vertex set
V
and edge set
E,
its edge ideal in
\mathbf{k}[x_{v}\mid v\in V]
is generated by the monomials
x_{u}x_{v}
for
uv\in E(G).

In discussing betti numbers
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})
of edge ideals, we assume from now that
j\geq 2i+2,
because otherwise they are trivially zero. For general combinatorial considerations regarding edge ideals, see for example
[
9
]
or
[
10
]
.
From a geometric stand point, the first interesting case is if almost all
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})
vanishes, except for if
j=i+2.
Then the ideal is said to have a
linear
resolution. In a seminal paper building a bridge between algebra and combinatorics, Fröberg proved
[
11
]
that an edge ideal has a linear resolution if and only if the graph is the complement of a chordal graph. The complement of a chordal graph is perfect, setting the backdrop for this paper. In Fröberg’s setting an infinite number syzygyies were set to vanish, but we study when only a single one does. The last years a number of interesting combinatorial results have been established with this methodology, for example Randriambololona
[
12
]
won the best paper award of Eurocrypt 2025 investigating the post-quantum cryptographic algorithm McEliece.

The main result of this paper is proved in Section 4: Corollary
27
states that the class of graphs with
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})=0
is
\chi
–bounded by

{\omega-1+2(j-i-2)\choose 2(j-i-2)}+{j-2\choose 2(j-i-2)+1},

which is a polynomial in
\omega
of degree
2(j-i-2).
For triangle-free graphs the slightly stronger result
\chi(G)\leq j-1
is proved in Corollary

.

In Section 5 we show in Corollary

(j-i)^{2}\geq j+i+2
, then almost all graphs

with
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})=0
are
\chi
-bounded by

{\omega-1+2(j-i-2)\choose 2(j-i-2)}.

In the final Section 6 we show in Corollary

that the graph colorings in our main result may be done in time

for graphs on
n

The chromatic number of a graph
G
is denoted by
\chi(G).
The order of the largest clique of

is denoted by

Definition 1
.

An hereditary class of graphs is
\chi
-bounded by
f
if
\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))
for all graphs
G
in the class.

The disjoint union of two graphs

and

is denoted

and multiples by

The join

is
G\cup H
with edges added between all of
G
and
H.
For a graph
G
and a set
\bf{H}
of graphs, let
G\cup{\bf H}=\{G\cup H\mid H\in{\bf H}\}
and
G+{\bf H}=\{G+H\mid H\in{\bf H}\}.
The induced subgraphs on

of

is denoted

Basic graphs on
n

the cycle

and the path

Wagon
[

]
proved the following influential result:

If
f_{1}^{\mathrm{W}}(\omega)=1
and

then the class of
pK_{2}
–free graphs is
\chi
–bounded by

In particular, the class of
2K_{2}
–free graphs is
\chi
–bounded by
{\omega+1\choose 2}.

The proof of Lemma
2
below builds on the proof of Proposition
1
by Wagon, who attributes the basic idea to Erdös and Hajnal. The main difference between the proofs is that the chromatic number for some induced subgraphs
G[C_{i,j}]
are better estimated using an idea by Bharati and Chodum
[
2
]
. But the main point of Lemma

is actually that it serves as a model for the more technical proof of Theorem

Lemma 2
.

If
{\bf H}
is a set of graphs and the class of
{\bf H}
–free graphs is
\chi
-bounded by
f_{\bf H},
then the class of
K_{2}\cup{\bf H}
–free graphs is
\chi
-bounded by

f_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1)f_{{\bf H}}(k).

Proof.

Let
G
be a
K_{2}\cup{\bf H}
–free graph with a clique on the vertices
1,2,\ldots,\omega=\omega(G).

For
1\leq i<j\leq\omega
define

C_{i,j}^{\prime}=\{v\in V(G)\setminus\{1,2,\ldots,\omega\}\mid vi,vj\not\in E(G).\}

For any induced subgraph
H
of
C_{i,j}^{\prime}
, the graph
K_{2}\cup H
on
\{i,j\}\cup V(H)
is an induced subgraph of
G.
By assumption,
G
is a
K_{2}\cup{\bf H}
–free graph, and, thus, the induced subgraph of
G
on
C_{i,j}^{\prime}
is an
{\bf H}
–free graph.

Set

C_{i,j}=C_{i,j}^{\prime}\setminus\left(\,\,\bigcup_{k=1}^{i-1}\bigcup_{l=k+1}^{\omega}C_{k,l}^{\prime}\quad\cup\quad\bigcup_{l=i+1}^{j-1}C_{i,l}^{\prime}\,\,\right).

If
v\in C_{i,j}
and
1\leq k<i
then
kv\in E(G)
as

v\in C_{i,j}^{\prime}\Rightarrow iv\not\in E(G)\textrm{ and }jv\not\in E(G)

and

v\not\in C_{k,j}^{\prime}\Rightarrow kv\in E(G)\textrm{ or }jv\in E(G).

If
v\in C_{i,j}
and
i<l<j
then
lv\in E(G)
as

v\in C_{i,j}^{\prime}\Rightarrow iv\not\in E(G)\textrm{ and }jv\not\in E(G)

and

v\not\in C_{i,l}^{\prime}\Rightarrow iv\in E(G)\textrm{ or }lv\in E(G).

It follows that any clique in
G[C_{i,j}]
extends to a larger clique in
G
by the
j-2
vertices

\{1,2,\ldots,i-1\}\cup\{i+1,i+2,\ldots,j-1\}.

Thus

\omega(G[C_{i,j}])\leq\omega(G)-j+2

and

\chi(G[C_{i,j}])\leq f_{{\bf H}}(\omega(G)-j+2).

So far we have partitioned the vertices in
G
outside the clique on
1,2,\ldots,\omega=\omega(G)
that has at least two non-edges to the clique. There cannot be a vertex with no non-edges to the clique, as the clique is maximal in
G.

For
1\leq i\leq\omega(G)
set

D_{i}^{\prime}=\left\{v\in V(G)\setminus\{1,2,\ldots,\omega\}\mid vi\not\in E(G);\{v1,v2,\ldots,v\omega\}\setminus vi\subseteq E(G)\right\}

If there would be an edge between two vertices
u
and
v
in
D_{i}^{\prime},
then the vertices

1,2,\ldots,i-1,i+1,\ldots,\omega,u,v

would constitute a clique on
\omega+1
vertices, which is a contradiction. Thus,
D_{i}^{\prime}
is independent. As there are no edges from
i
to vertices of
D_{i}^{\prime},
the set

D_{i}=\{i\}\cup D_{i}^{\prime}

is independent for all
i.
We only need one color for an independent set, and

\chi(G[D_{i}])=1\leq f_{{\bf H}}(1).

Coloring each part of the partition

V(G)\quad=\quad\bigcup_{i=1}^{\omega}D_{i}\quad\cup\quad\bigcup_{1\leq i<j\leq\omega}C_{i,j}

gives

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\chi(G)&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\omega}\chi(G[D_{i}])+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\omega}\chi(G[C_{i,j}])\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\omega}f_{{\bf H}}(1)+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\omega}f_{{\bf H}}(\omega-j+2)\\
&=&\displaystyle\omega f_{{\bf H}}(1)+\sum_{j=2}^{\omega}(j-1)f_{{\bf H}}(\omega-j+2)\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{j=2}^{\omega+1}(j-1)f_{{\bf H}}(\omega-j+2)\\
&=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1)f_{{\bf H}}(k)\end{array}

∎

This is completely elementary

Lemma 3
.

For non-negative integers
n
and
m,

\sum_{i=0}^{n}(n+1-i){i+m\choose m}={n+m+2\choose m+2}.

Proof.

The right hand side counts the number of order
m+2
subsets of
\{0,1,\ldots,n+m+1\}.
Put those subsets in buckets depending on the value of the second largest element of each subset. The value of the second largest element is
m+i
for
0\leq i\leq n.
There are
{i+m\choose m}
ways to select the
m
smallest elements and
(n+1-i)
ways to select the largest element. Summing up the sizes of the buckets gives the equality.
∎

Let
{\bf B}_{n,0}
be the set of graphs on
n\geq 2

n-1

{\bf B}_{n,d}=\bigcup_{m=2d}^{n-2}K_{n-m-2}+(K_{2}\cup{\bf B}_{m,d-1})

The bowtie is in

Definition 3

For any integers

and

let the function

\begin{array}[]{rcll}g_{n,d}(1)&=&1&\textrm{for $d\geq 0$ and $n\geq 2(d+1),$}\\

Theorem 18
.

For any integers
d\geq 0
and

the class of
{\bf B}_{n,d}
–free graphs is

–bounded by

Proof.

We are to show that
\chi(G)\leq g_{n,d}(\omega(G))
for every
{\bf B}_{n,d}
–free graph

First note that for graphs with

In calculations later on in the proof,

will sometimes be replaced by

The proof will be by induction on

The base case is

By definition,
{\bf B}_{n,0}
is the set of graphs on
n
vertices of maximal degree

A

–free graph

contains no induced subgraph on

or equivalently, the maximal degree of

is at most

As the maximal degree of

is at most

one may color it with

as desired.

Now to the induction step. We are provided with a
{\bf B}_{n,d}
–free graph

The induction step follows the setup of the proof of Lemma
2
. Find a clique on the vertices

and define the sets

as in that proof. For any induced subgraph

of

the induced subgraph of

is isomorphic to

If

then any induced subgraph

of

on

of

n

{\bf B}_{n,d}

{\bf B}_{n,d}\supseteq K_{j-2}+(K_{2}\cup{\bf B}_{n-j,d-1}).
This shows that

Now proceed as in the Proof of Lemma

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\chi(G)&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{\omega}\chi(G[D_{i}])+\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\omega}\chi(G[C_{i,j}])\\

Proposition 19
.

For
\omega\geq 1
it holds that

g_{n,d}(\omega)\leq{\omega-1+2d\choose 2d}+{n-2\choose 2d+1}

and it is sharp exactly when
(d=0,\omega=1,n>2)
or
(d>0,n>\omega+2d+1).

Proof.

First we verify the case
d=0,

{\omega-1+2d\choose 2d}+{n-2\choose 2d+1}={\omega-1\choose 0}+{n-2\choose 1}=1+n-2=n-1.

By definition,
g_{n,0}(\omega)=n-1
for
\omega>1,
so the proposition statement is an equality in those cases.
By definition,
g_{n,0}(\omega)=1
for
\omega=1,
so the proposition statement is an equality for
n=2
and a strict inequality for
n>2.

Now we continue by induction on
d>0.
Consider that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}g_{n,d}(\omega)&=&\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1)g_{\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d),d-1}(k)\\
&\leq&\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1)\left({k-1+2(d-1)\choose 2(d-1)}+{\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d)-2\choose 2(d-1)+1}\right).\\
\end{array}

Let’s break down the two parts of the sum. The first part is

\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1){k-1+2(d-1)\choose 2(d-1)}=\sum_{i=0}^{\omega-1}((\omega-1)+1-i){i+2d-2\choose 2d-2}={\omega-1+2d\choose 2d}

by Lemma
3
. The second part is

\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1){\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d)-2\choose 2(d-1)+1}.

The binomial coefficient vanishes unless
n+k-\omega-2\geq 2d+1,
or equivalently, that
k\geq\omega-n+2d+3.
The second part may be rewritten as:

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\displaystyle\sum_{k=\omega-n+2d+3}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1){n+k-\omega-4\choose 2d-1}&=&\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-2d-3}((n-2d-3)+1-i){i+2d-1\choose 2d-1}\\
&=&\displaystyle{n-2d-3+2d-1+2\choose 2d-1+2}\\
&=&\displaystyle{n-2\choose 2d+1},\\
\end{array}

with an application of Lemma
3
. Adding the two pieces up proves inequality.

Now to the question of when the inequality is an equality or sharp. For
d=0
it’s sharp exactly when
\omega=1
and
n>2.
For
d=1,
in the expansion of
g_{n,1}(\omega),
the terms are of the type:

g_{\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d),d-1}(k)

for
1\leq k\leq\omega.
The only one that could be sharp is for
k=1:

g_{\max(n-\omega-1,2),0}(1)

The condition is that
n-\omega-1>2,
or equivalently,
n>\omega+3
for it to be sharp.

For
d>1
we prove it by induction on
d.
The inequality for
g_{n,d}(\omega)
is sharp exactly when
n>\omega+2d+1.
We expanded
g_{n,d}(\omega)
into terms of the type

g_{\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d),d-1}(k).

By induction, the inequality for one of them is sharp exactly when

\max(n+k-\omega-2,2d)>k+2(d-1)+1.

It can never be that
2d>k+2(d-1)+1
, as
k\geq 1.
It remains that

n+k-\omega-2>2d\,\,\textrm{ and }\,\,n+k-\omega-2>k+2(d-1)+1.

The rightmost inequality implies the leftmost one, as
k\geq 1.
It simplifies to
the desired

n>\omega+2d+1

and we are done.
∎

The

\mathrm{Ind}(G)

The following three lemmas are elementary and essentially well-known:

Lemma 21

\dim\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathrm{Ind}(G))=0

Lemma 22

\dim\tilde{H}_{d+1}(\mathrm{Ind}(K_{2}\cup G))=\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(G))
for

\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(K_{m}+G))=\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(G))
for

This is central for this paper:

Lemma 24
.

If

then
\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(H))>0.

Proof.

First the case
d=0.
The graphs in
{\bf B}_{n,0}

are defined by that their maximal degree is
n-1.
Let
v
be a vertex of
H
of degree
n-1.
The complement of
H
is disconnected as
v
is isolated in it. By Lemma
21
,
\dim\tilde{H}_{0}(\mathrm{Ind}(H))>0.

Now the case
d>0
by induction. By definition

H\in{\bf B}_{n,d}=\bigcup_{m=2d}^{n-2}K_{n-m-2}+(K_{2}\cup{\bf B}_{m,d-1}).

Pick the
m
with
H\in K_{n-m-2}+(K_{2}\cup{\bf B}_{m,d-1}).
Then
H=K_{n-m-2}+(K_{2}\cup H^{\prime})
for some
H^{\prime}\in{\bf B}_{m,d-1}.
By induction,
\dim\tilde{H}_{d-1}(\mathrm{Ind}(H^{\prime}))>0.
By Lemma

\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(K_{2}\cup H^{\prime}))>0.
Finally, by Lemma

\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(H))=\dim\tilde{H}_{d}(\mathrm{Ind}(K_{n-m-2}+(K_{2}\cup H^{\prime})))>0.

∎

Reed and Yuditsky
[

]
defined an asymptotic version:

Definition 5
.

An hereditary class
\mathcal{C}
of graphs is
asymptotically
\chi
-bounded by

if

\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}{\mathbf{P}}[\chi(G)\leq f(\omega(G))\mid\textrm{$G$ is an order $n$ graph in the class $\mathcal{C}$}]=1.

Towards the Gyárfás-Sumner conjecture, they proved that for any tree
T,
the class of
T
–free graphs is asymptotically
\chi
-bounded by
\omega.

Definition 6
.

A betti number
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})
is

This theorem was proved by Engström and Orlich
[

]
.

.

Let
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})
be a parabolic betti number with

Then, for almost every graph with
\beta_{i,j}(I_{G})=0
we have:

1.

\beta_{k,l}(I_{G})=0
whenever

and

2.

parabolic
\beta_{k,l}(I_{G})>0
whenever

.

If

and

then the hereditary class of graphs with

is

-bounded by

{\omega-1+2(j-i-2)\choose 2(j-i-2)}.

Proof.

Consider

We have that

\begin{array}[]{rcl}\chi(G)&\leq&\displaystyle{\omega-1+2((2j-2i-2)-(j-i-2)-2)\choose 2((2j-2i-2)-(j-i-2)-2)}+{2j-2i-2-2\choose 2((2j-2i-2)-(j-i-2)-2)+1}\\

∎

6
Complexity theoretic results

In this section the time complexity for algorithms and subroutines is many times essentially
O(m)
for graphs with
n
vertices and
m
edges. But to be more precise, it is actually
O(m+n)
unless
certain corner cases are avoided. Unfortunately those corner cases are common in subroutines. Furthermore, working with
O()
–notation turns out to be unsuitable in recursions due to that statements like
O(1)+O(2)+\cdots+O(t)
does not make sense. Usually we will prove that algorithms run faster than
C\varphi(G)(m+n)
where
C
is a positive constant,
G
is a non-empty graph and
\varphi(G)
is a positive graph invariant.

All graph coloring in this text so far builds on repeated applications of Lemma
2
. The main complexity problem is that it requires one to find a maximum clique, and that is notoriously slow.
The first goal of this section is to replace the maximum clique in the proof of Lemma
2
by something weaker that is faster to find. A clique on
K=\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
in
G
is selected with

k=\omega(G)
to achieve the following two facts in the proof:

(1)

There is no
v\in V(G)\setminus K
adjacent to all vertices of
K.

(2)

There is no
u\in K
together with two adjacent vertices
v,w\in V(G)\setminus K
that are adjacent to all vertices of
K\setminus u.

A clique only satisfying condition (1) is maximal.

Andrade, Resende and Werneck
[
1
]
found a fast heuristic to find large independent sets by local improvements. As mentioned in their paper, it is also valid to find cliques, which is the relevant context for this paper. Transforming a clique by deleting
k-1
vertices and then adding
k
vertices to get a new larger clique is called a
k
–improvement
. If no
k
–improvement is possible, the clique is
k
–maximal
. To be 1-maximal is equivalent to ordinary maximal. It is easy to prove that:

Lemma 31
.

There is a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{1-imp}}
such that given any clique in a non-empty graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges one can find an 1-improvement (or prove that none exist) in
C_{\mathrm{1-imp}}(m+n)
time.

The heuristic by Andrade et al is fast in practice and they also proved that:

Lemma 32
.

There is a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{2-imp}}
such that given any 1-maximal clique in a non-empty graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges one can find an 2-improvement (or prove that none exist) in
C_{\mathrm{2-imp}}(m+n)
time.

Putting them together we may find a 2-maximal clique.

Lemma 33
.

There is a positive constant
C_{\mathrm{2-max}}
such that given any non-empty graph on
n
vertices and
m
edges with maximum clique size
\omega
one may find a 2-maximal clique in

C_{\mathrm{2-max}}\omega(m+n)
time.

Proof.

Start with an empty clique and use Lemma
31
and Lemma
32
until the clique is 2-maximal. The number of applications of them is in total at most
\omega,
as that is the maximum size of a clique in
G.
Set
C_{\mathrm{2-max}}=\max(C_{\mathrm{1-imp}},C_{\mathrm{2-imp}}).

∎

Lemma 34
.

If

-

{\bf H}
is a set of graphs,

-

the class of
{\bf H}
–free graphs is
\chi
-bounded by
f_{\bf H},
and

-

there exist a positive constant
C_{\bf H}
such that given any non-empty graph
G
on
n
vertices and
m
edges with maximum clique size
\omega
one may find a

f_{\bf H}(\omega)
–coloring of
G
in
C_{\bf H}\omega(m+n)
time,

then

-

the class of
K_{2}\cup{\bf H}
–free graphs is
\chi
-bounded by

f_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}(\omega)=\sum_{k=1}^{\omega}(\omega-k+1)f_{{\bf H}}(k),

-

and there exist a positive constant
C_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}
such that given any non-empty graph
G
on
n
vertices and
m
edges with maximum clique size
\omega
one may find a

f_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}(\omega)
–coloring of
G
in
C_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}\omega(m+n)
time.

Proof.

The statements on
\chi
–boundedness are all in Lemma
2
. The only new thing in Lemma
33
are the complexity theoretic statements. We follow the proof of Lemma
2
and work out the time used. This starts off with finding a 2-maximal clique instead of a maximum clique. It is found in time
C_{\mathrm{2-max}}\omega(m+n)
according to Lemma
33
.

Let
\tilde{\omega}
be the cardinality of the 2-maximal clique. The next step is to find the sets
C_{i,j}
for
1\leq i<j\leq\tilde{\omega}
and
D_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq\tilde{\omega}.
It is straightforward to do that in
C^{\prime}\omega(m+n)
time, for some constant
C^{\prime}.
Actually a minor adaptation of the algorithm to find the 2-maximal clique provides this information almost for free.

Each
D_{i}
is independent, and coloring all of them is linear in the number of vertices, say
C^{\prime\prime}n.

Now let
n_{i,j}
and
m_{i,j}
be the number of vertices and edges in each

respectively. Coloring all of them is done in

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq\tilde{\omega}}C_{\bf H}\omega(m_{i,j}+n_{i,j})\leq C_{\bf H}\omega(m+n)

time. In total at most
C_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}\omega(m+n)
time is used, where essentially one may set
C_{K_{2}\cup{\bf H}}=C_{\mathrm{2-max}}+C^{\prime}+C^{\prime\prime}+C_{\bf H}.

∎

A minor detail above is that if
\tilde{\omega}<\omega
then a better chromatic bound is actually achieved:

\sum_{k=1}^{\tilde{\omega}}(\omega-k+1)f_{{\bf H}}(k)

In the same way as Lemma
33
is the complexity version of Lemma
2
, the following Theorem
35
is the complexity version of Theorem
18
.

Theorem 35
.

For any integers
d\geq 0
and
n\geq 2(d+1)
there is a constant

such that every
{\bf B}_{n,d}
–free graph

vertices and

Proof.

The existence of the coloring is the content of Theorem

. To be proved is the complexity result. In the proof of Theorem

the initial part is that graphs of constant maximal degree are colored optimally. That can be done in
C^{\prime}(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})
time using a first-fit coloring. After that, a version of Lemma

with refined bookkeeping is applied
d

C^{\prime\prime}\omega(G)(m^{\prime}+n^{\prime})
for some
C^{\prime\prime}.
Set

∎

.

Lopsided HSS Iterative Method and Preconditioner for a class of Complex Symmetric Linear System This research was supported by National Key Research and Development Program of China, 2020YFA0709803; General program of Shanghai Natural Science Foundation, 23ZR1433900.
Yusong Zhang

Abstract
In this study, we propose the lopsided HSS (LHSS) iteration method for solving a class of complex symmetric indefinite systems of linear equations. This method employs an alternating iterative scheme, where each iteration entails solving two systems of equations with symmetric real coefficient matrices. This design is intended to reduce the high computational costs associated with complex arithmetic. Theoretical analysis shows that the upper bound of the convergence rate depends only on the maximum and minimum eigenvalues of the real symmetric matrices, as well as the iteration parameters. When the eigenvalues satisfy certain conditions, the method guarantees convergence for any positive iteration parameter. Building on this insight, we developed the preconditioned lopsided HSS iteration method (PLHSS). Theoretical results demonstrate that PLHSS exhibits superior convergence properties compared to the original method. Additionally, we derived the optimal parameters for the new approaches and corresponding optimal convergence rate. Furthermore, we derive the PLHSS preconditioner on the basis of the iterative method. The eigenvalues of the preconditioned matrix are well-clustered, and the eigenvectors are orthogonal with a specific inner product. Numerical experiments demonstrate the efficiency of the preconditioned GMRES and COCG methods. LHSS iteration methods and the relevant preconditioners show mesh size independent and parameter-insensitive convergence behavior for the test numerical examples. Keywords: Complex symmetric linear system, Hermitian and skew Hermitian splitting, preconditioner, surface acoustic waves equations.

Introduction

We consider the complex symmetric linear system of equations of the form

\displaystyle Ax=b,~

(1.1)

where the coefficient matrix
A=W+\mathrm{i}T\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
is a nonsingular complex matrix with

1.1

[

[

[

Over the past years, the solution of systems (
1.1

One of the most classical matrix splitting iterative methods, Hermitian/Skew-Hermitian Splitting (HSS) method
[
7
]
, stands out by explicitly exploiting the Hermitian/skew-Hermitian structure to achieve faster convergence. The modified HSS (MHSS) method
[
5
]
was proposed to reduce the computational complexity caused by complex arithmetic. It was proven to converge when the coefficient matrices the real part
W
and imaginary part
T
are symmetric positive definite and symmetric positive semidefinite, respectively. This idea inspired a series of variant methods, such as PMHSS
[
6
]
and LPMHSS
[
14
]
mthods, all of which have convergence guarantees under the condition of symmetric positive definite or semi-definite
W
and
T
. Several algorithms have also been designed to solve the case when
T
is indefinite, but they impose strict constraints on the indefinite nature of

. For example, the condition
-W\prec T\preceq W
is regarded as a necessary prerequisite in
[
21

12
]
and the implicit condition that
\varepsilon W+T
is a positive definite matrix for some
\varepsilon
in
[
23
]
. These conditions cannot be easily implemented in engineering applications, especially for problems with ill-conditioned matrices. However, the positive definiteness assumption, while sufficient for convergence, isn’t always required. For instance, study
[
11
]
has shown that PMHSS can achieve convergence even with positive a semidefinite
W
and an indefinite matrix T when applied to certain problems. These methods are also widely applied to solving a special class of block two-by-two real linear systems of equations
[
20
,
16
,
10
]

Matrix splitting iterative methods can provide high-quality preconditioners for ill-conditioned systems of linear algebraic equations, thereby enhancing the efficiency of Krylov subspace iteration methods. An effective preconditioner not only tightens the clustering of eigenvalues but also improves the condition number of the eigenvectors. The classical Krylov subspace methods for complex symmetric linear systems include the long-recurrence methods, such as generalized minimal residual (GMRES) method
[
17
]
and the short-recurrence methods, such as conjugate orthogonal conjugate gradient (COCG) method
[

[
18

This study aims to develop a type of lopsided HSS iterative methods for the complex symmetric, partially indefinite systems of the form (
1.1
). We establish the convergence results for the proposed LHSS iterative method and its preconditioned variants even
T
is symmetric indefinite.
The resulting methods overcome the fundamental limitation of existing approaches that require
T

The organization of the paper is as follows. In Section

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{split}\left(\alpha I+W\right)&x^{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}T\right)x^{\left(k\right)}+b\\

Denoted by
0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{n}:=\lambda_{\max}
and
\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{k}<0<\mu_{k+1}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{n}
the eigenvalues of the matrices
W
and

, respectively. Particularly,
\mu_{\min}
denotes the smallest eigenvalue of
T
in magnitude, i.e.,
\mu_{\min}=\min\{|\mu_{k}|,|\mu_{k+1}|\}
. We analyze the convergent behavior of the proposed LHSS method by the spectral radius of the iterative matrix

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A=W+\mathrm{i}T
, with symmetric positive definite matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and symmetric indefinite matrix
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
.
\lambda_{\max}
and
\mu_{\min}
are largest and smallest eigenvalues of
W
and
T
in magnitude, respectively. Then, the spectral radius of iterative matrix
G(\alpha)
in (
2.2
) satisfies
\rho\left(G\left(\alpha\right)\right)\leq\sigma\left(\alpha\right)
for any positive
\alpha
, where

\displaystyle\sigma\left(\alpha\right)=\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\alpha+\lambda_{\max}}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\mu_{\min}^{2}}}{\mu_{\min}}.

(2.3)

Furthermore, it holds that

(i) If
\lambda_{\max}\leq\mu_{\min}
, then
\sigma(\alpha)<1
for any
\alpha>0
, which means LHSS iteration scheme (
2.1
) is convergent unconditionally;

(ii) If
\lambda_{\max}>\mu_{\min}
, then
\sigma(\alpha)<1
if and only if

\displaystyle\alpha\in\mathcal{D}_{I}:=\left(0,\frac{2\lambda_{\max}\mu_{\min}^{2}}{\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\mu_{\min}^{2}}\right),

(2.4)

i.e., the iteration method (
2.1
) is convergent if condition (
2.4
) is true.

Proof.

Since
W
and
T
are symmetric, there exists orthogonal matrices
P
and
Q
, such that
P^{T}WP=\Lambda_{W},~Q^{T}TQ=\Lambda_{T}
, where

\displaystyle\Lambda_{W}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\cdots,\lambda_{n}\right),~\Lambda_{T}=\mathrm{diag}\left(\mu_{1},\mu_{2},\cdots,\mu_{n}\right).

It holds that

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(\alpha\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(\mathrm{i}T^{-1}W\left(\alpha I+W\right)^{-1}\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}T\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\rho\left(W\left(\alpha I+W\right)^{-1}\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}T\right)T^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\left\|W\left(\alpha I+W\right)^{-1}\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}T\right)T^{-1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|W\left(\alpha I+W\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}T\right)T^{-1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\Lambda_{W}\left(\alpha I+\Lambda_{W}\right)^{-1}\right\|_{2}\left\|\left(\alpha I-\mathrm{i}\Lambda_{T}\right)\Lambda_{T}^{-1}\right\|_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\alpha+\lambda_{j}}\cdot\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}+\mu_{j}^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}}}.

Furthermore, each component of this estimation formula can be expressed in the following form

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\alpha+\lambda_{j}}=\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\alpha+\lambda_{\max}},

and

\displaystyle\mathop{\max}\limits_{1\leq j\leq n}\sqrt{\frac{\alpha^{2}+\mu_{j}^{2}}{\mu_{j}^{2}}}=\frac{\sqrt{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}_{\min}}}{\mu_{\min}},

respectively. The upper bound of
\rho(G\left(\alpha\right))
given in (
2.3
) is obtained directly.

Therefore, upper bound
\sigma\left(\alpha\right)<1
is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha\left(\lambda_{\max}^{2}-\mu^{2}_{\min}\right)-2\mu^{2}_{\min}\lambda_{\max}<0.

(2.5)

If
\lambda_{\max}^{2}\leq\mu^{2}_{\min}
, then (
2.5
) holds true for any positive constant
\alpha>0
, i.e., the LHSS iteration (
2.1
) converges to the unique solution of the system (
1.1
); if
\lambda_{\max}^{2}>\mu^{2}_{\min}
, then the LHSS iteration (
2.1
) converges to the unique solution of the system (
1.1
) if and only if
\alpha
satisfies (
2.4
).
∎

Theorem
2.1
illustrates the range of parameter that enables the convergence of the LHSS iterative method (
2.1
). The following theorem provides a quasi-optimal parameter by minimizing the upper bound of the spectral radius.

Theorem 2.2
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.1
, the quasi optimal parameter
\alpha^{*}=\frac{\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}}
minimizes the upper bound
\sigma(\alpha)
(
2.3
), i.e.

\displaystyle\min\limits_{\alpha>0}\sigma(\alpha)=\sigma\left(\alpha^{*}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\sqrt{\lambda_{\max}^{2}+\mu_{\min}^{2}}}.

(2.6)

proof.

Notice that
\sigma(\alpha)>0
is continuous with respect to
\alpha
, and

\displaystyle\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\frac{\lambda_{\max}^{2}}{\mu^{2}_{\min}}\cdot\frac{\alpha^{2}+\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\left(\alpha+\lambda_{\max}\right)^{2}}.

Then we get

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}\sigma^{2}(\alpha)=\frac{2\alpha\lambda_{\max}-2\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\left(\alpha+\lambda_{\max}\right)^{3}}.

The square of upper bound function
\sigma^{2}(\alpha)
is monotonically decreasing on the interval
\left[0,\frac{\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}}\right]
and monotonically increasing on the interval
\left[\frac{\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}},+\infty\right)
. Hence,
\sigma(\alpha)
reaches its minimum value at
\alpha^{*}=\frac{\mu^{2}_{\min}}{\lambda_{\max}}
. Then

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(\alpha^{*}\right)\right)\leq\sigma\left(\alpha^{*}\right)=\frac{\lambda_{\max}}{\sqrt{\mu^{2}_{\min}+\lambda_{\max}^{2}}}<1.

The last inequality holds because of the non-singularity of matrix
T
, from which we obtain
\mu_{\min}\neq 0
.
∎

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\begin{split}\left(\alpha T+W\right)&x^{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}=\left(\alpha-\mathrm{i}\right)Tx^{\left(k\right)}+b\\
T&x^{\left(k+1\right)}=\mathrm{i}Wx^{\left(k+\frac{1}{2}\right)}-\mathrm{i}b.\end{split}\end{cases}

(3.12)

The iterative matrix is

\displaystyle G\left(T;\alpha\right)=\left(1+\mathrm{i}\alpha\right)T^{-1}W\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}T.

(3.13)

Denote the eigenvalues of matrix
T^{-1}W
by
\xi_{i}
,

\xi_{\max}^{-}=\mathrm{min}\{\xi_{i}<0\},\qquad\xi_{\max}^{+}=\mathrm{max}\{\xi_{i}>0\},\qquad i=1,2,\cdots,n

Then the eigenvalues of matrix
G\left(T;\alpha\right)
can be expressed as

\displaystyle\lambda\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=(1+\mathrm{i}\alpha)\left(\frac{\alpha}{\xi_{i}}+1\right)^{-1},~i=1,2,\cdots,n.

The convergence behavior is analyzed as follows.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
A=W+\mathrm{i}T
with sysmetric positive definite matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and symmetric indefinite matrix
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Then the spectral radius of iteration matrix (
3.13
) corresponding to PLHSS iteration method (
3.12
) could be written as

\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{\left|\xi_{i}\right|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{i}\right|}.

(3.14)

Suppose that
\xi_{\max}^{-}\in(-1,0)
, it holds that

(i) If
\xi_{\max}^{+}\leq 1
, then
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)<1
provided

\displaystyle\alpha\in\left(\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},+\infty\right);

(ii) If
\xi_{\max}^{+}>1
, then
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)<1
as long as
\xi_{\max}^{-}\xi_{\max}^{+}>-1
and

\displaystyle\alpha\in\left(\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},\frac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1}\right).

Proof.

To ensure

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\max\limits_{1\leq i\leq n}\frac{\left|\xi_{i}\right|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{i}\right|}<1,

it must hold that

(1+\alpha^{2})\xi_{i}^{2}<(\alpha+\xi_{i})^{2},\quad\mbox{for any}~\xi_{i},

which is equivalent to

\alpha(\xi_{i}^{2}-1)<2\xi_{i}.

(3.15)

For any
\xi_{i}\leq 1
, (
3.15
) holds true. For any
\xi_{i}>1
, (
3.15
) indicates that

\alpha<\dfrac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1}\leq\dfrac{2\xi_{i}}{(\xi_{i})^{2}-1}.

For any
-1<\xi_{i}<0
, it also holds that
1-\xi_{i}^{2}<0
. Inequality (
3.15
) indicates that

\alpha>\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}\geq\frac{-2\xi_{i}}{1-\xi_{i}^{2}}.

Therefore, we discuss the following cases:

(i)

When
\xi_{\max}^{+}\leq 1
,

\alpha>\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}};

(ii)

When
\xi_{\max}^{+}>1
,
\alpha
should satisfies

\alpha>\frac{2\xi_{i}}{\xi_{i}^{2}-1}\text{ for }-1<\xi_{i}<0,\quad\mbox{and }\quad\alpha<\frac{2\xi_{i}}{\xi_{i}^{2}-1}\text{ for }\xi_{i}>1.

It equivalent that

\alpha<\dfrac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\alpha>\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}.

The
\alpha
exists if
\xi_{\max}^{-}\xi_{\max}^{+}>-1
.

∎

Theorem 3.5
.

Denoted by
\Theta=\left(\xi_{\max}^{-}\right)^{-1}+\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}
. Based on the hypothesis in Theorem
3.4
, the optimal parameter

\displaystyle\alpha^{*}_{T}=\begin{cases}+\infty,&~\Theta\geq 0,\\
\mathrm{max}\{\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1},~-2\Theta^{-1}\},&~\Theta<0,\end{cases}

minimizes the spectral radius
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)
in (
3.14
), and the PLHSS iteration scheme (
3.12
) with
\alpha^{*}_{T}
converges to the solution of (
1.1
).

Proof.

We notice that

\displaystyle\alpha>\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},~-1<\xi_{\max}^{-}<0

is a necessary condition to promise the convergence of PLHSS iteration method with
V=T
based on Theorem
3.4
. In this situation, we have

\displaystyle-\alpha<\dfrac{2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}<\xi_{\max}^{-}\leq\xi_{i}\leq\xi_{\max}^{+},~i=1,2,\cdots,n,

which implies

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\xi_{i}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{i}\right|}\leq\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{-}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}\right|}\quad\mathrm{or}\quad\dfrac{|\xi_{i}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{i}\right|}\leq\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{+}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}\right|}:=\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right),~i=1,2,\cdots,n.

Therefore, the maximum value of
\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\dfrac{|\xi_{i}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{i}\right|}
is attained either at
\xi_{i}=\xi_{\max}^{-}
or at
\xi_{i}=\xi_{\max}^{+}
. Furthermore, the following inequality holds true:

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{-}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}\right|}>\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{+}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}\right|},~\alpha\leq-2\Theta^{-1},

\displaystyle\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{-}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}\right|}<\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{+}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}\right|},~\alpha>-2\Theta^{-1}.

Denote two functions on
(0,+\infty)
as

\displaystyle\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right):=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{-}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}\right|}=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\dfrac{-\xi_{\max}^{-}}{\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}},

\displaystyle\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right):=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\dfrac{|\xi_{\max}^{+}|}{\left|\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}\right|}=\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\dfrac{\xi_{\max}^{+}}{\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}}.

By calculating the derivative with respect to
\alpha
gives

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right)}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}=\frac{-\xi_{\max}^{-}\left(\alpha\xi_{\max}^{-}-1\right)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\left(\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{-}\right)^{2}},\quad\mbox{and}\quad\frac{\mathrm{d}\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right)}{\mathrm{d}\alpha}=\frac{\xi_{\max}^{+}\left(\alpha\xi_{\max}^{+}-1\right)}{\sqrt{1+\alpha^{2}}\left(\alpha+\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{2}},

which indicates
\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right)
is monotonically decreasing for
\alpha>0
, while the function
\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right)
is monotonically decreasing in the interval
\left(0,\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1}\right)
and monotonically increasing in the interval
\left(\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1},+\infty\right)
.

(i)

If
\Theta\geq 0
, then
\alpha>0>-2\Theta^{-1}
. Clearly this situation only occurs when
\xi_{\max}^{+}\leq 1
. The spectral radius could be written explicitly as

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right),~\alpha>\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},

which is monotonically decreasing within its domain. Therefore,
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)
reaches its minimum value at
\alpha^{*}_{T}=+\infty
.

(ii)

If
\Theta<0
, we analyze
\rho\big(G(T;\alpha)\big)
by considering two cases:
\xi_{\max}^{+}\leq 1
and
\xi_{\max}^{+}>1
. Theorem
3.4
implies distinct ranges of
\alpha
for each case.

(a)

If
\xi_{\max}^{+}\leq 1
, then
\alpha>\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}
. The spectral radius is

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=\begin{cases}\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right),~\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}<\alpha\leq-2\Theta^{-1},\\[10.0pt]
\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right),~\alpha>-2\Theta^{-1}.\end{cases}

In terms of the monotonicity of
\Xi_{1},\Xi_{2}
, we conclude
\alpha=-2\Theta^{-1}
minimizes
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)
on
\left(\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},-2\Theta^{-1}\right]
, while
\alpha=\mathrm{max}\{\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1},~-2\Theta^{-1}\}
minimizes it on
\left(-2\Theta^{-1},+\infty\right)
. Thus, the global minimizer over
\left(\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},+\infty\right)
is
\alpha_{T}^{*}=\mathrm{max}\{\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1},~-2\Theta^{-1}\}
.

(b)

If
\xi_{\max}^{+}>1
, then
\frac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}<\alpha<\frac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1}
. We have

\displaystyle\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)=\begin{cases}\Xi_{1}\left(\alpha\right),~\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}}<\alpha\leq-2\Theta^{-1},\\[10.0pt]
\Xi_{2}\left(\alpha\right),~-2\Theta^{-1}<\alpha<\dfrac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1}.\end{cases}

Analogously,
\alpha=\mathrm{max}\{\left(\xi_{\max}^{+}\right)^{-1},~-2\Theta^{-1}\}
minimizes
\rho\left(G\left(T;\alpha\right)\right)
in the interval
\left(\dfrac{-2\xi_{\max}^{-}}{1-(\xi_{\max}^{-})^{2}},\dfrac{2\xi_{\max}^{+}}{(\xi_{\max}^{+})^{2}-1}\right)
.

∎

4
PLHSS Preconditioners and Preconditioned Krylov Subspace Methods

The PLHSS iterative method (
3.2
) can be derived by matrix splitting

\displaystyle A=M\left(V;\alpha\right)-N\left(V;\alpha\right),

where

\displaystyle M\left(V;\alpha\right)=\mathrm{i}T+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}WV^{-1}T,

(4.1)

and

\displaystyle N\left(V;\alpha\right)=-W+\frac{\mathrm{i}}{\alpha}WV^{-1}T.

In addition, the splitting matrix
M\left(V;\alpha\right)
can serve as a preconditioner for solving the complex symmetric system (
1.1
), which is referred as the PLHSS preconditioner. According to the analysis in the last section,
W
and
T
can serve for
V
in
M\left(V;\alpha\right)
. Thereby, we obtain two preconditioners as follows

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{PLW}:=M(W;\alpha)=\mathrm{i}\frac{\alpha+1}{\alpha}T,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{PLT}:=M(T;\alpha)=\mathrm{i}(T+\frac{\mathrm{1}}{\alpha}W).

(4.3)

To gauge the performance of the above two preconditioners, we analyze the eigenvalues distribution and eigenvectors of the preconditioned matrices as follows.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
A=W+\mathrm{i}T
, with symmetric positive definite matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and symmetric indefinite matrix
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Let
\alpha
be a positive constant. Define
Z=W^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{2}}
. Denote by
\xi_{1},~\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}
the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix
Z\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and by
q_{1},~q_{2},\cdots,q_{n}
the corresponding orthogonal eigenvectors. Then the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{PLW}^{-1}A
are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{W,j}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\left(1-\mathrm{i}\xi_{j}\right),~j=1,2,\cdots,n,

and the corresponding eigenvectors are given by

\displaystyle x_{W,j}=W^{-\frac{1}{2}}q_{j},~j=1,2,\cdots,n.

Therefore, it holds that
\mathcal{P}_{PLW}^{-1}A=X_{W}\Lambda_{W}X_{W}^{-1}
, where
X_{W}=(x_{W,1},x_{W,2},\cdots,x_{W,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\Lambda_{W}=(\eta_{W,1},\eta_{W,2},\cdots,\eta_{W,n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, with
\kappa_{2}\left(X_{W}\right)=\sqrt{\kappa_{2}\left(W\right)}
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
A=W+\mathrm{i}T
, with symmetric positive definite matrix
W\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and symmetric indefinite matrix
T\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
. Let
\alpha
be a positive constant such that the matrix
\alpha T+W
is positive definite. Define
Z^{(\alpha)}=\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(T-\alpha W\right)\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Denote by
\tau_{1},~\tau_{2},\cdots,\tau_{n}
the eigenvalues of the symmetric matrix
Z^{(\alpha)}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
, and by
p_{1},~p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}
the corresponding orthogonal eigenvectors. Then the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{PLT}^{-1}A
are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{T,j}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\mathrm{i}}\left(1+\mathrm{i}\tau_{j}\right),~j=1,2,\cdots,n,

and the corresponding eigenvectors are given by

\displaystyle x_{T,j}=\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{j},~j=1,2,\cdots,n.

Therefore, it holds that
\mathcal{P}_{PLT}^{-1}A=X_{T}\Lambda_{T}X_{T}^{-1}
, where
X_{T}=(x_{T,1},x_{T,2},\cdots,x_{T,n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
and
\Lambda_{T}=(\eta_{T,1},\eta_{T,2},\cdots,\eta_{T,n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n\times n}
, with
\kappa_{2}\left(X_{T}\right)=\sqrt{\kappa_{2}\left(\alpha T+W\right)}
.

The proofs of the above two theorems can be found in the appendix.

Remark 4.1
.

Actually, we could also deduce the relationship between
\xi_{j}
and
\tau_{j}
through straightforward calculation. According to the definition of eigenvalues and eigenvectors,

\displaystyle\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}\left(T-\alpha W\right)\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{j}=\tau_{j}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{j}.

Then, with the notation
\tilde{p}_{j}=\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{j}

\displaystyle\left(1-\alpha\tau_{j}\right)T\tilde{p}_{j}=\left(\alpha+\tau_{j}\right)W\tilde{p}_{j},

which implies that
\frac{1-\alpha\tau_{j}}{\alpha+\tau_{j}}
is expressed as the eigenvalues of
T^{-1}W
.
T^{-1}W
has the same eigenvalues with the matrix
Z
denoted in Theorem 4.1, so we conclude that

\displaystyle\xi_{j}=\frac{1-\alpha\tau_{j}}{\alpha+\tau_{j}},

equally,

\displaystyle\tau_{j}=\frac{1-\alpha\xi_{j}}{\alpha+\xi_{j}}.

Therefore, the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{P}_{PLT}^{-1}A
could be written as

\displaystyle\eta_{T,j}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\xi_{j}}\left(1-\mathrm{i}\xi_{j}\right),~j=1,2,\cdots,n.

Remark 4.2
.

In fact,
\alpha\in\left(0,-\dfrac{\lambda_{\min}}{\mu_{1}}\right)
can reasonably serve as the parameter selection in Theorem
4.2
. Denote any eigenvalue of
\alpha T+W
as
\lambda
, We have that

\displaystyle 0=-\frac{\lambda_{\min}}{\mu_{1}}\mu_{1}+\lambda_{\min}\leq\alpha\mu_{1}+\lambda_{\min}\leq\lambda.

This indicates that
\alpha T+W
is positive definite, and thus
\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
exists.

Remark 4.3
.

[
8
]
It is well-known that the convergence rate of GMRES is intrinsically related to the eigenvector conditioning of the coefficient matrix. Denoted by
x_{0}
the initial guess,
r_{0}=b-Ax_{0}
is the initial residual. Let
\mathcal{P}_{k}
be the set of polynomials with degree at most k. For a diagonalizable matrix
A=X\Lambda X^{-1}
, with

\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{n})
, the k-th step iterative residual of the GMRES method satisfies

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert b-Ax^{(k)}\rVert_{2}}{\lVert r_{0}\rVert_{2}}\leq\kappa_{2}\left(X\right)\mathop{min}_{q\in\mathcal{P}_{k},q\left(0\right)=1}\mathop{max}_{1\leq i\leq n}|q(\lambda_{i})|,

where
\kappa_{2}(X)=\|X\|_{2}\|X^{-1}\|_{2}
is the condition number of the eigenvector matrix. Consequently, it is important to enhance the performance of Krylov subspace iteration method by improving eigenvector conditioning as well as clustering eigenvalues of preconditioned matrix.

While the PLHSS preconditioned GMRES method achieves convergence with the fewest iterations, the C-to-R preconditioned GMRES method wins in terms of speed, reaching convergence faster. The difference between their convergence rates is marginal. Unlike the nonsymmetric
\mathcal{P}_{CtoR}
in (
5.2
), the PLHSS preconditioners
\mathcal{P}_{PLW}
and
\mathcal{P}_{PLT}
are multiple of a symmetric positive definite matrix. Therefore, they can serve as the preconditioners for the COCG method, which is the three-term recurrence Krylov subspace method for solving the complex symmetric linear system of equations.

6
Conclusion

This study introduces a type of splitting iterative methods for solving the complex symmetric linear system of equations, along with its preconditioned Krylov subspace variants. Theoretical analysis guarantees that the methods is convergent when the imaginary part of the complex symmetric coefficient matrix is indefinite—a challenging scenario. Numerical tests reveal its efficiency over existed splitting-based iterative methods. The proposed matrix splitting method introduce the efficient preconditioners. These preconditioners, being scalar multiples of real symmetric positive matrices, integrate with short-recurrence Krylov solvers like COCG and COCR, making them ideal for large scale equation systems. The matrix splitting iteration methods can be also serve as the inner solver for complex symmetric indefinite saddle-point problems, demonstrating practical value in engineering applications such as modeling surface acoustic wave device equations.

Appendix A
Proof of Theorem 4.1

proof.

Define matrices

\displaystyle Q=\left(q_{1},q_{2},\cdots,q_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

and

\displaystyle D=\text{diag}\left(\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\cdots,\xi_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle Z=QDQ^{T}.

By straightforward computations we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{PLW}^{-1}A

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha+1\right)}T^{-1}\left(W+\mathrm{i}T\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha+1\right)\left(-\mathrm{i}\right)}\left(I-\mathrm{i}T^{-1}W\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\left(W^{-\frac{1}{2}}W^{\frac{1}{2}}-\mathrm{i}T^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{2}}W^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}W^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(I-\mathrm{i}W^{\frac{1}{2}}T^{-1}W^{\frac{1}{2}}\right)W^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=W^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q\left(\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\left(I-\mathrm{i}D\right)\right)Q^{T}W^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=X_{W}\Lambda_{W}X_{W}^{-1}.

Hence, the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{PLW}^{-1}A
are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{W,j}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+1}\left(1-\mathrm{i}\xi_{j}\right),~j=1,2,\cdots,n,

and the corresponding eigenvectors are given by
x_{W,j}=W^{-\frac{1}{2}}q_{j},~j=1,2,\cdots,n.

Besides, as
Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is orthogonal, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{W}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert W^{-\frac{1}{2}}Q\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert W^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{W}^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert Q^{T}W^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}\left(X_{W}\right)=\left\lVert X_{W}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{W}^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert W^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert W\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\kappa_{2}\left(W\right)}.

∎

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem 4.2

proof.

Define matrices

\displaystyle P=\left(p_{1},p_{2},\cdots,p_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}

and

\displaystyle D^{(\alpha)}=\text{diag}\left(\tau_{1},\tau_{2},\cdots,\tau_{n}\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle Z^{(\alpha)}=PD^{(\alpha)}P^{T}.

By straightforward computations we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{PLT}^{-1}A

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\mathrm{i}}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}\left(W+\mathrm{i}T\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\mathrm{i}\left(1-\mathrm{i}\alpha\right)}\left(I+\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}\left(T-\alpha W\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\mathrm{i}}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left[I+\mathrm{i}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\left(T-\alpha W\right)\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right]\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\left[\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\mathrm{i}}\left(I+\mathrm{i}D^{(\alpha)}\right)\right]P^{T}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle=X_{T}\Lambda_{T}X_{T}^{-1}.

Hence, the eigenvalues of the matrix
\mathcal{P}_{PLT}^{-1}A
are given by

\displaystyle\eta_{T,j}=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+\mathrm{i}}\left(1+\mathrm{i}\tau_{j}\right),~j=1,2,\cdots,n,

and the corresponding eigenvectors are given by
x_{T,j}=\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}p_{j},~j=1,2,\cdots,n.

Besides, as
P\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
is orthogonal, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{T}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}P\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}

and

\displaystyle\left\lVert X_{T}^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert P^{T}\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\alpha T+W\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}.

It then follows that

\displaystyle\kappa_{2}\left(X_{T}\right)=\left\lVert X_{T}\right\rVert_{2}\left\lVert X_{T}^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}=\left\lVert\left(\alpha T+W\right)^{-1}\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\left(\alpha T+W\right)\right\rVert_{2}^{\frac{1}{2}}=\sqrt{\kappa_{2}\left(\alpha T+W\right)}.

∎

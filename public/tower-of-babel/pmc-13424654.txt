Multi-scale temporal convolution attention network for state-of-charge estimation in Li-ion batteries
Authors: S Fouziya Sulthana, M Sivaramkrishnan, G Venkatesan, J Isaac JoshuaRamesh Lalvani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate estimation of the State of Charge (SOC) in lithium-ion batteries is critical for the safety, reliability, and efficiency of energy storage systems and electric vehicles (EVs). The traditional model-based SOC estimation approaches have their limitations when it comes to the issues of battery aging, dynamic load variations, and temperature fluctuations, while the existing deep learning methodologies mostly struggle to capture the short-term transients and long-term SOC evolution at the same time. To address these issues, the present study proposes a Multi-Scale Temporal Convolution Attention Network (MSTCAN), a fusion of multi-scale temporal convolutions for hierarchical feature extraction and an attention-based technique to highlight the most significant signals from voltage, current, and temperature measurements. The data set of Tesla Model-3 2170 Li-ion battery is prepared for processing by the utilization of data cleaning, min-max normalization, and cycle-based segmentation, resulting in enhanced feature quality and temporal representation improvements. An experiment was carried out where the different techniques were compared, and among them, MSTCAN achieving 1.25% Root Mean Square Error (RMSE), 0.97% Mean Absolute Error (MAE), 3.12% max error, and the coefficient of determination (R²) value of 0.998, thus surpassing the traditional and deep learning methods as well. The study’s findings affirm both the model’s robustness and its reliability, thus providing an attractive solution that could effectively upgrade the performance and even the efficiency of battery management system (BMS) in practical electric vehicle applications.Keywords: State of charge, Lithium-ion batteries, Battery management, Deep learning, MSTCANSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Lithium-ion (Li-ion) batteries are widely used in electric vehicles and renewable energy storage systems due to their high energy density, long cycle life, low self-discharge rate, and reliability1. Their versatility and superior performance have led to widespread adoption across applications such as electric vehicles, grid-level energy storage, and consumer electronics. This increasing deployment of Li-ion batteries has significantly raised the need for efficient battery monitoring and management strategies to ensure safe, reliable, and optimal operation under varying operating conditions2,3. A rapid increase in demand of almost 950 GWh by 2026 is shown in Fig. 1, which is an illustration of this market growth.

Global battery demand 2020–2026.

Despite their widespread use, lithium-ion batteries are highly sensitive to operating conditions such as overcharging, over-discharging, high current loads, and temperature variations, which can degrade performance and lead to safety issues such as thermal runaway. To ensure safe and reliable operation, a Battery Management System (BMS) is employed to monitor key parameters including voltage, current, and temperature, while performing essential functions such as charge balancing, thermal management, and fault protection4.

The arrangement of a BMS for Li-ion battery packs is illustrated in Fig. 2, where the continuous measurement of voltage, current, and temperature is done for each cell. These signals are analyzed to support important functions such as capacity estimation, SOC estimation, State of Health (SOH) assessment, and thermal management. The technique of cell balancing is applied to obtain even charge distribution among the cells and to extend the life of the battery. The controller takes these estimates as inputs and skillfully manages the relaxed charging and discharging cycles. Therefore, precise SOC estimation is one of the essential functions of BMS, which subsequently guarantees the reliable and efficient use of the battery in EV applications5,6.

The task of accurately estimating the SOC is one that is still extremely difficult despite its importance, mainly due to the fact that lithium-ion batteries in real life present characteristics that are highly non-linear, time-varying, and dependent on temperature. Traditional SOC estimation methods like the open-circuit voltage (OCV)-based techniques, Coulomb counting, and model-based approaches using equivalent circuit models or Kalman filters have a strong dependency on this formulation7. However, these methods typically face issues of integration errors that accumulate, dependence on initial SOC conditions, uncertainty in parameters, and low adaptability to the effects of battery aging and the changes in load profile. Their performance, thus, is limited in the case of complex electric vehicle operating situations that are characterized by varying currents, changing temperatures, and long-term decay8.

Over the last few years, the adoption of ML and DL data-driven methods has gone up to such a level that it already is an alternative to the conventional SOC estimation techniques. The DL models, in contrast to the physics-based and model-driven approaches, can extract directly from the battery’s raw data (such as temperature, voltage and current) the complex nonlinear relationships without entering the detailed electrochemical modeling process. State-of-the-art Recurrent neural networks (RNNs), long short-term memory (LSTM) networks, gated recurrent units (GRUs), convolutional neural networks (CNNs), and hybrid architectures are all different kinds of models that have been developed for SOC estimation, mainly because of their ability to capture temporal dependencies and nonlinear battery dynamics. These models demonstrate improved robustness against measurement noise, temperature variations, and battery aging effects, making them well-suited for real-world electric vehicle operating conditions9,10.

Even though the current deep learning–based SOC estimation methods show good performance, a lot of models depend on single-scale temporal feature extraction or recurrent structures, which might find it difficult to capture the short-term transient behavior and long-term SOC change under dynamic load conditions simultaneously. Additionally, the different battery signals and temporal features are usually given the same importance that leads to feature utilization being inefficient. This work proposes a MSTCAN for the accurate estimation of Li-ion battery SOC, motivated by the stated challenges. The model incorporates multi-scale temporal convolution to extract time features at different resolutions in a hierarchical manner and uses an attention mechanism to highlight the most informative features for SOC estimation dynamically.

Problem statement

Precise assessment of the SOC in Li-ion batteries remains a critical challenge for EVs and energy storage systems due to the highly nonlinear, time-varying, and temperature-dependent behavior of the batteries under real-world operating conditions. Traditional methods for estimating SOC, including open-circuit voltage (OCV) techniques, Coulomb counting, and model-based approaches, are plagued by accumulated errors, initial condition sensitivity, uncertainty in parameters, and limited adaptability to aging of battery and fluctuating load profiles. Nevertheless, deep learning–based methods have been able to capture complex battery dynamics over time; however, the current techniques are based on single-scale temporal feature extraction or recurrent architectures, which can result in the loss of both short-term transient behavior and long-term SOC evolution being captured at the same time. Moreover, these methods commonly give all battery signals and temporal features an equal treatment, which leads to the use of uninformative patterns through the less-than-optimal situation. Consequently, there is a demand for a strong, data-driven SOC estimation framework that is able to capture the multi-scale temporal dependencies and to selectively highlight the most relevant battery signals for the purpose of improving the estimation accuracy.

Novelty of the research

The key novelty of this work is the creation of a MSTCAN for SOC estimation. The proposed MSTCAN utilizes concurrent multi-scale temporal convolution branches that capture medium-term recovery phenomena, short-term transients, and long-term battery changes at the same time. An attention mechanism is added to the model in order to adaptively highlight the most significant features extracted from the voltage, current, and temperature signals. The proposed model is evaluated and trained on a large-scale real-world dataset that has been collected from Tesla Model-3 2170 lithium-ion cells under different load and temperature conditions. The results of the experiments show that MSTCAN can make very accurate and robust SOC estimations thus surpassing the performance of both the conventional and the existing deep learning-based methods. This way, the battery management systems can be more reliable and efficient in the case of electric vehicles.

Although recent works have explored multi-scale temporal convolution combined with attention mechanisms for SOC estimation, the proposed MSTCAN framework introduces significant improvements. Specifically, the model incorporates enhanced parallel multi-scale convolutional branches for more effective temporal feature extraction, a refined attention mechanism for dynamic feature prioritization, and extensive validation under varying temperature conditions and real-world drive cycles. These enhancements collectively contribute to improved estimation accuracy, robustness, and generalization compared to existing MSTCN-based approaches.

Summary of existing models.

In11, the researchers proposed a DL-based LSTM model that simulates the complex charging and discharge processes of Li-ion batteries and predicts the SOC accurately. The explicit time-dependent characteristics were revealed by employing a time series of battery data obtained under different working conditions. The training consisted of many cycles of charging and discharging, resulting in a estimation that was consistent throughout the epochs. The outcomes of the study indicated that LSTM networks had been successfully applied to SOC estimation in BMS. Nevertheless, the model focused mainly on the temporal features and thus neglect the electrochemical and spatial properties of the battery, which decreased its generalization ability when applied to changing conditions.

A hybrid ML model has been proposed in12 that uses a combination of Electrochemical Impedance Spectroscopy (EIS) with SHapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP)-based feature selection and an ASO-optimized LightGBM model for Li-ion battery SOC estimation. The study focused on large-capacity lithium iron phosphate batteries to avoid the disadvantages of traditional SOC estimation methods. The features of the frequency domain derived from EIS were used to identify the battery properties that are affected by SOC. The SHAP approach made the model easier to understand by indicating the primary features, while ASO boosted LightGBM’s prediction ability. In this case of BMS, however, the dependence on EIS measurements limits the use in real-time applications.

Authors in13 suggested a TLBO-DNN model which is an Integrated Deep Neural Network (DNN) based on Teaching-Learning Based Optimization (TLBO) hybrid model for estimating the SOC of Li-ion batteries in EVs. To address the non-linear battery behavior, the TLBO algorithm was applied to optimize the weights and biases of the DNN. Utilizing a comprehensive dataset of more than one million samples, the model was not only able to surpass the PSO-DNN, ARIMA, and SVM models but also achieved better MAE and RMSE scores. The experimentation results indicated that the BMW EV battery data had the least convergence and the highest estimation accuracy. Conversely, the method demonstrated error spikes under extreme conditions, which indicates its limited generalization ability.

The study presented in14 utilized machine learning for SOH prediction of Li-ion batteries for EVs and among the models utilized were supervised ones such as SVR, DT, KNN, and RF. The research work made use of the Sandia National Laboratories battery data and model performances were assessed by RMSE, MAE, MAPE, and MSE metrics. KNN and decision tree models proved to be the most accurate as they managed to capture battery nonlinearity to a great extent. Voltage, current, and temperature were the only measurable parameters needed for the method making it completely unplugged from the electrochemical modeling. However, it should be noted that the model performance was greatly influenced by data set characteristics and preprocessing quality.

In15, a comparative analysis of the classical ML and DL approaches to predict the Li-ion battery performance in EVs was presented. The evaluation was conducted on models such as SVM, DT, GPR, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM for estimating battery statuses like SoH, SoC, and SoP. The results showed that Bi-LSTM excelled traditional models in terms of prediction accuracy. The results were obtained by applying the metrics of MSE, MAE, RMSE, and R², and revealed that GPR was also one of the strong competitors in SoH estimating. However, the study suggested that it was predominantly the domain of hybrid and ensemble models, thereby emphasizing the need for further improvements by sophisticated AI methods and large datasets.

A hybrid DNN that integrated LSTM and GRU models was suggested in16 for the simultaneous SOC and SOH estimation of Li-ion batteries. The fusion of models not only defined the performance of the batteries but also showed to be effective in capturing the short-term fluctuations. The strength of the model was demonstrated at varying temperatures without depending on open-circuit voltage (OCV) and SOC lookup tables. In addition, the model required minimum data for training than conventional deep learning models. However, SOC and SOH were estimated separately, which opens the way for integrated estimation and further optimization.

In the paper17, the modular and scalable ML framework for state estimation of Li-ion battery and prediction that mainly focused on SOC and Remaining Useful Life (RUL). The proposed framework was composed of different models, such as LSTM, CNN, FFNN, AutoML, and classical regressors, along with standardized data preprocessing pipelines. It was effectively handled with heterogeneous datasets, various battery chemistries, and varying operating conditions. The authors of the paper reported that the method provided better electrochemical and thermal analyses of the battery, and also highlighted increased reproducibility and transparency in battery analytics. However, the method was mainly data-driven and did not thoroughly consider the alternative approach of physics-based battery modeling.

In18, a cloud-based DL framework for co-estimating SOC and SOH of lithium-ion batteries was presented. Long-range dependencies in space and time across pack, cell, and system levels were captured by transformer neural networks. The model with no prior knowledge of the cell chemistry or degradation automatically performed feature extraction through attention mechanisms. Field data collected via IoT improved the predictive accuracy in the case of real-life EV applications. The study was based on limited field datasets, which may have reduced the generalizability.

A hybrid CNN-Bi-LSTM model was proposed in19 for the accurate and efficient estimation of Li-ion batteries’ SOC in EVs. The architecture combined the best of CNNs for spatial feature extraction with the strength of the Bi-LSTM for temporal modeling, with the enhanced performance of an evolutionary intelligence-assisted Group Learning Algorithm (GLA) for hyperparameter tuning. The method was able to capture spatial and temporal battery data very well, thus outperforming traditional models that use only one type of data. The model was tested on six different EV driving cycles and achieved low error rates, thus showing fast convergence and high accuracy in estimation.

The various ML regression algorithms were compared in20 for their potential in real-time Li-ion battery SOC estimation in EVs. The comparison included Neural Network, Support Vector Machine (SVM), Gaussian Process Regression (GPR) and Ensemble Methods, all of which received input from diverse driving conditions’ data. Additionally, the battery voltage, current, temperature, and environmental factors was included as input variables to reveal the complex nonlinear relationship with SOC. GPR was the algorithm that exhibited the highest accuracy and robustness, successfully emulating the actual battery dynamics under different conditions. However, the study’s findings were constrained by high computational requirements and the necessity for a large-scale real-time data collection.

The paper research21 presents the development of a hybrid ML framework for the purpose of anomaly detection and predicting maintenance of Li-ion batteries. The hybrid model that used an Improved RF algorithm brought together physics-informed and data-driven methods. As a result, the precise SOC and battery health were determined while conducting extremely robust real-time anomaly detection at the same time. The method allowed the maintenance strategies to be based on prediction rather than reaction, thus leading to improved battery reliability and lifespan. However, the complex computations affected the study’s results and the scalability of the application was thus limited to industrial settings of small scale only.

In22, the authors presented a novel and highly precise method to estimate the SOC of li-ion batteries by integrating the IGA-GRU (immune genetic algorithm and gated recurrent unit) model with a multi-head attention mechanism. The immune genetic algorithm was applied to optimize the hyperparameters of the GRU, which led to a more robust model and also facilitated to avoid the issue of getting stuck in local optimal solutions. To a great extent, the sliding window preprocessing contributed to the diversity of the data and consequently led to the stability of the estimations that were made for the different operating conditions. Nevertheless, the model suffered from the drawbacks of high computational complexity, large data requirements, and being highly dependent on hardware, which altogether limited its deployment in resource-constrained systems.

In23, a technique for precise SOC estimation of li-ion batteries was introduced which integrates regression machine learning and wavelet denoising. An important phase of data preprocessing was the application of a wavelet transform which reduced the measurement noise and made the features more stable before the model training. The regression model of polynomial and ensemble was evaluated on multiple datasets from NASA, which resulted in the enhancement of estimation reliability and robustness. The experiments showed that using the wavelet-denoised inputs for the SOC estimation significantly outperformed the use of the raw data. Nevertheless, the method was limited by being validated on just one dataset with restricted operating conditions, which in turn, affected the overall applicability of the results.

In24 the implementation of a hybrid multi-layer deep neural network (HMDNN) for the precise SOC estimation in electric vehicles was presented. The model’s training was performed with the Mountain Gazelle Optimizer (MGO) which accelerated the convergence and enhanced the accuracy of the estimation. To prove the reliability of the method, real EV charging data were taken from various drive cycles and different temperature conditions. The MGO-DNN method was found to be superior to conventional optimization-based DNN models in the aspect of capturing the nonlinear relationship among battery current, voltage, and SOC. The study was limited by its focus on specific battery chemistries and predefined driving cycles, restricting broader generalization.

The study conducted in the paper25 has analyzed the BMS based on artificial intelligence techniques, particularly concentrating on the estimation of the lithium-ion batteries’ SOC and SOH. Various ML models, such as linear regression, gradient boosting, random forest, LightGBM, XGBoost, and SVM, were assessed through experiments. Among all these, the random forest regression gave the best result in forecasting the discharge behavior of the batteries. The research concluded that the application of AI methods in battery monitoring and electric vehicle performance would be a major progress forward. However, the approach had to deal the consequences of being both hard to scale and highly demanding in terms of computational power when very large datasets were used.

A feedforward DNN for predicting the SOC of lithium-ion batteries in EVs was proposed in26. The study employed correlation analysis and virtual functions to enhance estimation accuracy and develop an inverse multi-output neural network model. The proposed model enabled simultaneous estimation of SOC, voltage, current, speed, and mileage, supporting design and operational simulations. The multi-output inverse model outperformed conventional single-output deep neural networks in estimation capability. The approach was limited to lithium-ion batteries and could not be directly generalized to other battery chemistries.

Recent advancements in SOC estimation have increasingly focused on leveraging deep learning techniques to capture the nonlinear and dynamic behavior of lithium-ion batteries. A comprehensive review presented in recent literature highlights the growing adoption of deep learning-based SOC estimation methods, emphasizing their ability to model complex battery dynamics while also pointing out challenges such as generalization across operating conditions and real-time deployment constraints27.

Furthermore, multi-task learning approaches such as Cross-Stitch Networks have been proposed for the joint estimation of State of Charge (SOC) and State of Health (SOH), enabling effective feature sharing between related battery states and improving overall estimation accuracy28. In addition, continual learning-based frameworks have been introduced to address the issue of model adaptability across diverse battery chemistries and operating environments, allowing SOC estimation models to incrementally learn from new data without catastrophic forgetting29.

Notably, a recent multi-scale data-driven framework based on temporal convolution and attention mechanisms (MSTCN + Attention) has been proposed for online SOC estimation30. This approach demonstrates the effectiveness of combining multi-scale temporal feature extraction with attention mechanisms to improve estimation accuracy. However, the proposed MSTCAN model in this work differs in several key aspects, including enhanced multi-scale feature representation, improved attention-based feature refinement, and comprehensive evaluation across diverse temperature conditions and drive cycles, leading to superior robustness and estimation performance.

Proposed work

The current research introduces an innovative deep learning model referred to as the MSTCAN which is capable of predicting and estimating the state-of-charge of lithium-ion batteries. The SOC estimation procedure that is proposed in this work begins with the data collection from the cylindrical Li-ion battery cells of a Tesla Model-3 with NCA chemistry, which is the most widely used chemistry in EV applications. The experimental data is collected in a laboratory under strict conditions, with various temperature and load scenarios used to simulate realistic operating environments. The dataset that has been collected is composed of time-series data that include voltage, current, temperature, and reference SOC values collected during charging, discharging, and dynamic drive cycles. This dataset, which is rich and diverse, forms the groundwork for the development of a robust SOC estimation model that is trustworthy. The workflow of the proposed research model for battery SOC estimation using the research model is depicted in Fig. 3. The proposed MSTCAN model performs offline SOC estimation, where the model is trained and evaluated using pre-collected battery datasets. Real-time (online) SOC estimation can be considered as a potential extension of this work.

Workflow of the research model.

The collected dataset is preprocessed using a structured pipeline involving data cleaning, Min–Max normalization, and cycle-based segmentation, as described in Sect. 3.2. The preprocessed data ensures improved data quality and consistency for effective model training.

The preprocessed data is then used for SOC estimation using the proposed MSTCAN model. The model takes voltage, current, and temperature signals as input and extracts temporal features using parallel multi-scale temporal convolutional branches. These branches capture both short-term dynamics and long-term SOC trends. The extracted features are further refined using an attention mechanism that emphasizes the most informative features. Finally, fully connected layers are used to generate accurate SOC estimations.

The dataset is divided into training (70%), validation (10%), and testing (20%) subsets to ensure unbiased model evaluation. The model is evaluated using RMSE, MAE, R², and maximum error to assess estimation accuracy and reliability. The results demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed MSTCAN model for practical SOC estimation in battery management systems. The input sequence (look-back window) length is set to T = 100 time steps, enabling the model to capture both short-term fluctuations and long-term temporal dependencies in battery signals. The model performs one-step-ahead SOC estimation, where the SOC at the next time instant is predicted based on the input sequence.

Data preprocessing

To achieve accurate SOC estimation, it is necessary to use effective data preprocessing as a crucial step since unprocessed battery data usually contain noise, missing values, and scale variations. In this work, a structured preprocessing pipeline for data cleaning, Min-Max normalization, and cycle-based segmentation is utilized to enhance both data quality and learning efficiency.

Data cleaning

The raw battery data set consists of time-series data of voltage, current, temperature, and SOC that were taken during various operating conditions. The first step of the procedure is the investigation of the data set to find and remove the invalid samples which are made up of missing values, duplication of records, and inconsistent measurements. One of the main methods in noise reduction from the sensors is the application of a technique known as signal smoothing, which reduces high-frequency variations to a minimum while at the same time preserving important electrochemical features. In addition, the extreme values that are produced by either abrupt sensor malfunctions or incorrect data collection are detected and removed. This data cleaning process ensures that the input data for SOC estimation that is reliable and consistent.

Data normalization using min–max scaling

Initially, feature normalization is done after the data cleaning process. The goal of normalization is to bring input features to the same range and thus, make the model training more stable and, more efficient. Min-Max normalization is utilized in the preprocessing stage to achieve the same feature scaling and to provide the forecasting model of SOC with more accuracy. This normalization technique converts original data points into a predetermined range of [0,1], which not only results in improved numerical stability but also minimize the time for convergence during the model training phase. Since all input features have the same scale, Min-Max normalization prevents any single feature to dominate the training process and affect it negatively due to its large scale.

The selection of Min-Max normalization is based on the premise that it is simple to calculate and highly appropriate for handling features with different physical units like current, voltage, and temperature. The normalization technique, by confining all the features to a bounded interval, not only permits uniform parameter updates but also increases training efficiency in general. This is especially applicable for deep learning-based SOC estimation models where stable gradients are being seriously needed for accurate learning.

Cycle-based segmentation

The preprocessed battery time-series data is further segmented using a cycle-based approach to preserve the electrochemical characteristics of battery operation. In this method, continuous measurement signals are divided into distinct operating cycles based on the temporal behavior of the current signal. Each segmented cycle represents a complete battery operating event and is treated as an independent time-series sample for model training.

Cycle segmentation is performed using a rule-based strategy derived from the magnitude and direction of the battery current. Specifically, charging periods are identified when the current I(t) exceed a positive threshold Ith while discharging (driving) periods are identified when the current falls below a negative threshold -Ith. Rest periods are detected when the absolute current |I(t)| remains below the threshold Ith for a continuous duration Δt. A complete cycle is defined as a sequence of charging, discharging, and rest phases. The threshold Ith is selected based on sensor noise characteristics to avoid misclassification caused by minor fluctuations in the signal.

Unlike conventional sliding-window methods, cycle-based segmentation preserves the complete temporal evolution of battery states within each cycle. This enables the SOC estimation model to effectively capture meaningful patterns related to charge transfer, energy conversion, and voltage dynamics. Furthermore, this approach improves model generalization across varying load conditions, temperature variations, and dynamic driving scenarios.

Overall, cycle-based segmentation provides a physically meaningful and structured representation of battery behavior, thereby enhancing the accuracy and robustness of SOC estimation in practical battery management systems33.

MSTCAN for SOC estimation

The accurate SOC estimation of li-ion batteries is dependent on the proper modeling of the non-linear and time-varying correlations that exist between the different parameters like voltage, temperature and current signals during their charging and discharging cycles. The conventional machine learning and single-scale deep learning approaches are ineffective at capturing the transient behaviors of short duration and the long-term temporal patterns at the same time. To overcome this drawback, the MSTCAN is introduced, which will be capable of giving robust and precise SOC estimation.

The MSTCAN architecture suggested in this paper merges the multiple-scale temporal convolution, the attention mechanism, and the regression layers into a single framework, which is depicted in Fig. 5. The model aims to unveil unique temporal features across various time scales and at the same time, praise the most useful features for SOC estimation through a dynamic process. The total structure is made of four principal stages: input representation, multi-scale temporal feature extraction, attention-based feature refinement, and SOC regression.

Multi-scale temporal convolution (MSTC) module

The behavior of batteries is seen over different time frames because of the fast current changes, the slow post-voltage relaxation, and the gradual chemical aging of the battery. It is inadequate to use just one temporal convolution kernel to identify such different patterns. The MSTC module, therefore, uses several parallel one-dimensional convolutional branches, each with a different kernel size, which allows for the different temporal scales to be feature-extracted at the same time. From the perspective of signal processing, then each of the convolutional branches present in the MSTC module can be viewed as a temporal filter which is capable of learning. This filter will be operating at a particular frequency band. To be precise, the smaller kernel sizes will be capturing primarily high-frequency components which correspond to sudden current changes, while the larger ones will be capturing low-frequency ones, therefore, indicating the gradual SOC drift and thermal effects. The parallel convolution framework makes it possible to learn the complementary temporal features in a synchronous manner and without the need for feature extraction to be done manually.

Also, by means of batch normalization, the numerical stability of the learned temporal filters is preserved even under different operating conditions and similarly, the parallelization is a factor that lessens the impact of noise and uncertainty related to the sensors. This theoretical attribute is of great importance in practical BMS, as in these systems measurements are frequently affected by disturbances and quantization errors34.

Temporal feature aggregation module

After the completion of multi-scale feature extraction, a temporal feature aggregation module is used to consolidate and enhance the representations that have been extracted. The temporal feature aggregation module functions as both a feature compaction and a regularization mechanism. The module integrates convolution, nonlinear activation, pooling, and dropout to impose smoothness in the temporal domain while still allowing for the discriminative SOC-related patterns to be recognized. From the viewpoint of information theory, the pooling operation reduces on the redundant temporal information and thus the signal-to-noise ratio of the learned representation is improved. Moreover, dropout imposes stochastic regularization which lets the model to be more robust to unseen drive cycles and temperature conditions through the enhancement of feature diversity and reduction of feature co-adaptation.

Experimental analysis

Experimental setup

The proposed SOC estimation model was subjected to experimental evaluation performed in the PyTorch 2.0 DL framework and Python 3.8. Experiments were performed on a computer system with 16 GB RAM, an Intel® Core™ i7 processor with a clock speed of 3.60 GHz, and an NVIDIA RTX 3060 GPU that improved the computational efficiency of deep learning training and inference significantly. The Adam optimizer with an initial learning rate of 0.001 was used to ensure the convergence was stable and efficient. The training process was conducted for 100 epochs, allowing the Multi-Scale Temporal Convolution–Attention Network to effectively learn the temporal dependencies and nonlinear characteristics inherent in lithium-ion battery data. The proposed framework was trained to accurately estimate the SOC under varying operating conditions and drive cycles.

Evaluation metrics

The suggested model’s performance is assessed using RMSE, MAE, R2, and Max Error to assess estimation accuracy and robustness for SOC estimation.

RMSE calculates the square root of the mean squared differences between the actual and predicted SOC values. It gives great emphasis to larger errors, making it especially sensitive to substantial deviations.

Maximum Error represents the largest absolute deviation between the predicted and true SOC values across all samples. This metric is crucial for evaluating the most extreme estimation scenario, which is important for safety-critical BMS.

MAE computes the average of the absolute differences between the predicted and actual SOC values. It provides a straightforward interpretation of the average estimation error without emphasizing outliers.

The coefficient of determination (R²) is a statistic that quantifies the degree to which the actual SOC values correlate with the predicted values from the Multi-Scale Temporal Convolution–Attention Network proposed in this study. It expresses the extent to which the estimation model fills the gap of the variance in the estimated SOC. An R² value that is nearer to 1 suggests not only great estimation accuracy but also great model dependability.

Conclusion

The research introduced a MSTCAN as a solution for the accurate SOC estimation of Li-ion batteries via non-linear, time-dependent, and temperature-dependent model behaviors. The suggested approach is designed to consider both short-term and long-term effects of SOC using multi-scale temporal convolutions, plus an attention mechanism filters out the most informative features of voltage, current, and temperature signals. The dataset of Li-ion battery Tesla Model-3 2170 underwent preprocessing consisting of data cleansing, min-max normalization, and sliding window segmentation, thus obtaining a high-quality temporal representation for training. Experimental results demonstrate that MSTCAN achieves an RMSE of 1.25%, MAE of 0.97%, maximum error of 3.12%, and R² of 0.998, significantly outperforming conventional and existing deep learning-based models, confirming its superior estimation capability. The model is also compared with existing SOC estimation approaches, highlighting its enhanced accuracy and robustness across diverse operating conditions. While the framework is primarily designed for single-cell lithium-ion applications, it can also support multi-cell configurations and other Li-ion chemistries in future. In practical battery management systems, uncertainty quantification plays a crucial role in assessing the reliability of SOC estimates under varying operating conditions. Although the proposed MSTCAN model focuses on accurate point estimation, future work can incorporate uncertainty-aware techniques such as quantile regression, confidence-aware learning, or Bayesian deep learning approaches to provide prediction intervals along with SOC estimates. This would further enhance the reliability and safety of the model in real-world applications. Moreover, the implementation of real-time online learning can be a significant factor for the framework’s flexibility in coping with battery aging and changing environmental conditions. In addition to SOC estimation, SOH estimation can also be included, consequently, a smart and comprehensive BMS will be created that will not only ensure the safety but also maximize the efficiency of electric vehicles.

Integrated 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI models improve prostate cancer diagnosis and ISUP stratification, especially in the low-PSA cohort
Authors: Zhibo Dai, Yuhu Lv, Weiwei Ruan, Yongkang Gai, Xiaoli Lan, Chunxia Qin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTo evaluate the diagnostic efficacy of 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI-derived parameters in prostate cancer and their significance in risk stratification.MethodsA retrospective study was performed on patients who underwent ⁶⁸Ga-PSMA PET/MRI from November 2020 to April 2024, all with histopathological confirmation obtained from biopsy or prostatectomy. Parameters analyzed included SUVmax, PSMA tumor volume at a 40% SUVmax threshold (PTV40%), mean uptake in this volume (SUV40%mean), and uptake ratios to the liver (PLR), blood pool (PBpR), and parotid gland (PPgR). MRI was evaluated using PI-RADS v2.1 (scores ≥ 4 were defined as positive), and ADCmean and ADCmin were recorded. Diagnostic performance of PET and MRI parameters was compared using ROC analysis. Two logistic regression model sets were constructed: one for improved diagnosis performance in the low-PSA subgroup (PSA < 20 ng/mL) and one for International Society of Urological Pathology (ISUP) Grade Group (GG) stratification (GG 1–2 vs. GG 3–5). Model performance was assessed via LOOCV, 1,000 bootstrap resamples, and decision curve analysis.ResultsOf the 74 patients (mean age 65.9 ± 7.4 years), 52 had prostate cancer and 22 had benign lesions. PET parameters (SUVmax, PLR, PBpR, PPgR) and MRI-derived ADC values differed significantly between groups. An SUVmax cut-off of 7.665 yielded 76.9% sensitivity, 100% specificity (AUC = 0.920), while a PLR cut-off of 1.551 showed higher sensitivity (92.3%) and specificity (86.4%) (AUC = 0.907). In patients with PSA < 20 ng/mL, PLR showed higher sensitivity (84.6%) than SUVmax (53.8%). For the low-PSA subgroup, the Clinical+ADCmean+SUVmax model yielded a LOOCV AUC of 0.795, as opposed to 0.449 for the clinical-only model and 0.733 for the clinical+ADCmean model. For ISUP stratification, the ADCmean+SUVmax model reached a LOOCV AUC of 0.906. DCA validated the clinical utility of the optimal models.Conclusions68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI-derived parameters exhibit excellent diagnostic performance for prostate cancer. PLR demonstrates the highest sensitivity, especially in patients with low PSA. Combining PET and MR parameters—especially PET-guided ADCmean with SUVmax and PLR—improves diagnosis, potentially enables non-invasive ISUP Grade Group stratification, and may facilitate personalized management.Trial RegistrationNCT03756077. Background

Prostate cancer is the second most common cancer among men globally and the fifth leading cause of cancer-related mortality [1]. In China, the incidence of prostate cancer has been rising annually. Accompanied by a relatively poor prognosis, the five-year survival rate remains below the global average and is particularly low compared with that in developed countries such as those in Europe and the United States [2]. This discrepancy is mainly attributed to insufficient public awareness of prostate cancer screening and inadequate early diagnosis in China [3]. Prostate cancer risk stratification is critical for tailoring personalized therapy, optimizing resource allocation, and improving survival outcomes by distinguishing indolent tumors suitable for active surveillance from aggressive malignancies that require prompt intervention [4]. Therefore, there is an urgent need for more effective diagnostic and assessment methods.

Prostate-Specific Membrane Antigen (PSMA) is a transmembrane glycoprotein that is highly expressed on the surface of prostate cancer cells, whereas its expression is low in normal prostate tissues and other benign conditions [5]. As an emerging molecular imaging modality, PSMA PET/MRI enables specific detection of prostate cancer cells and exhibits high sensitivity and specificity in the diagnosis, staging, and therapeutic evaluation of prostate cancer [6]. Nevertheless, the role of various quantitative and semi-quantitative parameters derived from PSMA PET/MRI in the management of prostate cancer remains to be further elucidated. This study aims to evaluate the diagnostic performance and risk stratification value of PSMA PET/MRI-derived parameters for prostate cancer.

Methods

Patient characterization

This is a retrospective analysis of patient data from a study registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03756077) and approved by the institutional ethics committee (Approval No. 2018-S325). Inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients suspected of or diagnosed with prostate cancer based on clinical and other auxiliary examinations; (2) Patients who underwent whole-body 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI at our hospital from November 2020 to April 2024; (3) Interval between biopsy or radical prostatectomy and PET/MRI examination was within one month. Exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) Patients with other tumors at initial diagnosis; (2) Patients who had received any prostate cancer treatment prior to PET/MRI; (3) Patients with missing baseline serum PSA values. All patients were stratified according to pathological benignancy/malignancy status and ISUP grading system.

Radio-labeling of 68Ga-PSMA-617

68Ga was obtained by eluting a 68Ge/68Ga generator (ITG GmBH, Munich, Germany) with 0.05 N hydrochloric acid. Subsequently, 68GaCl3 was mixed with 20 µg (20 nmol) of PSMA precursor (Jiangsu Huayi Technology Co., Ltd., Changshu, China) in 1 ml of 0.25 M sodium acetate buffer and heated at 105 °C for 5 min. The product was purified using a C18 cartridge, followed by filtration through a 0.22 μm filter, with a radiochemical purity of > 95%.

Image acquisition and reconstruction

All patients were intravenously administered with 1.85-3.70MBq/Kg of 68Ga-PSMA-617, and whole-body PET/MRI (3.0T, SIGNA TOF-PET/MRI, GE Healthcare, WI, USA) scan was initiated 30–60 min later. After acquiring localization images, MR attenuation correction maps were acquired using Zero echo time (ZTE) sequences. PET data were acquired in three-dimensional mode with 5–6 bed positions and 3 min per bed position. Simultaneous MR and PET acquisitions were performed, including the high-resolution axial liver fast volume acquisition (LAVA)-Flex sequence, T2 weighted imaging (T2WI), and coronal T2 weighted fast recovery fast spin echo (FRFSE) sequence. Subsequently, dedicated pelvic PET/MRI was obtained, with MR sequences covering axial, coronal, and sagittal T2WI, diffusion weighted imaging (DWI; b values: 0/1000/1400 s/mm2). PET acquisition for the pelvic region was set at 10 min per bed position. All PET data were reconstructed using time-of-flight (TOF) information-assisted ordered-subset expectation maximization (OSEM) with 2 iterations and 28 subsets.

Image analysis

Image analysis was performed by readers blinded to the final histopathological results. All images were reviewed by two experienced nuclear medicine physicians and analyzed using a GE AW4.6 workstation.

On PET images, a three-dimensional (3D) volume of interest (VOI) was manually delineated over the prostate, with reference to MR images to avoid including uptake from adjacent organs such as the bladder and rectum, and the SUVmax was measured. The PSMA-positive tumor volume (PTV40%) and the mean uptake within this volume (SUV40%mean) were automatically segmented using a threshold of 40% SUVmax. Additionally, spherical 3D VOIs with diameters of 3 cm and 1 cm were placed in the right hepatic lobe and left parotid gland, respectively, to derive the SUVmean of the liver (SUVLiver) and parotid gland (SUVpg). Along the vessel wall near the origin of the ascending aorta, an ROI was drawn on three consecutive slices to obtain the SUVmean of the mediastinal blood pool (SUVbp). Ratios of prostate SUVmax to the SUVmean of the liver, parotid gland, and mediastinal blood pool were calculated as the prostate-to-liver ratio (PLR), prostate-to-parotid ratio (PPgR), and prostate-to-blood pool ratio (PBpR), respectively. The product of PTV40% and SUV40%mean was recorded as total PSMA uptake in the prostate region (Prostate-total-Uptake, PTU).

For the MR images, PI-RADS scores were assigned according to PI-RADS v2.1 criteria, with scores ≥ 4 regarded as positive. In addition, quantitative parameters ADCmean and ADCmin were obtained slice by slice within the PTV40% VOIs on ADC maps.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analysis was performed using MedCalc software. Normality of the data was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Quantitative data with a normal distribution were presented as the mean ± standard deviation (x̄±s), whereas non-normally distributed data were presented as the median (interquartile range) [M(Q1, Q3)]. Between-group comparisons were performed using the Mann-Whitney U test, Student’s t-test, and chi-square test, as appropriate. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated for each parameter according to the final pathological results. The optimal cutoff value was determined using the maximum Youden index, and the corresponding specificity and sensitivity were calculated accordingly. The McNemar test and DeLong tests were employed to compare diagnostic performance. Spearman’s correlation analysis was performed to evaluate the correlation between each parameter and the ISUP Grade Group.

Multiparametric model construction and validation

To investigate the synergistic value of combining parameters, we constructed and evaluated two distinct sets of multiparametric logistic regression models.

The first set of models was designed to improve diagnostic accuracy in the clinically challenging subgroup of patients with low-PSA levels (PSA < 20 ng/mL). We constructed a series of nested models starting with a clinical-only baseline, then incrementally added key imaging parameters—including ADCmean, PLR, and SUVmax—in a stepwise fashion.

The second set of models was developed for non-invasive stratification of ISUP Grade Group (GG 1–2 vs. GG 3–5) using imaging parameters alone. We systematically compared model performance using ADCmean, SUVmax, and PLR as individual predictors, as well as in various dual and triple combinations, to explore potential synergistic effects from multimodal imaging.

All models were rigorously evaluated using two independent internal validation methods: Leave-One-Out Cross-Validation (LOOCV) was employed to derive robust, low-bias point estimates for the Area Under the ROC Curve (AUC), while 1000 bootstrap resamples were used to assess model stability and calculate 95% confidence intervals (CI). Statistical significance of performance differences between models was tested via bootstrap-based paired t-test. Finally, the clinical utility of the best-performing models was evaluated using Decision Curve Analysis (DCA), based on the unbiased predictions generated from LOOCV.

Results

Patient demographics

A total of 74 patients were enrolled in this study, with an average age of 65.9 ± 7.4 years (range, 45–85 years). Pathological results were available for all patients. Biopsies were performed on 69 patients, and 21 patients received prostatectomy with subsequent pathological examination. Postoperative pathology results were prioritized if available. Final pathological outcomes confirmed 52 cases of prostate cancer and 22 cases of benign prostatic lesions. The benign lesions comprised 17 cases of benign prostatic hyperplasia, 2 cases of chronic prostatitis, 2 cases of chronic prostatitis accompanied by hyperplasia, and 1 case of acute prostatitis. Performance of multiparametric models in prostate cancer diagnosis and risk stratification

To further explore the value of multiparametric imaging, we constructed and evaluated several logistic regression models for two key clinical tasks. Based on the diagnostic performance of individual parameters and our subgroup analyses, key parameters including ADCmean, SUVmax, and PLR were selected for model combination.

For the diagnosis of prostate cancer in patients with PSA levels < 20 ng/mL, a model integrating clinical variables (age, PSA), ADCmean, and SUVmax (Clinical + ADC+SUVmax) achieved the best performance in stringent leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV), with an AUC of 0.795. This model’s performance was significantly better than that of the clinical-only baseline model (AUC = 0.449) and a clinical model augmented solely with ADCmean (AUC = 0.733). Discussion

Early diagnosis and risk stratification of prostate cancer are crucial for directing clinical management and improving patient survival. This study sought to evaluate the diagnostic performance of 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI-derived parameters for prostate cancer and their value in risk stratification. We focused on two key clinical objectives: enhancing diagnostic accuracy in the low-PSA subgroup (< 20 ng/mL) and enabling non-invasive stratification of ISUP Grade Group (GG 1–2 vs. GG 3–5). The combination of single-parameter analyses and multiparametric modeling underscores the clinical value of multimodal imaging.

SUVmax is widely used in clinical practice owing to its straightforward measurement and high reproducibility. However, the optimal cutoff value of SUVmax for PSMA PET in the diagnosis of prostate cancer has not been standardized. Zhong et al. [7] reported a cutoff value of 8.20, with a sensitivity of 70.6%, specificity of 100%, and an AUC of 0.897. Yan et al. identified a cutoff value of 9.37 with an AUC of 0.873, while Jiao et al. [8] prospectively validated an SUVmax cutoff value of 5.30 with an AUC of 0.779. In the present study, the optimal SUVmax cutoff value was determined to be 7.67, with an AUC of 0.920, which lies within the range of previously reported values. Discrepancies in cutoff values across studies may be attributed to several factors, including differences in sample size and composition, scanner performance, patient characteristics, and image processing protocols [9]. Accordingly, it remains challenging to establish a universal fixed SUVmax threshold for clinical decision-making [10], and larger-scale prospective studies are warranted to define the optimal cutoff value.

The Prostate Cancer Molecular Imaging Standardized Evaluation (PROMISE) system recommends lesion grading via visual comparison with reference organs including the blood pool, liver, and parotid gland, thereby improving the consistency and accuracy of PSMA PET interpretation [11]. The PROMISE criteria incorporate a PSMA expression score, in which PSMA expression is graded from 0 to 3 according to lesion uptake relative to the blood pool, liver, and parotid gland. The present study further quantifies such relative uptake values and explores their utility in the diagnosis of prostate cancer. Among the three ratio parameters (PLR, PBpR, PPgR), the lesion-to-liver ratio (PLR) exhibited the highest sensitivity and a relatively high Youden’s index. Previous studies have demonstrated that liver SUV serves as a reliable reference or normalization factor for 18F-FDG PET/CT with favorable reproducibility, and a 3-cm-diameter spherical volume of interest (VOI) is adequate for liver SUV measurement [12]. In PSMA PET imaging, intrahepatic variability of PSMA ligand uptake is also the lowest [13], rendering the liver an ideal reference organ. Importantly, variable importance analysis derived from our multiparametric model for low-PSA diagnosis indicated PLR as a significant predictor (Fig. 3B), supporting its ability to provide non-redundant diagnostic information complementary to other PET parameters.

The PSA “gray zone”, traditionally defined as 4–10 ng/mL, represents a common clinical dilemma due to its limited specificity. Elevated PSA within this range may indicate prostate cancer but is also frequently observed in benign prostatic conditions, resulting in diagnostic uncertainty and potentially unnecessary prostate biopsies. Thus, refining diagnostic strategies for this patient population is clinically imperative. Conventional PSA thresholds typically use 10 ng/mL as the upper limit of the “gray zone,” largely based on studies conducted in Western populations. However, a systematic review [14] demonstrated that, the risk of prostate cancer within the PSA gray zone is significantly lower in Asian populations. Tang et al. [15] reported no significant difference in cancer detection rates between Chinese patients with PSA levels of 4–10 ng/mL and 10–20 ng/mL. Accordingly, this study extended this range and performed subgroup analyses in patients with PSA < 20 ng/mL, with the goal of integrating novel imaging biomarkers to reduce unnecessary biopsies. Subgroup analysis revealed that the lesion-to liver ratio (PLR) and SUV40%mean, which reflects mean tracer uptake within the lesion, achieved higher sensitivity, Youden’s index, and accuracy. In contrast, diagnostic performance of SUVmax was attenuated in this low-PSA subgroup, suggesting that PLR can serve as a valuable complementary metric for diagnosing prostate cancer, especially in patients with low PSA levels. Both ADCmean and ADCmin also showed favorable diagnostic efficacy, consistent with previous studies [16, 17]. A combined diagnostic criterion integrating PLR and ADC values yielded optimal diagnostic performance for prostate cancer (sensitivity: 88.9%, specificity: 90.9%), with the potential to reduce unnecessary biopsies. Beyond single-parameter analysis, multiparametric modeling further improved diagnostic stratification. For patients with PSA < 20 ng/mL, the Clinical + ADC+SUVmax model significantly enhanced diagnostic performance, achieving a LOOCV AUC of 0.795. These findings underscore that although individual imaging parameters provide useful information, their synergistic combination—especially between PET-guided ADCmean and SUVmax— is crucial for addressing the diagnostic challenges in this clinically important subgroup.

Risk stratification of prostate cancer plays a pivotal role in clinical treatment decision-making [18]. According to the classification standards of the European Association of Urology (EAU), a Gleason score of 7 is used to distinguish intermediate- from high-risk disease [19]. The ISUP grading system further stratifies Gleason score 7 into ISUP Grade Group 2 (Gleason 3 + 4) and ISUP Grade Group 3 (Gleason 4 + 3) [19]. Mounting evidence indicates that patients with ISUP Grade Group 3 disease have significantly poorer survival and prognosis compared with those in group 2 [20, 21]. This distinction underscores the clinical necessity to reliably differentiate ISUP Grade Group 2 and 3 to avoid underestimation of tumor risk. The present study demonstrated that 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI and its derived parameters exhibit favorable performance in distinguishing ISUP Grade Group 1–2 from 3 to 5, with ADCmean and PLR showing robust diagnostic efficacy. Our multiparametric analysis further confirmed the considerable value of imaging parameters for prostate cancer risk stratification. Notably, for the discrimination of ISUP Grade Group 1–2 versus 3–5, a dual-parameter imaging model integrating PET-guided ADCmean and SUVmax (ADC+SUVmax) achieved outstanding performance, with a LOOCV AUC of 0.906. This synergistic combination highlights that comprehensive assessment of both anatomical and functional tumor features is indispensable for accurate pathological risk prediction.

This study demonstrated that 68Ga-PSMA PET/MRI and its derived parameters carry considerable value in the risk stratification of prostate cancer. SUV40%mean, which reflects the mean tracer uptake within the lesion, and ADCmean, which characterizes water molecule diffusion, showed moderate correlations with pathological grade. While a previous study reported a correlation between SUVmax and Gleason score [8], the present study found that SUV40%mean, derived using a percentage threshold approach, exhibited a stronger correlation with pathological grade grouping than SUVmax, likely because it better characterizes the overall biological activity of the lesion. Furthermore, ADCmean measured in PSMA-positive regions (by integrating PET/MRI information) was significantly correlated with ISUP Grade Group. Both ADCmean and SUV40%mean were identified as useful indicators associated with pathological grading. The consistent performance of PET-guided ADCmean in both diagnostic and risk stratification tasks underscores its potential as a robust and reliable fused imaging biomarker.

mpMRI is an important diagnostic tool for prostate cancer. PSMA PET imaging still has some false-negative results [22], hence high-resolution multi-sequence MRI is helpful for the diagnosis of prostate cancer using PSMA PET.

Our findings, particularly the success of the ADC+SUVmax model in ISUP Grade Group stratification, emphasize that the added value of specific combined imaging parameters in risk stratification is a substantial inherent benefit of the PET/MRI platform. While PET/MRI is generally more expensive than PET/CT, its ability to provide comprehensive and synergistic information in a single session could potentially offset costs associated with multiple separate imaging procedures or staging errors, particularly in centers where PET/MRI is already established. Future studies should directly compare the cost-effectiveness of PET/MRI versus PET/CT plus a separate diagnostic MRI in specific clinical scenarios [23, 24].

This study still has several limitations. First, it is a single-center retrospective study with a small sample size and uneven distribution, which may introduce selection bias. A significant proportion (50%) of our cohort presented with Gleason Grade Group 5 disease, representing a very high-risk population typically characterized by high PSMA expression. This skewness may have influenced the diagnostic performance metrics, potentially inflating the observed accuracy for this specific cohort and limiting the direct applicability of these findings to cohorts predominantly composed of lower-grade disease. Despite the use of robust internal validation methods (i.e., LOOCV and Bootstrap), external validation in larger, multi-center, prospective cohorts is essential to confirm the generalizability of our results. Second, the study only adopted a 40% segmentation threshold; further studies are needed to explore differences between various segmentation thresholds and their impacts on the results. In addition, some of the patients’ clinical information was missing, which compromised data integrity.

Conclusions

In summary, this study demonstrates that PSMA PET/MRI-derived multiparametric models offer significant advantages for prostate cancer diagnosis in low-PSA patients and for non-invasive ISUP Grade Group stratification. The combination of PET-guided ADCmean and SUVmax represents an effective approach to integrating complementary information from MR and PET to improve patient outcomes through more precise diagnosis and risk assessment. Its performance in our cohort underscores its potential as a valuable fused imaging biomarker.

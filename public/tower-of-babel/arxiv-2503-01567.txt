Hyperuniformity and hyperfluctuations of random measures in commutative spaces
Michael Björklund, Mattias Byléhn

Abstract
. We introduce the notion of Bartlett spectral measure for isometrically invariant random measures on proper metric commutative spaces. When the underlying Gelfand pair corresponds to a higher-rank, connected, simple matrix Lie group with finite center and a maximal compact subgroup, we prove that the number variance is asymptotically bounded above, uniformly across all random measures, by the volume raised to a power strictly less than 2. On Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces we define a notion of heat kernel hyperuniformity for random distributions that generalizes hyperuniformity of random measures, and we prove that every sufficiently well behaved spectral measure can be realized as the Bartlett spectral measure of an invariant Gaussian random distribution. We also compute Bartlett spectral measures for invariant determinantal point processes in commutative spaces, providing a spectral proof of hyperuniformity for infinite polyanalytic ensembles in the complex plane, as well as heat kernel non-hyperuniformity of the zero set of the standard hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function on the Poincaré disk.

1.
Introduction

In the context of statistical mechanics and condensed matter, notions of
long-range order
of a physical configuration is of fundamental interest, and are in many settings intimately related to the suppression of some type of
fluctuation
in the thermodynamic limit. For spatial homogeneous point configurations, realized mathematically as a translation invariant point process in Euclidean space, a fluctuation whose behavior indicates long-range order or disorder is that of mass or density. More precisely, the mass fluctuations in the thermodynamical limit is recorded by the
number variance
of the point process with respect to asymptotically large metric balls, and a translation invariant point process is
hyperuniform
as defined by Stillinger-Torquato in
[
37
]
if the number variance is asymptotically smaller than the volume of balls in the large radial limit, indicating that it is more well ordered than a classical ideal gas. Equivalently, one can define hyperuniformity of a point process spectrally in terms of its
structure factor
, or more generally
Bartlett spectrum
, having asymptotically faster decay than the volume of balls in the small frequency limit. In physical terms, hyperuniformity corresponds to sufficient suppression of low frequencies in the (idealized) Fraunhofer diffraction picture.

Hyperuniformity of invariant point processes has been well studied in Euclidean spaces, and have recently been extended to compact homogeneous geometries in
[
35
,
12
,
13
,
14
]
as well as hyperbolic spaces and regular trees in
[
6
,
16
]
. Invariant point processes and random measures on general locally compact second countable Hausdorff homogeneous spaces have been studied in
[
29
]
, and the motivation for this paper is to set up a framework for studying number variances and Bartlett spectra of point processes in non-compact
commutative spaces
, a class of homogeneous spaces on which there is a particularly well-behaved notion of Fourier transform for radial functions. These spaces include Euclidean and hyperbolic spaces, (bi-)regular trees, but also higher rank geometries including non-compact Riemannian symmetric spaces and Bruhat-Tits buildings.

We provide a notion of
Bartlett spectral measure
for random measures on commutative spaces, generalizing the Euclidean setting, and we extend this notion to invariant random distributions. Using the existence of such a spectral measure together with techniques of Gorodnik-Nevo we can show that for higher rank symmetric spaces that are quotients of connected simple matrix Lie groups with finite center by a maximal compact subgroup, number variances are asymptotically bounded from above, uniformly among all invariant random measures, by a power of the volume of balls strictly less than 2. We also provide a general framework for the number variance of the Bartlett spectral measure of invariant determinantal point processes on commutative spaces, which includes invariant Weyl-Heisenberg ensembles on Euclidean spaces and certain Gaussian analytic functions on complex Euclidean spaces and the hyperbolic plane. Finally, we provide a notion of
heat kernel hyperuniformity
on Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces, which is equivalent to spectral hyperuniformity introduced in
[
7
,
6
]
, and show that every sufficiently well behaved positive measure on the Fourier domain can be realized as the Bartlett spectral measure of some invariant random (tempered) distribution.

1.1.
Spectral measures of random measures

Let
(X\mathchar 59\relax d\mathchar 59\relax x_{o})
be a proper pointed metric space and
G
a group of isometries of
(X\mathchar 59\relax d)
acting transitively on
X
with compact stabilizer
K
of
x_{o}
, so that
X
is homeomorphic to
G/K
. By an
invariant random measure
on
X
we mean a probability measure
\mu
on the space
\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)
of positive Radon measures on
X
which is invariant under the push-forward action of
G
. On such a probability space one has natural families of random variables readily available: to every complex-valued bounded measurable function
f\in\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(X)
with compact support one can associate the
linear statistic

\mathbb{S}f:\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}f(p)=\int_{X}f(x)dp(x)

as well as the
discrepancy statistic

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{0}f(p)=\mathbb{S}f(p)-\mathbb{E}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{X}f(x)dp(x)-i_{\mu}\int_{X}f(x)dm_{X}(x)\,.

Here
m_{X}
denotes a fixed
G
-invariant reference measure on
X
and
i_{\mu}>0
is the
intensity
of
\mu
, namely the expected measure of a unit
m_{X}
-volume Borel set. An invariant random measure
\mu
is
locally square-integrable
if all linear statistics are square-integrable with respect to
\mu
, that is

\displaystyle\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)}\Big|\int_{X}f(x)dp(x)\Big|^{2}d\mu(p)<+\infty\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\forall\,f\in\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(X)\,.

In the context of hyperuniformity and various related notions, one is often interested in understanding the variance of linear statistics with respect to a given invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)}|\mathbb{S}_{0}f(p)|^{2}d\mu(p)\,.

In particular, when
f=\chi_{B_{r}(x_{o})}
is the indicator function of a
K
-invariant ball of radius
r>0
with respect to the fixed basepoint
x_{o}\in X
, we define the
number variance
of
\mu
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)=\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}\chi_{B_{r}(x_{o})})\,.

Heuristically, the physical significance of the number variance is that it records the macroscopic mass fluctuations of the random measure
\mu
along the sequence
B_{r}(x_{o})
when
r>0
is large.

We are interested in spectral decompositions of the variance of
K
-invariant linear statistics, especially for the indicator functions
\chi_{B_{r}(x_{o})}
. For the setup, our proper pointed metric space
X=G/K
as previously described is in addition assumed to be a
commutative space
, equivalently that
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
forms a
Gelfand pair
, which ensures the existence of
spherical functions

\omega:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, meaning non-zero bi-
K
-invariant functions satisfying the functional equation

\displaystyle\omega(g_{1})\omega(g_{2})=\int_{K}\omega(g_{1}kg_{2})dm_{K}(k)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad g_{1}\mathchar 59\relax g_{2}\in G\,.

The family of all
positive-definite
spherical functions for
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
are of main interest and we denote it by
\mathcal{S}^{+}=\mathcal{S}^{+}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. For Euclidean spaces these are simply the complex exponentials, for the hyperbolic plane they are Legendre functions and for regular trees they are combinations of Chebyshev polynomials. If
\varphi:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is an integrable bi-
K
-invariant function, for example the indicator of a
K
-invariant ball, then one defines its
spherical transform

\widehat{\varphi}:\mathcal{S}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
to be

\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)=\int_{G}\varphi(g)\omega(g^{-1})dm_{G}(g)\,.

For commutative spaces
X=G/K
as described, we show that
K
-invariant discrepancy statistics in
L^{2}_{0}(\mu)^{K}
for an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
X
can be decomposed in terms of a spectral measure on the space
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. To state the Theorem, we introduce for every
f\in\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(X)
a right-
K
-invariant function
\varphi_{f}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
\varphi_{f}(g)=f(g.x_{o})
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\mu
be an invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
X
. Then there is a unique positive Radon measure
\sigma_{\mu}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
such that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}(\omega)

for every
K
-invariant function
f\in\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(X)
.

The existence and uniqueness of the spectral measure
\sigma_{\mu}
has been utilized in
[
6
]
and
[
16
]
, where the main results rely on Theorem
1.1
. When
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, then the measure
\sigma_{\mu}
is the classical
Bartlett spectral measure
of
\mu
introduced by Bartlett in
[
5
]
, and for the commutative spaces
X
that we consider we will adopt the same terminology for
\sigma_{\mu}
. The existence and uniqueness of Bartlett spectral measures has been proven for invariant translation bounded random measures in the series
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
, and we emphasize that Theorem
1.1
includes many interesting random measures and point processes that are typically not translation bounded, for example determinantal/permanental point processes. Other names for the Bartlett spectral measure
\sigma_{\mu}
in the literature include
diffraction measure
and
spectral measure
. We note as a canonical example that if
\mu=\mathrm{Poi}(m_{X})
is the invariant Poisson point process with intensity measure
m_{X}
then the corresponding Bartlett spectral measure is the
spherical Plancherel measure

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
, satisfying

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)

for all bi-
K
-invariant
\varphi\in L^{2}(G)
.

Remark 1.2
.

The setup for Theorem
1.1
is an instance of a more general framework that we develop in Section
3
. In Theorem
3.2
we show that any linear
G
-equivariant map
\alpha:\mathscr{A}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
between a ”sufficiently large”
*
-algebra
\mathscr{A}(G)
of integrable functions to a
K
-spherical unitary representation
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
admits a spectral measure
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
in the sense that

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha(\varphi)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)

for all bi-
K
-invariant
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G)
. This general framework allows us to extend the notion of Bartlett spectral measures to random
distributions
on
X
. In the Appendix, we prove that every positive Radon measure
\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
whose spherical transform is a bi-
K
-invariant positive-definite distribution on
G
can be realized as the Bartlett spectral measure of a Gaussian random distribution on
X
.

1.2.
Maximal fluctuations in higher rank symmetric spaces

For invariant locally square-integrable random measures
\mu
on Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an application of the mean ergodic theorem allows one to show that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{r}(0))^{2}}=0\,.

From works of Gorodnik and Nevo, see for example the book
[
25
]
, this upper bound for the number variance holds for many non-compact symmetric spaces
X=G/K
in the sense that the metric balls
B_{r}(x_{o})\subset X
,
r>0
, form a mean ergodic sequence. How close one is able to get to this upper asymptotic bound is the topic of this Subsection.

Question
:
Given a proper metric commutative space
X=G/K
, is there for every
\varepsilon>0
an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu_{\varepsilon}
on
X
such that

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{\varepsilon}}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{X}(B_{r}(x_{o}))^{2-\varepsilon}}>0\,?

This question has been addressed in several works, and we recall some of them here.

In
[
19
, Section 5]
, it is shown that for every
\varepsilon>0
, there is a permanental point process
\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}
on the real line
\mathbb{R}
with positive-definite kernel
L_{\varepsilon}(t_{1}\mathchar 59\relax t_{2})=(1+|t_{1}-t_{2}|^{\alpha})^{-\zeta/2}
under the condition
\alpha\zeta=\varepsilon
for
\alpha\in(0\mathchar 59\relax 2]
and
\zeta>0
, such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{L_{\varepsilon}}}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}}([-r\mathchar 59\relax r])^{2-\varepsilon}}>0\,.

We expect that higher dimensional analogues of such permanental point processes also should fulfil the analogous result.

This has also been answered in hyperbolic geometries: In
[
15
, Theorem 1(c)]
, Buckley considers point processes governed by the zero sets of Gaussian analytic functions on the hyperbolic plane
\mathbb{H}^{2}=\mathrm{SU}(1\mathchar 59\relax 1)/\mathrm{U}(1)
, realized in the Poincaré disk model. These Gaussian analytic functions are given by

\displaystyle F_{t}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(t+n)}{n!\Gamma(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}z^{n}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad|z|<1\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
a_{n}
are complex standard Gaussian random variables and
t>0
. The point process associated with the random zero set of
F_{t}
is

\displaystyle\mu_{t}=\sum_{z:F_{t}(z)=0}\delta_{z}\,.

Buckley proves among other things that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{t_{\varepsilon}}}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}(B_{r}(0))^{2-\varepsilon}}>0\,.

This result was in turn generalized to higher dimensions by Massaneda and Pridhnani in
[
32
, Theorem 1.2]
, where they consider Gaussian analytic functions on higher-dimensional complex hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{d}=\mathrm{SU}(1\mathchar 59\relax d)/\mathrm{SU}(d)
in the unit ball model, which in multi-index notation is defined by

\displaystyle F_{t}(z)=\sum_{m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}\Big(\frac{\Gamma(t+|m|)}{m!\Gamma(t)}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{m}z^{m}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\lVert z\rVert<1\,.

Similarly to Buckley’s result, it is shown that for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a
t_{\varepsilon}>0
such that the random zero variety
\mu_{t_{\varepsilon}}
of
F_{t}
in
\mathbb{H}_{\mathbb{C}}^{d}
satisfies

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu_{t_{\varepsilon}}}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{H}^{d}_{\mathbb{C}}}(B_{r}(0))^{2-\varepsilon}}>0\,.

Using techniques due to Gorodnik-Nevo in
[
25
]
, we answer the Question above in the negative when
G
is a higher rank connected simple matrix Lie group with finite center and
K<G
a maximal compact subgroup. These groups have
Kazhdan’s Property
(T), meaning that the trivial
G
-representation is isolated in the unitary dual of
G
, and moreover the stronger property that there is a
q\in[2\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
such that all matrix coefficients of non-trivial irreducible representations with
K
-finite vectors are in
L^{q}(G)
. In particular, this integrability condition holds for the non-trivial positive-definite spherical functions on
G
. Using this, we prove that the number variance is asymptotically bounded above, uniformly across all random measures, by the volume raised to a power strictly less than 2.

Theorem 1.3
.

Suppose
G
is a higher rank connected simple matrix Lie group with finite center and
K<G
a maximal compact subgroup. Then there is a constant
\delta=\delta(G)\in(0\mathchar 59\relax 1)
such that for any invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
X=G/K
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{X}(B_{r}(x_{o}))^{2-\delta}}=0\,.

Gelfand pairs
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
as in Theorem
1.3
include for example

(\mathrm{SL}_{3}(\mathbb{R})\mathchar 59\relax\mathrm{SO}(3))\quad\mbox{ and }\quad(\mathrm{SO}(2\mathchar 59\relax 2)\mathchar 59\relax\mathrm{SO}(2)\times\mathrm{SO}(2))\,.

In light of Theorem
1.3
, we emphasize that the results mentioned above for permanental point processes and Gaussian analytic functions do not hold in the context of complex symmetric spaces coming from Gelfand pairs as in Theorem
1.3
. To give an explicit example, consider the Siegel upper half space

\displaystyle\mathbb{SH}_{d}=\Big\{Z=X+iY\in\mathrm{Mat}_{d\times d}(\mathbb{C}):X^{t}=X\,\mathchar 59\relax\,Y^{t}=Y\,\mathchar 59\relax\,\,Y>0\Big\}

of genus
d\geq 2
, which naturally carries the structure of a complex manifold. There is a metric on
\mathbb{SH}_{d}
compatible with the complex structure such that the group
\mathrm{Sp}(2d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{R})
of
(2d\times 2d)
-real symplectic matrices acts transitively and isometrically on the Siegel upper half space by fractional transformations,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\
C&D\end{pmatrix}.Z=(AZ+B)(CZ+D)^{-1}\,.

Moreover, the stabilizer for this action is the maximal compact subgroup

\mathrm{U}(d)=\mathrm{Sp}(2d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{R})\cap\mathrm{GL}(d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{C})\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that
\mathbb{SH}_{d}=\mathrm{Sp}(2d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{R})/\mathrm{U}(d)
defines a complex commutative space for the Gelfand pair
(\mathrm{Sp}(2d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{R})\mathchar 59\relax\mathrm{U}(d))
. Since
\mathrm{Sp}(2d\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{R})
is a simple connected Lie group and has real rank greater than or equal to
2
for
d\geq 2
, we can conclude the following from Theorem
1.3
.

Corollary 1.4
.

For the Siegel upper half spaces
\mathbb{SH}_{d}
,
d\geq 2
, the answer to the Question above is negative, in particular for all families of invariant permanental point processes and invariant zero sets of Gaussian analytic functions.

1.3.
Hyperuniformity and hyperfluctuations

A translation invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on Euclidean space
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
geometrically hyperuniform
if

\displaystyle\limsup_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\frac{\mathrm{NV}_{\mu}(r)}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{r}(0))}=0\,.

If this upper limit is instead positive and finite, we say that
\mu
has
Poissonian
fluctuations, and if the upper limit is infinite then we say that
\mu
is
hyperfluctuating
. In the spectral picture, an invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
spectrally hyperuniform
if

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{\mu}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}=0\,\mathchar 59\relax

and these to notions of hyperuniformity are equivalent, see
[
7
, Proposition 3.3]
for a proof.

For non-amenable Gelfand pairs, specifically real hyperbolic spaces

\mathbb{H}^{d}=\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax d)/\mathrm{SO}(d)\,\mathchar 59\relax

of dimension
d\geq 2
and
(p+1)
-regular trees

\mathbb{T}_{p+1}=\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q}_{p})/\mathrm{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})

for primes
p
(and more generally valence
d\geq 3
), a similar notion of geometric hyperuniformity as in the Euclidean case fails to admit any examples. The notion of spectral hyperuniformity however has been extended to these spaces in
[
6
,
16
]
. In the case of real hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, the positive-definite spherical functions are parameterized by two intervals
\lambda\in i(0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{d-1}{2}]\cup[0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
, corresponding to complementary and principal
\mathrm{SO}(d)
-spherical unitary irreducible representations respectively. An invariant locally square-integrable random measure
\mu
on real hyperbolic space is spectrally hyperuniform if
\sigma_{\mu}(i[0\mathchar 59\relax\tfrac{d-1}{2}])=0
and

(1.1)

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{\mu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}=0\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
d\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(\lambda)=\chi_{(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)}(\lambda)|c(\lambda)|^{-2}d\lambda
is the Plancherel measure and
c(\lambda)
is the Harish-Chandra
c
-function for
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax d)
. In the Appendix, we show for Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces that spectral hyperuniformity of an invariant locally square-integrable random measure is equivalent to what we call
heat kernel hyperuniformity
. To state the definition we consider the bi-
K
-invariant heat kernel
h_{t}
for
t>0
, which in Euclidean spaces is given by

\displaystyle h_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi t)^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{4t}}

and in real hyperbolic spaces by

\displaystyle h_{t}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t(\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{4}+\lambda^{2})}\omega_{\lambda}(g)\frac{d\lambda}{|c(\lambda)|^{2}}\,.

where
\omega_{\lambda}:G\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
are the principal series positive-definite spherical functions for
\mathrm{SO}^{\circ}(1\mathchar 59\relax d)
. The heat kernel
h_{t}
is smooth with Gaussian decay in both geometries, see for example
[
18
, Theorem 3.1 p.186]
for uniform estimates in the hyperbolic case, so we define heat kernel hyperuniformity in the generality of invariant random distributions as follows.

Definition 1.5
.

Let
\mu
denote an invariant locally square-integrable random distribution on
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{H}^{d}
.

(1)

If
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we say that
\mu
is heat kernel hyperuniform if

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}t^{d/2}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{t})=0\,.

(2)

If
X=\mathbb{H}^{d}
, we say that
\mu
is heat kernel hyperuniform if

\displaystyle\limsup_{t\rightarrow+\infty}t^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2}t}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{t})=0\,.

Proposition 1.6
.

A random distribution
\mu
on
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{H}^{d}
is spectrally hyperuniform if and only if it is heat kernel hyperuniform.

We prove this Proposition in the Appendix. As mentioned in Remark
1.2
, we also show that every positive Radon measure
\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
whose spherical transform is a positive-definite distribution on
G
can be realized as the Bartlett spectral measure of a Gaussian random distribution on
X=G/K
. For Euclidean and real hyperbolic geometries, such measures
\sigma
are precisely the tempered positive Radon measures on the positive-definite spherical functions. In the real hyperbolic case we are aware of essentially one spectrally hyperuniform random measure, an invariant random lattice orbit in the hyperbolic plane coming from a lattice
\Gamma<\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
constructed by Jenni in
[
27
, Section 1.2, Eq. B]
. For any tempered measure
\sigma
on the positive-definite spherical functions satisfying the spectral hyperuniformity criterion in Equation
1.1
, we can construct from it a spectrally hyperuniform Gaussian random (tempered)
distribution
.

Corollary 1.7
.

On real hyperbolic spaces
\mathbb{H}^{d}
, there are infinitely many heat kernel hyperuniform Gaussian random distributions.

1.4.
Determinantal point processes with equivariant kernels

We set up a general framework for determinantal point processes
\mu_{L}
goverened by continuous positive-definite hermitian kernels
L:X\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
satisfying the equivariance property

(1.2)

\displaystyle L(g.x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax g.x_{2})=c(g\mathchar 59\relax x_{1})\overline{c(g\mathchar 59\relax x_{2})}L(x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax x_{2})\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad g\in G\,\mathchar 59\relax\,x_{1}\mathchar 59\relax x_{2}\in X

for some measurable map
c:G\times X\rightarrow\mathbb{T}^{1}
, where
\mathbb{T}^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}
denotes the unit circle. If the bi-
K
-invariant function
\kappa_{L}(g)=|L(x_{o}\mathchar 59\relax g.x_{o})|^{2}
is square-integrable on
G
, then we compute the Bartlett spectral measure of
\mu_{L}
from Theorem
1.1
to be

\displaystyle d\sigma_{\mu_{L}}(\omega)=(L(x_{o}\mathchar 59\relax x_{o})-\widehat{\kappa}_{L}(\omega))d\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)

where
\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the Plancherel measure for
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. We provide explicit formulas for the Bartlett spectral measure for some well-studied invariant determinantal point processes.

For
infinite polyanalytic ensembles of pure type
in
\mathbb{C}
, that is, determinantal point processes
\mu_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
on
\mathbb{C}
with kernel of the form

\displaystyle L_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}(z_{1}\mathchar 59\relax z_{2})=\mathcal{L}_{n}(\tfrac{1}{2}|\lambda||z_{1}-z_{2}|^{2})\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{1}{4}|\lambda|(|z_{1}|^{2}+|z_{2}|^{2}-2z_{1}\overline{z}_{2})}\,\mathchar 59\relax\,\,\lambda\in\mathbb{R}\backslash\{0\}\mathchar 59\relax n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
\mathcal{L}_{n}
denotes the
n
’th Laguerre polynomial, the Bartlett spectral measure is given by

\displaystyle d\sigma_{\mu_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}}(\zeta)=\Big(1-\frac{2\pi}{|\lambda|}\mathcal{L}_{n}(\tfrac{2\pi^{2}}{|\lambda|}|\zeta|^{2})^{2}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{2\pi^{2}}{|\lambda|}|\zeta|^{2}}\Big)dA(\zeta)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\zeta\in\mathbb{C}\,.

Here,
A
denotes the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
and we note that
\mu_{2\pi\mathchar 59\relax 0}
is the well-known
infinite Ginibre ensemble
. From the Bartlett spectral measure one sees that
\mu_{\pm 2\pi\mathchar 59\relax n}
are the (spectrally) hyperuniform point processes in this family.

The processes
\mu_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
are examples of
Weyl-Heisenberg ensembles
introduced in
[
1
]
, and the kernels
L_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
can be defined in terms of unitary irreducible representations of the Heisenberg motion group
\mathrm{U}(d)\ltimes\mathbb{H}_{d}
, which is a covering group of the unitary motion group
\mathrm{U}(d)\ltimes\mathbb{C}^{d}
. We detail a general approach for determinantal point processes of Weyl-Heisenberg type on commutative spaces
X=G/K
with kernels coming from unitary representations of a covering group
\widetilde{G}

. In light of this, we observe that hyperuniformity of the processes
\mu_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
on
\mathbb{C}^{d}
corresponds to the governing representation of the Heisenberg motion group having
formal dimension
1
, meaning

\displaystyle\int_{\widetilde{G}/Z}|\langle\pi_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}(\widetilde{g})v_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}\mathchar 59\relax v_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}\rangle|^{2}dm_{\widetilde{G}/Z}(\widetilde{g}Z)=1

where
Z<\widetilde{G}
is the center and
\pi_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
is the irreducible unitary representation of the Heisenberg motion group with
\mathrm{U}(d)
-invariant cyclic unit vector
v_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
defining the kernel
L_{\lambda\mathchar 59\relax n}
.

We also compute the Bartlett spectral measure for the invariant zero set of the hyperbolic Gaussian analytic function

\displaystyle F_{1}(z)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}a_{n}z^{n}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad|z|<1\,\mathchar 59\relax

where
a_{n}
are again complex standard Gaussian random variables. Peres and Virág in
[

famously proved that the random zero set of

is equivalent as a point process to the determinantal point process in
\mathbb{H}^{2}
defined by the
Bergman kernel
, which on the Euclidean unit disk is given by
L_{o}(z_{1}\mathchar 59\relax z_{2})=\pi^{-1}(1-z_{1}\overline{z}_{2})^{-2}
. The modified kernel

\displaystyle L(z_{1}\mathchar 59\relax z_{2})=\frac{(1-|z_{1}|^{2})(1-|z_{2}|^{2})}{(1-z_{1}\overline{z_{2}})^{2}}

\displaystyle d\sigma_{L}(\lambda)

\displaystyle=(1-|\Gamma(\tfrac{3}{2}+i\lambda)|^{2})d\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(\lambda)=\chi_{(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)}(\lambda)(1-|\Gamma(\tfrac{3}{2}+i\lambda)|^{2})\pi\lambda\tanh(\tfrac{\pi\lambda}{2})d\lambda\,.

In particular, the determinantal point process

is not heat kernel/spectrally hyperuniform since

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{\mu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}=1-\Gamma(\tfrac{3}{2})^{2}>0\,.

2.1.
Function spaces

Given a locally compact second countable Hausdorff (lcsc) space
Y
equipped with its Borel
\sigma
-algebra, we let
\mathscr{M}_{+}(Y)
denote the space of positive Radon measures on
Y
. We will write
\mathscr{L}(Y)
for the complex-valued measurable functions on
Y
and let
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(Y)\mathchar 59\relax\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(Y)\subset\mathscr{L}(Y)
be the subspaces of bounded measurable functions and bounded measurable functions with compact support respectively. Given a measure
\nu\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(Y)
and
p\in[1\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
, we write

\displaystyle\mathscr{L}^{p}(Y\mathchar 59\relax\nu)=\Big\{f\in\mathscr{L}(Y):\int_{Y}|f(y)|^{p}d\nu(y)<+\infty\Big\}\,.

The corresponding Banach space of such functions modulo
\nu
-null equivalence is
L^{p}(Y\mathchar 59\relax\nu)
. We let
C(Y)\subset\mathscr{L}(Y)
be the space of continuous functions on
Y
,
C_{b}(Y)=C(Y)\cap\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(Y)
the bounded continuous functions,
C_{0}(Y)\subset C_{b}(Y)
the closed subspace of functions vanishing at infinity and
C_{c}(Y)=C(Y)\cap\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(Y)
the compactly supported continuous functions.

If
Y
has in addition a smooth structure, then we write
C^{\infty}(Y)
for the smooth functions on
Y
and
C_{c}^{\infty}(Y)=C^{\infty}(Y)\cap\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(Y)
for the subspace of compactly supported smooth functions.

Regarding actions, if
K
is a group acting on
Y
from the left and
\mathscr{A}(Y)
is a function space on
Y
we write
\mathscr{A}(Y)^{K}
for the subspace of left-
K
-invariant functions in
\mathscr{A}(Y)
. For a group
G
with subgroup
K<G
and a function space
\mathscr{A}(G)
on
G
, we will write
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
for the space of
bi-
K
-averages
of functions in
\mathscr{A}(G)
unless told otherwise, see Equation
2.3
for the definition of bi-
K
-averaging.

2.2.
Preliminaries on Gelfand pairs

Let
G
denote a unimodular, lcsc group with identity element
e\in G
and fix a Haar measure
m_{G}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(G)
on
G
. If
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
then their
convolution
is the function

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})(g)=\int_{G}\varphi_{1}(h)\varphi_{2}(h^{-1}g)dm_{G}(h)\,.

Alternatively, a change of variables allows one to write

\displaystyle(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})(g)=\int_{G}\varphi_{1}(gh^{-1})\varphi_{2}(h)dm_{G}(h)\,.

Moreover, we will consider the involutions
\overline{\varphi}(g)=\overline{\varphi(g)}\mathchar 59\relax\check{\varphi}(g)=\varphi(g^{-1})
and the
*
-involution
\varphi^{*}(g)=\overline{\varphi(g^{-1})}
for measurable functions
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}(G)
, in particular
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
. Then
\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
inherits a
*
-algebra structure under the operations of convolution and
*
-involution, which carries over to the quotient space
L^{1}(G)
, making it a Banach
*
-algebra. The left-regular action of
G
on measurable functions
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}(G)
will be denoted by

\displaystyle\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi(h)=\varphi(g^{-1}h)

and the associated action of
\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
on bounded measurable functions
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(G)
is

\displaystyle\lambda_{G}(\beta)\varphi(g)=\int_{G}\beta(h)\lambda_{G}(h)\varphi(g)dm_{G}(h)=(\beta*\varphi)(g)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\beta\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)\,.

Using this action one shows that the left-regular group action commutes with convolution,

\displaystyle\lambda_{G}(g)(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})=(\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi_{1})*\varphi_{2}

for all
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
and all
g\in G
.

Given a compact subgroup
K<G
, the
Hecke algebra
of the pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
is the algebra
C_{c}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
of bi-
K
-invariant compactly supported continuous complex-valued functions on
G
, endowed with the operation of convolution and the
*
-involution as above.

Definition 2.1

(Gelfand pair)
.

Let
G
be a lcsc group and
K<G
a compact subgroup. The pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
is
Gelfand
if the Hecke algebra
(C_{c}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)\mathchar 59\relax*)
is commutative.

We remark that the Gelfand property is equivalent to convolution being commutative on the space
\mathscr{L}^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
of measurable bi-
K
-invariant integrable functions. Gelfand pairs include abelian groups
G
with
K
trivial, connected semisimple Lie groups
G
with finite center and maximal compact subgroup
K<G
, automorphism groups of biregular trees with the corresponding root stabilizer as well as Euclidean and Heisenberg motion groups with the corresponding maximal compact subgroup of orthogonal/unitary transformations.

2.3.
Positive-definite spherical functions

Let
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
be a lcsc Gelfand pair with
K<G
compact. A
K
-spherical function
, or
spherical function
for short, for the pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
is a bi-
K
-invariant continuous function
\omega\in C(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
such that
\omega(e)=1
satisfying the functional equation

(2.1)

\displaystyle\int_{K}\omega(gkh)dm_{K}(k)=\omega(g)\omega(h)\,.

We denote the space of spherical functions for
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
by
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{S}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, endowed with the weak
∗
-topology from
C_{c}(G)^{*}
. Special attention will be paid to the
positive-definite
spherical functions. A continuous function
\beta\in C(G)
is positive-definite if, for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}_{c}^{\infty}(G)
,

\displaystyle\int_{G}(\varphi^{*}*\varphi)(g)\beta(g)dm_{G}(g)\geq 0\,.

Given a positive-definite function
\beta\in C(G)
, the classical GNS-construction yields a unitary
G
-representation
(\pi_{\beta}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H}_{\beta})
and a non-zero cyclic vector
v_{\beta}\in\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
such that
\beta(g)=\langle\pi_{\beta}(g)v_{\beta}\mathchar 59\relax v_{\beta}\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\beta}}
. Moreover, the triple
(\pi_{\beta}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\mathchar 59\relax v_{\beta})
is unique up to unitary equivalence. In particular, such
\beta
are uniformly continuous and bounded with
\lVert\beta\rVert_{\infty}=\beta(e)
. We denote by
\mathcal{S}^{+}\subset\mathcal{S}
the locally compact Hausdorff space of positive-definite spherical functions for
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. A spherical function
\omega\in\mathcal{S}
is positive-definite if and only if it is the matrix coefficient of a unitary
K
-spherical irreducible unitary
G
-representation
(\pi_{\omega}\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H}_{\omega})
. Here,
K
-spherical means that the subspace
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}^{K}
of
K
-invariant vectors is non-trivial, and by irreducibility we must have
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}^{K}=\mathbb{C}v_{\omega}
. In particular, the unit vector
v_{\omega}\in\mathcal{H}_{\omega}^{K}
is uniquely defined up to multiplication by a unit norm complex number. In this sense,
\mathcal{S}^{+}
can be identified with the
K
-spherical unitary dual

\widehat{G}^{K}
of equivalence classes of
K
-spherical irreducible unitary
G
-representations.

2.4.
The spherical transform

The positive-definite spherical functions introduced for Gelfand pairs
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
are in a sense a generalization of unitary characters on abelian groups, and they allow us to define an analogue of the Fourier transform for functions for the bi-
K
-invariant on
G
.

Definition 2.2

(Spherical transform)
.

Let
\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. The
spherical transform
of
\varphi
is the function
\widehat{\varphi}\in C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)=\int_{G}\varphi(g)\omega(g^{-1})dm_{G}(g)\,.

One verifies that the spherical transform satisfies
\widehat{\varphi^{*}}(\omega)=\overline{\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)}
for all
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
and when
\varphi\mathchar 59\relax\psi\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
are
bi-
K
-invariant
, the functional equation in Equation
2.1
can be used to show that

(2.2)

\displaystyle\widehat{(\psi^{*}*\varphi)}(\omega)=\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)\overline{\widehat{\psi}(\omega)}

for all
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
. We also note that the image
\{\widehat{\varphi}\in C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+}):\varphi\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)\}
can be shown to be dense in
C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
using the Stone-Weierstrass Theorem, since it separates points and vanishes nowhere.

Before moving on we introduce the bi-
K
-averaging map
\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{L}^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
,
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{\natural}
by

(2.3)

\displaystyle\varphi^{\natural}(g)=\int_{K\times K}\varphi(k_{1}gk_{2})dm_{K}^{\otimes 2}(k_{1}\mathchar 59\relax k_{2})\,\mathchar 59\relax

which allows us to naturally extend the spherical transform to
\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
as
\varphi\mapsto\widehat{\varphi}^{\natural}
. We note however that the
*
-homomorphism property in Equation
2.2
is in general
not
satisfied for functions
\varphi\mathchar 59\relax\psi\in\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
.

2.5.
Spherical Bochner’s Theorem

The classical Bochner Theorem states that a continuous function on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is positive-definite if and only if it is the Fourier transform of a positive finite Borel measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. The analogue of this statement for Gelfand pairs is the following, and a proof can be found in
[
4
, Theorem 2.5, p.58]
.

Theorem 2.3

(Godement-Bochner)
.

Let
\beta\in C(G)
be a bi-
K
-invariant positive-definite function. Then there is a unique finite positive Borel measure
\sigma_{\beta}
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\beta(g)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\omega(g)d\sigma_{\beta}(\omega)\,.

Remark 2.4
.

This result in
[
4
, Theorem 2.5, p.58]
is stated for Lie groups, but we emphasize that the proof works verbatim for a general lcsc Gelfand pair
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
with
K<G
compact.

Writing this result in terms of
K
-spherical matrix coefficients, we can using polarization also define complex-valued spectral measures for the non-diagonal matrix coefficients, see
[
21
, Prop. 2.53, p. 111]
for the abelian case.

Corollary 2.5

(Spectral measures for spherical matrix coefficients)
.

Let
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
be a
K
-spherical unitary
G
-representation. Then for every
v\mathchar 59\relax w\in\mathcal{H}^{K}
there is a unique complex-valued Borel measure
\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
with total variation
|\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}|(\mathcal{S}^{+})\leq\lVert v\rVert\lVert w\rVert
such that

\displaystyle\langle\pi(g)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\omega(g)d\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}(\omega)

for all
g\in G
. When
v=w
, the measure
\sigma_{v}:=\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax v}
is in addition a positive measure.

2.6.
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}
-action on the spherical unitary dual

If
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
is a unitary
G
-representation, it can be lifted to a bounded Banach
*
-algebra representation
\pi:L^{1}(G)\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H})
, uniquely defined by

\displaystyle\langle\pi(\beta)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{G}\beta(g)\langle\pi(g)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}dm_{G}(g)

for all
g\in G
and all
v\mathchar 59\relax w\in\mathcal{H}
. If
\beta\in L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
,
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
is
K
-spherical and

, Corollary
2.5
allows us to write this matrix coefficient as

(2.4)

\displaystyle\langle\pi(\beta)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\widehat{\beta}(\omega)d\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}(\omega)\,.

We will now extend this family of operators associated to functions in
L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
to functions in
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
, following the same argument as in
[
21
, Prop. 2.58, p. 115]
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
be a
K
-spherical unitary
G
-representation. Then the map
\widehat{\pi}:\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})\rightarrow\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}^{K})
given implicitly by

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)d\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}(\omega)

for all
v\mathchar 59\relax w\in\mathcal{H}^{K}
is a well-defined
*
-algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

If
\beta\in L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, then we set
\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\beta})=\pi(\beta)
. Since the image of
L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
under the spherical transform is dense in
C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
as mentioned in Subsection
2.4
, every
\phi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
can be approximated pointwise by some sequence
\widehat{\beta}_{n}\in C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
with
\beta_{n}\in L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. Since
\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}
has finite total variation by Corollary
2.5
for every
v\mathchar 59\relax w\in\mathcal{H}^{K}
, Equation
2.4
and dominated convergence implies that

\displaystyle\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi)v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}:=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\langle\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\beta}_{n})v\mathchar 59\relax w\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\widehat{\beta}_{n}(\omega)d\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)d\sigma_{v\mathchar 59\relax w}(\omega)\,.

Moreover,
\mathscr{B}(\mathcal{H}^{K})
is preserved under weak limits, so we see that
\widehat{\pi}(\phi)
defines a bounded linear operator on
\mathcal{H}^{K}
. To see that this yields a
*
-representation, note that

\displaystyle\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\beta}_{1})\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\beta}_{2})^{*}=\pi(\beta_{1})\pi(\beta_{2})^{*}=\pi(\beta_{1}*\beta_{2}^{*})=\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\beta}_{1}\overline{\widehat{\beta}_{2}})

for all
\beta_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\beta_{2}\in L^{1}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, so if
\phi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\phi_{2}\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
then using the same limiting argument as before yields
\widehat{\pi}(\phi_{1})\widehat{\pi}(\phi_{2})^{*}=\widehat{\pi}(\phi_{1}\overline{\phi_{2}})
.
∎

3.
Spectral measures

We prove existence and uniqueness of spectral measures as given in Theorem
3.2
stated below, and compute the mass of such spectral measures at the trivial spherical function
1\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
in Subsection
3.4
.

Let
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
be a lcsc Gelfand pair with
K
compact and let
\mathscr{A}(G)\subset\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)
be a
\lambda_{G}
-invariant
*
-subalgebra such that the
*
-algebra of bi-
K
-averaged functions
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)=\mathscr{A}(G)^{\natural}
is contained in
\mathscr{A}(G)
.

Assumption 1
: We will assume that for every spherical function
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, there is a function
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
such that

\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)\neq 0\,.

Remark 3.1
.

The
\lambda_{G}
-invariance of
\mathscr{A}(G)
together with Assumption 1 implies that spherical transforms of functions in
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
separate points in
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. To see this, let
\omega_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\omega_{2}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
and suppose
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
such that
\widehat{\varphi}(\omega_{1})=\widehat{\varphi}(\omega_{2})\neq 0
. Taking

such that
\omega_{1}(g)\neq\omega_{2}(g)
and considering
(\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi)^{\natural}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, then a computation utilizing the functional equation in Equation
2.1
yields

\displaystyle\widehat{(\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi)^{\natural}}(\omega_{1})=\omega_{1}(g^{-1})\widehat{\varphi}(\omega_{1})\neq\omega_{2}(g^{-1})\widehat{\varphi}(\omega_{2})=\widehat{(\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi)^{\natural}}(\omega_{2})\,.

Moreover, fix a
K
-spherical unitary
G
-representation
(\pi\mathchar 59\relax\mathcal{H})
. We will consider a linear
G
-equivariant map
\alpha:\mathscr{A}(G)\rightarrow\mathcal{H}
, meaning

\displaystyle\alpha(\lambda_{G}(g)\varphi)=\pi(g)\alpha(\varphi)

for all
g\in G
and all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G)
.
Note that
\alpha
maps
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
to
\mathcal{H}^{K}
. We moreover assume that
\alpha
is a linear

-map in the sense that

\displaystyle\alpha(\lambda_{G}(\beta)\varphi)=\pi(\beta)\alpha(\varphi)

for all
\beta\in L^{1}(G)
. The goal of this Section is to prove the following refinement of Bochner’s Theorem.

Theorem 3.2
.

There is a unique positive Radon measure
\sigma_{\alpha}\in\mathscr{M}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
such that

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha(\varphi)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)

for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
.

Remark 3.3

(The spherical Plancherel measure)
.

If
\mathscr{A}(G)\subset\mathscr{L}^{1}(G)\cap\mathscr{L}^{2}(G)
satsfies Assumption 1 and
\alpha:\mathscr{A}(G)\rightarrow L^{2}(G)
is the canonical map, then the
spherical Plancherel measure

\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}:=\sigma_{\alpha}
defined through Theorem
3.2
is the unique positive Radon measure on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
satisfying

\displaystyle\lVert\varphi\rVert_{L^{2}(G)}^{2}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}(\omega)

for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
.

3.1.
Mutual absolute continuity of Bochner measures

By Bochner’s Theorem, Theorem
2.3
, we know that for every
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
there is a unique finite positive Borel measure
\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi)}
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle\langle\pi(g)\alpha(\varphi)\mathchar 59\relax\alpha(\varphi)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\omega(g)d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi)}(\omega)\,.

Theorem
3.2
is then equivalent to showing that there is a unique positive Radon measure
\sigma_{\alpha}
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that

\displaystyle d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi)}(\omega)=|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)

as measures for all
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
.

The first step in proving Theorem
3.2
is to show that for
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, the measure
\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})}
is absolutely continuous with respect to the measures
\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1})}
and
\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{2})}
. The precise statement is the following.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. Then for every
\phi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{2})}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi\,d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi|\widehat{\varphi}_{2}|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1})}\,.

In particular,
|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2}\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{2})}=|\widehat{\varphi}_{2}|^{2}\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1})}
as measures on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
.

Proof.

Since
(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
is Gelfand and
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, then
\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2}=\varphi_{2}*\varphi_{1}
, so it suffices to prove the first equality in the statement. To do this we use Bochner’s Theorem for matrix coefficients in Corollary
2.5
and the
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
-homomorphism
\widehat{\pi}
from Lemma
2.6
to write

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi\,d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})}=\Big\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})\mathchar 59\relax\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

From the
L^{1}(G)
-equivariance of
\alpha
,

\displaystyle\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})=\alpha(\lambda_{G}(\varphi_{1})\varphi_{2})=\pi(\varphi_{1})\alpha(\varphi_{2})\,\mathchar 59\relax

so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi\,d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})\mathchar 59\relax\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\pi(\varphi_{1})\alpha(\varphi_{2})\mathchar 59\relax\pi(\varphi_{1})\alpha(\varphi_{2})\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\pi(\varphi_{1}^{*})\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\pi(\varphi_{1})\alpha(\varphi_{2})\mathchar 59\relax\alpha(\varphi_{2})\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}\,.

By definition of the
\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
-homomorphism
\widehat{\pi}
, the operator in the latter inner product can be rewritten using Lemma
2.6
as

\displaystyle\pi(\varphi_{1}^{*})\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\pi(\varphi_{1})=\widehat{\pi}(\overline{\widehat{\varphi}_{1}})\widehat{\pi}(\phi)\widehat{\pi}(\widehat{\varphi}_{1})=\widehat{\pi}(\phi|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2})\,.

Finally, using Bochner’s Theorem for matrix coefficents in Corollary
2.5
again, we get that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi\,d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1}*\varphi_{2})}

\displaystyle=\Big\langle\widehat{\pi}(\phi|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2})\alpha(\varphi_{2})\mathchar 59\relax\alpha(\varphi_{2})\Big\rangle_{\mathcal{H}}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{2})}\,.

∎

3.2.
Extending to the cone of positive spherical transforms

From Lemma
3.4
we know that
|\widehat{\varphi}_{1}|^{2}\cdot\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{2})}=|\widehat{\varphi}_{2}|^{2}\cdot\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{1})}
as finite measures on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
for all
\varphi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\varphi_{2}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
. In proving Theorem
3.2
we need to extend this identity to what we will call the
cone of positive spherical transforms
for the algebra
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
, defined as

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})=\Big\{\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\widehat{\varphi}_{i}|^{2}\in C_{0}(\mathcal{S}^{+}):\varphi_{i}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)\,\,\forall\,i=1\mathchar 59\relax\dots n\Big\}\,.

If
F=\sum_{i=1}^{n}|\widehat{\varphi}_{i}|^{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
then we define a finite positive Borel measure
\sigma_{F}
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
by

\displaystyle\sigma_{F}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{i})}\,.

Note that if
F_{1}\mathchar 59\relax F_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
are of the form

\displaystyle F_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\widehat{\varphi}_{i}|^{2}\quad\mbox{ and }F_{2}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widehat{\psi}_{j}|^{2}

then by Lemma
3.4
,

\displaystyle F_{1}\cdot\sigma_{F_{2}}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widehat{\varphi}_{i}|^{2}\sigma_{\alpha(\psi_{j})}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\sum_{j=1}^{n}|\widehat{\psi}_{j}|^{2}\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi_{i})}=F_{2}\cdot\sigma_{F_{1}}\,.

as measures on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. More concretely, we have that for every
F_{1}\mathchar 59\relax F_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
and every

,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)F_{1}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{2}}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)F_{2}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{1}}(\omega)\,.

Note that this equality of measures implies that

\mathrm{supp}(\sigma_{F_{1}})\subset\mathrm{supp}(F_{1})\quad\mbox{ and }\quad\mathrm{supp}(\sigma_{F_{2}})\subset\mathrm{supp}(F_{2})\,.

The remaining tool for the proof of Theorem
3.2
is the following Lemma, which is where we will make use of the Assumption 1 for
\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
in the beginning of Section
3
.

Lemma 3.5
.

For every compact subset
Q\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}
there is an
F\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
such that
F(\omega)>0
for all
\omega\in Q
.

Proof.

By Assumption 1 in the beginning of Section
3
, there is for every
\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
a
\varphi_{\omega}\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
such that
\widehat{\varphi}_{\omega}(\omega)\neq 0
, so that
|\widehat{\varphi}_{\omega}(\omega)|^{2}>0
. Since
\widehat{\varphi}_{\omega}
is continuous, there is an open subset
U_{\omega}\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that
|\widehat{\varphi}_{\omega}(\omega^{\prime})|^{2}>0
for all
\omega^{\prime}\in U_{\omega}
, which yields an open cover

\displaystyle Q\subset\bigcup_{\omega\in Q}U_{\omega}\,.

Since
Q
is compact, there are
\omega_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\dots\mathchar 59\relax\omega_{n}\in Q
such that

\displaystyle Q\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}U_{\omega_{i}}\,\mathchar 59\relax

so taking

finishes the proof.
∎

3.3.
Proof of Theorem
3.2

We now prove Theorem
3.2
using an argument that can be found for example in
[
30
, Section 26J-26K, p.98-100]
.

Proof of Theorem
3.2
.

As stated in Equation
3.1
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)F_{1}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{2}}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)F_{2}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{1}}(\omega)

for all
\phi\in\mathscr{L}^{\infty}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
and all
F_{1}\mathchar 59\relax F_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
. By Lemma
3.5
we may for each
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
take a function
F_{\phi}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
that is strictly positive on the compact support
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}
and consider

\displaystyle\sigma_{\alpha}(\phi):=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F_{\phi}}(\omega)}{F_{\phi}(\omega)}\,.

We claim that
\sigma_{\alpha}
is a well-defined positive continuous linear functional on
C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
with respect to the inductive topology over compact subsets.

First, to see that
\sigma_{\alpha}
is well-defined, suppose that
F_{1}\mathchar 59\relax F_{2}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
are strictly positive on
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)
. Then
\phi/F_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\phi/F_{2}
and
\phi/(F_{1}F_{2})
are compactly supported and continuous, so by Equation
3.1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F_{1}}(\omega)}{F_{1}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{F_{2}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{1}}(\omega)}{F_{2}(\omega)F_{1}(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{F_{1}(\omega)d\sigma_{F_{2}}(\omega)}{F_{2}(\omega)F_{1}(\omega)}=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F_{2}}(\omega)}{F_{2}(\omega)}\,.

To see that
\sigma_{\alpha}
is linear, let
\phi_{1}\mathchar 59\relax\phi_{2}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
,
c_{1}\mathchar 59\relax c_{2}\in\mathbb{C}
and let
F\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
be strictly positive on
\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{1})\cup\mathrm{supp}(\phi_{2})\supset\mathrm{supp}(c_{1}\phi_{1}+c_{2}\phi_{2})
, so that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}(c_{1}\phi_{1}(\omega)+c_{2}\phi_{2}(\omega))\frac{d\sigma_{F}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}=c_{1}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi_{1}(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}+c_{2}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi_{2}(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}\,.

For the continuity of
\sigma_{\alpha}
, note that if
Q\subset\mathcal{S}^{+}
is compact and
F_{Q}\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
is strictly positive on
Q
, then

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F_{Q}}(\omega)}{F_{Q}(\omega)}\Big|\leq\frac{\sigma_{F_{Q}}(\mathcal{S}^{+})}{\min_{\omega\in Q}F_{Q}(\omega)}\lVert\phi\rVert_{\infty}

for all
\phi\in C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\phi)\subset Q
.
We conclude that
\sigma_{\alpha}
is a positive continuous linear functional on
C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
, and hence a positive Radon measure on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
by the Riesz representation Theorem.

Next, we claim that
\sigma_{F}=F\cdot\sigma_{\alpha}
as measures on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
for any
F\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
. To see this, let
\phi\in C_{b}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
be a positive bounded and continuous function and
F\in\mathscr{C}_{+}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
. Take an increasing sequence of positive functions
\chi_{n}\in C_{c}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
such that the compact support
\mathrm{supp}(\chi_{n})
is contained in the open subset
U_{F}=\{\omega\in\mathcal{S}^{+}:F(\omega)>0\}
and such that
\chi_{n}\rightarrow 1
uniformly on
U_{F}
as
n\rightarrow+\infty
. Then
F
is strictly positive on the compact support of
F\cdot\chi_{n}
, so by the monotone convergence Theorem we see that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)d\sigma_{F}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\int_{U_{F}}\phi(\omega)F(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{U_{F}}\phi(\omega)F(\omega)\chi_{n}(\omega)\frac{d\sigma_{F}(\omega)}{F(\omega)}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\rightarrow+\infty}\int_{U_{F}}\phi(\omega)F(\omega)\chi_{n}(\omega)d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\phi(\omega)F(\omega)d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)\,\mathchar 59\relax

which proves the claim.
Finally, the conclusion of Theorem
3.2
now follows by taking
F=|\widehat{\varphi}|^{2}
for
\varphi\in\mathscr{A}(G\mathchar 59\relax K)
,

\displaystyle\lVert\alpha(\varphi)\rVert_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}d\sigma_{\alpha(\varphi)}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}d\sigma_{F}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}F(\omega)d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma_{\alpha}(\omega)\,.

∎

3.4.

In this Subsection we show that the spectral measure

Assumption 2

Lemma 3.7

Let

\mathcal{K}^{\tau(\beta)}=\mathcal{K}^{G}

Proof.

First, if

Conversely, if

Since

-invariant vectors are

Lemma 3.8

Proof of Proposition

On the other hand, if

4.

The commutative metric spaces that we consider are introduced in the first Subsection, and in Subsection

4.1.
Commutative spaces

Let

4.2.
Invariant random measures

Denote by
\mathscr{M}_{+}(X)

5.1.
Upper and lower bounds for counting statistics

Let

Lemma 5.1

Let

be an open pre-compact symmetric neighbourhood of the identity. Then for every Borel set

with

\displaystyle(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}})(s(x))\leq\chi_{D}(x)\leq(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}})(s(x))

Proof.

For the first inequality, a change of variables yields

\displaystyle(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}})(s(x))=\int_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}}\rho_{V}(s(x)g^{-1})dm_{G}(g)=\int_{s(x)(\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-})^{-1}\cap V}\rho_{V}(g)dm_{G}(g)\,.

\displaystyle(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}})(s(x))=\chi_{D}(x)\int_{s(x)(\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-})^{-1}\cap V}\rho_{V}(g)dm_{G}(g)\leq\chi_{D}(x)\,.

For the second inequality, we similarly write

\displaystyle(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}})(s(x))=\int_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}}\rho_{V}(s(x)g^{-1})dm_{G}(g)=\int_{s(x)(\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+})^{-1}\cap V}\rho_{V}(g)dm_{G}(g)\,.

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}(P_{K}(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}}))\leq\mathbb{S}\chi_{D}\leq\mathbb{S}(P_{K}(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}}))

from above to produce the following upper bound on the number variance of
D

\displaystyle\lVert\mathbb{S}_{0}\chi_{D}\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}\leq\lVert\mathbb{S}_{0}(P_{K}(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}}))\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}+\lVert\mathbb{S}_{0}(P_{K}(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}}))\rVert_{L^{2}(\mu)}+i_{\mu}m_{G}(\widetilde{D}_{V}^{+}\backslash\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-})\,.

The inequalities in Equation

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{0}(P_{K}(\rho_{V}*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}}))+i_{\mu}(m_{G}(\rho_{V}

\displaystyle*\chi_{\widetilde{D}_{V}^{-}})-m_{G}(\widetilde{D}))

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{S}_{0}\chi_{D}

Lemma 6.1

is

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}_{\mu_{\sigma}}(\mathbb{S}f)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}|\widehat{\varphi}_{f}(\omega)|^{2}d\sigma(\omega)\,.

∎

A.4.
Heat kernel hyperuniformity on Euclidean and real hyperbolic spaces

A.4.1.
Spectral hyperuniformity

The Euclidean setting
: The positive-definite spherical functions on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
are precisely the characters
x\mapsto\mathrm{e}^{-i\langle x\mathchar 59\relax y\rangle}
with
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and the Bartlett spectral measure
\sigma_{\mu}
of a translation invariant locally square-integrable distribution can be shown to be a positive tempered measure using the Paley-Wiener Theorem. Thus we restrict our attention to translation invariant random tempered distributions
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Spectral hyperuniformity of a random (tempered) distribution is defined as follows.

Definition A.4

(Euclidean spectral hyperuniformity)
.

A translation invariant locally square-integrable random (tempered) distribution is
spectrally hyperuniform
if

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{\mu}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}{\mathrm{Vol}_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(B_{\varepsilon}(0))}=0\,.

This definition equivalent to that of geometric hyperuniformity when
\mu
is a translation invariant locally square-integrable random measure on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, see
[

. If we let
\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}

\sigma_{\mu}
on

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}F(\lambda)d\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\lambda)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}F(\lVert y\rVert)d\sigma_{\mu}(y)\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R})^{\mathrm{even}}

then spectral hyperuniformity of
\mu
can equivalently be formulated as

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma^{\mathrm{rad}}_{\mu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\sigma_{\mathcal{P}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}=0\,\mathchar 59\relax

where

\mathrm{SO}(d)\ltimes\mathbb{R}^{d}

as

The real hyperbolic setting
: Consider real

\displaystyle\omega_{\lambda}(g)=\frac{2^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{d}{2})}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})}\sinh(\ell(g))^{2-d}\int_{0}^{\ell(g)}(\cosh(\ell(g))-\cosh(s))^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\cos(\lambda s)ds\,.

Theorem A.5

(Trombi-Varadarajan, real hyperbolic space)

The spherical transform extends to a

Since

The following definition of spectral hyperuniformity was introduced for random measures on

Definition A.6

Here, the spherical Plancherel measure is

A.4.2.
A general rank one criteria for spectral hyperuniformity

To summarize the previous Subsubsection, spectral hyperuniformity of an invariant locally square-integrable random distribution
\mu
on
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}\mathchar 59\relax\mathbb{H}^{d}
requires that the Bartlett spectral measure
\sigma_{\mu}
is supported on
(0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty)
and that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma_{\mu}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}=0

where
\alpha=d
for
X=\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\alpha=3
for
X=\mathbb{H}^{d}
. We prove the following reformulation of this property.

Proposition A.7
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is an even Schwartz function which does not vanish on
[-1\mathchar 59\relax 1]
and let
\sigma\in\mathscr{M}_{+}((0\mathchar 59\relax+\infty))
be a tempered measure. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\frac{\sigma((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])}{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}=0

if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}s^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(s\lambda)^{2}d\sigma(\lambda)=0\,.

The heart of the proof of this Proposition lies in the following technical Lemma.

Lemma A.8
.

Suppose that there are constants
\alpha\mathchar 59\relax\beta>0
,
\gamma\in(0\mathchar 59\relax 1)
,

and Borel functions

(i)

is increasing and such that

•

there is an

with

, and such that
\theta(r)\leq A\varepsilon r^{\alpha}
for all

•

there is a constant

\theta(r)\leq Br^{\beta}
for all

(ii)
\psi
is such that

(1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}\psi(r)dr<+\infty

(2)

(3)

Then

\displaystyle r_{\varepsilon}^{-(\alpha+1)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})\theta(r)dr\longrightarrow 0\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}\,.

Proof.

Let
I(\varepsilon)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})\theta(r)dr
and split the integral as
I(\varepsilon)=I_{1}(\varepsilon)+I_{2}(\varepsilon)+I_{3}(\varepsilon)
, where

\displaystyle I_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{r_{\varepsilon}^{1-\gamma}}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})\theta(r)dr

\displaystyle I_{2}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{r_{\varepsilon}^{1-\gamma}}^{M}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})\theta(r)dr

\displaystyle I_{3}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}^{\infty}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})\theta(r)dr\,.

By the assumptions on
\theta

\displaystyle I_{1}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq A\varepsilon\int_{0}^{r_{\varepsilon}^{1-\gamma}}r^{\alpha}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})dr\leq A\varepsilon r_{\varepsilon}^{\alpha+1}\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}\psi(r)dr\,\mathchar 59\relax

\displaystyle I_{2}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\theta(M)\int_{r_{\varepsilon}^{1-\gamma}}^{M}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})dr\leq\theta(M)r_{\varepsilon}\int_{r_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma}}^{\infty}\psi(r)dr

\displaystyle I_{3}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq B\int_{M}^{\infty}r^{\beta}\psi(r/r_{\varepsilon})dr=Br_{\varepsilon}^{\beta+1}\int_{Mr_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}^{\infty}r^{\beta}\psi(r)dr\,.

Dividing by

and using the assumptions made on

\displaystyle r_{\varepsilon}^{-(\alpha+1)}I(\varepsilon)\leq A\varepsilon\int_{0}^{\infty}r^{\alpha}\psi(r)dr

\displaystyle+\theta(M)(r_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma})^{\alpha/\gamma}\int_{r_{\varepsilon}^{-\gamma}}^{\infty}\psi(r)dr

\displaystyle+BM^{\beta-\alpha}(Mr_{\varepsilon}^{-1})^{\alpha-\beta}\int_{Mr_{\varepsilon}^{-1}}^{\infty}r^{\beta}\psi(r)dr\longrightarrow 0

Proof of Proposition

For the right implication, set

A.4.3.
Euclidean heat kernel hyperuniformity

The Euclidean heat kernel is the family of functions
h_{\tau}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with
\tau>0
given by

\displaystyle h_{\tau}(x)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{4\tau}}\,\mathchar 59\relax

or equivalently defined by its Fourier transform as

\displaystyle\widehat{h}_{\tau}(y)=\frac{1}{(4\pi\tau)^{d/2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\lVert x\rVert^{2}}{4\tau}}\mathrm{e}^{-i\langle x\mathchar 59\relax y\rangle}dx=\mathrm{e}^{-\tau\lVert y\rVert^{2}}\,\mathchar 59\relax\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\,.

Theorem A.9
.

An invariant locally square-integrable random tempered distribution
\mu
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is spectrally hyperuniform if and only if

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\,\tau^{d/2}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})=0\,.

Proof.

Since the heat kernel
h_{\tau}
is radial, the variance of its linear statistic with respect to
\mu
can be written in terms of the radialized Bartlett spectral measure
\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}
as

\displaystyle\tau^{d/2}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})=\tau^{d/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{h}_{\tau}(\lambda)^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\lambda)=\tau^{d/2}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2\tau\lambda^{2}}d\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\lambda)\,.

As mentioned, the measure
\sigma_{\mu}
is tempered as a consequence of the Paley-Wiener Theorem, so letting
s=\sqrt{\tau}
,
\alpha=d
,
F(t)=\mathrm{e}^{-t^{2}}
, then by Proposition
A.7
we have that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\tau^{d/2}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}s^{\alpha}\int_{0}^{\infty}F(s\lambda)^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}(\lambda)=0

if and only if
\sigma_{\mu}^{\mathrm{rad}}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])=o(\varepsilon^{d})
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
.
∎

A.4.4.
Hyperbolic heat kernel hyperuniformity

The heat kernel

\displaystyle h_{\tau}(g)=\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-t(\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{4}+\lambda^{2})}\omega_{\lambda}(g)\frac{d\lambda}{|c_{d}(\lambda)|^{2}}\,.

\displaystyle h_{\tau}(g)\asymp\tau^{-\frac{d}{2}}(1+\tau+\ell(g))^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(1+\ell(g))\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{4}\tau-\frac{d-1}{2}\ell(g)-\frac{1}{4\tau}\ell(g)}

and all

Theorem A.10
.

An invariant locally square-integrable random Harish-Chandra distribution
\mu

\mathbb{H}^{d}

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\,\tau^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})=0\,.

Proof.

The proof is similar to the Euclidean case in Theorem
A.9

\begin{split}\tau^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})&=\tau^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\Big(\int_{0}^{\infty}\widehat{h}_{\tau}(\lambda)^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)+\int_{0}^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\widehat{h}_{\tau}(i\lambda)^{2}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}(\lambda)\Big)\\

where
\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}
and
\sigma_{\mu}^{(c)}

and

First, assume that

\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}((0\mathchar 59\relax\varepsilon])=o(\varepsilon^{3})

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow+\infty}\,\tau^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(d-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})=\lim_{s\rightarrow+\infty}s^{3}\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-2(s\lambda)^{2}}d\sigma_{\mu}^{(p)}(\lambda)=0\,.

\tau^{3/2}\mathrm{e}^{\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{2}\tau}\mathrm{Var}_{\mu}(\mathbb{S}h_{\tau})\rightarrow 0

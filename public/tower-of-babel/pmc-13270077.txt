Non‐Cancer Causes of Death in Patients With Metastatic Hormone‐Sensitive Prostate Cancer: A Real‐World Analysis
Authors: Marta Garcia de Herreros, Clara Rodrigo, Laura Ferrer‐Mileo, Laia Fernández‐Mañas, Joel Mases, Nely Diaz, Antoni Vilaseca, Mariana Altamirano, Roser Gibanel, Maria José Ribal, Natalia Jimenez, Oscar Reig, Mariona Figols, Miguel Ángel Climent, Albert Font, Begoña Mellado
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ABSTRACTBackgroundAs overall survival improves in metastatic hormone‐sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC), non‐cancer causes of death are increasingly relevant.MethodsWe conducted a real‐world multicenter study of patients diagnosed with mHSPC treated with ADT alone or in combination. Causes of death before progression to castration‐resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) were classified as cancer‐ or non‐cancer‐related. Deaths were classified as non‐cancer‐related if they occurred in the absence of cancer progression.ResultsAmong 565 patients, 35 (6.2%) died from non‐cancer‐related causes without disease progression, mainly infections (34%) and cardiovascular events (20%).ConclusionsNon‐cancer mortality represents a meaningful proportion of deaths in mHSPC, supporting systematic cardiovascular risk evaluation and infection‐prevention strategies.

Introduction

As therapeutic intensification with docetaxel and androgen receptor pathway inhibitors (ARPIs) improves metastatic hormone‐sensitive prostate cancer (mHSPC) outcomes [1, 2], a growing proportion of deaths in this population arise from non‐cancer causes [3, 4]. Men with mHSPC usually present at a median age of around 70 years and frequently have comorbidities such as cardiovascular disease, chronic lung disease, metabolic syndromes, or chronic infections [5]. Androgen deprivation therapy (ADT) and ARPIs may exacerbate several risk factors (weight gain, dyslipidemia, hypertension, and insulin resistance) potentially increasing cardiovascular risk [6]. Besides, they induce bone loss and reduced muscle mass, which may also worsen frailty, reduce mobility, and contribute to vulnerability to infectious complications [7].

Population‐based studies suggest that 20%–40% of deaths among men with advanced prostate cancer are unrelated to malignancy, yet contemporary real‐world data specifically describing non‐cancer mortality in the mHSPC setting remain scarce. Understanding these patterns is increasingly important as more patients receive prolonged, intensified systemic therapy, underscoring the need for proactive prevention strategies.

We conducted an ambispective study characterizing non‐cancer deaths in mHSPC, aiming to identify their frequency, timing, and major contributing factors.

Methods

All patients diagnosed with mHSPC between January 2010 and January 2025 were included in an ongoing multicenter biomarker study. Here, we report the mortality data. Eligible patients received one of the following first‐line treatments: ADT alone, ADT plus docetaxel, ADT plus an ARPI, or triplet therapy (ADT plus docetaxel and an ARPI). Data extracted from electronic medical records included demographic information, comorbidities, functional status (ECOG performance status and Barthel Index), treatment details, and date and cause of death. Functional status was retrospectively collected from patients' electronic medical records, from the most recent clinical visit prior to initiation of first‐line treatment, when available. Comorbidities were categorized into six predefined groups: cardiovascular, respiratory, neuropsychiatric, rheumatologic, renal, and others. Causes of death were categorized as prostate cancer‐related or non‐cancer‐related. Non‐cancer‐related deaths were defined as those occurring during treatment for mHSPC without progression to castration‐resistant prostate cancer (CRPC) and were subclassified as infectious, cardiovascular, unknown, or miscellaneous. Overall survival (OS) was calculated from the date of mHSPC diagnosis to death, and CRPC‐free survival (CRPC‐FS) was defined as the time from mHSPC diagnosis to the development of CRPC, based on either radiological or PSA progression, whichever occurred first. Kaplan–Meier methodology was used for OS and CRPC‐FS estimates. Univariate and multivariate analyses of variables of interest were performed by a Cox regression analysis. Kruskal–Wallis tests compared continuous variables across cause‐of‐death categories; Fisher's exact test compared categorical variables.

Results

Of 565 patients with mHSPC, 93 (16.5%) received ADT alone, 125 (22.1%) ADT + docetaxel, 321 (56.8%) ADT + ARPI, and 26 (4.6%) triplet therapy. Median follow‐up was 44.6 months (range 4.5–228.9). Median CRPC‐FS and OS for the global cohort were 26.2 (95% CI 20.8–30.2) and 51.8 months (95% CI 44.9–61.3), respectively. Overall, 381 patients (67.4%) died. Of these, 35 deaths were attributed to non‐prostate cancer, accounting for 6.2% of all patients and 9.2% of all deaths, and all occurred before progression to CRPC (Figure S1). Among these patients, the median age was 77 years (range 51–92). Notably, 68,6% had metachronous disease, 65.7% low volume disease, and 8.6% had visceral metastases. Over half of the patients (51.4%) had ≥ 3 comorbidities, predominantly cardiovascular (91.4%) (Table S1). Most of the patients received ADT + ARPI (29/35, 82.9%), whereas fewer were treated with ADT + docetaxel (3/35, 8.6%), triplet therapy (1/35, 2.9%), or ADT alone (1/35, 2.9%). Median OS in the non‐cancer death group was 16 months (95% CI 12.6–43.8). The main causes of death were infectious (12/35, 34.3%), cardiovascular (7/35, 20%), noninfectious respiratory (3/35, 8.6%), and other causes detailed in Table 1. No deaths were associated with febrile neutropenia or were chemotherapy‐related. Most patients (25/35, 71%) were receiving active systemic therapy at the time of death, most often ADT + ARPI (12/25, 48%). Among these patients, 13.5% had previously required dose reductions. No significant association was observed between treatment type and cause of death (data not shown). Of note, 30 patients (85.7%) had undetectable PSA, defined as < 0.04 ng/mL, at the time of death.

Patients who died from infectious causes tended to be older and have poorer functional status than those who died from cardiovascular causes (median age 84 vs. 72 years, p = 0.05; median Barthel index 60 vs. 100, p = 0.03) (Table S2). All patients who died from cardiovascular causes had established cardiovascular risk factors (88.6% hypertension, 77.1% dyslipidemia, and 28.6% diabetes). Respiratory deaths occurred in patients with the highest comorbidity burden (median 5). Interestingly, patients with 3 or more comorbidities had a higher risk of early non‐cancer‐related death (univariate Cox HR 2.1; 95% CI 1.2–3.7, p = 0.039).

Discussion

In this large real‐world mHSPC cohort, non‐cancer causes accounted for a relevant proportion of early mortality, preceding CRPC progression. Infections and cardiovascular events were the predominant contributors, in line with previous evidence [3, 4, 8]. The absence of deaths related to febrile neutropenia supports the safety of docetaxel‐based intensification in appropriately selected patients.

Cardiovascular deaths occurred in younger patients with better functional status, consistent with concerns that ADT and ARPIs may accelerate cardiometabolic toxicity in predisposed individuals [6, 9]. Importantly, all patients in this subgroup had pre‐existing cardiovascular risk factors. These observations underscore the need for structured cardiovascular evaluation at baseline, including screening for hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, and prior ischemic disease. Early multidisciplinary management, ideally incorporating a cardio‐oncologist, may reduce preventable mortality [9]. Moreover, promoting healthy lifestyles and exercise programs may also be beneficial [10]. Infectious deaths were more frequent among older and frailer patients with higher comorbidity burden. This pattern highlights the importance of vaccination (influenza, pneumococcal, COVID‐19), comprehensive functional assessment, smoking‐cessation support, and early interventions to address frailty and sarcopenia.

Recent studies have proposed treatment de‐escalation or intermittent strategies in patients achieving profound PSA responses [11]. In our cohort, nearly 85% of patients had undetectable PSA at the time of death. These findings provide additional support for considering treatment intermittence in individuals with substantial comorbidity burden who achieve deep biochemical responses and had low risk/volume disease [11].

This study is limited by its retrospective design, potential heterogeneity in clinical practice across centers, and the need for better characterization of risk factors associated with non‐cancer mortality in mHSPC, their impact in different patient populations, and the duration and type of oncologic treatments involved.

Overall, these real‐world data reinforce the importance of assessing comorbidities and life expectancy when initiating systemic therapy in mHSPC. Integrating cardiovascular risk assessment, infection‐prevention measures, lifestyle education, and tailored follow‐up may help reduce non‐cancer mortality in this growing patient population.

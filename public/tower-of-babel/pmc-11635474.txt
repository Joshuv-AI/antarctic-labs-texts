ABSTRACT
AbstractAtherosclerosis (ASC) is characterized by foam cell-mediated plaque formation, vascular endothelial inflammation, and lipidosis and is the rudimentary cause of cardiovascular diseases. This is the pre-eminent global factor of mortality. This etiological paradigm is significantly influenced by several proteins, where 23 pivotal proteins involved in ASC were meticulously gleaned on the basis of literature studies. The crux of the present study was aimed to probe the drugability of four active phytochemicals from Crataegus oxyacantha (COC): epicatechin, gallate, tyramine, and vitexin against the selected 23 proteins. The molecular docking analysis was judiciously administered via Glide, the binding free energy was calculated in detail utilizing the prime molecular mechanics-generalized Born surface area (MM-GBSA) module, and a deeper comprehensive investigation of protein–ligand dynamic associations was elucidated through Desmond. Drawing from the upper echelons of our docking results, the molecular dynamics simulation outcomes revealed that the macrophage migration inhibitory factor and prethrombin-1 showed persistent binding nature with gallate. The bioactive compound known as gallate sourced from COC shows the best molecular association with pivotal proteins involved in ASC and has a promising therapeutic potential for drug development endeavors.

Introduction

Atherosclerosis (ASC) persists as the predominant cause of mortality globally, and the prevalence of myocardial infarction and stroke is augmenting in emerging nations.1 ASC is the predominant etiological factor of cardiovascular and cerebrovascular diseases that ensue from the agglomeration of atheromatous within arterial walls.2,3 The subsequent encumbrance of arterial lumens precipitates diminished perfusion of organs and tissues, which potentially culminates in Type 1 and Type 2 myocardial infarction and stroke. Concomitantly, lipid metabolism and transport proteins, structural and binding proteins, blood clotting and coagulation proteins, and inflammatory markers and mediators contribute to coronary thrombus pathogenesis.4−6 Amid the progression of atherosclerosis, lipids, inflammatory cells, smooth muscle cells, and necrotic detritus accrue within the intimal stratum of the vasculature subjacent to a protective lamina of endothelial cells.7−9 Atherogenesis is significantly influenced by several signaling proteins and transporters involved in lipid metabolism.10,11 Furthermore, structural and binding proteins such as collagen and elastin aid in maintaining the stability of the artery wall and preclude rupture.12 Additionally, fibrinogen and thrombin play an important role in hemostasis. However, if it is dysregulated, it may result in thrombus formation.13 Moreover, inflammatory markers and mediators such as cytokines and chemokines incite immune cells to migrate toward the site of the injury, but they may also aggravate inflammation and contribute to plaque instability.14,15 As a consequence, several factors contribute to the pathophysiology of thrombus development in coronary arteries.

Despite the fact that coronary thrombosis can be treated through several diagnostic treatments, there are still several impediments and constraints to conquering this condition.4,16 There are several medicaments that target the pathogenesis of coronary artery maladies that are currently accessible such as coumarin, fibrates, aspirin, and warfarin, but they are associated with several deleterious effects including liver malfunction, muscle toxicity, and serious bleeding complications.17 In addition, anti-inflammatory medicaments are also associated with immunosuppression and gastrointestinal ulceration, indicating the exigency of finding an alternative.18 Moreover, the major inflammatory makers targeting drugs such as nonsteroidal anti-inflammatory and corticosteroids exhibit adverse effects, including immunosuppressive effects and gastrointestinal ulcers, and this scenario indicates the necessity of finding an alternative medicament that can modulate these proteins without causing a significant side effect.19

Conventionally, plants are utilized to remediate multifarious maladies encompassing cardiocirculatory afflictions on account of the fact that composites extracted from plants exhibit a contingently beneficial pharmacological property.20 Phytoconstituents can facilely effectuate a propitious interaction with disparate cytological targets such as receptors, enzymes, transcriptional factors, and other signaling constituents.21,22 Additionally, phytoconstituents inhibit atherosclerotic thrombogenesis conceivably through propitious metabolic modification in animal models and clinical trials.23

Crataegus oxyacantha (COC) has been used as a herbal remedy for various cardiovascular disorders over the centuries.24,25 Therefore, this study focuses on the four phytochemicals present in COC (hawthorn), namely, epicatechin, gallate, tyramine, and vitexin. These studies postulate that these four phytocompounds possess cardio-beneficial attributes and that their pharmaceutic feasibility can be ascertained by associations with the principal proteins engrossed in the causation of atherogenesis.26,27 Therefore, the objective of the study is to investigate the associations between the four phytochemicals with 23 selected proteins that play important roles in different aspects of ASC using docking and molecular dynamics simulations to identify the pivotal residues involved in the interactions. Additionally, conformational changes, deviations, fluctuations, and the complexes of protein–ligand residue interactions over 1000 ns were also scrutinized to ascertain the perspicacity of the molecular mechanical function of these phytochemicals on atherogenesis. Meanwhile, the pharmaceutical feasibility of these four compounds from COC with these 23 proteins involved in atherogenesis has not yet been divulged. Henceforth, this study confers novel insights into phytochemical-based pharmaceutical interventions from COC against the optimal atherosclerotic target.

Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis of Potential Phytoconstituents with Vital Signaling Proteins of ASC Using Glide-Maestro (Schrödinger)

The tridimensional structure of proteins was utilized for molecular docking with potential phytoconstituents from COC. Protein–ligand docking was performed using Schrödinger’s Maestro suite to elucidate the binding interactions between ligands and the target proteins. This protocol commenced with the generation of the receptor grid using the Glide module, and the biologically relevant region of the protein (active site) was defined through the SiteMap tool. The grid box was set to 20 Å, which is ample space for flexible ligand docking. Additionally, the grid parameters included a van der Waals scaling factor of 1.0 and a partial charge cutoff of 0.25. Following successful grid generation, the receptor grid files were added for subsequent docking simulations. Ligand docking was executed via the standard precision (SP) method in Glide, and the docking protocol was incorporated with nitrogen inversion and ring conformations, which is imperative to explore ligand geometries and conformations. Discussion

On a global scale, the World Health Organization (WHO) posits an estimation of 17.9 million mortalities due to cardiovascular diseases.64 The World Heart Federation (WHF) predicts that this fatality ratio will increase in 2025.1 The etiology of cardiovascular demises is primarily caused by ASC. ASC can be treated by several medicines, but they have certain adverse effects.65,66 As phytochemicals exhibit fewer side effects, they have emerged as an efficacious approach to prevent and treat various maladies, which is why they are being rendered a popular choice for drug discovery.30,31 Henceforth, the purview of the study on phytochemicals inherent in COC is that it has been traditionally used to treat cardiovascular diseases.

Lead molecule identification from plant products plays a vital role in the development of new drugs to treat CVD. Zhao et al. detailed in their review the importance of working on natural products to develop new medicines for CVD. The present study does focus on the possibilities of the development of new drugs from the COC approach. Four distinct phytochemicals from COC were meticulously selected for molecular docking analysis against 24 key signaling proteins involved in atherosclerosis to discern their drugability. Initially, the druggable properties of these phytocompounds were analyzed (Table S1), followed by molecular analysis studies. These docking studies revealed that 83 interactions exceeded −4.0 kcal/mol. The top three interactions were MIF–gallate (−7.8429 kcal/mol), PDK–epicatechin (−7.69083 kcal/mol), and prethrombin-1–gallate (−7.67423 kcal/mol), which are detailed in Tables S2–S5. The scrutiny of molecular docking was further augmented with MM-GBSA, and the ensuing findings are chronicled in Tables S6–S9. Based on the three topmost docking outcomes, they revealed MM-GBSA ΔG binding values as follows: MIF–gallate (−49.35 kcal/mol), PDK–epicatechin (−76.79 kcal/mol), and prethrombin-1–gallate (−62.73 kcal/mol), as shown in Tables S6–S9. After obtaining these results, an extensive 1000 ns molecular dynamics simulation was conducted to elucidate prominent intricacies.

According to the RMSD plots (Figure 3a), gallate interacted with the MIF backbone from 173 to 288 ns, with sustained interactions throughout the 1000 ns simulation. Intriguingly, the PDK–epicatechin complex (Figure 3b) showed slight interaction with the PDK backbone until 477 ns, after which it was completely dissociated. The final RMSD plot of the prethrombin-1–gallate complex (Figure 3c) was stable from 355 ns, showing deviations from 437 to 795 ns and 797 to 832 ns and regaining stability until 982 ns. Indeed, this underscores the nature of the complex. In light of these outcomes, the three RMSD plots provide comprehensive insights into molecular interactions with PDK as well as gallate with the macrophage migration inhibitory factor and prethrombin-1.

According to the existing research, the ubiquitous protein MIF plays a cardinal role in the orchestration of macrophages by suppressing anti-inflammatory effects.67 MIF can activate macrophages through binding to CD74, concurrently influencing the modulation of C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 2 (CXCR2) and C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4) on macrophages, which impacts their chemotactic response to chemokines and macrophage migration.68 MIF also induces foam cell formation in atherosclerosis by several mechanisms.69 One more study reports that MIF enhances the activity of the scavengers’ receptors, such as steroid receptor RNA activator 1 (SRA1), CD36, and lectin-type oxidized LDL receptor 1 (LOX-1), on macrophages, which possess the ability to organize the oxidized low-density lipoprotein (oxLDL) present in arterial walls.70 Moreover, MIF can modulate these cholesterol metabolisms of macrophages by modulating the enzymes such as acetyl-CoA acetyltransferase 1 (ACAT1) and neutral cholesterol ester hydrolase (NCEH).71

The gradual accretion of oxLDL begets the foam cell formation that leads to the genesis of primary atherosclerotic lesions, which ultimately culminates in the provocation of thrombosis.72 Prethrombin-1 plays a pivotal role in the orchestration of thrombosis. Moreover, prethrombin-1 is the cardinal pathological continuum of atherosclerotic plaques through the induction of thrombin. The disrupted internal lamina prompts the expression of the prothrombinase complex on its surface.73 These prothrombinase complex cleaves prethrombin-1, engendering the active thrombin, and this cascade leads to the formation of atherosclerotic plaques.74 These deleterious plaques insidiously usher to thrombosis within the tunica intima, which compromises the functionality of the endothelium, which precipitates a diminution in blood flow and also instigates several complications to the myocardium such as ischemia, tissue damage, and organ failure. Numerous studies underscore the thrombin as a linchpin in the coagulation cascade for the formation of an intricate meshwork of blood clots.75 During normal physiological conditions, thrombin acts as a protective mantle that thwarts fluid loss in our body; however, untoward activation begets serious complications, which is a pivotal factor in the genesis of cardiovascular diseases.

Ergo, prothrombin-1, emerges as an important cause of thrombosis-mediated atherosclerosis within the vascular domain through prothrombin-mediated thrombin activation.76 As a consequence, the outcomes of this investigation unveil the sustained interactions of gallate with MIF and prethrombin-1, which elucidates the potential of gallate as a candidate for drug discovery endeavors.

The inhibition of the MIF activity engenders a diminution in immune cell recruitment, which mitigates the inflammation milieu within the coronary artery vessels. This inhibition of MIF concurrently forestalls redundant modulation of CD74-, CRCX2-, and CRCX4-mediated signaling pathways, impeding monocyte migration.67,77 The inhibitory effects on MIF also mitigate foam cell formation by augmentation of the uptake of oxLDL and decreasing the efflux of cholesterol from macrophages. Notably, MIF inhibition reduces the expression of tumor necrosis factor α (TNF-α), interleukin 1 β (IL-1β), and the tissue factor, which are proinflammatory cytokines leading to inflammation.78 Henceforth, MIF inhibition is a viable strategy to forestall or attenuate the progression of atherosclerotic lesions. Concomitantly, prethrombin-1 inhibition yields a reduction in the generation of thrombin, whose proclivity reduces blood clotting. Meanwhile, prethrombin-1-mediated thrombin inhibition additionally attenuates the secretion of elastin and collagen, leading to both extrinsic and intrinsic thrombosis inhibition within coronary artery vessels.79 Simultaneously, the curtailment activity of the prothrombinase complex also leads to the prevention or slowdown of atherosclerosis progression. Strength and Limitations

Strength

The study harnessed an extensive simulation span (1000 ns) molecular dynamics simulation, which provides a scrupulous scrutiny of the stability and interactions of the protein–ligand complexes. Targeting the selected key proteins can increase the potential therapeutic relevance for cardiac ASC. In addition, the annals of studies witnessed the existence of optimal phytochemicals in COC. For drug discovery, the use of natural compounds from COC can reduce untoward effects compared with synthetic drugs. Furthermore, the domain of in silico approaches has been employed in this study in order to unearth novel plant-based therapeutic avenues. The identified interactions (MIF–gallate, PDK–epicatechin, and prethrombin-1–gallate) may be the primary stride to initiate further preclinical exploration.

Limitations

This endeavor is entirely computational, which, while powerful, needs experimental validation. This study can be optimized by preclinical and clinical studies after its evaluation through an in vitro protein–ligand binding assay. However, the above experiments require huge funding support, longer time duration, and manpower, which are the limitations of the study.

Conclusions

Cardiac ASC is a pivotal causative factor for cardiovascular diseases, which is the formidable etiology of mortality in the global populace. COC has been a herbal remedy for various cardiovascular maladies over the centuries. Consequently, this study focuses on the elucidation of the secondary metabolites of COC. According to the molecular docking analysis, favorable interactions between the phytochemicals with pivotal signaling proteins underpinning ASC were revealed, where gallate eloquently interacted with MIF and prethrombin-1. A subsequent MM-GBSA analysis also provides the most favorable ΔG binding values, which affirm the preeminence of these complexes. Concurrently, the 1000 ns molecular dynamics simulation studies of MIF and prethrombin-1 with gallate affirmed their nature of conformational rigidity and steadfastness. In light of the present in silico investigations, further experimental studies can facilitate innovative pharmacological interventions.

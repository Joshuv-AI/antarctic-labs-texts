Assessment of dual-fusion-radiofrequency effects on cheek dermis and fascia elasticity using elastography
Authors: Yongfeng Yao, Fan Liu, Youyou Zhou, Jian Zheng, Xue Han, Mengmeng Liu, Lisi Liao, Linghu Wu, Jianglin Zhang, Fajin Dong
Published: 2025

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveFacial skin laxity arises from age-related loss of collagen and elastic fibers in the dermis and fascia, yet the effects of emerging dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology on these deeper layers remain poorly quantified. The purpose of this article is to evaluate the impact of dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology on skin dermal and fascial elasticity.MethodsThirty volunteers (6 males and 24 females) who had never received facial aesthetics or treatments were prospectively enrolled, and received a single session of dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology treatment(Alma Thermolift 2.0, Alma Lasers Ltd, Israeli). Shear-wave elastography (11 MHz linear probe, Mindray) was used to assess the mean, maximum, and minimum changes in the elasticity of the dermis and fascial layers of both cheeks, immediately after, and on day 1, day 3, day 7, day 21 of the treatment. Furthermore, the participants were classified by gender and age (below and above 40 years old) to assess the different effects of dual-fusion-radiofrequency treatment on facial elasticity.ResultsAfter treatment, the elasticity values of the cheek dermis and fascia first declined and then peaked on day 7, with mean elasticity values increasing by + 44% (right dermis), + 41% (right fascia), + 43% (left dermis) and + 30% (left fascia). By day 21 the values had decreased slightly but remained above baseline. The mean and maximum values of elasticity of the fascial layer of the right cheek were significantly higher in males than in females at post-treatment day 7 (P = 0.008, 0.045, respectively). There was no significant difference in the change in elasticity values between volunteers under 40 years of age and those over 40 years of age (P > 0.05).ConclusionsElastography was useful in assessing the improvement in the elasticity of the dermal and fascial layers of the cheeks with dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology. Our research provides evidence-based treatment optimization indicators for clinicians, enabling them to adjust the subsequent follow-up intervals and laying the foundation for future comparative studies.

Introduction

As aging advances, there is a natural physiological depletion of collagen and elastin fibers within the skin, which are the primary contributors to the development of sagging and wrinkles [1, 2].To address this phenomenon, the field of aesthetics has been actively engaged in the pursuit of effective methods to ameliorate the visual appearance and tactile quality of the skin.

In recent years, radiofrequency (RF) technology has emerged as a significant advancement in non-invasive therapeutic approaches within the field of medical aesthetics. Its applications have broadened beyond dermatological treatments to encompass various medical specialties, including cardiology, gynecology, and gastroenterology [3, 4].The technology operates by utilizing electromagnetic waves generated from high-frequency currents that penetrate the skin, targeting the dermis and subcutaneous tissues. This process induces heating, which stimulates the growth and reorganization of collagen fibers, thereby enhancing skin firmness and elasticity [5–8]. Higher elasticity values correlate with increased collagen and elastic-fiber density in the dermis and fascia.

Building upon the foundation of radiofrequency technology, dual-fusion-radiofrequency has garnered increasing attention in the field of medical aesthetics due to its superior therapeutic outcomes and enhanced treatment depth [9]. The technique offers the additional advantage of significantly reducing pain and discomfort during the procedure. Furthermore, the incorporation of high-frequency ultrasound probes has made it possible to visualize skin elasticity [10, 11], offering a novel perspective for assessing the efficacy of skin rejuvenation treatments.

Amidst the escalating societal demand for skin rejuvenation, selecting appropriate treatment strategies has become crucial for achieving the desired aesthetic outcomes [12, 13]. The facial cheek area, being a central focus in cosmetic research, particularly warrants our heightened attention [14].

The objective of this study is to assess changes in skin elasticity following dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology using elastography, while also exploring the variation in these changes across different genders and age groups for personalized clinical practice.

Patients and methods

Study design

This study prospectively enrolled 30 volunteers between February 2024 and April 2024. All participants provided full informed consent and voluntarily signed written consent forms. Each volunteer underwent dual-fusion-radiofrequency treatment on both cheeks and had the elasticity of the dermal and fascial layers assessed at pre-treatment, immediately post-treatment, and on days 1, 3, 7, and 21. The assessments included changes in the mean, maximum, and minimum values of elasticity. Furthermore, participants were categorized based on gender and age (below and above 40 years old) to evaluate the differential effects of dual-fusion-radiofrequency treatment on facial elasticity. Subjects

This study recruited participants from the outpatient department of our hospital. Dermatologists evaluated the applicants to determine their eligibility for treatment. The inclusion criteria were as follows: aged between 18 and 80 years; absence of systemic diseases or conditions that could alter skin thickness; no skin lesions or injuries on the head or face; and no history of other facial cosmetic or therapeutic treatments. Exclusion criteria included pregnancy and lactation; history of malignant tumors; infectious diseases and compromised immune systems; implantation of electronic devices such as pacemakers or automatic defibrillators; and individuals unable to cooperate with the treatment protocol. Participants were instructed to abstain from using any skincare or beauty products within 12 h prior to the treatment. Volunteers who met the criteria were continuously included in the study until the predetermined sample size (n = 30) was reached.

Dual-fusion-radiofrequency therapy

All volunteers underwent a single session of dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology treatment(Alma Thermolift 2.0, Alma Lasers Ltd, Israeli). All treatments were performed by a single board-certified dermatologist (8 years of experience) who had completed the manufacturer-certified training course for the Alma Thermolift 2.0 system. The treatment frequency was set at 40.68 MHz, with an applied power of 300 W. To achieve optimal therapeutic outcomes, the heating duration was individually tailored to each volunteer’s skin type and thickness, ensuring a comfortable subjective sensation for the patient༈a distinct feeling of warmth and tolerable prickling sensation༉and appropriate skin response ༈mild redness and noticeable edema༉.

Prior to the treatment, a conductive coupling agent was evenly applied to the treatment area to ensure uniform energy transmission and to enhance the thermal and electrical conduction between the treatment device and the skin. During the treatment, the applicator was gently pressed against the skin surface to avoid creating significant indentations. Throughout the procedure, no volunteer reported any intolerable discomfort.

Elastography

The elastography assessment was conducted by a radiologist with more than five years of experience. Utilizing the Mindray Ultrasound System and the L11-3U convex array probe (Mindray Bio-Medical Electronics Co., Ltd.), imaging of both cheeks was performed. The detection area starts from the infraorbital foramen directly below the eyeball, with an angle parallel to the nasal bridge, thus ensuring that the area collected each time is the same. Initially, a coupling agent was applied between the probe and the skin surface to ensure that the interfaces between the epidermis, dermis, and subcutaneous tissue were clearly distinguishable and parallel in the B-mode imaging. Subsequently, the system was switched to sound touch elastography (STE) mode, where the probe was secured without the application of any external pressure, while patients were guided to relax and maintain steady breathing until the image was stabilized. The image was then captured, and measurements were taken. Region of interest (ROI) was designated within the dermal and fascial layers, with each region being measured three times to obtain an average value that served as the final result. The mean, maximum, and minimum elasticity values (in kPa) were recorded for each ROI. The ROI is placed in the dermal layer and along the direction of the fascial layer where the display is the most. The fixed width of the ROI is the width of the sampling frame, and the depth is 1.5 mm. Figure 2a and b are schematic diagrams of elastic measurement.

Statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using SPSS 27.0 statistical software (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The mean (x) and standard deviation (SD) were used to express the results. A t-test was used to compare the variability between the ultrasound elasticity data. A significance level of P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Discussion

In this study, we employed elastography to evaluate the impact of dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology on the elasticity of the dermal and fascial layers of the cheeks, as well as the changes in elasticity values over time. The results indicate that elastography is a sensitive tool capable of effectively assessing increased skin elasticity post-treatment, consistent with enhanced collagen density and tissue tightening, with peak elasticity values observed on the seventh day following treatment.

Dermatological ultrasound examination, as an emerging diagnostic method, has demonstrated unique value in the field of medical aesthetics [15]. The measurement of skin elasticity may provide information regarding disease diagnosis and progression [10]. Traditional clinical assessments rely on palpation by physicians to observe the degree of degeneration in the patient’s skin and subcutaneous tissue, which is somewhat subjective. In contrast, elastography, which measures shear wave velocity, offers a non-invasive, quantitative, and accurate method for assessing skin elasticity. It has been widely applied in the diagnosis and treatment of skin laxity, scars, and connective tissue diseases [16–18]. Research by Xiang et al. [9] has confirmed that elastography has good consistency in measuring healthy skin elasticity. In this study, elastography revealed a significant increase in elasticity values of the dermal and fascial layers seven days after treatment, which corresponds with the increased collagen observed histologically [4, 19, 20]. The in vivo response to thermal wound healing includes three stages: inflammation, proliferation, and remodeling [21], which explains why the highest skin elasticity values were observed seven days post-treatment rather than immediately after treatment.

The skin has a layered structure, including the stratum corneum, epidermis, dermis, and subcutaneous tissue, each with different physical properties [22, 23]. Previous radiofrequency technologies mainly explored elasticity changes in the dermis, whereas our dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology, using a high power of 300 W, can penetrate deep into the skin’s fascial layer, elevating the elasticity values of the fascial layer. This indicates the potential of this technology in comprehensively tightening the skin, addressing deeper skin issues that traditional radiofrequency technologies could not resolve [6]. Research by Krueger et al. has found that skin elasticity is closely related to age [2, 24], with the elasticity of the dermis and subcutaneous tissue decreasing as age advances [22, 23]. Additionally, a reduction in skin hydration can lead to lower elasticity. Although radiofrequency treatment cannot directly improve skin hydration, it can enhance skin elasticity by stimulating the production of glycosaminoglycans [4].

We observed considerable variability in skin elasticity values, which may be related to the measurement location and the direction of skin fibers [25], given the anisotropic and heterogeneous nature of skin tissue [26]. Furthermore, we found that the elasticity values of both cheeks were similar and showed the same trend over time, reflecting the consistency in anatomical structure and physiological characteristics of the contralateral sites in healthy individuals [27]. This phenomenon is significant for the diagnosis and treatment of localized skin diseases, as the elasticity values of the contralateral skin may provide information about disease progression.

With age, the skin undergoes natural physiological changes, and the histopathology of aged skin differs from that of younger skin [28]. The effectiveness of radiofrequency treatment on younger and older skin is currently a matter of debate. Skin laxity typically becomes apparent around the age of 40 [29], hence our study chose 40 years old as the age cutoff to analyze changes in skin elasticity following radiofrequency treatment among volunteers of different age groups. Our results showed no significant difference in treatment effects between the under-40 and over-40 age groups, a finding consistent with the research of Erkiert-Polguj and Tay et al. [28, 30]. However, Friedman et al. [31] found poorer treatment outcomes in elderly patients aged 46–66 treated with RF. Royo et al. [29]also pointed out that younger patients have better treatment outcomes. The discrepancies in results may be attributed to individual differences, skin types, and assessment methods. Future research should consider more variables and adopt a unified assessment method to explore the impact of radiofrequency treatment on different age groups.

Gender differences are an important consideration in dermatological aesthetics, as men and women exhibit variations in skin thickness, sebum secretion, and hormonal levels, all of which can influence the skin’s response to treatment. However, our study results indicate that, aside from differences in the mean and maximum elasticity values of the fascial layer on the right cheek, no significant disparities were observed. This suggests that radiofrequency treatment is equally effective in enhancing facial skin elasticity for both males and females. The findings imply that, despite physiological differences in skin characteristics between the genders, the efficacy of radiofrequency treatment in improving skin elasticity does not show significant gender variability. Nevertheless, research on the impact of gender differences on treatment outcomes is still limited, and further exploration is needed in the future to assess whether treatment protocols should be adjusted based on gender to achieve optimal results.

This study has certain limitations. Firstly, the relatively small sample size may restrict the generalizability of the results, and future research should be conducted with larger cohorts across multiple centers. Furthermore, it is possible to further evaluate the minimum effective energy that can maximize collagen remodeling and minimize adverse reactions, and longitudinal cohort studies are needed to determine how many treatments are required to maintain the observed increase in elasticity. Secondly, this study did not include histological assessments; nonetheless, our findings still demonstrate improvements in skin elasticity following dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology. In the future, elasticity imaging can be combined with biopsy to further clarify the biological timeline of tissue remodeling induced by radiofrequency.

Conclusions

Elastography was useful in assessing the improvement in the elasticity of the dermal and fascial layers of the cheeks with dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology. Dual-fusion-radiofrequency technology was able to improve the elasticity of the dermis and fascia of the cheeks over time, and the results were comparable between genders and ages.
